Affine Beilinson-Bernstein localization at the critical level
Sam Raskin, David Yang

Abstract
. We prove the Frenkel-Gaitsgory localization conjecture describing regular Kac-Moody representations at critical level via eigensheaves on the affine Grassmannian using categorical Moy-Prasad theory. This extends previous work of the authors.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In
[
FG06
]
, Frenkel and Gaitsgory formulated a conjectural analogue of
Beilinson-Bernstein localization for the affine Grassmannian using
critical level Kac-Moody algebras and geometric Satake. In this paper,
we prove their conjecture.

We highlight
[
FG09
]
and
[
Ras20
]
as previous works
in this area. The former proves the affine localization conjecture
after passing to Iwahori equivariant objects on both sides.
The latter, due to the first author, proves the full conjecture
for rank
1
groups
G
.

We refer to the introductions of
[
FG06
]
,
[
FG09
]
, and
[
Ras20
]

for motivation and background on the subject. We particularly
refer to
[
Ras20
]
, which is close in spirit in many ways
to the present paper.

1.2.
Methods

Our methods have some overlap with
[
Ras20
]
, but are
different in their core. Specifically, as discussed in

[
Ras20
]
Section 1.14, we use a recent general
technique for categories with
G((t))
-actions and then specify
to Kac-Moody representations:
categorical Moy-Prasad
theory
. This subject was developed by the second author in
[
Yan21
]
,
where it was already used to study critical level Kac-Moody representations.

Roughly speaking, the major outstanding problem has been to show
essential surjectivity and
t
-exactness of the global sections functor

\Gamma^{\Hecke}:D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg},

see Section
3.3
. The idea is that

[
Yan21
]

almost
shows that
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}
is of
depth
0
. We would then be reduced to checking essential surjectivity
on Iwahori invariants, where it was treated by Frenkel-Gaitsgory.

The issue is in the
almost
. Specifically,

[
Yan21
]
showed that the subcategory

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\widehat{\reg}}\subset\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
generated by
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}

under colimits has depth
0
. But
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}

itself is finicky: for instance, the forgetful functor

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}\to\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
is
not actually conservative. Moreover, it is
not a priori clear that
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}
carries a

G((t))
-action, so it is not clear that depth is a meaningful notion
for it. We refer to
[
Ras20
]
Section 1.22 for related discussion.

This paper is dedicated to solving those problems. At the same time,
we also address the
t
-exactness, which is intimately related
to the above subtleties.

This overall strategy is in contrast to
[
Ras20
]
, which relied
heavily on Whittaker techniques in place of Moy-Prasad theory.

1.3.
Notation and Background

Throughout this paper, we fix a field
k
of characteristic
0
. We also fix a split reductive group
G
over
k
and a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
. The unipotent radical of
B
will be denoted by
N
, and the Cartan will be denoted by
T
. The weight and coweight lattices will be denoted by
X^{*}(T)
and
X_{*}(T)
, respectively. The Lie algebras of
G,B
, etc. will be denoted by
{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{b}
, etc.

We use
category
to mean
\infty
-category
and
DG category

to mean
presentable stable category over
k
.
Similarly, all t-structures will be assumed to be right complete and
compatible with filtered colimits (in particular, accessible).
We let
\mathsf{DGCat}
denote Lurie’s symmetric monoidal category of DG categories;
with denote the binary operation of its tensor product by
\otimes
.

We will frequently invoke the theory of D-modules on infinite-dimensional spaces developed in
[
Ras15
]
. Occasionally, we will also use the theory of ind-coherent sheaves on such spaces, for which the reader is referred to Section 6 of
[
Ras20a
]
.

We will be interested in group actions on categories, both weak and strong.
We understand group actions on categories to always imply actions
on
DG
categories.
This theory has been developed in
[
Ber17
]
and
[
Ras20a
]
. In general, when we speak of a group action without further specification, we are referring to a strong action. We will also need the following notation for convolution. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a category acted on by
G
(or by some other group), and
K\subseteq G
is some subgroup, then we have a convolution functor
D(G/K)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}^{K}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
. This functor will be denoted by
\overset{K}{\star}
. The overset
K
will often be omitted when the subgroup is clear from context.

2.
Moy-Prasad Preliminaries

2.1.
Categorical depth filtration

This subsection and the next give a quick overview of the results in Sections 2 and 3 of
[
Yan21
]
. We recommend looking there for a more detailed exposition.

First, we need to define Moy-Prasad subgroups. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
be the weight decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and fix a point
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
as well as a nonnegative real number
r
. Then
K_{x,r}
and
K_{x,r+}
are defined as the exponentiations of the Lie subalgebras

{\mathfrak{k}}_{x,r}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,x\rangle+i\geq r}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}t^{i}

and

{\mathfrak{k}}_{x,r+}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,x\rangle+i>r}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}t^{i}.

As shorthand, we write
P_{x}
for
K_{x,0}
. This is always a parabolic subgroup of
G((t))
, justifying the notation. We also write
L_{x}
for the reductive quotient
K_{x,0}/K_{x,0+}
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category, i.e., a category equipped with a strong
G((t))
-action. Then Lemma 2.2 of
[
Yan21
]
(which in turn is based on the main theorem of
[
BGO18
]
) states:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category acted on by
G((t))
. Then there is a natural
G((t))
-equivariant fully faithful embedding

a_{x,r}:D(G((t))/K_{x,r+})\underset{D(K_{x,r+}\backslash G((t))/K_{x,r+})}{\otimes}{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}

with a continuous
G((t))
-equivariant right adjoint
a_{x,r}^{R}
.

We denote the essential image of
a_{x,r}
by
{\mathcal{C}}_{K_{x,r+}\gen}
. In the case where this is all of
{\mathcal{C}}
, we say that
{\mathcal{C}}
is
K_{x,r+}
-generated. The following gives an equivalent characterization of
{\mathcal{C}}_{K_{x},r+\gen}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}_{K_{x,r+}\gen}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
is the smallest full subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}
which both contains
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}
and inherits a
G((t))
-action from
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be another full
G((t))
-subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}
which contains
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}
. Then we must necessarily have
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}={\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+}}
, so
{\mathcal{C}}_{K_{x,r+}\gen}={\mathcal{D}}_{K_{x,r+}\gen}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}
, as desired.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category acted on by
G((t))
. The
depth filtration
on
{\mathcal{C}}
is the
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-indexed filtration with
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}
the smallest full DG subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}
containing
{\mathcal{C}}_{K_{x,r+}\gen}
for every
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

Let us list some properties of the depth filtration.

Proposition 2.4
.

(1)

For a fixed
G
, there is a discrete set of rational numbers such that, for any
G((t))
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
, the depth filtration on
{\mathcal{C}}
can only jump at those numbers.

(2)

The depth filtration is exhaustive, i.e., we have

\operatorname{colim}{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}\cong{\mathcal{C}}.

(3)

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}={\mathcal{C}}
. Then for any other
G((t))
-category
{\mathcal{D}}
with
{\mathcal{D}}_{\leq r}=0
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{G((t))}{\mathcal{D}}\cong 0
.

Proof.

(1)

This is Lemma 2.6 of
[
Yan21
]
.

(2)

This is proven in the discussion after Definition 2.5 of
[
Yan21
]
.

(3)

This is Lemma 2.9 of
[
Yan21
]
.

∎

2.2.
Moy-Prasad generators

The main technical tool of
[
Yan21
]
is a construction of 2-categorical generators for each depth. Let us rephrase this result in a form amenable to our purposes.

Fix some depth
r>0
. For any
G((t))
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
, we have an action of
D(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})
(with the convolution monoidal structure) on
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}
. A priori,
K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+}
has only the structure of a group, but from unwinding the definitions we see that in fact it has a
k
-vector space structure. Thus, the Fourier-Deligne transform of D-modules (see e.g., Section 5.1 of
[
Ber17
]
) identifies the monoidal category
D(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})
with the monoidal category
D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*})
.

The vector space
K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+}
is naturally equipped with an
L_{x}
action, and hence so is the dual vector space
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*}
. Let
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ}
denote the locus of GIT-semistable elements, i.e., elements whose
L_{x}
orbit does not contain zero in its closure. Equivalently,
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ}
is the complement of the preimage of zero along
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*}\rightarrow(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*}//L_{x}
. This is an open subvariety of
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*}
. We define

{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\underset{D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*})}{\otimes}D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ}).

Note that the operation of
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariants commutes with both limits and colimits.

The complement of
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ}
, i.e., the closed locus of GIT-unstable elements, will be denoted by
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}
. In analogy with the definition of
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
, we define

{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},us}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\underset{D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*})}{\otimes}D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}).

The relevance of these definitions is explained by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Yan21
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category. Then the subcategories
{\mathcal{C}}_{<r}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}
coincide if and only if we have, for all
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
,

{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong 0.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq r}={\mathcal{C}}
. By Theorem 3.2 of
[
Yan21
]
, we have
D(G((t)))^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong D(G((t))/K_{x,r+})_{\geq r}
. In particular, part (3) of Proposition
2.4
implies that

{\mathcal{C}}_{<r}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong{\mathcal{C}}_{<r}\underset{G((t))}{\otimes}D(G((t))/K_{x,r+})_{\geq r}\cong 0.

We can therefore quotient
{\mathcal{C}}
by
{\mathcal{C}}_{<r}
and reduce to the case where
{\mathcal{C}}_{<r}\cong 0
. It remains to show, under our assumptions, that
{\mathcal{C}}
itself is trivial if and only if all the categories
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
are trivial.

The only if direction is now obvious, so let us treat the if direction. Assume
{\mathcal{C}}
is not trivial. Then some
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}
is nontrivial. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}
, or equivalently, that

{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},us}\cong{\mathcal{C}}\underset{G((t))}{\otimes}D(G((t)))^{K_{x,r+},us}\cong 0.

This again follows from part (3) of Proposition
2.4
and Theorem 3.2 of
[
Yan21
]
.
∎

Let us give some properties of the unstable locus. They will come in handy later when we need variations of Theorem
2.5
. We note that Lemma
2.6
is a key geometric input for Theorem 3.2 of
[
Yan21
]
(and hence for Theorem
2.5
as well.)

Lemma 2.6
.

There is an equality of subvarieties

(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}=L_{x}\cdot(\displaystyle\bigcup_{y\mid K_{x,r+}\subseteq K_{y,r}\subseteq K_{x,r}}(K_{y,r}/K_{x,r+})^{\perp}).

Proof.

Let
p
be a point of
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}
. By the Hilbert-Mumford criterion, there is a one-parameter subgroup
\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow L_{x}
which contracts
p
to the origin. This subgroup is necessarily a conjugate of a one-parameter subgroup of
T
corresponding to some
\beta\in X_{*}(T)
. Equivalently, there is some point
q\in L_{x}\cdot p
which is contracted by
\beta
, so there is an inclusion

q\in\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle>0}{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}^{*}t^{i_{\alpha}}\bigoplus.

Then for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, if we take
y=x+\epsilon\beta
, we have
K_{x,r+}\subseteq K_{y,r}\subseteq K_{x,r}
and

q\in(K_{y,r}/K_{x,r+})^{\perp}.

This implies the inclusion
p\in L_{x}\cdot(K_{y,r}/K_{x,r+})^{\perp}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

The variety
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}
is the union of a finite number of
L_{x}
-orbits.

Proof.

We rewrite the statement in terms of graded Lie algebras. Express
r
as a fraction
\frac{p}{q}
in lowest terms. Then we have a
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
grading on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, defined by

{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle x,\alpha\rangle\equiv\frac{pi}{q}\pmod{1}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}.

This grading is compatible with the Lie algebra structure in the sense that
[{\mathfrak{g}}_{i},{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{i+j}
.

In this language, the vector space
K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+}
can be identified with
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}
, and
L_{x}
can be identified with the exponentiation of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
. The lemma now follows from Theorem 4 of
[
Vin76
]
.
∎

The rest of this section will be devoted to the case of
r=0
. The naive analogue of Theorem
2.5
would say that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 0}
is trivial if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,0+}}
is trivial for all
x
. This is easy to prove, but we will need a more refined statement.

2.3.
Categorical representations of reductive groups

First we need to study the categorical representation theory of (finite type) reductive groups. Let
H
be a reductive algebraic group over
k
. (We use the letter
H
here to emphasize that it may differ from the group
G
fixed in the rest of the paper.) The Borel, Cartan, etc. subgroups of
H
will be denoted by
B_{H}
,
T_{H}
, etc.

Fix a
H
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
. Our main lemma relates the categories
{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}\cong{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
and
({\mathcal{C}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\cong{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(H/N_{H})^{T_{H},w}
. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism gives an action of
\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}
, where the
W_{H}
action on
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}
is the dot action. On the other hand, the tangent vector fields for the right action of
T_{H}
on
H/N_{H}
give an action of
\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})
on
({\mathcal{C}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}
.

Let
S
be the collection of elements
\check{\alpha}+\langle\rho,\check{\alpha}\rangle+n\in\Sym{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
where
\check{\alpha}
ranges over positive coroots of
H
and
n
ranges over positive integers. Then we define an affine scheme
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}
as
\Spec
of the localization
\Sym{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}[S^{-1}]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

There are adjoint functors

\operatorname{Loc}:{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})\rightleftarrows({\mathcal{C}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}):\Gamma

with
\operatorname{Loc}
fully faithful. Furthermore, if
{\mathcal{C}}
has a t-structure compatible with the
H
-action, then
\Gamma
is t-exact for the t-structures induced by Lemma
2.10
.

Proof.

To construct
\Gamma
and
\operatorname{Loc}
, it suffices to consider the universal case of
{\mathcal{C}}\cong D(G)
. In this case, we are looking for an adjoint pair

\operatorname{Loc}:{\mathfrak{h}}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})\rightleftarrows(D(H/N_{H}))^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}):\Gamma.

These are provided by the global sections and localization functors of
[
BB81
]
. By
loc. cit
., in this case
\operatorname{Loc}
is fully faithful and
\Gamma
is t-exact.

For general
{\mathcal{C}}
, we must still have
\Gamma\circ\operatorname{Loc}\cong\operatorname{id}
and so
\operatorname{Loc}
is still fully faithful. By Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.13
,
\operatorname{Loc}
and
\Gamma
are right t-exact. As
\Gamma
has left adjoint
\operatorname{Loc}
, we see that
\Gamma
must also be left t-exact, as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

The functor

D(H/N_{H})\underset{D(N_{H}\backslash H/N_{H})}{\otimes}{\mathcal{C}}^{N_{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

By
[
BGO18
]
, this functor is fully faithful. Denote the quotient category by
{\mathcal{D}}
. We need to show that
{\mathcal{D}}
is trivial.

A short calculation shows that
{\mathcal{D}}^{N_{H}}\cong 0
. Thus, fully faithfulness of
\operatorname{Loc}
in Lemma
2.8
implies that
{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w}\otimes_{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})
is trivial. We claim that the functor

{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})

is conservative. As
\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})
is self dual as a
\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})
-module, this functor can be rewritten as

\operatorname{Hom}_{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}(\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H}),{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}(\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}),{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w}),

which is conservative if the pushforward functor
f_{*}:\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})\rightarrow\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})
generates under colimits. As
f:{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H}
is faithfully flat, the image of
f_{*}
contains a skyscraper sheaf at each geometric point. And because
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H}
is smooth,
\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})
is generated under colimits by skyscraper sheaves, giving the conservativeness.

In our case, this shows that
{\mathcal{D}}^{H,w}
is trivial. By 1-affineness of
BH
(Theorem 2.2.2 of
[
Gai14
]
), this implies that
{\mathcal{D}}
is trivial, as desired.
∎

The t-structures appearing in Lemma
2.8
have the following source.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f:\Spec B\rightarrow\Spec A
be a flat morphism of affine schemes. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category equipped with a t-structure and an action of
A{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}
. Then there is a unique t-structure on
{{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{A\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}B\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}
for which the pullback and pushforward functors

f_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{C}}\underset{A\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}{\otimes}B\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}:f_{{\mathcal{C}},*}

are both t-exact. Also,
f_{{\mathcal{C}},*}
is conservative.

Proof.

To show conservativeness, note that this property of a right adjoint is preserved under tensor product. Thus, it suffices to check that
B\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}\rightarrow A\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
is conservative, which is obvious. Furthermore, by Barr-Beck-Lurie, the functor
f_{{\mathcal{C}},*}
is monadic.

The existence of the desired t-structure on will thus follow from t-exactness of the monad
f_{{\mathcal{C}},*}\circ f_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}
. This monad can be identified with the tensor product functor
-\otimes_{A}B:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
, where
B
is treated as an
A
-module. By Lazard’s theorem,
B
is a filtered colimit of finite free
A
-modules. Tensoring by a finite free
A
-module is clearly t-exact, so
-\otimes_{A}B
is also t-exact, as desired.
∎

2.4.
Depth zero

Now we can analyze the
r=0
case more fully. Recall that we denote the Iwahori subgroup by
I
and its unipotent radical by
I_{0}
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category. We have an equality of subcategories

{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 0}={\mathcal{C}}_{I_{0}\gen}\subset{\mathcal{C}}.

In particular,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 0}
is trivial if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}^{I_{0}}
is trivial.

Proof.

As
I_{0}
is a subgroup of the form
K_{x,0+}
, the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{0}\gen}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 0}
is clear. By Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to show that, for all
x\in X_{*}(T)
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,0+}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{0}\gen}
.

Note that for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, the subgroup
K_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}
does not depend on
\epsilon
. (Recall that
\check{\rho}
denotes the half-sum of positive coroots.) Indeed, unwinding the definitions, we have an equality of Lie algebras

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{k}}_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,x+\epsilon\check{\rho},\rangle+i>0}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,x\rangle+i>0}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}t^{i}\displaystyle+\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\langle\alpha,x\rangle+i\geq 0\\

To go from

Corollary 2.12
.

Let

be a

-category. Assume that, for all

Proof.

3.
Kac-Moody categories

To state the main theorem of this paper, we will need to define various categories of representations of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
. Recall that
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is defined as a central extension of the loop algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}((t))
, and that central characters are in bijection with invariant bilinear forms on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For the rest of this paper, we will only be concerned with critical level representations, i.e., those
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}
-modules where the center acts via the character corresponding to negative one half times the Killing form. Notationally, this will be reflected by a subscript, e.g.,
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}
.

This section is effectively a quick overview of the results of Sections 6-7 of
[
Ras20
]
, and we will often refer there for proofs.

3.1.
The category
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}

Our starting point is the category
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}

[
Ras20a
]

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}^{\heartsuit}

-representations. However, we warn the reader that

More precisely, consider the subcategory

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
is defined

Actually, in

contains objects in cohomological degree

The advantage of this renormalization is that

G((t))
. There is also an additional structure which we will need. Let

3.2.
The category
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\reg}

There is a natural closed subscheme

The abelian category of such representations is easy to define. However, as in the previous subsection, there are subtleties of renormalization in defining the derived category. We will define two categories, denoted by

Invariant subcategories

The goal of this section is to collect some results on invariant subcategories of
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})
and
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}
. These results form the concrete input needed for the proof of Theorem
3.10
.

4.1.
I_{0}
-invariants

The following theorem is the main fact we shall need about the behavior of
\Gamma^{\operatorname{Hecke,naive}}
on
I_{0}
-equivariant objects. It is essentially the main theorem of
[
FG09
]
. However,
[
FG09
]
does not state their result in terms of derived categories, so instead we use the phrasing of
[
Ras20
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
Ras20
]
Theorem 8.2.1,
[
FG09
]
Theorem 1.7)
.

The functor
\Gamma^{\operatorname{Hecke,naive}}
induces a t-exact equivalence

D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{I_{0},+}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}^{I_{0},+}

on eventually coconnective
I_{0}
-equivariant categories.

For the proof of Lemma
3.13
, we will need a related statement adapted to
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg}}
. Because of the lack of an a priori
G((t))
-action, we cannot speak directly of
I_{0}
-invariant objects in
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg}}
. Even if we could, there would be no convolution functor. Instead, as a proxy, we will look at the images of convolutions with
I_{0}
-invariant objects in
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any compact object
{\mathcal{G}}\in D(G((t))/I_{0})
, the functor

\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star-):D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{I_{0}}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg}}

is left t-exact up to shift.

Remark 4.3
.

The case of trivial
{\mathcal{G}}
is Lemma 8.2.2 of
[
Ras20
]
. The proof we give below is an adaptation of the one in
loc. cit
. to our case.

Proof.

Choose an integer
r
for which the conclusion of Lemma
7.1
is satisfied. We will show that
\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star-)[r]
is left t-exact. Because
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}
is right complete, it suffices to show that, for
{\mathcal{F}}\in D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{I_{0},\heartsuit}
, we have

\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}})\in(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg}})^{\geq r}.

Note that if we replace
\Gamma^{\Hecke}
with
\Gamma^{\Heckenaive}
, we have

\Gamma^{\Heckenaive}({\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}})\cong{\mathcal{G}}\star\Gamma^{\Heckenaive}({\mathcal{F}})\in(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive})^{\geq r}

by Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
7.1
. Thus, by Theorem
3.3
, it suffices to show that
\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}})
is eventually coconnective.

By Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.18 of
[
FG09
]
,
{\mathcal{F}}
can be written as a filtered colimit of objects
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\in D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{I_{0},\heartsuit}
admitting finite filtrations with subquotients of the form
\operatorname{ind}^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{i,j})\otimes_{\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg}}{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}
for
{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}\in D_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{I_{0},\heartsuit}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}\in\QCoh(\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg})^{\heartsuit}
. It thus suffices to show that, for any
i
and
j
, we have

\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star\operatorname{ind}^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{i,j})\underset{\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j})\in(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg}})^{\geq r},

or equivalently, that
\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star\operatorname{ind}^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{i,j})\otimes_{\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg}}{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j})
is eventually coconnective.

As
\QCoh(\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg})^{\heartsuit}
is compactly generated, we can immediately reduce to the case of
{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}
coherent. And as
\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg}
is an infinite-dimensional affine space, any coherent sheaf is perfect. Therefore, to prove eventual coconnectivity, the case of
{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}
coherent further reduces to the case where
{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}
is equivalent to the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Op_{\check{G}}^{\reg}}
.

So we just need to show that

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\Hecke}({\mathcal{G}}\star\operatorname{ind}^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}({\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}))

\displaystyle\cong\Gamma^{\Hecke}(\operatorname{ind}^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}({\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}))

\displaystyle\cong\Gamma^{\IndCoh}(\Gr_{G},{\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j})

is eventually coconnective. Applying Lemma
7.1
again, we see that
{\mathcal{G}}\star{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}\in D_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})^{\geq r}
. By Corollary 7.15.2 of
[
Ras20
]
,
\Gamma^{\IndCoh}(\Gr_{G},-)
is t-exact, giving the desired statement.
∎

4.2.
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariants

It will turn out that both
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})
and
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}
are purely of depth zero, i.e., are equal to their depth
\leq 0
truncations. By Theorem
2.5
, this is equivalent to saying that for all
x
and
r
, their
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariant categories are trivial.

The following lemma will help show this in both cases.

Lemma 4.4
.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category purely of depth zero, with a right action of a monoidal category
{\mathcal{A}}
that commutes with the
G((t))
-action. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-module category. Then the category

{\mathcal{C}}\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}}

is purely of depth zero.

(2)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category purely of depth zero, with a right coaction of a comonoidal category
{\mathcal{A}}
that commutes with the
G((t))
-action. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-comodule category. Then the category

{\mathcal{C}}\overset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}}

is purely of depth zero.

Proof.

Let us first treat the case of
{\mathcal{A}}
monoidal. We would like to show that

({\mathcal{C}}\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}})^{K_{x,r+},\circ}

is trivial for any choices of
x
and
r
. But the operation of
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariants commutes with tensor products, so we have

\displaystyle({\mathcal{C}}\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}})^{K_{x,r+},\circ}

\displaystyle\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\cong 0\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\otimes}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\cong 0.

as desired.

The case of
{\mathcal{A}}
comonoidal proceeds similarly, as taking
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariants also commutes with cotensor products.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The category
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})
is purely of depth zero.

Proof.

Start by noting that
D(G((t))/K_{0,0+})
is tautologically
K_{0,0+}
-generated and hence purely of depth zero. As
K_{0,0+}
is a normal subgroup of
K_{0,0}\cong G[[t]]
, with quotient
G
, we have

D(\Gr_{G})\cong D(G((t))/G[[t]])\cong D(G((t))/K_{0,0+})^{G}\cong D(G((t))/K_{0,0+})\underset{D(G)}{\otimes}\operatorname{Vect}.

Thus, by the first part of Lemma
4.4
,
D(\Gr_{G})
is purely of depth zero. As

D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})\cong D(\Gr_{G})\underset{\operatorname{Rep}(\check{G})}{\otimes}\QCoh(\Op_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{reg}}),

another application of Lemma
4.4
shows that
D^{\Hecke_{{\mathfrak{z}}}}_{\crit}(\Gr_{G})
is purely of depth zero, as desired.
∎

Now we treat the case of
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}
. We will need the following special case of Theorem 4.3 of
[
Yan21
]
, which computes the depth filtration on
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}
.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
Yan21
]
Theorem 4.3, Case
r=0
)
.

There is an equivalence of subcategories

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\leq 0}\cong\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}\overset{\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}})}{\otimes}\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}}),

where
\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}}
is the formal completion of
\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}
inside
\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
.

Properly equipped, let us proceed to the promised theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

The category
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}
is purely of depth zero.

Proof.

Note that
\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}})
has a natural coalgebra structure. Furthermore, as
\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\operatorname{reg}}\subseteq\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}},\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\operatorname{reg}})
is naturally a comodule over
\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}})
. Thus, we can write:

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}\overset{\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}})}{\otimes}\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\operatorname{reg}})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}\overset{\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}})}{\otimes}\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}}))\overset{\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}})}{\otimes}\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\operatorname{reg}})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\leq 0}\overset{\IndCoh^{*}(\widehat{\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\leq 0}})}{\otimes}\IndCoh^{*}(\Op_{\check{{\mathfrak{g}}}}^{\operatorname{reg}}).

The second part of Lemma
4.4
now implies the desired statement.

∎

5.
Generation

5.1.
Reduction step

As shorthand, we say that a (not necessarily fully faithful) functor
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
between categories generates under colimits if the essential image of
f
generates
{\mathcal{D}}
under colimits.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
be two categories and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
be a functor between them. Then
f
generates under colimits if and only if the quotient category, i.e., the colimit of the pushout diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

is trivial.

Proof.

It is well known (see e.g. Lemma I.1.5.4.3 of
[
GR17
]
) that
f
generates under colimits iff its (not necessarily continuous) right adjoint
f^{R}
is conservative. Conservativeness is equivalent to
f^{R}
having trivial kernel, and by Corollary 5.5.3.4 of
[
Lur09
]
, the kernel of
f^{R}
can be identified with the quotient of
f
, giving the desired statement.
∎

We will deduce Lemma
3.11
from the following auxillary statement.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}
be
G((t))
-categories and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
be a
G((t))
-equivariant functor. Then
f
generates under colimits if and only if the functors

f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ},

for all choices of
r>0
and
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, as well as the functor

f^{I_{0}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{I_{0}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{I_{0}},

all generate under colimits.

Proof.

First we show the “only if” direction. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the quotient of
{\mathcal{D}}
by
{\mathcal{C}}
. By Lemma
5.1
,
{\mathcal{E}}
must be trivial.

As the functor of
I_{0}
-invariants commutes with colimits, the quotient of
{\mathcal{D}}^{I_{0}}
by
{\mathcal{C}}^{I_{0}}
is
{\mathcal{E}}^{I_{0}}
. Similarly, the quotient of
{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
by
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
is
{\mathcal{E}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial, these quotients must also be trivial, and hence the corresponding functors must generate under colimits, as desired.

Now let us show the “if” direction. Reversing the logic, we see that we have

{\mathcal{E}}^{I_{0}}\cong 0

and, for all
r>0
and
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
,

{\mathcal{E}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong 0,

and we need to show that
{\mathcal{E}}\cong 0
. But this is exactly the statement of Corollary
2.12
.
∎

5.2.
The category
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}

Recall that we defined
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\operatorname{reg,naive}}
to be the full subcategory of
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}
generated by
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}^{+}
under colimits. We will need a few properties of this category which were proven in
[
Ras20
]
. As the proofs would take us slightly afield, we do not reproduce them here.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
Ras20
]
, Lemma 9.2.1)
.

There is a (necessarily unique) structure of
G((t))
-category on
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}
compatible with the inclusion into
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}
.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
Ras20
]
, Corollary 9.2.3)
.

\Gamma^{\operatorname{Hecke,naive}}
factors through
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}
.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
Ras20
]
, Corollary 9.2.4)
.

Let
K\subseteq G[[t]]
be a prounipotent group scheme. Then
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}^{K}
is the subcategory of
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}^{K}
generated under colimits by
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}^{K,+}
.

We will also need one new lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Choose
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
. Then

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong 0.

Proof.

Consider the map

\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}.

We will compute its kernel
{\mathcal{A}}
in two different ways. (The kernel of a map
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
is defined to be the pullback of
{\mathcal{C}}
along the map
0\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
.) First, since the operation of taking
(K_{x,r+},\circ)
-invariants commutes with limits, we have

{\mathcal{A}}\cong(\operatorname{ker}\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive})^{K_{x,r+},\circ}.

As the map
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}
is the inclusion of a full subcategory, it has trivial kernel, and so
{\mathcal{A}}
must also be trivial.

On the other hand, Theorem
4.7
shows that
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}_{\regnaive}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\cong 0
. Thus,
{\mathcal{A}}
can also be identified with
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
. Comparing these two computations, we get the desired equivalence.
∎

Now we are prepared to prove Lemma
3.11
.

5.3.
Proof of Lemma
3.11

Proof.

By Lemma

, we know that the image of
\Gamma^{\operatorname{Hecke,naive}}
lies in
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\crit}\widetilde{\text{\textendash}\operatorname{mod}}_{\regnaive}

6.
Exactness

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma
3.12
. We start by formulating a general criterion for a
G((t))
-equivariant functor to be left t-exact.

6.1.
Reduction step

We will use an analogue of Lemma
5.2
. To state it, let us note that for a
G((t))
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
with a compatible t-structure, Lemma
A.13
constructs a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}
be
G((t))
-categories equipped with compatible t-structures and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
be a
G((t))
-equivariant functor. Then
f
is left t-exact if and only if the functors

f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ},

for all choices of
r>0
and
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, as well as the functor

f^{I_{0}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{I_{0}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{I_{0}},

are all left t-exact.

Proof.

Let us quickly dispatch of the only if case. The left t-exactness of
f^{I_{0}}
is clear, and the left t-exactness of
f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
follows from the commutativity of the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\otimes D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+}}\otimes D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,\circ}).}

Indeed, the vertical arrows are conservative and t-exact by Lemma
A.13
, and the bottom arrow is left t-exact by Proposition
A.6
.

Now we treat the (much harder) if direction. We will show that for any choice of
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
r\geq 0
, the functor
f^{K_{x,r+}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+}}
is left t-exact. This implies that
f
is left t-exact. Indeed, for any object
c\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle f(c)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{colim}\operatorname{Oblv}(\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{K_{0,r+}}f(c))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{colim}\operatorname{Oblv}(f^{K_{0,r+}}(\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{K_{0,r+}}c))

and the result follows from left t-exactness of
\operatorname{Av}_{*}
.

Our proof will be by induction. First we deal with the base case.

6.1.1.
Depth zero

Assume
r=0
. We will emulate the proof of Theorem
2.11
. Recall from that proof that any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
gives the same subgroup
K_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}
, and that
K_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}
is a conjugate of
I_{0}
. In particular, the functor
f^{K_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}}
is left t-exact.

Let
N_{x}
denote the subgroup
(K_{x,0+}(P_{x}\cap N((t))))/K_{x,0+}
of
L_{x}
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.11
,
N_{x}
is a maximal unipotent subgroup of
L_{x}
. We have an equivalence
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x+\epsilon\check{\rho},0+}}\cong({\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,0+}})^{N_{x}}
. The left t-exactness of
f^{K_{x,0+}}
now follows from the following purely finite-dimensional lemma (taking
H
to be
L_{x}
and
g
to be
f^{K_{x,0+}}
):

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
H
be a reductive algebraic group over
k
, with Borel, Cartan, etc. subgroups denoted by
B_{H},T_{H}
, etc. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be two
H
-categories equipped with compatible t-structures, and let
g:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
be a
H
-equivariant functor. If
g^{N_{H}}:{\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}}
is left t-exact, then
g
is left t-exact.

Proof.

We argue via a sequence of commutative diagrams. They will implicitly introduce some functors
g_{i}
, which are all induced by
g
. First, examine the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}

The vertical arrows are conservative and t-exact by part (4) of Lemma
A.10
. As
g_{1}
is left t-exact by assumption, we deduce that
g_{2}
is left t-exact.

Next, we have:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}

By Lemma
2.10
, the vertical arrows are conservative and t-exact. From the previous diagram, we learned that
g_{2}
is left t-exact. Hence,
g_{3}
is left t-exact.

Now we use Lemma
2.8
. Compatibility of
\Gamma
with
H
-equivariant functors gives commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{B}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}

6.1.2.
Positive depth

Now we treat the inductive step. Assume that for all
s<r
and
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, we know that
f^{K_{x,s+}}
is left t-exact. Now, fixing a choice of

f^{K_{x,r+}}
is left t-exact.

There are two natural adjoint pairs

i_{{\mathcal{C}},*}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},us}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}:i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}

and

j_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}:j_{{\mathcal{C}},*}.

For any object

i_{{\mathcal{C}},*}i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c\rightarrow c\rightarrow j_{{\mathcal{C}},*}j_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c\rightarrow i_{{\mathcal{C}},*}i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c[1].

We have analogous functors
i_{{\mathcal{D}},*}
, etc. As
f
is
G((t))
-equivariant,
f^{K_{x,r+}}
intertwines the functors associated to
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
. Thus, we get an exact triangle

i_{{\mathcal{D}},*}f^{K_{x,r+},us}(i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c)\rightarrow f^{K_{x,r+}}(c)\rightarrow j_{{\mathcal{D}},*}f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}(j_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c)\rightarrow i_{{\mathcal{D}},*}f^{K_{x,r+},us}(i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}c)[1].

In particular, to show that
f
is left t-exact, it suffices to show the same for
i_{{\mathcal{D}},*}\circ f\circ i_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}
and
j_{{\mathcal{D}},*}\circ f\circ j_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{!}
. By Lemma
A.14
, the functors
i_{*}
and

are t-exact, and so their right adjoints
i^{!}
and
j_{*}
must be left t-exact. Thus, it suffices to show that
f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
and
f^{K_{x,r+},us}
are left t-exact. We assumed that
f^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
is left t-exact, so we can restrict our attention to the case of
f^{K_{x,r+},us}
.

More generally, for

and a functor

via tensor product. Let

be an open subvariety with complement

. The above argument then shows that if
f^{K_{x,r+},U}
and
f^{K_{x,r+},Z}
are left t-exact, then so is
f^{K_{x,r+},V}
.

By Lemma
2.7
,
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}
is the union of a finite number of
L_{x}
-orbits. We therefore can reduce the left t-exactness of
f^{K_{x,r+},us}
to the left t-exactness of the functors

for

L_{x}
-orbit in
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}
. Fix such an orbit

.

Now, Lemma

tells us that there exists a point

and

such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\underset{D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*})}{\otimes}D((K_{y,r}/K_{x,r+})^{\perp})

This leads to a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{x,r+},p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{y,r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}^{K_{y,r}}.}

As

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},O}}

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
B
be a finite type algebraic group and let
A\subseteq B
be a normal subgroup which is isomorphic to the algebraic group underlying a
k
-vector space (which, by abuse of notation, we also denote by
A
). Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a category acted on by
B
and let

be an additive character of

. If
O
denotes the
B

p
inside

and

denotes the stabilizer of

{\mathcal{E}}\underset{A}{\otimes}D(O)\cong(D(p)\underset{A}{\otimes}{\mathcal{E}})\underset{S_{p}}{\otimes}D(B).

Furthermore, if

is equipped with a t-structure compatible with the

Proof.

It suffices to consider the universal case of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(B)\underset{A}{\otimes}D(O)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(D(p)\underset{A}{\otimes}D(B))\underset{S_{p}}{\otimes}D(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(B)\otimes D(O)}

7.1.
Two auxilliary lemmas

7.1.1.
Boundedness of convolution

The following lemma shows (under appropriate hypotheses) that convolution is left t-exact up to shift. We actually will not use it in this section (though it was used earlier in this paper.) However, it explains why the statement of Lemma
7.3
is reasonable.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
K\subseteq G((t))
be a compact open subgroup and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a compact object of
D(G((t))/K)
. Then there is some integer
r
such that, for any
G((t))
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
with a compatible t-structure, the convolution functor

{\mathcal{G}}\star-:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}

sends
{\mathcal{C}}^{\geq 0}
to
{\mathcal{C}}^{\geq r}
.

Proof.

This is essentially Lemma 9.2.2 of
[
Ras20
]
. We reproduce the proof here.

By compactness of
{\mathcal{G}}
, it is supported on some (finite type) subscheme
S\subseteq G((t))/K
. Then
{\mathcal{G}}
has a finite resolution by inductions
\operatorname{ind}({\mathcal{G}}_{i})
of bounded below ind-coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\in\IndCoh(S)^{+}
. So we can assume that
{\mathcal{G}}
is of the form
\operatorname{ind}({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\in\IndCoh(S)^{\geq 0}
.

The functors of strong convolution with
\operatorname{ind}({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
and weak convolution with
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
are canonically isomorphic. By Lemma 10.16.1 of
[
Ras20a
]
, weak convolution with
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is left exact, as desired.
∎

7.1.2.
Boundedness and invariant categories

The other Lemma we prove will be used below in the proof of Lemma
7.3
. It will allow us “deequivariantize” boundedness statements on categories of weak invariants.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
H
be a finite type algebraic group and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category with a weak
H
action and a compatible t-structure. Assume that we are given a functor
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
with
{\mathcal{D}}
a category with a t-structure. Then

is left t-exact up to shift if and only if
f\circ\operatorname{Oblv}:{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
is left t-exact up to shift.

Proof.

The only if case follows immediately from Lemma
A.10
. Let us treat the if case. Taking an appropriate shift, we may assume that
f
is left t-exact.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an object in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\geq 0}
. Identifying
\operatorname{Oblv}\circ\operatorname{Av}^{H,w}
with the convolution functor
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}\star-:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
, we have

f({\mathcal{O}}_{H}\star{\mathcal{F}})\cong f(\operatorname{Oblv}(\operatorname{Av}^{H,w}{\mathcal{F}}))\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\geq 0},

using Lemma
A.10
again. It follows that for any projective coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{M}}
on
H
, we again have

f({\mathcal{M}}\star{\mathcal{F}})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\geq 0}.

As
H
is smooth, any coherent sheaf on
H
has a finite resolution by projective coherent sheaves. In particular, the skyscraper sheaf at the identity (which we denote by
1_{H}
) admits such a resolution. Thus, we can conclude that

f({\mathcal{F}})\cong f(1_{H}\star{\mathcal{F}})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{+}

as desired.
∎

7.2.
Reduction step

Before stating the key lemma, let us introduce a convenient notational shorthand. Assume we have a functor
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
G((t))
-category and
{\mathcal{D}}
is any category. For
K\subseteq G((t))
a compact open subgroup and
{\mathcal{G}}
a compact object of
D(G((t))/K)
, we will write
f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{K}
for the functor
f({\mathcal{G}}\star-):{\mathcal{C}}^{K}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
. When
{\mathcal{G}}
is the skyscraper sheaf at the identity,

can be identified with the composition
{\mathcal{C}}^{K}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
and will be denoted simply by
f|_{K}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
G((t))
-category with a compatible t-structure, and let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a category with a t-structure. Assume that
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
is a functor satisfying the following two properties.

•

For any compact object
{\mathcal{G}}
in
D(G((t))/I_{0})
,
f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{I_{0}}
is left t-exact up to shift.

•

For any point
x\in\mathbb{X}_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, real number
r>0
, and compact object
{\mathcal{G}}\in D(G((t))/K_{x,r+})
, the restriction of
f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{K_{x,r+}}
to
{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},\circ}
is left t-exact up to shift.

Then for any compact open subgroup
K\subseteq G((t))
and any compact object
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq D(G((t))/K)
,
f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{K}
is left t-exact up to shift.

Proof.

As every compact open subgroup contains some
K_{x,r+}
, it suffices to show that each

is left t-exact up to shift, for any
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
and

. We prove this by induction. At each step of the induction, it suffices to show left t-exactness up to shift of
f|_{K_{x,r+}}
. Indeed, the case of
f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{K_{x,r+}}
follows by replacing

with

.

7.2.1.
Depth zero

For the base case, we use the same setup as in Section
6.1.1
. In that notation, we need to show the left t-exactness up to shift of
({\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,0+}})^{N_{x}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
. Once again, taking
H
to be
L_{x}
and
g
to be
f|_{K_{x,0+}}
,this follows from a finite-dimensional lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
H
be a reductive algebraic group over
k
, with Borel, Cartan, etc. subgroups denoted by
B_{H},T_{H}
, etc. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a
H
-category with a compatible t-structure,
{\mathcal{B}}
be a category with a t-structure, and
g
be a functor
{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
. If
g_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{N_{H}}
is left t-exact up to shift for any compact
{\mathcal{G}}\in D(H/N_{H})
, then
g
itself is left t-exact up to shift.

Proof.

We claim that we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}))^{\geq 0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\operatorname{Loc}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where the functor from
({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}
to
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by
x\mapsto({\mathcal{D}}_{H/N_{H}}\overset{N_{H}}{\star}x)^{T_{H}}
. Indeed, to check the commutativity of the middle square it suffices to consider the universal case of
{\mathcal{A}}\cong D(H)
, where the commutativity follows by unwinding the definition of
\Gamma
.

Note that we have
\Gamma\circ\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\operatorname{Loc}\cong\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\Gamma\circ\operatorname{Loc}\cong\tau^{\geq 0}\cong\operatorname{id}
on
({\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\otimes_{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H})}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}))^{\geq 0}
. In particular, to show left t-exactness up to shift of the composition

{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}},

it suffices to show that the composed arrow from top left to bottom right lands in
{\mathcal{B}}^{+}
. As
\tau^{\geq 0}\circ\operatorname{Loc}
is left t-exact by definition and
({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\otimes_{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}})}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ})\rightarrow({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}
is t-exact by Lemma
2.10
, we are reduced to showing that
({\mathcal{A}}^{N_{H}})^{T_{H},w}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
is left t-exact up to shift.

This functor is given by
x\mapsto g(({\mathcal{D}}_{H/N_{H}}\overset{N_{H}}{\star}x)^{T_{H}})
. As
T_{H}
is a torus, this is a direct summand of the functor
x\mapsto g({\mathcal{D}}_{H/N_{H}}\overset{N_{H}}{\star}x)
, which is left t-exact up to shift by assumption.

Next, recall that Lemma
2.10
gives us a t-exact adjoint pair

p^{*}:{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\underset{\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H})}{\otimes}\QCoh({\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}):p_{*}.

Let us use this to show that
{\mathcal{A}}^{H,w}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
is left t-exact up to shift.

The Grothendieck-Cousin complex gives a finite resolution

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}}\rightarrow\displaystyle\bigoplus_{x\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}\mid\operatorname{codim}x=0}{\mathcal{E}}_{x}\rightarrow\displaystyle\bigoplus_{x\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}\mid\operatorname{codim}x=1}{\mathcal{E}}_{x}\rightarrow\cdots.

Here, for
x
a (not necessarily closed) point of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}
, the sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
is defined to be the colimit

\operatorname{colim}i_{k,*}i_{k}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}}[\operatorname{codim}x]

over the
k
th order neighborhood maps
i_{k}
.

Thus, for any object
{\mathcal{F}}\in({\mathcal{A}}^{H,w})^{\geq 0}
, to show that
g({\mathcal{F}})
is bounded below, it suffices to show that the
g({\mathcal{E}}_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})
are bounded below uniformly in
x
. Using the above expression of
{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
as a filtered colimit, we see that it suffices to show the same for each
g(i_{k,*}i_{k}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}//W_{H}}}[\operatorname{codim}x]\otimes{\mathcal{F}})
. And as each coherent sheaf
i_{k,*}i_{k}^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}//W_{H}}}[\operatorname{codim}x]
is an iterated extension of skyscraper sheaves at
x
, it in fact suffices to show that the
g(k_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})
are uniformly bounded below.

Because
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}
is faithfully flat, for any
x\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}//W_{H}
there is a
y\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*,\circ}
mapping to
x
. Fix such
x
and
y
. Then
k_{x}
is a direct summand of the pushforward of
k_{y}
, and thus
k_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
is a direct summand of
p_{*}(k_{y}\otimes p^{*}{\mathcal{F}})
. Therefore, it suffices to show that the
g(p_{*}(k_{y}\otimes p^{*}{\mathcal{F}}))
are uniformly bounded below. Since we previously showed that
g\circ p_{*}
is left t-exact up to shift, this follows from the
k_{y}\otimes p^{*}{\mathcal{F}}
being uniformly bounded below, which in turns follows from the existence of bounded length projective resolutions of
k_{y}
.

Finally, we can apply Lemma
7.2
to deduce that
g
is left t-exact up to shift, as desired.
∎

7.2.2.
Positive depth

Assume that for all
s<r
and
x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}

f_{{\mathcal{G}}}|_{K_{x,r+}}

For any quasi-projective variety
V

(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*}
, there is a functor

f|_{K_{x,r+},V}:{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+}}\underset{D((K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*})}{\otimes}D(V)\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}.

Assume that
U\subseteq V
is an open subvariety with complement

. Then, as in the proof of Lemma
6.1
, the left t-exactness up to shift of
f|_{K_{x,r+},V}
is reduced to the same property for
f|_{K_{x,r+},U}
and
f|_{K_{x,r+},Z}
. Once again, this reduces the desired statement to showing that
f|_{K_{x,r+},O}
is left t-exact up to shift for any
L_{x}
-orbit
O
in
(K_{x,r}/K_{x,r+})^{*,us}

By Lemma

and

can be identified with the composition

{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},p}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{x,r+},(K_{y,r}/K_{x,r+})^{\perp}}\cong{\mathcal{C}}^{K_{y,r}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}},

which is left t-exact up to shift by Lemma
A.14
and the inductive hypothesis.

Let

be the stabilizer group of

with respect to the action of
P_{x}/K_{x,r+}
on
O

Thus, it suffices to show that

First we show this for

be an object of

. To do so, we use a Grothendieck-Cousin argument as in the depth zero case. Recall that we have a resolution

Appendix A
Tensor products and t-structures

As in the rest of this paper, all categories will be assumed presentable and all t-structures will be assumed accessible, right complete, and compatible with filtered colimits. We will often implicitly invoke 1.4.4.11 of
[
Lur17
]
, which states that the data of an accessible t-structure on a presentable category
{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent to the data of a full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
which is presentable, closed under small colimits, and closed under extensions.
2
2

2

To avoid confusion, we note that we use the cohomological degree convention, while
[
Lur17
]
uses the homological convention. In particular, the category we call
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
is instead referred to as
{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
in
loc. cit
.
Furthermore, for any small collection of objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
, the category
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
generated by them under colimits and extensions is presentable (and thus defines a t-structure.)

A.1.
Tensor product

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
be two categories equipped with t-structures. Then we can equip
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
with a t-structure by defining
({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\leq 0}
to be generated (under colimits and extensions) by objects of the form
c\boxtimes d
, with
{\mathcal{C}}
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
and
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
. This t-structure will be right complete and compatible with filtered colimits by Theorem C.4.2.1 in
[
Lur18
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be categories equipped with t-structures and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be a right t-exact functor. Then, for any category
{\mathcal{D}}
with a t-structure,

f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}

is right t-exact.

Proof.

We need to show that
f\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
sends
({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\leq 0}
to
({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\leq 0}
. It suffices to check that, for
c
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
and
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
,

(f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}})(c\boxtimes d)\cong f(c)\boxtimes d\in({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\leq 0}.

But this follows from the right t-exactness of
f
.
∎

The stability of left t-exactness under tensor product is more subtle. We start with a simple observation.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be categories equipped with t-structures and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be a left t-exact functor with a left adjoint
f^{L}
. Then, for any category
{\mathcal{D}}
with a t-structure,

f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}

is left t-exact.

Proof.

Recall that left t-exactness of
f
is then equivalent to right t-exactness of
f^{L}
. By Lemma
A.1
,
f^{L}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
is right t-exact, so its right adjoint
f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
must be left t-exact, as desired.
∎

We will prove two other criteria for a tensor product to be left t-exact. Both revolve around the following notion.

Definition A.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a category equipped with a t-structure. We say that the t-structure is compactly generated if
{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
is generated under colimits by
{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
.

Example A.4
.

Let
X
be a quasiseparated scheme of finite type over a field. Then the natural t-structures on
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
,
\operatorname{IndCoh}(X)
, and
D(X)
are all compactly generated. For
\IndCoh(X)
, this is because
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
is closed under truncations, and the case of
D(X)
follows as
\operatorname{ind}(\IndCoh(X)_{\leq 0})
generates
D(X)
. For
\QCoh(X)
, this follows from a slight modification of the arguments in
[
TT90
]
.

For
{\mathcal{D}}
a DG category and
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
a compact object,
we let
{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathcal{D}}\to\operatorname{Vect}
denote the induced functor

\Hom({\mathcal{F}},-)
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
be DG categories with t-structures. Suppose

{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
.
Then the induced functor:

\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Vect}={\mathcal{C}}

is left t-exact.

Proof.

Note that the functors
-\otimes{\mathcal{F}}:\operatorname{Vect}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{D}}:{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}
are adjoint in the symmetric monoidal
2-category
\mathsf{DGCat}
. As
-\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
is right t-exact, we see that
{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}
is left t-exact. The result now follows from Lemma
A.2
.

∎

Our first left t-exactness criterion appears, e.g., as Lemma B.6.2. of
[
Ras21
]
, but we reproduce it here for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition A.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be categories equipped with t-structures and let
f:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be a left t-exact functor. Then, for any category
{\mathcal{D}}
with a compactly generated t-structure,

f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}

is left t-exact.

Proof.

Let
x
be an object of
({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\geq 0}
. We would like to show that
(f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}})(x)\in({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{\geq 0}
, or equivalently, that

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}}(c\otimes d[1],(f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}})(x))\cong 0

for all
c\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0},d\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
.

Because the t-structure on
{\mathcal{D}}
is compactly generated, we can assume that
d
is compact. We compute:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}}(c\otimes d[1],(f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}})(x))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(c[1],(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}})((f\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}})(x)))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(c[1],f((\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}})(x))).

Combining Lemma
A.5
with the left t-exactness of
f
, we see that this Hom space is trivial, as desired.

∎

The other left t-exactness criterion appears below as Proposition
A.9
. We first need a preliminary lemma.

Lemma A.7
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{D}}
is a DG category with a compactly generated
t-structure.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee,\prime}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
denote the
subcategory generated under colimits by objects of the form

{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}
for
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
.

Then
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
lies in

{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee,\prime}
if and only if the functor
\lambda:{\mathcal{D}}\to\operatorname{Vect}
is
left t-exact.

Proof.

Recall that the functor
{\mathcal{D}}^{c,op}\xrightarrow{{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}

extends to an equivalence
\operatorname{Ind}({\mathcal{D}}^{c,op})\cong{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
.
We wish to show that our left t-exact
\lambda
lies in
the subcategory
\operatorname{Ind}({\mathcal{D}}^{c,op}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0,op})\subset\operatorname{Ind}({\mathcal{D}}^{c,op})\cong{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
.
Concretely, this amounts to showing that for any

{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
and any map
\alpha:{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}\to\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
,
there exists
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
and a map

\beta:{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{F}}
such that the map
\alpha
factors
as

{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{{\mathbb{D}}\beta}{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\to\lambda.

For this, recall the map
{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}\to\lambda
amounts
to a point
\alpha\in\Omega^{\infty}\lambda({\mathcal{F}})
.
As
\lambda
is left
t
-exact, the map

\Omega^{\infty}\lambda(\tau^{\leq 0}{\mathcal{F}})\to\Omega^{\infty}\lambda({\mathcal{F}})
is an isomorphism. Now write

\tau^{\leq 0}{\mathcal{F}}
as a filtered colimit
\operatorname{colim}_{i}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}

with
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
;
we may do so because the t-structure is compactly generated.
Then the point
\alpha
comes from a point

\alpha_{i}\in\Omega^{\infty}\lambda({\mathcal{F}}_{i})
for some
i
;
taking
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\coloneqq{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
yields the claim.

∎

Corollary A.8
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{D}}
is a DG category with a compactly generated
t-structure and
{\mathcal{C}}
is a DG category with a t-structure.
Suppose
\lambda:{\mathcal{D}}\to\operatorname{Vect}
is left t-exact.

Then the induced functor

(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\lambda):{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\operatorname{Vect}={\mathcal{C}}

is left t-exact.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.5
, the result is true in the special case

\lambda={\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}}
with
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\leq 0}
.
By Lemma
A.7
, any left
t
-exact
\lambda
can
be written as a filtered colimit of functors of this form.
As our t-structures are assumed compatible with filtered
colimits, left t-exact functors are preserved under filtered colimits.
This yields the claim.

∎

Proposition A.9
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
are DG categories with compactly
generated t-structures and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a DG category with a t-structure.

Suppose
F:{\mathcal{D}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
is left t-exact.
Then the induced functor:

\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes F:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{2}

is left t-exact.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}\in({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{1})^{>0}
is given. We wish
to show that

(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes F)({\mathcal{G}})\in({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{2})^{>0}.

Equivalently, as the t-structure on
{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
is compactly
generated, it suffices to show that for

{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{2}^{c}\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{2}^{\leq 0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
,
we have

\Hom_{{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{2}}({\mathcal{H}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{F}},(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes F)({\mathcal{G}}))=0.

As in the proof of Lemma
A.5
, we may rewrite
the left hand side as

\Hom_{{\mathcal{C}}}({\mathcal{H}},(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes{\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}})\circ(\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes F)({\mathcal{G}}))=\Hom_{{\mathcal{C}}}({\mathcal{H}},F^{\vee}({\mathbb{D}}{\mathcal{F}})({\mathcal{G}})).

Observe that

is

{\mathcal{F}}

Therefore,

A.2.
Compatible t-structures

Let
H
be an affine algebraic group (in particular, of finite type). Following Appendix B of
[
Ras21
]
and Sections 10.9-13 of
[
Ras20a
]
, we will introduce a notion of compatibility between a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
and a
H
-action (strong or weak) on
{\mathcal{C}}
. No originality is claimed for any of the results in this section.

The key lemma is the following:

Lemma A.10

(
[
Ras21
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category with a weak action of
H
. Assume we have a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The functor
\operatorname{Oblv}\circ\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
is t-exact.

(2)

The functor
\operatorname{coact}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}
is t-exact.

(3)

The functor
\operatorname{act}:\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
is t-exact.

(4)

The category
{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}
admits a t-structure for which
\operatorname{Oblv}
and
\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}
are t-exact.

(5)

The
\QCoh(H)
-linear equivalence

\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}

induced by
\operatorname{coact}
is t-exact.

Proof.

This is Lemma B.3.1 of
[
Ras21
]
. Let us reproduce the proof.

First, note that we have an adjoint pair

p_{2}^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}:p_{2,*}

induced by the pullback and pushforward maps between
\operatorname{Vect}\cong\QCoh(\operatorname{Spec}k)
and
\QCoh(H)
. By Lemma
A.1
, both
p_{2}^{*}
and
p_{2,*}
are right t-exact. The adjunction then implies that
p_{2,*}
is also left t-exact, hence t-exact. Noting that
p_{2,*}
is conservative and that
p_{2,*}\circ p_{2}^{*}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{H}\otimes-
is t-exact, we see that
p_{2}^{*}
is t-exact as well.

To prove conditions (1) through (4) are equivalent, we use natural isomorphisms

p_{2,*}\circ\operatorname{coact}\cong\operatorname{act}\circ p_{2}^{*}\cong\operatorname{Oblv}\circ\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}.

To see these isomorphisms, note that by a standard tensor product argument, it suffices to check the universal case of
{\mathcal{C}}\cong\QCoh(H)
, where the desired isomorphisms follow as from the base change formula.

(1)\iff(2):
As
p_{2,*}
is t-exact and conservative, we see that
\operatorname{coact}
is t-exact if and only if
p_{2,*}\circ\operatorname{coact}\cong\operatorname{Oblv}\circ\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}
is t-exact, as desired.

(2)\implies(3):
As
\operatorname{act}
is right adjoint to
\operatorname{coact}
, we see that
\operatorname{act}
is left t-exact. To show right t-exactness, it suffices to note that
p_{2}^{*}
, viewed as a functor from
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}
to
(\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}})^{\leq 0}
, generates under colimits.

(3)\implies(1):
Clear.

(3)\iff(5):
The equivalence of (5) intertwines
p_{2}^{*}
and
\operatorname{coact}
, so
(5)
implies
(3)
. On the other hand, if
\operatorname{coact}
is t-exact, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{coact}
, restricted to a functor
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq 0}\rightarrow(\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}})^{\leq 0}
, generates under colimits. By adjunction, this would follow if we knew
\operatorname{act}
to be conservative and t-exact. The t-exactness follows from
(2)\implies(3)
, and to check conservativeness it suffices to consider the universal case of
{\mathcal{C}}\cong\QCoh(H)
, where it is obvious.

(4)\implies(1):
Clear.

(1)+(2)\implies(4):
This is the hardest implication to prove. Recall that
{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}
is the totalization of the cosimplicial category

{\mathcal{C}}\rightrightarrows\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\cr$\rightarrow$\crcr\cr$\rightarrow$\crcr\cr$\rightarrow$\crcr}}}\QCoh(H)\otimes\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\cdots.

To construct a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}^{H,w}
, it suffices to prove that all of the transition maps are t-exact. Consider a face map
f:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes(\QCoh(H))^{n}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes(\QCoh(H))^{n+1}
. We split into cases depending on the value of
n
.

For
n=0
there are two face maps,
p_{2}^{*}
and
\operatorname{coact}
. By our assumptions, both are t-exact.

Next, for
n=1
, there are three face maps,
p_{2}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\QCoh(H)},\operatorname{coact}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\QCoh(H)}
, and
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\Delta_{*}
, where
\Delta_{*}
is pushforward along the diagonal map
\Delta:H\rightarrow H\times H
. The first two maps are t-exact by Proposition
A.6
, and
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\Delta_{*}
is t-exact by Lemma
A.2
.

Finally, if
n>1
, then
f
is of the form
g\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\QCoh(H)}
for some face map
g:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes(\QCoh(H))^{n-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes(\QCoh(H))^{n}
. Using Proposition
A.6
again, this case follows by induction.

By construction,
\operatorname{Oblv}
is t-exact with respect to this t-structure. As
\operatorname{Oblv}
is conservative and
\operatorname{Oblv}\circ\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}
is t-exact, we see that
\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{w}
is t-exact, as desired.
∎

Definition A.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category with a weak action of
H
. A t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is said to be compatible with the
H
action if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of Lemma
A.10
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is instead endowed with a strong
H
-action, we say that a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is compatible with the
H
action if it is compatible with the underlying weak
H
-action.

In the case of a strong action on
{\mathcal{C}}
, note that the (strong) coaction functor
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H)
is still t-exact, up to shift. Indeed, the composition
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\QCoh(H)
is, up to shift, the weak coaction functor, which is t-exact by assumption. On the other hand, as
D(H)\rightarrow\QCoh(H)
is exact, conservative, and has a right adjoint, the same properties hold for
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\QCoh(H)
, which together imply that
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H)
is t-exact, as desired. This allows us to show:

Lemma A.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category with a strong action of
H
and a compatible t-structure. Then the invariant category
{\mathcal{C}}^{H}
has a natural t-structure with
\operatorname{Oblv}:{\mathcal{C}}^{H}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
t-exact.

Proof.

This follows by the same logic as in the proof of
(1)+(2)\implies(4)
in Lemma
A.10
. The only point requiring justification is the left t-exactness of
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\Delta_{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(H\times H)
, which is true by Proposition
A.9
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
A.12
, we can construct t-structures on
H
-tensor products.

Lemma A.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category with a right
H
-action and let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a category with a left
H
-action. Assume both
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
are equipped with compatible t-structures. Then
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}{\mathcal{D}}
has a natural t-structure, and there is a natural t-exact functor
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

Composing with the inversion map
D(H)\rightarrow D(H)
, we can make
{\mathcal{C}}
into a left
H
-category instead. Then
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
has a left
H\times H
action. We claim that there is a canonical equivalence
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}{\mathcal{D}}\cong({\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})^{H}
, where the
H
-invariants on the RHS is taken with respect to the diagonal copy of
H
inside
H\times H
. Indeed, there is an obvious such equivalence for
{\mathcal{C}}\cong{\mathcal{D}}\cong D(H)
, and the general case follows by tensoring.

Thus to equip
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}{\mathcal{D}}
with a t-structure satisfying the desired properties, it suffices to show that the natural t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
is compatible with the diagonal
H
action. In fact, it is compatible with the entire
H\times H
action. To see this, we need to show that the functor

\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}

is t-exact. By assumption, the functors
\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
and
\QCoh(H)\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
are t-exact. As they are also right adjoints, Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.2
tell us that their tensor product is t-exact, as desired.
∎

Finally, following Sections 10.9-13 of
[
Ras20a
]
, let us say something about the case where
H
is, rather than a finite type algebraic group, a Tate group indscheme with prounipotent tail (e.g.,
G((t))
.) As we will make only cursory use of this material, our treatment will only be a sketch. In this case, compatibility with a weak
H
-action on
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined to mean t-exactness of the equivalence

\IndCoh^{*}(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\IndCoh^{*}(H)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}

intertwining
\operatorname{act}
and
p_{2,*}
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
comes with a strong
H
-action, we impose one more requirement, namely, that for all (or equivalently, one) prounipotent compact subgroups
K\subseteq H
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{K}
is closed under truncations. This is equivalent to the existence of a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}^{K}
such that
{\mathcal{C}}^{K}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
is t-exact.

A.3.
Case of additive groups

Now take
H
to be a vector space
V
, treated as an algebraic group with group structure given by addition. By the Fourier-Deligne transform for
D
-modules, there is a t-exact equivalence
D(V)\cong D(V^{*})
intertwining the convolution monoidal structure on the left with the
\otimes^{!}
monoidal structure on the right. In particular, if
X
is a variety mapping to
V^{*}
, there is a natural
H
-action on
D(X)
.

We will be interested in categories of the form
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{A}D(X)
, for
{\mathcal{C}}
a
H
-category. Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
comes with a compatible t-structure. Then Lemma
A.13
gives a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(X)
. Furthermore, the proof of the lemma shows that this t-structure is compatible with the
H
-action.

The main result of this section is the following.

Lemma A.14
.

Assume that
H
corresponds to a vector space
V
, and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
H
-category with a t-structure compatible with the
H
-action. Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety mapping to
V^{*}
.

(1)

If
i:Z\rightarrow X
is a closed embedding, then
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes_{H}i_{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(Z)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(X)
is t-exact.

(2)

If
j:U\rightarrow X
is an open immersion, then
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes_{H}j^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(X)\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{H}D(U)
is t-exact.

Proof.

Let us first treat the case of closed embeddings. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}\underset{H}{\otimes}D(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}\underset{H}{\otimes}D(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}\otimes D(X)}

with vertical arrows conservative and t-exact. Thus, to show that the top arrow is t-exact, it suffices to show that the bottom arrow
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes i_{*}
is t-exact. But this follows from Lemma
A.1
and Proposition
A.9
.

Now we consider the case of open immersions. Again, we are reduced to showing that
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes j^{*}
is t-exact. And again, right t-exactness is automatic by Lemma
A.1
and left t-exactness holds because of Proposition
A.9
.
∎

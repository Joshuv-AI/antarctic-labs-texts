Fermionization of conformal boundary states
Hiromi Ebisu, Masataka Watanabe

Abstract
We construct the complete set of boundary states of two-dimensional fermionic CFTs using that of the bosonic counterpart. We see that there are two groups of boundary conditions, which contributes to the open-string partition function by characters with integer coefficients, or with 2 \sqrt{2} times integer coefficients. We argue that, using the argument of [JHEP 09 (2020) 018], this 2 \sqrt{2} indicates a single unpaired Majorana zero mode, and that these two groups of boundary conditions are mutually incompatible. We end the paper by mentioning a possible interpretation of the result in terms of the entanglement entropy.



I
Introduction

Bosonization is a procedure to get an equivalent bosonic theory out of a fermionic one
Coleman 1975
;
Gogolin et al. 2004
, or fermionization, vice versa.
They find many interesting applications in condensed matter as well as high-energy physics, serving for better understanding of systems with strongly interacting systems including fermions.
One primitive such example is the Luttinger liquid
Luttinger 1963
, where one finds a description of an interacting theory of one-dimensional fermions as a theory of free bosons.
They have also been attracting attention recently in the context of fermionic symmetry protected topological (SPT) phases
Gu and Wen 2014
;
Chen et al. 2011
;
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2011
;
Gaiotto and Kapustin 2016
;
Bhardwaj et al. 2017
;
Kaidi et al. 2020a
;
Kaidi et al. 2020b
.

Bosonization/fermionization are understood the simplest in
1+1
dimensions
Karch et al. 2019
.
In two-dimensions, the bosonization is simply a sum of spin structures, which will yield a bosonic theory with non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry.
Conversely, the fermionization is possible whenever the bosonic theory has non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, on which one tensors the Arf invariant (
i.e.,
the Kitaev chain

), and then gauges the common
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
subgroup

;

.

One can also bosonize or fermionize a theory on a manifold with boundaries.
In this case, one also needs to map boundary conditions under the duality –
This has been studied in two-dimensions in the context of open string worldsheet ending on D-branes, and as well as in higher-dimensions in the context of particle-vortex duality of Chern-Simons-matter theories

In spite of being equivalent, boundary conditions of fermionic theories are more interesting than that of the bosonic theories, because of the notion of mutual compatibility of boundary conditions once we put the theory on the cylinder.
This can already be seen for a simple theory like a free Majorana fermion –
There are two boundary conditions

Having said that, the main question we are going to ask in this paper is the following: Can we construct the complete set of boundary conditions of two-dimensional fermionic conformal field theories (CFTs), when one is given the complete set of boundary conditions of two-dimensional bosonic CFTs?
In two-dimensional CFTs, one can use the open-closed duality and quantify the question in terms of boundary states
Cardy 2004
.
In this language, we are going to find all the elementary boundary states of fermionic CFTs, with a slight caveat that the definition of elementary here be generalised so that the open-string loop consists not only of characters with integer coefficients, but also
\sqrt{2}
times integers, indicating that we allow for a real fermion on the boundary.
We are going to call this “generalised elementary” in this paper.

Fermionization is similar in spirit to orbifolding in that they are both a sum of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defect configurations with particular coefficients.
Thus, we will also discuss the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
orbifolding of boundary states along with fermionization, although the former has been studied many times before in the context of string worldsheet
Cappelli and D’Appollonio 2002
;
Matsubara et al. 2002
;
Fuchs and Schweigert 1997
;
Billo et al. 2001
.
This is not just being pedagogical, and we will see that comparing fermionization against orbifolding also serves as a small consistency check.
When we present the final result, we are going to see in a clear way how boundary states from different sectors are shuffled among themselves when we orbifold, bosonize, or fermionize the theory.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows.
In Section

We will also study the bosonization/fermmionization of boundary states for the Ising model (or equivalently for the theory of free Majorana fermion).
In Section
III

We conclude in Section

Let us introduce a notion of elementary boundary states

III
General case

We consider general CFTs with a global, non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

A global non-anomalous

The precise object we are looking for here is the elementary boundary conditions/states.
Since it will be important later, we reiterate what was reviewed in Sec.

Elementary boundary states

To see this concretely, consider the torus partition function of the orbifold theory and represent it in terms of that of the original theory,

\begin{split}&{\mathrm{Tr}}_{S^{1}}\left[e^{-\beta H_{\rm orbifold}}\right]\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathrm{Tr}}_{S^{1}}\left[e^{-\beta H}\right]+{\mathrm{Tr}}_{S^{1}}\left[ge^{-\beta H_{\rm orbifold}}\right]+({\tt twisted})\right)\end{split}

(24)

where
g
is our
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defect line and
({\tt twisted})
refers to the contribution from the twisted sector of the original theory.
Concentrating on the first two and expanding the trace in terms of states, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{{n_{\rm orbifold}}}\Braket{n_{\rm orbifold}|e^{-\beta H_{{\rm orbifold}}}|n_{\rm orbifold}}\\
=&\sum_{n\leq gn}\left[\left(\frac{\Bra{n}+\Bra{gn}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)e^{-\beta H}\left(\frac{\Ket{n}+\Ket{gn}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+({\tt twisted})\right]\end{split}

(25)

where
n\leq gn
means that we avoid summing over the same index twice.
It is therefore apparent that
\Ket{n_{\rm orbifold}}
corresponds to
\frac{\Ket{n}+\Ket{gn}}{\sqrt{2}}
, or other similar looking states coming from the twisted sector of the original theory.
Let us now concentrate on the state coming from the untwisted sector of the original theory.
In terms of the closed-string amplitude and in terms of the above picture using the cylinder, this means we have

\begin{split}&\Braket{n_{\rm orbifold}|\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Dno.png}}}}}\limits\limits_{\text{$D$-type}}^{\rm untwisted}|m_{\rm orbifold}}\equiv\Braket{n_{\rm orbifold}|e^{-\beta H_{\rm orbifold}}|m_{\rm orbifold}}\\
=&\left(\frac{\Bra{n}+\Bra{gn}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)e^{-\beta H}\left(\frac{\Ket{m}+\Ket{gm}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=\Braket{n|e^{-\beta H}|m}+\Braket{n|e^{-\beta H}|gm}=\Braket{n|{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Ano.png}}}}}\limits}|m}+\Braket{n|{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Acentre.png}}}}}\limits}|m}\end{split}

(26)

where we used
g^{2}=1
and the fact that
g
commutes with the Hamiltonian and can be moved topologically on the cylinder.
If one wishes to generalise to states coming from the twisted sector of the original theory, one finally gets the picture we have given in the beginning.

III.2
Boundary states in the original theory

In the untwisted sector of the original theory, one can classify boundary states into two groups, depending on whether they are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric or not.
We write the complete set of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric elementary boundary conditions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\rm free}\equiv\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm free}_{A}\right\}

(29)

while that of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
breaking elementary boundary conditions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\rm fixed}\equiv\mathcal{A}^{+}\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{-}\equiv\displaystyle\bigcup_{\pm}\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{\pm}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}

(30)

where
{\Ket{\widehat{b^{+}_{i}}}}
maps to
{\Ket{\widehat{b^{-}_{i}}}}
under the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and vice versa.
Here the subscript
A
means we are considering the
A
-type theory.
Incidentally, although
N_{A}^{\rm free,fixed}
are finite for rantional conformal field theories (RCFTs), those numbers lose their precise meaning for non RCFTs.
We speculate that the construction below works for non RCFTs as well at least when the number of conformal blocks is countably infinite.

On the other hand, in the twisted sector of the original theory, we denote the complete basis for boundary states (Ishibashi states in RCFTs) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm twisted}\equiv\left\{\left|\left.\tau_{i}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm twisted}_{A}\right\},

(31)

or, if we are interested in the complete set of elementary boundary states, we can write it as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm twisted}\equiv\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{i}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm twisted}_{A}\right\},

(32)

where
N_{A}^{f}\equiv N^{\rm free}_{A,D}=N^{\rm twisted}_{A,D}
as we will explain in Appendix
A
.
Some comments are in order.

•

Unlike the untwisted case, the open string spectrum of any linear combination of those states will always contain Virasoro characters with negative integer from the twisted sector.
The notion of “elementary” here is therefore slightly generalised to accommodate for such negative integers.

•

More precisely, we will define
\mathcal{A}^{\rm twisted}
using the information that the
D
-type symmetry breaking boundary states are elementary.
See later subsections for more explanation.

•

The boundary states in
\mathcal{A}^{\rm twisted}
are all invariant under the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
transformation.
The reason is the following: if we have such a twisted boundary state
{\Ket{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}}
that it is not invariant under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, then we have an operator
(\mathcal{O},\overline{\mathcal{O}})
in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd twisted sector.
Meanwhile, under fermionization, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd twisted sector maps to the fermionic NS sector –
This means that the operator
(\mathcal{O},\overline{\mathcal{O}})
must have a half-integer spin, which is obviously a contradiction.
6
6

6

We can also see this mathematically from the fact that the twisted odd torus partition function is given by
Z_{\rm twisted}-\mathcal{T}Z_{\rm twisted}
, and that
\left|\chi_{i}\right|
is invariant under the action of
\mathcal{T}
.

III.3
Boundary states for the orbifold theory

Let us now consider the elementary boundary states in the
D
-theory.
Although the result has already been reproduced many times before
Cappelli and D’Appollonio 2002
;
Matsubara et al. 2002
;
Fuchs and Schweigert 1997
;
Billo et al. 2001
, it is instructive to compare with the fermionic case.

The general idea here is the following.
First consider cylinder amplitudes between elementary boundary states of the
A
-theory.
By virtue of
e.g.,
(
22
), we get the cylinder amplitude of the
D
-theory, shown on the LHS.
We can then use this cylinder amplitude to infer the boundary states which reproduce it.

III.3.1
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
breaking boundary conditions

Let us first study the untwisted closed string amplitude in the
D
-theory.
Sandwiching the RHS of (
22
) by
{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}
, we get
7
7

7

The argument will be the same when we have two different boundary conditions on two ends.

\begin{split}&\Braket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}|\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Ano.png}}}}}\limits+\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Acentre.png}}}}}\limits+\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Aline.png}}}}}\limits+\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{{\includegraphics[width]{Acentreline.png}}}}}\limits|\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}\\
=&\Braket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}|q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right)}|\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}+\Braket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}|q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right)}|\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}},\end{split}

(33)

where we used that the last two terms are vanishing, since
{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{\pm}}}}
is in the untwisted sector of the original theory.
We then look for boundary states which correctly reproduces this cylinder partition function, which turn out to be

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right),

(34)

so that they are the boundary states (which might or might not be elementary unless we check) of the
D
-theory.

Likewise, for the twisted sector of the
D
-theory, the cylinder partition function

\displaystyle\Braket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}|q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right)}|\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}-\Braket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}|q^{\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12}\right)}|\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}},

(35)

is reproduced by a boundary state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}-{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right),

(36)

and they are also the boundary states (which might or might not be elementary unless we check) of the
D
-theory, this time in the twisted sector.

At this stage, these might not be elementary boundary conditions, and can be a sum of other elementary boundary conditions with integer coefficients.
Here, to reiterate, the elementary boundary states must reproduce the Virasoro character inside the open-string spectrum with integer coefficients, non-negative for the untwisted, and possibly negative for the twisted sector of the theory.
One can indeed check that this is so for states above among themselves, so that we conclude that these are elementary boundary conditions for the
D
-theory.

III.3.2
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric boundary conditions

By using the same argument as above, we can see that the closed string amplitude in the untwisted sector can be reproduced by the state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\right)=\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}},

(37)

or

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\right)=\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}},

(38)

and that there are no corresponding boundary states in the twisted sector.
Note that
\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}}
is invariant under the dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, while
\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}}
flips the sign under it, since they came from the twisted sector of the
A
-theory.

The question is if they are really elementary boundary states.
This time, one can easily see that the states are not elementary since they produces two times the vacuum character in the open-string channel.
In other words, these states like
\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}}
or
\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}}
should be a sum/subtraction of two elementary boundary states.
By demanding that they are elementary, as well as that they reproduce the boundary state of the original
A
-theory when dualised again, we can see that the only consistent choice
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\right).

(39)

The other choices like
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\right)
or
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\right)
are eliminated, because free boundary conditions must correspond to fixed boundary conditions under duality.
8
8

8

We thank Yunqin Zheng for private communication of the result.

Note that when the system has an enhanced symmetry, it might also be possible to rotate boundary states with the same Virasoro dimension to the above forms –
The result merely means that there must exist a canonical form (
39
), for the
D
-theory elementary boundary states.
Incidentally, they are called the fractional branes in the context of string theory
Cappelli and D’Appollonio 2002
.
It is also consistent with
N_{A}^{\rm fixed}=N_{A}^{\rm twisted}
.

Although we have already prepared the twisted sector boundary states
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
in the
A
-theory, one should understand this as a way of determining
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
in terms of
\left|\left.\tau_{A,i}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
, by requiring that (
39
) be elementary in the
D
-theory.
More concretely, one can write

\displaystyle{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}=\sum_{i}\frac{C_{ij}}{\sqrt{S_{0}^{{i}}}}\left|\left.\tau_{A,j}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\quad\text{where}\quad\sum_{j}\left|C_{ij}\right|^{2}=1,

(40)

and we determine
C_{ij}
, demanding that (
39
) are elementary boundary states.
For the simplest case where there is only one such
\left|\left.\tau\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
, the coefficient is simply
C=1
.
The coefficients for more general case has been determined using classifying algebra in
Fuchs and Schweigert 1997
;
Fuchs and Schweigert 1998
;
Fuchs and Schweigert 1999
;
Fuchs and Schweigert 2000
.
In this context, the matrix
C_{ij}
is related to the so-called fixed-point resolution matrix.

III.3.3
Final result and its consistency

We conclude that for the
D
-type theory, the complete set of elementary boundary conditions becomes the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{\rm free}&=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right)\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}\\
\mathcal{D}^{\rm fixed}&\equiv\mathcal{D}^{+}\bigcup\mathcal{D}^{-}\\
&=\bigcup_{\pm}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\right)\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{f}_{A}\right\}\\
{\mathcal{D}}^{\rm twisted}&=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}-{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right)\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}\end{split}

(41)

If the boundary states are fixed or free can be easily determined, by noting that the sign of
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
is flipped under the dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
This is also consistent with the general analysis that fixed boundary conditions map to free ones and vice versa under orbifolding.
9
9

9

One instance of this is the mapping of Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions under the particle-vortex duality.

As a first consistency check, one can see that the total number of elementary boundary states are unchanged by orbifolding, as they should be.
Also, as we explained previously, we trivially get integer coefficient sums of open-channel Virasoro characters for boundary conditions among boundary states in
\mathcal{D}^{\rm free}
or
\mathcal{D}^{\rm twisted}
.
Incidentally, the states in
\mathcal{D}^{\rm fixed}

are already elementary since we prepared them to be so, by tuning
C_{ij}
–
The fact that we have coefficients to tune was crucial in finding the elementary boundary states of the
D
-theory.

III.4
Boundary states for the fermionic theory

In the most part of this section, we will choose a specific convention for the parity symmetry and look at the
{F}
-type theory as defined in
Hsieh et al. 2019
.
We will also briefly discuss the
\tilde{F}
-type theory for consistency.

III.4.1
NS sector boundary conditions

For fermionic theories, we can just mimic the steps we discussed in the orbifold theory.
In the NS sector, the cylinder partition function (
27
) can be reproduced by boundary states from
{\Ket{\widehat{b^{\pm}_{A,i}}}}
and from
{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}
, which are both even under
(-1)^{F}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b^{+}_{A,i}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{b^{-}_{A,i}}}}\right)&\text{even under $(-1)^{F}$}\\
\sqrt{2}{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}&\text{even under $(-1)^{F}$}\end{cases}

(42)

Up to this point, the argument is almost similar to that of the
D
-theory, except that there are no states from
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
in the NS sector, while there was for the
D
-theory.
Also, the convention of the parity symmetry is so that the first states are odd under
(-1)^{F}
, while the second being even.

As we discussed in the previous subsection, the state
\sqrt{2}{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}
is not an elementary boundary condition in its strict sense of the word.
For example, if we place the former and the latter boundary states on two ends of a cylinder, the open string spectrum we read out comes with
\sqrt{2}
times integer coefficients.
As discussed in
Smith and Tong 2020a
;
Smith and Tong 2021
;
Smith and Tong 2020b
, this is an indication that we need to add a boundary Majorana fermion by hand, to cancel the anomaly created by the Majorana zero mode, or equivalently, that these two boundary states are incompatible.

III.4.2
R sector boundary states

In the R sector,
the cylinder partition function (
28
) can be reproduced by boundary states coming from
{\Ket{\widehat{b^{\pm}_{A,i}}}}
and from
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b^{+}_{A,i}}}}-{\Ket{\widehat{b^{-}_{A,i}}}}\right)&\text{odd under $(-1)^{F}$}\\
\sqrt{2}{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}&\text{even under $(-1)^{F}$}\end{cases}

(43)

The
\sqrt{2}
here requires even more attention than the NS case.
At first we would imagine that this comes from the Majorana zero mode again, but this cannot be the case here – The zero mode will multiply the
R
-sector partition function by zero.
The other possibility is that they appear as a combination of two elementary boundary states, but this does not happen generically.
We can therefore only conclude that states like
\sqrt{2}{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
does not appear in the fermionic theory.
This does not sound very symmetric, but it is not a contradiction. As we will explain below, the boundary states
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
instead become elementary for the
\tilde{F}
-type theory.

III.4.3
Final result and its consistency

We conclude that for the
{F}
-type theory, the complete set of elementary boundary conditions becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{{F}}^{\rm NS}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right)\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{{F}}^{\rm NS}_{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\sqrt{2}}{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{f}_{A}\right\}

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{{F}}^{\rm R}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}-{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\right)\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}

(46)

where two different groups of NS sector elementary boundary conditions were shown to be incompatible with each other.
The subscripts,
1
and
\sqrt{2}
, indicate that the ground state degeneracy is
1
or
2
respectively, when we put the same boundary condition on the two ends.
The result is consistent with the one in
Runkel and Watts 2020
, where several examples are considered.

For consistency and completeness, let us also study the boundary conditions for the
\tilde{F}
-type theory.
In order to do this, one can simply apply the same procedure to the
D
-theory, where we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{\rm NS}_{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{+}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{b_{A,i}^{-}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{\rm fixed}_{A}\right\}

(47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{\rm NS}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{f}_{A}\right\}

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{\rm R}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\middle|i=1,2,\dots,N^{f}_{A}\right\}

(49)

One can see that the boundary states which we threw away in the
F
-type theory is recovered in the
\tilde{F}
-type.
This is not surprising, since
F
- and
\tilde{F}
-type theories are in general different, even though the difference is just a result of the discrete choice in
(-1)^{F}
.

IV
Discussions and future directions

We have studied the complete set of elementary boundary states for two-dimensional fermionic CFTs.
In deriving them, we first used the construction of orbifold or fermionic theories using
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defect lines, and computed the closed string amplitude for the target theories, and then inferred the consistent boundary states for them.
We took maximal precaution that the resulting boundary states actually reproduce the open-string spectrum after
S
-transformation.
The consistency for the orbifold theory is that the coefficients in front of the open-channel Virasoro characters are integers, while for the fermionic theory we also allowed for
\sqrt{2}
times the integer.
We also argue that there are two classes of boundary states in the fermionic theory, because of the
\sqrt{2}
above, mutually inconsistent with each other when placed on two ends of a cylinder.
We have in the end found a consistent reshuffling of boundary states, as one moves from the
A
-type to
D
-,
F

\tilde{F}

There are several interesting future directions to consider.
First of all, the detailed analysis of the R sector boundary conditions are necessary.
It would be interesting to find examples where latter states in (
43
) is actually constructed as a sum of two elementary boundary states like
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,j}}}}\right)
.

Second, it is worthwhile to study other symmetries preserved by boundary states of fermionic theories.
It had been a folklore
Han et al. 2017
and has been proven recently
Thorngren and Wang 2020
that the existence of a symmetry preserving boundary condition implies the vanishing of the anomaly, for very general classes of symmetries and systems.
One can therefore now reverse the logic and use boundary states as a probe of anomaly in two-dimensional CFTs, or to study three-dimensional bosonic/fermionic SPT phases.
For example, it would be interesting to extend the analysis of
Smith 2021
to find fermionic theories with seperate holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parity symmetries, and study their anomalies.
It would also be interesting to see if the reverse statement is always true, as proposed in
Zhou 2019
;
Kikuchi and Zhou 2019
(See also
Numasawa and Yamaguchi 2018
).

Realization of the fermionic boundary states in a UV lattice model is also an important issue. If we introduce a finite (bosonic) spin chain with with an appropriate boundary condition by imposing a local magnetic field on a boundary, then we would obtain an eigenstate corresponding to a (bosonic) conformal boundary state. Using these eigenstates, together with our results would give rise to the desired Fermioninc boundary states. This strategy could work in e.g., a spin-
k/2
chain whose criticality is governed by
SU(2)_{k}
WZW CFT and the Potts models
(see
Hsieh et al. 2020
where the fermionization of bulk of the three states Potts model was numerically confirmed.).
One could also construct the fermionic boundary state in a 2D classical statistical lattice model where defect lines is explicitly described. As an example, in the classical 2D Ising lattice model, the work
Aasen et al. 2016
introduced the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
defect lines
which is crucial in our argument as seen from the diagram in e.g, (
26
).

It will also be interesting to do the same for supersymmetric theories in two-dimensions, so that one knows the complete set of supersymmetric boundary conditions and/or defects.
These pieces of information are to be used in supersymmetric localisation computation on the hemisphere or with defects.
This direction has already been started in
Gang and Yamaguchi 2008
and continued in
Makabe and Watts 2017
;
Runkel and Watts 2020
.

Higher-dimensional generalisation is also interesting. Although one cannot easily classify boundary conditions in higher-dimensions, it is already known that the particle-vortex duality maps Dirichlet type boundary conditions to Neumann type boundary conditions in some examples
Aitken et al. 2018a
;
Aitken et al. 2018b
;
Di Pietro et al. 2019
;
Di Pietro et al. 2020
.
It is interesting to find other examples of equivalent boundary conditions across duality.

Appendix A
The proof that
N^{\rm free}=N^{\rm twisted}

We prove that the number of free boundary states in the untwisted sector and that of twisted sector boundary states are the same.

The action of the Verlinde line
\mathcal{L}_{k}
to the CFT state can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}\Ket{\phi_{\ell}}=\frac{S_{k\ell}}{S_{0\ell}}\Ket{\phi_{\ell}}

(50)

In particular, when a system has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, there exist
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}\Ket{\phi_{\ell}}=\begin{cases}\Ket{\phi_{\ell}}&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is even}\\
-\Ket{\phi_{\ell}}&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is odd}\end{cases}

(51)

In other words,

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},\ell}}{S_{0,\ell}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is even}\\
-1&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is odd}\end{cases}

(52)

Meanwhile, the twisted sector partition function can be written as

\displaystyle Z_{\rm twisted}=\sum_{i,j}N_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},i}^{j}\chi_{i}\bar{\chi}_{j}

(53)

where

\displaystyle N_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},i}^{j}\equiv\sum_{\ell}\frac{S_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},\ell}S_{i,\ell}\bar{S}_{i,\ell}}{S_{0,\ell}}

(54)

Now, the free boundary condition
{\Ket{\widehat{f}}}
is defined such that the open-string spectrum (the
S
-transformation of
\chi_{f}
) only contains the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
even primaries.
This means that

\displaystyle S_{f,\ell}=\begin{cases}1&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is even}\\
0&\text{when $\phi_{\ell}$ is odd}\end{cases}

(55)

Let us first prove that the there is a corresponding boundary state in the twisted sector for every free boundary state in the untwisted sector,
i.e.,
N_{\mathbb{Z},f_{j}}^{f_{k}}=\delta_{i,j}
.
This can be proven as

\begin{split}N_{\mathbb{Z},f_{j}}^{f_{k}}&=\sum_{\ell}\frac{S_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},\ell}S_{f_{j},\ell}\bar{S}_{f_{k},\ell}}{S_{0,\ell}}\\
&=\sum_{\ell\in\text{even}}\frac{S_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},\ell}S_{f_{j},\ell}\bar{S}_{f_{k},\ell}}{S_{0,\ell}}=\sum_{\ell\in\text{even}}{S_{f_{j},\ell}\bar{S}_{f_{k},\ell}}=\delta_{i,j}\end{split}

(56)

The inverse is also true. What we want to prove is that when
N_{\mathbb{Z}_{2},i}^{i}=1
, we have
S_{i,\ell}=0
for odd
\phi_{\ell}
.
We have the relation

\displaystyle 1=N_{\mathbb{Z},i}^{i}=\sum_{\ell\in\text{even}}{S_{i,\ell}\bar{S}_{i,\ell}}-\sum_{\ell\in\text{odd}}{S_{i,\ell}\bar{S}_{i,\ell}},

(57)

but since we also have from unitarity that

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{\ell\in\text{even}}{S_{i,\ell}\bar{S}_{i,\ell}}+\sum_{\ell\in\text{odd}}{S_{i,\ell}\bar{S}_{i,\ell}}

(58)

we have proven that
S_{i,\ell}=0
for odd
\phi_{\ell}
.

Appendix B
Fixing the fixed boundary states in the
D
-theory

In this appendix, we show why (
39
) is the correct form of the boundary state in
D
-theory by eliminating other possible construction of elementary boundary states.
Let us split (
37
), pretending that we do not know the final answer for a moment as
\sqrt{2}{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}}={\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}^{+}}}}+{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}^{-}}}}

or in other words,

\displaystyle{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}^{\pm}}}}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\pey_{i}}}}\right)

(59)

Let us now assume that they are invariant under the dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and see that it leads to contradiction.
If these are invariant under the dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, by again orbifolding the theory, we will have untwisted sector boundary states, which are again sum/subtraction of elementary boundary states.
They look like

\displaystyle{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\pey_{i}}}},

(60)

and so
{\Ket{\widehat{\pey_{i}}}}
must also be an elementary boundary state in the untwisted sector.
The only possibility where it can happen is that
{\Ket{\widehat{\pey_{i}}}}
is actually one of
\left\{{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\right\}
.

Now we have seen that the candidate for the elementary boundary states is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,j}}}}\right),

(61)

and by similar arguments, and because the numbers of
{\Ket{\widehat{f_{A,i}}}}
and of
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
are the same, we also have boundary states like

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,j}}}}\right).

(62)

However, this means that by orbifolding we get untwisted sector boundary states,

\displaystyle{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}\pm{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,j}}}}.

(63)

Since
{\Ket{\widehat{\tau_{A,i}}}}
are in the twisted sector, this is a contradiction.

We have therefore proven that (
39
) is the correct choice for the
D
-type boundary states, modulo a slight caveat that when the theory has a larger symmetry than
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, free rotations of boundary states with the same Virasoro dimension can connect (
39
) to the above “pathological” boundary states.

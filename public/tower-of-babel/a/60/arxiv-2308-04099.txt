On the p p -divisibility of even K K -groups of the ring of integers of a cyclotomic field
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
Let k k be a given positive odd integer and p p an odd prime. In this paper, we shall give a sufficient condition when a prime p p divides the order of the groups K 2 ​ k ​ ( ℤ ⁡ [ ζ m + ζ m − 1 ] ) K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1}]) and K 2 ​ k ​ ( ℤ ⁡ [ ζ m ] ) K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}]) , where ζ m \zeta_{m} is a primitive m m th root of unity. When F F is a p p -extension contained in ℚ ⁡ ( ζ l ) \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{l}) for some prime l l , we also establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the order of K 2 ​ ( p − 2 ) ​ ( 𝒪 F ) K_{2(p-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{F}) to be divisible by p p . This generalizes a previous result of Browkin which in turn has applications towards establishing the existence of infinitely many cyclic extensions of degree p p which are ( p , p − 1 ) (p,p-1) -rational. 2
Power-sum denominator and Bernoulli number

We begin collecting certain facts about the power sum denominator and and Bernoulli number that will be required for the discussion in this paper. As a start, one recalls that the Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
are defined by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive even integer. Then the denominator of the Bernoulli number
B_{n}
is given by

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\mathrm{prime}\\
(p-1)\mid n\end{subarray}}p.

In particular, the denominator of the Bernoulli number
B_{n}
is squarefree with every prime divisor
\leq n+1
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the von Staudt-Clausen Theorem (cf.
[
34
, Theorem 5.10]
).
∎

We also recall the
n
th Bernoulli polynomial
B_{n}(x)
which are defined by

\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The relation between the Bernoulli numbers and Bernoulli polynomial is encoded in the following formula

B_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}B_{i}x^{n-i}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.2
.

Set
S_{n}(x):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\big(B_{n+1}(x)-B_{n+1}\big)
.

Clearly,
S_{n}(x)
is a polynomial with rational coefficients. Furthermore, for every positive integer
m
, one has

S_{n}(m)=\frac{1}{n+1}\big(B_{n+1}(m)-B_{n+1}\big)=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{n}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.3
.

For
n\geq 1
, the
n
th power-sum denominator is defined to be the smallest positive
integer
d_{n}
such that
d_{n}S_{n}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
with integer
coefficients. We also set
d_{0}=1
.

Some of the values of
d_{n}
are listed in
[
31
, Sequence A064538]
.

Remark 2.4
.

In view of (
2.2
),
d_{n}
can be thought as the smallest positive
integer
d_{n}
such that the following formal expression

\mbox{``}d_{n}(1^{x-1}+\cdots+a^{x-1})\mbox{''}

is a polynomial in
x
with integer
coefficients. This is the definition given in
[
16
, Definition 1]
.

We now record certain divisibility properties of these power-sum denominators that will be required for subsequent discussion. (For more detailed account on this topic, we refer readers to the article of Kellner and Sondow
[
16
]
and the reference therein.) Now, if
p
is a prime and
N
is an integer, we write
p\parallel N
to mean
p\mid N
but
p^{2}\nmid N
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For every
n\geq 1
, the following statements are valid.

(a)

Every prime divisor of
d_{n}
is
\leq n+1
.

(b)

The integer
d_{n}
is divisible by
n+1
and
\displaystyle\frac{d_{n}}{n+1}
is square-free. Furthermore, the prime divisor of
\displaystyle\frac{d_{n}}{n+1}
is less than or equal to
M_{n}
, where

M_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{n+2}{2},&\hbox{if $n$ is even;}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{n+2}{3},&\hbox{if $n$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

(c)

If
n+1
is a prime, then
(n+1)\parallel d_{n}
.

Proof.

Assertions (a) and (b) follow from
[
16
, Theorem 1]
. Finally, in view that
M_{n}<n+1
, assertion (c) is an immediate consequence of assertion (b).
∎

We now like to understand the polynomial
S_{n}(x)
more. In view of (
2.2
), it is then natural to start looking at the power sum
\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{n}
. To get a feel, we begin writing the power sum formulas for a few
n
’s.

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(m-1)m

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(m-1)m(2m-1)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(m-1)^{2}m^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{30}(m-1)m(2m-1)(3m^{2}-3m-1)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}(m-1)^{2}m^{2}(2m^{2}-2m-1)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}a^{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{42}(m-1)m(2m-1)(3m^{4}-6m^{3}+18m^{2}+3m+1)

The shape of the power sum formula is an inspiration for us towards formulating the next lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.
Then we have
S_{n}(x)=\frac{x-1}{d_{n}}f_{n}(x)
for a unique
f_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Furthermore, the following statements are valid.

(a)

One has
f_{n}(1)=d_{n}B_{n}
.

(b)

If
n
is an odd integer
\geq 3
, then
x-1
divides
f_{n}(x)
.

(c)

If
n+1
is an odd prime, then
n+1\nmid f_{n}(1)
.

Proof.

Since

S_{n}(1)=\frac{1}{n+1}\big(B_{n+1}(1)-B_{n+1}\big)=\sum_{a=0}^{0}a^{n}=0,

the polynomial
S_{n}(x)
has a factor
x-1
. Consequently, we have
S_{n}(x)=\frac{x-1}{d_{n}}f_{n}(x)
for a unique
f_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Differentiating (
2.1
) with respect to
x
, one obtains

S_{n}^{\prime}(x)=S_{n-1}(x)+B_{n},

where
S_{n}^{\prime}(x)
denotes the derivative of
S_{n}(x)
. Setting
x=1
yields
S^{\prime}_{n}(1)=B_{n}
. If
n
is an odd integer
\geq 3
, then the latter vanishes and we obtain assertion (b). On the other hand,
we have

S_{n}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{d_{n}}+\frac{x-1}{d_{n}}f_{n}^{\prime}(x).

Again, setting
x=1
and taking the above equality into account, we obtain

f_{n}(1)=d_{n}B_{n}
which is our assertion (a).

Finally, when
n+1
is an odd prime, Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.1
tell us that both
d_{n}
and the denominator of
B_{n}
are exactly divisible by
n+1
. Combining this with assertion (a), we have our assertion (c).
∎

Although we do not require this fact, we note that one can also show that

for every

3
Descent and Periodicity

In this section, we recall certain Galois descent property and periodicity of the even
K
-groups. As a start, we have the following descent result for extension of degree coprime to a prime
p
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields. Suppose that the prime
p
does not divide the order of the Galois group
\Gal(L/F)
. Then we have

K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})[p^{\infty}]\cong\Big(K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\Big)^{\Gal(L/F)}.

In particular, if
p^{a}
divides
\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
for some
a\geq 1
, then
p^{a}
also divides
\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
.

Proof.

The restriction map
K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\longrightarrow K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{L})^{\Gal(L/F)}
has kernel and cokernel annihilated by the order of the Galois group
\Gal(L/F)
(for instance, see
[
35
]
). Since the latter order is not divisible by
p
, the conclusion of the lemma follows. Alternatively, one can identify the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
with an appropriate second continuous cohomology group via the work of Rost-Voevodsky
[
33
]
and give a cohomological proof of the said isomorphism; details of which may be found in
[
22
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

We shall also require the following analog of the preceding lemma in the case of a
p
-extension.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields such that
|L:F|
is a
p
-power and that
L/F
is unramified outside
p
. Then we have

K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})[p^{\infty}]\cong\Big(K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{L})[p^{\infty}]\Big)_{\Gal(L/F)}.

In particular, if
p^{a}
divides
\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
for some
a\geq 1
, then
p^{a}
also divides
\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
.

Proof.

We only give a brief sketch of the ideas of the proof. The deep work of Rost-Voevodsky
[
33
]
allows one to identify the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
with an appropriate second continuous cohomology group. Under this identification, the required isomorphism in our lemma is then a consequence of Tate spectral sequence. For more details, we refer readers to
[
17
, Section 2, Proposition 2.9]
or
[
21
, Sections 3 and 4, Proposition 4.1.5]
.
∎

In order to state the next result, we require an introduction of more terminology.
Now, if
N
is an abelian group, we denote by
N[p]
the subgroup of
N
consisting of elements annihilated by
p
. Clearly, this subgroup
N[p]
comes equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-vector space structure, and so we may speak of the
p
-rank of
N
which is defined by

r_{p}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\big(N[p]\big).

We may now state the following periodicity result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F
be a number field. Then we have

r_{p}\big(K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\big)=r_{p}\big(K_{2k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\big),

whenever
k\equiv k^{\prime}

(
mod
|F(\zeta_{p}):F|)
. In particular, one has
p\mid\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})

if and only if
p\mid\#K_{2k^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
.

Proof.

See
[
23
]
.
∎

4
Lichtenbaum conjecture for totally real abelian number fields

From now on, we always let
\zeta_{F}(s)
denote the Dedekind zeta function of a number field
F
. It is well-known that this said function has an analytical continuation to the whole complex plane except at the point
s=1
at which it has a simple pole. Therefore, it makes sense to speak of
\zeta_{F}(-k)
for a positive integer
k
. Let
\mu_{\infty}
be the group of all the roots of unity of
\bar{F}
, where
\bar{F}
is the algebraic closure of
F
. For an integer
j\geq 1
, we write
\mu_{\infty}^{\otimes j}
for the
j
-fold tensor product of
\mu_{\infty}
with
\Gal(\bar{F}/F)
acting diagonally. Set
w_{j}(F)
to be the order of
(\mu_{\infty}^{\otimes j})^{\Gal(\bar{F}/F)}
for a number field
F
.

We can now state the following deep result which will be required in the discussion of the paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F
be a totally real abelian number field of degree
r
. Then for every odd integer
k\geq 1
, one has

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{r}w_{k+1}(F)\zeta_{F}(-k),&\mbox{if $k\equiv 1$ (mod 4)},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{r}}w_{k+1}(F)\zeta_{F}(-k),&\mbox{if $k\equiv 3$ (mod 4)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This identity was first conjectured by Birch and Tate for
K_{2}
(see
[
32
]
) and subsequently, Lichtenbaum extended their conjecture to general
K_{2k}

[
19
,
20
]
. It was probably due to the insight of Coates
[
11
,
12
]
that one might possibly prove this conjecture via the main conjecture of Iwasawa
[
15
]
. Later, Báyer and Neukirch
[
3
]
confirmed this vision of Coates by showing that the main conjecture of Iwasawa implies a cohomological version of Lichtenbaum’s conjecture (we refer readers to
[
3
]
for the precise formulation of this cohomological Lichtenbaum’s conjecture). This latter cohomological version is equivalent to the
K
-theoretical version via the Quillen-Lichtenbaum conjecture, which is now a theorem being a consequence of the monumental work of Rost-Voevodsky (
[
33
]
; also see Rognes-Weibel
[
30
]
). Prior to this, the main conjecture of Iwasawa has been proved by Mazur-Wiles
[
25
]
and Wiles
[
36
]
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. If
x
and
y
are two rational numbers such that they have the same
p
-adic valuations, we shall write
x\sim_{p}y
. Therefore, the equality in Theorem
4.1
can be presented in the following form

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\sim_{p}w_{k+1}(F)\zeta_{F}(-k)

for every odd integer
k\geq 1
and odd prime
p
. In fact, this is the original form of the conjecture of Lichtenbaum. The exact power of
2
(in the abelian context) was only later determined (see works of Kolster
[
18
]
and Rognes-Weibel
[
30
]
).

5
Proof of the main result

This section is concerned with proving the main results of the paper. As a start, let
\chi
be a nontrivial even Dirichlet character of order
p^{b}
and conductor
m
. Recall that the
generalized Bernoulli numbers
B_{n,\chi}
are defined by

\sum_{a=1}^{m}\frac{\chi(a)te^{at}}{e^{mt}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\chi}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

It follows from
[
34
, Proposition 4.1]
that one has

B_{k+1,\chi}=m^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{m}\chi(a)B_{k+1}(a/m).

Since the order of
\chi
is
p^{b}
, the values of
\chi
are
p^{b}
-th roots of unity. Therefore, it follows that
B_{k+1,\chi}
may be viewed as an element in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{N}})
for every
N\geq b
.

For a nonzero element
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{N}})
, we may write
\alpha=\beta/r
for some element
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]
and integer
r
. In the event that
r
is coprime to
p
, it then makes sense to speak of
\alpha

\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}]
which is essentially

\beta r^{-1}~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}].

In particular, if
\beta\in(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}]
and
r
is coprime to
p
, we shall write

\alpha\equiv 0\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}].

This convention will be frequently adopted in the subsequent discussion without further mention.
Armed with this, we can now state the following result
which will be a key component for the eventual proof of our main result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial even Dirichlet character of order
p^{b}
and conductor
m
, where
p
is an odd prime and
m
is a squarefree integer
>1
with the property that every of its prime divisor satisfies
\equiv 1

(\mathrm{mod}

p)
.
Suppose that either one of the following statements is valid.

(i)

p>k+2
.

(ii)

p=k+2
and at least one prime divisor
l
of
m
satisfies
v_{p}(l-1)\geq 2
.

Then we have
B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-b}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]
for every
N\geq b
.

Proof.

Since
(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]=(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-b}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]
, it suffices to prove the congruence for
N=b
. By
[
34
, Proposition 4.1]
, one has

B_{k+1,\chi}=m^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\chi(a)B_{k+1}(a/m)

noting that
\chi(m)=0
. The sum on the right can in turn be rewritten as

\displaystyle m^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\chi(a)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{i}(a/m)^{k+1-i}B_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k+1}{i}B_{i}m^{i-1}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\chi(a)a^{k+1-i}\right),

where we have made use of the fact that the sums
\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\chi(a)
and
\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\chi(a)a
vanish by virtue of
\chi
being a nontrivial even Dirichlet character. As the order of
\chi
is
p^{b}
, the values of
\chi
are
p^{b}
-th roots of unity. Therefore, the sum
\sum_{a=1}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1-1}\chi(a)a^{k+1-i}
can be viewed as an element in the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}]
. Consequently, we have

B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k+1}{i}B_{i}m^{i-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd(a,m)=1\end{subarray}}^{m-1}a^{k+1-i}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}]

(5.1)

noting that the congruence makes sense in view of our assumption that
p\geq k+2
and that the Bernoulli numbers appearing have denominators with prime divisors
\leq k
(cf. Lemma
2.1
). To continue, we set
m=l_{1}\cdots l_{s}
, where the
l_{i}
’s are distinct primes satisfying
p\mid l_{i}-1
. Now observe that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd(a,l_{1}\cdots l_{s})=1\end{subarray}}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1-i}=\sum_{a=1}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1-i}+\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}(-1)^{t}\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq s}(l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}})^{k+1-i}\sum_{a=1}^{\frac{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}}{l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}}-1}a^{k+1-i}\right).

(5.2)

In view of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we are essentially reduced to showing that sum of the form

\sum_{a=1}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1-i}

(5.3)

is divisible by
p
. By Lemma
2.6
, the latter sum is precisely

(l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1)\frac{f_{k+1-i}(l_{1}\cdots l_{s})}{d_{k+1-i}}.

We first consider the case that
p>k+2
. It then follows from Lemma
2.6
(c) that
d_{k+1-i}
is coprime to
p
for
i=0,1,...,k-2
. On the other hand, since each
l_{j}-1
is divisible by
p
, so is
l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1
. This therefore proves that the sum (
5.3
) is divisible by
p
when
p>k+2
.

We now turn to the case when
p=k+2
.
The argument in the preceding paragraph shows that the sum (
5.2
) is divisible by
p
for
i=1,...,k-1
. Therefore, it remains to verify that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd(a,l_{1}\cdots l_{s})=1\end{subarray}}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1}

is divisible by
p
. Since
p\parallel d_{k+1}
by Proposition
2.5
(c), we are reduced to showing that

d_{k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd(a,l_{1}\cdots l_{s})=1\end{subarray}}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1}

(5.4)

is divisible by
p^{2}
. Relabelling, if necessary, we may assume that
l_{s}\equiv 1
(mod
p^{2}
). We then proceed as in (
5.2
) and apply Lemma
2.6
to obtain

\displaystyle d_{k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\gcd(a,l_{1}\cdots l_{s})=1\end{subarray}}^{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1}a^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(l_{1}\cdots l_{s}-1)f_{k+1}(l_{1}\cdots l_{s})-l_{s}^{k+1}(l_{1}\cdots l_{s-1}-1)f_{k+1}(l_{1}\cdots l_{s-1})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{s-2}(-1)^{t}\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq s-1}(l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}})^{k+1}\Big(\frac{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}}{l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}}-1\Big)f_{k+1}\Big(\frac{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}}{l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{s-2}(-1)^{t+1}\left(\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}\leq s}(l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}l_{s})^{k+1}\Big(\frac{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}}{l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}l_{s}}-1\Big)f_{k+1}\Big(\frac{l_{1}\cdots l_{s}}{l_{j_{1}}\cdots l_{j_{t}}l_{s}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{s-1}(l_{1}\cdots l_{s-1})^{k+1}(l_{s}-1)f_{k+1}(l_{s}).

Using the fact that
l_{s}\equiv 1
(mod
p^{2}
), we see that the two terms in the first line cancel off modulo
p^{2}
. Similarly, one can check that the sum in the second line cancels off the sum in the third line modulo
p^{2}
. The final term in the last line is plainly
\equiv 0
(mod
p^{2}
). Thus, we conclude that the expression (
5.4
) is divisible by
p^{2}
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We are in position to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
k
a positive odd integer and
m
an integer
>1
. Define

s_{j}=\#\big\{l\mid m~\big|~v_{p}(l-1)=j\big\}

and set
\theta:=\max\{j~|~s_{j}\neq 0\}
. Suppose that
p\geq k+2
.
Then the power of
p
dividing the orders of the groups
K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1}])
is at least

\frac{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}\cdots+s_{\theta}}-p^{\delta s_{1}}+\delta}{p-1}+\sum_{j=2}^{\theta}\frac{p^{s_{j}+\cdots+s_{\theta}}-1}{p-1}p^{s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+(j-1)s_{j-1}+(j-1)(s_{j}+\cdots s_{\theta})-(j-1)},

where

\delta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{if $p>k+2$;}\\
1,&\hbox{if $p=k+2$.}\end{array}\right.

A similar divisibility assertion also holds for
K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}])
.

Proof.

Since the prime
p
is odd, Lemma
3.1
tells us that it suffices to prove the divisibility assertion for
K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1}])
. Now suppose that
p
divides
m
. We then write
m=p^{a}m^{\prime}
, where
a\geq 1
and
p\nmid m^{\prime}
. Note that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pm^{\prime}})
is a finite
p
-extension which is unramified outside the primes above
p
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pm^{\prime}})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m^{\prime}})
is a Galois extension of degree coprime to
p
. Therefore, it follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
that for the verification of the divisibility assertion for
K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1}])
, it is enough to verify the divisibility assertion for
K_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m^{\prime}}+\zeta_{m^{\prime}}^{-1}])
.

Hence, by virtue of the discussion in the preceding paragraph, we may, and will, assume that the integer
m
we start with is not divisible by the prime
p
. Taking Lemma
3.1
into account again, we may even assume that
m
is squarefree and that every prime divisor
l
of
m
satisfies
l\equiv 1
mod
p
. Let
F
be the maximal
p
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1})
. Via Lemma
3.1
again, we are reduced to prove the divisibility assertion for
K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
. Note that the values of
s_{j}
are unchanged throughout the whole reduction process. By our assumption on
m
, we see that

G:=\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{l\mid m}\mathbb{Z}/p^{v_{p}(l-1)}\mathbb{Z}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\theta}\big(\mathbb{Z}/p^{j}\mathbb{Z}\big)^{\oplus s_{j}},

and so the group
\widehat{G}
of linear characters of
G
has the same group structure. Set
\#\widehat{G}=p^{N}
. In particular, we have

N=s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+\theta s_{\theta}.

As
F
is a totally real abelian field, we may apply Theorem
4.1
to see that

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\sim_{p}w_{k+1}(F)\zeta_{F}(-k).

By Artin formalism, there is the following factorization

\zeta_{F}(-k)=\zeta(-k)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{G}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}L(\chi,-k),

where
\chi_{0}
denotes the trivial character and
L(\chi,s)
is the Dirichlet
L
-function attached to
\chi
. It’s well-known that

\zeta(-k)=-\frac{B_{k+1}}{k+1}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 10.00002ptL(\chi,-k)=-\frac{B_{k+1,\chi}}{k+1}

(for instance, see
[
34
, Theorem 4.2]
). Therefore, we have

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\sim_{p}\frac{w_{k+1}(F)B_{k+1}}{(k+1)^{p^{N}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{G}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}B_{k+1,\chi}.

(5.5)

We claim that

\frac{w_{k+1}(F)B_{k+1}}{(k+1)^{p^{N}}}\sim_{p}1.

(5.6)

Indeed, since
p\geq k+2
, the denominator term has no
p
contribution. On the other hand, it follows from
[
35
, Chap. VI, Proposition 2.2]
that the prime
p
divides
w_{k+1}(F)
if and only
p-1\mid k+1
. Furthermore, the same proposition tells us that
p\parallel w_{k+1}(F)
in the context of
p=k+2
. Hence we conclude that
p\nmid w_{k+1}(F)
or
p\parallel w_{k+1}(F)
accordingly to
p>k+2
or
p=k+2
. Since this
p
-divisibility property also holds for the denominator of
B_{k+1}
by Lemma
2.1
, our claim (
5.6
) thus follows.

Now, combining (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\sim_{p}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{G}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}B_{k+1,\chi}.

(5.7)

Set
\widehat{G}_{j}:=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}~|~\mathrm{ord}(\chi)=p^{j}\}
. Then we have the following factorization

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{G}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}B_{k+1,\chi}=\prod_{j=1}^{\theta}\Big(\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}_{j}}B_{k+1,\chi}\Big).

(5.8)

It follows from Proposition
5.1
that

\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}_{j}}B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-j}\#\widehat{G}_{j}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]

(5.9)

for
j\geq 2
. When
j=1
, the same proposition also tells us that

\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}_{1}}B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-1}\#\widehat{G}_{1}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]

(5.10)

provided that
p>k+2
.

Now suppose that
p=k+2
. Write
m=m^{+}m^{-}
, where every prime divisor of
m^{+}
satisfies
\equiv 1
(mod
p^{2}
). By Proposition
5.1
, we see that
B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-1}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]
whenever the conductor of
\chi
contains a prime divisor
\equiv 1
(mod
p^{2}
). On the other hand, characters with conductors not having this property must factor through
\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m^{-}})/\mathbb{Q})
, and so there are exactly
p^{s_{1}}-1
of these. Also, note that since such characters must necessarily have order
p
, we therefore only need to not count the contribution coming from these characters in
\widehat{G}_{1}
, and so we see that

\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}_{1}}B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{(\#\widehat{G}_{1}-p^{s_{1}}+1)p^{N-1}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]

(5.11)

Combining (
5.7
)
-
(
5.11
), we have

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\in(1-\zeta_{p^{N}})^{p^{N-1}(\#\widehat{G}_{1}-p^{\delta s_{1}}+\delta)+\sum_{j=2}^{\theta}p^{N-j}\#\widehat{G}_{j}}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{N}}]\cap\mathbb{Z}=p^{w}\mathbb{Z},

where

w=\left\lceil\frac{p^{N-1}(\#\widehat{G}_{1}-p^{\delta s_{1}}+\delta)+\sum_{j=2}^{\theta}p^{N-j}\#\widehat{G}_{j}}{p^{N-1}(p-1)}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{\#\widehat{G}_{1}-p^{\delta s_{1}}+\delta}{p-1}+\sum_{j=2}^{\theta}\frac{\#\widehat{G}_{j}}{p^{j-1}(p-1)}\right\rceil.

(5.12)

By an elementary abelian
p
-group calculation, one has

\#\widehat{G}_{j}=p^{s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+(j-1)s_{j-1}+j(s_{j}+\cdots+s_{\theta})}-p^{s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+(j-1)(s_{j-1}+s_{j}+\cdots+s_{\theta})}.

Putting this into (
5.12
), it then follows from a direct calculation that we obtain the quantity as asserted in the theorem.
∎

6
Revisiting and generalizing a result of Browkin

In this section, we return to study the situation of
p=k+2
in greater depth for a Dirichlet character of prime conductor. Our main result is as follows.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial even Dirichlet character of order
p
and conductor
l
, where
p
is an odd prime and
l
is a prime with the property that
p\mid l-1
.
Then
B_{p-1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}]
if and only if
v_{p}(l-1)\geq 2
.

Proof.

Write
k=p-2
.
By a similar argument to that in Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle B_{k+1,\chi}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k+1}{i}B_{i}l^{i-1}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{l-1}a^{k+1-i}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k+1}{i}B_{i}l^{i-1}\frac{(l-1)f_{k+1-i}(l)}{ld_{k+1-i}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}]

The sum on the right can be rewritten as

\frac{(l-1)f_{k+1}(l)}{ld_{k+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{k+1}{i}B_{i}l^{i-1}\frac{(l-1)f_{k+1-i}(l)}{ld_{k+1-i}}.

As seen in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, the sum on the right is divisible by
p
. Hence it follows that
B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}]
if and only if
\frac{(l-1)f_{k+1}(l)}{ld_{k+1}}
is divisible by
p
. Now, Proposition
2.5
(c) tells us that
p\parallel d_{k+1}
. Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
2.6
(c) that

f_{k+1}(l)\equiv f_{k+1}(1)\not\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~p).

Hence we see that
\frac{(l-1)f_{k+1}(l)}{ld_{k+1}}
is divisible by
p
if and only if
p^{2}\mid l-1
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We translate the preceding result to the context of
K
-groups.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
F
be a cyclic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
|F:\mathbb{Q}|=p
and
F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{l})
for some prime
l
.
Then
p
divides
\#K_{2(p-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
if and only if
v_{p}(l-1)\geq 2
.

Proof.

By (
5.7
), we have

\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\sim_{p}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}B_{k+1,\chi}.

(6.1)

Therefore, the prime
p
divides
\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
if and only if
B_{k+1,\chi}\equiv 0~\mathrm{mod}~(1-\zeta_{p^{b}})\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p^{b}}]
for some
\chi\in\widehat{\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})}-\{\chi_{0}\}
. The latter is precisely equivalent to
v_{p}(l-1)\geq 2
by Proposition
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

When
p=3
, Theorem
6.2
was proved by Browkin
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
. Therefore, our theorem can be thought as a generalization of Browkin’s result to higher
K
-groups.

We record the following quantitative observation which gives us a sense of how often the divisibility property in Theorem
6.2
is valid.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. For every prime
l\equiv 1

(
mod
p)
, we write
F_{l}
for the cyclic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
p
contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{l})
. Then one has

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\{l~prime\leq x~|~\mbox{$p$ divides $\#K_{2(p-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{l}})$ and $l\equiv 1$ $($mod $p)$}\}}{\#\{l~prime\leq x~|~\mbox{$l\equiv 1$ $($mod $p)$}\}}=\frac{~1~}{~p~}.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{a}(x)=\#\{l~\mathrm{prime}\leq x~|~l\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~p)\}
for
a=p,p^{2}
. By Proposition
6.2
, we have

\pi_{p^{2}}(x)=\#\{l~\mathrm{prime}\leq x~|~\mbox{$p$ divides $\#K_{2k}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{l}})$ and $l\equiv 1$ $($mod $p)$}\}.

On the other hand, the theorem of Dirichlet arithmetic progression tells us that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{a}(x)}{\pi(x)}=\frac{1}{\phi(a)}

for
a=p,p^{2}
, where
\pi(x)
is the number of primes
\leq x
and
\phi
is the Euler totient function. Therefore, it then follows that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{p^{2}}(x)}{\pi_{p}(x)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{p^{2}}(x)}{\pi(x)}\frac{\pi(x)}{\pi_{p}(x)}=\frac{\phi(p)}{\phi(p^{2})}=\frac{~1~}{~p~}

as required.
∎

is
(p,p-1)
-rational if and only if
\#K_{2p-4}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is not divisible by
p
. The discussion of this section therefore yields the following.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime. Suppose that
F
is a cyclic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
|F:\mathbb{Q}|=p
and
F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{l})
for some prime
l
. Then
F
is
(p,p-1)
-rational if and only if
p\parallel l-1
.

In particular, there exist infinitely many cyclic fields of degree
p
with prime conductor which are
(p,p-1)
-rational.

At the same time, there exist infinitely many cyclic fields of degree
p
with prime conductor which are not
(p,p-1)
-rational.

Proof.

The first assertion is an immediate consequence of Theorem
6.2
and the final two assertions follow from Corollary
6.4
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

We like to make a remark comparing
p
-rational number field and
(p,p-1)
-rational field in our context. By
[
27
, Theorem 3.7]
(or see
[
14
, Chap. IV, Examples 3.5.1]
), we see that every cyclic extension of degree
p
and conductor
l
with
l\equiv 1
mod
p
is necessarily
p
-rational. On the contrary, this statement is not true for the property of being a
(p,p-1)
-rational field. Indeed, the first assertion of Corollary
6.5
gives a precise characterization when such cyclic extension is
(p,p-1)
-rational.

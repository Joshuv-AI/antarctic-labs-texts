Integrals of Hyperbolic Tangent Function Thanks: Corresponding author (W. Chu): chu.wenchang@unisalento.it
Jing Li and Wenchang Chu

Abstract
. By means of the contour integration method, we evaluate, in closed form, a class of definite integrals involving hyperbolic tangent function.

2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 30E20, Secondary 11M32

There exist numerous interesting and challenging integrals in the
mathematical literature (see for example
[
2
,
9
]
).
However, the following improper integrals don’t seem to have been
examined previously

J(m,n):=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\tanh^{m}(z)}{z^{n}}dz,

(1)

where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
subject to
m\geq n\geq 2
and
m\equiv_{2}n
.
Here and forth
m\equiv_{2}n
stands for that
m
is congruent
to
n
modulo 2. The aim of this short paper is to establish
explicit formula for
J(m,n)
when
m
and
n
are considered
as two integer parameters. The final result states that the
integral value
J(m,n)
is expressed as a finitely linear
combination of
\frac{\zeta(m+n-2k+1)}{\pi^{m+n-2k}}
for

1\leq k\leq\left\lceil{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rceil
, where
\lceil{x}\rceil
denotes the
smallest integer not less than
x
.
This will be fulfilled by employing
the contour integration method.

A.
The contour integral

Rewriting the integrand in terms of the exponential function

T_{m,n}(z):=\frac{\tanh^{m}(z)}{z^{n}}=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{e^{z}+e^{-z}}\bigg)^{m}=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m},

(2)

we can express, by symmetry, the integral as

2J(m,n)=2\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}dz=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}dz.

Observe that
T_{m,n}(z)
is a meromorphic function
whose poles of
T_{m,n}(z)
are determined by

1+e^{2z}=0.

We find that all the poles of
T_{m,n}(z)
are given by

z_{k}=\bigg(k-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\pi i,\quad\text{where}\qquad{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

In order to evaluate the integral
J(m,n)
, consider the anti-clockwise
contour
\mathcal{L}
, consisting of two segments
[-\pi M,-\varepsilon]
and
[\varepsilon,\pi M]

along the real axis plus two semi-circles
\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathcal{C}(\pi M)
over
the upper half-plane centered at the origin with radii
\varepsilon
and
\pi M

(where
M\in\mathbb{N}
), respectively.
Hence, all the poles of
T_{m,n}(z)
inside the contour
\mathcal{L}

are of order
m
and given explicitly by

z_{k}=\bigg(k-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\pi i,\quad\text{where}\quad{k\in\mathbb{N}~\text{ with }~1\leq k\leq M}.

Then according to the residue theorem (see
[
3
]

and
[
8
, §3.3]
), we have the following equality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}}T_{m,n}(z)dz=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{M}\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)}T_{m,n}(z)dz+\int_{\mathcal{C}(\pi M)}T_{m,n}(z)dz

\displaystyle+\int^{-\varepsilon}_{-\pi M}T_{m,n}(z)dz+\int^{\pi M}_{\varepsilon}T_{m,n}(z)dz.

As we shall show in the next two sections that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)}T_{m,n}(z)dz=\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\pi M)}T_{m,n}(z)dz=0,

the limiting case of the above equation as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
M\to\infty

will reduce to the following identity.

Lemma 1
.

For two integers
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
subject to conditions
m\geq n\geq 2

and
m\equiv_{2}n
, the following integral-sum formula holds:

J(m,n)=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\tanh^{m}(z)}{z^{n}}dz=\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z).

B.
The integral along
\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)

Keeping in mind
m\geq n
, we have

\lim_{z\to 0}T_{m,n}(z)=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}=\begin{cases}1,&m=n,\\
0.&m>n.\end{cases}

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon
with
|\varepsilon|
being sufficiently small,
the function
T_{m,n}(z)
is bounded on
C_{\varepsilon}
, which implies that

\bigg|\int_{\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)}T_{m,n}(z)dz\bigg|=\mathcal{O}(1)\times\Big|\int_{\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)}dz\Big|=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\pi),

and consequently, the following limiting relation

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\varepsilon)}T_{m,n}(z)dz=0.

C.
The integral along
\mathcal{C}(\pi M)

Writing
z=\pi Me^{i\theta}
on the semi-circle
\mathcal{C}(\pi M)
,
we can explicitly express the modulus

\big|1+e^{2z}\big|^{2}=\Big|1+e^{2\pi M(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)}\Big|^{2}=1+e^{4\pi M\cos\theta}+2e^{2\pi M\cos\theta}\cos(2\pi M\sin\theta).

Observe that the squared modulus results in 4 for
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
,
and is equal to or greater than
(1-e^{2\pi M\cos\theta})^{2}
when

\theta\neq\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then we can choose a small
\delta>0
such that

\big|1+e^{2z}\big|^{2}\geq\begin{cases}3,&\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta;\\
(1-e^{-2\pi M\sin\delta})^{2},&0\leq\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\text{ and }\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta<\theta\leq\pi.\end{cases}

Therefore
(1+e^{2z})^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathcal{C}(\pi M)
,
which implies further that the function below is bounded too on
\mathcal{C}(\pi M)

\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}=\bigg(1-\frac{2}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}=\mathcal{O}(1).

Under the change in variable
z=\pi Me^{i\theta}
,
we estimate the integral

\Big|\int_{\mathcal{C}(\pi M)}T_{m,n}(z)dz\Big|=\mathcal{O}(1)\times\bigg|\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{ie^{(1-n)i\theta}}{(\pi M)^{n-1}}d\theta\bigg|=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\pi^{2-n}}{M^{n-1}}\Big)

Hence, we have shown that for
m\geq n\geq 2
:

\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}(\pi M)}T_{m,n}(z)dz=0.

D.
Computing residues

Finally, we have to determine the residues of
T_{m,n}(z)
at
z_{k}
explicitly.
When
m\geq n\geq 2
are small integers, it is not difficult to do this. However,
it becomes quite a tough task if
m
and
n
are considered as integer parameters.

Instead of calculating higher derivatives, we shall determine the residues
by extracting coefficients from formal power series (cf.
[
4
, §3.2]

and
[
10
, §2.1]
).
For the sake of brevity, denote by
[x^{n}]\phi(x)
the coefficient
of
x^{n}
in the formal power series
\phi(x)
. Keeping in mind that

e^{2z_{k}}=(-1)^{2k-1}=-1
, we can write

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)

\displaystyle=\big[(z-z_{k})^{-1}\big]\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\big[(z-z_{k})^{-1}\big]\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2z}+e^{2z_{k}}}{e^{2z}-e^{2z_{k}}}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\big[z-z_{k})^{-1}\big]\frac{1}{z^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{z-z_{k}}+e^{z_{k}-z}}{e^{z-z_{k}}-e^{z_{k}-z}}\bigg)^{m}.

By making the change of variables
y=z-z_{k}
, we have further

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)

\displaystyle=\big[y^{-1}\big]\frac{1}{(y+z_{k})^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}+e^{-y}}{e^{y}-e^{-y}}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\big[y^{-1}\big]\frac{(e^{y}+e^{-y})^{m}}{(2y)^{m}(y+z_{k})^{n}}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{-m}.

Then the above residue can be expressed as

\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)=\big[y^{m-1}\big]\frac{\mathrm{U}(y)\mathrm{V}(y)}{(y+z_{k})^{n}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
j\>\equiv_{2}\>m\end{subarray}}\binom{-n}{j-1}\big[y^{m-j}\big]\frac{\mathrm{U}(y)\mathrm{V}(y)}{z_{k}^{n+j-1}},

(3)

where
\mathrm{U}(y)
and
\mathrm{V}(y)
are two even functions and given by

\mathrm{U}(y)=\frac{(e^{y}+e^{-y})^{m}}{2^{m}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{V}(y)=\bigg\{1-\bigg(1-\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)\bigg\}^{-m}.

In order to extract the coefficient of
y^{m-j}
in (
3
),
it is sufficient to expand
\mathrm{U}(y)
and
\mathrm{V}(y)
on the right
to Maclaurin polynomials up to order
m
.

First by means of the binomial theorem,
\mathrm{U}(y)
can be written as

\mathrm{U}(y)=\Big(\frac{e^{y}+e^{-y}}{2}\Big)^{m}=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\lambda}\frac{e^{(m-2\lambda)y}}{2^{m}}.

(4)

Then by observing the initial terms of the Maclaurin series

\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}=1+\frac{y^{2}}{6}+\frac{y^{4}}{120}+\mathcal{O}(y^{6}),

we can expand
\mathrm{V}(y)
in succession as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(y^{m})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{-m}{\ell}\bigg(1-\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(y^{m})+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{-m}{\ell}\sum_{\mu=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\ell}{\mu}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\mu}.

Let
\mathrm{S}
stand for the above double sum.
According to the binomial relations

\displaystyle\binom{-m}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\mu}

\displaystyle=\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{-m-\mu}{\ell-\mu},

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=\mu}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell-\mu}\binom{-m-\mu}{\ell-\mu}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\mu}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu};

we can manipulate
\mathrm{S}
in the following manner:

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq\ell\leq\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell-\mu}\binom{-m}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\mu}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\mu\leq\ell\leq\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell-\mu}\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{-m-\mu}{\ell-\mu}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}\binom{-m}{\mu}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\mu}\sum_{\ell=\mu}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell-\mu}\binom{-m-\mu}{\ell-\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\mu}\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2y}\bigg)^{\mu}.

Applying further the binomial theorem to the rightmost function
and then making substitutions, we derive the following double
sum expansion

\mathrm{V}(y)=\mathcal{O}(y^{m})+\hskip-9.95845pt\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq\mu\leq\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\mu-\nu}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{\mu}{\nu}\frac{e^{(\mu-2\nu)y}}{(2y)^{\mu}}.

(5)

Now substituting (
4
) and (
5
) into (
3
),
we can further reformulate the residue as

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
j\>\equiv_{2}\>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{m}\binom{-n}{j-1}\binom{m}{\lambda}\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq\mu\leq\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\mu-\nu}}{2^{m+\mu}z_{k}^{n+j-1}}\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{\mu}{\nu}\big[y^{m-j}\big]\frac{e^{(m+\mu-2\nu-2\lambda)y}}{y^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
j\>\equiv_{2}\>m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{m}\binom{-n}{j-1}\binom{m}{\lambda}\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq\mu\leq\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\mu-\nu}}{z_{k}^{n+j-1}}\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{\mu}{\nu}\frac{(m+\mu-2\nu-2\lambda)^{m+\mu-j}}{2^{m+\mu}(m+\mu-j)!}.

Taking into account the binomial equality

\displaystyle\binom{-m}{\mu}\binom{-m-\mu-1}{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}\frac{(m)_{\mu}}{\mu!}\frac{(1+m+\mu)_{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu}}{(\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor-\mu)!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}\binom{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{\mu}\binom{m+\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{m}\frac{m}{m+\mu},

we find finally the following quadruplicate sum expression

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}T_{m,n}(z)=

\displaystyle\binom{m+\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
j\>\equiv_{2}\>m\end{subarray}}\binom{-n}{j-1}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{m}\sum_{\mu=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\mu}\frac{(-1)^{\mu+\nu}}{z_{k}^{n+j-1}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{m}{m+\mu}\binom{m}{\lambda}\binom{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{\mu}\binom{\mu}{\nu}\frac{(m-2\lambda+\mu-2\nu)^{m+\mu-j}}{2^{m+\mu}(m+\mu-j)!}.

E.
Conclusive theorem

To evaluate the sum of residues in Lemma
1
, it is enough to do that for

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z_{k}^{n+j-1}}=\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi i}\bigg)^{n+j-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{n+j-1}}=\frac{2^{n+j-1}-1}{(\pi i)^{n+j-1}}\zeta(n+j-1).

Consequently, we have proved the following general integral identity.

Theorem 2
.

Let
m
and
n
be the two natural numbers of the same parity with
m\geq n\geq 2
,
the following integral formula holds:

\displaystyle J(m,n)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\tanh^{m}(z)}{z^{n}}dz=\binom{m+\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq m\\
j\>\equiv_{2}\>m\end{subarray}}\binom{-n}{j-1}\frac{2^{n+j-1}-1}{(\pi i)^{n+j-2}}\zeta(n+j-1)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\lambda=0}^{m}\sum_{\mu=0}^{\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu+\nu}\frac{m}{m+\mu}\binom{m}{\lambda}\binom{\left\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rfloor}{\mu}\binom{\mu}{\nu}\frac{(m-2\lambda+\mu-2\nu)^{m+\mu-j}}{2^{m+\mu}(m+\mu-j)!}.

From this theorem, we assert that for each pair of
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

subject to
m\equiv_{2}n
and
m\geq n\geq 2
, the integral value

J(m,n)
results always in a finitely linear combination
of
\frac{\zeta(m+n-2k+1)}{\pi^{m+n-2k}}
with

1\leq k\leq\left\lceil{\frac{m}{2}}\right\rceil
. The initial values
of these integrals are recorded as follows:

\displaystyle J(2,2)

\displaystyle=\frac{14\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}},

\displaystyle J(3,3)

\displaystyle=-\frac{7\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}+\frac{186\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}},

\displaystyle J(4,2)

\displaystyle=\frac{56\zeta(3)}{3\pi^{2}}-\frac{124\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}},

\displaystyle J(4,4)

\displaystyle=-\frac{496\zeta(5)}{3\pi^{4}}+\frac{2540\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}},

\displaystyle J(5,3)

\displaystyle=-\frac{7\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}+\frac{310\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}}-\frac{1905\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}},

\displaystyle J(5,5)

\displaystyle=\frac{31\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}}-\frac{3175\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}}+\frac{35770\zeta(9)}{\pi^{8}},

\displaystyle J(6,2)

\displaystyle=\frac{322\zeta(3)}{15\pi^{2}}-\frac{248\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}}+\frac{762\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}},

\displaystyle J(6,4)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2852\zeta(5)}{15\pi^{4}}+\frac{5080\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}}-\frac{28616\zeta(9)}{\pi^{8}},

\displaystyle J(6,6)

\displaystyle=\frac{5842\zeta(7)}{5\pi^{6}}-\frac{57232\zeta(9)}{\pi^{8}}+\frac{515844\zeta(11)}{\pi^{10}},

\displaystyle J(7,7)

\displaystyle=-\frac{127\zeta(7)}{\pi^{6}}+\frac{1402184\zeta(9)}{45\pi^{8}}-\frac{1003030\zeta(11)}{\pi^{10}}+\frac{7568484\zeta(13)}{\pi^{12}}.

Among these formulae, the first one was proposed as a monthly problem
by Holland
[
5
]
, which has been the primary motivation
for the authors to carry on the present research.

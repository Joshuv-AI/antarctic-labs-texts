A closed manifold is a fat CW complex
Norio IWASE, Yuki Kojima

Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to introduce a new smooth version of a CW complex named a fat CW complex, and to show that it includes all closed manifolds, because existing smooth versions of CW complexes (e.g. [ Iwa22 ] ) do not have such property. We also verify that de Rham theorem holds for a fat CW complex and that a regular CW complex is reflexive in the sense of Y. Karshon, J. Watts and P. I-Zemmour. Further, any topological CW complex is topologically homotopy equivalent to a fat CW complex. So, a fat CW complex enjoys many nice properties.

5.
Basic Properties

In this section, we show some basic properties of a fat CW complex.

Let
X
be a collection of pairs
X^{(n)}
of diffeological spaces and attaching maps
h_{n}:\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\textstyle\coprod}\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\to X^{(n-1)}
.
Then it is clear that
X^{(n-1)}
is a closed subset of
X^{(n)}
under the
D
-topology.
We first show that a fat CW complex is a paracompactum.

Proposition 5.1
.

The
D
-topology of
X
is paracompact and Hausdorff.

Proof.

We show that
X^{(n)}
is paracompact and Hausdorff by induction on
n\geq-1
.
When
n=-1
, there is nothing to do.
So, we assume that we have done up to
n\!-\!1
,
n\geq 0
.
Firstly,
\mathbb{D}^{n,m}\approx\mathbb{R}^{n}\times D^{m}
is paracompact and Hausdorff as a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and so is its closed subset
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m}
.
Thus the coproducts
\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\textstyle\coprod}\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
and
\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\textstyle\coprod}\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
are paracompact and Hausdorff.
Secondly, since
i_{n}:\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\textstyle\coprod}\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\hookrightarrow\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\textstyle\coprod}\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
is a closed embedding in
Topology
, the pushout
X^{(n)}
of
i_{n}
and
\hat{i}_{n}:X^{(n-1)}\to X^{(n)}
is also paracompact and Hausdorff.
Finally, since “paracompact and Hausdorff”-ness is preserved under taking colimit of closed embeddings,
X=\operatorname{colim}\,X^{(n)}
is paracompact and Hausdorff.
∎

We then consider the inclusion map
\hat{i}_{n}:X^{(n-1)}\hookrightarrow X^{(n)}
,
n\!\geq\!0
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For each
n\!\geq\!0
, the map
\hat{i}_{n}:X^{(n-1)}\hookrightarrow X^{(n)}
is an induction.

Proof.

Assume that
P:U\to X^{(n)}
is a plot with its image contained in
\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{i}_{n})
.
Since there is a subduction
X^{(n-1)}\amalg\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\twoheadrightarrow X^{(n)}
by the definition of
X^{(n)}
, there is an open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
U
such that
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
can be pulled back to a plot from
U_{\alpha}
to either
X^{(n-1)}
or
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
for some
j\in J_{n}
.

In the first case,
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
is pulled back to a plot
P_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to X^{(n-1)}
as
P|_{U_{\alpha}}=\hat{i}_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P_{\alpha}
.

In the second case,
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
is pulled back to a plot
P_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
for some
j\in J_{n}
as
P|_{U_{\alpha}}=\hat{h}_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P_{\alpha}
.
Then
\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0ptP_{\alpha})\subset(\hat{h}_{n})^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{i}_{n}))=\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
in
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
.
Since
i_{n}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
is an induction, we may assume that
P_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
is a plot in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
, and hence we obtain
P|_{U_{\alpha}}=\hat{h}_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P_{\alpha}=\hat{i}_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}h_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P_{\alpha}
.

In either case,
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
is pulled back to a plot in
X^{(n-1)}
.
Thus
\hat{i}_{n}
is an induction.
∎

6.
Smooth Functions

Theorem 6.1
.

For any open covering
\mathcal{U}
on a fat CW complex, there is a smooth partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, we may assume that
\mathcal{U}
is of locally finite.
Let
X
be a fat CW complex with fat skeletons
\{X^{(n)}\}
, and
\mathcal{U}_{n}=\{\,U\cap X^{(n)}\mid U\in\mathcal{U}\,\}
.
We show that there is a smooth partition of unity on
X^{(n)}
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
by induction on
n\geq 0
.

(Case
n\!=\!0
)
Since
D^{m}
is a manifold with boundary, we have nothing to do.

(Case
n\!>\!0
)
By induction hypothesis, there is a smooth partition of unity
\{\,\rho_{U}\,\}_{U\in\mathcal{U}}
on
X^{(n-1)}
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}_{n-1}
.
By pulling back
\rho_{U}
’s by
h^{j}_{n}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\to X^{(n-1)}
the restriction of
h_{n}:\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\to X^{(n-1)}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
, we obtain a smooth partition of unity
\{\rho_{U}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}h^{j}_{n}\}_{U\in\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
,
j\in J_{n}
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}^{j}_{n}=\{\,h_{n}^{-1}(U)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\mid U\in\mathcal{U}\,\}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
, such that we obtain
\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\rho_{U}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}h^{j}_{n}}\subset h_{n}^{-1}(U)
.
Then we take a smooth extension
\hat{\rho}^{j}_{U}:\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\to\mathbb{R}
of
\rho_{U}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}h^{j}_{n}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\hat{\rho}^{j}_{U}}\subset\hat{h}_{n}^{-1}(U)
.
We also have a neighbourhood
V_{\!j}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
in
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
on which
\underset{V\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}\hat{\rho}^{j}_{V}\not=0
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{j}_{n}=\{\,\hat{h}_{n}^{-1}(U)\cap\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\mid U\in\mathcal{U}\,\}
an open cover of
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
.
Since
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
is a manifold with boundary, we have a smooth partition of unity
\{\hat{\sigma}_{U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
subordinate to
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{j}_{n}
so that
\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\hat{\sigma}_{U}}\subset\hat{h}_{n}^{-1}(U)\cap\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
and
\underset{V\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}\hat{\sigma}_{V}\not=0
.

Since
\mathcal{V}_{\!j}=\{\,V_{\!j},\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}\,\}
is an open covering of
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
, and hence there is a smooth partition of unity
\{\,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\,\}
subordinate to
\mathcal{V}_{\!j}
:

\displaystyle\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=1,\qquad\begin{cases}\,\chi_{1}:\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\chi_{1}}\subset\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)},\\[2.15277pt]
\,\chi_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\chi_{2}}\subset V_{\!j},\end{cases}

Using the above functions, we define smooth functions
\sigma^{j}_{U}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
as follows.

\displaystyle\check{\sigma}^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x})=\begin{cases}\,\chi_{1}(\mathbb{x}){\cdot}\hat{\sigma}^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x})+\chi_{2}(\mathbb{x}){\cdot}\hat{\rho}^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x}),&\mathbb{x}\in V_{\!j},\\[2.15277pt]
\,\hat{\sigma}^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x}),&\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\chi_{2}}.\end{cases}

Hence
\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\check{\sigma}^{j}_{U}}\subset\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\hat{\sigma}_{U}}\cup\operatorname{supp}\hskip 0.5pt{\hat{\rho}^{j}_{U}}\subset\hat{h}_{n}^{-1}(U)
and
\underset{V\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}\check{\sigma}^{j}_{V}\not=0
.
Then we define

\displaystyle\sigma^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x})=\frac{\check{\sigma}^{j}_{U}(\mathbb{x})}{\underset{V\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}\check{\sigma}^{j}_{V}(\mathbb{x})},\quad\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)},

which gives a smooth partition of unity on
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
subordinate to
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{j}_{n}
and is also an extension of a smooth partition of unity
\{\rho_{U}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}h^{j}_{n}\}_{U\in\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}^{j}_{n}
.
Smooth maps
\sigma^{j}_{U}
,
j\in J_{n}
and
\rho_{U}
are compatible data for us to obtain a smooth partition of unity on the pushout
X^{(n)}
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

For a fat CW complex, the de Rham theorem holds.

Let
X_{0}^{(n-1)}

=

X^{(n)}\smallsetminus\hat{h}_{n}(\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}(\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\mathbb{S}_{0}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}))
.
Since
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\mathbb{S}_{0}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}
is compact in
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
, so is
\hat{h}_{n}(\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}(\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\smallsetminus\mathbb{S}_{0}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)}))
in
X^{(n)}
, and hence
X_{0}^{(n-1)}
is
D
-open in
X^{(n)}
.
Since
X_{0}^{(n-1)}

=

X^{(n-1)}\smallsetminus h_{n}(\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}\partial_{0}\mathbb{S}^{n-1,m_{n}(j)})\subset X^{(n-1)}
,
X_{0}^{(n-1)}
is
D
-open in
X^{(n-1)}
, too.

Following
[
Iwa22
]
, we say that a diffeological space
X
has enough many smooth functions, if the
D
-topology of
X
has an open base of the form

, where
\pi
is a smooth function on

.
For example, by J. Watts
[
Wat12
]
and P. I-Zemmour
[

Theorem 6.3
.

A fat CW complex has enough many smooth functions.

Proof.

Let
X
be a fat CW complex and
U
be an open neighbourhood of an element
\mathbb{a}\in X
.
Then, since
X
is Hausdorff,
V=X\smallsetminus\{\mathbb{a}\}
is open, and hence
\mathbb{U}=\{U,V\}
is an open covering of
X
.
Hence, by Theorem
6.1
, there is a smooth partition of unity
\{\rho_{U},\rho_{V}\}
subordinate to
\mathbb{U}
.
Put
f=\rho_{U}
, and we have done.
∎

.

A parametrization

of a diffeological space

is said to be a pre-plot if it satisfies the following condition.

•

.

Let

be a fat CW complex, and

.

is continuous w.r.t.

-topology.

It is sufficient to show that, for any open subset

,

is open in

.

, and hence

.

, there exists a smooth function

7.
Regular Complex

In this section,
X
stands for a fat CW complex with skeleta
\{X^{(n)}\}
.

Defninition 7.1
.

Let us assume that
X
is equipped with attaching maps
h_{n}
,
n\geq 0
.
A fat CW complex
X
is said to be
regular
, if
h_{n}
is an induction onto a
D
-open subset of
X^{(n-1)}
,
n\geq 0
.
In this case, we also say that
X
is a regular CW complex.

Proposition 7.2
.

If
X
is regular, then, for each
n\!\geq\!0
, the map
\hat{h}_{n}:\underset{j\in J_{n}}{\coprod}\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}\to X^{(n)}
induced from the induction

is also an induction
onto a
D
-open subset of
X^{(n)}
.

Proof.

Since
h_{n}
is an induction, the pushout
\hat{h}_{n}
is naturally an induction.
So, we are left to show that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{h}_{n})
is
D
-open.
Suppose
P:U\to X^{(n)}
is a plot.
Then there is an open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
U
such that
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
can be pulled back to a plot from
U_{\alpha}
to either
X^{(n-1)}
or
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
for some
j\in J_{n}
, by the definition of
X^{(n)}
.

In the first case, we may assume that
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
is pulled back to a plot
P_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to X^{(n-1)}
as
P|_{U_{\alpha}}=\hat{i}_{n}{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P_{\alpha}
.
Then
(P|_{U_{\alpha}})^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{h}_{n}))=P_{\alpha}^{-1}((\hat{i}_{n})^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{h}_{n})))=P_{\alpha}^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{h}_{n})\cap X^{(n-1)})=P_{\alpha}^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pth_{n}))\subset U_{\alpha}
.
Since
\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pth_{n})
is

-open,
P_{\alpha}^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pth_{n}))
is open in

.

In the second case, we may assume that
P|_{U_{\alpha}}
is pulled back to a plot
P_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
for some

as

.

.

In either case,

, and hence
\operatorname{Im}\hskip 1.5pt(\hskip 1.0pt\hat{h}_{n}))

-open.

If

is regular, we can define its

-open subset

of

by induction as follows.

.

(2)

By J. Watts
[

]
, a manifold with corners is a Frölicher space, which can be embedded into
Diffeology
as a reflexive diffeological space.
Thus spheres and disks are all reflexive in
Diffeology
.
The following is our main result.

Theorem 8.1
.

A regular CW complex of finite dimension is reflexive.

Proof.

Let
X
be a fat CW complex satisfying

, for some

We show the statement by induction on
n\!\geq\!0
.
In the case when

In the case when

, and assume that
P:U\to X
is a parametrization satisfying that, for every smooth function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
,
f{\smash{\lower-0.43057pt\hbox{\scriptsize$\circ$\,}}}P:U\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function.
Since
X=X^{(n)}
, an open covering of
X
is given by
\mathbb{D}^{n,m_{n}(j)}
and

.
Then by Proposition

, we obtain that there is an open covering

of

goes through either

or

for some

.

In case when

can be described as a composition

, we have that
P^{\prime}_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow X^{(n-1)}
is smooth:
for a given smooth function

Now, we are ready to show Theorem
3.7
.

Let
\Theta_{1,1}:\mathbb{D}^{1,1}\to\mathbb{R}\times D^{1}
be the smooth inverse function of
\Phi_{1,1}
.
Then we obtain a smooth function
\Theta_{n,m}
for
(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{y})\in\mathbb{D}^{n,m}
by the following formula:

\displaystyle\Theta_{n,m}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{y})=(\frac{1}{\alpha(u,v)}{\cdot}\mathbb{x},\frac{1}{\beta(u,v)}{\cdot}\mathbb{y}),\quad(u,v)=\Theta_{1,1}(\|{\mathbb{x}}\|,\|{\mathbb{y}}\|).

Then we have
\Psi_{1,1}(u,v)=(\|{\mathbb{x}}\|,\|{\mathbb{y}}\|)
, and hence
(\|{\mathbb{x}}\|,\|{\mathbb{y}}\|)=(\alpha(u,v){\cdot}u,\beta(u,v){\cdot}v)
.
Let
(\mathbb{u},\mathbb{v})=\Theta_{n,m}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{y})
.
Then we obtain

(\|{\mathbb{u}}\|,\|{\mathbb{v}}\|)=(\frac{1}{\alpha(u,v)}{\cdot}\|{\mathbb{x}}\|,\frac{1}{\beta(u,v)}{\cdot}\|{\mathbb{y}}\|)=(u,v)
and

.
Thus
\Theta_{n,m}
is the inverse function of
\Phi_{n,m}
.
It completes the proof of the theorem.

(x,y)=\widehat{\Phi}_{1,1}(u,v)
, we have

\displaystyle x=\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$3$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}u-\phi(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$3$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}|u|{-}1)+\phi(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$3$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}|u|{-}2){\cdot}\cos(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$\pi$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}v),

\displaystyle y=v+\phi(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$3$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}|u|{-}1){\cdot}{\sin(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$\pi$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}v)}.

It then follows, by putting
u=1
, that

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453ptx}{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453ptu}(1,v)=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2}{\cdot}\lambda(1)+\frac{3}{2}{\cdot}\lambda(0){\cdot}\cos(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$\pi$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}v)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453ptx}{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453ptv}(1,v)=-\frac{\pi}{2}{\cdot}\phi(-\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$1$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}){\cdot}\sin(\mbox{\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{\footnotesize$\pi$}{\hskip-1.00006pt\mbox{\raise 0.5382pt\hbox{\small$/$}}\hskip-1.00006pt}\raise-1.07639pt\hbox{\footnotesize$2$}}{\cdot}v)=0,

which implies
\frac{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453pt(x,y)}{\operatorname{\partial}\hskip-0.28453pt(u,v)}(1,v)=0
, and
\widehat{\Phi}_{1,1}
is not a diffeomorphism.
Further, we obtain that the Jacobian of
\widehat{\Phi}_{n,m}
is zero on
S^{n}\times D^{m}
for all
n,\,m\geq 1
.
Since the compact subspace
S^{n}\times D^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times D^{m}
has a compact neighbourhood and
\mathbb{D}^{n,m}
is a subspace of the euclidean space which is Hausdorff,
\widehat{\Phi}_{n,m}
has continuous inverse near
\widehat{\Phi}_{n,m}(S^{n}\times D^{m})
, and hence
\widehat{\Phi}_{n,m}
is a homeomorphism.

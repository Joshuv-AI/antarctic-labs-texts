topology of singular set of semiconcave function via Arnaud’s theorem
Tianqi Shi, Wei Cheng and Jiahui Hong

Abstract
. We proved the (local) path-connectedness of certain subset of the singular set of semiconcave functions with linear modulus in general. In some sense this result is optimal. The proof is based on a theorem by Marie-Claude Arnaud (M.-C. Arnaud, Pseudographs and the Lax-Oleinik semi-group: a geometric and dynamical interpretation . Nonlinearity, 24 (1): 71-78, 2011.). We also gave a new proof of the theorem in time-dependent case.

1.
Introduction

The problem of singularities of semiconcave functions plays an important role in calculus of variation and optimal control, PDE, Hamiltonian dynamical systems and geometry. For convex Lagrangian on Euclidean space or finite dimensional manifold, the maximal regularity of the value function of the associated variational problem is semiconcavity. The study of the singularities of semiconcave functions in the literature can go back to the case of convex function which is a semiconvex function with linear modulus. The main theme of the previous study is the measure theoretic aspect of the set of singular points (
[
17
,
3
,
1
]
) and the dynamic aspect, i.e., the propagation of singularities of semiconcave functions (
[
2
,
9
]
). Recently, an intrinsic approach leads to some topological results of the cut locus as well as singular set of the viscosity solutions (
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
).

Let
M
be a compact and connected smooth manifold without boundary.
TM
and
T^{*}M
are the tangent and cotangent bundle of
M
respectively. A function
L=L(t,x,v):\mathbb{R}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
is called time-dependent
Tonelli Lagrangian
, if it is a function of class
C^{2}
and satisfies the following conditions:

(L1)

The function
v\mapsto L(t,x,v)
is strictly convex for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M
.

(L2)

There exists a superlinear function
\theta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}
function
c_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
, such that

\displaystyle L(t,x,v)\geqslant\theta(|v|_{x})-c_{0},\qquad\forall(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times TM.

(L3)

There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, such that

\displaystyle|L_{t}(t,x,v)|\leqslant C_{1}+C_{2}L(t,x,v),\qquad\forall(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times TM.

We call the Lagrangian
H
associated with
L
a
Tonelli Hamiltonian
, defined by

\displaystyle H(t,x,p)=\sup_{v\in T_{x}M}\{p(v)-L(t,x,v)\},\qquad(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{R}\times T^{*}M.

Given any
x,y\in M
, and
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we denote by
\Gamma_{x,y}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
the set of absolutely continuous curves
\gamma\in\text{AC}([t_{1},t_{2}],M)
with
\gamma(t_{1})=x
and
\gamma(t_{2})=y
. We call the function
h=h(t_{1},t_{2},x,y):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
the
fundamental solution
of the associated Hamilton-Jacobi equation, define by

\displaystyle h(t_{1},t_{2},x,y):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}^{t_{1},t_{2}}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}L(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\,ds,\qquad t_{1}<t_{2},\quad x,y\in M.

For any
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\in M
, and
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we define

\displaystyle T_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi(x):=\inf_{y\in M}\{\phi(y)+h(t_{1},t_{2},y,x)\},

\displaystyle\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi(x):=\sup_{y\in M}\{\phi(y)-h(t_{1},t_{2},x,y)\}.

The families
\{T_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\}
and
\{\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\}
of operators are called
negative and positive Lax-Oleinik evolution
respectively.

In this note, we will study the singular set of general semiconcave functions in the sense of topology, using some intrinsic observation based on a theorem by Marie-Claude Arnaud. We denote by
\Phi_{H}^{s,t}
the associated flow of time-independent Hamiltonian
H
from
s
to
t
. Arnaud’s theorem states that for
\phi\in\text{SCL}\,(M)
, the space of semiconcave functions (with linear modulus) on
M
and
0<t_{2}-t_{1}\ll 1
such that
\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi\in C^{1,1}
, then

\displaystyle\Phi_{H}^{t_{1},t_{2}}(\text{\rm graph}\,(D\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi))=\text{\rm graph}\,(D^{+}\phi),

where

\displaystyle\text{graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)=

\displaystyle\,\{(x,p)\in T^{*}M:x\in M,p\in D^{+}\phi(x)\},

\displaystyle\text{graph}\,(D\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi)=

\displaystyle\,\{(x,D\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi(x))\in T^{*}M:x\in M\}.

In other words, there is a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism between the Lipschitz graph of
D\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\phi
and the 1-graph
\text{graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)
. Thus, an immediate consequence is that
\text{graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)
is a
d
-rectifiable set, which is also an
(\mathcal{H}^{d},d)
-rectifiable set,
\Sigma^{\geqslant k}(\phi)=\{x\in M|\dim D^{+}\phi(x)\geqslant k\}
is countably
(\mathcal{H}^{d-k},d-k)
-rectifiable, then so is
\Sigma^{k}(\phi)=\{x\in M|\dim D^{+}\phi(x)=k\}
. One can also have some easy topology consequences,

\bullet

\text{ind}\,(\Sigma^{\geqslant k}(\phi))\leqslant d-k
for
k=0,\cdots,d
, where
\text{ind}\,(X)
stands for the small inductive dimension of a
T_{3}
topological space
X
.

\bullet

\Sigma^{\geqslant k}(\phi)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set for
k=0,\cdots,d
. In particular,
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)=\Sigma^{\geqslant 1}(\phi)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set, and in this case,
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
is of first category in the sense of Baire.
\Sigma^{d}(\phi)
is at most countable.

In the case that a semiconcave function
u
is a viscosity solution of Hamilton-Jacobi equation,
\text{Sing}\,(u)
is even locally path-connected (
[
6
,
7
]
). Main result of this paper is: Assume that
\phi\in\text{SCL}\,(M)
and
d\geqslant 2
. Then for any
A\subseteq\Sigma^{\geqslant 2}(\phi)
,
M\setminus A
is path-connected and locally path-connected. In particular, for
k=1,2,\cdots,d
,
\Sigma^{\leqslant k}(\phi)
are path-connected and locally path-connected subsets of
M
. We also give an example to conclude that, in some sense,
\Sigma^{\leqslant 1}(\phi)=\Sigma^{0}(\phi)\cup\Sigma^{1}(\phi)
is the smallest subset with path-connectedness and local path-connectedness, in general.

We also give a new proof of Arnaud’s theorem for time-dependent Hamiltonian. This new proof also provides some new information on the intrinsic nature of the propagation of singularities and its connection to the non-commutativity of Lax-Oleinik commutators
T_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\circ\breve{T}_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we introduced some preliminary materials on semiconcave function, Lax-Oleinik operator and some basic notions on rectifiability and dimension theory from topology. In section 3, we proved the path-connectedness and other topological properties of
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
and subsets of
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
for
\phi\in\text{SCL}\,(M)
. We also gave a new proof of Arnaud’s theorem for time-dependent Hamiltonian.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Semiconcave functions

We first recall some basic relevant notions on semiconcavity.

–

Let
\Omega
be an open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A function
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is called a semiconcave function (of linear modulus) with constant
C\geqslant 0
if for any
x\in\Omega
there exists
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(2.1)

\phi(y)\leqslant\phi(x)+\langle p,y-x\rangle+\frac{C}{2}|x-y|^{2},\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

–

The set of covectors
p
satisfying (
2.1
) is called the proximal superdifferential of
\phi
at
x
and we denote it by
D^{+}\phi(x)
.

–

Similarly,
\phi
is semiconvex if
-\phi
is semiconcave. The set of
D^{-}\phi(x)=-D^{+}(-\phi)(x)
is called the proximal subdifferential of
\phi
at
x
.

–

The set
D^{+}\phi(x)
is a singleton if and only if
\phi
is differentiable at
x
, and
D^{+}\phi(x)=\{D\phi(x)\}
. A point
x
is called a singular point of a semiconcave function
\phi
if
D^{+}\phi(x)
is not a singleton. We denote by
\text{Sing}\,(\phi)
the set of all singular points of
\phi
.

–

We call
p\in D^{*}\phi(x)
, the set of reachable gradients, if there exists a sequence
x_{k}\to x
as
k\to\infty
,
\phi
is differentiable at each
x_{k}
and
p=\lim_{k\to\infty}D\phi(x_{k})
. We have
D^{*}\phi(x)\subset D^{+}\phi(x)
and
D^{+}\phi(x)=\text{co}\,D^{*}\phi(x)
.

For more on the semiconcave functions, the readers can refer to
[
8
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

A function
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a semiconcave function with constant
C\geqslant 0
if and only if there exists a family of
C^{2}
-function
\{\phi_{i}\}
with
D^{2}\phi_{i}\leqslant CI
such that

\displaystyle\phi=\inf_{i}\phi_{i}.

Proposition
2.1
is an important and useful characterization of semiconcavity. Let
S
be a compact topological space and
F:S\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that

(a)

F(s,\cdot)
is of class
C^{2}
for all
s\in S
and
\|F(s,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}}
is uniformly bounded by some constant
C
,

(b)

D_{x}F(s,x)
is continuous on
S\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(c)

\phi(x)=\inf\{F(s,x):s\in S\}
.

We call such a function
\phi
a marginal function of a family of
C^{2}
-functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\phi(x)=\inf\{F(s,x):s\in S\}
be a marginal function of the family of
C^{2}
-functions
\{F(s,\cdot)\}_{s\in S}
.

(1)

\phi
is semiconcave with constant
C
.

(2)

D^{*}\phi(x)\subseteq\{D_{x}F(s,x):x\in\arg\min\{F(s,\cdot):s\in S\}\}
.

(3)

For each
x
, let
M(x)=\arg\min\{F(s,x):s\in S\}
. Then

\displaystyle D^{+}\phi(x)=\begin{cases}D_{x}F(s,x),&M(x)=\{s\}\ \text{is a singleton};\\
\mbox{\rm co}\,\{D_{x}F(s,x):s\in M(x)\},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

If
M
is a connected and compact smooth manifold without boundary, a function
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
is called semiconcave function, if there exists a family of
C^{2}
-functions
\{\phi_{i}\}
such that
\phi=\inf_{i}\phi_{i}
, and the Hessians of
\phi_{i}
’s is uniformly bounded above. The readers can refer to the appendix of the paper
[
13
]
for more discussion of the semiconcavity of functions on (even noncompact) manifold. However, because of the local nature of our main discussion, we will work on Euclidean space instead.

2.2.
Some aspects of rectifiability

For any metric space
(X,\rho)
and
s\in[0,+\infty)
, let
\mathcal{H}^{s}
be the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
X
. For any
E\subset X
, The
Hausdorff dimension
of a subset

is

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E):=\inf\{s\geqslant 0|\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=0\}=\sup\{s\geqslant 0|\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=+\infty\}.

Proposition 2.4
.

The Hausdorff measure satisfies the following properties.

(1)

The measure
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is Borel regular measure for all
s\in[0,+\infty)
;

(2)

\mathcal{H}^{d}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}
is the counting measure;

(3)

If
f\in\emph{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

, then we have
\mathcal{H}^{s}(f(E))\leqslant(\emph{Lip}\,(f))^{s}\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
;

(4)

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0\leqslant s<t<+\infty
. If
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)<+\infty
, then
\mathcal{H}^{t}(E)=0
. If
\mathcal{H}^{t}(E)>0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=+\infty
.

Proposition 2.5
.

(1)

\forall E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\dim_{H}E\leqslant d
. And for
s=\dim_{H}E
,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)<+\infty
or
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)>0
;

(2)

Assume that
F_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
i=1,2,\cdots
, then

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}F_{i})=\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\infty}\{\dim_{H}(F_{i})\}.

The following definition of rectifibaility with respect to Hausdorff measure comes from
[

]
.

Definition 2.6
.

(Rectifiability)
Assume that
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
m
is a positive integer.

(1)

E
is
m
-
rectifiable
if and only if there exists a Lipschitz map
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a bounded set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that
f(B)=E
;

(2)

E
is
countably

m
-

if and only if
E
equals the union of some countable family whose members are
m
-rectifiable;

(3)

E
is
countably

(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-
rectifiable
if and only if there exists a countably
m
-rectifiable set containing
\mathcal{H}^{m}
almost all of

;

(4)

E
is
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-

if and only if

is countably
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable and
\mathcal{H}^{m}(E)<+\infty
.

Remark 2.7
.

For rectifiablity, we have some comments as follows.

(1)

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
m
-rectifiable, then
E
is
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable;

This is straightforward from the facts that
E
is the image of a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
under a Lipschitz map
f
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{m}(E)=\mathcal{H}^{m}(f(B))\leqslant(\text{Lip}\,(f))^{m}\mathcal{H}^{m}(B)<+\infty.

(2)

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable, then
\dim_{H}(E)\leqslant m
;

(3)

E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is countably
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable if and only if
E
can be represented as a countable union of
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable sets;

Since
E
is countably
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable, it is equivalent to there exists a sequence of
m
-rectifiable sets
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{m}\left(E-\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}\right)=0.

Now let
V_{0}=E-\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}
, then
V_{0}
is
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable, and
E=\cup_{i=0}^{\infty}V_{i}
. Conversely, If
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of the
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable set and
E=\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}
, then for each
i
, there exists
\{V_{ij}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are the
m
-rectifiable sets, which satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{m}\left(V_{i}-\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}V_{ij}\right)=0.

Thus we can choose countable
m
-rectifiable sets
\{V_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{\infty}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{m}\left(E-\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{\infty}V_{ij}\right)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{m}\left(V_{i}-\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}V_{ij}\right)=0.

(4)

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a (countably)
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable set,
F\subseteq E
, then so is
F
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
W
is an
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)

The proof of

General topology and dimension theory

Assume that
X
is a topological space.

Definition 2.9
.

(small inductive dimension, see
[
12
, Definition 1.1.1]
) Assume that
X
is a
T_{3}
topological space, we define the
small inductive dimension
denoted by
\text{ind}\,(X)
, an integer ”inductively”:

(1)

\text{ind}\,(X)=-1
if and only if
X=\varnothing
;

(2)

\text{ind}\,(X)\leqslant n
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, if for every point
x\in X
and each neighbourhood
V\subseteq X
of the point
x
there exists an open set
U\subseteq X
such that
x\in U\subseteq V
and
\text{ind}\,(\partial U)\leqslant n-1
;

(3)

\text{ind}\,(X)=n
if
\text{ind}\,(X)\leqslant n
and
\text{ind}\,(X)>n-1
, i.e. the inequality
\text{ind}\,(X)\leqslant n-1
does not hold;

(4)

\text{ind}\,(X)=+\infty
if
\text{ind}\,(X)>n
for all
n=-1,0,1,\cdots
.

Theorem 2.10
.

(1)

For any metric space
X
,
\emph{ind}\,(X)\leqslant\dim_{H}(X)
;

(2)

For any
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
0\leqslant\emph{ind}\,(E)\leqslant\dim_{H}(E)\leqslant d
;

(3)

For every subspace
Y
of a separable metric space

, we have
\emph{ind}\,(Y)\leqslant\emph{ind}\,(X)
.

Proof.

The conclusion (1) follows from Theorem 6.3.10 of
[
11
]
. (2) can be obtained directly from (1). (3) is a direct corollary of Theorem 1.1.2 in
[
12
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

For a convex set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\dim\,(E)=\emph{ind}\,(E)=\dim_{H}(E)
, where
\dim\,(E)
is the dimension of convex set
E
(See
[
15
, Definition 2.1.1]
).

Proof.

For each point
x
in the relative interior of
E
, which we denote by
\text{ri}E
(See
[
15
, Section 2.1]
for more information), there exists
\delta>0
such that the intersection of the affine hull
\text{aff}E
and
d
-dimensional closed ball
B^{d}(x,\delta)
contains in
E
. And
\text{ind}\,(\partial(\text{aff}E\cap B^{d}(x,\delta)))=\text{ind}\,(S^{\dim(\text{aff}E)-1})=\dim\,(\text{aff}E)-1
. The arbitrariness of

and Theorem
2.10
imply that

\displaystyle\text{ind}\,(E)\geqslant\text{ind}\,(\text{ri}E)=\dim\,(\text{aff}\,E)=\dim\,(E).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\dim\,(E)=\dim\,(\text{aff}\,E)=\text{ind}\,(\text{aff}\,E)\geqslant\text{ind}\,(E).

Consequently, we have
\dim\,(E)=\text{ind}\,(E)
. Moreover, with Theorem

,

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E)\geqslant\text{ind}\,(E)=\dim\,(E)=\dim\,(\text{aff}\,E)=\dim_{H}(\text{aff}\,E)\geqslant\dim_{H}(E),

which completes the proof.
∎

3.
Topology of the singular set of semiconcave functions

Let
M
be a connected and closed manifold of dimension
d
. Let
\text{SCL}\,(M)
be the set of semiconcave functions on
M
. Let
H=H(x,p)
be any time-independent Tonelli Hamiltonian on
M
. This allows us to apply following Proposition
3.1
directly. If
t_{1}<t_{2}
, then
\breve{T}^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}=\breve{T}^{t_{2}+s}_{t_{1}+s}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we set
t=t_{2}-t_{1}
and
\breve{T}^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}=\breve{T}_{0}^{t}
. For any semiconcave function
\phi\in\text{SCL}\,(M)
, let

\displaystyle t_{\phi}:=

\displaystyle\,\sup\{t>0:\breve{T}_{0}^{t}\phi\in C^{1,1}(M)\}.

For any
\phi\in\text{SCL}(M)
, and
t\in(0,t_{\phi}]
, we denote by

\displaystyle\text{graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)=

\displaystyle\,\{(x,p)\in T^{*}M:x\in M,p\in D^{+}\phi(x)\},

\displaystyle\text{graph}\,(D\breve{T}_{0}^{t}\phi)=

\displaystyle\,\{(x,D\breve{T}_{0}^{t}\phi(x))\in T^{*}M:x\in M\}.

We call the set
\text{\rm graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)
the
pseudo-graph
of
D\phi
or
1-graph
of
\phi
. Now, let us recall a result by Marie-Claude Arnaud (
[
4
]
) at first.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi\in\text{\rm SCL}\,(M)
and
t_{0}>0
such that
\breve{T}^{t_{0}}_{0}\phi\in C^{1,1}(M)
. Then,
\breve{T}_{0}^{t}\phi\in C^{1,1}(M)
for all
t\in(0,t_{0}]
, and we have

(3.1)

\text{\rm graph}\,(D\breve{T}^{t}_{0}\phi)=\Phi_{H}^{-t}(\text{\rm graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)),\qquad\forall t\in(0,t_{0}],

where
\{\Phi^{t}_{H}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the Hamiltonian flow with respect to any time-independent Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
.

The original proof theorem of Arnaud is based some approximation of the graph for time-independent Lagrangian. We give a new proof of a refinement (Theorem
3.15
) of Proposition
3.1
in time dependent case with more geometric intuition. The relation (
3.1
) defines a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism between
\text{\rm graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)
and
\text{\rm graph}\,(D\breve{T}_{0}^{t}\phi)
by the Hamiltonian flow. This allows us to understand the singularities of
\phi
by means of the latter Lipschitz graph. Expect for some finer results obtain in
[
1
]
, this idea also leads to more topological results of the singular set of semiconcave functions.

3.1.
Singular set of semiconcave functions

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\phi\in\emph{SCL}\,(M)
,
\emph{graph}\,(D^{+}\phi)
is a
d
-rectifiable set, which is also an
(\mathcal{H}^{d},d)

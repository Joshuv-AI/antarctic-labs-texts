Maximal operators given by Fourier multipliers with dilation of fractional dimensions
Jin Bong Lee, Jinsol Seo

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate L p L^{p} bounds of maximal Fourier multiplier operators with dilation of fractional dimensions. For the Fourier multipliers, we suggest a criterion related to dimensions of dilation sets which guarantees L p L^{p} bounds of the maximal operators for each p p . Our criterion covers Mikhlin-type multipliers, multipliers with limited decay, and multipliers with slow decay.

1.
Introduction

Let
m
be a bounded function.
Then for a Schwartz functin
f
, a Fourier multiplier operator and its dilation
T_{m}(f)(x),T_{m(t)}(f)(x)
are given by

\widehat{T_{m}(f)}(\xi)=m(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\widehat{T_{m(t)}(f)}(\xi)=m(t\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

where
\widehat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
.
For a set
E\subset(0,\infty)
, we define the following maximal operators whose dilation is taken in
E
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}(f)(x)\coloneqq\sup_{t\in E}|T_{m(t)}f(x)|

The purpose of this paper is to investigate conditions for
m
which guarantee
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
bounds of
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
.

For
E=\{1\}
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}=T_{m}
, hence the Hörmander-Mikhlin condition is a sufficient condition for the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
bounds of
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
.

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|m(2^{j}\cdot)\widehat{\psi}(\cdot)\|_{L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad s>d/2.

Note that
\widehat{\psi}(\xi/2^{j})
is a frequency cut-off function on
|\xi|\sim 2^{j}
.
When
E=(0,\infty)
, Christ-Grafakos-Honzík-Seeger
[
4
]
proved that the Hörmander-Mikhlin condition (
1.1
) is not enough for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
bounds of
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
.
Moreover, they presented the following sufficient conditions for the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
bounds (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.2 and (1.12)]
):

Theorem A
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
and
E=(0,\infty)
. Suppose
m
satisfies

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|m(2^{j}\cdot)\widehat{\psi}(\cdot)\|_{L_{s}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}<\infty,

whenever
q>1
,
r=\min\{p,2\}
and
s>\frac{d}{r}+\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In addition, if (
1.2
) holds for
q>1
,
r=2
, and
s>\frac{d}{2}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
is a bounded map from
L^{\infty}
to
BMO
.

One can understand the condition (
1.2
) is an
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
analogue of (
1.1
).
we recommend
[
4
,
6
]
for more refined summability condition than (
1.2
).

In
[
8
]
, the authors of the present paper improved the regularity condition in (
1.2
) by
s>d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|+\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{2}\}
, but Sobolev spaces
L_{s}^{r}
is replaced into Besov spaces
B_{p_{0}}^{s}
with
1/p_{0}=|1/2-1/p|
and
q
is fixed by
2
.

When it comes to the case of
E
whose dimension is between
0
and
1
,
to the authors’ knowledge, there are not many results on abstract Fourier multipliers.
If the Fourier multipliers are given by Fourier transforms of surface-carried measures, however, one can find remarkable results on
L^{p}
bounds of associated maximal averages.
One of the most interesting objects is the spherical maximal functions.

\mathcal{M}_{sph}(f)(x):=\sup_{0<t}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

It is widely known due to E. M. Stein
[
16
]
(
d\geq 3
) and J. Bourgain
[
1
]
(
d=2
) that
\mathcal{M}_{sph}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
p>\frac{d}{d-1}
.
If one takes
t
as a lacunary sequence, then
\mathcal{M}_{sph}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p>1
due to C. P. Calderón
[
3
]
.

If one considers
E
with dimension strictly between
0
and
1
, Seeger, Wainger, and Wright
[
15
]
showed that
\mathcal{M}_{sph}^{E}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
p>1+\frac{\kappa(E)}{d-1}
and unbounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
1<p<1+\frac{\kappa(E)}{d-1}
.
The quantity
\kappa(E)
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa(E):=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\log N(E_{j},\delta)}{-\log\delta}\,,

where
E_{j}:=\big(2^{-j}E\big)\cap[1,2]
, and
N(E,\delta)
is the
\delta
-entropy number of
E
,

\displaystyle N(E,\delta):=\#\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,E\cap[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]\neq\emptyset\big\}\,.

One of classical examples of
E
with
\kappa(E)<1
is
E=\{1+n^{-a}\mid n=1,2,\dots\}
,
a>0
, which yields
\kappa(E)=(1+a)^{-1}
.
Further examples are introduced in
[
12
]
.
We note that for a compact
E
,
\kappa(E)
equals the Minkowski dimension,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{M}}(E)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\big\{a>0:\text{$\exists\,\,C>0$ such that $\forall\,\,0<\delta\leq 1$, $N(E,\delta)\leq C\delta^{-a}$}\big\}\,.

Later, Duoandikoetxea and Vargas
[
5
]
extended studies of
[
15
]
to compactly supported Borel measures and Fourier multipliers.
Precisely, they proved the following theorems:

Theorem B
.

(1)

Let
m
be the Fourier transform of a compactly supported finite Borel measure and
E\subset(0,\infty)
.
Assume that

|m(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-a}\quad\text{for some $a>\kappa(E)/2$}.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p>1+\kappa(E)/2a
.

(2)

Let
s=[d/2]+1
,
m\in C^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
E\subset(0,\infty)
.
Assume that

|\partial^{\alpha}m(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-a}\quad\text{for all $|\alpha|\leq s+1$ and some $a>\kappa(E)/2$}.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{2d}{d+2a-\kappa(E)}<p<2\frac{d-\kappa(E)}{d-2a}.

In case of the spherical average as in
[
15
]
, the decay factor of Theorem
B
is
a=\frac{d-1}{2}
.
Thus one can check that
(1)
of Theorem
B
recovers the result of
[
15
]
.

We pursue further study on
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
in view of
(2)
of Theorem
B
.
Unlike the previous researches, we take a different approach based on relation between the Assouad and the Aikawa dimensions, which allows us to construct a refined square function to control
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
.
Then, following the approach in
[
8
]
, we make use of fractional calculus, bi-linear interpolation, and
\Sigma^{2}
spaces introduced in
[
8
]
; some of these materials will be presented in Section
2
.
These ingredients allow us to improve the regularity condition for
m
to
s>d|1/2-1/p|+\kappa(E)\min\{1/2,1/p\}
.
Moreover, we provide a general result involving Fourier multipliers of limited decay, singular Fourier multipliers, and multipliers with slow decay.
For the Fourier multipliers with slow decay, we mean

(1.4)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\gamma}m(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\beta-\delta|\gamma|}

for some
\beta>0
and
0<\delta<1
.
One can understand
m
satisfying (
1.4
) as a simple analogue of the Hörmander symbol class.

We now introduce our main result.
Take
\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\widehat{\phi}\equiv 1
on
B(0,1)
and
\widehat{\phi}\equiv 0
on
B(0,2)^{c}
.
Define

(1.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{j}:=\widehat{\psi}(\cdot/2^{j})=\widehat{\phi}(\cdot/2^{j}):-\widehat{\phi}(\cdot/2^{j-1})\quad\text{for}\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

For a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
continuously embedded in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we denote
\Sigma^{2}(\mathcal{B})
the set of all
m\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(\mathcal{B})}^{2}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|m(2^{j}\cdot)\widehat{\psi}(\cdot)\big\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}<\infty\,.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
E\subset(0,\infty)
satisfy
\kappa(E)<1
.
Suppose that
m
is of class
\Sigma^{2}(B_{p_{0}}^{s})
with
1/p_{0}=|1/2-1/p|
and

(1.7)

\displaystyle s>\frac{d}{p_{0}}+\frac{\kappa(E)}{2}\,.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{m}^{E}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 1
.

We note that the endpoint result of Theorem
A
holds for arbitrary
E\subset\mathbb{R}
(See
[
4
, Corollary 4.2 and (4.16)]
).
Thus by interpolating Theorem
1.1
(for
p=2
) and the result of Theorem
A
(for
p=\infty
), the condition (
1.7
) is replaced by

\displaystyle s>\frac{d}{p_{0}}+\kappa(E)\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p}\right\}\,.

We emphasize that the condition for multipliers
m
in Theorem
1.1
encompasses the limited decay condition, so our result with Remark
1
recovers
(2)
of Theorem
B
(see examples below).
On the other hand, one can see from
(1)
of Theorem
B
that the case of
m
given by the Fourier transform of a measure yields larger range of
p
for
L^{p}
bounds on
\mathcal{M}_{m}
.
This phenomenon is related to geometric aspects of the measure.
We refer the result of Roos and Seeger
[
12
]
, which concerns
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates (
L^{p}
improving) of the sphercial maximal functions with
E\subset[1,2]
.
They proved that the sharp
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
bounds is closely related to the Minkowski and the Assouad dimensions of
E
.
We end this section with an example which is not given by the Fourier transform of a compactly supported finite Borel measure.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of
\Sigma^{2}
spaces

We introduce some useful properties of
\Sigma^{2}
spaces given in (
1.6
).

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

For any
1<p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s})
is a Banach space, where
B_{p}^{s}:=B_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Besov space.

(2)

For any
n=0,\,1,\,\ldots
,

\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(L^{n}_{2})}^{2}:=\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{2}^{n})}^{2}\simeq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{k}m(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2k-d}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,.

(3)

For any
1<p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{r>0}\|m(r\,\cdot\,)\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B^{s}_{p})}\lesssim\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B^{s}_{p})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\xi\cdot\nabla m(\xi)\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s})}\lesssim\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s+1})}\,.

(4)

Let
p_{0},\,p_{1}\in(1,\infty]
,
s_{0},\,s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any
0<\theta<1
,

\big[\Sigma^{2}(B_{p_{0}}^{s_{0}}),\Sigma^{2}(B_{p_{1}}^{s_{1}})\big]_{\theta}=\Sigma^{2}\big(B_{p_{\theta}}^{s_{\theta}}\big)\,,

where
[X,Y]_{\theta}
is an complex interpolation space,
1/p_{\theta}:=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
s_{\theta}:=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
.

Proof.

(1) and (4) are provided in
[
8
, Subsection 1.2]
, and (2) is provided in
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
.

(3) To prove the first inequality, let
r=2^{j_{0}}r^{\prime}
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r^{\prime}\in[1,2)
.
Note that for any
r^{\prime}\in[1,2)
,

\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(r^{\prime}\,\cdot\,)\equiv 1\quad\text{on}\quad\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\psi})\,,

which implies that

\displaystyle\|m(r\,\cdot\,)\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s})}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|m(r2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|m(r^{\prime}2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\,\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(r^{\prime}\,\cdot\,)\,\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}^{2}\,.

From properties of
B^{s}_{p}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\big\|m(r^{\prime}2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\,\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(r^{\prime}\,\cdot\,)\,\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\big\|m(r^{\prime}2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}_{n}(r^{\prime}\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\big\|m(r^{\prime}2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}(r^{\prime}2^{-n}\,\cdot\,)\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\big\|m(2^{j+n}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}

(note that
1\leq r^{\prime}<2
).
Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sup_{r>0}\|m(r\,\cdot\,)\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s})}^{2}\,

\displaystyle=\sup_{r^{\prime}\in[1,2)}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|m(r^{\prime}2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\,\sum_{|n|\leq 2}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(r^{\prime}\,\cdot\,)\,\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}^{2}\,.

Next, we prove the second inequality.
For fixed
k=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\xi_{k}D_{k}m\big)(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}=\,

\displaystyle\big\|D_{k}\big(m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\big)\cdot\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\big\|D_{k}\big(m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\,\big)\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}+\big\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)D_{k}\big(\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\,\big)\big\|_{B_{p}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}+\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)D_{k}\big(\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\,\big)\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}\,.

Since
\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}(\xi)
is smooth and
\sum_{|n|\leq 1}\widehat{\psi}_{n}\equiv 1
on
\mathrm{supp}\,\widehat{\psi}
, we obtain that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}+\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)D_{k}\big(\xi_{k}\widehat{\psi}\big)\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}\\
\lesssim\,&\sum_{|n|\leq 1}\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}_{n}\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}\simeq\sum_{|n|\leq 1}\|m(2^{j+n}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}\,.\end{split}

(
2.1
) and (
2.2
) imply

\displaystyle\|\xi_{k}D_{k}m(\xi)\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\big(\xi_{k}D_{k}m\big)(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\|_{B_{p}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|m(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)\widehat{\psi}\|_{B_{p}^{s+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|m\|_{\Sigma^{2}(B_{p}^{s+1})}^{2}\,.

Therefore the proof is completed.

2.2.
Fractional calculus

We define the Riemann-Liouville integrals and fractional derivatives of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
For
t>0
,

\displaystyle I_{0+}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s)ds\quad,\quad D_{0+}^{\alpha}F(t):=\frac{d}{dt}\big(I_{0+}^{1-\alpha}F\big)(t)

where
f
is a locally integrable function on
[0,\infty)
and
F
satisfies
I_{0+}^{1-\alpha}F
is absolutely continuous.
For further use of
I_{0+}^{\alpha}
and
D_{0+}^{\alpha}
, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.1,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
F\in C_{loc}\big([0,\infty)\big)\cap C^{0,\beta}_{loc}\big((0,\infty)\big)
for a fixed
\beta\in(0,1]
.
Then for any
\alpha\in(0,\beta)
,
D^{\alpha}_{0+}F
satisfies the following identity:

(2.3)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{0+}F(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{F(t)}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{F(t)-F(s)}{(t-s)^{1+\alpha}}ds\right)\,.

Moreover, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle F(t)=I_{0+}^{\alpha}D_{0+}^{\alpha}F(t).

It should be noted that
\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}F(0)
is added in the right-hand side of (
2.4
) in Lemma 2.1 of
[
8
]
, which is an error and should be replaced by Lemma
2.2
.
We also note that arguments in
[
8
]
are valid regardless of the error.
For further discussion on fractional calculus, we refer to
[
7
,
14
]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The first lemma provides a refined square function argument for maximal operators on fractal sets in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a set such that
\{2^{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset E
,
\alpha
and
\beta
be positive constants such that
0<\beta<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and
F\in C_{loc}\big([0,\infty)\big)\cap C^{0,\alpha+\varepsilon}_{loc}\big((0,\infty)\big)
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in E}|F(t)|^{2}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}d(s,\widetilde{E}_{j})^{-1+2\beta}|D^{\alpha}_{0+}F_{j}(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

where
F_{j}(s):=F(2^{j}s)
and
\widetilde{E}_{j}:=\big(2^{-j}E\big)\cap[1,2]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that for any
t\in E
,

\displaystyle|F(t)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1+\alpha}|D^{\alpha}_{0+}F(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1+2\beta}s^{2(\alpha-\beta)}|D^{\alpha}_{0+}F(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d(s,E)^{-1+2\beta}s^{2(\alpha-\beta)}|D^{\alpha}_{0+}F(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}d(s,E_{j})^{-1+2\beta}s^{2(\alpha-\beta)}\big|2^{j\alpha}\big(D^{\alpha}_{0+}F\big)(2^{j}s)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

Here, (
3.1
) is implied by that because
0<2(\alpha-\beta)<1
,

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1+2(\alpha-\beta)}s^{-2(\alpha-\beta)}\,\mathrm{d}s=N(\alpha,\beta)<\infty\,,

and (
3.2
) follows from that
\{2^{j}\,:\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset E
.
One can observe that

The second lemma provides an equivalent definition of the Minkowski dimension of compact sets.
This lemma is motivated by the relation between the Assuad dimension and the Aikawa dimension, introduced in

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\delta\leq 1}\delta^{a}N(E,\delta)\lesssim_{a}\int_{1}^{2}d(t,E)^{-1+a}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim_{a}1+\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{a}N(E,\lambda)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\lambda}\,.

In particular, the Minkowski dimension of

equals

\displaystyle\inf\Big\{b>0\,:\,\int_{1}^{2}d(t,E)^{-1+b}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\Big\}\,.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}d(t,E)^{-1+a}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(1-a)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2+a}\big|\{t\in[1,2]\,:\,d(t,E)<\lambda\}\big|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,.

For a fixed
\delta\in(0,1]

One can observe that if

on

This implies that

\displaystyle\delta N(E,\delta)=\Big|\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\delta}}I\Big|

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,

\displaystyle\big|\{t>0\,:\,d(t,E)\leq\delta\}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\,\,

\displaystyle(1-a)\delta^{1-a}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2+a}\big|\{t\in[1,2]\,:\,d(t,E)<\lambda\}\big|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq\,\,

\displaystyle 3(1-a)\delta^{1-a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2+a}\big|\{t\in[1,2]\,:\,d(t,E)<\lambda\}\big|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,.

Here, the last inequality follows from that because

we obtain that for any

\delta\leq(1-a)\delta^{1-a}\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2+a}|\{t\in[1,2]\,:\,d(t,E)<\lambda\}|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,.

Combining (
3

3.5

3.3

To prove the second inequality in (
3.3

where

Due to the definition of

By combining this with (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2+a}|\{t\in[1,2]\,:\,d(t,E)<\lambda\}|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{1}{1-a}+3\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{-1+a}N(E,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,.

Therefore the proof of (

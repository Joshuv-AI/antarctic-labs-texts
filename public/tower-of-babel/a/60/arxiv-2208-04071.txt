A Brightwell-Winkler type characterisation of NU {\rm NU} graphs Thanks: The author was supported by Korean NRF Basic Science Research Program (NRF-2022R1A2C1091566) funded by the Korean government (MEST) and the Kyungpook National University Research Fund.

Abstract
. In 2000, Brightwell and Winkler characterised dismantlable graphs as the graphs H H for which the Hom-graph Hom ⁡ ( G , H ) {\rm Hom}(G,H) , defined on the set of homomorphisms from G G to H H , is connected for all graphs G G . This shows that the reconfiguration version Recon Hom ​ ( H ) {\rm Recon_{Hom}}(H) of the H H -colouring problem, in which one must decide for a given G G whether Hom ⁡ ( G , H ) {\rm Hom}(G,H) is connected, is trivial if and only if H H is dismantlable. We prove a similar starting point for the reconfiguration version of the H H -extension problem. Where Hom ⁡ ( G , H , p ) {\rm Hom}(G,H;p) is the subgraph of the Hom-graph Hom ⁡ ( G , H ) {\rm Hom}(G,H) induced by the H H -colourings extending the H H -precolouring p p of G G , the reconfiguration version Recon Ext ​ ( H ) {\rm Recon_{Ext}(H)} of the H H -extension problem asks, for a given H H -precolouring p p of a graph G G , if Hom ⁡ ( G , H , p ) {\rm Hom}(G,H;p) is connected. We show that the graphs H H for which Hom ⁡ ( G , H , p ) {\rm Hom}(G,H;p) is connected for every choice of ( G , p ) (G,p) are exactly the NU {\rm NU} graphs. This gives a new characterisation of NU {\rm NU} graphs, a nice class of graphs that is important in the algebraic approach to the CSP {\rm CSP} -dichotomy. We further give bounds on the diameter of Hom ⁡ ( G , H , p ) {\rm Hom}(G,H;p) for NU {\rm NU} graphs H H , and show that shortest path between two vertices of Hom ⁡ ( G , H , p ) {\rm Hom}(G,H;p) can be found in parameterised polynomial time. We apply our results to the problem of shortest path reconfiguration, significantly extending recent results.

Introduction

In this paper, graphs may have loops but no multiple edges. Graphs without loops are
irreflexive
and graphs in which every vertex has a loop are
reflexive
. A path in a graph
H
between vertices
u
and
v
is called a
uv
-path.

Recall that a homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
from a graph
G
to a graph
H
, is a vertex map
\phi:V(G)\to V(H)
that preserves edges:

u\sim v\Rightarrow\phi(u)\sim\phi(v).

The
\Hom
-graph
\Hom(G,H)
is the graph on the set of
H
-colourings of
G
in which two such colourings
\phi
and
\psi
are adjacent if they satisfy the following for all pairs of vertices
u
and
v
of
G
:

u\sim v\Rightarrow\phi(u)\sim\phi^{\prime}(v).

While the
H
-colouring problem
\Hom(H)
asks for an instance graph
G
if the graph
\Hom(G,H)
is non-empty, the mixing version
\Mix_{\Hom}(H)
, called
H
-mixing, asks for an instance
G
if
\Hom(G,H)
is connected, and the reconfiguration version
\RH(H)
, known as
H
-recolouring, asks for an instance
(G,\phi,\psi)
if there is a path from
\phi
to
\psi
in
\Hom(G,H)
.

We say that a problem is tractable, or in
\Poly
, if it can be solved in polynomial time, and say that is in
\PSC
if it is
\PSPACE
-complete.

There have been several papers in the last 10-15 years determining the computational complexity of
\RH(H)
and
\Mix_{\Hom}(H)
for various graphs
H
. Many of these papers support the assertion that the complexity of the problems is closely related to topological properties of the graph
H
, or rather, of various simplicial complexes derived from
H
. For example, it is shown in
[
15
]
that
\RH(H)
is tractable for an irreflexive graph
H
if its neighbourhood complex is purely
1
-dimensional. It is shown in
[
14
]
that
\RH(H)
is in
\PSC
for reflexive
H
if it clique complex is a
2
-sphere. It is shown in
[
11
]
that
\Mix_{\Hom}(H)
is coNP-complete for reflexive
H
if its clique complex purely
1
-dimension and has non-trivial first homology.

All of these results are preceded by a result of Brightwell and Winkler from
[
5
]
. We define a graph
H
to be
\Hom
-trivial
if
\Hom(G,H)
is connected for all
G
. We use the word ‘trivial’ as in this case all instances of
\RH(H)
and
\Mix_{\Hom}(H)
are YES-instances, so both problems are trivial.
Dismantlable graphs are topologically trivial in that their clique complex deformation retracts to a point. In
[
5
]
, a paper well known for its characterisation of cop-win graphs as dismantlable graphs, Brightwell and Winkler gave several characterisations of dismantlable graphs. The following is the characterisation we are most interested in.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

A graph is dismantlable if and only if it is
\Hom
-trivial.

An
H
-precolouring

of

is a map
p:S\to V(H)
for some subset
S\subset V(G)
of its vertices. A homomorphism in
\bHom(G,H)

extends

p
if it restricts to
p
on
S
. We usually make the support
S
of

implicit, and write
|p|
for
|S|
.

Definition 1.2
.

For an
H
-precolouring
p
of
G
, let
\bHom(G,H;p)
be the subgraph of
\bHom(G,H)
induced by the vertices that extend

.

The well known homomorphism extension problem
\Ext(H)
ask for an instance
(G,p)
, where
p
is an
H
-precolouring of
G
, if
\bHom(G,H;p)
has a vertex. We make the following definitions.

Definition 1.3
.

The
H
-extension reconfiguration problem

\RE(H)
asks for an instance
(G,p,\phi,\psi)
with
\phi
and
\psi
in
\bHom(G,H;p)
if
\phi
and
\psi
are in the same component of
\bHom(G,H;p)
.
A graph

is
\Ext
-trivial
if
\bHom(G,H;p)
is connected for every pair
(G,p)
where
p
is an
H
-precolouring of a graph
G
.

Near-unanimity graphs, or
\NU
graphs, are an important class of graphs in the algebraic theory of
\CSP
, or constraint satisfaction problems. We will define them in the next section and discuss many of their nice properties.

In the setting of reflexive graphs,

graphs are a significant subclass of dismantlable graphs, and along the lines of
[

]
, Larose, Loten, and Zádori gave several characterisations of them in
[

]
. From these characterisations
the following, which gives an analogue of Theorem

for

Corollary 1.4

(
[

]
)
.

For a reflexive graph

the following are equivalent.

(1)

H
is an
\NU
graph.

(2)

H^{2}
dismantles to its diagonal.

(3)

H
is
\Ext
-trivial.

The definitions required for item

are also put off until the next section. We show now without definitions how Corollary
1.4
is indeed immediate from
[
12
]
by standard arguments. The equivalence of
(1)
and

is explicit in
[
12
]
as Theorem 1.1. As the more general Lemmas
3.4
and

give an alternate proof of the equivalence of

with the above two characterisations, we only sketch now how this follows from Theorem 3.1 of
[
12
]
. In Theorem 3.1 the authors show that if

is
\NU
, then all its

-subalgebras are connected. As
\Hom(G,H;p)
is a
|V(G)|
-subalgebra of

, this shows that

is
\Ext
-trivial. On the other hand, if

is
\Ext
-trivial, then in particular

is the identity on the diagonal copy of

in

, is connected. From this it is immediate that there is a path of idempotents between the projections in

-graphs from Theorem 3.1 of
[

.

Our first goal is to generalise Corollary
1.4
from reflexive graphs to all graphs. There are a couple of relevant papers that we should mention. In
[
13
]
the authors generalised
[
12
]
to more general relational structures. Their focus was characterisations of

structures, rather than
\NU

[
4
]
, the authors defined dismantlability for more general structures, and extended
[
5
]
to this definition. They related a structure being dismantlable to the triviality of an extension/mixing type problem. The problem is similar to our problem
\Mix_{\Ext}(H)
, but is not the same.

To extend Corollary
1.4
to all graphs, we need to alter several of our definitions for bipartite graphs. Indeed there are bipartite
\NU
graphs, but there cannot be bipartite
\Ext
-trivial graphs, as for bipartite

,
\Hom(G,H)
always has an even number of components.

Calling one partite set of a bipartite graph the

be the subgraph of
\Hom(G,H)
induced by those vertices that map black vertices of

to black vertices of

, and let
\Hom_{B}(G,H;p)=\Hom(G,H;p)\cap\Hom_{B}(G,H)
.

A bipartite graph

is

•

bipartite dismantlable
if it dismantles to an edge,

•

bipartite
\Hom
-trivial
if
\Hom_{B}(G,H)
is connected for all

, and

•

bipartite

-trivial
if
\Hom_{B}(G,H;p)
is connected for all
(G,p)
.

From
[
5
]
we get a bipartite version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

A graph is bipartite dismantlable if and only if it is bipartite
\Hom
-trivial.

In Section
3
we prove the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

A graph is an
\NU
graph if and only if it is
\Ext
-trivial or bipartite
\Ext
-trivial.

We make explicit mention here that a bipartite graph is by definition irreflexive, and that when we are talking of
\NU
graphs, ‘irreflexive’ and ‘bipartite’ coincide.

Our motivation for making Corollary
1.4
explicit, and extending it to Theorem
1.6
, is that we will apply it to the problem of Shortest Path Reconfiguration.
The Shortest Path Reconfiguration problem
\SPR
asks, for an instance
(G,u,v,P_{\phi},P_{\psi})
, where

and

are vertices of the graph

P_{\phi}
and

are shortest

-paths in

to

-paths in

In
[
3
]
it was shown that
\SPR
is in
\PSC
, and in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
it is shown the problem drops to

is restricted to various classes of graphs. In particular, where again a graph

is
\SP
-trivial
if every instance

of

is a YES-instance, it is shown in
[

]
that chordal graphs are

-trivial, and in
[
9
]
that grids and bridged graphs are
\SP
-trivial.

To relate this back to
\RE(G)
, we note that for
\RH(H)
, one often replaces the graph
\Hom(G,H)
with the subgraph

on the same vertices but having only those edges of
\Hom(G,H)
on which the endpoint maps differ on a single vertex. It is well known and easy to show that when

if and only if there is a path between them in

and

have the same components and can both be used as the reconfiguration graph for

\Hom(G,H;p)
and of the subgraph

With this, one sees that an instance
(G,u,v,P_{\phi},P_{\psi})
of

is just an instance
(P,p,\phi,\psi)
of
\RE(G)
where

is a path of length
d(u,v)
,

is the

-precolouring that fixes its endpoints to

and

, and

and

are maps of

onto

and

respectively. The

is
\Col(P,G;p)
.

So we get from Theorem
1.6
that

is
\SP
-trivial if it is an
\NU
graph. Indeed, as the existence of loops on

has no effect on
\SPR
, we get that the following.

Corollary 1.7
.

If
G
is an
\NU
graph then
G
, with loops removed, is
\SP
-trivial.

Chordal graphs, and most bridged graphs, are reflexive
\NU
graphs; grids are irreflexive

graphs. So while our result does not recover all the results of these two papers about

-trivial graphs.

In
[

it was shown that components of the reconfiguration graph

]

, when a graph

. This is done in Corollary

The bound on the diameter of

gives a rough bound on the diameter of

In Section

4.
Bounds on recolouring length

As mentioned in the introduction, the reconfiguration graph for

be a connected bipartite graph with a 3-
\NU
polymorphism, and let
e=vv^{\prime}
be a fixed edge of
H
. Let
u
be a vertex of
H
with maximum distance from
e
, without loss of generality assume it is closer to
v
than to

. There is a dismantling retraction of
u
to some vertex
u^{\prime}
that is closer to
v^{\prime}
than
u
is to
v
.

Proof of claim.

Let
d
be the distance from
u
to
v
and let
U_{d}
be the set of neighbours of
u
. By choice of

and the fact that
H
is bipartite, we get that every vertex of
U_{d}
has a walk of length
d
to
v^{\prime}
.

If we can show that the vertices in
U_{d}
have a common neighbour
u^{\prime}
at distance
d-1
from
v^{\prime}
, then

can dismantle to this, and we are done.

A subset
U
of
U_{d}
is ‘good’ if its vertices share a neighbour at distance
d-1
from
v^{\prime}
. We show that all subsets
U
of
U_{d}
are good. Towards contradiction, let
U
be an inclusion minimal subset of
U_{d}
that is not good. It must contain at least two vertices, so
k=|U|+1
is at least
3
.

Let
T
be the tree with
k
branches, the first having length
d-1
and the others having length
1
. Let
p
map the leaf of the long branch to
v^{\prime}
and biject the other leaves to
U
. As
H
is a 3-
\NU
graph, it has no critical trees with
3
or more leaves by Corollary
3.2
, so in particular,
(T,p)
is not a critical tree.

As any subset of
U
is good,
T\setminus\{e\}
admits a
p
-extension to
H
for any of the short branches
e
of
T
.
As the elements of
U
have the common neighbour
u
,
T\setminus\{e\}
admits a
p
-extension to
H
for any edge
e
in the long branch of
T
. As
(T,p)
is not critical, it must therefore admit a
p
-extending homomorphism
\phi
to
H
. The target of the degree
k
vertices of

under this homomorphism is a neighbour of the vertices of
U
, and has distance

to

. So

is good, which is our contradiction.
∎

Having completed the proof for bipartite
H
, the proof for general graphs follows by Lemma

by observing that if a dismantling step of

is efficient, then the symmetric shadow dismantling step of

is efficient. Indeed, the distance of a vertex

from

in

is exactly the distance of a vertex
(i,u)
from
(0,v)(1,v)
in
K_{2}\times H
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

For any connected 3-
\NU
graph
H
there is an efficient dismantling of
C_{\Delta}(H^{2})
to
\Delta(H^{2})
. Such a dismantling can be found in polynomial time.

Proof.

Again we prove the statement for bipartite graphs; the general statement follows, as in Lemma
4.5
, by taking a symmetric shadow dismantling.

Let
H
be a connected bipartite 3-
\NU
graph. It is enough to show that for any retract
R=r(H)
of
C_{\Delta}(H^{2})
containing
\Delta=\Delta(H^{2})
there is a vertex
u
of
R
that dismantles to some vertex closer to
\Delta
.

As any vertex
(u_{1},u_{2})
of
R
is in
C_{\Delta}(H^{2})
the vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are distance
2d
apart for some
d
, and the closest vertices to them in
\Delta
are vertices
(v,v)
where
v
is the middle vertex of a path
P
of length
2d
from
u_{1}
to
u_{2}
. In particular
d(v,u_{1})=d=d(v,u_{2})
.

Let
(u_{1},u_{2})
be a vertex of
R
that maximises this distance

, and let
P
and
v
be as above. The subgraph
H^{\prime}
of

of consisting of vertices of distance at most
d
from

is a retract of
H
(this follows by Claim

as one can retract any vertex at distance more than

from

) so is 3-
\NU
by Fact

.
R
is a retract of
(H^{\prime})^{2}
, so is also 3-
\NU
.

Where
u^{\prime}_{2}
is the neighbor of
u_{2}
on the path

,
v_{1}
is a vertex in

having maximum distance from the edge
u^{\prime}_{2}u_{2}
, and so by Lemma

4.5
dismantles to a vertex

closer to

.

It follows that
(u_{1},u_{2})
dismantles to
(u^{\prime},u_{2})
in
(H^{\prime})^{2}
and so
to
r((u^{\prime},u_{2}))
in

. As

is closer to the edge

than

is closer to the diagonal than

,
as needed.

To find an efficient dismantling, Claim

tells us we can simply order the vertices by their distance from the diagonal, and then processing the vertices farthest from the diagonal first, find a dominating neighbour closer to the diagonal and retract to it. This can be done in time

.

∎

We wonder if Theorem

For a vertex

5.
Shortest Path Reconfiguration

The shortest path reconfiguration graph
\SP(H,u,v)
is the graph on the set of shortest
uv
-paths in
H
where two are adjacent if they differ on a single vertex. The shortest path reconfiguration problem
\SPR
asks for an instance
(H,u,v,P_{\phi},P_{\psi})
if there is a path between
P_{\phi}
and
P_{\psi}
in
\SP(H,u,v)
.

We observed in the introduction that
\SP(H,u,v)=\Col(P_{d},H;p)
where
d
is the distance between
u
and
v
, so
H
is
\SP
-trivial if we get it from an
\Ext
-trivial graph by removing loops,
thus Theorem
1.6
this gives Corollary
1.7
: any
\NU
graph
H
, with loops removed, is
\SP
-trivial.

This is far from a characterisation of
\SP
-trivial graphs, as odd cycles are
\SP
-trivial while for an odd cycle of length at least
5
, no addition of loops can make it
into an
\NU
graph. A full characterisation of the
\SP
-trivial graphs would be interesting. It would have to deal with the following example which produces many
\SP
-trivial graphs that are not
\NU
graphs for whatever loops are added.

Example 5.1
.

If
H
is bipartite and
\SP
-trivial, and we add one edge
e
making an odd cycle, then it remains
\SP
-trivial. For any two vertices
u
and
v
, all shortest paths either use
e
or all shortest paths do not use
e
. If they do not, then they reconfigure, if they do, then we can reconfigure the paths from
u
to
e
and then reconfigure the paths from
e
to
v
.

We compare Corollary
1.7
with related results from
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.

•

In
[
3
]
it was shown that chordal graphs are
\SP
-trivial. Any chordal graph, with loops added to every vertex, is a k-
\NU
graph. So we recover this result.

•

In
[
9
]
it was shown that a hypercube is
\SP
-trivial.
(It was shown that
\SPR
can be solved in polynomial time, but the algorithm shows the graph is
\SP
-trivial.) A hypercube is a 3-
\NU
graph, so we recover this result.

•

In
[
9
]
it was shown, more generally, that any graph with a so-called triangle property is
\SP
-trivial. The triangle property is equivalent to the omission of certain critical tree obstructions with three leaves. This class of graphs is incomparable with the class of
\NU
graphs, but contains the class of 3-
\NU
graphs, so their proof also shows that 3-
\NU
graphs are
\SP
-trivial.

All results mentioned from
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
come with algorithms, polynomial in
|V(H)|
, for finding the shortest path in
\SP(H,u,v)
between two vertices. Though we find the shortest path between them in
\Hom(P_{d},H;p)
in polynomial time if
H
is
\NU
this does not find a shortest path between them in
\Col(P_{d},H;p)
. Doing this would be interesting.

5.1.
Diameter of the Shortest Path Reconfiguration graph

A
d
-instance
of
\SPR
is an instance
(H,u,v,\phi,\psi)
for which
d_{H}(u,v)=d
. It was shown in

[
3
]
that the
\SP
-reconfiguration graph
\SP(H,u,v)
has diameter
d-1
for chordal graphs
H
and
d
-instances. In
[
9
]
the diameter was shown to be at most
\binom{d-1}{2}
for
d
instances of hypercubes
H
.

From Proposition
3.7
we get the following.

Corollary 5.2
.

For k-
\NU
graphs
H
, the
\SP
-reconfiguration graph
\SP(H,u,v)
has diameter at most
(k-2)\binom{d+1}{2}
for any
d
-instance
(H,u,v,\phi,\psi)
of
\SPR
.

Proof.

Recall that if there is a path of length
\ell
from
\phi
to
\psi
in
\bHom(P_{d},H)
then Proposition
3.7
gives a path of length
(k-2)\ell
between them in
\bHom(P_{d},H;p)
. The proof was simple, is it easily seen to work to show that if there is a path of length
\ell
in
\bCol(P_{d},H)
, then there is one of length
(k-2)\ell
in
\bCol(P_{d},H;p)=\SP(H,u,v)
. So it is enough to show that there is a path of length
\binom{d+1}{2}
between
\phi(P_{d})
and
\psi(P_{d})
in
\bCol(P_{d},H)
.

It is a simple exercise to show that one can recolour a irreflexive path
\phi(P_{d})
on
d+1
to vertices to its initial edge by a recolouring sequence of length
{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}{\lceil d/2\rceil}
.
From here, we can move the
{\lceil d/2\rceil}
vertices at the second vertex of
\phi(P_{d})
to the second vertex of
\psi(P_{d})
in
{\lceil d/2\rceil}
recolouring steps, and then recolour back to
\psi(P_{d})
in another
{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}{\lceil d/2\rceil}
steps. This gives a path between
\phi
and
\psi
in
\bCol(P_{d},H)
of length

2{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}{\lceil d/2\rceil}+{\lceil d/2\rceil}=\textstyle\binom{d+1}{2},

as needed.
∎

In the case that
H
is a 3-
\NU
graph, we improve this using Corollary
4.10
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For any 3-
\NU
graph
H
and any
d
-instance
(H,u,v,\phi,\psi)
of
\SPR
there path between
\phi
and
\psi
in
\SP(H,u,v)
of length at most
d^{2}/2-1
.

Proof.

In a path
P_{d}=v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{d}
of length
d
, the distance between
\phi(v_{i})
and
\psi(v_{i})
is at most
2\cdot\min(i,d-i)
.
Computing

|\psi-\phi|\leq 2{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}{\lceil d/2\rceil}\leq d^{2}/2

the result follows by Corollary
4.10
by observing that all of these distances are even.
∎

We note that our bound of
d^{2}/2
, loses slightly in the irreflexive case to the tight bound of
\binom{d-1}{2}
given (for hypercubes) in
[
9
]
. But it applies to a larger class of graphs.

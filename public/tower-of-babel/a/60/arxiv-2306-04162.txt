Almost sharp global wellposedness and scattering for the defocusing conformal wave equation on the hyperbolic space
Chutian Ma

Abstract
. In this paper we prove a global well-posedness and scattering result for the defocusing conformal nonlinear wave equation in the hyperbolic space ℍ d , d ≥ 3 \mathbb{H}^{d},d\geq 3 . We take advantage of the hyperbolic geometry which yields stronger Morawetz and Strichartz estimates. We show that the solution is globally wellposed and scatters if the initial data is radially symmetric and lies in H 1 2 + ϵ ​ ( ℍ d ) × H − 1 2 + ϵ ​ ( ℍ d ) H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{H}^{d}) , ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the low regularity global well-posedness problem for the defocusing nonlinear wave equation on the hyperbolic space defined below:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}u+\mu|u|^{p-1}u=0\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}\\
&u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

The term
\mu|u|^{p-1}u
is known as non-linearity of power type. The equations are called defocusing when
\mu=1
, and focusing when
\mu=-1
.

In this paper, we consider strong
H^{s}
solutions as defined in
[
28
]
:

Definition 1.1

(Strong
H^{s}
solutions)
.

Given initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
where
H^{s},H^{s-1}
are the usual Sobolev spaces on the hyperbolic space, we say that u is a strong
H^{s}
solution to the wave equation (
1.1
) on time interval I if
u\in C^{0}_{t,loc}H^{s}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{H}^{d})
,
u_{t}\in C^{0}_{t,loc}H^{s-1}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{H}^{d})
solves (
1.1
) as distribution, i.e.

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(t)=cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}})u_{0}+\frac{sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}}u_{1}-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\frac{sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}}}|u|^{p-1}(s)u(s)ds

In addition to the relative weak concept of strong solution, well-posedness is usually defined and expected given ”good” initial data. In short, a well-posed solution depends continuously on the initial values. To make it precise:

Definition 1.2

(Well-posedness)
.

We say the initial value problem is locally well-posed in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
if for any
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
there exist
T>0
and a neighborhood U of
(u_{0},u_{1})
in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
, such that for any
(u_{0}^{\prime},u_{1}^{\prime})\in U
, there exists a unique solution u in
C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{s}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{d})\times C^{0}_{t}H_{x}^{s-1}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{d})
. Furthermore, the map
u_{0}^{\prime},u_{1}^{\prime}\rightarrow u
is continuous in U. If
T>0
can be chosen arbitrarily large (we do not require T to be
+\infty
), then we say the initial value problem is globally well-posed.

A natural question is how the solution evolves, including whether the solution is locally and globally well-posed, and its asymptotic behavior. For initial data with sufficient regularity and decay, solutions of nonlinear equations are known to converge to a linear solution as
t\rightarrow\infty
. This phenomenon is called scattering:

Definition 1.3
.

(Scattering). Suppose u solves (
1.1
) globally in time. We say that u scatters in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
if there exists
(u_{0}^{+},u_{1}^{+})
and
(u_{0}^{-},u_{1}^{-})
in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
such that

\displaystyle(u,u_{t})-S(t)(u_{0}^{+},u_{1}^{+})\rightarrow 0\ in\ H^{s}\times H^{s-1}\ as\ t\rightarrow+\infty

\displaystyle(u,u_{t})-S(t)(u_{0}^{-},u_{1}^{-})\rightarrow 0\ in\ H^{s}\times H^{s-1}\ as\ t\rightarrow-\infty

where S(t) is the propagator of the free wave equation.

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, we study the defocusing equations (
1.1
) on hyperbolic spaces with radially symmetric initial data. Hyperbolic geometry is known to yield stronger dispersion while the radially symmetric solutions tend to spread out and are less likely to concentrate. As a result, we prove the following global well-posedness and scattering result for the conformal wave equation (
1.1
). Assume the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle(u_{0},u_{1})\ is\ radially\ symmetric,

\displaystyle(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},\hskip 20.00003pt\epsilon>0

then

Theorem 1.4

(Global wellposedness and scattering for (
1.1
))
.

When
d\geq 3
, the solution to the defocusing conformal wave equation
(\ref{eq:nlw})
, i.e.
\mu=1
and
p=1+\frac{4}{d-1}
is globally well-posed and scatters under the assumption
(\ref{IVP})
. Moreover, there exists function
f_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

(1.4)

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}_{t,x}}\leq f_{\epsilon}(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}})

In other word, the scattering norm can be controlled by the (non-critical) norm, not the profile.

Remark 1.5
.

This result is almost sharp, in the sense that the regularity restriction in Theorem
1.4
can be brought close to the restriction predicted by the scaling symmetry (see (
1.9
)) by letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Remark 1.6
.

The proof for scattering is simplified on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
compared to the Euclidean proof, e.g. in
[
10
]
. This is due to a stronger Morawetz inequality provided by hyperbolic geometry.

1.2.
Review of Backgrounds

For wave equation (
1.1
), having
p\geq 2
is not enough to ensure global scatter solution, or even local solutions.
Let us review the Euclidean version of (
1.1
):

(1.5)

u_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u+\mu|u|^{p-1}u=0

The equation (
1.5
) is invariant under the following scaling symmetry. Suppose u solves (
1.1
) on the interval
[0,T]
. Denote

(1.6)

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda t,\lambda x)

Then
u_{\lambda}
solves (
1.1
) on
[0,\frac{T}{\lambda}]
. Denote

(1.7)

s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}

. We have

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s_{c}}}=\|u\|_{H^{s_{c}}}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s_{c}-1}}=\|\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s_{c}-1}}

The problem is called
s_{c}
critical as the
H^{s_{c}}\times H^{s_{c}-1}
norm is invariant under the scaling transformation. The case where
s>s_{c}
is called sub-critical, and
s<s_{c}
is called super-critical.

Scaling symmetry implies the following restriction on p and s must be satisfied in order for well-posedness to hold:

(1.9)

s\geq s_{c}

The heuristic is that in sub-critical cases, by choosing a small
\lambda
, one may trade longer existence in time at the cost of smaller
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
norm of the initial data. Or vice versa, a larger
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
tends to allow shorter time of existence. In super-critical case, scaling symmetry suggests that evolving small data in short time is as hard as evolving large data in long time. In particular, when
s<s_{c}
, suppose
(u,u_{t})\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
solves (
1.1
) with maximum interval of existence
[0,T]
. Due to finite speed of propagation, we may assume the data to have compact support. Then scaling symmetry allows us to generate solutions with arbitrary small
H^{s}
norm, support and interval of existence. Properly translating and summing yields initial data in
H^{s}
which fails to be extended in time at all. The authors of
[
8
]
constructed ill-posed data by using a small dispersion analysis paired with scaling. They showed for defocusing wave and Schrödinger equations, when
s<s_{c}
, there exists solution with arbitrarily small initial data in
H^{s}
norm which grows rapidly in arbitrarily small interval of time, which are counterexamples to well-posedness below critical regularity.

Above the critical regularity, it is conjectured that (
1.9
) guarantees global existence in the super-conformal range:
s_{c}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Assume
d\geq 3
and
s\geq s_{c}
. Then the initial value problem is conjectured to be globally well-posed in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
.

Remark 1.8
.

For focusing wave equations, there is an additional obstacle to well-posedness which is concentration along one light ray. See
[
21
]
for construction of counterexamples. This imposes another restriction on p, s that

(1.10)

s\geq s_{conf}=\frac{d+1}{4}-\frac{1}{p-1}

. This restriction only becomes stricter when
p<1+\frac{4}{d-1}
, or in other words
s_{c}<\frac{1}{2}
. The case where
s_{c}=s_{conf}=\frac{1}{2}
is called
conformal
as the equation is invariant under conformal transformations.

When
s\geq s_{c}
, one starts with local well-posedness. In general, local well-posedness results are relatively easy to obtain. They usually follows from Strichartz estimates and standard contraction mapping argument. Extending the solution globally is harder. A useful tool to is the conservation law. Wave equation (
1.1
) has conserved energy, defined by

(1.11)

E(t)=\int\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{t}^{2}+\mu\frac{1}{p+1}u^{p+1}

, assuming u has the required regularity and decay for (
1.11
) to make sense. In energy subcritical case, local well-posedness results ensure the solution can be extended over a small time interval whose length depends on the
H^{1}\times L^{2}
norm of the solution. For defocusing equations, i.e.
\mu=1
, the
H^{1}\times L^{2}
norm of the solution is controlled by the energy. This immediately proves global well-posedness.

Below energy state, i.e. when the initial data is in
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
for some
s<1
, the major difficulty to proving Theorem
1.4
is the lack of a conserved quantity which controls the
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
norm. Thus local well-posedness alone is not sufficient to extend the solution globally.

1.3.
Review of past results

In Euclidean spaces, such rough data results are well established for (
1.1
) as well as a wide range of dispersive equations. Bourgain in
[
6
]
studied cubic defocusing NLS below energy norm using the Fourier truncation method, proving global well-posedness for
s>\frac{11}{13}
. In short, Fourier truncation method splits the initial data into a high frequency part and a low frequency part and evolves them under respective equations. The high frequency evolution is rough but has small size, and will be treated as a perturbation. The low frequency evolution is large but has finite initial energy, which will be treated as a smoothed-up approximating solution. Since the high frequency evolution is small, its interaction with the low frequency evolution in the nonlinear terms is expected to be small, which causes the low frequency energy to be ”almost conserved”.

Various later works have employed similar methods in studying rough data IVP for wave equations.
[
18
]
proved global well-posedness for cubic defocusing NLW in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
\frac{3}{4}<s<1
. See also
[

]
,

]
,
[

]
,

]

[

Despite the fact that there is no scaling in hyperbolic spaces, the conjecture is still believed to be true on hyperbolic spaces. In fact, it has long been known that hyperbolic geometry strengthens the dispersion. Heuristically, this is because the volume on hyperbolic space grows exponentially. This fact provides larger room for the solution to disperse into.

For the Schrödinger equation, Banica in
[
4

proved dispersive estimates on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
which are stronger away from the origin compared to the Euclidean counterparts. In particular, radial symmetry data tends to spread out and is less likely to concentrate. Banica and Duyckaerts in

The same heuristic holds for wave equations on
\mathbb{H}^{d}

[

derived weighted (stronger than Euclidean) Strichartz estimates for radially symmetric wave equations on Damek-Ricci spaces (including
\mathbb{H}^{d}

[

studied shifted wave equations on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
where the Laplacian is shifted to compensate for its spectral gap. Strichartz estimates with a large family of admissible exponents are derived, as well as global well-posedness result for small data as an application. On
\mathbb{H}^{3}
in particular, Taylor and Metcalfe in
[

studied wave equations and established small data global well-posedness results for a broader range of power compared to that on Euclidean spaces. Tatatu in
[

1.4.
Outline of the paper

The outline of this paper is stated below.

In section 2, we list some preliminary results regarding analysis on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, as well as proving Strichartz estimate for wave equation with
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
initial data, which is slightly better than that in
[
2
]
.

In section 3, we construct a series of Morawetz potentials. In fact, some of the Morawetz potential functions we choose are not permitted on Euclidean spaces.

In section 4, we prove Theorem
1.4
using Fourier truncation method. We will split the initial data into a small, rough part and a large, smooth part. The small rough data evolves globally in time due to small data result. Thus our goal is to prove the global well-posedness of the evolution of the large smooth data, which satisfies the conformal wave equation with an error term caused by the interaction between the rough and smooth evolution. Our main idea is to utilize the Morawetz potentials to control these interaction terms.

\Delta&0\end{bmatrix}

and
H=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
\Delta-1+V&0\end{bmatrix}
.

Note that
\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}u\\
u_{t}\end{bmatrix}=e^{tH_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}
is the linear Klein Gordan without perturbation, thus satisfies

\|I_{1}e^{tH_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|(f,g)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

for admissible pair
(p,q,d,\theta,\frac{1}{2})
and
I_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}
.

Denote
A=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sqrt{|V|}&0\end{bmatrix}
and
B=\begin{bmatrix}-\sqrt{|V|}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
. Thus
H-H_{0}=AB

Duhamel’s formula yields

(2.13)

e^{tH}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}=e^{tH_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)H_{0}}ABe^{isH}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}ds

To prove that
\|I_{1}e^{tH}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|(f,g)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
, the first term in (
2.13
) automatically satisfies the estimate due to unperturbed Strichartz estimate. It only remains to prove the second term also maps
(f,g)\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}
. In fact, if we write the second term in the following form

\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)H_{0}}AB\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}ds=T_{1}T_{2}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}

where

(2.14)

g\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sqrt{|V|}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}e^{isH}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sqrt{|V|}\left(cos\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}+V}f+\frac{sin\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}+V}}{\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}+V}}g\right)\\
0\end{bmatrix}

and

(2.15)

T_{1}\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}=I_{1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)H_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sqrt{|V|}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix}ds

By (
2.9

T_{2}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow L^{2}_{t,x}\times 0
. Thus it suffices to prove that
T_{1}:L^{2}_{t,x}\times 0\rightarrow L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}

By Christ Kiselev Lemma, it suffices to show the mapping property for

(2.16)

\tilde{T_{1}}=I_{1}e^{tH_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sH_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sqrt{|V|}&0\end{bmatrix}ds

By Lemma
2.10
,
I_{1}e^{tH_{0}}
maps
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}
. Thus in order to prove (
2.16
), it suffices to show that
T_{3}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sH_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
\sqrt{|V|}&0\end{bmatrix}ds
maps
L^{2}_{t,x}\times 0
to
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. This can be seen by duality: given any
(\phi,\psi)\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle<T_{3}\begin{bmatrix}F\\
G\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\phi\\
\psi\end{bmatrix}>_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(s)\cdot\sqrt{|V|}\left(\frac{sin\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}}{\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}}\phi+cos\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}\psi\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|\sqrt{|V|}\left(\frac{sins\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}}{\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}}\phi+coss\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}}\psi\right)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

This proves

(2.18)

\|e^{t\sqrt{-\Delta+\rho^{2}+V}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

The nonlinear estimate follows from duality. This completes our proof of the perturbed Strichartz estimate on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

∎

(Strichartz Estimates for Wave Flow on

Global wellposedness and scattering of the solution to (

3.
Morawetz Estimates

In this section, we prove several Morawetz inequalities derived from choosing different potentials. Given smooth function
a(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
, let u be a radial solution to the modified NLW (
3.1
) with error term
\mathcal{N}

(3.1)

u_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}u+u^{p}=\mathcal{N}

For suitably defined functions
\phi,a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}-\{0\})
on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, define the Morawetz potential to be

(3.2)

M(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\phi\left(u_{t}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a+u_{t}u\frac{\Delta a}{2}\right)

In order to derive control from the Morawetz potential, we will have to impose certain restrictions on the choices of its component functions.

Assumption 3.1
.

a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}-\{0\})
is radially symmetric and satisfies

(3.3)

|\nabla a|\leq C,\hskip 20.00003pt\Delta a\geq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt\Delta^{2}a\leq 0

and
D^{2}a\geq 0
for any tangent vector v along radial direction.

and

Assumption 3.2
.

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d}-\{0\})
is radially symmetric and satisfies

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{r}\phi\geq 0\\
&|\nabla\phi|\leq\frac{C}{r},\hskip 20.00003pt|\Delta\phi|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\end{aligned}\right.

We first compute the time derivative of the Morawetz potential.

Lemma 3.3

(Generalized Morawetz Potential)
.

Suppose u is a radial symmetric solution to (
3.1
) on the time interval I, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{dM}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}a^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)u_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1})\phi\Delta au^{p+1}-\frac{1}{p+1}(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)u^{p+1}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\frac{1}{4}(\phi\Delta^{2}a+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta a+\Delta\phi\Delta a)u^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}\phi\mathcal{N}(\nabla a\cdot\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\phi\mathcal{N}u\Delta a

Proof.

We prove (1) for
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
and
u_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
. The general inequality case follows from a density argument.
Differentiating
M(t)
in t, we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{dM}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{H}}^{d}\phi(u_{tt}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a+u_{t}\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla a+\frac{1}{2}u_{tt}u\Delta a+\frac{1}{2}u_{t}^{2}\Delta a)

\displaystyle=I+II+III+IV

where I IV correspond to the four terms in (
3.6
) in order.
Using (
3.1
) and integrating in parts, we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\int\phi\Delta u\nabla u\cdot\nabla a

\displaystyle=\int\phi D^{2}a(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\nabla a\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}+\int(\nabla(\psi_{\epsilon}\phi)\cdot\nabla u)(\nabla a\cdot\nabla u)

\displaystyle=\int\phi D^{2}a(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Delta a|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi(\nabla\cdot\nabla a)|\nabla u|^{2}

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int\phi u^{p}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p+1}\int\phi u^{p+1}\Delta a-\frac{1}{p+1}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)u^{p+1}

(3.9)

II=\frac{1}{2}\int\Delta au_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)u_{t}^{2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle III_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi u\Delta u\Delta a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\nabla\phi\cdot u\nabla u\Delta a+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta a+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi u\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Delta a

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int(\Delta\phi\Delta a+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta a+\phi\Delta^{2}a)u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Delta a|\nabla u|^{2}

(3.11)

III_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int\phi u^{p+1}\Delta a

(3.12)

IV=-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Delta au_{t}^{2}

Collecting (
3.7
)-(
3.12
), we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{dM}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int\phi D^{2}a(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla a)u_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1})\int\phi\Delta au^{p+1}-\frac{1}{4}\int(\phi\Delta^{2}a+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta a+\Delta\phi)u^{2}

\displaystyle-\int\phi\mathcal{N}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Delta a\mathcal{N}u

Since u is radial, we have

D^{2}a(\nabla u,\nabla u)=\partial_{r}^{2}a(r)|\nabla u|^{2}

This proves the lemma.
∎

4.

3\leq d\leq 5

Since the initial data (
1.3
) lies below
H^{1}

H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}

In the following Proposition, we state the mechanism of Fourier truncation method.

Proposition 4.1
.

Given initial value problem (
1.1
),(
1.3
), we split the initial data into a high frequency small piece and a smoother large piece, in the following sense: for some

(4.1)

(u_{0},u_{1})=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})+(v_{0},v_{1})

(4.3)

(v_{0},v_{1})=(P_{\geq s}u_{0},P_{\geq s}u_{1})

\displaystyle\|(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1}}}\lesssim s^{\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-\epsilon_{1})}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\|(v_{0},v_{1})\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim s^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon)}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}

under the equation (

(\omega,\omega_{t})

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t}\|\omega\|_{H^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}

Now, suppose u(t,x) solves equation (

) on time interval I. We cut the high frequency evolution
\omega

. Let

be the energy of v for

), we have

(4.7)

E(0)\lesssim s^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}\lesssim_{\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}1

Our goal now is to show that the energy of v stays bounded throughout I. Suppose that is true, then by local well-posedness theory, v can be extended beyond I and global well-posedness follows. v solves the following equation on I:

(4.8)

\displaystyle v_{tt}-\Delta v+v^{\frac{d+3}{d-1}}=\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle v(0)=v_{0},\ \ v_{t}(0)=v_{1}

where
\mathcal{N}=-u^{\frac{d+3}{d-1}}+\omega^{\frac{d+3}{d-1}}+v^{\frac{d+3}{d-1}}

We will consider the cases where

and

4.1.
Case I:
3\leq d\leq 5

In this case, the error term
\mathcal{N}\lesssim|v^{\frac{d-1}{4}}\omega|+|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}|
.
And the derivative of
E(t)
writes as

(4.9)

\frac{dE(t)}{dt}=<\mathcal{N},v_{t}>\lesssim\left|\int v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega v_{t}\right|+\left|\int v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}v_{t}\right|

For heuristic, let us look at when
d=3
and the second term in
\mathcal{N}=\left|\int v\omega^{2}v_{t}\right|
. We can estimate it by

\displaystyle\left|\int v\omega^{2}v_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{6}\|v_{t}\|_{2}\|\omega\|_{6}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|_{6}^{2}E(t)

If we temporarily overlook the presence of the first term, the Grönwall inequality would give us
E(t)\lesssim e^{\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}}}\lesssim 1
. However, the term
\left|\int v^{2}\omega v_{t}\right|
cannot be estimated in the same fashion, for it yields
E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)
which is not enough for Gronwall inequality to rule out finite time blowup. For this purpose, we introduce the following modified energy, which takes advantage of the Morawetz potential defined in Section 3.

Definition 4.3

(Modified Energy)
.

Under the previous settings, define in terms of v,

(4.10)

\mathcal{E}(t)=E(t)-c_{1}M_{1}(t)-c_{2}M_{2}(t)-c_{3}M_{3}(t)-c_{4}M_{4}(t)

where
M_{j}
are defined in section 3, with
0<\delta<1
to be determined for
M_{2}
and
M_{4}
.
c_{j}>0
are small constants to be determined.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
0<\delta<1
, there exist
c_{j}>0
and

(1)

\mathcal{E}(t)\sim E(t)
for
t\in I

(2)

There exists
R>0
, such that the time derivative of
\mathcal{E}
is bounded by

(4.11)

\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}+\|e^{r}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-3}}_{x}(r>R)}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-3}}+\|e^{\frac{d-1}{4}r}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{4(d+1)}{d-1}}_{x}(r>R)}^{\frac{4(d+1)}{d-1}}

Let us postpone the proof of Proposition
4.4
and continue on to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. We will see that Theorem
1.4
is a direct result of Proposition
4.4
.

Proof of the theorem
1.4
:
Integrating (
4.11
) in time will yield

(4.12)

\sup_{t}\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}_{t,x}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}+\|e^{r}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-3}}_{t,x}(I\times\{r>R\})}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-3}}+\|e^{\frac{d-1}{4}r}\omega\|_{L^{\frac{4(d+1)}{d-1}}_{t,x}(I\times\{r>R\})}

Recall from Corallary

(4.13)

\sup_{t}\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0)+\delta_{0}

Combining the preceding estimate with part (1) of
4.4
yields

(4.14)

\sup_{t\in I}E(t)\lesssim\sup_{t\in I}\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0)+\delta_{0}

This proves global wellposedness.

Scattering follows directly from Morawetz estimate and (
4.14
)

(4.15)

\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}_{t,x}(I\times\mathbb{H}^{d})}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}\lesssim\sup_{t\in I}E(t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0)+\delta_{0}

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Now, we prove Proposition
4.4
.

Proof.

Part (1) of Proposition
4.4
easily follows from the boundedness of
|\nabla a|
and
|\phi|
(except for
M_{4}
)

|\int\phi(v_{t}\nabla a\cdot\nabla v)|\lesssim\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla a\|_{\infty}\|v_{t}\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}

By Hardy inequality,

|\int\phi(v_{t}\frac{v}{r})|\lesssim\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|v_{t}\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}

Thus

M_{j}(t)\lesssim E(t)

for j=1,2,3
For
M_{4}
,

\displaystyle|M_{4}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0<r\leq 1}|v_{t}\nabla v\cdot r^{\delta}logr|+|v_{t}\frac{v}{r}r^{\delta}logr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{t}\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}

This proves part (1) of Proposition
4.4
, by choosing
c_{j}
small enough.
In order to prove part (2), we estimate
\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}
using results from Corollary
3.5
and
3.6
. In fact, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{E}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{dE(t)}{dt}-c_{1}\frac{dM_{1}(t)}{dt}-c_{2}\frac{dM_{2}(t)}{dt}-c_{3}\frac{dM_{3}(t)}{dt}-c_{4}\frac{M_{4}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega v_{t}\|_{1}+\|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}v_{t}\|_{1}

\displaystyle-c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{C}\|e^{-r}\nabla v\|_{2}+\frac{1}{C}\|v\|_{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}-C\|\mathcal{N}\nabla v\|_{1}-C\|\mathcal{N}v\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-c_{2}\left(\frac{1}{C}\|r^{\frac{-1+\delta}{2}}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}+\frac{1}{C}\|r^{\frac{-3+\delta}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}-C\|\mathcal{N}\nabla v\|_{1}-C\|\mathcal{N}\frac{v}{r^{1-\delta}}\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-c_{3}\left(\frac{1}{C}\|e^{-r}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r>R)}^{2}-C\|e^{-r}\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}-C\|v\|_{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}-C\|q(x)u\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mathcal{N}\nabla u\|_{1}-C\|\mathcal{N}u\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-c_{4}\left(\frac{1}{C}\|r^{\frac{-1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}-C\|r^{\frac{-1+\delta}{2}}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}-C\|r^{-\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}}u\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}_{x}(r<R)}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}-C\|r^{\frac{-3+\delta}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-C\|\mathcal{N}\nabla u\|_{1}-C\|\mathcal{N}\frac{u}{r}\|_{1}\right)

Pick
c_{3}<<c_{1}
and

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega v_{t}\|_{1}+\|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}v_{t}\|_{1}+C\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega\nabla v\|_{1}+\|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\nabla v\|_{1}+C\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega v\|_{1}

\displaystyle+C\|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}v\|_{1}+C\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega\frac{v}{r}\|_{1}+C\|v\omega^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\frac{v}{r}\|_{1}

\displaystyle-c\|e^{-r}\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}-c\|e^{-r}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r>R)}^{2}-c\|r^{\frac{-1+\delta}{2}}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2}-c\|e^{-r}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r>R)}^{2}

We will first deal with the

\displaystyle\|v^{\frac{4}{d-1}}\omega v_{t}\|_{L^{1}_{x}(r<R)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{\frac{-1+\delta}{2}}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}\|r^{\frac{-3+\delta}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}(r<R)}^{2\alpha}\|r^{-\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}(1-\delta)}v\|_{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}\beta}\|r^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\delta}\omega\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\|r^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\delta}\omega\|_{\infty}\|r^{-\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}(1-\delta)}v\|_{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}

5.
Directions for future investigation

In the final part of the paper, we make a few comments on the directions for further research of the global wellposedness and scattering problem of nonlinear wave equation on the hyperbolic space.

1.
Sharp results
We proved global wellposedness and scattering for equation (
1.1
) with initial data in
H^{2}\times H^{2-1}
, for any
s>\frac{1}{2}
. The conjecture derived from the scaling symmetry predicts positive results for initial data in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. We refer to
[
10
]
for a proof of the sharp result on Euclidean space. Despite the hyperbolic geometry greatly simplifying the proof for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, the author was not able to develop the necessary harmonic analysis tools on hyperbolic spaces similar to those used in
[
10
]
to treat low regularity critical problem on Euclidean spaces, e.g. an easy-to-use Littlewood Paley frequency decomposition.

2.
Intercritical range
Intercritical wave equations between the conformal and energy critical state, namely with exponents
1+\frac{4}{d-1}<p<1+\frac{4}{d-2}
, remains to be studied on non-euclidean spaces. We refer to
[
14
]
for results on Euclidean spaces. The author expects that the hyperbolic geometry should allow the proof of global wellposedness and scattering to be simplified, at least for non-sharp exponents
s>s_{c}
.

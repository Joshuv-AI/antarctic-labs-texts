New bounds on the distance Laplacian and distance signless Laplacian spectral radii
Roberto C. Díaz, Ana I. Julio, Oscar Rojo

Abstract
Let G G be a simple undirected connected graph. In this paper, new upper bounds on the distance Laplacian spectral radius of G G are obtained. Moreover, new lower and upper bounds for the distance signless Laplacian spectral radius of G G are derived. Some of the above mentioned bounds are sharp and the graphs attaining the corresponding bound are characterized. Several illustrative examples are included.

05C50, 15A48

Keywords:

distance matrix; vertex transmission; Wiener index; distance Laplacian matrix; distance signless Laplacian matrix; spectral radius

Introduction

Let
G=\left(V(G),E(G)\right)
be a simple undirected graph on
n
vertices with vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
.

The distance between
u,v\in V(G)
for a connected graph
G
, denoted by
d(u,v)
, is the length of the shortest path connecting
u
and
v
. The Wiener index
W(G)
of a connected graph
G
is

W(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u,v\in V(G)}d(u,v)

and the transmission
Tr(v)
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is the sum of the distances from
v
to all other vertices of
G
, that is,

Tr(v)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d(v,u).

The graph
G
is said to be
k-

transmission regular
if
Tr(v)=k
for each vertex
v\in V(G)
.

The
distance matrix
of a graph
G
of order
n
is the
n\times n
matrix
\mathcal{D}(G)=\left(d_{i,j}\right)
, indexed by the vertices of
G
,
where
d_{i,j}=d(v_{i},v_{j})
. Two of the oldest works on the distance matrix are
[
19
]
(1935) and
[
26
]
(1938). In
[
10
]
(1964), the authors ask for the conditions under which a given a square symmetric matrix
with real and nonnegative entries is realized as the distance matrix of a graph. Different realizability problems of distance matrices are investigated in
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
6
]
, among many others papers. A remarkable result on the distance matrix, due to Graham and Pollak
[
12
]
(1971), is : If
T
is a tree of order
n\geq 2
with distance matrix
\mathcal{D}(T)
, then

\text{det}\mathcal{D}(T)=(-1)^{n-1}\left(n-1\right)2^{n-2}.

Thus, the determinant of the distance matrix of a tree depends only on its order. The result established by Graham and Pollak attracted much interest among algebraic graph theory researchers.

The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}(G)
are called the
distance eigenvalues
of
G
and denoted by

\partial_{1}(G)\geq\partial_{2}(G)\geq\ldots\geq\partial_{n}(G).

Some properties about the eigenvalues of the distance matrix are already known. For example, in
[
28
]
, the author characterizes the graphs with minimal spectral radius of the distance matrix in three classes of simple connected graphs with
n
vertices: with fixed vertex connectivity, matching number and chromatic number, respectively. In
[
14
]
, the authors characterize all connected graphs with
\partial_{n}(G)=-2
. Furthermore, they characterize all connected graphs of diameter
2
with exactly three distance eigenvalues when
\partial_{1}(G)
is not an integer. They also conjecture that the complete
k
-partite graph is determined by its distance spectrum. Later, in
[
25
]
, the author determines all graphs which satisfy
\partial_{n}(G)\in[-2.383\ ,\ 0]
. A very complete survey of the state of the art on distance matrices up to 2014 appears in
[
3
]
(see also

[
18
]
).

In
[
2
]
Aouchiche and Hansen introduce, for a connected graph
G
, the distance Laplacian matrix
\mathcal{L}(G)
and the distance signless Laplacian matrix
\mathcal{Q}(G)
as follows

\mathcal{L}(G)=\emph{Tr}(G)-\mathcal{D}(G)

and

\mathcal{Q}(G)=\emph{Tr}(G)+\mathcal{D}(G)

where

\emph{Tr}(G)=\emph{diag}[\emph{Tr}(v_{1}),\emph{Tr}(v_{2}),\ldots,\emph{Tr}(v_{n})]

is the diagonal matrix of the vertex transmissions in
G
.

In
[
2
]
, among other results, the above mentioned authors prove the equivalence between the distance signless Laplacian, distance Laplacian and the distance spectra for the class of transmission regular graphs. The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\mathcal{Q}(G)
are called the
distance Laplacian eigenvalues
and the
distance signless Laplacian eigenvalues
of
G
and they are denoted by

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\geq\partial_{2}^{L}(G)\geq\ldots\geq\partial_{n}^{L}(G)

and

\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)\geq\partial_{2}^{Q}(G)\geq\ldots\geq\partial_{n}^{Q}(G),

respectively.

\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\mathcal{Q}(G)
are both real symmetric matrices. From this fact and
Geršgorin’s Theorem, it follows that their eigenvalues are nonnegative real numbers. Let
1
be the all ones vector. Clearly each row sum of
\mathcal{L}(G)
is
0
. Then
\left(0,\mathbf{1}\right)
is an eigenpair of
\mathcal{L}(G)
and, since
G
is connected graph,
0
is a simple eigenvalue.

Moreover

trace(\mathcal{L}(G))=trace(\mathcal{Q}(G))=2W(G).

Clearly, if
G
is a
k-
transmission regular graph then

\mathcal{L}(G)=kI_{n}-\mathcal{D}(G)

and

\mathcal{Q}(G)=kI_{n}+\mathcal{D}(G)

where
I_{n}
is the identity matrix of order
n
; and, for
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\partial_{i}^{L}(G)=k-\partial_{n-i+1}(G)

and

\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)=k+\partial_{i}(G)

From the Perron-Frobenius Theory for nonnegative matrices, we have

Theorem 1

If
A
is a nonnegative matrix then its spectral radius
\rho(A)
is an eigenvalue of
A
, and it has an associated nonnegative eigenvector. Furthermore, if
A
is irreducible then
\rho(A)
is a simple
eigenvalue of
A
with an associated positive eigenvector.

In particular, since
\mathcal{Q}(G)
is a positive matrix,
\rho(\mathcal{Q}(G))
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathcal{Q}(G)
.

The Frobenius norm of an
n\times n
matrix
M=(m_{i,j})
is

\|M\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|m_{i,j}|^{2}}.

We recall if
M
is a normal matrix then

\|M\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}(M)|^{2}
where
\lambda_{1}(M),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(M)
are the eigenvalues of
M
. In particular,

\|\mathcal{L}(G)\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\partial_{i}^{L}(G))^{2}

and

\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G))^{2}.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
G
is a connected graph of order
n
and
K_{n}
denotes the complete graph on n vertices.

Some lower and upper bounds on
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
and
\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)
are already known. The purpose of this paper is to search for new bounds on these spectral radii. Section 2, is dedicated to upper bounds on
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
, we recall some known upper bounds as well as some previous results that allow us to derive new upper bounds. Finally, in Section 3, we recall some known results and we obtain new lower and upper bounds on
\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)
. In Section 2 as well as in Section 3, illustrative examples are given.

Theorem 7

Let

be an

arbitrary matrix with eigenvalues

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k},\dots,\lambda_{n}.

Let

\mathbf{x}=\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right]^{T}

be an eigenvector of

\lambda_{k}
and
let
\mathbf{q}
be any

has eigenvalues

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k}+\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{q},\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}.

We next derive our first new upper bound on
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
.

Theorem 8

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, let

p_{i}=\max_{j\neq i}d_{i,j}

and let

\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{L}(G)+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{p}^{T}.

(4)

Then

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}.

(5)

If the equality in (
5
) holds then
\mathcal{B}(G)
is a reducible matrix. This necessary condition for the equality in (
5
), it is not a sufficient condition. If
\mathcal{B}(G)
is a irreducible matrix the inequality in (
5
) is strict.

Proof
Since
(0,\mathbf{1})
is an eigenpair
for the distance Laplacian matrix
\mathcal{L}(G),
using Theorem
7
, we obtain that the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{B}(G)

are

\partial_{1}^{L}(G),\ldots,\partial_{n-1}^{L}(G),\mathbf{1}^{T}\mathbf{p}.

Moreover

\mathcal{B}(G)\mathbf{1}=\mathcal{L}(G)\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{1})=(\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{1})\mathbf{1}=(\mathbf{1}^{T}\mathbf{p})\mathbf{1}.

The entries of
\mathcal{B}(G)=(b_{i,j})
are

b_{i,i}=Tr(v_{i})+p_{i}

for
i=1,\ldots,n

and

b_{i,j}=-d_{i,j}+p_{j}

for
j\neq i
.
Then
\mathcal{B}(G)
is a nonnegative matrix.
From Theorem
1
, we obtain that
\rho(\mathcal{B}(G))=\mathbf{1}^{T}\mathbf{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
with eigenvector the all
ones vector
\mathbf{1}.

Therefore
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}.

Suppose that
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}.
Hence
\rho(\mathcal{B}(G))
is a repeated eigenvalue of the nonnegative matrix
\mathcal{B}(G)
and then, from Theorem
1
, it is a reducible matrix. The converse is not true (see Example
1
below).

Suppose that
\mathcal{B}(G)
is a irreducible matrix. From Theorem
1
,
\rho(\mathcal{B}(G))
is a simple eigenvalue and then
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
<
\rho(\mathcal{B}(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
.

\Box

We now recall the following lemma that will play an important role in getting another upper bound on
\partial_{1}^{L}(G).

Lemma 1

[
17
]
If
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\ldots\geq x_{m}
are real numbers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=0
then

x_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}.

The equality holds if and only if
x_{2}=\ldots=x_{m}=-\frac{x_{1}}{m-1}
.

Theorem 11

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
then

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\leq\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}+\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\bigg(\|\mathcal{L}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{(2W(G))^{2}}{n-1}\bigg)}.

(9)

The equality holds if and only if
G=K_{n}
or
G
is a connected graph with three distinct distance Laplacian eigenvalues:

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
,
\frac{2W(G)-\partial_{1}^{L}(G)}{n-2}
and
0
.

Proof
The distance Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
are

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\geq\partial_{2}^{L}(G)\geq\ldots\geq\partial_{n-1}^{L}(G)>\partial_{n}^{L}(G)=0.

Then
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\partial_{i}^{L}(G)=2W(G)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\partial_{i}^{L}(G))^{2}=\|\mathcal{L}(G)\|_{F}^{2}.
Moreover

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\bigg)=0.

Applying Lemma
1
, we get

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\bigg)^{2}}.

(10)

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\big(\partial_{i}^{L}(G)\big)^{2}-2\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\partial_{i}^{L}(G)+(n-1)\big(\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\big)^{2}

=\|\mathcal{L}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-2\frac{\big(2W(G)\big)^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{\big(2W(G)\big)^{2}}{n-1}=\|\mathcal{L}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{(2W(G))^{2}}{n-1},

the upper bounds (
9
) and (
10
) are equivalent. Moreover, from Lemma
1
, the equality in (
10
) holds if and only if

\partial_{2}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\ =\ \cdots\ =\ \partial_{n-1}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}\ =\ -\frac{\partial_{1}^{L}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n-1}}{n-2}.

Therefore, the equality in (
9
) holds if and only if
\partial_{2}^{L}(G)\ =\ \cdots\ =\ \partial_{n-1}^{L}(G)\ =\ \frac{2W(G)-\partial_{1}^{L}(G)}{n-2}
. If
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)=\frac{2W(G)-\partial_{1}^{L}(G)}{n-2}
then
G
is a graph in which the multiplicity of
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
is
n-1
and thus, from Theorem
3
,
G=K_{n}
. If
\partial_{1}^{L}(G)\neq\frac{2W(G)-\partial_{1}^{L}(G)}{n-2}
then
G
is a connected graph with three distinct distance Laplacian eigenvalues:

\partial_{1}^{L}(G)
,
\frac{2W(G)-\partial_{1}^{L}(G)}{n-2}
and
0
. The proof is complete.

\Box

For brevity, let

. We will prove that

\frac{(2n-3)^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}\Delta^{4}+4k^{4}-4\frac{2n-3}{n-1}\Delta^{2}k^{2}\geq 4(\Delta^{2}-k^{2})(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\bigg(\Delta^{2}-\frac{n}{n-1}k^{2}\bigg))\Leftrightarrow

\bigg(\frac{(2n-3)^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}-4\frac{n-2}{n-1}\bigg)\Delta^{4}+4\bigg(1-\frac{(n-2)n}{(n-1)^{2}}\bigg)k^{4}+4\bigg(-\frac{2n-3}{n-1}+\frac{n-2}{n-1}+\frac{(n-2)n}{(n-1)^{2}}\bigg)\Delta^{2}k^{2}\geq 0\Leftrightarrow

\bigg((2n-3)^{2}-4(n-2)(n-1)\bigg)\Delta^{4}+4\bigg((n-1)^{2}-(n-2)n\bigg)k^{4}

+4\bigg(-(2n-3)(n-1)+(n-2)(n-1)+n(n-2)\bigg)\Delta^{2}k^{2}\geq 0\Leftrightarrow

\Delta^{4}+4k^{4}-4\Delta^{2}k^{2}\geq 0\Leftrightarrow

\bigg(\Delta^{2}-2k^{2}\bigg)^{2}\geq 0.

Since the last inequality is clearly true, it follows that
c_{1}-c_{2}\geq 0
.

\Box

Lemma 3

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
p(x)
be a polynomial on
x
. Then

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\{R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\}\leq\ p(\partial^{Q}_{1}(G))\ \leq\ \max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\}.

(21)

Moreover, if the row sums of
p(\mathcal{Q}(G))
are not all equal, then both inequalities in (
21
) are strict.

Proof

\mathcal{Q}(G)
is a positive matrix. Then there exists a positive vector
\textbf{x}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{T}
such that
\mathcal{Q}(G)\textbf{x}=\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)\textbf{x}
. Then

p(\mathcal{Q}(G))\textbf{x}=p(\partial_{1}^{Q}(G))\textbf{x}.

We may assume
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1
. Hence

p(\partial_{1}^{Q}(G))=p(\partial_{1}^{Q}(G))\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p(\partial_{1}^{Q}(G))x_{i}

=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G))\textbf{x})_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G))).

Since the entries of
x
are positive and their is equal to
1
, we have

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\{R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\},

and thus the result follows.

\Box

Theorem 17

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices. Let
\emph{T}
and
\emph{t}
be the maximum and the minimum transmissions of
G
, respectively. Then, for any
u\in V(G)
,

2W(G)+(\emph{t}-1)Tr(u)-(n-1)\emph{t}\leq\sum_{v\neq u}d(u,v)Tr(v)

\leq\ 2W(G)+(\emph{T}-1)Tr(u)-(n-1)\emph{T}.

Proof

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\neq u}d(u,v)Tr(v)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum\limits_{v\neq u}Tr(v)+\sum\limits_{v\neq u}(d(u,v)-1)Tr(v)

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\sum\limits_{v\neq u}(d(u,v)-1)Tr(v).

(22)

Using (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\neq u}d(u,v)Tr(v)\

\displaystyle\geq\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{t}\sum\limits_{v\neq u}(d(u,v)-1)

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{t}\bigg(\sum\limits_{v\neq u}d(u,v)-(n-1)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{t}(Tr(u)-(n-1))

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)+(\emph{t}-1)Tr(u)-(n-1)\emph{t}.

Also, using (
22
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\neq u}d(u,v)Tr(v)\

\displaystyle\leq\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{T}\sum\limits_{v\neq u}(d(u,v)-1)

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{T}\bigg(\sum\limits_{v\neq u}d(u,v)-(n-1)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)-Tr(u)+\emph{T}(Tr(u)-(n-1))

\displaystyle=\ 2W(G)+(\emph{T}-1)Tr(u)-(n-1)\emph{T}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

\textbf{e}_{i}

Theorem 18

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices. Let
\emph{T}
and
\emph{t}
the maximum and the minimum transmissions of

, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\emph{t}-1+\sqrt{(\emph{t}-1)^{2}+8(\emph{t}^{2}+2W(G)-(n-1)\emph{t})}}{2}\ \leq\ \partial^{Q}_{1}(G)\

(23)

and

\displaystyle\partial^{Q}_{1}(G)\ \leq\ \frac{\emph{T}-1+\sqrt{(\emph{T}-1)^{2}+8(\emph{T}^{2}+2W(G)-(n-1)\emph{T})}}{2}\ .

(24)

Proof
Since
\mathcal{Q}(G)=Tr(G)+\mathcal{D}(G)
, we have

\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G)\ =\ Tr^{2}(G)+Tr(G)\mathcal{D}(G)+\mathcal{D}(G)Tr(G)+\mathcal{D}^{2}(G).

Therefore, the
i
-th row sum of
\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G)
is

\displaystyle R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G))\

\displaystyle=\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G)\textbf{1}

\displaystyle=\ \textbf{e}^{T}_{i}Tr^{2}(G)\textbf{1}+\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}Tr(G)\mathcal{D}(G)\textbf{1}+\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}\mathcal{D}(G)Tr(G)\textbf{1}+\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}\mathcal{D}^{2}(G)\textbf{1}

\displaystyle=\ 2\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}Tr^{2}(G)\textbf{1}+2\textbf{e}^{T}_{i}\mathcal{D}^{2}(G)\textbf{1}

\displaystyle=\ 2Tr^{2}(v_{i})+2\sum\limits_{v\neq v_{i}}d(v_{i},v)Tr(v).

(25)

From Theorem
17
and (
25
), we have

2Tr^{2}(v_{i})+2(2W(G)+(\emph{t}-1)Tr(v_{i})-(n-1)\emph{t})\ \leq\ R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G))

(26)

and

R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G))\ \leq\ 2Tr^{2}(v_{i})+2(2W(G)+(\emph{T}-1)Tr(v_{i})-(n-1)\emph{T}).

(27)

Let
p(x)=x^{2}-(\emph{t}-1)x
. The
i
-th row sum of
p(\mathcal{Q}(G))
is

\displaystyle R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\

\displaystyle=\ R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G)-(\emph{t}-1)\mathcal{Q}(G))

\displaystyle=\ R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G))-(\emph{t}-1)R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}(G))

\displaystyle=\ R_{i}(\mathcal{Q}^{2}(G))-2(\emph{t}-1)Tr(v_{i}).

(28)

From (
26
) and (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\

\displaystyle\geq\ 2Tr^{2}(v_{i})+4W(G)-2(n-1)\emph{t}.

(29)

\displaystyle R_{i}(p(\mathcal{Q}(G)))\

From this inequality and Lemma

\displaystyle 2\emph{t}^{2}+4W(G)-2(n-1)\emph{t}\ \leq\ p(\partial^{Q}_{1}(G))\ =\ (\partial^{Q}_{1}(G))^{2}-(\emph{t}-1)\partial^{Q}_{1}(G).

Finally, we derive another new upper bound on the largest distance singless Laplacian eigenvalue.

Theorem 19

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
then

\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)\leq\frac{2W(G)}{n}+\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{(2W(G))^{2}}{n}\bigg)}.

(30)

The equality holds if and only if
G=K_{n}
.

Proof
We have that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)=2W(G)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G))^{2}=\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\bigg)=0.

Applying Lemma
1
, we get

\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\bigg)^{2}}

(31)

with equality if and only if

\partial_{2}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\ =\ \cdots\ =\ \partial_{n}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\ =\ -\frac{\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}}{n-1}.

(32)

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)-\frac{2W(G)}{n}\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)\big)^{2}-2\frac{2W(G)}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{Q}(G)+n\big(\frac{2W(G)}{n}\big)^{2}

=\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-2\frac{\big(2W(G)\big)^{2}}{n}+\frac{\big(2W(G)\big)^{2}}{n}=\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{(2W(G))^{2}}{n},

the upper bound (
31
) is equivalent to

\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)\leq\frac{2W(G)}{n}+\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{Q}(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{(2W(G))^{2}}{n}\bigg)}

(33)

with the necessary and sufficient condition for the equality given in (
32
). We claim that the equality in (
33
) holds if and only if
G=K_{n}
. Suppose the equality in (
33
). Then the eigenvalues
\partial_{2}^{Q}(G),\ldots,\partial_{n}^{Q}(G)
satisfy (
32
) and thus

\partial_{2}^{Q}(G)=\ldots=\partial_{n}^{Q}(G).

Moreover, from the fact that
\mathcal{Q}(G)
is a positive matrix,
\partial_{1}^{Q}(G)
is a simple eigenvalue. Hence the equality in (
33
) implies that
G
has only two distinct distance signless Laplacian eigenvalues. Hence, from Lemma
4
,
G=K_{n}
. Conversely, using the fact that the distance signless Laplacian eigenvalues of
K_{n}
are
\partial_{1}^{Q}(K_{n})=2n-2
and
\partial_{i}^{Q}(K_{n})=n-2
, for
i=2,\ldots,n
, one can easily see that the equality in (
33
) holds. The proof is complete.

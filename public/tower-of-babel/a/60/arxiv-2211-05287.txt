1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mbox{Irr}(G)
the set of all irreducible characters of
G
. For any
\chi\in\mbox{Irr}(G)
the codegree of
\chi
, written
{\mbox{cod}}(\chi)
, is defined to be
{\mbox{cod}}(\chi)=\frac{|G:\ker(\chi)|}{\chi(1)}
. We denote
{\mbox{cod}}(G)=\{{\mbox{cod}}(\chi)\ |\ \chi\in\mbox{Irr}(G)\}
.
The concept of codegrees was originally considered in
[
6
]
where the codegree of
\chi
was defined as
\frac{|G|}{\chi(1)}
. The definition was later modified in
[
15
]
so that
{\mbox{cod}}(\chi)
is independent of
N
when
N\leq\ker(\chi)
. Many properties of codegrees have been studied, such as the relationship between the codegrees and the element orders, codegrees of
p
-groups, and groups with few codegrees.

Let
{\mbox{cd}}(G)=\{\chi(1)\ |\ \chi\in\mbox{Irr}(G)\}
. In the 1990s, Bertram Huppert studied the character degrees of simple groups have a very high degree of individuality. He conjectured a relationship between the structure of groups and its codegrees.

Huppert’s conjecture:
Let
H
be any finite non-abelian simple group and
G
a finite group such that
{\mbox{cd}}(G)
=
{\mbox{cd}}(H)
. Then
G\cong H\times A
, where
A
is abelian.

Recently, an alternative version of Huppert’s conjecture has been proposed by Qian (question 20.79 in the Kourovka Notebook).

Codegree version of Huppert’s conjecture:
Let
H
be any finite non-abelian simple group and
G
a finite group such that
{\mbox{cod}}(G)={\mbox{cod}}(H)
. Then
G\cong H
.

This conjecture has been considered and shown to hold for
{\mbox{PSL}}(2,q)
in
[
4
]
. In
[
1
]
, the conjecture was proven for
{}^{2}B_{2}(2^{2f+1})
, where
f\geq 1
,
{\mbox{PSL}}(3,4)
,
\mathrm{\textup{{A}}}_{7}
, and
\textup{{J}}_{1}
. The conjecture holds in the cases where
H
is
\textup{{M}}_{11},\textup{{M}}_{12},\textup{{M}}_{22},\textup{{M}}_{23},{\mbox{PSL}}(3,3),{\mbox{PSL}}(3,q)
where
4<q\not\equiv 1\ (\bmod\ 3)
,
{\mbox{PSU}}(3,q)
where
4<q\not\equiv-1\ (\bmod\ 3)
,
\textup{{G}}_{2}(2)^{\prime}
,
{\mbox{PSL}}(3,q)
where
4<q\equiv 1\ (\bmod\ 3)
,
{\mbox{PSU}}(3,q)
where
4<q\equiv-1\ (\bmod\ 3)
,
{\mbox{PSL}}(4,2)
, or
{}^{2}\textup{{G}}_{2}(3^{2f+1}),f\geq 1
by
[
8
,
12
,
9
]
. In this paper we prove the following result which shows that the conjecture holds in the cases where
H
is
\textup{{S}}_{4}(4),\textup{{U}}_{4}(2)
,
\textup{{S}}_{4}(q)\ (q\geq 4)
,
\textup{{U}}_{4}(3)
,
{}^{2}\textup{{F}}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
,
\textup{{J}}_{3}
,
\textup{{G}}_{2}(3)
,
\textup{{A}}_{9}
,
\textup{{J}}_{2}
,
{\mbox{PSL}}(4,3)
,
\textup{{M}}^{c}\textup{{L}}

\textup{{S}}_{4}(5)

,

,

. Then,

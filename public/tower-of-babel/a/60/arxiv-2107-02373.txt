Abstract
We report the first reconstruction of the B 0 → π 0 ​ π 0 B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0} decay mode at Belle II using samples of 2019 and 2020 data that correspond to 62.8 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} of integrated luminosity. We find 14.0 − 5.6 + 6.8 14.0^{+6.8}_{-5.6} signal decays, corresponding to a significance of 3.4 standard deviations and determine a branching ratio of ℬ ⁡ ( B 0 → π 0 ​ π 0 ) = [ 0.98 − 0.39 + 0.48 ± 0.27 ] × 10 − 6 \mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0})=[0.98^{+0.48}_{-0.39}\pm 0.27]\times 10^{-6} . The results agree with previous determinations and contribute important information to an early assessment of detector performance and Belle II’s potential for future determinations of α / ϕ 2 \alpha/\phi_{2} using B → π π \mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\rightarrow\pi\pi modes.

2
Belle II detector

A full description of the Belle II detector is given in Ref.
Belle-II:2010dht
. The detector consists of several subdetectors arranged in a cylindrical structure around the beam pipe. Compared to its predecessor Belle
Belle:2000cnh

\mu

The PXD is surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon strip detector, referred to as the silicon vertex detector, and a central drift chamber (CDC). A time-of-propagation counter and an aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov counter cover the barrel and forward endcap regions of the detector, respectively, and are essential for charged-particle identification (PID). The electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) makes up the remaining volume inside a superconducting solenoid, which operates at 1.5 T. A dedicated detector to identify
K^{0}_{L}
mesons and muons is installed in the outermost part of the detector. The z-axis of the laboratory frame is defined as the symmetry axis of the solenoid, and the positive direction is approximately given by the incoming electron beam. The polar angle
\theta
, as well as the longitudinal and the transverse direction are defined with respect to the z-axis. The ECL is most relevant for this work as it is the only subdetector that can detect photons.

4
Multivariate background suppression

The
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decay has large continuum background even relative to other charmless
B
decay modes because of its small branching ratio. To discriminate against such background, we use the FBDT classifier to combines 28 variables associated with event topology, which are known to provide statistical discrimination between
B
-meson signal and continuum background. We additionally require that these variables have correlations with
{\Delta E}
and
{M_{\rm bc}}
below 5%. We train the classifier to identify statistically significant signal and background features using unbiased simulated samples. We validate the input and output distributions of the classifier by comparing data with simulation using the control mode
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\pi^{0}
. No inconsistency is observed.

The optimal FBDT threshold is determined by maximizing the figure of merit
S/\sqrt{S+B}
, where
S
and
B
are the simulated signal and background yield, respectively, both determined in the signal-enhanced region
5.27~<{M_{\rm bc}}~<5.29{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
-0.1~<{\Delta E}~<0.15\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The resulting threshold criterion rejects 97.8% of the background while retaining 57.9% of the signal. The signal efficiency after
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
candidate reconstruction and continuum suppression is 21%. About 1.6% of selected events have more than one candidate. We choose the candidate with the minimum absolute deviation,
|dM(\pi^{0}_{1})|+|dM(\pi^{0}_{2})|
, of the reconstructed invariant masses from the known value. This is 98% efficient in selecting the correct
\mathit{{\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
. In addition, we reconstruct the vertex of the accompanying tag-side
B
meson and identify the flavor using a category-based flavor tagger
Abudinen:2019hcr
.

To validate the FBDT, 9.2
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of off-resonance data is used as it contains only continuum events. We apply the same
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
selection as discussed previously except for the requirements
M_{bc}>5.2{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
|{\Delta E}|<0.5\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
imposed to retain as many continuum events as possible. We impose the FBDT requirement and determine the continuum rejection to be 97.4%, which agrees with MC expectation.

Backgrounds due to non-signal
B
decays are denoted as
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
. All the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background effectively consists of
B^{+}\rightarrow\rho^{+}\pi^{0}
decays where the charged pion from the subsequent
\rho^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
is unreconstructed, and
\mathit{{\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{S}(\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0})\pi^{0}
decays where one of the
\pi^{0}
is unreconstructed. This background peaks at similar values of
{M_{\rm bc}}
and
T_{c}
but has
{\Delta E}
shifted to negative values due to missing energy from the unreconstructed particle. Since the topology of
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events is similar to the signal mode, the FBDT removes approximately the same fraction of
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
and signal events.

5
Determination of signal yields

Signal yields are determined with a three-dimensional (
{M_{\rm bc}},{\Delta E}
,
T_{c}
) simultaneous unbinned maximum likelihood fit for each
b
flavor,
q
, in 8 bins of the dilution factor,
r
.
T_{c}
is the log transform of the continuum suppression FBDT variable and is used to transform the FBDT output into a Gaussian-like shape. By convention
q=+1
tags a
\mathit{{\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
while
q=-1
tags a
\mathit{{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
. The dilution factor is determined by a category-based flavor tagger
Abudinen:2019hcr
with
r=0
meaning no flavor discrimination between
\mathit{{\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
and
\mathit{{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
and
r=1
meaning unambiguous flavor assignment. Bins are spaced so that each bin has an approximately equal number of candidates. Fit models are determined empirically from simulation, with shifts of peak positions in
{\Delta E}
determined in the
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\pi^{0}
control mode. A negative shift in
{\Delta E}
relative to simulated data is observed. The value of the shift favored by control data is determined using a likelihood-ratio test to be
-10
MeV. This is included in the
{\Delta E}
fit model for the signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
component.

To validate the MC
q\cdot r
distributions, we compare the
q\cdot r
fractions in experimental and simulated data restricted to a continuum-enriched sideband defined as
5.20<{M_{\rm bc}}<5.26{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
where continuum events are dominant. The continuum suppression requirement is removed to have a sufficient sample size. The results show excellent agreement.

For all components,
T_{c}
is modelled using the sum of a Gaussian and a bifurcated Gaussian with a common mean to avoid peak splitting. For the signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
components, the correlation between
{M_{\rm bc}}
and
{\Delta E}
is taken into account with a two-dimensional kernel density estimation. We employ a data-driven method to determine the parameters of the continuum background probability density function (PDF) by fitting to the sideband region defined as
5.24<{M_{\rm bc}}<5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
0.1<{\Delta E}<0.3\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The range is limited since selections too far from the signal region may accept candidates too far kinematically from the signal region. The
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background has a negligible contribution as within both ranges, only 0.07% survive and we expect
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
to be only a tenth of the total background. All signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
PDF parameters are fixed from simulated data while all continuum PDF parameters are fixed from experimental sideband data. The PDF parameters are identical for all
q.r
bins. The signal, continuum and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
bin fractions are fixed while the yields are allowed to float. The full PDF for the signal component is given by

P^{s}_{i}({M_{\rm bc}},{\Delta E},T_{c},q)=[1-q\times\Delta w_{i}+q(1-2w_{i})\times(1-2\chi_{d})\mathcal{A}_{CP}]P^{s}({M_{\rm bc}},{\Delta E},T_{c}),

(3)

where
q
is determined for the
i^{th}
bin of the data set,
P^{s}({M_{\rm bc}},{\Delta E},T_{c})
is the signal PDF in
{M_{\rm bc}}
,
{\Delta E}
, and
T_{c}
,
\mathcal{A}_{CP}
is the direct
{\it CP}
violation parameter,
\chi_{d}=0.1875\pm 0.0017
is the time-integrated
\mathit{{\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}\mathit{{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}^{0}
-mixing asymmetry,
w_{i}
is the wrong-tag fraction, and
\Delta w_{i}
is the difference in wrong tag fraction between positive and negative b-flavor tags for bin
i
. The values of
w_{i}
, and
\Delta w_{i}
are all fixed from MC. The
\mathcal{A}_{CP}
parameter is allowed to float but is not reported due to limited statistics.

8
Summary

We report the first reconstruction of the
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decay in Belle II using 62.8
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of data. An improved method of selecting photons for signal reconstruction,
photonMVA
, that utilizes a boosted decision tree rather than rectangular cuts is validated on experimental data. We find a signal yield of
14.0^{+6.8}_{-5.6}
events, corresponding to a significance of 3.4 standard deviations and determine
\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0})=(0.98^{+0.48}_{-0.39}\pm 0.27)\times 10^{-6}
, where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second, systematic. With much larger data samples that are expected in the near future, Belle II will be able to measure the direct CP violation parameter,
\mathcal{A_{CP}}(B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0})
and provide improved constraints on
\alpha/\phi_{2}
.

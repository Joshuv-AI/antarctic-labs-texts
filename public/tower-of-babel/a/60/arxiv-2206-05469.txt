On the boundedness of certain generalized Hilbert operators in ℓ p \ell^{p}
Nikolaos Athanasiou

Abstract
The Hilbert matrix ℋ n , m = ( n + m + 1 ) − 1 \mathcal{H}_{n,m}=(n+m+1)^{-1} has been extensively studied in previous literature. In this paper we look at generalized Hilbert operators arising from measures on the interval [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] , such that the Hilbert matrix is obtained by the Lebesgue measure. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for these operators to be bounded in ℓ p \ell^{p} and calculate their norm.



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation and history

The study of the classical Hilbert operator has its origins on the classical inequality of the same name.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Hilbert’s inequality)
Suppose that
(a_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
and
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
are sequences of non-negative terms and let
p,\hskip 1.42262ptq>1
be Hölder conjugates, i.e. such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Assume that
(a_{m})\in\ell^{p}
and
(b_{n})\in\ell^{q}
. Then there holds

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}\hskip 1.42262ptb_{n}}{m+n}\leq\frac{\pi}{\text{sin}\left(\frac{\pi}{p}\right)}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It was Hilbert who first proved the above for the special case
p=q=2
in one of his lectures on integral equations. His proof was published by Weyl (1908). The first proof for general
p,\hskip 1.42262ptq>1
was given by Hardy and Riesz
[
5
]
. Perhaps the best reference for the proof is
[
4
]
. A sharp version of Hilbert’s inequality is the following: For
(a_{m})_{m=0}^{\infty}
and
(b_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
both sequences of non-negative numbers such that
(a_{m})\in\ell^{p}
and
(b_{n})\in\ell^{q}
, there holds

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}\hskip 1.42262ptb_{n}}{m+n+1}\leq\frac{\pi}{\text{sin}\left(\frac{\pi}{p}\right)}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(1.1)

The Hilbert operator and its associated Hilbert matrix are now introduced as follows: Consider the operator
H
on
\ell^{p}
given by

H:(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\mapsto\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{m+n+1}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}.

The fact that this is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}
for
p>1
follows from Hilbert’s inequality as follows.

We recall that the norm of
(a_{n})\in\ell^{p}
is
\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.Moreover there must hold

\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\sup_{h\in(\ell^{p})^{*},\hskip 1.42262pt\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert h\rVert\leq 1}h((a_{n})),

where
h
is any element of the dual space
\ell^{q}
with norm
1
. Therefore

\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{p}}=\sup_{(b_{n})\in\ell^{q},\hskip 1.42262pt\hskip 1.42262pt\rVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\leq 1}\bigg\lvert\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}\bigg\rvert.

By fixing an arbitrary number
k
and taking the supremum over all sequences
(b_{n})\in\ell^{q}
such that
\lVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{q}}=k
, we arrive at the desired result.

The associated matrix

H=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{3}&\dots\\
\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{4}&\dots\\
\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{4}&\frac{1}{5}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

acting on column vectors whose entries are terms of a sequence in
\ell^{p}
is another way to look at the operator (we shall not be making a notational distinction between
H
and its associated matrix). Further historical notes and properties of the Hilbert matrix can be found in
[
1
]
.

Notice that each entry in
H
can be written as

H_{n,\hskip 1.42262ptk}=\int_{0}^{1}g_{n,k}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}t,

where
g_{n,k}(t):=\binom{n+k}{k}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{k}
.
Motivated by this integral representation, in the current paper we shall be looking at a class of generalized Hilbert operators, arising from replacing
\text{d}t
with finite, positive Borel measures. The definition is as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a finite, positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
. We define the generalized Hilbert matrix
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
by

\mathcal{C}^{\mu}_{n,k}:=\binom{n+k}{k}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{k}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t),

(1.2)

for
n,k
integers ranging from 0 to
\infty
.

The generalized Hilbert matrix can be viewed as an operator on
\ell^{p}
given by
\ell^{p}\ni(c_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\mapsto(d_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
, with

d_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}^{\mu}_{n,k}c_{k}.

(1.3)

Let us, finally, note that the corresponding question to the one addressed in this paper for the case of generalized Césaro operators was tackled in
[
3
]
and throughout this work, our general approach significantly borrows from the ideas presented in it.

1.2
Preliminaries

In this subsection we will be introducing a series of lemmata which will become relevant later on.

Lemma 1.3
.

There holds

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262pts^{n}=(1-s)^{-m-1},

for all
s\in(0,1).

Proof.

That the left-hand side converges (absolutely) is a direct consequence of the ratio test for series. One can use induction on
m
. For
m=0
, the result holds. Assume the result holds for
m=k
. Then we have

(1-s)^{-m-2}=\frac{1}{1-s}(1-s)^{-m-1}=\left(1+s+s^{2}+\dots\right)\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}s^{n}.

Since both series above converge absolutely, their product is given by the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{m+l}{m}\right)\hskip 1.42262pts^{n},

which is convergent by Mertens’ theorem on the Cauchy product of series. Therefore, all one has to show is that

\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{m+l}{m}=\binom{m+n+1}{m+1},

which itself can be shown by a straightforward induction, using the fact that

\binom{m+n+1}{m+1}+\binom{m+n+1}{m}=\binom{m+n+2}{m+1}.

∎

Lemma 1.4
.

There holds

\frac{1-t}{1-t\hskip 1.42262pte^{-x}}\geq e^{-\frac{t}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262ptx},

for
x
non-negative and
t\in[0,1)
.

Proof.

One can consider, for a fixed
t
, the function

f_{t}(x)=(1-t\hskip 1.42262pte^{-x})e^{-\frac{t}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262ptx}.

Differentiating with respect to
x
we see that the only critical point is at
x=0
, whence the result follows.
∎

Lemma 1.5
.

There holds

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{e}^{-a\hskip 1.42262ptx}x^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x=a^{-w},

for any
a>0
and
w>0
.

Proof.

As long as
a
is positive, the integral is well-defined and convergent. Recall that

\Gamma(\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x.

Setting
u=a\hskip 1.42262ptx
, we have
\text{d}x=\frac{\text{d}u}{a}
and the integral becomes

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}u^{w-1}a^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}u=a^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\Gamma(\omega).

The result follows.
∎

2
Boundedness of
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
on
\ell^{p}

In the current section we shall prove two theorems regarding the boundedness of
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
as an operator on
\ell^{p}
spaces. The first concerns the case where there are no Dirac masses at
0
or
1
, while the latter allows for the existence of such masses at the endpoints of
[0,1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
1\leq p\leq\infty
. If
\mu
has no Dirac masses at
0
or
1
, then the matrix
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is bounded as an operator from
\ell^{p}
to itself if and only if

\mathcal{N}^{\mu}_{p}:=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{\frac{1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, if that is the case, the operator norm equals
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}_{p}
(here we understand that when
p=\infty
, the integral becomes
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
).

Proof.

Suppose that
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{\frac{1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
is finite and there are no Dirac masses at
0
or
1
. We will, in the first instance, show that
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is bounded from
\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
and we will calculate its norm. We will then treat the case
p=\infty
.

We begin by defining, for a given sequence
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}
, the function
e_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

e_{n}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}t^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}.

(2.1)

We claim that
e_{n}
is well-defined on
[0,1]
and that there holds, for
t\in(0,1)
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{n}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{p}.

(2.2)

To check this, we first look at the case
p>1
. Here, Hölder’s inequality gives

e_{n}(t)\leq\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\underbrace{{\binom{n+m}{m}}^{q}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{q\hskip 1.42262ptm}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{q\hskip 1.42262ptn}}_{:=\hskip 1.42262ptB_{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(2.3)

where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, i.e.
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
.
To check that the first term on the right-hand side in (
2.3
) is convergent, the ratio test for series gives

\frac{B_{m+1}}{B_{m}}=\left(\frac{\binom{n+m+1}{m+1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m+1}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}}{\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}}\right)^{q}=\left(\frac{m+n+1}{m+1}\hskip 1.42262ptt\right)^{q}\to t^{q},

(2.4)

as
m\to\infty
. Hence, for
t\in[0,1)
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{B_{m+1}}{B_{m}}=t^{q}<t<1

and therefore the series is (absolutely) convergent. Finally, for
t=1
, the partial sums
\sum_{m=0}^{K}B_{m}=0
and hence the series equals zero. This shows that for all
t\in[0,1]
,
e_{n}(t)
is well-defined.

For
p=1
, it is enough to show that, for a fixed
t\in[0,1]
and for any fixed
n
, the sequence

c_{m}(n;t):=\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}(1-t)^{n}

is in
\ell^{\infty}
. Consider a biased coin with probability of heads
t
and probability of tails
1-t
. The probability of landing
m
heads in
(n+m)
throws for this coin is precisely
c_{m}(n;t)
. Hence each term is bounded by 1 and the result follows. This proves that
e_{n}(t)
is well-defined when
(a_{n})\in\ell^{1}
.

By Hölder’s inequality again, we have

\begin{split}e_{n}^{p}(t)&\leq\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}^{p}\right)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\right)^{p-1}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}^{p}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Summing over
n
, we get (for
0<t<1
),

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{n}^{p}&\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p-1}}\hskip 1.42262pt\binom{n+m}{m}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}^{p}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p-1}}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}^{p}\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt}a_{m}^{p},\end{split}

(2.6)

where we have used Lemma
1.3
. We now have

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{(by the D.C.T.)}\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t).\end{split}

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t),

(2.7)

or equivalently

\lVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\right)\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{p}}.

(2.8)

To show that
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu}
is the optimal constant (and hence the norm of the corresponding operator, when it is finite) we work as follows. Fix any
\eta>0
small. There exists a
\delta>0
such that

\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)>(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu}.

(2.9)

That such a
\delta
exists follows from the monotone convergence theorem, when applied for example to the functions
f_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\mathds{1}_{[\frac{1}{n},\frac{n-1}{n}]}
. We choose
c,\hskip 1.42262ptN,\hskip 1.42262pt\varepsilon
(in this order) such that

•

There holds
\left(1+\frac{1}{c}\right)^{-\frac{2}{p}}>1-\eta
.

•

There holds
N\geq\lceil\frac{c}{\delta}\rceil
, so that

\int_{\frac{c}{n}}^{1-\frac{c}{n}}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)>\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)>(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu},

for
n\geq N
.

•

There holds
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{p}
. Moreover, denoting
w=\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon
, we must choose
\varepsilon
so that the following two conditions also hold:

\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}t^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)>(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

(2.10)

and such that

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}(n+1)^{-p\hskip 1.42262ptw}>(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hskip 1.42262pt(n+1)^{-p\hskip 1.42262ptw}.

(2.11)

The latter condition can be met for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, since the
p-
series diverges for
p=1
and converges for
p>1
.

Finally, we pick the sequence
a_{n}=(n+1)^{-w}
. Using Lemma
1.5
, we have

a_{n}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-(n+1)\hskip 1.42262ptx}\hskip 1.42262ptx^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x

(2.12)

Using this and (
2.1
), we can get the following estimate for
e_{n}(t)
.

\begin{split}e_{n}(t)&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\hskip 1.42262ptx^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{e}^{-m\hskip 1.42262ptx}\text{d}x\\
&=\frac{(1-t)^{n}}{\Gamma(\omega)}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\hskip 1.42262ptx^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{e}^{-x})^{-n-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\hskip 1.42262ptx^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(\frac{1-t}{1-t\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{e}^{-x}}\right)^{n+1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\hskip 1.42262ptx^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{e}^{-(n+1)\frac{t}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262ptx}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t}\left(\frac{1+n\hskip 1.42262ptt}{1-t}\right)^{-w},\end{split}

(2.13)

where the penultimate step was obtained by Lemma
1.4
and the last step from Lemma
1.5
. When
\frac{c}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{c}{n}
, we therefore have

\begin{split}e_{n}(t)&\geq\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(1+\frac{1}{c}\right)^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt(n+1)^{-w}\\
&\geq(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}.\end{split}

(2.14)

Using (
2.14
), we have

\begin{split}b_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)&\geq\int_{\frac{c}{n}}^{1-\frac{c}{n}}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\geq(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{\frac{c}{n}}^{1-\frac{c}{n}}t^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\geq(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}t^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\geq(1-\eta)^{2}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}t^{-\frac{1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262pt(1-t)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\geq(1-\eta)^{3}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu},\hskip 8.53581pt\text{for}\hskip 2.84526ptn\geq N,\end{split}

(2.15)

hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}^{p}\geq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}b_{n}^{p}\geq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}(1-\eta)^{3\hskip 1.42262ptp}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu}\right)^{p}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}^{p}\geq(1-\eta)^{3\hskip 1.42262ptp+1}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu}\right)^{p}\hskip 1.42262pt\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{p}.

(2.16)

This establishes the ”if” direction. For the ”only if” direction, assume that
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is bounded as an operator on
\ell^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
. We claim that the integral
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\mu}
is finite. We shall make use of the same sequences as the ones just used. With the preceding notation, for an arbitrarily small but fixed
\eta>0
, we have for the sequences described above:

b_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\geq(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

(2.17)

and hence

\left(\sum b_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\begin{bmatrix}(1-\eta)\hskip 1.42262pt\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\hskip 1.42262pt\left(\sum a_{n}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.18)

From here we observe, given the boundedness of
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
, that
\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
is finite and uniformly bounded above by the operator norm as
\eta\to 0
. Moreover, it is not hard to establish that, as
\eta\to 0
, the way
\varepsilon
is chosen means that
\varepsilon\to 0
as well and hence the functions

(1-\eta)(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}\to\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{\frac{1}{p}}}

pointwise as
\eta\to 0
. Fix any
z>0
small. An application of the dominated convergence theorem on the interval
[z,1-z]
(on this fixed interval, the functions
(1-\eta)(1-t)^{w-1}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{-w}
are uniformly bounded above) now implies that the integral

\int_{z}^{1-z}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{\frac{1}{p}}}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

is finite and bounded above by the operator norm. As
z\to 0
, an application of the monotone convergence theorem gives the desired result.

We finally examine the case
p=\infty
. Assume that
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
is finite and consider a sequence
(a_{n})\in\ell^{\infty}
. We have

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\leq\begin{bmatrix}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}\\
&=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}.\end{split}

(2.19)

Taking the supremum over
n
, we have

\lVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}.

(2.20)

By taking
(a_{n})
to be a constant sequence different than the zero sequence, we see that the constant is optimal. Conversely, if the operator is bounded on
\ell^{\infty}
, picking any constant non-zero sequence
(a_{n})
, we see that the integral
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
is finite and the result follows.

∎

2.1
The case where
\mu
is allowed to have Dirac masses at
0
and/or
1

To allow for the possibility of Dirac masses at
0
or
1
, we need to be a bit more careful with semantics and notation.

2.1.1
Preliminary measure-theoretic notes

Given a measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
and a function
f
defined on
[0,1]
which is measurable with respect to
\mu
, we shall denote the integral
\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
by
\mu_{[0,1]}f
. In general, for any measurable subset
\mathcal{A}
of
[0,1]
, we will use
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}(f)
to denote the integral
\mu_{[0,1]}(f\cdot\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}})
, which is well-defined as
f\cdot\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
is
\mu-
measurable.

For a function
g
defined only on a subset
\mathcal{P}
of
[0,1]
, by a slight abuse of notation, we shall also use
\mu_{\mathcal{P}}(g)
to denote the integral

\int_{\mathcal{P}}g\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}{\mu}_{\mathcal{P}}(t),

where
\mu_{\mathcal{P}}
is the restriction of the measure on
\mathcal{P}
in the obvious way.

For a given measure
\mu
and for any function
f
on
[0,1]
, the following decomposition holds:

\mu_{[0,1]}(f)=\mu_{(0,1)}(f)+c_{0}\hskip 1.42262ptf(0)+c_{1}\hskip 1.42262ptf(1),

where
c_{1}:=\mu\left(\begin{Bmatrix}0\end{Bmatrix}\right)
and
c_{2}:=\mu\left(\begin{Bmatrix}1\end{Bmatrix}\right)
are the Dirac masses of the measure at
0
and
1
respectively. This, in turn, provides the following decomposition for the matrix
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
:

\mathcal{C}^{\mu}_{n,k}=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n,k}^{\mu}+\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{n,k}^{\mu},

(2.21)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n,k}^{\mu}:=\mu_{(0,1)}\left(g_{n,k}(t)\right)
and

\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}:=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}+c_{1}&c_{1}&c_{1}&c_{1}&\dots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\dots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\ddots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

2.1.2
Main Result

In this section we will show the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
\mu
contains Dirac masses
c_{0}
at
0
and
c_{1}
at
1
, with
\lvert c_{0}\rvert+\lvert c_{1}\rvert>0
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the only case when
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
can be a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}
is when
c_{1}\neq 0=c_{0}
and
p=1
. In that case, the operator is bounded if and only if

\int_{(0,1]}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)=\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}

is finite. Moreover, this expression, when finite, equals the norm of the operator. Finally, when
p=\infty
, the operator is bounded if and only if
c_{0}\neq 0=c_{1}
and the integral

\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

is finite. Moreover, the above, when finite, equals the norm of the operator on
\ell^{\infty}
.

Proof.

We begin by establishing the following two lemmata:

Lemma 2.3
.

If the measure
\mu
contains a Dirac mass at
0
, then the matrix is unbounded on
\ell^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

This holds for the following reason. The matrix
\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}
for
c_{0}\neq 0
does not even map
\ell^{p}
to itself, since

\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}+c_{1}&c_{1}&c_{1}&c_{1}&\dots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\dots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\ddots\\
c_{0}&0&0&0&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}\\
a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
a_{3}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(c_{0}+c_{1})\hskip 1.42262pta_{0}+c_{1}\hskip 1.42262pt(a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots)\\
c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pta_{0}\\
c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pta_{0}\\
c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pta_{0}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}

(2.22)

and clearly, as the sequence on the right-hand side eventually consists of the same non-zero (as long as
a_{0}\neq 0
) constant, it cannot be in
\ell^{p}
. Finally, since the matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}
comprises only positive entries, the sum
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}+\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}
also fails to map
\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{p}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If the measure
\mu
contains a Dirac mass at
1
and no Dirac mass at
0
, then the matrix
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is unbounded as an operator on
\ell^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
.

To establish this, notice that the first term of the sequence on the right-hand side of (
2.22
) is
c_{1}(a_{0}+a_{1}+\dots)
. In general, the
\ell^{p}
spaces are nested, in the sense that
\ell^{p}\subset\ell^{q}
for
p<q
and hence for each
p>1
there exists a sequence in
\ell^{p}
which is not in
\ell^{1}
. Since
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}
consists of only positive entries, it again follows that
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
does not map
\ell^{p}
to itself when
c_{1}\neq 0=c_{0}
and
p>1
. The only case left to examine, when the measure contains a Dirac mass, is when
c_{1}\neq 0=c_{0}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\mu}
is viewed as an operator on
\ell^{1}
.
In this case we claim that the operator is bounded if and only if

\mu_{(0,1]}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)=\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}

is finite and that, when finite, the expression above equals the operator norm.

Indeed, assume first that the expression above is finite. Proceeding as in (
2.6
), we get, for
0<t<1
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{n}(t)\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(t)

(2.23)

for all
n
. We now notice that

b_{0}=\int_{(0,1)}e_{0}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{1}}

(2.24)

and

b_{n}=\int_{(0,1)}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

(2.25)

for
n\geq 1
. Adding (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) together, we arrive at

\lVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{1}}\leq\left(\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}\right)\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{1}}

(2.26)

To show that this is the optimal constant, we again use the sequences described in the previous section. With the same notation as before, with
a_{n}=(n+1)^{-w}
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}&=b_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}=\int_{(0,1)}e_{0}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{(0,1)}e_{n}(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\\
&\geq\left((1-\eta)^{4}\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{1}\right)\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{1}}.\end{split}

(2.27)

Letting
\eta\to 0
, we obtain the desired result.

Finally, let us suppose that the operator
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is bounded on
\ell^{1}
. The same argument as in the previous case shows the finiteness of
\int_{(0,1]}\frac{1}{t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
.

We finally examine the case
p=\infty
. The proof is similar in spirit to the previous results, although a bit simpler. Indeed, if
c_{1}\neq 0
then it is clear from (
2.21
) that
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
does not map
\ell^{\infty}
to itself.

Assume that
\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)
is finite and consider a sequence
(a_{n})\in\ell^{\infty}
. We have

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\\
&\leq\begin{bmatrix}\int_{(0,1)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}+c_{0}a_{n}\\
&=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}+c_{0}a_{n}.\end{split}

(2.28)

Taking the supremum of (
2.28
) over
n
, we have

\lVert(b_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\hskip 1.42262pt\lVert(a_{n})\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}.

(2.29)

By taking
(a_{n})
to be a constant sequence different than the zero sequence, we see that the constant is optimal.

Conversely, assuming that the operator is bounded from
\ell^{\infty}
to itself, again picking a non-zero constant sequence, we see that

\begin{split}b_{n}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m}{m}\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{n}\hskip 1.42262ptt^{m}\hskip 1.42262pta_{m}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)+c_{0}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}\\
&=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)\end{bmatrix}\hskip 1.42262pta_{n}.\end{split}

(2.30)

From this, the finiteness of the operator norm implies the finiteness of

\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-t}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}\mu(t)

and the result follows.

∎

Ideals in the convolution algebra of periodic distributions
Amol Sasane

Abstract
. The ring of periodic distributions on ℝ 𝚍 \mathbb{R}^{\tt d} with usual addition and with convolution is considered. Via Fourier series expansions, this ring is isomorphic to the ring 𝒮 ′ ​ ( ℤ 𝚍 ) \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}) of all maps f : ℤ 𝚍 → ℂ f:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C} of at most polynomial growth (i.e., there exist a real M > 0 M>0 and a nonnegative integer 𝚖 {\tt m} such that for all 𝒏 = ( 𝚗 1 , ⋯ , 𝚗 𝚍 ) ∈ ℤ 𝚍 \bm{n}=({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d} , | f ⁡ ( 𝒏 ) | ≤ M ​ ( 1 + | 𝚗 1 | + ⋯ + | 𝚗 𝚍 | ) 𝚖 |f(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+|{\tt n}_{1}|+\cdots+|{\tt n}_{\tt d}|)^{\tt m} ), with pointwise operations. It is shown that finitely generated ideals in 𝒮 ′ ​ ( ℤ 𝚍 ) \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}) are principal, and ideal membership is characterised analytically. Calling an ideal in 𝒮 ′ ​ ( ℤ 𝚍 ) \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}) fixed if there is a common index 𝒏 ∈ ℤ 𝚍 \bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d} where each member vanishes, the fixed maximal ideals are described, and it is shown that not all maximal ideals are fixed. It is shown that finitely generated proper prime ideals in 𝒮 ′ ​ ( ℤ 𝚍 ) \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}) are fixed maximal ideals. The Krull dimension of 𝒮 ′ ​ ( ℤ 𝚍 ) \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}) is proved to be infinite, while the weak Krull dimension is shown to be equal to 1 1 .

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study ideals in a naturally arising ring
in harmonic analysis and distribution theory, namely the ring
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d})
of
periodic distributions with the usual addition
+
distributions, and with convolution
\ast
taken as multiplication. Via a Fourier series
expansion, the ring
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}),+,\ast)
is isomorphic to
the ring
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
consisting of all
f:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of at most polynomial growth,
with pointwise operations, and we recall this below.

1.1.
The ring of periodic
distributions.

For background on periodic distributions and its Fourier
series theory, we refer the reader to
[
2
, Chapter 16]

and
[
7
, pp.527-529]
.

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
be the set of natural numbers and
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integers. Consider the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
of all complex valued maps on

\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
of at most polynomial growth, that is,

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):=\Big\{f:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\;\Big|\;\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\textrm{ a real }M>0\;\exists\,{\tt m}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\;\textrm{ such that}\\
\forall\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d},\;|f(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}\end{array}\Big\},

where
\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,:=|{\tt n}_{1}|+\cdots+|{\tt n}_{\tt d}|
for all

\bm{n}=({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\;\!\tt d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is a unital
commutative ring with pointwise operations, and the multiplicative
unit element
1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}
is the constant function
\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\owns\bm{n}\mapstochar\rightarrow 1
. The set
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
equipped with pointwise
operations, is a commutative, unital ring. Moreover,

(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}),+,\cdot)
is isomorphic as a ring, to the ring

(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}),+,\ast)
, where

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d})
is the set of all periodic distributions
(with periods described by
{\mathbf{V}}
, see the definition below), with the usual pointwise addition of
distributions, and multiplication taken as convolution of
distributions.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of compactly supported infinitely many times differentiable complex valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}
, and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d})
the space of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}
.
For
\bm{v}\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\tt d}
, the

translation operator

{\mathbf{S}}_{{\bm{v}}}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d})
, is defined by

\langle{\mathbf{S}}_{\bm{v}}(T),\varphi\rangle=\langle T,\varphi(\cdot+\bm{v})\rangle
for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d})
.
A distribution
T\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d})
is called
periodic with a period

\bm{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
if

T={\mathbf{S}}_{\bm{v}}(T)
.
Let
{\mathbf{V}}:=\{\bm{v}_{1},\cdots,\bm{v}_{\tt d}\}
be a
linearly independent set of
{\tt d}
vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d}
. Let

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d})
denote the set
of all distributions
T
that satisfy

{\mathbf{S}}_{\bm{v}_{\tt k}}(T)=T
for all
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt d}\}
.
From
[
1
, §34]
,
T
is a tempered distribution, and from the
above it follows by taking Fourier transforms that

(1-e^{2\pi i\bm{v}_{\tt k}\cdot\bm{y}})\widehat{T}=0
, for

{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt d}\}
,
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\widehat{T}=\sum_{\bm{v}\in V^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\tt d}}\alpha_{\bm{v}}(T)\delta_{\bm{v}},

for some scalars
\alpha_{\bm{v}}(T)\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and where

V
is the matrix with its rows equal to the transposes of the column
vectors
{\bm{v}_{1},\cdots,\bm{v}_{\tt d}}
:

V^{\textrm{t}}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\bm{v}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{v}_{\tt d}\end{smallmatrix}\right],
with
V^{\textrm{t}}
denoting the transpose of the matrix
V
.
Also, in the above,
\delta_{\bm{v}}
denotes the usual Dirac
measure with support in
\bm{v}
, i.e.,

\langle\delta_{\bm{v}},\varphi\rangle=\varphi(\bm{v})
for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{R}}^{\tt d})
.
Then the Fourier coefficients
\alpha_{\bm{v}}(T)
give rise to an
element in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, and vice versa, every element in

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is the set of Fourier coefficients of some periodic
distribution. In fact, the ring

(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}),+,\ast)
of periodic distributions on

\mathbb{R}^{\tt d}
is isomorphic to the ring
(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}),+,\cdot).

In
[
5
]
, some algebraic-analytical properties of
(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}),+,\cdot)
were established; see also
[
4
]
. In this article, the structure of ideals in this ring is studied, akin to an analogous investigation in
[
8
]
for a ring of entire functions.

1.2.
Main results and organisation of the article

•

In §
2
, we show that finitely generated ideals in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
are principal,
and ideal membership is characterised analytically.

•

In §
3
, we describe fixed maximal ideals in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, and it is shown that not all maximal ideals are fixed.

•

In §
4
, we show that finitely generated proper prime ideals in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
are fixed maximal ideals. Also, the Krull dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is proved to be infinite, while the weak Krull dimension is shown to be equal to
1
.

2.
Finitely generated ideals

Proposition 2.1
.

g
is a divisor of
f
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
if and only if there exist a real number
M>0
and a nonnegative integer
{\tt m}
such that
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
,
|f(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}|g(\bm{n})|
.

Proof.

(‘If’ part:) Define
d:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

d(\bm{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{f(\bm{n})}{g(\bm{n})}&\textrm{if }g(\bm{n})\neq 0,\\
0&\textrm{if }g(\bm{n})=0.\end{array}\right.

Thus for
g(\bm{n})\neq 0
, we have
|d(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}
, and this also holds trivially when
g(\bm{n})=0
, since the left-hand side is
0
. Thus
d\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Moreover, for
g(\bm{n})\neq 0
, we have
d(\bm{n})g(\bm{n})=f(\bm{n})
, and
when
g(\bm{n})=0
, the inequality
|f(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}|g(\bm{n})|
yields
f(\bm{n})=0
too, showing that
d(\bm{n})g(\bm{n})=d(\bm{n})0=0=f(\bm{n})
. Hence
dg=f
, as wanted.

(‘Only if’ part:) Suppose that
d\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is such that
dg=f
. Since
d\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, there exist
M>0
and a nonnegative integer
{\tt m}
such that
|d(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}
. So
|f(\bm{n})|\!\leq\!|d(\bm{n})||g(\bm{n})|\!\leq\!M(1\!+\!\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}|g(\bm{n})|
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
.
∎

In particular,
f
is invertible in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
if and only if there exists a real number
\delta>0
and a nonnegative integer
{\tt m}
such that for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
,

|f(\bm{n})|\geq\delta(1+|\bm{n}|)^{-{\tt m}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Every finite number of elements
f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})

({\tt K}\in\mathbb{N})
have a greatest common divisor
d
. The element
d
is given
(
up to invertible elements
)
by
d(\bm{n})=\max\{|f_{1}(\bm{n})|,\cdots,|f_{\tt K}(\bm{n})|\}

(\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.

Proof.

Let
d(\bm{n})=\max\{|f_{1}(\bm{n})|,\cdots,|f_{\tt K}(\bm{n})|\}
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
.
Clearly
d\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. As
|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\leq|d(\bm{n})|
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
and all
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt K}\}
, Proposition
2.1
implies that
d
is a common divisor of
f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}
.

If
\widetilde{d}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is a common divisor of
f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}
, then by Proposition
2.1
again, there exist real
M_{\tt k}>0
and positive integers
{\tt m}_{\tt k}
, for each
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt K}\}
, such that

|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\leq M_{\tt k}(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{{\tt m}_{\tt k}}|\widetilde{d}(\bm{n})|
for all
{\bm{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
. Setting
M:=\max\{M_{1},\cdots,M_{\tt K}\}
and
m:=\max\{{\tt m}_{1},\cdots,{\tt m}_{\tt K}\}
, we get
|d(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}|\widetilde{d}(\bm{n})|
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
. By Proposition
2.1
,
\widetilde{d}
divides
d
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle
denote the ideal generated
{\tt K}\in\mathbb{N}
elements
f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Then

f\in\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle
if and only if there exists an
M>0
and a nonnegative integer
{\tt m}
such that

|f(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\textrm{ for all }\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}.

Proof.

(‘If’ part:) For
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt N}\}
, define

g_{\tt k}:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

g_{\tt k}(\bm{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\overline{f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})}}{\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}}f(\bm{n})&\textrm{if }{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}\neq 0,\\
0&\textrm{if }{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}=0.\end{array}\right.

If
Q(\bm{n}):={\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}\neq 0
, then
(by Cauchy-Schwarz in the last step),

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|g_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|=\frac{|\overline{f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})}|}{\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}}|f(\bm{n})|&\leq&\!\!\!\frac{\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|}{\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}}M(1\!+\!\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum\limits_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum\limits_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum\limits_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\\
&\leq&\!\!\!\frac{(\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{\tt j=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt j}(\bm{n})|^{2}}M(1\!+\!\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}\leq KM(1\!+\!\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}.\end{array}

So
g_{1},\cdots,g_{\tt K}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. We claim that

f_{1}g_{1}+\cdots+f_{\tt K}g_{\tt K}=f
. The evaluation of the left-hand side at an
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that
Q(\bm{n})\neq 0
is easily seen to be
f(\bm{n})
by the definition of
g_{1},\cdots,g_{\tt K}
. On the other hand, if
Q(\bm{n})=0
, then each
f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})=0
, and by the given inequality in the statement of the proposition, so is
f(\bm{n})=0
. Thus in this case the evaluations at
\bm{n}
of both sides of
f_{1}g_{1}+\cdots+f_{\tt K}g_{\tt K}=f
are zeroes.

(‘Only if’ part:) If
f\in\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle
, then there exist
g_{1},\cdots,g_{\tt K}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that
f=f_{1}g_{1}+\cdots+f_{\tt K}g_{\tt K}
. Let
M_{\tt k}>0
and
{\tt m}_{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt K}\}
, be such that
|g_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\leq M_{\tt k}(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{{\tt m}_{\tt k}}
(
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
). Then with
M:=\max\{M_{1},\cdots,M_{\tt K}\}
and
{\tt m}:=\max\{{\tt m}_{1},\cdots,{\tt m}_{\tt K}\}
, we get

|f(\bm{n})|\leq{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})||g_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}$}}}|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|.

∎

It follows from Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
that every finite generated ideal is principal. (Indeed,
\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle=\langle d\rangle
: That
f_{\tt k}\subset\langle d\rangle
for each
{\tt k}
is obvious as
d
divides
f_{\tt k}
, in turn showing
\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle\subset\langle d\rangle
. For the reverse inclusion,
|d(\bm{n})|\!=\!\max\{|f_{1}(\bm{n})|,\cdots,|f_{\tt K}(\bm{n})|\}\!\leq\!\sum_{{\tt k}=1}^{\tt K}|f_{\tt k}(\bm{n})|
(
\bm{n}\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
), and so by Proposition
2.3
,
d\in\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle
. Thus we get
\langle d\rangle\subset\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{\tt K}\rangle
.)

3.
Maximal ideals

Definition 3.1
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{i}
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is
fixed
if there exists an
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that for all
f\in\mathfrak{i}
,
f(\bm{k})=0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
, let
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):f(\bm{k})=0\}
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
is a fixed maximal ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Every fixed maximal ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is equal to
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
for some
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
.

Proof.

The fixedness of
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
is clear. We now show that maximality. As
1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Let
\mathfrak{i}
be an ideal such that
\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}\subsetneq\mathfrak{i}
. Suppose that
f\in\mathfrak{i}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
.
Then
f(\bm{k})\neq 0
. Define
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
by

g=1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}-\frac{f}{f(\bm{k})}
. As
g(\bm{k})=0
, we have
g\in\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}\subset\mathfrak{i}
. Also,
\frac{f}{f(\bm{k})}\in\mathfrak{i}
. Thus

1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}=g+\frac{f}{f(\bm{k})}\in\mathfrak{i}
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{i}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.

Next, let
\mathfrak{m}
be a fixed maximal ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Since
\mathfrak{m}
is fixed, there exists a
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that

\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. By the maximality of
\mathfrak{m}
, we conclude that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
.
∎

Example 3.3

(Non-fixed maximal ideals)
.

Let
({\tt k}_{\tt j})_{{\tt j}\in\mathbb{N}}
be any subsequence of the sequence of natural numbers.
Set
\bm{k}_{\tt j}=({\tt k}_{\tt j},\cdots,{\tt k}_{\tt j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
.
Define
\mathfrak{i}:=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):\lim_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}e^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}f(\bm{k}_{\tt j})=0\}.

Then
\mathfrak{i}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. (It is clear that if
f,g\in\mathfrak{i}
, then
f+g\in\mathfrak{i}
. If
f\in\mathfrak{i}
and
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, then there exist a real
M>0
and an
{\tt m}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that

|g(\bm{n})|\leq M(1+\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,\bm{n}\,\rule[-2.58334pt]{1.30005pt}{9.90276pt}\,)^{\tt m}
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
,
and so

|(fg)(\bm{k}_{\tt j})|\leq|f(\bm{k}_{\tt j})|M(1+{\tt d}{\tt k}_{\tt j})^{\tt m}=e^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}|f(\bm{k}_{\tt j})|e^{-{\tt k}_{\tt j}}M(1+{\tt d}{\tt k}_{\tt j})^{\tt m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}0,

showing that
fg\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.) Moreover,
\mathfrak{i}\neq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
since
1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}\not\in\mathfrak{i}
:
e^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}|1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}(\bm{k}_{\tt j})|=e^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}1>1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence there exists a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that
\mathfrak{i}\subset\mathfrak{m}
. We note that for each
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
,
\mathfrak{m}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
: Define
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}(\bm{n})=0
for all
\bm{n}\neq\bm{k}
and
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}(\bm{k})=1
. Then
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}\in\mathfrak{i}\subset\mathfrak{m}
, but
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}\not\in\mathfrak{m}_{\bm{k}}
as
1_{\{\bm{k}\}}(\bm{k})=1\neq 0
.

4.
Prime ideals

4.1.
Finitely generated proper prime ideals

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a finitely generated proper
prime ideal of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.
Then there exists an
\bm{n}_{*}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that

\mathfrak{p}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):f(\bm{n}_{*})=0\}.

Proof.

We carry out the proof in several steps.

Step 1.
\mathfrak{p}
is principal. If
\mathfrak{p}\!=\!\langle d\rangle
, then
d
has at most one zero.
As
\mathfrak{p}
is finitely generated, it is principal. Let
d\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
be such that
\mathfrak{p}=\langle d\rangle
.
Let
\bm{m},\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
be distinct and
d(\bm{n})=0=d(\bm{m})
. Define
a:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
a(\bm{k})=1
for all
\bm{k}\neq\bm{n}
and
a(\bm{n})=0
.
Then
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Also, let
b:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be defined by
b(\bm{k})=d(\bm{k})
for all
\bm{k}\not\in\{\bm{m},\bm{n}\}
,
b(\bm{m})=0
and
b(\bm{n})=1
. Then clearly
b\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
too (since it matches with
d
everywhere except at the single index
\bm{n}
). Now
(ab)(\bm{k})=d(\bm{k})
for all
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
: If
\bm{k}\not\in\{\bm{m},\bm{n}\}
this is clear from the definitions since the left-hand side is
1\cdot d(\bm{k})
, and if
\bm{k}=\bm{m}
or
\bm{n}
, then both sides are
0
. So
ab=d\in\langle d\rangle=\mathfrak{p}
. But
a\not\in\langle d\rangle
since otherwise
a=d\tilde{a}
for some
\tilde{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
and then
1=a(\bm{m})=d(\bm{m})\tilde{a}(\bm{m})=0\tilde{a}(\bm{m})=0
, a contradiction. Also,
b\not\in\langle d\rangle
since otherwise
b=d\tilde{b}
for some
\tilde{b}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
and then
1=b(\bm{n})=d(\bm{n})\tilde{b}(\bm{n})=0\tilde{b}(\bm{m})=0
, a contradiction.
So neither
a
nor
b
belong to
\langle d\rangle=\mathfrak{p}
, contradicting the primality of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Step 2.
Let

\textstyle\mathfrak{p}\!=\!\langle d\rangle

(as in Step 1). For each

\textstyle\bm{n}\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}

, let

\textstyle d(\bm{n})\!=\!|d(\bm{n})|e^{i\theta(\bm{n})}

for some
\theta(\bm{n})\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Define
h\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
by

\textstyle h(\bm{n})=\sqrt{|d(\bm{n})|}e^{i\theta(\bm{n})/2}

for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
.
Then
h^{2}=d\in\mathfrak{p}
, and as
\mathfrak{p}
is prime,
h\in\mathfrak{p}
.

Step 3.
d
has exactly one zero.
We will now show that there exists an
\bm{n}_{*}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that
d(\bm{n}_{*})=0
.
Suppose this is not true. Then by Step 1,
d(\bm{n})\neq 0
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
. If
h\in\mathfrak{p}
is as in Step 2, then there exists a
k\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that
h=kd
, i.e.,
\sqrt{|d(\bm{n})|}e^{i\theta(\bm{n})/2}=|d(\bm{n})|e^{i\theta(\bm{n})}k(\bm{n})
, which yields
1=d(\bm{n})(k(\bm{n}))^{2}
(
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
). Thus
dk^{2}=1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}
, showing that
d
is invertible in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, contradicting the properness of the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
.

Step 4. We now show that
\mathfrak{p}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):f(\bm{n}_{*})=0\}
.
That
\mathfrak{p}\subset\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):f(\bm{n}_{*})=0\}
is clear. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
be such that
f(\bm{n}_{*})=0
. Define
g:\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

g(\bm{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{f(\bm{n})}{d(\bm{n})}&\textrm{if }\bm{n}\neq\bm{n}_{*},\\
0&\textrm{if }\bm{n}=\bm{n}_{*}.\end{array}\right.

Then
f=dg
(note that
f(\bm{n})=d(\bm{n})g(\bm{n})
for
\bm{n}\neq\bm{n}_{*}
follows from the definition of
g
, and
f(\bm{n}_{*})=d(\bm{n}_{*})g(\bm{n}_{*})
too since both sides are
0
). As the
h
from Step 2 is in
\mathfrak{p}
, there exists a
k\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Z^{\tt d})
such that
h=kd
, and so for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
, we get
\sqrt{|d(\bm{n})|}e^{i\theta(\bm{n})/2}=|d(\bm{n})|e^{i\theta(\bm{n})}k(\bm{n})
, giving
1=|d(\bm{n})||k(\bm{n})|^{2}
.
Hence for
\bm{n}\neq\bm{n}_{*}
,
\frac{1}{|d(\bm{n})|}=|k(\bm{n})|^{2}\leq M(1+|\bm{n}|)^{\tt m}
for some
M>0
and a nonnegative integer
{\tt m}
. This estimate shows that
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, and hence
f=gd\in d\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})=\langle d\rangle=\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

4.2.
Krull dimension

Definition 4.2
.

The
Krull dimension
of a commutative ring
R
is the
supremum of the lengths of chains of distinct proper prime ideals of

R
.

Recall that the Hardy algebra
H^{\infty}
is the Banach algebra of bounded and holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, with pointwise operations and the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. In
[
9
]
, von Renteln showed that the Krull dimension of

H^{\infty}
is infinite. We adapt the idea given in
[
9
]
,
to show that the Krull dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is infinite too.
A key ingredient of the proof in
[
9
]
was the use of a canonical factorisation of
H^{\infty}
elements used to create ideals with zeroes at prescribed locations with prescribed multiplicities. Instead, we will look at the zero set in
\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, and use the notion of ‘zero-order’ introduced below.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
and
\bm{n}=({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
is such that
f(\bm{n})=0
, then we define the
zero-order
m(f,\bm{n})
by

m(f,\bm{n})\!=\!\min_{1\leq{\tt k}\leq{\tt d}}\max{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\Big\{$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\Big\{$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\Big\{$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\Big\{$}}}{\tt i}\!\in\!\mathbb{N}:\!\!{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}f({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{{\tt k}-1},{\tt n}_{\tt k}\!+\!{\tt j},{\tt n}_{{\tt k}+1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})=0\\
\textrm{ whenever }0\leq{\tt j}\leq{\tt i}-1\end{array}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\begin{array}[]{ll}f({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{{\tt k}-1},{\tt n}_{\tt k}\!+\!{\tt j},{\tt n}_{{\tt k}+1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})=0\\
\textrm{ whenever }0\leq{\tt j}\leq{\tt i}-1\end{array}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\begin{array}[]{ll}f({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{{\tt k}-1},{\tt n}_{\tt k}\!+\!{\tt j},{\tt n}_{{\tt k}+1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})=0\\
\textrm{ whenever }0\leq{\tt j}\leq{\tt i}-1\end{array}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\begin{array}[]{ll}f({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{{\tt k}-1},{\tt n}_{\tt k}\!+\!{\tt j},{\tt n}_{{\tt k}+1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})=0\\
\textrm{ whenever }0\leq{\tt j}\leq{\tt i}-1\end{array}$}}}\!\!{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\Big\}$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\Big\}$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\Big\}$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\Big\}$}}}.

If
f({\tt n}_{1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{{\tt k}-1},{\tt n}_{\tt k}\!+\!{\tt j},{\tt n}_{{\tt k}+1},\cdots,{\tt n}_{\tt d})=0
for all
{\tt j}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and all
{\tt k}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt d}\}
, then we set
m(f,\bm{n})=\infty
. If
f(\bm{n})\neq 0
, then we set
m(f,\bm{n})=0
. Analogous to the multiplicity of a zero of a (not identically vanishing) holomorphic function, the zero-order satisfies the following property.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textrm{(P1): If }f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})\textrm{ and }\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d},\\
\phantom{(P1):}\textrm{then }m(f+g,\bm{n})\geq\min\{m(f,\bm{n}),m(g,\bm{n})\}.\end{array}

The multiplicity of a zero
\zeta
of the pointwise product of two holomorphic functions is the sum of the multiplicities of
\zeta
as a zero of each of the two holomorphic functions. For the zero-order, we have the following instead:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textrm{(P2): If }f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})\textrm{ and }\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d},\\
\phantom{(P1):}\textrm{then }m(fg,\bm{n})\geq\max\{m(f,\bm{n}),m(g,\bm{n})\}.\end{array}

We will use the following known result; see
[
3
, Theorem, §0.16, p.6]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\mathfrak{i}
is an ideal in a ring
R,

M\subset R
is a set that is closed
under multiplication, and
M\cap\mathfrak{i}=\emptyset
, then there exists an
ideal
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathfrak{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}\cap M=\emptyset,
and
\mathfrak{p}

maximal with respect to these properties. Moreover
,
such an ideal
\mathfrak{p}

is necessarily prime.

Theorem 4.4
.

The Krull dimension of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is infinite.

Proof.

For
{\tt i}\in\{1,\cdots,{\tt d}\}
, let
\bm{e}_{\tt i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
be the vector all of whose components are zeroes except for the
{\tt i}^{\textrm{th}}
one, which is defined as
1
. For
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
, define
f_{\tt n}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
by

\displaystyle\!\!\!f_{\tt n}(2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1}\!+\!{\tt j}\bm{e}_{\tt i})=0\textrm{ if }{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\,1\!\leq\!{\tt i}\!\leq\!{\tt d},\,0\!\leq\!{\tt j}\!\leq\!{\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1},

\displaystyle\!\!\!f_{\tt n}(\bm{m})=1\textrm{ if }\bm{m}\not\in\{2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1}\!+\!{\tt j}\bm{e}_{\tt i}:{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\,1\!\leq\!{\tt i}\!\leq\!{\tt d},\,0\!\leq\!{\tt j}\!\leq\!{\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1}\}.

Note that
m(f_{\tt n},2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})\geq{\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1}
, but
for each fixed
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
{\tt K}_{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that the gap between the indices,

2^{{\tt k}+1}-2^{\tt k}=2^{\tt k}>{\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1}\textrm{ for all }{\tt k}>{\tt K}_{\tt n},

and so
m(f_{\tt n},2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})={\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1}
for all
{\tt k}>{\tt K}_{\tt n}
.
Hence

\lim\limits_{{\tt k}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m(f_{\tt n},2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}=\infty\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\lim\limits_{{\tt k}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m(f_{\tt n},2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{{\tt n}+1}}=1<\infty.

(1)

Let

\mathfrak{i}_{*}:=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):\exists\,{\tt k}_{0}(f)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\textrm{ such that }\forall\,{\tt k}>{\tt k}_{0}(f),\;f(2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})=0\}.

The set
\mathfrak{i}_{*}
is nonempty since
0\in\mathfrak{i}_{*}
. Clearly
\mathfrak{i}_{*}
is closed under
addition, and
fg\in\mathfrak{i}_{*}
whenever
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{*}
and
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. So
\mathfrak{i}_{*}
is
an ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. For
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Big\{f\in\mathfrak{i}_{*}:\lim\limits_{{\tt k}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}}=\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle M_{\tt n}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):\sup\limits_{{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}}<\infty\Big\}.

Clearly
f_{\tt n}\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
, and so
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
is not empty. Using
(P1), we see that if
f,g\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
, then
f+g\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
.
If
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
and
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
, then (P2) implies that
fg\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
. Hence
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.

The identity element
1_{\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}}\in M_{\tt n}
for all
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f,g\in M_{\tt n}
, then it follows from (P2) that
fg\in M_{\tt n}
. Thus

M_{\tt n}
is a nonempty multiplicatively closed subset of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.

It is easy to check that for all
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt n}+1}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
and
M_{\tt n}\subset M_{{\tt n}+1}
.
We now prove that the inclusions are strict for each
{\tt n}\in\mathbb{N}
. From
(
1
), it follows that
f_{\tt n}\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
but
f_{\tt n}\not\in\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt n}+1}
. Also
f_{\tt n}\in M_{{\tt n}+1}
and
f_{\tt n}\not\in M_{\tt n}
.

Next we show that
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}\cap M_{\tt n}=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}\cap M_{\tt n}
, then

\infty=\lim\limits_{{\tt k}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}}=\limsup\limits_{{\tt k}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}}\leq\sup\limits_{{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{\tt k}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}^{\tt n}}$}}}<\infty,

a contradiction. But
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}\cap M_{{\tt n}+1}\neq\emptyset
, since
f_{\tt n}\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt n}
and
f_{\tt n}\in M_{{\tt n}+1}
.

We will now show that the Krull dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is infinite by showing that
for all
{\tt N}\in\mathbb{N}
, we can construct a chain of strictly decreasing prime ideals

\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\subset\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{2}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{1}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.

Fix an
{\tt N}\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying Proposition
4.3
by taking
\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}+1}
and

M=M_{{\tt N}+1}
, we obtain the existence of a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
in

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
, which satisfies
\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}+1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\cap M_{{\tt N}+1}=\emptyset
.

We claim the ideal
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
satisfies

\textstyle(\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1})\cap M_{\tt N}=\emptyset

. Let
h=f+g\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
, where
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}
and
g\in\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
. Since
g\in\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
, by the construction
of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{{\tt N}+1}
it follows that
g\not\in M_{{\tt N}+1}
. But
M_{\tt N}\subset M_{{\tt N}+1}
, and so
g\not\in M_{\tt N}
as well. Thus there exists a
subsequence
({\tt k}_{\tt j})_{\tt j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
({\tt k})_{{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}}=\infty.

From (P1), we obtain

{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}}\geq\min\Big\{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}}\Big\}.

As
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}
, it follows that

\sup\limits_{{\tt j}\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}}\geq\min\Big\{\limsup\limits_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}},\limsup\limits_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{\tt N}}$}}}\Big\}\geq\infty.

Thus
h\not\in M_{\tt N}
. Consequently,
(\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1})\cap M_{\tt N}=\emptyset
.

Clearly
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
.
Applying Proposition
4.3
again, now taking
\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
and

M=M_{\tt N}
, we obtain the existence of a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
in

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that
\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\cap M_{\tt N}=\emptyset
.
Thus
\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
.
The first inclusion is strict as
f_{\tt N}\in\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{\tt N}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
. But
f_{\tt N}\not\in\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
(since
f_{\tt N}\in M_{{\tt N}+1}
and

\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\cap M_{{\tt N}+1}=\emptyset
by the construction of
\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}
). Thus
\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
.

Now consider the ideal
\mathfrak{i}:=\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\supset\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
and the multiplicatively closed set
M:=M_{{\tt N}-1}
of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
. Similar to the argument given above, we show below that
\mathfrak{i}\cap M=(\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N})\cap M_{{\tt N}-1}=\emptyset
.

Let
h=f+g\in\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}}
, where
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}
and
g\in\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
. Since
g\in\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
, by the construction
of
\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
,
g\not\in M_{\tt N}\supset M_{{\tt N}-1}
, and so
g\not\in M_{{\tt N}-1}
. Thus there exists a subsequence
({\tt k}_{\tt j})_{{\tt j}\in\mathbb{N}}
of
({\tt k})_{{\tt k}\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim\limits_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}}=\infty.

As
f\in\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}
,

\sup\limits_{{\tt j}\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(h,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}}\geq\min\Big\{\limsup\limits_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(f,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}},\limsup\limits_{{\tt j}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.87}{$\displaystyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\textstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}{\scalebox{0.87}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{m(g,2^{{\tt k}_{\tt j}}\bm{e}_{1})}{{\tt k}_{\tt j}^{{\tt N}-1}}$}}}\Big\}\geq\infty.

Thus
h\not\in M_{{\tt N}-1}
. So
(\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N})\cap M_{{\tt N}-1}=\emptyset
.

By Proposition
4.3
, taking
\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\supset\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}
and
M=M_{{\tt N}-1}
, there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}-1}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that

\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}-1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}-1}\cap M_{N-1}=\emptyset
.
Thus
\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}-1}
, and again the first inclusion is strict (because
f_{{\tt N}-1}\in\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}\subset\mathfrak{i}_{{\tt N}-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}
,
f_{{\tt N}-1}\in M_{\tt N}
and
M_{\tt N}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}=\emptyset
).

Proceeding in this manner, we obtain the chain of distinct prime ideals

\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}+1}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{\tt N}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{{\tt N}-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{1}.
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
.
As
{\tt N}\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary, it follows that the Krull dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is infinite.
∎

4.3.
Weak Krull dimension

Recall the following definition from
[
6
]
:

Definition 4.5
.

The
weak Krull dimension
of a commutative ring
R
is the supremum of the lengths of chains of distinct proper finitely generated prime ideals of
R
.

Theorem 4.6
.

The weak Krull dimension of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is
1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
be finitely generated proper prime ideals in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{2}
.
For each
{\tt i}\in\{1,2\}
, by Proposition
2.2
, there exists an
\bm{n}_{\tt i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{\tt i}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}):f(\bm{n}_{\tt i})=0\}
.
But as
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, it follows that
\bm{n}_{1}=\bm{n}_{2}
(by considering the function which is zero at all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d}\setminus\{\bm{n}_{2}\}
and equal to
1
at
\bm{n}_{2}
), and so
\mathfrak{p}_{1}=\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. So the weak Krull dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{\tt d})
is
1
.
∎

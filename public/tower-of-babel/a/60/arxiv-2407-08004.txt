Vyjayanthi Chari a , Lauren Grimley b , Zongzhu Lin c , Chad R. Mangum d , Christine Uhl e , Evan Wilson f

Abstract
We state and prove an analog of the Schur-Weyl duality for a quotient of the classical 2 2 -toroidal Lie algebra of type A A . We then provide a method to extend this duality to the m m -toroidal case, m > 2 m>2 .

[

[

,

,

,
[

,

. The duality is expressed either as a double-centralizer theorem or as an equivalence of categories for certain values of

Finite dimensional representations of loop algebras have been studied extensively. These representations are interesting because they are in general not completely reducible.
There are many interesting families of indecomposable
finite dimensional representations of loop algebras such as Kirillov-Reshetikhin modules which originally arose in the study of integrable models. Other well–known examples are the local Weyl modules, which were originally studied in
[
5
]

There are two main results in this paper. The first establishes a Schur-Weyl duality between finite dimensional representations of the two-loop algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1},s^{\pm 1}]

The paper is structured as follows. We first recall in §

4.2
Gluing representations for multiloop Lie algebras from loop Lie algebras

The following is elementary and will be useful for what follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A_{i}

, and consider

Let

Definition 10

such that
\rho_{i}=\rho\circ\nu_{i}
.

Proof.

We will prove this by induction on
m
. The statement is obvious when
m=1
. Assuming the statement for
m=k-1
we prove the result for case
m=k
. Let
\sigma\in S_{\ell}
and
\mathbf{r}\in\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\right)^{k}
with
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
. Using the inductive assumption, define

\rho(\mathbf{r},\sigma):=\rho\big((r_{1},\ldots,r_{k-1}),1\big)\rho_{k}(r_{k},\sigma).

The fact that
\rho
is well-defined, unique, and satisfies the condition
\rho_{i}=\rho\circ\nu_{i}
follow from the definition once we prove that
\rho
is shown to be a homomorphism of groups. This is done as follows: let
\tau\in S_{\ell}
and
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{k})
. Then

\displaystyle\rho((\mathbf{r},\sigma)(\mathbf{q},\tau))

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{r}+\sigma(\mathbf{q}),\sigma\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho\big((r_{1}+\sigma(q_{1}),\ldots,r_{k-1}+\sigma(q_{k-1})),1\big)\rho_{k}(r_{k}+\sigma(q_{k}),\sigma\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1}+\sigma(q_{1}),1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(r_{k-1}+\sigma(q_{k-1}),1)\rho_{k}(r_{k}+\sigma(q_{k}),\sigma\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)\rho_{1}(\sigma(q_{1}),1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(r_{k-1},1)\rho_{k-1}(\sigma(q_{k-1}),1)\rho_{k}(r_{k},1)\rho_{k}(\sigma(q_{k}),\sigma\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k}(r_{k},1)\rho_{1}(\sigma(q_{1}),1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(\sigma(q_{k-1}),1)\rho_{k}(\sigma(q_{k}),\sigma\tau)\hskip 56.9055pt(*)

where the third equality follows from the inductive definition of
\rho
, and the last equality from condition (ii). Now note that, for any
j\in[1,k]
, by condition (i) we have
\rho_{0}(\sigma)\rho_{j}(q_{j},1)=\rho_{j}(\sigma(q_{j}),\sigma)=\rho_{j}(\sigma(q_{j}),1)\rho_{0}(\sigma)
. Thus
(*)
is

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)

\displaystyle\cdots\rho_{k}(r_{k},1)\rho_{1}(\sigma(q_{1}),1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(\sigma(q_{k-1}),1)\rho_{0}(\sigma)\rho_{k}(q_{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k}(r_{k},1)\rho_{1}(\sigma(q_{1}),1)\cdots\rho_{0}(\sigma)\rho_{k-1}(q_{k-1},1)\rho_{k}(q_{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k}(r_{k},1)\rho_{0}(\sigma)\rho_{1}(q_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(q_{k-1},1)\rho_{k}(q_{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(r_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(r_{k-1},1)\rho_{k}(r_{k},\sigma)\rho_{1}(q_{1},1)\cdots\rho_{k-1}(q_{k-1},1)\rho_{k}(q_{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho\big((r_{1},\ldots,r_{k-1}),1\big)\rho_{k}(r_{k},\sigma)\rho\big((q_{1},\ldots,q_{k-1}),1\big)\rho_{k}(q_{k},\tau)

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{r},\sigma)\rho(\mathbf{q},\tau).

∎

Corollary 12
.

Let
M
be an
S_{\ell}
–module over
\mathbb{C}
. Assume that there is an
\operatorname{Aff}_{i}(S_{\ell})
–module structure on
M
, denoted by
M_{i}
, extending the
S_{\ell}
–module structure on
M
. Then there is a unique
\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
–module structure on
M
whose restriction to
\operatorname{Aff}_{i}(S_{\ell})
via
\nu_{i}
is
M_{i}
.

Proof.

Define an
\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
–module structure on
M
by declaring that
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m},\sigma)
act as
(n_{1},1)(n_{2},1)\cdots(n_{m},1)(0,\sigma)
with each
(n_{i},1)
denoting the
M_{i}
structure and
(0,\sigma)
the
S_{\ell}
–module structure. Since
(0,\sigma)(n,1)=(\sigma(n),\sigma)=(\sigma(n),1)(0,\sigma)
, we have that the product
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m},\sigma)(n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{m},\sigma^{\prime})=(n_{1}+\sigma(n^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,n_{m}+\sigma(n^{\prime}_{m}),\sigma\sigma^{\prime})
, and thus this action does indeed define an
\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
–module structure. That its restriction to
\operatorname{Aff}_{i}(S_{\ell})
via
\nu_{i}
is
M_{i}
is obvious. Uniqueness is a consequence of Lemma
4.1
(b) since the
\operatorname{Aff}_{i}(S_{\ell})
generate
\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
as an associative algebra.
∎

4.4
The Schur-Weyl duality for multiloop Lie algebras of type
A

We prove the main theorem of this section. It shows we can extend a known Schur-Weyl duality in the single loop case to the multiloop case by “gluing together several loops” at once, and it gives an alternative but less explicit way to extend Schur-Weyl duality to the toroidal setting. However this method is less reliant on the presentation of toroidal algebras as the affinization of affine algebras.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\ell,m\geq 1
be integers with
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
and
\ell\leq n
. Then

(a)

For any
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})]
–module
M

there is a unique
L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})
–module structure on
\mathcal{F}(M)=M\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[S_{\ell}]}V^{\otimes{\ell}}
extending the
L_{i}(\mathfrak{g})
–module structures given by Theorem
3
.

(b)

\mathcal{F}:\operatorname{Mod-}\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})]\to L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{-Mod}
is a fully faithful functor.

(c)

Let
L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{-Mod}^{\ell}
be the full subcategory of all
L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})
–modules whose restriction to
\mathfrak{g}
via
\iota_{0}^{m}
is locally finite dimensional and all composition factors are the composition factors of
V^{\otimes{\ell}}
. Then
\mathcal{F}:\operatorname{Mod-}\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})]\to L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})\operatorname{-Mod}^{\ell}
is a category equivalence.

Proof.

(a)

By Corollary
12
we see that the restricted
\operatorname{Aff}_{i}(S_{\ell})
–module structures
M_{i},1\leq i\leq m
combine to give a unique
\operatorname{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
–module structure, which must therefore be
M
. Let
L=\mathfrak{gl}\left(\mathcal{F}(M)\right)
, and define
\rho_{i}:L_{i}(\mathfrak{g})\to L,1\leq i\leq m
by
\rho_{i}(x\otimes t_{i}^{n_{i}})(m\otimes\mathbf{v})=(x\otimes t_{i}^{n_{i}}).(m\otimes\mathbf{v})
and check that the
\rho_{i}
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
8
.

(i)

For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\rho_{i}\circ\iota_{i,0}(x)=\rho_{i}(x\otimes t_{i}^{0})=\rho_{j}(x\otimes t_{j}^{0})=\rho_{j}\circ\iota_{j,0}(x)
.

(ii)

We show this in two steps. First, we show that for any sequence
(i_{1},\dots,i_{c})
of elements of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
and for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{c}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\big([\rho_{i_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{i_{1}}^{p_{1}}),[\rho_{i_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{i_{2}}^{p_{2}}),\cdots[\rho_{i_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{i_{c-1}}^{p_{c-1}}),\rho_{i_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{i_{c}}^{p_{c}})]\cdots]]\big).(m\otimes\mathbf{v})

=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},j}^{p_{1}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},j}^{p_{2}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},j}^{p_{c}}\otimes[x_{1},[x_{2},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]_{j}.\mathbf{v}\right)

where
\mathbf{y}_{i,j}^{p},1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq\ell,p\in\mathbb{Z}
is shorthand for
(p\mathbf{e}_{ij},1)\in\text{Aff}^{m}(S_{\ell})
where
\mathbf{e}_{ij}
is the
m
-tuple with the unit vector
\mathbf{e}_{j}
in the
i
th position and 0’s elsewhere. We use induction on
c
with the
c=1
case following from Theorem
3
. We compute:

\displaystyle\big([\rho_{i_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{i_{1}}^{p_{1}}),[\rho_{i_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{i_{2}}^{p_{2}}),\cdots[\rho_{i_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{i_{c-1}}^{p_{c-1}}),\rho_{i_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{i_{c}}^{p_{c}})]\cdots]]\big).(m\otimes\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\rho_{i_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{i_{1}}^{p_{1}})\left(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},j}^{p_{2}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{3},j}^{p_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},j}^{p_{c}}\otimes[x_{2},[x_{3},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]_{j}.\mathbf{v}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}[\rho_{i_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{i_{2}}^{p_{2}}),\cdots[\rho_{i_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{i_{c-1}}^{p_{c-1}}),\rho_{i_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{i_{c}}^{p_{c}})]\cdots]\left(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},k}^{p_{1}}\otimes(x_{1})_{k}.\mathbf{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\big(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},j}^{p_{2}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{3},j}^{p_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},j}^{p_{c}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},k}^{p_{1}}\otimes(x_{1})_{k}[x_{2},[x_{3},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]_{j}.\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\qquad-m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},k}^{p_{1}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},j}^{p_{2}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{3},j}^{p_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},j}^{p_{c}}\otimes[x_{2},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]_{j}(x_{1})_{k}.\mathbf{v}\big).

The last line is 0 unless
j=k
, in which case it becomes:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\big(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},j}^{p_{1}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},j}^{p_{2}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{3},j}^{p_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},j}^{p_{c}}\otimes[x_{1},[x_{2},[x_{3},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]]_{j}.\mathbf{v}\big).

It follows that

\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\in\mathfrak{g}}[\rho_{i_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{i_{1}}^{p_{1}}),[\rho_{i_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{i_{2}}^{p_{2}}),\cdots[\rho_{i_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{i_{c-1}}^{p_{c-1}}),\rho_{i_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{i_{c}}^{p_{c}})]\cdots]]=0

if

\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{c}\in\mathfrak{g}}[x_{1},[x_{2},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]=0.

(iii)

As in the previous step, we see that

\displaystyle\big([\rho_{i_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}),[\rho_{i_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}),\cdots[\rho_{i_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{i_{c-1}}^{p_{i_{c-1}}}),\rho_{i_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{i_{c}}^{p_{i_{c}}})]\cdots]]\big).(m\otimes\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\big(m.\mathbf{y}_{i_{1},k}^{p_{i_{1}}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{2},k}^{p_{i_{2}}}\mathbf{y}_{i_{3},k}^{p_{i_{3}}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{i_{c},k}^{p_{i_{c}}}\otimes[x_{1},[x_{2},[x_{3},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]]_{k}.\mathbf{v}\big).

If
(j_{1},\dots,j_{c})
is a permutation of
(i_{1},\dots i_{c})
then

\displaystyle\big([\rho_{j_{1}}(x_{1}\otimes t_{j_{1}}^{p_{j_{1}}}),[\rho_{j_{2}}(x_{2}\otimes t_{j_{2}}^{p_{j_{2}}}),\cdots[\rho_{j_{c-1}}(x_{c-1}\otimes t_{j_{c-1}}^{p_{j_{c-1}}}),\rho_{j_{c}}(x_{c}\otimes t_{j_{c}}^{p_{j_{c}}})]\cdots]]\big).(m\otimes\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\big(m.\mathbf{y}_{j_{1},k}^{p_{j_{1}}}\mathbf{y}_{j_{2},k}^{p_{j_{2}}}\mathbf{y}_{j_{3},k}^{p_{j_{3}}}\cdots\mathbf{y}_{j_{c},k}^{p_{j_{c}}}\otimes[x_{1},[x_{2},[x_{3},\cdots[x_{c-1},x_{c}]\cdots]]]_{k}.\mathbf{v}\big)

which is the same element of
M\otimes V^{\otimes\ell}
since the
\mathbf{y}_{i,j}
commute.

Therefore, the
\rho_{i}
thus defined satisfy the conditions of Proposition
8
. The existence of the
L^{m}(\mathfrak{g})
–module structure follows from Proposition
8
, and uniqueness follows from Lemma
4.1
.

(b)

As the functor is fully faithful in the
m=1
case, this statement follows from Theorem
3
, Proposition
8
, and Lemma
4.1
.

(c)

We have the restrictions
\mathcal{F}(M)\to\left(\mathcal{F}(M)|_{L_{i}(\mathfrak{g})}\right)\big|_{\mathfrak{g}}
via
\iota_{0}^{m}
which each satisfy the stated conditions. Then the statement follows from Theorem
3
, Proposition
8
, and Lemma
4.1
.

∎

Fluctuations of non-ergodic stochastic processes
G. George, L. Klochko, A.N. Semenov, J. Baschnagel, J.P. Wittmer

Abstract
We investigate the standard deviation δ ​ v ​ ( Δ ​ t ) \delta v(\Delta t) of the variance v ⁡ [ 𝐱 ] v[\mathbf{x}] of time series 𝐱 \mathbf{x} measured over a finite sampling time Δ ​ t \Delta t focusing on non-ergodic systems where independent “configurations” c c get trapped in meta-basins of a generalized phase space. It is thus relevant in which order averages over the configurations c c and over time series k k of a configuration c c are performed. Three variances of v ⁡ [ 𝐱 c ​ k ] v[\mathbf{x}_{ck}] must be distinguished: the total variance δ ​ v tot 2 = δ ​ v int 2 + δ ​ v ext 2 \delta v^{2}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\delta v^{2}_{\mathrm{int}}+\delta v^{2}_{\mathrm{ext}} and its contributions δ ​ v int 2 \delta v^{2}_{\mathrm{int}} , the typical internal variance within the meta-basins, and δ ​ v ext 2 \delta v^{2}_{\mathrm{ext}} , characterizing the dispersion between the different basins. We discuss simplifications for physical systems where the stochastic variable x ⁡ ( t ) x(t) is due to a density field averaged over a large system volume V V . The relations are illustrated for the shear-stress fluctuations in quenched elastic networks and low-temperature glasses formed by polydisperse particles and free-standing polymer films. The different statistics of δ ​ v int \delta v_{\mathrm{int}} and δ ​ v ext \delta v_{\mathrm{ext}} are manifested by their different system-size dependences.

2.1
Some notations

To state compactly the expressions developed below it is useful to introduce a few notations.
The
l
-average operator

\mathbf{E}^{l}{\cal O}_{lmn\ldots}\equiv\frac{1}{n_{l}}\sum_{l=1}^{n_{l}}{\cal O}_{lmn\ldots}\equiv{\cal O}_{mn\ldots}(n_{l})

The

\mathbf{V}^{l}{\cal O}_{lmn\ldots}\equiv\frac{1}{n_{l}}\sum_{l=1}^{n_{l}}\left({\cal O}_{lmn\ldots}-\mathbf{E}^{l}{\cal O}_{lmn\ldots}\right)^{2}.

Introducing the power-law operator
\mathbf{P}^{\alpha}{\cal O}\equiv{\cal O}^{\alpha}
,

The result

2.2.1
Ergodic systems

We remind first that in ergodic systems the terminal relaxation time

One may thus either compute the averages

2.2.3
Commuting and non-commuting operators

Since

\mathbf{E}^{c}\mathbf{E}^{k}\ {\cal O}[\mathbf{x}_{ck}]=\mathbf{E}^{k}\mathbf{E}^{c}\ {\cal O}[\mathbf{x}_{ck}]=\mathbf{E}^{l}\ {\cal O}[\mathbf{x}_{l}]={\cal O},

Extending our recent work focusing on ergodic stationary Gaussian stochastic processes

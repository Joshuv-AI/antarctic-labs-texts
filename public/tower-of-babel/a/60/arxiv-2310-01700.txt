Rooted labeled trees and exceptional sequences of type B n / C n B_{n}/C_{n}
Kiyoshi Igusa, Emre Sen

Abstract
. We show that exceptional sequences in the abelian tube of rank n n , which we denote 𝒲 n {\mathcal{W}}_{n} , are related to exceptional sequences of type B n B_{n} and C n C_{n} and to those of type B n − 1 B_{n-1} and C n − 1 C_{n-1} . 𝒲 n {\mathcal{W}}_{n} has n n − 1 n^{n-1} exceptional sequences. These are in 1 1 -to- n n correspondence from the n n n^{n} “augmented” rooted labeled trees with n n vertices which, in turn, are in bijection with exceptional sequences of type B n B_{n} and C n C_{n} . By determining the probability distribution of relative projectives in these exceptional sequences, we show there are ( 2 ​ n ) ! / n ! (2n)!/n! signed exceptional sequences in 𝒲 n {\mathcal{W}}_{n} and we show that these are in bijection with signed exceptional sequences of type B n − 1 B_{n-1} and C n − 1 C_{n-1} by combining the results of Buan-Marsh-Vatne and Igusa-Todorov.

1.
Rooted labeled trees

The relationship between trees, forests and exceptional sequences for quivers of type
A_{n-1}
and
C_{n}
is not new. It comes from
[
16
]
and
[
13
]
. In
[
16
]
it is shown that complete exceptional sequences for hereditary algebras of type
C_{n}
(or
B_{n}
) are given uniquely by a complete exceptional sequence of type
A_{n-1}
and two integers modulo
n
. In
[
13
]
a bijection is given between complete exceptional sequences for quivers of type
A_{n}
and rooted labeled forests with
n
vertices. This bijection was used to describe, in detail, the action of the braid group
B_{n}
on this set of forests.

Thus, a complete exceptional sequence for an algebra of type
C_{n}
is given by a rooted labeled forest with
n-1
vertices together with two integers modulo
n
. In this paper, the first integer will be used to label a root and add it to the forest giving a rooted labeled tree with
n
vertices. The second integer will added to the structure and be called an “augmentation”. Thus, there will be
n^{n}
augmented rooted labeled trees which, by
[
16
]
, are in bijection with complete exceptional sequences for algebras of type
C_{n}
.

The only new concept here is the action of
B_{n}
, the braid group on
n
strands, on the set of trees and augmented trees. We also need to set up notation and terminology for the rest of the paper.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
be the set of rooted trees with vertices labeled
1,\cdots,n
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
be the set of rooted labeled forests with
n
vertices. We sometimes consider
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
as a subset of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
. We also have a projection map

\pi:{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}

given by deleting the root of a tree, say

for

is an
n

there are exactly
n

given by the possible labels of the root to be inserted. Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}
has
n^{n-2}
elements, this implies that

has
n^{n-1}

2.
Oriented and pointed chord diagrams

Oriented chord diagrams are defined to be sets of oriented chords on a circle with
n
marked points which are pairwise “noncrossing” and ordered in a good way. We also define “pointed” chord diagrams which are oriented chord diagrams together with an added loop at some vertex in the “central region” of the interior of the circle. We show that there is a bijection between oriented chords
\gamma_{ij}
and objects
V_{ij}
in
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
, the abelian tube of rank
n
.

2.1.
Unoriented strand and chord diagrams

We review the definition and enumeration of unoriented chord diagrams and strand diagrams from
[
11
]
.

Take a circle with
n+1
marked points labeled
0
to
n
. We usually order these clockwise around the circle, however, other orderings are sometimes convenient. (See Figure
7
.) By a
chord
we mean a straight line segment connecting two of these marked points. There are
\binom{n+1}{2}=n(n+1)/2
chords. A pair of chords is
noncrossing
if they do not cross, i.e., do not meet except possibly at their endpoints.

We define a
chord diagram
to be a sequence
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{k})
of pairwise noncrossing chords having the property that, at every marked point, the chords
c_{i}
which have one endpoint at that marked point are ordered clockwise. It is easy to see that a chord diagram can have no oriented cycles since, in any oriented cycle, the chords must be numbered in increasing order as we go counterclockwise around the cycle and this is impossible. Thus, every region of the complement of a chord diagram has at least one part of its boundary being an arc on the circle.

Lemma 2.1
.

Every chord diagram of length
k<n
on a circle with
n+1
marked points can be completed to a chord diagram with
n
chords.

Proof.

The circle is divided into
n+1
arcs by the marked points. If
k<n
then one of the
k+1
regions in the complement of a chord diagram
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{k})
, call it
U
, has at least two arcs on its boundary. Suppose two arcs on the boundary of
U
are consecutive with endpoints the marked points
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
in clockwise order on the circle. Then
m_{2}
is not an endpoint of any of the given chords
c_{i}
and we take the chord connecting
m_{2},m_{3}
to the chord diagram and call it
c_{k+1}
. It will be clockwise from any
c_{i}
incident to
m+3
. If the boundary of
U
does not have consecutive arcs, we let
c_{k+1}
be the chord connecting the rightmost marked points of two of the arcs. This will be clockwise from any
c_{i}
incident to either of these marked points. Furthermore,
c_{k+1}
will not cross any
c_{i}
since
U
is convex.
∎

Remark 3.15

It follows from APR-tilting

n=4,\mu=1,k=1,\lambda_{0}=0,\lambda_{1}=1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The size of the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)
where
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
is

|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|={\frac{(k+1)!}{\prod n_{p}!}}

where
n_{p}
is the number of parts
\lambda_{i}=p
.

Proof.

Elements of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
consist of
L_{\mu}
followed by
k+1
linear graphs
L_{\lambda_{i}}
in some order. The number of these is
(k+1)!
divided by the indicated redundancy factor.
∎

Let
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
be the set of all pairs
(G,e)
where
G\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
and
e
is one of the
n
edges of
L_{n}
. Then we have:

|\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=n|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|.

Let
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
be the set of all
(G,e)\in\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
where
e
is one of the
k+1
edges of
L_{n}
not in
G
. Then, either
e=e_{\mu+1}
or
e=e_{i}
is one of the
k
other edges with
\mu+2\leq i\leq n
not in
G
. We have:

|\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)|=(k+1)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|.

Given
(G,e)\in\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
, consider what happens when the deleted edge
e
is put back into
G
. Then we get
G^{\prime}=G\cup\{e\}
where either

(1)

G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
where
\lambda^{\prime}
is
\lambda
with one part
\lambda_{i}
deleted and
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+\lambda_{i}+1
or

(2)

G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
where
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
is
\lambda
with two parts
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
deleted and one new part
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+1
added.

Given
G^{\prime}
as above, how may ways can we delete an edge
e
from
G^{\prime}
to obtain an element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
(with
G=G^{\prime}\backslash e
not necessarily the one we started with)?

(1)

If
G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
, there is only one possibility:
e=e_{\mu+1}
.

(2)

If
G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
we must first choose one of the
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}
parts of
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
equal to
c=\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}+1
. Then we have two cases depending on where
a=b
or
a\neq b
where
a=\lambda_{i},b=\lambda_{j}
. If
a=b
there is only one edge in
L_{c}
which can be deleted to produce two subgraphs isomorphic to
L_{a},L_{b}
. If
a\neq b
there are two edges that can be deleted. Thus the number of possible edges
e
that can be deleted from
G^{\prime}
to obtain an element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
is
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}2X(a,b)
where

X(a,b)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }$a=b$\\
1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We obtain an equality from these two counts of the same set
|\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)|
:

(5.1)

(k+1)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})|+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}n_{c}^{\prime\prime}2X(a,b)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})|

where the first sum is over all distinct
\lambda^{\prime}
equal to
\lambda
with one term
a=\lambda_{i}
deleted and
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+a+1
and the second sum is over all distinct
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
obtained from
\lambda
by deleting two parts
a=\lambda_{i}
and
b=\lambda_{j}
and adding the new part
c=a+b+1
.

We summarize (
5.1
): For each
\lambda^{\prime}
, each element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
gives one element of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
. For each
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, each element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
gives
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}2X(a,b)
elements of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
.

By counting the size of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
in two ways we obtain another formula for
|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|
.

Lemma 5.2
.

With the same notation as in (
5.1
) we have:

n|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mu+a+1)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})|+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,b)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})|.

Proof.

We construct a mapping
\varphi:\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)\to\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
. Each term on the right hand side of (
5.1
) corresponds to an element of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
. If we multiply this with the size of its inverse image in
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
we will obtain the required formula for the size of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
.

For any
G\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
let
G_{0},G_{1},\cdots,G_{k+1}
be the components of
G
in order and let
g_{i}\geq 0
be the number of edges in
G_{i}
. Thus,
g_{0}=\mu
and the other
g_{i}
are some permutation of the
{\lambda_{j}}
in
\lambda
. Let
d_{0},\cdots,d_{k}
be the missing edges in order. Thus
d_{i}
is the edge between
G_{i}
and
G_{i+1}
. For any
(G,e)\in\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
, let
\varphi(G,e)
be given as follows.

(1)

\varphi(G,e)=(G,e)
if
e\notin G
.

(2)

\varphi(G,e)=(G,d_{0})
if
e\in G_{0}\cup G_{1}
. Thus
\varphi^{-1}(G,d_{0})
has
\mu+g_{1}+1
elements.

(3)

\varphi(G,e)=(G,d_{i})
if
e\in G_{i+1}
and
i\geq 2
. So,
|\varphi^{-1}(G,d_{i})|=g_{i+1}+1
if
i\geq 2
.

Now we count the number of elements of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
corresponding to each element of the right hand side of (
5.1
).

(1)

Each element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
gives an element
(G,d_{0})\in\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
with
g_{0}=\mu,g_{1}=a
. This has
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+a+1
inverse image points in
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
.

(2)

Take
G^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
where
a=b
and
c=2a+1
. There are
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}
components of
G^{\prime\prime}
of size
c
(excluding
G_{0}^{\prime\prime}
). In each such component, the middle edge
p
is removed giving two new components of
G=G^{\prime\prime}\backslash p
both of size
a
. Then
(G,p)
is the corresponding element of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
with
a+1
inverse image points in
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
. This gives

(a+1)n_{c}^{\prime\prime}=n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,a)

elements of
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
since
X(a,a)=\frac{1}{2}
and
c+1=2a+2
.

(3)

Take
G^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
where
a\neq b
and
c=a+b+1
. There are
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}
components of
G^{\prime\prime}
of size
c
(excluding
G_{0}^{\prime\prime}
). In each such component there are two edges, say
p,q
, which can be deleted to produce an element of
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
. In
G^{\prime\prime}\backslash p
, the
L_{c}
component becomes
L_{a}\coprod L_{b}
and in
G^{\prime\prime}\backslash q
, the
L_{c}
becomes
L_{b}\coprod L_{a}
. In the first case the inverse image in
\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
has
b+1
elements, in the second case it has
a+1
elements for a total of

n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(a+1+b+1)=n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,b)

inverse image points since
X(a,b)=1
.

Adding these up gives the lemma.
∎

5.2.
Counting exceptional sequences of type
B_{n}
or
C_{n}

For
\mu\geq 0
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
a nonnegative partition of
n-\mu-k-1
, let
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
denote the set of all exceptional sequences
E_{\ast}=(E_{k+1},E_{k},\cdots,E_{1})
for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
whose perpendicular category
{\mathcal{E}}=(E_{k+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{1})^{\perp}
has type
B_{\mu}\times\prod A_{\lambda_{i}}
and let
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)
be the subset of
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
of exceptional sequences in which
E_{k+1}
is relatively projective, i.e., a projective object of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=(E_{k}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{1})^{\perp}
.

We will show that
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)|/|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=\frac{k+1}{n}
for all
\mu,\lambda
. For
k=0
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{0})=(n-\mu-1)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
consists of a single
\tau
-orbit, namely that of
P_{\mu+1}
, the
\mu+1
st projective object, and
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)=\{P_{\mu+1}\}
contains only that one element. Since every
\tau
-orbit contains
n
elements including one projective object, for
k=0
we have
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=n
and
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)|=1
. This is the
k=0
case of the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

For
k\geq 0
,
\mu\geq 0
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
a nonnegative partition of
n-\mu-k-1
we have

|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|=n^{k}|\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|=n^{k+1}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|=\frac{n^{k+1}(k+1)!}{\prod n_{p}!}

|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu,\lambda)|=n^{k}|\widetilde{}{\mathcal{S}}_{n}^{p}(\mu,\lambda)|=n^{k}(k+1)|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|=\frac{k+1}{n}|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|

where
n_{p}
is the number of parts
\lambda_{i}
of
\lambda
equal to
p
.

This will follow almost immediately from the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Using the notation in Equation (
5.1
) we have the following.

|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)|=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mu+a+1)|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})|+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,b)|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})|.

|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{p}(\mu;\lambda)|=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})|+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}n_{c}^{\prime\prime}2X(a,b)|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})|.

Proof.

Given an exceptional sequence
E_{\ast}=(E_{k+1},E_{k},\cdots,E_{1})\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
we consider the shorter exceptional sequence
E_{\ast}^{\prime}=(E_{k},\cdots,E_{1})
. There are two disjoint possibilities. Either
E_{\ast}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
or
E_{\ast}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
. We will see that there are
\mu+a+1
possibilities for
E_{k+1}
in the first case, one of which is relatively projective, and
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,b)
possibilities for
E_{k+1}
in the second case,
2n_{c}^{\prime\prime}X(a,b)
of which are relatively projective. This will prove both formulas in the lemma.

In more detail, take the first case
E_{\ast}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})
. The perpendicular category of
E_{\ast}^{\prime}
has type
B_{\mu^{\prime}}\times\prod A_{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}
and
E_{k+1}
lies in
B_{\mu^{\prime}}
where
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+a+1
. Furthermore,
E_{k+1}
must lie in the
\tau
orbit of the
\mu+1
st projective object of
mod\text{-}B_{\mu^{\prime}}
in order for
E_{\ast}=(E_{k+1},E_{\ast}^{\prime})
to lie in
{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda)
. There are
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+a+1
objects in that
\tau
orbit, one of which is projective. This gives the coefficients of
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime})|
in the two sums.

In the second case
E_{\ast}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu;\lambda^{\prime\prime})
where
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
is
\lambda
with parts of size
a,b
removed and a new part of size
c=a+b+1
added.
E_{k+1}
must lie in one of the
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}
copies of
A_{c}
which occur in the perpendicular category of
E_{\ast}^{\prime}
. When
a=b
,
E_{k+1}
must lie in the
\tau
orbit of the middle projective
P_{a+1}
and there are
(c+1)/2=(c+1)X(a,a)
object in that
\tau
-orbit. One of these is projective (and
1=2X(a,a)
). When
a\neq b
,
E_{k+1}
must lie in one of two
\tau
-orbits, that of
P_{a+1}
or
P_{b+1}
. The union of these has
a+b+2=(c+1)X(a,b)
number of element. Two of these
E_{k+1}
are projective (one in each
\tau
-orbit), making
2X(a,b)
projective elements. In both subcases of Case 2 there are
n_{c}^{\prime\prime}(c+1)X(a,b)
choices of
E_{k+1}
and
2n_{c}^{\prime\prime}X(a,b)
of these are (relatively) projective. This gives the coefficients in both
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
summands which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

By induction on
k
we have that
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})|=n^{k}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})|
and
|{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda^{\prime\prime})|=n^{k}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda^{\prime\prime})|
. Inserting these into the right hand side in Lemma
5.4
we obtain
n^{k+1}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|
in the first equation by Lemma
5.2
and
(k+1)n^{k}|{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(\mu,\lambda)|
in the second equation by (
5.1
). This proves both statements in the theorem.
∎

Theorem
5.3
implies the following with
k
replacing
k+1
.

Corollary 5.5
.

In a random exceptional sequence of
\ell
for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
, the probability that
E_{k}
for
k\leq\ell
is relatively projective is equal to
k/n
and this events is independent of the isomorphism class of the perpendicular category
(E_{k}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{1})^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Each isomorphism class of perpendicular categories is given by a pair
(\mu,\lambda)
and for each such pair the fraction of those
(E_{k},\cdots,E_{1})
with that perpendicular category for which
E_{k}
is relatively projective is
k/n
. So, the events are independent.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

The events
D_{k}
that
E_{k}
is relatively projective are independent for distinct
k
.

Proof.

For
j>k
, the event
D_{j}
depends only of the isomorphism class of the perpendicular category
(E_{k}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{1})^{\perp}
which is independent of
D_{k}
.
∎

Corollaries
5.5
and
5.6
give Theorem
F
. The next corollary is Corollary
C
(a).

Corollary 5.7
.

The number of exceptional sequences of length
k
for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
is
n^{k}\binom{n}{k}
.

Corollary 5.8
.

The number of signed exceptional sequences of length
k
for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
is

(n+1)\cdots(n+k)\binom{n}{k}=\frac{(n+k)!}{k!(n-k)!}.

Since signed exceptional sequences are in bijection with ordered cluster we have:

Corollary 5.9
.

The number of partial cluster of size
k
for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
is

\frac{(n+k)!}{k!k!(n-k)!}.

A
partial cluster
of size
k
is a rigid object in the cluster category having
k
nonisomorphic indecomposable summands. For example, when
k=1
, this number is
n(n+1)=n^{2}+n
which counts the
n^{2}
indecomposable modules and the
n
shifted projective modules
P_{i}[1]
. For
n=k=3
we have
3^{3}=27
exceptional sequence and
4\cdot 5\cdot 6=120
signed exceptional sequences giving
20
clusters for
B_{3}
and
C_{3}

In terms of generating functions, the distribution of relative projectives in an exceptional sequence for
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
is given as follows.

For any hereditary algebra
\Lambda
, let
f_{\Lambda,k}(z_{k},\cdots,z_{1})
be the
k
-variable generating function

f_{\Lambda,k}(z_{k},\cdots,z_{1})=\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}z^{\beta}

where the sum is over all multi-indices
\beta=(b_{k},\cdots,b_{1})\in\{0,1\}^{k}
and
a_{\beta}
is the number of exceptional sequences
(E_{k},\cdots,E_{1})
for
\Lambda
for which
E_{i}
is relatively projective when
b_{i}=1
and not relatively projective for
b_{i}=0
.

Corollary 5.10
.

The
k
-variable generating function for
\Lambda=B_{n}
or
C_{n}
is

f_{\Lambda,k}(z_{k},\cdots,z_{1})=\binom{n}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i+iz_{i})

Proof.

Since the probability of
E_{i}
being relatively projective are independent of each other by Corollary
5.6
, the generating function is given by multiplying the number of exceptional sequences by the product of the terms

1+(z_{i}-1){\mathbb{P}}(\text{$E_{i}$ is relatively projective})

Since
{\mathbb{P}}(\text{$E_{i}$ is relatively projective})=\frac{i}{n}
by Corollary
5.5
and the number of exceptional sequences is
n^{k}\binom{n}{k}
, the generating function is:

n^{k}\binom{n}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1+(z_{i}-1)\frac{i}{n}\right)=\binom{n}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n+(z_{i}-1)i)

as claimed.
∎

Corollary
5.7
also follows from this by plugging in
z_{i}=2
for all
i
.

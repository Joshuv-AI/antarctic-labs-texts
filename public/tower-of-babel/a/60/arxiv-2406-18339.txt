Convergence to equilibrium for a degenerate three species reaction-diffusion system
Saumyajit Das, Harsha Hutridurga

Abstract
. In this work, we study a 3 × 3 3\times 3 triangular reaction-diffusion system. Our main objective is to understand the long time behaviour of solutions to this reaction-diffusion system when there are degeneracies. More precisely, we treat cases when one of the diffusion coefficients vanishes while the other two diffusion coefficients stay positive. We prove convergence to equilibrium type results. In all our results, the constants appearing in the decay estimates are explicit.



Key words.
Reaction-Diffusion, Entropy method, Degenerate diffusion, Large time behaviour

Mathematics Subject Classification.
35K57, 35K65, 35Q92, 92E20, 39B62

1.
Introduction

Reaction-diffusion equations are among the most widely used differential equations in applications. These equations govern the evolution (in time) of species concentrations at various spatial locations that are simultaneously diffusing and undergoing chemical reactions. We consider a reaction-diffusion system that concerns the three species
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
and
\mathcal{X}_{3}
involved in the following reversible reaction:

\mathrm{$\mathcal{X}_{1}$+$\mathcal{X}_{2}$<>[][]$\mathcal{X}_{3}$}

The spatial domain is taken to be a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\mathrm{C}^{2+\nu}
boundary with
\nu>0
. For the unknowns
a,b,c:[0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, representing the concentrations of the three species
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{X}_{3}
respectively, we consider the following system of differential equations:

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a-d_{a}\Delta a&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b-d_{b}\Delta b&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}c-d_{c}\Delta c&=ab-c\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\nabla_{x}a\cdot n=\nabla_{x}b\cdot n&=\nabla_{x}c\cdot n=0\quad\mbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
a(0,\cdot)=a_{0};\,b(0,\cdot)=b_{0};\,&c(0,\cdot)=c_{0}\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
n(x)
denotes the outward unit normal to
\Omega
at the point
x\in\partial\Omega
. The initial data
a_{0},b_{0},c_{0}
are taken to be smooth up to the closure of the domain and strictly positive. The diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{b},d_{c}
are taken to be nonnegative. When all the diffusion coefficients are strictly positive (referred to, now onwards, as the non-degenerate setting), it is well-known that a unique strictly positive global-in-time
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
solution exists for the above model (see
[
11
,
12
]
), where the positiveness comes from the quasi-positive nature of the rate function
[
14
,
1
,
13
]
. The long time behaviour of solutions to (
1
) in the non-degenerate setting was addressed in
[
4
]
using the method of entropy. In this method, a Lyapunov functional (termed entropy) is found for the evolution equation. The negative of the time derivative of this entropy functional is referred to as the entropy dissipation functional. The entropy dissipation functional is then related back to the relative entropy via a functional inequality. This will then be followed by a Grönwall type argument to deduce convergence of relative entropy to zero. A Cziszár-Kullback-Pinsker type inequality that relates relative entropy and the
\mathrm{L}^{1}
-norm helps the authors in
[
4
]
to prove the convergence to equilibrium in the
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
-norm and to deduce that the decay is exponentially fast in time. In the proof of
[
4
]
, uniform boundedness of the solution
(a,b,c)
to (
1
) plays an important role. This uniform bound is available in the non-degenerate case.

In this paper, we discuss the long time behaviour of solutions to a couple of degenerate cases of the three species model (
1
). Our work is heavily inspired by
[
7
]
which dealt with a four species degenerate model where one of the species stops diffusing. The authors in
[
7
]
demonstrated a so-called
indirect diffusion effect
wherein an effective diffusion is felt by the non-diffusive species, thanks to the interplay between the diffusion from diffusive species and the underlying reversible reaction. Our work demonstrates that a similar indirect diffusion effect is present in the above three species model in the presence of degeneracies. The two degenerate reaction-diffusion systems that we study in this article are:

(2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a-d_{a}\Delta a&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}c-d_{c}\Delta c&=ab-c\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\nabla_{x}a\cdot n=\nabla_{x}b\cdot n&=\nabla_{x}c\cdot n=0\quad\mbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
a(0,\cdot)=a_{0};\,b(0,\cdot)=b_{0};\,&c(0,\cdot)=c_{0}\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a-d_{a}\Delta a&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b-d_{b}\Delta b&=c-ab\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}c&=ab-c\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\nabla_{x}a\cdot n=\nabla_{x}b\cdot n&=\nabla_{x}c\cdot n=0\quad\mbox{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
a(0,\cdot)=a_{0};\,b(0,\cdot)=b_{0};\,&c(0,\cdot)=c_{0}\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, the initial data are assumed to be smooth and strictly positive. The diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{c}
in (
2
) and the diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{b}
in (
3
) are assumed to be strictly positive. The existence of a smooth global-in-time positive solution to (
2
) was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. The existence of a smooth global-in-time positive solution to (
3
) was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 3.2]
if the dimension
N\leq 3
. The authors in
[
5
]
, however, prove the existence of a weak global-in-time positive solution to (
3
) in any arbitrary dimension.

The process of establishing various estimates in
[
7
]
has greatly motivated us to establish similar kind of estimates in dimension larger than three for the degenerate case (
2
) corresponding to the vanishing of the diffusion coefficient
d_{b}
. We further use various Neumann Green’s function results from
[
14
,
10
,
3
,
8
]
to get various estimates specifically for dimensions less than four.

Observe that both the models (
2
) and (
3
) satisfy the following mass conservation properties:

(4)

\displaystyle 0<M_{1}|\Omega|=:\int_{\Omega}\big(a(t,x)+c(t,x)\big)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\big(a(0,x)+c(0,x)\big)\,{\rm d}x,

(5)

\displaystyle 0<M_{2}|\Omega|=:\int_{\Omega}\big(b(t,x)+c(t,x)\big)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\big(b(0,x)+c(0,x)\big)\,{\rm d}x.

An homogenous (constant) equilibrium state
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
associated with these models should also satisfy the above conservation properties. Hence we should have

(6)

\displaystyle a_{\infty}+c_{\infty}=M_{1},\qquad b_{\infty}+c_{\infty}=M_{2}.

Moreover, at equilibrium, the rate function should vanish, i.e.

(7)

\displaystyle c_{\infty}=a_{\infty}b_{\infty}.

The relations (
6
) and (
7
) put together leads to a quadratic equation for
c_{\infty}
whose only admissible non-negative solution is

(8)

\displaystyle c_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}(1+M_{1}+M_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(1+M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}-4M_{1}M_{2}}.

The corresponding
a_{\infty}
and
b_{\infty}
can be computed using (
6
). Now onwards, we will be considering this unique homogeneous equilibrium state
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
. Next, we list a bunch of notations which will be used throughout this manuscript. These notations are inspired by those used in
[
4
]
.

•

The square roots of the species concentrations are denoted as

A:=\sqrt{a},\quad B:=\sqrt{b}=B,\quad C:=\sqrt{c}.

•

The square roots of the homogeneous equilibrium states are denoted as

A_{\infty}:=\sqrt{a_{\infty}},\quad B_{\infty}:=\sqrt{b_{\infty}},\quad C_{\infty}:=\sqrt{c_{\infty}}.

•

The average of a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is denoted as

\overline{f}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,{\rm d}x.

•

The deviations of the square roots of species concentrations from their averages are denoted as

\delta_{A}:=A-\overline{A},\qquad\delta_{B}:=B-\overline{B},\qquad\delta_{C}:=C-\overline{C}.

•

The parabolic cylinders are denotes as

\Omega_{\tau,T}:=(\tau,T)\times\Omega\qquad\mbox{ for }\,0\leq\tau<T.

•

The lateral boundary of the parabolic cylinders are denoted as

\partial\Omega_{\tau,T}:=(\tau,T)\times\partial\Omega\qquad\mbox{ for }\,0\leq\tau<T.

We consider the following entropy functional associated with (
2
) and (
3
):

(9)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c):=\int_{\Omega}\Big((a(\ln a-1)+1)+(b(\ln b-1)+1)+(c(\ln c-1)+1)\Big){\rm d}x.

While studying (
2
) in dimensions
N\geq 4
, we have been able to arrive at large time asymptotics of the solution only under certain closeness assumption on the non-zero diffusion coefficients. The precise assumption is the following:

Assumption 1
.

The non-zero diffusion coefficients
d_{a}
and
d_{c}
are said to satisfy the closeness assumption if

(10)

\displaystyle|{d_{a}-d_{c}}|<\frac{2}{C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2}},p^{\prime}}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}<\frac{1}{C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})},

where the constants
C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2}},p^{\prime}}
and
C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})
, are the parabolic regularity constant (see Theorem
12
in the Appendix) and the second order regularity constant (see Theorem
11
in the Appendix), respectively.

Now we are going to state our two main results.

Theorem 1
.

For
N\geq 4
, let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
2
). Let
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
be the associated equilibrium state given by (
6
)-(
8
). Let the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (
10
). Then, for any given positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists a time
T_{\varepsilon}
and two positive constants
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that for
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{2M_{1}}\|a-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\|b-b_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{(M_{1}+M_{2})}\|c-c_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{(9+2\sqrt{2})}{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}\mathcal{S}_{1}e^{-\mathcal{S}_{2}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{N-1}}}.

For
N<4
, let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
2
). Let
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
be the associated equilibrium state given by (
6
)-(
8
). Then, for any given positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists a time
T_{\varepsilon}
and two positive constants
\mathcal{S}_{3}
and
\mathcal{S}_{4}
such that for
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{2M_{1}}\|a-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\|b-b_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{(M_{1}+M_{2})}\|c-c_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{(9+2\sqrt{2})}{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}\mathcal{S}_{3}\,e^{-\mathcal{S}_{4}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{6}}}.

Observe that our above result is unconditional when the dimension
N\leq 3
, i.e. there is no closeness assumption on the non-zero diffusion coefficients
d_{a}
and
d_{c}
. Furthermore, the constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are determined by the initial data (see (
4
) and (
5
)).

Theorem 2
.

Let
N\leq 3
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
3
). Let
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
be the associated equilibrium state given by (
6
)-(
8
). Then, for any given positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists a time
T_{\varepsilon}
and two positive constants
\mathcal{S}_{5}
and
\mathcal{S}_{6}
such that for
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{2M_{1}}\|a-a_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2M_{2}}\|b-b_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{(M_{1}+M_{2})}\|c-c_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{(9+2\sqrt{2})}{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}\mathcal{S}_{5}\,e^{-\mathcal{S}_{6}(1+t)^{\frac{2-\epsilon}{3}}}.

In the reminder of this introduction, we briefly describe our strategy to arrive at the aforementioned large time behaviour via the entropy method. We start by defining the entropy dissipation functionals associated with the degenerate models (
2
) and (
3
):

(11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{For}\ d_{b}&=0,\\
D(a,b,c):=&\,4\,d_{a}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\sqrt{a}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\,4\,d_{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\sqrt{c}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}(ab-c)\ln\left(\frac{ab}{c}\right)\,{\rm d}x,\\
\text{For}\ d_{c}&=0,\\
D(a,b,c):=&\,4\,d_{a}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\sqrt{a}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\,4\,d_{b}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\sqrt{b}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}(ab-c)\ln\left(\frac{ab}{c}\right)\,{\rm d}x.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that both the dissipation functionals are positive. We recall here an algebraic inequality:

(x-y)(\ln{x}-\ln{y})\geq 4(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y})^{2},\ \ \forall x,y\geq 0.

This algebraic inequality gives a lower bound on the last term of the above entropy dissipation functionals whereas the classical Poincaré inequality gives a lower bound for the gradient terms. More precisely, we have

(12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\mbox{ for }\,d_{b}=0,\\
D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{B}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\mbox{ for }\,d_{c}=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
P(\Omega)
is the Poincaré constant of the domain
\Omega
(see Lemma
7
in the Appendix). Observe that the deviation term
\|\delta_{B}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
is missing in the above lower bound corresponding to the degenerate case
d_{b}=0
and the deviation term
\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
is missing in the lower bound corresponding to the degenerate case
d_{c}=0
. Note that, for the dissipation functional of the degenerate system (
2
), the Poincaré inequality actually yields

\left\{\begin{aligned} D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{ for }\,N\geq 4,\\
D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\qquad\quad\mbox{ for }N\leq 3.\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly, for the dissipation functional of the degenerate system (
3
), we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{B}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{ for }N\geq 4,\\
D(a,b,c)\geq&\frac{4\,d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\,d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{B}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+4\|AB-C\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\qquad\quad\mbox{ for }N\leq 3.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that, if
(a,b,c)
solves either of the system (
2
) or (
3
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\Big)=-D(a,b,c),

i.e. the relative entropy (relative with respect to the homogeneous equilibrium state) is non-increasing in time. The relative entropy has the following expression:

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})=\int_{\Omega}\big(a\ln{a}-a-a_{\infty}

\displaystyle\ln{a_{\infty}}+a_{\infty}\big)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\big(b\ln{b}-b-b_{\infty}\ln{b_{\infty}}+b_{\infty}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big(c\ln{c}-c-c_{\infty}\ln{c_{\infty}}+c_{\infty}\big)\,{\rm d}x.

The above expression rewrites as

(13)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})=\int_{\Omega}\left(a\ln{\left(\frac{a}{a_{\infty}}\right)}-a+a_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(b\ln{\left(\frac{b}{b_{\infty}}\right)}-b+b_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(c\ln{\left(\frac{c}{c_{\infty}}\right)}-c+c_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

Define a function
\Gamma:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(14)

\Gamma(x,y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{x\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-x+y}{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{ for }x\not=y,\\
&2\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{ for }x=y.\end{aligned}\right.

It can be shown (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, p.162]
for details) that the above defined function satisfies the following bound:

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,y)\leq C_{\Gamma}\max\left\{1,\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right\}

for some positive constant
C_{\Gamma}
. Note that using the function
\Gamma
defined above, the relative entropy can be rewritten as

E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(a,a_{\infty})(A-A_{\infty})^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(b,b_{\infty})(B-B_{\infty})^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(c,c_{\infty})(C-C_{\infty})^{2}.

Using the aforementioned bound for
\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\leq C_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\max\big\{1,\ln{(\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}+|\ln{a_{\infty}}|\big\}\|A-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{\Gamma}\max\big\{1,\ln{(\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}+|\ln{b_{\infty}}|\big\}\|B-B_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{\Gamma}\max\big\{1,\ln{(\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}+|\ln{c_{\infty}}|\big\}\|C-C_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We derive the growth rate of the solutions corresponding to (
2
) and (
3
) in Lemma
4
and in Proposition
8
respectively. Both these results assert that the growth of the solutions can at most be polynomial in time. This leads to

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\leq C_{1}(1+t)^{\varepsilon}\Big(\|A-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|B-B_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|C-C_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big),

for all
t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon\ll 1
is some positive quantity and
T_{\varepsilon}
depends on
\varepsilon
. Furthermore, the positive constant
C_{1}
depends only on the initial data, the domain
\Omega
and the dimension
N
.

Observe that, other than the logarithm of the growth of the supremum norm of the solution, the growth of relative entropy depends on the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the deviation of the
(A,B,C)
from
(A_{\infty},B_{\infty},C_{\infty})
. Recall from (
12
) that the dissipation functional is also related to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the deviations
\delta_{A}
,
\delta_{B}
and
\delta_{C}
. The following observation holds for all the three species. However, we choose to show it for the term
A
:

\|A-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 3\left(\|A-\overline{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\overline{A}-\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

The following observation says that the first term on the right hand side dominates the second term:

\displaystyle\|\overline{A}-\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=

\displaystyle|\Omega|\big|\overline{A}-\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}\big|^{2}=|\Omega|\left(\overline{A^{2}}+\overline{A}^{2}-2\overline{A}\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\left(\overline{A^{2}}-\overline{A}^{2}\right)\leq\|A-\overline{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Here we used the fact that
\displaystyle{\overline{A}\leq\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}}
, thanks to Hölder inequality. Hence

\|A-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\|A-\overline{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

This helps us deduce

\displaystyle\|A-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|B-B_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|C-C_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\|A-\overline{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|B-\overline{B}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|C-\overline{C}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 6\left(\|\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}-A_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{B^{2}}}-B_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{C^{2}}}-C_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Next we borrow a result from
[
4
]
, which says that there exists
C_{EB}>0
, depending only on the domain and the equilibrium state
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
such that

(15)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\overline{A^{2}}}-A_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\overline{B^{2}}}-B_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{C^{2}}}-C_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{EB}\left(\|\delta_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\delta_{B}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\delta_{C}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|C-AB\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

This indicates that to relate entropy with entropy dissipation, we need to relate dissipation with the missing term
\|\delta_{B}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
for the degenerate case (
2
) and similarly with the missing term
\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
for the degenerate case (
3
).

For the degenerate system (
2
), the relation between the missing term and the entropy dissipation is shown in Proposition
4
for dimensions
N\geq 4
. For dimensions
N\leq 3
, we obtain the relation in Proposition
5
. More precisely, we have obtained

\left\{\begin{aligned} D(a,b,c)\geq&\,\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\|\delta_{B}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\mbox{ for }N\geq 4,\\
D(a,b,c)\geq&\,\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{6}}\|\delta_{B}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\quad\mbox{ for }N\leq 3.\end{aligned}\right.

where
\hat{C}
is some positive constant, independent of time.

In order to obtain the above lower bound, we require an estimate on the growth (in time) of the
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)
norm of the degenerate species
b
for dimension
N\geq 4
and for dimension
N\leq 3
, we require a similar growth estimate of the
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)
norm of the degenerate species
b
. These estimates are obtained in Lemma
2
, Proposition
3
and Lemma
3
. More precisely, we have obtained

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}&\leq\hat{K}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\qquad N\geq 4,\\
\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}&\leq\hat{K}(1+t)^{\frac{5}{6}}\qquad N=1,2,3,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\hat{K}
is a positive constant independent of time. For dimension

, the above integral estimation of the species

follows from Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality whereas for dimension

, we need closeness assumption (

) on the other two non-zero diffusion coefficients

and
d_{c}
. Closeness assumption further helps us to estimate
\mathrm{L}^{p}
integral growth of

and

on a parabolic cylinder with unit height, for some large exponent

. It turns out if

and
d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (

, then there exists a positive constant
C_{0}>0
, depending on
p
and independent of time, such that

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}((\tau,\tau+1)\times\Omega)}+\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}((\tau,\tau+1)\times\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\qquad\forall\tau\geq 0,\ p>N.

The above result is proved in Lemma
1
. For dimension
N\geq 4
, this will lead us to prove our key
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)
estimate. These strategies are inspired from the article
[
7
]
, where the authors study existence and large time behaviour of a particular

quadratic degenerate reaction-diffusion system.

For the degenerate system (
3

\|\delta_{C}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
. In this article, for the degenerate case (
3

. For dimension

We establish the following relation between the entropy dissipation and the missing term

D(a,b,c)\geq\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\|A-\overline{A}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|B-\overline{B}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|C-\overline{C}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

In order to arrive at the above estimate we need a particular integral estimate of species
a
, which we will establish in Lemma

. The particular relation is the following

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{\frac{1}{3}}\qquad\forall t\geq 0

where
\tilde{C}>0
a constant, independent of time. This is the key estimate for the degenerate case (
3

These results, along with the Grönwall inequality, will lead us to the conclusion that the entropy functional decays sub-exponentially fast in time for both the degenerate systems (

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}{2M_{1}(9+2\sqrt{2})}\|a-a_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}{2M_{2}(9+2\sqrt{2})}\|b-b_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{(3+2\sqrt{2})|\Omega|}{(M_{1}+M_{2})(9+2\sqrt{2})}\|c-c_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Combining all these estimates we arrive at our main results: Theorem

Lemma 1
.

Let
p>N\geq 4
and let
p^{\prime}
be its Hölder conjugate. Let

be the solution to the degenerate system (

, depending only on the initial condition and the dimension

, such that

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}+\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0}\qquad\forall\tau>0.

Proof.

Define
d:=\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2}
. We can rewrite the equations corresponding to concentrations
a
and
c
in (
2
) as

(16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a-d\Delta a&=c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a,\\
\partial_{t}c-d\Delta c&=ab-c+(d_{c}-d)\Delta c.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth function such that
\phi(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=1\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle 0\leq\phi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\leq M\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in[0,\infty)

for some constant
M>0
. For an arbitrary
\tau>0
, consider
\phi_{\tau}:[\tau,\infty)\to[0,1]
defined as
\phi_{\tau}(s):=\phi(s-\tau)
for
s\in[\tau,\infty)
. Then, the product
\phi_{\tau}(t)a(t,x)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\left(\phi_{\tau}a\right)-d\Delta\left(\phi_{\tau}a\right)&=a\partial_{t}\phi_{\tau}+\phi_{\tau}(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\phi_{\tau}\nabla a\cdot n&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\phi_{\tau}(\tau)a(\tau,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Making the change of variable
t_{1}=t-\tau
in the above equation yields

(17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x))&-d\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x))\\
=a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}&+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\nabla a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\cdot n&=0\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
\phi_{\tau}(0+\tau)a(0+\tau,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly, the concentration
c
satisfies the following boundary value problem:

(18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)c(t_{1}+\tau,x))&-d\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)c(t_{1}+\tau,x))\\
=c(t_{1}+\tau,x)\partial_{t_{1}}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}&+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)(ab-c+(d_{c}-d)\Delta c)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\nabla c(t_{1}+\tau,x)\cdot n&=0\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
\phi_{\tau}(0+\tau)c(0+\tau,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
G_{d}
denotes the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. Then, we can express the solutions to (
17
) and (
18
) as follows:

(19)

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)(s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s

and

(20)

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)c(t_{1}+\tau,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)c(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)(ab-c+(d_{c}-d)\Delta c)(s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s.

Let us fix a non-negative
\theta\in\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})
. Let
\psi
be the solution to

(21)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}\psi(t_{1},x)+d\Delta\psi(t_{1},x)&=-\theta(t_{1},x)\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{0,2}\\
\nabla\psi(t_{1},x)\cdot n&=0\qquad\ \ \qquad\ \ \mbox{on}\ (0,2)\times\partial\Omega\\
\psi(2,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying the second order regularity estimate (see Theorem
11
in Appendix
A
for further details) to the above equation yields

(22)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq\frac{2C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

Multiplying the expression (
19
) by
\theta
and integrating over time and space yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)(s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}.

Substituting for
\theta
in terms of
\psi
, using (
21
), in the second term on the right hand side of the above expression followed by integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t_{1}}-d\Delta\right)G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)\left(\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)(s,y)\right)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right)\psi(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},t_{1},x,y)\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)(t_{1},y)\,{\rm d}y\right)\psi(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}.

Using the property of the Green’s function, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)(c-ab+(d_{a}-d)\Delta a)(t_{1},x)\psi(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}.

A further integration by parts in the second term on the right hand side of the above expression yields

(23)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)(\left(c-ab\right)\psi+(d_{a}-d)a\Delta\psi)(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1},

where we have also used Hölder inequality to bound the first term on the right hand side.

The following Green’s function estimate is available from
[
10
]
[
3
]
: there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
, depending only on the domain, such that

0\leq G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq\frac{K_{1}}{(4\pi(t_{1}-s))^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\kappa\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{(t_{1}-s)}}=:g_{d}(t_{1}-s,x-y),

for some constant

depending only on
\Omega
and the diffusion coefficient

\left\|g_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq K_{2}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{N}{2}\right)

for some constant
K_{2}>0
depending on

. We, in particular choose
\displaystyle{z=1+\frac{1}{N}}
. Observe that there exists a

such that

1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{1}{q}.

Hence, applying the Young’s convolution inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d}(t_{1},s,x,y)a(s+\tau,y)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|g_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1+\frac{1}{n}}(\Omega_{0,2})}\left\|a(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\left\|a(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

Using this estimate and the estimate of
\Delta\psi
from (
22
) in (

), we arrive at

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\left\|a(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\left(\left(c-ab\right)\psi\right)(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\left\|\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)a(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

Performing a similar set of computations on the equation (
20
) for
\phi_{\tau}c
yields

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)c(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\left\|c(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}+\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\left(\left(ab-c\right)\psi\right)(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle+C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\left\|\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)c(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

Adding the inequalities (
24
) and (
25
) and using positivity of

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\left(a+c\right)(t_{1}+\tau,x)\theta(t_{1},x)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\left\|\left(a+c\right)(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}

\displaystyle+C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\left\|\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\left(a+c\right)(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

As

, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1]

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\alpha}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{1}.

Hence by interpolation, we have

\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{1-\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}^{\alpha}\qquad\forall f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}\cap\mathrm{L}^{1}.

Using the above interpolation inequality and a duality argument, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)(a+c)(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq\frac{K_{2}M^{\alpha}M_{1}^{\alpha}|\Omega|^{\alpha}}{\left(1-C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\right)}\left\|\left(a+c\right)(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\partial_{s}\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}^{1-\alpha}.

To obtain bounds which are independent of
\tau
, let us take

\texttt{C}:=\frac{K_{2}MM_{1}^{\alpha}|\Omega|^{\alpha}}{\left(1-C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\frac{|d_{a}-d_{c}|}{d_{a}+d_{c}}\right)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\beta_{n}:=\left\|a+c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{n,n+1})}\,\mbox{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

As
\tau\geq 0
is arbitrary, we deduce from (

\beta_{n+1}\leq\texttt{C}\,\beta_{n}^{1-\alpha}\qquad\mbox{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the set

\Lambda:=\big\{n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{ such that }\quad\beta_{n}\leq\beta_{n+1}\big\}.

Observe that
\beta_{n}\leq\ {\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
for all

. Hence we deduce that

\beta_{n}\leq\max\big\{\beta_{0},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big\}.

Observe that the sum of concentrations

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(a+c)-\Delta(\mu(a+c))&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{T},\\

(a+c)(0,x)&=a_{0}+c_{0}\in{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}

\mu(t,x):=\left(\frac{d_{a}a+d_{c}c}{a+c}\right)(t,x)\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}.

Observe that
\mu

0<\min\{d_{a},d_{c}\}\leq\mu(t,x)\leq\max\{d_{a},d_{c}\}\qquad\mbox{ for all }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}.

As the diffusion coefficients
d_{a}
and
d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (
10

[

(see Theorem

from the Appendix for the precise statement), we arrive at

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\|a+c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\left(1+\max\{d_{a},d_{c}\}\frac{|d_{c}-d_{a}|\,C^{PRC}_{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2},p^{\prime}}}{2-|d_{c}-d_{a}|\,C^{PRC}_{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2},p^{\prime}}}\right)\|a_{0}+c_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

Hence we deduce

\beta_{n}\leq\max\left\{\left(1+\max\{d_{a},d_{c}\}\frac{|d_{c}-d_{a}|\,C^{PRC}_{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2},p^{\prime}}}{2-|d_{c}-d_{a}|\,C^{PRC}_{\frac{d_{a}+d_{c}}{2},p^{\prime}}}\right)\|a_{0}+c_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)},\,{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}.

Hence there exists a constant
C_{0}
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}+\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0}\qquad\forall\tau>0.

The following lemma derives the key integrability estimate for the concentration
b
which will play an important role in our analysis.

Lemma 2
.

Let
p>N\geq 4
and let
p^{\prime}
be its Hölder conjugate. Let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenrate system (
2
) and let the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (
10
). Then there exists a constant
K_{3}>0
, depending only on the initial data and the dimension N, such that

\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq K_{3}\,(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Proof.

Recall that
b
solves the equation

\partial_{t}b=c-ab.

Non-negativity of
a
and
b
implies that

\partial_{t}b\leq c.

Integrating over
(0,t)
yields

b(t,x)\leq b_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}c(s,x)\,{\rm d}s.

Raising it to the power
N
and employing Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\left(b(t,x)\right)^{N}\leq 2^{N-1}\left(\left(b_{0}(x)\right)^{N}+\left(\int_{0}^{t}c(s,x)\,{\rm d}s\right)^{N}\right)\leq 2^{N-1}(1+t)^{N-1}\left(\left(b_{0}(x)\right)^{N}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(c(s,x)\right)^{N}\,{\rm d}s\right).

Integrating the above inequality in the
x
variable over
\Omega
yields

(26)

\displaystyle\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega)}^{N}\leq 2^{N-1}(1+t)^{N-1}\Big(\left\|b_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega)}^{N}+\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega_{t})}^{N}\Big).

According to Lemma
1
, for any
p>N
and for any
\tau\geq 0
, we have

\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0},

where the constant
C_{0}>0
depends only on the dimension
N
, the domain
\Omega
and the initial data. Hölder inequality yields

\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\,\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{p-N}{p}}.

Hence we deduce that for any
t>0
,

\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega_{t})}^{N}\leq C_{0}^{N}\,\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{N(p-N)}{p}}\,(1+t).

Using this in (
26
) yields

\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega)}^{N}\leq 2^{N-1}(1+t)^{N-1}\left(\left\|b_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{N}\,\left|\Omega\right|+C_{0}^{N}\,\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{N(p-N)}{p}}\,(1+t)\right).

Define a constant
K_{3}>0
as follows:

K_{3}^{\frac{N(N-1)}{N-2}}:=2^{N-1}\left(M_{2}|\Omega|\right)^{\frac{N}{N-2}}\left(\left\|b_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{N}\,\left|\Omega\right|+C_{0}^{N}\,\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{N(p-N)}{p}}\right).

Thus we arrive at

\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{N}(\Omega)}\leq K_{3}^{\frac{N-1}{N-2}}\left(M_{2}|\Omega|\right)^{-\frac{1}{N-2}}(1+t).

Note that

\frac{2}{N}=\frac{\alpha}{N}+\frac{1-\alpha}{1}\qquad\mbox{ with }\,\alpha=\frac{N-2}{N-1}.

Hence by interpolation, we have

.

Applying Hölder inequality we arrive at the following estimates

Lemma 4
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be the solution of degenerate system (
2
). Furthermore, let for
p>\frac{N+2}{2}
, there exists a time independent constant
K_{c}>0
and
\mu_{c}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}+\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq K_{c}(1+\tau)^{\mu_{c}}\quad\forall\tau\geq 0

Then there exists constant
K_{\infty}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq&K_{\infty}(1+t)^{\mu}\qquad\forall t\geq 0\\
\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq&K_{\infty}(1+t)^{\mu}\qquad\forall t\geq 0\\
\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq&K_{\infty}(1+t)^{\mu}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

The species
a
is a positive subsolution of the following equation in the time interval
(0,2)
.

(34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a(t,x)-d_{a}\Delta a(t,x)\leq&c\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\nabla a(t,x)\cdot n=&0\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
a(0,x)=&a_{0}\qquad\quad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

The solution corresponding to the equation (
34
) can be expressed as:

a\leq\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,0,x,y)a_{0}(y)\rm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,s,x,y)c(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s.

where
G_{d_{a}}
denotes the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d_{a}\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. We use the fact that
\displaystyle{a_{0}(y)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,0,x,y)\rm{d}y\leq 1}
for all
t\in(0,2)
. It yields

\left|\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,0,x,y)a_{0}(y)\rm{d}y\right|\leq\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

We use the following Green’s function estimate is from
[
10
]
[
3
]
: there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
, depending only on the domain, such that

0\leq G_{d_{a}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq\frac{K_{1}}{(4\pi(t_{1}-s))^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\kappa\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{(t_{1}-s)}}=:g_{d_{a}}(t_{1}-s,x-y),

for some constant
\kappa>0
depending only on
\Omega
and the diffusion coefficient
d
. Note that

\left\|g_{d}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq K_{2}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{N}{2}\right)

for some constant positive
K_{2}
depending on
z
. We in particular choose
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{z}=1-\frac{1}{p}}
which is admissible because
\displaystyle{p>\frac{N+2}{2}}
. Hence

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,s,x,y)c(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right|\leq 23^{\mu_{c}}K_{2}K_{c}.

It further yields the pointwise bound for the species
a
in the unit parabolic cylinder in initial time. More precisely, we have

(35)

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+23^{\mu_{c}}K_{2}K_{c}.

Next we consider the differential relation
\partial_{t}b\leq c
. It yields

(36)

\displaystyle b\leq b_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}c.

Employing Minkowski’s integral inequality on the above estimate we obtain

\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+3^{\mu_{c}}2^{\frac{2p-1}{p}}K_{c}\right).

Using the estimate (
35
), we derive the following estimate

\|ab\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2K_{2}K_{c}\right)\left(\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+3^{\mu_{c}}2^{\frac{2p-1}{p}}K_{c}\right)=:K_{b,[0,1]}.

Similar to the arguments for the species
a
, the above estimates yields the following pointwise estimate for the species
c

\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq\|c_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+3^{\mu_{c}}K_{2}K_{b,[0,1]}=:K_{c,[0,1]}.

The pointwise estimate of the species
b
in unit time is a consequence of the above estimate. The relation (
36
) and the non-negativity of the species
b
, yields

\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2K_{c,[0,1]}.

Consider the following constant
\displaystyle{K_{\infty,[0,2]}:=\max\left\{\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+23^{\mu_{c}}K_{2}K_{c},K_{c,[0,1]},\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2K_{c,[0,1]}\right\}}
. This helps us deduce

\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})},\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})},\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq K_{\infty,[0,2]}.

Let
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth function such that
\phi(0)=0
,
\phi\big|_{[1,\infty)}=1\text{\ and \ }\phi^{\prime}\in[0,M_{\phi}]
for some positive constant
M_{\phi}
. Let’s denote
\phi_{\tau}(s)=\phi(s-\tau)
. The product function
\phi_{\tau}a
satisfies the following initial-boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi_{\tau}a-d_{a}\Delta\phi_{\tau}a=&\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}a+\phi_{\tau}(c-ab)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla\phi_{\tau}a.n=&0\qquad\qquad\ \ \qquad\qquad\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\phi_{\tau}a(\tau,x)=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying integrability estimation (see Theorem
11
from the Appendix) on the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq

\displaystyle C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)\|\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}a+\phi_{\tau}c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

(37)

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau+1,\tau+2})}\leq

\displaystyle C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})K_{c}(1+\tau)^{\mu_{c}}.

Next we consider the differential relation
\partial_{t}b\leq c
. It yields

b\leq b_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}c.

Employing Minkowski’s integral inequality on the above estimate we obtain

\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,\tau})}\leq(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+(1+\tau)^{1+\mu_{c}}K_{c}\right).

We derive the following integral estimate of

\|ab\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})K_{c}(1+\tau)^{\mu_{c}}+(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+(1+\tau)^{1+\mu_{c}}K_{c}\right)

where
p>\frac{N+2}{2}
. Let us denote the following positive constant

\displaystyle{K_{3}:=C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})K_{c}+\left(\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{c}\right)}
.
It yields

\|ab\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq K_{3}(1+t)^{2+\mu_{c}}\qquad\mbox{where}\ p>\frac{N+2}{2}.

Considering the same smooth cutoff function
\phi

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi_{\tau}c-d_{c}\Delta\phi_{\tau}c=&\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}c+\phi_{\tau}(ab-c)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla\phi_{\tau}c.n=&0\qquad\qquad\ \ \qquad\qquad\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\phi_{\tau}c(\tau,x)=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

Integrability estimation(see Theorem
11
from Appendix) on the above equation yields

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq

\displaystyle C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)\|\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}c+\phi_{\tau}ab\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

(38)

\displaystyle\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau+1,\tau+2})}\leq

\displaystyle C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})(K_{c}+K_{3})(1+\tau)^{2+\mu_{c}}.

The pointwise estimate of the species
b
in unit time is a consequence of the above estimate. The relation (
36
) and the non-negativity of the species
b
, yields

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau})}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\tau)\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|c\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}(1+\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\tau)\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\bigg(C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)+K_{\infty,[0,2]}\bigg)(1+M_{\phi})(K_{c}+K_{3})(1+\tau)^{3+\mu_{c}}.

By choosing the constant

K_{\infty}:=\max\left\{\begin{aligned} &K_{\infty,[0,2]},C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})K_{c},C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)(1+M_{\phi})(K_{c}+K_{3}),\\
&\|b_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\bigg(C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p)+K_{\infty,[0,2]}\bigg)(1+M_{\phi})(K_{c}+K_{3})\end{aligned}\right\}

and
\mu=3+\mu_{c}
, we arrive at our result.
∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
N\geq 4
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenrate system (
2
). Let the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (
10
). Then the entropy dissipation
D(a,b,c)
satisfies

(39)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\mathcal{K}\,(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\mbox{ for }t\geq 0,

where the positive constant
\mathcal{K}
depends only on the dimension N, the domain
\Omega
, the constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
in the mass conservation properties (
4
)-(
5
) and the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a}
and
d_{c}
.

Proof.

We rewrite the entropy dissipation functional as

(40)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)=4d_{a}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla A\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+4d_{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla C\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}(ab-c)\ln\left(\frac{ab}{c}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

We recall an algebraic identity which says that for all
p,q\geq 0
, there holds
(p-q)\left(\ln p-\ln q\right)\geq 4\left(\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{q}\right)^{2}
. Using this algebraic identity in the last term and employing the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality (see Theorem
7
from the Appendix for the precise statement) for the first two terms of the above dissipation functional yields

(41)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+4\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where
P(\Omega)=C\left(\Omega,\frac{2N}{N-2}\right)
is the Poincaré constant. From the above inequality, it follows that

(42)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\eta\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

for any
0\leq\eta\leq 4
. As a consequence of the Hölder inequality, the inequality (
41
) leads to

D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{c}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+4\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

It is apparent from the above lower bound that a term involving
\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
is missing. To arrive at a lower bound involving this missing term, we fix an arbitrary constant
\varepsilon>0
(to be chosen later) and distinguish two cases: a case corresponding to

(43)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}\leq\varepsilon

and another case corresponding to

(44)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}>\varepsilon.

We first treat the case corresponding to (
43
). Observe that

\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|\left(\delta_{A}+\overline{A}\right)B-\left(\delta_{C}+\overline{C}\right)\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|\left(\,\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right)+\left(B\delta_{A}-\delta_{C}\right)\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

We recall an algebraic identity which says that for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds
(p-q)^{2}\geq\frac{p^{2}}{2}-q^{2}
. Using this algebraic identity in the above equality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\left\|B\delta_{A}-\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-2\left\|B\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-2\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

thanks to the algebraic identity
(p-q)^{2}\leq 2p^{2}+2q^{2}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
. Employing the Hölder inequality in the second and third terms of the lower bound in the above inequality results in

\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-2\left\|b\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}-2\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}.

Next, using the key integrability estimate on
\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}
from Lemma
2
yields

(45)

\displaystyle\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-2K_{3}\,(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}-2\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}.

In order to relate the above lower bound to
\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
, we further analyse the following term:

\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Note that if
\overline{A}\geq\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, then

(46)

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\varepsilon\left\|B-\overline{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

To see this, factorising
\overline{A}B
as
\overline{C}(1+\mu(x))
, we get

\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\overline{C}^{2}\overline{\mu^{2}}\left|\Omega\right|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\left\|B-\overline{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{\overline{C}^{2}}{\overline{A}^{2}}\left\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\overline{C}^{2}}{\overline{A}^{2}}\overline{\mu^{2}}\left|\Omega\right|\leq\frac{\overline{C}^{2}\overline{\mu^{2}}\left|\Omega\right|}{\varepsilon}.

On the other hand, let us consider the case when
\overline{A}<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Note that

\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\left|\Omega\right|\left(\overline{A^{2}}-\overline{A}^{2}\right)\implies\overline{A^{2}}\leq\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\right),

where we have also used the fact that we are dealing with the case
\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon
. Observe that

\displaystyle\overline{C}^{2}=\overline{C^{2}}-\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\overline{C^{2}}+\overline{A^{2}}-\overline{A^{2}}-\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq M_{1}-\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{2}{\left|\Omega\right|}\right),

thanks to the mass conservation property (
4
), the bound on
\overline{A^{2}}
from above and the fact that
\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon
. Now, using the algebraic identity
(p-q)^{2}\geq\frac{p^{2}}{2}-q^{2}
, we arrive at

\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{2}\left(\overline{C}^{2}-2\overline{A}^{2}\overline{B^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{2}\left(M_{1}-\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{2}{\left|\Omega\right|}\right)-2\varepsilon M_{2}\right)

where we have used the aforementioned lower bound for
\overline{C}^{2}
, the mass conservation property (
5
) and that
\overline{A}<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Let us now choose

(47)

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\frac{M_{1}}{2}\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{\left|\Omega\right|+2+2M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|}.

With the above choice of
\varepsilon
, we obtain

(48)

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{A}B-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{M_{1}\left|\Omega\right|}{4}\geq\frac{M_{1}}{4M_{2}}\left\|B-\overline{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used the observation (
49
) from earlier. Using (
46
) and (
48
) in (
45
) and (
42
) helps us deduce that

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}-2\,\eta\,K_{3}\,(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\right)\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\left(\frac{4d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}-2\,\eta\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\right)\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\eta\varepsilon\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

for the case
\overline{A}\geq\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and that

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}-2\,\eta\,K_{3}\,(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\right)\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\left(\frac{4d_{c}}{P(\Omega)}-2\,\eta\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\right)\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}+\eta\frac{M_{1}}{8M_{2}}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

for the case
\overline{A}<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Next, observe that by taking

\eta(t):=\left(\frac{2\min\{d_{a},d_{c},2\}}{P(\Omega)\left(K_{3}+\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\right)+1}\right)(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}},

we obtain

D(a,b,c)\geq\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}\left|\Omega\right|}{2\left|\Omega\right|+4+4M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|},\frac{M_{1}}{8M_{2}}\right\}\eta(t)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Observe that the above choice of
\eta
does satisfy
0\leq\eta\leq 4
.
Hence we deduce in the case corresponding to (
43
) that

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)+\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{2d_{c}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}\left|\Omega\right|}{2\left|\Omega\right|+4+4M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|},\frac{M_{1}}{8M_{2}}\right\}\eta(t)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{K}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

for all
t\geq 0
, where

\mathcal{K}_{1}:=\min\left\{\frac{2d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)},\frac{2d_{c}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)},\min\left\{\frac{M_{1}\left|\Omega\right|}{2\left|\Omega\right|+4-4M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|},\frac{M_{1}}{8M_{2}}\right\}\left(\frac{\min\{d_{a},d_{c},2\}}{P(\Omega)\left(K_{3}+\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{N}}\right)+1}\right)\right\}.

The case corresponding to (

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\min\left\{d_{a},d_{c}\right\}\max\left\{\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}

(49)

\displaystyle\left\|B-\overline{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|B\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\overline{B}\left|\Omega\right|\leq\left\|B\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|b\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|,

which is a consequence of the mass conservation property (

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)+\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{2d_{c}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{2\varepsilon\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{N}}\min\left\{d_{a},d_{c}\right\}}{P(\Omega)M_{2}\left|\Omega\right|}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{K}_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

for all

yields the desired result.
∎

Proposition 6
.

Let
N\geq 4
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
2
). Let
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
be the associated equilibrium state given by (
6
)-(
8
). Let the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a},d_{c}
satisfy the closeness condition (
10
). Then, for any given positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists a time
T_{\varepsilon}
and two positive constants
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that

(50)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\leq\mathcal{S}_{1}\,e^{-\mathcal{S}_{2}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{N-1}}}\qquad\mbox{ for }\,t\geq T_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The relative entropy reads

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(a\ln{a}-a-a_{\infty}\ln{a_{\infty}}+a_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(b\ln{b}-b-b_{\infty}\ln{b_{\infty}}+b_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left(c\ln{c}-c-c_{\infty}\ln{c_{\infty}}+c_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

Using the relation (
7
), the above expression for the relative entropy becomes

(51)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(a\ln{\frac{a}{a_{\infty}}}-a+a_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(b\ln{\frac{b}{b_{\infty}}}-b+b_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(c\ln{\frac{c}{c_{\infty}}}-c+c_{\infty}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

Let us define a function
\Phi:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(52)

\Phi(x,y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{x\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-x+y}{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}}&\qquad\mbox{ for }x\not=y,\\
2&\qquad\mbox{ for }x=y.\end{aligned}\right.

It can be shown (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, p.162]
for details) that the above defined function satisfies the following bound:

(53)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y)\leq C_{\Phi}\max\left\{1,\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right\}

for some positive constant
C_{\Phi}
. Next we rewrite the relative entropy as

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(a,a_{\infty})(A-A_{\infty})^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Phi(b,b_{\infty})(B-B_{\infty})^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Phi(c,c_{\infty})(C-C_{\infty})^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Note that for any
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
q>0
, we have

\ln p-\ln q\leq\ln(1+\left|p\right|)+\left|\ln q\right|\leq 1+\ln\left|p\right|+\left|\ln q\right|

and for any
0<p<\frac{1}{2}
and
q>0
, we have

\ln p-\ln q\leq\left|\ln q\right|.

This helps us arrive at

(54)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-

\displaystyle E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(1+\ln(1+t)\right)\left(\left\|A-A_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|B-B_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|C-C_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

for all
t\geq 0
, where the positive constant
C_{1}
is given by

C_{1}:=C_{\Phi}\left(1+\left|\ln a_{\infty}\right|+\left|\ln b_{\infty}\right|+\left|\ln c_{\infty}\right|+\left|\ln K_{\infty}\right|+\mu\right).

Here the constants
K_{\infty}
and
\mu
are the ones appearing in the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
bounds on the concentrations from Lemma
4
. The factor
\left(1+\ln(1+t)\right)
in the above estimate of the relative entropy is due to the fact that Lemma
4
says that at least one of the concentrations has a polynomial (in time) bound on its
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm. It should be noted that having an uniform (in time) bound on all of the concentrations gets rid of this time factor. In
[
4
]
, the authors prove the following bound

(55)

\displaystyle\left\|A-A_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|B-B_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\left\|C-C_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

exploiting only the conservation properties (
4
)-(
5
). Furthermore, the constant
C_{2}
in the above bound depends only on the equilibrium states
A_{\infty},B_{\infty},C_{\infty}
and the constants
M_{1},M_{2}
from the conservation properties (
4
)-(
5
) (see
[
4
, Lemma 3.2, p.168]
for precise expression for the constant). We thus arrive at the following bound for the relative entropy using (
54
) and (
55
):

(56)

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-

\displaystyle E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\left(1+\ln(1+t)\right)\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

where the positive constant
C_{3}:=C_{1}C_{2}
. Hence, thanks to the lower bound (
39
) obtained in Proposition
4
and the lower bound (
41
), it follows from (
56
) that

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\leq C_{3}\left(1+\ln(1+t)\right)\max\left\{\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}},\frac{1}{4}\right\}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}D(a,b,c).

Note that for any given positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists a time
T_{\varepsilon}
such that

\ln(1+t)<(1+t)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{N-1}}\qquad\mbox{ for all }t\geq T_{\varepsilon}.

Hence we have

E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\leq C_{4}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2+\varepsilon}{N-1}}D(a,b,c)\qquad\mbox{ for all }t\geq T_{\varepsilon},

where the constant
C_{4}:=C_{3}\max\left\{\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}},\frac{1}{4}\right\}
. Recall that we have

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)=-D(a,b,c)\qquad\mbox{ for all }t>0.

Thus we have

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)\leq-\frac{1}{C_{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2+\varepsilon}{N-1}}\left(E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)\qquad\mbox{ for all }t\geq T_{\varepsilon}.

Integrating the above differential inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle(a(t,\cdot),b(t,\cdot),c(t,\cdot))-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\left(E(a(T_{\varepsilon},\cdot),b(T_{\varepsilon},\cdot),c(T_{\varepsilon},\cdot))-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)e^{-\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}(1+T_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{N-1}}}e^{-\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{N-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(E(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)e^{-\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}}e^{-\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{N-1}}}.

We have thus proved the sub-exponential decay (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(E(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)e^{-\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N-1}{C_{4}(1-\varepsilon)}.

∎

Proof of Theorem

:

We have already obtained sub-exponential decay (in time) of the relative entropy in Proposition

\displaystyle E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\geq\frac{\left(3+2\sqrt{2}\right)|\Omega|}{2M_{1}\left(9+2\sqrt{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(3+2\sqrt{2}\right)|\Omega|}{\left(M_{1}+M_{2}\right)\left(9+2\sqrt{2}\right)}\left\|c-c_{\infty}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.

3.
The case of
d_{c}=0

In this section, we shall devote our attention to the study of the degenerate model (
3
) which corresponds to the vanishing of the diffusion coefficient
d_{c}
. The existence of a smooth positive solution to (
3
) was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 3.2]
for all smooth initial non-negative data if the dimension
N\leq 3
. Our main objective of this section is to understand the large time behaviour of solutions to (
3
). Here, we choose to present our computations when the dimension
N=3
. Analogous results hold true in dimensions one and two as well. Note that we have the entropy equality

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(E(a,b,c)-E(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})\right)=-D(a,b,c)\qquad\mbox{ for all }t>0,

where
(a,b,c)
is the solution to the degenerate model (
3
) and
(a_{\infty},b_{\infty},c_{\infty})
is the corresponding equilibrium state given by (
6
) and (
8
). The dissipation functional
D
in the above equality is given by

D(a,b,c)=4d_{a}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla A\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+4d_{b}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla B\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}(ab-c)\ln\left(\frac{ab}{c}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

We arrive at the following straightforward lower bound for the dissipation:

(57)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+4\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

thanks to the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality and an algebraic identity which says that for all
p,q\geq 0
, there holds
(p-q)\left(\ln p-\ln q\right)\geq 4\left(\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{q}\right)^{2}
. An application of the Hölder inequality leads to

(58)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{b}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+4\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

It is apparent from the above inequality that the term involving
\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
is missing from its lower bound. A similar scenario was handled in the previous section while dealing with the missing
\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
term. Our strategy was to derive polynomial (in time) bounds on the supremum norms of the concentrations. Here too, we will adapt a similar approach. Note, however, that the proofs of most results in this section markedly differ from the proofs in the previous section. We begin with a time-dependent
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
estimate on certain combinations of the concentrations. This result is inspired by
[
6
, Theorem 3.1, p.495]
. Our proof argues along similar lines as in
[
6
]
while keeping track of the polynomial (in time) bound.

Lemma 5
.

Let
N\leq 3
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
3
). Then, there exist positive constants
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
such that for all
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(a^{2}(s,x)+a(s,x)c(s,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{1}\left(1+t\right),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(b^{2}(s,x)+b(s,x)c(s,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{2}\left(1+t\right).

Proof.

Adding up the equations for
a
and
c
in the degenerate model (
3
), we obtain

(59)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(a+c\right)-d_{a}\Delta a=0.

Let
H\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be arbitrary and let
Z:=\frac{a\,d_{a}}{a+c}
. Now consider the following backward parabolic problem:

(60)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\partial_{t}w-Z\Delta w&=H\,\sqrt{Z}\qquad\mbox{ in }\ \Omega_{T},\\
\nabla w\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega_{T},\\
w(T,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

In this duality approach, the idea is to multiply the equation (
59
) by the solution
w
to the backward problem (
60
) followed by an integration over
\Omega_{T}
leading to

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}w(0,x)\left(a_{0}(x)+c_{0}(x)\right)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(a+c\right)\partial_{t}w\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}d_{a}a\Delta w\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t=0,

thanks to integration by parts. Using the equation satisfied by
w
in (
60
), we deduce the following:

(61)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(a+c\right)\,H\,\sqrt{Z}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t=\int_{\Omega}w(0,x)\left(a_{0}(x)+c_{0}(x)\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq\left\|w(0,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|a_{0}+c_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where the inequality is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Our next objective is to get an estimate for
\left\|w(0,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
. To that end, multiply the evolution equation by
-\Delta w
and integrating with respect to the spatial variable yields

\int_{\Omega}\Delta w\partial_{t}w\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}Z\left(\Delta w\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\Omega}H\sqrt{Z}\Delta w\,{\rm d}x.

Performing an integration by parts in the first term on the left hand side (while using the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition from (
60
)) and employing Young’s inequality for the term on the right hand side yields

-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}Z\left(\Delta w\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}H^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}Z\left(\Delta w\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Integrating the above inequality on the interval
(0,T)
in the time variable results in

(62)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w(0,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Z\left(\Delta w\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}H^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t.

Hence, invoking Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality, we get

\int_{\Omega}\left(w(0,x)-\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\int_{\Omega}w(0,y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq P(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w(0,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

where
P(\Omega)
is the Poincaré constant. Note that integrating the evolution equation in (
60
) over
\Omega_{T}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w(0,x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Z\Delta w\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}H\sqrt{Z}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Z\left(\Delta w\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}H^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left\|\sqrt{Z}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|H\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\|\sqrt{Z}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}

thanks to the estimate from (
62
). Using the fact that
Z\leq d_{a}
, we deduce

(63)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}w(0,x)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{2}\leq 4d_{a}T\left|\Omega\right|\left\|H\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|w(0,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq 2\int_{\Omega}\left(w(0,x)-\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\int_{\Omega}w(0,y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{2}{\left|\Omega\right|}\left(\int_{\Omega}w(0,x)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{2}\leq\left(2P(\Omega)+8d_{a}T\right)\left\|H\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})},

thanks to (
62
) and (
63
). Going back to (
61
), we have thus obtained

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(a+c\right)\,H\,\sqrt{Z}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\left(2P(\Omega)+8d_{a}T\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|a_{0}+c_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|H\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

Since the above inequality holds true for arbitrary
H\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we deduce by duality that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(a+c\right)^{2}Z\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\left(2P(\Omega)+8d_{a}T\right)\left\|a_{0}+c_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Substituting for
Z
in the above inequality, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(a^{2}+ac\right)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\left(\frac{2P(\Omega)+8d_{a}}{d_{a}}\right)\left\|a_{0}+c_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}(1+T).

Proceeding exactly as above but working with the equation satisfied by
b+c
, we can obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(b^{2}+bc\right)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\left(\frac{2P(\Omega)+8d_{b}}{d_{b}}\right)\left\|b_{0}+c_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}(1+T).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6

Proposition 8
.

Let
N\leq 3
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
3
). Then, there exist positive constants
K_{\infty}
and
\mu
such that for all
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|a\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\infty}\left(1+t\right)^{\mu}

\displaystyle\left\|b\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\infty}\left(1+t\right)^{\mu}

\displaystyle\left\|c\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\infty}\left(1+t\right)^{\mu}

Proof.

Let
G_{d_{a}}
denote the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d_{a}\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. We can express the solution
a
as follows:

(67)

\displaystyle a(t,x)=\tilde{a}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t-s,x,y)\left[c-ab\right](s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s,

where
\tilde{a}
solves the following initial boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\tilde{a}-d_{a}\Delta\tilde{a}&=0\qquad\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega_{t},\\
\nabla\tilde{a}\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\ \mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega_{t},\\
\tilde{a}(0,x)&=a_{0}\qquad\mbox{ in }\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

We recall the following Gaussian bound on the Neumann Green’s function (see
[
10
, Theorem 2.2, p.37]
): there exist positive constants
\texttt{C}_{H},\kappa
such that

(68)

\displaystyle\left|G_{d_{a}}(t-s,x,y)\right|\leq\texttt{C}_{H}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\kappa\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{(t-s)}}=:g(t-s,x-y)

Also see
[
3
, Theorem 3.1, p.639]
for general parabolic operators. As a consequence we have the following bound on the solution
\tilde{a}
to the above homogeneous problem:

(69)

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{a}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{S}\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

for some positive constant
C_{S}
, independent of time, and for any
p\geq 1
. In (
67
), the positivity of
a
and
b
leads to

a(t,x)\leq\tilde{a}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t-s,x,y)c(s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s.

Using the aforementioned Gaussian bound, we arrive at

a(t,x)\leq\tilde{a}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}g(t-s,x-y)c(s,y)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}s.

Computing the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
norm in the
x
variable, the above inequality leads to

\left\|a(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{S}\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|g(t-s,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\left\|c(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}s,

thanks to the bound (
69
), the Minkowski’s integral inequality and the Young’s convolution inequality with

1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}.

Therefore, there exists a positive constant
C_{H,N,r}
such that

(70)

\displaystyle\left\|a(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{S}\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+C_{H,N,r}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|c(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}s

Taking
q=3
and
p=\infty
in the above bound, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|a(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+C_{H,N,r}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{6}}\left\|c(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+C_{H,N,r}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{6}}(1+s)\,{\rm d}s,

thanks to the bound from Lemma
6
. Hence we arrive at

\left\|a\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq K_{\infty}(1+t)^{\frac{18-N}{6}}

for some positive constant
K_{\infty}
. Arguing along exactly same lines, we can obtain an estimate of
b
in the supremum norm as well. Again, exploiting the positivity of
c
, we have from (
3
)

c(t,x)\leq c_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}a(s,x)b(s,x)\,{\rm d}s.

The above supremum norm estimates on
a
and
b
will help us arrive at the supremum norm estimate for
c
as well.
∎

Recall from the lower bound in (

\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
is apparently missing. Similar to Propositions
4

Proposition 9
.

Let
N\leq 3
and let
(a,b,c)
be the solution to the degenerate system (
3
). Then the entropy dissipation
D(a,b,c)
satisfies

(71)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\mathcal{K}_{c}\,(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\mbox{ for }t\geq 0,

where the positive constant
\mathcal{K}_{c}
depends only on the domain
\Omega
, the constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
in the mass conservation properties (
4
)-(
5
) and the nonzero diffusion coefficients
d_{a}
and
d_{b}
.

Proof.

It follows from (
57
) that

(72)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\frac{4d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\eta\left\|AB-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

for any
0\leq\eta\leq 4
. In order to relate the dissipation functional to the missing
\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
term, we work on the following term:

\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\delta_{A}+\overline{A}\right)\left(\delta_{B}+\overline{B}\right)-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3\left\|\overline{A}\,\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3\left\|\overline{B}\,\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3\left\|\delta_{A}\,\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used the following algebraic identities that hold for all
p,q,r\in\mathbb{R}
:

\left(p-q\right)^{2}\geq\frac{p^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\left(p+q+r\right)^{2}\leq 3\left(p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{2}\right).

Using the mass conservation property (
4
), we have

\int_{\Omega}A(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\leq\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}a(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\implies\overline{A}\leq M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, we have
\overline{B}\leq M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Hence we arrive at

\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3\left\|\delta_{A}\,\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Employing the Hölder inequality in the last three terms in the above lower bound leads to

\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3\left\|\delta_{A}^{2}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}

Using the algebraic identity
(p-q)^{2}\leq 2(p^{2}+q^{2})
in the last term of the above lower bound, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle-3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle-6\left\|A^{2}+\overline{A}^{2}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}.

By employing triangular inequality in the last term of the above lower bound, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+6M_{1}\right)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-6\left\|a\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\left(\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+6M_{1}\right)+6\ell_{3}\left(1+t\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)},

thanks to the estimate on
\left\|a\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}
from Lemma
6
. Now we claim that

\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\left\|C-\overline{C}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

To see this, factorising
C
as
\overline{A}\,\overline{B}(1+\mu(x))
, we get

\left\|\overline{A}\,\overline{B}-C\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\overline{A}^{2}\,\overline{B}^{2}\,\overline{\mu^{2}}\,\left|\Omega\right|\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\left\|C-\overline{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\overline{A}^{2}\,\overline{B}^{2}\left\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\overline{A}^{2}\,\overline{B}^{2}\,\overline{\mu^{2}}\,\left|\Omega\right|.

Putting it all together, we arrive at

\left\|AB-C\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\left(\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+6M_{1}\right)+6\ell_{3}\left(1+t\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}-3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}.

Hence it follows from (
72
) that

(73)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\left(\frac{4d_{a}}{P(\Omega)}-3\eta\,M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}+\frac{\eta}{2}\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{4d_{b}}{P(\Omega)}-\eta\,\left(\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+6M_{1}\right)+6\ell_{3}\left(1+t\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\right)\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}.

Then, it follows from (
73
) that the dissipation functional has the following lower bound:

(74)

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq\frac{1}{2}\eta(t)\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Observe that the above choice of
\eta
clearly satisfies
0\leq\eta\leq 4
. To conclude our proof, we write

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)+\frac{1}{2}D(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{A}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{2d_{b}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)}\left\|\delta_{B}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{4}\eta(t)\left\|\delta_{C}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used the lower bound from (
72

74

\mathcal{K}_{c}

\mathcal{K}_{c}:=\min\left\{\frac{2d_{a}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)},\frac{2d_{b}\left|\Omega\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{P(\Omega)},\frac{\min\left\{d_{a},d_{b},1\right\}}{P(\Omega)\left(3M_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(3M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+6M_{1}\right)+6\ell_{3}\right)+1}\right\}.

Lemma 7

There exists a positive constant

, depending only on

q
, such that

P(\Omega)\left\|\nabla f\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\left\|f-\overline{f}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{ for all }f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega),

where

q=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{2N}{N-2}&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N\geq 3,\\
\in[1,\infty)&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N=2,\\
\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N=1.\end{aligned}\right.

We refer to
P(\Omega)
as the Poincaré constant.

Theorem 11

(Second Order Regularity and Integrability estimation)
.

Let
d>0
and let
\tau\in[0,T)
. Take
\theta\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})
for some
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\psi
be the solution to the backward heat equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi+d\Delta\psi&=-\theta\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{\tau,T},\\
\nabla\psi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\ \ \mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[\tau,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(T,x)&=0\qquad\ \ \mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{SOR}
, depending only on the domain
\Omega
, the dimension
N
and the exponent
p
such that the following maximal regularity holds:

(76)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq\frac{C_{SOR}}{d}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}.

Moreover, if
\theta\geq 0
then
\psi(t,x)\geq 0
for almost every
(t,x)\in\Omega_{\tau,T}
. Furthermore, we have

(77)

\displaystyle\mbox{If }

\displaystyle p<\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\qquad\mbox{ for all }\,\,s<\frac{(N+2)p}{N+2-2p}

\displaystyle\mbox{If }

\displaystyle p=\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\qquad\mbox{ for all }\,\,s<\infty

where the constant
C_{IE}=C_{IE}(T-\tau,\Omega,d,p,s)
and

(78)

if

\displaystyle p>\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}.

The proof of (
76
) can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
. We refer to the constant
C_{SOR}
as the second order regularity constant. Proof of the estimates (
77
) can be found in
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
and the estimate (
78
) was derived in
[
15
, Lemma 4.6]
. We refer to the constant
C_{IE}
as the integrability estimation constant.

Theorem 12

(
p^{\textrm{th}}
order integrability estimation)
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and let
p^{\prime}
be its Hölder conjugate. Let
M(t,x)
be such that the following holds

\theta\leq M(t,x)\leq\Theta\quad\forall(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},

for some fixed positive constants
\theta,\Theta
. Let
\psi_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)
and let
\psi
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\left(M\psi\right)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\nabla\psi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,x)&=\psi_{0}(x)\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then the following estimate holds

\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\left(1+\Theta K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}\right)T^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)},

where the constant
K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}
is given by

K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}:=\frac{C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)}{1-C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)}\qquad\mbox{ provided we have }\qquad C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)<1.

Here, the constant
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{\prime}}
is the best constant in the following parabolic regularity estimate:

\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C^{PRC}_{r,p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})},

where
\phi,f:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are any two functions such that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})
and they satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi+r\Delta\phi&=f\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\nabla\phi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\phi(T,x)&=0\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

It has to be noted that
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{{}^{\prime}}}<\infty
for
r>0
and
C^{PRC}_{r,2}\leq\frac{1}{r}
and depends only on
r,p^{{}^{\prime}}
, the domain and on the dimension, i.e it is independent of time. Moreover, as
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{{}^{\prime}}}<\infty
, if we take the difference between
\theta
and
\Theta
sufficiently small, then we have the required property that

C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)<1.

Proof of the above theorem can be found in
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
.

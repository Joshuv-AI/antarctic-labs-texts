Cluster Configuration Spaces of Finite Type
Nima ARKANI-HAMED a, Song HE bcde and Thomas LAM f

Abstract
For each Dynkin diagram D D , we define a “cluster configuration space” ℳ D {\mathcal{M}}_{D} and a partial compactification ℳ ~ D {\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D} . For D = A n − 3 D=A_{n-3} , we have ℳ A n − 3 = ℳ 0 , n {\mathcal{M}}_{A_{n-3}}={\mathcal{M}}_{0,n} , the configuration space of n n points on ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} , and the partial compactification ℳ ~ A n − 3 {\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{A_{n-3}} was studied in this case by Brown. The space ℳ ~ D {\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D} is a smooth affine algebraic variety with a stratification in bijection with the faces of the Chapoton–Fomin–Zelevinsky generalized associahedron. The regular functions on ℳ ~ D {\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D} are generated by coordinates u γ u_{\gamma} , in bijection with the cluster variables of type D D , and the relations are described completely in terms of the compatibility degree function of the cluster algebra. As an application, we define and study cluster algebra analogues of tree-level open string amplitudes.

Introduction

1.1.
The configuration space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
of
n
distinct points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a smooth affine algebraic variety of dimension
n{-}3
, and it has a very well-studied Deligne–Knudsen–Mumford compactification
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
, which is a smooth projective algebraic variety. The boundary of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
consists of
2^{n{-}1}{-}n{-}1
divisors, satisfying factorization: each divisor is itself a product
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n_{1}}\times{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n_{2}}
, where
n_{1}{+}n_{2}=n{+}2
.

The real points
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
have the structure of a smooth real manifold with
(n{-}1)!/2
connected components. Fixing once and for all a (dihedral) ordering on
n
points, we let
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
denote the connected component where the
n
points are ordered on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})=S^{1}
. The closure
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{\geq 0}
of
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}
in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
is a stratified space that is homeomorphic to the face stratification of the associahedron polytope.

Let
W
denote the union of those boundary divisors in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
whose intersection with
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{\geq 0}
is empty, and let
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}:={\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}\setminus W
. The divisors that do intersect
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{\geq 0}
correspond to ways to divide
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
into two cyclic intervals, each of size greater than or equal to two. For example,
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,5}
has ten boundary divisors, and
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,5}
includes five of them, corresponding to the five sides of the pentagon (the associahedron of dimension two). Somewhat surprisingly, the partial compactification
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
is an affine algebraic variety, and its ring of regular functions has the following description. Let
u_{ij}
be variables labeled by the diagonals
(i,j)
(not including sides) of a
n
-gon
P_{n}
. Then
{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}\big]
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[u_{ij}]
modulo the relations

\displaystyle R_{ij}:=u_{ij}+\Bigg(\prod_{(k,\ell)\text{ crossing }(i,j)}u_{k\ell}\Bigg)-1,\qquad(i,j)\ \text{varying over all diagonals},

(1.1)

and
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\subset{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
is the locus where
u_{ij}\neq 0
. The
u_{ij}
are called
dihedral coordinates
. Brown
[
11
]
describes the same space using a presentation with more relations (see Section 10.1); the extra relations are implied by our smaller set. The
u_{ij}
are cross-ratios (see (
7.1
)) on
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
and appeared in the study of scattering amplitudes in string theory and for the bi-adjoint
\phi^{3}
-theory
[
1
]
.

1.2.
In this paper, we construct in an analogous manner two affine algebraic varieties
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\!\subset\!{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
for each Dynkin diagram
D
of finite type by considering the relations

\displaystyle R_{\gamma}:=u_{\gamma}+\prod_{\omega}u_{\omega}^{(\omega||\gamma)}-1.

(1.2)

Here,
\gamma
and
\omega
denote mutable cluster variables of a cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of type
D

[
18
]
, and
(\omega||\gamma)
denotes the compatibility degree. We call
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
the
cluster configuration space of type
D
. In the case
D=A_{n-3}
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}_{A_{n-3}}={\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
and
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{A_{n-3}}={\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
. Amongst many remarkable properties of these relations, let us immediately note that
u_{\gamma}=0
forces
u_{\omega}=1
for all
\omega
such that
(\gamma||\omega)\neq 0
(or equivalently,
(\omega||\gamma)\neq 0
). Thus, factorization is manifest in (
1.2
). Some of the results of this work were reported in
[
4
]
, and
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
is an example of the notion of “binary geometry” discussed therein.

Whereas
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
has a stratification indexed by the faces of the associahedron, the space
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
has a stratification (Proposition
3.5
) indexed by the faces of the Chapoton–Fomin–Zelevinsky generalized associahedron for
D^{\vee}

[
12
,
17
]
. We show (Theorem
3.3
) that
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
and
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
are smooth affine algebraic varieties and that the boundary stratification of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
is simple normal-crossing. These geometric properties depend on integrality properties of the normal fan
{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\vee})
of the generalized associahedron, and an isomorphism (Theorem
5.3
) between
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
and an affine open subset of the projective toric variety
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\vee})}
associated to
{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\vee})
. Like
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{\geq 0}
, the variety
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
contains a distinguished nonnegative part
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}
, which is a stratified space homeomorphic to the face stratification of the generalized associahedron (Theorem
8.1
). The positive part
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
is a distinguished connected component in
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
, and is cut out by the conditions
u_{\gamma}>0
. Though
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
is not compact,
\big({\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D},({\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0})_{\geq 0}\big)
satisfies the other properties of a positive geometry in the sense of
[
2
]
.

1.3.
The configuration space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
is isomorphic to the quotient of an open subset
{\mathring{{\rm Gr}}}(2,n)\subset{\rm Gr}(2,n)
of the Grassmannian of 2-planes by the diagonal torus
T\subset\operatorname{SL}_{n}
acting on
{\rm Gr}(2,n)
. Let
{\tilde{B}}
be a full rank acyclic extended exchange matrix of type
D
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
be the corresponding cluster algebra,
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)=\operatorname{Spec}\big({\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)\big)
be the cluster variety, and let
{\mathring{X}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)\subset X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
denote the locus where all cluster variables are non-vanishing. We show (Theorem
4.2
) that
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is isomorphic to the (free) quotient of
{\mathring{X}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
by the cluster automorphism group
T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
, generalizing the construction of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
from
{\rm Gr}(2,n)
. The functions
u_{\gamma}
are particular
T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
-invariant rational functions on
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
. The
u_{\gamma}
are related to some of the “cluster
X
-coordinates” in the sense of Fock and Goncharov
[
16
]
by the equation
u=X/(1+X)
. The cluster
X
-coordinates appearing here are exactly those encountered in the Auslander–Reiten walk through cluster variables, beginning from an acyclic quiver and mutating only on sources. It is important to note that while the
u_{\gamma}
are simply related to the cluster
X
-variables in this way, they are actually in bijection with the cluster
A
-variables, while in general there are more cluster
X
-variables than cluster
A
-variables.

We do not have a good understanding of the relationship between
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
and cluster
{\mathcal{X}}
-varieties; for example,
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
does not contain a collection of (cluster) torus charts.

Our approach depends crucially on the flexibility in the choice of
{\tilde{B}}
. When
{\tilde{B}}={\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{univ}}
is the extended exchange matrix for the universal coefficient cluster algebra
[
19
,
27
]
, the relation (
1.2
) is obtained from the primitive exchange relations of
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big)
by setting all mutable cluster variables to 1, and sending the universal frozen variables
z_{\gamma}
to
u_{\gamma}
. The non-primitive exchange relations give rise to other relations of the form
U+U^{\prime}=1
, where
U
and
U^{\prime}
are monomials in the
u_{\gamma}
-s.

When
{\tilde{B}}={\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{prin}}
is the extended exchange matrix for the principal coefficient cluster algebra, the functions
u_{\gamma}
become identified with certain ratios of the
F
-polynomials
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
. Bazier-Matte, Douville, Mousavand, Thomas, and Yildrim have shown
[
8
]
in the case that
D
is simply-laced that the Newton polytope of
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
has normal fan a coarsening of the
{\mathbf{g}}
-vector fan
{\mathcal{N}}\big(D^{\vee}\big)
of
D^{\vee}
, and this result was extended to skew-symmetric cluster algebras by Fei
[
15
]
. We extend via folding this description to the case that
D
is multiply-laced finite type Dynkin diagram. The identification of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
with an open subset of the toric variety
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\vee})}
depends crucially on this analysis.

As an application of our results on quotients of cluster varieties and on
F
-polynomials, we identify (Theorem
9.2
) the positive tropicalization
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
of the cluster configuration space with the cluster fan
{\mathcal{N}}\big(D^{\vee}\big)
. In particular, we resolve a conjecture of Speyer and Williams
[
29
, Conjecture 8.1]
on positive tropicalizations of cluster varieties of finite type; see also
[
22
]
.

1.4.

Inspired by similar questions for
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, we proceed with studying the topology of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
. We identify
{\mathcal{M}}_{B_{n}}
with the complement to the Shi-hyperplane arrangement and thereby compute point counts over finite fields, and the Euler characteristics of
{\mathcal{M}}_{B_{n}}({\mathbb{R}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{B_{n}}({\mathbb{C}})
. We give a configuration space style description of
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
(Proposition
7.5
) but were not able to determine whether
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
is a hyperplane arrangement complement. Nevertheless, we were able to compute the point count for
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, and the number of connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{R}})
. We found numerically the point counts for types
D_{4}
,
D_{5}
and
G_{2}
, and obtained numerically that the point count of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D_{4}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is not a polynomial in
q
but a quasi-polynomial.

1.5.
One of the main motivations for us are scattering amplitudes in string theory. In
[
3
]
, we introduced integral functions, called
stringy canonical forms
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}}\prod_{i}\frac{{\rm d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}X_{i}}\prod_{j}p_{j}({\mathbf{x}})^{-\alpha^{\prime}c_{j}},

(1.3)

where
p_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
is a positive Laurent polynomial. We showed in
[
3
]
that the leading order

\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}(\alpha^{\prime})^{n}{\mathcal{I}}
is a rational function that for fixed
c_{j}
-s coincides with the canonical rational function
[
2
]
of the Minkowski sum of the Newton polytopes of
p_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
. Tree-level
n
-point open superstring amplitudes are integrals on
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. It turns out that for a suitable parametrization of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, these amplitudes can be written as an integral
{\mathcal{I}}_{A_{n-3}}
in the form (
1.3
), where the
p_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
are the
F
-polynomials for the type
A_{n-3}
cluster algebra. The importance of the
u_{ij}
-variables appears in the rewriting (see
[
3
, Section 9]
or
[
10
, Section 3]
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{A_{n-3}}=\int_{({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}}\Omega(({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0})\prod_{(i,j)}u_{ij}^{\alpha^{\prime}X_{ij}}

of the open-string amplitude. The poles of
{\mathcal{I}}_{A_{n-3}}
are given by
X_{ij}=0
, and at this pole, the factorization of
{\mathcal{I}}_{A_{n-3}}
mimics the factorization of the equations (
1.1
). We define the
cluster string amplitude
(where
x_{\gamma}
,
x_{i}
are cluster variables of
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
of type
D
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{D}:=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}}\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0})\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}x_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{\gamma}}\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+m}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{i}}.

The poles of
{\mathcal{I}}_{D}
are made manifest by rewriting in terms of the
u_{\gamma}
-s, and the leading order of
{\mathcal{I}}_{D}
is controlled by the combinatorics of the generalized associahedron of
D^{\vee}
.

2
Background on cluster algebras and generalized associahedra

In this section we review basic facts concerning cluster algebras. The most important cluster algebra references for us are
[
8
,
31
]
. For cluster varieties, our conventions follow
[
23
]
.

2.1.

Let
D
be a finite Dynkin diagram with vertex set
I
, and let
A=(a_{ij})
denote the
n\times n
Cartan matrix of
D
, where
n=|I|
. Let
B
be a skew-symmetrizable exchange matrix, i.e., there exists a matrix
Z
with positive diagonal entries such that
ZB
is skew-symmetric. We say that
B=(B_{ij})
has type
D
if

\displaystyle a_{ij}=\begin{cases}2&\mbox{if}\quad i=j,\\
-|B_{ij}|&\mbox{if}\quad i\neq j.\end{cases}

In standard cluster algebra language,
B
corresponds to an
acyclic
initial seed of a cluster algebra of finite type
D
.
Given
D
, the possible exchange matrices
B
of type
D
are in bijection with orientations of the underlying tree of
D
: writing
i\to j
for the directed edges of this orientation,
we have

\displaystyle B_{ij}=\begin{cases}-a_{ij}&\mbox{if}\quad i\to j,\\
a_{ij}&\mbox{if}\quad j\to i,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For an
(n+m)\times n
extended exchange matrix
\tilde{B}
extending
B
, we let
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
denote the corresponding cluster algebra of geometric type
[
18
]
. By convention,
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
is the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra generated by all mutable cluster variables, all frozen variables, and the inverses of all frozen variables. We let
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
denote the
cluster variety

[
23
]
. This is a complex affine algebraic variety, and in general it differs from the union of cluster tori, which is sometimes called a cluster manifold.

2.2.

We say that
{\tilde{B}}
(or
{\mathcal{A}}
or
X
) has
full rank
if
{\tilde{B}}
has rank
n
. We say that
{\tilde{B}}
(or
{\mathcal{A}}
or
X
) has
really full rank
if the rows of
{\tilde{B}}
span
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. If
{\tilde{B}}
has full rank, then
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
is a smooth affine algebraic variety
[
24
, Theorem 7.7]
.

2.3.

Let
\Pi=\Pi(B)
be the indexing set for cluster variables, which depends only on
B
. Set
r:=|\Pi|
. For
\gamma\in\Pi
, we let
x_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
denote the corresponding cluster variable. (Abusing terminology, sometimes we will refer to elements of
\Pi
as cluster variables.) We give
\Pi
the structure of a simplicial complex, called the
cluster complex
, by declaring the maximal faces to be the clusters
\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\}
.

The set
\Pi
can be identified with the following set of pairs of integers:

\displaystyle\Pi=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\{(s,i)\,|\,0\leq s\leq r_{i}\},

(2.1)

where
r_{i}
,
i\in I
are some positive integers. The
initial cluster
is
\{(0,i)\,|\,i\in I\}
. We let
\Pi^{+}\subset\Pi
denote the subset of non-initial cluster variables, i.e., those
\gamma=(t,j)
with
t\neq 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
31
]
, the set
\Pi
is identified with the set of weights
\{c^{s}\omega_{i}\,|\,0\leq s\leq r_{i}\}
. We have chosen to index using the pairs of integers
(s,i)
instead. The choice
c
of a Coxeter element in
[
31
]
corresponds to our choice of an orientation of
D
in determining the exchange matrix
B
.

Remark 2.2
.

Starting from the initial cluster
\{x_{(0,i)}\,|\,i\in I\}
, the cluster
\{x_{(1,i)}\,|\,i\in I\}
is obtained by mutating each vertex of
I
once, always mutating at sources. This process is repeated to obtain all the cluster variables. In particular, the cluster variable
x_{(t,j)}
is obtained by mutation from
x_{(t-1,j)}
; see Proposition
2.5
for the exchange relation. We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for an explanation of this
Auslander–Reiten walk
, the relation to quiver representations, and many examples.

2.4.

There is an involution
{}^{*}\colon I\to I
sending
i
to
i^{*}
induced by the longest element of the Weyl group of the root system of
D
. This involution is the identity in all types except for
A_{n}
,
D_{2n+1}
,
E_{6}
, and in these types
{}^{*}\colon I\to I
is the non-trivial automorphism of
D
(as a graph). We shall use the notation
(-1,i):=(r_{i^{*}},i^{*})
; see
[
31
, Proposition 1.3]
.

2.5.
Each Dynkin diagram
D
has a dual denoted
D^{\vee}
defined by requiring that the Cartan matrix of
D^{\vee}
be transpose to that of
D
. Note that
D
and
D^{\vee}
have the same underlying tree. If
B
is an exchange matrix of type
D
, then we let
B^{\vee}
be the exchange matrix of type
D^{\vee}
associated to the same orientation of the underlying tree of
D
and
D^{\vee}
.
For dual exchange matrices
B
and
B^{\vee}
, the cluster variables
\Pi(B)
and
\Pi\big(B^{\vee}\big)
are naturally in bijection and under this bijection the cluster complexes are isomorphic.

2.6.
For
\gamma,\omega\in\Pi
, we let
(\omega||\gamma)
denote the
compatibility degree
, defined for example in
[
31
, Proposition 5.1]
. In
[
31
]
, the dependence of the compatibility degree on the choice of
c
(equivalent to our choice of
B
) is made explicit, but we have suppressed this dependence in our notation. By
[
31
, Section 5]
, the compatibility degrees for different choices of
B
are equivalent under an appropriate renaming of
\Pi
. Examples of the compatibility degree are given in Section 3.2.

We have
(\omega||\gamma)=0
if and only if
(\gamma||\omega)=0
and in this case we say that
\omega
and
\gamma
are compatible. Otherwise, we call
\omega
and
\gamma
incompatible. If
(\omega||\gamma)=(\gamma||\omega)=1
, we say that
\omega
and
\gamma
are
exchangeable
. The faces of the cluster complex consist of sets of cluster variables that are pairwise compatible.

2.7.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{{\operatorname{prin}}}={\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{prin}}\big)
denote the cluster algebra with principal coefficients
[
19
]
. Thus
{\tilde{B}}^{{\operatorname{prin}}}
is a
2n\times n
matrix whose top half is equal to
B
and bottom half is equal to the identity matrix. In this case, the initial mutable variables are denoted
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
and the principal frozen variables are denoted
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}
. We have a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-grading on
the principal coefficient cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{prin}}
given by

\displaystyle\deg(x_{i})=e_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\deg(y_{i})=-Be_{i}.

(2.2)

Each mutable cluster variable is homogeneous with respect to this grading, and we define the
{\mathbf{g}}
-vector
by
{\mathbf{g}}_{\gamma}:=\deg(x_{\gamma})
for
\gamma\in\Pi
.

2.8.

For
\gamma\in\Pi^{+}
, define the
F
-polynomial
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
by setting the initial cluster variables to 1 in the Laurent expansion of the cluster variable
x^{\operatorname{prin}}_{\gamma}
in the cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{prin}}
with principal coefficients:

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}):=x^{\operatorname{prin}}_{\gamma}(x_{i}=1,y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}).

By convention, we have
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})=1
if
\gamma
is initial. Computations of
{\mathbf{g}}
-vectors and
F
-polynomials are given in Examples
6.7
and
6.8
. Further examples can be found in
[
8
,
19
]
.

2.9.

The
cluster fan

{\mathcal{N}}(B)
is the collection of cones spanned by
\{{\mathbf{g}}_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{g}}_{\gamma_{s}}\}
as
\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}\}
varies over collections of cluster variables that belong to the same cluster, called
compatible
cluster variables. Recall that a cone
C
is called
simplicial
if
\dim(C)
is equal to the number of extremal rays of
C
, and a fan is called simplicial if all its cones are. A fan
{\mathcal{N}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called
smooth
if it is simplicial and for each maximal cone
C\in{\mathcal{N}}
the primitive integer vectors
{\mathbf{v}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{v}}_{n}
spanning
C
form an integral basis for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
12
,
17
,
21
]
)
.

The collection of cones
{\mathcal{N}}(B)
is a smooth, complete polyhedral fan.

A
generalized associahedron of type
B
is any polytope whose normal fan is equal to
{\mathcal{N}}(B)
.

4
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
as a quotient of a cluster variety

In this section we show that
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
can be obtained as a quotient of an open subspace
{\mathring{X}}
of the cluster variety
{\mathbf{X}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
by the action of the cluster automorphism torus
T
considered in
[
23
]
. An important role is played by principal and universal coefficients, where
{\tilde{B}}={\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{prin}}
or
{\tilde{B}}={\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{univ}}
. In particular, the defining relations of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
are obtained from the primitive exchange relations of
{\mathbf{X}}\big({\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big)
.

4.1.

Let
{\tilde{B}}
be a full rank extended exchange matrix. Let
T=T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
be the cluster automorphism group
[
23
]
of
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
: this is the group of algebra automorphisms
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)\to{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
such that for each (mutable or frozen) cluster variable
x
, we have
\phi(x)=\zeta(x)x
for
\zeta(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
. Thus,
T
acts on any cluster torus of the cluster variety
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
by scaling the coordinates. By
[
23
, Proposition 5.1]
, we have

\displaystyle T=\operatorname{Hom}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}\big).

(4.1)

Since
{\tilde{B}}
has full rank, the group
T
is a (possibly disconnected) abelian algebraic group of dimension
m
. The character group of
T
is the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. By definition, the torus
T
acts on each cluster variable by a character, and we denote the weight of the cluster variable
x\in{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
by
\operatorname{wt}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\tilde{B}}
have full rank. Then
{\tilde{B}}
has really full rank if and only if
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
has no torsion, or equivalently, the group
T
is connected, and thus a torus of dimension
m
.

Proof.

The rows of
{\tilde{B}}
span
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
if and only if
{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}={\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
if and only if
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
has no torsion.
∎

Let
X=X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
be the cluster variety, which is a smooth affine algebraic variety. Let
{\mathring{X}}\subset X
be the locus where all mutable cluster variables are non-vanishing. In terms of rings, we have

\displaystyle{\mathring{X}}:=\operatorname{Spec}\big({\mathcal{A}}[1/x\,|\,x\text{ is a mutable cluster variable}]\big).

Thus
{\mathring{X}}
is a smooth affine subvariety of the initial (or any) cluster subtorus of
X
, and it follows immediately from the definitions that the action of
T
preserves
{\mathring{X}}
, and furthermore the action of
T
is free on
{\mathring{X}}
. The geometric invariant theory quotient

\displaystyle{\mathring{X}}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}T:=\operatorname{Spec}\big({\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}\big]^{T}\big)

is again a smooth affine algebraic variety, and furthermore, there is a bijection between closed points of
{\mathring{X}}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}T
and
T
-orbits on
{\mathring{X}}
. We thus simply denote
{\mathring{X}}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}T
by
{\mathring{X}}/T
. Explicitly, the ring
{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}\big]^{T}
consists of all weight zero Laurent polynomials in cluster variables.

4.2.

Let
\tau\colon\Pi\to\Pi
be the bijection defined by
\tau(t,j)=(t-1,j)
for
0\leq t\leq r_{j}
. The primitive exchange relations are of the form

\displaystyle x_{\tau\gamma}x_{\gamma}=M+M^{\prime},

where

only involves frozen variables. For each primitive exchange relation, we define the rational function

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}:=\frac{M}{x_{\tau\gamma}x_{\gamma}}.

By definition,

, and it is easy to see that

is

-invariant. Thus

.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
{\tilde{B}}
is a full rank extended exchange matrix, acyclic and of finite type. Let

. Then the quotient

is a smooth affine variety isomorphic to

, and the isomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]\cong{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}\big]^{T}
is given by

.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that
{\tilde{B}}
is a full rank extended exchange matrix, acyclic and of finite type. Let
X=X^{\operatorname{prin}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
have principal coefficients. Then
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{prin}}/T^{\operatorname{prin}}
is isomorphic to the locus

, where all initial mutable cluster variables have been set to
1
. The coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]
is isomorphic to the subring of
{\mathbb{C}}(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
generated by
F_{\gamma}^{\pm 1}({\mathbf{y}})
and
y_{i}^{\pm 1}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
{\tilde{B}}
is a full rank extended exchange matrix, acyclic and of finite type. Let

have universal coefficients. Then
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}/T^{\operatorname{univ}}
is isomorphic to the locus

. The isomorphism

The relations in the following corollary will be discussed in further detail in Section 10.1.

Corollary 4.5

The ideal

has a natural set of generators of the form

, given by the images of all exchange relations of

The ideal

also contains the

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.
Recall that the mutable cluster variables of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{univ}}
are denoted

and the frozen variables are denoted

, where
\gamma\in\Pi
. Let

be the locus

, where all mutable cluster variables have been set to 1.

By Proposition

, the primitive exchange relations are of the form

\displaystyle x_{\tau\gamma}x_{\gamma}=z_{\gamma}S+\prod_{\omega\in\Pi}z_{\omega}^{(\omega||\gamma)},

where

is a monomial in the mutable cluster variables. So,

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}=\frac{z_{\gamma}S}{x_{\tau\gamma}x_{\gamma}}

and thus on

we have

and the relation

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}+\prod_{\omega\in\Pi}f_{\omega}^{(\omega||\gamma)}=1.

(4.2)

We will now show that the multiplication map gives an isomorphism

\displaystyle T^{\operatorname{univ}}\times X^{\operatorname{univ}}(1)\cong{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}},

or equivalently, every

-orbit on
{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}
intersects

in exactly one point. The character group of

is naturally isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+r}/{\tilde{B}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, which is a free abelian group of rank

. Thus each cluster variable

has a weight (or degree)

(see (
6.2
) for the weight of initial and frozen variables). By Proposition

. Thus we have a projection
{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\mapsto T^{\operatorname{univ}}
given by sending

to the coordinates

, and the fiber of this projection is

. This is an inverse to the multiplication map

T^{\operatorname{univ}}\times X^{\operatorname{univ}}(1)\cong{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}
.

We conclude that
{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}}\!\!\cong{\mathbb{C}}[X^{\operatorname{univ}}(1)]
. Now, any
T^{\operatorname{univ}}
-invariant function in

is a linear combination of
T^{\operatorname{univ}}
-invariant Laurent monomials in mutable and frozen variables. Each such Laurent monomial restricts to a Laurent monomial in the

-s on
{\mathbb{C}}[X^{\operatorname{univ}}(1)]

f_{\gamma}
and their inverses generate

4.2

satisfy. Finally, we check that the generators
f_{\gamma}
do not satisfy any further relations. Suppose we have a polynomial identity
p(f_{\gamma})=0
inside
{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}}

is equivalent to an equality

inside

is a Laurent polynomial. We claim that the primitive exchange relations allow us to eliminate all the non-initial cluster variables, i.e.,

\displaystyle q(x_{\gamma},z_{\gamma})=r(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},z_{\gamma})\mod\mbox{ ideal generated by primitive exchange relations},

where

{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}}

\deg(x_{1}),\dots,\deg(x_{n})
and
\deg(z_{\gamma})
,
\gamma\in\Pi
together span
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+r}
, and thus we can always multiply

by a

-invariant monomial so that the denominator involves only initial

and the

\displaystyle x_{\gamma}=\frac{M+M^{\prime}}{x_{\tau\gamma}}-x_{\gamma}R,

where

by an expression involving

and

will not appear again when this process is repeated. This proves our claim.

But

as an element of
{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]\subset{\mathbb{C}}(X^{\operatorname{univ}})

is 0, since

are algebraically independent. We conclude that

lies in the ideal generated by primitive exchange relations. Thus the ideal of relations satisfied by the

is generated by (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}},\qquad u_{\gamma}\longmapsto f_{\gamma}

and an isomorphism of varieties

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

By the defining property of universal coefficients, we have a homomorphism of rings
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\to{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)

\phi(x_{\gamma}^{\operatorname{univ}})=x_{\gamma}

{\mathcal{A}}

has rows equal to 0, or rows that are repeated.

and the monomials
\phi(z_{\gamma})

such that

is a Laurent monomial in

. Thus, we have
{\mathcal{A}}^{T}={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime T^{\prime}}

{\mathcal{A}}
has full rank, the quotient

has dimension

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}

Replacing
{\mathcal{A}}
by
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}

\psi\colon{\mathring{X}}\to{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}

C^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}

is surjective, the induced map

is injective and thus the inclusion of a subgroup.

We need to show that
\phi\colon{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{univ}}\big]^{T^{\operatorname{univ}}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\mathring{X}}\big]^{T}
is an isomorphism. For surjectivity, suppose that

is a Laurent monomial in mutable and frozen variables. Let

in

, where

are the last

The isomorphism

5.2.

We give another description of
R_{B}\subset{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{y}})
. Let
R({\mathbf{y}})=P({\mathbf{y}})/Q({\mathbf{y}})\in{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{y}})
be a rational function such that
P({\mathbf{y}}),Q({\mathbf{y}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbf{y}}]
have positive integer coefficients. Then
\operatorname{Trop}(R({\mathbf{y}}))
is the piecewise-linear function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
given by the formal substitution

\displaystyle y_{i}\mapsto Y_{i},\qquad(+,\times,\div)\mapsto(\min,+,-).

For example,
\operatorname{Trop}\big(\big(3y_{1}^{2}y_{2}+y_{2}^{2}\big)/(y_{2}+6y_{3})\big)=\min(2Y_{1}+Y_{2},2Y_{2})-\min(Y_{2},Y_{3})
. Note that the coefficients are unimportant since, for example,
\operatorname{Trop}(2y)=\operatorname{Trop}(y+y)=\min(Y,Y)=Y
.

The domains of linearity of the piecewise-linear function

define the structure of a complete fan on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A piecewise-linear function
L({\mathbf{Y}})\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called
nonnegative
, denoted
L({\mathbf{Y}})\geq 0
, if it takes nonnegative values on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The ring
R_{B}
is equal to the subring of
{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbf{y}})
generated by rational functions
R({\mathbf{y}})
satisfying

(1)

R({\mathbf{y}})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{a_{i}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})^{a_{\gamma}}
is a Laurent monomial in
y_{i}
and
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
,

(2)

\operatorname{Trop}(R({\mathbf{y}}))
is nonnegative.

Proof.

Let
R({\mathbf{y}})
be a Laurent monomial in
\{y_{i},F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})\}
. By Theorem
2.4
, the domains of linearity of the function
L({\mathbf{Y}})=\operatorname{Trop}(R({\mathbf{y}}))
is a coarsening of the negative of the cluster fan
-{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee})
. Thus
L({\mathbf{Y}})
is uniquely determined by
b_{\gamma}=L(-{\mathbf{g}}_{\gamma})
as
\gamma
varies over
\Pi
, and
{\mathbf{g}}_{\gamma}
denotes a
{\mathbf{g}}
-vector. As in the proof of Proposition
4.6
, we have
R({\mathbf{y}})=\prod_{\gamma}f_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})^{-b_{\tau\gamma}}
. The condition
L({\mathbf{Y}})\geq 0
is equivalent to
b_{\gamma}\geq 0
for all
\gamma\in\Pi
. Thus the subring of rational functions

satisfying (1) and (2) is exactly the subring
R_{D}
.
∎

5.3.

The Laurent polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}\big[y_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,y_{n}^{\pm 1}\big]
is the coordinate ring of an
n
-dimensional torus

. Recall that

Theorem 5.3
.

The affine scheme

is isomorphic to the affine open

in the projective toric variety

associated to the complete fan

. The subvariety

is identified with the intersection of

in

Proof.

For any
{\mathbf{g}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the quantity
\operatorname{Trop}(F({\mathbf{y}}))({\mathbf{g}})
is equal to the minimum value that the linear function
{\mathbf{Y}}\mapsto{\mathbf{Y}}\cdot{\mathbf{g}}
takes on the Newton polytope
P
of

. Thus by Theorem
2.4
, the outer normal fan of

is equal to
{\mathcal{N}}\big(B^{\vee}\big)
. Recall that a lattice polytope

is called

if for sufficiently large integers

is a sum
of
r
(not necessarily distinct) lattice points in

is very ample. So let

be such that

is very ample and let

be the set of all lattice points in

. For

, let

be the monomial with exponent vector

. Then

can be explicitly realized as the closure of the set of points

\displaystyle\Big\{\big[{\mathbf{y}}^{{\mathbf{v}}_{1}}:\cdots:{\mathbf{y}}^{{\mathbf{v}}_{k}}\big]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}\,|\,{\mathbf{y}}\in T_{\mathbf{y}}\Big\}

inside the projective space

. The polynomial

can be identified with a hyperplane section of

in this projective embedding, and the affine open

is generated by the functions

∎

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trop}(F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}))(-{\mathbf{g}}_{\omega})=-\dim\operatorname{Hom}(W_{\omega},W_{\tau\gamma}).

Suppose
\tau\gamma=(t,i)\in\Pi^{+}
. Then we have an Auslander–Reiten triangle in
D^{b}\big({\rm rep}\,Q^{\rm op}\big)

\displaystyle W_{(t-1,i)}\to E\to W_{(t,i)}\to W_{(t-1,i)}[1],\qquad\text{where}\quad E=\bigoplus_{i\to j}W_{(t,j)}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\to i}W_{(t-1,j)}.

We have an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{Hom}\big(W_{\omega},W_{(t-1,i)}\big)\to\operatorname{Hom}(W_{\omega},E)\to\operatorname{Hom}\big(W_{\omega},W_{(t,i)}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{0}{}\to\operatorname{Hom}\big(W_{\omega},W_{(t-1,i)}[1]\big)\to\cdots.

By the definition of Auslander–Reiten triangle, any map from
W_{\omega}
to
W_{(t,i)}
which is not an isomorphism factors through
E
. Thus

\displaystyle\big(\dim\operatorname{Hom}\big(W_{\omega},W_{(t-1,i)}\big)+\dim\operatorname{Hom}\big(W_{\omega},W_{(t,i)}\big)\big)-\dim\operatorname{Hom}(W_{\omega},E)=\delta_{\omega,(t,i)}

and this is exactly
A_{\gamma}(-{\mathbf{g}}_{\omega})
. Now if
\gamma\in\Pi^{+}
but
\tau\gamma
is initial, then we have an Auslander–Reiten triangle of the form

\displaystyle W_{(r_{i^{*}}-1,i^{*})}[-1]\to E\to W_{(0,i)}\to W_{(r_{i^{*}}-1,i^{*})}

and

\omega\in\Pi

, so again we have

. Finally, suppose that
\gamma=(0,i)

\displaystyle W_{(r_{i^{*}}-1,i^{*})}\to E\to W_{(0,i)}[1]\to W_{(r_{i^{*}}-1,i^{*})}[1].

In this case, our formula for

includes a factor of

{\mathbf{g}}

\big\{W_{(0,1)},\dots,W_{(0,n)}\big\}

[

).

Now, suppose that
D
is multiply-laced and let
{\tilde{D}}
be the simply-laced diagram that folds to
D
. Note that for
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|{\tilde{I}}|}
and
{\mathbf{v}}\in\big({\mathbb{R}}^{|{\tilde{I}}|}\big)^{\Gamma}
, we have
{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}=\nu({\mathbf{u}})\cdot\nu^{\vee}({\mathbf{v}})
.
By Proposition
6.3
,
\operatorname{Trop}(F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}))(-{\mathbf{g}}^{\vee}_{\omega})
is equal to the minimum value that the linear function
{\mathbf{Y}}\mapsto{\mathbf{Y}}\cdot\big({-}\sum_{{\tilde{\omega}}\in\nu^{-1}(\omega)}{\mathbf{g}}_{\tilde{\omega}}\big)

∎

Proof of Proposition

.

7
Examples of
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
as a configuration space

The space
{\mathcal{M}}_{A_{n-3}}
can be identified with the configuration space of
n
distinct points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. In this section, we investigate similar descriptions of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
in the cases
D=B_{n}
and
D=C_{n}
. We also consider the question of whether
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is a hyperplane arrangement complement.

So far we have considered
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
as a complex algebraic variety. However, the equations (
3.1
) make sense over the integers, and we may also consider
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
as a scheme over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. In particular, in this section we will also consider
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, the set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-points of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a finite field.

Throughout this section, we use the description of
\Pi
from Section 3.2.

7.1.
Hyperplane arrangements.

Let
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r}
be (affine) hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, with the assumption that the hyperplanes are defined over the integers. Let
Z({\mathbb{C}}):={\mathbb{C}}^{n}-(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup\cdots\cup H_{r})
. By our assumption
Z({\mathbb{R}})
and
Z({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
are also well-defined. The following result is well-known
[
25
,
30
]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

1.

There exists a polynomial
\chi(t)
so that
\chi(q)=\#Z({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where
q=p^{m}
is a prime power with sufficiently large
p
.

2.

The number of connected components
|\pi_{0}(Z({\mathbb{R}}))|
of the real hyperplane arrangement complement
Z({\mathbb{R}})
is given by
(-1)^{n}\chi(-1)
.

3.

The cohomology ring
H^{*}(Z({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{C}})
is generated by the classes of
\operatorname{dlog}f_{i}
, where
H_{i}=

\{f_{i}=0\}
, and we have
\sum_{i}\dim(H^{i}(Z({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{C}}))\;t^{i}=(-t)^{n}\chi(-1/t)
. Thus the Euler characteristic of
Z({\mathbb{C}})
is equal to
\chi(1)
.

Type
\boldsymbol{A_{n}}
.

. Let

.

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1&\cdots&1&0\\

where

, and two such matrices with parameters

(7.2)

We recognize the hyperplanes (

.

{\mathcal{M}}_{B_{n}}
is isomorphic to the complement in

Among many well-known properties, we obtain the following as immediate consequences using Theorem

Type

. Recall that

labeled by

(when frozen variables are inverted). Here,

.

Proposition 7.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\#{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=(q-n-1)(q-3)(q-5)\cdots(q-2n+1)

for
{\rm char}(q)>2
and

\displaystyle|\pi_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}))|=\frac{1}{2}\big(2^{n}(n+1)!+2^{n}n!\big)=2^{n-1}(n+2)n!.

Note that
|\pi_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}))|=|(\#{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))|_{q=-1}|
, agreeing with Theorem
7.1
, even though we do not know whether
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
is isomorphic to a hyperplane arrangement complement.

Proof.

For a field
k
, the points of
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}(k)
in general come from
{\mathring{Z}}_{n}(\bar{k})
, where
\bar{k}
denotes the algebraic closure of
k
.

First, suppose that
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
{\rm char}(q)>2
. Let
{\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
denote the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then the Galois group is topologically generated by one generator
\sigma
, called the
Frobenius automorphism
. It acts as the field automorphism
\sigma(x)=x^{q}
, and furthermore, we have
\sigma(x)=x
if and only if
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The map
\pi\colon{\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{q})
commutes with the action of
\sigma
. Thus if
\pi(z)=u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, we have

\displaystyle u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\Leftrightarrow\sigma(u)=u\Leftrightarrow\sigma(\pi(z))=\pi(z)\Leftrightarrow\pi(\sigma(z))=\pi(z),

and we have two possibilities: (1)
\sigma(z)=z
, or (2)
\sigma(z)=1/z
. For case (1), we are just counting
\#{\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Imposing the conditions (
7.5
), we get

\displaystyle\#{\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=(q-3)(q-5)\cdots(q-2n-1).

For case (2), the equation
\sigma(z)=1/z
is equivalent to
z_{i}^{q+1}=1
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
. There are
q+1
solutions to the polynomial equation
x^{q+1}-1
in
{\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. Two of the solutions are
x=\pm 1
. The other
q-1
solutions lie in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, since
x^{q+1}=1\implies x^{q^{2}-1}=1\implies x^{q^{2}}=x
. Thus in case (2), we are counting
n
-tuples
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
satisfying the condition
x^{q+1}=1
and the conditions imposed by (
7.5
). The conditions
z_{i}\neq\pm 1
are automatically satisfied, so we get

\displaystyle(q-1)(q-3)\cdots(q-2n+1).

In sum, taking into account the two-to-one covering
{\mathring{Z}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\#{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((2q-2n-2)(q-3)(q-5)\cdots(q-2n+1)\right)

\displaystyle=(q-n-1)(q-3)(q-5)\cdots(q-2n+1).

(Note that this point count is also the same as the hyperplane arrangement complement with hyperplanes
z_{i}+z_{j}
,
z_{i}-z_{j}
,
1+z_{i}
,
1-z_{i}
, though we do not know an explanation for this.)

Next let us consider
k={\mathbb{R}}
, so
\bar{k}={\mathbb{C}}
. In this case,
\sigma
is replaced by complex conjugation. So the same argument says that we should consider the two cases (1)
\bar{z}=z
, or (2)
\bar{z}=1/z
. For case (1), we are looking at
{\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
and by Theorem
7.1
we get

\displaystyle|\pi_{0}({\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}))|=2^{n}(n+1)!

since
\#{\mathring{Z}}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=(q-3)(q-5)\cdots(q-2n-1)
.

For case (2), we must have
z_{i}=\exp(i\theta_{i})
on the unit circle, where
\theta_{i}\in(\pi,\pi]
. The conditions (
7.5
) give

\displaystyle\theta_{i}\neq\theta_{j},\qquad\theta_{i}\neq 0,\qquad\theta_{i}\neq\pi,\qquad\theta_{i}+\theta_{j}\neq 0.

Thus the quantities
|\theta_{1}|,\dots,|\theta_{n}|
lie in
(0,\pi)
and satisfy
|\theta_{i}|\neq|\theta_{j}|
. There are
n!
regions in
|\theta|

2^{n}
sign choices going from
|\theta_{i}|
to
\theta_{i}
, giving

\displaystyle\big|\pi_{0}\big({\mathring{Z}}_{n}(S^{1})\big)\big|=2^{n}n!.

In sum, taking into account the two-to-one covering
{\mathring{Z}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle|\pi_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}))|=\frac{1}{2}\left(2^{n}(n+1)!+2^{n}n!\right)=2^{n-1}(n+2)n!,

as claimed.
∎

The variety
{\mathcal{M}}_{C_{n}}
can be identified with a subvariety of
{\mathcal{M}}_{A_{2n-1}}
. Sending

to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1&1&1&\cdots&1&1\\
z_{1}&z_{2}&\cdots&z_{n}&1&-z_{1}&\cdots&-z_{n}&1\end{bmatrix}

(7.6)

maps

. Note that the non-vanishing of (

and

8
Positive part

In this section, we define the nonnegative subspace
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
and show that it is diffeomorphic to the generalized associahedron of
D^{\vee}
.

8.1.
We define the positive part
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
as the subspace

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}:=\big\{x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})\,|\,u_{\gamma}(x)>0\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Pi\big\}

and the nonnegative part
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}\subset{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}:=\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}}\subset{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}}).

Intersecting with the stratification (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}=\bigsqcup_{F}{\mathcal{M}}_{F,>0},\qquad\text{where}\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{F,>0}={\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{F}({\mathbb{R}}).

Let
P
be an integer polytope and
{\mathcal{N}}(P)
denote its normal fan. It is well-known
[
20
, Chapter 4]
that the nonnegative part
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(P),\geq 0}
of the projective toric variety
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(P)}
is diffeomorphic to the polytope
P
. The following result thus follows from Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem 8.1
.

There is a face-preserving diffeomorphism between
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}
and the generalized associahedron of
D^{\vee}
.

The positive part
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}
is identical to the positive part
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee}),>0}
of the ambient projective toric variety, and
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}
is equal to one of the connected components of the smooth manifold
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
.

8.2.

The space
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
has a distinguished rational top-form
\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
, called the
canonical form
, that can be described in a number of ways. Suppose
{\tilde{B}}
is a full rank extended exchange matrix of type
D
. The cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
has a natural top-form
\Omega
which in any cluster
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n+m})
can be written (up to sign):

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{{\rm d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{{\rm d}x_{n+m}}{x_{n+m}},

which is the natural top-form on the corresponding cluster torus
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n+m}
. The cluster automorphism group
T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
can be identified with a subgroup of
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n+m}
, and the quotient group
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n+m}/T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
is again an algebraic torus
S=({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
. The inclusion
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n+m}\subset X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
identifies
S
birationally with
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
(note that neither
S
nor
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
contains the other, but the two share a common dense open subset). The torus
S
has a natural top-form, and the canonical form
\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
is the image of this form under the birational isomorphism between
S
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
.

Another way to obtain the canonical form of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is via Theorem
5.3
: any toric variety
X_{P}
has a canonical rational top-form
\Omega(X_{P})
, which is simply the (extension of the) natural top-form of the dense algebraic torus in
X_{P}
. Restricting
\Omega\big(X_{{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee})}\big)
to
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
gives a top-form on
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
which equals
\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
.

The pair
\big({\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D},{\mathcal{M}}_{D,\geq 0}\big)
is nearly a
positive geometry
in the sense of
[
2
]
, with
\Omega\big({\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}\big)=\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
as canonical form: the residue of
\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
along a divisor
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}(F)
is equal to the canonical form
\Omega\big({\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}(F)\big)
, which is simply the product of canonical forms corresponding to the factorization of Proposition
3.6
. This follows from the similar statement concerning
X_{{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee})}
, proven in
[
2
, Appendix G]
. However,
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{D}
is an affine variety rather than a projective variety, so it is not a positive geometry in the strict sense.

In the case
D=A_{n-3}
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}={\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, and the canonical form can be written as

\displaystyle\Omega\big(({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}\big)=\frac{{\rm d}z_{2}\cdots{\rm d}z_{n-2}}{(z_{2}-z_{1})(z_{3}-z_{2})\cdots(z_{1}-z_{n})},

where
(z_{1},z_{n-1},z_{n})=(0,1,\infty)
as in Section 7.2, and denominator factors equal to
\infty
are understood to be omitted.
This form is also called a cell-form in
[
9
]
and the condition that
\Omega(({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0})
only has poles along the boundary divisors of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}

(
and not elsewhere in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n}
)
is
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
. Combining with the above discussion, we have

Proposition 8.2
.

The pair
\big({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{0,n},({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{\geq 0}\big)
is a positive geometry with canonical form
\Omega\big(({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}\big)
.

9
Positive tropicalization

In this section, we consider the positive tropicalization of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
. We use our results to resolve a conjecture of Speyer and Williams
[
29
]
on positive tropicalizations of cluster algebras of finite type. We refer the reader to
[
29
]
for background on positive tropicalizations.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{R}}\big(\big(t^{1/n}\big)\big)
denote the field of Puiseux series over
{\mathbb{R}}
. We define
{\rm val}\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
by
{\rm val}(0)=\infty
and
{\rm val}(x(t))=r
if the lowest term of
x(t)
is equal to
\alpha t^{r}
, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
. We define
{\mathcal{R}}_{>0}\subset{\mathcal{R}}
to be the semifield consisting of Puiseux series
x(t)
that are non-zero and such that coefficient of the lowest term is a positive real number.

A point
u(t)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathcal{R}}_{>0})
is a collection
u(t)=\{u_{\gamma}(t)\in{\mathcal{R}}_{>0},\gamma\in\Pi\}
of (positive) Puiseux series satisfying the relations from Definition
3.1
. We define the
positive tropicalization

\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
as the closure of valuations

\displaystyle\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}:=\overline{\big\{{\rm val}(u_{\gamma}(t))_{\gamma\in\Pi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Pi}\,|\,u(t)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathcal{R}}_{>0})\big\}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\Pi}.

Lemma 9.1
.

The subspace
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is a (not complete) polyhedral fan inside the linear space
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Pi}
.

Proof.

We use the identification
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathring{X}}^{\operatorname{prin}}/T^{\operatorname{prin}}
from Theorem
4.3
. According to Proposition
4.6
, there is an invertible monomial transformation between the functions
u_{\gamma}=f_{\gamma}
and the set of functions
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}\cup\{F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})\,|\,\gamma\in\Pi^{+}\}
. Since each
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
is a positive Laurent polynomial in the
y_{i}
-s, it follows that each
u_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
is a subtraction-free rational function in the
y_{i}
-s. Thus the map
(u_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Pi}\mapsto(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
induces an isomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathcal{R}}_{>0})\cong{\mathcal{R}}_{>0}^{n}
. It follows that we have a homeomorphism
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The embedding of
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
in the (larger dimensional) linear space
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Pi}
endows it with the structure of a polyhedral fan.
∎

Theorem 9.2
.

The fan
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is isomorphic to the cluster fan
{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee})
.

Proof.

Under the isomorphism
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the fan structure of
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
gives a complete fan in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
whose maximal cones are the common domains of linearity of the piecewise linear functions
\operatorname{Trop}(u_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}))
. By Proposition
4.6
, we can equivalently take the common domains of linearity of the functions
\operatorname{Trop}(F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}))
,
\gamma\in\Pi^{+}
. It is well-known (see for example
[
6
, Section 11.1]
) that the resulting fan is the normal fan of the Newton polytope of the Laurent polynomial
\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi^{+}}F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
. By Theorem
2.4
, we deduce the isomorphism of fans
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\cong{\mathcal{N}}(B^{\vee})
.
∎

Now let
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
denote a cluster algebra of finite type, and
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
the corresponding cluster variety. Using the set of cluster variables
x_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in\Pi
and coefficient variables
x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+m}
, we have an embedding
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{|\Pi|+m}
. We define
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{|\Pi|+m}
as the closure of the image of
X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)({\mathcal{R}}_{>0})
under the map
{\rm val}\colon{\mathcal{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Note that the tropicalization
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
depends only on the cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
and not on the choice of initial cluster.

The projection map
(x_{\gamma},x_{i})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n+m})
from
{\mathbb{R}}^{|\Pi|+m}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
(onto the initial cluster variables) identifies
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
with a complete polyhedral fan in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Suppose that
{\tilde{B}}
is of full rank and has finite type
D
. Then
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
modulo its lineality space
L
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
.

Proof.

The translation action of the lineality space
L
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
is simply the tropicalization of the action of the automorphism torus
T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
(
4.1
). Thus
{\tilde{B}}
is of full rank if and only if
L
has dimension
m
if and only if
T\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
is of dimension
m
. In particular,
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)/L
is a polyhedral fan of dimension
n
.

Via the isomorphism of Theorem
4.2
, each
u_{\gamma}=f_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]
can be identified with a monomial in the cluster and coefficient variables
x_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in\Pi
and
x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+m}
. We thus have a linear projection map
p\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{|\Pi|+m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{|\Pi|}
(the tropicalization of the rational map sending

-s to

-s) mapping
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
surjectively to
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
. The fibers of

are exactly the orbits of the lineality space

acting on
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
. It follows that the fans
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)/L
and
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
are isomorphic.
∎

Noting that the fans
{\mathcal{N}}\big(B^{\vee}\big)
and
{\mathcal{N}}(B)
are combinatorially isomorphic, we deduce from Theorem

and Proposition
9.3
the following conjecture of Speyer and Williams
[

, Conjecture 8.1]
. The principal coefficient case was established in
[

]
and we thank Christian Stump for drawing our attention to this work.

Corollary 9.4
.

Suppose that
{\tilde{B}}
is of full rank and has finite type. Then
\operatorname{Trop}_{>0}X\big({\tilde{B}}\big)/L

11
Connected components and sign patterns

The permutation group
S_{n}
acts on the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
, permuting the
n
points and permuting the connected components. The presentation of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
using
u_{ij}
and the relations (
1.1
) depends on the choice of a dihedral ordering and the action of the symmetry group
S_{n}
is obscured. In this section, we use the extended
u
-equations to investigate the connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
, with the hope of uncovering an appropriate symmetry group for
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
in other types. While our results here are more speculative, we are able to construct new classes of
u
-equations.

A further motivation for studying connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
is the application to string amplitudes where it is important to consider canonical forms of different connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
; see Section 12.2 for a brief discussion.

We also define an analogue of an oriented matroid for
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
, called a “consistent sign pattern”, and it is conjectured that the number of consistent sign patterns is equal to the number of connected components.

11.1.
Consistent sign patterns.

A
consistent sign pattern
for type
D
is an element
(s_{\gamma})\in\{+,-\}^{\Pi}
such that for each extended
u
-equation (
10.1
)
\prod_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}^{\alpha_{\gamma}}+\prod_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}^{\beta_{\gamma}}=1
, we have that at least one of the signs
\prod_{\gamma}s_{\gamma}^{\alpha_{\gamma}}
and
\prod_{\gamma}s_{\gamma}^{\beta_{\gamma}}
is positive. In other words,
(s_{\gamma})
are possible signs for some solution
(u_{\gamma})
of extended
u
-equations. We could also call a consistent sign pattern a “uniform oriented matroid” for the
u
-variables.

In
[
4
]
, we made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 11.1
.

The number of connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{R}})
is given by the number of sign patterns of
u
-variables consistent with the extended
u
-equations for
D
.

11.2.
Type
\boldsymbol{A}
.
We consider
D=A_{n-3}
. The space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
has
(n{-}1)!/2
connected components, corresponding to the dihedral orderings of
n
points. In the positive connected component
({\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})_{>0}
, all the cross ratios
u_{ij}
are positive, indeed, we have
0<u_{ij}<1
. In other connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, some of the
u_{ij}
-s are negative. We find that the extended
u
-equations exclude those sign patterns for which both
[a,b|c,d]
and
[b,c|d,a]
are negative. Empirically, we find that precisely
(n{-}1)!/2
consistent sign patterns are allowed by the extended
u
-equations (
10.3
), and this count agrees with the number of connected components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Let us now consider the problem of finding new
u
-variables for other components of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}({\mathbb{R}})
, that is, we seek cross-ratios that are positive on that component. It suffices to consider the ordering that is obtained from the standard one by an adjacent transposition, e.g.,
(1^{\prime},2^{\prime},3^{\prime},\dots,n^{\prime})=(2,1,3,\dots,n)
. Using the following identities for the cross ratio
[a,b|c,d]
(see Section 10.1.1):

\displaystyle[a,b|d,c]=\frac{1}{[a,b|c,d]},\qquad[a,c|b,d]=-\frac{[a,b|c,d]}{[b,c|d,a]}\,

we find that the
u
-variables in this new ordering include

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{1,3}=[n^{\prime},1^{\prime}|2^{\prime},3^{\prime}]=[n,2|1,3]=\frac{-[n,1|2,3]}{1,2|3,n]}=\frac{-[n,1|2,3]}{\prod_{i=3}^{n{-}1}[1,2|i,i{+}1]}=\frac{-u_{1,3}}{u_{2,4}\cdots u_{2,n}},

From the analysis of Section 7.4, we know that

12
Outlook

We close with a few comments on open directions for future exploration.

12.1.
Understanding real components of cluster configuration space.

An open question immediately suggested by our investigations (see Section
11
) is understanding all the connected components of the real points of cluster configuration spaces. For
A_{n-3}
, there is a beautiful picture, where the complete space is tiled by “binary associahedra” corresponding to all
(n{-}1)!/2
orderings of
n
points on the projective line, and there is a similar complete picture of all the orderings for
C_{n}
by folding. These examples are especially easy to understand since we have a “linear model” for
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
in terms of a hyperplane arrangement. The connected components can also be easily understood in these examples, directly studying the space of solutions of the
u
-equations with different sign patterns. In general,
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
is not a hyperplane arrangement complement and it would thus be interesting to systematically study the question of connected components directly from the
u
-equations defining the space, as we have done in some examples in Section
11
.

It is natural to conjecture that some or all of the other real components of cluster configurations spaces (suitably compactified using the
u
-equations) are also positive geometries, and it would be interesting to determine their canonical forms.

In connection with determining canonical forms for general components, we state here without proof, a simple expression for the canonical forms of the positive component we have studied above, not in terms of cluster variables, but directly in terms of
u
-variables. Recall that the
u_{\gamma}
are in bijection with all the cluster variables. Consider any
acyclic
cluster
(x_{\gamma_{1}},\dots,x_{\gamma_{n}})
. Then, the canonical form is simply given by taking the wedge product

\displaystyle\Omega=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}u_{\gamma_{i}}}{u_{\gamma_{i}}(1-u_{\gamma_{i}})}.

(12.1)

As we noted in Section 10.2, acyclic seeds have the following feature: all the
u_{\gamma}
variables can be expressed rationally in terms of those in the initial seed. This idea can be extended to give canonical forms for other connected components of cluster configuration spaces. As a simple example, let us consider the description of the component discussed in Section
11
for the
G_{2}
case, where
b_{1}=-b^{\prime}_{1}<0
. We can readily check that all of the
(x,y)
variables can be rationally solved for in terms of either
(x_{2},x_{3})
or in terms of
(x_{3},x_{4})
. The canonical form is then given as

\displaystyle\Omega={\rm dlog}\frac{x_{2}}{1-x_{2}}{\rm dlog}\frac{x_{3}}{1-x_{3}}={\rm dlog}\frac{x_{3}}{1-x_{3}}{\rm dlog}\frac{x_{4}}{1-x_{4}}.

12.2.
Open and closed cluster string amplitudes.
The
stringy canonical forms
of
[
3
]
can be applied to the cluster configuration space
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
, and we obtain the
cluster string amplitude
.
For a cluster algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\tilde{B}}\big)
of full rank and of type
D
, the cluster string integral is defined to be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{D}(s):=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}}\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0})\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}x_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{\gamma}}\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+m}x_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{i}},

(12.2)

where
\{s_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Pi\}\cup\{s_{i}\,|\,i\in[n+1,n+m]\}

are parameters chosen so that the product
\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}x_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{\gamma}}

is
T
-invariant, and thus descends to a function on
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}
. Choosing
{\tilde{B}}={\tilde{B}}^{\operatorname{prin}}
, we may use Theorem
4.3
to rewrite (
12.2
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{D}({\mathbf{X}},\{c\})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}}\prod_{i}\frac{{\rm d}y_{i}}{y_{i}}y_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}X_{i}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi^{+}}F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})^{-\alpha^{\prime}c_{\gamma}},

(12.3)

where
({\mathbf{X}},\{c\})
are related linearly to
(s_{i},s_{\gamma})
. By
[
3
, Claim 2]
, (
12.3
) converges when the point
{\mathbf{X}}
belongs to the generalized associahedron
P(c)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi^{+}}c_{\gamma}P_{\gamma}
, where
P_{\gamma}
is the Newton polytope of
F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}})
. By
[
3
, Claim 2]
the leading order of
{\mathcal{I}}_{D}({\mathbf{X}},\{c\})
is the canonical function
\Omega(P(c))
of
P(c)
evaluated at
X
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}(\alpha^{\prime})^{n}{\mathcal{I}}_{D}({\mathbf{X}},\{c\})=\underline{\Omega}(P(c))({\mathbf{X}}).

(We refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
]
for background on canonical functions and canonical forms.)
In particular, the poles of
{\mathcal{I}}_{D}({\mathbf{X}},\{c\})
as
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
, all of which are simple, correspond bijectively to the facets of the generalized associahedron of
D^{\vee}
. By
[
3
, Section 9]
and Theorem
6.6
, we may also rewrite

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{D}({\bf U})=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}}\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0})\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}u_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}U_{\gamma}}

(12.4)

and the convergence condition is the very simple condition
U_{\gamma}>0
. By Proposition
4.6
, the
u_{\gamma}
and
\{y_{i},F_{\gamma}\}
are related by an invertible monomial transformation, and thus
\{U_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Pi\}
and
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}\cup\{c_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Pi^{+}\}
are related by an invertible linear transformation. (The matrix of this linear transformation has entries given by the integers
\operatorname{Trop}(F_{\gamma}({\mathbf{y}}))\big({-}{\mathbf{g}}^{\vee}_{\omega}\big)
that appeared in Section
6
.) We see from (
12.4
) that the
u
-variables
u_{\gamma}
are reverse-engineered from the cluster string integral: they are those monomials in cluster variables making the domain of convergence explicit.

As explained in
[
3
, Section 7]
, for generic exponents
X
, we expect that varying the cycle of integration (to something other than the cycle
{\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0}
) will span a space of integral functions of dimension equal to the absolute value of the Euler characteristic
|\chi({\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{C}}))|
. Indeed, it is especially natural to integrate over any of the other real connected components of the cluster configuration space, directly generalizing the basis of all (tree-level) open string amplitudes associated with type
A
.

We can also define the analog of “closed string” cluster amplitudes. The simplest object we can define (as in
[
3
]
) is the “mod square” of the open string integral

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{D}^{\rm{closed}}\big(\big\{U,\bar{U}\big\}\big)=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{C}})}\Omega({\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0})\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}u_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}U_{\gamma}}\wedge\bar{\Omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{D,>0})\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}\bar{u}_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}\bar{U}_{\gamma}},

where in order for the integrand to be single-valued, we must have that the exponents
\bar{U}_{\gamma}
differ from
U_{\gamma}
at most by integers.

As we have remarked, it is plausible that real components of cluster configuration space other than the region associated with
u_{\gamma}\geq 0
provide us with many different positive geometries
{\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}_{D}
, with associated canonical forms

\Omega\big({\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}_{D}\big)
. In this case we can extend the closed string integrals to be more generally labelled by pairs of these positive geometries,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}_{D},{\mathcal{M}}^{(j)}_{D}}^{\rm{closed}}\big(\big\{U,\bar{U}\big\}\big)=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}({\mathbb{C}})}\Omega\big({\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}_{D}\big)\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}u_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}U_{\gamma}}\wedge\bar{\Omega}\big({\mathcal{M}}^{(j)}_{D}\big)\prod_{\gamma\in\Pi}\bar{u}_{\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime}\bar{U}_{\gamma}}.

It is clear that a complete understanding of the space of open and closed string integrals will go hand-in-hand with a similarly complete understanding of the space of all connected real components of the cluster configuration space.

12.3.
Beyond finite type.

Finally, the most obvious open question is whether the notions of cluster configuration space presented in this paper can naturally be extended beyond finite-type cluster algebras. It is interesting to note that, as we have seen in (
12.4
) above, in the finite type case, the introduction of the
u
-variables is naturally reverse engineered, starting from the definition of the cluster string amplitude, see also
[
3
]
. This definition can be extended in various ways to define natural “compactifications” of the infinite-type configuration spaces, as recently been explored for the case of Grassmannian cluster algebras
[
5
]
. In these examples, the reverse-engineering of
u
-variables does not work as it does in finite type: amongst other things the polytope capturing the combinatorics of the boundary structure in these cases is typically not simple. But there may be other choices of stringy integral that are more natural from the perspective of finding good
u
-variables and “binary” realizations of general cluster configuration spaces.

Appendix A
A lemma in commutative algebra

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f\colon A\to B
be a surjective homomorphism of Noetherian commutative rings with identity. Let
S\subset A
be the multiplicative set generated by elements
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p}
such that

\displaystyle f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\dots,f(x_{p})\quad\text{are not zero-divisors in $B$}.

(A.1)

Suppose that

(1)

the localized homomorphism
S^{-1}f\colon S^{-1}A\to S^{-1}B
is an isomorphism, and

(2)

for each
i=1,2,\dots,p
the induced homomorphism
f_{i}\colon A/(x_{i})\to B/(f(x_{i}))
is an isomorphism.

Then
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
K
denote the kernel of
f
. Let
a\in K
be a nonzero element. Suppose that
x_{i}a\neq 0
in
A
for all
i
. Then the image of
a
in
S^{-1}A
is nonzero and it is in the kernel of
S^{-1}f\colon S^{-1}A\allowbreak\to S^{-1}B
. This contradicts (1). Thus
Ma=0
for some monomial
M
in the
x_{i}
-s. Replacing
a
by
M^{\prime}a
for some other monomial
M^{\prime}
, and using (
A.1
), we may assume that
a\in K
and
x_{i}a=0
for some
i=1,2,\dots,p
.

If
a\in(x_{i})
, then by (
A.1
), we have
a=x_{i}a_{1}
for a nonzero element
a_{1}\in K
. Repeating, we either find a nonzero element
a^{\prime}\in K
such that
a^{\prime}\notin(x_{i})
, or we have an ascending chain of ideals
(a)\subset(a_{1})\subset(a_{2})\subset\cdots
. In the former case, the image of
a^{\prime}
in
A/(x_{i})
is nonzero and in the kernel of
f_{i}
, contradicting (2). Thus we are in the latter case. Since
x_{i}
is not a unit and
A
is Noetherian, the chain of ideals stabilizes to a proper ideal
(a^{\prime})=I\subsetneq A
, and we thus have
(a^{\prime})=(a^{\prime\prime})
, where
a^{\prime\prime}=x_{i}a^{\prime}
and
x^{n}a^{\prime}=0
for some
n>0
. This is impossible: letting
m
be minimal such that
x_{i}^{m}a^{\prime}=0
we find that
x_{i}^{m-1}a^{\prime\prime}=0
which implies
x_{i}^{m-1}a^{\prime}=0
, a contradiction.
∎

Frobenius algebras associated with the α \alpha -induction for equivariantly braided tensor categories
Mizuki Oikawa

Abstract
Let G G be a group. We give a categorical definition of the G G -equivariant α \alpha -induction associated with a given G G -equivariant Frobenius algebra in a G G -braided multitensor category, which generalizes the α \alpha -induction for G G -twisted representations of conformal nets. For a given G G -equivariant Frobenius algebra in a spherical G G -braided fusion category, we construct a G G -equivariant Frobenius algebra, which we call a G G -equivariant α \alpha -induction Frobenius algebra, in a suitably defined category called neutral double. This construction generalizes Rehren’s construction of α \alpha -induction Q-systems. Finally, we define the notion of the G G -equivariant full center of a G G -equivariant Frobenius algebra in a spherical G G -braided fusion category and show that it indeed coincides with the corresponding G G -equivariant α \alpha -induction Frobenius algebra, which generalizes a theorem of Bischoff, Kawahigashi and Longo.

Introduction

In rational conformal field theory, we can construct two-dimensional conformal field theories from a chiral conformal field theory. Mathematically, it corresponds to finding nonnegative integral combinations of some functions on the complex upper half plane which is invariant under the action of the modular group
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
. Such combinations are called modular invariants. It is well-known that the modular invariants for the
SU(2)
Wess–Zumino–Witten models admit an A-D-E classification i.e. they correspond to the simply-laced Dynkin diagrams
[
CIZ87
]
[
Kat87
]

In the framework of algebraic quantum field theory, a chiral conformal field theory is modeled by a conformal net. When a conformal net

satisfies the property called

[
KLM01

[

It was shown by Böckenhauer, Evans and Kawahigashi

In this article, we generalize some of the classical results introduced above, which gives some evidence for this expectation. Preliminaries

2.1
Group actions on bicategories

In this subsection, we recall the notion of a group action on a monoidal category and that on a bicategory and give an appropriate notion of equivalence between bicategories with group actions, which turns out to be equivalent to the notion introduced in
[
BGM19
]
. Moreover, we prove a coherence theorem for
G
-actions on bicategories, see Theorem

[

An
action
of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal functor from the monoidal category
\underline{G}
of the elements of
G
with only identity morphisms to the monoidal category of monoidal endofunctors on
\mathcal{C}
.

We also have to consider group actions on bicategories in this article (see Subsections
3.1
and
5.2

[

, Section 1.5]
. Here we fix our notation: the composition of 1-cells of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}
or
\otimes
, for we regard bicategories as generalizations of monoidal categories, and often omitted. The associativity constraint is denoted by
a^{\mathcal{C}}
. The unit is denoted by
\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{C}}
, and the left and right unit constraints are denoted respectively by
l^{\mathcal{C}}
and
r^{\mathcal{C}}
. A pseudofunctor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
consists of a map
F
between 0-cells, functors
F_{A,B}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(A),F(B))
for
A,B\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
, natural isomorphisms
J^{F}_{A,B,C}:F_{B,C}(-)\otimes F_{A,B}(-)\cong F_{A,C}(-\otimes-)
for
A,B,C\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
\varphi^{F}_{A}:\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{D}}_{F(A)}\cong F_{A,A}(\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{C}}_{A})
for
A\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
. A pseudonatural transformation
\tau:F\to F^{\prime}
consists of 1-cells
\tau^{0}_{C}:F(C)\to F^{\prime}(C)
for
C\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and invertible 2-cells
\tau_{\lambda}:\tau^{0}_{C^{\prime}}F(\lambda)\cong F^{\prime}(\lambda)\tau^{0}_{C}
for 1-cells
\lambda:C\to C^{\prime}
. The vertical and horizontal compositions of pseudonatural transformations (see also
[
BGM19
, Section 1.1]
) are denoted respectively by
\circ
and
\ast
.

By stating the coherence theorem for pseudofunctors
[
Gur13
, Subsection 2.3.3]
in the form of “all diagrams commute”, we can see that the 2-cells obtained by vertically and horizontally composing components of
a^{\mathcal{C}}
,
l^{\mathcal{C}}
and
r^{\mathcal{C}}
of bicategories
\mathcal{C}
and
J^{F}
’s and
\varphi^{F}
’s of pseudofunctors
F
are indeed canonical, which allows us to suppress these 2-cells. In particular, we may suppress the constraints of the tricategory of bicategories. We can also introduce some graphical representations in the tricategory of bicategories, which are used only in this subsection. A pseudonatural transformation is represented as an arrow from top to bottom, and the vertical and horizontal compositions of pseudonatural transformations are represented respectively by the vertical and horizontal concatenation of arrows. Note that we do not have to be careful of relative vertical positions when taking horizontal concatenations since the comparison constraints between pseudonatural transformations (see
[
BGM19
, Section 1.1]
) are canonical by coherence.

An
action
of a group
G
on a bicategory

is a monoidal pseudofunctor (i.e. a trihomomorphism between monoidal bicategories, see
[

to

, where

denotes

denotes the monoidal bicategory of pseudofunctors from

to itself, see
[

, Sections 1.1 and 2]
. More explicitly, an action

consists of pseudofunctors (biequivalences)

from

to itself, pseudonatural equivalences

For a 1-cell

In this subsection, we recall the notions of a

Our terminologies on tensor category theory follow those in

Remark 2.9
.

Although tensor categories that arise in algebraic quantum field theory do not have zero objects as remarked in
[
Müg05
, Remark 2.11]
, because we are interested only in their rigid subcategories, which are semisimple, we regard such a subcategory as an abelian category by adding a zero object. Note that a linear functor on such a category is automatically exact.

When
\mathcal{C}
is moreover a multitensor category, an
action
of
G
is a monoidal functor from
\underline{G}
to the monoidal category of the tensor autoequivalences on
\mathcal{C}
. When
\mathcal{C}
is moreover pivotal (see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Definition 4.7.8]

pivotal action
of

is a monoidal functor from
\underline{G}
to the monoidal category of pivotal tensor autoequivalences on
\mathcal{C}
. Recall that a monoidal functor

is

\delta^{\mathcal{D}}F=F\delta^{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\delta^{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the pivotal structure of
\mathcal{C}
.

First, we recall the notion of a
grading
on a multitensor category. Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of additive categories. Its
direct sum

\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
is an additive category with a family of additive functors
\{I_{i}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}\}_{i\in I}
which has the universal property that for any additive category
\mathcal{D}
and a family of additive functors
\{F_{i}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{D}\}_{i\in I}
there exists an additive functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
with a family of natural isomorphisms
\sigma^{F}=\{\sigma^{F,i}:FI_{i}\cong F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that for any other such pair
(\tilde{F},\tilde{\sigma}^{F})
and a family of natural transformations
\{\tau^{i}:F_{i}\to\tilde{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
we have a unique natural transformation
\tau:F\to\tilde{F}
with
\tau^{i}\circ\sigma^{F,i}=\sigma^{\tilde{F},i}\circ\tau I_{i}
for any
i\in I
. Such a category
\mathcal{C}
always exists: indeed, we can explicitly give
\mathcal{C}
as the category of families of objects whose all but finite components are zero, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Section 1.3]
. The category
\mathcal{C}
is unique up to a unique equivalence by universality. We can indeed take
\sigma^{F,i}
’s to be identities, and in this case, we refer to the natural transformation
\tau
obtained by universality as the
extension
of the original family of natural transformations
\{\tau^{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

We can equivalently define the direct sum
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
to be an additive category which has
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s as its subcategories such that every object is a direct sum of objects of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{0\}
for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{i})
and
\mu\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{j})
with
i\neq j
. Indeed, the explicit construction above satisfies this property, and conversely by decomposing the objects of
\mathcal{C}
we obtain an additive equivalence from
\mathcal{C}
to
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

The objects in the set
\homog(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in I}\obj(\mathcal{C}_{i})
are called
homogeneous
. We write
\partial_{\mathcal{C}}\lambda=i
or simply
\partial\lambda=i
if
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{i})
. Since
\obj(\mathcal{C}_{i})\cap\obj(\mathcal{C}_{j})
consists of the zero objects of
\mathcal{C}
for

,
\partial
is single-valued on nonzero objects.

By definition, we can always decompose an object into a direct sum of homogeneous objects. The following lemma shows that such a decomposition, which we call a
homogeneous decomposition
, is essentially unique.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\lambda
be an object of an
I
-graded multitensor category and let
\lambda=\bigoplus_{i\in F}\lambda_{i}=\bigoplus_{j\in F^{\prime}}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
be decompositions in two ways with finite subsets
F
and
F^{\prime}
of
I
. Then, for every
i\in F\cap F^{\prime}
we have
\lambda_{i}\cong\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
as subobjects (i.e. there exists an isomorphism
f\in\operatorname{Hom}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}^{\prime})
such that
\iota_{i}=\iota^{\prime}_{i}\circ f
, where
\iota_{i}
and
\iota^{\prime}_{i}
denote the morphisms that embeds respectively
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
into
\lambda
) by a unique subobject isomorphism. Moreover,
\lambda_{i}\cong 0
for every
i\in F\setminus F^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\cong 0
for every
j\in F^{\prime}\setminus F
.

Proof.

Let
p_{i}
and
p_{i}^{\prime}
denote the projections of the decompositions
\lambda=\bigoplus_{i}\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda=\bigoplus_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
respectively, and let
\iota_{i}
and
\iota^{\prime}_{j}
denote the corresponding inclusions. Since
p_{i}\circ\iota_{j}^{\prime}=0
and
p_{j}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}=0
for
i\in F
and
j\in F^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}
, we have
(p_{i}\circ\iota_{i}^{\prime})\circ(p_{i}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i})=\sum_{j\in F^{\prime}}p_{i}\circ\iota_{j}^{\prime}\circ p_{j}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}=\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{i}}
and similarly
(p_{i}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i})\circ(p_{i}\circ\iota_{i}^{\prime})=\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}
for every
i\in F\cap F^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\iota_{i}^{\prime}\circ p_{i}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}=\sum_{j\in I}\iota_{j}^{\prime}\circ p_{j}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}=\iota_{i}
, which shows that
p_{i}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}^{\prime})
is a subobject isomorphism for every
i\in F\cap F^{\prime}
. Uniqueness follows from
p_{i}^{\prime}\circ\iota_{i}^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}
. When
i\in F\setminus F^{\prime}
, we have
p_{i}=\sum_{j\in F^{\prime}}p_{i}\circ\iota^{\prime}_{j}\circ p^{\prime}_{j}=0
. Then
\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{i}}=p_{i}\circ\iota_{i}=0
and therefore
\lambda_{i}\cong 0
. A similar argument gives
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\cong 0
for
j\in F^{\prime}\setminus F
.
∎

2.3
Equivariant Frobenius algebras

In this subsection, we recall the notion of a
G
-equivariant Frobenius algebra.

A
Frobenius algebra
in a monoidal category is an algebra object with a compatible coalgebra structure, see e.g.
[
FFRS06
, Section 2.3]
for the precise definition and more terminologies. The product, unit, coproduct, and counit of a Frobenius algebra
A
are denoted by
m_{A},\eta_{A},\Delta_{A}
, and
\varepsilon_{A}
respectively.

For a pivotal multitensor category
\mathcal{C}
, we say a Frobenius algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is
symmetric
if
\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
when
A^{\vee}
is taken to be
A
, see
[
FFRS06
, Definition 2.22]
. When
A
is moreover
special
(i.e.
m_{A}\circ\Delta_{A}
and
\varepsilon_{A}\circ\eta_{A}
are invertible scalars, see
[
FFRS06
, Definition 2.22(iii)]
), the product of the invertible scalars
m_{A}\circ\Delta_{A}
and
\varepsilon_{A}\circ\eta_{A}
is equal to
\dim A
. Since any symmetric special Frobenius algebra
A
has a unique corresponding coalgebra structure
(\tilde{\Delta}_{A},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{A})
with
m_{A}\tilde{\Delta}_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{A}\eta_{A}=\dim A
(see e.g.
[
Müg03
, Remark 3.14]
), we assume from the beginning that
m_{A}\Delta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and
\varepsilon_{A}\eta_{A}=\dim A
.

A
Q-system
in a
∗
-multitensor category is a triple
(\theta,w,x)
such that
(\theta,x^{\ast},w,x,(\dim\theta)w^{\ast})
is a special Frobenius algebra, see e.g.
[
BKLR15
, Definition 3.8]
. A Q-system is automatically symmetric by definition.

In this article, we have to consider
equivariant
Frobenius algebras. To define this notion, note that if
A
is an algebra (resp. Frobenius algebra) in a monoidal category with an action of a group
G
, then
{}^{g}A=({}^{g}A,{}^{g}m_{A},{}^{g}\eta_{A})
(resp.
{}^{g}A=({}^{g}A,{}^{g}m_{A},{}^{g}\eta_{A},{}^{g}\Delta_{A},{}^{g}\varepsilon_{A})
) is again an algebra (resp. Frobenius algebra).

Definition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a monoidal category with an action of a group
G
. A
G
-equivariant algebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a pair
(A,z^{A})
of an algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
and a
G
-equivariant structure
z^{A}
on
A
(i.e.
z^{A}_{gh}=z_{g}^{A}{}^{g}z_{h}^{A}
for
g,h\in G
, where we suppress the canonical isomorphism
(\chi_{g,h})_{A}:{}^{g}({}^{h}A)\cong{}^{gh}A
in Theorem
2.6
, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Definition 2.7.2]
) such that
z^{A}_{g}:{}^{g}A\cong A
is an algebra isomorphism for every
g\in G
. Similarly, a
G
-equivariant Frobenius algebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a pair
A=(A,z^{A})
of a Frobenius algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
and a
G
-equivariant structure
z^{A}
on
A
such that
z^{A}_{g}
is a Frobenius algebra isomorphism for every
g\in G
. A
G
-equivariant Q-system
in a
∗
-multitensor category is a
G
-equivariant Frobenius algebra

such that
A
is a Q-system and

is unitary for every

. A
homomorphism
between

-equivariant (Frobenius) algebras is a (Frobenius) algebra homomorphism that is

-equivariant. By an

between

-equivariant Q-systems, we always mean a unitary isomorphism.

Example 2.19
.

It is well-known

4.1
The neutral double of a
G

In this subsection, we introduce the neutral double construction (Definition
4.7
). For this, we begin with crossed products.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
G
-graded multitensor category and let
\mathcal{D}
be a multitensor category with an action
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}
of
G
. Then, we can define a bilinear bifunctor
\otimes:(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})\times(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
by putting
(c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2})\coloneqq c_{1}c_{2}\boxtimes{}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2}
for
c_{1}\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
,
c_{2}\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
and make
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
into a multitensor category, which is denoted by
\mathcal{C}\ltimes_{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{D}
or simply
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
. Another choice of the Deligne tensor product
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields a strictly isomorphic multitensor category. If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are tensor (resp. multifusion, resp. fusion) categories, then so is
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
has a
G
-grading
\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{C}_{g}\times\mathcal{D}
and
\otimes
is linear exact in each variable (see
[
EGNO15
, Proposition 4.2.1]
),
\otimes
can be extended to a linear exact functor on
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
and therefore on
(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})\times(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})
. Since

\displaystyle((c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2}))\otimes(c_{3}\boxtimes d_{3})

\displaystyle=(c_{1}c_{2})c_{3}\boxtimes{}^{\overline{c_{3}}}({}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2})d_{3}

\displaystyle(c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes((c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2})\otimes(c_{3}\boxtimes d_{3}))

\displaystyle=c_{1}(c_{2}c_{3})\boxtimes{}^{\overline{c_{2}c_{3}}}d_{1}({}^{\overline{c_{3}}}d_{2}d_{3})

for
c_{1}\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
,
c_{2},c_{3}\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
, we can define a natural isomorphism
a:(-\otimes-)\otimes-\cong-\otimes(-\otimes-)
to be the extension of the canonical isomorphism given by coherence (Theorem
2.6
), which satisfies the pentagon axiom by coherence. We put
\mathbf{1}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}}
and similarly define left and right unit isomorphisms by coherence. Thus,
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
turns into a monoidal category. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields a strictly isomorphic multitensor category by universality since we define the tensor product by extension.

Next, we show that
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is rigid and therefore a multitensor category. As in the proof of
[
Del90
, Proposition 5.17]
, it is enough to check the rigidity of the objects of
\mathcal{C}_{g}\times\mathcal{D}
for
g\in G
. We show that a left dual of
c\boxtimes d
for
c\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
d\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
is given by
c^{\vee}\boxtimes{}^{c}d^{\vee}
. Indeed, since
(c^{\vee}\boxtimes{}^{c}d^{\vee})(c\boxtimes d)=c^{\vee}c\boxtimes d^{\vee}d
and
(c\boxtimes d)(c^{\vee}\boxtimes{}^{c}d^{\vee})=cc^{\vee}\boxtimes{}^{c}(dd^{\vee})
, where we suppressed some isomorphisms by Theorem
2.6
, we can define evaluation and coevaluation maps by putting
\mathrm{ev}_{c\boxtimes d}\coloneqq\mathrm{ev}_{c}\boxtimes\mathrm{ev}_{d}
and
\mathrm{coev}\coloneqq\mathrm{coev}_{c}\boxtimes{}^{c}\mathrm{coev}_{d}
. The conjugate equations follow from those for
c
and
d
and therefore
(c\boxtimes d)^{\vee}=c^{\vee}\boxtimes{}^{c}d^{\vee}
. We can also show that
{}^{\vee}(c\boxtimes d)={}^{\vee}c\boxtimes{}^{c\vee}d
and therefore
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is rigid.
∎

We call
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
the
crossed product
of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
. Let us see that
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
inherits structures on
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a group, let
\mathcal{C}
be a
G
-graded pivotal multitensor category, and let
\mathcal{D}
be a pivotal multitensor category with a pivotal action of
G
. Then, the pivotal structures on
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
induce a pivotal structure
\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}
on
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields an isomorphic pivotal structure.

Proof.

Since
(c\boxtimes d)^{\vee\vee}=c^{\vee\vee}\boxtimes d^{\vee\vee}
for
c\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
d\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
by the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we can define a natural isomorphism
\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}:\mathrm{id}\cong(-)^{\vee\vee}
by putting
\delta_{c\boxtimes d}^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}\coloneqq\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c}\boxtimes\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
. Since the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
is pivotal, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{(c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2})}^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{c_{1}c_{2}\boxtimes{}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2}}^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}=\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c_{1}c_{2}}\boxtimes\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{{}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2}}=(\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c_{1}}\otimes\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c_{2}})\boxtimes({}^{\overline{c_{2}}}\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{d_{1}}\otimes\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{d_{2}})

\displaystyle=(\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c_{1}}\boxtimes\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{d_{1}})\otimes(\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{c_{2}}\boxtimes\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{d_{2}})=\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}_{c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1}}\otimes\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}_{c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2}}

for
c_{1}\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
,
c_{2}\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
by the monoidality of
\delta^{\mathcal{C}}
and
\delta^{\mathcal{D}}
. Thus,
\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}
is monoidal and therefore a pivotal structure on
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields an isomorphic pivotal structure since we define the pivotal structure by extension.
∎

.

When
\mathcal{C}
and

for

and

and

are split (i.e.
\en(\lambda)\cong k
for any simple object

, see

) semisimple spherical tensor categories, since every simple object of

for some simple objects

and

respectively of
\mathcal{C}
and

by

for every simple object

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
G
-crossed multitensor category and let
\mathcal{D}
be a multitensor category with an action
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}
of
G
. Then, an action of
G
is naturally induced on
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields a
G
-tensor isomorphic one. When
\mathcal{D}
is moreover pivotal and
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}
and
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}
are pivotal actions, the induced action on
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is again pivotal.

Proof.

Define a linear exact functor
\gamma(g)
for every
g\in G
by putting
\gamma(g)(c\boxtimes d)\coloneqq{}^{g}c\boxtimes{}^{g}d
for
c\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
d\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
. Since

\displaystyle\gamma(g)(c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes\gamma(g)(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2})

\displaystyle={}^{g}c_{1}{}^{g}c_{2}\boxtimes{}^{g\overline{c_{2}}g^{-1}}({}^{g}d_{1}){}^{g}d_{2}

\displaystyle\gamma(g)((c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})\otimes(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2}))

\displaystyle={}^{g}(c_{1}c_{2})\boxtimes{}^{g}({}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2})

for
c_{1}\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
,
c_{2}\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
by
\partial{}^{g}c_{2}=g\partial c_{2}g^{-1}
, we can define a canonical natural isomorphism
J^{\gamma(g)}
by coherence. We similarly have canonical natural isomorphisms
\chi_{g,h}^{\gamma}
for
g,h\in G
and
\iota^{\gamma}
by coherence. Thus,
\gamma
is an action of
G
. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields a
G
-tensor isomorphic action since we define the action by extension. The last statement follows from the definitions of
\gamma
and the pivotal structure
\delta^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}
, see the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}

. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}

Proof.

We already know that
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is a pivotal category with a pivotal action of

by Proposition

. Since
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{g}\bigoplus_{h}\mathcal{C}_{h}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{h^{-1}g}
follows from
[

, Theorem 27]
by the assumption of split semisimplicity, we put
(\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D})_{g}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{h}\mathcal{C}_{h}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}_{h^{-1}g}
for
g\in G
. Then, we have
(\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D})_{g}\otimes(\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D})_{h}\subset(\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D})_{gh}
for
g,h\in G
since
\partial((c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1})(c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2}))=\partial(c_{1}c_{2}\boxtimes{}^{\overline{c_{2}}}d_{1}d_{2})=\partial c_{1}\partial d_{1}\partial c_{2}\partial d_{2}
for
c_{1},c_{2},d_{1},d_{2}\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
by definition. Thus,
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is a

-graded multitensor category. Moreover,
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is
G
-crossed since
\partial^{\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}}({}^{g}c\boxtimes{}^{g}d)=g\partial cg^{-1}g\partial dg^{-1}=g\partial c\partial dg^{-1}
for

and

. Another choice of
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
only yields an isomorphic

-crossed structure since changing

preserves
\mathcal{C}_{h}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}_{h^{-1}g}
and therefore yields an isomorphic
G
-grading.
∎

Next, we show that if
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are moreover

G
-braiding on a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

For a
G
-braided multitensor category
\mathcal{C}
, define
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
to be
\mathcal{C}
as an abelian category with an action of
G
. Put
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}_{g}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}
for
g\in G
. Then, by putting
\lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}\mu\coloneqq{}^{\partial_{\mathcal{C}}\mu}\lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}\mu
for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}})
and
\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}})=\homog(\mathcal{C})
, and putting
\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{C}}
, we obtain a multitensor structure on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
. Moreover, by putting
b^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq b^{\mathcal{C}-}_{\lambda,\mu}
(see Definition
2.11
), we obtain a
G
-braiding on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
. We call
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
the
reverse
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
\mu,\nu\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
, we have
(\lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}\mu)\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}\nu={}^{\nu}({}^{\mu}\lambda\mu)\nu
and
\lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}(\mu\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}\nu)={}^{\nu\mu}\lambda({}^{\nu}\mu\nu)
, where the objects on the right-hand sides are regarded as objects of
\mathcal{C}
, since
\partial_{\mathcal{C}}({}^{\nu}\mu\nu)=\partial_{\mathcal{C}}\nu\partial_{\mathcal{C}}\mu
. Thus, we obtain an associative bilinear monoidal product by coherence and universality. We can similarly see that
\mathbf{1}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}}
is indeed a unit.

Next, we show that

Skew-Normal Diffusions
Max-Olivier Hongler, Daniele Rinaldo

Abstract
We present a class of scalar Markovian diffusion processes whose transition probability densities are skewed Gaussian distributions. Their stochastic dynamics involve nonlinear and time-dependent drifts driven by White Gaussian noise sources. The drifts are obtained via generalized h h -transforms of a novel class of local martingales. We can alternatively represent this class of processes as dynamic censoring models with partial observability and time-dependent correlations, which can be used as a skewness-inducing noise source for any diffusion process. We prove the invariance of our class of skew-Normal processes under linear transformations. We extend our results to Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusions.

Introduction

Skew-Normal probability laws extend the Gaussian ones by regulating the third moment by means of an extra parameter. This distribution has been introduced in the seminal paper by
Azzalini 1985
and subsequently vastly expanded (
Azzalini and
Capitanio 1999
,
Azzalini and
Dalla Valle 1996
,
Azzalini 2013
). One of such extensions, known as the extended skew-Normal distribution (
Azzalini 2005
) adds a fourth parameter which allows for flexibility in modeling both moments and tails.
The skew-Normal distribution and its extensions have yielded fruitful applications in numerous fields, such as statistical theory (
Capitanio et al. 2003
), environmental sciences (
Loperfido and
Guttorp 2008
and
Sharples and
Pezzey 2007
), econometrics (
Marchenko and
Genton 2012
), time series (
Jha and
Singh 2023
), finance (
Harvey et al. 2010
) and more. However, the probabilistic characterization of the skew-Normal distribution in diffusion processes and stochastic differential equations (SDE), to the best of our knowledge, has not yet been explored. This paper aims to fill this gap by unveiling a class of diffusion processes, called skew-Normal diffusions, whose transition probability densities belong to the class of skew-Normal distributions.

Our approach hinges on a change of measure based on a logarithmic transformation, also known as
h
-transform. Pioneered by
Doob 1957
,
h
-transforms are intimately connected to the theory of harmonic functions (
Kunita 1969
,
Ito and
Watanabe 1965
,
Fleming and
Sheu 1997
), and play a key role in conditioning a process on its behavior in its lifetime as well as in forcing a diffusion towards a final configuration (
Fleming 1977
,
Dai Pra 1991
,
Rogers and
Williams 2000
,
Chetrite and
Touchette 2015
).
Our construction of skew-Normal diffusions takes an entirely different approach than the existing work on skew Brownian motion (
Itô and
McKean 1963
,
Harrison and
Shepp 1981
). This literature studies Markov processes which are generated by a Brownian motion partly reflected at a barrier, and are solutions of stochastic differential equations involving a local time component (
Ouknine 1991
,
Lejay 2006
,
Appuhamillage et al. 2011
), finding both physical and financial applications (
Decamps
et al. 2004
,
Gairat and
Shcherbakov 2017
).
Our class of processes, on the other hand, is defined by SDEs in which the skewness is due to a nonlinear and time-inhomogeneous drift, and can be characterized as partially observable Brownian motions. Furthermore, this class of semimartingales is entirely tractable analytically, which is an uncommon occurrence when dealing with nonlinear SDEs.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we modify a general diffusion process by means of an
h
-transform of its TPD in reversed time, generating a diffusion that behaves according to a skewed version of the original transition density.
Starting from this result, in Sections
3
and
4
we construct a general class of skew-Normal diffusions from a standard Brownian motion.
Lastly, in Section
5
we present a series of applications and extensions to optimal transportation, lumpable Markov functions, dynamic censoring and partial observability models, as well as how skew-Normal diffusions can be used as noise source for general Itô diffusions.

2
H-transforms and skewed diffusions

We begin by making a general observation. For simplicity of the exposition and consistency with what follows, in this section we have focused on the scalar case: all the results remain valid for an arbitrary
d
-dimensional diffusion.
Consider the scalar diffusion process in the time interval
[0,T]
in the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
driven by the stochastic differential equation (SDE)

dX_{t}=f(X_{t},t)dt+\sigma(X_{t},t)dW_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0}.

(1)

For all
t\in[0,T]
and
c,x\in\mathbb{R}
, we assume
f(x,t)
to be bounded, continuous and satisfy Hölder conditions in
x
. We further assume
\sigma^{2}:=\sigma(x,t)^{2}
in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])
and
\sigma^{2},\sigma^{2}_{x}
to be bounded and satisfy Hölder conditions in
x
and
t
. The SDE (
1
) therefore admits a weak solution in
[0,T]
with initial condition
x_{0}
. The probability distribution
P(x,t):=P(x,t|x_{0},0)

that defines the behavior of
X_{t}
(its transition density) is a positive definite solution of the Kolmogorov forward equation (KFE)

\partial_{t}P(x,t)=-\partial_{x}f(x,t)P(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\sigma^{2}(x,t)P(x,t),\qquad P(x,0)=\delta(x-x_{0}),

(2)

where
\delta
is the Dirac delta function. Now, define
H
as the “backward” cumulative distribution of the process
X_{t}
:

H(x,t):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}P(y,T-t)dy.

We can then state the following:

Proposition 1
.

Let
X_{t}
be a weak solution of (
1
). Then the diffusion process in
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},Q)
given by

dX_{t}=\big[f(X_{t},t)+\sigma^{2}(X_{t},t)\nabla\log H(X_{t},t)\big]dt+\sigma(X_{t},t)dW_{t}

(3)

admits a weak solution in
[0,T]
with associated transition probability density
Q(x,t):=Q(x,t|x_{0},0)
given by

Q(x,t)=P(x,t)\frac{H(x,t)}{H(x_{0},0)}.

(4)

The measure
Q
skews the original measure
P
, and the skewness is time-dependent. At the final time
t=T
, Eq. (
4
) becomes

Q(x,T)=P(x,T)\frac{\mathbb{H}(x)}{H(x_{0},0)}

(5)

where
\mathbb{H}(x)
is the Heaviside step function
\mathbbm{1}[x\geq x_{0}]
, and the achievable states of the diffusion are restricted to the half-line
X_{T}\geq x_{0}
.

Proof.

We first note that we can write the function
H
as

H(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P(y,T-t)\mathbbm{1}[y\leq x]dy

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since
H
is at least
C^{2}
and positive definite and
P
solves the KFE (
2
), then by linearity one can see that
H
is a classical solution of the backward heat problem

\partial_{t}H(x,t)+f(x,t)\partial_{x}H(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(x,t)\partial_{xx}H(x,t)=0.

(6)

Since by construction
\mathbb{E}[H(x,t)]<\infty
, then
\mathbb{E}[H(x,t)/H(x_{0},0)]=1
, and the process
H(x,t)/H(x_{0},0)
is a positive martingale.
Using
H_{t}:=H(x,t)
we can then create a new measure Q given by
dQ=(H_{t}/H_{0})dP
. Then by Girsanov’s theorem we have that

dH_{t}=H_{t}dM_{t},

where
M_{t}
is the only (local) martingale such that
H_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[M_{t}]
. We then have

\displaystyle dH_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}H_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle dM_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H_{t})^{-1}dH_{t}=\frac{\partial_{x}H_{t}}{H_{t}}dW_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\log(\partial_{x}H_{t})dW_{t}.

Therefore, the process
dW_{t}-\partial_{x}\log(H(x,t))dt
is a
Q
-Brownian motion and (
3
) admits as transition density

Q(x,t|x_{0},t_{0})=P(x,t|x_{0},t_{0})\frac{H(x,t)}{H(x_{0},0)},

where
P
is the Wiener measure, which is Eq.(
4
).
This is an application of Doob’s
h-transform
(see
Dai Pra 1991
and
Rogers and
Williams 2000
).
Lastly, Eq.(
5
) is obtained by direct integration of the Dirac delta function, since
H_{x}(x,T)=P(x,0)=\delta(x-x_{0})
.
∎

We can now approach the main objective of this paper, which is to study diffusion processes that behave according to a skew-Normal distribution, as well as to explore the properties that generate the skewness.

3
Skew-Normal diffusions with time-dependent skewness

Let us begin with the standard Brownian motion
W_{t}
equipped with the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}
in the real domain. Its transition density
P
- the Wiener measure - is the positive definite and normalized solution of the corresponding Kolmogorov forward equation.
Using Proposition
1
, we search for extensions of the Brownian motion that generate a stochastic differential equation that behaves according to a transition density of the skew-Normal family.
The result is presented in the following Theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
t\in[0,T],T\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the couple of nonlinear diffusion processes described by the stochastic differential equations in the filtered probability space
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{F},Q)
given by

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left[\log h^{\pm}(x,t)\right]_{x=X_{t}^{\pm}}dt+dW_{t},\qquad X_{0}^{\pm}=x_{0},

(7)

\displaystyle h^{\pm}(X^{\pm}_{t},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\alpha_{t}X_{t}^{\pm}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds,\qquad\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T-t}},

is characterised by the transition probability densities

Q^{\pm}(x,t|x_{0},0):=Q^{\pm}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\alpha_{t}x}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\alpha_{0}x_{0}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds\right)^{-1}

(8)

which are the right-skewed (
Q^{+}
for
X^{+}_{t}
) and left-skewed (
Q^{-}
for
X^{-}_{t}
) extended skew-Normal distributions with time-dependent skewness
\alpha_{t}
. Furthermore, the process
h^{\pm}(X^{\pm}_{t},t)

is a martingale, and
\mathbb{E}h^{\pm}=1
.

Proof.

See
A
.
∎

Remark.
For
t=T
, the process is stopped and the drift in Eq.(
7
) becomes singular. Coherently with Proposition
1
, Eq.(
8
) reads as

Q^{\pm}(x,T)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi T}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\frac{\mathbb{H}(\pm x)}{h^{\pm}(x_{0},0)},

which is a half-Normal probability law bounded to the left at
x_{0}
. Furthermore, for
x_{0}=0
the transition density Eq.(
7
) is a skew-Normal distribution (with time-dependent skewness):

Q^{\pm}(x,t|0,0)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\alpha_{t}x}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds.

For all details on skew-Normal and extended skew-Normal distributions, we refer to
Azzalini 2013
.
Figure
1
presents an illustration of the time evolution of the transition density (
8
).
We highlight the fact that the parameter
x_{0}/\sqrt{T}
in an extended skew-Normal transition density with time-dependent skewness
is also sometimes called the
truncation
parameter. This is consistent with the property of (
7
) such that at
t=T
only part of the domain states remains achievable. This observation hints at the presence of a censoring process at play in the dynamics of skew-Normal diffusions: this is indeed the case, and we explore this aspect further in Section
5
.

Illustration of the evolution of the transition probability density for the right-skewed diffusion
X_{t}^{+}
with time-dependent skewness described by the SDE (
7
) for
T=10
, as time increases from
0.5
to
T
(thick red curve).

4
An extended class of skew-Normal diffusion processes

The diffusion process defined by Eq.(
7
) is exclusively defined for
t\in[0,T]
. One may wonder whether one can extend this kind of process to a class of skew-Normal diffusion processes that does not impose any time restriction.
The next Theorem addresses this question by uncovering a new class of skew-Normal diffusion processes.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the class of functions
\psi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,1]

and
\alpha_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
are solutions of the system of mixed ordinary differential and algebraic equations

\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{\Lambda_{t}}{\sqrt{\left[1-\Lambda_{t}^{2}\right]}},\qquad\dot{\Lambda}_{t}=\frac{1-\psi_{t}}{t}\Lambda_{t},

(9)

with
\psi_{0}=1
and
[1-\Lambda(t)^{2}]\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
for all
t
.
Then the dual diffusion processes
X^{\pm}_{t}
which are solutions of the stochastic differential equations

dX_{t}^{\pm}=\psi_{t}\partial_{x}\left[\log h_{l}^{\pm}(x,t)\right]_{x=X_{t}^{\pm}}dt+dW_{t},\qquad X_{0}=0,

(10)

where the class of processes
h^{\pm}_{l}(X_{t}^{\pm},t)
is given by

h_{l}^{\pm}(X_{t}^{\pm},t):=\psi_{t}\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha_{t}X_{t}^{\pm}}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds,

are characterised by the skew-Normal transition probability densities:

Q^{\pm}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\alpha_{t}x}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds.

(11)

The class of processes
h_{l}^{\pm}(X_{t}^{\pm},t)
,
where
\psi_{t}
and
\alpha_{t}
are defined by (
9
), are local martingales.

Proof.

See
B
.
∎

It can be easily seen that Eq. (
7
) is obtained by setting
\psi_{t}=1
, which yields
\Lambda_{t}=B,\alpha_{t}=\pm\frac{BC}{\sqrt{\left[1-B^{2}C^{2}t\right]}}
, where
B\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a constant. Using as boundary condition the requirement that
t\in[0,T]
and that
1-\Lambda_{t}^{2}>0
, we obtain the constants
BC=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}
, which yields the SDE with associated transition density (
8
). If a transition density with constant skewness
\alpha_{t}=\pm\alpha
is desired, using Theorem
4.1
after lengthy but straightforward computations yields the closed form
\psi_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(2+\alpha^{2}t)}{(1+\alpha^{2}t)}
. Figure
2
illustrates the evolution of the transition density for skew-Normal diffusions with constant skewness
\alpha=\pm 1
.

Figure 2:
Time evolution of the transition density (
11
) for the skew-normal diffusions
X_{t}^{\pm}
with unit skewness
\alpha=1
, which corresponds to
\psi_{t}:=\psi=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(2+t)}{(1+t)}
starting from
t=1
to
t=4
.

Remark
.
(The role of the initial condition)
We note that in order to have (
11
) as the probability law driving a SDE for a general initial condition
X_{0}=x_{0}
, the drift has to be modified
to include the initial condition.
This implies that a nonzero initial condition is equivalent to a translation of
X_{t}^{\pm}
, as the probability shift caused by the nonlinear drift is now dependent on its position with respect to the initial level
x_{0}
, and therefore
Q^{\pm}(x-x_{0},t|0,0)\neq Q^{\pm}(x,t|x_{0},0)
. The process defined by the weak solution of (
10
) is a Markov process for all initial conditions (as shown in the proof of Theorem
4.1
),
but we can only characterize explicitly the density (
11
) as a “stand-alone” probability density for
Q(x,t|0,0)
, and the analytical form of the general TPD
Q^{\pm}(x,t|x_{0},0)
is not known. This occurs since general diffusions do not enjoy translation invariance, unlike the Brownian motion. This result is the key determinant of the local nature of
h_{l}^{\pm}
. This result is furthermore consistent with the purpose of this paper to exploring how skew-Normal marginals emerge from modifications of a Brownian motion, which in the construction of a general SDE is 0 at
t=0
.

Theorem
4.1
highlights the flexibility in interpretation of the general class of skew-Normal diffusion processes that solve (
10
). The relationship between skewness
\alpha_{t}
and drift amplitude
\psi_{t}
given by Eq.(
9
) is suitable for a variety of modeling purposes. For example, by fixing
\psi_{t}=1/2
the resulting process is described by probability density with a time-dependent skewness proportional to the reciprocal of
\pm\sqrt{t}
. This specific time-dependence is a suitable feature for stochastic volatility models in finance: for example, empirical studies of the equity skew (
Carr and
Wu 2003
and
Azzone and
Baviera 2022
) observe how the S&P 500 short-time skew is proportional to
-1/\sqrt{t}
.

5
Applications and extensions

In this section we examine a number of applications, interpretations and extensions of skew-Normal
diffusions. We shall discuss optimal transportation, lumpable Markov functions, dynamic censoring and partial observability models, as well as how skew-Normal diffusions can be
used as noise source for general Itô diffusions.

5.1
Optimal transportation

Following
Dai Pra 1991
, one can show that the drift of the processes in Theorem
3.1
is optimal, in the sense that it minimizes the divergence between the Wiener measure and the skew-Normal measure for all
t\in[0,T]
. To develop this last point, define the Kullback-Leibler divergence
KL(p,q)
between any two measures
p
and
q
defined in the same measurable space as

KL(p,q)=\int\log\left(\frac{dp}{dq}\right)dq.

We can then state the following Corollary:

Corollary 5.0.1

(Connection with optimal transportation)
.

The drift given by

u^{*}_{t}=\partial_{x}\log h^{+}(x,t)|_{x=X^{\pm}_{t}}

is the solution of the control problem

\displaystyle\min_{u}

\displaystyle KL(P,Q^{+})

s.t.

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{+}=u_{t}dt+dW_{t}

where
t\in[0,T]
,
u_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}||u_{t}||^{2}dt<\infty
,
P
is the Wiener measure and
Q^{+}
is the right-skewed skew-Normal measure induced by (
7
) on
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
. The same result holds for
X^{-}_{t}
and the corresponding left-skewed
Q^{-}
.

Proof.

Since
h^{+}(X_{t},t)
is a martingale and
\mathbb{E}h^{+}(X_{t},t)=1
for
t\in[0,T]
, by Girsanov’s theorem we can write for all
t

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h^{+}(X_{t},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[h^{+}(X_{t},t)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}dW_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\log h^{+}(X_{t},t)^{2}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\exp\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\log h^{+}(X_{t},t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}\log h^{+}(x,s)_{x=X^{+}_{s}})^{2}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\exp\left[KL(P,Q^{+})-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}\log h^{+}(x,s)_{x=X^{+}_{s}})^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1

where we used the fact that
h^{+}(X_{t},t)
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative between the measures
P
and
Q
. This implies that

KL(P(x,t),Q(x,t))\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}\log h^{+}(x,s)_{x=X^{+}_{s}})^{2}ds

for
t\in[0,T]
. Since Theorem
3.1
shows that
h^{+}(X_{t}^{+},t)
is a martingale, we use Theorem 3.1 in
Dai Pra 1991
and can then say that
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{x}\log h(x,s))^{2}ds
is minimized when
h=h^{+}
and the inequality holds with equality.
∎

The problem is thus equivalent to an optimal control problem where one wants to transport a diffusion from one distribution to another under a quadratic cost constraint (i.e. “minimal energy”).
Summarising, Theorem
3.1
presents a stochastic differential equation whose transition probability density follows an extended skew-Normal law. The skewness continuously increases for times
t\in[0,T]\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
. At the end time
t=T
, one part of the domain (above or below
x_{0}
) is unattainable by
X_{t}^{\pm}
, which is effectively characterised by a half-Normal law.
In addition, the time-dependent drift of this conditioned process solves an optimal transport problem in which one minimizes the divergence between the Wiener (Gaussian) measure
P
and the skew-Normal measure
Q
. The diffusion process Eq.(
7
) is essentially a skewed Brownian bridge that forces the standard Brownian motion
t=0
to ultimately match a half-Normal process at time
t=T
. This matching task is optimally realised for all
t\in[0,T]
by minimising the Kullback-Leibler distance between the nominal Wiener measure
P(x,t|x_{0},0)
and the skew-Normal
Q^{\pm}(x,t|x_{0},0)
.

5.2
Skew-Normal diffusions as Markov functions

In an important contribution by Rogers and Pitman
Rogers and
Pitman 1981
, conditions are established for whether a function of a Markov process remains itself a Markov process. It is a natural observation to imagine a Gaussian distribution as the superposition of two skew-Normal distributions with opposite skewness. With this idea in mind, we want to construct a Markov process on the extended probability space
\{-1,+1\}\times\mathbb{R}
which lumped on the reduced space
\mathbb{R}
yields a Brownian motion. In other words, can the random superposition of two skew-Normal diffusions yield a Brownian motion? This question is answered in the following Corollary.

Corollary 5.0.2

(Brownian motion as random superposition of skew-Normal diffusions)
.

The superposition of of two
\mathcal{B}
-skew-Normal diffusions with opposite skewness
\pm\alpha_{t}
on the extended domain
\{-1,+1\}\times\mathbb{R}
given by

dX_{t}^{\mathcal{B}}=\partial_{x}\left\{\log\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\mathcal{B}\alpha_{t}x}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds\right\}_{x=X_{t}^{\mathcal{B}}}dt+dW_{t},

(12)

where
\alpha_{t}=1/\sqrt{T-t}
and
\mathcal{B}
is an asymmetric Bernoulli random variable that takes values
\{-1,+1\}
with respective probabilities
p_{-}(x_{0}),p_{+}(x_{0})
given by

p_{\pm}(x_{0}):=\int_{-\infty}^{\pm\frac{x_{0}}{\sqrt{T}}}e^{-s^{2}/2}ds,

(13)

is a standard Brownian motion.

Proof.

The Corollary requires to show that

\displaystyle P(x,t|x_{0},0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{2t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{-}Q^{-}(x,t|x_{0},0)+p_{+}Q^{+}(x,t|x_{0},0)

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[p_{-}{h^{-}(x,t)\over h^{-}(x_{0},0)}+p_{+}{h^{+}(x,t)\over h^{+}(x_{0},0)}\right]e^{-\frac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{2t}}

for some
p_{\pm}\in[0,1]
and where
p_{+}+p_{-}=1
. It can be easily verified that (
14
) holds when
p_{\pm}=p_{\pm}(x_{0})
where
p_{\pm}(x_{0})
is given by (
13
).
∎

For
x_{0}=0
, the Bernoulli variable is symmetric and
p_{-}=p_{+}=1/2
. Lastly, we note that a dual situation occurs in
Arcand
et al. 2020
, where a nonlinear-drifted diffusion process results as a superposition of two of oppositely drifted Brownian motions.

5.3
Dynamic censoring and partial observability

In Section
2
and
3
we have observed in passing how censoring dynamics are at play in the dynamics of skewed diffusion processes, since in the time interval
[0,T]
the achievable states are continuously reduced up to a half line at
t=T
. In this section we want to explore this mechanism in greater detail, and characterize our new class of processes described in Theorem
4.1
as both dynamic censoring processes as well as partially observable Brownian motions.
We can now complement the description of the class of diffusions Eq.(
10
), by exhibiting how the bi-dimensional correlated Brownian motion can itself generate a marginal skew-Normal transition density. To this aim, we now establish the following result:

Lemma 5.1

(Dynamic censoring model with correlations)
.

On the space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, consider the two-dimensional
(X_{t},Y_{t})
diffusion process with time-dependent correlations given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}=dW_{1,t},\\
dY_{t}=\rho_{t}dX_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho_{t}^{2}}dW_{2,t},\qquad\qquad\rho_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[-1,+1],\\
X_{0}=Y_{0}=x_{0},\end{array}\right.

(15)

where
W_{1,t}
and
W_{2,t}
are two independent
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted standard Brownian motions. If
Y_{t}
is only observable if positive, the censored posterior density
\Pi_{t}(x|y\geq 0))
is the skew-Normal density given by

\Pi(x,t|y\geq 0)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{\rho_{t}}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{t}^{2}}}x}e^{-s^{2}/2}ds.

(16)

Note that for
t\in[0,T]
and correlations
\rho_{t}=\pm\sqrt{t/T}\in[-1,+1]
(
15
) reduces to Eq.(
8
). Furthermore, by setting
\psi_{t}=\frac{1}{2}
in (
9
) yielding
e^{\Gamma(t)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
and
\alpha_{t}=\pm\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-C^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
. For all
C\in[0,1)
we obtain (
15
) with constant correlations.

Proof.

By construction, the process
(X_{t},Y_{t})
is a bivariate Gaussian with correlations
\rho_{t}
and respective variances
\langle X^{2}_{t}\rangle=t
and
\langle Y_{t}^{2}\rangle=\left[\rho_{t}^{2}+(1-\rho_{t}^{2})\right]t=t
. Its joint transition density is then given by :

P(x,y,t|0,0):=P(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi t\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}_{t}}}e^{-\left\{\frac{x^{2}-2\rho_{t}xy+y^{2}}{2t\left[1-\rho^{2}_{t}\right]}\right\}}.

By direct marginal integration one then obtains

\displaystyle\Pi(x,t|y\geq 0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}P(x,y,t)dy=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\left[1+{\rm Erf}\left(\frac{x\rho_{t}}{\sqrt{2t[1-\rho^{2}_{t}]}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{x\rho_{t}}{\sqrt{t[1-\rho^{2}_{t}]}}}e^{-s^{2}/2}ds,

where the last equality follows from the identity

\int_{-\infty}^{\pm x}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}}\left[1+{\rm Erf}\left(\frac{\pm x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right].

Lastly, the normalization
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi(x,t|y\geq 0)dx=1
follows directly from the anti-symmetry of error functions.
∎

Lemma
5.1
is a dynamic extension of the “hidden truncation” models presented by
Arnold et al. 2002
. Furthermore, the Lemma shows that the loss of information due to censoring on the process
Y_{t}
generates the skewness in the distribution, while preserving the Markovian character of the process.
Heuristically speaking, the information loss due to censoring is precisely balanced by the information gain due to non-vanishing correlations. Note that we are still firmly within our framework: setting the correlations as
\rho_{t}=\sqrt{t/T}
for

yields Eq.(

both Brownian motions are completely correlated, namely

, and hence the censoring

implies that both processes are characterised by the same half-Normal law. Lastly, (

\psi_{t}=\frac{1}{2}
, yielding
e^{\Gamma(t)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
and
\alpha_{t}=\pm\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-C^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
. For all

we obtain the model shown in Lemma

with constant correlations.

We can finally show how the class of skew-Normal diffusions shown in Theorem
4.1
can be interpreted a novel class of partially observable Brownian motions.
The quantity in the drift of the form
\frac{\phi(x)}{\Phi(\pm x)}
, where
\phi
and
\Phi
are respectively the density and cumulative distribution functions of the Gaussian distribution, is well-known in statistics as the inverse Mills ratio. This quantity is usually associated to the presence of censoring in variables: see for example the literature in econometrics on sample selection (
Hayashi 2011
).
The following Lemma shows how the class of processes
X_{t}^{\pm}
that solve the SDE defined in Theorem 3 can be interpreted as a dynamic selection model in which
\psi_{t}
calibrates the increments

and
\alpha_{t}
modulates the noise amplitude determining the range of the censored domain.

Lemma 5.2

(Dynamic selection model with time-dependent parameters)
.

The class of skew-normal diffusion processes described in Theorem
4.1
is equivalent to a class of dynamic selection models given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[dX_{t}^{\pm}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\psi_{t}\mathbb{E}\left[d\tilde{W}_{t}\ \big|s_{t}^{\pm}=1\right]

(17)

\displaystyle s_{t}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\left[X_{t}^{+}\geq\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle s_{t}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\left[X_{t}^{-}\leq\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}\right],

where
d\tilde{W}_{t}
is the increment of a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted standard Brownian motion.
The processes
X_{t}^{\pm}
are two partially observable Brownian motions.

Proof.

We can write Eq. (
10

dX_{t}^{\pm}=\pm\psi_{t}\alpha_{t}\frac{\phi(a_{t}y)}{\Phi(a_{t}y)}dt+dW_{t}.

From basic properties of the truncated Gaussian distribution, one can then see that the deterministic increments in
dt
of
X^{\pm}_{t}
are determined by the first moment of a centered Gaussian variable
z_{t}
with variance
1/\alpha_{t}
censored at the threshold of the realization at
t
of the process
X_{t}^{\pm}
:

dX_{t}^{\pm}=\pm\psi_{t}\ \mathbb{E}[z_{t}|z_{t}\gtreqless X_{t}^{\pm}]dt+dW_{t}.

These SDE can therefore be written as

dX_{t}^{\pm}=\pm\psi_{t}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}\frac{\tilde{W}_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\ \right|\left.\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}\frac{\tilde{W}_{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\gtreqless X_{t}^{\pm}\right]dt+dW_{t},

which have as deterministic increments the expectations of increments of left- and right-censored independent
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted Brownian motions
\tilde{W}_{t}
, truncated at
X_{t}^{\pm}
. Since the increment of
\tilde{W}_{t}
is stationary and independent from
dW_{t}
,
we then have that
\mathbb{E}(\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}|\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}<X_{t}^{+})=\phi(X_{t}^{+})/\Phi(X_{t}^{+})
and
\mathbb{E}(\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}|\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}>X_{t}^{-})=\phi(X_{t}^{-})/(1-\Phi(X_{t}^{-}))
.
Defining then the auxiliary variables as
s_{t}^{\pm}=\mathbbm{1}[\alpha_{t}^{-1/2}d\tilde{W}_{t}\lessgtr X_{t}^{\pm}]
, Eq.(
17
) follows,
and the processes within our class of skew-Normal diffusions (
10
) are therefore interpretable as partially observable Brownian motions.
∎

The literature on sample selection starts with the seminal paper by
Heckman 1979
, up to
Azzalini
et al. 2019
, who studied the role of the skew-Normal distribution in a static censoring model.
Here we have instead a dynamic censoring framework, where the increment

is partially observable: it is only observed in

if the “current” value

is greater (for
X^{+}
) or lesser (for

) than an independent random variable with zero mean and variance

.
The skewness in the distribution is therefore generated by the selection mechanism.
The increment

, whilst independent by construction from

, should be interpreted as the “previous” increment of the Brownian motion
W_{t}
:
introducing in the SDE described in Theorem

a general constant diffusion coefficient

one can show similarly as before that its solutions
behave according to a skew-Normal distribution with mean zero, variance
\sigma^{2}
and skewness
\pm\alpha_{t}
. One can then see how the selection equation in (
17

\sigma^{2}/\alpha_{t}
.
Note that if we choose

as in Eq.(

and

implied by the

5.4
Skew-Normal diffusions as noise source

We can now show how skew-Normal diffusions can be used as noise source for a general Itô diffusion, in order to generate a stochastic process that admits as transition density a skewed version of its Brownian motion-driven counterpart.

Let us start with a fully solvable example using the stationary Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, showing how skew-Normal diffusions are invariant to linear transformations. Let us consider the following 2-dimensional diffusion in the closed interval

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda X_{t}dt+dZ_{t}^{\pm},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\left\{\log h^{\pm}(z,t)\right\}_{z=Z_{t}^{\pm}}+dW_{t},\qquad Z_{0}=0,

3.1
. We can then state the following result:

The transition density

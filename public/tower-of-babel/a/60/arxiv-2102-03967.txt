The Embedded Homology of Hypergraph Pairs


Abstract
In this paper, we generalize the embedded homology groups of hypergraphs initially given in [ 2 ] and study the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs. We prove some long exact sequences as well as a Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs. Moreover, we briefly discuss the two-dimensional persistence for the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs.

Introduction

The relative homology groups of chain complex pairs is a generalization of the homology groups of a single chain complex. In classical homology theory, relative simplicial homology groups are used to study simplicial complex pairs (cf.
[
10
, p. 115]
and
[
11
, Chapter 3]
). In
[
7
]
, A. Grigor’yan, R. Jimenez, Y. Muranov and S.-T. Yau defined the relative path homology groups for digraphs and studied the Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms for the path homology.

In various disciplines of sciences and technologies, hypergraphs are models for complex networks. A hypergraph can be interpreted as a "virtual" simplicial complex with some non-maximal faces missing. The simplicial homology methods can be applied to investigate hypergraphs (cf.
[
2
,
12
,
13
]
).

Let
V
be a finite set with a total order
\prec
.
The elements of
V
are called
vertices
.
Let
\Delta[V]
be the standard (abstract) simplicial complex with vertices from
V
(cf.
[
10
, pp. 103-105]
and
[
2
, Example 1.1]
).
The set of the simplices in
\Delta[V]

is given by all the non-empty subsets of
V
.
For convenience, we just use the (abstract) simplicial complex
\Delta[V]
to denote the collection of all the non-empty subsets of
V
.

A
hypergraph

\mathcal{H}
with vertices from
V

is a subset of
\Delta[V]
(cf.
[
2
, p. 480]
) An element of
\mathcal{H}
is called a
hyperedge
of
\mathcal{H}
. For any hyperedge
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}
, we can write
\sigma
in the form

\displaystyle\sigma=\{v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\}

uniquely
where
n\geq 0
and
v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec\cdots\prec v_{n}
are distinct vertices in
V
. We define
n
to be the dimension of
\sigma
. We say that
\sigma
is of
dimension

n
or
\sigma
is an
n
-hyperedge
. We write
\dim\sigma=n
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph with vertices from
V
. If for any hyperedge
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}
and any non-empty subset
\tau\subseteq\sigma
we always have
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
, then we call
\mathcal{H}
an
(abstract) simplicial complex
. A hyperedge of a simplicial complex is called a
simplex
.

In 1991, A.D. Parks and S.L. Lipscomb
[
12
]
used simplicial complexes and simplicial homology to study hypergraphs. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. A.D. Parks and S.L. Lipscomb
[
12
]
defined the
associated simplicial complex

\Delta\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}
to be the simplicial complex obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by adding all the missing faces.
Specifically, by
[
12
]
, the associated simplicial complex
\Delta\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{H}=\{\sigma\in\Delta[V]\mid\sigma\subseteq\tau{\rm~for~some~}\tau\in\mathcal{H}\}.

(1.1)

It can be seen that
\Delta\mathcal{H}
is the smallest simplicial complex whose the set of the simplices contains all the hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
.

In 2020, supplementary to the associated simplicial complex
\Delta\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}
defined in
[
12
]
, the
lower-associated simplicial complex

\delta\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}
is defined by S. Ren
[
13
, Subsection 2.1]
as

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{H}=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{H}\mid{\rm~for~any~}\tau\subseteq\sigma{\rm~we~have~}\tau\in\mathcal{H}\}.

(1.2)

It can be seen that
\delta\mathcal{H}
is the largest simplicial complex whose the set of
the simplices is contained in the set of the hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
. In other words,
\delta\mathcal{H}
is the simplicial complex obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by removing all the hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
with missing faces.

Let
G
be an abelian group. We have a chain complex
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H})
with its coefficients in
G
.
We let
\partial_{*}
be the boundary map of
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H})
. For each
n\geq 0
, we write
G(\mathcal{H})_{n}
for the abelian group consisting of all the formal linear combinations of the
n
-hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
with coefficients in
G
. We have a graded abelian sub-group

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{H})_{*}=\{G(\mathcal{H})_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

of
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H})
.

In 2019, S. Bressan, J. Li, S. Ren and J. Wu
[
2
]
applied the path homology methods by A. Grigor’yan, Y. Lin, Y. Muranov and S.-T. Yau
[
8
,
9
]
to hypergraphs.
They defined in
[
2
]
the
infimum chain complex

{\rm Inf}(\mathcal{H})
and the
supremum chain complex

{\rm Sup}(\mathcal{H})
respectively as the largest sub-chain complex of
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H})
contained in
G(\mathcal{H})_{*}
and the smallest sub-chain complex of
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H})
containing
G(\mathcal{H})_{*}
. For each
n\geq 0
, it is given in
[
2
]
that

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{n}(\mathcal{H})=G(\mathcal{H})_{n}\cap\partial_{n}^{-1}G(\mathcal{H})_{n-1},~~~{\rm Sup}_{n}(\mathcal{H})=G(\mathcal{H})_{n}+\partial_{n+1}G(\mathcal{H})_{n+1}.

It is deduced from
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
that the canonical inclusion
\iota
of
{\rm Inf}_{*}(\mathcal{H})
into
{\rm Sup}_{*}(\mathcal{H})
induces an isomorphism of homology groups. The embedded homology of
\mathcal{H}
is defined in
[
2
]
as
H_{*}({\rm Inf}(\mathcal{H}))\cong H_{*}({\rm Sup}(\mathcal{H}))
, which is denoted as
H_{*}(\mathcal{H})
for short.

In this paper, we generalize the embedded homology groups of hypergraphs initially given in
[
2
]
and define the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs. Given a hypergraph pair
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}
is a sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
(cf. Definition
2
and Definition
8
), we prove in Section
3
that the homology of the quotient infimum chain complex
{\rm Inf}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}(A)
is canonically isomorphic to the homology of the quotient supremum chain complex
{\rm Sup}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}(\mathcal{A})
. We define this homology as the relative embedded homology groups of
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A})
.
We prove some long exact sequences for the embedded homology of hypergraph pairs
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A})
in Theorem
6.1
(main result I). Then we derive Corollary
6.2
from Theorem
6.1
. We also prove a Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the relative embedded homology groups of the hypergraph pair
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A})
in Theorem
7.1
(main result II). Then we derive Corollary
7.2
from Theorem
7.1
. Supplementary to Theorem
6.1
and Theorem
7.1
, we briefly discuss the two-dimensional persistence of the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs for the purpose of potential applications, by the end of this paper.

We give some preliminaries in Section
2
. We define the relative embedded homology groups of hypergraph pairs in Section
3
. We prove some auxiliary results in Section
4
. As a by-product, we give a cell structure for hypergraphs by using the relative embedded homology groups, in Section
5
. We prove Main Result I in Theorem
6.1
, Section
6
and prove Main Result II in Theorem
7.1
, Section
7
. Finally, we give a brief discussion on the two-dimensional persistence of the relative embedded homology groups, in Section
8

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes

Definition 1
.

Let

.

.

(cf.

with vertices from
V
such that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}

.

]

Definition 9
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\},\{v_{0},v_{1}\},\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\}\}
. Then

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{H})=0,

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\},\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}+\{v_{0},v_{1}\})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\},\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}).

(i). Let
\mathcal{A}=\{\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{0},v_{1}\}\}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is a simplicial complex, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{A})={\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{A})={\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{A})={\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A})=0.

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{A})={\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{A})=0,

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\})

where the boundary maps of the quotient chain complexes are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(\{v_{2}\})=0,~~~\partial_{1}(\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\})=0,

and

.

(ii). Let

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})={\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})={\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=0,

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{2}\},\{v_{1}\}-\{v_{0}\})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\}).

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{1}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})={\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=0,

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\})

where the boundary maps of the quotient chain complexes are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(\{v_{1}\})=\partial_{1}(\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\})=0,~~~\partial_{1}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\})=\{v_{1}\},

\displaystyle\partial_{2}(\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\})=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}.

Therefore,
H_{0}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=H_{1}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=H_{2}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=0
.

(iii). Let

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\},\{v_{1},v_{2}\}-\{v_{0},v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\}).

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\}),~~~{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\}),

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Inf}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})={\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})={\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=0,

\displaystyle{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{H})/{\rm Sup}_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})=\mathbb{Z}(\{v_{0}\},\{v_{2}\})

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(\{v_{0}\})=\partial_{0}(\{v_{1}\})=\partial_{0}(\{v_{2}\})=0,~~~\partial_{1}(\{v_{0},v_{1}\})=\{v_{1}\}-\{v_{0}\}.

4
Morphisms of hypergraph pairs and homomorphisms of the homology

In this section, we define the morphisms of hypergraph pairs and prove that such a morphism induces homomorphisms of the relative embedded homology groups. Moreover, we give some long exact sequences of homology groups for hypergraph pairs.

Let

and

(7.4)

Thus by Lemma
2.4
(i) and (
7.4
) we have

. Consequently, the hypergraph pairs

and

with

(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle H_{*}(\delta(\mathcal{H}(b)),\delta(\mathcal{H}(a)))\longrightarrow H_{*}(\mathcal{H}(b),\mathcal{H}(a))\longrightarrow H_{*}(\Delta(\mathcal{H}(b)),\Delta(\mathcal{H}(a)))

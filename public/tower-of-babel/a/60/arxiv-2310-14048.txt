Liouville-type results for the CR Yamabe equation in the Heisenberg group Thanks: The second author was supported by the NSERC Discovery Grant RGPIN-2022-04213.
Joshua Flynn, Jérôme Vétois

Abstract
. We obtain Liouville-type results for solutions to the CR Yamabe equation in ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , which extend a result obtained by Jerison and Lee for solutions in L 2 + 2 / n ​ ( ℍ n ) L^{2+2/n}\left(\mathbb{H}^{n}\right) . We obtain our results under either pointwise conditions or integral conditions at infinity. In particular, our results hold for all bounded solutions when n = 2 n=2 and solutions satisfying a pointwise decay assumption when n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . The proofs rely on integral estimates combined with a suitable divergence formula.



1.
Introduction and main result

We consider the CR Yamabe equation

4\Delta_{b}u=n^{2}u^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{H}^{n},

(1.1)

where
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is the Heisenberg group and
\Delta_{b}u:=-\Real u_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
is the Heisenberg sub-Laplacian (definitions are recalled in Section
2
). Jerison and Lee
[
JL2
]
obtained that the only solutions
u\in L^{2+2/n}\left(\mathbb{H}^{n}\right)
to (
1.1
) are the functions

u\left(z,t\right)=\frac{\left(4\Imag\lambda-\left|\mu\right|^{2}\right)^{n/2}}{\left|t+\sqrt{-1}\left|z\right|^{2}+\left<\mu,z\right>+\lambda\right|^{n}}\quad\forall\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

(1.2)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
are such that
\left|\mu\right|^{2}<4\Imag\lambda
. Moreover, these functions correspond to the extremal functions for the Sobolev inequality in the Heisenberg group. Garofalo and Vassilev
[
GV
]
extended Jerison and Lee’s result to groups of Heisenberg-type and partially symmetric solutions, namely solutions which are invariant with respect to the action of the orthogonal group in the horizontal layer of the Lie algebra. Due to the lack of invariance of the sub-Laplacian with respect to the standard reflections about hyperplanes, works in the literature have only been able to use moving plane arguments in restricted cases in this setting (see for example Birindelli and Prajapat
[
BP
]
and Garofalo and Vassilev
[
GV
]
). This is in contrast with the situation for the Yamabe equation in the Euclidean space (see Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
CGS
]
and Chen and Li
[
CLi
]
).

In this article, we extend Jerison and Lee’s result to solutions satisfying either pointwise conditions or integral conditions at infinity. Our first result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
u
be a positive solution to (
1.1
) such that

u\left(z,t\right)\leq C\left(\left|z\right|^{2}+\left|t\right|\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}\quad\forall\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}

(1.3)

for some constant
C>0
. Then
u
is of the form (
1.2
).

Remark 1.2
.

Catino, Li, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CLMR
]
recently posted an article on arXiv, where they obtained a result in the same direction as Theorem
1.1
. In the case where
n=1
, their result provides the remarkable full classification of solutions to (
1.1
). In the case where
n\geq 2
, they obtain the result under the assumption that

u\left(z,t\right)\leq C\left(\left|z\right|^{2}+\left|t\right|\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\quad\forall\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}

for some constant
C>0
. In a first version of our paper, independently from
[
CLMR
]
, we obtained Theorem
1.1
under the assumption that

u\left(z,t\right)\leq C\left(\left|z\right|^{2}+\left|t\right|\right)^{-p}\quad\forall\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}

for some constant
C>0
and
p>\frac{n\left(n-2\right)}{2\left(n-1\right)}
. This can be improved to
p\geq\frac{n-2}{2}
. We point out that to obtain the limit case where
p=\frac{n-2}{2}
, we now use an argument from
[
CLMR
]
in Lemma
2.2
.

The classification of bounded solutions for

, Chen and Lin

]
, Li and Zhu

Our second result (of which Theorem
1.1
is in fact a corollary) is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
u
be a positive solution to (
1.1
) such that

\int_{B_{R}\left(0\right)}u^{q}\leq CR^{2}\quad\forall R>1

(1.4)

for some constants
C>0
and
q\in\left(\frac{2n+1}{n},\frac{2n+2}{n}\right]
. Then
u
is of the form (
1.2
).

It is interesting to compare (
1.4
) with the estimate

\int_{B_{R}\left(0\right)}u^{q}\leq CR^{2n+2-nq}\quad\forall R>1,

(1.5)

which holds for all
q\in\left[0,\frac{n+2}{n}\right]
and all positive solutions to (
1.1
), without any further assumptions (see Lemma
2.5
). Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.4
by showing that the estimate (
1.4
) with
q=\frac{2n+2}{n}
can be obtained by putting together the pointwise estimate (
1.3
) with the integral estimate (
1.5
). Theorem
1.4
is more general than Theorem
1.1
: it applies to smaller values of
q
and solutions which do not satisfy a pointwise decay condition.

We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
in Section
2
. The proofs use an approach based on integral estimates. An approach of this type has recently been used by Ma and Ou
[
MO
]
for equations of type (1.1) with subcritical exponents in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CMR
]
and Ou
[
Ou
]
(see also Vétois
[
Vet
]
) for the critical
p
-Laplace equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The starting point of this method is to obtain a suitable divergence formula (see Lemma
2.1
). In our case, this formula is derived from and extends a celebrated formula introduced and used by Jerison and Lee
[
JL2
]
to characterize the extremal functions of Sobolev inequalities in the Heisenberg group and the CR Yamabe problem. Our extended version of this formula is comparable with the remarkable Obata-type formula found by Ou
[
Ou
]
*Proposition 2.3 in the context of the critical
p
–Laplace equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see also
[
Ob
]
for the original formula discovered by Obata in his work on the conformal transformations of the sphere). Once our divergence formula is established, we multiply it by some cutoff functions, integrate in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and estimate some remainder terms in a series of technical lemmas (see Lemmas
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
and
2.5
). By passing to the limit as the cutoff functions tend to the constant function equal to 1, we then obtain that both the torsion and Einstein curvature tensors of the contact form associated with the function
u
(i.e.
u^{2/n}\Theta
, where
\Theta
is the standard contact form on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
) vanish everywhere in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, from which, as in
[
JL2
]
, we infer that
u
must be of the form (
1.2
).

2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4

This section is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
. Given a smooth function
u
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we denote
u_{0}=\partial_{t}u
and for each
\alpha\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}

u_{\alpha}=u_{,\alpha}=Z_{\alpha}\left(u\right):=\partial_{z_{\alpha}}u+\sqrt{-1}\overline{z_{\alpha}}u_{0}

and

u_{\overline{\alpha}}=u_{,\overline{\alpha}}=Z_{\overline{\alpha}}\left(u\right):=\partial_{\overline{z_{\alpha}}}u-\sqrt{-1}z_{\alpha}u_{0}.

The vector fields
\left\{Z_{\alpha},Z_{\bar{\alpha}},\partial_{t}\right\}
form a basis of left-invariant vector fields for the complex Lie algebra of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and they satisfy
\left[Z_{\alpha},Z_{\bar{\alpha}}\right]=-2\sqrt{-1}\partial_{t}
. We recall that in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
equipped with its standard contact form, the Levi form is given by
2\delta_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
, and the sub-Laplacian can be written as

\Delta_{b}u=-\Real u_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}:=-\Real\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}Z_{\overline{\alpha}}\left(Z_{\alpha}\left(u\right)\right).

We will write
\partial u
to indicate the horizontal gradient of
u
and

B_{R}\left(0\right)=\left\{\left(z,t\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\,\left(\left|z\right|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{1/4}<R\right\}

for the Korányi ball. Throughout this section, we also denote

\left|V_{\alpha}\right|^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}V_{\alpha}\overline{V_{\alpha}},\quad\left|S_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}S_{\alpha\beta}\overline{S_{\alpha\beta}}\quad\text{and}\quad\big|T_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\big|^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}T_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\overline{T_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}}

for all tensors
V_{\alpha}
,
S_{\alpha\beta}
and
T_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}
.

We let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
). We define

f:=\frac{1}{n}\ln u-\ln 2.

(2.1)

It is easy to see that (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\Delta_{b}f=n\left|\partial f\right|^{2}+n\,e^{2f}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{H}^{n}.

(2.2)

For each
\alpha,\beta\in\left\{1,\dotsc,n\right\}
, we define

\displaystyle D_{\alpha\beta}:=f_{\alpha\beta}-2f_{\alpha}f_{\beta},

\displaystyle E_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}:=f_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{n}f_{\gamma\overline{\gamma}}\delta_{\alpha\overline{\beta}},

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}:=D_{\alpha\beta}f_{\overline{\beta}},

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}:=E_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}f_{\beta}\text{ and}

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}:=\sqrt{-1}f_{0\alpha}+gf_{\alpha},

Up to some constant factors,

and

\mathbb{H}^{n}

) can be rewritten as

f_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}+ng=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{H}^{n}.

(2.3)

m=0

[

]

with

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m\in\left[0,1\right]

and

\displaystyle\quad-\big|D_{\alpha}+E_{\alpha}\big|^{2}+c_{1}\left|G_{\alpha}+c_{2}D_{\alpha}+c_{3}E_{\alpha}\right|^{2}+c_{4}\left|D_{\alpha}+c_{5}E_{\alpha}\right|^{2}+c_{6}\left|E_{\alpha}\right|^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle:=3\left|g\right|^{-2}\left(\left|g\right|^{2}-mf_{0}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{3}\left(1-\frac{2m\sqrt{-1}f_{0}\sqrt{\left|g\right|^{2}-f_{0}^{2}}}{\left|g\right|^{2}-mf_{0}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\frac{2m\sqrt{-1}f_{0}\sqrt{\left|g\right|^{2}-f_{0}^{2}}}{\left|g\right|^{2}-mf_{0}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{3}\left(5-3m+\frac{4m\left(1-m\right)f_{0}^{2}}{\left|g\right|^{2}-mf_{0}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{5}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\left(4-3m\right)\left|g\right|^{2}-m\left(2+m\right)f_{0}^{2}+2m^{2}\left|g\right|^{-2}f_{0}^{4}}{\left(5-3m\right)\left|g\right|^{2}-m\left(1+m\right)f_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle c_{6}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\left(3-2m\right)\left|g\right|^{2}+m\left(5-6m\right)f_{0}^{2}}{\left(5-3m\right)\left|g\right|^{2}-m\left(1+m\right)f_{0}^{2}}.

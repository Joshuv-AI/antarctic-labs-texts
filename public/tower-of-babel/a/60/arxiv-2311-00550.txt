Thermal superpotential in designer gravity
David Choque, Raúl Rojas

Abstract
This study focuses on thermal superpotentials in asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetimes. We study the thermal superpotential and its use as a counterterm for the renormalization of the gravitational action in designer AdS theories with a self-interacting scalar field. Our findings, which are consistent with prior works, demonstrate that, for the thermal superpotential to be a suitable counterterm, it must have some specific form.

We focus on thermal (local) superpotentials, meaning that only in the asymptotic region do they relate to the scalar potential in the usual SUGRA way, but globally, the relationship is highly nontrivial and essentially depends on the existence of the event horizon. We do not intend to address the minimum energy theorem for this class of superpotentials. Instead, based on the proposal of

One important aspect concerning exact hairy black hole solutions is the study of the conserved charges. There exist various methods in the literature for such a task: the Hamiltonian method

Thus, in the context of the renormalization of the gravitational action and under the above-mentioned approach, we address in detail the study of the superpotential as a counterterm to regularize certain class of AdS theories in the presence of a minimally coupled self-interacting scalar field. The counterterm we shall be interested is expressed in terms of a “thermal” superpotential, which we introduce in a very similar way as in supergravity. It is worthy to mention that, in the regularization scheme in the context of the Hamilton-Jacobi approach

This paper is organized as follows: In Sec.

We also obtain the holographic Brown-York tensor in terms of the thermal superpotential and compute the conserved charges, i.e. the total energy, and we study the conditions under which the conformal symmetry of the CFT is preserved. This condition are compatible with the AdS symmetry and leads to a concrete boundary condition for the scalar field.
We end with Sec.

Thermal superpotential method

The gravitational action for Einstein-scalar theories

I=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V(\phi)\right]

Let us first construct

2.1
Variational principle

The goal in this section is to use the variational principle applied on (
16

a_{2}(A)

a_{3}(A)

\mathcal{W}(\phi)
(

\delta I^{E}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{h^{E}}\left[n^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\delta\phi-\frac{1}{\ell}\delta\mathcal{W}(\phi)\right]

(18)

The first term in (

is the normal unit to the boundary. By using (

h_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\left[-N(r)dt^{2}+S(r)d\Sigma_{k}^{2}\right]=\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}

\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}=-dt^{2}+\ell^{2}d\Sigma_{k}^{2}

\delta I^{E}\propto\left[(1+2a_{2}+A\dot{a}_{2})rA+(1+2a_{2})A\ddot{w}+2\dot{w}(1+a_{2}+\dot{a}_{2}A)+(\dot{a}_{3}A+3a_{3})A^{2}\right]\delta A+\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})

\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x=-\beta\sigma_{k}

\sigma_{k}
being the cross section area. We can fix the coefficient

(21)

With this result, the finite part of (

Using these partial results, the superpotential so far has the following relevant fall-off

\mathcal{W}(\phi)=-4+\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{A^{2}}\right)\phi^{2}+\left(\frac{w_{0}-w}{A^{3}}-\frac{2c_{1}\dot{w}}{A^{4}}\right)\phi^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{4})

2.2
Finiteness of the action

Now, we show that constant

in (

I^{E}_{\text{bulk}}+I^{E}_{GH}=-\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4}+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\kappa T}\left(-\frac{2r^{3}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{8k\ell^{2}+A^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}r-\frac{2A\dot{w}}{3\ell^{2}}+2\mu\right)+\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})

(24)

where
\sigma_{k}
is the area of the cross section and
\mathcal{A}
is the area of the event horizon. These two contributions are divergent in both
\sim r^{3}
and
\sim r
, as is well known. On the other hand, the proposed counterterm (
17

I^{E}_{\mathcal{W}}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\kappa T}\left(\frac{2r^{3}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{8k\ell^{2}+A^{2}-2c_{1}}{4\ell^{2}}r+\frac{w+A\dot{w}-w_{0}}{2\ell^{2}}-\mu\right)+\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})

(25)

I^{E}=I^{E}_{\text{bulk}}+I^{E}_{GH}+I^{E}_{\mathcal{W}(\phi)}=-\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4}+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\kappa T}\left(-\frac{c_{1}}{2\ell^{2}}r+\frac{w-w_{0}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{A\dot{w}}{3\ell^{2}}+\mu\right)

(26)

remains finite provided

[

(see Appendix

We can therefore conclude that the thermal superpotential that effectively regularizes the action by means of the counterterm (

\mathcal{W}(\phi)=-4-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+\left[\frac{w_{0}-w(A)}{A^{3}}\right]\phi^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{4})

2.3
Holographic stress tensor and asymptotic boundary condition

Let us now discuss some conformal properties of the dual holographic stress tensor,
\expectationvalue{\tau_{ab}^{dual}}
, and the asymptotic boundary condition of the scalar field. We consider the quasilocal Brown-York stress tensor, defined as
[
34
]

\tau_{ab}\equiv\frac{2}{\sqrt{-h}}\frac{\delta I}{\delta h^{ab}}

(28)

For the action under consideration, (
16

\tau_{ab}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\left[-K_{ab}+h_{ab}K+\ell\mathcal{G}_{ab}+\frac{1}{2\ell}h_{ab}\mathcal{W}(\phi)\right]

where
\mathcal{G}_{ab}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}h_{ab}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{ab}
is the Ricci tensor computed on the foliation

, given by the metric

The leading contribution to the components of the quasilocal stress tensor in the limit

where
\sigma_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}=d\Sigma_{k}^{2}

In order to obtain the energy of the system, the metric at the boundary must be able to be put in the form

The existence of the renormalization group is grounded in the AdS/CFT correspondence, which allows us to associate the radial coordinate
u
of the bulk with the energy scale. Therefore, it is natural to introduce a function
C(u)
, and in
[
44
]
, the c-theorem was formulated. In espace-times with event horizons, it is proposed that the ordinary superpotential be replaced by the thermal superpotential.

Likewise, building on the AdS/CFT conjecture, it becomes possible to construct a function

3.1
The

Let us obtain the c-function in terms of the superpotential function. A geometrical construction for the c-function was proposed in

4A{{}^{\prime\prime}}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}=0

\left[\ln{\frac{1}{(A')^{2}}}\right]^{\prime}\geq 0

\mathcal{C{{}^{\prime}}}=\mathcal{C}(u)

\left[\ln{\mathcal{C}(u)}\right]^{\prime}\geq 0

By comparing (

\mathcal{C}(u)=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{0}}{(A^{\prime})^{2}}

Now, in the lines of our previous section, we can work with the thermal superpotential in order to construct the
c
-function. Consider the following definitions which are compatible with equation (

\phi{{}^{\prime}}(u)=\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\phi}\,,\qquad A{{}^{\prime}}(u)=-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{W}(\phi)

The

-function has its origin in the holographic hypothesis. The formal definition is

where

is a scale energy. In string theory, the string coupling is described by the expectation value of dilaton,
\expectationvalue{e^{\phi}}

e^{\phi}
is idenfitied with the ’t Hooft coupling
e^{\phi}\equiv\lambda

of the metric (

From the definition (

whereas in the domain-wall coordinates given by (

These expressions for the
\beta

\beta(\phi)=-\frac{4}{\mathcal{W}(\phi)}\derivative{\mathcal{W(\phi)}}{\phi}\,e^{\phi}

4
Concrete examples

We would like to consider now two concrete examples where the method developed can be successfully applied. In the first example, we shall construct the thermal superpotential and we also shall study the RG-flow using this superpotential. In the second example, we first take the superpotential coming from the supergravity model and additionally we obtain the thermal superpotential being considered along our work. We emphasize on their differences by remarking that, in general, the black hole solutions coming from supergravity have a superpotential. Nonetheless, there is no a general proof that all black hole solutions can be obtained from a consistent truncation of a supergravity theory.

5
Discussion

In fake supergravity, it is a common procedure to reducing one of the second order differential equation into two ones
[
16
]
. In supergravity, this is consequence of the preservation of some number of supersymmetries. In either way, this is achieved by introducing the so-called superpotential. By following the same spirit of
[
17
,
18
]
, in Sec.
2
, we have reduced some of the second order differential equations of motion of the theory into two first order ones by introducing the thermal superpotential
\mathcal{W}(\phi)
. We have showed in detail that this superpotential can be effectively used to renormalize the Euclidean action in Einstein-scalar models with a self-interacting scalar field potential with mass
m^{2}=-2\ell^{-2}
in a way that is also compatible with the variational principle. For that, the asymptotic expansion of
\mathcal{W}
must be

\mathcal{W}(\phi)=-4-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}+\left[\frac{w_{0}-w(A)}{A^{3}}\right]\phi^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{4})

(84)

where
B(A)=dw(A)/dA
denotes the boundary condition of the scalar field, and
\phi(r)=A/r+B/r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})
, for
r
being the canonical radial coordinate.
Since the relevant fall-off of the thermal superpotential contains the information on the boundary condition for the scalar, the counterterm (
17
) containing the thermal superpotential completely replaces the scalar field boundary term used, for instance, in
[
42
,
50
]
.

In Sec.
3
, we obtained specific expressions for the function
\beta
in different coordinate systems (
51
), (
52
). When written in terms of the thermal superpotential, it takes a covariant form (
53
). Despite the strong similarity between the superpotential derived from SUGRA (
67
) and the thermal superpotential (
79
), the natural connection with the geometric construction of the function
\beta
only occurs with the thermal superpotential (
52
). It is imperative to emphasize that an exact relationship between the potential
V(\phi)
and its corresponding superpotential in the form of (
68
) cannot always be obtained.

In Sec.
4
we have applied this method to concrete gravity theories. Regarding the theory analyzed in Sec.
4.1
, it is worth to notice that, in
[
51
]
, it was previously obtained a superpotential in the flat sliced domain wall, in the case
k=1
for the cross section, and for the particular model
\alpha=0
in the dilaton potential (
4.1
). We were able to show that the superpotential remains unchanged for any
k
and
\alpha\neq 0
, for the case when there is a black hole horizon. We also obtained concrete expressions for the
\beta
and
c
-functions for this case.

Regarding the positive energy theorems
[
19
,
20
,
52
,
53
]
, gravitational theories involving a scalar field allow for positive mass if and only if the potential
V(\phi)
can be written in terms of the superpotential (
67
). However, there are black hole solutions (
4.1
) where the potential-superpotential relationship either does not exist or admits only a complex superpotential
[
48
]
. The thermal superpotential has the advantage of being constructed at the level of the equations of motion and, surprisingly, in the asymptotic region, the potential-superpotential relation (
10
) holds true, resembling the solutions that arise (
68
) and/or immerse in supergravity theories
[
12
]
. Concretely, consider the equation (
10
) in the asymptotic region, where

\frac{N(r)}{S(r)}\approx\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}+\cdots,\qquad\sqrt{S}\quantity(\frac{N}{S})^{\prime}\approx\frac{k}{r^{3}}+\cdots\qquad\frac{2k}{S(r)}\approx\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\cdots

With these expressions, is straightforward to see that

V=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{d\phi}\right)^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{W}^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})

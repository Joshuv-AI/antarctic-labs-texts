For our purpose, we recall some basic properties of the familiar gamma function
\Gamma
, and the digamma (or psi) function
\psi(x)=\Gamma^{\prime}(x)/\Gamma(x)
(

), which will be used extensively throughout the paper. The gamma function satisfies the reflection formula

\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}\quad(s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}).

(1.4)

see
[
13
, p. 253]
. Other important functional equations satisfied by the

\Gamma\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{(2k)!\sqrt{\pi}}{4^{k}k!};

(1.5)

see

, and the Gauss multiplication formula for

\Gamma(kx)=\frac{k^{kx-1/2}}{(2\pi)^{(k-1)/2}}\Gamma(x)\Gamma\left(x+\frac{1}{k}\right)\Gamma\left(x+\frac{2}{k}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(x+\frac{k-1}{k}\right);

(1.6)

see
[
13
, pg. 259]
. The beta function
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

B(s,t)=\int_{0}^{1}x^{s-1}(1-x)^{t-1}{\rm d}x=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(t)}{\Gamma(s+t)}\quad(\Re(s,t)>0).

(1.7)

The digamma function
\psi
and harmonic numbers
H_{n}
are related to

\psi(n+1)=-\gamma+H_{n}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0});

(1.8)

see
[

, where
\gamma=0.57721\cdots
is the Euler-Mascheroni

\psi
satisfies a reflection formula and a

\psi(s)-\psi(1-s)=-\pi\cot(\pi s)\quad(s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}),

(1.9)

\psi\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2\psi(2s)-\psi(s)-2\log 2\quad(s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}^{-});

\psi\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2H_{2k}-H_{k}+\psi(1/2),

The following lemma has a prominent role in the proofs of our main results given in the second section.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
x
be a non-zero real number. Then we have

\log\left[\left(\frac{1-x}{x}\right)^{m}+1\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\log\big[1-2x(1-x)(\varphi_{k}+1)\big]-m\log x

(1.11)

if
m\geq 2
is an even integer, and

\log\left[\left(\frac{1-x}{x}\right)^{m}+1\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\log\big[1-2x(1-x)(\varphi_{k}+1)\big]-m\log x

(1.12)

if
m\geq 1
is an odd integer. Thorough the paper the empty sum
\sum_{k=0}^{-1}(\cdot)
is taken to be zero. Here

\varphi_{k}=\cos\left(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{m}\right).

(1.13)

Proof.

Let
m
be a positive even integer. Then for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
we have

z^{m}+1=\prod_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(z-e^{\frac{\pi i(2k+1)}{m}}\right)\left(z-e^{-\frac{\pi i(2k+1)}{m}}\right).

Setting
z=\frac{1-x}{x}

(x\neq 0)
, and taking the logarithm of both sides, we get, after a little simplification,

\displaystyle\log\left(\left(\frac{1-x}{x}\right)^{m}+1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\log\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1-x}{x}-\cos\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{m}\bigg)^{2}+\sin^{2}\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{m}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\log\left(\frac{(1-x)^{2}-2x(1-x)\varphi_{k}+x^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\log\left[1-2x(1-x)(\varphi_{k}+1)\right]-m\log x.

Now if
m
is an odd positive integer, then we have

z^{m}+1=(z+1)\prod_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\left(z-e^{\frac{\pi i(2k+1)}{m}}\right)\left(z-e^{-\frac{\pi i(2k+1)}{m}}\right).

Now we set
z=\frac{1-x}{x}

(x\neq 0)
here and then we take the logarithm of both sides to get

\displaystyle\log\left(\left(\frac{1-x}{x}\right)^{m}+1\right)=-\log x

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\log\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1-x}{x}-\varphi_{k}\bigg)^{2}+\sin^{2}\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{m}\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\log x+\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\log\left(\frac{(1-x)^{2}-2x(1-x)\varphi_{k}+x^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\log\left[1-2x(1-x)(\varphi_{k}+1)\right]-m\log x

as required.
∎

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
q>1
be any real number. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left(x^{q-2}+1\right)\log\left(x^{mq}+1\right)}{x^{q}+1}{\rm d}x=\frac{m}{4q}\left(\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{2q-1}{2q}\right)-\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{q-1}{2q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{p}(\varphi_{k}+1)^{p}}{p}\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(p+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2p)!}

\displaystyle-\frac{m\pi}{q\sin\frac{\pi}{q}}\left(\psi\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-\psi(1)\right)

(2.1)

if
m\geq 2
is an even integer, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left(x^{q-2}+1\right)\log\left(x^{mq}+1\right)}{x^{q}+1}{\rm d}x=\frac{m}{4q}\left(\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{2q-1}{2q}\right)-\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{q-1}{2q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-3}{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{p}(\varphi_{k}+1)^{p}}{p}\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(p+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2p)!}

\displaystyle-\frac{m\pi}{q\sin\frac{\pi}{q}}\left(\psi\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-\psi(1)\right)

(2.2)

if
m\geq 1
is an odd integer. Here
\varphi_{k}
is as defined by (
1.13
) and the sum
\sum_{k=0}^{-1}(\cdot)
is taken to be vanish.

Proof.

Let
m\geq 2
be an even integer. Making the change of variable
x=\frac{1}{u}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(x^{mq}+1\right)}{x^{q}+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{q-2}\log\left(u^{mq}+1\right)}{u^{q}+1}{\rm d}u

\displaystyle-mq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{q-2}\log x}{u^{q}+1}{\rm d}u.

We can rewrite this as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left(x^{q-2}+1\right)\log\left(x^{mq}+1\right)}{x^{q}+1}{\rm d}x=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{q-2}\log\left(u^{mq}+1\right)}{u^{q}+1}{\rm d}u}_{A}

\displaystyle-mq\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{q-2}\log u}{u^{q}+1}{\rm d}u}_{B}+mq\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u^{q-2}\log u}{u^{q}+1}{\rm d}u}_{C}.

(2.3)

First we evaluate
A

\frac{1}{u^{q}+1}=t
or
u=\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\log\left(\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{m}+1\right){\rm d}t.

(2.4)

Now applying (
1.11
) gives

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\log\left(1-2t(1-t)(\varphi_{k}+1)\right){\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{q}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\log t{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{q}\underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[2(\varphi_{k}+1)]^{n}[t(1-t)]^{n}}{n}\right){\rm d}t}_{A_{1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{q}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}-1}\log t{\rm d}t}_{A_{2}}.

(2.5)

Since, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,1)
,
\sum_{\upsilon=1}^{n}\frac{[2(\varphi_{k}+1)]^{\upsilon}[t(1-t)]^{\upsilon}}{\upsilon}\leq H_{n}
, the dominated convergence theorem justifies interchanging the order of summation and integration. Thus, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{p}(\varphi_{k}+1)^{p}}{p}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p-\frac{1}{q}}t^{p+\frac{1}{q}-1}{\rm d}t.

(2.6)

From the gamma-beta functional relation (
1.7

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p-\frac{1}{q}}t^{p+\frac{1}{q}-1}{\rm d}t=\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(p+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2p)!}.

Using this in (
2.6

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{p}(\varphi_{k}+1)^{p}}{p}\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(p+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2p)!}.

(2.7)

Proceeding as we did before, after a simple calculation, we see that

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}t^{s}\bigg|_{s=1/q-1}(1-t)^{-1/q}{\rm d}t=\frac{d}{ds}B(s+1,1-1/q)\bigg|_{s=1/q-1},

[
12

B(.,.)
is the beta function. From the gamma-beta functional equation (

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\Gamma(s+1)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+2-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\bigg|_{s=\frac{1}{q}-1}=\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)(\psi(1/q)-\psi(1)).

By the reflection formula for the gamma function this becomes

\displaystyle A_{2}=\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{\pi}{q}}(\psi(1/q)-\psi(1)).

(2.8)

Combining (
2

2.7
) and (
2.8
) we arrive at

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{p}(\varphi_{k}+1)^{p}}{p}\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(p+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2p)!}

\displaystyle-\frac{m\pi}{q\sin\frac{\pi}{q}}(\psi(1/q)-\psi(1)).

(2.9)

Making the same change of variable
u=\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
again we find

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1/q}(1-t)^{1/q-1}\log t{\rm d}t-\frac{1}{q^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{1/q-1}(1-t)^{-1/q}\log t{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}(1-t)^{1/q-1}{\rm d}t\bigg|_{s=-\frac{1}{q}}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}(1-t)^{-1/q}{\rm d}t\bigg|_{s=\frac{1}{q}-1}.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\Gamma(s+1)\Gamma(1/q)}{\Gamma(s+1+1/q)}\bigg|_{s=-1/q}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q^{2}}\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\Gamma(s+1)\Gamma(1-1/q)}{\Gamma(s+2-1/q)}\bigg|_{s=1/q-1}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1/q)\Gamma(1-1/q)}{q^{2}}\left[\psi(1-1/q)-\psi(1/q)\right].

(2.10)

x^{3}+1=(x+1)(1-x+x^{2})
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(x^{3}+1\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}2+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(1-x+x^{2}\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x.

(4.31)

On the other hand, expanding the functions in the integrand into their power series it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(1-x+x^{2}\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k+1}(1-x)^{k-1}}{k+1}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}x^{k}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}x^{n+1}(1-x)^{k+1}}{k+1}{\rm d}x.

Inverting the order of integration and summation we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(1-x+x^{2}\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k+1}(1-x)^{k+1}}{k+1}.\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}x^{k}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}x^{n+1}(1-x)^{k+1}}{k+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{n+1}(1-x)^{k+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}B(n+2,k+2){\rm d}x.

Using the gamma-beta functional equation this gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(1-x+x^{2}\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k+1}\frac{(n+1)!(k+1)!}{(n+k+3)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(k+1)(n+k+3)\binom{n+k+2}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(n+k+2)\binom{n+k+1}{k}}.

Simplifying this gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(1-x+x^{2}\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(k-1)\binom{n+k}{k}}.

(4.32)

Substituting (
4.32
) in (
4.31
) the proof of (
4.30
) completes. Now let us prove (
4.29
). If we make the substitution
x=\frac{1}{u}
, and use
x^{3}+1=(x+1)(1-x+x^{2})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(x^{3}+1\right)}{x+1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{2}2}{2}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\log\left(t^{2}-t+1\right)-2\log t}{t^{2}+t}{\rm d}t.

Mathematica is able to evaluate the integral on the right-hand side and gives

\frac{1}{2}Li_{2}\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)+\frac{\log^{2}3}{4}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{36},

which completes the proof of (
4.29
).
∎

Example 18
.

We have

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(x^{3}+1\right)}{x^{2}+1}{\rm d}x=-\frac{5}{3}G+\frac{\pi}{8}\log 2+\frac{\pi}{3}\log\left(\sqrt{3}+2\right)

Proof.

Making the substitution
x=\frac{1}{t}
, one easily gets

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log\left(x^{3}+1\right)}{x^{2}+1}{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\log\left(t^{3}+1\right)}{t^{2}+1}{\rm d}t-\frac{3}{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\log x}{x^{2}+1}{\rm d}x

(4.33)

Mathematica
gives us

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\log\left(x^{3}+1\right)}{x^{2}+1}{\rm d}x=-\frac{G}{3}+\frac{1}{4}\pi\log(2)+\frac{1}{3}(2\pi)\log\left(\sqrt{3}+2\right)

(4.34)

and

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\log(x)}{x^{2}+1}{\rm d}x=G.

(4.35)

Combining the equations (
4.33
)- (
4.35
) the conclusion immediately follows.
∎

Remark 5.4

It is important to note that using the pochhammer symbol

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(k+\frac{1}{q}\right)\Gamma\left(k+1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}{(2k)!}=\frac{\pi}{\sin\frac{\pi}{q}}\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{q},1-\frac{1}{q};\frac{1}{2}:x\right){\rm d}x}{x}

Remark 5.5

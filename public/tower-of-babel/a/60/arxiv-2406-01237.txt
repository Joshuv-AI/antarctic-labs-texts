Characterisation of distal actions of automorphisms on the space of one-parameter subgroups of Lie groups
Debamita Chatterjee, Riddhi Shah

Abstract
. For a connected Lie group G G and an automorphism T T of G G , we consider the action of T T on Sub G , the compact space of closed subgroups of G G endowed with the Chabauty topology. We study the action of T T on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} , the closure in Sub G of the set of closed one-parameter subgroups of G G . We relate the distality of the T T -action on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} with that of the T T -action on G G and characterise the same in terms of compactness of the closed subgroup generated by T T in Aut ( G ) (G) when T T acts distally on the maximal central torus and G G is not a vector group. We extend these results to the action of a subgroup of Aut ( G ) (G) , and equate the distal action of any closed subgroup ℋ {\mathcal{H}} on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} with that of every element in ℋ {\mathcal{H}} . Moreover, we show that a connected Lie group G G acts distally on Sub G p {}^{p}_{G} by conjugation if and only if G G is either compact or it is isomorphic to a direct product of a compact group and a vector group. Some of our results extend those of Shah and Yadav.

2.
Properties and structure of

In this section, we discuss some basic properties of the Chabauty topology on the space

.

Let

For a compact set

U_{1}(K)=\{H\in{\rm Sub}_{L}\mid H\cap K=\emptyset\}

\{U_{1}(K)\mid K\mbox{ is compact}\}\cup\,\{U_{2}(U)\mid U\mbox{ is open}\}

{\rm Sub}_{L}

{\rm Sub}_{L}
is
compact and Hausdorff, and if

{\rm Sub}_{L}
is metrisable
(cf.
[
6
]

{\rm Sub}_{L}
is metrisable if
L
is a connected
Lie group. For a closed subgroup
H
of

, it is easy to see that
{\rm Sub}_{H}
carries
the subspace topology of
{\rm Sub}_{L}
. The following lemma is known (see e.g. Proposition E.1.2 in
[

]
)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a connected Lie group. A sequence

\{H_{n}\}\subset{\rm Sub}_{G}
converges to
H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}

if and only if the following hold:

{\rm(I)}

For
g\in G
, if there exists a subsequence
\{H_{n_{k}}\}
of

\{H_{n}\}
with
h_{k}\in H_{n_{k}}
,

, such that
h_{k}\to g
in

, then

.

{\rm(II)}

For every

, there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

h_{n}\in H_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
h_{n}\to h
.

The following lemma is a consequence of the well-known result that the set of discrete subgroups of a connected Lie group

Lemma 2.2

Let

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be the set of closed connected subgroups of
G
. We have
\overline{\mathfrak{C}}={\rm Sub}^{co}_{G}
.
By Proposition E.1.5 in
[
6
]
(see also Proposition 3.4(iii) in
[
8
]
, or also Corollary of Lemma 1.2 in

[
45
]
) the set
\mathfrak{D}
of discrete subgroups of
G
is open in
{\rm Sub}_{G}
and so
\{\{e\}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}\cap{\rm Sub}^{co}_{G}=\mathfrak{D}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{C}}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{D}\cap\mathfrak{C}}=\{\{e\}\}
. Hence the assertions hold.
∎

.

Note that the action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
is continuous for connected Lie groups
G
(cf.
[
42
]
, Lemma 2.4).
In particular, if
T
generates a relatively compact group in
{\rm Aut}(G)
, then it acts distally on
G

as well as on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
(see e.g.
[
31
]
, Lemma 4.2 for the latter statement).

A (continuous real) one-parameter subgroup in
G
is the image of a continuous
homomorphism from
\mathbb{R}
to
G
. Any one-parameter subgroup is
of the form
\{\exp tX\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
for some
X
in the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
G
. Moreover, if
H
is a one-parameter
subgroup in
G
, then so is
T(H)
for any
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Let

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G):=\{A\mid A\mbox{ is a closed one-parameter subgroup of }G\}.

Now
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is the closure (in
{\rm Sub}_{G}
) of the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
, i.e.
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}:=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)}
, which is compact and invariant under the action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
.

Note that if any one-parameter subgroup in a connected Lie group is not closed, then its closure is compact
and isomorphic to an
n
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq 2
.
If it is closed and bounded (compact), then it is isomorphic to

\mathbb{S}^{1}
. If it is unbounded (not relatively compact), then it is closed and isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
.

For
\{x_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, which may be a continuous one-parameter set or a one-parameter subgroup, we may just write

\{x_{t}\}
for brevity, i.e. we may omit the parameter set if there is no ambiguity.

Now we prove some elementary results about the structure of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. The following lemma shows that if
G
contains
any non-trivial compact connected abelian subgroup of
dimension at least 2, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
is not closed in
{\rm Sub}_{G}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then the following hold:

(1)

All compact connected abelian subgroups of
G
belong to
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.

(2)

If
\{x_{t}\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)\subset{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
and if
K
is a compact connected abelian subgroup of
G

which is centralised by each
x_{t}
.
Then
\{x_{t}\}K\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be a compact connected abelian subgroup of
G
. Then the set
F
of all finite order elements in
K
is
dense in
K
and each such element of
F
is contained in a closed one-parameter subgroup of
K
.
Since
K
is monothetic (i.e. it has a dense cyclic subgroup), we get that
K
is a limit of a sequence of
compact one-parameter subgroups of
K
in
{\rm Sub}_{K}
. Therefore,
K\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{K}\subset{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. Thus (1) holds.

Let
\{x_{t}\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)\subset{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
be such that each
x_{t}
centralises
K
. If
\{x_{t}\}
is compact, then

\{x_{t}\}K
is a compact connected abelian subgroup of
G
and hence
\{x_{t}\}K\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
from (1).
Now suppose
\{x_{t}\}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
k\in K
be such that it generates a
dense cyclic subgroup in
K
. Since
K
is exponential, there exists a one-parameter subgroup

\{k_{t}\}
, which is dense in
K
and
k=k_{1}
. Let

A_{n}=\{a_{t}(n)=x_{t/n}k_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then each
A_{n}
is a closed one-parameter subgroup
in
G
, and passing to a subsequence if necessary, we get that
A_{n}\to A
for some subgroup
A\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
Note that
\{x_{t}\}\times K
is a closed subgroup of
G
and it contains each
A_{n}
. Therefore,

A\subset\{x_{t}\}\times K
. Let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
be fixed. Since
a_{t}(n)=x_{t/n}k_{t}\to k_{t}
as
n\to\infty
, we get
that
k_{t}\in A
, and hence
K\subset A
. Let
b_{n}=a_{tn}(n)=x_{t}k_{tn}\in A_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Passing to a further subsequence of
\{b_{n}\}
if necessary,
we get that
b_{n}\to x_{t}h
for some
h\in K
, and hence,
x_{t}\in A
, as
K\subset A
.
Now
\{x_{t}\}\subset A
. Hence,
A=\{x_{t}\}\times K
and (2) holds.
∎

The following lemma relates the set
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
and a compact subset of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
which is invariant
under the action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
K
be a compact connected central subgroup of

G
and let
\pi:G\to G/K
be the natural projection. Then the following hold:

(1)

\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}\}
is a closed
(compact) subset of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
and
\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
is a closed
(compact) subset of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, and they are
T
-invariant if
T(K)=K
, where
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.

(2)

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G/K)=\{\pi(A)\mid A\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)\}
.

(3)

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}=\{\pi(H)\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
.

(4)

{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
is homeomorphic to
\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
, and the latter is the same set as
\{H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\mid HK=KH=H\}
.

Proof.

Let
K
and
\pi
be as above. Note that
K\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
by Lemma
2.3
. If
G=K
, then it is easy to see that

(1-4)
are trivially satisfied.

Now suppose that
G\neq K
. Since
K
is compact and central in
G
,
HK
is a closed subgroup of
G
for any
closed subgroup
H
of
G
. If
H_{n}K\to L
in
{\rm Sub}_{G}
, then since both
{\rm Sub}_{G}
and
K
are compact, it is easy to show that
for any limit point
H
of
\{H_{n}\}
,
L=HK
. Therefore,
\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}\}
is closed. Now if
H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, then there exists

\{A_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
, such that
A_{n}\to H
. By Lemma
2.3
(2),
A_{n}K\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. As
A_{n}K\to HK
and
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is closed,
we get that
HK\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. Now arguing as above, it is easy to show that
\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
is a closed subset of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
Moreover, if
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
with
T(K)=K
, then
T(HK)=T(H)K
for any
H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}
. Therefore, the sets mentioned
in (1) are invariant under the action of
{\rm Aut}(G)
if
T(K)=K
. Thus (1) holds.

If
A\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
, then
AK
is closed and
\pi(A)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G/K)
. Conversely, suppose
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G/K
) is nontrivial.
It is easy to see that
B_{1}:=\pi^{-1}(B)
is a closed connected nilpotent Lie subgroup of
G
. There exists
a neighbourhood
U
of
e
such that every element of
U
is contained in a one-parameter subgroup and
U\setminus K

is nonempty. Choose any
x\in U\setminus K
, there exists a one-parameter subgroup
\{x_{t}\}
with
x_{1}=x
. Then

\pi(\{x_{t}\})=B
and
B_{1}=\{x_{t}\}K
. As
K
is central in
G
we get that
B_{1}
is, in fact, abelian. If
\{x_{t}\}
is closed,
then it belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
and
\pi(\{x_{t}\})=B
. Now suppose it is not closed, then its closure is compact, and hence

B_{1}
is compact. This implies that the set of torsion elements is dense in
B_{1}
. Since
U\setminus K
is open, we can choose

y\in B_{1}\cap(U\setminus K)
such that
y^{n}=e
, for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
. Let
\{y_{t}\}
be a one-parameter subgroup in
B_{1}

such that
y_{1}=y
. Then
\pi(\{y_{t}\})=B
. Here,
\{y_{t}\}
is closed as
y_{1}^{n}=e
. Therefore,

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G/K)=\{\pi(A)\mid A\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)\}
, i.e. (2) holds.

Let
\pi^{\prime}:{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\to{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
be the map induced by
\pi
.
Then
\pi^{\prime}
is a continuous closed map. From (2), we get that
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G/K)}=\pi^{\prime}(\overline{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)})=\pi^{\prime}({\rm Sub}^{p}_{G})=\{\pi(H)\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
. Thus (3) holds.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}(K):=\{HK\mid H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\}
. By (1),
{\mathcal{S}}(K)
is closed in
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, and hence it is compact.
Observe that
{\mathcal{S}}(K)=\{H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\mid HK=KH=H\}
. By (3),
\pi^{\prime}({\mathcal{S}}(K))=\pi^{\prime}({\rm Sub}^{p}_{G})={\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
and it is
obvious that
\pi^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{S}}(K)}
is continuous and bijective, and hence it is a homeomorphism. Thus (4) holds.
∎

3.
Some properties of automorphisms and their actions on
G
and
{\mathrm{Sub}}^{p}_{G}

In this section, we discuss some properties and actions of automorphisms of a connected Lie group

G
on
G
as well as
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
and discuss some preliminary results which are useful.

For convenience, we introduce a class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
of Lie groups as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

A connected Lie group
G
is said to be in class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
if
G
has no compact central subgroup
of positive dimension
(
i.e. if
G
has no non-trivial compact connected central subgroup
)
.

The notation is inspired by the fact that the class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
is a subset of the class
\mathcal{C}

defined by Dani and McCrudden in
[
13
]
. The class
\mathcal{C}
consists of connected Lie groups
which admit a (real) linear representation with discrete kernel. Proposition 2.5 of
[
13
]

shows that
G
is in class
\mathcal{C}
if and only if for the radical
R
of
G
, the
intersection of
\overline{[R,R]}
with the center of
G
has no non-trivial compact subgroup.
Therefore, the class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
is a subset of the class
\mathcal{C}
. Note that any connected Lie group

G
admits a maximal compact connected central subgroup
M
, and
G/M
has no
non-trivial compact connected central subgroup and hence it belongs to class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. We may recall
a result of Dani in
[
12
]
that if
G
belongs to class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
, then
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic, i.e. it is a(n open) subgroup of finite index in an algebraic subgroup of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and in particular,
it has finitely many connected components.

It is known that
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
acts distally on
G
if and only if
{\rm d\,}T
acts distally on the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
G
,
and the latter statement is equivalent to the statement that all the eigenvalues of
{\rm d\,}T
have absolute value 1
(cf.
[
2
]
). It is also known that if
G
admits an automorphism
T
such that
{\rm d\,}T
has no
eigenvalue of absolute value 1 (equivalently, if
T
is expansive; see
[
38
]
, and also
[
7
]
), then
G
must
be nilpotent (this is well-known, see e.g.
[
38
]
). We will need the following lemma which is slightly stronger.
The lemma may be known, but we will give a short proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a connected non-nilpotent Lie group and let
T
be an automorphism of
G
. Then
at least one eigenvalue of
{\rm d\,}T
is a root of unity.

Proof.

Let
M
be the largest compact connected central subgroup of
G
.
Then
G/M
is in class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. As
G
is not nilpotent,
G/M
is also not nilpotent.
Since
M
is characteristic in
G
, we may replace
G
by
G/M
and assume that
G\in{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
.
If
T
has finite order, then
({\rm d\,}T)^{n}={\rm Id}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and the assertion follows in this case.
Now suppose
T
does not have finite order.

Since
G\in{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
,
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic (cf.
[
12
]
), and
hence it has finitely many connected components. Now we may replace
T
by its suitable
power and assume that
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
, the connected component of the identity
in
{\rm Aut}(G)
, and that
T
is contained in a non-trivial one-parameter subgroup (say)
\{T_{t}\}

of
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
as
T=T_{1}
. We will show that 1 is an eigenvalue of
{\rm d\,}T
.

Suppose
G
is solvable. Let
H=\{T_{t}\}\ltimes G
. Then
H
is solvable
and the nilradical of
H
, being contained in
G
, is the same as the nilradical
N
of
G
.
Therefore,
H/N
is abelian. Thus
T
acts trivially on
G/N
. As
G
is not nilpotent,
G\neq N
,
and hence 1 is an eigenvalue
{\rm d\,}T
.

Now suppose
G
is not solvable. Let
R
be the radical of
G
. Then
R
is characteristic in
G
, and let

\overline{T}_{t}
be the automorphisms of
G/R
induced by
T_{t}
for each
t
. It is enough to prove that 1 is an
eigenvalue of
{\rm d\,}\overline{T}
. Hence we may replace
G
by
G/R
and
\{T_{t}\}
by
\{\overline{T}_{t}\}

and assume that
G
is semisimple. Now
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
consists of inner automorphisms of
G
and

T_{t}={\rm inn}(x_{t})
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for some one-parameter subgroup
\{x_{t}\}\subset G
.
If
x_{1}=e
, then
T=T_{1}={\rm Id}
. Now suppose
x_{1}\neq e
. Then
\{x_{t}\}
is non-trivial.
Let
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
be such that
\exp tX=x_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. As
T(x_{t})=x_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
we have that
{\rm d\,}T(X)=X
and 1 is an eigenvalue of
{\rm d\,}T
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The proof above also shows that for a connected solvable Lie group
G
with
the nilradical
N
, for any
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
,
\overline{T}
has finite order, where
\overline{T}
is the automorphism of

G/N
induced by
T
. In particular, all the eigenvalues of
{\rm d\,}\overline{T}
on the Lie algebra of
G/N

are roots of unity.

For a connected Lie group
G
and
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, let
C(T)
denote the contraction group of
T

and it is defined as

C(T):=\{x\in G\mid T^{n}(x)\to e\mbox{ as }n\to\infty\}.

Note that
C(T)
is a connected nilpotent subgroup of
G,
and if it is closed in
G
, then it is simply
connected (see
[
43
]
, see also
[
21
]
). Both
C(T)
and
C(T^{-1})
are trivial
if and only if
T
acts distally on
G
(cf.
[
35
]
, Theorem 4.1). Note also that
C(T)
is
trivial if and only if all the eigenvalues of
{\rm d\,}T
have absolute value greater than or equal to 1
(cf.
[
35
,
2
]
).

We will need the following lemma which can be proven easily by using Theorem 1.1 of
[
14
]
,
Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.7 of
[
21
]
; we will give a sketch of proof. Note that the largest compact
connected central subgroup of
G
is characteristic in
G
. The lemma generalises a part of the
statement in Proposition 4.3 of
[
35
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
acts distally on the largest compact connected central subgroup of
G
.

(2)

The contraction groups
C(T)
and
C(T^{-1})
are closed, simply connected and nilpotent subgroups of
G
.

(3)

T
acts distally on every compact
T
-invariant subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

Let
M
be the largest compact connected central subgroup of
G
. If
G=M
, then it is easy to see that

(1-3)
are equivalent. Now suppose
G\neq M
. Let
\pi:G\to G/M
be
the natural projection and let
T^{\prime}\in{\rm Aut}(G/M)
be the automorphism induced by
T
on
G/M
.
As
G/M\in{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
, using Theorem 1.1 of
[
14
]
,
one can show that
C(T^{\prime})
is closed (see the proof of Proposition 4.3 of
[
35
]
for more details). Hence

\pi^{-1}(C(T^{\prime}))=C_{M}(T):=\{x\in G\mid T^{n}(x)M\to M\mbox{ in }G/M\}
is closed.
Suppose (1) holds, i.e.
T
acts distally on
M
. Then
C(T)\cap M=C(T|_{M})=\{e\}
.
As
C_{M}(T)
is closed, by Corollary 2.7 of
[
21
]
,
C(T)
is closed. Similarly,
C(T^{-1})
is
closed. The rest of the assertion in (2) follows from Corollary 2.4 in
[
43
]
.
This proves
(1)\implies(2)
.

Now suppose (2) holds, i.e.
C(T)
is closed. Let
K
be any compact
T
-invariant subgroup of
G
.
Then
C(T|_{K})=C(T)\cap K
is a compact subgroup of
C(T)
. Hence
C(T|_{K})
is trivial
(cf.
[
21
]
, Corollary 2.5). Similarly,
C((T|_{K})^{-1})
is trivial and
T
acts
distally on
K
(cf.
[
26
]
). Thus (3) holds.
(3)\implies(1)
is obvious.
∎

We note the following useful proposition which will enable us to work on a quotient Lie group which belongs to class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Let
K
be a
T
-invariant compact connected
central subgroup of
G
and let
\overline{T}
denote the automorphism of
G/K
induced by
T
.
If
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, then
\overline{T}
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
.

Proof.

Let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
and let
\pi:G\to G/K
be the natural projection. Here,

\overline{T}(\pi(H))=\pi(T(H))
for any
H\in{\rm Sub}_{G}
, and
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
is
\overline{T}
invariant.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}(K)=\{H\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}\mid HK=H\}
. It is a compact
T
-invariant subset of
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
and it is
homeomorphic to
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
under the map induced by
\pi
on
{\mathcal{S}}(K)
(cf. Lemma
2.4
(4)).
Suppose
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. Then the
T
-action on
{\mathcal{S}}(K)
is distal. As
\pi\circ T=\overline{T}\circ\pi
,
we get that
\overline{T}
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G/K}
.
∎

We will also need the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
and let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.
Then the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
consisting of all closed one-parameter subgroups of
G
is closed in
{\rm Sub}_{G}

and is the same as
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. Moreover,
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
if and only if
{\rm d\,}T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

In a simply connected nilpotent Lie group, all one-parameter subgroups are closed
and there is a one-to-one correspondence between non-trivial (closed) one-parameter subgroups of
G

and one-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The correspondence is via the exponential map
\exp:{\mathfrak{g}}\to G
with

\log
as its inverse. Namely; for
\{x_{t}\}
,
x_{t}=\exp tX
, for some
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\log x_{t}=tX
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Also the exponential map induces a homeomorphism from
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)
.
As
\exp\circ\,{\rm d\,}T=T\circ\exp
, it follows that
T
acts distally on
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(G)={\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
if and
only if
{\rm d\,}T
acts distally on
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}({\mathfrak{g}})={\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
∎

4.
Proofs of the main results

In this section, we prove the main results stated in the introduction.
Additionally, we get some corollaries which are derived from the main results. We give some examples
of Lie groups
G
with a nontrivial central torus on which all automorphisms act distally,
which is stronger than one of the conditions in Theorems
1.2
,
1.4
and
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

(1)
and
(2)
for nilpotent groups.

Let
G

be a (non-trivial) connected Lie group without any non-trivial compact connected central subgroup,
i.e.
G
belongs to class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. Let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.

(1):
Suppose
G
is abelian. Then it is a vector group isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that
{\mathcal{D}}=\{r\,{\rm Id}\mid r\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}\}
, where
{\rm Id}
is the identity matrix in
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
.
If
d=1
, then
{\rm GL}(1,{\mathbb{R}})
is abelian, and the assertion follows trivially. Now suppose
d\geq 2
. Then
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{RP}^{d-1}\sqcup\{\{e\}\}
and
T\in{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Both
\mathbb{RP}^{d-1}
and
\{\{e\}\}
are compact and
T
invariant.
Hence
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
if and only if
T
acts distally on
\mathbb{RP}^{d-1}
.
One can define an action of
T
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, the
boundary of the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, as
\overline{T}(x)=T(x)/\|T(x)\|
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
usual norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Here,
\overline{T}
is a homeomorphism and
\overline{T}(-x)=-\overline{T}(x)
.
As
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d-1}
is a quotient space of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
the action of
T
on
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d-1}
is the same as the action of
\overline{T}
on

{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}/\sim
, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation which identifies

x
with
-x
for each
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. It is easy to see that
T
acts distally
on
{\mathbb{RP}}^{d-1}
if and only if
\overline{T}
acts distally on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
The latter statement is equivalent to the following:
T\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{D}}
, where
{\mathcal{K}}
is a
compact subgroup of
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
and
{\mathcal{D}}
is the center of
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})

(this follows from Theorem 1 in
[
39
]
, see also
[
40
]
).

Conversely, for
G={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
T\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{D}}
, with a compact subgroup
{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
and the center

{\mathcal{D}}
of
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
, the action of
T
on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is same as that of
S
on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
for some
S\in{\mathcal{K}}

since
{\mathcal{D}}
acts trivially on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. As the compact subgroups of
{\rm Aut}(G)
act distally on any closed
invariant subspace of
{\rm Sub}_{G}
(
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
in this case), we have that
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(1).

(2):
Suppose
G
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
and it is non-abelian and nilpotent. Then

G
is simply connected as
G
has no compact central subgroup of positive dimension.
Suppose
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. We want to show
that
T
is contained in a compact subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
. Since
G
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
,

{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic as a subgroup of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{g}})
(cf.
[
12
]
).

As
G
is simply connected and nilpotent, by Lemma
3.6
,
{\rm d\,}T
acts
distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then from (1) above, we have that
{\rm d\,}T\in{\mathcal{K}}\{r\,{\rm Id}\mid r\neq 0\}

for some compact subgroup
{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\rm Id}
is the identity map on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Here,
{\rm d\,}T=\psi\,\circ\,r\,{\rm Id}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
and
\psi\in{\mathcal{K}}
. If
r=\pm 1
, then

{\rm d\,}T^{2}\in{\mathcal{K}}
and
T
generates a relatively compact subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
. If possible suppose

r\neq\pm 1
. Since
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

{\rm d\,}T^{n}
is contained in a connected almost algebraic subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{k}
of

{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
(which is identified with its image in
{\rm GL}({\mathfrak{g}})
), where
\mathbb{T}^{k}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}

for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore,
\psi^{n}\,\circ\,r^{n}\,{\rm Id}\in{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{k}\subset{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
.
Hence both
\psi^{n}
and
r^{n}\,{\rm Id}\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
. But
r^{n}\,{\rm Id}
is not a Lie algebra automorphism of
{\mathfrak{g}}

as
r^{n}\neq 1
; otherwise
r^{n}[X,Y]=r^{n}{\rm Id}[X,Y]=[r^{n}{\rm Id}(X),r^{n}{\rm Id}(Y)]=r^{2n}[X,Y]
, and it would imply that

[X,Y]=0
for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, which leads to a contradiction as
G
is not abelian. Thus,
r=\pm 1
and

T
is contained in a compact subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
. The converse statement is obvious.
Thus (2) holds if
G
is nilpotent.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let

. Suppose
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
Then we want to show that

acts distally on
G

.

Step 1:
Suppose

is simply connected and nilpotent. Since
G
is not a vector group,

is non-abelian and by the proof of Theorem
1.1
(2) for the nilpotent case,

is contained in
a compact subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)

acts distally on

.

Step 2:
Now suppose

is not a simply connected nilpotent group.
By Theorem 4.1 of
[
35
]
,
it is enough to prove that the contraction groups
C(T)
and
C(T^{-1})
are trivial. As
T
acts distally on the
maximal compact connected central subgroup

, by Lemma
3.4
, both

C(T)
and
C(T^{-1})
are closed, simply connected and nilpotent subgroups. Suppose
C(T)
is non-trivial.
If possible, suppose

is non-abelian. As it is
T
-invariant and

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{C(T)}

we get from Step 1 that

acts distally on
C(T)
, a contradiction. Thus
C(T)
is abelian and

C(T)
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as it is simply connected. By Theorem
1.1
(1),

T_{1}=T|_{C(T)}=\phi\circ\alpha\,I
for
some
\phi
contained in a compact subgroup of
{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

; here

denotes the

{\rm GL}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
, replacing
T
by

.

Step 3:
Suppose
G
is nilpotent but not simply connected. Then
M
as above is non-trivial. Since

acts
distally on

[
2
]

is unipotent for some

{\rm d\,}T
has an
eigenvalue which is a root of unity. If

3.2

{\rm d\,}T
has an eigenvalue which is a
root of unity. We may replace
T
by
T^{m}

, and assume that 1 is an eigenvalue of
{\rm d\,}T
.
Let

,

{\rm d\,}T(X)=X

, a one-parameter

\bar{A}

T|_{\bar{A}}={\rm Id}
and it follows that
\bar{A}
normalises
C(T)
and
\bar{A}\cap C(T)=\{e\}
.

We now show that
C(T)
normalises
\bar{A}
, which would imply that
\bar{A}\,C(T)
is
abelian as
C(T)
and
A
are so. Here,
T(\bar{A})=\bar{A}
and
\bar{A}\in{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}

T^{n}(c\bar{A}c^{-1})=T^{n}(c)\,\bar{A}\,T^{n}(c^{-1})\to\bar{A}.

Since
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
, we get that
c\bar{A}c^{-1}=\bar{A}
, for all
c\in C(T)
.
Thus
C(T)
normalises
\bar{A}
, and hence

is abelian.

Let

. Then
H
is a closed
T
-invariant abelian subgroup and

. First, suppose
H

is a vector group. By Theorem
1.1
(1),
T|_{H}\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{D}}
, for some compact subgroup
{\mathcal{K}}
in
{\rm GL}(H)
and
the center
{\mathcal{D}}
of
{\rm GL}(H)
, and thus all the eigenvalues of

have the same absolute value. Since

and
T|_{C(T)}
have eigenvalues of absolute value
\alpha\neq 1

must be trivial in
this case.

Step 4:
Now suppose
H
is not a vector group. Since
C(T)
is closed and the image of
A

is dense in
H/C(T)
, we get that
H=K\times C(T)
for the maximal compact subgroup
K
of
H
.
As
T
acts trivially on

and

is
T
-invariant, we get that
T|_{K}={\rm Id}
.

Let
\psi,S:H\to H
be such that
S|_{K}={\rm Id}
,
S|_{C(T)}=\alpha\,{\rm Id}
,
\psi|_{K}={\rm Id}
and

\psi|_{C(T)}=\phi
, where
\alpha
and
\phi
are as in Step 2. Now
T|_{H}=S\psi=\psi S
, and
\psi
is

{\rm Aut}(H)
. Therefore,
S
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{H}
(cf.
[
41
]

.

Let
\{z_{t}=x_{t}c_{t}\}
be a closed one-parameter subgroup, where

is closed and isomorphic
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
and
\{c_{t}\}\subset C(S)=C(T)
is a non-trivial (closed) one-parameter subgroup such that

S(c_{t})=c_{\alpha t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(as addition is the group operation on
C(T)={\mathbb{R}}^{d}

,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
for some nonzero

{\mathbb{N}}

, and hence,

.

Similarly, we can show that

Corollary 4.1
.

.

Before proving Theorem
1.3
, we first prove the following proposition which is a special case of the theorem.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,

and let
L

-invariant

acts trivially on both

Since
T
acts trivially on
G/L
and

, we have that
T
acts distally on
G
. In fact, all the eigenvalues
of
{\rm d\,}T
are equal to 1 and
T
is unipotent. Suppose
T
acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
. Let
U
be a neighbourhood
of the identity

such that every element of

is contained in a one-parameter subgroup. Since
G
is
connected,

generates

for all

.

Step 1:
If possible, suppose
T(x)\neq x
for some

. There exists a one-parameter
subgroup
\{x_{t}\}
in
G
such that
x_{1}=x
. Let
H=\overline{\{x_{t}\}L}
. Then
H
is a closed connected solvable
T
-invariant

and

is abelian. Let
\pi:G\to G/L

Then
\pi(H)=\overline{\pi(\{x_{t}\})}
. If
\pi(\{x_{t}\})
is unbounded, it is closed and it is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
and in this case

\{x_{t}\}
is also unbounded and closed. Moreover
H=\{x_{t}\}L
.

Now suppose
\pi(\{x_{t}\})
is relatively compact. As
H
is solvable, there exists a maximal compact
(connected, abelian) subgroup
K
in
H
such that
H=KL
. As
T(x)\neq x
and
T|_{L}={\rm Id}
,
we have that
T(h)\neq h
for some
h\in K
, and hence
T(h)\neq h
for some finite order element
h
in
K\cap U
.
There exists a closed (compact) one-parameter subgroup
\{h_{t}\}
in
K
such that
h_{1}=h
. Replacing

\{x_{t}\}
by
\{h_{t}\}
, we have in this case too that

H=\{x_{t}\}L
.

Now we have that
H=\{x_{t}\}L
is closed, where either
\pi(\{x_{t}\})
(as well as
\{x_{t}\}
) is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}

or
\{x_{t}\}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
and
T(x_{1})\neq x_{1}
. As
T
acts trivially on
G/L
, we have that
T(H)=H

and
T(x_{t})=x_{t}y_{t}
, for some
y_{t}\in L
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where

.

Step 2:
Let
A:=\{x_{t}\}
and let
\{n_{k}\}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
be an unbounded sequence
such that

. By Lemma
2.2
,

is not discrete as

is connected and non-trivial. Let

,

As
y_{t}\in L
,

, we get that
\pi(x_{t_{k}})\to\pi(b)

H/L
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
,
then we get that the sequence
\{t_{k}\}
is bounded. In the second case where
\{x_{t}\}
is compact, we
can choose
\{t_{k}\}
to be bounded. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

. Therefore,
x_{t_{k}}\to x_{t_{0}}
and
y_{t_{k}}\to y_{t_{0}}
and

, where

.

Step 3:
We first assume that

is simply connected. We show that

,

. As

. Since

is a one-parameter subgroup in
G

and

,

. This leads to a contradiction as

.

for all

Suppose that

be the maximal

. Then

is central in

\overline{T}
acts trivially on

G/M
, i.e.

,

. In particular, for
\{x_{t}\}
as above,

, where

,
for all

, and
y_{1}\neq e
. Since
M
is central in
G
,
y_{t}x_{s}=x_{s}y_{t}
,

, and
\{y_{t}\}
is a

\{y_{t}\}
is closed if
\{x_{t}\}

As
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
is compact, there exists an unbounded sequence
\{n_{k}\}
in
{\mathbb{N}}

,

,

. Thus

.
Now

, and we get that

.

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and let
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Suppose
T
is unipotent and it acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
.
We show that
T={\rm Id}
.

Step 1:
Suppose
G
is nilpotent. If possible, suppose
T\neq{\rm Id}
. Since
T
is unipotent,
by Proposition 3.10 of
[
42
]
, there exists an increasing sequence of closed connected normal
T-invariant subgroups
\{e\}=G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset\cdots\subset G_{n}=G
,
n\geq 2
, such that

T
acts trivially on each successive quotient group

,

, and

does not act
trivially on

. Then

. As

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
,
it acts distally on

. Now by Proposition
4.2
,

,
which leads to a contradiction. Therefore,

when

is nilpotent.

Step 2:
Suppose

is not nilpotent. Let

be the nilradical of

acts distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{N}
,

. Suppose

is solvable. As noted in Remark

, we have that the
automorphism induced by

on

is a finite order automorphism, and it is trivial since it is unipotent.
Now by Proposition
4.2
,
T={\rm Id}
.

Step 3:
Suppose
G
is not solvable. Let
R
be the radical of
G
. Then as
T
acts
distally on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{R}
, we have from Step 2 that

.

Now

is reductive. Let

be the natural projection. Then
\pi(G)=SR^{\prime}
,
an almost direct product of a
semisimple Levi subgroup
S
of
\pi(G)
and the radical
R^{\prime}=\pi(R)
of
\pi(G)
, where

is central in
\pi(G)
.
Let
{\overline{T}}
be the automorphism of
\pi(G)
induced by

. Then
{\overline{T}}
acts trivially on

. Also

{\overline{T}}
keeps

invariant as it is the commutator subgroup of
\pi(G)
. Now if
{\overline{T}}
acts trivially on

\pi(G)=G/N
, then by Proposition
4.2
,
T={\rm Id}
. If possible suppose
{\overline{T}}
does not act trivially on
\pi(G)
.
As
{\overline{T}}
is unipotent, there exists a nontrivial unipotent element

in

such that
{\overline{T}}={\rm inn}(u)
. Considering the Iwasawa

(see Lemma 6.50 of
[

]
).
Now

is connected, solvable and

Let

As

. This implies that

acts trivially on

acts trivially on
\pi(G)=G/N
,
and as noted above, by Proposition

.

The converse statement in the theorem is obvious.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(2)
for non-nilpotent case.

Let

. Then

,

∎

Now we want to prove Theorem
1.4
. We need an elementary result about certain automorphisms keeping
a maximal compact connected abelian subgroup (maximal torus) invariant. In a connected Lie group
G
, all maximal tori
are conjugate to each other. Any compact abelian subgroup of
G
is contained in a maximal torus, as
any maximal compact abelian subgroup of
G
is connected. If
A
is a maximal torus in
G
, then so is
T(A)
,
for any
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, and
T(A)
is conjugate to
A
. Any maximal compact subgroup of a connected solvable Lie
group is abelian (cf.
[
25
]
).

In
[
15
]
, any subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
which fixes elements of a maximal torus is shown to be almost algebraic.
Given any automorphism
T
of
G
, it need not keep any torus invariant. However, this is the case if
T
is contained
in a compact connected abelian subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
, as shown in the following elementary lemma. The lemma also
holds if we replace the radical
R
by any closed connected characteristic subgroup of
G
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. If
{\mathcal{K}}
is a compact connected abelian subgroup
of
{\rm Aut}(G)
, then there exists a maximal torus of
G

(resp. of the radical of
G
) on which every
automorphism in
{\mathcal{K}}
acts trivially.

Proof.

Let
A
be any maximal torus in a connected Lie group
G
. It is easy to see that for any closed connected normal
subgroup
L
of
G
,
A\cap L
is a maximal torus of
L
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a compact connected abelian subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
and let
H={\mathcal{K}}\ltimes G
; where the action of
{\mathcal{K}}

on
G
is by automorphisms. Then
H
is a connected Lie group and
G
is normal in
H
. Let
R
be the radical
of
G
. Then
R
is characteristic in
G
and hence normal in
H
. Let
A
be a maximal torus in
H
containing

{\mathcal{K}}
. Arguing as above, we get that
A\cap G
(resp.
A\cap R
) is a maximal torus in
G
(resp.
R
). As

{\mathcal{K}}\subset A
and
A
is abelian, we get that every automorphism in
{\mathcal{K}}
acts trivially on
A\cap G
(resp.
A\cap R
).
∎

subspace of
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
. More generally, we have also studied the actions of subgroups
{\mathcal{H}}
of
{\rm Aut}(G)

on
{\rm Sub}^{p}_{G}
under a condition that the
{\mathcal{H}}
-action on the maximal central torus of
G
is distal. This latter
condition is satisfied if
{\mathcal{H}}
is contained in
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
or more generally, if it is contained in an almost
algebraic subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
.

The following example of a connected solvable Lie group
G
, known as the Walnut group, is not covered
by Theorem
1.1
or any earlier theorems for the distal action of a general
automorphism on
{\rm Sub}^{a}_{G}
, as it has nontrivial central torus and
{\rm Aut}(G)
is not almost algebraic
(see
[
15
]
). But it is covered by theorems proven here.

Example 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group consisting of
\,3\times 3
real strictly upper triangular matrices.
There is a canonical action of
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
\mathbb{H}
which fixes elements of its center, which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
N=\mathbb{H}/D
, where
D
is a discrete central subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
N
is a connected 2-step
nilpotent group and the
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
-action on
\mathbb{H}
carries over to the action on
N
. Let
G=SO(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes N
,
the Walnut group. Since the center of
G
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
,
{\rm Aut}(G)
acts distally on it.
Thus Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
hold for the action of any
T\in{\rm Aut}(G)

and Theorem
1.5
holds for any subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
of
{\rm Aut}(G)
.

One can also take a non-solvable group
G=SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes N
, for
N
mentioned as above,
whose automorphism group is almost algebraic (see
[
15
]
). Theorems mentioned in the above example
hold also for this group. This group
G
is mentioned in Remark 4.8 of
[
42
]
, although it has a maximal torus
of dimension 2,
{\rm Aut}(G)
is almost algebraic (see
[
15
]
), and the conclusion in Theorem 4.1 of
[
42
]

mentioned in the remark holds for this
G
.

It would be interesting to characterise general automorphisms of any connected Lie group
G

which act distally on
{\rm Sub}_{G}
or its closed invariant subspace without
any condition involving the action on the central torus.

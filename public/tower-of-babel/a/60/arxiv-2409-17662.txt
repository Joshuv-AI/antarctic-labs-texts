On quadratic embeddability of bipartite graphs and theta graphs
Wojciech Młotkowski, Marek Skrzypczyk and Michał Wojtylak

Abstract
. We compute the quadratic embedding constant for complete bipartite graphs with disjoint edges removed. Moreover, we study the quadratic embedding property for theta graphs, i.e., graphs consisting of three paths with common initial points and common endpoints. As a result, we provide an infinite family of primary graphs which are not quadratically embeddable.

Introduction

0.1.
The problem

We consider undirected graphs
G=(V,E)
without self-loops,
with the standard distance
d(x,y)
defined on the set
V\times V
(we refer the reader to the Preliminaries for formal statements of the basic definitions).
A graph
H
is called isometrically embeddable in
G
if
H
is (isomorphic with) a subgraph of
G
and the distances between vertices in
H
are preserved when considered in
G
.
A special attention will lie in bipartite graphs. Besides the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
we recall another important example of a bipartite graph: the
hypercube
. Namely, let
S
be a finite set and let
H(S)
be a graph such that the vertices are the subsets of
S
; further, two vertices
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are adjacent if and only if the symmetric difference
N_{1}\triangle N_{2}
is a singleton set.
In a seminal paper
[
13
]
Djoković characterised graphs isometrically embeddedable in a hypercube by a condition that
G
is bipartite and the set

G(a,b)=\{x\in G\ |\ d(x,a)<d(x,b)\},

where vertices
a
and
b
are adjacent, is convex for all
a,b\in G
(i.e., if for all
x,y\in G(a,b)
,
d(x,z)+d(y,z)=d(x,y)
implies that
z\in G(a,b)
). The work has found numerous applications in graph theory and other branches of mathematics, see e.g.,
[
3
,
12
,
16
]
, and chemistry, e.g.,
[
2
,
21
,
22
]
.

Later on Roth and Winkler
[
35
]
showed that a bipartite graph
G=(V,E)
is isometrically embeddable in a hypercube if and only if it admits a
quadratic embedding
, i.e., a map
\varphi:V\to\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\|^{2}=d(x,y),\quad x,y\in V,

where
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|)
is some Hilbert space and
d(x,y)
is the graph distance.

This observation constitutes a connection with the general theory of quadratic embeddings by
Schoenberg, see
[
36
]
. In particular, a finite set
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with an arbitrary metric
d
is quadratically embeddable if and only if the matrix

q^{D}:=[q^{d(x_{i},x_{j})}]_{ij=1}^{n},\quad\text{where }D=[d(x_{i},x_{j}]_{ij=1}^{n},\ 0^{0}:=1,

is positive semidefinite for
q\in[0,1]
. This question is in connection with another classical task in harmonic analysis, which is to determine for a given symmetric matrix
D
(with zeros on the diagonal only)
the following set

\pi(G)=\{q\in\mathbb{R}:q^{D}\succeq 0\},

where
\succeq 0
means positive semidefinite. The set
\pi(G)
is nonempty since
0,1\in\pi(G)
and is closed and if
n>1
we have
\pi(D)\subseteq[-1,1]
due to existence of a principal minor of
q^{D}
of the form

\begin{bmatrix}1&q^{x}\\
q^{x}&1\end{bmatrix}

(x\neq 0)
. The concept of quadratic embedding (of a metric space in general) has been studied considerably
along with Euclidean distance geometry
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
14
,
19
,
20
,
24
]

and also appeared in harmonic analysis, for example in studying the Kazhdan property
(T)
of groups
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
28
,
29
]
, and related structures and quantum probability
[
8
,
10
,
17
,
28
,
29
]
.

Returning to the topic of graphs, the theory of quadratic embeddings for graphs was undertaken by Obata. In a series of coauthored papers
[
7
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
he constituted a general theory for determining whether a graph (not necessarily bipartite) admits a quadratic embedding. Several of these results will be cited and used later on in the current paper.
A graph
G
is said to be
of QE class
(
of non-QE class
) if it admits
a quadratic embedding (does not admit a quadratic embedding, respectively), see
[
33
]
. Moreover, a graph of non-QE class is called
primary
if it contains no isometrically embedded proper subgraphs of non-QE class
[
31
]
.

Main problem.
Obata showed all primary non-QE graphs on 5 and 6 vertices, as well as all complete multipartite primary non-QE graphs
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. The main problem is to find all primary non-QE graphs and whether they exist for each number of vertices greater than or equal 5.

We provide a positive answer to this question.
Our main tool for achieving this will be the following
improvement of the Djoković result. Namely, it was asked by Bożejko and answered in Tanaka
[
37
]
, to characterise the case
[-1,1]\subseteq\pi(D)
for a graph distance matrix
D
. We present the result summarising the research discussed above.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with at least two vertices, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is isometrically embeddable in a hypercube,

(ii)

G
is bipartite and
G(a,b)
is convex for each adjacent vertices
a,b\in G
,

(iii)

\pi(G)=[-1,1]
,

(iv)

G
is bipartite and has no quintuple of vertices
(v_{1},\dots,v_{5})
with
\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{v_{3},v_{4}\}\in E
,

d(v_{1},v_{3})=d(v_{2},v_{4})=d(v_{1},v_{4})-1=d(v_{2},v_{3})-1
,
d(v_{5},v_{2})=d(v_{5},v_{1})+1
,
d(v_{5},v_{3})=d(v_{5},v_{4})+1
.

Furthermore, if a quintuple as in
(iv)
exists, then
G
is of non-QE class, regardless if it is bipartite or not.

We will extend the result by providing a concept of another quintuple of vertices, working for non-bipartite graphs.

Another important notion in the theory is the
QE constant
of a graph
G
is defined by Obata in
[
33
]
as

\QEC(G)=\max\{f^{\top}Df:f^{\top}f=1,\textbf{1}^{\top}f=0\},

where
1
stands for the vector with all coefficients equal to 1.
Apparently, a graph
G
is of QE-class if and only if

\QEC(G)\leq 0
(in other words the distance matrix is
conditionally negative
). Properties of
\QEC(G)
and its values for some types of graphs has been studied by several authors
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
18
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
33
,

]
.
In the current work we will provide or estimate the QE-constant for several graphs that have not been studied so far.

0.2.
Our aproach and results

The intuition behind the research presented in the paper is the following. Recall that the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
is QE class only for
n=1
, or
m\geq 1
, or

m=n=2
and in general its
\QEC

\frac{2(m-1)(n-1)-2}{m+n}
, see Obata–Zakiyyah
[
33
]
.
Suppose now we take large
m,n
and remove consecutively its edges in a way that leaves the remaining graph connected. We receive in this way a family of graphs with the order given by inclusion (which, in general, is not the isometric embedding) with the maximal element
K_{m,n}
and several minimal elements. Among these minimal elements we may find graphs of QE class, e.g., the path
P_{n}
with
QEC(P_{n})=-\frac{1}{1+\cos{\pi/n}}
(cf.
[
25
]
). Our (successful) guess was that somewhere in between lies hidden the primary non-QE graph. The results of the research are as follows.

(1)

In Section
2
we compute the QE constant for almost complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}^{t}
, i.e. the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
, with
t
disjoint edges removed
(Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
).

(2)

In Sections
4
–
7
we study the QE property for theta graphs
\Theta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, see Definition
4.1
. An important step was characterization of those theta graphs
which admit Tanaka quintuple (or modified Tanaka quintuple), see Theorem
5.7

(and Theorem

4.2
and
4.3
, which
provide a sufficient and a necessary condition for
\Theta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
to be of QE class.

(3)

Finally, we prove that the family of primary non-QE graphs is infinite, namely,
each theta graph which is not of QE type is primary, Theorem
8.1
and Corollary
8.2
.

1.
Preliminaries

A
graph

G=(V,E)
is a pair of non-empty set
V
of vertices and a set
E
, where an edge is a set of two distinct vertices in
V
. A
walk
is a finite sequence of edges which joins a sequence of vertices. A graph is called
connected
if there is a finite walk for each pair of vertices.
In such case by
d(x,y)=d_{G}(x,y)
we denote the length of a shortest walk connecting
x
and
y
, such a walk is called
geodesic
. Note that
d(x,y)
is a metric on
V
, called as the
graph distance
. The
distance matrix
is defined by

D=d(x,y)_{x,y\in V}.

We will identify
D
with an
n\times n
real matrix by fixing an ordering of vertices. By
\mathbf{1}
we understand the vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
consisting of ones, the size
n
will be clear from the context.
A
bipartite graph
is a graph whose vertex set can be partitioned into two subsets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, such that no two vertices within the same set are adjacent.
The
complete bipartite graph

K_{m,n}
is the maximal bipartite graph.

A
quadratic embedding
of a finite connected graph
G=(V,E)
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a map
\varphi:V\to\mathcal{H}
satisfying

||\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)||^{2}=d(x,y),\quad x,y\in V,

where
||\cdot||
is the norm of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and
d(x,y)
is the graph distance.
A graph
G
is said to be
of QE class
(
of non-QE class
) if it admits
a quadratic embedding (does not admit a quadratic embedding, respectively).
The definition of
\QEC(G)
was given in the Introduction.

A graph
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is called a
subgraph
of
G=(V,E)
if
V^{\prime}\subset V
and
E^{\prime}\subset E
. Let both graphs are connected and hence admit graph distances of their own. We say that
H
is
isometrically embedded
in
G
if

d_{H}(x,y)=d_{G}(x,y)\quad x,y\in V^{\prime}.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs and assume that
H
is isometrically embedded in
G
. Note the following simple fact: if
H
is of non-QE class, so
G
is of non-QE class as well, furthermore
\QEC(H)\leq\QEC(G)
.
Hence, non-QE graphs can be divided into two different types. A graph of non-QE class is called
primary
if it contains no isometrically embedded proper subgraphs of non-QE class
[
31
]
. Otherwise, it is called
non-primary
.

Let
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
have a distinguished vertices
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
, respectively. A
star product
denoted by
G_{1}\star G_{2}
is a graph obtained by gluing vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. Recall that if both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are of QE class, then
G_{1}\star G_{2}
is also of QE class, cf.
[
33
]
.

We end the section by remarking that the
\QEC
constant is not (weakly) monotone with respect to removing disjoint edges from a bipartite graph.

For

,

The graphs

In particular, for

with parameters

of order

.
The graphs

,

are not connected,

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that

,
(t,m,n)\neq(3,3,3)
and that if

or

then

. Then

(2)

\QEC(K_{m,n}^{t})=2\lambda_{0}-2,

where
\lambda_{0}
is the maximal root of the polynomial

(3)

P(\lambda):=(m+n)\lambda^{3}+(2t-mn)\lambda^{2}+(2t-m-n)\lambda+(m-t)(n-t).

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq 3
we have

(4)

\QEC(K_{2,n}^{2})=\frac{n-8+\sqrt{5n^{2}-24n+32}}{n+2}.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

If
(t,m,n)
satisfies the assumptions then the distance matrix of
K_{m,n}^{t}
has the following form:

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}2\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m,m}-2\cdot I_{m}&\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{1}_{t,t}+2\cdot I_{t}&\mathbf{1}_{t,n-t}\\
\mathbf{1}_{m-t,t}&\mathbf{1}_{m-t,n-t}\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{1}_{t,t}+2\cdot I_{t}&\mathbf{1}_{t,m-t}\\
\mathbf{1}_{n-t,t}&\mathbf{1}_{n-t,m-t}\end{array}&2\cdot\mathbf{1}_{n,n}-2\cdot I_{n}\end{array}\right).

Let
f=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots y_{n})^{\top}
, with
f^{\top}f=1
and
f^{\top}{\mathbf{1}}=0
.
Then, since
\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}=-\sum_{i=0}^{m}x_{i}
, we get

f^{\top}Df=2\Big(\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\big)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}\Big)+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}+4\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}y_{i}+2\Big(\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\big)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{2}\Big)\\
=-2+2\Big(\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}y_{i}\Big).

The usual method of Lagrange multipliers will be used to find the maximum above, cf. Proposition 4.1 in
[
33
]
. Our Lagrangian function has the form

\mathcal{L}(f,\lambda,\mu)=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}x_{k}\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}y_{i}-\lambda\left(f^{\top}f-1\right)-\mu f^{\top}{\mathbf{1}}.

Comparing the partial derivatives to zero and putting
X:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
, we obtain:

(5)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=2X+2y_{i}-2\lambda x_{i}-\mu\qquad\hbox{for $i\leq t$,}

(6)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=2X-2\lambda x_{i}-\mu\qquad\hbox{for $t<i\leq m$,}

(7)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y_{i}}

\displaystyle=2x_{i}-2\lambda y_{i}-\mu\qquad\hbox{for $i\leq t$,}

(8)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y_{j}}

\displaystyle=-2\lambda y_{j}-\mu\qquad\hbox{for $t<j\leq n$,}

(9)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{m}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+\ldots+y_{n}^{2}-1,

(10)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\mu}

\displaystyle=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}+y_{1}+\ldots+y_{n}.

Let
\mathcal{S}(D)
be the set of all
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

satisfying (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (

) and (
10
)
and let
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(D):=\{\lambda:(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}(D)\}
,
\lambda_{\max}:=\max\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(D)
.
Then
\QEC(G)=-2+2\lambda_{\max}
.
For any graph which is not complete we have
\QEC(G)>-1
, cf.
[
6
]
, thus
\lambda_{\max}>\frac{1}{2}
.

Claim.
We have
1\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(D)
if and only if either
(t,m,n)=(1,2,3)
or

.

If

then we can put

,

,

,

,

Now assume that

Therefore

In this case

Now assume that

and

,

3.
Tanaka quintuple in a graph

Now we are going to present a result of
Tanaka
[

, who proved that in the case of bipartite graphs,

can be characterized by absence of some kind of 5-element subsets, see also
[

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence of vertices
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5})
in a graph
G=(V,E)
is called
Tanaka quintuple

if

(28)

\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{v_{3},v_{4}\}\in E,

(29)

d(v_{1},v_{3})=d(v_{2},v_{4})=d(v_{1},v_{4})-1=d(v_{2},v_{3})-1,

(30)

d(v_{5},v_{2})=d(v_{5},v_{1})+1,\qquad d(v_{5},v_{3})=d(v_{5},v_{4})+1.

Now we present Theorem 3 in
[
37
]
in a form adapted to our terminology.
The first part was not stated in
[
37
]
, but it easily follows from the proof.

Theorem 3.2

(Tanaka
[
37
]
)
.

If a graph
G
admits Tanaka quintuple then
G
is of non-
QE
class.
In addition, if
G
is a bipartite graph then
G
is of
QE
class
if and only if
G
has no Tanaka quintuple.

Let us collect some properties.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5})
is a Tanaka quintuple in a graph
G
.

If
(v_{1}=a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}=v_{3})
,
(v_{2}=b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}=v_{4})
are geodesics then

\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}\cap\{b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}=\emptyset\quad\hbox{and}\quad v_{5}\notin\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}.

If
(v_{5}=e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}=v_{1})
,
(v_{5}=f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{q}=v_{4})
are geodesics then

v_{4}\notin\{e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad v_{1}\notin\{f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{q}\}.

Proof.

Assume that
(v_{1}=a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}=v_{3})
,
(v_{2}=b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}=v_{4})
are geodesics
and that
a_{i}=b_{j}
for some
0<i,j<r
. If
i<j
then
d(v_{1},v_{4})\leq i+r-j<r
, which is impossible,
similarly if
j<i
. If
i=j
then
d(v_{1},v_{4})\leq r
, which contradicts (
29
).
Therefore these geodesics must be disjoint.
If
v_{5}=a_{i}
,
0<i<r

30

d(v_{5},v_{4})=r-i-1

Consequently

If
(v_{5}=e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}=v_{1})
is a geodesics and

,

then

,

The remaining statements can be proved in a similar manner.

\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 2
are all even or all odd.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) was already shown in Theorem
8.1
, let us consider a bipartite graph
G
satisfying (i) and suppose that it is not a theta graph. By Theorem
3.2
,
G
has a Tanaka quintuple
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5})
.

Let
X:=(v_{1}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}=v_{3})
and
Y:=(v_{2}=y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=v_{4})
be the geodesics.
Clearly
G
needs to have some extra edges, i.e., edges that are neither in
X
nor in
Y
, and clearly none of these edges cannot connect vertices, neither from
X
, nor from
Y
as this would violate the definition of geodesics. We analyse now several instances, obtaining each time a contradiction.

Assume that there is an edge other than
v_{1}v_{2}
and
v_{3}v_{4}
that connects a vertex from
X
with vertex from
Y
, then by Lemma
3.3
we still do not have a Tanaka quintuple.
Therefore, for the existence of Tanaka quintuple, there must exist a path
Z:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
with length at least 2, such that
Z\cap(X\cup Y)=\{z_{1},z_{n}\}
and
v_{5}\in Z
. Note that vertices
z_{1}
and
z_{n}
must be adjacent. Indeed, otherwise
G
contains properly
\Theta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 2
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are all even or all odd and consequently, by Theorem
4.3
,
G
contains a subgraph of non-QE class, contradiction.

Since the graph
G
is bipartite and vertices
z_{1}
and
z_{n}
are adjacent, the path
Z
has a length at least 3. Without loss of generality, assume that
z_{1},z_{n}\in X\cup\{v_{2}\}
. To satisfy the condition
d(v_{5},v_{3})=d(v_{5},v_{4})+1
, the graph
G
must contain a path
W:=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{i})
such that
w_{1}\in Y
and
w_{i}\in Z
. Clearly,
W
and
X
are disjoint, moreover
W
has at least one edge that belongs neither to
Y
nor to
Z
. We have
w_{i}\neq z_{1}
and
w_{i}\neq z_{n}
, otherwise
d(v_{1},v_{3})=d(v_{1},v_{4})+1
, which is a contradiction with existence of Tanaka quintuple.

Let
w_{i}=z_{j}
, where
j\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
, define the paths
Z_{1}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{j})
and
Z_{2}:=(z_{j},\ldots,z_{n})
. Then a subdivided wheel of non-QE class is a subgraph of
G
, see Example
8.3
. Observe that either
Z_{1}
or
Z_{2}
has length at least 2, without loss of generality assume the latter. Then
G
has a subgraph
\Theta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with paths
P_{\alpha}:=Z_{1}\cup\{z_{n}\}
,
P_{\beta}:=Z_{2}
and
P_{\gamma}:=W\cup\{w_{1},\ldots,v_{4},v_{3},\ldots,z_{n}\}
. Observe that by Theorem
4.3
the graph
\Theta(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is of non-QE class, which is a contradiction finishing the proof.
∎

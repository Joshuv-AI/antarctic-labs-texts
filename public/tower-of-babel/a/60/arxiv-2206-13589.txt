BPS invariants of symplectic log Calabi-Yau fourfolds
Mohammad Farajzadeh-Tehrani

Abstract
Using the Fredholm setup of [ 12 ] , we study genus zero (and higher) relative Gromov-Witten invariants with maximum tangency of symplectic log Calabi-Yau fourfolds. In particular, we give a short proof of [ 23 , Cnj. 6.2] that expresses these invariants in terms of certain integral invariants by considering generic almost complex structures to obtain a geometric count. We also revisit the localization calculation of the multiple-cover contributions in [ 23 , Prp. 6.1] and recalculate a few terms differently to provide more details and illustrate the computation of deformation/obstruction spaces for maps that have components in a destabilizing (or rubber) component of the target. Finally, we study a higher genus version of these invariants and explain a decomposition of genus one invariants into different contributions.

Logarithmic Fredholm theory

In this section, we review the Fredholm setup for the deformation-obstruction theory of relative/log maps in the virtually main stratum
\mathcal{M}_{g,\mathfrak{s}}(X,D,A)
introduced in
[
12
]
. The main output of this discussion will be the notion of logarithmic normal bundle (
2.17
) and the super-rigidity Lemma
2.4
.
We conclude this section with a relatively short proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let us start with a brief discussion of the classical Fredholm setup. Suppose
\Sigma
is a smooth closed Riemann surface with the complex structure
\mathfrak{j}
. Let
\textnormal{Map}_{A}(\Sigma,X)
denote the space of all smooth maps
u\colon\!\Sigma\!\longrightarrow\!X
that represent the homology class
A
, and

\mathcal{E}_{A}(\Sigma,X)\longrightarrow\textnormal{Map}_{A}(\Sigma,X)

be the infinite dimensional bundle whose fiber over
u
is
\Gamma(\Sigma,\Omega^{0,1}_{\Sigma}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}u^{*}TX)
. The Cauchy-Riemann (or
CR
) equation

\bar{\partial}u\equiv\frac{1}{2}\big(\textnormal{d}u+J\textnormal{d}u\circ\mathfrak{j})

(2.1)

can be seen as a section
\bar{\partial}\colon\textnormal{Map}_{A}(\Sigma,X)\!\longrightarrow\!\mathcal{E}_{A}(\Sigma,X)
. More precisely, we consider a Sobolev completion of these spaces for the Implicit Function Theorem to apply, but, by elliptic regularity, every solution of
\bar{\partial}u\!=\!0
will be smooth; see
[
32
, Appendix B]
. The linearization of the
\bar{\partial}
-section at any
J
-holomorphic map
u
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map

\textnormal{D}_{u}\bar{\partial}\colon\Gamma(\Sigma,u^{*}TX)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Sigma,\Omega^{0,1}_{\Sigma}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}u^{*}TX)

(2.2)

that is the sum of a
\mathbb{C}
-linear
\bar{\partial}
-operator and a compact operator. Therefore, it is a Fredholm operator and Riemann-Roch applies; i.e., it has finite dimensional kernel and co-kernel, and

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\textnormal{Def}(u)\!-\!\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\textnormal{Ob}(u)\!=\!2\big(\textnormal{deg}(u^{*}TX)\!+\!\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\!X(1\!-\!g)\big),

(2.3)

where

\textnormal{Def}(u)\!=\!\textnormal{ker}(\textnormal{D}_{u}\bar{\partial})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\textnormal{Ob}(u)\!=\!\textnormal{coker}(\textnormal{D}_{u}\bar{\partial}).

The first space corresponds to infinitesimal deformations of
u
(over the fixed smooth marked domain) and the second one is the obstruction space for integrating elements of
\textnormal{Def}(u)
to actual deformations.
If
\textnormal{Ob}(u)\!=\!0
, by Implicit Function Theorem
[
32
, Thm. A.3.3]
, in a small neighborhood
U
of
u
in
\textnormal{Map}_{A}(\Sigma,X)
, the set of
J
-holomorphic maps
V=U\cap\bar{\partial}^{-1}(0)
is a smooth manifold of real dimension (
2.3
), all the elements of
\textnormal{Def}(u)
are smooth, and
T_{u}V\cong\textnormal{Def}(u)
;
see
[
32
, Thm. 3.1.5]
. The manifold
V
carries a natural orientation.

For
(\omega,J)\!\in\!\textnormal{AK}(X,D)
, in
[
12
, Sec. 4]
, corresponding to every genus
g

k
-marked relative map in the virtually main stratum,

f\!=\![u,C=(\Sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})]\in\mathcal{M}_{g,\mathfrak{s}}(X,D,A),

(2.4)

we derive a
logarithmic linearization
of the CR operator/section at
u
, denoted by

\textnormal{D}^{\log}_{u}\bar{\partial}\colon\Gamma(\Sigma,u^{*}TX(-\log D))\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Sigma,\Omega^{0,1}_{\Sigma}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}u^{*}TX(-\log D)),

(2.5)

such that
TX(-\log D)
is the logarithmic tangent bundle introduced in
[
14
]
and constructed in detail in
[
16
]
.
After the following remark, we briefly digress from the main discussion and recall the construction of
TX(-\log D)
and some of its properties.
Then, we show how
\textnormal{D}^{\log}_{u}\bar{\partial}
can be used to study the deformation-obstruction spaces of
\mathcal{M}_{g,\mathfrak{s}}(X,D,A)
at
f
.

Remark 2.1
.

In the symplectic category, moduli spaces of relative maps were first studied by Ionel-Parker and Li-Ruan in
[
28
]
and
[
31
]
. In
[
17
]
, we give a detailed account of their approaches and streamline some aspects of their construction. The work of Ionel-Parker does not include a dedicated Fredholm setup for relative maps. Deformation spaces are defined as a subspace of the classical deformation space and the obstruction spaces are not precisely identified. The approach of Li-Ruan is based on working with manifolds with cylindrical ends as in Symplectic Field Theory. In this approach,
\textnormal{D}^{\log}_{u}\bar{\partial}
is defined over a subspace of

\Gamma\big(\Sigma^{*},\big(u|_{\Sigma^{*}}\big)^{*}T(X-D)\big),\qquad\Sigma^{*}=\Sigma-u^{-1}(D),

that is completed with weighted norms on the cylindrical parts of
\Sigma^{*}
and
X-D
. These weighted norms control the behavior of
f
at the infinity. As the following concrete definitions and structures indicate, our logarithmic Fredholm setup has the advantage of working with closed surfaces and is more user-friendly for applications such as explicitely identifying the deformation/obstruction spaces and localization calculations.

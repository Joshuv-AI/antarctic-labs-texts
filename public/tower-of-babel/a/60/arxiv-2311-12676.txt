Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence of minimal covers and strong minimal covers in the Weihrauch degrees. We characterize when a problem f f is a minimal cover or strong minimal cover of a problem h h . We show that strong minimal covers only exist in the cone below id \operatorname{id} and that the Weihrauch lattice above id \operatorname{id} is dense. From this, we conclude that the degree of id \operatorname{id} is first-order definable in the Weihrauch degrees and that the first-order theory of the Weihrauch degrees is computably isomorphic to third-order arithmetic.

1.
Introduction

In a partial order
(\mathcal{D},\leq)
, an element
\mathbf{a}
is a

minimal cover
of
\mathbf{b}
if
\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{a}
and there
is no
\mathbf{c}
such that
\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{a}
. In other
words, the interval between
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
is empty. We say
that
\mathbf{a}
is a
strong minimal cover
of
\mathbf{b}
if

\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{a}
and for all
\mathbf{c}
if
\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{a}

then
\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{b}
.

Understanding the properties and the distribution of minimal covers and
strong minimal covers can provide deep insights into the structure of a
partial order, and indeed there is an extensive literature on the
construction of minimal covers and strong minimal covers in the Turing
degrees. In contrast, despite its growing popularity, the structure of
Weihrauch degrees is vastly unexplored, as most of the efforts up to this
date have concentrated on the classification of the Weihrauch degrees of
specific problems. In particular, very little is known on the existence of
(strong) minimal covers in the Weihrauch degrees. In this paper, we fill this
gap by providing complete characterizations of minimal covers and strong
minimal covers in the Weihrauch degrees. We will see that this
analysis is then able to answer a number of other questions.

1.1.
Background

Weihrauch reducibility
[
13
,
6
,
1
]
classifies partial
multi-valued functions according to their uniform computational strength, and
it is often used to characterize the computability-theoretical complexity
of
\forall\exists
-statements. We briefly recall the main notions we need in this paper, and we refer the
reader to
[
1
]
for a more thorough presentation on Weihrauch
reducibility.

If
f
and
g
are partial multi-valued functions on Baire space (denoted
by
f,g:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
), we say that
f
is
Weihrauch
reducible
to
g
, and write
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}g
, if there are two computable
functionals
\Phi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Psi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(\forall p\in\operatorname{dom}(f))[\;\Phi(p)\in\operatorname{dom}(g)\land(\forall q\in g(\Phi(p)))\;\Psi(p,q)\in f(p)\;].

We use
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})
to
denote the structure of degrees induced by Weihrauch reducibility. In the
computable analysis literature, Weihrauch reducibility is often defined in a
more general context, where
f
and
g
are partial multi-valued functions
between represented spaces. However, it is well-known that every Weihrauch
degree contains a representative with domain and codomain
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
(see, e.g.,

[
1
, Lemma 11.3.8]
). In other words, in order to study the structure
of the Weihrauch degrees, there is no loss of generality in restricting our
attention to computational problems on Baire space. In what follows, with a small abuse of notation we will identify a natural number
n
with the infinite string constantly equal to
n
.

The Weihrauch degrees are known to form a distributive lattice where join
and meet are induced, respectively, by the following operators:

•

f\sqcup g
is the problem with domain
\{0\}\times\operatorname{dom}(f)\cup\{1\}\times\operatorname{dom}(g)
defined as

(f\sqcup g)(i,x):=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }i=0,\\
g(x)&\text{if }i=1.\end{cases}

•

f\sqcap g
is the problem with domain
\operatorname{dom}(f)\times\operatorname{dom}(g)

defined as

(f\sqcap g)(x,z):=\{0\}\times f(x)\cup\{1\}\times g(z).

The degree of the empty function is a natural bottom element in the
Weihrauch degrees. The existence of a top element is equivalent to the
failure of a (relatively weak) form of choice. In particular, under

\mathrm{ZFC}
, there is no top element in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})
.

The statements and proofs of our main theorems exploit the interplay between
Weihrauch and Medvedev reducibility. For the sake of completeness, we recall
some basic facts on Medvedev reducibility and refer the reader to

[
8
,
12
]
for more details. Given
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we
say that
A
is
Medvedev reducible
to
B
, and write

A\leq_{\mathrm{M}}B
, if there is a computable functional

\Phi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Phi(B)\subseteq A
. We write

(\mathcal{M},\leq_{\mathrm{M}})
for the degree structure induced by
Medvedev reducibility. It is well-known that the Medvedev degrees form a
distributive lattice with a top element (the degree of
\emptyset
) and a
bottom element (the degree of
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
).

There is a close connection between Weihrauch and Medvedev reducibility.
Indeed, we can rephrase the definition of Weihrauch reducibility as follows:

f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}g
iff there are two computable functionals

\Phi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Psi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Phi
witnesses
\operatorname{dom}(g)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\operatorname{dom}(f)
and, for every
p\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
,
\Psi(p,\cdot)
witnesses
f(p)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}g(\Phi(p))
. This suggests two possible embeddings of the
Medvedev degrees in the Weihrauch degrees
[
7
]
. For our
purposes, we explicitly mention the following one (see
[
3
, Section 5]
for a discussion of the other): For every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
d_{A}\colon A\to\{0^{\mathbb{N}}\}
be the constant function that
maps every element of
A
to the constantly
0
string.
The map
d:=A\mapsto d_{A}
induces a lattice embedding of

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}=(\mathcal{M},\geq_{\mathrm{M}})
in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})

[
7
, Lemma 5.6]
.

A simple inspection reveals that the range of the embedding
d
is exactly
the set of uniformly computable degrees. To see this, let

\operatorname{id}\colon\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the identity function on Baire space.
It is immediate that a multi-valued function
f
is uniformly
computable iff
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\operatorname{id}
. In fact, writing
\operatorname{id}_{X}
for
the restriction of
\operatorname{id}
to
X
, every problem
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\operatorname{id}
is
Weihrauch-equivalent to
\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{dom}(f)}
. In other words, the
uniformly computable problems are precisely those equivalent to one of the form

\operatorname{id}_{X}
for some
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, which is, in turn, equivalent to
d_{X}
. So the lower cone of
\operatorname{id}
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
, hence far from trivial. In particular, this implies that the
Medvedev degrees are first-order definable in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}},\mathbf{1})
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the Weihrauch degree of
\operatorname{id}
. The
question of whether
\mathbf{1}
is first-order definable in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})
was raised by Pauly during the conference “Computability and
Complexity in Analysis 2020” and the Oberwolfach meeting 2117
[
2
]
, see also
[
10
]
. Our results answer this question affirmatively.

The empty intervals in the Medvedev degrees have been fully characterized
in the literature. For every
p\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\{p\}^{+}:=\{({e})\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}q{}\,:\,{}\Phi_{e}(q)=p
and
q\not\leq_{\mathrm{T}}p\}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Dyment
[
4
, Cor. 2.5]
)
.

For every
A<_{\mathrm{M}}B
,
B
is a minimal cover of
A
iff

(\exists p\in A)[\;A\equiv_{\mathrm{M}}B\wedge\{p\}\text{ and }B\wedge\{p\}^{+}\equiv_{\mathrm{M}}B\;],

where
P\wedge Q:=({0})\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}P\cup({1})\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}Q
is the meet in the
Medvedev degrees.

The set
\{p\}^{+}
is the immediate successor of
\{p\}
in the
Medvedev degrees. In fact, the strong minimal covers in

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
are precisely those of the form
(\{p\}^{+},\{p\})
. This implies that the property of being a degree
of solvability (i.e., being Medvedev equivalent to a singleton) is
first-order definable in
(\mathcal{M},\leq_{\mathrm{M}})
(Dyment
[
4
, Cor. 2.1]
).

In particular, the fact that the lower cone of
\operatorname{id}
(in the Weihrauch
degrees) is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
immediately
yields:

Corollary 1.2
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
\operatorname{id}_{\{p\}}
is a strong minimal cover of

\operatorname{id}_{\{p\}^{+}}
.

Since
\mathrm{NR}:=\{q{}\,:\,{}q\not\leq_{\mathrm{T}}0\}\equiv_{\mathrm{M}}\{0^{\mathbb{N}}\}^{+}
, we also obtain:

Corollary 1.3
.

\operatorname{id}
is a strong minimal cover of
\operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{NR}}
.

1.2.
Our main theorems

Unlike the Medvedev degrees, there are no results describing the structure of
minimal covers and strong minimal covers in the Weihrauch degrees. Recently,
Dzhafarov, Lerman, Patey, and Solomon
[
5
]
showed that no
Weihrauch degree can be minimal. This result can be obtained as a corollary
of our first main theorem:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f
and
h
be partial multi-valued functions on Baire space. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is a minimal cover of
h
in the Weihrauch degrees.

(2)

f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup\operatorname{id}_{\{p\}}
for some
p
with
\operatorname{dom}(h)\not\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\{p\}
and
\operatorname{dom}(h)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\{p\}^{+}
.

The second main theorem provides a similar characterization for strong
minimal covers:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f
and
h
be partial multi-valued functions on Baire space. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is a strong minimal cover of
h
in the Weihrauch degrees.

(2)

There is
p\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\operatorname{id}_{\{p\}}
and
h\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\operatorname{id}_{\{p\}^{+}}
.

A multi-valued function is called
pointed
if it has a computable
point in its domain. A computational problem
f
is pointed iff

\operatorname{id}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}f
. In particular, the cone above
\operatorname{id}
is exactly the
cone of pointed degrees. Using
, we can
further characterize the non-pointed degrees.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
g
be a multi-valued function. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{id}\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}g
.

(2)

There are
f,h
such that
g\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h<_{\mathrm{W}}f
and

f
is a minimal cover of
h
.

Proof 1.7
.

The direction
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is straightforward as

implies that the bottom of a minimal cover cannot have
a computable point in its domain. To show that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
, observe
that if
\operatorname{id}\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}g
, then
g\leq_{\mathrm{W}}g\sqcup\operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{NR}}<_{\mathrm{W}}g\sqcup\operatorname{id}
. In particular, since
g\sqcup\operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{NR}}\sqcup\operatorname{id}_{\{0^{\mathbb{N}}\}}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}g\sqcup\operatorname{id}
, by
, the interval
g\sqcup\operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{NR}}<_{\mathrm{W}}g\sqcup\operatorname{id}
is empty.

Corollary 1.8
.

The pointed Weihrauch degrees are dense.

Our results provide two different first-order definitions of the degree of
\operatorname{id}
, thus the property of being uniformly computable is lattice-theoretic, answering the above-mentioned question by Pauly.

Theorem 1.9
.

The Weihrauch degree of
\operatorname{id}
is first-order definable in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})
. In particular, it is both:

(1)

the greatest degree that is a strong minimal cover, and

(2)

the least degree such that the cone of Weihrauch degrees above it is dense.

Proof 1.10
.

The first definition follows from
and the fact that
\operatorname{id}
is a strong minimal cover (
). The second is immediate from
.

Finally, the definability of
\operatorname{id}
implies that the first-order theory of the Weihrauch degrees is computably isomorphic to the third-order theory of
arithmetic, and therefore it is “as complicated as possible”.

Theorem 1.11
.

The first-order theory of the Weihrauch degrees, the first-order theory of
the Weihrauch degrees below
\operatorname{id}
, and the third-order theory of true
arithmetic are pairwise recursively isomorphic.

Proof 1.12
.

It is routine to check that Weihrauch reducibility between two multi-valued
functions
f
and
g
can be defined using a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
formula (with free
third-order variables
f
and
g
). This immediately implies that

\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{W}(\leq\operatorname{id}))\leq_{1}\mathrm{Th}_{3}(\mathbb{N})
and

\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{W})\leq_{1}\mathrm{Th}_{3}(\mathbb{N})
. The fact that the degree
of
\operatorname{id}
is first-order definable in
(\mathcal{W},\leq_{\mathrm{W}})

(

\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{W}(\leq\operatorname{id}))\leq_{1}\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{W})
. Since, as mentioned, the lower cone of
\operatorname{id}
is
isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
, the statement follows from
the fact that
\mathrm{Th}(\mathcal{M})\equiv_{1}\mathrm{Th}_{3}(\mathbb{N})

(
[
11
, Thm. 3.13]
and independently
[
9
, Thm. 2]

2.
Proof of the main theorems

Before proving the main theorems, we need some preliminary results. The
following lemma is a step towards proving the first main theorem. It
implicitly shows that if
f
is not a minimal cover of
h
, then there is a
uniform way to construct a problem
g
such that
h<_{\mathrm{W}}g<_{\mathrm{W}}f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h\leq_{\mathrm{W}}f
. If
f
is a minimal cover of
h
then there is
g

with
|\operatorname{dom}(g)|=1
such that
f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup g
.

Proof 2.2
.

We first outline the proof strategy: we construct a partial single-valued function

\xi:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}
in stages, so that
\xi
is defined on at
most
s
points by stage
s
. Let
F_{\xi}
be the problem defined as

F_{\xi}(p,\xi(p)):=f(p)
. The function
\xi
“scrambles” the domain of
f
: it
is immediate that, for every choice of the function
\xi
,

F_{\xi}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}f
. The converse reduction trivially holds when
\xi
is
computable, but it does not hold in general. The construction attempts to
build a function
\xi
so that

•

F_{\xi}\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h

•

h\sqcup F_{\xi}<_{\mathrm{W}}f
.

Since this would contradict our assumptions, we argue that the construction
must fail. The failure of our construction will result in the desired
function
g
.

The construction of
\xi
proceeds as follows: for every stage
s
, we define
a partial function
\xi_{s}
. We start the construction by letting

\xi_{0}:=\emptyset
. For the sake of readability, let us write

F_{s}:=F_{\xi_{s}}
.

At stage
s+1=2\langle e,i\rangle
, we extend
\xi_{s}
so that
F_{\xi}\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
via
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
. Since
\xi_{s}
has finite domain
and codomain
\mathbb{N}
, it has a total computable extension

\hat{\xi}:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}
. As observed,
F_{\hat{\xi}}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}f
, hence in particular
F_{\hat{\xi}}\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
via

\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
. By the definition of Weihrauch reducibility, there is some

p_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
such that either
\Phi_{e}(p_{0},\hat{\xi}(p_{0}))\notin\operatorname{dom}(h)

or, for some
q\in h(\Phi_{e}(p_{0},\hat{\xi}(p_{0})))
,

\Phi_{i}((p_{0},\hat{\xi}(p_{0})),q)\notin F_{\hat{\xi}}(p_{0},\hat{\xi}(p_{0}))=f(p_{0})
. Fix some
p_{0}
as above. Defining

\xi_{s+1}:=\xi_{s}\cup\{(p_{0},\hat{\xi}(p_{0}))\}
ensures that there is no
extension
\xi^{\prime}
of
\xi_{s+1}
such that
F_{\xi^{\prime}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
via the
functionals
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
.

At stage
s+1=2\langle e,i\rangle+1
, we try to extend
\xi_{s}
so that
f\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi}
via
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
. The construction stops
if
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
witness
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{s}
, as the same pair
of functionals would witness the reduction
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi^{\prime}}
for any extension
\xi^{\prime}
of
\xi_{s}
. In this case, we simply define
\xi:=\xi_{s}
. Assume therefore that
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
do not witness
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{s}
, and
let
p_{1}
be such that either
\Phi_{e}(p_{1})\notin\operatorname{dom}(h\sqcup F_{s})
or, for
some
q\in(h\sqcup F_{s})(\Phi_{e}(p_{1}))
,
\Phi_{i}(p_{1},q)\notin f(p_{1})
. If

•

\Phi_{e}(p_{1})\uparrow
, or

•

\Phi_{e}(p_{1})=(0,r)
for some
r\notin\operatorname{dom}(h)
, or

•

\Phi_{e}(p_{1})=(1,(p,k))
for some
p
such that
p\notin\operatorname{dom}(f)
or

p\in\operatorname{dom}(\xi_{s})
with
\xi_{s}(p)\neq k
, or

•

\Phi_{e}(p_{1})\in\operatorname{dom}(h\sqcup F_{s})
and for some
q\in(h\sqcup F_{s})(\Phi_{e}(p_{1}))
,
\Phi_{i}(p_{1},q)\notin f(p_{1})
,

then there is no extension
\xi^{\prime}
of
\xi_{s}
such that
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi^{\prime}}

via the functionals
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
, hence we can just define

\xi_{s+1}:=\xi_{s}
. The remaining case is that
\Phi_{e}(p_{1})=(1,(p,k))
for
some
p\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\setminus\operatorname{dom}(\xi_{s})
. We define
\xi_{s+1}:=\xi\cup\{(p_{1},k+1)\}
. Again, this ensures that there is no extension
\xi^{\prime}
of
\xi_{s+1}

such that
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{i}
witness that
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi^{\prime}}
.

Observe that, if for every
s+1=2\langle e,i\rangle+1
,
f\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{s}
, then, in the limit, we obtain a function
\xi
such that

h<_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi}<_{\mathrm{W}}f
, against the
assumption that
f
is a minimal cover of
h
. This implies that, for some

s
as above,
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{s}=h\sqcup F_{\xi}
. Moreover,

\xi
, and hence
F_{\xi}
, has finite domain, which in turn implies that

f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi}
.

Let
(p_{i})_{i<n}
be an enumeration of
\operatorname{dom}(\xi)
, and let
c_{i}
be the function with domain
\{p_{i}\}
defined as
c_{i}(p_{i}):=f(p_{i})
.
Since
\operatorname{dom}(\xi)
is finite, we immediately have
F_{\xi}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\bigsqcup_{i<n}c_{i}
. If, for all

,
c_{i}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
,
we would have
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
, which is a
contradiction. Hence, for some

,
h<_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup c_{i}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup F_{\xi}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}f
. The fact that
f
is a minimal
cover of
h
implies that
f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup c_{i}
. Letting
g:=c_{i}

concludes the proof.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
f
is a strong minimal cover of
h
, then there is
g\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}f
such
that
|\operatorname{dom}(g)|=1
.

Proof 2.4
.

By
, there is
g
with
|\operatorname{dom}(g)|=1
such that
f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup g
. Since
g\leq_{\mathrm{W}}f
, the fact that
f
is a
strong minimal cover of
h
, implies that
g\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}f
or
g\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
. The latter is readily seen to yield a contradiction, as
g\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h\Rightarrow h<_{\mathrm{W}}f\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h\sqcup g\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}h
,
hence
g\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}f
.

For every set
A
, let
\chi_{A}
denote the characteristic function of
A
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
f\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
, then there are at most countably many
D\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
.

Proof 2.6
.

We argue that each pair of potential reduction witnesses
\Phi,\Psi
for
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
can work for at most one choice of
D
. To see
this, assume that
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
via
\Phi,\Psi
. Notice
that if there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(\forall x\in\operatorname{dom}(f))(\forall y\in h(\Phi(x,n_{0})))(\exists z\in f(x))\;\Psi((x,n_{0}),y)=(0,z),

then we would have
f\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
, against the hypothesis.

This implies that, for every
D\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, if
\Phi,\Psi
witness
the reduction
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

there exists some
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
and some
y\in h(\Phi(x,n))
such that

\Psi((x,n),y)=(1,\chi_{D}(n))
. In other words, membership of
n
in
D
is
determined by the pair
\Phi,\Psi
, and hence the same pair cannot witness
the reduction
f\sqcap\chi_{E}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
for any set
E\neq D
.

In fact, if
f\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
then there are exactly countably many

D\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
if and only if

\operatorname{dom}(h)\leq_{\mathrm{M}}\operatorname{dom}(f)
. (Otherwise, it is never the case that
f\sqcap\chi_{D}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}h
.)

Lemma 2.7
.

If
f\not\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\operatorname{id}
and
f
has singleton domain, then for all
h

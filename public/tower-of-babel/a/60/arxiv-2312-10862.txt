Cohomology and Homotopy of Lie triple systems
Haobo Xia, Yunhe Sheng, Rong Tang

Abstract
. In this paper, first we give the controlling algebra of Lie triple systems. In particular, the cohomology of Lie triple systems can be characterized by the controlling algebra. Then using controlling algebras, we introduce the notions of homotopy Nambu algebras and homotopy Lie triple systems. We show that 2 2 -term homotopy Lie triple systems is equivalent to Lie triple 2 2 -systems, and the latter is the categorification of a Lie triple system. Finally we study skeletal and strict Lie triple 2 2 -systems. We show that skeletal Lie triple 2 2 -systems can be classified the third cohomology group, and strict Lie triple 2 2 -systems are equivalent to crossed modules of Lie triple systems.



1.
Introduction

Lie triple systems were originated from the research of symmetric spaces
[
5
]
. Jacobson firstly studied this system algebraically and named it Lie triple system
[
11
]
. Lister constructed a structure theory of Lie triple systems in
[
13
]
. The representation theory of Lie triple systems was given in
[
10
]
. Lie triple systems are very important algebraic structures and have closed connection with many other algebraic structures, such as Nambu algebras
[
7
]
, Leibniz algebras, Jordan algebras
[
4
]
and Lie algebras
[
20
]
. On the one hand, a Lie triple system is a special Nambu algebra. On the other hand, there is a Leibniz algebra structure on the space of fundamental objects. Lie triple systems also play important roles in numerical
analysis of differential equations
[
15
]
.

The cohomology of an algebraic structure is very important since it can endow invariants. In particular, the second cohomology group can classify deformation and extension problems.
Harris developed a cohomology theory of Lie triple systems using the universal enveloping algebra the Ext functor in
[
9
]
. On the other hand, Yamaguti constructed the representation and cohomology theory for Lie triple systems in
[
23
]
, without going out of a Lie triple system into an enveloping Lie algebra. Then Zhang explained this cohomology using the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of the corresponding Leibniz algebra of fundamental objects in
[
24
]
, which makes the cohomology of Lie-triple systems well understood. Deformations of Lie triple systems were further studied in
[
12
,
22
]
.

In general, there is another approach to give a cohomology theory of an algebraic structure, namely using the controlling algebra. A controlling algebra of an algebraic structure is a graded Lie algebra (sometimes,
L_{\infty}
-algebra) whose Maurer-Cartan elements are the given algebraic structure. For example, the controlling algebra for Lie algebra structures on a vector space
V
is given by the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}
on the graded vector space
\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}V,V)

[
16
]
, and the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}
of a Lie algebra
(V,\mu)
can be obtained by
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}f=(-1)^{k-1}[\mu,f]_{\mathsf{NR}}
for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V,V)
.

The first purpose of this paper is devoted to study the controlling algebra of Lie triple systems, and apply it to the existing cohomology theory. Observe that a Lie triple system is a special Nambu algebra, while the controlling algebra of Nambu algebras was already given in
[
17
]
. This motivate us to solve this problem by figuring out a subalgebra of the graded Lie algebra given in
[
17
]
. We further justify it by showing that the coboundary operator of a Lie triple system with coefficients in itself can be characterized by this controlling algebra structure.

As soon as there is the controlling algebra of an algebraic structure, we can introduce the homotopy of the given algebraic structure via replace a vector space by a graded vector space. For example, on the graded vector space
\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathsf{S}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})
associated to a graded vector space
\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}
, there is also the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket which makes it into a graded Lie algebra. Maurer-Cartan elements of this graded Lie algebra is exactly homotopy Lie algebra structures (also called
L_{\infty}
-algebras). Guided by this philosophy, we introduce the notion of homotopy Nambu algebras and homotopy Lie triple systems. We show that a homotopy Nambu algebra and a homotopy Lie triple system naturally gives rise to a
Leibniz_{\infty}
-algebra, which generalizes the classical result to the homotopy version. See
[
26
]
for the study of 3-Lie-infty-algebras.

Then we focus on the 2-term case and enrich 2-term homotopy Lie triple systems to be a 2-category. On the other hand, it is well known that 2-term homotopy algebras are equivalent to the categorification of the algebraic structure. Thus it is natural to study the categorification of Lie triple systems. See
[
2
]
for more details of categorification of Lie algebras. We introduce the notion of Lie triple 2-systems and show that the 2-category of 2-term homotopy Lie triple systems is equivalent to the 2-category of Lie triple 2-systems. We further classify skeletal Lie triple 2-systems using the third cohomology group of a Lie triple system, and show that strict Lie triple 2-systems are equivalent to crossed modules of Lie triple systems. Recently, 2-term
L_{\infty}
-triple algebras and the categorification of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was given in
[
25
]
using a different approach.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the controlling algebra of Lie triple systems. In Section
3
, we introduce the notions of homotopy Nambu algebras and homotopy Lie triple systems. In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of Lie triple 2-systems, and show that the 2-category of 2-term homotopy Lie triple systems is equivalent to the 2-category of Lie triple 2-systems. In Section
5
, first we classify skeletal Lie triple 2-systems using the third cohomology group of a Lie triple system. Then we introduce the notion of crossed modules of Lie triple systems, and show that strict Lie triple 2-systems are equivalent to crossed modules of Lie triple systems.

2.
The controlling algebra of Lie triple systems

In this section, we give the controlling algebra of Lie triple systems, i.e. a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are Lie triple systems. The main tools we use is the controlling algebras of Leibniz algebras and Nambu algebras.

A
Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
endowed with a linear map
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\,\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. Consider the graded vector space
CL^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}CL^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. It is known that
CL^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the
Balavoine bracket

(2)

\displaystyle[P,Q]_{\mathsf{B}}=P\bar{\circ}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\bar{\circ}P,\,\,\,\,\forall P\in CL^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in CL^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

is a graded Lie algebra (
[
3
]
), where
P\bar{\circ}Q\in CL^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

P\bar{\circ}Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}P\circ_{k}Q,

and
\circ_{k}
is defined by

(3)

\displaystyle(P\circ_{k}Q)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{(k-1)q}(-1)^{\sigma}P(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k-1)},Q(x_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}),x_{k+q+1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1}).

In particular, for
\Omega\in CL^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle([\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}})(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=2\Big(\Omega(\Omega(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})-\Omega(x_{1},\Omega(x_{2},x_{3}))+\Omega(x_{2},\Omega(x_{1},x_{3}))\Big).

Thus,
\Omega
defines a Leibniz algebra structure if and only if
[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}}=0
.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
)
A
Nambu algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
endowed with a linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(4)

\displaystyle[x,y,[z,w,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},w,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[z,[x,y,w]_{\mathfrak{g}},u]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[z,w,[x,y,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Let
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2p+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geq 0}C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Define a graded linear map
\Phi:C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,CL^{p}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g})
by

(5)

\displaystyle(\Phi f)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p},x\otimes y):=f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p},x)\otimes y+x\otimes f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p},y),

for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
f\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),
which is obviously a graded injective linear map.
It was proved in
[
17
]

that
\mathrm{Im}\Phi
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(CL^{*}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
.
Consequently, one can transfer the graded Lie algebra structure on
\mathrm{Im}\Phi
to a graded Lie algebra structure
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket
on
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
via

\displaystyle\left\llbracket P,Q\right\rrbracket=\Phi^{-1}\big([\Phi(P),\Phi(Q)]_{\mathsf{B}}\big),\,\,\forall P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

More precisely, the graded vector space
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geq 0}C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with
the graded bracket operation

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\times C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

defined by

\left\llbracket P,Q\right\rrbracket=P\circ Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\circ P
, where
P\circ Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

P\circ Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{(k-1)q}P\circ_{k}Q,

and for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the operation
\circ_{k},~k=1,\cdots,p,
is defined by

(6)

\displaystyle(P\circ_{k}Q)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q})\otimes y_{k+q},\mathfrak{X}_{k+q+1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},x_{k+q}\otimes Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},y_{k+q}),\mathfrak{X}_{k+q+1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x\right),

and
\circ_{p+1}
is defined by

(7)

\displaystyle(P\circ_{p+1}Q)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q)},x)\right),

is a graded Lie algebra.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
17
]

Maurer-Cartan elements of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
are the Nambu algebra structures on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

For
\pi\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},~x\in\mathfrak{g},
we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\pi,\pi\right\rrbracket(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{2},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\pi\circ\pi)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{2},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\pi\big(\pi(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2}),y_{2},x\big)+\pi\big(x_{2},\pi(x_{1},y_{1},y_{2}),x\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+2\Big(-\pi\big(x_{1},y_{1},\pi(x_{2},y_{2},x)\big)+\pi\big(x_{2},y_{2},\pi(x_{1},y_{1},x)\big)\Big),

which indicates that
\pi
is a Nambu algebra structure on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\left\llbracket\pi,\pi\right\rrbracket=0
. The proof is finished.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

With the above notations,
\Phi
is homomorphism from the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
to the graded Lie algebra
(CL^{*}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
. Consequently, there is a Leibniz algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
associated to a Nambu algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, which is defined by

(8)

\displaystyle[x\otimes y,z\otimes w]:=[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes w+z\otimes[x,y,w]_{\mathfrak{g}},\,\,\,\forall x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

See
[
7
,
8
]
for more details for the relation between Leibniz algebras and Nambu algebras and their applications.

Definition 2.4
.

[
11
]

A
Lie triple system
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
endowed with a linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(9)

\displaystyle[x,x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,

(10)

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y,z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[z,x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,

(11)

\displaystyle[x,y,[z,w,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},w,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[z,[x,y,w]_{\mathfrak{g}},u]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[z,w,[x,y,u]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

We note that Lie triple systems are specific classes of Nambu algebras. In order to characterise the Lie triple system structures on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, we should choose a graded Lie subalgebra of
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. For this purpose, consider the graded subspace
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geq 0}\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
of
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, where the space
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is the vector space of linear maps that satisfies the following conditions:

(12)

\displaystyle Q(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p-1},x,x,y)=0,

(13)

\displaystyle Q(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p-1},x,y,z)+Q(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p-1},y,z,x)+Q(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p-1},z,x,y)=0.

Theorem 2.5
.

With above notations,
(\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie subalgebra of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Moreover, its Maurer-Cartan elements are the Lie triple system structures on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By (
6
), for any
P\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~Q\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we deduce that
P\circ_{k}Q\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~k=1,\cdots,p-1
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\Big((-1)^{(p-1)q}P\circ_{p}Q+(-1)^{pq}P\circ_{p+1}Q\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q-1},x,x,y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Nambu-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x),x,y\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},x,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x),y\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p,q-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x,x,y)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{pq+q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},x,x,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},y)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{condition-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\Big((-1)^{(p-1)q}P\circ_{p}Q+(-1)^{pq}P\circ_{p+1}Q\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q-1},x,y,z)

\displaystyle+\Big((-1)^{(p-1)q}P\circ_{p}Q+(-1)^{pq}P\circ_{p+1}Q\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q-1},y,z,x)

\displaystyle+\Big((-1)^{(p-1)q}P\circ_{p}Q+(-1)^{pq}P\circ_{p+1}Q\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q-1},z,x,y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Nambu-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x),y,z\right)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},x,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},y),z\right)

\displaystyle+\underline{(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p,q-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x,y,z)\right)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{pq+q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},x,y,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},z)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},y),z,x\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},y,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},z),x\right)

\displaystyle+\underline{(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p,q-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},y,z,x)\right)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{(-1)^{pq+q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},y,z,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x)\right)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},z),x,y\right)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{(-1)^{(p-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},z,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},x),y\right)}

\displaystyle+\underline{(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p,q-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},z,x,y)\right)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{pq+q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(p-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p-1)},z,x,Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},y)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{condition-1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0.

Thus, we deduce that
P\circ Q\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

For
\pi\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},
we have

\displaystyle\pi(x,x,y)=0,\quad\pi(x,y,z)+\pi(y,z,x)+\pi(z,x,y)=0.

Moreover, by Theorem
2.2
, we deduce that
\pi
is a Lie triple system on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\left\llbracket\pi,\pi\right\rrbracket=0
. The proof is finished.

∎

At the end of this section, we recall the cohomology theory of Lie triple systems, and show that it can be recovered from the above controlling algebra.

Definition 2.6
.

[
23
]

A
representation
of a Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a vector space
V
is a pair
(V;\rho)
, where
\rho:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map satisfying

\displaystyle[\rho(x_{2},x_{1})-\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),\rho(y_{1},y_{2})]=\rho([x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}]_{\mathfrak{g}},y_{2})+\rho(y_{1},[x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho(y_{2},y_{3})\rho(x_{1},y_{1})-\rho(y_{1},y_{3})\rho(x_{1},y_{2})+\big(\rho(y_{2},y_{1})-\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\big)\rho(x_{1},y_{3}).

Example 2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie triple system. The linear map
\mathrm{ad}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by

\mathrm{ad}_{x,y}z=[z,x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

is a representation of the Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, which we call the
adjoint representation
.

An
n
-cochain on a Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with the coefficient in a representation
(V;\rho)
is a linear map

f:\otimes^{2n-1}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
satisfying

(14)

\displaystyle f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-2},x,x,y)=0,

(15)

\displaystyle f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-2},x,y,z)+f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-2},y,z,x)+f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-2},z,x,y)=0,

for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

For all
n\geq 1,
denote the space of
n
-cochains by
C^{n}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g};V).
The coboundary operator
\delta:C^{n}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g};V)\longrightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g};V)
is given by

(16)

\begin{split}&(\delta f)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)\\
={}&\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},[\mathfrak{X}_{j},\mathfrak{X}_{k}],\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[\mathfrak{X}_{j},z]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\big(\rho(y_{j},x_{j})-\rho(x_{j},y_{j})\big)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)\\
&+(-1)^{n+1}\big(\rho(y_{n},z)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},x_{n})-\rho(x_{n},z)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},y_{n})\big),\end{split}

for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},~i=1,2\dotsc,n
and
z\in\mathfrak{g}
.
[\mathfrak{X}_{j},\mathfrak{X}_{k}]
is defined by (
8
). It is proved in
[
23
]
that
\delta\circ\delta=0
.

We assume that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie triple system and set
\pi(x,y,z)=[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Consider the coboundary operator

\delta:C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
associated to the adjoint representation, then we have

Theorem 2.8
.

For all
f\in C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\delta f=(-1)^{n-1}\left\llbracket\pi,f\right\rrbracket,~\forall n=1,2,\dots.

Proof.

For all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},~i=1,2\dotsc,n
and
z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\left\llbracket\pi,f\right\rrbracket(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}(\pi\circ f-(-1)^{n-1}f\circ\pi)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\Bigg(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(0,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\pi\left(f(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(n-1)},x_{n})\otimes y_{n},z\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(0,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\pi\left(x_{n}\otimes f(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(n-1)},y_{n}),z\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(1,n-1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}\pi\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},f(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(2)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(n)},z)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k-1}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}f\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},x_{k+1}\otimes\pi(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},y_{k+1}),\mathfrak{X}_{k+2},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}f\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},\pi(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},x_{k+1})\otimes y_{k+1},\mathfrak{X}_{k+2},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z\right)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(n-1,1)}}(-1)^{\sigma}f\left(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(n-1)},\pi(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(n)},z)\right)\bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\big(\rho(y_{n},z)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},x_{n})-\rho(x_{n},z)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},y_{n})\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\big(\rho(y_{j},x_{j})-\rho(x_{j},y_{j})\big)f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},[\mathfrak{X}_{j},\mathfrak{X}_{k}],\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\hat{\mathfrak{X}}_{j},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[\mathfrak{X}_{j},z]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta f)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{X}_{n},z),

which finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Homotopy Nambu algebras and homotopy Lie triples systems

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}
be a graded vector space. Denote by
\mathsf{T}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})=\oplus_{i=0}^{+\infty}(\otimes^{i}\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})

\bar{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})=\oplus_{i=1}^{+\infty}(\otimes^{i}\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})

\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\bullet}

is the sum of
f_{i}:{{\otimes^{i}}\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}

Set
\mathbf{CL}^{n}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}):=\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\bar{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})
and

\mathbf{CL}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}):=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{CL}^{n}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}).

As the graded version of the Balavoine bracket given in
[
3
]
, the
graded Balavoine bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}}
on the graded vector space
\mathbf{CL}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})
is given
by:

(17)

\displaystyle\qquad[f,g]_{\mathsf{B}}:=f\bar{\circ}g-(-1)^{mn}g\bar{\circ}f,\,\,\,\,\forall f=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}f_{i}\in\mathbf{CL}^{m}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),~g=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}g_{j}\in\mathbf{CL}^{n}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),

where

(18)

\displaystyle f\bar{\circ}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}f_{i}\Big)\bar{\circ}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}g_{j}\Big):=\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\Big(\sum_{i+j=s+1}f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j}\Big),

while
f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j}\in\mathrm{Hom}({{\otimes^{s}}\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})
is defined by

f_{i}\bar{\circ}g_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{i}f_{i}\bar{\circ}_{k}g_{j}

and

is defined by

(19)

\begin{split}&(f_{i}\bar{\circ}_{k}g_{j})(v_{1},\cdots,v_{s})\\
={}&\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,j-1)}}(-1)^{\beta_{k}}\varepsilon(\sigma)f_{i}(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},g_{j}(v_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+j-2)},v_{k+j-1}),v_{k+j},\cdots,v_{s}),\end{split}

where
\beta_{k}=n(v_{\sigma(1)}+v_{\sigma(2)}+\cdots+v_{\sigma(k-1)})
.

Similar as the classical case,
(\mathbf{CL}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
is a graded Lie algebra.

∞

Definition 3.1
.

A
Leibniz
∞
-algebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{G}^{k}
equipped with a collection
(k\geq 1)
of linear maps
\theta_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}
of degree

\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\theta_{k}

(\mathbf{CL}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet},\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
. More precisely, for any homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-i+1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,i-1)}}(-1)^{\gamma_{k}}\varepsilon(\sigma)\theta_{n-i+1}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k-1)},\theta_{i}(x_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+i-2)},x_{k+i-1}),x_{k+i},\cdots,x_{n})=0,

where
\gamma_{k}=x_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+x_{\sigma(k-1)}

∞
-algebra by
(\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet},\{\theta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})

Denote by

\mathbf{C}^{n}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})=\oplus_{p=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}^{n+p-1}(\otimes^{p-1}(\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\bullet})\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\bullet},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}),

Theorem 3.2
.

5.
Skeletal and strict Lie triple
2
-systems

By Theorem
4.11
, we see that Lie triple
2
-systems and
2
-term homotopy Lie triple systems are equivalent. Thus, we will call a
2
-term homotopy Lie triple system a Lie triple
2
-system in the sequel.

A Lie triple
2
-system
(\mathcal{T}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{-1},\mathrm{d},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],J)
is called
skeletal
(
strict
) if
\mathrm{d}=0
(
J=0
).

In this section, first we classify skeletal Lie triple
2
-systems via the third cohomology group. Then, we introduce the notion of a crossed module of Lie triple systems, and show that they are equivalent to strict Lie triple
2
-systems.

Theorem 5.1
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between skeletal Lie triple
2
-systems and quadruples
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),V,\theta,\omega)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie triple system,
V
is a vector space,
\theta
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V,
and
\omega
is a
3
-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
V
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{T}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{-1},\mathrm{d}=0,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],J)
be a skeletal Lie triple
2
-systems. By (h) in Definition
4.3
, we see that
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]|_{\mathcal{T}_{0}}
satisfies the fundamental identity. Thus,
(\mathcal{T}_{0},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]|_{\mathcal{T}_{0}})
is a Lie triple system.
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
also gives rise to a map
\theta:\otimes^{2}\mathcal{T}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{-1}
by

(34)

\theta(x_{1},x_{2})(f)=[f,x_{1},x_{2}].

By (i), (j) and (k) in Definition
4.3
, we deduce that
\theta
is a representation of the Lie triple system
(\mathcal{T}_{0},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]|_{\mathcal{T}_{0}})
on
\mathcal{T}_{-1}
. Finally, by (l) in Definition
4.3
, we get that
J
is a 3-cocycle.

Conversely, given a Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),
a representation
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V,
and a
3
-cocycle
\omega
on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
V
, we define
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\mathfrak{g},

\mathcal{T}_{-1}=V
,
\mathrm{d}=0,
and
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
,
J
by

\displaystyle[x,y,z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle{[f,x,y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)(f),

\displaystyle{[x,f,y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta(x,y)(f),

\displaystyle{[x,y,f]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(y,x)(f)-\theta(x,y)(f),

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega.

Then it is straightforward to deduce that
(\mathcal{T}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{-1},\mathrm{d}=0,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],J)
is a skeletal Lie triple
2
-system. We omit details.
∎

Now we introduce the notion of a crossed module of Lie triple systems and show that they are equivalent to strict Lie triple
2
-systems.

Definition 5.2
.

A
crossed module of Lie triple systems
is a quadruple
((\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\mu,\theta)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
are Lie triple systems,
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a homomorphism of Lie triple systems, and
\theta:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h},f,g,h\in\mathfrak{g},
the following equalities hold:

(35)

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(x,y)(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu(f),x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(36)

\displaystyle\theta(\mu(f),\mu(g))(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h,f,g]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(37)

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(x,\mu(f))(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu(g),x,\mu(f)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(38)

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(\mu(f),x)(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu(g),\mu(f),x]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Remark 5.3
.

The more general notion of crossed modules of
n
-Leibniz algebras had been given in
[
6
]
, and the relation with the third cohomology group is established there.

Theorem 5.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between strict Lie triple
2
-systems and crossed modules of Lie triple systems.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{T}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{-1},\mathrm{d},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],J=0)
be a strict Lie triple
2
-system. Define
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{T}_{-1},\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{T}_{0},
and the following two bracket operations on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
:

(39)

\displaystyle~[f,g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g,h]=[\mathrm{d}f,g,\mathrm{d}h]=[f,\mathrm{d}g,\mathrm{d}h],

(40)

\displaystyle~[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z].

It is straightforward to see that both
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
are Lie triple systems. Let
\mu=\mathrm{d},
by Condition (a) in Definition
4.3
, we have

\mu[f,g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{d}[\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g,h]=[\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g,\mathrm{d}h]=[\mu(f),\mu(g),\mu(h)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

which implies that
\mu
is a homomorphism of Lie triple systems. Define
\theta:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\theta(x,y)(f)=[f,x,y].

By Conditions (i), (j) and (k) in Definition
4.3
, we can obtain that
\theta
is a representation. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(x,y)(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta(x,y)(f))=\mathrm{d}[f,x,y]=[\mathrm{d}f,x,y]=[\mu(f),x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\theta(\mu(f),\mu(g))(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h,\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g]=[h,f,g]_{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(x,\mu(f))(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta(x,\mu(f))(g))=\mathrm{d}[g,x,\mu(f)]=[\mathrm{d}g,x,\mu(f)]=[\mu(g),x,\mu(f)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\mu(\theta(\mu(f),x)(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta(\mu(f),x)(g))=\mathrm{d}[g,\mu(f),x]=[\mathrm{d}g,\mu(f),x]=[\mu(g),\mu(f),x]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Therefore we obtain a crossed module of Lie triple systems.

Conversely, a crossed module of Lie triple systems gives rise to a strict Lie triple
2
-system, in which
\mathcal{T}_{-1}=\mathfrak{g},

\mathcal{T}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathrm{d}=\mu
, and the trilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
is given by

\displaystyle[x,y,z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle{[f,x,y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)(f),

\displaystyle{[x,f,y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta(x,y)(f),

\displaystyle{[x,y,f]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(y,x)(f)-\theta(x,y)(f).

The crossed module conditions give various conditions for strict Lie triple
2
-systems. We omit details. The proof is completed.
∎

Volume complexity of dS bubbles Preprint: OU-HET-1171
Roberto Auzzi, Giuseppe Nardelli, Gabriel Pedde Ungureanu, Nicolò Zenoni

Abstract
In the framework of the static patch approach to de Sitter holography introduced in Susskind:2021esx , the growth of holographic complexity has a hyperfast behaviour, which leads to a divergence in a finite time. This is very different from the AdS spacetime, where instead the complexity rate asymptotically reaches a constant value. We study holographic volume complexity in a class of asymptotically AdS geometries which include de Sitter bubbles in their interior. With the exception of the static bubble case, the complexity obtained from the volume of the smooth extremal surfaces which are anchored just to the AdS boundary has a similar behaviour to the AdS case, because it asymptotically grows linearly with time. The static bubble configuration has a zero complexity rate and corresponds to a discontinuous behaviour, which resembles a first order phase transition. If instead we consider extremal surfaces which are anchored at both the AdS boundary and the de Sitter stretched horizon, we find that complexity growth is hyperfast, as in the de Sitter case.

Introduction

The AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena:1997re

theoretical laboratory to address many important open questions
in quantum gravity. However, our observed universe is rather different

It is interesting to investigate generalizations of holography
for de Sitter (dS) spacetime. This is a challenging problem,
because in dS there is no natural notion

With the exception of the very small bubble,
in all cases an observer outside the horizon can not directly
see the dS bubble at any time, because it is
screened by the black hole horizon.
The presence of the dS bubble is instead
detected by the volume complexity,
because the extremal surface always penetrates
the dS region of the geometry.

The traditional way to apply the CV proposal
is to consider the volume of extremal surfaces which are anchored
at the AdS boundary and which are smooth both in the AdS and in the
dS portions of the spacetime. This prescription can be applied
both for large and small bubbles. With the exception of the static bubble configuration,
we find that at late time holographic complexity grows linearly
with the same slope as for the BTZ black hole.
In the fine-tuned case of the static bubble,
we find instead that volume complexity is time-independent.

The time dependence of complexity that we obtain
reveals that the hyperfast growth
is not necessarily related to the exponential growth
of spacetime, which is a feature of large bubbles.
If we consider the prescription in which
smooth extremal surfaces are anchored
just at the AdS boundary, we find that the dS portion of the extremal
surface always remains in the dS static patches,
without entering the regions behind the dS horizon.
This is the reason why the exponential growth
of large bubbles fails to be detected by
volume complexity.

As proposed in
Freivogel:2005qh
,
the boundary dual of a large bubble configuration
should be a a quantum field theory in a mixed state.
This observation can be justified as follows.
For time-reversal symmetric bubbles, eq. (
I.2
) implies that

\lambda\mu\leq\lambda\mu_{0}\leq 1\,,

(I.5)

which is equivalent to

S_{\rm BH}\leq S_{\rm dS}\,,

(I.6)

where
S_{\rm BH}

is the entropy of the external AdS black hole
and
S_{\rm dS}
is the entropy of
the internal dS static patch
S_{\rm dS}
,
i.e.

S_{\rm BH}=2\pi\sqrt{\mu}\,,\qquad S_{\rm dS}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,.

(I.7)

For large bubbles, the interior region contains a portion of the
dS horizon, so the number of degrees of freedom
accessible from the AdS boundary is less than the number of degrees of freedom
of the internal dS region. We are then forced to interpret
large bubbles configurations in AdS/CFT
as gravity duals of a density matrix,
obtained by tracing over a part of the degrees of freedom
of the dS region inside the bubble.

In the context of static patch horizon holography in dS

Susskind:2021esx
;
Shaghoulian:2021cef
;
Shaghoulian:2022fop
,
we can conjecture that the purification of the dual mixed
state is a generalization of thermofield double state

in which the CFT living at the boundary of AdS and the
quantum system living on the stretched dS horizon
are entangled. This suggests another way to apply the CV proposal
in the case of very large bubbles: we can anchor
extremal surfaces both
at the AdS boundary and at the dS static patch stretched horizon.
In this case, we find that extremal surfaces cross the dS horizon
and tend to bend towards the future infinity.
The complexity growth is hyperfast and diverges in finite time,
as in the dS case.

III
Volume functional

According to the CV conjecture
Stanford:2014jda
,
complexity of the boundary state is proportional to the volume
of a maximal codimension-one surface anchored at the given boundary time.
The volume complexity
C_{V}
is usually normalized as

C_{V}=\frac{\mathcal{V}}{GL}\,,

(III.1)

where
\mathcal{V}
is the volume of the maximal slice,
G
the Newton’s constant and
L

the AdS radius.
In this section we discuss the volume of extremal surfaces in both the AdS and the dS parts of the geometry.
Then, we address the matching condition on the domain wall.

Due to spherical symmetry, the extremal surface can be parameterized as

r=r(l)\,,\qquad t=t(l)\,,

(III.2)

where
l
is a single-valued coordinate along the surface.
Since the coordinate
t
is singular nearby the horizons,
it is useful to write the volume functional in both the versions of the EF coordinates
u
and
v
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{i,o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\int\mathcal{L}\,dl\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\,\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(v^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}+2r^{\prime}v^{\prime}_{i,o}}

(III.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\,\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(u^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}-2r^{\prime}u^{\prime}_{i,o}}\,,

where
′
denotes a derivative with respect to
l
.
The extremal surface is characterized by a conserved quantity
P_{i,o}
, which in the
(v,r)
and in the
(u,r)
coordinates reads

\displaystyle P_{i,o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial v^{\prime}_{i,o}}=\frac{r\,(-f_{i,o}v^{\prime}_{i,o}+r^{\prime})}{\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(v^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}+2r^{\prime}v^{\prime}_{i,o}}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{i,o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u^{\prime}_{i,o}}=\frac{r\,(-f_{i,o}u^{\prime}_{i,o}-r^{\prime})}{\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(u^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}-2r^{\prime}u^{\prime}_{i,o}}}\,,

respectively.

The volume functional in eq. (
III.3
) is invariant
under reparameterization in
l
. To fix this gauge freedom,
it is convenient to impose the conditions

\displaystyle\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(v^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}+2r^{\prime}v^{\prime}_{i,o}}=r\qquad{\rm or}

\displaystyle\sqrt{-f_{i,o}(u^{\prime}_{i,o})^{2}-2r^{\prime}u^{\prime}_{i,o}}=r\,,

(III.5)

in such a way that the volume functional becomes

\mathcal{V}_{i,o}=2\pi\int r^{2}\,dl\,.

(III.6)

With this gauge choice, the conserved quantity
P_{i,o}
can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle P_{i,o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{i,o}v^{\prime}_{i,o}+r^{\prime}\,,\qquad v^{\prime}_{i,o}=\frac{r^{\prime}-P_{i,o}}{f_{i,o}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{i,o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{i,o}u^{\prime}_{i,o}-r^{\prime}\,,\qquad u^{\prime}_{i,o}=\frac{-r^{\prime}-P_{i,o}}{f_{i,o}}\,.

By inserting this back into the gauge constraint, we get

\displaystyle(r^{\prime})^{2}+U_{i,o}(r)=P_{i,o}^{2}\,,\qquad U_{i,o}(r)=-f_{i,o}(r)\,r^{2}\,,

\displaystyle r^{\prime}=\pm\sqrt{P_{i,o}^{2}+f_{i,o}(r)\,r^{2}}\,,

(III.8)

which is valid in both the EF coordinate systems
(v,r)
and
(u,r)
.
The conserved quantity
P_{i,o}
can also be written as

P_{i,o}=-f_{i,o}\,t^{\prime}_{i,o}\,.

(III.9)

Note that
U_{i,o}(r)
in eq. (
III.8
)
can be interpreted as effective potentials, see figure
9

for qualitative plots.

Figure 9:
Effective potential for the extremal surface in the dS region (left)
and in the AdS one (right).

The extremum of
r(l)
represents a turning point
r_{t}

for the extremal surface.
We can have a turning point both in the external BTZ region
and in the internal dS one:

•

In the external asymptotically AdS region,
r_{t,{\rm AdS}}
is a minimum of

, given by

r_{t,{\rm AdS}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu+\sqrt{\mu^{2}-4P_{o}^{2}}}{2}}\,.

(III.10)

For a turning point to exist, it is necessary to require

P_{o}^{2}\leq{P}_{\rm max}^{2}=\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}\,.

(III.11)

•

In the internal dS region,
r_{t,{\rm dS}}
is a maximum of
r(l)
,
given by

r_{t,{\rm dS}}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4\lambda\,P_{i}^{2}}}{2\lambda}}\,.

(III.12)

The difference in the time coordinate

t_{i,o}(r_{2})-t_{i,o}(r_{1})=\mp\int^{r_{2}}_{r_{1}}\frac{P_{i,o}}{f_{i,o}\,\sqrt{P_{i,o}^{2}+f_{i,o}(r)\,r^{2}}}dr\,,

(III.13)

where the
-

while the

In the integral in eq. (

the Cauchy principal value prescription should be used

With the convention

\mathcal{V}_{i,o}=2\pi\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\,\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{P_{i,o}^{2}+f_{i,o}(r)\,r^{2}}}\,dr\,.

(III.14)

III.1
A refraction law for the extremal surface

To determine the codimension-one extremal surfaces,
we solve
eqs. (
III.8
) and (
)
both in the interior and in the exterior of the bubble.
Then,
we match the two solutions on top of the domain wall,
imposing that the total volume is extremal.
Physically, the extremal surface is somehow ”refracted” by the
domain wall.
In appendix
B

which describes both the interior and the exterior of the bubble in terms of
the same time coordinate
t_{i}
, we derive the refraction condition
in the thin wall approximation.

By spherical symmetry, it is not restrictive to focus just on
the time and radial coordinates.
We denote by
x^{\mu}_{i,o}(l)
the coordinates of the extremal codimension-one
surface and by
X^{\mu}_{i,o}(\tau)
the trajectory of the domain wall.
We then introduce the tangent vector to the extremal
surface
\frac{dx^{\mu}_{i,o}}{dl}
and the velocity vector of the domain wall
\frac{dX^{\mu}_{i,o}}{d\tau}
, namely

\displaystyle\frac{dx^{\mu}_{i,o}}{dl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t^{\prime}_{i,o}(l),r^{\prime}_{i,o}(l))\,,

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{\mu}_{i,o}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\dot{T}_{i,o}(\tau),\dot{R}(\tau))\,.

(III.15)

The matching condition on top of the domain wall is

(g_{i})_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}_{i}}{dl}\frac{dX^{\nu}_{i}}{d\tau}=(g_{o})_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}_{o}}{dl}\frac{dX^{\nu}_{o}}{d\tau}\,.

(III.16)

The details of the derivation are in appendix
B
.
A similar result was derived in
Iglesias-Zemmour
for geodesics.

Given an extremal surface intersecting the domain wall at some value
of the radial coordinate
R
, we denote by

the value of

computed at the intersection,
i.e.

\rho_{i,o}(R)=r^{\prime}_{i,o}(l_{0})\,\qquad{\rm where}\qquad r_{i,o}(l_{0})=R\,.

(III.17)

By means of eq. (
III.9
),
we can write the matching condition (

P_{i}\frac{d{T}_{i}}{dR}+\frac{\rho_{i}(R)}{f_{i}(R)}=P_{o}\frac{d{T}_{o}}{dR}+\frac{\rho_{o}(R)}{f_{o}(R)}\,.

(III.18)

The setup with an extremal surface crossing
a null shell of matter with negligible thickness
was studied in
Balasubramanian:2011ur
for geodesics
and in
Chapman:2018dem
;
Chapman:2018lsv
for codimension-one surfaces.
This case formally corresponds to a domain wall moving at the speed of light and the result is consistent with eq. (
III.18

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we investigated the time dependence of volume complexity
in a class of asymptotically AdS
3
spacetimes which include a dS
3
bubble in their interior.
We first focused on extremal surfaces attached just at the
AdS boundary and smooth everywhere into the interior spacetime.
With the exception of the static bubble configuration,
we found that complexity asymptotically grows linearly
as a function of time, with the same rate as for the BTZ black hole.
For large bubbles, the asymptotic value of the complexity rate is always reached from below. For small bubbles,
the asymptotic limit can be instead reached either from below (case 1) or from above (case 2), depending on the parameter space
(see section
IV.1.2
).

The static bubble configuration gives rise to a time-independent complexity,
so it does not match the expectation,
generically satisfied by AdS black holes,
that complexity rate at late time is of the same order of magnitude as
TS
,
with
T
the temperature and
S
the entropy of the system
Susskind:2014moa
.
This fine-tuned solution, which is realized for
\mu=\mu_{0}
,
interpolates between the small and the large bubble regimes.
As soon as a small perturbation is introduced,
the static bubble limit can be achieved
in two different ways:

•

If the limit
\mu\to\mu_{0}
in the parameter space is approached
from the large bubble configuration,
the complexity rate remains frozen to zero
for an initial amount of time which tends to infinity for
\mu\to\mu_{0}
,
see the right panel of figure
24
.
From this side, the asymptotic complexity rate of the BTZ black hole
is recovered in a continuous way after an arbitrarily large time.

•

If the limit
\mu\to\mu_{0}
is approached from the small
bubble region of the parameter space,
the static behavior of complexity emerges
from a class of extremal surfaces with non-maximal volume, see figure
23
.
According to the CV prescription,
the non-maximal extremal solutions
should be discarded for
\mu\neq\mu_{0}
in favor of the global maximum,
as shown by the dashed line in figure
17
.
For
\mu\to\mu_{0}
the complexity rate resembles a step function,
thus the limit is discontinuous, as in a first order phase transition.

Interestingly, the discarded solutions would give rise to a negative complexity rate,
as for the two-dimensional centaur geometries studied in
Chapman:2021eyy
.
We may contemplate the possibility that
other physical configurations could exist, which resemble our geometry nearby the discarded extremal surfaces and in which the global maximum is cut away.
If such a surgery were performed, it would give rise to
a negative complexity rate, resembling the two-dimensional case studied
in
Chapman:2021eyy
, in which no black hole horizon is present in the AdS region of the geometry.

With both procedures, for
\mu\to\mu_{0}
the BTZ asymptotic complexity rate
is recovered at late time. The limit is continuous from the large
bubble direction, while it is discontinuous from the small bubble side.
In this sense, the static bubble can be seen as a fine-tuned critical configuration
separating qualitatively different behaviors in the parameter space.

From the point of view of local observables on the AdS boundary,
the static bubble configuration is not distinguishable from
the eternal BTZ black hole. The same holds for entanglement entropy
of subregions located on the AdS boundary.
To detect the discontinuous nature of the static bubble limit,
other quantum information probes, such as holographic complexity, are required.

Contrary to the result found in

Susskind:2021esx
;
Jorstad:2022mls
for dS,
if we consider smooth extremal surfaces attached just to the AdS boundary
there is no hyperfast growth of complexity.
In section
V
,
we checked that hyperfast growth is recovered
in the very large bubble case
if we consider
extremal surfaces anchored both
at the AdS boundary and at the dS stretched horizon.
This choice should correspond to complexity of a pure thermofield
double state which involves both an AdS and a dS boundary.
Defining the boundary time
t_{b}
as in eq. (
V.4
),
we find that the volume complexity diverges at
t_{b}=t_{\rm cr}

given in eq. (
V.26
), independently of the
parameter
\alpha_{t}
in eq. (
V.3
).

It is tempting to suggest the following interpretations for the two different
ways to apply the CV conjecture in AdS geometries with very large dS bubbles:

•

the volume of smooth extremal surfaces
anchored just at the boundary of AdS
is proportional to
the complexity of a mixed CFT state, obtained by
tracing over the dS degrees of freedom in the thermofield double state.
This situation is reminiscent of the
subregion complexity proposal
Alishahiha:2015rta
;
Carmi:2016wjl
;
Agon:2018zso
.

•

the volume of the extremal surface
anchored both at the AdS boundary and at the dS static patch horizon
is proportional to the complexity of the pure product state.

Why the latter choice of boundary conditions is possible just for very large bubbles is an interesting question.
Small bubble solutions do not contain a stretched horizon
in the dS portion of the geometry, so the only implementable prescription
is to attach the extremal surfaces just to the AdS boundary.
In this case, we can conjecture that the dual CFT state
does not arise from a partial trace over a
pure state involving a dS boundary.

(B.10)

With our gauge choice, the conserved quantity can be written as

\hat{P}^{2}=\left(\frac{g+fh^{2}}{f}\right)(r^{\prime})^{2}-gr^{2}\,.

(B.11)

Using eqs. (
B.6
) and (
B.9
), together with the fact that
g
is continuous on the bubble, we find

(r^{\prime}_{i})^{2}=\frac{f_{i}}{f_{o}}(r^{\prime}_{o})^{2}\,.

(B.12)

From eq. (
II.27
), we finally get

P_{o}^{2}=\frac{f_{o}(R)}{f_{i}(R)}P^{2}_{i}=\mu_{0}^{2}\,P_{i}^{2}\,.

(B.13)

B.2
Dynamical bubble

For dynamical bubbles,
the functions
f,g,h
depend on
t_{i}
.
All the derivatives

\partial_{t_{i}}f\,,\qquad\partial_{t_{i}}h\,,\qquad\partial_{r}f\,,\qquad\partial_{r}h\,,

will have a Dirac delta contribution localized on the surface of the bubble

t_{i}=T_{i}(\tau)\,,\qquad r=R(\tau)\,.

We impose the condition that
this delta function contribution is constant on the surface of the bubble:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d\,f(r,t_{i})}{d\tau}\right|_{\rm bubble}=\frac{d\,f(R(\tau),T_{i}(\tau))}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\dot{T}_{i}\,\partial_{t_{i}}f+\dot{R}\,\partial_{r}f=0\,,

(B.14)

which implies

\partial_{t_{i}}f=-\frac{dR}{dT_{i}}\,\partial_{r}f\,.

(B.15)

So we expect

\displaystyle\partial_{r}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{dR}{dT_{i}}\right)^{2}}}\,\delta(r-R(\tau))\,\Delta f\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{i}}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{dR}{dT_{i}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{dR}{dT_{i}}\right)^{2}}}\,\delta(r-R(\tau))\,\Delta f\,,

where
\Delta f
is the discontinuity of
f
on the surface of the bubble.
An analogous equation holds for
h
.

Let us introduce the variables
s
and
w
such that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}t_{i}\\
r\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi\\
\sin\psi&\cos\psi\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}s\\
w\end{array}\right)\,,

(B.16)

where

\displaystyle\sin\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{R}}{\sqrt{\dot{R}^{2}+\dot{T}_{i}^{2}}}\,,\qquad\cos\psi=\frac{\dot{T}_{i}}{\sqrt{\dot{R}^{2}+\dot{T}_{i}^{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle\tan\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dR}{dT_{i}}\,.

(B.17)

The derivative of
f

with respect to
s
is

\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}=\frac{\partial t_{i}}{\partial s}\partial_{t_{i}}f+\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\partial_{r}f=\frac{\dot{T}_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}f+\dot{R}\,\partial_{r}f}{\sqrt{\dot{R}^{2}+\dot{T_{i}}^{2}}}\,.

(B.18)

A similar property is valid for
h
.
From eq. (
B.15
), we thus find
that the relations
\partial_{s}f=0
and
\partial_{s}h=0
are satisfied by the Dirac delta term.

Going back to the volume functional in eq. (
B.7
),
we can express it in the
(s,w)
coordinates.
In the approximation in which we consider just the
”fast” dependence of the Lagrangian due to discontinuities
at the two sides of the domain wall, the Lagrangian density
is independent of
s
.
We have then a conserved quantity
of the form

\hat{P}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial s^{\prime}}=(hr^{\prime}-gt_{i}^{\prime})\cos\psi+\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{f}+ht_{i}^{\prime}\right)\sin\psi\,.

(B.19)

The quantity
\hat{P}
is not globally conserved on the codimension-one extremal surface,
but just before and after the collision with the Dirac delta domain wall.
Plugging the gauge fixing condition (
B.8
) in,
we get

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{g+fh^{2}}{fg}r^{\prime}\sin\psi\pm\frac{(g\cos\psi-h\sin\psi)}{g}\right.

(B.20)

\displaystyle\left.\sqrt{\frac{g+fh^{2}}{f}(r^{\prime})^{2}-g\,r^{2}}\right)^{2}\,.

The conserved quantities inside and outside the domain wall are

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sin\psi}{f_{i}}r_{i}^{\prime}\pm\sqrt{(r_{i}^{\prime})^{2}-f_{i}r^{2}}\cos\psi\right)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{2}_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f_{o}}r_{o}^{\prime}\sin\psi\pm{\left(G\cos\psi+\frac{dG}{dr}\,t_{i}\,\sin\psi\right)}\right.

(B.21)

\displaystyle\left.\sqrt{(r_{o}^{\prime})^{2}-f_{o}\,r^{2}}\right)^{2}\,.

Combining the condition
\hat{P}_{i}^{2}=\hat{P}^{2}_{o}

with eqs. (
B.17
) and (
B.1
), we find

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f_{i}}r_{i}^{\prime}\pm\sqrt{(r_{i}^{\prime})^{2}-f_{i}\,r^{2}}\frac{dT_{i}}{dR}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{f_{o}}r_{o}^{\prime}\pm\frac{dT_{o}}{dR}\sqrt{(r_{o}^{\prime})^{2}-f_{o}\,r^{2}}\right)^{2}\,.

(B.22)

We can consider the following crosschecks of eq. (
B.22
):

•

For
\psi=0
, it reproduces the static bubble result in eq. (
B.13
).

•

For a bubble moving at the speed of light, we have

\frac{dT_{i,o}}{dR}=\pm\frac{1}{f_{i,o}}\,.

(B.23)

Specializing eq. (
B.22
)
to eq. (
B.23
)
and combining with eq. (
),
we find
V^{\prime}_{i}=V^{\prime}_{o}
or
U^{\prime}_{i}=U^{\prime}_{o}
, depending on the choice of sign.
This is in agreement with the results in
Chapman:2018dem

Chapman:2018lsv

Using the notation in eq. (

the refraction condition (

(g_{i})_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}_{i}}{dl}\frac{dX^{\nu}_{i}}{d\tau}=\pm(g_{o})_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}_{o}}{dl}\frac{dX^{\nu}_{o}}{d\tau}\,.

(B.24)

By continuity with the case where there is no bubble into the system,

Appendix C
Technical details for smooth extremal surfaces

C.1
Solution with
P_{o}<0

With the assumption of negative
P_{o}
and with the choice of sign for
\frac{dT_{o}}{dR}
in eq. (
IV.3
), eq. (
IV.2
)
is solved by

\displaystyle P_{o}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{4}+\mu R^{2}+\frac{\left((\mu+1)^{2}+R^{4}\left(\kappa^{2}(\lambda-1)+(\lambda+1)^{2}\right)+R^{2}\left(\kappa^{2}(\mu-1)-2(\lambda+1)(\mu+1)\right)\right)^{2}}{4\kappa^{4}R^{2}\left(1-\lambda R^{2}\right)}\,.

\displaystyle\rho_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{R^{4}\left(\kappa^{2}(\lambda-1)+(\lambda+1)^{2}\right)-R^{2}\left(2(\lambda+1)(\mu+1)-\kappa^{2}(\mu-1)\right)+(\mu+1)^{2}}{2\kappa^{2}R\sqrt{1-\lambda R^{2}}}\,,

(C.1)

A direct calculation gives that
\rho_{o}(R_{\rm max})
in eq. (
C.1
)
is the opposite of
\rho_{o}(R_{\rm max})
in eq. (
IV.4
).
The same holds true for
\rho_{o}(R_{\rm min})
.
So, the solution in eq. (
C.1
) corresponds to a ”reflection” of the codimension-one extremal surface,
and as such should be discarded.
Note that both the solutions in eqs. (
IV.5
)
and (
C.1
) vanish for
r=R_{\rm max}=R_{\rm min}
.

C.2
Sign of
\rho_{o}(R)
for small bubbles

The function
\rho_{o}(R)
in eq. (
IV.4
) vanishes for
R=R_{0}
, where

R_{0}(\mu)=\sqrt{\frac{1-\mu}{\kappa^{2}+\lambda-1}}\,.

(C.2)

Therefore,
if either
R_{0}(\mu)
is not a real number or

R_{0}>R_{\rm max}
, the sign of
\rho_{o}(R)

does not change along the surface of the bubble.
Conversely, if
R_{0}(\mu)
is real and
R_{0}<R_{\rm max}
,

\rho_{o}(R)
changes sign along the bubble.
With a direct calculation, it can be checked that

R_{0}(\mu_{s})=\sqrt{\mu_{s}}=R_{\rm max}(\mu_{s})\,.

(C.3)

There are then four possible behaviors of
\rho_{o}(R)
on the domain wall:

A)

\mu<\mu_{s}
and
\lambda+\kappa^{2}>1
. The function

\rho_{o}(R)
is always positive.

B)

\mu<\mu_{s}
and
\lambda+\kappa^{2}<1
. When
R_{0}
is real,

\rho_{o}(R)
is positive for
R_{0}<R\leq R_{\rm max}

and negative for
0<R<R_{\rm 0}
.
When
R_{0}
is imaginary,
\rho_{o}(R)
is always positive.

C)

\mu>\mu_{s}
and
\lambda+\kappa^{2}>1
.
When
R_{0}
is real,

\rho_{o}(R)
is negative for
R_{0}<R\leq R_{\rm max}

and positive for
0<R<R_{\rm 0}
.
When
R_{0}
is imaginary,
\rho_{o}(R)
is always negative.

D)

\mu>\mu_{s}
and
\lambda+\kappa^{2}<1
. The function

\rho_{o}(R)
is always negative.

With reference to subsection
IV.1.2
,
from these considerations we find that
the region of parameter space in bullet A) belongs to case 2,
while the part of parameter space in bullet D) belongs to case 1.

C.3
Behavior of the complexity rate for

In this appendix, we determine the explicit value of the quantity

).

Let us first consider very small bubbles.
Since for

,
we should use eq. (

) to compute

.
By symmetry,

(C.4)

where

(C.5)

and

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1-\lambda R_{\rm max}^{2}}\,(1+\mu-R_{\rm max}^{2}(1+\lambda+\kappa^{2}))\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\rm max}^{2}(R_{\rm min}^{2}-R_{\rm max}^{2})(R_{\rm max}^{2}-\mu)

(C.6)

\displaystyle(\lambda+(\kappa-1)^{2})(\lambda+(\kappa+1)^{2})\,.

We can use the explicit integral

\displaystyle F(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{-1}{r(r^{2}-\mu)^{3/2}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu\sqrt{r^{2}-\mu}}-\frac{2}{\mu^{3/2}}\tan^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{r+\sqrt{\mu}}{r-\sqrt{\mu}}}

(C.8)

In the case of not so small bubbles,

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H-\int^{\Lambda}_{r_{t,{\rm AdS}}+\delta}\frac{r^{2}}{(P_{o}^{2}+f_{o}(r)r^{2})^{3/2}}dr

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{r_{t,{\rm AdS}}+\delta}\frac{r^{2}}{(P_{o}^{2}+f_{o}(r)r^{2})^{3/2}}dr

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{o}^{2}+(r_{t,{\rm AdS}}+\delta)^{2}\,f_{o}(r_{t,{\rm AdS}}+\delta)}}

\displaystyle=

Consistently, eqs. (
C.8
)
and (

where

and

\displaystyle\tilde{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{2\sqrt{1-\lambda R_{\rm min}^{2}}\,(1+\mu-R_{\rm min}^{2}(1+\lambda+\kappa^{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda+(\kappa-1)^{2})(\lambda+(\kappa+1)^{2})\,.

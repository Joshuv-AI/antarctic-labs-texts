Off–Diagonal Two Weight Bumps for Fractional Sparse Operators
Rob Rahm

Abstract
. In this paper, we continue some recent work on two weight boundedness of sparse operators to the "off–diagonal" setting. We use the new "entropy bumps" introduced in by Treil–Volberg ( [ 22 ] ) and improved by Lacey–Spencer ( [ 9 ] ) and the "direct comparison bumps" introduced by Rahm–Spencer ( [ 20 ] ) and improved by Lerner ( [ 12 ] ). Our results are "sharp" in the sense that they are sharp in various particular cases. A feature is that given the current machinery and advances, the proofs are almost trivial.

1.
Introduction

The topic of this article is two–weight bump conditions for sparse operators
in the “off–diagonal” setting (i.e
q>p
).
We continue the line of investigation
concerning entropy bumps that began with Treil–Volberg in
[
22
]

and was continued in
[
9
,
19
,
20
]
and the
line of investigation concerning "direct comparison bumps" introduced in

[
20
]
and continued and improved in
[
12
]
.

One innovation that needs to be mentioned is that all of the results
apply to general measures, rather than to measures that are absolutely
continuous and have a density. Throughout,
\mu
and
\nu
are two locally finite
measures. Quantities like

\langle\nu f\rangle_{Q}
mean
\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}fd\nu
. And the maximal function
applied to
\nu
(i.e.
M\nu(x)
is defined using the averages

\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}:=\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}d\nu
).

We will be concerned with sparse
operators of the form (
0\leq\alpha<d
):

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}\nu f:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}(\left|Q\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\left|\nu f\right|\rangle_{Q})1\!\!1_{Q},\hskip 36.135pt\textnormal{ where }\hskip 36.135pt\langle\nu f\rangle_{Q}:=\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}fd\nu.

A more general class of these operators are studied in
[
6
]
.
Unlike that paper, we make no
assumption about the weights being in
A_{\infty}
and we only concentrate
on the off–diagonal case.

There are many results like this in the literature.
The main contributions of this paper
are (1) the results are new; (2) the proofs are succinct and highlight the development
of this area and (3) indicate the challenges in proving various "separated bump
theorems".

The first two deal with entropy bumps. It was proven in the
p=q=2
case in

[
22
]
and in the general

p=q
case in
[
9
]
; both with
\alpha=0
. There is also a
version in
[
19
]
that has the "wrong" homogeneity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}
be a sparse operator and

1<p<q<\infty
then

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{q}(\nu)\right\|\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{A_{p,q},\mathcal{S}}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}^{\frac{1}{q}}+[\mu,\nu]_{A_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}},\mathcal{S}}[\mu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where

\displaystyle[\nu,\mu]_{A_{p,q},\mathcal{S}}:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\mu(Q)^{\frac{1}{q}}\nu(Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}}

and

\displaystyle\rho(Q;\nu):=\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{P}M(\nu 1\!\!1_{P})\hskip 36.135pt\textnormal{and}\hskip 36.135pt[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(Q;\nu),

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}
be a sparse operator and

1<p<q<\infty
then:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\nu)\right\|\lesssim S_{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}(\mu,\nu)+S_{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{E}_{q^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\nu,\mu),

where:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,q}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{Q}\frac{\mu(Q)^{\frac{1}{q}}\nu(Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\rho(Q;\nu)^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon(\rho(Q;\nu))^{\frac{1}{q}}

and
\varepsilon
is a monotonic increasing function that satisfies

S_{\mathcal{E}}:=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon(2^{r})^{-1}<\infty.

The next theorem was introduced in
[
20
]
and improved in

[
12
]
(for
p=q
and
\alpha=0
):

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}
be a sparse operator and

1<p<q<\infty
and
p>1
then:

\displaystyle\left\|T\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\nu)\right\|\lesssim S_{\mathcal{D}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\mu,\nu)+S_{\mathcal{D}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{D}_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\nu,\mu),

where:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\mu,\nu):=\sup\frac{{\mu(Q)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\nu(Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\varepsilon(\langle\nu\rangle_{Q})^{\frac{1}{q}}.

and
\varepsilon
is a function that is decreasing on
(0,1)
increasing on

(1,\infty)
and satisfies

S_{\mathcal{D}}:=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\varepsilon(2^{r})^{-1}
.

2.
Background and Discussion

It was noted in
[
14
,
7
,
13
]
that the standard Muckenhoupt condition
was necessary but not sufficient for the two–weight boundedness of operators of interest. The
purpose of the various "bumps" are to replace the standard

A_{p}
(or
A_{p,q}
in our case) condition with a slightly bigger condition that is
sufficient to give two–weight boundedness. This is a well–developed area,
see
[
16
,
18
,
4
,
10
,
15
,
11
,
3
,
2
,
5
,
1
]
and the references therein for
more information.

The "entropy bumps" in Theorem
1.2
were introduced by Treil–Volberg in

[
22
]
in the
p=q=2
case. This was extended to the general
p=q
case
by Lacey–Spencer in
[
9
]
.
These operators were also studied in the off–diagonal
p\leq q
,
\alpha>0
setting
in
[
19
,
17
]
.
Our theorem here is "sharp" in the sense that when

\nu
and
\mu
are
A_{\infty}
, we recover the
sharp results of, for example
[
3
,
2
,
8
]
.
The "direct comparison bumps" were introduced in
[
20
]
and improved in

[
12
]
.

One feature of the proofs is that they
only
require
A_{p,q}
and

A_{\infty}
data for the cubes in the sparse collection. Compare this with the
proofs in, for example,
[
6
]
that require the weight be in

A_{\infty}
. This small difference is what allows us to do the "bootstrapping"
argument in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In addition, it is
the off–diagonal setting that allows us to replace the normal norm in the testing
inequalities with
L^{1}
norms (this is Proposition
3.1
below; see also

[
2
]
*Theorem 1.1 and similar ideas in
[
1
]
). This is why off–diagonal
results are sharper than
on–diagonal results. Indeed, in the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
if we had to work with
L^{q}
norms in the testing constants, we would have to use the
triangle inequality
to estimate the sum over
a\geq 0
and this would require that
\sum_{a\geq 0}\varepsilon(2^{a})^{-\frac{1}{q}}
be finite – and this is a stronger assumption than
what we have here (indeed: this is exactly what happens in
[
19
]

in the on–diagonal setting).
Based on comparisons with Orlicz conjectures, the following conjecture is made
(Lerner almost proves this in
[
12
]
):

Conjecture 2.1
.

\left\|T\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\nu)\to L^{p}(\mu)\right\|\lesssim\mathcal{D}_{p,p}(\nu,\mu)+\mathcal{D}^{\ast}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mu,\nu)
.

The Orlicz, entropy, and direct comparison bumps are not strictly comparable. The
Orlicz bumps are the most established but require the most in terms of local
integrability (and they apply only to measures with densities). The entropy bumps
are guaranteed to be bounded in the one weight setting (and record important
A_{\infty}

information about the operator norms). The direct comparison bumps require the least
in terms of local integrabilty but are not as popular and the information
they record (comparison to Lebesgue measure) does not seem to be as relevant
as
A_{\infty}
data in the weighted theory. An advantage is that they are easier
to verify than Orlicz bumps or entropy bumps. The measures

\displaystyle d\nu(x)=\frac{dx}{x(1-\log x)^{2}},\hskip 36.135ptd\mu(x)=x^{2}dx

satisfy the two weight conditions in
[
12
]
or even in
[
20
]
. Yet

\nu
is not in
L\log L
and so neither the Orlicz nor the entropy bumps will detect
the boundedness of sparse operators with these weights.

3.
Preliminaries and Notation

A collection of cubes,
\mathcal{S}
, is called
\lambda
–Sparse (
0<\lambda<1
) if
for every
Q\in\mathcal{S}
there is a set
E_{Q}\subset Q
with

\left|E_{Q}\right|\geq\lambda\left|Q\right|
and the sets
\{E_{Q}:Q\in\mathcal{S}\}
are pairwise
disjoint. If
R\in\mathcal{S}
then using
the pairwise disjointness of the
\{E_{Q}\}
we have the following well–know estimate:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:Q\subset R}\nu(Q)\simeq\int_{R}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}:Q\subset R}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}1\!\!1_{E_{Q}}\leq\int_{R}M(\nu 1\!\!1_{R})=\rho(R;\nu)\nu(R).

A consequence of
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.1 is (note the
L^{1}
norms):

Proposition 3.1
.

For
1<p<q
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}(\nu\cdot):L^{p}(\nu)\to L^{q}(\mu)\right\|\lesssim\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{T}^{\ast}

where:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathcal{S}\textnormal{ is sparse}}\sup_{R\in\mathcal{S}}\nu(R)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}(\left|Q\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu 1\!\!1_{R}\rangle_{Q})^{q}1\!\!1_{E_{Q}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathcal{S}\textnormal{ is sparse}}\sup_{R\in\mathcal{S}}w(R)^{-1/q^{\prime}}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}(\left|Q\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\mu\rangle_{Q})^{p^{\prime}}1\!\!1_{E_{Q}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

Indeed,
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.1 says that if
p<q
then:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{{\alpha,\mathcal{S}}}\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\nu)\to L^{q}(\mu)\right\|\simeq\left\|M_{{\alpha}}\nu\cdot:L^{p}(\nu)\to L^{q}(\mu)\right\|+\left\|M_{{\alpha}}\nu\cdot:L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\nu)\right\|,

where

M_{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{Q}\left|Q\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\left|f\right|\rangle_{Q}1\!\!1_{Q}(x)

is the fractional maximal operator. The well–known (
[
21
]
) testing
conditions for this operator reduce to the ones in Proposition
3.1
. This
is because the linearization of
M_{\alpha}
is the function inside the norm
in this proposition and the pairwise
disjointness of the
\{E_{Q}\}
allows us to pass the exponent
q
(and
p^{\prime}
)
under the sum.
∎

4.
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

We begin with a lemma that will be used in the proof of both of
these theorems.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a sparse collection. With notation as established
above, for every
R\in\mathcal{S}
there holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
be the maximal cubes in
\mathcal{S}
contained
in
R
.
The sum above can be organized as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)=\sum_{Q^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}(\frac{\mu(Q)\nu(Q)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{q-q\frac{\alpha}{d}}})\nu(Q)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

The inner sum is dominated by:

\displaystyle[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}\nu(Q)^{\frac{q}{p}}\leq[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}\nu(Q^{\ast})^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}\nu(Q)\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}\nu(Q^{\ast})^{\frac{q}{p}}

The "
\leq
" is trivial and the "
\lesssim
" uses (
3.1
).
Using the fact that
\frac{q}{p}>1
. Using the maximality of the cubes
Q^{\ast}

this can be summed over
Q^{\ast}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
to

[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We show

\mathcal{T}\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}^{\frac{1}{q}}

(the estimate
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\lesssim[\mu,\nu]_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime},\mathcal{S}}[\mu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}
is dual).
Observe that we can estimate the
q^{\textnormal{th}}
power of the norm in the
definition of
\mathcal{T}
using Lemma
4.1
as

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}^{q}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Taking
q^{\textnormal{th}}
roots we conclude that

\mathcal{T}\lesssim[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}}^{\frac{1}{q}}
as
claimed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

\mathcal{T}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)

(the estimate
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mu,\nu)
is dual).
Let
\mathcal{S}_{a}
be those cubes with
2^{a}<\rho(Q;\nu)\leq 2^{a+1}
.
Using Lemma
4.1
, the

q^{\textnormal{th}}
power of the norm in the
definition of
\mathcal{T}
can be estimated as follows

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)=\sum_{a\geq 0}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)\lesssim\sum_{a\geq 0}[\nu,\mu]_{p,q,\mathcal{S}_{a}}^{q}[\nu]_{A_{\infty},\mathcal{S}_{a}}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}

Multiplying and dividing the summands by
\varepsilon(2^{a})
and using
the fact that for the cubes in question,
\varepsilon(2^{a})\leq\varepsilon(\rho(Q;\nu))
and

\rho(Q;\nu)\simeq 2^{a}
, the summands are estimated as:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon(2^{a})}\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}}\left(\frac{\mu(Q)^{\frac{1}{q}}\nu(Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\rho(Q;\nu)^{\frac{1}{q}}\varepsilon(\rho(Q;\nu))^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{q}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon(2^{a})}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)^{q}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Using the summability of
\varepsilon
this can be
summed in
a
to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)^{q}\nu(R)^{\frac{q}{p}}
. Taking
q^{\textnormal{th}}
roots gives the desired estimate.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We will show

\mathcal{T}\lesssim\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)
(the estimate

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\lesssim\mathcal{D}_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mu.\nu)
is dual).
Let
\mathcal{S}_{a}
be those cubes with
2^{a}<\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}\leq 2^{a+1}
.
(observe that
-\infty<r<\infty
) and let
\mathcal{S}_{a}^{\ast}
be the maximal cubes
in
\mathcal{S}_{a}
. As above, the
q^{\textnormal{th}}
power of the
norm in the definition of
\mathcal{T}
can be
organized as follows:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}:Q\subset R}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}_{a}^{\ast}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}\left|Q\right|^{q\frac{\alpha}{d}}\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}^{q}\mu(Q).

Concerning the inner sum, this is (similar to the above):

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{a}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}(\frac{\mu(Q)\nu(Q)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}}{\left|Q\right|^{q-q\frac{\alpha}{d}}})\nu(Q)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)^{q}\frac{1}{\varepsilon(2^{a})}\nu(Q^{\ast})^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{r}:Q\subset Q^{\ast}}\nu(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)^{q}\frac{\nu(Q^{\ast})^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\varepsilon(2^{a})},

where for the "
\lesssim
" we multiplied and divided by

\varepsilon(2^{a})
and used the fact that

\langle\nu\rangle_{Q}\simeq 2^{a}
. The “
\leq
” is obtained using sparseness
combined with
\nu(Q)\simeq 2^{a}\left|Q\right|
for the cubes in question.
Similar to the above proof, the summability condition on
\varepsilon
implies that

\mathcal{T}\lesssim S_{\mathcal{D}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{p,q}(\nu,\mu)
.

Truncated Theta Series Related to the Jacobi Triple Product Identity Thanks: B.F. partially supported by Simons Foundation Collaboration Grant 635835
Cristina Ballantine, Brooke Feigon

Abstract
. The work of Andrews and Merca on the truncated Euler’s pentagonal number theorem led to a resurgence in research on truncated theta series identities. In particular, Yee proved a truncated version of the Jacobi Triple Product (JTP) identity. Recently, Merca conjectured a stronger form of the truncated JTP identity. In this article we prove the first three cases of the conjecture and several related truncated identities. We prove combinatorially an identity related to the JTP identity which in particular cases reduces to identities conjectured by Merca and proved analytically by Krattenthaler, Merca and Radu. Moreover, we introduce a new combinatorial interpretation for the number of distinct 5 5 -regular partitions of n n .

1.
Introduction

A partition
\lambda
of
n
is a non-increasing sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of positive integers that add up to
n
. We refer to the integers
\lambda_{i}
as the parts of
\lambda
. As usual, we denote by
p(n)
the number of partitions of
n
. Since the empty partition
\emptyset
is the only partition of
0
, we have
p(0)=1
. For a thorough introduction to the theory of partitions we refer the reader to
[
1
,
4
]
.

We use the Pochhammer symbol: for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}

(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j}).

The generating function for the sequence
p(n)
is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{i}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

Throughout this article, we assume
q
is a complex number with
|q|<1
so that all products and series converge absolutely.

The earliest theta series identity is likely Euler’s pentagonal number theorem
[
1
, Eq. (1.3.1)]
:

(1)

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j-1)/2}=(q;q)_{\infty}.

Multiplying both sides in (
1
) by
1/(q;q)_{\infty}
, leads to the recurrence relation

p(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-1}p(n-j(3j\pm 1)/2)

for
n\geq 1
.

In 1951, Shanks
[
18
]
considered a truncation of the theta series occurring in (
1
) and proved that

(2)

\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j-1)/2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j}(q;q)_{k}q^{\binom{j+1}{2}+jk}}{(q;q)_{j}}.

With few exceptions
[
19
,
3
]
the subject was dormant for more than half a century. Then, in 2012, Andrews and Merca
[
5
]
obtained a new truncation of Euler’s pentagonal number theorem. They showed that

(3)

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}q^{j(3j+1)/2}(1-q^{2j+1})=1+(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\binom{k}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\begin{bmatrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{bmatrix},

where

\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}}&\text{if $0\leqslant k\leqslant n$},\\
\ \\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

As explained in
[
5
]
, combinatorially, (
3
) means that

(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=-(k-1)}^{k}(-1)^{j}p(n-j(3j-1)/2)=M_{k}(n),

where
M_{k}(n)
is the number of partitions of
n
in which
k
is the smallest integer that is not a part of the partition and there are more parts greater than
k
than there are less than
k
.

Work of Merca
[
17
, Corollary 17]
shows that Shank’s truncation (
2
) leads to the following inequality which holds for all
n,k\geq 1
:

(4)

(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}p(n-j(3j-1)/2)\geq 0.

Xia and Zhao
[
22
]
showed that the left hand side of (
4
) equals the number of partitions of
n
in which every integer less than or equal to
k
appears as a part at least once and the first part larger than
k
appears at least
k+1
times.

The work of Andrews and Merca opened up the study of truncated theta series and linear partition inequalities and many articles followed. For example, Guo and Zeng
[
10
]
obtained truncations of Gauss’ identities
[
1
, (2.2.12), (2.2.13)]
, and Yee
[
23
]
and also Wang and Yee
[
21
]
found a truncation of the Jacobi triple product identity

(5)

\frac{1}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{Rn(n-1)/2+Sn}=1,

where
R
and
S
are positive integers with
1\leq S<R/2
.
Wang and Yee show explicitly that the truncations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-(m-1)}^{m}(-1)^{n}q^{Rn(n-1)/2+Sn}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-m}^{m}(-1)^{n}q^{Rn(n-1)/2+Sn},

when expanded as
q
-series, have non-negative coefficients.

Motivated by these results, Merca
[
14
]
investigated truncations of Jacobi’s triple product written in the form

(6)

\frac{1}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{Rn(n-1)/2+Sn}-(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}=0

and made a stronger conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

[
14
, Conjecture 4.3]
For
1\leq S<R
,
k\geq 1
, the theta series

(7)

\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2-Sj}\big(1-q^{S(2j+1)}\big)

has non-negative coefficients.

We define

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{S,R,k}(n)q^{n}:=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(q^{S};q^{R})_{\infty}(q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{j(j+1)R/2-jS}(1-q^{(2j+1)S})

(8)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{k-1}(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty},

and we write
a_{k}(n)
for
a_{S,R,k}(n)
when
S
and
R
are clear from the context.
As mentioned in
[
14
]
, Conjecture
1
is equivalent to showing
a_{S,R,k}(n)\geq 0
for all
n\geq 0
,
k\geq 1
,
1\leq S<R
.

In this article we prove Conjecture
1
for
k\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
k\in\{1,2,3\}
and
1\leq S<R
, then the theta series (
7
) has non-negative coefficients.

Let

(9)

\rho_{R}(n):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}&\text{ if }n=Ri(3i-1)/2\text{ for some }i\in\mathbb{Z}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, (
1
) with
q
replaced by
q^{R}
, implies that
(q^{R};q^{R})_{\infty}
is the generating function for the sequence
\rho_{R}(n)
.

When
R=3,S=1
,
for all
n\geq 0,k\geq 1
, we have

(10)

a_{1,3,k}(n)=(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=-(k-1)}^{k}(-1)^{j}b_{3}(n-j(3j-1)/2)-(-1)^{k-1}\rho_{3}(n),

where
b_{\ell}(n)
denotes the number of
\ell
-regular partitions of
n
, i.e., partitions with no parts divisible by
\ell
.

Remark 1
.

From
[
5
, Lemma 1.2]
it follows that the
q
-series on the right hand side of (
1
) for
R=3,S=1
equals

(q^{3};q^{3})_{\infty}\sum_{n\geq 0}^{\infty}M_{k}(n)q^{n}.

Hence, if Conjecture
1
is true for
R=3
and
S=1
, then for
k\geq 1
,

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(M_{k}(n-T_{3j})-M_{k}(n-T_{3j+2}))\geq 0,

where
T_{j}=j(j+1)/2
is the
j
th triangular number.

We prove the following related theorem, which is weaker than Conjecture
1
for
R=3
,
S=1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
. Then for all
n\geq 0
,

(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}b_{3}(n-j(3j-1)/2)+(-1)^{k-1}\rho_{3}(n)\geq 0.

Note that Theorem
1.2
together with (
10
) shows that for all
n\geq 0
,
k\geq 1
,

a_{1,3,k}(n)+b_{3}(n-k(3k-1)/2)\geq 0.

Following the proof of Theorem
1.2
we also give a combinatorial interpretation for
a_{1,3,k}(n)+b_{3}(n-k(3k-1)/2)
.

Inequalities for
b_{6}(n)
analogous to those in Theorem
1.2
were conjectured in
[
9
]
. We give a proof of the conjecture.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
9
, Conjecture 3]

For
n\geq 0,k>0

(11)

(-1)^{k-1}\left(\rho_{6}(n)-\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}b_{6}(n-j(3j-1)/2)\right)\geq 0,

with strict inequality if
n\geq(k+1)(3(k+1)-1)/2
.

We also give a combinatorial interpretation of the numbers on the left hand side of (
11
).

Remark 2
.

In
[
9
, Conjecture 3]
, the inequality (
11
) is conjectured to be strict for
n\geq k(3k+1)/2
. This is, in fact, not true as seen from the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

When
R=4,S=1
, by considering the parity of triangular numbers, we see that Conjecture
1
is equivalent to Conjecture 4 in
[

]
, which states that for

the

(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{T_{n}}q^{T_{n+2k}}

has non-negative coefficients. Here we used Euler’s identity

\frac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}=(-q;q)_{\infty}.

We prove that a weaker version of the conjecture is eventually true. As usual, we denote by
Q(n)
the number of partitions of
n
with distinct parts, which by Euler’s identity is also the number of partitions of
n
with odd parts.
Let
c_{1}(n)=Q(n)
and, if
k\geq 2
, let
c_{k}(n)
be the sequence whose generating function is

(12)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(n)q^{n}=(-1)^{k-1}(-q;q)_{\infty}\left((-1)^{k-1}q^{T_{2(k-1)}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}(-1)^{j}(q^{T_{2j}}-q^{T_{2j+1}})\right).

Theorem 1.4
.

If
k\geq 1
there exists

such that

if

Preliminaries and notation

In this section we discuss some background on partitions and set the notation used frequently in the article.

We write
\lambda\vdash n
to mean that
\lambda
is a partition of
n
. We say that
\lambda

n

\lambda

n

|\lambda|

\lambda

\lambda

\lambda

We also work with vectors of partitions and write

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(n)

. In general, if
\mathcal{A}(n)

satisfying certain conditions, we set
\mathcal{A}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{A}(n).
Moreover, if

is a partition counting function, say

, we use the convention that

The Ferrers diagram of a partition

\lambda_{i}

.

such that the inequality is true for

.

5.
Combinatorial proof of an identity related to the Jacobi Triple Product

In this section we give both analytic and combinatorial proofs of an identity which includes as particular cases
the conjectural identities (6.7), (6.10), (6.12), (6.13) in
[
14
]
.

First, we recall the Jacobi Triple Product identity in its general form. See, for example,
[
12
]
where a combinatorial proof is provided.
For integers
m
and
s
such that
0<s<m
, and complex numbers
q
and
z
with
|q|<1
and
z\neq 0
, we have

(18)

(zq^{s},z^{-1}q^{m-s},q^{m};q^{m})_{\infty}=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}(-z)^{i}q^{m(i^{2}-i)/2+si}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
m,s
be positive integers such that
m\geq 2
and
0<s<m
. Then,

(19)

\frac{(q^{s},q^{2m-s},q^{2m};q^{2m})_{\infty}(q^{2m-2s},q^{2m+2s};q^{4m})_{\infty}}{(q^{s},q^{m-s};q^{m})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(mn+s)}.

Corollary 5.2
.

If
m=5
, then for
s=1,2,3,4
, we obtain the respective identities (6.7), (6.10), (6.12), (6.13) in
[
14
]
.

Remark 3
.

In
[
13
]
, the authors
point out that identities (6.7), (6.10), (6.12), (6.13) in
[
14
]
follow from the Jacobi Triple Product identity.

Analytic proof.

Since

\displaystyle(q^{s};q^{m})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q^{s};q^{2m})_{\infty}(q^{m+s};q^{2m})_{\infty}

\displaystyle(q^{2m+2s};q^{4m})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q^{m+s};q^{2m})_{\infty}(-q^{m+s};q^{2m})_{\infty},

to prove identity (
19
), we need to show that

(20)

\displaystyle(-q^{m+s};q^{2m})_{\infty}(-q^{m-s};q^{2m})_{\infty}(q^{2m};q^{2m})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(mn+s)},

which is precisely (
18
) with
q
replaced by
q^{2}
and
z
replaced by
-q^{m-s}
.
∎

Combinatorial proof.

To simplify the exposition, we first define a sign reversing involution to prove combinatorially the trivial cancellation

\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=1.

We say that an involution defined on a set of pairs (or tuples) of partitions is sign reversing, if the involution changes the parity of the length of the first partition in each pair (or tuple). Let
\mathcal{QP}(n)
be the set of pairs of partitions
(\lambda,\mu)\vdash n
with
\lambda
a distinct partition and
\mu
an unrestricted partition. The map
F:\mathcal{QP}(n)\setminus\{(\emptyset,\emptyset)\}\to\mathcal{QP}(n)\setminus\{(\emptyset,\emptyset)\}
defined by

F(\lambda,\mu)=\begin{cases}(\lambda\cup(\mu_{1}),\mu\setminus(\mu_{1}))&\text{ if }\lambda_{1}<\mu_{1}\\
(\lambda\setminus(\lambda_{1}),\mu\cup(\lambda_{1}))&\text{ if }\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\end{cases}

is a sign reversing involution. Recall that, following our convention, if
\nu=\emptyset
, then
\nu_{1}=0
. Since
(q;q)_{\infty}/(q;q)_{\infty}
is the generating function for

qp_{e-o}(n):=|\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{QP}(n),\ell(\lambda)\textrm{ even}\}|-|\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{QP}(n),\ell(\lambda)\textrm{ odd}\}|,

this completes the argument.

We continue with the combinatorial proof of Theorem
5.1
.

If
m=2
, then (
19
) becomes

(21)

\frac{(q,q^{3},q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{2},q^{6};q^{8})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})^{2}_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(2n+1)}.

Using the transformation
F
above on pairs of partitions with odd parts, we have that (
21
) is equivalent to

(22)

\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}.

This is Gauss’s identity
[
2
, Identity (1.3)]
with
q
replaced by
-q
. However, since
\ell_{o}(\lambda)\equiv|\lambda|\mod 2
, Andrews’ combinatorial proof of Gauss’ identity given in
[
2
]
also proves (
21
) combinatorially.

For the remainder of the proof let
m\geq 3
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}(n)
the set consisting of pairs of partitions
(\lambda,\mu)\vdash n
such that

•

\lambda
is a partition with distinct parts congruent to

s
or
-s
modulo
2m
or congruent to

\mu

or

m-s

and

\mathcal{A}_{o}(n):=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{A}(n)\mid\ell(\lambda)\text{ odd}\}.

.

If

\lambda=\lambda^{0,2m}\cup\lambda^{\pm s,2m}\cup\lambda^{\pm(2m+2s),4m},\hskip 7.22743pt\mu=\mu^{\pm s,2m}\cup\mu^{\pm(m+s),2m}

and we consider several cases.

Case 1:

(i.e., there is at least one part congruent to

in

or in
\mu

.

Hence,
\varphi
is a sign reversing involution on the subset of pairs

in
\mathcal{A}(n)
with
\lambda^{\pm s,2m}\cup\mu^{\pm s,2m}\neq\emptyset
.

Case 2:

\lambda^{\pm s,2m}\cup\mu^{\pm s,2m}=\emptyset
and (

has at least one repeated part). In this case the involution is similar to that of

. Let

be the largest part in

Concluding remarks

In this article we considered several conjectures and identities that first appeared in
[

]
. Identities (6.7), (6.10), (6.12) and (6.13) from
[
14
]
are proved using the Jacobi Triple Product identity in
[
13
]
. We generalized these identities and gave both analytic and combinatorial proofs. Considerations of similar identities from
[
14
]
, and their proofs in
[
13
]
led us to discover an interesting result about the number of distinct
5
-regular partitions of
n
. Theorem
1.1
proves the cases
k=1,2,3
of Conjecture 4.3 in
[
14
]
which concerns the non-negativity of the coefficients of a certain truncated theta function. The theorem gives inequalities involving the number of partitions of
n
with parts congruent to
\pm S\mod R
and it also leads to results involving the generalizations of the minimal excludant statistic for partitions.

At the end of
[
14
]
, Merca made another conjecture about the non-negativity of the coefficients of a truncated series. Suppose
R\geq 2
and
1\leq S<R
and define

T_{\pm}(q,k,S,R):=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n\mp 1)R/2\pm 3nS}(1-q^{S\pm nR}).

Conjecture 6.15 on
[

]
states that, for all

, each of

,
-T_{-}(q,k,S,R)
, and

We establish the first two parts of the conjecture.

Theorem 7.1
.

For
1\leq S<R
and
k\geq 1
,

(i)

T_{+}(q,k,S,R)
has non-negative coefficients;

(ii)

T_{-}(q,k,S,R)
has non-positive coefficients.

Proof.

Let
1\leq S<R
and
k\geq 1
.

To show that for the
q
-series

has non-negative coefficients, we rewrite it as

\displaystyle T_{+}(q,k,S,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n-1)R/2+3nS}(1-q^{n(R-S)}+q^{n(R-S)}-q^{S+nR})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{2R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n-1)R/2+3nS}\,\frac{1-q^{n(R-S)}}{1-q^{R-S}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{(q^{S+R},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n-1)R/2+3nS+nR-nS}\,\frac{1-q^{(n+1)S}}{1-q^{S}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{2R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n-1)R/2+3nS}(1+q^{R-S}+q^{2(R-S)}+\cdots+q^{(n-1)(R-S)})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{(q^{S+R},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2+2nS}(1+q^{S}+q^{2S}+\cdots+q^{nS}).

Clearly, this
q
-series has non-negative coefficients.

If
k\geq 2
, we rewrite

\displaystyle-T_{-}(q,k,S,R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2-3nS}(1-q^{S-nR})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2-3nS+S-nR}(1-q^{n(R-S)}+q^{n(R-S)}-q^{nR-S})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{2R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2-3nS+S-nR}\,\frac{1-q^{n(R-S)}}{1-q^{R-S}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{(q^{S+R},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2-3nS+S-nR+nR-nS}\,\frac{1-q^{(n-1)S}}{1-q^{S}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{S},q^{2R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n-1)R/2-3nS+S}(1+q^{R-S}+q^{2(R-S)}+\cdots+q^{(n-1)(R-S)})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{(q^{S+R},q^{R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}q^{n(3n+1)R/2-4nS+S}(1+q^{S}+q^{2S}+\cdots+q^{(n-2)S}).

If
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle-T_{-}(q,1,S,R)=\frac{q^{R-2S}}{(q^{S},q^{2R-S};q^{R})_{\infty}}-T_{-}(q,2,S,R).

Clearly, in each case
-T_{-}(q,1,S,R)
has non-negative coefficients.
∎

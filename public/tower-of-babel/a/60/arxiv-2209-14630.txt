Classification of solutions for the planar isotropic L p L_{p} dual Minkowski problem
Haizhong Li, Yao Wan

Abstract
. In his beautiful paper [ 1 ] , Ben Andrews obtained the complete classification of the solutions of the planar isotropic L p L_{p} Minkowski problem. In this paper, by generalizing Ben Andrews’s result we obtain the complete classification of the solutions of the planar isotropic L p L_{p} dual Minkowski problem, that is, for any p , q ∈ ℝ p,q\in\mathbb{R} we obtain the complete classification of the solutions of the following equation: u 1 − p ​ ( u θ 2 + u 2 ) q − 2 2 ​ ( u θ ​ θ + u ) = 1 on ​ 𝕊 1 . u^{1-p}(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=1\quad\text{on}\ \mathbb{S}^{1}. To establish the classification, we convert the ODE for the solution into an integral and study its asymptotic behavior, duality and monotonicity.

1.
Introduction

The Brunn-Minkowski theory is the classical core of the convex geometry, which focuses on studying geometric measures and geometric inequalities of convex bodies, see, for example Schneider
[
29
]
. The Minkowski problem is of central importance in the Brunn-Minkowski theory, which asks to characterize the surface area measure. Besides, the Aleksandrov problem characterizes the integral measure, and the dual Aleksandrov problem characterizes the dual integral measure. These problems are influential problems not only in geometric analysis, but also in the theory of fully nonlinear partial differential equations.

The
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowski theory and the dual Brunn-Minkowski theory are two theories that fundamentally extended the classical Brunn-Minkowski theory, which were initiated by Lutwak
[
26
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
. Lutwak
[
27
,
28
]
introduced the
L_{p}
surface measure
and proposed the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, which asks to characterize the
L_{p}
surface area measure. The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem contains critical problems such as the classical Minkowski problem (
p=1
), the logarithmic Minkowski problem (
p=0
) and the centro-affine Minkowski problem (
p=-n
) as special cases, and has been extensively studied, see, for example
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
23
]
. Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
15
]
introduced the dual curvature measure and proposed the dual Minkowski problem, which asks to characterize the dual curvature measure. The dual Minkowski problem contains critical problems such as the logarithmic Minkowski problem (
q=n
) and the Aleksandrov problem (
q=0
) as special cases, and has been studied in
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
13
,
31
,
32
]
.

The
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem was posed by Lutwak, Yang and Zhang in
[
24
]
, and unifies all Minkowski type problems mentioned so far. The
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem contains critical problems such as the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem (
q=n
), the dual Minkowski problem (
p=0
) and the
L_{p}
Aleksandrov problem (
q=0
) as special cases, and has been intensively studied by many authors, see, for example
[
2
,
8
,
7
,
9
,
16
,
20
,
30
]
.

When the given measure
\mu
has a density
f
, the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problems becomes the following Monge-Ampère type equation on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
:

(1.1)

u^{1-p}(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{q-n}{2}}\det(\nabla^{2}u+uI)=f,

where
f
is a given positive smooth function on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
u
is the support function of a convex body, and
\nabla u
and
\nabla^{2}u
are the gradient and the Hessian of
u
on the unit sphere with respect to an orthonormal basis, respectively.

We call the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem with
f=1
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
the planar isotropic
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem, which is concerned with the following equation

(1.2)

u^{1-p}(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=1,

where
u:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to(0,+\infty)
is the support function of a closed planar curve. The classification of solutions for Eq.(
1.2
) is of great interest to the study of the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem. We call a solution
u
of (
1.2
) to be an embedded (immersed, resp.) solution if it corresponds to the support function of an embedded (immersed, resp.) curve.

The aim of this paper is to classify the solutions of Eq.(
1.2
) for any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
. When
q=2
, the planar
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem becomes the planar
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, and Eq.(
1.2
) can be deduced to the following equation

(1.3)

u^{1-p}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=1.

If
p\neq 1
, let
\alpha=\frac{1}{1-p}
, then Eq.(
1.3
) is equivalent to

(1.4)

\kappa^{\alpha}=\langle x,\nu\rangle,

where
x
is a closed planar curve with outer unit normal
\nu
and curvature
\kappa
. This equation is closely related to the asymptotic behavior of the generalized curve shortening problem. The classification of the embedded solutions of Eq.(
1.4
) has been completely obtained by Ben Andrews in his beautiful paper
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

If
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{8},\infty)
, then the only embedded solution of Eq.(
1.4
) is a circle with radius
1
centred at the origin except for
\alpha=\frac{1}{3}
where they could be ellipses. If
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{8})

1

\Gamma_{k,\alpha}
with

. The curves

tends to

.

On the other hand, the nonuniqueness of solutions of Eq.(

when

either when

Recently, Liu-Lu

already studied the dual Minkowski problem (

) must be constant when

and

2.
The period function

Let
u(\theta)
be the support function of the solution for the planar isotropic
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem, that is,
u
satisfies the following second-order ordinary differential equation

(2.1)

u^{1-p}(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=1.

For
q\neq 0
, notice that (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}((u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}})_{\theta}=\frac{2}{p}(u^{p})_{\theta},

\displaystyle p\neq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}((u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}})_{\theta}=2(\log u)_{\theta},

\displaystyle p=0,

thus a first integral of (
2.1
) is given by

(2.2)

\begin{split}&(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-\frac{q}{p}u^{p}=E,\quad\quad\quad\quad p\neq 0,\\
&(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-q\log u=E,\quad\quad\quad p=0.\end{split}

for some constant
E
.

Since the solution of (
2.1
) is a periodic positive function of
\theta
, with maximum and minimum values of
u
determined by
p,\ q
and
E
. For convenience, we parameterize the solutions by the ratio
r
of the maximum and minimum values
u_{\pm}
of
u
: it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle u_{\pm}^{q}-\frac{q}{p}u_{\pm}^{p}=E,

\displaystyle p\neq 0,

\displaystyle u_{\pm}^{q}-q\log u_{\pm}=E,

\displaystyle p=0,

then we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}&E=\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-1)}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-r^{q})}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right),\quad p\neq 0,\\
&E=\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}-\log\left(\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}\right),\quad p=0.\end{split}

It is well known that the solution of (
2.1
) is unique when
p>q
, and is unique up to a constant when
p=q
, see, e.g.
[
16
,
9
]
. Hence we only consider the case
p<q
from now on.

It follows from (
2.3
) that
E(r)
is increasing for
r\geq 1
when
q>0
, while
E(r)
is decreasing for
r\geq 1
when
q<0
. Moreover,
E\to\frac{p-q}{p}
as
r\to 1
for
p\neq 0
and
E\to 1
as
r\to 1
for
p=0
;
E\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
for
p\leq 0
and
E\to 0
as
r\to\infty
for
p>0
.

Using (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain

(2.4)

\begin{split}&u_{\theta}=\pm\left[\left(\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-1)}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-r^{q})}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right)+\frac{q}{p}u^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-u^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad p\neq 0,\\
&u_{\theta}=\pm\left[\left(\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}-\log\left(\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}\right)+q\log u\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-u^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad p=0.\end{split}

Thus the total curvature of the unique (up to rotation) smooth, strictly locally convex curve segment with monotone support function satisfying (
2.2
) and endpoints tangent to the circles of radius
1
and
r
about the origin is given by

\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{\theta_{-}}^{\theta_{+}}d\theta=\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}\frac{du}{u_{\theta}}.

(1)

If
p\neq 0
and
q\neq 0
, denote
x=\frac{u}{u_{-}}\in(1,r)
, the period function
\Theta
becomes

(2.5)

\begin{split}\Theta(p,q,r)&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\left(\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-1)}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-p}}\left(\frac{q(r^{p}-r^{q})}{p(r^{q}-1)}\right)(u_{-})^{-q}+\frac{q}{p}(u_{-})^{p-q}x^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-x^{2}}}\\
&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-x^{2}}}.\end{split}

(2)

If
p=0
, denote
x=\frac{u}{u_{-}}\in(1,r)
, the period function
\Theta
becomes

(2.6)

\begin{split}\Theta(0,q,r)&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\left(\left(\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}-\log\left(\frac{q\log r}{r^{q}-1}\right)\right)(u_{-})^{-q}+(u_{-})^{-q}\log(u_{-})^{q}+q(u_{-})^{-q}\log x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-x^{2}}}\\
&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{r^{q}-1}{\log r}\log x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-x^{2}}}.\end{split}

(3)

If
q=0
, a similar calculation yields

\displaystyle\log(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})-\frac{2}{p}u^{p}=E,

where

\displaystyle E=\frac{2}{p}\log\left(\frac{p\log r}{r^{p}-1}\right)-\frac{2\log r}{r^{p}-1}.

Then the period function
\Theta
becomes

(2.7)

\begin{split}\Theta(p,0,r)&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(u_{-})^{-2}\exp{\left(2\log(u_{-})-\frac{2}{p}(u_{-})^{p}+\frac{2}{p}(u_{-})^{p}x^{p}\right)}-x^{2}}}\\
&=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{r^{\frac{2(x^{p}-1)}{r^{p}-1}}-x^{2}}}.\end{split}

In particular, when
p<q=2
, it follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) that

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\Theta(p,2,r)=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\frac{r^{p}-r^{2}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}-x^{2}}},\quad p\neq 0,\\
&\Theta(0,2,r)=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}-1}{\log r}\log x-x^{2}}},\quad p=0,\end{split}

which are the same as the functions
\Theta\left(\frac{1}{1-p},r\right)
in
[
1
]
.

3.
Special values and asymptotic behaviors

We only consider the case
(p,q)\in D
, where
D=\{(p,q):\ p<q\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\Theta
is continuous on
D\times(1,\infty)
, and has following limiting values:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\to-\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\arccos\frac{1}{r},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to+\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\arccos\frac{1}{r},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{q-p}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle p<0<q,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{q}{q-p}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle 0\leq p<q.

Moreover, we also have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Theta(1,2,r)=\pi,

\displaystyle r>1,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Theta(-2,-1,r)=\pi,

\displaystyle r>1,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Theta(-2,2,r)=\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle r>1.

Proof.

At first, it follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\to-\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{r^{2}-x^{2}}}=\arccos\frac{1}{r},

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to+\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{r^{2}-x^{2}}}=\arccos\frac{1}{r}.

Now let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, and set
x^{\beta}=v
, where

\displaystyle v=\frac{r^{\beta}-1}{2}z+\frac{r^{\beta}+1}{2},\quad\quad z\in(-1,1).

Then the change of variables formula implies that

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\tilde{I}(p,q,\beta,r,z)}},

where

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\tilde{I}(p,q,\beta,r,z)=\frac{4\beta^{2}}{(r^{\beta}-1)^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}v^{\frac{q(\beta-1)}{\beta}}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}v^{\frac{p+q(\beta-1)}{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-v^{2}\right],\quad p\neq 0,\\
&\tilde{I}(0,q,\beta,r,z)=\frac{4\beta^{2}}{(r^{\beta}-1)^{2}}\left[v^{\frac{2(\beta-1)}{\beta}}\left(1+\frac{r^{q}-1}{\beta\log r}\log v\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-v^{2}\right].\end{split}

When
r
is close to
1
, a routine computation by Taylor expansions gives

(3.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(p,q,\beta,r,z)=(q-p)(1-z^{2})\left[1+\frac{z}{2}\left(\beta-\frac{2q-p}{3}\right)(r-1)+o(r-1)\right],

from which it follows that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{(q-p)(1-z^{2})}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{q-p}}.

For the completeness of the paper, we provide the proof of (
3.10
) in the Appendix
A
.

When
r
tends to infinity, let
y=\frac{z+1}{2}\in(0,1)
and choose
\beta=1
, we have

(3.12)

\begin{split}\tilde{I}(0,q,1,r,z)=&4\left[1-y^{2}+\frac{2\log y}{q\log r}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log r}\right)\right],\\
\tilde{I}(p,q,1,r,z)=&4\left[1-y^{2}+(2-2y)\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2}{q}(1-y^{p})r^{p}+o\left(r^{p}\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right],\quad p<0<q,\\
\tilde{I}(p,q,1,r,z)=&4\left[y^{\frac{2p}{q}}-y^{2}+\left(\left(2+\frac{2p}{q}\frac{1-y}{y}\right)y^{\frac{2p}{q}}-2y\right)\frac{1}{r}\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\frac{2}{q}\left(y^{\frac{2p}{q}}-y^{\frac{(2-q)p}{q}}\right)\frac{1}{r^{p}}+o\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{r^{p}}\right)\right],\quad 0<p<q,\end{split}

from which it follows that

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad p<0<q,\\
&\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{y^{\frac{2p}{q}}-y^{2}}}=\frac{q}{q-p}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2},\quad 0\leq p<q.\end{split}

Finally, (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) can be deduced by direct calculation.
∎

Next we give the asymptotic behaviors of
\Theta(p,q,r)
when
r
tends to infinity.

Theorem 3.2
.

When
r
tends to
1
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{q-p}}+\frac{\pi}{96\sqrt{q-p}}(q^{2}-pq+p^{2}+3p-3q)(r-1)^{2}+o\left((r-1)^{2}\right),\quad q>0.

When
r
tends to infinity, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Theta(0,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2q\log r}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log r}\right),

\displaystyle q>0,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y^{p}-1}{(1-y^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy\cdot r^{p}+o\left(r^{p}\right),

\displaystyle-1<p<0,\ q>-p.

Proof.

If
q>0
, we choose
\beta_{0}=\frac{2q-p}{3}>0
because of the asymptotic expansion (
3.10
). Some tedious computation gives rise to

\begin{split}\tilde{I}(p,q,\beta_{0},r,z)&=(q-p)(1-z^{2})\left[1+\left(\frac{(2-q)(q-p)}{16}(1-z^{2})\right.\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\left.\left.+\frac{(q-2p)(q+p)}{432}(5-z^{2})\right)(r-1)^{2}+o((r-1)^{2})\right],\end{split}

as
r
tends to
1
. Then we deduce

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\tilde{I}(p,q,\beta_{0},r,z)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\left[1+\left(\frac{(2-q)(q-p)}{16}(1-z^{2})+\frac{(q-2p)(q+p)}{432}(5-z^{2})\right)(r-1)^{2}+o((r-1)^{2})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{(q-p)(1-z^{2})}}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(2-q)(q-p)}{16}(1-z^{2})+\frac{(q-2p)(q+p)}{432}(5-z^{2})\right)(r-1)^{2}+o((r-1)^{2})}{\sqrt{(q-p)(1-z^{2})}}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{q-p}}+\frac{\pi}{96\sqrt{q-p}}(q^{2}-pq+p^{2}+3p-3q)(r-1)^{2}+o\left((r-1)^{2}\right),

as
r
tends to
1
.

If
q>0
, it follows from (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle\Theta(0,q,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\tilde{I}(0,q,1,r,z)}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}+\frac{2\log y}{q\log r}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log r}\right)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-\frac{\log y}{q(1-y^{2})\log r}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log r}\right)}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}dy=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2q\log r}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log r}\right),

as
r
tends to infinity.

If
-1<p<0
and

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{\tilde{I}(p,q,1,r,z)}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}+\frac{2}{q}(1-y^{p})r^{p}+o\left(r^{p}\right)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-\frac{1-y^{p}}{q(1-y^{2})}\cdot r^{p}+o\left(r^{p}\right)}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}dy=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y^{p}-1}{(1-y^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy\cdot r^{p}+o(r^{p}),

as

tends to infinity.

∎

4.
Duality

Theorem 4.1
.

The following duality relations hold:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Theta\left(\frac{pq}{p-q},q,r^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\right)=\frac{q-p}{q}\Theta(p,q,r),

\displaystyle p<q,\ q>0,\ r>1,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Theta\left(p,\frac{pq}{q-p},r^{\frac{p-q}{p}}\right)=\frac{p-q}{p}\Theta(p,q,r),

\displaystyle p<q,\ p<0,\ r>1,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Theta(-q,-p,r)=\Theta(p,q,r),

\displaystyle p<q,\ r>1.

Proof.

Inspired by the observation of Ben Andrews
[
1
]
for the case where
q=2
, we prove the duality (
4.1
) as follows. Since the case where
p=0
in (
4.1
) is trivial, we may assume
p\neq 0
.

Denote
\Theta=\Theta(p,q,r)
, and let
u:[0,\Theta]\to\mathbb{R}
be the monotone solution of (
2.1
) with
u_{\theta}(0)=u_{\theta}(\Theta)=0
and
u(\Theta)=ru(0)
. Let
w:[0,\beta\Theta]\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
w(\tau)=u(\theta)^{\beta}
, where
\beta=\frac{q-p}{q},\ \tau=\beta\theta
.

Since
u
satisfies the following equations

\displaystyle u^{1-p}(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=1,\quad u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2}=\left(\frac{q}{p}u^{p}+E\right)^{\frac{2}{q}},

where
E
is given by (
2.3
), then we have

\displaystyle w_{\tau\tau}+w

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}u^{\beta-2}u_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}u^{\beta-1}u_{\theta\theta}+w

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}w^{\frac{\beta-2}{\beta}}\left[\left(\frac{q}{p}w^{\frac{p}{\beta}}+E\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-w^{\frac{2}{\beta}}\right]+\frac{1}{\beta}w^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left[w^{\frac{p-1}{\beta}}\left(\frac{q}{p}w^{\frac{p}{\beta}}+E\right)^{\frac{2}{q}-1}-w^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right]+w

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}Ew^{\frac{\beta-2}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\frac{q}{p}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)w^{\frac{\beta+p-2}{\beta}}\right]\left(\frac{q}{p}w^{\frac{p}{\beta}}+E\right)^{\frac{2}{q}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}Ew^{\frac{\beta-2}{\beta}}\left(\frac{q}{p}w^{\frac{p}{\beta}}+E\right)^{\frac{2-q}{q}},

and

\displaystyle w_{\tau}^{2}+w^{2}

\displaystyle=u^{2(\beta-1)}\left(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2}\right)=w^{\frac{2(\beta-1)}{\beta}}\left(\frac{q}{p}w^{\frac{p}{\beta}}+E\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}.

It follows that

(4.4)

\displaystyle w^{1-\frac{pq}{p-q}}(w_{\tau}^{2}+w^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(w_{\tau\tau}+w)=\frac{p}{p-q}E.

Note that the left hand side of (
4.4
) is homogeneous of degree
\frac{q^{2}}{q-p}>0
. Hence scaling
w
by a constant factor gives a solution of (
2.1
) with
p
replaced by
\frac{pq}{p-q}
, which satisfies
w_{\tau}(0)=w_{\tau}(\beta\Theta)=0
and
w(\beta\Theta)=r^{\beta}w(0)
. Therefore, we obtain

(4.5)

\Theta\left(\frac{pq}{p-q},q,r^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\right)=\frac{q-p}{q}\Theta(p,q,r).

To show the duality relation (
4.3
), define

(4.6)

\tilde{u}(\eta)=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{\theta}\in[0,\Theta]\\
|\tilde{\theta}-\eta|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(\tilde{\theta}-\eta)}{u(\tilde{\theta})},\quad\quad\eta\in[0,\Theta],

where
u:[0,\Theta]\to\mathbb{R}
is given as above.

First, notice that at the critical point
\theta
of
\frac{\cos(\tilde{\theta}-\eta)}{u(\tilde{\theta})}
with
|\theta-\eta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\cos(\theta-\eta)}{u(\theta)}\right)_{\theta}=\frac{-u\sin(\theta-\eta)-u_{\theta}\cos(\theta-\eta)}{u^{2}}=0,

then it follows from
u_{\theta\theta}+u>0
that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\cos(\theta-\eta)}{u(\theta)}\right)_{\theta\theta}=\frac{-(u_{\theta\theta}+u)\cos(\theta-\eta)}{u^{2}}<0,

which implies that
\frac{\cos(\theta-\eta)}{u(\theta)}
can have only maxima as critical points. If
\eta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, using
u_{\theta}(0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left.\left(\frac{\cos(\tilde{\theta}-\eta)}{u(\tilde{\theta})}\right)_{\tilde{\theta}}\right|_{\tilde{\theta}=0}=\frac{\sin(\eta)}{u(0)}>0,

thus the maximum of
\frac{\cos(\tilde{\theta}-\eta)}{u(\tilde{\theta})}
is never achieved at
0
. Similarly, the maximum is never achieved at
\Theta
if
\eta\in(\Theta-\frac{\pi}{2},\Theta)
. Therefore, the maximum of (
4.6
) is only taken at the interior of
[0,\Theta]
for
\eta\in(0,\Theta)
. Moreover, it is obvious that
\tilde{u}(0)=\frac{1}{u(0)}
and
\tilde{u}(\Theta)=\frac{1}{u(\Theta)}.

Next suppose that
\theta=\theta(\eta)
attains the maximum of (
4.6
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle u_{\theta}=u\tan(\eta-\theta),

so that

\displaystyle u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2}=\frac{1}{\tilde{u}^{2}},\quad u_{\theta}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)\frac{d\theta}{d\eta}=-\frac{\tilde{u}_{\eta}}{\tilde{u}^{3}}.

Since
u_{\theta\theta}+u>0
, differentiating both sides of (
4.7
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta}{d\eta}=\frac{u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2}}{u(u_{\theta\theta}+u)}>0.

Thus
\theta=\theta(\eta)
is a monotone increasing function, and the following relations hold:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\frac{\tilde{u}_{\eta}}{\tilde{u}}=-\frac{u_{\theta}}{u},\quad\frac{d\eta}{d\theta}=\tilde{u}^{2}u(u_{\theta\theta}+u).

On the other hand, fix
\theta_{0}\in(0,\Theta)
and let
|\eta-\theta_{0}|<\frac{\pi}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\cos(\eta-\theta_{0})}{\tilde{u}(\eta)}\right)_{\eta}=\frac{-\tilde{u}\sin(\eta-\theta_{0})-\tilde{u}_{\eta}\cos(\eta-\theta_{0})}{{\tilde{u}}^{2}}=0

if and only if

\displaystyle\tan(\eta-\theta_{0})=-\frac{\tilde{u}_{\eta}}{\tilde{u}}=\frac{u_{\theta}}{u}=\tan(\eta-\theta(\eta)),

which implies that
\frac{\cos(\eta-\theta_{0})}{\tilde{u}(\eta)}
attains its critical value when
\theta(\eta)=\theta_{0}+k\pi,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

It follows that

(4.9)

\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{\eta}\in[0,\Theta]\\
|\tilde{\eta}-\theta|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(\tilde{\eta}-\theta)}{\tilde{u}(\tilde{\eta})}=\frac{\cos(\eta(\theta)-\theta)}{\tilde{u}(\eta(\theta))}=u(\theta).

The same argument gives

(4.10)

\displaystyle u=(\tilde{u}_{\eta}^{2}+\tilde{u}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\frac{u_{\theta}}{u}=-\frac{\tilde{u}_{\eta}}{\tilde{u}},\quad\frac{d\theta}{d\eta}=u^{2}\tilde{u}(\tilde{u}_{\eta\eta}+\tilde{u}).

Using (
4.8
) and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle(u_{\theta\theta}+u)(\tilde{u}_{\eta\eta}+\tilde{u})=u^{-3}\tilde{u}^{-3},

then

\displaystyle 1=u^{1-p}(u_{\theta}^{2}+u^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}(u_{\theta\theta}+u)=(\tilde{u}_{\eta}^{2}+\tilde{u}^{2})^{-\frac{1-p}{2}}\tilde{u}^{2-q}(\tilde{u}_{\eta\eta}+\tilde{u})^{-1}u^{-3}\tilde{u}^{-3},

which implies

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{1+q}(\tilde{u}_{\eta}^{2}+\tilde{u}^{2})^{\frac{-p-2}{2}}(\tilde{u}_{\eta\eta}+\tilde{u})=1.

Noting that
\frac{\tilde{u}(0)}{\tilde{u}(\Theta)}=\frac{u(\Theta)}{u(0)}=r
, we obtain

(4.11)

\Theta(-q,-p,r)=\Theta(p,q,r).

It is apparent from (
4.5
) and (
4.11
) that the duality relation (
4.2
) holds.
∎

In fact, we have a duality relation of the solution for the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem with a prescribed function
f=1
in
[
7
]
.

By use of the duality relation (

), we give special values and asymptotic behaviors for the case where

.

Corollary 4.3
.

We have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{p}{p-q}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\quad\quad p<q\leq 0.

When

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{q-p}}+\frac{\pi}{96\sqrt{q-p}}(q^{2}-pq+p^{2}+3p-3q)(r-1)^{2}+o\left((r-1)^{2}\right),\quad p<0.

5.
Monotonicity in
p
and
q

In this section, we will prove that
\Theta(p,q,r)
is monotone in
p
and
q
. More specifically, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

\Theta(p,q,r)
is monotone increasing in
p
and is monotone decreasing in
q
.

5.1.
Monotonicity in
p

To show
\Theta(p,q,r)
is monotone increasing in
p
, denote

\displaystyle I(p,q,r,x)=\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-x^{2},

and observe that if either
p<\bar{p}<0
, or
0<p<\bar{p}
, or
p<0<\bar{p}
with
q>0
, we have

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle:=(I(p,q,r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-(I(\bar{p},q,r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}-\frac{r^{\bar{p}}-r^{q}}{r^{\bar{p}}-1}-\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{\bar{p}}-1}x^{\bar{p}},

this is zero for
x=1
and for
x=r
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(x^{1-p}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}\right)=-\bar{p}(\bar{p}-p)x^{\bar{p}-p-1}\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{\bar{p}}-1}<0,

from which it follows that
Q(x)
can have only maxima as critical points in
x
, and therefore that
Q(x)>0
, or equivalently,
I(p,q,r,x)>I(\bar{p},q,r,x)
for all
x\in(1,r)
. Then

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)-\Theta(\bar{p},q,r)=\int_{1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I(p,q,r,x)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{I(\bar{p},q,r,x)}}\right)dx<0.

If
q<0
, a similar argument shows that
Q(x)<0
, equivalently,
I(p,q,r,x)>I(\bar{p},q,r,x)
for all
x\in(1,r)
. Thus
\Theta(p,q,r)
is also increasing in
p
for
q<0
.

If
q=0
, denote

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\log\left(I(p,0,r,x)+x^{2}\right)}{\log r}-\frac{\log\left(I(\bar{p},0,r,x)+x^{2}\right)}{\log r}=\frac{2(x^{p}-1)}{r^{p}-1}-\frac{2(x^{\bar{p}}-1)}{r^{\bar{p}}-1},

a similar argument shows that
Q(x)>0
, equivalently,
I(p,q,r,x)>I(\bar{p},q,r,x)
for all
x\in(1,r)
. Thus
\Theta(p,0,r)
is increasing in
p
.

5.2.
Monotonicity in
q

Actually, the monotonicity in
q
of
\Theta(p,q,r)
can be deduced from the monotonicity in
p
and the duality (
4.3
). For completeness, we gives a direct proof of the monotonicity in
q
.

If
0<q<\bar{q}
, we have

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle:=(I(p,q,r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-(I(p,\bar{q},r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}-\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{\bar{q}}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{\bar{q}}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{\bar{q}}},

this is zero for
x=1
and for
x=r
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(x^{1-p}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right)=-p^{2}x^{p-1}\cdot\frac{q}{\bar{q}}\left(\frac{q}{\bar{q}}-1\right)\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{\bar{q}}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{\bar{q}}-1}{r^{p}-1}x^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{\bar{q}}-2}\left(\frac{r^{\bar{q}}-1}{r^{p}-1}\right)^{2}>0,

from which it follows that
P(x)
can have only minima as critical points in
x
, and therefore that
P(x)<0
, or equivalently,
I(p,q,r,x)<I(p,\bar{q},r,x)
for all
x\in(1,r)
. Then

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)-\Theta(p,\bar{q},r)=\int_{1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I(p,q,r,x)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{I(p,\bar{q},r,x)}}\right)dx>0.

In particular, when
p=0
we denote
P(x)=(I(0,q,r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-(I(0,\bar{q},r,x)+x^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}
, then the monotonicity of
\Theta(0,q,r)
in
q
follows in a similar manner.

If either
q<\bar{q}<0
or
q<0<\bar{q}
, a similar argument shows that
P(x)>0
, equivalently,
I(p,q,r,x)<I(p,\bar{q},r,x)
for all
x\in(1,r)
. Thus
\Theta(p,q,r)
is also decreasing in
q
.

If either
q<\bar{q}=0
or
q=0<\bar{q}
, since
\Theta(p,q,r)
is continuous in
q
, then the monotonicity of
\Theta(p,q,r)
in
q
holds in this case.

6.
Monotonicity in
r

Due to the asymptotic behavior (
3.10
) when
r
is close to
1
, we choose the following transformation:

(6.1)

\displaystyle x^{\beta_{0}}=v=\frac{r^{\beta_{0}}-1}{2}z+\frac{r^{\beta_{0}}+1}{2},\quad z\in(-1,1),

where
\beta_{0}=\frac{2q-p}{3}
. Then we obtain

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Theta(p,q,r)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{J(p,q,r,z)}},

where
J(p,q,r,z)=\tilde{I}(p,q,r,\beta_{0},z)
, i.e.

(6.3)

\begin{split}&J(p,q,r,z)=\frac{4\beta_{0}^{2}}{(r^{\beta_{0}}-1)^{2}}\left[v^{\frac{2(\beta_{0}-1)}{\beta_{0}}}\left(\frac{r^{p}-r^{q}}{r^{p}-1}+\frac{r^{q}-1}{r^{p}-1}v^{\frac{p}{\beta_{0}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-v^{2}\right],\quad p\neq 0,\\
&J(0,q,r,z)=\frac{4\beta_{0}^{2}}{(r^{\beta_{0}}-1)^{2}}\left[v^{\frac{2(\beta_{0}-1)}{\beta_{0}}}\left(1+\frac{r^{q}-1}{\beta_{0}\log r}\log v\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}-v^{2}\right].\end{split}

Using the duality relation (
4.3
), we may assume
q>0
, then
\beta_{0}>0
. Let
t=\frac{r^{\beta_{0}}+1}{r^{\beta_{0}}-1}\in(1,+\infty)
and
z\in(-1,1)
, define

(6.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(p,q,t,z)=\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{2}}J(p,q,r,z).

Our purpose is to show that
\tilde{J}(p,q,t,z)
is monotone in
t
for each

.

6.1.
The case
p\neq 0

If
p\neq 0
, assume
\rho=\frac{p}{q}
. Note that

\displaystyle r=\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}},\quad v=\frac{t+z}{t-1},

then we have

(6.5)

\tilde{J}(p,q,t,z)=(z+t)^{2}\left[K_{\rho}(t,z)^{\frac{2}{q}}-1\right],

where

\displaystyle K_{\rho}(t,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta_{0}}}-\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{q}{\beta_{0}}}}{\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta_{0}}}-1}\left(\frac{t+z}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{-q}{\beta_{0}}}+\frac{\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{q}{\beta_{0}}}-1}{\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta_{0}}}-1}\left(\frac{t+z}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{\beta_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{1-\rho}-B^{1-\rho}}{A^{-\rho}-B^{-\rho}}+\frac{A-B}{A^{\rho}-B^{\rho}},\quad A=\left(\frac{t+1}{t+z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2-\rho}},\ B=\left(\frac{t-1}{t+z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2-\rho}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}=(z+t)^{2}\left[\frac{2}{z+t}\left(K_{\rho}^{\frac{2}{q}}-1\right)+\frac{2}{q}K_{\rho}^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\frac{\partial K_{\rho}}{\partial t}\right]>0

is equivalent to

(6.6)

\frac{\partial K_{\rho}}{\partial t}>\frac{q}{z+t}\left(K_{\rho}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}-K_{\rho}\right).

At first we consider the case where
\rho=-1
. Then

\displaystyle K_{-1}(t,z)=\frac{t^{2}+2tz+1}{(z+t)^{2}}.

A direct computation gives rise to

(6.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{-1}}{\partial t}=\frac{2(z^{2}-1)}{(z+t)^{3}}=\frac{2}{z+t}\left(1-K_{-1}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}(-2,2,t,z)\equiv 0,\quad t>1,\ |z|<1.

Hence we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta}{\partial r}(-2,2,r)=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\frac{\partial t}{\partial r}\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}\frac{dz}{2\tilde{J}^{\frac{3}{2}}}=0,

and then

\displaystyle\Theta(-2,2,r)\equiv\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\Theta(-2,2,r)=\frac{\pi}{2}.

Furthermore, we have

Theorem 6.1
.

For each
t>1
and
z\in(-1,1)
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}(-q,q,t,z)\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}<0,\quad 0<q<2\\
>0,\quad q>2.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for each
r>1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta}{\partial r}(-q,q,r)\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}<0,\quad 0<q<2\\
>0,\quad q>2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It follows from (
6.7
) and weighted average inequalities that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{-1}}{\partial t}=\frac{2}{z+t}\left(1-K_{-1}\right)<\frac{q}{z+t}\left(K_{-1}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}-K_{-1}\right),\quad 0<q<2,

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{-1}}{\partial t}=\frac{2}{z+t}\left(1-K_{-1}\right)>\frac{q}{z+t}\left(K_{-1}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}-K_{-1}\right),\quad q>2.

Using the characterization (
6.6
), we obtain (
6.1
). Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta}{\partial r}(-q,q,r)=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\frac{\partial t}{\partial r}\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}\frac{dz}{2\tilde{J}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}<0,\quad 0<q<2\\
>0,\quad q>2.\end{array}\right.

∎

The general case will be discussed later.

6.2.
The case

If

(6.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(0,q,t,z)=(z+t)^{2}\left[L(t,z)^{\frac{2}{q}}-1\right],

where

\displaystyle L(t,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t+z}{t-1}\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left[1+\frac{\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}-1}{\log\left(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\right)}\log\left(\frac{t+z}{t-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{B\log A-A\log B}{\log A-\log B},\quad A=\left(\frac{t+1}{t+z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},\ B=\left(\frac{t-1}{t+z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{J}}{\partial t}(0,q,t,z)=(z+t)^{2}\left[\frac{2}{z+t}\left(L^{\frac{2}{q}}-1\right)+\frac{2}{q}L^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\right]>0

is equivalent to

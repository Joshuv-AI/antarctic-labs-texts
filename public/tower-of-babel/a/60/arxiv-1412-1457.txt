Remark on Continued Fractions, Möbius Transformations and Cycles Thanks: On leave from Odessa University.
Vladimir V. Kisil

Abstract
. We review interrelations between continued fractions, Möbius transformations and representations of cycles by 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrices. This leads us to several descriptions of continued fractions through chains of orthogonal or touching horocycles. One of these descriptions was proposed in a recent paper by A. Beardon and I. Short. The approach is extended to several dimensions in a way which is compatible to the early propositions of A. Beardon based on Clifford algebras.

2.
Continued Fractions

We use the following compact notation for a continued fraction:

(1)

K(a_{n}|b_{n})=\cfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}+\cfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}+\cfrac{a_{3}}{b_{3}+\ldots}}}=\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{}{+}\frac{a_{3}}{b_{3}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{}{+\ldots}.

Without loss of generality we can assume
a_{j}\neq 0
for all

j
. The important particular case of
simple
continued fractions,
with
a_{n}=1
for all
n
, is denoted by
K(b_{n})=K(1|b_{n})
. Any
continued fraction can be transformed to an equivalent simple one.

It is easy to see, that continued fractions are
related to the following linear-fractional
(Möbius) transformation, cf.
[
16
,
18
,
17
,
2
]
:

(2)

S_{n}=s_{1}\circ s_{2}\circ\ldots\circ s_{n},\qquad\text{where}\quad s_{j}(z)=\frac{a_{j}}{b_{j}+z}.

These Möbius transformation are considered as bijective maps of the
Riemann sphere
\dot{\mathbb{C}{}}=\mathbb{C}{}\cup\{\infty\}
onto itself.
If we associate the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
to a liner-fractional transformation
z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
, then the composition of two
such transformations corresponds to multiplication of the
respective matrices. Thus, relation (
2
)
has the matrix form:

(3)

\begin{pmatrix}P_{n-1}&P_{n}\\
Q_{n-1}&Q_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}\\
1&b_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{2}\\
1&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{n}\\
1&b_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

The last identity can be fold into the recursive formula:

(4)

\begin{pmatrix}P_{n-1}&P_{n}\\
Q_{n-1}&Q_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}P_{n-2}&P_{n-1}\\
Q_{n-2}&Q_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{n}\\
1&b_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

This is equivalent to the main recurrence relation:

(5)

\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n}=b_{n}P_{n-1}+a_{n}P_{n-2}\\
Q_{n}=b_{n}Q_{n-1}+a_{n}Q_{n-2}\end{array},\quad n=1,2,3,\ldots,\quad\text{with }\begin{array}[]{cc}{P_{1}}={a_{1}},&{P_{0}}=0,\\
{Q_{1}}={b_{1}},&Q_{0}=1.\end{array}

The meaning of entries
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
from the
matrix (
3
) is revealed as follows. Möbius
transformation (
2
)–(
3
)
maps
0
and
\infty
to

(6)

\frac{P_{n}}{Q_{n}}=S_{n}(0),\qquad\frac{P_{n-1}}{Q_{n-1}}=S_{n}(\infty).

It is easy to see that
S_{n}(0)
is the
partial quotient
of (
1
):

(7)

\frac{P_{n}}{Q_{n}}=\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{}{+\ldots+}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\,.

Properties of the sequence of partial quotients

\left\{\frac{P_{n}}{Q_{n}}\right\}
in terms of sequences
\{a_{n}\}

and
\{b_{n}\}
are the core of the continued fraction
theory. Equation (
6
) links partial
quotients with the Möbius map (
2
). Circles
form an invariant family under Möbius transformations, thus their
appearance for continued fractions is natural. Surprisingly, this
happened only recently in
[
4
]
.

3.
Möbius Transformations and Cycles

If
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
is a matrix with real entries then for the purpose of
the associated Möbius transformations
M:z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
we may assume that
\det M=\pm 1
. The
collection of all such matrices form a group. Möbius maps commute
with the complex conjugation
z\mapsto\bar{z}
. If
\det M>0

then both the upper and the lower half-planes are preserved; if
\det M<0
then the two half-planes are swapped. Thus, we can treat
M

as the map of equivalence classes
z\sim\bar{z}
, which are labelled
by respective points of the closed upper half-plane. Under this
identification we consider any map produced by
M
with
\det M=\pm 1
as the map of the closed upper-half plane to itself.

The characteristic property of Möbius maps is that circles and lines
are transformed to circles and lines. We use the word
cycles

for elements of this Möbius-invariant
family
[
20
,
14
,
12
]
. We abbreviate a cycle given
by the equation

(8)

k(u^{2}+v^{2})-2lv-2nu+m=0

to the point
(k,l,n,m)
of the three dimensional projective space

P\mathbb{R}^{3}{}
. The equivalence relation
z\sim\bar{z}
is
lifted to the equivalence relation

(9)

(k,l,n,m)\sim(k,l,-n,m)

in the space of cycles, which again is compatible with the Möbius
transformations acting on cycles.

The most efficient connection between cycles and Möbius
transformations is realised through the construction, which may be
traced back to
[
19
]
and was subsequently
rediscovered by various authors
[
7
]
*§ 4.1

[
8
]

[
11
]
*§ 4.2, see
also
[
12
,
14
]
. The construction associates a cycle

(k,l,n,m)
with the
2\times 2
matrix
{C}=\begin{pmatrix}l+\mathrm{i}n&-m\\
k&-l+\mathrm{i}n\end{pmatrix}
, see discussion in
[
14
]
*§ 4.4 for a
justification. This identification is Möbius covariant: the Möbius
transformation defined by
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
maps a cycle with matrix
{C}
to the cycle with
matrix
M{C}M^{-1}
. Therefore, any Möbius-invariant relation between cycles
can be expressed in terms of corresponding matrices. The central role
is played by the Möbius-invariant inner product
[
14
]
*§ 5.3:

(10)

\left\langle{C},{\tilde{C}}\right\rangle=\Re\mathop{tr}({C}\overline{{\tilde{C}}}),

which is a cousin of the product used in GNS construction of

C^{*}
-algebras. Notably, the relation:

(11)

\left\langle{C},{\tilde{C}}\right\rangle=0\quad\text{ or }\quad k\tilde{m}+m\tilde{k}-2n\tilde{n}-2l\tilde{l}=0,

describes two cycles
{C}=(k,l,m,n)
and

{\tilde{C}}=(\tilde{k},\tilde{l},\tilde{m},\tilde{n})

orthogonal in Euclidean geometry. Also, the inner
product (
10
) expresses the Descartes–Kirillov
condition
[
11
]
*Lem. 4.1(c)

[
14
]
*Ex. 5.26 of
{C}
and

{\tilde{C}}
to be externally tangent:

(12)

\left\langle{C}+{\tilde{C}},{C}+{\tilde{C}}\right\rangle=0\quad\text{or}\quad(l+\tilde{l})^{2}+(n+\tilde{n})^{2}-(m+\tilde{m})(k+\tilde{k})=0,

where the representing vectors
{C}=(k,l,n,m)
and

{\tilde{C}}=(\tilde{k},\tilde{l},\tilde{m},\tilde{n})
from

P\mathbb{R}^{3}{}
need to be normalised by the conditions

\left\langle{C},{C}\right\rangle=1
and

\left\langle{\tilde{C}},{\tilde{C}}\right\rangle=1
.

It is natural to look for multidimensional generalisations of
continued fractions. A geometric approach based on Möbius
transformation and Clifford algebras was proposed
in

e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ij},

is the Kronecker delta. An element of

associated with vectors
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}{}

\overline{ab}=\bar{b}\bar{a}

An important observation is that any non-zero vectors

Lemma 6.1

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group and χ \chi be an irreducible character of G G , the number cod ( χ ) = | G : ker ( χ ) | / χ ( 1 ) {\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G:{\mathrm{ker}}(\chi)|/\chi(1) is called the codegree of χ \chi . Also, cod ⁡ ( G ) = { cod ⁡ ( χ ) | χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) } {\mathrm{cod}}(G)=\{{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\ |\ \chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)\} . For d ∈ cod ⁡ ( G ) d\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G) , the multiplicity of d d in G G , denoted by m G ′ ​ ( d ) m^{\prime}_{G}(d) , is the number of irreducible characters of G G having codegree d d . A finite group G G is called a T k ′ T^{\prime}_{k} -group for some integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , if there exists d 0 ∈ cod ⁡ ( G ) d_{0}\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G) such that m G ′ ​ ( d 0 ) = k m^{\prime}_{G}(d_{0})=k and for every d ∈ cod ⁡ ( G ) − { d 0 } d\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G)-\{d_{0}\} , we have m G ′ ​ ( d ) = 1 m^{\prime}_{G}(d)=1 . In this note we characterize finite T k ′ T^{\prime}_{k} -groups completely, where k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 is an integer.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group. Also let
{\rm cd}(G)
be the set of all irreducible character degrees of
G
, that is,

{\rm cd}(G)=\{\chi(1)|\;\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)\}
, where
{\rm Irr}(G)
is the set of all complex irreducible characters of
G
.
For a character
\chi
of
G
, the codegree of
\chi
is defined as
{\rm cod}(\chi):=|G:{\rm ker}(\chi)|/\chi(1)
(see
[
14
]
).
Set
{\rm cod}(G):=\{{\rm cod}(\chi)|\;\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)\}
. It is well known that
{\rm cod}(G)
may be used to provide information on the structure of the group
G
(see for instance
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
14
]
).

For
d\in{\rm cd}(G)
, the multiplicity of degree
d
, denoted by
m_{G}(d)
, is the number of irreducible characters of
G
having the same degree
d
, i.e.,
m_{G}(d)=|\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)|\chi(1)=d\}|
. Many people considered the relations between the multiplicities of irreducible character degrees of finite groups and the structure of the groups. Let
{\rm Irr_{1}}(G)
be the set of nonlinear irreducible characters in
{\rm Irr}(G)
and
{\rm cd}_{1}(G)
be the set of degrees of the characters in
{\rm Irr}_{1}(G)
. If for a finite group
G
, there exists a nonnegative integer
n
such that
|{\rm cd}_{1}(G)|=|{\rm Irr}_{1}(G)|-n
, then
G
is called a
D_{n}
-group. In
[
2
]
, Berkovich et al. gave the classification of
D_{0}
-groups. Then, Berkovich and Kazarin in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
classified all
D_{1}
-groups. Also
D_{2}
-groups have been classified by Liu and Lu (see
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
).

Inspired by the definition of
D_{n}
-groups, we define new families of finite groups called
D^{\prime}_{n}
-groups, that are the groups
G
satisfying
|{\rm cod}(G)|=|{\rm Irr}(G)|-n
, for some nonnegative integer
n
. The second author classified
D^{\prime}_{0}
-groups, and showed that they are
C_{2}
and
S_{3}
(see
[
8
]
).

A finite group
G
is said to be a
T_{k}
-group for some integer
k\geq 1
, if there exists a nontrivial degree

d_{0}\in{\rm cd}(G)
such that
m_{G}(d_{0})=k
and that for every
d\in{\rm cd}(G)-\{1,d_{0}\}
, the multiplicity of
d

in
G
is trivial, that is,
m_{G}(d)=1
. Tong-Viet in
[
16
]
showed that if
G

is a non-solvable
T_{k}
-group for some integer
k\geq 1
, then
k=2
and
G
is isomorphic to

{\rm PSL}_{2}(5)
or
{\rm PSL}_{2}(7)
. Analogously, one can define a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group for some integer
k\geq 1
based on the set
{\rm cod}(G)
, that is a finite group
G
in which there exists

d_{0}\in{\rm cod}(G)
such that
m^{\prime}_{G}(d_{0})=k

and for every
d\in{\rm cod}(G)
-\{d_{0}\}
, we have

m^{\prime}_{G}(d)=1
. In this paper we wish to study
T^{\prime}_{k}
-groups, for some integer
k\geq 1
. In fact, we characterize these groups completely and we prove the following theorem:

Main Theorem.
Let
G
be a finite
T_{k}^{\prime}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
. Then one of the following occurs:

(a)

G
is non-solvable if and only if
k=2
and
G\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
G\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
.

(b)

G
is solvable if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

G\cong C_{4}
;
S_{4}
; Suz
(2)\cong C_{5}\rtimes C_{4}
or Dic
{}_{12}\cong
SmallGroup(12,1). Moreover
k=2
.

(2)

G\cong C_{2}\times S_{3}
;
D_{18}
or Frobenius group
C_{3}^{2}\rtimes Q_{8}
. Moreover
k=3
.

(3)

G
is an elementary abelian
p
-group, for some prime
p
, or
G
is an extra-special
2
-group. In particular
k=|G/G^{\prime}|-1
.

(4)

G
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group
C_{2}^{\beta}\rtimes C_{2^{\beta}-1}
, for some integer
\beta
, where
p=2^{\beta}-1
is prime. Moreover
k=p-1
.

(5)

G
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group
C_{p}^{\beta}\rtimes C_{2}
, for some prime
p
and integer
\beta
. Moreover
k=(p^{\beta}-1)/2
.

If
N\unlhd G
and
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
, then
{I}_{G}(\theta)
denotes the inertia group of
\theta
in
G
, and
{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
denotes the set of all irreducible constituents of
\theta^{G}
. Moreover,
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)={\mathrm{Irr}}(G)-{\mathrm{Irr}}(G/N)
. By a rational group, we mean a group whose all irreducible characters are rational-valued. By
n_{p}
we mean, the
p
-part of
n
, where
n
is a natural number while, by
n_{q}(G)
we mean the number of Sylow
q
-subgroups of
G
. For the rest of the notations, we follow
[
9
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some known results of irreducible characters which will be needed throughout the proofs.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
K
be a normal abelian subgroup of
G
. Then
|G:I_{G}(\lambda)|\leq|K|-1
, for each
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
.

The above remark follows by the fact that there is a correspondence between
G
-orbits of
{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
and
G
-conjugacy classes of
K
. We use this remark, several times through the proofs.

Lemma 2.2
.

(see
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
)
Let
G
be a finite group acting on a module
M
over a finite field and
1\in S\subseteq M
be a union of some
G
-orbits of
M
. Also, assume
q
is a prime divisor of the order of
G

such that for every
v\in M\setminus S
,
C_{G}(v)
contains a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
as a normal subgroup.
Assume
for each
1\not=x\in S
,
q
divides
|\mathrm{orb}_{G}(x)|
. Then,
|M|-|S|=n_{q}(G)(|C_{M}(Q)|-1)
for some
Q\in\mathrm{Syl}_{q}(G)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(see
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
)
Let
S
be a finite non-abelian simple group.

(1) If
S
is a sporadic simple group, the Tits group or an alternating group of degree at least 7, then
S
has two nontrivial irreducible characters, with distinct degrees and both extendible to
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
.

(2) If
S
is a simple group of Lie type in characteristic
p
and
S\not\cong

{}^{2}F_{4}(2)^{\prime}
, then the Steinberg character of
S
, denoted by
St_{S}
, of degree
|S|_{p}
, is extendible to
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
. Furthermore, if
S\not\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(3^{f})
for some integer
f\geq 2
, then
S
possesses an irreducible character
\theta
such that
\theta(1)\not=|S|_{p}
and
\theta
also extends to
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(see
[
5
, Lemma 5]
)
Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
such that
N=S_{1}\times\dots\times S_{t}
, where
S_{i}\cong S
is a non-abelian simple group. If
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(S)
extends to
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
, then
\sigma\times\dots\times\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)
extends to
G
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(see
[
3
, Theorem E]
)
Let
N\trianglelefteq G
, where
N
is a
p
-group and
G/N
is solvable. Let

\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)
be invariant in
G
, and assume that the members of
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\theta)
have distinct degrees. Then,
G
is a
p
-group and
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\theta)=\{\chi\}
. In particular,
\chi(1)=\sqrt{|G|/|N|}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, where
k\geq 1
is an integer. Then
G
is abelian if and only if
G
is an elementary abelian
p
-group, for some prime
p
; or
G\cong C_{4}
.

Proof.

Since the ’if’ part of the statement is clear, we only prove the ’only if’ part. Note that for every
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
,
{\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda)=o(\lambda)
, where
o(\lambda)
is the order of
\lambda
in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)\cong G
. So,
G
does not have a homomorphic image isomorphic to
C_{p}\times C_{q}
, for distinct primes
p
and
q
. Thus,
G
is a
p
-group, for some prime
p
. First assume that
p\not=2
. If
G
has a factor group isomorphic to
C_{p^{2}}
, then
m^{\prime}_{G}(p)
and
m^{\prime}_{G}(p^{2})
are greater than one, a contradiction. So
G
is an elementary abelian
p
-group. Now assume that
p=2
. If
G
contains an element of order greater than 2, then by easy calculation we can see that
G\cong C_{4}
. So in this case either
G
is an elementary abelian 2-group or
G\cong C_{4}
, and the result follows.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
K
be a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
. In addition, let
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
,
\delta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
and
\nu\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{G}(\delta)|\delta)
.

(a)

There exist
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
,
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{G}(\lambda)|\lambda)
, such that
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|I_{G}(\lambda)|/(|\ker(\chi)|\theta(1))
and also
|I_{G}(\delta)|/(|\ker(\nu^{G})|\nu(1))\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G)
.

(b)

If
\ker(\chi)\cap K=1
, then
|K|
divides
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
.

(c)

If
\chi
is faithful, then
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=p
, where
p
is a prime divisor of
|G|/|K|
.

Proof.

(a) It is an immediate consequence of Clifford’s theorem,
[
9
, Theorem 6.11]
.

(b) First, note that we can consider
\chi
as an irreducible character of
\overline{G}=G/\ker(\chi)
, and adopt the bar convention for this quotient group. Since
K\cap\ker(\chi)=1
, we have
\overline{K}=K\ker(\chi)/\ker(\chi)\cong K
. Now, using part (a), we have

{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=\frac{|I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)|}{\theta(1)}=|\overline{K}|\frac{|I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)|}{|\overline{K}|\theta(1)}=|K|\frac{|I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)|}{|K|\theta(1)},

for some
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\overline{K})
and
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)|\lambda)
. By,
[
9
, Theorem 6.11]
,
\theta(1)
is a divisor of
|I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)|/|K|
, so
|K|
divides
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
.

(c) Now, assume
\ker(\chi)=1
, and
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=p
, for some prime
p
dividing
|G/K|
. Hence, using (a) and (b), we have
|K|=p
and there exist
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
, and
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{G}(\lambda)|\lambda)
, such that
|I_{G}(\lambda)/K|=\theta(1)
. It follows that
K\leq{\bf Z}(P)
, for every
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
. On the other hand, the argument in
[
9
, page 84]
implies that
|I_{G}(\lambda)/K|=\theta(1)=1
. Then,
I_{G}(\lambda)=K
, which is not possible, as
P\leq I_{G}(\lambda)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable
T_{k}^{\prime}
-group and
K
be an abelian normal subgroup of
G
of order
r^{a}
, for some prime
r
and positive integers
a
and
k
. Assume, also
m^{\prime}_{G}(n)>1
, for some integer
n
.

(a)

Let
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
. Assume
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=n
and
\chi
is faithful, for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
. Then there exists integer
b
, such that
I_{G}(\lambda)/K
is a group of order
r^{2b}
.

(b)

Assume
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=n
and
\chi
is faithful for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
. Then, Sylow
r
-subgroups of
G/K
have order
r^{2c}
for some integer
c
, and Hall
r^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G/K
act on
K
, Frobeniusly.

Proof.

(a) As
G
is a
T_{k}^{\prime}
-group, and
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=n
, for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
, using Lemma
2.7
(a), it follows that the members of
{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{G}(\lambda)|\lambda)
must have distinct degrees. Now using Lemma
2.5
, we have
|I_{G}(\lambda)|/|K|=r^{2b}
, for some integer
b
.

(b) As
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
, then for each
R\in\mathrm{Syl}_{r}(G)
, we have
K\cap{\bf Z}(R)\not=1
. Therefore, as
K\cong{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
, there exists
1\not=\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
such that
I_{G}(\lambda)/K
, contains
R/K
. Now, using part (a),
|R/K|
must be an even power of
r
. Also
I_{G}(\lambda)/K
does not contain any
r^{\prime}
-element for each nontrivial
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(K)
, so it follows that Hall
r^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G/K
act on
K
, Frobeniusly.

3.
Non-solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-groups

In this section, we aim to characterize non-solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-groups, where
k\geq 1
is an integer, via the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S
be a finite non-abelian simple
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
. Then
S\cong{\rm PSL}_{2}(5)
or
{\rm PSL}_{2}(7)
.

Proof.

Since
S
is simple, for every nontrivial
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(S)
,
\ker(\chi)=1
and so
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/\chi(1)
. So
S
is a
T_{k}
-group and using
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
the result follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
M
be a non-abelian minimal normal subgroup of
G
such that
G/M\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Then
G
is not a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

We argue by the contradiction and suppose that
G
is a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
. By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
we get that,
M
contains an irreducible character
\lambda
which is extendible to
G
. Since
G/M
contains two distinct irreducible characters whose degrees are
3
, then according to Gallagher’s theorem (
[
9
, Corollary 6.17]
), there are
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
whose degrees are
3\lambda(1)
. We show that
\ker(\chi_{i})=1
, for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
\ker(\chi_{i})\not=1
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
, then
G=\ker(\chi_{i})\times M
, implying that
M
is a non-abelian simple group. Since
G/\ker(\chi_{i})
is a
T^{\prime}_{l}
-group, for some integer
l
, then by Lemma
3.1
, it should be isomorphic to
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Hence
G\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(q)^{2}
for some
q\in\{5,7\}
or
G\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)\times{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
. In all cases
G
has
4
distinct faithful characters of degree
9
, and
2
distinct irreducible characters with degree
3
and kernel
M
, implying that
m^{\prime}_{G}(|G|/9)=4
and
m^{\prime}_{G}(|G|/(3|M|))=2
, which is a contradiction. So
\ker(\chi_{1})=\ker(\chi_{2})=1
and
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{1})={\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{2})=|G|/(3\lambda(1))
. On the other hand,
G/M
has two distinct irreducible characters with the same codegree
|G|/(3|M|)
which is different from
|G|/(3\lambda(1))
, causing a contradiction.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
M
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
such that
G/M\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Then
G
is not a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Note that by Lemma
3.2
,
M
is abelian. Let
G
be a counterexample. First we prove that
G
is perfect.
If
G^{\prime}\cap M=1
, then
G=G^{\prime}\times M
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
be a nontrivial irreducible character and
\chi_{i}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G^{\prime})
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, be characters with degree
3
. Then,
\ker(\chi_{i}\times\lambda)=1
, for
i=1,2
, and so we get a contradiction by our assumption as
m^{\prime}_{G}(|G|/(3|M|))>1
and
m^{\prime}_{G}(|G|/3)>1
. Therefore,
M\leq G^{\prime}
and
G
is perfect. Let
1_{M}\not=\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
and
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
. If
\ker(\chi)\not=1
, then
G=\ker(\chi)\times M
, which is a contradiction, as
G
is perfect. Hence all characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|M)
are faithful. Then, by Lemma
2.7
(b),
|M|
is a divisor of
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
.

We remark that
C_{G}(M)=M
, since otherwise
M\leq\mathbb{M}(G/M)
, where
\mathbb{M}(G/M)
is the Schur multiplier of
G/M
, and
G\cong{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(q)
, for
q\in\{5,7\}
. It is a contradiction, as
G
is a
T_{k}^{\prime}
-group.

Note that
|G|/(3|M|)\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G/M)
has nontrivial multiplicity. We claim that
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=|G|/(3|M|)
, for every
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
. Suppose
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/(3|M|)
, for some
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
and
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
. Since
M
is an elementary abelian group, it follows that
|M|=2,4,5,7
or
8
. As
C_{G}(M)=M
, by the normalizer-centralizer theorem, the only possibility is
G/M\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
and
|M|=8
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.7
(a),
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/(3|M|)=8\times 7=|M|\times|I_{G}(\lambda)|/(|M|\theta(1))
, for some
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(I_{G}(\lambda)|\lambda)
. Note that,
A=I_{G}(\lambda)/M
is a subgroup of
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Hence, looking at the subgroups of
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
either
|A|=7
or
|A|=21
, implying that
8=|M|\leq|G:I_{G}(\lambda)|\in\{8,8\times 3\}
, which is impossible, by Remark
2.1
. Therefore, all characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|M)
have distinct codegrees and so different degrees. As,
C_{G}(M)=M
, we deduce that
I_{G}(\lambda)\not=G
, for every
1\not=\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
. So
I_{G}(\lambda)/M
is a proper subgroup of
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
and so it is solvable, for every
1\not=\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
. Now using Lemma
2.8
(a), we get that
I_{G}(\lambda)/M=1
or
C_{2}^{2}
, for all
1\not=\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
. Therefore, applying Lemma
2.5
,
{\mathrm{cd}}(G|M)\subseteq\{30,60\}
when
G/M\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
, and
{\mathrm{cd}}(G|M)\subseteq\{84,168\}
when
G/M\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Hence
{\mathrm{cd}}(G/M)\cap{\mathrm{cd}}(G|M)=\emptyset
.
Thus,
G
is a
T_{2}
-group, and using
[
16
]
we get a contradiction.

Proof of the Main Theorem case (a):

Let
G\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
. Then for every nontrivial
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
,
\ker(\chi)=1
and so
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/\chi(1)
. By
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
,
G
is a
T_{2}
-group and so
G
is a
T_{2}^{\prime}
-group.
Now, we concentrate on the ’only if’ part. Let
G
be a counterexample with minimal order. Let
M
be the last term of the derived series of
G
, and
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
contained in
M
such that
M/N
is a chief factor of
G
.

First, we claim that
G
is perfect. Suppose false. Hence
M
is a proper subgroup of
G
. Note that,
G/N
is a finite non-solvable
T^{\prime}_{l}
-group, for some integer
l\geq 1
. If
N\not=1
, then by the minimality of
|G|
,
G/N\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
, and consequently
G=M
, a contradiction. Hence,
N=1
. Let
C=C_{G}(M)
. If
C\not=1
, then by the minimality of
|G|
we deduce that
M\cong G/C\cong{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5)
or
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)
and so
G\cong M\times C
. Since
C
is solvable, it has a nontrivial linear character, say
\zeta
. Let also
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
be two distinct irreducible characters of
M
of degree
3
. Then
\chi_{1}\times\zeta
and
\chi_{2}\times\zeta
are two irreducible characters with the same degree
3
whose kernels are isomorphic to
\ker(\zeta)
. Therefore, they have the same codegree
|G|/(3|\ker(\zeta)|)
and it is different from
|G|/(3|C|)={\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{i}\times 1)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, a contradiction. Hence
C=1
and
G
is a subgroup of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)
.

Using Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, there exists
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(M)
which is extendible to
G
. We claim that
G/M
is a
D_{0}
-group or an abelian group. Otherwise,
G/M
possesses at least two distinct nonlinear irreducible characters with the same degree
d>1
, and also since
G/M
is solvable, it has at least two distinct linear characters. So by Gallagher’s theorem, there exists four distinct characters
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
, such that
\lambda_{1}(1)=\lambda_{2}(1)=\lambda(1)
and
\psi_{1}(1)=\psi_{2}(1)=\lambda(1)d
. Note that, as
M
is the only minimal normal subgroup of
G
,
\ker(\chi)=1
, for all
\chi\in\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}
. Hence,
m^{\prime}_{G}({\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda_{1}))>1
and
m^{\prime}_{G}({\mathrm{cod}}(\psi_{1}))>1
, implying that
G
is not a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
G/M
is a
D_{0}
-group or an abelian group, as desired. Now we claim that
|G/G^{\prime}|=2
. Otherwise, there exist at least two distinct nontrivial irreducible characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G/G^{\prime})
with the same codegree. On the other hand, by Gallagher’s theorem, there exist at least two distinct extensions of
\lambda
to
G
with the same codegree, which causes a contradiction. So
G/G^{\prime}
is a cyclic group of order
2
. Therefore, looking at the classification of the
D_{0}
-groups (see
[
2
]
), we get that the only possibilities for
G/M
is being isomorphic to either
S_{3}
or
C_{2}
. Recall that, by Gallagher’s theorem, the multiplicity of
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
, for some
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|\lambda)
is greater than one, where
\chi
is an extension of
\lambda
. Noting that
\ker(\psi)=1
, for all
\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|M)
, we deduce the multiplicity of every character degree in
{\mathrm{cd}}(G|M)\setminus\{\lambda(1)\}
must be trivial, as
G
is a
T_{k}^{\prime}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
. As
{\mathrm{cd}}(G/M)\subseteq\{2\}
, we deduce that
{\mathrm{cd}}(G|M)\cap{\mathrm{cd}}(G/M)=\emptyset
. Then, we conclude that
G
should be a
T_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
. Now, looking at the structure of non-solvable
T_{k}
-groups (see
[
16
]
), we get a contradiction.
\Box

Hence
G=M
is perfect. Let
K
be a maximal normal subgroup of
G
. Then
G/K
is a simple
T^{\prime}_{r}
-group, for some integer
r\geq 1
. Lemma
3.1
implies that
G/K\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5),{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)\}
. If
K=1
, then we are done, so we may assume
K
is not trivial. Let
L
be a normal subgroup of
G
contained in
K
such that
K/L
is a chief factor of
G
. Then we work on
\overline{G}=G/L
and we use the bar convention for this quotient group. As,
\overline{G}/\overline{K}\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(5),{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(7)\}
, by Lemma
3.3
, we get a contradiction.

4.
Solvabel
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group

In this section we aim to study solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-groups for some integer
k\geq 1
, and prove Case (b) of the main theorem, through Lemmas
4.1
-
4.9
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite non-abelian
p
-group, for some prime
p
. Then
G
is a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group for some integer
k\geq 1
, if and only if
p=2
and

G
is an extra-special group.

Proof.

First, we prove that
G
is a rational group and
p=2
. Let
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|G^{\prime})
and
\epsilon
be a
|G|
th root of unity and
\delta\in
Gal
(\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon)/\mathbb{Q})
. Then
\chi^{\delta}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
and
\chi^{\delta}(1)=\chi(1)
.
As
|\ker(\chi^{\delta})|=|\ker(\chi)|
, we have
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi^{\delta})={\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
. We show that
|G|/|\ker(\chi)G^{\prime}|
is not trivial. Suppose false, and take
K
to be a normal subgroup of
G
, contained in
G^{\prime}
, such that
G^{\prime}/K
is a cheif factor of
G
and
\ker(\chi)\cap G^{\prime}\leq K
. Hence,

\frac{G}{K}\cong\frac{G^{\prime}}{K}\times\frac{\ker(\chi)K}{K}

is an abelian group, implying that
G^{\prime}\leq K
, a contradiction. Therefore by Lemma
2.7
(c),
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\not=p
. So, as
G
is a
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k
, and
m^{\prime}_{G}(p)>1
, we get that
\chi^{\delta}=\chi
. Hence
\chi(x)
is rational for all
x\in G
. So, all nonlinear irreducible characters of
G
are rational-valued,
implying that
|G|
is even by
[
9
, (3.16), p. 46]
, and it follows that
p=2
. Note that by Lemma
2.6
, either
G/G^{\prime}\cong C_{4}
or
G/G^{\prime}
is an elementary abelian
2
-group. Since
G
is a
2
-group, the first case is not possible and so linear characters of
G
are also rational-valued. Hence
G
is a rational group, as wanted.

Assume
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
, contained in
G^{\prime}
, such that
G^{\prime}/K
is a chief factor of
G
. Write
\overline{G}=G/K
and adopt the bar convention for this quotient group.
Remark that
\overline{G}^{\prime}
is a central subgroup of
\overline{G}
of order
2
. Then,
{\bf Z}(\overline{G})\cong\overline{N}\times\overline{G}^{\prime}
, where
\overline{N}
is an elementary abelian group, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
.

We prove that
{\bf Z}(\overline{G}/\overline{N})=\overline{G}^{\prime}\overline{N}/\overline{N}
. Suppose false, then there exists an element
x
out of
\overline{G}^{\prime}\overline{N}
, such that for all elements
y\in\overline{G}
,
[x,y]\in\overline{N}
. On the other hand
[x,y]\in\overline{G}^{\prime}
, implying that
[x,y]=1
and so
x\in{\bf Z}(\overline{G})=\overline{G}^{\prime}\overline{N}
, a contradiction, as wanted. Therefore,
\overline{G}/\overline{N}
is an extra-special group. Now, we aim to prove that
\overline{N}=1
. Suppose false. Let
\overline{N}_{1},\overline{N}_{2}
be two different subgroups of
{\bf Z}(\overline{G})
of order
|\overline{N}|
, not containing
\overline{G}^{\prime}
. Then, replacing
\overline{N}
with each one of
\overline{N}_{i}
’s, for
i=1,2
, similar to the above discussion, we have
\overline{G}/\overline{N}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, are extra-special groups with the same order. Therefore,
\overline{G}
has two distinct nonlinear irreducible characters with the same codegree different from
2
, a contradiction. Thus,
\overline{N}=1
. Hence
\overline{G}
is an extra-special group. If
K
is trivial, we are done. Hence, we may assume
K
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Then
K\leq{\bf Z}(G)
.

Note that,
K={\bf Z}(G)
, as otherwise
{\bf Z}(G)=K\times T
, where
TK/K={\bf Z}(\overline{G})
(as
{\bf Z}(G)/K\leq{\bf Z}(G/K)
and
|{\bf Z}(G/K)|=|G^{\prime}/K|=2
), hence replacing
K
by
T
in the above discussion, we have
G/T
and
G/K
are isomorphic and so
G
has two distinct nonlinear irreducible characters with the same codegree, a contradiction. Then,
{\bf Z}(G)=K
and
\chi
is faithful for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
, yielding that
|G/K|
is an square, by Lemmas
2.7
(c) and
2.8
(b), a contradiction. So
K=1
, as wanted.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group, for some integer
k\geq 1
, and
G/G^{\prime}\cong C_{p}^{\alpha}
, for some prime
p
and integer
\alpha
. Assume also
G^{\prime}
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
whose order is co-prime to
|G/G^{\prime}|
. Then one of the following occurs:

(1)

G\cong C_{2}\times S_{3}
;

(2)

G\cong D_{2q}
for some prime
q\geq 3
; or

(3)

G
is a Frobenius group
C_{2}^{\beta}\rtimes C_{2^{\beta}-1}
, where
p=2^{\beta}-1
. In particular
\beta
is odd.

Proof.

Let
|G^{\prime}|=q^{\beta}
, for some prime
q
and integer
\beta
and
C_{G}(G^{\prime})=G^{\prime}\times C
, where
C
is an elementary abelian
p
-group. Then
C\leq{\bf Z}(G)
and
G=C\times T
, where
T
is a subgroup of
G
containing
G^{\prime}
. Remark that
T
is not isomorphic to
G^{\prime}
. Note that,
\chi
is faithful, for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(T|T^{\prime})
, as
\ker(\chi)\leq C_{T}(T^{\prime})=T^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
and
\ker(\chi)\cap T^{\prime}=1
.

Firstly, assume that
|T|_{p}>2
, then
m^{\prime}_{T}(p)>1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.7
(c), we get that all characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(T|T^{\prime})
have distinct degrees and so
T
is a
D_{0}
-group. Looking at the structure of
D_{0}
-groups we get that
T
is a Frobenius group, with cyclic Frobenius complement of order
p
and

p=q^{\beta}-1
. This is not possible, unless
q=2
and
\beta
is odd. Hence
p\not=2
. Now we show that in this case
C
is trivial. Assume,
C=C_{p}^{\alpha_{1}}
and
\alpha_{1}\geq 1
. Since
p\not=2
, we deduce that
|{\mathrm{Irr}}(C)\setminus\{1_{C}\}|>1
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(C)
be two distinct nontrivial characters and
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(T)
be the only nonlinear irreducible character of
T
. Then
|\ker(\lambda_{1}\times\chi)|=|\ker(\lambda_{2}\times\chi)|=|C|/p
and
\lambda_{1}\times\chi(1)=\lambda_{2}\times\chi(1)=\chi(1)
, so
{\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda_{1}\times\chi)={\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda_{2}\times\chi)
. Note that
{\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda_{1}\times\chi)
is not equal to
p
, by Lemma
2.7
(c), a contradiction, as both multiplicities of
{\mathrm{cod}}(\lambda_{1}\times\chi)
and
p
are not
trivial. Hence
C=1
. So
G
is the described group in case (3) of the Lemma.

Now we assume
|T|_{p}=p=2
. Recall that,
C_{T}(T^{\prime})=T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
is a minimal normal subgroup of
T\cong G/C
, where
T
is a Frobenius group whose Frobenius complements are cyclic groups of order
2
. Therefore,
T\cong G/C\cong D_{2q}
. First assume
q>3
. We prove that
C
is trivial. Suppose false. In this case
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G/C)
contains two distinct nonlinear irreducible characters
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(C)
be a nontrivial irreducible character. Then,
|\ker(\chi_{1}\times\lambda)|=|\ker(\chi_{2}\times\lambda)|=|\ker(\lambda)|=|C|/2
and so
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{1}\times\lambda)={\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{2}\times\lambda)=2{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{1})
, hence
m^{\prime}_{G}(2{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{1}))>1
. Noting that
m^{\prime}_{T}({\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{1}))=(q-1)/2>1
, we get a contradiction. Therefore
C=1
. So
G\cong D_{2q}
, a described group in case (2) of the lemma.

The last possibility is that
q=3
and
G\cong C_{2}^{\alpha-1}\times D_{6}
. Hence, in this case
m^{\prime}_{G}(6)=2^{\alpha-1}-1
and
m^{\prime}_{G}(2)=2^{\alpha}-1
. Therefore, either
\alpha=1
or
\alpha=2
, which means that either
G\cong C_{2}\times S_{3}
or
G\cong D_{6}
, which are the groups in case (1) and (2) of the lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group for some integer
k\geq 1
and
K<G^{\prime}<G
be a normal series of
G
. Assume also
G/K
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group
C_{2^{\beta}}\rtimes C_{p}
, for some integer
\beta
and a prime number
p=2^{\beta}-1
. Then,
K=1
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a counterexample with minimal order. Therefore, we may assume
K
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. First of all, we show that
\ker(\chi)=1
, for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
. Suppose false. Then, there exists
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
such that
\ker(\chi)K/K
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G/K
, implying that
G^{\prime}\cong K\times T
, where
T\cong\ker(\chi)
(Note that
\ker(\chi)\not\cong G/K
, as
|G/G^{\prime}|
is a prime). Now, we work on group
\overline{G}=G/T
, and we adopt bar convention for this quotient group. Note that
\overline{K}=\overline{G}^{\prime}
. As
|G/G^{\prime}|
is prime, we deduce that
(|\overline{G^{\prime}}|,|\overline{G}/\overline{G}^{\prime}|)=1
. So the hypothesis of the Lemma
4.2
, is provided for
\overline{G}
. Applying Lemma
4.2
,
|K|=|\overline{G}^{\prime}|=2^{\beta}
. Hence both groups
G/K
and
G/T
have faithful irreducible characters with the same codegree
2^{\beta}
and so
m^{\prime}_{G}(2^{\beta})>1
, which is a contradiction, as
m^{\prime}_{G}(p)>1
. So
T=1
, as wanted.

Hence, by Lemmas
2.7
(c) and
2.8
(b), we get that
G/K
acts on
K
Frobeniusly, which is a contradiction, as
G/K
is a Frobenius group itself and it is well-known that a complement of a Frobenius group is not Frobenius.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group for some integer

, and
K<G^{\prime}<G
be a normal series of

.

Proof.

Let
G
be a counterexample with minimal order. Then, we may assume

of order

, for some prime
p
and integer

. Let
T=\ker(\chi)
for some

. Then,

Let

. Assume

. Then

, where

. Therefore,

and

. Note that,

and both

and

contain faithful irreducible characters whose codegrees are
3
. So
m^{\prime}_{G}(3)>1
, a contradiction. Therefore,

or
1
. Set

. Now, we consider the group
\overline{G}=G/S
and we adopt the bar convention for this quotient group. Then
\ker(\chi_{1})=1
, for all
\chi_{1}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\overline{G}|\overline{K})

\overline{G}/\overline{K}\cong S_{3}
or

. By Lemmas
2.7
(c) and

(b),
\overline{G}/\overline{K}
acts on

Finally, let

. If

contains a Sylow

-subgroup of

, then

and so

. Hence,

has a normal abelian subgroup
L
of order

. Looking at the classification of groups of order 24, we see

or

SmallGroup
(24,8)
. In the first case there exists
2
distinct faithful characters with the same degree

. In the second case
S=
Socle
(G)=C_{3}\times C_{2}
, so every

is faithful, and

is a group of order

, for all
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(S)
of order

above each irreducible character

of order

, a contradiction.

So

does not contain a Sylow

-subgroup of

and hence

or 1. Let
S=\Pi_{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)}\ker(\chi)
. Note that
S\cong C_{2}
or 1. Setting
\overline{G}=G/S
, we see that
\ker(\chi)=1
, for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\overline{G}|\overline{K})
. Then by Lemmas
2.7
(c) and
2.8
(a),
I_{\overline{G}}(\lambda)/\overline{K}\cong C_{2}^{2}
or
1
, for all
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\overline{K})
. Applying Lemma
2.5
, we have
{\mathrm{cd}}(\overline{G}|\overline{K})\subseteq\{6,12\}
. Since
{\mathrm{cd}}(\overline{G}|\overline{K})\cap{\mathrm{cd}}(\overline{G}/\overline{K})=\emptyset

must be
a
T_{1}
-group(

-group).
Looking at the classification of the

-groups(see

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a solvable
T^{\prime}_{k}
-group for some

, and
K<G^{\prime}<G
be a normal series of

and

is a minimal normal subgroup of

of order

, for some prime

. Then

.

Proof.

We show that
C_{G}(K)=K
. Suppose false, then
G^{\prime}=T\times K
, where
T\cong C_{3}
. As
|G/G^{\prime}|=2
, we deduce that

. Therefore,
G/T
satisfies Lemma
4.2
, and so
|K|=r
is an odd prime other than
3
. By Lemma
4.3
we get a contradiction. Hence
C_{G}(K)=K
. Then
\chi
is faithful for all
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)
. Remark that,
I_{G}(\lambda)/K
does not contain the Sylow
3
-subgroup of

, for any

, as otherwise

, contradicting the fact that

. Hence,
I_{G}(\lambda)/K\cong 1
or

, for every

, implying that

-orbit sizes of nontrivial elements of

are either

or

. As Sylow subgroups of

are cyclic,

extends to

, for all

. If

, then

(a). Also, if

Let

be the number of

-orbits of size

. As

is a

-group, by the above discussion, we have the following possibilities for the number of

is a Frobenius group.

, we get that

. On the other hand, by Lemma

(b) the Sylow

-subgroup of

, Frobeniusly, yielding that

. Since

, these five characters belong to at least 3 distinct

. If there exist two

-orbits of size

, which is a contradiction. If there exists only one

-orbit with size

, then it follows that we have one faithful irreducible character of degree

and
8
distinct faithful irreducible characters of degree

. On the other hand
|G|=96<6^{2}+8.3^{2}

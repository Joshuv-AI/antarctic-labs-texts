Existence and Asymptotic Behavior of Ground States for Rotating Bose-Einstein Condensates
Yujin Guo, Yong Luo and Shuangjie Peng

Abstract
We study ground states of two-dimensional Bose-Einstein condensates with repulsive ( a > 0 a>0 ) or attractive ( a < 0 a<0 ) interactions in a trap V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) rotating at the velocity Ω \Omega . It is known that there exist critical parameters a ∗ > 0 a^{*}>0 and Ω ∗ := Ω ∗ ​ ( V ⁡ ( x ) ) > 0 \Omega^{*}:=\Omega^{*}(V(x))>0 such that if Ω > Ω ∗ \Omega>\Omega^{*} , then there is no ground state for any a ∈ ℝ a\in{\mathbb{R}} ; if 0 ≤ Ω < Ω ∗ 0\leq\Omega<\Omega^{*} , then ground states exist if and only if a ∈ ( − a ∗ , + ∞ ) a\in(-a^{*},+\infty) . As a completion of the existing results, in this paper, we focus on the critical case where 0 < Ω = Ω ∗ < + ∞ 0<\Omega=\Omega^{*}<+\infty and classify the existence and nonexistence of ground states for a ∈ ℝ a\in{\mathbb{R}} . Moreover, for a suitable class of radially symmetric traps V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) , employing the inductive symmetry method, we prove that up to a constant phase, the ground states must be real-valued, unique and free of vortices as Ω ↘ 0 \Omega\searrow 0 , no matter whether the interactions of the condensates are repulsive or not.



Keywords:
Bose-Einstein condensate; rotational velocity; ground states; free of vortices

[

,

,
24
,

Various interesting quantum phenomena have been so far observed in BECs experiments, such as the critical-mass collapse

]
, the center-of-mass rotation in rotating traps

As addressed in
[

24
,
27

can be well described

F_{\Omega,a}(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u|^{4}\big)dx-\Omega\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}x^{\perp}\cdot(iu,\,\nabla u)dx,\ \ u\in{\mathcal{H}},

(1.1)

where the space

is defined as

{\mathcal{H}}:=\Big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C}):\ \int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}V(x)|u|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\},

(1.2)

x^{\perp}=(-x_{2},x_{1})
with

(iu,\,\nabla u)=i(u\nabla\bar{u}-\bar{u}\nabla u)/2

Here the two-body interaction of the rotating BECs can be either repulsive (

e(\Omega,a):=\inf_{\{u\in{\mathcal{H}},\,\|u\|^{2}_{2}=1\}}F_{\Omega,a}(u),\,\ \hbox{$\Omega\geq 0$, $a\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

(1.3)

1.1

{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by (
1.2

We first introduce the non-rotational case

F_{a}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u|^{4}\big)dx.

When

1.1
Main results

In this subsection we shall introduce the main results of the present paper. We are first concerned with the existence and non-existence of minimizers for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
, provided that the critical rotational velocity
\Omega^{*}=\Omega^{*}(V(x))
defined in (
1.9
) satisfies
\Omega^{*}<+\infty
.
Towards this aim, we consider

V(x)=A|x|^{2}+W(x),\,\ \hbox{where $A>0$ and $W(x)\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{2})$ satisfies $\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{W(x)}{|x|^{2}}=0$,}

(1.11)

so that the assumption (
1.8
) holds in a natural way.
Under the assumption (
1.11
), our first main result of this paper is devoted to the following existence and non-existence of minimizers for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
a^{*}>0
is defined by (
1.6
), and
V(x)
satisfies (
1.11
) such that
\Omega^{*}=2\sqrt{A}>0
is defined by (

(i).

If
W(x)
satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}W(x)=+\infty
, then

admits minimizers if and only if
a\in(-a^{*},\infty)
.

(ii).

If
W(x)\equiv C\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
e(\Omega^{*},a)
admits minimizers if and only if
a\in(-a^{*},0]
.

When the non-constant function
W(x)
is bounded uniformly in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the existence and non-existence of minimizers for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
are more complicated. The following theorem addresses this case:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
a^{*}>0
is defined by (
1.6
), and
V(x)
satisfies (
1.11
) such that
\Omega^{*}=2\sqrt{A}>0
is defined by (
1.9
). If
W(x)\not\equiv const.
further satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}W(x)=B\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then we have

(i).

If
W(x)>B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, then

admits no minimizer for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii).

If
W(x)<B

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, then there exists a constant

such that

(a).

If

, there is at least one minimizer for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
.

(b).

If
a\in(-\infty,-a^{*}]\cup(a_{\ast},+\infty)
, there is no minimizer for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
(ii) shows that the constant
a_{\ast}\in(0,+\infty]
of Theorem
1.2
(ii) can be characterized as

a_{\ast}:=\sup\big\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,e(\Omega^{*},a)<2\sqrt{A}+B\,\big\}.

(1.12)

We remark that the energy

.
Theorem

(ii) does not address the existence of minimizers for

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Additionally, if

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\big[B-W(x)\big]|x|^{s}>0,\quad\hbox{where $0<s<2$,}

then the following corollary shows that

Corollary 1.3

Under the assumptions of Theorem

satisfies (

.

Related to Corollary

The above results also illustrate that whether the minimizers of

It should also be mentioned that the mass-subcritical version of

-\Delta\psi+2i\,(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla\psi)+|x|^{2}\psi=\lambda\psi\quad\hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2}

were fully employed.
Note from
[

that the first eigenvalue of (

\lambda_{1}=2

and all the first eigenfunctions are given by

S:=\Big\{e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}f(x):f(x)\,\ \hbox{is any entire function

where the entire function means the complex analytic function on

.

We now recall the non-rotational case of
e(\Omega,a)
at
\Omega=0
, i.e.,
e(a):=e(0,a)
defined in (
1.4
). By the variational theory, any minimizer
u_{a}
of

satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta u_{a}+V(x)u_{a}=\mu_{a}u_{a}-a|u_{a}|^{2}u_{a}\ \ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(1.16)

where
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the associated Lagrange multiplier satisfying

\mu_{a}=e(a)+\frac{a}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{a}|^{4}dx.

(1.17)

Since
F_{a}(u)\geq F_{a}(|u|)
, we obtain that
|u_{a}|
is also a minimizer of
e(a)
and
F_{a}(u_{a})=F_{a}(|u_{a}|)
holds. Moreover, we can deduce from (

by the maximal principle. Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u_{a}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\nabla|u_{a}|\big|^{2}dx
and
|u_{a}|>0
, we conclude that there exists a constant
\theta\in(0,2\pi]
such that
u_{a}(x)\equiv|u_{a}(x)|e^{i\theta}
holds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Hence, up to a constant phase, any minimizer
u_{a}
of
e(a)
must be positive.

Under the assumption that the trap
V(x)
is radially symmetric and satisfies

(
V
).

V(x)=V(|x|)\in C^{1,\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
V^{\prime}(|x|)\geq 0
,
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=+\infty
and there exists some constant
p\geq 2
such that
|V(x)|,\,\ |\nabla V(x)|\leq C|x|^{p}
as
|x|\to\infty
,

combining
[

with
[
19
, Corallary 1.1]
yields immediately the following uniqueness of positive minimizers for

:

Theorem B.

(
[
27
,
19
]
) Suppose that
a^{*}>0
is defined by (
1.6
), and
V(x)
satisfies the assumption
(V)
. For any
a\in[0,+\infty)
and a.e.

, then

admits a unique positive minimizer

, which must be radially symmetric.

Based on the uniqueness of Theorem B, the second main result of this paper is the following nonexistence of vortices for any fixed
a\in[0,+\infty)
and a.e.
a\in(-a^{*},0)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
a^{*}>0
is defined by (
1.6
),
V(x)
satisfies (
1.8
) and the assumption
(V)
. Then for any fixed
a\in[0,+\infty)
or a.e.
a\in(-a^{*},0)
, there exists a sufficiently small

such that, up to a constant phase, all minimizers of

are real-valued, unique and free of vortices for any
\Omega\in[0,\delta)

The assumption

of Theorem

can be removed in the repulsive case, since it is used only in the attractive case to guarantee the uniqueness of positive minimizers for
e(a)
.
The proof of Theorem

gives us that for any sufficiently small
\Omega\in[0,\delta)
, any minimizer

of

satisfies

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where
\theta:=\theta(\Omega)\in[0,2\pi)

\Omega
. Thus
|u_{\Omega}|=|u_{0}|>0
never vanishes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
\Omega\searrow 0
, which implies that it is free of vortices for ground states of
e(\Omega,a)
as
\Omega\searrow 0
.

We next illustrate the general strategy of proving Theorem
1.4
. Denote
\{\Omega_{n}>0\}
to be a sequence satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega_{n}=0
. Choose some suitable constant
\theta_{n}\in[0,2\pi)
such that the minimizer
u_{n}
of
e(\Omega_{n},a)
satisfies the following orthogonal condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{0}Im(u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})dx\equiv 0\,\ \hbox{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}$,}

(1.18)

where
u_{0}
is the unique positive minimizer of
e(a)
, see Theorem B.
As the first step, we shall follow (
1.18
) to prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}=u_{0}\,\ \hbox{uniformly in}\,\ L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C}).

(1.19)

Rewrite now
u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}=q_{n}+ir_{n}
, where
q_{n}
and
r_{n}
denote the real and imaginary parts of
u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}
, respectively.
One can show that
(q_{n},r_{n})
satisfies the following elliptic system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{n}q_{n}&=\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla r_{n})\ \ &\mbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\mathcal{L}_{n}r_{n}&=-\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla q_{n})\ \ &\mbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.20)

where the operator
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{n}:=-\Delta+V(x)-\mu_{n}+a|u_{n}|^{2},

(1.21)

and
\mu_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier. The refined analysis gives that
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{0}
as
n\to\infty
,
where
\mu_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the Lagrange multiplier associated to
u_{0}
. By applying (
1.19
), we then follow the system (
1.20
) to investigate the linearized
operators
\mathcal{L}:=-\Delta+V(x)-\mu_{0}+au_{0}^{2}
and
\mathcal{N}:=-\Delta+V(x)-\mu_{0}+3au_{0}^{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. As the second step, this yields finally Corollary
4.1
on the first estimates of
q_{n}-u_{0}
and
r_{n}
as
n\to\infty
.

Stimulated by the inductive symmetry method (cf.
[
17
]
), in the third step we shall follow Corollary
4.1
to further prove the inductive process of Lemma
4.2
in the following sense: once we have an estimate
on the non-radial part of
q_{n}-u_{0}
, then a better estimate on the non-radial part of
q_{n}-u_{0}
can
be derived. As a consequence, we finally prove that the non-radial part of
q_{n}-u_{0}
is arbitrarily small and
r_{n}\equiv 0
as
n\to\infty
. This implies finally that
u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}\equiv q_{n}\equiv u_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
\Omega_{n}\searrow 0
, and Theorem
1.4
is therefore proved, see Section 4 for more details.

Similar nonexistence results of Theorem
1.4
in the repulsive case
a>0
were obtained in
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
and the references therein by applying vortex ball constructions, jacobian estimates, and some other arguments, all of which make full use of the refined energy analysis and the Ginzburg-Landau theory. As far as we know, the above mentioned arguments however seem not applicable for the attractive case of
e(\Omega,a)
, since it does not admit the variational structure of the Ginzburg-Landau type. The above proof strategy shows that Theorem
1.4
is established essentially by the inductive symmetry method, which was first imposed in
[

]
to study the nonexistence of vortices for
e(\Omega,a)
in a different situation where
0<\Omega<\Omega^{*}
is fixed and
a\searrow-a^{*}
. This approach relies more on the refined analysis of the governing equations of minimizers. The advantage of this approach lies in the fact that Theorem
1.4
can be proved by a uniform approach,
i.e,
no matter whether the interactions of the condensates are repulsive (
a>0
) or not. As a byproduct, Theorem
1.4
therefore yields the first result that the nonexistence of vortices for repulsive BECs under rotation can be investigated by the inductive symmetry method.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we shall prove Theorem
1.1
on the existence and non-existence of minimizers for
e(\Omega^{*},a)
. In Section 3, we first investigate the limit behavior of

as

, based on which we then address the detailed analysis of the linearized problem (

). In Section 4 we shall complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
by the inductive symmetry method.

2

e(\Omega^{*},a)

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem

where the equality is attained (cf.

]
) at the unique positive radial solution

w=w(|x|)>0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w^{4}dx,

(2.2)

and

w(x)\,,\ |\nabla w(x)|=O(|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-|x|})\quad\text{as \ $|x|\to\infty$.}

(2.3)

Given any vector function
\mathcal{A}\in L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we have the following diamagnetic inequality
[
25
]
:

|(\nabla-i\mathcal{A})u|^{2}\geq\big|\nabla|u|\big|^{2}\,\ \hbox{a.e. on }\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\ \,u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C}).

(2.4)

We also recall from
[

the following compactness lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose

satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=\infty
, then the embedding

Applying Lemma

as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
A=1

\Omega^{*}=2
. If

e(2,a)=e(\Omega^{*},a)

.

(i). Assume
a\in(-a^{*},+\infty)
and
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}W(x)=+\infty
, hence we get that
e(2,a)>-\infty
. By the constraint condition, we may assume
W(x)\geq 0
for simplicity. Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence of
e(2,a)
. We then deduce from (
2.1
) and (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle e(2,a)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}+W(x)|u_{n}|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\big)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\frac{a^{*}+sgn(a)|a|}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}+W(x)|u_{n}|^{2}\Big)dx,

which implies that
\{u_{n}\}

, where

is defined in (

Taking a subsequence if necessary, now we may assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C})
as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, since
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}W(x)=+\infty
, we also obtain from Lemma
2.1
that up to a subsequence if necessary,
\{|u_{n}|\}
converges to
\{|u_{0}|\}
strongly in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}})

(2\leq p<\infty)
. We thus have

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}=1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
. By the weak lower semicontinuity, we further obtain that
e(2,a)=F_{2,a}(u_{0})
, and hence
e(2,a)
admits a minimizer
u_{0}
if
a\in(-a^{*},+\infty)
.

(ii). Without loss of generality, by the constraint we may assume
W(x)\equiv C=0
so that
\Omega^{*}=2
, and

F_{2,a}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u|^{2}dx+\frac{a}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|u|^{4}dx,\ \ u\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.5)

The proof is then divided into two cases:
a\in(-a^{*},0]
and
a\in(0,+\infty)
.

(1). We first consider the case
a\in(-a^{*},0]
. The existence of minimizers of
e(2,a)=e(\Omega^{*},a)
at

is trivial. Indeed, it follows from
[
29
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
11
, Remark 2.5]
) that if
u\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies
\|u\|^{2}_{2}=1
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u|^{2}dx\geq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}dx=2,

(2.6)

where the equality is attained at
u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}
. On the other hand, by choosing
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}
as a test function, one can obtain that
e(2,0)\leq 2
, which hence yields that

e(2,0)=2
. Therefore,
e(2,0)
admits at least one minimizer which is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}
.

Next, we shall use the concentration-compactness lemma
[
28
]
to deal with the case
a\in(-a^{*},0)
. Note from (
2.1
) and (
2.4
) that for any
u\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\|u\|^{2}_{2}=1
,

F_{2,a}(u)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\nabla|u|\big|^{2}dx+\frac{a}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{4}dx\geq\frac{a^{*}-|a|}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{4}dx.

(2.7)

Therefore,
e(2,a)
is bounded from below in this case. Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence of
e(2,a)
, i.e.,
\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}=1
and

e(2,a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{2,a}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}-\frac{|a|}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\Big)dx\Big\}.

We then derive from (
2.7
) that there exists a constant
C(a)>0
, depending only on
a
, such that
\{u_{n}\}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{n}|^{4}dx\leq C(a)\,\ \hbox{and}\,\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}dx\leq C(a).

(2.8)

Denote

f_{n}(x):=|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}+|u_{n}|^{2}\geq 0,

(2.9)

so that the nonnegative sequence
\{f_{n}\}
is bounded uniformly in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
in view of (
2.8
). By the concentration-compactness lemma
[
28
, Lemma I.1]
, either the vanishing or the dichotomy or the compactness holds for some subsequence of
\{f_{n}\}
.

We first claim that the vanishing

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(y)}f_{n}(x)dx=0,\quad\forall R>0

(2.10)

cannot occur. On the contrary, suppose that (
2.10
) holds true.
We then get from
[
36
, Lemma 1.21]
that up to a subsequence if necessary,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{n}|^{4}dx=0.

(2.11)

Following
[
29
, Theorem 2]
, we thus obtain from (

e(2,a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}-\frac{|a|}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\Big)dx\Big\}\geq 2,

which however contradicts to the estimate

e(2,a)\leq F_{2,a}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\Big)=2-\frac{|a|}{\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-2|x|^{2}}dx<2.

(2.12)

Hence, the vanishing cannot occur.

To rule out the dichotomy, we first claim that

e(2,a)<e_{\lambda}(2,a)+e_{1-\lambda}(2,a)\qquad\hbox{for any $\lambda\in(0,1)$,}

(2.13)

where

Indeed, one can check from the definition of

that

\lambda F_{2,a}(u)=F_{2,a}(\sqrt{\lambda}u)-\frac{|a|\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{4}dx\qquad\hbox{for any $\lambda\in(0,1)$.}

(2.15)

Moreover, let
\{\sqrt{\lambda}v_{1,n}\}
and
\{\sqrt{1-\lambda}v_{2,n}\}
be the minimizing sequences of
e_{\lambda}(2,a)
and
e_{1-\lambda}(2,a)
, respectively. The argument of proving the above vanishing gives that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|v_{1,n}|^{4}dx\geq\delta\quad\hbox{and}\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|v_{2,n}|^{4}dx\geq\delta,

(2.16)

where
\delta>0
is independent of
n
.
Combining (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) then yields that

\displaystyle e(2,a)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big[\lambda F_{2,a}(v_{1,n})+(1-\lambda)F_{2,a}(v_{2,n})\Big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big[F_{2,a}\big(\sqrt{\lambda}v_{1,n}\big)+F_{2,a}\big(\sqrt{1-\lambda}v_{2,n}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{|a|\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|v_{1,n}|^{4}+|v_{2,n}|^{4})dx\Big]

\displaystyle\leq e_{\lambda}(2,a)+e_{1-\lambda}(2,a)-|a|\lambda(1-\lambda)\delta

\displaystyle<e_{\lambda}(2,a)+e_{1-\lambda}(2,a),

and the claim (
2.13
) is hence proved.

Based on (
2.13
), we now prove that the dichotomy cannot occur. Indeed, taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{n}(x)dx=\alpha>0.

(2.17)

By contradiction, suppose that the dichotomy occurs. Then there exist
\beta\in(0,\alpha)
,
0<2R_{0}<R_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
and
\{y_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\Big|\int_{B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}f_{n}(x)dx-\beta\Big|\leq\varepsilon,\,\ \Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}/B_{2R_{n}}(y_{n})}f_{n}(x)dx-(\alpha-\beta)\Big|\leq\varepsilon\,\ \hbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

(2.18)

Set

u_{1n}(x):=\psi\big(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}}\big)u_{n}(x),\,\ \,\ u_{2n}(x):=\phi\big(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{n}}\big)u_{n}(x),

where
0\leq\psi(x)\leq 1
and
0\leq\phi(x)\leq 1
are smooth cut-off functions and satisfy:
\psi\equiv 1
if
|x|\leq 1
, and
\psi\equiv 0
if
|x|\geq 2
;
\phi\equiv 0
if
|x|\leq 1
, and
\phi\equiv 1
if
|x|\geq 2
.
By the definition of
\{u_{1n}\}
and
\{u_{2n}\}
, we have

dist(supp
u_{1n}
, supp
u_{2n}
)
\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

(2.19)

We claim that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\Big|\|u_{1n}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}}-\beta\Big|\leq C\varepsilon,\quad\Big|\|u_{2n}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}}-(\alpha-\beta)\Big|\leq C\varepsilon,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u_{1n}-u_{2n}\|_{H_{A}^{1}}\leq C\varepsilon,

where we denote

H_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}}):=\big\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}}):\,(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}})\big\}

(2.21)

associated with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{A}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We now prove the claim (
2.20
). Actually, we obtain from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\Big|\|u_{1n}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}}-\beta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\|u_{1n}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}}-\int_{B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}f_{n}(x)dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}f_{n}(x)dx-\beta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{2R_{0}}(y_{n})/B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}\Big\{\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})\big[\psi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}})u_{n}\big]\big|^{2}+|\psi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}})u_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}\big|^{2}-|u_{n}|^{2}\Big\}dx\Big|+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R_{0}}(y_{n})/B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}\Big(3|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}|^{2}+2\Big|\nabla\psi\Big(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}}\Big)\Big|^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}+2|u_{n}|^{2}\Big)dx+\varepsilon

Similarly, we can also get that as
n\to\infty
,

\big|\|u_{2n}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}}-(\beta-\alpha)\big|\leq C\varepsilon.

Following (
2.17
) and (
2.18

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u_{1n}-u_{2n}\|_{H_{A}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R_{n}}(y_{n})/B_{R_{0}}(y_{n})}\Big|\big(\nabla-ix^{\perp}\big)\Big(1-\psi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}})-\phi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{n}})\Big)u_{n}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big|\Big(1-\psi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{0}})-\phi(\frac{x-y_{n}}{R_{n}})\Big)u_{n}\Big|^{2}dx

We now conclude from above that the claim (

Since the embedding

is continuous, cf.
[

, we derive from (

\big|F_{2,a}(u_{n})-F_{2,a}(u_{1n})-F_{2,a}(u_{2n})\big|\leq C\varepsilon,

(2.22)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(|u_{n}|^{p}-|u_{1n}|^{p}-|u_{2n}|^{p}\big)dx\Big|\leq C\varepsilon\quad\hbox{for any $p\in[2,\infty)$}.

(2.23)

Moreover, following (
2.1
), (
2.6
) and (
2.20

\delta>0
such that for

,

\begin{split}F_{2,a}(u_{in})&\geq(1-\frac{a}{a^{*}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{in}|^{2}dx\\
&\geq\frac{2(a^{*}-a)}{3a^{*}}\|u_{in}\|^{2}_{H_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geq\delta\quad\hbox{for all $n$, }\end{split}

(2.24)

which then implies that there exist subsequences of
\{u_{1n}\}
and
\{u_{2n}\}
, still denoted by
\{u_{1n}\}
and
\{u_{2n}\}
, satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{1n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\theta
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{2n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=1-\theta
for some constant

by applying (
2.23
).
Since
e_{\lambda}(2,a)
is continuous in
\lambda
, we conclude from (

2.24

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{2,a}(u_{1n})\geq e_{\theta}(2,a),\,\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{2,a}(u_{2n})\geq e_{1-\theta}(2,a),

which then yield that
e(2,a)\geq e_{\theta}(2,a)+e_{1-\theta}(2,a)
holds for some constant
\theta\in(0,1)
in view of (
2.22
). This however contradict to
(
2.13

Since both the vanishing and the dichotomy cannot occur, we now conclude that only the compactness
occurs for the sequence
\{f_{n}\}
, i.e., there exists
\{x_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}/B_{R}(x_{n})}f_{n}(x)dx\leq\varepsilon,\quad\hbox{if $R=R(\varepsilon)>0$ is large enough.}

(2.25)

Since
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C})
, we obtain from (
2.9
) and (
2.25
) that
\{u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n})\}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
by
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
and thus relatively compact in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

(2<p<+\infty)
by Hölder inequality. Note that

\tilde{u}_{n}(x):=\hat{u}_{n}(x)e^{-ix_{n}\cdot x^{\perp}}=u_{n}(x+x_{n})e^{-ix_{n}\cdot x^{\perp}}

satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})\tilde{u}_{n}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\big(\nabla-ix^{\perp}\big)\big(\hat{u}_{n}e^{-ix_{n}\cdot x^{\perp}}\big)\big|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}\big|^{2}dx.

(2.26)

Moreover, since
|\tilde{u}_{n}(x)|=|u_{n}(x+x_{n})|
holds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, by (
2.17
) and (
2.25
) we now derive from above that there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{\tilde{u}_{n}\}
, of
\{\tilde{u}_{n}\}
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{u}_{n}=u_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in $L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})$ ($2\leq p<\infty$) for some $u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}$,}

(2.27)

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{n}\big|^{2}dx=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})\tilde{u}_{n}\big|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{0}\big|^{2}dx.

Therefore,
e(2,a)
admits at least one minimizer
u_{0}
in the case
a\in(-a^{*},0)
.

(2). We now consider the case
a\in(0,\infty)
.
By selecting a suitable eigenvalue function of (
1.14
) as a test function, we first prove that

e(2,a)\leq 2,\ \ a\in(0,\infty).

(2.28)

Towards the proof of (
2.28
), motivated by
[
2

3

, we define the following
lattice

\ell:=v(\mathbb{Z}\oplus e^{\frac{2i\pi}{3}}\mathbb{Z})=v(m+ne^{\frac{2i\pi}{3}}),

(2.29)

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
are arbitrary integers, and the constant
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is chosen such that
\frac{\sqrt{3}v^{2}}{2}>\pi
. Let
Q
be a unit cell (i.e., a regular hexagon) centered at the origin, whose area satisfies
|Q|=\frac{\sqrt{3}v^{2}}{2}
. Here
\frac{1}{|Q|}
denotes an average spatial density of the point in the lattice.
Choose the following test function

\psi_{R}(z)=A_{R}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}}\prod_{j\in\ell\bigcap B_{R}(0)}(z-j),

(2.30)

where we identify any complex number
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
with the point
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and the Lebesgue measure
dz
with
dxdy
, respectively. Here
A_{R}>0
is chosen properly such that
\|\psi_{R}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=1
. Obviously,
\psi_{R}\in S
, where
S
is defined by (
1.15
), and
(2,\psi_{R})
is the first eigenpair of (
1.14
). We hence obtain from (
1.14

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})\psi_{R}\big|^{2}dx=2.

(2.31)

Moreover, it follows from
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
that as
R\to\infty
,

|\psi_{R}(z)|\to\psi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\sigma}|\eta(z)|e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\quad\hbox{in}\,\ L^{p}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2},(1+|z|^{2})dz\big),\ \ p\geq 1,

(2.32)

where

\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}=1-\frac{\pi}{|Q|}\to 0\,\ \hbox{as $|Q|\to\pi$},

(2.33)

and
|\eta(z)|
is periodic and vanishes at each point of the lattice
\ell
, see more details in
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
. We should remark that the main feature of the periodic lattice
\ell
is to
modify the decay of the Gaussian from
e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}}
to
e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}
, where
\sigma>0
depends on
the area of
Q
through the relation (
2.33
).
Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi_{R}(z)|^{4}dz&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}\sigma^{4}}|\eta(z)|^{4}e^{-\frac{2|z|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}dz\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}}|\eta(\sigma z)|^{4}e^{-2|z|^{2}}dz\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-2|z|^{2}}dz\leq\frac{C}{\sigma^{2}}\to 0\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ \sigma\to\infty,\end{split}

(2.34)

and thus

\displaystyle e(2,a)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\sigma\to\infty,\,R\to\infty}F_{2,a}(\psi_{R})

\displaystyle=2+\lim_{\sigma\to\infty,\,R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi_{R}|^{4}dz\leq 2,\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ a\in(0,\infty),

i.e., the estimate (
2.28
) holds true.

We now assume that
e(2,a)
admits a minimizer
u_{a}
for any
a\in(0,\infty)
. We then obtain from (
2.6
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|(\nabla-ix^{\perp})u_{a}\big|^{2}dx\geq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{a}|^{2}dx=2,

which and (
2.5

e(2,a)>2,\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ a\in(0,\infty),

a contradiction to (
2.28
). We therefore conclude that there is no minimizer for
e(2,a)
in the case
a\in(0,\infty)
. This therefore completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
∎

Proof of Theorem

.
Without loss of generality, as before we may assume

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

We first consider the case

3

Towards the above purpose, we denote

a complex-valued minimizer of

, where

as

1.4
, recall from Theorem B that

(3.3)

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, let

be a complex-valued minimizer of

, where

as

1.

The energy

satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}e(\Omega_{n},a)=e(a),

(3.4)

where the energy
e(a)
is defined in (

).

2.

There exists
\theta_{n}\in[0,2\pi)
such that
u_{n}

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}=u_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},\mathbb{C}),

where
u_{0}>0
is the unique positive minimizer of

, and the Lagrange multiplier

where

3.

u_{n}

(3.7)

where the constant

depends only on

.

Proof.
1. Taking
u_{0}
as a test function, we obtain the following upper bound estimate:

\lim_{n\to\infty}e(\Omega_{n},a)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\Omega_{n},a}(u_{0})=e(a).

(3.8)

On the other hand, we claim that there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
n
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2}\big)dx\leq C\,\ \hbox{uniformly in}\,\ n.

(3.9)

Indeed, following (
3.8
), we obtain from (
2.1
) and (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle 1+e(a)

\displaystyle\geq e(\Omega_{n},a)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}-\Omega_{n}x^{\perp}\cdot(iu_{n},\nabla u_{n})+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\big|\nabla|u_{n}|\big|^{2}+\big[V(x)-\frac{\Omega_{n}^{2}}{4}|x|^{2}\big]|u_{n}|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\big|\nabla|u_{n}|\big|^{2}+\frac{a}{2}|u_{n}|^{4}\Big)dx-C

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{a^{*}+a}{2}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{n}|^{4}dx-C\quad\hbox{uniformly in $n$.}

Since

is fixed, we deduce from above that

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}-\Omega_{n}x^{\perp}\cdot(iu,\nabla u)+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\big(V(x)-C\big)|u_{n}|^{2}\Big]dx\quad\hbox{uniformly in $n$.}

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{n}^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}dx

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}e(\Omega_{n},a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\Omega_{n},a}(u_{n})\\

(3.11)

(2). If

Lemma 3.3

Under the assumptions of Theorem

and

(b)

Case 1:

\psi_{m+1}\equiv\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{n}u_{0}dx\Big)u_{0},\ \ \hbox{and}\ \ T_{m+1}\equiv w_{n}^{\perp}=w_{n}-\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{n}u_{0}dx\Big)u_{0}.

Note from (

, and

(4.13)

Therefore, we deduce from (

), (

By Lemma

|w_{n}^{\perp}(x)|\leq CA^{m}\varepsilon_{n}^{m+1}e^{-\frac{1}{16}|x|},\,\ |\nabla w_{n}^{\perp}(x)|\leq CA^{m}\varepsilon_{n}^{m+1}e^{-\frac{1}{32}|x|}\ \ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},

which shows that the claim (

(4.15)

Similarly, we have

(4.16)

Since

, we get from (

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}w_{n}:&=(\mu_{n}-\mu_{0})(u_{0}+w_{n})-a(q_{n}+2u_{0})w_{n}^{2}-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+\Omega_{n}(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n})\\
&=\Omega_{n}\big(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n}\big)-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+B_{1,n}(x)+B_{2,n}(x)\quad\hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\end{split}

where the radially symmetric function

satisfies

\begin{split}B_{1,n}(x)&=(\mu_{n}-\mu_{0})(u_{0}+\psi_{m,n})-a\big(\psi_{m,n}^{3}+3u_{0}\psi_{m,n}^{2}\big),\end{split}

(4.18)

and the non-radially symmetric function

\begin{split}B_{2,n}(x)=&(\mu_{n}-\mu_{0})T_{m,n}-a\big(3\psi_{m,n}^{2}+3\psi_{m,n}T_{m,n}\\
&+T^{2}_{m,n}+6u_{0}\psi_{m,n}+3u_{0}T_{m,n}\big)T_{m,n}.\end{split}

(4.19)

Following Lemma
3.2
(3) and (
4.18
), there exists a unique function
\psi_{m+1,n}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}\psi_{m+1,n}=B_{1,n}(x)\quad\hbox{in\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$,}\end{split}

(4.20)

and moreover,

We now define

(4.21)

where
\psi_{m+1,n}(x)
is given by (
4.20

T_{m+1,n}(x)
satisfies

\mathcal{N}T_{m+1,n}=\Omega_{n}\big(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n}\big)-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+B_{2,n}(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(4.22)

due to (
4.17
).
Following (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.15
), we deduce that there exists a constant

such that

|\Omega_{n}(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n})-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}|\leq CA^{m}\varepsilon_{n}^{m+1}e^{-\frac{1}{16}|x|}\ \ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(4.23)

and

|B_{2,n}(x)|\leq CA^{m}\varepsilon_{n}^{m+1}e^{-|x|},\,\ \ |\nabla B_{2,n}(x)|\leq CA^{m}\varepsilon_{n}^{m+1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x|}\ \ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

(4.24)

In view of (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), we conclude from Lemma

(2
a
) that the claim (
4.11
) also holds true for the case

.

Step 2.
Under the decompositions (
4.12
) or (
4.21
), we shall prove that (
4.9
) holds for
m+1
, which then completes the proof of the lemma. Towards this aim, actually it suffices to show that

decays faster than (
4.11
) as

.

Recall from (

), (
4.13
), (
4.21
) and (

) that

satisfies the following system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{N}T_{m+1,n}&=\Omega_{n}\big(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n}\big)-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+\hat{B}_{2,n}(x)\ \ &\mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\mathcal{L}_{n}r_{n}&=-\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla T_{m+1,n})\ \ &\mbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.25)

where
\hat{B}_{2,n}(x)=(\mu_{n}-\mu_{0})q_{n}
if
a=0
, and
\hat{B}_{2,n}(x)={B}_{2,n}(x)
if

.
Multiplying the first equation of (
4.25
) by
T_{m+1,n}
and the second one of (
4.25
) by
r_{n}
, respectively, we then have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+|\nabla T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\Big[V(x)-\mu_{0}+3au_{0}^{2}\Big]|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}\\
&\quad\quad=\Big[\Omega_{n}\big(x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla r_{n}\big)-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+\hat{B}_{2,n}\Big]T_{m+1,n}\quad\mbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$},\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta r_{n}^{2}+|\nabla r_{n}|^{2}+\Big[V(x)-\mu_{n}+a|u_{n}|^{2}\Big]r_{n}^{2}=-\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla T_{m+1,n})r_{n}\quad\mbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

By the diamagnetic inequality, we have

|\nabla T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\frac{\Omega^{2}_{n}|x|^{2}}{4}|r_{n}|^{2}+\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla T_{m+1,n})r_{n}\geq 0\quad\mbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$},

(4.26)

and

|\nabla r_{n}|^{2}+\frac{\Omega^{2}_{n}|x|^{2}}{4}|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}-\Omega_{n}(x^{\bot}\cdot\nabla r_{n})T_{m+1,n}\geq 0\quad\mbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}$}.

By the exponential decay (
3.7
) of
|u_{n}|
, we also get from (
4.10
), (
4.14
) and (
4.24
) that as

\displaystyle\big(-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+\hat{B}_{2,n}\big)T_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(-ar_{n}^{2}q_{n}+\hat{B}_{2,n}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+C\varepsilon_{n}^{2(m+1)}A^{2m}e^{-2|x|}\quad\mbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}/B_{R}$},

is sufficiently large.

\frac{1}{2}V(x)-\mu_{0}+3a|u_{0}|^{2}+a|u_{n}|^{2}\geq 3\quad\hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2}/B_{R},

\begin{split}\quad&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\big(|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+r_{n}^{2}\big)+\frac{5}{2}\big(|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+r_{n}^{2}\big)\\

(4.28)

We next address the desired exponential decay of

Note from (

\begin{split}&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\Big[V(x)-\mu_{0}+3au_{0}^{2}\Big]|\nabla T_{m+1,n}|^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}|\nabla\partial_{i}T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[\partial_{i}V(x)+6au_{0}\partial_{i}u_{0}\Big]T_{m+1,n}\partial_{i}T_{m+1,n}\\

By the exponential decay of

and

, we calculate from (

) and (
4.28
) that as

,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[\partial_{i}V(x)+6au_{0}\partial_{i}u_{0}\Big]T_{m+1,n}\partial_{i}T_{m+1,n}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[|\partial_{i}V(x)|^{2}+C\Big]|T_{m+1,n}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{4}|\partial_{i}T_{m+1,n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CA^{2m}\varepsilon_{n}^{2(m+1)}e^{-|x|}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla T_{m+1,n}|^{2}\quad\hbox{in\,\ ${\mathbb{R}}^{2}/B_{R}$},

where and below

(V)

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Set
T_{1,n}(x)=w^{\perp}_{n}(x)=w_{n}-\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{n}u_{0}dx\big)u_{0}
and
\psi_{1,n}=\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{n}u_{0}dx\big)u_{0}

,

and

if

. We then obtain from Corollary
4.1
(2) that

|T_{1,n}(x)|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}e^{-|x|},\ \ |\nabla T_{1,n}(x)|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x|}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \,{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(4.35)

where
C_{1}>0
is independent of
n>0
.
Set

if

if

, where

\psi_{2,n}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is the unique solution of

\mathcal{N}\psi_{2,n}=(\mu_{n}-\mu_{0})u_{0}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \,{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

It then follows from Lemma
3.2
(3) that
\psi_{2,n}(|x|)
is radially symmetric. Moreover, based on (
4.35
), the same argument of proving Lemma
4.2
gives that there exists a constant

, independent of

|T_{2,n}(x)|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}e^{-|x|},\ \ |\nabla T_{2,n}(x)|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x|}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \,{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\quad\mbox{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

, from which we have

(4.36)

4.2
, we deduce from (

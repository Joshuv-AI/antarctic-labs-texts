A two variable Rankin–Selberg integral for GU ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ) {\mathrm{GU}}(2,2) and the degree 5 L L -function of GSp 4 {\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}
Antonio Cauchi, Armando Gutierrez Terradillos

Abstract
. We give a two-variable Rankin–Selberg integral for generic cusp forms on PGL 4 \mathrm{PGL}_{4} and PGU 2 , 2 \mathrm{PGU}_{2,2} which represents a product of exterior square L L -functions. As a residue of our integral, we obtain an integral representation on PGU 2 , 2 \mathrm{PGU}_{2,2} of the degree 5 L L -function of GSp 4 {\mathrm{GSp}}_{4} twisted by the quadratic character of E / F E/F of cuspidal automorphic representations which contribute to the theta correspondence for the pair ( PGSp 4 , PGU 2 , 2 ) (\mathrm{P}{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4},\mathrm{P}{\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2}) .

1.
Introduction

Automorphic
L
-functions are one of the cornerstones of the Langlands program. They provide a bridge between number theory and representation theory, dispensing a profound link between the arithmetic of algebraic objects such as elliptic curves and harmonic analysis on reductive groups. Automorphic
L
-functions are defined by the data of a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
on a reductive group
G
and of a finite dimensional representation
r
of the Langlands group
{}^{L}G
. Their zeros or poles are often linked to the non-vanishing of certain periods of
\pi
and to the participation of
\pi
as a Langlands functorial transfer or theta lift of an automorphic representation of another reductive group.
One of the main approaches to study the analytic properties of automorphic
L
-functions are via Rankin–Selberg integrals. Among those, the exotic family of Rankin–Selberg integrals in more than one complex variable represent products of
L
-functions and have applications to the study of their simultaneous poles. For instance, in
[
3
]
Bump, Friedberg, and Ginzburg gave integrals in two complex variables on
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}
,
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{6}
, and
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{8}
, each representing the product of the spin and the standard
L
-functions. Similarly, in
[
18
]
Pollack and Shah gave a two-variable integral on
{\mathrm{GU}}(2,2)
, which represents the product of the standard and the exterior square
L
-functions. In this article, we consider a new two-variable Rankin–Selberg integral, which for a globally generic cusp form on
{\mathrm{GU}}(2,2)
or
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
, represents a product of exterior square
L
-functions. As a residue of this integral, we obtain an integral representation of a twist of the degree
5

L
-function of
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}
, which gives a new instance of the curious phenomenon where a Rankin–Selberg integral of a cusp form on one group is used to represent an
L
-function of a different cusp form on another group.

We now describe our main results in more detail. Let
F
be a number field and denote by
{\bf A}
its adeles; we also let
E/F
be a quadratic field extension of
F
which defines the unitary group
{\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2/F}
. We let
E^{*}_{B}(g,s,z)
be the normalized two variable Borel Eisenstein series for
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4/F}
. If
\Pi
is a generic cuspidal automorphic representation of either
{\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2/F}
or
\mathrm{GL}_{4/F}
with trivial central character, and
\varphi
is a cusp form in the space of
\Pi
, we define

I^{*}(\varphi,s,z):=\int_{{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}(F)Z_{{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}}({\bf A})\setminus{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}({\bf A})}\!\!\!\!\!E^{*}_{B}(g,s,z)\varphi(g)dg.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places of
F
containing the ramified places for
\Pi
, for
E/F
(when working with
{\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2}
), and the archimedean places of
F
. In Theorem
3.4
and (
8
), we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

I^{*}(\varphi,s,z)=L^{\Sigma}(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2})L^{\Sigma}(z,\Pi,\wedge^{2})I_{\Sigma}(\varphi,s,z),

where
I_{\Sigma}(\varphi,s,z)
, which is the product of the local integrals at places in
\Sigma
, can be made non-zero at
(s,z)
for a certain choice of
\varphi
.

Let now
\Pi
be a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{PGU}_{2,2}({\bf A})
and let
E^{*}_{P}(g,s)
be the normalized degenerate Siegel Eisenstein series for
{\rm GSp}_{4}
. As
E^{*}_{P}(g,s)
can be obtained as the residue at
z=1
of
E^{*}_{B}(g,s,z)
, the residue at
z=1
of
I^{*}(\varphi,s,z)
is the integral

J^{*}(\varphi,s)=\int_{{\rm GSp}_{4}(F)Z_{{\rm GSp}_{4}}({\bf A})\backslash{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf A})}E^{*}_{P}(g,s)\varphi(g)dg.

We note that
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
is the quasi-split counterpart of the integral for the group
{\rm SO}_{3,3}
(hence
\mathrm{PGL}_{4}
) studied in
[
2
]
and can be regarded as the degenerate companion of the integral for
{\rm SO}_{4,2}
studied in
[
19
]
and
[
17
]
.

J^{*}(\varphi,s)
unfolds to the Shalika period of
\varphi
, which is non-zero if and only if the partial twisted exterior square
L
-function
L(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2})
has a simple pole at
s=1
(
cf
.
[
7
]
). When any of these hold,
\Pi
comes from a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation
\sigma
of
{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf A})
with trivial central character, by means of a theta correspondence for
({\rm PGSp}_{4},{\rm PGU}_{2,2})
(
cf
.
[
16
, Theorem B]
), and one has that

L^{\Sigma}(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2})=L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/F})\zeta_{F}^{\Sigma}(s),

where
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/F})
denotes the partial degree 5
L
-function for
{\rm GSp}_{4}
twisted by the quadratic Hecke character associated to the quadratic extension
E/F
which defines
{\rm GU}_{2,2}
. Theorem
1.1
and an analysis of the integral
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
at the points where
E^{*}_{P}(g,s)
might obtain a pole let us show the following (
cf
. Theorems
4.10
and
4.15
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\Pi
appears in the theta lift of
\sigma
in the sense of
[
16
, Theorem B]
, where
\sigma
is a globally generic irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. For a given cusp form
\varphi
in
\Pi
, there exist a cusp form
\varphi^{\prime}
in
\Pi
and section
f
defining the Borel Eisenstein series such that

J^{*}(\varphi,s)=L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/F})\cdot\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\infty}}I(\varphi_{v}^{\prime},f_{v},s,1).

where
\Sigma
is a finite set of places containing the set
\Sigma_{\infty}
of archimedean places and the ramified places for
\Pi
,
\sigma
, and
E/F
. Furthermore, the integral
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
and the partial
L
-function
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/F})
extend to holomorphic functions for all
s
.

One novelty of our integral representation
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
of the degree 5
L
-function of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
is that it might be used to address motivic questions. Indeed, since
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2,2}
have Shimura varieties, our integral seems to have applications to the study of the conjectural “standard” degree 5 motive associated to cohomological cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, such as the construction of an Euler system for the corresponding Galois representation, similar in spirit to the one given in work in progress by Skinner and Sangiovanni Vincentelli for the symmetric square of a modular form.

2.1.
Groups

2.1.1.
Definitions

Denote by

and define

\mathbf{G}(R):={\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2}(R)=\{(g,m_{g})\in\mathrm{GL}_{4,F}(R\otimes_{F}E)\times\mathrm{GL}_{1,F}(R):\bar{g}^{t}Jg=m_{g}J\},

\bar{\bullet}

\mathbf{H}(R):={\mathrm{GSp}}_{4,F}(R)=\{(g,m_{g})\in\mathrm{GL}_{4,F}(R)\times\mathrm{GL}_{1,F}(R):g^{t}Jg=m_{g}J\},

Let

2.2.1.
Dual groups

Recall that the dual group of
\mathbf{G}
is

{}^{L}\mathbf{G}=(\mathrm{GL}_{4}({\bf C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\bf C}))\rtimes{\rm Gal}(E/F),

with the action of the non-trivial element
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
given by (cf.
[
1

)

(g,\lambda)\mapsto(\Phi_{4}{}^{t}g^{-1}\Phi_{4},\lambda{\rm det}(g)),\text{ where }\Phi_{4}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&&1\\
&&-1&\\
&1&&\\
-1&&&\end{smallmatrix}\right).

The dual group of
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}
is the derived subgroup of
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
, namely

{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}={\rm SL}_{4}({\bf C})\rtimes{\rm Gal}(E/F).

Recall also that
{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}={\rm Spin}_{5}({\bf C})\simeq{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf C})
. Under this exceptional isomorphism, the standard representation
W_{4}
of
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf C})
coincides with the spin representation of
{\rm Spin}_{5}({\bf C})
.

2.2.2.
The twisted exterior square representation

The standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}({\bf C})
on
{\bf C}^{4}
, with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, induces the representation
\wedge^{2}
on
\wedge^{2}{\bf C}^{4}\simeq{\bf C}^{6}
by the rule
(\wedge^{2}g)\cdot(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=g\cdot e_{i}\wedge g\cdot e_{j}
. Consider the six dimensional representation

\mathrm{GL}_{4}({\bf C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}({\bf C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{6}({\bf C})

given by
(g,\lambda)\mapsto(\wedge^{2}g)\lambda
. By
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
,
\wedge^{2}
extends to a representation of
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
. Indeed, notice that both representations
\wedge^{2}
and
\wedge^{2}\circ\tau
, with
1\neq\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, have same highest weight. Hence they are isomorphic, i.e. there exists
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{6}({\bf C})
such that
(\wedge^{2}\circ\tau)(g,\lambda)=A^{-1}(\wedge^{2}(g,\lambda))A
. Choose
A
with
{\rm Tr}(A)>0
and extend
\wedge^{2}
to
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
by sending

(g,\lambda,1)\mapsto(\wedge^{2}g)\lambda,\,(1,1,\tau)\mapsto A.

The resulting representation, which we still denote by
\wedge^{2}:{}^{L}\mathbf{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{6}({\bf C})
, is called the (twisted) exterior square representation of
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
. We still denote by
\wedge^{2}:{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{6}({\bf C})
the composition of
{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}\hookrightarrow{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
with
\wedge^{2}
.

If we restrict the exterior square representation along the embedding
{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}\hookrightarrow{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G},\,g\mapsto(g,1)
, we have

(1)

\wedge^{2}_{|{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf C})}={\rm std}\oplus\mathbf{1},

where
{\rm std}:{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{5}({\bf C})
is the irreducible representation of highest weight
(1,1)
and gives the composition of the projection
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf C})\simeq{\rm Spin}_{5}({\bf C})\to{\rm SO}_{5}({\bf C})
with the standard representation of
{\rm SO}_{5}({\bf C})
.

3.
The two variable Rankin–Selberg integral

3.1.
Global integral and unfolding

Let
s,z
be complex variables. We denote by
I_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(s,z)
the induced representation of
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
consisting of smooth functions
f(\cdot,s,z):\mathbf{H}({\bf A})\to{\bf C}
such that,
\forall n\in U_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}),\forall t\in T_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})
, we have

(5)

f(ntg,s,z)=\chi_{s,z}(t)f(g,s,z),

for
\chi_{s,z}(t(a,b;\mu)):=|a|^{s+z+1}|b|^{s-z+1}|\mu|^{-s-1}
.
This representation has trivial central character. Moreover, the restriction to
P_{\mathbf{H}}
of
\chi_{s,0}
is
\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{\tfrac{1}{3}(s+1)}
.

We let
f\in I_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(s,z)
be a standard section, which factorizes as
\otimes_{v}f_{v}
, and define the two variable Borel Eisenstein series

(6)

\displaystyle{\rm Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,z):={\sum_{\gamma\in{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)\backslash\mathbf{H}(F)}}f(\gamma g,s,z).

The sum converges absolutely for
{\rm Re}(s)
and
{\rm Re}(z)
big enough and it extends to a meromorphic function on
{\bf C}^{2}
. Let
(\Pi,V_{\Pi})
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{PG}({\bf A})
. Given a cusp form
\varphi\in V_{\Pi}
, we define

I(\varphi,f,s,z):=\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\!\!\!\!\!{\rm Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,z)\varphi(g)dg.

Let
\psi
be a non-trivial additive character of
{\bf A}/F
. We define a principal character
\chi:U_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf A})/U_{\mathbf{G}}(F)\to{\bf C}^{\times}
as follows:

\chi\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x&y&a\\
&1&b&\bar{y}\\
&&1&-\bar{x}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=\psi(\mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(\alpha x)-b),

where
\alpha\in E
is such that
\overline{\alpha}=-\alpha\neq 0
. We consider the Whittaker model of a cusp form
\varphi
of
\mathbf{G}
with respect to
\chi
, i.e.

W_{\varphi}(g):=\int_{[U_{\mathbf{G}}]}\varphi(ng)\chi(n)dn.

Proposition 3.1
.

The zeta integral unfolds to

I(\varphi,f,s,z)=\int_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\!\!\!\!\!\!f(wg,s,z)W_{\varphi}(g)dg,

where

w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Proof.

We start by unfolding the Eisenstein series along the Klingen parabolic
Q_{\mathbf{H}}
to get

\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\!\!\!\!\!\!\varphi_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}}(g)\cdot\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\gamma\in{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)\backslash Q_{\mathbf{H}}(F)}f(\gamma g,s,z)dg,

where
\varphi_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}}(g)
denotes the constant term of
\varphi
along
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
. We now Fourier expand
\varphi_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}}
along
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}\setminus N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}
to get

\varphi_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}}(g)=\sum_{\chi:[N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}\setminus N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}}\varphi_{\chi}(g),

with
\varphi_{\chi}(g)=\int_{[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}]}\varphi(n^{\prime}g)\chi^{-1}(n^{\prime})dn^{\prime}
. Any character
\chi:[N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}\setminus N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
can be described as follows. Recall that the characters
\chi:[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}/[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}},N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}]]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
are of the form
n^{\prime}\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}_{E/F}(\alpha x+\beta y)),
where
\alpha,\beta\in E
,
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character on
F\backslash{\bf A}
, and where we have written

Lemma 3.5

For any

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\left(V_{(0,1,0)}\right)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}V_{(0,k-2i,0)}.

with kernel

(

∎

Lemma 3.6

For any

, we have

V_{(0,n,0)}\otimes V_{(0,m,0)}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 0\\
\sum m_{i}=m\\
m_{2}+m_{3}\leq n\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!V_{(m_{2},n+m_{1}-m_{2}-m_{3},m_{2})}.

Proof.

Following the discussion in
[
6
, §25.3]

[
6
, (A.8)]
(
cf

[
15
, I.9]
for a proof of the formula). If we let

denote the Young diagram with rows of size

V_{(0,n,0)}\otimes V_{(0,m,0)}=\oplus_{\lambda}N_{n,m,\lambda}V_{\lambda},

where

is the number of strict expansions of
D_{n}
by
D_{m}
of shape the Young diagram of

. In our case

is either zero or one; hence we are left to determine the set of strict expansions of
D_{n}
by
D_{m}
. Recall that an expansion of
D_{n}
by
D_{m}
is a Young diagram obtained by first adding
m

in each of these so that no two 1’s are in the same column and then adding other
m

the number of 1’s up to position

is greater or equal to the number of 2’s up to position
t

m

(n+\ell_{1},n,\ell_{2})
such that
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=m
and
\ell_{2}\leq n
. If we let
(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
be any partition of
m
, the

can be added by appending
m_{1}
boxes to the second row,
m_{2}
to the third, and
m_{3}
to the fourth so that
\ell_{1}\geq m_{1}+m_{2}
,
\ell_{2}\geq m_{3}
,
\ell_{2}+m_{2}\leq n
. This implies that
\ell_{1}=m_{1}+m_{2}
and
\ell_{2}=m_{3}
, hence any strict expansion of
D_{n}
by

E_{P_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f)
,
E_{Q_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f^{\prime})
for
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
associated respectively to the parabolics
P_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}=P_{\mathbf{H}}\cap{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
and
Q_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}=Q_{\mathbf{H}}\cap{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
, and to the elements
r(g_{f})f\in I_{P({\bf A})}^{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf A})}\nu_{P}^{s+1}
,
r(g_{f})f^{\prime}\in I_{Q({\bf A})}^{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}({\bf A})}\nu_{Q}^{2s+1}
, where the inductions are not normalized. Here we denoted

\displaystyle\nu_{P}:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\star\\
&J_{2}{}^{t}g^{-1}J_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|{\rm det}(g)\right|,

\displaystyle\nu_{Q}:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&\\
&g&\\
&&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|a\right|.

With respect to
[
12
]
, the Eisenstein series
E_{P_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f)
, resp.
E_{Q_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f^{\prime})
have complex variable

shifted by
-1/2
. We now apply
[
12
, Proposition 1.10]
, which says that, given
\omega
the longest element of the quotient of Weyl groups
W_{M_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}}\backslash W_{\mathbf{H}}
and

the corresponding intertwining operator, then

{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f)=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{Q_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},1/2,{\rm Res}_{s=1}M_{\omega}(r(g_{f})f)).

As

is an intertwining map, the action of

commutes with it, hence we have

{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f)=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{Q_{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}}(g_{1},1/2,{\rm Res}_{s=1}r(g_{f})M_{\omega}(f)).

This implies that
{\rm Res}_{s=1}{\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f)
equals to

, i.e.

can be taken to be
{\rm Res}_{s=1}M_{\omega}(f)
. After normalizing

(see also
[
12
, Proposition 1.8]
).

4.1.3.
The constant term of the Borel Eisenstein series

The following informal discussion will not be used in the article, but we add it as a motivation for the normalization of the Borel Eisenstein series and the existence of its poles along the line
z=1
.

Let
W_{\mathbf{H}}
denote the Weyl group of
\mathbf{H}
; also let
\Lambda_{s,z}:=(2z-1)\beta_{1}+(s-z)\beta_{2}\in X^{*}(T_{\mathbf{H}})
, where
\beta_{1},\;\beta_{2}
denote the fundamental weights of
\mathbf{H}
. Let
S
be a finite set of places such that the defining datum of the Eisenstein series
E_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,z)
is unramified outside
S
. The analytic properties of
E_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,z)
are reflected in its constant term, that is equal to

\sum_{\omega\in W_{\mathbf{H}}}M(\omega,\Lambda_{s,z})f(g,s,z),

where
M(\omega,\Lambda_{s,z})f(g,s,z)=\int_{(U_{\mathbf{H}}\cap\omega\overline{U}_{\mathbf{H}}\omega^{-1})({\bf A})}f(\omega^{-1}ug,s,z)du
, with
\overline{U}_{\mathbf{H}}
the unipotent radical of the lower-triangular Borel, is the well known intertwining map. The Gindikin-Karpelevic formula shows that the poles of
M(\omega,\Lambda_{s,z})
are exactly the ones of the following functions:

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha>0\\
\omega\alpha<0\end{subarray}}\zeta^{S}_{F}(\left<\Lambda_{s,z},\alpha^{\vee}\right>),

where
\alpha
ranges through the positive roots for
\mathbf{H}
such that
\omega\alpha
is negative.
Therefore, the only possible poles are located along the lines determined by
\left<\Lambda_{s,z},\alpha^{\vee}\right>=1
. Concretely, these are
\{z=1\}
,
\{s=1\}
,
\{s-z=1\}
, and
\{z+s=2\}
. In fact, the constant term is a combination of the following zeta functions:

\zeta^{S}_{F}(2z-1),\;\;\zeta^{S}_{F}(s-z),\;\;\zeta^{S}_{F}(s+z-1),\;\;\zeta^{S}_{F}(2s-1).

4.1.4.
Residues of the Borel Eisenstein series

In the following, we relate the residue of the Borel Eisenstein series along the line
z=1
to the Siegel Eisenstein series. Let
f\in I_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(s,z)
be the section defining
\mathrm{Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,z,s)
, then

\mathrm{Res}_{z=1}\mathrm{Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,z,s)=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in P_{\mathbf{H}}(F)\setminus\mathbf{H}(F)}r(f)(\gamma^{\prime}g),

where
r(f)(g)=\mathrm{Res}_{z=1}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in B_{\mathbf{H}}(F)\setminus P_{\mathbf{H}}(F)}f(\gamma g,s,z)\right)
. Since
B_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash P_{\mathbf{H}}\simeq B_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, we have

f\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})}^{\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\left(\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{\frac{1}{3}(s+1)}I_{B_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}({\bf A})}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\bf A})}\delta_{B_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}}^{z}\right).

As the residue of degenerate Eisenstein series on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
is constant, we get that
r(f)(g)\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
. Summing up, we have obtained the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

For
f
and
r(f)
as above we have that
\mathrm{Res}_{z=1}\mathrm{Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,z,s)={\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,r(f))
.

Similarly, we have a formula for the residue along
s-z=1
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{s-z=1}\mathrm{Eis}_{B_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,z,s)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,2z+1/2,r^{\prime}(f)),

where
r^{\prime}(f)(g)=\mathrm{Res}_{s-z=1}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in B_{\mathbf{H}}(F)\setminus Q_{\mathbf{H}}(F)}f(\gamma g,s,z)\right)
.

4.2.

The Shalika subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
is defined as follows. For any

is given by

S(R)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&&\\
c&d&&\\
&&a&-b\\
&&-c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\alpha&x\\
&1&y&\overline{\alpha}\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\;:\;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\

Note that the Shalika group

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}

&{\rm det}(h)J_{2}{}^{t}h^{-1}J_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

of

is trivial.

Recall that we have fixed a non-degenerate character
\psi:F\backslash{\bf A}\to{\bf C}^{\times}
. Given
\eta\in E^{\times}
such that
\bar{\eta}=-\eta
, we define

\chi_{\eta}:N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}(F)\backslash N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}({\bf A})\longrightarrow{\bf C}^{\times},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\alpha&x\\

&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\psi(\eta\alpha-\eta\overline{\alpha}).

By a slight abuse of notation, denote also by

the character on

. Let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})

Definition 4.3
.

For a cusp form
\varphi
in the space of

\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(g):=\int_{{\bf A}^{\times}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\bf A})}\int_{N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}(F)\backslash N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}({\bf A})}\varphi\left(nh^{*}g\right)\chi_{\eta}^{-1}(n)dndg.

\Pi

in
\Pi

In

(
[

)

Let

does not vanish on the space of
\Pi
.

(2)

4.3.

L
-function of
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}

Let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places containing the archimedean places and all the ramified places for
\Pi

Proposition 4.6
.

The integral

unfolds to

\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\bf A})N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f(h,s)\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(h)dh.

Proof.

We start by unfolding the Eisenstein series; for
{\rm Re}(s)
big enough, we get

\int_{P_{\mathbf{H}}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f(h,s)\varphi(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over the unipotent radical

of

\displaystyle\int_{M_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\int_{N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)\backslash N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})}f(nh,s)\varphi(nh)dndh=\int_{\cdots}f(h,s)\int_{N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)\backslash N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})}\varphi(nh)dndh,

.
Now Fourier expanding over
[N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}]

the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic of

where the sum runs over the characters
\chi:[N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
and where we have denoted

\varphi_{\chi}(h)=\int_{N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}(F)\backslash N_{P_{\mathbf{G}}}({\bf A})}\varphi(n^{\prime}h)\chi^{-1}(n^{\prime})dn^{\prime}.

Any character

y&\bar{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right))),
where

,

&1&y&\bar{\alpha}\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;\text{ with }x,y\in{\bf A},\alpha\in{\bf A}_{E}.

If

\chi

&-\eta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&x\\

\displaystyle J(\varphi,f,s)

\varphi
is a cusp form in
\Pi
.

Proposition 4.11
.

The integral
J^{\prime}(\varphi,f^{\prime},s)
is identically zero.

Remark 4.12
.

Notice that if the induced datum of the Klingen Eisenstein series is not degenerate, the corresponding integral calculates the
\wedge^{2}\times{\rm Std}

L
-function on
{\mathrm{GU}}_{2,2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, as we show in forthcoming work.

Proof of Proposition
4.11
.

We start by unfolding the Eisenstein series. For big enough
{\rm Re}(s)
, we get

\int_{{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f^{\prime}(h,s))\varphi(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over the unipotent radical
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
of
{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f^{\prime}(h,s)\int_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)\backslash N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})}\varphi(nh)dndh.

We now Fourier expand over
[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]
. The integral equals

\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f^{\prime}(h,s)\sum_{\chi}\varphi_{\chi}(h)dh,

where the sum runs over the characters
\chi:[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
. As shown in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, any character
\chi:[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
is of the form
\chi_{\alpha,\beta}:n\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}_{E/F}(\alpha x+\beta y))
, with
\alpha,\beta\in E
such that
\bar{\alpha}=-\alpha
and
\bar{\beta}=-\beta
. Again from the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we know that
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)
acts on the space of characters of
[N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]
with two orbits, the trivial one and an open one, with a representative given by the character
\chi_{\alpha,0}
with
\bar{\alpha}=-\alpha\neq 0
. By cuspidality of
\varphi
along
N_{Q_{\mathbf{G}}}
, we have

(21)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(\varphi,f^{\prime},s)=\int_{M_{\alpha}(F)N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f^{\prime}(h,s)\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(h)dh,

where
M_{\alpha}(F)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&&\\
&a&b&\\
&&d&\\
&&&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\;\right\}=L_{\alpha}(F)N_{\alpha}(F)
denotes the stabiliser in
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(F)
of
\chi_{\alpha,0}
, with Levi part (resp. unipotent part)
L_{\alpha}
(resp.
N_{\alpha}
). Collapse the sum over
[N_{\alpha}]
to get

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(\varphi,f^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{\alpha}(F)N_{\alpha}({\bf A})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f^{\prime}(h,s)\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dndh.

Notice that the inner integral
\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dn
equals the Fourier coefficient of
\varphi
with respect to the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and the choice of a character on it which is degenerate (as it’s only supported on the entry (1,2)). As a consequence,
\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dn
contains, as an inner integral, the period of
\varphi
over the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic of
\mathbf{G}
, which vanishes by cuspidality of
\varphi
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.4.2.
Periods over
{\mathrm{GSp}}_{4}

For any cusp form
\varphi
on
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
, we consider

\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(g):=\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\varphi(hg)dh.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\Pi
be a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
. For any cusp form
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
, the period
\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(g)=0
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathbf{H}
, the result is implied by showing that, for any
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
,

\varphi^{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}(g):=\int_{[{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}]}\varphi(hg)dh=0.

We show the latter by contradiction. Suppose that there exists
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
for which
\varphi^{{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}}(g)\neq 0
. This defines a non-trivial
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}
-functional on
\Pi
. Let
v
be an unramified place for
\Pi
which splits in
E
. We can further assume that
g_{v}=1
. Since
v
splits, we can identify
\Pi_{v}
with an unramified generic representation
\pi_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(F_{v})
with trivial central character. As the linear span
R_{v}
of the
\textbf{P}\mathbf{G}(F_{v})
-right translates of
\varphi
is isomorphic to
\pi_{v}
, the map

R_{v}\ni\varphi^{\prime}\mapsto\left(g^{\prime}\mapsto\int_{[{\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}]}\varphi^{\prime}(hgg^{\prime})dh\right)

defines an injective intertwining map in
{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{GL}_{4}(F_{v})}(\pi_{v},{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{4}(F_{v})))
. This contradicts the genericity of
\pi_{v}
(
cf
.
[
11
, Theorem 3.2.2]
). The Lemma follows.
∎

4.4.3.
Location of poles

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\Pi
be a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
, with trivial central character and let
\varphi
be a cusp form in
\Pi
. The integral
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
is holomorphic for all
s
. In particular, if
\Pi
is the small theta lift of a
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})^{+}
-factor of a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation
\sigma
of
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
, then
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/F})
is holomorphic for all
s
, with
\Sigma
a finite set of places containing all the ramified places of
\Pi
,
\sigma
, and
E/F
.

Proof.

Recall that the normalized Eisenstein series
E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)
achieves poles at
s=1,2
of order at most one, thus
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
may have a pole at these two values.

At
s=1
, the residue of the integral equals to

\displaystyle{\rm Res}_{s=1}J^{*}(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{s=1}\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Res}_{s=1}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,1/2,f_{1/2}^{\prime})\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot J^{\prime}(\varphi,f_{1/2}^{\prime},1/2),

where the third equality follows from Proposition
4.1
and
C=\frac{{\rm Res}_{s=1}(\zeta^{\Sigma}_{F}(s))\zeta^{\Sigma}_{F}(3)}{2\zeta^{\Sigma}_{F}(2)}
. The latter integral vanishes by Proposition
4.11
, hence
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
is holomorphic at
s=1
.

At
s=2
there exists
f\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
such that
{\rm Res}_{s=2}E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)
is constant. Therefore

\displaystyle{\rm Res}_{s=2}J^{*}(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Res}_{s=2}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot\int_{\mathbf{H}(F)Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\varphi(h)dh.

Hence,
J^{*}(\varphi,s)
achieves a pole at
s=2
if
\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(1)\neq 0
. However, the latter is always zero because of Lemma
4.14
. This proves the first statement. The second one follows from this and from Theorem
4.10
.
∎

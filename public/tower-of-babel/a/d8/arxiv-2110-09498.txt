Depinning in integer-restricted Gaussian Fields and BKT phases of two-component spin models
Michael Aizenman, Matan Harel, Ron Peled, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
. For a family of integer-valued height functions defined over the faces of planar graphs, we establish a relation between the probability of connection by level sets and the spin-spin correlations of the dual O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) symmetric spin models formulated over the graphs’ vertices. The relation is used to show that in two dimensions the Villain spin model exhibits non-summable decay of correlations at any temperature at which the dual integer-restricted Gaussian field exhibits depinning. For the latter, we devise a new monotonicity argument through which the recent alternative proof by Lammers of the existence of a depinning transition in two-dimensional graphs of degree three, is extended to all doubly-periodic graphs, in particular to ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} . Essential use is made of the inequality of Regev and Stephens-Davidowitz, which allows also an alternative (to absolute-value FKG) proof of convergence of the height-function’s distribution in the infinite-volume limit. Similar results are established for the X ​ Y XY spin model and its dual Bessel random height function. Taken together these statements yield a new perspective on the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless phase transition in O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) spin models, and complete a new proof of depinning in two-dimensional integer-valued height functions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
An outline

The subject of this paper is a pair of phenomena, each of which is special to models formulated over
d=2
dimensional graphs, that are linked through a duality relation. One is the depinning transition in random height functions, such as the integer-restricted Gaussian field (
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
), and the other is the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) phase with slowly decaying correlations in
O(2)
-invariant two-component spin systems, such as the Villain and the XY spin models.
Each of these has been previously analyzed through its relation to a lattice system of integer charges with the two-dimensional Coulomb interaction (of logarithmic strength).
The results presented here employ probabilistic tools which yield another perspective on the existence of the two phenomena, and the link between the two.

To present the results in their simplest context first, we start by considering the dual pair of the Villain two-component spin model on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

and the integer-restricted Gaussian field on this graph’s dual, which is also
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
in this case.
However, we also explain how the analysis extends to other doubly-periodic graphs, and to other combinations of interest. Those include the
XY
spin model and its dual, which is an integer-valued field whose Gibbs factor involves a modified Bessel function.

Beyond the example of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, our discussion applies to connected graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
which are
planar
and
doubly-periodic
– in the sense that there exists an embedding of
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that the natural action of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by translation is a
{\mathcal{G}}
-automorphism.
Furthermore, it will be assumed that under the above embedding the graph’s vertex set has no accumulation point. We call graphs with the last property
tame
.

Among the graphs meeting these conditions, one finds the standard square, triangular, and hexagonal lattices, and also graphs which are only quasi-transitive (i.e. of a finite periodicity cell). One may observe that if
{\mathcal{G}}
is doubly-periodic and tame, then so is its dual graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
.

We denote by
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
the graph distance, by
\Lambda_{v,r}
the set of vertices
u
for which
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(v,u)\leq r
, and also write
\Lambda_{L}\equiv\Lambda_{v_{0},L}
with
v_{0}
a fixed vertex to which we refer as the origin.

Following are the two leading examples of models and questions we study, and the main results in that context.

1.2.
The
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
and its depinning transition

The integer-restricted Gaussian field (
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
) over a locally finite and connected graph
{\mathcal{G}}
of vertex set
\mathcal{V}
and edge set
\mathcal{E}

has, as its basic variables, the values of the random function
n:\mathcal{V}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
finite-volume partition function in
\mathcal{V}_{L}\equiv\mathcal{V}\cap[-L,L]^{2}
, which is taken here with the Dirichlet boundary conditions, is

(1.1)

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda,L}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}\equiv\sum_{n:\mathcal{V}_{L}\to{\mathbb{Z}}:\left.n\right|_{\partial\mathcal{V}_{L}}=0}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}}\left(n_{u}-n_{v}\right)^{2}\right).

At any given
\lambda
, the probability of
n
is given by the configuration’s normalized contribution to the above sum.

Quite generally,
Gaussian domination
holds in the sense that the fluctuations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
are upper bounded by those of the corresponding Gaussian free field (GFF)
[
26
]
(cf.
[
36
]
).

In two dimensions, such models exhibit a
localization–delocalization
, or
de-pinning
, phase transition, which can also be presented in terms of
symmetry breaking
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
is said to be
pinned
, or exhibit
localization
, at a specified
\lambda
iff for every
x\in\mathcal{V}
,

(1.2)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[n_{x}^{2}\right]<\infty\,.

The model is said to
depin
, or be
delocalized
, if in the limit
L\to\infty
the height variance diverges and, furthermore,
the surface fluctuates away, with

(1.3)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}(|n_{x}|\leq t)=0

for each
x\in\mathcal{V}
and
t<\infty
.

We postpone to Section 2 the more detailed presentation of the known results (and references) on the existence of the limit in (
1.2
), the complementarity of the above two conditions, and the limiting states’ basic properties. Here, let us just note that
in the pinned phase, the system admits an infinite collection of infinite-volume Gibbs equilibrium states which are limits of the finite states with boundary values set at a common value
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and hence differ by simple shifts. On doubly-periodic and tame graphs, which is the case of interest here, the limiting states will be ergodic.

The realizability of both phases at different values of
\lambda
is limited to two dimensions:
in one dimension, random height functions exhibit Brownian-bridge-type fluctuations, whereas in
d>2
dimensions, the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
is pinned at all temperatures.

For
d=2
, localization at large enough
\lambda
is not hard to prove using a Peierls-type argument. Delocalization has been viewed as the more difficult phase to establish.
The occurrence of delocalization for the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and other doubly-periodic graphs was first established in
[
24
]
using the dual Coulomb gas perspective. Recently, a new proof was presented by Lammers
[
38
]
for doubly-periodic planar graphs of maximum degree
3
.
The proof is based on an
influence-percolation
argument, for which the limitation on the graph’s degree plays an important role. The removal of this limitation is among the first results presented here.

The following summarizes the results presented here concerning the de-pinning phase transition per-se.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any locally finite and connected graph, there exists
\lambda_{c}({\mathcal{G}})\in[0,\infty]
such that
the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
is pinned for
\lambda>\lambda_{c}({\mathcal{G}})
and depinned for
\lambda<\lambda_{c}({\mathcal{G}})
. For planar, doubly-periodic graphs with those properties,

0<\lambda_{c}({\mathcal{G}})<\infty
. In the case of
the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
at
\lambda=\frac{2}{3}\ln 2
is delocalized (and by implication
\lambda_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\geq\frac{2}{3}\ln 2
).

In the proof, we extend Lammers’ result’s applicability using a series of surgeries, through which the model’s graph is changed into one of smaller degrees; this procedure is accompanied by controlled increases in the couplings. The transformations are rather natural, but the key point in the proof is that, in each step of the process, the height variance can only
decrease
.
This is done through a pair of relatively recent inequalities for Gaussian measures on lattices, due to Regev and Stephens-Davidowitz
[
47
]
. In our case, the lattice in question will be the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
configuration space.
These inequalities also imply that the height variance is monotone in the model’s coupling strength
\lambda
and thus, as expected, for each graph the depinning transition occurs at a single transitional value
\lambda_{c}
.
The stated bound on
\lambda_{c}
builds on Lammers’ proof of delocalization on cubic graphs at
\lambda\leq 2\ln 2
.

1.3.
From depinning in
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
to slow decay of correlations in spin models

The spin models discussed here are systems of two component spins associated with the vertices of a graph. Their configurations form a random
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued function
\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
with nearest-neighbor interaction and
O(2)
rotational symmetry. Their BKT phase transition is unique to two dimensions.

The example which we discuss first is the Villain model, with spins

\sigma_{x}=\mathrm{e}^{i\theta_{x}}
, whose partition function is

(1.4)

Z^{\mathrm{Vill},{\mathcal{G}}}_{\beta}=\int_{\theta\in[-\pi,\pi)^{\mathcal{V}}}\left\{\prod_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}}\left[\sum_{m_{uv}\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\theta_{u}-\theta_{v}+2\pi m_{uv}\right)^{2}}\right]\,\right\}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{d}{\theta_{x}}\,,

The corresponding Gibbs equilibrium state is given by the probability distribution
that is obtained by normalizing the measures integrated in
eq.

1.4
.

This model can be given a probabilistically appealing
interpretation, with the Gibbs measure presented
as the marginal distribution, restricted to the vertices, of a more extended random spin function defined over the graph’s edges. The distribution of the extended model is formed by linking Wiener processes, each describing Brownian motion on the circle, tied at the vertices through a continuity constraint.
This formulations is used below to derive a correlation inequality which is new for this model, and is of relevance for our main result (cf.
appendix

A
). It also allows to prove the convergence of
the model’s correlation function in the infinite-volume limit through the known
XY
Ginibre inequality. Hitherto the latter was not recognized to be applicable to this model.

The model has two phases which differ in the nature of their
spin-spin correlations. At high temperatures, as is typical in statistical mechanics, the correlations decay
exponentially fast. Their low-temperature
BKT phase
is characterized by the persistence of power-law
slow decay

(without long-range order which is ruled out for the two-dimensional system by the Mermin–Wagner theorem).

Our second result is the following link between the two phenomena described above.

Theorem 1.3
.

For the Villain model on a doubly-periodic and tame graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, at any
\beta

(1.5)

\langle\sigma_{u}\cdot\sigma_{v}\rangle^{Vill,{\mathcal{G}}}_{\beta}\geq\Pr{}^{{\mathcal{G}}^{*}}_{\lambda=1/\beta}\left(u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}v\right)

where on the right is the probability that the two sites at which the spins are evaluated lie on a common level line (a concept defined more carefully below) of the random integer-valued height function
n
of the model’s dual
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
.

This relation is then used to deduce our main result for the Villain model.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a planar, doubly-periodic, tame graph. Then for any
\beta
at which the dual
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
model delocalizes at
\lambda=1/\beta
, the correlation function does not decay faster than
\rm{Const.}/d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)
, satisfying

(1.6)

\forall L<\infty:\qquad\max_{x\in{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in{\mathcal{G}}\\
d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)=L\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle^{Vill,{\mathcal{G}}}_{\beta}\right)\geq\,1\,.

where
\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle
can also be replaced by the smaller correlation function computed for the system’s restriction to

\{u\in{\mathcal{G}}:d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)\leq L\}

Combined with the proven existence of delocalization (i.e.
\lambda_{c}({\mathcal{G}}^{*})>0
) this yields a new perspective on the BKT phase of the Villain spin model on planar graphs.

The proof of
1.4
is in two steps: i) using the relation (
1.5
), we show that, if the dual
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
random surface depins, then the spin-spin correlations cannot decay exponentially fast and instead are bounded from below by a certain power-law, ii) simplify and improve the lower bound by establishing the following dichotomy for the Villain model, which is known to be valid for a range of other statistical mechanical systems.

Lemma 1.5
.

(The two-point function dichotomy) At any
0<\beta<\infty
, the spin-spin correlation function of the Villain model on a doubly-periodic and tame graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, decay either exponentially fast, or not faster than
\rm{Const.}/d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)
. More explicitly: either

(1.7)

\langle\sigma_{0}\cdot\sigma_{x}\rangle^{Vill,{\mathcal{G}}}_{\beta}\leq A(\beta)\,\mathrm{e}^{-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,y)/\xi(\beta)}

at some finite
A(\beta)
and
\xi(\beta)
, or else (
1.6
) holds.

The proof is based on our extension of a Simon-Lieb type inequality to the Villain model, by which we join it to other known systems for which such a principle applies (references given below). Instrumental for that is the presentation of the Villain model as the metric graph limit of an
XY
model. This relation is presented here in
appendix

A
.

After presenting the above-listed results for the Villain and the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
models, we extend the analysis to other dual pairs including, in particular,
the
XY
spin model and its dual random height function
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF}
.

2.
Depinning in the integer-restricted Gaussian field

The main result in this section is the proof of
1.2
.

The
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
model and its two phases were presented in the introduction.
Its partition function is invariant under uniform shifts of the boundary conditions by an integer
k
, to
n_{u}=k
at all
u\in\partial\mathcal{V}_{L}
, in which case the corresponding Gibbs state will be shifted accordingly. Uniform shifts therefore represent potential symmetry of the system’s infinite-volume Gibbs states. In the pinned phase this symmetry is broken.

On any graph, the height fluctuations of the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
are upper bounded by those of the corresponding Gaussian free field (GFF)
[
26
]
(cf.
[
36
]
) in the sense that for every
x,y\in\mathcal{V}_{L}
,

(2.1)

\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[(n_{x}-n_{y})^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\text{GFF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[(\phi_{x}-\phi_{y})^{2}\right]\,.

Furthermore, for every
f:\mathcal{V}_{L}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[\exp(\left<f,n\right>)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\text{GFF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[\exp(\left<f,\phi\right>)\right].

The limit in (
1.2
) exists by the model’s absolute-value FKG property, which was established by Lammers–Ott
[
39
]
. By (
2.1
), if (
1.2
) holds for a single
x
then it holds for all
x
.

Quite generally, the marginal distributions of
(n_{x})
are log-concave. This important statement, which was proven by
Sheffield
[
51

, implies that when
eq.

1.2
is violated not only does the second moment diverge, but the entire support of the probability distribution drifts to infinity, in the sense that for any
t<\infty
,

(2.3)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\,\,\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,L}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}\left[\left\{|n_{x}|\leq t\right\}\right]\ =\ 0\,.

In particular,
for every
x\in\mathcal{V}
,

(2.4)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\,\,\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}}_{\lambda,L}\left[|n_{x}|\right]\ =\ \infty\,.

However, it still the case that, in both the pinned and depined phases, the distribution of the gradients of
n
, or equivalently of each of the difference variables
\{n_{x}-n_{y}\}_{x,y\in\mathcal{V}}
, remains tight (though not uniformly in
\|x-y\|
).
In this sense, the system behaves similarly to the lattice

2D
Gaussian free field, free of the
\mathbb{Z}
-valued constraint.

The GFF does not undergo a phase transition since, by its Hamiltonian’s homogeneity, the field’s distributions at different temperatures are related via simple rescaling by
\sqrt{\lambda}

{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
there are two natural scales: that of the discretization step (here
1

1/\sqrt{\lambda}

The delocalized phase for the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}

[
24

[

for shift-invariant planar graphs of maximum degree

3.
The Villain and XY spin models

3.1.
A pair of
O(2)
symmetric spin models

Prototypical examples of ferromagnetic systems with
O(2)
symmetry are arrays of two-component unit spins, attached to the sites
\mathcal{V}
of a homogeneous graph
\mathcal{G}
with, e.g., the XY ferromagnetic interaction

(3.1)

H^{\mathrm{XY}}(\sigma)=-\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\,\sigma_{u}\cdot\sigma_{v}=-\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}}J_{u,v}\,\cos(\theta_{u}-\theta_{v})\,.

at
J_{u,v}\geq 0
.
The spins are presented here in two equivalent ways: as two-component unit vectors
\sigma_{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, or as complex unitary numbers
\mathrm{e}^{i\theta_{x}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.

Since the phenomena discussed here are unique to two dimensions, the graphs
\mathcal{G}
of main interest are doubly-periodic and tame. The graphs may be finite or infinite, the latter case conveniently viewed as the infinite-volume limit of its finite restrictions. Thus
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is studied as a limit (
L\to\infty
) of its truncated version
\mathcal{V}_{L}=[-L,L]^{2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
By default we take the model with free boundary conditions.

The basic
O(2)
spin model, commonly referred to as the
XY
model, has as its partition function (at the nearest-neighbor interaction and free boundary conditions)

\displaystyle Z^{XY,{\mathcal{G}}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\left[-\pi,\pi\right)^{\mathcal{V}}}\prod_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{e}^{\beta J_{u,v}\cos(\theta_{u}-\theta_{v})}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{d}{\theta_{x}}\,

\displaystyle=\operatorname{e}^{\beta\|J\|}\int_{(\mathcal{S}^{1})^{\mathcal{V}}}\prod_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{\beta J_{u,v}}{2}\|\sigma_{u}-\sigma_{v}\|^{2}}\prod_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{d}{\sigma_{u}}\,.

with
\|J\|=\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}}J_{x,y}
.

In the Villain version of the
O(2)
model
[
57
]
each of the interaction factors in
section

3.1
is replaced through the following substitution

(3.3)

\operatorname{e}^{\beta J_{u,v}\cos(\theta_{u}-\theta_{v})}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{m_{uv}\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{\beta J_{u,v}}{2}\left(\theta_{u}-\theta_{v}+2\pi m_{uv}\right)^{2}}\,.

The resulting partition function is presented in (
1.4
).

In each case, the corresponding Gibbs equilibrium states is given by the probability distributions over
the spin configurations that is obtained by normalizing the measures integrated in
section

3.1
and
eq.

1.4
through the corresponding partition functions.

In what follows, we shall focus our attention on the ferromagnetic spin models with the nearest-neighbor couplings
J_{u,v}=\mathbbm{1}[d_{\mathcal{G}}(u,v)=1]
.

The
XY
and the Villain spin systems are similar in terms of the physics they model:

i)

in each the systems’ symmetries include uniform spin rotations

ii)

the weights assigned to the spin configurations have the same periodicity in
\theta
and same maxima, in the vicinity of which they agree up to the fourth order (after a trivial correction by factors of
\operatorname{e}^{\beta}
).

Of the two, the Villain version has a simpler dual, the duality being accomplished through a Fourier transform aided by the Poisson summation formula.

In addition, as is shown and applied below, the Villain model can be presented as a metric-graph continuum limit of an
XY
spin system. There is also a mathematical relation in the converse direction (see
9.6
).

Of particular interest is the behavior of the correlation function

(3.4)

\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}=\lim_{L\to\infty}\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta,L}

where
\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}=\cos(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y})

and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\beta,L}
denotes the expectation value with respect to the finite-volume Gibbs equilibrium state in
\Lambda(L)=[-L,L]^{2}
at the inverse temperature
\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}
with free boundary conditions. The limit’s existence for both the basic

model and its Villain version follows by the Ginibre correlation inequalities
[
28
,
26

. These imply that
\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta,L}
is monotone increasing in

, and more generally in the volume.

By general arguments
[
13
,
52
]
,
or more model-specific bounds
[

]
, at high enough temperatures the spin-spin correlations decay exponentially fast. That is, for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
, at some
\beta_{c}>0
,

(3.5)

\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}\leq A(\beta)\operatorname{e}^{-m(\beta)\|x-y\|}\qquad\mbox{with $m(\beta)>0$, $A(\beta)<\infty$}\,.

In dimensions
d>2
, such models exhibit also a low-temperature phase with continuous symmetry breaking, where
\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}>0

[
25
]
. However, by
the Mermin–Wagner theorem, that does not occur in two dimensions
[
15
,
44
,
42
]
.

Nevertheless, as was pointed out by Berezinskii
[

]
and Kosterlitz and Thouless
[

[

, two-component spin systems with a rotation-invariant interaction do exhibit a low temperature phase at which

It is expected that such behavior does not occur for systems in which the

[
45
]

Let

{\widetilde{q}}(n):=\operatorname{argmax}_{q\in\Set{n_{x^{-}}+\frac{1}{2},\dots,n_{x^{+}}-\frac{1}{2}}}\left\{\mathbb{P}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}^{*}}_{\lambda}\left[A_{\gamma_{yx}^{j},e}^{{\widetilde{q}}}\,\big|\,n_{x^{+}},n_{x^{-}}\right]\right\}.

The maximum is attained since the specified values of
n
leave only

|n_{x^{+}}-n_{x^{-}}|
options for
q
at which the probability does not vanish.

Recalling the definitions

\mathcal{N}^{+,+}(f_{0})=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}:q>0}\mathcal{N}_{q}^{+}(f_{0})\,,\qquad\mathcal{N}^{-,-}(f_{0})=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}:q<0}\mathcal{N}_{q}^{-}(f_{0})\,,

and summing the inequality in

over all

, we may conclude that

(8.2)

\displaystyle 4\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{+,+}(f_{0})+\mathcal{N}^{-,-}(f_{0})]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{(x,y)\in Q_{j}}\mathbb{P}[A_{\gamma_{yx}^{j},e}^{q}]=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{(x,y)\in Q_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{A_{\gamma_{yx}^{j},e}^{q}}\right].

Once the sum over
q
is placed within the expectation, its convergence becomes clear since

(8.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{A_{\gamma_{yx}^{j},e}^{q}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|n_{x^{+}}-n_{x^{-}}|\chi_{A_{\gamma_{yx}^{j},e}^{{\widetilde{q}}}}\,\right]

(Hölder, for any
\varepsilon>0
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|n_{x^{+}}-n_{x^{-}}|^{1/\varepsilon}\right]^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}\left[A_{\gamma_{yx^{j}},e}^{{\widetilde{q}}}\right]^{1-\varepsilon}

(Using
eq.

8.1
and defining
C_{\varepsilon}
)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\beta}\,\left(\langle\sigma_{x}\cdot\sigma_{y}\rangle\right)^{1-\varepsilon}\,.

where the first inequality is deduced through the intermediate conditioning on the values of
n
at
x^{+}
and
x^{-}
, by noticing that under this information the number of relevant values of
q
is at most
|n_{x^{+}}-n_{x^{-}}|
, and the one whose contribution to the sum is maximal is, by definition
{\widetilde{q}}(n)
.

The finiteness of
C_{\varepsilon}
is a special case of the Gaussian domination principle which is recalled below, applied to
v=\delta_{x^{+}}-\delta_{x^{-}}
.
This, combined with the last two inequalities, yields the claimed
eq.

5.4
.
∎

At the end of the argument we used:

Lemma 8.2
.

For the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
on any finite graph,
v\in\ker(-\Delta)^{\perp}
and
\varepsilon>0

(8.4)

\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle v,n\rangle|^{1/\varepsilon}\right]^{\varepsilon}\leq D_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{2\langle v,-\Delta^{-1}v\rangle}{\lambda}}

where
D_{\varepsilon}:=\left(\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right)\right)^{\varepsilon}<\infty

Proof.

The Gaussian domination bound on the generating function

2.10
states

(8.5)

\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF},\mathcal{G}^{*}}_{\lambda}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\langle n,v\rangle}\right]\leq\exp\left(+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\langle v,-\Delta^{-1}v\rangle\right)\qquad(v\perp\ker-\Delta)\,.

This, through the exponential Chebyshev stratagem yields the estimate

\mathbb{P}\left[\left\{|\langle v,n\rangle|\geq t\right\}\right]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{2}t^{2}/\langle v,-\Delta^{-1}v\rangle\right)\,.

The claim then follows through the expectation value’s layer-cake representation
\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\Set{X\geq t}\right]\mathrm{d}{t}

∎

9.
A broader class of models

The results presented above for the Villain model and the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
are extended below (in
section

10
) to a class of other dual pairs.
Of natural interest is the
XY
model and its dual, which for reasons explained below we call the integer-valued Bessel field (
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF}
). However the proofs will be cast in more general terms, covering
O(2)
spin models whose dual height functions can be presented as distributed under annealed Gaussian interactions. Following are the definitions of the relevant terms.

Throughout this section,
{\mathcal{G}}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is a finite truncation of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In
appendix

B
the construction is extended to more general doubly-periodic planar graphs.

9.1.
Annealed Gaussian interactions

Definition 9.1

(Annealed Gaussian interactions)
.

A potential function
U:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be an
annealed Gaussian interaction
if there exists a finite non-negative Borel measure
\mu_{U}
on
\left[0,\infty\right)
such that for all
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(9.1)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-U\left(q\right)\right)=\int_{\lambda\in\left[0,\infty\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda q^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}{\mu_{U}(\lambda)}\,.

To explain the terminology let us note that inserting (
9.1
) in (
9.2
) right below one finds that the corresponding height function is governed by random Gaussian couplings. However, while their initial distribution is that of independent random variables with the distribution
\mu_{U}
,
in the resulting joint distribution of
(n)
and
(\lambda)
their probability
is affected by the relative weight of the partition function at the joint values of

\{\lambda_{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}}
. In this sense the role played here by the random couplings fits the statistic-mechanical notion of an
annealed disorder
.

Definition 9.2

(
{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F}
random height function)
.

Given a function

U:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
we denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F}
the family of integer-valued random height functions associated with the finite graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}
and the function
U
, with probability measure

(9.2)

\mathbb{P}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},{\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}}(n)=\frac{1}{Z^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},{\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}}}\prod_{\Set{x,y}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}}\exp\left(-U\left(n_{x}-n_{y}\right)\right)\,\mathbbm{1}[\left.n\right|_{\partial\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}=0]\,,

where
Z^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},{\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}}
is the normalizing factor (the finite graph’s partition function, with the Dirichlet boundary conditions).

To every height function of the
{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F}
form is associated a dual
O(2)
spin model. The two are related through Fourier transform, as in the example spelled in (
9.7
) below.

Definition 9.3

(The dual spin model)
.

Given a
{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F}
on a graph
{\mathcal{G}}^{\ast}
, its dual spin model is a random spin function
\sigma_{x}=\mathrm{e}^{i\theta_{x}}
defined over the vertex set of
{\mathcal{G}}
with the probability distribution

(9.3)

\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}^{O(2),U,\mathcal{G}}(\theta)}=\frac{1}{Z^{O(2),U,\mathcal{G}}}\left(\prod_{\Set{x,y}\in\mathcal{E}}G_{U}(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y})\right)\mathrm{d}{\theta}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}{\theta}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\left[-\pi,\pi\right)^{|\mathcal{V}|}
(free boundary conditions)
and

G_{U}\left(\varphi\right):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\operatorname{i}m\varphi-U\left(m\right)\right)\,.

It should be noted that if
U
is an annealed Gaussian interaction then
G_{U}
is strictly positive. Furthermore, under this correspondence also the spin-spin correlation duality relation (
4.5
) persists. It should be noted that under this duality,
G_{U}
corresponds to a "physical" spin model only if it is non-negative.

The class of annealed Gaussian interactions includes the afore-studied integer-restricted Gaussian field
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{GF}
, for which
\mu_{U}=\delta_{\lambda}
. In this special case
under the duality relation the spin parameter
\beta
reappears as the inverse of the coupling strength
\lambda
of the dual
{\mathbb{Z}}
GF. In general the correspondence is less explicit. It is however still true that large spin-
\beta
implies large fluctuations for the
{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F}
, in the sense that the corresponding probability distribution of
n_{x}
, conditioned on its neighbors, flattens as
\beta\to\infty
.

9.2.
Divisibility and convexity

In addition to the above characterization of the interaction we shall be assuming
U
is convex. For
10.1
, we would require also the following condition

Definition 9.4

(divisibility)
.

An interaction
U
is said be divisible iff
e^{-U}
is decomposable through a continuous or discrete convolution, as either of the two following conditions:

(9.4a)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-U(a-b)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\exp(-\widetilde{U}(a-c))\,\exp(-\widetilde{U}(c-b))\,\mathrm{d}{c}\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb{R}),

(9.4b)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-U(a-b)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-\widetilde{U}(a-c))\,\exp(-\widetilde{U}(c-b))\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb{Z})\,,

with
\widetilde{U}
a convex annealed Gaussian interaction.

Remark 9.5
.

For graphs of degree higher than
4
we shall require a higher degree of divisibility. For that purpose we say that the interaction is
r-fold divisible
if

(9.5)

\exp(-U)=[\exp(-\widetilde{U})]^{*r}

where
*
is either the continuum
eq.

9.4a
or the discrete convolution operator as in
eq.

9.4b
.
9.4
corresponds then to
r=2
divisibility.

9.3.
The
XY
model and its dual, the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF}

The conditions listed above are all applicable to the
XY
model.
Its partition function, which was mentioned already in (
3.1
), is

(9.6)

Z^{XY,\mathcal{G}}_{\beta}\equiv\int_{\theta:\mathcal{V}\to[-\pi,\pi)}\mathrm{d}{\theta}\prod_{\Set{x,y}\in\mathcal{E}}\exp\left(\beta\cos\left(\theta_{x}-\theta_{y}\right)\right)

where integration is w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure on
[-\pi,\pi)^{|\mathcal{V}|}
.

The model’s dual is obtained through the Fourier transform, by which
on each edge the function of
\varphi=\theta_{x}-\theta_{y}
is presentable as

\displaystyle\exp\left(\beta\cos\left(\varphi\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\exp\left(-\operatorname{i}\varphi m\right)I_{m}(\beta)\,,

(9.7)

\displaystyle I_{m}(\beta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\varphi=-\pi}^{\pi}\exp\left(\beta\cos(\varphi)+\operatorname{i}\varphi m\right)\mathrm{d}{\varphi}\,,

I_{m}(\beta)
being the modified Bessel function.
Inserting this in (
9.6
) and integrating over the angles yields the dual representation of the partition function in terms of a sum over height functions
n:\mathcal{V}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{Z}
with the Bessel weights

(9.8)

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-|\mathcal{E}|}Z^{{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF},\mathcal{G}^{\ast}}_{\beta}=\sum_{n:\mathcal{V}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{Z}:\left.n\right|_{\partial\mathcal{V}^{\ast}}=0}\prod_{\Set{x,y}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}}I_{n_{x}-n_{y}}\left(\beta\right)

We shall refer to
n:\mathcal{V}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{Z}
with the corresponding normalized probability measure as the integer-valued Bessel field, and denote it by
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF}
.

The next statement rests on the observation, which was presented to the third author by A. Raoufi
[
46
]
,
that the Gibbs equilibrium measure of the
XY
model
can be presented as the annealed distribution of a spin model with Villain interactions of random strengths, whose initial distribution is given by an i.i.d. process. For the completeness of the presentation, we reproduce here Raoufi’s argument.

Lemma 9.6
.

The integer-valued Bessel field (the dual of the
XY
model) has an annealed Gaussian interaction.

Proof.

The proof starts with the observation that the
XY
Gibbs factor
\exp\left(\beta\cos\left(\varphi_{v}-\varphi_{u}\right)\right)

for a pair of spins with values
\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{i}\varphi_{u}}
and
\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{i}\varphi_{v}}
,
coincides with the transition amplitude of a Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, between a pair of points which happen to be on the unit circle.
Since in this case the transition amplitude depends only on the angular difference, it is natural to track the Browning motion

\left(\gamma(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in polar coordinates, as

\gamma(t)=B(t)\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{i}\varphi_{\gamma}(t)}\,.

The modulus
B(t):=\left\|\gamma(t)\right\|
has the distribution of the two-dimensional Bessel process starting at
B(0)=1
and conditioned on

B(1/\beta)=1
.
Conditioned on the process
B(t)
, the accumulated angle
\varphi_{\gamma}(t)
forms a continuous martingale, whose variance
grows at the rate

(9.9)

\eta(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{B(t^{\prime})^{2}}\mathrm{d}{t^{\prime}}\,.

(cf.
[
32
, Corollary 18.7]
).

Hence, denoting
\eta(1/\beta)=1/\lambda
,

(9.10)

\exp\left(\beta\cos\left(\varphi\right)\right)=\mathrm{e}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\lambda}(\varphi-2\pi m)^{2}\right)\,\mu(d\lambda)

with
\mu
the probability distribution of
\eta(1/\beta)^{-1}
conditioned on
B(1/\beta)=1
.

The assertion made for the
{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{BF}
random height function follows from (
9.10
) through the
Fourier transform by which the two models are related.
∎

Lemma 9.7
.

The potential corresponding to the modified Bessel function, i.e.

U(m)=-\log\left(\frac{I_{m}(\beta)}{I_{0}(\beta)}\right)\,,

is convex and divisible.

Proof.

Convexity of
U
is established in
[
54
, (1.9)]
).
Divisibility in the sense of (
9.4b
) is valid in this case since

(9.11)

\displaystyle I_{n-m}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{n-l}(\beta_{1})I_{l-m}(\beta_{2})\qquad(n,m\in\mathbb{Z};\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0)\,.

which follows from the convolution theorem for the Fourier series and the factorization

\mathrm{e}^{(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\cos(\varphi)}=\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{1}\cos(\varphi)}\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{2}\cos(\varphi)}\qquad(\varphi\in\mathbb{R})\,.

∎

10.4.
Pinning implies the existence of infinite-volume translation invariant measure

We close by proving the technical result mentioned above — pinning implies the existence of an infinite-volume Gibbs measure. The argument presented bypasses the absolute-value FKG property, and uses annealed RSD sublattice monotonicity (

Proposition 10.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\infty}^{\ast}

\Set{\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}_{L}

\mathcal{G}_{\infty}^{\ast}

Remark.
The statement actually holds more broadly, with
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\infty}
allowed to be a general quasi-transitive graph. Planarity enters only through the proof of
10.6

Proof.

We may assume that
\Set{\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}_{L}
is an increasing sequence of sets, in the sense of inclusion.
D.1
implies that, for any
x\in\mathcal{V}^{\ast}_{\infty}
,
\{\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}[n_{x}^{2}]\}_{L}
is an increasing sequence, and hence has a limit (which may be infinite, but is finite for at least one

\Set{\mathcal{G}_{L}^{1,\ast}}_{L}
and
\Set{\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2,\ast}}_{L}

\Set{\mathcal{G}_{L}^{3,\ast}}_{L}

\mathcal{G}_{1}^{3,\ast}=\mathcal{G}_{1}^{1,\ast}
and choosing the
(L+1)

\mathcal{G}_{L}^{3,\ast}
from the other exhausting sequence. Since
\lim_{L}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{3,\ast}}[n_{x}^{2}]
exists, it is the same as the subsequential limits along the odd or even elements, which give the limit over
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{1,\ast}
and
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2,\ast}

By translating the exhausting sequences, we deduce that
\lim_{L}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}[n_{x}^{2}]
is the same for all vertices in the same orbit under the action of translations on
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\infty}

\mathcal{G}^{\ast}_{\infty}
is doubly periodic (and thus has finitely many orbits), we conclude that
\Set{\lim_{L}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}[n_{x}^{2}]}_{x\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}}
takes on finitely many values, at least one of which is finite by assumption. Using
eq.

10.10

\lim_{L}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}\left[(n_{x}-n_{y})^{2}\right]<\infty

and

\left\{\mathbb{P}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}\right\}_{L}

We may repeat the same argument as above, replacing

with
\exp(an_{x})
for any

, and conclude that
\lim_{L}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L}^{\ast}}[\exp(an_{x})]
exists (though it may be infinite), and is independent of the choice of exhausting sequence. Since

is convex and symmetric about
0
, any finite-volume law of

is log-concave (see
[

Let us take a subsequence of

(10.14)

\lim_{j}\mathbb{E}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}_{L_{j}}^{\ast}}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{x\in S}a_{x}n_{x}\right)\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{x\in S}a_{x}n_{x}\right)\right].

Appendix A
A Lieb-Rivasseau type inequality for the Villain model

The practical goal of this section is to establish that the Villain model’s spin-spin correlation functions obey the stated inequality, from which follows the dichotomy that was stated in

XY

\mathrm{e}^{\mathcal{F}^{XY}_{N}(\theta_{u},\theta_{v})}=\mathrm{e}^{o(1)}\widetilde{C}_{\beta,N}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta J_{u,v}[(\theta_{v}-\theta_{u})+2\pi k]^{2}}

The above implies that in the limit

model’s spins
along the vertices of
{\mathcal{G}}
converges to that of the standard Villain model.

Furthermore, applying Donsker’s theorem

]
on the convergence of the paths of random walks to those of Brownian motion, we conclude that the distribution of the full spin function

along the edges of
\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{G}})

L^{\infty}
distance in the optimal coupling between a pair of random continuous functions.
The stated convergence of the correlation functions readily follows.

Let us stress that in the above analysis the limit
N\to\infty
is taken first, before the graph’s infinite-volume limit. That is however sufficient for our purpose. ∎

Let
\ell:=\lceil\log_{2}(d)\rceil
, with
d
the degree of
\mathcal{G}
. Divide each edge of the original graph
\mathcal{G}
into
r=2\ell

•

Appendix C
The Regev Stephens-Davidowitz monotonicity theory

For the completeness of presentation, we summarize below the key points of the Regev Stephens-Davidowitz monotonicity theory
[
47
]
, and state their relation to the case of interest here.

A lattice
\mathcal{L}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a set of the form
\mathcal{L}:=L\mathbb{Z}^{k}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
L\in\operatorname{Mat}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
. Given a positive-definite matrix
A:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
a random field
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
associated with
\left(A,\mathcal{L}\right)
has as its partition function

(C.1)

Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}:=\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{L}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle)\,,

and the probability distribution of its configurations is given by their normalized contributions to the sum.

The corresponding moment generating function is

(C.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[v\right]:=\mathbb{E}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[\exp\left(\langle v,\psi\rangle\right)\right]\qquad(v\in\mathbb{R}^{k})\,.

The following result is an important tool for the derivation of monotonicity estimates.

Theorem C.1
.

For any lattice
\mathcal{L}
and a positive matrix
A\geq 0

(C.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u\right]\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[v\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u+v\right]\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u-v\right]}\qquad(u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{k})\,.

One may note that in the Gaussian case, where the moment generating function is the exponential of a quadratic form, the relation holds as equality (due to the Pythagorean theorem).

Proof.

Rewriting the LHS in term of the Cartesian product of lattices
\mathcal{L}^{2}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}
:

(C.4)

\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u\right]\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[v\right]=Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}^{-2}\sum_{\Psi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Psi,A\oplus A\Psi\rangle+\langle\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix},\Psi\rangle\right)\,.

Under the linear transformation by
T=\begin{bmatrix}\mathds{1}_{k}&\mathds{1}_{k}\\
\mathds{1}_{k}&-\mathds{1}_{k}\end{bmatrix}
,
the lattice
T\mathcal{L}^{2}
is the disjoint union of translates of
(2\mathcal{L})^{2}
:

(C.5)

T\mathcal{L}^{2}=\bigsqcup_{w\in\mathcal{L}/2\mathcal{L}}(2\mathcal{L}+w)^{2}\,.

The matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}T
is unitary and
[T,M\oplus M]=0
for any
n\times n
matrix
M
and. Hence for any
\psi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}

(C.6)

-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Psi,A\oplus A\Psi\rangle+\langle\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix},\Psi\rangle=-\frac{1}{4}\langle T\Psi,A\oplus AT\Psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\begin{bmatrix}u+v\\
u-v\end{bmatrix},T\Psi\rangle\,.

Substituting this in
eq.

C.4
one gets

(C.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u\right]\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[v\right]=Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}^{-2}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/2\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{L}+w}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle u+v,\psi\rangle\right)\right)\times\\
\times\left(\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{L}+w}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle u-v,\psi\rangle\right)\right)\,.\end{split}

In particular, with
v=0
the last equation yields

(C.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u\right]=Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}^{-2}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/2\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{L}+w}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle u,\psi\rangle\right)\right)^{2}\,.

Applying now the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on the RHS of
eq.

C.7
w.r.t. the sum
\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/2\mathcal{L}}
and using
eq.

C.8
to re-identify
\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u+v\right],\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[u-v\right]
yields the statement.
∎

As was pointed out in
[
47
]
, from the inequality
eq.

C.3
one may deduce

sub-lattice monotonicity
of the moment generating function that was stated above as:

2.1
.

\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]\leq\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{L}}\left[v\right]\qquad(v\in\mathbb{R}^{k},A\geq 0)\,.

for any sub-lattice
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{L}

Proof.

Since

is a sub-lattice, we have the disjoint decomposition

\displaystyle Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}Z_{A,\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{M}+w}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle\right)\right)Z_{A,\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\langle w,Aw\rangle)Z_{A,\mathcal{M}}^{2}\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[-Aw\right]\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]\,.

Applying now

on the last two factors of

\displaystyle Z_{A,\mathcal{L}}Z_{A,\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{M}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\langle w,Aw\rangle)Z_{A,\mathcal{M}}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[-Aw+v\right]+\mathbb{M}_{A,\mathcal{M}}\left[-Aw-v\right]\right)

(

(Use

∎

(D.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}^{\ast},\mathcal{L}}[v]

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}^{\ast},\mathcal{M}}\left[\mathbb{M}_{A_{\lambda},\mathcal{M}}\left[v\right]\right]=\mathbb{M}^{{\mathbb{Z}}U\mathrm{F},\mathcal{G}^{\ast},\mathcal{M}}[v]\,.

.
∎

Appendix E
Gaussian decomposition of power-law interactions

As a supplement to the discussion of annealed Gaussian interactions (
section

9.1
), let us add the following example. Its proof serves to highlight the notion’s relation with Bernstein’s theorem.

Lemma E.1
.

The potential functions
U_{\alpha}(q)=\lambda|q|^{\alpha}

with
\alpha\in(0,2)
are presentable as annealed Gaussian interactions (in the sense of
9.1
).

Proof.

The decomposability into a superposition of Gaussians is related to
Bernstein’s theorem on completely monotone functions. The latter states that

(E.1)

\exp\left(-U\left(\sqrt{t}\right)\right)=\int_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda t\right)\mathrm{d}{\mu_{U}(\lambda)}\qquad(t>0)

for some probability measure
\mu_{U}
on
[0,\infty)
iff the function

F(t)=\exp\left(-U\left(\sqrt{t}\right)\right)

is "totally monotone", i.e., is continuous on
\left[0,\infty\right)
, smooth on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, and satisfies

(E.2)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}F^{(k)}(t)\geq 0\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0},t>0)\,.

To prove the present claim we apply the Bernstein’s criterion to the function

F_{\alpha}(t)=\exp(-\lambda t^{\alpha/2})\,.

Its regularity properties are clear. To establish
eq.

E.2
, we claim that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
,

(E.3)

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{(k)}(t)=F_{\alpha}(t)(-1)^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{M_{k}}a_{r}t^{p_{r}}

with
a_{r}\geq 0
and
p_{r}<0
for all
r=1,\dots,M_{k}
, and
M_{k}<\infty
.

The proof is by induction on
k
. At
k=0
,
eq.

E.3
is trivially true, and for
k=1
,

F_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)=-\lambda\,\frac{\alpha}{2}F_{\alpha}(t)\,t^{\alpha/2-1}<0\,.

Assuming the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{(k+1)}(t)

\displaystyle=\partial F(t)(-1)^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{M_{k}}a_{r}t^{p_{r}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\,\frac{\alpha}{2}\,F_{\alpha}(t)t^{\alpha/2-1}\,(-1)^{k}\,\sum_{r=1}^{M_{k}}a_{r}t^{p_{r}}+F_{\alpha}(t)(-1)^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{M_{k}}a_{r}p_{r}t^{p_{r}-1}

\displaystyle=F_{\alpha}(t)(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{r=1}^{M_{k}}a_{r}\left(\lambda\frac{\alpha}{2}\,t^{p_{r}+\alpha/2-1}+|p_{r}|t^{p_{r}-1}\right)

where in the last expression we applied the condition
p_{r}<0
.
Since also
\alpha/2-1<0
, the assumed structure extends also to
k+1
. ∎

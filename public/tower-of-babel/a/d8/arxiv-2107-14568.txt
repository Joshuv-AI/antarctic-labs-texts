Band strutures of hybrid graphene quantum dots with magnetic flux
\COSAIauthorblockN Bouchaib Lemaalem \COSAIauthorblockA Laboratory of Theoretical Physics Faculty of Sciences Chouaïb Doukkali University El Jadida, Morocco bouchaib.lemaalem@gmail.com, \COSAIauthorblockN Youness Zahidi \COSAIauthorblockA EMAFI Polydisciplinary Faculty Sultan Moulay Selimane University Khouribga, Morocco zahidi.youness@gmail.com, \COSAIauthorblockN Ahmed Jellal \COSAIauthorblockA Laboratory of Theoretical Physics Faculty of Sciences Chouaïb Doukkali University El Jadida, Morocco a.jellal@ucd.ac.ma

Abstract
We study the band structures of hybrid graphene quantum dots subject to a magnetic flux and electrostatic potential. The system is consisting of a circular single layer graphene surrounded by an infinite bilayer graphene. By solving the Dirac equation we obtain the solution of the energy spectrum in two regions. For the valley K K , it is found that the magnetic flux strongly acts by decreasing the gap and shifting energy levels away from zero radius with some oscillations, which are note observed for null flux case. As for the valley K ′ K^{\prime} , the energy levels rapidly increase when the radius increases. A number of oscillations appeared that is strongly dependent on the values taken by the magnetic flux.

Introduction

Quantum dots (QDs) in graphene
are very small particles with unique electronic and optical properties

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Since they
are highly tunable, then they can serve as interesting building blocks for materials that might be used to advance a wide range of applications such as solar cells, medical imaging and quantum computing.
Because of the Klein
tunneling effect and the absence of the gap in the energy spectrum,
Dirac fermions cannot be confined by electrostatic potentials

[
7
]
. One solution to overcome such situation is to realize
QDs for instance using thin single-layer graphene (SLG) strips
[
8
,
9
]

or nonuniform magnetic fields
[
10
]
. Generally,
the electronic and optical
properties of fermions in graphene depend on
shapes and edges of QDS
[
11
]
.

On the other hand, A-B bilayer graphene (BLG) consists of two SLG sheets
where
the A and B atoms in different layers are on top of each other

[
12
]
, called also Bernal stacking. The most important interaction
between the two layers is represented by a direct overlap integral
between A and B atoms on top of each other. BLG presents some particularities for instance
an external electric field, realized by external gate potentials, can induce a tunable band gap in its energy spectrum contrary to SLG.

Very recently a new generation of circular graphene QDs has been proposed based on a hybrid system
[
13
]
. Indeed, it was demonstrated that charge carriers can be confined in SLG and BLG islands in a hybrid QD-like structure made of SLG-BLG junctions. As a result, it is found that
the energy
levels exhibit characteristics of interface states in addition to the
emergence of
anti-crossings and closing of the band gap in the
presence of a bias potential.

Motivated by the results reported in
[
13
]
, we consider a geometry made
of a circular SLG QD subjected to a magnetic flux and surrounded by an infinite BLG sheet as depicted in Fig.
1
. We solve Dirac equation in both regions and determine in the first stage the eigenspinors. To derive equations governing the energy levels, we use the zigzag boundary conditions at interface. We numerically analyze our results and show that
the magnetic flux differently acts on the energy levels for both valleys
K
and
K^{\prime}
.

2.1
Eigenspinors for the two valleys

To determine the eigenspinors of fermions in monolayer graphene
subjected to magnetic flux, we consider the basis

\Psi^{\tau}(r,\theta)=(\Psi^{\tau}_{A},\Psi^{\tau}_{B})^{T}
in polar coordinates
(r,\theta)

because of the system symmetry. The valley index
\tau=+1
refers to
K
and
\tau=-1
to
K^{{}^{\prime}}

By showing that
[H,J_{z}]=0
, then the separability imposes
such as

\Psi^{\tau}(r,\theta)=e^{im\theta}\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{\tau}_{A}(r)\\
ie^{-i\tau\theta}\chi^{\tau}_{B}(r)\end{pmatrix}

(3)

where
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots
are eigenvalues of

L_{z}
associated to the total angular momentum
J_{z}=L_{z}+\frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_{z}
. To proceed further, let us
write the Hamiltonian (
1

H=v_{F}\hbar\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}&\pi_{+}\\
\pi_{-}&u_{1}\end{pmatrix}

(4)

in terms of the operators

\displaystyle\pi_{\pm}=e^{\pm i\varphi}\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\pm\frac{\tau}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\pm i\frac{\Phi}{\xi}\right)

such that the change of variable
\xi=\frac{r}{r_{0}}
and
the dimensionless quantities
\varepsilon=\frac{Er_{0}}{\hbar v_{F}}
,
u_{1}=\frac{U_{1}r_{0}}{\hbar v_{F}}

The eigenvalue equation
H\Psi(\xi,\theta)=\varepsilon\Psi(\xi,\theta)

allows to find two coupled equations

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}-\frac{\tau m-1+\Phi}{\xi}\right)\chi^{\tau}_{B}=\left(\varepsilon-u_{1}\right)\chi^{\tau}_{A}

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\frac{\tau m+\Phi}{\xi}\right)\chi^{\tau}_{A}=-\left(\varepsilon-u_{1}\right)\chi^{\tau}_{B}

(7)

By injecting (
7
) into (
6
) we obtain a second order differential equation for
\chi_{A}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}-\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\left(\tau m+\Phi\right)^{2}+\left(\varepsilon-u_{1}\right)^{2}\right]\chi^{\tau}_{A}=0

(8)

having the Bessel function as solution

\displaystyle\chi^{\tau}_{A}(\xi)=N^{\tau}J_{\nu}(\lambda\xi)

(9)

where we have set
\lambda=\varepsilon-u_{1}

and a new quantum number
\nu=\tau m+\Phi
depending on
the valley index
\tau
and flux
\Phi

N^{\tau}
is a constant of normalization. Now replacing (
9

7

second component

\displaystyle\chi^{\tau}_{B}(\xi)=-\tau N^{\tau}J_{\nu-\tau}(\lambda\xi)

(10)

With the help of some relations between Bessel functions, we finally obtain
the eigenspinors in monolayer graphene, which are

\Psi^{\tau}(\xi,\theta)=e^{im\theta}\begin{pmatrix}N^{\tau}J_{\nu}(\lambda\xi)\\
ie^{i\tau\theta}\tau N^{\tau}J_{\nu+\tau}(\lambda\xi)\end{pmatrix}

(11)

To achieve our task we consider the BLG region described by
Hamiltonian for the valley
K

(\tau=+1)

H^{+}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{+}+u_{-}&p_{+}&t^{{}^{\prime}}&0\\
p_{-}&u_{0}+\delta&0&0\\
t^{{}^{\prime}}&0&u_{0}-\delta&p_{-}\\
0&0&p_{+}&u_{0}-\delta\end{pmatrix}

(12)

in the basis
(\Phi_{A_{1}}^{+},\Phi_{B_{1}}^{+},\Phi_{A_{2}}^{+},\Phi_{B_{2}}^{+})

\chi^{+}_{B_{2}}(\xi)\\
ie^{i\theta}\chi^{+}_{A_{1}}(\xi)\end{pmatrix}

and then the eigenvalue equation gives rise to the set

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\xi}-\frac{m-1}{\xi}\right)\chi^{+}_{B_{1}}=\left(\varrho-u_{-}\right)\chi^{+}_{A_{1}}-t{{}^{\prime}}\chi^{+}_{B_{2}}

(14)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\xi}+\frac{m}{\xi}\right)\chi^{+}_{A_{1}}=-\left(\varrho-u_{-}\right)\chi^{+}_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\xi}+\frac{m+1}{\xi}\right)\chi^{+}_{A_{2}}=\left(\varrho+u_{-}\right)\chi^{+}_{B_{2}}(\rho)-t{{}^{\prime}}\chi^{+}_{A_{1}}

with

2.2
Zigzag boundary conditions

To derive equations describing the energy levels for the two valleys,
we use
the zigzag boundary conditions
[
15

\xi=1

\displaystyle\Psi^{\tau}_{A}(1,\theta)=\Phi_{A_{1}}^{\tau}(1,\theta)

(31)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\tau}_{B}(1,\theta)=\Phi_{B_{1}}^{\tau}(1,\theta)

(32)

\displaystyle 0=\Phi_{A_{2}}^{\tau}(1,\theta)

Consequently, (
31
-
33

\displaystyle M^{+}\begin{pmatrix}N^{+}\\
N^{+}_{1}\\
N^{+}_{2}\end{pmatrix}=0

for the valley

such that the matrix is

M^{+}=\begin{pmatrix}-J_{\nu}(\lambda)&K_{m}^{+}&K_{m}^{-}\\
J_{\nu-1}(\lambda)&a_{+}K_{m-1}^{+}&a_{-}K_{m-1}^{-}\\

\displaystyle a_{\pm}=\frac{\mu_{\pm}}{\varrho-u_{-}},\qquad b_{\pm}=\frac{\left(\varrho-u_{-}\right)^{2}+\mu_{\pm}^{2}}{\left(\varrho-u_{-}\right)t{{}^{\prime}}}

Regarding

N^{-}_{1}\\

At this level we point out that the energy levels for both valleys
are solutions of the two determinants

\displaystyle\det M^{+}=0,\qquad\det M^{-}=0

It is clear that form the complexity of the special functions

4
Conclusion

We have studied the influence of a magnetic flux on the energy levels
of a hybrid graphene. More precisely, we have considered a graphene quantum dot surrounded by a infinite bilayer graphene. Our result showed that the energy levels can oscillate, increase and shift under appropriate choice of flux and variation of the dot radius. We have found that the energy levels of the valleys
K
and
K^{\prime}
present different behaviors.

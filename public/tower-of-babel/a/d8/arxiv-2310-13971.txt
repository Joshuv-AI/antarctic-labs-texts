A well-balanced second-order finite volume approximation for a coupled system of granular flow
Aekta Aggarwal, Veerappa Gowda G. D, Sudarshan Kumar K

Abstract
A well-balanced second-order finite volume scheme is proposed and analyzed for a 2 × 2 2\times 2 system of non-linear partial differential equations which describes the dynamics of growing sandpiles created by a vertical source on a flat, bounded rectangular table in multiple dimensions. To derive a second-order scheme, we combine a MUSCL type spatial reconstruction with strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta time stepping method. The resulting scheme is ensured to be well-balanced through a modified limiting approach that allows the scheme to reduce to well-balanced first-order scheme near the steady state while maintaining the second-order accuracy away from it. The well-balanced property of the scheme is proven analytically in one dimension and demonstrated numerically in two dimensions. Additionally, numerical experiments reveal that the second-order scheme reduces finite time oscillations, takes fewer time iterations for achieving the steady state and gives sharper resolutions of the physical structure of the sandpile, as compared to the existing first-order schemes of the literature.

at the single time step is given by

\displaystyle{v}_{i}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}({{v}^{0}_{i}+{v}^{0,**}_{i}}{})

we finally arrive at:

\displaystyle{v}_{i}^{1}

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{0}+\frac{\theta}{2}\begin{cases}\Delta t-\lambda(\Delta t-\Delta x),\,\,\,&i=K+1\\

-\lambda(\Delta t+2\theta\Delta x),\,\,\,&i\geq K+3\\
\Delta t-\lambda(\Delta x-\theta\Delta t),\,\,\,&i=K-1\\
-\lambda(-\Delta t),\,\,\,&i=K-2\\
-\lambda(\theta\Delta t),\,\,\,&i=K-3\\
0,\,\,\,&i\leq K-4\\
\Delta t-\lambda(\Delta t-\Delta x),\,\,\,&i=K\end{cases}

This shows that the second-order scheme (

5

Second-order adaptive scheme in one-dimension

To produce a well-balanced second-order scheme, we propose a modification to the second-order scheme (
22
) based on the
idea introduced in
[
31
,
23
,
19
]
. The main principle involves using the the second-order scheme (
22
) away from the steady states and reducing it to the first-order scheme (
8
) near the
steady states. This modification results in a second-order scheme that is well-balanced. The measure of the closeness to a steady state
is determined through the following procedure.
We define a
smooth function
\Theta
such that

\displaystyle\Theta(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{x^{2}}{x^{2}+\Delta x^{2}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(35)

Note that
\Theta(0)=0
and
\Theta(x)\approx 1
for
x\neq 0
and sufficiently small
\Delta x.

For each
(i,n)\in(\mathcal{M}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathcal{N}_{T}
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Theta_{i}^{n}&:=\Theta(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{n})\\
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{n}&:=\mathcal{E}^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}^{n}_{{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\mathcal{E}^{n}_{{i+\frac{1}{2}}}&:=\sqrt{(G_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})^{2}+[H_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}]^{2}}\end{split}

(36)

where

\displaystyle[H^{n}_{{i+\frac{1}{2}}}]

\displaystyle:=H^{n}_{{i+\frac{1}{2}}}-H^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle H_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}

\displaystyle=H(\alpha_{i}^{n},v_{i}^{n},\alpha_{i+1}^{n},v_{i+1}^{n},B_{i},B_{i+1})

\displaystyle G_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}

\displaystyle=G(\alpha_{i}^{n},v_{i}^{n},\alpha_{i+1}^{n},v_{i+1}^{n})

Here,
G
and
H
are given by (
9
) and (
10
), respectively.
In each Runge-Kutta stage, previously defined left and right states (
2.2
) are modified for the linear function
z=\alpha,v
and
B
in
[x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{{i+\frac{1}{2}}}]
as follows:

\displaystyle z_{i+\frac{1}{2},L}^{n}

\displaystyle=z_{i}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{i}^{n}Dz^{n}_{i},\quad z_{i-\frac{1}{2},R}^{n}=z_{i}^{n}-\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{i}^{n}Dz^{n}_{i}

(37)

\displaystyle z_{i+\frac{1}{2},L}^{n,*}

\displaystyle=z_{i}^{n,*}+\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{i}^{n}Dz^{n,*}_{i},\quad z_{i-\frac{1}{2},R}^{n,*}=z_{i}^{n,*}-\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{i}^{n}Dz^{n,*}_{i}

(38)

where
Dz^{n}_{i},Dz^{n,*}_{i}
are defined
as before in Section
2
. Using these modified values (
37
) and (
38
), we compute
u^{n,*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},u^{n,**}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},v^{n,*}_{i}
and
v^{n,**}_{i}.
The resulting adaptive second-order scheme is now expressed as

\begin{split}u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}&=\frac{1}{2}({u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n,**}}{})\\
v_{i}^{n+1}&=\frac{1}{2}({v_{i}^{n}+v^{n,**}_{i}}{})\end{split}

(39)

Theorem 3

The adaptive scheme (
39
) is well-balanced and second-order accurate away from the steady state.

Proof
To prove that the scheme is well-balanced, it suffices to show that

(u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{1},v_{i}^{1})=(u_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{0},v_{i}^{0})=(\overline{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},\overline{v}_{i})

where
(\overline{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},\overline{v}_{i})
are the discrete steady states, as given in the proof of Theorem
2
.
As the first-order scheme is well-balanced (see
[
3
]
), it follows that
\Theta_{i}^{0}=0.
Consequently, in the RK step-1 of the adaptive scheme (
39
), we obtain

\displaystyle u^{0,*}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=u^{0}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\overline{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}},\quad v^{0,*}_{i}=v^{0}_{i}=\overline{v}_{i}\mbox{ and }\alpha^{0,*}_{i}=\alpha^{0}_{i}=\overline{\alpha}_{i}

Similarly, in the RK step-2, we obtain,

\displaystyle u^{0,**}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\overline{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\mbox{ and }v^{0,**}_{i}=\overline{v}_{i}

Therefore, from the adaptive scheme (
39
), we get
{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{1}=\overline{u}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}

and
{v}_{i}^{1}=\overline{v}_{i}.
Further, we observe that
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{i}\neq 0
away from the steady state, leading to the fact that
\Theta^{0}_{i}\approx 1
for sufficiently small
\Delta x
(see
[
19
,
23
,
31
]
). This shows that the adaptive scheme (
39
) is second-order accurate away from the steady state.

\Box

). Finally, the adaptive second-order scheme is derived by modifying the slopes using

6.3

Computation of the terms
B^{x}
and
B^{y}
in two dimensions

The computation of the terms
B^{x}
and
B^{y}
is as given in
[
3
,
1
]
. For completeness, we briefly review the construction in this section. Absorbing the source term with the convection terms is done by decomposing the source function
f
using the concept of
Transport Rays
(blue lines in Fig.
2
). This yields better results at the steady states, see
[
1
,
3
]
.
The terms
B^{x}
and
B^{y}
are defined as

B^{x}(x,y)=\intop_{0}^{x}f_{1}(\xi,y)d\xi\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall y\in[0,1],\quad B^{y}(x,y)=\intop_{0}^{y}f_{2}(x,\xi)d\xi\,\,\,\,\,\forall x\in[0,1]

where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
have to be chosen appropriately such that the source term
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
. The splitting is given as

f_{1}=f\cos^{2}\varphi,\quad f_{2}=f\sin^{2}\varphi

(46)

where
\varphi
is
the angle which the transport ray
R_{(x,y)}
makes with the positive
x
-axis. In the case of open table problem, the transport rays are as given in Fig.
2
(a) and the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are determined as

(a)

(b)

\Omega
and the boundary
\Gamma
with transport rays.

f_{1}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}f(x,y)&\,\,\mbox{if}&\{x\geq y,x+y\geq 1\}\cup\{x\leq y,x+y\leq 1\}\\
\\
0&\,\,\mbox{otherwise}&\end{array}\right.

(47)

and

f_{2}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}f(x,y)&\,\,\mbox{if}&\{x\geq y,x+y\leq 1\}\cup\{x\leq y,x+y\geq 1\}\\
\\

(48)

For more details, refer to

.
In the case of partially open table problem, illustrated in Fig.

and the functions

). The numerical approximation of

and

are given by

B_{i,k}^{x}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{i}}f_{1}(\xi,y_{k})d\xi,\quad B_{i,k}^{y}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y_{k}}f_{2}(x_{i},\xi)d\xi

6.4

Boundary conditions

Here, we consider two types of problems similar to the one dimensional case.

Case 1 (open table problem):
in this case, for computing the
u
variable, we impose the boundary conditions by setting

\displaystyle u^{n}_{\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{M+\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}=0\quad

\displaystyle u^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},M+\frac{1}{2}}=0,\quad i,k\in\mathcal{M}

Moreover, to compute the fluxes at the interior vertices of the boundary cells, we need the slopes in the boundary cells. For simplicity, we set these slopes to zero. The boundary conditions for
v
are prescribed through the fluxes
H^{x}
and
H^{y}
at the boundary. As we set the slopes in all boundary cells to zero, the corresponding fluxes are given by

\displaystyle H_{\frac{1}{2},k}^{n,x}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1,k}^{n}v_{1,k}^{n}-B^{x}_{1,k},\quad H_{M+\frac{1}{2},k}^{n,x}=-\alpha_{M,k}^{n}v_{M,k}^{n}-B^{x}_{M,k}

\displaystyle H_{i,\frac{1}{2}}^{n,y}

\displaystyle=-\beta_{i,1}^{n}v_{i,1}^{n}-B^{y}_{i,1},\quad H_{i,M+\frac{1}{2}}^{n,y}=-\beta_{i,M}^{n}v_{i,M}^{n}-B^{y}_{i,M},\quad i,k\in\mathcal{M}

Case 2 (partially open table problem):
we consider the partially open table problem in two-dimensions, where we choose the domain with wall boundaries as given in
Fig.
2
(b) (see
[
1
]
).
In this scenario, the evolution of the solution
u
at the boundary vertices involves prescribing its values appropriately. For the solution
v,
we impose boundary conditions through numerical fluxes. The conditions for
i,k\in\mathcal{M}
are outlined in the following lines:

•

Left vertical boundary

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{\frac{3}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}+h\max(\alpha^{n}_{2,k+\frac{1}{2}},0)

\displaystyle H^{n,x}_{\frac{1}{2},j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\alpha^{n}_{1,k}v^{n}_{1,k}-B^{x}(x_{1},y_{k}),&\mbox{ if }\alpha^{n}_{1,k}\geq 0\\
-B^{x}(x_{0},y_{k})&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

•

Right vertical boundary

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{M+\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{M-\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}}+h\max(\alpha^{n}_{M-1,k+\frac{1}{2}},0)

\displaystyle H^{n,x}_{M+\frac{1}{2},k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\alpha^{n}_{M,k}v^{n}_{M,k}-B^{x}(x_{M},y_{k}),&\mbox{ if }\alpha^{n}_{M,k}\leq 0\\
-B^{x}(x_{M+1},y_{k})&{\mbox{otherwise}}\end{cases}

•

Bottom horizontal boundary

\displaystyle u_{i+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}0&\,\,\mbox{if}&x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0.5\\
u_{i+\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}}^{n}+h\max(\beta_{i+\frac{1}{2},2}^{n},0)&\,\,\mbox{if}&x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}>0.5\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle H^{n,y}_{i,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\beta^{n}_{i,1}v^{n}_{i,1}-B^{y}(x_{i},y_{1}),&\mbox{ if }x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0.5\mbox{ and }\beta_{i,1}\geq 0\\
-B^{y}(x_{i},y_{-1})&\mbox{ if }x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0.5\mbox{ and }\beta_{i,1}<0\\
-\beta_{i,1}v^{n}_{i,1}-B^{y}(x_{i},y_{1}),&\mbox{ if }x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}>0.5\end{cases}

•

Top horizontal boundary

\displaystyle u^{n+1}_{i+\frac{1}{2},M+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},M-\frac{1}{2}}+h\max(\beta^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},M-1},0)

\displaystyle H^{n,y}_{i,M+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\beta^{n}_{i,M}v^{n}_{i,M}-B^{y}(x_{i},y_{M}),&\mbox{ if }\beta^{n}_{i,M}\leq 0\\
-B^{y}(x_{i},y_{M+1})&{\mbox{otherwise}}\end{cases}

Finally, at the corner vertices of the rectangular domain, solution
u
is computed by taking the average of solution computed through horizontal and vertical directions.
Note that all the boundary conditions are imposed in each stage of the RK time stepping.

Conclusion

In this study, we address the challenges associated with numerically approximating the Hadler and Kuttler (HK) model, a complex system of non-linear partial differential equations describing granular matter dynamics. Focusing on second-order schemes, we study the issues such as initial oscillations and delays in reaching steady states. We present a second-order scheme that incorporates a MUSCL-type spatial reconstruction and a strong stability preserving Runge-Kutta time-stepping method, building upon the first-order scheme. Through an adaptation procedure that employs a modified limiting strategy in the linear reconstruction, our scheme achieves the well-balance property. We extend our analysis to two dimensions and demonstrate the effectiveness of our adaptive scheme through numerical examples. Notably, our resulting scheme significantly reduces initial oscillations, reaches the steady state solution faster than the first-order scheme, and provides a sharper resolution of the discrete steady state solution.

Simplicity, bounded normal generation, and automatic continuity of groups of unitaries
Abhinav Chand, Leonel Robert

Abstract
. We show that the commutator subgroup of the group of unitaries connected to the identity in a simple unital C*-algebra is simple modulo its center. We then go on to investigate the role of “regularity properties” in the structure of the special unitary group of a C*-algebra. Under mild assumptions, we show that this group has the invariant automatic continuity property and a unique polish group topology. Strengthening our assumptions in the case of simple C*-algebras, we show that the special unitary group modulo its center has bounded normal generation. These results apply to all simple purely infinite C*-algebras and too all simple nuclear C*-algebras in the “classifiable class”. We show with counterexamples how our conclusions may in general fail if no regularity conditions are imposed on the C*-algebra.



1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
U_{0}(A)
denote the connected component of the identity of the unitary group of
A
. Let
DU_{0}(A)
denote the commutator (or derived) subgroup
of
U_{0}(A)
. If
A
is the C*-algebra of
n\times n
matrices
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then
DU_{0}(A)
—i.e., the special unitary group
SU(n)
—is perfect and simple modulo its center. We are thus led to ask whether these properties hold in more generality. If
A
has no 1-dimensional representations, then indeed
DU_{0}(A)
is perfect (
[
48
, Theorem 6.2]
). On the other hand, since non-commutative closed two-sided ideals of
A
give rise to non-central normal subgroups of
DU_{0}(A)
, for the simplicity question it is natural to assume that
A
itself be a simple C*-algebra. This question, and its counterpart with invertible elements instead of unitaries, has been investigated by several authors, going back to Kadison in the 50s, working in the setting of von Neumann algebra factors, followed by de la Harpe, Skandalis, Thomsen, Ng, Ruiz, among others (see
[
28
,
14
,
13
,
53
,
36
]
). In
[
48
]
the second author settled the question of simplicity modulo the center in the setting of invertible elements: the commutator subgroup of the connected component of the identity of the group of invertible elements of a simple unital C*-algebra is simple modulo its center. In the case of unitaries, the methods used in
[
48
]
also indicate a path to a proof, but key technical questions on Lie ideals were left unanswered in
[
48
]
.

The starting point of this paper is to address the technical points left unsolved in
[
48
]
. First, working in the additive setting, we prove that for a simple unital C*-algebra
A
, a
noncentral abelian subgroup
V
of
iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
(the skewadjoint elements of
A
) that is invariant under conjugation by
DU_{0}(A)
must contain
[A,A]\cap iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
(the skewadjoint elements in the linear span of the commutators). This partially answers a question raised by Marcoux in
[
35
, Question 4]
. Then, using techniques from
[
48
]
, we pass from the “additive” to the “multiplicative” setting to prove the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

If
A
is simple and unital then
DU_{0}(A)
is simple modulo its center.

While proving these results we take care to establish much finer versions that apply to non-simple C*-algebras, and that have a quantitative nature (phrased in terms of generating sets of normal subgroups). Our aim is to apply these finer results to questions on automatic continuity and bounded normal generation. In doing this, we follow in the footsteps of parallel results obtained by Dowerk and Thom for von Neumann algebra factors in
[
17
,
16
]
. These applications still revolve around
DU_{0}(A)
, but in situations where it naturally carries a Banach-Lie group structure, as it agrees with the group
SU_{0}(A)
.

We denote by
SU_{0}(A)
the subgroup of
U_{0}(A)
generated by
\{e^{ih}:h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}\}
.
It is not difficult to show that
SU_{0}(A)
is a path connected normal subgroup of
U_{0}(A)
containing
DU_{0}(A)
and contained in the kernel of the de la Harpe-Skandalis determinant. We regard
SU_{0}(A)
as the Banach-Lie subgroup of
U_{0}(A)
associated to the Lie algebra of skewadjoint commutators

\overline{[A,A]}\cap iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
. As such,
SU_{0}(A)
comes endowed with the topology having for basis of neighborhoods at the identity the sets

\{e^{ih}:h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}},\,\|h\|<\epsilon\}\quad(\epsilon>0).

The exponential length on
SU_{0}(A)
is defined as

\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(u)=\Big\{\inf\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}\|:u=\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{h_{j}}\hbox{ and }h_{j}\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}\hbox{ for all }j\Big\}.

This gives rise to a metric
(u,v)\mapsto\mathrm{el}(u^{*}v)
that is left and right invariant and induces the topology on
SU_{0}(A)
(
[
2
, Theorem A]
). Note: the topology
on
SU_{0}(A)
is in general not the same as the topology induced by its inclusion in
U_{0}(A)
(where the topology on the latter is that induced by the norm). In fact,
SU_{0}(A)
can be dense in
U_{0}(A)
in the norm topology, e.g., if
A
is simple, infinite dimensional, and of real rank zero.

Under the assumption that
[A,A]
is a closed set, which is present for many C*-algebras, we have that
DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)
, and so
DU_{0}(A)

is a complete metric space.

Let us say that a C*-algebra
A
has
bounded commutators generation
if
[A,A]
is closed, and furthermore there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
such that
if
h\in\overline{[A,A]}
then

h=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[x_{i},y_{i}]

for some
x_{j},y_{j}\in A
such that
\|x_{j}\|\cdot\|y_{j}\|\leq C\|h\|
for all
j
.

Bounded commutators generation is not an uncommon property of C*-algebras.
We shall return to this point later.
But first, let us state our automatic continuity result.

Following Dowerk and Thom (
[
17
, Definition 8.8]
), let us say that a topological group
G
has the invariant automatic continuity property if any group homomorphism

\phi\colon G\to H
, where
H
is a topological separable SIN group, is continuous. (A SIN group is one with a basis of neighborhoods of the identity invariant under conjugation.)

Theorem B
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra with bounded commutators generation and a full square zero element (i.e.,
x\in A
such that
x^{2}=0
and the ideal generated by
x
is
A
). The following are true:

(1)

SU_{0}(A)
has the invariant automatic continuity property.

(2)

If
A
is separable, then
SU_{0}(A)
admits a unique polish group topology.

Following Dowerk and Thom (
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
), let us say that a simple group
G
has bounded normal generation (BNG) if for any
g\in G\backslash\{1\}
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G=(\{hg^{\pm 1}h^{-1}:h\in G\})^{n}
.

We introduce here a weaker version of the BNG property for topological groups. Let us say that a topological group
G
has local bounded normal generation if for any
g\in G\backslash\{1\}
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\{hg^{\pm 1}h^{-1}:h\in G\})^{n}

is a neighborhood of the identity.

Theorem C
.

Let
A
be a simple unital C*-algebra with bounded commutators generation. The following are true:

(1)

SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
is a simple group with local BNG.

(2)

If
SU_{0}(A)
is bounded as a metric space, then
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
has BNG.

If
A
is a traceless unital C*-algebra, then by a theorem of Pop
A=[A,A]
and
A
has bounded commutators generation. It follows that in this case

DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)=U_{0}(A)
,
and Theorems
B
and
C
apply directly to
U_{0}(A)
(endowed with the norm topology). Let us single out the well studied case of purely infinite simple C*-algebras: If
A
is a unital, separable, simple, purely infinite C*-algebra, then
U_{0}(A)
has the automatic invariant continuity property and a unique polish group topology. Moreover, since the exponential length of simple purely infinite C*-algebras is finite (
[
40
]
),
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
is a simple group with bounded normal generation.

Combining the invariant automatic continuity property with results on the structure of Lie algebra isomorphisms by Ara-Mathieu and Brešar, we show that the group structure of
U_{0}(A)
suffices as a classification invariant for prime traceless C*-algebras. More specifically, if
A
and
B
are separable prime traceless unital C*-algebras containing full square zero elements, and
U_{0}(A)\cong U_{0}(B)
as groups, then
A
is either isomorphic or anti-isomorphic to
B
(Corollary
6.5
). The question whether C*-algebras can be classified up to (anti-)isomorphism by their unitary groups has been studied in
[
1
]
, but it remains in general not well understood.

In contrast to the traceless case, if
A
has at least one tracial state, then
U_{0}(A)
has discontinuous automorphisms. This is well-known in the case of the classical unitary group (i.e.
A=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
), and the argument can be adapted to an arbitrary C*-algebra with a tracial state (Theorem
6.6
). So we must turn to
SU_{0}(A)
in this case. In order to apply Theorems
B
and
C
, we must
ensure that the C*-algebra has the property of bounded commutators generation.
For unital C*-algebras with tracial states, this property is known to hold assuming some form of regularity on the C*-algebra. Here are some classes of C*-algebras with bounded commutators generation (see Theorem
6.1
):

•

C*-algebras of finite decomposition rank (in the sense of Kirchberg and Winter) and no finite dimensional representations.

•

C*-algebras with strict comparison of positive elements by traces and almost divisible Cuntz semigroup.

•

Exact C*-algebras tensorially absorbing the Jiang-Su algebra.

Theorems
B
and
C
(i) can be readily applied to the C*-algebras in these classes (the existence of full square zero elements holds automatically in each case).

By Theorem
C
(ii), in order to guarantee BNG we must verify the boundedness of
SU_{0}(A)
(which is also necessary to have BNG).
We prove below that if
A
is a simple C*-algebra with strict comparison of positive elements by traces, stable rank one, and finite exponential rank, then
SU_{0}(A)
is bounded.
All necessary hypotheses are present for the simple nuclear C*-algebras recently classified in the Elliott program: If
A
is a separable simple unital C*-algebra with finite nuclear dimension and in the UCT class, then
SU_{0}(A)
has the automatic invariant continuity property and a unique polish group topology, and
SU_{0}(A)
modulo its center is a simple group with BNG (Corollary
7.6
).

In the final section of the paper we turn to the construction of counterexamples. These show that bounded commutators generation cannot be altogether dropped in Theorem
C
. In
[
46
]
the second author constructed examples of simple unital C*-algebras without bounded commutators generation. Essentially the same construction allows us to prove the following theorem.

Theorem D
.

There exists a simple unital AH C*-algebra
A
with a unique tracial state and the following properties:

(1)

DU_{0}(A)/Z(DU_{0}(A))
is a simple group without BNG.

(2)

The inclusion of
DU_{0}(A)
in
SU_{0}(A)
is proper.

(3)

There exist exponentials
e^{ih_{1}},e^{ih_{2}},\ldots\in SU_{0}(A)
such that
\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(e^{ih_{n}})\to\infty
,
where
\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(\cdot)
denotes the exponential length in
U_{0}(A)
. In particular,

SU_{0}(A)
is unbounded as a metric space.

To prove this theorem we first find topological obstructions to “short exponential length on commutators” in the unitary groups of homogeneous C*-algebras. A simple C*-algebra is then obtained putting the result for homogeneous C*-algebras through the Villadsen machine. We note that, although a general recipe for obtaining C*-algebras of infinite exponential length and having more than one tracial state was provided in
[
40
]
, examples with a unique tracial state are harder to come by and did not previously exist in the literature. Theorem
D
shows that
[
40
, Question 4.4]
has a negative answer.

The following estimates around the exponential function are easy to derive. We include their proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0<|t|<1
,
x,y\in A
, and
v\in A
.

(1)

We have that

[v,x]=\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)+t\Delta_{1}.

where
\|\Delta_{1}\|\leq\|v\|\cdot e^{2\|x\|}
.

(2)

We have that

[[v,x],y]=\frac{1}{t^{2}}(e^{ty}\Big(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v\Big)e^{-ty})-\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)+t\Delta_{2}

where

\|\Delta_{2}\|\leq\|v\|\cdot(e^{2\|x\|+2\|y\|}+2\|y\|e^{2\|x\|})

Proof.

(i) Recall that

e^{x}ve^{-x}=v+[v,x]+\frac{1}{2!}[[v,x],x]+\frac{1}{3!}[[[v,x],x],x]]+\cdots

(Put differently,
\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{x}}=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{x}}
; see
[
26
, Proposition 5.16]
). Replacing
x
by
tx
we get

e^{tx}ve^{-tx}=v+t[v,x]+t^{2}\Delta_{1},

where

\|\Delta_{1}\|\leq\|v\|\frac{e^{2\|x\||t|}-1-2|t|\|x\|}{t^{2}}\leq\|v\|e^{2\|x\|}.

Isolating
[v,x]
the formula of the lemma readily follows.

(ii) Using (i), we have

\displaystyle[[v,x],y]

\displaystyle=[\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)+t\Delta_{1},y]

\displaystyle=[\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v),y]+t[\Delta_{1},y]

Using (i) again on the first term on the right side we get

[[v,x],y]=\frac{1}{t}\left(e^{ty}\Big(\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)\Big)e^{-ty}-\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)\right)+t\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}+t[\Delta_{1},y].

where

\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}\|\leq\Big\|\frac{1}{it}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)\Big\|e^{2\|y\|}\leq\|v\|e^{2\|x\|}e^{2\|y\|}.

Combining this estimate with (i), the estimate for
\Delta_{2}=\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}+[\Delta_{1},y]
readily follows.
∎

3.
Additive setting

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let

(3.1)

\mathcal{D}=\{[a,b]:a,b\in A_{\mathrm{sa}},\,\|a\|,\|b\|\leq 1\}\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}.

Given

and a set

we denote by

the set of sums of
n

elements of
X
.

In this section we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra containing a full square zero element. Let
V\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
be a fully noncentral set invariant under conjugation by
DU_{0}(A)
and multiplication by
-1
. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\sum^{n}V
.

For our applications to automatic continuity we will need a finer version of this theorem which says that the function
V\mapsto n
, assigning
to a set
V
as in the theorem the least
n
such that
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\sum^{n}V
, is locally bounded in a
suitable sense. We state this theorem next. Given sets
X,Y\subseteq A
, we write
X\subseteq_{\epsilon}Y
to indicate that
X\subseteq\{x\in A:d(x,Y)\leq\epsilon\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A
and
V\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Then there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a finite set
F\subseteq V
, and
\epsilon>0
, such that
if
V^{\prime}\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
is a fully noncentral set invariant under conjugation by
DU_{0}(A)
and multiplication by
-1
, and
F\subseteq_{\epsilon}V^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\sum^{n}V^{\prime}
.

The proofs of the preceding theorems will follow after a series of lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
and
V\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Then there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.2)

1=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j}v_{j}y_{j},

where
x_{j},y_{j}\in A
,
\|x_{j}\|,\|y_{j}\|<1
, and
v_{j}\in\pi_{3}(V^{8})
for all
j=1,\ldots,M
. (Here
\pi_{3}
is the nc-polynomial defined in (
2.1
).)

Proof.

Since
V
is fully noncentral,
\pi_{3}(V^{8})
is a full set, by Lemma
2.2
. Therefore, the unit
1\in A
is expressible as a finite sum of terms of the form
xvy
, with
x,y\in A
and
v\in\pi_{3}(V^{8})
. Enlarging the number of terms if necessary we may
assume that
\|x\|,\|y\|<1
for each of these terms. The lemma is thus proved.
∎

For the remainder of this section we assume that

Lemma 3.4

Any

Proof.

In equation (

from Lemma

, and let

be the number of terms of the form

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

. There exists

, where

Proof.

We start by writing

as a sum of

terms of the forms (a) and (b) of Lemma

.

Let us consider a term of the form

, with

and

. By Lemma
2.6
(i),

[v,x]=\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)+t\Delta_{1},

where
\|\Delta_{1}\|\leq\|v\|e^{2\|x\|}
. Observe that
\frac{1}{t}(e^{tx}ve^{-tx}-v)

\frac{1}{t}V

Since

(as we have assume that

is contained in the open unit ball)

Let us consider a term of the form
[[v,y],z]

and

. By Lemma

(ii),

[[v,y],z]=\frac{1}{t^{2}}e^{tz}\Big(e^{ty}ve^{-ty}-v\Big)e^{-tz}-\frac{1}{t}(e^{ty}ve^{-ty}-v)+t\Delta_{2}

Again, since

,

\frac{1}{t}V
, plus the error term

.
Since

Adding all the equations for the terms of the forms

Remark 3.8
.

If
A
is a simple unital C*-algebra other than
\mathbb{C}
, then it contains a non-zero (hence full) square zero element,
by Glimm’s halving lemma. If
A
has no 1-dimensional representations and stable rank one,
then again the existence of a full square zero element is guaranteed by
[
3
, Theorem 9.1]
. So
in these cases we can drop the existence of a full square zero element from the hypotheses of the previous theorem (the fullness of the set
[H,A]
already precludes the existence of 1-dimensional representations). If

has no finite dimensional representations and real rank zero, then again it contains a full square zero element by

[

, Corollary 2.4]
. It is an open problem, known as the “Global Glimm halving problem", whether every unital C*-algebra without finite dimensional representations must contain a full square zero element.

The special unitary group

Let us briefly recall the definition of the de la Harpe-Skandalis determinant. We refer the reader to

Let

Define

(A/\overline{[A,A]})\,/\,(2\pi i\{T(p)-T(q):p,q\in M_{\infty}(A)\hbox{ projections}\}),

We are interested in

\sum_{j=1}h_{j}\in\overline{[A,A]}+2\pi\{Tr(p)-Tr(q):p,q\in M_{\infty}(A)\hbox{ projections}\}.

The group

such that in any one (and all) representations as a

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

There is a path

(2)

generated by
\{e^{ih}:h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}\}

(3)

such that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{k}\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}

(4)

There is a path

starting at 1 and ending at

such that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{T}(\eta|_{[0,t]})=0
for all

.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Choose

, where

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) This is obvious.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) The path

∎

Let

Clearly,

We regard

Then

The exponential length on

It is easily established that

Remark 5.2
.

The topology on
SU_{0}(A)
need not be that induced by the topology on
U_{0}(A)
. Take for example

to be an infinite dimensional simple C*-algebra of real rank zero with at least one tracial state
(e.g. the CAR C*-algebra

). Then

is a norm dense proper subgroup of

(
[
20
]
). In particular,

is not complete under the uniform structure induced
by the norm topology.

We summarize various properties of

in the following theorem:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. The following are true:

(1)

DU_{0}(A)
is a dense subgroup of
SU_{0}(A)
. If
\overline{[A,A]}=[A,A]
, then
DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)
.

(2)

If
A
is traceless, then
DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)=U_{0}(A)
, and the topology on
SU_{0}(A)
is that induced by the norm on
A
.

(3)

If
A
is separable, then
SU_{0}(A)
agrees with the path connected component of 1 in
\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
.

(4)

If
A
has stable rank one, then
SU_{0}(A)=\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
.

(5)

If
A
has real rank zero, then
SU_{0}(A)=\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
.

(6)

If
A
is simple, then
SU_{0}(A)
is topologically simple modulo its center.

Proof.

(i) This follows at once from the continuity of

\overline{[A,A]}\ni h\mapsto e^{ih}\in SU_{0}(A)
and the fact that
DU_{0}(A)
is generated by the set
\{e^{ih}:h\in[A,A]\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}\}
(
[
48
, Theorem 6.2]
).

(ii) By Pop’s theorem
[
42
, Theorem 1]
, if
A
is a traceless unital C*-algebra then

A=[A,A]
. Since the set
\{e^{ih}:h\in[A,A]\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}\}
is a generating set
of
DU_{0}(A)
, it follows that
U_{0}(A)=DU_{0}(A)
, and a fortiori
SU_{0}(A)=U_{0}(A)
.

(iii)
Claim
: If
[t_{1},t_{2}]\ni t\mapsto u(t)
is a continuous path in
\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
such that
\|u(t)-1\|<1
for all

,

\log(u(t))\in\overline{[A,A]}
for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
. Proof:
Let
h(t)=\log(u(t))
for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, which is defined since
\|u(t)-1\|<1
,
and such that
h(t_{1})=0
. Let
\tau
be a tracial state. Then

varies continuously, and by the description of
\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
(see (
5.1
)), ranges in the set

2\pi i\{\tau(p)-\tau(q):p,q\in M_{\infty}(A)\hbox{ projections}\}.

This set is countable, since
A
is separable and the value of a trace on a projection is invariant
under homotopy. Hence,
t\mapsto\tau(h(t))
is constant, and so it is 0 as
h(t_{1})=0
. This shows
\tau(h(t))=0
for all tracial states
\tau
, which in turn implies that

for all

. This proves the claim.

In virtue of the claim just established, if
u\in\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
is connected to 1 through a path

inside

and such that

for all

,
then the path lies entirely in
SU_{0}(A)
. We can drop the assumption that
\|u(t)-1\|<1

by partitioning the path into small enough pieces.

(iv) Let
u\in\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
, and write

, where

h_{k}\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
for all

. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}h_{k}=h+2\pi iTr(p)-2\pi iTr(q),

for some

and projections
p,q\in M_{\infty}(A)
.
By
[
48
, Lemma 6.1]
,

is equal modulo commutators (in
DU_{0}(A)
)
to
e^{ih}e^{2\pi iTr(p)}e^{-2\pi iTr(q)}
. Since
DU_{0}(A)\subseteq SU_{0}(A)
and

e^{ih}\in SU_{0}(A)
, it remains to prove that any unitary of the form

v=e^{2\pi iTr(p)}
, with
p\in M_{n}(A)
a projection, belongs to
SU_{0}(A)
.
Consider the projection loop
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto e^{2\pi itp}
in
M_{n}(A)
. By Rieffel’s theorems

[
43
, Corollary 8.6]
and
[
44
, Proposition 2.6]
, the loop
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto e^{2\pi itp}
is homotopic
to a loop
\beta\colon[0,1]\to U_{0}(A)
(here we have used the stable rank one hypothesis). Now
\eta(t)=e^{2\pi itTr(p)}\beta^{-1}(t)

is a path in
U_{0}(A)
connecting

to
e^{2\pi iTr(p)}
and

\widetilde{\Delta}_{T}(\eta)=\Delta(t\mapsto e^{2\pi itTr(p)})-\Delta(\beta)=2\pi iTr(p)-2\pi iTr(p)=0.

It follows by Lemma

that

, as desired.

(v) Let
u\in\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
. By Lin’s
[
33
, Theorem 5]
,
u=e^{ih_{1}}e^{ih_{2}}
,
where
\|h_{1}\|\leq\pi
and the norm of

can be made arbitrarily small. Choosing
h_{2}

of a sufficiently small norm we have that
e^{ih_{1}}e^{ih_{2}}=e^{i(h_{1}+h_{2})}e^{ic}
, where

(
[
38
, Lemma 2.2]
). Since
e^{ic}\in SU_{0}(A)
,
it remains to show that if
e^{ih}\in\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
,
then
e^{ih}\in SU_{0}(A)
. We have that
h=h^{\prime}+2\pi(Tr(p)-Tr(q))
,
with
h^{\prime}\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
and

projections.
As in the proof of (iv),

is equivalent modulo commutators to

By Zhang’s

such that

and

is a diagonal matrix with projections

.
So

is equivalent modulo

(vi) Let

be a closed normal subgroup not contained in the center of

(thus, not contained in the center of

A
. Since

.

Remark 5.4
.

Consider the inclusions

DU_{0}(A)\subseteq SU_{0}(A)\subseteq\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A).

Both inclusions may be proper, though it is often the case that all three sets agree (e.g., by a combination of the results in the preceding theorem).
If
A=M_{n}(C(X))
, for
X
a compact Hausdorff space, then
DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)
by
[
53
, Proposition 1.3]
, and it is not difficult to show that
SU_{0}(A)=\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
.
The examples that we construct in Section
8
below show failure of the first inclusion
for some simple AH C*-algebras.

The question whether
SU_{0}(A)=\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
—equivalently, in the separable case, whether
\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)

is a path connected subset of
U_{0}(A)
—is closely related to
whether the C*-algebra
A
has determinant unitary rank equal to 1, in the sense of Gong, Lin, and Xue
[
23
]
.
In
[
23
, Theorem 5.13]
, an example is given of a homogeneous C*-algebra with determinant unitary rank
>1
.
This is also an example where
SU_{0}(A)\neq\ker\Delta_{T}\cap U_{0}(A)
. Briefly described,
A=pM_{4}(C(S^{4}))p
, where
S^{4}
denotes the 4-dimensional sphere and

p\in M_{4}(C(S^{4}))
is a suitably chosen rank 2 projection. By the results in
[
23
]
there exists a projection
q\in M_{2}(A)
equivalent to the trivial rank one projection such that for no loop

\alpha\colon[0,1]\to U_{0}(A)
can one have
\widetilde{\Delta}_{T}(\alpha)=2\pi iTr(q)+\overline{[A,A]}
. It follows that

does not belong to
SU_{0}(A)
, since a path
\eta
of zero determinant connecting
1
to
u
concatenated with the path

[0,1]\ni t\mapsto e^{2\pi i(1-t)Tr(q)}
would provide one such loop.
On the other hand,
u
is clearly in the kernel of the determinant.

6.
Automatic continuity

Let us recall the definition of the property of bounded commutators generation given in the introduction.
Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. We say that
A
has bounded commutators generation if there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
such that for all
h\in\overline{[A,A]}
we have

h=\sum_{j=1}^{n}[x_{j},y_{j}]

for some
x_{j},y_{j}\in A
such that
\|x_{j}\|\cdot\|y_{j}\|\leq C\|h\|
for all
j
.

In the following theorem we gather some classes of C*-algebras known to have bounded commutators generation:

Theorem 6.1
.

The following classes of unital C*-algebras have bounded commutators generation:

(1)

traceless C*-algebras,

(2)

C*-algebras having finite decomposition rank and without finite dimensional representations,

(3)

pure C*-algebras (i.e., with almost unperforated and almost divisible Cuntz semigroup)
where every bounded 2-quasitrace is a trace,

(4)

exact C*-algebras tensorially absorbing the Jiang-Su C*-algebra.

Proof.

(i) This is Pop’s
[
42
, Theorem 1]
.

(ii) This follows from
[
46
, Theorem 1.2]
.

(iii) This follows from
[
37
, Theorem 4.10]
.

(iv) This follows from (iii), as a Jiang-Su stable C*-algebra is pure. Moreover, exactness implies that bounded 2-quasitraces are traces, by Haagerup’s theorem (
[
24
]
). Thus, the conditions in (iii) are met.
∎

Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{D}
the subset of
[A,A]\cap iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
defined in (
3.1
).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra containing a full square zero element and having bounded commutators generation. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}^{N}

is a neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
for all
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

By the property of bounded commutators generation, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
is of norm
\leq 1
, then

ih=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}

for some
z_{j}\in\mathcal{D}
. Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
be as in Lemma
4.1
applied to
n=N
elements. Let
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
. Then

e^{\epsilon ih}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{\epsilon z_{j}^{\prime}},

where
z_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
for all
j
. Hence, the set
\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}^{N}
contains all
e^{ih}
, with
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
of norm
\leq\epsilon
, and it is thus a neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
.
If on the other hand
\epsilon\geq\epsilon_{0}
, then
\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}
contains

\{e^{\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}
, and so again
\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}^{N}
is a neighborhood of the identity.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra containing a full square zero element and having bounded commutators generation. Let
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
be fully noncentral.
For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let

H_{\alpha}=\{ue^{\pm i\alpha h}u^{*}:u\in SU_{0}(A)\}.

Then there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
such that the sets
H_{\alpha}^{n}
,
with
0<\alpha\leq\delta
, form a basis of neighborhoods of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
, there exist
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0

such that for each
0<\alpha\leq\delta
the set
H^{n_{1}}_{\alpha}
contains

\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}
for some
\epsilon>0
. Then, with
N\in\mathbb{N}
as in Lemma
6.2
,
H_{\alpha}^{n_{1}N}
contains

\{e^{\epsilon z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}^{N}
, and it is thus a neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
.

Set

. Let

be an arbitrary neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
. Choose

, symmetric neighborhood of the identity invariant under conjugation and such that

. Then,
e^{i\alpha h}\in W
for a small enough

∎

Let us recall the invariant Steinhaus property, introduced by Dowerk and Thom (
[
17
]
).
Let
G
be a topological group. A subset
W
of
G
is called (left) countably syndetic if countably many left translates of
W
cover
G
; i.e.,

G=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}W
for some
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots\in G
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The group
G
is said to have the invariant Steinhaus property with exponent

n\in\mathbb{N}
if for any
W\subseteq G
that is countably syndetic, symmetric (
W=W^{-1}
), and invariant under conjugation,
W^{n}
is a neighborhood of the identity.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra containing a full square zero elements and having bounded commutators generation. Then
SU_{0}(A)
has the invariant Steinhaus property.

Proof.

Let us choose
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
fully noncentral (i.e.,
h=[x^{*},x]
, with
x
a full square zero element; see Lemma
4.4
).
Let
H_{\alpha}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\delta>0
be as in Theorem
6.3
. Let us show that
SU_{0}(A)
has the invariant Steinhaus property with exponent
2n
.

Let
W
be a subset of
SU_{0}(A)
that is symmetric, invariant under conjugation, and countably syndetic. Say
SU_{0}(A)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}W
for some
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots
in
SU_{0}(A)
. Then for some
k
the set
g_{k}W\cap\{e^{i\alpha h}:\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\}
is uncountable. Using that

is symmetric, it follows that
W^{2}

contains
e^{i\alpha_{0}h}
for some
0<\alpha_{0}\leq\delta
. Hence,
H_{\alpha_{0}}^{n}\subseteq W^{2n}
.
Thus,

is a neighborhood of the identity, by Theorem

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

(i) By the previous theorem,
SU_{0}(A)
has the invariant Steinhaus property, which by

[
17
, Proposition 8.10]
implies that
SU_{0}(A)
has the invariant automatic continuity property.

(ii) Let
\phi\colon U_{0}(A)\to U_{0}(A)
be a group automorphism. Let us show that
\phi
is continuous, from which the desired result immediately follows.
By
[
6
, Corollary 3]
, to show that a group isomorphism of polish groups

is continuous, it suffices to show that for all
U\subseteq G_{2}
ranging in a basis of neighborhoods of the identity,
\psi^{-1}(U)
is an analytic set in

.

Let

be fully noncentral (guaranteed to exist by

). Let

,

, and

, so that

. Let

\phi^{-1}(H_{\alpha}^{n})=\{uv_{\alpha}^{\pm 1}u^{-1}:u\in SU_{0}(A)\}^{n}.

∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\phi\colon U_{0}(A)\to U_{0}(B)
be a group isomorphism, where
A
and
B
are prime, unital, traceless C*-algebras
containing full square zero elements. Then
\phi
is the restriction to
U_{0}(A)
of either an isomorphism or an anti-isomorphism between
A
and
B
.

Proof.

Since
A
and
B
are traceless,
SU_{0}(A)=U_{0}(A)
and
SU_{0}(B)=U_{0}(B)
. So Theorem
B
applies directly to
U_{0}(A)
and
U_{0}(B)
. By the invariant automatic continuity property of these groups,
\phi
is a homeomorphism. Recall that
U_{0}(A)
and
U_{0}(B)
are the Banach-Lie groups of the Lie algebras of skewadjoint elements
iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
and
iB_{\mathrm{sa}}
. Thus,
by the functoriality of the Lie algebra of a Banach-Lie group (
[

), there exists a Lie algebras homomorphism

such that

for all

.
Moreover, since
\phi
is an isomorphism, so is
\psi
. Let us extend

to

by setting

\psi(a+ib)=-i\psi(ia)+\psi(ib),

for
a,b\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
. We readily check that
\psi
is again a Lie algebras isomorphism between
A
and
B
. By
[
4
, Theorem 6.5.24]
(alternatively, by
[
9
]
),

\phi
is either an isomorphism or an anti-isomorphism of C*-algebras. In either case we get that

\phi(e^{ih})=e^{\psi(ih)}=\psi(e^{ih})

for all

. It follows that
\phi
is the restriction of
\psi
to
U_{0}(A)
.
∎

If
A
is a unital traceless C*-algebra containing a full square zero element, then the equality

and Theorem

imply that

does not have discontinuous automorphisms. This is in contrast with the tracial case.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
A
be a separable unital C*-algebra with at least one tracial state. Then
U_{0}(A)
admits discontinuous automorphisms.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of the same result for
U_{0}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
from
[
6
]
.

Let
\tau\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
be a tracial state. Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{T}
denote the group of scalar unitaries in the kernel of
\Delta_{\tau}
. From the description of the kernel of
\Delta_{\tau}
(
5.2
) we see that

\Gamma=\{e^{2\pi i\theta}:\theta\in\tau(K_{0}(A))\}.

Since
A
is separable, this group is countable (as
K_{0}(A)
is countable). Let us choose a discontinuous automorphism
\alpha\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
that fixes
\Gamma
. (To get one, let
V
be the vector subspace of
\mathbb{R}
spanned by
\tau(K_{0}(A))
, where the scalar field is
\mathbb{Q}
. Choose a basis of
V
, a fortiori a countable set, and extend it to a Hamel basis of
\mathbb{R}
. Using this basis, choose a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear transformation
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that is the identity on
\tau(K_{0}(A))
, but that is otherwise discontinuous.
Now define
\alpha(e^{2\pi i\theta})=e^{2\pi if(\theta)}
, which is well defined since
f
is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\tau(K_{0}(A))
.)

Any unitary
v\in U_{0}(A)
is expressible in the form
zu
, where
z\in\mathbb{T}
and
u\in\ker\Delta_{\tau}
.
To see this, choose any path
\eta\colon[t_{1},t_{2}]\to U_{0}(A)
connecting
1
to
v
, and set
z=e^{2\pi i\theta}
, with
\theta=\tilde{\Delta}_{\tau}(\eta)
.
Then
v=zu
with
u\in\ker\Delta_{\tau}
.

Let us define
\tilde{\alpha}\colon U_{0}(A)\to U_{0}(A)
by
\tilde{\alpha}(zu)=\alpha(z)u
, where
z\in\mathbb{T}
and
u\in\ker\Delta_{\tau}
. We readily verify that
this is a well defined map: If
z_{1}u_{1}=z_{2}u_{2}
, with
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
and
u_{1},u_{2}\in\ker\Delta_{\tau}
, then

z_{1}z_{2}^{-1}=u_{2}u_{1}^{-1}\in\Gamma.

Since
\alpha
is the identity on
\Gamma
,
\alpha(z_{1}z_{2}^{-1})=u_{2}u_{1}^{-1}
, which in turn implies that

\alpha(z_{1})u_{1}=\alpha(z_{2})u_{2}
. Hence
\tilde{\alpha}
is well defined. It is then easily shown that
\tilde{\alpha}
is a bijective group homomorphism. Since the restriction of
\tilde{\alpha}
to the scalar unitaries agrees with
\alpha
,
\tilde{\alpha}
is necessarily discontinuous.
∎

7.
Bounded normal generation

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra containing a full square zero element and having bounded commutators generation. Let
H\subseteq SU_{0}(A)
be a symmetric, fully noncentral set invariant under conjugation. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
H^{n}
is a neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, there exists
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
H^{n_{1}}
contains
\{e^{z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}
, where
\mathcal{D}\subseteq iA_{\mathrm{sa}}
as defined in (
3.1
).
On the other hand, by Lemma
6.2
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{e^{z}:z\in\mathcal{D}\}^{N}
is a neighborhood of the identity in

. It follows that
H^{n_{1}N}
is also a neighborhood of the identity, thus proving the theorem.
∎

Specializing the theorem above to simple C*-algebras, we obtain Theorem
C

from the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

If
A=\mathbb{C}
, the theorem is trivial. Assume thus that this is not the case. Observe then that
A
contains nonzero square zero elements, by Glimm’s halving lemma, and these are automatically full since
A
is simple.

(i) Let
g
be an element in
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
distinct from the identity. Let
w\in SU_{0}(A)
be any lift of
g
, necessarily noncentral (and fully noncentral, as
A
is simple). Let
H=\{uw^{\pm 1}u^{*}:u\in SU_{0}(A)\}
. By Theorem
7.1
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
H^{n}

contains a neighborhood of the identity in
SU_{0}(A)
. Then
H^{n}
is mapped onto a neighborhood of the identity by the quotient map. This establishes local bounded normal generation for
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
.

(ii) Suppose that
SU_{0}(A)
is bounded as a metric space. Claim: For any neighborhood of the identity
V\subseteq SU_{0}(A)
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
SU_{0}(A)=V^{m}
. Proof:
By
[
2
, Theorem A]
, the boundedness of
SU_{0}(A)
under the exponential length metric implies that
SU_{0}(A)

is coarsely bounded. This, by
[
49
, Theorem 1.4]
, implies that there exist a finite set
F\subseteq SU_{0}(A)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
SU_{0}(A)=(FV)^{k}
. Since
SU_{0}(A)

is also connected, the finite set
F
is contained in
V^{k^{\prime}}
for a large enough
k^{\prime}
. Hence
SU_{0}(A)=V^{(k^{\prime}+1)k}
, proving the claim.

Let
g
be an element in
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
distinct from the identity. Let us apply the claim just established to
H^{n}
, with
H
and
n
as in the proof of (i). Then
U_{0}(A)=H^{nm}
. Passing to the quotient, we obtain bounded normal generation for
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
.
∎

A class of C*-algebras to which all results of this section and the previous section apply is the purely infinite simple C*-algebras.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let

be a simple, unital, purely infinite C*-algebra. Then
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
has bounded normal generation,

has the automatic invariant continuity property, and if

is separable then

Since

is traceless,

and

has bounded commutators generation, by Pop’s theorem. Hence,

. Moreover, by
[

, the exponential length of

is at most

. Hence,
U_{0}(A)
is bounded. The previous theorem then implies that
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}

.
∎

In Theorem
6.1
we have already recalled various classes of C*-algebras that have bounded commutators generation. We now turn to the question of boundedeness of
SU_{0}(A)
, in order to produce more examples of C*-algebras where the group
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
has bounded normal generation.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Then
\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(e^{i[x^{*},x]})\leq\pi
for all
x\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
.

Proof.

If
x=0
the lemma is trivially true, so assume that this is not the case. Write
x=Cy
, with
y\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
of norm 1 and
C=\|x\|
. It suffices to prove the lemma in the case that
y
is the canonical generator in the universal C*-algebra generated by a square zero element of norm
\leq 1
. This C*-algebra is
M_{2}(C_{0}(0,1])
, with
y=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
. Let us thus assume that we are in this set-up. Then

e^{i[x^{*},x]}=e^{iC^{2}[y^{*},y]}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{iC^{2}t^{2}}&0\\
0&e^{-iC^{2}t^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

This is a unitary in the unitization of
M_{2}(C_{0}(0,1])
.

Let
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}\leq 1
be the points where
Ct^{2}
is an integer multiple of
\pi
.
Choose
\delta>0
such that the intervals
(t_{k}-2\delta,t_{k}+2\delta)
, with
k=1,\ldots,n
, are pairwise disjoints.
Define
f\in C_{0}((0,1])_{+}
as follows:

f(t)=\begin{cases}Ct_{k}^{2}&\hbox{if $t\in(t_{k}-\delta,t_{k}+\delta)$ for some $k$,}\\
Ct^{2}&\hbox{if $t\notin\bigcup_{k}(t_{k}-2\delta,t_{k}+2\delta)$,}\\
\hbox{linear}&\hbox{for $t\in(t_{k}-2\delta,t_{k}-\delta)$ and $t\in(t_{k}+\delta,t_{k}+2\delta)$ and all $k$.}\end{cases}

Let
\epsilon>0
. Choose
\delta
smaller if necessary, so that the function
f
defined above satisfies that
|f(t)-Ct^{2}|<\epsilon
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Now let
g(t)=f(t)-Ct^{2}
for
t\in[0,1]
, so that

Ct^{2}=f(t)+g_{+}(t)-g_{-}(t).

Set

x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&f^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad y_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&g_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad y_{2}=y_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&g_{-}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is straightforward to verify that

[x^{*},x]=[(x^{\prime})^{*},x^{\prime}]+[y_{1}^{*},y_{1}]+[y_{2}^{*},y_{2}].

Since the summands on the right-hand side commute, we have that

e^{i[x^{*},x]}=e^{i[(x^{\prime})^{*},x^{\prime}]}e^{i[y_{1}^{*},y_{1}]}e^{i[y_{2}^{*},y_{2}]}.

Observe that
\|y_{1}\|=\|g_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|<\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so that
\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(e^{i[y_{1}^{*},y_{1}]})<\epsilon
. Similarly, we have that
\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(e^{i[y_{2}^{*},y_{2}]})<\epsilon
.

To complete the proof, let us show that
e^{i[(x^{\prime})^{*},x^{\prime}]}
is unitarily equivalent to

e^{i[(x^{\prime\prime})^{*},x^{\prime\prime}]}
for some
x^{\prime\prime}
such that
\|x^{\prime\prime}\|\leq\sqrt{\pi}
. Define
f_{1}\in C(0,1]

such that
0\leq f_{1}(t)\leq\pi
for all
t\in[0,1]
, and either
f(t)-f_{1}(t)
or
f(t)+f_{1}(t)
is an integer multiple
of
\pi
for all
t
. Let

x^{\prime\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&f_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\|x^{\prime\prime}\|\leq\sqrt{\pi}
and

e^{i[(x^{\prime})^{*},x^{\prime}]}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{if}&0\\
0&e^{-if}\end{pmatrix},\quad e^{i[(x^{\prime\prime})^{*},x^{\prime\prime}]}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{if_{1}}&0\\
0&e^{-if_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

These two unitaries are unitarily equivalent. This stems from the fact that the unordered pairs
\{e^{if(t)},e^{-if(t)}\}
and
\{e^{if_{1}(t)},e^{-if_{1}(t)}\}
agree for all
t
, and further,
the functions
e^{if(t)}
agree
e^{-if(t)}
on an open neighborhood of the values of
t
at which they cross. A unitary
t\mapsto w(t)
conjugating
e^{i[(x^{\prime})^{*},x^{\prime}]}
and
e^{i[(x^{\prime\prime})^{*},x^{\prime\prime}]}
is gotten by setting
w(t)
equal to either the identity matrix or the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
for
t
outside the intervals
(t_{k}-\delta,t_{k}+\delta)
, and inside these intervals
t\mapsto w(t)
is defined as any path connecting these two matrices.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra of nuclear dimension at most 1. Then
\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(e^{ih})\leq 4\pi
for all
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
.
By
[
38
, Theorem 3.2]
,
there exist
sequences
h_{n}^{(1)},h_{n}^{(2)},h_{n}^{(3)},h_{n}^{(4)}
in
\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
such that

(1)

h_{n}^{(1)}+h_{n}^{(2)}+h_{n}^{(3)}+h_{n}^{(4)}\to h
,

(2)

[h_{n}^{(1)}+h_{n}^{(2)},h_{n}^{(3)}+h_{n}^{(4)}]\to 0
,
[h_{n}^{(1)},h_{n}^{(2)}]\to 0
,

[h_{n}^{(3)},h_{n}^{(4)}]\to 0
,

(3)

h_{n}^{(k)}=[(x_{n}^{(k)})^{*},x_{n}^{(k)}]
with
x_{n}^{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
for
k=1,2,3,4
and all

.

We now use
[
38
, Lemma 2.2]
, which states that if
a_{n},b_{n}
are bounded sequences of selfadjoint elements
such that
[a_{n},b_{n}]\to 0
then
e^{i(a_{n}+b_{n})}e^{-ia_{n}}e^{-ib_{n}}\to 0

in
SU_{0}(A)
. Using this lemma (repeatedly) we deduce that

e^{ih_{n}^{(1)}}e^{ih_{n}^{(2)}}e^{ih_{n}^{(3)}}e^{ih_{n}^{(4)}}\to e^{ih}

in

. On the other hand, by Lemma

, the exponential length in

of

is bounded by

∎

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. The exponential rank of
A
is defined as the least
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that any unitary
u\in U_{0}(A)
is a product of at most
m
exponential unitaries (i.e., of the form
e^{ih}
with

h\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
). If no such
m
exists then the exponential rank of
A
is set equal to
\infty
. If
A
has exponential rank
m+1
, and the products of
m
exponentials form a dense subset of
U_{0}(A)
, then
A
is also said to have exponential rank
m+\epsilon
.

Next, let us recall the property of strict comparison by traces in a simple unital C*-algebra.
(There is also a version of this property for arbitrary C*-algebras; e.g., see
[
37
]
.) Let
A
be a simple unital C*-algebra. Then
A
said to have strict comparison of positive elements by traces if
for all
a,b\in(A\otimes\mathcal{K})_{+}
such that
d_{\tau}(a)<d_{\tau}(b)
for all tracial states
\tau

on
A
we have that
a\precsim_{\mathrm{Cu}}b
. (Here
d_{\tau}(c):=\lim_{n}\tau(c^{\frac{1}{n}})
and
\precsim_{\mathrm{Cu}}
denotes the Cuntz comparison relation, i.e.,
d_{n}^{*}bd_{n}\to a
for some sequence
d_{n}\in A\otimes\mathcal{K}
.) This property, introduced by Blackadar in
[
7
]
, is a C*-algebraic analogue of the “comparison of projections by traces" properties of factors.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
A
be a simple unital C*-algebra. Suppose that
A
has stable rank one, strict comparison by traces, and finite exponential rank. Then
SU_{0}(A)
is bounded. Consequently,
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
has BNG.

Proof.

If
A
is a matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the theorem is well known. Let us thus assume that
A
is non-elementary.
Then, under the hypotheses of the theorem,
A
is a pure C*-algebra, i.e., its Cuntz semigroup is almost unperforated and almost divisible. Almost unperforation of the Cuntz semigroup is implied by strict comparison of positive elements by traces (
[
ERS
]
), which
A
is assumed to have. For simple non-elementary unital C*-algebras of stable rank one, almost divisibility in the Cuntz semigroup follows automatically from almost unperforation, by Thiel’s
[
51
, Theorem 8.11]
. Since
A
is pure and has strict comparison of positive elements by traces, it has bounded commutators generation
(Theorem
6.1
). Thus, the BNG property for
SU_{0}(A)/Z(SU_{0}(A))
will indeed follow once we have shown that

SU_{0}(A)
is bounded.

To show that
SU_{0}(A)
is bounded we shall apply the “special subalgebra technique" also employed in
[
38
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
27
]
. More specifically, by
[
38
, Theorem 4.1]
, there exists a C*-subalgebra
B\subseteq A
such that

(1)

[B,B]=[A,A]\cap B
,

(2)

B
has nuclear dimension 1 (in fact,
B
is of the form
C\otimes W
, where
C
is an AF C*-algebra and
W
is the Jacelon-Razak algebra),

(3)

every non-invertible selfadjoint element
h\in A
with connected spectrum is approximately unitarily equivalent to some
h^{\prime}\in B
.

The proof now proceeds along a series of claims, each time expanding the set of elements in
SU_{0}(A)
on which the exponential length

Proof.

By the classification theorem of Gong, Lin, and Niu in

Remark 7.7
.

In
[
18
]
Dowerk introduced the property of “topological bounded normal generation”. A topological group
G
has this property if for any
g\in G
distinct from the unit there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{hg^{\pm 1}h^{-1}:h\in G\}^{n}
is dense in
G
.
In
[
15
]
examples are given of nonsimple groups with topological bounded normal generation. The C*-algebras covered in Corollary
7.6
provide us with new examples of such groups. Take
A
an infinite dimensional simple unital AF C*-algebra. Since
A
has at least one tracial state, there are noncentral unitaries in
U_{0}(A)
with nonzero determinant. Hence,
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
is not simple, as
DU_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
is a proper normal subgroup of
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
. In particular,
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
does not have BNG. Let us show that
U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
has topological bounded normal generation. Let
u\in U_{0}(A)
be noncentral. Then
u^{\prime}=(u,e^{ih})
is noncentral for some
h\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
. By the bounded normal generation of
DU_{0}(A)
, there exists
n
such that

DU_{0}(A)=(\{v(u^{\prime})^{\pm 1}v^{*}:v\in DU_{0}(A)\})^{n}\subseteq(\{vu^{\pm 1}v^{*}:v\in DU_{0}(A)\})^{2n}.

Since
A
has real rank zero,
U_{0}(A)=\overline{DU_{0}(A)}^{\|\cdot\|}
(
[
20
]
). Thus,
(\{vu^{\pm 1}v^{*}:v\in DU_{0}(A)\})^{2n}
is dense in
U_{0}(A)
. This shows that

U_{0}(A)/\mathbb{T}
has topological bounded normal generation.

8.
Counterexamples

Let us first introduce notation and recall a known lemma on the exponential length in
U_{0}(A)
. Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Given
u\in U_{0}(A)
, we define its exponential length as

\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(u)=\inf\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|h_{j}\|:u=\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{ih_{j}},\,h_{j}\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}\hbox{ for all }j\Big\}.

Clearly, for
u\in SU_{0}(A)
we have that
\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(u)\leq\mathrm{el}_{SU_{0}(A)}(u)
(the latter defined in (
5.3
)). These two quantities are often different. We call
(u,v)\mapsto\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(u^{*}v)
the exponential distance in
U_{0}(A)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\theta<\pi
. Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra and let
u\in U_{0}(A)
be such that
\mathrm{el}_{U_{0}(A)}(u)<m\theta
. Then there exist
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
such that
u=\prod_{j=1}^{m}e^{ih_{j}}

and
\|h_{j}\|<\theta
for all
j
.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
45
, Theorem 2.6]
. By assumption,

u=\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{if_{j}},

were
f_{j}\in A_{\mathrm{sa}}
for all
j
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|<m\theta
. Increasing
n
if necessary, we can assume that
\|f_{j}\|<\delta
for all
j
, where we choose
\delta>0
such that
0<\frac{m\theta}{\theta-\delta}<m+1
. Let us now break-up the list
e^{if_{1}},\ldots,e^{if_{n}}
into groups of consecutive exponentials such that the norms of the exponents in each group add up to less than
\theta
, but
\geq\theta-\delta
. It is then easily derived that the number of groups obtained in this way must be at most
m
. The product of the exponentials in each of these groups can be written as
e^{ih}
, with
\|h\|<\theta
, by
[
45
, Corollary 2.2]
. We thus have
u=\prod_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}e^{ih_{j}}
, with
\|h_{j}\|<\theta
for all
j
and
m^{\prime}\leq m
. Setting
h_{j}=0
for
m^{\prime}<j\leq m
the lemma readily follows.
∎

8.1.
Homogeneous C*-algebras

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space (below we will fix
X=(S^{2})^{n}
). Let us introduce notation and recall some standard facts around the projections in
M_{\infty}(C(X))
.

Let
p,q\in M_{\infty}(C(X))
be projections. Then
p
and
q
are said to be Murray-von Neumann equivalent if
p=v^{*}v
and
q=vv^{*}
for some
v\in M_{\infty}(C(X))
. We denote the Murray-von Neumann class of a projection
p\in M_{\infty}(C(X))
by
[p]
.

Suppose that
p\in M_{m}(C(X))
and
q\in M_{n}(C(X))
. Let us denote by
p\oplus q
the projection

\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&q\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n+m}(C(X)).

One has that
[p\oplus q]
depends only on
[p]
and
[q]
. Setting

[p]+[q]:=[p\oplus q]
defines the addition operation in the Murray-von Neuman monoid of projections.

Let us denote by
\mathrm{her}(p)
the C*-algebra
pM_{\infty}(C(X))p
, i.e., the hereditary C*-subalgebra generated by
p
. We adopt a 2x2 matrix notation for the elements of
\mathrm{her}(p\oplus q)
. More specifically, we represent
a\in\mathrm{her}(p\oplus q)
as the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}pap&paq\\
qap&qaq\end{pmatrix}.

We will make reference to the complex vector bundle associated to a projection. The complex vector bundle on
X
associated to
p
is
(X,E,\pi)
, where

E=\{(x,v)\in X\times\mathbb{C}^{m}:p(x)v=v\},

and
\pi\colon E\to X
is the projection onto the first coordinate. We denote this vector bundle by
\eta_{p}
. The isomorphism class of
\eta_{p}

depends only on
[p]
.

Let
p\in M_{\infty}(C(X))
be a projection. Let us denote by
e(p)\in H^{*}(X)
the Euler class of the vector bundle
\eta_{p}
. If
p
has rank
k
, then
\eta_{p}
has dimension
k
over
\mathbb{C}
, and
2k
over
\mathbb{R}
, so
e(p)\in H^{2k}(X)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From this point on we fix
X=(S^{2})^{n}
, where
S^{2}
denotes the 2-dimensional sphere.

Let us recall a few standard facts on the homology and cohomology groups (with integer coefficients) of
X
. By Kunneth’s theorem,

H^{*}(X)\cong Z[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}]\,/\,(\alpha_{j}^{2}:j=1,\ldots,n),

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in H^{2}(X)
are induced by the projections
\pi_{j}\colon X\to S^{2}
onto each sphere factor
and the choice of a generator in
H^{2}(S^{2})
.

For each choice of indices
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
, let
S_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}\subseteq X
denote the cartesian product with
S^{2}
at the indices
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
and a fixed point (choose any) at the remaining indices. The inclusion
S_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}\subseteq X
gives rise to homology classes
[S_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}]\in H_{*}(X)
, image of the fundamental homology class of
S_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}
in
H^{*}(X)
. By Poincare duality, we have that
H_{n-k}(X)\cong H^{k}(X)
for
k=0,\ldots,n
, where the isomorphism is given by
\alpha\mapsto\alpha\cap[X]
(cap product with the fundamental homology class of

). Moreover, we can compute that

(\alpha_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{k}})\cap[X]=[S_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{n-k}^{\prime}}],

where the indices on the right side run through the complement of the indices on the left side.

Let
\kappa\colon X\to[-1,1]
be defined as

(8.1)

\kappa((x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n}))=x_{1}.

Let

) is still valid. Let
Z\subseteq X
denote the zero set of the off-diagonal elements (
8.3
). The transversality to the zero section implies that
Z
is an orientable submanifold of
X
whose homology class
[Z]
in
H_{*}(X)
is equal to the Poincare dual of the Euler class of
\eta_{q}^{\oplus 5km}
(
[
8
, Proposition 12.8]
). This Euler class is

e(q^{\oplus 5km)})=e(q)^{5km}
.
By assumption,
e(q)^{5km}
is a nonempty sum of monomials
\alpha_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{l}}
, where
i_{j}\neq 1
for all
j
. Using that

\alpha_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{l}}\cap[X]=[S_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{n-l}^{\prime}}],

where the indices on the right side run through the complement of the indices on the left side, we obtain that
[Z]
is a sum of homology classes coming from subproducts of spheres of the form

[S_{1,i_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{n-l}^{\prime}}]
(i.e., all the terms include the index
1
). Let us decompose
Z
into connected components—which are also oriented submanifolds—and pick a connected component
Z^{\prime}
. Then
[Z^{\prime}]
is again a sum of homology classes coming from subproducts of spheres, where all the terms include the first sphere, i.e., have the form
[S_{1,i_{2},\ldots,i_{l}}]
.

Claim
: The projection map
\pi_{1}\colon X\to S^{2}
onto the first sphere maps
Z^{\prime}\subseteq X
onto
S^{2}
. Proof: Suppose it does not. Then
Z^{\prime}
is contained in
X^{\prime}=(S^{2}\backslash\{x\})\times S_{2}\times\cdots\times S_{2}
for some
x\in S^{2}
. The inclusion of
X^{\prime}
in
X
induces a map

H_{*}(X^{\prime})\to H_{*}(X)
whose range is contained in the subgroup
generated by the classes
[S_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{l}}]
with
j_{1}\neq 1
. This is impossible, since the homology class
[Z^{\prime}]
belongs to the range of this map, as the inclusion
of
Z^{\prime}
in
X
factors through the inclusion of
X^{\prime}
in
X
.

Having established our claim, let us restrict (
8.2
) to the set
Z^{\prime}
. Since the elements
v_{j},w_{j},h_{j}
are simultaneously of diagonal form on
Z^{\prime}
, comparing the top left corners we get that

\|e^{it\kappa}p+(1_{k}-p)-(a_{1}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(2)})\cdots(a_{m}^{(1)},a_{m}^{(2)})\cdot e^{ia_{1}^{(3)}}\cdots e^{ia_{m}^{(3)}}|\|<|e^{i\frac{t_{0}}{k(m+1)}}-1|,

where this relation is now taken in
M_{k}(C(Z^{\prime}))
. Here
a_{j}^{(1)}
and
a_{j}^{(2)}
are unitaries in
M_{k}(C(Z^{\prime}))
, and
a_{j}^{(3)}
is selfadjoint such that
\|a_{j}^{(3)}\|<\frac{t_{0}}{k(m+1)}
for all
j
. This in turn implies that

e^{it\kappa}p+(1_{k}-p)=(a_{1}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(2)})\cdots(a_{m}^{(1)},a_{m}^{(2)})\cdot e^{ia_{1}^{(3)}}\cdots e^{ia_{m}^{(3)}}\cdot e^{ib},

where
b\in M_{k}(C(Z^{\prime}))
is selfadjoint and
\|b\|<\frac{t_{0}}{k(m+1)}
(
[
45
, Proposition 2.4]
). Evaluating at an arbitrary
x\in Z^{\prime}
and comparing the determinants of both sides we get

\mathrm{rank}(p)t\kappa(x)-\mathrm{Tr}(b(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}(a_{j}^{(3)}(x))\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}

for all
x\in Z^{\prime}
. Since
Z^{\prime}
is connected,

\mathrm{rank}(p)t\kappa(x)-\mathrm{Tr}(b(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}(a_{j}^{(3)}(x))

is constant for
x\in Z^{\prime}
. This is impossible for
t\geq t_{0}
. Indeed, on one hand the variation of
\mathrm{rank}(p)t\kappa(x)
on the set
Z^{\prime}
is
2\cdot\mathrm{rank}(p)t\geq 2t_{0}
, as
\kappa
maps
Z^{\prime}
onto
[-1,1]
. On the other hand, the variation of
\mathrm{Tr}(b(x))+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{Tr}(a_{j}^{(3)}(x))
on all
X
is less than

2t_{0}
, as
a_{j}^{(3)}(x)
and
b(x)
are
k\times k
matrices of norm
<\frac{t_{0}}{k(m+1)}
.
∎

8.2.
Simple inductive limit

Let us now describe simple inductive limits. We follow the example from
[
46
, Section 6]
, which in turn is based on Villadsen’s “second type” construction in
[
55
]
.

Theorem 8.3
.

There exists a simple unital AH C*-algebra with a unique tracial state and the following property:
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
of norm
\leq 1
and such that

(1)

e^{ih}
is not contained in the norm closure of the set
(\{(v,w):v,w\in U_{0}(A)\})^{m}
,

(2)

the exponential distance from
e^{iCh}
to the set
(\{(v,w):v,w\in U_{0}(A)\})^{m}
is
\geq(m+1)\pi

for large enough
C>0
.

Proof.

Let
(k_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of natural numbers. We will use this sequence to construct the C*-algebra
A
as an inductive limit. Then, by letting
(k_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
grow sufficiently fast, we will show that
A
has the desired properties.

For each
n=1,\ldots
, let
X_{n}=(S^{2})^{k_{n}}
. Let
P_{n}\in M_{2^{n}}(C(X_{n}))
be the rank one projection
P_{n}=P^{\otimes k_{n}}
,
where
P\in M_{2}(C(S^{2}))
is a projection whose first Chern class is a generator of
H^{2}(S^{2})
. Let us now form the product

Y_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
. Observe that
Y_{n}
is the cartesian product of
(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j})
2-dimensional spheres.

Consider the projection
p_{n}\in M_{\infty}(C(Y_{n}))
defined by

p_{n}(y)=P_{1}(x_{1})^{\oplus l_{1}}\oplus P_{2}(x_{2})^{\oplus l_{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus P_{n}(x_{n})^{\oplus l_{n}},

where
y=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in Y_{n}
. Let us set the numbers
l_{n}
recursively such that
l_{1}=1
and
l_{n+1}=\mathrm{rank}(p_{n})
for
n\geq 1
. (In fact, this yields
l_{n}=2^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
.) Choose for each
n
a point
c_{n}\in Y_{n}
.

Let
\phi_{n}\colon\mathrm{her}(p_{n})\to\mathrm{her}(p_{n+1})
be a homomorphism defined as follows:

\phi_{n}(f)(y,x_{n+1})=\begin{pmatrix}f(y)&0\\
0&f(c_{n})\otimes P_{n+1}(x_{n+1})\end{pmatrix},

for all
(y,x_{n+1})\in Y_{n}\times X_{n+1}
. On the right-hand side of this formula we have used the 2x2 matrix notation for

\mathrm{her}(p_{n+1})=\mathrm{her}(p_{n}\oplus P_{n+1}^{\oplus l_{n+1}})
. Also,

f(c_{n})\otimes P_{n+1}

is regarded as an element in
\mathrm{her}(P_{n+1}^{\oplus l_{n+1}})
via the identifications

\mathrm{her}(P_{n+1}^{\oplus l_{n+1}})\cong M_{l_{n+1}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathrm{her}(P_{n+1})

and
\mathrm{her}(p_{n}(c_{n}))\cong M_{l_{n+1}}(\mathbb{C})
(recall that
p_{n}
has rank
l_{n+1}
).

It is known that choosing the points
c_{n}\in Y_{n}
suitably, one can arrange for the inductive limit C*-algebra
A:=\varinjlim(\mathrm{her}(p_{n}),\phi_{n})
to be simple and have a unique tracial state (see
[
55
]
). The uniqueness of tracial state comes from the fact that if
\sigma
is a tracial state on
\mathrm{her}(p_{m+n})
then
\sigma\circ\phi_{m,m+n}
is an average of
2^{n}
traces out of which
2^{n}-1
do not depend on
\sigma
(coming from point evaluations).

Let us now describe how to recursively define the sequence
(k_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
so that the resulting inductive limit C*-algebra has the desired properties.
Define
k_{1}=1
. Assume that
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
have been chosen. Choose
M_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

[p_{n}]\leq M_{n}[1_{Y_{n}}]
. Now choose
k_{n+1}\geq 5nM_{n}l_{n+1},k_{n}
, and continue this process ad infinitum.

To prove the theorem, it suffices to find for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
a selfadjoint element
a_{m}
in
\overline{[\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}),\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1})]}
of norm
1
such that

(a)

e^{ia_{m}}
is within a distance of at least
\epsilon_{m}>0
from any product of
m
commutators in
U_{0}(\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}))
,

(b)

e^{iC_{m}a_{m}}
is within distance
\geq(m+1)\pi
from any product of
m
commutators in
U_{0}(\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}))
for a large enough
C_{m}
.

Moreover, these properties are not destroyed by moving
a_{m}
along the inductive limit.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that
Y_{m}
is a product of 2-dimensional spheres. Let
\pi_{1}\colon Y_{m}\to S^{2}
be the projection onto the first of the sphere factors in
Y_{m}
. Let
\kappa\colon Y_{m}\to[-1,1]
be defined as in (
8.1
). Let
a_{m}\in\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1})
be given by

a_{m}(y,x_{m+1})=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa(y)p_{m}(y)&0\\
0&-\kappa(y)P_{m+1}^{\oplus l_{m+1}}(x_{m+1})\end{pmatrix},

for all
(y,x_{m+1})\in Y_{m+1}
, Since
a_{m}
has pointwise zero trace, it is in the kernel of every bounded trace. Hence,
a_{m}\in\overline{[\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}),\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1})]}
. Observe also that
\|a_{m}\|=1
.

Let us verify that the conditions are met to apply Theorem
8.2
.
Recall that
M_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that

[p_{m}]\leq M_{m}[1_{Y_{m}}]
.
Let
\widetilde{P}_{m+1}\in M_{\infty}(C(Y_{m+1}))
be defined as the pull back of
P_{m+1}
along the projection onto
X_{m+1}
, i.e.,

(8.4)

\widetilde{P}_{m+1}(y,x_{m+1})=P_{m+1}(x_{m+1}),\quad\hbox{for $(y,x_{m+1})\in Y_{m}\times X_{m+1}$.}

A routine Euler class
computation shows that

e(\widetilde{P}_{m+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{m+1}}\alpha_{j,m+1},

where we have denoted by
\alpha_{j,m+1}\in H^{2}(Y_{m+1})
the cohomology classes coming from projecting onto the
k_{m+1}
2-dimensional spheres in
X_{m+1}=(S^{2})^{k_{m+1}}
. Observe that
e(\widetilde{P}_{m+1})
belongs to the subring generated by elements in
H^{2}(Y_{m+1})
arising from the sphere factors in
X_{m+1}
, and thus omits all the generators coming from the sphere factors in
Y_{m}
. Moreover, from our choice of the sequence
(k_{n})_{n}
,

k_{m+1}\geq 5mM_{m}l_{m+1}
, and so

e(\widetilde{P}_{m+1}^{\oplus l_{m+1}})^{5mM_{m}}=e(\widetilde{P}_{m+1})^{5mM_{m}l_{m+1}}\neq 0.

We can therefore apply Theorem
8.2
with
k=M_{m}
to conclude that the exponential distance from
e^{ia_{m}}
to the set of products of
m
commutators in
U_{0}(\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}))
is at least
\epsilon_{m}=\frac{1}{M_{m}}
. Moreover, also by Theorem
8.2
, the exponential distance from
e^{iC_{m}a_{m}}
, with
C_{m}=M_{m}(m+1)\pi
, to the set of products of
m
commutators in
U_{0}(\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1}))
is at least
(m+1)\pi
.

Next we show that moving
a_{m}
along the inductive limit does not change its properties. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider the image of
a_{m}\in\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1})
in
\mathrm{her}(p_{m+n})
by the connecting homomorphism
\phi_{m+1,m+n}\colon\mathrm{her}(p_{m+1})\to\mathrm{her}(p_{m+n})
. We regard
\mathrm{her}(p_{m+n})
as
\mathrm{her}(p_{m}\oplus q_{m,n})
, with

(8.5)

q_{m,n}(x)=P_{m+1}(x_{m+1})^{\oplus l_{m+1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus P_{m+n}(x_{m+n})^{\oplus l_{m+n}},

for
x=(x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{m+n})\in Y_{n+m}
. Then

\phi_{m+1,m+n}(a_{m})(y,x)=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa(y)p_{m}(y)&\\
&*\end{pmatrix},

for
(y,x)\in Y_{m}\times(X_{m+1}\times\cdots\times X_{m+n})
.
A routine Euler class computation shows that
e(q_{m,n})
belongs to a subring of
H^{*}(Y_{m+n})
generated by cohomology classes in
H^{2}(Y_{m+n})
coming from the sphere factors in the product
X_{m+1}\times\cdots\times X_{m+n}
. Moreover, from our choice of
(k_{n})_{n}
,

e(q_{m,n})^{5mM_{m}}\neq 0
. Hence, applications of Theorem
8.2
show that the exponential distance from

e^{i\phi_{m+n}(a_{m})}
to the products of
m
commutators in
U_{0}(\mathrm{her}(p_{m+n})
is still
\geq\frac{1}{M_{m}}
, and
the distance from
e^{iC_{m}\phi_{m+n}(a+m)}
to the same set is
\geq(m+1)\pi
, where
C_{m}=M_{m}(m+1)\pi
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
D
.

Let
A
be the C*-algebra from Theorem
8.3
.

(i) The simplicity of
DU_{0}(A)/Z(DU_{0}(A))
has already been proven in Theorem
A
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
DU_{0}(A)/Z(DU_{0}(A))
has bounded normal generation.
Since
DU_{0}(A)/Z(DU_{0}(A))
is non-abelian, it follows that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that every element of
DU_{0}(A)/Z(DU_{0}(A))
is a product of
n
commutators.
This in turn implies that every element of
DU_{0}(A)
is a product of
n
commutators times a scalar unitary.
Since the exponential length of any scalar unitary is at most
\pi
, the exponential distance from any element of
DU_{0}(A)
to the set of products of
n
commutators in
U_{0}(A)
is at most
\pi
. Through the density of
DU_{0}(A)
in
SU_{0}(A)

(Theorem
5.3
(i)), this extends to all elements of
SU_{0}(A)
. This is in contradiction with Theorem
8.3
(ii).

(ii) Suppose that
DU_{0}(A)=SU_{0}(A)
. Let

K_{m}=(\{(u,v):u,v\in U_{0}(A)\})^{m}.

Then
SU_{0}(A)=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\overline{K_{m}}
, where the closure of
K_{m}
is taken in the topology of
SU_{0}(A)
. Since
SU_{0}(A)
is complete,

\overline{K_{m}}
has non-empty interior for some
m
, by Baire’s theorem. It follows that
\overline{K_{2m}}
contains a neighborhood of the identity of the form
\{e^{ih}:h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}},\,\|h\|<\epsilon\}
. Hence, for a sufficiently large
n
,
\overline{K_{n}}
contains all
e^{ih}
with
h\in\overline{[A,A]}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}}
and
\|h\|\leq 1
. This contradicts Theorem
8.3
(i).

(iii) This is clearly a consequence of Theorem
8.3
.
∎

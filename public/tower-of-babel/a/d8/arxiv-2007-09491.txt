On Grauert’s examples of complete Kähler metrics
Sahil Gehlawat and Kaushal Verma

Abstract
. Grauert showed that the existence of a complete Kähler metric does not characterize domains of holomorphy by constructing such metrics on the complements of complex analytic sets in a domain of holomorphy. In this note, we study the holomorphic sectional curvatures of such metrics in two prototype cases namely, ℂ n ∖ { 0 } , n ≥ 2 \mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\},n\geq 2 and 𝔹 N ∖ A \mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and A ⊂ 𝔹 N A\subset\mathbb{B}^{N} is a hyperplane of codimension at least two. This is done by computing the Gaussian curvature of its restriction to the leaves of a suitable holomorphic foliation of these two examples. We also examine this metric on the punctured plane ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{\ast} and show that it behaves very differently in this case.

1.
Introduction

In addition to showing that every domain of holomorphy admits a real analytic, complete Kähler metric, Grauert
[
8
]
showed that the converse is false in general by constructing such metrics on the complement of a closed complex analytic set in a given domain of holomorphy. This was done by first explicitly producing such a metric on

(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\},n\geq 1
and then pulling it back to a domain of the form
D\setminus A
(where
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain of holomorphy and
A\subset D
is a complex analytic set) by means of the defining equations for
A
. The possible degeneracies that may occur in pulling it back are taken care of by adding a suitable metric to it.

The primary purpose of this note is to study the holomorphic sectional curvature of this metric on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
where
n\geq 2
. In what follows,
n\geq 2
unless stated otherwise. Furthermore, as an example, we apply Grauert’s construction to the pair
D=\mathbb{B}^{N}
, the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
and
A=\{z_{1}=z_{2}=\ldots=z_{n}=0\}
,
2\leq n\leq N
, to get such a metric on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
, and also obtain information about its holomorphic sectional curvature.

To recall the construction of this metric on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
(in addition to
[
8
]
, see
[
9
]
for instance), note that the real analytic function
u:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
u(t)=(t-1)/(t\log t)
is positive on
(0,\infty)
and satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}tu^{2}(t)\;dt<\infty\;\text{and}\;\int_{0}^{1}u(t)\;dt=\infty.

as a direct calculation shows. For
t\in(0,\infty)
, set
v(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tau u^{2}(\tau)\;d\tau
and
U(t)=\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)/\tau\;d\tau
so that
U^{\prime}(t)=v(t)/t
. Finally, define
H(z)=U(|z|^{2})
and let
g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=\partial^{2}H/\partial z_{\alpha}\partial\overline{z}_{\beta}
for
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
. A computation shows that

g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(z)=U^{\prime\prime}(|z|^{2})\overline{z}_{\alpha}z_{\beta}+U^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\delta_{\alpha\beta}

and hence for a tangent vector
X=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
based at
z\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
,

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(z)X_{\alpha}\overline{X}_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(U^{\prime\prime}(|z|^{2})\overline{z}_{\alpha}z_{\beta}+U^{\prime}(|z|^{2})\delta_{\alpha\beta}\right)X_{\alpha}\overline{X}_{\beta}.

The right side simplifies as

\left(u^{2}(|z|^{2})-\frac{v(|z|^{2})}{|z|^{4}}\right)|\langle X,z\rangle|^{2}+\frac{v(|z|^{2})}{|z|^{2}}|X|^{2}=u^{2}(|z|^{2})|\langle X,z\rangle|^{2}+\frac{v(|z|^{2})}{|z|^{2}}\left(|X|^{2}-\frac{|\langle X,z\rangle|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}\right)

where
\langle X,z\rangle=X_{1}\overline{z}_{1}+X_{2}\overline{z}_{2}+\ldots+X_{n}\overline{z}_{n}
is the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then shows that the prescription

\tilde{g}(z,X)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(z)X_{\alpha}\overline{X}_{\beta}\geq 0

and hence defines a real analytic Kähler pseudometric, with global potential
H
, on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
. By replacing
g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(z)
with
g_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}(z)+\delta_{\alpha\beta}
amounts to working with
g(z,X)=\tilde{g}(z,X)+|X|^{2}
which is clearly positive and hence produces the desired metric. We will refer to
g
as the Grauert metric on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
and record the expression

g(z,X)=\left(1+\frac{v(|z|^{2})}{|z|^{2}}\right)|X|^{2}+\left(u^{2}(|z|^{2})-\frac{v(|z|^{2})}{|z|^{4}}\right)|\langle X,z\rangle|^{2}

for future use. Note that
g(z,X)
is rotation invariant in the sense that
g(z,X)=g(\mathbb{U}z,\mathbb{U}X)
for every unitary rotation
\mathbb{U}
.

By
[
17
]
, recall that the holomorphic sectional curvature of
g
at
z\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
along a tangent direction
X
can be recovered as the supremum of the Gaussian curvatures of all possible nonsingular holomorphic disks through
z
which are tangent to
X
. In other words, let
\phi
be a holomorphic map from a neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathbb{C}
into

(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
with
\phi(0)=z
and
d\phi(0)=X
. Let
K_{\phi^{\ast}g}(0)
be the Gaussian curvature of the pull back metric
\phi^{\ast}g
which is well defined near the origin. Then,
K_{g}(z,X)
, the holomorphic sectional curvature of
g
at
z
along
X
satisfies

K_{g}(z,X)=\sup K_{\phi^{\ast}g}(0)

where the supremum is taken over all such
\phi
. Let
K^{\pm}_{g}(z)
be the supremum and infimum respectively of
K_{g}(z,X)
over all tangent vectors
X
at
z
.

Let
\phi
be such an embedding from a neighbourhood of the origin into
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
with
\phi(0)=z
and
d\phi(0)=X
. For a given unitary rotation
\mathbb{U}
, the map
\mathbb{U}\circ\phi
defines an embedding at
\mathbb{U}\circ\phi(0)=\mathbb{U}z
with
d(\mathbb{U}\circ\phi)(0)=\mathbb{U}X
. Note that

(\mathbb{U}\circ\phi)^{\ast}g=(\phi^{\ast}\circ\mathbb{U}^{\ast})g=\phi^{\ast}g

since
g
is rotation invariant. Therefore,

K_{\phi^{\ast}g}(0)=K_{(\mathbb{U}\circ\phi)^{\ast}g}(0)

which shows that
K_{g}(z,X)=K_{g}(\mathbb{U}z,\mathbb{U}X)
and hence
K^{\pm}_{g}(z)=K^{\pm}_{g}(\mathbb{U}z)
. Since this holds for every
\mathbb{U}
,
K^{\pm}_{g}
are constant on spheres centered at the origin.

As
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
,
n\geq 2
, is not a domain of holomorphy, Shiffman’s theorem
[
14
]
implies that
K^{+}_{g}
cannot be non-positive everywhere. Thus, there must be points arbitrarily close to the origin and holomorphic tangent vectors
X
based at them along which
K_{g}(X)>0
. This was strengthened by Balas
[
1
]
who showed that
every
rotation invariant, complete Kähler metric on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
admits holomorphic tangent vectors, based at points arbitrarily close to the origin, along which it is positively curved. This was done by considering tangent vectors that are orthogonal to the radial direction. However, it turns out that much more can be said for the Grauert metric
g
by computing the curvature of its restriction to the leaves of the holomorphic vector field

(1.1)

X=X(z)=z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+z_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}+\ldots+z_{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n-1}}+\alpha z_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}},\;\;\alpha<0

which has an isolated singularity at the origin. Let
\mathcal{F}^{n}_{X}
be the foliation induced by
X
. For
z\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{z}\subset\mathcal{F}^{n}_{X}
be the leaf passing through
z
. Let
\kappa(z)
be the Gaussian curvature of
g|\mathcal{L}_{z}
, the restriction of
g
to
\mathcal{L}_{z}
, at
z
. By estimating
\kappa
near the origin and noting that
K^{+}_{g}\geq\kappa
provides a lower bound for
K^{+}_{g}
near the origin, and a similar calculation, that is independent of this specific
X
, yields information on
K_{g}(z,X)
and hence
K^{-}_{g}
far away from the origin.

Theorem 1.1
.

For the Grauert metric
g
on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
, where
n\geq 2
,
K^{+}_{g}(z)\rightarrow+\infty
as
|z|\rightarrow 0
. Furthermore, there exists
R>0
such that
K^{-}_{g}(z)<0
for all

|z|\geq R
.

As alluded to earlier, we will now consider a specific example of a complete metric that arises by applying Grauert’s construction to
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
where
A=\{z_{1}=z_{2}=\ldots=z_{n}=0\}
,
2\leq n\leq N
and
N\geq 2
. The Grauert metric
g
on
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
can be pulled back via the natural projection
\pi:\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}

\pi(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})

to get
\pi^{\ast}g
which is a Kähler pseudometric on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
. By adding to this a complete metric on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
say,
\tilde{g}
, gives
\pi^{\ast}g+\tilde{g}
which is complete on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
. Note that the choice of
\tilde{g}
is not unique and as a prototype, we will consider
the Bergman metric

\tilde{g}_{B}=(N+1)\left(\frac{|\langle z,dz\rangle|^{2}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}+\frac{|dz|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)

which is a complete Kähler, constant curvature example. Thus,
g_{B}=\pi^{\ast}g+\tilde{g}_{B}

is an example of a complete Kähler metric on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
and the corresponding functions
K^{\pm}_{g_{B}}
are well defined. Note that
X
, when viewed as a vector field on

\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
, is singular along
A
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{N}_{X}
be the foliation induced by
X
on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
. Fix
w=(0,\ldots,0,w_{n+1},\ldots,w_{N})\in A
, let
r^{2}=1-|w|^{2}
and consider the slice

D_{w}=\{z\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:z_{j}=w_{j},\;n+1\leq j\leq N\}.

Note that
D_{w}\setminus A
can be identified with a punctured ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of radius
r
. Also, for
z\in D_{w}
, the entire leaf
\mathcal{L}_{z}\subset\mathcal{F}^{N}_{X}
passing through
z
is contained in
D_{w}
. Let
\mathcal{K}(z)
be the Gaussian curvature of
g_{B}|\mathcal{L}_{z}
, the restriction of
g_{B}
to
\mathcal{L}_{z}
, at
z
. Again, as
K^{+}_{g_{B}}(z)\geq\mathcal{K}(z)
, it suffices to estimate
\mathcal{K}(z)
near
w
in order to obtain a lower bound for
K^{+}_{g_{B}}(z)
near
w
. In addition,
\mathcal{K}(z)
can be estimated directly near the boundary of
D_{w}\setminus A
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For the metric
g_{B}
on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
,
K^{+}_{g_{B}}(z)\rightarrow+\infty
as
z\rightarrow w
along
D_{w}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{K}(z)\rightarrow-6/(N+1)
as
|z|\rightarrow 1
along
D_{w}
.

The concluding section contains a discussion of this metric on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, i.e., when
n=1
, as well as miscellaneous remarks related to the computations that follow. Other aspects of complete metrics on complements of analytic sets can be found in
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
12
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

We will begin by computing a general expression for the Gaussian curvature of the leaves of a foliation induced by an arbitrary holomorphic vector field
X
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\text{sing}(X)=\{0\}
. For

z\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
, let
Z=Z(T)
be the solution of

\dot{Z}=dZ/dT=X(Z(T)),\;Z(0)=z

where
Z
is defined in a neighbourhood of the origin in the plane. Since
\dot{Z}=X(Z)
, the pull back metric
Z^{\ast}g(T)=h(T)|dT|^{2}
where

(2.1)

h(T)=\left(1+\frac{v(|Z|^{2})}{|Z|^{2}}\right)|X(Z)|^{2}+\left(u^{2}(|Z|^{2})-\frac{v(|Z|^{2})}{|Z|^{4}}\right)|\langle Z,X(Z)\rangle|^{2}.

Its curvature

(2.2)

\kappa(z)=-2(h(T))^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log h(T)\big|_{T=0}=-2(h(0))^{-3}\left(h(0)\;\partial\overline{\partial}h(0)-\partial h(0)\;\overline{\partial}h(0)\right)

which will be simplified by computing expressions for
\partial h,\overline{\partial}h
and
\partial\overline{\partial}h
. For this, abbreviate
u(|Z|^{2}),v(|Z|^{2})
by just
u,v
and likewise for their derivatives with the understanding that they are functions of
|Z|^{2}
. Also, note that

(2.3)

\partial|Z|^{2}=\langle X(Z),Z\rangle,\;\;\overline{\partial}|Z|^{2}=\langle Z,X(Z)\rangle,\;\;\partial X(Z)=DX(Z)(X(Z))\;\text{and}\;\overline{\partial}X(Z)=0.

Repeated use of these identities in simplifying the resulting expressions shows that

\partial h=f_{1}(T)+f_{2}(T)+f_{3}(T)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\overline{\partial}h=\overline{f_{1}(T)}+\overline{f_{2}(T)}+\overline{f_{3}(T)}

where

\displaystyle f_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\left(u^{2}-\frac{v}{|Z|^{4}}\right)\left(2|X(Z)|^{2}\langle X(Z),Z\rangle+\langle DX(Z)(X(Z)),Z\rangle\langle Z,X(Z)\rangle\right),

\displaystyle f_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{v}{|Z|^{2}}\right)\langle DX(Z)(X(Z)),X(Z)\rangle,\;\text{and}

\displaystyle f_{3}(T)

\displaystyle=\left(2uu^{\prime}-\frac{u^{2}}{|Z|^{2}}+\frac{2v}{|Z|^{6}}\right)\langle X(Z),Z\rangle|\langle X(Z),Z\rangle|^{2}.

Differentiating once again

\overline{\partial}f_{1}(T)=\left(2uu^{\prime}-\frac{u^{2}}{|Z|^{2}}+\frac{2v}{|Z|^{6}}\right)A_{1}+\left(u^{2}-\frac{v}{|Z|^{4}}\right)\left(A_{2}+2|X(Z)|^{4}+|\langle DX(Z)(X(Z)),Z\rangle|^{2}\right)

where the
A_{i}
’s are multi-term expressions that can be explicitly written, but their exact form is not important. The relevant point is that
A_{1},A_{2}
both contain either
\langle X(Z),Z\rangle
or its conjugate as a factor. Similarly,

\overline{\partial}f_{2}(T)=\left(u^{2}-\frac{v}{|Z|^{4}}\right)A_{3}+\left(1+\frac{v}{|Z|^{2}}\right)|DX(Z)(X(Z))|^{2}

where
A_{3}
contains
\langle Z,X(Z)\rangle
as factor and finally, it can be seen that every term in
\overline{\partial}f_{3}(T)
also contains either
\langle X(Z),Z\rangle
or its conjugate as a factor. Thus,
\overline{\partial}\partial h=\overline{\partial}f_{1}(T)+\overline{\partial}f_{2}(T)+\overline{\partial}f_{3}(T)
can be expressed as the sum of several terms that can be grouped depending on whether they contain
\langle X(Z),Z\rangle
(or its conjugate) or not.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:\langle z,X(z)\rangle=0\}
. This is precisely the set where the vector field
X
is orthogonal to the radial direction. Note that
\mathcal{S}
may be empty as the example of
X=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
shows. But assuming that
\mathcal{S}\not=\emptyset
, note that for
Z(0)=z\in\mathcal{S}
,

h(0)=\left(1+\frac{v}{|z|^{2}}\right)|X(z)|^{2},\;f_{2}(0)=\left(1+\frac{v}{|z|^{2}}\right)\langle DX(z)(X(z)),X(z)\rangle

and
f_{1}(0)=f_{3}(0)=0
. Hence

\partial h(0)=\left(1+\frac{v}{|z|^{2}}\right)\langle DX(z)(X(z)),X(z)\rangle=\overline{\overline{\partial}h(0)}

Further, on
\mathcal{S}
, all the
A_{i}
’s that occur in the expressions for
\overline{\partial}f_{i}
vanish and hence

\overline{\partial}f_{1}(0)=\left(u^{2}-\frac{v}{|z|^{4}}\right)\left(2|X(z)|^{4}+|\langle DX(z)(X(z)),z\rangle|^{2}\right),\;\;\overline{\partial}f_{2}(0)=\left(1+\frac{v}{|z|^{2}}\right)|DX(z)(X(z))|^{2}

and
\overline{\partial}f_{3}(0)=0
, and thus

\overline{\partial}\partial h(0)=\left(u^{2}-\frac{v}{|z|^{4}}\right)\left(2|X(z)|^{4}+|\langle DX(z)(X(z)),z\rangle|^{2}\right)+\left(1+\frac{v}{|z|^{2}}\right)|DX(z)(X(z))|^{2}.

Putting all this together in (2.2) and writing
|V\wedge W|^{2}=|V|^{2}|W|^{2}-|\langle V,W\rangle|^{2}
for vectors
V,W\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
leads to
\kappa(z)=N(z)/D(z)
where
N(z)=-2(A(z)+B(z))
with

(2.4)

\displaystyle A(z)

\displaystyle=(|z|^{4}u^{2}-v)(2|X(z)|^{6}+|\langle DX(z)(X(z)),z\rangle|^{2}|X(z)|^{2}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle B(z)

\displaystyle=(|z|^{2}+v)|z|^{2}|X(z)\wedge DX(z)(X(z))|^{2}

and

(2.6)

D(z)=(|z|^{2}+v)^{2}|X(z)|^{6}

after some simplification.

Note that
B(z)\geq 0
. Further, the sign of
A(z)
, and hence
\kappa(z)
, is determined by looking at

(|z|^{4}u^{2}-v)
. Setting
t=|z|^{2}
, the derivative of the function
\eta:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\eta(t)=t^{2}u^{2}(t)-v(t)

satisfies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\eta^{\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(tu^{2}(t)+2t^{2}u(t)u^{\prime}(t)\right)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{(t-1)^{2}\log t}{t(\log t)^{3}}\left(1+2\left(\frac{1}{t-1}-\frac{1}{\log t}\right)\right)\right)=+\infty.

Thus,
\eta(t)\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and hence there exists
R>0
such that
A(z)>0
, and consequently
\kappa(z)<0
, for

z\in\mathcal{S}\cap\{|z|^{2}>R^{2}\}
.

2.1.
Behaviour near infinity:

Two observations can be made about the calculations so far. One, the critical value of
R
beyond which
\kappa<0
does
not
depend on the vector field
X
as along as
z\in\mathcal{S}
– it only depends on the functions
u,v
that define the Grauert metric
g
, and second, all these calculations are purely local, i.e., what is needed is a non-singular holomorphic vector field that is defined in some neighbourhood contained in
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\ast}
.

Pick a
z_{0}
with
|z_{0}|>R
and let
V
be a holomorphic tangent vector at
z_{0}
. By Wu
[
17
]
, there are local holomorphic coordinates
(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})
defined in a neighbourhood
\Omega
of
z_{0}=0
in which
V=\partial/\partial w_{n}|_{0}
and the Gaussian curvature of the restriction of the Grauert metric
g
to the holomorphic disc
\{w_{1}=w_{2}=\ldots=w_{n-1}=0\}
equals
K_{g}(z_{0},V)
. The assignment
p\mapsto\partial/\partial w_{n}|_{p}
defines a non-vanishing holomorphic vector field
X(p)
in
\Omega
and the leaves of the induced foliation are given by
\{w_{1}=c_{1},w_{2}=c_{2},\ldots,w_{n-1}=c_{n-1}\}
for small values of
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that
X(0)=V
. Now suppose, in addition, that
\langle z_{0},V\rangle=0
. The calculations above show that
K_{g}(z_{0},V)=\kappa(z_{0})<0
and hence
K^{-}_{g}(z_{0})\leq K_{g}(z_{0},V)<0
and by rotation invariance, it follows that
K^{-}_{g}(z)<0
for all
|z|=|z_{0}|
.

2.2.
Behaviour near the origin:

To estimate
K^{+}_{g}
near
z=0
, the vector field
X
in (1.1) will be used. Write
X(z)=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,\alpha z_{n})
where
\alpha<0
and compute
DX(z)(X(z))=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,\alpha^{2}z_{n})
. For
n\geq 3
,

\mathcal{S}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:\langle z,X(z)\rangle=|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\ldots|z_{n-1}|^{2}+\alpha|z_{n}|^{2}=0\}

is a smooth quadric whose Levi form has exactly one zero eigenvalue and
(n-2)
non-zero eigenvalues everywhere. When
n=2
,

\mathcal{S}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}:\langle z,X(z)\rangle=|z_{1}|^{2}+\alpha|z_{2}|^{2}=0\}

and is smooth Levi flat, but in either case, the origin
0\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{S}\cup\{0\}
is a real algebraic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at the origin. On

\mathcal{S}
, the various terms in (2.4) - (2.6) simplify as

|z|^{2}=(1-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2},\;\;|X(z)|^{2}=(\alpha^{2}-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2},\;\;|DX(z)(X(z)|^{2}=(\alpha^{4}-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2}

and

\langle DX(z)(X(z)),z\rangle=(\alpha^{2}-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2},\;\;\langle DX(z)(X(z)),X(z)\rangle=(\alpha^{3}-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2}.

Further,

\displaystyle|z|^{2}|X(z)\wedge DX(z)(X(z))|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{2}\left((\alpha^{2}-\alpha)(\alpha^{4}-\alpha)|z_{n}|^{4}-(\alpha^{3}-\alpha)^{2}|z_{n}|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-\alpha)^{3}|z_{n}|^{6}.

Therefore,
A(z),B(z)
and
D(z)
can be determined and hence

(2.7)

\kappa(z)=-\frac{2\left(3|z|^{4}u^{2}(|z|^{2})-2v(|z|^{2})+|z|^{2}\right)}{\left(|z|^{2}+v(|z|^{2})\right)^{2}}

for
z\in\mathcal{S}
. The interesting thing here is that this expression is independent of
\alpha
. To analyze
\kappa(z)
, write
|z|^{2}=t
and consider
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

f(t)=-\frac{2(3t^{2}u^{2}(t)-2v(t)+t)}{(t+v(t))^{2}}

which is clearly real analytic on
(0,\infty)
.

Claim:

f(1)<0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)=+\infty
.

Recall that
u(t)=(t-1)/(t\log t)
and
v(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tau u^{2}(\tau)\;d\tau
. To show that
f(1)<0
, it will suffice to prove that
v(1)<2
since
u(1)=1
. Change variables by writing

\tau=e^{-x}
to get

v(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\tau(\log\tau)^{2}}\;d\tau=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-x})^{2}}{x^{2}}\;dx=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-e^{-x})^{2}}{x^{2}}\;dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-x})^{2}}{x^{2}}\;dx

and note that
(1-e^{-x})^{2}/x^{2}\leq 1
on
[0,1)
and
(1-e^{-x})^{2}<1
on
[1,\infty)
which respectively imply that the first term is at most
1
and the second is strictly less than
1
. Thus,
v(1)<2
.

For the second claim, observe that both
u(t),tu^{2}(t)\rightarrow+\infty
and
t^{2}u^{2}(t),v(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
. By substituting for
u,u^{\prime},v
and
v^{\prime}
, and repeatedly using L’Hôpital’s rule,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)=-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{2\left(4tu^{2}(t)+6t^{2}u(t)u^{\prime}(t)+1\right)}{2t+2tv(t)u^{2}(t)+2v(t)+2t^{2}u^{2}(t)}=+\infty.

Therefore, there exists a
c\in(0,1)
such that
f(c)=0
. By the real analyticity of
f
and the fact that
f(t)\rightarrow+\infty
near
t=0
, there can be only finitely many such
c
. Let
a>0
be the minimum of the zeros of
f
in
(0,1)
. Hence, for
z\in\mathcal{S}\cap\{|z|^{2}<a\}
,
\kappa(z)>0
and this implies that
K^{+}_{g}(z)>0
. By rotation invariance,

K^{+}_{g}>0
everywhere in the punctured ball
\{|z|^{2}<a\}\setminus\{0\}
. In fact,
K^{+}_{g}(z)\rightarrow+\infty
as
|z|\rightarrow 0
. The proof of Theorem 1.1 is complete.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Recall that the metric
g_{B}=\pi^{\ast}g+\tilde{g}_{B}
, where

\pi^{\ast}g(z,X)=\left(1+\frac{v(|\pi(z)|^{2})}{|\pi(z)|^{2}}\right)|\pi(X)|^{2}+\left(u^{2}(|\pi(z)|^{2})-\frac{v(|\pi(z)|^{2})}{|\pi(z)|^{4}}\right)|\langle\pi(X),\pi(z)\rangle|^{2}

for
(z,X)\in\mathbb{(}B^{N}\setminus A)\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\tilde{g}_{B}
is the Bergman metric on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. For
z\in D_{w}
, let
Z=Z(T)
be the solution of

\dot{Z}=dZ/dT=X(Z(T)),\;\;Z(0)=z\in D_{w}.

Since
X
as in (1.1) is being viewed as a vector field on
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
, it follows that
Z_{j}(T)\equiv w_{j}
for
n+1\leq j\leq N
. Also,

|Z_{1}(T)|^{2}+|Z_{2}(T)|^{2}+\ldots+|Z_{n}(T)|^{2}<1-|w|^{2}=r^{2}.

Furthermore,
\pi:D_{w}\setminus A\rightarrow B^{n}(0,r)\setminus\{0\}
is a biholomorphism and let

\tilde{Z}(T)=\pi(Z(T))=(Z_{1}(T),Z_{2}(T),\ldots,Z_{n}(T))\in\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)\setminus\{0\}

and set
\tilde{z}=\pi(z)
. For
\tilde{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)
, define

\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})=z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+z_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}+\ldots+z_{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n-1}}+\alpha z_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}},\;\;\alpha<0

and denote the corresponding foliation by
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{X}}
. Then
\text{sing}(\tilde{X})=\{0\}=\pi(w)
and note that
\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})=\pi\circ X\circ\pi^{-1}(\tilde{z})
and
|X(Z)|^{2}=|\tilde{X}(\tilde{Z})|^{2}
. Also, note that
|Z(T)|^{2}=|\tilde{Z}(T)|^{2}+|w|^{2}
which gives
1-|Z(T)|^{2}=r^{2}-|\tilde{Z}(T)|^{2}
and
\langle Z(T),X(Z(T))\rangle=\langle\tilde{Z}(T),\tilde{X}(\tilde{Z}(T))\rangle
.

With
\mathcal{S}
as before and
w\in A
, let

\mathcal{S}_{w}=\mathcal{S}\cap D_{w}=\{z\in D_{w}\setminus A:|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\ldots+\alpha|z_{n}|^{2}=0\}

which describes the locus along which the radial vector
z
(along the slice
D_{w}
) is orthogonal to
\tilde{X}
, i.e.,
\langle\tilde{z},\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})\rangle=\langle z,X(z)\rangle=0
. As before, for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{w}
,

|\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})|^{2}|\langle D\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})(\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})),\tilde{z}\rangle^{2}=|\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})|^{6}=|\tilde{z}|^{2}|D\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})(\tilde{X}(\tilde{z}))\wedge\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})|^{2}.

Using the above observations, writing
\tilde{Z}=\tilde{Z}(T)
and computing as before,
Z^{\ast}g_{B}(T)=h_{B}(T)|dT|^{2}
where

h_{B}(T)=\left(1+\frac{v(|\tilde{Z}|^{2})}{|\tilde{Z}|^{2}}+\frac{N+1}{r^{2}-|\tilde{Z}|^{2}}\right)|\tilde{X}(\tilde{Z})|^{2}+\left(u^{2}(|\tilde{Z}|^{2})-\frac{v(|\tilde{Z}|^{2})}{|\tilde{Z}|^{4}}+\frac{N+1}{(r^{2}-|\tilde{Z}|^{2})^{2}}\right)|\langle\tilde{Z},\tilde{X}(\tilde{Z})\rangle|^{2}.

The calculations leading to
\mathcal{K}(z)
, the curvature of
Z^{\ast}g_{B}(T)
, are similar in spirit to those in Theorem 1.1. Indeed, what is required are expressions for
h_{B}(0),\partial h_{B}(0),\overline{\partial}h_{B}(0)
and
\partial\overline{\partial}h_{B}(0)
when
Z(0)=z\in\mathcal{S}_{w}
. Of these, the easiest to write down is

(3.1)

h_{B}(0)=\left(1+\frac{v}{|\tilde{z}|^{2}}+\frac{N+1}{r^{2}-|\tilde{z}|^{2}}\right)|\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})|^{2}

where
u,v
are understood to be functions of
|\tilde{z}|^{2}
. For the others, several pages of messy calculations along similar lines as in Theorem 1.1 yield rather unwieldy expressions for
\partial h_{B},\overline{\partial}h_{B}
and
\partial\overline{\partial}h_{B}
which simplify considerably when
Z(0)=z\in\mathcal{S}_{w}
to give:

(3.2)

h_{B}(0)\;\partial\overline{\partial}h_{B}(0)-\partial h_{B}(0)\;\overline{\partial}h_{B}(0)=I(\tilde{z})\cdot J(\tilde{z})

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle I(\tilde{z})

\displaystyle=\left(|\tilde{z}|^{2}+v+\frac{(N+1)|\tilde{z}|^{2}}{(r^{2}-|\tilde{z}|^{2})}\right)\frac{|\tilde{X}(\tilde{z})|^{6}}{|\tilde{z}|^{6}},\;\text{and}

(3.4)

\displaystyle J(\tilde{z})

\displaystyle=\left(3|\tilde{z}|^{4}u^{2}-2v+|\tilde{z}|^{2}+\frac{(N+1)|\tilde{z}|^{2}(r^{2}+2|\tilde{z}|^{2})}{(r^{2}-|\tilde{z}|^{2})^{2}}\right).

Finally, by combining (3.1) – (3.4)

\mathcal{K}(z)=-P(\tilde{z})/Q(\tilde{z})

where

\displaystyle P(\tilde{z})

\displaystyle=2\left(3|\tilde{z}|^{4}u^{2}-2v+|\tilde{z}|^{2}+\frac{(N+1)|\tilde{z}|^{2}(r^{2}+2|\tilde{z}|^{2})}{(r^{2}-|\tilde{z}|^{2})^{2}}\right),\;\text{and}

\displaystyle Q(\tilde{z})

\displaystyle=\left(|\tilde{z}|^{2}+v+\frac{(N+1)|\tilde{z}|^{2}}{(r^{2}-|\tilde{z}|^{2})}\right)^{2}.

To analyze
\mathcal{K}(z)
, write
|\tilde{z}|^{2}=t
and define
f:(0,r^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

f(t)=-\frac{2\left(3t^{2}u^{2}(t)-2v(t)+t+\frac{(N+1)t(r^{2}+2t)}{(r^{2}-t)^{2}}\right)}{\left(t+v(t)+\frac{(N+1)t}{(r^{2}-t)}\right)^{2}}

which is real analytic on
(0,r^{2})
. Theorem 1.2 is a consequence of the following:

Claim:

f(r^{2}/2)<0
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow r^{2}}f(t)\rightarrow-6/(N+1)
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)\rightarrow+\infty
.

Note that the numerator in
f(r^{2}/2)
is

-2\left(3(r^{2}/2)^{2}u^{2}(r^{2}/2)-2v^{2}(r^{2}/2)+r^{2}/2+4(N+1)\right)

and this will be negative if it can be shown that
v(r^{2}/2)<2(N+1)
. But since
r^{2}/2<1/2
,
v(1)<2
and
v
is an increasing function on
(0,\infty)
, it follows that
v(r^{2}/2)<v(1)<2<2(N+1)
.

To determine the behaviour of
f
near the origin, note that both the numerator and denominator in
f
approach zero as
t\rightarrow 0
and repeated use of L’Hôpital’s rule shows that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)=+\infty
. Finally, note that near
t=r^{2}
, both
(3t^{2}u^{2}-2v+t)
and
(t+v)
are bounded while the remaining terms in
f
approach
+\infty
. Simplifying them gives

\lim_{t\rightarrow r^{2}}f(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow r^{2}}-2(r^{2}+2t)/(t(N+1))=-6/(N+1).

When
n=N
,
A=\{0\}
and
\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus A
is the punctured ball with the metric
g_{B}
. In this case, there is no need to work with slices for then
X
as in (1.1) has an isolated singularity at the origin. All computations above remain valid in this case as well.

4.
Concluding Remarks

(i) When
n=1
, note that

(4.1)

g(z,X)=\left(1+|z|^{2}u^{2}(|z|^{2})\right)|X|^{2}

as a bit of simplification shows. The curvature properties of this metric on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
are very likely to be considered as part of folklore knowledge, but our inability to find any explicit reference only piqued our curiosity and made us include the following observations.

The conformal density
h(z)=\left(1+|z|^{2}u^{2}(|z|^{2})\right)\rightarrow+\infty
as
|z|\rightarrow 0
and a calculation shows that
the curvature of
g
is
\kappa(z)=f(t),t=|z|^{2}
, where
f(t)=-P(t)/Q(t)
with

P(t)=2\left(u^{2}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime}+2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}+6tuu^{\prime}+2t^{2}u^{3}u^{\prime}-2t^{3}(uu^{\prime})^{2}\right)

and
Q(t)=(1+tu^{2})^{3}
.

Claim:

f\leq 0
on
(0,\infty)
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)=-4
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}f(t)=0
.

To compute the limits of
f
as
t\rightarrow 0
and
t\rightarrow\infty
, write
P(t)=2(M_{1}(t)+M_{2}(t))
where

M_{1}(t)=2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}+2t^{2}u^{3}u^{\prime}-2t^{3}u^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}\;\text{and}\;M_{2}(t)=u^{2}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime}+6tuu^{\prime}.

which upon substituting for
u,u^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}
become

M_{1}(t)=2t^{3}(t-1)^{2}\tilde{M}_{1}(t)/(t\log t)^{6},\;\text{and}\;M_{2}(t)=\tilde{M}_{2}(t)/t^{2}(\log t)^{4}

where

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left((t-1)^{2}-t(\log t)^{2}\right),\;\text{and}

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=(t-1)^{2}(\log t)^{2}+2(\log t)^{2}+6(t-1)^{2}-8(t-1)\log t+2(t-1)(\log t)^{2}-4(t-1)^{2}\log t

It can now be seen that
M_{2}(t)/(tu^{2})^{3}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
and
M_{1}(t)/(tu^{2})^{3}\rightarrow 2
and hence
f(t)\rightarrow-4
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

Further, both
\tilde{M}_{1}
and
\tilde{M}_{2}
approach
+\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and hence
M_{1},M_{2}>0
for large
t
. Since
u>0,u^{\prime}\leq 0
and
u^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
on
(0,\infty)
, each of the terms
6tuu^{\prime},2t^{2}u^{3}u^{\prime}
and
-2t^{3}(uu^{\prime})^{2}
are non-positive, and hence for large
t
,

0\leq M_{2}(t)=u^{2}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime}+6tuu^{\prime}\leq u^{2}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime}

and

0\leq M_{1}(t)=2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}+2t^{2}u^{3}u^{\prime}-2t^{3}(uu^{\prime})^{2}\leq 2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}.

Both
(u^{2}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime})/(tu^{2})^{3}
and
2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}/(tu^{2})^{3}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
and thus,
f\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Finally, the assertion that
f\leq 0
on
(0,\infty)
is equivalent to the subharmonicity of
\log h
on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
. For this, it suffices to show that
\partial\overline{\partial}\log(|z|^{2}u^{2}(|z|^{2})=\partial\overline{\partial}\log u^{2}(|z|^{2})\geq 0
on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
. Now compute

\displaystyle\partial\overline{\partial}\log u(|z^{2}|)

\displaystyle=u^{-2}\left(uu^{\prime}+|z|^{2}\left(uu^{\prime\prime}-(u^{\prime})^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=t^{-3}(\log t)^{-4}\left((t-1)^{2}-t(\log t)^{2}\right).

Ignoring the positive multiplicative factor
t^{-3}
, the sign of
\partial\overline{\partial}\log u(|z^{2}|)
is determined by

(\log t)^{-4}\left((t-1)^{2}-t(\log t)^{2}\right).

Writing
t=e^{x}
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}
now, reduces this to

x^{-4}(e^{x}-1)^{2}-x^{-2}e^{x}

whose value near
x=0
is positive since its limit at
x=0
is
1/12
. Away from
x=0
, the positivity of this expression is equivalent to the assertion that
\sinh^{2}(x/2)\geq(x/2)^{2}
which is evidently true.

Thus, the Grauert metric on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
is an example of a non-positively curved conformal metric whose limiting behaviour is described above. Given that
\kappa\rightarrow-4
near
z=0
, the fact that
\kappa\rightarrow 0
for large
|z|
is not surprising and in fact, is expected. Indeed, if
\kappa
were to approach a negative constant as
|z|\rightarrow\infty
, then
g
would be an example of a metric on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
that is non-positively curved everywhere and, in addition, satisfies
\kappa\leq-a<0
outside a compact set in
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
. This would contradict a version of the Schwarz lemma – see Troyanov
[
15
]
.

(ii) When
n\geq 2

|z|

\kappa\rightarrow+\infty
along
\mathcal{S}
near the origin. All these considerations are related to the paradigm of identifying Hermitian metrics which are negatively curved when restricted to the leaves of a given foliation – for example, see

,

]
,

]
,

]
,

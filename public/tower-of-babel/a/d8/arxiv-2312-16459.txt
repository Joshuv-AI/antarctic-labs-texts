1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be the closed unit disk. A function
B
on
\mathbb{D}
is said a finite Blaschke product with order
n
if it has the following form

B(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_{j}}{1-\bar{a_{j}}z},\ z\in\mathbb{D},

for some
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and some
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
be the classical Bergman space on
\mathbb{D}
and denote
M_{h}
for the multiplication operator on
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
by a function
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
. A previously open question proposed by R. G. Douglas (Question 6 in
[
6
]
) is whether the operator
M_{B}
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}M_{z}
on
\oplus_{1}^{n}\mathrm{L}^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
, where
n
is the order of the finite Blaschke product
B
?

The question was answered in the affirmative (see
[
17
]
or
[
9
]
).
Later, the question was also answered in the affirmative on many other analytic function Hilbert spaces, such as weighted Bergman spaces
\mathrm{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
(see
[
18
]
), Sobolev disk algebra
\mathrm{R}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
20
]
or
[
14
]
), and Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
(see
[
19
]
). Recently in
[
11
]
, Hou and Jiang proved that the question still holds on the weighted Hardy space of polynomial growth, which covers the weighted Bergman space, the weighted Dirichlet space, and many weighted Hardy spaces defined without measures.

Douglas’s question originated in the study of reducing subspaces of analytic multiplication operators on Bergman space. Before the question was proposed, there had been many studies of the reducing subspaces of the multiplication operator by a finite Blaschke product (see
[
21
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
10
]
). An application of Douglas’s question, together with the technology of strongly irreducible operator and
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group, is the similarity of analytic Toeplitz operators (e.g. Theorem 1.1 in
[
18
]
), which is analogous to the result on Hardy space (see
[
3
]
).

Note that
M_{B}
is also equal to the analytic function calculus
B(M_{z})
, then we can describe Douglas’s question in the following general form: let
T
be a certain bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
H
and suppose
\sigma(T)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then for any finite Blaschke product
B
, is the operator
B(T)
similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}T
on
\oplus_{1}^{n}H
, where
n
is the order of
B
?

Obviously, an operator
T
satisfies Douglas’s question if and only if so does its adjoint
T^{*}
. Since the adjoints
M_{z}^{*}
of the multiplication operators
M_{z}
by
z
on many analytic function Hilbert spaces are in Cowen-Douglas class
B_{1}(\mathbb{D})
proposed in
[
5
]
, the works mentioned earlier correspond to Douglas’s question in the case that
T
is in
B_{1}(\mathbb{D})
. In that way, how about Douglas’s question in the case that
T
is in Cowen-Douglas class
B_{n}(\mathbb{D})(n>1)
?

For a long time, there has been a lack of sufficient understanding of Cowen-Douglas class
B_{n}(\Omega)
for the higher rank case. A new but important subclass
FB_{n}(\Omega)
has been introduced by G. Misra in
[
13
]
.
All irreducible homogeneous operators in
B_{n}(\mathbb{D})
are in
FB_{n}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
13
]
), and the class
FB_{n}(\Omega)
(or even its subclass
CFB_{n}(\Omega)
) is norm dense in
B_{n}(\Omega)
(see
[
16
]
). G. Misra etc. proved the operator in
FB_{n}(\Omega)
possesses a flag structure. It is also proved that the flag structure is rigid, that is, the unitary equivalence class of the operator and the flag structure determine each other (see
[
13
]
).

In this paper, we investigate a family of operators in class
FB_{2}(\mathbb{D})
and give a negative answer to Douglas’s question.

Denote by
\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
the set of all analytical functions on
\mathbb{D}
and denote by
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
the set of all analytical functions on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be a weighted Hardy space with weight sequence
\alpha
on
\mathbb{D}
and denote
M_{\alpha,h}
for the multiplication operator by
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be another weighted Hardy space with weight sequence
\beta
on
\mathbb{D}
and denote
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}
for the multiplication operator by
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
from
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
to
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
. These concepts will be introduced in detail later.

The following theorem is the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant weighted Hardy spaces with property A for some integer
n_{0}\geq 2
. Suppose the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy

\sup_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta(k)}{\alpha(k)}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k)}{k\beta(k)}=0.

Denote
T
be the operator

\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}^{*}&M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}\\
0&M_{\beta,z}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}

on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
, where
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
.

If for any finite Blaschke product
B
,
B(T)
is similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}T
, where
n
is the order of
B
, then
h=0
.

In other words, under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
, for any non-zero function
h
in
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
, the operator

\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}^{*}&M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}\\
0&M_{\beta,z}^{*}\end{pmatrix}

doesn’t satisfy Douglas’s question.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Weighted Hardy space

In the present article, we denote the power series coefficients of an analytical function
f
as
(\hat{f}(k))_{k=0}^{\infty}
, i.e.
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}
.

2.1.1.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha(k))_{k=0}^{\infty}
be a positive sequence, then the weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
with weight sequence
\alpha
on
\mathbb{D}
is defined as

\left\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})\colon\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\alpha(k)^{2}<\infty\right\}.

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
has an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
defined as

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)\overline{\hat{g}(k)}\alpha(k)^{2},

where
f,g\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
.
In the present article, we always assume the weight sequence
\alpha
satisfying

(2.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k+1)}{\alpha(k)}=1.

In this case,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is a Hilbert space and contains
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. What’s more, if a complex sequence
a=(a(k))_{k=0}^{\infty}
satisfies
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a(k)|^{2}\alpha(k)^{2}<\infty
and a function
f
is defined as
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a(k)z^{k}
, then
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
and hence
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
.

The norm of
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is
\|f\|_{\alpha}=(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\alpha(k)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let
e_{k}(z)=z^{k}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
\{e_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms an orthogonal basis of
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
(thus,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is separable). In addition,
\|e_{k}\|_{\alpha}=\alpha(k)
and
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)e_{k}
in the sense of norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
.

It can be checked that for each
\omega\in\mathbb{D}
, the linear function
\delta_{\omega}\colon f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to f(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}
is continuous. Then by Riesz representation Theorem,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
has a reproducing kernel
\{k_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\mathbb{D}}
, i.e. for each
\omega\in\mathbb{D}
,
k_{\omega}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\langle f,k_{\omega}\rangle_{\alpha}=f(\omega)
whenever
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
.

2.1.2.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be a weighted Hardy space. The multiplier algebra
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
of
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is defined as the set of all
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that
hf\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
for any
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
.
For each
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
, one can define a multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,h}\colon f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to hf\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
, which is bounded by closed graph Theorem. Sometimes
M_{\alpha,h}
is also abbreviated as
M_{h}
. As well known,
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\cap\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is the set of all bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. The Mapping
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})\to M_{\alpha,h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
is an algebraic monomorphism with
1\to I
, and for each
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
,
M_{\alpha,h}
is invertible if and only if
\frac{1}{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
.

As well known, the assumption (
2.1
) guarantees that the multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,z}
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\|M_{\alpha,z}\|=\sup_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha(k+1)}{\alpha(k)}
. Denote
\{M_{\alpha,z}\}^{\prime}
for the commutant algebra of
M_{\alpha,z}
, i.e. the set of all
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
such that
XM_{\alpha,z}=M_{\alpha,z}X
.
Then it can be verified that
X
is in
\{M_{\alpha,z}\}^{\prime}
if and only if
X
is equal to
M_{\alpha,h}
for some
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
.

Furthermore, the assumption (
2.1
) also guarantees that
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
(see Lemma 3.4 in
[
7
]
or see
[
11
]
). Then it can be verified that the spectrum
\sigma(M_{\alpha,z})
of
M_{\alpha,z}
is contained in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and for any
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
, the analytic function calculus
h(M_{\alpha,z})
is
M_{\alpha,h}
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. The multiplier
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
from
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
to
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
is defined as the set of all
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that
hf\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
for any
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
.
For each
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, one can define a multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}\colon f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to hf\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
, which is bounded by closed graph Theorem. Sometimes
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is also abbreviated as
M_{h}
. Obviously,
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
.

In addition, it can also be verified that
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
satisfies
XM_{\alpha,z}=M_{\beta,z}X
if and only if
X
is equal to
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}
for some
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
.

Remark 2.1
.

A simple example of weighted Hardy space involved in the present article is the Hilbert space
\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}
is defined as the weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\lambda}}
with weight sequence
\alpha_{\lambda}(k)=(k+1)^{\lambda}
,
k=0,1,\dots
.
This type of space contains many classical analytic function spaces on
\mathbb{D}
. For example, the classical Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(
\lambda=0
), the classical Bergman space
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
(
\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}
) and the classical Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
(
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
).

Remark 2.2
.

A complicated example of weighted Hardy space involved in the present article is the weighted Hardy space of polynomial growth (which has been studied in
[
11
]
recently). A weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
(usually assume
\alpha(0)=1
) is called of polynomial growth if the weight sequence
\alpha
satisfies

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(k+1)|\frac{\alpha(k)}{\alpha(k-1)}-1|<\infty.

The weighted Hardy spaces of polynomial growth cover the weighted Bergman space, the weighted Dirichlet space, and many weighted Hardy spaces defined without measures.

2.2.
Cowen-Douglas operators

Recall some basic concepts of the Cowen-Doug-las operator class
B_{n}(\Omega)
, which was introduced in
[
5
]
, and the subclass
FB_{2}(\Omega)
of
B_{2}(\Omega)
, which was introduced in
[
13
]
.

Let
H
be a complex separable Hilbert space and
\mathcal{L}(H)
denote the collection of bounded linear operators on
H
. For
\Omega
a connected open subset of
\mathbb{C}
and
n
a positive integer, let
B_{n}(\Omega)
denote the operators
T
in
\mathcal{L}(H)
which satisfy:

(a)
\Omega\subseteq\sigma(T)=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{C}\colon T-\omega\ \text{not invertible}\right\}
;

(b)
\mathrm{Ran\,}(T-\omega)=H
for
\omega\in\Omega
;

(c)
\vee_{\omega\in\Omega}\,\mathrm{Ker\,}(T-\omega)=H
; and

(d)
\dim\mathrm{Ker\,}(T-\omega)=n
for
\omega\in\Omega
.

As well known, the adjoint
M_{\alpha,z}^{*}
of the multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,z}
acting on the weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is in class
B_{1}(\mathbb{D})
.

The operator class
FB_{2}(\Omega)
is the set of all bounded linear operators
T
of the form

\begin{pmatrix}T_{0}&S\\
0&T_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
are in class
B_{1}(\Omega)
and the operator
S
is a non-zero intertwiner between them, i.e.
T_{0}S=ST_{1}
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. Throughout this paper, we will denote
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
as the operator

\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}^{*}&M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}\\
0&M_{\beta,z}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}

on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
for a function
h
in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
.
One can see that
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is in class
FB_{2}(\mathbb{D})
whenever
h
is a non-zero function.

3.
Two propositions

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. Let
T=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
, where
h
is a non-zero function in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. To investigate whether for any finite Blaschke product
B
,
B(T)
is similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}T
, where
n
is the order of
B
, we will treat
B
in two cases: the case of
B(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar{a}z}
and the case of
B(z)=z^{n}
. In
4
, we deal with the first case and the main point is Proposition
4.3
. In
5
, we deal with the second case and the main points are Proposition
5.6
and Proposition
5.10
. Before that, in
3
, we will first prove the following two propositions: Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.4
.

In this paper, we write
T\sim\tilde{T}
for two bounded linear operators
T
and
\tilde{T}
if
T
is similar to
\tilde{T}
(i.e. there exists an invertible operator
X
such that
TX=X\tilde{T}
).

Let
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
be two bounded linear operators on Hilbert spaces
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
respectively. Denote
\sigma_{T_{0},T_{1}}(X):=T_{0}X-XT_{1}
for any
X\in\mathcal{L}(H_{1},H_{0})
. Then a linear operator
\sigma_{T_{0},T_{1}}\colon\mathcal{L}(H_{1},H_{0})\to\mathcal{L}(H_{1},H_{0})
can be defined. Let
\sigma_{T}
be the operator
\sigma_{T,T}
.

From Lemma 2.18 in
[
13
]
and Theorem 2.19 in
[
13
]
, a useful conclusion can be obtained that for any
T
in class
B_{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathrm{Ker\,}\sigma_{T}\cap\mathrm{Ran\,}\sigma_{T}=\left\{0\right\}
. But we need to slightly generalize this.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. If the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy condition

(3.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k)}{k\beta(k)}=0,

then
\mathrm{Ker\,}\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}\cap\mathrm{Ran\,}\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}=\left\{0\right\}
.

Proof.

Clearly, we just need to explain that for any
Y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, the equations
YM_{\alpha,z}=M_{\beta,z}Y
and
Y=XM_{\alpha,z}-M_{\beta,z}X
, for some
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, imply
Y=0
.

The equation
YM_{\alpha,z}=M_{\beta,z}Y
is equivalent to
Y=M_{\alpha,\beta,h}
for some
h\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. Then we have
Y=XM_{\alpha,z}-M_{\beta,z}X
,
XM_{\alpha,z}=M_{\beta,z}X+M_{\alpha,\beta,h}
. Hence,
Xz^{k}=zXz^{k-1}+hz^{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots
. Let
g:=Xz^{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
. Then by induction, we have that
Xz^{k}=z^{k}g+kz^{k-1}h
,
k=1,2,\dots
.

Let
Xz^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{jk}z^{j}
,
k=0,1,\dots
. Then

(3.2)

\langle Xz^{k},z^{l}\rangle=\langle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{jk}z^{j},z^{l}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{jk}\langle z^{j},z^{l}\rangle=x_{lk}\beta(l)^{2},

where
k,l=0,1,\dots
. On the other hand,

z^{k}g(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{g}(j)z^{j+k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{g}(j-k)z^{j},

z^{k}h(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{h}(j)z^{j+k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{h}(j-k)z^{j},

where
k=0,1,\dots
, and
\hat{g}(j)=0=\hat{h}(j)
whenever
j<0
. Hence,

(3.3)

\begin{split}\langle Xz^{k},z^{l}\rangle&=\langle z^{k}g+kz^{k-1}h,z^{l}\rangle\\
&=\langle z^{k}g,z^{l}\rangle+k\langle z^{k-1}h,z^{l}\rangle\\
&=\langle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{g}(j-k)z^{j},z^{l}\rangle+k\langle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{h}(j-k+1)z^{j},z^{l}\rangle\\
&=\hat{g}(l-k)\beta(l)^{2}+k\hat{h}(l-k+1)\beta(l)^{2},\end{split}

where
k=1,2,\dots
and
l=0,1,\dots
. Note that
Xz^{0}=g
, equation (
3.3
) still holds for
k=0
. From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it follows that

x_{lk}=\hat{g}(l-k)+k\hat{h}(l-k+1),

where
k,l=0,1,\dots
.

Let
t=0,1,\dots
,
k=1,2,\dots
, and
l=k+t-1
. Then
x_{k+t-1,k}=\hat{g}(t-1)+k\hat{h}(t)
. Hence,

(3.4)

\frac{x_{k+t-1,k}}{k}=\frac{\hat{g}(t-1)}{k}+\hat{h}(t)\to\hat{h}(t),\ k\to\infty,\ \text{for}\ t=0,1,\dots.

From condition (
3.1
) and assumption (
2.1
), we can see that

\frac{\alpha(k)}{k\beta(k+t-1)}\to 0,\ k\to\infty,\ \text{for}\ t=0,1,\dots.

From (
3.2
), it follows that

\begin{split}|x_{k+t-1,k}|&=\frac{|\langle Xz^{k},z^{k+t-1}\rangle|}{\beta(k+t-1)^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\|X\|\|z^{k}\|_{\alpha}\|z^{k+t-1}\|_{\beta}}{\beta(k+t-1)^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\alpha(k)}{\beta(k+t-1)}\|X\|.\end{split}

Hence,

(3.5)

\frac{|x_{k+t-1,k}|}{k}\leq\frac{\alpha(k)}{k\beta(k+t-1)}\|X\|\to 0,\ k\to\infty,\ \text{for}\ t=0,1,\dots.

From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), it can be observed that
\hat{h}(t)=0
when
t=0,1,\dots
. This implies
h=0
and consequently
Y=M_{\alpha,\beta,h}=0
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. The weight sequences of the Hilbert spaces
\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\lambda}}
and
\mathrm{H}_{(\mu)}=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\mu}}
are
\alpha_{\lambda}(k)=(k+1)^{\lambda}
and
\alpha_{\mu}(k)=(k+1)^{\mu}
, respectively. Then the weight sequences
\alpha=\alpha_{\lambda}
and
\beta=\alpha_{\mu}
satisfy the condition (
3.1
) if and only if
\lambda-\mu<1
.

The following proposition, which can be used to deal with two similar operators in
FB_{2}(\Omega)
, is Proposition 3.3 in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
X
is an invertible operator intertwining two operators
T
and
\tilde{T}
in
FB_{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.
XT=\tilde{T}X
, then
X
is upper triangular:

X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&X_{12}\\
0&X_{22}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and the operators
X_{11}
and
X_{22}
are invertible.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces, where the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy condition (
3.1
). If the operators
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
and
T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
are similar, where
h
and
\tilde{h}
are two non-zero functions in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, then there exist
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
h_{2}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
such that
\frac{1}{h_{1}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
,
\frac{1}{h_{2}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, and
h=\tilde{h}h_{1}h_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
T:=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}\sim\tilde{T}:=T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
, there exists an invertible operator
X
such that
XT=\tilde{T}X
. According to Lemma
3.3
and
T
and
\tilde{T}
are in
FB_{2}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
X
is upper triangular:

X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&X_{12}\\
0&X_{22}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and the operators
X_{11}
and
X_{22}
are invertible.

From
XT=\tilde{T}X
, we have

X_{11}M_{\alpha,z}^{*}=M_{\alpha,z}^{*}X_{11},

X_{22}M_{\beta,z}^{*}=M_{\beta,z}^{*}X_{22},

X_{11}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}+X_{12}M_{\beta,z}^{*}=M_{\alpha,z}^{*}X_{12}+M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}^{*}X_{22}.

The first two equations show that
X_{11}=M_{\alpha,g_{1}}^{*}
and
X_{22}=M_{\beta,g_{2}}^{*}
for some
g_{1}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
g_{2}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. The third equation shows that

X_{11}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}-M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}^{*}X_{22}=\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}(X_{12}).

Since
M_{\alpha,g_{1}}^{*}=X_{11}
and
M_{\beta,g_{2}}^{*}=X_{22}
are invertible, we have
\frac{1}{g_{1}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
\frac{1}{g_{2}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. What’s more, we can directly calculate

\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}(X_{11}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}-M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}^{*}X_{22})=0.

Therefore,
X_{11}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}-M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}^{*}X_{22}
is in
\mathrm{Ran\,}\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}\cap\mathrm{Ker\,}\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}
. According to Proposition
3.1
, this implies
X_{11}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}=M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}^{*}X_{22}
and consequently
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}M_{\alpha,g_{1}}=M_{\beta,g_{2}}M_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
. Thus,
hg_{1}=g_{2}\tilde{h}
.

Finally, take
h_{1}=\frac{1}{g_{1}}
and
h_{2}=g_{2}
, and then we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. Let
h
and
\tilde{h}
be in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. If there exist
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
h_{2}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
such that
\frac{1}{h_{1}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
,
\frac{1}{h_{2}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, and
h=\tilde{h}h_{1}h_{2}
, then
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
and
T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
are similar.

Proof.

To see this, take the invertible operator

X=\begin{pmatrix}(M_{\alpha,h_{1}}^{-1})^{*}&0\\
0&M_{\beta,h_{2}}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix},

then, by simple calculation,
XT_{\alpha,\beta,h}X^{-1}=T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
,
and this implies that
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
and
T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
are similar.
∎

4.
The case of
B(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar{a}z}

Recall a basic concept of weakly homogeneous operators, which was introduced by Clark and Misra
[
2
]
. Denote by
\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
the analytic automorphism group of
\mathbb{D}
, which is the set of all analytic bijection from
\mathbb{D}
to itself. As well know, a function
\phi
is in
\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if it has the following form:

\phi(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar{a}z},\ z\in\mathbb{D},

for some
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and some
a\in\mathbb{D}
. A bounded linear operator
T
on a complex separable Hilbert space
H
is called weakly homogeneous if
\sigma(T)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and for any
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
,
\phi(T)
is similar to
T
.

We say a weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be
M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant
if for each
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
,
f\circ\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
whenever
f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be a M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant weighted Hardy space. Then for each
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
, one can define a composition operator
C_{\alpha,\phi}\colon f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to f\circ\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
, which is bounded by closed graph Theorem. What’s more,
C_{\alpha,\phi}
is invertible and
C_{\alpha,\phi}^{-1}=C_{\alpha,\phi^{-1}}
. By simple calculation,
\phi(M_{\alpha,z})C_{\alpha,\phi}=M_{\alpha,\phi}C_{\alpha,\phi}=C_{\alpha,\phi}M_{\alpha,z}
. Thus,
M_{\alpha,z}
is a weakly homogeneous operator.

Remark 4.1
.

It is known that for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the Hilbert space
\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}
is M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant (see
[
22
]
,
[
4
]
).

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem 3.1 in
[
11
]
shows that each weighted Hardy space of polynomial growth is M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant.

The main idea of the next proposition originates from Theorem 3.16 in
[
8
]
. Here we use Proposition
3.4
to improve it in part.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant weighted Hardy spaces. Suppose the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy condition (
3.1
). If the operator
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is weakly homogeneous, where
h\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\cap\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, then
h
is zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
or
h
is non-zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

If
h
is a zero function on
\mathbb{D}
, then
h
is a zero function on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
since
h\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. So we suppose that
h
is a non-zero function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
and let
\tilde{\phi}
be the function defined by
\tilde{\phi}(z)=\overline{\phi(\overline{z})}
. Then
\tilde{\phi}
is also in
\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
T:=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is weakly homogeneous,
\tilde{\phi}(T)\sim T
. From
M_{\alpha,z}^{*}M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}=M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}M_{\beta,z}^{*}
, it can be calculated that

\tilde{\phi}(T)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}(M_{\alpha,z}^{*})&\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}(M_{\alpha,z}^{*})M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}\\
0&\tilde{\phi}(M_{\beta,z}^{*})\\
\end{pmatrix}\\
=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,\phi}^{*}&M_{\alpha,\beta,h\phi^{\prime}}^{*}\\
0&M_{\beta,\phi}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

M
\ddot{o}
bius invariance guarantees that the composition operators
C_{\alpha,\phi}
and
C_{\beta,\phi}
are invertible and that
C_{\alpha,\phi}^{-1}=C_{\alpha,\phi^{-1}}
and
C_{\beta,\phi}^{-1}=C_{\beta,\phi^{-1}}
. Then, by simple calculation,

\begin{pmatrix}C_{\alpha,\phi}^{*}&0\\
0&C_{\beta,\phi}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}(T)\begin{pmatrix}C_{\alpha,\phi}^{*}&0\\
0&C_{\beta,\phi}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\\
=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}^{*}&M_{\alpha,\beta,(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})}^{*}\\
0&M_{\beta,z}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

So

T=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}\sim\tilde{\phi}(T)\sim T_{\alpha,\beta,(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})}.

Since
\phi
is a bijection from
\mathbb{D}
to itself and
\phi^{\prime}(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{2}}
is non-zero everywhere on
\mathbb{D}
, then
(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})
is a non-zero function on
\mathbb{D}
. Consequently, according to Proposition
3.4
, there exists
h_{1}=h_{1,\phi}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
h_{2}=h_{2,\phi}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
such that
\frac{1}{h_{1}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
,
\frac{1}{h_{2}}\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, and
h=(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})h_{1}h_{2}
. Thus,

(4.1)

h(\phi(z))=h(z)\phi^{\prime}(z)h_{1}(\phi(z))h_{2}(\phi(z)),\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, similar to the last part of the proof of Theorem 3.16 in
[
8
]
, we can obtain that
h
is non-zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two M
\ddot{o}
bius invariant weighted Hardy spaces. Let
h
be in
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\cap\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
. If
h
is zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
or
h
is non-zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then
T=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is weakly homogeneous.

Proof.

It is trivial if
h
is zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. So we suppose that
h
is non-zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
\frac{1}{h}\in\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
and let
\tilde{\phi}
be the function defined by
\tilde{\phi}(z)=\overline{\phi(\overline{z})}
.
From the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we see that
\tilde{\phi}(T)\sim T_{\alpha,\beta,(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})}
. It is not hard to see that the function
\tilde{h}:=(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})
is in
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
and non-zero everywhere on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\in\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
. Since
h=\tilde{h}\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}h
and
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is contained in both
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, then according to Remark
3.5
,
T=T_{\alpha,\beta,h}\sim T_{\alpha,\beta,(h\circ\phi^{-1})(\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi^{-1})}
. Thus,
\tilde{\phi}(T)\sim T
. Since
\tilde{\phi}
is arbitrary in
\mathrm{Aut\,}(\mathbb{D})
, we obtain that
T
is weakly homogeneous.
∎

5.
The case of
B(z)=z^{n}

5.1.
Power of a special operator

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be a weighted Hardy space and
n
be a positive integer. Denote

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}:=\vee\left\{z^{j+kn}\right\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\ j=0,1,\dots,n-1.

Then, it can be verified that

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0}\dotplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1}\dotplus\cdots\dotplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1}

is a Hilbert direct sum, and
\left\{z^{j+kn}\right\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
. What’s more,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
is an invariant subspace of the multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,z^{n}}=M_{\alpha,z}^{n}
.

We say that a weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
has
property A
for a positive integer
n
, if there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(n)>0
such that

\alpha(k)\leq c_{1}\alpha(j+kn)

and

\alpha(j+kn)\leq c_{2}\alpha(k)

for any
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
and
k=0,1,\dots
.

Inspired by
[
1
]
, we give the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
be a weighted Hardy space with property A for a positive integer
n
. Then for each
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
, the map

X_{\alpha,j}\colon f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{j+kn}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}

is an invertible bounded linear operator. Moreover, for each
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
,
M_{\alpha,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}}

X_{\alpha,j}

=

X_{\alpha,j}

M_{\alpha,z}
. Thus,
M_{\alpha,z^{n}}
is similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}M_{\alpha,z}
.

Proof.

Let
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
, then

\begin{split}\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{j+kn}\|^{2}&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\alpha(j+kn)^{2}\\
&\leq c_{2}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\alpha(k)^{2}=c_{2}^{2}\|f\|^{2},\end{split}

which implies
g=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{j+kn}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
and
\|g\|\leq c_{2}\|f\|
. Thus, it can be seen that
X_{\alpha,j}
is not only well defined but also a bounded linear operator. Obviously,
X_{\alpha,j}
is an injection.

Let
g=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}(k)z^{j+kn}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
, then

\begin{split}\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}(k)z^{k}\|^{2}&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}(k)|^{2}\alpha(k)^{2}\\
&\leq c_{1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}(k)|^{2}\alpha(j+kn)^{2}=c_{1}^{2}\|g\|^{2},\end{split}

which implies that
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}(k)z^{k}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
g=X_{\alpha,j}f
. Thus,
X_{\alpha,j}
is a surjection. Therefore,
X_{\alpha,j}
an invertible operator.

The remaining conclusions can be easily checked.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Form the assumption (
2.1
), we can see that a weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
has property A for a positive integer
n
if and only if there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(n)>0
such that

c_{1}\leq\frac{\alpha(n-1+kn)}{\alpha(k)}\leq c_{2}

for any
k=0,1,\dots
.

Remark 5.3
.

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the weighted Hardy spaces
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\lambda}}=\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}
has property A for any positive integer
n
. In fact, this follows Remark
5.2
since

\frac{\alpha_{\lambda}(n-1+kn)}{\alpha_{\lambda}(k)}=n^{\lambda}

for any
k=0,1,\dots
.

Remark 5.4
.

Each weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
of polynomial growth has property A for any positive integer
n
.

Proof.

The weighted Hardy space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
is of polynomial growth means that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{k+1}{k+N+1}\leq\frac{\alpha(k)}{\alpha(k-1)}\leq\frac{k+N+1}{k+1}

(see
[
11
]
).
Then for each
k=0,1,\dots
, whenever
n-1+kn>k
,

\prod_{j=k+1}^{n-1+kn}\frac{j+1}{j+N+1}\leq\frac{\alpha(n-1+kn)}{\alpha(k)}=\prod_{j=k+1}^{n-1+kn}\frac{\alpha(j)}{\alpha(j-1)}\leq\prod_{j=k+1}^{n-1+kn}\frac{j+N+1}{j+1}.

Form

\prod_{j=k+1}^{n-1+kn}\frac{j+N+1}{j+1}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{n(k+1)+j}{k+1+j}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{N}n=n^{N},

we get

\frac{1}{n^{N}}\leq\frac{\alpha(n-1+kn)}{\alpha(k)}\leq n^{N}.

The last inequality holds for each
k=0,1,\dots
. Thus, this remark follows Remark
5.2
.
∎

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces. It is not hard to check that the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
;

(b)
1\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
;

(c)
\sup_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta(k)}{\alpha(k)}<\infty
.

If one of the conditions above is true, the multiplication operator
M_{\alpha,\beta,1}
is the inclusion mapping
\iota\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
. In addition,
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha})\subseteq\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
.

Remark 5.5
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. The weighted Hardy spaces
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\lambda}}=\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha_{\mu}}=\mathrm{H}_{(\mu)}
satisfy
\mathrm{H}_{(\lambda)}\subseteq\mathrm{H}_{(\mu)}
if and only if
\lambda-\mu\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces with property A for a positive integer
n
. Suppose
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
. Denote
T=T_{\alpha,\beta,1}
, then
T^{n}
is similar to
T\oplus(\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1}\tilde{T})
, where
\tilde{T}=T_{\alpha,\beta,z}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove
(T^{*})^{n}\sim T^{*}\oplus(\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1}\tilde{T}^{*})
, where

T^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}&0\\
M_{\alpha,\beta,1}&M_{\beta,z}\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \tilde{T}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z}&0\\
M_{\alpha,\beta,z}&M_{\beta,z}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

From
M_{\alpha,\beta,1}M_{\alpha,z}=M_{\beta,z}M_{\alpha,\beta,1}
, it can be calculated that

(T^{*})^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z^{n}}&0\\
nM_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}&M_{\beta,z^{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&\frac{1}{n}I\\
\end{pmatrix}(T^{*})^{n}\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&\frac{1}{n}I\\
\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z^{n}}&0\\
M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}&M_{\beta,z^{n}}\\
\end{pmatrix}=:A,

it followed that
(T^{*})^{n}\sim A
.

Let

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-1},

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,0},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle K_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-2},

then

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}=K_{0}\dotplus K_{1}\dotplus\cdots\dotplus K_{n-1}

is Hilbert direct sum. One can check that
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,j}
are invariant subspace of
M_{\alpha,z^{n}}
and
M_{\beta,z^{n}}
, respectively, and that

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-1},

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,0},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-2}.

Hence,
K_{0},K_{1},\dots,K_{n-1}
are invariant subspace of
A
.

To complete the proof, it is enough to prove that

A|_{K_{0}}\sim T^{*},\;A|_{K_{1}}\sim\tilde{T}^{*},\;\dots,\;A|_{K_{n-1}}\sim\tilde{T}^{*}.

This implies that
(T^{*})^{n}\sim A\sim T^{*}\oplus(\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1}\tilde{T}^{*})
.

It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle A|_{K_{0}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0}}&0\\
P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-1}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0}}&M_{\beta,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-1}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle A|_{K_{1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1}}&0\\
P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,0}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1}}&M_{\beta,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,0}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle A|_{K_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\alpha,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1}}&0\\
P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-2}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1}}&M_{\beta,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-2}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma
5.1
shows that for any
j=0,1,\dots,n-1
, the maps
X_{\alpha,j}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}
and
X_{\beta,j}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,j}

are invertible operators. Take the invertible operators

X_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{\alpha,0}&0\\
0&X_{\beta,n-1}\\
\end{pmatrix},\,X_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{\alpha,1}&0\\
0&X_{\beta,0}\\
\end{pmatrix},\,\dots,\,X_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{\alpha,n-1}&0\\
0&X_{\beta,n-2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

By calculation we obtain that
M_{\alpha,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,j}}X_{\alpha,j}=X_{\alpha,j}M_{\alpha,z}
,
M_{\beta,z^{n}}|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,j}}X_{\beta,j}=X_{\beta,j}M_{\beta,z}
and that

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-1}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,0}}X_{\alpha,0}

\displaystyle=X_{\beta,n-1}M_{\alpha,\beta,1},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,0}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,1}}X_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle=X_{\beta,0}M_{\alpha,\beta,z},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta,n-2}}M_{\alpha,\beta,z^{n-1}}P_{\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha,n-1}}X_{\alpha,n-1}

\displaystyle=X_{\beta,n-2}M_{\alpha,\beta,z}.

The above equation indicates that

A|_{K_{0}}X_{0}=X_{0}T^{*},\ A|_{K_{1}}X_{1}=X_{1}\tilde{T}^{*},\ \dots,\ A|_{K_{n-1}}X_{n-1}=X_{n-1}\tilde{T}^{*},

and hence

A|_{K_{0}}\sim T^{*},\;A|_{K_{1}}\sim\tilde{T}^{*},\;\dots,\;A|_{K_{n-1}}\sim\tilde{T}^{*}.

∎

5.2.
Dissimilarity

In the remaining part of this section, we need some conclusions related to strongly irreducible operators (‘strongly irreducible’ is also abbreviated as ‘(SI)’).

Denote
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathcal{B})
for the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group of a Banach algebra
\mathcal{B}
.
For a bounded linear operator
A
on a Hilbert space
H
, denote
\left\{A\right\}^{\prime}
for the commutant algebra of
A
and
A^{(n)}
for the
n
copies
\oplus_{n}^{n}A
of
A
.
Then the following theorem is Theorem 1 in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
T=A_{1}^{(n_{1})}\oplus A_{2}^{(n_{2})}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{k}^{(n_{k})}
, where
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{k}
are strongly irreducible Cowen-Douglas operators,
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
are not similar whenever
i\neq j
, and
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}
are positive integers. Then

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T\right\}^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

The following proposition, which is Proposition 2.22 in
[
13
]
, gives a characterization of strong irreducibility in
FB_{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 5.8
.

An operator

T=\begin{pmatrix}T_{0}&S\\
0&T_{1}\end{pmatrix}

in
FB_{2}(\Omega)
is strongly irreducible if and only if
S\notin\mathrm{Ran\,}\sigma_{T_{0},T_{1}}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces, where the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy condition (
3.1
). Then

(a) for any non-zero function
h
in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
, the operator
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is strongly irreducible.

(b) for any non-zero functions
h
and
\tilde{h}
in
\mathrm{Mult}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha},\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta})
such that
h
and
\tilde{h}
have different zero points on
\mathbb{D}
, the operators
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
and
T_{\alpha,\beta,\tilde{h}}
are not similar.

Proof.

(a) Since
\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}(M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*})=0
, it follows that
M_{\alpha,\beta,h}^{*}\notin\mathrm{Ran\,}\sigma_{M_{\alpha,z}^{*},M_{\beta,z}^{*}}
according to
Proposition
3.1
. Then
T_{\alpha,\beta,h}
is strongly irreducible by Lemma
5.8
.

(b) Clearly, this is a corollary of Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
be two weighted Hardy spaces with property A for a positive integer
n\geq 2
. Suppose
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\beta}
and suppose the weight sequences
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy condition (
3.1
). Denote
T=T_{\alpha,\beta,1}
, then
T^{n}
is not similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}T
.

Proof.

Proposition
5.6
shows that
T^{n}\sim T\oplus(\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1}\tilde{T})=T^{(1)}\oplus\tilde{T}^{(n-1)}
, where
\tilde{T}=T_{\alpha,\beta,z}
. Lemma
5.9
shows that
T
and
\tilde{T}
are strongly irreducible Cowen-Douglas operators and
T\nsim\tilde{T}
.
Assume
T^{n}
is similar to
\oplus_{1}^{n}T=T^{(n)}
, then

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T^{(1)}\oplus\tilde{T}^{(n-1)}\right\}^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T^{n}\right\}^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T^{(n)}\right\}^{\prime}).

But from Theorem
5.7
, it follows that

\mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T^{(1)}\oplus\tilde{T}^{(n-1)}\right\}^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2},\ \ \mathrm{K}_{0}(\left\{T^{(n)}\right\}^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

So we get a contradiction since
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ncong\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

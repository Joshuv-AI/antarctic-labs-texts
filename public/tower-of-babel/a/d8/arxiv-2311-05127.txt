On a radial projection conjecture in 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
Paige Bright, Ben Lund, and Thang Pham

Abstract
. A conjecture on radial projections in 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{d} states that if E ⊂ 𝔽 q n E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} with q k − 1 < | E | ≤ q k q^{k-1}<|E|\leq q^{k} for some k ∈ { 1 , … , n − 1 } k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\} , then # ⁡ { y ∈ 𝔽 q n ∣ | π y ​ ( E ) | < | E | } ≤ q k . \#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid|\pi^{y}(E)|<|E|\}\leq q^{k}. The case k = n − 1 k=n-1 was recently proved by the second and third listed authors and Thu (2022). In this paper, we resolve this conjecture in all ranges of k k .



1.
Introduction

For any point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the radial projection from
y
, denoted by
\pi^{y}
, is the map from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{y\}
to the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
defined by

\pi^{y}(x):=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}.

For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\pi^{y}(E)=\{\pi^{y}(x):x\in E\setminus\{y\}\},

as the radial projection of
E
from
y
.

The main question on this topic is to estimate the dimension of the sets

\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\pi^{y}(E))=0\right\}~~\mbox{and}~~\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\dim_{H}\pi^{y}(E)<\dim_{H}(E)\right\},

when
\dim_{H}(E)>d-1
and
\dim_{H}(E)\leq d-1
, respectively, where
\mathcal{H}^{d}
denotes the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

If
\dim_{H}(E)>d-1
, Mattila and Orponen
[
4
]
proved that

(1)

\dim_{H}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\pi^{y}(E))=0\}\leq 2(d-1)-\dim_{H}(E).

This result is sharp due to a construction of Orponen
[
6
]
of showing that for any
\alpha\in(d-1,d]
one can construct a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
\alpha
such that
\dim_{H}\{y:\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\pi^{y}(E))=0\}=2(d-1)-\alpha
.

If
\dim_{H}(E)\leq d-1
, Liu
[
3
]
proved that

(2)

\dim_{H}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\dim_{H}\pi^{y}(E)<\dim_{H}(E)\}\leq\min(2(d-1)-\dim_{H}(E),\dim_{H}(E)+1).

Liu and Orponen
[
3
]
conjectured the following strengthening of
Eq.

2
, which was proved by Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
8
]

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set with
\dim_{H}(E)\in(k-1,k]
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
. Then

\dim_{H}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\dim_{H}\pi^{y}(E)<\dim_{H}(E)\right\}\leq k.

In a discrete setting, say
\mathbb{F}^{d}
with
\mathbb{F}
is an arbitrary field, for a given point
y\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
, the radial projection
\pi^{y}
maps a point
x\neq y
to the line that contains
x
and
y
. In this paper, we focus on the case of finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is a prime power.

As in the continuous setting, the main question is to study the magnitude of the sets

\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon|\pi^{y}(E)|\leq q^{d-1}\right\}~~\mbox{and}~~\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon|\pi^{y}(E)|\leq|E|\right\},

when
|E|\geq q^{d-1}
and
|E|<q^{d-1}
, respectively.

The first investigation on this question was made by the second, third listed authors and Thu in
[
2
]
. Their first result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and let
M
be a positive integer, with
|E|\geq 6q^{d-1}
and
M\leq 4^{-1}q^{d-1}
.
Then,

(3)

\#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon|\pi^{y}(E)|\leq M\}<12q^{d-1}M|E|^{-1}.

The parameter
M
makes
Theorem

1.2
more general than the natural discrete analog to
Eq.

1
.
The authors of
[
2
]
conjectured the following continuous analog to
Theorem

1.2
, for which a slightly weaker version was proved by the first author and Gan
[
1
]
, and the full statement was proved by Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set with
\dim_{H}(E)>d-1
, and let
s\leq d-1
be a real number.
Then,

\dim_{H}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\dim_{H}\pi^{y}(E)<s\}\leq d-1+s-\dim_{H}(E).

The authors of
[
2
]
also conjectured the following analog to
Theorem

1.1
, and proved it in the case that
k=d-1
.
Here, we prove it in full generality.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
q^{k-1}<|E|\leq q^{k}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
.
Then,

\#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:|\pi^{y}(E)|<50^{-1}|E|\}\leq 10q^{k}.

Theorem

1.4
is tight up to constants, as can be seen by taking
E
to be a subset of the points contained in a
k
-dimensional affine subspace
\Gamma
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
, in which case
|\pi^{y}(E)|\leq q^{k-1}\ll|E|
for each
y\in\Gamma
.

Our proof relies on a subspace sampling argument that allows us to reduce the problem to the following, which is a stronger version of
Theorem

1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
30q^{k}\leq|E|\leq q^{k+1}
, and let
M\leq 4^{-1}q^{k}
for
k\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
.
Then,

(4)

\#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\colon|\pi^{y}(E)|\leq M\}<300q^{k}M|E|^{-1}.

The continuous version of
Theorem

1.5
was proved as
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

2.
Notation, terminology, and basic facts about finite geometry

The number of
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is

\binom{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n}-q)\ldots(q^{n}-q^{k-1})}{(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}-q)\ldots(q^{k}-q^{k-1})}.

Indeed, the numerator counts the number of ordered
k
-tuples of linearly independent vectors in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, and the denominator counts the number of ordered
k
-tuples of linearly independent vectors in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k}
.
Similarly, the number of
k
-dimensional subspaces that contain a fixed
\ell
-dimensional subspace (for
\ell\leq k
) is
\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}_{q}
.

For a linear subspace
\Gamma
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
and a point
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, denote

\pi_{\Gamma}(x)=\Gamma+x
.
For a set of points
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, denote

\pi_{\Gamma}(S)=\bigcup_{x\in S}\pi_{\Gamma}(x)
.
In our application,
\Gamma
will be
(n-k-1)
-dimensional.
Under this assumption, the space
\pi_{\Gamma}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})
of translates of
\Gamma
is a
k+1
-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and we consider the projections
\pi_{\Gamma}(x)
of points in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
to be elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}
.
For
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}
, we denote
\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(y)
to be the set of points
x
such that
\pi_{\Gamma}(x)=y
; note that
\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(y)
is the set of points in an affine
(n-k-1)
-plane.
Similarly, for a set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}
, we denote
\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(S)=\bigcup_{y\in S}\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(y)
.

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.5

To prove
Theorem

1.5
, we first find a
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Gamma
such that
\pi_{\Gamma}
nearly preserves the size of
E
and the size of

T=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}:|\pi^{y}(E)|\geq M\},

and then apply
Theorem

1.2
to the projected sets in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}
.

We first show that, provided that neither
|E|
nor
|T|
is too large, there is a projection that roughly preserves the size of both sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A,B\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, with
|A|<q^{k+1}
and
|B|<q^{k+1}
.
Then, there exists an
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Gamma
such that
|\pi_{\Gamma}(A)|\geq 5^{-1}|A|
and
|\pi_{\Gamma}(B)|\geq 5^{-1}|B|
.

Proof.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, with
|X|<q^{k+1}
.
Let
R
be the set of triples
(\Lambda,u,v)
where
\Lambda
is an affine
(n-k-1)
-plane and
u
and
v
are distinct points of
X
that are contained in
\Lambda
.
We count
|R|
in two different ways.
First, we take the sum over
(n-k-1)
-planes
\Lambda
of the number of pairs of points of
X
contained in
\Lambda
.
Second, we take the sum over pairs of points
P
of
X
of the number of
(n-k-1)
-planes that contain
P
.
In detail,

|R|=\sum_{\Gamma\in G(n,n-k-1)}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}\binom{|X\cap\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(y)|}{2}=\binom{n-1}{n-k-2}_{q}\binom{|X|}{2}.

Note that

|G(n,n-k-1)|=\binom{n}{n-k-1}_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{k+1}-1}\binom{n-1}{n-k-1}_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{n-k-1}-1}\binom{n-1}{n-k-2}_{q}.

Hence, the expected number of pairs of distinct points of
X
with the same image under
\pi_{\Gamma}
for a uniformly random
\Gamma
is

E_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}\binom{|X\cap\pi^{-1}_{\Gamma}(y)|}{2}\right)=|R|\,|G(n,n-k-1)|^{-1}=\frac{q^{n-k-1}-1}{q^{n}-1}\binom{|X|}{2}.

By Markov’s inequality, the following condition is satisfied for at least
(3/4)|G(n,n-k-1)|
of the
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspaces
\Gamma
:

(5)

\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}\binom{|X\cap\pi^{-1}_{\Gamma}(y)|}{2}\leq 4\cdot\frac{q^{n-k-1}-1}{q^{n}-1}\binom{|X|}{2}.

In particular,
Eq.

5
is simultaneously satisfied for
X=A
and
X=B
for at least half of the
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspaces
\Gamma
.

Now suppose that
Eq.

5
is satisfied for a fixed set
X
and
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Gamma
, and for each
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}
, denote
X(y)=|X\cap\pi^{-1}_{\Gamma}(y)|
.
Using the assumption that
|X|\leq q^{k+1}
,

\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}X(y)^{2}=2\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}\binom{X(y)}{2}+\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}X(y)<8q^{-k-1}\binom{|X|}{2}+|X|\leq 5|X|.

Combining this with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

|X|^{2}=\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}}X(y)\right)^{2}\leq|\pi_{\Gamma}(X)|\sum_{y\in\Gamma^{\perp}}X(y)^{2}\leq 5|\pi_{\Gamma}(T)|\,|X|.

Hence,
|\pi_{\Gamma}(X)|\geq 5^{-1}|X|
.
As noted above, there is a choice of
\Gamma
so that
Eq.

5
is simultaneously satisfied for both
A
and
B
.
∎

In order to apply
Lemma

3.1
to
T
, we need to already know that
|T|
is not too large.
The following weak bound proved in
[
2
]
is sufficient for this purpose.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
2
]

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
and let
1<C<|E|
. Then,

\#\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}:|\pi^{y}(E)|<C^{-1}|E|\}<(C-1)^{-1}q|E|.

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.5
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Denote
|E|=cq^{k}\geq 30q^{k}
, and

T:=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}:|\pi^{y}(E)|<M\}.

Since
M<4^{-1}q^{k}<(4c)^{-1}|E|
,
Proposition

3.2
implies that
|T|\leq(4c-1)^{-1}q|E|<3^{-1}q^{k+1}
.
Since both
|E|\leq q^{k+1}
and
|T|\leq q^{k+1}
,
Lemma

3.1
implies that there is an
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Gamma
such that
\pi_{\Gamma}(T)\geq 5^{-1}|T|
and
\pi_{\Gamma}(E)\geq 5^{-1}|E|
.

For
y\in T
, let
L_{y}
be the set of lines that contain
y
and at least one point of
E
, and let
L_{y}^{\pi}
be the set of lines that contain
\pi_{\Gamma}(y)
and at least one point of
\pi_{\Gamma}(E)
.
Note that
|L_{y}|\geq|L^{\pi}_{y}|
.
Indeed, the image of each line
\ell\in L_{y}
under
\pi_{\Gamma}
is either a line in
L^{\pi}_{y}
or the point
\pi(y)
.
Hence,
|\pi^{\pi_{\Gamma}(y)}(\pi_{\Gamma}(E))|=|L^{\pi}_{y}|\leq|L_{y}|\leq M
, and so

\pi_{\Gamma}(T)\subseteq\{w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}:|\pi^{w}(\pi_{\Gamma}(E))|\leq M\}.

Since
|\pi(E)|\geq 5^{-1}|E|\geq 6q^{k}
and
M\leq 4^{-1}q^{k}
, we can apply
Theorem

1.2
to obtain

5^{-1}|T|\leq|\pi_{\Gamma}(T)|\leq\#\{w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k+1}:|\pi^{w}(\pi_{\Gamma}(E))|\leq M\}\leq 12q^{k}M|\pi_{\Gamma}(E)|^{-1}\leq 60q^{k}M|E|.

This immediately implies the claimed bound
|T|\leq 300q^{k}M|E|
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.4

Define

T:=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon|\pi^{y}(E)|<|E|/50\}.

We want to show that
|T|<10q^{k}
.

Since
|E|<q^{k}
,
Proposition

3.2
gives us
|T|<q^{k+1}/49<q^{k+1}
.
Thus, by
Lemma

3.1
, there exists an
(n-k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Gamma
such that
|\pi_{\Gamma}(T)|\geq|T|/5
and
|\pi_{\Gamma}(E)|\geq|E|/5
. As the proof of
Theorem

1.5
, we know that

\pi_{\Gamma}(T)\subseteq\left\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+1}\colon|\pi^{w}(\pi_{\Gamma}(E))|\leq|E|/50\right\}\subseteq\left\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k+1}\colon|\pi^{w}(\pi_{\Gamma}(E))|\leq|\pi_{\Gamma}(E)|/10\right\}.

Thus, by
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
(which is a version of the
k=d-1
case of
Theorem

1.4
with appropriate constants), we have

|\pi_{\Gamma}(T)|\leq 10q^{k},

provided that
q^{k-1}<|\pi_{\Gamma}(E)|\leq q^{k}
. The upper bound is clear since
|\pi_{\Gamma}(E)|\leq|E|
. The lower bound follows if
|E|\geq 5q^{k-1}
. However, if
|E|\leq 5q^{k-1}
, then
Proposition

3.2
implies
|T|\leq q^{k}/2
.

In other words, we have proved that for
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
q^{k-1}<|E|\leq q^{k}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-2\}
.
Then,

\#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:|\pi^{y}(E)|<50^{-1}|E|\}\leq 10q^{k}.

This completes the proof.

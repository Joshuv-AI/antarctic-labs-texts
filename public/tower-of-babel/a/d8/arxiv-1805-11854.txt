Convergence rates of nonlinear inverse problems in Banach spaces: conditional stability and weaker norms


Abstract
. In this short note, we formulate the convergence rates of the well known Tikhonov regularization scheme for solving the nonlinear ill-posed problems in Banach spaces. For deriving the convergence rates, we employ the novel smoothness concept of conditional stability estimates in terms of weaker norms. Moreover, we show that our results are applicable on two ill-posed inverse problems. AMS Subject Classifications : 47 47 J 06 06 , 65 65 J 20 20 , 47 47 J 20 20 , 47 47 A 52 52

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

1.1.
Introduction

Let
F:D(F)\subset U\to V
be a nonlinear operator between the Banach spaces
U
and
V
, where
D(F)
is the domain of
F
. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm of
U
and
V
. We study the nonlinear ill-posed problems governed by the operator equation

F(u)=v,\quad u\in D(F),v\in V.

(1)

Since, in practice, the exact data is not always available, we look for the approximate solutions of (
1
). In general, variational regularization (in particular, Tikhonov regularization)
[
5
]
, regularization with sparsity constraints
[
15
]
, regularization via iterative methods
[
9
,
16
]
etc., are used for finding the stable approximations of the exact solution of (
1
). When
U
and
V
are Hilbert spaces, a widely used regularization method is Tikhonov regularization, which involves the minimization of the functional

\|F(u)-v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\alpha\|u-u_{0}\|^{2},\ u\in D(F),\ \alpha>0,

where
v^{\prime}
is some noisy approximation of
v
and
u_{0}\in U
is an initial guess of the exact solution of (
1
). Because of its tendency to smooth the solutions, Tikhonov regularization does not yield satisfactory output in Hilbert spaces, especially if there are jumps or sparsity in the structure of exact solution. So, in the recent years other types of Tikhonov regularization have been explored in Banach spaces, see
[
5
,
15
,
16
]
. More specifically, the following generalized Tikhonov regularization is widely considered in the Banach spaces

T_{\alpha}(u,\delta):=\|F(u)-v^{\delta}\|^{p}+\alpha R(u),\ u\in D(F),

(2)

with
1\leq p<\infty
,
R:U\to[0,\infty]
is a stabilizing functional which is convex and proper, i.e.
D(R)\neq\emptyset
, where
D(R)=\{u\in U:R(u)<\infty\}
is the domain of
R
,
\alpha
is the regularization parameter and
v^{\delta}
is some noisy approximation of
v\in V
satisfying

\|v^{\delta}-v\|\leq\delta.

(3)

Typically, for the minimizers of Tikhonov regularization, the following four issues, i.e., existence, stability, convergence to the exact solution and convergence rates are of the most interest
[
15
]
. For the Tikhonov minimizers of (
2
), these four issues are well addressed in
[
15
]
.
The determination of convergence rates have a very long tradition in inverse problems (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
17
]
). In order to derive the convergence rates, some kind of smoothness of the exact solution is utilized in terms of the source conditions or variational inequalities
[
5
,
15
,
16
]
. One of the recently developed smoothness concepts is to study the convergence rates in Hilbert as well as Banach spaces is that of conditional stability estimates
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
17
]
.
In this paper, we are peculiarly interested in the convergence rates at which minimizers of (
2
) with
R(u)=\|u-u_{0}\|^{2}
for an initial guess
u_{0}
of the exact solution, converges to the exact solution of (
1
). The highlight of our work is that we employ the following novel smoothness concept of conditional stability in weaker norms to derive the convergence rates:

\|u-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}\leq C\ \eth(\|F(u)-F(u^{\dagger})\|),\ C>0,

(4)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\eta}
is a norm weaker than the strong norm
\|\cdot\|
(because it is induced from a topology weaker than the norm topology),
\eth
is an index function, i.e.
\eth:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is such that
(i)

\eth(0)=0
,
(ii)

\eth
is strictly monotonically increasing and continuous; and
u^{\dagger}
is a
R
-minimizing solution of (
1
).
We recall that an element
u^{\dagger}\in D
, where
D:=D(F)\cap D(R)
, is called a
R
-minimizing solution of (
1
) if it satisfies (
1
) and minimizes the functional
R
, i.e.,
R(u^{\dagger})=\min\{R(u):u\in D\ \text{such that}\ F(u)=v\}.
We remark that if the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\eta}
is replaced with the usual norm
\|\cdot\|
in (
4
), then it becomes the usual conditional stability estimate
[
16
]
.

1.2.
Preliminaries and assumptions

In this section, we revisit some well known definitions and results required in this paper. Moreover, we also discuss the various assumptions that are needed to prove our main result.

Bregman distance
:
Let
R:U\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0,\infty\}
be a convex and proper functional having subdifferential
\partial R
and
u\in U
be such that
\zeta\in\partial R(u)\subset U^{*}
. Here
U^{*}
is the dual space of
U
. Then, with respect to
R
, the Bregman distance
D_{\zeta}(u^{*},u)
for any element
u^{*}\in U
from
u
is given by

D_{\zeta}(u^{*},u):=R(u^{*})-R(u)-\langle\zeta,u^{*}-u\rangle_{U^{*},U},

(5)

where
\langle\zeta,u^{*}-u\rangle_{U^{*},U}
denotes the dual pairing between
U^{*}
and
U
. By definition of Bregman distance, it is clear that
D_{\zeta}(\cdot,u)
is defined at
u
only if
\partial R(u)\neq\emptyset
.

For variational regularization methods, it is more appropriate to show the convergence of minimizers of (
2
) in Banach spaces via Bregman distances instead of Ljapunov functional or conventional norm based functionals
[
14
]
.

Remark 1
.

If we consider
R(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}
, then
(\ref{5})
gives
D_{\zeta}(u^{*},u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{*}-u\|^{2}
. Further, one can observe that
D_{\zeta}(\cdot,\cdot)
is similar to a metric but, in general, it does not satisfy the symmetric property and the triangular inequality.

To assure the well-posedness, convergence (to the exact solution), and stability of the Tikhonov regularized solutions of (
2
), the following assumptions are required.

Assumption 1
.

(1)
The Banach spaces
U
and
V
are associated with the topologies
O_{U}
and
O_{V}
, respectively, and these topologies are coarser than the norm topology.

(2)
The norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
is sequentially lower semi-continuous with respect to
O_{V}
.

(3)
The functional
R:U\to[0,\infty]
is sequentially lower semi-continuous with respect to
O_{U}
as well as convex.

(4)

D=D(F)\cap D(R)\neq\emptyset
.

(5)
The level sets
M_{\alpha}(K)
, for every
\alpha>0
and
K>0
, are sequentially pre-compact and closed with respect to
O_{U}
, where
M_{\alpha}(K):=\{u\in D:T_{\alpha}(u,0)\leq K\}.

(6)
The restriction of
F
on
M_{\alpha}(K)
is sequentially continuous with respect to
O_{U}
and
O_{V}
, for every
\alpha>0
and
K>0
.

The next lemma is a well-known result on the existence, stability and convergence of Tikhonov minimizers of (
2
), provided Assumption
1
is satisfied.

Lemma 1
.

[
15
, Chapter 3]

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then:

(1)
the minimizer of the Tikhonov functional
(\ref{2})
exists for any
\alpha>0
and
v^{\delta}\in V
.

(2)
the minimizers of
(\ref{2})
are stable in the sense that if
\{v^{k}\}
is a sequence converges to
v^{\delta}\in V
, with respect to the norm topology, then the each corresponding sequence
\{u^{k}\}
of minimizers of
(\ref{2})
, with
v^{\delta}
replaced by
v^{k}
, has a convergent subsequence
\{u^{k^{\prime}}\}
with respect to
O_{U}
. The limit of the subsequence
\{u^{k^{\prime}}\}
is a minimizer of
(\ref{2})
.

(3)
if there exists a solution of
(\ref{1})
in
D
and
\alpha:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
fulfills
\alpha(\delta)\to 0
and
(\alpha(\delta))^{-1}\delta^{p}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
, then the minimizers of the Tikhonov functional
(\ref{2})
converges to the solution of
(\ref{1})
.

2.
Convergence rates in terms of weaker norms and examples

In this section, we formulate the convergence rates for Tikhonov regularization scheme (
2
) by incorporating the stability estimates (
4
) in the form of Theorem 1.

2.1.
Main result

Theorem 1
.

Let
F:D(F)\subset U\to V
be a nonlinear operator between the Banach spaces
U
and
V
. Moreover, let the following assumptions hold:

(1)

u^{\dagger}
is a
R
-minimizing solution of
(\ref{1})
.

(2)
Assumption
1
holds with
R(u)=\|u-u_{0}\|^{p}
.

(3)

u_{0}
is in some neighborhood of
u^{\dagger}
, i.e., there exists some
K_{1}>0
such that

\|u_{0}-u^{\dagger}\|\leq K_{1}.

(6)

(4)

F:D(F)\subset U\to V
satisfies the following conditional stability estimate:

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\eta}\leq C\ \eth(\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|),

(7)

for an index function
\eth
, provided
\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|\leq M
, where
M
and
C
are positive constants and
\|\cdot\|_{\eta}
is an appropriate norm which induces a topology coarser than the norm topology on
U
.

Further, let
\alpha=\alpha(\delta)
be such that
0<\alpha(\delta)\leq\alpha_{\max}
,
\frac{\delta^{p}}{\alpha}\leq c
for
c>0
,
M
and
K_{1}
satisfy

(c+K_{1}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{1}\leq M,\ \ K_{1}^{p}\leq\frac{K_{2}}{\alpha_{\max}}

(8)

for some
K_{2}>0
. Let
u_{\alpha}^{\delta}
be the minimizer of
(\ref{2})
. Then, for
\alpha(\delta)\sim\delta^{p-\epsilon}
, we have

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}=O\big(\eth\big(\delta^{\frac{p-\epsilon}{p}}\big)\big),\ \ \text{for}\ \delta\to 0,\ \text{where}\ 0<\epsilon<p.

(9)

Proof.

From the definition of
u_{\alpha}^{\delta}
, (
3
) and (
6
), we have that

\|F(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})-v^{\delta}\|^{p}+\alpha\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u_{0}\|^{p}\leq\|F(u^{\dagger})-v^{\delta}\|^{p}+\alpha\|u^{\dagger}-u_{0}\|^{p}\leq\delta^{p}+\alpha K_{1}^{p}.

(10)

Consequently, the non-negativity of the norm gives

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u_{0}\|^{p}\leq\frac{\delta^{p}}{\alpha}+K_{1}^{p}\leq c+K_{1}^{p}.

(11)

By using (
6
), (
8
) and (
11
) along with the triangular inequality, we derive that

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|\leq\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u_{0}\|+\|u_{0}-u^{\dagger}\|\leq M.

The last inequality implies that (
7
) is applicable if we take
u_{1}=u_{\alpha}^{\delta}
and
u_{2}=u^{\dagger}
. Hence, (
7
) and (
3
) along with the definition of index function yield

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}\leq C\ \eth(\|F(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})-F(u^{\dagger})\|)\leq C\ \eth(\big(\|F(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})-v^{\delta}\|+\delta\big).

(12)

To this end, we deduce from (
10
) and (
8
) that

\|F(u_{\alpha}^{\delta})-v^{\delta}\|^{p}\leq\|F(u^{\dagger})-v^{\delta}\|^{p}+\alpha K_{1}^{p}\leq\alpha\bigg(\frac{\delta^{p}}{\alpha}+\frac{K_{2}}{\alpha_{\max}}\bigg).

Plugging this estimate in (
12
) to obtain

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}\leq C\ \eth\bigg(\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{p}}{\alpha}+\frac{K_{2}}{\alpha_{\max}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\delta\bigg).

Further, as
\frac{\delta^{p}}{\alpha}\leq c
, we get

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}\leq C\ \eth\bigg(\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(c+\frac{K_{2}}{\alpha_{\max}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\delta\bigg).

Finally, for the a-priori choice of
\alpha=\alpha(\delta)\sim\delta^{p-\epsilon}
with
0<\epsilon<p
, we get

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}=O\big(\eth\big(\delta^{\frac{p-\epsilon}{p}}\big)\big)\ \ \text{for}\ \delta\to 0,

where
0<\epsilon<p
. This completes the proof. ∎

Remark 2
.

It is worth to mention that the regularization is only used in Theorem
1
to constrain the minimizers of
(\ref{2})
to a set on which the conditional stability estimate
(\ref{7})
hold.

2.2.
Examples

Next, we discuss two examples of ill-posed inverse problem on which the result of Theorem
1
can be applied. In the first example, we consider the inverse problem which involves the determination of a potential function from the knowledge of Neumann to Dirichlet map for the wave equation (cf.
[
3
]
). In the second example, we discuss an ill-posed classical inverse scattering problem.

Example 1
(
Inverse problem related with a wave equation
).
We consider the wave equation

\begin{cases}\begin{split}u_{tt}-\Delta u+qu=0\qquad\text{for all}\ (x,t)\ \in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{t}=0\qquad\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\Omega\ \text{and}\ t=0,\hskip 11.38109pt\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=f\qquad\qquad\text{for all}\ (x,t)\ \in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{split}\end{cases}

(13)

where
\Omega
is a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
having a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
for
n\geq 2
,
T>\
diameter
(\Omega)
,
q=q(x)
is the potential function and
\nu
is the unit outward normal. The associated Neumann to Dirichlet map
\Lambda_{q}
with
(\ref{13})
is defined as follows:

\Lambda_{q}:f\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))\to u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T)).

Let us consider the inverse problem of determining the potential function
q=q(x)
from
\Lambda_{q}
.
Bao and Yun
[
3
]
, established the following conditional stability estimate for the above-mentioned inverse problem.

Conditional stability estimate
:
Suppose that
\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M^{\beta}
for some
\beta>\frac{n}{2}+1
,
M>0
and
\|q_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M
,
i=1,2
. Then, we have

\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Lambda_{q_{1}}-\Lambda_{q_{2}}\|_{*}^{1-\epsilon},

(14)

where
0<\epsilon<1
and
C>0
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{*}
denotes the operator norm from
L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))
to
H^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))
and
H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the
Sobolev space of order
\beta
.

For our convenience, we write the inverse problem under consideration as

F:q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\mapsto\Lambda_{q}\in B\big(L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T)),H^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))\big),

(15)

where
B\big(L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T)),H^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))\big)
is the Banach space of all bounded linear maps from
L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))
to
H^{1}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))
.
Let us assume that there exists a
R
-minimizing solution
q^{\dagger}
of (
15
) corresponding to the wave equation (
13
), i.e.,
F(q^{\dagger})=\Lambda_{q}
. Also let the initial approximation
q_{0}
of the exact solution be such that
\|q^{\dagger}-q_{0}\|_{H^{\beta}}\leq K_{1}
for some
K_{1}>0
. It can be easily seen that assumption
4
of Theorem
1
is satisfied via the conditional stability estimate (
14
) with the index function
\eth(s)=s^{1-\epsilon}
,
0<\epsilon<1
. Therefore, Theorem
1
provides the convergence rates

\|q_{\alpha}^{\delta}-q^{\dagger}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=O\bigg(\delta^{\frac{(1-\epsilon)(p-\epsilon_{1})}{p}}\bigg),

provided the regularization parameter is such that
\alpha(\delta)\sim\delta^{p-\epsilon_{1}}
, where
\epsilon_{1}<p
,
q_{\alpha}^{\delta}
is the minimizer of the following Tikhonov functional
\|F(q)-\Lambda_{q}^{\delta}\|_{*}^{p}+\alpha\|q-q_{0}\|_{H^{\beta}}^{p}
and Assumption
1
holds.

Example 2
(
Inverse scattering problem
). The classical inverse scattering problem
is concerned with the recovery of refractive index of a medium in the presense of near or far field measurements of scattered time-harmonic acoustic waves
[
6
]
. The forward problem can be defined as follows: Given one or several wave
(
s
)

u^{i}
which solves the Helmholtz equation
\Delta u^{i}+k^{2}u^{i}=0
and a refractive index
\eta=1-f
, determine the total field
(
s
)

u=u^{i}+u^{s}
such that

\Delta u+k^{2}\eta u=0,\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}

(16)

\frac{\partial u^{s}}{\partial z}-\iota ku^{s}=O\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\bigg),\ \text{as}\ |x|=z\to\infty.

(17)

The condition
(\ref{17})
is known as the Sommerfeld radiation condition.
The corresponding inverse problem is the reconstruction of the refractive index
\eta=1-f
from the far field data
u^{\infty}
. Note that every solution of
(\ref{16})
satisfying
(\ref{17})
has the following asymptotic behavior

u(x)=u^{i}(x)+\frac{e^{\iota kz}}{z}\bigg(u^{\infty}(\hat{x})+O\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\bigg)\bigg),\ \text{as}\ |x|=z\to\infty,

uniformly for all directions
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{t}
is in the set
\mathbb{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|=1\}
.
Mathematically, the forward operator
F
is the mapping

F:D(F)\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}):F(f)=u^{\infty}.

In
[
6
, Corollary 2.5]
, it has been shown that, under certain conditions, the inverse problem under consideration fulfills the following conditional stability estimate

\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{H^{m}}\leq K\big(\ln(3+\|F(f_{1})-F(f_{2})\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S})})\big)^{-\mu\theta},\ \ 0<\theta<1,

where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are such that

\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\leq C_{s},\ \ s\neq 2m+\frac{3}{2}

for
\frac{3}{2}<m<s,

K>0,C_{s}>0
and
H^{m}
is the Sobolev space. Clearly, an estimate of the form
(\ref{4})
holds with the index function
\eth(t)=\big(\ln(3+t^{-2})\big)^{-\mu\theta}
, where
0<\theta<1
and

2.3.
Comparison Analysis

In this section, we compare the convergence rates results of Tikhonov regularization method obtained in Theorem 1 with other notable works in this direction.

2.3.1.
Comparison with the results of
[
16
]

Schuster et al.
[

\frac{1}{p}\|F(u)-v^{\delta}\|^{p}+\alpha\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{W}^{q},\ u\in D(F)\cap W\subset U,

M_{R}=\{u\in D(F)\cap W:\|u\|_{W}\leq R\}
and
\eth

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|=O\big(\eth(\delta)\big)\ \ \text{for}\ \alpha=\alpha(\delta)\sim\delta^{p},

where

are

\|u_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\|_{\eta}=O\big(\eth\big(\delta^{\frac{p-\epsilon}{p}}\big)\big)\ \ \text{for}\ \alpha=\alpha(\delta)\sim\delta^{p-\epsilon},

3.
Discussion

We have obtained the convergence rates of Tikhonov regularization scheme for non-linear ill-posed problems in Banach spaces by incorporating the novel smoothness concept of conditional stability estimates in weaker norms. Moreover, the theory presented in Theorem
1
is complemented with two concrete examples. Also, we have compared our convergence rates results with several other notable works that engaged conditional stability estimates to derive the convergence rates of Tikhonov regularization and shown that they are of the same order.

One of the important future questions related to this work is to determine the optimal rates of convergence that can be achieved via conditional stability estimates. In this direction, the notable works of
[
4
,
13
]
can be used as a reference.

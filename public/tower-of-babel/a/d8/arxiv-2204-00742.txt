Introduction

The main result of this paper is a generalization of an inequality for the convolution and the rearrangement by Wang–Madiman
[
WM14

The rearrangement of a measurable function is defined as follows.

Definition 1.1

The rearrangement

2.1
The definition of the optimal constants

In this subsection, we define the optimal constant of (the reverse) Young’s inequality.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
For
P:=(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{N})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
with

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{k}}>N-1,

we let the real number
q(P)>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(P)}=1-N+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{k}}.

(1)

When
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{N},q(P)\neq 1
, we define

\displaystyle C(P):=\frac{1}{B\circ q(P)}\prod_{k=1}^{N}B(p_{k}).

(2)

For a unimodular locally compact group
G
, we define

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{B}}:=\{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{N})\mid\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{N}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\;\|\phi_{1}\|_{p_{1}}=\|\phi_{2}\|_{p_{2}}=\cdots=\|\phi_{N}\|_{p_{N}}=1\}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{O}(P,G)

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}*\cdots*\phi_{N}\|_{q(P)}\mid(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{N})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{R}(P,G)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}*\cdots*\phi_{N}\|_{q(P)}\mid(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{N})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\}.

(3)

Let
G
and
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
be as in
(2)
.
For
N=2
, we define
\mathcal{B}
as the set of all elements of
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
with (
1.1
) and

\displaystyle Y_{O}(P,G)

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}\|_{q(P)}\mid(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathcal{B}\},

\displaystyle Y_{R}(P,G)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}\|_{q(P)}\mid(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathcal{B}\}.

We note that
C(P)<1<q(P)
holds for any

and

holds for any

When

Thus, for any unimodular locally compact group

\displaystyle Y_{O}(P,G)\leq\tilde{Y}_{O}(P,G),\quad Y_{R}(P,G)\geq\tilde{Y}_{R}(P,G)

Remark 2.2

(
[
Sat21
]
, Remark 2.4 (3))
.

The following three conditions
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(3)
are equivalent for any locally compact group

(1)

One has

.

(2)

The locally compact group

has an open compact subgroup.

(3)

The identity component

of

If the equivalent conditions

,
(2)
, and

are satisfied, then we have

.
Thus, (

are satisfied.

(a)

One has
\mu(\mathrm{supp}\;\phi_{1})+\mu(\mathrm{supp}\;\phi_{2})\leq\mu(G_{0})
.

(b)

The identity component

is not compact.

In particular, if

is open (e.g.,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=1,\quad\phi_{1}(g)\phi_{2}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})=\phi_{1}(g)^{p_{1}/p_{2}^{\prime}}\phi_{2}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})^{p_{2}/p_{1}^{\prime}}(\phi_{1}(g)^{p_{1}}\phi_{2}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})^{p_{2}})^{1/p}

3
The Hausdorff–Young inequality

In this section, we give an upper bound of the optimal constant
H(p,G)
of the Hausdorff–Young inequality (Corollary
3.5
).
Klein–Russo proved that
H(p,G)
is determined only by the optimal constant of Young’s inequality when
p^{\prime}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
(Fact
3.4
).
Thus, Corollary
1.3
bounds
H(p,G)
from above in this case.

There are several equivalent definitions of
H(p,G)
(Remark
3.2
).
In this paper, we adopt the definition by Russo (Definition
3.1
) based on Kunze’s argument
[
Kun58
, Section 5]
.
Let
l_{\phi}\colon L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
be the convolution operator defined as
l_{\phi}(\xi):=\phi*\xi
for any function
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
on a unimodular locally compact group
G
.
For
\phi\in L^{1}(G)
,
l_{\phi}
is the bounded operator defined on all elements of
L^{2}(G)
by Fact
2.3
.
We define the optimal constant
H(p,G)
of the Hausdorff–Young inequality as follows.

Definition 3.1

(Russo,
[
Rus74
, Section 1]
)
.

We define the constant
H(p,G)
as

\displaystyle H(p,G):=\sup\{\|\gamma\|_{2}^{2/p^{\prime}}\mid\gamma\in L^{2}(G),\;\exists\phi\in L^{1}(G),\;\|\phi\|_{p}=1,\;l_{\gamma}=|l_{\phi}|^{p^{\prime}/2}\}

for any unimodular locally compact group
G
and
1<p\leq 2
.

Remark 3.2
.

There are some equivalent definitions of
H(p,G)
.

(1)

Kunze defined a space
\mathcal{F}L^{p^{\prime}}(G)
of operators on
L^{2}(G)
to formulate the Hausdorff–Young inequality (Fact
3.3
) by an argument of the gage space on
L^{2}(G)
by Segal
[
Seg53
, Section 5]
.
Russo defined
H(p,G)
as the optimal constant of this inequality (Definition
3.1
).
Haagerup generalized the definition of

for any von Neumann algebra
[

]
(see also
[
Nel74
]
and
[

]

(2)

When

[

, Section 2]
.
Since the

-norm of the Haar measure (Plancherel’s theorem), the optimal constant of this inequality is equal to that of
(1)

[

(3)

Kosaki defined

as the interpolation space between von Neumann algebra

and its predual

corresponds to

When

\displaystyle H(p,G)=\sup\{\||l_{\phi}|^{p^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(G)\to L^{\infty}(G)}^{1/p^{\prime}}\mid\|\phi\|_{p}=1\}.

Some properties of the rearrangement

In this section, we show some essential properties of the rearrangement and the layer cake representation to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a measurable function on a measure space
(G,\mu)
.

(1)

We have

\displaystyle(\phi^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})=\left(-\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2},\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2}\right)

for any
t\geq 0
.

(2)

We have

\displaystyle 1_{A}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &A&&\text{if}\;t<1\\
&\emptyset&&\text{if}\;t\geq 1\end{aligned}\right.

for any
t\geq 0
and subset
A\subset G
.

(3)

We have

\displaystyle 1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{*}=1_{(\phi^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}=1_{(-\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2,\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2)}

for any
t\geq 0
.

(4)

We have

\displaystyle\phi(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}(g)dt

for any
g\in G
.

(5)

If
\mu
is a Haar measure on a unimodular locally compact group
G
, then we have

\displaystyle\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}*1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g)dt_{1}dt_{2}

for any
g\in G
and measurable functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, where we write
L_{1}(t):=\phi_{1}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})
and
L_{2}(t):=\phi_{2}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})
.

(6)

If a pointwise increasing sequence of functions
\phi_{n}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
converges pointwise to
\phi
, then
\phi_{n}^{*}
is a pointwise increasing sequence converging pointwise to
\phi^{*}
.

Proof.

(1)

Since

\displaystyle x\in(\phi^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\phi^{*}(x)>t

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\inf\{t^{\prime}>0\mid\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t^{\prime}}))\leq 2|x|\}>t

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))>2|x|

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x\in\left(-\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2},\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2}\right)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\phi^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})=\left(-\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2},\frac{\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))}{2}\right).

(2)

We have

\displaystyle 1_{A}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})=\{g\in G\mid 1_{A}(g)>t\}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &A&&\text{if}\;t<1\\
&\emptyset&&\text{if}\;t\geq 1\end{aligned}\right..

(3)

Since

\displaystyle 1_{(\phi^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}=1_{(-\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2,\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2)}

by
(1)
, it suffices to show

\displaystyle 1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{*}=1_{(-\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2,\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2)}.

(5.1)

We have

\displaystyle 1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{*}(x)=\inf\{t^{\prime}>0\mid\mu(1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t^{\prime}}))\leq 2|x|\}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since

\displaystyle\mu(1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t^{\prime}}))=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))&&\text{if}\;t^{\prime}<1\\
&0&&\text{if}\;t^{\prime}\geq 1\end{aligned}\right.

by
(2)
, we have

\displaystyle\inf\{t^{\prime}\geq 0\mid\mu(1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t^{\prime}}))\leq 2|x|\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&&\text{if}\;2|x|<\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))\\
&0&&\text{if}\;2|x|\geq\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=1_{(-\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2,\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t}))/2)}(x).

Thus, we obtain (
5.1
).

(4)

We obtain

\displaystyle\phi(g)=\int_{0}^{\phi(g)}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})}(g)dt.

(5)

We have

\displaystyle\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}(g)=\int_{G}\phi_{1}(g^{\prime})\phi_{2}(g^{\prime-1}g)dg^{\prime}.

Since

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(g^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})dt_{1},\quad\phi_{2}(g^{\prime-1}g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dt_{2}

hold by
(4)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\phi_{1}(g^{\prime})\phi_{2}(g^{\prime-1}g)dg^{\prime}=\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})dt_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dt_{2}dg^{\prime}.

We get

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})dt_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dt_{2}dg^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{G}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dg^{\prime}dt_{1}dt_{2}

by Fubini’s theorem.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{G}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dg^{\prime}=1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}*1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g),

we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{G}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}(g^{\prime})1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g^{\prime-1}g)dg^{\prime}dt_{1}dt_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{L_{1}(t_{1})}*1_{L_{2}(t_{2})}(g)dt_{1}dt_{2}.

(6)

Since
\phi_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})\subset\phi_{n+1}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})\subset\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>t})
for any
n
, we have
\phi_{n}^{*}\leq\phi_{n+1}^{*}\leq\phi^{*}
.

Here
\mu(\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>\phi^{*}(x)-\epsilon}))>2|x|
holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
.
We fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
.
Since the pointwise increasing sequence
\phi_{n}
converges pointwise to
\phi
, there exists (sufficiently large)
n
with
\mu(\phi_{n}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>\phi^{*}(x)-\epsilon}))>2|x|
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(x)-\epsilon\leq\phi_{n}^{*}(x)\leq\phi^{*}(x)

holds.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}^{*}(x)=\phi^{*}(x)

and hence
\phi_{n}^{*}
is a pointwise increasing sequence converging pointwise to
\phi^{*}
.
∎

In this subsection, we show the following lemma to prove Theorem

Lemma 6.1
.

We have

\displaystyle f_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{T(\omega)}\circ\alpha(\omega)d\omega,\quad t:=\int_{\Omega}T(\omega)d\omega

for any measurable functions

on a measure space
\Omega
.
Furthermore, the equality holds if
T(\omega)\leq\alpha(\omega)
almost everywhere.

Proof.

When
\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega\leq t
, we have

\displaystyle f_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega\right)=0\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{T(\omega)}\circ\alpha(\omega)d\omega.

When
\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega\geq t
, we have

\displaystyle f_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\omega)d\omega-t=\int_{\Omega}(\alpha(\omega)-T(\omega))d\omega\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{T(\omega)}\circ\alpha(\omega)d\omega.

Furthermore, the equality holds if
T(\omega)\leq\alpha(\omega)
almost everywhere.
∎

\nu((t_{1},t_{2}])=f_{+}^{\prime}(t_{2})-f_{+}^{\prime}(t_{1})
for any
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle f(y)=f_{+}^{\prime}(0)y+\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t}(y)d\nu(t)

for any
y\geq 0
.

Example 7.2
.

Suppose
G
,
\phi_{1}
, and
\phi_{2}
are as in Theorem
1.2
and
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
is integrable.
Since

\displaystyle f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)=f_{+}^{\prime}(0)\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}(g)+\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t}\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)d\nu(t)

by Fact
7.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg=f_{+}^{\prime}(0)\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}\|+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f_{t}\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})\|d\nu(t)

(7.1)

by integrating over
g\in G
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx=f_{+}^{\prime}(0)\|\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}\|+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f_{t}\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})\|d\nu(t).

(7.2)

Since
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}
are integrable by Fubini’s theorem and

\displaystyle\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}\|=\|\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}\|,

(7.3)

(
7.1
) and (
7.2
) are well-defined.
Thus, we obtain (
1.3
) by (
6.1
) and (
7.3
).

Theorem
1.2
follows from Example
7.2
when
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
is integrable,
f(y)
is continuous at
y=0
, and
f_{+}^{\prime}(0)>-\infty
.
Here we show Theorem
1.2
by using Example
7.2
when
f(y)
is not continuous at
y=0
or
f_{+}^{\prime}(0)=-\infty
.
We define
f_{(n)}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle f_{(n)}(y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &nf\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)y&&\text{if}\;y\leq\frac{1}{n}\\
&f(y)&&\text{if}\;y\geq\frac{1}{n}\end{aligned}\right..

The function
f_{(n)}(y)
is convex and continuous at
y=0
.
We have

\displaystyle f_{(n)+}^{\prime}(0)=nf\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)>-\infty.

(7.4)

Since
f
is not continuous at
y=0
or
f_{+}^{\prime}(0)=-\infty
, there exists
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
f
is negative on
(0,1/n^{\prime}]
.
We have
0\geq f_{(n)}\geq f
on
[0,1/n^{\prime}]
for any
n\geq n^{\prime}
by the convexity of
f
.

Remark 7.3
.

Since Theorem
1.2
is clear when
\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg=-\infty
or
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx=\infty
, it suffices to show Theorem
1.2
when

\displaystyle\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg

\displaystyle>-\infty,

(7.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx

\displaystyle<\infty.

(7.6)

For any
n\geq n^{\prime}
, we have
0\geq f_{(n)}\geq f
on
[0,1/n^{\prime}]
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{G}f_{(n)}\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{(n)}\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx<\infty

(7.7)

by Example
7.2
, (
7.4
) and (
7.6
).
Since the pointwise decreasing sequence
f_{(n)}
converges to
f
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}f_{(n)}\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg,

(7.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{(n)}\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx

(7.9)

by the monotone convergence theorem.
Thus, we have (
1.3
) by (
7.7
), (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) and hence we obtain Theorem
1.2
when
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}

In this section, we show Theorem

8.1
The case where

When

can be obtained by a similar argument as in Section

.
In this case, we may assume that

is positive by replacing

by
\max(f,0)
, and (
7.6
) holds by Remark
7.3
.
Since
f(0)=0
and
f
is positive,
f(y)
is continuous at
y=0
and
f_{+}^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
holds.
Thus, there exists a Borel measure
\nu
in Fact
7.1
.
Since (
7.2
) holds by a similar argument as in Example
7.2
(both sides of (

8.2
The case where

is monotonically decreasing

In this subsection, we show Theorem
1.2
when
f
is monotonically decreasing.
There exist pointwise increasing sequences
\phi_{1,n},\phi_{2,n}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
of integrable functions converging to
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, respectively.
By Example
5.2

(2)
,
\phi_{1,n}*\phi_{2,n}
and
\phi_{1,n}^{*}*\phi_{2,n}^{*}
converge pointwise to

and
\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}

Since

f\circ(\phi_{1,n}*\phi_{2,n})

The convex function
f
is negative by

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})(g)dg

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}f\circ(\phi_{1,n}*\phi_{2,n})(g)dg,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x)dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ(\phi_{1,n}^{*}*\phi_{2,n}^{*})(x)dx

by the monotone convergence theorem.

8.3
The completion of Theorem
1.2

Theorem
1.2
was obtained in some cases in Section
7
, Subsection
8.1
, and Subsection
8.2
.
In this subsection, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
by showing that these cases exhaust all the cases of Theorem
1.2
.
That is, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose a convex function
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
with
f(0)=0
is not monotonically decreasing.
We let
t>0
with
f(t)<0
.

(1)

There exists
t^{\prime}>t
with
f(t^{\prime})=0
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle f(y)\leq 0

(8.1)

holds for any
0\leq y\leq t^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle f(y)\geq 0

(8.2)

holds for any
y\geq t^{\prime}
.

(2)

Let
t^{\prime}
be as in
(1)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{t\max(-f(y),0)}{-f(t)}+\frac{t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}

for any
y\geq 0
.

(3)

Suppose measurable functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on a unimodular locally compact group
G
satisfy (
1.2
) and (
7.6
).
Then we have

\displaystyle\|f_{\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}(x_{0})}\circ(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})\|\leq\|f_{\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}(x_{0})}\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})\|<\infty

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
with
f\circ(\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*})(x_{0})<0
.

(4)

Suppose there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
in
(3)
.
If (
7.5
) holds, then
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
is integrable.

Proof.

(1)

Since the convex function
f
is not monotonically decreasing, there exists
t^{\prime\prime}>t
with
f(t^{\prime\prime})>0
.
The convex function
f
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and hence there exists
t^{\prime}>t
with
f(t^{\prime})=0
by the intermediate value theorem.
Since
f(0)=f(t^{\prime})=0
holds and
f
is convex, we obtain
f(y)\leq 0
for any
0\leq y\leq t^{\prime}
, and
f(y)\geq 0
for any
y\geq t^{\prime}
.

(2)

When
0\leq y\leq t
, we have (
8.1
) and hence

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{tf(y)}{f(t)}=\frac{t\max(-f(y),0)}{-f(t)}\leq\frac{t\max(-f(y),0)}{-f(t)}+\frac{t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}

by the convexity of
f
.

When
t\leq y\leq t^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle y=\frac{t(t^{\prime}-y)+t^{\prime}(y-t)}{t^{\prime}-t}=\frac{t(t^{\prime}-y)+t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}.

(8.3)

Since
f
is convex,

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\prime}-y}{t^{\prime}-t}\leq\frac{f(y)}{f(t)}

holds by (
8.1
).
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle y=\frac{t(t^{\prime}-y)+t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}\leq\frac{t\max(-f(y),0)}{-f(t)}+\frac{t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}.

When
t^{\prime}\leq y
, we also have (
8.3
) and hence we obtain

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}\leq\frac{t\max(-f(y),0)}{-f(t)}+\frac{t^{\prime}f_{t}(y)}{t^{\prime}-t}.

(3)

Let
\beta:=\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
,
\psi:=\phi_{1}^{*}*\phi_{2}^{*}
and
t:=\psi(x_{0})
.
Since
\psi
is an even function by Example
5.2

(1)
, we may assume
x_{0}\geq 0
.
We have
\|f_{t}\circ\beta\|\leq\|f_{t}\circ\psi\|
by (
6.1
) and hence it suffices to show
\|f_{t}\circ\psi\|<\infty
.
Let
t^{\prime}
be as in
(1)
.
We have

\displaystyle f_{t^{\prime}}(y)\leq\frac{(t^{\prime}-t)f(y)}{-f(t)}

for any
y\geq t^{\prime}
by the convexity of
f
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|f_{t^{\prime}}\circ\psi\|\leq\frac{t^{\prime}-t}{-f(t)}\int_{G}f\circ\psi(g)dg

holds and hence we have
\|f_{t^{\prime}}\circ\psi\|<\infty
by (
7.6
).
Since
\psi
is an even function which is monotonically decreasing on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by Example
5.2

(1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{t}\circ\psi\|=\int_{-x_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\psi(x)-t)dx\leq\|f_{t^{\prime}}\circ\psi\|+(t^{\prime}-t)\int_{-x_{0}}^{x_{0}}dx<\infty.

(4)

Let
\beta
,
t
, and
t^{\prime}
be as in
(3)
.
Since

\displaystyle\|\beta\|\leq\frac{t\|\max(-f\circ\beta,0)\|}{-f(t)}+\frac{t^{\prime}\|f_{t}\circ\beta\|}{t^{\prime}-t}

by
(2)
and (
8.2
), we obtain
\|\beta\|<\infty
by
(3)
and (
7.5
).
∎

Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem

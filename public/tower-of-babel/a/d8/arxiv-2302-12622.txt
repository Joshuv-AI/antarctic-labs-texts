Geometry Transition in Spinfoams
Marios Christodoulou, Fabio D’Ambrosio, Charalampos Theofilis

Abstract
We show how the fixed-spin asymptotics of the EPRL model can be used to perform the spin-sum for spin foam amplitudes defined on fixed two-complexes without interior faces and contracted with coherent spin-network states peaked on a discrete simplicial geometry with macroscopic areas. We work in the representation given in Han and Krajewski 2014 . We first rederive the latter in a different way suitable for our purposes. We then extend this representation to 2-complexes with a boundary and derive its relation to the coherent state representation. We give the measure providing the resolution of the identity for Thiemann’s state in the twisted geometry parametrization. The above then permit us to put everything together with other results in the literature and show how the spin sum can be performed analytically for the regime of interest here. These results are relevant to analytic investigations regarding the transition of a black hole to a white hole geometry. In particular, this work gives detailed technique that was the basis of estimate for the black to white bounce appeared in Christodoulou and D’Ambrosio 2018 . These results may also be relevant for applications of spinfoams to investigate the possibility of a ‘big bounce’.

III
The Path Integral Representation of The Lorentzian EPRL Amplitude

In this section we give a different derivation of the path integral representation of the EPRL amplitude discovered in
Han and Krajewski 2014
. In that work, the authors employed group theoretical methods which allowed them to give a path-integral representation for the case of a 2-complex without boundary that precisely captures the number of degrees of freedom, without the need of introducing auxiliary spinorial variables such as those appearing in the coherent state representation. That has the advantage of rendering the critical point equations in the asymptotic analysis particularly transparent. However, because the method used in
Han and Krajewski 2014
for the derivation differs significantly from the coherent state representation techniques it becomes difficult to combine it with the analysis carried out in
Han and Zhang 2013
, which is what we want to do here. The derivation of the representation in
Han and Krajewski 2014
that we give here uses similar techniques as in
Han and Zhang 2013
. This then allows us to extend the representation of
Han and Krajewski 2014
to two-complexes with boundary. The contraction of the EPRL amplitude with boundary coherent states (that is, performing the spin sum) then becomes straightforward by combining the results of
Han and Krajewski 2014
and
Han and Zhang 2013
. This is done in Section
IV.2
.

First we fix notation and terminology. We will consider a topological two-complex
\mathcal{C}
with a non-empty boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
and bulk
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{C}\backslash\Gamma
. The two-complex is assumed to be dual to a four dimensional simplicial triangulation and consists of a collection of five-valent vertices
v
connected by edges
e
which in turn bound faces
f
. All vertices belong to the bulk
\mathcal{B}
, but some of the edges which emanate from a vertex intersect the boundary and therefore terminate at a node
n
. Nodes are four-valent and connected by links
\ell

when they are bounded by vertices and edges and we also write
\textsf{f}\in\mathcal{B}
. If a face is bounded by vertices, edges, nodes and links it is said to be a
boundary face
and we write
\textsf{f}\in\Gamma
with a slight abuse of notation (
\Gamma
is a graph, it has no faces, it is a boundary link of the face

that belongs to
\Gamma

The EPRL amplitude is a map

defined on the two-complex

, which associates complex numbers to states from the boundary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}=L^{2}\left[SU(2)^{L}/SU(2)^{N}\right]
. A group element

is associated to every half edge in the bulk (see Figure

. If an edge originating from

terminates at a node

. Links carry

group elements

IV
Approximation of the EPRL Amplitude on Tree-Level Two-Complexes

In the previous section we derived the Lorentzian EPRL amplitude in the Krajewski-Han path integral representation
Han and Krajewski 2014
in a formalism suitable for our purposes, and extended it to two-complexes with boundary. The arising boundary terms and the extended amplitude are summarized in (
63

64
), (
65
).

The large spin asymptotic of the bulk partial amplitude in
Engle et al. 2008b
have been studied in detail in
Han and Krajewski 2014
using of the coherent state representation of the EPRL amplitude. The results corroborate the ones derived in
Han and Zhang 2013
. In
Han and Zhang 2013
, the authors also gave a detailed analysis of the boundary partial amplitude, again using the coherent state representation. With the analysis of the previous section, we can now combine these results to proceed to the coherent state representation of the transition amplitude. This simply amounts to inserting resolutions of the identity in terms of
SU(2)
coherent states at each bulk face and does not affect the asymptotics of the boundary partial amplitude as performed in
Han and Zhang 2013
. Therefore, all results obtained in the coherent state representation will carry over to the Krajewski-Han path integral representation.

The above will be used in this section to develop an approximation of the holomorphic EPRL amplitude (
74
) defined on a special class of two-complexes. On a general two-complex, it is a difficult task to perform the spin-sum analytically while keeping the approximation scheme under control. Attempts in this direction can be found in
Han and Zhang 2016
;
Oliveira 2018
;
Speziale 2017
. Another option that has been explored is to use symmetry reduced models
Bahr and Steinhaus 2016a
;
Bahr and Steinhaus 2016b
;
Bahr and Belov 2018
. Here, we only consider what we call tree-level two-complexes
\mathcal{T}
: these are dual to a four-dimensional simplicial triangulation of spacetime as before, but with the additional restriction that they only have boundary faces
\textsf{f}\in\Gamma:=\partial\mathcal{T}
. That is, there are no faces which lie completely in the bulk. Considering only these two-complexes allows us to carry the out the calculation to the end. This comes from the observation that the subset of extrinsic boundary states (
32

II.2
) are sharply peaked on spin values
\omega_{\ell}
, which are taken to correspond to macroscopic classical areas of a discrete boundary geometry. This peakedness manifests itself in the Gaussian weight factors
\exp(-(j_{\ell}-\omega_{\ell})^{2}t_{\ell})
present in (
III.3
). These weight factors provide a strong regulator for the boundary face amplitude and they allow to truncate the spin-sums over boundary spins while keeping the approximation under control.

The class of is quite restrictive, but, it is relevant for existing studies of possible physical applications for spinfoams. A concrete example of such a two-complex can be found in
Christodoulou et al. 2016
;
Christodoulou and D’Ambrosio 2018
;
Marios 2018
, where it has been used to model the transition of a black hole into a white hole. It is of course desirable to consider also bulk faces. This which is beyond the scope of the present work and is left for future analysis.

Truncated Spin-Sums, Triangle Inequalities and Semiclassicality

The holomorphic amplitude in the highest weight approximation defined on a tree-level two-complex is formally given by

\displaystyle W^{t_{\ell}}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})=\mathcal{N}\sum_{\{j_{\ell}\}}\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{C})}\left(\prod_{\textsf{v}}\mathrm{d}\arc{g}_{\textsf{v}\textsf{e}}\right)\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}d_{j_{\ell}}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}\frac{d_{j_{\ell}}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Omega}_{\textsf{v}\textsf{e}\textsf{f}}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}\frac{d^{4}_{j_{\ell}}}{\pi^{4}}\int_{(\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1})^{4}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Omega}_{s\ell t}\right)\e^{\sum_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}j_{\textsf{f}}F_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell\in\Gamma}G_{\ell}}.

(75)

In what follows it is not necessary to keep track of all the details given in the precise definitions of the previous sections. In order to make the discussion in this section more concise we drop most of the indices referring to the structure of the two-complex and rewrite the amplitude as

\displaystyle W^{t}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})=\mathcal{N}\sum_{\{j_{\ell}\}\in D^{k}_{\omega}}\mu_{j}\e^{-t\sum_{\ell}(j_{\ell}-\omega_{\ell})^{2}}\e^{i\gamma\sum_{\ell}\zeta_{\ell}j_{\ell}}\int_{D_{g,\textbf{z}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{g,\Omega}\e^{\sum_{\ell}j_{\ell}F_{\ell}(g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})}.

(76)

The notation

\displaystyle\int_{D_{g,\textbf{z}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{g,\Omega}:=\int_{SL(2,\mathbb{C})}\left(\prod_{\textsf{v}}\mathrm{d}\arc{g}_{\textsf{v}\textsf{e}}\right)\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Omega}_{\textsf{v}\textsf{e}\textsf{f}}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}\int_{(\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1})^{4}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Omega}_{s\ell t}\right)

(77)

has been introduced to summarize all
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}
integrals while the notation

\displaystyle\mu_{j}:=\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}d_{j_{\ell}}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}d^{4}_{j_{\ell}}\right),

(78)

represents the summation “measure”, and where irrelevant factors of
\pi
have been absorbed into the normalization
\cal{N}
. Moreover, the summation over boundary spins is only performed over the domain

D^{k}_{\omega}:=\underset{\ell}{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\times$}}}\left\{\left\lfloor\omega_{\ell}-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2t}}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\omega_{\ell}+\frac{k}{\sqrt{2t}}\right\rfloor\right\}\quad\text{with }\quad 0<k\in\mathbb{N}.

(79)

The symbol
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
denotes the floor function which, in this article, is defined to be the largest
half integer
number equal to or less than
x
. The restriction to the summation domain
D_{\omega}^{k}
implements the truncation of the spin-sum discussed in the introduction of this section.
7
7

7

That this is a good approximation of the actual sum follows from the fact that the partial amplitude is an oscillating and finite function of the spins. The Gaussian weights therefore strongly dominate. Further justification is provided by the procedure performed in
Han and Zhang 2016
, where the author introduced a regulator
\sim\e^{-j}
to study phase transitions in large spin foams. Here, the coherent states naturally provide us with the stronger regulator
\sim\e^{-j^{2}}
.
The Gaussian weight factors (
III.3
) regulate the spin-sums while the parameter
k
acts as cut-off. It measures how many standard deviations
\sigma=1/\sqrt{2t}
the summation moves away from the peak
\omega_{\ell}
and is of order unit.

The main subtlety that needs to be addressed in what follows is that the sums in (
76
) cannot immediately be treated as independent. The summand vanishes when the triangle inequalities among the spins are not satisfied. More precisely, the summand in (
76
) vanishes whenever any one of the intertwiner spaces associated to the nodes of the two-complex is of dimension zero. Therefore, in order to treat the sums as independent and exchange them with the integrals, the spin-sums need to be restricted to spin-configurations for which the intertwiner space is always non-trivial. Let us now see that this is not an issue for the set up of this work.

To implement this requirement and since by assumption the nodes of the two-complex are four-valent we define the set

\displaystyle D_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\{j_{\ell}\}\bigg|\dim\text{Inv}_{SU(2)}\left[\overset{4}{\underset{\ell=1}{\bigotimes}}\mathcal{H}_{j_{\ell}}\right]>0\quad\forall\textsf{n}\in\Gamma\right\}

\displaystyle\text{ }=\left\{\{j_{\ell}\}\bigg|\min\left(j_{1}+j_{2},j_{3}+j_{4}\right)-\max\left(|j_{1}-j_{2}|,|j_{3}-j_{4}|\right)+1>0\quad\forall\textsf{n}\in\Gamma\right\}.

(80)

This is the set of all spin configurations
\{j_{\ell}\}
for which the intertwiner spaces over the whole boundary graph
\Gamma
are non-trivial. To adequately truncate the spin-sums we must now choose the cut-off parameter
k
such that

\displaystyle\{j_{\ell}\}\in D^{k}_{\omega}\subseteq D_{\Gamma}.

(81)

To rewrite this condition, it is convenient to split the boundary spins
j_{\ell}
into fixed background contributions
\lambda a_{\ell}
and
fluctuations

s_{\ell}
, i.e.

j_{\ell}=\lambda a_{\ell}+s_{\ell}\quad\text{with}\quad\omega_{\ell}\equiv\lambda a_{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{\ell}\in\left\{-\left\lfloor\frac{k}{\sqrt{2t}}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\frac{k}{\sqrt{2t}}\right\rfloor\right\}\quad\forall\ell\in\Gamma.

(82)

In this decomposition the
a_{\ell}
’s are assumed to be of order unit in
\lambda
and
\lambda\gg 1
. Combining (
82
) with (
81
) leads to

\displaystyle\lambda\,a_{\text{sum}}-\frac{2k}{\sqrt{2t}}+1>\lambda\,a_{\text{diff}}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{2t}}

\displaystyle a_{\text{sum}}:=\min

\displaystyle\left(a_{1}+a_{2},a_{3}+a_{4}\right)\quad\quad a_{\text{diff}}:=\max\left(|a_{1}-a_{2}|,|a_{3}-a_{4}|\right)

(83)

which can be rearranged to

\lambda\,\left(a_{\text{sum}}-a_{\text{diff}}\right)\sqrt{t}>\frac{4k}{\sqrt{2}}-\sqrt{t}\approx\frac{4k}{\sqrt{2}}

(84)

since
t
was assumed to be much smaller than unit (
30
). By the assumptions of this section, the difference
\lambda\,a_{\text{diff}}
is negligible compared to the sum
\lambda\,a_{\text{sum}}
and hence (
84
) is satisfied when the semiclassicality condition (
II.2
) holds. The semiclassicality condition can also be read as a geometricity condition on the coherent states. It imposes that the intrinsic states (
34
) have spins which are well within the triangle inequalities. This in turn means that the coherent states are composed of a superposition of intrinsic coherent states (
34
) each peaked on a triangulation of a spacelike hypersurface.

Next we turn to the dimension factors
d_{j_{\ell}}
. From (
84
) and applying the decomposition (
82
) we get from (
78
)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}(2j_{\ell}+1)\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}(2j_{\ell}+1)^{4}\right)\approx\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}2j_{\ell}\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}(2j_{\ell})^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{N_{\mathcal{C}}}\left(\prod_{\textsf{f}\in\Gamma}\prod_{\textsf{e}\in\textsf{f}}(\lambda a_{\ell}+s_{\ell})\right)\left(\prod_{\ell\in\Gamma}(\lambda a_{\ell}+s_{\ell})^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\lambda a_{\ell}\right)^{N_{\mathcal{C}}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{s_{\ell}}{\lambda a_{\ell}}\right)\right)

(85)

Dropping
\mathcal{O}(s_{\ell}/\lambda\,a_{\ell})
is justified when
|s_{\ell}|\ll\lambda\,a_{\ell}
which is equivalent to
\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}\ll\lambda\,a_{\ell}\sqrt{t}
. But this again follows from by the semiclassicality condition (
II.2
), since
k
is of order unit and hence we can safely drop the
\mathcal{O}(s_{\ell}/\lambda\,a_{\ell})
term.

IV.2
Performing the Spin-Sum

Due to the semiclassicality condition, the spin-sums over the finite summation domain
D^{k}_{\omega}
(with
k
chosen appropriately) can be treated as independent. After applying the decomposition (
82
) to the holomorphic amplitude (
76
), it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle W^{t}_{\Gamma}(H_{\ell})=\mathcal{N}\int_{D_{g,\textbf{z}}}\mu_{j}\mathrm{d}\mu_{g,\Omega}\mathcal{U}(g,\textbf{z};t,H_{\ell})\e^{\lambda\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})}

(86)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(g,\textbf{z};t,H_{\ell}):=\prod_{\ell}\left(\sum_{s_{\ell}\in D^{k}_{\omega}}\e^{-s^{2}_{\ell}t+(i\gamma\zeta_{\ell}+F_{\ell}(g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}))s_{\ell}}\right),\qquad\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}):=\sum_{\ell}(a_{\ell}F_{\ell}(g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})+i\gamma\zeta_{\ell}a_{\ell})

(87)

The large parameter
\lambda
only appears linearly in the exponent and the newly defined function
\mathcal{U}
is continuous in the variables
g
and
z
. Hence, the generalized stationary phase theorem
Hörmander 2003
may be applied.
The critical point equations

\displaystyle\text{Re}\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})=\delta_{g}\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})=\delta_{\textbf{z}}\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})

(88)

are exactly those of the fixed-spin asymptotics of
Han and Zhang 2013
and hence their results can directly be used here. The data
H_{\ell}
provided by the semiclassical states is either Regge-like, in which case there will be a geometrical critical point corresponding to one of three possible types of simplicial geometries, or there will be no critical point. We may assume the data
(\omega_{\ell},n_{\ell(\textsf{n})})
to be Regge-like and moreover we may choose it such that vector geometries are excluded. Also need to mention that there are
2^{N}
critical points.

By virtue of the stationary phase theorem we have the following estimation for the amplitude

W^{t}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})=N\sum_{c}\mu_{j}\lambda^{M^{c}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{H}_{c}(a_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})\mathcal{U}(g_{c},z_{c};t,H_{l})\e^{\lambda\Sigma(a_{\ell},g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right),

(89)

where
\mathcal{H}_{c}
contains the determinant of the Hessian of
\Sigma
. The important point to keep for physical applications is that in the first order approximation, the scale
\lambda
appears only as an overall scaling factor
\lambda^{M^{c}_{\mathcal{C}}}
and as a linear term in the exponential. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{c}
does not depend on
\lambda
.

We proceed to evaluate
\mathcal{U}
at the critical point by using

F_{\ell}(g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})=-i\gamma\,\phi_{\ell}(s_{c(\textsf{v})},a_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}),

(90)

where
\phi_{\ell}(s_{c(\textsf{v})},a_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})
is the Palatini deficit angle. Thus,
\mathcal{U}
evaluated at
c
reads

\mathcal{U}(g_{c},\textbf{z}_{c};t,H_{\ell})=\prod_{\ell}\left(\sum_{s_{\ell}\in D^{k}_{\omega}}\e^{-s^{2}_{\ell}t+i\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell}(g,\textbf{z};\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}))s_{\ell}}\right)

(91)

Since the phase
i\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})
is purely imaginary and independent of
s_{\ell}
, the sum is dominated by the exponential damping factor
\exp(-s_{\ell}^{2}t)
. It can reasonably be expected that due to this exponential damping the sum converges very fast and that it is therefore a good approximation to remove the cut-off
k
and sum
s_{\ell}
from
-\infty
to
\infty
for all
\ell\in\Gamma
. This allows us to get a closed analytic expression for the spin-sums, which approximates them well:

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{\ell}=-\infty}^{\infty}\e^{-s^{2}_{\ell}t+i\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})s_{\ell}}=2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})^{2}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{i\pi\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})}{t},\e^{-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{t}}\right),

(92)

where

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}(u,q):=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}\cos(2nu)

(93)

is the third Jacobi theta function. Hence,

\mathcal{U}(g_{c},\textbf{z}_{c};t,H_{\ell})\approx\prod_{\ell}2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})^{2}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{i\pi\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})}{t},\e^{-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{t}}\right).

(94)

Substituting everything to (
89
) we obtain

W^{t}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})=\mathcal{N}\sum_{c}\lambda^{N}\mu(a)\prod_{\ell}\left(\e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})^{2}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{i\pi\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})}{t},\e^{-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{t}}\right)\right)e^{\sum_{\ell}(-\lambda i\gamma a_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(s_{c(\textsf{v})},a_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})+i\lambda\gamma\zeta_{\ell}a_{\ell})}

(95)

The power
N
is in general a half integer that depends on the rank of the hessian at the critical point and the combinatorics of the 2-complex C. The function
\mu(a)
includes the summation measure over the spins and the Hessian evaluated at the critical point.

In Appendix
B
it is explained that for our purposes
\theta_{3}\approx 1
can be approximated by unit. Thus, we obtain

W^{t}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})\approx\mathcal{N}\sum_{c}\lambda^{N}\mu(a)\prod_{\ell}e^{\frac{-{\gamma}^{2}}{4t}(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})^{2}+i\gamma(\zeta_{\ell}-\phi_{\ell})\omega_{\ell}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right)

(96)

The above result can be generalized to include all geometric cases of critical points. Following the same procedure we arrive at

W^{t}_{\mathcal{T}}(H_{\ell})\approx\mathcal{N}\sum_{c}\lambda^{N}\mu(a)\prod_{\ell}e^{\frac{-{\Delta_{\ell}}^{2}}{4t}+i\Delta_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right)

(97)

where,
\Delta_{\ell}:=\gamma\zeta_{\ell}-\beta\phi_{\ell}(a_{\ell})+\Pi_{\ell}
.

We take a moment to go through the various quantities appearing in this formula as we have introduced a few important subtleties regarding the different kinds of geometrical critical points that we neglected in the derivation above. The
\Pi_{\ell}
contribution accounts for an extra phase in the Lorentzian intertwiners, see
Bianchi and Ding 2012
;
Barrett et al. 2010
.
The power
N
is in general a half integer that depends on the rank of the hessian at the critical point and the combinatorics of the two-complex
\mathcal{C}
. The function
\mu(a)
includes the summation measure over the spins and the Hessian evaluated at the critical point. The important point here is that neither the summation measure nor the Hessian scale with
\lambda
.

The estimation (
97
) is valid for all three types of possible geometrical critical points. If
\omega_{\ell}
and
\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}
specify a Lorentzian geometry, then

\beta=\gamma

(98)

and

\Pi_{\ell}=\begin{cases}0&\text{thick wedge}\\
\pi&\text{thin wedge}\end{cases}

(99)

If
\omega_{\ell}
and
\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}
specify a degenerate geometry, then the dihedral angles
\phi_{\ell}(\omega_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})
either vanish or are equal to
\pi
, according to whether we are in a thick or thick wedge. By abuse of notation, we express this simply by setting
\beta=0
in this case and keeping
\Pi_{\ell}

If
\omega_{\ell}
and
\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})}
specify a Euclidean geometry, then we have

\beta=1

and

\Pi_{\ell}=0

(101)

The function
\phi_{\ell}(s_{c}(v);\delta_{\ell},\textbf{n}_{\ell(\textsf{n})})

Appendix A
Review of
SU(2)
and
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
Representation Theory

Let
\mathcal{V}^{j}
with
j\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
be the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
2j
in two complex variables
\textbf{z}=(z_{0},z_{1})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. More precisely, there exist coefficients
(a_{0},\dots,a_{2j})\in\mathbb{C}^{2j+1}
such that

\displaystyle P(\textbf{z})=\sum_{k=0}^{2j}a_{k}\,z_{0}^{k}\,z_{1}^{2j-k},

(A.1)

which has the obvious property
P(\lambda\textbf{z})=\lambda^{2j}P(\textbf{z})

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. In order to obtain a representation of
SU(2)
on the vector space
\mathcal{V}^{j}
we define the action of
h\in SU(2)
as

\displaystyle h\rhd P(\textbf{z})=P(h^{\intercal}\textbf{z})\quad\forall P\in\mathcal{V}^{j}.

(A.2)

and it is easy to verify that the two defining properties of a representation, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathds{1}\rhd P=P\quad\text{and}\quad(g_{1}g_{2})\rhd P=g_{1}\rhd(g_{2}\rhd P)

(A.3)

are satisfied. The so defined representation is finite-dimensional with
\dim\mathcal{V}^{j}=2j+1
and one shows without much effort that it is also irreducible. Since all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
SU(2)
are isomorphic to one another we can relate the representation over
\mathcal{V}^{j}
to the more familiar representation in terms of vectors
\mathinner{|{jm}\rangle}\in\mathcal{H}^{j}
by defining a linear map
\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{V}^{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{j}
with the properties

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(P_{m}^{j})=\mathinner{|{jm}\rangle}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{I}(h\rhd P)=h\rhd\mathcal{I}(P).

(A.4)

This map allows us to determine a basis
P_{m}^{j}
of
\mathcal{V}^{j}
. All we need is to do is to compare the action of
h\in SU(2)
on
P_{m}^{j}
and
\mathinner{|{jm}\rangle}
. We therefore consider both sides of the equation

\displaystyle h\rhd P_{m}^{j}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}(h\rhd\mathinner{|{jm}\rangle})

(A.5)

separately and compare them in the end. Using the ansatz (
A.1
) for
P_{m}^{j}
and the group action (
A.2
) we get after some lengthy algebra

\displaystyle h\rhd P_{m}^{j}=\sum_{|l|\leq j}\sum_{|q|\leq j}a_{j+q}\left[\frac{(j+q)!(j-q)!}{(j+l)!(j-l)!}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}D_{lq}^{j}(h)z_{0}^{j+l}z_{1}^{j-l}.

(A.6)

for the left hand side of (
A.5
). The evaluation of the right hand side is straightforward and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(h\rhd\mathinner{|{jm}\rangle})=\sum_{|r|\leq j}\sum_{|s|\leq j}a_{j+s}\,D_{rm}^{j}(h)\,z_{0}^{j+s}\,z_{1}^{j-s}.

(A.7)

Comparing these expressions term by term, i.e. by setting
l=s
we obtain the condition

\displaystyle a_{j+q}\left[\frac{(j+q)!(j-q)!}{(j+s)!(j-s)!}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}D_{sq}^{j}(h)\overset{!}{=}a_{j+s}\,D_{rm}^{j}(h).

(A.8)

This equation can only be satisfied for
s=r
and
q=m
from which it follows that

\displaystyle a_{j+m}\sqrt{(j+m)!(j-m)!}=a_{j+s}\sqrt{(j+s)!(j-s)!}.

(A.9)

Since
m
is a fixed label we deduce that
a_{j+s}
has to be of the form

\displaystyle a_{j+s}=\frac{C\delta_{sm}}{\sqrt{(j+m)!(j-m)!}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z})=\sum_{|s|\leq j}a_{j+s}\,z_{0}^{j+s}\,z_{1}^{j-s}=C\frac{z_{0}^{j+m}\,z_{1}^{j-m}}{\sqrt{(j+m)!(j-m!)}}

(A.10)

for some constant
C\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. This constant can easily be fixed by requiring that the basis
P_{m}^{j}
be orthonormal with respect to an appropriate inner product on
\mathcal{V}^{j}
. When defining such an inner product we need to keep in mind convergence issues arising from integrating complex polynomials over
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. This excludes the Lebesgue measure and suggests the use of the measure

\displaystyle\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}:=\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\mathrm{d}\text{Re}\left(z_{0}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\text{Im}\left(z_{0}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\text{Re}\left(z_{1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\text{Im}\left(z_{1}\right),

(A.11)

where the exponential damping factor ensures convergence. Hence, we can devise a well-defined and
SU(2)
invariant inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathcal{V}^{j}\times\mathcal{V}^{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{j}}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}\,\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\,\overline{f(\textbf{z})}\,g(\textbf{z}).

(A.12)

A nice property of this inner product is that it factorizes into separate integrations over
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
. After a change to polar coordinates, one is left with simple integrals over Gaussian moments. It is therefore straight forward to check

\displaystyle\langle P_{m}^{j},P_{n}^{j}\rangle=|C|^{2}\,\delta_{mn}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|C|^{2}=1.

(A.13)

We choose
C=1
for simplicity. The inner product (
A.12
) allows us to write the resolution of identity on
\mathcal{H}^{j}
in terms of the basis polynomials
P_{m}^{j}
and this in turn will allow us to express the Wigner matrices in terms of complex polynomials. As an intermediate step, we define the ket

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{j\,\textbf{z}}\rangle}:=\sum_{|m|\leq j}\overline{P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z})}\mathinner{|{j\,m}\rangle}

(A.14)

which by inspection has the property

\displaystyle\left\langle{j\,\textbf{z}}|{j\,m}\right\rangle=P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z}).

(A.15)

We can then write the identity on
\mathcal{H}^{j}
as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}\,\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\,\mathinner{|{j\,\textbf{z}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{j\,\textbf{z}}|}=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{j}},

(A.16)

as can be checked by direct computation. Using the above resolution of identity we find for the Wigner matrices

\displaystyle D_{mn}^{j}(h)

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{j\,m}|}h\mathinner{|{j\,n}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{j\,m}|}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}\,\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\,h\mathinner{|{j\,\textbf{z}}\rangle}\left\langle{j\,\textbf{z}}|{j\,n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}\,\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}h\rhd\left\langle{j\,m}|{j\,\textbf{z}}\right\rangle\left\langle{j\,\textbf{z}}|{j\,n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\mathrm{d}^{4}\textbf{z}\,\frac{\textsf{e}^{-\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle}}{\pi^{2}}\overline{P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z})}\,P_{n}^{j}(h^{\intercal}\textbf{z}).

(A.17)

To get from the first to the second line we used the fact that
h
is acting on
\overline{P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z})}
inside
\mathinner{|{j\,\textbf{z}}\rangle}
. In the third line we used
h\rhd\overline{P_{m}^{j}(\textbf{z})}=\overline{P_{m}^{j}(\overline{h}\textbf{z})}=P_{m}^{j}(h\overline{\textbf{z}})
to perform the change of variables
\tilde{\textbf{z}}=\overline{h}\textbf{z}
which produces the
h^{\intercal}\textbf{z}
argument of
P_{n}^{j}
(after dropping the tilde). As we will see in a moment, it is possible to generalize this method to the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
case.

We recall that the principal series of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
is labeled by two parameters
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}\equiv(k,p)\in\mathbb{R}\times\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}
be the (infinite-dimensional) vector space of homogeneous meromorphic functions in two complex variables
\textbf{z}=(z_{0},z_{1})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where homogeneity now means

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda\textbf{z})=\lambda^{ik+p-1}\,\overline{\lambda}^{\,ik-p-1}\,\Phi(\textbf{z})\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}\text{ and }\forall\Phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}.

(A.18)

By defining the action of
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\Phi\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}
as

\displaystyle g\rhd\Phi(\textbf{z})=\Phi(g^{\intercal}\textbf{z})

(A.19)

we obtain an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}
. Moreover, the
\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}
representation splits into irreducible representations
\mathcal{V}^{j}
of the
SU(2)
subgroup

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{j=|p|}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}^{j},

(A.20)

where
j
increases in integer steps. This fact allows us to define an injection at the fixed value
p=j

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{V}^{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{(k,j)}

\displaystyle P(\textbf{z})

\displaystyle\mapsto\Phi(\textbf{z})=\left\langle{\textbf{z}}|{\textbf{z}}\right\rangle^{ik-j-1}P(\textbf{z}),

(A.21)

which has indeed the correct homogeneity properties.

Appendix B
The approximation
\vartheta_{3}\approx 1

Omitting details not relevant here and focusing only on one link for notational simplicity, the amplitude we would like to compute is given by

W(A,\zeta)\simeq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\textsf{e}^{-t(A-j)^{2}+i\gamma\zeta j}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\,\textsf{e}^{jF(g,z)}\quad,\quad g\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}),z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}.

(B.1)

Using the splitting
j=A+s
into fixed background geometry and fluctuations we get

W(A,\zeta)\simeq\textsf{e}^{i\gamma\zeta A}\sum_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}\textsf{e}^{-ts^{2}+i\gamma\zeta s}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\,\textsf{e}^{(A+s)F(g,z)}.

(B.2)

Note that
\textsf{e}^{i\gamma\zeta A}
is a pure phase (also in the general case when several links are present) and can therefore be neglected in what follows. Moreover, we used the approximation that
s\in(-\infty,\infty)
. The usual spin foam asymptotic analysis tells us that

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z)\,\textsf{e}^{(A+s)F(g,z)}\sim\textsf{e}^{-i\gamma\phi(g,z)A}\,\textsf{e}^{-i\gamma\phi(g,z)s}.

(B.3)

We neglected here the Hessian and some numerical factors. Also, the phase
\textsf{e}^{-i\gamma\phi(g,z)A}
can be neglected in what follows. We are hence left with

W(A,\zeta)\sim\sum_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}\textsf{e}^{-ts^{2}+i\gamma(\zeta-\phi)s}.

(B.4)

Now, the sum (
B.4
) can be written down in closed form in terms of known functions as

\sum_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}\textsf{e}^{-ts^{2}+i\gamma(\zeta-\phi)s}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta-\phi)^{2}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{i\pi\gamma(\zeta-\phi)}{2t},\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{t}}\right),

(B.5)

where

\vartheta_{3}\left(u,q\right)=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}\cos(2nu)

(B.6)

is one of Jacobi’s Theta functions. Hence, we have for the amplitude

W(A,\zeta)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta-\phi)^{2}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{i\pi\gamma(\zeta-\phi)}{2t},\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{t}}\right)

(B.7)

If we approximate
\vartheta_{3}
with
1
we obtain

W(A,\zeta)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4t}(\zeta-\phi)^{2}}

(B.8)

As we will see (
B.8
) is good an approximation to (
B.7
) in our setting. First, note that the two expressions have a significant qualitative difference: the former is periodic in
\zeta
while the latter is not. We can read off the periodicity directly from the left hand side of (
B.5
):

\textsf{e}^{i\gamma(\zeta-\phi)s}\Rightarrow\text{The period is }\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}.

(B.9)

Let’s examine carefully this periodicity. Since
\textsf{e}^{-ts^{2}}
is always positive, we see that the maxima of the sum are located at

\zeta_{k}=\phi+\frac{2\pi k}{\gamma}\quad,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\,\,\phi\in[0,2\pi).

(B.10)

The value of the maxima is then given by

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\textsf{e}^{\frac{-k^{2}\pi^{2}}{t}}\vartheta_{3}\left(-\frac{ik\pi^{2}}{t},\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{t}}\right)\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\vartheta_{3}\left(0,\textsf{e}^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{t}}\right).

(B.11)

In general we will have
K=\left\lfloor 2\gamma\right\rfloor
full periods in the interval
\zeta\in[0,4\pi)
and
M=1+\left\lfloor\gamma\left(2-\frac{\phi}{2\pi}\right)\right\rfloor
maxima.
Now, we can exploit the freedom in restricting the value of the parameter
\gamma
. Since
\zeta\in[0,4\pi)
we find from
\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}\geq 4\pi
that for
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
the periodicity of
W(A,\zeta)
is not at all a problem. There will be less than one period and exactly one maximum in the interval
[0,4\pi)
.

For completeness, we also note that
\phi
essentially just moves around the maxima along the
\zeta
-axis, while
t
determines their height and the spread of the Gaussians, as can be seen from (
B.11
). It is also easy to see that the imaginary part of (
B.5
) is exactly zero and that the real part is larger or equal to zero for all values of
\zeta,\phi
and
t
.

In summary, when
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
we can safely use (
B.8
) instead of the more complicated result (
B.7
). This is consistent with the fixing of the value of
\gamma
that comes from calculating Black Hole entropy using LQG
Ashtekar et al. 1998
;
Ashtekar et al. 2005
;
Meissner 2004
.

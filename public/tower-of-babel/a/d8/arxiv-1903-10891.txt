On Star critical Ramsey numbers related to large cycles versus complete graphs
Chula J. Jayawardene

Abstract
Let K n K_{n} denote the complete graph on n n vertices and G , H G,H be finite graphs without loops or multiple edges. Consider a two-coloring of edges of K n K_{n} . When a copy of G G in the first color, red, or a copy of H H in the second color, blue is in K n K_{n} , we write K n → ( G , H ) K_{n}\rightarrow(G,H) . The Ramsey number r ⁡ ( G , H ) r(G,H) is defined as the smallest positive integer n n such that K n → ( G , H ) K_{n}\rightarrow(G,H) . Star critical Ramsey r ∗ ​ ( G , H ) r_{*}(G,H) is defined as the largest value of k k such that K r ⁡ ( G , H ) − 1 ⊔ K 1 , k → ( G , H ) K_{r(G,H)-1}\sqcup K_{1,k}\rightarrow(G,H) . In this paper, we find r ∗ ​ ( C n , K m ) r_{*}(C_{n},K_{m}) for m ≥ 7 m\geq 7 and n ≥ ( m − 3 ) ​ ( m − 1 ) n\geq(m-3)(m-1) .

Notation

For ease of reference, we borrow the notation used in
[

8
,

. Given a graph

. Given a graph

\Gamma(v)
, i.e.

. We write

for
\Gamma(v)\cup\{v\}
. The minimum degree of a graph
G(V,E)
, denoted by
\delta(G)
, is defined as
\min\{d(v)|v\in V\}
. Given a graph
G
and a non-empty subset
S
of
V
,
the induced subgraph of
S
in
G
denoted by

is defined as the subgraph obtained by deleting all the vertices of
S^{c}
from
G
. Moreover,
G\setminus S
is defined as
G[V(G)\setminus S]
. Given a graph
G
and two disjoint subgraphs

and

of

, we denote the set of edges between

and

by

and define

as the subgraph of

obtained by deleting all the edges of

from

.

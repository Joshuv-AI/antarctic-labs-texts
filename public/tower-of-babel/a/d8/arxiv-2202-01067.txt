A generalization of Kannan and Fisher fixed point theorems with an application to Volterra type integral equations
Rivu Bardhan, Cenep Özel, Liliana Guran

Abstract
In this paper, we discuss about the independent types of infinite extensions to a general version of Kannan [ 5 ] and Fisher [ 3 ] of which the well-known Kannan and Fisher theorems come as a corollaries. We also provide a strong connection between continuous Kannan operator and Fisher operator in a restricted type of metric space. We also provide an application of the main theorem of this paper, in the field of integral equations.



Keywords:
Fixed point, Geraghty’s theorem, Fisher’s theorem, Kannan’s theorem, complete metric space, infinite dimension.

AMS Subject classification:
47H10, 54H25.

1
Introduction and Preliminaries

The fixed point theory is an important tool of nonlinear analysis very useful in the proof of the existence of a solution of different types of equations. Banach has given in 1922 the fundamental theorem of contraction operators (see
[
2
]
) starting a new research direction. Later, Kannan and Fisher have given, totaly independent, two type of contractions, which leads to a unique fixed point for the operators. But, it is not hard to notice that the two contractions defined in
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
has a similar pattern.

Recently, in the paper of M. Alshumrani, H. Aydi, S. Hazra, C. Özel (see
[
1
]
) introduced the notion of
\eta
-dimensional extension of the Geraghty’s result, which give a new way to extend the Banach’s fixed point result.

Let us recall further, some results given in related literature, useful in the development of our new results.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
4
]
) Let
(\overline{X},d)
be a complete metric space and
\mathcal{U}:\overline{X}\to\overline{X}
be a
mapping. If
\mathcal{U}
satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{U}\overline{x},\mathcal{U}\overline{y})\leq\beta(d(\overline{x},\overline{y}))d(\overline{x},\overline{y})

where
\beta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1)
is a function which satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\beta(t_{n})=1~implies\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=0.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
5
]
) Let
(\overline{X},d)
be a complete metric space and
\mathcal{U}:\overline{X}\to\overline{X}
be a
mapping. If
\mathcal{U}
satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{U}\overline{x},\mathcal{U}\overline{y})\leq c(d(\mathcal{U}\overline{x},\overline{x})+d(\mathcal{U}\overline{y},\overline{y}))

where

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
3

(\overline{X},d)
be a complete metric space and
\mathcal{U}:\overline{X}\to\overline{X}
be a

\mathcal{U}

Theorem 1.4
.

(
[
1
]
) Let
(\overline{X},d)
be a complete metric space and
\eta\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{U}:\overline{X}^{\eta}\to\overline{X}

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{U}(\overline{w}_{1},...,\overline{w}_{\eta}),\mathcal{U}(\overline{w}_{2},...,\overline{w}_{\eta+1}))

We denote by

The aim of this paper is to generalize and extend Theorem
[

and Theorem
[
5

An application of our main result is also presented in the last part of the paper, to prove the existence and the uniqueness of a solution of a Volterra integral type equation.

2
Main Results

In this section, we introduce some new different classes of generalized contraction mappings. Also, we discuss about some sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of fixed points for these new classes of mappings. Then, we generalize the notions of Gergathy function and Kannan function, which would be key tools in proving our new theorems. Moreover, to strengthen this new notions we provide some illustrative examples.

Throughout our paper, (unless otherwise stated) we assume that
\left(X,d\right)
is a
complete

metric

space
and
\eta\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax,\widehat{n+\eta}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax+1,\widehat{n+\eta+1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{T_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n(p)+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n(p)+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{x}_{m(p)+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{m(p)+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=0.

Therefore,
2\epsilon_{0}=0~\Rightarrow\epsilon_{0}=0.
Contradiction. Therefore,
\{\overline{x}_{n+\eta}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence.

Since,
\overline{X}
is a complete metric space, there exists
\overline{w}\in\overline{X}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\overline{x}_{n+\eta}=\overline{w}.

Next, we show that
\overline{w}
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{U}
. We have,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha B_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)+2\gamma K_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)+2\delta F_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

Now,

\displaystyle B_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\overline{x}_{n+\eta-1},\overline{w}\right)

Therefore,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=0

and

\displaystyle K_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{x}_{n+\eta-1}\right)+d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\overline{x}_{n+\eta},\overline{x}_{n+\eta-1}\right)+d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right).

Therefore,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}K_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right).

Next, we show that,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right).

\displaystyle F_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)+d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{x}_{n+\eta-1}\right)

\text{i.e., }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\geq d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)

Thus,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{\eta}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right).

Again,

\displaystyle d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)\leq d\left(\overline{x}_{n+\eta},\overline{w}\right)+d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq d\left(\overline{x}_{n+\eta},\overline{w}\right)+\beta\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)\geq 1\geq\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{x}_{n+\char 16\relax-1,\widehat{n+\eta-1}}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=0

\displaystyle d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)=0;\text{ i.e., }\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)=\overline{w}.

is unique. Let us suppose, on the contrary, that
T

\displaystyle\leq\beta\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{v}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)\right)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{U}_{\eta}}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty},\left(\overline{v}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\left[\alpha\{d\left(\overline{w},\overline{v}\right)+2\gamma\{d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{w}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{w}\right)+d\left(\mathcal{U}\left(\left(\overline{v}\right)_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}\right),\overline{v}\right)\}\right.

Contradiction. Therefore,

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Application to Volterra type integral equations

The Volterra integral equation was introduced by Vito Volterra in the end of
19^{th}
century. Later, at beginning of the
20^{th}
century, the Romanian mathematician Traian Lalescu studied in his PhD thesis this type of equations, under the supervision of Émile Picard. Volterra integral equations have many applications in different domains as demography, economic processes, physics and astrophysics. Then, the aim of our section is to put in evidence the fixed point method for proving the existence of a solution for a Volterra type integral equation.

Let us recall the usual Volterra integral equation of first kind as

\overline{w}(t)=\lambda\int_{a}^{t}\mathbb{K}(t,\overline{w}(s))ds,

where
\lambda
is a non-zero real and
\mathbb{K}:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a real valued continuous function. We generalise in this way the notion of usual Volterra integral equation.

We know that
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}
is the countable product of
\mathbb{R}
. We restricted this set and define
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}
as,

\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}=\{\overline{w}:\overline{w}=(\overline{w}_{1},\overline{w}_{2},...,\overline{w}_{\eta},\overline{w}_{\eta},...)\};

for some
\eta\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R},
then
D=[a,b]\times[a,b]\times[a,b]\times...
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}[a,b]
denote the set of all continuous function on
[a,b].
Then,
d:\mathcal{C}[a,b]\times\mathcal{C}[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be defined by

\displaystyle d\left(f(\overline{x}),g(\overline{x})\right)=\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\left|f(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x})\right|e^{-m\overline{x}}

(5)

for all
f(\overline{x}),~g(\overline{x})\in\mathcal{C}[a,b].
So,
(\mathcal{C}[a,b],d)
is a complete metric space.

Let us define a integral equation, where the unknown function is an infinite-tuple of the special form (element of
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}
) as follows

\displaystyle(\overline{w}_{i,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)=\lambda\int_{a}^{t}\mathbb{K}(t,(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(s))ds;

(6)

where
\mathbb{K}:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.

In the section, we assured the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the integral equation (
6
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and
f\in\mathbb{G}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. If the given condition holds:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)-\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq f\left(L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)e^{-mt}\right)L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)

where,

\displaystyle L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\{\left|\overline{w}_{\eta}(t)-\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))dt\right|+\left|\overline{v}_{\eta}(t)-\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))dt\right|\right\}+

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\left\{\left|\overline{w}_{\eta}(t)-\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))dt\right|+\left|\overline{v}_{\eta}(t)-\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))dt\right|\right\};

for some
\alpha\in[0,1];

then, the integral equation (
6
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

In order to find a solution of the integral equation (
6
) we have to transform the problem into an equivalent fixed point problem.

Let us define a function
T:\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}[a,b]
by,

\mathcal{U}\left((\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(\overline{x})\right)=\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)dt;

where
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(D)
is considered as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}_{\eta}.

Therefore, we have to find the fixed point of
\mathcal{U}
.

Now, using (
5
), we have,

\displaystyle d\left(\mathcal{U}\left((\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(\overline{x})\right),\mathcal{U}\left((\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(\overline{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\left|\mathcal{U}\left((\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(\overline{x})\right)-\mathcal{U}\left((\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(\overline{x})\right)\right|e^{-m\overline{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\left|\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)dt-\lambda\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)dt\right|e^{-m\overline{x}}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\left|\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)dt-\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)dt\right|e^{-m\overline{x}}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\left|\mathbb{K}\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)-K\left(\overline{x},(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t)\right)\right|dt~e^{-m\overline{x}}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\sup_{\overline{x}\in[a,b]}\int_{a}^{\overline{x}}\leq f\left(L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{i,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)e^{-mt}\right)L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)e^{-m\overline{x}}.

We have,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&L\left(\overline{x},(\overline{w}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t),(\overline{v}_{\char 16\relax,\hat{\eta}})_{\char 16\relax=1}^{\infty}(t))\right)e^{-m\overline{x}}\\

Conclusions

In this paper we considered some metric hypothesis can be applied to extend the Banach, Kannan and Fisher’s theorem in its most general setup possible. We used this new idea to find some infinite extensions of the other type of contractions and its related operators to prove the existence of fixed points. There are some open questions which arise from these extensions.

1.

If we consider uncountable product of the complete metric space
(X,d)
the resultant space
\Pi_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is not a metric space. Can we prove a generalizations of the above results in these type of topological spaces, which can be expressed as uncountable product of complete metric space?

2.

Can all the results be proved for the notion of coincidence points?

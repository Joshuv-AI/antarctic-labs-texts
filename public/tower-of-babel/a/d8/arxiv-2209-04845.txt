Shokurov’s index conjecture for quotient singularities
Yusuke Nakamura, Kohsuke Shibata

Abstract
. We prove Shokurov’s index conjecture for quotient singularities.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14E18; Secondary 14E30, 14B05

1.
Introduction

Shokurov conjectured that the Gorenstein index of a

\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein germ can be bounded in terms of its dimension and minimal log discrepancy.

Conjecture 1.1

(Shokurov, cf.
[
12
]
*Question 5.2)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, there exists a positive integer
r(n,a)
with the following condition.

•

If an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
and a closed point
p\in X
satisfy

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)=a
,
then the Cartier index of
K_{X}
at
p
is at most
r(n,a)
.

Conjecture
1.1
may be useful to attack the ACC conjecture (see
[
23
]
)
since the ACC conjecture is known for varieties with a fixed Gorenstein index
and boundaries with fixed coefficients (
[
20
]
).
Conjecture
1.1
is known to be true in the following cases:

(
1.1
.1)

When
(n,a)=(2,0)
, Conjecture
1.1
can be confirmed
by the classification of surface singularities (cf.
[
22
]
*Corollary 5.10).

(
1.1
.2)

When
n=2
, Conjecture
1.1
is proved by G. Chen and J. Han
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.4.
They proved Conjecture
1.1
for pairs (see
[
4
]
*Conjecture 6.3 for the generalized formulation).

(
1.1
.3)

When
(n,a)=(3,0)
, Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Ishii in
[
10
]
and Fujino in
[
7
]
.
Fujino in
[
7
]
also proved that Conjecture
1.1
for
a=0
and arbitrary
n
assuming
another conjecture on some boundedness of the birational automorphisms (see
[
7
]
*Theorem 0.2 for the detail).

(
1.1
.4)

When the considered
X
is a terminal threefold,
Conjecture
1.1
follows from the classification by Kawamata
[
14
]
.
J. Han, J. Liu, and Y. Luo in
[
8
]
*Theorem 1.5 proved Conjecture
1.1
for three-dimensional terminal pairs.

(
1.1
.5)

When the considered
X
is a canonical threefold,
Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Kawakita
[
12
]
.

(
1.1
.6)

When the considered
X
has toric singularities,
Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Ambro
[
2
]
(cf.
[
3
]
*Section 5.4).

(
1.1
.7)

When the considered
X
has only quotient singularity, Moraga
[
17
]
shows the existence of
\epsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(for each
n
)
such that Conjecture
1.1
holds for
(n,a)=(n,\epsilon_{n})
.

The following theorem is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(
=
Theorem
3.4
)
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds for quotient singularities.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to use Jordan’s theorem (Theorem
3.2
)
on finite linear groups and reduce it to the abelian quotient case,
where Conjecture
1.1
is proved by Ambro (see (
1.1
.6) above).
This idea is due to Moraga
[
17
]
.

In Section
4
, we prove Shokurov’s Gorensteiness conjecture for quotient singularities.
This conjecture states that we can take
r(n,n-1)=1
in Conjecture
1.1
.

Notation

•

We basically follow the standard notions and terminologies in
[
9
]
and
[
16
]
.

•

Throughout this paper,
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
We say that
X
is a
variety
if

X
is an integral scheme that is separated and of finite type over
k
.

2.
Preriminaries

First, we clarify the definition of quotient singularities in this paper.
For a local ring
A
, let
\widehat{A}
denote the completion of
A
at the maximal ideal.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
n
and let
p\in X
be a closed point.
We say that
X
has a
quotient singularity
at
p
if
there exists a finite subgroup
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M,x_{0}}
, where

M=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
is the quotient variety obtained by the natural linear action of
G
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
,
and
x_{0}\in M
is the image of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.

Next, we recall the definition of the Gorenstein indices and the minimal log discrepancies for
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein normal singularities.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
p\in X
be a closed point.
Suppose that
X
has a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularity at
p
(i.e.
rK_{X}
is Cartier at
p
for some positive integer
r
).

(1)

The
Gorenstein index
of
X
at
p
is the smallest positive integer
r
such that
rK_{X}
is Cartier at
p
.

(2)

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
and let
E
be a prime divisor on
X^{\prime}
.
Then the
log discrepancy

a_{E}(X)
is defined by

a_{E}(X):=1+\operatorname{ord}_{E}(K_{X^{\prime}/X}),

where
K_{X^{\prime}/X}:=K_{X^{\prime}}-f^{*}K_{X}
is the relative canonical divisor.
We define
the
minimal log discrepancy

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)
at
p
by

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\{p\}}a_{E}(X)

if
\dim X\geq 2
, where the infimum is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
X
with center
c_{X}(E)=\{p\}
.
When
\dim X=1
, we define
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\{p\}}a_{E}(X)
if
\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\{p\}}a_{E}(X)\geq 0
, and
we define
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X):=-\infty
otherwise.
It is known that
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\}
in general (cf.
[
15
]
*Corollary 2.31).

For a finite group
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
and
g\in G
, we define
d(G)
and
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)
as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
let
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
be a finite subgroup.
Let
d:=\#G
be the order of
G
, and let
\xi\in k
be a primitive
d
-th root of unity.

(1)

We define a positive integer
d(G)
by

d(G):=\min\bigl\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ \big|\ \text{$(\det(g))^{\ell}=1$ holds for any $g\in G$}\bigr\}.

(2)

Let
g\in G
.
Since
g
has finite order,
g
is conjugate to a diagonal matrix
\operatorname{diag}(\xi^{e_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{e_{n}})

with
1\leq e_{i}\leq d
.
Then, we define
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e_{i}}{d}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The notation
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)
defined in Definition
2.3
(2) is not usually used outside of this paper.
A prime
′
is added to distinguish it from the usual age (denoted by
\operatorname{age}(g)
) defined in some literatures (cf.
[
16
]
*Definition 3.20).
Note that we have the relation
\operatorname{age}(g)=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)-\#\{1\leq i\leq n\mid e_{i}=d\}
.

Remark 2.5
.

In Theorem
2.6
and Proposition
2.8
below, we assume that
G
does not contain a pseudo-reflection.
In this remark, we will explain that this assumption is not harmful in applications.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
let
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
be a finite subgroup.
Let
G_{\rm pr}\leqslant G
be the subgroup generated by the pseudo-reflections in
G
.
Then, we have
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/(G/G_{\rm pr})
by the Chevalley-Shephard-Todd Theorem (cf.
[
16
]
*Subsection 3.18).
Therefore, when considering a quotient variety
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
, we may assume that
G
contains no pseudo-reflection.

In
[
24
]
, Weston proved that the Gorenstein index of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
at
the image
x_{0}
of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
coincides with
d(G)
when
G

has no pseudo-reflections.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
24
]
*Theorem 2.2)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
let
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
be a finite subgroup.
Suppose that
G
does not contain a pseudo-reflection.
Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
be the image of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.
Then,
d(G)
coincides with the Gorenstein index of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
at
x_{0}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Weston’s theorem
[
24
]
*Theorem 2.2 treats a general setting,
where the finite group
G
may contain a pseudo-reflection.
Note that in the notation in
[
24
]
*Hypotheses 2.1,
we have
d_{ij}=1
and
g^{\prime}_{j}=g_{j}
for any
i
and
j
when
G
does not contain a pseudo-reflection.

The minimal log discrepancy of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
at
x_{0}
can be described by
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
n
,
G
and
x_{0}
be as in Theorem
2.6
.
Then it follows that

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\min\{\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)\mid g\in G\}.

Proof.

First, note that this formula is well-known when
G
is an abelian group (see Remark
2.9
).

For
\gamma\in G
, let
\langle\gamma\rangle\leqslant G
denote the subgroup generated by
\gamma
, and
let
x_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/\langle\gamma\rangle
denote the image of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.
Then, by
[
19
]
*Corollary 4.12 (see also
[
19
]
*Remark 4.13), we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\min_{\gamma\in G}\operatorname{mld}_{x_{\gamma}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/\langle\gamma\rangle\bigr).

Therefore, the assertion follows from the cyclic quotient case.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

When
G
is an abelian group, Proposition
2.8
seems to be well-known to experts.
However, since we could not find a good reference, we give a proof below for the reader’s convenience.
We refer to
[
5
]
for the standard notions on toric varieties.

Suppose that
G
in Proposition
2.8
is an abelian group.
Since
G
is a finite abelian group, its elements are simultaneously diagonalizable.
Hence, we may assume that
G
consists of diagonal matrices from the beginning.
Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\xi\in k
be as in Definition
2.3
.
Each
g\in G
can be written as
g=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\xi^{e_{1}^{(g)}},\ldots,\xi^{e_{n}^{(g)}}\bigr)
.
We define
u^{(g)}:=\bigl(e_{1}^{(g)}/d,\ldots,e_{n}^{(g)}/d\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
define a lattice
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

N:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\sum_{g\in G}\mathbb{Z}u^{(g)}
.
Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the standard basis
(i.e.
u_{i}
is the vector with
1
in the
i
-th coordinate and
0
’s elsewhere).
Let
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}=N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the cone spanned by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
.
Let
U_{\sigma,N}
denote the toric variety corresponding to
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then we have
U_{\sigma,N}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
(cf.
[
5
]
*Example 1.3.20).

Since
G
does not contain a pseudo-reflection,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in N
are primitive vectors
(i.e. there are no integer
\ell\geq 2
and
u\in N
satisfying
u_{i}=\ell u
).
Therefore, the support function
\varphi:\sigma\to\mathbb{R}
of the
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
K_{U_{\sigma,N}}

is given by the function satisfying
\varphi((a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\sum_{i}a_{i}
for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Furthermore,
we have the one-to-one correspondence between the primitive vectors
u
in
\operatorname{int}(\sigma)\cap N
and
the torus invariant divisors
D_{u}
over
U_{\sigma,N}
whose centers are the torus invariant closed point.
Then, we have
a_{D_{u}}(U_{\sigma,N})=\varphi(u)
by
[
5
]
*Lemma 11.4.10.
Therefore, we get

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\min_{u\in\operatorname{int}(\sigma)\cap N}\varphi(u)=\min_{u\in(0,1]^{n}\cap N}\varphi(u),

and the right hand side is equal to
\min\{\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)\mid g\in G\}
since
we have
\varphi(u^{(g)})=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)
for
g\in G
.

Remark 2.10
.

In the same setting as in Proposition
2.8
, it is known that

\operatorname{mld}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\min\{\operatorname{age}(g)\mid g\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}\},

where
\operatorname{mld}(X):=\inf_{E}a_{E}(X)
is the total log discrepancy
(see Remark
2.4
for the difference between
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)
and
\operatorname{age}(g)
).
This formula is proved by Yasuda
[
25
]
using the motivic integration method.
Proposition
2.8
can also be directly proved in a similar way without reducing the abelian quotient case.
Indeed, it follows from
[
19
]
*Theorem 4.8 or
[
18
]
*Theorem 7.9.

3.
Shokurov’s index conjecture for quotient singularities

In this section, we prove Shokurov’s index conjecture for quotient singularities.
First, note that Ambro in
[
2
]
proved
this conjecture for toric varieties, in particular for abelian quotient singularities.

Theorem 3.1

(Ambro
[
2
]
)
.

Conjecture
1.1
is true for toric singularities,
in particular for abelian quotient singularities.

We use Jordan’s theorem on finite linear groups.

Theorem 3.2

(Jordan’s theorem on finite linear groups
[
11
]
)
.

For a positive integer
n
,
there exists a positive integer
c_{n}
with the following condition:

•

Suppose that
K
is a field of characteristic zero and that

G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
is a finite subgroup.
Then, there exists an abelian normal subgroup
H\leqslant G

such that its index satisfies
[G:H]\leq c_{n}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Although Jordan’s theorem is usually asserted for
K=\mathbb{C}
,
it holds for any field
K
of characteristic zero with the same bound
c_{n}
.
Since
G
is a finite subgroup,
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K^{\prime})
holds for some subfield
K^{\prime}\subset K
with
\#K^{\prime}=\aleph_{0}
.
Since such
K^{\prime}
can be embedded into
\mathbb{C}
, the assertion follows from the case where
K=\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Conjecture
1.1
is true for quotient singularities.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
Suppose that a variety
X
of dimension
n
has a quotient singularity at a closed point
p\in X
.
Furthermore, assume
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)=a
.
By the definition of quotient singularities (Definition
2.1
),
there exists a finite subgroup
G\leqslant\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{M,x_{0}}
, where

M=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
is the quotient variety and
x_{0}\in M
is the image of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.
Let
G_{\rm pr}\leqslant G
be the subgroup generated by the pseudo-reflections in
G
.
Then, by replacing
G
with
G/G_{\rm pr}
, we may assume that
G
does not contain a pseudo-reflection (cf.
[
16
]
*3.18).
Note that the minimal log discrepancy is preserved under completion (cf.
[
18
]
*Remark 3.1).
Furthermore, the Gorenstein index is also preserved under completion (cf.
[
6
]
*Proposition A.14).
Therefore, we may assume
X=M=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
and
p=x_{0}
from the beginning.

By Jordan’s theorem (Theorem
3.2
), we can take an abelian normal subgroup
H\leqslant G
such that
[G:H]\leq c_{n}
.
By Proposition
2.8
, we can take
g\in G
and
h\in H
such that

a=\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g),\quad\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/H\bigr)=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(h).

We set
c^{\prime}:=c_{n}!
and

h^{\prime}:=h\cdot(ghg^{-1})\cdot(g^{2}hg^{-2})\cdots(g^{c^{\prime}-1}hg^{-(c^{\prime}-1)}).

Since
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we have
g^{i}hg^{-i}\in H
for any
i\geq 0
.
Furthermore, since
[G:H]\leq c_{n}
, we have
g^{c^{\prime}}\in H
, and hence
g^{c^{\prime}}hg^{-c^{\prime}}=g^{c^{\prime}}g^{-c^{\prime}}h=h
.
Therefore, we have

gh^{\prime}g^{-1}=(ghg^{-1})\cdot(g^{2}hg^{-2})\cdots(g^{c^{\prime}-1}hg^{-(c^{\prime}-1)})\cdot h=h^{\prime}.

Let
\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle\leqslant G
denote the subgroup generated by
g
and
h^{\prime}
.
Since we have

\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)=\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G\bigr)=\min_{\alpha\in G}\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(\alpha)\leq\min_{\alpha\in\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle}\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(\alpha)\leq\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g),

it follows that

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle\bigr)=\min_{\alpha\in\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle}\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(\alpha)=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)=a.

Since
\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle
is an abelian group, by Theorem
3.1
,
we can conclude that
d(\langle g,h^{\prime}\rangle)
divides
r^{\prime}_{1}(n,a)
for some positive integer
r^{\prime}_{1}(n,a)
depending only on
n
and
a
.
Since
\det(h^{\prime})=(\det(h))^{c^{\prime}}
, the order of
\det(h)
divides
c_{n}!\cdot r^{\prime}_{1}(n,a)
.
In particular,
\operatorname{mld}_{x_{0}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/H\bigr)=\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(h)

is contained in the finite set
\frac{1}{c_{n}!\cdot r^{\prime}_{1}(n,a)}\mathbb{Z}\cap(0,n]
.
By applying Theorem
3.1
to
H
, we can conclude that

d(H)
divides
r^{\prime}_{2}(n,a)
for some positive integer
r^{\prime}_{2}(n,a)
depending only on
n
and
a
.
Since
[G:H]\leq c_{n}
, we have
\alpha^{c_{n}!}\in H
for any
\alpha\in G
.
Therefore,
d(G)
divides
c_{n}!\cdot d(H)
, and in particular,
d(G)
divides
c_{n}!\cdot r^{\prime}_{2}(n,a)
.
We complete the proof.
∎

4.
Shokurov’s Gorensteinness conjecture for quotient singularities

In this section,
we prove Shokurov’s Gorensteinness conjecture (Conjecture
4.1
(3)) for quotient singularities.
This conjecture states that we can take
r(n,n-1)=1
in Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 4.1

(Shokurov
[
21
]
*Propblem 5)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety,
and let
p\in X
be a closed point.
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

We have
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)\leq n
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)>n-1
, then
X
is smooth at
p
.

(3)

If
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)=n-1
, then
X
is Gorenstein at
p
.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
1
]
, Ambro proved Conjecture
4.1
(1) and (2) for toric singularities
(see Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 in
[
1
]
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Conjecture
4.1
is true when
X
has a quotient singularity at
p
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X
has a quotient singularity at
p
.
In the same way as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
,
we may assume that
X
is of the form
X=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}/G
,
where
G
is a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(k)
,
and
p
is the image
x_{0}
of the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
.
We may also assume that
G
does not contain pseudo-reflections.

Conjecture
4.1
(1) easily follows from Proposition
2.8
.

Suppose that
X
is singular at
p
.
Then, it follows that
G\not=\{1_{G}\}
since
G
does not contain pseudo-reflections.
Take any
g\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
.
Let
d
be the order of
g
, and let
\xi\in k
be a primitive
d
-th root of unity.
Suppose that
g
is conjugate to the diagonal matrix

\operatorname{diag}(\xi^{e_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{e_{n}})
with
1\leq e_{i}\leq d
.
We may assume that
e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{n}
.
Let
\ell
be the maximum integer satisfying
e_{\ell}<d
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{1}}{d}+\cdots+\frac{e_{\ell}}{d}+n-\ell,

\displaystyle\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{e_{1}}{d}\right)+\cdots+\left(1-\frac{e_{\ell}}{d}\right)+n-\ell.

Note that we have
\ell\geq 2
since
g
is not a pseudo-reflection.
By Proposition
2.8
, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)\leq\min\bigl\{\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g),\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g^{-1})\bigr\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)+\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g^{-1})\bigr)=n-\frac{\ell}{2}\leq n-1.

Therefore, Conjecture
4.1
(2) is proved.

Suppose
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(X)=n-1
.
Then, by the inequalities above,
it follows that
\operatorname{age}^{\prime}(g)=n-1
for any
g\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
.
Therefore,
K_{X}
is Cartier at
p
by Theorem
2.6
.
Since quotient singularities are always Cohen-Macaulay,

X
is Gorenstein at
p
. Hence, Conjecture
4.1
(3) is proved.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Conjecture
4.1
(3) is true for toric singularities.

Proof.

Let
M:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and let
N:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
be its dual lattice.
Let
X=U_{\sigma}
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein affine toric variety
corresponding to a strongly rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
p\in U_{\sigma}
be a closed point.
For a face
\sigma^{\prime}
of
\sigma
,
O(\sigma^{\prime})
denotes the torus orbit corresponding to
\sigma^{\prime}
(cf.
[
5
]
*Theorem 3.2.6).
Let
\sigma_{p}
be the face of
\sigma
corresponding to the torus orbit
T_{N}\cdot p
of
p
(i.e.
T_{N}\cdot p=O(\sigma_{p})
).
Then,
U_{\sigma_{p}}
is an open subset of
U_{\sigma}
containing
p
.
Therefore, replacing
\sigma
with
\sigma_{p}
, we may assume that
T_{N}\cdot p=O(\sigma)
from the beginning.

Suppose that
\sigma
is generated by primitive vectors
u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\in N
.
Since
U_{\sigma}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, the support function
\varphi:\sigma\to\mathbb{R}
of
K_{U_{\sigma}}
is given by

\varphi(u)=\langle m,u\rangle
for some
m\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
c:=\dim\sigma
, and let
\tau\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the cone generated by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{c}\in N
.
Since
\tau
is a simplicial cone,
the corresponding toric variety
U_{\tau}
has abelian finite quotient singularities (cf.
[
5
]
*Theorem 11.4.8).
Furthermore, the same
m\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}
gives the support function of
K_{U_{\tau}}
.
Since
T_{N}\cdot p=O(\sigma)
, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(U_{\sigma})=\dim O(\sigma)+\operatorname{mld}_{\eta_{O(\sigma)}}(U_{\sigma})=n-c+\min_{u\in\operatorname{int}(\sigma)\cap N}\langle m,u\rangle

by
[
1
]
*Proposition 2.1.
Here,
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta_{O(\sigma)}}(U_{\sigma})
is defined as
\inf_{c_{U_{\sigma}}(E)=\overline{O(\sigma)}}a_{E}(X)
,
where the infimum is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
U_{\sigma}
with center
c_{U_{\sigma}}(E)=\overline{O(\sigma)}
.
Similarly, for any
x_{\tau}\in O(\tau)\subset U_{\tau}
, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x_{\tau}}(U_{\tau})=n-c+\min_{u\in\operatorname{int}(\tau)\cap N}\langle m,u\rangle.

Since
\operatorname{int}(\tau)\subset\operatorname{int}(\sigma)
, we conclude

\operatorname{mld}_{p}(U_{\sigma})\leq\operatorname{mld}_{x_{\tau}}(U_{\tau}).

Suppose
\operatorname{mld}_{p}(U_{\sigma})=n-1
.
Then, we have
\operatorname{mld}_{x_{\tau}}(U_{\tau})\geq n-1
for any
x_{\tau}\in O(\tau)
.
Therefore, by Theorem
4.3
,
K_{U_{\tau}}
is Cartier at any
x_{\tau}\in O(\tau)
.
Since
U_{\tau}
is the only torus invariant open subset of
U_{\tau}
containing
O(\tau)
, we conclude that

K_{U_{\tau}}
is a Cartier divisor on
U_{\tau}
.
Hence, there exists
m^{\prime}\in M
such that
m^{\prime}
gives the support function of
K_{U_{\tau}}
.
Since
m-m^{\prime}\in\tau^{\perp}\cap M_{\mathbb{Q}}=\sigma^{\perp}\cap M_{\mathbb{Q}}
(cf.
[
5
]
*Theorem 4.2.8),

m^{\prime}
also gives the support function of
K_{U_{\sigma}}
.
This proves that
K_{U_{\sigma}}
is also a Cartier divisor.
Since toric varieties are always Cohen-Macaulay,
we conclude that
U_{\sigma}
is also Gorenstein.
∎

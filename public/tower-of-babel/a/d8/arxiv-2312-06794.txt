Superinvariants Below Critical Loop Order
Renata Kallosh

Abstract
Investigation of on-shell superinvariants is the standard way to identify the candidate UV divergences in supergravity. Geometric on-shell superinvariants in 4d supergravity at 𝒩 ≥ 4 \mathcal{N}\geq 4 are available starting at the critical loop order L = 𝒩 L=\mathcal{N} Kallosh:1980fi ; Howe:1980th . However, more than 10 years ago, it was conjectured that the UV divergencies in supergravity might appear earlier, for L = 𝒩 − 1 L=\mathcal{N}-1 , due to the existence of harmonic superspace superinvariants based on Grassmann analytic superfields Bossard:2011tq . Here we show that these harmonic superinvariants at the nonlinear level are inconsistent, because Grassmann analyticity breaks local ℋ \mathcal{H} symmetry of the 𝒩 ≥ 4 \mathcal{N}\geq 4 on-shell superspace. Therefore, the “puzzling enhanced cancellation” of UV divergences for L = 𝒩 − 1 L=\mathcal{N}-1 in superamplitude calculations for L = 3 , 𝒩 = 4 L=3,\,\mathcal{N}=4 and L = 4 , 𝒩 = 5 L=4,\,\mathcal{N}=5 is explained by nonlinear local supersymmetry consistent with unbroken local ℋ \mathcal{H} symmetry.

\mathcal{N}\geq 4
.

To summarize, the inconsistency with local
U(\mathcal{N})
symmetry in
Bossard:2011tq
is that, on one hand, this local symmetry is claimed to be not gauge-fixed. But actually, harmonic coordinates are treated by analogy with harmonic superspace coordinates
Galperin:2001seg
or projective superspace coordinates in
\mathcal{N}=2
case. For example, in
Kuzenko:2008ep
;
Kuzenko:2008ry
, whose strategy is used in
Bossard:2011tq
, it is explained that the
u_{i}^{+}
serve merely to totally symmetrize all
SU(2)
indices
i
. Also, they present the explicit dependence of
u^{+}{}_{i}
on two
{SU(2)\over U(1)}
coset coordinates but only after the local
SU(2)
symmetry was gauge-fixed in the coset directions. And since there was no
\mathcal{G}
duality symmetry, no compensating field dependent
SU(2)
was required. It allowed a consistent construction of the harmonic or projective superspace in
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity. This, however, is not possible in
\mathcal{N}\geq 4
supergravities.

It is not surprising therefore that 12 graphs in
\mathcal{N}=4
,
L=3

Bern:2012cd
and
82 diagrams in
\mathcal{N}=5
,
L=4
cancelled
Bern:2014sna
: the
L=\mathcal{N}-1
harmonic superinvariants in
Bossard:2011tq
break local
U(\mathcal{N})
symmetry and, as a result of the algebra (

7
Discussion

In 4d supergravity at
\mathcal{N}\geq 4
the critical loop order
L_{cr}={\mathcal{N}}
is the loop order where the geometric superinvariants
Kallosh:1980fi
;
Howe:1980th
have both local
\mathcal{H}
symmetry and global duality
\mathcal{G}
symmetry of the relevant on-shell superspace. Any subspace of the whole superspace with less fermionic directions and no additional coordinates, like harmonic ones, breaks the local
\mathcal{H}
symmetry of the on-shell superspace.

In 4d
\mathcal{N}\geq 4
supergravity, an attempt was made in
Bossard:2011tq
to construct a harmonic on-shell superspace with the property that there are
{1\over\mathcal{N}}
BPS superinvariants built in a subspace of the whole superspace, where the number of fermionic directions instead of
4\mathcal{N}
is equal to
4(\mathcal{N}-1)
.
This corresponds to candidate counterterms at
L=L_{cr}-1=\mathcal{N}-1
.

In an on-shell harmonic superspace
Bossard:2011tq
, the integrability condition for the existence of Grassman analytic superfields, i.e., superfields independent on some of the fermionic directions,
requires that harmonic superspace curvature components in a coset direction
{H\backslash\mathcal{H}}

to vanish, namely

(R_{\hat{\alpha}\,\hat{\beta}})^{1}{}_{r}=(R_{\hat{\alpha}\,\hat{\beta}})^{r}{}_{\mathcal{N}}=(R_{\hat{\alpha}\,\hat{\beta}})^{1}{}_{\mathcal{N}}=0

(82)

This is necessary for the G-analyticity condition

D_{\gamma}^{1}B_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}=\bar{D}_{\dot{\gamma}\mathcal{N}}B_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}=0

(83)

to be consistent with the harmonic superspace geometry.

However, harmonic superspace curvature components in
H
-direction do not vanish

(R_{\hat{\alpha}\,\hat{\beta}})^{1}{}_{1}=R_{\hat{\alpha}\,\hat{\beta}})^{\mathcal{N}}{}_{\mathcal{N}}\neq 0

(84)

since the harmonic superspace Lagrangian is built from these non-vanishing components of the curvature

{\cal L}=(R_{\alpha\,\dot{\beta}})^{1}{}_{1}(R_{\beta\,\dot{\alpha}})^{1}{}_{1}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,.

(85)

But to keep some curvatures vanishing and some not vanishing is only possible if local symmetries in the coset direction are broken: namely, there are
4\mathcal{N}-6
constraints on local
\mathcal{H}=U({\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}})
symmetry of the tangent space of the standard superspace
Brink:1979nt
;
Howe:1981gz
with parameters

\Lambda^{i}{}_{j}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})

u^{1}{}_{i}\Lambda^{i}{}_{j}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})u^{j}{}_{r}=u^{r}{}_{i}\Lambda^{i}{}_{j}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})u^{j}{}_{\mathcal{N}}=u^{I}{}_{1}\Lambda^{i}{}_{j}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})u^{j}{}_{\mathcal{N}}=0

(86)

And since local
U({\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}})
symmetry of the tangent space is broken, it also means nonlinear local supersymmetry is broken, according to the algebra (
1
).

At the linear level, in the unitary gauge with physical scalars only, harmonic superspace
L=\mathcal{N}-1
superinvariants, constructed in
Bossard:2011tq
, are eligible superinvariants. At the linear level, the compensating
(S)U(\mathcal{N})
transformation, required to preserve duality
\mathcal{G}
symmetry, is just a global field independent
\mathcal{H}
transformation. Therefore, harmonic coordinates are consistent with global
\mathcal{H}
symmetry.
But at the nonlinear level, the field-dependent compensating
SU(\mathcal{N})
transformation is incompatible with harmonic coordinates, and the
L=L_{cr}-1=\mathcal{N}-1
superinvariants break nonlinear supersymmetry.

The loop computation in
\mathcal{N}=4
,
L=3

Bern:2012cd
, and in
\mathcal{N}=5
,
L=4

Bern:2014sna
show that UV infinities supported by
\mathcal{N}-1
harmonic superinvariants cancel. We explained here that
\mathcal{N}-1
harmonic superinvariants break nonlinear
\mathcal{H}
symmetry and nonlinear supersymmetry. Therefore these symmetries explain
the absence of UV divergences in these cases.

Appendix A
\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspace and G-analyticity

Harmonic superspace constructions are known to be successful in super Yang-Mills theory with
\mathcal{N}\leq 3

Galperin:2001seg
. Minkowski space is the simplest example of a coset space
{G\over H}
where
G
is the Poincaré group and
H
is the Lorentz group. The coset parameters are coordinates

x^{a}
,
a=0,1,2,3

M^{4}={G\over H}={\{L_{ab},P_{a}\}\over\{L_{ab}\}}=x^{a}

(87)

The superspace is a space
where Poincaré supersymmetry algebra can be realized in the same way as the Poincaré algebra is realized in Minkowski space. Poincaré superalgebra has an outer automorphism group
U(\mathcal{N})
(also known as R symmetry). The spinor generators
Q_{\alpha}^{i}
and
\bar{Q}^{\dot{\alpha}i}
transform according to the fundamental representations
{\bf\mathcal{N}}
and
{\bf\bar{\mathcal{N}}}
of
U(\mathcal{N})
, respectively.

The real superspace has coordinates

\mathbb{R}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}={G\over H}={\{L_{ab},su(\mathcal{N}),P_{a},Q_{\alpha}^{i},\bar{Q}^{\dot{\alpha}i}\}\over\{L_{ab},su(\mathcal{N})\}}=(x^{a},\theta_{i}^{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i})

(88)

Here
H
is the group of automorphisms
SL(2,C)\times SU(\mathcal{N})
.

For example, harmonic superspace for
\mathcal{N}=2
theories is obtained by keeping only the
U(1)
part of the automorphism group
SU(2)
in the subgroup
H
:

\mathbb{HR}^{4+2|8}={G\over H}={\{L_{ab},P_{a},Q_{\alpha}^{i},\bar{Q}^{\dot{\alpha}i},su(2)\}\over\{L_{ab},u(1)\}}=(x^{a},\theta_{i}^{\alpha},\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}i},u^{+}_{i})\equiv(X^{A})\,,\qquad i=1,2

(89)

Two new even coordinates
u^{+}_{i}
correspond to the coset
SU(2)/U(1)
, a two-dimensional sphere
S^{2}

and
u_{i}^{-}
is its inverse,
u^{+i}u_{i}^{-}=1
. The spinor derivatives are defined as follows

D_{\alpha}^{\pm}=u^{\pm}_{i}D_{\alpha}^{i}\,,\qquad\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\pm}=u^{\pm}_{i}\bar{D}_{\dot{a}}^{i}

(90)

The main advantage of the harmonic superspace is the existence of a new invariant subspace containing only half of the original Grassmann variables. The analytic superspace is defined as follows

\mathbb{HA}^{4+2|4}={G\over H}={\{L_{ab},P_{a},Q_{\alpha}^{i},\bar{Q}^{\dot{\alpha}i},T^{\pm\pm},T^{0}\}\over\{L_{ab},T^{0},Q_{\alpha}^{+},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}\}}

(91)

Here
T^{\pm\pm},T^{0}
are generators of
SU(2)
and

Q_{\alpha}^{+}=u^{+}_{i}Q_{\alpha}^{i}\,,\qquad\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}=u^{+}_{i}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{i}\

(92)

The generators
L_{ab},T^{0},Q_{\alpha}^{+},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}
form a closed algebra. Derivatives in the harmonic directions are introduced

\partial^{\pm\pm}=u^{\pm i}{\partial\over\partial u^{\mp i}}\,,\qquad\partial^{0}=u^{+i}{\partial\over\partial u^{+i}}-u^{-i}{\partial\over\partial u^{-i}}\ \

(93)

Under
SU(2)
the new coordinates transform as follows

(u^{\pm}_{i})^{\prime}=\Lambda_{i}{}^{j}(u^{\pm}_{j})e^{\pm i\psi(\Lambda,u)}

(94)

where
\Lambda_{i}{}^{j}
is an element of
SU(2)
and
\psi
is a local phase of induced
U(1)
transformation, depending on
\Lambda
and
u^{+}_{i}
.

The condition of the Grassmann analyticity (G-analyticity) is now possible in the analytic harmonic superspace.

D_{\alpha}^{+}\Phi(x_{A},\theta^{\pm}_{A},\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{A},u)=0\,,\qquad\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}\Phi(x_{A},\theta^{\pm}_{A},\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{A},u)=0\ .

(95)

It means that there is a basis where the superfield depends only on 1/2 of fermionic coordinates

\Phi(x_{A},\theta^{+}_{A},\bar{\theta}^{+}_{A},u^{\pm}_{i})\ ,

(96)

where

x_{A}^{a}=x^{a}-2i\theta^{(i}\sigma^{a}\bar{\theta}^{j)}u^{+}_{i}u^{-}_{j}\,,\qquad\theta^{\pm}_{A\,\alpha}=u^{\pm}_{i}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}\,,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{\pm}_{A\,\dot{\alpha}}=u^{\pm}_{i}\bar{\theta}^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(97)

Grassmann-analyticity defining derivatives
D_{\alpha}^{+}
and
\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}
form a commutative algebra with the the harmonic derivative
D^{++}
.

Under
SU(2)
transformations with parameters
\Lambda_{i}{}^{j}
spinorial derivatives in eq. (
19
), supersymmetry charges in eq. (
92
) and analytic space coordinates in eq. (
97
) are
SU(2)
invariant.

In
\mathcal{N}=2
an example of the analytic superfield is an off-shell hypermultiplet. In a superspace without harmonic variables it is
q^{i}(X)
. on-shell
D_{\alpha}^{(i}q^{j)}(X)=0
, but off-shell there is a harmonic superfield which is G-analytic

q^{+}(X,u)=q^{i}(X)u_{i}^{+}\,,\qquad D_{\alpha}^{+}q^{+}(X,u)=\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{+}q^{+}(X,u)=0\ .

(98)

Note that the superfield
q^{+}(X,u)
and its G-analyticity conditions defined in eq. (
98
) are invariant under
SU(2)
transformations with superspace-independent parameters
\Lambda_{i}{}^{j}
.

Conformal supergravities are associated with the superconformal algebra
SU(2,2|\mathcal{N})
; at the nonlinear level, they are available only at
\mathcal{N}\leq 4
. In superspace, conformal supergravities were described in
Howe:1981gz
for 4d

\mathcal{N}\leq 4
by explicitly gauging
SL(2,\mathbb{C})\times U(\mathcal{N})
and identifying the relevant constraints on the torsion of curved superspace. It is therefore not accidental that, for example, conformal
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity has a local in superspace
U(2)
symmetry, which is in the structure group of the conformal superspace.

In case of
\mathcal{N}=2
Poincaré supergravity is obtained from conformal one in
Howe:1981gz
, by de-gauging
U(2)
symmetry, so that the tangent space
is just
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
.
The following is a short version of a derivation of Poincaré
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity superspace in
Howe:1981gz
. One begins with
SL(2,\mathbb{C})\times U(2)
connections in conformal supergravity

\hat{\Omega}_{A}{}^{B}=\Omega_{A}{}^{B}+\Sigma_{A}{}^{B}\ ,

(99)

where
\hat{\Omega}_{A}{}^{B}
is
SL(2,\mathbb{C})\times U(2)
connection and
\Omega_{A}{}^{B}
is
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
connection and proceed with de-gauging by requiring restrictions on
\Sigma_{A}{}^{B}
so that the superspace has no internal symmetry anymore. First, the local
U(1)
is gauge-fixed, and afterwards also the local
SU(2)
. One of the important restrictions explaining the difference between off-shell and on-shell superspace is

\Sigma^{ij}_{\alpha\,k}=2\delta^{i}_{k}\rho^{j}_{\alpha}-\delta^{j}_{k}\rho^{i}_{\alpha}\ .

(100)

When constraints on torsion were imposed, as well as de-gauging constraints, the superspace Bianchi identities were solved consistently. In the final off-shell superspace with the tangent group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
there are two covariant superfields

M_{\alpha\beta}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})

(101)

A superfield equation of motion is

\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(x,\theta,\bar{\theta})=0

(102)

Thus, all non-vanishing components of torsion and curvatures on-shell depend on one superfield
M_{\alpha\beta}
. There is no obstruction to adding harmonic coordinates to this superspace since there is no local
SU(2)
symmetry, and all geometric Bianchi identities were solved consistently. This will be different in the case of the on-shell
\mathcal{N}\geq 4
supergravities, where the local
(S)U(\mathcal{N})
has a different nature and global duality symmetries
\mathcal{G}
play an important role.

Harmonic
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace for Poincaré supergravity is described in
Galperin:2001seg
, starting with a conformal
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace. To gauge-fix extra local symmetries, the compensator superfields are introduced. They are used to gauge fix the local
SU(2)
so that the gauge-fixed local
SU(2)
is compatible with harmonic coordinates
u_{i}^{\pm}
, i=1,2 in
\mathcal{N}=2
Poincaré supergravity.

The construction of
\mathcal{N}\geq 4
harmonic superspace in
Bossard:2011tq
, where
L=\mathcal{N}-1
counterterms are claimed to exist, follows the strategy of
\mathcal{N}=2
projective superspace
in
Kuzenko:2008ep
;
Kuzenko:2008ry
.
We will explain the reason why the construction in projective superspace in
Kuzenko:2008ep
and the consequent studies of the action of
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity in
Kuzenko:2008ry
are consistent with the presence of isotwistor variables
u^{+}_{i}
,
i=1,2
. These isotwistor variables
u_{i}^{+}
are considered to be inert with respect to supergravity gauge group
SU(2)
. It is stressed that
u_{i}^{+}
are constant.

In
Kuzenko:2008ep
;
Kuzenko:2008ry
the fermionic derivatives are defined as

D^{+}_{\alpha}:=u_{i}^{+}D_{\alpha}^{i},\,D^{-}_{\alpha}:=u_{i}^{-}D_{\alpha}^{i}

(103)

But since
u^{\pm}_{i}
are constant, they cannot transform under local
SU(2)
.
The reason why this is not a problem in
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity in
Kuzenko:2008ep
;
Kuzenko:2008ry
is the following. The action derived in
Kuzenko:2008ep
is invariant under the super-Weyl transformations generated by a covariantly chiral parameter
\sigma
where
\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{i}\sigma=0
. It also has a local
U(2)
symmetry. To get
\mathcal{N}=2
Poincaré supergravity, one must gauge-fix various extra local symmetries, including the local
SU(2)
.

The action of conformal supergravity in eq. (1.2) of
Kuzenko:2008ry
depends on a superconformal compensator, an off-shell tensor multiplet described by a symmetric real superfield
H^{ij}(x,\theta)
. When
H^{ij}
is constrained

H^{ij}=-{i\over 2}(\sigma_{1})^{ij}\,G\,,\qquad(\sigma_{1})^{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)^{ij}

(104)

the action does not have local
SU(2)
symmetry anymore, only the local U(1) is left. The local U(1) gauge connection is
\Phi_{\alpha}^{i\,jk}=i\,\Sigma^{i}_{\alpha}(\sigma_{1})^{ij}
. One can further use the de-gauging procedure for
\mathcal{N}=2
as in
Howe:1981gz
, where one makes a choice

\Sigma^{i}_{\alpha}=0
so that
SU(2)
gauge symmetry is fixed and only global
SU(2)
remains. This is consistent with the fact that the isotwistor coordinates
u_{i}^{+}
and their inverse are inert under local
SU(2)
.

Therefore in
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity a contraction of the type (
103
)
is acceptable, local
SU(2)
is gauge-fixed in superspace.

Appendix B
Superinvariants below critical loop order in
d>4

Dimensional analysis in superspace to establish the value of
L_{cr}
starting from which the geometric superinvariants exist was performed in
Kallosh:2023css
. Here we reproduce the result for
L_{cr}
obtained in
Kallosh:2023css
in a slightly more convenient form.
A dimension of the gravitational coupling is

{\rm dim}\Big[{1\over\kappa^{2}}\Big]={\rm dim}[M_{Pl}^{2}]=d-2
.
For integer dimensions
d\geq 4
we determine
the critical loop order defined by an on-shell counterterm which is a geometric whole superspace integral with the Lagrangian invariant under global
\mathcal{G}
and local
\mathcal{H}
.

S_{cr}=\kappa^{2\,(L_{cr}-1)}\int d^{4\mathcal{N}}d^{d}x\,det\,E\,\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)\ .

(105)

In such case, the minimal dimension superfield
\mathcal{H}
-invariant Lagrangian depends on 4 spinor dimension 1/2 superfields and their superspace derivatives.

{\rm dim}\Big[\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)\Big]=2+n\ ,\qquad n>0

(106)

In the presence of
n
, one can always make
L_{cr}
an integer.
The reason
n
is positive is that all covariant superspace derivatives, as well as superfield-dependent torsions and curvatures, have positive dimension. The counting goes as follows

{\rm dim}[\kappa^{2}]\,(L_{cr}-1)+{\rm dim}\,[\rm full\,\,superspace]+{\rm dim}\,[\mathcal{L}]=0\,,

(107)

so that the action is dimensionless, and we find

-(d-2)(L_{cr}-1)+2\mathcal{N}-d+2+n=0\ ,

(108)

It follows that

L_{cr}={2\mathcal{N}+n\over(d-2)}\ ,\qquad n\geq 0

(109)

In each case, we need to find a minimal value of
n
, which makes
L_{cr}
an integer. The result for the case of maximal supergravity is

\displaystyle d=4,\,L_{cr}=8:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{14}\int d^{4}\,x\,D^{10}R^{4}+\dots\,\quad\quad\quad n=0

(110)

\displaystyle d=5,\,L_{cr}=6:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{10}\int d^{5}\,x\,D^{12}R^{4}+\dots\,\quad\quad\quad n=2

(111)

\displaystyle d=6,\,L_{cr}=4:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{6}\int d^{6}\,x\,D^{10}R^{4}+\dots\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad n=0

(112)

\displaystyle d=7,\,L_{cr}=4:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{6}\int d^{7}\,x\,D^{14}R^{4}+\dots\,\,\quad\quad\quad n=4

(113)

\displaystyle d=8,\,L_{cr}=3:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{4}\int d^{8}\,x\,D^{12}R^{4}+\dots\,\,\quad\quad\quad n=3

(114)

\displaystyle d=9,\,L_{cr}=3:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa^{4}\int d^{9}\,x\,D^{15}R^{4}+\dots\,\,\quad\quad\quad n=5

In
Bern:1998ug
;
Bern:2007hh
;
Bern:2008pv
;
Bern:2009kd
;
Bern:2012uf
;
Bern:2018jmv

the first divergences in maximal supergravities have been found at
5\leq d\leq 9

\displaystyle d=9,\qquad L_{UV}=2<L_{cr}=3

(115)

\displaystyle d=8,\qquad L_{UV}=1<L_{cr}=3

(116)

\displaystyle d=7,\qquad L_{UV}=2<L_{cr}=4

(117)

\displaystyle d=6,\qquad L_{UV}=3<L_{cr}=4

(118)

\displaystyle d=5,\qquad L_{UV}=5<L_{cr}=6

(119)

It appears that in all cases where we have the information about UV divergences in
d\geq 5
from loop computations, the UV divergences were discovered at the loop order below what we have defined as
L_{cr}
. According to our assertion, the nonlinear supersymmetry invariants cannot
be constructed unless they come from a whole superspace integral. Therefore, it follows from (
119

\mathcal{H}
-symmetry.
At the linearized level
in
d>4
, the relevant
{\mathcal{G}\over\mathcal{H}}
supergravity superinvariants below critical loop order were constructed in harmonic superspace in

;
Bossard:2014lra

L_{cr}={2\mathcal{N}+n\over(d-2)},\,n>0

d=4
can now be applied to
d>4
. We focus on maximal supergravities in each dimension. In all cases, the physical scalars are in the coset space

{\mathcal{G}\over\mathcal{H}}\quad:\quad{E_{6(6)}\over USp(8)}\,,\quad{E_{5(5)}\over USp(4)\times USp(4)}\,,\quad{E_{4(4)}\over USp(4)}\,,\quad{E_{3(3)}\over U(2)}\,,\quad{GL(2)\over SO(2)}

for

\mathcal{H}

The geometric whole superspace invariants at

\mathcal{H}

For example, in maximal
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supergravity in six dimensions the relevant harmonic variables parametrize one of
USp(4)\times USp(4)
as follows
Bossard:2014lra
:

{USp(4)\over U(2)}
.
This provides the Grassmann analytic superfields and linearized superinvariant describing 3-loop UV divergence of d=6 maximal supergravity. As in 4d case, the fact that harmonic variables break local

symmetry can be established using the superspace geometry. This means that there is no nonlinear generalization of the

BPS superinvariant presented in eq. (3.32) in

;

One can also check, case by case, that all harmonic superinvariants below critical order have no nonlinear generalization.

The reason why in 4d there was a claim in
Bossard:2011tq
that superinvariants below critical order exist in harmonic superspace compatible with nonlinear supersymmetry is the fact that harmonic Lagrangian depended on geometric spinorial superfields and had dimension 2. But in all
d>4

To summarize, harmonic superinvariants at
L<L_{cr}
are useful to describe the UV divergences that did occur at

L<L_{cr}
and show that local
\mathcal{H}
symmetry and nonlinear supersymmetry are broken by quantum corrections. This means that computations in
Bern:1998ug
;
Bern:2007hh
;
Bern:2008pv
;
Bern:2009kd
;
Bern:2012uf
;
Bern:2018jmv

Only in 4d case it is not known yet if perturbative quantum field theory corrections are consistent with all symmetries of the theory. The 4-loop cancellation of UV divergence in
\mathcal{N}=5
indicates a possibility that 4d maximal supergravity might be UV finite perturbatively. But more loop computations will be necessary to find out the actual UV properties of higher loop corrections in 4d maximal supergravity.

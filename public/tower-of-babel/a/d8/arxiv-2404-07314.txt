Motivic Lefschetz theorem for twisted Milnor hypersurfaces Thanks: Both authors were partially supported by NSERC Discovery grant RGPIN-2022-03060
Rui Xiong, Kirill Zainoulline

Abstract
. We show that the Grothendieck-Chow motive of a smooth hyperplane section Y Y of an inner twisted form X X of a Milnor hypersurface splits as a direct sum of shifted copies of the motive of the Severi-Brauer variety of the associated cyclic algebra A A and the motive of its maximal commutative subfield L ⊂ A L\subset A . The proof is based on the non-triviality of the (monodromy) Galois action on the equivariant Chow group of Y L Y_{L} .



1.
Introduction

Milnor hypersurfaces, their twisted forms and hyperplane sections appear in many different contexts and provide an important source of examples of algebraic varieties in various areas of mathematics. In the present notes we focus on the study of their Grothendieck-Chow motives
[
Ma68
]
using the (monodromy) action of the Galois group on the respective equivariant Chow groups
[
To97
,
Br97
]
.

1.1.
Twisted Milnor hypersurfaces

Following
[
LM07
, 2.5.3]
the Milnor hypersurface
X_{0}
of dimension
2n-3
,
n\geq 3
over a field
F
is a zero divisor of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}(1)
on the product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
or, equivalently, it is the flag (incidence) variety

X_{0}=\{V_{1}\subset V_{n-1}\subset F^{n}\},\;\text{ where }\dim_{F}V_{i}=i.

There is another description of
X_{0}
in terms of right ideals
[
KMRT
, §1B]
:

X_{0}=\{I_{1}\subset I_{n-1}\subset\operatorname{End}(F^{n})\},\;\text{ where }\dim_{F}I_{i}=ni.

The identification is given by
(V_{1}\subset V_{n-1})\mapsto(I_{1}\subset I_{n-1})
with
I_{i}=\operatorname{Hom}(F^{n},V_{i})\subset\operatorname{End}(F^{n})
.

Using the Bruhat decomposition one can show that the Grothendieck-Chow motive of
X_{0}
splits as a direct sum of (shifted) Tate motives
[
Ko91
]
:

\operatorname{M}(X_{0})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-2}\big(\mathbb{Z}(i)\oplus\mathbb{Z}(2n-3-i)\big)^{\oplus(i+1)},\;\text{ where }\mathbb{Z}(i)=\mathbb{Z}(1)^{\otimes i}\text{ and }\mathbb{Z}(0)=\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{Spec}F).

Let
X
be a projective homogeneous
G
-variety over
F
, where
G
is a linear algebraic group over
F
such that
G_{\bar{F}}\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{n}
and
X_{\bar{F}}\simeq(X_{0})_{\bar{F}}
, where
X_{\bar{F}}=X\!\times_{\operatorname{Spec}F}\operatorname{Spec}\bar{F}
denotes the base change to the separable closure
\bar{F}
of
F
. We call
X
a twisted Milnor hypersurface.

Such
X
provides an example of a
G
-flag variety in the sense of
[
MPW
, §1]
or the so-called twisted flag variety. The Galois group
\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)
acts naturally on
G(\bar{F})
, its maximal torus and the respective Weyl group
W
via the so-called
\star
-action (see
[
CM06
,
MPW
]
). The kernel of this
\star
-action defines the smallest field extension
F_{G}/F
such that
G_{F_{G}}
is of inner type (it has no non-split quasi-split forms). There are two basic families of twisted Milnor hypersurfaces: the inner hypersurfaces (
F_{G}=F
) and the quasi-split hypersurfaces (
G
is quasi-split).

For the study of inner hypersurfaces we refer to
[
CPSZ
, §2]
). In this case
G=\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(A)
for some central simple algebra
A
over
F
, and by
[
CPSZ
, Cor.2.3]
the hypersurface
X
can be identified with a certain Grassmann bundle over the Severi-Brauer variety
\operatorname{SB}(A)
. Its motive
\operatorname{M}(X)
then splits as a direct sum of shifted copies of the motive
\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{SB}(A))
. For properties of the latter see
[
Ka00
,
Ka96
]
. Observe that both
\operatorname{M}(X)
and
\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{SB}(A))
do not contain Artin motives (motives of non-trivial field extensions) as direct summands.

In the quasi-split case,
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{G}/F)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by a non-trivial automorphism of the Dynkin diagram, and the variety
X
is a Hermitian quadric over
F_{G}
, i.e., a variety of isotropic lines in the respective Hermitian space (see
[
SZ10
]
). By
[
SZ10
, Thm. C]
, the zero-dimensional part of
\operatorname{M}(X)
consists of (shifted) motives of
\operatorname{Spec}F_{G}
.

For further properties and examples of non-inner and non-quasi-split hypersurfaces (unitary projective spaces corresponding to algebras with involutions of the second kind), we refer to
[
Ka12
]
.
Observe that all these results are in line with the main result of
[
CM06
]
, which implies that the zero-dimensional part of the motive of a
G
-flag variety is generated by motives of subfields of
F_{G}
containing
F
.

1.2.
Hyperplane sections

In our paper we consider only inner hypersurfaces. From this point on we assume
F
contains a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\zeta
,
n\in F^{\times}
, and
X
is an inner hypersurface over
F
.

We introduce our main object, a smooth hyperplane section
Y
of
X
as follows. We choose
{a}\in F^{\times}
so that
L=F[\sqrt[n]{{a}}]
is a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
. Given
b\in F^{\times}
we consider a cyclic algebra

A=F\langle u,v\rangle\big/\left<u^{n}={a},\,v^{n}={b},\,uv=\zeta vu\right>.

This is a central simple algebra over
F
of degree
n
which splits over
L
. The
G
-flag variety
X
for
G=\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(A)
is then given by

X=\left\{I_{1}\subset I_{n-1}\subset A\colon\text{$I_{i}$ is a right ideal of $A$ of reduced dimension $i$}\right\}.

We define its smooth hyperplane section
Y
to be

Y=\left\{(I_{1}\subset I_{n-1})\in X\colon uI_{1}\subset I_{n-1}\right\}.

Observe that
Y
is not a twisted flag variety (it is not a twisted form of a projective homogeneous variety). Our major result says that (up to ‘phantoms’, i.e., up to motives
M
such that
M_{L}=0
)

Theorem
.

\operatorname{M}(Y)=\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{SB}(A))\oplus\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{SB}(A))(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{SB}(A))(n-3)\oplus\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{Spec}L)(n-2)
.

Remarks
.

1. This result looks quite surprising, as contrary to
Y
the motive of
X
does not contain Artin motives.
Moreover, if
A
splits (e.g.
b\in\operatorname{Nrd}(A)
), then
\operatorname{M}(Y)
consists of Tate motives and a unique Artin motive in the middle dimension:

\operatorname{M}(Y)=\big(\!\oplus_{*}\!\mathbb{Z}(*)\big)\oplus\operatorname{M}(\operatorname{Spec}L)(n-2).

2. The main result of
[
Se08
]
provides an example of motivic decomposition of a hyperplane section of generalized Severi-Brauer variety
\operatorname{SB}_{3}(A)
, where
A
has degree
6
. This decomposition also does not contain Artin motives. Observe that as in

4.1.
The localization for

We consider a graph with

[ij]
,

[ij]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{jk}}}{{\text{------}}}[ik]\quad\text{ and }\quad[ij]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{ik}}}{{\text{------}}}[kj],\quad\text{ where all $i,j,k$ are distinct,}

and
\alpha_{ij}=t_{i}-t_{j}\in T^{*}
,
t_{i}\colon diag(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\mapsto z_{i}^{-1}

Lemma 4.1
.

The image of
\operatorname{CH}_{T_{L}}(Y_{L})
in
\oplus_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{Z}}T^{*}
under the horizontal maps of the diagram of Theorem
3.6

\operatorname{CH}_{T_{L}}(Y_{L})\simeq\big\{(\varphi_{ij})_{ij}:\alpha|\varphi_{ij}-\varphi_{kh}\text{ for any edge $[ij]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\text{---}}}[kh]$}\big\}.

Moreover, the Poincaré pairing is given by

(5)

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{Y_{L}}=\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\varphi_{ij}\psi_{ij}}{\prod_{s\neq i,j}\alpha_{is}\alpha_{sj}}.

The first part of the lemma is a consequence of results of
[

[
Br97
, §3.4]

[

, Def.3.6]

C
over

-fixed point

(i)

a root-conic curve connecting
[ij]
and
[ji]
with weight
\alpha_{ij}
at
[ij]

\mathbb{P}^{1}\ni[x\colon y]\mapsto\big(\operatorname{span}(xe_{i}+ye_{j})\subset\operatorname{span}(e_{1},\ldots,\,\not{\!e}_{i},\ldots,\,\not{\!e}_{j},\ldots,e_{n},xe_{i}+ye_{j})\big),

[ij]
and
[ik]

at

(for distinct

and
[kj]

4.2.
The Artin motive support

For
1\leq\ell\leq n
we define the following cycles in
\oplus_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb{Z}}T^{*}

\gamma_{\ell}=(\gamma^{\ell}_{ij})_{ij},\;\text{ where }\gamma^{\ell}_{ij}=\delta_{i,\ell}{\textstyle\prod}_{s\neq i,j}\alpha_{is}.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

(i)

\gamma_{\ell}\in\operatorname{CH}_{T}^{n-2}(Y_{L})
,
(ii)

\widehat{\sigma}\gamma_{\ell}=\gamma_{\sigma(\ell)}
,
\sigma\in\Gamma_{L}
, and
(iii)

\langle\gamma_{k},\gamma_{\ell}\rangle_{Y_{L}}=(-1)^{n-2}\delta_{k,\ell}
.

Example 4.3
.

For
n=5
there are
20
vertices which can be distributed as vertices of a regular dodecahedron. Two types of edges then form two families (compounds) of
5
tetrahedra:

\hrefhttps://cubicbear.github.io/Compounds

Each
\gamma^{\ell}
,
1\leq\ell\leq 5
is supported over a single tetrahedron on the left diagram, and the monodromy action is given by rotations.

Proof.

(i) For the edge
[ij]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{jk}}}{{\text{---}}}[ik]
we have

\alpha_{jk}\mid\delta_{i,\ell}\Big({\textstyle\prod}_{s\neq i,j}{\alpha_{is}}-{\textstyle\prod}_{s\neq i,k}{\alpha_{is}}\Big)=\delta_{i,\ell}(\alpha_{ik}-\alpha_{ij}){\textstyle\prod}_{s\neq i,j,k}\alpha_{is}=\delta_{i,\ell}\alpha_{jk}{\textstyle\prod}_{s\neq i,j,k}\alpha_{is}.

For the edge
[ij]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{ik}}}{{\text{---}}}[kj]
we have

\alpha_{ik}\mid\delta_{i\ell}\prod_{s\neq i,j}\alpha_{is}-\delta_{k\ell}\prod_{s\neq k,j}\alpha_{ks}
, since
\alpha_{ik}
divides both terms.

(ii) We have

\sigma\gamma^{\ell}_{\sigma^{-1}(i)\sigma^{-1}(j)}=\delta_{\sigma^{-1}(i),\ell}\prod_{\sigma(s)\neq i,j}\alpha_{i\sigma(s)}=\gamma^{\sigma(\ell)}_{ij}
.

(iii) Since
\gamma_{k}\gamma_{\ell}=0
for
k\neq\ell
, we may assume
k=\ell
.
By (
5
) we have

\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{\ell},\gamma_{\ell}\rangle_{Y_{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq\ell}\frac{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{\ell s}^{2}}{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{\ell s}\alpha_{sj}}=\sum_{j\neq\ell}\frac{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{\ell s}}{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{sj}}.

Consider this expression as a polynomial
f(t)
in
t=t_{\ell}
of degree at most
n-2
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}(t_{1},\ldots,\widehat{t_{\ell}},\ldots,t_{n})
.
Observe that for each
i\neq\ell
we have

f(t_{i})=\sum_{j\neq\ell}\frac{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{is}}{\prod_{s\neq\ell,j}\alpha_{sj}}=\frac{\prod_{s\neq\ell,i}\alpha_{is}}{\prod_{s\neq\ell,i}\alpha_{si}}=(-1)^{n-2}.

Then by the Lagrange interpolation

.
∎

Let
\overline{\gamma}_{\ell}
denote the image of
\gamma_{\ell}
in
\operatorname{CH}^{n-2}(Y_{L})

f=\overline{\gamma}_{1}\in\operatorname{CH}^{n-2}(Y\times\operatorname{Spec}L)\quad\text{ and }\quad g=(-1)^{n-2}\overline{\gamma}_{1}\in\operatorname{CH}^{n-2}(\operatorname{Spec}L\times Y).

The composite

Moreover, the motive

,

Proof.

By Lemma

and Corollary

which implies the first part of the theorem. As for the second part observe that by Lemma

Recall that there is a

.

\langle\overline{\gamma}_{\ell},\overline{h}\gamma\rangle_{Y_{L}}=0

Corollary 4.7

Since, the pairing

Higgs decay into massive
b
-quarks at NNLO QCD

in the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme

Abstract

We present a fully differential description of a decay of a scalar
Higgs boson into massive
b
-quarks valid at next-to-next-to-leading
order (NNLO) in perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD).
We work within the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme
extended to accommodate massive partons.
We include the loop-induced contribution involving a Higgs coupling to a top quark.
We test our calculation against results existing in the literature,
comparing the predictions for the total decay width and jet rates.

Introduction

After the Higgs boson discovery by the ATLAS and CMS collaborations in
2012, the study of Higgs boson properties has become one of the major
research avenues in particle physics.
Since the mass of the Higgs boson has already been precisely
measured
[
1
]
, all couplings between the Higgs boson and
other Standard Model (SM) particles can be accurately predicted.
Nevertheless, these couplings can be modified by New Physics that lies
beyond the SM. Hence, actual measurements of those couplings can provide
important constraints on many extensions of the SM.

The Higgs boson decay into a pair of
b
-quarks is the most common
decay channel of the Higgs boson and it is essential to many New
Physics searches.
Indeed, it plays a particularly important role when considering rare
Higgs boson production modes, which benefit from the large
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}

branching fraction.
Although such measurements are often very challenging, due to
overwhelming QCD backgrounds, the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay has already been
observed by both ATLAS and
CMS
[
2
,
3
]
.
The upcoming years of data taking at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC)
will allow for further exploration and use of this Higgs boson decay
channel.

In order to fully utilise the data collected at the LHC, a good
theoretical understanding of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process is required.
The next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD corrections have been available for
a long time
[
4
,

,

,

Mass effects at

[

.

2.1
Notation

We start with a short introduction that will set the stage for our
calculation. We consider the Higgs boson decaying into a pair of

b
-quarks

\displaystyle H(q_{1})\longrightarrow b(q_{2})+\bar{b}(q_{3})\,,

(2.1)

with
q_{1}^{\,2}=M_{H}^{\,2}
and
q_{2}^{\,2}=q_{3}^{\,2}=m_{b}^{\,2}
.
The leading order (LO) partial decay width of the Higgs boson into a
pair of
b
-quarks is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathrm{LO}}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M_{H}}\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1})|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|^{2}\,,

(2.2)

where
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1})
is the two-particle phase-space volume
element for the production of two
b
-quarks with total momentum

q_{1}
, and
|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|^{2}
is the squared tree-level matrix element.
For brevity, we list only final-state particles in the sub- and
superscripts, since the initial state is always an on-shell Higgs
boson at rest. Upon integration we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathrm{LO}}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}}{16\pi}y_{b}^{2}M_{H}\beta^{3}\,,

(2.3)

where
N_{c}=3
is the number of colours,

\beta=\sqrt{1-4m_{b}^{2}/M_{H}^{2}}
and
y_{b}
stands for the
b
-quark
Yukawa coupling,
y_{b}=m_{b}(2\sqrt{2}G_{F})^{1/2}
.

The Higgs boson decay width into
b
-quarks receives radiative
corrections that can be systematically calculated in perturbative
QCD. We use the following notation for the perturbative expansion of
the width

\displaystyle\Gamma^{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathrm{LO}}}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}\left[{1+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}}\right)\gamma_{1}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}}\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})}\right]\,.

(2.4)

In
Eq.

2.4
,
\alpha_{s}
is the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
QCD strong
coupling constant defined in a theory with
n_{f}=n_{l}+1
quark
flavours, where
n_{l}
is the number of massless flavours.

It is useful to define a shorthand notation that denotes an integral
over the Lorentz-invariant phase space of particles involved in a
particular (sub)process.
Similar to the notation in Ref.
[
25
]
, we define

\displaystyle{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{X}})=\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{X}}}(q_{1})|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{X}}}|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}({b\bar{b}{X}})\,,

(2.5)

where
{b\bar{b}{X}}
denotes the constituents of the final state of a
considered subprocess,
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{X}}}(q_{1})
and
|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{X}}}|^{2}

stand for the Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure and the squared
amplitude of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{X}}
process, respectively.
The momentum
q_{1}
refers to the initial state Higgs boson, while

\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}
is an infrared-safe observable that depends on the kinematical
configuration of the particles involved in the process.
We will use the notation

\displaystyle\langle A\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\Phi A

(2.6)

to denote the integration of some quantity
A
over the phase space,

\mathrm{d}\Phi
.

2.2
Outline of the subtraction scheme

One of the challenges in higher-order QCD calculations is the
appearance of infrared singularities when massless particles become
soft or collinear.
Dimensional regularisation
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
can be
used to regulate these singularities which show up as poles in the
dimensional regularisation parameter
\epsilon=(4-d)/2
in both real and
virtual amplitudes.
For an infrared-safe observable these poles cancel between real and
virtual corrections and collinear factorisation contributions once the
loop and phase-space integrals over the singular regions are
performed
[
37
,
38
]
.
The observables depend on the momenta of the real emission partons so
that numerical integration of the phase-space integrals is desirable
from the standpoint of flexibility and often also required due to the
complexity of the observable, which may involve, e.g., complicated
kinematic constraints and jet algorithms.

It follows from the factorisation theorems of QCD that the integrand of
the cross-section, i.e., the combination of squared matrix elements and
phase-space measure, scales as
E_{i}^{-1+a\epsilon}\mathrm{d}{E_{i}}
in soft limits
and as
\theta_{ij}^{-1+b\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\theta_{ij}
in collinear limits of
massless partons, where
E_{i}
is the energy of the soft parton,

\theta_{ij}
is the angle between the collinear partons and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
\epsilon
acts as a regulator for logarithmic divergences
in the real-emission phase-space integrals. For the numerical
integration it is necessary to explicitly extract and cancel the poles
in
\epsilon^{-1}
and to regulate the integrals in such a way that the
expansion in
\epsilon
can be performed at the integrand level.

A number of methods have been developed to accomplish that. Here, we
follow the nested soft-collinear subtraction
scheme
[
25
,
17
,
26
,
27
]

which is closely related to the sector-improved residue subtraction
scheme
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
As with all subtraction schemes, the general idea is to introduce
subtraction terms for each singular limit which regulate the integrand
and to add back these subtraction terms integrated over the unresolved
phase space. Schematically, for an integral of a function
F
over the
phase space, we write

\displaystyle\langle F\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F-OF\rangle+\langle OF\rangle\,,

(2.7)

where
O
is an operator which extracts the asymptotic behaviour of

F
and the phase space in a singular limit. The term

\langle OF\rangle
, which is integrated over the unresolved phase
space in
d
dimensions, then carries explicit poles in
\epsilon^{-1}
,
while the regulated term
\langle F-OF\rangle
is expanded in
\epsilon

at the integrand level. We apply
Eq.

2.7

recursively to regulate all singular limits.

The nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme consists of the following
steps.

1.

Introduce subtraction terms for the soft limits. At NNLO this
involves up to two single-soft limits and the double-soft limit.

2.

Introduce a partition of unity for the phase space that isolates
the collinear limits,
1=\sum_{\{p\}}w_{\{p\}}
, where the
sum runs over the sets of partons that can produce collinear
singularities and the functions
w_{\{p\}}
go to zero whenever
two partons which are not in
{p}
become collinear, thereby
regulating integrand in that limit.

3.

In each collinear partition, choose a suitable phase-space
parametrisation in terms of angles and energies of the partons
that can become unresolved.

4.

Use sector decomposition
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
to map all singularities to the boundaries of
the region of integration so that
the singularities can be easily extracted upon using

Eq.

2.7
. In order to generate
the limits of the matrix elements, we use the standard QCD
factorisation formulae for the soft and collinear limits.
All necessary expressions up to NNLO can be found, e.g., in the
appendices of Ref.
[
30
]
.

The
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process with massive
b
-quarks is particularly simple in
this context since there are no triple- or double-collinear limits
that involve
b
-quarks so that step 2 can be avoided. Moreover, by
choosing an appropriate phase-space parametrisation in step 3, the
sector decomposition of step 4 only yields a single collinear
subsector.

The calculation will be subdivided into pieces so that the
cancellation of
\epsilon^{-1}
poles can be shown without making reference
to the explicit form of the matrix elements. In particular, we will
organise the contributions into sets according to the multiplicity of
the resolved final state and the loop order of the matrix elements.
By combining this with a suitable phase-space parametrisation which
decouples the integrations over the unresolved and resolved parts of
phase space, we demonstrate pole cancellation for each phase-space
point of the resolved configuration separately.
In
Sections

3
and
4
we explain the application of
this scheme to the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process in greater detail.

2.3
IR poles of virtual amplitudes

The one- and two-loop virtual amplitudes that we encounter in an NNLO
calculation feature ultraviolet (UV) divergences that can be removed
using a suitable renormalisation procedure. We employ a hybrid scheme in
which we renormalise quark and gluon fields, the quark masses and the
Yukawa coupling in an on-shell scheme, while we use the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme
for the strong coupling. The details of our renormalisation choice are
described in
Appendix

A
.

At this point, the renormalised amplitudes are free of UV divergences.
Nevertheless, they still contain poles in
\epsilon^{-1}
which are of IR
origin. These poles can be predicted from general considerations

[
42
,
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
. They factorise in the form

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}|\mathcal{F}\rangle\,,

(2.8)

where
|\mathcal{M}\rangle
is the UV-renormalised amplitude,
\mathbf{Z}
is
an operator in colour space which contains poles in
\epsilon^{-1}
and

|\mathcal{F}\rangle
is a finite remainder which does not contain any
poles. Expanding all pieces in the strong coupling, we find

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}\rangle={}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle+|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(2)}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle+\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}\rangle+|\mathcal{F}^{(2)}\rangle\right)\,.

(2.9)

Note that this notation leaves all powers of the strong coupling
related to real emissions implicit inside the amplitudes.
On the one hand, we can use
Section

2.3
as a prediction
in order to check the
\epsilon^{-1}
poles of the UV-renormalised
amplitudes. On the other hand, we can also use

Section

2.3
to define the finite remainders, i.e.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle\,,

(2.10)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}^{(1)}\rangle-\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{(2)}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}^{(2)}\rangle-\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}\rangle-\mathbf{Z}^{(2)}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}\rangle\,,

(2.12)

and express all formulae in terms of these. This is useful for showing
pole cancellation since it allows us to make the pole terms explicit
without specifying the matrix elements that they multiply. The pole
terms are multiplied by lower order quantities, as expected. Note that
we only include the
\epsilon^{-1}
poles in the definition of the

\mathbf{Z}
operator, cf. Refs.
[
45
,
50
]
.

In general,
\mathbf{Z}
is an operator acting on vectors in colour
space, which expresses non-trivial correlations between different
colour configurations
[
51
,
50
]
.
However, in our case, the coefficients can be expressed in terms of
simple colour factors since we only require virtual amplitudes with up
to three coloured particles (
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
and
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
). The expansion
coefficients
\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}
for the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(C_{F}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})+2\gamma_{Q}^{(0)})\,,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{(2)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\epsilon^{2}}(C_{F}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})+2\gamma_{Q}^{(0)})(C_{F}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})+2\gamma_{Q}^{(0)}-2\beta_{0}(n_{l}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}(C_{F}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(1)}(v_{23})+2\gamma_{Q}^{(1)})+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(C_{F}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})+2\gamma_{Q}^{(0)})\beta_{0,Q}\ln\left({\frac{\mu_{R}^{2}}{m_{b}^{2}}}\right)\,,

(2.14)

where
\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(i)}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})
are the massless and
massive cusp anomalous dimensions,

v_{23}=\sqrt{1-m_{b}^{4}/({q_{2}}\cdot{q_{3}})^{2}}
,
\beta_{0}(n_{l})
is the
zeroth-order coefficient of the QCD
\beta
-function with
n_{l}
massless
flavours,
\beta_{0}(n_{l})=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}T_{F}n_{l}
and

\beta_{0,Q}=-\frac{4}{3}T_{F}
. The
\gamma_{Q}^{(i)}
denote the expansion
coefficients of the anomalous dimensions of the massive quark and
\mu_{R}

is the renormalisation scale. We collect the necessary formulae in

Section

B.1
.
For the
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
process, where we only need the one-loop amplitude,
we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}(-C_{A}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(0)})+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\left[\gamma_{g}^{(0)}+2\gamma_{Q}^{(0)}+\left(C_{F}-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\right)\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp,Q}}^{(0)}(v_{23})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(0)}\left(\ln\left(\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{2}}\cdot{q_{4}})}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{3}}\cdot{q_{4}})}\right)+2i\pi\right)\right]\,.

(2.15)

The gluon anomalous dimension
\gamma_{g}^{(0)}
is also given in

Section

B.1
.

2.4
Phase-space parametrisation

In this section we outline the parametrisation of the real-emission
phase space that we employ throughout the calculation.
NNLO corrections to Higgs decays involve contributions with up to two
real emissions accompanying the Born process. In our case, the Born
process consists of the Higgs boson decaying into massive
b
-quarks
(
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
) and we include final states with one additional gluon
(
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
) as well as two additional massless partons (
H\to{b\bar{b}{gg}}
or

H\to{b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}{}}}
)
1
1

1

The
H\to{b\bar{b}{{b\bar{b}{}}}}
contribution is finite. Thus,
it can be integrated using standard techniques and we do not
discuss it here.
.

The guiding principle behind the construction outlined in this section
is, first, to explicitly parametrise the energies and angles that are
responsible for the soft and collinear singularities and, second, to
decouple the real-emission phase space from the phase space of the
reduced process once a parton becomes unresolved.
We note that we work in the Higgs boson rest frame throughout the
paper.

The phase-space measure for an emission of a single massless parton
in
d=4-2\epsilon
space-time dimensions reads

\displaystyle[\mathrm{d}{}q_{i}]={}

\displaystyle(\mu_{R}^{2})^{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d-1}q_{i}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}(2E_{i})}\,,

(2.16)

where we denote the parton momentum by
q_{i}
and its energy by
E_{i}
.
Note that we also include a global factor
(\mu_{R}^{2})^{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}
that
originates from the strong coupling renormalisation, see discussion
below
Appendix

A
. We do not introduce an upper bound on the
energy of the emitted gluon since it naturally appears due to the
energy-momentum conserving
\delta
-function once the measure in

Eq.

2.16
is considered as a part of a specific process.

Single-emission phase space

We start with the process

\displaystyle H(q_{1})\longrightarrow b(q_{2})+\bar{b}(q_{3})+g(q_{4})\,,

(2.17)

and discuss its phase-space parametrisation.

The phase-space measure reads

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}(q_{1})

\displaystyle={}\int[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{4})

\displaystyle={}(\mu_{R}^{2})^{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d-2}\hat{q}_{4}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int\mathrm{d}E_{4}(E_{4})^{d-3}\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{4}),

(2.18)

where
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(Q)
stands for the Born phase space of the two

b
-quarks with total momentum
Q
, and
\hat{q}_{4}
determines the
direction of the gluon momentum,

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{4}^{\mu}={}

\displaystyle q_{4}^{\mu}/E_{4}\,.

(2.19)

We further parametrise the gluon energy as

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle=E_{\mathrm{max}}\,\xi_{1},

(2.20)

with
E_{\mathrm{max}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\beta^{2}M_{H}
and

\beta=\sqrt{1-4m_{b}^{2}/M_{H}^{2}}
. This finally leads us to

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}(q_{1})={}

\displaystyle 2^{1-2\epsilon}E_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left({\frac{\mu_{R}^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}}\right)^{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{4}^{(2-2\epsilon)}}{2(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta}{(\eta(1-\eta))^{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}\xi_{1}^{1-2\epsilon}\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{4})\,.

(2.21)

The
\mathrm{d}\Omega_{4}
element denotes the angular integral over the
direction of
\hat{q}_{4}
. The remaining angular integrals can be
performed using

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Omega^{(a)}

\displaystyle={}\frac{2\pi^{a/2}}{\Gamma(a/2)}\,.

(2.22)

For a single gluon emission, the only unresolved limit is the
single-soft one, i.e.
\xi_{1}\to 0
. Obviously, this removes
q_{4}

from the overall momentum conservation and the integration over the
unresolved phase space of
q_{4}
decouples from the Born phase space.
Thus, in that limit we just replace
\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{4})
by

\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1})
in
Section

2.4
.

Double-emission phase space

We now focus on the parametrisation of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{gg}}
phase space. Note
that, since we consider
b
-quarks to be massive, there are no
singularities associated with kinematic configurations where gluons
become collinear to
b
-quarks. Therefore, we do not need to partition
the phase space into subsectors, which are usually necessary to
disentangle the collinear singularities. Instead, we work with a
global parametrisation.
In this section we focus on the two-gluon emission case since the
parametrisation of the
q\bar{q}
emission phase space is nearly identical.
We comment on the differences where necessary.

We consider the process

\displaystyle H(q_{1})\longrightarrow b(q_{2})+\bar{b}(q_{3})+g(q_{4})+g(q_{5})\,.

(2.23)

We denote the sum of the gluon momenta by
q_{45}=q_{4}+q_{5}
. The
phase space measure then reads

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}(q_{1})={}

\displaystyle\int[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]\int[\mathrm{d}{}q_{5}]\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{45})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(\mu_{R}^{2})^{2\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d-2}\hat{q}_{4}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d-2}\hat{q}_{5}}{2(2\pi)^{d-1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(\mu_R^2)^{2$\epsilon$}}\times\int\mathrm{d}E_{4}(E_{4})^{d-3}\int\mathrm{d}E_{5}(E_{5})^{d-3}\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{45}),

(2.24)

where the vectors
\hat{q}_{4}
and
\hat{q}_{5}
determine the
directions of the two gluons and the limits of the energy integrals
are so that the whole phase space is covered.

It is convenient to introduce an energy ordering among the gluons by
partitioning the phase space via

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle={}\Theta(E_{4}-E_{5})+\Theta(E_{5}-E_{4})\,

(2.25)

which leads to the split

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}(q_{1})={}

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}^{E_{4}>E_{5}}(q_{1})+\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}^{E_{5}>E_{4}}(q_{1})\,.

(2.26)

Throughout the article, we describe calculations only for the region
with
E_{4}>E_{5}
; the other region can easily be covered by performing
the same steps with the gluon momenta swapped,

q_{4}\leftrightarrow q_{5}
.
2
2

2

Note that thanks to the symmetry
between the gluons we have

\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}(q_{1})=2\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}^{E_{4}>E_{5}}(q_{1})
. However when considering a
q\bar{q}
emission such a
simplification may only be used if the symmetry

q\leftrightarrow{\bar{q}}
also holds for the observable under
consideration. Otherwise the two parts of the phase space,
introduced in
Eq.

2.26
, have to be considered
separately.
Hence, we parametrise the gluon energies as

[
31
]

\displaystyle E_{4}

\displaystyle={}E_{45,\mathrm{max}}\,\xi_{1}\left(1-\frac{\xi_{2}}{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle E_{5}

\displaystyle={}E_{45,\mathrm{max}}\,\xi_{1}\frac{\xi_{2}}{2}\,,

(2.27)

where
E_{45,\mathrm{max}}
is to be chosen such that the integration
ranges
\xi_{1}\in[0,1]
and
\xi_{2}\in[0,1]
span the whole phase
space. Using momentum conservation and considering a configuration
where the two
b
-quarks are produced at threshold, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{45,\mathrm{max}}

\displaystyle={}\frac{M_{H}\beta^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}\bar{q}_{45}^{2}}}\,,

(2.28)

where
\bar{q}_{45}=q_{45}/q^{0}_{45}
, which is only a light-like momentum
when
q_{5}
is soft or
q_{4}
and
q_{5}
are collinear. In this way, we
effectively parametrise the sum of the gluon energies
(
\xi_{1}=E_{45}/E_{45,\mathrm{max}}
) and their ratio (
\xi_{2}=2E_{5}/E_{45}
).

We also explicitly parametrise the angle
\theta_{45}
between the two
gluons as follows

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos\theta_{45})\,.

(2.29)

The first step in the phase-space construction is to choose a
direction for
\hat{q}_{4}
. Here, we explicitly parametrise angle

\theta_{4}
between the emission and the
\hat{z}
-axis.
Next, we fix the direction of
\hat{q}_{5}
relative to
\hat{q}_{4}
using
the angle
\theta_{45}
between them and the angle
\phi
which is the
azimuthal angle of
\hat{q}_{5}
around the direction of
\hat{q}_{4}
.
Given the directions
\hat{q}_{4}
and
\hat{q}_{5}
as well as
\xi_{2}
,
we calculate the vector
\bar{q}_{45}
via

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{45}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\xi_{2}}{2}\right)\hat{q}_{4}+\frac{\xi_{2}}{2}\hat{q}_{5}\,.

(2.30)

This is sufficient to use
Eq.

2.28
in order to calculate the
upper bound on the energy and from that also the individual energies

E_{4}
and
E_{5}
, which fully determines
q_{4}
and
q_{5}
.
Then we generate a Born phase-space configuration with invariant mass

Q^{2}=(q_{1}-q_{45})^{2}
in its rest frame; this is a back-to-back
configuration of the two
b
-quarks.
Finally, we boost the Born configuration to have total momentum

Q=q_{1}-q_{45}
in the Higgs rest frame in order to restore
momentum conservation.
The corresponding phase-space measure reads

\displaystyle{\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{gg}}}^{E_{4}>E_{5}}(q_{1})={}2^{2\epsilon-2}(\mu_{R}^{2})^{2\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{4}^{(2-2\epsilon)}}{2(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{4}(\sin\theta_{4})^{1-2\epsilon}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{5}^{(1-2\epsilon)}}{2(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta}{\eta^{\epsilon}(1-\eta)^{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi(\sin\phi)^{-2\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\xi_{2}E_{45,\mathrm{max}}^{4-4\epsilon}\xi_{1}^{3-4\epsilon}\xi_{2}^{1-2\epsilon}(2-\xi_{2})^{1-2\epsilon}\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1}-q_{45})\,.

(2.31)

The unparametrised angles in
\mathrm{d}\Omega_{4}
and
\mathrm{d}\Omega_{5}
can be
integrated in the end using
Eq.

2.22
.

The parametrisation shown in
Section

2.4
achieves the desired
decoupling of the emission phase space in unresolved limits. In the
collinear (
\eta\to 0
) and single-soft (
\xi_{2}\to 0
) limits the
energy bound
E_{45,\mathrm{max}}
simplifies to
E_{\mathrm{max}}=\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}M_{H}
and becomes independent of
\eta
and
\xi_{2}
. In the
single-soft limit the momentum
q_{5}
decouples from the energy-momentum
conserving
\delta
-function and the integrations over
\eta
and

\xi_{2}
decouple from the resolved phase space. In the collinear limit
the momentum conservation depends only on the sum of momenta
q_{45}
,
which is the on-shell momentum of the massless parent parton of the
splitting and is independent of
\xi_{2}
. Again, the integrations over

\eta
and
\xi_{2}
decouple from the resolved phase space. Note that the
collinear and the single-soft limits both yield the same resolved
configuration. Furthermore, in the double-soft limit, both gluons
decouple from the momentum conservation and the integrals over
\xi_{1}
,

\xi_{2}
and
\eta

2.5
Pole vs.
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
Yukawa coupling

Already in the first calculation of the radiative corrections to the
Higgs boson decay rate to fermions discussed in
Ref.
[
4
]
, it has been recognised that the result
expressed in terms of the on-shell Yukawa coupling contains large
logarithms in the limit
m_{b}/M_{H}\ll 1
. It has also been shown there
that these large logarithms can be avoided by reexpressing the result in
terms of the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
Yukawa coupling evaluated at the renormalisation
scale
\mu=M_{H}
.

The Yukawa coupling in the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme
\overline{y}_{b}(\mu)
is related to
the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass
\overline{m}_{b}(\mu)
via

\displaystyle\overline{y}_{b}(\mu)={}

\displaystyle(2\sqrt{2}G_{F})^{1/2}\,\overline{m}_{b}(\mu)\,,

(2.32)

where
\mu
is the renormalisation scale.
Thus, the relation between the on-shell and
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
Yukawa couplings
can be deduced from the corresponding relation between the masses,
which we need up to

\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha_{s}^{2}\right)

[
52
,
53
]
. It reads

\displaystyle y_{b}^{2}={}

\displaystyle\overline{y}_{b}^{2}(\mu)\left[{1+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\pi}}\right)r_{1}(m_{b},\mu)+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\pi}}\right)^{2}r_{2}(m_{b},\mu)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})}\right]\,,

(2.33)

where the coefficients
r_{i}(m_{b},\mu)
are presented in

Section

B.2
.

Note that we only reexpress the overall Yukawa coupling in this way,
but we keep the mass dependence of the matrix elements and kinematical
invariants in terms of the pole mass, similar to
Ref.
[
20
]
.

The total decay width and its expansion coefficients computed with the

\overline{\textrm{MS}}
Yukawa coupling are denoted with a bar, i.e.

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}_{{\mathrm{LO}}}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}\left[{1+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}}\right)\overline{\gamma}_{1}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}}\right)^{2}\overline{\gamma}_{2}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})}\right]\,,

(2.34)

where the expansion coefficients in the two schemes are related by

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}_{1}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+r_{1}\,,

(2.35)

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}_{2}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}={}

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+r_{1}\gamma_{1}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}+r_{2}\,.

(2.36)

As discussed before, the large logarithmic corrections to the total
decay width can be mitigated by reexpressing the result in terms of the
running
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass of the
b
-quark.
However, in a fully differential calculation these logarithms partially
enter through corrections related to real emissions.
In this case, they arise during phase-space integration of the
emission. A priori, since the mass of the
b
-quark is small compared
to the Higgs mass, one could be worried about possible numerical
instabilities when working with a fully differential
calculation. However, it turns out that in our implementation they do
not pose serious numerical problems.

3
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay at NLO

In this section we briefly describe the calculation of the NLO QCD
corrections to the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay with massive
b
-quarks.
Although such a calculation is straightforward, we find it useful to
review it in order to clarify our notation and conventions. At this
order of perturbation theory we need to consider real (R) and virtual
corrections (V).

Nowadays the fully differential treatment of this decay mode can
easily be obtained using the
FKS
[
54
,
55
]
or
Catani-Seymour
[
51
,
56
,
57
]

subtraction schemes. At NLO, our approach is essentially equivalent to
the FKS subtraction method.

3.1
Real contribution

At NLO we consider one real emission in addition to the Born process,
which means that we need to integrate the function

\displaystyle{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})={}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}(q_{1})|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})\,,

(3.1)

over the
{b\bar{b}{g}}
phase space. This integral is divergent in four
dimensions, due to the soft singularity of the gluon.
However, there are no collinear singularities since they are regulated
by the
b
-quark mass.

We define a projection operator that allows us to extract the soft
divergence and to regulate the limit. Given a quantity
A
that depends
on the momenta, we define a projection operator for the soft limit of
momentum
q_{4}
as

\displaystyle S_{4}A={}

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi_{1}\to 0}A\,,

(3.2)

where
\xi_{1}
refers to the parametrisation of

Section

2.4
.
We define the operator to act on all quantities to the right of the

S_{4}
symbol, extracting the leading asymptotic behaviour in

\xi_{1}
of the quantity
A
if the actual limit does not exist.

Denoting the identity operation by
I
, we can immediately write

\displaystyle{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})={}

\displaystyle{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})+{S_{4}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\,,

(3.3)

where the first term is now regularised in the soft limit and can be
integrated in four dimensions. The soft singularity is exposed in the
second term in
Eq.

3.3
, which therefore needs to be
evaluated in
d
dimensions. Once the soft limit is taken, the only
remaining dependence on
\xi_{1}
in
{S_{4}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})
is the
leading behaviour
\xi_{1}^{-1-2\epsilon}
. Thus, the
\xi_{1}
integration
becomes trivial and the soft singularity manifests itself as an
explicit
\epsilon^{-1}
pole.

In order to show pole cancellation pointwise in the Born phase space,
we split the real emission contribution into two parts. The finite
contribution is given by

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{(I-S_{4})}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})+g_{s}^{2}C_{F}\left({\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,4}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,4}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,4}}\right)S_{4}[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,.

(3.4)

Here, we use the factorisation formula for the soft limit as discussed
in
Section

C.2
, where also the eikonal factors

\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{ij,k}
are defined.
Moreover, we have the unresolved contribution, which contains the
integrated subtraction term and reads

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{S_{4}}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\left\langle g_{s}^{2}C_{F}\left({\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,\mathrm{int}}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,\mathrm{int}}}\right){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,,

(3.5)

with the integrated eikonal factors
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{ij,\mathrm{int}}
given in

Section

D.2
.
Here, the integral over the unresolved phase space of the gluon was
performed and we are only left with the phase-space integral over the
underlying Born process.

3.2
Virtual contribution

For the virtual contribution, the phase-space integration is the same
as that for the Born process, but we need to consider a one-loop
virtual amplitude.
Although this amplitude has an
\epsilon^{-1}
pole, the singular part can
be written as a product of a tree-level matrix element and a
kinematics-dependent coefficient, as indicated in

Section

2.3
. We have

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle={}

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle+2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}})\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle\,,

(3.6)

where the term with the
\mathbf{Z}
operator contains an explicit

\epsilon^{-1}
pole, while the second term is finite.
As a shorthand we introduce

\displaystyle{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})={}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}(q_{1})\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\,.

(3.7)

Accordingly, we define two contributions to the virtual correction: the
virtual finite contribution

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle

(3.8)

and the virtual unresolved contribution

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,.

(3.9)

The expansion coefficients of the
\mathbf{Z}
operator are given in

Eq.

2.13
.

3.3
Pole cancellation

At this point we can combine all contributions that enter the NLO
calculation.
We remind the reader that the
{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}
and

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}
terms are free of
\epsilon^{-1}
poles, while the

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}
and
{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}
terms feature

\epsilon^{-1}
poles.

Expanding the explicit results for the real unresolved contribution
from
Eq.

3.5
up to
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
we find

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left[{\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)4C_{F}\left[{1+\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{2\beta}\log\left({\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\right)}\right]\langle{}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle}\right]+\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{0}\right)\,.

(3.10)

Analogously, the virtual unresolved contribution, defined in

Eq.

3.9
, yields

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left[{\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)4C_{F}\left[{1+\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{2\beta}\log\left({\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\right)}\right]\langle{}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle}\right]\,.

(3.11)

Note that the constant term in
\epsilon
is absent since our definition of
the
\mathbf{Z}
operator of
Eq.

2.8
includes only
\epsilon^{-1}

poles.

Obviously, the poles of the two contributions cancel.
A crucial part of the argument is to notice that the

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}
part, after integrating out the real emission,
is a function of a Born-like phase-space configuration, as is the

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{U}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}
contribution. This allows us to demonstrate the
pole cancellation for any point of the
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}
phase space and
without specifying the explicit form of the
\mathrm{LO}
matrix element.

4
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay at NNLO

We now consider the NNLO QCD corrections to the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process keeping
the full dependence on the
b
-quark mass.
To this end, we need to consider several contributions including

•

the double-real contribution (RR) – where the leading-order
decay is accompanied by an emission of a pair of massless partons
(
H\to{b\bar{b}{gg}}
and
H\to{b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}{}}}
) or an additional pair of
b
-quarks
(
H\to{b\bar{b}{{b\bar{b}{}}}}
);

•

the real-virtual contribution (RV) – where we consider one-loop
virtual corrections to the process
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
;

•

the double-virtual contribution (VV) – where we consider
two-loop virtual corrections to the Born process
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
.

Except for the
H\to{b\bar{b}{{b\bar{b}{}}}}
subprocess, all these contributions are
divergent in four dimensions, due to soft and collinear singularities.
For that reason, we follow the general method recapitulated in

Section

2.2
and adopt a subtraction scheme that allows
us to regulate all singular limits and treat the divergent integrals
analytically in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions.

Apart from these divergent contributions we distinguish the subprocess

H\to{b\bar{b}{{b\bar{b}{}}}}
which enters the calculation at
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha_{s}^{2}\right)
. Indeed,
this subprocess is finite in four dimensions because of the non-zero

b
-quark mass. Hence, it does not require any regularisation. It is
calculated by directly integrating the squared tree-level amplitude over
the phase space
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}b\bar{b}}}
. In our implementation we use the
sequential algorithm
[
58
]
to generate kinematic
configurations and the phase-space measure.

Finally, a distinct class of corrections to the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay that
appears at second order of perturbation theory is related to Feynman
diagrams where the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
or
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
transition is induced by the
Higgs boson coupling to gluons via a top-quark loop.
This contribution is finite on its own and, hence, can be studied
separately – we defer the discussion of these top-quark mediated
corrections to
Section

4.5
.

All tree-level amplitudes that we use in this paper are calculated
using the spinor-helicity formalism.
3
3

3

For a review, see e.g.
Ref.
[

]
.
The treatment of massive external
particles follows along the lines of Appendix A of
Ref.
[

]
.
The one-loop amplitudes are calculated using a combination of
Passarino-Veltman reduction
[

, to express them
through one-loop scalar integrals, and spinor-helicity techniques, to

.
We assemble the two-loop using the two-loop scalar heavy-quark form

It is useful to stress that we show cancellation of all

without referring to the specific form of the matrix elements. The

to the construction of only four-dimensional matrix elements.

4.1
Double-real contribution

In this section we only focus on the
H\to{b\bar{b}{gg}}
and
H\to{b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}{}}}

subprocesses. The
H\to{b\bar{b}{{b\bar{b}{}}}}
process is completely finite on its own
and does not require any regularisation procedure. For the record we
write

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{{b\bar{b}b\bar{b}}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}b\bar{b}})\rangle\,,

(4.1)

where the shorthand
{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}b\bar{b}})
is introduced in

Eq.

2.5
.

Thanks to the non-zero
b
-quark mass, the singularity structure of
the double-real contribution is simple. Indeed, we only need to take
into account three possible limits:

•

the soft limit (
S_{5}
) – where the energy of one of the partons
vanishes, i.e.
\xi_{2}\to 0
;

•

the double-soft limit(
S_{45}
) – where the energies of both
additional partons vanish at a similar rate, i.e.
\xi_{1}\to 0
;

•

the collinear limit (
C_{45}
) – where the momenta of the two
additional partons become collinear to each other, i.e.
\eta\to 0
.

The variables
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
and
\eta
refer to the parametrisation
introduced in
Section

2.4
.

We define projection operators that allow us to extract divergences in
each of the singular regions. Given a quantity
A
which depends on the
momenta of the
b
-quarks and gluons, we define the action of the
projection operators as follows

\displaystyle S_{45}A={}

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi_{1}\to 0}A\,,

\displaystyle S_{5}A={}

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi_{2}\to 0}A\,,

\displaystyle C_{45}A={}

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}A\,.

(4.2)

Again, we note that taking limits in
Eq.

4.2

should be understood as extracting the most singular part of the
quantity
A
in a particular limit whenever the limit in the
conventional sense does not exist.

With these operators, we construct a nested subtraction formula which
extracts all singularities of the double-real contribution,

\displaystyle{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})={}

\displaystyle{(I-S_{45})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})+{S_{45}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{(I-S_{5})(I-S_{45})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})+{S_{5}(I-S_{45})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})+{S_{45}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{(I-S_{5})(I-S_{45})(I-C_{45})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})+{S_{5}(I-S_{45})(I-C_{45})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})

\displaystyle+{(I-S_{5})(I-S_{45})C_{45}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})+{S_{5}(I-S_{45})C_{45}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})

\displaystyle+{S_{45}}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})\,.

(4.3)

To derive
Section

4.1
, we start by regularising the
double-soft singularity (
S_{45}
), followed by further
regularisation of the single-soft limit (
S_{5}
). In the last step
we introduce a subtraction term for the collinear singularity
(
C_{45}
).
Moreover, as these operators commute with each other

[
25
]
, we have a freedom to choose which limit to take
first. Note that in
Section

4.1
we start with the
collinear limit, where appropriate, having in mind the simplicity of the
corresponding factorisation formulae.

We subdivide
Section

4.1
into separate contributions
according to the final state multiplicity. This allows us to discuss
pole cancellation for each of these contributions separately.

The first term on the right-hand side of
Section

4.1

represents the fully regulated double-real contribution. Therefore, it
can be evaluated in four dimensions using standard numerical techniques.
We write

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{gg}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{(I-S_{5})(I-S_{45})(I-C_{45})}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})\rangle\,,

(4.4)

and similarly for the
q\bar{q}
emission

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle n_{l}\,\langle{(I-S_{45})(I-C_{45})}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})\rangle+(q\leftrightarrow\bar{q})\,,

(4.5)

where
n_{l}
is the number of massless quark flavours and the last term
corresponds to the phase-space region with
E_{5}>E_{4}
. Note that in
case of the
q\bar{q}
emission we do not subtract the single-soft limit
(
S_{5}
) since this limit is not singular in case of a
g\to q\bar{q}

splitting. The explicit form of the subtraction terms generated by the
limit operators can be constructed using the factorisation formulae
collected in
Appendix

C
.

The next three terms on the right-hand side of

Section

4.1
are regulated in the double-soft limit,
but they are evaluated in at least one of the other two limits
(
C_{45}
or
S_{5}
). As this leaves one of the real emissions
unresolved, we call this contribution single-unresolved.
Since we perform the integral over
[\mathrm{d}{}q_{5}]
analytically, we do not need
to further regulate the
S_{5}
and
C_{45}
limits. Thus, we
rearrange these terms such that for the gluon emissions they read

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{({b\bar{b}{gg}})}={}

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\bigl(S_{5}(I-S_{45})(I-C_{45})+(I-S_{5})(I-S_{45})C_{45}

\displaystyle+S_{5}(I-S_{45})C_{45}\bigr){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(S_{5}(I-S_{45})+(I-S_{45})C_{45}-S_{5}(I-S_{45})C_{45}\bigr){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle g_{s}^{2}\left({\mathcal{P}^{(0)}_{gg,\mathrm{int}}-\mathcal{P}^{(0),\mathrm{soft}}_{gg,\mathrm{int}}+C_{A}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{24,\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{34,\mathrm{int}}-\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,\mathrm{int}})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{-C_{F}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,\mathrm{int}}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,\mathrm{int}})}\right)(\xi_{1}/2)^{-2\epsilon}{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\Bigr\rangle\,.

(4.6)

For the
q\bar{q}
emission, we obtain

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle 2n_{l}\,\langle{(I-S_{45})C_{45}}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2n_{l}\left\langle g_{s}^{2}\mathcal{P}^{(0)}_{q\bar{q},\mathrm{int}}(\xi_{1}/2)^{-2\epsilon}{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\right\rangle\,,

(4.7)

where we use the fact that in the collinear limit any infrared-safe
observable must be symmetric under
q\leftrightarrow\bar{q}
, which
leads to the factor
2
in
Section

4.1
.
To get from the first to the second lines of

Sections

4.1
and
4.1
, we use the factorisation formulae given
in
Appendix

C
and integrate them over the unresolved phase
space
[\mathrm{d}{}q_{5}]
, which yields the integrated splitting functions

\mathcal{P}^{(0)}_{ij,\mathrm{int}}
and the integrated eikonal factors
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{ij,\mathrm{int}}
.
Their calculation is described given in
Appendix

D
. The
soft-regulated matrix element
{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})
has
the same form as the real finite contribution at NLO,
Eq.

3.4
.

Finally, the last term in
Section

4.1
is evaluated
in the double-soft limit. In this case, both partons are unresolved and
we call this contribution double-unresolved. We find

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{({b\bar{b}{gg}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{S_{45}}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{gg}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle g_{s}^{4}\mathrm{D}{\hskip-1.0pt}\mathrm{Soft}^{(0)}_{gg,\mathrm{int}}(q_{2},q_{3}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,,

(4.8)

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle 2n_{l}\,\langle{S_{45}}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2n_{l}\left\langle g_{s}^{4}\mathrm{D}{\hskip-1.0pt}\mathrm{Soft}^{(0)}_{q\bar{q},\mathrm{int}}(q_{2},q_{3}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,.

(4.9)

The double-soft emissions decouple from the hard process and the whole
term can be written as a product of the double-soft function and the
Born matrix element. After performing the integration over the momenta
of the soft partons, we denote the integrated double-soft function as

\mathrm{D}{\hskip-1.0pt}\mathrm{Soft}^{(0)}_{ij,\mathrm{int}}(q_{2},q_{3})
. Details of this calculation are presented
in
Section

D.4
.

4.2
Real-virtual contribution

We need to consider the
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
amplitude at one-loop level and
integrate its product with the tree-level amplitude for the
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}

process over the phase space
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}
.
In the following, we use the shorthand

\displaystyle{}F_{LV}({b\bar{b}{g}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}(q_{1})\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}\rangle\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})\,.

(4.10)

As for the real contribution at NLO, we regulate the soft singularity
using the soft limit operator
S_{4}
defined in
Eq.

3.2

and find

\displaystyle{}F_{LV}({b\bar{b}{g}})

\displaystyle={(I-S_{4})}F_{LV}({b\bar{b}{g}})+{S_{4}}F_{LV}({b\bar{b}{g}})\,.

(4.11)

We split the one-loop amplitude into a singular part, containing all

\epsilon^{-1}
poles from loop integrals, and a finite remainder, see

Section

2.3
. We write

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}})\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}\rangle+2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}\rangle\,,

(4.12)

where the coefficient of the
\mathbf{Z}
operator is given in

Section

2.3
and the second term contains the finite remainder of
the one-loop amplitude defined in
Eq.

2.10
.

In total, we split the calculation into four contributions, which need
to be combined with the corresponding terms from the double-real and
double-virtual contributions to show pole cancellation.
The first one is given by the soft-regulated terms containing the
finite remainder of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
matrix element. It is free of

\epsilon^{-1}
poles and thus we refer to it as the real-virtual finite
contribution. It reads

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{F}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{(I-S_{4})}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})

\displaystyle+g_{s}^{2}C_{F}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,4}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,4}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,4})

\displaystyle\hphantom{-}\times\left({S_{4}[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)\left[{\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{23,4}+Z_{A}^{(1)}+Z_{\alpha_{s}}^{(1)}}\right]_{\epsilon^{0}}S_{4}[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})}\right)\Bigr\rangle\,,

(4.13)

where the necessary formulae for the factorisation of the one-loop
matrix element in the soft limit are given in
Appendix

C
and
the the renormalisation constants
Z_{A}
and
Z_{\alpha_{s}}
are given in

Appendix

A
. The notation
[\ldots]_{\epsilon^{0}}
indicates that the

\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{0}\right)
term of the expression between the brackets should be
taken. We use
{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})
in analogy to the definition in

Eq.

3.7
.

The corresponding term containing the soft-regulated
\mathbf{Z}
operator
for the
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
process is called real-virtual single-unresolved
contribution and is given by

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)(I-S_{4})\,2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})

\displaystyle+g_{s}^{2}\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)C_{F}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,4}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,4}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,4})

\displaystyle\hphantom{-}\times\left({\left[{\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{23,4}+Z_{\alpha_{s}}^{(1)}+Z_{A}^{(1)}}\right]_{\mathrm{poles}}+2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}})}\right)S_{4}[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\Bigr\rangle\,,

(4.14)

where the subscript “poles” refers to taking only the pole terms up to

\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
into account.

The remaining two contributions are evaluated in the soft limit and are
integrated over the unresolved phase space
[\mathrm{d}{}q_{4}]
. The integrated
subtraction term containing the finite remainder carries the superscript

\mathrm{FR}
and reads

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{S_{4}}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{g}})\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\left\langle g_{s}^{2}C_{F}\left({\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,\mathrm{int}}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,\mathrm{int}}}\right){}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\right\rangle\,.

(4.15)

Again, the
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{ij,\mathrm{int}}
correspond to integrated eikonal factors
discussed in
Section

D.2
.

Finally, the contribution containing the soft limit of the
\mathbf{Z}

operator integrated over the unresolved phase space is referred to as
the real-virtual double-unresolved contribution. We obtain

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)S_{4}\,2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle g_{s}^{2}\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)C_{F}\left({\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{\mathrm{int}}-\left[{Z_{\alpha_{s}}^{(1)}+Z_{A}^{(1)}+2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}})}\right]}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{\hphantom{g_{s}^{2}\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)C_{F}}\times(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{22,\mathrm{int}}-2\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{23,\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{33,\mathrm{int}})}\right){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\Bigr\rangle\,.

(4.16)

The integrated one-loop soft function
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{\mathrm{int}}
is defined and
calculated in
Section

D.3
.

4.3
Double-virtual contribution

For the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
process, the virtual corrections are described by a
single form factor,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=F_{s}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle\,.

(4.17)

In
Eq.

4.17
,
|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle
is the tree-level
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}

amplitude, and
F_{s}
is the scalar heavy-quark form factor, which can
be computed perturbatively as

\displaystyle F_{s}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})={}

\displaystyle 1+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{4\pi}}\right)F_{s}^{(1)}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})

\displaystyle\hphantom{1}+\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{4\pi}}\right)^{2}F_{s}^{(2)}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})+\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\alpha_{s}^{3}\right)\,.

(4.18)

The expansion coefficients
F_{s}^{(k)}
depend on Higgs and
b
-quark
masses, the renormalisation scale
\mu_{R}
and the regulator
\epsilon
. The
two-loop heavy-quark form factor was computed in
Ref.
[
65
]
up to
\epsilon^{0}
terms and to higher
orders in
\epsilon
in Refs.
[
68
]
and

[
64
]
. In our calculation we use the results of
Ref.
[
64
]
.

We again use the
\mathbf{Z}
operator to split the double-virtual
correction into divergent terms, that contain all
\epsilon^{-1}
poles, and
a finite remainder.
In this case, given the functions
F_{s}^{(1)}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})
and

F_{s}^{(2)}(M_{H}^{2},m_{b}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})
we reconstruct the finite remainders

|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle
and
|\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle
using the
definition in
Eq.

2.10
. We also checked that all

\epsilon^{-1}
poles of the form factor are in agreement with the

\mathbf{Z}
operator prediction of
Eqs.

2.13
and
2.3
.
The finite double-virtual contribution reads

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{F}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\left[{2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}|\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}\rangle}\right]\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{kin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\Bigr\rangle\,.

(4.19)

Furthermore, we distinguish two contributions that contain all

\epsilon^{-1}
poles. First, we have a term that is proportional to the
finite remainder of the one-loop
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
matrix element. It is given by

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}){}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\Bigr\rangle\,.

(4.20)

The other contribution containing
\epsilon^{-1}
poles comes with the Born

H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
matrix element; it reads

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)^{2}\left({2\operatorname{Re}(\mathbf{Z}^{(2)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}})+{\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{Z}^{(1)}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}}\right){}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\Bigr\rangle\,.

(4.21)

The expansion coefficients of the
\mathbf{Z}
operator are given in

Eqs.

2.13
and
2.3
.

4.4
Pole cancellation

After collecting formulae for all NNLO contributions in the preceeding
subsections, we demonstrate pole cancellation.
We begin by considering

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}^{\mathrm{FR}}{}={\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{}+{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{}
. From the
real-virtual contribution,
Section

4.2
, we obtain the pole term

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left[{\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)4C_{F}\left[{1+\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{2\beta}\log\left({\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\right)}\right]\langle{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle}\right]+\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{0}\right)\,,

(4.22)

and the explicit expansion of the double-virtual contribution,

Eq.

4.20
, yields

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{FR}}{({b\bar{b}{}})}\rangle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left[{\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)4C_{F}\left[{1+\frac{1+\beta^{2}}{2\beta}\log\left({\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\right)}\right]\langle{}F_{LV}^{\mathrm{fin}}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle}\right]\,,

(4.23)

which obviously cancels in the sum with
Eq.

4.22
.

For the single-unresolved contribution we have

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}^{\mathrm{SU}}{}={\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{}+{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{}
. The
double-real contribution,
Sections

4.1
and
4.1
, has an expansion
which can be cast into the form

\displaystyle{2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{({b\bar{b}{gg}}+{b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}})}\rangle}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Biggl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)\Biggl[\frac{2C_{A}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\biggl[4C_{F}+\beta_{0}(n_{l})+\frac{C_{A}-2C_{F}}{v_{23}}\log\left({\frac{1+v_{23}}{1-v_{23}}}\right)

\displaystyle+2C_{A}\log\left({\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{2}}\cdot{q_{4}})}}\right)+2C_{A}\log\left({\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{3}}\cdot{q_{4}})}}\right)\biggr]\Biggr]{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\Biggr\rangle+\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{0}\right)\,.

(4.24)

A similar expansion holds for the real-virtual single-unresolved
contribution,
Section

4.2
,

\displaystyle{2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{SU}}{({b\bar{b}{g}})}\rangle}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Biggl\langle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)\Biggl[-\frac{2C_{A}}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\biggl[4C_{F}+\beta_{0}(n_{l})+\frac{C_{A}-2C_{F}}{v_{23}}\log\left({\frac{1+v_{23}}{1-v_{23}}}\right)

\displaystyle+2C_{A}\log\left({\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{2}}\cdot{q_{4}})}}\right)+2C_{A}\log\left({\frac{m_{b}\mu_{R}}{2({q_{3}}\cdot{q_{4}})}}\right)\biggr]\Biggr]{(I-S_{4})}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{g}})\Biggr\rangle\,.

(4.25)

As before, the poles of the two contributions cancel.

Finally, we turn to the double-unresolved contribution, given by

{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}={\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}+{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}+{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}
. The real-virtual and double-virtual contributions
are known analytically, but we only have numerical results for the
double-real contribution at our disposal. Therefore, we demonstrate
pole cancellation numerically. We write the pole terms as

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}^{\mathrm{DU}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}}\right)^{2}\left[{\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{DU},(-3)}}{\epsilon^{3}}+\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{DU},(-2)}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{DU},(-1)}}{\epsilon}}\right]\langle{}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle+\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{0}\right)\,,

(4.26)

and further subdivide the pole coefficients according to colour factors,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{DU},(k)}={}

\displaystyle C_{F}C_{A}\,\mathcal{C}_{C_{F}C_{A}}^{\mathrm{DU},(k)}+C_{F}^{\,2}\,\mathcal{C}_{C_{F}^{2}}^{\mathrm{DU},(k)}+C_{F}T_{F}n_{l}\,\mathcal{C}_{C_{F}T_{F}n_{l}}^{\mathrm{DU},(k)}+C_{F}T_{F}\,\mathcal{C}_{C_{F}T_{F}}^{\mathrm{DU},(k)}\,.

(4.27)

4.5
Top-quark contribution to the

An additional contribution that enters the

b
-quarks are generated by a gluon splitting. The corresponding
diagrams are shown in
Figs.

1(a)
and
1(b)
and their
contributions to the decay rate are separately finite.

This contribution to the total
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
width has been computed in
Refs.
[

as an expansion in powers of

x_{t}=(M_{H}^{2}/m_{t}^{2})
.
An exact result, incorporated into a fully differential calculation,
has been recently published in Ref.
[
13
]
. At the level
of the total decay width, the differences between the approximate and
the exact results are very small for realistic Higgs and top-quark
masses. Since we aim at testing our calculation against results
reported in Ref.
[
20
]
, we will use the result of
Ref.
[
11
]
to include the top-quark contribution.

Note that this contribution should naively scale with one power of

y_{b}
and one power of
y_{t}
, hence we refer to this as the
y_{b}y_{t}

contribution. However, the amplitudes of
Fig.

1
can
interfere with the respective Born amplitudes only if a helicity flip
occurs on one of the
b
-quark lines. This mechanism provides an
additional power of
m_{b}
.
Moreover, the top-quark loop leads to a suppression factor of

1/m_{t}
, which makes the overall scaling of the
y_{b}y_{t}
contribution
similar to all other terms, i.e. proportional to a square of the

b
-quark mass.

We write the
y_{b}y_{t}
contribution to the total decay width
as
[
11
]

\displaystyle\Gamma^{y_{b}y_{t}}={}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathrm{LO}}}\,\left({\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}}\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{y_{b}y_{t}}\,,

(4.28)

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}^{y_{b}y_{t}}={}

\displaystyle\beta^{-3}\left({f_{2}^{S,0}+x_{t}f_{2}^{S,1}+x_{t}^{\,2}f_{2}^{S,2}+x_{t}^{\,3}f_{2}^{S,3}+\mathcal{O}(x_{t}^{\,4})}\right)

(4.29)

and the coefficients
f_{2}^{S,k}
are given in Eq. (2) of
Ref.
[
11
]
. The factor of
\beta^{-3}
appears since the
normalisation factor in
Eq.

4.28
involves the LO width for
the massive
b
-quarks, in contrast to Ref.
[
11
]
.

Since the contribution in
Eq.

4.28
starts only at

\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, changes due to decoupling,

Eq.

A.12
, or due to translating the on-shell Yukawa
coupling to the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme,
Eq.

2.33
, are of
higher order in
\alpha_{s}
and are discarded.

The real-virtual amplitude related to the diagram in

Fig.

1(b)
is computed using the same techniques as the
remaining one-loop amplitudes. The double-virtual amplitude of

Fig.

1(a)
is obtained from the result of
Ref.
[
11
]
, see
Eq.

4.28
, by subtracting the
integrated real-virtual contribution of
Fig.

1(b)
.

5
Results

In this section, we summarise our calculation and present results for
the total decay width of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{}}
decay and a selection of jet rates.
We compare these predictions to results already available in the
literature.
The main goal is to scrutinise our calculation as much as possible to
ensure its correctness.

The value of the strong coupling is set to
\alpha_{s}(M_{Z})=0.1181
with

M_{Z}=91.1876~\mathrm{GeV}

[
58
]
. The evolution is
performed at two-loop order with five active flavours using the
package
RunDec

[
69
,
70
]
.
We use a value for the Higgs boson mass of

M_{H}=125.09~\mathrm{GeV}

[
1
]
.
As a starting point for the
b
-quark mass, we use

\overline{m}_{b}(\mu=\overline{m}_{b})=4.18~\mathrm{GeV}

[
58
]
. From this
we calculate the pole mass
m_{b}=4.78~\mathrm{GeV}
, using the two-loop
matching formula at a matching scale of
\mu=\overline{m}_{b}(\overline{m}_{b})

implemented in the package
RunDec
.
For the value of the Fermi constant we use

G_{F}=1.166378\times 10^{-5}~\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}

[
58
]
.
The central renormalisation scale is taken to be equal to the mass of
the Higgs boson,
\mu_{R}=M_{H}
, and for the scale uncertainty
estimation we vary it by a factor of
1/2
and
2
.

5.1
Overview of the calculation

We combine all contributions discussed in
Sections

3
and
4
to
obtain the full NLO and NNLO results.
At leading order in QCD we have

\displaystyle 2M_{H}\,\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{{\mathrm{LO}}}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{}{}F_{LM}({b\bar{b}{}})\rangle\,.

(5.1)

At NLO we combine real and virtual corrections

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\delta{\mathrm{NLO}}}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{R}}{}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{V}}{}\rangle\,,

(5.2)

which contain finite and unresolved parts as discussed in

Section

3
.
The situation is slightly more complicated at NNLO where there are
more ingredients entering the final result. The result reads

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\delta{\mathrm{NNLO}}}^{{b\bar{b}{}}}{}\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{VV}}{}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RV}}{}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{\mathrm{RR}}{}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{{b\bar{b}b\bar{b}}}{}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}_{{b\bar{b}{}}}^{y_{b}y_{t}}{}\rangle\,.

(5.3)

The double-virtual, real-virtual and double-real contributions consist
of finite, single- and double-unresolved parts as outlined in

Section

4
.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we presented an independent calculation of the NNLO QCD
corrections to the Higgs boson decay into massive
b
-quarks.
We worked in the framework of the nested soft-collinear subtraction
scheme introduced in
Refs.
[
25
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
.

A complete discussion of all necessary NNLO contributions was
presented in
Section

4
.
In particular, we demonstrated cancellation of all
\epsilon^{-1}
poles,
related to soft and collinear singularities of QCD amplitudes.
The cancellation was obtained pointwise in phase space, without
referring to a specific form of the matrix elements.
Furthermore, a full treatment of the
b
-quark mass allowed for an
inclusion of the additional contribution which originates from a
direct interaction of top quarks with the Higgs boson, at a
differential level.

Our fully differential calculation was implemented in a computer
program.
We carefully tested it by performing a number of cross-checks with
results available in the literature both for the total decay
width
[
10
,
20
]
and jet
rates
[
20
]
.

Appendix A
Renormalisation

The subtraction scheme that we apply in this calculation is formulated
in terms of UV-renormalised amplitudes. Here, we describe the
renormalisation prescriptions used in our calculation.

We employ a hybrid scheme in which we renormalise the quark and gluon
fields, the quark masses and the Yukawa coupling in an on-shell
scheme, whereas we use the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}{}
scheme with five active flavours
for the strong coupling constant. We write

\displaystyle\alpha_{s,b}

\displaystyle=(\mu_{R}^{2})^{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}Z_{\alpha_{s}}\alpha_{s}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{b}^{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Z_{\psi}}\psi_{b}\,,

\displaystyle G_{\mu}^{0,a}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Z_{A}}G_{\mu}^{a}\,,

\displaystyle m_{b}^{0}

\displaystyle=Z_{m}m_{b}\,,

\displaystyle y_{b}^{0}

\displaystyle=Z_{m}y_{b}\,,

(A.1)

where

S_{\epsilon}=(4\pi)^{-\epsilon}\exp(\epsilon\gamma_{\mathrm{E}})
.
4
4

4

agrees with the renormalisation scheme in
[

difference has already been discussed in

C.1
Single-collinear factorisation

In order to discuss the single-collinear limit of the
H\to{b\bar{b}{gg}}
and

H\to{b\bar{b}{q\bar{q}}}
matrix element, we have to define a perpendicular direction

k_{\perp}^{\mu}
which determines how the collinear limit is approached. We
choose

\displaystyle k_{\perp}^{\mu}={}

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\hat{q}_{5}-\hat{q}_{4}}{\|\hat{q}_{5}-\hat{q}_{4}\|}\,,

(C.1)

where
\eta
refers to the phase-space parametrisation in

Section

2.4
.
Then the factorisation formula in the single-collinear limit
reads

\displaystyle C_{45}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{ij}}}|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{({q_{4}}\cdot{q_{5}})}\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\mu}|\mathcal{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{ij}(z,k_{\perp};\epsilon)|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\nu}\rangle\,,

(C.2)

where
ij
stands for either the
gg
or
q\bar{q}
channel and

|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\nu}\rangle
is the spin-correlated
H\to{b\bar{b}{g}}
amplitude
with the gluon polarisation vector removed, i.e.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}\rangle={}

\displaystyle(\varepsilon^{\mu}(q_{4},\lambda))^{\ast}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\mu}\rangle\,.

(C.3)

The splitting functions
\mathcal{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{ij}
carry Lorentz indices
which are contracted with the corresponding index of the gluon. For the

gg
and
q\bar{q}{}
channels they read

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{gg}(z,k_{\perp};\epsilon)={}

\displaystyle 2C_{A}\left[{-g^{\mu\nu}\left({\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{1-z}{z}}\right)-2(1-\epsilon)z(1-z)\frac{k_{\perp}^{\mu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}}{k_{\perp}^{\,2}}}\right]\,,

(C.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(0),\mu\nu}_{q\bar{q}}(z,k_{\perp};\epsilon)={}

\displaystyle T_{F}\left[{-g^{\mu\nu}+4z(1-z)\frac{k_{\perp}^{\mu}k_{\perp}^{\nu}}{k_{\perp}^{\,2}}}\right]\,,

(C.5)

with

\displaystyle z={}

\displaystyle\frac{E_{4}}{E_{4}+E_{5}}=1-\frac{\xi_{2}}{2}\,.

(C.6)

As the factorisation formula above indicates, we have to evaluate
spin-correlated matrix elements. In the term where they are contracted
with the metric tensor
g^{\mu\nu}
, the result corresponds to an
uncorrelated matrix element via the polarisation sum. This leads to

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\mu}|(-g^{\mu\nu})|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\nu}\rangle={}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}}}|^{2}\,.

(C.7)

For four-dimensional matrix elements, the product

k_{\perp}^{\mu}|\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{{b\bar{b}{g}},\nu}\rangle
can be linked to helicity amplitudes
using the fact that the perpendicular vector
k_{\perp}^{\mu}
may be decomposed
as

\displaystyle k_{\perp}^{\mu}={}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\lambda}({\varepsilon(q_{4},\lambda)}\cdot{k_{\perp}})(\varepsilon^{\mu}(q_{4},\lambda))^{\ast}\,.

(C.8)

This is true since, by construction,
k_{\perp}^{\mu}
lies in the plane
perpendicular to the momentum of a gluon,
q_{4}
, which is spanned by
the polarisation vectors
\varepsilon^{\mu}(q_{4},\lambda)
.

For the integrated subtraction terms of the double-real contribution

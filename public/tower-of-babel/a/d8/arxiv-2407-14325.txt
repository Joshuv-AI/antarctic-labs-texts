Abstract
. We study Schrödinger operators on ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} H = ( − ∂ 2 ∂ x 1 2 ) α / 2 + ( − ∂ 2 ∂ x 2 2 ) α / 2 + V , H=\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}+\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}+V, for α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) and some sufficiently regular, radial, confining potentials V V . We obtain necessary and sufficient conditions on intrinsic ultracontractivity for semigroups { e − t ​ H : t ≥ 0 } \{e^{-tH}:\,t\geq 0\} . We also get sharp estimates of first eigenfunctions of H H .



1.
Introduction

The following anisotropic, singular Lévy operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3N}

L^{(rel)}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{3k-2}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{3k-1}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{3k}^{2}}+m^{2}}+Nm,

where
m>0
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, plays an important role in some models of relativistic quantum mechanics. For example, in Lieb and Seiringer’s book
[
26
, see formula (3.2.6)]
it is a kinetic term in the relativistic Schrödinger Hamiltonian for
N
electrons. It appears also in the relativistic spinless Salpeter equation for a two-body system, see e.g.
[
29
, formula (12)]
, and in the model of a relativistic gravitating system of
N
neutral particles, see e.g.
[
26
, formula (3.2.11)]
.

In our paper we study Schrödinger operators based on the following anisotropic, singular Lévy operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, which is called the cylindrical fractional Laplacian

L^{(\alpha)}=-\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}-\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2},

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
. For
\alpha=1
this operator may be viewed as a simplified version of
L^{(rel)}
. We believe that methods from our paper will be useful in investigating Schrödinger operators based on
L^{(rel)}
.

Schrödinger operators considered in this paper have the following form

Hf(x)=-L^{(\alpha)}f(x)+V(x)f(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

where potentials
V
satisfy the following assumptions.

Assumptions (A).
Let
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be a radial function, which has a profile function
q:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
(that is
q(|x|)=V(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
) satisfying the following conditions.

a)
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}q(x)=\infty
,

b)
q
is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
,

c)
q
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
,

d) there exists
C_{0}\geq 1
such that for every
x\in[0,\infty)

q(x+1)\leq C_{0}(q(x)+1).

Typical examples of potentials
V
satisfying assumptions (A) are
V(x)=|x|^{\beta}
,
V(x)=\exp(\beta|x|)
for
\beta>0
. On the other hand, functions
V(x)=\exp(|x|^{\beta})
for
\beta>1
fail to satisfy condition d).

The Schrödinger operator
H
generates a semigroup of symmetric operators
\{e^{-tH}:\,t\geq 0\}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, such that
e^{-tH}:\,L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for
t>0
. It is well known that
T_{t}:=e^{-tH}
is an integral operator, whose kernel is given by the function
u_{t}(x,y)
, i.e.
T_{t}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dy
,
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
t>0

[
15
, Chapter 2.B]
. For any
t>0
the kernel
u_{t}(x,y)
is continuous, bounded and positive on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(the proof of this property is similar to the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
25
]
). Each
T_{t}
for
t>0
is a compact operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(see e.g.
[
19
]
, Lemmas 1 and 9 for a general argument) and the spectrum of
H
consists of a sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\ldots,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty.

The corresponding eigenfunctions
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
may be chosen so that they form an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, all
\varphi_{n}
are continuous and bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and the first eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}
is strictly positive on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Clearly, we have
T_{t}\varphi_{n}(x)=e^{-\lambda_{n}t}\varphi_{n}(x)
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t>0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

It is well known that the Schrödinger semigroup
\{T_{t}:\,t\geq 0\}
has the following probabilistic Feynman–Kac representation. For any
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
t>0
the following equality holds (in the sense of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
)

(1.1)

T_{t}f(x)=E^{x}\left(\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}V(X_{s})\,ds\right)f(X_{t})\right),

which allows us to use probabilistic methods.

The most important result of this paper deals with intrinsic ultracontractivity (IU) for the semigroup
\{T_{t}:\,t\geq 0\}
and estimates of the first eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}
. IU was introduced by E. B. Davies and B. Simon in
[
13
]
. The semigroup
\{T_{t}:\,t\geq 0\}
is called intrinsically ultracontractive if and only if for any
t>0
there exist positive constants
c_{1,t},c_{2,t}
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we have

c_{1,t}\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(y)\leq u_{t}(x,y)\leq c_{2,t}\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(y).

The main result of this paper is the following theorem (recall that
q
is a profile function for
V
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
V
satisfies assumptions (A). Then
\{T_{t}:\,t\geq 0\}
is intrinsically ultracontractive if and only if

(1.2)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{q(x)}{\ln{x}}=\infty.

Moreover, if (
1.2
) holds, then there exist constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\alpha,q)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(\alpha,q)>0
, such that for every
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\begin{multlined}\frac{c_{1}}{(|x_{1}|+1)^{1+\alpha}(|x_{2}|+1)^{1+\alpha}(q(\min(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|))+1)(q(|x|)+1)}\leq\varphi_{1}(x)\\
\leq\frac{c_{2}}{(|x_{1}|+1)^{1+\alpha}(|x_{2}|+1)^{1+\alpha}(q(\min(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|))+1)(q(|x|)+1)}.\end{multlined}

IU has been introduced in
[
13
]
for very general semigroups. Important examples of such semigroups are those generated by Dirichlet Laplacians on domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and by Schrödinger operators
H=-\Delta+V
both on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as well as on domains of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(with Dirichlet boundary conditions). IU for such classical semigroups has been studied in e.g.
[
13
,
12
,
1
,
14
]
. In the recent years IU and ground state estimates have also been studied for Schrödinger semigroups generated by
H=-L+V
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
V
is a confining potential in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
L
is a generator of a symmetric Lévy process in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which Lévy measure has a density (with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
) satisfying some regularity conditions, see e.g.
[
20
,
9
,
21
]
.

The cylindrical fractional Laplacian
L^{(\alpha)}
, which we consider in our paper, is the generator of the process
X=(X^{(1)},X^{(2)})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, which components
X^{(1)}
,
X^{(2)}
are independent, one-dimensional, symmetric, standard
\alpha
-stable processes. Note that
L^{(\alpha)}
is a singular Lévy operator, because the Lévy measure of the corresponding process
X
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(it is supported by coordinate axes). The study of Schrödinger operators based on such singular operator requires new methods. In particular in Section 4 we use some new probabilistic techniques in order to estimate
T_{t}\mathds{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(x)
.

The main motivation of our research comes from an attempt to thoroughly analyze Schrödinger operators
H=-L^{(rel)}+V
, which appear in some models of relativistic physics. However, another motivation comes from mathematics. We are interested in understanding Schrödinger operators
H=-L+V
, when
L
is a singular, nonlocal Lévy operator, that is
L
is a generator of a jump Lévy process with a singular Lévy measure. Typical examples of such processes are
X=(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(d)})
, which components
X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(d)}
are independent, one-dimensional jump processes. Such processes
X
appear naturally in many models. In recent years singular, nonlocal Lévy operators have been intensively studied both by probabilists and specialists in PDEs, see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
28
]
.
An interesting open problem is the following.

Open question.
What are the first eigenfunction estimates and conditions for IU for Schrödinger semigroups corresponding to operators
H=-L+V
, when
L
is a general symmetric stable operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(not necessarily isotropic)?

Such general symmetric stable operators have been studied in e.g.
[
27
,
16
,
17
]
.

In our paper we use some probabilistic ideas from
[
25
]
, however, there are essential differences between our paper and
[
25
]
due to singularity of the Lévy measure of the process
X
. We also borrow some arguments from
[
6
]
(see Lemma 4.5 in
[
6
]
).

The techniques used in the paper are quite complicated therefore we concentrate on the case of dimension
d=2
. Our goal is to present the idea, not to achieve maximal generality with respect to dimension. We do not want the technicalities to obscure the approach we propose.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce notation and collect known facts needed in the sequel. In Section 3 we obtain lower bound estimates of
T_{t}\mathds{1}_{D}(x)
. The most difficult and important is Section 4, where we prove an upper bound of
T_{t}\mathds{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(x)
. In Section 5 we use the results obtained in two previous sections to prove the main result of our paper – Theorem
1.1
.

Preliminaries

In the whole paper we assume that
\alpha\in(0,2)

V

Let

be a cylindrical
\alpha
-stable process in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Its components

,

are independent, one-dimensional, symmetric, standard
\alpha
-stable processes. The characteristic function of

, where

,

. The transition density of

is denoted by
\tilde{p}(t,x,y)
,

,

. The transition density of
X
is denoted by
p(t,x,y)
,
t>0
,
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Clearly, we have
p(t,x,y)=\tilde{p}(t,x_{1},y_{1})\tilde{p}(t,x_{2},y_{2})
for any
t>0
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. By
\tau_{D}
we denote the first exit time for an open set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, i.e.
\tau_{D}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\,X_{t}\notin D\}
. By
p_{D}(t,x,y)
we denote the density of the process
X
started at
x
and killed on exiting an open set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

p_{D}(t,x,y)=p(t,x,y)-E^{x}(\tau_{D}\leq t,p(t-\tau_{D},X(\tau_{D}),y)),

for
x,y\in D
. Let
a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a<b
,
c<d
. For
D=(a,b)\times(c,d)
,
t>0
,

,

(2.1)

p_{D}(t,x,y)=\tilde{p}_{(a,b)}(t,x_{1},y_{1})\tilde{p}_{(c,d)}(t,x_{2},y_{2}),

where
\tilde{p}_{U}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is the transition density of the process
X^{(1)}
killed on exiting an open set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

It is well known that the Lévy measure of the process
X
is given by

\mu(dz_{1}\,dz_{2})=\delta_{0}(dz_{1})\times\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{|z_{2}|^{1+\alpha}}dz_{2}+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{|z_{1}|^{1+\alpha}}dz_{1}\times\delta_{0}(dz_{2}),

where
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\alpha 2^{\alpha-1}\Gamma((1+\alpha)/2)\pi^{-1/2}(\Gamma(1-\alpha/2))^{-1}
and
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

For any
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we denote

\mu_{y}(dz_{1}\,dz_{2})=\delta_{y_{1}}(dz_{1})\times\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{|z_{2}-y_{2}|^{1+\alpha}}dz_{2}+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{|z_{1}-y_{1}|^{1+\alpha}}dz_{1}\times\delta_{y_{2}}(dz_{2}).

Now we state a generalization of the Ikeda-Watanabe formula (expressing the distribution of
(\tau_{D},X(\tau_{D}))
) for the process

(cf.
[

.

Assume that
D
is open, nonempty, bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and

. Then for any
x\in D
,

(2.2)

P^{x}(X(\tau_{D})\in A,t_{1}<\tau_{D}<t_{2})=\int_{D}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}p_{D}(s,x,y)\,ds\mu_{y}(A)\,dy.

The proof is almost the same as the proof of
[
25

and it is omitted. Let

with

,

. When

where

\tilde{\tau}_{(a,b)}^{(1)}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\,X_{t}^{(1)}\notin(a,b)\}
and

\tilde{\tau}_{(c,d)}^{(2)}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\,X_{t}^{(2)}\notin(c,d)\}
.

We will study IU of the semigroup
\{T_{t}:\,t\geq 0\}
checking the following conditions.

Condition 2.2
.

There exists an open, bounded and nonempty set

For any open, bounded and nonempty set

such that for any

It is known (see e.g. arguments from

Now we present some estimates, which will be needed in the sequel.

.

For any

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{r}{n}}}{n(n+1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{r}{n+1}}}{n(n+1)}

The above result follows from Lemmas 5.2 and 5.6 from

We denote

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

P^{x}\left(X(\tau_{B_{a}})\in\frac{D}{2},t_{1}<\tau_{B_{a}}<t_{2}\right)\geq\frac{c(t_{0})\,(t_{2}-t_{1})}{(|x_{1}|+1)^{1+\alpha}}.

Sets

We may assume that

From the Ikeda-Watanabe formula (

\frac{c}{(|x_{1}|+1)^{1+\alpha}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}P^{x}(\tau_{B_{a}}>s)\,ds.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}

\begin{split}h(a,x,t)&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2i}(C_{0}q(|x_{1}|)+C_{0})}\,\frac{c_{1}(t)\,\left(\frac{t}{2i}-\frac{t}{2(i+1)}\right)}{(|x_{1}|+1)^{1+\alpha}}\\

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\displaystyle U_{x}

Lemma 5.1

Polarizations of Artin monomial ideals
Gunnar Fløystad, Ine Gabrielsen, Amir Mafi

Abstract
. We show that any polarization of an Artin monomial ideal defines a triangulated ball. This settles a conjecture of A.Almousa, H.Lohne and the first author [ 3 ] . Geometrically, polarizations of ideals strictly containing ( x 1 a 1 , … , x n a n ) (x_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{a_{n}}) define full-dimensional triangulated balls on the sphere which is the join of boundaries of simplices of dimensions a 1 − 1 , ⋯ , a n − 1 a_{1}-1,\cdots,a_{n}-1 . Every full-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay sub-complex of this joined sphere is of this kind, and these balls are constructible. Such a triangulated ball has a dual cell complex which is a sub-complex of the product of simplices of dimensions a 1 − 1 , ⋯ a n − 1 a_{1}-1,\cdots a_{n}-1 . The associated cellular complex of this gives the minimal free resolution of the Alexander dual ideal of the triangulated ball. When the product of simplices is a hypercube, in many examples these dual cell complexes enables a classification of the range of polarizations of the Artin monomial ideal. We also show that the squeezed balls of G.Kalai [ 32 ] derive from polarizations of Artin monomial ideals.

1.
Introduction

A polarization of an Artin monomial ideal is a squarefree monomial
ideal and so corresponds to a simplicial complex via the Stanley-Reisner (SR)
correspondence.
We prove a fundamental theorem on the combinatorial geometry of monomial
ideals:

For any polarization of an
Artin
monomial ideal, the associated
simplicial complex has a topological realization which is a
ball

(save when the Artin monomial ideal is a complete intersection, then
the polarization gives a sphere).

This proves a conjecture by A. Almousa, H. Lohne, and the first author

[
3
, Conjecture 3.2]
. We give further a detailed geometric
analysis and understanding of polarizations of Artin monomial
ideals in
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
For a finite set
V
, let
\Delta(V)
be the geometric simplex
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{V}
with vertices
V
. For finite sets
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n}

the product polytope

\Pi(A_{\bullet})=\Delta(A_{1})\times\Delta(A_{2})\times\cdots\times\Delta(A_{n})

(if each cardinality
|A_{i}|=2
this is a hypercube), is dual (polar) to
the simplicial polytope which is the join of boundaries

\partial\Delta(A_{\bullet})=\partial\Delta(A_{1})*\partial\Delta(A_{2})*\cdots*\partial\Delta(A_{n}),

(this is a hyperoctahedron when each
|A_{i}|=2
).
The triangulated balls obtained from polarizations are precisely
the full-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay sub-complexes of
\partial\Delta(A_{\bullet})
.
Moreover we show such sub-complexes
are
constructible
simplicial complexes.

These sub-complexes have dual cell sub-complexes
of the product polytope
\Pi(A_{\bullet})
, which are
acyclic
, i.e. for
restrictions to every sub-cell
\Pi(R_{\bullet})
of
\Pi(A_{\bullet})

(where
R_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
), all
homologies vanish. In many example cases this enables simple analysis
and classification of all polarizations of an Artin monomial ideal,
Section
7
.

We proceed to recall the notions of separation and polarization,
with an example. We further give more detailed descriptions of the above.

the

Let us inform on a line of research leading to our results.
In
[
9
]
, T.Bier
constructed simplicial spheres
from a simplicial complex
X
on a set
V
. Essentially
the idea is that
X
and its Alexander dual should glue together
to give a simplicial sphere, as in the topological setting of
Alexander duality,
[
27
]
, see
[
35
, Sec.5.6]
for an argument.

In
[
11
]
Björner, Pfaffenholz, Sjöstrand and Ziegler
generalized this from
down-sets in Boolean posets (that is, simplicial complexes) to
down-sets in any poset.
In addition they proved (by a counting
argument) that Bier
spheres were so numerous that most of them
could not be realized as convex polytopes. Bier spheres
were considerably generalized by D’Alì, Nematbakhsh and the first
author in
[
16
]
: Given any poset
P
let

\mathcal{I}
be a finite down-set in the set of order-preserving maps

{\rm{Hom}}(P,{\mathbb{N}})
.
This gives rise to the so called letterplace ideals

L(\mathcal{I};P)
, see Subsection
11.3
or
[
21
]
,
which are SR-ideals of triangulated balls and whose boundaries
are simplicial spheres, again with a simply defined SR-ideal.
Our main result here is a further considerable generalization of
letterplace ideals.
In particular since even the double specialization to
Bier spheres is so numerous that they do not in general
have convex realizations (though recently shown
to be star-shaped
[
31
]
),
the same holds for the simplicial spheres given here.

2.1.
Simplicial complexes and squarefree monomial ideals

Let
V
be a finite set. A simplicial complex on
V
is
a family
X
of subsets of
V
such that if
F\in X

and
G\subseteq F
, then also
G\in X
. The
F
are the

faces
of
X
and the dimension of
F
is the cardinality
|F|-1
.
Maximal faces are
facets
. If all facets have the same
dimension,
X
is
pure
. If
X
is the full power set of
V
, then

X
is the
simplex
on
V
and is denoted
\Delta(V)
.
The
boundary
\partial X
of a pure
simplicial complex
X
is the smallest simplicial
complex containing all faces of
X
which are on only one facet, and
are properly contained in this facet.
A simplicial complex
X
has a natural topological realization
|X|
in the
space
{\mathbb{R}}^{V}
,
[
42
]
.

Let
k
be a field and
k[V]
the polynomial ring with elements of
V

as variables. If
R\subseteq V
denote by
m(R)=\prod_{v\in R}v
the
associated monomial. Such a monomial is
squarefree
,
and if an ideal is generated by such monomials, it is also
squarefree
.

Given a simplicial complex
X
we have its associated Stanley-Reisner (SR)
ideal
I=I_{X}
which is the ideal generated by the monomials

m(R)
such that
R
is
not
in
X
. The quotient ring
k[V]/I
is
the Stanley-Reisner ring of
X
.

Simplicial complexes form a lattice with join
X\cup Y
, the union
of the families
X
and
Y
, and meet
X\cap Y
.
The squarefree monomial ideals also form a lattice
with join the sum of ideals, and meet the intersection of ideals.

The Stanley-Reisner correspondence gives
a one-to-one correspondence between the lattice of
simplicial complexes on
V

and the lattice of squarefree monomial ideals in
k[V]
, with order relation
reversed. Hence the join
X\cup Y
corresponds to the meet of ideals

I_{X}\cap I_{Y}
, and the meet
X\cap Y
corresponds to the sum of ideals

I_{X}+I_{Y}
.

2.2.
Alexander duality

The Alexander dual monomial ideal of
I(=I_{X})

is the monomial ideal
J=I^{\vee}
generated
by all monomials
m
which have a non-trivial common divisor with every monomial
in
I
. This is an involutive correspondence. The
squarefree monomials in
J
are the
m(F^{c})
where the
F
are faces
of
X
, and
F^{c}=V\backslash F
its complement. In particular the
minimal generators
of
J
are the monomials
m(F^{c})
of the complements of facets
F

of
X
, see
[
28
, Lemma 1.5.3]
.

Alexander duality gives a bijection between lattices of ideals
with order relation reversed. Hence the Alexander dual of the join

I+L
is the meet
I^{\vee}\cap L^{\vee}
, and the dual of
I\cap L
is

I^{\vee}+L^{\vee}
.

2.3.
External join of simplicial complexes

Let
X
be a simplicial complex on
V
, and
Y
a simplicial complex
on a disjoint vertex set
W
. The (external) join of
X
and
Y
is
the simplicial complex
X*Y
on

given by:

The statement of the following lemma is analogous to the idea of

Lemma 2.2

3.
Polarizations and their Alexander duals

In polarizations of Artin ideals

This polarization

We show that the Alexander dual of

It is generated

3.1.
Joins of boundaries of simplices

Recall that
\Delta(V)
is the simplex on the finite set

The SR-ideal generated by the single monomial

a sphere

is the cardinality of

Let

Denote

4.
Polarizations define triangulated balls

The previous section shows that

containing the complete intersection

with linear resolutions,

and cardinalities

Rainbow ideals
are

We investigate ideals of type

when such ideals have linear resolution.
We show that rainbow ideals have

balls by the Stanley-Reisner correspondence.

4.1.
The main result

The following is the key result leading to our main statement.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a subset

has

By Subsection

First recall the following:

Using this, Theorem

4.2.

We proceed to prove Theorem

First we recall a general fact. Let

be a polynomial ring
which is

We write the

For

Fact 4.4
.

The complex

is a minimal free
graded resolution of

In
[

Now given a polynomial ring

We give this a

and

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

be ideals in

Consider the product ideals
(b_{j})(I_{j})\subseteq k[B\cup X]
, which
we simply denote as
b_{j}I_{j}
. Also for

denote

If the ideal

has

-linear resolution, then each

has

-linear resolution.

Proof.

First we claim: For each

has
(d+1)
-linear resolution.
As a special case each

has
(d+1)
-linear
resolution, and so each

has linear resolution.
To see this take

and consider the degree

{\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{i\in R}e_{i}+Ne_{m+1}
.
By restricting to

The subset

In particular, this gives an explicit homological criterion on

5.1.
The cell complex and cellular complex associated
to
T

A finite set

\Delta(A)=\{\sum_{a\in A}\gamma_{a}\cdot a\,|\,0\leq\gamma_{a}\leq 1,\sum_{a\in A}\gamma_{a}=1\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{A}.

The set of vertices of

.

When

Definition 5.2

,

\Pi(R_{\bullet})

\sum_{i}|R_{i}|-n

A subset

of

\Pi(R_{\bullet})

The cell complex

The (augmented) chain complex:

A vertex

The (augmented) cellular complex:

Proof.

In short, this is a consequence of

.
Thus

The cellular complex is

5.2.
The criterion on

to correspond to a polarization

We now get a completely explicit description of the resolution of

Theorem 5.6

Let

Let

Both
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
and

have free bases mapping bijectively to the

for

So there is an isomorphism
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\cong{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(T)

Assume

. Consider the diagram:

\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{p}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}(T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Since

,
the right vertical map

is also injective on

The map

is then injective since

.

.

Proposition 5.11
.

A saturated cell sub-complex
X=C(T)
is acyclic iff

•

X_{a},X_{-a}
and
X_{/a}
are acyclic,

•

Whenever
X_{a|R}
and
X_{-a|R}
are non-empty, then

X_{/a|R}
is non-empty.

In this situation,
X^{a}
is also acyclic.

Proof.

Let
R_{\bullet}\subseteq^{*}A_{\bullet}
and
for simplicity denote
X_{|\Pi(R_{\bullet})}
by
X_{|R}
.
By (
9
)
we have a Mayer-Vietoris sequence:

\tilde{H}_{i+1}(X_{|R})\rightarrow\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{/a|R})\rightarrow\tilde{H}_{i}(X^{a}_{|R})\oplus\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{-a|R})\rightarrow\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{|R})\rightarrow\tilde{H}_{i-1}(X_{/a|R})\rightarrow\cdots.

Assume
X
is acyclic.
Since
X_{a}
and
X_{-a}
are restrictions,
they are acyclic. Further
X^{a}
is homotopy equivalent to
X_{a}
, and
similarly
X^{a}_{|R}
is homotopy equivalent to
X_{a|R}
.
Thus

\tilde{H}_{i}(X^{a}_{|R}),\quad\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{-a|R}),\quad\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{|R})

all vanish for

. Thus

is acyclic.
Also, when
X_{a|R}
and
X_{-a|R}
are non-empty, the sequence above
when

gives that
\tilde{H}_{-1}(X_{/a|R})
vanishes, and so

X_{/a|R}
is non-empty.

Conversely assume
X_{a},X_{-a}
and

are all acyclic.
The sequence above gives that all
\tilde{H}_{i}(X_{|R})
vanish except possibly
in the following case:

and

is empty and

X_{|R}
is not empty.
But in this case at least one of

or

is empty,

.
∎

6.
Minimal irreducible decomposition and

Given an Artin monomial ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
.
The question we address here is what does
T
and
C(T)
look like
for polarizations

of
{\mathfrak{j}}
? How can we make various polarizations
of
{\mathfrak{j}}
by working with the geometry of the cell complex

?

We show there is a one-to-one correspondence between the maximal
faces of
C(T)
and the components of the
minimal irreducible decompositions of the
Artin monomial ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
. This is what guides us to
find the possible
T
’s which gives polarizations

of

,
see Section

.

6.1.
Maximal cells in
C(T)

We first need some essential structural properties of the maximal cells
in
C(T)
for an acyclic
T\subseteq A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
C(R_{\bullet})
be maximal in
C(T)
. Suppose
R_{1}
has at
least two elements, and that
a\in R_{1}
is one of them.
Let
\overline{R}_{\bullet}
be
R_{\bullet}
but with
R_{1}
replaced by

R_{1}\backslash\{a\}
.
Then
C(\overline{R}_{\bullet})
is a maximal cell in
C(T_{/a})
.
In particular, since
T_{/a}\subseteq T_{-a}
,
there is a maximal cell in
C(T_{-a})
containing

C(\overline{R}_{\bullet})
.

Proof.

Let
C(R^{\prime}_{\bullet})
be a maximal cell in
C(T_{/a})
with

R^{\prime}_{i}\supseteq{R}_{i}
for
i\geq 2
,
and
R^{\prime}_{1}\supseteq R_{1}\backslash\{a\}
. By definition of

T_{/a}
, adding
a
to

and keeping the
rest of the
R^{\prime}_{i}
unchanged, gives a new
R_{\bullet}^{\prime\prime}

and cell
C(R_{\bullet}^{\prime\prime})
. This cell must be maximal in
C(T)

as
C(R^{\prime}_{\bullet})
is maximal in
C(T_{/a})
. But then

C(R_{\bullet}^{\prime\prime})
must be
C(R_{\bullet})
, and so we must have each

R_{i}^{\prime}=\overline{R}_{i}
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
C(R_{\bullet})
and
C(S_{\bullet})
be distinct maximal cells in
an acyclic cell complex
C(T)
. Then there are indices
i
and
j

such that we have strict inclusions

(10)

R_{i}\subset S_{i},\quad S_{j}\subset R_{j}.

Proof.

Since
C(T_{-a})
is acyclic when
C(T)
is, by Lemma
6.1

we may by successive restrictions for
a\in A_{i}\backslash(R_{i}\cup S_{i})
,
assume that
R_{i}\cup S_{i}=A_{i}
for each
i
.

We want to show we cannot have the negation of (
10
).
So assume the negation. Write
R_{i}\not\sim S_{i}
if none of them
is included in the other. We may then assume that there is
0\leq p\leq n

such that
R_{i}\supseteq S_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,p
and
R_{i}\not\sim S_{i}

for
i=p+1,\ldots,n
.

Our proof is by induction on
n
and the size of the
A_{i}
.
As an induction base
we note two cases:
i) If
n=1
they cannot both be maximal. ii)
We also cannot have all
|S_{i}|=1
,
since then
C(S_{\bullet})
is a point and
would not be connected to the rest, contradicting
that
C(T)
is acyclic.

Case
p\geq 1
: So
R_{1}\supseteq S_{1}
.
Assume
R_{1}=S_{1}
. Let
a\in S_{1}
. If the cardinalities
|R_{1}|=|S_{1}|

is
\geq 2
, we may by Lemma
6.1
get
smaller maximal sets in
C(T_{/a})
. Since
T_{/a}
is acyclic,
this is against induction. If
R_{1}=S_{1}
is of
cardinality
1
, we may simply drop the first factor and reduce
n
.

So assume
R_{1}
is strictly bigger than
S_{1}
.
Let
a\in R_{1}\backslash S_{1}
(so
|R_{1}|\geq 2
). We then get
a maximal
C(R^{\prime}_{\bullet})
in
C(T_{-a})
with
R^{\prime}_{i}\supseteq R_{i}

for each
i\geq 2
. Also
R^{\prime}_{1}\supseteq S_{1}
.
Again since
T_{-a}
is acyclic,
we get a contradiction by induction, since we have no strict

R^{\prime}_{i}\subset S_{i}
.

Case
p=0
: So we have
R_{i}\not\sim S_{i}
for each
i
.
If some
R_{i}\cap S_{i}
is non-empty (then both
|R_{i}|,|S_{i}|\geq 2
),
we may use Lemma
6.1
, and get a contradiction by induction.
So we may assume every
R_{i}\cap S_{i}
is empty.

At least one of the
R_{i}
has more than one element, say
R_{1}
.
Let
a\in R_{1}
. By restricting away from
a
, we get
a maximal
C(R^{\prime}_{\bullet})
in
C(T_{-a})
with
R^{\prime}_{i}\supseteq R_{i}

for each
i\geq 2
, and so none
R^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq S_{i}
for
i\geq 2
.
We cannot have
R_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq S_{1}
, since
R_{1}
and
S_{1}
had
empty intersection, and
|R_{1}|\geq 2
.
By induction we get a contradiction.
∎

6.2.
Regular linear subspaces

Let

be a finite dimensional

-vector space and
L\subseteq V

a linear subspace.

Definition 6.3
.

For a polynomial ring

let

If

has a basis which is a regular sequence for

a

for

Lemma 6.4
.

is a regular linear space for

iff
Tor_{i}(S/I,S/L)=0
for
all

Proof.

This is by
[

]
Corollary 17.5 and Theorem 17.6.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

be ideals. Let

be a regular linear space for

and

is a regular linear space for

(11)

\frac{(I\cap J)+L}{L}=\left(\frac{I+L}{L}\right)\cap\left(\frac{J+L}{L}\right).

We have a short exact sequence

By tensoring with

).
∎

The following rather trivial result will also be used.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let

, and suppose

In the quotient ring

\frac{(\cap_{u}(K_{u}))+(a)}{(a)}=\frac{(K)+(a)}{(a)}=\bigcap_{u\in U}\left(\frac{(K_{u})+(a)}{(a)}\right).

Note that all ideals above are

-graded, with

denoting

is the power of

occurring.

a. It is clear that

Let

Due to the

is in each

and so in

∎

6.3.
Minimal irreducible decompositions

Let now
T\subseteq A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}
be an acyclic subset.
It gives the ideal

I(T)
with Alexander dual ideal
J(T)
, a polarization of an Artin
monomial ideal.

For each
i
the variable differences
a^{i}_{\ell}-a^{i}_{k}
between
variables in
A_{i}
,
generate a linear subspace
L_{i}
.
Let
L=L_{1}+L_{2}+\cdots+L_{n}
. So

k[A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n}]/L\cong k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}].

Let
R_{\bullet}\subseteq^{*}A_{\bullet}
. It gives
S=R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}
.
We write
I(R_{\bullet}),J(R_{\bullet})
for
I(S),J(S)
.
Then
C(S)
is the single cell
C(R_{\bullet})
, and

J(S)=J(R_{\bullet})=(m(R_{1}),\ldots,m(R_{n})).

Thus

(13)

\frac{J(S)+L}{L}\subseteq\frac{k[A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n}]}{L}

identifies as

(14)

(x_{1}^{r_{1}},x_{2}^{r_{2}},\cdots,x_{n}^{r_{n}})\subseteq k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],\quad r_{i}=|R_{i}|.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
T\subseteq A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}
be an acyclic subset,
which is equivalent to
J(T)
being a polarization of an Artin monomial ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}
(by Corollary
5.7
and Proposition
3.6
).
Let
C(R^{k}_{\bullet}),k=1,\ldots,m
be the maximal cells in
C(T)
.
Denote
{\mathfrak{j}}_{k}=(x_{1}^{r^{k}_{1}},x_{2}^{r^{k}_{2}},\ldots,x_{n}^{r^{k}_{n}})
,
the irreducible monomial ideal where
r^{k}_{i}
is the cardinality
of
R^{k}_{i}
.

a.

J(T)=J(R^{1}_{\bullet})\cap J(R^{2}_{\bullet})\cap\cdots\cap J(R^{m}_{\bullet}).

b.

The minimal irreducible decomposition of
{\mathfrak{j}}
is

(15)

{\mathfrak{j}}={\mathfrak{j}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{j}}_{2}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{j}}_{m}.

So the components of the minimal irreducible decomposition of
{\mathfrak{j}}

are in bijection to the maximal cells of
C(T)
.

Proof.

Part a. We have

I(T)=I(R^{1}_{\bullet})+I(R^{2}_{\bullet})+\cdots+I(R^{m}_{\bullet}).

The Alexander dual is then

J(T)=J(R^{1}_{\bullet})\cap J(R^{2}_{\bullet})\cap\cdots\cap J(R^{m}_{\bullet}).

Part b. We must
show:

(16)

{\mathfrak{j}}=\frac{J(T)+L}{L}=\frac{J(R^{1}_{\bullet})+L}{L}\cap\frac{J(R^{2}_{\bullet})+L}{L}\cap\cdots\cap\frac{J(R^{m}_{\bullet})+L}{L}

which shows (
15
). In addition we must show
the minimality of the decomposition.

We have

(17)

I(T)=I(T^{a})+I(T_{-a}),\quad I(T_{/a})=I(T^{a})\cap I(T_{-a}).

The first is clear. To see the second, since
I(T^{a})=I(T_{a})+I(T_{/a})
,
the intersection

(18)

I(T^{a})\cap I(T_{-a})=I(T_{a})\cap I(T_{-a})+I(T_{/a})\cap I(T_{-a}).

The last term is
I(T_{/a})
. As for the middle term, by the
arguments of Proposition

4.5
it has
(n+1)
-linear resolution. A monomial generator
of this intersection must have the form
aa_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}
where

a_{i}\in A_{i}
. Then
a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\in I(T_{-a})
and
aa_{2}\cdots a_{n}\in I(T_{a})
. So
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\in T_{/a}
. Thus the left
side of (
18
) is
I(T_{/a})
.

On the Alexander dual side, (
17
) becomes:

J(T)=J(T^{a})\cap J(T_{-a}),\quad J(T_{/a})=J(T^{a})+J(T_{-a}).

If
C(T)
has only one maximal cell we are done by Equations
(
13
) and (
14
).

Suppose now
C(T)
has at least two maximal cells. Let
a
be an element
of some
A_{i}
, say
a\in A_{1}
which is on at least one maximal cell
but not all.

Then
J(T^{a})
and
J(T_{-a})
are ideals properly containing
J
, and
both
T^{a},T_{-a}
and
T_{/a}
, are acyclic subsets of
A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}
, by Proposition
5.11
.
Thus by Lemma
6.5

\frac{J(T)+L}{L}=\left(\frac{J(T^{a})+L}{L}\right)\cap\left(\frac{J(T_{-a})+L}{L}\right).

Both
T^{a}
and
T_{-a}
are properly included in
T
.
Furthermore a maximal cell
C(R^{j}_{\bullet})
of
C(T)
is either in

C(T^{a})
(if
a\in R^{j}_{1}
) or in
C(T_{-a})
(if
a\not\in R^{j}_{1}
).
Let
M^{a}
denote the set of
j
in the former,
and
M_{-a}
the
j
in the latter. Then by induction and
Lemma
6.6
:

\frac{J(T)+L}{L}=\cap_{j\in M^{a}}\left(\frac{J(R^{j}_{\bullet})+L}{L}\right)\bigcap\cap_{j\in M_{-a}}\left(\frac{J(R^{j}_{\bullet})+L}{L}\right).

This show Equation (
16
).

To show minimality, suppose there was some
p\in[m]
such
that

(19)

\cap_{i\in[m]\backslash\{p\}}{\mathfrak{j}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{j}}_{p}.

Let
\text{Mon}(n)
be the monomials in the polynomial ring
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
and if
K\subseteq k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

is a monomial ideal, write
\text{Mon}(n)=N(K)\sqcup\text{Mon}(K)
as a disjoint
decomposition, where
\text{Mon}(K)
are the monomials in
K
and
N(K)
are
the normal monomials, those not in
K
.
Expression (
19
) corresponds to

\cup_{i\in[m]\backslash\{p\}}N({\mathfrak{j}}_{i})\supseteq N({\mathfrak{j}}_{p}).

But writing
{\mathfrak{j}}_{p}=(x_{1}^{r^{p}_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{r^{p}_{n}})
,
the maximal element (for the division order in
\text{Mon}(n)
) of

is
the monomial
x_{1}^{r^{p}_{1}-1}x_{2}^{r^{p}_{2}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{r^{p}_{n}-1}
. If this
is in

, then each
r^{i}_{k}\geq r^{p}_{k},k=1,\ldots,n
.
But this contradicts Proposition
6.2
.
Thus we cannot have the situation of (

).
∎

For
S\subseteq A=A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n}
, write
S_{i}=S\cap A_{i}
.
We say
S

covers

if
for every maximal cell
C(R_{\bullet})
in

, there is some

such
that
R_{i}\subseteq S_{i}
. Note in particular that each

covers

.

Corollary 6.8
.

The polarization
J(T)
is generated by the monomials

m(S_{\bullet})
such that
S
covers
T
.

Proof.

It is clear that
S
covers
T
iff
m(S_{\bullet})\in J(R_{\bullet})
for each maximal cell
C(R_{\bullet})
of
C(T)
.
∎

For an
irreducible
Artin monomial ideal
{\mathfrak{j}}=(x_{1}^{r_{1}},x_{2}^{r_{2}},\ldots,x_{n}^{r_{n}})
define its

to be

\text{psize}\,({\mathfrak{j}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}-1).

(It is the number of variable powers

in the
quotient ring
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/{\mathfrak{j}}
.)
The following will also be useful.

Lemma 6.9
.

For any maximal cells
C(R_{\bullet}^{i})
and
C(R_{\bullet}^{j})

in

, the intersection
C(R_{\bullet}^{i})\cap C(R_{\bullet}^{j})

.

Proof.

Let

. Then the intersection

. Thus

I(R_{\bullet}^{i})\cap I(R_{\bullet}^{j})\supseteq I(P_{\bullet})
.
Taking Alexander duals this gives

Then for the associated indecomposable Artin ideals in

{\mathfrak{j}}^{i}+{\mathfrak{j}}^{j}\subseteq{\mathfrak{j}}^{\prime},\quad{\mathfrak{j}}^{\prime}=\frac{J(P_{\bullet})+L}{L}.

Whence

∎

6.4.
Standard polarization

Fix a total order on each
A_{i}
. So
A_{i}=\{a^{i}_{1}<a^{i}_{2}<\cdots<a^{i}_{\alpha_{i}}\}
. For each
0\leq r_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}

.
Each
{\mathbf{r}}=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
gives a monomial

m({\mathbf{r}})=x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{r_{n}}
. The
standard polarization
of
the monomial
m({{\mathbf{r}}})
is

m^{{\mathbf{r}}}(A_{\bullet})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}m^{r_{i}}(A_{i}).

If
{\mathfrak{j}}=(x^{{\mathbf{r}}^{1}},\ldots,x^{{\mathbf{r}}^{N}})
, its
standard polarization
is

J=(m^{{\mathbf{r}}^{1}}(A_{\bullet}),\ldots,m^{{\mathbf{r}}^{N}}(A_{\bullet})).

Example 6.10
.

Consider
{\mathfrak{j}}=(x_{1}^{3}x_{2},x_{2}^{4}x_{3}^{2},x_{1}x_{3}^{3})

and
C:=A_{3}
, and write

instead of
a^{2}_{i}

for the elements of
B
, and similarly for
C
. The standard polarization
of
{\mathfrak{j}}
is then:

J^{st}=(a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}b_{1},b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}c_{1}c_{2},a_{1}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}).

It is well-known that the standard polarization of any monomial
ideal is a polarization,
[
28
, Prop.1.6.2]

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
{\mathfrak{j}}
be an Artin monomial ideal, with

{\mathfrak{j}}=\cap_{k=1}^{m}{\mathfrak{j}}_{k}
its minimal irreducible decomposition,
where
{\mathfrak{j}}_{k}=(x_{1}^{r^{k}_{1}},x_{2}^{r^{k}_{2}},\ldots,x_{n}^{r^{k}_{n}})
.
Then the standard polarization of

is

where

.

Proof.

That

is immediate to see. Let then
m

be a monomial in the intersection. Each

has a generator

which divides

is

m^{{\mathbf{r}}}(A_{\bullet})
for a suitable
{\mathbf{r}}
.
Furthermore
x^{{\mathbf{r}}}
is the least common multiple of the

x_{i_{k}}^{r_{i_{k}}}
. So this is in the intersection
\cap_{k=1}^{m}{\mathfrak{j}}_{k}
,
and so
x^{{\mathbf{r}}}
is in
{\mathfrak{j}}
. Write
x^{{\mathbf{r}}}=x^{{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}x^{{\mathbf{p}}}

where

. Then

, and

is a multiple
of

.
∎

Corollary 6.12
.

Let
I^{st}=I(T)
be the Alexander dual of the standard polarization

.
The maximal cells of

are the

8.
Liaison for squarefree monomial ideals

Liaison is a standard and well-developed technique in algebraic
geometry,
[
36
,
30
,
47
]
. Here we apply it to squarefree monomial
ideals where it takes a simple form.
Our objective is to show that if
T\subseteq A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}

is acyclic, then the complement subset
T^{c}
is also acyclic.
Our argument for this is entirely algebraic. One could envision
a more topological argument using Alexander duality, but

We now consider linkage of squarefree monomial ideals with respect to

For simplicial complexes, we have the following

Proposition 8.1
.

Let

be simplicial sub-complexes of

if and only if

is the simplicial complex
generated by

In particular, for

is not in

ii.

If
F
is in
X
, it is not a facet of
Y
,

iii.

The set of facets of

are contained in the set of
facets of

Proof.

Let

be the simplicial complex generated by

Z\backslash X
, and
I_{Y}=(I_{Z}:I_{X})
.
We show firstly

and
secondly
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
.
If

is a face of

, the monomial
m(F)\not\in I_{Y}
.
Then there is

(equivalently

),
such that

.
This monomial has

as a factor so

is in

and not in

. The upshot is that
Y\subseteq Y^{\prime}
.

Conversely let

be a facet of

. Then

and

. If

, then

But since

is squarefree, we have

So

, giving

. Hence

.

The three consequences are immediate.
∎

The boundary

For an acyclic

Proposition 9.1

The boundaries of

and

\partial X(T)=\partial X(T^{c})=X(T)\cap X(T^{c}).

Proof.

Every codimension one

\partial\Delta(A_{\bullet})
is on two facets.
The boundary of
X(T)
consists of those codimension one faces
on only one facet of
X(T)
. But then the other facet in
\partial\Delta(A_{\bullet})

containing this codimension one face must be in
X(T^{c})
.
Conversely, let
F
be a face in

. Then there
are facets
[{\mathbf{a}}]^{c}
in

and
[{\mathbf{b}}]^{c}
in

containing

. If
{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbf{b}}
have

{\mathbf{a}}
to
{\mathbf{b}}
of length

at a time. Then every

{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
on this path will have

. At some

{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in T
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{i+1}\in T^{c}
. Their intersection
is a codimension one face i) on the boundaries of

and

, and

. This shows the statement about the boundaries.

That

is the defining ideal is clear
since this is the ideal of

. Since

and
X(T^{c})

are simplicial balls, their boundaries are simplicial spheres
(which have dimension one less that

and

[

, Corollary 5.6.5]
.

The complements of the facets of

(

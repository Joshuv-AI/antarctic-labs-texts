A gradient estimate for positive functions on graphs Journal: ***
Yong Lin, Shuang Liu, Yunyan Yang

Abstract
We derive a gradient estimate for positive functions, in particular for positive solutions to the heat equation, on finite or locally finite graphs. Unlike the well known Li-Yau estimate, which is based on the maximum principle, our estimate follows from the graph structure of the gradient form and the Laplacian operator. Though our assumption on graphs is slightly stronger than that of Bauer, Horn, Lin, Lippner, Mangoubi, and Yau (J. Differential Geom. 99 (2015) 359-405), our estimate can be easily applied to nonlinear differential equations, as well as differential inequalities. As applications, we estimate the greatest lower bound of Cheng’s eigenvalue and an upper bound of the minimal heat kernel, which is recently studied by Bauer, Hua and Yau (Preprint, 2015) by the Li-Yau estimate. Moreover, generalizing an earlier result of Lin and Yau (Math. Res. Lett. 17 (2010) 343-356), we derive a lower bound of nonzero eigenvalues by our gradient estimate.

Gradient estimate

In this section, we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
.
Special cases
(i)-(iv)
are immediate consequences of (
3

Hence it suffices to prove (

Since

\displaystyle 2\Gamma(u)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}u^{2}(y)+\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{\mu(x)}{w_{xy}}\left(\frac{w_{xy}}{\mu(x)}u(y)\right)^{2}+u^{2}(x)\frac{{\deg}(x)}{\mu(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d\left(\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{\mu(x)}u(y)\right)^{2}+D_{\mu}u^{2}(x).

Noting that

\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{\mu(x)}u(y)=\Delta u(x)+u(x)\frac{{\deg}(x)}{\mu(x)}

and using an elementary inequality
\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\leq a+b
,
\forall a,b\geq 0
,
we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\Gamma(u)(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{\mu(x)}u(y)+\sqrt{D_{\mu}}u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\left(\Delta u(x)+u(x)\frac{{\deg}(x)}{\mu(x)}\right)+\sqrt{D_{\mu}}u(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\Delta u(x)+(\sqrt{D_{\mu}}+\sqrt{d}D_{\mu})u(x).

This leads to (
3
) and thus ends the proof of the theorem.

,
to the minimal heat kernel
P_{t}(x,y)
,

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P_{(1+\delta)t}(x^{\prime},y)\exp\left\{\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{({\rm dist}(x,x^{\prime}))^{2}}{\delta t}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P_{(1+\delta)t}(x^{\prime},y)\exp\left\{\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{1}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\},\quad\forall x^{\prime}\in B_{x}(\sqrt{t}).

Integrating the above inequality on
B_{x}(\sqrt{t})
with respect to
x^{\prime}
, we have

{\rm Vol}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t}))P_{t}(x,y)\leq\exp\left\{\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{1}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in B_{x}(\sqrt{t})}\mu(x^{\prime})P_{(1+\delta)t}(x^{\prime},y).

(15)

Note that

h(y,s)=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in B_{x}(\sqrt{t})}\mu(x^{\prime})P_{s}(x^{\prime},y)
is also a positive solution to the heat equation.
Applying again the Harnack inequality, Theorem
3
, to
h(y,s)
with
T_{1}=(1+\delta)t
and
T_{2}=(1+2\delta)t
,
we have

{\rm Vol}(B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))h(y,(1+\delta)t)\leq\exp\left\{\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{1}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in B_{y}(\sqrt{t})}\mu(y^{\prime})h(y^{\prime},(1+2\delta)t).

This together with (
15
) implies that

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{2\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{2}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm Vol}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t})){\rm Vol}(B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in B_{x}(\sqrt{t})}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in B_{y}(\sqrt{t})}\mu(x^{\prime})\mu(y^{\prime})P_{(1+2\delta)t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

Let
t\geq\max\{\beta{\rm dist}(x,y),1\}
. Obviously
t\geq\frac{1}{1+2\delta}\max\{\beta{\rm dist}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t}),B_{y}(\sqrt{t})),1\}
. Let
\gamma
,
0<\gamma\leq 1
, be fixed.
It follows from Lemma
8
that
there exists a constant
C_{1}
depending only on
\gamma
,
\beta
and
D_{\mu}
such that

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{2\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{2}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}\right\}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Vol}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t})){\rm Vol}(B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))}}

(16)

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-(1-\gamma)\lambda^{\ast}(1+2\delta)t-C_{1}\frac{\left({\rm dist}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t}),B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))\right)^{2}}{4(1+2\delta)t}\right\}.

If
{\rm dist}(x,y)>2\sqrt{t}
, then
{\rm dist}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t}),B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))\geq{\rm dist}(x,y)-2\sqrt{t}
and thus

\frac{\left({\rm dist}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t}),B_{y}(\sqrt{t})\right)^{2}}{4(1+2\delta)t}\geq\frac{({\rm dist}(x,y))^{2}}{4(1+4\delta)t}-\frac{1}{2\delta}.

(17)

It is easy to see that (
17
) still holds if
{\rm dist}(x,y)\leq 2\sqrt{t}
. Inserting (
17
) into (
16
),
we have

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Vol}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t})){\rm Vol}(B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))}}\exp\left\{2\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\delta t+\frac{2}{\delta}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}+\frac{C_{1}}{2\delta}\right\}

(18)

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-(1-\gamma)\lambda^{\ast}(1+2\delta)t-C_{1}\frac{\left({\rm dist}(x,y)\right)^{2}}{4(1+4\delta)t}\right\}.

Note that
t\geq 1
. Choosing
2\delta=\epsilon/\sqrt{t}
in (
18
), we obtain for
t\geq\max\{\beta{\rm dist}(x,y),1\}
,

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Vol}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t})){\rm Vol}(B_{y}(\sqrt{t}))}}\exp\left\{\sqrt{t}\left(D_{\mu}\epsilon+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\epsilon+\frac{4}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}+\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-(1-\gamma)\lambda^{\ast}t-C_{1}\frac{\left({\rm dist}(x,y)\right)^{2}}{4(1+2\epsilon)t}\right\}.

Denoting
C_{2}=D_{\mu}\epsilon+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\epsilon+\frac{4}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{\max}}{w_{\min}}}+\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}
, we finish the proof of the theorem.

\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem
6
. Note that
\int_{V}\Delta ud\mu=0
and that if

-\Delta u=\lambda u
, then
-\Delta(cu)=\lambda cu
for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
We can assume
-\Delta u=\lambda u
with
\sup u=1
and
\inf u<0
.
Take
x_{1},x_{\ell}\in G
such that
u(x_{1})=\sup u=1
,
u(x_{n})=\inf u<0
,

x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{\ell}
be the shortest path connecting
x_{1}
and
x_{\ell}
, where
(x_{i},x_{i+1})\in E
. Then

\ell\leq D
. For any
\beta>1
, note that

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})|}{\beta-u(x_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\mu(x_{i})}\sum_{x_{i}y\in E}\frac{\mu(x_{i})}{w_{x_{i}y}}w_{x_{i}y}(u(x_{i})-u(y))^{2}}}{\beta-u(x_{i})}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\frac{\sqrt{2\Gamma(u)(x_{i})}}{\beta-u(x_{i})}.

Since
\beta-u>0
and
u\leq 1
, we have by using Theorem
1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\Gamma(u)}}{\beta-u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\Gamma(\beta-u)}}{\beta-u}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\left(\frac{\Delta(\beta-u)}{\beta-u}+D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\left(\frac{\lambda u}{\beta-u}+D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\left(\frac{1}{\beta-1}\lambda+D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right).

This together with (
19
) implies

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{|u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})|}{\beta-u(x_{i})}\leq D{d}\left(\frac{1}{\beta-1}\lambda+D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right).

(20)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{|u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})|}{\beta-u(x_{i})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\log\left(1+\frac{|u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})|}{\beta-u(x_{i})}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\log\frac{\beta-u(x_{i+1})}{\beta-u(x_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\frac{\beta-u(x_{\ell})}{\beta-u(x_{1})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}.

Combining (
20
) and (
21
), we have

\lambda\geq(\beta-1)\left(\frac{1}{Dd}\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}-D_{\mu}-\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right).

Choose
\beta
such that
\frac{1}{Dd}\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}-D_{\mu}-\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}=\frac{1}{Dd}
. We obtain

\lambda\geq\frac{1}{Dd\left(\exp\left\{1+Dd\left(D_{\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{\mu}}{d}}\right)\right\}-1\right)}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

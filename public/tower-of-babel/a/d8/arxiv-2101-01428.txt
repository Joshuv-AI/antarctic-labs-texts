On a class of fully nonlinear elliptic equation in dimension two
Filomena Pacella, David Stolnicki

Abstract




Abstract:
We study existence and asymptotic behavior of radial positive solutions of some fully nonlinear equations involving Pucci’s extremal operators in dimension two. In particular we prove the existence of a positive solution of a fully nonlinear version of the Liouville equation in the plane. Moreover for the
\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}
operator, we show the existence of a critical exponent and give bounds for it.

Keywords:
Fully nonlinear equations; asymptotic behavior; critical exponent.

MSC2020:
35J60, 35B09, 34A34.

1
Introduction

In this paper we study positive radial solutions for the fully nonlinear Lane Emden equation driven by Pucci’s extremal operators:

-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}(D^{2}u)=u^{p}\qquad\text{ in }\Omega\\

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is either the ball
B_{R}
of radius
R>0
, centered at the origin or the whole plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In the case of the ball we will impose the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition.

We recall that for a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2,
the Pucci’s operators are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u)=\Lambda\sum_{\mu_{i}>0}\mu_{i}+\lambda\sum_{\mu_{i}<0}\mu_{i}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}u)=\lambda\sum_{\mu_{i}>0}\mu_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{\mu_{i}<0}\mu_{i}

where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
are the ellipticity constants and
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}(D^{2}u)
,
i=1,\ldots,N
are the eigenvalues of the hessian matrix
D^{2}u
. Associated to
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
are dimension like numbers
\tilde{N}_{+}
and
\tilde{N}_{-}
defined as

\tilde{N}_{+}=\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}(N-1)+1\qquad\text{ and }\tilde{N}_{-}=\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}(N-1)+1

(2)

These numbers allow to give estimates for the exponent
p
for which existence or nonexistence of solutions of (
1
) in
B_{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{N}
holds when
N\geq 3
and
\tilde{N}_{+}>2
, (note that
\tilde{N}_{-}
is always larger than two if
N\geq 3
).

Indeed a first result obtained in
[
4
]
shows that if
N\geq 3
and
p\leq\frac{\tilde{N}_{\pm}}{\tilde{N}_{\pm}-2}
then no nontrivial positive viscosity supersolutions of (
1
) exist in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Using this result, the existence of positive solutions in bounded domains, not necessarily radial, was proved in
[
14
]
for the same range of exponents.

In the radial setting Felmer and Quaas in
[
8
]
(see also
[
9
]
) provided, for
N\geq 3
, the existence of critical exponents
p_{+}^{*}
for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
and
p_{-}^{*}
for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
satisfying

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{\tilde{N}_{+}}{\tilde{N}_{+}-2},\frac{N+2}{N-2}\right\}<\;

\displaystyle p_{+}^{*}<\frac{\tilde{N}_{+}+2}{\tilde{N}_{+}-2}

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}<\;

\displaystyle p_{-}^{*}<\frac{N+2}{N-2}

which are thresholds for the existence of radial positive solutions of (
1
) in the ball or in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

More precisely they proved that existence in
B_{R}
holds if and only if
p<p_{\pm}^{*}
, while existence in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
holds if and only if
p\geq p_{\pm}^{*}
. In the last case a complete classification of the solutions was given, according to the decay at infinity. Recently, the same kind of results has been obtained in
[
12
]
for more general equations with an alternative proof.

In dimension N=2, the situation for the two operators
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+},\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
is different. Indeed, by (
2
) we have that
\tilde{N}_{+}<2
, while
\tilde{N}_{-}>2
, for
\lambda<\Lambda
. In the first case the result of
[
4
]
still implies the nonexistence of nontrivial solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for the equation (
1
) for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
for every
p\in(1,\infty)
.

As a consequence a positive radial solution of

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}D^{2}u=u^{p}\qquad\text{ in }B_{R}\\
u>0\qquad\text{ in }B_{R}\\
u=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial B_{R}\end{cases}

(4)

exists for every
1<p<\infty
as in
[
14
]
. Thus no critical exponent as in (
3
) can be defined for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
, in dimension 2.

Instead in the case of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
the number
\tilde{N}_{-}-2
is still positive which suggests that a critical exponent
p^{*}_{-}
as in higher dimensions could exist, though, any upper bound for it as in (
3
), could not be given, since
N=2
.

In this paper we obtain new results for (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, but of different kind for each one of the two operators.

In the case of
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
, when a unique radial positive solution of (
4
) in the ball exists for every
p>1
( see Theorem
2.4
), we study the asymptotic behavior of
u_{p}
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. This is done by analyzing the rescaled function

\displaystyle z_{p}=z_{p}(r)=\frac{p}{u_{p}(0)}(u(\varepsilon_{p}r)-u_{p}(0)),

(5)

for
r=|x|
, and

\varepsilon_{p}^{-2}=p\cdot u_{p}(0)^{p-1},

as in the semilinear case (
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
). As a byproduct, we obtain the existence of a radial solution of the ”fully nonlinear Liouville equation” in the plane. As far as we know, this is the first existence result for this equation.

More precisely we get:

Theorem 1.1
.

The function
z_{p}
converges, up to a subsequence, in
\mathcal{C}_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to a radial function
z
which satisfies:

\displaystyle-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}z)(x)=e^{z}.

(6)

Moreover
z
is negative, radially decreasing and, as a function of
|x|
, changes concavity only once at
|x|=R_{0}=2\sqrt{2\lambda}
. Finally:

z(x)=\log\left(\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8\lambda}|x|^{2})^{2}}\right)\text{ in }B_{R_{0}}.

In the case of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
, we are able to prove that in dimension
N=2
, a critical exponent
p_{-}^{*}
having similar features as the one for
N\geq 3
indeed exists and we provide bounds for it.

As mentioned before, to get an upper bound for
p_{-}^{*}
is not obvious since the corresponding estimate in higher dimensions in (
3
) blows up when
N=2
.

We get it through the existence of another relevant exponent, denoted by
\tilde{p}_{-}
, which is responsible for the existence or lack of periodic orbits for a related dynamical system that we study, following the approach of
[
12
]
.

As it will be made clear in Section 3, the periodic orbits of this auxiliary dynamical system are related to the nonexistence of solutions in the ball, and possibly allow the existence of entire oscillating radial solutions for (
1
).

Referring to Definition 3.5 for fast, slow, or pseudo-slow decaying solutions we state the main result for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Critical Exponent)
.

Let the dimension
N
be two, there are exponents
p_{-}^{*}
and
\tilde{p}_{-}
satisfying:

\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}<p_{-}^{*}\leq\tilde{p}_{-}\leq\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}+\frac{4}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2+\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)^{2}}

(7)

with
\tilde{N}_{-}
as in (
2
), such that, considering equation (
1
) for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
:

i)

for
p<p_{-}^{*}
there is no nontrivial radial positive solution of problem (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, while, for every
R>0
there is a unique positive radial solution in
B_{R}
.

ii)

if
p=p_{-}^{*}
there is a unique fast decaying radial positive solution of (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

iii)

for
p_{-}^{*}<p<\tilde{p}_{-}
there is a unique positive radial solution of (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which may be either pseudo-slow or slow decaying.

iv)

for
p>\tilde{p}_{-}
there is a unique slow decaying solution of (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

v)

for
p>p_{-}^{*}
there is no positive radial solution of (
1
) in
B_{R}
.

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
uniqueness is meant up to scaling.

We recall that, by using the moving plane method, it is proved in
[
5
]
that every positive solution of (
1
) in the ball
B_{R}
satisfying
u=0
on
\partial B_{R}
is radial. Thus, by Theorem
1.2
, we have that such a solution in
B_{R}
exists if and only if
p<p_{-}^{*}
.

Then, we could study the asymptotic behavior of
u_{p}
, as
p\nearrow p_{-}^{*}
, to understand the limit profile of these solutions.

As for the higher dimensional case (
[
2
]
) we get:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N=2
,
p_{-}^{*}
as in Theorem
1.2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, for the solution
u_{p_{\varepsilon}}
of (
1
) (for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
) in the ball
B_{R}
, with
p_{\varepsilon}=p_{-}^{*}-\varepsilon
, the following statements hold:

i)

M_{\varepsilon}=u_{p_{\varepsilon}}(0)\rightarrow\infty

ii)

the rescaled function

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{u_{p_{\varepsilon}}(0)}u_{p_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{x}{u_{p_{\varepsilon}}(0)^{\frac{p_{\varepsilon}-1}{2}}}\right)

converges up to a subsequence, to a limit function
U
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where
U
is the unique positive solution of:

\displaystyle-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}U)(x)=U^{p_{-}^{*}}\qquad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}

satisfying
U(0)=1

iii)

u_{p_{\varepsilon}}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathcal{C}_{loc}^{2}(\bar{B}_{R}\setminus\{0\})
.

The proof of the previous theorem is similar to that of
[
2
]
for the analogous results in higher dimension, though the statement iii) could be obtained more easily analyzing the dynamical system introduced in
[
12
]
. Also, the results about the energy invariance of
[
2
]
can be easily extended to the two-dimensional case.

Finally, a classification of solutions of (
1
) singular at the origin similar to the one of Theorem 1.8 of
[
12
]
follows in the same way, with obvious changes.

We conclude with some remarks about higher dimensions which are related to our results for
N=2
.

First when the dimension
N
is greater than two, we may consider the case where
\tilde{N}_{+}
as defined in (
2
) is smaller than two. Then the results of
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
still imply that there is, for every
p\in(1,\infty)
, a unique radial positive solution
u_{p}
of (
4
) in the ball
B_{R}
.

In particular, the approach we use to treat the two dimensional problem may be immediately applied in this setting producing results analogous to those of Theorem
1.1
.
Thus we get solutions of the Liouville equation (
6
) in some higher dimensions. Note that even for the semilinear case, where the Pucci operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
is replaced by the Laplacian the corresponding equation (
6
) has different features according to the dimension (see
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
and references therein). It would be interesting to investigate problem (
6
) in the fully nonlinear setting in all dimensions and also for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
.

Concerning the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
, the approach we have used in Section 3 to estimate
p_{-}^{*}
as in (
7
) can also be used in higher dimensions to have an estimate of the critical exponent from above better than the one given in (
3
) and to determine the existence of oscillating solutions. This will be done in a forthcoming paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we study the equation (
1
) for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
proving Theorem
1.1
. Section 3 is devoted to the Pucci Operator
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
. After recalling several preliminaries about an associated dynamical system we prove a result on the nonexistence of periodic orbits for such a system. This allows to prove Theorem
1.2
. We end Section 3 with the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.1
Some Preliminary Results

Here we present some needed classical results.

Theorem 2.1

(Pucci’s lemma
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open and let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
function. Then for every
x\in\Omega
there is a (
\lambda
,
\Lambda
) elliptic matrix
A(x)
depending on
u
such that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u)(x)=Tr(A(x)D^{2}u(x))

(8)

and
A(x)
is a measurable function with respect to
x
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
L
be an uniformly elliptic linear operator with measurable bounded coefficients, and
\Omega,u
as above. If
u\geq 0
and
Lu=0
in
\Omega
, then for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}}u\leq C\inf_{\Omega}u

(9)

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
L,\Omega
and
u
be as in Theorem
2.2
. If
Lu=f
in
\Omega
, for some
f\in\mathcal{L}_{loc}^{n}(\Omega)
, then for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
we have

\displaystyle||u||_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})}u\leq C\left(\sup_{\Omega}u+||f||_{\mathcal{L}_{loc}^{n}(\Omega)}\right)

(10)

For
p>1
, we consider the Dirichlet problem (
4
) and recall known results for it.

Theorem 2.4
.

For every
p>1
the problem (
4
) admits a unique solution
u_{p}
which is radial, i.e, with an abuse of notation,
u_{p}(x)=u_{p}(|x|)
for
|x|=r\in[0,R]
. Furthermore
u_{p}
satisfies:

i)

u_{p}(0)=\max u_{p}

ii)

u_{p}
is strictly decreasing

iii)

u_{p}
changes concavity only once at a point
y_{p}
and is concave around the origin.

Proof.

The existence of a positive solution of (
4
) for every
p>1
derives from the nonexistence of solutions of the analogous equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
4
]
). Indeed if entire solutions do not exist, then apriori estimates hold which allow to prove the existence of a solution of (
4
) as in
[
14
]
.

The radial symmetry of
u_{p}
and i)-ii) have been proved in
[
5
]
by the moving plane method. The uniqueness follows by the invariance by scaling of the equation and the uniqueness of the initial value problem for the corresponding ODE.

Finally, iii) can be proved exactly as in
[
9

using the Emden-Fowler analysis.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The uniqueness of the radius
y_{p}
where

can be obtained also as in

analyzing the flow induced by an associated dynamical system. This method also works in dimension two, and we will use it to study the problem for

2.2
Asymptotic behavior of rescaled solutions

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the solution
u_{p}
when the exponent
p
goes to
+\infty
.

Recalling that the parameter
\varepsilon
is defined by

\varepsilon_{p}^{-2}=p\cdot u_{p}(0)^{p-1}

we prove the following preliminary result.

Lemma 2.6
.

It holds :

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow+\infty}{\varepsilon_{p}^{-2}}=+\infty

Proof.

The proof is based on the fact that
u_{p}(0)^{p-1}
does not converge to 0. Assume otherwise, then for
p
sufficiently big, we have:

\displaystyle u_{p}(0)^{p-1}<\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+},B_{R})

where
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+},B_{R})
is the first eigenvalue of the Pucci operator on
B_{R}
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

In particular, this implies that

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u_{p})(x)=u_{p}(x)^{p}<\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+},B_{R})\cdot u\qquad\text{ in }B_{R}\\
u_{p}>0\qquad\text{ in }B_{R}\\
u_{p}=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial B_{R}\end{cases}

This is a contradiction with the definition of the first eigenvalue. Therefore we conclude that
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}p\cdot u_{p}(0)^{p-1}=\infty
.

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

The rescaled function
z_{p}
defined in (
5
) converges in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, up to a subsequence, to a solution
z
of the Liouville equation (
6
).

Proof.

A simple computation gives for
x\in B_{R/\varepsilon_{p}}:

\displaystyle D^{2}z_{p}(x)=\frac{p}{u_{p}(0)}\cdot\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\cdot D^{2}u_{p}(\varepsilon_{p}\cdot x)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}z_{p})(x)=\frac{p}{u_{p}(0)}\cdot\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u_{p})(\varepsilon_{p}\cdot x)

Since
u_{p}
is a solution to Problem (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}z_{p})(x)=\frac{-p}{u_{p}(0)}\cdot\varepsilon_{p}^{2}u_{p}^{p}(\varepsilon_{p}\cdot x)=-\left(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right)^{p}

It is clear from the above expression that we are done once we prove that
z_{p}
converges up to a subsequence in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

It follows from Theorem
2.1
that for every
p
, there is a
(\lambda,\Lambda)
elliptic matrix
A_{p}(x)
such that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}z_{p})(x)=-Tr(A_{p}(x)D^{2}z_{p}(x))=\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p}

(11)

Let
B_{R_{1}}
be the ball of radius
R_{1}>0
centered at the origin and consider
w
the solution of the problem:

\begin{cases}-Tr(A_{p}(x)D^{2}w_{p}(x))=\left(1+\frac{z_{p}(x)}{p}\right)^{p}\qquad\text{ in }B_{R_{1}}\\
w_{p}=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial B_{R_{1}}\end{cases}

(12)

It follows from the definition of
z_{p}
that
(1+\frac{z_{p}}{p})

\in(0,1]
. Therefore it follows from the Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci (
[
10
]
) estimate and maximum principle that
0\leq w_{p}\leq C(\lambda,R_{1})
.

For
x\in B_{R_{1}}
define the auxiliary function
\varPsi_{p}(x)=z_{p}(x)-w_{p}(x)
which solves the problem

\begin{cases}-Tr(A_{p}(x)D^{2}\varPsi_{p}(x))=0\qquad\text{ in }B_{R_{1}}\\
\varPsi_{p}=z_{p}\qquad\text{ on }\partial B_{R_{1}}.\end{cases}

(13)

It follows from Theorem
2.2
, applied to
-\varPsi_{p}
, that there is a constant
C_{1}(\Lambda/\lambda,R_{1})
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\cdot\varPsi_{p}(0)\leq C_{1}\cdot\sup_{{B_{R_{1}}}}\varPsi_{p}\leq\inf_{{B_{R_{1}/2}}}\varPsi_{p}.

Note that
\varPsi_{p}(0)=z_{p}(0)-w_{p}(0)\geq C
. Therefore we conclude that

\displaystyle-\inf_{{B_{R_{1}/2}}}\varPsi_{p}=\sup_{B_{R_{1}/2}}|\varPsi_{p}|\leq C(\Lambda,\lambda,R_{1}).

Since
w_{p}
is also uniformly bounded we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R_{1}/2}}|z_{p}|\leq C(\Lambda,\lambda,R_{1}).

We proceed by using the
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
estimates (Theorem
2.3
) to state that there are
\alpha(\lambda,\Lambda)
and
C(\Lambda,\lambda,R_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R_{1}/4}}[z_{p}]_{{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}}\leq C.

Note that

\displaystyle\left[1+\frac{z_{p}}{p}\right]_{{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}}\leq\left[z_{p}\right]_{{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}}.

Therefore it follows from the
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
estimates for the Pucci operator that

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R_{1}/8}}|z_{p}|_{{\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}}}\leq C(\Lambda,\lambda,R_{1}).

Thus from the Arzela-Ascoli theorem there is a subsequence
z_{p_{k}}
which, for every
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
locally converges to a limit function z in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha^{\prime}}
in
B_{R_{2}}
, for a sufficiently small
R_{2}
. Since
R_{1}
is arbitrary, the convergence holds locally over the whole plane.

∎

2.3
The fully nonlinear Liouville equation

In the previous section, we have shown that, up to a subsequence, the radial functions
z_{p}
converge to a function
z
which solves (
6
) which is a fully nonlinear version of the Liouville equation. Now we proceed in describing such a function in particular proving that
z
changes concavity only once.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The convergence of the rescaled function
z_{p}
is just the statement of Proposition
2.7
. Hence the existence of a radial solution
z
of (
6
) which is negative and decreasing is deduced by that. Now we show that the limit function
z
changes concavity only once. Thus we consider the only radius
y_{p}
where
u_{p}
changes concavity ( iii) of Theorem
2.4
).

We consider the three possible cases:

1.

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=\infty
.

2.

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=0
.

3.

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=R_{0},\qquad 0<R_{0}<\infty
.

Case 1: Since
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=\infty
, the limit equation being satisfied by
z
is

\begin{cases}-\lambda\Delta z&=e^{z}\qquad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
z(0)&=0,\end{cases}

(14)

because
z
is concave and decreasing on the whole space. From the classification of solution of the Liouville equation by Chen-Li
[
3
]
, we know that the solution of (
14
) is:

\displaystyle z(x)=\log\left(\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8\lambda}|x|^{2})^{2}}\right).

(15)

We conclude that Case 1 is not possible since such a solution is not concave in the whole space.

Case 2: Since
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=0
, the limit ODE being satisfied by
z(x)=z(|x|)
is

\begin{cases}-\Lambda z^{\prime\prime}(r)-\lambda\frac{z^{\prime}(r)}{r}=e^{z(r)}\qquad\text{ in }(0,\infty)\\
z(0)=0,\end{cases}

(16)

and
z
is a nonpositive, decreasing and convex function.

First observe that we may rewrite the above equations as

\displaystyle-(r^{\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}}\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=\frac{r^{\lambda/\Lambda}}{\Lambda}e^{\varphi(r)}\qquad r\in(0,\infty).

Integrating between
0
and
r
since
z
is negative so that
e^{z}\leq 1
we obtain:

\displaystyle r^{\lambda/\Lambda}\left(z^{\prime}(r)\right)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{-s^{\lambda/\Lambda}}{\Lambda}e^{z(s)}ds\geq\frac{-1}{\Lambda}r^{1+\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}}\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda+\Lambda}.

Dividing by
r^{\lambda/\Lambda}
we obtain

\displaystyle 0\geq z^{\prime}(r)\geq\frac{-1}{\lambda+\Lambda}\cdot r,

and taking the limit as
r
goes to zero we get
z^{\prime}(0)=0
. In particular this implies that
z
is positive around the origin since
z(0)=0,z^{\prime}(0)=0
and
z
is convex. This contradicts the fact that
z
is negative.

Case 3: Since
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{y_{p}}{\varepsilon_{p}}=R_{0}
, the limit equations being satisfied by
z(x)=z(|x|)
are

\begin{cases}-\lambda z^{\prime\prime}(r)-\lambda\frac{z^{\prime}(r)}{r}=e^{z(r)}\qquad\text{ in }(0,R_{0})\\
-\Lambda z^{\prime\prime}(r)-\lambda\frac{z^{\prime}(r)}{r}=e^{z(r)}\qquad\text{ in }(R_{0},\infty)\\
z(0)=0\\
z^{\prime\prime}(R_{0})=0,\end{cases}

(17)

and
z
is a nonpositive, decreasing function, concave in
(0,R_{0})
and convex in
(R_{0},\infty)
.

As in the previous case, we may repeat the same procedure by exchanging
\Lambda
by
\lambda
and obtain that
z^{\prime}(0)=0
.

Therefore
z
satisfies the following initial boundary value problem

\begin{cases}-\lambda z^{\prime\prime}(r)-\lambda\frac{z^{\prime}(r)}{r}=e^{z(r)}\qquad\text{ in }(0,R_{0})\\
z(0)=0\\
z^{\prime}(0)=0\end{cases}

(18)

Since such a problem admits only one solution it must be given by

\displaystyle z(r)=\log\left(\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{8\lambda}r^{2})^{2}}\right)

Note that the above function changes concavity only once in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
at
2\cdot\sqrt{2\cdot\lambda}
. Therefore
R_{0}=2\cdot\sqrt{2\cdot\lambda}
. In particular, due to the fact that
z
is
\mathcal{C}_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we obtain the following:

•

z(R_{0})=-2\cdot\ln 2

•

z^{\prime}(R_{0})=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}

Then by (
17
)
z
satisfies this other initial value problem

\begin{cases}\Lambda z^{\prime\prime}(r)-\lambda\frac{z^{\prime}(r)}{r}=e^{z(r)}\qquad\text{ in }(0,R_{0})\\
z(R_{0})=-2\cdot\ln 2\\
z^{\prime}(R_{0})=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\end{cases}

(19)

There is only one solution to problem (
19
) and therefore we obtain that
z
is given by gluing the solutions of the two initial boundary value problems (
18
) and (
19
).
∎

The Problem for
\mathcal{M}^{-}

We consider the problem

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}u)(x)=u(x)^{p}\qquad\text{ in }\Omega\\
u>0\qquad\text{ in }\Omega\\
\end{cases}

(20)

where
\Omega
is either
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or the ball

, centered at the origin, with radius

. In the last case we assume:

u=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial\Omega

(21)

The first step to study the above problem is understanding whether a critical exponent for the existence of solutions of (
20
) can be defined or not.

To this aim we are going to use the approach of
[
12
]
which involves an auxiliary dynamical system. Thus we start by introducing it together with some preliminary results from
[
12
]
.

3.1
Preliminaries on a Dynamical System

Since we are interested in radial solutions to (
20
) we write
u=u(r)=u(|x|)
as an expression of
u
in radial coordinates. The eigenvalues of
D^{2}u
are the simple eigenvalue
u^{\prime\prime}(r)
and
\frac{u^{\prime}(r)}{r}
which has multiplicity
(N-1)
(see
[
8
]
).

Thus
u
satisfies a corresponding ODE from which it is easy to deduce that
u
is decreasing as long as it is positive, concave in an interval
(0,\tau_{0})
and changes concavity at least once (see
[
8
]
or
[

]
)

Hence (
20

u^{\prime\prime}=M(-\Lambda r^{-1}u^{\prime}-u^{p}),

(22)

where

M(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{s}{\Lambda}\qquad\text{ if }s\leq 0\\
\frac{s}{\lambda}\qquad\text{ if }s>0\\
\end{cases},

(23)

Next we introduce the following auxiliary functions:

\displaystyle X(t)=-\frac{ru^{\prime}(r)}{u(r)},\qquad Z(t)=-\frac{ru(r)^{p}}{u^{\prime}(r)}\qquad\text{for }t=\ln r

(24)

whenever
u(r)\neq 0,u^{\prime}(r)\neq 0

Since
\,u>0\,
and
\,u^{\prime}<0\,
, we have that the above quantities belong to the first quadrant of the
(X,Z)
plane.

In the new variables, the equation (
22
) becomes the following autonomous dynamical system

\displaystyle\left(\dot{X},\dot{Z}\right)=F(X,Z)=\left(f(X,Z),g(X,Z)\right)

(25)

where the dot
\cdot
stands for derivation with respect to t and
f,g
are given by:

\displaystyle f(X,Z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X(X+\frac{Z}{\Lambda})&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{+}\\
X(X-(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)+\frac{Z}{\lambda})&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{-}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptg(X,Z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Z(2-pX-\frac{Z}{\Lambda})&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{+}\\
Z(\tilde{N}_{-}-pX-\frac{Z}{\lambda})&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{-}\end{array}\right.

where the regions
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
are:

\displaystyle R^{+}

\displaystyle=\{(X,Z)\,|\,Z>\Lambda\}

(26)

\displaystyle R^{-}

\displaystyle=\{(X,Z)\,|\,0<Z<\Lambda\}

(27)

Note that whenever
(X(t),Z(t))
belongs to the line

\ell=\{(X,Z\,|\,Z=\Lambda)\}

(28)

then the corresponding solution
u
of (

) satisfies
u^{\prime\prime}=0
. Hence
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
represent, in terms of the new variables
(X,Z)
, the regions of concavity and convexity of
u
, respectively.

Other important sets which are relevant to study the dynamics induced by (
25
) are:

\ell_{1}=\{(X,Z)\,|\,Z=\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2-X)\}

(29)

which is the set where
\dot{X}=0
, and

\ell_{2}=\{(X,Z)\in R^{+}\,|\,Z=\Lambda({2}-pX)\}\cup\{(X,Z)\in R^{-}\,|\,Z=\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-pX)\}

(30)

which is the set where
\dot{Z}=0
.

Then we need to consider the stationary points for (
25
) and their classification.

We recall that for
p\leq\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
is already known that there exists a positive solution of (
20
) with Dirichlet boundary conditions in the ball
B_{R}
(
[
14
]
).

Therefore, to determine the critical exponent for
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
only the values of
p
larger than
\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
are important, hence, we just state all is needed for
p>\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The ODE system (
25
) admits the following stationary points:

i)

N_{0}=(0,2\Lambda)
which is a saddle point.

ii)

A_{0}=(\tilde{N}_{-}-2,0)
which is a saddle point.

iii)

M_{0}=\left(\frac{2}{p-1},\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2-\frac{2}{p-1})\right)
which is a source for
\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}<p<\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
, a center for
p=\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
, a sink for
p>\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}

iv)

O=(0,0)
which is a saddle point.

F

3.2
Periodic Orbits

For the sequel it is important to see for which range of the exponent
p
there are no periodic orbits of (
25
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
. Then for the system (
25
) it holds:

i)

no periodic orbits exist if
\,1<p<\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}.

ii)

there exists
\,p_{o}>\max\{3,\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}\}\,
such that for
\,p>p_{o}
there is no periodic orbit.

Proof.

The statement i) was already proved in Proposition 2.10 of
[
12
]
. We include the proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Note that a periodic orbit must necessarily contain a stationary point in its interior. In our case, the only one which can be in it is the point
M_{0}
. Moreover by the direction of the vector field
F
(see (
25
)) on the lines
L_{1}=\{(X,Z)\,|\,Z=2\Lambda\}
and
L_{2}=\{(X,Z)\,|\,X=\tilde{N}_{-}-2\}
we deduce that no periodic orbit can intersect them. Hence, a periodic orbit must be contained in
[0,\tilde{N}_{-}-2]\times[0,2\Lambda]
.

Consider
\varphi(X,Z)=X^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}
, where
\beta=\frac{3-p}{p-1}
. Set
\Phi(X,Z)=\partial_{X}(\varphi f)+\partial_{Z}(\varphi g)
, with
f
and
g
as in (
25
), i.e

\displaystyle\Phi(X,Z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}\frac{4}{p-1}&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{+}\\
-X^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}\frac{p(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)-(\tilde{N}_{-}+2)}{p-1}&\mbox{if }(X,Z)\in R^{-}\end{array}\right.

Suppose there is a periodic orbit which encloses a region D. It follows from the Green’s area formula that:

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\partial D}\varphi\{fdX-gdZ\}=\int_{D}\Phi dXdZ=\int_{R^{+}\cap D}\Phi dXdZ+\int_{R^{-}\cap D}\Phi dXdZ,

where the first equality holds since
dX=fdt
and
dZ=gdt
.

From this it follows that for
p\neq\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}
, any periodic orbit must intersect both regions
R^{+},R^{-}
.

It is clear that, for
p
satisfying
1<p<\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2},

\Phi
is positive and the above identity is a contradiction. Hence i) holds.

Now assume
p>3
(which implies that
\beta<0
). In the region
R^{+}
, we have
\Lambda<Z<2\Lambda
, which implies
Z^{\beta}<\Lambda^{\beta}
. Also, in
R^{-}
,
0<Z<\Lambda
, hence
-Z^{\beta}<-\Lambda^{\beta}
. Hence the following bound holds:

0=\int_{R^{+}\cap D}\Phi dXdZ+\int_{R^{-}\cap D}\Phi dXdZ\leq\\
\frac{4\Lambda^{\beta}}{p-1}\int_{R^{+}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ-\frac{p(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)-(\tilde{N}_{-}+2)}{p-1}\Lambda^{\beta}\int_{R^{-}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ

(31)

In order to conclude the proof it is sufficient to check that the ratio of the areas
|\frac{R^{+}\cap D|}{|R^{-}\cap D|}

\nrightarrow

\infty
as
p
goes to infinity. Indeed if this holds, taking the limit as
p
goes to infinity we get a contradiction and the result follows.

Indicative of the flow for
p>\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}

Let
P_{1}=(x_{1},\Lambda),P_{2}=(x_{2},\Lambda)
be two points where the orbit intersects the concavity line
\ell
, note that since
P=(\frac{1}{p},\Lambda)
is the only point where

is zero, this implies the uniqueness of
P_{1},P_{2}
. Since
\dot{X}>0
in

, it follows that
R^{+}\cap D
is contained in the rectangle
Q_{1}=[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[\Lambda,2\Lambda]
. Also since

above the line
\ell_{1}
defined in (

) the rectangle
Q_{2}=[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2),\Lambda]

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{|R^{+}\cap D|}{|R^{-}\cap D|}\leq\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})\Lambda}{(x_{2}-x_{1})(\Lambda-\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2))}=\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda-\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)}.

Therefore the right hand side cannot converge to

as

, so the existence of

\text{Define }\tilde{p}_{-}=\inf\left\{p\in(\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2},\infty)\;|\;\nexists\text{ periodic orbits }\forall p^{\prime}>p\right\}

(32)

Theorem 3.3

(Bound for
\tilde{p}_{-}
)
.

It holds:

\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}\leq\tilde{p}_{-}\leq\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2}+\frac{4}{\tilde{N}_{-}-2+\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)^{2}}

.

Proof.

If
p>3
and a periodic orbit exists, then, going back to (
31
), it holds:

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{4\Lambda^{\beta}}{p-1}\int_{R^{+}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ-\frac{p(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)-(\tilde{N}_{-}+2)}{p-1}\Lambda^{\beta}\int_{R^{-}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ

Considering the rectangles
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
defined in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we have:

\int_{R^{+}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ\leq\int_{Q_{1}}X^{\alpha}dXdZ\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda-\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)}\int_{Q_{2}}X^{\alpha}dXdZ\leq\\
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda-\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)}\int_{R^{-}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ

Therefore

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda-\lambda(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)}\cdot\frac{4\Lambda^{\beta}}{p-1}-\frac{p(\tilde{N}_{-}-2)-(\tilde{N}_{-}+2)}{p-1}\Lambda^{\beta}\right)\int_{R^{-}\cap D}X^{\alpha}dXdZ

).

We recall that for an orbit

With the same proofs as in

then
\Gamma_{p}
is bounded and remains in the rectangle

if there exists

such that

and
u_{p}>0

We prove now a crucial result about nonexistence of solutions for the problem (

Theorem 3.5
.

For
p>\tilde{p}_{-}
, defined in (
32
), there are no solutions of (
20
) in the ball.

Proof.

We recall that
\tilde{p}_{-}\geq\frac{\tilde{N}_{-}+2}{\tilde{N}_{-}2}.
Assume that there is a solution of (
20
) in the ball for some
p>\tilde{p}_{-}
. Then we would have a trajectory
\Gamma_{p}=\Gamma_{p}(t)=(X_{p}(t),Z_{p}(t))
starting from

such that
\Gamma_{p}
blows up in finite time, after intersecting the line

(see Lemma
3.4
). On the other hand, if we consider the unique trajectory
\tau_{p}
whose
\omega
-limit is
A_{0}
(which is a saddle point for

and backtrack it we should be in either one of the following cases:

a)

\alpha(\tau_{p})
is a stationary point or a periodic orbit around

.

b)

\tau_{p}
blows up backward in finite time.

The case a) is not possible. Indeed
M_{0}
is a sink and no periodic orbit exists for

, by definition (

N_{0}
cannot be the
\alpha
-limit of
\tau_{p}
because

is a nondegenerate saddle point, so that, only the trajectory
\Gamma_{p}
starts from
N_{0}
and
\omega(\Gamma_{p})
is not
A_{0}
since
\Gamma_{p}
blows up in finite time.

Also case b) is not possible because
\tau_{p}
is necessarily bounded because it stays in the regions enclosed by the
X
axis,
Z
axis, the orbit
\Gamma_{p}
and the line
L_{1}
from which any orbit can only exit in forward time (by the direction of the vector field on

), see Figure

.

Thus we have a contradiction which implies that

Before proving Theorem

Definition 3.6
.

Let

be a radial solution of (

\mathbb{R}^{2}

\alpha=\frac{2}{p-1}

]
in terms of the dynamical system (

), a fast decaying, slow decaying or pseudo-slow decaying solution corresponds to an orbit
\Gamma_{p}
such that
\omega(\Gamma_{p})=

A_{0}
, or
M_{0}

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It is not difficult to see that

On the cauchy problem with degenerate diffusion and nonlocal nonlinear sources
Shen Bian

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the analysis of non-negative solutions for a generalisation of the parabolic equation with porous medium like nonlinear diffusion and nonlinear nonlocal reaction. We investigate under which conditions equilibration between two competing effects, repulsion modelled by nonlinear diffusion and aggregation modelled by nonlinear reaction, occurs. Precisely, we exhibit that the qualitative behavior of solutions is decided by the nonlinear diffusion which is chosen in such a way that its scaling and the reaction term coincide, i.e. that there is a critical exponent m + 2 / n m+2/n for the reaction exponent α , \alpha, solutions exist globally with uniformly upper bounds in the case of (i) 1 ≤ α < m + 2 / n 1\leq\alpha<m+2/n for any initial data, (ii) α > m + 2 / n \alpha>m+2/n for small initial data and (iii) α = m + 2 / n \alpha=m+2/n for small mass capacity M 0 M_{0} . In the case of (ii) and (iii), the decay properties of the solution are also discussed. Moreover, numerical simulations are carried out to verify the theoretical analysis and explore other issues that lie beyond the scope of the analysis.



1
Introduction

In this work, we analyse qualitative properties of non-negative solutions in dimension
n\geq 3
for the degenerate equation of the type

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u^{m}+\chi u^{\alpha}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u(x,t)dx\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\end{array}\right.

where
\chi>0,m>1,\alpha\geq 1
. (
1
) is related to many equations arising from population dynamics
[
11
,
17
]
,
u
is the density of the population. The purpose of nonlinear diffusion
\Delta u^{m}
with
m>1
is to model the local repulsion of population, this can be interpreted as taking into account anti-crowding effects
[
10
]
. The reaction term presents a growth factor of logistic type defined in terms of the total mass of the population which is a competitive term limiting such growth, where the resources of the environment can be consumed nonlocally.
\chi u^{\alpha}
can also be interpreted as the nonlinear source in the process of diffusion
[
1
]
, which is called heat source for
\chi>0
and cold source for
\chi<0.
In the case of
\alpha=1
, the coefficient
\chi M_{0}
is sometimes called Malthusian parameter which induces an exponential growth for low density populations. The case
\alpha=2
, which is the motivation of this work, considers the addition of sexual reproduction to the model with the reproduction rate proportional to the square of the density
[
30
]
. Nonlocal type reaction terms can also describe Darwinian evolution of a structured population density or the behavior of cancer cells with therapy as well as polychemotherapy
[
21
,
22
]
. The main feature of this class of equations is the interplay between the degeneracy in the principal part and the growth of the forcing term.

A fundamental property of the solutions to (
1
) is the formal boundedness of the total mass of the system

\displaystyle m(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u(x,t)dx

(1.3)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m(t)=\chi\left(M_{0}-m(t)\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u^{\alpha}dx.

(1.4)

If the initial mass
m_{0}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{0}dx>M_{0}
, then
m(t)
decreases in time and
M_{0}\leq m_{0}
for all
t>0.
Thus we find that
u(x,t)
is a subsolution of the porous medium equation
v_{t}=\Delta v^{m}
which admits a global solution for any
m>1

[
26
,
27
]
. By the comparison principle, all solutions of (
1
) exist globally. When the initial mass
m_{0}<M_{0}
, then
m(t)
increases in time and

m_{0}\leq m(t)\leq M_{0}.
Therefore, we assume that the initial mass satisfies

m_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{0}dx<M_{0}

throughout this paper. In this sense,
M_{0}
can be considered as the carrying capacity
[
24
]
.

In any dimension
n\geq 3,
we will concentrate on a particular choice of the nonlinear reaction exponent

\displaystyle\alpha=m+2/n

which produces a balance in the mass-invariant scaling of diffusion and reaction. Indeed, let
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{n}u(\lambda x,t)
of same mass as
u
, the diffusion term
\lambda^{2+nm}\Delta u_{\lambda}^{m}
has the same scaling as the reaction term
\lambda^{n\alpha}u_{\lambda}^{\alpha}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\lambda}dx\right)
if and only if
nm+2=n\alpha
or equivalently
\alpha=m+2/n.
The dynamics in (
1
) are governed by the interaction between nonlinear diffusion and reaction. We are interested in three cases: critical case
\alpha=m+2/n
, subcritical case
\alpha<m+2/n
, supercritical case
\alpha>m+2/n
. Under what conditions which of the two competing items dominates will be explored in this paper.

1.1
Comments on the non-degenerate case
m=1

In the non-degenerate case
m=1,
there is a vast body of literature on the semi-linear reaction-diffusion equations
u_{t}=\Delta u+F(u)
in bounded domain, cf. the papers
[

,
15
,

,

,

F(u)=\int_{\Omega}u^{p}dx-\beta u^{q}

becomes more influential as population grows such that the equation possesses the comparison principle which helps in proving the existence of global solutions by virtue of the boundedness of

[

where the equation is equipped with a decreasing Lyapunov functional. Yet there are few results on this type of equations in the whole space. This is partially due to the apparent lack of a good Lyapunov functional and the unboundedness of the domain. More importantly, the comparison principle (which has been used in the equation with local reaction term for the global existence
[

Before turning to the nonlocal term in the whole space, we firstly mention the following fundamental work of Fujita
[

the local classical solution blows up in finite time (the same is true for
\alpha=1+2/n

[

,
16
]
). The natural guess is that if

remains positive, our model has similar structure to Fujita equation. However, the result obtained in our previous paper
[
4
]
gives an opposite consequence. That’s, for
1<\alpha<1+2/n
, our model admits a global classical solution as long as the initial value
u_{0}(x)
is nontrivial.

1.3
Structure of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
prepares some preliminary lemmas. The following sections are devoted to the detailed proof of the existence of weak solutions for the three cases. The point here is to establish the result with
all necessary details: regularized problem, uniform estimates, passing to the limit in the
regularization parameter.

Precisely, in Section
3
, a regularized equation is constructed for which the global strong solution exists. Firstly, a key maximal time of local existence criterion for solutions of the regularized problem is established, see Proposition
3.2
. Then a priori estimates of the local solution have been derived, see Proposition
3.3
for a detailed study of the regularity properties of the solutions. Furthermore, by Moser iterative method, we prove that the solution is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
space for almost any positive
t
, see Proposition
3.7
.

Section
4
displays the global existence of a weak solution to (
1
) by passing the regularized parameter to zero. The main difficulty comes from the nonlocal term
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}udx
and we prove the three cases in which we use the standard arguments relying on the evolution of the second moment of solutions. We also derive the decay rate of global solutions in the critical case and the supercritical case.

Finally, in Section
5
, series of numerical experiments are carried out to verify the results of the earlier sections and explore other issues that lie beyond the scope of the analysis. Further problems and open questions for the nonlinear dynamics of (
1
) are also addressed using numerical simulations.

Regularized problem

In order to justify the formal arguments of the priori estimates (which will be given in Proposition

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\partial t}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}^{m}+\varepsilon\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\chi u_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}dx\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0,\\

(Time global strong solution)
Let

Suppose that

satisfies (

For the proof of Theorem

, Proposition

Proposition 3.2
.

(Time local existence and blow-up criteria) Let
n\geq 3,\alpha\geq 1,m>1.
Suppose that
u_{0\varepsilon}
satisfies (

is a non-negative strong solution of (

then we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T_{\max}}\|u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\infty.

Proof.
The proof will be carried out as follows. The first route we shall follow here is to consider the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\left((mu_{\varepsilon}^{m-1}+\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right)+\chi u_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0\varepsilon}(x),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where

is a non-negative function. We show the local existence of a strong solution of (
3
). Secondly, by the fixed point theorem we prove the local existence of the strong solution of (
3
). Finally, we state that the local solution satisfies a blow-up criterion and thus close the proof. In the following precise discussions, we will deal with the nonlinear reaction
\alpha>1
and the linear reaction
\alpha=1
respectively.

Step 1
(Local existence of the non-negative solution of (
3

u_{\varepsilon}
in (

\displaystyle(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\left((mf^{m-1}+\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right)+\chi f^{\alpha-1}u_{\varepsilon}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right)

\displaystyle=(mf^{m-1}+\varepsilon)\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+m\nabla f^{m-1}\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\chi f^{\alpha-1}u_{\varepsilon}\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right).

(3.12)

Here
\alpha>1.
The proof is refined in the spirit of
[
9
,
25
]
. We shall use the notation

\displaystyle X_{T}:=\{

\displaystyle f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,n+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})),f_{t}\in L^{n+2}(Q_{T}):

\displaystyle f\geq 0,~\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,n+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))}+\|f_{t}\|_{L^{n+2}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle\qquad~~\leq c_{1}\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{W^{2,n+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+c_{2}\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+c_{3}\}

(3.13)

for some constants

By Theorem 9.1 of
[

with

, it follows that (

) corresponding to the initial data

has the unique strong solution

Hence we can define a mapping

\displaystyle\Phi:~f\in X_{T}\mapsto u^{f}_{\varepsilon}\in W(Q_{T}).

(3.14)

Now we claim that
u^{f}_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})).
In the following, we use
u_{\varepsilon}
instead of
u^{f}_{\varepsilon}
for simplicity. Multiplying (
3
) by
|u_{\varepsilon}|^{k-2}u_{\varepsilon}(k>1)
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{k}dx

\displaystyle=-(k-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(mf^{m-1}+\varepsilon)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{k-2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\chi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f^{\alpha-1}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{k}dx\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right)

which follows that for

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\chi M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{\alpha-1}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\chi M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}ds.

Taking

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}e^{\chi M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{\alpha-1}~T}.

The nonnegativity of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u_{\varepsilon}^{-}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(mf^{m-1}+\varepsilon)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{-}|^{2}dx+\chi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f^{\alpha-1}|u_{\varepsilon}^{-}|^{2}dx\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right)

It follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq e^{\chi M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{\alpha-1}T}~\|u_{0\varepsilon}^{-}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=0

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\geq 0,~~a.e.~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(3.17)

This allows us to integrate (
3
) over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}dx=\chi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f^{\alpha-1}u_{\varepsilon}dx\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}hdx\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}e^{\chi M_{0}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{\alpha-1}~T}.

(3.18)

Now we can see that there exists

Substituting (
3

3

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T_{1}}\|w\|_{L^{n+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{n+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))}.

The proof for the case

Step 2

3

\displaystyle C(k,M_{0})\|u\|_{L^{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{r}

\displaystyle C(k,M_{0})\|u\|_{L^{k+\alpha-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{r\theta}\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{r(1-\theta)}

\displaystyle C(k,M_{0})\|u\|_{L^{k+\alpha-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{r\theta},

. Moreover, a tedious calculation assures

if and only if

\displaystyle 1\leq\alpha<m+2/n.

(3.36)

, by Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\left(\|u\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{k}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha-2}{k-1}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{k+\alpha-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{k+\alpha-1}\|u\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{k-1}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{k+\alpha-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{k+\alpha-1}M_{0}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{k-1}}.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u^{k}dx+\frac{km_{0}\chi}{2M_{0}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{k-1}}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{k}\right)^{\frac{k+\alpha-2}{k-1}}\leq C(k,m_{0},M_{0}).

(3.39)

Setting

\displaystyle y(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u^{k}dx,~~a=\frac{k+\alpha-2}{k-1},~~\eta=C(k,m_{0},M_{0})

(3.40)

in Lemma
2.4
one has that for any

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C,~~\mbox{for any }t>0,

(3.41)

where
C
is a constant depending on
\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
,
k,m_{0},M_{0},\alpha,m,n,\chi
.

On the other hand, we integrate (
3.37
) from
0
to
T
in time to obtain that for any

from which we derive that for any

and

(3.43)

.

which gives the following regularities that for any

\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right),~~u\in L^{k+\alpha-1}\left(0,T;L^{k+\alpha-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right).

(3.81)

(2)
p_{0}<k<\infty

Following

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k}dx

\displaystyle-km(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k-2}u^{m-1}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+k\chi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}u^{\alpha}dx\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}udx\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4mk(k-1)}{(k+m-1)^{2}}\|\nabla(u-N)_{+}^{\frac{k+m-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}+k\chi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}(u-N+N)^{\alpha}dx\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}udx\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4mk(k-1)}{(k+m-1)^{2}}\|\nabla(u-N)_{+}^{\frac{k+m-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}+k\chi M_{0}2^{\alpha-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}\left((u-N)^{\alpha}+N^{\alpha}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4mk(k-1)}{(k+m-1)^{2}}\|\nabla(u-N)_{+}^{\frac{k+m-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}+k\chi M_{0}2^{\alpha-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k+\alpha-1}dx

\displaystyle+k\chi M_{0}N^{\alpha}2^{\alpha-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}dx.

The terms involving

(3.84)

\displaystyle\int_{N<u\leq N+1}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N<u\leq N+1}1dx\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{N<u\leq N+1}udx\leq\frac{m(t)}{N},

\displaystyle\int_{u>N+1}(u-N)_{+}^{k-1}dx

Collecting everything together (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{4mk(k-1)}{(k+m-1)^{2}}\|\nabla(u-N)_{+}^{\frac{k+m-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}+k\chi M_{0}N^{\alpha-1}2^{\alpha-1}m(t)+k\chi M_{0}N^{\alpha}2^{\alpha-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(u-N)_{+}^{k}dx

\displaystyle+k\chi M_{0}2^{\alpha-1}S_{n}^{-1}\|\nabla(u-N)_{+}^{\frac{k+m-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\|(u-N)_{+}\|_{L^{\frac{n(\alpha-m)}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{\alpha-m}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\chi,M_{0},m)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2\alpha+m-1}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2\alpha}dx\right).

(4.9)

Integrating (

\displaystyle C(\chi,M_{0},m)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2\alpha+m}dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2\alpha}dxdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\nabla u_{0\varepsilon}^{m}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{0\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\frac{4m\varepsilon}{(m+1)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u_{0\varepsilon}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}|^{2}dx.

By (

and thus from (

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have identified a critical exponent
\alpha=m+2/n
for equation (
1
) that separates blow-up solutions from those that exist globally, and we have described the structure in three cases. Firstly, for the subcritical case
1\leq\alpha<m+2/n
(the diffusion dominates for large population), the global existence and uniformly boundedness of a weak solution to (
1
) are obtained. Then for the critical case
\alpha=m+2/n
, there exists an upper bound
M_{\ast}
such that the solution exists globally for
m_{0}<M_{0}<M_{\ast}.
Thirdly, for the supercritical case
\alpha>m+2/n
where the logistic growth term dominates for large population, there exists a universal constant
C_{p_{0}}
such that the solution will exist globally with initial data satisfying
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<C_{p_{0}}
. In addition, for the critical case
\alpha=m+2/n
and the supercritical case
\alpha>m+2/n
, numerical simulations illustrate that for any given initial mass
m_{0},
there exists a critical threshold
M_{c}
to separate finite time blow-up and global existence. Precisely, at the time that the population grows, the competitive effect of the logistic growth term becomes more influential such that finite time aggregation occurs when
M_{0}>M_{c}
(the position and number of the blow-up points will change for different
M_{0}
). Inversely, for
m_{0}<M_{0}<M_{c},

M_{0}
is too small to prevent the population from spreading around although the growth term is dominant at the beginning (see Figure
3
(a3)), finally the density converges to the compactly supported steady profile. This suggests us to further describe that how is
M_{c}
selected for any given
m_{0}
, which is a challenging question in our future research.

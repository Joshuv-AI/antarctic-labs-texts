CERTAIN FLOOR FUNCTION SUMS OVER POWERS Joshua Stucky


Abstract
We study the sums S f ​ ( x ) = ∑ n ≤ x f ⁡ ( ⌊ x n ⌋ ) S_{f}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right) when f f is supported on r r th powers with r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 . This restriction allows us to give nontrivial estimates for one of the error terms in the asymptotic expansion of S f ​ ( x ) S_{f}(x) . We also state several conjectures related to our results.



1. Introduction and Statement of Results

Since their recent introduction by Bordellés et. al.
[
1
]
, there has been a fair amount of research into the sums

S_{f}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right),

where
f
is an arithmetic function and
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
denotes the integer part function. Under mild assumptions on the growth of
f
, one can establish decent asymptotic estimates for such sums. For instance, Wu
[
8
]
and Zhai
[
9
]
showed independently that if
f(n)\ll n^{\alpha}
for some
0\leq\alpha<1
, then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\right),

(1.1)

where here and throughout we denote

C_{f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n(n+1)}.

(1.2)

This estimate has been improved by a number of authors for functions satisfying various additional properties. For instance, if one has a mean square estimate

\sum_{n\leq x}\left|f(n)\right|^{2}\ll x^{\alpha}

for some
\alpha\in[1,2)
, then Wu and Zhao
[
11
]
have shown that

C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{2+3\alpha}{8}}\right).

In a different direction, one can use exponential sum estimates to improve (
1.1
) when
f
has a convolution structure, say
f=1*g
, or an analogous structure such as that given by Vaughan’s identity in the case
f=\Lambda
(see, e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
).

In this article, we introduce another property that
f
may possess that allows us to improve (
1.1
). Specifically, we consider functions of the form

f(n)=\begin{cases}h(d)&\text{if $n=d^{r}$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

The reason we consider such functions is that when
r\geq 2
, we can give a nontrivial estimate for one of the error terms arising in the asymptotic formula for
S_{f}
, namely

S_{f}^{\dagger}(x;A)=\sum_{n<A}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right).

(1.4)

In all previous works, this quantity is estimated trivially using a point-wise upper bound for
f
. For functions supported on
r
th powers, we are able to improve this estimate by understanding the spacing between the integers
n
for which there exists an integer
d
satisfying

\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor=d^{r}.

We do so by following the method of Roth and Halberstam (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
for a nice survey of these ideas). As an example of our results, if
h(n)\ll n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2r-1})
, then for
A\ll x^{\frac{1}{r+1}}
,

S_{f}^{\dagger}\left(x;A\right)\ll\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}A^{\frac{1}{r(2r-1)}}x^{\frac{r-1}{r(2r-1)}},

(1.5)

which is better than the trivial estimate

S_{f}^{\dagger}\left(x;A\right)\ll A\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}

(1.6)

so long as
A\geq x^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}
. Using Lemma
2.2
, we will prove the following generalization of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and let

\eta=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $r$ is odd},\\
0&\text{if $r$ is even}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

If
f
is given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{r+2-\eta}{2r-2+2\eta})
, then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{2(1+\alpha)}{3r+\eta}}\right),

(1.8)

where
C_{f}
is given by
(
1.2
)
.

We note that the trivial estimate (
1.6
) leads to

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r+1}}\right),

which is weaker than (
1.8
) when
r\geq 3
. The form of Theorem
1.1
is someone unnatural, owing primarily to the restriction on the range of
A
in Lemma
2.2
. It seems reasonable that the following simpler and stronger result should hold for a more restricted range of
\alpha
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. If
f
is given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2r-1}]
, then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2r}}\right),

where
C_{f}
is given by
(
1.2
)
.

It is possible that Conjecture
1.2
holds for a larger range of
\alpha
. The reason to consider only the range given in Conjecture
1.2
is that if we take
A
maximally in (
1.5
), then

S_{f}^{\dagger}\left(x;x^{\frac{1}{r+1}}\right)\ll x^{\frac{r}{(r+1)(2r-1)}+\frac{\alpha}{r+1}}\ll x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2r}}

precisely when
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2r-1}
. Conjecture
1.2
then follows from the estimate

\bigg|\sum_{n\leq x^{\frac{1}{r+1}}}\psi\left(\frac{x}{n^{r}+\delta}\right)\bigg|\ll x^{\frac{r}{(r+1)(2r-1)}},

where
\delta=0
or
1
and
\psi(t)=t-\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}
. Using estimates for exponential sums, we are able to prove this in the case
r=2
, as well as an estimate for general
r
assuming the existence of certain exponent pairs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
f
be given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2r-1}]
. Suppose there exists an exponent pair
(k,\ell)
such that
\ell=rk
. Then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{\alpha}{r+1}+\frac{k}{k+1}}\log x++x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2r}}\right),

where the factor
\log x
is present only when
\alpha=0
.

This implies the following improvements to Theorem
1.1
in the cases
r=2,3,4
.

Corollary 1.4
.

1.

Let
r=2
and let
f
be given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{3}]
. Then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{\alpha}{3}+\frac{2}{9}}\log x+x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{4}}\right),

2.

Let
r=3
and let
f
be given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{5}]
. Then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{\alpha}{4}+\frac{11}{64}}\log x\right).

3.

Let
r=4
and let
f
be given by
(
1.3
)
with
h
satisfying
\left|h(d)\right|\ll d^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{7}]
. Then

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{\alpha}{5}+\frac{1}{7}}\log x\right).

In each case,
C_{f}
is given by
(
1.2
)
and the factor
\log x
is present only when
\alpha=0
. In particular, Conjecture
1.2
holds in the case
r=2
.

In light of Theorem
1.3
, we also make the following general conjecture regarding exponent pairs, which may be of independent interest.

Conjecture 1.5
.

For any real
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 1
, there exists an exponent pair
(k,\ell)
such that

\left|\frac{\ell}{k}-r\right|<\varepsilon.

to denote the condition
N<n\leq 2N

2. A Spacing Lemma

In this section, we prove Lemma
2.2
, which is the main input needed for Theorem
1.1
. To do so, we require the following lemma concerning certain polynomial identities. It is a special case of Lemma 2 of Huxley and Nair
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
be a positive integer. For each integer
l
with
1\leq l\leq r
, there exist polynomials
P_{l}
and
Q_{l}
such that, as
x\to 0
,

(1)

\left|P_{l}(x)(1-x)^{r}-Q_{l}(x)\right|\ll\left|x\right|^{2l-1}
;

(2)

P_{l}
and
Q_{l}
have degree
l-1
;

(3)

the coefficients of
P_{l}
and
Q_{l}
are nonzero integers.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
and let
f
be given by
(
1.3
)
. Suppose
h(d)\leq Cd^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha,C\geq 0
. Then for each integer
l
with
1\leq l\leq r
, there exists a constant
C_{r}
depending only on
r
such that for
A\leq C_{r}x^{\frac{r+1-l}{2r+1-l}}
, we have

\sum_{n<A}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right)\ll x^{\frac{l-1}{r(2l-1)}+\frac{\alpha}{r}}\left(A^{\frac{r+1-l}{r(2l-1)}-\frac{\alpha}{r}}+1\right).

(2.1)

Proof.

As stated in the introduction, we follow the method described in Section 1 of
[
2
]
. Trivially

\sum_{n<A}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right)\ll\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{D}}_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\ll D\ll x^{1/r}}D^{\alpha}T(D),

(2.2)

where

T(D)=\left|\left\{d\sim D:d^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\ \text{for some $n$}\right\}\right|.

Suppose
d,d-a\in T(D)
so that

d^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor,\qquad(d-a)^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor.

(2.3)

Removing the floor brackets and rearranging, we have

n_{1}=\frac{x}{d^{r}}+O\left(\frac{x}{D^{2r}}\right),\qquad n_{2}=\frac{x}{(d-a)^{r}}+O\left(\frac{x}{D^{2r}}\right).

(2.4)

Using Lemma
2.1
, there exist polynomials
P_{l},Q_{l}
such that for
a=o(D)
, we have

\left|P_{l}\left(\frac{a}{d}\right)\left(1-\frac{a}{d}\right)^{r}-Q_{l}\left(\frac{a}{d}\right)\right|\ll\left(\frac{a}{D}\right)^{2l-1}.

Multiplying through by
d^{r+l-1}
, we have

\left|P(a,d)\left(d-a\right)^{r}-Q(a,d)d^{r}\right|\ll a^{2l-1}D^{r-l}

(2.5)

for some homogeneous polynomials
P_{0}(a,d),Q_{0}(a,d)
of degree
l-1
with integer coefficients. Consider now the modified difference

\mathcal{D}=P_{0}(a,d)n_{1}-Q_{0}(a,d)n_{2}.

By (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

\left|\mathcal{D}\right|\ll\frac{xa^{2l-1}}{D^{l+r}}+\frac{x}{D^{2r+1-l}}.

The inequality
A\leq C_{r}x^{\frac{r+1-l}{2r+1-l}}
implies

D\gg\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\geq C_{r}^{\prime}x^{\frac{1}{2r+1-l}}

(2.6)

for some constant
C_{r}^{\prime}
. Taking
C_{r}
sufficiently small, we see that there is a constant
C_{r}^{\prime\prime}
such that

\left|\mathcal{D}\right|\leq C_{r}^{\prime\prime}\frac{xa^{2l-1}}{D^{l+r}}+\frac{1}{2}.

(2.7)

Since
\mathcal{D}
is an integer, it follows that if
a\leq C_{r}^{\prime\prime\prime}D^{\frac{l+r}{2l-1}}x^{-\frac{1}{2l-1}}=L
, say, for some sufficiently small constant
C_{r}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, then
\left|\mathcal{D}\right|<1
, so
\mathcal{D}=0
.

Suppose now that
I
is a subinterval of
(D,2D]
of length at most
L
. Using the above analysis, we will show that
I
contains at most
2l
elements of
T(D)
. Suppose that
d,d-a,d-a-b\in T(D)
, so

d^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor,\qquad(d-a)^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor,\qquad(d-a-b)^{r}=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n_{3}}\right\rfloor.

Then since
\left|I\right|\leq L
, it follows that

\displaystyle P_{0}(a,d)n_{2}-Q_{0}(a,d)n_{1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle P_{0}(a+b,d)n_{3}-Q_{0}(a+b,d)n_{1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle P_{0}(b,d+a)n_{3}-Q_{0}(b,d+a)n_{2}

\displaystyle=0

We multiply the second of these by
P_{0}(b,d+a)
and the third by
P_{0}(a+b,d)
, then subtract to see that

P_{0}(a+b,d)Q_{0}(b,d+a)n_{2}-P_{0}(b,d+a)Q_{0}(a+b,d)n_{1}=0.

(2.8)

We view
d
and
a
as fixed and view the left hand side as a polynomial in
b
. Note that, by construction,
P_{0}(a+b,d)Q_{0}(b,d+a)
and
P_{0}(b,d+a)Q_{0}(a+b,d)
have the same leading coefficient as polynomials in
b
, namely
v_{P}v_{Q}
, where
v_{P}
and
v_{Q}
are the leading coefficients of
P_{l}
and
Q_{l}
, respectively. Clearly we cannot have
n_{1}=n_{2}
, and therefore the left side of (
2.8
) is a polynomial of degree
2l-2
. Thus (
2.8
) has at most
2l-2
solutions in
b
, and it follows that any interval
I
of length at most
L
contains at most
2l
elements of
T(D)
. Dividing the interval
(D,2D]
into
O(DL^{-1})
intervals of length at most
L
, we deduce that

T(D)\ll\frac{D}{L}+1\ll\left(\frac{x}{D^{r-l+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2l-1}}.

We conclude the proof of Lemma
2.2
by substituting this into (
2.2
) and executing the dyadic sum in
D
.

∎

3. Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3

3.1.  Main and Error Terms

Similar to previous works, we decompose
S_{f}(x)
via

S_{f}(x)=S_{f}^{\dagger}(x;B)+S_{f}^{\flat}(x;A;B)+S_{f}^{\sharp}(x;A),

where

\displaystyle S_{f}^{\dagger}(x;B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq B}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n}\right\rfloor\right),

\displaystyle S_{f}^{\flat}(x;A;B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}h(d)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}+1}\right\rfloor\right),

\displaystyle S_{f}^{\sharp}(x;A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\leq\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}h(d)\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}+1}\right\rfloor\right),

and
1\leq B\leq A\leq\sqrt{x}
are parameters to be chosen. We treat these sums as follows.

For
S_{f}^{\dagger}(x;B)
, we apply Lemma
2.2
. In the range

1\leq l\leq r,\qquad\alpha<\frac{r+1-l}{2l-1},\qquad B\leq C_{r}x^{\frac{r+1-l}{2r+1-l}},

(3.1)

we have

S_{f}^{\dagger}(x;B)\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}x^{\frac{l-1}{r(2l-1)}}B^{\frac{r+1-l}{r(2l-1)}}.

(3.2)

For
S_{f}^{\flat}(x;A;B)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|S_{f}^{\flat}(x;A;B)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\left|h(d)\right|\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}+1}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}d^{\alpha}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d^{r}+1}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\leq x\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\frac{d^{\alpha}}{d^{r}(d^{r}+1)}+\left|\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|,

where

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}(x;A,B)=\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}d^{\alpha}\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}+\delta}\right).

(3.3)

Since
r\geq 2
, we have
\alpha<2r-1
by (
3.1
). Thus

S_{f}^{\flat}(x;A;B)\ll\frac{A^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r}}+\left|\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|.

(3.4)

Finally, for
S_{f}^{\sharp}(x;A)
, we remove the floor brackets to get

S_{f}^{\sharp}(x;A)=x\sum_{d\leq\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\frac{h(d)}{d^{r}(d^{r}+1)}+\sum_{d\leq\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}h(d)\left(\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}+1}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}}\right)\right).

The first sum is

x\sum_{d\leq\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\frac{h(d)}{d^{r}(d^{r}+1)}=C_{f}x+O\left(\frac{A^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r}}\right),

and thus

S_{f}^{\sharp}(x;A)=C_{f}x+O\bigg(\frac{A^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r}}+\left|\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\sharp}(x;A)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\sharp}(x;A)\right|\bigg),

(3.5)

where

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\sharp}(x;A)=\sum_{d\leq\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}h(d)\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}+\delta}\right).

(3.6)

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.4
), and (
3.5
), we have, in the range (
3.1
),

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(\frac{A^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r}}+\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}x^{\frac{l-1}{r(2l-1)}}B^{\frac{r+1-l}{r(2l-1)}}+\mathcal{E}(x;A,B)\right),

(3.7)

where

\mathcal{E}(x;A,B)=\left|\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\sharp}(x;A)\right|+\left|\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\sharp}(x;A)\right|

(3.8)

and
\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\sharp}
are given by (
3.3
) and (
3.6
), respectively. We note the trivial bound

\mathcal{E}(x;A,B)\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{r}}.

(3.9)

3.2.  Proof of Theorem
1.1

Using the trivial estimate (
3.9
) with
B=A
and recalling that we will choose
A\leq\sqrt{x}
, we have, in the range (
3.1
)

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\left(\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}+x^{\frac{l-1}{r(2l-1)}}A^{\frac{r+1-l}{r(2l-1)}}\right)\right).

The optimal choice
A=x^{\frac{l}{r+l}}
gives the estimate

S_{f}(x)=C_{f}x+O\left(x^{\frac{1+\alpha}{l+r}}\right).

However, we must choose
A
subject to (
3.1
), and this requires

l\leq\frac{r+1}{2}.

We conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
by choosing
l=\frac{r+\eta}{2}
.

3.3.  Proof of Theorem
1.3

Recall that

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}(x;A,B)=\sum_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<d<\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}d^{\alpha}\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}+\delta}\right).

Subdividing into dyadic intervals and applying partial summation, we have

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\ll\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{D}}_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\ll D\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}D^{\alpha}\max_{D\leq D^{\prime}\leq 2D}\left|\sum_{D<d\leq D^{\prime}}\psi\left(\frac{x}{d^{r}+\delta}\right)\right|.

We estimate the sum over
d
via the following lemma, which is a special case of Lemma 4.3 of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Graham and Kolesnik)
.

Let
g(n)\asymp yn^{-r}
for some
r>0
and
g\in C^{1}([N,2N])
with
N\geq 1
. Then for any exponent pair
(k,\ell)
and any
N^{\prime}\in[N,2N]
, we have

\sum_{N<n\leq N^{\prime}}\psi(g(n))\ll y^{\frac{k}{k+1}}N^{\frac{\ell-rk}{k+1}}+\frac{N^{r+1}}{y}.

Applying this to
\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}
yields

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\ll\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{D}}_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\ll D\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}D^{\alpha}\left(x^{\frac{k}{k+1}}D^{\frac{\ell-rk}{k+1}}+\frac{D^{r+1}}{x}\right).

(3.10)

By hypothesis, there exists an exponent pair
(k,\ell)
with
\ell=rk
, and so

\mathcal{E}_{\delta}^{\flat}(x;A,B)\ll\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{D}}_{\left(\frac{x}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\ll D\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}D^{\alpha}\left(x^{\frac{k}{k+1}}+\frac{D^{r+1}}{x}\right)\ll\left(\frac{x}{B}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{r}}\left(x^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\log x+\frac{x^{\frac{1}{r}}}{B^{1+\frac{1}{r}}}\right),

where the factor
\log x

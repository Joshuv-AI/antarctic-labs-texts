Optimal Convergence Rate for Mirror Descent Methods with special Time-Varying Step Sizes Rules
Mohammad S. Alkousa, Fedor S. Stonyakin, Asmaa M. Abdo, Mohammad M. Alcheikh

Abstract
. In this paper, the optimal convergence rate O ( N − 1 / 2 ) O\left(N^{-1/2}\right) (where N N is the total number of iterations performed by the algorithm), without the presence of a logarithmic factor, is proved for mirror descent algorithms with special time-varying step sizes, for solving classical constrained non-smooth problems, problems with the composite model and problems with non-smooth functional (inequality types) constraints. The proven result is an improvement on the well-known rate O ( N − 1 / 2 log ( N ) ) O\left(N^{-1/2}\log(N)\right) for the mirror descent algorithms with the time-varying step sizes under consideration. It was studied a new weighting scheme assigns smaller weights to the initial points and larger weights to the most recent points. This scheme improves the convergence rate of the considered mirror descent methods, which in the conducted numerical experiments outperform the other methods providing a better solution in all the considered test problems.



1.
Introduction

The first method for unconstrained minimization of non-smooth convex function was proposed with fixed step size in
[
30
]
. In the next years, there were developed several strategies for choosing the step sizes
[
28
]
. Among them, are fixed step length, non-summable diminishing step, square summable but not summable step
[
11
]
, Polyak step
[
28
]
, and many others (see Table
1
, below). The optimal convergence rate of the subgradient method is known as
O\left(N^{-1/2}\right)
, where
N
is the total number of iterations performed by the algorithm
[
4
,
12
,
19
,
24
]
. This rate was proved by using many rules of the step size. One of them is fixed (constant), namely
\gamma_{k}=O(1)/\sqrt{N}
for
k=1,\ldots,N
(with special chosen of the constant
O(1)
), which minimizes the upper bound of the difference between the best value of the objective function attained (i.e., the best value observed during the minimization process) and the optimal value. But most of the time it is preferable to use the time-varying step size since the constant step size rule requires the a priori knowledge of the total number of iterations employed by the method. From the mentioned fixed steps, the inspiration was to take
\gamma_{k}=O(1)/\sqrt{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,N
, but the convergence rate, with this rule, became sub-optimal
O\left(N^{-1/2}\log(N)\right)
. Recently in
[
38
]
, for the projected subgradient method it was proved the optimal convergence rate using the previously mentioned time-varying step size with a new weighting scheme for the generated points each iteration of the algorithm.

The next important advancement in this area is related to the development of the mirror descent method which originated in
[
22
,
23
]
and was later analyzed in
[
5
]
. It is considered as the non-Euclidean extension of standard subgradient methods. This method is used in many applications, see
[
8
,
17
,
25
,
26
,
34
]
and references therein. The standard subgradient methods employ the Euclidean distance function with a suitable step size in the projection step. Mirror descent extends the standard projected subgradient methods by employing a nonlinear distance function with an optimal step size in the nonlinear projection step
[
21
]
. The mirror descent method is also applicable to optimization problems in Banach spaces where gradient descent is not
[
14
]
. An extension of the mirror descent method for constrained problems was proposed in
[
6
,
23
]
.
Usually, the step size and stopping criterion for the mirror descent method require knowing the Lipschitz constant of the objective function and constraints, if any. Adaptive step sizes, which do not require this information, are considered for unconstrained problems in
[
7
]
, and for constrained problems in
[
6
]
.

In addition to the previously mentioned problems, there is a more general class of problems, which is non-smooth optimization problems with non-smooth functional (inequality types) constraints (see problem (
5.1
) or its equivalence (
5.2
)). This class of problems arises and attracts widespread interest in many areas of modern large-scale optimization and its applications
[
9
,
29
]
. On continuous optimization with functional constraints, there is a long history of studies. The monographs in this area include
[
10
,
27
]
. Some of the works on first-order methods for convex optimization problems with convex functional constraints include
[
3
,
16
,
20
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
36
]
for the deterministic setting and
[
1
,
2
,
18
,
37
]
for the stochastic setting.

As we pointed out the mirror descent algorithm was proposed and analyzed a long time ago for different step size rules. However, the optimal convergence rate
O(N^{-1/2})
of the algorithm was first proved, as for the subgradient method, with the fixed step size
\gamma_{k}=O(1)/\sqrt{N}
for
k=1,\ldots,N
. But, when the step sizes are time-varying, namely
\gamma_{k}=O(1)/\sqrt{k}
, it was proved the sub-optimal convergence rate
O(N^{-1/2}\log(N))
, see
[
4
,
12
,
19
,
24
]
for details. Although the constant step size rule is relatively easy to analyze it has the disadvantage of requiring the a priori knowledge of the total number of iterations employed by the method. In practical situations, the number of iterations is not fixed a priori, and stopping criteria different than merely fixing the total number of iterations is usually imposed. This is why dynamic, or time-varying (i.e., non-constant) step size rules are important
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we extend the results of
[
38
]
to the mirror descent methods. We proved the optimal convergence rate, without the presence of a logarithmic factor, for mirror descent methods with special time-varying step sizes, for solving classical constrained non-smooth problems (see Sect.
3
), constrained composite problems (see Sect.
4
), and problems with functional constraints (see Sect.
5
). We demonstrate the efficiency of the theoretical studied results by some conducted experiments for some constrained (with and without functional constraints) optimization problems, such as best approximation problem, Fermat-Torricelli-Steiner problem, smallest covering ball problem and the maximum of a finite collection of linear functions.

The paper consists of an introduction and 5 main sections. In Sect.
2
we mentioned the basic facts and tools and mirror descent basics. Sect.
3
devoted to the proof of the optimal convergence rate of the mirror descent algorithm with time-varying step size rules for solving the classical constrained non-smooth optimization problem. In Sect.
4
, the same analysis that in Sect.
3
, performed for more general optimization problems, namely the constrained composite optimization problem. Section
5
is devoted to the problem with functional constraints, which is a more general class of non-smooth optimization problems. In Sect.
6
, we present some numerical experiments which demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms and analysis.

2.
Fundamentals

Let
(\mathbf{E},\|\cdot\|)
be a normed finite-dimensional vector space, with an arbitrary norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
\mathbf{E}^{*}
be the conjugate space of
\mathbf{E}
with the following norm

\|y\|_{*}=\max\limits_{x\in\mathbf{E}}\{\langle y,x\rangle:\|x\|\leq 1\},

where
\langle y,x\rangle
is the value of the continuous linear functional
y\in\mathbf{E}^{*}
at
x\in\mathbf{E}
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbf{E}^{n}
be a compact convex set, and
\psi:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a proper closed differentiable and
\sigma
-strongly convex (called prox-function or distance generating function) with
\sigma>0
. The corresponding Bregman divergence is defined as

V_{\psi}(x,y)=\psi(x)-\psi(y)-\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

For the Bregmann divergence, it holds the following inequality

(2.1)

V_{\psi}(x,y)\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

In what follows, we denote the subdifferential of
f
at
x
by
\partial f(x)
, and the subgradient of
f
at any point
x
by
\nabla f(x)\in\partial f(x)
. Let
\operatorname{dom}(f)
denote the domain of the function
f
, and
\operatorname{dom}(\partial f)
denote the set of points of subdifferentiability of
f
, i.e.,

\operatorname{dom}(\partial f)=\left\{x\in\mathbf{E}:\partial f(x)\neq\emptyset\right\}.

The following identity, known as the three points identity, is essential in the analysis of the mirror descent method.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Three points identity)
[
13
]

Suppose that
\psi:\mathbf{E}\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is proper
closed, convex, and differentiable over
\operatorname{dom}(\partial\psi)
. Let
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}(\partial(\psi))
and
c\in\operatorname{dom}(\psi)
. Then it holds

(2.2)

\langle\nabla\psi(b)-\nabla\psi(a),c-a\rangle=V_{\psi}(c,a)+V_{\psi}(a,b)-V_{\psi}(c,b).

Fenchel-Young inequality.
For any
a\in\mathbf{E},b\in\mathbf{E}^{*}
, it holds the following inequality

(2.3)

\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{\|a\|^{2}}{2\lambda}+\frac{\lambda\|b\|_{*}^{2}}{2},\quad\forall\lambda>0.

3.
Mirror descent method for constrained problem

In this section, we consider the following constrained optimization problem

(3.1)

\min_{x\in Q}f(x),

where
f
is a convex Lipschitz continuous function (non-smooth) with Lipschitz constant
M_{f}>0
, i.e.,

(3.2)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq M_{f}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

For problem (
3.1
), we use Algorithm
1
, under consideration

(3.3)

\max_{x\in Q}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x)\leq V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})<\infty,

where
x^{1}\in Q
is a chosen (dependently on
Q
) initial point and
x^{*}
is an optimal solution of (
3.1
) nearest to
x^{1}
.

Algorithm 1

Mirror Descent Method.

0:

step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, initial point
x^{1}\in Q
s.t. (
3.3
) holds, number of iterations
N
.

1:

for

k=1,2,\ldots,N

do

2:

Calculate
\nabla f(x^{k})\in\partial f(x^{k})
,

3:

x^{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in Q}\left\{\langle x,\nabla f(x^{k})\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})\right\}
.

4:

end

for

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
be an
M_{f}
-Lipschitz convex function, and assume
\|\nabla f(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{f},\forall x\in Q
. Then for problem (
3.1
), by Algorithm
1
, with a positive non-increasing sequence of step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, for any fixed
m\geq-1
, it satisfies the following inequality

(3.4)

f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\left(\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}\right),

where
\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}x^{k}
, and
x^{*}
is an optimal solution of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{f}(x):=\langle x,\nabla f(x^{k})\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})
, then from Algorithm
1
we have
x^{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\tilde{f}(x)
. By the optimality condition, we get

\langle\nabla\tilde{f}(x^{k+1}),x-x^{k+1}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

Thus,

\langle\gamma_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})+\nabla\psi(x^{k+1})-\nabla\psi(x^{k}),x-x^{k+1}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

In particular for
x=x^{*}
, we have

(3.5)

\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k})-\nabla\psi(x^{k+1})-\gamma_{k}\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{*}-x^{k+1}\rangle\leq 0.

By using the definition of a subgradient of
f
at
x^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{k}\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k})-\nabla\psi(x^{k+1})-\gamma_{k}\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{*}-x^{k+1}\rangle}_{:=J_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k+1})-\nabla\psi(x^{k}),x^{*}-x^{k+1}\rangle}_{:=J_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\langle\gamma_{k}\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle}_{:=J_{3}}.

From (
3.5
), we find
J_{1}\leq 0
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k})-\nabla\psi(x^{k+1}),x^{*}-x^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})+V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})\right)

\displaystyle=V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k}).

By the Fenchel-Young inequality (
2.3
), with
\lambda=\sigma>0
, we find

J_{3}\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|^{2}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|^{2}.

From (
2.1
), we have

V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.

Thus, we get the following inequality

(3.6)

f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2\sigma}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}.

By multiplying both sides of (
3.6
) by
\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}
, and taking the sum from
1
to
N
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}.

Since
f
is a convex function, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[f\left(\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}x^{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right).

Therefore, by setting
\hat{x}:=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}x^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{2})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{N})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{N+1})\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{N})+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{2})-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{2})

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=3}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

Due to (
3.3
), we find that
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})\leq V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1}),\,\forall k=2,\ldots,N
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\left(-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}.

Finally, by dividing by
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}
, we get the desired inequality

f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\left(\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}\right).

∎

\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}\leq M_{f}

V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta
, then by substitution in (
3.4
) we find

f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{M_{f}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\left(\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right).

But, for any
m\geq 1
and
N\geq 1
,

\int_{0}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}dk\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}\Longrightarrow\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}\geq\frac{2\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+2}}{m+2},

and

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\leq N\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m-1}=\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1},\quad\forall m\geq 1,N\geq 1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{f}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{m+2}{2\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+2}}\left(\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{f}(m+2)(1+\theta)}{2\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

∎

Remark 3.5

In comparison with the sub-optimal convergence rate (

Let us mention the following key lemma for the analysis of the Algorithm

Lemma 4.2

[

]

Let

Theorem 4.3

From (

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-u\rangle

5.
Mirror descent method for problems with functional constraints

In this section, we focus on a more general class of problems, which is a class of optimization problems with functional constraints (inequality constraints)

and
\|\nabla g(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{g}
, for all
x\in Q
. Then for problem (
5.2
), with positive non-increasing sequences of step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}^{f}\}_{k\geq 1},\{\gamma_{k}^{g}\}_{k\geq 1}
, for any fixed
m\geq-1
, and
\varepsilon>0
, after
N
iterations of Algorithm
3
, it satisfies the following inequality

(5.3)

\displaystyle f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\Bigg(\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\varepsilon\sum_{k\in J}\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{-m}}\Bigg),

where
\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{k}
, and
x^{*}
is an optimal solution of (
5.2
).

Proof.

Similar to what was done in proving Theorem
3.1
, we find that for all
k\in I
,

(5.4)

\frac{f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m-1}},

and for all
k\in J
,

(5.5)

\frac{g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}.

By taking the summation, in each side of (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), over productive and non-productive steps, with
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{f}
if
k\in I
and
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{g}
if
k\in J
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right)+\sum_{k\in J}\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{-m}\left(g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}.

Also, in a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, and since for any
k\in J
, it holds

(5.6)

g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})\geq g(x^{k})>\varepsilon>0,

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\varepsilon\sum_{k\in J}\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{-m}}.

Since
f
is a convex function, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)

\displaystyle\left[f\left(\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}\left(f(x^{k})-f(x^{*})\right).

Therefore, by setting
\hat{x}:=\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{k}
, we get

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\varepsilon\sum_{k\in J}\left(\gamma_{k}^{g}\right)^{-m}}.

By dividing both sides of (
5.7
) by
\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}
, we get the desired inequality (
5.3
).

Now, let us show that
|I|\neq 0
. For this, let us assume that
|I|=0
, therefore
|J|=N
, i.e., all steps are non-productive.
From (
5.6
), we get

(5.8)

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}>\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}=\frac{\varepsilon M^{m}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m},

and for all
k\in J=\{1,\ldots,N\}
with the assumption that
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta
for some
\theta>0
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{m+1}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m+1}}\left(\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right).

But, one can numerically verify that for a sufficiently big number of iterations
N
(dependently on suitable values of the parameters
\theta>0,m\geq-1,M>0,\varepsilon>0,\sigma>0
), the following inequality holds

(5.9)

\frac{M^{m+1}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m+1}}\left(\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon M^{m}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}.

Therefore, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}<\frac{\varepsilon M^{m}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}.

So, we have a contradiction with (
5.8
). This means that
|I|\neq 0
.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the reverse inequality of (
5.9
), i.e.,

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}\leq\frac{M}{\varepsilon\sqrt{2\sigma}}\left(\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right),

for any
m\geq-1,M>0,\theta>0,\sigma>0
and
\varepsilon\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}
, holds for at least
N=1
. This means that by choosing
\varepsilon\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\,(\forall M>,\sigma>0)
, we have at least one productive step for any
m\geq-1
and
\theta>0
.

Remark 5.3

(Stopping criterion for Algorithm
3
)
.

Let
x^{1}\in Q
be an initial point of Algorithm
3
, such that
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1}
for some
\theta_{1}>0
. From inequality (
5.7
) (which is equivalent to the inequality (
5.3
)), with
\gamma_{q}=\gamma_{q}^{f}
if
q\in I
and
\gamma_{q}=\gamma_{q}^{g}
if
q\in J
, with

(5.10)

\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{i},

for any
k\geq 1
, we find

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)<\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{i}\right)^{-m}+\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}-\Bigg(\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{i}\right)^{-m}-\frac{\theta_{1}}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g}\right)^{m-1}}\Bigg).

From this, without relying on prior knowledge of the number of iterations that the algorithm performs, we can set for any
k\geq 1
,

(5.11)

\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}^{m}}\geq\frac{\theta_{1}}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g}\right)^{m-1}},

as a stopping criterion of Algorithm
3
. As a result, we conclude

\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)<\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m},

i.e.,
f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\leq\varepsilon
.

For all
i\in I
it holds that
g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon
, and since
g
is convex, then we have

g(\hat{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon.

Thus after the stopping criterion (
5.11
) is met we find that
\hat{x}
, which is given in (
5.10
), represents an
\varepsilon
-solution to problem (
5.2
).

Now let us analyze the convergence of Algorithm
3
, by taking the following time-varying step size rules

(5.12)

\gamma_{k}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{k}^{f}:=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{M_{f}\sqrt{k}},\quad\text{if}\;k\in I,\\
\gamma_{k}^{g}:=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{M_{g}\sqrt{k}},\quad\text{if}\;k\in J.\end{cases}

Let us assume that
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1},
for some
\theta_{1}>0
,
M:=\max\{M_{f},M_{g}\}
. By using (
5.12
), and since
\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}\leq M_{f}\leq M
and
\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}\leq M_{g}\leq M
, then for any
m\geq 1
, from Theorem
5.1
we have

\displaystyle f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle<\frac{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m}}{M^{m}\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\Bigg(\frac{\theta_{1}M^{m+1}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m+1}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{M^{m+1}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m-1}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\cdot\left(\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\cdot\left(\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+N\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}.

Now, by setting
\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\leq\varepsilon
and since
|I|\leq N
, we get

\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}N\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,

\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})}{\sqrt{N}\sqrt{2\sigma}}\leq\varepsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\geq\frac{M^{2}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}.

Hence, we can formulate the following result.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
f
and
g
are Lipschitz convex functions with constants
M_{f}>0
and
M_{g}>0
, respectively. Assume that
\|\nabla f(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{f}

, and
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1},
for some
\theta_{1}>0
, and let
\varepsilon>0,M:=\max\{M_{f},M_{g}\}
. Then after

(5.13)

N=\left\lceil\frac{M^{2}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rceil=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

iterations of Algorithm
3
, for any fixed
m\geq 1
, with step size rules given in (
5.12
), it satisfies

f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})<\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad g(\hat{x})\leq\varepsilon,

where
\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum\limits_{k\in I}\left(\gamma_{k}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{k}
.

The estimate (
5.13
), is optimal for the class of non-smooth optimization problems under consideration.

Now, by setting
m=0
in (
5.3
), with
\overline{x}=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{k\in I}x^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle f(\overline{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{|I|}\left(\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})}{\gamma_{N}}+\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}\gamma_{k}^{f}}{2\sigma}+\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}\gamma_{k}^{g}}{2\sigma}\right),

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\left(\frac{M\theta_{1}\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}+\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}+\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{|I|\sqrt{2\sigma}}\left(\theta_{1}\sqrt{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\leq\frac{M}{|I|\sqrt{2\sigma}}\left(\theta_{1}\sqrt{N}+2\sqrt{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M\sqrt{N}(2+\theta_{1})}{|I|\sqrt{2\sigma}}.

Now, by setting
\frac{M\sqrt{N}(2+\theta_{1})}{|I|\sqrt{2\sigma}}\leq\varepsilon
and since
|I|\leq N
, we get

\frac{M(2+\theta_{1})}{\sqrt{N}\sqrt{2\sigma}}\leq\varepsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\geq\frac{M^{2}(2+\theta_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}.

Hence, for
m=0
, we can formulate the following result.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
f
and
g
are Lipschitz convex functions with constants
M_{f}>0
and
M_{g}>0
, respectively. Assume that
\|\nabla f(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{f}

\|\nabla g(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{g}

V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1},
for some

\varepsilon>0,M:=\max\{M_{f},M_{g}\}

iterations of Algorithm

, with

, and step size rules given in (

f(\overline{x})-f(x^{*})<\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad g(\overline{x})\leq\varepsilon,

.

Remark 5.6
.

By setting
m=-1
in (
5.3
), we deduce the sub-optimal convergence rate for Algorithm
3
. In this case, with
\tilde{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}x^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\tilde{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\sqrt{k}}\cdot\left(\theta_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\right)

5.1.
Modification of mirror descent method for problems with many functional constraints

For solving problem (
5.1
), when we have several functional constraints, we can modify Algorithm
3
in a similar way as in
[
31
]
. When we have a non-productive step
k
, i.e., it holds that
g(x^{k})>\varepsilon
, then instead of calculating a (sub)gradient of the functional constraint with max-type
g(x)=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\{g_{i}(x)\}
, we calculate a (sub)gradient of one functional
g_{i}
, for which we have
g_{i}(x^{k})>\varepsilon
. This idea of the proposed modification allows for saving the running time of the algorithm due to consideration of not all functional constraints (when their number is big) on non-productive steps. The proposed modification is listed as Algorithm
4
below.

Algorithm 4

Modification of mirror descent method for problems with many functional constraints.

0:

\varepsilon>0
, initial point
x^{1}\in Q
,
M=\max\{M_{f},M_{g}\}
,
\theta_{1}>0
such that
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1}
,
m\geq-1
,
\sigma>0
.

1:

I\longrightarrow\emptyset,J\longrightarrow\emptyset.

2:

Set
k=0

3:

repeat

4:

if

g_{i}(x^{k})\leq\varepsilon,\;\forall i=1,2,\ldots,p

then

5:

L_{k}=\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*},

6:

\gamma_{k}^{f}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{L_{k}\sqrt{k}},

7:

x^{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in Q}\left\{\langle x,\nabla f(x^{k})\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{f}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})\right\},

8:

k\longrightarrow I
”productive step”

9:

else

10:

(i.e.,
\exists q=q(k)\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
, s.t.,
g_{q(k)}>\varepsilon
)

11:

L_{k}=\|\nabla g_{q(k)}(x^{k})\|_{*},

12:

\gamma_{k}^{g_{q(k)}}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{L_{k}\sqrt{k}},

13:

x^{k+1}=\arg\min_{x\in Q}\left\{\langle x,\nabla g_{q(k)}(x^{k})\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{g_{q(k)}}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})\right\},

14:

k\longrightarrow J
”non-productive step”

15:

end

if

16:

until

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{L_{i}\sqrt{i}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m}\geq\theta_{1}\left(\frac{M\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m+1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m+1}}\Bigg(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\sqrt{i}\right)^{m-1}\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{m+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j\in J}\left(\sqrt{j}\right)^{m-1}\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{m+1}\Bigg).

16:

\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum\limits_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{i}.

For Algorithm
4
, we have the following result.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
f
and
g_{i}\,(i=1,2,\ldots,p)
are Lipschitz convex functions with constants
M_{f}>0
and
M_{g_{i}}>0\,(i=1,2,\ldots,p)
, respectively. Assume that, for all
x\in Q
, we have
\|\nabla f(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{f}
and
\|\nabla g_{i}(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{g}\,\forall i=1,2,\ldots,p
, where
M_{g}=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\{M_{g_{i}}\}
, and
V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{1})\leq\theta_{1}
, for some
\theta_{1}>0
, and let
\varepsilon>0
,
M=\max\{M_{f},M_{g}\}
. Then for problem (
5.1
), after stopping Algorithm
4
, for any fixed
m\geq-1
, it satisfies the following inequalities

(5.14)

f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})<\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}(\hat{x})\leq\varepsilon,\,\forall i=1,\ldots,p.

where
\hat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum\limits_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}x^{i}
, and
x^{*}
is an optimal solution of (
5.1
).

Proof.

Similar to what was done in proving Theorem
5.1
, we find that for all
i\in I
,

(5.15)

\frac{f(x^{i})-f(x^{*})}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{i})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{i+1})}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}},

and for all
j\in J
, there is
q(j)\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
, such that

(5.16)

\frac{g(x^{j})-g(x^{*})}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{j})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{j+1})}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m-1}}.

By taking the summation, in each side of (
5.15
) and (
5.16
), over productive and non-productive steps, with
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i}^{f}
if
i\in I
and
\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}
if
i\in J
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\left(f(x^{i})-f(x^{*})\right)+\sum_{j\in J}\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{-m}\left(g_{q(j)}(x^{j})-g_{q(j)}(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{i})-V_{\psi}(x^{*},x^{i+1})\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m-1}}.

Since for any
j\in J
, it holds

g_{q(j)}(x^{j})-g_{q(j)}(x^{*})\geq g_{q(j)}(x^{j})>\varepsilon>0,

and since
f
is a convex function, we get

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{\theta_{1}}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m-1}}-\varepsilon\sum_{j\in J}\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{-m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{1}}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{\left(\gamma_{j}^{g_{q(j)}}\right)^{m-1}}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\gamma_{i})^{-m}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}.

But
\gamma_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{*}\sqrt{k}}
or
\gamma_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{\|\nabla g_{q(k)}(x^{k})\|_{*}\sqrt{k}}
and in both cases we have
(\gamma_{k})^{m+1}\geq\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{M\sqrt{k}}\right)^{m+1}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}+\theta_{1}\left(\frac{M\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m+1}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}\sqrt{i}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}\sqrt{j}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m-1}-\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{L_{i}\sqrt{i}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\quad=\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}-\Bigg(\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{L_{i}\sqrt{i}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m}-\theta_{1}\left(\frac{M\sqrt{k}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\sigma})^{m+1}}\sum_{i\in I}(\sqrt{i})^{m-1}\|\nabla f(x^{i})\|_{*}^{m+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\sigma})^{m+1}}\sum_{j\in J}(\sqrt{j})^{m-1}\|\nabla g_{q(j)}(x^{j})\|_{*}^{m+1}\Bigg).

Therefore, from stopping criterion of Algorithm
4
, we get

\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}\right)\left(f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})\right)<\varepsilon\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}.

Thus, by dividing both sides of the previous inequality by
\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}
, we get the desired inequality
f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})<\varepsilon
.

For all
i\in I
it holds that
g_{s}(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon\,\forall s\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
, and since
g_{s}\,(\forall s\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\})
are convex functions, then we have

g_{s}(\hat{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}g_{s}(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon,\;\forall s\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\}.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

For any
m\geq 1
, let us assume that Algorithm
4
works
N
iterations. Then we have

\displaystyle\qquad f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle<\frac{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m}}{M^{m}\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\Bigg(\theta_{1}\left(\frac{M\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\right)^{m+1}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{M^{m+1}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}}{\left(\sqrt{2\sigma}\right)^{m-1}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\cdot\left(\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\cdot\left(\theta_{1}\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}+N\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}.

Now, by setting
\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\leq\varepsilon
and since
|I|\leq N
, we get

\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}N\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m}}\leq\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})\left(\sqrt{N}\right)^{m+1}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}\left(\sqrt{k}\right)^{m}}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,

\frac{M(1+\theta_{1})}{\sqrt{N}\sqrt{2\sigma}}\leq\varepsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\geq\frac{M^{2}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}.

Therefore, we find that after

N=\left\lceil\frac{M^{2}(1+\theta_{1})^{2}}{2\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rceil=O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

iterations of Algorithm
4
, for any fixed
m\geq 1
, it satisfy
f(\hat{x})-f(x^{*})<\varepsilon
and
g_{i}(\hat{x})\leq\varepsilon\,\forall i\in\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
.

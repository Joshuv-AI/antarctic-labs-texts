Laura Casabella, Teresa Yu

Abstract
. Over a field of characteristic two, we develop a theory of standard monomials for polynomial rings modulo a Frobenius power of the maximal ideal generated by all variables. As a result, we obtain a filtration by modular 𝐆𝐋 n \mathbf{GL}_{n} -representations whose characters are given by particular truncated Schur polynomials, thus proving a conjecture by Gao–Raicu–VandeBogert in the characteristic two case.



1.
Introduction

There has been recent interest in studying positive characteristic analogues of classical results on flag varieties in characteristic zero, such as the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem on the cohomology of line bundles on the complete flag variety (see
[
4
]
for a survey). The study of flag varieties is connected to commutative algebra, representation theory, and combinatorics through standard monomial theory and determinantal ideals
[
1
]
. In this paper, we provide results towards developing an analogue of standard monomial theory in positive characteristic by considering determinantal ideals “modulo Frobenius”.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field (for now, of arbitrary characteristic), and let
R=\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with bigrading
\deg(x_{i})=(1,0),\deg(y_{i})=(0,1)
. Let
I\subset R
be the bihomogeneous ideal generated by the
2\times 2
minors of the generic matrix

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{n}\\
y_{1}&y_{2}&\cdots&y_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Then,
R
has a
\mathbf{k}
-basis consisting of products of minors of the matrix, and this basis is indexed by semistandard Young tableaux (SSYT) with entries in
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
; the basis elements are called
standard monomials
. In particular, for
a\geq b\geq d
, the
\mathbf{k}
-vector space
(I^{d}/I^{d+1})_{(a,b)}
has a basis indexed by semistandard Young tableaux of shape
(a+b-d,d)
. This basis and its properties are useful for studying many aspects of
I
and
R/I

[
1
, Ch. 3]
. For example, this
\mathbf{k}
-vector space has an action of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
, and since standard monomials are weight vectors, the character of this representation is

(1)

\left[(I^{d}/I^{d+1)})_{(a,b)}\right]=s_{(a+b-d,d)},

where
s_{(a+b-d,d)}
is the Schur polynomial in
n
variables indexed by the partition
(a+b-d,d)
.

When
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=p>0
, one can work modulo a Frobenius power of the ideal generated by the variables of
R
, and consider the quotient ring

\overline{R}=R/(x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p},y_{1}^{p},\ldots,y_{n}^{p}),

and the ideal
\overline{I}=I\overline{R}
. In
[
2
,
7
]
, the authors give modular analogues of Schur polynomials. The
p
-truncated complete symmetric polynomials

h_{d}^{(p)}\in\mathbf{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
are defined by

h_{d}^{(p)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=d\\
0\leq i_{j}<p\end{subarray}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{i_{n}},

and the
p
-truncated Schur polynomials

s^{(p)}_{(a,b)}\in\mathbf{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
are defined by

s^{(p)}_{(a,b)}=\det\begin{pmatrix}h^{(p)}_{a}&h^{(p)}_{a+1}\\
h^{(p)}_{b-1}&h^{(p)}_{b}\end{pmatrix}.

It is natural to ask whether the analogue of (
1
) holds for
p
-truncated Schur polynomials. Although one can find immediate counterexamples (see for instance
[
4
, Section 6]
), in
[
4
, Conjecture 6.1]
the authors conjecture that the analogue does hold for
a-b\geq p-1
. In this paper, we give a complete answer to the question in the
p=2
case by finding the character of
(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1})_{(a,b)}
for all
a\geq b\geq d
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
p=2
, and
a\geq b\geq d
. Then,

\left[\left(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}\right)_{(a,b)}\right]=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}&\quad\text{if $a=b=d$},\\
\\
s^{(2)}_{(a,a)}+\sum_{j=2}^{a}(-1)^{j}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}&\quad\text{if $a-1=b-1=d$},\\
\\
s^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Our overall proof strategy is to find a basis indexed by a class of tableaux introduced in
[
4
]
, called
2
-semistandard Young tableaux
; such tableaux of shape
(a,b)
index the monomials appearing in
s^{(2)}_{(a,b)}
. This basis will be obtained by taking the images of particular classical standard monomials in
\overline{R}
, and this ensures that the elements are in
\overline{I}^{d}
for an appropriate
d
. We then use tableaux combinatorics to show that the images of all classical standard monomials are in the span of the particular subset taken to form our basis, and this will be sufficient to obtain the character formulas.

1.1.
Modular representation theory of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}

If
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
, then the representation theory of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
over
\mathbf{k}
is very well-understood: every simple polynomial representation is given by
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
, where
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(-)
is the Schur functor corresponding to a partition
\lambda
with at most
n
rows. The highest weight of this simple module is
\lambda
, and its character is the Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}
in
n
variables.

If
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=p>0
, then representations of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
are much more difficult to understand. Let
F^{p}\subset\text{Sym}^{p}
denote the
Frobenius power functor
. Then the
truncated symmetric power functor
is given by

T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{d}(\mathbf{k}^{n})=\text{coker}\,\left(F^{p}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\otimes\text{Sym}^{d-p}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\to\text{Sym}^{d}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\right).

Doty–Walker conjectured that the characters of all simple
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representations could be understood via tensor products of representations of the form
T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{d}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
, leading to the study of
p
-truncated Schur polynomials
[
2
,
7
]
. More recently, generalizations of the
h^{(p)}_{d}
polynomials have been studied in the context of symmetric function theory
[
3
,
6
]
. The
p
-truncated Schur polynomials are virtual
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-characters, and
[
4
, Conjecture 6.1]
predicts a particular
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representation realizing some of them as actual characters.

As
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representations, we have that

\overline{R}_{(a,b)}=T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{a}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\otimes T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{b}(\mathbf{k}^{n})

with character
h^{(p)}_{a}\cdot h^{(p)}_{b}
. Our main result shows that for
p=2
and
a>b
,
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
has a filtration by
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representations with characters
s^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}
for
0\leq d\leq b
.

A related open question concerns which simple
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representations can be realized as a composition factor of tensor products of truncated symmetric powers. As in the characteristic zero case, simple modules are indexed by partitions
\lambda
corresponding to the highest weight of the module. A partition
\lambda
is called
p
-restricted
if
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}\leq p-1
for all
i
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Conjecture 6.4,
[
4
]
)
.

If
a-b\geq p-1
, then every composition factor
L(\lambda)
of
T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{a}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\otimes T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{b}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
is
p
-restricted.

For
p=2
, this conjecture is a straightforward consequence of Pieri’s rule; it is provided in §
5.1
.

-basis for

(1)

(2)

a=b=d

(3)

In case (1), we will see that the basis is indexed by

3.1.

3.4.
2-straight tableaux

In the previous subsection, we defined the sets
\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}\subset\overline{I}^{d}_{(a,b)}
. They consist of images of classical standard monomials in
\overline{R}
.

Definition 3.4
.

The elements of
\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}
for any
a\geq b\geq d
are called
2
-standard monomials
.

Our goal is now to show that the images of all classical standard monomials are in the span of
2
-standard monomials. We do this by analyzing semistandard tableaux.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
a\geq b\geq d
. We say a semistandard tableau
T\in\Tab_{(a+b-d,d)}
is
2
-straight
with respect to
(a,b,d)
if it is in the image of
\Phi_{a,b,d}
.

We say
T

can be
2
-straightened
with respect to
(a,b,d)
if there exist some
m\geq 0
and
2
-straight tableaux
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\in\Tab_{(a+b-d,d)}
such that
\overline{F}_{T,a}
can be written modulo
\overline{I}^{d+1}
as

\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{F}_{T_{i},a}\in\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}.

The case where
m=0
means that
\overline{F}_{T,a}\in\overline{I}^{d+1}
, and this includes if
\overline{F}_{T,a}=0
in
\overline{R}
.

In what follows, we characterize
2
-straight tableaux. In §
4
, we will use this characterization and the
2
-straightening rules to show that all semistandard tableaux can be
2
-straightened.

3.4.1.

We characterize semistandard tableaux of shape
(a,a)

be given by the product of minors

Proposition 3.6
.

A semistandard tableau

is
2
-straight with respect to
(a,a,a)
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

An index
i
does not appear more than two times in

, and
\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.

(2)

If
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}
for some

, then
\beta_{i-1}<\alpha_{i}
.

(3)

If
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}
for some
i=1,\ldots,a-1

\beta_{j}<\alpha_{j+2}
for

if such a
j

j
does not exist, then
\beta_{a-1}<\beta_{a}
.

(4)

The second row is strictly increasing except possibly in the last column:

\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\cdots<\beta_{a-2}<\beta_{a-1}\leq\beta_{a}.

(5)

If
\beta_{a-1}=\beta_{a}

\beta_{j-2}<\alpha_{j}
for

\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
.

4.2.
Induction step for
a-1=b=d
case

We now consider the case where
a-1=b=d
. The arguments are similar to those of the previous case with
a=b=d
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
a\geq 2
, and
T\in\Tab_{(a,a-1)}
. Then
\overline{F}_{T,a}
can be written modulo
\overline{I}^{d+1}
as

\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{F}_{T_{i},a},

where
T_{i}\in\Tab_{(a,a)}
and
T_{i}^{(d)}\in\Tab_{(a,a)}
is
2
-straight.

Proof.

This follows as for the proof of Lemma
4.2
. Each time classical straightening must be applied, the index in the
a^{\text{th}}
column strictly increases, and so the process of straightening and
2
-straightening must eventually terminate.
∎

It therefore suffices to show that if
T\in\Tab_{(a,a-1)}
with
T^{(a-1)}
already
2
-straight, then
T
can be
2
-straightened. We further suppose that no index
i
appears more than twice in
T
. If
T
is not already
2
-straight, then we have the following cases to consider:

(1)

\alpha_{a-1}=\alpha_{a}
;

(2)

\beta_{a-2}=\beta_{a-1}
;

(3)

\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}
for some
i\leq a-2
, and
\beta_{j}=\alpha_{j+2}
for
j=i,\ldots,a-3
.

In each of these cases, one can adapt the proofs from the
a=b=d
case (Lemmas
4.3
,
4.4
,
4.5
). The differences are that the box corresponding to
\beta_{a}
is removed, and one needs to show that the resulting tableaux all have strictly increasing bottom rows, which holds in all of the cases.

This proves Theorem
4.1
in the case of
a-1=b=d
.

4.3.
Induction step for other cases

We now consider all other cases of
a\geq b\geq d
. In particular, if
a>b
, then
a-d\geq 2
. The arguments from the previous subsection can be applied here as well to show that if conditions (1), (2), or (4) do not hold, then a semistandard tableau can be
2
-straightened.

The following lemmas (Lemma
4.9
and
4.10
) show that if condition (5) does not hold for a semistandard tableau
T
, then
\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv 0
in
\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose
a>b\geq d
with
a-d\geq 2
, or
a-2=b-2\geq d
. If
T\in\Tab_{(a+b-d,d)}
with
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}
for some
i\geq d+1
, then
\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv 0
in
\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}
.

Proof.

If
i\in\{d+1,\ldots,a-1\}
, then
x_{\alpha_{i}}^{2}
divides
F_{T,a}
, so its image is zero in
\overline{R}
. Similarly, if
i\in\{a+1,\ldots,a+b-d-1\}
, then
y_{\alpha_{i}}^{2}
divides
F_{T,a}
.

Now suppose
\alpha_{a}=\alpha_{a+1}
. Then the image of

x_{\alpha_{d+1}}x_{\alpha_{d+2}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{a}}y_{\alpha_{a}}y_{\alpha_{a+2}}\cdots y_{\alpha_{a+b-d}}

is equal to the image of

(x_{\alpha_{d+1}}y_{\alpha_{a}}+x_{\alpha_{a}}y_{\alpha_{d+1}})x_{\alpha_{d+2}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{a}}y_{\alpha_{a+2}}\cdots y_{\alpha_{a+b-d}}.

This latter element is in
I
, since
d+1<a
and so
[\alpha_{d+1},\alpha_{a}]
is indeed a
2
-minor. Thus,
\overline{F}_{T,a}=\overline{F}_{T^{\prime},a+1}
, where
T^{\prime}
is obtained from
T
by moving a box containing
\alpha_{a}
to the end of the second row.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose
a-1=b-1=d
. If
T\in\Tab_{(a+1,a-1)}
with
\alpha_{a}=\alpha_{a+1}
, then
\overline{F}_{T,a}\in\overline{I}^{d+1}
.

Proof.

We have that

[\alpha_{a-1},\beta_{a-1}]x_{\alpha_{a}}y_{\alpha_{a}}=[\alpha_{a-1},\alpha_{a}][\beta_{a-1},\alpha_{a}].

Thus, if
S
is the tableau of shape
(a,a)
given by

S=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{a-2}\,\,\hbox{\vtop{\halign{&\opttoksa@YT={\font@YT}\getcolor@YT{\save@YT{\opttoksb@YT}}\nil@YT\getcolor@YT{\startbox@@YT\the\opttoksa@YT\the\opttoksb@YT}#\endbox@YT\cr\lower 0.39993pt\vbox{\kern 0.19997pt\hbox{\kern 0.39993pt\vbox to20.39996pt{\vss\hbox to20.00003pt{\hss$\alpha_{a-1}$\hss}\vss}\kern-20.39996pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt}\kern-0.19997pt\kern-20.39996pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt}&\lower 0.39993pt\vbox{\kern 0.19997pt\hbox{\kern 0.39993pt\vbox to20.39996pt{\vss\hbox to20.00003pt{\hss$\beta_{a-1}$\hss}\vss}\kern-20.39996pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt}\kern-0.19997pt\kern-20.39996pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt}\cr\lower 0.39993pt\vbox{\kern 0.19997pt\hbox{\kern 0.39993pt\vbox to20.39996pt{\vss\hbox to20.00003pt{\hss$\alpha_{a}$\hss}\vss}\kern-20.39996pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt}\kern-0.19997pt\kern-20.39996pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt}&\lower 0.39993pt\vbox{\kern 0.19997pt\hbox{\kern 0.39993pt\vbox to20.39996pt{\vss\hbox to20.00003pt{\hss$\alpha_{a}$\hss}\vss}\kern-20.39996pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\vrule height=20.39996pt,width=0.39993pt}\kern-0.19997pt\kern-20.39996pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt\kern 20.00003pt\hrule width=20.7999pt,height=0.39993pt}\crcr}}\kern 690.4pt}

then we have

\overline{F}_{T,a}=\overline{F}_{S,a}\in\overline{I}^{d+1}.

∎

), then the tableau can be

Lemma 4.11
.

Let

and
\gamma\neq\delta\in[n]

\gamma

\beta_{m}

\overline{F}_{T,m+2}\in\overline{I}^{m+2}.

\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv 0
in
\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\Tab_{(m+2,m+2)}
be given by

\alpha

\beta_{m}

\beta_{1}

\delta

,

\overline{F}_{T,m+2}=\overline{F}_{S,m+1}=[\gamma,\delta]x_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{\beta_{i}}y_{\beta_{i}}.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let

, and let

be given by

\alpha

\delta

\beta_{1}

\gamma

Then

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{T,m+3}

\displaystyle=x_{\gamma}x_{\delta}\left(x_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{\beta_{i}}y_{\beta_{i}}\right),

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose
a-d\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
. Let

with

•

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
;

•

\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i+2}
for

;

•

rows are strictly increasing.

Then
T
can be
2
-straightened.

Proof.

If
\beta_{d}<\alpha_{d+2}
, then

is already
2
-straight. If
\beta_{d}=\alpha_{d+2}
, then
\overline{F}_{T,a}=0
.

Now suppose
\beta_{d}>\alpha_{d+2}
. We have the following four cases:

(1)

\beta_{d}<\alpha_{a+1}
, with
\beta_{d}\neq\alpha_{i}
for all

;

(2)

\beta_{d}>\alpha_{a+1}
, with
\beta_{d}\neq\alpha_{i}
for all

;

(3)

\beta_{d}\leq\alpha_{a}
, with
\beta_{d}=\alpha_{i}
for some

;

(4)

\beta_{d}\geq\alpha_{a+1}
, with
\beta_{d}=\alpha_{i}
for some

: Let

be the tableau obtained from

by removing the box with
\alpha_{d+2}
from the first row, replacing
\beta_{d}
with
\alpha_{d+2}
in the second row, and putting a box with
\beta_{d}
in the first row so that the first row is increasing. Then

is
2
-straight of the same shape, as we have that
\alpha_{d+2}<\alpha_{d+3}
, and we have

\overline{F}_{T,a}=\overline{F}_{T^{\prime},a}.

Case (2)
: Let
S
be the tableau of shape
(a+b-d-1,d+1)
obtained from
T
by moving the box with index
\alpha_{a+1}
to the end of the second row. Let

from the first row, replacing

\beta_{d}
in first row so that the first row remains increasing; in particular, the last

2

\overline{F}_{T,a}+\overline{F}_{T^{\prime},a}=\overline{F}_{S,a}.

\overline{F}_{S,a}\in\overline{I}^{d+1}
and
\overline{F}_{T^{\prime},a}\in\overline{I}^{d}

\overline{F}_{T,a}\equiv\overline{F}_{T^{\prime},a}
in
\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}

2
-straightened.

Case (3)

4.12

, we have that
\overline{F}_{T,a}
is divisible by

, and so it is equal to

in
\overline{R}
.

: Let

and

from the first row (recall that

Since

Combining the above results, we have proved Theorem

j
where
\beta_{j}\neq\alpha_{j+2i}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a\geq 1
and
0\leq i\leq a-1
. Then there is a weight-preserving bijection between the sets

\mathcal{D}_{a,i}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{a,i+1}.

Proof.

We first give a weight-preserving map
\Phi:\mathcal{D}_{a,i}\to\mathcal{C}_{a,i+1}
. Let
T\in\mathcal{D}_{a,i}
, with
j
the maximal index where
\beta_{j}\neq\alpha_{j+2i}
(so
\beta_{j}>\alpha_{j+2i}
). If
j=a-i
, then move the box corresponding to
\beta_{j}
to the end of the first row. Else, swap the boxes

\underbrace{\alpha_{j+2i+1},\ldots,\alpha_{a+i}}_{\text{$a-i-j$ boxes}}\leftrightarrow\underbrace{\beta_{j},\ldots,\beta_{a-i}}_{\text{$a-i-j+1$ boxes}}.

Then
\Phi(T)\in\mathcal{C}_{a,i+1}
.

We now give an inverse map
\Psi:\mathcal{C}_{a,i+1}\to\mathcal{D}_{a,i}
. Let
T\in\mathcal{C}_{a,i+1}
. If
\beta_{j}=\alpha_{j+2i}
for
j=1,\ldots,a-i
, then swap the boxes

\underbrace{\alpha_{2i},\ldots,\alpha_{a+i}}_{\text{$a-i+1$ boxes}}\leftrightarrow\underbrace{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{a-i}}_{\text{$a-i$ boxes}}.

Else, let
j
be the maximal index where
\beta_{j}\neq\alpha_{j+2i}
(so
\beta_{j}<\alpha_{j+2i}
), and swap the boxes

\underbrace{\alpha_{j+2i},\ldots,\alpha_{a+i}}_{\text{$a-i-j+1$ boxes}}\leftrightarrow\underbrace{\beta_{j+1},\ldots,\beta_{a-i}}_{\text{$a-i-j$ boxes}}.

Then
\Psi(T)\in\mathcal{C}_{a,i}
. One can check that
\Phi,\Psi
are inverse to each other.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

For
a\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{D}
denote the set of
2
-semistandard tableaux of size
(a,a)
that do not have identical rows. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{D}}t^{T}.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{D}
is exactly equal to
\mathcal{D}_{a,0}
, so
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{D}}t^{T}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{C}_{a,1}}t^{T}
by Lemma
5.2
. For any
0\leq i\leq a
, we have

s^{(2)}_{(a+i,a-i)}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{D}_{a,i}}t^{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{C}_{a,i}}t^{T}.

In addition,

s^{(2)}_{2a,0}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{C}_{a,a}}t^{T}.

Combining these statements with Lemma
5.2
gives the desired result.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
a\geq b\geq d
. Then,

\left[\left(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}\right)_{(a,b)}\right]=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}&\quad\text{if $a=b=d$},\\
\\
s^{(2)}_{(a,a)}+\sum_{j=2}^{a}(-1)^{j}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}&\quad\text{if $a-1=b-1=d$},\\
\\
s^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
a\geq b
, we have that
[\overline{R}_{(a,b)}]=\sum_{d=0}^{b}s^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}
.
By Proposition
2.3
, this is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{b}\left(\sum_{T\in\Tab^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}}t^{T}\right).

By Theorem
4.1
,
\mathcal{B}_{a,b}=\bigcup_{d=0}^{b}\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}
spans
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
. Then,

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(\overline{R}_{(a,b)})\leq\#\mathcal{B}_{a,b}\leq\#\left(\bigcup_{d=0}^{b}\Tab^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}\right)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(\overline{R}_{(a,b)}),

and so we have that
\mathcal{B}_{a,b}
is indeed a
\mathbf{k}
-basis for
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
. By construction,
\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}\subset\overline{I}^{d}_{(a,b)}
. Thus, by downwards induction on
d
, we have that the residue classes of elements of
\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}
are a
\mathbf{k}
-basis for
(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1})_{(a,b)}
.
For any
2
-semistandard tableau
T
, the basis element
G_{T,a}
is a weight vector with
t^{\text{wt}(G_{T,a})}=t^{T}
. Then, if
a>b
or
a-2=b-2\geq d
, we have

\left[\left(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1}\right)_{(a,b)}\right]=\sum_{G_{T,a}\in\mathcal{B}_{a,b,d}}t^{\text{wt}(G_{T,a})}=\sum_{T\in\Tab^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}}t^{T}=s^{(2)}_{(a+b-d,d)}.

By Proposition
5.3
, we have the desired character formula for the
a=b=d
case. Finally, for the
a-1=b-1=d
case, we see that the character is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{G_{T,a}\in\mathcal{B}_{a,a,a-1}}t^{\text{wt}(G_{T,a})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\Tab^{(2)}_{(a+1,a-1)}}t^{T}+\sum_{T\in\left(\Tab^{(2)}_{(a,a)}\setminus\mathcal{D}\right)}t^{T}

\displaystyle=s^{(2)}_{(a+1,a-1)}+s^{(2)}_{(a,a)}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{a}(-1)^{j-1}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}\right)

\displaystyle=s^{(2)}_{(a,a)}+\left(\sum_{j=2}^{a}(-1)^{j}s^{(2)}_{(a+j,a-j)}\right).

∎

5.1.
Simple modular representations

A direction of future work is to try to find the simple composition factors of
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
in the
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=2
case. For
a>b
, the following proof shows that if
L(\mu)
is a simple composition factor of
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
, then it must be
2
-restricted, i.e.,
\mu
is a partition consisting of two columns with different lengths or is a single column.

Proof of Conjecture
1.2
for
p=2
.

In characteristic 2, we have that
T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{a}(-)
is the Schur functor
\textbf{S}_{(1^{a})}(-)
. Then by Pieri’s rule,
T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{a}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\otimes T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{b}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
has a filtration with graded pieces
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
, where
\lambda
is a partition with at most two columns. In particular, every
\lambda
appearing in the filtration has the form
(a+i,b-i)^{\dagger}
, where
0\leq i\leq b
. Since
a-b\geq 1
, each
\lambda
appearing can never have two columns of the same length.

For a given
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
appearing in the filtration, any simple composition factor
L(\mu)
of
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
must be such that
\mu\leq\lambda
under the dominance order, as
\lambda
is the highest weight occurring in
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
. This implies that
\mu
must also have at most two columns, and they cannot have the same length. Therefore, any
\mu
for which
L(\mu)
appears as a composition factor of
T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{a}(\mathbf{k}^{n})\otimes T_{p}\,\text{Sym}^{b}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
must be
2
-restricted.
∎

By
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
,
s^{(2)}_{(a,b)}
is the character of the simple
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representation
L((a,b)^{\dagger})
if
(a,b)
is
Carter
, meaning the highest power of
2
dividing the hook-length is constant down each column. Walker further showed that if
a>b
and
(a,b)
is not Carter, then
s^{(2)}_{(a,b)}
is not the character of a simple
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
-representation for
n\gg 0
. Thus, for many
a\geq b\geq d
, one knows that
(\overline{I}^{d}/\overline{I}^{d+1})_{(a,b)}
is not simple.

5.2.
Exterior algebras

Cauchy’s identity states that the exterior algebra
\bigwedge(\mathbf{k}^{2}\boxtimes\mathbf{k}^{n})
has a filtration by
(\mathbf{GL}_{2}\times\mathbf{GL}_{n})
-representations
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k}^{2})\boxtimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{\dagger}}(\mathbf{k}^{n})
. This identity holds for any
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})
, and it gives a decomposition into simple representations if
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
.

In this paper, the ring
\overline{R}
is equal to the exterior algebra since
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})=2
. The equality of symmetric polynomials
s^{(2)}_{(a,b)}=s_{(a,b)^{\dagger}}
shows that for
a>b
, the characters of our filtration of
\overline{R}_{(a,b)}
agrees with those of the filtration given by Cauchy’s identity.

It would be interesting to try to extend this line of work to any
\text{char}(\mathbf{k})
. Developing standard monomial theory for exterior algebras could lead to interesting algebraic and geometric results, as in the case for classical standard monomial theory and symmetric algebras.

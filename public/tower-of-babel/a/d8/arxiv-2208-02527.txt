Weighted non-autonomous 𝐋 𝒒 ​ ( 𝐋 𝒑 ) \bm{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{L}^{p})} maximal regularity for complex systems under mixed regularity in space and time
Sebastian Bechtel

Abstract
. We show weighted non-autonomous L q ​ ( L p ) \mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathrm{L}^{p}) maximal regularity for families of complex second-order systems in divergence form under a mixed regularity condition in space and time. To be more precise, we let p , q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p,q\in(1,\infty) and we consider coefficient functions in C t β + ε \mathrm{C}_{t}^{\beta+\varepsilon} with values in C x α + ε \mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon} subject to the parabolic relation 2 ​ β + α = 1 2\beta+\alpha=1 . If p < d / α p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}} , we can likewise deal with spatial H x α + ε , d / α \mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}} regularity. The starting point for this result is a weak ( p , q ) (p,q) -solution theory with uniform constants. Further key ingredients are a commutator argument that allows us to establish higher a priori spatial regularity, operator-valued pseudo differential operators in weighted spaces, and a representation formula due to Acquistapace and Terreni. Furthermore, we show p p -bounds for semigroups and square roots generated by complex elliptic systems under a minimal regularity assumption for the coefficients.

Fix a finite time

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\div_{x}A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u&=f+\div_{x}F\quad\mathrm{in}\quad(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\div_{x}A(t,x)\nabla_{x}u&=f,\qquad\mathrm{in}\quad(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\

1.1.
Roadmap

In this roadmap, we intend to give the reader an extensive overview of our strategy. Our proof follows a classical approach due to Acquistapace and Terreni, but incorporates an a priori improvement of weak solutions in the spatial variable using a commutator argument.

The starting point is a weak solution theory for the generalized problem (
P’
). This generalization permits us to use an approximation argument later on. Classically, this is due to Lions in the Hilbertian situation. Fackler used the result of Prüss and Schnaubelt
[
40
]
to have a
(p,q)
-version of Lions’ result at hand. We cannot do this, as
[
40
]
does not yield implied constants that are uniform in the coefficients. However, we will need such a control for the a priori improvement of weak solutions in the spatial variable. We will come back to this at the very end of this roadmap. Hence, instead, we employ a framework of Dong and Kim
[
18
]
to treat complex systems in divergence form over spaces of the type
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
. Another advantage of the result of Dong and Kim are weighted estimates in time for weak solutions. This will be done in Section
4
, and consists of relating their notions with ours, as well as verifying an oscillation condition.

As is classical in the Acquistapace–Terreni approach, we derive a representation formula for weak
(p,q)
-solution in Section
5.1
. The formula reads

(5)

\displaystyle u({t^{*}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s}\bigr)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the operator
\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa
replaces the operator
\mathcal{L}_{t^{*}}
when passing from (
P
) to (
P’
) with regularized coefficients.
For maximal regularity, we have to estimate the term
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)u({t^{*}})
. Formally, this leads to the operators

(6)

\displaystyle S_{1}(u)({t^{*}})\mapsto

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{*}}(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s})u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(7)

\displaystyle S_{2}(f)({t^{*}})\mapsto

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

The commutation between
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)
and the integral in
S_{1}
will be justified during the proof of our main result. Consequently, to establish maximal regularity, we have to bound the operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. This is the topic of Section
5
. Observe, however, that the operator
S_{2}
acts on the data
f
, but
S_{1}
acts on the weak
(p,q)
-solution
u
. This has the following effect: for
S_{2}
, we plainly desire to show
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
-bounds. These will follow from a weighted and operator-valued pseudo-differential operator result. For
S_{1}
, however, the target space is still
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
, but higher regularity of weak solutions lets us vary the norm of the data space. To be more precise, in the classical approach as employed by Fackler
[
22
]
, the data space is
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
. The fundamental gain in our approach is that we will replace that data space by the space
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p})
. This has the effect that less restrictive kernel bounds for
S_{1}
compared to
[
22
]
suffice. We give more details on this in a moment.

Let us come back to the operator
S_{2}
. The classical approach is to rewrite this operator as a pseudo-differential operator. This will be presented in Section
5.3
. To do so, we have to restrict to a class of more regular right-hand sides
f
. This is, however, not a restriction, since we can use a standard approximation argument for the equation. This will be explained in Step 1 in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
7
. We emphasize that this approximation argument does not rely, yet, on the explicit control of implicit constants for weak
(p,q)
-solutions. Eventually,
[
38
]
leads to boundedness of
S_{2}
provided we can verify that
(\tau,s)\mapsto 2\pi i\tau(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa))^{-1}
satisfies some
R
-boundedness and regularity conditions. The precise assumption and its verification are presented in Lemma
5.5
. This uses two ingredients. First, that the coefficients are
\mathrm{C}_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty})
. Second, that the operators
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)
are jointly
R
-sectorial. Let us remark that the results in
[
38
]
are not weighted, but we will explain the necessary changes.

Uniform

-sectoriality is treated in Section
3.5
. On the one hand, we have to carefully trace the constants in well-known results on

-boundedness (more precisely, the approach based on off-diagonal bounds from
[

]
). On the other hand, we combine the elliptic solvability theory of Dong and Kim (see Proposition

) with recent advances around the Kato square root property
[

to eventually prove
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
-boundedness for the semigroup generated by
-(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)
with uniform constants in Theorem

[

]
, we are able to also treat complex systems. This is because we do not rely on the Gaussian bounds from
[

]

We come back to the operator
S_{1}
. As already mentioned, the plan is to show the boundedness

S_{1}\colon\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p})\to\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}).

This will turn out to be sufficient owing to the a priori estimate
\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}
for weak solutions – this is the higher spatial regularity that was already alluded before. The (weighted) boundedness for
S_{1}
follows from a good bound of convolution type for its integral kernel (this is the reason for ther Hölder condition in time), and Lemma
2.5
. The kernel bound is established in Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
. Lemma
5.2
is in some sense the central ingredient of this paper, as it is the only result that uses the full mixed regularity in time and space. There, we use the spatial regularity of our coefficients to have
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
-multipliers at our disposal (Lemma

), which eventually leads to estimates against
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p}
. The spatial Sobolev condition for the coefficients is optimal for this multiplier result.

The missing piece is the higher spatial regularity of weak solutions, the subject of Section
6
. Recall for this that the
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p}
-norm can be given by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\cdot\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}}
, where
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
is the fractional derivative of order
\alpha
. Our plan is to control the latter term by showing that
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u(t,x)
is a weak
(p,q)
-solution for some admissible right-hand side. Formally, one has

(8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u)-\div_{x}B(t,x)\nabla_{x}(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u)+\kappa(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u)=\partial^{\alpha}_{x}f-\div_{x}[B(t,\cdot),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}]\nabla_{x}u.

Then, the right-hand side is in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
if the commutator
[B(t,\cdot),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}]
is
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
-bounded (up to some absorption term in the case of
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}
coefficients). Owing to the spatial regularity of the coefficients, the latter fact is true according to Lemma
6.1
. Nevertheless, there remain some technical difficulties. In the first place,
u
is only in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
, so neither can we plug
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u
into the equation, nor can we justify the necessary calculations to show (
8
). The way out are an approximation argument in which we use regularized coefficients in conjunction with the difference quotient method (see Steps 1 and 2 in the proof of Proposition
6.2
), and the fact that on the whole space
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
and
\nabla
commute. Note that this step also excludes spatial weights, since then the norm would not be translation invariant anymore. Afterwards, when we want to take the limit in order to get back to our original equation, it is crucial to have control over the implied constants in the weak
(p,q)
-solution theory from Theorem
4.1
in terms of the coefficients.

Function spaces and weights

In this section, we review some facts from function space theory and Muckenhoupt weights, thereby introducing also our notation and some further conventions. However,
we assume that the reader is familiar with standard function space and weighted theory. For further background, the reader can, for instance, consult the monographs
[
46
]
for function spaces and
[
26
]
for Muckenhoupt weights.

2.1.
Spatial smoothness spaces

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, write
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s,p}
for the Bessel potential space of order
s
and integrability
p
. For a positive integer
k
one has
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{k,p}=\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{k,p}
. We also put
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s,p}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s,p}
. Our convention is that we use the
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}
-scale to denote regularity of solutions, and the
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}
-scale to measure regularity of coefficients. The fractional Sobolev spaces respect the usual lifting property. Also, the
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
inner product extends to a duality pairing between the spaces
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s,p}
and
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-s,p^{\prime}}
. Moreover, the
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s,p}
spaces interpolate naturally by means of the complex interpolation method due to Calderón–Lions.

Introduce the functional

(9)

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}f(x)\coloneqq\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{|y|\leq 1}|f(x+rh)-f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r}{r^{1+2\alpha}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
0<\alpha<1
, then the space
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
consists of all
f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
such that
S^{\alpha}f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
, and
f\mapsto\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\|S^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}
defines an equivalent norm on
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
, see
[
45
, Thm. 2.3]
. This leads to the following multiplier result.

Lemma 2.1

(Multiplier on fractional Sobolev spaces)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
0<\alpha<1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
X=\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}
if
p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
and
X=\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon}
otherwise. Then functions in
X
are multipliers on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
and one has the estimate

(10)

\displaystyle\|mf\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}}\lesssim\|m\|_{X}\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}},

where the implicit constant depends on
\alpha
,
p
,
\varepsilon
, and dimension.

Proof.

We appeal to the aforementioned characterization. First,
\|mf\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\leq\|m\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}
, and
\|m\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|m\|_{X}
is clear when
X
is a Hölder space, and follows from the (fractional) Sobolev embedding theorem when
X
is a Sobolev space.

Next, an expansion of
S^{\alpha}(mf)(x)
and the triangle inequality show

S^{\alpha}(mf)(x)\leq\|m\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}S^{\alpha}f(x)+|f(x)|S^{\alpha}m(x),

compare with
[
45
, Thm. 2.1]
. The first term can be estimated with the arguments from the beginning of the proof, this time using
S^{\alpha}f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
. For the second term, we distinguish cases for
X
.

Case 1
:
X=\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon}
.
In the definition of
S^{\alpha}m(x)
, we split the integral in
r
at height
1
. If
r\leq 1
, we use the Hölder regularity of
m
, to estimate this part by a constant (independent of
x
). Similarly, when
r\geq 1
, we use boundedness of
m
. In summary,
S^{\alpha}m(x)
is bounded by a constant depending linearly on
\|m\|_{\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}
, which concludes this case.

Case 2
:
X=\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}
.
With the relation
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}-\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{d}}\eqqcolon\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}
(observe that
q
is finite by hypothesis on
p
), we use Hölder’s inequality to give
\|fS^{\alpha}m\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{q}}\|S^{\alpha}m\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}}
. By choice of
q
, one has the Sobolev embedding
\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

The operator
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
is defined as the (unbounded) Fourier multiplication operator on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
with symbol
|\xi|^{\alpha}
. It extrapolates to a bounded operator
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
and we keep writing
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
.

The mapping
f\mapsto\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}
yields another equivalent norm on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
, see
[
44
, p. 133]
.

Sometimes, we also use the Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,p}
with
s\geq 0
. They consist of all functions
f
in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
such that the norm

(11)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,p}}\coloneqq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{|y-x|^{s}}\right|^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|y-x|^{d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite. By real interpolation, one has for
t>s\geq 0
the continuous inclusion
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{t,p}\subseteq\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,p}
.

2.2.
Muckenhoupt weights and parabolic spaces

Definition 2.3

(Muckenhoupt weights)
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
. A locally integrable function
w\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is a
Muckenhoupt weight
for
q
, write
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
, if the quantity

(12)

\displaystyle[w]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}\coloneqq\sup_{I}\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}w^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q-1}

is finite, where the supremum is taken over all intervals
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. If
q
is clear from the context, define the
dual weight
to
w
by
w^{\prime}\coloneqq w^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}
.

Example 2.4

(Power weights)
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
-1<\kappa<q-1
. Consider the weight
w(t)=t^{\kappa}
. Then
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
. Weights of this type are called
power weights
. Such weights are prototypical for the application of our theory in non-linear problems.

Let
X
be a spatial smoothness space,
q\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
. We consider the weighted parabolic spaces
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;X)\coloneqq\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,T,w;X)
and
\mathrm{W}_{t}^{1,q}(w;X)
, where the latter space consists of all
u\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;X)
with
\partial_{t}u
again in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;X)
. Note that functions in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;X)
are locally integrable by the
\mathrm{A}_{q}
-condition, hence the distributional derivative is well-defined.

If
Y\subseteq X
is dense, then
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(Y)
is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;X)
. One has the usual duality relation
(\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q^{\prime}}(w^{\prime};X^{*}))^{*}=\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;X)
, which extends the pairing between unweighted spaces. The following well-known lemma is a handy substitute for Young’s convolution inequality in the context of weighted spaces (here,
X=\mathbb{C}
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be measurable, radial, decreasing and integrable. Then

(13)

\displaystyle|(k\ast f)(x)|\lesssim\|k\|_{1}\mathcal{M}f(x)\qquad(f\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the maximal operator. In particular, if
q\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
, one has the weighted estimate

(14)

\displaystyle\|k\ast f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w)}\lesssim\|k\|_{1}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w)}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w)).

3.
Uniform estimates for elliptic operators

In Section
, we have introduced the elliptic operators
\{\mathcal{L}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
. We will associate parts in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
with these operators, and show
uniform
bounds for their associated semigroups and square roots. We will also transfer semigroup bounds to the space
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
. The cornerstone for the results in this section is the well-posedness result for parabolic systems in divergence form due to Dong and Kim
[
18
]
.

3.1.
Elliptic coefficients

We stay slightly more general here, which will become handy for technical reasons later on, for instance in Section
6
.
That being said, we introduce the following class of regular elliptic coefficients, which includes the coefficients of the non-autonomous problems studied in this article.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
N\geq 0
. Denote by
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\gamma,N)
the class of
elliptic coefficients
with coefficient bounds
\Lambda
and
\lambda
that are
\mathrm{C}^{\gamma}
with norm at most
N
. More precisely, this class consists of all functions
B\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{dm\times dm}
which satisfy

(15)

\displaystyle|B(x)|\leq\Lambda\quad\&\quad\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}\operatorname{Re}(B(x)^{k\ell}\xi^{k}\,|\,\xi^{\ell})\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2}\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{dm}),

and the regularity condition

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{|B(x+h)-B(x)|}{|h|^{\gamma}}\leq N\qquad(h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}).

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
A({t^{*}},\cdot)\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
. In the case
p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
, this follows from embedding results for smoothness spaces, see
[
46
, Thm. 2.8.1. (e)]
.

3.2.
Elliptic systems and weak
(p,q)
-solutions

We associate with a coefficient function
B
a form and an operator
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\gamma,N)
. Define the form

(17)

\displaystyle b\colon\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\times\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad b(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}B(x)\nabla_{x}u(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla_{x}v(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and associate with it the operator

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\colon\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2}\quad\text{via}\quad\langle\mathcal{B}u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2},\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}}=b(u,v)\qquad(u,v\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}).

Given a family
\{\mathcal{B}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
induced by coefficients
B(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\gamma,N)
and a parameter
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, associate with them the non-autonomous evolution problem

(P’)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t)+\mathcal{B}_{t}u(t)+\kappa u(t)=f(t),\qquad u(0)=0.

The following definition makes precise what we understand under a solution to (
P’
). With the choices
\mathcal{B}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}
and
\kappa=0
, this clarifies in particular the solution concept for the problem (
P
) from the introduction.

Definition 3.4
.

Given
f\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, call a function

u\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})

a
weak
(p,q)
-solution
of (
P’
),
if
u(0)=0
,
and if the integral equation

(IE)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}-\varphi^{\prime}(s)(u(s)\,|\,g)+\varphi(s)b_{s}(u(s),g)+\kappa\varphi(s)(u(s)\,|\,g)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(s)\langle f(s),g\rangle_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p},\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

holds for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{C})
and
g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 3.5
.

(i)

Functions in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w)
with
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
are locally integrable, hence the pairings in (
IE
) are well-defined.

(ii)

It follows from duality that a weak
(p,q)
-solution
u
of (
P’
) has a weak derivative
\partial_{t}u(t)
in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
that coincides with
f(t)-\mathcal{B}(t)u(t)-\kappa u(t)
for almost all
t
.

(iii)

A weak
(p,q)
-solution is continuous at
0
with values in
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
, which renders the initial condition meaningful. For the weighted case, this is presented in
[
24
, Lem. 4.1]
.

(iv)

Existence and uniqueness of weak
(p,q)
-solutions are independent of the parameter
\kappa
. Indeed, if
u
is a weak
(p,q)
-solution to the parameter
\kappa
, then
v(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa t}u(t)
is a weak
(p,q)
-solution to the right-hand side
\mathrm{e}^{\kappa s}f
with
\kappa=0
, and vice versa. Note that this imports a dependence on
T
for the implicit constants.

(v)

The integral equation (
IE
) extends to
g\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}
by continuity.

The parameter
\kappa
is supposed to be taken sufficiently large (in particular, we tacitly assume
\kappa\geq 1
). This is quantified by the results in
[
18
]
. In particular, we can ensure ellipticity in this way. We emphasize that the choice of
\kappa
can be made uniform in the quantities mentioned in Agreement
1
.

Let us agree for the rest of this section that
B
denotes any fixed coefficient function from the class
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
. Implicit constants are allowed to depend on
p
,
\Lambda
,
\lambda
,
\varepsilon
,
M
, and dimensions.

As a consequence of ellipticity, there is some
\omega\in[0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}})
depending on
\Lambda
,
\lambda
, and
\kappa
such that the numerical range of
b+\kappa(\cdot\,|\,\cdot)_{2}
is contained in the closed sector
\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega}
of opening angle
2\omega
. Furthermore, using Definition
3.1
and the Lax–Milgram lemma,
\mathcal{B}+\kappa+\rho
is invertible for all
\rho\geq 0
. In particular,
\mathcal{B}+\kappa
is itself invertible as an operator
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2}
.

As a consequence of the Hölder regularity of the coefficients,
\mathcal{B}+\kappa
extrapolates moreover to an isomorphism
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
for
all

p\in(1,\infty)
. The argument divides into two steps. First, the autonomous problem associated with
\mathcal{B}+\kappa
is well-posed according to
[
18
]
. We will give further information on that result and its applicability in our context in Section
4
, see in particular Lemma
4.2
.
Second, the well-posedness of the original elliptic problem together with an estimate for its solutions follow by applying a cutoff argument to a stationary solution
[
17
, Proof of Thm. 2.2]
. The result can then be summarized as follows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The operator
\mathcal{B}+\kappa
extrapolates to an invertible operator
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
. Given
f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
, write
u\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}
for the unique solution to the equation
(\mathcal{B}+\kappa)u=f
. Then, one has the estimate
\|u\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The solutions provided by Proposition
3.6
are
compatible
to Lax–Milgram solutions in the following sense. Given
f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}\cap\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2}
, let
u
be the solution in
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}
provided by Proposition
3.6
, and
v
be the solution in
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}
provided by the Lax–Milgram lemma. Then
u
and
v
coincide. Indeed, this is a consequence of local compatibility in complex interpolation scales
[
32
, Thm. 8.1]
and the fact that Proposition
3.6
provides a solution for
all

p\in(1,\infty)
.

Remark 3.8
.

The result in
[
18
]
only requires that
\kappa
is larger than a certain threshold quantified by the parameters fixed in Agreement
1
. Hence, to ensure that all results in Section
3
remain true when
\kappa
is replaced by
\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
, we pick
\kappa
a bit larger for good measure. We will exploit this observation in Section
5
.

3.3.
The elliptic operator on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
and mapping properties

In virtue of the embedding
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}\subseteq\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,2}
,
define the part of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
and denote it as an abuse of notation also by the symbol
B
(it will be clear from the context if
B
denotes the coefficient function or the part in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
). Of course, the part of
\mathcal{B}+\kappa
in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
coincides with
B+\kappa
.
One has that

is a densely defined, invertible, and m-
\omega
-sectorial operator in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
with domain
\dom(B+\kappa)=\dom(B)
. In particular,
-(B+\kappa)
generates a holomorphic semigroup of contractions
\{\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\}_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}-\omega}}
on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
. We will tacitly employ some properties of the sectorial functional calculus of
B+\kappa
. The reader can consult
[
28
, Chap. 7]
for further background.

Owing to
[
10
, Lem. 7.3]
, we deduce
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
-bounds for the semigroup generated by
-(B+\kappa)
as a consequence of Proposition
3.6
and Remark
3.7
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\varphi\in(0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}-\omega)
. One has the estimate

(19)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\qquad(z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi},f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}).

Remark 3.10
.

In
[
10
, Lem. 7.3]
, only the case
p\geq 2
is presented. The case
p\leq 2
either follows by a duality argument with
\mathcal{B}^{*}+\kappa
, or by repeating the calculation in
[
10
]
, but changing the order in which
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-calculus and
(\mathcal{B}+\kappa)^{-1}
are applied.

3.4.
Square roots and bounds on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}

As an m-
\omega
-sectorial operator,
B+\kappa
possesses a square root
(B+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. It acts as an isomorphism
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,2}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
according to the solution of the Kato square root problem
[
8
]
. As a consequence of coefficient regularity,
(B+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}
extrapolates to an isomorphism
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
for
all

p\in(1,\infty)
.
Similar ideas were already employed in
[
22
]
, but relying on the Gaussian property, which was only established in the scalar case
m=1
and is notably more technical. Instead, we use recent results established by the author in
[
10
, Thm. 1.1]
.
Indeed, in the case
p\leq 2
, its application is justified by Theorem
3.9
, whereas in the case
p\geq 2
,
we appeal to Proposition
3.6
in conjunction with Remark
3.7
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then
(B+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}
extrapolates to a (compatible) isomorphism
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
.

Theorem
3.11
allows us to translate the
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
-bounds for
\{\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\}_{\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}}
from Theorem
3.9
to
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
-bounds.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\varphi\in[0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}-\omega)
. One has the estimate

(20)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\qquad(z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi},f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}).

In particular,
\{\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\}_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}}
extrapolates to a semigroup on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
with generator
-(\mathcal{B}+\kappa)
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}
and
f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
. As a primer, let us show

(21)

\displaystyle\|(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}.

We employ a duality argument. To this end, let
h\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
. Note that the coefficient class
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
is invariant under taking adjoints. Calculate using Kato’s square root property and Theorem
3.11
(applied with
B^{*}
and
p^{\prime}
instead of
B
and
p
) that

(22)

\displaystyle|((B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\,|\,h)|

\displaystyle=|(f\,|\,(B^{*}+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h)|

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\|(B^{*}+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}

(24)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Duality lets us conclude this first claim.

Next, write

\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}f=\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}(B+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f=(B+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f.

Let
g\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
, and calculate similarly as above, but using furthermore Theorem
3.9
, that

(25)

\displaystyle|\langle\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle=|(\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\,|\,(B^{*}+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}g)|

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{p}\|(B^{*}+\kappa)^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|_{p^{\prime}}

(27)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}.

Duality and (
21
) lead to
\|\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\lesssim\|(B+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}
.
∎

3.5.
Uniform
R
-sectoriality

As a preparation for Section
5.3
, we show
R
-sectoriality for the set of operators
\{B+\kappa\;;\;B\in\mathcal{C}\}
, where
\mathcal{C}
consists of all operators associated with coefficients in
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
, and where the
R
-bound only depends on the quantified parameters from Agreement
1
. For further background on
R
-boundedness and
R
-sectoriality, the reader can consult
[
33
]
.

Proposition 3.13

(
R
-sectoriality of
\mathcal{C}
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\varphi\in[0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}-\omega)
. Then, the set
\{\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\;;\;z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi},B\in\mathcal{C}\}
satisfies the square function estimate

(28)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\mathrm{e}^{-z_{j}(B_{j}+\kappa)}f_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr\|_{p}\lesssim\Bigl\|\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|f_{j}|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr\|_{p}\quad\bigl(z_{j}\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi},B_{j}\in\mathcal{C},f_{j}\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}).

In particular, for
z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}
and
B\in\mathcal{C}
fixed, the operator
\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}
extends from
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
to a bounded operator
S_{B}(z)
on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
,
\{S_{B}(z)\}_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}}
is a strongly continuous and analytic semigroup on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
, and the set
\{S_{B}(z)\;;\;z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi},B\in\mathcal{C}\}
is
R
-bounded with

-bound depending only on the parameters fixed in Agreement
1
.

Remark 3.14
.

Proposition
3.13
shows in particular that the semigroup in
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
is
R
-sectorial of the same angle as the semigroup on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
. Hence, we keep writing
\omega
instead of, say,
\omega_{R}
.

Before we come to the justification of Proposition
3.13
, let us record an important consequence that we will need later on in Section
5.3
.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\psi\in[\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}},\pi-\omega)
. Let
-B_{p}^{\kappa}
denote the generator of the semigroup
\{S_{B}(t)\}_{t>0}
from Proposition
3.13
. Then the set
\{z(z+B_{p}^{\kappa})^{-1}\;;\;z\in\mathrm{S}_{\psi},B\in\mathcal{C}\}
of operators on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
is
R
-bounded, and the
R
-bound depends only on the quantities fixed in Agreement
1
.

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathrm{S}_{\psi}
and
B\in\mathcal{C}
. Split
\arg(z)=\varphi+\tilde{\varphi}
, where
|\varphi|\in[0,\psi-\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}})
and
|\tilde{\varphi}|\in[0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}})
. The operator
(z+B_{p}^{\kappa})^{-1}
can be represented using the Laplace transform
[
28
, Prop. 3.4.1 d)]
via

(29)

\displaystyle(z+B_{p}^{\kappa})^{-1}=\mathrm{e}^{-i\varphi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t|z|\mathrm{e}^{i\tilde{\varphi}}}S_{B}(t\mathrm{e}^{-i\varphi})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Then, the claim follows from
[
33
, Ex. 2.15]
. Indeed, they show that
z(z+B_{p}^{\kappa})^{-1}
is contained in the strong closure of the absolute convex hull of the semigroup generated by
-B_{p}^{\kappa}
. Hence,
\{z(z+B_{p}^{\kappa})^{-1}\;;\;z\in\mathrm{S}_{\psi},B\in\mathcal{C}\}
is contained in the strong closure of the absolute convex hull of
\{S_{B}(z)\;;\;z\in\mathrm{S}_{\psi-\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}},B\in\mathcal{C}\}
. But taking the strong closure of the absolute convex hull of a set of operators preserves
R
-boundedness with the same
R
-bound, so we conclude using Proposition
3.13
.
∎

Given
1\leq r<2<s\leq\infty
such that
p\in(r,s)
, and
B\in\mathcal{C}
, Proposition
3.13
is a consequence of so-called
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{r}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{s}
off-diagonal estimates
for
\{\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\}_{z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}}
. The general approach in the context of homogeneous spaces was presented in
[
33
]
, and for dependence of the implied constants, see
[
11
, Sec. 5]
. To be more precise, we suppose that, for some
c>0
and for all measurable sets
E,F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z\in\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}
, one has the bound

(30)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}_{F}\mathrm{e}^{-z(B+\kappa)}\mathbf{1}_{E}f\|_{s}\lesssim|z|^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2s}}-\nicefrac{{d}}{{2r}}}\mathrm{e}^{-c\frac{\dist(E,F)^{2}}{|z|}}\|\mathbf{1}_{E}f\|_{r}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{r}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}).

Inequality (
30
) for
r=s=2
is known under the name
Gaffney estimates
and is well-known in the literature. A proof of this result that carefully keeps track of the implicit constants can be found in
[
10
, Prop. 3.3]
. Likewise, (
30
) is known for

,

, and with

, as a consequence of the
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
-bounds for the semigroup provided by Theorem
3.9
and
[
6
, Prop. 3.2 (1)]
. In this case, we speak of
hypercontractivity
of the semigroup. Finally, (
30
) is then a consequence of interpolation of Gaffney estimates with hypercontractivity, taking duality and composition into account.

4.
Existence and uniqueness of weak
(p,q)
-solutions

In this section, we consider a family of operators
\{\mathcal{B}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
associated with coefficients
B(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
that
depend
\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\varepsilon}
on
t
.
1
1

1

Say that a family
\{B_{t}\}_{0<t<T}\subseteq\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\alpha,M)

depends
\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\beta}
on
t
if
B_{t}\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\alpha,M)
and the mapping

is
\beta
-Hölder continuous with values in
\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha}

lies in the class
\mathrm{C}_{t}^{\beta}
.

The prototype for such a family of operators is the family
\{\mathcal{L}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
from Section
1
(keep Remark
3.2
in mind).
We aim to prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the associated problem (
P’
) in the sense of Definition
3.4
.
To do so, we recast our original problem in the framework originating from the works of Dong and Kim
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
This includes the introduction of a global extension in time of our original problem on
\mathbb{R}
as outlined in
[
15
, Rem. 1]
.
Implicit constants in this section are allowed to depend on
p
,
q
,
[w]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}
,
\Lambda
,
\lambda
,
\alpha,\beta
, Hölder regularity, and dimensions.

We begin by extending our coefficient family
\{B_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
to all of
\mathbb{R}
. We extend constantly at the endpoints, that is, we set
B_{t}\coloneqq B_{0}
for all
t<0
and
B_{t}\coloneqq B_{T}
for all
t>T
.
For such
t
, we associate of course also a form
b_{t}
with
B_{t}
.
Note that this extension does not affect the assumed Hölder regularity of the coefficients.
Furthermore, we isometrically extend the right-hand side
f\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
outside of
(0,T)
by zero to arrive at a function in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
, which we denote by
F
. Also in the sequel, we will systematically denote functions on
\mathbb{R}
by capital letters to better distinguish them from their local analogs.
Given the extensions of
\{B_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
and
f
, we look for solutions
U\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
fulfilling the extended integral equation

(EIE)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\Phi^{\prime}(s)(U(s)\,|\,g)+\Phi(s)b_{s}(U(s),g)+\kappa\Phi(s)(U(s)\,|\,g)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(s)\langle F(s),g\rangle_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p},\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

where we use test functions
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Dong and Kim solved a similar problem in
[
18
]
.
They show that, for a given
F\in\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
F=F_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}F_{i}
,
F_{j}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
, there exists a solution
U\in\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the integral equation

(DKIE)

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}-(U(s)\,|\,\Psi^{\prime}(s))+b_{s}(U(s),\Psi(s))+\kappa(U(s)\,|\,\Psi(s))\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle F(s),\Psi(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

for all test functions
\Psi\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We explain and compare the used function spaces in the sequel of this section.
For the notion of weak solutions employed by Dong and Kim, see also
[
15
, p. 896]
and
[
16
, p. 3286]
.
Furthermore, solutions to (
DKIE
) are subject to the a priori estimate

(31)

\displaystyle\kappa\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\partial_{i}U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}\lesssim\|F_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|F_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}

according to
[
18
, Thm. 7.2]
, where the implicit constant depends on
p
,
q
,
[w]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}
,
\Lambda
,
\lambda
, dimension, and the parameters
\gamma
and
R_{0}
appearing in Lemma
4.2
.
In particular, choosing
F=0
in (
31
) shows the uniqueness of solutions to (
DKIE
).

The rest of this section is divided into two steps:
First, we will relate the solution concepts of (

) and (

) and show that the former implies the latter. Eventually, this leads to a solution for the original problem (
P’
).
Second, we will check the validity of the regularity assumptions on
\{B_{t}\}
from
[

, Thm. 7.2]
to harvest the results of the first step.
At the end of the day, this will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
f\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
, there exists a unique weak
(p,q)
-solution
u
to (
P’
), and one has the estimate

(32)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})}+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}+\kappa\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})}\,.

Step 1
: Compatibility with Dong and Kim.
In order to solve (
DKIE
), Dong and Kim consider right-hand sides
F
in the spaces
\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
These spaces are isomorphic to the spaces
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
as can bee seen from a parabolic variant of
[
3
, Thm. 3.9]
.
This means that the admissible right-hand sides for (
DKIE
) and (
EIE
) coincide.
Now,
[
18
, Sec. 8]
gives the existence of a solution
U
to (
DKIE
) in the regularity class
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which denotes the closure of
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the space
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since we work spatially in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathring{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
A function
U\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is by its very definition an element of
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
.
Conversely a function in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
that satisfies (
EIE
) is a member of
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p,q,w}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For complete definitions of the above function spaces, the reader can consult
[
16
, p. 3284]
and
[
18
, Sec. 4]
.

Comparing the classes of test functions employed in (
EIE
) and (
DKIE
) reveals that Dong and Kim use a larger class of test functions in their integral formulation.
In particular, this shows that a solution to (
DKIE
) is also a solution to (
EIE
).
On the other hand, recall that a function
U\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
solving (
EIE
)
is also an admissible function for (
DKIE
).
Using the fact that the tensors
\Phi(t)g(x)
with
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are dense in

\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},w^{\prime};\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p^{\prime}})
, we deduce by continuity (compare with Remark
3.5

(v)
) that (
EIE
) in particular remains to hold for test functions in

\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, we get that
U
is also a solution for (
DKIE
), and is as such again unique.

Next, we focus on the a priori estimate (
31
) and its relation to the maximal regularity estimate in Theorem
4.1
.
Recall
\kappa\geq 1
. Then, we have

(33)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})}\lesssim\kappa\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa^{1/2}\|\partial_{i}U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}\lesssim\kappa\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})}.

Up to now, we have only worked out the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the extended integral equation (
EIE
).
Hence, it remains to get back to (
P’
).
Recall that
F=0
outside of the interval
(0,T)

by construction.
Consequently,
U=0
on
(-\infty,0)
by uniqueness, hence
U(0)=0
by continuity (see Remark
3.5

(iii)
).
Additionally, the solution
U\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R},w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
that has been constructed via the method above gives rise to a restriction
u=U|_{(0,T)}\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p})
.
Then
u
satisfies

by continuity and solves (

).
This shows that
u
is the unique
(p,q)
-solution of (
P’
).

Let

\displaystyle\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{Q}_{r}(t,x)}\left|B^{k\ell}_{s}(y_{1},\hat{y})-\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{Q}_{r}^{\prime}(t,\hat{x})}B^{k\ell}_{\tau}(y_{1},\hat{z})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{z}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\gamma\quad(k,\ell=1,\dots,m),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{r}(t,x)\coloneqq(t-r^{2},t)\times\mathrm{B}_{r}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Q}_{r}^{\prime}(t,\hat{x})\coloneqq(t-r^{2},t)\times\mathrm{B}_{r}^{\prime}(\hat{x}),

Let

\displaystyle\Bigr|B_{s}^{k\ell}(y_{1},\hat{y})-\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{Q}_{r}^{\prime}(t,\hat{x})}B^{k\ell}_{\tau}(y_{1},\hat{z})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{z}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathrm{Q}_{r}^{\prime}(t,\hat{x})}\left|B^{k\ell}_{s}(y)-B^{k\ell}_{\tau}(y)\right|+\left|B^{k\ell}_{\tau}(y_{1},\hat{y})-B^{k\ell}_{\tau}(y_{1},\hat{z})\right|\,\mathrm{d}\hat{z}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

from Section

. First, we derive a solution formula for weak

5.1.
Representation formula by Acquistapace and Terreni

For a weak
(p,q)
-solution
u
of (
P’
), we rely on a well-known representation formula due to Acquistapace and Terreni in
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
given pointwise by

(
\heartsuit
)

\displaystyle u({t^{*}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s}\bigr)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

In the unweighted situation, the proof is well-known in the literature
[
2
,
12
,
27
,
22
]
, but we give a streamlined version that directly works with absolute continuity.

Proof of (

♡

‣
5.1
).

Consider on
[0,{t^{*}}]
the function
v(s)=\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}u(s)
. Moreover, let
0\leq\tau<{t^{*}}
. We claim the identity

(41)

\displaystyle v(\tau)=v(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}u(s)+\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}u^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Before we turn to the proof of (
41
), we show how it implies (

♡

‣
5.1
). Note that the function
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}u(s)+\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}u^{\prime}(s)
is in
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p})
, since

is a weak
(p,q)
-solution (keep Remark
3.5

(ii)
in mind) and the semigroup is bounded on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
owing to Proposition

. Hence, by Lebesgue’s theorem, we can take the limit
\tau\to{t^{*}}
on the right-hand side of (
41
). Equally, we can take this limit on the left-hand side, owing to the facts that
u
is uniformly continuous over
[0,{t^{*}}]
with values in
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
, and the family
\{\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\}_{0\leq s\leq{t^{*}}}
is strongly continuous and bounded as a family of operators on

. Then, plugging in the actual definition of

yields (

♡

‣
5.1
).

Let us come back to the proof of (
41
). On the interval
[0,\tau]
,
s\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}
, considered as a family of operators on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}
, has a bounded derivative due to Proposition

and analyticity. As

is a weak

-solution,

∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
,
Y
be Banach spaces,
\tau>0
,
\{T(s)\}_{0\leq s\leq\tau}
be a differentiable family of operators
X\to Y
with bounded derivative, and
g\colon[0,\tau]\to X
be absolutely continuous. Then
s\mapsto T(s)g(s)\in Y
is an absolutely continuous function on
[0,\tau]
with derivative
T^{\prime}(s)g(s)+T(s)g^{\prime}(s)
.

Proof.

The assumption on
T
implies in particular that
s\mapsto T(s)
is absolutely continuous on
[0,\tau]
. Now, use absolute continuity of both
T
and
g
, and the Fubini–Tonelli theorem, to give

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}T^{\prime}(s)g(s)+T(s)g^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(43)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}T^{\prime}(s)\Bigl(g(0)+\int_{0}^{s}g^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{\tau}\Bigl(T(0)+\int_{0}^{s}T^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Bigr)g^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(44)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}T^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,g(0)+T(0)\int_{0}^{\tau}g^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}T^{\prime}(s)g^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}s.

All remaining integrals can now be evaluated using absolute continuity, and we only remain with
T(\tau)g(\tau)-T(0)g(0)
after having canceled all superfluous terms. Rearranging terms gives the claim.
∎

Motivated by (

♡

‣
5.1
), we are going to consider the operators

(45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{1}(u)({t^{*}})\mapsto&\int_{0}^{t^{*}}(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s})u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\\
S_{2}(f)({t^{*}})\mapsto&(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Up to some technicalities, boundedness of

5.2.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

The operator
\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s}\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1+\alpha,p}}\lesssim|{t^{*}}-s|^{\beta+\varepsilon}.

Let

Put

if

\displaystyle|\langle(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s})f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B({t^{*}},x)-B(s,x))\nabla f(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla g(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|(B({t^{*}},\cdot)-B(s,\cdot))\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}}\|\nabla g\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-\alpha,p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|B({t^{*}},\cdot)-B(s,\cdot)\|_{X}\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1-\alpha,p^{\prime}}}.

Using the regularity of

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}-\mathcal{B}_{s})f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1+\alpha,p}}\lesssim|{t^{*}}-s|^{\beta+\varepsilon}\|\nabla f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}}\leq|{t^{*}}-s|^{\beta+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
s\in(0,{t^{*}})
. The operator
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}
acts as a bounded operator

(51)

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1+\alpha,p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim|{t^{*}}-s|^{-\beta-1}.

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to show that
((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)}

\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1-\alpha,p^{\prime}}
with norm controlled by
|{t^{*}}-s|^{-\beta-1}
. We are going to show that
((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)}
maps
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}
with estimate against
|{t^{*}}-s|^{-1}
and
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}
with norm controlled by
|{t^{*}}-s|^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}
. Then the claim is a consequence of complex interpolation, keeping the relation
\nicefrac{{(\alpha-3)}}{{2}}=-\beta-1
in mind.

Note that as a consequence of Theorem
3.9
, the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-calculus of
(B_{t^{*}})^{*}
is bounded on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}
with control of the implicit constants. Hence, in conjunction with Theorem
3.11
, calculate

(52)

\displaystyle\|((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)((B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa)}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim({t^{*}}-s)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\|[\mathbf{z}^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathbf{z}}](({t^{*}}-s)(B_{t^{*}})^{*}+\kappa))f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}}

(53)

\displaystyle\lesssim({t^{*}}-s)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}}.

The calculation for
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}
is similar. This completes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Boundedness of
S_{2}

Recall the operator
S_{2}
from (
45
).
The aim of this subsection is to show the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
, then one has the estimate

(54)

\displaystyle\|S_{2}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2})).

Implicit constants only depend on the quantities from Agreement
1
.

In the unweighted case, it is well-known in the literature
[
29
,
38
,
22
]
that such bounds for
S_{2}
follow from the boundedness of some pseudo-differential operator with operator-valued kernel. For the reader’s convenience, we include a proof. For further background on such pseudo-differential operators, the reader may consult
[
31
,
38
]
and the references therein. In Remark
5.6
, we will comment on the extension to the weighted setting.

For technical reasons, we extend
f
by
0
outside of
(0,T)
, and we extend the operator family
\{\mathcal{B}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
to
\mathbb{R}
constantly at the endpoints (we performed the same extension already in Section
4
). Using the vector-valued Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
30
, Sec. 2.4.c]
for further information) and the Fubini–Tonelli theorem (its application is justified by integrability of
\mathcal{F}f
and exponential decay of the semigroup), calculate

(55)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}({t^{*}}-s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}({t^{*}}-s)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}f(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi is\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-({t^{*}}-s)(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}({t^{*}}-s)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi is\tau}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathcal{F}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{I}(\tau,{t^{*}})\mathcal{F}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

where
\mathrm{I}(\tau,{t^{*}})
is implicitly defined by the latest identity. Using the transformation
u={t^{*}}-s
and the relationship between a semigroup and its generator in virtue of the Laplace transform (apply for instance
[
28
, Prop. 3.4.1 d)]
to
\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
), deduce

(56)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}(\tau,{t^{*}})=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\tau{t^{*}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-u(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi iu\tau}\,\mathrm{d}u=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\tau{t^{*}}}(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa))^{-1}.

Plug this back into (
55
) to conclude with the definition of
S_{2}
that

(57)

\displaystyle S_{2}(f)({t^{*}})=(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa))^{-1}\mathcal{F}f(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\tau{t^{*}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

The integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa))^{-1}\mathcal{F}f(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\tau{t^{*}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau
is finite, so we can commute
(\mathcal{B}_{t^{*}}+\kappa)
with the integral in (
57
) owing to Hille’s theorem.
This means that
S_{2}(f)
can be represented as the pseudo-differential operator with symbol

(\tau,s)\mapsto(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa)(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa))^{-1}.

Of course, by expansion, we can equally study boundedness of the pseudo-differential operator associated with the symbol
(\tau,s)\mapsto 2\pi i\tau(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa))^{-1}
. In the following lemma, we study this symbol thoroughly.

Lemma 5.5
.

For all
\ell\geq 0
, the symbol
a(\tau,s)=2\pi i\tau(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa))^{-1}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{1,0}(\varepsilon,\ell,\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
, that is to say, there is some constant
C>0
such that, for
k=0,\dots,\ell
, one has the
R
-bound

(58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl\{(1+|\tau|)^{k}\partial_{\tau}^{k}a(\tau,s)\;;\;s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\bigr\}\leq C,

and for
s,h,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
one has the regularity condition

(59)

\displaystyle\bigl\|\partial_{\tau}^{k}\bigl[a(\tau,s)-a(\tau,s+h)\bigr]\bigr\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\leq C|h|^{\varepsilon}(1+|\tau|)^{-k}.

Proof.

For brevity, we rescale
2\pi\tau
to
\tau
in the definition of the symbol
a
.
Fix
\varphi\in(\omega,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}})
and
\psi\in(\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}},\pi)
such that
\varphi+\psi<\pi
, and let
s,h\in\mathbb{R}
. Define on
\mathrm{S}_{\psi}\cup\mathrm{B}(0,\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})
the function
A(\lambda)=(1+\lambda)\bigl[(\lambda+(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\kappa))^{-1}-(\lambda+(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\kappa))^{-1}\bigr]
.

Step 1
: Reduction to the case
k=0
. Since the function
A
is holomorphic in
\lambda
and defined on a sector that strictly includes the half-plane as well as a ball around the origin of fixed radius, the regularity condition reduces, as a consequence of Cauchy’s formula for derivatives, to boundedness of
A
, which is the case
k=0
(but in a larger region). With a similar auxiliary function, the same is true for the
R
-boundedness condition, see
[
33
, Ex. 2.16]
.

Step 2
: Verification of the case
k=0
. The
R
-boundedness condition follows directly from Corollary
3.15
. Hence, it only remains to show that the function
A
is bounded in operator norm with control against
|h|^{\varepsilon}
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{S}_{\psi}\cup\mathrm{B}(0,\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})
, expand
A(\lambda)
using the functional calculus as

(F1)

\displaystyle(1+\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda+\mathbf{z}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}}\right](B_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})

(F2)

\displaystyle\times{}(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigl[\mathcal{B}_{s+h}-\mathcal{B}_{s}\bigr](\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(F3)

\displaystyle\times{}(1+\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda+\mathbf{z}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}}\right](B_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}).

Using composition, we can treat all three factors separately. The decay in
|h|
comes from (
F2
), whereas the other two are merely bounded. Moreover, (
F1
) and (
F3
) have the same structure, so we only present the estimate for (
F1
). Recall that, according to Remark
3.8
, all results from Section
3
can be applied to
\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
.

Define on
\mathrm{S}_{\varphi}
the function
g_{\lambda}=\mathbf{z}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+\mathbf{z}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-1}
. As a consequence of Theorem
3.9
, the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-calculus of
B_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
is bounded on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
, compare with the proof of Lemma
5.3
. This means that we have to bound
\|g_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}
in an appropriate way. Using the reverse triangle inequality on sectors and the case distinction
|z|\geq|\lambda|+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
and
|z|\leq|\lambda|+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}
, we indeed find readily
\|g_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}+|\lambda|)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. Using
(\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}}+|\lambda|)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\approx(1+|\lambda|)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
, the factor in front of
g_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})
cancels out, which completes the treatment of (
F1
).

It remains to treat (
F2
).
The crucial ingredient is the estimate

(60)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{s}-\mathcal{B}_{s+h}\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\lesssim|h|^{\varepsilon},

whose proof follows the lines of Lemma
5.2
, but it suffices to have coefficients in
\mathrm{C}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty})
.
Recall from Theorem
3.11
the estimate
\|(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}
for
f\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{2}
. The same estimate holds of course if
\mathcal{B}_{s}
and
p
are replaced by
(\mathcal{B}_{s})^{*}
and
p^{\prime}
. Hence, we can estimate by duality and using (
60
) that, for
g\in\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}
,

(61)

\displaystyle|((\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}-\mathcal{B}_{s})(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\,|\,g)|

(62)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle|((\mathcal{B}_{s+h}-\mathcal{B}_{s})(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\,|\,((\mathcal{B}_{s})^{*}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}g)|

(63)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}-\mathcal{B}_{s})(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{-1,p}}\|((\mathcal{B}_{s})^{*}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}g\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p^{\prime}}}

(64)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle|h|^{\varepsilon}\|(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1,p}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}}

(65)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle|h|^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p^{\prime}}}.

Consequently,

(66)

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{B}_{s}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}-\mathcal{B}_{s})(\mathcal{B}_{s+h}+\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim|h|^{\varepsilon}.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

We have already seen that the bound for
S_{2}
follows from the bound for the pseudo-differential operator associated with the symbol
a(\tau,t)=2\pi i\tau(2\pi i\tau+(\mathcal{B}_{t}+\kappa))^{-1}
. It was shown in
[
38
, Thm. 5]
that boundedness for such a pseudo-differential operator follows if the symbol
a
is in
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{1,0}(\varepsilon,6,\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
and

. The condition on

was just verified in Lemma

. Dependence of implicit constants becomes apparent from an inspection of the proof. Moreover, the result in
[

]
extends to

, see Remark

.
∎

Remark 5.6

(Weighted operator-valued pseudo-differential operators)
.

The proof of
[
38
, Thm. 5]
consists of 4 steps: 1) Decomposition of a general symbol into an
“error symbol”
and a symbol that is smooth in
s
. 2) Representation of smooth symbols by elementary symbols. 3) Estimate for pseudo-differential operators associated with an error symbol. 4) Estimate for pseudo-differential operators associated with an elementary symbol. Steps 1) and 2) stay, of course, valid. In Step 3), Schur’s test is used, but the kernel estimate directly falls under the scope of Lemma
2.5
. Finally, in Step 4), a vector-valued Littlewood–Paley decomposition, a vector-valued Mikhlin theorem and
R
-boundedness of Littlewood–Paley operators are used. These ingredients remain true in the weighted setting, see
[
36
]
or
[
23
]
. Finally, Schur’s test is used once again, this time with a more complicated kernel bound, which nevertheless can be captured by Lemma
2.5
.

6.
Higher regularity of weak solutions

In this section, we consider a family of operators
\{\mathcal{B}_{t}\}_{0<t<T}
associated with coefficients
B(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
. If
p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
, we assume in addition that
B\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}})
, otherwise we require
B\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon})
. Note that we do not require any regularity in time in this section. Provided that the associated problem (
P’
) admits a solution, we show higher spatial regularity for this solution in Proposition
6.2
. This is based on a commutator argument that already appeared in
[
7
]
. Implicit constants are allowed to depend on
p
,
q
,
[w]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}
,
\Lambda
,
\lambda
,
\alpha
,
\varepsilon
,
\kappa
, Hölder constants, and dimensions.

Recall the fractional derivative
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
from Section
2
. We use the representation of
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
as a hypersingular integral to show the following commutator estimates.

Lemma 6.1

(Commutator estimates)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume that
b
is a smooth and bounded scalar function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then the commutator
[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]
, initially defined on
\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}
, extends to a bounded operator on
\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
,
and satisfies the estimate

(67)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}).

Moreover, if
p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
, then, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(68)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\varepsilon\|b\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\qquad(f\in\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}).

Proof.

Observe that, since
[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]\colon\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha,p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}
is bounded, it suffices, in virtue of density and Fatou’s lemma, to establish (
67
) for
f
smooth and bounded.

According to
[
42
, Sec. 25.4]
, the fractional derivative
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
acts on bounded and smooth functions
g
as the
hypersingular integral
given for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(69)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g(x)=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{g(y)-g(x)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

We can apply this identity to
f
and
bf
in virtue of the assumption on
b
and the reduction at the beginning of this proof. Consequently, the commutator can be written as

(70)

\displaystyle[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]f(x)

\displaystyle=c\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{b(y)f(y)-b(x)f(x)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y-b(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)

(71)

\displaystyle=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(b(y)-b(x))f(y)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Let
h\leq 1
and split the integral into the regions
|y-x|\leq h
and
|y-x|\geq h
to rewrite the latest expression as

(72)

\displaystyle c\int_{|y-x|\geq h}\frac{(b(y)-b(x))f(y)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y+c\int_{|y-x|\leq h}\frac{(b(y)-b(x))f(y)}{|y-x|^{d+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\eqqcolon\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}.

Use boundedness of
b
for term
\mathrm{I}
to estimate
|\mathrm{I}|\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\geq h}|\cdot|^{-d-\alpha}\ast|f|\bigr)(x)
. Note that
\|\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\geq h}|\cdot|^{-d-\alpha}\|_{1}=h^{-\alpha}\|\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\geq 1}|\cdot|^{-d-\alpha}\|_{1}\lesssim h^{-\alpha}
by scaling.

Part 1
: Hölder coefficients.
We specify
h=1
. Use Hölder-regularity of
b
to bound
|\mathrm{II}|\lesssim[b]_{\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\leq 1}|\cdot|^{-d+\varepsilon}\ast|f|\bigr)(x)
. The convolution kernel
\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq 1}|y|^{-d+\varepsilon}
is likewise integrable. In summary, Young’s convolution inequality yields the claim.

Part 2
: Sobolev coefficients.
Write
|y-x|^{\alpha}=|y-x|^{-\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}|y-x|^{\alpha+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}
, and use Hölder’s inequality to estimate

(73)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{II}|\leq\Bigl(\int_{|y-x|\leq 1}|y-x|^{-d+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon p^{\prime}}}{{4}}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl(\int_{|y-x|\leq h}\left|\frac{|b(y)-b(x)||f(y)|}{|y-x|^{\alpha+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}}\right|^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x|^{d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The first factor is bounded by a constant depending on
d
,
p
, and
\varepsilon
. Using this estimate and the bound for
\mathrm{I}
in conjunction with Young’s convolution inequality yields

(74)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim h^{-\alpha}\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|y-x|\leq h}\left|\frac{(b(y)-b(x))f(y)}{|y-x|^{\alpha+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}}\right|^{p}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|y-x|^{d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now use Hölder’s inequality with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}-\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{d}}\eqqcolon\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}
(observe that
q
is finite by the assumption
p<\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
) to bound the second term in (
74
) by

(75)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|y-x|\leq h}\left|\frac{b(y)-b(x)}{|y-x|^{\alpha+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}}}\right|^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|y-x|^{d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\times\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|y-x|\leq h}|f(y)|^{q}|y-x|^{\nicefrac{{q\varepsilon}}{{4}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|y-x|^{d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

The first factor is dominated by the
\mathrm{B}^{\alpha+\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}}_{\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}},\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}
norm of
b
, which in turn is under control by the
\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}
norm of
b
. Furthermore, the second factor is controlled by
h^{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{q}}
in virtue of Fubini’s theorem. Finally, we bound
\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{q}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}
using boundedness of the fractional integral, see for instance
[
42
, Thm. 2.5.2]
, where we use again the restriction on
p
.

Plugging everything back into (
74
) gives

(76)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{x}^{\alpha},b]f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}\lesssim h^{-\alpha}\|b\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}+h^{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}}\|b\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p}}.

Choosing
h
sufficiently small gives the claim.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Given a weak
(p,q)
-solution
u
of (
P’
) for some right-hand side
f\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})
, one has higher spatial regularity in the sense
u\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p})
together with the estimate

(77)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{W}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{1+\alpha,p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(w;\mathrm{L}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{p})}.

Proof.

The proof divides into four steps.

Step 1
: Regularization of the equation.
Let
\rho\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be positive with integral one and define the usual mollifier sequence
\rho_{n}(x)\coloneqq n^{d}\rho(nx)
. Put
B_{n}\coloneqq\rho_{n}\ast_{x}B
, where
\ast_{x}
denotes convolution in the
x
-variable. One has

(78)

\displaystyle B_{n}(t,x)\xi\cdot\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n}(y)B(t,x-y)\xi\cdot\eta\,\mathrm{d}y\qquad(\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{dm}),

hence
B_{n}
is elliptic with the same bounds as
B
. In conjunction with the calculation

(79)

\displaystyle\|B_{n}(t,x)-B_{n}(t,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n}(z)\|B(t,x-z)-B(t,y-z)\|\,\mathrm{d}z

(80)

\displaystyle\leq M|x-y|^{\varepsilon},

this shows that
B_{n}
is again in the class
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda,\lambda,\varepsilon,M)
. If
p\geq\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}
, the calculation in (
79
) moreover shows
B_{n}\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon})
, where the norm is controlled by
M
. Otherwise,
B_{n}\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathrm{H}_{\vphantom{t}x}^{\alpha+\varepsilon,\nicefrac{{d}}{{\alpha}}})
, since the Bessel potential commutes with mollification. Similarly to (
79
), we derive for fixed
n
using smoothness of
\rho
that
B_{n}
is Lipschitz in the
x
variable uniformly in
t
.
Now, according to Theorem
4.1
, there exist unique weak

The solutions

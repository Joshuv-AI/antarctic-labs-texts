On length-preserving and area-preserving inverse curvature flow of planar curves with singularities Thanks: This work is supported by the Fundamental Research Funds for the Central Universities (Nos.3132023202, 3132022206).
Yunlong Yang, Yanwen Zhao, Jianbo Fang, Yanlong Zhang

Abstract
. This paper aims to investigate the evolution problem for planar curves with singularities. Motivated by the inverse curvature flow introduced by Li and Wang (Calc. Var. Partial Differ. Equ. 62 (2023), No. 135), we intend to consider the area-preserving and length-preserving inverse curvature flow with nonlocal term for ℓ \ell -convex Legendre curves. For the area-preserving flow, an ℓ \ell -convex Legendre curve with initial algebraic area A 0 > 0 A_{0}>0 evolves to a circle of radius A 0 π \sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{\pi}} . For the length-preserving flow, an ℓ \ell -convex Legendre curve with initial algebraic length L 0 L_{0} evolves to a circle of radius L 0 2 ​ π \frac{L_{0}}{2\pi} . As the by-product, we obtain some geometric inequalities for ℓ \ell -convex Legendre curves through the length-preserving flow.

1.
Introduction

The evolution problem for curves is a fundamental problem in geometry and topology, which has gained much attention in pure mathematics and has been widely applied in fields such as computer vision, image processing, and material science. One of the most well-known models may be the curve shortening flow of planar curves which is equivalent to a nonlinear parabolic equation for curvature. Normally, the Frenet frame cannot be built at singular points, so it is quite difficult to define the evolution problem for curves with singularities even in the plane.

The aim of this paper is to study evolution problems for some planar curves with singularities. Before introducing the models of this paper, we first illustrate basic definitions and notation about curves with singularities in order to comprehend associated evolution problems.

1.1.
Legendre curves

The curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
is referred to an
Legendre curve
, if there exists a unit vector
field
\nu:I\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfying

(1.1)

\langle\gamma^{\prime}(\theta),\nu(\theta)\rangle=0

for any
\theta\in I
.
Here,
\gamma^{\prime}(\theta)=\frac{d\gamma}{d\theta}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product.
Notably, when the vector field
\nu
is smooth, this special class of curves is termed
frontal
as introduced in
[
9
]
.
This definition allows us to establish the Frenet frame even at singular points.

Let
\gamma
be a frontal.
In this context, the pair
({\nu(\theta),\mu(\theta)})
, satisfying
\mu(\theta)=J(\nu(\theta))
, constitutes a moving frame along the curve
\gamma(\theta)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
J
represents a counterclockwise rotation of
\frac{\pi}{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The Frenet formula for
\gamma
is defined as:

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu^{\prime}(\theta)\\
\mu^{\prime}(\theta)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\ell(\theta)\\
-\ell(\theta)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu(\theta)\\
\mu(\theta)\end{array}\right),

Here,
\ell(\theta)=\langle\nu^{\prime}(\theta),\mu(\theta)\rangle
, and there exists a smooth function
\beta
such that

\gamma^{\prime}(\theta)=\beta(\theta)\mu(\theta).

Consequently, a Legendre curve
\gamma
is regular if and only if
\beta
never vanishes. The pair
(\ell,\beta)
serves as a crucial invariant for Legendre curves, commonly referred to as the
curvature pair
of Legendre curves, as discussed in
[
10
]
. A straightforward computation reveals the relationship between the conventional curvature
\kappa
and
\beta
for Legendre curves, specifically,
\kappa=\frac{\ell}{|\beta|}
.
Also, it is easy to see that the singular points on a Legendre curve satisfy
\beta=0
. Furthermore, if both
\ell
and
\beta
maintain consistent sign, the Legendre curve is convex, and vice versa, as detailed in
[
10
]
. In essence, Legendre curves can be seen as an extension of the convex curves.
When
\ell>0
, an Legendre curve is denoted as an
\ell
-convex Legendre curve
. Remarkably, as established in
[
33
, Lemma 3.2]
, a Legendre curve can be transformed into one with
\ell=1
through reparametrization. Therefore, for the sake of simplicity, we may exclusively focus on Legendre curves with
\ell=1
.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an
\ell
-convex Legendre curve with
\ell=1
. According to
[
33
, Lemma 3.3]
, the curve
\gamma
can be expressed as

(1.2)

\gamma(\theta)=p(\theta)(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)+p^{\prime}(\theta)(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),

Here,
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
is denoted as the unit vector field
\nu
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The function
p(\theta)
serves the same role as the support function for a convex curve and is still called the
support function
for the
\ell
-convex Legendre curve
\gamma
in

[
33
]
.
Notably,
p(\theta)>0
and
p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta)>0
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, curve
\gamma
turns into a convex curve. Moreover, in line with
[
33
, Remark 3.5]
, we have

(1.3)

\beta(\theta)=p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta).

Much like convex curves, the algebraic length
L
and the algebraic area
A
of
\gamma
can be defined, as detailed in
[
33
, Definition 3.4]
, by the following expressions

(1.4)

\displaystyle L=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta))d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}p(\theta)d\theta,

(1.5)

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}p(\theta)(p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta))d\theta.

It’s worth noting that replacing
p
with
-p
only alters the sign of
L
while keeping the sign of
A
. Therefore, we may pay attention to
\ell
-convex curves with
L\geq 0
.
Additionally, it’s important to highlight that the algebraic area
A
of a Legendre curve may be positive, zero, or even negative values.

If we consider the
\ell
-convex curve
\gamma
with a support function expressed as

(1.6)

p(\theta)=a_{0}+\sum_{k\geq 1}(a_{k}\cos k\theta+b_{k}\sin k\theta),

then, through integration by parts and Parseval’s identity, we obtain

(1.7)

\displaystyle L=2\pi a_{0},

(1.8)

\displaystyle A=\pi a_{0}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}(1-k^{2})(a_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{2}),

see e.g.
[
33
]
.
Additionally, the coefficients
a_{1}
and
b_{1}

appeared in (
1.6
) own great significance in determining the position of the curve
\gamma
.
Concretely,

a_{1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p(\theta)\cos\theta d\theta

and

b_{1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p(\theta)\sin\theta d\theta.

The point
(a_{1},b_{1})
is commonly referred to be the
Steiner point
for convex curves, see
[
43
, p.50]
.

Due to the isoperimetric inequality for
\ell
-convex Legendre curves established in
[
33
, Theorem 4.1]
, if its algebraic length
L
of such a curve is equal to zero, then its algebraic area
A
must be a negative value, unless the curve degenerates into a single point. However, when
L
is greater than zero, the situation for
A
becomes notably intricate. For instance, consider the support function of
\gamma
in the form of
p(\theta)=2+\sin 2\theta
.
In this case, the algebraic area
A
equals to
\frac{5\pi}{2}
.
Alternatively, if
p(\theta)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}+\sin 2\theta
, the corresponding
A
evaluates to zero.
Lastly, for the support function
p(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}+\sin 2\theta
, the associated algebraic area
A
becomes
-\frac{5\pi}{4}
; see Figure
1
.

1.2.
Evolution problems and main theorems

Inspired by the impressive inverse curvature flow researched by Li and Wang
[
33
]
,
we intend to consider the evolution problem
for
\ell
-convex Legendre curves as follows

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}=\frac{\ell(\beta-\lambda(t))^{\prime}}{\ell^{2}+\beta^{2}}\mu+(\beta-\lambda(t))\nu,\\
\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=-\frac{\beta(\beta-\lambda(t))^{\prime}}{\ell^{2}+\beta^{2}}\mu,\end{cases}

where
\lambda(t)
is a nonlocal term.

The first purpose of this paper is to study nonlocal
flows with
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta

or
\lambda(t)=\frac{L}{2\pi}
.
From the evolution equations for
L
and
A
(see Lemma
2.1
), the flow (
1.9
) is area-preserving
when
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta
, and it is length-preserving when
\lambda(t)=\frac{L}{2\pi}
.

For the area-preserving flow, one can get

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0}
be an initial
\ell
-convex Legendre curve with
\ell(\theta,0)=1
. If
the algebraic area of
\gamma_{0}
is positive, then for
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta
, the flow (
1.9
) exists in the time interval
[0,+\infty)
. Meanwhile, the evolving curve is still
\ell
-convex, preserves the algebraic area, and converges
to a circle of radius
\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{\pi}}

in the
C^{\infty}
sense as time
t
goes to infinity.

Remark 1.2
.

The initial algebraic area
A_{0}>0
, as mentioned in the area-preserving flow of Theorem
1.1
, stands as an indispensable prerequisite for the evolving
\ell
-convex Legendre curves. In fact, if
A_{0}\leq 0
, there is the possibility that
L_{0}=0
. This makes nonlocal term
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta
no sense.
For example, consider that the support function of
\gamma_{0}
is
p_{0}(\theta)=\sin\theta+2\cos\theta
.
In this case, the algebraic area
A_{0}=0
.
And if the support function
p_{0}(\theta)=\sin\theta+2\cos\theta+\sin 2\theta+2\cos 2\theta
, its algebraic area
A_{0}<0
. In both situations, the associated algebraic length
L_{0}=0
.

For the length-preserving flow, one has

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\gamma_{0}
be an initial
\ell
-convex Legendre curve with
\ell(\theta,0)=1
. For
\lambda(t)=\frac{L}{2\pi}
, the flow (
1.9
) exists in the time interval
[0,+\infty)
. Meanwhile, the evolving curve is still
\ell
-convex, preserves the algebraic length and converges
to a circle of radius
\frac{L_{0}}{2\pi}
.
Specially, if
L_{0}=0
, then it converges to a point.

In essence, the flow (
1.9
) is an inverse flow for
\ell
-convex Legendre curves. In particular, when the initial curve becomes a convex curve, this flow represents Gao-Pan-Tsai’s area-preserving model
[
14
]
when
\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta
, and corresponds to Pan-Yang’s length-preserving flow
[
38
]
when
\lambda(t)=\frac{L}{2\pi}
. Unlike previous work on the inverse curvature flow for convex curves, see
[
13
,
14
]
, the long-term existence of the length-preserving and area-preserving flow for
\ell
-convex Legendre curves does not necessitate additional curvature conditions.
More insights into the inverse curvature flow for convex curves can be found in
[
29
]
, and the related literature therein.

As a natural extension of this research, Gage
[

For an
\ell
-convex Legendre curve
\gamma
with algebraic length

L
and algebraic area
A
,
Li and Wang
[
33
]
have established a set of geometric inequalities:

(1.10)

\displaystyle L^{2}-4\pi A\geq 0,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta-2A\geq 0,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta-2A-8\left(\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi}-A\right)\geq 0,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{\prime 2}d\theta-2\left(\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi}-A\right)\geq 0.

Inequality (
1.10
) constitutes the isoperimetric inequality for
\ell
-convex Legendre curves.
Differing from the classical isoperimetric inequality, it remains valid even for non-simple curves. Notably, the equalities in (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) hold if and only if
\gamma
is a circle, while the equalities in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) hold if and only if the support function of
\gamma
follows the form

(1.14)

p(\theta)=a_{0}+a_{1}\cos\theta+b_{1}\sin\theta+a_{2}\cos 2\theta+b_{2}\sin 2\theta.

The proofs of these inequalities rely on
Fourier series.
For additional geometric inequalities related to convex curves, we refer the readers to
[

31

and the references therein. It should be noted that if the support function of an

-convex Legendre curve
is form (

2.
Some facts and basic lemmas

As mentioned in
[
33
]
, in order to investigate the inverse curve flow for
\ell
-convex curves while preserving the Legendrian condition (
1.1
), the pair
(\gamma,\nu)
must be considered, as opposed to solely
\gamma
. This involves the following equations

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}=\frac{\ell f^{\prime}}{\ell^{2}+\beta^{2}}\mu+f\nu,\\
\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=-\frac{\beta f^{\prime}}{\ell^{2}+\beta^{2}}\mu,\end{cases}

where
f
is a smooth function about the evolving Legendre curve
\gamma
.
It is worth noting that, since altering the tangential vector field does not affect the flow, for the sake of simplifying the associated analysis, one can introduce an appropriate tangential vector field
(\frac{\beta^{2}f^{\prime}}{\ell(\ell^{2}+\beta^{2})}\mu,\frac{\beta f^{\prime}}{(\ell^{2}+\beta^{2})}\mu)
(see
[
33
, p.20]
) such that

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{\ell}\mu+f\nu,\\
\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial t}=0.\end{cases}

Following some straightforward calculations, one can deduce

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial t}=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial t}=0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial t}=\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)^{\prime}+f\ell.

As a direct consequence of (
2.4
), it follows that
\ell(\theta,t)=1
if the initial curve is with
\ell(\theta,0)=1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
\ell(\theta,0)=1
.
Based on the discussions above and through elementary computations, similar to those outlined in
[
33
, pp.133-135]
, we can derive the evolution equations for essential geometric quantities.

Lemma 2.1
.

The evolution equations for the support function
p
, algebraic length
L
, algebraic area
A
and the quantity
\beta
are

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=f,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{dL}{dt}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}fd\theta,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{dA}{dt}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta fd\theta,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial t}=f_{\theta\theta}+f.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under flow (
2.2
) when
f=\beta-\lambda(t)

and
\lambda(t)
is a nonlocal term, the evolving curve is always
\ell
-convex.

Proof.

In order to show that
\gamma(\theta,t)
is still
\ell
-convex, by
[
33
, Remark 3.5]
, we only need to check

(2.10)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,t)\cos\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,t)\sin\theta d\theta=0.

By (
2.9
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\cos\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta_{t}\cos\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta_{\theta\theta}+\beta-\lambda(t)\right)\cos\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\cos\theta d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\cos\theta d\theta-\lambda(t)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\theta d\theta=0.

Similarly,
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\sin\theta d\theta=0
. Again from the fact that
\gamma_{0}
is
\ell
-convex, it yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,t)\cos\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,0)\cos\theta d\theta=0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,t)\sin\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(\theta,0)\sin\theta d\theta=0,

which concludes the desired result.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Under flow (
2.2
) when
f=\beta-\lambda(t)

and
\lambda(t)
is a nonlocal term, then

(2.11)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{(i)}\cos\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{(i)}\sin\theta d\theta=0

holds for
i\geq 1
, where
\beta^{(i)}
represents the
i
-th derivative
of
\beta
.

Proof.

For
i=1
, from integration by parts and (
2.10
), it yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta_{\theta}\cos\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\sin\theta d\theta=0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta_{\theta}\sin\theta d\theta=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\cos\theta d\theta=0.

Assume that the assertion of (
2.11
) holds
for
i-1
. By the induction hypothesis, one can compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{(i)}\cos\theta d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{(i-1)}\sin\theta d\theta=0.

In the same way,
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{(i)}\sin\theta d\theta=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Under flow (
2.2
) when
f=\beta-\lambda(t)
,
if
\lambda(t)
has uniform bounds, then
this flow exists in the time interval
[0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{0}

is an
\ell
-convex Legendre curve,
|\beta(\theta,0)|\leq M_{0}

holds for a constant
M_{0}
only depending on
\gamma_{0}
.
Equation (
2.9
) is uniformly parabolic when

\lambda(t)
is uniformly bounded.
By the maximum principle for uniformly parabolic equations, one has
|\beta(\theta,t)|\leq M_{0}

on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T)
. From the standard regularity theory for uniformly parabolic
equations (see Krylov
[
30
]
), it yields that

|\beta^{(i)}|\leq M_{i}

holds for
i\geq 1
, where

M_{i}
is a constant independent of time. Hence, the flow (
2.2
) exists in the time interval
[0,+\infty)
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Under flow (
2.2
) when
f=\beta-\lambda(t)
,
if the evolving curve is always
\ell
-convex
and the flow exists in the time interval
[0,+\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\left|\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right|\leq\Lambda_{1}e^{-3t},

where
\Lambda_{1}
is a constant only depending on

\gamma_{0}
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.9
) and integration by parts that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)\left(\beta_{t}-\frac{L_{t}}{2\pi}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)\left(\beta_{t}-\frac{L_{t}}{2\pi}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)\left(\beta_{\theta\theta}+\beta-\lambda(t)-\frac{L_{t}}{2\pi}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)_{\theta}^{2}d\theta+2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}-\lambda(t)-\frac{L_{t}}{2\pi}\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)d\theta

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\beta-\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)d\theta=0,

Proposition
4.4
deduces that the evolving curve
\gamma(\cdot,t)
cannot escape to infinity. Then, Proposition
4.2

implies that the evolving curve converges to a circle of radius
\frac{L_{0}}{2\pi}
and the limit circle becomes a point when
L_{0}=0
. At last, Lemma
4.3

says that the flow (
4.1
) converges
in the
C^{\infty}
sense as time
t
goes to
infinity.
∎

5.
Some applications

In this section, we obtain some geometric
inequalities for

\ell
-convex Legendre curves via the length-preserving inverse curvature flow (
4.1
).

First, we show the isoperimetric inequality
for
\ell
-convex curves.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\gamma
be an
\ell
-convex curve with algebraic length
L
and algebraic area
A
,
then

(5.1)

L^{2}-4\pi A\geq 0

with equality if and only if
\gamma

is a circle.

Proof.

Under flow (
4.1
), since it is
length-preserving, from Lemma
4.1
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
the quantity
U=L^{2}-4\pi A
is strict decreasing
unless the evolving curve is a circle.
Thus,
U(t)\geq U(\infty)=0
and equality holds
if and only if the evolving curve is a circle.
This deduces the desired result.
∎

Next, we show the generalizations
for inequalities (
1.11
), (
1.12
)
and (
1.13
).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\gamma
be an
\ell
-convex curve of algebraic length
L

(5.2)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}(\theta)d\theta\geq 2A+\tau\left(\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi}-A\right)

holds for any
\tau\leq 8
,
and the equality holds if
\gamma
is a circle.
Moreover, for any
\tau<8
, if the equality in (
5.2
) holds, then
\gamma
is a circle,
and for
\tau=8
, if the equality holds,
then the support function of
\gamma
is of the form
p(\theta)=a_{0}+a_{1}\cos\theta+b_{1}\sin\theta+a_{2}\cos 2\theta+b_{2}\sin 2\theta
.

Proof.

Consider the quantity
W=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta-2A-\tau\left(\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi}-A\right)
. Compute that

\displaystyle\frac{dW}{dt}=2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta\beta_{t}d\theta+(\tau-2)A_{t}.

From (
2.5
), integration by parts and
(
4.2
), the above expression
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{dW}{dt}=-2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta_{\theta}^{2}d\theta+\tau A_{t}.

Together with (
2.12

1.4
) and (
4.2

\displaystyle\frac{dW}{dt}\leq(\tau-8)A_{t}=(\tau-8)\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta^{2}d\theta-\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}\right).

\tau<8

W

W(t)\geq W(\infty)=0

5.2
) and with equality holds if and only if the evolving curve is a circle.

Note that the inequality (
2.12

.
This implies that the quantity
W
is strict decreasing in the case
\tau=8
. Hence,

W(t)\geq W(\infty)=0
, that is, (
5.2
)
holds and with equality if and only if the support function of evolving curve
is of form
p(\theta,t)=\frac{L}{2\pi}+a_{1}(t)\cos\theta+b_{1}(t)\sin\theta+a_{2}(t)\cos 2\theta+b_{2}(t)\sin 2\theta
.
∎

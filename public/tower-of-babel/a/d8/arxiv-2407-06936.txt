Robust Partial Least Squares Using Low Rank and Sparse Decomposition
Farwa Abbas Hussain Ahmad Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering School of Computer and Mathematical Sciences Imperial College London University of Adelaide, Australia f.abbas20@imperial.ac.uk hussain.ahmad@adelaide.edu.au

Abstract
This paper proposes a framework for simultaneous dimensionality reduction and regression in the presence of outliers in data by applying low-rank and sparse matrix decomposition. For multivariate data corrupted with outliers, it is generally hard to estimate the true low dimensional manifold from corrupted data. The objective of the proposed framework is to find a robust estimate of the low dimensional space of data to reliably perform regression. The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm is demonstrated experimentally for simultaneous regression and dimensionality reduction in the presence of outliers in data.



I
Introduction

In classical Multivariate Linear Regression (MLR), the objective is to model relationship between response variables
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
and predictors
{\boldsymbol{X}}
by fitting a linear map
\mathbf{\Theta}
to observed samples such that
{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}={\boldsymbol{X}}\mathbf{\Theta}
where
{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}
is the measurement error. The solution to this problem i.e.
\hat{\mathbf{\Theta}}=({\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{X}})^{-1}{\boldsymbol{X}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Y}}
can be found by solving the following program.

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}},\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{F}^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}={\boldsymbol{X}}\mathbf{\Theta}.

However, if the model is also prone to error then accounting for both the measurement and modelling errors in the equation
{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}=({\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}})\mathbf{\Theta}
using Total Least Squares (TLS) corresponds to solving the objective mentioned below.

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}},\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{F}^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}=({\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}})\mathbf{\Theta}.

The above program tries to find a linear map from
{\boldsymbol{X}}
to
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
to model the relationship between predictors and responses while penalizing the errors in both
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
. In most practical cases, there are strong correlations between subsets of predictors. Therefore, they tend to span a low dimensional subspace and hence the covariance matrix turns out to be singular, making the linear regression problem ill-conditioned
[
1
]
. To resolve this problem, one possible solution is to project each sample onto the low-dimensional subspace spanned by uncorrelated predictors and form a smaller set of new orthogonal predictors, each being the linear combination of original set of predictors. This can be done by finding singular value decomposition of
{\boldsymbol{X}}
such that
{\boldsymbol{X}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}
where
{\boldsymbol{Q}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}
are the scores and loadings matrices respectively. The new set of orthogonal predictors in
{\boldsymbol{Q}}
can be regressed to
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
by regression equation
{\boldsymbol{Y}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}\mathbf{\Theta}+{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}
and the solution to this new problem is
\tilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}=({\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Q}})^{-1}{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Y}}
. It must be noted that our new covariance matrix is now invertible even in the presence of strong correlations between predictors. This is the main idea behind Principal Component Regression (PCR)
[
2
]
. However PCA, being an unsupervised dimensionality reduction approach, computes latent components while completely disregarding the information obtained from responses and hence, the predictive information can probably be discarded and noise may be preserved inducing a poor regression model
[
3
]
.

In order to estimate the low dimensional subspace by taking into account the information provided by both predictors and responses, we aim to find a low rank approximation of both
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
by solving the following optimization problem.

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}},\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{*}+\|{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{*}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }{\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}},{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}.\hskip 28.45274pt

We can argue that Partial Least Squares (PLS)
[
4
]
is an iterative algorithm that fundamentally tries to solve the above mentioned objective by maximizing the covariance between predictors and responses. It aims to find the first latent component
{\vec{t}}={\boldsymbol{X}}{\vec{w}}
by finding weights
{\vec{w}}
while maximizing the covariance
\operatorname{cov}({\boldsymbol{X}}{\vec{w}},{\boldsymbol{Y}})
subject to
{\vec{w}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\vec{w}}=1
. The subsequent latent components are found by singular value decomposition of residual matrix that is updated iteratively. Unlike conventional PLS method that computes the scores matrix as
{\boldsymbol{Q}}={\boldsymbol{X}}{\boldsymbol{W}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{W}})^{-1}
, we observe that it can rather be estimated in a flexible way by optimizing the objective in (
3
) without imposing any explicit form. Unlike PCA that induces an orthogonal projection
{\boldsymbol{Q}}({\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Q}})^{-1}{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}
matrix, PLS algorithm instead induces an oblique projection
{\boldsymbol{W}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{W}})^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}
matrix in a supervised way
[
5
]
. We argue that the optimization program in (
3
) tries to find the low dimensional manifold similar to the one found by PLS iterative algorithm without finding any oblique projections. Moreover, we claim that we can achieve the same objective without requiring a weight matrix
{\boldsymbol{W}}
for regression or dimensionality reduction.

Related Work

PLS algorithm has widely been used in chemometrics

]
, clinical chemistry

, industrial process control

. However, its effectiveness is restricted due its extreme sensitivity to outliers in data
[

]
. Several methods have been proposed in past decades to develop a robust PLS model that include
[

,

,
18
,

,
20
]
. Primarily, these methods have proposed to use a robust estimate of covariance matrix to mitigate the effect of outliers in data. Two most commonly used methods to estimate a robust covariance matrix are Orthogonalized Gnanadesikan-Kettenring (OGK)
[
21
]
and Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD)
[
22
]
. However, the utility of these methods is restricted to the cases where the number of samples are very large as compared to the number of predictors which is unrealistic in many situations
[
23
]
.

It can be observed that, instead of minimizing
\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{F}^{2}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{F}^{2}
as in conventional PLS method, we can minimize instead
\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{1}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{1}
to get rid of outliers in data. Motivated by this observation, we propose to employ a new low-rank and sparse decomposition framework for simultaneously decomposing both predictor and response matrices. We refer to our proposed algorithm as Robust PLS (RPLS). We have shown experimentally that by allowing sparse errors in both predictors and responses, the low-dimensional space can be discovered reliably in the presence of outliers.

In contrast to iterative PLS scheme, we propose a new way to find the decompositions
{\boldsymbol{X}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}+{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}+{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}
that does not involve any power method or oblique projections at all. We introduce a novel framework to perform regression by finding projections onto the latent space estimated by a robust method. More precisely, we make the assumption that after removing errors in both
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
, the low rank matrices
{\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}
can be related through regression equation as
{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{\Theta}
. Moreover, if
{\boldsymbol{Q}}
is constrained to have orthonormal columns i.e.
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Q}}={\boldsymbol{I}}
then our regression equation reduces to
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{\Theta}
. Our method is straightforward and also handles outliers in data. We show the usefulness of proposed framework for simultaneous regression and dimensionality reduction. We, further, show that by performing regression after projecting onto the low dimensional space, the response variable can be predicted in a reliable and robust way.

II-A

Our Contributions

In this paper, we propose a novel method to formulate PLS regression problem by introducing a low-rank and sparse decomposition framework. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that solves the PLS regression problem in an optimization framework by effectively penalizing the outliers in data. In particular, we make the following contributions:

1.

We propose a novel way to formulate PLS regression problem as a low rank and sparse decomposition problem by minimizing the proposed objective using Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM).

2.

We propose a regression approach based on projection onto a latent space.

3.

Finally, we demonstrate experimentally the superior performance of our proposed method on both synthetic and real world datasets corrupted with outliers.

Mathematical Formulation

Motivated by the approach discussed in
[
24
]
, we develop a robust PLS model in low-rank and sparse decomposition framework. More formally, we aim to find
{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
(such that
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Q}}={\boldsymbol{I}}
),
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times k}
and
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k}
such that
{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and
{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
are low-rank matrices where
k
is an upper bound on the rank of
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
i.e.,
\text{rank}({\boldsymbol{X}})\leq k
and
\text{rank}({\boldsymbol{Y}})\leq k
. We aim to find a low rank approximation for
{\boldsymbol{X}}
and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}
while ensuring the error matrices
{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}
to be sparse. The problem can be mathematically formulated as follows.

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{Q}},{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}},{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{1}+\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{1}+\lambda_{1}\|{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{*}+\lambda_{2}\|{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\text{ subject to }{\boldsymbol{X}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}+{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\text{ , }{\boldsymbol{Y}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}+{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\text{ and }{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}{\boldsymbol{Q}}={\boldsymbol{I}}.

(4)

4
). The partial augmented Lagrangian function of the program in (
4
) can be written as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{Q}},{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}},{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}},{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}},{\boldsymbol{L}},{\boldsymbol{M}})=\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{1}+\lambda_{1}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}\|_{*}+\|{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{1},

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}\|_{*}+\langle{\boldsymbol{L}},{\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\|{\boldsymbol{X}}-{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{x}}\|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle+\langle{\boldsymbol{M}},{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\|{\boldsymbol{Y}}-{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{y}}^{\mathrm{T}}-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{y}}\|_{F}^{2},

(5)

where

and

and

{\boldsymbol{Q}}

and
\lambda_{2}
are weights of the second and fourth terms respectively.

COMPUTATION COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS

In the above algorithm the computational cost in all the updates is primarily due to svd and matrix multiplications.
In equation (
6
), for matrix

the computational complexity of full svd is
\mathcal{O}(nk^{2})

(n>k)
. Similarly the next two updates (
7
) , (
8
) involve svd having costs

, and
\mathcal{O}(rk^{2})
respectively. The cost of matrix multiplications
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{(k+1)}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}}^{(k+1)\mathrm{T}}
in (

) and (

. Similarly, the cost associated with

V
EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

In this section we compare the performance of our proposed algorithm on both synthetic and real world datasets. The experiments on synthetic data show the performance on dimensionality reduction and the real dataset is evaluated for both dimensionality reduction and regression. All the experiments have been performed on MATLAB 2018a with Core i5 CPU (2.50 GHz), 8 GB RAM and Windows 10 operating system.

V-A

Results on Synthetic dataset

For randomly generated synthetic data, the predictor matrix is generated randomly having dimensions
150\times 40
i.e. we have
150

40

5

Sample size is deliberately made larger as compared to the number of predictors so that existing methods for robust PLS can be applied. We compare our results with traditional PLS and PLS models robustified by using frequently used robust estimates of covariance matrix i.e. PLS-OGK and PLS-MCD. Four responses are being generated from linear functions of randomly selected predictors and are then stacked in a

To simulate the effect of presence of outliers, we have introduced two different types of outliers in data. First ones are the sparse outliers, that are added randomly to some of the predictors and responses. Second type of outliers perturb lowest
10\%

VI
Conclusion

This paper proposed a novel framework for dimensionality reduction and regression by finding a low-rank and sparse decompositions for both predictors and responses. Experiments have shown that the proposed algorithm performs better as compared to the other techniques in presence of outliers and corruptions. We further show that our method approximates the regression function better than the existing algorithms and hence achieves smallest error while making predictions on unseen data in the presence of outliers.

Equidistribution of α ​ p θ \alpha p^{\theta} with a Chebotarev condition and applications to extremal primes
Amita Malik, Neha Prabhu

Abstract
. We establish a joint distribution result concerning the fractional part of α ​ p θ \alpha p^{\theta} for θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) , α > 0 \theta\in(0,1),\ \alpha>0 , where p p is a prime satisfying a Chebotarev condition in a fixed finite Galois extension over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . As an application, for a fixed non-CM elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} , an asymptotic formula is given for the number of primes at the extremes of the Sato-Tate measure modulo a large prime ℓ \ell . These are precisely the primes p p for which the Frobenius trace a p ​ ( E ) a_{p}(E) satisfies the congruence a p ​ ( E ) ≡ [ 2 ​ p ] mod ℓ a_{p}(E)\equiv[2\sqrt{p}]\bmod\ell . We assume a zero-free region hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions of number fields.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

For a real number
x
, let
[x]
and
\{x\}
denote the integer and fractional part of
x
, respectively.
The study of fractional parts of arithmetic sequences has been of great interest and importance. For instance, distribution of
\{\alpha\gamma\}
, where
\alpha
is a fixed non-zero real number, and
\gamma
runs over the imaginary parts of zeros of
L
-functions has been studied in various settings, see
[
Rad74
,
Hla75
,
Fuj93
,
MR20
]
. Another example would be of lacunary sequences and polynomials, in particular,
\{\alpha n^{2}\}
for which we refer the reader to
[
RSZ01
,
RZ02
,
Zah95
,
Wey16
]
and the references therein.

The study of effective equidistribution of
p^{\theta}
for
0<\theta<1
and related sequences such as
\{\alpha p^{\theta}+\beta\}
is important due to its connection to the infinitude of primes of the form
n^{2}+1
; this is equivalent to the statement that
\{\sqrt{p}\}<p^{-1/2}
holds for infinitely many primes
p
. Such sequences have been studied extensively, see
[
Bal83
,
Har83
,
Bai04
,
BH91
,
BK85
,
MS03
]
to name a few. A major breakthrough in this direction was achieved by Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
FI98
]
where they show that the polynomial
x^{2}+y^{4}
captures its primes, followed by Heath-Brown
[
HB01
]
for the primes of the form
x^{3}+2y^{3}
. More recently, there has been study of fractional parts of
\sqrt{p}
with
p
being of a certain type, namely
p+2\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
, the set of positive integers with at most
r
prime factors counted with multiplicity, see for example
[
Cai13
,
Mat09
,
SW13
]
.

In this paper, we study joint distribution for the fractional parts of prime powers where the primes also satisfy a Chebotarev condition. It is well-known that for
0<\theta<1
,

\#\{p\leq x:\{p^{\theta}\}<\delta\}\sim\delta\pi(x)

with a power saving error term. Here,
\pi(x)
denotes the number of primes not exceeding
x
.
On the other hand, for a fixed Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
and a union of conjucgacy classes
C
in
G:=\mbox{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
, the celebrated Chebotarev Density Theorem implies that

\#\{p\leq x:p\text{ unramified in }L,\sigma_{p}\in C\}\sim\frac{|C|}{|G|}\pi(x)

as
x\to\infty
, where
\sigma_{p}
is the conjugacy class of the Frobenius automorphism associated with any prime in
L
lying above
p
. Here the focus is on the distribution of
\{\alpha p^{\theta}\}
with
\sigma_{p}\in C
, which we now describe. To simplify exposition, henceforth when we write
\sigma_{p}\in C
, we assume that
p
is unramified in
L
, unless mentioned otherwise.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\omega\geq 1
,
0\leq\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}\leq 1
with
\delta:=\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
be fixed. Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension with
n_{L}=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
. For
\theta/2<\Delta\leq 1/2
fixed, assume that
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no zeros in the region
\Re s>1-\Delta

and for
\omega\geq 1
,

\alpha^{1/4}(\omega n_{L}/\delta)^{1/2}(\log x)^{2}\ll_{\theta}{x^{\Delta/2-\theta/4}}.

Then the following holds uniformly in
\delta,\alpha
and
\omega
.

\displaystyle\#\{x<p\leq 2x:\delta_{1}\leq\{\alpha p^{\theta}\}<\delta_{2}\text{ and }\sigma_{p}\in C\}-\delta\frac{|C|}{|G|}\pi(x)

\displaystyle\quad\ll_{\theta}\frac{|C|}{|G|}n_{L}\log x\left(\frac{(\delta\omega)^{1/2}\alpha^{1/4}}{n_{L}^{1/2}}{x^{1-\Delta/2+\theta/4}}+\frac{\delta\omega}{\alpha^{1/2}}x^{1-\theta/2}\log x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\delta n_{L}\omega)^{1/2}\alpha^{1/4}{x^{\Delta/2+\theta/4}}\log x+\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{\delta x}{\omega\log x}.

We note that for all our results, setting
\Delta=1/2
is equivalent to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for
\zeta_{L}(s)
. However, we do obtain a power saving over the main term even for weaker zero-free regions, as long as
\Delta>\theta/2
.

Setting
\alpha=1,\theta=1/2
, and
\delta_{1}=0
in Theorem
1.1
, one obtains the following generalization of
[
Bal85
]
that investigates the distribution of fractional parts of
p^{\theta}
with
p\equiv a\bmod q
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume the notations and hypotheses as in Theorem
1.1
. Then

\displaystyle\#\{x<p\leq 2x:\{\sqrt{p}\}<\delta\text{ and }\sigma_{p}\in C\}-\delta\pi_{C}(x,L)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}\left((\delta\omega n_{L})^{1/2}x^{{9/8-\Delta/2}}\log x+n_{L}\delta\omega x^{3/4}(\log x)^{2}+\frac{\delta x}{\omega\log x}\right).

The above results are of a similar flavour as some other interesting joint distribution theorems such as asymptotics for the number of Charmichael numbers composed of primes satisfying the Chebotarev condition studied in
[
BGY13
]
. In
[
AG15
]
, an asymptotic estimate for the number of Piatetski-Shapiro primes up to
x
satisfying the Chebotarev condition is derived.

Using essentially the same ideas as in Theorem
1.1
, one can prove a similar result, stated below, where the bounds for the fractional part of the prime are themselves a function of the prime.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha,\lambda>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
be fixed. Consider a finite Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
and let
n_{L}=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
and
d_{L}
denote the absolute discriminant of
L
. For
\theta/2<\Delta\leq 1/2
fixed, assume that
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no zeros in the region
\Re s>1-\Delta
. Then, for
\omega\geq 1
, as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\#\{x<p\leq 2x:\{\alpha p^{\theta}\}<p^{-\lambda}\text{ and }\sigma_{p}\in C\}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq 2x\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}p^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{x^{1-\lambda}}{\omega\log x}+\omega{\alpha^{1/2}}\ x^{\theta/2}\log d_{L}\log^{3}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|C|}{|G|}{\alpha^{1/2}}\log^{3}x\left(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log x\right)\left(\omega^{2}x^{{1-\Delta+\theta/2}}+\frac{\omega x^{1-\lambda-\theta/2}}{\alpha}\right).

Setting
\theta=1/2,\alpha=1
and
\lambda=1/4-\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
, we obtain the following asymptotic result, in the spirit of Landau’s prime counting problem where the primes satisfy
\{\sqrt{p}\}<p^{-1/2}
i.e.,
p-1
is a perfect square.

Corollary 2
.

Assume the notations and hypotheses in Theorem
1.2
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\#\{x<p\leq 2x:\{\sqrt{p}\}<p^{-1/4+\epsilon}\text{ and }\sigma_{p}\in C\}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq 2x\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}p^{-1/4+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{x^{3/4+\epsilon}}{\omega\log x}+\omega x^{1/4}\log d_{L}\log^{3}x+\frac{|C|}{|G|}\omega^{2}x^{{5/4-\Delta}}\log^{3}x\left(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log x\right).

1.1.
Applications

Our main application of Theorem
1.1
is towards the extremal prime counting function for elliptic curves without complex multiplication (CM). Let
E
denote a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
N_{E}
. For a prime
p
of good reduction,
E
reduces to an elliptic curve over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and we denote by
a_{p}(E)
the trace of the Frobenius automorphism acting on the points of
E
over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
. Then,
a_{p}(E)=p+1-\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and the Hasse bound
|a_{p}(E)|\leq[2\sqrt{p}]
holds. As the name suggests, extremal primes are those for which
[a_{p}(E)]=\pm[2\sqrt{p}]
, i.e. the primes at the ends of the Sate-Tate distribution. It is not yet known whether there are infinitely many such primes for a single non-CM elliptic curve. We refer the reader to Section
3
for more details on the background and results on extremal primes in the literature. Using Theorem
1.1
, we prove the following result on primes satisfying the extremality condition modulo a large enough prime
\ell
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve and
L=\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])
denote the
\ell
-torsion field of
E
with
\ell
being a large prime. For
1/4<\Delta\leq 1/2
fixed, assume that
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no zeros in the region
\Re s>1-\Delta
. Then, for
\ell\ll x^{{2\Delta/9-1/18}}\omega^{-2/9}\log^{-8/9}x
,
\omega\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\#\left\{x<p\leq 2x:p\nmid N_{E},\;a_{p}(E)\equiv[2\sqrt{p}]\bmod\ell\right\}-\frac{\pi(x)}{\ell}

\displaystyle\quad\ll_{E}\frac{x}{\omega\ell\log x}+\omega^{1/2}\ell^{5/4}x^{{9/8-\Delta/2}}\log x+\omega\ell^{7/2}x^{3/4}(\log x)^{2}.

The inspiration to study the quantity in Theorem
1.3
comes from the work of Serre
[
Ser72
]
and Murty-Murty-Saradha
[
MMS88
]
towards the Lang-Trotter conjecture, which predicts that for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
fixed,

\#\left\{p\leq x:p\nmid N_{E},\,a_{p}(E)=t\right\}\sim C_{E,t}\ x^{1/2}/\log{x},\quad\text{as }x\to\infty,

where
C_{E,t}
is a constant depending on
E
and
t
. In the case of upper bounds towards the Lang-Trotter conjecture, better estimates are known when
a_{p}(E)=0
. In a similar spirit, we obtain better estimates for the joint distribution in the particular case of
a_{p}(E)\equiv 0\mod\ell
. To be precise, the following holds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses as in Theorem
1.3
and
\ell
be a large prime with
\ell\ll x^{2\Delta/7-1/14}(\log x)^{-8/7}\omega^{-2/7}
. Then

\displaystyle\#\left\{x<p\leq 2x:p\nmid N_{E},\ a_{p}(E)\equiv[2\sqrt{p}]\equiv 0\bmod\ell\right\}-\frac{\pi(x)}{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ll_{E}\frac{x}{\ell^{2}\omega\log x}+\frac{\omega^{1/2}x^{9/8-\Delta/2}\log x}{\ell^{1/4}}+\omega\ell^{3/2}x^{3/4}(\log x)^{2}.

Moreover, choosing
\omega=1
and
\ell=x^{{2\Delta/9-1/18}}\log^{-8/9}x
in Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following upper bound for the number of extremal primes up to
x
.

Corollary 3
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses as in Theorem
1.3
. Then for
x
large,

\left\{x<p\leq 2x:p\nmid N_{E},\;a_{p}(E)=[2\sqrt{p}]\right\}\ll_{E}x^{19/18-2\Delta/9}(\log x)^{-1/9}.

Remark 1
.

Similar results can be obtained for extremal primes at the other end, i.e. when
a_{p}(E)=-[2\sqrt{p}]
, using essentially the same arguments as presented here. Moreover, because of the generality in Theorem
1.1
, one can write versions of Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
3
where
\sqrt{p}
is replaced by
p^{\theta}
for
\theta<1/2
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. We begin with the joint distribution result adapting the ideas of Balog
[
Bal83
]
and Lagarias-Odlyzko
[
LO77
]
and prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in Section
2
. Extremal primes and proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
are discussed in Section
3
. Lastly, in Section
4
, we provide details of the results needed in Section
2
.

2.
Joint distribution results

In this section, we give proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Remark.
In what follows, we may assume that the parameters
\alpha
and
\theta
satisfy
\alpha x^{\theta}\geq 1
since for
\alpha x^{\theta}<1
,
\alpha x^{\theta}=\{\alpha x^{\theta}\}\in[\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, and therefore the desired quantity can be computed using the Prime Number Theorem.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

To start with, we capture the fractional parts in the desired interval as follows:

\displaystyle[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}]-[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\delta_{1}\leq\{\alpha p^{\theta}\}<\delta_{2}$;}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

First, we obtain the result when
x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}
for

x_{j}:=[x(1+j/B)]+1/2
for
j=0,\ldots,B
and
B=[\omega]
. Summing over
j
then establishes the result for
x<p\leq 2x
.

With
\delta=\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}
, the length of the interval, we set

\displaystyle U_{-}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha x_{j}^{\theta}}{\delta},\qquad U_{+}:=\frac{\alpha x_{j+1}^{\theta}}{\delta}.

Then for
x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}
, we have

\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{-}}\right)-\delta_{1}\leq\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{2}\leq\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{+}}\right)-\delta_{1}.

Note that we are interested in the sum

S:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}]-[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{2}]

in order to bound the number of primes
x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}
such that
\sigma_{p}\in C
and
\delta_{1}\leq\{\alpha p^{\theta}\}<\delta_{2}.
This implies

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{+}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]

and

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{-}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right].

Therefore, using

\frac{\alpha p^{\theta}}{U_{\pm}}=\delta+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\delta}{\omega}\right),

in order to obtain the claimed asymptotics for
S
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]-\frac{\alpha p^{\theta}}{U_{\pm}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log x\left((\delta n_{L}/\omega)^{1/2}\alpha^{1/4}x^{1-\Delta/2+\theta/4}+\frac{\delta n_{L}}{\alpha^{1/2}}x^{1-\theta/2}\log x\right)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\quad+(\delta n_{L}/\omega)^{1/2}\alpha^{1/4}x^{\Delta/2+\theta/4}\log x.

We may write the summand

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}1\ -\sum_{m\leq\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}}1.

Define the sequence
(a_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

a_{m}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\frac{1}{3}\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Set

A_{\delta_{1}}(M):=\sum_{m\leq M-\delta_{1}}a_{m}=\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}\ f\left(\frac{m+\delta_{1}}{M}\right)

where

f(y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }0<y<1\\
1/2&\text{ if }y=1\\
0&\text{ if }y>1.\end{cases}

Using the inverse Mellin transform, for
\sigma>0
, we write

f(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\frac{y^{-s}}{s}~ds.

For
y=(m+\delta_{1})/M
, this gives

(2.3)

A_{\delta_{1}}(M)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{a_{m}}{(m+\delta_{1})^{s}}\frac{M^{s}}{s}~dx.

Thus, using the definition of the sequence
(a_{m})
, we rewrite (
2.2
) as

\displaystyle\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}a_{m}\ -\sum_{m\leq(\alpha p^{\theta})\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}}a_{m}

\displaystyle=A_{\delta_{1}}(\alpha p^{\theta})-A_{\delta_{1}}\left(\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)\right).

In order to estimate the integrals
A_{\delta_{1}}(\alpha p^{\theta})
and
A_{\delta_{1}}\left(\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)\right)
, given by (
2.3
), we make use of the truncated Perron’s formula
[
Tit86
, Lemma 3.12]
, and obtain

\displaystyle\left[\alpha p^{\theta}-\delta_{1}\right]-\left[(\alpha p^{\theta})\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{1}}^{1/2+iT_{1}}L(s)H(s)\ p^{\theta s}~ds

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{O}\Bigg(\sum_{\frac{1}{3}\alpha x^{\theta}<m+\delta_{1}\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}}\!\min\left\{1,T_{1}^{-1}\left|\log\frac{\alpha p^{\theta}}{m+\delta_{1}}\right|^{-1}\right\}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{O}\Bigg(\sum_{\frac{1}{3}\alpha x^{\theta}<m+\delta_{1}\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}}\!\min\left\{1,T_{1}^{-1}\left|\log\frac{\alpha p^{\theta}}{m+\delta_{1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)\right|^{-1}\right\}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{1}}^{1/2+iT_{1}}L(s)H(s)p^{\theta s}~ds+\operatorname{O}\Bigg(\frac{\alpha x^{\theta}}{T_{1}}\log(\alpha x^{\theta})\Bigg),

where

H(s):=\frac{1}{s}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)^{s}\right)\ll\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}

and

L(s):=\alpha^{s}\sum_{\frac{1}{3}\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\frac{1}{(m+\delta_{1})^{s}}.

Note that
T_{1}
will be chosen so that
\alpha x^{\theta}\log(\alpha x^{\theta})\ll{\delta}T_{1}
so as to obtain a true error term. Summing over the primes, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\ -\delta_{1}\right]-\left[\alpha p^{\theta}\left(1-\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\right)-\delta_{1}\right]\Bigg)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.06749pt=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{1}}^{1/2+iT_{1}}L(s)H(s)F(-\theta s)~ds+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\alpha x^{\theta}}{T_{1}}\log(\alpha x^{\theta})\pi_{C}(x_{j},L)\right),

where

\displaystyle F(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}p^{-s}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\pi_{C}(x_{j},L):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}1.

First, we compute the above integral in the smaller range, up to
T_{0}:=\alpha x^{\theta}
. Observe that in the range
|t|\leq T_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle H(s)=\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{|s-1|}{U_{\pm}^{2}}\right)

and

L(s)=\alpha^{s}\frac{(3\alpha x^{\theta})^{1-s}-(\alpha x^{\theta}/3)^{1-s}}{1-s}+\operatorname{O}\left(x^{-\theta\Re(s)}\right).

Therefore, for the integral in (
2.4
) in the range
|t|\leq T_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{0}}^{1/2+iT_{0}}L(s)H(s)F(-\theta s)\,ds

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\alpha p^{\theta}\;\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{0}}^{1/2+iT_{0}}(\alpha p^{\theta})^{s-1}\frac{(3\alpha x^{\theta})^{1-s}-(\alpha x^{\theta}/3)^{1-s}}{1-s}~ds

(2.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\delta}{\alpha}x^{-3\theta/2}\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}|F(-\theta/2-i\theta t)|\;dt\right).

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the error term (
2.6
) is bounded by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\delta}{\alpha}x^{-3\theta/2}T_{0}^{1/2}\left(\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}|F(-\theta/2-i\theta t)|^{2}~dt\right)^{1/2}.

Also, by applying the mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials, we have

\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}\left|F(-\theta/2-i\theta t)\right|^{2}~dt\ll x^{\theta}\pi_{C}(x_{j},L)\;\left(T_{0}+x/\omega\right).

Inserting this estimate in (
2.7
), the error term (
2.6
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\ll\delta T_{0}^{1/2}(\alpha x^{\theta})^{-1}\pi_{C}(x_{j},L)^{1/2}\left(T_{0}^{1/2}+x^{1/2}/\omega^{1/2}\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\ll\delta\pi_{C}(x_{j},L)^{1/2}\left(1+x^{(1-\theta)/2}(\alpha\omega)^{-1/2}\right),

using
T_{0}=\alpha x^{\theta}
. We now compute the integral in (
2.5
) to obtain the desired main term. The change of variable
w=1-s
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{U_{\pm}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\alpha p^{\theta}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{0}}^{1/2+iT_{0}}\frac{1}{w}\left(\left(\frac{3x^{\theta}}{p^{\theta}}\right)^{w}-\left(\frac{x^{\theta}}{3p^{\theta}}\right)^{w}\right)~dw.

By Perron’s formula, for all values
x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}
,

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1/2-iT_{0}}^{1/2+iT_{0}}\frac{1}{w}\left(\left(\frac{3x^{\theta}}{p^{\theta}}\right)^{w}-\left(\frac{x^{\theta}}{3p^{\theta}}\right)^{w}\right)~dw=1+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{T_{0}}\right).

Therefore (
2.5
) becomes

(2.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<p\leq x_{j+1}\\

3.

In this section, we provide proofs of Theorems

Lemma 4.1

\int_{T^{\prime}}^{2T^{\prime}}|L(1/2+it)|^{2}~dt\ll\alpha T^{\prime}+{\alpha}^{2}x^{\theta}\log(\alpha x^{\theta}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{T^{\prime}}^{2T^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}(m+\delta_{1})^{-1}+\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<k\neq m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\frac{(k+\delta_{1})^{-1/2+it}}{(m+\delta_{1})^{1/2+it}}~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\frac{\alpha T^{\prime}}{(m+\delta_{1})}+\operatorname{O}\left(\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<k<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\frac{\alpha((m+\delta_{1})(k+\delta_{1}))^{-1/2}}{\log((m+\delta_{1})/(k+\delta_{1}))}\right).

and rewriting the sum in the error term, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\sum_{r=1}^{(m+\delta_{1})/9}\frac{\alpha}{(m+\delta_{1})\sqrt{1-r/(m+\delta_{1}})\log\left(1-r/(m+\delta_{1})\right)}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}\sum_{r=1}^{(m+\delta_{1})/9}\frac{\alpha}{r}\ll\alpha^{2}x^{\theta}\log(\alpha x^{\theta}).

This gives us

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha T^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha x^{\theta}/3-\delta_{1}<m\leq 3\alpha x^{\theta}-\delta_{1}}(m+\delta_{1})^{-1}+\operatorname{O}\left(\alpha^{2}x^{\theta}\log(\alpha x^{\theta})\right)

\displaystyle\ll

4.2.
Estimation of
F(-\theta/2-i\theta t)

For each sub-interval
[x_{j},x_{j+1}]\subset[x,2x]
we prove the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be fixed. Consider a finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
and a normal extension
L
over
K
with Galois group Gal
(L/K)=G
. Let
C
be a union of
b
conjugacy class in
G
, define

F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}<N(\mathfrak{p})\leq x_{j+1}\\
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in C\end{subarray}}(N\mathfrak{p})^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}.

Assume that the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no zeros in the strip
\Re s>1-\Delta
where
\theta/2<\Delta\leq 1/2
.
Then

\displaystyle F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}x^{1-\Delta+\theta/2}(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log T^{\prime})\left(\frac{\log T^{\prime}}{\log x}+\frac{x^{\Delta}}{T^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+x^{\theta/2}\left(\log d_{L}+bn_{K}+bn_{K}\frac{x\log x}{T^{\prime}}\right)

uniformly for
0<T^{\prime}\leq\tau\leq 2T^{\prime}
.

The proof follows along the same lines as in
[
LO77
]
. The function
F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)
here is similar to that of
\pi_{C}(x,L/K)
in
[
LO77
]
, the main difference being a shift in the complex variable
s=\sigma+it
by
\theta/2+i\theta\tau
. While the shift in the real part by
\theta/2
results in a factor of
x^{\theta/2}
tagging along with the error terms obtained in
[
LO77
]
, the shift in the imaginary part is where the saving is obtained. To be precise, we choose a contour that is a box which avoids the real axis, therefore the only poles in the interior are the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\chi,L/E)
, and a pole at
-\theta/2-i\theta\tau
. In particular, the residue from the pole at
s=1
that makes up the main term in the proof by Lagarias-Odlyzko
[
LO77
]
does not appear here, giving us a power saving under the assumed zero-free region hypothesis, which is equivalent to GRH when
\Delta=1/2
.

We now provide details of the proof.

Proof.

We first consider the function

\Psi_{C}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\mathfrak{p}^{m}\in(x_{j},x_{j+1}]\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{:unramified}\\
\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]^{m}=C\end{subarray}}\frac{\log N\mathfrak{p}}{N\mathfrak{p}^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}

for a single conjugacy class
C
. We use partial summation to pass on to the bounds for
F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)
. As in
[
LO77
]
, in order to use Hecke
L
-functions, we need to consider the ramified primes as well, (which are later removed). For
\Re s>1
, let

(4.1)

Z(s):=-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi}\bar{\chi}(g)\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi,L/E),

where
\chi
runs over the irreducible characters of
H=Gal(L/E)
and
E
is the fixed field of
H
, the cyclic subgroup of
G
generated by a chosen element
g\in C
. Note that

Z(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p},m}\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})(N\mathfrak{p})^{-ms}

where for an unramified prime
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K},

\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\left[\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right]^{m}=C\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and
|\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\leq 1
if
\mathfrak{p}
ramifies in
L
.
We use Perron’s formula to estimate
F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)
by considering partial sums of
Z(s)
, which include the ramified primes as well. Define

(4.2)

I(x_{j},T):=I_{C}(x_{j},T,\theta,\tau)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\sigma_{0}-iT}^{\sigma_{0}+iT}Z(s-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)\frac{{x_{j+1}}^{s}-{x_{j}}^{s}}{s}ds

where
T=\theta T^{\prime}/2
and
\sigma_{0}=1+\theta/2+1/\log x
. Then,

\displaystyle|\Psi_{C}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)-I(x_{j},T)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|I(x_{j},T)-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
x_{j}<N\mathfrak{p}\leq x_{j+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})}{(N\mathfrak{p})^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}\right|

(4.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
x_{j}<N\mathfrak{p}\leq x_{j+1}\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{:ramified}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})}{(N\mathfrak{p})^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}\right|.

The two terms on the right hand side of the above equation are now estimated. Using Lemma 3.1 of
[
LO77
]
, we have,

\displaystyle\left|I(x_{j},T)-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
x_{j}<N\mathfrak{p}\leq x_{j+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})}{(N\mathfrak{p})^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
N\mathfrak{p}^{m}=x_{j+1}\text{ or }N\mathfrak{p}^{m}=x_{j}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\log N\mathfrak{p}}{(N\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{-\theta/2}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}}{T}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
N\mathfrak{p}^{m}\neq x_{j+1}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{x_{j+1}}{N\mathfrak{p}^{m}}\right)^{\sigma_{0}}\min\left(1,T^{-1}\left|\log\frac{x_{j+1}}{N\mathfrak{p}^{m}}\right|^{-1}\right)\frac{\log N\mathfrak{p}}{(N\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{-\theta/2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
N\mathfrak{p}^{m}\neq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{x_{j}}{N\mathfrak{p}^{m}}\right)^{\sigma_{0}}\min\left(1,T^{-1}\left|\log\frac{x_{j}}{N\mathfrak{p}^{m}}\right|^{-1}\right)\frac{\log N\mathfrak{p}}{(N\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{-\theta/2}}.

Following arguments from
[
LO77
]
to estimate the terms on the right side of the above inequality, and noting that
x_{j}\leq 2x
for each
j=0,\ldots,B
,
we get

(4.4)

I(x_{j},T)-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
x_{j}<N\mathfrak{p}\leq x_{j+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})}{(N\mathfrak{p})^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}\ll x^{\theta/2}n_{K}\log x+n_{K}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{T}+n_{K}\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log^{2}x}{T}.

Moreover,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p},m\\
x_{j}<N\mathfrak{p}\leq x_{j+1}\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{:ramified}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Theta(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\log(N\mathfrak{p})}{(N\mathfrak{p})^{m(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)}}\ll x^{\theta/2}\log x\log d_{L}.

Putting (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) together in (
4.2
), we see that

(4.6)

\Psi_{C}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)=I(x_{j},T)+\operatorname{O}\left(x^{\theta/2}\log x\left(\log d_{L}+n_{K}+\frac{n_{K}x\log x}{T}\right)\right).

Next, we estimate
I(x_{j},T)
. From (
4.2
) and (
4.1
), we have

I(x_{j},T)=-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi}\bar{\chi}(g)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\sigma_{0}-iT}^{\sigma_{0}+iT}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(s-{\theta}/{2}-i\theta\tau,\chi,L/E\right)\frac{x_{j+1}^{s}-x_{j}^{s}}{s}~ds,

where
\chi
runs through irreducible characters of
H=\langle g\rangle
,
T^{\prime}\leq\tau\leq 2T^{\prime}
is fixed, and
T=\theta T^{\prime}/2
.
We make the change of variable
s\leftrightarrow s-\frac{\theta}{2}-i\theta\tau
to rewrite

I(x_{j},T)=-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi}\frac{\bar{\chi}(g)}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{1+\frac{1}{\log x}-iT-i\theta\tau}^{1+\frac{1}{\log x}+iT-i\theta\tau}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(s,\chi,L/E\right)\frac{x_{j+1}^{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}-x_{j}^{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}~ds.

Abbreviating
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi,L/E)
by
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)
, we evaluate for each character
\chi
of
H
, the integral

I_{\chi}(x_{j},T):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{1+\frac{1}{\log x}-iT-i\theta\tau}^{1+\frac{1}{\log x}+iT-i\theta\tau}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\frac{x_{j}^{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}~ds

for each
j=0,1,\ldots,B
. We may assume that
T+\theta\tau
and
T-\theta\tau
don’t coincide with the imaginary part of a zero of any of the
L(s,\chi)
. To estimate this integral, we move the line of integration and consider the integral over a rectangle and apply Cauchy’s theorem. More specifically, for
J:=m+\frac{1}{2}
where
m
is a non-negative integer, let
B_{T,J,\theta}
be the positively oriented rectangle with vertices at
1+\frac{1}{\log x}-i(T+\theta\tau),\,1+\frac{1}{\log x}+i(T-\theta\tau),\,-J-\frac{\theta}{2}+i(T-\theta\tau)
and
-J-\frac{\theta}{2}-i(T+\theta\tau).
Observe that this box does not intersect the real-axis, because
T-\theta\tau<0
for all
\tau\in[T^{\prime},2T^{\prime}]
. Define

I_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J):=\frac{x_{j}^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{2\pi i}\int_{B_{T,J,\theta}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\frac{x_{j}^{s}}{s+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}~ds.

Now we estimate the error term

(4.7)

\displaystyle R_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J):=I_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)-I_{\chi}(x_{j},T)

uniformly for each
j=0,\ldots,B
. Here, the error
R_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)
consists of sum of one vertical integral
V_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)
, and two horizontal integrals
H_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)
and
H^{*}_{\chi}(x_{j},T)
which we now estimate, following the line of proof in
[
LO77
, Section 6, Lemma 6.2]
. We deduce

\displaystyle V_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{T}^{-T}\frac{x_{j}^{-J+it}}{-J+it}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(-J-\theta/2+i(t-\theta\tau),\chi)~dt

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{-J}}{J}T\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log(|T+\theta\tau|+|J+\theta/2|\right);

\displaystyle H_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)

\displaystyle:=\frac{x_{j}^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{-J-\theta/2}^{-1/4}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma-iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma-i(T+\theta\tau),\chi)~d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{x_{j}^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{-J-\theta/2}^{-1/4}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma+iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2+iT}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+i(T-\theta\tau),\chi)~d\sigma

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{-1/4+\theta/2}}{T\log x}\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right);

and lastly

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\chi}(x_{j},T)

\displaystyle:=\frac{x_{j}^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{-1/4}^{1+1/\log x}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma-iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma-i(T+\theta\tau),\chi)~d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{x_{j}^{\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{-1/4}^{1+1/\log x}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma+iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2+iT}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+i(T-\theta\tau),\chi)~d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1/4}^{1+1/\log x}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-(T+\theta\tau)|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{~d\sigma}{\sigma-i(T+\theta\tau)-\rho}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-1/4}^{1+1/\log x}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma+\theta/2+iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2+iT}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma+(T-\theta\tau)|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma+i(T-\theta\tau)-\rho}

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}}{T\log x}(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|)\right),

where the sum above is taken over the non-real zeros
\rho
of
L(s,\chi)
and
A(\chi)=d_{E}N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(f_{\chi})
,
f_{\chi}
being the conductor of
\chi
. The proof of Lemma
6.3
in
[
LO77
]
can be modified to show that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-1/4}^{1+1/\log x}\frac{x_{j}^{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}}{\sigma+\theta/2-iT}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-(T+\theta\tau)|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sigma-i(T+\theta\tau)-\rho}~d\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\ll\frac{x^{1+1/\log x}}{T\log x}n_{\chi}(T+\theta\tau)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\ll\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|).

Here
n_{\chi}(t)
denotes the number of zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)
with
0<\beta<1
and
|\gamma-t|\leq 1
. A similar estimate holds for the sum over
\rho
with
|\gamma+(T-\theta\tau)|\leq 1
. Therefore, for each
j=0,1,\ldots,B
,

H_{\chi}^{*}(x_{j},T)\ll\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right).

Note that the estimate for
H_{\chi}(x_{j},T)
is bounded above by the estimate for
H_{\chi}^{*}(x_{j},T)
. Therefore, from (
4.7
),

\displaystyle R_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right)

(4.8)

\displaystyle+\frac{x^{-J}}{J}T\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log(|T-\theta\tau|+|J+\theta/2|\right).

We remark here that one only needs to consider the first term above; the second term goes to zero as
J\to\infty
. Next, by Cauchy’s theorem,
I_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)
is the sum of the residues at the poles of the integrand inside
B_{t,J}
. For our specified contour, the poles occur only at the non-real zeros of
L(s,\chi)
, and at
s=-\theta/2-i\theta\tau
. This gives

(4.9)

I_{\chi}(x_{j},T,J)=\sum\limits_{|\gamma+\theta\tau|<T}\frac{x_{j}^{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}+\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau).

The term
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)
is estimated using the following lemma which is a slightly general version of Lemma 6.2 in
[
LO77
]
and can be proved essentially using the same arguments, so we omit the details here.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma\leq-\theta/2
and
|s+m|\geq\theta/2
for all non-negative integers
m
, then

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\ll\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log(|s|+2).

Applying these bounds, we get

(4.10)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)\ll\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log(|\theta/2+i\theta\tau|+2).

Using (
4.10
), (
4.9
) and (
4.2
) in (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle I_{\chi}(x_{j},T)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|\gamma+\theta\tau|<T}\frac{x_{j}^{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{O}\left(\left(\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}+1\right)\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right)\right).

We plug this into the definition of
I(x_{j},T)
to obtain

\displaystyle I(x_{j},T)-S(x_{j+1},T)+S(x_{j},T)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}\left(\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}+1\right)\sum_{\chi}\left(\log A(\chi)+n_{E}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}\left(\frac{x^{1+\theta/2}\log x}{T}+1\right)(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log|T+\theta\tau|),

where

S(y,T):=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi}\bar{\chi}(g)\sum\limits_{|\gamma+\theta\tau|<T}\frac{y^{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}}{\rho+\theta/2+i\theta\tau}.

Under our assumption of a zero-free region
\Re s>1-\Delta
where
\theta/2<\Delta\leq 1/2
, and with slight modification to the calculation in the proof of Theorem
9.1
of
[
LO77
]
, and summing over
\chi
, we get

\displaystyle S(x_{j},T)\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}x^{1-\Delta+\theta/2}\log T(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log|T+\theta\tau|)

Finally, using the above bounds in (
4.2
) and recalling (
4.6
), we conclude

\displaystyle\Psi_{C}(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|C|}{|G|}x^{1-\Delta+\theta/2}\left(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log|T+\theta\tau|\right)\left(\log T+\frac{x^{\Delta}}{T}\log x\right)

\displaystyle\quad+x^{\theta/2}\log x\left(\log d_{L}+n_{K}+n_{K}\frac{x\log x}{T}\right).

Using partial summation and
\tau\leq 2T^{\prime}
, and setting
T=\theta T^{\prime}/2
, one gets the desired bounds for
F
in the case of a fixed conjugacy class
C
.

Next, suppose
C=\cup_{m=1}^{b}C_{m}
is a union of
b
conjugacy classes for some integer
b\geq 1
. Then, using the estimates established for each conjugacy class
C_{m}
and summing over
m
, we have

\displaystyle F(-\theta/2-i\theta\tau)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{b}|C_{j}|}{|G|}x^{1-\Delta+\theta/2}\bigg(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log T^{\prime}\bigg)\left(\frac{\log T^{\prime}}{\log x}+\frac{x^{\Delta}}{T^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+x^{\theta/2}\left(\log d_{L}+bn_{K}+bn_{K}\frac{x\log x}{T^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|C|}{|G|}x^{1-\Delta+\theta/2}\bigg(\log d_{L}+n_{L}\log T^{\prime}\bigg)\left(\frac{\log T^{\prime}}{\log x}+\frac{x^{\Delta}}{T^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+x^{\theta/2}\left(\log d_{L}+bn_{K}+bn_{K}\frac{x\log x}{T^{\prime}}\right),

noting that the error term in (
4.5
) that contributes
x^{\theta/2}\log d_{L}
remains unchanged whether
C
is a single class or a union of conjugacy classes. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

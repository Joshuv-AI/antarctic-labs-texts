Moduli spaces of 6 × 6 6\times 6 skew matrices of linear forms on ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} with a view towards intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds Thanks: The first author was supported by the EPSRC New Horizons Grant EP/V047299/1.
Christian Böhning, Hans-Christian Graf von Bothmer, Lukas Buhr

Abstract
. It is well known that every smooth cubic threefold is the zero locus of the Pfaffian of a 6 × 6 6\times 6 skew-symmetric matrix of linear forms in ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} . To compactify the space of such Pfaffian representations of a given cubic and to study the construction in families as well as for singular or reducible cubics, it is thus natural to consider the incidence correspondence of Pfaffian representations inside the product of the space of semistable skew-symmetric 6 × 6 6\times 6 matrices of linear forms in ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} and the space of cubics. Here we describe concretely the irreducible component of this incidence correspondence dominating the space of skew matrices.

Let
R
be the graded polynomial ring over
\mathbb{C}
in variables
x_{0},\dots,x_{4}
of weight
1
, and let
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathbb{C}^{5})^{\vee}\otimes\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{C}^{6}\bigr)
be the projective space of skew-symmetric

-matrices with entries linear forms on
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. The group
G=\mathrm{GL}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
acts on
\mathcal{S}
by acting trivially on
(\mathbb{C}^{5})^{\vee}
and naturally on
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{C}^{6}
. This corresponds to performing simultaneous row and column operations on skew-symmetric
6\times 6
-matrices. We denote by

\mathscr{M}:=\mathcal{S}^{ss}//G

the good quotient of the locus of semistable points in
\mathcal{S}
by the action of
G
. Let

\pi\colon\mathcal{S}^{ss}\to\mathscr{M}

be the canonical projection. We define the subset
\mathcal{S}^{ps}\subset\mathcal{S}^{ss}
to be the subset of those points whose orbits in
\mathcal{S}^{ss}
are closed. We recall that every fibre of
\pi
contains a unique closed orbit, i.e. the orbit of an element in
\mathcal{S}^{ps}
. Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{P}\bigl(H^{0}\bigl(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(3)\bigr)\bigr)
be the space of cubic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
Consider the incidence correspondence

\mathcal{T}=\bigl\{([M],[F])\mid\mathrm{Pf}(M)\in(F)\bigr\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{ss}\times\mathcal{C}

with its two projections

\pi_{1}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{S}^{ss},\quad\pi_{2}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{C}.

The condition
\mathrm{Pf}(M)\in(F)
is to be understood as the vanishing of all
2\times 2
minors of the matrix containing in its rows the coefficients of
\mathrm{Pf}(M)
and
F
with respect to some basis of the degree
3

x_{0},\dots,x_{4}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{ss}_{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{ss}
be the subset consisting of matrices with Pfaffian zero, and let

\mathcal{T}_{0}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}^{ss}_{0})=\mathcal{S}^{ss}_{0}\times\mathcal{C}.

Notice that
\pi_{1}
is one-to-one onto its image outside of the subset
\mathcal{T}_{0}\subset\mathcal{T}
.
Denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}
the closure of
\pi_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}^{ss}-\mathcal{S}^{ss}_{0})

\mathcal{T}
.

The group
G

\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{C}
if we let it act trivially on
\mathcal{C}
. Then

is the locus of semistable points for this action, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}
is clearly a

-invariant irreducible closed subset of
\mathcal{S}^{ss}\times\mathcal{C}
. The good categorical quotient
(\mathcal{S}^{ss}\times\mathcal{C})//G
is nothing but
\mathscr{M}\times\mathcal{C}
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}
maps to an irreducible closed subset of
\mathscr{M}\times\mathcal{C}
, which we denote by
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
.

Our goal is to describe the points of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}_{0}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}\cap\mathcal{T}_{0}
more explicitly, which we will achieve in Theorem
2.1
. Our initial interest in this problem stems from the fact that
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}\to\mathcal{C}
may then be viewed as the
universal family of
G
-equivalence classes of Pfaffian representations of cubic threefolds
, the fibres are projective, and there is a fibre over any cubic, singular, reducible, whatsoever. Also base-changing to curves inside
\mathcal{C}
and discarding components of the resulting total spaces that do not dominate the base curves, we can talk about one-parameter degenerations of (compactified) spaces of
G
-equivalence classes of Pfaffian representations. In a forthcoming article, we will show that the fibre of
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
over a smooth cubic threefold
X
coincides with the Maruyama-Druel-Beauville moduli space of equivalence classes of semistable sheaves on
X
with Chern classes
c_{1}=0,c_{2}=2,c_{3}=0
, or equivalently, the intermediate Jacobian of
X
blown up in the Fano surface of lines
[
Beau02
]
. Thus

gives us a way to construct explicit degenerations of these birational models of the intermediate Jacobians, which, we hope, will pave a way to decide unresolved questions about the cycle theory on such intermediate Jacobians: the most famous perhaps being if, realising these intermediate Jacobians as Prym varieties of double covers of smooth plane quintic curves, half of the class of the Prym curve is an algebraic cohomology class for very general
X
. By
[
Voi17
]
a positive answer to this is equivalent to the cubic being Chow zero universally trivial, which in turn is necessary for the cubic to be stably rational. It is a famous open problem if very general cubic threefolds are stably rational or not.

2.
The main theorem

Here we prove

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
[M]
be a point in
\mathcal{S}^{ss}_{0}
and
X=V(F)\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
a cubic threefold. Let
\overline{Y}\subset X
be the scheme defined by the
4\times 4
Pfaffians of

and the cubic polynomial

([M],[F])
is a point in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}_{0}
if and only if

Consider the tangent cone of an algebraic scheme

at the point
p
. Choose an affine chart as above with
p=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=(0,\dots,0)
.
The tangent cone
TC_{p}(X)
is the subscheme of

given as the zero locus of the leading terms of all elements in the ideal
I_{X}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})

If

is the degree at most

The tangent cone of a variety in a given point is often difficult to compute, but
the degree
2

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a variety,
p\in X
a point. Then

p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a point in the degree
2
approximation of the tangent cone if and only if there exists a point
p^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that the
2
-jet

j:=p+\epsilon p^{\prime}+\epsilon^{2}p^{\prime\prime}

is contained in
X
.

Proof.

Without restriction we can assume
p=0
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in I_{X}
be polynomials whose initial terms generate
I_{\leq 2}
. We can assume that these have expansions

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle:=f_{1}^{\prime}+f_{1}^{\prime\prime}+h.o.t.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\vdots

\displaystyle f_{k}

\displaystyle:=f_{k}^{\prime}+f_{k}^{\prime\prime}+h.o.t.

\displaystyle f_{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\quad\quad f_{k+1}^{\prime\prime}+h.o.t.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\vdots

\displaystyle f_{m}

\displaystyle:=\quad\quad f_{m}^{\prime\prime}+h.o.t.

with
f_{1}^{\prime}\dots f_{k}^{\prime}
linearly independent linear forms, and
f_{1}^{\prime\prime},\dots,f_{m}^{\prime\prime}
homogeneous quadratic polynomials, of which
f_{k+1},\dots,f_{m}
are linearly independent.

Now a
2
-jet
j=p+\epsilon p^{\prime}+\epsilon^{2}p^{\prime\prime}
lies on

if and only if
f_{i}(j)=0
for

and

is in the degree

For given

this is a set of linear equations for

. Since
f_{1}^{\prime},\dots,f_{k}^{\prime}
are linearly independent, this set of linear equations has a solution.

Let conversely

be a

-jet on

lies on the degree

Lemma 2.13

Proposition 2.14

Let
p=([M],[F])
be a point in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}_{0}

\overline{Y}\subset X

.
Then

contains a curve of degree

Proof.

We divide the proof into cases, depending on whether
[M]
is of type (a)-(f).

Case 1:
[M]
of type (a), (b) or (d)
In these cases
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is smooth in

because of Corollary
2.12
. In such a point there must be a tangent vector to
\mathcal{T}
that is not tangent to
\mathcal{T}_{0}

1
-jet centred at

in
\mathcal{T}
:

j=([M+\epsilon M_{1}],[F+\epsilon F_{1}])

such that

\mathrm{Pf}(M+\epsilon M_{1})=\mathrm{Pf}(M)+\epsilon G=\epsilon G

with

is a linear combination of the
4\times 4
sub-Pfaffians of

with coefficients entries of

[

4\times 4

Case 2:
[M]
of type (c) or (e)
If the tangent space to
\mathcal{T}
at

\mathcal{T}_{0}
at

. Choose a Gröbner basis

with respect to some monomial ordering refining the order given by total degree. Let

be a polynomial which is not in

. The reduction
\bar{f}
of this polynomial modulo the Gröbner basis

\bar{f}

.

In cases (c), (e) the ideal of the tangent cone

Case 3:
[M]
of type (f)

There exists a pair of formal power series

Case 1:

[M]

Soft thermodynamics of gravitational shock wave
Shuwei Liu, Beni Yoshida

Abstract
The gravitational shock waves have provided crucial insights into entanglement structures of black holes in the AdS/CFT correspondence. Recent progress on the soft hair physics suggests that these developments from holography may also be applicable to geometries beyond negatively curved spacetime. In this work, we derive a remarkably simple thermodynamic relation which relates the gravitational shock wave to a microscopic area deformation. Our treatment is based on the covariant phase space formalism and is applicable to any Killing horizon in generic static spacetime which is governed by arbitrary covariant theory of gravity. The central idea is to probe the gravitational shock wave, which shifts the horizon in the u u direction, by the Noether charge constructed from a vector field which shifts the horizon in the v v direction. As an application, we illustrate its use for the Gauss-Bonnet gravity. We also derive a simplified form of the gravitational scattering unitary matrix and show that its leading-order contribution is nothing but the exponential of the horizon area: 𝒰 = exp ⁡ ( i ​ Area ) \mathcal{U}=\exp(i\text{Area}) .

6.3
Pre-symplectic form

\mathbf{\Omega}^{(G)}(\delta g,\mathcal{L}_{\chi}g)

(116)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}^{(G)}(g,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\alpha}}g,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\beta}}g)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\alpha}}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\hat{\beta}}-\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\beta}}\mathbf{\Theta}_{\hat{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{1}{2}\beta(x)\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\alpha}}g_{\mu\nu}(dx)_{u}.

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \delta\int_{\Sigma_{B}}\mathbf{Q}_{\chi,\text{field}}-\delta\int_{\Sigma_{B}}\mathbf{Q}^{(M)}_{\chi,\text{field}}=0.\ }

7.2
Induced metric

The remaining task is to evaluate the induced metric on
\Sigma_{B}

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(\vec{y})\equiv(\beta(\vec{y}),\alpha(\vec{y}),\vec{y})

(126)

results from the metric variation. The induced metric on

\displaystyle d\sigma^{2}\equiv ds^{2}|_{(u,v,x)=X(\vec{y})}\equiv\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\vec{y})dy^{i}dy^{j}

(127)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\vec{y})=g_{\mu\nu}(X(\vec{y}))\frac{\partial X^{\mu}}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial X^{\nu}}{\partial y^{j}}.

(128)

We shall Taylor expand
\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\vec{y})
for small

\displaystyle g_{uv}(X(\vec{y}))\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y^{j}}=\left(F(0,0)+\frac{\partial^{2}F(u,v)}{\partial u\partial v}\alpha\beta+\cdots\right)\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y^{j}}.

(129)

Looking at
(\mu,\nu)=(i,j)

\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\vec{y})

\displaystyle g_{ij}(X(\vec{y}))dy^{i}dy^{j}=\left(G(0,0)+\frac{\partial^{2}G(u,v)}{\partial u\partial v}\alpha\beta+\cdots\right)h_{ij}(\vec{y})dy^{i}dy^{j}.

We observe that the leading correction to
\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\vec{y})
is at the order of
\alpha\beta
. In the expansion of Eq. (

\frac{\partial^{2}F(u,v)}{\partial u\partial v}\alpha\beta\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y^{j}}

, and hence is negligible. Then we find

\begin{split}d\sigma^{2}&\approx 2F(0,0)\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y^{j}}dy^{i}dy^{j}+\left(G(0,0)+\frac{\partial^{2}G(u,v)}{\partial u\partial v}\alpha\beta\right)h_{ij}(\vec{y})dy^{i}dy^{j}\\
&=d\sigma^{2}_{*}+2F(0,0)\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y^{j}}dy^{i}dy^{j}+\frac{\partial^{2}G(u,v)}{\partial u\partial v}\alpha\beta h_{ij}(\vec{y})dy^{i}dy^{j}\end{split}

(131)

where

represents the unperturbed induced metric when

\displaystyle d\sigma_{*}^{2}=G(0,0)h_{ij}dy^{i}dy^{j}.

(132)

To summarize, we have obtained the variation of the induced metric:

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{G}_{ij}=2F\partial_{i}\alpha\partial_{j}\beta+G_{,uv}\alpha\beta h_{ij}

(133)

where

and

are evaluated at

Recall that the first order variation of the determinant is

\displaystyle\delta\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{G}^{ij}\delta\mathcal{G}_{ij}.

(134)

Hence the variation of the determinant at
\Sigma_{B}
is given by

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \delta\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}=\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{2G}\Big(2F(\partial^{j}\alpha)(\partial_{j}\beta)+(d-2)G_{,uv}\alpha\beta\Big).\ }

(135)

Note
\mathcal{G}^{ij}=g^{ij}=\frac{h^{ij}}{F}
at
u=v=0
. Observe
\partial_{j}\Big(\beta(\partial^{j}\alpha)\sqrt{h}\Big)=(\partial_{j}\beta)(\partial^{j}\alpha)\sqrt{h}+\beta\partial_{j}\Big((\partial^{j}\alpha)\sqrt{h}\Big)
.
After integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \delta\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}=\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{2G}\Big(-2F\beta(\Delta_{h_{ij}}\alpha)+(d-2)G_{,uv}\alpha\beta\Big).\ }

(136)

Note that total derivatives do not contribute to the surface integral of the Noether charge.

Finally, we derive the shock wave equations of motion. Using the expression of deformed area density Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta{\mathbf{Q}_{\chi,\text{field}}}=-(\mathbf{d^{d-2}x})_{uv}\frac{\kappa\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}}{8\pi}\frac{1}{2GF}\Big(-2F\beta(\Delta_{h_{ij}}\alpha)+(d-2)G_{,uv}\alpha\beta\Big)

then the constraint Eq. (

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\frac{1}{2GF}\Big(-2F(\Delta_{h_{ij}}\alpha)+(d-2)G_{,uv}\alpha\Big)\beta=\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}4p^{(v)}F\delta(x)\kappa\beta.

(138)

We can arrange it into the following form

\displaystyle\Big((\Delta_{h_{ij}}\alpha)-\frac{(d-2)}{2}\frac{G_{,uv}}{F}\alpha-32\pi GFp^{(v)}\delta(x)\Big)\beta\kappa\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}=0.

(139)

Hence we recover the shock wave equation of motion

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{ij}}\alpha(x)-\frac{d-2}{2}\frac{G_{,uv}}{F}\alpha(x)=32\pi p^{(v)}GF\delta(x).

(140)

Measuring shock waves

10
Outlook

In this work, we have derived a simple microscopic thermodynamic relation for the gravitational shock waves by providing a generic framework to construct the Noether charge for them.

We anticipate that our formalism can be applied to a wide variety of geometries in order to study the effect of the gravitational backreaction. One interesting future problem is to apply a similar method to stationary black holes where thermodynamic treatment involves angular momentum
[
36
]
.

We did not follow the standard treatment of the soft hair physics in characterizing the gravitational shock waves. Instead, we opted to work on the gravitational shock wave solutions on a more generic setting. It would be interesting to translate our results to the language of the soft hair physics and study the consequence of the finiteness of the black hole entropy. Also, it is an interesting future problem to generalize the expression of the gravitational scattering matrix so that it would be applicable to generic soft charges which may be beyond the gravitational shock waves. Developing a generic framework for measurement of soft charges is also an interesting problem.

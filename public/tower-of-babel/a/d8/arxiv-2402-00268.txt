Relation between timelike and spacelike entanglement entropy
Wu-zhong Guo, Song He, Yu-Xuan Zhang

Abstract
In this study, we establish a connection between timelike and spacelike entanglement entropy. Specifically, for a diverse range of states, the timelike entanglement entropy is uniquely determined by a linear combination of the spacelike entanglement entropy and its first-order temporal derivative. This framework reveals that the imaginary component of the timelike entanglement entropy primarily originates from the non-commutativity between the twist operator and its first-order temporal derivative. Furthermore, we analyze the constraints of this relation and highlight the possible extension to accommodate more complex state configurations.

Despite notable progress in this field, there remains a significant gap in our comprehensive understanding of the physical significance of timelike EE. A question arises regarding its interpretation as a concept of entropy, especially given its general complex-valued nature. A crucial objective is to understand the origin of the imaginary component of timelike EE. While it is possible to establish timelike EE through the analytical continuation of spacelike EE, the extent of the intrinsic relationship between these two aspects of entanglement entropy remains unclear.
This paper aims to construct and explore the intricate relationship between these two facets of EE, demonstrating that timelike EE
can be linked to spacelike EE and its time derivative on the Cauchy surface at
t=0
.

From the perspective of correlators, it is natural to establish a connection between timelike correlators and spacelike ones. For a theory with Hamiltonian
H
, the dynamics of a local operator
O(t,\vec{x})
is given by
O(t,\vec{x}):=e^{iHt}O(0,\vec{x})e^{-iHt}
, which can be expressed formally as

\displaystyle O(t,\vec{x})=\sum_{m}\frac{(it)^{m}}{m!}[H,...,[H,O(0,\vec{x})]],

(2)

which means the operator
O(t,\vec{x})
can be expressed as linear combinations of operators
[H,...,[H,O(0,\vec{x})]]
on the Cauchy surface at
t=0
. Similarly, for another operator
O^{\prime}(t^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})
, a similar decomposition can be performed. Consequently, the correlator
\langle O(t,\vec{x})O^{\prime}(t^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\prime})\rangle
can be formally represented as linear combinations of spacelike correlators. However, in general, the expression becomes too complex to be practically useful.

Since in 2-dimensional CFTs,
\sigma_{n}
can be considered as a local operator in the CFT
n
theory, one can also define its time evolution by the Hamiltonian
H^{(n)}
. The timelike EE can be computed through the analytical continuation of (
1
) with
\tau\to it+\epsilon
. By utilizing (
2
) for the twist operator, one anticipates that the timelike EE can be linked to correlators on the Cauchy surface at
t=0
. However, our findings extend beyond this expectation. We will demonstrate in various cases that the timelike EE is equivalent to a linear combination of
solely
spacelike EE and its first-order time derivative.

II
Derivation of the relation for vacuum state

Timelike entanglement entropy
.
Consider the vacuum state
|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle
, where

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\rho_{0,A})^{n}:=\langle\sigma_{n}(\tau,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle

(3)

\displaystyle=(w_{1}-w_{2})^{-2h_{n}}(\bar{w}_{1}-\bar{w}_{2})^{-2\bar{h}_{n}},

with
w_{i}=\tau_{i}+ix_{i}
(

We are interested in an interval between
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
in Minkowski spacetime with these two points being timelike. The timelike Rényi entropy can be obtained by Wick rotation
\tau\to it+\epsilon
. More generally, for any interval between
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime}):=\frac{\log\text{tr}(\rho_{0,A})^{n}|_{\tau\to it+\epsilon,\ \tau^{\prime}\to it^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}}{1-n}

(4)

\displaystyle=\frac{2h_{n}}{n-1}\log\left[\Delta s^{2}+2i(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})(t-t^{\prime})\right],

where
\Delta s^{2}=-(t-t^{\prime})^{2}+(x-x^{\prime})^{2}
and
\epsilon>\epsilon^{\prime}

For spacelike separation,
S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
is the spacelike Rényi entropy, satisfying the symmetry
S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)
. For timelike separation with
t>t^{\prime}
, where
\Delta s^{2}<0

S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{12}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log[-\Delta s^{2}]+\frac{i\pi c}{12}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right).

(5)

In this case,
S_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)=S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})^{*}
, and the imaginary part is given by

\displaystyle\text{Im}[S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-S_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi c}{12}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right).

Taking the limit
n\to 1
yields the timelike entanglement entropy.

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{6}\log\Big[\frac{\beta^{2}}{\pi^{2}\delta^{2}}\sinh\left(\frac{\pi(v-v^{\prime})}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{6}}\times\sinh\left(\frac{\pi(u-u^{\prime})}{\beta}\right)\Big]+\frac{i\pi c}{6}.

. Generalizing to multiple intervals is a highly non-trivial task. However, for states with a holographic dual, the Ryu-Takayanagi (RT) formula allows for the evaluation of entanglement entropy to the leading order in

IV
The significance of the relation

A significant observation is that terms without time derivatives in Eq. (
II
) exclusively involve entanglement between four points at
t=0
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. Scaling the UV cutoff
\delta\to c\delta
results in a constant shift in both timelike and spacelike RE or EE. The constant from the left-hand side and the right-hand side cancel each other out.

To understand the physical meaning of the relation (
II
), we can introduce the operator

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{n}:=i[H^{(n)},\sigma_{n}],

(18)

where
H^{(n)}
is the Hamiltonian of CFT
n
. It can be shown that
\dot{\sigma}_{n}
can be taken as time derivative with respect to Lorentzian time
t
.
Similarly, we define
\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}:=i[H,\tilde{\sigma}_{n}]
. Therefore, we would have the correlators involving of
\dot{\sigma}_{n}
and
\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}
, such as
\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}\tilde{\sigma}_{n}|\Psi\rangle
,
\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}|\Psi\rangle
and
\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}|\Psi\rangle
.
By the definition of Rényi entropy, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S_{n}=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle},

(19)

or equally,

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle=\partial_{t}e^{(1-n)S_{n}}.

(20)

Applying Eq. (
7

\sigma_{n}
, we generally anticipate that operators from the given theory may appear in the integral on the right-hand side of (
7

\sigma_{n}(0,x)
,
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(0,x)
,
\dot{\sigma}_{n}(0,x)
, and
\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}(0,x)

All instances indicate that the imaginary part of timelike entanglement entropy originates from the term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,-u^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,v^{\prime}).

For the timelike EE, we have

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(0,\bar{x}),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(0,-u^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(0,\bar{x}),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(0,v)]|\Psi\rangle.

Refer to Appendix E for additional details. The commutator

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(0,\bar{x}),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(0,\bar{y})]|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2i\pi c}{3}(1-n)\delta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+O(1-n)^{2}.

(22)

Note that this commutator is specific to the discussed states; additional terms at order

may exist in more general cases, represented by
\mathcal{A}_{i}(\bar{x})\delta(\bar{x}-\bar{y})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S=-\sum_{i}\partial_{t}\lambda_{i}\log\lambda_{i}=-\int_{0}^{\lambda_{m}}d\lambda\log\lambda\ \mathcal{P}_{t}(\lambda),

where

Rajabpour:2011pt
;
Chen:2013kpa
. The OPE of twist operators in CFT
n
can be formally written as

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(w,\bar{w})\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(w^{\prime},\bar{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K}d_{K}(w-w^{\prime})^{-2h_{n}+h_{K}}(\bar{w}-\bar{w}^{\prime})^{-2\bar{h}_{n}+\bar{h}_{K}}\mathcal{X}_{K},

(25)

where
\mathcal{X}_{K}
denotes the operators in CFT
n
, and
d_{K}
are the OPE coefficients. The computation of RE and EE involves evaluating the expectation value
\langle\Psi|\mathcal{X}_{K}|\Psi\rangle
and determining the coefficients
d_{K}
. To explore EE for arbitrary intervals, analytical continuation is employed, yielding a result expressed as an infinite summation:

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=S_{0}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})+\sum_{k}a_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{2h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{2\bar{h}_{k}},

where
S_{0}
denotes the EE for the vacuum state.
Note that we include the OPE coefficients and the expectation values of the operators in the coefficients
a_{k}
. For simplicity, we consider
a_{k}
as constants, which is reasonable when the length of the interval is short enough.

Since
S_{0}
satisfies the relation (
II
), we only need to consider the summation terms in (
V
). In the following, we still assume
-u<-u^{\prime}<v^{\prime}<v
. For the timelike separation
-u+u^{\prime}<0
, there may be new imaginary contributions to the timelike EE from the summation terms. One could directly check that generally, the result (
V
)
does not
satisfy the relation (
II
).

However, if the summation terms are the following form

\displaystyle\sum_{m}a_{m}\left([-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{m}+[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{m}\right),

(26)

where
m
are positive integers. One could check the above terms satisfy the relation (
II
). It can be shown that the thermal states or the general states dual to pure AdS
3
can be written in the above form.

Let us explain why the above form is only correct for the states dual to AdS
3
. It is known that these theories have a sparse light spectrum
Hartman:2014oaa
. The main contributions to the OPE of twist operators come from the vacuum family, such as the lowest contributions from the stress-energy tensor
T
and
\bar{T}

Asplund:2014coa
. Thus, the powers of the expansion (
26
) should be integers, and
m\geq 2
. It has been shown that in this case, the OPE can be factored into the product of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts. Consequently, the entanglement entropy can be expanded in the form (
26
).

The relation (
II
) does not hold for arbitrary state. The example provided in (
V
) illustrates the emergence of new imaginary contributions from the summation terms. While the right-hand side of (
II
) is inadequate to account for these additional contributions. Nevertheless, we anticipate that by introducing further terms from other operators on the right-hand side of (
II
), it is possible to establish a connection between timelike EE and its spacelike counterpart, extending potentially beyond first-order derivatives. In cases where OPE of twist operators is permissible, a perturbative construction of this connection becomes feasible.

VI
Discussion

In this paper, we establish a connection between timelike and spacelike entanglement entropy. Our findings reveal that, within a broad class of states, timelike entanglement entropy can be expressed as a sum over spacelike entanglement entropy and its first derivative. This prompts a natural question: how can our results be extended to encompass more general scenarios, including diverse states and higher-dimensional theories?

An additional noteworthy observation is that timelike entanglement entropy finds an interpretation through pseudo entropy. As demonstrated in
Guo:2023aio
, a sum rule links pseudo Rényi entropy to Rényi entropy. An intriguing avenue for future exploration is to investigate whether the relation (
II
) serves as a specific instance of the general sum rule proposed in
Guo:2023aio
.

Appendix D
Appendix D: Imaginary part of timelike entanglement entropy

Suppose
-u<-u^{\prime}<v^{\prime}<v
. The imaginary part comes from the term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,-u^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,v^{\prime}).

By definition we have

\displaystyle\text{Im}[S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]=\frac{1}{2}\left[S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-S(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)\right].

(66)

For the spacelike EE we have
S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)
, thus the following term does not contribute to the imaginary part.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Big(S(0,-u;0,-u^{\prime})+S(0,-u;0,v^{\prime})+S(0,v;0,-u^{\prime})+S(0,v;0,v^{\prime})\Big).

(67)

Similarly, by symmetry, we can show the last term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}S(0,\bar{x};0,\bar{x}^{\prime}),

(68)

also does not contribute to the imaginary part. In the main text, we show the first-order derivative of EE is associated with the correlator involving
\sigma_{n}
and
\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}
. The term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}}S(0,-u;0,\bar{x}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}}S(0,v;0,\bar{x}^{\prime})\rangle,

(69)

is related to the correlator
\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(0,-u)\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}(0,\bar{x}^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle
. Since
\bar{x}^{\prime}\in(-u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
, the two operators are spacelike, thus there is no imaginary part for this term.

Therefore, the terms contributing to the imaginary part are:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,-u^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}S(0,\bar{x};0,v^{\prime}).

(70)

The origin of the imaginary part is the non-commutativity of
[\sigma_{n}(0,x),\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{n}(0,\bar{x}^{\prime})]
at the point
x=x^{\prime}
.

Appendix E
Appendix E: OPE of EE for general cases

In general cases, the OPE of twist operators cannot be factored as a product of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts. This is because the operators
\mathcal{X}_{K}(w,\bar{w})\neq\mathcal{X}_{K}(w)\mathcal{X}_{K}(\bar{w})
. However, if we only consider contributions from the vacuum family, the operators would be descendants of the identity operators, such as the stress-energy tensor
T(w)
and
\bar{T}(\bar{w})
. The OPE of
T
and
\bar{T}
is trivial, thus the contributions from
T(w)\bar{T}(\bar{w})
can be written as the product of the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts
\langle T(w)\rangle\psi\langle\bar{T}(\bar{w})\rangle_{\psi}
. It is also important that the OPE coefficients
d_{K}
for the operator
T(w)\bar{T}(\bar{w})
also factor as
d_{T\bar{T}}=d_{T}d_{\bar{T}}
. The argument can be generalized to other higher-dimensional descendants. As a result, the OPE of twist operators can be written as
\log\langle\sigma_{n}(w,\bar{w})\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(w^{\prime},\bar{w}^{\prime})
can be expressed as a summation over the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts. Thus, the entanglement entropy takes the form as in Eq.(
26
) in the main text.

Now let us check if the form Eq.(
26
) does satisfy the relation Eq.(
II
).
We will only need to consider the terms
\Delta S:=S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-S_{0}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
.
This can be done by directly computing. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\Delta S(0,-u;0,-u^{\prime})+\Delta S(0,-u;0,v^{\prime})+\Delta S(0,v;0,-u^{\prime})+\Delta S(0,v;0,v^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}a_{m}\Big[\left(u^{\prime}+v+i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(u^{\prime}+v-i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(u^{\prime}-u+i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(u^{\prime}-u-i\epsilon\right)^{m}

\displaystyle+\left(-u-v^{\prime}+i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(-u-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(-v^{\prime}+v+i\epsilon\right)^{m}+\left(-v^{\prime}+v-i\epsilon\right)^{m}\Big]

(71)

It can also be shown there is no contribution from the following terms,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Delta S(0,-u;0,\bar{x}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Delta S(0,v;0,\bar{x}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}\Delta S(0,\bar{x};0,-u^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\partial_{t}\Delta S(0,\bar{x};0,v^{\prime}).

By using

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Delta S(0,\bar{x};0,\bar{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{m}a_{m}\left[(m-1)m\left(-(x-\bar{x}+i\epsilon)^{m-2}-(x-\bar{x}-i\epsilon)^{m-2}\right)\right],

we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}d\bar{x}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}d\bar{x}^{\prime}\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Delta S(0,\bar{x};0,\bar{x}^{\prime}).

(74)

It is easy to check that the sum of (

\displaystyle\sum_{m}a_{m}\left[(-1)^{m}(u-u^{\prime})^{m}+(v-v^{\prime})^{m}\right],

which is just the

\sum_{m}a_{m}\left[(u-u^{\prime})^{m}+(v-v^{\prime})^{m}\right]

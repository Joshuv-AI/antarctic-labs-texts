Nef and abundant divisors, semiampleness and canonical bundle formula
Priyankur Chaudhuri

Abstract
. In this paper, we use canonical bundle formulas to prove various generalizations of an old theorem of Kawamata on the semiampleness of nef and abundant log canonical divisors. In particular, we show that for klt pairs ( X , B ) (X,B) with K X + B K_{X}+B effective, L ∈ Pic ⁡ X L\in\Pic X nef, nefness and abundance of K X + B + L K_{X}+B+L implies semiampleness. This essentially extends Kawamata’s theorem to the setting of generalized abundance.



1.
Introduction

This paper will be concerned with the following:

Question 1
.

Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair and
L\in\Pic X
nef such that
K_{X}+B+L
is nef and abundant, then is
K_{X}+B+L
semiample?

This is clearly related to the following conjecture of Lazić and Peternell
[
13
]
.

Conjecture 2
.

(Generalized Abundance) Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair with
K_{X}+B
pseudoeffective and
L
a nef Cartier divisor on
X
such that
K_{X}+B+L
is also nef. Then there exists a semiample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
M
on
X
such that
K_{X}+B+L\equiv M
.

†
†

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 14E30

Note that we are not assuming pseudoeffectivity of
(K_{X}+B)
in our question.
There are two reasons why one might expect this to be true. Firstly, in
[
4
]
, the author has shown that generalized abundance does continue to hold for many uniruled varieties. Secondly, if
L\in\Pic X
is a large multiple of a strictly nef divisor, then under the above assumptions, one can show that
K_{X}+B+L
is ample. So it is natural to ask for a version in which the pseudoeffectivity of
K_{X}+B
is not required. The surface case of this question is very easily seen to be true. We now state our main results which settle certain special cases. Essentially, they show that Question 1 has a positive answer in the following two situations:

(1)

If
L
is a birational pushforward of a semiample divisor (in fact, it does not need to be nef here).

(2)

If
K_{X}+B\geq 0
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
be a generalized klt pair,
H
a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
H-(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X})
is nef and abundant. Assume further that
\mathbf{M}
is b-semiample,
\kappa(aH-(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}))\geq 0
and
\nu(aH-(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}))=\nu(H-(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}))
for some

. Then

is semiample.

The case
\mathbf{M}=0
of the above theorem is due to Kawamata
[
10
]
and Fujino
[
8
]
. Our approach of using canonical bundle formula is similar to that of Fujino’s.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair with
\kappa(K_{X}+B)\geq 0
,
H
and
L
nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors on
X
such that
H-(K_{X}+B+L)
is nef and abundant. Assume further that
\kappa(aH-(K_{X}+B+L))\geq 0
and
\nu(aH-(K_{X}+B+L))=\nu(H-(K_{X}+B+L))
for some

. Then

This will be obtained as a consequence of a generalized canonical bundle formula (Theorem 14) to be proved in the third section.

Remark 5

Thus in particular, if
(X,B)
is a klt pair with

2.
Preliminaries

We will always work with
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors.

Definition 2.1

(Abundant divisors)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
D
be a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with Iitaka dimension
\kappa(X,D)
. The
numerical dimension
of
D
is defined as
\nu(X,D)=max\{e|D^{e}\not\equiv 0\}
. In general,
\kappa(X,D)\leq\nu(X,D)
.
D
is
abundant
if
\kappa(X,D)=\nu(X,D)
. Examples include big nef divisors, semiample divisors and their pullbacks.

Definition 2.2

(b-divisors (see
[
5
]
))
.

Let
X
be a normal variety. A
b-divisor

\mathbf{D}
on
X
is a collection of Weil divisors
\mathbf{D}_{Y}
on higher birational models
Y\rightarrow X
of
X
that are compatible under pushforward.
\mathbf{D}_{Y}
is called the
trace
of
\mathbf{D}
on
Y
. The
Cartier closure
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
X
is the b-
\mathbb{Q}
divisor
\overline{\mathbf{D}}
with trace
\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{Y}:=\rho^{*}(D)
on a birational model
\rho:Y\rightarrow X
. A b-
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathbf{D}
on a normal variety
X
is
b-
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
if it is the Cartier closure of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on some birational model of
X
; in this situation, we say that
\mathbf{D}

descends
to
Y
.
\mathbf{D}
is called
b-nef
(resp.
b-semiample
) if it is descends to a nef (resp. semiample)
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on some birational model over
X
. Let
(X,B)
be a sub-pair consisting of a normal variety
X
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
X
such that
K_{X}+B
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. The
discrepancy b-divisor

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}(X,B)
is defined by
K_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+B)+\mathbf{A}_{Y}
for all models
Y\xrightarrow{f}X
.
(X,B)
is called
sub-klt
(resp
sub-lc
) if all coefficients of
\mathbf{A}_{Y}(X,B)
are
>-1
(resp.
\geq-1
) for some (and hence, by
[
11
, Lemma 2.30]
, for all) birational models

. Thus in particular, if
X
is smooth, all coefficents of

are less than
1
and
B
has normal crossing support, then
(X,B)
is sub-klt. In case

, we drop the prefix sub.

Definition 2.3

(Generalized pairs and their singularities(see
[
3
]
))
.

A
generalized sub-pair

(X,B+\mathbf{M})
consists of a normal projective variety
X
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor
\mathbf{M}
on
X
such that:

(1)

K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

(2)

\mathbf{M}
is b-nef.

When

, we drop the prefix sub.

Let
(X,B)
be a generalized sub-pair and
Y\xrightarrow{\mu}X
a higher birational model of
X
. Let

be defined by
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+\mathbf{M}_{Y}=\mu^{*}(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X})
. We say that
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is

(resp.

) if every coefficient of

is less than
1
(resp. less than or equal to

). We will call them sub-gklt and sub-glc in short. The

is then defined by
\mathbf{A}_{Y}(X,B+\mathbf{M})=-B_{Y}
. Note that
\mathbf{M}_{Y}
does not contribute to the singularities of
(Y,B_{Y}+\mathbf{M})
once it descends to a nef divisor on

, i.e.
\mathbf{A}(Y,B_{Y}+\mathbf{M}_{Y})=\mathbf{A}(Y,B_{Y})
and thus
(Y,B_{Y}+\mathbf{M}_{Y})
is gklt (resp. glc) iff
(Y,B_{Y})
is klt (resp. lc).

Definition 2.4

(Generalized klt-trivial fibrations)
.

Let
(X,B)
be a pair consisting of a normal variety
X
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
X
. A morphism
f:(X,B)\rightarrow Y
,
Y
normal is

if

(1)

is surjective with connected fibers,

(2)

(X,B)
is sub-klt over the generic point of
Y
,

(3)

there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
and a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L
on
X
such that
K_{X}+B+L\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}D

(4)

there exists a log-resolution
\pi^{{}^{\prime}}:(X^{{}^{\prime}},B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}})\rightarrow(X,B)
such that if
f^{{}^{\prime}}:(X^{{}^{\prime}},B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}})\rightarrow Y

f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\rceil\right)=1

The following fact seems to be well-known to the experts. I will include a proof for the convenience of readers and for want of a reference.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f:(X,B)\rightarrow Y
be a generalized klt-trivial fibration,
\sigma:Y^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow Y
a generically finite morphism from a normal variety
Y^{{}^{\prime}}
. Let
X^{{}^{\prime}}
denote a desingularization of the main component of
X\times_{Y}Y^{{}^{\prime}}
and let
f^{{}^{\prime}}:(X^{{}^{\prime}},B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}})\rightarrow Y^{{}^{\prime}}
be the induced morphism. Then
f^{{}^{\prime}}
is generalized klt-trivial.

Proof.

Only item
(4)
in the above definition needs to be verified. Since
(X^{{}^{\prime}},B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}})
is sub-klt, the rank is surely
\geq 1
. We only need to show it is
\leq 1
and may assume that
X
itself is smooth. Let
\pi:(X^{{}^{\prime}},B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}})\rightarrow(X,B)
be the induced morphism.

(1)

Suppose first that
\sigma
is birational. Then there exists an effective, integral,
\pi
-exceptional divisor
E^{{}^{\prime}}
on
X^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
K_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X})+E^{{}^{\prime}}
. The condition
K_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}+B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B)
then gives
B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}+E^{{}^{\prime}}=\pi^{*}B_{X}
. If we can show that
\sigma\circ f^{{}^{\prime}}:X^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow Y^{{}^{\prime}}
satisfies rank
(\sigma\circ f^{{}^{\prime}})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right\rceil)=1
, then rank
f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right\rceil)=1
also since
\sigma\circ f^{{}^{\prime}}
and
f^{{}^{\prime}}
have the same general fiber. Thus we may assume
\sigma=id
and thus
f\circ\pi=f^{{}^{\prime}}
. Now,

f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right\rceil)=f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-\pi^{*}B_{X}+E^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil)

=f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-\pi^{*}B_{X}}\right\rceil+E^{{}^{\prime}})
(since
E^{{}^{\prime}}
is integral)

\subset f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\pi^{*}\left\lceil{-B_{X}}\right\rceil+E^{{}^{\prime}})

=f_{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\pi^{*}(\left\lceil{-B_{X}}\right\rceil)+E^{{}^{\prime}})=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lceil{-B_{X}}\right\rceil)\otimes\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(E^{{}^{\prime}}))

=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lceil{-B_{X}}\right\rceil)
(by Hartog’s theorem since
E^{{}^{\prime}}
is
\pi
-exceptional).

Thus rank
f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right\rceil)\leq 1
.

(2)

Now suppose that
\sigma
is finite. In this case also, there exists an integral divisor
R^{{}^{\prime}}\geq 0
such that
K_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X})+R^{{}^{\prime}}
(Supp (
R^{{}^{\prime}}
) is the ramification locus of
\pi
). This gives
B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}+R^{{}^{\prime}}=\pi^{*}B_{X}
. To show that rank
f^{{}^{\prime}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(\left\lceil{-B_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}}\right\rceil)=1
, it suffices to show that
R^{{}^{\prime}}
is not
f^{{}^{\prime}}
-horizontal. Now
[
9
, Chapter 3, Proposition 10.1 (b)]
implies that
f^{-1}(Sm(\sigma))\subset Sm(\pi)
(
Sm
denotes the smooth locus of the respective morphism) or in other words,
f(Branch(\pi))=f(\pi(R^{{}^{\prime}}))\subset Branch(\sigma)
. Thus
R^{{}^{\prime}}
can not be
f^{{}^{\prime}}
-horizontal.

∎

3.
Nef and abundant divisors

We start off by proving two easy results. They provide positive evidence for Question 1. Recall that an
almost strictly nef
(ASN) divisor means a birational pullback of a strictly nef Cartier divisor. See
[
4
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
14
]
for some recent developments around ASN divisors.

Lemma 7
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety,
M\in\Pic X
be almost strictly nef (ASN) and abundant. Then
M
is big.

Proof.

By
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
, there exist morphisms
X\xleftarrow{\mu}X^{{}^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{f}Y
, where
\mu
is birational,
f
a contraction and
X^{{}^{\prime}}
and
Y
are smooth and projective such that

(8)

M^{{}^{\prime}}:=\mu^{*}M=f^{*}(M_{Y})

for some
M_{Y}\in\Pic Y
nef and big. If
\dim Y=\dim X
, then
f
is generically finite and
M
is already big. So assume that
\dim Y<\dim X
. By definition of ASN, there exists
X\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0}
birational and
M_{0}\in\Pic X_{0}
strictly nef such that
\pi^{*}(M_{0})=M
. Let
E\subset X
be the exceptional locus of
\pi
. Then for all curves
C\not\subset E
,
(M\cdot C)=(\pi^{*}(M_{0})\cdot C)>0
. If
F\subset X^{{}^{\prime}}
is a general fiber of
f
, then
F\not\subset E
. Then there is a curve
C\subset F
such that
C\not\subset E
and thus
(M\cdot C)>0
. This is not possible by (
8
). Therefore,
\dim Y=\dim X
and
M
is big.
∎

Corollary 9
.

Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair,
L\in\Pic X
strictly nef such that
K_{X}+B+t_{0}L
is abundant for some
t_{0}>2\cdot\dim X
. Then
K_{X}+B+tL
is ample for all
t>2\cdot\dim X
.

Proof.

K_{X}+B+t_{0}L
is strictly nef by the cone theorem and abundant, hence big by above lemma. Then
2(K_{X}+B+t_{0}L)-(K_{X}+B)
is nef and big and thus
K_{X}+B+t_{0}L
is semiample by the basepoint free theorem. Now it is easy to see that a strictly nef and semiample divisor is ample, thus
K_{X}+B+t_{0}L
is ample. The conclusion now follows from
[
15
, Lemma 1.3]
(or rather its log version which has the same proof).
∎

The following generalization of the basepoint-free theorem will be indispensible for us. It can be proved by a minor modification of the usual arguments (

Theorem 10
.

Let
(X,B)
be a sub-klt pair,
H
a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
. Assume that

(1)

rH-(K_{X}+B)
is nef and big for some

.

(2)

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lceil{\mathbf{A}(X,B)}\right\rceil+j\overline{\mathbf{H}}))\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(jH))
for all

Then

Lemma 11
.

Let
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
be a sub-generalized klt pair,
H
nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
H-(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X})

(2)

Torus actions on quotients of affine spaces
Ana-Maria Brecan, Hans Franzen

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the locus of fixed points of a torus action on a GIT quotient of a complex vector space by a reductive complex algebraic group which acts linearly. We show that, under the assumption that
G
acts freely on the stable locus, the components of the fixed point locus are again GIT quotients of linear subspaces by Levi subgroups.

Keywords.
Geometric quotients, torus actions, torus fixed points, destabilizing one-parameter subgroups

1. Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with actions of tori on geometric quotients of linear actions of reductive complex algebraic groups on vector spaces. Such geometric quotients appear very often. One prominent class of examples are moduli spaces of quivers without relations; see
e.g.

[
Rei08
]
for an overview of this theory. Geometric quotients of vector spaces arise in other contexts as well. For example, every toric variety whose fan satisfies some mild hypotheses can be realized as such a quotient; see
[
Cox97
, Theorem 2.1]
. Other instances where GIT quotients of vector spaces are investigated are
[
ES89
,
Hal10
,
Hos14
]
.

We study actions of a torus
\mathscr{T}
on a GIT quotient of the form
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
, where
G
is a reductive complex algebraic group,
V
is a finite-dimensional representation of
G
, and
\theta
is a character of
G
. We assume that this torus action comes from a linear
\mathscr{T}
-action on
V
that commutes with the action of
G
. Our main objective in this paper is to determine the locus of fixed points of this torus action.

In the case of quiver moduli, this question has been studied by Weist in
[
Wei13
]
. More precisely, he considers torus actions on moduli spaces of stable quiver representations which are given by scaling of the maps along the arrows of the quiver. He shows that the fixed point locus of such a torus action decomposes into irreducible components, each of which is isomorphic to a moduli space of stable representations of a covering quiver.

We generalize this result to GIT quotients of the form
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
. We need to make the assumption that the action of
G
on the stable locus is free. This is automatically satisfied in the context of quiver moduli spaces.

Our main result, Theorem
6.3
, asserts that, under the assumption from the previous paragraph, the fixed point locus of the action of a torus on
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
decomposes into irreducible components, each of which is again a stable GIT quotient of a linear subspace of
V
by a Levi subgroup of
G
. More precisely, we fix a maximal torus
T
in
G
and let
W
be its Weyl group. For every morphism
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
of algebraic groups, we set
V_{\rho}=\{v\in V\mid t.v=\rho(t)v\text{ (all $t\in\mathscr{T}$)}\}
and define
G_{\rho}
to be the centralizer in
G
of
\im\rho
.

Theorem

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

The
\mathscr{T}\!
-fixed point locus
(V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G)^{\mathscr{T}}
decomposes into connected components
\smash{\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{\rho}}\smash{F_{\rho}}
indexed by a full set of representatives of morphisms of tori
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
up to conjugation with
W
. The component
F_{\rho}
equals the image of
V_{\rho}\cap V^{\st}(G,\theta)
in
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
, it is irreducible, and it is isomorphic to

V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}.

The index set of this disjoint union is infinite, but there are only finitely many
\rho
for which
F_{\rho}
is non-empty. This is discussed in Section
7
.

There are two main ingredients of the proof of the above main result. The first is concerned with the description of the intersection of the (semi-)stable locus with
V_{\rho}
.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Let
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
be a morphism of algebraic groups. Then

(1)

V_{\rho}\cap V^{\sst}(G,\theta)=V_{\rho}^{\sst}(G_{\rho},\theta)
;

(2)

V_{\rho}\cap V^{\st}(G,\theta)=V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)
.

The proof of the first statement uses Kempf’s theory of optimal destabilizing one-parameter subgroups
[
Kem78
]
. For the second statement, we need to define, associated with a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda
of
G
and a vector
v\in V
, a Zariski-closed subset
Y_{[\lambda]}(v)
of
G/P_{\lambda}
and find a
\mathscr{T}\!
-fixed point of it. The definition of
Y_{[\lambda]}(v)
can be found in Section
3
.

The second major auxiliary result is Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The induced morphism
i_{\rho}\colon V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}\to V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
is a closed immersion.

To show this, it is enough to prove that
i_{\rho}
is proper, injective (on
\mathbb{C}
-valued points) and that it induces an injective map on tangent spaces. We show all three assertions in Section
5
. Properness is shown using a result of Luna.

Throughout the paper, we work over the field of complex numbers. All results hold over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, though. In positive characteristic, Lemma
4.1
, which is used to assign to every component of the fixed point locus a morphism
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
of algebraic groups, fails; see Example
4.2
. Therefore, we do not know how to describe the fixed point locus over a field of positive characteristic.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we fix the setup. Section
3
recalls Kempf’s theory of optimal destabilizing one-parameter subgroups; we define the sets
Y_{[\lambda]}(v)
. In Section
4
, we show that lifts of fixed points induce a morphism
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
, and we prove Theorem
4.6
. Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.1
. In Section
6
, the main result is stated and proved. Section
7
is concerned with finiteness conditions on the index set of the irreducible components of the fixed point locus.
In Sections
8
and
9
, we apply our theory to some classes of quotients. We discuss quiver moduli and show how to derive Weist’s result
[
Wei13
, Theorem 3.8]
from ours in Section
8
. In Section
9
, we discuss the case where
G
is a torus and compare our description of the fixed point locus with the characterization in terms of the toric fan.

2. Setup

Let us introduce the setup with which we are going to work throughout the article. We are interested in GIT quotients of complex vector spaces by actions of reductive complex algebraic groups. As references for actions of algebraic groups and (geometric) invariant theory, the reader may consult, for instance,
[
Bri10
,
Dol03
,
MFK94
]
.

We work over the complex numbers. Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group. Let
V
be a finite-dimensional representation of
G
. For the action of an element
g\in G
on a vector
v\in V
, we write
gv
. We consider
V
as an affine space, and we are interested in GIT quotients for this action. Let
X^{*}(G)
be the group of characters of
G
.
For
\chi\in X^{*}(G)
, we define

\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,\chi}=\{f\in\mathbb{C}[V]\mid f(gv)=\chi(g)f(v)\text{ all $g\in G$, $v\in V$}\}

and call
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,\chi}
a
\chi
-semi-invariant function.
Fix
\theta\in X^{*}(G)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
v\in V
.

(1)

We call
v
a
\theta
-
semi-stable
point if there exist
n>0
and
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,n\theta}
such that
f(v)\neq 0
. Let
V^{\sst}(G,\theta)\subseteq V
be the subset of
\theta
-semi-stable points.

(2)

The point
v
is called
\theta
-
stable
if
v
is
\theta
-semi-stable and has finite stabilizer and
G\cdot v
is closed in
V^{\sst}(G,\theta)
. Let
V^{\st}(G,\theta)
be the set of
\theta
-stable points.

(3)

We call
v
a
\theta
-
unstable
point if
v
is not
\theta
-semi-stable. Let
V^{\unst}(G,\theta)
be the set of
\theta
-unstable points.

Remark 2.2
.

For a character
\chi\in X^{*}(G)
, we define the
G
-linearized line bundle
L(\chi)
as the trivial line bundle together with the
G
-action on the total space
G\times V\times\mathbb{C}\to V\times\mathbb{C}
given by
g\cdot(v,z)=(gv,\chi(g)z)
. Then

H^{0}(V,L(\chi))^{G}=\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,\chi};

i.e.

G
-invariant sections of
L(\chi)
are the same as
\chi
-semi-invariant functions. This shows that a point
v\in V
is
\theta
-semi-stable if and only if it is semi-stable with respect to
L(\theta)
in the sense of Mumford (see
[
MFK94
, Definition 1.7]
). Our notion of
\theta
-stability agrees with proper stability with respect to
L(\theta)
, as defined in
[
MFK94
, Definition 1.8]
.

We define the graded ring
\mathbb{C}[V]_{\theta}^{G}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,n\theta}
. We obtain a good categorical quotient

\pi\colon V^{\sst}(G,\theta)\longrightarrow V^{\sst}(G,\theta)/\!\!/G:=V/\!\!/_{L(\theta)}G=\Proj\left(\mathbb{C}[V]_{\theta}^{G}\right).

We also define
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G:=\pi(V^{\st}(G,\theta))
. The restriction of
\pi
gives a geometric quotient.

Let us recall the Hilbert–Mumford criterion for (semi-)stability from
[
MFK94
]
. We are using the form stated in King’s paper
[
Kin94
, Proposition 2.5]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Hilbert–Mumford criterion)
.

Let
v\in V
.

(1)

The point
v
is
\theta
-semi-stable if and only if
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle\geq 0
for every one-parameter subgroup
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
for which
\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v
exists.

(2)

The point
v
is
\theta
-stable if and only if
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle>0
for every non-trivial one-parameter subgroup
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
for which
\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v
exists.

We will assume the following to hold throughout the text.

Assumption 2.4
.

We suppose that
G
acts freely on
V^{\st}(G,\theta)
.

Assumption
2.4
guarantees that
\pi\colon V^{\st}(G,\theta)\to V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
is a principal
G
-bundle in the étale topology. This follows from Luna’s slice theorem
[
Lun73
, Section III.1]
. Étale local triviality implies smoothness of
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
.

Consider a torus
\mathscr{T}:=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{T}
acts linearly on
V
in such a way that the actions of
G
and
\mathscr{T}
commute.

Lemma 2.5
.

The subsets
V^{\st}(G,\theta)
and
V^{\sst}(G,\theta)
are
\mathscr{T}\!
-invariant.

Proof.

Both assertions are easily proved using the Hilbert–Mumford criterion. We will only show the
\mathscr{T}\!
-invariance of the stable locus. The invariance of the semi-stable locus is entirely analogous.

Let
v\in V^{\st}(G,\theta)
and
t\in\mathscr{T}
. Suppose that
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
is a one-parameter subgroup for which
\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)t.v
exists. As the actions of
\mathscr{T}
and
G
commute, we get
\eta(z)t.v=t.\eta(z)v
, and hence

\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v=t^{-1}.\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)t.v

exists. The stability of
v
implies
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle>0
.
∎

This implies that the
\mathscr{T}\!
-action descends to an action on the quotient
V^{\sst}(G,\theta)/\!\!/G
and to
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
. We are going to describe the fixed point locus of the latter action.

Remark 2.6
.

As we work in characteristic zero, the group
G
is linearly reductive, so the finite-dimensional representation
V
decomposes into irreducible representations, say
V\cong V_{1}^{n_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{l}^{n_{l}}
, where
V_{1},\ldots,V_{l}
are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible representations of
G
. An application of Schur’s lemma shows that
t\in\mathscr{T}
leaves the summand
V_{i}^{n_{i}}
invariant. Moreover, as
\mathbb{C}
is algebraically closed, Schur’s lemma also tells us that
t
acts on
V_{i}^{n_{i}}\cong V_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
as an element of
\GL_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
. The thus obtained
\mathscr{T}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
can be diagonalized: we find a basis
e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,n_{i}}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
such that
\mathscr{T}
operates on
e_{i,a}
with weight
w_{i,a}\in X^{*}(\mathscr{T})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. This means that every vector
v\in V
can be decomposed in a unique way as
v=\smash{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{l}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{n_{i}}v_{i,a}\otimes e_{i,a}
with
v_{i,a}\in V_{i}
and a torus element
t\in\mathscr{T}
acts on
v
by

t.v=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{l}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{n_{i}}w_{i,a}(t)v_{i,a}\otimes e_{i,a}.

The irreducible representations of
G
are completely classified. Choose a maximal torus
T
of
G
. Let
D\subseteq X^{*}(T)
be the set of dominant weights. For every
\lambda\in D
, there exists a unique representation
M_{G}(\lambda)
of
G
whose highest weight is
\lambda
. For a weight
\mu\in X^{*}(T)
, the dimension
d_{\lambda,\mu}
of the weight space
M_{G}(\lambda)_{\mu}
is combinatorially determined as the number of ways to write
\lambda-\mu
as a sum of positive roots.

Let
T
be a maximal torus of
G
. Let
\mu\in X^{*}(T)
. By
V_{\mu}(T)
we denote the weight space of weight
\mu
. In the same vein, for
w\in X^{*}(\mathscr{T})
, we let
V_{w}(\mathscr{T})
be the weight space of weight
w
with respect to the
\mathscr{T}\!
-action. As the actions commute, we obtain an action of
T\times\mathscr{T}
. The weight space
V_{(\mu,w)}(T\times\mathscr{T})
agrees with
V_{\mu}(T)\cap V_{w}(\mathscr{T})
.
Let
N_{T}(V)=\{\mu\in X^{*}(T)\mid V_{\mu}(T)\neq 0\}
be the set of weights of the
T
-action on
V
. Define
N_{\mathscr{T}}(V)
and
N_{T\times\mathscr{T}}(V)
in just the same way.

3. Optimal destabilizing one-parameter subgroups

The theory of optimal destabilizing one-parameter subgroups is developed by Kempf in
[
Kem78
]
; see also
[
Kem18
]
for a transcript of a preliminary version of the article which contains simpler, more accessible versions of the main results. Kempf’s theory is used by Hesselink
[
Hes78
]
, Kirwan
[
Kir84
]
, and Ness
[
Nes84
]
to give a stratification of the unstable locus. A treatment of Kempf’s theory for instability with respect to a character can be found in Hoskin’s paper
[
Hos14
]
. We are going to recall the basics of this theory for the convenience of the reader.

Assume that
v
is unstable with respect to the
G
-action and the stability condition
\theta
. Then, by the Hilbert–Mumford criterion, there exists a one-parameter subgroup
\eta
of
G
such that
\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v
exists and
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle<0
.

Kempf shows in
[
Kem78
]
how to find a one-parameter subgroup which is “most responsible” for the instability of
v
. To this end, we again fix a maximal torus
T\subseteq G
and choose an inner product
(\underline{\ \ },\underline{\ \ })
on the vector space
X_{*}(T){R}
which is
W
-invariant (
i.e.

(w\lambda w^{-1},w\mu w^{-1})=(\lambda,\mu)
for all
\lambda,\mu\in X_{*}(T){R}
) and for which
(\lambda,\lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}
provided that
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
. We thus obtain a
W
-invariant norm
\|\underline{\ \ }\|
on
X_{*}(T){R}
whose square takes integral values on
X_{*}(T)
.
For any
\eta\in X_{*}(G)
, there exists a
g\in G
such that
g\eta g^{-1}\in X_{*}(T)
. By the Weyl group invariance of the norm,
setting

\|\eta\|:=\|g\eta g^{-1}\|

is well defined. By definition, the value of
\|\eta\|
is invariant under conjugation by
G
.

Now for any
v\in V
, we set

m_{G}^{\theta}(v):=\inf\left\{\frac{\langle\theta,\eta\rangle}{\|\eta\|}\ \middle|\ 1\neq\eta\in X_{*}(G)\text{ such that }\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v\text{ exists}\right\}.

Obviously,
v\in V^{\unst}(G,\theta)
if and only if
m_{G}^{\theta}(v)<0
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
v\in V^{\unst}(G,\theta)
. A one-parameter subgroup
\lambda\in X_{*}(G)
is called
adapted
to
v
if

•

\lim_{z\to 0}\lambda(z)v
exists, and

•

\frac{\langle\theta,\lambda\rangle}{\|\lambda\|}=m_{G}^{\theta}(v)
.

We call a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda

primitive
if it is not divisible by any integer
d\geq 2
.
Let

\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v):=\{\lambda\in X_{*}(G)\mid\lambda\text{ is primitive and adapted to $v$}\}.

Let
G_{\lambda}
be the centralizer of the image of
\lambda
inside
G
, and let
P_{\lambda}
be the closed subgroup

P_{\lambda}=\left\{g\in G\ \middle|\ \lim_{z\to 0}\lambda(z)g\lambda(z)^{-1}\text{ exists}\right\}.

Then
P_{\lambda}
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi factor
G_{\lambda}
. It can also be described as the closed subgroup whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
is the sum of the non-negative weight spaces
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda}_{n}
with respect to the adjoint action of
\lambda
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The following result is
[
Kem78
, Theorem 2.2]
; we use the form stated in
[
Hos14
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Kempf)
.

Let
v\in V^{\unst}(G,\theta)
. Then:

(1)

We have
\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

We have
\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(gv)=g\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)g^{-1}
.

(3)

For any two
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)
, the parabolic subgroups
P_{\lambda}
and
P_{\lambda^{\prime}}
agree. We denote this subgroup by
P_{v}
.

(4)

The subset
\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)\subseteq X_{*}(G)
is a
P_{v}
-conjugacy class.

(5)

For every maximal torus
H\subseteq P_{v}
, there exists a unique
\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)\cap X_{*}(H)
.

Remark 3.3
.

If the vector
v
is
\theta
-semi-stable, then we have
\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)\neq\emptyset
as well, and for a given torus
H
, there is still a primitive one-parameter subgroup
\lambda
of
H
which is adapted to
v
, but
\lambda
need not be unique. This is because
[
Kem18
, Lemma 1]
requires a certain function to attain a positive value somewhere, which is not necessarily true if
v
is semi-stable. Hence, we cannot conclude that
P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, nor will
\Lambda_{G}^{\theta}(v)
be a conjugacy class.

For our purposes, we need some further results of this kind. Fix a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda_{0}\in X_{*}(G)
. Denote by
P_{0}:=P_{\lambda_{0}}
its associated parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
[\lambda_{0}]:=[\lambda_{0}]_{G}
be the
G
-conjugacy class inside
X_{*}(G)
. Define

f\colon[\lambda_{0}]\longrightarrow G/P_{0}

by
f(\lambda)=P_{\lambda}
. Note that we have identified the closed points of
G/P_{0}
with the set of all parabolic subgroups of
G
which are conjugate to
P_{0}
. The map
f
is well defined as for
\lambda=g\lambda_{0}g^{-1}
, we have
f(\lambda)=P_{\lambda}=gP_{0}g^{-1}
. The above argument also shows that
f
is
G
-equivariant and surjective.

Lemma 3.4
.

The fiber of
f
in
P\in G/P_{0}
is a single
P
-conjugacy class.

Proof.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in[\lambda_{0}]
be such that
f(\lambda)=f(\mu)=P
. Choose
g,h\in G
such that
\lambda=g\lambda_{0}g^{-1}
and
\mu=h\lambda_{0}h^{-1}
. Then
P=gP_{0}g^{-1}=hP_{0}h^{-1}
and thus
p_{0}:=g^{-1}h\in P_{0}
. We see that

\mu=h\lambda_{0}h^{-1}=gp_{0}\lambda_{0}p_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}=\bigl(\underbrace{gp_{0}g^{-1}}_{=:p\in P}\bigr)\,g\lambda_{0}g^{-1}\left(gp_{0}g^{-1}\right)^{-1}=p\lambda p^{-1}.

Conversely, if
f(\lambda)=P
and
\mu=p\lambda p^{-1}
for some
p\in P
, then it is easy to see that
f(\mu)=P
.
∎

Now let
v\in V
and consider the set

L_{G}(v):=\left\{\lambda\in X_{*}(G)\ \middle|\ \lim_{z\to 0}\lambda(z)v\text{ exists}\right\}.

This set can be described as follows. Consider
V
as a representation
V(\lambda)
of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
via
\lambda
, and consider the weight space decomposition
V(\lambda)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(\lambda)
,
i.e.

V_{n}(\lambda):=\{w\in V\mid\lambda(z)w=z^{n}w\text{ (all $z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}$)}\}.

Decompose the vector
v
as
v=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}(\lambda)
with
v_{n}(\lambda)\in V_{n}(\lambda)
. We obtain
L_{G}(v)=\{\lambda\in X_{*}(G)\mid v_{n}(\lambda)=0\text{ (all $n<0$)}\}
.

Remark 3.5
.

For
\lambda\in L_{G}(v)
and
p\in P_{\lambda}
, we have
p\lambda p^{-1}\in L_{G}(v)
.

Definition 3.6
.

We define
Y_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v):=f([\lambda_{0}]\cap L_{G}(v))
.

Proposition 3.7
.

The set
Y_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)
is a Zariski-closed subset of
G/P_{0}
.

Proof.

To show this proposition, we consider the subset

X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v):=\left\{g\in G\ \middle|\ \lim_{z\to 0}g\lambda_{0}(z)g^{-1}v\text{ exists}\right\}.

Evidently,
X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)
is the inverse image of
Y_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)
under the quotient map
\pi\colon G\to G/P_{0}
. This shows that
X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)
is
P_{0}
-invariant. As
\pi
maps
P_{0}
-invariant closed subsets of
G
to closed subsets of
G/P_{0}
, the proposition will follow from the lemma below.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The subset
X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)
is Zariski closed in
G
.

Proof.

We introduce some notation. Let
V_{n}:=V_{n}(\lambda_{0})
and
V_{\geq 0}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\geq 0}V_{m}
. For
g\in G
, we let

V_{n}^{(g)}:=V_{n}\left(g\lambda_{0}g^{-1}\right)=gV_{n}

and
\smash{v_{n}^{(g)}}=v_{n}(g\lambda_{0}g^{-1})\in V_{n}^{(g)}

\displaystyle X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)

\displaystyle=\left\{g\in G\mid v_{n}^{(g)}=0\text{ (all $n<0$)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{g\in G\mid g^{-1}v\in V_{\geq 0}\right\}.

This can be translated into a fiber product diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{[\lambda_{0}]}(v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{\geq 0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{-1}v}

which shows that

Let

5. An embedding of quotients

We now look at the inclusion of the linear subspace
V_{\rho}
into
V
. The intersection with

yields a closed embedding
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)\to V^{\st}(G,\theta)
. It is
G_{\rho}
-invariant. We obtain a morphism
i_{\rho}\colon V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}\to V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
. We will show the following in this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

The induced morphism
i_{\rho}\colon V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}\to V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
is a closed immersion.

In a previous version of this paper, the above theorem was only shown under the assumption that there were no
T
-invariants for a maximal torus

of

. Sergey Mozgovoy suggested to us an argument to get rid of this assumption. It goes along the lines of
[
Moz23
, Lemma 6.6]
and uses the machinery of étale slices. After we received his suggestion, we found a related but different argument which allows us to show a finiteness statement on the affine quotients, which might be of independent interest. This is Proposition
5.4
. From this proposition and two other lemmas, Theorem
5.1
will follow.

We use the following criterion for closed immersions. It can be stated in greater generality. We have adapted it to our situation. A proof of (a more general version of) the criterion below is due to Osserman.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of complex varieties. If

(1)

f
is proper,

(2)

f
is injective on closed points, and

(3)

the induced map
d_{x}f\colon T_{x}X\to T_{f(x)}Y
is injective for every closed point
x\in X
,

then
f
is a closed immersion.

We will prove these three properties for the morphism
i_{\rho}
. We need some auxiliary results. The following is the main result of
[
Lun75
]
.

Theorem 5.3

(Luna)
.

Let
X
be an affine variety,
G
a reductive algebraic group which acts on
X
, and
H\subseteq G
a closed reductive subgroup. Then
N_{G}(H)
is reductive, and
X^{H}/\!\!/N_{G}(H)\to X/\!\!/G
is a finite morphism.

The proof uses the machinery of étale slices, which requires the ground field to be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. Let us mention that a version of this result exists in positive characteristic as well; see
[
BGM19
, Theorem 1.1]
. We use Luna’s result to show the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

The morphism
V_{\rho}/\!\!/G_{\rho}\to V/\!\!/G
is finite.

Proof.

Let

, where

is the rank of
\mathscr{T}
. On

We obtain the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V/\!\!/G}}

.

The map

Proof.

Let

We now consider the tangent spaces. The tangent space of a

Let

x_{2}=gx_{1}
. Then

t.x_{2}=\rho_{2}(t)x_{2}=\rho_{2}(t)gx_{1}

and on the other hand

t.x_{2}=t.gx_{1}=gt.x_{1}=g\rho_{1}(t)x_{1}.

This implies that
g^{-1}\rho_{2}(t)gx_{1}=\rho_{1}(t)x_{1}
, and the stability of
x_{1}
implies that
\rho_{1}(t)=g^{-1}\rho_{2}(t)g
as the
G
-action on the stable locus is free.
∎

We fix a maximal torus
T\subseteq G
. Let
W=N_{G}(T)/T
be the corresponding Weyl group.
Now observe that for any
g\in G
, we get
F_{\rho_{x}}=F_{\rho_{gx}}
and
\rho_{gx}=g\rho_{x}g^{-1}
. The image of the morphism
\rho_{x}
is a torus; it is therefore contained in a maximal torus of
G
. As far as the sets
F_{\rho_{x}}
are concerned, we may hence without loss of generality assume the image of
\rho_{x}
to be contained in the fixed maximal torus
T\subseteq G
—for if not, we may replace
x
with
gx
for a suitable
g
as all maximal tori are conjugate. By a slight abuse of notation, we also denote the morphism
\mathscr{T}\to T
by
\rho
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
be morphisms. Assume that there exists an
h\in G
such that
\rho_{2}=h\rho_{1}h^{-1}
. Then there exists a
g\in N_{G}(T)
such that
\rho_{2}=g\rho_{1}g^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in T
, and suppose they are conjugate. Then, by
[
Car93
, Proposition 3.7.1]
, there exists a
g\in N_{G}(T)
such that
s_{2}=gs_{1}g^{-1}
. The book
[
Car93
]
deals with finite groups of Lie type, but the proposition holds true for every algebraic group which has a BN pair.

Hence for every
t\in\mathscr{T}
, there exists a
g_{t}\in N_{G}(T)
such that
\rho_{2}(t)=g_{t}\rho_{1}(t)g_{t}^{-1}
. The element
g_{t}
is unique up to
N_{G}(T)\cap C_{G}(\rho_{1}(t))
. Let
U=\{t\in\mathscr{T}\mid C_{G}(\rho_{1}(t))=C_{G}(\rho_{1})\}
. This is an open dense subset of
\mathscr{T}
. Recall that
C_{G}(\rho_{1})=G_{\rho_{1}}
. We obtain a map
U\to N_{G}(T)/(N_{G}(T)\cap G_{\rho_{1}})
. This map is locally constant, and the irreducibility of
U
forces it to be constant, say of value
w
. We choose a lift
g
of
w
to
N_{G}(T)
. Then
\rho_{2}(t)=g\rho_{1}(t)g^{-1}
for all
t
in the dense subset
U
. This proves the lemma.
∎

We now state our main result, the description of the components of the fixed point locus of
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
under the action of
\mathscr{T}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The
\mathscr{T}\!
-fixed point locus
(V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G)^{\mathscr{T}}\!
decomposes into connected components
\smash{\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{\rho}}\smash{F_{\rho}}
indexed by a full set of representatives of morphisms of tori
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
up to conjugation with
W
. The component
F_{\rho}
equals
\pi(V_{\rho}\cap V^{\st}(G,\theta))
, it is irreducible, and it is isomorphic to

V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}.

Proof.

We have seen that every fixed point is contained in some subset
F_{\rho}
which was defined as
\pi(V_{\rho}\cap V^{\st}(G,\theta))
.
By Lemma
6.1
, different conjugacy classes yield disjoint
F_{\rho}
. It suffices to consider morphisms
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
up to Weyl group action by Lemma
6.2
and the reasoning before the lemma. By Theorem
4.6
,
F_{\rho}=\pi(\smash{V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)})
, and by Theorem
5.1
, the morphism
i_{\rho}
provides an isomorphism from
\smash{V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}}
onto its image, which is
F_{\rho}
, and which is closed. A variety of the form
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}
is obviously irreducible.
∎

7. A necessary condition for a non-empty fixed point component

The index set of the disjoint union in Theorem
6.3
is infinite; however, there are just finitely many
\rho
for which
F_{\rho}
is non-empty. It seems hard in this generality to give a necessary and sufficient condition for
F_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
. Still, it is possible to find a necessary condition which leaves us with just finitely many possibilities.

Recall the notation
N_{T}(V)
for the set of weights of the action of
T
on
V
. We also want to introduce the symbol
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)
for the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace of
X^{*}(T){Q}
which is spanned by
N_{T}(V)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

If there exists a vector
v\in V
with a finite stabilizer, then
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)=X^{*}(T){Q}
.

Proof.

Assume there are
\mu\in X^{*}(T)-(\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)\cap X^{*}(T))
. Let
N_{T}(V)=\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}\}
. Then we find a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to T
such that
\langle\mu,\lambda\rangle\neq 0
while
\langle\mu_{i},\lambda\rangle=0
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. The subgroup
\lambda(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
acts trivially on
V
, and as
\lambda
is non-trivial, this subgroup
\lambda(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is infinite.
∎

In particular, we see that
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)=X^{*}(T){Q}
is necessary for the existence of stable points for the
G
-action (with respect to any stability parameter
\theta
).

Proposition 7.2
.

(1)

Let
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
be a morphism. If
F_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V_{\rho})=X^{*}(T){Q}
.

(2)

The set
\{\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to T\text{ morphism }\mid\mathscr{N}_{T}(V_{\rho})=X^{*}(T){Q}\}
is finite.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Lemma
7.1
and the characterization of
F_{\rho}
as
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G_{\rho},\theta)/G_{\rho}
. The second claim follows from the identity
N_{T}(V_{\rho})=\{\mu\in X^{*}(T)\mid(\mu,\rho^{*}(\mu))\in N_{T\times\mathscr{T}}(V)\}
(see Remark
4.5
) and the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be lattices. Let
E\subseteq X\times Y
be a finite subset. For a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
f\colon X\to Y
, consider
E_{f}:=\{x\in X\mid(x,f(x))\in E\}
. Then the set

\left\{f\in\Hom{Z}(X,Y)\mid\langle E_{f}\rangle{Q}=X{Q}\right\}

is finite.

Proof.

Let
F
be the set in question. Let
E_{X}
and
E_{Y}
be the images of
E
under the projections
X\times Y\to X
and
X\times Y\to Y
. Assume that
F\neq\emptyset
. Then
\langle E_{X}\rangle{Q}=X{Q}
.

We equip
X{R}
and
Y{R}
with norms, both denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. For
f\in\Hom(X{R},Y{R})
, let
\left\lVert f\right\rVert=\max_{\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq 1}\left\lVert f(x)\right\rVert
be the operator norm associated with these two norms. Define

R:=\max_{y\in E_{Y}}\left\lVert y\right\rVert.

Let
B\subseteq E_{X}
be a subset which is an
\mathbb{R}
-basis of
X{R}
. Then we find a unique scalar product on
X{R}
for which
B
is an orthonormal basis. Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{B}
be the associated norm on
X{R}
, and let
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B}=\max_{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{B}\leq 1}\left\lVert f(x)\right\rVert
be the corresponding operator norm on
\Hom(X{R},Y{R})
. As
\Hom(X{R},Y{R})
is finite-dimensional, this norm is equivalent to the operator norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
from above. We find
d_{B},D_{B}>0
such that
d_{B}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B}\leq D_{B}\left\lVert f\right\rVert
for all
f\in\Hom(X{R},Y{R})
. Let
d>0
be the minimum of all
d_{B}
, where
B
ranges over all bases
B\subseteq E_{X}
.

Now let
f\in F
. Then
\langle E_{f}\rangle{R}=X{R}
and
f(E_{f})\subseteq E_{Y}
. Choose a basis
B=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq E_{f}
of
X{R}
. Let
x=\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}x_{i}\in X
be such that
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{B}\leq 1
. This implies
\smash{\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}=\sumop\displaylimits\lvert\lambda_{i}\rvert^{2}\leq 1
, where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
. It is well known that
\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{2}
. Now

\displaystyle\left\lVert f(x)\right\rVert\leq\underbrace{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\lambda_{i}\rvert}_{\leq\sqrt{n}}\underbrace{\left\lVert f(x_{i})\right\rVert}_{\leq R}\leq R\sqrt{n}

and therefore
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B}\leq R\sqrt{n}
. This tells us
\left\lVert f\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{d_{B}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B}\leq\frac{R\sqrt{n}}{d}
. We have shown that the set
\{f\in\Hom(X{R},Y{R})\mid\langle E_{f}\rangle{R}=X{R}\}
is bounded. The set
F
is the set of lattice points of this set and is therefore finite.
∎

8. Application: Quiver moduli

Before we discuss the general theory of torus actions on quiver moduli, let us look at a well-known example first.

Example 8.1
.

Let
G=\GL_{m}(\mathbb{C})
, which acts on
V=M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{C})
, the space of
m\times n
matrices, by left multiplication. Let
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{m}
be the natural representation of
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. Then
V\cong V_{0}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Assume that
m\leq n
. Let
\theta=\det\in X^{*}(G)
. The ring
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G,\det}
is generated by the maximal minors of an
m\times n
matrix. This shows that a matrix
A
is
\theta
-semi-stable if and only if its rank is
m
. Every semi-stable point is therefore stable. The quotient
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
identifies with the Grassmannian
\Gr_{n-m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by assigning to a matrix
A
of full rank its kernel. Fix the maximal torus
T
of diagonal matrices. The character lattice is
X^{*}(T)=\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{m}
, where
\varepsilon_{i}(\diag(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}))=s_{i}
. Note that the first fundamental weight is
\omega_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}
and the natural representation
V_{0}
has highest weight
\omega_{1}
.

Let
\mathscr{T}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
We choose integers
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
. We let
\mathscr{T}
act on
V
by right multiplication with
\operatorname{diag}(t^{w_{1}},\ldots,t^{w_{n}})
. Let us assume that
w_{1}\geq\ldots\geq w_{n}
, and let
0=p_{0}<p_{1}<\ldots<p_{k}=n
be such that

w_{1}=\dots=w_{p_{1}}>w_{p_{1}+1}=\dots=w_{p_{2}}>\dots>w_{p_{k-1}+1}=\dots=w_{n}.

Define
q_{j}=p_{j}-p_{j-1}
for
j=1,\ldots,k
.

We now consider the fixed point components. Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to T
be a one-parameter subgroup; it is given by
\rho(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t^{r_{1}},\ldots,t^{r_{m}})
. As we consider one-parameter subgroups up to conjugation by
W=S_{m}
, we may assume
r_{1}\geq\ldots\geq r_{m}
. Let
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{l}=m
be such that

r_{1}=\dots=r_{s_{1}}>r_{s_{1}+1}=\dots=r_{s_{2}}>\dots>r_{s_{l-1}+1}=\dots=r_{m},

and define
t_{i}=s_{i}-s_{i-1}
for
i=1,\ldots,l
. We get
G_{\rho}=\GL_{t_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\times\dots\times\GL_{t_{l}}(\mathbb{C})
, viewed as block-diagonal matrices in
\GL_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. The linear subspace
V_{\rho}\subseteq V
by definition consists of all matrices
A=(a_{ij})
for which right multiplication with
\diag(t^{w_{1}},\ldots,t^{w_{n}})
agrees with left multiplication with
\diag(t^{r_{1}},\ldots,t^{r_{m}})
. This forces
a_{ij}=0
whenever
r_{i}\neq w_{j}
. Let
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,l\}
be the subset of those
i
for which there exists a
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

r_{s_{i}}=w_{p_{j}}.

Such an index
j
is unique. Let
I=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{u}\}
. The index corresponding to
i_{\nu}
will be denoted by
j_{\nu}
. We hence obtain an increasing sequence
j_{1}<\dots<j_{u}
. As a
G_{\rho}
-representation, the subspace
V_{\rho}
is isomorphic to

\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\nu=1}^{u}M_{t_{i_{\nu}}\times q_{j_{\nu}}}(\mathbb{C}),

where
G_{\rho}
acts on the
\nu^{\mathrm{th}}
summand by left multiplication with the
i_{\nu}^{\mathrm{th}}
factor in
G_{\rho}
.

For
F_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
, it is necessary by Proposition
7.2
that
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V_{\rho})=X^{*}(T){Q}
, which says that a matrix in
V_{\rho}
must have no zero rows. Thus
I=\{1,\ldots,l\}
. (This tallies with the observation that an
m\times n
matrix with rank
m
cannot have zero rows.) We get
i_{\nu}=\nu
and
k\geq l
. A matrix
A\in V_{\rho}
therefore has the following block shape:

\begin{pmatrix}\ldots&\boxed{t_{1}\times q_{j_{1}}}&\ldots&\\
&&\ldots&\boxed{t_{2}\times q_{j_{2}}}&\ldots\\
&&&&&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}\\
&&&&&&\ldots&\boxed{t_{l}\times q_{j_{l}}}&\ldots\\
\end{pmatrix}

A matrix of this block shape can only have maximal rank if
q_{j_{i}}\geq t_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,l
. If this is satisfied, then the fixed point component is, by Theorem
6.3
, isomorphic to

F_{\rho}\cong\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{l}\Gr_{t_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{q_{j_{i}}}).

The one-parameter subgroups
\rho
which occur are parametrized by the following finite set of data:

•

a number
l\leq\min\{m,k\}
and a sequence
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{l}=m
,

•

a sequence
1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{l}\leq k

such that
t_{i}:=s_{i}-s_{i-1}\leq q_{j_{i}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,l
.

Torus actions on quiver moduli contain Example
8.1
as a special case. Let
Q
be a quiver,
i.e.
an oriented graph. Formally,
Q
consists of two sets
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
and two maps
s,t\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
. The set
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices of
Q
, the set
Q_{1}
is the set of arrows, and for
a\in Q_{1}
, the vertices
s(a)
and
t(a)
are the source and target of
a
, respectively. We assume that for any two vertices
i
and
j
, there are just finitely many
a\in Q_{1}
such that
s(a)=i
and
t(a)=j
. A representation
M
of
Q
is a collection of complex vector spaces
(M_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
together with
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps
(M_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}
such that
M_{a}\colon M_{s(a)}\to M_{t(a)}
. If
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in Q_{0}}M_{i}
is finite-dimensional, we say that
M
is finite-dimensional and call
\dimvect M:=(\dim{C}M_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\smash{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}}
the dimension vector of
M
. Given two representations
M
and
N
of
Q
, a homomorphism
f\colon M\to N
is a collection
f_{i}\colon M_{i}\to N_{i}
of linear maps such that
N_{a}f_{s(a)}=f_{t(a)}M_{a}
holds for all
a\in Q_{1}
. In this way we obtain a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category. We refer to
[
ASS06
, Chapter II]
for more details.

Let
\Lambda(Q)=\{\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\mid\alpha_{i}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }i\}
, and let
\Lambda_{+}(Q)=\Lambda(Q)\cap\smash{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}}
.
Fix a vector
\alpha\in\Lambda_{+}(Q)
. Fix complex vector spaces
V_{i}
of dimension
\alpha_{i}
. Consider the (finite-dimensional) vector space

V:=R(Q,\alpha):=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{a\in Q_{1}}\Hom(V_{s(a)},V_{t(a)}).

An element of
V
can be regarded as a representation of
Q
of dimension vector
\alpha
. The group
G_{\alpha}=\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL(V_{i})
acts on
V
by change of basis. Two elements of
V
are isomorphic as representations if and only if they lie in the same
G_{\alpha}
-orbit. The central subgroup
\Delta:=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cdot\id
acts trivially on
V
. Therefore, the action descends to an action of
G:=PG_{\alpha}:=G_{\alpha}/\Delta
.

We will make a connection between weights of the action of a maximal torus of
G
and the support
\supp(\alpha)=\{i\in Q_{0}\mid\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}
of the dimension vector. To this end, fix a basis of every vector space
V_{i}
. Let
T_{\alpha}
be the maximal torus of
G_{\alpha}
of diagonal matrices with respect to our choices of bases. The image of
T_{\alpha}
under
G_{\alpha}\to PG_{\alpha}=G
is a maximal torus
T
of
G
. For
i\in Q_{0}
and
r\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha_{i}\}
, let
y_{i,r}\in X^{*}(T_{\alpha})
be the character which selects from
t=(t_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
the
r^{\mathrm{th}}
diagonal entry of the matrix
t_{i}
. Then
X^{*}(T_{\alpha})
is the free abelian group generated by the basis elements
y_{i,r}
, and

X^{*}(T)=\left\{\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in Q_{0}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}a_{i,r}y_{i,r}\ \middle|\ \sumop\displaylimits_{i\in Q_{0}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=1}^{\alpha_{i}}a_{i,r}=0\right\}\subseteq X^{*}(T_{\alpha});

it is generated as an abelian group by all differences
y_{j,s}-y_{i,r}
. Recall that
N_{T}(V)
is the set of weights of the
T
-action on
V
and
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)
is its
\mathbb{Q}
-linear span inside
X^{*}(T){Q}
. As
G
acts by conjugation, we have

N_{T}(V)=\left\{y_{t(a),s}-y_{s(a),r}\mid a\in Q_{1},\ r\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha_{s(a)}\},\ s\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha_{t(a)}\}\right\}.

Lemma 8.2
.

The full subquiver supported on
\supp(\alpha)
is connected if and only if
\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)=X^{*}(T){Q}
.

Proof.

We may without loss of generality assume that
\supp(\alpha)=Q_{0}
. To show the direction from left to right, assume that
Q
is connected. This means that for any two vertices
i
and
j
, there exists an unoriented path which connects them. More precisely, there exist arrows
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and signs
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

s(a_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}})=i,\quad t(a_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}})=s(a_{2}^{\varepsilon_{2}}),\quad\ldots,\quad t(a_{k-1}^{\varepsilon_{k-1}})=s(a_{k}^{\varepsilon_{k}}),\quad t(a_{k}^{\varepsilon_{k}})=j.

Here,
a^{1}:=a
and
a^{-1}
is a formal symbol for which we define
s(a^{-1})=t(a)
and
t(a^{-1})=s(a)
. Given a character
y_{j,s}-y_{i,r}
, we choose an unoriented path connecting
i
and
j
as above and indices
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k},s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}
with
r_{\nu}\in\{1,\ldots,\smash{\alpha_{s(a_{\nu}^{\varepsilon_{\nu}})}}\}
and
s_{\nu}\in\{1,\ldots,\smash{\alpha_{t(a_{\nu}^{\varepsilon_{\nu}})}}\}
such that

r_{1}=r,\quad s_{1}=r_{2},\quad\ldots,\quad s_{k-1}=r_{k},\quad s_{k}=s.

This yields

y_{j,s}-y_{i,r}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\nu=1}^{k}\left(y_{t(a_{\nu}^{\varepsilon_{\nu}}),s_{\nu}}-y_{s(a_{\nu}^{\varepsilon_{\nu}}),r_{\nu}}\right)\in\mathscr{N}_{T}(V).

Suppose conversely that
Q
is not connected. Then there exist two non-empty disjoint subsets
C,D\subseteq Q_{0}
such that
C\sqcup D=Q_{0}
and such that there are no arrows from one to the other. We let
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
act on
V_{i}
by weight
0
if
i\in C
and by weight
1
if
i\in D
. This provides us with a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to T
; let
\varphi^{*}\colon X^{*}(T)\to\mathbb{Z}
be the induced homomorphism on characters. If
i\in C
and
j\in D
, then

\varphi^{*}(y_{j,s}-y_{i,r})=1

for any
r\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha_{i}\}
and
s\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha_{j}\}
. But as all arrows in
Q
start and end in either
C
or
D
, we see that
N_{T}(V)\subseteq\ker(\varphi^{*})
. Therefore,
y_{j,s}-y_{i,r}\notin\mathscr{N}_{T}(V)
.
∎

Let
\theta=(\theta_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
be such that
\theta^{T}\cdot\alpha=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\theta_{i}\alpha_{i}=0
. We may interpret
\theta
as a character of
G_{\alpha}
by the assignment
g\mapsto\smash{\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in Q_{0}}}\smash{\det(g_{i})^{\theta_{i}}}
. The vanishing condition guarantees that it descends to a character of
G
; by a slight abuse of notation, we also denote this character by
\theta
. By
[
Kin94
, Proposition 3.1]
a representation
M\in V
is semi-stable with respect to
\theta
if and only if
\theta(U)\geq 0
for all subrepresentations
U\subseteq M
; here
\theta(U)
is by definition
\theta^{T}\cdot\dimvect U
. Moreover,
M
is
\theta
-stable if and only if
\theta(U)>0
for all non-zero proper subrepresentations
U
of
M
. As the
\theta
-stable representations are the simple objects in an appropriate abelian category (see
[
Rei03
, Lemma 2.3]
), Schur’s lemma tells us that the automorphism group of a
\theta
-stable representation
M
is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Therefore,
G
acts freely on the stable locus
V^{\st}(G,\theta)
. Assumption
2.4
is hence fulfilled. Set

M^{\theta\hypst}(Q,\alpha):=V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G.

Now assume that
Q_{0}
(and hence also
Q_{1}
) is a finite set.
Let
\mathscr{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{Q_{1}}
. Let a group element
t=(t_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\in\mathscr{T}
act on
M=(M_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\in V
by

9. Application: Torus quotients

We are going to discuss quotients
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
in the case when
G
is a torus. In this case,
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
is a toric variety.
We will first describe the toric structure of
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
. We believe this should be well known, but we could not find it in the literature in the form we need it for our purposes. As a reference for toric geometry we use Fulton’s book
[
Ful93
]
.

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
m
and
G=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
a torus which we assume to act linearly on
V
. Let
a\colon G\to\GL(V)
be the induced morphism of algebraic groups. Decompose
V
into weight spaces
V=\smash{\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\chi\in X^{*}(G)}}V_{\chi}
. Let
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{p}
be the characters which occur in this decomposition, and let
e_{s,1},\ldots,e_{s,m_{s}}
be a basis of
V_{\chi_{s}}
for
s=1,\ldots,p
. Let
T
be the maximal torus of
\GL(V)
which corresponds to the basis
(e_{1,1},\ldots,e_{1,m_{1}},\ldots,e_{p,1},\ldots,e_{p,m_{p}})
of
V
. Then
a
factors through a morphism of tori
G\to T
, which we also call
a
. Concretely,

a(g)=(\underbrace{\chi_{1}(g),\ldots,\chi_{1}(g)}_{m_{1}\text{ times}},\ldots,\underbrace{\chi_{p}(g),\ldots,\chi_{p}(g)}_{m_{p}\text{ times}}).

Let
x_{s,k}\in X^{*}(T)
be the respective coordinate function.

Let
\theta\in X^{*}(G)
. We describe the semi-stable and the stable locus with respect to
\theta
.
Let

I=\{(s,k)\mid s\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\ k\in\{1,\ldots,m_{s}\}\}.

Write a vector
v\in V
as
\smash{\sumop\displaylimits_{(s,k)\in I}}v_{s,k}e_{s,k}
. We define
\supp(v)=\{(s,k)\in I\mid v_{s,k}\neq 0\}
. Let
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
be a one-parameter subgroup. Then

\eta(z)v=\sumop\displaylimits_{(s,k)\in I}z^{\langle\chi_{s},\eta\rangle}v_{s,k}e_{s,k},

so the limit
\lim_{z\to 0}\eta(z)v
exists if and only if
\langle\eta,\chi_{s}\rangle\geq 0
for all
(s,k)\in\supp(v)
. This is a condition which depends just on the support of
v
, so
\theta
-semi-stability and
\theta
-stability do as well. Let us be more precise. Let
\pr\colon I\to\{1,\ldots,p\}
be the projection to the first coordinate. For
S\subseteq I
, we define

\tau_{S}:=\cone(\chi_{s})_{s\in\pr(S)}\subseteq X^{*}(G){R}.

It is a rational convex polyhedral cone. Its dual

\displaystyle\tau_{S}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\{y\in X_{*}(G){R}\mid\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\text{ (all $x\in\tau_{S}$)}\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in X_{*}(G){R}\mid\langle\chi_{s},y\rangle\geq 0\text{ (all $s\in\pr(S)$)}\}

is again a rational convex polyhedral cone. Denote by
\theta^{\vee}
the dual of the ray through
\theta
, and let
\theta^{+}=\{y\in X_{*}(G)\mid\langle\theta,y\rangle>0\}
. The Hilbert–Mumford criterion tells us the following.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
v\in V
and
S:=\supp(v)
.

(1)

The vector
v
is
\theta
-semi-stable if and only if
\tau_{S}^{\vee}\subseteq\theta^{\vee}
.

(2)

The vector
v
is
\theta
-stable if and only if
\tau_{S}^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\theta^{+}
.

We say a subset
S\subseteq I
is
\theta
-
semi-stable
if
\tau_{S}^{\vee}\subseteq\theta^{\vee}
, and we say
S
is
\theta
-
stable
if
\tau_{S}^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\theta^{+}
. The notions of (semi-)stability depend only on
\pr(S)
.

Lemma 9.2
.

If
S\subseteq I
is
\theta
-stable, then
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
spans
X^{*}(G){R}
.

Proof.

Assume that the
\mathbb{R}
-linear span
U
of
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
is a proper subspace of
X^{*}(G){R}
. Then there exists a
y\in X_{*}(G)\setminus\{0\}
such that
\langle x,y\rangle=0
for all
x\in U
. Therefore, both
y
and
-y
lie in
\tau_{S}^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\theta^{+}
, which implies
\langle\theta,y\rangle>0
and
\langle\theta,-y\rangle>0
, giving a contradiction.
∎

We call
S

minimally

\theta
-
stable
if
S
is
\theta
-stable and if no proper subset of
S
is
\theta
-stable.

Lemma 9.3
.

For a
\theta
-stable subset
S\subseteq I
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

The subset
S
is minimally
\theta
-stable.

(2)

We have
\left|S\right|=r
.

(3)

The set
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
is a basis of
X^{*}(G){R}
.

Proof.

Let us assume
S
is minimally stable, and let us show that
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
is a basis of
X^{*}(G){R}
. By Lemma
9.2
, it is enough to prove linear independence. Assume there are
(s^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in S
such that
\chi_{s^{\prime}}
lies in the span of the
\chi_{s}
with
(s,k)\in S^{\prime}:=S\setminus\{(s^{\prime},k^{\prime})\}
. Then
\tau_{S^{\prime}}^{\vee}=\tau_{S}^{\vee}
, and therefore
S^{\prime}
is also stable. This contradicts the minimality of

.

Now let
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
be a basis of
X^{*}(G){R}
. As
\dim X(G){R}=\dim G=r
, it is obvious that
\left|S\right|=r
.

Finally, if
\left|S\right|=r
, then there can be no proper
\theta
-stable subset

of

because the span of

∎

shows in particular that

Let
\mathscr{T}
be the cokernel of

Let

\displaystyle\Phi:=\Phi^{\st}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\{J\subseteq I\mid\tau_{J^{c}}^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\theta^{+}\}=\{J\subseteq I\mid J^{c}\text{ is $\theta$-stable}\},

In the above equation,

Lemma 9.4
.

Let

(1)

The elements
\pi_{*}\varepsilon_{s,k}
with

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
\sigma_{J}
is a simplex of dimension
\left|J\right|
.

(2)

If

\sigma_{J^{\prime}}
is a face of
\sigma_{J}
. Every face of
\sigma_{J}
is of this form.

(3)

For

\sigma_{J_{1}}\cap\sigma_{J_{2}}=\sigma_{J_{1}\cap J_{2}}
.

Proof.

(
1
) Assume there are
\lambda=\smash{\sumop\displaylimits_{(s,k)\in J}}a_{s,k}\varepsilon_{s,k}\in X_{*}(T)
with

. Then there exists an
\eta\in X_{*}(G)\setminus\{0\}

. For all

because

is supported on

\langle\chi_{s},\eta\rangle=0
for all

. In particular,

. As

, which implies

, giving a contradiction.

(
2
) The cone
\sigma_{J^{\prime}}
is the intersection of
\sigma_{J}
with the supporting hyperplanes
(\pi_{*}\varepsilon_{s,k})^{\bot}
, where

(
3
) Assume there are

. Then there exists an

\langle\chi_{s},\eta\rangle=\langle x_{s,k},\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\rangle=a_{s,k}-b_{s,k}=a_{s,k}\geq 0.

As

, this implies
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle>0
. But the same argument shows that we also have
\langle\theta,-\eta\rangle>0
. We therefore again have a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 9.5
.

The toric fan of

is
\Delta^{\st}(\theta)
.

Proof.

For
J\in\Phi

\sigma_{J}^{+}=\smash{\cone(\varepsilon_{s,k})_{(s,k)\in J}}

\smash{\sigma_{J}^{+}}

U_{\sigma_{J}^{+}}=\{v\in V\mid J^{c}\subseteq\supp(v)\}.

For

with

\theta
-stable by Lemma

We define
\Delta^{+}=\{\sigma_{J}^{+}\mid J\in\Phi\}
.
We have argued that the toric variety

associated with the fan
\Delta^{+}
is the stable locus
V^{\st}(G,\theta)
.
It is enough to show that there is a map
\pi\colon X_{\Delta^{+}}\to X_{\Delta}
which is a categorical
G
-quotient. Consider the map

\pi_{J}\colon U_{\sigma_{J}^{+}}\longrightarrow U_{\sigma_{J}}

induced by
\pi\colon T\to\mathscr{T}
.
By definition and an easy verification,

U_{\sigma_{J}}=\Spec\mathbb{C}\left[M\cap\sigma_{J}^{\vee}\right]=\Spec\mathbb{C}\left[M\cap\left(\pi_{*}\sigma_{J}^{+}\right)^{\vee}\right]=\Spec\mathbb{C}\left[M\cap(\pi^{*})^{-1}\left(\left(\sigma_{J}^{+}\right)^{\vee}\right)\right].

The ring
\mathbb{C}[M\cap(\pi^{*})^{-1}((\sigma_{J}^{+})^{\vee})]
agrees with
\mathbb{C}[X^{*}(T)\cap(\sigma_{J}^{+})^{\vee}]^{G}
, and therefore
\pi_{J}
is a universal categorical
G
-quotient. By Lemma
9.4
(
3
), the maps
\pi_{J}
and
\pi_{J^{\prime}}
agree on the intersection and therefore glue to
\pi\colon X_{\Delta^{+}}\to X_{\Delta}
which, also by Lemma
9.4
(
3
), is a categorical
G
-quotient.
∎

We return to the action of
\mathscr{T}
. By general theory
[
Ful93
, Section 3.1]
, the torus
\mathscr{T}
acts on
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G=X_{\Delta}
with finitely many orbits. The orbits are labeled by the faces of
\Delta
. In our case, the orbit corresponding to
\sigma_{J}
is

(#)

O_{\sigma_{J}}=\{\pi(v)\mid v\in V\text{ such that }\supp(v)=J^{c}\}/G.

The dimension of
O_{\sigma_{J}}
is
\dim V-\dim G-\dim\sigma_{J}=m-r-\left|J\right|
. The fixed points of the
\mathscr{T}\!
-action are therefore the orbits
O_{\sigma_{J}}
associated with those
J\in\Phi
for which
\left|J\right|=m-r
. By Lemma
9.3
, a set
J
with cardinality
m-r
is maximal in
\Phi
, and every maximal member of
\Phi
has cardinality
m-r
.

We should like to apply our results to this setup and compare them with the description of the fixed points coming from the toric fan. In order to do so, we need
\mathscr{T}
to act linearly on
V
. For this we choose, once and for all, a section
c\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
of
\pi\colon T\to\mathscr{T}
. Then
\mathscr{T}
acts on
V
via
t.v=c(t)v
. This
\mathscr{T}\!
-action automatically commutes with the
G
-action, and the induced
\mathscr{T}\!
-action on
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
agrees with the
\mathscr{T}\!
-action coming from the
T\!
-action on
V
.

The group
G
is already a torus. Let
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
be a morphism of algebraic groups. We get
G_{\rho}=G
and

\displaystyle V_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V\mid t.v=\rho(t)\cdot v\text{ (all $t\in\mathscr{T}$)}\}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V\mid c(t)v=a\rho(t)v\text{ (all $t\in\mathscr{T}$)}\}.

For
(s,k)\in I
, let
c_{s,k}\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be such that
c(t)=(c_{s,k}(t))_{(s,k)\in I}\in T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{I}
. Then
c(t)v=a\rho(t)v
holds if and only if
c_{s,k}(t)v_{s,k}=\chi_{s}(\rho(t))v_{s,k}
for all
(s,k)\in I
.
Define the set
S_{\rho}\subseteq I
as

S_{\rho}=\{(s,k)\in I\mid c_{s,k}(t)=\chi_{s}(\rho(t))\text{ (all $t\in\mathscr{T}$)}\}.

We see that
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
S_{\rho}
is
\theta
-stable.

Proposition 9.6
.

Let
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
be a morphism of algebraic groups.

(1)

If
S_{\rho}
is
\theta
-stable, then
S_{\rho}
is minimally
\theta
-stable.

(2)

If
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
are two morphisms of algebraic groups such that
S_{\rho_{1}}=S_{\rho_{2}}
, then
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
.

(3)

For every minimally
\theta
-stable subset
S\subseteq I
, there exists a morphism
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
such that
S_{\rho}=S
.

Proof.

(
1
) Let
S:=S_{\rho}
. By Lemma
9.3
, it is enough to show that
\left|S\right|\leq r
. Let
U
be the
\mathbb{R}
-linear span of
(x_{s,k})_{(s,k)\in S}
inside
X^{*}(T){R}
. This is an
\left|S\right|
-dimensional subspace. Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{*}(\mathscr{T}){R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{*}(\mathscr{T}){R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{*}(T){R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{*}(G){R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{*}}

whose bottom row is exact. We know that
\dim U=\dim a^{*}(U)+\dim(U\cap\ker a^{*})
. Let
u\in U\cap\ker a^{*}=U\cap\im\pi^{*}
; write it as
u=\pi^{*}v
. Then
v=c^{*}\pi^{*}v=c^{*}u=\rho^{*}a^{*}u=0
as
u\in\ker a^{*}
. Therefore,
U\cap\ker a^{*}=0
and thus

\left|S\right|=\dim U=\dim a^{*}(U)\leq\dim X^{*}(G){R}=r.

(
2
) Let
S:=S_{1}=S_{2}
. We have
\chi_{s}(\rho_{1}(t))=c_{s,k}(t)=\chi_{s}(\rho_{2}(t))
for all
(s,k)\in S
. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r}
be a basis of
X_{*}(G)
. It is enough to show that
\rho_{1}\varepsilon_{i}=\rho_{2}\varepsilon_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
. Consider
\eta_{i}\in X_{*}(G)
defined by

\eta_{i}(z)=\rho_{1}(\varepsilon_{i}(z))\rho_{2}(\varepsilon_{i}(z))^{-1}.

Then
\langle\chi_{s},\eta_{i}\rangle=0
for all
(s,k)\in S
. This implies that both
\eta_{i}
and
-\eta_{i}
belong to
\tau_{S}^{\vee}
. If
\eta_{i}
is non-trivial, it follows that
\langle\theta,\eta_{i}\rangle>0
and
\langle\theta,-\eta_{i}\rangle>0
, giving a contradiction. We have shown that
\rho_{1}\varepsilon_{i}=\rho_{2}\varepsilon_{i}
.

(
3
) If
S
is minimally
\theta
-stable, then
\smash{(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}}
is a basis of
X^{*}(G){R}
and therefore also a basis of
X^{*}(G){Q}
. Consider the homomorphism of abelian groups
\mathbb{Z}^{S}=X^{*}((\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{S})\to X^{*}(G)
which maps the basis vector
x_{s,k}
to
\chi_{s}
,
i.e.
the restriction of
a^{*}
. It induces a homomorphism of bialgebras

\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{S}]=\mathbb{C}[(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{S}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[X^{*}(G)]=\mathbb{C}[G].

This yields a morphism of algebraic groups, which on closed points is given as

a_{S}\colon G\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{S},\quad g\longmapsto(\chi_{s}(g))_{(s,k)\in S}.

As
\mathbb{C}[(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{S}]\to\mathbb{C}[G]
is injective,
a_{S}
is dominant. Moreover, because
(\chi_{s})_{(s,k)\in S}
generates
X^{*}(G){Q}
, the morphism
a_{S}
is finite. This implies that
a_{S}
is surjective.
By our Assumption
2.4
,
G
acts freely on the non-empty set

\{v\in V^{\st}(G,\theta)\mid\supp(v)=S\}.

As a consequence,
a_{S}
is also injective. In characteristic zero, a bijective morphism of algebraic groups is an isomorphism. We define

\rho:=a_{S}^{-1}\pr_{S}c,

where
\pr_{S}\colon T\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{S}
is the projection. Then

a_{S}\rho=a_{S}a_{S}^{-1}\pr_{S}c=\pr_{S}c,

which means
\chi_{s}(\rho(t))=c_{s,k}(t)
for all
(s,k)\in S
. We have shown that
S\subseteq S_{\rho}
. But
S
is
\theta
-stable, so
S_{\rho}
is as well. By (
1
), it is then minimally
\theta
-stable, which forces
S=S_{\rho}
.
∎

Proposition
9.6
shows that the description of the fixed points using the toric fan agrees with our description. More precisely, we have the following.

Corollary 9.7
.

The assignment
\rho\mapsto S_{\rho}^{c}
defines a bijection between the set

\{\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G\mid\rho\text{ morphism of algebraic groups such that }V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)\neq\emptyset\}

and the set of maximal members of
\Phi^{\st}(\theta)
. Moreover, for every
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
for which
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)\neq\emptyset
, let
J:=S_{\rho}^{c}
. Then the subsets
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
and
O_{\sigma_{J}}
of
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
agree and consist of a single point.

Proof.

The bijectivity of
\rho\mapsto S_{\rho}^{c}
was already established in Proposition
9.6
. The second statement follows from the description (
#
‣
9
) of
O_{\sigma_{J}}
and from

V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)=\{v\in V\mid\supp(v)=S_{\rho}\}.\qed

We finish this section, and the paper, with an example.

Example 9.8
.

Fix a number
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Consider the vector space
V=\mathbb{C}^{4}
with the action of
G=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
given by

(g,h)\cdot(x_{0},x_{1},y,z)=(gx_{0},gx_{1},hy,g^{d}hz).

This means, as a
G
-representation,
V=\mathbb{C}(1,0)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}(0,1)\oplus\mathbb{C}(d,1)
. Let
\theta\colon G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the character given by
\theta(g,h)=g^{d+1}h
. Using the Hilbert–Mumford criterion, we can show that

V^{\sst}(G,\theta)=V^{\st}(G,\theta)=\{v=(x_{0},x_{1},y,z)\mid(x_{0},x_{1})\neq(0,0)\neq(y,z)\}.

We see that the quotient
V^{\st}(G,\theta)/G
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d))
, the
d^{\mathrm{th}}
Hirzebruch surface.

Observe that
G
acts freely on the stable locus. So Assumption
2.4
is fulfilled. We get
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{4}
, and the morphism
a\colon G\to T
is given by

a(g,h)=(g,g,h,g^{d}h).

The morphism
\pi\colon T\to\mathscr{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
defined by
\pi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{4})=(z_{1}z_{2}^{-1},z_{3}z_{2}^{d}z_{4}^{-1})
is a cokernel of
a
. Let
I=\{1,\ldots,4\}
and
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{4}
be the basis of
X_{*}(T)
consisting of the embeddings in the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
diagonal entry. We read off

\Phi=\{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,4\}\mid\{1,2\}\nsubseteq J\nsupseteq\{3,4\}\},

and by Proposition
9.5
, the toric fan is
\Delta=\{\cone(\pi^{*}\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in J}\mid J\in\Phi\}
. The maximal elements of
\Phi
are
\{1,3\}
,
\{2,3\}
,
\{1,4\}
, and
\{2,4\}
. They correspond to the fixed points

\pi(0,1,0,1),\quad\pi(1,0,0,1),\quad\pi(0,1,1,0),\quad\pi(1,0,1,0).

A section of
\pi
is given by
c\colon\mathscr{T}\to T
, which sends
(t_{1},t_{2})
to
(t_{1},1,t_{2},1)
. For
\rho\colon\mathscr{T}\to G
, we obtain

V_{\rho}=\{v\in V\mid c(t)v=a\rho(t)v\text{ (all $t\in\mathscr{T}$)}\}.

Let
\rho(t)=(t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}},t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}})
. Then for
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{4})
, the condition
c(t)v=a\rho(t)v
is equivalent to the four equations

\displaystyle t_{1}v_{1}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}v_{1},

\displaystyle v_{2}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}v_{2},

\displaystyle t_{2}v_{3}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}}v_{3},

\displaystyle v_{4}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{dm_{1}+n_{1}}t_{2}^{dm_{2}+n_{2}}v_{4}.

There are four possibilities for
\rho
which admit a non-empty stable locus
V_{\rho}^{\st}(G,\theta)
. They are
\rho_{1}(t)=(t_{1},t_{2})
,
\rho_{2}(t)=(1,t_{2})
,
\rho_{3}(t)=(t_{1},\smash{t_{1}^{-d}})
, and
\rho_{4}(t)=(1,1)
. They give the fixed points

\pi(1,0,1,0),\quad\pi(0,1,1,0),\quad\pi(1,0,0,1),\quad\pi(0,1,0,1).

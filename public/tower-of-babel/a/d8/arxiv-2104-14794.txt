On a unified double zeta function of Mordell–Tornheim type
Shin-ya Kadota, Takuya Okamoto, Masataka Ono, Koji Tasaka

Abstract
. We consider a double zeta function of Mordell–Tornheim type and compute its values at non-positive integer points. We then discuss a possible generalization of the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture for all integer points.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M32

1.
Introduction

The double zeta function of Mordell–Tornheim type is defined by

(1.1)

\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}m^{-s_{1}}n^{-s_{2}}(m+n)^{-s_{3}},

which converges absolutely when
\Re(s_{1}+s_{3}),\Re(s_{2}+s_{3})>1
and
\Re(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3})>2
(see
[
16
, Theorem 2.2]
).
The special values of this function at positive integer points are first studied by Tornheim
[
18
]
and independently by Mordell
[
14
]
for the case
s_{1}=s_{2}=s_{3}
, and also rediscovered by Witten
[
19
]
in his volume formula for certain moduli spaces related to theoretical physics (see also Zagier’s number theoretical treatment
[
20
]
).
As a function, Matsumoto
[
10
, Theorem 1]
proves that the function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
can be analytically continued to the whole
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
-space.
Its true singularities are also determined (see also
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]
).
As an application, it is clarified that non-positive integers
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
are points of indeterminancy, i.e., the values of
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
at non-positive integer points depend on a limiting process.
Explicit formulas for these values in terms of generalized Bernoulli numbers are computed by Komori
[
7
, Theorem 3]
.

In this paper, we wish to study special values of the function

(1.2)

\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=(-1)^{s_{3}}\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})+(-1)^{s_{2}}\zeta_{MT}(s_{3},s_{1};s_{2})+(-1)^{s_{1}}\zeta_{MT}(s_{2},s_{3};s_{1}),

where
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
are complex variables and set
(-1)^{s}=e^{\pi is}
.
The function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
originates from the previous work in
[
1
]
, where they introduce the values of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
at positive integer points as a Mordell–Tornheim type analogue of the symmetric multiple zeta values (see also
[
17
]
).
One of main results of this paper is an explicit evaluation for coordinatewise limits of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
at non-positive integer points.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any non-negative integers
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
a,b,c\in\{1,2,3\}
such that
\{a,b,c\}=\{1,2,3\}
, we have

(1.3)

{\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{c}\rightarrow 0}}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(-m_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1},-m_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},-m_{3}+{\varepsilon}_{3})=\begin{cases}1&(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(0,0,0)\\
0&(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\neq(0,0,0)\end{cases}.

The contents of this paper are as follows.
In order to motivate our work, we briefly recall the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture on finite/symmetric multiple zeta values in §2.
§3 gives a result on true singularities of the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
.
§4 is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
.
Finally, in §5, we discuss a possible generalization of the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture for the multiple zeta function of Mordell–Tornheim type.

2.
Background

This paper is to mimic the study of the unified multiple zeta function introduced by Komori
[
9
]
.
We briefly review his results to motivate our work.

For each
r\geq 0
, we call a tuple
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
of positive integers an index and
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
the weight.
We regard the empty index
\emptyset
as the unique index of weight 0 (and
r=0
).
We set
F(\emptyset)
to be a unit element for any function
F
on indices.

We first recall the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture
[
6
]
.
For each index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
, define the multiple harmonic sum
H_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})
by

(2.1)

H_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}\leq n}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

The empty sum is understood as 0.
If the last component
k_{r}
is greater than 1, the limit at
n\rightarrow\infty
exists and is called the multiple zeta value, denoted by

(2.2)

\zeta(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

Kaneko and Zagier introduce two different types of multiple zeta values constructed from the above objects.
One of them is the finite multiple zeta value
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})
defined for each index
\boldsymbol{k}
by

(2.3)

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\big(H_{p-1}(\boldsymbol{k})\mod p\big)_{p}

in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=\big(\prod_{p}{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\big/\big(\bigoplus_{p}{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\big)
, where
p
runs over all primes.
Another is the symmetric multiple zeta value
\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})
defined for each index
\boldsymbol{k}
by

(2.4)

\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{a=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{a+1}+\cdots+k_{r}}\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{a})\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{a+1})\mod\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z}

in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}/\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z}
, where we denote by
\mathcal{Z}
the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra generated by all multiple zeta values and by
\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}
the shuffle regularized multiple zeta value.
Let
\mathbb{I}^{+}
be the set of all indices.
The main conjecture of Kaneko and Zagier states that for a finite subset
\{a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}\}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

(2.5)

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0.

In
[
9
]
, Komori defines the function
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
by

(2.6)

\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{a=0}^{r}(-1)^{s_{a+1}+\cdots+s_{r}}\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{a})\zeta(s_{r},\ldots,s_{a+1}).

It is a function analogue of symmetric multiple zeta values.
A crucial feature is that the function
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is entire.
Thus, one can study the values
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\in{\mathbb{C}}
for any tuple
\boldsymbol{k}
of integers.
These values are conjecturally elements in the polynomial ring
\mathcal{Z}[\pi i]
over
\mathcal{Z}
.
Indeed, for all index
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}
, Komori
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
shows that
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\in\mathcal{Z}[\pi i]
, and also that

(2.7)

\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\equiv\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})\mod\pi i\mathcal{Z}[\pi i].

Now, assuming the conjecture
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\in}}\mathcal{Z}[\pi i]
, one extends the definition of the symmetric multiple zeta values
\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})
to any tuple
\boldsymbol{k}
of integers by

(2.8)

\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\mod\pi i\mathcal{Z}[\pi i].

Hereafter, we denote by
\mathbb{I}
the set of all tuple
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\ (r\geq 0)
of integers.
A natural question to ask is then whether the conjecture (
2.5
) holds for all tuple of integers.
Namely, for a finite subset
\{a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}\}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

(2.9)

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\zeta_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0.

Note that, since there is no convergence issue, the finite multiple zeta values
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})
are already defined for all
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}
and that they span the same space with the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space generated by the set
\{\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}\}
(see
[
5
]
).
The main result of Komori
[
9
, Theorem 1.5]
states that the conjecture (
2.9
) holds when we restrict
\boldsymbol{k}
to tuples of non-positive integers.
A partial evidence for depth 2 case is also given in
[
9
, Proposition 1.6]
.

In this paper we wish to study the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture (
2.5
) for multiple zeta values of Mordell–Tornheim type, which are independently established in
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
.
Our terminology follows
[
1
]
.
In this analogy, for each
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{I}^{+}
, let

(2.10)

\omega_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n\\
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

and define

(2.11)

\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\big(\omega_{p}(\boldsymbol{k})\mod p\big)_{p}\in\mathcal{A}

for
r\neq 1
.
We call this the finite multiple omega value for short.
Note that the finite multiple omega value is, up to sign, equal to the finite multiple zeta value of Mordell–Tornheim type, which is first studied by Kamano
[
4
]
.
As a real counterpart of this, we define the symmetric multiple omega value
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})
for
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{I}^{+}
with
r\neq 1
by

(2.12)

\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{a=1}^{r}(-1)^{k_{a}}\zeta_{MT}(\underbrace{k_{1},\ldots,k_{a-1}}_{a-1},\underbrace{k_{a+1},\ldots,k_{r}}_{r-a};k_{a})\in\mathcal{Z}/\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z},

where

(2.13)

\zeta_{MT}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r-1};k_{r})=\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r-1}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r-1})^{k_{r}}}

are the multiple zeta values of Mordell–Tornheim type which can be written as
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combinations of multiple zeta values (
2.2
) (see
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
).
A similar object to
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})
is introduced by a different method in
[
17
, Definition 4.5]
(which in the case
\mathcal{S}
differs in a sign).
The main conjecture of
[
1
, Conjecture 1.5]
(see also
[
17
, §4]
) is that for a finite subset
\{a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+},r\neq 1\}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

(2.14)

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0.

It can be seen from
[
1
, Theorem 1.6]
that the conjecture (
2.14
) is supported by the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture (
2.5
).

An ultimate goal of this project is to establish a Mordell–Tornheim analogue of Komori’s conjecture (
2.9
).
For that purpose, we need to find a suitable function whose values at integer points modulo
\pi^{2}
satisfy the same relations with the finite multiple omega values.
As a natural candidate, we consider the following function:

(2.15)

\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{a=1}^{r}(-1)^{s_{a}}\zeta_{MT}(\underbrace{s_{1},\ldots,s_{a-1}}_{a-1},\underbrace{s_{a+1},\ldots,s_{r}}_{r-a};s_{a}).

Similarly to (
2.7
), the congruence
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k})\equiv\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})\mod\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z}
holds for all
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}^{+}
, since
\zeta_{MT}(\boldsymbol{k})
converges absolutely for any
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{I}^{+}
with
r\neq 1
.
As a function, we see from
[
11
, Theorem 1]
that the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
can be meromorphically continued to
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and its possible singularities are on

(2.16)

\displaystyle s_{j_{1}}+s_{j_{2}}=1-l\quad(1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq r,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}),

(2.17)

\displaystyle s_{j_{1}}+s_{j_{2}}+s_{j_{3}}=2-l\quad(1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq r,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}),

(2.18)

(2.19)

\displaystyle s_{j_{1}}+\cdots+s_{j_{r-1}}=r-2-l\quad(1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r-1}\leq r,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}),

(2.20)

\displaystyle s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}=r-1.

While the function
\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
cancels all possible singularities which come from singularities of multiple zeta functions, some of the above singularities of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
will be true.
Thus the generalization of the conjecture (
2.14
) via the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is not straightforward.
In this paper, as a part of the future project, we explicate the situation for the case
r=3
and then discuss a possible generalization of (
2.14
) to all integer points.

3.
Singularities

By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, the true singularities of the function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
lie on

s_{1}+s_{3}=1-l,\ s_{2}+s_{3}=1-l
(
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
) and
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2
.
Hence the possible singularities of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
lie on the subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
defined by one of the equations:

(3.1)

s_{a}+s_{b}=1-l\ \ (1\leq a<b\leq 3,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}),\quad s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2.

We now prove that these are true singularities.

Theorem 3.1
.

All points on the subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
defined by one of the equations
s_{a}+s_{b}=1-l

(1\leq a<b\leq 3,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
and
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2
are true singularities of the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
.

Proof.

We first recall the relevant material from
[
10
]
.
For
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
0<\eta<1
, let

(3.2)

I(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};M-\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(M-\eta)}\Gamma(s_{3}+z)\Gamma(-z)\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+z)\zeta(s_{2}-z)\,dz,

where the path of integration is the vertical line from
M-\eta-i\infty
to
M-\eta+i\infty
.
From
[
10
, Eq. (5.3)]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta_{MT}&(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})=\frac{\Gamma(s_{2}+s_{3}-1)\Gamma(1-s_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{3})}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\binom{-s_{3}}{k}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+k)\zeta(s_{2}-k)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(s_{3})}I(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};M-\eta),\end{split}

where
M
is a sufficiently large positive integer. We find that the integral
I(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};M-\eta)
is holomorphic on the region

D_{M}=\{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}\mid\ \Re s_{3}>-M+\eta,\Re(s_{1}+s_{3})>1-M+\eta,\Re s_{2}<1+M-\eta\},

because in
D_{M}
the poles of the integrand are not on the path of integration. Since
M
is arbitrarily large, by (
3
), the function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
can be meromorphically continued to
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. We note that, in the case
s_{2}=l
for a positive integer
l
, the first and the second terms on the right-hand side of (
3
) are singular, where
M>l
, but these singularities cancel each other. Indeed, put
s_{2}=l+{\varepsilon}
for a positive integer
l
.
Using the well-known asymptotic formula

(3.3)

\Gamma(s)=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{-n}}{(-n)!}\frac{1}{s-n}+\gamma_{n}+O(|s-n|)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}),

where
\gamma_{n}
is the constant term of the expansion of
\Gamma(s)
at
s=n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(s_{3}+l-1+{\varepsilon})\Gamma(1-l-{\varepsilon})}{\Gamma(s_{3})}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l-1+{\varepsilon})

(3.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{-s_{3}}{l-1}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l-1)\zeta(1+{\varepsilon})+O(1)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(s_{3}+l-1)}{\Gamma(s_{3})}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l-1)\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{-s_{3}}{l-1}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l-1)\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}+O(1)

(3.8)

\displaystyle=O(1).

We now prove that
s_{1}+s_{3}=1-l\ (l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
determines the true singularity of the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
.
Take
M\geq l+1
.
From (
3
) the singular part of
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
corresponding to
s_{1}+s_{3}=1-l
comes from

(3.9)

{\binom{-s_{3}}{l}}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l)\zeta(s_{2}-l).

Since
\zeta_{MT}(s_{2},s_{3};s_{1})=\zeta_{MT}(s_{3},s_{2};s_{1})
, the corresponding singular part of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
is

(3.10)

\displaystyle(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{-s_{1}}{l}

\displaystyle\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l)\zeta(s_{2}-l)+(-1)^{s_{3}}\binom{-s_{3}}{l}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l)\zeta(s_{2}-l)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\left\{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{-s_{1}}{l}+(-1)^{s_{3}}\binom{-s_{3}}{l}\right\}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l)\zeta(s_{2}-l)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\left((-1)^{s_{1}}-(-1)^{-s_{1}}\right)\binom{-s_{1}}{l}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{3}+l)\zeta(s_{2}-l).

Since

\left((-1)^{s_{1}}-(-1)^{-s_{1}}\right)\binom{-s_{1}}{l}\zeta(s_{2}-l)\not\equiv 0,

we see that
s_{1}+s_{3}=1-l
determines the true singularity.
Note that we have
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{\sigma(1)},s_{\sigma(2)},s_{\sigma(3)})
for any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.
Thus,
s_{a}+s_{b}=1-l\ \ (1\leq a<b\leq 3,\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
determine the true singularities.

For the case
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2
, by (
3
) the corresponding singular part of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
comes from

\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)\times\sum_{j=0}^{2}(-1)^{s_{3+j}}\frac{\Gamma(s_{2+j}+s_{3+j}-1)\Gamma(1-s_{2+j})}{\Gamma(s_{3+j})},

where we set
s_{a+j}=s_{b}
if
a+j\equiv b\mod 3
for
b=1,2,3
and
a,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Hereafter, we abuse the same notation for other triplets of complex numbers or variables.
Since
s_{2}+s_{3}=2-s_{1}
, by Euler’s reflection formula

\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)=\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi s},

we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle(-1)^{s_{3}}\frac{\Gamma(s_{2}+s_{3}-1)\Gamma(1-s_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{3})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s_{3}}\frac{\Gamma(1-s_{1})\Gamma(1-s_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{3})}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\Gamma(s_{1})\Gamma(s_{2})\Gamma(s_{3})}\frac{(-1)^{s_{3}}}{\sin\pi s_{1}\sin\pi s_{2}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\prod_{a=1}^{3}\Gamma(s_{a})\sin\pi s_{a}}(-1)^{s_{3}}\sin\pi s_{3}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\prod_{a=1}^{3}\Gamma(s_{a})\sin\pi s_{a}}\frac{(-1)^{2s_{3}}-1}{2i}.

Since

\frac{\pi^{2}}{2i\prod_{a=1}^{3}\Gamma(s_{a})\sin\pi s_{a}}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\big((-1)^{2s_{3+j}}-1\big)\not\equiv 0

holds for the case
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2
, we conclude that
s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=2
determines the true singularity of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Our proof of Theorem
3.1
shows that if
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
is a singular point of the function
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
, then it is a point of indeterminancy.

Remark 3.2
.

Desingularizations of the double zeta function of Mordell–Tornheim type are studied in
[
3
,
15
]
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we first study an asymptotic behavior of the function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
at
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=(-m_{1},-m_{2},-m_{3})
for non-negative integers
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
, and then, give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, let
M=2+m_{1}+m_{3}
.
Then
(-m_{1},-m_{2},-m_{3})\in D_{M}
, so the integral
I(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};M-\eta)
is analytic at
(-m_{1},-m_{2},-m_{3})
. Hence we have the estimation

(4.1)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(s_{3})}I(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};M-\eta)=O(|s_{3}+m_{3}|).

Note that a similar argument can be found in
[
13
, §5]
.

We now give the asymptotics of the function
\zeta_{MT}(s_{1},s_{2};s_{3})
.
Let
B_{k}
be the
k
-th Seki–Bernoulli number defined by

(4.2)

\frac{te^{t}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

For non-negative integers
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
, let
m=m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+2
and define rational numbers
b(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})
and
c(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})
by

(4.3)

\displaystyle b(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})

\displaystyle=m_{1}!m_{2}!m_{3}!\binom{m-1}{m_{3}}\frac{B_{m}}{m!},

(4.4)

\displaystyle c(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+n_{2}=m_{3}\\
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{m_{3}}{n_{1}}\frac{B_{m_{1}+n_{1}+1}}{m_{1}+n_{1}+1}\frac{B_{m_{2}+n_{2}+1}}{m_{2}+n_{2}+1}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
a,b,c\in\{1,2,3\}
such that
\{a,b,c\}=\{1,2,3\}
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\varepsilon_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\varepsilon_{c}\rightarrow 0}\zeta_{MT}(-m_{1}+\varepsilon_{1},-m_{2}+\varepsilon_{2};-m_{3}+\varepsilon_{3})

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\varepsilon_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\varepsilon_{c}\rightarrow 0}\left\{(-1)^{m_{2}}b(m_{2},m_{3};m_{1})\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}}+(-1)^{m_{1}}b(m_{3},m_{1};m_{2})\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{3}}+c(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})\right\}.

Proof.

Let
m=m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+2
.
For the first term on the right side of (
3
), we use (
3.3
) and the expansion
\Gamma(s)=(n-1)!+O(|s-n|)
at
s=n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
to obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(-m_{2}-m_{3}-1+{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3})\Gamma(1+m_{2}-{\varepsilon}_{2})}{\Gamma(-m_{3}+{\varepsilon}_{3})}\zeta(1-m+{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3})

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\left\{(-1)^{m_{3}}m_{3}!{\varepsilon}_{3}+O(|{\varepsilon}_{3}|^{2})\right\}\left\{\frac{(-1)^{m_{2}+m_{3}+1}}{(m_{2}+m_{3}+1)!}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}+\gamma_{-m_{2}-m_{3}-1}+O(|{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}|)\right\}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\{m_{2}!+O(|{\varepsilon}_{2}|)\right\}\left\{\zeta(1-m)+O(|{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}|)\right\}

(4.10)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m_{2}}b(m_{2},m_{3};m_{1})\frac{{\varepsilon}_{3}}{{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(\left|\frac{{\varepsilon}_{2}{\varepsilon}_{3}}{{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}\right|\right)+O\left(\left|\frac{{\varepsilon}_{3}({\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3})}{{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}\right|\right)+O(|{\varepsilon}_{3}|)+O\left(\left|\frac{{\varepsilon}_{3}^{2}}{{\varepsilon}_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}\right|\right)

Here, for the last equality we have used
\zeta(1-m)=-B_{m}/m\ (m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1})
.

For the second term on the right side of (
3
), let
M=2+m_{1}+m_{3}
.
Since

(4.13)

\binom{m-{\varepsilon}}{k}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\binom{m}{k}+O(|{\varepsilon}|)&(m\geq k),\\
\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-m}m!(k-m-1)!}{k!}{\varepsilon}+O(|{\varepsilon}|^{2})&(m<k)\end{cases}

holds for
k,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\binom{m_{3}-{\varepsilon}_{3}}{k}\zeta(-m_{1}-m_{3}+k+{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3})\zeta(-m_{2}-k+{\varepsilon}_{2})

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m_{1}+m_{3}}\binom{m_{3}-{\varepsilon}_{3}}{k}\zeta(-m_{1}-m_{3}+k+{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3})\zeta(-m_{2}-k+{\varepsilon}_{2})

(4.16)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{m_{3}-{\varepsilon}_{3}}{m_{1}+m_{3}+1}\zeta(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3})\zeta(1-m+{\varepsilon}_{2})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=c(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})+(-1)^{m_{1}}b(m_{3},m_{1};m_{2})\frac{{\varepsilon}_{3}}{{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\quad+O(|{\varepsilon}_{3}|)+O(|{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3}|)+O(|{\varepsilon}_{2}|)+O\left(\left|\frac{{\varepsilon}_{3}^{2}}{{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}\right|\right)+O\left(\left|\frac{{\varepsilon}_{2}{\varepsilon}_{3}}{{\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{3}}\right|\right).

Here, for the last equality we have also used the Laurent expansion of
\zeta(s)
at
s=1
. Thus by (
4.1
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we use generating functions.
Let

(4.19)

\displaystyle B(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 0}(-1)^{m_{1}+m_{2}}b(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})\frac{t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}t_{3}^{m_{3}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!m_{3}!},

(4.20)

\displaystyle C(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 0}(-1)^{m_{3}}c(m_{1},m_{2};m_{3})\frac{t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}t_{3}^{m_{3}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!m_{3}!}.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

(4.21)

\sum_{j=0}^{2}\big(B(t_{1+j},t_{2+j},t_{3+j})+C(t_{1+j},t_{2+j},t_{3+j})\big)=1,

where we again put
t_{a+j}=t_{b}
if
a+j\equiv b\mod 3
for
b=1,2,3
and
a,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Set

(4.22)

\beta(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{B_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}t^{k}=\frac{e^{t}}{e^{t}-1}-\frac{1}{t}.

One computes

(4.23)

\displaystyle B(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m_{3}\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+m_{2}=l\\
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}\right)(-1)^{l}\binom{l+m_{3}+1}{m_{3}}\frac{B_{l+m_{3}+2}}{(l+m_{3}+2)!}t_{3}^{m_{3}}

(4.24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m_{3}\geq 0}\frac{t_{1}^{l+1}-t_{2}^{l+1}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}(-1)^{l}\binom{l+m_{3}+1}{m_{3}}\frac{B_{l+m_{3}+2}}{(l+m_{3}+2)!}t_{3}^{m_{3}}

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+m_{3}+1=k\\
l,m_{3}\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{l+1}\binom{k}{m_{3}}(t_{2}^{l+1}-t_{1}^{l+1})t_{3}^{m_{3}}\right)\frac{B_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}

(4.26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left((t_{3}-t_{2})^{k}-t_{3}^{k}-(t_{3}-t_{1})^{k}+t_{3}^{k}\right)\frac{B_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta(t_{3}-t_{2})-\beta(t_{3}-t_{1})}{t_{1}-t_{2}},

and

(4.28)

\displaystyle C(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0}\frac{B_{m_{1}+n_{1}+1}}{m_{1}+n_{1}+1}\frac{B_{m_{2}+n_{2}+1}}{m_{2}+n_{2}+1}\frac{t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}(-t_{3})^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!n_{1}!n_{2}!}

(4.29)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{2}\left(\sum_{m_{j},n_{j}\geq 0}\frac{B_{m_{j}+n_{j}+1}}{(m_{j}+n_{j}+1)!}\binom{m_{j}+n_{j}}{m_{j}}t_{j}^{m_{j}}(-t_{3})^{n_{j}}\right)

(4.30)

\displaystyle=\beta(t_{1}-t_{3})\beta(t_{2}-t_{3}).

Substituting these expressions into the left side of (
4.21
) and then using

(4.31)

\sum_{j=0}^{2}\frac{1}{(t_{1+j}-t_{2+j})(t_{3+j}-t_{2+j})}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{2}\frac{e^{t_{1+j}-t_{3+j}}}{e^{t_{1+j}-t_{3+j}}-1}\frac{e^{t_{2+j}-t_{3+j}}}{e^{t_{2+j}-t_{3+j}}-1}=1,

we get the desired result.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.32)

\displaystyle{\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{c}\rightarrow 0}}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(-m_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1},-m_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},-m_{3}+{\varepsilon}_{3})

(4.33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(-1)^{m_{1+j}+m_{2+j}}b(m_{1+j},m_{2+j};m_{3+j}){\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{c}\rightarrow 0}}\frac{(-1)^{{\varepsilon}_{1+j}}{\varepsilon}_{1+j}+(-1)^{{\varepsilon}_{2+j}}{\varepsilon}_{2+j}}{{\varepsilon}_{1+j}+{\varepsilon}_{2+j}}

(4.34)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{2}{(-1)^{m_{3+j}}c(m_{1+j},m_{2+j};m_{3+j})}.

Since

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(0,0,0),\\

Since the left side of (

5.
Conclusion

By Theorem
1.1
, one can define the symmetric double omega values
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
for non-positive integers
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
by

(5.1)

\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})={\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{a}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{b}\rightarrow 0}\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{c}\rightarrow 0}}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1},k_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},k_{3}+{\varepsilon}_{3})\mod\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z},

which are either 0 or 1.
On the other hand, for
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
one computes

(5.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+n_{2}<p\\
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1\end{subarray}}n_{1}^{-k_{1}}n_{2}^{-k_{2}}(p-n_{1}-n_{2})^{-k_{3}}\mod p\right)_{p}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m_{2}=2}^{p-1}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{m_{2}-1}m_{1}^{-k_{1}}(m_{2}-m_{1})^{-k_{2}}(-m_{2})^{-k_{3}}\mod p\right)_{p}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{-k_{2}-k_{3}}\sum_{l=0}^{-k_{2}}(-1)^{l}\binom{-k_{2}}{l}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1}+k_{2}+l,k_{3}-l)

in
\mathcal{A}
.
Hence by
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
, for non-positive integers
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
we have

(5.5)

\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=\begin{cases}(1)_{p}&(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(0,0,0)\\
(0)_{p}&(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\neq(0,0,0)\end{cases}.

Therefore the conjecture (
2.14
) holds when we restrict
\boldsymbol{k}
to elements in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}_{\leq 0}
.
Moreover, for integers
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
such that
k_{1}\leq k_{2}=0\leq k_{3}
, as a consequence of
[
9
, Proposition 1.6]
, we obtain the correspondence between the limit value (
5.1
) and
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
, because we have
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1},0,k_{3})=(-1)^{k_{3}}\zeta_{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1},k_{3})
and
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},0,k_{3})=(-1)^{k_{3}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},k_{3})
.

Now a possible analogue of (
2.9
) for multiple zeta values of Mordell–Tornheim type is as follows.
Suppose that a certain limit
{\displaystyle\lim_{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}}^{\prime}\ \omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}})
exists in
\mathcal{Z}
for all
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}
.
Then one can define the symmetric multiple omega values
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})\in\mathcal{Z}/\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z}
for all

(5.6)

\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})={\lim_{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}}^{\prime}\ \omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}})\mod\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z}.

One would ask if

(5.7)

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{I}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=0

holds for a finite subset

of

.

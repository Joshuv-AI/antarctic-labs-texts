TVDO: Tchebycheff Value-Decomposition Optimization for Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
Xiaoliang HuThe Nanjing University of Science and TechnologyNanjing, China Peter_Hu_XL@njust.edu.cn Pengcheng GuoThe Nanjing University of Science and TechnologyNanjing, China glmmer007@njust.edu.cn Chuanwei ZhouThe Nanjing University of Science and TechnologyNanjing, China cwzhou@njust.edu.cn Tong ZhangThe Nanjing University of Science and TechnologyNanjing, China tong.zhang@njust.edu.cn Zhen CuiThe Nanjing University of Science and TechnologyNanjing, China zhen.cui@njust.edu.cn

Abstract
In cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) settings, the centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) becomes customary recently due to the physical demand. However, the most dilemma is the inconsistency of jointly-trained policies and individually-optimized actions. In this work, we propose a novel value-based multi-objective learning approach, named Tchebycheff value decomposition optimization (TVDO), to overcome the above dilemma. In particular, a nonlinear Tchebycheff aggregation method is designed to transform the MARL task into multi-objective optimal counterpart by tightly constraining the upper bound of individual action-value bias. We theoretically prove that TVDO well satisfies the necessary and sufficient condition of individual global max (IGM) with no extra limitations, which exactly guarantees the consistency between the global and individual optimal action-value function. Empirically, in the climb and penalty game, we verify that TVDO represents precisely from global to individual value factorization with a guarantee of the policy consistency. Furthermore, we also evaluate TVDO in the challenging scenarios of StarCraft II micromanagement tasks, and extensive experiments demonstrate that TVDO achieves more competitive performances than several state-of-the-art MARL methods.

In many realistic multi-agent settings, an individual agent often only captures local/partial observation or communication due to the limitation of environment. Hence, the learning of decentralized policies is required on the condition of the local state/action observations of each agent. The advantage of decentralized learning is two-fold: i) reduce the computation burden of exponential grows in joint state and action space; ii) facilitate the use of conventional single-agent reinforcement learning methods. They roughly fall into two categories: value-decomposition and policy-decomposition MARL. The value-decomposition, which work we follow, is to factorize the global value function into individual value function to make sure the consistency between global and individual optimal policy.

Even with these great progresses in value decomposition, almost above methods cannot guarantee the complete expressiveness of IGM condition except for QTRAN and QPLEX. Although QTRAN and QPLEX provide theoretical guarantee for factorizing cooperative MARL tasks, they require an extra constraint (e.g. affine transformation(QTRAN) or the positive importance weights(QPLEX)). In this work, we propose a novel MARL method called Tchebycheff Value Decomposition Optimization (TVDO) by using decomposition-based multi-objective optimization (MOO) in centralised training and decentralised execution fashion. The cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning task is converted to a multi-objective optimization problem. Specifically, we introduce a novel nonlinear aggregation function, based on Tchebycheff approach, to achieve the global optimum through tightly constraining the upper bound of individual action-value bias. Preliminaries

Problem Statement

The cooperative MARL task could be regarded as a decentralized partially observable markov decision process(Dec-POMDP)
[
29
]
. Formally, the task can be defined as a tuple
{<A,S,O,U,T,P,R,\gamma>}
where each element is described as follows:

{A}
is the team of agents
\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}\}
, in which
{i\in N}
is the index of agent.

{S}
denotes the finite set of environmental state, which can be only used in training process. In particular, the next state of the current state
{s\in S}
is
{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in S}
.
The joint observation at timestep
{t}
is denoted as
{o_{t}}=\{o^{1}_{t},o^{2}_{t},\cdots,o^{N}_{t}\}
, which consists of local observation of each agent in its own view.

{U}
is the set of joint action, where
{\mu_{t}}=\{\mu^{1}_{t},\mu^{2}_{t},\cdots,\mu^{N}_{t}\}\in U
denotes the entire group of each individual action at timestep
{t}
. Note that the space of joint action is discrete or continuous.

{T}
denotes the joint action-observation histories, in which the action-observation history of each timestep
{t}
is
{\tau_{t}=\{o_{1},\mu_{1},o_{2},\mu_{2},\cdots,o_{t-1},\mu_{t-1},o_{t}\}\in T}
. In particular, the joint observation of current timestep
{o_{t}}
is included in the history.

{P(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,\mu)}
is the state transition function, which denotes the probability from global state
{s\in S}
to next state
{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in S}
when all agents take the joint action
{\mu\in U}
.
After each agent takes individual action
{\mu^{i}\in U,i=1,2,\cdots,N}
under the environment state
{s\in S}
, the team of agents receive an immediately global reward
{r=R(s,\mu)}
shared with all agents.

{\gamma}\in(0,1]
is the discount factor.
According to the above formulation, we use the state value function
{V(s)}
to measure the value of sate
{s\in S}
, where
{V(s):=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t-1}R(s_{t},\mu_{t})|s_{1}=s;\mu_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t})}]}
. Meanwhile, the action-value function
{Q(s,\mu)}
denotes the global value of current state
{s\in S}
when the joint action
{\mu\in U}
is taken by agents. Specifically, the action-value function is
{Q(s,\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\sim P(\cdot|s,\mu)}[R(s,\mu)+\gamma V(s^{{}^{\prime}})]}
.
Due to the partial observability that each agent can not obtain fully environmental state information, the state
{s_{t}}
is replaced by the action-observation history
{\tau_{t}}
in the action-value function
{Q(s_{t},\mu_{t})}
. Thus, the action-value function is approximately expressed as
{Q(\tau_{t},\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{t+1:\infty},\mu_{t+1:\infty}}[R_{t}|\tau_{t},\mu_{t}]}
, where
{R_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{i}r_{t+i}}}
denotes the discounted return.

CTDE

In realistic multi-agent environments, each agent only captures partial observation because of the limitation of environment. Therefore, the learning of decentralized policies is required on the condition of the local observation of each agent. Perhaps the most naive training method for MARL tasks is independent Q-learning(IQL)
[
10
]
, which learns the policy of each agent independently. However, due to the instability of partial observability, the decentralized policy may not converge to a global optimal solution in the limit of infinite exploration. The most commonly alternative solution is to employ the paradigm of centralized training with decentralized execution(CTDE)
[
11
]
, where each agent learns the policy by optimizing individual action-value function based on its own observation and the global state in training phase, and agent makes its decision with local observation at execution time.

For any factorizable MARL tasks, Individual-Global-Max (IGM)

14
]
is a significant condition to keep the consistency between global and individual policy in the centralized training process, which can be formulated as follows.

\arg\max\limits_{\mu}{Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\arg\max\limits_{\mu_{1}}{Q_{1}(\tau_{1},\mu_{1})}\\
\vdots\\
\arg\max\limits_{\mu_{n}}{Q_{n}(\tau_{n},\mu_{n})}\end{array}\right).

(1)

According to the Eq.(
1
), we note that the solution of the greedy global action is equivalent to choose greedily individual policy under the joint action-observation history
\tau
for an arbitrary task whose action-value function
{Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)}
can be factorized the group of individual action-value function
{[Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})]^{n}_{i=1}}
. In other words, for a factorizable task, IGM condition is the sufficient and necessary condition of the action-value function be factorized for a factorizable task.

\textbf{QMIX}:\frac{\partial{Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)}}{\partial{Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})}}\textgreater 0,\forall i\in N.

(3)

However, both of them are built on the condition of the structure constraints, and only satisfy the sufficient condition of IGM. In contrast, QTRAN

\geq 0,\mu\neq\overline{\mu}\end{array}\right.

where

\quad V_{jt}(\tau)=\max\limits_{\mu}{{Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})}}.

(4)

\textbf{Qatten}:Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)=c(\tau)+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i,h}(\tau)Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})}.

(5)

\textbf{QPLEX}:Q_{jt}(\tau,\mu)=V_{jt}(\tau)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}(\tau,\mu)A_{i}(\tau,\mu_{i})}.

(6)

As discussed in QPLEX

]
, Qatten
[

]
is an extensive work of VDN, which uses the multi-head attention mechanism to approximate joint action-value function(Eq.(

Weighted QMIX
[

uses weighted projection that places more importance on the better joint actions to solve the suboptimal policy problem existed in QMIX, but still needs the monotonicity constraint.
QPLEX
[

In addition, QPLEX well presents a guaranteed IGM condition while require a extra constraint that the positive importance weights in the duplex dueling component. In this paper, we aim to develop a novel MARL method without extra limitation to address this challenge that represents precisely from global to individual value factorization with the guarantee of the policy consistency.

4
Method

The key idea of our TVDO method is to transform the multi-agent reinforcement learning task into a multi-objective optimization problem, and introduce a nonlinear aggregation method for the global policy optimum by tightly constraining the upper bound of individual action-value bias.

4.1
Derivation of Tchebycheff Value-Decomposition

The general multi-objective optimization (MOO) problem
[
30
]
is to optimize simultaneously two or more objective functions and search the Pareto-optimal solution
[
31
]
, which is formulated as:

\min_{x}f(x)=\min_{x}[f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\cdots,f_{K}(x)],

(7)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,M

(8a)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle h_{j}(x)=0,\quad j=1,2,\cdots,L

(8b)

where function
{f_{k}(x)}
is the k-th objective function and
{k=1,2,\cdots,K}
. The constraints of the MOO problem consist of
{M}
inequality constraints
{[g_{i}(x)\leq 0]_{i=1}^{M}}
and
{L}
equality constraints
{[h_{j}(x)=0]_{j=1}^{L}}
. Besides, solution
{x=[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]}
is a vector of decision variables.

The decomposition strategy
[
32
]
is one of the basic ideas to solve MOO problems in traditional mathematical programming, which has developed a lot of decomposition-based multi-objective optimization (MOOD) methods. A classical MOOD method called Tchebycheff approach
[
33
]
is proposed to transform the original MOO problem into a single-objective Optimization (SOO) counterpart by a nonlinear aggregation function, in which optimal objective function is defined as:

\min\left\{{g^{tche}(x|Z^{*})}=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq K}{\left|f_{k}(x)-Z_{k}^{*}\right|}\right\},

(9)

where
{Z^{*}}
, composed of the best accessible objective function values, is a reference point
{Z_{k}^{*}=\min\{f_{k}(x)|x\in D\}}
and
{D}
is the set of feasible solutions.
We note that minimization of Tchebycheff objective function corresponds to a min-max problem from Eq.(
9
). In other words, the problem could be understood as seeking a tight upper bound
\alpha
of all objective bias:

|Z_{k}^{*}-Z_{k}|=|Z_{k}^{*}-Z_{k}(x)|\leq\alpha,k=1,\cdots,K
,
where
{Z_{k}(x)}
denotes the solution
{x}
of k-th objective function in the objective space. Note that
{|Z_{k}^{*}-Z_{k}|}
represents the bias between the best feasible solution point and the solution
{x}
point for the k-th objective function.

By the definition of IGM condition, the objective of factorizable cooperative MARL tasks is accessible to transform maximize the global action-value function
{Q_{g}(\tau,\mu)}
into maximize the action-value function
{Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})}
of each agent
{i}
by choosing local greedy action instead of joint greedy policy selection. Therefore, the objective function of MARL tasks can be formulated as follows:

\arg\!\max_{\mu}Q_{g}(\tau,\mu)=\arg\!\max_{[\mu_{i}]_{i=1}^{n}}[Q_{1}(\tau_{1},\mu_{1}),\cdots,Q_{n}(\tau_{n},\mu_{n})].

(10)

Thus, we consider the issue of local greedy action selections in the cooperative MARL task as a problem of simultaneously optimizing multiple objectives where each objective function, as shown in Equ. (
10
), use different domains
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{n}
. Inspired by the idea of Tchebycheff approach
[
33
]
that seeks a tight upper bound of all objective bias and optimizes the upper bound, we construct a new nonlinear aggregation approach for arbitrarily factrorizable MARL tasks, whose objective function is as follows:

\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})}-Q_{g}(\tau,\mu)+E_{g}(\tau,\mu)\right\},

(11)

where

E_{g}(\tau,\mu)=\rho\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}},

Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})
denotes the optimal action value of agent
{a_{i}}
based on individual action-observation history
{\tau_{i}}
.
{\rho}
is a hyperparameter. In particular, we note that
{E_{g}(\tau,\mu)}
is the maximum bias of all agents between their own policies and the optimal action. Therefore, our optimal objective is to minimize the maximum bias
{E_{g}(\tau,\mu)}
to realise factorizing the action-value function of MARL tasks.

4.3
Tchebycheff Value Decomposition Optimization

In order to handle the cooperative MARL task, we design a novel deep reinforcement learning framework TVDO based on the architechure of VDN
[
23
]
, which allows the end-to-end learning of policy. According to the theoretical guarantee of decomposition, we konw that Eq. (
12
) should be satisfied, if the global action-value function in a factorizable task can be factorized into individual action-value function. Thus, the framework of TVDO naturally satisfies Theorem
4.1
.

For each agent
{i}
, we learn an independent agent network to estimate the individual action-value function
Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})
, parameterized by
\theta_{i}
, which takes its current observation
o^{t}_{i}
and the last action
\mu_{i}^{t-1}
as input at each timestep, and obtain action value
Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\cdot)
. Then,
Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i}^{t})
is produced by selecting
\mu_{i}^{t}
in
{\epsilon-greedy}
way. The global joint action value network is used to approximate
Q_{g}(\tau,\mu)
, parameterized by
\phi_{i}
.

In order to learn the efficient decomposition of global action value, we stabilize the learning by introducing a nonlinear constraint(Eq.(
12
)) with a novel nonlinear aggregation function. So our major goal in centralized training is the sum of action-value function should track the joint action-value function in the sense that their optimal actions are equivalent.
We use the idea that freezing the parameters of the target network for a fixed number of steps while updating the online network, introduced in DQN
[
34
]
, to design our algorithm.
However, there is a challenge that how to choose the parameter
\rho
. According to
Theorem
4.1
, we can observe that the parameter
\rho
should be in the range of
\frac{Q_{g}(\tau,\overline{\mu})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})}}{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}}}
to
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}}}{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}}}
.
Since the optimal global action-value function
Q_{g}(\tau,\overline{\mu})
can not be precisely estimated during training, we use the upper bound of the range to update the parameter
\rho
in the way of soft update introduced in SAC
[
35
]
.
Therefore, the parameter
\rho
can be approximated as:

\overline{\rho}\leftarrow m\rho+(1-m)\cdot\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}}}{\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}{\{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|\}}},

(13)

where

Based on the above idea, TVDO minimizes the following loss to train the joint action-value network in the paradigm of
CTDE
:

L_{td}(;\theta)=[\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{g}(\tau,\mu)+E_{g}(\tau,\mu)]^{2},

(14)

where

E_{g}(\tau,\mu)=\rho\cdot\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}{\left|Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\mu_{i})-Q_{i}(\tau_{i},\overline{\mu}_{i})\right|}.

where
\theta
and
\overline{\theta}

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel MARL method called Tchebycheff value-decomposition optimization (TVDO) that coverts the cooperative MARL task to a MOO problem by introducing a new nonlinear aggregation function based on Tchebycheff approach. In particular, the nonlinear function realises the global optimum by tightly constraining the upper bound of individual action value bias. Then, our theoretical analysis demonstrates that TVDO represents precisely the factorization from global action-value function to individual action-value function with the guarantee of the joint and local policy consistency, which is equivalent to IGM condition. Empirically, in the climb and penalty game, we verify that TVDO represents precisely from global to individual value factorization with an guarantee of the policy consistency, and it also outperforms substantially the state-of-the-art decomposed value-based and policy-based methods on several decentralised micromanagement tasks in StarCraft II.

Appendix E
Limitations

To show limitations of the proposed method, we conduct experiments on the 6h_vs_8z scenario, which is classified as ’Super-Hard’. As shown in Figure
4
(i), TVDO as well as all baselines fail to solve the task. 6h_vs_8z contains of 6 Hydralisks and 8 Zealots. In 6h_vs_8z, the only winning strategy is requiring all Hyralisks to ambush enemy units in their path and then attack together when the Zealots approaching. Since all approaches employ noise-based exploration, agents struggle to identify states that are worth exploring and hardly coordinate exploration efforts to those states. Without improved exploration technique, we found it to be extremely challenging for any methods to pick up this strategy
[
36
]
. This demonstrates that efficient exploration for MARL is still a challenging and open problem.

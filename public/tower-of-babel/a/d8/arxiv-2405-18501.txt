Abstract
. For every large enough n n , we explicitly construct a body of constant width 2 2 that has volume less than 0.9 n ​ Vol ​ ( 𝔹 n CLOSE 0.9^{n}\text{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n} ), where 𝔹 n {\mathbb{B}}^{n} is the unit ball in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . This answers a question of O. Schramm.

Primary 52A20; Secondary 52A40, 28A75, 49Q20

1.
Introduction

A convex body
K
in the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
has constant width
w
if the length of the orthogonal projection of
K
onto any line is equal to
w
.

By the isodiametric inequality in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(see, e.g.,
[
G
]
*Th. 8.8, p. 152), the greatest volume body of constant width 2 is the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
. On the contrast, the problem of finding the least volume body of constant width (Blaschke-Lebesgue problem) is more difficult and remains open for all
n\geq 3
, see for instance
[
CG
]
*Ch. 7,
[
CFG
]
*Part A 22,
[
MMO
]
*p.334-335 for a history of the problem.

Let
{\rm Vol}(K)
denote the volume of a body
K
. A body
K
is said to have effective radius
r
if
{\rm Vol}(K)={\rm Vol}(r{\mathbb{B}}^{n})
. Let
r_{n}
denote the smallest effective radius of a body of constant width
2
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Evidently,
r_{n}\leq 1
. Schramm
[
Schr
]
established the first non-trivial lower bound
r_{n}\geq\sqrt{3+\tfrac{2}{n+1}}-1
and asked (see also the survey of Kalai
[
K
]
*Problem 3.4) if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
r_{n}\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all
n\geq 2
.
We answer the question of Schramm in the affirmative by proving that
r_{n}<0.9
for all sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 1
.

For every sufficiently large
n
, there is a body
M
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of constant width
2
with
{\rm Vol}(M)<0.9^{n}{\rm Vol}({\mathbb{B}}^{n})
.

2.
Preliminaries and the construction of
M

For every positive integer
n
, let
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;:\;|x|=1\}
be the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
\omega_{n}
and
\Omega_{n}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{n}
be, respectively, the surface area of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
and the volume of
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
. Define
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
to be the positive orthant, i.e.,

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;:\;x_{i}\geq 0\;\text{for all}\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}.

Define
S={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and let

L:=\left(\sqrt{2}S\right)\cup\left((\sqrt{2}-2)S\right)\,.

Now we define

(1)

M:=\bigcap_{x\in L}(x+2{\mathbb{B}}^{n}).

Before we prove that

2

Note that any vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
has a unique representation as
v=v_{+}-v_{-}
with
v_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and
v_{+}\cdot v_{-}=0
. More precisely, for a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, the
i
-th coordinates of the vectors
v_{+}
and
v_{-}
are
\max\{v_{i},0\}
and
\max\{-v_{i},0\}

Define a set

A:=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}\;\colon\;a^{2}+(b+\sqrt{2})^{2}\leq 2^{2}\}.

Claim 2
.

If
v\in M
, then
(|v_{+}|,|v_{-}|)\in A
.

Proof.

Define for every nonzero
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
the vector
\widehat{x}=\frac{1}{|x|}x\in S
. Claim
2
holds trivially for
v=0
, so assume that
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a nonzero vector.

If
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
, then
v=v_{+}
and the distance from
v
to
(\sqrt{2}-2)\widehat{v}\in L
is
|v|+2-\sqrt{2}
, and so
|v|\leq\sqrt{2}
and
(|v_{+}|,|v_{-}|)=(|v|,0)\in A
. Otherwise,
\widehat{v}_{-}
is well-defined, and the squared distance from
v
to
\sqrt{2}\widehat{v}_{-}\in L
is
|v_{+}|^{2}+(|v_{-}|+\sqrt{2})^{2}
, so
(|v_{+}|,|v_{-}|)\in A
. ∎

Claim 3
.

The set
M
, as defined in (
1
), is a body of constant width
2
. Moreover
M=\widetilde{M}
, where

\widetilde{M}=\{v-w\;:\;v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},\;(|v|,|w|)\in A\}.

Proof.

First we show that
\widetilde{M}
has diameter at most
2
. Indeed, let
v_{1}-w_{1},v_{2}-w_{2}\in\widetilde{M}
. Then

\displaystyle|(v_{1}-w_{1})-(v_{2}-w_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|(v_{1}+w_{2})-(v_{2}+w_{1})|^{2}\leq|v_{1}+w_{2}|^{2}+|v_{2}+w_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(|v_{1}|+|w_{2}|)^{2}+(|v_{2}|+|w_{1}|)^{2}=:d^{2}.

Now, let
A^{\prime}
denote the set obtained from
A
by reflecting it about the line

, see Figure

. Then

is the distance between
(|v_{1}|,|w_{1}|)\in A
and
(-|w_{2}|,-|v_{2}|)\in A^{\prime}
. Both
A
and
A^{\prime}
are contained in a Reuleaux triangle
\mathcal{R}
with vertices
(\sqrt{2},0)
,
(0,-\sqrt{2})
, as depicted in Figure

. So
(|v_{1}|,|w_{1}|),(-|w_{2}|,-|v_{2}|)\in\mathcal{R}

\mathcal{R}

, we conclude that

. Hence the diameter of
\widetilde{M}
is at most
2
.

By Claim
2
,
M\subseteq\widetilde{M}
. On the other hand, since

and
\widetilde{M}
has diameter at most 2, we have

\widetilde{M}\subseteq\bigcap_{x\in\widetilde{M}}(x+2{\mathbb{B}}^{n})\subseteq\bigcap_{x\in L}(x+2{\mathbb{B}}^{n})=M.

Thus
\widetilde{M}=M
.

3.
Estimate for
{\rm Vol}(M)

In order to prove the Theorem, we will prove that there is a positive
\sigma<0.9
such that

(2)

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq(n+1)\sigma^{n}\Omega_{n}.

We will estimate the volume of
M
in each of the
2^{n}
coordinate orthants. We say that an orthant
Q
is a
(k,n-k)
orthant if
Q
consists of the points with exactly
k
positive and
n-k
negative coordinates.

If
Q
is the
(n,0)
orthant, we have
{\rm Vol}(M\cap Q)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^{n}\Omega_{n}
, as
M\cap Q=\sqrt{2}{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
. Similarly, if
Q
is the
(0,n)
orthant, we have
{\rm Vol}(M\cap Q)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(2-\sqrt{2}\right)^{n}\Omega_{n}
.

Let now
Q
be a
(k,n)
orthant with
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then, by Claim
2
,

{\rm Vol}(M\cap Q)\leq{\rm Vol}(\{v\in Q\;:\;(|v_{+}|,|v_{-}|)\in A\}).

Let
Q_{+}
and
Q_{-}
be the positive orthants in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}
corresponding to the positive and the negative coordinates of
Q
, respectively. If we consider an infinitesimal rectangle
R=[a,a+da]\times[b,b+db]
with
R\subset A
, the set
\{v\in Q\;:\;(|v_{+}|,|v_{-}|)\in R\}
corresponds to the Cartesian product of the portions of the spherical shells
\{v^{\prime}\in Q_{+}\;:\;|v^{\prime}|\in[a,a+da]\}\times\{v^{\prime\prime}\in Q_{-}\;:\;|v^{\prime\prime}|\in[b,b+db]\}
, and the corresponding shell volumes in these orthants are
\frac{\omega_{k}}{2^{k}}a^{k-1}\;da
and
\frac{\omega_{n-k}}{2^{n-k}}b^{n-k-1}\;db
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}(M\cap Q)

\displaystyle\leq\iint\limits_{A}\frac{\omega_{k}}{2^{k}}\frac{\omega_{n-k}}{2^{n-k}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\frac{1}{2^{n}}k(n-k)\;\Omega_{k}\Omega_{n-k}\iint\limits_{A}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db.

Since the number of
(k,n-k)
orthants is exactly
\binom{n}{k}
, we obtain the estimate

(3)

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\frac{\Omega_{n}}{2^{n}}\left((\sqrt{2})^{n}+(2-\sqrt{2})^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k(n-k)\binom{n}{k}\frac{\Omega_{k}\Omega_{n-k}}{\Omega_{n}}\iint\limits_{A}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db\right).

Now, let
\alpha,\beta>0
be some real numbers such that the triangle
T_{\alpha,\beta}:=\{a,b\geq 0\;:\;\frac{a}{\alpha}+\frac{b}{\beta}\leq 1\}
contains the set
A
(see Figure
3
). Note that the condition
A\subseteq T_{\alpha,\beta}
is equivalent to the statement that the distance from the line
\frac{a}{\alpha}+\frac{b}{\beta}=1
to
(0,-\sqrt{2})
is at least
2
, i.e.,
\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})\geq 2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}
.

Then for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, we have

\iint\limits_{A}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db\leq\iint\limits_{T_{\alpha,\beta}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\alpha^{k}\beta^{n-k}\iint\limits_{T_{1,1}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db.

Now, note that
k(n-k)\iint\limits_{T_{1,1}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}
, see Claim
5
in the Appendix for the proof, and so

k(n-k)\binom{n}{k}\frac{\Omega_{k}\Omega_{n-k}}{\Omega_{n}}\iint\limits_{A}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db\leq\frac{\alpha^{k}\Omega_{k}\cdot\beta^{n-k}\Omega_{n-k}}{\Omega_{n}}.

Since
\alpha\geq\sqrt{2}
and
\beta\geq 2-\sqrt{2}
, (
3
) implies

(4)

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\frac{\Omega_{n}}{2^{n}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha^{k}\Omega_{k}\cdot\beta^{n-k}\Omega_{n-k}}{\Omega_{n}}\right).

Finally,
\alpha{\mathbb{B}}^{k}\times\beta{\mathbb{B}}^{n-k}\subseteq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
, which implies
\frac{\alpha^{k}\Omega_{k}\cdot\beta^{n-k}\Omega_{n-k}}{\Omega_{n}}\leq\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\right)^{n}
, and so for every
(\alpha,\beta)
with
A\subseteq T_{\alpha,\beta}
, we have

(5)

r_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n+1)^{1/n}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}.

Let

s=\min\{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\;:\;\alpha,\beta>0,\;\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})\geq 2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\}.

Then

(6)

r_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n+1)^{1/n}s.

A human verifiable proof of
s<1.8
is obtained by choosing
\alpha=1.5
and
\beta=0.7\sqrt{2}
. For such values we have
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=3.23<3.24=1.8^{2}
. On other hand,
\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})>1.5\cdot 1.7\cdot\sqrt{2}>3.6\geq 2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}
. The middle inequality is equivalent to
1.7\sqrt{2}>2.4
, i.e.,
2.89\cdot 2>5.76
.

So
s<1.8
and inequality (
6
) finishes the proof of the Theorem.

Remark 1
.

We will verify in the Appendix that
\frac{1}{2}s
is the least positive root of the equation
8x^{6}-76x^{4}+54x^{2}+1=0
, whose numerical value is
0.89071\ldots
, see Claim
6
.

Corollary 4
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
r_{n}\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, there is
n_{0}
such that for
n>n_{0}
we have
r_{n}<0.9
. For every
n\in\{2,\ldots,n_{0}\}
, the body
M_{n}
that we constructed is a body of constant width
2
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
different from
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
. So by the equality part of the isodiametric inequality (see, e.g.,
[
G
]
*Th. 8.8, p. 152),
\varepsilon_{n}:=1-\left(\frac{{\rm Vol}(M_{n})}{{\rm Vol}({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\right)^{1/n}>0
. Therefore we can take
\varepsilon=\min\{0.1,\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n_{0}}\}>0
.
∎

Appendix

Claim 5
.

Let
T_{1,1}=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\;:\;x+y\leq 1\}
. Then for all integers
n
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, we have
k(n-k)\iint\limits_{T_{1,1}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}
.

Proof.

First,

\iint\limits_{T_{1,1}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1-a}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;db\;da=\frac{1}{n-k}\int_{0}^{1}a^{k-1}(1-a)^{n-k}\;da.

Now, for a variable
x
consider

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}a^{k-1}(1-a)^{n-k}\;da\right)x^{k-1}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-a+ax)^{n-1}\;da

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{n}-1}{(x-1)n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}x^{k-1}.

Comparing the coefficients at
x^{k-1}
in
P(x)
, we get
\int_{0}^{1}a^{k-1}(1-a)^{n-k}\;da=\frac{1}{n\binom{n-1}{k-1}}
, and so

k(n-k)\iint\limits_{T_{1,1}}a^{k-1}b^{n-k-1}\;da\;db=\frac{k}{n\binom{n-1}{k-1}}=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}.\qed

Claim 6
.

Let
s=\min\{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\;:\;\alpha,\beta>0,\;\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})\geq 2\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\}
. Then
x=\frac{1}{2}s
is the least positive root of
P(x):=8x^{6}-76x^{4}+54x^{2}+1=0
, which is also the only root of
P(x)
on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
s
that
x
is the least positive number for which there exist
\alpha,\beta>0
such that
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=4x^{2}
and
\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})\geq 4x
, or equivalently for which

(7)

\max\{\alpha(\beta+\sqrt{2})\;:\;\alpha,\beta>0,\;\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}=4x^{2}\}\geq 4x.\

Now, the function
f(\beta)=\sqrt{4x^{2}-\beta^{2}}(\beta+\sqrt{2})
satisfies
f^{\prime}(0)=2x>0
,
f(2x)=0
, and is positive on
[0,2x)
. So the maximum of
f
is attained at a critical point on
(0,2x)
. The critical point satisfies the quadratic equation
2\beta^{2}+\sqrt{2}\beta-4x^{2}=0
, whose only positive root is

b=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}}.

The condition (
7
) is now equivalent to the inequality
f(b)\geq 4x
, or
f^{2}(b)\geq 16x^{2}
, or

(4x^{2}-b^{2})(b^{2}+2\sqrt{2}b+2)\geq 16x^{2}.

Since
b^{2}=2x^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}b
, we can rewrite this as

(2x^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}b)(2x^{2}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}b+2)\geq 16x^{2},

or, equivalently,

4x^{4}+4x^{2}+\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\sqrt{2}\right)b+\frac{3}{2}b^{2}\geq 16x^{2}.

Replacing
b^{2}
with
2x^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}b
once again, we get

\displaystyle 4x^{4}+\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)b

\displaystyle\geq 9x^{2},

\displaystyle 4x^{4}+\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{2\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 9x^{2},

\displaystyle 4x^{4}-2x^{2}-\frac{1}{8}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}}\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 9x^{2}.

The last inequality can be rewritten as

\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}}\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{8}+11x^{2}-4x^{4}.

Note that the right hand side is positive for
x\in(0,1)
and the argument after (
6
) implies
x<0.9
. So the last inequality can be squared, and we are looking for the least positive
x
such that

\left(\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}\right)\left(4\sqrt{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{8}+11x^{2}-4x^{4}\right)^{2},\text{i.e.},

\left(\frac{1}{8}+2x^{2}\right)\left(32x^{4}+4x^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\right)\geq 16x^{8}+121x^{4}+\frac{1}{64}-88x^{6}-x^{4}+\frac{11}{4}x^{2},\quad\text{or}

64x^{6}+12x^{4}+\frac{3}{4}x^{2}+\frac{1}{64}\geq 16x^{8}-88x^{6}+120x^{4}+\frac{11}{4}x^{2}+\frac{1}{64},

which after canceling
\frac{1}{64}
, moving all terms to one side and dividing by
2x^{2}
turns into

P(x)=8x^{6}-76x^{4}+54x^{2}+1\leq 0.

Note that
P(0)=1>0
,
P(1)=-13<0
and
P(+\infty)=+\infty
. So
P
has at least one root on
(0,1)
and at least one root on
(1,+\infty)
. Combining this with Descartes’ rule of signs, we see that
P(x)
has exactly two roots on
(0,\infty)
. Thus the least positive
x
with
P(x)\leq 0
is the least positive root of
P
, which is also the only root of
P
on
(0,1)
.
∎

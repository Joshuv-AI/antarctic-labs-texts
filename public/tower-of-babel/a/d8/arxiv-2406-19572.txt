Existence and regularity results for a Neumann problem with mixed local and nonlocal diffusion
Craig Cowan, Mohammad El Smaily and Pierre Aime Feulefack

Abstract
In this paper, we consider an elliptic problem driven by a mixed local-nonlocal operator with drift and subject to nonlocal Neumann condition. We prove the existence and uniqueness of a solution u ∈ W 2 , p ​ ( Ω ) \displaystyle u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega) of the considered problem with L p \displaystyle L^{p} -source function when p \displaystyle p and s \displaystyle s are in a certain range.

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we are interested in the study of the following problem

\begin{split}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} Lu:=-\Delta u(x)+(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-q(x)\cdot\nabla u(x)+a(x)u&=f(x)&&\text{ in \quad$\displaystyle\Omega$}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0&&\text{ on }\quad\partial\Omega\\
{\mathcal{N}}_{s}u&=0&&\text{ on }\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

This combined with (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) yield

\frac{\left|\nabla u_{1}(x)\right|}{N+2s}\leq C_{1}+C_{4}.

Hence, for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we have

\left|\nabla u_{1}(x)\right|\leq C.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The following results hold:

1.

Suppose
\displaystyle\frac{N-1}{2N}<s<1/2
and
\displaystyle N<p<\frac{1}{1-2s}
. Then, the mapping
\displaystyle u\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s}\widetilde{u}
is continuous and compact from
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
to
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)
.

2.

Suppose
\displaystyle p>N
and
\displaystyle 1/2\leq s<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}
. The mapping
\displaystyle u\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s}\widetilde{u}
is continuous and compact from
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
to
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

1. For the convenience of the reader we show the continuous argument and compact argument in separate steps. Let
\displaystyle u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
with
\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq 1
and let
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
and then note we have

(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{u}(x)=I+II+III

where

I(u)(x)=\int_{\{y\in\Omega:|y-x|\leq 1\}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

II(u)(x)=\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}:|y-x|\leq 1\}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

\text{and }\penalty\ III(u)(x)=\int_{\{y:|y-x|\geq 1\}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy.

Note that

|I(u)(x)|\leq\int_{\{y\notin\Omega:|y-x|\leq 1\}}\frac{C|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\leq C_{2},

since
\displaystyle s<\frac{1}{2}
. Also, note that

|III(u)(x)|\leq\int_{\{y:|y-x|\geq 1\}}\frac{C}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

since
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}
is bounded on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
and hence
\displaystyle|III(u)(x)|
is bounded in
\displaystyle\Omega
by some
\displaystyle C
. We now estimate
\displaystyle II(u)
. Using
\displaystyle z=y-x
we have

|II(u)(x)|\leq\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x+z)-\widetilde{u}(x)|}{|z|^{N+2s}}dz.

Note that

|\widetilde{u}(x+z)-\widetilde{u}(x)|\leq|z|\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+tz)|dt.

Hence, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\int_{\Omega}|II(u)(x)|^{p}dx&\leq&\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s-1}}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+tz)|dtdz\right)^{p}dx\\
&\leq&C\int_{\Omega}\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s-1}}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+tz)|^{p}dtdzdx\quad\mbox{ \footnotesize{(Jensen's inquality applied twice)}}\\
&=&C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+tz)|^{p}dx\right)dzdt\\
&\leq&C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s-1}}\left(\int_{|x|\leq R}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}dx\right)dzdt,\end{array}

for some large
\displaystyle R
and hence we have

\int_{\Omega}|II(u)(x)|^{p}dx\leq C_{2}\int_{|x|\leq R}|\nabla\widetilde{u}(x)|^{p}dx.

Combining this with the results on
\displaystyle I(u)
and
\displaystyle III(u),
we see that
\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and is continuous from
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
.

We now consider the compactness. Since
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
is a reflexive space its sufficient to show that if
\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
then
\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}_{m}\rightarrow 0
in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)
. Let
\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
and hence it converges to zero in
\displaystyle W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and uniformly in
\displaystyle\Omega
.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{(F(x))^{2}\nabla\widetilde{u}_{m}(x)}{N+2s}&=&\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}(y)-u_{m}(\hat{x}))|x-y|^{-N-2s}dy\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{-N-2s-2}(x-y)dy\\
&&+\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{-N-2s}dy\int_{\Omega}(u_{m}(\hat{x})-u_{m}(y))|x-y|^{-N-2s-2}(x-y)dy.\end{array}

From this we see that
\displaystyle|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{m}(x)|\rightarrow 0
a.e. in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
and note that we can use the result of Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{m}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle W^{1,p}(B_{R})
for all
\displaystyle 0<R<\infty

Let
\displaystyle x\in\Omega

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega}|J_{1}(u_{m})(x)|^{p}dx\\

for some large
\displaystyle R
and we know this goes to zero from the earlier results.

Let
\displaystyle R>1

\begin{array}[]{lll}|III(u_{m})(x)|&\leq&\int_{\{y:1\leq|y-x|\leq R\}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}_{m}(x)|+|\widetilde{u}_{m}(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\\
&&+\int_{\{y:|y-x|\geq R\}}\frac{|\widetilde{u}_{m}(x)|+|\widetilde{u}_{m}(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\\

where

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|III(u_{m})(x)|^{p}dx\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle R\rightarrow\infty
.

&\qquad\quad+\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|z|>1\}}\frac{[\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(x+z)]+[\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(x-z)]}{|z|^{N+2s}}\ dz,\end{array}

The following result is a maximum principle that we will use in the proof of existence of a solution.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set,
\displaystyle q
and
\displaystyle a
be smooth with
\displaystyle a(x)\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\Omega

(2.12)

Then,
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}\equiv 0
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle u
denote the solution and, for notation, we also let
\displaystyle u
denote
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}
outside of
\displaystyle\Omega
. Suppose
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}

such that
\displaystyle u(x_{0})=\inf_{\Omega}u

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\partial\Omega

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle x_{m}\notin\overline{\Omega}
such that
\displaystyle x_{m}\rightarrow x_{0}

0=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)-u(x_{m})}{|y-x_{m}|^{N+2s}}dy.

0=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)-u(x_{0})}{|y-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}dy,

which shows that
\displaystyle u=C=const.
is constant in
\displaystyle\Omega
and hence constant in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then note from the equation we have
\displaystyle a(x)C=f(x)
in
\displaystyle\Omega
and this shows that
\displaystyle u=C\geq 0
provided
\displaystyle a(x)
not identically zero which we have assumed and hence we are done. We now suppose
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\Omega
is such that
\displaystyle u(x_{0})=\inf_{\Omega}u
and we suppose
\displaystyle u(x_{0})<0
.

We can also suppose that
\displaystyle u(x_{0})<\inf_{\partial\Omega}u
. Note that, from the definition of
\displaystyle u_{1},
we get
\displaystyle u(x)>u(x_{0})
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

\displaystyle\sigma>0
such that
\displaystyle u(x_{0})+10\sigma<\min_{\partial\Omega}u

\displaystyle u(x_{0})+10\sigma<0

\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
small set
\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\delta(x)>\varepsilon\}
and
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\delta(x)<\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}>0

which gives

u(x)\geq u(x_{0})+\frac{8\sigma\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{0}}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy}.

From this we can show there is some
\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon_{0}}>0

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon_{0}}<1

\displaystyle u(x)\geq u(x_{0})+8\sigma c_{\varepsilon_{0}}
for all
\displaystyle x\notin\overline{\Omega}
.

Let
\displaystyle\eta
denote a standard radial mollifier with
\displaystyle\eta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow 0

but by the monotonicity of

\inf_{\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+6\sigma<\inf_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{0}}}u\leq\inf_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}u

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}

for
\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}
and hence the minimum is contained in the interior of
\displaystyle\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
.
We now want to show that
\displaystyle\min_{\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\leq u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{10}
with:

u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{0})<u(x_{0})+8\sigma c_{\varepsilon_{0}},

(2.13)

We consider the three cases:

(i)
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
with
\displaystyle\delta(x)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
,
(ii)
\displaystyle x\notin\Omega
with
\displaystyle\delta(x)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

and (iii)
\displaystyle x\notin\Omega
with
\displaystyle\delta(x)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

Case (i).

Here we have
\displaystyle x\in\Omega
with
\displaystyle\delta(x)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
So in this case we have

u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)=\int_{|y-x|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y-x)u(y)dy,

and note the integral can be decomposed as

\int_{|y-x|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},y\in\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y-x)u(y)dy+\int_{|y-x|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},y\notin\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y-x)u(y)dy

and from this we see that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle(u(x_{0})+8\sigma c_{\varepsilon_{0}})\int_{|y-x|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y-x)dy

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(x_{0})+8\sigma c_{\varepsilon_{0}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\displaystyle u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.

Case (ii).

In this case we have from the definition of
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}
that

\begin{split}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dy&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\int_{|y-z|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z-y)u(z)dzdy\\
&\geq\inf_{\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dy,\end{split}

where we have used the computations of Case
\displaystyle(i)
.

Case (iii).

This follows similarly.

From the above we have, for small enough
\displaystyle\varepsilon
, that
\displaystyle u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})=\min_{\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
(some
\displaystyle x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\in\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
) and
\displaystyle u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})+6\sigma<\inf_{\partial\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
. Also note we have (take
\displaystyle\tau=\varepsilon/2
)

\displaystyle L(u^{\tau})(x)=f^{\tau}(x)\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\Omega_{\tau}
and at
\displaystyle x_{\tau}
we have

-\Delta u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})+(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})+q(x_{\tau})\cdot\nabla u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})=f^{\tau}(x_{\tau})-a(x_{\tau})u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})\geq 0.

(2.14)

But
\displaystyle-\Delta u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})\leq 0
,
\displaystyle\nabla u^{\tau}(x_{\tau})=0
and note that

(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})=\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})-u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(y)}{|x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

and note
\displaystyle y\mapsto u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})-u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(y)
is continuous in
\displaystyle y
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
, nonpositive and not identically zero. From this we see that
\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})<0
and this contradicts (
2.14
).

∎

3
Proof of existence of a solution

For
\displaystyle u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in\mathbb{R},
we define

L_{\gamma}u(x)=-\Delta u+\gamma(-\Delta)^{s}\tilde{u}(x)+a(x)u(x)+q\cdot\nabla u(x),\quad x\in\Omega

and we consider the family of indexed problems

\begin{split}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{\gamma}u&=f&&\text{ in \quad$\displaystyle\Omega$}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0&&\text{ on }\quad\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(3.1)

Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
be the set

{\mathcal{A}}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} \gamma\in[0,1]:\penalty\ \exists C_{\gamma}>0\text{ such that for all }f\in L^{p}(\Omega),\eqref{LgammaEq}\text{ has a solution }\\
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\qquad\end{aligned}\right\}.

(3.2)

In (
3.2
), we take the constant
\displaystyle C_{\gamma}
to be the smallest constant such that
\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
holds for all functions
\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. In other words, if
\displaystyle C_{\gamma}>\varepsilon>0
then there exists
\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\geq(C_{\gamma}-\varepsilon)\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.3)

By classical theory,
\displaystyle 0\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Our goal is to show that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
is both open and closed and since
\displaystyle[0,1]
is connected we then see that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}\in\{\emptyset,[0,1]\}
, and since its non empty, we must have
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}=[0,1]
and in particular
\displaystyle 1\in{\mathcal{A}}
which corresponds to the result we are trying to prove.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
is closed.

We prove
\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
is closed. Let
\displaystyle\gamma_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
\displaystyle\gamma_{m}\rightarrow\gamma
and let
\displaystyle C_{m}=C_{\gamma_{m}}
denote the associated constant. We first consider the case of
\displaystyle C_{m}
bounded. Let
\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}}=1
. Since
\displaystyle\gamma_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}
there is some
\displaystyle u_{m}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
which satisfies (
3.1
), with
\displaystyle\gamma_{m}
in place of
\displaystyle\gamma
, and
\displaystyle\|u_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq C_{m}\|f\|_{L^{p}}=C_{m}
. Note we can rewrite the problem as

-\Delta u_{m}+au_{m}+q\cdot\nabla u_{m}=f+\gamma_{m}\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}_{m}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(3.4)

with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u_{m}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Since
\displaystyle C_{m}
is bounded then we have
\displaystyle u_{m}
bounded in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
and by passing to a subsequence we can assume that
\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup u
in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq\liminf_{m}\|u_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq C_{1}
. Also note by our earlier compactness result we have
\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}_{m}\rightarrow\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}
in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)
and this (along with the above weak
\displaystyle W^{2,p}
convergence) is sufficient convergence to pass to the limit in
(
3.4
). This shows that
\displaystyle\gamma\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

We now consider the case of
\displaystyle C_{m}\rightarrow\infty
. Then there is some
\displaystyle f_{m}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle u_{m}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
which solves
\displaystyle L_{\gamma_{m}}u_{m}=f_{m}
in
\displaystyle\Omega
with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u_{m}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
and

\|u_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}}\geq(C_{m}-1)\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p}}.

By normalizing we can assume
\displaystyle\|u_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}=1
and hence
\displaystyle\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0
. By passing to subsequences we can assume that
\displaystyle u_{m}\rightharpoonup u
in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
and strongly in
\displaystyle W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. As before we rewrite the equation for
\displaystyle u_{m}
by

-\Delta u_{m}+au_{m}+q\cdot\nabla u_{m}=f_{m}+\gamma_{m}\Delta^{s}\widetilde{u}_{m}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(3.5)

with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u_{m}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. If
\displaystyle u=0
then note the right hand side of (
3.5
) converges to zero in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega)
and by standard elliptic theory we have
\displaystyle u_{m}\rightarrow 0
in
\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
which contradicts the normalization of
\displaystyle u_{m}
. We now assume
\displaystyle u\neq 0
. By compactness we can pass to the limit in (
3.5
) to see that
\displaystyle u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
satisfies
\displaystyle L_{\gamma}u=0
in
\displaystyle\Omega
with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
which contradicts
Theorem
2.6
.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}
is open.

Let
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and take
\displaystyle|\varepsilon|
small; when
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\in\{0,1\}
we need to restrict the sign of
\displaystyle\varepsilon
. Our goal is to show that
\displaystyle\gamma=\gamma_{0}+\varepsilon\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Fix
\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}}=1
and since
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}
there is some
\displaystyle v_{0}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
which solves
\displaystyle L_{\gamma_{0}}v_{0}=f
in
\displaystyle\Omega
with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}v_{0}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. We look for a solution of (

) of the form
\displaystyle u=v_{0}+\phi
. Writing out the details one sees we need
\displaystyle\phi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)

L_{\gamma_{0}}\phi=\varepsilon\Delta^{s}\widetilde{v}_{0}+\varepsilon\Delta^{s}\widetilde{\phi}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(3.6)

with
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\phi=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle W^{2,p}(\Omega)
.

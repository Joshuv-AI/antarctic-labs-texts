Normalized ground states for 3D dipolar Bose-Einstein condensate with attractive three-body interactions
Yongming Luo and Athanasios Stylianou

Abstract
We study the existence of normalized ground states for the 3D dipolar Bose-Einstein condensate equation with attractive three-body interactions: − Δ ​ u + β ​ u + λ 1 ​ | u | 2 ​ u + λ 2 ​ ( K ∗ | u | 2 ) ​ u − | u | 4 ​ u = 0 . \displaystyle-\Delta u+\beta u+\lambda_{1}|u|^{2}u+\lambda_{2}(K*|u|^{2})u-|u|^{4}u=0. (DBEC) When λ 2 = 0 \lambda_{2}=0 or u u is radial, ( DBEC ) reduces to the cubic-quintic NLS − Δ ​ u + β ​ u + λ 1 ​ | u | 2 ​ u − | u | 4 ​ u = 0 , \displaystyle-\Delta u+\beta u+\lambda_{1}|u|^{2}u-|u|^{4}u=0, (CQNLS) which has been recently studied by Soave in [ 31 ] . In particular, it was shown that for any λ 1 < 0 \lambda_{1}<0 and c > 0 c>0 , ( CQNLS ) possesses a radially symmetric ground state solution with mass c c and for λ 1 ≥ 0 \lambda_{1}\geq 0 , ( CQNLS ) has no non-trivial solution. We show that by adding a dipole-dipole interaction to ( CQNLS ), the geometric nature of ( CQNLS ) changes dramatically and techniques as the ones from [ 31 ] cannot be used anymore to obtain similar results. More precisely, due to the axisymmetric nature of the dipole-dipole interaction potential, the energy corresponding to ( DBEC ) is not stable under symmetric rearrangements, hence conventional arguments based on the radial symmetry of solutions are inapplicable. We will overcome this difficulty by appealing to subtle variational and perturbative methods and prove the following: (i) If the pair ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) (\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) is unstable and λ 1 < 0 \lambda_{1}<0 , then for any c > 0 c>0 , ( DBEC ) has a ground state solution with mass c c . (ii) If the pair ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) (\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) is unstable and λ 1 ≥ 0 \lambda_{1}\geq 0 , then there exists some c ∗ = c ∗ ​ ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) ≥ 0 c^{*}=c^{*}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\geq 0 such that for all c > c ∗ c>c^{*} , ( DBEC ) has a ground state solution with mass c c . Moreover, any non-trivial solution of ( DBEC ) in this case must be non-radial. (iii) If the pair ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) (\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) is stable , then ( DBEC ) has no non-trivial solutions.

Palais-Smale sequences with vanishing virial

2.6

We first show that the number

Lemma 2.8
.

For all
c>0
we have
\gamma(c)\in(0,\frac{\mathcal{S}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}]
.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(c)
. Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Sobolev inequalities we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}=A(u)=-\frac{3}{4}B(u)+C(u)\leq C(c^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|^{3}_{2}+\|\nabla u\|^{6}_{2}).

Since
c\neq 0
we can divide by
\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}
and modify the constants to obtain that

\inf_{u\in V(c)}\|\nabla u\|_{2}>0.

Since

E(u)=E(u)-\frac{1}{3}Q(u)=\frac{1}{6}(A(u)+C(u))\geq\frac{1}{6}A(u)=\frac{1}{6}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}

for
u\in V(c)
, the lower bound follows by taking infimum over
V(c)
on both sides. It is left to show the upper bound. We define

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{1}\|u\|_{4}^{4}-\frac{1}{6}\|u\|_{6}^{6},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(u)

\displaystyle:=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\lambda_{1}\|u\|_{4}^{4}-\|u\|_{6}^{6}.

Then
\tilde{E}(u)
and
\tilde{Q}(u)
are the energy and virial corresponding to the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\beta u+\lambda_{1}|u|^{2}u-|u|^{4}u=0

(2.7)

respectively. For a radially symmetric
u
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(u)

\displaystyle:=E(u),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(u)

\displaystyle:=Q(u).

Now we define

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=\varphi(x)\cdot(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=c^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}}{\|u_{|\varepsilon}\|_{2}},

(2.9)

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a real radial cut-off function with
c\equiv 1
in
B_{1}
,

\phi\equiv 0
in
B_{2}^{c}
and
\phi
is radially decreasing. Denote by
\tilde{t}^{*}
the number
introduced in Lemma
2.3
, but corresponding to (
2.7
). Due to

[
31
, Lem. 6.4]
there exist

such that

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(v_{\varepsilon}^{\tilde{t}^{*}})\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}+C_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{2}\varepsilon=\frac{\mathcal{S}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}})

(2.10)

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Since
v_{\varepsilon}^{\tilde{t}^{*}}
is radially
symmetric, using (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) we obtain that
v_{\varepsilon}^{\tilde{t}^{*}}\in V(c)
and

. The upper
bound follows by the definition of
\gamma(c)
.
∎

Lemma 2.10

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{\rm GN}:=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\},B(u)<0}\frac{\|u\|_{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{3}}{-B(u)}>0.

(2.11)

Let

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{2}=n,\quad-B(u_{n})=1,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}=n^{-\frac{1}{3}}(\mathrm{C}_{\rm GN}+o(1))^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

(2.13)

Using Lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
be unstable and let
c\in(0,\infty)
. If

\gamma(c)\in\big(0,\frac{\mathcal{S}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}\big)
, then (

) has at least

Proof.

let
(u_{n},\beta_{n})_{n}
and
(u,\beta)
be the PS-sequence and its limit deduced from Corollary
2.6
. Using the splitting properties given by
[
12
, Theorem 1]
for

-norms and by
[

for

we obtain that

\displaystyle A(u_{n}-u)+A(u)

\displaystyle=A(u_{n})+o(1),

\displaystyle B(u_{n}-u)+B(u)

\displaystyle=B(u_{n})+o(1),

\displaystyle C(u_{n}-u)+C(u)

\displaystyle=C(u_{n})+o(1),

\displaystyle D(u_{n}-u)+D(u)

\displaystyle=D(u_{n})+o(1),

E(u_{n}-u)+E(u)=E(u_{n})+o(1).

From the lower semicontinuity of the

-norm we obtain that

\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=c.

By Lemma
2.7

u\neq 0
. Consequently, we infer that
u\in V(c_{1})
for some

. We also have
\gamma(c)=E(u_{n})+o(1)
from Lemma
2.6

E(u_{n}-u)+\gamma(c_{1})\leq E(u_{n}-u)+E(u)=E(u_{n})+o(1)=\gamma(c)+o(1).

(3.1)

On the other hand, direct calculation results in

-Q(v)+3E(v)=\frac{1}{2}A(v)+\frac{1}{2}C(v)

(3.2)

for all

. Using

and
Q(u_{n})=o(1)

Q(u_{n}-u)=Q(u_{n}-u)+Q(u)=Q(u_{n})+o(1)=o(1).

Inserting this into (
3.2
), we conclude that
E(u_{n}-u)\geq o(1)
, since the right-hand side of (
3.2

2.9

\displaystyle A(u_{n}-u)

\displaystyle=o(1),

\displaystyle C(u_{n}-u)

\displaystyle=o(1).

But

and

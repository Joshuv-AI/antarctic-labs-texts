Abstract
. In this paper we consider a semiclassical version of the wave equations with singular Hölder time-dependent propagation speeds on the lattice ℏ ​ ℤ n \hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n} . We allow the propagation speed to vanish leading to the weakly hyperbolic nature of the equations. Curiously, very much contrary to the Euclidean case considered by Colombini, de Giorgi and Spagnolo [ 2 ] and by other authors, the Cauchy problem in this case is well-posed in ℓ 2 ​ ( ℏ ​ ℤ n ) \ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}) . However, we also recover the well-posedness results in the intersection of certain Gevrey and Sobolev spaces in the limit of the semiclassical parameter ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the wave equation on the lattice

\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x=\hbar k,~k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\},

depending on a (small) parameter
\hbar>0
, and in the behaviour of its solutions as
\hbar\to 0
.
The Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}
and is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}u(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(u(k+\hbar v_{j})+u(k-\hbar v_{j})\right)-2nu(k),

where
v_{j}
is the
j^{th}
basis vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, having all zeros except for
1
as the
j^{th}
component.

The semiclassical analogue of the classical wave equation with time-dependent coefficients on the lattice
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is given by the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}u(t,k)-\hbar^{-2}a(t)\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}u(t,k)+b(t)u(t,k)=f(t,k),~~\textrm{ with }~~t\in(0,T],\\
u(0,k)=u_{0}(k),\quad k\in{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}},\\
\partial_{t}u(0,k)=u_{1}(k),\quad k\in{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

for the time-dependent propagation speed
a=a(t)\geq 0
and bounded real-valued functions
b=b(t)\geq 0
, as well as
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T],{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})})
.
The equation (
1.1
) is the semiclassical discretisation of the classical wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Cauchy problem in the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}v(t,x)-a(t)\Delta v(t,x)+b(t)v(t,x)=f(t,x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\\
\partial_{t}v(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\Delta
is the usual Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Here we can think of the Cauchy data in (
1.1
) as the evaluations of the Cauchy data from (
1.2
) on the lattice
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

There is an extensive literature concerning (
1.2
) going back to the seminal paper by Colombini, de Giorgi and Spagnolo
[
2
]
.
In particular, some very peculiar things can happen with the solvability of (
1.2
).
For example, it has been shown by Colombini and Spagnolo in
[
5
]
and by Colombini, Jannelli and Spagnolo in
[
3
]
that even in one space dimension, the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) does not have to be well-posed in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
if
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
is not strictly positive or if it is in the Hölder class
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
.
Therefore, to ensure the well-posedness of (
1.2
), one is forced to work in Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{\gamma}^{s}
.

In the analysis of (
1.2
), it makes sense to distinguish between the following four cases depending on the properties of the propagation speed
a(t)
, with
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
denoting the Hölder class (see (
2.1
)):

Case 1:
a\in{{\rm Lip}}([0,T])
,
\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0,

Case 2:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T]),
with
0<\alpha<1
,
\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0,

Case 3:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T]),
with
l\geq 2,

a(t)\geq 0,

Case 4:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
, with
0<\alpha<2
,
a(t)\geq 0.

Indeed, the proofs of all these cases are different, also yielding results of different type in several cases. Here, Case 1 is the best ‘classical’ situation, Case 2 is lower regularity strictly hyperbolic case, Case 3 is the regular weakly hyperbolic case, and Case 4 is the worst one when
a
is of low regularity and may also vanish.
Let us briefly summarise the known results for these cases, which we formulate, for simplicity, for the case
f\equiv 0
.

Case 1: For any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, if the initial Cauchy data
(u_{0},u_{1})
are in the Sobolev spaces
H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then there exists a unique solution of (
1.2
) in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T],H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
.

Case 2: If the initial Cauchy data
(u_{0},u_{1})
are in
\gamma^{s}\times\gamma^{s}
, then there exists a unique solution of (
1.2
) in
\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T],\gamma^{s})
, provided that

1\leq s<1+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.

(1.3)

Case 3: If the initial Cauchy data
(u_{0},u_{1})
are in
\gamma^{s}\times\gamma^{s}
, then there exists a unique solution of (
1.2
) in
\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T],\gamma^{s})
, provided that

1\leq s<1+\frac{l}{2}.

(1.4)

Case 4: If the initial Cauchy data
(u_{0},u_{1})
are in
\gamma^{s}\times\gamma^{s}
, then there exists a unique solution of (
1.2
) in
\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T],\gamma^{s})
, provided that

1\leq s<1+\frac{\alpha}{2}.

(1.5)

The situation in Case 1 is a classical result. Case 2 was shown in
[
2
]
with further extensions in
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
for equations of higher order on
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, respectively. Case 3 was shown, in particular, in
[
10
]
, and the higher dimensional case including low order terms was analysed in
[
8
]
. For the analysis in Case 4 on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
[
7
]
can be referred.

The situation with (
1.1
) is in a striking difference with the above results for (
1.2
), in the sense that (
1.1
) is always well-posed in
{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
for all the Cases 1-4.
In some sense, it is natural since no regularity issues on the lattice are involved, so there is no noticeable loss of regularity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0.
Assume
b\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
satisfies
b(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then in all the cases, Case 1-Case 4, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well-posed in
{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
. In particular,
if
u_{0},u_{1}\in{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T],{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})})
, then for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\in{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
. Moreover, for each
\hbar>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\hbar,T}>0
such that

\|u(t)\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\|\partial_{t}{u}(t)\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\\
\leq C_{\hbar,T}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\|{u}_{1}\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})})}\right),

(1.6)

for all
u_{0},u_{1}\in{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})})
.

At the same time, we can explain why there is such a difference in the well-posedness results between the settings of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
For this we need to specify the constants
C_{\hbar,T}
appearing in (
1.6
), especially their dependence on
\hbar
. Clearly, this constant may go to infinity as
\hbar\to 0
, especially in Cases 2-4. The following theorem shows that under the assumptions that the solutions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
exist, they can be recovered in the limit as
\hbar\to 0
. We require a little additional Sobolev regularity to ensure that the convergence results are global on the whole of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be the solutions of the Cauchy problems (
1.1
) on
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and (
1.2
) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, respectively, with the same Cauchy data
u_{0},u_{1}
. Now,

1)

for the Case 1, if

(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
s\geq 5
for
n\leq 3
and
s>3+\frac{n}{2}
for
n\geq 4
, and

2)

for the Case 2-Case 4, if
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\left(\gamma^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)\times\left(\gamma^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
satisfies the assumptions (
1.3
)-(
1.5
) and
p\geq 5
,

then for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\|\partial_{t}u(t)-\partial_{t}v(t)\|_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\to 0\;\textrm{ as }\;\hbar\to 0.

(1.7)

Thus, when
\hbar\to 0
, for the above mentioned space, we actually recover the well-posedness results on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where the solution
u
in the above statement becomes restricted to the lattice
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

We note that the symbolic calculus of pseudo-differential operators on lattices (or pseudo-difference operators) has been developed in
[
1
]
. In particular, the symbol of
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}
defined by

\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)=e^{-2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}(e^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta})

is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(e^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}(k+hv_{j})\cdot\theta}+e^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}(k-hv_{j})\cdot\theta}\right)-2ne^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}\right)

(1.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(e^{2\pi i\theta_{j}}+e^{-2\pi i\theta_{j}}\right)-2n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cos(2\pi\theta_{j})-2n,

with
(k,\theta)\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, and it is independent of
k
(and of
\hbar
).

Definition 1.3
.

(Symbol class
\left.S^{m}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right)\right)
. Let
m\in(-\infty,\infty)
.
We say that a function
\sigma:\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
belongs to

if
\sigma(k,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}^{n}\right)
for all
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and for
all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha,\beta,\hbar}
such that

\left|D_{\theta}^{(\beta)}\Delta_{k}^{\alpha}\sigma(k,\theta)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\hbar}(1+|k|)^{m-|\alpha|},

(1.9)

for all
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Op}(\sigma)
the pseudo-difference operator with symbol

given by

\mathrm{Op}(\sigma)f(k):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}\sigma(k,\theta)\widehat{f}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad k\in\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

(1.10)

where

\widehat{f}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}f(k),~~~\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{n},~k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(1.11)

We refer to Section
3
for further explanations in this direction, and to
[
1
]
for thorough details.
The proof of Theorem

, given in Section

.
Difference equations on lattices, including Schrödinger equations have been studied (e.g. in

2.
Preparatory estimates

In the next proposition, we prove certain energy estimates for second order ordinary differential
equations with explicit dependence on parameters. This will be crucial in the
second part of this paper when we will be considering distributional coefficients.
It extends the result obtained in
[
17
]
from the point of view of inclusion of the lower order terms and the explicit control on the constants.

Notation-wise, we write that
a\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])
if for some constant
L_{a}
, we have

|a(t)-a(s)|\leq L_{a}|t-s|^{\alpha},

(2.1)

for all
t,s\in[0,T].
The smallest
L_{a}
in the above inequality will be denoted by

\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\beta>0
be positive constants, let
b(t)
be a bounded real-valued function and let
a(t)
be a function satisfying one of the following conditions:

Case 1:
a\in{{\rm Lip}}([0,T])
,
a_{0}:=\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0,

Case 2:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T]),
with
0<\alpha<1
,
a_{0}:=\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0,

Case 3:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T]),
with
l\geq 2,

a(t)\geq 0,

Case 4:
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
, with
0<\alpha<2
,
a(t)\geq 0.

Consider the following Cauchy problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{\prime\prime}(t)+\beta^{2}a(t)v(t)+b(t)v(t)=0,\quad t\in(0,T],\\
v(0)=v_{0}\in\mathbb{C},\\
v^{\prime}(0)=v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Then the following holds:

Case 1 :

There exist two positive constants
C_{1},K_{1}>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{1}e^{K_{1}T\langle\beta\rangle^{2}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

(2.3)

holds for all
\beta>0
.

Case 2 :

There exist two positive constants
C_{2},K_{2}>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{2}e^{K_{2}T\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

(2.4)

for any
0<s\leq\frac{1}{2}
and for all
\beta>0
.

Case 3 :

There exist two positive constants
C_{3},K_{3}>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{3}e^{K_{3}\langle\beta\rangle^{6-\frac{4}{\sigma}}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

(2.5)

with
\sigma=1+\frac{l}{2}
and for all
\beta>0
.

Case 4 :

There exist two positive constants
C_{4},K_{4}>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{4}e^{K_{4}T\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

(2.6)

for any
0<s\leq\frac{\alpha+2}{4}
and for all
\beta>0
.

The constants
C_{j}
’s and

Proof.

First, we reduce the problem (
2.2
) to a first order equation. We define

V(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}iv(t)\\
\partial_{t}v(t)\end{matrix}\right),~~~~~V_{0}:=\left(\begin{matrix}i{v_{0}}\\
{v_{1}}\end{matrix}\right),

(2.7)

and the matrices

A(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
a(t)&0\end{matrix}\right),~~~B(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1-\beta^{2}\\
b(t)&0\end{matrix}\right).

(2.8)

This allows us to reformulate the given second order system (
2.2
) as the first order system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}V^{\prime}=i\beta^{2}A(t)V(t)+iB(t)V(t),\\
V(0)=V_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

Now we will treat each case separately.

Case 1:
a\in\text{Lip}([0,T]),a_{0}:=\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0
.

We observe that the eigenvalues of the matrix
A(t)
are given by
\pm\sqrt{a(t)}.
The symmetriser
S
of
A,
i.e., the matrix such that

SA-A^{\ast}S=0,

is given by

S(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}a(t)&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Consider

\displaystyle\left(S(t)V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(t)|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|v(t)|^{2}+\max_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|V(t)|^{2}.

Similarily

\displaystyle\left(S(t)V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|V(t)|^{2}.

(2.11)

If we now define the energy

E(t):=\left(S(t)V(t),V(t)\right),

then from (
2.10
), and (
2.11
), it follows that

\min_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|V(t)|^{2}\leq E(t)\leq\max_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}|V(t)|^{2}.

(2.12)

Since
a\in{\textbf{Lip}}([0,T])
, there exist two constants
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
such that

a_{0}=\min\limits_{t\in[0,T]}a(t)~~~\textrm{ and }~~~a_{1}=\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}a(t).

Thus, if we set

c_{0,a}=\min\{a_{0},1\}
and
c_{1,a}=\max\{a_{1},1\}
, then
the inequality (
2.12
) becomes

c_{0,a}|V(t)|^{2}\leq E(t)\leq c_{1,a}|V(t)|^{2}.

(2.13)

Then we can calculate

\displaystyle E_{t}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(S_{t}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+\left(S(t)V_{t}(t),V(t)\right)+\left(S(t)V(t),V_{t}(t)\right)

(2.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(S_{t}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\beta^{2}\left(S(t)A(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\left(S(t)B(t)V(t),V(t)\right)-

\displaystyle i\beta^{2}\left(S(t)V(t),A(t)V(t)\right)-i\left(S(t)V(t),B(t)V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(S_{t}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\beta^{2}\left(\left(SA-A^{\ast}S\right)V(t),V(t)\right)+

\displaystyle i\left(\left(SB-B^{\ast}S\right)V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(S_{t}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\left(\left(SB-B^{\ast}S\right)V(t),V(t)\right).

From the definition of
S
and
B

\displaystyle SB-B^{\ast}S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}0&a(1-\beta^{2})-b\\
b-a(1-\beta^{2})&0\end{matrix}\right).

Then

\displaystyle\|SB-B^{\ast}S\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}.

(2.15)

From using (
2.15
) in (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle E_{t}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}|V(t)|^{2}+(\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])})|V(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)|V(t)|^{2}.

Applying (
2.13
) in (

), we get

\displaystyle E_{t}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)E(t)

(2.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa E(t),

where
\kappa=c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)>0

\displaystyle E(t)\leq e^{\kappa T}E(0)=e^{c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)T}E(0).

(2.18)

Therefore, putting together (
2.18
) and (
2.13
) we obtain

\displaystyle c_{0,a}|V(t)|^{2}\leq E(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)T}E(0)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1,a}e^{c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)T}|V(0)|^{2}.

If we set,
C_{1}=c_{0,a}^{-1}c_{1,a}e^{c_{0,a}^{-1}\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)T}
, and
K_{1}=c_{0,a}^{-1}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}
, then using the definition of
V(t)
and (

), that is,

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{1}e^{K_{1}T\langle\beta\rangle^{2}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

finishing the proof of Case 1.

Case 2:

a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T]),
with
0<\alpha<1
,
a_{0}:=\min_{[0,T]}a(t)>0.

Here we follow the
method developed by Colombini and Kinoshita in
[
4
]
. We look for solutions of the form

V(t)=e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\det H(t))^{-1}H(t)W(t),

(2.20)

where

•

s\in\mathbb{R}
depends on
\alpha
as will be determined in the argument;

•

the function
\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T])
is real-valued with
\rho(0)=0
;

•

W(t)
is the energy;

•

H(t)
is the matrix defined by

H(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t)&\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t)\end{matrix}\right),

where for all
\epsilon\in(0,1],

\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t)
and
\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t)
are regularisations of the eigenvalues of the matrix
A(t)
of the form

\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t):=\left(-\sqrt{a}\ast\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t),

\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t):=\left(+\sqrt{a}\ast\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t),

with
\{\phi_{\epsilon}(t)\}_{\epsilon>0}
, being a family of cut-off functions defined starting from a non-negative even function
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),
with
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\phi=1,
by setting
\phi_{\epsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\phi(\frac{1}{\epsilon}).
By construction, it follows that
\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon},\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,T]).

We observe the inequality

\displaystyle\det H(t)=\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t)-\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t)\geq 2\sqrt{a_{0}}.

(2.21)

Moreover, using the Hölder regularity of
a(t)
of order
\alpha
and, for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t)+\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(-\sqrt{a}\ast\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t)+\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{a(t-y)}\phi_{\epsilon}(y)dy-\sqrt{a(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{a(t-\epsilon x)}\phi(x)dx-\sqrt{a(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|a(t-\epsilon x)-a(t))|}{\sqrt{a(t-\epsilon x)}+\sqrt{a(t)}}\phi(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Similarly, we can compute
|\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t)-\sqrt{a(t)}|
and we get

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon}(t)+\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(a)\epsilon^{{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{2}^{\epsilon}(t)-\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(a)\epsilon^{{\alpha}},

(2.22)

where

\displaystyle c(a)=\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

(2.23)

Now substituting the suggested solution (
2.20
) in (
2.9
), we get

-\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{H(t)W(t)}{\det H(t)}+e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{H_{t}(t)W(t)}{\det H(t)}+e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{H(t)W_{t}(t)}{\det H(t)}\\
-e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\det H)_{t}(t)\frac{H(t)W(t)}{(\det H(t))^{2}}\\
=i\beta^{2}A(t)e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{H(t)W(t)}{\det H(t)}+iB(t)e^{-\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{H(t)W(t)}{\det H(t)}.

Multiplying both sides by
e^{\rho(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\det H(t)H^{-1}(t)
, we get

W_{t}(t)=\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}W(t)-H^{-1}(t)H_{t}(t)W(t)+(\det H)_{t}(t)(\det H(t))^{-1}W(t)\\
+i\beta^{2}H^{-1}(t)A(t)H(t)W(t)+iH^{-1}(t)B(t)H(t)W(t).

This leads to the equality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|W(t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}(W_{t}(t),W(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}|W(t)|^{2}-2{\rm Re}\left(H^{-1}(t)H_{t}(t)W(t),W(t)\right)+

\displaystyle 2(\det H(t))^{-1}(\det H)_{t}(t)|W(t)|^{2}+2\beta^{2}{\rm Im}\left(H^{-1}(t)A(t)H(t)W(t),W(t)\right)+

\displaystyle 2{\rm Im}\left(H^{-1}(t)B(t)H(t)W(t),W(t)\right).

We note that

2{\rm Im}\left(H^{-1}AHW,W\right)\leq\|H^{-1}AH-(H^{-1}AH)^{\ast}\|\|W\|^{2}.

(2.24)

Thus, we obtain

{}\frac{d}{dt}\left|W(t)\right|^{2}\leq\big(2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+2\|H^{-1}(t)H_{t}(t)\|+2|\left(\det H(t)\right)^{-1}\left(\det H\right)_{t}(t)|+\big.\\
\big.\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|H^{-1}AH-(H^{-1}AH)^{\ast}\|+\|H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}\|\big)\|W\|^{2}.

(2.25)

Using the techniques from
[
9
,
17
]
, we estimate the above terms as follows:

(1)

\|H^{-1}(t)H_{t}(t)\|\lesssim{\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}-1}
,

(2)

|\left(\det H(t)\right)^{-1}\left(\det H\right)_{t}(t)|\lesssim\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}-1}
,

(3)

\|H^{-1}AH-(H^{-1}AH)^{\ast}\|\lesssim\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}}
,

(4)

\|H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}\|\lesssim\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

Indeed, we deal with these four terms as follows:

(1)

Since
H^{-1}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}&-1\\
-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}&1\end{matrix}\right)
and
H_{t}(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}&\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}\end{matrix}\right)
, it follows that the entries of the matrix
H^{-1}H_{t}
are given by the functions
\frac{\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{j}}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}.
We compute for
j=2
, i.e.,
\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}(t)
, can be estimated by

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{a}\ast\partial_{t}\phi_{\epsilon}(t)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{a(t-\rho\epsilon)}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)d\rho\right|

(2.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\sqrt{a(t-\rho\epsilon)}-\sqrt{a(t)}\right|\phi^{\prime}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|a(t-\rho\epsilon)-a(t)|}{\sqrt{a(t-\rho\epsilon)}+\sqrt{a(t)}}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(a)\epsilon^{\alpha-1},

where
c(a)
given by (
2.23
) comes from the Hölder continuity of
a(t)
, and also using
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}=0.

Then using (
2.21
), and (
2.26
), we get

\|H^{-1}(t)H_{t}(t)\|\leq\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{4a_{0}}\epsilon^{\alpha-1}\lesssim\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{\alpha-1}.

(2)

We can estimate

\left|(\det H(t))^{-1}(\det H)_{t}(t)\right|=\left|\frac{\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\partial_{t}\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}\right|\leq\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}-1}\lesssim\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}-1}.

(3)

In this case, we calculate the matrix that we are interested in, that is,

H^{-1}AH-(H^{-1}AH)^{\ast}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}\left(\begin{matrix}0&-2a+(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1})^{2}+(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})^{2}\\
2a-(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1})^{2}-(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})^{2}&0\end{matrix}\right).

(2.27)

Since
(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1})^{2}=(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})^{2}
, and recalling inequality (
2.21
), we see that the desired norm can be estimated if we estimate the function
|a(t)-(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})^{2}|

A straightforward calculation using (
2

\displaystyle\left|a(t)-(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\sqrt{a(t)}-(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})\right)\left(\sqrt{a(t)}+(\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2})\right)\right|

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(a)\epsilon^{{\alpha}}\left|\sqrt{a(t)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{a(t-s)}\phi_{\epsilon}(s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c(a)\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\epsilon^{{\alpha}},

where

given by (
2.23
) comes from the Hölder continuity of
a(t)
.
Using (
2.28
), and (
2.21

\displaystyle\|H^{-1}AH-(H^{-1}AH)^{\ast}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2c(a)\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}}.

(4)

Next we estimate the term
\|H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}\|.

For that, we explicitly write the matrix we are interested in, that is

H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1}}\left(\begin{matrix}0&2(1-\beta^{2})(\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2}-2b\\
-2(1-\beta^{2})(\lambda_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2}+2b&0\end{matrix}\right).

(2.30)

Consequently, using (
2.21
), we get

\displaystyle\|H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\right)

(2.31)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

Combining estimates (1)-(4) above, we get the estimate for the derivative of the energy, that is

\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}\lesssim\left(2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+{\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}-1}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\epsilon^{{\alpha}}\right.\\

We choose

\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}\lesssim\left(-2K_{2}\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\langle\beta\rangle^{2(1-{\alpha})}+\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}\langle\beta\rangle^{2(1-{\alpha})}\right.\\
\quad+\left.\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\right)|W(t)|^{2}.

(2.33)

Since

, this implies

\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}\lesssim\left(-2K_{2}\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+\kappa\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\right)|W(t)|^{2},

(2.34)

where

\kappa=\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}+\frac{\|a\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{a_{0}}+\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

(2.35)

If we choose

and

\dfrac{1}{s}\geq 2

for all

and
\beta>0

\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}\leq 0.

(2.36)

Now by using monotonicity of
|W(t)|

\rho(0)=0

|W(t)|\leq\|H(0)\|^{-1}|\det H(0)||V(0)|.

(2.37)

Now from (
2.20
) and (

\displaystyle|V(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|H(t)\|\left|\det(H(t))\right|^{-1}e^{K_{2}t\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\left|W(t)\right|

(2.38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\|H(t)\|}{\|H(0)\|^{-1}\left|\det H(t)\right|^{-1}}\left|\det H(0)\right|e^{K_{2}t\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V(0)|.

Note that the definition of

and
(
2.21
)
imply

{\|H(t)\|}{\|H(0)\|^{-1}\left|\det H(t)\right|^{-1}}\left|\det H(0)\right|\lesssim\frac{\|\sqrt{a}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}=\frac{\|{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

(2.39)

Then (
2.38
) and (
2.39
) allow one to estimate

\displaystyle|V(t)|\lesssim\frac{\|{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}e^{K_{2}T\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V(0)|.

(2.40)

If we set,
C_{2}=C^{{}^{\prime}}\frac{\|{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}
, for some absolute constant
C^{{}^{\prime}}>0
, then by definition of
V(t)
, we obtain (
2.4
), that is,

|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\leq C_{2}e^{K_{2}T\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(|v_{0}|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}),

finishing the proof of Case 2.

Case 3:

a\in\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T]),
with
l\geq 2,

a(t)\geq 0.

Consider the quasi-symmetriser of
A(t),
that is, a family of coercive, Hermitian matrices of the form

Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t):=S(t)+\epsilon^{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}a(t)&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right),

(2.41)

for all
\epsilon\in(0,1],
so that
(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}A-A^{\ast}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon})

goes to zero as
\epsilon
goes to zero. The associated perturbed energy will be given by

E_{\epsilon}(t):=\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right).

We proceed by estimating this energy. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V_{t}(t),V(t)\right)+\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V_{t}(t)\right)

(2.42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\beta^{2}\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+

\displaystyle i\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)B(t)V(t),V(t)\right)-i\beta^{2}\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),A(t)V(t)\right)-

\displaystyle i\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),B(t)V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+i\beta^{2}\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)+

\displaystyle i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)B(t)-B^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right).

Now we will estimate the above terms as follows:

(1)

i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)\leq\epsilon\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right),

(2)

i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)B(t)-B^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)\leq\\
\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\left(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\epsilon^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)|V(t)|^{2}.

(2.43)

Indeed, we will deal with these two terms as follows:

(1)

Since
Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon^{2}{\tiny\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)}
, simple calculations will give

\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)=(a(t)+\epsilon^{2})|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}.

(2.44)

Now we have

\displaystyle i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\epsilon^{2}(v_{2}\bar{v_{1}}-v_{1}\bar{v_{2}})

(2.45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\epsilon^{2}2i\text{Im}(v_{2}\bar{v_{1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon 2|\epsilon v_{1}||v_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon(\epsilon^{2}|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon((a(t)+\epsilon^{2})|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}).

From (
2.44
) and (
2.45
), we get

\displaystyle i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)\leq\epsilon\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right).

(2)

Since
Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)B(t)-B^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)=\left((1-\beta^{2})(a(t)+\epsilon^{2})-b(t)\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)
, this implies

i\left((Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)B(t)-B^{\ast}(t)Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t))V(t),V(t)\right)\\
\ \leq\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\left(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\epsilon^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)|V(t)|^{2}.

(2.46)

Next we estimate the perturbed energy. From (
2.44
), and for
\epsilon\leq 1,
we get

\displaystyle\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a(t)+\epsilon^{2})|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}

(2.47)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1)(|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1)|V(t)|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a(t)+\epsilon^{2})|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}

(2.48)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}|v_{1}|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}|v_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}|v_{1}|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}|v_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}\epsilon^{2}|V(t)|^{2}.

From (
2.47
) and (
2.48
), we get

c^{-1}_{1}\epsilon^{2}|V(t)|^{2}\leq\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)\leq c_{1}|V(t)|^{2},

(2.49)

where
c_{1}=\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1.

Using the above estimates, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\epsilon\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+

(2.50)

\displaystyle\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\left(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\epsilon^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)|V(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\epsilon E_{\epsilon}(t)+

\displaystyle c_{1}\epsilon^{-2}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\left(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\epsilon^{2}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\right)E_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\epsilon E_{\epsilon}(t)+c_{1}\epsilon^{-2}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}E_{\epsilon}(t)+

\displaystyle c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}E_{\epsilon}(t)+c_{1}\epsilon^{-2}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}E_{\epsilon}(t),

which gives

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq\Bigg[\frac{\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)}{\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\epsilon+c_{1}\epsilon^{-2}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])})\Bigg.\\
\Bigg.+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\Bigg]E_{\epsilon}(t),

(2.51)

where
c_{1}=\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1.
We will use the following estimate:

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\left(\frac{d}{dt}Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)}{\left(Q^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(t)V(t),V(t)\right)}dt\leq c_{T}c_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}},

(2.52)

where

for some
c_{T}>0
, depending only on

, which we assume for a moment. From (
2.51
) and (
2.52
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{\epsilon}(t)

(2.53)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(c_{T}c_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}+\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\epsilon+c_{1}\epsilon^{-2}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])})+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\right)E_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\kappa E_{\epsilon}(t),

where

.

Choosing,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{2l}{\sigma}}e^{\left((c_{T}c_{1}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}+1)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{2}{\sigma}}+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2+\frac{2l}{\sigma}}(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])})+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\right)T}|V(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}\langle\beta\rangle^{4-\frac{4}{\sigma}}e^{\left((c_{T}c_{1}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}+1)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{2}{\sigma}}+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{6-\frac{4}{\sigma}}(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])})+c_{1}\langle\beta\rangle^{2}\right)T}|V(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}e^{\left(1+c_{1}\left(c_{T}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}+c_{1}^{-1}+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1\right)T\right)\langle\beta\rangle^{6-\frac{4}{\sigma}}}|V(0)|^{2}

.

where

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{a^{\prime}(t)|V_{1}(t)|^{2}}{(a(t)+\epsilon^{2})|V_{1}|^{2})^{1-\frac{1}{l}}|V(t)|^{\frac{2}{l}}}dt

\displaystyle c_{T}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}.

yielding (

) where

{}\left|\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1,\alpha}(t)+\sqrt{a(t)}\right|\leq c_{1}\epsilon^{\alpha},

{}\left|\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2,\alpha}(t)-\sqrt{a(t)}\right|\leq c_{2}\epsilon^{\alpha},

\displaystyle{}\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}

2.21

\left|a(t)-(\lambda_{i,\alpha}^{\epsilon})^{2}\right|\leq(2+\|\sqrt{a}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])})^{2}\epsilon^{2\alpha}.

{\tiny\frac{1}{\lambda^{\epsilon}_{2,\alpha}-\lambda^{\epsilon}_{1,\alpha}}\left(\begin{matrix}0&-2b+(1-\beta^{2})\left((\lambda_{1,\alpha}^{\epsilon})^{2}+(\lambda_{2,\alpha}^{\epsilon})^{2}\right)\\

\displaystyle 2\left(\left(+\sqrt{a}\ast\phi_{\epsilon}\right)(t)+2\epsilon^{\alpha}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\|H^{-1}BH-(H^{-1}BH)^{\ast}\|

{}\frac{d}{dt}|W(t)|^{2}\lesssim(2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\beta\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+2\|\sqrt{a}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])}\epsilon^{-1}+2\langle\beta\rangle^{2}(2+\|\sqrt{a}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}([0,T])})^{2}\epsilon^{\alpha}\\

By the standard methods of ordinary differential equations, Proposition
2.1
, has the following corollary for the corresponding inhomogeneous equations.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
T>0
and
\beta>0
be positive constants, let
b=b(t)\geq 0
, and
g(t)
be bounded real-valued functions in
L^{\infty}([0,T])
, and let
a\not\equiv 0
be a function satisfying one of the conditions of Proposition
2.1
.
Consider the following Cauchy problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w^{\prime\prime}(t)+\beta^{2}a(t)w(t)+b(t)w(t)=g(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
w(0)=w_{0}\in\mathbb{C},\\
w^{\prime}(0)=w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}.\end{array}\right.

(2.84)

Let
v(t)
be the solution of (
2.84
), with
g=0
, that is, it satisfies (
2.2
), and the respective estimates in Proposition
2.1
. Then we have the estimate

|w(t)|^{2}+|w^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\lesssim|v(t)|^{2}+|v^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+(1+\beta^{2})^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])},

(2.85)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, with the constant independent of
\beta
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we denote

W(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}iw(t)\\
\partial_{t}w(t)\end{matrix}\right),~~~~~W_{0}:=\left(\begin{matrix}i{w_{0}}\\
{w_{1}}\end{matrix}\right),

(2.86)

and we will use the matrices

A(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
a(t)&0\end{matrix}\right),~~~B(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1-\beta^{2}\\
b(t)&0\end{matrix}\right)~~{\textrm{ and }}~~~G(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
g(t)\end{matrix}\right).

(2.87)

In this notation (
2.84
), becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}W^{\prime}=i\beta^{2}A(t)W(t)+iB(t)W(t)+G(t),\\
W(0)=W_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.88)

Consequently, we consider the following two equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}V_{I}^{\prime}=i\left(\beta^{2}A(t)+B(t)\right)V_{I}(t),\\
V_{I}(0)=W_{0},\end{array}\right.

(2.89)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}V_{II}^{\prime}=i\left(\beta^{2}A(t)+B(t)\right)V_{II}(t)+G(t),\\
V_{II}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.90)

Then the solution of (
2.88
), is given by
W=V_{I}+V_{II}.

From the general theory of ordinary differential equations we can say that if

\Phi(t;s):=\left(\begin{matrix}\phi_{1}(t;s)\\
\phi_{2}(t;s)\end{matrix}\right),

(2.91)

is the solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi^{\prime}(t,s)=i\left(\beta^{2}A(t)+B(t)\right)\Phi(t,s),\quad s\leq t,\\
\Phi(s;s)=G(s),\end{array}\right.

(2.92)

then

\Phi(t,s)=e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}G(s).

Then the solution of (
2.90
) is given by

V_{II}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(t;s)ds
.
Consequently, we have

W(t)=V_{I}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}G(s)ds.

(2.93)

Here, on the right hand side, we will estimate the matrix term
e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}
. Since
A(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
a(t)&0\end{matrix}\right)
and
B(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1-\beta^{2}\\
b(t)&0\end{matrix}\right)
, this implies that

M(t):=\beta^{2}A(t)+B(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
\beta^{2}a(t)+b(t)&0\end{matrix}\right).

This gives
e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}=e^{i\tilde{M}}
where denoting
\tilde{M}:=\int_{s}^{t}M(\tau)d\tau
, we can abbreviate writing

\tilde{M}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\tau\\
m&0\end{matrix}\right),
where
\tau=t-s>0
and
m=\int\limits_{s}^{t}(\beta^{2}a(\tau)+b(\tau))d\tau\geq 0,
since
a(t),b(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
. For now we will consider the case
m\neq 0
. It can be readily seen from the matrix multiplication and induction that

\tilde{M}^{2k}=(\tau m)^{k}I

and

\tilde{M}^{2k+1}=(\tau m)^{k}\tilde{M},

for
k=0,1,2,....

Then we have

\displaystyle e^{i\tilde{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\tilde{M})^{n}}{n!}

(2.94)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\tilde{M})^{2k}}{(2k)!}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\tilde{M})^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{2k}(\tau m)^{k}}{(2k)!}I+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{2k+1}(\tau m)^{k}}{(2k+1)!}\tilde{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\sqrt{\tau m})^{2k}}{(2k)!}I+\frac{i}{\sqrt{\tau m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\sqrt{\tau m})^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\tilde{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\sqrt{\tau m})I+\frac{i}{\sqrt{\tau m}}\sin({\sqrt{\tau m}})\tilde{M}.

Simple calculations will give us

\|\tilde{M}\|\leq T(1+\beta^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}).

(2.95)

From (
2.94
) and (
2.95
), we have

\|e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}\|\leq 1+T(1+\beta^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}).

(2.96)

Now, when
m=0
, we have

\tilde{M}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\tau\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)
and
e^{i\tilde{M}}=I+i\tau\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right),

so that in this case we have

\|e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}\|\leq 1+T.

(2.97)

Thus from (
2.96
) and (
2.97
), we get

\|e^{i\int_{s}^{t}\left(\beta^{2}A(\tau)+B(\tau)\right)d\tau}\|\leq C(1+\beta^{2}),

(2.98)

where
C=\max\{1+T+T\|b\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])},T\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\}.
Combining (
2.93
) and (
2.98
), we get

\displaystyle|W(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|V_{I}(t)|^{2}+2C^{2}(1+\beta^{2})^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])}

(2.99)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|V_{I}(t)|^{2}+(1+\beta^{2})^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])},

for all
t\in[0,T]
, with the constant independent of
\beta
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Proof of the main result

We now proceed to prove Theorem
1.1
. We first recall a few facts concerning the Fourier analysis on the lattice
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
on the lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the space of rapidly decreasing functions
u:\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, that is,
u\in\mathcal{S}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
if for any
L<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{u,L,\hbar}
such
that

|u(k)|\leq C_{u,L,\hbar}(1+|k|)^{-L},\quad\text{ for all }k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},

(3.1)

where
|k|=\hbar\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}k^{2}_{j}\right)^{1/2}
. The Fourier transform
\widehat{u}
of
u:\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined as

\widehat{u}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)e^{-2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta},~~~\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{n},~k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(3.2)

The Plancherel formula takes the form

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left|\widehat{u}(\theta)\right|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\widehat{u}(\theta)\overline{\widehat{u}(\theta)}d\theta

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{h}k\cdot\theta}\sum_{l\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\overline{u(l)}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{h}l\cdot\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left|u(k)\right|^{2}.

The scalar product in the Hilbert space
{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
is given by

(u,v)_{l^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)\overline{v(k)}.

We note that the symbol of
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}
defined by

\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)=e^{-2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}(e^{2\pi\frac{i}{\hbar}k\cdot\theta})

is given by

\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(e^{2\pi i\theta_{j}}+e^{-2\pi i\theta_{j}}\right)-2n=2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cos(2\pi\theta_{j})-2n,

(3.4)

with
(k,\theta)\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.
We also note that it is independent of
k
and
\hbar
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Our aim is to reduce the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) to a form
allowing us to apply Corollary
2.2
. In order to do this, we take the Fourier
transform of (
1.1

(3.5)

Formally recalling the notation used in Proposition
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
, we write

v(t):=\widehat{u}(t,\theta),\quad\beta^{2}:=-\hbar^{-2}\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)=\hbar^{-2}\left(2n-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cos(2\pi\theta_{j})\right),

(3.6)

as well as

v_{0}:=\widehat{u}_{0}(\theta),\quad v_{1}:=\widehat{u}_{1}(\theta),\quad g(t):=\widehat{f}(t,\theta).

(3.7)

Therefore, equation (

) becomes

v^{\prime\prime}(t)+\beta^{2}a(t)v(t)+b(t)v(t)=g(t),\quad t\in[0,T]

with

and all other functions depending on

as a parameter.

2.2

(3.8)

Recalling the notation (
3.6

3.7

(3.9)

.

Case 2:

4.
Limit as
\hbar\to 0

In this section, we compare the solutions of (

) on

) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Consider two Cauchy problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}u(t,k)-a(t)\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}u(t,k)+b(t)u(t,k)=g(t,k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\

\partial_{t}u(0,k)=u_{1}(k),\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}v(t,x)-a(t)\mathcal{L}v(t,x)+b(t)v(t,x)=g(t,x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\\
\partial_{t}v(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
From the equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
denoting
w:=u-v
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}w(t,k)-a(t)\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}w(t,k)+b(t)w(t,k)=a(t)\left(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L}\right)v(t,k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
w(0,k)=0,\\
\partial_{t}w(0,k)=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

Our aim is to reduce the Cauchy problem (
4.3
) to a form
allowing us to apply Corollary
2.2
. In order to do this, we take the Fourier
transform of (
4.3
) with respect to
k\in{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. This gives

\partial^{2}_{t}\widehat{w}(t,\theta)-\hbar^{-2}a(t)\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)\widehat{w}(t,\theta)+b(t)\widehat{w}(t,\theta)=\widehat{f}(t,\theta),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{n},

(4.4)

where
f(t,k):=a(t)\left(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L}\right)v(t,k)
, and
\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta)=\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(\theta)
is independent of
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Since
w_{0}=w_{1}=0
, from Proposition
2.1
, it follows that the solution of corresponding homogeneous equation of (
4.3
) is identically zero. Now applying Corollary
2.2
in (
4.4
), we get

|\widehat{w}(t,\theta)|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\widehat{w}(t,\theta)|^{2}\lesssim(1-\hbar^{-2}\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}}(k,\theta))^{2}\|\widehat{f}(\cdot,\theta)\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])}.

(4.5)

Similar to the proof of (
1.6
), by recalling Plancherel formula (
3.3
) and Fourier transform, we have

\left\|w(t)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\leq C\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)a(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L})v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\\
\leq C\left\|a\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L})v(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

(4.6)

where
C>0
is independent of
\hbar
.

Now we will estimate the term
\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L})v(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
.
Let
\phi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then by Taylor’s theorem with the Lagrange’s form of the remainder, we have

\phi(\xi+\mathbf{h})=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 3}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\phi(\xi)}{\alpha!}\mathbf{h}^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|=4}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\phi(\xi+\theta_{\xi}\mathbf{h})}{\alpha!}\mathbf{h}^{\alpha},

(4.7)

for some
\theta_{\xi}\in(0,1)
depending on
\xi
. Let
v_{j}
be the
j^{th}
basis vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, having all zeros except for
1
as the
j^{th}
component and then by taking
\mathbf{h}=v_{j}
and
-v_{j}
in (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\phi(\xi+v_{j})=\phi(\xi)+\phi^{(v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2!}\phi^{(2v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{3!}\phi^{(3v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{4!}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi+\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j}),

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle\phi(\xi-v_{j})=\phi(\xi)-\phi^{(v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2!}\phi^{(2v_{j})}(\xi)-\frac{1}{3!}\phi^{(3v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{4!}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi-\tilde{\theta}_{j,\xi}v_{j}),

(4.9)

for some
\theta_{j,\xi},\tilde{\theta}_{j,\xi}\in(0,1)
. Using (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we have

\phi(\xi+v_{j})+\phi(\xi-v_{j})-2\phi(\xi)=\phi^{(2v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi+\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j})+\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi-\tilde{\theta}_{j,\xi}v_{j})\right).

Since
\delta_{\xi_{j}}^{2}\phi(\xi)=\phi(\xi+v_{j})+\phi(\xi-v_{j})-2\phi(\xi)
, where
\delta_{\xi_{j}}\phi(\xi):=\phi(\xi+\frac{1}{2}v_{j})-\phi(\xi-\frac{1}{2}v_{j}),
is the usual central difference operator, it follows that

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi_{j}}^{2}\phi(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{(2v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi+\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j})+\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi-\tilde{\theta}_{j,\xi}v_{j})\right).

(4.10)

Now by adding all the above
n
-equations for
j=1,\dots,n
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{j}}^{2}\phi(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\phi^{(2v_{j})}(\xi)+\frac{1}{4!}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi+\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j})+\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi-\tilde{\theta}_{j,\xi}v_{j})\right).

(4.11)

Let us define a function
V_{\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by
V_{\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi(\xi):=\phi(\xi-\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j}),
then we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{j}}^{2}\phi(\xi)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi_{j}^{2}}\phi(\xi)=\frac{1}{4!}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(V_{-\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi)+V_{\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\xi)\right).

(4.12)

Now we extend this to
\hbar{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Consider a function
\phi_{\hbar}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\phi_{\hbar}(\xi):=\phi(\hbar\xi)
. Clearly
\phi_{\hbar}\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if we take
\phi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\phi_{\hbar}(\xi)-\mathcal{L}\phi_{\hbar}(\xi)=\frac{1}{4!}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(V_{-\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi_{\hbar}^{(4v_{j})}(\xi)+V_{\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}\phi_{\hbar}^{(4v_{j})}(\xi)\right),

(4.13)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is the discrete difference Laplacian on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. One can quickly notice that

V_{-\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi_{\hbar}^{(4v_{j})}(\xi)=\phi_{\hbar}^{(4v_{j})}(\xi+\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j})=\hbar^{4}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi+\hbar\theta_{j,\xi}v_{j})=\hbar^{4}V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi).

(4.14)

Therefore, the equation (
4.13
) becomes

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\hbar^{2}\mathcal{L}\right)\phi(\hbar\xi)=\frac{\hbar^{4}}{4!}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi)+V_{\hbar\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi)\right),

(4.15)

whence we get

\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)\left(\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\hbar^{2}\mathcal{L}\right)\phi(\hbar\xi)=\frac{\hbar^{4}}{4!}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi)\right.+\\
\left.\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{\hbar\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}(\hbar\xi)\right).

(4.16)

Hence, it follows that

\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)(\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}-\mathcal{L})\phi\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\hbar^{4}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right.\\
+\left.\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{\hbar\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}\phi^{(4v_{j})}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right].

(4.17)

Since in Case 1,
v(t,\cdot)\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, by Sobolev embedding theorem
\left(\right.
see e.g.
[
20
, Excercise 2.6.17]
\left.\right)
, we have

s>k+\frac{n}{2}\implies H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subseteq C^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(4.18)

Thus for
s>3+\dfrac{n}{2},
we have
H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subseteq C^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and if
v(t,\cdot)\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then
v^{(4v_{j})}(t,\cdot)\in H^{s-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subseteq H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
whenever
s\geq 5
, which gives

\lim\limits_{\hbar\to 0}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}v^{(4v_{j})}(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\left\|(I-\mathcal{L})v^{(4v_{j})}(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

(4.19)

Combining the above assumptions, we can choose
s\geq 5
for
n\leq 3
and
s>3+\frac{n}{2}
for
n\geq 4
. On the other hand for Case 2, 3 and 4, we have
v(t,\cdot)\in\gamma^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, under the assumptions (
1.3
)-(
1.5
) and
p\geq 5
. Now from (
4.17
), it follows that

\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)(\mathcal{L}-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})v(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\lesssim\\
\hbar^{4}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{-\hbar\theta_{j}v_{j}}v^{(4v_{j})}(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\right.+\\
\left.\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\|\left(I-\hbar^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)V_{\hbar\tilde{\theta}_{j}v_{j}}v^{(4v_{j})}(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\right].

(4.20)

Using (
4.6
), (
4.19
) and (
4.20
), we get
\left\|w(t)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}+\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|^{2}_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\to 0
as
\hbar\to 0.
Hence
\left\|w(t)\right\|_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\to 0
and
\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|_{{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}}\to 0
as
\hbar\to 0
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
. ∎

Ulrich bundles of arbitrary rank on Segre-Veronese varieties
F. Malaspina

Abstract
. We generalize the results by Eisenbud and Schreyer about Ulrich bundles over Veronese varieties to Segre-Veronese varieties. We discuss the range where we have natural cohomology and we construct multigraded resolutions and monads for Ulrich bundles of any rank. Moreover we give cohomological characterizations for significant families of bundles.

Primary: 14J60; Secondary: 13C14, 14F05

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the
N
-dimensional projective space over an
algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. If
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a smooth closed submanifold of dimension
n\geq 1
we set
\cal{O}_{X}(h):=\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)\otimes\cal{O}_{X}
.

Among vector bundles

\cal{V}
on such an
X
, the simplest ones from the cohomological point of view satisfy the vanishing

H^{i}\big(X,\cal{V}(th)\big)=0,\qquad\forall\ i=1,\dots,n-1,\ t\in\mathbb Z.

Such vector bundles are called arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (aCM for
short).

There has been increasing interest on the classification of aCM bundles on various projective varieties, which is important in a sense that the aCM bundles are considered to give a measurement of complexity of the underlying space. Moreover the aCM bundles are a crucial ingredient for the study of any bundle on
X
as it is showed in
[
16
]
. A special type of aCM bundles, called the Ulrich bundles, are the ones achieving the maximum possible minimal number of generators given by
\deg(X)rank(\cal{V})
. These bundles, originally studied for computing Chow forms,
conjecturally exist over any variety (see
[
10
]
) and they
are characterized by the linearity of the minimal graded free resolution
over the polynomial ring of their module of global sections. Many recent papers are devoted to the study of Ulrich bundles over significant varieties (see
[
3
,
1
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
).

In
[
9
]
the authors give families of arbitrary large dimensions over Segre varieties (except
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
). In
[
11
]
it is shown that every Ulrich bundle over a rational normal scroll of dimension two is obtained as an extension between two direct sums of line bundles and, in case of quartic scrolls, all the aCM bundles are classified. In
[
2
]
are classified Ulrich vector bundles of arbitrary rank on smooth projective varieties of minimal degree of any dimension. They are characterized as the bundles admitting a special type of filtration. A consequence of this result is that the moduli spaces of Ulrich bundles are zero-dimensional. The case
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is very peculiar, in fact there exists only a finite number of aCM bundles which are not Ulrich; in
[
12
]
all the aCM bundles are classified. The case of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
is much more complicated and has been studied in
[
15
]
. The bigraded resolutions
of Ulrich bundles of arbitrary rank are constructed. Cohomological characterizations of the Ulrich bundles
\cal{V}
with
h^{1}(\cal{V}\otimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})=0
, or with
h^{2}(\cal{V}\otimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1))=0
, or obtained as pullback from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, are proved. The case of Veronese varieties has been studied in
[
10
]
. The authors show that every Ulrich bundle has natural cohomology and can be obtained from a graded resolution.

The aim of this article is to generalize the cohomological characterizations of Ulrich bundles
from
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
to Segre Veronese varieties
\Sigma
obtained by embedding
\mathbb P^{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb P^{n_{s}}
with
\cal{O}(k_{1},\dots,k_{s})
(
s>0,k_{1},\dots,k_{s}\geq 1)
. More precisely we show that every Ulrich bundle
\cal{V}
on
\Sigma
is regular according to both the two different notions of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity given in
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
, moreover it has natural cohomology (as in
[
10
]
on Veronese varieties) in a suitable range. Then we compute the cohomology of
\cal{V}
tensored with the pullbacks of the
i
bundles with suitable twists.
We introduce the following notation:

\alpha_{i}^{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}}=h^{i}(\cal{V}(-ih)\otimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}^{a_{s}}(a_{s}))

where
0\leq a_{i}\leq n_{i}
and we show that

h^{k}(\cal{V}(-ih)\otimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}^{a_{s}}(a_{s}))=0

if
k\neq i
.

Thanks to a Beilinson type spectral sequence constructed with suitable exceptional collections we prove that, for
q=0,\dots,n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
,

\cal{V}(-qh)
is the homology of the monad

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{B}_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\oplus_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=q}\cal{O}(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{s})^{\oplus\alpha_{q}^{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{B}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

where
\cal{B}_{1}
is defined by the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}(-n_{1},\dots,-n_{s})^{\oplus\alpha_{q}^{n_{1},\dots,n_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\dots\xrightarrow{\ \ }\oplus_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=q+1}\cal{O}(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{s})^{\oplus\alpha_{q}^{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{B}_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

and
\cal{B}_{2}
is defined by the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{B}_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\oplus_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=q-1}\cal{O}(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{s})^{\oplus\alpha_{q}^{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\dots\xrightarrow{\ \ }\oplus_{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=1}\cal{O}(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{s})^{\oplus\alpha_{q}^{a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Notice that for
q=0,q=1
and
q=n_{1}+\dots+n_{1}
we obtain a multigraded resolution.

Here we summarize the structure of this article. In section
2
we make the cohomological computations related to an Ulrich bundle and we discuss the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and the range where we have natural cohomology. In section
3
we prove the main results for the case of biprojective spaces. In section
4
we give cohomological characterizations for significant families of Ulrich bundles. In section
5
we deal with the general case.

2.
Cohomology of Ulrich bundles on Segre Veronese varieties

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{n_{1},\dots,n_{s}}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{s}}
be the Segre-Veronese variety obtained by embedding
X=\mathbb P^{n_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb P^{n_{s}}
with
\cal{O}(k_{1},\dots,k_{s})
(
s>0,k_{1},\dots,k_{s}\geq 1)
. Let
d=n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
and
h=k_{1}h_{1}+\dots+k_{s}h_{s}
. We have

\deg(\Sigma_{n_{1},\dots,n_{s}}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{s}})=(k_{1}h_{1}+\dots+k_{s}h_{s})^{s}=k_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots k_{s}^{n_{s}}\deg(\Sigma_{n_{1},\dots,n_{s}})=k_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots k_{s}^{n_{s}}\frac{d!}{\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}(n_{i})!}.

Moreover we have

\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\rangle}{h_{1}^{n_{1}+1},\dots,h_{s}^{n_{s}+1}}

and

\omega_{X}\cong\cal{O}_{X}(-(n_{1}+1)h_{1}-\dots-(n_{s}+1)h_{s}).

We will simply denote
\cal{O}_{X}(a_{1}h_{1}+\dots+a_{s}h_{s})
by
\cal{O}_{X}(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})
.

Let us denote by
{\displaystyle e^{r}_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{r}+1\\
j\end{array}\right)}
for
r=1,\dots,s
.

We will often use the following exact sequences obtained as pullbacks of Koszul sequences from
\mathbb P^{n_{r}}
:

(1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}(0,\dots,-n_{r}-1,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{n_{r}}}(0,\dots,-n_{r},\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{1}}(0\dots,-1,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

and for
a_{r}=1,\dots,n_{r}-1
,
l=1,\dots,s

(2)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}(0,\dots,a_{r}-n_{r}-1,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{n_{r}}}(0,\dots,a_{r}-n,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cdots

\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{a_{r}+1}}(0,\dots,-1,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}}^{a_{r}}(a_{r})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

(3)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{r}}}^{a_{r}}(a_{r})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{{a_{r}}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cdots

\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}^{e^{r}_{1}}(0,\dots,a_{r}-1,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}(0,\dots,a_{r},\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

(4)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}(a_{l}-n_{l}-1,0,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}^{e^{l}_{n_{l}}}(a_{l}-n,0,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cdots

\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}^{e^{l}_{a_{l}+1}}(-1,0,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l}}}^{a_{l}}(a_{l})\boxtimes\cal{O}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

(5)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l}}}^{a_{l}}(a_{l})\boxtimes\cal{O}\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}^{e^{l}_{{a_{l}}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cdots

\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}^{e^{l}_{1}}(a_{l}-1,0,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}^{a_{1}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{l-1}}}^{a_{l-1}}(a_{l-1})\boxtimes\cal{O}(a_{r},0,\dots,0)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

We will use the following definition of Ulrich bundles (see
[
10
]
):

Definition 2.1
.

A bundle
\cal{V}
on
\Sigma
is Ulrich if
H^{i}(\cal{V}(jh))=0
for any
i
and
j=-1,\dots,-d

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\cal{V}
be an Ulrich bundle on
\Sigma
.

(i)

For any
i>0
,
H^{i}(\cal{V}(-ih)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
.

(ii)

For any
i<d
,
H^{i}(\cal{V}(-(i+1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\leq 0
.

Proof.

H^{i}(\cal{V}(jh))=0
for any
i
and
j=-1,\dots,-d
since
\cal{V}
is Ulrich.

Since
H^{d}(\cal{V}(-dh))=0
,
H^{d}(\cal{V}(-dh)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
.

Let assume
(i)
for any index
i\geq i_{0}>1
. We prove
(a)
for
i_{0}-1
.

From (
1
) tensored by
\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,1,\dots,0)
since for any
k\geq 0

H^{i_{0}-1+k}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,-k,\dots,0))=H^{i_{0}-1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h))=0,

we get

H^{i_{0}-1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,1,\dots,0))=0.

From (
1
) tensored by
\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{r}+1,\dots,j_{s})
with
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
since for any
k\geq 0

H^{i_{0}-1+k}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{r}-k,\dots,j_{s}))=0,

we get

H^{i_{0}-1}(H^{i}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0

if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
. So
(i)
is proved.

Since
H^{0}(\cal{V}(-h))=0
,
H^{0}(\cal{V}(-h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\leq 0
.

Let assume
(ii)
for an index
i\leq i_{0}<d-1
. We prove
(ii)
for
i_{0}+1
.

From (
3
) tensored by
\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,n_{r},\dots,0)
since for any
k\geq 0

H^{i_{0}+1-k}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,k,\dots,0))=H^{i_{0}+1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O})=0,

we get

H^{i_{0}+1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,-1,\dots,0))=0.

From (
3
) tensored by
\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,n_{r}+j_{r},\dots,j_{s})
with
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\leq 0
, since for any
k\geq 0

H^{i_{0}+1-k}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{l}+k,\dots,j_{s}))=0,

we get

H^{i_{0}+1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}+2)h)\otimes\cal{O}(j_{1},\dots,j_{s}))=0

when
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\leq 0
and
(ii)
is proved.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

In particular an Ulrich bundle on
\Sigma
satisfies the two different notions of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
given in
[
4
]
and
[

]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\cal{V}
be an Ulrich bundle on
\Sigma
. Let

be integers

and

.

(a)

For any
i>0
,
H^{i}(\cal{V}(-ih)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(j_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{r}}}^{a_{r}}(a_{r}+1+j_{r})\dots\boxtimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}(j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
.

(b)

For any
i<d
,
H^{i}(\cal{V}(-(i+1)h)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(j_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{r}}}^{a_{r}}(a_{r}+j_{r})\dots\boxtimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{s}}}(j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\leq 0
.

(c)

For any
i>0
,
H^{i}(\cal{V}(-ih)\otimes{}^{a_{1}}(a_{1}+1+j_{1})\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes{}^{a_{l}}(a_{l}+1+j_{l})\boxtimes\cal{O}(j_{l+1},\dots,j_{s}))=0
if
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\geq 0
.

(d)

For any
i<d

if

(a)

2
) tensored by

for

, we get

when

.

(a)
for any index

. We prove
(a)
for

.

From (
2
) tensored by

, since for any

H^{i_{0}-1+k}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h)\otimes\cal{O}(0,\dots,-k,\dots,0))=H^{i_{0}-1}(\cal{V}(-(i_{0}-1)h))=0,

) tensored by

, from (
3
) tensored by

a=h^{1}(\cal{V}\otimes\cal{O}(-2k_{1},-1-k_{2}))=0
we obtain
\cal{V}(-h)=\cal{O}(k_{1}-1,-1)^{\oplus b}\oplus\cal{O}(-1,0)^{\oplus c}

. Similarly if

we get
\cal{V}=\cal{O}(k_{1}-1,2k_{2}-1)
.
∎

.

\cal{V}

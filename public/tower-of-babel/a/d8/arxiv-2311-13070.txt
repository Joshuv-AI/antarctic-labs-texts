Congruence modules in higher codimension and zeta lines in Galois cohomology
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Chandrashekhar B. Khare, Jeffrey Manning, Eric Urban

Abstract
. This work builds on that in [ 8 ] where a notion of congruence modules in higher codimension is introduced. The main new results are a criterion for detecting regularity of local rings in terms of congruence modules, and a more refined version of a result tracking the change of congruence modules under deformation is proved. Number theoretic applications include the construction of canonical lines in certain Galois cohomology groups arising from adjoint motives of Hilbert modular forms.

11F80 (primary); 13C10, 13D02 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number,
\mathcal{O}
the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and
R
a complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra morphism
\lambda\colon R\to\mathcal{O}
such that the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)
, is regular.

In
[
8
]
we develop an analog of the Wiles-Lenstra-Diamond numerical criterion in arbitrary codimension, with the original criterion (see
[
4
,
15
]
) corresponding to the codimension 0 case. This gives a criterion for finitely generated
R
-module
M
to have a free direct summand and for
R
to be a complete intersection ring. This involves two invariants associated to
\lambda
: the torsion
\Phi_{\lambda}(R)
of the cotangent space of
\lambda
, and the congruence module
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
. The codimension
c
of the augmentation
\lambda
is the height of
\mathfrak{p}
, or equivalently under our regularity assumption, the dimension of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We denote the category of such pairs
(R,\lambda)
by
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
. The earlier work of
[
4
]
corresponds to the case

in which case
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}=\Phi_{\lambda}(R)
.

Wiles uses the criterion in his work on the modularity of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
to go from modularity lifting theorems in the minimal case to those in the non-minimal case. In
[
8
]
the numerical criterion in higher codimension is used along the same lines to prove integral modularity lifting results for non-minimal lifts in situations of positive defect which arise in considering Galois representations over imaginary quadratic fields.

In this work we explore the meaning of the invariants
\Phi_{\lambda}(R)
and
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
in certain number theoretic situations, relating them to the index of zeta elements in global Galois cohomology groups.

To begin with we focus on one of the key ideas of
[
8
]
, namely the definition and properties of congruence modules, and congruence ideals, associated to an augmentation
\lambda
in arbitrary codimension. This is the content of Section
2
. The highlights are a characterisation, Theorem
2.6
, of regularity of rings
(A,\lambda)
in our category
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
in terms of vanishing of the invariants
\Phi_{\lambda}(A)
and
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
); a more transparent description, in §
2.10
, of the connection between duality and our congruence modules than in the earlier paper, and a refinement, Theorem
2.32
, of a result about deformation invariance of Wiles defect.

Section
3
focuses on number theoretic applications, and concerns the congruence ideal attached to an augmentation of Hida’s ordinary Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}^{\rm ord}
, or ordinary deformation ring
R^{\rm ord}
, arising from cohomological Hilbert modular forms
f
over totally real fields
F
. The functorial properties of the congruence ideal are used to relate it to lines (that is to say, free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank one) in the Galois cohomology with coefficients in
\operatorname{ad}\rho_{f}
; see Theorem
3.7
. The index of their image under global-to-local restriction maps to singular local Galois cohomology
H^{1}_{{\rm ord}/f}(G_{p},\operatorname{ad}\rho_{f})
is related to classical congruence module of
f
, and to special values of adjoint
L
-function

L^{\rm alg}(1,\operatorname{ad}\rho_{f})\,,

by work of Hida
[
7
]
and its generalization by Dimitrov
[
5
]
. This is connected to the “zeta elements” of
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
. The terminology is due to Kato
[
9
]
, who used it for elements of Galois cohomology he constructs in a related context, arising from Beilinson-Flach classes. In
[
14
]
exact sequences of Selmer groups are used while here we use in addition our congruence modules in higher codimension, allowing one to eliminate some hypotheses which arise from relying on
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems.

This connection arises from the following circumstance. Let
\boldsymbol{t}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}t_{1},\dots,t_{c}
be indeterminates,
\Lambda_{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}[\![{\boldsymbol{t}}]\!]
the power-series ring, and

the natural augmentation. Fix
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
equipped with a finite flat map

of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras over
\mathcal{O}
, so that composite map

is the augmentation. For

one has a map

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)(=A_{0}[\ker\lambda])\to\bigwedge^{c}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2},\mathcal{O})\,,

whose cokernel is

. Abstractly both domain and range are simply
\mathcal{O}
’s. In applications when

is a nearly ordinary deformation ring and

arises from the classical form

, the range is a Selmer group as

, with local conditions

such that

for

and

is the unramified subspace

. When

is a nearly ordinary Hecke algebra

.

2.1

For any finitely generated

We say an

Theorem 2.2
.

With
\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
as above, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The local ring
A
is regular at
\lambda
.

(2)

The rank of the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}_{\lambda}
is
0pt\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
.

(3)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
is torsion.

(4)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
is torsion for each finitely generated
A
-module

.

Moreover, when these conditions hold the
\mathcal{O}
-module

is cyclic. ∎

Condition (2) is that the embedding dimension of the ring

equals its Krull dimension, so (1)
\Leftrightarrow

[

]
; see also
[

]

2.3
.

A noetherian local ring

is regular if and only if the map

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(k,R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(k,k)

induced by the canonical surjection
R\to k
to the residue field of
R
, is nonzero. When this is the case, the map above is nonzero in (upper) degree
\dim R
.

The result below is implicit in the proof of (1)
\Rightarrow
(4) in Theorem
2.2
, in
[
8
]
. We make it explicit, for it is used also in proving Lemma

and Theorem

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of noetherian rings such that the ideal
\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon)
is generated by a regular sequence, and set
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R-\dim S
. For any
R
-module
M
the map below is bijective:

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{R}(S,\varepsilon\otimes_{R}M)\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{R}(S,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{R}(S,S\otimes_{R}M)\,.

Proof.

The Koszul complex, say
K
, on any regular sequence generating
\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon)
is a minimal resolution of
S
as an
R
-module. The map
\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{R}(S,\varepsilon\otimes_{R}M)
is the one obtained in cohomology in (upper) degree
c
from the morphism in the upper row of the following commutative diagram of complexes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,S\otimes_{R}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,R)\otimes_{R}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,R)\otimes_{R}(S\otimes_{R}M)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,\varepsilon\otimes M)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(K,R)\otimes(\varepsilon\otimes M)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

The vertical maps are isomorphisms because

is a finite free
R
-complex. It is clear that the map in the lower row induces a bijection in cohomology in the top degree,

. Thus the same holds for the one in the upper row, as claimed.
∎

We denote
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
the category whose objects are pairs
(A,\lambda)
satisfying the equivalent conditions in Theorem

. A morphism
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda)\to(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
in this category is a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

over
\mathcal{O}
; that is to say, with

. We write
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
for the subcategory of

consists of pairs

such that

.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
and finitely generated

-module

the map

\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\lambda\otimes_{A}M)\colon\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)

is one-to-one.

Proof.

Set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
and let
\varepsilon\colon R\to E
be the map obtained by localizing

at
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
; here

is the residue field of
R
, which is also the field of fractions of
\mathcal{O}
. Since injectivity of a map of torsion-free
\mathcal{O}
-modules can be detected after passing to the field of fractions, it suffices to check that the map

\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\lambda\otimes_{A}M)_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{c}(E,\varepsilon\otimes_{R}M)

is one-to-one. Since
R
is regular the ideal
\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon)
is generated by a regular sequence of length

; see
[
2
, Proposition 2.2.4]
. It remains to apply Lemma
2.4
.
∎

In the work of Hida
[
7
]
and Ribet
[
12
]
congruence modules (for codimension
c=0
) are attached to augmentations
\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{O}
of Hecke algebras
\mathbb{T}
that act faithfully on certain localized Betti cohomology groups

. They measure the complexity of
\mathbb{T}
and their vanishing is equivalent to
\mathbb{T}
being smooth, namely just
\mathcal{O}
. Analogously we show in the result below that for rings

the vanishing of either the congruence module
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
or
\Phi_{\lambda}(A)
, the torsion part of the cotangent module, at any augmentation
\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
implies
A
is smooth.

Unlike most results in
[
8
, Part 1]
, the following theorem does not make assumptions on the depth of the ring

.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
, the local ring
A
is regular if and only if
\Phi_{\lambda}(A)=0
, if and only if
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)=0
.

Proof.

We first verify that
A
is regular if and only if
\Phi_{\lambda}(A)=0
. As
A
is a complete
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, one has
A\cong P/I
where
P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}[\![{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}}]\!]
, a ring of formal power series over
\mathcal{O}
, the ideal
I\subseteq(\varpi)(\boldsymbol{t})+(\boldsymbol{t})^{2}
, and

\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
is quotient by
(\boldsymbol{t})
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
be a minimal generating set for

. The cotangent module
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2}
depends only on

and the linear part of the

, in the following sense: By our assumption on

, each

has an unique expression of the form

(2.7)

f_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{u_{ij}}t_{j}+g_{i}\qquad\text{with $u_{ij}\in(\varpi)\mathcal{O}$ and $g_{i}\in(\boldsymbol{t})^{2}$.}

Then one has a presentation

\mathcal{O}^{m}\xrightarrow{\ (u_{ij})\ }\mathcal{O}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2}\longrightarrow 0\,.

The torsion part of
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2}
is zero if and only if
(u_{ij})=0
, that is to say,
(\boldsymbol{f})\subseteq(\boldsymbol{t})^{2}
. Since
A
is regular at
\lambda
this condition is equivalent to

, as desired.

Next we verify the claim that
A
is regular if and only if
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)=0
.

When
A
is regular,
\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O})
is generated by a regular sequence of length
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0pt(\operatorname{Ker}\lambda)
; see
[
2
, Proposition 2.2.4]
. Thus Lemma

yields that the map
\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\lambda)
is one-to-one so
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)=0
.

Assume
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)=0
. To verify that
A
is regular it suffices to verify that the map

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}(k,\varepsilon)\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{A}(k,A)\to\operatorname{Ext}_{A}(k,k)\,,

induced by the quotient map
\varepsilon\colon A\to k
, is non-zero, for then Lescot’s result
2.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module. The exact sequence

(2.8)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\ \varpi\ }\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow 0

of

-modules induces exact sequences of

0\longrightarrow k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(\mathcal{O},M)\xrightarrow{\ \eth^{i+1}(M)\ }\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(k,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i+1}(\mathcal{O},M)[\varpi]\longrightarrow 0

For what follows the relevant point is that the maps

are inclusions. Set

and consider the following commutative diagram of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c+1}_{A}(k,A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c+1}_{A}(k,\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c+1}_{A}(k,k)}

It is easy to verify that the hypothesis

∎

Remark 2.9
.

Consider the ordinary Hida Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}
of tame level
N
. It is finite flat over
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}[\![{t}]\!]
, with
t
the weight variable, and
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}/(t)=\mathbb{T}
is a classical Hecke algebra, acting faithfully on
H^{1}(X_{1}(Np),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Consider an augmentation
\lambda\colon\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}\to\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{O}
arising from a (
p
-stabilized) newform
f\in S_{1}(\Gamma_{1}(Np^{r}))
. The vanishing of the congruence module of
\mathbb{T}
for the augmentation
\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{O}
implies
\mathbb{T}=\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}=\Lambda
while the vanishing of the congruence module for
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}\to\mathcal{O}
implies that
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}
is smooth, while
\mathbb{T}
may not be smooth. In other words
\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}})=0
implies
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ord}}=\mathcal{O}[\![{x}]\!]
, furthermore
x
can be taken to be the weight variable
t
if and only if the classical congruence module
\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})=0
also vanishes.

Next we describe a pairing associated with the definition of congruence modules. This too appears in
[
8
]
, but does not play a major role in the development there. The presentation below is more transparent, and is used to give another perspective on some of the subsequent results.

2.10
.

For any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, one has natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}

\displaystyle\cong{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}{(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(M,\mathcal{O}),{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}})\,.

Thus, the map

\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\lambda\otimes M)\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}

whose cokernel is the congruence module of
M
, is adjoint to the map

\langle-,-\rangle_{M}\colon{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\,.

The
congruence ideal
of
M
, with respect to the augmentation
\lambda
, is the image of this pairing:

\eta_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Image}\langle-,-\rangle_{M}\,.

Localizing at
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
, it is easy to verify that the free
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(M)
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})
have the same rank and that
\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
has rank one, so

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M))\leq\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}_{\lambda}(M)\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M))\,.

Here
\operatorname{rank}_{\lambda}(M)
denotes the rank of
M
at
\lambda
, that is to say, the rank of the
A_{\lambda}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
. In particular, when this rank equals
1
, the length of the congruence module can be computed from the pairing.

The pairing above is induced–by passage to torsion-free quotients–by the natural pairing given by composition of morphisms:

(2.11)

\langle-,-\rangle_{M}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\,.

Namely,
\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)
can be realized as
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O},M[c])
, the morphisms in the derived category of
A
from
\mathcal{O}
to
M[c]
, and given such a morphism
f
and a map
g\colon M\to\mathcal{O}
, the pairing above is

\langle f,g\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g\circ f\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}[c]\,.

In terms of the Yoneda interpretation of

as equivalence classes

of exact sequences, the pairing is given by taking push-out along

.

Cohen-Macaulay modules

When

, local duality yields an identification

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M^{\vee})\quad\text{where $M^{\vee}\cong H_{c+1}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,\omega_{A}))$}

Here

; see

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M^{\vee})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\,.

See
[

The pairing (

Consider the exact sequence

Applying

Lemma 2.14
.

For any

, and any

-module

-modules

The isomorphism above assigns to any

) along

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The case

Since

\displaystyle M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}

The case

With this description, for

is torsion-free, as in the case

is just the image of the pairing above.

Structure of
\operatorname{F}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O})

A key input in the development of the commutative algebraic properties of the congruence module is a structure theorem for
\operatorname{F}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O})
. The Yoneda product gives
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
the structure of a graded
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, and this is inherited by its torsion-free quotient,
\operatorname{F}^{*}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
. The remarkable fact
[
8
, Theorem 6.8]
is that although the Ext-algebra itself can be highly non-commutative, and infinite,
\operatorname{F}^{*}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
is just an exterior algebra generated by its degree one component

\operatorname{F}^{1}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}_{\lambda},\mathcal{O})\,.

See
2.15
for the isomorphism above. As explained in
[
8
, Introduction]
, this may be seen an an integral version of a result, due to Serre, on the structure of the Ext algebra of a regular local ring. The proof of this structure theorem for
\operatorname{F}^{*}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
uses ideas from the theory of differential graded algebras. For the present purpose the important takeaway is that there is an natural isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

(2.16)

\bigwedge^{c}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2},\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})\,.

The naturality assertion is that given any morphism
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda)\to(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
, the induced map
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2}\to\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
gives rise to commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{c}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2},\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{c}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{2},\mathcal{O})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

of maps of
\mathcal{O}
-modules. This leads to the following
invariance of domain
property for congruence modules; see
[

, Theorem 7.4]
.

Theorem 2.17
.

Given a surjective map
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda)\to(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
, and an

\mathcal{O}
-modules
\Psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})\cong\Psi_{\lambda}(M^{\prime})

Since
\varphi
is surjective,
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\cdot A^{\prime}=\operatorname{Ker}\lambda^{\prime}
. The hypotheses in the statement above imply that
\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}\colon A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}\to A^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
is surjective map of regular local rings of dimension

and hence an isomorphism.

Freeness criterion

Fix
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M
. For any
A
-module
X
one has a map

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},X)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},X)\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},X\otimes_{A}M)

where the one on the right is a Künneth map. This is functorial in
X
, and one gets the commutative diagram below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}{(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}{(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}}

The horizontal maps are one-to-one, by Lemma
2.5
. Moreover, the one on right is an isomorphism, as can be verified easily. It follows that the map on the left is one-to-one. This justifies the following result.

Lemma 2.18
.

The diagram above induces a natural surjective map of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

a_{\lambda}(M)\colon\Psi_{\lambda}(A)^{\mu}\twoheadrightarrow\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\,,\quad\text{where $\mu\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{\lambda}(M)$.}

In particular there is an equality

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(M)=\mu\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(A)-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ker}(a_{\lambda}(M))\,.\qed

When
A
is Gorenstein and
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay,
\operatorname{Ker}(a_{\lambda}(M))
can be interpretted as a“stable” cohomology module of the pair
(A,M)
. This identification leads to the criterion below for detecting free summands of

; see
[
8
, Theorem 9.2]
.

Theorem 2.19
.

With notation as above, when
A
Gorenstein and
M
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay,
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(M)=\mu\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
if and only if

M\cong A^{\mu}\oplus W\quad\text{and $W_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}=0$,}

as
A
-modules. In this case, when
\mu\neq 0
the
A
-module
M
is faithful. ∎

Isomorphism criteria

The preceding results leads to a criterion for detecting isomorphisms between rings, in terms of congruence modules.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a surjective map of complete local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. Assume there exists an augmentation
\lambda\colon B\to\mathcal{O}
such that

(A,\lambda\varphi)
and
(B,\lambda)
are in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
for some
c\geq 0
, and either of the following conditions hold:

(1)

The ring
A
is Gorenstein,
B
is Cohen-Macaulay, and

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda\varphi}(A)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(B)\,;

(2)

The ring
B
is complete intersection and

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda\varphi}(A)=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(B)\,.

Then the map
\varphi
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

(1) The hypotheses imply that
\varphi
is an isomorphism at
\lambda\varphi
so
\operatorname{rank}_{\lambda\varphi}B=1
. Thus Theorem
2.19
implies that
B
is a faithful
A
-module, so
\operatorname{Ker}\varphi=(0)
.

(2) is an simple argument using the Jacobi-Zariski sequence arising from maps
A\to B\to\mathcal{O}
and Nakayama’s Lemma; see
[
8
, Lemma 5.10]
for details.
∎

The isomorphism (
2.16
) is also a critical input in tracking the behavior of congruence modules under deformations.

.

In the context above, let

It is enough to consider the case when

Wiles defect

Fix a pair
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
and a finitely generated
A
-module

. Since

is regular at
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
, and in particular a domain, the

-module

has a rank. The

of

at

is the integer

\delta_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{\lambda}(M)\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(A)-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\,.

In particular the Wiles defect of

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(A)-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(A)\,.

We refer to

, Introduction]

Theorem

) give the following result, which is

.

One has

. ∎

implies the following (which is

.

In particular

When

First we verify that

is complete intersection, that is to say, isomorphic to

for some regular sequence

The second equality is by Theorem

Next we verify that when

Quantitative metastability of the Tikhonov-Mann iteration for countable families of mappings
Horaţiu Cheval

Abstract
In this paper, we obtain rates of metastability for the Tikhonov-Mann iteration for countable families of mappings in CAT(0) spaces. This iteration was recently defined by the author in the setting of W W -hyperbolic spaces as a generalization of the strongly convergent version of the Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration introduced by Boţ and Meier for finding common fixed points of families of nonexpansive mappings in Hilbert spaces, and as an extension of the Tikhonov-Mann iteration for single mappings, for which Leuştean and the author obtained rates of asymptotic regularity in W W -hyperbolic spaces. Keywords: Tikhonov-Mann iteration, Rates of metastability, Common fixed points, Proof mining Mathematics Subject Classification: 47J25, 47H09, 03F10

Introduction

Boţ and Meier
[
4
]
considered the following iterative method for
finding a common fixed point of a family
(T_{n}:H\to H)
of nonexpansive self-mappings of
H
:

\displaystyle x_{0}\in H,\quad x_{n+1}=(1-\lambda_{n})\beta_{n}x_{n}+\lambda_{n}T_{n}(\beta_{n}x_{n}),

(1)

where
(\lambda_{n})
and
(\beta_{n})
are sequences in
[0,1]
.
It is proved in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
that,
under certain conditions on the parameters of the iteration and on the family
(T_{n})
,

(x_{n})
converges strongly to a point in
\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{n})
.

We say that a
W
-hyperbolic space
[
13
]
is a metric space
(X,d)
together with
a mapping
W:X\times X\times[0,1]\to X
, satisfying the following axioms, for all
x,y,z\in X
and all
\lambda,\theta\in[0,1]
:

(W1)

d(z,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\leq(1-\lambda)d(z,x)+\lambda d(z,y)
;

(W2)

d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y,(1-\theta)x+\theta y)=\left\lvert\lambda-\theta\right\rvert d(x,y)
;

(W3)

(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x
;

(W4)

d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\lambda)y+\lambda w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)
.

W(x,y,\lambda)
is meant to be read as an abstract convex combination between the points
x
and
y
of parameter
\lambda
.
For this reason, the notation
(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y:=W(x,y,\lambda)
is normally used instead.
Examples of
W
-hyperbolic spaces include real normed spaces, Busemann spaces
[
23
]
or CAT(0) spaces
[
1
,
5
]
.
The following generalization to
W
-hyperbolic spaces of iteration (
1
)
was studied by the author in
[
6
]
. Let
(X,d,W)
be a
W
-hyperbolic space and

(T_{n}:X\to X)
be a family of nonexpansive mappings.
The
Tikhonov-Mann iteration
associated to
(T_{n})
, of parameters

(\lambda_{n}),(\beta_{n})\subset[0,1]
and anchor point
u\in X
, generates a sequence
(x_{n})
by

\displaystyle x_{0}\in X,\quad\quad\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} x_{n+1}&=(1-\lambda_{n})u_{n}+\lambda_{n}T_{n}u_{n},\\
u_{n}&=(1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}x_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

Note that if
X
is a Hilbert space and
u=0
, one recovers iteration (
1
).

The main results of this paper, which are part of the program of
proof mining

[
14
,
16
,
17
]
concern the metastability (in the sense of Tao
[
26
,
27
]
)
of iteration (
2
) in CAT(0) spaces, with quantitative information in the form of
rates of metastability
.
A sequence
(a_{n})
in a metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
metastable
if

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\forall f\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\forall i,j\in[n,f(n)]\left(d(a_{i},a_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right),

where
[n,f(n)]
is the set
\left\{n,n+1,\ldots,f(n)\right\}
, and a rate thereof is a function providing an
upper bound on
n
for each
k
and
f
.
Metastability is equivalent to the Cauchy property of
(a_{n})
, hence also to its convergence in complete spaces,
but this equivalence is not effective, meaning that generally one cannot transform rates of metastability
into quantitative information on the convergence of
(a_{n})
. The importance of metastability
in the context of proof mining lies in that, while rates of convergence cannot be extracted in general,
by moving to this equivalent formulation, one can guarantee the extractability of rates via logical metatheorems.

The main results of
[
6
]
consist of rates of (
(T_{n})
-)asymptotic regularity
(i.e. rates of convergence for
d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\to 0
and
d(x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})\to 0
) for (
2
).
The single mapping case
T_{n}=T
was studied by Leuştean and the author in
[
8
]
,
as a nonlinear generalization of the Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration with Tikhonov regularization
terms proved strongly convergent by Boţ, Csetnek and Meier
[
3
]
in Hilbert spaces,
and for which Dinis and Pinto
[
10
]
computed rates of metastability.
As shown in
[
7
]
, the strong convergence of the Tikhonov-Mann iteration
already follows in CAT(0) spaces from results in
[

]
, from which Schade and Kohlenbach
extracted rates of metastability in
[

]
.
Another generalization of the Tikhonov-Mann iteration is the alternating Halpern-Mann iteration
introduced by Dinis and Pinto in

with rates of asymptotic regularity having been obtained in the more general setting of

As mentioned in the introduction, one important example of

\displaystyle d^{2}(z,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\leq(1-\lambda)d^{2}(z,x)+\lambda d^{2}(z,y)-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(x,y).

Leuştean

showed that CAT(0) spaces are uniformly convex with modulus

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d(a,x)&\leq r\\

The following metric generalization of the inner product was introduced in
[
2
]
,
and allows one to extend several arguments from Hilbert space to a nonlinear setting.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. The
Berg-Nikolaev quasilinearization

is the mapping
\left\langle\overrightarrow{\cdot\cdot},\overrightarrow{\cdot\cdot}\right\rangle:X^{2}\times X^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(d^{2}(x,v)+d^{2}(y,u)-d^{2}(x,u)-d^{2}(y,v)\right).

(5)

The quasilinearization mapping is characterized by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
2
]

The Berg-Nikolaev quasilinearization is the unique mapping satisfying the following,
for all
x,y,u,v,w\in X
:

(i)

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{xy}\right\rangle=d^{2}(x,y)
;

(ii)

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle=\left\langle\overrightarrow{uv},\overrightarrow{xy}\right\rangle
;

(iii)

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\overrightarrow{yx},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle
;

(iv)

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{vw}\right\rangle=\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uw}\right\rangle
.

Furthermore, if
X
is a CAT(0) space, one has that the following Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds,
for all

:

\displaystyle\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{uv}\right\rangle\leq d(x,y)d(u,v).

We now introduce some quantitative notions required to express our results.
Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
(a_{n})
be a sequence in
X
.
We say that a function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is a
rate of convergence
for
(a_{n})
to a point
a\in X
if

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\forall n\geq\varphi(k)\left(d(a_{n},a)\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right),

and that it is a
Cauchy modulus
for
(a_{n})
if

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\forall n\geq\varphi(k)\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\left(d(a_{n+j},a_{n})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

(a_{n})
is said to be
asymptotically regular
if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},a_{n+1})=0
,

T
-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},Ta_{n})=0

and
(T_{n})
-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},T_{n}a_{n})=0
,
where
T:X\to X
and
(T_{n}:X\to X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. A rate of (
T
-,
(T_{n})
-)asymptotic regularity
is a rate of convergence to
0
for the respective sequences.

A function
\mu:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}
is called a
rate of metastability
for the sequence
(a_{n})
if

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\forall f\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\exists n\leq\mu(k,f)\forall i,j\in[n,f(n)]\left(d(a_{i},a_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right),

where
[n,f(n)]=\left\{n,n+1,\ldots,f(n)\right\}
.
We may restrict the definition of metastability to only quantify over monotone (i.e. nondecreasing) functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,
as we can replace
f
with the monotone function
f^{M}(k)=\max\limits_{i\leq k}f(i)
.

Finally, a
rate of divergence
for a series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
of nonnegative real numbers
is a function
\theta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}b_{i}\geq n\right).

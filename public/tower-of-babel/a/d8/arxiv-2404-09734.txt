Abstract
This letter investigates the weighted sum rate maximization problem in movable antenna (MA)-enhanced systems. To reduce the computational complexity, we transform it into a more tractable weighted minimum mean square error (WMMSE) problem well-suited for MA. We then adopt the WMMSE algorithm and majorization-minimization algorithm to optimize the beamforming and antenna positions, respectively. Moreover, we propose a planar movement mode, which constrains each MA to a specified area, we obtain a low-complexity closed-form solution. Numerical results demonstrate that the MA-enhanced system outperforms the conventional system. Besides, the computation time for the planar movement mode is reduced by approximately 30% at a little performance expense.



I
Introduction

With the advancement of multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technologies, wireless communication systems have witnessed a substantial increase in capacity due to the utilization of additional spatial degrees of freedom (DoFs). However, the conventional MIMO systems equipped with fixed-position antennas (FPAs) face challenges in further enhancing performance. Since the last century, the concept of antenna arrays with arbitrarily distributed elements
[
1
]
has gradually surfaced. This concept suggests that fewer, non-uniformly deployed elements can match the performance as of equally spaced arrays. Recently, with the gradual maturation of micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), and liquid metal technologies, movable antenna (MA)
[
2
]
, also known as fluid antenna (FA)
[
3
]
, flexible antenna
[
4
]
, and flexible-position MIMO
[
5
]
, has been proposed to optimize physical antenna placement adaptively according to the time-varying dynamic of wireless channels to enhance MIMO system performance.

Several studies have emerged to analyze and optimize the MA-enhanced MIMO systems. For instance, in
[
6
,
7
]
, the authors focused on the channel estimation problem and utilized compressed sensing-based methods to efficiently estimate the angles of departure, angles of arrival, and complex coefficients of the multi-path components, which ensures the MA systems to reap performance gains. Also, based on the estimated channel,
[
8
]
analyzed the maximum channel gain achieved by a single receive MA as compared to its FPA counterpart, and
[
9
]
leveraged the advantages of MA to improve signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of multiple destinations for jointly decoding a common message. Currently, most research focused on rate maximization
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and power control
[
15
,
16
]
. In
[
10
,
11
]
, the transceiver equipped with MAs was considered to enhance the performance of point-to-point MIMO systems. Furthermore, in
[
15
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
, the authors considered a multiuser transmission scenario where only the BS is equipped with MAs. Specifically, limited by the realistic electromechanical devices, the motions of the MA elements were modeled as discrete movements in
[
15
]
. In addition, in
[
16
]
, the authors considered a multiuser transmission scenario where only users are equipped with MAs. However, most works employ the gradient descent algorithm and continuously try to obtain a suitable step size satisfying the constraints, so the computational complexity is relatively high.

In this letter, we investigate a popular weighted sum-rate (WSR) maximization problem in an MA-enhanced multiuser MIMO system, where both BS and users are equipped with MAs. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We formulate a WSR maximization problem and transform it into a more tractable weighted sum mean-square error minimization problem that is compatible with MAs.

•

We employ the block coordinate descent (BCD) method to optimize all variables alternately and adopt a planar movement mode to achieve a closed-form solution for optimizing antenna positions, thereby efficiently reducing the computational complexity.

•

Numerical results demonstrate that the MA-enhanced systems ensure superior performance over the conventional systems. Besides, the planar movement mode significantly reduces the computational complexity at a little performance expense.

System model: an MA-enhanced multiuser MIMO system.

As shown in Fig.

M
MAs serves
K
single-MA users. Compared with the conventional communication system, the position of each MA in the transmit region
\mathcal{C}^{t}
and the receive region
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{r},k=1,\cdots,K
, can be adjusted to exploit the spatial diversity gain via a controller, e.g., stepper motors
[
2
]
or liquid metal
[
3
]
.

Let
\mathbf{t}_{m}=(x_{m},y_{m})^{T}
and
\mathbf{r}_{k}=(x_{k},y_{k})^{T}
denote the position of MA
m
at the BS and the position of the MA at user
k
, respectively, which are established by the classical Cartesian coordinate system. By stacking the positions of all antennas together, we denote the position vectors of the BS and users as
\mathbf{t}=(\mathbf{t}_{1}^{T},\cdots,\mathbf{t}_{M}^{T})^{T}
and
\mathbf{r}=(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{T},\cdots,\mathbf{r}_{K}^{T})^{T}
, respectively. To avoid potential electrical coupling between adjacent MAs and upgrade the antenna efficiency in practical implementation, a minimum inter-MA distance
D
is required between each pair of MAs at BS
[
2
]
, i.e.,

\|\mathbf{t}_{i}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|\geq D,\forall i\neq j.

(1)

For the MA-enhanced system, the channel response depends on the position vector and the channel vector between the BS and user
k
can be represented by a function of the position vector, i.e.,
\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
. Hence, the discrete-time signal received at user
k
is written as

y_{k}=\mathbf{h}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{w}_{k}s_{k}+n_{k},

(2)

where
\mathbf{w}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
and
s_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
represent the beamformer and the unit-power complex valued information symbols intended for user
k
, respectively;
n_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
models the additive noise with zero mean and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Based on (
2
), the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) experienced at user
k
is obtained as

\gamma_{k}\triangleq\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{k}|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{i\neq k}|\mathbf{h}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{i}|^{2}+\sigma^{2}}.

(3)

II-A

Field-Response-Based Channel Model

Let
L_{k}^{t}
and
L_{k}^{r}
denote the total numbers of transmit and receive channel paths from the BS to user
k
. Then, the signal propagation phase difference of the
l
-th transmit path for user
k
between the
m
-th MA and the reference point
\mathbf{t}_{0}=[0,0]^{T}
at the BS and the
l
-th receive path between the MA and reference point
\mathbf{r}_{0}=[0,0]^{T}
at user
k
are, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\rho_{l,k}^{t}(\mathbf{t}_{m})=\mathbf{t}_{m}^{T}\mathbf{n}_{l,k}^{t}

\displaystyle=x_{m}\sin\theta_{l,k}^{t}\cos\phi_{l,k}^{t}+y_{m}\cos\theta_{l,k}^{t},

(4)

\displaystyle\rho_{l,k}^{r}(\mathbf{r}_{k})=\mathbf{t}_{m}^{T}\mathbf{n}_{l,k}^{r}

\displaystyle=x_{k}\sin\theta_{l,k}^{r}\cos\phi_{l,k}^{r}+y_{k}\cos\theta_{l,k}^{r},

where
\mathbf{n}_{l,k}^{t}\triangleq(\sin\theta_{l,k}^{t}\cos\phi_{l,k}^{t},\cos\theta_{l,k}^{t})^{T}
and
\mathbf{n}_{l,k}^{r}\triangleq(\sin\theta_{l,k}^{r}\cos\phi_{l,k}^{r},\cos\theta_{l,k}^{r})^{T}
;
(\theta_{l,k}^{t},\phi_{l,k}^{t})
and
(\theta_{l,k}^{r},\phi_{l,k}^{r})
are the elevation and azimuth AoAs for the
l
-th transmit and receive path between user
k
and the BS, respectively. Accordingly, the field-response vector of the receive channel paths between user
k
and the
m
-th MA at the BS is given by
[
8
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle=\left[e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\rho_{1,k}^{t}(\mathbf{t}_{m})},\cdots,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\rho_{L_{k}^{t},k}^{t}(\mathbf{t}_{m})}\right]^{T},

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})

\displaystyle=\left[e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\rho_{1,k}^{r}(\mathbf{r}_{k})},\cdots,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\rho_{L_{k}^{r},k}^{r}(\mathbf{r}_{k})}\right]^{T}.

As a result, the channel vector between user
k
and the BS is given by

\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})=\mathbf{F}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}),

(6)

where
\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{t})\triangleq(\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{1}),\cdots,\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{M}))\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{k}\times M}
denotes the field-response matrix at the BS, and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{k}^{t}\times L_{k}^{r}}
is the path-response matrix, which represents the multi-path response between all the transmit and receive channel paths. As can be observed, the movement introduced by MA causes more significant variations of phases than the amplitudes
[
13
]
.

II-B

Problem Formulation

In this letter, we aim to maximize the weighted sum rate of all the users by jointly designing the transmit beamformer and the antenna position vectors at both the BS and the
K
users, subject to the minimum inter-MA distance (
1
) at the BS and the transmit power constraints. Let
\mathbf{W}\triangleq(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{K})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
. The WSR maximization problem is formulated as

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}}{\text{maximize}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}\log(1+\gamma_{k})

(7a)

subject to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{w}_{k}\|^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}},

(7b)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{t}_{i}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}\geq D,\forall i\neq j,

(7c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{t},\quad\mathbf{r}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{r},

(7d)

where the weight
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
represents the priority of user
k
.
P_{\mathrm{max}}
denotes the maximum transmit power. The problem is challenging mainly due to the nonconvexity of the objective function (
7a
) and the constraints (
7c
), which is an obstacle for the development of an optimal solution. To reduce the complexity and transform it into a more tractable problem, we consider the linear receive beamforming strategy so that the estimated signal at user
k
is given by

\hat{s}_{k}=u_{k}^{*}y_{k},

(8)

where
\mathbf{u}\triangleq(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{K})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
represents the receive beamformer. We assume the independence of
s_{k}
and
n_{k}
. Then, the expected mean-square error (MSE) can be written as

\displaystyle e_{k}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|\hat{s}_{k}-s_{k}|^{2}\right]

(9)

\displaystyle=1+|u_{k}|^{2}(\sigma^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{K}|\mathbf{h}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{j}|^{2})

\displaystyle-2\mathrm{Re}(u_{k}^{*}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{k}).

where
\mathrm{Re}(\cdot)
denotes the real part of complex scalar. Then, inspired by the weighted sum mean-square error minimization (WMMSE) algorithm proposed for conventional multiuser MIMO systems
[
17
]
, we introduce an auxiliary optimization vector variable
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{K})^{T}
and establish a more tractable MA-enabled WMMSE problem as follows

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}{\text{minimize}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}(v_{k}e_{k}-\log(v_{k}))

(10a)

subject to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{w}_{k}\|^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}},

(10b)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{t}_{i}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}\geq D,\forall i\neq j,

(10c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{t},\quad\mathbf{r}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{r},

(10d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{0}.

(10e)

Remark:
The equivalence between (
7
) and (
10
) can be proved by deriving the optimal
u_{k},v_{k},\forall k
, in Eq. (
10a
) and substituting them back into Eq. (
10a
), which is similar to
[
17
, Appendix A]
for the traditional FPA MIMO system.

III
Block Coordinate Descent Method for MA-Enhanced Multiuser MIMO System

BCD method decomposes the optimization variables into multiple blocks and optimizes different blocks in each iteration with the other blocks fixed.

III-A

Transceiver Beamformer Design

According to the principle of BCD, we first investigate the optimization of beamformer
\mathbf{W}
and related variables
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
for fixed MA position vectors
\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}
. The beamformer design problem accordingly reduces to

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}{\text{minimize}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}(v_{k}e_{k}-\log(v_{k}))

(11a)

subject to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{w}_{k}\|^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}},

(11b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\geq\mathbf{0}.

(11c)

This problem has been studied extensively in the literature, and one of the well-known methods to obtain a stationary solution is the WMMSE algorithm with the following iterative updating rule
[
17
, Table I]
:

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}|\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\mathbf{w}_{i}|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\mathbf{w}_{k},

(12a)

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(1-u_{k}^{*}\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\mathbf{w}_{k}\right)^{-1},

(12b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}u_{k}v_{k}\left(\mu\mathbf{I}_{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}v_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{h}_{i}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbf{h}_{k}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k}),

(12c)

where
\mu\geq 0
is the optimal dual variable for the transmit power constraint in (
11b
). To describe the computation of
\mu
, we let
\mathbf{w}_{k}(\mu)
denote the right-hand side of (
12c
). When the matrix
\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}v_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{h}_{i}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}
is invertible and
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{w}_{k}(0)\|^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
, then
\mathbf{w}_{k}=\mathbf{w}_{k}(0)
, otherwise the optimal
\mu
should satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{w}_{k}(\mu)\|^{2}=P_{\mathrm{max}}
. Note that the left-hand side is a decreasing function in
\mu
for
\mu>0
. Hence, it can be obtained via one dimensional search techniques, e.g., bisection method.

III-B

MA Position Design at BS

To reduce the computational complexity, we resort to alternative optimization to design MA positions at the BS. For fixed
\mathbf{W}
,
\mathbf{t}_{j},\forall j\neq m
, and
\mathbf{r}
, the problem of designing MA
m
position at BS accordingly reduces to

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}^{t}}{\text{minimize}}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\mathbf{f}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathbf{A}_{k,m}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})+\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{b}_{k,m}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}))\right)

(13a)

subject to

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}\geq D,\forall j\neq m,

(13b)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{k,m}

\displaystyle\triangleq\alpha_{k}v_{k}|u_{k}|^{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{m,:}\|^{2}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}^{H},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{k,m}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2\alpha_{k}v_{k}\bigg(|u_{k}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\bigg(w_{m,j}^{*}\sum_{n\neq m}w_{n,j}

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})^{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{n})\bigg)-u_{k}w_{m,k}^{*}\bigg)\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}).

Next, we propose to leverage the minorization maximization (MM)
[
18
]
technique to update antenna position vector. The key to the success of MM lies in constructing a surrogate function satisfying the upper bound property for both objective function and constraints. Now, we introduce some Lemmas that facilitate the implementation of MM.

Lemma 1
.

The quadratic form
\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}
, where
\mathbf{L}
is a Hermitian matrix, can be upper bounded as
[
18
, Example 13]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{x}^{H}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{x}+2\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{x}^{H}(\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{M})\mathbf{x}_{0})+\mathbf{x}_{0}^{H}(\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{L})\mathbf{x}_{0},

(15)

where
\mathbf{M}\succeq\mathbf{L}
. Equality is achieved at
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{0}
.

Lemma 2
.

The linear form
z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})=\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{b}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}))
, where
\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})
is the field-response vector defined in (
5
), can be lower bouneded and upper bounded as
[
2
, Eq. (26) and Appendix A-B]
:

\displaystyle z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle\geq z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})+\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0})

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0}),

(16a)

\displaystyle z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})+\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{1}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{m,0}),

(16b)

where

\displaystyle\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{b}^{H}\nabla\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0}))^{T},

(17)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})

\displaystyle=j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(\mathbf{n}_{1,k},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{L_{k}^{t},k})^{T}\odot(\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0}),\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m,0})).

Equality is achieved at
\mathbf{t}_{m}=\mathbf{t}_{m,0}
.

Remark:
The proof of Eq. (
16a
) is provided in
[
2
, Eq. (26) and Appendix A-B]
. Thus, we can derive Eq. (
16b
) directly by replacing
\mathbf{b}
with
-\mathbf{b}
in (
16a
).

From the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, at iteration
(n)
, a minorizing function for the constraint is constructed at
\mathbf{t}_{m}=\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}
as follows:

\|\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}\geq\frac{(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}-\mathbf{t}_{j})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{j})}{\|\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}}.

(18)

Based on Lemmas
1
-
2
, at iteration
(n)
, a majorizing function for the objective function is constructed at
\mathbf{t}_{m}=\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\mathbf{f}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathbf{A}_{k,m}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})+\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{b}_{k,m}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}))\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathrm{Re}(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}))+\mathrm{const.}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{t}_{m}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+(\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})^{T}\mathbf{t}_{m}+\mathrm{const.}

where

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2(\mathbf{A}_{k,m}-\alpha_{k}v_{k}|u_{k}|^{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{m,:}\|^{2}

(20)

\displaystyle\cdot\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})\|^{2}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})+\mathbf{b}_{k,m},

\displaystyle z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{Re}(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})).

Therefore, the antenna position vector
\mathbf{t}_{m}
is updated as

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\underset{\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}^{t}}{\text{argmin}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{t}_{m}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})^{T}\mathbf{t}_{m}

(21a)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to }\frac{(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}-\mathbf{t}_{j})^{T}(\mathbf{t}_{m}-\mathbf{t}_{j})}{\|\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)}-\mathbf{t}_{j}\|_{2}}\geq D,\forall j\neq m,

(21b)

which is a typical convex quadratic programming (QP) problem and can be efficiently solved by quadprog or CVX.

III-C

MA Position Design at Users

For fixed
\mathbf{W}
,
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{t}
, each
\mathbf{r}_{k}
can be updated in parallel since they are independent in (
10
). Specifically, the problem of designing MA position at user
k
accordingly reduces to

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{r}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{r}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{C}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})+\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{d}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}))

(22)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{K}|u_{k}|^{2}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{t})^{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq-2u_{k}^{*}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{w}_{k}.

Similar to Lemmas
1
-
2
, at iteration
(n)
, a majorizing function for the objective function is constructed at
\mathbf{r}_{k}=\mathbf{r}_{k}^{(n)}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{r}_{k})\mathbf{C}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})+\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{d}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}))

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{r}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+(\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}^{(n)})-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k}\|_{1}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{(n)})^{T}\mathbf{r}_{k}+\mathrm{const.}

where

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2(\mathbf{C}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{K}|u_{k}|^{2}\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{w}_{j}\|^{2}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k}^{(n)})+\mathbf{d}_{k}

(25)

\displaystyle z_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{Re}(\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{r}_{k})).

Therefore, the MA position at user
k
is updated as

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{k}^{(n+1)}=\underset{\mathbf{r}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{r}}{\text{argmin}}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{r}_{k}\|^{2}

IV
Low-Complexity Design

To reduce the computational complexity, we propose the planar movement mode at BS, where each MA is only allowed to move in given planar area and the minimum distance between any two areas is set as
D
to avoid coupling effect. To distinguish, the movement mode described in Section III is referred to as the general movement mode.

IV-A

Planar Movement Mode at BS

The position optimization problem (

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}^{t}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\mathbf{f}_{k}^{H}(\mathbf{t}_{m})\mathbf{A}_{k,m}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m})+\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{b}_{k,m}^{H}\mathbf{f}_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}))\right),

where
\mathcal{C}_{m}^{t}

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n+1)}=\underset{\mathbf{t}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}^{t}}{\text{argmin}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\|\mathbf{t}_{m}\|^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle+(\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}\|_{1}\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})^{T}\mathbf{t}_{m}

IV-B

Convergence and Complexity Analysis

Convergence analysis:
Based on the WMMSE algorithm for transmit beamformer design and the MM algorithm for MA position design, a stationary point is guaranteed for each subproblem
[
17
,
18
]
. Therefore, the initial equivalent function is guaranteed to increase monotonically, and our proposed algorithm is convergent.

Complexity analysis:
The complexity of updating
\mathbf{w}
at each iteration of the subproblem is
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}M^{2})

[
17
, §IV]
. The complexity of updating
\mathbf{t}
mainly depends on computing coefficients
\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{k,m}
and
\nabla z_{k}(\mathbf{t}_{m}^{(n)})
and solving the QP problem. Therefore, the complexity of the general movement mode is
\mathcal{O}(KM^{2}+KML_{t}L_{r}+KML_{t}^{2}+M^{2.5})
, while for the planar movement mode, it is
\mathcal{O}(KM^{2}+KML_{t}L_{r}+KML_{t}^{2}+M)
at each iteration of subproblem. Similarly, the complexity of updating
\mathbf{r}
is
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}M+KML_{t}L_{r}+KML_{r}^{2}+K)
.

VI
Conclusion

In this letter, we employed the MAs at both BS and users to improve the weighted sum rate of wireless networks. We adopted BCD algorithm to optimize all variables, and then confined each antenna to a designated area, i.e., planar movement mode, to reduce the computational complexity. Simulation results confirmed the huge potential of MAs in improving the performance of future communications systems.

Abstract
. We prove that the existence of 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} incomparable selective ultrafilters implies the existence of a Wallace semigroup whose cube is countably compact. In addition, assuming the existence of 2 𝔠 2^{\mathfrak{c}} incomparable selective ultrafilters and 2 < 2 𝔠 = 2 𝔠 2^{<2^{\mathfrak{c}}}=2^{\mathfrak{c}} , we obtain torsion-free topological groups with respect to Comfort’s question on the countable compactness of (infinite) powers of a topological group.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Some history

In the Open Problems in Topology, W. W. Comfort asked in 1990 the following question: Is there, for every cardinal number
\alpha\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, a topological group
G
such that
G^{\gamma}
is countably compact for all cardinals
\gamma<\alpha
, but
G^{\alpha}
is not countably compact?

Tomita
[
8
]
showed in 2005 that it is consistently affirmative for every cardinal
\kappa\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
. These examples are groups of order 2 and were built from the existence of selective ultrafilters.

In 2019, Tomita
[
10
]
proved that there exists a consistent torsion-free group that answer this question affirmatively for each
\alpha\leq\omega
. The example is in fact a free Abelian group, but as shown in
[
11
]
, the countable power of a topological free Abelian group is not countably compact. Thus, some different torsion-free group should be used for larger
\alpha
.

We construct an example, for every infinite cardinal
\alpha\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, using an appropriate group topology on
\mathbb{Q}^{(2^{\mathfrak{c}})}
. The construction rely on the technique developed in Bellini, Rodrigues and Tomita
[
1
]
, who constructed a
p
-compact group topology on
\mathbb{Q}^{(\kappa)}
for every infinite cardinal
\kappa=\kappa^{\omega}
and a selective ultrafilter
p
, together with some combinatorial properties of many incomparable selective ultrafilters as it was done in
[
8
]
.

Wallace
[
13
]
asked whether a both-sided cancellative countably compact semigroup is a topological group. This problem divides in two: the conditions that a semigroup must be algebraically a group and the conditions that make semigroup topology on a group have a group topology. It was shown that pseudocompact semigroup topologies in a group are group topologies
[
6
]
.

Robbie and Svetlichny
[
7
]
were the first to show that there exists a both-sided cancellative semigroup which is not a group. Such semigroups have been called Wallace semigroups since then. Other examples were obtained using Martin’s Axiom for countable poses,
\mathfrak{c}
selective ultrafilters and a single selective ultrafilter.

On the other hand, Grant
[
5
]
showed that there no Wallace semigroups that are sequentially compact. This motivated him to ask the following questions about other properties related to the power of the semigroup:

(1)

Is every
p-
compact cancellative topological semigroup a topological group?

(2)

Is
S
a topological group if
S\times S
is countably compact?

Tomita
[
9
]
showed that there are no
p
-compact Wallace semigroups (that is, if
S
is a Wallace semigroup then
S^{2^{\mathfrak{c}}}
is not countably compact). This answers
(1)
.

For the second question, Boero and Tomita
[
2
]
gave a consistent example of a Wallace semigroup whose square is countably compact. The example was a semigroup of a free Abelian group whose square is countably compact.

In
[
12
]
it was shown that it is consistent that there exists a free Abelian group whose every finite power is countably compact, but it was not possible to obtain from it a Wallace semigroup whose finite powers are countably compact.

In this paper, we obtain a Wallace semigroup whose cube is countably compact making use of the fact that the example is a semigroup of a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space rather than a free Abelian group.

Basic notation and terminology

In what follows,
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
will denote the subset of non-negative rationals,
\mathbb{N}
the subset of positive integers. Let

Let

For any

The set of all free ultrafilters on

Throughout this paper, we fix an infinite cardinal

2.
Countable compactness,
p-

We assume that all topological spaces are Hausdorff.
In this section, we review some basic facts about countable compactness,
p-

Definition 2.1
.

A topological space
X
is countably compact if every countable open cover has a finite subcover.

It is not difficult to show that a topological space
X
is countably compact if, and only if, every sequence in
X
has an accumulation point.

Furthermore, as each accumulation point of a sequence is also a
p-
limit of that sequence, for some ultrafilter
p

Definition 2.2
.

Given
p\in\omega^{*}
, we say that a topological space
X
is
p-
compact if every sequence in
X
has a
p-
limit.

Every
p-
compact space is countably compact. We already know that Tychonoff’s theorem stands for
p-
compactness. Thereby, every power of a
p-
compact space is countably compact. In addition, by Ginsburg and Saks’ theorem

Theorem 2.6 of
[

is countably compact if, and only if,

is
p-

On other hand, we will need the following class of ultrafilters.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
p\in\omega^{*}
is selective if, for every partition
\{P_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}
of
\omega
, exists

such that

or exists

such that

, for each

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

be a selective ultrafilter. If
\{B_{n}\mid n\in\omega\}\subset p
, then there exists an increasing sequence

, for each

Now, we present a pre-order on

Definition 2.5

Given

The next result gives us some combinatorial properties of many incomparable selective ultrafilters.

Lemma 2.6

[

Let

p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{d}\left(\frac{1}{N}h_{\beta}(n)\right)=\varphi_{d}\left(\frac{1}{N}\chi_{\beta}\right)
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in A_{\xi}
and
\xi\in[\omega,\mathfrak{c})
.

Hence, the group topology on
\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c})}
induced by these homomorphisms is such that
p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}h_{\beta}(n)=\frac{1}{N}\chi_{\beta}
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in A_{\xi}
and
\xi\in[\omega,\mathfrak{c})
. Therefore, for every
\xi\in\mathfrak{c}\setminus\omega
,
\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\})}
is
p_{\xi}-
compact and
p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}f_{n}\chi_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\})}
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in F_{\xi}
.
∎

]
for the particular case

.

Theorem 5.3
.

If exists
\mathfrak{c}
incomparable selective ultrafilters, then there exists a Wallace semigroup
S
such that
S^{3}
is countably compact.

Proof.

Let
\{F_{\xi}\mid\omega\leq\xi<\mathfrak{c}\}
,
\{p_{\xi}\mid\omega\leq\xi<\mathfrak{c}\}
and
\tau
be a group topology on
\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c})}
as in Lemma
5.2
. We define
S=\{r\chi_{0}\mid r\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\}\oplus\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\})}
and notice that
(S,\tau)
is a Wallace semigroup.

For every sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subset S^{3}
, there exists
r^{1},r^{2},r^{3}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{\omega}
and
g^{1},g^{2},g^{3}\in\left(\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\})}\right)^{\omega}
such that
y_{n}=(r^{1}_{n}\chi_{0},r^{2}_{n}\chi_{0},r^{3}_{n}\chi_{0})+(g^{1}_{n},g^{2}_{n},g^{3}_{n})
, for each
n\in\omega
. By Proposition
5.1
, exists
h:\omega\to\omega
strictly increasing and
\xi\in\mathfrak{c}\setminus\omega
such that

\displaystyle(r^{1}\circ h)(n)\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}a_{f}.f(n)\chi_{0},

\displaystyle(r^{2}\circ h)(n)\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{2}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}b_{f}.f(n)\chi_{0},

\displaystyle(r^{3}\circ h)(n)\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{3}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}c_{f}.f(n)\chi_{0},

for every
n\in\omega
, where
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
a_{f},b_{f},c_{f}\in\mathbb{Q}
for any
f\in F_{\xi}
. We can consider
a_{f}=\frac{m_{a_{f}}}{n_{a_{f}}}
,
b_{f}=\frac{m_{b_{f}}}{n_{b_{f}}}
and
c_{f}=\frac{m_{c_{f}}}{n_{c_{f}}}
, where
m_{a_{f}},m_{b_{f}},m_{c_{f}}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n_{a_{f}},n_{b_{f}},n_{c_{f}}\in\mathbb{N}
, for any
f\in F_{\xi}
.

Then,

\displaystyle p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}r^{1}_{n}\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}m_{a_{f}}.\left(p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{a_{f}}}f(n)\chi_{0}\right)\in S,

\displaystyle p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}r^{2}_{n}\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{2}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}m_{b_{f}}.\left(p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{b_{f}}}f(n)\chi_{0}\right)\in S,

\displaystyle p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}r^{3}_{n}\chi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{3}\chi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F_{\xi}}m_{c_{f}}.\left(p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{c_{f}}}f(n)\chi_{0}\right)\in S.

Furthermore, we have
p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g^{i}_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}^{(\mathfrak{c}\setminus\{0\})}
, for every
1\leq i\leq 3
. Therefore,
p_{\xi}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}y_{n}\in S^{3}
. Hence,
S^{3}
is countably compact.
∎

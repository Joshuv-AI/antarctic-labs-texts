Walking on Words (Extended version)
Ian Pratt-Hartmann

Abstract
Any function f f with domain { 1 , … , m } \{1,\dots,m\} and co-domain { 1 , … , n } \{1,\dots,n\} induces a natural map from words of length n n to those of length m m : the i i th letter of the output word ( 1 ≤ i ≤ m 1\leq i\leq m ) is given by the f ⁡ ( i ) f(i) th letter of the input word. We study this map in the case where f f is a surjection satisfying the condition | f ⁡ ( i + 1 ) − f ⁡ ( i ) | ≤ 1 |f(i{+}1){-}f(i)|\leq 1 for 1 ≤ i < m 1\leq i<m . Intuitively, we think of f f as describing a ‘walk’ on a word u u , visiting every position, and yielding a word w w as the sequence of letters encountered en route . If such an f f exists, we say that u u generates w w . Call a word primitive if it is not generated by any word shorter than itself. We show that every word has, up to reversal, a unique primitive generator. Observing that, if a word contains a non-trivial palindrome, it can generate the same word via essentially different walks, we obtain conditions under which, for a chosen pair of walks f f and g g , those walks yield the same word when applied to a given primitive word. Although the original impulse for studying primitive generators comes from their application to decision procedures in logic, we end, by way of further motivation, with an analysis of the primitive generators for certain word sequences defined via morphisms.

Introduction

Take any word over some alphabet, and, if it is non-empty, go to any letter in that word. Now repeat the following any number
of times (possibly zero):
scan the current letter, and print it out;

The motivation for the study of primitive generators comes from the study of the decision problem in (fragments of) first-order logic, in presentations
where the logical variables are taken to be

Call a first-order formula

It was shown in
[

that the problem of determining validity for adjacent formulas is decidable.
A key notion in analysing this fragment is that of an

Let
f^{\prime}\colon[1,m]\rightarrow[1,n]
be a walk. If
1\leq j\leq m

given by

the

f^{\prime}

at

up to and including the

We next proceed to define an operation of

has three forms, depending on whether it occurs at the start, in the middle, or at the end of the walk.
For any

(
1\leq k<m

initial vacillation on

f^{\prime}

over

[1,k{+}1]
to be the walk

f(i)=\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(k{+}1{-}(i{-}1))&\text{if $i\leq k+1$}\\
f^{\prime}(i{-}k)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus

arises by executing the first

in reverse order and then continuing to execute
f^{\prime}
from the second step as normal.
Likewise, we define the

f^{\prime}

over

f^{\prime}(m{-}(i{-}m))&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus

arises by executing

Finally, for any

(

over

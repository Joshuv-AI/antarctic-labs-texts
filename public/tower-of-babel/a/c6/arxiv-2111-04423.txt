1
Introduction

Let
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
\binom{[n]}{k}
be the collection of all its
k
-subsets. For a
k
-graph
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
, the matching number of
\mathcal{F}
, denote by
\nu(\mathcal{F})
, is the maximum number of pairwise disjoint members of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
contain a rainbow matching if there exist
s
pairwise disjoint sets
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
.

One of the most important open problems in extremal set theory is to determine the maximum of
|\mathcal{F}|
subject to
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, which is known as the celebrated Erdős matching conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Erdős Matching Conjecture
[
5
]
)
.

Let
n\geq(s+1)k
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
. If
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\left\{\binom{k(s+1)-1}{k},\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s}{k}\right\}.

(1.1)

The two simple constructions showing that (
1.1
) is best possible (if true) are

\mathcal{E}(n,k,s)=\left\{E\in{[n]\choose k}\colon E\cap[s]\neq\emptyset\right\}\text{ and }{[(s+1)k-1]\choose k}.

The case
s=1
is the classical Erdős-Ko-Rado Theorem
[
15
]
. Erdős and Gallai
[
6
]
proved (
1.1
) for
k=2
. The
k=3
case is settled in
[
10
]
. For general
k
, Erdős proved that (
1.1
) is true and up to isomorphic
\mathcal{E}(n,k,s)
is the only optimal family provided that
n>n_{0}(k,s)
. The bounds for
n_{0}(k,s)
were subsequently improved by Bollobás, Daykin and Erdős
[
3
]
, Huang, Loh and Sudakov
[
14
]
. The current best bounds establish (
1.1
) for
n>(2s+1)k
(
[
9
]
) and for
s>s_{0}
,
n>\frac{5}{3}sk
(
[
11
]
).

A rainbow version of Erdős matching conjecture was proposed by Aharoni, Howardand
[
1
]
and independently Huang, Loh, Sudakov
[
14
]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
1
,
14
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
. If

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{i}|>\max\left\{\binom{ks-1}{k},\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s+1}{k}\right\},

(1.2)

for all
1\leq i\leq s
, then
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
contain a rainbow matching.

Using a junta method, Keller and Lifshitz
[
16
]
showed the validity of (
1.2
) for
n>f(s)k
, where
f(s)
is an unspecified and a very quickly growing function of
s
. In
[
12
]
, Frankl and Kupavskii improved the bound to
12sk(2+\log s)
. By using the absorbing method, Lu, Wang and Yu
[
19
]
proved (
1.2
) for
n\geq 2k(s+1)
and
s>s_{0}(k)
. By applying the concentration inequality for random matchings developed in
[
11
]
, Kupavskii
[
17
]
proved (
1.2
) for
n\geq 3e(s+1)k
and
s>10^{7}
.

Let
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
and let
V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}
be pairwise disjoint sets with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
. Define the direct product
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
as the collection of all subsets
F
of
\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}
with
|F\cap V_{i}|=k_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. By the algebraic method, Frankl
[
8
]
proved an Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem for direct products as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
is intersecting and
2\leq n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
. Then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}.

In this paper, we mainly consider a direct product version of Erdős matching conjecture. We determine the maximum number of edges in a subfamily of the direct product with matching number at most
s
for each
n_{i}
sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\ell,s,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
n_{i}\geq 4\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
. If
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\left[\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}-\binom{n_{i}-s}{k_{i}}\right]\prod_{j\neq i\atop 1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(1.3)

Let
S_{i}
be an
s
-subset of
V_{i}
. Set
\vec{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{\ell})
and
\vec{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell})
. Define

\mathcal{E}_{i}(\vec{\mathstrut n},\vec{\mathstrut k},s)=\left\{E\in\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{j}}{k_{j}}\colon E\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset\right\}.

The constructions
\mathcal{E}_{i}(\vec{\mathstrut n},\vec{\mathstrut k},s)
show that (
1.3
) is best possible.

By the random matching technique developed by Frankl and Kupavskii
[
11
]
, we obtain a condition on the number of edges for a family of direct products to contain a rainbow matching for each
n_{i}
sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\ell,s,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
n_{i}\geq 8\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
. If
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free, then there exists
1\leq t\leq s
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\left[\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}-\binom{n_{i}-s+1}{k_{i}}\right]\prod_{j\neq i\atop 1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(1.4)

The constructions
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{2}=\dots=\mathcal{F}_{s}=\mathcal{E}_{i}(\vec{\mathstrut n},\vec{\mathstrut k},s)
show that (
1.4
) is best possible.

2
Extremal problem for matchings on direct products

Frankl and the first author
[
13
]
proved an upper bound on the sum of sizes of overlapping families. In this section, by a probabilistic argument we transfer it to an upper bound on the size of
\mathcal{F}\subset\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with matching number at most
s
. By using this upper bound, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{m}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
and
m\geq s+1
. If one cannot choose pairwise disjoint edges from
s+1
distinct families of
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{m}
, we say these families are
s
-overlapping.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{m}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
be
s
-overlapping families with
n\geq(s+1)k
and
m\geq s+1
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\leq\max\left\{s\binom{n}{k},ms\binom{n-1}{k-1}\right\}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we prove the following lemma via a probabilistic argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
and
s+1\leq n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
. If
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\frac{(s+1)k_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}.

Proof.

Let
m=\lfloor\frac{n_{2}}{k_{2}}\rfloor
and let
B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{m}
be an
m
-matching chosen from
\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
uniformly at random. Define

\mathcal{F}(B_{i})=\left\{A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}\colon A\cup B_{i}\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

for
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
,
\mathcal{F}(B_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{F}(B_{m})
are
s
-overlapping. Note that
m\geq s+1
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{F}(B_{i})|\leq\max\left\{s\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}},ms\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}\right\}.

n_{1}/k_{1}\leq n_{2}/k_{2}
implies that
n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\lfloor n_{2}/k_{2}\rfloor+1=m+1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{F}(B_{i})|\leq(m+1)s\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}.

(2.1)

Taking the expectation on both sides of
(
2.1
)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}|\mathcal{F}(B_{i})|\leq(m+1)s\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}.

(2.2)

Since
B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{m}
are chosen from
\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
uniformly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}|\mathcal{F}(B_{i})|=\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}|\mathcal{F}(B)|\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}}=\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}}.

(2.3)

By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)s\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}.

Since
m=\lfloor\frac{n_{2}}{k_{2}}\rfloor>s
, it follows that

\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)s<s+1

and the lemma follows.

The following operation, called shifting, was invented by Erdős, Ko and Rado
[
4
]
and further developed by Frankl
[
7
]
. Let
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
. Define

S_{ij}(\mathcal{F})=\{S_{ij}(F)\colon F\in\mathcal{F}\},

where

S_{ij}(F)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(F\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\},&j\in F,i\notin F\text{ and }(F\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}\notin\mathcal{F};\\[5.0pt]
F,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It is well known (cf.
[
7
]
) that shifting does not increase the matching number.

Let us recall some properties of the shifting operator. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
V_{i}=\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2},\dots,v_{i,n_{i}}\}

for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Define a partial order
\prec
on
\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}
such that

v_{i,1}\prec v_{i,2}\prec\dots\prec v_{i,n_{i}}

for each
i
and vertices from different parts are incomparable. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
is called shifted if
S_{ab}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}
for all
a,b\in\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}
with
a\prec b
. Frankl
[
7
]
proved that repeated application of shifting to any family eventually produces a shifted family.

Let
k=k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{\ell}
. For two different edges
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}
and
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k}\}
in
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
, define
A\prec B
iff there exists a permutation
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{k}
of
[k]
such that
a_{j}\prec b_{\sigma_{j}}
or
a_{j}=b_{\sigma_{j}}
for
j=1,\dots,k
. It is shown in
[
7
]
that if
\mathcal{F}
is a shifted family,
A\prec B
and
B\in\mathcal{F}
then
A\in\mathcal{F}
as well.

In the proof of the following lemma, we need the following inequality, which were already used in
[
3
]
. For
n\geq(2s+1)k
,

\displaystyle\binom{n-s}{k}/\binom{n}{k}\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{n-k}\right)^{k}\geq 1-\frac{ks}{n-k}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(2.4)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\ell,s,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
n_{i}\geq 4\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
has matching number at most
s
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}-\min_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}-x_{i}}{k_{i}}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{F}
is shifted. We prove the lemma by induction on
s
. The case
s=1
is verified in Theorem
1.1
. Assume that the lemma holds for the case
s-1
. Let
V_{i}=\{v_{i,j}\colon j\in[n_{i}]\}
. For each
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}
, define

\mathcal{F}(x)=\{F\setminus\{x\}\colon x\in F\in\mathcal{F}\}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{F}(\overline{x})=\{F\colon x\notin F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.

\nu(\mathcal{F}(\overline{v_{i,1}}))\leq s-1
for some
i\in[\ell]
.

By the induction hypothesis, we have

|\mathcal{F}(\overline{v_{i,1}})|\leq\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}.

Then,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{F}(\overline{v_{i,1}})|+|\mathcal{F}({v_{i,1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}+\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}-x_{j}}{k_{j}}

and the lemma holds.

Case 2.

\nu(\mathcal{F}(\overline{v_{i,1}}))=s
for all
i\in[\ell]
.

Let
M
be an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}(\overline{v_{i,1}})
and let
U
be the set of vertices covered by
M
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, each edge in
\mathcal{F}(v_{i,1})
intersects
U
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}({v_{i,1}})|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-sk_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}},

where
n_{j}^{\prime}=n_{j},k_{j}^{\prime}=k_{j}
for all
j\neq i
and
n_{i}^{\prime}=n_{i}-1,k_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}-1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}({v_{i,1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\left[\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}-\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-sk_{p}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-sk_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}sk_{p}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-1}{k_{p}^{\prime}-1}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-sk_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}sk_{p}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-1}{k_{p}^{\prime}-1}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}k_{p}^{\prime}}{n_{p}^{\prime}}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}k_{p}^{\prime}}{n_{p}^{\prime}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}.

Note that
\frac{k_{i}^{\prime}}{n_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{k_{i}-1}{n_{i}-1}\leq\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}
and
\binom{n_{i}^{\prime}}{k_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}({v_{i,1}})|\leq\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(2.5)

Let
M^{\prime}
be an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
and let
U^{\prime}
be the set of vertices covered by
M^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is shifted, we may assume that

U^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{v_{i,1},\dots,v_{i,sk_{i}}\}.

For each
i\in[\ell]
and
j\in[sk_{i}]
, define

\mathcal{F}[v_{i,j}]=\left\{T\in\bigsqcup_{p=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{p}^{\prime}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}\colon T\cup\{v_{i,j}\}\in\mathcal{F}\right\},

where
V_{p}^{\prime}=V_{p},\ k_{p}^{\prime}=k_{p}
for
p\neq i
and
V_{i}^{\prime}=V_{i}\setminus\{v_{i,1},\dots,v_{i,j}\},\ k_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}-1
. By shiftedness, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[v_{i,1}]\supseteq\mathcal{F}[v_{i,2}]\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathcal{F}[v_{i,sk_{i}}]

(2.6)

for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell
.

We claim that
\nu(\mathcal{F}[v_{i,s+1}])\leq s
for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell
. Otherwise, let
E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{s+1}
be a matching of size
s+1
in
\mathcal{F}[v_{i,s+1}]
. By (
2.6
),
E_{1}\cup\{v_{i,1}\},E_{2}\cup\{v_{i,2}\},\dots,E_{s+1}\cup\{v_{i,s+1}\}
is a matching of size
s+1
in
\mathcal{F}
, a contradiction. Thus
\nu(\mathcal{F}[v_{i,s+1}])\leq s
for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell
. Note that
n_{i}\geq(s+1)k_{i}
implies that
\frac{n_{i}-s-1}{k_{i}-1}\geq\frac{n_{i}}{k_{i}}\geq\frac{n_{1}}{k_{1}}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we get for each
i

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}[v_{i,s+1}]|\leq\frac{(s+1)k_{1}}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{i}-s-1}{k_{i}-1}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\leq\frac{(s+1)k_{1}k_{i}}{n_{1}n_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(2.7)

Since
M^{\prime}
is a largest matching in
\mathcal{F}
,
U^{\prime}
is a vertex cover of
\mathcal{F}
. By (
2.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{sk_{i}}|\mathcal{F}[v_{i,j}]|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(s|\mathcal{F}[v_{i,1}]|+(sk_{i}-s)|\mathcal{F}[v_{i,s+1}]|\right).

(2.8)

Substitute (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) into (
2.8
), we arrive at

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(s\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}+s(k_{i}-1)\frac{(s+1)k_{1}k_{i}}{n_{1}n_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq s\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\cdot\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}+\frac{2sk_{1}k_{i}^{2}}{n_{1}n_{i}}\right).

Since
n_{i}\geq 4\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
, we have

\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{sk_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\leq\frac{1}{4\ell},\mbox{ and }\frac{2sk_{i}^{2}}{n_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{2\ell^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4\ell}.

Moreover,
\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}
. Hence, by inequality (
2.4
) we obtain that

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\frac{s}{2}\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\leq s\binom{n_{1}-s}{k_{1}-1}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\leq\left(\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}-\binom{n_{1}-s}{k_{1}}\right)\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

and this completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
2.3
, we are left to show that

\min_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}-x_{i}}{k_{i}}=\min_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\binom{n_{i}-s}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

Set

f(x)=\binom{n_{i}-x}{k_{i}}\binom{n_{j}-c+x}{k_{j}}.

Computing its derivative, we have

f^{\prime}(x)=\binom{n_{i}-x}{k_{i}}\binom{n_{j}-c+x}{k_{j}}\left[\sum_{q=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{1}{n_{j}-c+x-q}-\sum_{p=0}^{k_{i}-1}\frac{1}{n_{i}-x-p}\right].

Set

g(x)=\sum_{q=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{1}{n_{j}-c+x-q}-\sum_{p=0}^{k_{i}-1}\frac{1}{n_{i}-x-p}.

It is easy to see that
g(x)
is a decreasing function in the range
[k_{j}-n_{j}+c,n_{i}-k_{i}]
. Moreover,

g(k_{j}-n_{j}+c)=\sum_{q=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{1}{k_{j}-q}-\sum_{p=0}^{k_{i}-1}\frac{1}{n_{i}-p+n_{j}-k_{j}-c}>1-\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}-k_{i}}>0

and

g(n_{i}-k_{i})=\sum_{q=0}^{k_{j}-1}\frac{1}{n_{j}-c+n_{i}-k_{i}-q}-\sum_{p=0}^{k_{i}-1}\frac{1}{k_{i}-p}<\frac{k_{j}}{n_{j}-k_{j}}-1<0.

Let
x_{0}
be the unique zero of
g(x)
in the range
[k_{j}-n_{j}+c,n_{i}-k_{i}]
. It follows that
f(x)
is increasing in the range
[k_{j}-n_{j},x_{0}]
and decreasing in the range
[x_{0},n_{i}-k_{i}]
. Then we have

\min_{0\leq x\leq c}f(x)=\min\left\{\binom{n_{i}-c}{k_{i}}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}},\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}\binom{n_{j}-c}{k_{j}}\right\}.

Thus,

\min_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}-x_{i}}{k_{i}}=\min_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\binom{n_{i}-s}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

and the theorem follows.

Extremal problem for rainbow matchings on direct products

Frankl and Kupavskii

]
proved a concentration inequality for the intersections of a

-uniform hypergraph and a random matching. In this section, by the concentration property of the bipartite graph defined on two random matchings, we establish a condition on the number of edges for a family of direct products to contain a rainbow matching.

Aharoni and Howard

provided a tight condition on the number of edges for a family of bipartite graphs to contain a rainbow matching.

Lemma 3.1

(
[

)

Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s}
be bipartite graphs with the same partite sets
L
and
R

We recall that the Kneser graph

\binom{[n]}{k}

Lemma 3.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

The eigenvalues of the adjacent matrix of
KG(n,k)
are
(-1)^{i}\binom{n-k-1}{k-i}
with multiplicity
\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n}{i-1}
,
i=0,1,\dots,k
.
(
Here
\binom{n}{-1}=0
.
)

We also need the following result due to Alon and Chung
[
2
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a
d
-regular graph on
n

A
be its adjacent matrix. Let
d=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq-d

\lambda=\max\{|\lambda_{2}|,|\lambda_{n}|\}
. For

By considering the bipartite graph defined on two random matchings, we prove the following lemma, which is key to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\ell,s,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
3s\leq n_{1}/k_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. If
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free, then there exists
1\leq t\leq s
such that

|\mathcal{F}_{t}|<\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

Proof.

Suppose that

|\mathcal{F}_{t}|=\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

for all
1\leq t\leq s
. Set
m=\lfloor n_{1}/k_{1}\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\}
be an
m
-matching chosen from
\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}
uniformly at random and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\}
be an
m
-matching chosen from
\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
uniformly at random. For each
t=1,\dots,s
, we construct a bipartite graph
G_{t}
with partite sets
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
where we have an edge
(A_{i},B_{j})
iff
A_{i}\cup B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Note that a rainbow matching in
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s}
gives a rainbow matching in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
. Thus, by Lemma
3.1
and the union bound, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\Pr(e(G_{t})\leq(s-1)m)<\frac{1}{s}

for each
1\leq t\leq s
. Let
X=e(G_{t})
and
X_{ij}
be the indicator function of the event that
A_{i}\cup B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Clearly, we have

X=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}X_{ij}.

Set
\alpha=|\mathcal{F}_{t}|/\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}
. Take expectation on both sides, we obtain that

\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X)=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\Pr[A_{i}\cup B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{t}|}{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}}=\alpha m^{2}.

Since
n_{1}/k_{1}\geq 3s
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha m=\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{k_{1}}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdot\frac{n_{1}-k_{1}}{k_{1}}\geq 6s\left(1-\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)\geq 6s-2.

(3.1)

By the Chebyshev inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\Pr(X\leq(s-1)m)\leq\Pr\left(X\leq\frac{\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X)}{6}\right)\leq\Pr\left(|X-\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X)|\geq\frac{5}{6}\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X)\right)\leq\frac{36}{25}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Var}(X)}{\alpha^{2}m^{4}}.

(3.2)

Now we need to give an upper bound for
\mathrm{Var}(X)
. For
1\leq i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}\leq m
, define

\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)-\alpha^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X^{2})-\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[m]}\left(\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}[X_{ij}X_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]-\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}[X_{ij}]\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}[X_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[m]}\left(\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)-\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)\Pr\left(A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[m]}\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}).

(3.3)

We derive upper bounds on
\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
for
i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[m]
by distinguishing four cases.

Case 1.

i\neq i^{\prime}
and
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

Let
H
be a graph on the vertex set
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}

and with the edge set formed by pairs of disjoint sets in
\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
. Let
\lambda
be the second largest absolute eigenvalue of adjacent matrix of
H
. Frankl
[
8
]
observed that the adjacency matrix of
H
is the Kronecker product of the adjacency matrices of the Kneser graphs. By Lemma
3.2
, it follows that

\lambda=\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}-k_{1}}\prod_{1\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}-k_{p}}{k_{p}}.

Since
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
j\neq j^{\prime}
, we have
(A_{i}\cup B_{j})\cap(A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}})=\emptyset
. Note that
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
and
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
are chosen uniformly at random. The probability that
A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
equals to the probability that a uniform chosen edge from
E(H)
is an edge of the induce subgraph
H[\mathcal{F}_{t}]
, that is,

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big)=\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}])}{e(H)}.

Let
D
be the degree of
H
and
N
be the number of vertices of
H
. It is easy to see that
D=\prod_{1\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}-k_{p}}{k_{p}}
and
N=\prod_{1\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}
. Since
H
is
D
-regular, we have
e(H)=\frac{DN}{2}
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq\left|\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}])}{e(H)}-\alpha^{2}\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{D}\alpha(1-\alpha)\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}-k_{1}}\alpha.

(3.4)

Case 2.

i\neq i^{\prime}
and
j=j^{\prime}
.

For
B\in\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
, define

\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)=\left\{K\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}\colon K\cup B\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}
. Let
H
be the Kneser graph on the vertex set
\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}
.
Since
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
j=j^{\prime}
, the event that
A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
is equivalent to the event that a uniform chosen edge of
H
is an edge in
H[\mathcal{F}_{t}(B_{j})]
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}\Pr\left(A_{i},A_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)|B_{j}=B\right)\Pr\left(B_{j}=B\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)])}{e(H)}\cdot\Pr\left(B_{j}=B\right).

(3.5)

Set
\alpha(B)=|\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)|/|\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}|
.
Let
D
be the degree of
H
and
N
be the number of vertices in
H
. Clearly,
D=\binom{n_{1}-k_{1}}{k_{1}}\mbox{ and }N=\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}
.
Let
\lambda
be the second largest absolute eigenvalue of adjacent matrix of
H
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have
\lambda=\binom{n_{1}-k_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}
. By lemma
3.3
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)])}{e(H)}-\alpha^{2}(B)\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{D}\alpha(B)(1-\alpha(B))\leq\alpha(B)(1-\alpha(B)).

(3.6)

By (
3
) and (
3.6
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)-\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}\left(\alpha(B)(1-\alpha(B))+\alpha^{2}(B)\right)-\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}\alpha(B)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)|}{\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}\sum_{B\in\bigsqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}}|\mathcal{F}_{t}(B)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{t}|}{\prod_{1\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\alpha

(3.7)

Case 3.

i=i^{\prime}
and
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

Similarly, let
H
be a graph on the vertex set
\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}

and with the edge set formed by pairs of disjoint sets in
\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
. Let
\lambda
be the second largest absolute eigenvalue of adjacent matrix of
H
. Since the adjacency matrix of
H
is the Kronecker product of the adjacency matrices of the Kneser graphs. By Lemma
3.2
, it follows that

\lambda=\frac{k_{2}}{n_{2}-k_{2}}\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}-k_{p}}{k_{p}}.

For each
A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}
, define
\mathcal{F}_{t}(A)=\left\{K\in\sqcup_{i=2}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}\colon A\cup K\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}
.
Set
\alpha(A)=|\mathcal{F}_{t}(A)|/\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}
.
Let
D
be the degree of
H
and let
N
be the number of vertices in
H
. Clearly,

D=\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}-k_{p}}{k_{p}}
and
N=\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\sum_{A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}}\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}(A)])}{e(H)}\cdot\Pr\left(A_{i}=A\right).

By Lemma
3.3
, we have for
A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}

\left|\frac{e(H[\mathcal{F}_{t}(A)])}{e(H)}-\alpha^{2}(A)\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{D}\alpha(A)(1-\alpha(A))\leq\alpha(A)(1-\alpha(A)).

Thus,

\displaystyle\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)-\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}}\sum_{A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}}\left(\alpha(A)(1-\alpha(A))+\alpha^{2}(A)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}}\cdot\sum_{A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}}\alpha(A)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}}\sum_{A\in\binom{V_{1}}{k_{1}}}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{t}(A)|}{\prod_{2\leq p\leq\ell}\binom{n_{p}}{k_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\alpha.

(3.8)

Case 4.

i=i^{\prime}
and
j=j^{\prime}
.

In this case,
\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j},A_{i^{\prime}}\cup B_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\Pr\left(A_{i}\cup B_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)=\alpha
. Hence,

\displaystyle\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=\alpha-\alpha^{2}\leq\alpha.

(3.9)

Substitute (
3.4
), (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) into (
3
), we have

\mathrm{Var}(X)=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[m]}\theta(i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}-k_{1}}\alpha m^{2}(m-1)^{2}+2\alpha m^{3}+\alpha m^{2}.

Note that
m=\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{k_{1}}\rfloor\leq\frac{n_{1}}{k_{1}}
implies
\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}-k_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{m-1}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X)\leq\frac{1}{m-1}\alpha m^{2}(m-1)^{2}+2\alpha m^{3}+\alpha m^{2}=3\alpha m^{3}.

(3.10)

Substitute (
3.10
) into (
3.2
), we arrive at

\Pr(X\leq(s-1)m)\leq\frac{36}{25}\cdot\frac{3\alpha m^{3}}{\alpha^{2}m^{4}}=\frac{36\cdot 3}{25\alpha m}.

Moreover, (
3.1
) implies that
\alpha m\geq 6s-2\geq 5s
. Thus, we obtain that

\Pr(X\leq(s-1)m)\leq\frac{36\times 3}{25\alpha m}\leq\frac{36\times 3}{25\times 5}\cdot\frac{1}{s}<\frac{1}{s},

and the lemma follows.

The following lemma shows that the shifting operator preserves the rainbow matching free property of a family of uniform hypergraphs, which is due to Huang, Loh and Sudakov
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.5

(Huang-Loh-Sudakov
[
14
]
)
.

If the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
are rainbow matching free and
i,j\in[n]
with
i<j
, then
S_{ij}(\mathcal{F}_{1}),...,S_{ij}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
are still rainbow matching free.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\ell,s,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}
be integers with
n_{i}\geq 8\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}\subseteq\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{i}}{k_{i}}
with
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
. If
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free, then there exists
1\leq t\leq s
such that

|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}-\min_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s-1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}-x_{i}}{k_{i}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
n_{1}/k_{1}\leq n_{2}/k_{2}\leq\dots\leq n_{\ell}/k_{\ell}
. By Lemma
3.5
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is shifted for all
i=1,\dots,s
. We prove the theorem by induction on
s
. The case
s=1
is trivial. Assume that the lemma holds for the case
s-1
and we have to show the lemma holds for the case
s
.

Let
V_{i}=\{v_{i,j}\colon j\in[n_{i}]\}
. For every
x\in\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}
and
1\leq t\leq s
, define

\mathcal{F}_{t}(x)=\{F\setminus\{x\}\colon x\in F\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{F}_{t}(\overline{x})=\{F\colon x\notin F\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\}.

We distinguish into two cases.

Case 1.
There exist
r\in[s]
and
i\in[\ell]
such that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{r-1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\mathcal{F}_{r+1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,

\mathcal{F}_{s}(\overline{v_{i,1}})
are rainbow matching free.

By the induction hypothesis, there exists
t\in[s]\setminus\{r\}
such that

|\mathcal{F}_{t}(\overline{v_{i,1}})|\leq\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}.

Then,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{F}_{t}(\overline{v_{i,1}})|+|\mathcal{F}_{t}({v_{i,1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}+\binom{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{y_{1}+\dots+y_{\ell}=s-1}\binom{n_{i}-1-y_{i}}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}-y_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}-\min_{x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}-x_{j}}{k_{j}}

and the lemma holds.

Case 2.

\mathcal{F}_{1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{r-1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\mathcal{F}_{r+1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}(\overline{v_{i,1}})
contain a rainbow matching for all
r\in[s]
and
i\in[\ell]
.

Let
M
be a rainbow matching in
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{r-1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\mathcal{F}_{r+1}(\overline{v_{i,1}}),\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}(\overline{v_{i,1}})\right\}
and let
U
be the set of vertices covered by
M
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free, each edge of
\mathcal{F}_{r}(v_{i,1})
intersects
U
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{r}({v_{i,1}})|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-(s-1)k_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}},

where
n_{j}^{\prime}=n_{j},k_{j}^{\prime}=k_{j}
for all
j\neq i
and
n_{i}^{\prime}=n_{i}-1,k_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}-1
. Then,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{r}({v_{i,1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\left[\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}-\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-(s-1)k_{p}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-(s-1)k_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}(s-1)k_{p}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-1}{k_{p}^{\prime}-1}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}-(s-1)k_{j}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}(s-1)k_{p}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}-1}{k_{p}^{\prime}-1}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{(s-1)k_{p}k_{p}^{\prime}}{n_{p}^{\prime}}\binom{n_{p}^{\prime}}{k_{p}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\leq p-1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j\geq p+1}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{(s-1)k_{p}k_{p}^{\prime}}{n_{p}^{\prime}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}.

Note that
\frac{k_{i}^{\prime}}{n_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{k_{i}-1}{n_{i}-1}\leq\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}
and
\binom{n_{i}^{\prime}}{k_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\binom{n_{i}}{k_{i}}
. It follows that for every
i\in[\ell]
and
r\in[s]

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{r}({v_{i,1}})|\leq\frac{(s-1)k_{i}}{n_{i}}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{k_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(3.11)

For
j\in[\ell]
, let
S_{j}=\{v_{j,1},\dots,v_{j,s}\}
and let
V_{j}^{\prime}=V_{j}\setminus S_{j}
. For every
r\in[s]
and
i\in[\ell]
, define

\mathcal{F}_{r}[v_{i,s}]=\left\{E\in\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}^{\prime}}\colon E\cup\{v_{i,s}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{r}\right\},

where
k_{j}^{\prime}=k_{j}
for all
j\neq i
and
k_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}-1
.
Define

\mathcal{F}_{r}^{*}[v_{i,s}]=\left\{E\cup\{u\}\colon E\in\mathcal{F}_{r}[v_{i,s}],\ u\in V_{i}^{\prime}\setminus E\right\}.

Claim 1.
There exists
t\in[s]
such that for all
i\in[\ell]

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i,s}]|\leq\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}-s}{k_{j}}.

(3.12)

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that for every
t\in[s]
there is an
i_{t}\in[\ell]
such that

|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i_{t},s}]|>\frac{6sk_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}-s}{k_{j}}.

Note that

\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i_{t},s}]\subseteq\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{V_{j}^{\prime}}{k_{j}}.

By Lemma
3.4
, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{*}[v_{i_{1},s}],\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}^{*}[v_{i_{s},s}]
contain a rainbow matching. Let
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}^{*}[v_{i_{1},s}],\dots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}^{*}[v_{i_{s},s}]
be such a matching. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i_{t},s}]
, we see that there exist
E_{1}\subset F_{1},\dots,E_{s}\subset F_{s}
such that
E_{1}\cup\{v_{i_{1},s}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},E_{2}\cup\{v_{i_{2},s}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}\dots,E_{s}\cup\{v_{i_{s},s}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
. Note that
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are all shifted. It follows that
E_{1}\cup\{v_{i_{1},1}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{1},E_{2}\cup\{v_{i_{2},2}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{2},\dots,E_{s}\cup\{v_{i_{s},s}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
is a rainbow matching, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free.

By Claim 1, there exists
t\in[s]
such that (
3.12
) holds for all
i\in[\ell]
. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i,s}]
, we see that for each
E\in\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,s}]
there are
|V_{i}^{\prime}\setminus E|=(n_{i}-s)-(k_{i}-1)
choices of
u
such that
E\cup\{u\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i,s}]
. Moreover, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i,s}]
we have
|F\cap V_{i}^{\prime}|=k_{i}
. It follows that at most
k_{i}
sets in
\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,s}]
are contained in
F
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{*}[v_{i,s}]|\geq\frac{n_{i}-s-k_{i}+1}{k_{i}}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,s}]|.

(3.13)

By (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain that for all
i=1,2,\ldots,\ell
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,s}]|\leq\frac{6sk_{1}k_{i}}{n_{1}(n_{i}-s-k_{i}+1)}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

(3.14)

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\mathcal{F}_{t+1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
contain a rainbow matching. Let
M^{\prime}
be such a matching and let
U^{\prime}
be the set of vertices covered by
M^{\prime}
. By shiftedness, we may assume that

U^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\{v_{i,1},\dots,v_{i,(s-1)k_{i}}\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{s}
are rainbow matching free, it follows that every edge of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
intersects
U^{\prime}
. Thus,

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{(s-1)k_{i}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,j}].

By shiftedness, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,1}]\supseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,2}]\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,(s-1)k_{i}}]

for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(s-1)|\mathcal{F}_{t}[{v_{i,1}}]|+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(k_{i}-1)(s-1)|\mathcal{F}_{t}[v_{i,s}]|.

(3.15)

Substitute (
3.11
) and (
3.14
) into (
3.15
), we arrive at

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Bigg((s-1)^{2}\Big(\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{k_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\Big)\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}+6s(s-1)\frac{k_{i}(k_{i}-1)}{n_{i}-s-k_{i}+1}\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\Bigg).

Since
\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}
, we have

|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Bigg((s-1)^{2}\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{k_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}+6s(s-1)\frac{k_{i}(k_{i}-1)}{n_{i}-s-k_{i}+1}\Bigg).

Since
n_{i}\geq 8\ell^{2}k_{i}^{2}s
and
\ell\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big((s-1)^{2}\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\frac{k_{p}^{2}}{n_{p}}\Big)=\frac{(s-1)^{2}\ell^{2}}{8\ell^{2}s}\leq\frac{s-1}{8},

and

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{6s(s-1)k_{i}(k_{i}-1)}{n_{i}-s-k_{i}+1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{6s(s-1)k_{i}(k_{i}-1)}{16\ell k_{i}(k_{i}-1)s+16\ell k_{i}s-s-k_{i}+1}\leq\frac{3(s-1)}{8}.

Moreover,
\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\leq\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}
. Hence, by inequality (
2.4
) we obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(s-1)}{2}\binom{n_{1}-1}{k_{1}-1}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq(s-1)\binom{n_{1}-s+1}{k_{1}-1}\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{n_{1}}{k_{1}}-\binom{n_{1}-s+1}{k_{1}}\right)\prod_{j=2}^{\ell}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}},

and this completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

As the same to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we have

\min_{x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}=s}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{n_{i}-x_{i}}{k_{i}}=\min_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\binom{n_{i}-s}{k_{i}}\prod_{j\neq i}\binom{n_{j}}{k_{j}}.

Thus, Lemma
3.6
implies the theorem.

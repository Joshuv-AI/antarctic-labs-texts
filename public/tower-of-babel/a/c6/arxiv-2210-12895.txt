An inf-sup approach to semigroup wellposedness for a compressible flow–incompressible fluid interactive PDE system
George Avalos, Paula Egging

Abstract
This work presents qualitative and numerical results on a system of partial differential equations (PDEs) which models certain fluid-fluid interaction dynamics. This system models a compressible fluid in a domain Ω + ⊂ ℝ 2 {\Omega^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} , coupled to an incompressible fluid modeled by Stokes flow in domain Ω − ⊂ ℝ 2 {\Omega^{-}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} , with the strong coupling implemented through certain boundary conditions on the shared interface, Γ \Gamma . The wellposedness of this system is established by means of constructing for it a semigroup generator representation. This representation is accomplished by eliminating one of the pressure variables via identifying it as the solution of a certain boundary value problem, while the wellposedness is established via a nonstandard usage of the Babuska-Brezzi Theorem. In later sections, we demonstrate how the earlier constructive proof of wellposedness naturally lends itself to a certain finite element method (FEM), by which to numerically approximate solutions of the given coupled PDE system. This FEM is provided with error estimates and associated rates of convergence. Key terms: Fluid-Fluid Interaction, Strongly Continuous Semigroups, Inf-Sup Approaches, Finite Element Interpolation



1
Introduction

In this work, we consider a system of partial differential equations (PDEs) which has practical applications in modeling various fluid-fluid interactions which occur in nature, e.g. the interaction between the ocean and atmosphere, two different fronts of air, etc. A proper modeling of such phenomena can aid in the understanding of current dynamics in the climate, as well as to project models of future climate so as to aid in energy planning (see
[
13
]
). Of course, for fully coupled climate models, the monolithic solution, which treats the entire system as a single unified system, is desired. However, for practical considerations, the full model is always partitioned into submodels, with each component simulated separately. The coupling of these components is achieved through the exchange of boundary condition information in such a way that certain interface conditions are satisfied.
In such models, it is natural to assume the atmosphere (gaseous) can be modeled by a compressible fluid, while the ocean (water) is modeled by an incompressible (divergence-free) fluid (see
[
18
]
).
The focus of Sections 2-5 is on the abstract mathematical results demonstrating the Hadamard wellposedness of such a fluid-fluid interactions system. Section 6 describes how the proof method employed in previous sections lends itself to the derivation of a numerical method to generate approximate solutions to the given PDE system.

4
Main Result

With the elimination of the

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\

\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u^{+}}+\text{div}\,\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})-\nabla p^{+}\\

from whence we obtain

\nabla\pi_{0}^{-}=-G_{1}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{-}-G_{2}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{+}-G_{3}p^{+}_{0}
.

With
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
defined as such, we now present the main result. That is, we demonstrate semigroup wellposedness for

\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, the proof of which is based on the classic Lumer-Phillips Theorem.

Theorem 1

(i)

The operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is maximal dissipative. Therefore, by the Lumer-Phillips Theorem, it generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

Let
\lambda>0
and
[\mathbf{f},g,\mathbf{h}]\in\mathcal{H}
be given. (By part (i), there exists
[\mathbf{u^{+}},p^{+},\mathbf{u^{-}}]\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
which solves (
40
).) Then
\mathbf{u^{-}}
and
p^{-}
can be characterized as the solution to the variational system (
99
). The remaining unknown terms can then be characterized by

\displaystyle\mathbf{u^{+}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{\lambda}(\mathbf{u^{-}})+\tilde{\mu}([\mathbf{f},g]^{T})

\displaystyle p^{+}

\displaystyle=q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{u^{-}})+\tilde{q}([\mathbf{f},g]^{T}),

where
[\mu_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}]
are defined in (
52
) and
[\tilde{\mu},\tilde{q}]
are defined in (
67
).

5
Proof of Theorem 1

5.1
Dissipativity

In this section, we aim to show
\mathcal{R}e(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})_{H}\leq 0
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{H}
. However, due to the non-zero ambient flow
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathcal{A}
as
defined is not dissipative. Therefore, we introduce the bounded perturbation
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
of the generator
\mathcal{A}
:

\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}-\frac{\text{div}(\mathbf{U})}{2}\begin{bmatrix}I&0&0\\
0&I&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\,\,\,\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).

(31)

With this
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, the standard perturbation result in
[
10
]
can be applied, yielding semigroup generation for the original
\mathcal{A}
of (
29
). So we aim to show that
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is maximal dissipative.

Considering the inner-product for the state-space
\mathcal{H}
, for
\mathbf{v}=[\mathbf{u^{+}},p^{+},\mathbf{u^{-}}]^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}+(\text{div}\,\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\nabla p^{+},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}),p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}+(\Delta\mathbf{u^{-}},\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}-(\nabla p^{-},\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}.

(32)

Applying Green’s Theorem and the fact that
\mathbf{u^{+}}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}\setminus\Gamma}=0
to several terms, we have

•

For the second term,
(\text{div}\,\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}=-(\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}+\langle\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})\vec{\nu},\mathbf{u^{+}}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

•

For the third term, we have
-(\nabla p^{+},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}=(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}-\langle p^{+},\mathbf{u^{+}}\cdot\vec{\nu}\rangle_{\Gamma}
.

•

For the eighth term,
(\Delta\mathbf{u^{-}},\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}=-(\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}},\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}-\left\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{u^{-}}}{\partial\vec{\nu}},\mathbf{u^{-}}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}
, where we subtract, not add, the boundary term since
\vec{\nu}
points
into

\Omega^{-}
.

•

For the ninth term, we have
-(\nabla p^{-},\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}=(p^{-},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{-}}))_{\Omega^{-}}+\langle p^{-}\vec{\nu},\mathbf{u^{-}}\rangle_{\Gamma}
, again adding the boundary term, not subtracting, because
\vec{\nu}
points
into

{\Omega^{-}}
.

Substituting these in yields

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}+(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle+\langle\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})\vec{\nu}-p^{+}\vec{\nu},\mathbf{u^{+}}\rangle_{\Gamma}-(\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}),p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle-(\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}},\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}})_{\Omega^{-}}+(p^{-},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{-}}))_{\Omega^{-}}-\left\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{u^{-}}}{\partial\vec{\nu}}-p^{-}\vec{\nu},\mathbf{u^{-}}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}.

(33)

Noting the boundary conditions in (
3
), the boundary terms here cancel out. Furthermore,

\displaystyle(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}-(\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}),p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle=(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}-\overline{(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}}

\displaystyle=2i\text{Im}(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}.

Also, by virtue of the
\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{-}})=0
condition, the
(p^{-},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{-}}))_{\Omega^{-}}
term drops out.

So we have

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle+2i\text{Im}(p^{+},\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle-(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}-\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})p^{+},p^{+})_{\Omega^{+}}-||\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}}||^{2}_{\Omega^{-}}.

(34)

For further simplification, now note that for functions
q,\tilde{q}\in L^{2}(\Omega^{+})
and with
\vec{\nu}=[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]^{T}
,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q,\tilde{q})_{{\Omega^{+}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{+}}(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q)\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\Omega^{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{+}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}U_{1}\\
U_{2}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}q\\
D_{2}q\end{bmatrix}\right)\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\Omega^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{+}}(U_{j}D_{j}q)\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\Omega^{+}\text{ (next applying Green's Theorem)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega^{+}}U_{j}q\textbf{$\nu$}_{j}\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\partial\Omega^{+}-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{+}}qD_{j}(U_{j}\bar{\tilde{q}})\,d\Omega^{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega^{+}}\cancelto{0\text{ since }\mathbf{U}\cdot\vec{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega^{+}}=0}{(\mathbf{U}\cdot\textbf{$\nu$})}q\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\partial\Omega^{+}

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{+}}q\bar{\tilde{q}}DjU_{j}\,d\Omega^{+}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{+}}qU_{j}D_{j}\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\Omega^{+}\right)\text{ (product rule)}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{+}}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})(q\bar{\tilde{q}})\,d\Omega^{+}-\int_{\Omega^{+}}q\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\bar{\tilde{q}}\,d\Omega^{+}.

Thus, if
q=\tilde{q}
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q,q)_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle=-(\text{div}(\mathbf{U}),|q|^{2})_{\Omega^{+}}-(q,\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q)_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle=-(\text{div}(\mathbf{U}),|q|^{2})_{\Omega^{+}}-\overline{(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q,q)}_{\Omega^{+}}.

(35)

So adding
\overline{(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q,q)}_{\Omega^{+}}
to both sides, we have

2\mathcal{R}e(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q,q)_{\Omega^{+}}=-(\text{div}(\mathbf{U}),|q|^{2})_{\Omega^{+}}.

(36)

Similarly, for
\mathbf{u^{+}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})
, we have

2\mathcal{R}e(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u^{+}},\mathbf{u^{+}})_{\Omega^{+}}=-(\text{div}(\mathbf{U}),|\mathbf{u^{+}}|^{2})_{\Omega^{+}}.

(37)

Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle(\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{{\Omega^{+}}}

\displaystyle=(2\nu\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}})+\tilde{\lambda}[I_{2}\cdot\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}})]I_{2},\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{{\Omega^{+}}}

\displaystyle=2\nu||\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}})||^{2}_{{\Omega^{+}}}+\lambda([I_{2}\cdot\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}})]I_{2},\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{{\Omega^{+}}}

\displaystyle=2\nu||\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}})||^{2}_{{\Omega^{+}}}+\lambda(\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}}))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(38)

Thus, applying (
36
), (
37
), and (
5.1
) to (
34
), we have

\mathcal{R}e\left(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=-(\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}}),\epsilon(\mathbf{u^{+}}))_{\Omega^{+}}-||\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}}||^{2}_{\Omega^{-}}\leq 0.

(39)

Thus,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is dissipative.

5.2
Maximality

In this section we show the maximality of the operator
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
on the space
\mathcal{H}
. To this end, we establish the
range condition
,
Range(\lambda I-\hat{\mathcal{A}})=\mathcal{H}
for parameter
\lambda
sufficiently large. Our proof will make use of the classic Babuska-Brezzi Theorem, which we state here (see e.g.,
[
11
]
).

Theorem 2

(Babuska-Brezzi Theorem)
Let
\Sigma
,
M
be Hilbert spaces and
a:\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
and
b:\Sigma\times M\to\mathbb{R}
be bilinear forms which are continuous. Let

Z=\{\sigma\in\Sigma\,|\,b(\sigma,q)=0\,\,\text{ for every }q\in M\}.

Assume that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is
Z
-elliptic, i.e. there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

a(\sigma,\sigma)\geq\alpha||\sigma||^{2}_{\Sigma},

for every
\sigma\in Z
. Assume further that there exists a constant
\beta>0
such that

\sup_{\tau\in\Sigma}\frac{b(\tau,q)}{||\tau||_{\Sigma}}\geq\beta||q||_{M}\,\,\text{ for every }q\in M.

Then if
\kappa\in\Sigma
and
\ell\in M
, there exists a unique pair
(\sigma,p)\in\Sigma\times M
such that

\displaystyle a(\sigma,\tau)+b(\tau,p)

\displaystyle=(\kappa,\tau)\,\,\text{ for every }\tau\in\Sigma,

\displaystyle b(\sigma,q)

\displaystyle=(\ell,q)\,\,\text{ for every }q\in M.

Throughout, we will also make use of the Lax-Milgram Lemma, also stated here for convenience (see
[
5
]
).

Theorem 3

(Lax-Milgram Lemma)
Let
V
be a Hilbert space,
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous bilinear form, and
b:V\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous linear form. Further assume that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is
V
-elliptic, i.e. there exists some constant
\alpha>0
such that

a(v,v)\geq\alpha||v||_{V}^{2}\,\,\text{ for all }v\in V.

Then there exists a unique
\hat{u}\in V
such that
a(\hat{u},v)=b(v)\text{ for all }v\in V
.

Establishing the range condition is equivalent to finding some
[\mathbf{u^{+}},p^{+},\mathbf{u^{-}}]\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
which satisfies, for arbitrary
[\mathbf{f},g,\mathbf{h}]\in\mathcal{H}
, the abstract equation

(\lambda I-\hat{\mathcal{A}})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}\\
p^{+}\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{f}\\
g\\
\mathbf{h}\end{bmatrix}.

(40)

Component-wise, this gives the following system of equations:

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{u^{+}}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u^{+}}-\text{div}\,\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{u^{+}}+\nabla p^{+}=\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{+},

(41)

\displaystyle\lambda p^{+}+\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{+}})+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla p^{+}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})p^{+}=g

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{+},

(42)

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{u^{-}}-\Delta\mathbf{u^{-}}+\nabla p^{-}=\mathbf{h}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{-}.

(43)

Further, since
[\mathbf{u^{+}},p^{+},\mathbf{u^{-}}]\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
, we have the additional relations

\displaystyle\mathbf{u^{+}}=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma,

(44)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u^{-}}=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial{\Omega^{-}}\setminus\Gamma,

(45)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u^{+}}=\mathbf{u^{-}}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma,

(46)

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})\vec{\nu}-p^{+}\vec{\nu}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{u^{-}}}{\partial\vec{\nu}}-\pi^{-}\vec{\nu}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma,

(47)

\displaystyle\text{div}(\mathbf{u^{-}})=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }{\Omega^{-}}.

(48)

We aim to establish suitable bilinear forms in
\mathbf{u^{-}}
and
p^{-}
, with the goal of applying the Babuska-Brezzi Theorem. To this end, consider (
43
). Multiplying through by
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\partial\Omega^{-}\setminus\Gamma}(\Omega^{-})
and integrating over
{\Omega^{-}}
, along with Green’s Theorem and the boundary condition, gives

(\lambda\mathbf{u^{-}},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}-(\Delta\mathbf{u^{-}},\varphi)_{{\Omega^{-}}}+(\nabla p^{-},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}=(\mathbf{h},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}},

(49)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
is the usual
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-inner product on
\Omega
(or
L^{2}
-inner product, as appropriate), with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
denoting the boundary integral on
\Gamma
.

A few application of Green’s Theorem and then boundary conditions gives

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{u^{-}},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}+(\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}},\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}-(p^{-},\text{div}(\varphi))_{\Omega^{-}}+\left\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{u^{-}}}{\partial\vec{\nu}}-p^{-}\vec{\nu},\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{h},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}},

(50)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{u^{-}},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}+(\nabla\mathbf{u^{-}},\nabla\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}-(p^{-},\text{div}(\varphi))_{\Omega^{-}}+\left\langle\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})-p^{+}\vec{\nu},\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{h},\varphi)_{\Omega^{-}}.

(51)

We will consider the
\left\langle\sigma(\mathbf{u^{+}})\vec{\nu}-p^{+}\vec{\nu},\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma}
term next, but first need to define some maps and results regarding them.

Lemma 4

The solution map
D_{\lambda}:\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\to\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma}(\Omega^{+})\times L^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
given by

D_{\lambda}(\varphi)=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{\lambda}(\varphi)\\
q_{\lambda}(\varphi)\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\mu_{\lambda}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mu_{\lambda}-\text{div}\,\sigma(\mu_{\lambda})+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mu_{\lambda}+\nabla q_{\lambda}=0&\text{ in }{\Omega^{+}},\\
\lambda q_{\lambda}+\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda})+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})q_{\lambda}=0&\text{ in }{\Omega^{+}},\\
\mu_{\lambda}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi|_{\Gamma}&\text{ on }\Gamma,\\
\mu_{\lambda}|_{\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma}=0&\text{ on }\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma\end{cases}

(52)

is wellposed, giving the existence and uniqueness of solutions
[\mu_{\lambda}(\varphi),\,q_{\lambda}(\varphi)]
in
\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma}(\Omega^{+})\times L^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
for
\varphi
in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

First, to resolve the “pressure” term
q_{\lambda}
, we apply Theorem 9.1 in
[
2
]
, which states, among other things, that for a given
\sigma\in L^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
, there exists some
\gamma\in L^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
such that
\lambda\gamma+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\gamma=\sigma
is satisfied in the weak sense with
||\gamma||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}||\sigma||_{\Omega^{+}}
for a sufficiently large
\lambda
.

It is assumed
\mu_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})
, so we have that
-\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
. Thus, applying Theorem 9.1 to

\lambda q_{\lambda}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})q_{\lambda}=-\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda})\,\,\text{ in }{\Omega^{+}}

(53)

gives solution
q_{\lambda}[\mu_{\lambda}]
with

||q_{\lambda}||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}||\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda})||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}||\mu_{\lambda}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}

for sufficiently large
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{U})
.

Note that this only gives solution
q_{\lambda}[\mu_{\lambda}]
in the weak sense; that is, for any
\varphi\in\mathbf{L}^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
, we have

\lambda(q_{\lambda},\varphi)_{\Omega^{+}}+(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q_{\lambda},\varphi)_{\Omega^{+}}+\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})q_{\lambda},\varphi)_{\Omega^{+}}=-(\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda}),\varphi)_{\Omega^{+}}.

(54)

But since this holds for all
\varphi\in\mathbf{L}^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
, it holds in particular for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}({\Omega^{+}})
. Thus,

\lambda q_{\lambda}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla q_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})q_{\lambda}=-\text{div}(\mu_{\lambda})

(55)

a.e. in
{\Omega^{+}}
.

Next,
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\partial{\Omega^{-}}\setminus\Gamma}({\Omega^{-}})
and the Sobolev Trace map is surjective, so there exists some
\gamma_{0}^{+}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{-}}))
such that
\gamma_{0}\gamma_{0}^{+}(\mathbf{g})=\mathbf{g}
for all
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.
Furthermore, since
{\Omega^{-}}
and
{\Omega^{+}}
are symmetric across
\Gamma
, there exists some
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}^{+}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}}))
. (For example, if
\Gamma
lies on the
x
-axis, let
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}^{+}=-\gamma_{0}^{+}
.)

Letting
\tilde{\mu}=\tilde{\gamma}_{0}^{+}(\varphi|_{\Gamma})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\partial{\Omega^{+}}\setminus\Gamma}({\Omega^{+}})
with
\tilde{\mu}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
, we now aim to find
\mu_{0}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})
such that

\displaystyle\lambda(\mu_{0},\psi)_{\Omega^{+}}+(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mu_{0},\psi)_{\Omega^{+}}+(\sigma(\mu_{0}),\epsilon(\psi))_{\Omega^{+}}+\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mu_{0},\psi)_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle=(q_{\lambda},\text{div}(\psi))_{\Omega^{+}}-(\lambda\tilde{u}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\tilde{\mu},\psi)_{\Omega^{+}}-(\sigma(\tilde{\mu}),\epsilon(\psi))_{\Omega^{+}}

(56)

for all
\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})
.
With this
\mu_{0}
, we would then take
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{0}+\tilde{\mu}
.

To show the existence of such a
\mu_{0}
, we aim to apply the Lax-Milgram Lemma to

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=F(\mathbf{v}),

where for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})
,

a(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})\times\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\lambda(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega^{+}}+(\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega^{+}}+(\sigma(\mathbf{u}),\epsilon(\mathbf{v}))_{\Omega^{+}}+\frac{1}{2}(\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega^{+}}

(57)

and

F(\cdot):\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

F(\mathbf{v})=(q_{\lambda},\text{div}(\mathbf{v}))_{\Omega^{+}}-(\lambda\tilde{\mu}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\tilde{\mu},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega^{+}}-(\sigma(\tilde{\mu}),\epsilon(\mathbf{v}))_{\Omega^{+}}.

(58)

With a view of applying Lax-Milgram, note that by linearity of the integral and partial derivatives,
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
F(\cdot)
are clearly linear.

Furthermore,
F(\cdot)
is bounded. Indeed, for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})
,

\displaystyle|F(\mathbf{v})|

\displaystyle\leq|(q_{\lambda},\text{div}(\mathbf{v}))_{\Omega^{+}}|+|(\lambda\tilde{\mu}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\tilde{\mu},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega^{+}}|+|(\sigma(\tilde{\mu}),\epsilon(v))_{\Omega^{+}}|

\displaystyle\leq||q_{\lambda}||_{\Omega^{+}}||\text{div}(\mathbf{v})||_{\Omega^{+}}+||\lambda\tilde{\mu}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(\mathbf{U})\tilde{\mu}||_{\Omega^{+}}||\mathbf{v}||_{\Omega^{+}}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+||\sigma(\tilde{\mu})||_{\Omega^{+}}||\epsilon(\mathbf{v})||_{\Omega^{+}}

Here, note that
||\text{div}(\mathbf{v})||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq||\mathbf{v}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}
and
||\mathbf{v}||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq||\mathbf{v}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}
. Additionally,
||\sigma(\tilde{\mu})||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq C||\tilde{\mu}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}
and
||\epsilon(\mathbf{v})||_{\Omega^{+}}\leq\tilde{C}||\mathbf{v}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}
.
Furthermore,
q_{\lambda}\in L^{2}({\Omega^{+}})
so
||q_{\lambda}||_{\Omega^{+}}<\infty
. And finally, by Sobolev embedding,
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbf{C}^{1}(\overline{{\Omega^{+}}})
, so
||\mathbf{U}||_{\infty}<\infty
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|F(\mathbf{v)}|

\displaystyle\leq C(||q_{\lambda}||_{\Omega^{+}}+\lambda||\tilde{\mu}||_{\Omega^{+}}+||\mathbf{U}||_{\infty}||\tilde{\mu}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}+||\tilde{\mu}||_{\Omega^{+}}+||\tilde{\mu}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})})||\mathbf{v}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tilde{\mu},\mathbf{U},q_{\lambda}}||\mathbf{v}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}({\Omega^{+}})}.

(59)

Also note that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded. Indeed, for every
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega^{+}})
,

p^{+}(t)\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}(t)\end{bmatrix}=e^{\mathcal{A}t}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{+}\\
p^{+}_{0}\\
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{-}\end{bmatrix}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I-\frac{t}{n}\mathcal{A}\right)^{-n}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{+}\\
p^{+}_{0}\\
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{-}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{+}\\
p^{+}_{0}\\
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{-}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}.

(126)

Setting
\lambda=\frac{n}{t}
in this equation gives

(\lambda I-\mathcal{A})^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u^{+}}(t)\\
p^{+}(t)\\
\mathbf{u^{-}}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\lambda^{n}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{+}\\
p^{+}_{0}\\
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{-}\end{bmatrix},

(127)

which can be solved using the scheme derived from the maximality argument. By choosing
n
large enough to approximate the exponential semigroup operator, one can then recover the solution to the time dependent system at any time
t\geq 0
.

Here we outline a numerical implementation of obtaining approximate solutions using the finite element method (FEM) and associated convergence results with respect to the discretization parameter,
h
. We conclude by providing a specific “test problem” as a numerical example, with associated error analysis.

6.1
Finite element formulation

The finite element method is a numerical implementation of the Ritz-Galerkin method using certain basis functions defined on a mesh on the domain. In this case, both domains,
{\Omega^{+}}
and
{\Omega^{-}}
, are divided into triangles, with basis functions associated with points, or “nodes”, on the mesh. In this finite dimensional setting, the system is then set forth as a matrix-vector equation whose solution gives the coefficients of each basis function (see
[
5
]
). In this section, we show that the discrete FEM formulation of (
41
) - (
40
) is wellposed.

In what follows, the domains
{\Omega^{-}}
and
{\Omega^{+}}
will be taken as before, that is, two rectangles sharing a common interface,
\Gamma

, the initial task in the present finite dimensional setting is to numerically resolve the fluid and pressure variables in

, and

\hskip 61.42993pt-b(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{-},\mathbf{\psi}_{h}^{-})&=0\,\,\,\ \,\,\hskip 23.84923pt\forall\,\,\mathbf{\psi}_{h}^{-}\in\Pi_{h}^{-}.\end{cases}

or Theorem 3.1 in
[

]

From these, as mentioned above, we immediately recover the fluid and pressure approximations in

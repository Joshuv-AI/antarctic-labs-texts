Functional equation of the p p -adic L L -function of Bianchi modular forms
Luis Santiago Palacios

Abstract
. Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field with class number 1, in this paper we obtain the functional equation of the p p -adic L L -function of small slope p p -stabilised Bianchi modular forms. Then, using p p -adic families of Bianchi modular forms, we extend our result to Σ \Sigma -smooth base-change Bianchi modular forms.



1.
Introduction

Fix
p
a rational prime. Via the theory of overconvergent modular symbols, Pollack and Stevens (see
[
PS11
]
and
[
PS13
]
, or for an exposition,
[

]

-adic

In
[
Wil17
]
was developed an analogue of the work of
[

]
for the case of Bianchi modular forms, that is, the case of automorphic forms for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
be a cuspidal Bianchi modular form of weight
(k,k)
and level
\mathfrak{n}
, if
\mathcal{F}

(p)|\mathfrak{n}

]
was constructed
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
, the
p
-adic
L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
, the two-dimensional rigid space of
p
-adic characters on the ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
.

In this paper,

has class number 1.

Our first result is the functional equation of

where
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is a small slope Bianchi modular form obtained by successively stabilising at each different prime
\mathfrak{p}
above

.

A functional equation for the classical
L
-function attached to a Bianchi modular form is given in
[
JL70
]
. In Section
3
, we recover the following reformulation:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
be a Bianchi newform and
\psi
be a Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
with
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{f})=1
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
, we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})|\nu|^{k}\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}(-\nu)\tau(\psi^{-1})}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}),

where
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
, and
\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})=\pm 1
is the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{F}
for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
.

In theorem above,
\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\cdot)
is the
L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
renormalised by Deligne’s
\Gamma
-factors at infinity,
\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{f}}\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
with
\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
the restriction of
\psi
to
K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
,
\psi_{\infty}
is the infinite part of
\psi
,
\tau(\cdot)
is the Gauss sum of
[
Wil17
, §1.2.3]
and
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}
is the adelic norm.

We then obtain the functional equation of
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
in the small slope case:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{p}
be a small slope
p
-stabilisation of a newform
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
with
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
and
(p)\nmid\mathfrak{n}
. Then for all
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
, the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
satisfies the following functional equation

L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k}),

where
\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})=\pm 1
is the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{F}
for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
,
x_{-\nu,p}
is the idele associated to
-\nu
defined in Remark
6.1
and
\sigma_{p}^{k,k}
is as in equation (
6.1
).

The proof uses three ingredients, namely: (a) the main theorem in
[
Wil17
]
, i.e. the construction and interpolation of
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
for a Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
; (b) the complex functional equation obtained in Theorem
1.1
and (c) the work done in
[
Loe14
]
that uniquely determines
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
by its values on the
p
-adic characters
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
coming from a Hecke character
\psi
as in Theorem
1.1
with conductor
\mathfrak{f}|p^{\infty}
(see Section
6.1
for the definition of
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
), when
\mathcal{F}
has small slope at every
\mathfrak{p}|p
.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.1
the level of
\mathcal{F}
must be coprime with the conductor of
\psi
. On the other hand, for the
p
-adic setting of Theorem
1.2
,

needs to be in the level and also its proof use Hecke characters

with conductor
\mathfrak{f}|p^{\infty}
then the level and the conductor are not coprime. As a consequence we are forced to work first with Bianchi newforms of prime-to-

.

The construction of the
p
-adic

-function in
[

]
and then the functional equation in Theorem

depend of the small slope condition of the Bianchi modular form

. It is natural to ask for the
p
-adic
L
-function when

does not have small slope, i.e. the critical slope case. In
[
BSW21
]
such function was constructed for certain base-change Bianchi modular forms. We briefly describe the construction.

Let

be a finite slope eigenform, with
p|N
, new or
p
-stabilised of a newform, regular, non CM by
K
, decent and such that the base-change to
K
, denoted by
f_{/K}
, is
\Sigma
-smooth (see Conditions
6.8
for more details) and let

be a neighbourhood of

such that the weight map

is étale except possibly at

. Then, after shrinking

, in
[

]
was constructed the

-adic

-function

\mathcal{L}_{p}:V_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))\rightarrow L

for sufficiently large

, such that for any classical point

with small slope base-change

we have
\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,-)=c_{y}L_{p}(f_{y/K},-)
, where
c_{y}\in L^{\times}
is a
p
-adic period at

and
L_{p}(f_{y/K},-)
is the
p
-adic
L
-function of
f_{y/K}
. Now, suppose that
f_{/K}
has critical slope and is
\Sigma
-smooth; then the missing
p
-adic
L
-function of
f_{/K}
is defined to be the specialisation
L_{p}({f_{/K}},-):=\mathcal{L}_{p}(x_{f},-)
, where
x_{f}\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the point corresponding to

.

Our second result is the functional equation of
L_{p}(f_{/K},-)
, in particular with no non-critical assumption on

.

We can transfer the functional equation in Theorem
1.2
to
\mathcal{L}_{p}
by shrinking
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that there exist a Zariski-dense set
S\subset V_{\mathbb{Q}}
of classical points

satisfying that
f_{y/K}
is a small slope successively stabilisation at every
\mathfrak{p}|p
of a Bianchi newform in
S_{(k_{y},k_{y})}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
with
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
being the prime-to-
p
part of the level of
f_{/K}
and
k_{y}\equiv k\pmod{p-1}
. Then we obtain:

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
y\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}

and all
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
we have

\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))^{k/2}\langle\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})\rangle^{k_{y}/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa^{-1}w_{\mathrm{Tm}}^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{k_{y}}).

Where
\omega_{\mathrm{Tm}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\omega_{\mathrm{Tm},\mathfrak{p}}
with
\omega_{\mathrm{Tm},\mathfrak{p}}
denoting the Teichmüller character at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\langle x\rangle:=\omega_{\mathrm{Tm}}(x)^{-1}x
for
x\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}
.

Finally, since the
p
-adic
L
-function of a critical slope
\Sigma
-smooth base-change
f_{/K}
is defined to be the specialisation
L_{p}({f_{/K}},-):=\mathcal{L}_{p}(x_{f},-)
, then from specialising in Theorem
1.4
we obtain the following:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\Sigma
-smooth base-change to
K
of a modular form satisfying Conditions
6.8
, let
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
be the prime-to-
p
part of the level of
\mathcal{F}
, then for all
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
satisfies the following functional equation

L_{p}(\mathcal{F},\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F},\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k}).

Notice that corollary above not only gives the functional equation for
\Sigma
-smooth critical slope base-change Bianchi modular forms, but also for
\Sigma
-smooth small slope base-change Bianchi newforms at
p
, this case is interesting, considering for example, that when
p
is split in
K
, small slope is automatic.

2.
Bianchi modular forms

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper, we fix
p
a rational prime and take
K
to be an imaginary quadratic field with class number 1 and ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, let
\delta=\sqrt{-D}
(where
-D
is the discriminant of
K
) be a generator of the different ideal
\mathcal{D}
of
K
,
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. At each prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
, denote by
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
the completion of
K
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
the ring of integers of
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
and fix a uniformiser
\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}
at
\mathfrak{q}
. Denote the adele ring of
K
by
\mathbb{A}_{K}=K_{\infty}\times\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
where
K_{\infty}
are the infinite adeles and
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{f}
are the finite adeles. Furthermore, define
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K}}:=\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
to be the finite integral adeles. Let
n\geqslant 0
be an integer and denote by
V_{n}(R)
the space of homogeneous polynomials in two variables of degree
n
over a ring
R
. Note that
V_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an irreducible complex right representation of
\mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, denote it by
\rho_{n}
.

For a general Hecke character
\psi
of
K
, for each prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
we denote by
\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
the restriction of
\psi
to
K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
and for an ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we define
\psi_{I}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|I}\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}
; we also write
\psi_{\infty}
for the restriction of
\psi
to the infinite ideles, and
\psi_{f}
for the restriction to the finite ideles.

2.2.
Background

Let
\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})=\left\{(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K}}):c\in\mathfrak{n}\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\right\}
and let
\varphi
be a Hecke character whose conductor divides
\mathfrak{n}
and with infinity type
(-k-2v_{1},-k-2v_{2})
for
k\geq 0
,
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
integers. For
u_{f}=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
we set
\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(u_{f})=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{n}}\varphi_{\mathfrak{q}}(d_{\mathfrak{q}})
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say a function
\Phi:\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})\rightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})
is a
cuspidal automorphic form over
K
of weight
\lambda=[(k,k),(v_{1},v_{2})]
, level
\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
and central action
\varphi
if it satisfies:

(i)

\Phi
is left-invariant under
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(K)
;

(ii)

\Phi(zg)=\varphi(z)\Phi(g)
for
z\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}\cong Z(\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))
, where
Z(G)
denote the centre of the group
G
;

(iii)

\Phi(gu)=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(u_{f})\Phi(g)\rho_{2k+2}(u_{\infty})
for
u=u_{f}\cdot u_{\infty}\in\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})\times\mathrm{SU_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
;

(iv)

\Phi
is an eigenfunction of the operator
\partial
, where
\partial/4
denotes a component of the Casimir operator in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathbb{C}
, and where we consider
\Phi(g_{\infty}g_{f})
as a function of
g_{\infty}\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C}).

(v)

\Phi
satisfies the cuspidal condition that for all
g\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
, we have

\int_{K\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}}\Phi\left((\begin{smallmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})g\right)du=0

where
du
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{A}_{K}
.

The space of such functions will be denoted by
S_{\lambda}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is possible to define automorphic forms over
K
of weight
\lambda=[(k_{1},k_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})]
, for distinct integers
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
but since we are dealing with cusp forms we assume
k_{1}=k_{2}=k
(see
[
Hid94
, §2.5, Cor 2.2]
).

A cuspidal automorphic form
\Phi
of weight
\lambda
and level
\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
descends to give a function
F:\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})
, via
F(g):=\Phi(g)
for
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{3}:=\{(z,t):z\in\mathbb{C},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
be the hyperbolic space and since
\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})=Z(\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C}))\cdot\mathrm{B}\cdot\mathrm{SU_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathrm{B}=\left\{(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}):z\in\mathbb{C},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right\}\cong\mathcal{H}_{3}
, we can descend further using ii) and iii) in Definition
2.1
to obtain a function

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{H}_{3}\longrightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle(z,t)\longmapsto t^{v_{1}+v_{2}-1}F(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}).

Remark 2.3
.

There are two ways to define the function
\mathcal{F}
in the literature.

(1)

In accounts such as
[
Cre81
]
,
[
CW94
]
and
[
Byg98
]
, all of which deal predominantly with weight
[(0,0),(0,0)]
and trivial central action; such function is defined simply restricting to B.

(2)

In
[
Gha99
]
and
[
Wil17
]
such
\mathcal{F}
is defined as in (
2.1
) for weight
[(k,k),(0,0)]
.

For the two definitions above, the modularity condition satisfied by such
\mathcal{F}
resulting is different, but there is a clear bijection between the sets of functions that arise.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}_{3}\rightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})
is a
cuspidal Bianchi modular form
if comes from
\Phi
, a cuspidal automorphic form over
K
(in the sense of definition
2.1
) by the descent described above.

Note that for
\gamma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}):=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(K)\cap\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, a Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the following automorphic condition:

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}(\gamma\cdot(z,t))=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)^{-1}\mathcal{F}(z,t)\rho_{2k+2}(J(\gamma;(z,t))),

where
\gamma\cdot(z,t)
denotes the action of
\rm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
and
J(\gamma;(z,t)):=(\begin{smallmatrix}cz+d&\overline{c}t\\
-ct&\overline{cz+d}\end{smallmatrix})
.

We denote by
S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
the space of cuspidal Bianchi modular forms
\mathcal{F}
that come from cuspidal automorphic forms
\Phi\in S_{\lambda}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
and then they satisfy (
2.2
). Also denote by
S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
when
\varphi
has trivial conductor.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL_{2}}(\mathbb{C})
and let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
, then define a new function

by

(2.3)

(\mathcal{F}|_{\gamma})(z,t):=det(\gamma)^{-k/2-v_{1}}\overline{det(\gamma)}^{-k/2-v_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\gamma\cdot(z,t))\rho_{2k+2}^{-1}\left(J\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{det(\gamma)}};(z,t)\right)\right).

Remark 2.6
.

Note that
\mathcal{F}\in S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
satisfies:

\mathcal{F}|_{g}(0,1)=F(g)
for

2)
\left(\mathcal{F}|_{\gamma}\right)(z,t)=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(d)^{-1}\mathcal{F}(z,t)

2.3.
Fourier-Whittaker expansions

Let
\Phi:\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{K})\rightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})
be a cuspidal automorphic form of weight
\lambda=[(k,k),(v_{1},v_{2})]
. Then
\Phi
has the following Fourier expansion (see
[
Hid94
, Thm 6.1]
)

(2.4)

\Phi\left[(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})\right]=|t|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}c(\alpha t\delta,\Phi)W(\alpha t_{\infty})e_{K}(\alpha z)\;\;\text{for}\;\;(\begin{smallmatrix}t&z\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{K}),

where:

i)

the Fourier coefficient
c(\cdot,\Phi)
is a function on the fractional ideals of
K
, with
c(I,\Phi)=0
for
I
non-integral;

ii)

e_{K}
is an additive character of
K\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}
defined by

e_{K}=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\;\mathrm{prime}}(e_{p}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})\right)\cdot(e_{\infty}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}),

for

e_{p}\left(\sum_{j}d_{j}p^{j}\right)=e^{-2\pi i\sum_{j<0}d_{j}p^{j}}\;\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;\;e_{\infty}(r)=e^{2\pi ir};

iii)

W:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow V_{2k+2}(\mathbb{C})
is the Whittaker function

W(s):=\sum_{n=0}^{2k+2}\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\frac{1}{s^{v_{1}}\overline{s}^{v_{2}}}\left(\frac{s}{i|s|}\right)^{k+1-n}K_{n-(k+1)}(4\pi|s|)X^{2k+2-n}Y^{n},

where
K_{n}(x)
is a modified Bessel function.

If our cuspidal automorphic form,
\Phi
, corresponds to a cuspidal Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
, then the Fourier expansion stated above descends to the following Fourier expansion of
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
Gha99
]
):

\mathcal{F}(z,t)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{2k+2}\mathcal{F}_{n}(z,t)X^{2k+2-n}Y^{n},

(2.5)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(z,t):=t\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
n\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}\left[c(\alpha\delta)\frac{1}{\alpha^{v_{1}}\overline{\alpha}^{v_{2}}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{i|\alpha|}\right)^{k+1-n}K_{n-k-1}(4\pi|\alpha|t)e^{2\pi i(\alpha z+\overline{\alpha z})}\right].

Here to ease notation we have written
c(\alpha\delta)
instead
c(\alpha\delta,\Phi)
.

2.4.
Hecke operators

As with classical modular forms, we can extend the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on functions, given by (
2.3
), to the group ring of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
; Hecke operators will be defined by particular elements on this group ring.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
generated by the fixed uniformiser
\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
be a Bianchi modular form of weight
\lambda=[(k,k),(v_{1},v_{2})]
we define the Hecke operator

\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}|_{T_{\mathfrak{q}}}:=\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k+2v_{1}}\overline{\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{k+2v_{2}}\left[\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b\\
0&\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{matrix}\right)$}}+\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi_{\mathfrak{q}})^{-1}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}\right].

When
\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{n}
we denote
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
by
U_{\mathfrak{q}}
and

\mathcal{F}|_{U_{\mathfrak{q}}}:=\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k+2v_{1}}\overline{\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{k+2v_{2}}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b\\
0&\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{matrix}\right)$}}.

We can similarly define Hecke operators for each ideal
I
of
K
. Indeed, let
I=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathfrak{q}^{r}
where
\mathfrak{q}^{r}
exactly divides
I
, then the Hecke operator
T_{I}
is totally determined by the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
for
\mathfrak{q}|I
.

Remark 2.7
.

The Hecke operators on Bianchi modular forms defined above are the descent of the Hecke operators defined in
[
Wei71
, Chap.VI]
) on automorphic forms over
K
by the action of double cosets.

In the same way as in the rational case (elliptic modular forms), for Bianchi modular forms of weight
\lambda=[(k,k),(v_{1},v_{2})]
and level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
with
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
there is a Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
defined by

\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}:=\nu^{k/2+v_{1}}\overline{\nu}^{k/2+v_{2}}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
with
\lambda=[(k,k),(v_{1},v_{2})]
,
then for
0\leqslant n\leqslant 2k+2
we have

(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}})_{2k+2-n}(0,t)=t^{-2k-2}(-1)^{n}\nu^{n-k-1}|\nu|^{-n}\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(0,\frac{1}{|\nu|t}\right).

Proof.

Note that if
\gamma=(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{smallmatrix})
then
\gamma\cdot(0,t)=(0,1/(|\nu|t))
and

\displaystyle\rho_{2k+2}^{-1}\left(J\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{det(\gamma)}};(0,t)\right)\right)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\left(J\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{det(\gamma)}};(0,t)\right)^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)\right)^{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\begin{matrix}0&-\nu^{-1/2}t^{-1}\\
\overline{\nu}^{-1/2}t^{-1}&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)\right)^{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}-\nu^{-1/2}t^{-1}Y\\
\overline{\nu}^{-1/2}t^{-1}X\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}

where
\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}=(X^{2k+2},X^{2k+1}Y,...,X^{2k+2-n}Y^{n},...,XY^{2k+1},Y^{2k+2})^{t}
.

Then,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}})(0,t)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\nu^{k/2+v_{1}}\overline{\nu}^{k/2+v_{2}}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}}(0,t)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(0,1/(|\nu|t))\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}-\nu^{-1/2}t^{-1}Y\\
\overline{\nu}^{-1/2}t^{-1}X\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}.

∎

Twisted series of Bianchi modular forms

For the rest of the paper we fix the weight
\lambda=[(k,k),(0,0)]
, i.e.
v_{1}=v_{2}=0
as in
[
Wil17
]
, then we say
\Phi
has weight
(k,k)
and denote
S_{\lambda}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
by
S_{(k,k)}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
and
S_{\lambda}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
by
S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
. The importance of the general definition with
(v_{1},v_{2})
in previous sections will become clear in this section when we twist Bianchi modular forms by Hecke characters.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\Phi\in S_{(k,k)}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
and
\psi
be a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
. Define the twisting operator
R(\psi)
by

\Phi|R(\psi)(g):=\psi(det(g))\sum_{[a]\in(\mathfrak{f}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{K})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(a)\Phi(g(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})),\;\;\text{for}\;\;g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{K}).

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Phi\in S_{(k,k)}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
where
\varphi
has infinity type
(-k,-k)
and conductor dividing
\mathfrak{n}
and let
\psi
be a Hecke character of infinity type
(q,r)
and conductor
\mathfrak{f}
. Then
\Phi|R(\psi)\in S_{\iota}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{m}),\varphi\psi^{2})
where
\iota=[(k,k),(-q,-r)]
and
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{f}^{2}
.

Proof.

See
[
Hid94
, §6, (6.7)]
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Note that for
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
, if
\mathcal{F}
is the descent of a cuspidal automorphic form
\Phi\in S_{(k,k)}(\Omega_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi)
and we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\psi}
the descent of
\Phi|R(\psi)
then by
[
Hid94
, (6.9)]
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\psi}=\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b/f\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}=\psi_{\infty}(f)\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b)\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b/f\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}},

which is analogous to the twist of a modular form by a Dirichlet character in
[
Iwa97
, Thm 7.4 (7.30)]
up to a factor of a Gauss sum (and
\psi_{\infty}
) which then appears in the Fourier expansion of
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
Hid94
, (6.8)]
).

Lemma 2.12
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
then
\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}})
.

Proof.

Note that
(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{smallmatrix})
normalizes the group
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
, explicitly
(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{smallmatrix})\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{smallmatrix})
where
\gamma^{\prime}=(\begin{smallmatrix}d&-c/\nu\\
-b\nu&a\end{smallmatrix})
if
\gamma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})
. Hence, for
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
we have

(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}})|_{\gamma}=|\nu|^{k}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}\gamma}=|\nu|^{k}\mathcal{F}|_{\gamma^{\prime}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(\gamma)\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}},

where in last equality we use that
ad\equiv 1\mod{\mathfrak{n}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}^{-1})
be a Bianchi modular form and let
\psi
be a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
with
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{f})=1
. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(f)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)_{\psi^{-1}},

for
(m)=\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{f}^{2}=(\nu)(f)^{2}
,
m=\nu f^{2}
.

Proof.

Since for any
v
we have the identity
\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{b}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
m&0\end{matrix}\right)$}=\scalebox{0.8}{$f$}\cdot\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}
and choosing
v
such that
bv\nu\equiv-1
(mod
f
) to bring
(\begin{smallmatrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{smallmatrix})
into
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
, then

(2.6)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{b}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}W_{\mathfrak{m}}}&=|m|^{k}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{b}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
m&0\end{matrix}\right)$}}=|m|^{k}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$f$}\cdot\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}\\
&=|m|^{k}|f^{2}|^{-k}\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
\nu&0\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}\\
&=|m|^{k}|f^{2}|^{-k}|\nu|^{-k}\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}\\
&=\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}f&-v\\
-b\nu&\frac{1+bv\nu}{f}\end{matrix}\right)$}\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(f)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}.\end{array}

Where in the last equalities we use that
m=\nu f^{2}
and
\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}})
by Lemma
2.12
. Since we have that
bv\nu\equiv-1
(mod
f
), then
\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b)=\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v)^{-1}
. Now multiplying (
2.6
) by the latter and summing over the reduced residue class of
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
we obtain

\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b)\mathcal{F}|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b/f\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}W_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(f)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\sum_{v\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}.

Multiplying by
\psi_{\infty}(f)
in both sides and using Remark
2.11
we have

\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{n}}(f)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}(f)\sum_{v\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right)|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{v}{f}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}.

Finally, since
\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v)^{-1}=\psi_{\infty}(f)(\psi_{\infty}(f)^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v)^{-1})=\psi_{\infty}(f)\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(v/f)^{-1}
we obtain the result.
∎

The proposition above is a generalization to the Bianchi setting of
[
Iwa97
, Thm 7.5]
up to a Gauss sum and
\psi_{\infty}
as explained in Remark
2.11
.

3.
L
-function

3.1.
Definition of the
L
-function

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character with conductor
\mathfrak{f}
, for each ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{m}}\mathfrak{q}^{n_{\mathfrak{q}}}
coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
, we define
\psi(\mathfrak{m})=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|\mathfrak{m}}\psi_{\mathfrak{q}}(\pi_{\mathfrak{q}})^{n_{\mathfrak{q}}}
with
\mathfrak{q}=(\pi_{\mathfrak{q}})
a prime ideal; and
\psi(\mathfrak{m})=0
if
\mathfrak{m}
is not coprime to
\mathfrak{f}
. In an abuse of notation, we write
\psi
for both the idelic Hecke character and the function it determines on ideals; will always be clear from the context which formulation we mean.

Let
\Phi
be an automorphic form, define the twist of the
L
-function of
\Phi
by
\psi
by

L(\Phi,\psi,s)=\sum_{0\neq\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}}c(\mathfrak{m},\Phi)\psi(\mathfrak{m})N(\mathfrak{m})^{-s},

where
c(\cdot,\Phi)
are the Fourier coefficients of
\Phi
.

Remark 3.1
.

In
[
Wei71
]
, it is proved that the twisted
L
-function converges absolutely in some suitable right half-plane. The
L
-function can be written in terms of an integral formula, then via meromorphic continuation, this integral gives the definition of the
L
-function on all of
\mathbb{C}
. In fact, a little more work shows that this function is an
analytic
continuation and the
L
-function is holomorphic on the whole complex plane.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Bianchi modular form corresponding to the automorphic form
\Phi
, then

L(\Phi,\psi,s)=L(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)=w^{-1}\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}c(\alpha\delta)\psi((\alpha\delta))N((\alpha\delta))^{-s},

where
w=|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|
and
c(\cdot)
are the Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{F}
as in (
2.5
).

It is convenient to think the twisted
L
-function as a function on Hecke characters instead as a complex function of one variable. Then we put

L(\mathcal{F},\psi)=L(\mathcal{F},\psi,1).

We
complete
the
L
-function by adding the appropriate factors at infinity. If the infinity type of
\psi
is
(q,r)
then we define

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi):=\frac{\Gamma(q+1)\Gamma(r+1)}{(2\pi i)^{q+1}(2\pi i)^{r+1}}L(\mathcal{F},\psi),

where
\Gamma
is the usual Gamma function. This is the
L
-function renormalised by Deligne’s
\Gamma
-factors at infinity.

In
[
Wil17
, Thm 2.11]
is proved:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
then for a Hecke character
\psi
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)c_{q,r}(b/f),

where
\tau(\psi^{-1})
is the Gauss sum from
[
Wil17
, §1.2.3.]
and for
a\in K

c_{q,r}(a):=2\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}(-1)^{k+r+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\mathcal{F}_{k+q-r+1}(a,t)dt.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in Theorem
3.2
, the coefficient
c_{q,r}(a)
differs from
[
Wil17
, Prop. 2.9]
where there is a slight error with the sub-index of
\mathcal{F}
.

In
[
Hid94
, Thm. 8.1]
is proved that the “critical” values of the
L
-function can be controlled, i.e. there exists a period
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and a number field
E
such that

(3.1)

\frac{\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)}{\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\in E(\psi),

where
E(\psi)
is the number field generated by the values of
\psi
.

3.2.
Functional equation of the complex
L
-function

For the rest of the paper we work on the space
S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
, in particular we henceforth assume the central action
\varphi
has trivial conductor. We also use the fact that if
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
is a Bianchi
newform
, i.e., it is an eigenform for all the Hecke operators and is not induced from a Bianchi modular form with level properly dividing
\mathfrak{n}
; then
\mathcal{F}
is an eigenvector for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
with
\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}=\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathcal{F}
for
\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})=\pm 1
(see
[
CW94
, §2]
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
be a newform with
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
, then for a Hecke character
\psi
of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
with
(f,\nu)=1
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
, we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})|\nu|^{k}\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}(-\nu)\tau(\psi^{-1})}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
we know that for a Hecke character
\psi
of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)c_{q,r}(b/f)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)\left[\frac{2(-1)^{k+r+1}}{\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\mathcal{F}_{k+q-r+1}(b/f,t)dt\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{q+1}4}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\left[\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)\mathcal{F}_{k+q-r+1}(b/f,t)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{q+1}4}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\mathcal{F}_{\psi,k+q-r+1}(0,t)dt,

where last equality comes from Remark
2.11
.

Changing variable
t\rightarrow 1/(|m|t)
we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{(-1)^{q+1}4|m|^{-q-r-1}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-q-r-2}\mathcal{F}_{\psi,k+q-r+1}(0,1/(|m|t))dt.

Recall that
\mathcal{F}_{\psi}
has weight
[(k,k),(-q,-r)]
then by Lemma
2.8
we know that

(\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}})_{k-q+r+1}(0,t)=t^{-2k-2}(-1)^{k+q-r+1}m^{q-r}|m|^{-(k+q-r+1)}\mathcal{F}_{\psi,k+q-r+1}(0,1/(|m|t))

and replacing
\mathcal{F}_{\psi,k+q-r+1}(0,1/(|m|t))
above we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{q+1}4|m|^{-q-r-1}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-q-r-2}\left[t^{2k+2}(-1)^{k+q-r+1}m^{-q+r}|m|^{k+q-r+1}(\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}})_{k-q+r+1}(0,t)\right]dt.

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+r}4m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-q-r}(\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}})_{k-q+r+1}(0,t)dt.

By Proposition
2.13
since
(\nu,f)=1
we have
\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}(\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}})_{\psi^{-1}}
, also
\mathcal{F}
is a newform, then
\mathcal{F}_{\psi}|_{W_{\mathfrak{m}}}=\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\psi^{-1}}
and we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{(-1)^{k+r}4m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-q-r}\mathcal{F}_{\psi^{-1},k-q+r+1}(0,t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+r}4m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-q-r}\left[\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}(b/f)\mathcal{F}_{k-q+r+1}(b/f,t)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+r}4m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}(b/f)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-q-r}\mathcal{F}_{k-q+r+1}(b/f,t)dt.

Now note that the integral above is exactly the integral appearing on the coefficient
c_{k-q,k-r}(b/f)
, more explicitly

c_{k-q,k-r}(b/f)=\frac{2(-1)^{r+1}}{\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k-q+r+1\end{matrix}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-q-r}\mathcal{F}_{k-q+r+1}(b/f,t)dt

and since
{\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k-q+r+1\end{matrix}\right)}={\left(\begin{matrix}2k+2\\
k+q-r+1\end{matrix}\right)}
, we have

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}2m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}(b/f)c_{k-q,k-r}(b/f).

The Hecke character
\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}
has conductor
\mathfrak{f}
and infinity type
(k-q,k-r)
and we know by Theorem
3.2
that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2}{Dw\tau((\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})^{-1})}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)c_{k-q,k-r}(b/f)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2|f|^{2k}}{Dw\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi^{-1}_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)c_{k-q,k-r}(b/f).

Finally we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}2m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k+q+r}2|f|^{2k}}{Dw\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}\right]^{-1}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{q+r+1}m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{\tau(\psi^{-1})\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)|f|^{2k}}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})|\nu|^{k}\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}(-\nu)\tau(\psi^{-1})}\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}),

where we used that

\displaystyle\frac{m^{-q+r}|m|^{k-2r}\psi_{\infty}(f)^{2}}{|f|^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|m|^{k}\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(m)\psi_{\infty}(f^{2})}{|f|^{2k}}=\frac{|\nu f^{2}|^{k}\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(\nu f^{2})\psi_{\infty}(f^{2})}{|f|^{2k}}

\displaystyle=|\nu|^{k}\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(\nu)

and
(-1)^{q+r}\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(\nu)=\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(-\nu)
.
∎

\rho_{2k+2}^{-1}\left(J\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{det(\gamma)}};(0,t)\right)\right)\left(\begin{matrix}X\\

Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}=|\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}|^{-k-2}\mathcal{F}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}z,|\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}|t)\left(\begin{matrix}\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1/2}X\\
\overline{\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{1/2}Y\end{matrix}\right)^{2k+2}.

Then computing the
n

(3.3)

\mathcal{G}_{n}(z,t)=|\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}|^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{n}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}z,|\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}|t)\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}}{|\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}|}\right)^{k+1-n}.

(ii) Now, we relate

3.2

\psi

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{G},\psi)

\displaystyle=(*)\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\mathcal{G}_{k+q-r+1}(b/f,t)dt

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{G},\psi)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\infty}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}N(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}(*)\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/f)^{\times}}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(b/f)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q+r}\mathcal{F}_{k+q-r+1}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}b/f,t)dt

4.
Modular symbols

In this section we introduce Bianchi modular symbols. These are algebraic analogues of Bianchi modular forms that are easier to study
p
-adically.

Let
\Delta_{0}:=\mathrm{Div}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(K))

\mathcal{H}_{3}

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)

\Gamma

\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma}(V):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{0},V)

\Gamma

(\phi|\gamma)(D):=(\phi|\gamma D)|\gamma=\phi(D)

where
\Gamma

(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\cdot r=\frac{ar+b}{cr+d}

For a ring
R

(2)

X,Y

k

\gamma\cdot P\left[\left(\begin{matrix}X\\
Y\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{X}\\
\overline{Y}\end{matrix}\right)\right]=P\left[\left(\begin{matrix}dX+bY\\

c&d\end{matrix}\right).

Remark 4.1
.

This induces a right action on the dual space

0&1\end{matrix}\right)$}}.

When

we denote

by

and

(\phi|_{U_{\mathfrak{q}}}):=\sum_{b\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}}\phi|_{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}1&b\\

We can define Hecke operators over Bianchi modular symbols for each ideal
I
of
K
in the analogous way as for Bianchi modular forms, then the Hecke algebra acts on
\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(V_{k,k}^{*}(\mathbb{C}))
and by
[
Hid94

and
[
Har87
]
, we have:

Proposition 4.3
.

There is a Hecke-equivariant injection

S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(V_{k,k}^{*}(\mathbb{C})),\;\;\mathcal{F}\mapsto\phi_{\mathcal{F}}.

Let
\mathcal{X}^{k-q}\mathcal{Y}^{q}\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{k-r}\overline{\mathcal{Y}}^{r}\in V_{k,k}^{*}(\mathbb{C})

5.
Overconvergent modular symbols

Part (ii) of Proposition
4.5
allows us to see the modular symbol
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}
as having values in
V_{k,k}^{*}(L)
for a sufficiently large
p
-adic field
L
. For suitable level groups, one can then replace this space of polynomials with a space of
p
-adic distributions and obtain the so called
overconvergent modular symbols
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}(L)
denote the space of locally analytic functions on
\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
defined over
L
. We equip this space with a weight
(k,k)
-action of the semigroup

\Sigma_{0}(p):=\left\{\scalebox{0.8}{$\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)$}\in M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}):p|c,a\in(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times},ad-bc\nequal 0\right\}

by setting

\gamma\cdot\zeta(z)=(a+cz)^{k}\zeta\left(\frac{b+dz}{a+cz}\right).

Denote
\mathcal{A}_{k,k}(L)
the space
\mathcal{A}(L)
equipped with the action above.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathcal{A}_{k,k}(L),L)
denote the space of locally analytic distributions on
\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
defined over
L
, equipped with a weight
(k,k)
right action of
\Sigma_{0}(p)
given by
\mu|\gamma(\zeta)=\mu(\gamma\cdot\zeta)
.

Remark 5.2
.

When
p
ramifies as
\mathfrak{p}^{2}
in
K
, we can consider instead
\Sigma_{0}(p)
, the larger group coming from condition
c\in\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
the fixed uniformiser at
\mathfrak{p}
.

For
\Gamma\subset\Sigma_{0}(p)
, define the space of
overconvergent modular symbols of weight
(k,k)
and level
\Gamma
to be
\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L))
.

There is a natural map
\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L)\rightarrow V_{k,k}^{*}(L)
given by dualising the inclusion of
V_{k,k}(L)
into
\mathcal{A}_{k,k}(L)
. When
(p)|\mathfrak{n}
for
p
split in
K
or when
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n}
for
p
inert or ramified this map induces a
specialisation map

\rho:\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(V_{k,k}^{*}(L)),

noting that the source is well-defined since
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})\subset\Sigma_{0}(p)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

(Control theorem,
[
Wil17
, Cors. 5.9, 6.13]
). For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
, let
\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\in L^{\times}
. If
v(\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}})<(k+1)/e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}|p
, then the restriction of the specialisation map

\rho:\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L))^{\{U_{\mathfrak{p}}=\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}|p\}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(V_{k,k}^{*}(L))^{\{U_{\mathfrak{p}}=\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}|p\}}

to the simultaneous
\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenspaces of the
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
operators is an isomorphism. Here recall that
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}|p
.

Definition 5.4
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
is an eigenform with eigenvalues
\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\mathfrak{p}|p
, we say
\mathcal{F}
has
small slope
if
v(\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}})<(k+1)/e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}|p
. We say
\mathcal{F}
has
critical slope
if it has no small slope.

Thus if
\mathcal{F}
has small slope, using the above control theorem, we get an associated overconvergent modular symbol
\Psi_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{Symb}_{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}(\mathcal{D}_{k,k}(L))
by lifting the corresponding classical modular symbol.

6.
p
-adic
L
function

6.1.
Constructing the
p
-adic
L
-function

The
p
-adic
L
-function of a Bianchi modular form
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the locally analytic distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
, the two-dimensional rigid space of
p
-adic characters on
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}):=K^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\prod_{v\nmid p}\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
, that interpolates the classical
L
-values of
\mathcal{F}
and satisfy certain growth properties. We recall its key properties.

Recall that there is a bijection between algebraic Hecke characters of conductor dividing
p^{\infty}
and locally algebraic characters of
\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty})
such that if
\psi
corresponds to
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
, both are equal when we restrict to the adeles away from the infinite place and the primes above
p
.

If
\psi
is an algebraic Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|p^{\infty}
and infinity type
(q,r)
, then, fixing an isomorphism
\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}_{p}
, we associate to
\psi
a
K^{\times}
-invariant function

\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x):(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times})_{\mathfrak{f}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p},\;\;\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x):=\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(x)\sigma_{p}^{q,r}(x),

where

(6.1)

\sigma_{p}^{q,r}(x):=\begin{cases}x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{q}x_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{r}&:p\ \mbox{splits as}\ \mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}},\\
x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{q}\overline{x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{r}}&:p\ \mbox{inert or ramified}.\end{cases}

Remark 6.1
.

For
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, we have
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x_{\alpha,p})=(\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})_{(p)}(\alpha)=\psi_{(p)}(\alpha)\alpha^{q}\overline{\alpha}^{r}
for
(x_{\alpha,p})_{\mathfrak{q}}=\alpha
when
\mathfrak{q}|(p)
and
(x_{\alpha,p})_{\mathfrak{q}}=1
otherwise.

We now can construct the
p
-adic
L
-function of a small slope eigenform
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
. First, associate to
\mathcal{F}
a classical Bianchi eigensymbol
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}
with coefficients in a
p
-adic field
L
, and lift it to its corresponding unique overconvergent Bianchi eigensymbol
\Psi
. Define the
p
-adic
L
-function of
\mathcal{F}
as the locally analytic distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
by

L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-):=\Psi(\{0\}-\{\infty\})|_{(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}}.

Then,
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
satisfies the interpolation and admissibility properties desired (see
[
Wil17
, Defs. 5.10, 6.14]
) and we obtain the main theorem (Theorem 7.4) in
[
Wil17
]
for class number
1
:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a cuspidal Bianchi modular eigenform of weight
(k,k)
and level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
, where
(p)|\mathfrak{n}
, with
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalues
\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
v(\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}})<(k+1)/e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}|p
. Let
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}
be a complex period as in (
3.1
). Then there exists a locally analytic distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
such that for any Hecke character of
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|(p^{\infty})
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
, we have

(6.2)

L_{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\right)\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi),

with
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)
as in (
3.2
).

The distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F},-)
is
(h_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|p}
-admissible, where
h_{\mathfrak{p}}=v_{p}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}})
, and hence is unique.

Remark 6.3
.

1) There is a slight error in
[
Wil17
]
, where the term

is incorrect in the case where
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{f}
.

2) When
p
ramifies as
\mathfrak{p}^{2}
, in the Theorem above it suffices
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n}
instead
(p)|\mathfrak{n}
.

6.2.
Functional equation of the
p
-adic
L
-function

6.2.1.
The small slope case

In this section we obtain the functional equation of the
p
-adic
L
-function of a small slope
p
-stabilisation of a cuspidal Bianchi modular eigenform.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{p}
be a Bianchi modular form obtained by successively stabilising at each different prime
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
a newform
\mathcal{F}\in S_{(k,k)}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))
, with
\mathfrak{n}=(\nu)
prime to
(p)
. Recall that
\mathcal{F}
is an eigenform for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
, with
\mathcal{F}|_{W_{\mathfrak{n}}}=\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathcal{F}
with
\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})=\pm 1
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For any Hecke character
\psi
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
with
(f,\nu)=1
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
we have

\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\right)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\right)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}),

where
\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\left[\frac{-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})|\nu|^{k}\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}(-\nu)\tau(\psi^{-1})}\right]
and
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}
are the
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
-eigenvalues of
\mathcal{F}_{p}
for each
\mathfrak{p}|p
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
we have

(6.3)

\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})

Now let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime over
p
, if we define
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}
as a
\mathfrak{p}-
stabilisation of
\mathcal{F}
, we obtain by Lemma
3.6
the following relations between the
\Lambda
-function of
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
\mathcal{F}

(6.4)

\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}},\psi)=Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi),

(6.5)

\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})=Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}).

Putting (
6.4
), (
6.3
) and (
6.5
) together

\displaystyle Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}},\psi)

\displaystyle=Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi)

\displaystyle=Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\Lambda(\mathcal{F},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}).

Note that if
p
is inert or ramified we are done and
\mathcal{F}_{p}=\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. If
p
split we have to do one more stabilisation, let
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
be the other prime above
p
.

If we define
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
as the
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}-
stabilisation of
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and doing the same process above, we obtain

\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\right)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}},\psi)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\right)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}).

Putting
\mathcal{F}_{p}=\mathcal{F}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
when
p
split we obtain the result.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

If
\mathcal{F}_{p}
has small slope, then for any Hecke character
\psi
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|(p^{\infty})
with
\mathfrak{f}=(f)
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
, the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
satisfies

L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k/2}\psi^{-1}_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x_{-\nu,p})L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k}),

where
x_{-\nu,p}
is the idele associated to
-\nu
defined in Remark
6.1
and
\sigma_{p}^{k,k}
as in equation (
6.1
).

Proof.

By Theorem
6.2
we have the following interpolations

(6.6)

L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\right)\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi).

(6.7)

L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\right)\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k}).

By (
6.6
), Lemma
6.4
and (
6.7
) we have

\displaystyle L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi)\right)\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})\right)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi^{-1})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\left[\frac{Dw\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})}{(-1)^{k+q+r}2\lambda_{\mathfrak{f}}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}}\right]^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F},\psi)\tau(\psi^{-1})\tau(\psi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{-k})^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}})

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})|\nu|^{k}\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(-\nu)L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}}).

By Remark
6.1
we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{f}}^{-1}(-\nu)\psi_{\infty}^{-1}(-\nu)

\displaystyle=\psi^{-1}_{(p)}(-\nu)(-\nu)^{-q}(-\overline{\nu})^{-r}

\displaystyle=(\psi^{-1}_{p-\mathrm{fin}})_{(p)}(-\nu)

\displaystyle=\psi^{-1}_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x_{-\nu,p}),

and noting that for a finite idele
x
we have

(\psi^{-1}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x)=\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}^{-1}(x)(|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{K}}^{k})_{p-\mathrm{fin}}(x)=\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}^{-1}(x)\sigma_{p}^{k,k}(x)=(\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k})(x),

we obtain the result.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

For
\mathcal{F}_{p}
as above with small slope, the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
satisfies the following functional equation

L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k}),

for all
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
.

Proof.

Define a new distribution
L_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
by

L_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa):=L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa)+\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k,k})

for any
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
.

Since
v_{p}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})<(k+1)/e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}|p
, the distribution
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
is
(h_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|p}
-admissible, where
h_{\mathfrak{p}}=v_{p}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Then
L_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
is
(h_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|p}
-admissible.

In
[
Loe14
]
it is proved that a distribution
(h_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|p}
-admissible like

is uniquely determined by its values on the
p
-adic characters
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
that arise from Hecke characters
\psi
of conductor
\mathfrak{f}|(p^{\infty})
and infinity type
0\leqslant(q,r)\leqslant(k,k)
. By Proposition
6.5
we have that
L_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{p},\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}})=0
for all
\psi_{p-\mathrm{fin}}
, then
L_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)=0
and the functional equation of
L_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{p},-)
follows.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

In the case when

Example:
Suppose

as

Conditions 6.8

Up to shrinking

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
as in Remark
6.9
, then for every
y\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))

\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))^{k/2}\langle\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})\rangle^{k_{y}/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa^{-1}w_{\mathrm{Tm}}^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{k_{y}}),

where
\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})=\pm 1
is the eigenvalue of
f_{y/K}
for the Fricke involution
W_{\mathfrak{n}}
,
x_{-\nu,p}
is the idele associated to
-\nu
defined in Remark (
6.1
).

Proof.

Consider
S\subset V_{\mathbb{Q}}
as in Remark
6.9
and note that for
y\in S
, by Theorem
6.6
, the distribution
L_{p}(f_{y/K},-)
satisfies for all
\kappa\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Cl}_{K}(p^{\infty}))
the following functional equation

L_{p}(f_{y/K},\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k_{y}/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}L_{p}(f_{y/K},\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k_{y},k_{y}}),

multiplying both sides by the
p
-adic period
c_{y}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})^{k_{y}/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa^{-1}\sigma_{p}^{k_{y},k_{y}}).

Note that
N(\mathfrak{n})=w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))\langle\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})\rangle
where each factor is well defined because
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
for all
\mathfrak{p}|p
, also, since

we have
w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))^{k_{y}/2}=w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))^{k/2}
, then for all
y\in S

\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa)=-\epsilon(\mathfrak{n})w_{\mathrm{Tm}}(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n}))^{k/2}\langle\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})\rangle^{k_{y}/2}\kappa(x_{-\nu,p})^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{p}(y,\kappa^{-1}w_{\mathrm{Tm}}^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{k_{y}}).

Finally, since
S
is Zariski-dense on
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then the functional equation hold for every
y\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
∎

Let

-smooth boundary

be the Laplace-Beltrami operator and

-vector field on

It is well known that the diffusion process

(with reflecting boundary if

L:=\Delta+b
is exponentially ergodic, with a unique invariant probability measure
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x
for some
C^{2}
-function

on
M
, where
\text{\rm{d}}x
is the volume measure. By the law of large numbers,
\mu
is
approximated by the empirical measures
\mu_{T}
as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where

\mu_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta_{X_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}t,\ \ T>0,

and
\delta_{X_{t}}
is the Dirac measure at the point
X_{t}
. We investigate the convergence rate of
\mu_{T}
to
\mu
under the
p
-Wasserstein distance for
p\in[1,\infty)
:

(1.1)

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\nu,\nu^{\prime}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}\bigg(\int_{M\times M}\rho(x,y)^{p}\,\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P},

where
\mathscr{P}
is the set of all probability measures on
M
,
\mathscr{C}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is the class of all couplings of
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
, and
\rho
is the Riemannian distance on
M
.

Write
L=\hat{L}+Z
, where
\hat{L}:=\Delta+\nabla V
is the symmetric part of
L
, and
Z:=b-\nabla V

is the anti-symmetric part with

{\rm div}_{\mu}\,Z=0,
i.e.

\int_{M}(Zf)\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0,\ \ f\in C^{1}(M).

According to
[
31
]
, for every
0\neq f\in L^{2}(\mu)
with
\mu(f):=\int_{M}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0,
we have

\sqrt{T}\mu_{T}(f)\rightarrow N(0,2{\bf V}(f))\ \text{ weakly\ as\ }T\rightarrow\infty,

where
N(0,2{\bf V}(f))
is the centered Normal distribution with variance
2{\bf V}(f)
,
{\bf V}(f)
is defined by

{\bf V}(f):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(fP_{t}f)\text{\rm{d}}t\in(0,\infty),

and
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the Markov semigroup for
X_{t}
, i.e.

P_{t}f(x):=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X_{t})],\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in M,\ f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M).

Here, and in what follows,
\mathbb{E}^{x}
stands for the expectation taken for the underly Markov process starting from
x
at time
0
. In general, for any probability measure
\nu
on the state space,

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}
denotes the expectation taken for the underly Markov process with initial distribution
\nu
.

For two positive variables
A
and
B
, we denote
A\preceq B
if
A\leq cB
holds for some constant
c>0
,
and write
A\asymp B
if
A\preceq B
and
B\preceq A
.

[
28
, Theorem 1.2]
provides the following estimates on the convergence rate of
\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{T},\mu):

\bullet

When

, for any
(p,q)\in[1,\infty)\times[1,\infty)
, then for large
T>0

(1.2)

(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{2q}(\mu_{T},\mu)])^{\frac{1}{q}}\asymp T^{-1}\text{\ uniformly\ in\ }\nu\in\mathscr{P}.

\bullet

When

, (
1.2
) holds provided

p\in[1,\infty)
and

.

\bullet

When

) holds provided

\bullet

When

for large

\bullet

There exists a constant

and

2
Main results under a general framework

We first recall the framework studied in
[
28
]
, then state the main results derived in the present paper.

2.1
The framework

Let
(M,\rho)
be a bounded length space, let
\mathscr{P}
be the set of all probability measures on
M
, let
\mathscr{B}_{b}(M)
be the class of bounded measurable functions on
M
, and let
C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)
be the set of all bounded Lipschitz continuous functions on
M
.
For any

p\in[1,\infty)
, the
p
-Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{p}
is defined as in (
1.1
).
Let
\hat{X}_{t}
be a reversible Markov process on
M
with the unique invariant probability measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}
having full support
M
. Throughout the paper, we simply denote
\mu(f)=\int_{M}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu
for
f\in L^{1}(\mu).

The Markov semigroup
\hat{P}_{t}
of
\hat{X}_{t}
is formulated as

\hat{P}_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(\hat{X}_{t})],\ \ t\geq 0,\,x\in M,\,f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M),

where
\mathbb{E}^{x}
stands for the expectation
for the underlying Markov process starting at point
x
. In general, for any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
,
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}
stands for the expectation for the underlying Markov process with initial distribution
\nu
.

Let
(\hat{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}))
and

(\hat{L},\mathscr{D}(\hat{L}))
be, respectively, the associated symmetric Dirichlet form and self-adjoint generator in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
We assume that
C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)
is a dense subset of
\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
under the
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{1}
-norm

\|f\|_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{1}}:=\sqrt{\mu(f^{2})+\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)},

and

\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,g)=\int_{M}\Gamma(f,g)\text{\rm{d}}\mu,\ \ f,g\in C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)

holds for
a symmetric square field (carré du champ operator)

\Gamma:C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)\times C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{b}(M),

such that for any
f,g,h\in C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)
and
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),
we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Gamma(f,f)(x)}=|\nabla f(x)|:=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{\rho(x,y)},\ \ x\in M,

\displaystyle\Gamma(fg,h)=f\Gamma(g,h)+g\Gamma(f,h),\ \ \ \Gamma(\phi(f),h)=\phi^{\prime}(f)\Gamma(f,h).

Moreover, we assume that
\hat{L}
satisfies the chain rule

\hat{L}\Phi(f)=\Phi^{\prime}(f)\hat{L}f+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(f)|\nabla f|^{2},\ \ \ f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{L})\cap C_{b,{\rm L}}(M),\,\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Let

Z:C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{b}(M)

be a bounded vector field with
{\rm div}_{\mu}Z=0
, i.e. it satisfies

\displaystyle Z(fg)=fZg+gZf,\ \ Z(\phi(f))=\phi^{\prime}(f)Zf,\ \ \ f,g\in C_{b,{\rm L}}(M),\ \phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\infty}:=\inf\big\{K\geq 0:\ |Zf|\leq K|\nabla f|,\ f\in C_{b,{\rm L}}(M)\big\}<\infty,

\displaystyle\mu(Zf):=\int_{M}(Zf)\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0,\ \ \ f\in C_{b,{\rm L}}(M).

Consequently,
Z
uniquely extends to a bounded linear operator from
\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
to
L^{2}(\mu)
satisfying

(2.1)

\mu(fZg)=-\mu(gZf),\ \ \ f,g\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}),

and

\mathscr{E}(f,g):=\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,g)+\mu(fZg),\ \ \ f,g\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})=\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})

is a (non-symmetric) conservative Dirichlet form with generator

L:=\hat{L}+Z,\ \ \ \mathscr{D}(L)=\mathscr{D}(\hat{L}),

which satisfies the chain rule

L\Phi(f)=\Phi^{\prime}(f)Lf+\Phi^{\prime\prime}(f)|\nabla f|^{2},\ \ \ f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{L})\cap C_{b,{\rm L}}(M),\,\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Assume that
L
generates a unique diffusion process
X_{t}
on
M
. The associated Markov semigroup is given by

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X_{t})],\ \ x\in M,\,t\geq 0,\,f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M).

By Duhamel’s formula,
P_{t}
and
\hat{P}_{t}
have the following relation

(2.2)

P_{t}f=\hat{P}_{t}f+\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}\{Z\hat{P}_{t-s}f\}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}),\,t\geq 0.

We investigate the convergence of empirical measures

\mu_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta_{X_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}t
to
\mu
under the Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{p}
.

2.2
Upper bound estimate

To estimate
(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)])^{\frac{1}{q}}
from above, we make the following assumption.

(A)

The following conditions hold for some
d\in[1,\infty)
and an increasing function
K:[2,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
.

\bullet

Nash inequality.
There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.3)

\mu(f^{2})\leq C\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\mu(|f|)^{\frac{4}{d+2}},\ \ f\in\mathscr{D}_{0}:=\big\{f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}):\ \mu(f)=0\big\}.

\bullet

Continuity of symmetric diffusion.
For any
p\in[2,\infty)
,

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\rho(\hat{X}_{0},\hat{X}_{t})^{p}]=\int_{M\times M}\rho(x,y)^{p}\hat{p}_{t}(x,y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y)\leq K(p)t^{\frac{p}{2}},\ \ t\in[0,1],

where
\hat{p}_{t}
is the heat kernel of
\hat{P}_{t}
with respect to
\mu
.

\bullet

Boundedness of Riesz transform.
For any
p\in[2,\infty)
,

(2.5)

\|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq K(p)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)},\ \ f\in L^{p}(\mu)\text{ with }\mu(f)=0.

It is well known that
(A)
holds for the (reflecting) diffusion process generated by
\hat{L}:=\Delta+\nabla V
considered in Introduction.

Besides the elliptic diffusion process on compact manifolds, some criteria on the Nash inequality (
2.3
) are available in
[
23
, Section 3.4]
. In general, (
2.3
) implies that for some constant
c_{0}>0
,

(2.6)

\|\hat{P}_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{q}(\mu)}\leq c_{0}(1\wedge t)^{-\frac{d(q-p)}{2pq}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{1}t},\ \ t>0,\ 1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty,

and that
-\hat{L}
has purely discrete spectrum with all eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
, which are listed in the increasing order counting multiplicities, satisfy

(2.7)

\lambda_{i}\geq c_{1}i^{\frac{2}{d}},\ \ i\geq 0,

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. The Markov semigroup
\hat{P}_{t}
generated by
\hat{L}
has symmetric heat kernel
\hat{p}_{t}
with respect to
\mu
formulated as

(2.8)

\hat{p}_{t}(x,y)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{i}t}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y),\ \ t>0,\,x,y\in M.

All these assertions can be found for instance in
[
9
]
.

The condition (
2.4
) is natural for diffusion processes due to the growth property
\mathbb{E}|B_{t}-B_{0}|^{p}\leq ct^{\frac{p}{2}}
for the Brownian motion
B_{t}
. There are plentiful results on the boundedness condition (
2.5
) for the Riesz transform, see
[
4
,
7
,
8
]
and references therein.

The following result shows that under assumption
(A)
, the convergence rate of
(\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu))^{\frac{1}{q}}
is given by

\gamma_{d}(T):=\begin{cases}T^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\textrm{if }\ d\in[1,4),\\
T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log T},&\textrm{if }\ d=4,\\
T^{-\frac{1}{d-2}},&\textrm{if }\ d\in(4,\infty).\end{cases}

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
(A)
.
Then for any
(k,p,q)\in(1,\infty]\times[1,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
, there exists a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.9)

\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c\|h_{\nu}\|_{L^{k}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{q}}\gamma_{d}(T),\ \ T\geq 2,\ \nu=h_{\nu}\mu\in\mathscr{P}\text{ with }h_{\nu}\in L^{k}(\mu),

where
\nu=h_{\nu}\mu
means
\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}=h_{\nu}.

To show that
\gamma_{d}(T)
is the convergence rate of
\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}
uniformly in
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
, we restrict
p
to the set

I(d):=[1,\infty)\cap\bigg[1,\frac{2d}{(d-2)^{+}}\lor\frac{d(d-2)}{2}\bigg]=\begin{cases}[1,\infty),\ &\text{if}\ d\in[1,2],\\
[1,\frac{2d}{d-2}],\ &\text{if}\ d\in(2,4],\\
[1,\frac{d(d-2)}{2}],\ &\text{if}\ d\in(4,\infty).\end{cases}

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume
(A)
with
(
2.4
)
strengthened as

(2.10)

\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\rho(\hat{X}_{0},\hat{X}_{t})^{p}]\leq K(p)t^{\frac{p}{2}},\ \ p\in[1,\infty),\ t\in[0,1].

Then for any
p\in I(d)
and
q\in(0,\infty)
, there exists a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.11)

\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)]\leq c\gamma_{d}(T)^{q},\ \ T\geq 2.

When
p\notin I(d)
, for any
q\in(0,\infty)
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.12)

\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)]\leq c\gamma_{d}(T)^{\beta q},\ \ T\geq 2.

Consequently, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty)
,

\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(T^{-1})}\log\bigg[\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}^{q}(\mu_{T},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{2\lor(d-2)}.

2.3
Lower bound estimate

To derive sharp lower bound estimates, we make the following assumption
(B)
which holds in particular for the (reflecting) diffusion operator
\hat{L}:=\Delta+\nabla V
on a
d
-dimensional compact connected Riemannian manifold, since in this case conditions (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) are well known,
and the other conditions have been verified by
[
30
, Lemma 5.2]
. For
M
being a smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (
2.15
) is known as Sard’s lemma (see
[
13
, p130, Excercise 5.5]
) and
has been discussed in
[
5
, Section 3.1.6]
. The function
f_{\xi}
in (
2.16
) is called Lusin’s approximation of
h
(see
[
2
,
19
]
).

(B)

Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
be all eigenvalues of
-\hat{L}
listed in the increasing order with multiplicities. There exist constants
\kappa>0
and
d\in[1,\infty)
such that

(2.13)

\lambda_{i}\leq\kappa\,i^{\frac{2}{d}},\ \ \ i\geq 0,

(2.14)

\mathbb{W}_{1}(\nu\hat{P}_{t},\mu)\leq\kappa\,\mathbb{W}_{1}(\nu,\mu),\ \ t\in[0,1],\,\nu\in\mathscr{P}.

Moreover, for any
f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
,

(2.15)

\mu\big(\{|\nabla f|>0,f=0\}\big)=0,

and there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
f
such that

(2.16)

Proof.

q+\varsigma\in J(d)
. Then by (
6.10
) and Chebyshev’s inequality, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
q+\varsigma
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mu}\big(|\Xi_{\varepsilon}(T)|>r\big)\leq\mathbb{P}^{\mu}\big(|\Xi(T)|>r\big)\leq 1\wedge(Cr^{-q-\varsigma}),\ \ r>0,\,T\geq 1,\,\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Hence, according to the dominated convergence theorem on the measure space
(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{q-1}\text{\rm{d}}r)
, in order to prove (
6.28
), it is enough to show that for some proper choice of
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(T)\in(0,1)
with
\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\varepsilon(T)=0
,

(6.29)

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T,\varepsilon},\mu)-\Xi_{\varepsilon}(T)\}^{-}>r\big)=0,\ \ r>0.

By the argument used the proof of
[
28
, Theorem 2.4, p.35-37]
, under
(C)
we have

(6.30)

\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T,\varepsilon},\mu)-\Xi_{\varepsilon}(T)\}^{-}>r\big)\leq\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(B_{T,\varepsilon}^{c}),\ \ \varepsilon\in(0,1),

where
\theta
is the constant in the assumption
(C)
, and

B_{T,\varepsilon}:=\big\{\|f_{T,\varepsilon}-1\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq c_{1}T^{-\frac{2+2\theta}{2+3\theta}}\big\}

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. By Chebyshev’s inequality and (
2.6
) for
(2,\infty)
in place of
(p,q)
, we find a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(B_{T,\varepsilon}^{c})\leq c_{1}^{-1}T^{\frac{2+2\theta}{2+3\theta}}\mathbb{E}^{\mu}\big[\|f_{T,\varepsilon}-1\|_{\infty}^{2}\big]\leq c_{2}T^{\frac{2+2\theta}{2+3\theta}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mu}\big[\|f_{T,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}-1\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\big].

Combining this estimate with (
6.6
), it follows that for the choice
\varepsilon=T^{-\zeta}
with sufficiently small
\zeta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(B_{T,\varepsilon}^{c})=0.

By (
6.30
), this implies (
6.29
), and hence finishes the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

We only prove the first assertion, as the second can be proved in the same way.

(a) For any
r\in(0,\infty)
, we define

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{T,r}:=\frac{1}{T-r}\int_{r}^{T}\delta_{X_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}t,\ \ T>r,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{T,r}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{r}^{T+r}\delta_{X_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}t,\ \ T>0.

By (
4.13
), there exists a constant
c(r)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(6.31)

\nu_{r}:=\nu P_{r}^{*}\leq c(r)\mu,\ \ \nu\in\mathscr{P}.

Then the Markov property, (
2.9
) and (
6.1
) imply

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)-\bar{\Xi}_{r}(T)\}^{+}|^{q}\big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu_{r}}\big[|\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T-r},\mu)-\Xi(T-r)\}^{+}|^{q}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c(r)2^{q-1}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mu}\big[|\{(T-r)\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T-r},\mu)-\Xi(T-r)\}^{+}|^{q}+r^{q}\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2q}(\mu_{T-r},\mu)\big]=0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)-\tilde{\Xi}_{r}(T)\}^{+}|^{q}\big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu_{r}}\big[|\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T},\mu)-\Xi(T)\}^{+}|^{q}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c(r)\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mu}\big[|\{T\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T},\mu)-\Xi(T)\}^{+}|^{q}=0.

So, it remains to verify that for any
r\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
,

(6.32)

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}T\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T},\mu)-\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)|^{q}\big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=0,

(6.33)

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}T\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\tilde{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)-\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)|^{q}\big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=0.

(b) Verification of (
6.32
). Noting that for
\mu_{r}:=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\delta_{X_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}t
, we have

(6.34)

\mu_{T}=(\theta-rT^{-1})\bar{\mu}_{T,r}+rT^{-1}\mu_{r}+(1-\theta)\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\ \ \theta\in(rT^{-1},1),

by (
3.17
) and the triangle inequality, we obtain

(6.35)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{T},\bar{\mu}_{T,r})\leq\sqrt{\theta}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\bar{\mu}_{T,r}+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r},\ \bar{\mu}_{T,r}\big)\\
&\leq\sqrt{\theta}\Big[\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\bar{\mu}_{T,r}+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r},\ (1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\mu+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r}\big)\\
&\qquad\quad+\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\mu+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r},\ \mu\big)+\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu\big)\Big],\ \ \theta\in(rT^{-1},1).\end{split}

By (
3.17
), we obtain

(6.36)

\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\bar{\mu}_{T,r}+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r},\ (1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\mu+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r}\big)\leq\mathbb{W}_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu),

and by Lemma
3.5
, we find a constant
c_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
T>r
and
\theta\in(rT^{-1},1)
,

(6.37)

\begin{split}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((1-\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})\mu+\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\mu_{r},\ \mu\big)\leq c_{1}\cdot\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}rT^{-1},&\textrm{if }d\in[1,2),\\
\theta^{-1}rT^{-1}\log(1+\theta r^{-1}T),&\textrm{if }d=2,\\
(\theta^{-1}rT^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}},&\textrm{if }d\in(2,4).\end{cases}\end{split}

Moreover, by (
6.31
), the Markov property and (
2.9
), we find a constant
c_{2}(r)>0
such that

(6.38)

\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2q}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu_{r}}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2q}(\mu_{T-r},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c_{2}(r)T^{-1},\ \ T\geq 2r+2.

Combining (
6.35
)-(
6.38
) together, we find a constant
c_{3}>0
, depending on
r\in(0,\infty)
, such that for any
T\geq 2r+2
and
\theta\in(rT^{-1},1)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2q}(\mu_{T},\bar{\mu}_{T,r})]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c_{3}\cdot\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}T^{-2}+\theta T^{-1},&\textrm{if }d\in[1,2),\\
\theta^{-1}T^{-2}\log^{2}(\theta T)+\theta T^{-1},&\textrm{if }d=2,\\
\theta^{-\frac{2}{d}}T^{-\frac{d+2}{d}}+\theta T^{-1},&\textrm{if }d\in(2,4).\end{cases}

Choosing

\displaystyle\theta=\begin{cases}T^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\textrm{if }d\in[1,2],\\
T^{-\frac{2}{d+2}},&\textrm{if }d\in(2,4),\end{cases}

which is in

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2q}(\mu_{T},\bar{\mu}_{T,r})]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\preceq\begin{cases}T^{-\frac{3}{2}},&\textrm{if }d\in[1,2),\\
T^{-\frac{3}{2}}\log^{2}(T),&\textrm{if }d=2,\\
T^{-\frac{d+4}{d+2}},&\textrm{if }d\in(2,4),\end{cases}\ \ \ q\in[1,\infty).

Combining this with (

\displaystyle T\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T},\mu)-\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)|^{q}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq T\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[|\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T},\bar{\mu}_{T,r})+2\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{T},\bar{\mu}_{T,r})\mathbb{W}_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{T,r},\mu)|^{q}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

So, (
6.32
) holds.

We have

By using this formula replacing (

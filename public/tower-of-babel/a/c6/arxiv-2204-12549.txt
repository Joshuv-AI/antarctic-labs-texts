ON L ∞ L^{\infty} ESTIMATES FOR FULLY NONLINEAR PARTIAL DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS 1 1 1 Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation under grants DMS-1855947 and DMS-22-03273.


Abstract
Sharp L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates are obtained for general classes of fully non-linear PDE’s on non-Kähler manifolds, complementing the theory developed earlier by the authors in joint work with F. Tong for the Kähler case. The key idea is still a comparison with an auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation, but this time on a ball with Dirichlet boundary conditions, so that it always admits a unique solution. The method applies not just to compact Hermitian manifolds, but also to the Dirichlet problem, to open manifolds with a positive lower bound on their injectivity radii, to ( n − 1 ) (n-1) form equations, and even to non-integrable almost-complex or symplectic manifolds. It is the first method applicable in any generality to large classes of non-linear equations, and it usually improves on other methods when they happen to be available for specific equations.



Bin Guo and Duong H. Phong

1
Introduction

A priori estimates are essential to partial differential equations, and of these,
L^{\infty}
estimates are of particular importance, as they are usually also required for other estimates. In particular, the modern theory of partial differential equations can be argued to have started with the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory of
L^{\infty}
estimates for linear equations in divergence form. The importance of
L^{\infty}
estimates for non-linear equations was recognized early on in the case of complex Monge-Ampère equations on Kähler manifolds, where they were first obtained by Yau
[
54
]
in his celebrated solution of the Calabi conjecture.
A sharp version was subsequently obtained by Kolodziej
[
39
]
using pluripotential theory. Very recently, an alternative proof of Kolodziej’s estimates using only PDE methods was provided by the authors in joint work with F. Tong
[
32
]
. This proof also gave new and sharp estimates for general classes of non-linear equations on Kähler manifolds, thus providing a first step towards a De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory for fully non-linear equations in complex geometry.

The goal of the present paper is to obtain sharp
L^{\infty}
estimates for fully non-linear equations in non-Kähler settings. There is a strong incentive for this, as non-Kähler settings appear increasingly frequently in complex geometry, symplectic geometry, and theoretical physics (see e.g.
[
21
,
23
,
24
,
42
,
44
]
for surveys). Extensions from Kähler to non-Kähler settings are typically technically much more complicated, as new torsion terms can proliferate and integration by parts is unwieldy. However, there are usually considerable new conceptual difficulties as well, because of a lack of cohomological constraints. In particular, because of this, a basic idea from
[
32
]
, namely a comparison with an auxiliary global complex Monge-Ampère equation, runs immediately into difficulties: in the absence of suitable cohomological constraints, the auxiliary equation may not be solvable.

In this paper, we shall show that comparisons with auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations are still of powerful assistance, but the auxiliary equations should not be defined globally, but only on an open ball with Dirichlet conditions. The existence and regularity of the auxiliary problem which we then need is a classic result of Caffarelli, Kohn, Nirenberg, and Spruck
[
4
]
, which plays the same role for the Dirichlet problem as Yau’s existence theorem
[
54
]
did in
[
32
]
for the compact Kähler case. An important point is that the desired global estimates can still be obtained from the local estimates obtained in this manner near a minimum. This requires an adaptation of the original arguments of Blocki
[
2
]
, combined with weak Harnack inequalities for supersolutions of linear elliptic equations
[
26
]
, as well as an estimate of Kolodziej
[
39
]
which generalizes the one-dimensional estimate of Brezis-Merle
[
3
]
.

The resulting method for
L^{\infty}
estimates, using only comparisons with a local equation, turns out to be both flexible and powerful. It bypasses the complicated torsion terms and integration by parts plaguing non-Kähler geometry mentioned above, and it applies to whole new classes of fully non-linear equations, not just on compact Hermitian manifolds, but also to the Dirichlet problem, to open manifolds, to
(n-1)
-form equations, and to equations on almost-complex manifolds. The extension to almost-complex manifolds, using auxiliary real Monge-Ampère equations, is in particular of considerable interest,
as non-integrable almost-complex manifolds have revealed themselves to be effective intermediaries between complex geometry and symplectic geometry (see e.g.
[
37
,
13
,
50
,
20
]
). On the other hand, if we assume the Kähler condition, then comparisons with a global equation as in
[
32
]
are possible, and we obtain
L^{\infty}
estimates which have the great advantage of remaining uniform over classes of Kähler metrics possibly degenerating to the boundary of the Kähler cone. Such a uniformity is essential for many applications to algebraic geometry (see e.g.
[
19
,
14
,
31
]
). Thus the present paper and
[
32
]
address different geometric requirements in complex geometry, and together, they are a natural candidate for what may be viewed as a De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory for non-linear equations in this area.

We describe now more fully our results.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a connected complex manifold of complex dimension
n
, and
\omega=\sum_{jk}ig_{\bar{k}j}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}
be a Hermitian metric. Let
f:\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}_{+}
be
a function satisfying the following conditions:

(1)
\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}
is a symmetric cone with

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}

(1.1)

where
\Gamma_{k}
is the cone of vectors
\lambda

for

. Here

\lambda
. In particular,
\Gamma_{1}
is the half-space defined by
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0
, and
\Gamma_{n}

\lambda_{j}>0

(2)

is symmetric in

and it is homogeneous of degree one;

(3)
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}>0
for each

and
\lambda\in\Gamma
;

(4) There is a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\geq\gamma,\quad\forall\lambda\in\Gamma.

(1.2)

Familiar examples of such nonlinear operators
f(\lambda)
include
the complex Monge-Ampère operator
f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j})^{1\over n}
,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
;
the complex Hessian operator
f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=(\sigma_{k}(\lambda))^{1\over k}
,

\Gamma=\Gamma_{k}
where
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
is the
k
-th symmetric polynomial in the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}

[
5
,
17
,
51
]
;
and the

[

,

]
,

f(\lambda)=\Big(\prod_{I}\lambda_{I}\Big)^{\frac{n!}{(n-p)!p!}}
,
where

runs over all distinct multi-indices

,
\lambda_{I}=\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}
, and
\Gamma
is the cone defined by

p

Clearly, the above conditions (1-4) are preserved on the intersection of the individual cones by finite linear combinations with positive coefficients of operators

satisfying them. Remarkably, it has recently been shown by Harvey and Lawson
[

that the set of operators

satisfying the conditions (1-4) is quite large, and actually includes all invariant Garding-Dirichlet operators.

For any
C^{2}
function

, we set
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
and let

be the endomorphism defined by
\omega_{\varphi}
relative to
\omega

(h_{\varphi})^{j}{}_{i}=g^{j\bar{k}}(g_{\varphi})_{\bar{k}i}
, where

denotes the components of
\omega
and
(g^{i\bar{j}})
its inverse. We denote by
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
the (unordered) vector of eigenvalues of
h_{\varphi}
.
Given a smooth function
F
on
X
, we consider the fully nonlinear partial differential equation

f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\mbox{ and }\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma.

(1.3)

Such equations were introduced systematically in the foundational paper by Caffarelli, Nirenberg, and Spruck
[
5
]
, and occur now regularly in geometric analysis, e.g.
[
27
,
51
]
.

Theorem 1

Assume that
(X,\omega)
is a compact Hermitian manifold without boundary, and consider the equation (
1.3
) with the operator
f(\lambda)
satisfying the conditions (1-4).
Then for any
p>n
and any
C^{2}
solution
\varphi
to (
1.3
), normalized to satisfy

\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, we have

{\sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C,

(1.4)

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
X,\omega,n,p,\gamma
, and
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
. Here the norm
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
is defined by

\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}=\int_{X}(1+|F|^{p})e^{nF}\omega^{n}

and is sometimes called the
p
-th Nash entropy of the function
e^{nF}
.

We survey briefly the previous known results related to this theorem. The case
(X,\omega)
Kähler was established in
[
32
]
, including even a stronger version allowing degenerations of the underlying Kähler class
\omega
generalizing the case of the Monge-Ampère equation obtained in
[
19
,
14
]
. So we concentrate on the case of
(X,\omega)
non-Kähler, which is the focus of the present paper. In this case, for the Monge-Ampère equation,
L^{\infty}
estimates in the non-Kähler case were first obtained by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
48
]
, building on earlier works of Cherrier
[
10
]
, and a pointwise upper bound for
e^{nF}
was needed.
The stronger version requiring only weaker entropy bounds as described in Theorem
1
was first obtained by Dinew and Kolodziej
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
39
]
, using a non-trivial extension of pluripotential theory to the Hermitian setting. More recently a new approach using the theory of envelopes has been developed by Guedj and Lu
[
29
]
. The proof of Theorem
1
is arguably simpler than in all these approaches. More importantly, it is a PDE-based proof, and can apply to many more equations. The estimates in Theorem
1
appear to be the first
L^{\infty}
estimates obtained in any generality for fully non-linear equations on compact Hermitian manifolds.

It may be worth noting an intriguing consequence of Theorem
1
, or rather of its proof, as we shall see later in Section §3.
Given a
C^{2}
function
\varphi
such that
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0
defines a Hermitian metric on
X
, in general the following identity does not hold

\int_{X}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=\int_{X}\omega^{n},

unless extra conditions
[
15
]
are put on

. So it is interesting to estimate the lower/upper bound of the volume of
(X,\omega_{\varphi}),
i.e. the integral
\int_{X}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}
.
To this end, we define the relative volume
e^{nF}
of
\omega_{\varphi}
with respect to
\omega
by
e^{nF}=\omega_{\varphi}^{n}/\omega^{n}
. Then
\varphi
can be viewed as the solution of a complex Monge-Ampère equation:

(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(1.5)

Normalizing
\varphi
so that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, we can apply Theorem
1
and its proof and obtain:

Corollary 1

Given
p>n
and
K>0
, suppose
e^{nF}
satisfies
\int_{X}e^{nF}|F|^{p}\omega^{n}\leq K
, then there is a constant
c>0
which depends on

and
K
such that

\int_{X}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=\int_{X}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\geq c.

(1.6)

Thus an upper bound on the
p
-th entropy of relative volume

implies a positive lower bound of the
L^{1}
-norm of
e^{nF}
. This is trivial if
\omega
is Kähler, but not in general. We remark that if
X
admits a closed real
(1,1)
-form
\beta
whose Bott-Chern class
[\beta]\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,{\bf C})
is nef and big (i.e.

is of
Fujiki class
{\mathcal{C}}
), by a weak transcendental Morse inequality of Demailly
[
13
]
, equation (
1.6
) holds even without the assumption on
\int_{X}e^{nF}|F|^{p}\omega^{n}
. This is based on an observation of Tosatti
[
47
]
. For the convenience of readers, we provide a proof (see Lemma
9
in Section §
3
) of this fact using the arguments in
[
13
]
.

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
1
is to employ a
local
auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equations as in
[
32
]
and a localized argument similar to that in
[
2
]
. As stressed above, this method is effective in a vast range of different geometric situations. We discuss some of these next.

The Dirichlet problem on Hermitian manifolds

We consider first the case of the Dirichlet problem. Thus let
X
be an open subset with smooth boundary in a larger Hermitian manifold
(\tilde{X},\omega)
.
Let
f(\lambda)
be an operator satisfying the conditions (1-4),
\rho\in C^{0}(\partial X)
, and consider the following Dirichlet boundary value problem on
X
:

f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F}\quad\mbox{in }X,\quad\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma,\quad\varphi=\rho\ {\rm on}\ \partial X.

(1.7)

Theorem 2

Given
p>n
, suppose
\varphi\in C^{2}(X)\cap C^{0}(\overline{X})
is a solution to the above Dirichlet problem (
1.7
). Then there is a constant
C>0
which depends only on
X,\omega,n,p,\gamma
and,
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
, such that

{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial X)}+C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}^{2}).

(1.8)

We observe that, for complex Monge-Ampère and Hessian equations, and if
\omega
is Kähler, then
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}
can be bounded in terms of the other parameters (see §4.6). More importantly, for general equations
f(\lambda)
, there are very few
L^{\infty}
estimates, if any, which require only weak integral bounds on the right hand side
e^{F}
such as in Theorem
2
. For example, the
L^{\infty}
estimates obtained by S.Y. Li in
[
40
]
are based on the construction of subsolutions, and as is usually the case with subsolutions, they require
L^{\infty}
bounds on the right hand side.

Open Hermitian manifolds

Next, we consider the case of a noncompact complex manifold
(X,\omega)
, where
\omega
is a complete Hermitian metric on
X
. We shall make the following assumption:

(A) There exists a constant
r_{0}>0
, so that, at each point
x\in X
, there exists a holomorphic coordinates chart
(U_{x},z)
centered at
x
satisfying

\{|z|<2r_{0}\}\subset U_{x},\quad\frac{1}{2}\omega\leq i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq 2\omega\mbox{ in }U_{x}.

(1.9)

With the assumption (
1.9
), we introduce two quantities associated to
\varphi
and
e^{F}
, which we assume are bounded. Given a fixed
p>n
, we set

K_{\varphi}=\sup_{x\in X}\int_{U_{x}}|\varphi|\omega^{n},\quad K_{F}=\sup_{x\in X}\int_{U_{x}}e^{nF}|F|^{p}\omega^{n}.

(1.10)

Theorem 3

Let
(X,\omega)
be a noncompact Hermitian manifold satisfying the assumption (A).
Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap C^{2}(X)
be a solution to (
1.7
).
Then there is a constant
C>0
which depends on
n,p,\omega,\gamma
and
K_{\varphi},K_{F}
such that

{\sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C+{\rm liminf}_{z\to\infty}(-\varphi)_{+}

where
C
is a constant depending on
X,\omega,n,p,\gamma,r_{0}
, and
K_{\varphi},K_{F}
.

We remark that the assumption (
1.9
) is satisfied if the metric
\omega
is Kähler, the Riemannian curvature is bounded and the injectivity radius of
(X,\omega)
at each point is bounded from below by some positive number (see
[
46
]
). In particular, it holds for
asymptotically locally Euclidean
(ALE) Kähler manifolds, or under some asymptotic conditions on the (Kähler) metric
\omega
near infinity, such as the
asymptotically conical
(AC) and
asymptotically cylindrical
(ACyl) conditions, which have been extensively studied in recent years. Typically the conditions which have been imposed in the literature on the open complete Kähler case are stronger than in the above theorem, see e.g.
[
46
]
. But more importantly, we are not aware of previous results for general fully non-linear equations on open complete Hermitian manifolds.

General
(n-1)
-form equations with gradient terms

We show now how the local comparison method developed in this paper can apply to other second-order equations and not just functions of the eigenvalues of the standard Hessian
h_{\varphi}
. Well-known examples of such equations arose in
[
22
,
44
]
and in the solution of the Gauduchon conjecture
[
45
,
51
,
44
]
, and they have been attracting considerable attention ever since
[
27
,
25
]
.

More specifically,
let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with
\partial X=\emptyset
and
\omega_{h}
be another Hermitian metric on
X
. Fix a smooth
(1,0)
-form
\theta
on
X
, set

\displaystyle\chi[\varphi]=i(\varphi_{j}\bar{\theta}_{k}+\theta_{j}\varphi_{\bar{k}})dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k},

(1.11)

and consider the endomorphism
\tilde{h}_{\varphi}
of the tangent bundle
T^{1,0}(X)
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\varphi}=\omega^{-1}\cdot\big\{\omega_{h}+{1\over n-1}((\Delta_{\omega}\varphi)\omega-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)+\chi[\varphi])\big\}

(1.12)

where

\Delta_{\omega}\varphi=n\frac{i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}}

is the complex Laplacian of
\varphi
with respect to
\omega
. We consider the fully non-linear equation

f(\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\quad\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma,

(1.13)

with
\varphi
normalized by
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
. We have then:

Theorem 4

Given
p>2n
, there exists a constant
C>0
which depends on
n,\gamma,\omega,p,\theta,\omega_{h}
and
\|e^{2nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}(X,\omega)}
such that

{\sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C.

Previously, this equation had received most attention when
f(\lambda)=(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j})^{1\over n}
,
\chi[\varphi]=0
, and was known in this case as the
(n-1)
-form Monge-Ampère equation.
It was shown by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
49
]
to be solvable up to a modification of
F
by a suitable additive constant. This required a priori estimates of all orders, of which, as stressed in
[
49
]
, the
L^{\infty}
estimate is a major one.

In an earlier version of the present paper (arXiv:2204.12549), we had given a new and simpler proof of the
L^{\infty}
estimate of Tosatti and Weinkove using our present method. Our proof was subsequently extended by Klemyatin, Liang, and Wang
[
38
]
to more general operators
f(\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}])
satisfying the structural conditions (1-4).

The above Theorem
4
shows that
L^{\infty}
bounds can still be established even with the incorporation of gradient terms
\chi[\varphi]
in the equation
f(\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}])
. Such terms are actually required for the solution of the Gauduchon conjecture
[
45
]
, and in general, they can lead to considerable additional difficulties in a priori estimates (see e.g.
[
45
,
28
]
). Here, we show that these terms can readily be handled by our method, just by using as auxiliary equation the Dirichlet problem for a
real
Monge-Ampère equation instead of the usual complex one. The only cost of using a real auxiliary equation is the constraint
p>2n
in the
p
-Nash entropy, instead of the milder condition
p>n
when
\chi=0
(arXiv:2204.12549,
[
38
]
). It is not known whether
p>n
would suffice when
\chi\not=0
.

Fully non-linear equations on almost-complex manifolds

While the importance of almost-complex structures has been known for a long-time, the structures which are not integrable had been comparatively neglected. This situation changed drastically in the last twenty years or so, as basic geometric structures, notably forms in lower dimensions or symplectic structures, have led to non-integrable almost-complex structures. We refer to
[
37
,
13
,
50
]
for more detailed discussions. Here we would like to point out that the methods of this paper readily adapt to produce
L^{\infty}
estimates for general classes of non-linear equations, even on almost-complex manifolds.

Let
X
be a compact manifold without boundary of real dimension
2n
, equipped with an almost complex structure
J
, that is, an endomorphism of the tangent bundle satisfying
J^{2}=-id
. The complexified tangent bundle
T_{{\bf C}}X
can be decomposed as a direct sum of
T^{(1,0)}X
and
T^{(0,1)}X
, which are the eigenspaces of
J
with eigenvalues
\sqrt{-1}
and
-\sqrt{-1}
, respectively. An almost Hermitian metric
g
on
X
is a Riemannian metric that is compatible with
J
, i.e.
g(Y,Z)=g(JY,JZ)
for any vector fields
Y,Z
. The associated Hermitian form
\omega
is a real positive definite
(1,1)
-form defined by
\omega(Y,Z)=g(JY,Z)
. For a smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, we define
i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
to be

\displaystyle i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(d(Jd\varphi))^{(1,1)},

(1.14)

the
(1,1)
-part of the
2
-form
\frac{1}{2}d(Jd\varphi)
. As in other sections, we write
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
, and let
h_{\varphi}=\omega^{-1}\cdot\omega_{\varphi}
be the endomorphism of the tangent bundle determined by the
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{\varphi}
and the metric
\omega
.

With this notation, we can consider the same equation on an almost-complex manifold
X
as in (
1.3
), for a given non-linear operator
f:\Gamma\to{\bf R_{+}}
satisfying the conditions (1-4) in the Introduction, and a given smooth right hand side
e^{F}
. We have then the following general
L^{\infty}
estimate for fully non-linear equations on almost-complex manifolds:

Theorem 5

Under the above assumptions, and with
\varphi
normalized to satisfy
{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\omega,\gamma,p>2n
and
\|e^{2nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}(X,\omega^{n})}
such that

{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C.

Only in the particular case when
f
is the Monge-Ampère operator was an
L^{\infty}
bound for
\varphi
obtained before
[
11
]
, and an
L^{\infty}
bound for
e^{F}
was required.

Potentials on almost-Kähler manifolds

Once we leave the realm of Kähler geometry, a severe impediment is the lack of a
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. However, if the failure of the Kähler condition is due to the non-integrability of the almost-complex structure, we are in the realm of almost-Kähler geometry, and we can still characterize almost-Kähler metrics with symplectic form by a potential.

More specifically, let
(X,J)
be an almost-complex manifold. An almost-Kähler metric is a metric
g(U,V)
which is
J
-compatible, i.e.,
g(JU,JV)=g(U,V)
, and whose associated
2
-form
\omega_{g}(U,V)=g(JU,V)
is closed. Given a tame symplectic form
\Omega
, that is, a closed
2
-form
\Omega
satisfying
\Omega(U,JU)>0
, we can consider the class of almost-Kähler metrics
g
with
[\omega_{g}]=[\Omega]
. It has been pointed out by Donaldson
[
13
]
that there is a natural version of the Calabi-Yau problem in this context, namely find an almost-Kähler metric
\tilde{g}
with
[\omega_{\tilde{g}}]=[\Omega]
and given volume form. He showed that a positive answer to this question would have important consequences in symplectic geometry.

In
[
50
]
, it has been shown that the answer is indeed positive if one can establish
L^{\infty}
estimates for the potential
\varphi
defined for any metric
\tilde{g}
with
[\omega_{\tilde{g}}]=[\Omega]
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tilde{g}}\varphi=2n-2n{\omega_{\tilde{g}}^{n-1}\wedge\Omega\over\omega_{g}^{n}},\quad{\rm sup}_{X}\varphi=0.

(1.15)

Such estimates had been reduced first in
[
53
]
to an exponential integral estimate for
\varphi
, and then to an
L^{p}
estimate in
[
50
]
. The methods of this paper can readily reduce them to an
L^{1}
estimate:

Theorem 6

Let
\tilde{g}
be an almost-Kähler metric on an almost-complex manifold
X
of dimension
2n
, with
[\omega_{\tilde{g}}]=[\Omega]
where
\Omega
is a tame symplectic form. Then there is a constant

depending only on the total volume of

such that

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{X}|\varphi|\leq C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(X,e^{nF}dV_{\tilde{g}})}),

(1.16)

where

This estimate first appeared in our recent survey
[
30
]
in honor of S.S. Chern. We were informed by V. Tosatti that the methods of
[
50

can also be extended to derive it. Our main interest in including a brief discussion in this paper is to illustrate how a feature of almost-Kähler metrics, namely the lack of first derivative terms in the metric in the Laplace-Beltrami operator, is particularly conducive to our methods.

This paper is a revised and expanded version of a paper that was first posted as arXiv: 2204:12549. We have added new material on gradient terms in
(n-1)
-form equations, on general fully non-linear equations on almost-complex manifolds, and on the Calabi-Yau equation on tame symplectic manifolds, all to show how to adapt our method of local auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations to very different geometric situations.

The key local estimate

Fix the Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega)
. We assume that there exists
r_{0}>0
so that, at any point
x_{0}\in X
, there exists a local holomorphic coordinate system

centered at
x_{0}
, with
\omega(x_{0})=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}
and

\displaystyle{1\over 2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq\omega\leq 2i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\ {\rm in}\ B(x_{0},2r_{0})

(2.1)

where

is the Euclidian ball of radius
2r_{0}
. When
X
is compact, with or without boundary, then such an
r_{0}
clearly exists, and can be viewed as determined by
X
and
\omega
. Any dependence on
r_{0}
can be absorbed into a dependence on
X
and
\omega
.
When
X
is not compact, the existence of such an

is an assumption that we always make. Without loss of generality we may assume
r_{0}\leq 1/2
throughout the paper.

Lemma 1

Let
\varphi
be a
C^{2}
solution on a Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega)
of the equation

\displaystyle f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F}

(2.2)

where the operator
f(\lambda)
satisfies the conditions (1-4) spelled out in Section §1.
Let
x_{0}
be any point in
X
, and assume that

\displaystyle\varphi(z)\geq\varphi(x_{0})\ {\rm for}\ z\in B(x_{0},2r_{0}).

(2.3)

Fix any
p>n
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\varphi(x_{0})\leq C

(2.4)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
(X,\omega)
,
p
,
\gamma
,
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
, and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}
. Here the
L^{1}
and
L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}
norms are on the ball
B(x_{0},2r_{0})
with respect to the volume form
\omega^{n}
.

We will assume
-\varphi(x_{0})\geq 2
, otherwise Lemma
1
already holds.

We break the proof into several steps. The first step is the most important, and requires a comparison of the solution
\varphi
of (
2.2
) with the solution of an auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation. More precisely, set

\displaystyle u_{s}(z)=\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+{1\over 2}|z|^{2}-s

(2.5)

for each
s
with
0<s<2r_{0}^{2}
. It is convenient to set
\Omega=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
. Then
u_{s}>0
on
\partial\Omega
, and thus the set
\Omega_{s}
defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=\{z\in\Omega;u_{s}(z)<0\}

(2.6)

is an open and nonempty set with compact closure in
\Omega
. Set

\displaystyle A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(2.7)

Formally, the auxiliary equation which we would like to consider is the following Dirichlet problem for the complex Monge-Ampère equation on the ball
\Omega
,

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s})^{n}={(-u_{s})\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(-u_{s})\over A_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\ {\rm in}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{s}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,

(2.8)

where
\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(x)
is the characteristic function of the positive real axis, and
\psi_{s}
is required to be plurisubharmonic,
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s}\geq 0
. Since the right hand side would have singularities, we choose a sequence of smooth
positive
functions
\tau_{k}(x):{\bf R}\to{\bf R}_{+}
which converges to the function
x\cdot\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(x)
as

. Let then the function
\psi_{s,k}
be defined as the solution of the following Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}={\tau_{k}(-u_{s})\over A_{s,k}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\ {\rm in}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{s,k}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial\Omega

(2.9)

with
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k}\geq 0
, and
A_{s,k}
is defined by

\displaystyle A_{s,k}=\int_{\Omega}\tau_{k}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(2.10)

By the classic theorem of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
4
]
, this Dirichlet problem admits a unique solution
\psi_{s,k}
which is of class
C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
. The maximum of
\psi_{s,k}
can only be attained in
\partial\Omega
, and thus
\psi_{s,k}\leq 0
. By definition of the constants
A_{s,k}
, we also have
A_{s,k}\to A_{s}
as

, and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=1.

(2.11)

Lemma 2

Let
u_{s}
be a
C^{2}
solution of the fully non-linear equation (
2.2
) and
\psi_{s,k}
be the solutions of the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
2.9
) as defined above. Then we have

\displaystyle-u_{s}\leq\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{n\over n+1}\ {\rm on}\ \bar{\Omega}

(2.12)

where
\varepsilon
is the constant defined by
\varepsilon^{n+1}=A_{s,k}\gamma^{-1}{(n+1)^{n}\over n^{2n}}
.

Proof.
We have to show that the function

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{n\over n+1}-u_{s}

(2.13)

is always
\leq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}
. Let
x_{\max}\in\overline{\Omega}
be a maximum point of
\Phi
. If
x_{\max}\in\overline{\Omega}\backslash\Omega_{s}
, clearly
\Phi(x_{\max})\leq 0
by the definition of
\Omega_{s}
and the fact that
\psi_{s,k}<0
in
\Omega
. If
x_{\max}\in\Omega_{s}
, then we have
i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi(x_{\max})\leq 0
by the maximum principle.

Let
G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}}
be the linearization of the operator
\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])
. It follows from the structure conditions of
f
that
G^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite at
h_{\varphi}
and

{\rm det}G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{f^{n}}{\rm det}(\frac{\partial f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{f(\lambda)^{n}}.

The assumption that
f(\lambda)
is homogeneous of degree 1 implies that

{\rm tr}_{G}\omega_{\varphi}=\sum_{i,j}G^{i\bar{j}}(\omega_{\varphi})_{\bar{j}i}=1.

We calculate at the point
x_{\max}
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle({\rm tr}_{G}i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi)(x_{\max})=\sum_{i,j}G^{i\bar{j}}\Phi_{\bar{j}i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}({\rm tr}_{G}i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})+\frac{\varepsilon n}{(n+1)^{2}}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{n+2}{n+1}}|\nabla\psi_{s,k}|^{2}_{G}

\displaystyle\quad-{\rm tr}_{G}\omega_{\varphi}+{\rm tr}_{G}(\omega-\frac{1}{2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}({\rm tr}_{G}i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})-1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}({\rm det}G)^{1/n}[{\rm det}(\omega^{-1}\cdot i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})]^{1/n}-1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}\gamma^{1/n}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{(-u_{s})^{1/n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}-1.

Here in the fourth line we use (
2.1
) and in the fifth line we apply the arithmetic geometric inequality. Hence at
x_{\max}
, we have

-u_{s}\leq A_{s,k}\gamma^{-1}\frac{(n+1)^{n}}{n^{2n}\varepsilon^{n}}(-\psi_{s,k})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}=\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{\frac{n}{n+1}},

by the choice of
\varepsilon.
This implies
\Phi(x_{\max})\leq 0
, and the claim is proved. Q.E.D.

Lemma 3

,

for some constant

Clearly, Theorem

The main property of the functions

in Lemma

that we need is an exponential integral estimate, which can be stated as follows. Let
\Omega\subset{\bf C}^{n}
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain.
Then there are constants
\alpha>0
and

that depend only on

(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}e^{-\alpha\psi}dV\leq C,

(2.16)

for any

with

, and
\int_{\Omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}=1
.
This exponential estimate is due to Kolodziej
[
39
]
, and it is a generalization of the inequality of Brezis-Merle
[
3
]
in complex dimension one.
While it has some similarity with the
\alpha
-invariant in Kähler geometry, it is a very different estimate as it requires information on the Monge-Ampère volume of

[

using pluripotential theory. Recently an elementary and elegant proof was given in
[

Returning to the proof of Lemma

\frac{(-u_{s})^{(n+1)/n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}\leq C(n,\gamma)(-\psi_{s,k}),\mbox{ in }\Omega_{s}.

Take a small
\beta=\beta(n,\alpha,\gamma)>0
such that
\beta C(n,\gamma)\leq\alpha
where

is as in (

\int_{\Omega_{s}}\,{\rm exp}\,\Big(\beta\frac{(-u_{s})^{(n+1)/n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}\Big)\omega^{n}\leq\int_{\Omega}\,{\rm exp}\,\Big(\beta C(n,\gamma)(-\psi_{s,k})\Big)\omega^{n}\leq C,

by the uniform integral estimate (

). We can now take the limit

and obtain the following exponential estimate for
u_{s}
,

Lemma 4

For any
0<s<s_{0}
, the functions
u_{s}
satisfy the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{\beta{(-u_{s})^{n+1\over n}\over A_{s}^{1/n}}\big\}\omega^{n}\leq C

(2.18)

where

and

The next step is to show that this exponential inequality for
u_{s}
implies the following estimate for
A_{s}
:

Lemma 5

Fix
p>n
, and let
\delta_{0}={p-n\over pn}>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle A_{s}\leq C_{0}(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n})^{1+\delta_{0}}

(2.19)

where
C_{0}
is a constant depending only on
n,p,\gamma,\omega
and
\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
.

Proof.
As in
[
32
]
, we apply Young’s inequality. For our purposes, it is convenient to state Young’s inequality as follows. Let
\eta:{\bf R}_{+}\to{\bf R}_{+}
be a positive strictly increasing function with
{\rm lim}_{x\to 0^{+}}\eta(x)=0
. Let
\eta^{-1}
be the inverse function of
\eta
, so that
\eta(\eta^{-1}(y))=y
and
\eta^{-1}(\eta(x))=x
. Then for any
U,V\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle UV\leq\int_{0}^{U}\eta(x)dx+\int_{0}^{V}\eta^{-1}(y)dy.

(2.20)

If we make a change of variables
y=\eta(x)
in the second integral, this inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle UV\leq\int_{0}^{U}\eta(x)dx+\int_{0}^{\eta^{-1}(V)}x\eta^{\prime}(x)dx

(2.21)

The first integral on the right hand side can be estimated by
U\eta(U)
, while the second integral can be estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta^{-1}(V)}x\eta^{\prime}(x)dx\leq\eta^{-1}(V)\int_{0}^{\eta^{-1}(V)}\eta^{\prime}(x)dx=\eta^{-1}(V)\eta(\eta^{-1}(V))=V\eta^{-1}(V).

(2.22)

Thus we arrive at the following inequality, which suffices for our purposes,

\displaystyle UV\leq U\eta(U)+V\eta^{-1}(V).

(2.23)

We return to the proof of Lemma
5
. We apply the preceding Young’s inequality with
\eta(x)=(\,{\rm log}\,(1+x))^{p}
,
U=e^{nF(z)}
, and a general positive function
V
.
Then
\eta^{-1}(y)={\rm exp}(y^{1\over p})-1
, and we find

\displaystyle e^{nF}V\leq e^{nF}(\,{\rm log}\,(1+e^{nF}))^{p}+V\big\{{\rm exp}(V^{1\over p})-1\big\}.

(2.24)

Specializing further to
V=v(z)^{p}
for a general non-negative function
v(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle v(z)^{p}e^{nF}\leq e^{nF}(\,{\rm log}\,(1+e^{nF}))^{p}+v^{p}(e^{v}-1)\leq e^{nF}(1+n|F|)^{p}+C_{p}e^{2v(z)}.

(2.25)

Let now

\displaystyle v(z)={\beta\over 2}\frac{(-u_{s})^{(n+1)/n}}{A_{s}^{1/n}}

(2.26)

and integrate over
\Omega_{s}
to obtain

({\beta\over 2})^{p}\int_{\Omega_{s}}\frac{(-u_{s})^{(n+1)p/n}}{A_{s}^{p/n}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\leq\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}(1+n|F|)^{p}\omega^{n}+\int_{\Omega_{s}}{\rm exp}\big\{{\beta\frac{(-u_{s})^{(n+1)/n}}{A_{s}^{1/n}}}\big\}\omega^{n}.

By Lemma
5
, the right hand side is bounded by a constant
C(n,p,\beta,\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}},r_{0})
. Thus we have

\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})^{\frac{p(n+1)}{n}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\leq C(n,p,\beta,r_{0})A_{s}^{\frac{p}{n}}.

(2.27)

This implies the following upper bound for
A_{s}
: by Hölder’s inequality, we can write

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})^{p(n+1)/n}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\Big)^{n/(p(n+1))}\cdot\Big(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{s}^{1/(n+1)}\cdot\Big(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}

where
q>1
is chosen such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{n}{p(n+1)}=1
. This is the inequality to be proved in Lemma
5
. Q.E.D.

We can now derive growth estimates for the function
\phi(s)
defined by

\displaystyle\phi(s)=\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}

(2.28)

following the original strategy of De Giorgi
[

Lemma 6

The function
\phi(s)
is a monotone increasing function of
s\in(0,s_{0})
which satisfies
{\rm lim}_{s\to 0^{+}}\phi(s)=0
,
\phi(s)>0
for
s\in(0,s_{0})
, and

t\phi(s-t)\leq C_{0}(\phi(s))^{1+\delta_{0}},\quad\mbox{for all }0<t<s<s_{0}.

(2.29)

Proof.
The monotonicity of
\phi
is clear from the definition and the relation
\Omega_{s^{\prime}}\subset\Omega_{s}
if
0<s^{\prime}<s<s_{0}
.
Note that
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}
for any
s>0
. Being a nonempty open set,
\Omega_{s}
has positive measure. So
\phi(s)>0
for any
s>0
.
Furthermore,
\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+{1\over 2}|z|^{2}
is strictly positive in any small punctured neighborhood of
x_{0}
,
so we have

\cap_{s>0}\overline{\Omega_{s}}=\{x_{0}\}.

This shows that
\phi(s)\to 0
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

For any
t\in(0,s)
and any

, we have

0>u_{s-t}(z)=u_{s}(z)+t,\quad\mbox{i.e. }-u_{s}(z)>t>0.

This observation shows that

A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}\geq\int_{\Omega_{s-t}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}\geq t\int_{\Omega_{s-t}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(2.30)

Note that
\phi(s)=\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}
for

, then equations (

) and (

) imply that

t\phi(s-t)\leq C_{0}(\phi(s))^{1+\delta_{0}},\quad\mbox{for all }0<t<s<s_{0}.

(2.31)

This finishes the proof of Lemma

. Q.E.D.

Lemma 7

Let
\phi(s)
be a function on
(0,s_{0})
satisfying all the properties listed in the previous lemma. There there is a constant
c_{0}>0
, depending only on
s_{0}
,
C_{0}
, and
\delta_{0}

so that

\displaystyle\phi(s_{0})\geq c_{0}.

(2.32)

Proof.
We apply the iteration argument in
[
39
]
.

Given the initial number
s_{0}
, we define inductively a
decreasing
sequence of positive numbers
\{s_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
as follows: given
s_{j}
let

s_{j+1}=\sup\{0<s<s_{j}|~\phi(s)\leq\frac{1}{2}\phi(s_{j})\}.

The continuity of
\phi(s)
implies that
s_{j+1}
is the largest
s<s_{j}
such that
\phi(s)=\frac{1}{2}\phi(s_{j})
. Thus we have
\phi(s_{j+1})=\frac{1}{2}\phi(s_{j})
and
\frac{1}{2}\phi(s_{j})<\phi(s)\leq\phi(s_{j})
for all
s\in(s_{j+1},s_{j})
. Iterating this identity we infer that

\phi(s_{j})=2^{-j}\phi(s_{0}),\quad\forall j.

In particular,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\phi(s_{j})=0
and Lemma
6
implies that
\lim_{j\to\infty}s_{j}=0
, which exists since
\{s_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
is a bounded decreasing sequence of positive numbers.

From (
2.31
), we get that for any
s\in(s_{j+1},s_{j})

\frac{1}{2}(s_{j}-s)\phi(s_{j})\leq(s_{j}-s)\phi(s)\leq C_{0}\phi(s_{j})^{1+\delta_{0}},

so
s_{j}-s\leq 2C_{0}\phi(s_{j})^{\delta_{0}}
. Letting
s\to s_{j+1}
we obtain

-s_{j+1}+s_{j}\leq 2C_{0}\phi(s_{j})^{\delta_{0}}\leq 2C_{0}2^{-\delta_{0}j}\phi(s_{0})^{\delta_{0}}.

Taking summation over
j
, we conclude that

s_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(s_{j}-s_{j+1})\leq\frac{2C_{0}}{1-2^{-\delta_{0}}}\phi(s_{0})^{\delta_{0}}.

Thus we have

\phi(s_{0})\geq c_{0}

(2.33)

for some constant
c_{0}>0
depending on
n,\omega,C_{0},s_{0}
. Q.E.D.

Proof of Lemma 1
.
Note that on

(2.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}^{\prime}(\,{\rm log}\,\,W)W^{1\over p}\leq C_{p}^{\prime\prime}(1+|W|).

Substituting back this inequality in the initial inequality (
2
), and estimating the integral over
\Omega_{s}
by the full integral over
\Omega
,
we find

\displaystyle\,{\rm log}\,(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1/2}\cdot\phi(s_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}+C_{p}^{\prime\prime}\int_{\Omega}(1+{-\varphi(z)-{1\over 2}|z|^{2}\over(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}+C_{p}^{\prime\prime}(C_{1}(\omega)+{\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(\omega)\over(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}})

In particular,

\displaystyle\,{\rm log}\,(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1/2}\cdot\phi(s_{0})\leq{1\over 2}{\max}\big\{\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}+C_{p}^{\prime\prime}C_{1}(\omega),C_{p}^{\prime\prime}{\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(\omega)\over(-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0})^{1\over 2}}\big\}.

Since
\phi(s_{0})
is bounded from below by Lemma
7
, it follows immediately that
-\varphi(x_{0})-s_{0}
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
X,\omega,n,p,\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}
. The proof of Theorem
1
is complete. Q.E.D.

We remark that compared to the compact Kähler case in
[
32
]
, the constant
C>0
in Theorem
1
does not depend on the “energy”
E(\varphi)=\int_{X}(-\varphi)e^{nF}\omega^{n}
. This is because the function
\psi_{s,k}
in the auxiliary equation (
2.9
) is
local
and
plurisubharmonic
in
\Omega_{s}
, while its counterpart in
[
32
]
is
global
and
\omega
-plurisubharmonic. Thus we do not need to shift the function
-\psi_{s,k}
in (
2.13
) by a constant depending on
E(\varphi)
. The proof of Theorem
1
is in particular simpler since it does not require an argument for
bounding the energy by the entropy
as in
[
32
]
.

We also observe one feature of the auxiliary equation which sets it apart from many other auxiliary equations in the literature such as in
[
7
]
: it depends itself on the unknown function
u
. This may seem to limit its applicability, but as we see from Lemma
1
, it turns out that for our purposes, very little is needed from the auxiliary functions
\psi_{s,k}
.

3
The case of Hermitian compact manifolds without boundary

Once we have Lemma
1
, it is easy to establish all the theorems listed in the Introduction. In this section, we begin with the case of
X
Hermitian compact without boundary, and give the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.
Since
X
is compact without boundary, the function
\varphi(z)
must attain its minimum at some interior point
x_{0}
. By Lemma
1
,
-\varphi(x_{0})
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
X,\omega,p,n,\gamma,\|e^{nF}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
, and
\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(B(x_{0},2r_{0}))}
. Thus the proof is complete once we have proved the following lemma giving a bound on the
L^{1}
-norm of
\varphi
for which
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
.

Lemma 8

For any
\varphi\in C^{2}(X)
such that
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
and
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
depending only
n,\omega
such that

\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C.

Proof.
Note that
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma_{1}
means that
{\rm tr}_{\omega}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)>0
, that is, in local coordinates, the following holds

g^{i\bar{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}(-\varphi)}{\partial z_{i}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}<n.

(3.1)

If
\omega
is Kähler, the lemma follows easily from the Green’s formula. In general, we will apply local elliptic estimates to
-\varphi
over a finite cover of
(X,\omega)
. More precisely, we fix three sets of open covers
\{U_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
,
\{U_{\ell}^{\prime}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
and
\{U_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
, such that
U_{\ell}\subset\subset U^{\prime}_{\ell}\subset\subset U_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
for each
\ell
. We can also view
U_{\ell}
as a Euclidean ball of radius
r_{\ell}
and
U_{\ell}^{\prime},U_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}
as concentric balls with radii
2r_{\ell},3r_{\ell}
, respectively. The choice of these covers is fixed and depends only on
(X,\omega)
. Assume
\sup_{X}\varphi=0=\varphi(p_{1})
is achieved at some point

.

We can apply the standard elliptic estimate (see e.g. Theorem 8.18 in
[
26
]
) to the equation of
(-\varphi)
as in (
3.1
) on
U_{1}^{\prime}\subset U_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, to conclude

\int_{U_{1}^{\prime}}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C[(-\varphi)(p_{1})+1]=C_{1},

(3.2)

for some constant
C_{1}>0
depending on
U_{1}^{\prime}\subset U_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
\omega
only. If
U_{1}^{\prime}\cap U_{2}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then from (
3.2
) we get

\int_{U_{1}^{\prime}\cap U_{2}^{\prime}}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C_{1},

which by mean-value theorem implies that there is some point

such that
-\varphi(p_{2})\leq C_{1}^{\prime}
for some
C_{1}^{\prime}>0
. We can now apply again the elliptic estimate to
(-\varphi)
which satisfies (

) in

to conclude that

\int_{U_{2}^{\prime}}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C[(-\varphi)(p_{2})+1]\leq C_{2},

for some uniform constant
C_{2}>0
. We can repeat this argument finitely many times, to deduce that
\int_{U_{\ell}^{\prime}}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C_{\ell}
for each
\ell
, since
\{U_{\ell}^{\prime}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
is an open cover of
(X,\omega)
, which is assumed to be connected. From this, it is easy to see the bound of
L^{1}
-norm of
(-\varphi)
. Lemma
8
is proved.

The proof of Lemma
1
already contains a proof of Corollary
1
. More precisely,
\varphi
is by definition a solution on
X
of the complex Monge-Ampère equation

f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=\Big(\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\Big)^{1/n}=e^{F}

which does satisfy the conditions (1-4) described in the Introduction. Let
x_{0}
be a point in
X
where
\varphi
attains its minimum. Then Lemmas
2
-
6
apply, implying
that there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
depending only on

and

such that

\phi(s_{0})=\int_{\Omega_{s_{0}}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\geq c_{0}.

It is clear that
\phi(s_{0})\leq\int_{X}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}
. Q.E.D.

We now provide a proof of the inequality (
1.6
), without the extra assumption on the
p
-th entropy of
e^{nF}
, but under an additional condition that
X
admits a smooth
closed
real
(1,1)
-form
\beta
whose Bott-Chern class is nef and big. The proof of Lemma
9
is motivated by an observation of Tosatti
[
47
]
using the Morse inequality of Demailly
[
13
]
.

Lemma 9

Under the assumption above, there exists a constant
c>0
that depends on
n,\omega
and
\beta
such that

\int_{X}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}\geq c,

for any
C^{2}
-function
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega)
.

Proof of Lemma
9
.
By a weak transcendental Morse inequality of Demailly
[
13
]
, one can show (see e.g. Theorem 3.1 in
[
47
]
) that for
any

C^{2}
function
u
, the following holds,

0<\int_{X}\beta^{n}=\int_{X}\beta_{u}^{n}\leq\int_{X(\beta_{u},0)}\beta_{u}^{n},

(3.3)

where
\beta_{u}=\beta+i\partial\bar{\partial}u
, and
X(\beta_{u},0)
denotes the subset of
X
where the
(1,1)
-form
\beta_{u}
is nonnegative definite.

Since
\beta
is a fixed
(1,1)
-form and
\omega
is positive definite, there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that
\beta\leq\Lambda\omega
on
X
. Applying (
3.3
) to
u=\Lambda\varphi
for any
\varphi\in C^{2}\cap PSH(X,\omega)
gives

\displaystyle 0<\int_{X}\beta^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X(\beta_{u},0)}(\beta+i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda\varphi)^{n}\leq\int_{X(\beta_{u},0)}(\Lambda\omega+\Lambda i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}\int_{X}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n},

since
0\leq\beta+i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda\varphi\leq\Lambda\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda\varphi
over the set
X(\beta_{u},0)
. Q.E.D.

4
The case of Hermitian manifolds with boundary

We consider now the case of the Dirichlet problem for the equation (
1.7
) for an operator
f(\lambda)
satisfying the conditions (1-4), and give the proof of Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
. In the case of the Dirichlet problem, we cannot normalize the solution to satisfy
{\sup}_{X}\varphi=0
, and we need to establish an upper bound for
\varphi
. However, this can be readily done by comparing
\varphi
to a solution of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplace-Beltrami equation on
X
. More precisely, let
h
be the solution of the equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega}h=n+1\ {\rm in}\ X,\quad h=0\ \ {\rm on}\ \partial X.

(4.1)

where
\Delta_{\omega}h=n(i\partial\bar{\partial}h\wedge\omega^{n-1})\omega^{-n}
is the complex Laplacian defined by the Hermitian metric
\omega
. In the Kähler case,
\Delta_{\omega}
coincides with the Laplace-Beltrami equation, and the unique solvability of the corresponding Dirichlet problem is well-known. It has been shown by Z. Lu
[
41
]
that the unique solvability of the Dirichlet problem still holds for general Hermitian metrics
\omega
on compact manifolds
X
with smooth boundary. Thus the function
h
exists and is unique, and a dependence on it can be absorbed into a dependence on
(X,\omega)
.

Now
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
implies that
\Delta_{\omega}\varphi\geq-n
. Thus
\Delta(\varphi+h)\geq 1
on
X
, which implies that
\varphi+h
must attain its maximum on the boundary. Hence

\displaystyle{\sup}_{X}(\varphi+h)\leq{\sup}_{\partial X}(\varphi+h)={\rm sup}_{\partial X}\rho.

(4.2)

This gives an upper bound for
\varphi
by a constant depending only on
X,\omega
, and
\rho
.

We can now replace
\varphi
by
\varphi-{\sup}_{X}\varphi
, and thus
\varphi
is a non-positive solution
to (
1.7
). Suppose
\inf_{X}\varphi=\varphi(x_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
. If
x_{0}\in\partial X
, we are done. Otherwise we assume
x_{0}\in X
and

\varphi(x_{0})\leq\inf_{\partial X}\rho-1.

(4.3)

Take a local coordinates system
(B(x_{0},2r_{0}),z)
centered at
x_{0}
and (
2.1
) holds.

If
B(x_{0},2r_{0})\subset\subset X
, we can apply directly Theorem
1
and Lemma
6
, which gives the desired bound
for
-\inf_{X}\varphi
, modulo an
L^{1}(X,\omega)
-bound of
(-\varphi)
.

If
B(x_{0},2r_{0})\cap\partial X\neq\emptyset
, we let
s_{0}=2r_{0}^{2}
,
s\in(0,s_{0})
, and consider the function

u_{s}(z)=\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}-s,\quad\forall z\in\Omega=B(x_{0},2r_{0})\cap X.

(4.4)

We observe that on
\partial B(x_{0},2r_{0})\cap X
,
u_{s}\geq 2r_{0}^{2}-s>0
; and on the other half of
\partial\Omega
,
\forall z\in B(x_{0},2r_{0})\cap\partial X
, we have by (
4.3
)

u_{s}(z)=\rho(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}-s\geq 1-s>0.

Thus we have
u_{s}|_{\partial\Omega}>0
. We can extend the function
u_{s}
to a smooth function on
B(x_{0},2r_{0})
satisfying
u_{s}>0
on
B(x_{0},2r_{0})\backslash\Omega
. Since only the region when
u_{s}<0
is under consideration, the estimates in §2 are independent of the extension of
u_{s}
. We also extend
F
to be smooth function on
B(x_{0},2r_{0})
, and it will be clear from (
4.5
) that the extension is again irrelevant.

Denote the sub-level set of
u_{s}
,
\Omega_{s}=\{z\in\Omega|u_{s}(z)<0\}
. By the discussions above,
\Omega_{s}\subset\subset X
. As in Section §2, we consider the following (auxiliary) Dirichlet boundary problem (with the extended functions
u_{s}
and
F
above)

(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=\frac{\tau_{k}(-u_{s})}{A_{s,k}}e^{nF}\omega^{n},\quad\mbox{in }B(x_{0},2r_{0}),

(4.5)

and
\psi_{s,k}\equiv 0
on
\partial B(x_{0},2r_{0})
. Here
\tau_{k}
and
A_{s,k}
are the same as in Section §2. We note that the calculations in Section §2 are performed essentially in
\Omega_{s}
, which is strictly contained in
X
. Therefore, we still obtain Theorem
1
and Lemma
6
, and hence a lower bound of
\varphi(x_{0})=\inf_{X}\varphi
, depending on an
L^{1}
bound of
(-\varphi)
. We remark that because
\partial X\neq\emptyset
, the
a priori

L^{1}
-norm of
\varphi
as in Lemma
8
in general does not hold.

We now focus on complex Monge-Ampère and Hessian equations with
\omega
being Kähler, i.e.
d\omega=0
. We rewrite the equation (
1.3
) as (
1\leq k\leq n
)

(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=e^{\tilde{F}}\omega^{n}\mbox{ in }X,\,\,\varphi=\rho\mbox{ on }\partial X.

(4.6)

Here we denote
\tilde{F}=kF
to simplify notations. We will write
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\in\Gamma_{k}
. When
k=n
, (
4.6
) reduces to the Monge-Ampère equation. Since
\varphi
is always bounded above depending on
\omega
and
\rho
, we may assume
\sup_{X}\varphi\leq 0
, and that
\inf_{X}\varphi=\varphi(x_{0})\leq\inf_{\partial X}\rho-1
for some
x_{0}\in X^{\circ}
, otherwise we are done.

For simplicity of notations, we consider the function
\tilde{\varphi}=\min(0,\varphi-\inf_{\partial X}\rho)
, which is Lipschitz in
X
and
\tilde{\varphi}|_{\partial X}\equiv 0
. We calculate

\displaystyle\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)(\omega_{\varphi}^{k}-\omega^{k})\wedge\omega^{n-k}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega+\cdots+\omega^{k-1})\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\frac{1}{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}i\partial\tilde{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\partial}\tilde{\varphi}\wedge(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega+\cdots+\omega^{k-1})\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge(\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega+\cdots+\omega^{k-1})\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)\bar{\partial}\tilde{\varphi}\wedge\partial\Big((\omega_{\varphi}^{k-1}+\omega_{\varphi}^{k-2}\wedge\omega+\cdots+\omega^{k-1})\wedge\omega^{n-k}\Big)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{n}\int_{X}\Big|\nabla\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}\Big|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}

where the integral in (
4.7
) vanishes since
\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)|_{\partial X}\equiv 0
, and that in (
4.8
) is zero since we assume
\omega
is Kähler.

On the other hand, the left-hand-side of the equation above satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)(\omega_{\varphi}^{k}-\omega^{k})\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)(e^{\tilde{F}}-1)\omega^{n}\leq\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)e^{\tilde{F}}\omega^{n}

since
\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)\geq 0
. We can now apply Young’s inequality as in (
2.24
) with
p=1
,
v={1\over 4}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)
to write

\displaystyle\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)e^{\tilde{F}}\leq 4\,e^{\tilde{F}}(1+|\tilde{F}|)+C\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{X}\,{\rm log}\,(-\tilde{\varphi}+1)(\omega_{\varphi}^{k}-\omega^{k})\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int_{X}e^{\tilde{F}}(1+|\tilde{F}|)\omega^{n}+C\int_{X}\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\int_{X}\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}\omega^{n}.

Combining the above, we obtain

\int_{X}\Big|\nabla(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)\Big|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\leq C+C\int_{X}(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)\omega^{n}.

Observing that the function
(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)\equiv 0
on
\partial X
, we can apply the Poincaré inequality on
({\overline{X}},\omega)
to get (
C_{P}
below is the Poincaré constant)

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{P}\int_{X}\Big|\nabla(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)\Big|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C+C\int_{X}(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\Big(\int_{X}(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2},

from which we easily get
\int_{X}(\sqrt{-\tilde{\varphi}+1}-1)^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C
, hence

\int_{X}(-\tilde{\varphi})\omega^{n}\leq C.

Since
\tilde{\varphi}
differs from
\varphi
by a uniform constant that depends only on
\inf_{\partial X}\rho
, this proves the
L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})
-bound of the function
|\varphi|
. Q.E.D.

The case of non-compact Hermitian manifolds

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
3
, which is analogous to that of Theorem

, so we only point out the very minor differences.

Proof of Theorem
3
. First note we are allowing a dependence on the (local)

norm of
\varphi
in the constant

in Theorem

. With such an allowance, the upper bound of
\varphi
is local since
\Delta_{\omega}\varphi\geq-n

).

Next we derive the lower bound for

. Let
{\rm liminf}_{z\to\infty}\varphi>-B
for some positive constant

with

for

. If

6
The case of
(n-1)
-form type nonlinear equations

We come now to the
(n-1)

.

Proof of Theorem
4
.
We first fix a constant
C_{\theta}=C_{\theta}(\omega_{h},\theta)
such that

.
Recall that
\varphi
is a
C^{2}
solution to the
(n-1)
-form nonlinear equation (
1.13
). For notational convenience, we denote

\tilde{\omega}=\omega_{h}+\frac{1}{n-1}((\Delta_{\omega}\varphi)\omega-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)+\chi[\varphi]

to be the
(1,1)
-form on the left-side of (
1.13
), and
\tilde{h}_{\varphi}=\omega^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\omega}
. Write
G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[\tilde{h}_{\varphi}])}{\partial\tilde{h}_{i\bar{j}}}
to be linearized operator of
\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[\cdot])
which is positive definite by (3) of the assumptions on
f
, and we will denote
{\rm tr}_{G}\alpha=G^{i\bar{j}}\alpha_{i\bar{j}}
for any
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
. Write
g=(g_{i\bar{j}})
to be the metric associated to the background Hermitian metric
\omega
and
g^{i\bar{j}}
its inverse.

As in
[
49
,
38
]
, we define a tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
by

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{n-1}\Big(({\rm tr}_{G}\omega)g^{i\bar{j}}-G^{i\bar{j}}\Big).

Lemma 10

The tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite at any point, and satisfies

{\rm det}(\Theta^{i\bar{j}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{e^{nF}}{\rm det}(g^{i\bar{j}}),\mbox{\, on }X.

(6.1)

Proof.
Given any
x_{0}\in X
, we can choose local holomorphic coordinates around
x_{0}
such that
g_{i\bar{j}}|_{x_{0}}=\delta_{ij}
and
\tilde{\omega}|_{x_{0}}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
is diagonal. Note that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
and
G^{i\bar{j}}|_{x_{0}}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}=\mu_{i}\delta_{ij}
for
\mu_{i}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0
.
Therefore, at
x_{0}
, we have

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{n-1}(\sum_{k\neq i}\mu_{k})\delta_{ij}

which is clearly positive definite. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle{\rm det}(\Theta^{i\bar{j}})|_{x_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k\neq i}{\mu_{k}}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\prod_{k\neq i}{\mu_{k}}\Big)^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mu_{i}}=\frac{1}{f^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{\gamma}{f^{n}},

where the inequality follows from the arithmetic-geometric (AG) inequality and the last equality follows from the structural condition (
1.2
) on
f
. The proof of the lemma is complete since
x_{0}\in X
is an arbitrary point in
X
.
Q.E.D.

Next, we take a minimum point
x_{0}
of
\varphi
, i.e.
\varphi(x_{0})=\inf_{X}\varphi
. Let
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
be a local holomorphic coordinate system centered at
x_{0}
, such that on the Euclidean ball
\Omega=B(x_{0},2r_{0})\subset{\bf C}^{n}
defined by these coordinates, the following holds

\frac{1}{2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq\omega\leq 2i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}.

(6.2)

We fix a small constant
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
which will be determined later. Let
z_{i}=x_{2i-1}+\sqrt{-1}x_{2i}
. Hence
\{x_{a}\}_{a=1}^{2n}
is a local real coordinates system.
Let
s_{0}=\epsilon^{\prime}r_{0}^{2}
. For any
s\in(0,s_{0})
, we define a function
u_{s}
similar to that in (

).

The case of tame symplectic forms

The proof of Theorem
6
is analogous to that of Theorem
5
, so we only point out the main differences. Recall
m=2n
is the real dimension. We denote the Riemannian metric associated to
\Omega
by

g(U,V)={1\over 2}(\Omega(U,JV)+\Omega(V,JU)).

Around a point
x_{0}\in X
in which

achieves the minimum, we take a local

under which the metric

satisfies (

The almost-Kähler condition implies that the Calabi-Yau equation
\tilde{\omega}^{n}=e^{nF}\Omega^{n}
is equivalent to the real equation

, where as before we use the letters

\tilde{g}^{ab}\tilde{\Gamma}_{ab}^{c}=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{cd}J^{b}{}_{a}\frac{\partial J^{a}{}_{b}}{\partial x_{d}}-\tilde{g}^{ab}J^{c}{}_{d}\frac{\partial J^{d}{}_{b}}{\partial x_{a}},

which implies that the Laplace-Beltrami operator

is independent of the first order derivatives of

We define the function
u_{s}
as in (

) and solve the auxiliary real Monge-Ampère equation (

\varepsilon
and
\Lambda
, we consider the test function

enables us to apply the maximum principle the linear operator

, and with appropriate choice of the constants

, we can derive that
\Phi\leq 0
. The rest of the proof is similar to other sections, so we omit the details.

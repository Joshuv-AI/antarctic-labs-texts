Ellis Wormholes in Anti-De Sitter Space
Jose Luis Blázquez-Salcedo, Xiao Yan Chew, Jutta Kunz, Dong-han Yeom

Abstract
We construct traversable wormholes with anti-de Sitter asymptotics supported by a phantom field. These wormholes are massless and symmetric with respect to reflection of the radial coordinate η → − η \eta\to-\eta . Their circumferential radius decreases monotonically from radial infinity to their single throat. Analogous to their asymptotically flat counterparts, these anti-de Sitter wormholes possess an unstable radial mode.



I
Introduction

In order to resolve the information loss paradox of black holes
Hawking:1976ra
, there have been numerous candidate proposals (see e.g.
Chen:2014jwq
for a review), but many of them were not successful and several candidates have been critized, eg., black hole conplementarity
Yeom:2008qw
;
Yeom:2009zp
or the firewall conjecture
Chen:2015gux
. In this regard, one of the possible alternatives is to introduce non-local interactions between the inside and outside of the horizon
Yeom:2016qec
;
Hwang:2017yxp
. In order to realize this idea without violating known principles of quantum mechanics, the so-called the ER=EPR conjecture has been proposed
Maldacena:2013xja
, where this idea is partly supported by holography
Almheiri:2019hni
. According to the conjecture, for a given entangled particle (Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen; EPR), there exists its dual geometry as the Einstein-Rosen (ER) bridge, which is the most well-known example of non–traversable wormholes in general relativity (GR), where it can be naturally obtained from the Schwarzschild black hole by maximally extending the solution
Einstein:1935tc
. This implies that through the ER bridge, there can exist a conspiracy between the earlier part of Hawking radiation and the inside degrees of freedom.

When the ER=EPR conjecture was first proposed, people assumed that the ER bridge must be non-traversable, because there is no information transfer via EPR pairs. However, later it was noticed that the ER bridge can become traversable if one considers non-perturbative effects
Chen:2016nvj
or correlations between two asymptotic boundaries
Maldacena:2018lmt
. If one constructs a traversable wormhole from a well-defined dual field theory, it might be consistent with the ER=EPR conjecture. On the other hand, if a traversable wormhole can be obtained from a more classical or semi-classical construction, it may indicate that the original proposal of the ER=EPR conjecture may be inconsistent or the proposal should be restricted. In order to do this, the violation of the averaged null energy condition would be required
Hartman:2016lgu
, which would be possible if one did not consider the expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor of the entire histories
Chen:2016nvj
(i.e., if one would consider only a specific non-perturbative process
Chen:2015gux
;
Sasaki:2014spa
;
Chen:2017suz
;
Chen:2018aij
) or if the theory would violate causality a little due to modifications of gravity (e.g.,

Hochberg:1990is
;
Fukutaka:1989zb
;
Ghoroku:1992tz
;
Furey:2004rq
;
Bronnikov:2009az
;
Kanti:2011jz
;
Sushkov:2011jh
;
Kanti:2011yv
;
Antoniou:2019awm
;
Ibadov:2020btp
).

Keeping these motivations in mind, we note that there are various models of wormholes that connect asymptotically locally AdS spaces
Lemos:2003jb
;
Korolev:2014hwa
;
Anabalon:2018rzq
;
Franciolini:2018aad
;
Mironov:2018uou
;
Anabalon:2020loe
;
Nozawa:2020gzz
;
Chatzifotis:2020oqr
. However in this work, we focus at obtaining wormhole with AdS asymptotics, which correspond to the AdS generalization of the asymptotically flat Ellis wormholes
Ellis:1973yv
;
Ellis:1979bh
;
Bronnikov:1973fh
. These represent analytically constructed wormhole solutions in GR, where the throat is supported by a phantom field, which is a real–valued scalar field that has an opposite sign of the kinetic term. Thus, it is a simple type of exotic matter that violates the null energy condition. This is necessary for the construction of traversable wormholes in GR, in order to prevent the throat from collapse (see e.g.
Visser:1995cc
).
This type of exotic matter can be used to explain the accelerated expansion of the universe
Caldwell:1999ew
;
Carroll:2003st
;
Gibbons:2003yj
;
Hannestad:2005fg
. Besides the Ellis wormholes, which might be the simplest example for traversable and asymptotically flat wormholes in GR
Ellis:1973yv
;
Ellis:1979bh
;
Bronnikov:1973fh
, a phantom field has also be employed to construct black holes
Bronnikov:2005gm
;
Chen:2016yey
, 4–dimensional black rings
Kleihaus:2019wck
, or star–like objects
Dzhunushaliev:2008bq
.

The Ellis wormhole has a very simple geometrical structure, it possesses only a single throat that connects two asymptotically flat regions. It violates the averaged null energy condition, and possesses an unstable radial mode
Gonzalez:2008wd
;
Gonzalez:2008xk
;
Torii:2013xba
;
Bronnikov:2012ch
. It has been generalized to higher dimensions
Torii:2013xba
, and to the rotating case in four dimensions
Kashargin:2007mm
;
Kashargin:2008pk
;
Kleihaus:2014dla
;
Chew:2016epf
and five dimensions
Dzhunushaliev:2013jja
. In addition, properties of rotating Ellis wormholes have been studied in modified gravity, e.g., in scalar–tensor theory
Chew:2018vjp
. It has also been shown that a phantom field may no longer be necessary for the construction of wormholes, when the gravity sector is suitably modified
Hochberg:1990is
;
Fukutaka:1989zb
;
Ghoroku:1992tz
;
Furey:2004rq
;
Bronnikov:2009az
;
Kanti:2011jz
;
Sushkov:2011jh
;
Kanti:2011yv
;
Antoniou:2019awm
;
Ibadov:2020btp
.

In fact, wormholes in GR can also be supported only by fermions
Blazquez-Salcedo:2019uqq
, and recently, traversable wormholes have been constructed in the Einstein–Maxwell–Dirac system
Blazquez-Salcedo:2020czn
.

In addition, configurations of wormholes with mixed phantom and ordinary fields have been constructed, for example, Ellis wormholes immersed in bosonic matter
Dzhunushaliev:2014bya
;
Hoffmann:2017jfs
;
Hoffmann:2017vkf
;
Hoffmann:2018oml
or mixed neutron star–wormhole systems
Dzhunushaliev:2011xx
;
Dzhunushaliev:2012ke
;
Dzhunushaliev:2013lna
;
Dzhunushaliev:2014mza
;
Aringazin:2014rva
. Since wormholes can represent compact objects that might mimic black holes, in order to distinguish them from black holes, a number of astrophysical signatures of wormholes have been pointed out that might allow to search for their existence in the near future. Examples are their shadows
Nedkova:2013msa
;
Gyulchev:2018fmd
;
Amir:2018szm
, gravitational lensing
Abe:2010ap
;
Toki:2011zu
;
Takahashi:2013jqa
;
Cramer:1994qj
;
Perlick:2003vg
;

, accretion disks around wormholes

, and their ringdown phase with the associated emission of gravitational waves
Blazquez-Salcedo:2018ipc
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we briefly introduce our theoretical setup comprising the phantom field and the metric ansatz. In addition, we derive the set of coupled differential equations, we describe the numerical methods employed to solve these equations, and we study the asymptotic behavior of the metric functions. In particular, we discuss the mass of the wormholes in asymptotically AdS spacetimes and the geometric properties of the wormholes, as well as the violation of the energy conditions. In Sec.
III
, we present and discuss our numerical results for the wormhole solutions. In Sec.
IV
, we study the stability of the wormholes by calculating the unstable mode of the radial perturbations of the metric and the phantom field. Finally, in Sec.
V
, we summarize our work and present an outlook.

II
Theoretical Setting

II.1
Theory

We consider the Einstein–Hilbert action including the cosmological constant
\Lambda
and the Lagrangian for the matter field
\mathscr{L}_{m}

S_{\text{EH}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{16\pi G}(R-2\Lambda)+\mathscr{L}_{\text{ph}}\right]\,,

(1)

where
\Lambda
is related to the AdS length
l
by
\Lambda=\pm 3/l^{2}
, and
\mathscr{L}_{\text{ph}}
is the Lagrangian of the phantom field
\psi
,

\mathscr{L}_{\text{ph}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial^{\mu}\psi\,.

(2)

By varying the action with respect to the metric, we obtain the Einstein equations,

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=2\kappa T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3)

where
\kappa=4\pi G
, and the stress–energy tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
is given by

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial^{\alpha}\psi-\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi\,.

(4)

We obtain the massless Klein–Gordon equation by varying with respect to the phantom field,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\partial^{\mu}\psi\right)=0\,.

(5)

In order to construct wormhole solutions with AdS asymptotics, we employ the following line element with a quasi–isotropic radial coordinate
\eta
,

ds^{2}=-F(\eta)N(\eta)dt^{2}+\frac{p(\eta)}{F(\eta)}\left[\frac{d\eta^{2}}{N(\eta)}+h(\eta)(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2})\right]\,,

(6)

where
N(\eta)=1-\Lambda\eta^{2}/3
and
h(\eta)=\eta^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}
with
\eta_{0}
the throat parameter. In pure Einstein gravity
(\Lambda=0)
, the above metric describes a static Ellis wormhole, that possesses two asymptotically flat regions
\Sigma_{\pm}
as
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
. The analytical solution for static Ellis wormholes is given by

p(\eta)=1\,,\quad F(\eta)=e^{f(\eta)}\,,

(7)

with

f(\eta)=\frac{2M}{\eta_{0}}\left[\arctan\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{0}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right]\,,

(8)

and
M
is the mass of the Ellis wormholes on
\Sigma_{+}
. However, when
\Lambda<0

the above metric should possess two asymptotically AdS regions as
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
.

II.2
Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)

By substituting the line element Eq. (
6
) into the Einstein equations, we obtain a set of second–order nonlinear ODEs for the metric functions,

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}-\frac{F}{p}p^{\prime\prime}+\left[\frac{p^{\prime}}{2p}+\frac{\eta(6N-h\Lambda)}{3hN}\right]F^{\prime}-\frac{\eta F(6N-h\Lambda)}{3phN}p^{\prime}+\frac{3F}{4p^{2}}p^{\prime 2}-\frac{5}{4F}F^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle-\frac{6hFN+3\Lambda ph^{2}-2h\Lambda\eta^{2}F-3hF-3\eta^{2}FN}{3Nh^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\kappa F\psi^{\prime 2}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda\eta}{3FN}F^{\prime}+\frac{\eta(3N-h\Lambda)}{3phN}p^{\prime}+\frac{p^{\prime 2}}{4p^{2}}-\frac{F^{\prime 2}}{4F^{2}}+\frac{3N\eta^{2}F+3\Lambda ph^{2}-3hF-2h\Lambda\eta^{2}F}{3Fh^{2}N}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\psi^{\prime 2}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle p^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\eta(3N-h\Lambda)}{3hN}p^{\prime}-\frac{2\Lambda\eta p}{3FN}F^{\prime}-\frac{p^{\prime 2}}{p}+\frac{p}{2F^{2}}F^{\prime 2}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle+\frac{2p(3hFN-2h\Lambda\eta^{2}F+3\Lambda ph^{2}-Fh^{2}\Lambda-3\eta^{2}FN)}{3FNh^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\kappa p\psi^{\prime 2}\,,

(11)

where the prime denotes the derivative of the functions with respect to the radial coordinate
\eta
.

From the massless Klein–Gordon equation Eq. (
5
) for the phantom field we obtain a first integral,

\psi^{\prime}=\frac{D}{hN\sqrt{p}}\,,

(12)

where
D
is a constant. In the asymptotically flat case it is interpreted as the scalar charge of the phantom field.
We eliminate the term
\psi^{\prime 2}
in Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
) by adding Eq. (
9
) and (
11
), respectively, to Eq. (
10
). Then we obtain the following ODEs for the metric functions
f
and
p
,

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{\prime 2}}{F}+\frac{\Lambda\eta F}{3pN}p^{\prime}-\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{2p}+\frac{2\eta(3N-h\Lambda)}{3hN}\right)F^{\prime}+\frac{2\Lambda(2\eta^{2}F+Fh-3ph)}{3Nh}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle p^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime 2}}{2p}-\frac{\eta(3N-h\Lambda)}{Nh}p^{\prime}+\frac{2p(3F-3FN+4\Lambda\eta^{2}F+\Lambda hF-6\Lambda ph)}{3hFN}\,.

(14)

In order to study the asymptotic behavior of the metric functions in the limit
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, we perform the series expansion for Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) to obtain the asymptotic expansion for the functions

\displaystyle F(\eta)

\displaystyle=F_{\infty}+\frac{F_{\infty}\eta_{0}^{2}}{3\eta^{2}}-\frac{F_{\infty}\eta^{2}_{0}\left(\Lambda\eta_{0}^{2}-12\right)}{15\Lambda\eta^{4}}+O(\eta^{-6})\,,

(15)

\displaystyle p(\eta)

\displaystyle=F_{\infty}-\frac{F_{\infty}\eta_{0}^{2}}{3\eta^{2}}+\frac{F_{\infty}\eta^{2}_{0}\left(14\Lambda\eta_{0}^{2}+27\right)}{45\Lambda\eta^{4}}+O(\eta^{-6})\,.

(16)

We observe that the odd terms vanish identically. With these expansions, the large-
\eta
expansions of
g_{tt}
and
g^{\eta\eta}
for the wormhole are given by

\displaystyle-g_{tt}\Big|_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Lambda F_{\infty}\eta^{2}}{3}+F_{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda\eta^{2}_{0}}{9}\right)+\frac{F_{\infty}\eta^{2}_{0}}{15\eta^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\Lambda\eta^{2}_{0}}{3}\right)+O(\eta^{-4})\,,

(17)

\displaystyle g^{\eta\eta}\Big|_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Lambda\eta^{2}}{3}+1-\frac{2\Lambda\eta^{2}_{0}}{9}+\frac{\eta^{2}_{0}\left(7\Lambda\eta^{2}_{0}+81\right)}{135\eta^{2}}+O(\eta^{-4})\,.

(18)

Since the odd terms vanish, we find that the metric functions at
\eta\rightarrow-\infty
have exactly the same asymptotic expansions as the metric functions at
\eta\rightarrow\infty
.
According to the asymptotic expansions, the appropriate boundary conditions to be imposed on the metric functions at infinity are given by

F(\pm\infty)=p(\pm\infty)=1\,,

(19)

and thus
F_{\infty}=1
.

Considering the expression for the mass of the wormholes as, for instance,
obtained from the Ashtekar–Magnon–Das formalism
Ashtekar:1984zz
;
Ashtekar:1999jx
;
Das:2000cu

the vanishing of the odd terms also implies that the mass of these symmetric wormholes vanishes,
as it happens in the symmetric case also for an asymptotically flat space.

In additon, the series expansion at
\eta=0
is given by

\displaystyle F(\eta)

\displaystyle=F_{0}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}(F_{0}-3p_{0})\eta^{2}+O(\eta^{4})\,,

(20)

\displaystyle p(\eta)

\displaystyle=p_{0}+\frac{\Lambda p_{0}(F_{0}-6p_{0})}{3F_{0}}\eta^{2}+O(\eta^{4})\,.

(21)

By using Eq. (
12
), we may rewrite Eq. (
10
) as

D^{2}=\frac{N^{2}ph^{2}}{4F^{2}}F^{\prime 2}-\frac{Np\Lambda\eta h^{2}}{3F}F^{\prime}-\frac{N^{2}h^{2}}{4p}p^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{3}N\eta h(3N-h\Lambda)p^{\prime}-\frac{Np(3N\eta^{2}F+3\Lambda ph^{2}-3Fh-2h\Lambda\eta^{2}F)}{3F}\,.

(22)

This equation can be used to monitor the accuracy of the numerical computation by ensuring that
D
is constant on the grid in the full domain of integration. We note that in GR without a cosmological constant, for a generic static Ellis wormhole the mass
M
and the scalar charge
D
are related by

D^{2}=M^{2}+\eta_{0}^{2}\,.

(23)

We solve Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) numerically by using the ODE solver package COLSYS, which tackles boundary value problems for systems of nonlinear coupled ODEs based on the Newton–Raphson method
Ascher:1979iha
. Employing an adaptive grid selection procedure and using more than 1000 points, COLSYS provides the solutions with high accuracy together with an error estimate.
To integrate the ODEs in the full interval
-\infty
to
+\infty
, we compactify the radial coordinate
\eta
in the numerical calculations as follows:
\eta=\eta_{0}\tan(\pi x/2)
with
x\in[-1,1]
. Moreover, we change to dimensionless variables by introducing the following rescaled variables/parameters in the above ODEs,

\tilde{\psi}=\sqrt{\kappa}\psi\,,\quad\eta=\eta_{0}\tilde{\eta}\,,\quad\Lambda=\frac{\tilde{\Lambda}}{\eta_{0}^{2}}\,.

(24)

In the remaining sections we will omit the tilde for convenience. After the rescaling, the only free parameter left is
\Lambda
.

II.3
Geometric Properties

To study the geometric properties of the wormholes, we now define
the circumferential radius
R_{c}
as

R_{c}(\eta)=\sqrt{\frac{ph}{F}}\,.

(25)

A wormhole throat is a minimal surface of the wormhole. Therefore it has to satisfy the following conditions,

\frac{dR_{c}}{d\eta}\Bigg|_{\eta=\eta_{\text{th}}}=0\,,\qquad\frac{d^{2}R_{c}}{d\eta^{2}}\Bigg|_{\eta=\eta_{\text{th}}}>0\,,

(26)

where
\eta_{\text{th}}
is the radial coordinate of the throat.
If a wormhole has an equator with radial coordinate
\eta_{\text{eq}}
, this requires

\frac{dR_{c}}{d\eta}\Bigg|_{\eta=\eta_{\text{eq}}}=0\,,\qquad\frac{d^{2}R_{c}}{d\eta^{2}}\Bigg|_{\eta=\eta_{\text{eq}}}<0\ .

(27)

An equator is typically located between two throats, and thus signals a double throat configuration.

A wormhole throat can be visualized
by embedding the equatorial plane
(\theta=\pi/2)
in Euclidean space
(\rho,\varphi,z)
.
Using cylindrical coordinates, this implies for the above metric parametrization

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{FN}d\eta^{2}+\frac{ph}{F}d\phi^{2}\,

(28)

\displaystyle=d\rho^{2}+dz^{2}+\rho^{2}d\phi^{2}\,.

(29)

We then obtain the expression for
z
by comparison,

z=\pm\int\sqrt{\frac{p}{FN}-\left(\frac{d\rho}{d\eta}\right)^{2}}d\eta\,,\quad\rho\equiv R_{c}\,,

(30)

where the sign of
z
depends on the sign of the radial coordinate
\eta
. For the special case of the massless symmetric Ellis wormhole without cosmological constant
(\Lambda=0,F=p=1)
this yields

z=\int_{0}^{\eta}\sqrt{1-\frac{{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}}{{\eta^{\prime}}^{2}+{\eta^{\prime}}_{0}^{2}}}\,d\eta^{\prime}=\text{arcsinh}\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{0}}\right)\,.

(31)

II.4
Null Energy Condition (NEC)

As noted above, the construction of wormholes requires the violation of the energy conditions. Here, we focus on the NEC, which states that

T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}\geq 0\,,

(32)

for all (future–pointing) null vectors
k_{\mu}
which satisfy
k_{\mu}k^{\mu}=0
. We note that the violation of the NEC also implies the violation of the weak and strong energy conditions.

Since the wormhole spacetime is spherically symmetric, there are two choices of null vector
Antoniou:2019awm
,

k_{\mu}=\left(g_{tt},\sqrt{-\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{\eta\eta}}},0,0\right)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad k_{\mu}=\left(1,0,\sqrt{-\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{\theta\theta}}},0\right)\,,

(33)

which yield two expressions to test NEC violation,

-T^{t}\,_{t}+T^{\eta}\,_{\eta}\geq 0\,,\quad-T^{t}\,_{t}+T^{\theta}\,_{\theta}\geq 0\,.

(34)

Evaluating the above expressions explicitly,

-T^{t}\,_{t}+T^{\eta}\,_{\eta}=-\frac{D^{2}F}{p^{2}h^{2}N}<0\,,\quad-T^{t}\,_{t}+T^{\theta}\,_{\theta}=0\,,

(35)

shows that the NEC is always violated.

IV
Linear Stability

The investigation of the stability of wormholes is of considerable relevance. It is well-known that the asymptotically flat static Ellis wormholes in GR possess an unstable radial mode
Gonzalez:2008wd
;
Gonzalez:2008xk
;
Torii:2013xba
. Therefore, we will now address the stability of static Ellis wormholes with AdS asymptotics by studying radial perturbations on these new background solutions.

We start by introducing the following Ansatz for the line element

ds^{2}=-e^{\nu(t,\eta)}N(\eta)dt^{2}+e^{-\lambda(t,\eta)}\frac{p(\eta)}{N(\eta)}d\eta^{2}+p(r)h(\eta)e^{-\sigma(t,\eta)}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2})\,,

(36)

where

\displaystyle\nu(t,\eta)=\nu_{0}(\eta)+\epsilon\nu_{1}(\eta)e^{-i\omega t}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\lambda(t,\eta)=\lambda_{0}(\eta)+\epsilon\lambda_{1}(\eta)e^{-i\omega t}\,,\quad\sigma(t,\eta)=\sigma_{0}(\eta)+\epsilon\sigma_{1}(\eta)e^{-i\omega t}\,,

(37)

and the relation with the background metric is

e^{\nu_{0}}=e^{-\lambda_{0}}=e^{-\sigma_{0}}=F\,.

(38)

For the phantom field we employ the Ansatz

\displaystyle\Psi(t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\psi(\eta)+\epsilon\Psi_{1}(\eta)e^{-i\omega t}\,.

(39)

A mode with eigenvalue
\omega^{2}
is unstable and increases exponentially when
\omega^{2}<0
.

Inserting the Ansätze into the scalar field equation,
we find

\Psi_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{2p}+\frac{2\eta}{h}-\frac{2\Lambda\eta}{3N}\right)\Psi_{1}^{\prime}+\omega^{2}\frac{p\Psi_{1}}{F^{2}N^{2}}=0\,,

(40)

when choosing the simple gauge–fixing

\lambda_{1}=-\nu_{1}+2\sigma_{1}\,.

(41)

We compute the unstable mode numerically by using COLSYS to solve Eq. (
49

\omega^{2}
as the eigenvalue. In general the perturbation function
Z
does not vanish at the infinities (this is the case because these are spherical perturbations of an asymptotically AdS configuration). However the perturbation equation requires that the derivative of the perturbation function vanishes at the boundaries,
\partial_{\eta}Z(-\infty)=\partial_{\eta}Z(\infty)=0

49

\eta_{p}
which we typically choose to be above the throat. The eigenvalue
\omega^{2}
is found when

1\%
or smaller.

We exhibit the eigenvalue

Hence we conclude that the change of asymptotics does not stabilize the wormhole, since the unstable mode of the asymptotically flat solution can be continued smoothly for arbitrary values of cosmological constant. In fact, note that the larger the magnitude of the cosmological constant (i.e., the shorter the AdS length) the larger the value of the unstable mode becomes.

In this figure we also show in green a nodeless normal mode with
\omega^{2}>0

This is different to the asympotically flat case, where the perturbations oscillate while being damped exponentially in time (quasinormal modes). With AdS asymptotics however, the oscillation is not damped, but reflected at the boundaries, and it is possible to find normal modes like the one we include in the figure. Note that the frequency grows as we decrease the AdS length. And in the limit
\Lambda\to 0

\omega^{2}=0

V
Conclusion and Outlook

We have constructed numerically the globally regular solutions of static Ellis wormholes with AdS asymptotics. The wormhole solutions are symmetric with respect to
\eta\to-\eta
and consequently massless. Our numerics indicate that these solutions exist for any value of the negative cosmological constant, although in this work we have focused the discussion on the range
-100\leq\Lambda<0
. The AdS asymptotics of the metric is reflected in the corresponding dependence of the metric components, where the
g_{tt}
component behaves as
\sim\eta^{2}
at spatial infinity, while
g_{\eta\eta}
behaves as
\sim-\eta^{-2}
.

The wormholes possess a single throat which, because of the symmetry and the radial coordinate that we have used, is located at the position
\eta=0
. By decreasing
\Lambda
, the (scaled) circumferential radius of the throat decreases monotonically, approaching zero in the limit of infinite coupling constant. In this case the circumferential coordinate tends towards the modulus of the radial coordinate
\eta
. The wormholes violate the null energy condition, as implied by the presence of a phantom field.

In addition, we have also studied stability of these wormholes against spherically symmetric perturbations. Using the same approach as for the asymptotically flat Ellis wormholes, we have shown that the asymptotically AdS wormholes are also unstable against radial linear perturbations. The unstable mode tends to the value of the asymptotically flat case when
\Lambda\to 0
, and it increases in magnitude as the AdS length is decreased. We have also shown the existence of normal modes, which are allowed by the box-like properties of the spacetime.

Since asymptotically flat Ellis wormholes can rotate
Kashargin:2007mm
;
Kashargin:2008pk
;
Kleihaus:2014dla
;
Chew:2016epf
;
Dzhunushaliev:2013jja
, it will be interesting to construct the rotating generalizations of these new Ellis wormholes with AdS asymptotics.

Furthemore, if a wormhole solution is allowed by a string-inspired model in the asymptotically AdS background, it will open a new window toward the information loss paradox. In this model, can the philosophy of the ER=EPR conjecture still be true? What is the physical meaning of the violation of the causality in such a theory? Can the same method of
Almheiri:2019hni
be applicable in this theory? We leave these interesting questions for future projects.

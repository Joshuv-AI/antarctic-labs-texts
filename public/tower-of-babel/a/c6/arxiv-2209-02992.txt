Support τ \tau -tilting modules and semibricks over group graded algebras
Simion Breaz, Andrei Marcus, George Ciprian Modoi

Abstract
. We consider a finite dimensional strongly G G -graded algebra A A with self-injective 1 1 -component B B , and in our main result we prove that the induction from B B to A A of a basic support τ \tau -tilting pair of B B -modules is a support τ \tau -tilting pair ( M , P ) (M,P) of A A -modules if and only if M M is G G -invariant. A similar statement holds for the restriction from A A to B B , so our results may be viewed as Clifford and Maschke type theorems for 2 2 -term silting complexes. We also give applications to semibricks and the associated wide subcategories.

1.
Introduction

This article is motivated by the work done by R. Koshio and Y. Kozakai in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
. They consider a block algebra
B

G

2
-term tilting complexes over
B

2
-term tilting complexes over
\tilde{B}
, where
\tilde{B}
is a block algebra of

covering

\tau
-tilting modules, semibricks, simple minded collections. Their results rely on the study of the behaviour of these objects under the functor
\tilde{B}\cdot\mathrm{Ind}_{G}^{\tilde{G}}

We show here that the assumptions of
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
can be significantly relaxed. We build upon the ideas and results of
[
6
]
on induction of silting complexes, and of
[
14
]

[
15
]
and
[
16
]

We consider a finite dimensional strongly
G
-graded algebra

with
1
-component
B
over an arbitrary field

\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}=A\otimes_{B}-

is always separable, while

is separable when the characteristic of

does not divide the order

of

. The crucial observation made in Theorem

is that if

is self-injective, then

proved a similar result for

when
\mathrm{char}\,k\nmid|G|
, and we also simplify his proof in that case, avoiding the use of Clifford extensions.

This has a number of consequences which allow us to show in Theorem

that if

is a support
\tau
-tilting
B
-module, then
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}M
is a support
\tau
-tilting

-module if and only if
M
is
G
-invariant. A similar result involving
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}
also holds, and we point out that there is a general phenomenon which can be expressed in terms of Frobenius and separable functors that will be discussed in a subsequent paper. Nevertheless, several of our results stated for strongly group graded algebras do not hold for much more general Frobenius ring extensions.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
2

2
-term silting complexes, support
\tau
-tilting modules and semibricks.

Section

In Section

B
respectively
A

[
10
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
11
]

In Section

3

Note that we have at our disposal the induction and restriction functors, and also silting and cosilting complexes. Therefore, many of our statements may have four variants, but our main concern here is the induction of

2.1

Let
B

[

2

\mathrm{silt}\,B

\mathrm{s}\tau

\mathrm{tilt}\,B

Let
\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{proj}\,B)

2\textrm{-silt}\,B
, and let

be the support
\tau
-tilting module corresponding to

\tau
-tilting

We will assume that

\mathbf{P}=(P_{1}\oplus P\overset{[f\,0]}{\longrightarrow}P_{0})

Denote

D=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{proj}\,B)}(\mathbf{P})^{\textrm{op}}\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad F=\mathrm{End}_{B}(M)^{\textrm{op}}.

As in

be the left-finite semibrick corresponding bijectively to
M

L=R(M,M)=\mathrm{rad}_{F}M.

Note that if
M
is basic, then
S
is also basic.

2.2
.

Consider the Nakayama functor

\nu:\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{proj}\,B)\to\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{inj}\,B)

and let
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}:=\nu\mathbf{P}
Then
\nu\mathbf{P}\in 2\textrm{-}\mathrm{cosilt}\,B
.

Let
M^{\prime}=H^{-1}(\nu\mathbf{P})\in\mathrm{s}\tau^{-1}\textrm{-}\mathrm{tilt}\,B
and
S^{\prime}=\mathrm{soc}_{B}(M)\in\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{R}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{sbrick}\,B
. Denote also

D^{\prime}:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{inj}\,B)}(\nu\mathbf{P})^{\textrm{op}}\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad F^{\prime}=\mathrm{End}_{B}(M^{\prime})^{\textrm{op}}.

Then
\nu
induces an isomorphism between
D
and
D^{\prime}
.

2.3
.

We consider the decomposition
\mathbf{P}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
, where
P_{i}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{proj}\,B)
are indecomposable. We know that
n=|B|
. Then we have the decomposition
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P^{\prime}_{i}
, where
P^{\prime}_{i}=\nu P_{i}\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{inj}\,B)
are indecomposable.

Let
M_{i}=H^{0}(P_{i})
, hence

. Let
L_{i}=\sum_{f\in\mathrm{rad}_{A}(M,M_{i})}\mathrm{Im}f

L=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}

Let
M^{\prime}_{i}:=H^{-1}(\nu P_{i})

S^{\prime}_{i}=\bigcap_{f\in\mathrm{rad}_{A}(M^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime})}\mathrm{Ker}f

and
S^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}S^{\prime}_{i}

2.4
.

By the results of

(1)

(2)

(3)

2.6

In the following we will prove a slight generalization of

In order to do it, let us recall that if
\mathbf{P}:P_{1}\overset{f}{\to}P_{0}
is a complex of projective

0
and
1
, we can associate to it, as in
[
2

, the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}=\{K\in B\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}\mid\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{b}(A)}(\mathbf{P},K[1])=0\}
. We know from
[
2

\mathbf{P}

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}=\mathrm{Fac}(H^{0}(\mathbf{P}))

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}:B\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}\to A\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}:\mathcal{G}
is a pair of functors such that
\mathcal{F}
is a left adjoint of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathrm{add}\,M.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
A
and
B
are
k
-algebras, and that
\mathcal{F}:B\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}\to A\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}:\mathcal{G}
are functors such that

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is a left adjoint for
\mathcal{G}
,

(b)
for every
K\in A\textrm{-}\mathrm{mod}
the canonical morphism
\mathcal{FG}(K)\to K
is surjective, and

(c)

\mathcal{F}
preserves the projective property (or, equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is exact).

If
\mathbf{P}
is a
2
-silting complex with
H^{0}(\mathbf{P})=M
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})\in 2\textrm{-}\mathrm{silt}\,A
;

(2)

\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathrm{Fac}\,M.

Moreover, if

(d)

\tau\mathcal{GF}(M)\cong\mathcal{G}(\tau\mathcal{F}(M))
,
the above conditions are equivalent to

(3)

M
is
\mathcal{F}
-stable.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathcal{G}
, it is easy to see
H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P}))=\mathcal{F}(M)
and that
K\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}
if and only if
\mathcal{G}(K)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}
(see the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
).

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Assume that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})
is a silting complex. Then
\mathcal{F}(M)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}
, hence
\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}=\mathrm{Fac}\,M
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) Suppose that
\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathrm{Fac}\,M.
For every
K\in\mathcal{D}_{L(\mathbf{P})}
we have
\mathcal{G}(K)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}
, hence there is an epimorphism
M^{n}\to\mathcal{G}(K)
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is right exact, we obtain an epimorphism
\mathcal{F}(M)^{n}\to\mathcal{FG}(K)
, and using the hypothesis (b) we conclude that
K\in\mathrm{Fac}\,\mathcal{F}(M)
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}\subseteq\mathrm{Fac}\,\mathcal{F}(M)
. The inclusion
\mathrm{Fac}\,\mathcal{F}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}
follows from the following observations: by (2) and the equivalence mentioned in the beginning of the proof we have
\mathcal{F}(M)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}
, and the class
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{P})}
is closed with respect to epimorphic images.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) We associate to
\mathbf{P}
the support
\tau
-tilting pair
(P,M)
. We know that
\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}
, hence

\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\mathcal{GF}(M),\tau M)=0

and

\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(P,\mathcal{GF}(M))=0.

Moreover, by using our hypotheses, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(M,\tau\mathcal{GF}(M))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(M,\mathcal{G}(\tau\mathcal{F}(M)))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathcal{F}(M),\tau\mathcal{F}(M))=0.

By
[
1

we deduce that
\mathcal{GF}(M)\in\mathrm{add}\,M
.
∎

3.
Modules over group graded algebras and the Auslander-Reiten translation

3.1
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{g\in G}A_{g}
be a finite dimensional strongly
G
-graded
k
-algebra with 1-component
A_{1}=B
, so
\phi:B\to A
is the inclusion. In this particular case,
(\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B},\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B})
is a Frobenius pair, and
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}
is separable. Moreover, if the characteristic of
k
does not divide the order of
G
, then
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}
is also separable. A more general result on the separability of
\phi
is given in
[
17
, Proposition 2.1]
. Note that these conditions have been considered in
[
18
]
and
[
5
, Section III. 4]
.

3.2
.

We will also rely on other properties of the ring extension
\phi:B\to A
in the
G
-graded context. Recall that for any
g\in G
, the functor
A_{g}\otimes_{B}-:\mathrm{mod}\,B\to\mathrm{mod}\,B
is an autoequivalence with quasi-inverse
A_{g^{-1}}\otimes_{B}-
. Therefore, the group
G
acts on the isomorphism classes of
B
modules, and we denote
{}^{g}Y=A_{g}\otimes_{B}Y
.

The next theorem is crucial in our study of support
\tau
-tilting modules. The proofs of its two statements are, up to a point, very similar. The second statement is essentially in
[
8
, Section 5]
, but we give here an argument which avoids Dade’s theory of Clifford extensions over group graded algebras.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that the algebra
B
is self-injective.

1)
Let
Y
be a
B
-module. Then
A\otimes_{B}\mathrm{P}(Y)\simeq\mathrm{P}(A\otimes_{B}Y)
. Moreover, if
P_{1}\to P_{0}\to Y\to 0
is a minimal projective presentation, then
A\otimes_{B}P_{1}\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0}\to A\otimes_{B}Y\to 0
is a minimal projective presentation.

2)
Assume that
|G|
is invertible in
k
, and let
X
be an
A
-module. Then
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{P}(X)\simeq\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X)
. Moreover, if
Q_{1}\to Q_{0}\to X\to 0
is a minimal projective presentation, then
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}Q_{1}\to\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}Q_{0}\to\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X\to 0
is a minimal projective presentation.

Proof.

1) We know that
Y
is projective if and only
A\otimes_{B}Y
is projective.

We show that
Y
is projective-free if and only
A\otimes_{B}Y
is projective-free. Indeed, assume that
A\otimes_{B}Y
is projective-free. By the Krull-Schmidt theorem, we may write
Y=Y^{\prime}\oplus Y^{\prime\prime}
, where
Y^{\prime}
is projective, and
Y^{\prime\prime}
is projective-free. We get
A\otimes_{B}Y=A\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}\oplus A\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime\prime}
, where
A\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}
is projective. But our assumption forces
A\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}=0
, hence
Y^{\prime}=0
, so
Y
is projective-free. Conversely, assume that
Y
is projective-free, and let
A\otimes_{B}Y=X^{\prime}\oplus X^{\prime\prime}
, where
X^{\prime}
is projective, and
X^{\prime\prime}
is projective-free. We get
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}A\otimes_{B}Y=\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}A\otimes_{B}Y
is projective-free, because it is the direct sum of the
G
-conjugates of
Y
, and
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}
is projective. Our assumption forces
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}=0
, hence
X^{\prime}=0
, so
A\otimes_{B}Y
is projective-free.

Consider the exact sequence
0\to K\to P_{0}\to Y\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,B
, where
P_{0}=\mathrm{P}(Y)
is a projective cover of
Y
, and
K=\Omega(Y)
is projective-free. We obtain the exact sequence
0\to A\otimes_{B}K\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0}\to A\otimes_{B}Y\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,A
. Note that
\mathrm{P}(A\otimes_{B}Y)
is a direct summand of
A\otimes_{B}P_{0}
, but by the above,
A\otimes_{B}K
is projective-free, hence
\mathrm{P}(A\otimes_{B}Y)\simeq A\otimes_{B}\mathrm{P}(Y)
.

2) It is well known (see
[
17
]
) that under our assumptions, the functor
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}
is separable, hence
X
is projective if and only
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
is projective.

We show
X
is projective-free if and only if
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
is projective-free. Indeed, assume that
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
is projective-free, and write
X=X^{\prime}\oplus X^{\prime\prime}
, where
X^{\prime}
is projective, and
X^{\prime\prime}
is projective-free. Then
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X=\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime\prime}
, where
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}
. Our assumption forces
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X^{\prime}=0
, hence
X^{\prime}=0
, so
X
is projective-free. Conversely, assume that
X
is projective-free, and write
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X=Y^{\prime}\oplus Y^{\prime\prime}
, where
Y^{\prime}
is projective and
Y^{\prime\prime}
is projective-free. We get
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X=\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}Y^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}Y^{\prime\prime}
. But
X
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
, and since
X
is projective-free, we deduce by 1) that
X
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}Y^{\prime\prime}
.
It follows that
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}Y^{\prime\prime}
. But
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}Y^{\prime\prime}
is a direct sum of
G
-conjugates of
Y^{\prime\prime}
, hence it is projective-free. Therefore,
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X
is projective-free.

Consider the exact sequence
0\to K\to Q_{0}\to X\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,A
, where
Q_{0}=\mathrm{P}(X)
is a projective cover of
X
, and
K=\Omega(X)
is projective-free. We obtain the exact sequence
0\to\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}K\to\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}Q_{0}\to\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,B
. Note that
\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X)
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}Q_{0}
, but by the above,
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}K
is projective-free, hence
\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}X)\simeq\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{P}(X)
.
∎

3.4
.

Recall that if

If

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be a

-module, and denote

. Then

\mathrm{D}(A\otimes_{B}Y)\simeq\mathrm{D}Y\otimes_{B}A

-modules.

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}Y,A)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(Y,B)\otimes_{B}A
as

-graded right
A
-modules.

3)

\nu_{A}(A\otimes_{B}Y)\simeq A\otimes_{B}\nu_{B}(Y)
as
G
-graded
A
-modules.

4)
If

is self-injective, then
\mathrm{Tr}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}Y)\simeq\mathrm{Tr}_{B}Y\otimes_{B}A
as
G
-graded right
A
-modules.

5)
If

is self-injective, then
\tau_{A}(A\otimes_{B}Y)\simeq A\otimes_{B}\tau_{B}(Y)
as
G
-graded
A
-modules.

Proof.

1) We have that
\mathrm{D}X
is a right
A
-module, where
(fa)(x)=f(ax)
for all

,
x\in X
and

. It is clear from
3.4
that
\mathrm{D}X
is a

-graded right

-module with

-component isomorphic to the right
B
-module
\mathrm{D}Y
. The statement follows by follows by
[
7
, Theorem 2.8]
.

2) We have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X,A)
is a right
A
-module, where
(fa)(x)=f(x)a
for all

,
x\in X
and

. By

has a

-grading, where, via restriction of scalars to

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X,A)_{g}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(X_{h},A_{hg}),

for all

, where recall that

. It is clear that that

becomes a

, Theorem 2.8]
.

3) As

4) We may assume that

3.8

Let

-module. Then
\mathrm{End}_{A}(A\otimes_{B}Y)^{\textrm{op}}

-graded

The

and

The

3.9
.

In particular,

acts on the isomorphism classes of projective summands of

. If

is an idempotent, then the projective

-module

is

-invariant if and only if

is a crossed product of

and

In this case,

is a

-graded

-bimodule. Note that

is the image of the multiplication map

-graded subbimodule of
{}_{A}A_{A}
. Moreover, the ideal

of

is

-invariant, that is,

for all

, and

-graded ideal of

-graded, with

.

The

-stable if and only if

Both statements are obvious, taking into account that

, and that

.
∎

.

Finally, we recall from

the “stable Clifford correspondence”. Assume that

-invariant

4.

We continue with the notations and assumptions of the preceding section. Moreover, from now on

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
(M,P)
is a support
\tau
-tilting pair, where

is basic and

. The following statements are equivalent:

\mathrm{(1)}

is a
G
-invariant

-module.

\mathrm{(2)}

{}^{g}\mathrm{Fac}\,M=\mathrm{Fac}\,M
for all

\mathrm{(3)}

is a
G
-invariant

-module.

\mathrm{(4)}

\mathbf{P}
is
G
-invariant in the category of complexes of
B
-modules.

Proof.

The equivalence of the statements is an immediate consequence of
[
1
, Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 2.7]
and Lemma

.
∎

The next theorem is result the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
(M,P)
is a basic support
\tau
-tilting pair of
B
-modules. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

A\otimes_{B}\mathbf{P}\in 2\textrm{-}\mathrm{silt}\,A
.

(2)

(A\otimes_{B}M,A\otimes_{B}P)
is a support
\tau
-tilting pair of
A
-modules.

(3)

M
is a
G
-invariant.

In this case, if

is an idempotent such that

then
A\otimes_{B}P
may me identified with
Ae
, and
A\otimes_{B}M
is a
\tau
-tilting
A/AeA
-module.

Proof.

For the equivalence of (1) and (3) we will apply Proposition
2.7
for
\mathcal{F}=A\otimes_{B}-
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}-
. In order to do this, we only have to verify the condition (d). By using Proposition
3.5
and Corollary

, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(A\otimes_{B}M)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\tau(A_{g}\otimes_{B}M)\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}A_{g}\otimes_{B}\tau M

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}A\otimes_{B}\tau M\cong\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}\tau(A\otimes_{B}M),

and the proof is complete.

To prove that (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2), we first assume that
A\otimes_{B}\mathbf{P}\in 2\textrm{-}\mathrm{silt}\,A
, where
A\otimes_{B}\mathbf{P}=(A\otimes_{B}P_{1}\oplus A\otimes_{B}P\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0})
. We have that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A\otimes_{B}\mathbf{P})\simeq A\otimes_{B}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P})\simeq A\otimes_{B}M
. Moreover, by Theorem
3.3
1),

A\otimes_{B}P_{1}\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0}\to A\otimes_{B}M\to 0

is a minimal projective presentation. By
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
it follows that
(A\otimes_{B}M,A\otimes_{B}P)
is a support
\tau
-tilting pair.

Conversely, assume that
(A\otimes_{B}M,A\otimes_{B}P)
is a support
\tau
-tilting pair. Since by Theorem
3.3
1),
A\otimes_{B}P_{1}\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0}\to A\otimes_{B}M\to 0
is a minimal projective presentation, it follows by
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
that
A\otimes_{B}\mathbf{P}=(A\otimes_{B}P_{1}\oplus A\otimes_{B}P\to A\otimes_{B}P_{0})
is a 2-silting complex.

For the last statement, we have seen in
3.9
that
AeA=BeA=AeB
and that
A/AeA
is a strongly
G
-graded
k
-algebra. It also follows that
AeA
annihilates
A\otimes_{B}M
, and that
M
is a
G
-invariant
B/BeB
-module. Since
M
is a
\tau
-tilting

-module, the above arguments imply that
A\otimes_{B}M\simeq A/AeA\otimes_{B/BeB}M
is a tilting
A/AeA
-module.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If we are in the hypothesis (3), we can see directly that

Corollary 5.2

If

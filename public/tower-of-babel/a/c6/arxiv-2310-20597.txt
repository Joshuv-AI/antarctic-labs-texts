A General Class of Fatigue-life Distributions of Birnbaum-Saunders Type


Abstract
The derivation of Birnbaum-Saunders (BS) fatigue-life distribution is based on the asymptotic normality of crack damages’ partial sums. We address the situation when this fails and the crack damages possess heavy-tailed distribution. In this note, a general class of fatigue-life distributions of BS type is proposed. Keywords: Birnbaum-Saunders, heavy-tail, Mallows distance.

Introduction

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
be a sequence of random variables (r.v.’s) each representing the cumulative fatigue damage over a cycle. It is assumed that
X_{j}^{\prime}s
are non-negative, independent and identically distributed (i.i.d) with a common distribution
F_{X}
. The Birnbaum-Saunders (BS) fatigue-life model (cf.
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
) proposes a distribution
\rho_{n}
the smallest number of cycles
N_{*}
such that the partial sum
S_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
exceeds a given threshold value
s_{*}
.
The derivation of BS fatigue-life distribution is based on the asymptotic normal approximation
S_{n}\cong N(n\mu_{X},n\sigma_{X}^{2})
where
\mu_{X}\neq 0
and
\sigma_{X}>0
are, respectively, the mean and the variance of
F_{X}
. More specifically, let

N_{*}=\inf\left\{n;S_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}>s_{*}\right\}

and let
T_{*}
be the continuous counterpart of
N_{*}
. Then the distribution of
T_{*}
satisfies,

\mathbb{P}(T_{*}\leqslant t)=\Phi\left(\frac{1}{a}\,\xi\left(\frac{t}{b}\right)\right),~~t>0,

(1)

where
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function (c.d.f.) of
N(0,1)
,

a=\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\sqrt{\mu_{X}s_{*}}},\quad b=\frac{s_{*}}{\mu_{X}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi(x)=\sqrt{x}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}},\quad x>0.

(2)

Now, we assume that the crack damages
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
possess a heavy-tailed distribution in the sense that
\mathbb{E}(X_{k}^{2})=\infty
.
Then the asymptotic behavior of normalized partial sums cannot be Gaussian. In this case, the class of stable distributions
\{S_{\alpha}(\sigma,\beta,\mu);0<\alpha\leqslant 2\}
plays a central role, a similar role the normal distribution (
\alpha=2
) plays among distributions with finite second moments.
A useful tool to handle stable laws is provided by the Mallows (or Wasserstein) distance
d_{r}(F,G)
that measures the discrepancy between two distribution functions
F
and
G
.
For
r>0
define

d_{r}(F,G)=\inf_{(X,Y)}\left\{\mathbb{E}|X-Y|^{r}\right\}^{1/r},\quad X\overset{d}{=}F,~Y\overset{d}{=}G,

where the infimum is taken over all random vectors
(X,Y)
with marginal distributions
F
and
G
, respectively, and
\overset{d}{=}
denotes equality in distribution. Besides applications to several statistical methods such as bootstraps and goodness-of-fit tests (cf.
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
), this metric has also been successfully used to establish central limit theorems (CLT) type results for heavy-tailed distributions (see
[
8
]
or
[
1
]
).

Our Proposition
2.3
shows that, for
1<\alpha<2
, if there exists an
\alpha
-stable distribution
G_{\alpha}
such that
d_{\alpha}(F_{X},G_{\alpha})<\infty
, then

S_{n}\cong S_{\alpha}\left(\sigma n^{1/\alpha},0,n\mu_{X}\right),

(3)

where
\sigma>0
is a constant. And this leads us to a general class of BS type fatigue-life distributions,

\mathbb{P}(T_{*}\leqslant t)=\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{a}\,\xi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{b}\right)\right),\quad t>0,

(4)

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is the c.d.f. of
G_{\alpha}
(cf. Proposition
2.4
).
Also, if
\alpha=2
then (
4
) reproduces (
1
) and (
2
) (see Remark
2.5
).
For
G_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,\mu)
, estimation issues concerning
\alpha
and
\sigma
are also addressed, see, (
9
) and (
10
).

2
General Birnbaum-Saunders Distribution

First, we state some properties of stable distributions. For an extensive treatment on the matter, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1

For
0<\alpha\leqslant 2
, we say that
S_{\alpha}(\sigma,\beta,\mu)
is an
\alpha
-
stable distribution
with scale parameter
\sigma>0
, skewness parameter
|\beta|\leqslant 1
and shift parameter
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, if for any
n\geqslant 2
, there are real numbers
d_{n}
such that

Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n}\overset{d}{=}n^{1/\alpha}Y+d_{n},\quad Y\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(\sigma,\beta,\mu),

where
Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots
are independent copies of
Y
. If
\beta=0
we say that
Y
has a strictly
\alpha
-stable distribution.

Proposition 2.2

Let
Y\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(\sigma,\beta,\mu)
. Then

(a)

In general
\sigma>0
does not represent the variance of the distribution. Except for Gaussian case,
S_{2}(\sigma,0,\mu)=N(\mu,\sigma^{2})
.

(b)

If
\alpha>1
then
\mathbb{E}(Y)=\mu
and
d_{n}=\mu(n-n^{1/\alpha})
.

(c)

If
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha<2
then
\mathbb{E}|Y|^{\alpha^{\prime}}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Y|^{\alpha}<\infty
.

(d)

If
\beta=0
then for constant
a
and
b
we have
aY+b\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(|a|\sigma,0,a\mu+b)
.

(e)

The characteristic function of
S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,0)
is given by
\varphi_{S_{\alpha}}(t)=e^{-\sigma^{2}t^{2}}
.

As for the Mallows distance, it is worth pointing out that, from the practical point of view, it is fairly simple to compute. The representation result from
[
5
]
shows that: for
r\geqslant 1
, we have

d_{r}^{r}(F,G)=\mathbb{E}|X^{*}-Y^{*}|^{r}=\int|x-y|^{r}{\rm d}(F(x)\wedge G(y)),

(5)

where
X^{*}\overset{d}{=}F
,
Y^{*}\overset{d}{=}G
and
(X^{*},Y^{*})\overset{d}{=}F\wedge G
. That is,

\mathbb{P}(X^{*}\leqslant x,Y^{*}\leqslant y)=\min\{F(x),G(x)\},\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Proposition 2.3

Let
1<\alpha<2
and assume that
d_{\alpha}(F_{X},G_{\alpha})<\infty
, where
G_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,\mu)
. Then

\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,0),

(6)

and

S_{n}\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(\sigma n^{1/\alpha},0,n\mu_{X}),

(7)

with
\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}
denoting convergence in distribution.

Proof.
(a) Theorem 1 from
[
1
]
shows that for a sequence of independent r.v.’s satisfying Lindeberg type conditions, we have

d_{\alpha}(F_{n},G_{\alpha})\underset{n}{\longrightarrow}0,\quad F_{n}\overset{d}{=}\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}+n^{1/\alpha}\mu}{n^{1/\alpha}}.

Since we are dealing with i.i.d. r.v.’s the conditions are trivially satisfied. Let
X\overset{d}{=}G_{\alpha}
and take the joint distribution

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}+n^{1/\alpha}\mu}{n^{1/\alpha}}\leqslant x,Z\leqslant z\right)=\min\left\{F_{n}(x),G_{\alpha}(z)\right\}.

Using the representation (
5
) we can write

d_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(F_{n},G_{\alpha})=\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}+n^{1/\alpha}\mu}{n^{1/\alpha}}-Z\right|^{\alpha}\underset{n}{\longrightarrow}0.

From Proposition
2.2
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}-Z^{\prime}\right|^{\alpha}\underset{n}{\longrightarrow}0,

where
Z^{\prime}=Z-\mu\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,0)
. And (
6
) follows from the moment convergence of order
\alpha
.

(b) Since
({S_{n}-n\mu_{X}})/({\sigma n^{1/\alpha}})\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(1,0,0)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N_{*}\leqslant n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{n}>s_{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}>\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{s_{*}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle 1-\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{s_{*}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right),

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is the c.d.f. of
S_{\alpha}(1.0,0)
. And (
7
) follows readily.

\square

Proposition 2.4

Let
1<\alpha\leqslant 2
and assume
d_{\alpha}(F_{X},G_{\alpha})<\infty
, where
G_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,\mu)
. Then a general class of fatigue-life distributions of Birnbaum-Saunders type can be generated by the relation

\mathbb{P}(T_{*}\leqslant t)=\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}}\,\xi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{b_{\alpha}}\right)\right),\quad t>0,

(8)

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is the c.d.f. of
S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,0)
,

\displaystyle a_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\mu_{X}^{1/\alpha}s_{*}^{1-1/\alpha}},\quad b_{\alpha}=\frac{s_{*}}{\mu_{X}}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\alpha}(x)=x^{1-1/\alpha}-\frac{1}{x^{1/\alpha}},\quad x>0.

Proof.
(a) For
1<\alpha<2
, since (
6
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N_{*}\leqslant n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}>\frac{s_{*}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle 1-\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{s_{*}-n\mu_{X}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n\mu_{X}-s_{*}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right).

Set the reparametrization
a_{\alpha}={1}/({\mu_{X}^{1/\alpha}s_{*}^{1-1/\alpha}})
and
b_{\alpha}={s_{*}}/{\mu_{X}}
. And write

\displaystyle\frac{n\mu_{X}-s_{*}}{n^{1/\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{X}^{1/\alpha}s_{*}^{1-1/\alpha}\left[n^{1-1/\alpha}\left(\frac{\mu_{X}}{s_{*}}\right)^{1-1/\alpha}-\frac{1}{n^{1/\alpha}}\left(\frac{s_{*}}{\mu_{X}}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}}\left[n^{1-1/\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{b_{\alpha}}\right)^{1-1/\alpha}-\frac{1}{n^{1/\alpha}}b_{\alpha}^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}}\left[\left(\frac{n}{b_{\alpha}}\right)^{1-1/\alpha}-\left(\frac{b_{\alpha}}{n}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}}\xi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n}{b_{\alpha}}\right).

For (
8
), let
T_{*}
and
t
be the continuous counterpart of
N_{*}
and
n
.

(b) Let
\alpha=2
. Since
S_{2}(\sigma,0,\mu)=N(\mu,2\sigma^{2})
,
G_{2}
possesses moments of all order. Condition
d_{2}(F_{X},G_{2})<\infty
assures that
F_{X}
has finite variance. Then

\frac{S_{n}-n\mu_{X}}{\sqrt{n}}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}N(0,\sigma_{X}^{2})=S_{2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\sqrt{2}},0,0\right).

In this case, we necessarily have
\Phi_{2}
the c.d.f. of
S_{2}(\sigma,0,0)
where
\sigma={\sigma_{X}}/{\sqrt{2}}
.
Exactly the same proof as in (a) shows (
8
) holds for
\alpha=2
.

\square

Remark 2.5

(a)

It is worth pointing out that (
8
) for
\alpha=2
coincides with (
1
) and (
2
). Setting
\alpha=2
we get
\xi_{2}=\xi
,
a_{2}={1}/{\sqrt{\mu_{X}s_{*}}}
and
b_{2}={s_{*}}/{\mu_{X}}
. Now,

\Phi_{2}(y)=\Phi_{S_{2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\sqrt{2}},0,0\right)}(y)=\Phi_{N(0,\sigma_{X}^{2})}(y)=\Phi_{N(0,1)}(\sigma_{X}^{-1}y)

and

\Phi_{2}\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\,\xi_{2}\left(\frac{t}{b_{2}}\right)\right)=\Phi_{N(0,1)}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{X}a_{2}}\,\xi_{2}\left(\frac{t}{b_{2}}\right)\right).

From (
2
), we have
a=\sigma_{X}a_{2}
and
b=b_{2}
.

(b)

Unlike the Gaussian case, unfortunately for
1<\alpha<2
there is no close form for the c.d.f.
\Phi_{\alpha}
.
However, computationally it is interesting to express (
8
) in terms of
H
-functions (see
[
11
]
and the references therein).
It follows from reference
[
11
, Proposition 1]
that

\mathbb{P}(T_{*}\leqslant t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}\sigma}\xi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{b_{\alpha}}\right)H_{1,2}^{3,3}\left[\left|\frac{1}{a_{\alpha}\sigma}\xi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{b_{\alpha}}\right)\right|\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1-1/\alpha,1/\alpha),(0,1),(1/2,1/2)\\
(0,1),(1/2,1/2),(-1,1)\end{array}\right.\right],

where the
H
-function (cf.
[
10
]
) is defined by

H_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[z\Big|\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{1},A_{1}),\cdots,(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{1},B_{1}),\cdots,(b_{q},B_{q})\end{array}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}+B_{j}s)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s)}{\prod_{k=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}-B_{j}s)\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}+A_{j}s)}z^{-s}{\rm d}s,

in which
0\leqslant m\leqslant q
,
0\leqslant n\leqslant p
(not both
m
and
n
simultaneously zero),
A_{j}>0
,
j=1,\cdots,p
,
B_{k}>0
,
k=1,\cdots,q
,
a_{j}
and
b_{k}
are complex numbers such that no poles of
\Gamma(b_{k}+B_{k}s)
,
k=1,\cdots,m,
coincide with poles of
\Gamma(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s)
,
j=1,\cdots,n
, with
\Gamma
being the complete gamma function.

L
is a suitable contour
w-i\infty
to
w+i\infty
,

w\in\mathbb{R}
, separating the poles of the two types mentioned above. For more details, see
[
10
]
.

For the approximation (
3
) the stability and scaling parameters,
\alpha
and
\sigma
, remain to be estimated. Using the well-known Hill’s estimate (cf.
[
7
]
), we have

\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{j=n-k_{n}+1}^{n}\log\frac{X_{(j)}^{\prime}}{X_{(n-k_{n})}^{\prime}}\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{\alpha},

(9)

where
\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}
denotes convergence in probability,
k_{n}\uparrow\infty
,
{k_{n}}/{n}\underset{n}{\rightarrow}0
,
X_{j}^{\prime}=X_{j}-\mu_{X}
and

X_{(1)}^{\prime}\leqslant X_{(2)}^{\prime}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant X_{(n)}^{\prime}

are the order statistics of
(X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,X_{n}^{\prime})
.

As for the scaling parameter
\sigma
, we make use of the results from
[
6
, Corollary 1]
. Consider the characteristic function

\varphi_{Y}(t)=\mathbb{E}\{\exp({itY})\}=\exp({-\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha}}),\quad Y\overset{d}{=}S_{\alpha}(\sigma,0,0).

The inverse transform of
\varphi_{Y}
is given by

l_{\alpha,\sigma}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(xt)\exp({-\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha}}){\rm d}t

and its value at point
x=0
,

l_{\alpha,\sigma}(0)=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha\sigma}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right).

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
and define

Y_{j}^{(n)}=\frac{X_{(j-1)k_{n}+1}^{\prime}+\cdots+X_{jk_{n}}^{\prime}}{k_{n}^{1/\alpha}}

and

\hat{l}_{n}(0)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{n}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}}\mathds{1}(|Y_{j}^{(n)}|\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}),

where
r_{n}=[{n}/{k_{n}}]
, the largest integer and
\mathds{1}(\cdot)
, the indicator function, then besides (
9
) we also have

\hat{\sigma}_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(1/\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}\big[\hat{l}_{n}(0)\big]^{-1}\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\sigma.

(10)

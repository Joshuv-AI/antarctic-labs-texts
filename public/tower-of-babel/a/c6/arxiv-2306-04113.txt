Congruence lattices of infinite semidistributive lattices
George Grätzer, J. B. Nation

Abstract
. An FN lattice F F is a simple, infinite, semidistributive lattice. Its existence was recently proved by R. Freese and J. B. Nation. Let 𝖡 n \mathsf{B}_{n} denote the Boolean lattice with n n atoms. For a lattice K K , let K + K^{+} denote K K with a new unit adjoined. We prove that the finite distributive lattices: 𝖡 0 + , 𝖡 1 + , 𝖡 2 + , … \mathsf{B}_{0}^{+},\mathsf{B}_{1}^{+},\mathsf{B}_{2}^{+},\dots can be represented as congruence lattices of infinite semidistributive lattices. The case n = 0 n=0 is the Freese-Nation result, which is utilized in the proof. We also prove some related representation theorems.

2.
Semidistributivity

A lattice
L
is
meet-semidistributive
, if the following implication holds:

(SD
∧
)

w=x\wedge y=x\wedge z\text{ implies that }w=x\wedge(y\vee z)\text{ for $x,y,z,w\in L$}.

The following statement is well-known.

Lemma 7
.

Let
L
be a meet-semidistributive
L
with
u<v\in L
.
Then the set

I_{u,v}=\{\,x\in L\mid v\wedge x=u\,\}

is an ideal in
\textup{f{}il}(u)
.
Conversely, if
I_{u,v}
is an ideal in
\textup{f{}il}(u)
whenever
u<v
, then
L
is meet-semidistributive.

Dually,
L
is
join-semidistributive
, if the following implication holds:

(
SD
∨
)

u=x\vee y=x\vee z\text{ implies that }u=x\vee(y\wedge z)\text{ for $x,y,z,u\in L$}.

A lattice
L
is
semidistributive

if it is both meet-semidistributive and join-semidistributive.

Let

Define

(1)

We order the set

Definition 8

For

(\{u_{0},u_{1}\},\leq_{u})

Lemma 9

The following statements hold:

(i)

(ii)

The following lemma is well-known.
It holds more generally for doubling intervals, but not

Lemma 10

If
L
is a semidistributive lattice, then so is

.

We define the congruence

The congruence

Proof.

Since

.
∎

We can start with a finite antichain

(2)

L[U]=(L-\{u\})\cup\bigcup(\,\{u_{0},u_{1}\}\mid u\in U\,)

The obvious analogue of Lemma

4.
Representing the three-element chain and
(\mathsf{B}_{2})_{++}

There are only two finite distributive lattices that are known not to be the congruence
lattice of a finite semidistributive lattice minimally
[
8
]
.
The minimality refers to the fact that neither can they be a filter in
\Con L
,
since a homomorphic image of a finite semidistributive lattice is semidistributive.
These are the three-element chain and the lattice
(\mathsf{B}_{2})_{++}
.

In Section
5
,
we will utilize two constructions to represent each of these
as the congruence lattice of an infinite semidistributive lattice.

Lemma 12

(Construction 1)
.

Let
F
be an FN lattice.
Define the lattice
F^{+}
as the lattice
F
with a new unit adjoined.
Then
F^{+}
is an infinite semidistributive lattice
and the congruence lattice of
F^{+}
is the three-element chain, because
F
has no largest element.

Proof.

Obviously, the lattice
F^{+}
is an infinite semidistributive lattice.
Indeed, the only nontrivial congruence of the lattice
F^{+}

is the congruence with one nontrivial congruence class, namely,
F
.
∎

Lemma 13

(Construction 2)
.

Let
F
be an FN lattice and let
u\in F
.
We double the element
u
and claim that
F[u]
is an infinite semidistributive lattice
and the congruence lattice of
F[u]
is the three-element chain.

Proof.

By Lemma
10
,
the lattice
F[u]
is an infinite semidistributive lattice
and by Lemma
11
,
the lattice
F[u]
has only one nontrivial congruence, namely,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
,
so the congruence lattice of
F[u]
is the three-element chain.
(This uses

or

F
has neither.)
∎

In constructing a semidistributive lattice to represent

Claim 14

Proof.

First, we note that the lattice

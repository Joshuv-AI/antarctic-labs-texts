An effective field theory approach to quarkonium at small transverse momentum Preprint: LA-UR-19-31110
Sean Fleming, Yiannis Makris, and Thomas Mehen

Abstract
In this work we apply effective field theory (EFT) to observables in quarkonium production and decay that are sensitive to soft gluon radiation, in particular measurements that are sensitive to small transverse momentum. Within the EFT framework we study χ Q \chi_{Q} decay to light quarks followed by the fragmentation of those quarks to light hadrons. We derive a factorization theorem that involves transverse momentum distribution (TMD) fragmentation functions and new quarkonium TMD shape functions. We derive renormalization group equations, both in rapidity and virtuality, which are used to evolve the different terms in the factorization theorem to resum large logarithms. This theoretical framework will provide a systematic treatment of quarkonium production and decay processes in TMD sensitive measurements.

Introduction

Transverse momentum distributions (TMDs) have been the subject of many theoretical and phenomenological studies in recent years. TMD parton distribution functions (TMDPDFs), which encode information about the three-dimensional distribution of partons in the nucleon, are usually studied through the

for processes involving jets). In these theorems the differential cross section is written in terms of various perturbative and non-perturbative functions up to power corrections. While the perturbative elements can be calculated in a systematic expansion in the strong coupling, the non-perturbative elements need to be fit from experiments. The true power of factorization is that non-perturbative matrix elements are universal and thus can be extracted from one process and used to make predictions for other experiments. Furthermore, factorization separates different scales appearing in a cross section so that renormalization group (RG) evolution can be used to resum logarithms of ratios of disparate scales which is often needed for convergence of the perturbative expansion. RG evolution is a key procedure for the consistent extraction and use of the universal non-perturbative matrix elements since different experiments can operate at widely separated scales.

The extraction of non-perturbative TMDs is achieved by parametrizing the form of these distributions and fitting the transverse momentum differential cross section against experimental data from various processes that are sensitive to the TMDs of interest. For example, the Drell-Yan process gives access to the quark TMDPDFs, but is relatively insensitive to the gluon TMDs. In fact, while unpolarized quark TMDPDFs are extensively studied both experimentally and theoretically, the gluon TMDPDF is not well constrained. This is because quarks can interact via electroweak processes which lead to final states that do not intereact strongly, hence only probing the TMDPDF. Processes in which the final state contains hadrons (which can absorb or emit collinear and soft radiation) are sensitive to other TMD distributions, such as TMD fragmentation functions (TMDFFs) and TMD soft functions. Gluon fusion processes, which potentially can be used to extract the gluon TMDPDF are dominated by hadronic final states, so that understanding soft final state radiation is vital. Processes proposed to study gluon TMDs in collider experiments include inclusive, exclusive, and associated quarkonium production processes
Accardi:2012qut

Despite many successes, the NRQCD factorization approach is only effective when the quarkonium is produced with relatively large transverse momenta. Intuitively, in this kinematic regime, emissions of soft and ultra-soft gluons from the heavy quark pair cannot alter the large transverse momentum of the quarkonium state. Ignoring these soft emissions, the quarkonium transverse momentum is then determined from the hard process and infrared (IR) divergences that are present in perturbative calculations of the partonic process are absorbed into the non-perturbative LDMEs and collinear PDFs. However, when quarkonia are produced with small transverse momenta, this soft gluon factorization assumption must be relaxed. In Refs.

;
Fleming:2003gt
;
Fleming:2006cd

It was found that promoting the LDMEs into quarkonium shape functions is necessary for accurately accounting for soft radiation from the heavy quark pair.

A central point of this paper is to introduce TMD quarkonium shape functions which will appear generically for TMD observables involving quarkonia.

2
SCET and vNRQCD

SCET and vNRQCD are effective field theories that describe the degrees of freedom of QCD in the soft and collinear, and non-relativistic limits, respectively. Both theories are well established and address a wide spectrum of physical phenomena. We aim to study a TMD dependent observable in
\chi_{J}
decaying to light quarks followed by their fragmentation to light hadrons. vNRQCD is needed for the heavy quarkonium in the initial state and SCET is needed for the energetic hadrons as well as soft radiation in the final state. Our aim in this section is not to give a comprehensive review of those EFTs but rather to introduce the elements and notation necessary for the analysis we pursue.

2.1
Non-relativistic QCD

There are three important scales that appear when studying the dynamics of non-relativistic heavy quarks: the mass of the heavy quark,
m
, the size of their momentum in the quarkonium rest frame,
m\text{v}
, and their kinetic energy,
m\text{v}^{2}
. The distance
r\sim 1/(m\text{v})
gives an estimate on the size of the quarkonium state and the separation between the heavy quark-antiquark pair. The non-relativistic kinetic energy
\Delta E\sim m\text{v}^{2}
is of the same order as the energy splittings of radial excitations. We refer to
m\text{v}
and
m\text{v}^{2}
as the soft and ultra-soft scales respectively. Correspondingly, gluons that have all of their four-momentum components scaling as
m\text{v}
and
m\text{v}^{2}
are called soft and ultra-soft gluons respectively. While the ultra-soft scale is well within the non-perturbative regime the soft scale is about 1.4 GeV for bottomonium and and about 800 MeV for charmonium.

The effective theory of vNRQCD is a version of non-relativistic QCD introduced in Ref.
Luke:1999kz
and recently formulated in a manifestly gauge invariant form in Ref.
Rothstein:2018dzq
. What we find appealing about this version of NRQCD is the clear distinction of soft and ultra-soft degrees of freedom and the use of label-momentum notation. Both aspects are crucial for this work. We work in the limit where the measurement is sensitive to the kinematics of the heavy quark-antiquark pair (in the quarkonium rest frame) and therefore it is critical to separate the various infrared degrees of freedom. Using the four-vector
v^{\mu}=(1,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{0}}})
, the four-momenta of the heavy quark,
p_{Q}^{\mu}
, can be decomposed as follows,

p^{\mu}_{Q}=mv^{\mu}+k^{\mu}\,,

(1)

where
k^{0}
is the kinetic energy and
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}
is the three momenta of the heavy quark. Since the heavy quarks we consider are on-shell i.e.,
p_{Q}^{2}=m^{2}
, then in the non-relativistic limit, where the three momenta are small compared to the mass,
|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}|\sim m\text{v}
, with
\text{v}\ll 1
we have

\displaystyle p^{2}_{Q}=m^{2}+mk^{0}+(k^{0})^{2}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}^{2}=m^{2}\,,

(2)

which has a solution only if
k^{0}\sim m\text{v}^{2}
. Thus, the components of
k^{\mu}
scale as

k^{\mu}\sim m(\text{v}^{2},\text{v},\text{v},\text{v})\,.

(3)

In the presence of both soft and ultra-soft modes it is important to separate the small components of the four-momenta,
k^{\mu}
, into soft (label, denoted
\ell^{\mu}
) and ultra-soft (residual, denoted
r^{\mu}
) parts:

p^{\mu}_{Q}=mv^{\mu}+\ell^{\mu}+r^{\mu}\,,

(4)

where

\displaystyle r^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim m(\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2})\;,

\displaystyle\ell^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim m(0,\text{v},\text{v},\text{v})\,.

(5)

The QCD heavy quark field,
\Psi
, can then be decomposed into the vNRQCD heavy quark field,
\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)
, as follows,

\Psi(x)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}e^{-imt-i{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}}\,\frac{1+v\!\!\!/\penalty}{2}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)\,.

(6)

The soft,
A_{\ell}^{\mu}
, and ultra-soft,
A_{us}^{\mu}
, gluon fields have momenta which scale (all four components) as soft (
\sim m\text{v}
) or ultra-soft (
\sim m\text{v}^{2}
), respectively.

The Lagrangian of the EFT can then be written in terms of those fields in the following form
Luke:1999kz

\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\Big(iD^{0}-\frac{({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}}-i{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}})^{2}}{2m}\Big)\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}+\mathcal{L}^{(n>2)}+(\psi\to\chi,T\to\bar{T})+\mathcal{L}_{s}(A_{q}^{\mu})+\mathcal{L}^{V}(\psi,\chi,A_{q}^{\mu})\;,

(7)

where
\chi
is the heavy antiquark field and
\mathcal{L}^{(n)}
are higher order terms and the superscript
n
denotes the suppression in powers of
v
of
\mathcal{L}^{(n)}
relative to the leading order Lagrangian. In Eq. (
7
),
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is the soft gluon and ghost part of the Lagrangian, and
\mathcal{L}^{V}
contains the potential terms which have the following generic structure,

Double soft gluon emissions:

\displaystyle\;\;\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime},\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\Big(A^{\mu}_{\ell}\;A^{\nu}_{\ell^{\prime}}\Big)\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime}}U_{\mu\nu}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime},\ell,\ell^{\prime})\;,

Heavy quark-antiquark potential:

\displaystyle\;\;\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime}}\Big(\psi^{{\dagger}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}T^{A}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime}}\Big)\Big(\chi^{{\dagger}}_{-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\bar{T}^{A}\chi_{-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime}}\Big)V({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}^{\prime})\;.

Recent developments in ref.
Makris:2019ttx
include Glauber and Coulomb interactions of the heavy quark fields with the collinear and soft partons correspondingly. The label momentum operator
Bauer:2001ct
,
\mathcal{P}^{\mu}=(\mathcal{P}^{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}})
, is defined such that it projects only onto the label momentum space,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mu}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)

\displaystyle=\ell^{\mu}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mu}A_{\ell}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\ell^{\mu}A_{\ell}^{\nu}\;.

(8)

and the covariant derivative is:
iD^{\mu}\equiv i\partial^{\mu}-gA_{us}^{\mu}(x)
.

It is important to note that in the Lagrangian there is no interaction with a heavy quark/anti-quark and a single soft gluon, since such terms will put the heavy quarks too far off-shell. At order
\alpha_{s}
interactions with two soft gluons are allowed and are contained in the potential part of the Lagrangian. These interaction make it impossible to decouple the soft gluons form the heavy quarks, which is why they must appear in the same TMD matrix element. Another important observation is that the first term in the Lagrangian of Eq. (
7
) is the only term in which the ultrasoft gluon couples to the heavy quarks at leading order. As a result
the ultra-soft gluon can be decoupled from the heavy quarks using a BPS
Bauer:2001yt
;
Rothstein:2018dzq
field redefinition,

\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}^{(0)}(x)\equiv Y_{v}(x)\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)\;,

(9)

where
Y_{v}(x)
is the ultra-soft gluon time-like Wilson line,

Y_{v}(x)=\exp\Big(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dt^{\prime}A_{us}^{0}(t+t^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}})\Big)\;.

(10)

Of course, this field redefinition will simplify the leading Lagrangian but it will introduce ultra-soft Wilson lines into
\mathcal{L}_{s}
and the sub-leading terms
\mathcal{L}^{(n>0)}
. We will not be working with most of those terms but the next-to-leading contribution,

\mathcal{L}^{(1)}=-\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}^{{\dagger}}(x)\frac{-i{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}}}{m}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}(x)\;,

(11)

is of interest. After the BPS field redefinition this term becomes,

\mathcal{L}^{(1)}=-g\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}{\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}^{(0)}}^{{\dagger}}(x)\frac{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{B}}}_{us}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}}}{m}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\ell}}}}^{(0)}(x)\;.

(12)

where
B^{\mu}_{us}(x)=-g^{-1}Y_{v}^{{\dagger}}(x)(iD^{\mu})Y_{v}(x)
, is the ultra-soft gluon building block.

2.2
Soft collinear effective theory

The soft collinear effective theory has been used successfully in a wide variety of topics including TMD phenomenology. Particularly, the version of SCET that we are considering is SCET
{}_{\text{II}}
which involves the collinear and soft degrees of freedom (in contrast to SCET
{}_{\text{I}}
which involves the ultra-soft). The power-counting parameter of SCET is usually denoted by
\lambda
. The scaling of the relevant degrees of freedom, in light-cone coordinates
(p^{+},p^{-},p^{\perp})
, can be written in terms of
\lambda
as

collinear:

\displaystyle\;\;\;p_{c}^{\mu}\sim Q(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda)_{n}

soft:

\displaystyle\;\;\;p_{s}^{\mu}\sim Q(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)_{n}\,.

(13)

Q
is the typical hard scale and
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
is the four-vector along which we expand in light-cone coordinates,

p^{\mu}=\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}p^{-}+\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}p^{+}+p_{\perp}^{\mu}\,,

(14)

where
p^{-}=n\cdot p
,
p^{+}=\bar{n}\cdot p
, and
\bar{n}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)
such that
n^{2}=\bar{n}^{2}=0
and
n\cdot\bar{n}=2
. We separate the label and residual components of momenta as follows:

\displaystyle p^{\mu}_{c}

\displaystyle=\ell^{\mu}+r^{\mu}\;

where

\displaystyle\ell^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim Q(0,1,\lambda)\;,

and

\displaystyle r^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim Q(\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2})\,.

(15)

The EFT collinear quark fields
\xi_{n,\ell}
are then defined as

\xi_{n,\ell}(x)=P_{n}\;q_{n,\ell}(x)\;,

(16)

where
P_{n}=\not{n}\not{\bar{n}}/4
and
q_{n,\ell}
is the QCD label momentum partitioned field

\Psi(x)=\sum_{\ell\neq 0}e^{-i\ell\cdot x}q_{n,\ell}(x)\;.

(17)

The collinear (
A_{n,\ell}^{\mu}
) and soft (
A_{s,\ell}^{\mu}
) gluons are defined similarly:

A^{\mu}(x)=\sum_{\ell\neq 0}e^{-i\ell\cdot x}A_{\ell}^{\mu}(x)\;,

(18)

where
A^{\mu}(x)
are the full theory bosons and
A^{\mu}_{\ell}=A^{\mu}_{n,\ell}+A^{\mu}_{s,\ell}
. The SCET Lagrangian can be found in Refs.
Bauer:2000yr
;
Bauer:2001ct
;
Bauer:2001yt
.

Invariance under collinear, soft, and ultra-soft gauge transformations requires that particular combinations of fields appear (rather than
\xi_{n}
,
A_{n}
, and
A_{s}

\displaystyle\xi_{n,\ell}(x)

\displaystyle\to S_{n}(x)\chi_{n,\ell}(x)\equiv S_{n}(x)W_{n}^{{\dagger}}(x)\xi_{n,\ell}(x)\;,

\displaystyle A_{n,\ell}^{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{S}^{ab}_{n}B_{n,\perp}^{b\mu}(x)\equiv-\frac{1}{g}\mathcal{S}^{ab}_{n}\text{Tr}\Big(T^{b}W_{n}^{{\dagger}}(x)(\mathcal{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}-gA_{n,\perp}^{\mu})W_{n}(x)\Big)\;,

(19)

where
W_{n}
and
S_{n}
are the collinear and soft Wilson lines respectively, defined as:

\displaystyle W_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\text{P}\exp\Big(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}ds\bar{n}\cdot A_{n}(x+\bar{n}s)\Big)\;,

\displaystyle S_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\text{P}\exp\Big(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dsn\cdot A_{s}(x+ns)\Big)\;.

(20)

Note that collinear fields cannot directly interact with soft gluons. As a result the leading SCET Lagrangian does not include interactions of soft and collinear gluons or quarks. On the other hand in full QCD it is possible to have such interactions through Glauber gluon exchanges

In this section we have introduced two power counting parameters, one for vNRQCD (
v
), and one for SCET (
\lambda
). Its is natural to ask about the hierarchy between these small parameters. We are interested in two distinct cases:

•

\text{v}\sim\lambda
: The two EFTs overlap in the region of soft dynamics. Particularly the soft gluon of one theory is the same as in the other. In this case the two EFTs merge into a single one containing all degrees of freedom. We will refer to this EFT as SCET
{}_{\text{Q}}
.

•

\text{v}\ll\lambda
: There is no overlap and thus we need to work with both EFTs. Factorization theorems for this region can be achieved by matching from SCET
{}_{\text{Q}}
onto vNRQCD.

For the rest of this paper we will be assuming
\text{v}\sim\lambda
and use SCET
{}_{\text{Q}}
.

3
Operators, Matching, Reparametrization Invariance, and Gauge Invariance

In this section we consider the operators that appear in the hard sector of the Lagrangian by performing tree level matching of the QCD diagrams with an arbitrary number of soft gluon emissions from the heavy quark and antiquark lines.
We then study the simple but non-trivial example of
q\bar{q}\to Q\bar{Q}

+
gluons. We match these QCD diagrams onto NRQCD operators with soft Wilson lines and the
Q\bar{Q}
in relative S- and P-waves. We apply reparameterization invariance (RPI) of the EFT to show that two of the operators appearing in the P-wave channel are related to the S-wave operator by RPI transformations. Finally, we discuss gauge invariance in the effective theory and show that our results respect all gauge symmetries of the EFT.

It should be noted that in the current formulation of the SCETQ operators we cannot distinguish between the past and future going Wilson lines. Such a distinction is important when discussing universality of soft functions and properties of rapidity divergences. Although this analysis can be done in SCETQ we do not pursue this in this work. Such an analysis will be useful for discussing cancellation of rapidity divergences to all orders in perturbation theory, which we leave for future work. In addition, in our results no transverse gauge link is present, contrary to other soft functions such as those in Drell- Yan and electron-positron annihilation. 3.4
Gauge invariance

In this subsection, we investigate the gauge transformation properties of the three operators that appeared in the explicit matching calculation presented earlier in the example
q\bar{q}\to Q\bar{Q}
. Within the EFT there are three types of gauge transformation: collinear, soft, and ultra-soft. We demonstrate that all three operators are invariant under these transformations. Collinear fields transform only under collinear and ultra-soft gauge transformations and soft fields only under soft and ultra-soft. Potential (heavy quark/antiquark) and ultra-soft fields transform only under ultra-soft gauge transformations. The exact form of the transformations for the relevant fields and building blocks are shown in Tab.
1
.

We begin with the transformation of the collinear sector, which appears in all three of the relevant operators:

\bar{\chi}_{\bar{n}}\gamma^{i}S^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{n}}T^{a}S_{n}\chi_{n}\;.

(64)

This term is invariant under the collinear transformations, since the building block
\chi_{n}
is the only operator that contains collinear fields and is invariant by construction. Under soft and ultra-soft gauge transformations:

\bar{\chi}_{\bar{n}}\gamma^{i}S^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{n}}T^{a}S_{n}\chi_{n}\to\bar{\chi}_{\bar{n}}\gamma^{i}S^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{n}}V_{s/us}T^{a}V^{{\dagger}}_{s/us}S_{n}\chi_{n}=\mathcal{V}_{s/us}^{ab}\bar{\chi}_{\bar{n}}\gamma^{i}S^{{\dagger}}_{\bar{n}}T^{b}S_{n}\chi_{n}\,,

(65)

where
\mathcal{V}_{s/us}^{ab}
is the gauge transformation matrix in the adjoint representation. Since the generators of the adjoint representation,
t^{c}_{ab}

t^{c}_{ab}=if^{acb}
, the following relation is satisfied

\mathcal{V}_{ba}=(\mathcal{V}^{-1})_{ab}=(\mathcal{V}^{{\dagger}})_{ab}\,.

(66)

This gauge dependence needs to be cancelled by the heavy quark sector, which we now consider. Each of the operators contains heavy (potential) fields and soft fields, and the analysis is simpler if we consider them separately. Obviously since these terms do not contain any collinear fields, they do not transform under collinear gauge transformations.

|\chi_{J}\rangle
state is summed over all possible polarizations. For the
P
-waves to project onto the individual
J=
0,1, and 2 we can use the projection operators discussed in Ref.
Petrelli:1997ge
or the helicity decomposition techniques in Ref.
Braaten:1996jt
. Note this projection is only possible since we sum over the polarization of the quarkonium state. For polarized quarkonium states interference terms of the same helicity but different values of
J
are not excluded although they might be simplified using approximate heavy quark spin symmetry of NRQCD. Applying the projection operators at the matrix element level we obtain the
P
-wave color-singlet quarkonium shape function

S^{\perp}_{\chi_{J}\to{}^{3}P_{J}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})=(2J+1)\frac{g^{2}}{N^{2}_{c}\;t_{F}}\;\mathcal{A}_{J}^{ij}\;\text{tr}\Big\langle\chi_{J}\Big|\psi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\chi\Big[\frac{B_{s}^{a,i}}{m\;v\cdot\mathcal{P}}\Big]\mathcal{S}^{ba}_{v}(S_{\bar{n}}^{{\dagger}}T^{b}S_{n})\\
\times\;\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}}_{\perp})(S_{n}^{{\dagger}}T^{c}S_{\bar{n}})\mathcal{S}_{v}^{dc}\Big[\frac{B_{s}^{d,j}}{m\;v\cdot\mathcal{P}}\Big]\chi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\psi\Big|\chi_{J}\Big\rangle\,,

(89)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}^{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-1}\delta_{\perp}^{ij}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}^{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-1}\delta^{ij}-\frac{1}{(d-1)(d-2)}\delta_{\perp}^{ij}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}^{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d+1}\delta^{ij}-\frac{d-3}{(d+1)(d-1)(d-2)}\delta_{\perp}^{ij}\,,

(90)

where
\delta_{\perp}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}-\delta^{iz}
. Similarly to the
S
-wave case the NRQCD bilinears are individually traced over color and Dirac indices. In the following sub-sections we give the
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s})
expressions for each of the various functions in Eq. (

).

4.2
The hard function

The hard function for the quark-antiquark fusion processes can be extracted from Eq. (132) of Ref.
Petrelli:1997ge
:

\displaystyle H(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\Big\{\ln^{2}\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)+3\ln\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-2B({}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1})\Big\}-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}\ln\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)\,,

(91)

\displaystyle B({}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{F}\bigg(-8+\frac{2}{3}\pi^{2}\bigg)+C_{A}\bigg(\frac{50}{9}+\frac{2}{3}\ln 2-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\bigg)-\frac{10}{9}n_{f}t_{F}\,.

(92)

Note the Coulomb singularities in Eq. (132) of Ref.
Petrelli:1997ge
need to be dropped as they are reproduced in the EFT, and the
1/\epsilon
divergences are dropped as well, as they cancel against the combined counterterm of the EFT.
The running of the hard function is the “opposite” of the combined running of the three TMD functions in Eq. (
85
), and the combined counterterm can also be extracted from Eq. (132) of Ref.
Petrelli:1997ge
:

Z=1-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\Big\{\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)+\frac{3}{2\epsilon}\Big\}-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,.

(93)

Then the anomalous dimension can be determined through standard methods, from which we infer

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}^{H}(\mu)

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\Big\{\ln\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)+\frac{3}{2}\Big\}-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{\pi}\,.

(94)

The hard function then satisfies the RGE

\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}H(\mu)=\gamma_{\mu}^{H}(\mu)H(\mu)\,.

(95)

It is important to note the additional divergence and associated logarithm that is proportional to
C_{A}
. Since the divergences from the TMDFFs at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
are proportional to
C_{F}
and since the total cross section needs to be independent of the renormalization scale, a corresponding term needs to be generated from the real soft emission diagrams. The only other element in the factorization theorem that does depend on the production mechanism is the shape function,
S^{\perp}_{\chi\to[n]}
. Thus, it is expected that such a contribution will be present in the shape function.

4.3
The TMDFF

The TMD functions that appear in the factorization theorem are nonperturbative for
k_{\perp}\sim\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
, but for
k_{\perp}\gg\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
can be expanded in powers of
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}/k_{\perp}
. The expansion can be written as a convolution of short distance matching coefficients and the collinear fragmentation functions (FF). For quark fragmentation we have
Echevarria:2014rua
;
Bain:2016rrv

D^{\perp}_{q/h}(x,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\;\mathcal{J}_{q/j}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)\;D_{j/h}\Big(\frac{x}{z};\mu\Big)\,.

(96)

The matching coefficient and FF need to be evaluated at the same factorization scale,
\mu
, before convolving. To this end we evaluate the matching coefficients at their characteristic scale and the FFs we evolve using the standard DGLAP renormalization group equations. The additional scale,
\nu
, that appears in the arguments of the unsubtracted TMD fragmentation function and the matching coefficients in Eq. (
96
) comes from the
\eta
-regulator (see Refs.
Chiu:2011qc
;
Chiu:2012ir
) which we use to regulate rapidity divergences. Rapidity divergences remain unregulated in pure dimensional regularization (dim-reg). We refer to this scale as the rapidity scale or simply rapidity.
The renormalized matching coefficients at next-to-leading order (NLO) are given by (see Ref.
Echevarria:2014rua
;
Bain:2016rrv
):

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{q/j}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{qj}\delta(1-z)\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})

(97)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{s}T_{qj}}{\pi}\Big\{\Big[\delta_{ij}\delta(1-z)\ln\Big(\frac{\omega^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\Big)+\bar{P}_{jq}(z)\Big]\frac{1}{2\pi\mu^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\mu^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2}}\bigg)_{+}+c_{qj}(z)\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})\Big\},

where
\omega
is the minus component of the initiating parton with

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{qq}(z)=P_{qq}(z)-\bar{\gamma}_{q}\delta(1-z)=(1+z^{2})\frac{1}{(1-z)_{+}},

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{gq}(z)=P_{gq}(z)=\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z},

(98)

and

{c}_{qq}(z)=\frac{1-z}{2},\;\;{c}_{qg}(z)=\frac{z}{2}\,.

(99)

Here
T_{qq}=T_{qg}=C_{F},
, and
\bar{\gamma}_{q}=\bar{\gamma}_{\bar{q}}=3/2
. The
\mu
-anomalous dimension for the TMDFF is:

\gamma_{\mu,q}^{D}(M,\nu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\Big\{\ln\Big(\frac{\nu^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big)+\bar{\gamma}_{q}\Big\}\;.

(100)

The rapidity renormalization group (RRG) equation has a convolutional form

\frac{d}{d\ln\nu}D^{\perp}_{i/h}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)=\int\frac{d^{2}k^{\prime}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\gamma_{\nu,i}^{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}^{\prime}_{\perp},\mu)\;D^{\perp}_{i/h}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}^{\prime}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)

(101)

where

\gamma_{\nu,k}^{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp},\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\frac{1}{\pi\mu^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\mu^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}^{2}_{\perp}}\bigg)_{+}\,.

(102)

Eq. (
101
) can be put a multiplicative form by Fourier transform to transverse position space,
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}}
-space. Denoting
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}}
-space quantites with a tilde, Eq. (
101
) transforms to

\frac{d}{d\ln\nu}\tilde{D}^{\perp}_{i/h}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}};\mu,\nu)=\tilde{\gamma}_{\nu,i}^{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)\;\tilde{D}^{\perp}_{i/h}(z,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}};\mu,\nu)\,

(103)

where

\tilde{\gamma}_{\nu,q}^{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\Big\{\ln\bigg(\frac{b^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\bigg)+\bar{\gamma}_{q}\Big\}\,.

(104)

4.4
The shape functions

The analysis of the shape function is the main topic of this paper. We calculate the TMD shape functions arising in the decay to NLO. The calculation is performed within the EFT approach and we use the EFT Lagrangian and Feynman rules for the calculations of the corresponding diagrams. At the end of this section our goal is to determine the RGEs satisfied by the shape functions. As in the case of the TMDFF, in the regime of interest the shape functions are non-perturbative objects, but for
|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}_{\perp}|\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}
the soft modes become perturbative and the TMD shape function can be expressed as a product of NRQCD LDMEs at the usoft scale and perturbative coefficients at the soft scale. Then the total shape function defined in Eqs.(
88
) and (
89
) can be written as:

{S}^{\perp}_{\chi_{J}\to{}^{2s+1}L^{[1,8]}_{J}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu_{0},\nu_{0})=\sum_{n}{C}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu_{0},\nu_{0},\mu)\times\langle\mathcal{O}^{[n]}\rangle^{\mu}_{\chi_{J}}\;.

(105)

This matching procedure it is essentially the standard NRQCD factorization applied to the shape function instead of the cross section. For the process we are considering and for each value of
J
there are two LDMEs onto which we match:

\displaystyle\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{J}|\psi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}T^{a}\chi\cdot\chi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}T^{a}\psi|\chi_{J}\rangle\;,

\displaystyle\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}

\displaystyle=(2J+1)\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\langle\chi_{J}|\psi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\chi\chi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\psi|\chi_{J}\rangle\;.

(106)

At NLO only these LDMEs appear in the matching but at higher orders there might be others as well. Note that the integrated shape functions reduce to

\displaystyle\int d^{2}q_{\perp}S^{\perp}_{\chi_{J}\to{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-2}{d-1}\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}\;,

\displaystyle\int d^{2}q_{\perp}S^{\perp}_{\chi_{J}\to{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle^{1B}_{\chi_{J}}\,,

(107)

where

\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle^{1B}_{\chi_{J}}=(2J+1)\frac{g^{2}}{N^{2}_{c}}\mathcal{A}_{J}^{ij}\Big\langle\chi_{J}\Big|\psi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\chi\Big[\frac{B_{s}^{a,i}}{m\;v\cdot\mathcal{P}}\Big]\Big[\frac{B_{s}^{a,j}}{m\;v\cdot\mathcal{P}}\Big]\chi^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\raise 3.99997pt\hbox{${}^{\leftrightarrow}$}\hskip-10.5pt\mathcal{P}}}}\;\psi\Big|\chi_{J}\Big\rangle

(108)

The color-octet S-wave shape function

The color-octet
S
-wave shape function at LO is given by simply evaluating it at
g_{s}=0

S^{\perp,\text{LO}}_{\chi\to{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)=\frac{d-2}{d-1}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})\,4m^{2}\eta^{{\dagger}}\sigma^{i}T^{a}\xi\times\xi^{{\dagger}}\sigma^{i}T^{a}\eta\,,

(110)

where

(111)

At NLO where only diagrams with one soft or ultra-soft gluon are needed, the contributions can be categorized as follows (note we do not show the heavy-quark self energy contributions as they are well known):

1.

Ultra-soft gluon exchanges between heavy quarks and/or antiquarks. After the BPS field redefinition the interactions of the heavy quarks through ultra-soft gluons are pushed to the soft sector of the vNRQCD Lagrangian as well as to subleading terms in the Lagrangian. This induces usoft Wilson lines along the directions

,
n
and
\bar{n}
to appear in operators. We will suppress these Wilson lines in the operators since, with the exception of the interaction we consider here, usoft interactions only produce uninteresting scaleless integrals that we drop in our scheme. However, we will consider one subleading operator, the so-called chromo-electric dipole transition, as it leads to mixing between the
{}^{3}P^{[J]}_{1}
and
{}^{3}P^{[J]}_{8}
channels. After the BPS field redefinition this operator has the form:

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}-g\psi^{{\dagger}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\frac{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{B}}}_{us}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mathcal{P}}}}}{m}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}+(\psi\to\chi,T\to\bar{T})\,.

(112)

The diagrams associated with this contribution are shown in Fig.
3
. Note that the virtual contributions, diagrams (e) and (f), will mix operators that contribute to
\chi_{J}
decay only at higher orders in the relative velocity expansion. Thus we only consider real contributions, diagrams (a)-(d).

2.

Ultra-soft gluon exchanges between heavy quarks and ultra-soft Wilson lines. These contributions involve a single iteration of the interaction term in Eq.(
112
) and a single insertion of an ultra-soft gluon from any of the corresponding Wilson lines. Adding the corresponding diagrams shown in the first line of Fig.
4

3.

Ultra-soft gluon exchanges between ultra-soft Wilson lines. The corresponding diagrams are shown in the second line of Fig.
4

Thus, independent of scheme these diagrams may be set to zero if the corresponding zero-bin subtractions are set to zero as well.

4.

Coulomb interactions between heavy quarks and antiquarks. This contribution is generated through the coulomb interaction term in the vNRQCD Lagrangian
Luke:1999kz

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}}\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}}{({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}})^{2}}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}T^{a}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}}\chi^{{\dagger}}_{-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}}\bar{T}^{a}\chi_{-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}}\,.

(113)

The associated diagrams are shown in Fig.
5

5.

Soft gluon exchanges between soft Wilson lines. The real contributions are the only pieces with non-trivial dependence on transverse momentum. The virtual gluons contributions only give scaleless integrals that we set to zero in our scheme. The relevant (real emission) diagrams contributing at this order are shown in Fig.

S^{\perp,\text{NLO}}_{\chi\to{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})=\frac{8\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{9\pi m^{2}}\sum_{J}\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle_{\text{LO}}\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})-2\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})+c_{J}\Big)\,,

(131)

where

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{5}{6}\;,

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{7}{12}\;,

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{41}{60}\;.

(132)

Up to color factors the calculation of the color octet shape function is the same. Reevaluating the color factors using

d^{abc}d^{abd}=4B_{F}\delta^{cd}

(133)

we get

S^{\perp,\;\text{NLO}}_{\chi\to{}^{3}P^{[8]}_{J}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})=\frac{8\alpha_{s}B_{F}}{9\pi m^{2}}\sum_{J}\langle{}^{3}P^{[8]}_{J}\rangle_{\text{LO}}\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})-2\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})+c_{J}\Big)\,.

(134)

Matching in the perturbative region at NLO

As discussed before, in the region where
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}\ll\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}
we can match the shape function onto usoft vNRQCD operators. The matching is performed by expanding the left and right hand side of Eq. (
105
) to the same order. This results in recursive relations

\displaystyle C^{i}_{\text{LO}}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[i]}\rangle_{\text{LO}}

\displaystyle=S^{\perp,\;\text{LO}}

\displaystyle C^{i}_{\text{NLO}}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[i]}\rangle_{\text{LO}}

\displaystyle=S^{\perp,\;\text{NLO}}-C^{i}_{\text{LO}}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[i]}\rangle_{\text{NLO}}

From a direct comparison with Eqs.(
110
) and (
128
) we find:

\displaystyle C^{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}_{\text{LO}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\delta^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})\;,

\displaystyle C^{{}^{3}P^{[1/8]}_{J}}_{\text{LO}}

\displaystyle=0\;.

(136)

Next to obtain the NLO matching coefficients we need the NLO
S
-wave matrix element. These diagrams have already been calculated; namely diagrams (a)-(h) in Figs.
3
,
4
and
5
. We find

\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle_{\text{NLO}}=\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3\pi m^{2}}\Big\{C_{F}\sum_{J}\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle_{\text{LO}}+B_{F}\sum_{J}\langle{}^{3}P^{[8]}_{J}\rangle_{\text{LO}}\Big\}\;\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\text{UV}}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\text{IR}}}\Big)\\
+(C_{F}-\frac{1}{2}C_{A})\;\frac{\pi\alpha_{s}}{\text{v}}\;\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle_{\text{LO}}\,.

(137)

Note that this matrix element introduces a non-trivial dependence on the
P
-wave matrix elements at leading order. We discuss the significance of this in the following sections. Using this result we obtain the matching coefficients at NLO

\displaystyle C^{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}_{\text{NLO}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\Big(S^{\perp}_{\text{DY}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{\pi}\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})\Big)\;,

\displaystyle C^{{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}}_{\text{NLO}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{9\pi m^{2}}\Big(-2\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})+c_{J}\Big)\;,

\displaystyle C^{{}^{3}P^{[8]}_{J}}_{\text{NLO}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\alpha_{s}B_{F}}{9\pi m^{2}}\Big(-2\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})+c_{J}\Big)\;.

(138)

Here we introduce the renormalized Drell-Yan function:

S^{\perp}_{\text{DY}}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\Big\{4\mathcal{L}_{0}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})\ln\Big(\frac{\nu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)-4\mathcal{L}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}^{2},\mu^{2})-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\delta({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp})\Big\}\,.

(139)

Next we discuss the form of various divergences and how those cancel or renormalized in order to obtain finite and meaningful predictions. At the end of this section we describe the RG evolution properties of the shape function and give the prescription to resum logarithms in the TMD spectrum of
\chi_{J}
decay.

Treatment of divergences

Before proceeding with renormalization and RG evolution we wish to point out some salient points regarding divergences in our calculation. First we discuss the importance of the operator mixing terms arising from diagrams (a)-(d) in Fig.
3
. This is followed by a discussion of the soft-soft interaction in diagram (l) of Fig.
6
.

Channel mixing:

As mentioned earlier, diagrams (a)-(d) give part of the NLO contribution of the
{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}
LDME and the result has the spinor structure of the
P
-wave LDME. Although they give a scaleless contribution and should be set to zero in our scheme, doing so would lead one to miss the significant role these diagrams play. Specifically, they lead to operator mixing under renormalization. If we had set this contribution to zero and taken the
1/\epsilon
in Eq. (
134
) as a UV divergence needing to be absorbed into the
P
-wave shape function we would have missed this mixing. This is why we choose not to discard this particular contribution.

Non-singlet divergences from diagram (l):

As pointed out earlier diagram (l) which describes soft radiation from the color octet
S
-wave
Q\bar{Q}
state results in a divergence in Eq. (
124
) proportional to
C_{A}
. This divergence is accompanied by
\ln(q_{\perp}/\mu)
that needs to be resummed through RG evolution. The presence of this divergence (and the associated logarithm) serves also as a non-trivial check of factorization at NLO. The scale independence of the cross section requires a corresponding logarithm proportional to
C_{A}
to be present in another of the functions in the factorized decay rate. Since the (un-subtracted) TMDFFs are universal and channel independent this term needs to appear in the corresponding hard function. Indeed, in the virtual cross section calculation (see Ref.
Petrelli:1997ge
) we find such a term.

Renormalization, evolution, and resummation

The shape function we are considering here is related to the transverse momentum measurement of the quarkonium state. As expected the perturbative calculation suffers from rapidity divergences along with the usual IR and UV divergences. The rapidity scale dependence introduced by the necessary regulator is described by the rapidity renormalization group evolution (RRGE). In this section we address both the virtuality and rapidity evolution equations one at a time.

As discussed in Sect.
4.3
running which involves a convolution in
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp}
, can be made multiplicative under a Fourier transform to
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}}
-space. In this section, for simplicity we will work with quantities in
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}}
-space, which are denoted by the tilde. Note some quantities like the LDMEs do not depend on transverse momentum and are unaffected by transforming.

First we consider the virtuality RGE which has non-trivial matrix structure. The RG equations satisfied by the matching coefficients
C_{n}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)
in Eq. (
105
) and the LDMEs they multiply are:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\tilde{C}_{n}(b;\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\Big(\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu}^{S}\delta^{nm}+\gamma^{nm}_{C}\Big)\tilde{C}_{m}(b;\mu,\nu)\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[n]}_{\chi_{J}}\rangle^{(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\gamma^{nm}_{\mathcal{O}}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[m]}\rangle^{(\mu)}_{\chi_{J}}\;,

(140)

where by consistency
\tilde{\gamma}^{S}_{\mu}=-\tilde{\gamma}^{H+2D}_{\mu}
, and
\tilde{\gamma}^{H+2D}_{\mu}
is the sum of the hard and TMDFF anomalous dimensions. From consistency of the factorization theorem we also have

\gamma^{T}_{C}=-\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}\;.

(141)

In this section we calculate the anomalous dimensions in Eq. (
140
) and solve the corresponding RG equations. We begin our analysis with the LDMEs and and then perform the analogous analysis for the matching coefficients. At the order we are working we need only to consider two channels:
{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}
and
{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{0}
. These two channels are the leading channels in the velocity expansion based on the NRQCD power-counting.

We define the renormalized LDMEs,
\langle\mathcal{O}^{[n]}\rangle^{(\mu)}_{\chi_{J}}
as follows,

\langle\mathcal{O}^{[n]}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}=Z_{\mathcal{O}}^{nm}\langle\mathcal{O}^{[m]}\rangle^{(\mu)}_{\chi_{J}}\,,

(142)

and the anomalous dimension is given in terms of the renormalization matrix
Z
:

\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}^{nk}\equiv-(Z_{\mathcal{O}}^{-1})^{nm}\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}Z_{\mathcal{O}}^{mk}\,.

(143)

Since at this order only
\langle\mathcal{O}^{[1]}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}\equiv\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle_{\chi_{J}}
needs to be renormalized we have

\displaystyle Z_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\frac{4\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{3\pi m^{2}}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(144)

which gives the LDMEs anomalous dimension matrix:

\displaystyle\gamma_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{3\pi m^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(145)

We can now solve the RG equation to obtain the evolved LDMEs. At next-leading-logarithmic accuracy (NLL or
\textrm{NLL}^{\prime}
) we have

\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle^{(\mu)}_{\chi_{J}}=\mathcal{U}_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}^{1m}(\mu,\mu_{f})\langle\mathcal{O}^{[m]}\rangle^{(\mu_{f})}_{\chi_{J}}\,.

(146)

where in practice the scale
\mu_{f}
is the scale at which the LDMEs are extracted and
\mu
the scale at which they are evaluated. Most recent extraction use
\mu_{f}=2m_{Q}\simeq M
. The evolution kernel
\mathcal{U}_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}(\mu,\mu_{f})
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}(\mu,\mu_{f})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}(\mu,\mu_{f})\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle\omega_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}(\mu,\mu_{f})

\displaystyle=-\frac{8C_{F}}{3m^{2}\beta_{0}}\ln\Big(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{f})}\Big)\,.

(147)

As mentioned already, this is not a new results since the scale dependence of the NRQCD long distance matrix elements is a well known fact, see for example Refs.
Bodwin:1994jh
;
Petrelli:1997ge
. Nevertheless it is instructive to reproduce these results here.

To perform the analogous analysis for the matching coefficients
C_{n}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{k}}}_{\perp};\mu,\nu)
in Eq. (
105
) we work in Fourier transform space where the RG equations simplify from convolutions to simple multiplications. We use the formulas collected in Appendix
B
. The renormalized matching coefficients are defined as follows

\tilde{C}_{n}(b)=Z_{C}^{nm}(b;\mu,\nu)\tilde{C}_{m}(b;\mu,\nu)\,.

(148)

Since
C_{2}
starts at order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
the renormalization matrix cannot be determined uniquely at this accuracy. From the perturbative calculations of the matching coefficients we have

\displaystyle Z_{C}(b;\mu,\nu)=Z_{S}(b;\mu,\nu)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}-\frac{4\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{3\pi m^{2}}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(149)

where

Z_{S}(b;\mu,\nu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{2\pi}\Big\{-\frac{4}{\eta}\Big[L_{b}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big]+\frac{2}{\epsilon}\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\ln\Big(\frac{\nu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]\Big\}+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{A}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,,

(150)

with

L_{b}=\ln\Big(\frac{b^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\Big)\;.

(151)

The corresponding anomalous dimension is given in terms of the renormalization matrix

\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu}^{S}\delta^{nk}+\tilde{\gamma}^{nk}_{C}\equiv-(Z_{C}^{nm}(b,\mu))^{-1}\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}Z_{C}^{mk}(b,\mu)\;.

(152)

The
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
contribution to the
Z_{S}
renormalization kernel multiplying the leading
{}^{3}P_{0}^{[1]}
shape function will result in
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
terms which we have not calculated yet. Therfore the renormalization matrix cannot uniquely be determined at this order and it is easier to determine the anomalous dimension by simply taking the derivative of the renormalized matching coefficients. Note that this means that the order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
contribution to the corresponding diagonal term (in this case the one associated with
{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{0}
) cannot be determined. We complete the missing element by consistency of factorization. To show that this is the correct completion through explicit calculation one needs to perform the NNLO calculation of the associated shape function. This gives

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}^{S}

\displaystyle=-(\gamma_{\mu}^{H}+2\gamma_{\mu,q}^{D})=-2\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\ln\Big(\frac{\nu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{\pi}\;,

\displaystyle\gamma_{C}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{3\pi m^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(153)

The solution of the RG equation for the matching coefficients is then given by

\tilde{C}^{n}(b;\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{U}_{S}(\mu,\mu_{0},\nu)\times\mathcal{U}_{C}^{nm}(\mu,\mu_{0})\tilde{C}^{m}(b;\mu_{0},\nu)\;,

(154)

where

\mathcal{U}_{C}^{T}(\mu,\mu_{0})=\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{O}}^{-1}(\mu,\mu_{0})=\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu_{0},\mu)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu_{0},\mu)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(155)

and
\mathcal{U}_{S}
is a function we give in the Appendix
B
. We now have all the ingredients to write the virtuality evolution of the total shape function

\tilde{S}^{\perp}(b;\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{U}_{S}(\mu,\mu_{0})\Big[\tilde{C}(b;\mu_{0},\nu)\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu_{0},\mu_{f})\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle^{(\mu_{f})}\Big]\,.

(156)

This is our result for the virtuality evolved shape function. We next consider the rapidity-RG evolution which is the same as in traditional TMD observables. The rapidity evolution of the shape function should match the evolution of the unsubtracted TMDFFs. Furthermore the rapidity scale dependence is isolated in the matching coefficients and thus we can obtain the corresponding anomalous dimension considering only the diagonal element of the renormalization matrix
Z_{S}
. The rapidity scale dependence of the shape function is described by:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\nu}\tilde{S}^{\perp}(b;\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\nu}^{S}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu,\nu)\times\tilde{S}^{\perp}(b;\mu,\nu)\;,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu}^{S}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)

\displaystyle=Z_{S}^{-1}\frac{d}{d\nu}Z_{S}\,,

(157)

where

\gamma_{\nu}^{S}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)=-2\gamma_{\nu,q}^{D}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)=-2\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}L_{b}\,.

(158)

The fully evolved and resummed shape function is then given in terms of the rapidity and virtuality evolution kernels,

\tilde{S}^{\perp}(b;\mu,\nu)=\mathcal{U}_{S}(\mu,\mu_{0},\nu)\mathcal{V}_{S}(\nu,\nu_{0},\mu_{0})\Big[\tilde{C}(b;\mu_{0},\nu_{0})\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu_{0},\mu_{f})\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle^{(\mu_{f})}\Big]\,,

(159)

where

\mathcal{V}_{S}(\nu,\nu_{0},\mu)=\exp\Big[\gamma_{\nu}^{S}({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}},\mu)\ln\Big(\frac{\nu}{\nu_{0}}\Big)\Big]\,.

(160)

Note that the rapidity and virtuality evolution are coupled and the order or path we wish to choose for the two-dimensional evolution changes the form of the evolution kernels. See Ref.
Scimemi:2018xaf
on a recent treatment of double scale evolution. The path of evolution we choose here is
(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})\to(\mu_{0},\nu)\to(\mu,\nu)
. In the RG and RRG evolution the initiating scales
\mu_{0}
and
\nu_{0}
are usually chosen to minimize large logarithms in the Fourier space.

In the small transverse momentum limit when
q_{\perp}\sim\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
the expression in the square brackets of Eq.(
159
) is sensitive to non perturbative effects and the matching coefficients
C_{n}
should be convolved with a model function,
f_{n}

\tilde{C}_{n}(b;\mu_{0},\nu_{0})\to\tilde{C}_{n}(b;\mu_{0},\nu_{0})\times f_{n}(b)\;.

(161)

Since the two shape functions in this process mix under RG evolution we assign the same model function to the matching coefficient for both shape functions. Thus at the order we are working (NLL) only one model function is introduced.

4.5
Resummed cross section and numerics

Here we combine the results of the past section to obtain the NLL resummed cross section for the annihilation of
\chi_{J}
to light quarks which further decay into hadrons.
As was already discussed in the past section, we consider both rapidity and virtuality renormalization group equations. In order to resum all logarithms up to NLL, the various elements of factorization need to be evaluated at the same virtuality and rapidity scales. We choose the final scales of evolution to be
\mu=\mu_{b}=2\,\textrm{Exp}(-\gamma_{E})/b
and
\nu=M_{\chi}
, in impact parameter space. Starting from the factorization theorem and using the results of Appendix
B
we have

\frac{1}{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{d\Gamma^{\chi_{J}}}{d^{2}q_{\perp}dz_{1}dz_{2}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}bdb\;J_{0}(bq_{\perp})\;\mathcal{U}_{H}(\mu_{b},M_{\chi})\mathcal{V}_{S}(M_{\chi},\mu_{b},\mu_{b})D_{q/H_{1}}(z_{1};\mu_{b},M_{\chi})D_{\bar{q}/H_{2}}(z_{2};\mu_{b},M_{\chi})\\
f_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}(b)\times\Big[1+\frac{\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle}{\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle}\omega_{{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}}(\mu_{b},M_{\chi})\Big]\,.

(162)

For the discussion that follows the model function
f_{{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}}
is not implemented.
The perturbative evolution kernels
\mathcal{U}_{H}
and
\mathcal{V}_{S}
are constructed as described in Appendix
B
. The non-perturbative part of the rapidity evolution is usually implemented through a model in the rapidity anomalous dimension:

\gamma_{\nu}^{S}(b,\mu)\to\gamma_{\nu}^{S}(b,\mu)+g_{K}(b)\,.

(163)

The model function
g_{K}(b)
is chosen such that it vanishes in the small
b
limit so that we recover the perturbative prediction. Also from the operator product expansion we can show that at leading order
g_{K}(b)
is quadratic in
b
. The model we use here was introduced in Ref.
COLLINS1985199
and subsequently used in various phenomenological studies,

g_{K}(b)=-g_{2}b^{2}\,.

(164)

The parameter
g_{2}
is a non-perturbative parameter that needs to be extracted from the experimental data but is universal among various processes. The effect of this implementation is to widen and shift the differential spectrum to larger values, see the plot on the left-hand-side of Fig.
8
.

The rapidity anomalous dimension is not the only non-perturbative parameter needed to obtain a prediction. The LDMEs also need to be extracted and are also considered universal parameters. It is important to notice that for the shape of the spectrum only the ratio

\rho=\langle{}^{3}S^{[8]}_{1}\rangle m^{2}/\langle{}^{3}P^{[1]}_{J}\rangle

(165)

is needed. The value of this ratio also modifies the shape of the distribution. The smaller the ratio we use the wider the distribution becomes, see the right-hand-side of Fig.
8
.

Differential cross section for the process
\chi_{b0}\to\pi^{+}+\pi^{-}+X
at NLL. We demonstrate the effect of varying the various non-perturbative parameters such as the LDMEs and the rapidity anomalous dimension.

To demonstrate the numerical impact of the non-perturbative parameters we show the differential cross section for various values of these parameters in Fig.
8
. For these plot we choose
H_{1,2}=\pi^{+/-}
with
z_{1}=z_{2}=0.6
. The collinear fragmentation functions at NLO are taken from Ref.
Hirai:2007cx
. It is also important to note that the scale
\mu_{b}
becomes non-perturbative in the region of integration and eventually the integrand diverges as we reach the Landau pole. For this reason we implement an upper cutoff in the

integral in Eq.(

) at
b_{\text{max}}=5\;\text{GeV}^{-1}
. This value is determined by searching where the integral in Eq. (

This suggests that for well constrained LDMEs one can use quarkonium production or decays to access non-perturbative aspects of TMD distributions.

5
Summary and outlook

The goal of this paper is to study factorization and resummation for transverse momentum dependent observables involving quarkonium production or decay process. In the first part of the paper we give a diagrammatic analysis involving an arbitrary number of soft gluon emissions from a heavy quark pair. We derived the general form of the operators involving soft Wilson Lines for both S- and P-wave production and decay (see Eqs.
30
and
41
). We showed how RPI constrains the form of these operators and explains their structure. This symmetry constrains the matching coefficients of these operators to be the same to all orders in perturbation theory. Using this analysis we obtained the operators and the associated matching coefficients contributing at NLL for the process
q\bar{q}\to Q\bar{Q}[n]
. Finally we showed that the operators are invariant under collinear, soft, and ultra-soft gauge transformation.

As an application of our methods, we derived the factorization and resummation for the process
\chi_{J}\to q\bar{q}
, followed by fragmentation to identified hadrons
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. The cross section studied is differential in the relative transverse momentum of the two hadrons. This particular process demonstrates some new features one encounters in the studies of quarkonium with observables that are transverse momentum dependent. We showed that a new TMD element appears in the place of soft functions. The new functions which we refer to as quarkonium shape functions encode all the information about soft radiation from light (collinear) partons but as well as from the heavy quark pair (see also Ref.
Echevarria:2019ynx
). The contribution from the heavy quarks can be non-trivial even in the case of color singlet channels. Mixing between different production mechanisms can be generated by RG evolution. We derive the mixed renormalization group equations satisfied by the quarkonium shape functions for this particular example and solve those up to NLL accuracy.

At the end of this paper we construct the NLL resummed cross section. An important result of our analysis is that we find that the differential cross section is sensitive to both the values of the LDMEs and the non-perturbative model of the rapidity anomalous dimension. This demonstrates that the LDMEs need to be well-constrained in order to extract parameters related to the TMDs. This has important implications for phenomenological studies of transverse momentum dependent observables involving quarkonium. Our analysis does not include gluon decay channel but it would be straight forward to include them and this does not change this qualitative observation.

Extending our analysis to spin-triplet S-wave quarkonia such as
\Upsilon(1S)
and
J/\psi
involves one more non-trivial step. For example the process
\Upsilon(1\text{S})\;\text{or}\;J/\psi\to gg+X
gets significant contribution from the color singlet mechanism
{}^{3}S^{[1]}_{1}
. Although this channel does not contribute at leading power in
\lambda
it is enhanced by
\text{v}^{-4}
compared to the color octet and
\lambda
-leading mechanisms:
{}^{1}S^{[8]}_{0}
and
{}^{3}P^{[8]}_{0,2}
. Therefore, in order to obtain reasonable and consistent results resummation of sub-leading logarithms from the color singlet channel is necessary.

Expanding this analysis to the deep inelastic scattering process which is relevant for for the proposed electron-ion collider (EIC) is of major importance
Bacchetta:2018ivt
. The partonic hard scattering process
g+\gamma^{*}\to Q\bar{Q}[n]+X
which is the dominant mechanism for quarkonium production at
-q^{2}\gtrsim M_{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}
can give access to gluon TMDs. As we have shown in this paper quarkonium shape functions are important for this process and need to be included in future analyses. Resummation of large logarithms similar to what we have performed in this paper will be important as well. We will study this issue in future work.

Appendix A
Definitions, notation, and formulae

We will work in the chiral representation of Dirac matrices:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{\mu}\\
\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\text{where}\;\;\;\sigma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=(1,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}})\;,\;\;\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}=(1,-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}})\,.

(166)

The Dirac spinors in this representation then take the form:

\displaystyle u(p)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\;\xi\\
\sqrt{p\cdot\bar{\sigma}}\;\xi\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle v(p)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\;\eta\\
-\sqrt{p\cdot\bar{\sigma}}\;\eta\end{pmatrix}\,.

(167)

Using
p_{Q/\bar{Q}}=(P+/-q)/2
, the
q
and
\lambda
expansion of the spinors is given by

\displaystyle u(p_{Q})

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\gamma}}}}{4m}+\cdots\Big)u^{(0)}(1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda))\;,

\displaystyle v(p_{\bar{Q}})=\Big(1-\frac{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{q}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\gamma}}}}{4m}+\cdots\Big)v^{(0)}(1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda))\,,

(168)

where the ellipsis denotes terms of order
\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
or higher. The normalized rest frame spinors
u^{(0)}
and

Appendix B
Evolution and resummation

In this appendix we discuss both virtuality and rapidity renormalization group equations and the solutions of those equations. All factorization elements (hard, soft, soft-collinear, collinear-soft, and jet) satisfy renormalization group equations, but only transverse momentum dependent quantities have rapidity RGEs.

B.1
Renormalization group evolution

The RGEs we consider have the following form

\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}F(\mu)=\gamma^{F}_{\mu}(\mu,\alpha_{s})F(\mu)=\Big[\Gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{S}]\ln\Big(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{F}}\Big)+\Delta\gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{S}]\Big]F(\mu),

(179)

where
\gamma_{\mu}^{F}
is the virtuality anomalous dimension. We refer to the first term in the square brackets as the cusp part since
\Gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{s}]
is proportional to the cusp anomalous dimension,
\Gamma_{\text{cusp}}
, and the second term,
\Delta\gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{S}]
, as the non-cusp part. Both the cusp and the non-cusp terms have an expansion in the strong coupling. For the cusp term we have

\Gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{s}]=(\Gamma_{F}^{0}/\Gamma_{\text{cusp}}^{0})\Gamma_{\text{cusp}}=(\Gamma_{F}^{0}/\Gamma_{\text{cusp}}^{0})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{1+n}\Gamma_{\text{cusp}}^{n},

(180)

and similarly the non-cusp part is given by

\Delta\gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha_{S}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{1+n}\gamma_{F}^{n}\,.

(181)

The solution to the RGE in Eq.(
179
) is

\displaystyle F(\mu)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})F(\mu_{0})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})=\exp\left(K_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})\right)\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{m_{F}}\right)^{2\;\omega_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})},

(182)

with

\displaystyle K_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=2\int_{\alpha(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha(\mu)}\frac{d\alpha}{\beta[\alpha]}\Gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha]\int_{\alpha(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha}\frac{d\alpha^{\prime}}{\beta[\alpha^{\prime}]}+\int_{\alpha(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha(\mu)}\frac{d\alpha}{\beta[\alpha]}\Delta\gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha],

(183)

\displaystyle\omega_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha(\mu)}\frac{d\alpha}{\beta[\alpha]}\Gamma^{F}_{\mu}[\alpha].

(184)

Since in this work we are interested only in the NLL and
\textrm{NLL}^{\prime}
result we keep only the first two terms in the perturbative expansion of the cusp part (i.e.,
\Gamma_{0}^{F}
,
\Gamma^{0}_{\text{cusp}}
, and
\Gamma^{1}_{\text{cusp}}
) and only the first term form the non-cusp part (
\gamma_{F}^{0}
). Performing this expansion we get

\displaystyle K_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{F}^{0}}{2\beta_{0}}\ln r-\frac{2\pi\Gamma_{F}^{0}}{(\beta_{0})^{2}}\Big[\frac{r-1-r\ln r}{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}+\left(\frac{\Gamma^{1}_{\text{cusp}}}{\Gamma^{0}_{\text{cusp}}}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\frac{1-r+\ln r}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{8\pi\beta_{0}}\ln^{2}r\Big],

(185)

\displaystyle\omega_{F}(\mu,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma_{F}^{0}}{2\beta_{0}}\Big[\ln r+\left(\frac{\Gamma^{1}_{\text{cusp}}}{\Gamma^{0}_{\text{cusp}}}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}}\right)\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}{4\pi}(r-1)\Big],

(186)

where
r=\alpha(\mu)/\alpha(\mu_{0})
and
\beta_{n}
are the coefficients of the QCD
\beta

\beta[\alpha_{s}]=\mu\frac{d\alpha_{s}}{d\mu}=-2\alpha_{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{1+n}\beta_{n}\;.

(187)

A geometric framework for discrete time port-Hamiltonian systems
Karim Cherifi, Hannes Gernandt, Dorothea Hinsen, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
Port-Hamiltonian systems provide an energy-based formulation with a model class that is closed under structure preserving interconnection. For continuous-time systems these interconnections are constructed by geometric objects called Dirac structures. In this paper, we derive this geometric formulation and the interconnection properties for scattering passive discrete-time port-Hamiltonian systems.



1
Introduction

For some major classes of continuous-time (linear) control systems, in particular port-Hamiltonion (pH) systems, it is well-established that staying close to the underlying physics requires a general geometric framework. This has lead to the definition of continuous-time port-Hamiltonian systems via Dirac, Lagrange and monotone structures, see e.g.
[
6
,
12
,
18
,
15
,
16
]
. In this paper this framework will be extended to discrete-time descriptor systems
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
9
]
of the form

\displaystyle Ex_{k+1}

\displaystyle=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k},\quad y_{k}=Cx_{k}+Du_{k},\quad k\geq 0,\quad x_{0}=x^{0},

(1)

with sequences of vectors
x_{k}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
,
u_{k},y_{k}\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots
,
E,A\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}
,
C\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
. Here
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, and we allow a possibly singular
E
.
Discrete-time descriptor systems of the form (
1
) are called
scattering passive
if there exists a storage function
V:\mathbb{K}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying
V(0)=0
and the
dissipation inequality

V(Ex_{k+1})-V(Ex_{k})\leq\|u_{k}\|^{2}-\|y_{k}\|^{2}\quad\text{for all $k\geq 0$}

(2)

for all combinations of
u_{k}\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
for which a solution of (
1
) exists.

In
[
4
]
a definition of discrete-time scattering pH descriptor systems was presented for the case that the system is
causal
, i.e. the solution
x_{k}
at index
k
does not depend on future inputs. (This has been characterized in
[
4
]
by the Kronecker index of the pair
(E,A)
being at most one). In this case the system can be transformed to a reduced standard discrete-time system

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}\tilde{x}_{k}+\mathcal{B}u_{k},\quad\tilde{y}_{k}=\mathcal{C}\tilde{x}_{k}+\mathcal{D}u_{k},\quad k\geq 0,\quad\tilde{x}_{0}=\tilde{x}^{0},

(3)

with sequences of vectors
\tilde{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{K}^{r}
,
u_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots
,
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{r\times r}
,
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{r\times m}
,
\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times r}
,
together with an algebraic equation that uniquely specifies the remaining
n-r
components of
x_{k}
. A causal system of the form (
1
) is called
discrete-time scattering pH system
if for the reduced standard system (
3
) (denoted by
(I_{r},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
), there exists a Hermitian matrix
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{H}>0
such that for the norm weighted with
\textrm{diag}(\mathcal{X},I_{m})
the inequality

\displaystyle\Big\|\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{A}&\mathcal{B}\\
\mathcal{C}&\mathcal{D}\end{bmatrix}\Big\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 1

(4)

holds.
In (
4
) the weight
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
defines the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
of the reduced system according to
\mathcal{H}(\tilde{x}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{x}^{H}\mathcal{X}\tilde{x}
, where
\tilde{x}^{H}
denotes the complex conjugate transpose of
\tilde{x}
.

In this paper, we extend and define the geometric system formulation to scattering passive discrete-time pH systems of the form (
3
). Once this is defined, we discuss the interconnection of multiple discrete-time pH systems in analogy to the continuous-time case, where it is known that the interconnection of multiple pH systems results again in a pH system.
As special case we also discuss norm preserving interconnections.

It is shown in
[
10
,
11
]
that interconnections using Dirac structures not only preserve the passivity of the interconnected system but also the pH system structure. With these results in mind, it is interesting to analyze whether this property holds for the
scattering pH formulation of discrete-time systems using contractive interconnections.
Here the difficulty is that we have considered only scattering pH formulations of causal descriptor systems. However, even the interconnection of two standard state-space discrete-time systems may lead to a non-causal discrete-time descriptor system. We therefore restrict ourselves to the case that in the interconnection causality is preserved.

2
Preliminaries

The geometric formulation of discrete-time pH systems will be based on using subspaces
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
with the following additional structural properties.

Definition 2.1
.

A subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
is called
contractive
if
\|w\|\leq\|v\|
holds for all
(v,w)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}
is called
maximally contractive
if
\mathcal{M}
is not a proper subspace of a contractive subspace of
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
. A subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
is called
norm preserving
if
\|v\|=\|w\|
holds for all
(v,w)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}
is called
maximally norm preserving
if
\mathcal{M}
is not a proper subspace of a norm preserving subspace of
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
. A subspace
\mathcal{M}=\ima\left[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{1}^{H}\\
M_{2}^{H}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
,
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is called
monotone
if

\displaystyle M_{2}M_{1}^{H}+M_{1}M_{2}^{H}\geq 0,

(5)

and
maximally monotone
if
\mathcal{M}
is not a proper subspace of a monotone subspace of
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Recently, monotone subspaces were used in the continuous-time pH context
[
6
,
12
]
, as an extension of results given in
[
17
]
, where subspaces
\mathcal{M}
which satisfy (
5
) with equality were considered. In this special case,
\mathcal{M}
is called a
Dirac structure
. Closely related to this are

Lagrangian subspaces

\mathcal{L}=\ima\left[\begin{smallmatrix}L_{1}\\
L_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, for some
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
which satisfy
L_{2}^{H}L_{1}=L_{1}^{H}L_{2}
.

In the following, we will focus on contractive subspaces to describe discrete-time pH systems which can be seen as a counterpart of monotone structures for the continuous-time case. More precisely, the relation between monotone and contractive subspaces is established by a subspace variant of the well-known Cayley transformation, see also
[
7
]
.

For a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
and for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, the
Cayley transform
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M}):=\{(v,w)\penalty\ |\penalty\ (\alpha(v+w),-\beta(v-w))\in\mathcal{M}\}.

With this, we can give the following characterization of contractive and monotone subspaces.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\ima\begin{bmatrix}M_{1}^{H}\\
M_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}
for some
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times r}
be contractive. Then the following assertions hold.

(a)

\mathcal{M}
is contractive if and only if
M_{2}M_{2}^{H}-M_{1}M_{1}^{H}\leq 0
.

(b)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
one has
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M})=\ima\begin{bmatrix}\beta M_{1}^{H}+\alpha M_{2}^{H}\\
\beta M_{1}^{H}-\alpha M_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}
.

(c)

Let
\alpha\overline{\beta}>0
. If
\mathcal{M}
is monotone then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M})
is contractive. Conversely, if
\mathcal{M}
is contractive and if
\tfrac{|\alpha|}{|\beta|}\leq 1
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M})
is monotone.

(d)

\mathcal{M}
is maximally contractive if and only if
\dim\mathcal{M}=n
.

Proof.

(a)

For all
(v,w)\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists some
z\in\mathbb{K}^{r}
such that
(v,w)=(M_{1}^{H}z,M_{2}^{H}z)
holds. Hence,
\mathcal{M}
is contractive if and only if for all
z\in\mathbb{K}^{r}
the inequality

z^{H}M_{2}M_{2}^{H}z=\|M_{2}^{H}z\|^{2}=\|w\|^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}=\|M_{1}^{H}z\|^{2}=z^{H}M_{1}M_{1}^{H}z

holds.

(b)

By definition, we have
(v,w)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M})
if and only if

\alpha(v+w)=M_{1}^{H}z,\quad-\beta(v-w)=M_{2}^{H}z

holds for some
z\in\mathbb{K}^{r}
. Hence

v=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{-1}M_{1}^{H}+\beta^{-1}M_{2}^{H})z,\quad w=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{-1}M_{1}^{H}-\beta^{-1}M_{2}^{H})z

and after multiplication with
2\alpha\beta
we obtain the formula in (b).

(c)

If
\mathcal{M}
is monotone, then
\alpha\overline{\beta}>0
yields

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\beta M_{1}^{H}-\alpha M_{2}^{H})^{H}(\beta M_{1}^{H}-\alpha M_{2}^{H})-(\beta M_{1}^{H}+\alpha M_{2}^{H})^{H}(\beta M_{1}^{H}+\alpha M_{2}^{H})

\displaystyle=-\overline{\beta}\alpha M_{1}M_{2}^{H}-\overline{\alpha}\beta M_{2}M_{1}^{H}-\overline{\beta}\alpha M_{1}M_{2}^{H}-\overline{\alpha}\beta M_{2}M_{1}^{H}

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\overline{\beta}(M_{1}M_{2}^{H}+M_{2}M_{1}^{H})

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Hence, (a) and (b) imply that
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{M})
is contractive. Conversely, if
\mathcal{M}
is contractive, then
\alpha\overline{\beta}>0
and
\tfrac{|\alpha|}{|\beta|}\leq 1
imply

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\beta M_{1}^{H}+\alpha M_{2}^{H})^{H}(\beta M_{1}^{H}-\alpha M_{2}^{H})+(\beta M_{1}^{H}-\alpha M_{2}^{H})^{H}(\beta M_{1}^{H}+\alpha M_{2}^{H})

\displaystyle=2|\beta|^{2}(M_{1}M_{1}^{H}-\tfrac{|\alpha|^{2}}{|\beta|^{2}}M_{2}M_{2}^{H})

\displaystyle\geq 2|\beta|^{2}(1-\tfrac{|\alpha|^{2}}{|\beta|^{2}})M_{2}M_{2}^{H}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(d)

Observe that the image representation of the Cayley transformed relation is given by

\displaystyle\beta\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}M_{1}^{H}+M_{2}^{H}\\
\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}M_{1}^{H}-M_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}I_{n}&I_{n}\\
\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}I_{n}&-I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}M_{1}^{H}\\
M_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}.

(6)

Since the block matrix on the right-hand side of (
6
) is invertible, the dimension of the subspace is preserved under the Cayley transformation. Furthermore, we know from (c) that the Cayley transform relates monotone and contractive subspaces. It is also known that a monotone subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}
is maximal, i.e. it is not contained in a monotone subspace of larger dimension, if and only if
\dim\mathcal{M}=n
holds, see
[
6
, Lemma 3.6]
. Hence, maximally contractive subspaces are mapped to maximally monotone subspaces via the Cayley transformation, where the latter subspaces are
n
-dimensional. Hence assertion (d) follows.

∎

In this section we have introduced the concept of contractive and monotone subspaces and their coordinate representations. In the next section we introduce the geometric formulation of discrete-time port-Hamiltonian systems.

3
Geometric formulation of discrete-time port-Hamiltonian systems

In this section, we introduce a geometric coordinate-free formulation of discrete-time port-Hamiltonian systems. As a motivation, we consider first the special case of pH systems without input and output variables in continuous time. The case of continuous-time
dissipative Hamiltonian (dH) descriptor systems

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}Ex(t)=(J-R)Qx(t),\quad J=-J^{H},\quad R=R^{H}\geq 0,\quad Q^{H}E=E^{H}Q

(7)

was investigated in
[
8
]
. Based on
[
17
]
, it was shown in
[
6
]
that a geometric formulation of (
7
) is given by

\displaystyle(z(t),-\dot{z}(t))\in\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{L}=\{(z,w)\penalty\ |\penalty\ (w,v)\in\mathcal{M},\penalty\ (z,v)\in\mathcal{L}\},

(8)

where
z:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{n}
is a continuously differentiable function,
\mathcal{M}
is a monotone subspace and
\mathcal{L}
is a Lagrange subspace.

An immediate approach to obtain a discrete-time formulation is to integrate in (
8
) using the trapezoidal rule.
This leads to the following
geometric formulation of a discrete-time scattering dH system

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h}{2}(z_{k}+z_{k+1}),-(z_{k+1}-z_{k})\right)\in\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{L}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(z_{k},z_{k+1})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\frac{h}{2},1}(\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{L}).

(9)

If we consider for simplicity
\mathcal{L}=\{(x,x)\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}\}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{L}
is a monotone subspace, and, invoking Proposition
2.2
, we find that the subspace
{\mathcal{C}}_{\frac{h}{2},1}(\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{L})
in (
9
) is contractive.

After this motivation, we give a geometric definition based on the analogy to continuous-time pH descriptor systems in
[
18
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A geometric representation of a pH system with state space
\mathbb{K}^{n}
and external dimension
m
is given by a triple
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R})
consisting of

•

a maximal norm-preserving subspace
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n+r+m}\times\mathbb{K}^{n+r+m}
,

•

a maximal contractive subspace
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{r}\times\mathbb{K}^{r}
.

By a solution of the discrete-time pH system
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{C})
we understand an input-state-output trajectory
((u_{k})_{k\geq 0},(x_{k})_{k\geq 0},(y_{k})_{k\geq 0})
for which there exist sequences
(f_{k,R})_{k\geq 0}
and
(e_{k,R})_{k\geq 0}
, and such that for all
k\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle(x_{k+1},f_{k,R},y_{k},x_{k},e_{k,R},u_{k})\in\mathcal{N},\quad(f_{k,R},e_{k,R})\in\mathcal{C}.

(10)

The solutions of a geometric discrete-time pH system satisfy the following power balance equation

\|x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\|f_{k,R}\|^{2}+\|y_{k}\|^{2}=\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\|e_{k,R}\|^{2}+\|u_{k}\|^{2},\quad k\geq 0.

Rearranging terms and employing the contractivity implies that

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|x_{k}\|^{2}\leq\|u_{k}\|^{2}-\|y_{k}\|^{2},\quad k\geq 0,

(11)

which shows that the geometric definition of discrete-time scattering pH systems leads to scattering passive systems.

The geometric definition introduced in
[
18
]
also includes an additional Lagrange structure to generalize the concept of the Hamiltonian. An extension of Definition
3.1
in this direction is rather straight forward, but left for future work. Here one introduces Lagrangian effort variables
e_{L,k}
which must fulfill
(x_{k+1},e_{k,L})\in\mathcal{C}_{\frac{h}{2},1}(\mathcal{L})
for some
h>0
and one has to replace
x_{k}
in the left hand side expression in (
10
) by
e_{k,L}
.

4
Contractive interconnection of scattering passive systems

It is well known that the loss-less interconnection of continuous-time pH systems using Dirac subspaces preserves the pH system structure
[
10
,
11
]
. In this section, we present an analogous interconnection result for discrete-time pH systems. A related approach describing the interconnection of Dirac subspaces of scattering passive systems was given in
[
3
]
and it is based on the use of the
Redheffer star product
and wave variable representations of effort and flow variables.

To describe the system interconnection, we restrict ourselves to the interconnection of two scattering passive systems given by

\displaystyle E_{1}x_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle=A_{1}x_{1,k}+B_{1}u_{1,k},

\displaystyle y_{1,k}

\displaystyle=C_{1}x_{1,k}+D_{1}u_{1,k},

(12)

\displaystyle E_{2}x_{2,k+1}

\displaystyle=A_{2}x_{2,k}+B_{2}u_{2,k}

\displaystyle y_{2,k}

\displaystyle=C_{2}x_{2,k}+D_{2}u_{2,k}

(13)

for coefficient matrices of appropriate sizes and where the inputs and outputs of the systems are partitioned as

u_{i,k}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{i,k}^{1}\\
u_{i,k}^{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad y_{i,k}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{i,k}^{1}\\
y_{i,k}^{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad B_{i}=[B_{i}^{1},B_{i}^{2}],\quad C_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}^{1}\\
C_{i}^{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad D_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{i}^{11}&D_{i}^{12}\\
D_{i}^{21}&D_{i}^{22}\end{bmatrix},\quad i=1,2,

where the components
u_{i,k}^{1}
and
y_{i,k}^{1}
are available for coupling.
In the following we consider a contractive interconnection
\mathcal{M}
of the scattering passive systems (
12
) and (
13
) given by

\displaystyle(u_{1,k}^{1},u_{2,k}^{1},y_{1,k}^{1},y_{2,k}^{1})\in\mathcal{M},\quad\left\|\begin{bmatrix}y_{1,k}^{1}\\
y_{2,k}^{1}\end{bmatrix}\right\|\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}u_{1,k}^{1}\\
u_{2,k}^{1}\end{bmatrix}\right\|,\quad k\geq 0.

(14)

A special case of such an interconnection (
14
) is given by

\displaystyle y_{1,k}^{1}=u_{2,k}^{1},\quad u_{1,k}^{1}=y_{2,k}^{1}

(15)

and was used in
[
3
]
for the interconnection of Dirac structures. It is closely related to the Redheffer star product which was studied in
[
13
,
14
]
.

Then,we have the following main result on structure preserving interconnection. To show that the scattering pH structure is preserved, we restrict to the case
E_{1}=E_{2}=I_{n}
because of spatial limitations. However, the result can be generalized for descriptor systems having index at most one.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider scattering passive systems (
12
) and (
13
), i.e. (
2
) holds, together with an interconnection via (
14
). Then the following holds:

(a)

The interconnected system given by (
12
), (
13
) and (
14
) is scattering passive.

(b)

If the systems (
12
) and (

) are discrete time scattering pH, i.e. (
4
) holds, and let
E_{1}=E_{2}=Id
and
I-D_{1}^{11}D_{2}^{11}
be invertible, then the interconnected system (
15
) is equivalent to a discrete-time pH system.

(c)

Let (
12
) and (
13
) be scattering pH, i.e. (
4
) holds for some positive definite
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}>0
, and fulfill
E_{1}=E_{2}=Id
. If
u_{i,k}=u_{i,k}^{1}
and
y_{i,k}=y_{i,k}^{1}
holds for
i=1,2
and all

, then the interconnection (
15
) leads to a contractive closed loop system of the form

\begin{bmatrix}x_{1,k+1}\\
x_{2,k+1}\end{bmatrix}=\hat{A}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1,k}\\
x_{2,k}\end{bmatrix},\quad\|\hat{A}z\|_{\hat{\mathcal{X}}}\leq\|z\|_{\hat{\mathcal{X}}},\quad\quad\text{where}\quad\|z\|_{\hat{\mathcal{X}}}:=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{X}_{1}&0\\
0&\mathcal{X}_{2}\end{bmatrix}z\right\|.

Proof.

If we consider a kernel representation
\mathcal{M}=\ker[M_{1}^{1},M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{1},M_{2}^{2}]
for some matrices of appropriate sizes, then the interconnected system can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&0&B_{1}^{1}&0&0&0\\
0&A_{2}&0&B_{2}^{1}&0&0\\
C_{1}^{1}&0&D_{1}^{11}&0&-I&0\\
0&C_{2}^{1}&0&D_{2}^{11}&0&-I\\
0&0&M_{1}^{1}&M_{1}^{2}&M_{2}^{1}&M_{2}^{2}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{1}^{2}&0\\
0&B_{2}^{2}\\
D_{1}^{12}&0\\
0&D_{2}^{12}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1,k}\\
x_{2,k}\\
u_{1,k}^{1}\\
u_{2,k}^{1}\\
y_{1,k}^{1}\\
y_{2,k}^{1}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}&0&0\\
0&E_{2}&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C_{1}^{2}&0&D_{1}^{21}&0&0&0\\
0&C_{2}^{2}&0&D_{2}^{21}&0&0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}^{22}&0\\
0&D_{2}^{22}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1,k}^{2}\\
u_{2,k}^{2}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}y_{1,k}^{2}\\
y_{2,k}^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(16)

Since the systems (

and

\displaystyle V_{i}(E_{i}x_{i,k+1})-V_{i}(E_{i}x_{i,k})\leq\|u_{i,k}\|^{2}-\|y_{i,k}\|^{2},\quad i=1,2.

Furthermore, by the contractive interconnection (

\|y_{1,k}^{1}\|^{2}+\|y_{2,k}^{1}\|^{2}=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}y_{1,k}^{1}\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}_{k+1})-\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}_{k})\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\|u_{i,k}^{j}\|^{2}-\|y_{i,k}^{j}\|^{2}\leq\|u_{1,k}^{2}\|^{2}+\|u_{2,k}^{2}\|^{2}-\|y_{1,k}^{2}\|^{2}-\|y_{2,k}^{2}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{u}_{k}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|^{2},

which is the dissipation inequality for the interconnected system with respect to the scattering supply rate and the quadratic storage function
\mathbf{V}
. Hence, the interconnected system is scattering passive, which proves (a).

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1,k}\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{1,k}^{2}\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C_{1}^{2}&0&D_{1}^{21}&0\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}D_{1}^{22}&0\\

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a geometric approach to scattering passive discrete time pH systems which is based on contractive subspaces. It also discussed the interconnection of discrete time pH systems and we showed that the interconnection of two pH systems results in another pH system. We also had a closer look at the Redheffer interconnection and showed that the interconnection in this case also results in a pH system.

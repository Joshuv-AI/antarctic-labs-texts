On spectral distribution of sample covariance matrices from large dimensional and large k k -fold tensor products
Benoît Collins, Jianfeng Yao, Wangjun Yuan

Abstract
We study the eigenvalue distributions for sums of independent rank-one k k -fold tensor products of large n n -dimensional vectors. Previous results in the literature assume that k = o ⁡ ( n ) k=o(n) and show that the eigenvalue distributions converge to the celebrated Marčenko-Pastur law under appropriate moment conditions on the base vectors. In this paper, motivated by quantum information theory, we study the regime where k k grows faster, namely k = O ⁡ ( n ) k=O(n) . We show that the moment sequences of the eigenvalue distributions have a limit, which is different from the Marčenko-Pastur law. As a byproduct, we show that the Marčenko-Pastur law limit holds if and only if k = o ⁡ ( n ) k=o(n) for this tensor model. The approach is based on the method of moments.

Introduction

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
be a family of i.i.d. centered complex random variables with unit variance. Denote
\mathbf{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

\{\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{(l)}:1\leq\alpha\leq m,1\leq l\leq k\}
be a family of i.i.d. copies of

, and let

for

. Let
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots\}
be a sequence of real numbers, and
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
be a

matrix. Consider the

Hermitian matrix

\displaystyle M_{n,k,m}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\tau_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}^{*}.

(1.1)

Therefore, each

-dimensional vector

is a

-fold tensor product of
n
-dimensional i.i.d. vectors, and

is a sum of

independent rank-
1
Hermitian matrices of dimension

.

The simplest case of

and
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
was studied in the seminal paper
[
8
]
where the celebrated Marčenko-Pastur law was derived under appropriate moment conditions on the entries of the base vector
\mathbf{y}
and the limiting scheme where
n\to\infty
and
m/n\to c>0
. Many subsequent improvements on the Marčenko-Pastur law appeared in the literature, including
[
10
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
.
These papers were able to deal with the model (
1.1
) with general sequence
\{\tau_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}
.
Notably, the latter paper extended the law to a broad family of
\mathbf{y}
-vectors, called
good vectors
, that includes the current setting with i.i.d. components.
Later, for the setting
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
, the necessary and sufficient conditions on
Y_{1}
that the Marčenko-Pastur law serves as the limiting spectral distribution (LSD) of
M_{n,1,m}
were carried out in
[
11
]
.

Recently,
[
7
]
considered the
k
-fold tensor model
M_{n,k,m}
and established a LSD for its
n^{k}
real-valued eigenvalues.
For the special case
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
, the LSD is exactly the Marčenko-Pastur law.
A central limit theorem (CLT) is also established for a class of linear spectral statistics following the approach of
[
6
]
.
The main setup
[
7
]
is that the tensor product number

must be small enough compared to the space dimension

.
Precisely,
k/n\to 0
is required for the validity of the LSD while
k\equiv 2
is required when

for the validity of the CLT.
It is natural to consider the setting where

is large.
Noting that large

means more dependence among the entries of the vector
Y_{1}
, it is expected that the LSD of
M_{n,k,m}
does not obey the Marčenko-Pastur law when
k
is large enough.
However, the martingale moment bound employed
[
7
]
is less useful when
k
is large, and the method cannot be directly extended to the case
k=O(n)
.
The present paper deals with the case
k=O(n)
for the model (
1.1
) and shows that the empirical spectral distribution (ESD) of
M_{n,k,m}
with
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
converges to the Marčenko-Pastur law if and only if
k=o(n)
.
Therefore, the matrix
M_{n,k,m}
with
k=O(n)
can be seen as a new example of
bad vectors
, for which the necessary and sufficient condition in
[
11
]
does not hold.

Another motivation for studying the model (
1.1
) is from quantum information theory.
In
[
1
]
, the model
M_{n,k,m}
was introduced as a quantum analog of the classical probability problem of allocating
r
balls into
s
bins.
The random vector
Y_{1}
was interpreted as random product states in
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes k}
.
When
k
is fixed and
m=cn^{k}
for some
c>0
,
[
1
]
established the convergence in expectation of the normalized trace of moments
n^{-k}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}
, which coincide with the corresponding moments of the Marčenko-Pastur law.
In quantum physics and quantum information theory, it is natural to investigate the behavior of a large number of quantum states.
In
[
12
]
, the quantum entanglement of structured random states was characterized, and the spectral density of the reduced state was studied when the number of the quantum states
k
is large.
In
[
4
]
, the asymptotic behavior of the average entropy of entanglement for elements of an ensemble of random states associated with a graph was studied when the dimension of the quantum subsystem is large.

This paper considers the scenario where
k
grows to infinity quickly. Namely, we assume that there exist constants
c,d\in(0,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{k}{n}\to d,\quad\dfrac{m}{n^{k}}\to c.

(1.2)

Let us add one more word of motivation for the choice of regime
k=O(n)
. In hindsight, this is a natural threshold in our quest for limiting theorems, and from the point of view of probability theory, our result extends existing results.
However, the need for large

is real in Quantum Information Theory. Actually, a typical real world scenario would be
n
fixed (the dimension of the system and

would go to infinity – the system can be used many times, i.e., one works on regularized quantities.
In practice, we do not know how to fix

Our approach is based on the method of moments. Under appropriate moment conditions on the base variable
\xi_{1}
and the sequence of coefficients

, we derive the limits for the spectral moments of

under the limiting scheme (

). A striking fact from our result is that contrary to all the previous results,

]
, the limiting spectral moments found here involve the

th moment of the base variable

.

2
Almost sure convergence of spectral moments

For fixed
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
,
for each value
t
appearing in
\alpha
,
we count its frequency by

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)=\left|\{j\in[p]:\alpha_{j}=t\}\right|.

(2.1)

The main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
d>0
and the following moment conditions hold.

1.

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
p
-th moment
m_{p}=E[|\xi_{1}|^{p}]<\infty
.

2.

For all
q\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{j}^{q}\to m_{q}^{(\tau)},\ m\to\infty.

(2.2)

We have

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\big]=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right)\exp\left(d(m_{4}-1)\sum_{t=1}^{s}\binom{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}{2}\right).

(2.3)

Here
\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
is a special class of graphs that will be defined in the course of the proof (see Lemma
3.1
).

(ii)

For all fixed
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, if

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)<\infty.

In particular, (i) and (ii) imply that
n^{-k}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}
converge almost surely to the limit given in the r.h.s. of (
2.3
).

The proof of the theorem is given in Section
3
.

Remark 2.1
.

Here, we require the random variable
\xi_{1}
to have finite moments of any order. For most matrix models, such moment conditions can be removed by a standard truncation argument. However, the centralization step in the truncation argument fails for our matrix model. More precisely, let
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l)}
be the truncated vector for all
1\leq\alpha\leq m
and
1\leq l\leq k
, then we have the following identity

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}-\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(1)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(1)}\big]\big)\otimes\cdots\otimes\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}\big]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{k}\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l-1)}\otimes\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l)}\big]\otimes\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l+1)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l+1)}\big]\big)\otimes\cdots\otimes\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}\big]\big).

(2.4)

Note that for
1\leq l\leq k
, the
n^{k}\times m
matrix, whose
\alpha
-th column is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l-1)}\otimes\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l)}\big]\otimes\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l+1)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(l+1)}\big]\big)\otimes\cdots\otimes\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}-\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\alpha}^{(k)}\big]\big),

has rank at most
n^{k-1}
. Thus, the
n^{k}\times m
matrix, whose
\alpha
-th column is (
2.1
), has rank at most
kn^{k-1}
. Hence, by
[
3
, Theorem A.44]
, the sup norm of the difference of the cumulative distribution functions in the centralization step does not exceed
k/n
, which is not negligible when
d>0
.

Let
\theta=e^{d(m_{4}-1)}
. The limit moments in (
2.3
), say
\gamma_{p}
, are polynomial functions of
\theta
.
The first four moments are

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=c\theta+c^{2},

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}

\displaystyle=c\theta^{3}+3c^{2}\theta+c^{3},

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}

\displaystyle=c\theta^{6}+4c^{2}\theta^{3}+2c^{2}\theta^{2}+6c^{3}\theta+c^{4}.

However, for higher exponent
p
, some computing code is needed to find an explicit expression for
\gamma_{p}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The limiting moment sequence
(\gamma_{p})
grow to infinity extremely fast with

. To see this, let
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
. Then
\gamma_{p}
is lower bounded by the first term (
s=1
) in (

) is

In particular, it is not clear whether the moment sequence

Assume

Here

(ii)

For all fixed

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)<\infty.

In particular, (i) and (ii) imply that

converge almost surely to the limit given in the r.h.s. of (

The proof of the proposition is given in Appendix

Finally, comparing the two cases

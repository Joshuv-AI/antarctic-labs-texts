Standing waves for nonlinear Hartree type equations: existence and qualitative properties
Eduardo de Souza Böer, Ederson Moreira dos Santos

Abstract




Abstract:
We consider systems of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u+u=\dfrac{2p}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta v+v=\dfrac{2q}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q-2}v\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

for
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\max\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{N},1\right\}<p,q<2^{*}
and
\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}<p+q<2^{*}_{\alpha}
, where
I_{\alpha}
denotes the Riesz potential,

2^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2N}{N-2}\ \ \text{for}\ \ N\geq 3,\\
+\infty\ \ \text{for}\ \ N=1,2,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad 2^{*}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}\ \ \text{for}\ \ N\geq 3,\\
+\infty\ \ \text{for}\ \ N=1,2.\end{array}\right.

This type of systems arises in the study of standing wave solutions for a certain approximation of the Hartree theory for a two-component attractive interaction. We prove existence and some qualitative properties for ground state solutions, such as definite sign for each component, radial symmetry and sharp asymptotic decay at infinity, and a regularity/integrability result for the (weak) solutions. Moreover, we show that the straight lines
p+q=\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}
and
p+q=2^{*}_{\alpha}
are critical for the existence of solutions.

Mathematics Subject Classification:

35B06, 35B40, 35J47, 35J50, 35J60, 35Q40, 35Q92.

Key words
.
Hartree type equations, Choquard type systems, Existence of solutions, Regularity of solutions, Radial symmetry, Asymptotic decay.

1
Introduction

Consider the two-components Hartree type equations

\begin{cases}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi=\Delta\varphi+\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\left(I_{\alpha}\ast\left|\psi\right|^{q}\right)\left|\varphi\right|^{p-2}\varphi\quad\text{in}\ \ (0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\vskip 10.0pt\\
-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi=\Delta\psi+\dfrac{2q}{p+q}\left(I_{\alpha}\ast\left|\varphi\right|^{p}\right)\left|\psi\right|^{q-2}\psi\quad\text{in}\ \ (0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, for
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential, also called Coulumb type potential, defined by

I_{\alpha}(x)=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N-\alpha}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}2^{\alpha}}\,\dfrac{1}{|x|^{N-\alpha}},

\Gamma
stands for the Gamma function, and
\ast
denotes the convolution.

As presented in
[
16
,
18
,
1
]
, Hartree type equations with the Coulomb potential
I_{N-1}
are used as models to describe the interaction between electrons in the Hartree-Fock theory in quantum chemistry. In addition, systems of the form considered in this paper are used for modeling many physical situations, and for a discussion of how the Hartree type equations appears as a mean-field limit for many-particles boson systems, we refer to
[
9
,
12
]
.

In this paper we study the existence of standing wave solutions
(\varphi,\psi)=e^{it}(u,v)
, with
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, of (
1.1
). This yields that
(u,v)
must be solution of the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\dfrac{2p}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta v+v=\dfrac{2q}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q-2}v\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

also called Choquard type system.

In the case with
p=q
and
u=v
, system (
1.2
) becomes the Choquard equation

-\Delta u+u=(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \text{ in }\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.3)

This equation arises in physics, with
N=3
,
p=2
and
\alpha=2
, as a modelling problem for the quantum mechanics of a polaron at rest, first studied by Fröhlich and Pekar in 1954; see
[
10
,
8
,
26
]
. Then, in 1976, Choquard introduced this equation in the study of an electron trapped in its hole
[
13
]
. Moreover, in 1996, Penrose has derived this equation while discussing about the self gravitational collapse of a quantum-mechanical system in
[
22
]
. After these pioneering works, Choquard equations have been extensively studied and the literature is quite vast. We cite the works
[
20
,
21
]
and the references therein for a more detailed overview, being the former influential to this work. Finally, Choquard equations has a natural extension for
N=2
, here with a logarithmic kernel, derived from the fundamental solution of the Laplacian operator, for which we refer to
[
7
]
.

Much less results are known regarding the study of standing wave solutions of (
1.1
). We cite
[
2
]
, where existence and stability of standing wave solutions of (
1.1
) are studied in the case with
p=q
and
N=2,3
, and
[
24
]
for the study of standing waves for a related system. We also mention that Zhang and Chen
[
5
]
studied

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+A(x)u=\dfrac{2p}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta v+B(x)v=\dfrac{2q}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q-2}v\ \ \textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

with
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p,q<c_{\alpha}
, for
c_{\alpha}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
and
c_{\alpha}=\infty
if
N=1,2
, and proved the existence of positive ground state solutions for (
1.4
). They considered the positive potentials
A
and
B
as bounded and periodic (in particular,
A=B=1
). However, up to our knowledge, no qualitative properties of these solutions (such as regularity/integrability, symmetry, asymptotic decay) are available in the literature.

In this paper we present a more complete picture about the existence of solutions and ground state solutions of (
1.2
). Namely, we expand the region on the
(p,q)
-plan where positive solutions to (
1.2
) exist and we prove: the definite sign for each component
u
and
v
, radial symmetry and sharp asymptotic decay at infinity for ground state solutions of (
1.2
); a regularity result for the (weak) solutions of (
1.2
); we prove that the straight lines
p+q=\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}
and
p+q=2^{*}_{\alpha}
are critical for the existence of solutions, in the sense that if
(p,q)
is on or below the first, or if it is on or above the second, then no nontrivial solution of (
1.2
) exists. We also prove some general results that can be useful in other frameworks. For example, the Brezis-Lieb type result for double sequences as given by Proposition
4.2
.

We start establishing our hypotheses on
p,q
for the existence of solutions for (
1.2
). Our bounds for
p
and
q
take into consideration the both versions of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev theorems (Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
) and Sobolev embeddings, namely

\max\left\{\dfrac{2\alpha}{N},1\right\}<p,q<2^{*}\mbox{ \ \ and \ \ }\dfrac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}<p+q<2^{*}_{\alpha},

(H1)

where

2^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2N}{N-2}\ \ \text{for}\ \ N\geq 3,\\
+\infty\ \ \text{for}\ \ N=1,2,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad 2^{*}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}\ \ \text{for}\ \ N\geq 3,\\
+\infty\ \ \text{for}\ \ N=1,2.\end{array}\right.

At this point we stress that in the case with
p=q
,
u=v
, where system (
1.2
) becomes the Choquard equation, condition (
H1
) becomes

\frac{N-2}{N+\alpha}<\frac{1}{p}<\frac{N}{N+\alpha},

which appears in
[
20
]
for the existence of solutions to (
1.3
).

We state next our main results, and we refer to Definition
2.5
ahead for the precise definition of (weak) solution of (

).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

,
N\geq 1
, and

be as in (

). Then system (
1.2
) has a ground state solution

such that

and
v>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, if
(u,v)
is a ground state solution of (

), then
|u|>0
and
|v|>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
(u,v)
is a solution of (
1.2
), then
(u,-v)

(-u,v)

1.2

.

Now we turn to a result regarding the regularity/integrability of solutions of (
1.2
). In comparison with the scalar case
[

, see also
[

[

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

,

,

as in (

), and

).

(a)

If

then

and

(b)

If

p<\dfrac{N}{N-\alpha}\mbox{\ \ \ and \ \ \ }q<\dfrac{N}{N-\alpha},

2
Variational framework

In this section we set the variational framework associated to problem (
1.2
) and prove some technical results. We consider
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
endowed with its usual norm
\|\cdot\|=\left(\|\,|\nabla\cdot|\,\|_{2}^{2}+\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
stands for the usual norm for the
L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space, and
E=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the norm
\|(u,v)\|_{E}=\left(\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

Since the systems in this paper depend on Riesz potentials, we recall the well known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
, and we will refer to it as (
HLS1
) for short.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
be such that
\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N}
. Consider
f\in L^{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L^{\theta_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, there exists a constant
K_{0}=K_{0}(N,\alpha,\theta_{1})
, independent of
f
and
g
, such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)|x-y|^{-(N-\alpha)}g(y)dxdy\right|\leq K_{0}\|f\|_{\theta_{1}}\|g\|_{\theta_{2}}.

(HLS1)

Moreover, as in
[
20
, eq. (1.3)]
, the following result, also known as Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality plays an important role in our arguments.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
and
s\in\left(1,\frac{N}{\alpha}\right)
. Then, for any
\varphi\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(I_{\alpha}\ast\varphi)\in L^{\frac{Ns}{N-\alpha s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|I_{\alpha}\ast\varphi|^{\frac{Ns}{N-\alpha s}}dx\leq K_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi|^{s}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N-\alpha s}},

(HLS2)

where
K_{1}=K_{1}(N,\alpha,s)
is a positive constant.

For using variational methods to treat (
1.2
), from Proposition
2.1
and the Sobolev embeddings of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
\frac{N-2}{2N}\leq\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we want to classify the numbers
p>0
and
q>0
such that there exist
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
satisfying the following conditions:

\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1,\quad\dfrac{N-2}{2N}<\frac{1}{\theta_{1}p}<\frac{1}{2},\ \ \ \dfrac{N-2}{2N}<\frac{1}{\theta_{2}q}<\frac{1}{2}\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }\dfrac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\dfrac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\dfrac{N+\alpha}{N},

(2.1)

which are equivalent to

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
p,q\geq 1
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1
be such that
\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N}
. Suppose that
(u_{n})\subset L^{\theta_{1}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(v_{n})\subset L^{\theta_{2}q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are bounded sequences such that
u_{n}\to u
and
v_{n}\to v
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})|v_{n}-v|^{q}dx\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})|v_{n}-v|^{q}dx=

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[I_{\alpha}\ast(|u_{n}|^{p}-|u_{n}-u|^{p})]|v_{n}|^{q}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})](|v_{n}-v|^{q}-|v_{n}|^{q})dx.

Lemma
4.1
guarantees that
|u_{n}|^{p}-|u_{n}-u|^{p}\to|u|^{p}
in
L^{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Also observe that

\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}>\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-1=\frac{\alpha}{N}.

Then, from (

), we infer that
I_{\alpha}\ast(|u_{n}|^{p}-|u_{n}-u|^{p})\to I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p}
in
L^{N\theta_{1}/(N-\alpha\theta_{1})}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. On the other hand,
|v_{n}|^{q}\rightharpoonup|v|^{q}
in
L^{\theta_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\frac{N-\alpha\theta_{1}}{N\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{N-\alpha\theta_{1}}{N\theta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{N+\alpha}{N}=1.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[I_{\alpha}\ast(|u_{n}|^{p}-|u_{n}-u|^{p})]|v_{n}|^{q}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx,\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

(4.2)

Next, observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})](|v_{n}-v|^{q}-|v_{n}|^{q})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[I_{\alpha}\ast(|v_{n}-v|^{q}-|v_{n}|^{q})]|u_{n}-u|^{p}dx.

Arguing as before,
I_{\alpha}\ast(|v_{n}-v|^{q}-|v_{n}|^{q})\to I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q}
in
L^{N\theta_{2}/(N-\alpha\theta_{2})}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|u_{n}-u|^{p}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with

\frac{N-\alpha\theta_{2}}{N\theta_{2}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}=1,

yielding that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(I_{\alpha}\ast u_{n}-u|^{p})](|v_{n}-v|^{q}-|v_{n}|^{q})dx\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

(4.3)

Therefore, the result follows from (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
∎

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\leq k_{p,q}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[S_{\alpha,p,q}(u_{n},v_{n})\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}\right.\vskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.-S_{\alpha,p,q}(u_{n}-u,v_{n}-v)\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})|v_{n}-v|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}\right]^{\frac{p+q}{p+q-2}}\vskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mathcal{N}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}-\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})|v_{n}-v|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}\right]^{\frac{p+q}{p+q-2}}.

(4.4)

Since
p+q>2
, by Lemma
4.2
, if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}-\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}-u|^{p})|v_{n}-v|^{q}}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}<1,

which would lead (
4.4
) to a contradiction.

Therefore,

\|(u,v)\|_{E}^{2}\leq\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|(u_{n},v_{n})\|_{E}^{2}=2\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx.

(4.5)

Thus, from (
4.5
) and Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that
\overline{t}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\leq I(\overline{t}u,\overline{t}v)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\overline{t}^{\,p+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx

\displaystyle=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{p})|v_{n}|^{q}dx=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}I(u_{n},v_{n})=c_{\mathcal{N}}.

Consequently,
\overline{t}=1
and
I(u,v)=c_{\mathcal{N}}
, with
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0
. Then, by the maximum principle, e.g. as in
[
23
, Proposition 2.1]
, we infer that
u>0
and
v>0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

If
(u,v)
is a ground state solution of (
1.2
) so is
(|u|,|v|)
, and the result follows from the maximum principle, again as in
[
23
, Proposition 2.1]
.
∎

5
Symmetry of positive ground state solutions

In this section we prove that any positive ground state solution is radial, namely Theorem
1.3
. To this aim, we use some arguments based on polarization as in
[
4
,
20
]
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a closed half-space and
\sigma_{H}
be the reflection with respect to
\partial H
. We define the polarization of a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
u^{H}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, as

u^{H}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\{u(x),u(\sigma_{H}(x))\},\mbox{ \ \ if }x\in H,\\
\min\{u(x),u(\sigma_{H}(x))\},\mbox{ \ \ \ if }x\notin H.\end{array}\right.

We start with the following characterization of a radial function, as presented in
[
20
, Lemma 5.4]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
u\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If
u\geq 0
and for every closed half-space
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
u^{H}=u
or
u^{H}=u\circ\sigma_{H}
, then there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
v:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a nondecreasing function such that for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
u(x)=v(|x-x_{0}|)
.

That said, to prove Theorem
1.3
, we show that any (positive) ground state solution
(u,v)
satisfies the hypotheses in Lemma
5.1
and that the center of symmetry
x_{0}
is the same for both components
u
and
v
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
be such that
\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=2
. Consider
f\in L^{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
g\in L^{\theta_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
any closed half-space. If
f>0
,
g>0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R^{N}}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy,

then either
u^{H}=u
and
v^{H}=v
or
u^{H}=u\circ\sigma_{H}
and
v^{H}=v\circ\sigma_{H}
.

Proof.

First of all, observe that both integrals are finite by (
HLS1
). Moreover, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\dfrac{f(x)g(y)+f(\sigma_{H}(x))g(\sigma_{H}(y))}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{H}\int_{H}\dfrac{f(x)g(\sigma_{H}(y))+f(\sigma_{H}(x))g(y)}{|x-\sigma_{H}(y)|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(y)+f^{H}(\sigma_{H}(x))g^{H}(\sigma_{H}(y))}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{H}\int_{H}\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(\sigma_{H}(y))+f^{H}(\sigma_{H}(x))g^{H}(y)}{|x-\sigma_{H}(y)|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy.

On the other hand, for all
x,y\in H
, for
u,v\geq 0
,
\alpha<N
and the definition of
u^{H}
and
v^{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x)g(y)+f(\sigma_{H}(x))g(\sigma_{H}(y))}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}+\dfrac{f(x)g(\sigma_{H}(y))+f(\sigma_{H}(x))g(y)}{|x-\sigma_{H}(y)|^{N-\alpha}}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(y)+f^{H}(\sigma_{H}(x))g^{H}(\sigma_{H}(y))}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}+\dfrac{f^{H}(x)g^{H}(\sigma_{H}(y))+f^{H}(\sigma_{H}(x))g^{H}(y)}{|x-\sigma_{H}(y)|^{N-\alpha}},

thanks to
|x-y|\leq|x-\sigma_{H}(y)|
for all
x,y\in H
.
Thus, from hypothesis, the equality must hold a.e. in (
5.1
). Considering the sets
\Omega_{1}=\{x\in H\ ;u^{H}(x)=u(x)\}
,
\Omega_{2}=\{x\in H\ ;u^{H}(x)=u(\sigma_{H}(x))\}
,
\Omega_{3}=\{y\in H\ ;v^{H}(y)=v(y)\}
and
\Omega_{4}=\{y\in H\ ;v^{H}(y)=v(\sigma_{H}(y))\}
, it follows that the equality holds in (
5.1
) a.e if, and only if, either
u^{H}=u
and
v^{H}=v
or
u^{H}=u\circ\sigma_{H}
and
v^{H}=v\circ\sigma_{H}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be as in the conditions of Lemma
5.2
. Then,
f
and
g
are radial functions with a common center of symmetry.

Proof.

From Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
, there are
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
f
is radially symmetric with center at
x_{0}
and
v
is axially symmetric with center at
x_{1}
. Suppose that
x_{0}\neq x_{1}
. In this case, consider the straight segment
L
joining
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
and the closed half-space
H
such that
x_{0}\in H
,
\partial H
is orthogonal to
L
and intersects its middle point. Thus, from Lemma
5.2
, either
f^{H}=f
and
g^{H}=g
or
f^{H}=f\circ\sigma_{H}
and
g^{H}=g\circ\sigma_{H}
. If the first one happens, then
g
must be constant and this contradicts the integrability of
g
. The second case induces a similar contradiction. Therefore,
x_{0}=x_{1}
.
∎

It remains to prove that any positive ground state solution
(u,v)
of (
1.2
) is such that
f=u^{p}
and
g=v^{q}
verify the hypotheses of Lemma
5.2
, which is proved below.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(u,v)
be a positive ground state solution of (
1.2
) and

be any closed half-space of

. Then

is also a ground state solution of (

).

Proof.

Since

Let

and

be the polarization of

and
v
, respectively. Since

and

such that

\displaystyle I(\overline{t}_{H}u^{H},\overline{t}_{H}v^{H})=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)(\overline{t}_{H})^{2}\|(u^{H},v^{H})\|_{E}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\dfrac{(\|(u^{H},v^{H})\|_{E}^{2})^{\frac{p+q}{p+q-2}}}{\left(2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u^{H}|^{p})|v^{H}|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q-2}}}.

\|(u,v)\|_{E}^{2}=\|(u^{H},v^{H})\|_{E}^{2}\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u^{H}|^{p})|v^{H}|^{q}dx,

we infer that

and

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\|(u,v)\|_{E}^{2}\leq I(\overline{t}_{H}u^{H},\overline{t}_{H}v^{H})=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\dfrac{(\|(u^{H},v^{H})\|_{E}^{2})^{\frac{p+q}{p+q-2}}}{\left(2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u^{H}|^{p})|v^{H}|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q-2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\dfrac{(\|(u,v)\|_{E}^{2})^{\frac{p+q}{p+q-2}}}{\left(2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q-2}}}=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p+q}\right)\|(u,v)\|_{E}^{2}=c_{\mathcal{N}}.

Therefore, we conclude that
\overline{t}_{H}=1
and
I(u^{H},v^{H})=I(u,v)
. Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u^{H}|^{p})|v^{H}|^{q}dx.\qed

(5.2)

Proof of Theorem

Let

Let

Step 1.
The start of the bootstrap process: If

Indeed, suppose that

a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta\underline{u}+(2-p)\underline{u}=(2-p)\dfrac{2p}{p+q}I_{\alpha}(x)\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx-\dfrac{\mu}{|x|^{\delta}}\right),\mbox{ \ in \ }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1},\\
\underline{u}(x)=u^{p-2}(x),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ \ in \ }\partial B_{1},\\
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\underline{u}(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

Applying the linearity of the operator
-\Delta+1
and Lemma
7.4
to each equation with
\lambda=2-p
and
\beta=N-\alpha
, and,
\lambda=2-p
and
\beta=N-\alpha+\delta
, concluding that

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{\underline{u}(x)}{I_{\alpha}(x)}=\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx.

By the comparison principle,
\underline{u}\leq u^{2-p}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
, which implies

\liminf\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{u^{2-p}(x)}{I_{\alpha}(x)}\geq\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx.

(7.10)

On the other hand, applying the Young’s inequality to the kernel, we have

\dfrac{2p}{p+q}(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\leq\left(\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(2-p)(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})^{\frac{1}{2-p}}+(p-1)u.

From (
7.9
) and the mean value theorem, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
, it follows that

(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{q})^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(x)\leq I_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(x)\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}+\dfrac{\nu}{|x|^{\delta}}\right).

Hence, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
, we infer that

-\Delta u+(2-p)u\leq\left(\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(2-p)I_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(x)\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}+\dfrac{\nu}{|x|^{\delta}}\right).

Consider, now,
\overline{u}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1})
a solution

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta\overline{u}+(2-p)\overline{u}=\left(\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(2-p)I_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(x)\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}+\dfrac{\nu}{|x|^{\delta}}\right),\mbox{ \ in \ }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1},\\
\overline{u}(x)=u(x),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ \ in \ }\partial B_{1},\\
\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\overline{u}(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

Once again, using the linearity of the operator
-\Delta+1
and applying the Lemma
7.4
to each of the new equations, we conclude that

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{\overline{u}(x)}{I_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2-p}}(x)}=\left(\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}.

Therefore, by the comparison principle,
u\leq\overline{u}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
and

\limsup\limits_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{u^{2-p}(x)}{I_{\alpha}(x)}\leq\dfrac{2p}{p+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}dx,

concluding the proof.
∎

8
A non-existence result

In this section we establish the region of a
(p,q)
-plan where no nontrivial solution for (
1.2
) exists. To achieve this aim, we will use a Pohozǎev-type identity.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<N
,
p>1,q>1,\theta_{1}>1,\theta_{2}>1
be such that
\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{N+\alpha}{N}
. If
(u,v)
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) such that
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W_{loc}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,\theta_{1}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W_{loc}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,\theta_{2}q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\dfrac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})dx+\dfrac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}+v^{2}dx=2\dfrac{N+\alpha}{p+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|v|^{q}dx.

Proof.

The proof can be done similarly as
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
, considering
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
in
B_{1}
and defining, for
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f_{\lambda}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1,\theta_{1}p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g_{\lambda}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1,\theta_{2}q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

f_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla u(x)\mbox{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }g_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla v(x),

respectively.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

From the fact that
(u,v)
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) and applying Proposition
8.1
, we infer that

\left(\dfrac{N-2}{2}-\dfrac{N+\alpha}{p+q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\left(\dfrac{N}{2}-\dfrac{N+\alpha}{p+q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}+v^{2}dx=0.

From this identity, if
(p+q)(N-2)\geq 2(N+\alpha)
, then
\dfrac{N}{2}-\dfrac{N+\alpha}{p+q}>0
, which imply that
u=v=0
. On the other hand, if
(p+q)N\leq 2(N+\alpha)
, then
\dfrac{N-2}{2}-\dfrac{N+\alpha}{p+q}<0
, which imply that
\nabla u=\nabla v=0
, and then
u=v=0
.
∎

Exact solutions to homogeneous and quasi-homogeneous systems of nonlinear ODEs Journal: arXiv.org
Andrei D. Polyanin, Alexei I. Zhurov

Abstract
This note considers fairly general quasi-homogeneous systems of first-order nonlinear ODEs and homogeneous systems of second-order nonlinear ODEs that contain arbitrary functions of several arguments. It presents several exact solutions to these systems in terms of elementary functions.



1
Brief introduction

Systems of ordinary differential equations are a common object of study in various
scientific disciplines. Many exact solutions to such systems can be found in the
handbooks
kam
,
polzai2018
. The article
cal2021
presented partial solutions
to a class of systems of first-order nonlinear ordinary differential equations with
homogeneous polynomial right-hand sides. The current note deals with more general
(than in
cal2021
), quasi-homogeneous systems of first-order nonlinear ODEs as
well as homogeneous systems of second-order nonlinear ODEs. It presents several exact
solutions to these systems in terms of elementary functions.

2
Quasi-homogeneous systems of nonlinear first-order ODEs

Consider the following quasi-homogeneous systems of
N
first-order ordinary differential
equations for the unknowns
x_{1}=x_{1}(t)
, …,
x_{N}=x_{N}(t)
:

x^{\prime}_{n}=x_{n}^{m_{n}+1}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}^{m_{2}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux_{3}^{m_{3}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux_{N}^{m_{N}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

(1)

where
F_{n}(\dots)
are given arbitrary functions,
m_{n}
are arbitrary constants, and

N
is an arbitrary positive integer.

System (
1
) preserves its form under the transformation

t=\lambda^{-m_{1}}\bar{t},\quad\ x_{n}=\lambda^{m_{1}/m_{n}}\bar{x}_{n},\quad\ n=1,\ldots,N,

where
\lambda>0
is an arbitrary constant.

Assuming that
m_{n}\not=0
(
n=1,\ldots,N
), we look for an exact solution to system
(
1
) in the form

x_{n}(t)=a_{n}(1+Ct)^{-1/m_{n}},\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

(2)

where
C
is an arbitrary constant and
a_{n}=x_{n}(0)
are constants
(initial values of the unknowns) to be determined.

The system of equations (
1
) admits an exact solution of the form (
2
)
with the constants
a_{n}
related by the algebraic (or transcendental) constraints

a_{n}^{m_{n}}m_{n}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mua_{2}^{m_{2}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}^{m_{3}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}^{m_{N}/m_{1}}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)+C=0,\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

(3)

Example 1
. For
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{N}=m
, the quasi-homogeneous system
(
1
) simplifies to become a homogeneous system that can be represented as

x^{\prime}_{n}=x_{n}^{m+1}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

(4)

where
F_{n}(\dots)
are arbitrary functions. The system of ODEs (
4
) admits an
exact solution of the form (
2
) with
m_{n}=m
, where the constants
a_{n}
are
related by the constraints

a_{n}^{m}mF_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)+C=0,\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

The note
eq2021
presents system (
4
) and its solution for positive
integer
m
.

In the degenerate case
m=0
, exact solutions to system (
4
) can be sought in the
exponential form

x_{n}(t)=a_{n}\exp(-Ct),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

where
C
is an arbitrary constant. The constants
a_{n}
and
C
are related by the
constraints

F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)+C=0,\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

Example 2
.
The homogeneous system of ODEs (
4
) can be represented in the equivalent form

x^{\prime}_{n}=x_{1}^{m+1}G_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

(5)

where
G_{n}(\dots)=(x_{n}/x_{1})^{m+1}F_{n}(\dots)
.
Suppose that the functions
G_{n}
are all multivariate polynomials of degree
M=m+1

such that

G_{n}=\sum\alpha_{n\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{N}}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)^{\!\mu_{2}}\ldots\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)^{\!\mu_{N}},\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

where
\alpha_{n\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{N}}
are some constants and
\mu_{2}
, …,
\mu_{N}

are some nonnegative integers. Then system (
5
) becomes

x^{\prime}_{n}=\sum c_{n\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{N}}x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\ldots x_{N}^{\mu_{N}},\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

where
c_{n\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{N}}
are some constants and

\mu_{1}=M-\mu_{2}-\cdots-\mu_{N}
. We see that
\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{N}=M
. If
\mu_{1}

is also a nonnegative integer, we obtain system (1) from
cal2021
. It admits the
exact solution

x_{n}(t)=a_{n}(1+Ct)^{1/(1-M)},\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

where
C
is an arbitrary parameter and the constants
a_{n}
satisfy the algebraic
constraints

Ca_{n}=(1-M)\sum_{\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{N}=M}c_{n\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{N}}a_{1}^{m_{1}}\ldots a_{N}^{m_{N}},\ \ \ \ \ \ n=1,\ldots,N.

3
Homogeneous systems of nonlinear second-order ODEs

Now we look at homogeneous systems of second-order ODEs of the form

x^{\prime\prime}_{n}=x_{n}^{m}(x^{\prime}_{n})^{k}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

(6)

where
F_{n}(\dots)
are arbitrary functions of their arguments,
m
and
k
are arbitrary constants,
and
N
is an arbitrary positive integer.

Assuming all unknowns to be proportional, we look for a particular solution to system
(
6
) in the special form

x_{n}=a_{n}y,\quad y=y(t),\quad n=1,\ldots,N,

(7)

where
a_{n}
are constants to be determined. As a results, we arrive at the following
second-order ODE for
y
:

y^{\prime\prime}=\lambda y^{m}(y^{\prime})^{k},

(8)

where
\lambda
is an arbitrary constant, while
a_{n}
satisfy the relations

a_{n}^{m+k-1}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)=\lambda,\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

It is noteworthy that equation (
8
) is solvable and its general solution
can be represented in implicit form.

For arbitrary
m
and
k
such that
m+k-1\not=0
, equation (
8
) admits
the simple power-law particular solution

y=A(1+Ct)^{\sigma},\quad\sigma={\mskip 1.0muk-2\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mum+k-1\mskip 1.0mu},\quad A=\biggl[{\mskip 1.0muC^{2-k}(\sigma-1)\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mu\lambda\sigma^{k-1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr]^{\textstyle{\mskip 1.0mu1\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mum+k-1\mskip 1.0mu}},

where
C
is an arbitrary constant.

For
m+k-1=0
, equation (
8
) admits the exponential partial solution

y=B\exp(Ct),\quad\lambda=C^{2-k},

where
B
and
C
are arbitrary constants.

Below are two examples of more complicated solutions to equation (
8
) and system
(
6
).

Example 3
.
In the special case of
m=1
and
k=0
, system (
6
) becomes

x^{\prime\prime}_{n}=x_{n}F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mux_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mux^{\prime}_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr),\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

Its has two different exact solutions depending on the sign of
\lambda
. These are
obtained from the second-order linear equation (
8
) with
m=1
and
k=0
:

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}[C_{1}\exp(-\beta t)+C_{2}\exp(\beta t)]

\displaystyle\text{if \ $\lambda=\beta^{2}>0$};

(9)

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}[C_{1}\cos(\beta t)+C_{2}\sin(\beta t)]

\displaystyle\text{if \ $\lambda=-\beta^{2}<0$},

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are arbitrary constants. The constants

satisfy the constraints

F_{n}\biggl({\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},{\mskip 1.0mua_{3}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu},\dots,{\mskip 1.0mua_{N}\mskip 1.0mu\over\mskip 1.0mua_{1}\mskip 1.0mu}\biggr)=\pm\beta^{2},\qquad n=1,\ldots,N,

where the upper sign refers to the first group of solutions in (
9
), while the

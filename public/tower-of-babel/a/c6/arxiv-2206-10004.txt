Quantitative bounds for products of simplices in subsets of the unit cube
Polona Durcik, Mario Stipčić

Abstract
. For each 1 ≤ i ≤ n 1\leq i\leq n , let k i ≥ 1 k_{i}\geq 1 and let Δ i \Delta_{i} be a set of vertices of a non-degenerate simplex of k i + 1 k_{i}+1 points in ℝ k i + 1 \mathbb{R}^{k_{i}+1} . If A ⊆ [ 0 , 1 ] k 1 + 1 × ⋯ × [ 0 , 1 ] k n + 1 A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1} is a Lebesgue measurable set of measure at least δ \delta , we show that there exists an interval I = I ⁡ ( Δ 1 , … , Δ n , A ) I=I(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n},A) of length at least exp ⁡ ( − δ − C ⁡ ( Δ 1 , … , Δ n ) ) \exp(-\delta^{-C(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})}) such that for each λ ∈ I \lambda\in I , the set A A contains Δ ′ 1 × ⋯ × Δ ′ n \Delta^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times\Delta^{\prime}_{n} , where each Δ i ′ \Delta_{i}^{\prime} is an isometric copy of λ ​ Δ i \lambda\Delta_{i} . This is a quantitative improvement of a result by Lyall and Magyar. Our proof relies on harmonic analysis. The main ingredient in the proof are cancellation estimates for forms similar to multilinear singular integrals associated with n n -partite n n -regular hypergraphs.

1.
Introduction

Finite point configurations in large but otherwise arbitrary subsets of Euclidean spaces have been a topic of several recent investigations
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
In this paper we study existence of isometric copies of dilates of products of non-degenerate simplices in sets of positive Lebesgue measure in the unit cube.
The main focus is on a quantitative lower bound on the size of the family of dilates whose isometric copies are detected in the set, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
k_{i}\geq 1
and let

\Delta_{i}=\{0,v_{i}^{1},\ldots,v_{i}^{k_{i}}\},

where
v_{i}^{1},\ldots,v_{i}^{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}}
are linearly independent vectors. Let
\delta\in(0,1/2]
. There exists a constant
C=C(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})>0
such that the following holds. For every measurable set
A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1}
with the Lebesgue measure
|A|\geq\delta
, there is an interval
I=I(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n},A)\subseteq(0,\infty)
of length at least

(\exp(\delta^{-C(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})}))^{-1}

such that for every
\lambda\in I
and
1\leq i\leq n
one can find
x_{i},y_{i}^{1},\ldots,y_{i}^{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}+1}
satisfying

y_{i}^{l}\cdot y_{i}^{m}=\lambda v_{i}^{l}\cdot\lambda v_{i}^{m}\quad\textup{for}\quad 1\leq l,m\leq k_{i}

and

\Delta^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times\Delta^{\prime}_{n}\subseteq A
,
where

\Delta^{\prime}_{i}=\{x_{i},x_{i}+y_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}+y_{i}^{k_{i}}\}
.
That is,

\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
is an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta_{i}
.

The case of a single simplex, i.e. case
n=1
of Theorem
1
, is a corollary of a stronger quantitative finitary result by Bourgain
[
1
, Proposition 3]
. The exact quantitative bound on the length of the interval is not written explicitly in
[
1
]
, but it can be tracked down from its proof in
[
1
, Section 3]
.
Lyall and Magyar
[
14
]
proved a version of Theorem
1
for an arbitrary finite product of simplices but
with the lower bound on the length of the interval
I
being a reciprocal of a tower of exponentials

(\exp(\exp(\cdots\exp(\delta^{-C(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})}))\cdots)))^{-1}

of length
n
. Their proof is based on a weak hypergraph regularity lemma developed in the setting of Euclidean spaces. Kovač and the first author
[
3
]
proved Theorem
1
when
k_{i}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, in which case each simplex has only two points, i.e. their product is the vertex set of an
n
-dimensional rectangular box.
Theorem
1
is thus an extension of
[
3
]
and a quantitative improvement of
[
14
]
.

In analogy with the papers
[
1
]
and
[
14
]
we formulated Theorem
1
in the lowest dimension possible, i.e. for sets
A\subseteq[0,1]^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{d_{n}}
where
d_{i}=k_{i}+1
. As in these papers, our result extends to all higher dimensions
d_{i}\geq k_{i}+1
with mainly notational changes throughout the proof.

Our approach builds on the methods from
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, which detect simplices through certain counting forms. Following
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, in Section
2
we split the counting forms into their structured, error, and uniform parts and analyze each of the parts in the subsequent sections. The error part is handled through a certain pigeonholing argument. The main novelty of this paper is that to estimate the error part, we need to show cancellation in multilinear singular integral forms associated with
n
-partite
n
-regular hypergraphs.

Certain dyadic models of the singular integral forms that we consider in this paper are known to be bounded in an open range of Lebesgue spaces. This was shown by Kovač
[
9
]
, who proved
L^{p}
estimates for multilinear forms associated with graphs, and the second author
[
15
]
, who extended this result to hypergraphs. On the other hand, boundedness of the continuous variants of the forms in
[
9
]
and their modified versions, which appear in this paper, is still unknown in general. The only currently known bounds are in the special case of hypercubes, which are hypergraphs having two vertices in each partition class. This case was investigated by Slavikova, Thiele, and the first author
[
6
]
, who proved estimates for the associated forms in an open range of Lebesgue exponents.
However, to establish Theorem
1
, one does not need to show
L^{p}
boundedness of the forms that arise in the proof since a weaker statement suffices to deduce our combinatorial result. Namely, it is enough to establish only non-trivial quantitative cancellation bounds for the corresponding singular integral forms. That is, if the singular integral kernel consists of finitely many consecutive scales, it suffices to show a quantitative improvement over the trivial bound in the number of scales.
Moreover, to prove Theorem
1
it is enough to assume that all input functions are supported in the unit cube and bounded by
1
.
The proof of our cancellation estimates uses an extension of the methods used to estimate singular Brascamp-Lieb type integrals as in
[
3
,
6
,
9
]
, to the hypergraph setting.

A connection between cancellation estimates and Euclidean Ramsey theorems was made explicit in
[
3
]
, which deals with arithmetic progressions in the unit cube. The paper
[
3
]
shows that if
n\geq 3
,
p\in[1,\infty)\setminus\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
and the dimension
d
is large enough, then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,d)
such that the following holds. If
\delta\in(0,1/2]
and
A\subseteq[0,1]^{d}
has Lebesgue measure at least
\delta
, then there is an interval
I
whose length depends only on
n,p,d
, and
\delta
, such that for all
\lambda\in I
, the set
A
contains arithmetic progression
x,x+y,\ldots,x+(n-1)y\in A
with
\|y\|_{\ell^{p}}=\lambda
. Here, the length of
I
is a reciprocal of the double exponential of a negative power of
\delta
when
n=3
or
4
and the triple exponential when
n\geq 5
, which reflects the best known bounds in Szemerédi’s theorem. The case
n=3
also follows along the lines of the proof by Cook, Magyar, and Pramanik
[
2
]
. However, we point out that in contrast with Theorem
1
, the size of the gap
y
in
[
3
]
cannot be measured in all
\ell^{p}
norms, in particular not in
\ell^{2}
, as generalizations of Bourgain’s counterexample
[
1
]
show. Moreover, the result in
[
3
]
is at present only known in sufficiently large dimensions.

In
[
3
]
, bounds for the corresponding error part follow from cancellation estimates for singular integrals similar to the multilinear Hilbert transform. While non-trivial cancellation for the truncations of the multilinear Hilbert transform to finitely many consecutive scales was first shown by Tao
[
17
]
and later also by Zorin-Kranich
[
18
]
, and Kovač, Thiele and the first author
[
5
]
, its boundedness on Lebesgue spaces
is still one of the major open problems in harmonic analysis. The only known bounds exist for the bilinear Hilbert transform due to Lacey and Thiele
[
11
]
.
In contrast to this, we expect that boundedness of continuous variants of multilinear singular integrals associated with hypergraphs, which arise in the proof of Theorem
1
, is within reach of current techniques. This is a problem for future investigation. In view of this, we aim to keep the estimates for the error part and consequently also the proof of Theorem
1
as short as possible, i.e. we do not aim to optimize the bound in the number of scales for the singular integral as it would not improve the quantitative bound in Theorem
1
.

There are related questions on subsets of the Euclidean space of positive upper Banach density, where one is concerned with finding all large dilates of a fixed geometric configuration. The upper Banach density of a measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as

\overline{\delta}(A):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|A\cap(x+[0,N]^{d})|}{N^{d}}>0.

The simplest example is a two-point pattern, for which it holds that whenever
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies
\overline{\delta}(A)>0
, then there is a threshold
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A)
such that for each
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,
A
contains points
x,y
with
|x-y|=\lambda
.
This was conjectured by Székely
[
16
]
, the first proof was announced by Furstenberg, Katznelson and Weiss
[
8
]
, while in the meantime it was also independently shown in
[
1
]
and by Falconer and Marstrand
[
7
]
.
The paper
[
1
]
also generalizes this result for a two-point pattern and shows existence of isometric copies of all large dilates a fixed non-degenerate
(k+1)
-point simplex, in sets of positive upper Banach density in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.
The paper
[
12
]
extends this result to distance graphs, while the papers
[
13
,
14
]
extend it also to products of non-degenerate simplices. More precisely, in
[
14
]
it is shown that if one is given
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}
as in Theorem
1
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{n}+1}
satisfies
\overline{\delta}(A)>0
,
then there exists
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(A,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, the set
A
contains
\Delta_{1}^{\prime}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{n}^{\prime}
, where each
\Delta_{i}^{\prime}
an isometric copy of
\lambda\Delta_{i}
.

The corresponding result for all large dilates of rectangular boxes in sets of positive upper Banach density was also obtained in
[
4
]
using harmonic analysis techniques, but with some loss in the dimension of the ambient space. As commented in
[
3
]
, the arguments therein also give the corresponding result for cubes in sets of positive upper Banach density and they extend to boxes as well. Similarly, the techniques used in this paper can be extended to reprove the full result from
[
14
]
in sets of positive upper Banach density, provided one shows
L^{p}
boundedness for the forms associated with hypergraphs, which arise in the error part, or cancellation estimates to arbitrary, not necessarily consecutive scales.

Recently, Kovač
[
10
]
initiated the study of density theorems for anisotropic point configurations. The paper
[
10
]
established existence of all large dilates of
anisotropic simplices, anisotropic boxes, and an anisotropic version of the result on distance trees, respectively, extending several results contained in
[
1
,
14
]
, and
[
12
]
to the anisotropic setting. Proving anisotropic versions of Theorem
1
and the corresponding results in sets of positive density is yet another possible line of investigation.

For two non-negative quantities
A,B
we write
A\lesssim B

C>0

A\leq CB

A\sim B

2.
Counting forms and their decomposition

We begin this section by defining counting forms which detect isometric copies of dilates of products of fixed non-degenerate simplices in a subset of the unit cube.

Given
\Delta=\{0,v^{1},\ldots,v^{d}\}
, where
v^{1},\ldots,v^{d}
are linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we shall view them as vertices of a simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by identifying
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
First we recall the definitions of suitable spherical measures associated with
\lambda\Delta
,
\lambda>0
, as defined in
[
10
]
and previously used in
[
1
,
13
,
14
]
.

For each
1\leq j\leq d
, let us write
v^{j}=|v^{j}|u^{j}
where
u^{j}
are unit vectors. For
2\leq j\leq d
, the orthogonal projection of
u^{j}
onto
\textup{span}\{u^{1},\ldots,u^{j-1}\}
is given by

\beta_{j,1}u^{1}+\beta_{j,2}u^{2}+\ldots\beta_{j,j-1}u^{j-1}

for some
\beta_{j,1},\ldots,\beta_{j,j-1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\widetilde{\sigma}
denote the normalized surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. For
2\leq j\leq d
let

\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Delta,x^{1},\ldots,x^{j-1}}

be the normalized surface measure on the
(d-j+1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
centered at

\beta_{j,1}\frac{x^{1}}{|x^{1}|}+\beta_{j,2}\frac{x^{2}}{|x^{2}|}+\ldots\beta_{j,j-1}\frac{x^{j-1}}{|x^{j-1}|}

with radius being equal to

\textup{dist}(u^{j},\textup{span}\{u^{1},\ldots,u^{j-1}\}),
which lies in the
(d-j+2)
-dimensional plane in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
that is orthogonal to
\textup{span}\{u^{1},\ldots,u^{j-1}\}
.

For a Borel measure
\mu
and a Borel set
A

we define

\mu_{\lambda}(A):=\mu(\lambda^{-1}A)

and we write
\sigma^{\Delta}:=\widetilde{\sigma}_{|v^{1}|}
,
\sigma^{\Delta,x^{1},\ldots,x^{j-1}}:=\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Delta,x^{1},\ldots,x^{j-1}}_{|v_{j}|}
.
We also define the measure
\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}
by

\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(y^{1},\ldots,y^{d})\,d\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta,y^{1},\ldots,y^{d-1}}(y^{d})\cdots d\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta,y^{1}}(y^{2})\,d\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta}(y^{1})

where
A
is a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)}
. The support of
\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}
is the set of all points
y
such that
\{0,y^{1},\ldots,y^{d}\}
is isometric to
\lambda\Delta
. Note that the definition of
\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}
is symmetric in the choice of the order of integration in
y^{1},\ldots,y^{d}
. This
can be seen by rewriting
\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}
using the Haar measure on
SO(d+1,\mathbb{R})
, see for instance formulae (1) and (9) in
[
1
]
. By
d\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}(\cdot-c)
we will denote integration with respect to the measure
\sigma_{\Delta,\lambda}
translated by
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)}
.

Let us fix
n\geq 1
and the dimensions
k_{i}\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq n
. We also fix vertices of non-degenerate simplices of
k_{i}
points
\Delta_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}+1},\,1\leq i\leq n
.

For any tuple of integers
L=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})
satisfying
1\leq l_{i}\leq k_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
, let

\mathcal{H}_{L}
be the set of all functions
h\colon~\{1,\ldots,n\}\rightarrow\mathbbm{N}\cup\{0\}
satisfying
h(i)\leq l_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. We will write
L+1:=L+(1,\ldots,1)
.
We will also denote

K:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\quad k:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n},\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{K}.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
and

x=(x^{0}_{1},\ldots,x^{l_{1}}_{1},x_{2}^{0},\ldots x_{2}^{l_{2}},\ldots,x_{n}^{0},\ldots,x_{n}^{l_{n}})\in\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(L+1)}

we define
\Pi_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(L+1)}\to\mathbb{R}^{k+n}
by

\Pi_{h}{x}:=(x^{h(1)}_{1},\ldots,x^{h(n)}_{n}).

Now we are ready to define the counting forms which detect isometric copies of dilates of our simplices.
For
\lambda>0
and a measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k+n}

we define the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(K+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{k_{i}})\,dx_{i}^{0}.

Note that if

\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)>0,

then
A
contains
\Delta_{1}^{\prime}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{n}^{\prime}
, where each
\Delta_{i}^{\prime}
is an isometric copy of the dilated simplex
\lambda\Delta_{i}
.

If
\varphi
is a function on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote
\varphi_{t}(x):=t^{-m}\varphi(t^{-1}x)
.
Let
g
be the Gaussian

g(x):=e^{-\pi|x|^{2}}.

The dimension of Gaussians will always be understood from the context and will not be part of the notation. For
\varepsilon>0
we define a smoothed form

\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(K+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma}_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}\ast{g}_{\varepsilon\lambda}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{k_{i}})\Big)\,dx.

In the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
it recovers
\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)
. This can be seen similarly as in
[
3
, Section 3]
.

Theorem
1
will be deduced from the following three lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
\delta\in(0,1/2]
, and a measurable set
A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1}
with
|A|\geq\delta
,

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\geq C\delta^{(k_{1}+1)(k_{2}+1)\cdots(k_{n}+1)}.

(2.1)

Lemma 3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, positive integer
J
, any
\lambda_{j}\in(2^{-j},2^{-j+1}]
,
1\leq j\leq J
, and any measurable set
A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\big|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda_{j}}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda_{j}}(A)\big|\leq CJ^{1-2^{-n}}\varepsilon^{-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(k_{i}+2)}.

(2.2)

Lemma 4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\lambda,\,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and any measurable set
A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1}
,

\big|\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)\big|\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.3)

Let us first show how to deduce Theorem
1
from these three lemmas. We decompose

\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)=\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)+(\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A))+(\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)).

Let

C_{1}
-
C_{3}
be the constants that appear in Lemmas
2
-
4
, respectively. We may assume
C_{1}\in(0,1],C_{2},C_{3}\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\delta\in(0,1/2]
,
\kappa:=(k_{1}+1)\cdots(k_{n}+1)
, and
\varrho:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(k_{i}+2)
.
Take

\varepsilon:=(C_{1}\delta^{\kappa})^{2}(3C_{3})^{-2},\quad J:=\lfloor(3C_{2}C_{1}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-\varrho-1}\delta^{-\kappa})^{2^{n}}\rfloor+1.

Note that

J\lesssim\delta^{-\kappa 2^{n}(2\varrho+3)},

which gives

2^{-J}\geq 2^{-C\delta^{-\kappa 2^{n}(2\varrho+3)}}=e^{-C^{\prime}\delta^{-\kappa 2^{n}(2\varrho+3)}}

for some constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
.

By (
2.2
), there exists
1\leq j\leq J
such that for each
\lambda\in(2^{-j},2^{-j+1}]
,

\big|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\big|\leq C_{2}J^{-2^{-n}}\varepsilon^{-\varrho-1}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{3}\delta^{\kappa}.

Combined with (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we obtain the desired estimate

\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)\geq\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)-\big|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\big|-\big|\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)\big|

\geq C_{1}\delta^{\kappa}-\frac{C_{1}}{3}\delta^{\kappa}-\frac{C_{1}}{3}\delta^{\kappa}=\frac{C_{1}}{3}\delta^{\kappa}>0.

3.
The structured part: Proof of lemma
2

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2]
and
A\subseteq[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times[0,1]^{k_{n}+1}
with
|A|\geq\delta
.
Let
m
be the unique positive integer such that
\lambda\in(2^{-m},2^{-m+1}]
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\mathcal{Q}_{m}^{k_{i}}
be a collection of all dyadic cubes in
[0,1]^{k_{i}+1}
with side length
2^{-m}
. We write
\mathcal{Q}_{m}:=\mathcal{Q}_{m}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{Q}_{m}^{k_{n}}
.

We partition the domain of integration of each
x_{i}^{r_{i}}
into congruent cubes of side length
2^{-m}
and restrict the domain of integration so that for each
i
, all
x_{i}^{r_{i}}
,
1\leq r_{i}\leq k_{i}
, lie in the same cube from
\mathcal{Q}_{m}
. This yields

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\geq 2^{-D}\sum_{(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\in\mathcal{Q}_{m}}\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}*g_{\lambda}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{k_{i}})\,dx

where
D=m(K+1)\cdot(K+1)
and
Q^{k_{i}}_{i}
denotes the
k_{i}
-fold Cartesian product of
Q_{i}
.

Since the measure
\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}
is supported in the ball
B(0,\lambda C_{\Delta_{i}})
in
\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}
of radius
\lambda C_{\Delta_{i}}
centered at the origin, where
C_{\Delta_{i}}
is the diameter of
\Delta_{i}
,
for each
1\leq i\leq n
and
y\in B(0,\lambda R)
,

\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}*g_{\lambda}(y)=\lambda^{-k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}}e^{-\pi|\lambda^{-1}(y-u)|^{2}}d\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}(u)

\geq\lambda^{-k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}e^{-\pi(C_{\Delta_{i}}+R)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}}d\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}(u)=\lambda^{-k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}e^{-\pi(C_{\Delta_{i}}+R)^{2}}

The last equality follows after integrating in
u^{k},u^{k-1}
, all the way to
u^{1}
, where
u=(u^{1},\ldots,u^{k})
, using that the spherical measures are normalized.
Therefore, with
R^{2}=k_{i}(k_{i}+1)
we obtain

\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}\ast g_{\lambda}\gtrsim\lambda^{-k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{[-\lambda,\lambda]^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}}\gtrsim 2^{mk_{i}(k_{i}+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{[-2^{-m},2^{-m}]^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}}.

Estimating
\sigma_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}*g_{\lambda}
from below for each

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\gtrsim 2^{-m(k+n)}\sum_{(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\in\mathcal{Q}_{m}}\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)

\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[-2^{-m},2^{-m}]^{k_{i}(k_{i}+1)}}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{k_{i}})\,dx.

Since for
1\leq r_{i}\leq k_{i}
both
x_{i}^{r_{i}}
and
x_{i}^{0}
belong to the same cube
Q_{i}
, this gives

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\gtrsim 2^{-m(k+n)}\sum_{(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\in\mathcal{Q}_{m}}\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\,dx.

To complete the proof of the estimate (
2.1
), we claim that

\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\,dx\geq\bigg(\fint_{Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\bigg)^{\kappa}

(3.1)

where
\kappa=(k_{1}+1)\cdots(k_{n}+1)
.
This claim then implies

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{\lambda}(A)\gtrsim 2^{-m(k+n)}\sum_{(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\in\mathcal{Q}_{m}}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\bigg)^{\kappa}

\geq\bigg(2^{-m(k+n)}\sum_{(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n})\in\mathcal{Q}_{m}}\fint_{Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\bigg)^{\kappa}=\bigg(\int_{[0,1]^{k+n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\bigg)^{\kappa}\geq\delta^{\kappa}.

The second inequality follows by applying Jensen’s inequality for the normalized counting measure over the set of cardinality
2^{m(k+n)}
.
This finishes the proof the lemma, up to verification of the bound (
3.1
).

The estimate (
3.1
) can be proven by induction on
n
. We rewrite

\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\,dx=\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n}^{k_{n}+1}}\prod_{a=0}^{k_{n}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(n)=a}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\,dx

=\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}+1}}\Big(\fint_{Q_{n}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(n)=0}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)dx_{n}^{0}\Big)^{k_{n}+1}dx^{\prime}

(3.2)

where
x^{\prime}=(x^{0}_{1},\ldots,x^{k_{1}}_{1},x_{2}^{0},\ldots x_{2}^{k_{2}},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}})
.
From here we see that the claim holds for
n=1
since in this case, (
3.1
) is an equality.
For the induction step we
use Fubini and Jensen’s inequality for the integral over
x^{\prime}
, which bounds (
3.2
) from below by

\Big(\fint_{Q_{n}}\fint_{Q_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n-1}^{k_{n-1}+1}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(n)=0}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{n}^{0}\Big)^{k_{n}+1}.

By the induction hypothesis applied to the set

\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{k+n-k_{n}-1}:(y,x_{n}^{0})\in A\}

for a fixed
x_{n}^{0}
, we estimate the last expression from below by

\bigg(\fint_{Q_{n}}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{n-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(y)dy\bigg)^{(k_{1}+1)\cdots(k_{n-1}+1)}dx_{n}^{0}\bigg)^{(k_{n}+1)}.

Another application of Jensen’s inequality, this time for the integral over
x_{n}^{0}
, gives (
3.1
). This completes the proof of Lemma
3
.

4.16
).

It remains to consider the case
v\leq\tau
, which will also prove the base case.
If
v\leq\tau
, then
l_{v}=1
.
First we observe that for any
1\leq z\leq n
and any
L,a,b,\alpha
as in (
4.13
),
the form
\Theta^{(z)}_{{L},a,b,{\alpha}}
is non-negative if
l_{z}=1
and
\alpha_{z}^{0}=\alpha_{z}^{1}
. Indeed, using (
4.15
) we can write it up to a positive constant as

\sum_{m=1}^{k_{z}+1}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(L+1)}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{z}+1}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{L}:h(z)=0}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)(\partial_{m}g)_{s\alpha_{z}^{1}}(x_{r}^{0}-q)\,dx_{r}^{0}\Big)^{2}

\times\Big(\prod_{i=1,i\neq z}^{n}g_{s\alpha_{i}}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{l_{i}})\,dx_{i}\Big)\,dq\,\frac{ds}{s}.

Furthermore, by the fundamental theorem of calculus in
s
, the Leibniz rule, the heat equation (
4.4
), and the identity (
4.2
), we have for any
L,a,b,\alpha
as above

\sum_{z=1}^{n}\Theta^{(z)}_{{L},a,b,{\alpha}}=2\pi\big(\Xi_{L,a,\alpha}-\Xi_{L,b,\alpha}\big),

(4.22)

where for any
L,a,\alpha
as above we have denoted

\Xi_{L,a,\alpha}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(K+1)\cdot(L+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{L}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{a\alpha_{i}}((x_{i}^{r}-x_{i}^{0})_{r=1}^{l_{i}})\,dx.

We also have the estimate

\Xi_{L,a,\alpha}(A)\lesssim 1,

(4.23)

can be seen by localizing
(x_{1}^{0},\ldots,x_{n}^{0})
to the cube
[0,1]^{k+n}
, bounding all functions
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
by
1
, integrating first in
x_{i}^{r}
for all
r\neq 0
and finally in
x_{i}^{0}
.

Recall that to finish the proof of the lemma it remains to estimate (
4.21
) for
z=v

where
v\leq\tau
. By non-negativity of (
4.21
) for each
1\leq z\leq\tau
,
it then suffices to estimate

\sum_{z=1}^{\tau}\Theta^{(z)}_{\widetilde{L},a,b,\widetilde{\alpha}}.

In order to bound this sum for
1\leq\tau\leq n
, by (
4.22
) and (
4.23
)
applied to
\widetilde{L},a,b,\widetilde{\alpha}
it suffices to estimate the form (
4.21
) for each
z>\tau
. However this has already been achieved.
∎

5.
The uniform part: Proof of lemma
4

In this section we proceed similarly as in
[
13
]
and
[
10
]
, with the necessary modifications arising from the multilinear setting.

We again use the fundamental theorem of calculus, the Leibniz rule and the heat equation (
4.4
), which yields

\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{\vartheta}_{\lambda}(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{v=1}^{n}N(v,\lambda,\vartheta,\varepsilon,A),

(5.1)

where
N(v,\lambda,\vartheta,\varepsilon,A)
has been defined in (
4.1
).
We write out each
N(v,\lambda,\vartheta,\varepsilon,A)
as in (
4.3
). By symmetry it again suffices to
consider the case
v=1
and the summand for
w=k_{1}
.

In the summand for
w=k_{1}
in
N(1,\lambda,\vartheta,\varepsilon,A)
we consider the integrand in
p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}
and
x_{1}=(x_{1}^{0},\ldots,x_{1}^{k_{1}}),
i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}*(\Delta g)_{t\lambda}(x_{1}^{k_{1}}-x_{1}^{0})\,g_{t\lambda}\big((x_{1}^{r}-x_{1}^{0}-p_{1}^{r})_{r=1}^{k_{1}-1}\big)\,d\nu\,dx_{1}

(5.2)

where

d\nu=d\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots p_{1}^{k_{1}-2}}(p_{1}^{k_{1}-1})\cdots d\sigma_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1}}(p_{1}^{1}).

Integrating in
x_{1}^{k_{1}}
we rewrite it as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(2k_{1}-1)(k_{1}+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(1)\neq k_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)\mathcal{F}\ast{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}\ast(\Delta g)_{t\lambda}(x_{1}^{0})

\times g_{t\lambda}\big((x_{1}^{r}-x_{1}^{0}-p_{1}^{r})_{r=1}^{k_{1}-1}\big)\,d\nu\,dx_{1}^{0}\cdots dx_{1}^{k_{1}-1},

(5.3)

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the function

x_{1}^{k_{1}}\mapsto\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(1)=k_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x).

By Jensen’s inequality applied to the probabilistic measure

g_{t\lambda}\big((x_{1}^{r}-x_{1}^{0}-p_{1}^{r})_{r=1}^{k_{1}-1}\big)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]^{k_{1}+1}}(x_{1}^{0})\,d\nu\,dx_{1}^{0}\cdots dx_{1}^{k_{1}-1}

for fixed
p_{1}^{r}
,
the square of (
5.3
) is bounded by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(2k_{1}-1)(k_{1}+1)}}\Big(\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}:h(1)\neq k_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\Pi_{h}x)\Big)|\mathcal{F}\ast{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}\ast(\Delta g)_{t\lambda}(x_{1}^{0})|^{2}

\times g_{t\lambda}\big((x_{1}^{r}-x_{1}^{0}-p_{1}^{r})_{r=1}^{k_{1}-1}\big)\,d\mu\,dx_{1}^{0}\cdots dx_{1}^{k_{1}-1}.

Bounding all characteristic functions that do not make up
\mathcal{F}
by one and integrating in
x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{k_{1}-1}
, we estimate the last display by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}}|\mathcal{F}\ast{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}\ast(\Delta g)_{t\lambda}(x_{1}^{0})|^{2}\,dx_{1}^{0}\,d\nu.

By Plancherel’s theorem in
x_{1}^{0}
, this equals

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}}|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{{\sigma}}_{\lambda}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{(\Delta g)_{t\lambda}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\nu.

(5.4)

We will use the following estimate for the spherical measure that can be found in
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
:

|\widehat{{\sigma}}^{\Delta_{1},p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}}(\xi)|\lesssim\textnormal{dist}(\xi,\textnormal{span}\{p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+1}
.
Together with

\widehat{\Delta g}(\xi)=-4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}},

this allows us to estimate (
5.4
) by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}(k_{1}+1)}}\ |\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)|^{2}\textnormal{dist}(\lambda\xi,\textnormal{span}\{p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\})^{-1}|t\lambda\xi|^{4}e^{-2\pi|t\lambda\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi\,d\nu.

(5.5)

Next, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(k_{1}+1)(k_{1}-1)}}\textnormal{dist}(\lambda\xi,\textnormal{span}\{p_{1}^{1},\ldots,p_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\})^{-1}\,d\nu\lesssim|\lambda\xi|^{-1}

This estimate is proven in Section 3.3 of
[
10
]
. Applying this to (
5.5
) yields a bound by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+1}}|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)|^{2}|\lambda\xi|^{-1}|t\lambda\xi|^{4}e^{-2\pi|t\lambda\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi\lesssim t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+1}}|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq t,

For the second inequality we used that

y\mapsto y^{3}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
is uniformly bounded, while for the last
inequality we applied Plancherel one more time.
This gives an estimate for (
5.2
) by
t^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Finally, applying this estimate first to (
5.2
) and then integrating in (
5.1
) in the remaining variables, we see that

|\mathcal{N}^{\vartheta}_{\lambda}(A)-\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}(A)|\lesssim\int_{\vartheta}^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k+n-k_{1}-1}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]^{k+n-k_{1}-1}}(x_{2}^{0},\ldots,x_{n}^{0})\,dx_{2}^{0}\cdots dx_{n}^{0}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Letting
\vartheta\to 0
yields (
2.3
).

Cesàro operator on the spaces
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))

In this Section, we will discuss the Cesàro operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))
, which is the set of vector function
\vec{f}(\vec{x})=(u_{0}(\vec{x}),u_{1}(\vec{x}),u_{2}(\vec{x})\cdots u_{N}(\vec{x}))
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{N+1}
with
\vec{x}=(\mathbf{x},y)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N},y)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{N+1}
, where
y>0
and
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Let the measure
d\mu(\mathbf{x})
to denote as

d\mu(\mathbf{x})=|x_{1}|^{2\lambda_{1}}|x_{2}|^{2\lambda_{2}}\cdots|x_{N}|^{2\lambda_{N}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{N},\ \ \hbox{where}\,0<\lambda_{i}<\infty\ \hbox{for}\,1\leq i\leq N.

Then
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))
can be defined as the set of vector function
\vec{f}(\vec{x})
satisfying

\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))}=\sup\limits_{y>0}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\vec{f}(\vec{x})|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\Big)^{1/p}<\infty,\ \hbox{where},\ \ 0<p<\infty.

L_{\mu}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
denote as the set of measurable function
f(\mathbf{x})
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
satisfying
\|f\|_{L_{\mu}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=\Big(c_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\Big)^{1/p}

<\infty
, with

c_{\lambda}^{-1}=2^{\lambda+1/2}\Gamma(\lambda+1/2)
. To avoid confusion, we will use the symbol
\vec{f}
to denote as a vector function, and
f
to
denote as the ordinary measurable function.

Let
\alpha>0
, we write
\phi_{\alpha}(t)=\alpha(1-t)^{\alpha-1}
for
0<t<1
. For
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{x},y)=(u_{0}(\mathbf{x},y),u_{1}(\mathbf{x},y),\cdots,u_{N}(\mathbf{x},y))\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))
, we define the Cesàro operator
C_{\alpha}
by the form as

(C_{\alpha}u_{j})(\mathbf{x},y)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1}u_{j}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt,\ \ \hbox{for},\ \,0\leq j\leq N

where
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},t^{-1}\mathbf{x}=(t^{-1}x_{1},t^{-1}x_{2},\cdots,t^{-1}x_{N})
.
Then
C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}
and
\left|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}\right|
can be given by

C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}=(C_{\alpha}u_{0},C_{\alpha}u_{1},\cdots,C_{\alpha}u_{N}),

\left|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}\right|=\bigg((C_{\alpha}u_{0})^{2}+(C_{\alpha}u_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(C_{\alpha}u_{N})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

It is easy to verify the following Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
:

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{x},y)=(u_{0}(\mathbf{x},y),u_{1}(\mathbf{x},y),\cdots,u_{N}(\mathbf{x},y))\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))
, where
0<p<\infty,

\left|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}\right|\leq C_{\alpha}\left|\overrightarrow{f}\right|.

Proposition 2.2
.

[Minkowski’s inequality] For
1\leq p<\infty
,
\ \mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
,
if

\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|f(\mathbf{x},t)|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}dt=M<\infty

then

\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(\mathbf{x},t)dt\right|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|f(\mathbf{x},t)|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}dt=M<\infty.

By Proposition
2.2
, we could prove the following Proposition
2.3

Proposition 2.3
.

Cesàro operator
C_{\alpha}
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))
for
1\leq p<\infty
:

\|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))}\leq C\|\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))}.

C is dependent on
\alpha
,
p
and
d\mu
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.1
and the Minkowski’s inequality in Proposition
2.2
, we could conclude:

\displaystyle\|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y>0}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|C_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{f}(\mathbf{x},y)\right|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y>0}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1}\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{y>0}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left|\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)\right|t^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{1}\sup_{y>0}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)|^{p}d\mu(\mathbf{x})\right|^{1/p}t^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{1}\|\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))}t^{-1}t^{\beta/p}\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\overrightarrow{f}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}_{+},d\mu(\mathbf{x}))},\ \ \ \ \hbox{where\, $\beta=N+\sum_{i=1}^{N}2\lambda_{i}$.}

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
0<p\leq 1
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if
\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}<\infty
, then

\left(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}=1.

In this case, we have
|a_{i}|\leq 1
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so

\left(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|\right)\leq\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}|a_{i}|^{1-p}\leq\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}=1.

Then we could obtain:

\left(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}.

This proves the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.5

,

,
k\leq-1

(1)

, we could conclude:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1/2}t^{-1}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)|\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}t^{-1}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)|\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\left|\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}t^{-1}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(t^{-1}\mathbf{x},t^{-1}y\right)|\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt\right|^{p}.

\displaystyle C\xi_{k}^{-1}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(\xi_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{x},\xi_{k}^{-1}y\right)|\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}\phi_{\alpha}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}|\overrightarrow{f}\left(\xi_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{x},\xi_{k}^{-1}y\right)|^{p}.

In the same way we could conclude that:

For

The Dunkl transfrom for

\displaystyle({\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}f)(\xi)=c_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)E_{\lambda}(-ix\xi)|x|^{2\lambda}dx,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}},

Since

(5)

where

(6)

For

for

,
and 0 otherwise. From
[

]
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(x\xi)E_{\lambda}(t\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}E_{\lambda}(z\xi)d\nu_{x,t}(z),\quad x,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\ \penalty\ \hbox{and}\penalty\ \ \xi\in{\mathbb{C}},

where

,

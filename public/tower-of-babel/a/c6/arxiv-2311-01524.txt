Theorem 1.1

Then

-invariant

The assumption of property AP on the group

We present now a brief outline of the proof. Let

is in the norm closure of

. As the set

is convex, using the Hahn-Banach theorem, it

to show, in fact, that
\mathbb{E}(x)
is in the weak closure of

. In the
the first case, we construct a net of completely positive maps in

which

, and
successively using strengthened versions of Rokhlin-type theorems due to

and Ocneanu

apply the strengthened version of Ocneanu’s theorem just for finite cyclic

interest. Under the assumption of pointwise finite Rokhlin dimension, the idea
is similar, although somewhat more straightforward, as we can construct the

Once we find the Rokhlin towers in

version of the Kaplansky density theorem to obtain a net of orthogonal elements

\sigma

Definition 2.1

An discrete group

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group, and let

.
Then

\varepsilon
-paves any finite

Connes’ noncommutative Rokhlin theorem
[

for single automorphism is stated for automorphisms of finite von Neumann
algebras with an invariant trace.
We need a more general version that does not assume the trace is
invariant. The proof of this stronger version goes through almost
verbatim as in
[
8
]
(also see

[
25
, Chapter XVII]
), only that the use of the Ergodic theoretic
Rokhlin lemma for measure preserving actions is replaced by the Rokhlin theorem
for nonsingular actions (
[
17
]
). We nonetheless provide a
full proof for the reader’s convenience. The Rokhlin theorem for essentially free measure class preserving actions of amenable groups was announced in
[
17
]
. There is a short note in
[
18
]
, where the authors outline how to generalize the result from measure preserving systems to measure class preserving ones. We could not find complete proof in published literature; however, one can be found in a Ph.D. thesis by Jarrett
[

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(N,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra, with
\tau
a
faithful normal tracial state. Assume that

\alpha:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{Aut}(N)
is free. Then, for any positive
integer
n
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a partition of unity

\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
such that

\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-E_{j+1}\right\|_{\tau}<\varepsilon,\penalty\ j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
E_{n+1}=E_{1}

Proof.

Let
(N,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra. We can decompose

N
into an infinite direct sum
\overline{\bigoplus}_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}
where each summand is invariant, the action on the center

Z(N_{0})
is free, the action on the center

Z(N_{1})
is trivial, and for each
k>0
, the action on

Z(N_{k})
is
k

projection

such that the restriction to
PZ(N_{k})
has a shorter period. It suffices to prove the
statements for each of those cases separately.

Let us first assume that
\alpha
acts trivially on the center

Z(N)
. Let
n
be a fixed positive integer and

\varepsilon>0
be given. Denote by
\mathcal{U}(N)
the
unitary group
N

\|x\|_{1}=\tau(|x|)
. Consider the collection

\mathcal{E}=\left\{\left(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n};U\right)\right\}

(1)

\left\{E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n}\right\}
are mutually orthogonal equivalent projections.

(2)

and
\|U-1\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon\tau\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\right)
.

(3)

U\alpha_{1}(E_{i})U^{*}=E_{i+1}
,

It follows from
[
8

that

is a non-empty set. (It is assumed in
[
8

[
25

by setting
\left(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n};U\right)\leq\left(E_{1}^{\prime},E_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,E_{n}^{\prime};U^{\prime}\right)

\mathcal{F}
be a

ordered subset of

defined by

\mathcal{O}\left(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n};U\right)=\tau\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\right)

is order-preserving. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
contains a cofinal sequence

y_{m}=\left\{\left(E_{1,m},E_{2,m},\ldots,E_{n,m};U_{m}\right)\right\}.

Using (2), we see that

\displaystyle\left\|U_{m+1}-U_{m}\right\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon\tau\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i,m+1}-E_{i,m}\right),

and hence,
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|U_{m+1}-U_{m}\|_{1}<\infty
. Since
\mathcal{U}(N)

is complete with respect to the
\|.\|_{1}
-norm, we see that
\lim_{m\to\infty}U_{m}=U

exists and is in

. Moreover,
\{E_{i,m}\}_{m}

for each

i=1,2,\ldots,n
. We now observe that
\left(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n};U\right)

satisfies (1), (2) and (3). Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
admits a maximal element,
which we denote by
\left(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n};U\right)
, by mild abuse of
notation. We claim that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}=1
. Suppose not. Set

P=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\neq 0
.
From (3), it follows that
U\alpha_{1}(P)U^{*}=P
. Therefore,

\tilde{\alpha}=\text{Ad}(U)\circ\alpha_{1}
leaves
PNP
invariant. Moreover,

\tilde{\alpha}
also acts freely on

Denote

Using
[

\left\|\tilde{U}-P\right\|_{1,PNP}<\varepsilon\tau_{P\mathcal{N}P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\right)\text{ and
}\tilde{U}\tilde{\alpha}(P_{j})\tilde{U}^{*}=P_{j+1},\penalty\ j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Consequently, we observe that
\tilde{U}U\alpha_{1}(P_{i})U^{*}\tilde{U}^{*}=P_{i+1}

for

\displaystyle\left\|V+(1-P)-1\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\left\|U^{\prime}-1\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-E_{j+1}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-E_{j+1}\right\|\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-E_{j+1}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-E_{j+1}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=2\left\|\alpha_{1}(E_{j})-U\alpha_{1}(E_{j})U^{*}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\|U-1\|_{1}=4\varepsilon,\penalty\ j=1,2,\ldots,n.

(PAP)^{\prime}\cap(PA^{**}P)^{\omega}\subseteq(P(A\otimes D)P)^{\prime}\cap(PA^{**}P)^{\omega}
, it suffices to show

(PAP)^{\prime}\cap(PAP,\varphi|_{PAP})_{\omega}\subseteq(P(A\otimes D)P)^{\prime}\cap(PAP,\varphi|_{PAP})_{\omega}
. To this end, let
(c_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{N}},PAP)
such that

\lim_{n\to\omega}\|c_{n}a-ac_{n}\|^{\#}_{\varphi|_{PAP}}=0,\,\,\,\forall a\in A.

Pick
(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{N}},A)
such that
c_{n}=Pz_{n}P
for
z_{n}\in A
.
Let
y\in P(A\otimes D)P
, we show

\lim_{n\to\omega}\|c_{n}y-yc_{n}\|^{\#}_{\varphi|{P(A\otimes D)P}}=0.

For this, set
M:=\text{sup}\{\|z_{n}\|,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, which we may assume is nonzero; otherwise there is nothing to prove. Take
\varepsilon>0
and let
\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})\in A\otimes D
such that

\left\|y-P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4M}.

Now take
L=\text{max}\{\|d_{j}\|,\,j=1,2,\ldots,k\}
and
S=\text{max}\{\|c_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}c_{n}\|^{1/2},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\,k=1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Now choose
W\in\omega
such that for all
n\in W
and for
j=1,2,\ldots,k
we have

\|c_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}c_{n}\|^{1/2}_{\varphi|_{PAP}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2kSL}.

Note that for
x\otimes y\in A\otimes_{\text{min}}D
we have

\begin{split}\|P(x\otimes y)P\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}^{2}&=|\varphi(P(x^{*}x\otimes y^{*}y)P)|\\
&=|\varphi(P(1\otimes y^{*}y)PP(x^{*}x\otimes 1)P)|\\
&\leq\varphi(P(x^{*}xx^{*}x\otimes 1)P)^{1/2}\varphi(P(1\otimes y^{*}yy^{*}y)P)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\|P(x\otimes 1)P\|\|y\|^{2}\|P(x\otimes 1)P\|_{\varphi|_{PAP}}.\end{split}

For any
n\in W
we have

\begin{split}\|[c_{n},y]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}&\leq\|[c_{n},y-P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P]\|+\|[c_{n},P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}\\
&\leq 2\|c_{n}\|\|y-P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P\|+\|[c_{n},P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\|[c_{n},P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|[c_{n},P\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}\otimes d_{j})P]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}&\leq\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}P((z_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}z_{n})\otimes d_{j})P\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|P((z_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}z_{n})\otimes d_{j})P\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|c_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}c_{n}\|^{1/2}\|d_{j}\|\|c_{n}a_{j}-a_{j}c_{n}\|_{\varphi|_{PAP}}^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\end{split}

Thus, for all
n\in W
we have
\|[c_{n},y]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}<\varepsilon
. Similarly, one gets
\|[c_{n},y]^{*}\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}\underset{n\to\omega}{\longrightarrow}0
, hence
\|[c_{n},y]\|_{\varphi|_{P(A\otimes D)P}}^{\#}\underset{n\to\omega}{\longrightarrow}0
.

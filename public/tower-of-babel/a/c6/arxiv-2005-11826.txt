Boundary asymptotics of the relative Bergman kernel metric for curves
Robert Xin Dong

Abstract
We study the behaviors of the relative Bergman kernel metrics on holomorphic families of degenerating hyperelliptic Riemann surfaces and their Jacobian varieties. Near a node or cusp, we obtain precise asymptotic formulas with explicit coefficients. In general the Bergman kernels on a given cuspidal family do not always converge to that on the regular part of the limiting surface, which is different from the nodal case. It turns out that information on both the singularity and the complex structure contributes to various asymptotic behaviors of the Bergman kernel. Our method involves the classical Taylor expansion for Abelian differentials and period matrices.

The spaces of degenerating Riemannn surfaces correspond to paths in the moduli space leading to the boundary points, and are obtained from compact surfaces by shrinking finitely many closed loops to points, called nodes. Near nodal singularities of general curves, Wentworth

Our first motivation is to write down explicitly the asymptotic coefficients, which involve the complex structure information and reflect the geometry of the base varieties and their singularities,
for various families of hyperelliptic curves degenerating to singular ones with nodes or cusps.
Our second motivation is to investigate whether similar convergence results hold true for the Bergman kernel on cuspidal degenerating families of curves, in comparison with

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section 1, we state our main results on the degeneration of the Bergman kernel. In Section 2, we study the Bergman kernel on the normalization of general algebraic curves. In Sections 3–4, we work on nodal curves. In Sections 5–6 and 7–8, we work on cuspidal curves of types I and II, respectively. Our results on the Jacobian varieties are proved in Section 9.

1
Main results

Throughout this paper, a smooth algebraic curve means a Riemann surface, and we consider the complex analytic families of hyperelliptic curves
X_{\lambda}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,y^{2}=h_{\lambda}(x)P(x)\}
of genus
g\geq 2
, parameterized by
\lambda\in\Delta^{*}
.
Here,
P
denotes a polynomial of degree
2g-2
with complex roots

such that
1<|a_{1}|<|a_{2}|<\cdots<|a_{2g-2}|
.
For each
\lambda\in\Delta^{*}
,

is a degree
3
polynomial with distinct roots of absolute values less than or equal to
1

X_{\lambda}
there exist globally defined Abelian differentials

\omega_{i}:=\frac{x^{i-1}dx}{y},\,i=1,\ldots,g.

(1.1)

By its classical construction, the hyperelliptic curve
X_{\lambda}
can be realized as a 2-sheeted ramified covering of the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(see
[
14
]
). Take a homology basis
\delta_{i}
and

of
H_{1}(X_{\lambda},\mathbb{Z})
such that their intersection numbers are
\delta_{i}\cdot\delta_{j}=0=\gamma_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}

\delta_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}=\delta_{i,j}=-\gamma_{j}\cdot\delta_{i}
(here
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker
\delta
).
Then, for the above
\omega_{i}\in H^{0}(X_{\lambda},\mathcal{K})
, we set

A_{i,j}:=\int_{\delta_{j}}\omega_{i},\quad B_{i,j}:=\int_{\delta_{j}}\gamma_{i}.

(1.2)

By the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relation, the matrix
(A_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq g}
is invertible and define the normalized period matrix
Z:=A^{-1}B
. Then,
Z
is symmetric and has a positive definite imaginary part, i.e.,
\Imagine Z>0.
With respect to the chosen homology basis, we call the matrices
A,B
and
Z
, the
A
-period matrix,
B
-period matrix, and normalized period matrix, respectively.
Moreover, we use the symbol “
\sim
” to denote that the ratio of its both sides tends to
1
, as
\lambda\to 0
; in the case of matrices, it refers to the entrywise ratio.

2
Bergman kernel on hyperelliptic curves

The Bergman kernel, defined in (
0.1
), is independent of the choices of the orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
holomorphic top-forms.
We consider the complex analytic families of hyperelliptic curves
X_{\lambda}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,y^{2}=h_{\lambda}(x)P(x)\}
of genus
g\geq 2
, parameterized by
\lambda\in\Delta^{*}
.
It is known (cf.
[

]

\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}

\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{g}((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{i,j}\,\omega_{i}\otimes\overline{\omega_{j}},

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{X_{\lambda}}\omega_{i}\wedge\overline{\omega_{j}}=(\Imagine Z)_{ij},

(2.1)

which is independent of the choice of the homology basis. Since each

Nodal family

A nodal Riemann surface
X
is a connected complex space such that every point
p\in X
has a neighborhood isomorphic to either a disk in
\mathbb{C}
or
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,|x|,|y|<1\,,xy=0\}

degenerates to a singular curve
X_{0}
with a non-separating node.
In the above
z
-coordinate,

\omega_{i}=\frac{2z^{2(i-1)}dz}{\sqrt{(z^{2}-\lambda)(z^{2}-1)P(z^{2})}},\,i=1,\ldots,g,

and the Bergman kernel
\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}
can be written as

\sum_{i,j=1}^{g}((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{i,j}(z^{i}\overline{z}^{j})^{2}\,\frac{4dz\otimes d\bar{z}}{|z^{4}(z^{2}-\lambda)(z^{2}-1)P(z^{2})|}=:{k}_{\lambda}(z)dz\otimes d\bar{z}.

(2.2)

Particularly, if
P=(x-a)(x-b)
, then (
2.2
) reduces to

4\frac{((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{1,1}+((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{1,2}\overline{z}^{2}+((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{2,1}{z}^{2}+((\Imagine Z)^{-1})_{2,2}|z|^{4}}{|(z^{2}-1)(z^{2}-a)(z^{2}-b)(z^{2}-\lambda)|}dz\otimes d\bar{z}.

(2.3)

The pinching coordinate could be used for general (possibly non-hyperelliptic) algebraic curves degenerating to a nodal curve, which represents a boundary point of the Deligne-Mumford compactification of moduli space (see

For a compact Riemann surface of genus

In this section, we consider a family of genus two curves

(3.1)

where

is an elliptic curve

given in (

). Let

c_{1}:=\int_{1}^{a}\frac{\sqrt{ab}dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}

be a constant depending on

. The Bergman kernels on

and on the normalization of

are denoted by
\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}
and

Theorem 3.1
.

For
X_{\lambda}
defined by (
3.1
), as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}\to\kappa_{0}
;

for small

,

\log{k}_{\lambda}(z)-\log{k}_{0}(z)\sim\frac{\pi}{-\log|\lambda|}{\frac{\Imagine c+({z}^{2}+\overline{z}^{2})\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}}{|z|^{4}}}.

In fact, the results in Section 4 on general hyperelliptic curves largely rely on our proofs in this section. To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need the following two lemmata by analyzing the asymptotics of the
A
-period matrix and
B
-period matrix on

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-2\pi}{\sqrt{ab}}&c_{2}\\
0&\frac{-2c_{1}}{\sqrt{ab}}\end{array}\right)+\Hi(\lambda),

where

c_{2}:=\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}
and
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{\Hi(\lambda)}{\lambda}
is a finite matrix.

Proof.

To estimate the four entries of
A
one by one, we use the choice of cycles
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
specified in Section 2. Firstly,
A_{1,1}=\int_{\delta_{1}}\omega_{1}
, where
\delta_{1}
only contains
0
and
\lambda
. By the substitutions
t={\lambda}^{-1}
and
s={x}^{-1}
, we get the dual cycle
\tilde{\delta}_{1}
which contains
\{\infty,t\}
so that
-\tilde{\delta}_{1}
contains
\{0,1,{a}^{-1},{b}^{-1}\}
, for
|s|\in(1,|t|)
. As
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{-s^{-2}ds}{\sqrt{s^{-1}(s^{-1}-t^{-1})(s^{-1}-1)(s^{-1}-a)(s^{-1}-b)}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{-\sqrt{s}\sqrt{t}ds}{\sqrt{ab(s-1)(s-a^{-1})(s-b^{-1})(s-t)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{\sqrt{s}ds}{\sqrt{-ab(s-1)(s-a^{-1})(s-b^{-1})}}\left(1+\frac{s}{2t}+\Hi\left(\frac{s^{2}}{{t}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{ds}{-s\sqrt{-ab}}\left(1+\frac{s}{2t}+\Hi\left(\frac{s^{2}}{{t}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{ds}{-s\sqrt{-ab}}\left(1+\frac{s}{2t}+\Hi(\frac{s^{2}}{{t}^{2}})\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s}^{-1}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-\sqrt{-ab}}\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\left(1+\Hi(t^{-1})\right)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{-\sqrt{ab}}\left(1+\Hi({\lambda})\right).

Notice that we have used the Maclaurin expansion

\frac{1}{\sqrt{s-a}}=\begin{cases}{\sqrt{-a}^{-1}}\left(1+\Hi\left({s}{{a^{-1}}}\right)\right),&|s|<|a|;\\
\sqrt{s}^{-1}\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}a}})\right),&|s|>|a|.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Secondly, look at
A_{21}=\int_{\delta_{1}}\omega_{2}
and similarly it holds that

\displaystyle A_{21}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\frac{ds}{-s^{2}\sqrt{-ab}}\left(1+\frac{s}{2t}+\Hi\left(\frac{s^{2}}{{t}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(1+\Hi(s^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-\sqrt{-ab}}\int_{-\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2t}+\Hi(t^{-2})\right)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{-\sqrt{ab}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}+\Hi(\lambda^{2})\right).

Thirdly, since
\delta_{2}
contains
\{0,\lambda,1,a\}
, as
\lambda\to 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle A_{12}=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{2x}+\Hi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\Hi\left(\lambda\right)\right).

Lastly,

A_{22}=\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\Hi(\lambda)\right)=-2\int_{1}^{a}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\Hi(\lambda)\right).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

B\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-2\log\lambda}{\sqrt{-ab}}&d_{1}\\
\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-ab}}&d_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
d_{1}:=-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}
and
d_{2}:=-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}.

Proof.

As
t\to\infty
, we make use of the following computations (cf.
[
14
]
).

\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s\sqrt{s-t}}=\left.\frac{-2}{\sqrt{t}}\sqrt{-1}\log\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{s}}+\sqrt{\frac{t}{s}-1}\right)\right|_{1}^{t}\sim\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\sqrt{t}}\log t.

(3.3)

\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{2}\sqrt{s-t}}=\left.\frac{\sqrt{s-t}}{ts}\right|_{1}^{t}+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s\sqrt{s-t}}=\frac{-\sqrt{1-t}}{t}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2t\sqrt{t}}\log t\sim-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\sqrt{t}}.

(3.4)

In particular, (
3.4
) yields the boundedness of

\int_{1}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{s\sqrt{s-t}}\Hi(s^{-1})ds.

More generally, for any integer
\alpha\geq 1
, as
t\to\infty
, it holds that

\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{\alpha+1}\sqrt{s-t}}=\left.\frac{\sqrt{s-t}}{\alpha ts^{\alpha}}\right|_{1}^{t}+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha t}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{\alpha}\sqrt{s-t}}\sim-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\alpha\sqrt{t}}.

(3.5)

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we use the choice of cycles
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
specified in Section 2 to estimate the four entries of
B
one by one.
Firstly, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), for
|s|\in(1,|t|)
,

\displaystyle B_{11}=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\lambda}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{t}^{1}\frac{-\sqrt{s}\sqrt{t}ds}{\sqrt{ab(s-1)(s-a^{-1})(s-b^{-1})(s-t)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{1}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{s}\sqrt{t}ds}{\sqrt{(s-t)ab}}\frac{1}{s\sqrt{s}}\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s\sqrt{s-t}}\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{-2\log\lambda}{\sqrt{-ab}},

as
\lambda\to 0
.
Secondly, by (
3.4
),

B_{21}=-\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{2}\sqrt{s-t}}\left(1+\Hi({{s^{-1}}})\right)\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-ab}}.

Finally, similar to
A_{12}
,

B_{12}\sim-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}},

and

B_{22}\sim-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}.

∎

Combining Lemmata
3.2
and
3.3
with (
2.3
), we get the asymptotics of the Bergman kernels.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Notice that the period is defined as

c:=\frac{\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}}{\int_{1}^{a}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)}}}=\tau\left(\frac{1-b}{1-a}\right),

(3.6)

where
\tau(\cdot)
is the inverse of the elliptic modular lambda function.
On the normalization of the nodal curve
X_{0}
, by (
2.8
), in the local coordinate
z=\sqrt{x}
, the Bergman kernel is exactly

\kappa_{0}=k_{0}(z)dz\otimes d\bar{z}
, where

k_{0}(z)=\frac{4|z|^{2}}{(\Imagine c)|(z^{2}-1)(z^{2}-a)(z^{2}-b)|}.

(3.7)

By Lemma
3.2
, as
\lambda\to 0
,

A^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-2\pi}{\sqrt{ab}}&c_{2}\\
\Hi(\lambda)&\frac{-2c_{1}}{\sqrt{ab}}\end{array}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\sqrt{ab}}{2\pi}&\frac{\sqrt{ab}c_{2}}{{2\pi d_{2}}}\\
\Hi(\lambda)&\frac{\sqrt{ab}}{-2c_{1}}\end{array}\right).

Let
Z=A^{-1}B
denote the normalized period matrix of
X_{\lambda}
. Then, it follows that

Z\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\log\lambda&\frac{abc_{2}d_{2}+\sqrt{ab}{2c_{1}d_{1}}}{{-4\pi c_{1}}}\\
{-\sqrt{-1}}{c_{1}}^{-1}&c\end{array}\right),\quad\Imagine Z\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\log|\lambda|}{\pi}&-\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}\\
-\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}&\Imagine c\end{array}\right).

So, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

(\Imagine Z)^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\pi}{-\log|\lambda|}&\frac{-\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}\pi}{(\log|\lambda|)\Imagine c}\\
\frac{-\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}\pi}{(\log|\lambda|)\Imagine c}&({\Imagine c})^{-1}\end{array}\right).

By (
2.3
) and (
3.7
),

\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}\to\kappa_{0}.
Moreover, in the local coordinate
z=\sqrt{x}
near
(0,0)
,

{k}_{\lambda}(z)-{k}_{0}(z)\sim\frac{4\pi}{|(z^{2}-1)(z^{2}-a)(z^{2}-b)(z^{2}-\lambda)|}\frac{1+\frac{\Real\{c_{1}^{-1}\}}{\Imagine c}({z}^{2}+\overline{z}^{2})}{-\log|\lambda|},

which yields the conclusion.

∎

Since the moduli space of genus-two curves is 3 dimensional, one may consider the more general family

, or towards one another,

, and move

and

.

Lemma 4.2

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem

\frac{-2\sqrt{-1}}{\sqrt{P(0)}}

Proof.

Firstly, by (

) and (

), for

,

\displaystyle B_{11}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-1)\cdot P(x)}}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda}^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-P(0)}}(1+\Hi(x))

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}{\sqrt{-1}}\log\lambda,

and

For Column

to get that

which is exactly the same as the corresponding entry of

when

∎

Now we will give a proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

\Imagine(A_{0}^{-1}\star)&\Imagine Z_{0}\end{array}\right),

Theorem 5.1

For

Lemma 5.2

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem

and

where the third equality holds due to the

\displaystyle A_{21}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\lambda^{1/4}}{-\sqrt{ab}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u-1)du}{\sqrt{u(u-1)(u-2)}}\left(1+\Hi\left(\sqrt{\lambda}(u-1)\right)\right)

contain only

and

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}=:c_{6}.

Lastly,

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\Hi\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{x}\right)\right)

Lemma 5.3

c_{7}\lambda^{3/2}&\frac{-2c_{3}}{\sqrt{ab}}\end{array}\right),

where
c_{7}:=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{v^{-1}-1}dv
and
c_{3},c_{6}
are the same as in Lemma
5.2
.

Proof.

Letting
{x=\lambda^{2}(1-v)}
,
we consider the integrals

\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-\sqrt{-1}\lambda^{-1}dv}{\sqrt{v(v-1)(v-1+\lambda^{-1})}}\sim\frac{-\lambda^{-1}}{2\sqrt{-1}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dv}{v\lambda^{-1/2}}=\frac{-\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}},

where
\mathcal{C}
is a large cycle containing
0,1
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{xdx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\lambda}{\sqrt{-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(v-1)dv}{\sqrt{v(v-1)(v-1+\lambda^{-1})}}\sim\frac{-\lambda^{3/2}}{\sqrt{-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{v-1}{v}}dv.

We estimate the four entries one by one. Firstly, let
\delta_{1}
only contain
0
and
\lambda^{2}
, and similar to the proof of Lemma
5.2
we know that

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-a}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-b}}\left(1+\frac{x}{2a}+\Hi(x^{2})\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{2b}+\Hi(x^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-2}{-\sqrt{ab}}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-2\pi}{\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Secondly,

A_{21}\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{xdx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{ab}}\frac{\lambda^{3/2}}{\sqrt{-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{v-1}{v}}dv=:c_{7}\lambda^{3/2}.

Thirdly, let
\delta_{2}
contain only
0,\lambda,\lambda^{2}
and
a
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle A_{12}=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})(x-a)(x-b)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}\left(1+\Hi\left(\frac{\lambda}{x}\right)\right)\left(1+\Hi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}=:c_{6},

Lastly,

A_{22}\sim\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}.

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
7.1
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

B\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{2\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{\lambda}}&d_{3}\\
\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\sqrt{-\lambda}&d_{4}\end{array}\right),

where
d_{3},d_{4}
are the same as in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

By
[
25
]
or (
3.3
), it is known that, as
\lambda\to 0
,

\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_{\lambda}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u(u-1)(u-\lambda)}}\sim\frac{\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Firstly, let
\gamma_{1}
contain only
\lambda
and
\lambda^{2}
, and we get that

\displaystyle B_{11}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})(x-a)(x-b)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{-\sqrt{ab}}\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}\left(1+\Hi(x)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Secondly, by (
3.4
) and the substitutions
t={\lambda^{-1}}
and
x=\lambda{s^{-1}}
, as
\lambda\to 0
,

B_{21}\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{xdx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})}}=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{ab}}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{2}\sqrt{s-t}}\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{ab}}\sqrt{-\lambda}.

Thirdly,

\displaystyle B_{12}=

\displaystyle-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-\lambda)(x-\lambda^{2})(x-a)(x-b)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx\left(1+\Hi\left({\lambda}{x^{-1}}\right)\right)\left(1+\Hi\left({\lambda^{2}}{x^{-1}}\right)\right)}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}:=d_{3}.

Lastly,

B_{22}\sim-2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-a)(x-b)}}:=d_{4}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

On the regular part of the cuspidal curve
X_{0}
, (
5.3
) gives the formula for the Bergman kernel
\kappa_{0}=k_{0}(z)|dz|^{2}
in the local coordinate
z=\sqrt{x}
. By Lemma
7.2
, as
\lambda\to 0
,

A^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-2\pi}{\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{\lambda}}&c_{6}\\
c_{7}\lambda^{3/2}&\frac{-2c_{3}}{\sqrt{ab}}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\sim\frac{ab\sqrt{\lambda}}{{4\pi}{}{c_{3}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-2c_{3}}{\sqrt{ab}}&-c_{6}\\
-c_{7}\lambda^{3/2}&\frac{-2\pi}{\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{\lambda}}\end{array}\right).

Let
Z=A^{-1}B
denote the normalized period matrix of
X_{\lambda}
. Then, it follows that

Z\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\pi}&\frac{{{ab}\sqrt{\lambda}}}{4\pi c_{3}}\left(\frac{-2c_{3}}{\sqrt{ab}}d_{3}-c_{6}d_{4}\right)\\
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{-1}}c_{3}^{-1}&\tau\end{array}\right),\quad\Imagine Z\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\log|\lambda|}{\pi}&\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
-\Real\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}c_{3}^{-1}\right\}&\Imagine\tau\end{array}\right)

So, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,

(\Imagine Z)^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\pi}{-\log|\lambda|}&\frac{1}{\log|\lambda|}\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
\frac{-\pi}{\log|\lambda|\Imagine\tau}\Real\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}c_{3}^{-1}\right\}&({\Imagine\tau})^{-1}\end{array}\right).

By (
2.7
) and (
5.3
),

\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}\to\kappa_{0}.
Moreover, in the local coordinate
z=\sqrt{x}
near
(0,0)
,

{k}_{\lambda}(z)-{k}_{0}(z)\sim\frac{4\pi}{|(z^{2}-a)(z^{2}-b)(z^{2}-\lambda)(z^{2}-\lambda^{2})|}\cdot\frac{1+\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})}{-\log|\lambda|},

which yields the conclusion.

∎

8
Cusp II: hyperelliptic curves

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
. When
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g-2}\}
,
X_{\lambda}
defined in (
1.5
) has genus
g
. To prove Theorem
1.3
, we use the choice of cycles
\delta_{j},\gamma_{j}
specified in Section 2, and need to analyze on
X_{\lambda}
the asymptotics of its
A
-period matrix and
B
-period matrix, denoted by
A
and
B
, respectively. Meanwhile, for the normalization
Y:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,y^{2}=xP(x)\}
of genus
g-1
, denote its
A
-period matrix and
B
-period matrix by
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
, respectively.

Lemma 8.1
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.3
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

A\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda P(0)}}&\Hi(1)\\
*&A_{0}\\
\end{array}\right),

where
**
is a column vector with
g-1
rows whose entries are at most
\Hi(\lambda^{3/2}).

Proof.

Firstly, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}(1+\Hi(x))

\displaystyle\sim-2\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda P(0)}}.

A_{21}\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}\frac{xdx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\frac{-\lambda^{3/2}}{\sqrt{-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{v-1}{v}}dv.

Here we change the variable by letting
x=\lambda^{2}(1-v)
. In general, for
2\leq i\leq g
,

A_{i1}\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\frac{-\lambda^{3/2}}{\sqrt{-1}}\int_{0}^{1}{\lambda}^{2i-4}(1-v)^{i-2}\sqrt{\frac{v-1}{v}}dv=\Hi(\lambda^{2i-2.5}).

Secondly, let
\delta_{2}
contain only
0,\lambda^{2},\lambda
and
a_{1}
(but
a_{2},\ldots,a_{2g-2}
). Then,

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})P(x)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{xP(x)}}\left(1+\Hi\left(\frac{{\lambda}}{{x}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{xP(x)}}.

In general, for
A_{i2}
, there is an extra
x^{i-1}
in the original integrand above, so

A_{i2}\sim\int_{\delta_{2}}\frac{x^{i-2}dx}{\sqrt{xP(x)}}.

Thirdly, for
j\geq 2
, let
\delta_{j}
contain only
0,\lambda^{2},\lambda,a_{1},\ldots,a_{2j-3}
(but
a_{2j-2},\ldots,a_{2g-2}
). In general, the entry
A_{ij}
is asymptotic to the same integrand along
\delta_{j}
instead of along
\delta_{2}
, i.e.,

A_{ij}\sim\int_{\delta_{j}}\frac{x^{i-2}dx}{\sqrt{xP(x)}},

which is exactly the same as the corresponding entry of
A_{0}
when
i\geq 2
.

∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.3
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, it holds that

B\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}&\Hi(1)\\
\heartsuit&B_{0}\end{array}\right),

where
\heartsuit
is a column vector whose entries are
\frac{-2\lambda^{i-2}\sqrt{-\lambda}}{\sqrt{P(0)}}
, for
2\leq i\leq g
.

Proof.

\displaystyle B_{11}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})\cdot P(x)}}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}(1+\Hi(x))

\displaystyle\sim-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-2}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\frac{\sqrt{-1}\log\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Thus, for
2\leq i\leq g
, by (
3.5
),

\displaystyle B_{i1}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{x^{i-1}dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}(1+\Hi(x))

\displaystyle\sim-2\int_{\lambda^{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{x^{i-1}dx}{\sqrt{x(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{P(0)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda^{i-2}}{\sqrt{P(0)}}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{ds}{s^{i}\sqrt{s-t}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-2\lambda^{i-2}\sqrt{-\lambda}}{\sqrt{P(0)}},

where the last equality holds due to the substitutions
t={\lambda^{-1}}
and
x=\lambda s^{-1}
.
For Column
j
,
2\leq j\leq g
, we use Taylor expansion of
\sqrt{(x-{\lambda})(x-{\lambda}^{2})}^{-1}
to get that

B_{ij}\sim-2\int_{a_{2j-3}}^{a_{2j-2}}\frac{x^{i-2}dx}{\sqrt{xP(x)}}.

which is exactly the same as the corresponding entry of
B_{0}
when
i\geq 2
.

∎

Now we will give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
8.1
and the block matrix inversion, we know that

A^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda P(0)}}{2\pi}&\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
\Hi(\lambda^{2})&A_{0}^{-1}\end{array}\right),

where both
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{(\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}))^{T}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}
and
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{\Hi(\lambda^{2})}{\lambda^{2}}
are finite column vectors with
g-1
rows.
Therefore, as
\lambda\to 0
,

Z=A^{-1}B\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{-\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}{\log\lambda}&\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
A_{0}^{-1}\heartsuit&A_{0}^{-1}B_{0}\end{array}\right),\quad\Imagine Z\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{\log|\lambda|}{-\pi}&\Hi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
\Imagine(A_{0}^{-1}\heartsuit)&\Imagine Z_{0}\end{array}\right),

and

(\Imagine Z)^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{-\pi}{\log|\lambda|}&\Hi(({\log|\lambda|})^{-1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\\
(\Imagine Z_{0})^{-1}\Imagine(A_{0}^{-1}\heartsuit)\frac{\pi}{\log|\lambda|}&(\Imagine Z_{0})^{-1}\end{array}\right).

In fact, since
Z
is symmetric, the off-diagonal block matrices in each matrix above concerning
Z
are the transpose of each other.
On the regular part of the cuspidal curve
X_{0}
, the formula for the Bergman kernel
\kappa_{0}=k_{0}(z)|dz|^{2}
is given in
(
2.9
). This together with (
2.6
) will imply that
\kappa_{X_{\lambda}}\to\kappa_{0}
, as
\lambda\to 0
. Moreover, it holds that

{k}_{\lambda}(z)-{k}_{0}(z)\sim\frac{4\pi}{|(z^{2}-\lambda)(z^{2}-\lambda^{2})P(z^{2})|}\frac{1-2\Real\sum\limits_{i=1}^{g-1}\left((\Imagine Z_{0})^{-1}\Imagine(A_{0}^{-1}\heartsuit)\right)_{i}{z}^{2i}}{-\log|\lambda|},

which yields that

\psi-\log{k}_{0}(z)\sim\frac{\pi}{-\log|\lambda|}\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{g-1}((\Imagine Z_{0})^{-1})_{i,j}(z^{i}\overline{z}^{j})^{2}}.

∎

9
Jacobian varieties

We will give a proof of Theorem
1.5
by using the results obtained in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
. Let
X_{\lambda}
be a compact curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and let
Z
be its period matrix with respect to some chosen homology basis. The Jacobian variety of
X_{\lambda}
, which is denoted by
\text{Jac}(X_{\lambda})
, is then identified with the
g
-dimensional complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}+Z\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. It is well known that the Abel-Jacobi (period) map
X_{\lambda}\to\text{Jac}(X_{\lambda})
is a holomorphic embedding, and the Bergman kernel on a smooth algebraic curve is the pull back of the Euclidean metric from the Jacobian variety via this map.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By definition (
0.1
), the Bergman kernel on
\text{Jac}(X_{\lambda})
can be written as
\mu_{\lambda}(dw^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw^{g})\otimes(d\overline{w^{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\overline{w^{g}})
, under the coordinate
(w^{1},\ldots,w^{g})
induced from
\mathbb{C}^{g}
, where
\mu_{\lambda}=(\det(\Imagine Z))^{-1}
. After adding a one-point compactification at
\infty
, one may assume that the curve
X_{\lambda}
is compact.
For
X_{\lambda}
defined in (
1.3
) or (
1.5
), as
\lambda\to 0
, it holds that

\det(\Imagine Z)=\frac{\log|\lambda|}{-\pi}\det(\Imagine Z_{0})+\Hi(1)\to+\infty,

which yields the first part of the conclusion in Theorem
1.5
. For
X_{\lambda}
defined in (
1.4
),
more careful analysis in the proof of Theorem
1.2
shows that as
\lambda\to 0
,

\Imagine Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1+\Hi(\lambda^{{1}/{2}})&\Hi(\lambda^{{1}/{4}})\\
\Hi(\lambda^{{1}/{4}})&\Imagine Z_{0}+\Hi(\lambda^{{1}/{2}})\end{array}\right),\quad\det(\Imagine Z)=\det\Imagine Z_{0}+\Hi(\lambda^{{1}/{2}})<+\infty,

which yields the second part of the conclusion.

∎

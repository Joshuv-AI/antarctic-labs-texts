A generalization of van der Corput’s difference theorem with applications to recurrence and multiple ergodic averages
\gname Sohail \sname Farhangi

Abstract
We prove a generalization of van der Corput’s difference theorem for sequences of vectors in a Hilbert space. This generalization is obtained by establishing a connection between sequences of vectors in the first Hilbert space with a vector in a new Hilbert space whose spectral type with respect to a certain unitary operator is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We use this generalization to obtain applications regarding recurrence and multiple ergodic averages when we have measure preserving automorphisms T T and S S that do not necessarily commute, but T T has a maximal spectral type that is mutually singular with the Lebesgue measure.

Let

(2.1)

(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{SA}(\mathcal{H})
is spectrally Lebesgue, and
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is bounded and spectrally singular, then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}f_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(2.25)

In particular, we have that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(2.26)

Proof 5
.

Firstly, we observe that the smallest subspace of
\mathcal{H}
containing
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is separable, so it is unitarily isomorphic to a subspace of
L^{2}([0,1],\mathcal{L})
, hence we may assume without loss of generality that
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{SA}(L^{2}([0,1],\mathcal{L})
. Next, let
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{SA}(L^{2}(X,\mathcal{L}))
given by
g_{n}=c_{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]}
, and observe that for any permissible triple
P=((c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty})
and
h\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle g_{n+h},g_{n}\rangle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n+h}\overline{c_{n}},

(2.27)

so
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is spectrally singular. We may now use Lemma
2.5
to see that

0=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}g_{n}\right|\right|=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}f_{n}\right|\right|.

(2.28)

For the latter half of the theorem, it suffices to observe that the sequence
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
given by
c_{n}=1
for all
n
will always have
\delta_{0}
as its spectral measure, and is consequently a spectrally singular sequence.

This shows us that the following theorem, which is the main result of this section, is indeed a generalization of Theorem
1.2

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{SA}(\mathcal{H})
. If

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle f_{n+h},f_{n}\rangle\right|^{2}<\infty

(2.29)

for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
, then
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is spectrally Lebesgue.

Proof 6
.

Let
P:=\left((f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\right)
be a permissible triple and
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}(P)
. Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
[0,1]
for which

\hat{\mu}(h)=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}\langle f_{n+h},f_{n}\rangle=\left\langle(f_{n+h})_{n=1}^{\infty}.(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.

(2.30)

Since the fourier coefficients of
\mu
are square summable, we may define

by

(2.31)

and we see that
\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2\pi ihx}gd\mathcal{L}(x)=\hat{\mu}(h)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2\pi ihx}d\mu(x)

Remark 2.8
.

It is worth mentioning that a sequence
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
being square summable is a sufficient but not necessary condition for the existince of a measure

for which

could, in principle, be generalized by trying to weaken the condition of Equation (

To see that a similar simplification cannot be made in general, we will show that the property of being the Fourier coefficients of a measure

depends on more than just the magnitude of the coefficients. Consider a strongly mixing measure preserving system

that has singular spectrum, such as the one constructed in
[

. Let

be such that

, and observe that

that there exists a measure

Using standard linearity arguments, we now see that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]}(n^{k}\alpha)\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S_{1}^{-n}A\cap S_{2}^{-n}A\cap\cdots\cap S_{k-1}^{n}A\right)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap S_{1}^{-n}A\cap S_{2}^{-n}A\cap\cdots\cap S_{k-1}^{n}A\right)>0,

where the existence of the last limit follows from
[
43

, and the last inequality follows from
[

.
\square

.

2.3
that

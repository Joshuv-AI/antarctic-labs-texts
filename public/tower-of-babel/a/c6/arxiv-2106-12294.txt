Improved convergence rates and trajectory convergence for primal-dual dynamical systems with vanishing damping
Radu Ioan Boţ, Dang-Khoa Nguyen

Abstract
In this work, we approach the minimization of a continuously differentiable convex function under linear equality constraints by a second-order dynamical system with asymptotically vanishing damping term. The system is formulated in terms of the augmented Lagrangian associated to the minimization problem. We show fast convergence of the primal-dual gap, the feasibility measure, and the objective function value along the generated trajectories. In case the objective function has Lipschitz continuous gradient, we show that the primal-dual trajectory asymptotically weakly converges to a primal-dual optimal solution of the underlying minimization problem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result which guarantees the convergence of the trajectory generated by a primal-dual dynamical system with asymptotic vanishing damping. Moreover, we will rediscover in case of the unconstrained minimization of a convex differentiable function with Lipschitz continuous gradient all convergence statements obtained in the literature for Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method.



Key Words.
Augmented Lagrangian method, primal-dual dynamical system, damped inertial dynamics, Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method, Lyapunov analysis, convergence rate, trajectory convergence

1.1
Problem statement and motivation

In this paper we will deal with the optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{rl}\min&f\left(x\right),\\
\textrm{subject to}&Ax=b\end{array}

(1.1)

where

\begin{cases}\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\textrm{ are real Hilbert spaces};\\
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}\textrm{ is a continuously differentiable convex function};\\
A\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}\textrm{ is a continuous linear operator and }b\in\mathcal{Y};\\
\textrm{the set }\mathbb{S}\textrm{ of primal-dual optimal solutions of }\eqref{intro:pb}\textrm{ is assumed to be nonempty}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Problems of type (

The object of our investigations will be a second-order dynamical system with asymptotic vanishing damping term associated with the optimization problem (

Our main aim is to study the asymptotic behaviour of the generated trajectories to a primal-dual optimal solution as well as to derive fast rates of convergence for the primal-dual gap, the feasibility measure, and the objective function value along these.

The interplay between continuous-time dissipative dynamical systems and numerical algorithms for solving optimization problems has been subject of an intense research activity. It is well-known for unconstrained optimization problems that damped inertial dynamics are a natural way to accelerate these systems. In line with the seminal work of Polyak on the heavy ball method with friction
[

\mathrm{AVD}

43
,

1.2
Our contributions

For a primal-dual dynamical system with asymptotically vanishing damping term associated to the augmented Lagrangian formulation of (
1.1
) we will show fast convergence for the primal-dual gap, the feasibility measure, and the objective function value along the generated trajectories, and, consequently, improve existing results in the literature. We will prove the existence and uniqueness of the trajectories as global twice continuously differentiable solutions of the dynamical system provided the gradient of the objective function is Lipschitz continuous. In the same setting, we will also prove that the primal-dual trajectory asymptotically weakly converges to a primal-dual optimal solution of (
1.1
), which is the first result of this type in the literature addressing such dynamical systems.

Last but not least, we will show how the asymptotic analysis and the obtained results can be straightforwardly transferred to continuous-time methods with vanishing damping terms approaching optimization problems with separable objectives and block variables linearly coupled in the constraints. Moreover, we will rediscover in case of the unconstrained minimization of a convex differentiable function with Lipschitz continuous gradient all convergence statements obtained in the literature for Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method introduced in
[
53
,
12
]
.

The primal-dual dynamical approach with vanishing damping

2.1
Augmented Lagrangian formulation

Consider the saddle point problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right)

(2.1)

associated to problem (
1.1
), where
\mathcal{L}\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the Lagrangian function

\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right):=f\left(x\right)+\left\langle\lambda,Ax-b\right\rangle.

Under the assumptions (
1.2
),
\mathcal{L}
is convex with respect to
x\in\mathcal{X}
and affine with respect to
\lambda\in\mathcal{Y}
. A pair
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is said to be a saddle point of the Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}
if for every
\left(x,\lambda\right)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\right)\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda_{*}\right).

(2.2)

If
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
then
x_{*}\in\mathcal{X}
is an optimal solution of (
1.1
), and
\lambda_{*}\in\mathcal{Y}
is an optimal solution of its Lagrange dual problem. If
x_{*}\in\mathcal{X}
is an optimal solution of (
1.1
) and a suitable constraint qualification is fulfilled, then there exists an optimal solution
\lambda_{*}\in\mathcal{Y}
of the Lagrange dual problem such that
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
. For details and insights into the topic of constraint qualifications for convex duality we refer to
[
23
,
25
]
.

The set of saddle points of
\mathcal{L}
, called also primal-dual optimal solutions of (
1.1
), will be denoted by
\mathbb{S}
and, as stated in the assumptions, it will be assumed to be nonempty. The set of feasible points of (
1.1
) will be denoted by
\mathbb{F}:=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}\colon Ax=b\right\}
and the optimal objective value of (
1.1
) by
f_{*}
.

The system of primal-dual optimality conditions for (
1.1
) reads

\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)&=0\\
\nabla_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)&=0\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)+A^{*}\lambda_{*}&=0\\
Ax_{*}-b&=0\end{cases},

(2.3)

where
A^{*}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
denotes the adjoint operator of
A
.

For
\beta\geq 0
, we consider also the augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
associated with (
1.1
)

\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x,\lambda\right):=\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right)+\dfrac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert Ax-b\right\rVert^{2}=f\left(x\right)+\left\langle\lambda,Ax-b\right\rangle+\dfrac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert Ax-b\right\rVert^{2}.

(2.4)

For every
(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{F}\times\mathcal{Y}
it holds

f\left(x\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x,\lambda\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right).

(2.5)

If
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
, then we have for every
\left(x,\lambda\right)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x_{*},\lambda\right)\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda_{*}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x,\lambda_{*}\right).

In addition,

\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)&=0\\
\nabla_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)&=0\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)+A^{*}\lambda_{*}&=0\\
Ax_{*}-b&=0\end{cases}.

(2.6)

2.2
Associated monotone inclusion problem

The optimality system (
2.3
) can be equivalently written as

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)=0,

(2.7)

where

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y},\quad\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(x,\lambda\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right)\\
-\nabla_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}\left(x,\lambda\right)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f\left(x\right)+A^{*}\lambda\\
b-Ax\end{pmatrix},

(2.8)

is the maximally monotone operator associated with the convex-concave function
\mathcal{L}
. Indeed, it is immediate to verify that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
is monotone. Since it is also continuous, it is maximally monotone (see, for instance,
[
23
, Corollary 20.28]
). Therefore
\mathbb{S}
can be interpreted as the set of zeros of the maximally
monotone operator
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
, which means that it is a closed convex subset of
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
(see, for instance,
[
23
, Proposition 23.39]
).

Applying the fast continuous-time approaches recently proposed in
[
19
,
5
]
to the solving of (
2.7
) would require the use of the Moreau-Yosida approximation of the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
, for which in general no close formula is available. The resulting dynamical system would therefore not be formulated in the spirit of the full splitting algorithm, which is undesirable from the point of view of numerical computations.

\displaystyle\ \theta^{2}t^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\beta}\Bigl(\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)\Big|\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\Bigr)+\theta^{2}t^{2}\left\langle\mu-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{x_{*},\mu}\left(t\right)\right\rVert^{2}+\dfrac{\xi}{2}\left\lVert\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)-\left(x_{*},\mu\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t\right)+\theta^{2}t^{2}\left\langle\mu-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle+\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+1+\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right)+\theta^{2}t^{2}\left\langle\mu-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle,

where the last inequality is due to (
3.5
) and (
3.14
). This is nothing else than (
3.19
).
∎

We can now formulate and prove the main convergence rate results of the paper

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\left(x,\lambda\right)\colon\left[t_{0},+\infty\right)\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
be a solution of (

PD

-

AVD

) and
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
. The following statements are true:

\rm{(i)}

for every
t\geq t_{0}
it holds

0\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(t\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(t\right)\right)+\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert\leq\dfrac{C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}},

(3.27)

where

C_{4}:=C_{0}+2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right)>0;

(3.28)

\rm{(ii)}

for every
t\geq t_{0}
it holds

-\dfrac{\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}\leq f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-f_{*}\leq\dfrac{\left(1+\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert\right)C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}.

(3.29)

Proof.

\rm{(i)}

We fix
s\geq t_{0}
and define

\mu\left(s\right):=\begin{cases}\lambda_{*}+\dfrac{Ax\left(s\right)-b}{\left\lVert Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rVert},&\textrm{ if }Ax\left(s\right)-b\neq 0,\\
\lambda_{*},&\textrm{ if }Ax\left(s\right)-b=0.\end{cases}

(3.30)

It is clear that
\mu\left(s\right)\in\mathbb{B}\left(\lambda_{*};1\right)
. For brevity, we set

\sigma:=\dfrac{1-2\theta}{\theta}\geq 0.

Since
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
, we have
\left(x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)\right)\in\mathbb{F}\times\mathbb{B}\left(\lambda_{*};1\right)
. Lemma
3.1
combined with the relation (
3.26
) ensure that for every
t\geq t_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma\theta^{2}t\mathcal{G}_{\beta}\Bigl(\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)\Big|\left(x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)\right)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma\theta^{2}t\left\langle\mu\left(s\right)-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle.

(3.31)

We will prove that for every
t\geq t_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle\theta^{2}t^{2}\Bigl(f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\mu\left(s\right),Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}=C_{0}+2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right).

(3.32)

The first inequality follows from the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}
. To show the later one, we multiply both sides of (
3.31
) by
t^{\sigma}>0
and use integration by parts, for
\sigma>0
, or just integrate (
3.32
), for
\sigma=0
, to deduce that for every
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle t^{\sigma}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t\right)-t_{0}^{\sigma}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t_{0}\right)

\displaystyle-\sigma\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{\sigma-1}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(\tau\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma\theta^{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{\sigma+1}\left\langle\mu\left(s\right)-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(\tau\right)-b\right\rangle d\tau.

(3.33)

By using (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) we further obtain for every
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle t^{\sigma}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ t_{0}^{\sigma}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(t_{0}\right)+\sigma\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{\sigma-1}\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\mu\left(s\right)}\left(\tau\right)d\tau

\displaystyle-\sigma\theta^{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{\sigma+1}\left\langle\mu\left(s\right)-\lambda_{*},Ax\left(\tau\right)-b\right\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ t_{0}^{\sigma}C_{0}+\sigma\left(2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right)\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\tau^{\sigma-1}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ t_{0}^{\sigma}C_{0}+\left(2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right)\right)\bigl(t^{\sigma}-t_{0}^{\sigma}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ t^{\sigma}\bigl(C_{0}+2\mathcal{E}_{x_{*},\lambda_{*}}\left(t_{0}\right)+\theta\left(\alpha-1\right)\bigr),

which is equivalent to (
3.32
).

Now, since (
3.32
) is true for every
t\geq t_{0}
, it is fulfilled also for
t:=s\geq t_{0}
, which means that

\theta^{2}s^{2}\Bigl(f\left(x\left(s\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\mu\left(s\right),Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rangle\Bigr)\leq C_{4}.

By the definition of
\mu\left(s\right)
in (
3.30
), if
Ax\left(s\right)-b\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle f\left(x\left(s\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\mu\left(s\right),Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle f\left(x\left(s\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\lambda_{*},Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rangle+\left\lVert Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(s\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(s\right)\right)+\left\lVert Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rVert,

while, if

, we can also write

\displaystyle f\left(x\left(s\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\mu\left(s\right),Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rangle=f\left(x\left(s\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)+\left\langle\lambda_{*},Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(s\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(s\right)\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(s\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(s\right)\right)+\left\lVert Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rVert.

For both scenarios, the estimate (

) becomes

\theta^{2}s^{2}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(s\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(s\right)\right)+\left\lVert Ax\left(s\right)-b\right\rVert\Bigr)\leq C_{4}.

Since

has been arbitrarily chosen, this gives proves (

).

\rm{(ii)}

Since
\mathcal{L}\left(x\left(t\right),\lambda_{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{*},\lambda\left(t\right)\right)\geq 0
, a direct consequent of (

\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert\leq\dfrac{C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}.

(3.34)

From (

) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we can also deduce for every

that

\displaystyle f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)\leq\dfrac{C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}-\left\langle\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}+\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\left(1+\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert\right)C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}.

(3.35)

On the other hand, the convexity of
f
together with the fact that
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
guarantee for every

\displaystyle f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right),x\left(t\right)-x_{*}\right\rangle=-\left\langle A^{*}\lambda_{*},x\left(t\right)-x_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert\geq-\dfrac{\left\lVert\lambda_{*}\right\rVert C_{4}}{\theta^{2}t^{2}}.

(3.36)

By combining (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) we obtain the desired statement.
∎

Theorem 4.1

For every initial condition

Proof.

We observe that
\left(x,\lambda\right)\colon\left[t_{0},+\infty\right)\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is a solution of (

PD

-

AVD

) if and only if
\left(x,\lambda,y,\nu\right)\colon\left[t_{0},+\infty\right)\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\begin{cases}\dot{x}\left(t\right)=y\left(t\right)\\

\dot{y}\left(t\right)=-\dfrac{\alpha}{t}y\left(t\right)-\nabla f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-A^{*}\left(\lambda\left(t\right)+\theta t\nu\left(t\right)\right)-\beta A^{*}\Bigl(Ax\left(t\right)-b\Bigr)\\

(4.1)

\Bigl(x\left(t_{0}\right),\lambda\left(t_{0}\right),y\left(t_{0}\right),\nu\left(t_{0}\right)\Bigr)=\Bigl(x_{0},\lambda_{0},\dot{x}_{0},\dot{\lambda}_{0}\Bigr)\end{cases}.

Next we will show that

is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets and chose to this end arbitrary

and
\delta>0
. For

\displaystyle\left(t,z,\zeta,u,\rho\right),\left(\widetilde{t},\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{\rho}\right)\in

\displaystyle\left[t_{1},t_{2}\right]\times\mathbb{B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times\mathbb{B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times\mathbb{B}\left(0;\delta\right)\times\mathbb{B}\left(0;\delta\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\left[t_{0},+\infty\right)\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y},

we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert F\left(t,z,\zeta,u,\rho\right)-F\left(\widetilde{t},\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{\rho}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert+

\displaystyle\left\lVert\dfrac{\alpha}{t}u-\dfrac{\alpha}{\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{u}+\nabla f\left(z\right)-\nabla f\left(\widetilde{z}\right)+A^{*}\left(\zeta-\widetilde{\zeta}+\theta\left(t\rho-\widetilde{t}\widetilde{\rho}\right)\right)+\beta A^{*}A\left(z-\widetilde{z}\right)\right\rVert+

\displaystyle\left\lVert\dfrac{\alpha}{t}\rho-\dfrac{\alpha}{\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{\rho}-\Bigl(A\bigl(z-\widetilde{z}+\theta\left(tu-\widetilde{t}\widetilde{u}\right)\bigr)\Bigr)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert+\left(\beta\left\lVert A\right\rVert^{2}+\|A\|+\ell\right)\left\lVert z-\widetilde{z}\right\rVert+\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert\zeta-\widetilde{\zeta}\right\rVert+

\displaystyle\alpha\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{t}u-\dfrac{1}{\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\theta\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert t\rho-\widetilde{t}\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert+\alpha\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{t}\rho-\dfrac{1}{\widetilde{t}}\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert+\theta\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert tu-\widetilde{t}\widetilde{u}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\dfrac{\alpha}{t}+\theta t\left\lVert A\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\left(1+\dfrac{\alpha}{t}+\theta t\left\lVert A\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert+\left(\beta\left\lVert A\right\rVert^{2}+\|A\|+\ell\right)\left\lVert z-\widetilde{z}\right\rVert+

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert\zeta-\widetilde{\zeta}\right\rVert+\alpha\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert\right)\left\lvert\dfrac{1}{t}-\dfrac{1}{\widetilde{t}}\right\rvert+\theta\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\widetilde{\rho}\right\rVert\right)\left\lvert t-\widetilde{t}\right\rvert

(4.3)

Proof.

The determinant role in the proof is the fact that, for every

\ell-
Lipschitz continuous, relation (

\displaystyle\ -\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\beta}\Bigl(\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)\Big|\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\Bigr),\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)-\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle\nabla f\left(x\left(t\right)\right),x_{*}-x\left(t\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle A^{*}\lambda_{*},x_{*}-x\left(t\right)\right\rangle+\beta\left\langle A^{*}\left(Ax\left(t\right)-b\right),x_{*}-x\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -\left(f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-f\left(x_{*}\right)\right)-\dfrac{1}{2\ell}\left\lVert\nabla f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}-\left\langle\lambda_{*},Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rangle-\beta\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)+A^{*}\lambda\left(t\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)-\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)\right\rVert+\left\lVert A^{*}\left(\lambda\left(t\right)-\lambda_{*}\right)\right\rVert=o\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\quad\textrm{as}\quad t\to+\infty,

while Theorem
3.4
gives

\left\lVert\nabla_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)\right\rVert=\left\lVert Ax\left(t\right)-b\right\rVert=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\quad\textrm{as}\quad t\to+\infty.

∎

We are now in the position to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\left(x,\lambda\right)\colon\left[t_{0},+\infty\right)\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
be a solution of (

PD

-

AVD

) and
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
. Then
\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)
converges weakly to a primal-dual optimal solution of (
1.1
) as
t\to+\infty
.

Proof.

We have seen in Lemma
4.4
that the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\left\lVert\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)-\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\right\rVert
exists for every
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
, which proves condition (i) of Opial’s Lemma (see Lemma
A.3
).

In order to prove condition (ii), we consider
\left(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{\lambda}\right)
an arbitrary weak sequential cluster point of
\bigl(x\left(t\right),\lambda\left(t\right)\bigr)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, which means that there exists a sequence
\left\{\left(x\left(t_{n}\right),\lambda\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
such that

\left(x\left(t_{n}\right),\lambda\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\rightharpoonup\left(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{\lambda}\right)\quad\textrm{as}\ n\to+\infty.

Theorem
4.7
and Theorem
3.4
allow us to deduce that

\nabla f\left(x\left(t_{n}\right)\right)+A^{*}\lambda\left(t_{n}\right)\to\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)+A^{*}\lambda_{*}=0\quad\textrm{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

and

Ax\left(t_{n}\right)-b\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\quad n\to+\infty,

respectively. Since the graph of the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
introduced in (
2.8
) is sequentially closed in
\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\right)^{\mathrm{weak}}\times\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\right)^{\mathrm{strong}}
(cf.
[
23
, Proposition 20.38]
), we have that

\begin{cases}\nabla f\left(\widetilde{x}\right)+A^{*}\widetilde{\lambda}&=\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)+A^{*}\lambda_{*}=0\\
A\widetilde{x}-b&=Ax_{*}-b=0\end{cases}.

In other words,
\left(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{\lambda}\right)
belongs to
\mathbb{S}
and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

In case
A:=0

Lemma A.1
.

Let
0<\delta\leq r\leq+\infty
and
h\colon[\delta,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a continuous function. For every
\alpha>1
it holds

\int_{\delta}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\delta}^{t}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)ds\right)dt\leq\dfrac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{\delta}^{r}h\left(t\right)dt.

If
r=+\infty
, then equality holds.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\delta}^{t}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)ds\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta}^{r}\int_{\delta}^{t}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)dsdt=\iint_{\mathcal{A}}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)d\mathcal{A},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\left(s,t\right)\colon\delta\leq t\leq r,\delta\leq s\leq t\right\}=\left\{\left(t,s\right)\colon\delta\leq s\leq r,s\leq t\leq r\right\}.

Thus, by applying Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\delta}^{t}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)ds\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta}^{r}\int_{s}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)dtds=\int_{\delta}^{r}s^{\alpha-1}h\left(s\right)\left(\int_{s}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}dt\right)ds,

from which we get the desired estimate, as

\int_{s}^{r}\dfrac{1}{t^{\alpha}}dt=\dfrac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{s^{\alpha-1}}-\dfrac{1}{r^{\alpha-1}}\right)\leq\dfrac{1}{\left(\alpha-1\right)s^{\alpha-1}}.

If
r:=+\infty,
then the above inequality is an equality.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Consider the optimization problem (
1.1
). If
\nabla f
is
\ell-
Lipschitz continuous,
then for every
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right),\left(x_{**},\lambda_{**}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
it holds

\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)=\nabla f\left(x_{**}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad A^{*}\lambda_{*}=A^{*}\lambda_{**}.

Proof.

Let
\left(x_{*},\lambda_{*}\right),\left(x_{**},\lambda_{**}\right)\in\mathbb{S}
. We have
Ax_{*}=Ax_{**}=b
.
According to the Baillon-Haddad theorem
[
23
, Corollary 18.17]
,
\nabla f
is
\ell^{-1}
-cocoercive, which means

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\ell}\left\lVert\nabla f\left(x_{**}\right)-\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla f\left(x_{**}\right)-\nabla f\left(x_{*}\right),x_{**}-x_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle A^{*}\left(\lambda_{**}-\lambda_{*}\right),x_{**}-x_{*}\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda_{*}-\lambda_{**},Ax_{**}-Ax_{*}\right\rangle=0,

where the first equation comes from (

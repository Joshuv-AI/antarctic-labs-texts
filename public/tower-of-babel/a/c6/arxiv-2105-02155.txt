On smoothing estimates in modulation spaces and the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with slowly decaying initial data
Robert Schippa

Abstract
. We show new local L p L^{p} -smoothing estimates for the Schrödinger equation in modulation spaces via decoupling inequalities. Furthermore, we probe necessary conditions by Knapp-type examples for space-time estimates of solutions with initial data in modulation and L p L^{p} -spaces. The examples show sharpness of the smoothing estimates up to the endpoint regularity in a certain range. Moreover, the examples rule out global Strichartz estimates for initial data in L p ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) for d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and p > 2 p>2 , which was previously known for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . The estimates are applied to show new local and global well-posedness results for the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation on the line. Lastly, we show ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -decoupling inequalities for variable-coefficient versions of elliptic and non-elliptic Schrödinger phase functions.

1.
Introduction

In this article we show new space-time estimates for the Schrödinger equation with slowly decaying data outside
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in X.\end{array}\right.

In the present work we consider modulation spaces
X=M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
2\leq p<\infty
, which are compared to initial data in
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces
X=L_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\langle D\rangle^{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
2\leq p<\infty
. The latter initial data were recently considered by Dodson–Soffer–Spencer
[
23
]
(see also
[
24
]
) and by Mandel
[
45
]
.

Modulation spaces are likewise used to model slowly decaying initial data. Feichtinger introduced modulation spaces in
[
26
]
. He provided a more recent account with
[
27
]
emphasizing the role of modulation spaces in signal processing; see also the textbook by Gröchenig
[
30
]
. As the body of literature is huge, we refer to the PhD thesis of Chaichenets
[
16
]
and references therein for a more exhaustive account on modulation spaces in the context of Schrödinger equations.

For the definition of modulation spaces, consider the Fourier multipliers

(\Box_{k}f)\hat{(}\xi)=\sigma_{k}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

with
(\sigma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
a smooth partition of unity, adapted to the translated unit cubes
Q_{k}=k+[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
. For the precise definition, we refer to
[
16
, Section 2.1]
. We can suppose that
\sigma_{k}(\xi)=\sigma_{0}(\xi-k)
. The norm is defined by

\|f\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}}=\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle k\rangle^{qs}\|\Box_{k}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

If
s=0
, we write
M_{p,q}
. Modulation spaces are closely related with
L^{p}
-spaces. For illustration, we collect embedding properties: By the embedding of
\ell^{p}
-spaces and Bernstein’s inequality, we have

(2)

\displaystyle M^{s}_{p,q_{1}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow M^{s}_{p,q_{2}}\quad(q_{1}\leq q_{2}),

(3)

\displaystyle M^{s}_{p_{1},q}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow M^{s}_{p_{2},q}\quad(p_{1}\leq p_{2}).

Rubio de Francia’s inequality (
[
53
]
) and duality yield

(4)

\displaystyle M_{p,p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow L^{p}\hookrightarrow M_{p,p}\quad(2\leq p\leq\infty),

(5)

\displaystyle M_{p,p}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow L^{p}\hookrightarrow M_{p,p^{\prime}}\quad(1\leq p\leq 2).

Furthermore, we can trade regularity for summability as

(6)

M^{s_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{s_{2}}_{p,q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\qquad s_{1}-s_{2}>d\big(\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{q_{1}}\big)>0

by applying Hölder’s inequality (cf.
[
16
, Proposition 2.31]
). By Plancherel’s theorem,
M_{2,2}\sim L^{2}
.

Let
U(t)=e^{it\Delta}
denote the propagator of (
1
), and let
Uf
denote the free solution for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Our first result are new smoothing estimates

(7)

\|Uf\|_{L^{p}([-1,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

via
\ell^{2}
-decoupling. We prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
d\geq 1
,
p\geq 2
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(A)

If
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
, then
(
7
) holds true provided that
s>\max\big(0,\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q}\big)
.

(B)

If
\frac{2(d+2)}{d}\leq p\leq\infty
and
2\leq q\leq\infty
, then (
7
) holds true provided that
s>d-\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}
.

(C)

If
\frac{2(d+2)}{d}\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q\leq 2
, then (
7
) holds true provided that
s>2\big(1-\frac{1}{q}\big)\big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}\big)
.

(D)

If
q=1
, then (
7
) holds true with
s=0
.

(E)

If
d=1
,
p=4
, and
q=2
, then we find (
7
) to hold for
s=0
.

The key argument in the proof of the estimates for
q\geq 2
are the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequalities for the paraboloid due to Bourgain–Demeter (cf.
[
12
]
). It turns out that after localization in space and parabolic rescaling the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality yields Strichartz estimates in modulation spaces by a kernel estimate. For the proof of (E), we use as substitute for decoupling a reverse square function estimate, which is known to hold without
\varepsilon
-loss. Originally, Wolff
[
66
]
brought up decoupling for the cone to analyze
L^{p}
-smoothing estimates for the (half-)wave equation:

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{p}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We refer to
[
47
,
48
]
and
[
56
, Chapter 8]
for further reading.

Regarding Strichartz estimates in modulation spaces, it seems the above space-time estimates were previously not investigated in the literature. Space-time estimates with an additional window decomposition were shown by B. Wang
et al.

[

,

[

for

[

We shall also compare Strichartz estimates in modulation spaces with
L^{p}
-smoothing estimates for Schrödinger equations, which were first discussed by Rogers

:

(8)

\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Rogers showed that the validity of (

The decoupling inequality can serve as a common base for (

) and (

).

Secondly, we give new necessary conditions for estimates of the kinds

(9)

\|Uf\|_{L^{p}([-1,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and

(10)

\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I;L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{s}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
I\in\{[-1,1],\mathbb{R}\}
. We prove the following necessary conditions:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
. Necessary for (
9
) to hold true is

(11)

s\geq\max\big(0,d-\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}\big).

Necessary for (
10
) to hold for
I=[-1,1]
is

(12)

s\geq\max\big(0,d-\frac{d}{q}-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{r}\big),\quad q\geq r.

If
I=\mathbb{R}
, we have the additional conditions

(13)

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\leq\frac{d}{r}.

We refer to Section
2
for the discussion of further conditions.

Proposition
1.2
shows that the estimates in Theorem
1.1
are sharp up to the endpoint regularity for
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
and for (
\frac{2(d+2)}{d}\leq p\leq\infty
and
2\leq q\leq\infty
). Moreover, the examined examples show that global estimates (
10
) for
I=\mathbb{R}
and
s=0
are impossible for
p>2
. Mandel
[
45
]
previously showed this for
d\geq 2
with a more explicit example.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that
d\geq 1
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and
r\in(2,\infty]
. Then, there is no
C
such that the estimate

(14)

\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds true for any
f\in L_{rad}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A major difference to
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces,
p\neq 2
, is that the propagator
U(t)
is bounded on modulation spaces. Already by Bényi
et al.

[
4
]
was proved the bound for
2\leq p\leq\infty
:

(15)

\|U(t)\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{d\big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big|}.

Chaichenets
[
16
, Section 3.1]
observed by duality that the same bound holds for
1\leq p\leq 2
, and sharpness was initially shown by Cordero–Nicola
[
21
]
. In
[
16
, Section 3.1]
Gaussians were used as window functions in the definition of modulation spaces. Then the sharpness follows by computation of the kernel for a window. In Section
2
we recover the sharpness of (
15
) by the discussed examples.

Corollary 1.4

(
[
16
, Section 3.1]
)
.

The estimate (
15
) is sharp for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We think that this gives a more robust proof of sharpness. In fact, all the results proved in Sections
2
and
3
have straight-forward counterparts for fractional Schrödinger equations

(16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u&=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\end{array}\right.

for
\alpha>1
or non-elliptic Schrödinger equations, e.g.,

(17)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+(\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2})u&=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).\end{array}\right.

The latter follow by considering the decoupling inequalities from
[
13
]
.
Also generalizations to variable coefficients seem possible under additional assumptions.
As decoupling inequalities for variable coefficient versions of (
17
) are not explicit in the literature, we take the opportunity to prove them here. As the results are more technical to state, we refer to Subsection
5.1
. Section

is based on Chapter 7 of the author’s PhD thesis

]
.

We point out that the examples from Section
2
rely on (non-)stationary phase estimates, for which the precise form of the phase function is not important, as long as the characteristic surface has non-vanishing Gaussian curvature.

We apply the Strichartz estimates to the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

(18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=|u|^{2}u\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=f\in D,\end{array}\right.

where
D\in\{L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}),M^{s}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}
. We prefer to work in these slightly larger spaces as the Duhamel term is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Denote the solution space by
S
. For the proof of local well-posedness we apply the
L^{p}
-smoothing estimates to the homogeneous equation and use the usual
L^{2}
-based inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates for the Duhamel integral. We shall see that using modulation spaces allows to save Sobolev regularity: (
18
) has a local solution if
f\in M^{s}_{6,2}
for any
s>0
, but considering
f\in L^{6}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
requires
s>\frac{1}{3}
. Moreover, our local results hold likewise for the focusing equation, see below.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. (
18
) is analytically well-posed in the spaces
D\in\{L^{4}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}),M_{4,2}(\mathbb{R})\}
,
S_{T}=L_{t}^{\frac{24}{7}}([0,T],L^{4}(\mathbb{R}))
, and in the spaces
D\in\{L^{6}_{\frac{1}{3}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}),M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}
,
S_{T}=L_{t}^{3}([0,T],L^{6}(\mathbb{R}))
, i.e., there is
T=T(\|f\|_{D})
such that there is a unique solution in
S_{T}
.

Furthermore, for
D\in\{M_{4,2}(\mathbb{R}),M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}
, we have
u\in C([0,T],D+L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
with continuous dependence on the initial data.

We remark that one could also add regularity in
L^{p}
-spaces to bound the propagator in
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
via fixed-time estimates in order to obtain continuous curves in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. However, one always leaves the space of initial values as the Schrödinger propagator is unbounded in
L^{p}
for
p\neq 2
.

As the NLS is one of the most prominent nonlinear dispersive equations, the body of literature on its well-posedness is vast. To put our results into context, we only mention few results and also refer to the references therein. Tsutsumi

,

, using complete integrability. Outside

-based Sobolev spaces, we mention the early works by Vargas–Vega
[

[

in Fourier Lebesgue spaces. Hyakuna
[

[

considered generalized energy spaces

for

. Chaichenets
et al.

[

showed the first global results in modulation spaces

[

showed how linear Strichartz estimates combined with Galilean invariance and orthogonality arguments yield low regularity well-posedness in the periodic case (see also

Furthermore, Kenig–Ponce–Vega
[

pointed out that the Galilean invariance of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(19)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=-|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\

u_{\omega,N}(x,t)=e^{-it(N^{2}-\omega^{2})}e^{iNx}f_{\omega}(x-2tN),\quad f_{\omega}(x)=\omega f(\omega x),\quad f(x)=\sqrt{2}\text{sech}(x).

. Thus, the corresponding result of Theorem

2.
Necessary conditions

The purpose of this section is to collect necessary conditions to find the following estimates to hold:

(21)

\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}([-1,1],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s}_{r,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and

(22)

\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I;L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We shall use three Knapp-type examples, which are essentially well-known in the literature
[
59
,
52
]
. However, it seems that these have not been examined in the above contexts.

We start with the
anisotropic Knapp example at unit frequencies
, which was previously used to determine the range of integrability coefficients for the
L^{2}
-based Strichartz estimate (
22
) (cf.
[
59
,
58
]
). Consider

(23)

\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=\chi_{(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)}(\xi_{1})\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{2})\ldots\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{d}).

We compute

(24)

\|f\|_{L_{s}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\varepsilon^{d-\frac{d}{r}},\text{ and }\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{M^{s}_{r,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\varepsilon^{d-\frac{d}{r}}

for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq r,t\leq\infty
.

We observe

\begin{split}Ug_{\varepsilon}(x,t)&=C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(x.\xi+t|\xi|^{2})}\chi_{(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)}(\xi_{1})\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{2})\ldots\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{d})d\xi\\
&=C_{d}e^{ix_{1}}e^{it}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i((x+2te_{1}).\xi+t|\xi|^{2})}\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{1})\ldots\chi_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}(\xi_{d})d\xi.\end{split}

Hence,
|Uf(x,t)|\gtrsim\varepsilon^{d}
provided that
t\in[-\varepsilon^{-2},\varepsilon^{-2}]
and
|x+2te_{1}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}
.

Suppose that (
22
) holds true with
I=\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\varepsilon^{d-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}}\lesssim\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s}_{r,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\|f\|_{L^{r}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{d-\frac{d}{r}},

which requires

(25)

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\leq\frac{d}{r}.

For
I=[-1,1]
, we find

\varepsilon^{d-\frac{d}{q}}\lesssim\|Uf\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I;L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s}_{r,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\|f\|_{L^{r}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{d-\frac{d}{r}}.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, this yields
q\geq r
.

The variant of the
anisotropic Knapp-example at high frequencies
rules out gain of derivatives: For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
consider

(26)

\hat{f}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\chi_{(\lambda-1,\lambda+1)}(\xi_{1})\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi_{2})\ldots\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi_{d}).

We note that

(27)

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{M^{s}_{r,t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{r}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sim\lambda^{s}.

Setting
\varepsilon=\lambda^{-1}
, we find

f_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{d}g_{\varepsilon}(\lambda x).

Furthermore, we find by change of variables and the computation for the unit frequency anisotropic Knapp-example:

\|Ug_{\varepsilon}(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L_{t}^{p}([-1,1];L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\lambda^{-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}}\|Ug_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{p}([-\lambda^{2};\lambda^{2}];L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\gtrsim\lambda^{-d}.

By (
27
) the validity of (
22
) or (
21
) requires
s\geq 0
.

Finally, we examine the

Lastly, we show Corollary

, which asserts the sharp time-dependence in the fixed time estimate in modulation spaces.

Proof of Corollary

.

3.

-decoupling implies Strichartz in modulation spaces

In this section we show Theorem

. In the remainder of the section let

and

s(p,d)=\begin{cases}0,&\quad 2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d},\\
\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p},&\quad\frac{2(d+2)}{d}<p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

To conclude Theorem

, it is enough to prove the

\displaystyle\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{s(p,d)+\varepsilon}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(36)

\displaystyle\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{M_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The remaining estimates follow after frequency localization and Hölder’s inequality in the
\ell^{q}
-spaces.

(

) is a consequence of
\ell^{2}
-decoupling. After decoupling, this follows via a kernel estimate. The proof of (

\mathcal{E}f(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(x.\xi+t|\xi|^{2})}f(\xi)d\xi.

Recall the

-decoupling theorem due to Bourgain–Demeter
[
12
]
:

Theorem 3.1

(
\ell^{2}
-decoupling for the paraboloid)
.

Let
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq\{\xi\,:\,|\xi|\leq 4\}
. Then, for any
R\geq 1
, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|\mathcal{E}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{d+1}(0,R))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{s(p,d)}\big(\sum_{\Box:R^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\text{cube}}\|\mathcal{E}f_{\Box}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(w(B(0,R))}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In the above display

denotes a smooth version of the indicator function on
B(0,R)
with high polynomial decay off
B(0,R)
; see Subsection
5.1
for further explanation. We show that Theorem
3.1
implies Strichartz estimates in modulation spaces firstly for frequency localized functions:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\{\xi:\frac{\lambda}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq 4\lambda\}
. Then,
we find the following estimate to hold

(37)

\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times I)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon+s(p,d)}\|f\|_{M_{p,2}}.

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We rescale to unit frequencies

\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}=\lambda^{-\frac{d+2}{p}}\|Ug\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}

with
g(x)=f(x/\lambda)
and
\text{supp}\hat{g}\subseteq B(0,4)
.

Next, we cover
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a finitely overlapping family of
\lambda^{2}
-balls
B\in\mathcal{B}
to write

\|Ug\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}\leq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\|Ug\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}.

We use translation invariance to shift the center of
B\in\mathcal{B}
into the origin:

\|Ug\|_{L^{p}(B\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}=\|Ug_{B}\|_{L^{p}(B(0,\lambda^{2})\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}.

This is amenable to
\ell^{2}
-decoupling:

(38)

\|Ug_{B}\|_{L^{p}(B_{d}(0,\lambda^{2})\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s(p,d)+\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\Box:\lambda^{-1}-\text{cube}}\|Ug_{B,\Box}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(w_{B_{d+1}(0,\lambda^{2}))})}\big)^{1/2}

and by inverting the translation

(39)

\|Ug\|_{L^{p}(B\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s(p,d)+\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\Box:\lambda^{-1}-\text{cube}}\|Ug_{\Box}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B\times[0,\lambda^{2}]})}^{2}\big)^{1/2}.

We sum (
39
) over
B\in\mathcal{B}
in
\ell^{p}
and use Minkowski’s inequality to find

\|Ug\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\lambda^{2}])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s(p,d)+\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\Box:\lambda^{-1}-\text{cube}}\|\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(t)Ug_{\Box}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\big)^{1/2}

with
\chi_{\lambda^{2}}
denoting a rapidly decaying function off
[0,\lambda^{2}]
.

The claim follows by a fixed-time kernel estimate.
We compute the kernel with
a_{\lambda}
denoting the indicator function of the
\lambda^{-1}
-box centered at
\xi_{0}
:

K(x,t)=\int e^{i(x.\xi+t|\xi|^{2})}a_{\lambda}(\xi)d\xi

Via a change of variables and Galilean symmetry, we find

\begin{split}K(x,t)&=\lambda^{-d}e^{ix.\xi_{0}}e^{i\frac{t|\xi_{0}|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\int e^{i\big(\frac{x.\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda}+\frac{2\xi_{0}.\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda}t\big)}e^{it\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}a(\xi)d\xi=\lambda^{-d}e^{ix.\xi_{0}}e^{i\frac{t|\xi_{0}|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\int e^{i\frac{\xi^{\prime}.x}{\lambda}}e^{it\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}a(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}.\end{split}

By non-stationary phase, we find the following estimate for
|t|\leq\lambda^{2}
:

|K(x,t)|\lesssim_{N}\lambda^{-d}(1+\lambda^{-1}|x|)^{-N}.

For
|t|\geq\lambda^{2}
, we have the rough bound:

|K(x,t)|\lesssim_{N}\lambda^{-d}\begin{cases}1,\quad&|x|\lesssim|t|/\lambda,\\
\big(1+\lambda^{-1}|x|\big)^{-N},\quad&|x|\gg|t|/\lambda.\end{cases}

Hence, we find

\|K(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\big(1+\lambda^{-2}|t|\big)^{d}.

Integration in time gives

\|\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(t)Ug_{\Box}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{p}}\|g_{\Box}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We conclude the proof by inverting the change of variables:

\begin{split}\|Uf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1])}&=\lambda^{s(p,d)+\varepsilon-\frac{d+2}{p}}\big(\sum_{\Box:\lambda^{-1}\text{cube}}\|Ug_{\Box}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(w_{B_{d+1}(0,\lambda^{2})})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s(p,d)}\lambda^{-\frac{d}{p}}\big(\sum_{\Box:\lambda^{-1}-\text{cube}}\|g_{\Box}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{\varepsilon+s(p,d)}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\Box_{k}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

∎

In the special case
d=1
,
p=4
we can remove the derivative loss entirely:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

-intervals in Fourier space. Substituting (

By Galilean invariance and a related kernel estimate we prove the following:

.

Let

. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

4.
Solving the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with slowly decaying initial data

In the following we solve the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(45)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in D\end{array}\right.

outside
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces.

4.1.
Local well-posedness of the cubic NLS for slowly decaying initial data

In this subsection we prove new local well-posedness results. The local results do not take advantage of the defocusing effect, and the results in this section also hold for the focusing equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=-|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in D.\end{array}\right.

The smoothing estimates are the key ingredient to estimate the homogeneous solution. In the following we use the terminology due to Bejenaru–Tao
[
2
, Section 3]
. For further reference, we write (
45
) as abstract evolution equation

(46)

u=L(f)+N_{3}(u,u,u),

where
u
takes values in some solution space
S
,
L:D\to S
is a densely defined linear operator, and the trilinear operator
N_{3}:S\times S\times S\to S
is likewise densely defined. As in
[
2
, Section 3]
, we refer to (
46
) as quantitatively well-posed in the spaces
X
,
S
, if the estimates

(47)

\displaystyle\|Lf\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{D},

(48)

\displaystyle\|N_{3}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{S}\|u_{2}\|_{S}\|u_{3}\|_{S}

hold true for all
f\in D
, and
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in S
and some constant
C
. This implies analytic well-posedness (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 3]
) and an expression of the solution in terms of its Picard iterates: We define the nonlinear maps
A_{m}:D\to S
for
m=1,2,\ldots
by the recursive formulae

\displaystyle A_{1}f

\displaystyle=Lf,

\displaystyle A_{m}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\geq 1,\\
m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m\end{subarray}}N_{3}(A_{m_{1}}f,A_{m_{2}}f,A_{m_{3}}f)\text{ for }m>1.

Then we have the homogeneity property

A_{m}(\lambda f)=\lambda^{m}A_{m}(f)\text{ for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\;m\geq 1\text{ and }f\in D,

and the Lipschitz bound derived from (
47
) and (
48
)

\|A_{m}(f)-A_{m}(g)\|_{S}\leq\|f-g\|_{D}C_{1}^{m}\big(\|f\|_{D}+\|g\|_{D}\big)^{m-1}.

Furthermore, we have the absolutely convergent (in
S
) power series expansion

u[f]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{m}(f)

for all
f\in B_{D}(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. In case of (
45
), we have
A_{1}f=Lf=(U(t)f)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and for
m>1
,

if not

for some

.

admits expansion into ternary trees of depth

N_{3}(u,u,u)(t)\in L^{2}
by (
54
) and
(U(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
a
C_{0}
-group in
L^{2}
. For the second term, we use again Strichartz estimates to find

\big\|\int_{t}^{t+\delta}e^{i((t+\delta)-s)\Delta}(|u|^{2}u)(s)ds\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{\delta\in I}L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|^{3}_{L_{t}^{r}(I,L^{p})}.

By multilinearity, we see by similar arguments that for differences of solutions

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{C([0,T],M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}+L^{2})}\to 0

for
\|u(0)-\tilde{u}(0)\|_{M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}+L^{2}}\to 0
provided that
T=T(\|u(0)\|_{M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}+L^{2}},\|\tilde{u}(0)\|_{M^{\varepsilon}_{6,2}+L^{2}})
is chosen small enough, according to the local existence time in
L_{t}^{r}([0,T],L^{p})
. The proof is complete.

∎

We remark that we have some flexibility in the solution space
S=L_{t}^{p}([0,T],L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}))
. For small initial data, we can likewise iterate in
L_{t,x}^{4}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
or
L_{t,x}^{6}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
. For
p>6
, although the sharp linear estimates are still at disposal, it is not clear how to apply inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates as directly as above.

4.2.
Improved local results for slowly decaying data

In this section we consider initial data in
L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
6<p\leq\infty
, and improve the local result due to Dodson–Soffer–Spencer
[
23
]
by passing through modulation spaces. In
[
23
]
the authors used an iteration in
L^{p}
-spaces, which is costly in terms of derivatives because the propagator is not bounded in
L^{p}
-spaces. In
[
23
]
local well-posedness is proved in
L^{4n+2}_{s(n)}
,
n\geq 2
,
s(n)=2n+2
. Note that the number of required derivatives goes to infinity as
n\to\infty
, which is not the case for the improved result in Theorem
4.8
. For simplicity, we focus on the small data case with
T=1
. Additionally, we give a second proof of local well-posedness in
L^{p}
-based Sobolev spaces, which makes use of another solution space, but allows to lower the regularity further.

Let
n\geq 2
. The idea in
[
23
]
to solve (
45
) with initial data in
L^{4n+2}_{s(n)}
is to split the expansion

u[f]=\sum_{m\geq 1}A_{m}(f)=Lf+N_{3}(u,u,u)

not into linear and nonlinear part, but to consider higher Picard iterates

(55)

\begin{split}u^{0}(t)&=Lf,\\
u^{1}(t)&=N_{3}(u^{0},u^{0},u^{0}),\quad u^{2}(t)=N_{3}(u^{0}+u^{1},u^{0}+u^{1},u^{0}+u^{1})-u^{1}(t),\ldots\\
u^{j}(t)&=N_{3}(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k})-\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}u^{k}(t).\quad(j\geq 2)\end{split}

and to prove existence of
v\in S^{0}([-1,1]\times\mathbb{R})=L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}
, which solves

v=u-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j}.

This is equivalent to

(56)

v=N_{3}(u,u,u)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u^{j}=N_{3}(v+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},v+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},v+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u^{j}.

The key technical aspect is that
u^{j}
contains only terms of the form
A_{k}
with
k\geq 2j+1
. For
u^{i}
,
i=0,1
, this is clear. For
j\geq 2
, note the following identity

(57)

\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}u^{k}=N_{3}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k}\big).

For
j=2
, (
57
) is immediate from the definition. For
j\geq 3
, rewrite (
57
) as

u^{j-1}=N_{3}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k}\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{j-2}u^{k},

which corresponds to the definition of
u^{j-1}
by (
55
). Now, to show that
u^{j}
contains only terms of the form
A_{k}
with
k\geq 2j+1
for
j\geq 2
, we use (
57
) to rewrite

\begin{split}u^{j}(t)&=N_{3}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k}\big)-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k}\\
&=N_{3}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}u^{k}\big)-N_{3}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k}\big)\\
&=N_{3}(u^{j-1},u^{j-1},u^{j-1})+3N_{3}\big(u^{j-1},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k}\big)+3N_{3}\big(u^{j-1},u^{j-1},\sum_{k=0}^{j-2}u^{k}\big).\end{split}

This approach requires to prove estimates for
A_{m}(f)
directly. We use the following lemma, which is a consequence of Hölder’s and Young’s inequality:

Lemma 4.3

(
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
s\geq 0
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|f_{1}f_{2}\|_{M^{s}_{p,1}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{M^{s}_{p_{1},1}}\|f_{2}\|_{M^{s}_{p_{2},1}}.

We have the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,2n-1\}
. With the above notations, we find

(58)

\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\frac{4n+2}{m}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{m}_{L^{4n+2}_{s}}

for
s>1-\frac{1}{4n+2}
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
. We use the embedding
M_{p,1}\hookrightarrow M_{p,p^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
to argue

\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\frac{4n+2}{m}}}\lesssim\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}M_{\frac{4n+2}{m},1}}.

By iterating Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}M_{\frac{4n+2}{m},1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{m}_{M_{4n+2,1}}\lesssim_{s}\|f\|^{m}_{M^{s}_{4n+2,4n+2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{s}_{4n+2}}^{m}

for
s>1-\frac{1}{4n+2}
.
∎

We find by a similar argument

\begin{split}\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}&\lesssim\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}M_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M_{\infty,1}}^{m}\\
&\lesssim\|f\|^{m}_{M_{4n+2,1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{m}_{L^{s}_{4n+2}}\text{ for }s>1-\frac{1}{4n+2}\end{split}

and remark that the iteration yields

\|A_{m}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|^{m}_{M^{\tilde{s}}_{4n+2,2}}\text{ for }\tilde{s}>\frac{1}{2}.

However,
\tilde{s}>\frac{1}{2}
, which regularity suffices for local well-posedness by the algebra property. Hence, the argument is not helpful to improve the local well-posedness theory in modulation spaces, but will be useful in the next section to prove global results.

In the following, for fixed
n\geq 2
, let

(59)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{4n+2},

(60)

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}.

A variant of the argument proves the following:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
0\leq j\leq n-1
, and
u^{j}
as in (
55
). Then, for
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}\leq 1
such that

\|u^{j}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}+\|u^{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\frac{4n+2}{2j+1}}}\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha+\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{4n+2}}

holds true provided that
\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\alpha+\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{4n+2}}\leq\varepsilon_{n}
, and

\|u^{j}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}+\|u^{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\frac{4n+2}{2j+1}}}\lesssim\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}+\varepsilon}f\|_{M_{4n+2,2}}

provided that
\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}+\varepsilon}f\|_{M_{4n+2,2}}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
.

The iteration is the same as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, with additional terms estimated with
u^{j}
also in
M_{\infty,1}
. We can prove existence of
v
with the above estimates at hand:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
n\geq 2
, and
\varepsilon_{n},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}\leq 1
as in Lemma
4.5
. Then, there is a unique
v\in S^{0}
satisfying (
56
).

Proof.

We rewrite (
56
) modulo order of the arguments in
N_{3}
as

\begin{split}v&=N_{3}(v,v,v)+3N_{3}(v,v,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})+3N_{3}(v,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})\\
&\qquad+N_{3}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u^{j}.\end{split}

By Theorem
4.2
and
u^{j}\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}
by Lemma
4.5
, we find

\displaystyle\|N_{3}(v,v,v)\|_{S^{0}}\lesssim\|v\|^{3}_{S^{0}},\;\|N_{3}(v,v,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})\|_{S^{0}}\lesssim\varepsilon\|v\|^{2}_{S^{0}},\;\|N_{3}(v,\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})\|_{S^{0}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|v\|_{S^{0}}.

We rewrite the last term by (
57
) as

N_{3}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})-N_{3}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j})

and estimate again via Strichartz estimates

\begin{split}&\quad\|N_{3}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j})-N_{3}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}u^{j})\|_{S^{0}}\\
&\lesssim\|u^{n-1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\frac{4n+2}{2n-1}}}\big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|u^{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{4n+2}}\big)^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2n+1}.\end{split}

The claim follows from applying the contraction mapping principle.
∎

We have proved the following local well-posedness result for slowly decaying data:

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
n\geq 2
, and
f
,
\varepsilon_{n}
, and
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
as in Proposition
4.6
. Let
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{4n+2}=\frac{1}{2}
. Then, there is
u\in L_{t}^{p}([0,1],L^{4n+2}(\mathbb{R}))
, which satisfies (
46
). Furthermore, for
\|f_{1}\|_{L^{4n+2}_{\alpha+\varepsilon}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{4n+2}_{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\leq\varepsilon_{n}
or
\|f_{1}\|_{M^{\tilde{\alpha}+\varepsilon}_{4n+2,2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{M^{\tilde{\alpha}+\varepsilon}_{4n+2,2}}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
, we have for the corresponding solutions
\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{p}([0,1],L^{4n+2})}\to 0
as
\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{4n+2}_{\alpha+\varepsilon}}\to 0
, or
\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{M^{\tilde{\alpha}+\varepsilon}_{4n+2,2}}\to 0
, respectively.

Proof.

The claim follows as
v\in L_{t}^{p}([0,1],L^{4n+2})
by Proposition
4.6
and
u^{j}\in L^{\infty}_{t}([0,1],L^{4n+2})
. Hence,

u=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j}+v=Lf+N_{3}(u,u,u)\in L_{t}^{p}([0,1],L^{4n+2}),

and the continuity of the data-to-solution mapping follows from multilinearity.
∎

In the following we consider again initial data in
L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
6<p\leq\infty
, but choose another solution space. We prove the following, which also covers
p=\infty
with a finite derivative loss:

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
6<p\leq\infty
and
s>1-\frac{1}{p}
. Then, we find (
45
) to be quantitatively well-posed with
D=L^{p}_{s}
and
S=L_{t}^{\infty}([0,1],M_{\infty,1})
.

Proof.

The linear estimate (
47
) follows from
\big(e^{it\Delta}\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
being a
C_{0}
-group on
M_{\infty,1}
and the embeddings (
3
) and (
4
):

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([0,1],M_{\infty,1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{M_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M_{p,1}}\lesssim_{s}\|f\|_{M_{p,p}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

The nonlinear estimate follows from Minkowski’s inequality and Lemma
4.3
:

\|N_{3}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{S}=\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}(u_{1}\overline{u}_{2}u_{3})(s)ds\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}M_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\overline{u}_{2}u_{3}\|_{L_{t}^{1}M_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|u_{i}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}M_{\infty,1}}=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|u_{i}\|_{S}.

Hence, the claim follows from
[
2
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

4.3.
Global well-posedness for slowly decaying initial data

Next, we show global results in modulation spaces stated in Theorem
1.6
. We use the blow-up alternative due to Dodson–Soffer–Spencer
[
23
, Section 3]
. Since we work with initial data in modulation spaces, for which we have improved homogeneous Strichartz estimates, this requires less Sobolev regularity compared to the
L^{p}
-case.

Let
w(t)=U(t)f
denote the first Picard iterate, and let
v(t)=u(t)-w(t)
. Then
v
satisfies

v(t)=N_{3}(v+w,v+w,v+w).

We have the following blow-up alternative:

Lemma 4.9

(Blow-up alternative)
.

Let
s>0
, and
f\in M_{4,2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
T^{*}
is maximal such that
u\in L_{t}^{\frac{24}{7}}([0,T],L^{4}(\mathbb{R}))
for
T<T^{*}
, but
u\notin L_{t}^{\frac{24}{7}}([0,T^{*}],L^{4}(\mathbb{R}))
, then

\lim_{t\to T^{*}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty.

The same blow-up alternative holds for
f\in M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in the solution space
L_{t}^{3}([0,T],L^{6}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

We shall only look into the case
f\in M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as the second claim follows by an easy variation. We argue by contradiction. Let
(t_{n})\subseteq[0,T^{*})
,
t_{n}\to T^{*}
, but

(61)

\|v(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C.

Firstly, letting
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
,
f_{1}\in M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})
and
f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\|Lf_{1}(t)\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\|f_{1}\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|Lf_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

Hence,
\|Lf(t)\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\|f\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. But together with (
61
), this implies that there is a sequence
t_{n}\to T^{*}
with

\|u(t_{n})\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\tilde{C}.

Since the local existence time of solutions in
L_{t}^{3}([0,T],L^{6}_{x}(\mathbb{R}))
only depends on
\|f\|_{M^{s}_{6,2}(\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
by Theorem
1.5
, we see that we can continue the solutions beyond
T^{*}
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Hence, for the proof of global well-posedness it suffices to show

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(T)

for some non-decreasing function
C:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. Let

\displaystyle M(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int|v|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle E(v)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2}|v_{x}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|v|^{4}dx,

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2}|v_{x}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(|v+w|^{4}-|w|^{4})dx.

Note that a priori it is not clear that
E(v(t))
is finite for
t\neq 0
. The following computations are carried out for initial data from a suitable a priori class, say
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. This ensures all quantities to be finite and allows to justify integration by parts arguments. Since we prove bounds depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}}
for
p,q<\infty
, the arguments are a posteriori justified by density and well-posedness.

For the homogeneous solutions, we observe by the fixed time estimate in modulation spaces and their embedding properties,

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{M^{s}_{4,2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{8}}\|w(0)\|_{M^{s}_{4,2}},\quad\|w(t)\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|w(t)\|_{M_{4,4/3}}\lesssim\|w(t)\|_{M^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}_{4,2}}.

By Sobolev embedding, we have for any
t\in[0,T]

(62)

\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle w(t)\|_{L^{4}}+\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{T}\|w(0)\|_{M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{4,2}}.

For
L^{6}
-based modulation spaces, we find by the same embedding properties

(63)

\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\langle\partial_{x}\rangle w(t)\|_{L^{6}}\lesssim_{T}\|w(0)\|_{M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{6,2}}.

Hence, Hölder’s inequality and the assumptions on the initial data imply

E\leq C(T)(\tilde{E}+M+1).

By Lemma
4.9
, solutions in
L^{p}([0,T],L^{q})
with
p
,
q
as in Theorem
1.6
for any
T>0
follow from the following:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
f\in M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{4,2}
or
f\in M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{6,2}
. For all
T>0
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}M(v(t))+E(v(t))\lesssim_{T}1.

Moreover, Theorem
1.6
follows from this proposition by piecing together the local solutions. We turn to its proof:

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

Let

(f,g)=\Re\int f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx.

We aim to bound the quantity
M(v)+\tilde{E}(v)+1
by Grønwall’s lemma. For the derivative of
M
, we compute

\begin{split}\partial_{t}M(v)=(v,v_{t})&=(v,-i(v_{xx}+|v+w|^{2}(v+w))\\
&=(v,2|v|^{2}w+v^{2}\overline{w}+2|w|^{2}v+w^{2}\overline{v}+|w|^{2}w).\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality, we compute for
\|w(t)\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim_{T}1

\partial_{t}M(v)\lesssim_{T}E(v)^{\frac{3}{4}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim_{T}M(v)+E(v)+1.

Similarly, for
\|w(t)\|_{L^{6}}+\|w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{T}1
, we find

\partial_{t}M(v)\lesssim_{T}M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{T}M(v)+E(v)+1.

For the derivative of
\tilde{E}
, we compute

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\int\frac{1}{2}|v_{x}|^{2}dx=(-v_{t},v_{xx})&=-(v_{t},-iv_{t}+|v+w|^{2}(v+w))\\
&=-(v_{t},|v+w|^{2}(v+w))\end{split}

and

\partial_{t}\int\frac{1}{4}(|v+w|^{4}-|w|^{4})dx=(v_{t}+w_{t},|v+w|^{2}(v+w))-(w_{t},|w|^{2}w).

Hence,

\partial_{t}\tilde{E}(v)=(w_{t},|v+w|^{2}(v+w)-|w|^{2}w).

This expression has total homogeneity four in
v
,
w
. Let
(h_{v},h_{w})
denote the homogeneity in
v
,
w
. Then we have to estimate the cases
(1,3)
,
(2,2)
,
(3,1)
. Let the collected terms be denoted by
A_{(h_{v},h_{w})}
. If (
62
) holds true, then applications of Hölder’s inequality give

\begin{split}|A_{(1,3)}|&=|(w_{xx},2|w|^{2}v+\bar{v}w^{2})|=|(w_{x},2(\overline{w}_{x}wv+\overline{w}w_{x}v+|w|^{2}v_{x}+w_{x}w\overline{v})+w^{2}v_{x})|\\
&\lesssim_{T}E(v)^{\frac{1}{4}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
|A_{(2,2)}|&=|(w_{xx},2|v|^{2}w+\overline{w}v^{2})|=|(w_{x},2(v\overline{v}_{x}w+\overline{v}v_{x}w+|v|^{2}w_{x}+\overline{w}vv_{x})+\overline{w}_{x}v^{2})|\\
&\lesssim_{T}E(v)^{\frac{3}{4}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
|A_{(3,1)}|&=|(w_{xx},|v|^{2}v)|=|(w_{x},2|v|^{2}v_{x}+v^{2}\overline{v}_{x})|\lesssim_{T}E(v).\end{split}

If (
63
) holds true, then by another application of Hölder’s inequality, we find

|A_{(1,3)}|\lesssim_{T}M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|A_{(2,2)}|\lesssim_{T}M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}E(v)^{\frac{1}{2}}+M(v)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|A_{(3,1)}|\lesssim_{T}E(v).

Consequently,

\partial_{t}\tilde{E}(v)\lesssim_{T}(1+M(v)+E(v))\lesssim_{T}(1+M(v)+\tilde{E}(v)).

Thus,

\partial_{t}(1+M(v)+\tilde{E}(v))\leq C(T)(1+M(v)+\tilde{E}(v))

and Grønwall’s inequality yields

1+M(v)+\tilde{E}(v)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{T}C(t)dt}

with
C(t)=C(t,\|u_{0}\|_{M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{4,2}})
, or
C(t)=C(t,\|u_{0}\|_{M^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{6,2}})
, respectively. Hence,

M(v)+E(v)\lesssim_{T}1.

∎

At last, we remark how the argument of
[
23
, Section 3]
also implies global well-posedness for
f\in L^{4n+2}_{s}
for
n\geq 2
and
s>\alpha+1
(cp. (
59
)) or
f\in M^{\tilde{s}}_{4n+2,2}
, where
\tilde{s}>\tilde{\alpha}+1
(cp. (
60
)). Let
u_{l}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u^{j}
, where
u^{j}
is given as in (
55
) and consider
u=v+u_{l}
. A blow-up alternative remains valid by the construction of the solution via Strichartz estimates. For this purpose we perceive (

Let

5.
Variable-coefficient decoupling inequalities for non-elliptic Schrödinger equations

In this section we prove variable-coefficient decoupling inequalities for elliptic and hyperbolic phase functions. We start with describing the set-up in Subsection
5.1
and then carry out the proof in Subsection
5.2
.

5.1.
Variable-coefficient oscillatory integral operators

We consider smooth functions

a\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;a=a_{1}\otimes a_{2}
,
0\leq a_{1},a_{2}\leq 1
and
\phi:B^{d+1}(0,1)\times B^{d}(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which we shall refer to as amplitude and phase function.

We associate the oscillatory integral operator

(65)

Tf(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\phi(t,x,\xi)}a(t,x,\xi)f(\xi)d\xi

and the rescaled versions

(66)

T^{\lambda}f(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\lambda\phi(t/\lambda,x/\lambda,\xi)}a(t/\lambda,x/\lambda,\xi)f(\xi)d\xi

for different classes of phase functions.

Subject of discussion are variable-coefficient generalizations of the phase function

\phi_{hyp}(t,x;\xi)=\langle x,\xi\rangle+\frac{t\langle\xi,I_{d}^{k}\xi\rangle}{2},\quad I^{k}_{d}=diag(1,\ldots,1,\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{k}),\quad 0\leq k\leq d/2.

Set also
I_{d}=diag(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

In the following we shall always assume that there are at most as many negative eigenvalues as positive eigenvalues, which is no loss of generality since time reversal
t\rightarrow-t
flips signs.

We define the Gauss map by

(67)

G:B^{d+1}\times B^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d},\quad G(z;\xi)=\frac{G_{0}(z;\xi)}{|G_{0}(z;\xi)|};\quad z=(t,x),

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
B_{m}
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and

(68)

G_{0}(z;\omega)=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{z}\phi(z;\xi)

with the standard identification
\bigwedge^{d}\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

We impose the following conditions on the phase function:

\begin{split}&H1)\quad\text{rank}\;\partial^{2}_{\xi x}\phi(z;\xi)=d\quad\forall\;(z,\xi)\in B^{d+1}\times B^{d},\\
&H2)\quad\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\left.\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z;\xi),G(z;\xi_{0})\rangle\right\rvert_{\xi=\xi_{0}}\text{ is non-degenerate}.\end{split}

H1)
is a non-degeneracy condition, and
H2)
implies that the constant coefficient approximation of
\phi
is the adjoint Fourier restriction operator (i.e. extension operator) associated to a non-degenerate surface.

Contrary to the constant-coefficient case
\phi_{hyp}
, rescaling
(t,x)\rightarrow(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\;\xi\rightarrow\xi/\lambda
yields no exact symmetry. Therefore, it is useful to quantify the conditions
H1)
and
H2)
. Before doing so, we point out the following more precise versions of
H1)
and
H2)
, which one may assume without loss of generality:

\begin{split}&H1^{\prime})\quad\det\partial^{2}_{\xi x}\phi(z;\xi)\neq 0\text{ for all }(z;\xi)\in T\times X\times\Xi=Z\times\Xi;\\
&H2^{\prime}_{[k]})\quad\partial_{t}\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi(z;\xi)\text{ is non-degenerate for all }(z;\xi)\in Z\times\Xi\\
&\quad\quad\text{ and has exactly }k\text{ negative eigenvalues.}\end{split}

Here,
T,X,\Xi
denote balls of radius less or equal to one around the origin.
To reduce from
H1)
and
H2)
to the conditions in the above display, one applies a rotation in space-time. This gives
G(0;0)=e_{d+1}
, and then one uses a partition of unity to suitably localize the support. Moreover, the implicit function theorem implies the existence of smooth functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
taking values in
X
and
\Omega
, respectively, such that

(69)

\partial_{x}\phi(z;\Psi(z;\xi))=\xi

and

(70)

\partial_{\xi}\phi(t,\Phi(t,x;\xi);\xi)=x.

The first identity allows us to find a graph parametrization
\xi\mapsto(\partial_{z}\phi(z;\Psi(z;\xi)))=(\xi,(\partial_{t}\phi)(z;\Psi(z;\xi)))
for a hypersurface
\Sigma_{z}
with non-vanishing curvature. From differentiating the second identity we find
\partial_{x}\Phi(0;0)=\partial^{2}_{x\xi}\phi(0;0)^{-1}
.

Later on,
H1^{\prime})
and
H2^{\prime})
are quantified. It turns out that one can perceive any phase function satisfying
H1^{\prime})
and
H2^{\prime})
after introducing a partition of unity and rescaling as small smooth perturbations of
\phi_{hyp}=\langle x,\xi\rangle+\frac{t\langle\xi,I^{d}_{k}\xi\rangle}{2}
.

For
h\in C^{2}(B^{d}(0,1),\mathbb{R})
let the extension operator
E_{h}
be given by

E_{h}f(t,x)=\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}e^{i(x\xi+th(\xi))}f(\xi)d\xi,

where
f\in L^{2}
,
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq B^{d}(0,1)
and define a smooth weight function, which is essentially a characteristic function on some ball
B^{d+1}(\overline{z},R)
,
\overline{z}=(\overline{t},\overline{x})
:

w_{B(\overline{z},R)}(t,x)=(1+R^{-1}|x-\overline{x}|+R^{-1}|t-\overline{t}|)^{-N}

for some large integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
, which is fixed later.

We define the decoupled
L^{p}
-norm for variable coefficient operators for

1\leq R\leq\lambda
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{R}
denotes a finitely overlapping family of
R^{-1/2}
balls covering
B^{d}(0,1)
. Set

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p,R}_{dec}(S)}=\left(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{R}}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(S)}\right)^{1/2}

for
S
measurable and

(71)

\alpha(p,k)=\begin{cases}k\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p}\right),\;&2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2-k)}{d-k},\\
\frac{d}{4}-\frac{d+2}{2p},\;&\frac{2(d+2-k)}{d-k}\leq p<\infty.\end{cases}

We recall the constant-coefficient
\ell^{2}
-decoupling theorem proved in
[
12
,
13
]
:

Theorem 5.1
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.2, p. 280]

Let
R\geq 1
,
N\geq 10
,

,

as in (

be a

-function with Hessian

having modulus of eigenvalues in

for some

. Then, we find for

with

the following estimate to hold:

\|E_{h}f\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}})}\lesssim_{C,N,\varepsilon}R^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon}\|E_{h}f\|_{L^{p,R}_{dec}(w_{B_{R}})}

provided that
N\geq N(d,p)
.

Strictly speaking, this result was proved in
[
13
]
only for the hyperboloid
h(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}
. However, the arguments from
[
50
]
, which are illustrated in the context of elliptic surfaces in
[

, yield the more general translation invariant case in a straight-forward manner. See also the discussion below.

Originally, decoupling inequalities were studied for the cone by Wolff in
[
43
,
66
]
to make progress on
L^{p}
-smoothing estimates (cf.
[
47
,
48
]
) for the wave equation. These estimates were refined (cf.
[

,

]
) until the breakthrough result of Bourgain-Demeter (cf.
[

,

]
) where sharp decoupling inequalities for the paraboloid were proved. Subsequently, the result was generalized to hyperboloids (cf.
[
13
]
). These results also give estimates for exponential sums, in particular essentially sharp Strichartz estimates on irrational tori.

The theory was also extended to non-degenerate curves (cf.
[
15
]
). As already pointed out in Beltran-Hickman-Sogge

]
, the decoupling theory seems to extend to the variable coefficient case sharply divergent from the

-estimates for oscillatory integral operators. In fact, it is well known that there are strictly less estimates admissible in the constant coefficient case due to Kakeya compression (cf.
[

,

,

).

Our first result is the following extension of Theorem
5.1
:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
,
n,M\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq d/2
and
\alpha(p,k)
like in (
71
). Suppose that
(\phi,a)
satisfies
H1^{\prime})
and
H2^{\prime}_{[k]})
. Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

(72)

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\phi,M,a}\lambda^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon}\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p,\lambda}_{dec}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}+\lambda^{-M}\|f\|_{2}.

For variable-coefficient generalizations of the phase function
\phi_{cone}(t,x;\xi)=\langle x,\xi\rangle+t|\xi|
associated to the adjoint Fourier restriction problem of the cone this was carried out in
[
3
]
. The proof of Theorem
5.2
adapts this general strategy from
[
3
]
to prove variable-coefficient decoupling from constant-coefficient decoupling: on small spatial scales the variable-coefficient oscillatory integral operator is well-approximated by a constant-coefficient operator. It is enough to make progress on this small scale because it extends to any scale by means of parabolic rescaling. Moreover, Iosevich–Liu–Xi
[
40
]
investigated decoupling inequalities for phase functions with a symmetric Carleson–Sjölin condition. More recently, Hickman–Iliopoulou
[
37
]
also showed narrow decoupling inequalities in the variable-coefficient indefinite signature case.

Already in the context of constant coefficients, approximating one surface by another on small scales and recovering arbitrary scales by rescaling was used to derive decoupling estimates for more general elliptic surfaces or the cone (cf.
[
12
, Section 7, 8]
), see also
[
50
,
33
]
.

It seems plausible that a similar approximation derives the variable-coefficient cone decoupling from the variable-coefficient paraboloid decoupling. Recently, in
[
35
]
was shown by the same approximation that broad-narrow considerations are also valid for the cone. We do not pursue this line of argument.

We recall different consequences of Theorem
5.2
: The variable-coefficient
\ell^{2}
-decoupling implies a stability theorem for exponential sums which is proved using the argument in
[
12
]
for the constant-coefficient case. Moreover, on small spatial scales the broad-narrow considerations from the constant-coefficient case extends to the variable- coefficient case. As used above, the decoupling theorem implies Strichartz and smoothing estimates without further arguments (e.g. dispersive estimates for the propagator).

5.2.3.
Approximation by extension operators

Let
(\phi,a)
be a datum of type
1
giving rise to the oscillatory integral operator

and recall that we assume the amplitude function to be of product type:
a(z;\xi)=a_{1}(z)a_{2}(\xi)
. Further, recall that

\xi\mapsto(\nabla_{x,t}\phi^{\lambda})(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))

is a graph parametrisation of a hypersurface
\Sigma_{\overline{z}}
. Thus, we have

(90)

\langle z,(\nabla_{x,t}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))\rangle=\langle x,\xi\rangle+th_{\overline{z}}(\xi)

for all

with

where

.

Moreover, from the definition of

we have

(91)

\begin{split}\xi&=\partial_{x}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)),\\
I_{d}&=\partial^{2}_{x\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))(\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)),\\
0&=\partial^{3}_{x\xi\xi}\phi(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\xi))(\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))^{2}+\partial^{2}_{x\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\xi))\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi).\end{split}

And consequently, we find for
1
-normalized data

(92)

\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)&\sim I_{d},\\
|\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)|&\ll 1.\end{split}

Let
E_{\overline{z}}
denote the extension operator associated to

given by

where
a_{\overline{z}}(\xi)=a_{2}\circ\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)|\det\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi)|
.

We shall see that on small spatial scales

is effectively approximated by

and vice versa. We record the following consequence of dealing with
1
-normalized data:

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(\phi,a)
be a type
1
datum. Each eigenvalue
\mu
of
\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}h_{\overline{z}}
satisfies

on

. For elliptic phase functions of type

we have

on

Proof.

From the definition of

we find

\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}h_{\overline{z}}(\xi)&=(\partial_{t}\partial_{\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi),\\
\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}h_{\overline{z}}(\xi)&=(\partial_{t}\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))(\partial_{\xi}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))^{2}+\partial_{t}\partial_{\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi))\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\xi),\end{split}

and the claim follows from (
92
).
∎

This becomes needful when it comes to applying the constant-coefficient

-decoupling theorem, which we repeated in Theorem
5.1
, because Lemma
5.6
ensures uniformity of the constant from the decoupling inequality.

In the following we analyze

for

and

. The containment property can be assumed due to the margin condition. We see that the desired approximation identity holds on this spatial scale: we perform a change of variables
\xi=\Psi^{\lambda}(\overline{z};\tilde{\xi})
and expand

around

to find

where

Lemma 5.7

-normalized datum

to hold provided that

|b_{k}(v)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|k|)^{-2N},

and inverting the change of variables gives

|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}(v)|\lesssim_{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|k|)^{-2N}|T^{\lambda}\underbrace{[e^{i\langle k,\partial_{z}\phi^{\lambda}(\overline{z},\cdot)\rangle}f]}_{\tilde{f}_{k}}(\overline{z},v)|.

From a similar argument to the one from the proof of (
94
), we have

(101)

\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(0,K)})}\lesssim_{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|k|)^{-2N}\|(T^{\lambda}\tilde{f}_{k})\chi_{B(\overline{z},2\lambda^{1/2})}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(\overline{z},K)})}+\lambda^{-\delta N/2}\|f\|_{2}.

The
k=0
term is alright because it yields the desired quantity. For the higher order terms we use the estimate (
93
) and (
100
) to conclude

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},k\neq 0}(1+|k|)^{-2N}\|(T^{\lambda}\tilde{f}_{k})\chi_{B(\overline{z},2\lambda^{1/2})}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(\overline{z};K)})}\\
&\lesssim_{N}2^{-N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},k\neq 0}(1+|k|)^{-N}\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B((k,0),K)})}\\
&\lesssim_{N}2^{-N}\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(0,K)})}.\end{split}

Choosing
N
large enough depending on
n
and
p
this quantity can be absorbed into the lefthandside of (
101
) which yields the claim.
∎

5.2.4.
Conclusion of the proof

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

To show Proposition
5.3
for fixed parameters
d
,
\varepsilon
and
N=N(d,\varepsilon)
, it is enough to prove that

(102)

\mathfrak{D}_{1,k}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda;R)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}1\text{ for all }1\leq R\leq\lambda^{1-\varepsilon/d}.

We perform an induction on the radius, and with the base case (small
R
) readily settled, we contend the following induction hypothesis:

There is a constant
\overline{C}_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
such that
\mathfrak{D}_{1,k}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{\prime};R^{\prime})\leq\overline{C}_{\varepsilon}
holds for all
1\leq R^{\prime}\leq R/2
and all
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfying
R^{\prime}\leq(\lambda^{\prime})^{1-\varepsilon/d}
.

We use the approximation lemma on a small spatial scale and lift the resulting estimates to the correct spatial scales through parabolic rescaling: Let
\mathcal{B}_{K}
denote a family of finitely-overlapping
K
-balls covering
B_{R}
for some
2\leq K\leq\lambda^{1/4}
. After breaking
B_{R}
into
B(\overline{z};K)
-balls the estimate from Lemma
5.7
implies

(103)

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim\big(\sum_{B(\overline{z};K)\in\mathcal{B}_{K}}\|T^{\lambda}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B(\overline{z};K))}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim\big(\sum_{B(\overline{z};K)\in\mathcal{B}_{K}}\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(0;K)})}^{p}\big)^{1/p}.

We apply the constant-coefficient decoupling theorem (Theorem
5.1
) to each small scale and find after reverting the change of coordinates (again using that we are dealing with
1
-normalized data):

(104)

\begin{split}\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B(0,K)})}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}K^{\varepsilon/2+\alpha(p,k)}\|E_{\overline{z}}f_{\overline{z}}\|_{L^{p,K}_{dec}(w_{B(0,K)})}\\
&\lesssim K^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon/2}\big(\sum_{\sigma:K^{-1/2}-ball}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(w_{B(\overline{z};K)})}\big)^{1/2}+\lambda^{-N/8}K^{2d}\|f\|_{2}.\end{split}

Moreover, this estimate holds uniformly in
\overline{z}
by virtue of the uniform estimates on the Hessian of
h_{\overline{z}}
derived in Lemma
5.6
. We plug (
104
) into (
103
) to find after using Minkowski’s inequality:

(105)

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim K^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon/2}\left(\sum_{\sigma:K^{-1/2}-ball}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}})}\right)^{1/2}+\lambda^{-N/8}K^{2d}R^{d}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, apply Lemma
5.5
to each
T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}
which gives the estimate

(106)

\begin{split}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{R}})}&\leq\mathfrak{D}^{\varepsilon}_{1,k}(\lambda/(\overline{C}K^{2}),R/(\overline{C}K^{2}))(R/K^{2})^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p,R}_{dec}(w_{B_{R}})}\\
&+R^{2(d+1)}(\lambda/R)^{-N/8}\|f_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

We note that
\mathfrak{D}_{1,k}^{\varepsilon}(\lambda/(\overline{C}K^{2}),R/(\overline{C}K^{2}))\lesssim_{\varepsilon}1
according to our induction hypothesis. Plugging (
106
) into (
105
) gives after applying orthogonality

\begin{split}\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}&\leq C_{\varepsilon}\overline{C}_{\varepsilon}K^{\varepsilon/2}(R/K^{2})^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{\sigma:K^{-1/2}-ball}\|T^{\lambda}f_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{p,R}_{dec}(w_{B_{R}})}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad+R^{2(d+1)}(\lambda/R)^{-N/8}\|f\|_{2}\\
&\leq C_{\varepsilon}\overline{C}_{\varepsilon}K^{-\varepsilon/2}R^{\alpha(p,k)+\varepsilon}\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p,R}_{dec}(w_{B_{R})}}+R^{2(d+1)}(\lambda/R)^{-N/8}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\end{split}

and we see that induction closes.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

To finish the proof of Theorem
5.2
, we break the support of
f\in L^{2}(B^{d}(0,1))
into
\rho^{-1}
-balls,
\rho=\rho(\phi)
, so that after parabolic rescaling we are dealing with a normalized phase function
\tilde{\phi}
. We can apply Proposition
5.3
to
\tilde{\phi}
, and the proof is completed using Lemma
5.5
.
∎

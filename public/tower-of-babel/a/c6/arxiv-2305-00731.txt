On the weighted inertia-energy approach to forced wave equations
Edoardo Mainini and Danilo Percivale

Abstract
. We show convergence of minimizers of weighted inertia-energy functionals to solutions of initial value problems for a class of nonlinear wave equations. The result is given for the nonhomogeneous case under a natural growth assumption on the forcing term.



AMS Classification Numbers (2010):
49J45, 70H30

Key Words:
Calculus of Variations, Nonlinear Wave Equation,
Weighted Variational Integrals

1.
Introduction

The variational approach by De Giorgi to second order initial value problems is based on the minimization of
weighted inertia-energy
(WIE) functionals.
We consider the following nonlinear nonhomogeneous wave equation of defocusing type

(1.1)

w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=-|w|^{r-2}w+f(t,x)\quad\mbox{ in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

where
r>1
, along with the initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)
and
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
We introduce the associated WIE functional

(1.2)

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(w):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|w^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\,|w|^{r}-fw\right\}\,dx\,dt,\qquad\varepsilon>0,

to be minimized
among a suitable class of competing functions
w:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the same given initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)
,
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)
. Here, the prime
′
symbol stands for time derivative,
\nabla,\Delta
are the gradient and Laplacian in the spatial variables, and
f
is a given space-time depending forcing term.

It was conjectured by De Giorgi in
[
3
]
, see also
[
4
]
, that if
r=2k
for a positive integer
k
, if
f\equiv 0
and
w_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
subject to the initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)
,
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, with
w_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
w_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
then
w_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\to w(t,x)
for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
w
is a solution of the initial value problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=-|w|^{r-2}w\quad\mbox{ in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
&\\
&w(0,x)=w_{0}(x),\;\;\;w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}\end{array}\right.

This conjecture has been proved by Serra and Tilli in
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
in the following form: if
r>1
,
f\equiv 0
,
(\varepsilon_{n})\subset(0,1)
is a vanishing sequence, and if
w_{n}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
subject to the initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, then, along a suitable subsequence,
w_{n}
converge a.e. in space-time to a solution of the initial value problem
(
1.3
).
The constructed solution is a weak solution satisfying the natural energy inequality, and the result by Serra and Tilli
[
10
]

is thus recovering De Giorgi’s conjecture if
r=2k
as soon as such solutions are known to be unique, for instance in the subcritical regime
1<k<\frac{N}{N-2}
, see for instance
[
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
, that is, for every
k>1
if
N=2
, and for
k=2
if
N=3
.
On the other hand, if
N\geq 3
, for supercritical
k
, global uniqueness of weak solutions is not known in general and this restricts the convergence result of
[
10
]
to subsequences as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Therefore, uniqueness of variational solutions (i.e., obtained as limit of minimizers of the WIE functional) is still an open problem in the supercritical case, even for smooth compactly supported initial data. This problem is difficult due to the ill-posedness issues (see for instance
[
6
,
17
]
) of supercritical defocusing wave equations.

It is also possible to consider WIE functionals of the form

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(w):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|w^{\prime\prime}|^{2}-f\,w\right\}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}W(w(t,\cdot))\,dt,\,\qquad\varepsilon>0,

where the term

(1.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}|w(t,x)|^{r}\right)\,dx

in functional (
1.2
)
has been replaced by a more general functional of the Calculus of Variations
W(w(t,\cdot))
, in order to obtain a variational approximation scheme to hyperbolic PDEs having the following formal structure

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&w^{\prime\prime}-DW(w(t,\cdot))=f(t,x)\quad\mbox{ in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
&\\
&w(0,x)=w_{0}(x),\ w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)\quad\mbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\end{array}\right.

where
DW
is the Gateaux differential of
W
. This has been achieved in
[
11
]
, where the result of
[
10
]
has been extended to this generalized framework, still in the homogeneous case
f\equiv 0
.
Nonhomogeneous equations have been investigated in
[
18
]
. More precisely, letting
f\in L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for every
T>0
,
in
[
18
]
it is shown that there exists an approximating family
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
of functions, converging to
f
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in
L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for every
T>0
, such that by letting
w_{\varepsilon}
minimize the WIE functional

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|w^{\prime\prime}|^{2}-f_{\varepsilon}\,w\right\}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}W(w(t,\cdot))\,dt,\,\qquad\varepsilon>0,

subject to suitable initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)
,
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)
, then up to subsequences there holds
w_{\varepsilon}\to w
a.e. in space-time, and
w
is a solution to problem (
1.5
) featuring the same initial data.

The results in
[
18
]
make use of a specific construction of the approximating sequence
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
,
with
f_{\varepsilon}
identically vanishing for small and for large
t
, not allowing for the choice
f_{\varepsilon}\equiv f
.
On the other hand, our objective will be to obtain convergence of minimizers of the original functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
(which feature the given forcing term
f
) to the solution of the associated initial value problem (
1.5
), at least under some unavoidable growth assumptions on
f
with respect to
t
(see also Proposition
4.6
below). We will reach this result by considering the model case in which
W
is in the form (
1.4
), so that functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is reduced to
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
, and with a sharp growth assumption on
f
, i.e., the Laplace transformability of the map
t\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|f(t,x)|^{2}\,dx
(the result would be easier under stronger conditions like global boundedness of such a map, as assumed in our recent note
[
9
]
about the ode case). In fact, we shall consider the more general WIE functional

(1.6)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(w):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|w^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\,|w|^{r}-F(t,x,w)\right\}\,dx\,dt,\quad\varepsilon>0,

where
r>1
and
((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}})\ni(t,x,v)\mapsto F(t,x,v)
is gloabally Lipschitz in the variable
v
(detailed hypotheses on
F
will be introduced in the next section). Letting
G:={\partial F}/{\partial v}
,
the associated equation is

(1.7)

w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=-|w|^{r-2}w+G(t,x,w)\quad\mbox{ in $(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

Therefore, in our main result stated in Theorem
2.1
below we will prove that if
w_{\varepsilon}
minimizes
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
subject to the initial conditions
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, then along a vanishing sequence
w_{\varepsilon}
converge a.e. in space-time to a solution of the initial value problem for equation (
1.7
) with same initial data
w_{0},w_{1}
.

Concerning further results in the literature about WIE approach to second order initial value problems, we mention the proof of the De Giorgi conjecture in a weaker version in
[
13
]
. General semilinear equations, including dissipative equations, are treated in
[
8
,
11
]
, see also
[
19
]
where the nonhomogeneous case is considered. Moreover, the WIE approach applies for ode’s, we refer to the approach to Lagrangian Mechanics in
[
7
]
, where the authors treat systems of the form
\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}-\nabla U(\mathbf{y})=0
for some given potential function
U
. The system of ode’s
\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{f}(t)
is analyzed in our recent note
[
9
]
, which is in fact a basic application of the technique that we present in this paper. The proof we will develop combines the estimates for hyperbolic equations from
[
10
,
11
,
18
]
with the approach from
[
9
]
.

Plan of the paper

In Section
2
we introduce the notation and the assumptions of the theory, and then we state the main result.
Section
3
provides a couple of preliminary estimates. In Section
4
we prove existence of minimizers and deduce an Euler-Lagrange equation for (a time rescaled version of) functional (
1.6
). We also discuss the sharpness of our growth assumptions. In section
5
we prove the main result. An appendix includes further technical lemmas.

2.
Main result

In this section we introduce the assumptions on the function
F
appearing in (
1.6
) and we provide the functional framework for the main result.

2.1.
Assumptions

Let
(t,x,v)\mapsto F(t,x,v)
be a

L^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}})
function. We suppose
that

(2.1)

\mbox{for a.e. $(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$, the map $v\mapsto F(t,x,v)$ belongs to $C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})$},

we define

(2.2)

G(t,x,v):=\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(t,x,v)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f(t,x):=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}\left|G(t,x,v)\right|

and we assume that

(2.3)

f\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\quad\forall\ T>0.

Moreover we assume that there exists
\varepsilon_{F}\in(0,1/2)
such that

(2.4)

C_{F}:=\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|F(t,x,0)|\,dx\,dt<+\infty

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle K_{F}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-{t}/{(2\varepsilon_{F})}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt<+\infty.

Some remarks on the above assumptions are in order.
It is worth noticing that by (
2.1
) the function
(t,x)\to G(t,x,v)
is Lebesgue measurable for every
v\in{\mathbb{R}}
and the function
v\to G(t,x,v)
is continuous for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Therefore for every measurable
u:(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
the function
(t,x)\to G(t,x,u(t,x))

is measurable and (
2.2
) entails

\left|G(t,x,u(t,x))\right|\leq f(t,x)\;\;\;\mbox{for a.e.}\ (t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

whence by (
2.3
) the function
(t,x)\to G(t,x,u(t,x))
belongs to
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))
for every
T>0
.
(
2.3
) implies in particular that, for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, there holds
\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}\left|G(t,x,v)\right|<+\infty
so that the map
v\mapsto F(t,x,v)
is globally Lipschitz on
{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.

(2.6)

|F(t,x,v_{1})-F(t,x,v_{2})|\leq f(t,x)|v_{1}-v_{2}|\qquad\mbox{for every $v_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and every $v_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

and then
G
coincides with the partial distributional derivative of
F
with respect to its third variable.
We notice that the main assumption (
2.5
) entails existence of the Laplace transform of the function
(0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
in the half space
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathcal{R}ez>\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{F}}\}
.
An assumption of this kind is necessary for the theory, as we discuss in Proposition
4.6
later on.
On the other hand, (
2.4
) requires the existence of the Laplace transform of the function
(0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\|F(t,\cdot,0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
along with some control on its behavior for small
t
.

A relevant case is
F(t,x,v)=b(t,x)\,v
so that the above assumptions reduce to
b\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))
for every
T>0
along with the requirement that
t\mapsto\|b(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
is Laplace transformable. With this choice of
F
, the equation associated with functional (
1.6
), i.e., equation (
1.7
), reduces to the form (
1.1
).
More generally, we may let
F(t,x,v)=b(t,x)\psi(v)
with
b
as above, with the additional assumption that
\psi
is a
C^{1}
function such that
\psi^{\prime}
is bounded over
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\psi(0)=0
. This includes, for instance, the classical sine-Gordon equation and the variable mass sine-Gordon equation
[
5
]
, if
\psi(v)=\cos v-1
.

2.2.
Rescaled energy functionals

Let
r>1
, let us introduce the Banach space

H:=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),

with dual space denoted by
H^{\prime}
, and for every
v\in H
let

W(v):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\frac{1}{r}\|v\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We denote by
DW
the Gateaux derivative of
W
and we let

(DW(v),u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla u+|v|^{r-2}vu,\,\qquad v\in H,\;\;u\in H.

We define now

\displaystyle H^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})):=\displaystyle\bigcap_{T>0}H^{2}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))

and for every
w_{0},\ w_{1}\in H
we let

(2.7)

\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}:=\{u\in\mathcal{U}:u(0,\cdot)=w_{0}(\cdot),\,u^{\prime}(0,\cdot)=\varepsilon w_{1}(\cdot)\},\quad\varepsilon>0,

where

(2.8)

\mathcal{U}:=\left\{u\in H^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})):\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt<+\infty\right\}.

We recall from
[
11
,
18
]

the following elementary estimate. For every
u\in\mathcal{U}
we have

(2.9)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\leq 2\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt

and the same if
u
is replaced with
u^{\prime}
.
As a consequence, if
\varepsilon<1
, for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
there hold

(2.10)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt\leq 2\|w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle+8\|w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+16\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt.

Under the validity of the assumptions of Subsection
2.1
, we set now for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
, where
\varepsilon_{F}\in(0,1/2)
is the number appearing in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), and for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}

(2.12)

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}F(\varepsilon t,x,u(t,x))\,dx\,dt.

By (
2.4
) and by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(u)\right|\leq C_{F}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|F(\varepsilon t,x,u(t,x))-F(\varepsilon t,x,0)|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)u(t,x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}+\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

therefore by (
2.11
), by Young inequality and by a very simple estimate that follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) and whose proof is postponed to Lemma
3.1
below, we get the existence of a constant
K_{F}^{*}
, only depending on
f
and
\varepsilon_{F}
, such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(u)\right|\leq C_{F}+\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}+16K_{F}^{*}+\frac{1}{32}\|w_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\frac{1}{8}\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt.

Therefore we may define

(2.14)

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}+W(u(t,\cdot))\right\}dt-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(u)

and we see that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
the functionals
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
are well defined and finite for every
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
.

2.3.
Main result

We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that the hypotheses
of
Subsection
2.1

hold true and let

w_{0},\ w_{1}\in H
. Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
there exists a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to problem

\min\{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u):u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}\}.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\varepsilon_{F})
be a vanishing sequence, let
u_{\varepsilon_{n}}
minimize
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
over
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{0}
and let
w_{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x):=u_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\frac{t}{\varepsilon_{n}},x)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, there exist a subsequence
(\varepsilon_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
w\in H^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

w_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}\to w
weakly in
H^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
k\to+\infty

and such that
w
satisfies

\mbox{$w\in W^{2,\infty}((0,T);H^{\prime})\cap H^{1}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\cap L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\cap L^{r}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\;$ $\forall\,T>0$},

w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=-|w|^{r-2}w+G(t,x,w)\quad\mbox{ in $\;\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})$},

\mbox{$w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)$ for a.e. $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$, $\;\;w^{\prime}(0,x)=w_{1}(x)$ for a.e. $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}$},

along with the following energy inequality

(2.15)

\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(0)}+\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,ds}\qquad\mbox{for a.e. $t>0$,}

where
\mathcal{E}(t):=\dfrac{1}{2}\|w^{\prime}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+W(w(t,\cdot))
and
f
is defined by (
2.2
).

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth noticing that functional
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
is a rescaling of the original functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
from (
1.6
), and if
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
, then

w_{\varepsilon}
minimizes indeed
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}

over
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{0}:=\{w\in\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}:w(0,\cdot)=w_{0}(\cdot),\,w^{\prime}(0,\cdot)=w_{1}(\cdot)\}
, where

\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{w\in H^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})):\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|w^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}\,dt<+\infty\right\}.

Remark 2.3
.

If we change the definitions of
H
and
W
letting
H=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
W(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
, and accordingly we redefine functional (
2.14
), with the same proof we obtain the analogous convergence result of rescaled minimizers of functional
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
to a solution of the initial value problem for the equation
w^{\prime\prime}-\Delta w=G(t,x,w)
.
We also expect that our result extends to the case in which
W
is a more general functional as in
[
11
,
18
]
thus covering equations of the form
w^{\prime\prime}-DW(w)=G(t,x,w)
.

3.
Some preliminary estimates

In this section the assumptions of Subsection
2.1
are understood to hold. In particular,
useful consequences of the assumptions (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) are obtained in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
there holds

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\leq K_{F}^{*}

and more generally for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
and every
\alpha\geq 0
there holds

\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha}e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\leq K_{F}^{*}(\alpha),

where

K_{F}^{*}(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha}e^{-t/\varepsilon_{F}}}{\varepsilon_{F}^{\alpha+1}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt+\Gamma(\alpha+1)\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,\alpha+1);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))}

and
K_{F}^{*}:=K_{F}(0)
. Here,
\Gamma
denotes the Euler Gamma function.

Proof.

By changing variables, since
\varepsilon_{F}<1
and since the map
(0,t)\ni\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon^{-1}e^{-t/\varepsilon}
is increasing for every
t>0
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{-1}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{-1}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(1-e^{-1/\varepsilon})\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,1);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon_{F}^{-1}e^{-t/\varepsilon_{F}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt

which is the desired estimate, the right hand side being finite thanks to (
2.3
) and (
2.5
). The more general estimate is obtained with the same argument, taking into account that
\varepsilon_{F}<1
, that the map
(0,t/(\alpha+1))\ni\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}t^{\alpha}e^{-t/\varepsilon}
is increasing for every
t>0
and that by definition of the Gamma function
\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,dt=\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)
. We notice that
K_{F}^{*}(\alpha)
is finite for every
\alpha\geq 0
thanks to (
2.3
) and (
2.5
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
s>0
and every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
, let

(3.1)

Q_{\varepsilon}(s):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{-2}te^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t+s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt.

Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
, we have
Q_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
for every
T>0
and in particular

\|Q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq M_{F}(T):=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,2T+1);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))}+{\varepsilon_{F}^{-2}}\,e^{T/\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon_{F}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt.

Proof.

We shall use the notations
N_{\varepsilon}(t):=\varepsilon^{-2}te^{-t/\varepsilon}
and
b_{f}(t):=\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
. We notice that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt=1
.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
, every
s>0
and every
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)b_{f}(t+s)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T+1}N_{\varepsilon}(t)b_{f}(t+s)\,dt+\int_{T+1}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)b_{f}(t+s)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\|b_{f}(\cdot+s)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T+1)}+\int_{T+1}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon_{F}}(t)b_{f}(t+s)\,dt,

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
(0,t/2)\ni\varepsilon\mapsto N_{\varepsilon}(t)
is an increasing function for every
t>0
, and here we have
\varepsilon_{F}<1/2<(T+1)/2
. Therefore for every
s>0
and every
T>0

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\|b_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T+1+s)}+\int_{T+s+1}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon_{F}}(t-s)b_{f}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\|b_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T+1+s)}+{\varepsilon_{F}^{-2}}\,e^{s/\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon_{F}}b_{f}(t)\,dt

and the right hand side is finite due to the assumptions on
f
. This shows that
Q_{\varepsilon}(s)
is well defined for every
s>0
and moreover we deduce that for every
T>0

\|Q_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq\|b_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,2T+1)}+{\varepsilon_{F}^{-2}}\,e^{T/\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon_{F}}b_{f}(t)\,dt

thus proving the result.

The function
Q_{\varepsilon}
from Lemma
3.2
is defined as a convolution and we may deduce its behavior as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Indeed,
if
T>0
we have
Q_{\varepsilon}\to b_{f}
in
L^{1}(0,T)
, where
b_{f}(t):=\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
. This is a consequence of the following approximation lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N_{\varepsilon}(t):=\varepsilon^{-2}te^{-t/\varepsilon}
,
t\geq 0
,
\varepsilon>0
. Let
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,+\infty)
and
\delta>0
be such that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\delta}|b(t)|\,dt<+\infty
, and suppose
b\in L^{1}(0,T)
for every
T>0
. Then
N_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)b(\cdot+s)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
for every
s>0
and every
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
. Moreover, letting
b_{\varepsilon}(s):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)b(t+s)\,dt
, for every
T>0
we have
b_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(0,T)
and

b_{\varepsilon}\to b
in
L^{1}(0,T)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

For every
s>0
and every
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)|b(s+t)|\,dt=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{s}^{+\infty}(t-s)e^{-(t-s)/\varepsilon}|b(t)|\,dt\leq\varepsilon^{-2}e^{s/\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon}|b(t)|\,dt<+\infty,

and if
T>0
,
estimating in analogous way we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|b_{\varepsilon}(s)|\,ds=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{e^{s/\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{s}^{+\infty}(t-s)e^{-t/\varepsilon}|b(t)|\,dt\,ds\leq\frac{Te^{T/\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t/\varepsilon}|b(t)|\,dt<+\infty

so that indeed
N_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)b(\cdot+s)\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
and
b_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(0,T)
.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
let

\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(z):=\delta^{-2}(\delta-\varepsilon)^{2}e^{-z/\delta}N_{\varepsilon}(-z){\mathbbm{1}}_{(-\infty,0]}(z),\qquad\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}(z):=\delta^{2}(\delta-\varepsilon)^{-2}e^{-z/\delta}b(z){\mathbbm{1}}_{(0,+\infty)}(z).

It is readily seen that the family
(\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)}
is an approximate identity on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
1
,
2
]
) and that
\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{b}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
\tilde{b}(z):=e^{-z/\delta}b(z){\mathbbm{1}}_{(0,+\infty)}(z).
Therefore,
\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{b}\to\tilde{b}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, thus Young’s convolution inequality entails
\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}*\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{b}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. But for every
s>0
we have

\displaystyle(\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}*\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon})(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(s-z)\tilde{b}_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz=e^{-s/\delta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}N_{\varepsilon}(t)b(s+t)\,dt=e^{-s/\delta}b_{\varepsilon}(s)=:b^{*}_{\varepsilon}(s),\\

hence
b^{*}_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{b}
in
L^{1}(0,+\infty)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which entails

b_{\varepsilon}\to b
in
L^{1}(0,T)
for every
T>0
as claimed.

Also in this section the assumptions of Subsection

The first step towards the proof of the main theorem is to minimize functional

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
.
There exists a solution to the minimization problem

(4.1)

\min\left\{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u):u\in\mathcal{U}^{0}_{\varepsilon}\right\}.

Moreover, there exists an explicit constant
\bar{C}
(only depending on
F
,
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
) such that if
u_{\varepsilon}
is solution to (
4.1
) there holds

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}+W(u_{\varepsilon}(t))\right\}\,dt\leq\bar{C},

Proof.

Since
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{F}<1
, estimate (
2.13
) shows that
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded from below over
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
by a constant that depends only on
F,w_{0},w_{1}
.

We notice that the function
w_{0}(x)+t\varepsilon w_{1}(x)
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
.
Moreover, since
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{F}<1/2
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, by (
2.4
) and by Lemma
3.1
we have similarly as in estimate (
2.13
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(w_{0}+\varepsilon tw_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w_{0}+t\varepsilon\nabla w_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}|w_{0}+\varepsilon tw_{1}|^{r}-F(\varepsilon t,x,w_{0}+\varepsilon tw_{1})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\|\nabla w_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})})+2^{r-1}\Gamma(r+1)(\|w_{0}\|^{r}_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\|w_{1}\|^{r}_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})})

\displaystyle+C_{F}+\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}}\,(2\|w_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})})^{1/2},

showing in particular that

\inf\left\{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u):u\in\mathcal{U}^{0}_{\varepsilon}\right\}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
be a minimizing sequence for (
4.1
). It is not restrictive to assume that the minimizing sequence satisfies
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n})\leq 1+\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(w_{0}+t\varepsilon w_{1})
for every

, and this implies, along with the previous estimate of
J_{\varepsilon}(w_{0}+t\varepsilon w_{1})
and (

\bar{C}
, only depending on

, such that for every

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}|u_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}|u_{n}(t,x)|^{r}\right\}\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\bar{C}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

and since
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{F}<1
we conclude that

(4.3)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}|u_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}|u_{n}(t,x)|^{r}\right\}\,dt\,dx\leq\bar{C}.

Therefore, by taking also (
2.10
)-(
2.11
) into account, the sequence
(e^{-t/2}u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in

, in particular it converges weakly to some suitable
v
in
L^{2}((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
up to extracting a subsequence. Moreover, if
Q_{T_{R}}:=(0,T)\times B_{R}
, where
B_{R}
is a ball of radius
R

\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we obtain that
u_{n}

u_{n}^{\prime}
and
\nabla u_{n}
enjoy uniform bounds in

, thus up to further subsequences,

converges to a suitable

strongly in

(and then pointwise) by Rellich theorem. The pointwise convergence allows to identify the limit, i.e.,

.
By (
4.3
) and the weak lower semicontinuity of

norms,

satisfies (
4.2
).

We further notice that by (
2.6
) and Lemma
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{n}(t,x))-F(\varepsilon t,x,u(t,x))|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{Q_{R,T}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q_{R,T}^{c}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}}\left(\iint_{Q_{R,T}}|u_{n}(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\iint_{Q_{R,T}^{c}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt

for every
T>0
,
R>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We split the last integral in an integral over
(T,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
plus an integral over
(0,T)\times B_{R}^{c}
, and we estimate as

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}}\int_{T}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\|u_{n}(t,\cdot)-u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}}\,e^{-T/2}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{n}(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{C_{*}\,K_{F}^{*}\,e^{-T}},

where the latter inequality is due to (

satisfies (
4.3

satisfies (
4.2
), by letting

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}^{c}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|u_{n}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\,dx\,dt\leq\sqrt{C_{*}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}^{c}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and the right hand side vanishes as

, since Lemma
3.1
implies that

belongs to

.
Therefore, since

strongly converge to

in

, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{n}(t,x))-F(\varepsilon t,x,u(t,x))|\,dx\,dt

and by taking the limit as

The latter convergence, along with the semicontinuity of

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}),

4.1

Finally, assuming that

The first estimate is proven in the same way.
In order to check the third estimate, we notice that by Jensen inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\xi(t)|e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)e^{s}|\varphi(s)|\,ds\right)e^{-t}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\varphi(s)|\left(\int_{s}^{+\infty}(t-s)e^{-(t-s)}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\varphi(s)|\left(\int_{s}^{+\infty}(t-s)e^{-(t-s)}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds

which is the desired result since

Q_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varepsilon^{-2}te^{-t/\varepsilon}\|f(t+\varepsilon s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,ds=\int_{s}^{+\infty}(t-s)e^{-(t-s)}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt

follows by change of variables.

We state a first order minimality condition satisfied by a solution to (
4.1
).
We stress that it is obtained by taking advantage of Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, which exploit the results in the appendix that are also based on a first variation argument obtained by a different perturbation of the minimizer.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{F})
and let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution to problem (
4.1
).
Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,+\infty))
and let
\xi
be defined by (
4.8
). Let
h\in H
. Then there holds

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}u^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\xi(t)\,(DW(u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)),h)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\,G(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\,\xi(t)h(x)\,dx\,dt=0.

Proof.

Let us first consider the mapping

(-1,1)\ni\delta\mapsto\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x)|^{r}\,dx\,dt.

Here, the integral is finite for every
\delta\in(-1,1)
, because both
e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r}
and
e^{-t}|\xi(t)h(x)|^{r}
are in
L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
thanks to the fact that
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
4.2
) and since
|\xi(t)|\leq C^{*}_{\varphi}t
for every
t>0
, where
C^{*}_{\varphi}:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s}|\varphi(s)|\,ds
.
We claim that such a mapping is differentiable at
\delta=0
and more precisely that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta r}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left(|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x)|^{r}-|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r}\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\xi(t)h(x)|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r-2}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\,dt

Indeed, while pointwise a.e. convergence of the integrands is clear (as
r>1
), by Lagrange theorem we have for every
0<|\delta|<1

\displaystyle e^{-t}\left|\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x)|^{r}-|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r}}{\delta r}\right|\leq e^{-t}(|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|+|\xi(t)h(x)|)^{r-1}|\xi(t)h(x)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}(1\vee 2^{r-2})\left(|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r-1}+|\xi(t)h(x)|^{r-1}\right)\,|\xi(t)h(x)|,

where the right hand side is integrable, because
e^{-t}|\xi(t)h(x)|^{r}\in L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as already observed and

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r-1}|\xi(t)h(x)|\,dx\,dt\leq\|h\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}|\xi(t)|\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|^{r-1}_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\,dt<+\infty

thanks to Lemma
4.4
. Therefore, the claim follows by dominated convergence. The same argument shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{2\delta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)\nabla h(x)|^{2}-|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\xi(t)\nabla h(x)\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\,dt.

On the other hand we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)+\delta\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t,x))|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)\,dx\,dt+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

where all the integrals are finite thanks to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, to (
4.2
) and to the fact that
\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)=e^{t}\varphi(t)
so that
\xi^{\prime\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,+\infty))
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\delta}{\Bigg|_{\delta=0}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)+\delta\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt=2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)h(x)u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)\,dx\,dt.

Moreover,
the mapping
\delta\mapsto\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x))\,dx\,dt
is differentiable at
\delta=0
with derivative equal to

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}G(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\,\xi(t)h(x)\,dx\,dt.

This can be seen by dominated convergence as well.
Indeed, (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) imply that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x))-F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))}{\delta}=G(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\,\xi(t)h(x)

for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, and in order to find a dominating function we notice that (
2.6
) implies

e^{-t}\,\frac{|F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\delta\xi(t)h(x))-F(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))|}{|\delta|}\leq e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|\xi(t)||h(x)|

where the right hand side is integrable over
(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, since by (
2.5
), having
|\xi(t)|\leq C^{*}_{\varphi}t
for every
t>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}f(\varepsilon t,x)|\xi(t)|h(x)|\,dx\,dt\leq\|h\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}|\xi(t)|^{2}\|f(\varepsilon t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<+\infty.

We conclude that the mapping
(-1,1)\ni\delta\mapsto\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+\delta\xi h)
is differentiable at
\delta=0
. Since
\xi(0)=\xi^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have
u_{\varepsilon}+\delta\xi h\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
for every
\delta\in(-1,1)
, and since
u_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer for
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
over
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
we deduce that (
4.9
) holds.

We close this section by showing that our main result is not achievable without a growth assumption like (
2.5
), because the energy functional
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
may easily get unbounded from below over
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
. We consider for simplicity the case
F(t,x,v)=b(t,x)\,v
, so that the assumptions on

from Subsection
2.1
reduce to the following:
b\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}))
for every

, and the map
t\mapsto\|b(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
is Laplace transformable.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Suppose that
F(t,x,v)=\eta(t)\varphi(x)\,v
, where
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is nonnegative and
\eta\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,+\infty)
is nonnegative and such that
\eta\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
for every
T>0
. Let
w_{1}\in H
be nonnegative, let
w_{0}\in H
and suppose that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\varphi(x)w_{0}(x)\,dx>0
. Assume that
\eta
is not Laplace transformable, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\delta}\eta(t)\,dt=+\infty
for every
\delta>0
.
Then
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
is unbounded from below over
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
.

Proof.

We equivalently prove that functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is unbounded from below over
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
(see Remark
2.2
).
With the given choice of
F
, the function
f
from (
2.2
) is given by
f(t,x)=\eta(t)\varphi(x)
, and
the assumptions (
2.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are trivially satisfied. On the other hand, by Jensen inequality the map
t\mapsto\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
is not Laplace transformable so that (
2.5
) does not hold.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

w_{n}(t,x):=w_{0}(x)\zeta_{n}(t)+tw_{1}(x)\zeta_{n}(t)
, where
\zeta_{n}(t):=\zeta(2^{-n}t)
and
\zeta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
is smooth, decreasing, compactly supported and satisfies the following properties:
\zeta(t)=1
for every
t\in[0,1]
and
|\zeta^{\prime}(t)|+|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 1
for every
t\in[0,+\infty)
.
Therefore, as
n\to+\infty
, the sequence
(\zeta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges pointwise and monotonically to
1
over
[0,+\infty)
, and the sequences
(\zeta_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge pointiwse to
0
over
[0,+\infty)
.
It is clear that
w_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since

w_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)=w_{0}(x)\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)+2w_{1}(x)\zeta_{n}^{\prime}(t)+t\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)w_{1}(x),

it follows by dominated convergence that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}|w_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt=0,

and similarly

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}W(w_{n}(t,\cdot))\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}W(w_{0}+tw_{1})\,dt.

On the other hand, letting
C_{0}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}w_{0}(x)\varphi(x)\,dx>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}F(\varepsilon t,x,w_{n}(t,x))\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\eta(\varepsilon t)\varphi(x)(w_{0}(x)\zeta_{n}(t)+tw_{1}(x)\zeta_{n}(t))\,dx\,dt\geq C_{0}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t/\varepsilon}\eta(\varepsilon t)\zeta_{n}(t)\,dt

where the right hand side goes to
+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
by monotone convergence.
We conclude that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(w_{n})=-\infty.

\displaystyle=K_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\tau)-\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(t-\tau)e^{-(t-\tau)}\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(\varepsilon t,x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\,u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\,dt.

Proof.

By taking
g(t)=t
in Lemma
6.1

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}te^{-t}L_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}L_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}D_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(\varepsilon s,x,u_{\varepsilon}(s,x))\,u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)\,dx\,ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}(DW(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)),w_{1})\,ds

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(\varepsilon s,x,u_{\varepsilon}(s,x))w_{1}(x)\,dx\,ds,

therefore, since
\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}W(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot))\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}L_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds,

from (
2.5
) we obtain

(6.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}W(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot))\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}L_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}f(\varepsilon s,x)\,|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)|\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}|(DW(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)),w_{1})|\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}f(\varepsilon s,x)\,|w_{1}(x)|\,dx\,ds,

and we may estimate the terms in the right hand side uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Indeed,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}L_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds
is the left hand side of (
4.2
) and therefore it gets estimated by
\bar{C}
as therein. We treat
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}f(\varepsilon s,x)\,|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)|\,dx\,ds
by means of (
6.7
) with
g(t)=t
, and the integral in the right hand side of (

) gets estimated by a constant

w_{0},w_{1}
and
F
by means of (
2.10
) and (
4.2
). Similarly we use (
6.6
) for estimating
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}f(\varepsilon s,x)|w_{1}(x)|\,dx\,ds
. Moreover, by Young inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}se^{-s}|(DW(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)),w_{1})|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}se^{-2}[|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)||\nabla w_{1}(x)|+|u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-1}|w_{1}|]\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-s}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{2}|\nabla w_{1}(x)|^{2}+\frac{s^{r}}{r}|w_{1}(x)|^{r}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{2}+\frac{r-1}{r}|u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r}\right)\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla w_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}+\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{r}\|w_{1}\|^{r}_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+r\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}W(u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot))\,ds

and we conclude by estimating the right hand side by means of (

), thus getting (

, it is also clear that the last two terms of (

\varepsilon\to 0
, because by applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality along with (

) and Lemma
3.1
we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}se^{-s}f(\varepsilon s,x)\,|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)|\,dx\,ds\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}(2)}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}(2)}\left(2\varepsilon^{2}\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K_{F}^{*}(2)}\left(2\varepsilon^{2}\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+8\varepsilon^{2}\bar{C}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, by taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0

Proof of Lemma

.

We define
K_{\varepsilon}
and

as in Lemma

A computation shows that

for a.e.

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-3\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}(s-t)e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-(s-t)}(s-t)\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial v}(\varepsilon s,x,u_{\varepsilon}(s,x))\,u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)\,dx\,ds,

\displaystyle\leq-3\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}(s-t)e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-(s-t)}(s-t)\,f(\varepsilon s,x)\,|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,x)|\,dx\,ds,

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-3\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}(s-t)e^{-(s-t)}D_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}(s-t)e^{-(s-t)}\|f(\varepsilon s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}(s-t)e^{-(s-t)}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for a.e.

. By means of a suitable version of the Gronwall lemma, it is shown in the proof of

that the latter estimate implies that for every

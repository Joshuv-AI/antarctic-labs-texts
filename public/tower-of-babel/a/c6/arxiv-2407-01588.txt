Global well-posedness, scattering and blow-up for the energy-critical, Schrödinger equation with indefinite potential in the radial case
Jun Wang, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we study the well-posedness theory and the scattering asymptotics for the energy-critical, Schrödinger equation with indefinite potential { i ∂ t u + Δ u − V ( x ) u + | u | 4 N − 2 u = 0 , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ N × ℝ , u | t = 0 = u 0 ∈ H 1 ​ ( ℝ N ) , \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\ \left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right. where V ⁡ ( x ) : ℝ N → ℝ V(x):\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} is indefinite and satisfies appropriate conditions. Using contraction mapping method and concentration compactness argument, we obtain the well-posedness theory in proper function spaces and scattering asymptotics. Moreover, we get a positive ground state solution which is radially symmetric by using variational methods. This paper extends the results of [ 20 ] (Invent. Math) to the potential equation and develops the recent conclusions.

Introduction and main results

This paper studies the well-posedness theory and the scattering asymptotics for the energy-critical, focusing, Schrödinger equation with potential

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
V(x):\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is indefinite and satisfies appropriate conditions.

When
V(x)=0
, Kenig and Merle in
[
20
]
study the
\dot{H}^{1}
critical non-linear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u\pm|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{array}\right.

Here the
-
sign corresponds to the defocusing problem, while the
+
sign corresponds to the focusing problem. They obtained the global well-posedness, scattering and blow-up results in the radial case and
3\leq N\leq 5
. Recently, Oh and Wang in
[
25
]
considered global well-posedness for one-dimensional cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation by introducing a new function space. For
N=2
,
[
8
]
established global well-posedness results in the defocusing case, posed on the two-dimensional unit ball. For high dimensions case, Tao et.al in
[
29
]
established global well-posedness and scattering for solutions to the defocusing mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For more well-posedness results, please refer to
[
32
,
16
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

When
V(x)\neq 0
, two situations arise. If
V(x)
is nonnegative potential(inverse square potential), Lu et al. in
[
23
]
studied the scattering/blowup dichotomy below the ground state in the focusing case and proved scattering in
H^{1}
for arbitrary data in the defocusing case. For more nonnegative potential results, please refer to
[
18
,
15
,
34
,
22
]
. If
V(x)
haves a negative part, it is not easy to handle. More precisely, it is very difficult for us to obtain the existence of the ground state solution of (
1.1
) and some necessary variational estimates. It is worth mentioning that the above mentioned papers only consider the subcritical energy case, so it is natural to ask whether similar conclusions hold for the critical energy case? The answer is affirmative, moreover, we have also proved that the solution blow up in infinite time as long as the initial energy is less than
0
.

In order to analyze the scattering asymptotics of (
1.1
), we need to prove the existence of standing wave solutions to (
1.1
), that is,
\Psi(x,t)=

e^{i\lambda t}u(x),\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, so we get the equation

-\Delta u+V(x)u=|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.2)

where
V(x):\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is indefinite. This proof is based on the variational method, please refer to section 6 for details.

If the solution
u
of (
1.1
) has sufficient decay at infinity and smoothness, it satisfies the conservation of mass

M(u(t)):=\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

(1.3)

and the conservation of energy

E(u(t))=E(u_{0}),

(1.4)

where
E(u(t))
is defined by

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u(x,t)\right|^{2}+V(x)|u(x,t)|^{2})dx-\frac{N-2}{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t)|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

and the energy space is
H^{1}
.

In this paper, the potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is assumed to satisfy the following assumptions

V\in\mathcal{K}\cap L^{\frac{N}{2}}

(1.5)

and

\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N),

(1.6)

where
\alpha(N)
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\mathcal{K}
is a class of Kato potentials with

\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(y)|}{|x-y|}dy

and
V_{-}(x):=\min\{V(x),0\}
is the negative part of
V
. Suppose
\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define

\mathcal{L}^{n}=-\Delta+V(x-x_{n})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{L}^{\infty}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta+V(x-x_{\infty}),&\text{ if }&x_{n}\rightarrow x_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta+V_{\infty},&\text{ if }&\left|x_{n}\right|\rightarrow\infty.\end{array}\right.

In particular,
\mathcal{L}\left[\phi\left(x+x_{n}\right)\right]=\left[\mathcal{L}^{n}\phi\right]\left(x+x_{n}\right)
.

Remark 1.1
.

A typical example of potentials satisfying (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is the following Yukawa-type potential

V(x)=c|x|^{-\sigma}e^{-a|x|},\quad c\in\mathbb{R},\quad\sigma\in(0,N-1),\quad a>0.

The genuine Yukawa potential corresponds to
\sigma=1
. The nonlinear Schrödinger equation with Yukawa potential appears in a model describing the interaction between a meson field and a fermion field(see
[
33
]
). Note that

\|V\|_{L^{q}}=|c|\left[N\alpha(N)(aq)^{q\sigma-N}\Gamma(N-q\sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

(1.7)

and

\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}}=2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)|c|a^{\sigma-N+1}\Gamma(N-1-\sigma),

(1.8)

where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. In fact,

\displaystyle\|V\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-q\sigma}e^{-aq|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c|\left(N\alpha(N)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N-1-q\sigma}e^{-aqr}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c|\left[N\alpha(N)(aq)^{q\sigma-N}\Gamma(N-q\sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}},

which proves (
1.7
). To obtain (
1.8
), consider

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(y)|}{|x-y|}dy=|c|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-a|y|}}{|y|^{\sigma}|x-y|}dy.

If
x=0
, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-a|y|}}{|y|^{1+\sigma}}dy=N\alpha(N)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ar}r^{N-2-\sigma}dr=N\alpha(N)a^{\sigma-N+1}\Gamma(N-1-\sigma).

If
x\neq 0
, it holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-a|y|}}{|y|^{\sigma}|x-y|}dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{e^{-ar}}{r^{\sigma}|x-r\theta|}r^{N-1}drd\theta=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ar}r^{N-2-\sigma}I(x,r)dr,

where
r=|y|
and

I(x,r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{x}{r}-\theta\right|}d\theta.

Take
A\in O(N)
such that
Ae_{1}=\frac{x}{|x|}
with
e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
, we see that

I(x,r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{|x|}{r}Ae_{1}-\theta\right|}d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{|x|}{r}e_{1}-\theta\right|}d\theta.

By change of variables, we get

\displaystyle I(x,r)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\mathbb{S}^{N-2}}\frac{d\eta}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{|x|}{r}-s\right)^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-2}}\frac{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}d\zeta}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{|x|}{r}-s\right)^{2}+1-s^{2}}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{S}^{N-2}\right|\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{|x|}{r}-s\right)^{2}+1-s^{2}}}

\displaystyle=(N-1)\alpha(N-1)\frac{r}{|x|}\left(\frac{|x|}{r}+1-\left|\frac{|x|}{r}-1\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2(N-1)\alpha(N-1),&|x|\leq r,\\
2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)\frac{r}{|x|},&|x|\geq r.\end{array}\right.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int\frac{e^{-a|y|}}{|y|^{\sigma}|x-y|}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)}{|x|}\int_{0}^{|x|}e^{-ar}r^{N-1-\sigma}dr+2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)\int_{|x|}^{\infty}e^{-ar}r^{N-2-\sigma}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)a^{\sigma-N+1}\Gamma(N-1-\sigma)

\displaystyle+2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)\left(\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{0}^{|x|}e^{-ar}r^{N-1-\sigma}dr-\int_{0}^{|x|}e^{-ar}r^{N-2-\sigma}dr\right).

Consider

f(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-ar}r^{N-1-\sigma}dr-\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-ar}r^{N-2-\sigma}dr,\quad\lambda>0.

We see that if
0<\sigma<N-1
, then

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}f(\lambda)=0.

Moreover,

f^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-ar}r^{N-1-\sigma}dr<0,\quad\forall\lambda>0.

This shows that
f
is a strictly decreasing function, hence
f(\lambda)<0
for all
\lambda>0
. Thus for
x\neq 0
,

\int\frac{e^{-a|y|}}{|y|^{\sigma}|x-y|}dy<2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)a^{\sigma-N+1}\Gamma(N-1-\sigma).

We conclude that

\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}}=2(N-1)\alpha(N-1)|c|a^{\sigma-N+1}\Gamma(N-1-\sigma),

which proves (
1.8
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that

Remark 1.3
.

If the operator
-\Delta+V
is positive definite, then we can use the mountain pass theorem to obtain the ground state solution of the equation. If the operator
-\Delta+V
is indefinite, we can also obtain the existence of nontrivial solutions(Sobolev subcritical case) via Morse theory, but we can not determine whether this solution is ground state. The critical case is even more complicated, and there are currently no results regarding the ground state solution.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
V\in C\cap\mathcal{K}\cap W^{1,\frac{N}{2}}
,
\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N)
,
\|V_{-}\|_{\frac{N}{2}}\leq S

and
\nabla V(x)\cdot x\leq 0
,

. Assume that

E\left(u_{0}\right)<E(W),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|^{2}dx<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla W|^{2}dx

and

in

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|u(t)-e^{it\mathcal{L}}u_{0,+}\right\|_{H^{1}}=0,\ \lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|u(t)-e^{it\mathcal{L}}u_{0,-}\right\|_{H^{1}}=0.

Remark 1.4
.

If the operator
-\Delta+V
is indefinite and the nonlinear term satisfies the sobolev subcritical growth, we can obtain a nontrivial solution. However, it is difficult to obtain the scattering results. A very obvious difficulty is that the energy corresponding to this nontrivial solution may not necessarily be positive, so we can not obtain the necessary variational estimates(such as Lemma

).

In section 2, we provide some notations and some important lemma in the proof of main theorems. In the next section, we aim to prove theorem
1.1

After that, we prove the existence of positive ground state solution by using variational methods in section 6. Finally, we will obtain the scattering asymptotics. To achieve this goal, we need some new variational estimates and compactness results, that is, sections 7, 8 and 9.

3
Well-posedness

First, we recall the dispersive estimate for the linear propagator
e^{-it\mathcal{L}}
, but for simplicity, we assume that the negative part of a potential is small.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Dispersive estimate). If
V\in\mathcal{K}\cap L^{\frac{N}{2}}
and
\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N)
, then

\left\|e^{-it\mathcal{L}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{1}}

for any
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

By Beceanu-Goldberg
[
5
]
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{L}
does not have an eigenvalue or a nonnegative resonance. We claim that
\mathcal{L}
is positive, that is, if
V\in\mathcal{K}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V||u|^{2}dx\leq\frac{\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.1)

In particular, if
\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N)
, then

\left(1-\frac{\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{L}u\bar{u}dx\leq\left(1+\frac{\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In fact, observe that

\displaystyle\left\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(-\Delta)^{-1}|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V(x)|\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(y)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)|x-y|}u(y)dy\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V(x)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(y)|}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)|x-y|}dy\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)|x-y|}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|V|_{\mathcal{K}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(x)|}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)|x-y|}|u(y)|^{2}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\frac{|V|_{\mathcal{K}}}{N(N-2)\alpha(N)}\right]^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then, (
3.1
) follows by the standard
TT^{*}
argument with
T=|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla|^{-1}
. Therefore, it has no negative eigenvalue. Moreover, by Ionescu-Jerison
[
17
]
, there is no positive eigenvalue or resonance.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
V\in\mathcal{K}\cap L^{\frac{N}{2}}
and
\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N)
, there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{1}\left\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{p}},\quad\|f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{2}\left\|(1+\mathcal{L})^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{p}},

where
1<p<q<\infty,\ 1<p<\frac{N}{s},\ 0\leq s\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{N}
.

Proof.

Consider the heat operator
e^{-t(a+\mathcal{L})}
. Let
a=0
or
1
. According to the Theorem 2 in
[
27
]
, we know that
e^{-t(a+\mathcal{L})}
obeys the gaussian heat kernel estimate,

0\leq e^{-t(a+\mathcal{L})}(x,y)\leqslant\frac{A_{1}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-A_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}},\ \forall t>0,\ \forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
A_{1},A_{2}>0
. Applying it to

(a+\mathcal{L})^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(a+\mathcal{L})}t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}dt,

it is easy to see that the kernel of
(a+\mathcal{L})^{-\frac{s}{2}}
satisfies

\left|(a+\mathcal{L})^{-\frac{s}{2}}(x,y)\right|\leq C\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-s}},

where
C>0
. This implies that
\left\|(a+\mathcal{L})^{-\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}
with
p,q,s
in Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

(Norm equivalence)
If
V\in\mathcal{K}\cap L^{\frac{N}{2}}
and
\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<N(N-2)\alpha(N)
, then

\left\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{r}}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,r}},\quad\left\|(1+\mathcal{L})^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{r}}\sim\|f\|_{W^{s,r}},

where
1<r<\frac{N}{s}
and
0\leq s\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
a=0
or
1
. We claim that

\|(a+\mathcal{L})f\|_{L^{r}}\sim\|(a+\Delta)f\|_{L^{r}},\quad\forall 1<r<\frac{N}{2}.

Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|(a+\mathcal{H})f\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(a-\Delta)f\|_{L^{r}}+\|Vf\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(a-\Delta)f\|_{L^{r}}+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{Nr}{N-2r}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(a-\Delta)f\|_{L^{r}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|(a-\Delta)f\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(a+\mathcal{L})f\|_{L^{r}}+\|Vf\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(a+\mathcal{L})f\|_{L^{r}}+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{N}{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{Nr}{N-2r}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(a+\mathcal{L})f\|_{L^{r}}.

Next, we claim that the imaginary power operator
(a+\mathcal{L})^{iy}
satisfies

\left\|(a-\Delta)^{iy}\right\|_{L^{r}\rightarrow L^{r}},\left\|(a+\mathcal{L})^{iy}\right\|_{L^{r}\rightarrow L^{r}}\leq C\langle y\rangle^{\frac{N}{2}},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }\forall 1<r<\infty.

Indeed, since the heat kernel operator
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
obeys the gaussian heat kernel estimate (see the proof of Lemma
3.2
, these bounds follow from
[
26
]
.
Combining the above two claims, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|(a+\mathcal{L})^{z}f\right\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\langle\operatorname{Im}z\rangle^{\frac{N}{2}}\left\|(a-\Delta)^{z}f\right\|_{L^{r}},

\displaystyle\left\|(a-\Delta)^{z}f\right\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\langle\operatorname{Im}z\rangle^{\frac{N}{2}}\left\|(a+\mathcal{L})^{z}f\right\|_{L^{r}}

for
1<r<\infty
when
\operatorname{Re}z=0
and for
1<r<\frac{N}{2}
when
\operatorname{Re}z=1
. Finally, applying the Stein-Weiss complex interpolation, we prove the norm equivalence lemma.
∎

Now, we get the existence and uniqueness of solution to (

) by using contraction mapping method.

Lemma 3.4
.

,

4
Blow up solutions

In this section, we will investigate the blow up solutions of (
1.1
). Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be radial and satisfy

\psi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}r^{2},&\text{ for }r\leqslant 1\\
0,&\text{ for }r\geqslant 2\end{array}\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leqslant 1\quad\text{ for }r=|x|\geqslant 0.\right.

For a fixed
R>0
, we define the rescaled function
\psi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

\psi_{R}(r):=R^{2}\psi\left(\frac{r}{R}\right).

(4.1)

Next we will show that

1-\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)\geqslant 0,\quad 1-\frac{\psi_{R}^{\prime}(r)}{r}\geqslant 0,\quad N-\Delta\psi_{R}(r)\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }r\geqslant 0.

(4.2)

Indeed, this first inequality follows from
\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\frac{r}{R})\leq 1
. We obtain the second inequality by integrating the first inequality on
[0,r]
and using that
\psi_{R}^{\prime}(0)=0
. Finally, we see that last inequality follows from

N-\Delta\psi_{R}(r)=1-\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)+(N-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\psi_{R}^{\prime}(r)\right)\geqslant 0.

Besides (
4.2
),
\psi_{R}
admits the following properties, which can be easily checked.
We define

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]:=2\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}(t)\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla u(t)dx=2\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}(t)\partial_{j}\psi_{R}\partial_{j}u(t)dx.

Define the self-adjoint differential operator

\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}:=i\left(\nabla\cdot\nabla\psi_{R}+\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\right),

which acts on functions according to

\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}f=i\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)f\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla f)\right).

It’s easy to check that

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]=\left\langle u(t),\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle.

Next, we show the following useful lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
, and
u\in H_{\text{rad }}^{1}
is a solution of (
1.1
). Let
\psi_{R}
be as in (
4.1
),
T^{*}
be the maximal existence time of solution
u(t)
in
H_{\text{rad }}^{1}
. Then for sufficiently large
R
, it holds

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8E(u(t)),\quad t\in\left[0,T^{*}\right).

Proof.

By taking the derivative of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]
with respect to time
t
and using the equation of
u(t)
, for any
t\in[0,T)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),\left[-\Delta,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}\right]u(t)\right\rangle+\left\langle-|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle+\left\langle u(t),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where
[X,Y]\equiv XY-YX
denotes the commutator of operators
X
and
Y
.
According to the localized radial virial estimate in
[
7
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),\left[-\Delta,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}\right]u(t)\right\rangle\leq 8\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CR^{-2},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle-|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)u\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla u)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\left(|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\Delta\psi_{R}\right)|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx-\frac{4}{N}\int_{|x|\geq R}(\Delta\psi_{R}-N)|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+\frac{4}{N}\int_{|x|\geq R}(N-\Delta\psi_{R})|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+2CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle u(t),\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\left(|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\Delta\psi_{R}\right)|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+\frac{4}{N}\int_{|x|\geq R}(N-\Delta\psi_{R})|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+2CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CR^{-2}-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+4CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8C\left\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}+CR^{-2}-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+4CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
R
. When
R>1
is sufficiently large, then

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8CE(u(t))=8CE(u_{0}).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
u
be such that the corresponding solution to (
1.1
) exists on the maximal time
T^{*}
. If
T^{*}<\infty
, then we are done. If
T^{*}=\infty
, we show (
1.9
). We suppose that
u(t)
exists for all times
t\geq 0
, i.e.
T^{*}=\infty
.
It follows from Lemma
4.1
and conservation of mass, for
R\gg 1
large enough,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8CE(u_{0}):=-A^{*}<0,\quad t\geq 0.

From this, we infer that

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq-A^{*}t+\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u_{0}],\quad t\geq 0.

On the one hand, let
T_{0}=\frac{2\left|\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u_{0}]\right|}{A^{*}}>0
, then for any
t\geq T_{0}
, we have

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq-\frac{1}{2}A^{*}t<0.

(4.3)

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.3
and the conservation of mass, we see that for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\psi_{R}\right)\left(\left\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\left\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\psi_{R}\right)\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\psi_{R}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we have used the interpolation estimate

\left\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

This combined with (
4.3
) yields that for any
t\geq T_{0}
,

A^{*}t\leq-2\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq C\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This shows that

\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq Ct,\quad t\geq T_{0}.

It means that

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}}=\infty.

∎

5
Perturbation theory

In this section, we will study the long-time perturbation theory.

Proposition 5.1
.

(Perturbation theory)
Let
\tilde{u}:I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a solution to the perturbed Schrödinger
equation with general nonlinearity

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq E,

(5.1)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N-2}}L^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N-2}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq L

(5.2)

for some
E,L>0
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq M
for some
M>0
and let
t_{0}\in I
. There exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(E,L,M)>0
such that if

\left\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}\left(t_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon,

(5.3)

\left\|e\right\|_{L_{I}^{\infty}H^{1}\cap L_{I}^{2}L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}}\leq\varepsilon

(5.4)

for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then the unique global solution
u
to (
1.1
) with
u\left(t_{0}\right)=u_{0}
satisfies

\left\|u-\tilde{u}\right\|_{L_{I}^{\infty}H^{1}\cap L_{I}^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N-2}}W^{1,{\frac{2N(N+2)}{N^{2}+4}}}}\leq C(E,L,M)\varepsilon,

(5.5)

where
C(E,L,M)
is a non-decreasing function of
E,L
and
M
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
t_{0}=0\in I
. From Theorem
1.1
we know that
u
exists globally and

\|u\|_{L_{I}^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N-2}}W^{1,{\frac{2N(N+2)}{N^{2}+4}}}}\leq\rho,

so we need to get (
5.5
).

Let

w:=u-\tilde{u}\ \text{and}\ A(t):=\|w\|_{L_{(I\cap[-T,T])}^{\infty}H^{1}\cap L_{(I\cap[-T,T])}^{\frac{2(N+2)}{N-2}}W^{1,{\frac{2N(N+2)}{N^{2}+4}}}}.

Note that

i\partial_{t}\tilde{w}+\Delta\tilde{w}-V(x)\tilde{w}+|\tilde{u}+w|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}(\tilde{u}+w)-|\tilde{u}|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}\tilde{u}=-e,

which is equivalent to the integral equation

w(t)=e^{it\mathcal{L}}w_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{L}}[|\tilde{u}+w|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}(\tilde{u}+w)-|\tilde{u}|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}\tilde{u}]dt^{\prime}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{L}}edt^{\prime}.

Then by Strichartz, Hölder, (
5.1
), (
5.2
), (
5.3

6
Positive ground state solution

In this section, we will prove the existence of standing wave solutions of (
1.1
). More precisely, we can obtain a positive ground state solution which is radially symmetric. Obviously, solutions of problem (
1.2
) can be obtained by looking for critical points of the functional
I:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}dx,

where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
. In order to state our main results, we introduce some notations. The Aubin-Talenti constant
[
1
]
is denoted by
S
, that is,
S
is the best constant in the Sobolev embedding
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{E}:=

\|\nabla u\|_{2}
. It is well known
[
28
]
that the optimal constant is achieved by (any multiple of)

U_{\varepsilon,y}(x)=[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\ \varepsilon>0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(6.1)

which are the only positive classical solutions to the critical Lane-Emden equation

-\Delta w=w^{2^{*}-1},\quad w>0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Now, we begin proving that
I
satisfies the geometric assumptions of the mountain pass theorem.

Lemma 6.1
.

I
has a mountain pass geometry, that is,

\mathrm{(i)}
there exist
\alpha,\rho>0
such that
I(v)\geq\alpha
for all
v\in E
such that
\|v\|_{E}=\rho
,

\mathrm{(ii)}
there exists
e\in E
with
\|e\|_{E}>\rho
such that
I(e)<0
.

Proof.

\mathrm{(i)}
By using Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\frac{N}{2}}S^{-1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2^{*}\cdot S^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0

when
\|u\|_{E}
is small enough. Hence, there exist
\alpha,\rho>0
such that
I(v)\geq\alpha
for all
v\in E
such that
\|v\|_{E}=\rho
.

\mathrm{(ii)}
Taking
v_{0}(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we get

\displaystyle I(tv_{0}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla v_{0}\right|^{2}+V(x)|v_{0}|^{2})dx-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{0}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left(1+\left\|V\right\|_{\frac{N}{2}}S^{-1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}dx-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{0}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0

when
t
is large enough. Therefore, we can choose
e=t_{0}v_{0}(x)
for some
t_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{(ii)}
holds.
∎

According to Lemma
6.1
and using a variant of the mountain pass theorem without the Palais-Smale condition, we can find a sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset E
such that

I\left(v_{n}\right)\rightarrow c\ \text{and}\ I^{\prime}\left(v_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ in }E^{*}

(6.2)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where

c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t))

and

\Gamma:=\left\{\gamma\in C\left([0,1],E\right):\gamma(0)=0,I(\gamma(1))<0\right\}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that
\{u_{n}\}\subset E
satisfies (
6.2
), then
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
.

Proof.

For
n
large enough, we obtain that

\displaystyle c+1+\|u_{n}\|_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2})dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2^{*}}dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}+V(x)|u_{n}|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\frac{N}{2}}S^{-1}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{E}^{2},

which implies that

is bounded.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

c<\frac{1}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a radial cut-off function such that
\psi\equiv 1
for

and
\psi\equiv 0
for

, where

is some positive constant. Define, for

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{2-N}{2}}U(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}),

It follows from
[

that

\|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}=\|U_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2}=S^{\frac{N}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}dx+O\left(\varepsilon^{N-2}\right)=S^{\frac{N}{2}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{N-2}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|U_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2^{*}}dx+O\left(\varepsilon^{N}\right)=S^{\frac{N}{2}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{N}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\left(\int_{0}^{2}\psi(r)dr\right)\varepsilon+O\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right),&\text{ if }N=3,\\
d\varepsilon^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|+O\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right),&\text{ if }N=4,\\

where

is a positive constant. Note that

\displaystyle I(tu_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+V(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})dx-\frac{t^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle h(t)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx.

Clearly,

for

small and

as

, so

Hence, we have

\displaystyle I(t_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+V(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})dx-\frac{t_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+\frac{\max V}{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}+\frac{\max V}{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left[\frac{S^{\frac{N}{2}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{N-2}\right)}{(S^{\frac{N}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{N-2}))^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}}\right]^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{N-2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2}}.

We complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),t\in\left[0,T_{+}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)
. Then:

i)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\psi dx=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\nabla u\nabla\psi dx

ii)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\psi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\nabla V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta^{2}\psi|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx.

Proof.

By (
1.1
) and direct calculation, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{Re}\psi\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\bar{u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{Re}\left[i\Delta u-iV(x)u+i|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\right]\bar{u}\psi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u\bar{u}\psi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\bar{u}\nabla\psi dx

because of
\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}\psi dx=0
and part (i) follows.

Using (i), we know that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\bar{u}\nabla\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx+\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx\right].

On the one hand,

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\left(\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx.

On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\overline{\Delta u}\nabla udx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\overline{\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx,

so

-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi(\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u)\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi V(x)u\overline{\nabla u}dx-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi(|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u)\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi V(x)\nabla|u|^{2}dx+\frac{N-2}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\nabla(|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\nabla V(x)|u|^{2}dx+\frac{N-2}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx.

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\nabla V(x)|u|^{2}dx+\frac{N-2}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\nabla V(x)|u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta^{2}\psi|u|^{2}dx-\frac{4}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}dx,

which implies that the conclusion is valid.
∎

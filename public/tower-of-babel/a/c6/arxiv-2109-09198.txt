Hyperelliptic values of the Gamma function
Jan Lügering

Abstract
We show how to calculate particular values of the Gamma function for specific rational arguments in the interval (0,1) without using the Elliptic K-function. By introducing hyperelliptic generalisations of the trigonometric functions, we obtain a halving formula. We show that the constants used in this formula are transcendental in specific rational cases. We generalise Euler’s arcsine proof and state a useful formula by Königsberger. Finally, we apply the Gamma values to various equations like the area of superellipses.

Introduction

The Gamma function can be defined for
Re(x)>0
as

\Gamma(x):=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}\cdot e^{-t}\ dt

(1)

Recall the Legendre duplication formula for
x>0
:

\Gamma(x)\cdot\Gamma(x+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2x-1}}\cdot\Gamma(2x)

(2)

and Gauss multiplication formula for
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus-\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \Gamma(x)\cdot\Gamma(x+\frac{1}{n})\cdot...\cdot\Gamma(x+\frac{n-1}{n})=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n^{nx-\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\Gamma(nx)

(3)

The Euler Beta function can be defined for
Re(w)>0
and
Re(z)>0
as:

B(w,z):=\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{w-1}\cdot(1-t)^{z-1}\ dt

(4)

The central identity in the theory of the Beta function is
remmert
:

\forall\ Re(w),Re(z)>0:\ B(w,z)=\frac{\Gamma(w)\cdot\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z+w)}

(5)

We also have the Euler relation:

\forall\ x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}:\ \Gamma(x)\cdot\Gamma(1-x)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi x)}

(6)

The lemniscatic arcsine function is defined as
siegel
:

\arcsl(x):=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{4}}}\ dr

(7)

with the lemniscate constant

\varpi:=2\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{4}}}\ dr

(8)

in analogy to

\arcsin(x):=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\ dr

(9)

and

\pi:=2\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\ dr

(10)

We have the fascinating integral identity
euler
:

A\cdot B:=(\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-r^{4}}}\ dr)\cdot(\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{1-r^{4}}}\ dr)=\frac{\pi}{4}

(11)

Our goal is to generalize these functions and identities and apply this knowledge to calculate fractional values of the Gamma function.

Definition 1

We define for

\pi_{s}:=2\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{s}}}\ dx

Definition 2
.

The generalised arcsine is defined for

as:

\arcs_{n}(x):=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{n}}}\ dx,\ x\in V_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}[-1,1]&\text{for n even}\\
(-\infty,1],&\text{for n odd}\\
\end{array}\right\}

Proposition 3
.

\forall\ \lvert x\rvert<1:\arcs_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{x^{nk+1}}{4^{k}\cdot(nk+1)}

(14)

and in particular we have

\frac{\pi_{n}}{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{1}{4^{k}\cdot(nk+1)}

(15)

Proof.

Let
\lvert x\rvert\leq r<1
be arbitrary.

\arcs_{n}(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{k}\cdot(-1)^{k}\cdot y^{nk}\ dy=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\cdot\frac{y^{nk}}{4^{k}}\ dy

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\cdot\frac{y^{nk}}{4^{k}}
converges uniformally for
\lvert y\rvert\leq r
we obtain:

=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{x^{nk+1}}{4^{k}\cdot(nk+1)}\ ,\forall\ \lvert x\rvert\leq r

For every fixed
\lvert x\rvert<1
and in particular for
x=1
, the series converges by comparison with the arcsine series. Abel’s limit theorem yields the result.
∎

Our next goal is to find a halving formula by proving an auxiliary formula.

Proposition 4
.

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})^{2}\cdot 2^{\frac{2}{n}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{n})\cdot n}=\frac{\pi_{n}}{2}

(16)

Proof.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

On the one hand we have:

B(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m}{n})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(\frac{m}{n})}{\Gamma(\frac{m}{n}+\frac{1}{2})}

On the other hand:

B(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m}{n})=B(\frac{m}{n},\frac{1}{2})=\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{m}{n}-1}\cdot(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ dt

Substituting
t:=u^{n}
yields:

=n\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{u^{m-1}}{\sqrt{1-u^{n}}}\ du

Therefore

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(\frac{m}{n})}{\Gamma(\frac{m}{n}+\frac{1}{2})}=n\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{u^{m-1}}{\sqrt{1-u^{n}}}\ du

For
m=1
we obtain:

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2})\cdot n}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{n}}}\ du=\frac{\pi_{n}}{2}

\implies\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2})}=\frac{n\cdot\pi_{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})\cdot 2}

Rearranging the Legendre duplication formula for
x>0
yields:

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(x+\frac{1}{2})}=\frac{\Gamma(x)\cdot 2^{2x-1}}{\Gamma(2x)}

Setting
x=\frac{1}{n}
:

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2})}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})\cdot 2^{\frac{2}{n}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{n})}

Comparing both equations implies:

\frac{n\cdot\pi_{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})\cdot 2}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})\cdot 2^{\frac{2}{n}-1}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{n})}

∎

Transcendence

Now what is the nature of the constants

Thanks to a theorem by Theodor Schneider in

For all

Corollary 17
.

For all

it holds, that

is transcendental.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
be arbitrary.

In the proof of proposition
4
, we deduced:

B(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m}{n})=n\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{u^{m-1}}{\sqrt{1-u^{n}}}\ du

Generalising Euler’s Arcsine Proof

Theorem 18
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

\frac{\pi_{n}^{2}}{8\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt}=1+\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2l+1)!!}{(2l+2)!!}\cdot\frac{1}{n\cdot(l+1)+1}\cdot\prod\limits_{k=0}^{l}\frac{2\cdot(nk+2)}{n+2\cdot(nk+2)}

(42)

Proof.

We have the Taylor series expansion

arcs_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{t^{nk+1}}{4^{k}\cdot(nk+1)}

(43)

In other words:

arcs_{n}(t)=t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)\cdot...\cdot 5\cdot 3\cdot 1}{2k\cdot...\cdot 4\cdot 2}\cdot(\frac{t^{nk+1}}{(nk+1)})

(44)

Observing that:

\frac{1}{2}\cdot(arcs_{n}(x))^{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{arcs_{n}(t)}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt

(45)

We obtain:

\frac{1}{2}\cdot(arcs_{n}(x))^{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)\cdot...\cdot 5\cdot 3\cdot 1}{2k\cdot...\cdot 4\cdot 2}\cdot(\frac{1}{nk+1})\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{t^{nk+1}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt

(46)

Next, define

I_{l}(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{t^{l+n}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt=\int\limits_{0}^{x}t^{l+1}\cdot\frac{t^{n-1}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt

(47)

Integrating by parts and multipliying the integrand with
\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}
:

I_{l}(x)=-\frac{2}{n}\cdot x^{l+1}\cdot\sqrt{1-x^{n}}+\frac{2(l+1)}{n}\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{t^{l}\cdot(1-t^{n})}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt

(48)

Setting
x=1
we obtain:

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{l+n}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt=\frac{2(l+1)}{n+2(l+1)}\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{l}}{\sqrt{1-t^{n}}}\ dt

(49)

Now, mapping
l\mapsto(nl+1)
the result follows.

∎

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})}{n\cdot\pi_{n}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2+n}{2n})\cdot 2^{-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}

∎

It is possible, that hyperelliptic constants
\pi_{n}
appear for certain values with sums of type:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(nk+1)^{2}},\ n\in\mathbb{N}

(71)

(On updating and researching for this paper, reading between the lines, I noticed that C.L. Siegel implicitly treated
\zeta(3)
as an ”independent” constant that cannot be expressed by any known constants whatsoever and I think that it is more of a ”logarithmic nature”.)

By the functional equation

\zeta(s)=2^{s}\cdot\pi^{s-1}\cdot\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2})\cdot\Gamma(1-s)\cdot\zeta(1-s)

we compute for
s=\frac{3}{4}

\frac{\zeta(\frac{3}{4})}{\zeta(\frac{1}{4})}=\sqrt{(2+\sqrt{2})\cdot 2\varpi}

(72)

Now setting

we have

\frac{\zeta(\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}})}{\zeta(\frac{1}{2^{n}})}=\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt{...}}}\cdot\sqrt{2^{n-1}\cdot\pi_{2^{n}}\sqrt{...\sqrt{2^{n-1}\cdot\pi_{2}}}}}{\sqrt[2^{n}]{2\pi_{2}}}

therefore

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \frac{\zeta(\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}})}{\zeta(\frac{1}{2^{n}})}=\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt{...}}}\cdot\sqrt{2^{n-1}\cdot\pi_{2^{n}}\sqrt{...\sqrt{2^{n-1}\cdot\pi_{4}\sqrt{2^{n-2}}}}}

(73)

with
n
nested square-roots, respectively.

A superellipse is defined for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a,b>0
as

C_{n}:\ \lvert\frac{x}{a}\lvert^{n}\ +\ \lvert\frac{y}{b}\lvert^{n}\ =1

It is well known, that the area is given by

A_{C_{n}}=4\cdot ab\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{n})^{2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n+2}{n})}=4\cdot ab\cdot\frac{1}{2n}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{n})^{2}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{n})}

(75)

For the case
n=2^{\nu}
we calculate

A_{C_{2^{\nu}}}=4\cdot ab\cdot\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}\cdot\frac{2^{\nu-1}\cdot\pi_{2^{\nu}}\cdot\sqrt{2^{\nu-1}\cdot\pi_{2^{\nu-1}\cdot\sqrt{...}}}}{\sqrt{2^{\nu-2}\cdot\pi_{2^{\nu-1}\cdot\sqrt{...}}}}

With (
\nu-1

\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}:\ A_{C_{2^{\nu}}}=\pi_{2^{\nu}}\cdot ab\cdot\sqrt{2\sqrt{2\sqrt{2\sqrt{...}}}}

where
0

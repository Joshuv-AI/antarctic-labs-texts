Spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations with potential for wave-like decaying null infinity
Chuxiao Liu 1,4, Xiao Zhang 2,3,4

Abstract
. We prove the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with small initial data and with wake-like decaying null infinity for the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations with potential, where the scalar potential V V satisfies ( 1.13 ) and ( 1.14 ).

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar field equations with potential

We provide some well-known facts about the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar field equations with potential; for details, see, e.g.
[
4
,
18
]
.
In general relativity, metrics of spherically symmetric spacetime can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-gqdu^{2}-2gdudr+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\right)

(1.1)

in Bondi coordinates, where
g(u,r)
and
q(u,r)
are
C^{2}
and nonnegative over
(0,\infty)
.
Denote by
D
the derivative along the incoming light rays

\displaystyle D=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}-\frac{q}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}.

The null frames are given by

\displaystyle\vec{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{gq}}D,\quad\vec{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{gq^{-1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\quad e_{1}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\quad e_{2}=\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}.

Let
T_{\mu\nu}
be a symmetric 2-tensor which is spherically symmetric and divergence-free, i.e., it satisfies that

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0.

Define

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{R}{2}g_{\mu\nu},\quad E_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}-8\pi T_{\mu\nu}.

For spherically symmetric metrics, the only nonzero components are

\displaystyle E(\vec{n},\vec{l}),\,\,E(\vec{l},\vec{l}),\,\,E(\vec{n},\vec{n}),\,\,E(e_{1},e_{1}),\,\,E(e_{2},e_{2}).

By the twice contacted Bianchi identities,
E_{ij}
is divergence-free. This implies that if

\displaystyle E(\vec{n},\vec{l})=E(\vec{l},\vec{l})=0,

(1.2)

then

\displaystyle E(e_{1},e_{1})=E(e_{2},e_{2})=0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(E(\vec{n},\vec{n})gqr^{2}\Big)=0,

where

\displaystyle E(\vec{n},\vec{n})gqr^{2}=-\frac{\partial\ln g}{\partial u}rq+\frac{\partial q}{\partial u}r+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\ln g}{\partial r}rq^{2}-8\pi r^{2}T(D,D).

(1.3)

We introduce the first regularity condition at
r=0
.

Regularity Condition I
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\Big(E(\vec{n},\vec{n})g(u,r)q(u,r)r^{2}\Big)=0.

(1.4)

Under (
1.4
), the Einstein field equations with the energy-momentum tensor
T_{ij}
are equivalent to (
1.2
).

Assume that the energy-momentum tensor is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+g_{\mu\nu}V(\phi),

(1.5)

where
\phi(u,r)
is a real,
C^{2}
scalar field over
(0,\infty)
, and
V(\phi)
is the scalar potential. The divergence-free condition of
T_{\mu\nu}
gives that

\displaystyle\Box\phi=-\frac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial\phi}.

(1.6)

Throughout the paper, we denote by

\displaystyle\bar{f}(u,r)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{f(u,r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}}

the integral average of
f(u,r)
with respect to
r
. Define

\displaystyle h=\frac{\partial(r\phi)}{\partial r}.

(1.7)

It is clear that

\displaystyle E(\vec{n},\vec{l})=0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(rq)}{\partial r}=g+8\pi gr^{2}V(\phi),

(1.8)

\displaystyle E(\vec{l},\vec{l})=0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ln g}{\partial r}=4\pi r\Big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\Big)^{2}.

(1.9)

We introduce the second regularity condition at
r=0
as well as the boundary condition at
r=\infty
.

Regularity Condition II
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\Big(r\phi(u,r)\Big)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\Big(rq(u,r)\Big)=0.

(1.10)

Boundary Condition
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}g(u,r)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}q(u,r)=1.

(1.11)

Under (
1.10
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) give

\displaystyle\phi=\bar{h},\quad q=\bar{g}+\frac{8\pi}{r}\int_{0}^{r}gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds.

Under (
1.11
), (
1.1
) is asymptotically flat, and (
1.9
) gives

\displaystyle g=\exp\left\{-4\pi\int_{r}^{\infty}{(h-\bar{h})^{2}\frac{ds}{s}}\right\}.

Therefore, under the regularity conditions (
1.4
), (
1.10
), and the boundary condition (
1.11
), the Einstein-scalar field equations with scalar potential
V
are equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} g&=\exp\left\{-4\pi\int_{r}^{\infty}{(h-\bar{h})^{2}\frac{ds}{s}}\right\},\\
q&=\bar{g}+\frac{8\pi}{r}\int_{0}^{r}gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds,\\
Dh&=\frac{1}{2r}(g-q)(h-\bar{h})+4\pi gr(h-\bar{h})V(\bar{h})+\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V(\bar{h})}{\partial\bar{h}}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(1.12)

The Bondi mass
M_{B}(u)
for each
u
and the final Bondi mass
M_{B1}
are given by
[
1
,
13
]

\displaystyle M_{B}(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-q\Big),\quad M_{B1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M_{B}(u).

The Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M(u)
for each
u
and the final Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M_{1}
are given by
[
4
,
13
]

\displaystyle M(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-\frac{q}{g}\Big),\quad M_{1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M(u).

1.2.
Main results

(i)\,\,V=0
. In this case, Christodoulou first studied the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with small initial data, and of generalized solutions with large initial data, for particle-like decaying null infinity
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. In particular, he proved that the solution satisfies the following uniformly decaying estimates

\displaystyle\big|h(u,r)\big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{3}},\quad\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{4}},

and the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete with vanishing final Bondi-Christodoulou mass.

Christodoulou also showed the unique spherically symmetric global solution exists for the characteristic initial-value problem for small bounded variation norms under the double null coordinates
[
7
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\Omega^{2}dudv+r^{2}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\big).

Later, his result was extended to the more general situation by Luk, Oh and Yang, and they showed the unique spherically symmetric global solution exists and
the resulting spacetime is future causally geodesically complete if the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{u}^{v}\big|\Phi(v^{\prime})\big|dv^{\prime}\leq\epsilon\big(v-u\big)^{1-\gamma},\quad|\Phi(v)|+\Big|\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial v}(v)\Big|\leq\epsilon\end{aligned}

where
\gamma
,
\epsilon
are certain positive constants. However, the solution does not have uniformly decaying estimates unless the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{v\in[u_{0},\infty)}{\bigg\{(1+v)^{\epsilon}|\Phi(v)|+(1+v)^{\epsilon+1}|\partial_{v}\Phi(v)|\bigg\}}\leq A_{0}

further for some
A_{0}>0
and
\epsilon>1

[
14
,
16
]
.

In
[
13
]
, Liu and Zhang studied the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with small initial data, and of generalized solutions with large initial data, for wave-like decaying null infinity. They proved that the solution satisfies the following uniformly decaying estimates

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}}

for
0<\epsilon\leq 2
, and the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete with vanishing final Bondi mass.

(ii)\,\,V\neq 0
. In
[
2
]
, Chae proved the global existence and uniqueness of the spherically symmetric Einstein-(nonlinear)Klein-Gordon system for small initial data with scalar potential

\displaystyle V(\phi)=-\frac{1}{p+1}|\phi|^{p+1}

for particle-like decaying null infinity, where
p\geq k
,
k=3
,
4
. He proved that the solution satisfies the following uniformly decaying estimates

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{k-1}},\quad\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{k}}.

It was extended to the more general situation by Wijayanto, Fadhilla, Akbar and Gunara
[
18
]
where the scalar potential satisfies

\displaystyle|V(\phi)|+\left|\frac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial\phi}\right||\phi|+\left|\frac{\partial^{2}V(\phi)}{\partial\phi^{2}}\right||\phi|^{2}\leq K_{0}|\phi|^{p+1},

(1.13)

where
p\geq k\geq 3
, constant
K_{0}>0
. (The complex version of (
1.13
) was already used for the Einstein-Maxwell-Higgs system
[
3
]
.)

In this paper, we prove the following theorem for wave-like decaying null infinity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,2]
. Given initial data
h(0,r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
, denote

\displaystyle d=\sup\limits_{r\geq 0}{\left\{(1+r)^{1+\epsilon}\left(|h(0,r)|+\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(0,r)\right|\right)\right\}}.

Then there exists
\delta>0
such that if
d<\delta
, there exists a unique global classical solution

h(u,r)\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty))

of (
1.12
) with
h(0,r)
as the initial data for scalar potential
V(\phi)
satisfying (
1.13
) where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &p\geq 2+\epsilon,\quad&\sqrt{2}-1<\epsilon\leq 2,\\
&p>\frac{3+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon},&\sqrt{2}-1\geq\epsilon>0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.14)

and the solution satisfies the following uniformly decaying estimates

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}}.

Moreover, the final Bondi mass vanishes and the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete.

For
\epsilon
,
p
satisfying the assumption of Theorem
1.1
, we define

\displaystyle\omega=\min{\{p,3\}}>2,\quad\mu=\min\left\{1+\epsilon,\,\,(\omega-1)\epsilon+\omega-2\right\}>1

throughout the paper. It is straightforward that

\displaystyle(p-1)\epsilon+p-2>1\Longrightarrow 1<(\omega-1)\epsilon+(\omega-2)\leq 2\epsilon+1.

We adopt the main argument of
[
13
]
to derive the wave-like decaying estimates with nontrivial scalar potential in order to prove the theorem. It can be easily followed from
[
18
]
when
p\geq 3
. However, it needs much careful and nontrivial analysis to achieve it when
p<3
.

We refer to
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
17
]
and the references therein for existence and uniqueness of Einstein fields equations coupled with scalar, Maxwell and Klein-Gordon fields on non-spherically symmetric metrics for non-wave-like decaying null infinity.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we derive the main estimates for the solution of the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar equations with scalar potential. In Section 3, we prove the contraction mapping property for wave-like decaying solutions. In Section 4, we prove the main theorem.

2.
Main estimates

In this section, we follow the arguments in
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
18
]
to derive some key estimates. Denote
X
the space of all
C^{1}
function
h(u,r)

defined on
[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)
such that the following norm

\displaystyle\|h\|_{X}=\sup\limits_{u\geq 0}\sup\limits_{r\geq 0}{\left\{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}\left(|h(u,r)|+\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\right)\right\}}

is finite. Let

\displaystyle B(x)=\Big\{f\in X\,\Big|\,\|f\|_{X}\leq x\Big\}

be the closed ball of radius
x
in
X
. As in
[
18
]
, consider the mapping

\displaystyle h\mapsto\mathfrak{F}(h)

which is defined as the solution of the following equation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D\mathfrak{F}=&\frac{1}{2r}(\mathfrak{F}-\bar{h})(g-q)+4\pi gr(\mathfrak{F}-\bar{h})V+\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar{h}}\\
=&\left(\frac{g-q}{2r}+4\pi grV\right)\mathfrak{F}-\left(\frac{g-q}{2r}+4\pi grV\right)\bar{h}+\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar{h}}.\end{aligned}

(2.1)

with the initial data

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(0,r)=h(0,r).

Let
r(u)=\chi(u;r_{0})
be the characteristic which satisfies the ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{du}=-\frac{q(u,r)}{2},\quad r(0)=r_{0}.

Denote
r_{1}=\chi(u_{1};r_{0})
. Integrating (
2.1
) along the characteristic
\chi
, we can obtain the explicit expression of
\mathfrak{F}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathfrak{F}(u_{1},r_{1})=&h(0,r_{0})\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{u_{1}}{\left[\frac{g-q}{2r}+4\pi grV\right]_{\chi}du}\right\}\\
&+\int_{0}^{u_{1}}{\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{u_{1}}{\left[\frac{g-q}{2r}+4\pi grV\right]_{\chi}du^{\prime}}\right\}[f]_{\chi}du}.\end{aligned}

(2.2)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,2]
. Given the initial data
h(0,r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle d=\|h(0,r)\|_{X}=\sup\limits_{r\geq 0}{\left\{(1+r)^{1+\epsilon}\left(|h(0,r)|+\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(0,r)\right|\right)\right\}}.

Assume

\displaystyle\|h(u,r)\|_{X}=x.

Then the solution of (
2.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{F}(h)(u,r)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(d+x^{3}+x^{p}+x^{p+2})\exp\left[C(x^{2}+x^{p+1})\right]}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\mathfrak{F}(h)}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(d+x^{3}+x^{p}+x^{p+2})\exp\left[C(x^{2}+x^{p+1})\right]}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}}P(x).

(2.4)

Moreover, there exists
x_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in(0,x_{0})
,
d\leq F_{1}(x)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(B(x))\subset B(x).

Here

\displaystyle P(x)=

\displaystyle 1+x^{2}+x^{p+1}+x^{p+3},

\displaystyle F_{1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{x\exp\left[-A(x^{2}+x^{p+1})\right]}{A(1+x^{2}+x^{p+1}+x^{p+3})}-(x^{3}+x^{p}+x^{p+2}),

and
A
and
C
are some positive constants.

Proof:
Let
c=\frac{6}{\epsilon|1-\epsilon|}
for
\epsilon\neq 1
and
c=24
for
\epsilon=1
. In
[
13
]
, we proved that if

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{x}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\leq\frac{x}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}

for some constant
x>0
, then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle|\bar{h}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2x}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\left(1+u\right)^{\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)},

(2.5)

\displaystyle|h-\bar{h}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{cxr\left(1+u\right)^{1-\epsilon}}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{2}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle|g-\bar{g}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{3}\frac{r^{2}}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon-1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{3}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(u,r)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{3}\right).

(2.8)

These estimates yield the following claim.

Claim
: Let
C_{1}=\frac{2^{p+2}K_{0}}{\omega(\omega-1)(\omega-2)\epsilon^{p+1}}
. Then, for
p>2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}{gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds}\leq\frac{C_{1}x^{p+1}r^{3}}{\left(1+u\right)^{(\omega+1)\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{\omega}}.

(2.9)

Proof of Claim:
If
2<p<3
, using (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{r}{gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds}&\leq\frac{K_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}}{\epsilon^{p+1}\left(1+u\right)^{(p+1)\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{r}{\frac{s^{2}}{(1+u+s)^{p+1}}ds}\\
&\leq\frac{K_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}\left[F(r)+2\left(1+u\right)^{p-1}r^{3}\right]}{p(p-1)(p-2)\left(1+u\right)^{(p+1)\epsilon+p}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p}},\\
\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle F(r)=

\displaystyle 2\left(1+u\right)^{2}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p}-p(p-1)\left(1+u\right)^{p}\left(1+u+r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2p(p-2)\left(1+u\right)^{p+1}\left(1+u+r\right)

\displaystyle-(p-1)(p-2)\left(1+u\right)^{p+2}-2\left(1+u\right)^{p-1}r^{3}.

It is straightforward that

\displaystyle F^{(4)}(r)\leq 0.

By analyzing the monotonicity, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &F^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\leq F^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=\big[2p(p-1)(p-2)-12\big]\left(1+u\right)^{p-1}\leq 0\\
\Longrightarrow&F^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq F^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\Longrightarrow F^{\prime}(r)\leq F^{\prime}(0)=0\Longrightarrow F(r)\leq F(0)=0.\end{aligned}

This implies (
2.9
).

If
p\geq 3
, we can use a similar argument to prove that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}{gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}}{\epsilon^{p+1}\left(1+u\right)^{4\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{r}{\frac{s^{2}}{(1+u+s)^{4}}ds}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}r^{3}}{3\epsilon^{p+1}\left(1+u\right)^{4\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{3}}.

Therefore, (
2.9
) follows.

Let
C_{2}=\frac{4\pi c^{2}}{3}+8\pi C_{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |g-q|\leq&|g-\bar{g}|+\frac{8\pi}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds}\\
\leq&\frac{C_{2}r^{2}(x^{2}+x^{p+1})}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon+2-\omega}\left(1+u+r\right)^{\omega}}.\end{aligned}

(2.10)

Denote

\displaystyle k(x)=\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{3}\right)-8\pi C_{1}x^{p+1},\quad x\geq 0.

Clearly,
k(x)
is monotonically decreasing and

\displaystyle k(0)=1,\quad k(\infty)=-\infty.

Then there exists
x_{1}>0
such that for any
x\in[0,x_{1})
,

\displaystyle 0<k(x)\leq 1.

Thus, on
[0,x_{1})
,

\displaystyle q\geq\bar{g}(u,0)-\left|\frac{8\pi}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{gs^{2}V(\bar{h})ds}\right|\geq k(x)>0.

(2.11)

Therefore

\displaystyle r(u)=r_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{u}^{u_{1}}{q(u,\chi(u;r_{0}))du}\geq r_{1}+\frac{k}{2}(u_{1}-u).

This yields

\displaystyle 1+r(u)+u\geq 1+r_{1}+\frac{k}{2}(u_{1}-u)+u\geq\frac{k}{2}(1+r_{1}+u_{1}).

Taking
u=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|h(0,r_{0})|\leq\frac{d}{(1+r_{0})^{1+\epsilon}}\leq\frac{2^{1+\epsilon}d}{k^{1+\epsilon}(1+r_{1}+u_{1})^{1+\epsilon}}.

(2.12)

Let
C_{3}=\frac{C_{2}}{2\epsilon}+\frac{2^{p+3}K_{0}\pi}{\epsilon^{p+2}}
. Using (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{u_{1}}&{\left[\frac{1}{2r}\left|(g-q)\right|+4\pi gr|V(\bar{h})|\right]_{\chi}du}\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\left[\frac{C_{2}r^{2}(x^{2}+x^{p+1})}{2r\left(1+u\right)^{\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{4\pi rK_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}}{\epsilon^{p+1}\left(1+u\right)^{(p+1)\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p+1}}\right]_{\chi}du\\
\leq&C_{3}(x^{2}+x^{p+1}).\end{aligned}

(2.13)

Now we derive (
2.3
). Using (
2.7
) and (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{2r}(g-q)\bar{h}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}r^{2}(x^{2}+x^{p+1})}{2r\left(1+u\right)^{\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{2x}{\epsilon\left(1+u\right)^{\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}(x^{3}+x^{p+2})}{\epsilon\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}},

\displaystyle|4\pi grV(\bar{h})\bar{h}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi rK_{0}2^{p+1}x^{p+1}}{\epsilon^{p+1}\left(1+u\right)^{(p+1)\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p+1}}\frac{2x}{\epsilon\left(1+u\right)^{\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p+4}\pi K_{0}x^{p+2}}{\epsilon^{p+2}\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon+1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V(\bar{h})}{\partial\bar{h}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}K_{0}x^{p}r}{2\epsilon^{p}\left(1+u\right)^{p\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p}}\leq\frac{2^{p-1}K_{0}x^{p}}{\epsilon^{p}\left(1+u\right)^{p\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{p-1}}.

Let
C_{4}=\max{\left\{\frac{2^{p-1}K_{0}}{\epsilon^{p}},\,\,2C_{3}\right\}}
, and

\displaystyle f=-\left(\frac{g-q}{2r}+4\pi grV\right)\bar{h}+\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar{h}}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |f|&\leq\left|-\frac{1}{2r}(g-q)\bar{h}\right|+|4\pi grV(\bar{h})\bar{h}|+\left|\frac{gr}{2}\frac{\partial V(\bar{h})}{\partial\bar{h}}\right|\\
&\leq\frac{C_{4}(x^{3}+x^{p}+x^{p+2})}{\left(1+u\right)^{(\omega-1)\epsilon+\omega-2}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}.\end{aligned}

This implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{u_{1}}{[f]_{\chi}du}&\leq\int_{0}^{u_{1}}{\left[\frac{C_{4}(x^{3}+x^{p}+x^{p+2})}{\left(1+u\right)^{(\omega-1)\epsilon+\omega-2}\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}\right]_{\chi}du}\\

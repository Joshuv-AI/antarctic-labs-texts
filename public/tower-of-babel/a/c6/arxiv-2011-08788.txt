2.
Background

Here we collect the results that we will need in this article and give some basic background information. Given a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
one may attach various numerical measures for the complexity of this action.

(1)

The first dynamical degree:

\lambda_{1}(f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}((f^{n})^{*}H\cdot H^{\dim X-1})^{\frac{1}{n}}

Here
H
is an ample divisor on
X
. The limit can be shown to exist and be independent of the choice of
H
.

(2)

Given a point
P\in X(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
f^{n}(P)
is defined for all
n
we define the upper arithmetic degree

\overline{\alpha_{f}}(P):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{H}^{+}(f^{n}(P))^{\frac{1}{n}}

and the lower arithmetic degree

\underline{\alpha_{f}}(P):=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{H}^{+}(f^{n}(P))^{\frac{1}{n}}

where
H
is an ample divisor,
h_{H}
is a choice of height function for
H
, and
h_{H}^{+}(P)=\max\{1,h_{H}(P)\}
. We think of the lower and upper arithmetic degrees as arithmetic measures of the complexity of
f
.

A related and interesting conjecture to the Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture is the following.

4.1.
The case of a finitely generated nef cone

Let
X
be a normal projective variety defined over a number field

with a finitely generated (not necessarily rational) nef cone, and Picard number
\rho(X)=\rho
. Suppose that we are given a
f\colon X\rightarrow X
is a surjective endomorphism. Suppose that the nef cone of

has rays

. Since the action of

is a linear isomorphism at the level of vector spaces that preserves the nef cone it preserves the boundary and so acts as a permutation on the rays. After iterating

we may assume that

fixes all of the rays. Since the nef cone is a full dimensional pointed cone, we have that

In particular, by taking a linearly independent set of rays say
v_{1},...,v_{\rho}
we have that the action of

on

is diagonalizable over
\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore if

for some

To see why let

So

Since since some

we see that

for some

and that

.

We restrict to the case of a variety with Picard number

Proposition 4.13
.

Let

In fact in the setting of Picard number 2 we may assume that the nef cone and Pseudo-effective cone coincide.

Proposition 4.14

From the perspective of the minimal model program (

As we want to deal with integral eigendivisors we need to work with varieties whose nef cone contains a rational ray on the boundary. The following elementary computation shows that in the presence of a surjective endomorphism with distinct eigenvalues, the boundary is rational, or completely irrational. That is, we cannot have an irrational ray and a rational ray.

Proposition 4.15

First note that

5.

The methods developed in this article have applications in the case of projective bundles. Given a variety

generates a ray of

\kappa(\mathcal{L})\geq 0
we have that
\kappa(\mathcal{L})>0
and we obtain the Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture by (
2.6
). So we may assume that
K_{X}=0
. As
\kappa(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})\geq 0
we see that
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
is pseudo-effective. If
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
is big then it is also ample as we have assumed the nef cone and pseudo-effective cone coincide. In a Fano variety any nef divisor has an effective multiple. Since we have assumed that
f
has a good eigenspace we may apply (
4.8
) to obtain the Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture. Thus we may assume that
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
generates a boundary ray of the pseudo-effective cone which we have assumed is the nef cone. Therefore
f^{*}(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})\equiv_{\textnormal{Num}}\lambda(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})
. Suppose first that
\kappa(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})>0
. Then by
[
16
, Prposition 1.6]
the Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture holds for
f
. Assume that
\kappa(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})=0
. We have that
f^{*}(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})-R=-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
where
R
is some effective divisor. The adjunction formula tells us that

f^{*}(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})=-K_{\mathbb{P}E}+R

We now use the argument found in
[
16
, Proposition 9.2 (2)]
. By
[
16
, Proposition 6.1]
applied to
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
we have that
f^{-1}(\textnormal{supp}(-K_{\mathbb{P}E}))=\textnormal{supp}(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})
. Write
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
where the
D_{i}
are prime divisors and the
a_{i}>0
. Then after iterating
f
we have that
f^{-1}(\textnormal{supp}(D_{i}))=\textnormal{supp}(D_{i})
. Since
D_{i}
is a prime divisor this tells us that
f^{*}D_{i}=\mu_{i}D_{i}
for some number
\mu_{i}
. On the other hand. Since
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
is not the pull back of some line bundle on
X
we have that
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}\equiv_{\textnormal{Num}}r\mathcal{L}
where
r
is the rank of
E
. Thus we have that
D_{i}\equiv_{\textnormal{Num}}b_{i}\mathcal{L}
as
\mathcal{L}
is extremal. Since we have that
f^{*}D_{i}\equiv_{\textnormal{Num}}\lambda_{i}D_{i}
we have that each
\mu_{i}=\lambda
. In conclusion we may write
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}=D
where
D
is some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor with
f^{*}D=\lambda D
and
\kappa(D)\geq 0
. As we have assumed the existence of a good eigenspace we may apply (
4.12
) to obtain
\kappa(D)\neq 0
and so
\kappa(D)>0
. Therefore by
(\ref{prop:yohsuke})
the Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture follows.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
C
be an elliptic curve defined over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
F_{r}
be the rank
r
Atiyah bundle on
C
. Then for any
d\geq 1
we have that

\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}=\bigoplus_{r_{i}}F_{r_{i}}

for some integers
r_{i}
.

Proof.

Recall that we have a canonical surjection

\psi\colon F_{r}^{\otimes d}\rightarrow\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}\rightarrow 0

By (
5.3
) we have that
F_{r}^{\otimes d}=\bigoplus_{i}F_{l_{i}}
for some integers
l_{i}
. Because
\deg F_{r}=0
we have
\deg\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}=0
. By taking the dual endomorphism we have a mapping

0\rightarrow(\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r})^{*}\rightarrow(E^{\otimes d})

Since we are in characteristic 0 and
F_{r}^{*}=F_{r}
we have that
(\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r})^{*}=\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}^{*}=\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
and
(F_{r}^{\otimes d})^{*}=F_{r}^{\otimes d}
. Thus
\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
is a sub-bundle of
F_{r}^{\otimes d}
. Since
F_{r}^{\otimes d}
is semi-stable of degree zero (being the tensor product of semi-stable vector bundles) we have that
\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
is also semi-stable and degree
0
. Now let
W
be an indecomposable component of
\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
. Since
\deg\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}=0
we have that
\deg W\leq 0
as otherwise
W
destabilizes
\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
. Now suppose that
\deg W<0
. Then since
\deg\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}=0
there is some other indecomposable component
W^{\prime}
with
\deg W^{\prime}>0
. This contradicts the argument just given. So
\deg W=0
. Thus each component of
\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}
is degree
0
. It follows that each component is also semi-stable, being a degree zero sub-bundle of a semi-stable bundle of degree degree zero. From (
5.3
) we have that

\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r}=\bigoplus_{j}F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j}

where
L_{j}
is some degree 0 line bundle. Now we have that

\hom(F_{r}^{\otimes d},\textnormal{Sym}^{d}F_{r})=\hom(\oplus_{i}F_{l_{i}},\oplus_{j}F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})=\oplus_{i,j}\hom(F_{l_{i}},F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})

Now we have that
\hom(F_{l_{i}},F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})=H^{0}(C,F_{l_{i}}^{*}\otimes F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})=H^{0}(C,(F_{l_{i}}\otimes F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})
as the Atiyah bundle is self dual. Suppose that
L_{j}\neq\mathcal{O}_{C}
for some
j
. Then as
L_{j}
is of degree
0
we have that
H^{0}(F_{l_{i}}\otimes F_{r_{j}}\otimes L_{j})=0
by (
5.3
). However, this is a contradiction as
\psi
is a surjection. So we have that each
L_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
and the claim follows.

∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
E
be a semi-stable degree zero vector bundle on an elliptic curve
C
.

(1)

We may write
E=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}F_{r_{k}}\otimes L_{k}
where
F_{r_{k}}
are the rank
r_{k}
Atiyah bundles and
L_{k}
is a degree zero vector bundle.

(2)

If
E=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}F_{r_{k}}\otimes L_{k}
with
L_{i}
degree zero line bundles with
L_{0}=0
then
\kappa(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})\geq 0
.

Proof.

As
E
is semi-stable of degree 0 every sub-sheaf has degree at most
0
. Let
W_{0},...,W_{N}
be the components of
E
. If
\deg W_{i}<0
as
\sum_{k=0}^{N}\deg W_{i}=0
there must be some
W_{i}
with
\deg W_{i}>0
which would destabilize
E
. Thus every indecomposable component is degree zero. It follows from the definition of semi-stable that each irreducible component is also semi-stable. By (
5.3
) we have that
W_{i}=F_{r_{i}}\otimes L_{i}
for some degree 0 line bundle as needed. We now turn to part 2. We have from the Euler exact sequence that

-K_{\mathbb{P}E}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E}(r)-\det E

where
r
is the rank of
E
. Then

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathbb{P}E,-K_{\mathbb{P}E})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E}(r)\otimes\det E^{-1})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(C,\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E}(r))\otimes\det H^{-1})=H^{0}(C,\textnormal{Sym}^{r}(E)\otimes\det E^{-1})

We have

\textnormal{Sym}^{r}(E)=\bigoplus_{i_{0}+...+i_{N}=r}\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{i_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})\otimes L_{k}^{\otimes i_{k}}

from our assumed decomposition of
E=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}F_{r_{k}}\otimes L_{k}
. Therefore

\textnormal{Sym}^{r}(E)\otimes\det E^{-1}=\bigoplus_{i_{0}+...+i_{N}=r}\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{i_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})\otimes L_{k}^{\otimes i_{k}}\otimes\det E^{-1}

Let
j_{0}=r-N
and
j_{k}=1
for
k=1,...,N
. Then
\sum_{k=0}^{N}j_{k}=r-N+N=r
. Therefore

\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{j_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})\otimes L_{k}^{\otimes j_{k}}\otimes\det E^{-1}

is a direct summand of
\textnormal{Sym}^{r}(E)\otimes\det E^{-1}
. Since
\det E=\sum_{k=0}^{N}L_{k}
and
L_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
we have that

\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{j_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})\otimes L_{k}^{\otimes j_{k}}\otimes\det E^{-1}=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{j_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})

since
\sum_{k=0}^{N}j_{k}L_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}j_{k}L_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}L_{k}
using
L_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
and the construction of the
j_{k}
. We have shown that
\bigotimes_{k=0}^{N}\textnormal{Sym}^{j_{k}}(F_{r_{k}})
(which has a global section by (
5.4
)) is a direct summand of
\textnormal{Sym}^{r}(E)\otimes\det E^{-1}
. Therefore
-K_{\mathbb{P}E}
has a non-zero global section and
\kappa(-K_{\mathbb{P}E})\geq 0
as required.

∎

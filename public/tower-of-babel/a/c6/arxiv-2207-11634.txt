Abstract
. The objective of this study is to advance the theory concerning positive summing operators. Our focus lies in examining the space of positive strongly p p -summable sequences and the space of positive unconditionally p p -summable sequences. We utilize these in conjunction with the Banach lattice of positive weakly p p -summable sequences to present and characterize the classes of positive strongly ( p , q ) (p,q) -summing operators, positive ( p , q ) (p,q) -summing, and positive Cohen ( p , q ) (p,q) -nuclear operators. Additionally, we describe these classes in terms of the continuity of an associated tensor operator that is defined between tensor products of sequences spaces.

Introduction and background

The spaces of sequences with values in a Banach lattice are intimately related

(p,q)

(p,q)
-summing operators to characterize those operators
whose adjoints are positive
(q^{\ast},p^{\ast})
-summing operators.

In

Tensor products have proved to be a useful tool for the theory of operator
ideals. Indeed, the excellent monograph
[
9
]
deals with the tensor

study of the structure of several spaces of summing linear operators.

The following characterizations provide nice examples of how tensor products
come into the theory of summing operators:

•

An operator

is absolutely

-summing (see

[

•

Let

[

]

[
9

An operator

The interplay between tensor products and positive summing operators have not
been explored yet. In this paper, first we utilize sequences in Banach lattice
spaces to define and characterize certain classes of positive summing
operators. Then we describe these classes in terms of the continuity of the
canonically defined tensor product operator

for adequate

and
tensor norms
\alpha

\beta

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle T(x_{i}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\||\langle T(\frac{x_{i}}{\|x_{i}\|}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle T(\frac{x_{i}}{\|x_{i}\|}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|^{q^{\ast}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{q^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{\Upsilon_{p}}.\|(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{q}.\|(y_{i}^{\ast})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{p^{\ast},|\omega|}.

This implies that
T
is positive strongly
(p,q)
-summing and
\|T\|_{D_{p,q}^{+}}\leq\|T\|_{\Upsilon_{p,q}}.

Conversely,
assume that
T
is positive strongly
(p,q)
-summing. Let
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}

be a finite sequence in
B_{E}
and
(y_{i}^{\ast})_{i=1}^{n}
in
(Y^{\ast})^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle T(z_{i}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|^{q^{\ast}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{q^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in B_{\ell_{q}}}|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\langle T(z_{i}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in B_{\ell_{q}}}|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle T(\lambda_{i}z_{i}),y_{i}^{\ast}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{D_{p,q}^{+}}.\sup_{(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in B_{\ell_{q}}}\|(\lambda_{i}z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{q}.\|(y_{i}^{\ast})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{p^{\ast},|\omega|}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{D_{p,q}^{+}}.\sup_{(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in B_{\ell_{q}}}\|(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{q}.\|(y_{i}^{\ast})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{p^{\ast},|\omega|}

\displaystyle=\|T\|_{D_{p,q}^{+}}.\|(y_{i}^{\ast})_{i=1}^{n}\|_{p^{\ast},|\omega|}.

This means that
T
is positive
(p,q)
-majorizing and
\|T\|_{\Upsilon_{p,q}}\leq\|T\|_{D_{p,q}^{+}}.

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

T\in\mathcal{L}(E,Y)
is positive
p
-majorizing if and only if
T
is
positive strongly
p
-summing.

5.

Now we are interested to characterize the aforementioned classes using

The Wittstock injective tensor Product and Fremlin projective tensor
Product.
For Banach lattices

Wittstock
[
24

\left\|u\right\|_{i}=\inf\left\{\sup\left\{\left\|T_{v}(x^{\ast})\right\|:x^{\ast}\in B_{X_{+}^{\ast}}\right\}:v\in C_{i},u\pm u\in C_{i}\right\}

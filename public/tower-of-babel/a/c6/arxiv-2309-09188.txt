Simon Conjecture and the v -number of monomial ideals
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. Let I ⊂ S I\subset S be a graded ideal of a standard graded polynomial ring S S with coefficients in a field K K , and let v ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{v}(I) be the v \operatorname{v} -number of I I . In previous work, we showed that for any graded ideal I ⊂ S I\subset S generated in a single degree, then v ⁡ ( I k ) = α ⁡ ( I ) ​ k + b \operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b , for all k ≫ 0 k\gg 0 , where α ⁡ ( I ) \alpha(I) is the initial degree of I I and b b is a suitable integer. In the present paper, using polarization, we extend Simon conjecture to any monomial ideal. As a consequence, if Simon conjecture holds, and all powers of I I have linear quotients, then b ∈ { − 1 , 0 } b\in\{-1,0\} . This fact suggest that if I I is an equigenerated monomial ideal with linear powers, then v ⁡ ( I k ) = α ⁡ ( I ) ​ k − 1 \operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1 , for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We verify this conjecture for monomial ideals with linear powers having depth ⁡ S / I = 0 \operatorname{depth}S/I=0 , edge ideals with linear resolution, polymatroidal ideals, and Hibi ideals.

1.
Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigoplus_{d}S_{d}
be the standard graded polynomial ring with
n
variables and coefficients in a field
K
, and let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be the graded maximal ideal. We denote the set of associated primes of
I
by
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, and by
\operatorname{Max}(I)
the set of associated primes of
I
that are maximal with respect to the inclusion. The concept of
\operatorname{v}
-number was introduced in
[
7
]
, and further studied in
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
17
,
20
,
25
,
26
,
29
,
30
,
32
]
. Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
at
\mathfrak{p}
is defined as

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \min\{d\ :\ \textit{there exists}\ f\in S_{d}\ \textit{such that}\ (I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\}.

Whereas, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
is defined as

\operatorname{v}(I)\ =\ \min\{d\ :\ \textit{there exists}\ f\in S_{d}\ \textit{such that}\ (I:f)\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. In
[
17
]
the asymptotic behaviour of the function
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
, called the
\operatorname{v}
-function
of
I
, was investigated. It is known by Brodmann
[
4
]
that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
stabilizes for large
k
. That is,
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. A prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset S
such that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
, is called a
stable prime of
I
. The set of the stable primes of
I
is denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Thus, for all
k\gg 0
,

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k}).

It is expected that for any graded ideal
I\subset S
,
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
becomes a linear function in
k
for
k\gg 0

[
17
, Conjecture 4.5]
. Such a conjecture has been proved when
I
is generated in a single degree, when
I
does not have embedded stable primes
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
, and for all monomial ideals in two variables
[
17
, Theorem 5.1]
.

To compute the
\operatorname{v}
-number, we use the following result of Grisalde, Reyes and Villarreal
[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
. Let
M=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}M_{d}\neq 0
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. We call
\alpha(M)=\min\{d:M_{d}\neq 0\}
, the
initial degree
of
M
, and set
\omega(M)=\max\{d:(M/\mathfrak{m}M)_{d}\neq 0\}
. The bar
\overline{\phantom{p}}
denotes the residue class modulo
I
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. The following hold.

(a)

If
\mathcal{G}=\{\overline{g_{1}},\dots,\overline{g_{r}}\}
is a homogeneous minimal generating set of
(I:\mathfrak{p})/I
, then

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\deg(g_{i})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq r\ \textit{and}\ (I:g_{i})=\mathfrak{p}\}.

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

(c)

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\geq\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I)
, with equality if
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Max}(I)
.

(d)

If
I
has no embedded primes, then
\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

In this paper, we study the
\operatorname{v}
-function of monomial ideals with linear powers.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we show in Theorem
2.3
that for an equigenerated graded ideal
I\subset S
, we have
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for some integer
b
, and all
k\gg 0
. In general, the integer
b
depends on
\textup{char}(K)
, as we show in Example
2.4
. However, if
I
is a monomial ideal, then
b
does not depend on
\textup{char}(K)
(Proposition
2.5
) and moreover
b\geq-1
(Theorem
2.3
(c)). Indeed, the lower bound
\alpha(I)k-1\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
(Proposition
2.2
) always holds. In general, we also have the lower bound
\alpha(I)k\leq\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})
for the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. If
I
has linear powers, then this lower bound is achieved for all
k
. In view of this fact, it is natural to expect that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
, when
I
is a monomial ideal with linear powers (Conjecture
2.6
). Next, we investigate this conjecture.

A particular class of monomial ideals with linear powers is that of equigenerated monomial ideals whose all powers have linear quotients. Linear quotients is the algebraic counterpart of the concept of shellability in simplicial complexes theory. A famous conjecture of Simon
[
31
, Conjecture 4.2.1]
can be expressed, in algebraic terms, by saying that any squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in a single degree
d
and having linear quotients, can be extended to the squarefree Veronese ideal
I_{n,d}
by linear quotients (Conjecture
3.1
). We extend this conjecture to all monomial ideals (Conjecture
3.2
). Surprisingly, by using polarization, it turns out that the conjectures are equivalent (Proposition
3.4
). For another instance of such an use of polarization, see
[
11
, Proposition 1.8]
. As a consequence, if Simon conjecture holds, then for any monomial ideal generated in a single degree and whose all powers have linear quotients, either
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
or
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k
, for all
k\gg 0
.

The behaviour of the
\operatorname{v}
-number of monomial ideals under polarization was studied in
[
30
]
. The authors showed that
\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})\leq\operatorname{v}(I)
and equality holds in some special cases. In Theorem
4.1
, surprisingly we show that the equality
\operatorname{v}(I)=\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})
holds for any monomial ideal
I
.

Finally, we prove that Conjecture
2.6
holds: when
\operatorname{depth}S/I=0
(Theorem
3.6
), for edge ideals with linear resolution (Theorem
5.1
), for polymatroidal ideals (Theorem
5.5
) and for Hibi ideals (Theorem
5.8
).

2.
The
\operatorname{v}
-function of monomial ideals

In this section, we summarize some basic facts about the
\operatorname{v}
-function of monomial ideals. Hereafter, for a positive integer
n
, we set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. If
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal, then each associated prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
is a
monomial prime ideal
. That is,
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{A}=(x_{i}:i\in A)
for some nonempty subset
A
of
[n]
.

The following result
[
30
, Proposition 3.11]
, due to Saha and Sengupta, is a special case of
[
17
, Proposition 4.3]
. It provides an useful general method to bound
\operatorname{v}(I)
from above, when
I
is a monomial ideal.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
f\in S\setminus I
a monomial. Then,

\operatorname{v}(I)\leq\operatorname{v}(I:f)+\deg(f).

On the other hand, one always has

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then,

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\geq\alpha(I)-1,\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ \mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I).

In particular,
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\geq\alpha(I)k-1
, for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
and let
u\in S
be a monomial such that
(I:u)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\deg(u)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
. Then
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{A}=(x_{i}:i\in A)
for some
A\subseteq[n]
. Thus
x_{i}u\in I
for all
i\in A
. In particular,
\deg(x_{i}u)\geq\alpha(I)
. Hence,
\deg(u)\geq\alpha(I)-1
, as desired.

∎

From the work of
[
17
]
, the next result follows.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal generated in a single degree.

(a)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, there exists an integer
b_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a linear function in
k
, for all
k\gg 0
.

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for all
k\gg 0
, where
b=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}b_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(c)

If
I
is a monomial ideal, then
b_{\mathfrak{p}},b\geq-1
, for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

Proof.

The proof of
[
17
, Theorem 3.1(b)]
shows that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b_{\mathfrak{p}}
, for all
k\gg 0
and all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, for some
b_{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Setting
b=\min\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}b_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}(\alpha(I)k+b_{\mathfrak{p}})\ =\ \alpha(I)k+b,

for all
k\gg 0
, and (a) and (b) follow. Part (c) follows from Proposition
2.2
.

∎

Recall that a graded ideal
I\subset S
has a
d
-linear resolution
if it is generated in a single degree
d
, and for all
i\geq 0
,
\beta_{i,j}(I)=0
if
j\neq i+d
. Equivalently, an equigenerated ideal
I
has linear resolution, if and only if, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity is as small as possible, namely
\operatorname{reg}(I)=\alpha(I)
. We say that
I
has
linear powers
if
I^{k}
has a linear resolution, for all
k\geq 1
. Famous examples of monomial ideals with linear powers are given in the following list.

(i)

Edge ideals with linear resolution
[
23
, Theorem 10.2.6]
.

(ii)

Polymatroidal ideals
[
23
, Corollary 12.6.4]
.

(iii)

Hibi ideals
[
23
, Corollary 10.2.9 and Theorem 9.1.13]
, or
[
8
, Corollary 4.11]
.

In general, the
\operatorname{v}
-function of a graded ideal
I
depends on the characteristic of
K
, even when
I
has linear powers.

Example 2.4
.

Indeed, consider the principal ideal
I=(x^{2}+y^{2})
of
S={\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]
. Then
I^{k}
is principal as well, and thus
I
has linear powers. Since
x^{2}+y^{2}
is an irreducible polynomial over
{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]
,
I
is a prime ideal, and thus
I^{k}
is a
I
-primary ideal and
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\{I\}
for all
k
. Note that

(I^{k}:I)/I^{k}\ =\ (((x^{2}+y^{2})^{k}):(x^{2}+y^{2}))/I^{k}\ =\ ((x^{2}+y^{2})^{k-1})/I^{k}

for all
k\geq 1
. Hence,
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{I}(I^{k})=2k-2
for all
k\geq 1
.

On the other hand, if
I=(x^{2}+y^{2})
is an ideal of
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
, then
(x^{2}+y^{2})^{k}=(x+iy)^{k}(x-iy)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Setting
\mathfrak{p}_{1}=(x+iy)
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}=(x-iy)
, then
I^{k}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{k}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k}
is the primary decomposition of
I^{k}
and
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}\}
for all
k\geq 1
. Note that

(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p}_{1})/I^{k}\ =\ (((x^{2}+y^{2})^{k}):(x+iy))/I^{k}\ =\ ((x+iy)^{k-1}(x-iy)^{k})/I^{k}

for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(I^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. By symmetry, we also have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(I^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
.

However, the primary decomposition of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
does not depend on the characteristic,
\textup{char}(K)
, of the field
K

[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
. Next, since each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
is a monomial prime ideal, it follows from
[
23
, Proposition 1.2.2]
that the monomial
K
-basis of
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
is independent from
\text{char}(K)
. This observations show that

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then, the functions
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
and all
k\gg 0
, and the function
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
for
k\geq 1
, do not depend upon
\textup{char}(K)
.

Now, suppose that
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then, the regularity of
I^{k}
attains its minimum value possible, namely
\alpha(I^{k})=k\alpha(I)
. Hence,
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k
, for all
k\geq 1
. Thus it is natural to expect that the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I^{k}
attains its minimal possible value for all
k
, namely
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
.

Conjecture 2.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ =\ \alpha(I)k-1,\ \ \text{for all}\ k\geq 1.

If
I\subset S
is a graded ideal with linear powers, but not a monomial ideal, then the above conjecture is false, as the ideal
(x^{2}+y^{2})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]
of Example
2.4
shows.

Similarly, if
I
is a monomial ideal, but does not have linear powers, Conjecture
2.6
is no longer valid. Next example is due to Terai
[
6
, Remark 3]
. If
\textup{char}(K)\neq 2
, the Stanley Reisner ideal
I=(abd,abf,ace,adc,aef,bde,bcf,bce,cdf,def)
of the minimal triangulation of the projective plane has a linear resolution, but
I^{2}
has not. By using
Macaulay2

[
19
]
, we have
\operatorname{v}(I)=\alpha(I)=3
and
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=3k-1
for all
k\geq 2
.

3.
The monomial Simon conjecture

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
G(I)
be the minimal monomial generating set of
I
. We say that
I
has
linear quotients
if there exists an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
of
G(I)
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
is generated by variables, for
j=2,\dots,m
.

The monomial ideal of
S
generated by all squarefree monomial ideals of degree
1\leq d\leq n
is called the
Veronese ideal
of degree
d
of
S
, and it is denoted by
I_{n,d}
. For instance,
I_{4,3}=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{2}x_{4},x_{1}x_{3}x_{4},x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})
.

Let
I\subset J
be monomial ideals with
G(I)\subset G(J)
. We say that
I
can be
extended to
J
by linear quotients
if the set
G(J)\setminus G(I)
can be ordered as
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
such that
(I,v_{1},\dots,v_{j-1}):v_{j}
is generated by variables, for all
j=2,\dots,r
.

The famous Simon conjecture
[
31
, Conjecture 4.2.1]
states that the skeleton of any simplex is
extendably shellable
, see
[
2
]
and the references therein for more details on this topic. In algebraic terms, the conjecture can be equivalently stated as follows.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal with linear quotients such that
G(I)\subset G(I_{n,d})
. Then
I
can be extended to
I_{n,d}
by linear quotients.

It is natural to extend the above conjecture to all monomial ideals.

Conjecture 3.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear quotients such that
G(I)\subset G(\mathfrak{m}^{d})
. Then
I
can be extended to
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
by linear quotients.

We refer to the above conjecture as the
monomial Simon conjecture
. Since the squarefree part of an ideal with linear quotients has linear quotients
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
, this latter conjecture implies the usual Simon conjecture. Surprisingly, however, it turns out that the two statements are equivalent, as we show next.

Proposition 3.3
.

Simon conjecture is equivalent to the monomial Simon conjecture.

We use
polarization
. Let
u=x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{b_{n}}\in S
be a monomial. The
x_{i}
-degree
of
u
is the integer
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=b_{i}
for all
i
. The
polarization
of
u
is the monomial

u^{\wp}\ =\ \prod_{i=1}^{n}(\prod_{j=1}^{b_{i}}x_{i,j})\ =\ \prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
b_{i}>0\end{subarray}}x_{i,1}x_{i,2}\cdots x_{i,b_{i}}

in the polynomial ring
K[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[b_{i}]]
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Following
[
14
]
, the
bounding multidegree
of
I
is the vector
{\bf deg}(I)=(\deg_{x_{1}}(I),\dots,\deg_{x_{n}}(I))
, with
\deg_{x_{i}}(I)=\max_{u\in G(I)}\deg_{x_{i}}(u)
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. The
polarization
of
I
is defined to be the squarefree ideal
I^{\wp}
of
S^{\wp}=K[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[\deg_{x_{i}}(I)]]
such that
G(I^{\wp})=\{u^{\wp}:u\in G(I)\}
.

Attached to the polarization of
I
, we have the specialization map
\pi:S^{\wp}\rightarrow S
defined by setting
\pi(x_{i,j})=x_{i}
for all
i
and
j
.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

We only need to show that the Simon conjecture implies the monomial version. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in degree
d
with linear quotients. Say with order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
is generated by variables, for
j=2,\dots,m
. Then,
[
24
, Lemma 3.3]
(see, also,
[
8
, Lemma 4.10]
) implies that
I^{\wp}
also has linear quotients with order
u_{1}^{\wp},\dots,u_{m}^{\wp}
. Denote by
J
the Veronese ideal of degree
d
of
S^{\wp}
. By our assumption,
I^{\wp}
can be extended to
J
by linear quotients. Thus, there is an order
u_{1}^{\wp},\dots,u_{m}^{\wp},v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
of
G(J)
such that
J
has linear quotients with respect to this order. Applying the specialization map, we obtain an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m},\pi(v_{1}),\dots,\pi(v_{r})
of generators of
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
, for
j=2,\dots,m
, and
(u_{1},\dots,u_{m},\pi(v_{1}),\dots,\pi(v_{j-1})):\pi(v_{j})
, for
j=2,\dots,r
, are generated by variables. Removing all minimal generators of
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
in this order wherever they appear again, we obtain an order of the minimal generators of
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
whose beginning is
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
has linear quotients with respect to this order. Hence,
I
can be extended to
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
by linear quotients, and the monomial Simon conjecture holds.

∎

As a consequence, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume the monomial Simon conjecture. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree and having linear quotients. Then

\alpha(I)-1\leq\operatorname{v}(I)\leq\alpha(I).

Proof.

Let
\alpha(I)=d
. If
I=\mathfrak{m}^{d}
, then
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
,
(I:\mathfrak{m})/I=\mathfrak{m}^{d-1}/\mathfrak{m}^{d}
and so
\operatorname{v}(I)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=d-1=\alpha(I)-1
. Suppose now that
I\neq\mathfrak{m}^{d}
. Then,
I
can be extended to
\mathfrak{m}^{d}
by linear quotients. Hence there exists a monomial
u\in\mathfrak{m}^{d}\setminus I
such that
(I:u)
is generated by variables. Applying Proposition
2.1
, we get

\operatorname{v}(I)\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}(I:u)+\deg(u)=0+d=\alpha(I).

This inequality together with Proposition
2.2
yields the conclusion.

∎

The next result supports Conjecture
2.6
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume the monomial Simon conjecture. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in single degree whose all powers have linear quotients. Then, either
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
or
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k
, for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
(c),
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for all
k\gg 0
, with
b\geq-1
. On the other hand, Proposition
3.4
implies that
b\leq 0
. Thus either
b=-1
or
b=0
.

∎

We end the section, with the next general result which supports Conjecture
2.6
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers, such that
\operatorname{depth}S/I=0
. Then,

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1,\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(I)=d
. By
[
23
, Proposition 10.3.4]
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{k})
is a nonincreasing function. Hence,
\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

On the other hand, by
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
, the condition
\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}=0
is equivalent to
G(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})_{dk-1}\neq 0
, for all
k\geq 1
. Here
G(J)_{j}
denotes the set of all monomials of degree
j
belonging to
G(J)
. Since
I^{k}
is generated in degree
dk
, it follows that
\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{k})=dk-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence, Theorem
1.1
(c) implies that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})=\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{k})=dk-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Equality follows from Proposition
2.2
.

∎

4.
Reduction to the squarefree case

In
[
30
]
, the authors studied the behaviour of the
\operatorname{v}
-number of monomial ideals under polarization. They showed that
\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})\leq\operatorname{v}(I)
and equality holds in some special cases. In this section, surprisingly we show that the equality
\operatorname{v}(I)=\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})
holds for all monomial ideals.

As in the previous section, let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal, and let
I^{\wp}
be the polarization of
I
in the polynomial ring
S^{\wp}=K[x_{i,j}:i\in[n],j\in[\deg_{x_{i}}(I)]]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then,

(a)

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})
with
\pi(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}
.

(b)

For all
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})
, we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})
, where
\mathfrak{p}=\pi(\mathfrak{q})
.

(c)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, we have

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{q}(I^{\wp})\ :\ \mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp}),\pi(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}\}.

(d)

\operatorname{v}(I)=\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})
.

Proof.

(a) is proved in
[
13
, Corollary 3.7]
.

(b) Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})
and let
\mathfrak{p}=\pi(\mathfrak{q})
be, thanks to part (a), the corresponding prime of
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Suppose that
f\in S^{\wp}
is a monomial such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})=\deg(f)
and
(I^{\wp}:f)=\mathfrak{q}
. In view of
[
23
, Proposition 1.2.2]
it is clear that
\mathfrak{p}=\pi(\mathfrak{q})=\pi(I^{\wp}:f)=(I:\pi(f))
. Thus,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\leq\deg(\pi(f))=\deg(f)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})
.

(c) Let
f\in S
be a monomial such that
(I:f)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\deg(f)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
. Then, by
[
24
, Lemma 3.3]
we have that
(I^{\wp}:f^{\wp})
is prime and
\mathfrak{p}=(I:f)=\pi((I^{\wp}:f^{\wp}))
. Thus
(I^{\wp}:f^{\wp})=\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})
. This shows
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
and together with part (b) we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})
. Since
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{r}}(I^{\wp})
for all
\mathfrak{r}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})
such that
\pi(\mathfrak{r})=\mathfrak{p}
, it follows that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I)
and so, also
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
, is equal to
\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{r}}(I):\mathfrak{r}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp}),\pi(\mathfrak{r})=\mathfrak{p}\}
.

(d) By part (c) we obtain that

\operatorname{v}(I)\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)}(\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{\wp})\\
\pi(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I^{\wp})).

By part (a), we conclude that the above minimum is
\operatorname{v}(I^{\wp})
, as desired.

∎

5.
The
\operatorname{v}
-number of monomial ideals with linear powers

In this section, we study equigenerated monomial ideals
I
arising from combinatorial contexts. In particular, we show that for any ideal
I
in the list (i)-(ii)-(iii) at page
2
, Conjecture
2.6
holds true.

5.1.
Edge ideals with linear resolution

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]
and edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the monomial ideal
I(G)
of
S
generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
such that
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. A graph
G
is
complete
if every
\{i,j\}
with
i,j\in[n]
,
i\neq j
, is an edge of
G
. The
open neighbourhood
of
i\in V(G)
is the set

N_{G}(i)=\{j\in V(G):\{i,j\}\in E(G)\}.

Whereas, the
closed neighbourhood
of
i\in V(G)
is defined as
N_{G}[i]=N_{G}(i)\cup\{i\}
.

A graph
G
is called
chordal
if it has no induced cycles of length bigger than three. Recall that a
perfect elimination order
of
G
is an ordering
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of its vertex set
V(G)
such that
N_{G_{i}}(v_{i})
induces a complete subgraph on
G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
\{i,i+1,\dots,n\}
. Hereafter, if
1,2,\dots,n
is a perfect elimination order of
G
, we denote it by
x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots>x_{n}
.

A famous theorem of Dirac guarantees that a finite simple graph
G
is chordal if and only if
G
admits a perfect elimination order
[
10
]
.

The
complementary graph

G^{c}
of
G
is the graph with vertex set
V(G^{c})=V(G)
and where
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G^{c}
if and only if
\{i,j\}\notin E(G)
. A graph
G
is called
cochordal
if and only if
G^{c}
is chordal. In
[
18
]
, Fröberg proved that
I(G)
has a linear resolution if and only if
G
is cochordal.

By Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
2.5
, for any graph
G
and all
k\gg 0
, we have

\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\ =\ 2k+b(G),

where
b(G)\geq-1
is a constant independent from
\textup{char}(K)
. In particular,
\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\geq 2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. As a consequence of Dirac and Fröberg theorems, we show that for any edge ideal with linear resolution this lower bound is achieved.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
I(G)
be the edge ideal of a graph
G
. Suppose that
I(G)
has a linear resolution. Then,

\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\ =\ 2k-1,\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\geq 1.

The proof is based upon the next lemma. If
A
is a subset of
V(G)
, the induced subgraph of
G
on
A
, is the graph on vertex set
A
and edges
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
such that
i,j\in A
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
I(G)
be an edge ideal with linear resolution, and let
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}
be a perfect elimination order of
G^{c}
. Then,

(I(G):x_{1})\ =\ (x_{j}:j\in N_{G}(1)).

(1)

Proof.

It is clear that

(x_{j}:j\in N_{G}(1))\ \subseteq\ (I(G):x_{1}).

To end the proof, we show the opposite inclusion. Let
x_{k}x_{\ell}\in I(G)
and suppose that both
k
and
\ell
are not in
N_{G}(1)
. Then
\{1,k\},\{1,\ell\}\in E(G^{c})
, that is
k,\ell\in N_{G^{c}}(1)
. Since,
x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots>x_{n}
is a perfect elimination order of
G^{c}
, it follows that that
N_{G^{c}}(1)
induces a complete subgraph of
G_{2}^{c}
, where
G_{2}^{c}
is the induced subgraph of
G^{c}
on the vertex set
\{2,\dots,n\}
. Since
k,\ell>1
, it follows that
\{k,\ell\}\in E(G^{c})
, in contradiction with
\{k,\ell\}\in E(G)
. Thus either
k\in N_{G}(1)
or
\ell\in N_{G}(1)
and formula (
1
) follows.

∎

As a consequence, we obtain by a different method the next result already proved in
[
26
, Proposition 3.19]
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
I(G)
be the edge ideal of a graph
G
. Suppose that
I(G)
has a linear resolution. Then,
\operatorname{v}(I(G))=1
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|V(G)|\geq 2
with the base case being trivial. Let
|V(G)|>2
. Let
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}
be a perfect elimination order of
G^{c}
. Then, by Lemma
5.2
, equation (
1
) holds. Thus, by Proposition
2.1
,
\operatorname{v}(I(G))\leq\operatorname{v}(I(G):x_{1})+\deg(x_{1})=0+1=1
. On the other hand, by Proposition
2.2
,
\operatorname{v}(I(G))\geq\alpha(I(G))-1=1
. The assertion follows.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. Suppose that
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Then, the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
shows that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k+1})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})+\omega(I)
for all
k\geq 1
.

Now, we are in the position to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By the previous result,
\operatorname{v}(I(G))=1
. Therefore, for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I(G))
, we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I(G))=1
. By
[
27
, Theorem 2.15]
, we have

\operatorname{Ass}(I(G))\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I(G)^{2})\subseteq\dots\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I(G)^{k})\subseteq\cdots.

Hence,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. By
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
,
(I(G)^{k+1}:I(G))=I(G)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Since
\alpha(I(G)^{k+1})=2(k+1)
and
\omega(I(G))=2
, by Remark
5.4
and Proposition
2.2
, we have

2(k+1)-1\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I(G)^{k+1})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I(G)^{k})+2

for all
k\geq 1
. By induction on
k\geq 1
, we may assume that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I(G)^{k})=2k-1
. The above chain of inequalities gives
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I(G)^{k+1})=2(k+1)-1=\alpha(I(G)^{k+1})-1
. By Proposition
2.2
it follows that
\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq 1
, as well.

∎

5.2.
Polymatroidal ideals

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree. Then
I
is called
polymatroidal
if the following exchange property holds: for all
u,v\in G(I)
and all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
there exists
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
.

Such a name is justified by the fact that the set of the multidegrees of the minimal generators of
I
is the set of the bases of a
discrete polymatroid

[
23
, Chapter 12]
.

This class of monomial ideals is very rich. Indeed, it includes

(i)

Graphic matroids.
They are the ideals generated by the monomials
\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
, for all spanning forests
F
of a finite simple graph
G
on
n
vertices.

(ii)

Transversal polymatroidal ideals.
They are of the form
I=\mathfrak{p}_{A_{1}}\mathfrak{p}_{A_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{A_{r}}
, for some finite collection
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}
of arbitrary nonempty subsets of
[n]
.

(iii)

Ideals of Veronese type.
Let
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a vector with non negative entries. Then, the ideal of
Veronese type
(d,{\bf c})
is defined as

I_{n,d,{\bf c}}\ =\ (x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S\ :\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=d,\ \ a_{i}\leq c_{i},\ \text{for}\ i\in[n]).

Polymatroidal ideals also satisfies a dual exchange property
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
, namely: for all
u,v\in G(I)
and all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)<\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
there exists
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)>\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in G(I)
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1,\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\geq 1.

The proof is based upon the next lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal generated in degree
\alpha(I)\geq 2
. Then
(I:x_{i})
is again a polymatroidal ideal generated in degree
\alpha(I)-1
, for all
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

We may assume that
i=1
. We can write
I=x_{1}I_{1}+I_{2}
, where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are the unique monomial ideals of
S
such that

\displaystyle G(x_{1}I_{1})\

\displaystyle=\ \{u\in G(I):x_{1}\ \text{divides}\ u\},

\displaystyle G(I_{2})\

\displaystyle=\ \{u\in G(I):x_{1}\ \text{does not divide}\ u\}.

We claim that
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
. It is enough to show that
G(I_{2})\subseteq I_{1}
. Let
u\in G(I_{2})
and let
v\in G(x_{1}I_{1})
. Then
\deg_{x_{1}}(u)=0<\deg_{x_{1}}(v)
. Thus, by the dual exchange property, we can find
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)>\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{1}(u/x_{j})\in G(I)
. Hence
x_{1}(u/x_{j})\in x_{1}I_{1}
, and so
u/x_{j}\in I_{1}
. Consequently,
u\in I_{1}
too, and thus
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
.

By the previous paragraph, we have
(I:x_{1})=I_{1}+I_{2}=I_{1}
. It is clear that
I_{1}
is equigenerated in degree
\alpha(I)-1
. It remains to prove that
I_{1}
is polymatroidal. Let
u_{1},v_{1}\in G(I_{1})
and
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u_{1})>\deg_{x_{i}}(v_{1})
. Our job is to find
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u_{1})<\deg_{x_{j}}(v_{1})
and
x_{j}(u_{1}/x_{i})\in G(I_{1})
. Set
u=x_{1}u_{1}
and
v=x_{1}v_{1}
. Then
u,v\in G(x_{1}I_{1})\subset G(I)
and
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
. Since
I
is polymatroidal, there exists
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
. We claim that
x_{1}
divides
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
. Indeed
x_{1}
divides
u
. If
i\neq 1
, then
x_{1}
divides
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
as well. If
i=1
, since
x_{1}
divides
v
and
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)>0
, it follows that
x_{1}^{2}
divides
u
and so
x_{1}
divides
x_{j}(u/x_{i})=x_{j}(u/x_{1})
. Therefore, in any case
x_{1}
divides
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
. Hence,
(x_{j}(u/x_{i}))/x_{1}=x_{j}(u_{1}/x_{i})\in G(I_{1})
and the proof is complete.

∎

We are ready for the proof of the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

Firstly, we show that
\operatorname{v}(I)=\alpha(I)-1
. We proceed by strong induction on
\alpha(I)\geq 1
. If
\alpha(I)=1
, then
I=\mathfrak{p}_{A}
for some
A\subseteq[n]
,
\alpha(I)=1
and
\operatorname{v}(I)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{A}}(I)=0
. Suppose
\alpha(I)>1
. By the previous proposition,
(I:x_{1})
is a polymatroidal ideal and
\alpha(I:x_{1})=\alpha(I)-1
. By induction hypothesis,
\operatorname{v}(I:x_{1})=\alpha(I:x_{1})-1=\alpha(I)-2
. Hence, by Proposition
2.1
,

\operatorname{v}(I)\leq\operatorname{v}(I:x_{1})+\deg(x_{1})=\alpha(I)-1.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\operatorname{v}(I)\geq\alpha(I)-1
. Equality follows.

Let
k>1
. It is well–known that the product of polymatroidal ideals is polymatroidal
[
23
, Theorem 12.6.3]
. Hence,
I^{k}
is a polymatroidal ideal generated in degree
\alpha(I)k
. By what shown above,
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I^{k})-1=\alpha(I)k-1
.

∎

5.3.
Hibi ideals

Let
(P,\succeq)
be a finite partially ordered set (a
poset
, for short) with
P=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
. A
poset ideal
of
P
is a subset
\mathcal{I}
of
P
such that if
p_{i}\in P
,
p_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
and
p_{i}\preceq p_{j}
, then
p_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
. To any poset ideal
\mathcal{I}
of
P
, we associate the monomial

u_{\mathcal{I}}=(\prod_{p_{i}\in\mathcal{I}}x_{i})(\prod_{p_{i}\in P\setminus\mathcal{I}}y_{i})\in S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. The set of all poset ideals of
P
is denoted by
\mathcal{J}(P)
. Then the
Hibi ideal
of
P
is the monomial ideal of
S
defined as

H_{P}\ =\ (u_{\mathcal{I}}\ :\ \mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{J}(P)).

Example 5.7
.

Consider the poset
(P,\succeq)
with
P=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
,

p_{1}\prec x_{2}
and
p_{1}\prec p_{3}
. The poset
(P,\succeq)
and the distributive lattice
\mathcal{J}(P)
are depicted below:
x_{1}
x_{2}
x_{3}
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
\{x_{1}\}
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
\{x_{1},x_{3}\}
\emptyset
(P,\succeq)
\mathcal{J}(P)

Then,
H_{P}=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},\,x_{1}x_{2}y_{3},\,x_{1}y_{2}x_{3},\,x_{1}y_{2}y_{3},\,y_{1}y_{2}y_{3})
.

The Hibi ideal
H_{P}
is equigenerated in degree
|P|
. It is well known that Hibi ideals have linear powers. Next, we calculate the
\operatorname{v}
-function of
H_{P}
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
H_{P}
be a Hibi ideal. Then,

\operatorname{v}(H_{P}^{k})=k|P|-1,\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\geq 1.

In order to prove the theorem, we need some preliminary lemmata.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
H_{P}
be a Hibi ideal. Then,

\operatorname{Ass}(H_{P}^{k})\ =\ \{(x_{i},y_{j}):p_{i}\preceq p_{j}\},\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\geq 1.

Proof.

By
[
21
, Corollary 1.2]
the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(H_{P})
is a normal domain. Hence,
H_{P}
is a normal ideal (see, also,
[
9
, Corollary 3.5]
). Thus, by
[
28
, Proposition (4.7)]
,
(H_{P}^{k+1}:H_{P})=H_{P}^{k}
, for all
k\geq 1
. By
[
23
, Theorem 9.1.13]

H_{P}
is the cover ideal
J(G)
of a Cohen–Macaulay bipartite graph
G
. Next, by
[
32
, Theorem 6.10]
we have that
J(G)^{k}=J(G)^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
, that is, ordinary and symbolic powers of
J(G)=H_{P}
coincide. Since
H_{P}
is a squarefree monomial ideal, by
[
23
, Proposition 1.4.4 and Corollary 1.3.6]
we have

H_{P}^{k}\ =\ H_{P}^{(k)}\ =\ \bigcap_{p_{i}\preceq p_{j}}(x_{i},y_{j})^{k}.

Hence,
\operatorname{Ass}(H_{P}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(H_{P})
for all
k\geq 1
.

∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
H_{P}
be a Hibi ideal. Then

(a)

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(H_{P})=|P|-1
, for all
p_{i}\in P
.

(b)

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P})=|P|+|\{p_{\ell}\in P:p_{i}\prec p_{\ell}\prec p_{j}\}|
, if
p_{i}\neq p_{j}\in P
with
p_{i}\prec p_{j}
.

Proof.

(a) Let
\mathcal{I}
be the smallest poset ideal containing
p_{i}
. Hence, up to a suitable relabeling we may assume that
\mathcal{I}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{i-1},p_{i}\}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{I}\setminus\{p_{i}\}
is again a poset ideal. Thus
x_{1}\cdots x_{i}y_{i+1}\cdots y_{n},x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i}\cdots y_{n}\in H_{P}
. This shows that
(H_{P}:x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i+1}\cdots y_{n})=(x_{i},y_{i})
. Hence
\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(H_{P})\leq|P|-1
. By Proposition
2.2
, we conclude that
\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(H_{P})=|P|-1
, as wanted.

(b) Let
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
be the two smallest poset ideals of
P
containing
p_{i}
,
p_{j}
, respectively. Since
p_{i}\prec p_{j}
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}_{2}
. After a suitable relabeling, we may assume that
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{i-1},p_{i}\}
and
\mathcal{I}_{2}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{i},\dots,p_{j-1},p_{j}\}
. Consider the monomial

u\ =\ x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i}(\!\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j-1}x_{\ell}y_{\ell})x_{j}y_{j+1}\cdots y_{n}.

Then, it is easily seen that
(H_{P}:u)=(x_{i},y_{j})
. Therefore,

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P})\ \leq\ \deg(u)=|P|+|\{p_{\ell}\in P:p_{i}\prec p_{\ell}\prec p_{j}\}|.

To show the converse inequality, let
v
be a monomial such that
(H_{P}:v)=(x_{i},y_{j})
and
\deg(v)=\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P})
. Then
v\notin H_{P}
, but
x_{i}v\in H_{P}
and
y_{j}v\in H_{P}
. The definition of
H_{P}
implies that
x_{i}v=w_{1}u_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}
and
y_{j}v=w_{2}u_{\mathcal{J}_{2}}
, where
x_{i}
divides
u_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}\in G(H_{P})
but not
v
,
y_{j}
divides
u_{\mathcal{J}_{2}}\in G(H_{P})
but not
v
, and
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are suitable monomials. Since
x_{i}
does not divide
v
but
y_{j}v\in H_{P}
, it follows that
y_{i}
divides
v
. Hence
y_{i+1}\cdots y_{j-1}
divides
v
, because if for some
\ell
,
y_{\ell}
does not divide
v
, then
x_{\ell}
should divide
y_{j}v
and since
p_{i}\prec p_{\ell}
this would imply that
x_{i}
divides
v
, which is impossible. On the other hand, since
y_{j}
does not divide
v
, but
x_{i}v\in H_{P}
, it follows that
x_{j}
divides
v
, and in particular divides
u_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}
, hence
x_{1}\cdots x_{i}\cdots x_{j-1}
divides
u_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}
, and so
x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}x_{i+1}\cdots x_{j-1}
divides
v
.

Summarizing our reasoning, we have shown that
x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i}(\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j-1}x_{\ell}y_{\ell})x_{j}
divides
v
. Since
x_{i}v\in H_{P}
, a monomial
z
of the type
z_{j+1}\cdots z_{n}
, with
z_{\ell}\in\{x_{\ell},y_{\ell}\}
for
\ell=j+1,\dots,n
, divides
v
. Hence
x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i}(\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j-1}x_{\ell}y_{\ell})x_{j}z
divides
v
and so

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P})=\deg(v)\

\displaystyle\geq\ \deg(x_{1}\cdots x_{i-1}y_{i}(\!\prod_{\ell=i+1}^{j-1}x_{\ell}y_{\ell})x_{j}z)\ =\ n+(j-i-1)

\displaystyle=\ |P|+|\{p_{\ell}\in P:p_{i}\prec p_{\ell}\prec p_{j}\}|,

as desired.

∎

Let
(P,\succeq)
be a finite poset, with
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
. Following
[
8
]
, for any integer
k\geq 1
, we construct a new poset
(P(k),\succeq_{k})
defined as follows:

-

P(k)\ =\ \{p_{i,1},p_{i,2},\dots,p_{i,k}\ :\ i=1,\dots,n\}
,

-

p_{i,r}\succeq_{k}p_{j,s}
if and only if
p_{i}\succeq p_{j}
and
r\geq s
.

In order to preserve the structure of Hibi ideals, we innocuously modify polarization. Let
1\leq\ell\leq k
and
i\in[n]
, then we set

\displaystyle(x_{i}^{\ell})^{\wp}\

\displaystyle=\ x_{i,1}x_{i,2}\cdots x_{i,\ell},

\displaystyle(y_{i}^{\ell})^{\wp}\

\displaystyle=\ y_{i,\ell}y_{i,\ell-1}\cdots y_{i,k+1-\ell},

and extend the polarization of an arbitrary monomial in the obvious way. In other words, with respect to the usual polarization, we are just applying the relabeling of the variables
y_{i,\ell}\mapsto y_{i,k+1-\ell}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
\ell=1,\dots,k
.

8
, Theorem 4.9]
.

Let

be a finite poset. Then
(H_{P}^{k})^{\wp}=H_{P(k)}
for all

Finally, Theorem
5.8
follows immediately from the next result.

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
H_{P}
be a Hibi ideal and
k\geq 1
any integer. Then

(a)

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(H_{P}^{k})=|P|k-1
, for all
p_{i}\in P
.

(b)

\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P}^{k})=|P|k+|\{p_{\ell}\in P:p_{i}\prec p_{\ell}\prec p_{j}\}|
, if
p_{i}\neq p_{j}\in P
with
p_{i}\prec p_{j}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.9
,
\operatorname{Ass}(H_{P}^{k})=\{(x_{i},y_{j}):p_{i}\preceq p_{j}\}
. Whereas, by Lemma
5.11
, we have
(H_{P}^{k})^{\wp}=H_{P(k)}
. Hence, by Theorem
4.1
(c) and Lemma
5.9
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(H_{P}^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ \min_{1\leq\ell\leq h\leq k}\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i,\ell},y_{i,h})}(H_{P(k)}),\ \ \text{for all}\ p_{i}\in P,\ \text{and},

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i},y_{j})}(H_{P}^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ \min_{1\leq\ell\leq h\leq k}\operatorname{v}_{(x_{i,\ell},y_{j,h})}(H_{P(k)}),\ \ \text{for all}\ p_{i}\prec p_{j}.

A quick calculation shows that (a) and (b) indeed hold.

Optimal divergence rate of the focusing Gibbs measure
Guopeng Li, Rui Liang, and Yuzhao Wang

Abstract
. We study the focusing Gibbs measure with critical/supercritical potentials. In particular, we prove asymptotic formulae for the frequency approximation of the partition function, which captures the optimal divergence rate of the partition function as the frequency truncation is removed.

1.2.
Divergence of the partition function

Before stating our main results, we shall recall the following definitions. Given a dyadic number
N
, we define the frequency projector
\pi_{N}
by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\pi_{N}f=\sum_{|n|\leq N}\widehat{f}(n)e^{\mathrm{i}n\cdot x},

if
f
is a function defined on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
Then, for each fixed
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\pi_{N}u(x)
is
a mean-zero real-valued Gaussian random variable with variance

\displaystyle\sigma_{N}=\mathbb{E}\big[|\pi_{N}u(x)|^{2}\big]=\sum_{|n|\leq N}\frac{1}{|n|^{2s}}<C,

which is finite provided
s>\frac{d}{2}
.
We also define the truncated Gibbs measure
\rho_{s,d,p,K,N}
by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho_{s,d,p,K,N}(u)=Z_{s,d,p,K,N}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\|\pi_{N}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq K\}}e^{\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\pi_{N}u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x}\mathrm{d}\mu_{s,d}(u).

In this sense, we prove the following results for the focusing Gibbs measure
\rho_{s,d,p,K,N}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
s>\frac{d}{2}
, and
p>2
. Given
K>0
and
dyadic number
N>0
, the partition functions

Z_{s,d,p,K,N}

is defined as

(1.8)

\displaystyle Z_{s,d,p,K,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,d}}\Big[\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\|\pi_{N}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq K\}}e^{\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\pi_{N}u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x}\Big],

where
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,d}}
denotes an expectation with respect
to the law
\mu_{s,d}
of the random Fourier series in
(
1.2
).
Then, we can describe
Z_{s,d,p,K,N}
in the following ways
:

(i)

For
p>\frac{4s}{d}+2
and any
K>0
, we have

(1.9)

\log Z_{s,d,p,K,N}=\frac{K^{p}}{p}C_{\operatorname{GNS}}N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}+o(N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d})

as
N\to\infty
,
where

C_{\operatorname{GNS}}^{-1}:=\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|u\|^{\frac{(p-2)d}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+\frac{p-2}{2s}(2s-d)}\cdot{\|u\|^{-p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}
.

(ii)

For

p=\frac{4s}{d}+2
and
K\geq\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
we have

(1.10)

\displaystyle\log Z_{s,d,p,K,N}=\frac{\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{2}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{K}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\bigg)^{p}-\bigg(\frac{K}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}+o(N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d})

as
N\to\infty
,
where
\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\big(\frac{2}{p}C_{\operatorname{GNS}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}
.

(iii)

When
p<\frac{4s}{d}+2
holds or
p=\frac{4s}{d}+2

and

0<K<\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
hold, we have

(1.11)

|\log Z_{s,d,p,K,N}|\lesssim_{s,p,K}1.

In particular, under the conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
with
K>\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we have

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}Z_{s,d,p,K,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{s,d}}\Big[\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\|\pi_{N}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq K\}}e^{\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\pi_{N}u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x}\Big]=\infty.\end{split}

Theorem
1.1
not only provides the proof of the non-normalizability result for the focusing Gibbs measure
\rho_{s,d,p,K}
due to
[
16
,
26
,
18
]
, but also provides a precise rate (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) of divergence of the partition function
Z_{s,d,p,K,N}
. More importantly, together with
[

,
Theorem

(ii) quantifies the phase transition at the critical exponent, i.e. for
p=\frac{4s}{d}+2
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log Z_{s,d,p,K,N}}{N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}}=\begin{cases}0,&0<K\leq\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})};\\
F(s,d,p,K),&K>\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\end{cases}

where

F(s,d,p,K)=\frac{\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{2}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{K}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\bigg)^{p}-\bigg(\frac{K}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg).

The proof of Theorem

In particular, we use the Boué-Dupuis variational formula

1.3.
Discrete analogy

Sharp divergence rate of the partition function to focusing Gibbs measure was previously observed in
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
.
In particular, they considered the discrete approximation of
the
d
-dimensional torus,
which is different from our (frequency approximation) approach.
To be more precise, let a discrete approximation of the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}
represented as

V=\Big\{0,\frac{1}{N},\frac{2}{N},\cdots,\frac{N-1}{N}\Big\}^{d}.

Then let
h=\frac{1}{N}
denote the ‘distance’ between two neighbouring lattices, and
f=(f_{x})_{x\in V}
be any map from
V
to
\mathbb{C}
. The discrete Hamiltonian associated with the discrete focusing NLS is

(1.14)

\displaystyle H(f):=\frac{2}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\sim y}\bigg|\frac{f_{x}-f_{y}}{h}\bigg|^{2}-\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in V}|f_{x}|^{4}.

Up to scaling by a constant, (
1.14
) is the discrete analogue of the continuous Hamiltonian (
1.4
) with
s=1
. Let

L(f):=\sum_{x\in V}|f_{x}|^{2}.

Then the mass of
f
is
N^{-d}L(f)
.
The discrete Gibbs measure associated to the discrete Hamiltonian (
1.14
) is given by

(1.15)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\widetilde{\mu}_{N}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}}e^{-H(f)}\mathbf{1}_{\{L(f)\leq KN^{d}\}}\prod_{x\in V}{\rm d}f_{x},

which can be shown to be invariant under the discrete NLS dynamics. Here
K
is a positive cutoff, and
\widetilde{Z}
is the normalization constant (partition function). Then, it is shown in
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
,

(1.16)

\displaystyle\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}=F(K)N^{d}+o(N^{d}),

or

(1.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}}{N^{d}}=F(K),

where
F(K)
is a function of the cutoff size
K
defined in
[
9
, (4) and (5)]
.
We remark that (
1.16
) is similar to that of (
1.9
) and (
1.10
), whereas (
1.17
) corresponds to (
1.13
). These formulae give the sharp divergence rates of the partition function under physical and frequency space approximations, respectively. However,
[
9
]
only considered singular cases
d\geq 3
and
s=1
, similar to that in
[
25
]
; hence, no phase transition happens with respect to the cutoff size
K
. We anticipate our method to detect the phase transition for regular discrete focusing Gibbs measures, which is deferred to our future research.

2.1.
Variational formulation

In proving Theorem
1.1
, we make use of the variational formula for
the partition function. Let
W(t)
denote a mean-zero cylindrical Brownian

L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

W(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}B_{n}(t)e^{\mathrm{i}n\cdot x},

where
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}

mutually independent complex-valued Brownian motions. Then define a centred
Gaussian process

(2.3)

Y_{s}(t)=\mathrm{D}^{-s}W(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{B_{n}(t)}{|n|^{s}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\cdot x}.

We note that
Y_{s}(t)
is well-defined and

\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}(1)|^{2}]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|B_{n}(1)|^{2}]}{|n|^{s}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{|n|^{s}}<\infty,

provided

. In particular, we have

\operatorname{Law}(Y_{s}(1))=\mu_{s,d},

where
\mu_{s,d}
is the massless Gaussian free field given by the law of
(
1.2
).

Let
\mathbb{H}_{a}
be the space of drifts, which consists of

spatially mean-zero
progressively measurable (with respect to the filtration generated by

) processes
belonging to
L^{2}([0,1];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.
One of the key tools in this paper is the following Boué-Dupuis
variational formula
[
3
,
40
]
. See also
[
7
]
for the infinite-dimensional setting.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a dyadic number
N
. We denote by

(2.4)

Y_{N}:=\pi_{N}Y_{s}(1),

2.2.
Fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need the sharp
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We first recall the
definition of the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined
by the norm:

\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi.

We recall the following result.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and let
(i)

p>2
if
d<2s
, and
(ii)

2<p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2s}
if
d\geq 2s
.
Consider the functional

(2.6)

J^{d,p,s}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{\frac{(p-2)d}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2+\frac{p-2}{2s}(2s-d)}}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the minimum

(2.7)

C_{\operatorname{GNS}}^{-1}=C_{\operatorname{GNS}}(d,p,s)^{-1}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}J^{d,p,s}(u)

is attained at a function
Q\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is easy to see that functions
u(x):=cQ(b(x-a))
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
,
b>0
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, are
minizers of the functional (
2.6
). Therefore, we may assume
that

(2.8)

\begin{split}\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&=\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\\
\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}&=\frac{2}{p}\|Q\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.\end{split}

Under this specified scaling, we have
H_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(Q)=0
, where

H_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Furthermore, this
Q
solves the following semilinear elliptic equation on

\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(p-2)d\mathrm{D}^{2s}Q+(4s+(p-2)(2s-d))Q-4s|Q|^{p-2}Q=0.

In the following, we restrict ourselves to (
2.8
) unless
specified otherwise. In particular, we have

(2.9)

C_{\operatorname{GNS}}=\frac{p}{2}\|Q\|^{2-p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For a function
u
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we define the
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
semi-norm via

\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2s}|\widehat{u}(n)|^{2}.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
18
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}1
. And let

(i)

p>2
if
d<2s
;

(ii)

2<p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2s}
if
d\geq 2s
.

Then, given small
\delta>0
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq(C_{\operatorname{GNS}}(d,p,s)+\delta)\,\|u\|^{\frac{(p-2)d}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2+\frac{p-2}{2s}(2s-d)}+\delta^{-1}C\|u\|^{p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

for

, where
C_{\operatorname{GNS}}

is the constant defined in (

) and (
2.9
).

2.3.
Solitons and progressively measurable approximations

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,\frac{1}{2}))
be a bump function with
\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}=1

and
\psi_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-d}\psi(\frac{x}{\delta})
where
0<\delta\ll 1
.
Define
\phi_{\delta}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{[-\frac{1}{2}+2\delta,\frac{1}{2}-2\delta]^{d}}*\psi_{\delta}(x)
.
Then the following properties hold

(i)

\phi_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(ii)

\phi_{\delta}(x)=1
for
x\in[-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta,\frac{1}{2}-3\delta]^{d}
,

(iii)

\phi_{\delta}(x)=0
for
x\in([-\frac{1}{2}+\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta]^{d})^{c}
.

Let
N
be a dyadic number.
We then define
Q_{N}
to be

(2.10)

Q_{N}(x):=\psi_{\delta}N^{\frac{d}{2}}Q(Nx).

Moreover,
Q_{N}
satisfies the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
N
be a large dyadic number and
Q_{N}

be defined in (
2.10
), then
Q_{N}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then

\begin{split}&(\mathrm{i})\,\|Q_{N}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq N^{2s}\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+o(N^{2s});\\
&(\mathrm{ii})\,\|Q_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{p}=N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+o(N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}).\end{split}

Proof.

Let us prove (i) first. (
2.8
), (
2.10
), and fractional Leibniz rule (see
[
15
,
17
]
) gives

\|Q_{N}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq N^{2s}\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+CN^{s+\varepsilon}\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which, together with (
2.8
), gives (i).

Second, let us prove (ii). From the fact that
Q\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we know that

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|(1-\psi_{\delta}(N^{-1}x))Q(x)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}=0,

thereby getting

\begin{split}\|Q_{N}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}&\geq N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|(1-\psi_{\delta}(N^{-1}x))Q(x)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\\
&=N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+o(N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d})\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|Q_{N}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}&\leq N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+CN^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|(1-\psi_{\delta}(N^{-1}x))Q(x)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}^{p}\\
&\qquad+CN^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|(1-\psi_{\delta}(N^{-1}x))Q(x)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\\
&=N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}\|Q\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+o(N^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}),\end{split}

which gives (ii). Here we use (
2.10
) and the mean value theorem.
∎

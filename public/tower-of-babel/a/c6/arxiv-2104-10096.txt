Mock hyperbolic reflection spaces and Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank
Tim Clausen and Katrin Tent

Abstract
We define the notion of mock hyperbolic reflection spaces and use it to study Frobenius groups, in particular in the context of groups of finite Morley rank including the so-called bad groups . We show that connected Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank and odd type with nilpotent complement split or interpret a bad field of characteristic zero. Furthermore, we show that mock hyperbolic reflection spaces of finite Morley rank satisfy certain rank inequalities, implying in particular that any connected Frobenius group of odd type and Morley rank at most ten either splits or is a simple non-split sharply 2 2 -transitive group of characteristic ≠ 2 \neq 2 of Morley rank 8 8 or 10 10 .

Introduction

This paper contributes to the study of groups acting on geometries arising naturally from conjugacy classes of involutions. We define the notion of a mock hyperbolic reflection space and use it to study certain Frobenius groups. Such an approach to the classification of groups and their underlying geometries based on involutions was developed by Bachmann
[
3
]
.
Mock hyperbolic reflection spaces generalize real hyperbolic spaces and their definition is motivated by the geometry arising from the involutions in certain non-split sharply
2
-transitive groups.

The points of such a mock hyperbolic space are given by a conjugacy class of involutions and we view the conjugation action by an involution in the space as a point-reflection. More precisely,
a conjugacy class of involutions in a group forms a
mock hyperbolic reflection space
if it admits the structure of a linear space such that three axioms are satisfied:
three points are collinear if and only if the product of their point-reflections is a point-reflection,
for any two points there is a unique midpoint, i.e. a unique point reflectiong one point to the other, and
given two distinct lines there is at most one point reflecting one line to the other.

We will consider in particular mock hyperbolic reflection spaces arising from Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank.
One of the main open problems about groups of finite Morley rank is the
Algebraicity Conjecture
, which states that any infinite simple group of finite Morley rank should be an algebraic group over an algebraically closed field. While the conjecture was proved by Altınel, Borovik, and Cherlin
[
2
]
in the characteristic
2
setting, it is still wide open in general and in particular in the situation of small (Tits) rank. The conjecture would in fact imply that any sharply
2
-transitive group of finite Morley rank and, more generally, any Frobenius group of finite Morley rank splits.

A Frobenius group is a group
G
together with a proper nontrivial malnormal subgroup H, i.e. a subgroup
H
such that
H\cap H^{g}=\{1\}
for all
g\in G\setminus H
. (Note that if
G
is a
bad group
of finite Morley rank with Borel subgroup
B
then
B<G
is a Frobenius group.) A classical result due to Frobenius states that finite Frobenius groups split, i.e. they can be written as a semidirect product of a normal subgroup and the subgroup H. In the setting of finite groups the methods used by Frobenius play an important role in the classification of CA-groups, CN-groups, and groups of odd order. For groups of finite Morley rank, all of the corresponding classification problems are still wide open.

Sharply
2
-transitive groups of finite Morley rank came to renewed attention since recently the first sharply
2
-transitive groups without non-trivial abelian normal subgroup were constructed in characteristic
2
in
[
15
]
(see also
[
18
]
) and in characteristic
0
in
[
16
]
. However, as we show below, these groups do not have finite Morley rank. We also show that specific non-split sharply
2
-transitive groups of finite Morley rank would indeed be direct counterexamples to the Algebraicity Conjecture.

We prove the following splitting criteria for groups with an associated mock hyperbolic reflection space:

Theorem 1.1
.

If

6.10

We then consider mock hyperbolic reflection spaces arising from Frobenius groups.

Let

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G

b)

Midpoints exist and are unique, i.e. given

in

.

c)

Given two distinct lines there is at most one point reflecting one line to the other. In other words, if
\lambda^{i}=\lambda^{j}
for

\lambda^{i}=\lambda=\lambda^{j}
.

We say that

\ell_{ij}\in\Lambda
for all
i\neq j\in Q

Given a group

For a group

.

3
Sharply 2-transitive groups

A permutation group
G
acting on a set
X
,
|X|\geq 2
, is called
sharply 2-transitive
if it acts regularly on pairs of distinct points, or, equivalently, if
G
acts transitively on
X
and for each
x\in X
the point stabilizer
G_{x}
acts regularly on
X\setminus\{x\}
. For two distinct elements
x,y\in X
the unique
g\in G
such that
(x,y)^{g}=(y,x)
is an involution. Hence the set
J
of involutions in
G
is non-empty and forms a conjugacy class.

The (permutation) characteristic of a group
G
acting sharply 2-transitively on a set
X
is defined as follows: put
\mathrm{char}(G)=2
if and only if involutions have no fixed points. If involutions have a (necessarily unique) fixed point, the
G
-equivariant bijection
i\mapsto\mathrm{fix}(i)
allows us to identify the given action of
G
on
X
with the conjugation action of
G
on
J
. Thus in this case, the set
S\setminus\{1\}
of nontrivial translations also forms a single conjugacy class. We put
\mathrm{char}(G)=p
(or
0
) if translations have order
p
(or infinite order, respectively).
For the standard examples of sharply 2-transitive groups, namely
K\rtimes K^{*}
for some field
K
, this definition of characteristic agrees with the characteristic of the field
K
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
G
be a sharply 2-transitive group of characteristic
\mathrm{char}(G)\neq 2
. Since
G
acts sharply
2
-transitively by conjugation on the set
J
of involutions in
G
, the following properties are easy to see:

a)

\mathrm{Cen}(i)
acts regularly on
J\setminus\{i\}
,

b)

the set
J
acts regularly on itself by conjugation, i.e. Condition b) of
Definition

2.1
holds.

c)

J^{\cdot 2}\cap\mathrm{Cen}(i)=\{1\}
for all
i\in J
.

In particular, a nontrivial translation does not have a fixed point.

In order to define the lines for a mock hyperbolic reflection space on
J
, we need the following equivalent conditions to be satisfied:

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\mathrm{char}(G)\neq 2
, the following conditions are equivalent:

a)

Commuting is transitive on
J^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}
.

b)

iJ\cap kJ
is uniquely 2-divisible for all involutions
i\neq k\in J
.

c)

\mathrm{Cen}(ik)=iJ\cap kJ
is abelian and is inverted by
k
for all
i\neq k\in J
.

d)

The set
\{\mathrm{Cen}(\sigma)\setminus\{1\}:\sigma\in J^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}\}
forms
a partition of
J^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}
.

Furthermore, these conditions are satisfied in split sharply 2-transitive groups, whenever
\mathrm{char}(G)=p\neq 0,2
or in case
G
satisfies the descending chain condition for centralizers, so in particular if
G
has finite Morley rank.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Note that since
(ij)^{2}=ii^{j}\in iJ
every element of
iJ
has a unique square-root in
iJ
. Let
\tau\in iJ\cap kJ
. By assumption the group
A=\langle\mathrm{Cen}(\tau)\cap J^{\cdot 2}\rangle\leq\mathrm{Cen}(\tau)
is abelian. Moreover,
A\cap J=\emptyset
by Remark
3.1
. Hence the square-map is an injective group homomorphism from
A
to
A
.

There is
\sigma_{i}\in iJ
such that
\sigma_{i}^{2}=\tau
and therefore
\sigma_{i}\in\mathrm{Cen}(\tau)\cap iJ
because commuting is transitive. Similarly we find
\sigma_{k}\in\mathrm{Cen}(\tau)\cap kJ
such that
\sigma_{k}^{2}=\tau
. Since the square-map is injective, it follows that
\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{k}\in iJ\cap kJ
. Therefore
iJ\cap kJ
is uniquely 2-divisible.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) is contained in Lemma 11.50 iv of
[
6
]
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(d) and (d)
\Rightarrow
(a) are obvious.

These conditions hold in split sharply 2-transitive groups by Theorem
3.5
. Furthermore, if
\mathrm{char}(G)=p\neq 0,2
or if
G
satisfies the descending chain condition for centralizers, these conditions hold by Lemma 11.50 of
[
6
]
.
∎

The examples constructed in
[
15
]
(see also
[
18
]
) show that in characteristic 2 these conditions need not be satisfied. The non-split examples in characteristic 0 constructed in
[
16
]
satisfy the assumptions and it is an open question whether non-split sharply 2-transitive groups exist in characteristic 0 which fail to satisfy these conditions. Note that Lemma 2.3 and 5.3 of
[
16
]
imply that the maximal near-field in these examples is planar.

Assume now that the conditions of
Proposition

3.2
are satisfied. Then for
i\neq j\in J
we put

\ell_{ij}=\{k\in J:ij\in kJ\}\mbox{ and }\Lambda=\{\ell_{ij}\colon i\neq j\in J\}.

By

3.2
,

satisfies Conditions a) and b) of

2.1
and we have

\ell_{ij}=\{k\in J:ij\in kJ\}=i\ \mathrm{Cen}(ij)=\{k\in J:(ij)^{k}=ji\}.

The point-line geometry

is equivalent to the incidence geometry considered by Borovik and Nesin in Section 11.4 of
[

]
.

If

is a line, then

is a split sharply
2
-transitive group

and corresponds to the maximal near-field. If the maximal near-field is planar, then

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that

is sharply 2-transitive,

are satisfied. Assume moreover that the maximal near-field is planar. If

4
Uniquely 2-divisible Frobenius groups

In this section we will construct (partial) mock hyperbolic reflection spaces from uniquely
2
-divisible Frobenius groups with abelian Frobenius complement. This construction makes use of K-loops and quasidirect products.

4.1
K-Loops and quasidirect products

K-loops are non-associative generalizations of abelian groups. They are also known as Bruck loops and gyrocommutative gyrogroups.
We mostly follow Kiechle’s book
[
12
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A groupoid
(L,\cdot,1)
is a
K-loop
if

a)

it is a loop, i.e. the equations

ax=b\quad\text{and}\quad xa=b

have unique solutions for all
a,b\in L
,

b)

it satisfies the Bol condition, i.e.

a(b\cdot ac)=(a\cdot ba)c

for all
a,b,c\in L
, and

c)

is satisfies the automorphic inverse property, i.e. all elements of
L
have inverses and we have

(ab)^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}

for all
a,b\in L
.

Given
a\in L
let
\lambda_{a}:L\rightarrow L
be defined by
\lambda_{a}(x)=ax
. Given
a,b\in L
we define the precession map

\delta_{a,b}=\lambda_{ab}^{-1}\lambda_{a}\lambda_{b}.

These maps are characterized by

a\cdot bx=ab\cdot\delta_{a,b}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in L.

If
L
is a K-loop, then the precession maps are automorphisms and we set

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(L)=\langle\delta_{a,b}:a,b\in L\rangle\leq\mathrm{Aut}(L).

The following identities will be used in this section:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
L
be a K-loop,
a,b\in L
, and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(L)
. Then the following identities hold:

a)

\alpha^{-1}\delta_{a,b}\alpha=\delta_{\alpha^{-1}(a),\alpha^{-1}(b)}
,

b)

\delta_{a,a^{-1}}=\mathrm{id}
,

c)

\delta_{a,ba}=\delta_{a,b}
,

d)

\delta_{a,b}=\delta_{b,a}^{-1}
,

e)

\delta_{a,b}=\delta_{a^{-1},b^{-1}}
.

Proof.

The first identity follows from
[
12
, 2.4]
. The second identity is a consequence of
[
12
, 6.1]
. Theorem 6.4 of
[
12
]
shows the third identity. The remaining two identities are part of
[
12
, Theorem 3.7]
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a group. A subset
L\subseteq G
is a twisted subgroup of
G
if and only if
1\in L
,
L^{-1}\subseteq L
, and
aLa\subseteq L
for all
a\in L
.

Note that twisted subgroups are closed under the square-map. A twisted subgroup is
uniquely
2
-divisible
if the square map is bijective.

Proposition 4.4

(Theorem 6.14 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a group with a uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup
L\subseteq G
. Then

a\otimes b=a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}

makes L into a K-loop
(L,\otimes,1)
and integer powers of elements in
L
agree in
G
and
(L,\otimes)
. Moreover, given
a,b\in L
the precession map
\delta_{a,b}
is given by conjugation with

d_{a,b}=b^{1/2}a^{1/2}(a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})^{-1/2}.

Proposition 4.5

(Theorem 2.13 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let
L
be a K-loop and let
\mathcal{A}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(L)
a group of automorphisms such that
\mathcal{D}(L)\subseteq\mathcal{A}
. Then

a)

The quasidirect product
L\rtimes_{Q}\mathcal{A}
given by the set
L\times\mathcal{A}
together with the multiplication

(a,\alpha)(b,\beta)=(a\cdot\alpha(b),\delta_{a,\alpha(b)}\alpha\beta)

forms a group with neutral element
(1,\mathrm{id})
. Inverses are given by

(a,\alpha)^{-1}=(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}),\alpha^{-1}).

b)

L\rtimes_{Q}\mathcal{A}
acts faithfully and transitively on
L
by

(a,\alpha)(x)=a\alpha(x)\quad\text{for all }(a,\alpha)\in L\rtimes_{Q}\mathcal{A},x\in L.

4.2
Mock hyperbolic reflection spaces from uniquely 2-divisible Frobenius groups

Let
H<G
be a uniquely 2-divisible Frobenius group with abelian complement
H
.

We set
L
to be the K-loop
L=(G,\otimes)
where
\otimes
is defined by

a\otimes b=a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}.

Set
\mathcal{A}=G\times\langle\epsilon\rangle<\mathrm{Aut}(L)
where
\epsilon
inverts all elements of
L
. Put
\mathcal{G}=L\rtimes_{Q}\mathcal{A}
. Let
J
be the set of all involutions in
\mathcal{G}
and put
\iota=(1,\epsilon)\in J
.

Lemma 4.6
.

a)

J=L\times\{\epsilon\}
.

b)

\mathrm{Cen}(\iota)=1\times\mathcal{A}
.

c)

for all
i,j\in J
there is a unique
k\in J
such that
j=i^{k}
.

Proof.

a) Fix
(a,\alpha)\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(a,\alpha)^{2}=(1,\mathrm{id})
. Note that

(a,\alpha)(a,\alpha)=(a\otimes\alpha(a),\delta_{a,\alpha(a)}\alpha^{2}).

Now
a\otimes\alpha(a)=1
implies
\alpha(a)=a^{-1}
and therefore
\delta_{a,\alpha(a)}=\mathrm{id}
.
Hence we must have
\alpha^{2}=\mathrm{id}
.

If
\alpha=\mathrm{id}
, then
a\otimes\alpha(a)=a^{2}
, therefore
a^{2}=1
and thus
a=1
. In that case
(a,\alpha)=(1,\mathrm{id})
is the neutral element in
\mathcal{G}
.

This shows
J=L\times\{\epsilon\}
because
\epsilon
is the only involution in
\mathcal{A}
.

b) Fix
(a,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Cen}(\iota)
. We have

\displaystyle(a,\alpha)(1,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=(a,\alpha\epsilon)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle(1,\epsilon)(a,\alpha)

\displaystyle=(a^{-1},\epsilon\alpha).

Hence
(a,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Cen}(\iota)
if and only if
a=a^{-1}
if and only if
a=1
.

c) Take involutions
(a,\epsilon),(b,\epsilon),(c,\epsilon)\in J=L\times\{\epsilon\}
. Then

\displaystyle(b,\epsilon)(a,\epsilon)(b,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=(b,\epsilon)(a\otimes b^{-1},\delta_{a,b^{-1}})

\displaystyle=(b\otimes(a^{-1}\otimes b),\delta_{b,a^{-1}\otimes b}\delta_{a,b^{-1}}\epsilon)

\displaystyle=((b\otimes a^{-1/2})^{2},\epsilon).

Hence we have
(a,\epsilon)^{(b,\epsilon)}=(c,\epsilon)
if and only if
b\otimes a^{-1/2}=c^{1/2}
. The loop conditions ensure that for all
a,c\in L
there is a unique

satisfying this equation.
∎

Now set
\lambda_{0}=H\times\{\epsilon\}\subseteq J
and put
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{0}^{g}:g\in\mathcal{G}\}
.
We view elements of
\Lambda
as lines and we view involutions as points. Note that
\Lambda
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant and all lines are conjugate.

The following will be shown in this section:

Theorem 4.7
.

a)

(J,\Lambda)
is a partial mock hyperbolic reflection space in
\mathcal{G}
.

b)

If
G
is full, i.e., if
G=\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g}
,
then
(J,\Lambda)
is a mock hyperbolic reflection space.

c)

Suppose

are pairwise distinct such that the lines
\ell_{ij}
and
\ell_{ik}
exist and assume that

are not collinear. Then
\mathrm{Cen}_{\mathcal{G}}(i,j,k)=1
. In particular,
\mathcal{G}
acts faithfully on

.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\lambda
be a line containing
\iota
. Then
\lambda
is of the form

\lambda=H^{g}\times\{\epsilon\}

for some

.

Proof.

We have
\lambda=\lambda_{0}^{g}
for some

. Hence elements of
\lambda
are of the form

\displaystyle(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}),\alpha^{-1})(c,\epsilon)(a,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}),\;\alpha^{-1})(c\otimes a^{-1},\;\delta_{c,a^{-1}}\epsilon\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1})\otimes\alpha^{-1}(c\otimes a^{-1}),\;\delta_{\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}),\alpha^{-1}(c\otimes a^{-1})}\delta_{c,a^{-1}}\alpha\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}\otimes(c\otimes a^{-1})),\;\alpha^{-1}\delta_{a^{-1},c\otimes a^{-1}}\delta_{c,a^{-1}}\alpha\epsilon)

for some

.

Note that

. We assume

. Hence

for some

. This implies

.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Any two distinct points are contained in at most one line.

Lemma 4.10
.

Fix distinct involutions

and suppose

exists in

. Then

\displaystyle\ell_{ij}=

\displaystyle\{k\in J:ij\in kJ\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\in J:(ij)^{k}=(ij)^{-1}\}.

We may assume that
\ell_{ij}=H\times\{\epsilon\}
and

for some

.
The second equality is easy and therefore we only show the first equality.

We first show
\ell_{ij}\subseteq\{k\in J:ij\in kJ\}

Take

. Then

The Frobenius complement

This shows

and hence

.

We now show
\supseteq
for the first equality:
Suppose

. We have to show that

is an element of

. We have

(c,1)=(a,\epsilon)(b,\epsilon)=(a\otimes b^{-1},\delta_{a,b^{-1}})

and hence

and

and therefore

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose

. Then

\alpha

.

Proof.

Given

\displaystyle(a,\alpha)^{-1}(c,\epsilon)(a,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{-1}(a^{-1}),\alpha^{-1})(c\otimes a^{-1},\delta_{c,a^{-1}}\epsilon\alpha)

\displaystyle=

5
Mock hyperbolic reflection spaces in groups of finite Morley rank

We now turn to the finite Morley rank setting. We refer the reader to
[
6
]

[
14
]
for a general introduction to groups of finite Morley rank.

Convention
.

In the context of finite Morley rank we say that a definable property
P
holds for
Morley rank
k
many elements
if the set defined by
P
has Morley rank
k
. In a slight abuse, we may also say that
P
holds for

generically many
elements of a definable set

if the set of elements in

not satisfying

has smaller Morley rank than

.

We will repeatedly make use of the following:

Proposition 5.1

(Exercise 11 and 12 on p. 72 of
[
6
]
)
.

If
G
is a group of finite Morley rank and
G
does not contain an involution, then
G
is uniquely
2
-divisible.

Now let
G
be a group of finite Morley rank and let

be a conjugacy class of involutions such that
\mathrm{MD}(Q)=1
. Moreover, we assume that
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{P}(Q)
is a
G
-invariant definable family of subsets of

such that each
\lambda\in\Lambda
is of the form

\lambda=\{k\in Q:ij\in kQ\}

for any

.

Definition 5.2
.

We call

a

if

is a partial mock hyperbolic reflection space and for each

the set

is generic in

.

Remark 5.3
.

Let

be a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space.

a)

The condition in the above definition is equivalent to the statement that

\{(i,j)\in Q^{2}:i\neq j,\ell_{ij}\text{ exists in }\Lambda\}\subseteq Q\times Q

is a generic subset of
Q^{2}
.

b)

Write

\Lambda(k)=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:\mathrm{MR}(\lambda)=k\}.

Fix

and set

Since
\mathrm{MD}(Q)=1
there is exactly one

such that

is a generic subset of

.
In that case

is a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space. Hence we may
assume from now on that all lines in

have the same Morley rank
.

c)

If
(Q,\Lambda)
is a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space of finite Morley rank in which all lines have Morley rank

, then we have

and
\mathrm{MD}(\Lambda)=1
for

. The set of translations

has Morley rank

and Morley degree

.

If

and

are definable sets, then we write

Proposition 5.4

(Lemma 4.3 of
[
20
]
)
.

Let

be a group acting definably on a set

-stable structure. Let

. Then there is a

.

By
Theorem

2.14
a mock hyperbolic reflection space consists of one line if and only if the set of translations forms a normal subgroup.
For generic mock hyperbolic reflection spaces the following will be shown in this section:

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose

.

.

For the remainder of this section we assume that
(Q,\Lambda)
is a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space in a group of finite Morley rank

satisfies the assumptions in

5.1

Definition 5.6

A definable subset

Lemma 5.7

Let

be a definable set of Morley rank 2k and Morley degree 1. The following are equivalent:

is a generic projective plane,

b)

\mathrm{MR}(\Lambda_{X})\geq 2k

c)

The set of

such that
\mathrm{MR}(\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{X}:x\in\lambda\})=k
is generic in
X
.

If
X
is a definable set, then we say that a property

(resp. generically many

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose

is a generic projective plane. Then set of

.

Proof.

Let

Moreover, we have

∎

Lemma 5.9

2k\leq X_{\alpha}\leq n-l
for generically many
\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})
. In particular,

.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{MR}(\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap T)=\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})+k
and

\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})}\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap T\subseteq T

and
T
has rank
3n
. Therefore a generic
\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})
must satisfy the inequality

\mathrm{MR}(\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}}))+\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})+k\leq\mathrm{MR}(T)=3n.

Moreover, we have
\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})\geq 2k
by
Lemma

5.17
. Hence

2k\leq\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})\leq\mathrm{MR}(T)-k-\mathrm{MR}(\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}}))=n-l

for generically many
\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})
.
∎

Proposition 5.19
.

Set
l=\mathrm{MR}(\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}}))-(2n-k)
. Then
n>2k+l
. In particular,
n>2k
.

Proof.

Assume not. Then
n=2k+l
and
\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})=2k
for generically many
\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})
.
Set
M=\{\alpha\in\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}}):\mathrm{MR}(X_{\alpha})=2k\}
. This is a generic subset of
\mu(T_{\mathrm{perf}})
.
We have

\mathrm{MR}(\bigcup_{\alpha\in M}\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap T)=(2n-k+l)+3k=6k+3l=3n.

Hence
\bigcup_{\alpha\in M}\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap T
is a generic subset of
Q\times Q\times Q
.
Note that
\mathrm{MR}(M)=3k+3l
. Thus we can find
\alpha\in M
such that
\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap T
has rank
3k
and contains rank
3k
many perfect triples. Set

and
\Lambda_{X}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:\lambda\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\textstyle\subset\\[-0.86108pt]
\textstyle\sim\end{subarray}}X\}
.
Now

5.17
implies that for a generic

the set

has Morley rank

. Hence
\mathrm{MR}(\Lambda_{X})=2k
and therefore a degree

must be a generic projective plane. This contradicts

.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

5.5
.

Set

. By

we have

6
Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank

We now consider Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank. If

is a group of finite Morley rank and

is a Frobenius complement in

, then

is definable by Proposition 11.19 of
[

]
. If

splits as

, then

is also definable by Proposition 11.23 of
[

]
.

Epstein and Nesin showed that if

is a Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and

is finite, then

splits (Theorem 11.25 of
[
6
]
). As a consequence it suffices to consider connected Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank (Corollary 11.27 of
[

]
).

Solvable Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank split and their structure is well understood
[
6
, Theorem 11.32]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank with Frobenius complement
H

and let
X\subseteq H\setminus\{1\}
be a definable
H
-normal subset such that
\mathrm{MR}(X)=\mathrm{MR}(H)
.
Then
\bigcup_{b\in G}X^{b}\subseteq G
is a generic subset of
G
.

Proof.

Set
n=\mathrm{MR}(G)
and
k=\mathrm{MR}(H)
.
Consider the map
\alpha:G\times X\rightarrow G,\;(b,x)\mapsto x^{b}
.
If
x^{b}=y^{c}
for
x,y\in X
,
b,c\in G
, then
bc^{-1}
must be contained in
N_{G}(H)=H
. Therefore we have

\alpha^{-1}(x^{b})=\{(c,x^{bc^{-1}})\in G\times X:bc^{-1}\in H\}.

Hence all fibers of
\alpha
have Morley rank
k
. This shows that
\alpha(G\times X)=\bigcup_{b\in G}X^{b}
must have Morley rank
n
and hence is a generic subset of
G
.
∎

Groups of finite Morley rank can be classified by the structure of their
2
-Sylow subgroups. In case of Frobenius groups this classification is simpler:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank with Frobenius complement
H
. Then
H
is connected and
G
lies in one of the following mutually exclusive cases:

a)

H
contains a unique involution, i.e.
G
is of odd type;

b)

G
does not contain any involutions, i.e.
G
is of degenerate type;

c)

G\setminus(\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g})
contains involutions, i.e.
G
is of even type.

Proof.

We first show that
H
must be connected: If
H
is not connected, then
\bigcup_{g\in G}(H^{0}\setminus\{1\})^{g}
and
\bigcup_{g\in G}(H\setminus H^{0})^{g}
would be two disjoint generic subsets of
G
. This is impossible because
G
is connected.

If
H
contains an involution, then Delahan and Nesin showed that this involution must be unique and moreover all involutions in
G
are conjugate and hence
G\setminus(\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g})
cannot contain any involution (Lemma 11.20 of
[
6
]
). In particular,
G
is of odd type because the connected subgroup
H
contains a unique involution.

If
G\setminus(\bigcup_{g\in G}C^{g})
contains an involution, then the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 2]
shows that
G
is of even type.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If
H<G
is of even type, then Altınel, Berkman, and Wagner showed in
[
1
]
that there is a definable normal subgroup
N
such that
N\cap H=1
and
N
contains all involutions of
G
. By Lemma 11.38 of
[
6
]
either
G=N\rtimes H
splits or
HN/N<G/N
is a Frobenius group of finite Morley rank.
Now if
HN/N<G/N
splits, then it is easy to see that
H<G
must split. Hence a non-split Frobenius group of minimal Morley rank cannot be of even type. Therefore to show that all Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank split, it suffices to consider Frobenius groups of odd and degenerate type.

6.1
Frobenius groups of odd type

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type. Note that
G
contains a single conjugacy class of involutions which we denote by
J
. Moreover,
J
has Morley degree
1
.

Proposition 6.4

(Proposition 11.18 of
[
6
]
)
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions.
If
a\in J^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}
and
i\in J
, then
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\cap\mathrm{Cen}(i)=\{1\}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions. Fix distinct involutions
i,j\in J
.

a)

If
a\in iJ\setminus\{1\}
, then
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\subseteq iJ
is a uniquely
2
-divisible abelian group;

b)

iJ
is uniquely
2
-divisible;

c)

J
acts regularly on itself, i.e., given
i,j\in J
there is a unique
k\in J
such that
j=i^{k}
;

d)

iJ\cap jJ
is uniquely
2
-divisible;

e)

\mathrm{Cen}(ij)=iJ\cap jJ
;

f)

The family
\{\mathrm{Cen}(a)\setminus\{1\}:a\in Q^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}\}
forms a partition of
J^{\cdot 2}\setminus\{1\}
.

Proof.

1) By the previous proposition we have
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\cap\mathrm{Cen}(i)=\{1\}
for all involutions. In particular,
\mathrm{Cen}(a)
does not contain an involution and hence is uniquely
2
-divisible by
Proposition

5.1
. Note that
i
acts on
\mathrm{Cen}(a)
as a fixed point free involutionary automorphism. Hence by
Proposition

2.6

\mathrm{Cen}(a)
is abelian and inverted by
i
.

2) Fix
a=ip\in iQ\setminus\{1\}
. Since
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\subseteq iJ
is uniquely
2
-divisible we have
a=b^{2}
for some
b=iq\in iQ
. If
a=c^{2}
for another element
c=ir\in iJ
, then
ii^{p}=ii^{r}
and hence
pr\in\mathrm{Cen}(i)\cap Q^{\cdot 2}=\{1\}
. Thus
b=c
.

3) Note that
j=i^{p}
if and only if
ij=ii^{p}=(ip)^{2}
. Since
iJ
is uniquely
2
-divisible,
p
exists and is unique.

4) It suffices to show that
iJ\cap jJ
is
2
-divisible. Given
a\in iJ\cap jJ
we have
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\subseteq iJ\cap jJ
and hence
a=b^{2}
for some
b\in\mathrm{Cen}(a)\subseteq iJ\cap jJ
.

5) By 3) we have
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)\subseteq iJ\cap jJ
. Hence it remains to show that
iJ\cap jJ\subseteq\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
.
Given
a\in iJ\cap jJ
,
a
is inverted by
i
and
j
and hence
a\in\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
.

6) Suppose
c\in\mathrm{Cen}(a)\cap\mathrm{Cen}(b)
for some
c\neq 1
. Then
a,b\in\mathrm{Cen}(c)
and hence
a\in\mathrm{Cen}(b)
because
\mathrm{Cen}(c)
is abelian. This implies
\mathrm{Cen}(b)\subseteq\mathrm{Cen}(a)
because
\mathrm{Cen}(b)
is abelian. Hence
\mathrm{Cen}(a)=\mathrm{Cen}(b)
by symmetry. This implies 5).
∎

Given two distinct involutions
i\neq j
in
J
we define the line

\ell_{ij}=\{k\in Q:(ij)^{k}=(ij)^{-1}\}.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions.
Let
i\neq j\in J
. Then
i\ell_{ij}=\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
.

Proof.

Clearly
i\ell_{ij}\subseteq\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
. On the other hand we have
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)\subseteq iJ
by Part 6 of
6.5
.
Given
\sigma=ik\in\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
, we have

(ji)^{k}=(ij)^{ik}=(ij)^{\sigma}=ij.

Therefore
(ij)^{k}=(ij)^{-1}
and thus
k\in\ell_{ij}
.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions. Fix
i\neq j\in J
and
let
p,q\in\ell_{ij}
be distinct involutions. Then
\ell_{pq}=\ell_{ij}
.

Proof.

We have
pq\in\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
and hence
\mathrm{Cen}(pq)=\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
. Moreover,
ip\in\mathrm{Cen}(ij)=\mathrm{Cen}(pq)
and hence

\ell_{pq}=p\mathrm{Cen}(pq)=i\mathrm{Cen}(ij)=\ell_{ij}.

∎

Hence the set
J
together with the above notion of lines satisfies conditions a) and b) of
Definition

2.1
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions.
Let
i,j\in J
be distinct involutions and let

be a subgroup of
G
such that
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)\leq T\leq N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))
. Then
T=\mathrm{Cen}(ij)(T\cap\mathrm{Cen}(i))
.

Proof.

Note that
G
can be decomposed as
G=iJ\mathrm{Cen}(i)
and put
\lambda=\ell_{ij}
.
Given
t\in T
, we can write
t=ikg
for (unique) elements
k\in J
,
g\in\mathrm{Cen}(i)
. Then

\lambda=\lambda^{t}=\lambda^{kg}.

In particular,
i\in\lambda^{k}\cap\lambda
. If
\lambda^{k}\cap\lambda=\{i\}
, then
k=i\in\lambda
. If
\lambda^{k}=\lambda
, then
k\in\lambda
by part a) of
Lemma

2.7
.
Therefore
t=ikg\in\mathrm{Cen}(ij)(T\cap\mathrm{Cen}(i))
.
∎

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and
J
its set of involutions.
If
i\neq j
are two distinct involutions in
J
, then

N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))\cap Q^{\cdot 2}=\mathrm{Cen}(ij).

Proof.

Assume there is
a\in(N_{B}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))\cap J^{\cdot 2})\setminus\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
and consider the group

. Note that

is abelian and hence

is a solvable subgroup of

. By the previous lemma we have

. Now Theorem 9.11 of
[
6
]
implies that

and

are conjugate. This is impossible.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of Morley rank

and odd type and let

be the set of all involutions in

.

a)

J
forms a mock hyperbolic reflection space and all lines in

b)

Choose

such that

is a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space such that all lines are of Morley rank

. If

a) We first show that

forms a mock hyperbolic reflection space. We already know that conditions a) and b) of

Fix a line

. Then

.

splits by

we may assume

. Then again by

B
has a connected normal subgroup

of rank

On the other hand

As a direct consequence we get the following known corollary (which also follows from Lemma 11.21 and Theorem 11.32 of

).

Corollary 6.11
.

Let

This follows directly from

Proposition 6.12
.

Let

be a connected non-split Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type and let

be the associated mock hyperbolic reflection space. If generic lines have Morley rank

, then

is a non-split sharply

-transitive group of characteristic

.

Set

.
The set of translations has Morley rank

and is not generic in

. On the other hand,
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
acts on

without fixed points. Therefore
\mathrm{MR}(\mathrm{Cen}(i)\leq 2n

Remark 6.13
.

Proposition 6.14
.

Let

be a connected Frobenius group of Morley rank at most

and odd type. Then either

splits or

is a simple non-split sharply

-transitive group of Morley rank

.

Assume

does not split.

. Since the set of translations is not generic in

and

6.2
Frobenius groups of odd type with nilpotent complement

Delahan and Nesin showed that a sharply
2
-transitive group of finite Morley rank of characteristic
\neq 2
with nilpotent point stabilizer must split (Theorem 11.73 of
[
6
]
). We will show that the same is true for a Frobenius group of odd type if the lines in the associated mock hyperbolic reflection geometry are strongly minimal or if there is no interpretable bad field of characteristic
0
.

We fix a connected Frobenius group
H<G
of finite Morley rank of odd type and we denote the set of involutions by
J
.
By
Theorem

6.10

J
forms a mock hyperbolic reflection space with infinite lines. Recall that
H=\mathrm{Cen}(i)
if
i
is the unique involution in
H
. If
\lambda
is a line containing
i
, then
N_{G}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\cdot 2}\rtimes N_{H}(\lambda)
is a split Frobenius group.

Lemma 6.15
.

If
i\neq j
are involutions, then
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
has infinite index in
N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))
. In particular,
N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\ell_{ij})
is infinite.

Proof.

Otherwise
\bigcup_{g\in G}\mathrm{Cen}(ij)^{g}\subseteq J^{\cdot 2}
would be generic in
G
. This is impossible since
\bigcup_{i\in J}\mathrm{Cen}(i)
is generic in
G
and the elements of
J^{\cdot 2}
do not have fixed points. Therefore
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
has infinite index in
N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))
.

Now
N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))=N_{G}(\ell_{ij})
and
N_{G}(\ell_{ij})\cap J=\ell_{ij}
forms a mock hyperbolic reflection space (consisting of one line). Therefore
N_{G}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))=i\ell_{ij}N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\ell_{ij})
and thus
N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\ell_{ij})
is infinite.
∎

If the point stabilizer in a sharply
2
-transitive group of characteristic
\neq 2
with planar maximal near-field contains an element
g\not\in\{1,i\}
such that
g
normalizes all lines containing
i
, then by
[
17
]
the sharply
2
-transitive group splits. We are going to prove a similar result for Frebenius groups of finite Morley rank of odd type.

If
A
is a group, then we write
A^{*}=A\setminus\{1\}
.

Lemma 6.16
.

Let
\lambda
be a line containing
i\in J
and fix a definable solvable subgroup
A\leq N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\lambda)
. Then
A^{*}i\lambda\cup\{1\}=A^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Note that
H=\lambda^{\cdot 2}\rtimes A
is a solvable Frobenius group of finite Morley rank. By Theorem 11.32 of
[
6
]
we have
H=\lambda^{\cdot 2}\cup\bigcup_{j\in\lambda}A^{j}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 6.17
.

Let
\Lambda
be a set of lines on
J
such that
(J,\Lambda)
forms a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space. Suppose there exists a definable infinite solvable normal subgroup
A\trianglelefteq\mathrm{Cen}(i)
such that
A\leq\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\lambda)
. Then
H<G
splits.

Proof.

We may assume that all lines in
\Lambda
have Morley rank
k
. Since
i
is central in
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
, we may also assume that
i\in A
.

Now set
J_{i}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}\lambda=\{j\in J\setminus\{i\}:\ell_{ij}\text{ exists}\}\cup\{i\}
.
By the previous lemma we have
A^{*}i\lambda\cup\{1\}=A^{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
.
Hence we have

A^{*}iJ_{i}\cup\{1\}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}A^{*}i\lambda\cup\{1\}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}A^{\lambda}=A^{J_{i}}.

Therefore

A^{*}iJ\cup\{1\}\approx A^{*}iJ_{i}\cup\{1\}=A^{J_{i}}\approx A^{J}=A^{\mathrm{Cen}(i)J}=A^{G}.

Put
N=\mathrm{Stab}^{\approx}(A^{G})
. Then
A\leq N
and
N\trianglelefteq G
is a normal subgroup. Hence
A^{G}\leq N
.
Now
Proposition

5.4
implies that
A^{G}\approx N
.
Note that

\mathrm{MR}(N)=\mathrm{MR}(J)+\mathrm{MR}(A).

Moreover,
J\subseteq N
, therefore
J^{\cdot 2}\subseteq N
and thus
\mathrm{MR}(N)\geq 2n-k
. Note that
A
acts fixed point free on any line
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
and therefore
\mathrm{MR}(A)\leq k
. In conclusion

n-k\leq\mathrm{MR}(N)-\mathrm{MR}(J)=\mathrm{MR}(A)\leq k

and therefore
n\leq 2k
. Now
Proposition

5.19
implies that
H<G
splits.
∎

Corollary 6.18
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type. If
H
is a minimal group, i.e., if
H
does not contain an infinite proper definable subgroup, then
H<G
splits.

Proof.

The assumptions and
Lemma

6.15
imply that
N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\lambda)=\mathrm{Cen}(i)
holds for all

in
J
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
. If
H=\mathrm{Cen}(i)
, then
H=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}N_{H}(\lambda)
and

is abelian.
Therefore
Proposition

6.17
implies that
H<G
splits.
∎

We can use Zilber’s Field Theorem to find interpretable fields in Frobenius groups of odd type.

Proposition 6.19

(Theorem 9.1 of
[
6
]
)
.

Let

be a group of finite Morley rank where

and

are infinite definable abelian subgroups and

is

-minimal, i.e., there are no definable infinite

-invariant subgroups. Assume that

. Then there is an interpretable field

,

, and

Let
\lambda=\ell_{ij}
be a line. Then
N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\lambda)
is infinite and acts on
\lambda^{\cdot 2}=\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
by conjugation. Take a minimal subgroup
A\leq N_{\mathrm{Cen}(i)}(\lambda)
. Since the action of
A
on
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
has no fixed points we can find an infinite
A
-minimal subgroup
B\leq\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
on which
A
acts faithfully. Moreover,
B
must be abelian because
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
is an abelian group. Hence by
Proposition

6.19
there is an interpretable field
K
such that
B\cong K_{+}
,
A\leq K^{*}
, and
A
acts by multiplication.

In particular, if the line
\lambda
is strongly minimal, then
K
is strongly minimal and
A\cong K^{*}
.

If
A
is a proper subgroup of
K^{*}
, then
K
is a bad field, i.e., an infinite field of finite Morley rank such that
K^{*}
has a proper infinite definable subgroup. By
[
4
]
bad fields of characteristic
0
exist. However, it follows from work of Wagner
[
19
]
that if
\mathrm{char}(K)\neq 0
, then
K^{*}
is a good torus, i.e., every definable subgroup of
K^{*}
is the definable hull of its torsion subgroup. We refer to
[
7
]
for properties of these good tori.

Theorem 6.20
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type. Fix
\Lambda
such that
(J,\Lambda)
is a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space.
Moreover, assume that
H
has a definable nilpotent normal subgroup
N
such that
N\cap N_{H}(\lambda)
is infinite for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
.

If all lines in
\Lambda
are strongly minimal or if
G
does not interpret a bad field of characteristic
0
, then
H<G
splits.

Proof.

We may assume that
N
is connected.
Let
T
be a maximal good torus in
N
. As a consequence of the structure of nilpotent groups of finite Morley rank (Theorem 6.8 and 6.9 of
[
6
]
)
T
must be central in
N
. By Theorem 1 of
[
7
]
any two maximal good tori are conjugate. Therefore
T
is the unique maximal good torus in
N
. Since a connected subgroup of a good torus is a good torus, the assumptions (and the previous discussion) imply that
N_{H}(\lambda)\cap T
is infinite for all lines
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
. By Lemma 2 of
[
7
]
the family
\{N_{H}(\lambda)\cap T:\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}\}
is finite. Hence after replacing
\Lambda
by a generic subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
we may assume that
\{N_{H}(\lambda)\cap T:\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}\}
consists of a unique infinite abelian normal subgroup of

. Now
Proposition

6.17
implies that
H<G
splits.
∎

6.3
Frobenius groups of degenerate type

We now use mock hyperbolic spaces to study Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank and degenerate type.
A geometry with similar properties, but defined on the whole group, was used by Frécon in his result on the non-existence of bad groups of Morley rank
3
.

Lemma 6.21
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of Morley rank
n
and of degenerate type. Suppose the Frobenius complement
H
is abelian and of Morley rank
k
. Then
n\geq 2k+1
and if
n=2k+1
, then
G
contains a definable normal subgroup
N
of Morley rank
k+1
.

Proof.

Note that
G
is uniquely
2
-divisible and hence
a\otimes b=a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
defines a K-loop structure on
G
. Let
L=(G,\otimes)
denote the corresponding K-loop and set
\mathcal{A}=G\times\langle\epsilon\rangle<\mathrm{Aut}(L)
where
\epsilon
is given by inversion. Now let
\mathcal{G}
be the quasidirect product
\mathcal{G}=L\rtimes_{Q}\mathcal{A}
.

By
Theorem

4.7
the involutions
J
in
\mathcal{G}
form a partial mock hyperbolic reflection space and since
\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g}\subseteq G
is a generic subset of
G
the involutions must form a generic mock hyperbolic reflection space.
Moreover,
\mathrm{MR}(J)=n
and each line has Morley rank
k
.
Now the lemma follows from
Theorem

5.5
.
∎

Theorem 6.22
.

Let
H<G
be a connected Frobenius group of Morley rank
n
and of degenerate type. Suppose the Frobenius complement
H
is abelian and of Morley rank
k
. Then
n\geq 2k+1
.

If
n=2k+1
, then
G
splits as
G=N\rtimes H
for some definable connected normal subgroup
N
of Morley rank
k+1
.
Moreover, if
N
is solvable, then there is an interpretable field
K
of characteristic
\neq 2
such that
G=K_{+}\rtimes H
,
H\leq K^{*}
, and
H
acts on
K_{+}
by multiplication.

Proof.

By the previous lemma we may assume
n=2k+1
. Then
G
contains a definable normal subgroup
N
of rank
k+1
and we may assume that
N
is connected.

Note that

\mathrm{MR}(\bigcup_{g\in G}(N\cap H)^{g})=k+1+\mathrm{MR}(N\cap H)
and
\mathrm{MR}(N)=k+1
. Therefore
N\cap H
must be finite. If
N\cap H
is non-trivial, then
(N\cap H)<N
is a connected Frobenius group and hence
N\cap H
must be connected. Therefore
N\cap H=\{1\}
.

The semidirect product
N\rtimes H
has rank
2k+1
and hence is generic in
G
. Therefore
G=N\rtimes H
splits.

Now assume that
N
is solvable. Then
N
is nilpotent by Theorem 11.59 of
[
6
]
and hence
\mathrm{Cen}(u)\leq N
for all
u\in N\setminus\{1\}
(
[
6
]
Exercise on page 215). Note that
u^{G}
cannot be generic in
N
because
G
does not contain involutions. Therefore
\mathrm{MR}(u^{G})\leq k
and hence
\mathrm{MR}(\mathrm{Cen}(u))\geq k+1
. Therefore
\mathrm{Cen}(u)=N
and hence
N
is abelian.

We now show that
N
is
H
-minimal: Let
A\leq N
be a
H
-invariant subgroup. We may assume that
A
is connected. Given
a\in A\setminus\{1\}
we have
\mathrm{Cen}(a)\cap H=\{1\}
and therefore
a^{H}\subseteq A
has rank
k
. If
A
has rank
k
, then
a^{H}
is generic in
A
and therefore
A
must contain an involution. This is a contraddiction.
Therefore
A=\{1\}
or
A=N
and hence
N
is
H
-minimal.

By
Proposition

6.19
there must be an interpretable field
K
such that
N=K_{+}
,
H\leq K^{*}
, and
H
acts on
N
by multiplication.
∎

Sharply 2-transitive groups of finite Morley rank

Let
G
be a sharply
2
-transitive group of finite Morley rank with
\mathrm{char}(G)\neq 2
and let

denote the set of involutions in

. By
Corollary

3.4
(or by

6.10
) the set

forms a mock hyperbolic reflection space.

We set
n=\mathrm{MR}(Q)
and
k=\mathrm{MR}(\mathrm{Cen}(ij))
for involutions
i\neq j\in Q
. Note that

does not depend on the choice of
i
and

and

if and only if

is split. By
[
6
]
Prop. 11.71 we have

and we will improve this inequality below.

Since

acts sharply
2
-transitively on

, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{MR}(G)=2n
and
\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 2})=2n-k
. Moreover,

and
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
have Morley degree 1 by Lemma 11.60 of
[
6
]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

a)

The set
iQ
is indecomposable for all
i\in Q
.

b)

\langle Q^{\cdot 2}\rangle
is a definable connected subgroup. In particular, there is a bound
m
such that any
g\in\langle Q^{\cdot 2}\rangle
is a product of at most
m
translations.

Proof.

a) Fix an involution
i\in Q
. The set
iQ
is normalized by
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
, hence it suffices to check indecomposability for
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
-normal subgroups. If
H\leq G
is a
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
-normal subgroup of
G
, then either
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)\leq H
for all
j\in Q\setminus\{i\}
or
H\ \cap\ \mathrm{Cen}(ij)
has infinite index in
\mathrm{Cen}(ij)
for all

. Therefore the set

is indecomposable.

b) Since
\langle Q^{\cdot 2}\rangle=\langle iQ\rangle
, this follows from Zilber’s indecomposability theorem using (a).
∎

Remark 7.2
.

By Proposition
7.1
(b) it is easy to see that the non-split examples of sharply 2-transitive groups of characteristic
0
constructed in
[

]
do not have finite Morley rank.

Lemma 7.3
.

For any
g\in G\setminus Q
the set
\{i\in Q\colon gi\mbox{ has a fixed point}\}
is generic in
Q
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
. For any
j\in Q
there is a unique
i_{j}\in Q
swapping
j
and
j^{g}
. Then
gi_{j}
centralizes

, so has a fixed point.
If
i_{j}=i_{k}
for some

, then by sharp 2-transitivity it follows that
g=i_{j}=i_{k}\in Q
.
Hence for
g\notin Q
, the
i_{j},j\in Q
, are pairwise distinct and hence
\{i_{j},j\in Q\}
has Morley rank

.
∎

Let
\mu:G^{3}\longrightarrow G
be the multiplication map, i.e.,
\mu(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})=g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

We have
\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 3})>\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 2})
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 3})=\mathrm{MR}(iQ^{\cdot 3})\geq\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 2})>\mathrm{MR}(Q)=n
and hence

is not a generic subset of

.

For

we let

be the set of all involutions

is a translation.
By Lemma

and Remark

MR(X_{\alpha})<n
for all

.

Let
\mathrm{MR}(Q^{\cdot 3})=2n-k+l
for some

. There is a generic set of
\alpha\in Q^{\cdot 3}\setminus Q
such that
\mathrm{MR}(\mu^{-1}(\alpha))=n+k-l
. Set

for such an
\alpha\in Q^{\cdot 3}\setminus Q
. If

, then
\mathrm{MR}(\{j\in Q:rs\in jQ\})=k
and hence
\mathrm{MR}(\mu^{-1}(\alpha))=\mathrm{MR}(X)+k

.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let

be a non-split sharply

-transitive group of finite Morley rank.

is simple and a counterexample to the Cherlin-Zilber Conjecture.

Proof.

Since

∎

If

8
Further remarks

A finite uniquely
2
-divisible K-loop is the same as a finite B-loop in the sense of Glauberman
[
9
]
. As a consequence of Glauberman’s
Z^{*}
-Theorem
[
10
]
finite B-loops are solvable.
Following Glauberman we say that a K-loop
L
is
half-embedded
in some group
G
if it is isomorphic to a K-loop arising from a uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup of
G
as in
Proposition

4.4
. B-loops and uniquely
2
-divisible K-loops can always be half-embedded in some group and that group can be chosen to be finite if the loop is finite (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 of
[
9
]
).
This allows us to restate Glauberman’s result for twisted subgroups:

Proposition 8.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a group and let
L\subseteq G
be a finite uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup. Then
\langle L\rangle
is solvable.

As a consequence finite mock hyperbolic spaces must consist of a single line:

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose
Q
forms a finite mock hyperbolic reflection space in a group
G
. Then
Q
consists of a single line.

Proof.

We may assume that
G
acts faithfully on
Q
. Let
i\in Q
be an involution. Then it is easy to see that
iQ
is a finite uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup in
G
. Therefore
\langle iQ\rangle
is solvable. Moreover,
\mathrm{Cen}(i)\leq N_{G}(\langle iQ\rangle)
and
G
can be decomposed as
G=iQ\mathrm{Cen}(i)
. Therefore
\langle iQ\rangle
is a solvable normal subgroup of
G
. It follows that
G
contains a non-trivial abelian normal subgroup. Now
Theorem

2.14
implies that
Q
consists of a single line.
∎

In the context of groups of finite Morley rank we do not know if every uniquely
2
-divisible K-loop of finite Morley rank can be definably half-embedded into a group of finite Morley rank.
The following would be a finite Morley rank version of Glauberman’s theorem:

Conjecture 8.3
.

Let
G
be a connected group of finite Morley rank with a definable uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup
L
of Morley degree
1
such that
G=\langle L\rangle
. Then
G
is solvable.

Note that this conjecture would imply the Feit-Thompson Theorem for connected groups of finite Morley rank: If
G
is a connected group of finite Morley rank of degenerate type, then
G
is uniquely
2
-divisible and hence
8.3
(applied to
L=G
) would imply that
G
is solvable.

Moreover, it would imply that Frobenius groups of finite Morley rank split: For Frobenius groups of degenerate type this would follow from solvability. If
G
is a connected Frobenius group of finite Morley rank and odd type with involutions
J
and lines
\Lambda
, then it suffices to show that
G
has a non-trivial definable solvable normal subgroup (in that case
G
has a non-trivial abelian normal subgroup and hence splits by
Theorem

2.14
). Note that
iJ
is a uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup. If
G
is sharply
2
-transitive, then
Proposition

7.1
shows that
\langle iJ\rangle
is definable and connected and hence should be solvable by
8.3
.

For the general case consider the family
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{\mathrm{Cen}(ij)^{0}:j\in J\setminus\{i\}\}
. By Zilber’s indecomposability theorem the subgroup
N=\langle H:H\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\rangle
is definable and connected. Moreover, it is easy to see that
N\cap iJ
must be generic in
iJ
and
N
must be normalized by
\mathrm{Cen}(i)
. Therefore
N
must be a normal subgroup of
G=iJ\mathrm{Cen}(i)
and clearly
N=\langle N\cap iJ\rangle
. Hence
8.3
would imply that
N
is solvable.

If Frobenius groups of odd and degenerate type split, then
Remark

6.3
shows that Frobenius groups of even type also split.

If the twisted subgroup in the statement of
8.3
is strongly minimal, then we show that
G
must be
2
-nilpotent:

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
G
be a connected group of finite Morley rank with a definable strongly minimal uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup

such that
G=\langle L\rangle
. Then
G
is
2
-nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
x\otimes y=x^{1/2}yx^{1/2}
be the corresponding K-loop structure on
L
.
If
(L,\otimes)
is an abelian group, then
[
12
, Theorem 6.14, part (3)]
implies
[[a,b],c]=1
for all
a,b,c\in L
and therefore
G=\langle L\rangle
must be
2
-nilpotent.
Therefore it suffices to show that
(L,\otimes)
is an abelian group.

Put
T=N_{G}(L)/\mathrm{Cen}(L)
. Then
T\leq\mathrm{Aut}((L,\otimes))
and we may consider the quasidirect product
\mathcal{G}=L\rtimes_{Q}T
.
As stated in
Proposition

4.5
the group
\mathcal{G}=L\rtimes_{Q}T
acts transitively and faithfully on
L
by

(a,\alpha)(x)=a\otimes\alpha(x)

and
T
is the stabilizer of
1\in L
.
Note that
L^{\prime}=L\times\{1\}
is a uniquely
2
-divisible twisted subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
. Hence
a\otimes^{\prime}b=a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
defines a K-loop structure on

. By
[
12
, Theorem 6.15]
the K-loops
(L,\otimes)
and
(L^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
are isomorphic. Therefore it suffices to show that
(L^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
is an abelian group.

Hrushovski’s analysis of groups acting on strongly minimal sets (Theorem 11.98 of
[

]
) shows that
\mathrm{MR}(\mathcal{G})\leq 3
. Moreover, if
\mathrm{MR}(\mathcal{G})=3
, then

acts sharply
2
-transitively on

which is impossible since
T
is a group of automorphisms of
(L,\otimes)
.

If
\mathrm{MR}(\mathcal{G})=2
, then

is a standard sharply
2
-transitive group

(and the corresponding permutation groups coincide). Since

acts without fixed points and the fixed point free elements of

are precisely the elements of

,

is contained in
K_{+}
. Therefore
\otimes^{\prime}
agrees with the group structure on

and hence
(L^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
is an abelian group.

Now assume
\mathrm{MR}(\mathcal{G})=1
. We argue similarly to the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 5, part (v)]
.

Consider the finite twisted subgroup
L^{\prime\prime}=\{a\mathcal{G}^{0}:a\in L^{\prime}\}
of

. Since
L^{\prime}
is uniquely
2
-divisible the map
L^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow L^{\prime\prime},a\mapsto a^{2}
is surjective and hence a bijection since

is finite. Hence we may define a K-loop structure
x\mathcal{G}^{0}\otimes^{\prime\prime}y\mathcal{G}^{0}=x^{1/2}yx^{1/2}\mathcal{G}^{0}
on

. The natural map
L^{\prime}\rightarrow L^{\prime\prime}
is a surjective homomorphism from
(L^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
to
(L^{\prime\prime},\otimes^{\prime\prime})
with kernel

.

In particular,
L^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}
is a normal subloop of

. Since

is finite and
\mathrm{MD}(L^{\prime})=1
this implies
L^{\prime}=L^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{G}^{0}
and hence

. The group
\mathcal{G}^{0}
is strongly minimal and thus abelian. Therefore
\otimes^{\prime}
agrees with the group structure on
\mathcal{G}^{0}
and therefore
(L^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
is an abelian group.
∎

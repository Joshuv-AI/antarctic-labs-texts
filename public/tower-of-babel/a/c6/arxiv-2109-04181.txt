Independence complex of the lexicographic product of a forest
Kengo Okura

Abstract
. We study the independence complex of the lexicographic product G ⁡ [ H ] {G}[{H}] of a forest G G and a graph H H . We prove that for a forest G G which is not dominated by a single vertex, if the independence complex of H H is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres, then so is the independence complex of G ⁡ [ H ] {G}[{H}] . We offer two examples of explicit calculations. As the first example, we determine the homotopy type of the independence complex of L m ​ [ H ] {L_{m}}[{H}] , where L m L_{m} is the tree on m m vertices with no branches, for any positive integer m m when the independence complex of H H is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of n n copies of d d -dimensional sphere. As the second one, for a forest G G and a complete graph K K , we describe the homological connectivity of the independence complex of G ⁡ [ K ] {G}[{K}] by the independent domination number of G G .

Independence complexes of graphs are no less important than other simplicial complexes constructed from graphs and have been studied in many contexts.
In particular, the independence complexes of square grid graphs are studied by Thapper

This is precisely the situation which we have to deal with in this paper. We need topological approaches in case that discrete Morse theory is not available. For example, it is effective to represent an independence complex of a graph as a union of independence complexes of subgraphs, as in Engström
[

]

\displaystyle\simeq\bigvee_{k}\left(\left(\left(\bigvee_{i}S^{a_{i}}\right)\sqcup\left(\bigvee_{j}S^{b_{j}}\right)\right)*S^{c_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigvee_{k}\left(\left(\left(\bigvee_{i}S^{a_{i}}\right)*S^{c_{k}}\right)\vee\left(\left(\bigvee_{j}S^{b_{j}}\right)*S^{c_{k}}\right)\vee S^{c_{k}+1}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigvee_{k}\left(\left(\bigvee_{i}S^{a_{i}+c_{k}+1}\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{j}S^{b_{j}+c_{k}+1}\right)\vee S^{c_{k}+1}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\bigvee_{i,k}S^{a_{i}+c_{k}+1}\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{j,k}S^{b_{j}+c_{k}+1}\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{k}S^{c_{k}+1}\right).

Therefore, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove the theorem by induction on
|V(G)|
. Before we start, we confirm two cases.

First, suppose that
G
is a star on at least
2
vertices, namely
|V(G)|\geq 2
and there exists
v\in V(G)
such that
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)=\emptyset
. We have
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in N_{G}(v)=G\setminus\{v\}
since
G
is a forest. So, by Theorem
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle I({G}[{H}])

\displaystyle=I(H)\sqcup I({(G\setminus\{v\})}[{H}])

\displaystyle=I(H)\sqcup\left(\mathop{*}_{|V(G)|-1}I(H)\right).

Since
|V(G)|-1\geq 1
, the join of copies of
I(H)
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres by Lemma
3.6
. Therefore,
I({G}[{H}])
is homotopy equivalent to a disjoint union of two wedge sums of spheres.

Next, suppose that
G
has no edges. Then
I({G}[{H}])
is the join of
|V(G)|
copies of
I(H)
, which is a wedge sum of spheres by Lemma
3.6
.

Now we start the induction.
The forest
G
with
|V(G)|\leq 2
is isomorphic to one of
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
and
L_{1}\sqcup L_{1}
. They are included in the above cases. Hence, for a forest
G
with
|V(G)|\leq 2
,
I({G}[{H}])
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres or a disjoint union of two wedge sums of spheres.

Assume that for any forest
G^{\prime}
such that
|V(G^{\prime})|\leq n
,
I({G^{\prime}}[{H}])
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres or a disjoint union of two wedge sums of spheres. Let
G
be a forest with at least one edge such that
|V(G)|=n+1
and
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)\neq\emptyset
for any
v\in V(G)
. Then, since
G
is a forest, there exists
w\in V(G)
such that
N_{G}(w)=\{v\}
for some
v\in V(G)
(namely a leaf
w
of
G
). We write
G_{1}=G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)
and
G_{2}=G\setminus\{v,w\}
. Then,
G_{1},G_{2}
are forests such that
|V(G_{1})|\leq n-1
,
|V(G_{2})|\leq n-1
.

Since
G_{1}=G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)
is not empty, it follows from Theorem
3.1
that

\displaystyle I({G}[{H}])\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({G_{1}}[{H}])\vee\left(I({G_{1}}[{H}])*I(H)\right)\vee\left(I({G_{2}}[{H}])*I(H)\right).

By the assumption of the induction,
I({G_{1}}[{H}])
and
I({G_{2}}[{H}])
are homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres or a disjoint union of two wedge sums of spheres. Therefore, by Lemma
3.6
,
I({G}[{H}])
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres.
So, the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

For a graph
H
, suppose that
I(H)
is contractible. Then, for a forest
G
, we have
I({G}[{H}])\simeq I(G)
. We can prove this fact in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Example 3.8
.

Recall that a graph
G
is
chordal
if it contains no cycle of length at least
4
. Kawamura
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that the independence complex of a chordal graph is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres. In particular, Ehrenborg and Hetyei
[
6
, Corollary 6.1]
proved that the independence complex of a forest is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to a single sphere. So, it follows from Theorem
1.2
and Remark
3.7
that
I({G}[{H}])
is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of spheres if
G
is a forest and
H
is a chordal graph.

Proof of Theorem

.

For
m=1,2,3
, it follows from Proposition

that

\displaystyle I({L_{1}}[{H}])

\displaystyle=I(H)\simeq{\bigvee}_{n}S^{k},

\displaystyle I({L_{2}}[{H}])

\displaystyle=I(H)\sqcup I(H)\simeq\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right),

\displaystyle I({L_{3}}[{H}])

\displaystyle=I(H)\sqcup(I(H)*I(H))

\displaystyle\simeq\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)*\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n}\left(S^{k}*\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n}\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}*S^{k}\right)\right)

For

, let

and

. Then we have

and
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)=L_{r}\neq\emptyset
. So, by Theorem

, we obtain

\displaystyle I({L_{r+3}}[{H}])

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\vee\left(I({L_{r}}[{H}])*I(H)\right)\ \vee\left(I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])*I(H)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\vee\left(I({L_{r}}[{H}])*\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)\ \vee\left(I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])*\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}I({L_{r}}[{H}])*S^{k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])*S^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{r}}[{H}])\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])\right).

(1)

Define a CW complex
X_{m,n,k}
for

,

and

by

\displaystyle X_{m,n,k}=\bigvee_{d\geq 0}\left(\bigvee_{p\geq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{m,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right),

where

\displaystyle N_{m,n,k}(p,d)

We note that

for non-negative integers
p,d
if and only if

and

, namely

The above inequality implies that

. So, it follows that

\displaystyle X_{m,n,k}=\bigvee_{0\leq p\leq\frac{m+1}{2}}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,\frac{m}{3}\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{m+p-2}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{m,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right).

I({L_{m}}[{H}])\simeq X_{m,n,k}
for
m\geq 4
. First, the explicit descriptions of
X_{1,n,k}
,
X_{2,n,k}
and
X_{3,n,k}
are obtained as follows.

\displaystyle X_{1,n,k}=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leq p\leq 1}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,\frac{1}{3}\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{1+p-2}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{1,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{p=0,1}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,\frac{1}{3}\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{p-1}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{1,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigvee_{-\frac{2}{3}\leq d\leq-\frac{1}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{1,n,k}(0,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{k\leq d\leq k}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{1,n,k}(1,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bigvee}_{N_{1,n,k}(1,k)}S^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bigvee}_{n^{1}\binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{2}}S^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}.

\displaystyle X_{2,n,k}=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leq p\leq\frac{3}{2}}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,\frac{2}{3}\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{2+p-2}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{p=0,1}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,\frac{2}{3}\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{p}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigvee_{-\frac{1}{3}\leq d\leq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(0,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{k\leq d\leq k+\frac{1}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(1,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(0,0)}S^{0}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{N_{2,n,k}(1,k)}S^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bigvee}_{n^{0}\binom{1}{0}\binom{1}{1}}S^{0}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n^{1}\binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{1}}S^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{0}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{2n}S^{k}\right).

\displaystyle X_{3,n,k}=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leq p\leq 2}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,1\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{3+p-2}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{p=0,1,2}\left(\bigvee_{pk-1+\max\left\{p,1\right\}\leq d\leq pk+\frac{p+1}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigvee_{0\leq d\leq\frac{1}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(0,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)\vee\left(\bigvee_{k\leq d\leq k+\frac{2}{3}}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(1,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ \vee\left(\bigvee_{2k+1\leq d\leq 2k+1}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(2,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(0,0)}S^{0}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(1,k)}S^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\ \vee\left({\bigvee}_{N_{3,n,k}(2,2k+1)}S^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bigvee}_{n^{0}\binom{1}{0}\binom{1}{0}}S^{0}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n^{1}\binom{1}{1}\binom{2}{0}}S^{k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}\binom{2}{2}\binom{3}{3}}S^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{0}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}}S^{2k+1}\right).

We next show that

\displaystyle X_{m+3,n,k}=\Sigma X_{m,n,k}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{m,n,k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{m+1,n,k}\right).

(2)

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}\left(N_{m,n,k}(p,d-1)+n\cdot N_{m,n,k}(p,d-k-1)+n\cdot N_{m+1,n,k}(p,d-k-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}\left(n^{p}\binom{(d-1)-pk+1}{p}\binom{p+1}{3((d-1)-pk+1)-m}\right.

\displaystyle\ +n^{p+1}\binom{(d-k-1)-pk+1}{p}\binom{p+1}{3((d-k-1)-pk+1)-m}

\displaystyle\ \left.+n^{p+1}\binom{(d-k-1)-pk+1}{p}\binom{p+1}{3((d-k-1)-pk+1)-(m+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}\left(n^{p}\binom{d-pk}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-pk)-m}\right.

\displaystyle\ +n^{p+1}\binom{d-(p+1)k}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-(p+1)k)-m}

\displaystyle\ \left.+n^{p+1}\binom{d-(p+1)k}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-(p+1)k)-(m+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}n^{p}\binom{d-pk}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-pk)-m}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{p\geq 0}n^{p+1}\binom{d-(p+1)k}{p}\binom{p+2}{3(d-(p+1)k)-(m+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}n^{p}\binom{d-pk}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-pk)-m}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{q=p+1\geq 1}n^{q}\binom{d-qk}{q-1}\binom{q+1}{3(d-qk)-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}n^{p}\binom{d-pk+1}{p}\binom{p+1}{3(d-pk)-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}N_{m+3,k}(p,d).

So, we conclude that

\displaystyle\Sigma X_{m,n,k}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{m,n,k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{m+1,n,k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{d\geq 0}\left(\bigvee_{p\geq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{m,n,k}(p,d-1)+n\cdot N_{m,n,k}(p,d-k-1)+n\cdot N_{m+1,n,k}(p,d-k-1)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{d\geq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{\sum_{p\geq 0}\left(N_{m,n,k}(p,d-1)+n\cdot N_{m,n,k}(p,d-k-1)+n\cdot N_{m+1,n,k}(p,d-k-1)\right)}S^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{d\geq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{\sum_{p\geq 0}N_{m+3,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{d\geq 0}\left(\bigvee_{p\geq 0}\left({\bigvee}_{N_{m+3,n,k}(p,d)}S^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{m+3,n,k}

as desired.

Now, we are ready to finish the proof by induction on
m
. By Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{2}}[{H}])

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq S^{1}\vee\Sigma\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\vee\Sigma\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq S^{1}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k+1}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=S^{1}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{2n}S^{k+1}\right),

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{3}}[{H}])

\displaystyle\simeq\Sigma\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}}S^{2k+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq S^{1}\vee\Sigma\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\vee\Sigma\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}}S^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq S^{1}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k+1}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}}S^{2k+2}\right).

So, it follows that

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{m}}[{H}])\simeq\Sigma X_{m,n,k}

for
m=1,2,3
. Assume that
\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\simeq\Sigma X_{r,n,k}
and
\Sigma I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])\simeq\Sigma X_{r+1,n,k}
for some

. By recursive relations (
1
) and (
2
), we have

\displaystyle I({L_{r+3}}[{H}])

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{r}}[{H}])\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{r}}[{H}])\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{r+1}}[{H}])\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma X_{r,n,k}\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{r,n,k}\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}X_{r+1,n,k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{r+3,n,k}.

Therefore, we obtain that

for any

∎

Example 4.1
.

Kozlov
[
13
, Proposition 5.2]
proved that

\displaystyle I(C_{n})

\displaystyle\simeq\left\{\begin{aligned} &S^{k-1}\vee S^{k-1}&&(n=3k),\\
&S^{k-1}&&(n=3k+1),\\
&S^{k}&&(n=3k+2).\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore, we can determine the homotopy types of
I({L_{m}}[{C_{n}}])
for any
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 3
by Theorem
1.3
.

Recall that the homological connectivity of a space
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{conn}_{H}(X)
, is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(X)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-2&&(X=\emptyset),\\
&k&&(\widetilde{H}_{i}(X)=0\text{ for any $i\leq k$, }\widetilde{H}_{k+1}(X)\neq 0),\\
&\infty&&(\widetilde{H}_{i}(X)=0\text{ for any $i$ }),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\widetilde{H}_{i}(X)
is the reduced
i
th homology group of
X
.
Though Theorem
1.3
completely determines the homotopy type of
I({L_{m}}[{H}])
with
I(H)\simeq{\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}
, it is hard to obtain the homological connectivity of
I({L_{m}}[{H}])
immediately from Theorem
1.3
. Here we compute the homological connectivity of
I({L_{m}}[{H}])
as a corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
H
be a graph such that
I(H)\simeq{\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}
with
n\geq 1
,
k\geq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({L_{3l+i}}[{H}]))=\left\{\begin{aligned} &l-2&&(i=0),\\
&k+l-1&&(i=1),\\
&l-1&&(i=2).\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
1.3
that there is a recursive relation

\displaystyle I({L_{m+3}}[{H}])

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({L_{m}}[{H}])\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{m}}[{H}])\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{m+1}}[{H}])\right).

So, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({L_{m+3}}[{H}]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left\{\mathrm{conn}_{H}(\Sigma I({L_{m}}[{H}])),\mathrm{conn}_{H}(\Sigma^{k+1}I({L_{m+1}}[{H}]))\right\}.

The base cases are

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({L_{1}}[{H}]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{conn}_{H}\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)=k-1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({L_{2}}[{H}]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{conn}_{H}\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\right)=-1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({L_{3}}[{H}]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{conn}_{H}\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n}S^{k}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{n^{2}}S^{2k+1}\right)\right)=-1.

Therefore, we can prove the corollary by induction.
∎

We move on to the second example.
We denote the complete graph on
n
vertices by
K_{n}
. For
n\geq 2
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle I(K_{n})={\bigvee}_{n-1}S^{0}.

As the second example in this section, we show that the homological connectivity of
I({G}[{K_{n}}])
for any forest
G
is determined by the
independent domination number
of
G
when
n\geq 2
. For a graph
G
and a subset
S\subset V(G)
,
S
is a
dominating set
of
G
if
V(G)=\bigcup_{u\in S}\overline{N}_{G}(u)
.
The domination number
\gamma(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set of
G
. The relationship between the domination number of
G
and the homological connectivity of
I(G)
was argued by Meshulam
[
14
]
, who proved that for a chordal graph
G
,
i<\gamma(G)
implies
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(I(G))=0
(
[
14
, Theorem 1.2 (iii)]
). This is equivalent to state that
\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I(G))\geq\gamma(G)-2
.
This theorem can be used to deduce a result of Aharoni, Berger and Ziv
[
2
]
.

A dominating set
S
of
G
is called
an independent dominating set
if
S
is an independent set. The independent domination number
i(G)
is the minimum cardinality of an independent dominating set of
G
. It is obvious that
i(G)\geq\gamma(G)
since an independent dominating set is a dominating set.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a forest. Then, for any
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({G}[{K_{n}}]))=i(G)-2.

(3)

Proof.

We first consider two cases.

•

If
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)=\emptyset
for some
v\in V(G)
, then we have
i(G)=1
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({G}[{K_{n}}]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{conn}_{H}\left(\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}S^{0}\right)\sqcup\left({\bigvee}_{(n-1)^{|V(G)|-1}}S^{|V(G)|-2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-1

by Theorem
3.1
.

•

If
G
has no edges, then we have
i(G)=|V(G)|
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({G}[{K_{n}}]))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{conn}_{H}\left({\bigvee}_{(n-1)^{|V(G)|}}S^{|V(G)|-1}\right)

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-2.

Therefore, equation (
3
) holds in these two cases.

We prove the theorem by induction on
|V(G)|
. Since
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
and
L_{1}\sqcup L_{1}
are included in the above two cases, equation (
3
) holds for
G
such that
|V(G)|\leq 2
.
Assume that (
3
) holds for any forest
G^{\prime}
such that
|V(G^{\prime})|\leq r
with
r\geq 2
. Let
G
be a forest such that
|V(G)|=r+1
and there exists
v,w\in V(G)
such that
N_{G}(w)=\{v\}
and
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)\neq\emptyset
.
By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle I({G}[{K_{n}}])\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}])\vee\left(I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}])*\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}S^{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ \vee\left(I({(G\setminus\{v,w\})}[{K_{n}}])*\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}S^{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Sigma I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}])\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}\Sigma I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}])\right)

\displaystyle\ \vee\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}\Sigma I({(G\setminus\{v,w\})}[{K_{n}}])\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bigvee}_{n}\Sigma I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}])\right)\vee\left({\bigvee}_{n-1}\Sigma I({(G\setminus\{v,w\})}[{K_{n}}])\right).

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({G}[{K_{n}}]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left\{\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))}[{K_{n}}]))+1,\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({(G\setminus\{v,w\})}[{K_{n}}]))+1\right\}.

G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)
and
G\setminus\{v,w\}
are the forests which satisfy
|V(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))|\leq r-1
,
|V(G\setminus\{v,w\})|\leq r-1
. So, by the assumption of induction, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{conn}_{H}(I({G}[{K_{n}}]))=

\displaystyle\min\left\{i(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))-1,i(G\setminus\{v,w\})-1\right\}.

Here, we have
i(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))\geq i(G)-1
. It is because if there exists an independent dominating set
S
of
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)
with
|S|<i(G)-1
, then
S\cup\{v\}
is an independent dominating set of
G
such that
|S\cup\{u\}|<i(G)
, a contradiction. For the same reason, we also have
i(G\setminus\{v,w\})\geq i(G)-1
.

An independent dominating set of
G
must contain either
v
or
w
since
N_{G}(w)=\{v\}
. If there exists an independent dominating set
S
of
G
such that
|S|=i(G)
and
v\in S
, then
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{v\}
is an independent dominating set of
G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v)
with
|S^{\prime}|=i(G)-1
since
S\cap\overline{N}_{G}(v)=\{v\}
. Thus, in this case, we obtain
i(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))=i(G)-1
. If there exists an independent dominating set
S
of
G
such that
|S|=i(G)
and
w\in S
, then
S^{\prime\prime}=S\setminus\{w\}
is an independent dominating set of
G\setminus\{v,w\}
with
|S^{\prime\prime}|=i(G)-1
since
v\notin S
. So, in this case, we get
i(G\setminus\{v,w\})=i(G)-1
.

Above argument shows that

\displaystyle\min\left\{i(G\setminus\overline{N}_{G}(v))-1,i(G\setminus\{v,w\})-1\right\}=i(G)-2.

Therefore, equation (
3
) holds for
G
. By induction, we get the desired conclusion.

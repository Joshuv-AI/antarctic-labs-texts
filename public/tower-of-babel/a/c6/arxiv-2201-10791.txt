Digraph analogues for the Nine Dragon Tree Conjecture
Hui Gao Daqing Yang

Abstract
The fractional arboricity of a digraph D D , denoted by γ ⁡ ( D ) \gamma(D) , is defined as γ ⁡ ( D ) = max H ⊆ D , | V ⁡ ( H ) | > 1 ⁡ | A ⁡ ( H ) | | V ⁡ ( H ) | − 1 \gamma(D)=\max_{H\subseteq D,|V(H)|>1}\frac{|A(H)|}{|V(H)|-1} . Frank in [Covering branching, Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) 41 (1979), 77-81] proved that a digraph D D decomposes into k k branchings, if and only if Δ − ​ ( D ) ≤ k \Delta^{-}(D)\leq k and γ ⁡ ( D ) ≤ k \gamma(D)\leq k . In this paper, we study digraph analogues for the Nine Dragon Tree Conjecture. We conjecture that, for positive integers k k and d d , if D D is a digraph with γ ⁡ ( D ) ≤ k + d − k d + 1 \gamma(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1} and Δ − ​ ( D ) ≤ k + 1 \Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1 , then D D decomposes into k + 1 k+1 branchings B 1 , … , B k , B k + 1 B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1} with Δ + ​ ( B k + 1 ) ≤ d \Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d . This conjecture, if true, is a refinement of Frank’s characterization. 1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider graphs or digraphs which may have multiple edges or arcs but no loops.
For a graph
G
, a
decomposition of
G
consists of edge-disjoint subgraphs with union
G
; a
decomposition of a digraph
D
is defined similarly. The
arboricity of
G
is the minimum number of forests needed to decompose it. The
fractional arboricity of
G
is defined as

\gamma(G):=\max_{H\subseteq G,|V(H)|>1}\frac{|E(H)|}{|V(H)|-1}.

This notion was introduced by Payan
[
13
]
; see also
[
1
,
6
]
.
For a digraph
D
, the
fractional arboricity of
D
is defined to be the fractional arboricity of its underlying graph, written as
\gamma(D)
.
The celebrated Nash-Williams’ Theorem states a necessary and sufficient condition for the arboricity of
G
to be at most
k
:

Theorem 1.1
(Nash-Williams’ Theorem,
[
12
]
)

A graph
G
can be decomposed into
k
forests if and only if
\gamma(G)\leq k
.

When
k<\gamma(G)<k+1
, by Theorem
1.1
,
G
decomposes into
k+1
forests. However, when
\gamma(G)=k+\epsilon
is closer to
k
than to
k+1
, then not only does
G
decompose into
k+1
forests, but one of the forests can be small (Refer
[
3
, Corollary 3.7]
for this).
Montassier, Ossona de Mendez, Raspaud, and
Zhu
[
11
]
gave two definitions of this special forest, called
\epsilon
-forest
:
(i)
a forest whose maximum degree is bounded by a function of
\epsilon
,
(ii)
a forest consisting of small subtrees, i.e. subtrees whose sizes are bounded by a function of
\epsilon
. Based on the two different definitions, they proposed the NDT (Nine Dragon Tree) Conjecture and Strong NDT Conjecture.
A graph is
d
-bounded
if its maximum degree is at most
d
.

Conjecture 1.2
(The NDT Conjecture,
[
11
]
)

For non-negative integers
k
and
d
, if
G
is a graph with
\gamma(G)\leq k+\frac{d}{k+d+1}
, then
G
decomposes into
k+1
forests with one being
d
-bounded.

Conjecture 1.3
(The Strong NDT Conjecture,
[
11
]
)

For non-negative integers
k
and
d
, if
G
is a graph with
\gamma(G)\leq k+\frac{d}{k+d+1}
, then
G
decomposes into
k+1
forests, with one of them consisting of subtrees of sizes at most
d
.

In
[
11
]
, Montassier et al. proved the fractional arboricity bound is sharp and the NDT Conjecture holds when
(k,d)=(1,1)
or
(1,2)
. Many partial results
[
2
,
10
,
14
]
on the NDT Conjecture came until Jiang and Yang
[
9
]
proved it. The strong NDT Conjecture is only proved for cases
(k,d)=(1,2)

[
10
]
and
d=1

[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.4
(The NDT Theorem,
[
9
]
)

The NDT Conjecture is true.

This paper will study some directed versions of Conjectures
1.2
and
1.3
. We shall use the concepts of ‘arborescence’ and ‘branching’, which are well studied in digraphs, and are the directed versions of ‘tree’ and ‘forest’.

Let
D=(V,A)
be a digraph.
A subdigraph of
D
is spanning if its vertex set is
V
. A subdigraph
F
(it may not be spanning) of
D
is an
r
-arborescence
if its underlying graph is a tree,
r\in V(D)
and for any
u\in V(F)
, there is exactly one directed path in
F
from
r
to
u
. We say that the vertex
r
is the
root of the arborescence
F
.

A
branching

B
in
D
is a spanning subdigraph each component of which is an arborescence, and the
root set

R(B)
of
B
consists of all roots of its components. Frank
[
5
]
characterized a digraph which decomposes into
k
branchings.

For
v\in V(D)
, let
\partial_{D}^{-}(v)
denote the set of arcs in
D
with
v
as their head,
\partial_{D}^{+}(v)
denote the set of arcs in
D
with
v
as their tail,
d_{D}^{-}(v):=|\partial_{D}^{-}(v)|
and
d_{D}^{+}(v):=|\partial_{D}^{+}(v)|
.

\Delta^{-}(D):=\max_{v\in V}d_{D}^{-}(v)
, and
\Delta^{+}(D):=\max_{v\in V}d_{D}^{+}(v)
.

Theorem 1.5
(
[
5
]
)

A digraph
D
decomposes into
k
branchings if and only if
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k
and
\gamma(D)\leq k
.

Analogous to the NDT Conjecture
1.2
, we propose the following conjecture as its corresponding directed version.

Conjecture 1.6

For positive integers
k
and
d
, if
D
is a digraph with

\gamma(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}

and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, then
D
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.

In Section
2
, we shall prove Conjecture
1.6
for cases
d\leq k
. Note that in Conjecture
1.6
, the condition of
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
is necessary: if
D
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k+1}
, then
\Delta^{-}(B_{i})\leq 1
for
1\leq i\leq k+1
, thus
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
.
In Section
4
, by constructing a series of acyclic bipartite digraphs, we show the following theorem, which says
that the bound of
\gamma(D)
given in Conjecture
1.6
is best possible.

Theorem 1.7

For positive integers
k,d
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an acyclic bipartite digraph
D
such that
\gamma(D)<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, but
D
can not be decomposed into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.

It is temping to make some analogous conjecture in digraphs corresponding to the Strong NDT Conjecture
1.3
.
Unfortunately, we construct an example next which shows some naively analogous conjecture is not true.

Construct a sequence of digraphs whose underlying graphs are trees,
D_{0}\subseteq D_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq D_{n}
:

(i)

D_{0}
is a single vertex;

(ii)
for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, construct
D_{i+1}
from
D_{i}
by adding
(k+1)^{i+1}
new vertices and
(k+1)^{i+1}
new arcs such that for already existing vertices
v\in V(D_{i})
,
d_{D_{i+1}}^{-}(v)=k+1
.
As an example, Figure
1
shows the sequence of digraphs
D_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, of
k=1
and
n=3
.

Figure 1:
k=1,n=3

Note that in
D_{n}
, for any
v\in V(D_{n-1})
,
d_{D_{n}}^{-}(v)=k+1
; for any
u\in V(D_{n})\setminus V(D_{n-1})
,
d_{D_{n}}^{-}(u)=0
, and
u
is an in-neighbor of some vertex in
V(D_{n-1})\setminus V(D_{n-2})
. This leads to the fact that, up to isomorphism,
D_{n}
has only one decomposition into
k+1
branchings, each containing a directed path of length
n
.

Since
\Delta^{-}(D_{n})=k+1
and
\gamma(D_{n})=1
,
this example shows that for
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, the fractional arboricity
1+\epsilon
(where
\epsilon
is any small positive value)
can not guarantee that a digraph decomposes into
k+1
branchings with one consisting of arborescences of bounded sizes. So it seems that it is still a puzzle to solve even for a conjecture along this direction.

A slightly different sparseness condition places a bound on the average vertex degree in all subgraphs. The
maximum average degree of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\rm mad}(G)
, is defined as

{\rm mad}(G):=\max_{H\subseteq G}\frac{2|E(H)|}{|V(H)|}.

For a digraph
D
, let
{\rm mad}(D):={\rm mad}(G[D])
, where
G[D]
is the underlying graph of
D
.

A graph is a
pseudo-forest
if there exists an orientation
\sigma
of
G
such that
\Delta^{-}(G^{\sigma})\leq 1
,
where
G^{\sigma}
is the oriented graph of
G
under
\sigma
.
Hakimi
[
8
]
characterized a graph which decomposes into
k
pseudo-forests:

Theorem 1.8
(
[
8
]
)

A graph
G
decomposes into
k
pseudo-forests if and only if
{\rm mad}(G)

\leq

2k
.

Fan, Li, Song and Yang
[
4
]
proved the pseudo-forest analogue of the NDT Theorem. Later, Grout and Moore
[
7
]
proved the pseudo-forest analogue of the Strong NDT Conjecture.

Theorem 1.9
(
[
4
]
)

For non-negative integers
k
and
d
, if
G
is a graph with
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(G)

\leq

k+\frac{d}{k+d+1}
, then
G
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-forests with one being
d
-bounded.

Theorem 1.10
(
[
7
]
)

For non-negative integers
k
and
d
, if
G
is a graph with
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(G)

\leq k+\frac{d}{k+d+1}
, then
G
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-forests
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k+1}
such that each component of
F_{k+1}
has at most
d
edges.

A
pseudo-branching

C
in a digraph
D
is a spanning subdigraph with
\Delta^{-}(C)\leq 1
.

The same example above in the analysis for an analogue in digraphs corresponding to the Strong NDT Conjecture
1.3

says we need some more (unknown) ingredients to have an analogue in digraphs corresponding to Theorem
1.10
.
Here we give a digraph analogue to Theorem 1.9, stated below:

Theorem 1.11

For positive integers
k
and
d
, if
D
is a digraph with
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D)

\leq

k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}

and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, then
D
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k},C_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(C_{k+1})\leq d
.

Theorem
1.11
serves as one more piece of evidence to support Conjecture
1.6
.
We shall prove Theorem
1.11
in Section 3. The bound on
{\rm mad}(D)
in Theorem
1.11

is best possible, this shall be explained in Section
4
.

We need some notation for our proofs.
Suppose
D=(V,A)
is a digraph.
For
X\subseteq V
, let
N_{D}^{-}(X)=\{y\in V:y\notin X,\mbox{ and }\exists x\in X,\mbox{ such that }\overrightarrow{yx}\in A\}
,

N_{D}^{+}(X)=\{z\in V:z\notin X,\mbox{ and }\exists x\in X,\mbox{ such that }\overrightarrow{xz}\in A\}
,

D[X]
denote the subdigraph of
D
induced by
X
. When
X=\{v\}
,
we write
N_{D}^{-}(v)
for
N_{D}^{-}(\{v\})
, and name it the in-neighbors of
v
;
N_{D}^{+}(v)
for
N_{D}^{+}(\{v\})
, and name it the out-neighbors of
v
. Sometimes, for simplicity, we use
A[X]
to denote the arc set of
D[X]
.

For
A_{0}\subseteq A
, we define
D-A_{0}:=(V,A\setminus A_{0})
. When
A_{0}=\{a_{0}\}
, write
D-a_{0}
for
D-\{a_{0}\}
. For an arc
a_{1}\notin A
with its ends in
V
, we define
D+a_{1}:=(V,A\cup\{a_{1}\})
.
Let
\Omega
be a finite set. For a function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, define
\widetilde{f}:2^{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
as
\widetilde{f}(X)=\sum_{x\in X}f(x)
, where
X\subseteq\Omega
.

2
The branching analogue for the NDT Theorem

In this section, we prove the cases
d\leq k
of Conjecture
1.6
. Our proof relies on the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

For digraph
D=(V,A)
, suppose
\{S,T\}
is a partition of
V
,
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a non-negative function. If for any
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq T
,

\widetilde{f}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|,

(1)

then there exists a branching
B
in
D
such that

(i)

for any
v\in T
,
d_{B}^{-}(v)=1
;

(ii)

for any
v\in V
,
d_{B}^{+}(v)\leq f(v)
.

Proof.

Initialize the branching
B
with vertex set
V
and empty arc set.
If
T=\emptyset
, then
B
is the branching as demanded.
Suppose
T\neq\emptyset
.

In the proof, we use three dynamic variables
S^{*}
,
T^{*}
and
f^{*}
, their values will be updated in the process.
Initially, let
S^{*}:=S
,
T^{*}:=T
and
f^{*}:=f
.
We shall maintain the following three properties at any time of our proof:

(a)

for any
v\in T\setminus T^{*}
,
d_{B}^{-}(v)=1
;

(b)

for any
v\in S^{*}
,
d_{B}^{+}(v)+f^{*}(v)\leq f(v)
;

(c)

for any
v\in T^{*}
,
d_{B}^{+}(v)=d_{B}^{-}(v)=0
.

Note that initially Properties
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
are satisfied.
By Inequality (
1
),
\widetilde{f}(N_{D}^{-}(T))

=

\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(T^{*}))\geq|T^{*}|=|T|>0
;
since
\{S,T\}
is a partition of
V
,
N_{D}^{-}(T)\subseteq S
;
it follows that there exists
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(T^{*})\subseteq S^{*}
such that
f^{*}(s_{0})>0
.

In the proof, we shall construct the demanded branching
B
by adding ‘suitable’ arcs one by one. To find a suitable arc, we begin with an
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(T^{*})\subseteq S^{*}
such that
f^{*}(s_{0})>0
. Then we look for a ‘suitable’
t_{0}\in T^{*}
such that
s_{0}t_{0}\in A
.
Then we update
B:=B+\overrightarrow{s_{0}t_{0}}
,
S^{*}:=S^{*}+t_{0}
,
T^{*}:=T^{*}-t_{0}
and
f^{*}(s_{0}):=f(s_{0})-1
.
It is clear that
\{S^{*},T^{*}\}
is still a partition of
V
,
B
is a branching, and Properties
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
are still satisfied.
This ‘suitable’
t_{0}
needs to satisfy that after we do the update above, the following inequality still holds:

\mbox{for any }\emptyset\neq X\subseteq T^{*},~\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|.

(2)

The above process continues until
T^{*}=\emptyset
.
Then Properties
(a)
and
(b)
imply the final branching
B
is the one as demanded.

Now suppose at the induction step of the above process,
T^{*}\neq\emptyset
. Inequality (
2
) holds by induction hypothesis.
By Inequality (
2
), there exists an
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(T^{*})\subseteq S^{*}
such that
f^{*}(s_{0})>0
.

Next we show that there exists a
t_{0}\in T^{*}
such that
s_{0}t_{0}\in A
and Inequality (
2
) still holds after we update
B:=B+\overrightarrow{s_{0}t_{0}}
,
S^{*}:=S^{*}+t_{0}
,
T^{*}:=T^{*}-t_{0}
and
f^{*}(s_{0}):=f^{*}(s_{0})-1
.

Case 1.
Suppose there exists no
X\subseteq T^{*}
such that
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)
and
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))=|X|
.
Then for any
X\subseteq T^{*}
such that
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)
, by Inequality (
2
),
we have

\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))>|X|.

(3)

Since
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(T^{*})
, there exists a
t_{0}\in T^{*}
such that
s_{0}t_{0}\in A
. Update
B:=B+\overrightarrow{s_{0}t_{0}}
,
S^{*}:=S^{*}+t_{0}
,
T^{*}:=T^{*}-t_{0}
and
f^{*}(s_{0}):=f^{*}(s_{0})-1
.

We show that Inequality (
2
) still holds in the induction step.
Let
X\subseteq T^{*}
. If
s_{0}\notin N_{D}^{-}(X)
, then by the induction hypothesis of Inequality (
2
),
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|
. If
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)
, then by (
3
), in the induction step,
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))+1>|X|
, thus
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|
.

Case 2 (not Case 1).
Suppose there exists some
X_{0}\subseteq T^{*}
such that
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{0})
and
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{0}))=|X_{0}|
.
Define

\mathcal{E}=\{X\subseteq T^{*}:s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)\text{ and }\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))=|X|\}.

Next we show two claims.

Claim 2.2

For
X_{1},X_{2}\subseteq T^{*}
such that
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
,

\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}))+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{2}))\geq\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})).

(4)

Moreover, if the equality holds, then
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})
.

Proof.

For
X\subseteq V
, define
\chi_{X}:V\rightarrow\{0,1\}
as

\chi_{X}(v)=\begin{cases}1,&v\in X,\\
0,&v\notin X.\end{cases}

Then
\widetilde{f^{*}}(X)=\sum_{v\in V}f^{*}(v)\chi_{X}(v)
.
Next we show that for any
v\in V
,

\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})}(v)+\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})}(v)\geq\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})}(v)+\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(v).

(5)

If
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})}(v)=1
, that is
v\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})
, then
v\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})
or
N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
; thus
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})}(v)=1
or
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})}(v)=1
.
If
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(v)=1
, then
v\notin X_{1}\cap X_{2}
; thus
v\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})
(if
v\notin X_{1}
) or
v\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
(if
v\notin X_{2}
).
If
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})}(v)=\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(v)=1
, then
v\notin X_{1}\cup X_{2}
; thus
\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(v)=1
implies
v\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
.
These three cases prove Inequality (
5
).

It follows from (
5
) that

\begin{split}&\sum_{v\in V}f^{*}(v)\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})}(v)+\sum_{v\in V}f^{*}(v)\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})}(v)\\
\geq~~&\sum_{v\in V}f^{*}(v)\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})}(v)+\sum_{v\in V}f^{*}(v)\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(v).\end{split}

(6)

This proves
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}))+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{2}))\geq\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2}))
.

By assumption,
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
and
f^{*}(s_{0})>0
. If the equality of (
4
) (or equivalently, the equality of (
6
)) holds, it follows from (
5
) that

\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})}(s_{0})+\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})}(s_{0})=\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})}(s_{0})+\chi_{N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}(s_{0}).

Since
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{2})
, we have
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\cap N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})
.
This proves the claim.

Claim 2.3

If
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, then
X_{1}\cap X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Note that
|X_{1}|+|X_{2}|=|X_{1}\cup X_{2}|+|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|
.
Combining this with Inequality (
4
) of Claim
2.2
, we have

\begin{split}&\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}))-|X_{1}|+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{2}))-|X_{2}|\\
\geq~&\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))-|X_{1}\cup X_{2}|+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2}))-|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|.\end{split}

(7)

Since
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, we have
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{i}))=|X_{i}|
(
i=1,2
). It follows from (
7
) that

0\geq\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))-|X_{1}\cup X_{2}|+\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2}))-|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|.

(8)

By Inequality (
2
),
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))\geq|X_{1}\cup X_{2}|
and
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2}))\geq|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|
; combining Inequality (
8
),
we have
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}))=|X_{1}\cup X_{2}|
and
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2}))=|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|
.

So the equality of (
7
) holds, therefore the equality of (
4
) holds. Since
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
,
s_{0}\in N^{-}_{D}(X_{1})\cap N^{-}_{D}(X_{2})
. By Claim
2.2
, we have
s_{0}\in N^{-}_{D}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}),N_{D}^{-}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})
.
Hence,
X_{1}\cap X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
.

For
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, define
X_{1}\prec X_{2}
if and only if
X_{1}\subseteq X_{2}
. By Claim
2.3
, we can suppose
\emptyset\neq X_{0}\in\mathcal{E}
is a minimal element in
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\prec
.
We pick a
t_{0}\in X_{0}
with
s_{0}t_{0}\in A
.
Update
B:=B+\overrightarrow{s_{0}t_{0}}
,
S^{*}:=S^{*}+t_{0}
,
T^{*}:=T^{*}-t_{0}
and
f^{*}(s_{0}):=f^{*}(s_{0})-1
.

We are left to show that Inequality (
2
) still holds after these updates.

Suppose
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq T^{*}
.
If
s_{0}\notin N_{D}^{-}(X)
, then by induction hypothesis of (
2
),

\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|
.
Next, suppose
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)
, and suppose to the contrary of Inequality (
2
),
that
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))<|X|
, then
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))+1\leq|X|
.
By induction hypothesis of (
2
), we have
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))+1\geq|X|
.
Thus
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))+1=|X|
. Combining
s_{0}\in N_{D}^{-}(X)
, we have
X\in\mathcal{E}
before the updates. Applying Claim
2.3
, we have
\emptyset\neq X\cap X_{0}\in\mathcal{E}
. Notice the fact that
t_{0}\in X_{0}\setminus X
. Hence,
X\cap X_{0}\subsetneqq X_{0}
, but this contradicts the minimality of
X_{0}
.
The contradiction proves
\widetilde{f^{*}}(N_{D}^{-}(X))\geq|X|
. This proves
Case 2
, thus Proposition
2.1
.

Next we use Proposition
2.1
proving the cases
d\leq k
of Conjecture
1.6
.

Theorem 2.4

For positive integers
k
and
d
with
d\leq k
, if
D=(V,A)
is a digraph with
\gamma(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}

and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, then
D
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.

Proof.

Note that since
d\leq k
, we have
\gamma(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}\leq k
.
Suppose
S=\{v\in V:d_{D}^{-}(v)\leq k\}
,
T=\{v\in V:d_{D}^{-}(v)=k+1\}
, then
\{S,T\}
is a partition of
V
. Define
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
as
f(v)=d
for all
v\in V
.

If
T=\emptyset
, then
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k
. As
\gamma(D)\leq k
, by Theorem
1.5
,
D
decomposes into
k
branchings. So we suppose
T\neq\emptyset
.

For
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq T
, let
D_{X}
denote the subdigraph of
D
induced by
X\cup N_{D}^{-}(X)
. Then
d^{-}_{D_{X}}(v)=k+1
for each
v\in X
, and
|A(D_{X})|\geq\sum_{v\in X}d_{D_{X}}^{-}(v)=(k+1)|X|
.
By the assumption
\gamma(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}=\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}
,
we have

\frac{(k+1)|X|}{|X|+|N_{D}^{-}(X)|}\leq\frac{|A(D_{X})|}{|V(D_{X})|}<\frac{|A(D_{X})|}{|V(D_{X})|-1}\leq\gamma(D)\leq\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}.

It follows that
d|N_{D}^{-}(X)|>|X|
, that is
\widetilde{f}(N_{D}^{-}(X))>|X|
.

By Proposition
2.1
, there exists a branching
B_{k+1}
such that:
(i)
for any
v\in T
,
d_{B_{k+1}}^{-}(v)=1
;
(ii)
for any
v\in V
,
d_{B_{k+1}}^{+}(v)\leq f(v)=d
, that is
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
. Since
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
and
d_{B_{k+1}}^{-}(v)=1
for
v\in T
,
\Delta^{-}(D-A(B_{k+1}))\leq k
.
Since
\gamma(D-A(B_{k+1}))\leq\gamma(D)\leq k
, by Theorem
1.5
,
D-A(B_{k+1})
decomposes into
k
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
. This proves that
D
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
. This proves Theorem
2.4
.

3
The pseudo-branching analogue for the NDT Theorem

In this section, we prove an analogue in digraphs corresponding to Theorem
1.9
, which is the following theorem. Note that Theorem
1.11
is also a pseudo-branching analogue of Conjecture
1.6
in digraphs.

Theorem
1.11
.
For positive integers
k
and
d
, if
D
is a digraph with
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D)

\leq

k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}

and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, then
D
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k},C_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(C_{k+1})\leq d
.

Proof.

The proof is by contradiction. Suppose
D
is a counterexample of Theorem
1.11
with minimal number of arcs.
By Theorem
1.8
, we suppose
D
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k},C_{k+1}
such that
\lambda=\sum_{v\in V(D)}\max\{d^{+}_{C_{k+1}}(v)-d,0\}
is minimum. Then
\Delta^{+}(C_{k+1})>d
and
\lambda>0
. We name
\lambda
the residue of this decomposition.

Claim 3.1

For vertices
v\in V(D)
, if
d^{-}_{D}(v)>0
, then
d^{-}_{D}(v)=k+1
.

Proof.

As
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, we have
d^{-}_{D}(u)\leq k+1
for all
u\in V(D)
.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists some vertex
v\in V(D)
such that
0<d_{D}^{-}(v)\leq k
. By the minimality of
A(D)
,
D-\partial_{D}^{-}(v)
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{k},C^{\prime}_{k+1}
such that
\Delta^{+}(C^{\prime}_{k+1})\leq d
. Suppose
\partial_{D}^{-}(v)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{l}\}
, where
1\leq l\leq k
. For
1\leq i\leq l
,

C^{\prime}_{i}+a_{i}
is still a pseudo-branching. Hence,
D
decomposes into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C^{\prime}_{1}+a_{1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{l}+a_{l}
,
C^{\prime}_{l+1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{k+1}
such that
\Delta^{+}(C^{\prime}_{k+1})\leq d
, a contradiction to the assumption.

For all
v\in V(D)
, since each
C_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq k+1
) is a pseudo-branching,
d_{C_{i}}^{-}(v)\leq 1
.
Throughout the proof, we fix a vertex
v_{0}
such that
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{0})>d
.
An
alternating trail (from
v_{0}
)
is a vertex-arc sequence
v_{0}\overrightarrow{a_{1}}v_{1}\overleftarrow{a_{2}}v_{2}

\ldots

\overrightarrow{a_{2t-1}}v_{2t-1}\overleftarrow{a_{2t}}v_{2t}

such that
\overrightarrow{a_{1}}\overleftarrow{a_{2}}

\ldots

\overrightarrow{a_{2t-1}}\overleftarrow{a_{2t}}
are distinct and alternating in directions along the trail,

\overrightarrow{a_{2i-1}}=\overrightarrow{v_{2i-2}v_{2i-1}}\in A(C_{k+1})

and
\overleftarrow{a_{2i}}=\overleftarrow{v_{2i-1}v_{2i}}\in A(D)
for
1\leq i\leq t
. An alternating trail is allowed to have an odd number of arcs by omitting the last arc in the above definition.

Note the following fact:
For
1\leq i\leq t
, since
\overrightarrow{a_{2i-1}}=\overrightarrow{v_{2i-2}v_{2i-1}}\in A(C_{k+1})
and
d_{C_{k+1}}^{-}(v_{2i-1})=1
, we derive that
\overleftarrow{a_{2i}}\notin A(C_{k+1})
.

Claim 3.2

If
v_{0}\overrightarrow{a_{1}}v_{1}\overleftarrow{a_{2}}v_{2}

\ldots

\overrightarrow{a_{2t-1}}v_{2t-1}\overleftarrow{a_{2t}}v_{2t}

is an alternating trail, then
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{2i})\geq d
for
1\leq i\leq t
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, and
i_{0}
is the minimum such that
1\leq i_{0}\leq t
and
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{2i_{0}})<d
.
For
1\leq i\leq i_{0}
, we suppose
\overleftarrow{a_{2i}}\in A(C_{j_{i}})
, then
C_{j_{i}}\neq C_{k+1}
.

Do the following updates in turn:

C_{k+1}:=C_{k+1}-\overrightarrow{a_{1}}+\overleftarrow{a_{2}}
and
C_{j_{1}}:=C_{j_{1}}-\overleftarrow{a_{2}}+\overrightarrow{a_{1}}
,

\ldots
,
C_{k+1}:=C_{k+1}-\overrightarrow{a_{2i_{0}-1}}+\overleftarrow{a_{2i_{0}}}
and
C_{j_{i_{0}}}:=C_{j_{i_{0}}}-\overleftarrow{a_{2i_{0}}}+\overrightarrow{a_{2i_{0}-1}}
.
Observe that, after this series of updates,
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k},C_{k+1}
are still pseudo-branchings;
d^{+}_{C_{k+1}}(v_{0})
is decreased by
1
,
d^{+}_{C_{k+1}}(v_{2i_{0}})
is increased by
1
, and
d^{+}_{C_{k+1}}(v)
does not change for
v\neq v_{0}
or
v_{2i_{0}}
. Hence,
\sum_{v\in V(D)}\max\{d^{+}_{C_{k+1}}(v)-d,0\}
is decreased by
1
, this contradicts that originally we have picked a decomposition with the minimum residue
\lambda
.

Next we define

V(v_{0}):=\cup V(Q),\mbox{ and }A(v_{0}):=\cup A(Q),\mbox{ where }{{Q}\mbox{ is an alternating trail}\mbox{ from }v_{0}}.

Let
D(v_{0})=(V(v_{0}),A(v_{0}))
. The following observation comes from the definitions.

Observation 3.3

(i)

\partial_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{0})\subseteq A(v_{0})
;

(ii)

For
v\in V(v_{0})
, if
d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)>0
, then
\partial_{D}^{-}(v)\subseteq A(v_{0})
;

(iii)

For
v\in V(v_{0})
, if
d_{D(v_{0})}^{+}(v)>0
, then
\partial_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v)\subseteq A(v_{0})
.

Let
Z_{1}=\{v\in V(v_{0}):d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)>0\}
,
Z_{2}=V(v_{0})\setminus Z_{1}
.
For
v\in Z_{1}
, since
d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)>0
, by Observation
3.3

(ii)
, we have
\partial_{D}^{-}(v)\subseteq A(v_{0})
; applying Claim
3.1
, we have
d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)=d_{D}^{-}(v)=k+1
;
thus
|A(v_{0})|=\sum_{v\in Z_{1}}d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)=(k+1)|Z_{1}|
.

For
v\in Z_{2}
, since
d_{D(v_{0})}^{-}(v)=0
, we have
d_{D(v_{0})}^{+}(v)>0
.
By Observation
3.3

(iii)
,
\partial_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v)\subseteq A(v_{0})
.
Since each
C_{k+1}
is a pseudo-branching, for any vertex
u
,
d_{C_{k+1}}^{-}(u)\leq 1
; thus, in
D(v_{0})
, each vertex in
Z_{2}
has at least
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v)

distinct out-neighbors in
Z_{1}
, thus

|Z_{1}|\geq\sum_{v\in Z_{2}}d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v)
.
Applying Claim
3.2
, we have
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v)\geq d
.

By Observation
3.3

(i)
,
\partial_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{0})\subseteq A(v_{0})
. Recall that
d_{C_{k+1}}^{+}(v_{0})>d
. This proves
|Z_{1}|>d|Z_{2}|
(no matter if
v_{0}\in Z_{1}
or
v_{0}\in Z_{2}
).
It follows that

\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D(v_{0}))\geq\frac{|A(v_{0})|}{|V(v_{0})|}=\frac{(k+1)|Z_{1}|}{|Z_{1}|+|Z_{2}|}>\frac{(k+1)|Z_{1}|}{|Z_{1}|+\frac{1}{d}|Z_{1}|}=k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}.

This contradicts the assumption that
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D)\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}
, and finishes the proof.

4
Sharpness of the bounds

In this section, by constructing a series of acyclic bipartite digraphs, we prove Theorem
1.7
, which says the bound of
\gamma(D)
given in Conjecture
1.6
is best possible. The same digraphs will be used to show the bound of
{\rm mad}(D)
in Theorem
1.11

is best possible, this shall be explained at the end of this section.

Suppose
G=(V,E)
is a graph and
D
a digraph on the same vertex set
V
. For
X,Y\subseteq V
,
denote by
[X,Y]_{G}
the number of edges
uv\in E(G)
with
u\in X
and
v\in Y
, by
[X,Y]_{D}
the number of arcs
\overrightarrow{uv}\in A(D)
with
u\in X
and
v\in Y
.

Theorem
1.7
.
For positive integers
k,d
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an acyclic bipartite digraph
D
such that

\gamma(D)<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon

and
\Delta^{-}(D)\leq k+1
, but
D
can not decompose into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.

Proof.

For positive integers
k,d
and any
\epsilon>0
, since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limits{\frac{d(k+1)n}{(d+1)n-1}}=\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
, there is an integer
n
such that
\frac{d(k+1)n}{(d+1)n-1}<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
and
n\geq k+1
.
Define

U=\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{n-1}\}
;
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.
For convenience,
for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
(or index
t
in
u_{t}
), since
(i+tn)\mod{n}=i
, we regard
i+tn
as
i
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, let
W_{i}
be a set of
d
vertices, such that, for distinct
i,j
, the sets
W_{i}
and
W_{j}
are disjoint from each other.
Define
D_{0}
as a bipartite digraph with vertex bipartition
U
and
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}W_{i}
, and

\begin{split}A(D_{0})&=\{\overrightarrow{u_{i+j}w}:u_{i+j}\in U,w\in W_{i},\mbox{ and }0\leq j\leq k\}\\
&=\{\overrightarrow{u_{i}w}:u_{i}\in U,w\in W_{i-j},\mbox{ and }0\leq j\leq k\}.\end{split}

By taking two disjoint copies
D_{1},D_{2}
of
D_{0}
and identifying two copies of
u_{0}
into a single vertex
u_{0}^{*}
, we obtain a digraph
D
. We shall show that
D
satisfies the theorem.
It is clear that
\Delta^{-}(D)=k+1
,
D
is acyclic and bipartite.

Claim 4.1

Suppose
D_{0}
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{k}
,
B^{\prime}_{k+1}
such that
\Delta^{+}(B^{\prime}_{k+1})

\leq

d
, then
d_{B^{\prime}_{k+1}}^{+}(u)=d
for all
u\in U
.

Proof.

For
w\in\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}W_{i}
,
since
k+1=d^{-}_{D_{0}}(w)=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}d_{B^{\prime}_{j}}^{-}(w)\leq k+1

(the last inequality comes from the fact that
B^{\prime}_{j}
is a branching, thus
d_{B^{\prime}_{j}}^{-}(w)\leq 1
), we deduce that

d_{B^{\prime}_{j}}^{-}(w)=1
(for
1\leq j\leq k+1
).
So

\begin{split}dn=|\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}W_{i}|=\sum_{w\in\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}W_{i}}d_{B^{\prime}_{k+1}}^{-}(w)&=[U,\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}W_{i}]_{B^{\prime}_{k+1}}\\
&=\sum_{u\in U}d_{B^{\prime}_{k+1}}^{+}(u)\leq\Delta^{+}(B^{\prime}_{k+1})n\leq dn.\end{split}

Therefore
d_{B^{\prime}_{k+1}}^{+}(u)=d
for all
u\in U
.

Assume that
D
decomposes into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.
Then
D_{l}
(
l=1
,
2
) decomposes into branchings
B_{1}[V(D_{l})],\ldots,B_{k+1}[V(D_{l})]
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1}[V(D_{l})])\leq d
.
By Claim
4.1
,
d_{B_{k+1}[V(D_{l})]}^{+}(u_{0}^{*})=d
. Thus
d_{B_{k+1}}^{+}(u_{0}^{*})=2d
, this contradicts that
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.
This proves that
D
can not decompose into
k+1
branchings
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k},B_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(B_{k+1})\leq d
.

In the rest of the proof, it suffices for us to show that
\gamma(D)<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
.
Note that

\frac{|A(D)|}{|V(D)|-1}=\frac{2dn(k+1)}{(2(dn+n)-1)-1}=\frac{d(k+1)n}{(d+1)n-1}=\frac{|A(D_{0})|}{|V(D_{0})|-1}<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon.

(9)

By the construction and symmetry between
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
of
D
, we have
\gamma(D_{0})=\gamma(D)
. To see this, let
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq V(D)
. If
X\subseteq V(D_{1})
or
V(D_{2})
, then by definition,
|A[X]|\leq\gamma(D_{0})(|X|-1)
. Otherwise,
|A[X]|=|A[X_{1}]|+|A[X_{2}]|
and
|X_{1}|+|X_{2}|\leq|X|+1
, where
X_{l}=X\cap V(D_{l})
for
l=1,2
. Then we have
|A[X]|=|A[X_{1}]|+|A[X_{2}]|\leq\gamma(D_{0})(|X_{1}|-1)+\gamma(D_{0})(|X_{2}|-1)\leq\gamma(D_{0})(|X|-1)
. Hence, if
|X|>1
, then
\frac{|A[X]|}{|X|-1}\leq\gamma(D_{0})
; thus
\gamma(D)\leq\gamma(D_{0})
. Since
D_{0}
is isomorphic to a subdigraph of
D
, we have
\gamma(D_{0})\leq\gamma(D)
. So
\gamma(D_{0})=\gamma(D)
.

Suppose
V^{*}\subseteq V(D_{0})
,
D_{0}[V^{*}]
witnesses
\gamma(D_{0})
, and subject to this,
V^{*}
is maximal.

In Claim
4.3
we shall prove
that if
V^{*}\subsetneqq V(D_{0})
, then
\frac{|A[V^{*}]|}{|V^{*}|-1}\leq k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}
.
Since

\frac{|A(D_{0})|}{|V(D_{0})|-1}=\frac{d(k+1)n}{(d+1)n-1}>\frac{d(k+1)n}{(d+1)n}=k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}
, we have
V^{*}=V(D_{0})
.
Then by Inequality (
9
), this will prove
\gamma(D)<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
.

Claim 4.2

For
i_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, if
V^{*}\cap W_{i_{0}}\neq\emptyset
, then
W_{i_{0}}\subseteq V^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
D_{0}[V^{*}]
witnesses
\gamma(D_{0})
,
\frac{|A[V^{*}]|}{|V^{*}|-1}=\gamma(D_{0})
. Then for
w\in V^{*}\cap W_{i_{0}}
, we have

\frac{\gamma(D_{0})(|V^{*}|-1)-d_{D_{0}}^{-}(w)}{(|V^{*}|-1)-1}=\frac{|A[V^{*}]|-d_{D_{0}}^{-}(w)}{(|V^{*}|-1)-1}=\frac{|A[V^{*}-w]|}{|V^{*}-w|-1}\leq\gamma(D_{0}).

This gives us
d_{D_{0}}^{-}(w)\geq\gamma(D_{0})
.

Assume there exists
w^{\prime}\in W_{i_{0}}\setminus V^{*}
. Add
w^{\prime}
to
D_{0}[V^{*}]
. Note that
d_{D_{0}[V^{*}+w^{\prime}]}^{-}(w^{\prime})=d_{D_{0}}^{-}(w)\geq\gamma(D_{0})
. Then

\gamma(D_{0}[V^{*}+w^{\prime}])\geq\frac{|A[V^{*}+w^{\prime}]|}{|V^{*}+w^{\prime}|-1}=\frac{|A[V^{*}]|+d_{D_{0}[V^{*}+w^{\prime}]}^{-}(w^{\prime})}{(|V^{*}|-1)+1}\geq\gamma(D_{0}).

Therefore
D_{0}[V^{*}+w^{\prime}]
also witnesses
\gamma(D_{0})
, this contradicts the maximality of
V^{*}
.

By Claim
4.2
, suppose
V^{*}=U^{*}\cup(\cup_{i\in I}W_{i})
, where
U^{*}\subseteq U
,
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Let
D^{*}=D_{0}[V^{*}]
, then
D^{*}
is a bipartite graph with vertex bipartition
U^{*}
and
\cup_{i\in I}W_{i}
,
and
|V(D^{*})|=|U^{*}|+|\cup_{i\in I}W_{i}|=|U^{*}|+d|I|
.

Claim 4.3

If
V^{*}\subsetneqq V(D_{0})
, then

\frac{|A(D^{*})|}{|V(D^{*})|-1}=\frac{|A[V^{*}]|}{|V^{*}|-1}\leq\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}=k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}
.

Proof.

If
|U^{*}|>|I|
, then
|V(D^{*})|\geq|I|+1+d|I|=(d+1)|I|+1
.
For any
w\in\cup_{i\in I}W_{i}
, we have
d_{D^{*}}^{-}(w)\leq d_{D_{0}}^{-}(w)\leq k+1
. Thus

\frac{|A(D^{*})|}{|V(D^{*})|-1}=\frac{\sum_{w\in\cup_{i\in I}W_{i}}d_{D^{*}}^{-}(w)}{|V(D^{*})|-1}\leq\frac{d|I|(k+1)}{(d+1)|I|}=\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}.

If
|U^{*}|<|I|
, then
|V(D^{*})|\geq|U^{*}|+d(|U^{*}|+1)\geq(d+1)|U^{*}|+1
. For any
u\in U^{*}
, we have
d_{D^{*}}^{+}(u)\leq d_{D_{0}}^{+}(u)\leq d(k+1)
. Thus

\frac{|A(D^{*})|}{|V(D^{*})|-1}=\frac{\sum_{u\in U^{*}}d_{D^{*}}^{+}(u)}{|V(D^{*})|-1}\leq\frac{d(k+1)|U^{*}|}{(d+1)|U^{*}|}=\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}.

In the rest of proofs, we suppose
|U^{*}|=|I|<n
, then
|V(D^{*})|=(d+1)|I|
.
Suppose
G_{0}
is a bipartite graph with vertex bipartition
U
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
E(G_{0})=\{iu_{i+j}:i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mbox{ and }0\leq j\leq k\}
. Then
G_{0}
is
(k+1)
-regular.

Claim 4.4

Suppose
U^{*}\subsetneqq U
,
I\subsetneqq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and
|U^{*}|=|I|
. Then
[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose
E^{*}
is the set of edges between
U^{*}
and
I
in
G_{0}
. For
0\leq j\leq k
, let
M_{j}:=\{u_{i+j}i\in E(G_{0}):i\in I\}
, then
M_{j}
is a matching of
G_{0}
, and the set of edges between
U
and
I
in
G_{0}
are the disjoint union of
M_{0},\ldots,M_{k}
.
We prove the claim by contradiction. Assume that
[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}<\frac{k+1}{2}
. Then less than
\frac{k+1}{2}
of
\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k}
contain edges between
U\setminus U^{*}
and
I
. So more than
\frac{k+1}{2}
of
\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k}
are contained in
E^{*}
.

Suppose there exists
0\leq j_{0}\leq k
such that
M_{j_{0}},M_{j_{0}+1}\subseteq E^{*}
.
Since
|U^{*}|=|I|
,
M_{j_{0}}\subseteq E^{*}
implies
U^{*}=\{u_{i}:i\in I+j_{0}\}
, and
M_{j_{0}+1}\subseteq E^{*}
implies
U^{*}=\{u_{i}:i\in I+j_{0}+1\}
. Hence,
I+j_{0}=I+j_{0}+1
. Thus
I=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, but this contradicts
I\subsetneqq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Therefore we can suppose there exists no
j_{0}
(
0\leq j_{0}\leq k
) such that
M_{j_{0}},M_{j_{0}+1}\subseteq E^{*}
.
Since more than
\frac{k+1}{2}
of
\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{k}
are contained in
E^{*}
, if
k
is odd, then there exists some
0\leq j_{0}\leq k
such that
M_{j_{0}},M_{j_{0}+1}\subseteq E^{*}
, this is a contradiction.

For the last case, we have
k
is even, and exactly
\{M_{2i}:0\leq i\leq\frac{k}{2}\}
are contained in
E^{*}
.
Since
|U^{*}|=|I|
,
M_{0}\subseteq E^{*}
implies
U^{*}=\{u_{i}:i\in I\}
, and
M_{2}\subseteq E^{*}
implies
U^{*}=\{u_{i}:i\in I+2\}
. Hence,
I=I+2
. Thus
n
is even,
I=2\mathbb{Z}_{n}
or
I=2\mathbb{Z}_{n}+1
, and
U^{*}=\{u_{i}:i\in I\}
. Then
M_{1}
is contained in the set of edges between
U\setminus U^{*}
and
I
, thus
[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}\geq|M_{1}|=\frac{n}{2}\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
, but this contradicts the assumption
[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}<\frac{k+1}{2}
.

Since
[U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}+[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}=[U,I]_{G_{0}}=(k+1)|I|
, Claim
4.4
implies
[U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}=(k+1)|I|-[U\setminus U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}\leq(k+1)|I|-\frac{k+1}{2}
.

To count
A(D^{*})
, note that for any
u\in U^{*}
and
i\in I
, each edge
ui
in
G_{0}
corresponds to
d
arcs from
u
to
W_{i}
in
D^{*}
. Hence,
|A(D^{*})|=[U^{*},\cup_{i\in I}W_{i}]_{D}=d[U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}
. Then

\frac{|A(D^{*})|}{|V(D^{*})|-1}=\frac{d[U^{*},I]_{G_{0}}}{(d+1)|I|-1}\leq\frac{d(k+1)|I|-\frac{d(k+1)}{2}}{(d+1)|I|-1}\leq\frac{d(k+1)|I|-\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}}{(d+1)|I|-1}=\frac{d(k+1)}{d+1}.

This proves Claim
4.3
.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem
1.7
shows the bound of Theorem
2.4
is sharp. For positive integers
k,d
and
\epsilon>0
, let
D
be the digraph as stated in Theorem
1.7
. Then
\frac{1}{2}{\rm mad}(D)\leq\gamma(D)<k+\frac{d-k}{d+1}+\epsilon
.
Since
D
is acyclic, a subdigraph of
D
is a branching if and only if it is a pseudo-branching. Hence,
D
can not decompose into
k+1
pseudo-branchings
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k},C_{k+1}
with
\Delta^{+}(C_{k+1})\leq d
. This shows the bound of Theorem
1.11
is sharp.

Spectra of Compact Quotients of the Oscillator Group
Mathias FISCHER and Ines KATH

Abstract
This paper is a contribution to harmonic analysis of compact solvmanifolds. We consider the four-dimensional oscillator group Osc 1 {\rm Osc}_{1} , which is a semi-direct product of the three-dimensional Heisenberg group and the real line. We classify the lattices of Osc 1 {\rm Osc}_{1} up to inner automorphisms of Osc 1 {\rm Osc}_{1} . For every lattice L L in Osc 1 {\rm Osc}_{1} , we compute the decomposition of the right regular representation of Osc 1 {\rm Osc}_{1} on L 2 ​ ( L \ Osc 1 ) L^{2}(L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1}) into irreducible unitary representations. This decomposition allows the explicit computation of the spectrum of the wave operator on the compact locally-symmetric Lorentzian manifold L \ Osc 1 L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1} .

Introduction

Let
G
be a Lie group and
L
be a cocompact discrete subgroup in
G
. Then we can consider the right regular representation of
G
on
L^{2}(L\backslash G)
, which is unitary. This representation can be decomposed into irreducible unitary representations. It is a classical result that this decomposition is a discrete sum and each summand appears with finite multiplicity. We will call the set of irreducible representations that appear in this decomposition together with their multiplicities the
spectrum
of
L\backslash G
. It allows to compute the spectrum of differential operators on
L\backslash G
that come from bi-invariant operators on
G
.

One of the basic questions in harmonic analysis is to determine the spectrum of
L\backslash G
. For semisimple
G
, this question is very deep and has been studied for decades. For arithmetic
L
, it is related to the Langlands program. In any case, only for very few irreducible representations, their multiplicities can be explicitly determined. For nilpotent
G
, the situation is quite different. Here explicit computations are possible and there are closed formulas for the multiplicity of every irreducible subrepresentation
[
CG
,
H71
,
R71
]
. Compared to nilmanifolds, solvmanifolds are less systematically studied, even in low dimensions. The first comprehensive texts on the subject were a book by Brezin
[
Br
]
and a long article by Howe
[
Ho77
]
, both published in the 70s. To date, there has been surprisingly little further research except for exponential solvable Lie groups
[
FL
]
. We think that solvable Lie groups deserve much more interest. They play an important role in pseudo-Riemannian geometry. There are large families of solvable Lie groups that admit a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric. In particular, quotients of these groups by discrete subgroups are locally-symmetric spaces.

Indeed, our motivation to write this paper comes from Lorentzian geometry. We are interested in the spectrum of the wave operator on certain compact Lorentzian locally-symmetric spaces. These spaces arise as quotients of an oscillator group by a discrete subgroup. Oscillator groups are solvable and admit a bi-invariant Lorentzian metric. The wave operator can be considered as minus the quadratic Casimir operator, which is bi-invariant on the group. Hence its spectrum can be easily computed if the decomposition of the right regular representation is known, see Section
. We will see that there exist lattices such that the spectrum is discrete as well as lattices for which the quotient has a non-discrete spectrum.

For solvable Lie groups, we prefer to speak of lattices instead of discrete cocompact subgroups. By definition, a lattice in a Lie group
G
is a discrete subgroup
L
with the property that the quotient space
L\backslash G
has finite invariant measure. Lattices in a solvable Lie group are uniform, that is, they are the same as discrete cocompact subgroups.

The above mentioned book by Brezin contains an informal discussion of ideas how to attack the problem of decomposing the regular representation in the solvable case. There are also some explicit calculations for a series of four-dimensional examples. These examples are semi-direct products of the Heisenberg group
H
by the real line in which selected examples of lattices are considered. Inexplicably, the book has only very few citations and Brezin’s ideas were not picked up and developed except in
[
Hu
]
. Here we will continue the study of the mentioned four-dimensional groups. We will concentrate on the case where
ℝ
acts on
H/Z(H)
by rotations, which gives exactly the four-dimensional oscillator group. We will treat this example more systematically than it was done in
[
Br
]
. In particular, we consider quotients of this group by arbitrary lattices and not only by “suitable” ones as done in
[
Br
]
, see Remark
for more information. We will use a somewhat different approach than proposed by Brezin and are able to completely determine the spectrum for all compact quotients.

Let us introduce the four-dimensional oscillator group and its Lie algebra in more detail. The four-dimensional oscillator algebra
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}
is a Lie algebra spanned by a basis
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
T
, where
Z
spans the centre and the remaining basis elements satisfy the relations
[X,Y]=Z
,
[T,X]=Y
,
[T,Y]=-X
. This Lie algebra is strongly related to the one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator. Indeed, the Lie algebra spanned by the differential operators
P:={\rm d}/{\rm d}x
,
Q:=x
,
H=\big(P^{2}+Q^{2}\big)/2
and the identity
I
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}
. In differential geometry, the oscillator algebra
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}
is also called warped Heisenberg algebra. It appears in the classification of (connected) isometry groups of compact Lorentzian manifolds as the Lie algebra of one of such groups
[
Z98
]
.

The oscillator group is the simply-connected Lie group
{\rm Osc}_{1}
that is associated with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}
. It is a semi-direct product of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group
H
by the real line
ℝ
, where
ℝ
acts by rotation on the quotient of
H
by its centre. In particular, it is solvable. As mentioned above, it admits a bi-invariant Lorentzian metric. Moreover, this group contains lattices. So, each lattice
L
in
{\rm Osc}_{1}
gives rise to a compact Lorentzian manifold
L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1}
.

The group
{\rm Osc}_{1}
is not exponential. However, it is of type I. Hence, irreducible unitary representations can be determined by the orbit method, which was originally developed by Kirillov for nilpotent Lie groups and generalised to type I solvable Lie groups by Auslander and Kostant. The polynomials
z
and
x^{2}+y^{2}+2zt
(in the obvious notation) generate the algebra of invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{*}
. The level sets
\big\{z=c,\,x^{2}+y^{2}+2zt=cd\big\}\cong{\mbox{\Bb R}}^{2}
with fixed
c\not=0
and
d\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
are the generic coadjoint orbits. The set
\{z=0\}
is stratified into two-dimensional cylindrical orbits
\big\{x^{2}+y^{2}=a,\,z=0\big\}
,
a>0
, and orbits consisting of one point
\{x=y=z=0,\,t=d\}
. The latter orbits correspond to one-dimensional representations
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
. Each cylindric
orbit
corresponds to a set
{\mathcal{S}}_{a}^{\tau}
,
\tau\in S^{1}
, of infinite-dimensional representations and each generic orbit corresponds to a an infinite-dimensional representation
{\mathcal{F}}_{c,d}
, cf.
[
Ki04
]
.

The study of lattices in oscillator groups was initiated by Medina and Revoy
[
MR
]
. Note, however, that the classification results in
[
MR
]
are not correct due to a wrong description of the automorphism group of an oscillator group. Lattices of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
(as subgroups) were classified up to automorphisms of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
by the first author
[
F
]
. Since outer automorphisms can change the spectrum of the quotient, here we need a classification only up to inner automorphisms of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. We derive such a classification in Section
4
. First we classify the abstract groups (up to isomorphism) that appear as lattices of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. We will call these groups
discrete oscillator groups
. These groups are semi-direct products of a discrete Heisenberg group by
ℤ
. A discrete Heisenberg group is generated by a central element
\gamma
and elements
\alpha,\beta
satisfying the relation
[\alpha,\beta]=\gamma^{r}
for some
r\in{\mbox{\Bb N}}_{>0}
. It is characterised by
r
and will be denoted by
H_{1}^{r}({\mbox{\Bb Z}})
. If
L=H_{1}^{r}({\mbox{\Bb Z}})\rtimes{\mbox{\Bb Z}}
is a discrete oscillator group and if
\delta
is a generator of the
ℤ
-factor, then the action of
\delta
on the quotient of
H_{1}^{r}({\mbox{\Bb Z}})
by its centre has order
q\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
. For
q\not=1
,
L
is almost determined by
r
and
q
: for fixed
r
,
q
, there are only one or two non-isomorphic discrete oscillator groups. If
q=1
, then the situation is slightly more complicated, see Section
4.1
. Each discrete oscillator group has different realisations as a lattice in
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. More exactly, given a discrete oscillator group
L
we consider the set
{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
of lattices of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
isomorphic to
L
, where we identify subgroups that differ only by an inner automorphisms of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. We want to parametrise
{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
. First we observe, that every lattice arises from a normalised and unshifted one by outer automorphisms of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
that can be viewed as rescaling and shifting. Here, “normalised” means that the covolume of the lattice obtained by projection of
L\cap H
to
H/Z(H)\cong{\mbox{\Bb R}}^{2}
equals one. The explanation of the notion “unshifted” is more involved, see Section
4.2
. Basically, it means that the lattice is adapted to the fixed splitting of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
into the normal subgroup
H
and a complement
ℝ
. Hence, we get a description of
{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
by the subset
{\mathcal{M}}_{L,0}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{L}
of normalised unshifted lattices modulo inner automorphisms and by two continuous parameters corresponding to rescaling and shifting. For
q\in\{3,4,6\}
, the set
{\mathcal{M}}_{L,0}
is discrete and can be parametrised by the generator
\lambda\in 2\pi/q+2\pi{\mbox{\Bb Z}}
of the projection of
L
to the
ℝ
-factor of
{\rm Osc}_{1}=H\rtimes{\mbox{\Bb R}}
. For
q\in\{1,2\}
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{L,0}
is parametrised by a continuous parameter
(\mu,\nu)
in a fundamental domain
{\mathcal{F}}
of the
\mathop{{\rm SL}}(2,{\mbox{\Bb R}})
-action on the upper halfplane and some further discrete parameters besides
\lambda
. The final classification result for lattices in
{\rm Osc}_{1}
up to inner automorphisms is formulated in Theorems
4.12
and
4.15
. In particular, we determine a set of representatives of unshifted normalised lattices, which we will call
standard lattices
.

For the computation of the spectrum of a quotient
L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1}
, we will concentrate on normalised and unshifted lattices. The spectrum for arbitrary lattices can easily be derived from these. The first step is to consider only lattices that are generated by a lattice in the Heisenberg group
H
and an element of the centre of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. Such lattices will be called
straight
. For a straight lattice
L
, we derive a Fourier decomposition for functions in
L^{2}(L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
. For the calculations, we use another model of the oscillator group, i.e., a group
G
that is isomorphic to
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. Lattices in
G
will usually be denoted by
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma\subset G
corresponds to a straight lattice in
{\rm Osc}_{1}
under this isomorphism, then a (continuous) function in
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
is periodic in three of the four variables and it is not hard to describe its Fourier decomposition explicitly. From this decomposition, we obtain the spectrum. Having solved the problem for straight lattices, we can turn to standard lattices. We show that each standard lattice
L
is generated by a straight lattice
L^{\prime}\subset L
and an additional element
l\in L
. We can identify
L^{2}(L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
with the space of functions in
L^{2}(L^{\prime}\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
that are invariant under
l
. The isotypic components of
L^{2}(L^{\prime}\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
are invariant by
l
. Hence, it remains to determine the invariants of the action of
l
on each of the isotypic components. This is done in Section
8
, where in practice we again use
G
instead of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
and pass from the lattice
L\subset{\rm Osc}_{1}
to the corresponding lattice
\Gamma\subset G
.
Since, up to an inner automorphism of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
, every normalised and unshifted lattice
L
is equal to a standard lattice, we obtain the spectrum of
L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1}
from our computations for the standard lattices. The representation
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
appears in
L^{2}(L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
for
d=n/\lambda
,
n\in{\mbox{\Bb Z}}
. The discrete parameter
\tau
for which the representation
{\mathcal{S}}_{a}^{\tau}
appears depends only on
\lambda
if
q\in\{2,3,4,6\}
. For
q=1
, it depends also on the above mentioned additional discrete parameters of
L
. The parameter
a
depends on
\lambda
and, for
q\in\{1,2\}
, in addition on
(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Finally, the representation
{\mathcal{F}}_{c,d}
appears for
(c,d)=\big(rm,n/(q\lambda)\big)
,
m\in{\mbox{\Bb Z}}_{\not=0}
,
n\in{\mbox{\Bb Z}}
, if
q\in\{2,3,4,6\}
. For
q=1
, the parameter
d
of the representation depends besides on
\lambda
on one of the additional discrete parameters of
L
. For each irreducible unitary representation of type
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
or
{\mathcal{F}}_{c,d}
, we compute the multiplicity with which it appears in
L^{2}(L\backslash{\rm Osc}_{1})
. Furthermore, for each summand of type
{\mathcal{S}}_{a}^{\tau}
we describe
a
and
\tau
explicitly in terms of the parameters of the lattice. However, the exact determination of the multiplicity of
{\mathcal{S}}_{a}^{\tau}
for a
given
real number
a\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
remains a number theoretic problem, see Remark
.

Finally, given an arbitrary lattice
L^{\prime}
, then we can determine an (outer) automorphism
F\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\rm Osc}_{1})
that transforms
L^{\prime}
into a normalised and unshifted lattice
L
. This allows the computation of the spectrum of
L^{\prime}
from that of
L
since we can control the action of
F
on the irreducible representations of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. We summarise the results in Section
at the end of the paper.

2
The four-dimensional oscillator group

The 4-dimensional oscillator group is a semi-direct product of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group
H
by the real line. Usually, the Heisenberg group
H
is defined as the set
H={\mbox{\Bb C}}\times{\mbox{\Bb R}}
with multiplication given by

\displaystyle(\xi_{1},z_{1})\cdot(\xi_{2},z_{2})=\bigg(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},z_{1}+z_{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\bigg),

where
\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\mathop{{\rm Im}}\big(\overline{\xi_{1}}\xi_{2}\big)
. Hence in explicit terms, the oscillator group is understood as the set
{\rm Osc}_{1}=H\times{\mbox{\Bb R}}
with multiplication defined by

\displaystyle(\xi_{1},z_{1},t_{1})\cdot(\xi_{2},z_{2},t_{2})=\bigg(\xi_{1}+{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t_{1}}\xi_{2},z_{1}+z_{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}\omega\big(\xi_{1},{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t_{1}}\xi_{2}\big),t_{1}+t_{2}\bigg),

where
\omega(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\mathop{{\rm Im}}\big(\overline{\xi_{1}}\xi_{2}\big)
.

In the following we want to describe the automorphisms of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
, see
[
F
]
for a proof.
For
\eta\in{\mbox{\Bb C}}
, let
F_{\eta}\colon{\rm Osc}_{1}\rightarrow{\rm Osc}_{1}
be the conjugation by
(\eta,0,0)
. Furthermore, we define an automorphism
F_{u}
of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
for
u\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
by

\displaystyle F_{u}\colon\ (\xi,z,t)\longmapsto(\xi,z+ut,t).

(2.1)

Finally, consider an
ℝ
-linear isomorphism
S
of
ℂ
such that
S({\rm i}\xi)=\mu{\rm i}S(\xi)
for an element
\mu\in\{1,-1\}
and for all
\xi\in{\mbox{\Bb C}}
. Then

\mu=\mathop{{\rm sgn}}(\det S)
and also

\displaystyle F_{S}\colon\ (\xi,z,t)\longmapsto(S\xi,\det(S)z,\mu t)

(2.2)

is an automorphism of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
. Each automorphism
F
of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
is of the form

\displaystyle F=F_{u}\circ F_{\eta}\circ F_{S}

for suitable
u\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
,
\eta\in{\mbox{\Bb C}}
and
S\in\mathop{{\rm GL}}(2,{\mbox{\Bb R}})
as considered above. Besides
F_{\eta}
also
F_{S}
is an inner automorphism if
S\in\mathop{{\rm SO}}(2,{\mbox{\Bb R}})
.

3
Irreducible unitary representations of
\boldsymbol{{\rm Osc}_{1}}

The irreducible unitary representations of the oscillator group were determined for the first time by Streater
[
St67
]
. Actually, in
[
St67
]
, not the simply-connected group
{\rm Osc}_{1}
was studied but a quotient of this group which is a semidirect product of the Heisenberg group and the circle and can be considered as a matrix group.

The group
{\rm Osc}_{1}
is not exponential, i.e., the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\to{\rm Osc}_{1}
is not a diffeomorphism. More exactly,
\exp
is not surjective. However,
{\rm Osc}_{1}

has the following weaker property which ensures that one can describe its irreducible unitary representations.
A locally compact group
G
is said to be of type I if every factor representation of
G
is isotypic. It is known that the universal cover of any connected solvable algebraic group is of type I, see
[
Pu69
, p. 440]
. In particular,
{\rm Osc}_{1}
is of type I. The irreducible unitary representations of solvable Lie groups of type I can be determined by applying a generalised version of Kirillov’s orbit method, see
[
AK71
,
Ki99
,
Ki04
]
for a description of this method. An explicit computation of the irreducible unitary representations of
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}
can be found in
[
Ki04
, Section 4.3]
, where the oscillator Lie algebra is called diamond Lie algebra.
The difference between the representations of the non-simply-connected group considered in
[
St67
]
and the representations of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
lies in an additional parameter
\tau\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}/{\mbox{\Bb Z}}
for the irreducible representations corresponding to cylindrical orbits, see
(ii)
below.

Let us recall the results of
[
Ki04
, Section 4.3]
. Note that the case
c<0
in item
(iii)
below is not discussed in
[
Ki04
]
. We consider coadjoint orbits of
{\rm Osc}_{1}
in
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{*}={\mbox{\Bb C}}\oplus{\mbox{\Bb R}}\oplus{\mbox{\Bb R}}
, which are represented by

(i)

(0,0,d)\in\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{*}
,
d\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
,

(ii)

(a,0,0)
,
a\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
,
a>0
,

(iii)

(0,c,d)
,
c,d\in{\mbox{\Bb R}}
,
c\not=0
.

The orbit of
(0,0,d)
is a point, the orbit of
(a,0,0)
is diffeomorphic to
S^{1}\times{\mbox{\Bb R}}
, and the orbit of
(0,c,d)
for

is diffeomorphic to
{\mbox{\Bb R}}^{2}
. We will describe the representations corresponding to
(ii)
and
(iii)
only on the Lie algebra level. Let
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
T
be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{c}_{1}={\mbox{\Bb C}}\oplus{\mbox{\Bb R}}\oplus{\mbox{\Bb R}}
,
The non-vanishing brackets of basis elements are

,

and

.

(i)

{\mathcal{C}}_{d}:=\big(\sigma_{(0,0,d)},{\mbox{\Bb C}}\big)
,
\sigma_{(0,0,d)}(\xi,z,t)={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}dt}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}_{a}^{\tau}:=\big(\sigma:=\sigma_{(a,0,0)}^{\tau},L^{2}\big(S^{1}\big)\big)
,

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}(Z)(\varphi)=0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}(X-{\rm i}Y)(\varphi)=2\pi{\rm i}a{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\varphi,

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}(T)(\varphi)=\varphi^{\prime}+{\rm i}\tau\varphi

for

\displaystyle\sigma_{*}(T)(\phi_{n})={\rm i}(n+\tau)\,\phi_{n}.

(iii)

For

, we consider the Hilbert space

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{c}({\mbox{\Bb C}}):=\bigg\{\varphi\colon{\mbox{\Bb C}}\to{\mbox{\Bb C}}\text{ holomorphic }\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(\xi)|^{2}{\rm e}^{-\pi c|\xi|^{2}}c\,{\rm d}\xi<\infty\bigg\}

4

\boldsymbol{{\rm Osc}_{1}}

Oscillator groups can be defined in every even dimension as a generalisation of the classical four-dimensional one. The study of lattices in such groups was initiated by Medina and Revoy
[

{\rm Osc}_{1}
is not correct, see Remark

{\rm Osc}_{1}
were classified up to automorphisms of

4.1

Consider the discrete Heisenberg group

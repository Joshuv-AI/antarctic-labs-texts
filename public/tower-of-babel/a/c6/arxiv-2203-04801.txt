Extensions of valuations to rational function fields over completions
Arpan Dutta

Abstract
. Given a valued field ( K , v ) (K,v) and its completion ( K ^ , v ) (\widehat{K},v) , we study the set of all possible extensions of v v to K ^ ​ ( X ) \widehat{K}(X) . We show that any such extension is closely connected with the underlying subextension ( K ⁡ ( X ) | K , v ) (K(X)|K,v) . The connections between these extensions are studied via minimal pairs, key polynomials, pseudo-Cauchy sequences and implicit constant fields. As a consequence, we obtain strong ramification theoretic properties of ( K ^ , v ) (\widehat{K},v) . We also give necessary and sufficient conditions for ( K ⁡ ( X ) , v ) (K(X),v) to be dense in ( K ^ ​ ( X ) , v ) (\widehat{K}(X),v) .

Introduction

Given a valued field
(K,v)
and a rational function field
K(X)
in one variable over
K
, it is a deep and classical problem to give a satisfactory characterization and description of all extensions of
v
to
K(X)
. It is sufficient to understand the set of all extensions of
v
to the polynomial ring
K[X]
, since the extended valuation necessarily satisfies
v(f/g)=vf-vg
for all
f,g\in K[X]
. The typical example of such an extended valuation is the Gauß valuation: for a polynomial
f(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}X^{i}\in K[X]
, we set
vf:=\min\{vc_{i}\}
. Note that we have
vX=0
in the Gauß valuation. Assigning an arbitrary value lying in some ordered abelian group containing
vK
to
X
and shifting the origin of the polynomial to an arbitrary element of
K
gives the monomial valuations, which are prototypical examples of the so-called valuation transcendental extensions. Not every extension of
v
to
K(X)
is valuation transcendental; however, the rest can be realized as limits of a certain class of valuation transcendental extensions. A thorough understanding of this extension problem has given rise to a whole host of literature and several different inter-connected notions. In this article, we undertake a systematic study of the extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
, where
\widehat{K}
denotes the completion of the valued field
(K,v)
. Our investigation shows that the extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is intimately connected with the underlying extension
(K(X)|K,v)
. The connection between these extensions is further explored in detail in terms of the aforementioned objects, viz. minimal pairs, key polynomials and implicit constant fields. Our first important result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field,
(\widehat{K},\widehat{v})
be its completion and
w
be an extension of
v
to
K(X)
. Fix an extension
\overline{\widehat{v}}
of
\widehat{v}
to the algebraic closure
\overline{\widehat{K}}
of
\widehat{K}
. Fix a common extension
\overline{w}
of
w
and
\overline{v}
to
\overline{K}(X)
, where
\overline{v}:=\overline{\widehat{v}}|_{\overline{K}}
. Then there exists a common extension of
\overline{\widehat{v}}
and
\overline{w}
to
\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)
. Further, every such common extension restricts to the same valuation (up to identification of equivalent valuations)
\widehat{w}
on
\widehat{K}(X)
.

(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is
induced by
(K(X)|K,w)
,
\overline{\widehat{v}}
and
\overline{w}
. When the valuations
\overline{\widehat{v}}
and
\overline{w}

(K(X)|K,w)

•

given an extension
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|K,v)
of valued fields, the extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is induced by
(K(X)|K,v)
.

During our preparation, we explore the strong connections of the induced extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
with the extension
(K(X)|K,w)

Case I:
(K(X)|K,w)
is valuation transcendental

Then
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is also valuation transcendental. Further, a minimal pair of definition for
w
over
K
also forms a minimal pair of definition for
\widehat{w}
over
\widehat{K}
(Corollary
3.2
). We have the following subcases:

Case IA:
vK
is cofinal in
wK(X)

In this case, a complete sequence of key polynomials for
w
over
K

\widehat{w}
over
\widehat{K}
(Theorem
3.1
). Moreover, the extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|K(X),\widehat{w})
is immediate (Corollary
3.2
).

Case IB:
(K(X)|K,w)
admits a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)

Take the minimal polynomials
Q_{a}
and
\widehat{Q}_{a}
of
a
over
K
and
\widehat{K}
respectively. Then a complete sequence of key polynomials for
\widehat{w}
over
\widehat{K}
is obtained by taking an initial segment of a complete sequence of key polynomials for
w
over
K
, and replacing
Q_{a}
by
\widehat{Q}_{a}
(Theorem
3.4
).

Case II:
(K(X)|K,w)
is valuation algebraic

There are again two subcases:

Case IIA:
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is valuation algebraic

In this case, a complete sequence of key polynomials for
w
over
K
forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for
\widehat{w}
over
\widehat{K}
. Further,
(\widehat{K}(X)|K(X),\widehat{w})

4.2
). In this case, we will say that
(K(X)|K,w)
is
valuation algebraic of type I
.

Case IIB:
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is valuation transcendental

In this case,
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
has a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
where
a\in\overline{\widehat{K}}\setminus\overline{K}
. A complete sequence of key polynomials for
\widehat{w}
over
\widehat{K}
is obtained by taking an initial segment of a complete sequence of key polynomials for
w
over
K
, and adjoining
\widehat{Q}_{a}
. The element
a
is
uniquely
determined in the following way: take sequences
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq\overline{w}(X-\overline{K})
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq\overline{K}
such that
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is cofinal in
\overline{w}(X-\overline{K})
and
\overline{w}(X-a_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}
for all
\nu<\lambda
. Then
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(\overline{K},\overline{v})
and has
a
as the unique limit in
\overline{\widehat{K}}
(Theorem
4.2
). In this case, we will say that
(K(X)|K,w)
is
valuation algebraic of type II
.

Observe that the induced valuation
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is determined uniquely once we fix
\overline{\widehat{v}}
and a common extension
\overline{w}
of
w
and
\overline{v}
to
\overline{K}(X)
. It is a natural question to inquire about the connections between two induced valuations corresponding to the choice of two distinct common extensions. This has been explored in the next result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let notations and assumptions be as in Theorem
1.1
. Take two common extension
\overline{w}
and
\overline{w}_{1}
of
w
and
\overline{v}
to
\overline{K}(X)
. Consider the corresponding induced extensions
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
and
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w}_{1})
. Then,

(i)
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is valuation algebraic if and only if
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w}_{1})
is valuation algebraic,

(ii)
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is valuation transcendental if and only if
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w}_{1})
is valuation transcendental.

When the induced extensions are valuation transcendental, then we can choose corresponding minimal pairs of definition
(a,\gamma)
and
(a_{1},\gamma)
over
\widehat{K}
such that
a
and
a_{1}
are conjugates over
\widehat{K}
. Further, if
a,a_{1}\in\overline{K}
, then they are also conjugates over
K
.

We can employ our findings to obtain strong ramification theoretic properties of the completion
\widehat{K}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field and fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{\widehat{K}}
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i)
(K,v)
is separably defectless
\Longleftrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is separably defectless
\Longleftrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is defectless.

(ii)
(K,v)
is separably tame
\Longleftrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is separably tame
\Longleftrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is tame.

(iii)
(K,v)
is separable-algebraically maximal
\Longleftrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is separable-algebraically maximal.

(iv)
K
is separable-algebraically closed
\Longleftrightarrow

\widehat{K}
is separable-algebraically closed
\Longleftrightarrow

\widehat{K}
is algebraically closed.

(v)
(K,v)
is algebraically maximal
\Longrightarrow

(\widehat{K},v)
is algebraically maximal. However, the converse is not true.

Given an algebraic extension
(L|K,v)
of valued fields, it is well-known that
\widehat{K}
is contained in
\widehat{L}
. However, this containment fails to hold when the extension is transcendental. If this containment was to hold true in the simplest possible case, that is when
L=K(X)
, then we would necessarily have the chain
K(X)\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}
. As a consequence,
K(X)
would lie dense in
\widehat{K}(X)
. In Section
8
we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for this to hold. We first illustrate the failure of
\widehat{K}
to be contained in
\widehat{K(X)}
whenever
(K(X)|K,v)
is either value transcendental with a unique pair of definition or valuation algebraic of type II (Proposition
8.1
). We then obtain the following result in the complimentary case:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. Assume that either
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation transcendental such that
vK
is cofinal in
vK(X)
, or that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic of type I. Take
\alpha\in v\widehat{K}(X)
and polynomials
f,g\in\widehat{K}[X]
. Then there exist polynomials
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\in K[X]
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\deg f=\deg f^{\prime},\,\,\deg g=\deg g^{\prime},

\displaystyle\,\,vf=vf^{\prime},\,\,vg=vg^{\prime},

\displaystyle v(f-f^{\prime})>\alpha,\,\,v(g-g^{\prime})>\alpha,

\displaystyle\,\,v(\frac{f}{g}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}})>\alpha.

Note that we actually obtain the stronger result that the polynomial ring
K[X]
lies dense in the polynomial ring
\widehat{K}[X]
under the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
.

Finally, given an extension of valued fields
(\overline{\widehat{K}(X)}|K,v)
, we study the connections between the implicit constant fields
IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
and
IC(K(X)|K,v)
. The implicit constant field of the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is defined to be the relative algebraic closure of
K
in the henselization
K(X)^{h}
of
K(X)
. It follows directly from the definition that
\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
, where
\widehat{K}^{h}
denotes the henselization of
\widehat{K}
. We obtain the following result in Section
9
:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
(\overline{\widehat{K}(X)}|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is not valuation algebraic of type II. Then,

\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)=IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v).

As an immediate corollary, we obtain that
IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is the completion of
IC(K(X)|K,v)
provided
(K,v)
is henselian,
IC(K(X)|K,v)|K
is a finite extension and
(K(X)|K,v)
is not valuation algebraic of type II.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, we denote the algebraic closure and the separable-algebraic closure of a field
K
by
\overline{K}
and
K^{\mathrm{sep}}
. If
L
and
K
are subfields of a larger field
\Omega
, then the
compositum
of
L
and
K
will be denoted by
L.K
. A valued field
(K,v)
is a field
K
equipped with a valuation
v
. An extension of valued fields
(L|K,v)
is an extension of fields
L|K
,
v
is a valuation on
L
and
K
is endowed with the restricted valuation. The value group
v(K^{\times})
of
(K,v)
will be denoted by
vK
and the residue field by
Kv
. The value of an element
a\in K
will be denoted by
va
and its residue by
av
.

2.1.
Density and Ramification theory

Let
(L|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. We will say that
(K,v)
is
dense
in
(L,v)
if for every element
a\in L
and for every
\gamma\in vL
, there exists some
b\in K
such that
v(a-b)>\gamma
. If this holds, then the extension
(L|K,v)
is
immediate
, that is,
vL=vK
and
Lv=Kv
. The maximal extension in which
(K,v)
is dense is said to be the
completion
of
(K,v)
, which is unique up to valuation preserving isomorphism. We denote the completion of
K
by
\widehat{K}
. It is well-known that when
(L|K,v)
is algebraic, then
\widehat{K}\subseteq\widehat{L}
. Moreover, if
(L|K,v)
is a finite extension of valued fields, then
\widehat{L}=L.\widehat{K}
.

A valued field
(K,v)
is said to be
henselian
if the valuation
v
admits a unique extension to
\overline{K}
. Every valued field admits a
henselization
, that is, a minimal algebraic extension which is henselian. The henselization is uniquely determined once we fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K}
. Hence we can talk of
the
henselization of
(K,v)
which we will denote by
K^{h}
. The extension
(K^{h}|K,v)
is an immediate, separable-algebraic extension. If
(L|K,v)
is an algebraic extension, then
L^{h}=L.K^{h}
. In particular, every algebraic extension of a henselian valued field is again henselian.

The fact that
(K^{h}|K,v)
is an algebraic extension implies that
\widehat{K}
is contained in the completion of
K^{h}
. The completion of a henselian field is again henselian [
References
, Theorem 32.19]. As a consequence, we have the following chain of inclusions:

K^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K^{h}},

where
\widehat{K}^{h}
denotes the henselization of
\widehat{K}
, and
\widehat{K^{h}}
denotes the completion of
K^{h}
.

Let
(L|K,v)
be a finite extension of valued fields. Further, assume that
v
admits a unique extension to
L
. Then the
Lemma of Ostrowski
gives us that

[L:K]=(vL:vK)[Lv:Kv]p^{d},

where
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
is the
characteristic exponent
of
(K,v)
, that is,
p=\operatorname{char}Kv
whenever
\operatorname{char}Kv>0
, and
p=1
otherwise. The integer
p^{d}
is said to be the
defect
of the extension
(L|K,v)
and is denoted by
d(L|K,v)
. If
p^{d}=1
, then we say that the extension is
defectless
.

Observe that we can always apply the Lemma of Ostrowski to extensions of henselian valued fields. An algebraic extension
(L|K,v)
of henselian valued fields will be said to be defectless if every finite subextension is defectless. A henselian field
(K,v)
will be said to be (separably) defectless if the extension
(\overline{K}|K,v)
is a defectless extension (similarly for
(K^{\mathrm{sep}}|K,v)
). An arbitrary valued field
(K,v)
will be said to be (separably) defectless if so is its henselization.

An algebraic extension
(L|K,v)
of henselian valued fields is said to be
tame
if every finite subextension
(E|K,v)
satisfies the following conditions:

(TE1)
\operatorname{char}Kv
does not divide
(vE:vK)
,

(TE2) the residue field extension
Ev|Kv
is separable,

(TE3)
(E|K,v)
is defectless.

A henselian valued field admits a unique maximal tame extension [
References
, Theorem 11.1]. It is called the
absolute ramification field of
(K,v)
and we will denote it by
K^{r}
.

A henselian field
(K,v)
is said to be a (separably) tame field if every (separable-algebraic) algebraic extension
(L|K,v)
is a tame extension. Equivalently,
(K,v)
is tame field if and only if
\overline{K}=K^{r}
and
(K,v)
is a separably tame field if and only if
K^{\mathrm{sep}}=K^{r}
.

A valued field is said to be
maximal
if it does not admit any nontrivial immediate extension. If it does not admit any nontrivial immediate algebraic extension, it is said to be
algebraically maximal
. It has been shown by Krull [
References
] that any formal power series field
k((t))
equipped with the
t
-adic valuation is a maximal valued field.

2.2.
Krasner’s Lemma

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field and fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K}
. Take
a\in\overline{K}\setminus K
which is not purely inseparable over
K
. The
Krasner constant
of
a
over
K
is defined as

\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K):=\max\{v(\sigma a-\tau a)\mid\sigma,\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}\,(\overline{K}|K)\text{ and }\sigma a\neq\tau a\}.

When
v
admits a unique extension from
K
to
K(a)
, we observe that

\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K):=\max\{v(\sigma a-a)\mid\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\,(\overline{K}|K)\text{ and }\sigma a\neq a\}.

The following is the important Krasner’s Lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field. Assume that
a,b\in\overline{K}
such that
v(a-b)>\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
. Then
K(a,b)|K(b)
is a purely inseparable extension. In particular, if
a
is separable-algebraic over
K
, then
a\in K(b)
.

2.3.
Pseudo-Cauchy sequences

A well-ordered set
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
in a valued field
(K,v)
, where
\lambda
is a limit ordinal, is said to be a
pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(pCs) if
v(z_{\nu_{1}}-z_{\nu_{2}})<v(z_{\nu_{2}}-z_{\nu_{3}})
for all
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}<\lambda
. It follows from the triangle inequality that
v(z_{\nu}-z_{\rho})=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})
for all
\nu<\rho<\lambda
. Set
\gamma_{\nu}:=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})
. An element
y
in some valued field extension
(L,v)
of
(K,v)
is said to be a
limit
of
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
if
v(y-z_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}
for all
\nu<\lambda
. Note that a pCs need not have a unique limit. Indeed, it follows from the triangle inequality that for
y,y^{\prime}\in L
,

y\text{ and }y^{\prime}\text{ are limits of }\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\text{ if and only if }v(y-y^{\prime})>\gamma_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu<\lambda.

A pCs
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
in
(K,v)
is said to be a
Cauchy sequence
if
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is cofinal in
vK
. A Cauchy sequence in
(K,v)
admits a unique limit in the completion
\widehat{K}
.

Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
and a pCs
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
over
K
. It follows from [
References
, Lemma 5] that the sequence
\{vf(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is either ultimately constant, or it is ultimately monotonically increasing. If
\{vf(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant for all polynomials
f
over
K
, then
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is said to be a pCs of
transcendental type
. Otherwise, it is of
algebraic type
.

2.4.
Extensions of
v
from
K
to
K(X)

The extension
(K(X)|K,v)
satisfies the famous Abhyankar inequality:

\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK+\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]\leq 1,

where
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-dimension of the divisible hull
(vK(X)/vK)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
. This inequality is a consequence of [
References
, Chapter VI, §10.3, Theorem 1]. The extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is said to be
value transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK=1
and
residue transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]=1
. The extension is said to be
valuation transcendental
if it is either value or residue transcendental. Otherwise, it is said to be
valuation algebraic
. It follows from [
References
, Lemma 3.3] that
(K(X)|K,v)
is value (residue) transcendental if and only if
(L(X)|L,v)
is also value (residue) transcendental, where
L
is an arbitrary algebraic extension of
K
. Further,
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic if and only if
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v)
is immediate.

Take a polynomial
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}X^{i}\in K[X]
and an element
a\in K
. We have a unique expansion
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}C_{i}(X-a)^{i}
, where the coefficients
C_{i}
are given by the
Hasse derivative
:

C_{i}=c_{i}+\binom{i+1}{i}ac_{i+1}+\dotsc+\binom{n}{i}a^{n-i}c_{n}.

Take
\gamma
in some ordered abelian group containing
vK
. We define the map
v_{a,\gamma}:K[X]\longrightarrow vK+{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma
by setting

v_{a,\gamma}f(X):=\min\{vC_{i}+i\gamma\},

and extend
v_{a,\gamma}
canonically to
K(X)
. Then
v_{a,\gamma}
is a valuation transcendental extension of
v
from
K
to
K(X)
[
References
, Lemma 3.10]. By definition,
v_{a,\gamma}(X-b)=\min\{v_{a,\gamma}(X-a),v(a-b)\}\leq v_{a,\gamma}(X-a)
for all
b\in K
. It follows that
v_{a,\gamma}(X-a)=\gamma=\max v_{a,\gamma}(X-K)
, where

v_{a,\gamma}(X-K):=\{v_{a,\gamma}(X-c)\mid c\in K\}.

Observe that
v_{0,0}
is nothing but the Gauß valuation.

2.5.
Minimal pairs

Let
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. It has been observed in [
References
, Theorem 3.11] that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation transcendental if and only if
v=v_{a,\gamma}
for some
a\in\overline{K}
and
\gamma\in v\overline{K}(X)
. Moreover,
(K(X)|K,v)
is value transcendental if and only if
\gamma\notin v\overline{K}
, that is, if and only if
\gamma
is not a torsion element modulo
vK
. Such a pair
(a,\gamma)\in\overline{K}\times v\overline{K}(X)
is said to be a
pair of definition for
v
over
K
. A valuation transcendental extension can admit multiple pairs of definition. Indeed, given a pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
v
over
K
, it has been observed in [
References
, Proposition 3] that
(b,\gamma)\in\overline{K}\times v\overline{K}(X)
forms another pair of definition if and only if

v(a-b)\geq\gamma.

A pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
is said to be a
minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K
if

v(a-b)\geq\gamma\Longrightarrow[K(a):K]\leq[K(b):K]\text{ for all }b\in\overline{K}.

It then follows from the well-ordering principle that a valuation transcendental extension always admits a minimal pair of definition.

Let
(K(X)|K,v)
be a valuation transcendental extension. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K}(X)
and take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
v
over
K
. Then the following cases are possible [
References
, Theorem 3.7]:

(i)
\gamma>v\overline{K}
. Then
v
is value transcendental and
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
v
over
K
.

(ii)
vK
is cofinal in
vK(X)
. Then there exists a minimal pair of definition
(b,\gamma)
for
v
over
K
such that
b
is separable-algebraic over
K
.

Now assume that
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
v
over
K
. Take any
f(X)\in K[X]
and write
f(X)=z\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z_{i})
where
z,z_{i}\in\overline{K}
. Then
vf=vz+\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(X-z_{i})
. By definition,
v(X-z_{i})=\min\{\gamma,v(a-z_{i})\}
. The fact that
\gamma>v\overline{K}
implies that
v(X-z_{i})\in v\overline{K}
whenever
a\neq z_{i}
. It follows that
vf\in v\overline{K}
whenever
f(a)\neq 0
. Otherwise
vf>v\overline{K}
.

2.6.
Key polynomials

Let
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. For a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
, we define

\delta(f):=\max\{v(X-a)\mid a\text{ is a root of }f\}.

A root
a
of
f
such that
\delta(f)=v(X-a)
is said to be a
maximal root of
f
. A monic polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
is said to be a
key polynomial for
v
over
K
if

\deg f<\deg Q\Longrightarrow\delta(f)<\delta(Q)\text{ for all }f(X)\in K[X].

Given a monic polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
and an arbitrary polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
, we have a unique expansion
f=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_{i}Q^{i}
, where
f_{i}(X)\in K[X]
with
0\leq\deg f_{i}<\deg Q
. Consider the map

v_{Q}:K[X]\longrightarrow vK(X)

given by
v_{Q}(f):=\min\{vf_{i}+ivQ\}
. Extend
v_{Q}
canonically to
K(X)
. A sufficient condition for
v_{Q}
to be a valuation on
K(X)
is that
Q
be a key polynomial for
v
over
K
[
References
, Proposition 2.6], but it is not a necessary condition [
References
, Proposition 2.3]. A family
\Lambda
of key polynomials for
v
over
K
is said to form a
complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(CSKP1)
\delta(Q)\neq\delta(Q^{\prime})
for
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Lambda
with
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
,

(CSKP2)
\Lambda
is well ordered with respect to the ordering given by
Q<Q^{\prime}
if and only if
\delta(Q)<\delta(Q^{\prime})
,

(CSKP3) for any
f(X)\in K[X]
, there exists some
Q\in\Lambda
such that
\deg Q\leq\deg f
and
vf=v_{Q}f
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields. Take
a\in\overline{K}
and set
\gamma:=v(X-a)
. Take
f(X)\in K[X]
. Then,

\displaystyle vf

\displaystyle>v_{a,\gamma}f\text{ if and only if }\delta(f)>\gamma,

\displaystyle vf

\displaystyle=v_{a,\gamma}f\text{ if and only if }\delta(f)\leq\gamma.

Proof.

Write
f(X)=z\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z_{i})
where
z,z_{i}\in\overline{K}
. By definition,
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})=\min\{\gamma,v(a-z_{i})\}
. It then follows from the triangle inequality that
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})\leq v(X-z_{i})
. As a consequence,

v_{a,\gamma}f\leq vf.

Further,
v_{a,\gamma}f<vf
if and only if
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})<v(X-z_{i})
for some
i
, which holds if and only if
v(X-z_{i})>\gamma=v(a-z_{i})
. The lemma now follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields. Take a key polynomial
Q(X)
for
v
over
K
and a maximal root
a
of
Q
. Then,

v_{a,\delta(Q)}|_{K(X)}=v_{Q}.

Proof.

By definition,
(a,\delta(Q))
is a pair of definition for
v_{a,\delta(Q)}
over
K
. Take another pair of definition
(a^{\prime},\delta(Q))
. Then
v(a-a^{\prime})\geq\delta(Q)=v(X-a)
and hence
v(X-a^{\prime})\geq\delta(Q)
. Take the minimal polynomial
Q^{\prime}(X)
of
a^{\prime}
over
K
. The fact that
v(X-a^{\prime})\geq\delta(Q)
implies that
\delta(Q^{\prime})\geq\delta(Q)
. As a consequence of
Q(X)
being a key polynomial for
v
over
K
, we conclude that
\deg Q^{\prime}\geq\deg Q
, that is,
[K(a^{\prime}):K]\geq[K(a):K]
. It follows that
(a,\delta(Q))
is a minimal pair of definition for

over

. In light of [

, Theorem 1.1], we conclude that
v_{a,\delta(Q)}|_{K(X)}=v_{Q}
.
∎

The following result is now immediate in light of Lemma
2.2
and Lemma

:

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields. Take a polynomial

and a key polynomial
Q(X)
for

over

. Then,

vf=v_{Q}f\text{ if and only if }\delta(f)\leq\delta(Q).

As an immediate consequence, we observe that a complete sequence of key polynomials
\{Q_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}
for the extension

necessarily satisfies that

\{\delta(Q_{\nu})\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}\text{ is a cofinal subset of }v(X-\overline{K}).

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields such that

is valuation transcendental. Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for

over

and the minimal polynomial

of

over

. Then there exists a complete sequence of key polynomials for

over

Proof.

The fact that

. Take a complete sequence of key polynomials

for

over

\{\delta(Q_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\in\Lambda}

over

2.7.
Continuity of roots

We refer the reader to [
References
] for a proof of the following important theorem:

Theorem 2.6

(Continuity of roots)
.

Let
(K,v)
be an arbitrary valued field. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K}
. Take the Gauß valuation
v_{0,0}
extending
v
to
\overline{K}(X)
. Take
\alpha\in vK
and
f,f^{\prime}\in K[X]
. Let
\deg f=\deg f^{\prime}=n
and take the leading coefficients
c_{n}
and
c^{\prime}_{n}
of
f
and
f^{\prime}
respectively. If

v_{0,0}(f-f^{\prime})>n^{n}\alpha-3n^{n}(v_{0,0}f-vc_{n})+vc_{n},

then we can write
f(X)=c_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z_{i})
and
f^{\prime}(X)=c^{\prime}_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z^{\prime}_{i})
such that
v(z_{i}-z^{\prime}_{i})>\alpha
for all
i
.

As an immediate corollary, we obtain the following result which plays an important role throughout the rest of this article:

Lemma 2.7
.

Take an extension of valued fields
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|K,v)
and a polynomial
f(X)\in\widehat{K}[X]
. Assume that
\delta(f)<\alpha
for some
\alpha\in vK
. Then there exists
f^{\prime}(X)\in K[X]
such that
\deg f=\deg f^{\prime}
,
vf=vf^{\prime}
and
\delta(f)=\delta(f^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Write
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}X^{i}=c_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z_{i})
. By the continuity of roots, there exists
\delta\in vK
such that after suitable renumbering,

v(z_{i}-z^{\prime}_{i})>\alpha\text{ for all }i\text{ whenever }v(c_{i}-c^{\prime}_{i})>\delta\text{ for all }i,

where
c^{\prime}_{n}\neq 0
and
\sum_{i=0}^{n}c^{\prime}_{i}X^{i}=c^{\prime}_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z^{\prime}_{i})
. Choose suitable
c^{\prime}_{i}\in K
and write
f^{\prime}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c^{\prime}_{i}X^{i}\in K[X]
. Then
\deg f=\deg f^{\prime}
. Further,

v(z_{i}-z^{\prime}_{i})>\alpha>\delta(f)\geq v(X-z_{i})\text{ for all }i.

It then follows from the triangle inequality that
v(X-z_{i})=v(X-z^{\prime}_{i})
for all
i
. As a consequence, we obtain that
\delta(f)=\delta(f^{\prime})
. Moreover, observe that we can always choose
c^{\prime}_{n}\in K
satisfying
v(c_{n}-c^{\prime}_{n})>\max\{\delta,vc_{n}\}
. Hence
vc_{n}=vc^{\prime}_{n}
and as a consequence,

vf^{\prime}=vc^{\prime}_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(X-z^{\prime}_{i})=vc_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(X-z_{i})=vf.

∎

of

Let

3.1.
vK
is cofinal in

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation transcendental and that

is cofinal in

v
to
\overline{\widehat{K}}
. Take a complete sequence of key polynomials

for

over

. Then

forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for the induced extension

Proof.

Take any
\nu\in\Lambda
and suppose that

is not a key polynomial for

over
\widehat{K}
. Then there exists some polynomial

. The assumption that

is cofinal in

. Applying Lemma
2.7
, we obtain a polynomial

\deg f^{\prime}<\deg Q_{\nu}
and
\delta(f^{\prime})\geq\delta(Q_{\nu})
. However, this contradicts the fact that
Q_{\nu}
is a key polynomial for
v
over

. We have thus obtained that
Q_{\nu}
is a key polynomial for
v
over
\widehat{K}
for all

.

Take any

that there exists some polynomial

such that

forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for

over

over
\widehat{K}
now yields that

.

.

(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)

(a,\gamma)

\gamma>v\overline{K}=v\overline{\widehat{K}}

.

Take a complete sequence of key polynomials

for

over

, we observe that

and the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

over

.

It follows from our preceding discussions that

\displaystyle K(X)\overline{v}_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{K}(X)\overline{\widehat{v}}_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}},

\displaystyle(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})\text{ is a minimal }

\displaystyle\text{pair of definition for }\overline{\widehat{v}}_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}\text{ over }\widehat{K}.

Further,

\displaystyle wK(X)=\bigcup_{\nu<\lambda}\overline{v}_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}K(X)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\nu<\lambda}\widehat{K}(X)\overline{\widehat{v}}_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}=\widehat{K}(X)\widehat{w}.

Remark 4.1
.

Contrary to the valuation transcendental case, it is not immediately clear why an extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
induced by a valuation algebraic extension
(K(X)|K,w)
is defined in a unique manner. To look into this matter more closely, we take distinct sequences
\{\gamma^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}\}_{\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{w}(X-\overline{K})
and
\{a^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}\}_{\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{K}
satisfying the conditions
(C1)
and
(C2)
. Observe that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is of transcendental type in
(\overline{\widehat{K}},\overline{\widehat{v}})
if and only if the same holds for
\{a^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}\}_{\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}}
. The valuation
\overline{\widehat{w}}
is defined uniquely in this scenario by [
References
, Theorem 2].

We now assume that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs of algebraic type in
(\overline{\widehat{K}},\overline{\widehat{v}})
. Take the limit
a\in\overline{\widehat{K}}
of
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
. By construction, the sequence
\{a^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}\}_{\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}}
admits
X
as a limit. The fact that
\overline{\widehat{w}}(X-a)>\overline{v}\overline{K}
implies that
\overline{\widehat{w}}(X-a)>\gamma^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}
for all
\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}
. As a consequence,
a
is also a limit of
\{a^{\prime}_{\nu^{\prime}}\}_{\nu^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime}}
. The valuation
\overline{\widehat{w}}
is thus uniquely defined.

We gather our findings in the next theorem. For ease of notation, we will again denote all the valuations by the same letter
v
.

We now investigate whether we can modify Proposition
3.5
. Assume that

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto(1,0),

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto(0,\alpha)\text{ for all }\alpha\in G,

is an order preserving isomorphism. Recall that two valuations

After identifying equivalent valuations, we can thus assume that

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields such that
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is valuation transcendental with a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
. After identifying equivalent valuations, assume that
v\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)=({\mathbb{Z}}\oplus v\overline{K})_{\text{lex}}
, where
\gamma=(1,0)
and
\alpha\mapsto(0,\alpha)
for all

(i)
a\in\overline{K}
. Then
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is induced by the valuation transcendental extension

(ii)

. Then
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is induced by the valuation algebraic extension

(K(X)|K,v)

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields such that
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is valuation algebraic. Then
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is induced by the valuation algebraic extension
(K(X)|K,v)
. Moreover, the extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|K(X),v)

Proof.

Consider the chain
v\widehat{K}=vK\subseteq vK(X)\subseteq v\widehat{K}(X)
. The fact that

is a torsion group implies that
vK(X)/vK
is a torsion group. Similarly,

is an algebraic extension. Take sequences
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq v(X-\overline{K})
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq\overline{K}
satisfying the conditions
(C1)
and
(C2)
. Then
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs of transcendental type in
(\overline{K},v)
with limit
X
. Suppose that

is of algebraic type in
(\overline{\widehat{K}},v)
and take the limit

. Set
\gamma:=v(X-a)
. Then
\gamma>\gamma_{\nu}
for all
\nu<\lambda
. The fact that
(\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)|\overline{\widehat{K}},v)
is an immediate extension implies that
\gamma\in v\overline{\widehat{K}}=v\overline{K}
. Consequently, we can choose

such that

, whence

is a limit of

is a pCs of transcendental type in

is a limit of

is induced by

from the uniqueness assertion of [

for all

, that is,

In other words,

is a valuation preserving isomorphism of
\overline{K}
over

. By continuity, we can lift
\sigma
to a valuation preserving isomorphism
\widehat{\sigma}
of
\widehat{\overline{K}}
over
\widehat{K}
, that is,
\widehat{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Gal}\,(\widehat{\overline{K}}|\widehat{K})
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\overline{v}}\circ\widehat{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\overline{v}}\text{ on }\widehat{\overline{K}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}|_{\overline{K}}

\displaystyle=\sigma.

(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},\widehat{w})
is valuation transcendental with a minimal pair of definition

over
\widehat{K}

and Proposition

, we observe that

is valuation transcendental with

as a minimal pair of definition for
\overline{w}
over

. Since the extension induced by a valuation transcendental extension is again valuation transcendental, it follows that

\overline{w}_{1}
over

. Since
\overline{w}
and

\widehat{K}

over
\widehat{K}

Lemma 7.1

Proposition 8.1

\widehat{K}

\widehat{K}(X)\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}

. The fact that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic implies that
vK(X)/vK
is a torsion group. It follows that
v\widehat{K}(X)/vK
is also a torsion group. However, this yields a contradiction, as the fact that
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is value transcendental implies that
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,v\widehat{K}(X)/v\widehat{K}=1
, that is,
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,v\widehat{K}(X)/vK=1
.

(K(X)|K,v)
admits a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for some

. Then
\gamma>v\overline{K}
. Suppose that
\widehat{K}\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}
. Then
\widehat{K(a)}=\widehat{K}(a)\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}(a)=\widehat{K(a,X)}
. So without any loss of generality we can assume that
a\in K
. Consequently,
\gamma=v(X-a)\in vK(X)
. Take some
b\in\widehat{K}\setminus K
. The assumption
\widehat{K}\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}
implies that there exist coprime polynomials
f(X),g(X)\in K[X]
such that

v(\frac{f(X)}{g(X)}-b)>\gamma.

The fact that
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
v
over
K
implies that
vg\in v\overline{K}
whenever
g(a)\neq 0
. Otherwise
vg>v\overline{K}
. In either case, we can conclude that

v(f(X)-bg(X))>\gamma+vg>v\overline{K}=v\overline{\widehat{K}}.

Set
h(X):=f(X)-bg(X)\in\widehat{K}[X]
. Note that
(a,\gamma)
is also the unique pair of definition for
v
over
\widehat{K}
. Then the condition
vh>v\overline{\widehat{K}}
necessarily implies that
h(a)=0
, that is,

(1)

f(a)=bg(a).

The conditions

,

imply that
f(a),g(a)\in K
. The coprimality of
f(X)
and
g(X)
implies that
f(a)
and

can not vanish simultaneously. But then (
1
) contradicts the assumption that

. Hence
\widehat{K}
is not contained in
\widehat{K(X)}
.

(K(X)|K,v)
being valuation algebraic of type I implies that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}
is a pCs of transcendental type in
(\overline{\widehat{K}},v)
with limit
X
. It now follows from the proof of [
References
, Theorem 2] that for any polynomial
h(X)\in\widehat{K}[X]
, we have

vh=v_{\nu}h\text{ for all }\nu\in\Lambda\text{ sufficiently large}.

Further, the fact that
Q_{\nu}
is a key polynomial for
v
over
K
with maximal root
a_{\nu}
implies that
(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{\nu}
over
K
. It then follows from Corollary
3.2
that
(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
is also a minimal pair of definition for
v_{\nu}
over
\widehat{K}
. In light of [
References
, Theorem 5.1], we then conclude that

(3)

vh\geq v_{\nu}h\text{ for all polynomials }h(X)\in\widehat{K}[X],\text{ for all }\nu\in\Lambda.

Take
f,g\in\widehat{K}[X]
. Choose
\nu\in\Lambda
large enough such that
vf=v_{\nu}f
and
vg=v_{\nu}g
. Fix an extension of
v_{\nu}
to
\overline{\widehat{K}}(X)
. The fact that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic implies that
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v)
is immediate. Consequently,
\gamma_{\nu}\in v\overline{K}
. It follows that
(K(X)|K,v_{\nu})
is residue transcendental. In particular,
vK
is cofinal in
v_{\nu}K(X)
. Take
\alpha_{0}\in vK
such that

\alpha_{0}>\max\{\alpha,vf,vg\}.

From our preceding discussions in the valuation transcendental case, we obtain
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\in K[X]
such that

\displaystyle\deg f=\deg f^{\prime},\,\,\deg g=\deg g^{\prime},

\displaystyle\,\,v_{\nu}f=v_{\nu}f^{\prime},\,\,v_{\nu}g=v_{\nu}g^{\prime},

\displaystyle v_{\nu}(f-f^{\prime})>\alpha_{0},\,\,v_{\nu}(g-g^{\prime})>\alpha_{0},

\displaystyle\,\,v_{\nu}(\frac{f}{g}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}})>\alpha_{0}.

As a consequence of (
3
), we then have that

\displaystyle v(f-f^{\prime})\geq v_{\nu}(f-f^{\prime})>\alpha_{0}>\alpha,

\displaystyle v(g-g^{\prime})\geq v_{\nu}(g-g^{\prime})>\alpha_{0}>\alpha.

The fact that
\alpha_{0}>\max\{vf,vg\}
then yields the following in light of the triangle inequality:

vf=vf^{\prime}\text{ and }vg=vg^{\prime}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle v(\frac{f}{g}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=v(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)-v(gg^{\prime})=v(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)-2vg

\displaystyle=v(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)-2v_{\nu}g

\displaystyle\geq v_{\nu}(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)-2v_{\nu}g

\displaystyle=v_{\nu}(\frac{f}{g}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}})>\alpha_{0}>\alpha.

We have thus proved the theorem.
∎

9.
Implicit constant fields

Given an extension of valued fields
(\overline{K(X)}|K,v)
, the implicit constant field of the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is defined as

IC(K(X)|K,v):=\overline{K}\cap K(X)^{h}.

Thus
IC(K(X)|K,v)
is a separable-algebraic extension of
K^{h}
. As a consequence, the extension
IC(K(X)|K,v)|K^{h}
is simple whenever it is finite.

We now assume that
(\overline{\widehat{K}(X)}|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields. Then,

IC(K(X)|K,v)=\overline{K}\cap K(X)^{h}\subseteq\overline{\widehat{K}}\cap\widehat{K}(X)^{h}=IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v).

The fact that
\widehat{K}^{h}
is a subset of
IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
then implies the following:

(4)

\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v),

where
\widehat{K}^{h}
denotes the henselization of
\widehat{K}
. We can now give a
proof of Proposition
1.5
:

Proof.

We first assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation transcendental such that it admits a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
. Then the induced extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
also has
(a,\gamma)
as the unique pair of definition. It follows from [
References
, Theorem 1.1] that
K^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq K^{h}(a)
and hence
IC(K(X)|K,v)
is a finite extension of
K^{h}
. Take
b\in K^{\mathrm{sep}}
such that

IC(K(X)|K,v)=K^{h}(b).

It follows from [
References
, Theorem 7.2] that
K^{h}(a)|K^{h}(b)
is a purely inseparable extension. Consequently, the extension
\widehat{K}^{h}(a)|\widehat{K}^{h}(b)
is also purely inseparable [
References
, V, §6, Proposition 6.5]. Further, the fact that
b
is separable over
K
implies that
\widehat{K}^{h}(b)|\widehat{K}
is separable. It follows that
\widehat{K}^{h}(b)
is the separable-algebraic closure of
\widehat{K}
in
\widehat{K}^{h}(a)
. In light of [
References
, Theorem 7.2], we conclude that

IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)=\widehat{K}^{h}(b).

We now assume that either
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation transcendental such that
vK
is cofinal in
vK(X)
or
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic of type I. Take any
b\in IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
. Then
b
is separable-algebraic over
\widehat{K}^{h}
. We have the inclusions
K^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K^{h}}
. In light of Corollary
7.2
, we can then choose
a
separable-algebraic over
K^{h}
such that
\widehat{K}^{h}(b)=\widehat{K}^{h}(a)
. The fact that
\widehat{K}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)^{h}
then implies that

\widehat{K}(X)^{h}(b)=\widehat{K}(X)^{h}(a).

By definition,
b\in\widehat{K}(X)^{h}
. It follows that
a\in\widehat{K}(X)^{h}
. We have observed in Theorem
1.4
that
K(X)
lies dense in
\widehat{K}(X)
. Then
K(X)\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}
. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{\widehat{K(X)}}
. Upon taking henselizations, we have

K(X)^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K(X)^{h}}.

We then obtain from Corollary
7.2
that
[\widehat{K}(X)^{h}(a):\widehat{K}(X)^{h}]=[K(X)^{h}(a):K(X)^{h}]
. Consequently,
a\in K(X)^{h}
. The fact that
a\in\overline{K}
then implies that
a\in IC(K(X)|K,v)
. We have thus obtained that
b\in\widehat{K}^{h}(a)\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)
and as a consequence,

IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v).

The proposition now follows from (
4
).
∎

Remark 9.1
.

In the context of Proposition
1.5
we obtain that

IC(K(X)|K,v)\text{ is dense in }IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v).

Indeed, we have the inclusion
\widehat{K}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K^{h}}
. The fact that
IC(K(X)|K,v)|K^{h}
is an algebraic extension implies that
\widehat{K^{h}}
is contained in the completion of
IC(K(X)|K,v)
. Consequently,
\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)
is contained in the completion of
IC(K(X)|K,v)
.

Corollary 9.2
.

Let notations and assumptions be as in Proposition
1.5
. Assume further that
(K,v)
is henselian and
IC(K(X)|K,v)|K
is a finite extension. Then

IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)\text{ is the completion of }IC(K(X)|K,v).

Since we do not have a precise description of the implicit constant field for valuation algebraic extensions, the problem appears to be more complicated when
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic of type II. However, we have a satisfactory answer when the henselization of
K
is separably tame.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
(\overline{\widehat{K}(X)}|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic of type II. Further, assume that
K^{h}
is separably tame. Then,

\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)=IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v).

Proof.

We have observed in [
References
, Section 5] that we can construct sequences
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq v(X-\overline{K})
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq\overline{K}
such that
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is cofinal in
v(X-\overline{K})
,
v(X-a_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}
and
(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}
over
K
for all
\nu<\lambda
.
(K(X)|K,v)
being a valuation algebraic extension implies that
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v)
is immediate and hence
\gamma_{\nu}\in v\overline{K}
. In light of [
References
, Proposition 3.6], we can thus further assume that
a_{\nu}
is separable-algebraic over
K
. The assumption that
K^{h}
is separably tame implies that
K^{\mathrm{sep}}=K^{r}
. It now follows from [
References
, Theorem 6.2] that

IC(K(X)|K,v)=K^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda).

It follows from Theorem
4.2
that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(\overline{K},v)
and the unique limit
a\in\widehat{\overline{K}}
is algebraic over
\widehat{K}
. Moreover,
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for the induced extension
(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
. It then follows from [
References
, Theorem 7.2] that
IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)
is the separable-algebraic closure of
\widehat{K}^{h}
in
\widehat{K}^{h}(a)
. Therefore,
IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)=\widehat{K}^{h}
whenever
a
is purely inseparable over
\widehat{K}^{h}
. The inclusions
\widehat{K}^{h}\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}.IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)=\widehat{K}^{h}
then imply that all the containments are actually equalities. We have thus proved the assertion in this case.

Otherwise,
a
is not purely inseparable over
\widehat{K}^{h}
. So
\mathrm{kras}\,(a,\widehat{K}^{h})
is well-defined. Note that
\mathrm{kras}\,(a,\widehat{K}^{h})\in v\overline{\widehat{K}}=v\overline{K}
. As a consequence of
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
being a Cauchy sequence in
(\overline{K},v)
, we can take some
a_{\nu}
such that
v(a-a_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}>\mathrm{kras}\,(a,\widehat{K}^{h})
. It now follows from Krasner’s Lemma that
\widehat{K}^{h}(a,a_{\nu})|\widehat{K}^{h}(a_{\nu})
is purely inseparable. Consequently,
\widehat{K}^{h}(a,a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)|\widehat{K}^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)
is purely inseparable. The relations
K^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)=IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)\subseteq\widehat{K}^{h}(a)
then imply that

\widehat{K}^{h}(a)|\widehat{K}^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)\text{ is purely inseparable}.

Since we chose
a_{\nu}
to be separable-algebraic over
K
, the extension
\widehat{K}^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)|\widehat{K}^{h}
is also separable-algebraic. It now follows from [
References
, Theorem 7.2] that

IC(\widehat{K}(X)|\widehat{K},v)=\widehat{K}^{h}(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda).

We have thus proved the proposition.
∎

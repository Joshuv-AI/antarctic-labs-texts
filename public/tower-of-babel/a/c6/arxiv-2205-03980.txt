Dynamical and qKZ equations modulo p s p^{s} , an example
Alexander Varchenko

Abstract
. We consider an example of the joint system of dynamical differential equations and qKZ difference equations with parameters corresponding to equations for elliptic integrals. We solve this system of equations modulo any power p n p^{n} of a prime integer p p . We show that the p p -adic limit of these solutions as n → ∞ n\to\infty determines a sequence of line bundles, each of which is invariant with respect to the corresponding dynamical connection, and that sequence of line bundles is invariant with respect to the corresponding qKZ difference connection.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z_{1},z_{2})
,

\displaystyle\Phi(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)=t^{-\lambda}(t-z_{1})^{-\mu}(t-z_{2})^{-\mu},

where
\lambda,\mu
are rational numbers.
Consider the column vector

(1.1)

\displaystyle I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)=\int_{C}\Big(\frac{\Phi}{t-z_{1}},\frac{\Phi}{t-z_{2}}\Big)dt,

where
C\subset{\mathbb{C}}-\{0,z_{1},z_{2}\}
is a contour
on which the integrand takes its initial value when
t
encircles
C
.
As a function of
{\boldsymbol{z}}
, the vector
I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)
extends to a multi-valued analytic function on

\{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\ |\ a_{1}a_{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})\neq 0\}
.

The function
I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)
satisfies the differential and difference equations,

(1.2)

\displaystyle z_{1}\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{1}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda-\mu&-\mu\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\frac{\mu z_{1}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\
1&-1\end{bmatrix}\right)I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu),

\displaystyle z_{2}\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{2}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
-\mu&-\lambda-\mu\end{bmatrix}+\frac{\mu z_{2}}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\
1&-1\end{bmatrix}\right)I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu),

(1.3)

\displaystyle I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+1,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\lambda+\mu}{z_{1}\lambda}&\frac{\mu}{z_{1}\lambda}\\
\frac{\mu}{z_{2}\lambda}&\frac{\lambda+\mu}{z_{2}\lambda}\end{bmatrix}I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)\,.

If
\lambda,\mu,\lambda+2\mu\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, then all solutions of these equations are given by integrals

I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)
(with different choices of
C
)
up to multiplication by a scalar 1-periodic function of
\lambda
, this fact follows from
[
V1
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Up to a gauge transformation, equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
) are the simplest
example of the trigonometric
KZ
differential
equations and dynamical difference equations, see
[
TV1
,
MV
]
.
They are also the simplest example
of the dynamical differential equations and
qKZ
difference equations,
see
[
TV2
,
TV3
]
. Up to a gauge transformation, they are the equivariant quantum differential equations and

qKZ
difference equation associated with the cotangent bundle of projective line, see
[
TV3
]
.
The family of functions
I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)
, labeled by contours
C
, are the hypergeometric solutions of these
equations constructed in
[
MV
,
TV2
]
. In particular, see the integral
I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)

(gauge transformed) in
[
TV2
, Section 7.4]
. We call equations (
1.2
) the dynamical differential equations and equation (
1.3
) the
qKZ
difference equation.

In this paper we discuss polynomial solutions of equations
(
1.2
) and (
1.3
) modulo
p^{n}
, where
p
is a prime integer and
n
is a positive integer.
We also discuss the
p
-adic limit of these solutions as
n\to\infty
.

More precisely, we consider the following problem.
For
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, let

\Lambda(\lambda_{0})=\{\lambda_{0}+l\ |\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
be the arithmetic sequence with
initial term
\lambda_{0}
and step
1
. For a positive integer
\ell
, let

\Lambda(\lambda_{0},\ell)=\{\lambda_{0}+l\ |\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,|\lambda_{0}+l|<\ell\}
be an interval of
the sequence
\Lambda
.

Problem.
Let
p
be an prime integer,
\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. For any

n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, find a sequence of column vectors

(1.4)

\displaystyle I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda;\ell;n)=(I_{1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda;\ell;n),I_{2}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\ell;n)),\qquad\lambda\in\Lambda(\lambda_{0},\ell),

such that

(\operatorname{i})

the coordinates of these vectors are polynomials in
{\boldsymbol{z}}
with integer coefficients,

(\operatorname{ii})

each of the vectors
I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda;\ell;n)
satisfies modulo
p^{n}
differential equations (
1.2
)
with parameter
\mu=\mu_{0}
;

(\operatorname{iii})

this sequence of vectors satisfies modulo
p^{n}
the difference
equation (
1.3
)
with parameter
\mu=\mu_{0}
.

We may also require that the vectors are functorial in the following sense.
If
\lambda\in\Lambda(\lambda_{0},\ell_{0})
for some
l_{0}
, then the vector
I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda;\ell;n)
does not depend on
\ell
for

\ell\geqslant\ell_{0}
. Having a solution
\{I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda;\ell;n)\}
of this problem we may study the
p
-adic limit of
the vectors as
n\to\infty
.

In this paper we construct a solution of this problem for

(1.5)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})=\big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big).

We also describe the
p
-adic limit of our solution. It turns out that the limit is not a solution
of equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) over a

-adic field, as one may naively think,

1.2

and invariant with respect to the discrete

connection, defined by equation (

Notice that there is no such a line bundle if we consider the same differential and difference equations

The choice of parameters in (

This choice is technically,
arithmetically easier than the choice of an arbitrary pair

although a similar construction can be performed for a wide class of parameters

Quantum differential equations and associated

difference equations, as well as their solutions, is a

with applications in representation theory, algebraic geometry, theory of special function, to name
a few. It would be interesting to study how the properties of these equations
and their solutions are reflected in the solutions of the same equations modulo powers of a prime integer
and in their
p
-adic limits.

In Section

) and (

In Section

On the

3.2.
Solutions

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{s}
, define the master polynomial,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda):=t^{(p^{s}-\lambda)/2}(t-z_{1})^{(p^{s}-1)/2}(t-z_{2})^{(p^{s}-1)/2}.

Define the column vector

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=\big(\Psi_{s,1}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda),\Psi_{s,2}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\big):=\,\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\Big(\frac{1}{t-z_{1}},\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big).

The coordinates of
\Psi_{s}
are polynomials in
t,{\boldsymbol{z}}
with integer coefficients.
We denote

(3.3)

\displaystyle I_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=(I_{s,1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda),I_{s,2}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)):=\{\Psi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\}_{s}\,,

the coefficient of
t^{p^{s}-1}
in
\Psi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
,
and

(3.4)

\displaystyle T_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda):=\{\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\}_{s}\,,

the coefficient of
t^{p^{s}-1}
in
\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
.
For every
\lambda\in\Lambda_{s}
, the functions
I_{s,1},I_{s,2},T_{s}
are polynomials in
z_{1},z_{2}

with integer coefficients.

We have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1-p^{s}}{2}\,I_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\,=\,{\operatorname{grad}}_{\boldsymbol{z}}T_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\,.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{s}
,
i=1,2
. Then the vector
I_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
satisfies the
congruence

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)I_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{s}}.

Proof.

We have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}=\Big(\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2t}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2(t-z_{1})}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2(t-z_{2})}\Big)\Phi_{s}\,.

Also

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\partial\Psi_{s,1}}{\partial t}=\Phi_{s}\Big(-\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2}\frac{1}{t(t-z_{1})}-\frac{p^{s}-3}{2}\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})^{2}}-\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})(t-z_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{s}\Big(\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2z_{1}}\Big[\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}\Big]-\frac{p^{s}-3}{2}\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})^{2}}-\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big]\Big).

Hence

\displaystyle-\Phi_{s}\frac{p^{s}-3}{2}\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})^{2}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\frac{\Phi}{t-z_{1}}\Big)\,+

\displaystyle+\ \Phi_{s}\Big(\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2z_{1}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t}\Big]+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\frac{\Phi_{s}}{t-z_{1}}\Big)\,+\Phi_{s}\Big(\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2z_{1}}\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle+\ \Phi_{s}\frac{1}{z_{1}}\Big(\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big)-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\frac{\Phi_{s}}{t-z_{1}}\Big)\,-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}\

\displaystyle+\ \Phi_{s}\Big(\frac{2p^{s}-\lambda-1}{2z_{1}}\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2z_{1}}\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big]\Big).

We have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\frac{\Phi_{s}}{t-z_{1}}\Big)\Big\}_{s}\equiv 0,\ \ \Big\{\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}\Big\}_{s}\equiv 0,\ \ \frac{2p^{s}-\lambda-1}{2}\equiv-\frac{\lambda+1}{2},\ \ \frac{2p^{s}-1}{2}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}

modulo
p^{s}
. Hence

\displaystyle 2z_{1}\frac{\partial I_{s,1}}{\partial z_{1}}\ \equiv\ -(\lambda+1)I_{s,1}-I_{s,2}+\frac{z_{1}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}(-I_{s,1}+I_{s,2})\pmod{p^{s}}\,.

We also have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Psi_{s,2}}{\partial z_{1}}=-\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{\Phi_{s}}{(t-z_{1})(t-z_{2})}=-\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\frac{\Phi_{s}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}-\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big].

Hence

\displaystyle 2z_{1}\frac{\partial I_{s,2}}{\partial z_{1}}\ \equiv\ \frac{z_{1}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}(I_{s,1}-I_{s,2})\pmod{p^{s}}\,.

Equation (
3.6
) for
i=1
is proved.
Equation (
3.6
) for
i=2
is proved similarly.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
s>e
be positive integers, and
\lambda,\lambda+2\in\Lambda_{e}
. Then the vector
I_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
satisfies the
congruence :

(3.9)

\displaystyle I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle K(z,\lambda)I({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\,\pmod{p^{s-e}}.

Proof.

Equation (
3.7
) can be written as

\displaystyle-\frac{\Phi_{s}}{t}=-\frac{2}{p^{s}-\lambda}\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{p^{s}-\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}+\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big)\Phi_{s}\,.

Hence

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Psi_{s,1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)=\frac{\Phi_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)}{t(t-z_{1})}=-\frac{\Phi_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)}{z_{1}}\Big[\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{t-z_{1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)}{z_{1}(t-z_{1})}-\frac{2}{(p^{s}-\lambda)z_{1}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{p^{s}-\lambda}\Big(\frac{1}{z_{1}(t-z_{1})}+\frac{1}{z_{1}(t-z_{2})}\Big)\Phi_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)

By (
3.8
) the term
\big\{\frac{2}{(p^{s}-\lambda)z_{1}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{s}}{\partial t}\big\}_{s}
is divisible at least by

. Hence (

) implies

\displaystyle I_{s,1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)\equiv\frac{\lambda+1}{z_{1}\lambda}I_{s,1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)+\frac{1}{z_{1}\lambda}I_{s,2}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\pmod{p^{s-e}}.

Similarly we obtain

\displaystyle I_{s,2}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)\equiv\frac{\lambda+1}{z_{2}\lambda}I_{s,2}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)+\frac{1}{z_{2}\lambda}I_{s,1}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\pmod{p^{s-e}}.

Theorem
3.2
is proved.
∎

A congruence
F(x)\equiv G(x)\pmod{p^{s}}
for two polynomials in some variables
x
with integer coefficients
is understood
as the divisibility by
p^{s}
of all coefficients of
F(x)-G(x)
.

Let
F_{1}(x)
,
F_{2}(x)
,
G_{1}(x)
,
G_{2}(x)
be polynomials
such that
F_{2}(x)
,
G_{2}(x)
are both nonzero modulo
p
.
Then the congruence

F_{1}(x)/F_{2}(x)\equiv G_{1}(x)/G_{2}(x)
modulo
p^{s}

is understood as the congruence

\displaystyle F_{1}(x)G_{2}(x)\equiv G_{1}(x)F_{2}(x)\pmod{p^{s}}\,.

Recall the master polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=t^{(p^{s}-\lambda)/2}(t-z_{1})^{(p^{s}-1)/2}(t-z_{2})^{(p^{s}-1)/2},

in particular,

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};1)=t^{(p-1)/2}(t-z_{1})^{(p-1)/2}(t-z_{2})^{(p-1)/2}.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{e}
and
s>e
we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=\Phi_{e}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\Phi_{1}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};1)^{p^{e}+p^{e+1}+\dots+p^{s-e-1}},

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{s-e}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};1)=\Phi_{1}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};1)^{1+p+\dots+p^{s-e-1}}\,.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{e}
and
s\geqslant e
,
the Newton polytope of
\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
with respect to the variable
t
is the interval

\big[\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2},\,\frac{p^{s}-\lambda}{2}+p^{s}-1\big]
.

(4.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{For\,a\,positive\,integer}\,k,\,\operatorname{the\,point}\,kp^{s}-1\,\operatorname{lies\,in\,this\,interval\,only\,if}\,k=1.

Recall that

T_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=\{\Phi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\}_{s}
is
the coefficient of
t^{p^{s}-1}
in
\Psi_{s}(t;{\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
.

(4.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{For}\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{e},\,\operatorname{the\,polynomial}\,T_{s}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\,\operatorname{is\,nonzero\,modulo}\,p\,,

by Lemma
5.7

5.

5.1.
Unramified extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
For every
m
, there is a unique unramified extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
in

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of degree
m
, denoted by
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
.
This can be obtained by attaching to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
a primitive root of
1
of order
p^{m}-1
.
The norm
|\cdot|_{p}
on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
extends to a norm
|\cdot|_{p}
on

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
.
Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}=\{a\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}\mid|a|_{p}\leqslant 1\}

denote the ring of integers in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}
. The ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}

has the unique maximal ideal

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{p}^{(m)}=\{a\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{(m)}\mid|a|_{p}<1\},

such that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{(m)}\big/\mathbb{M}_{p}^{(m)}
is isomorphic to the finite field

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
.

For every
t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
there is a unique
\tilde{t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{(m)}
that is a lift of
t
and such that

\tilde{t}^{p^{m}}=\tilde{t}
. The element
\tilde{t}
is called the Teichmuller lift of
t
.

Domain

For

and

We have the partition

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)}=\bigcup_{t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}}D_{t,1}\,.

Recall
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z_{1},z_{2})
. For

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{B}\ =\ \{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{2}\,\mid\ |B({\boldsymbol{a}})|_{p}=1\}.

Let

to

Then

is the union of unit polydiscs,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{B}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}\\

\displaystyle\{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{2}\mid\ |B({\boldsymbol{a}}^{p^{k}})|_{p}=1\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(\lambda)\big(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\lambda)s({\boldsymbol{z}})\big),

\displaystyle\tau\big(\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)s({\boldsymbol{z}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda+2)\big(\tau s({\boldsymbol{z}})\big),\qquad i=1,2.

Denote

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigcup\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\times\{\lambda\}\ \subset\ ({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{(m)})^{2}\times\Lambda.

For any
({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]
, the vector

\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)
is nonzero by Lemma
5.8
.

For any
({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]
, let
\mathcal{L}_{({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)}\subset\mathcal{W}

be the one-dimensional vector subspace generated by
the vector
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup\nolimits_{({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]}\,\mathcal{L}_{({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)}\times\{({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)\}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]

is an analytic line subbundle of the trivial bundle

\mathcal{W}\times\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]\to\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]

Theorem 5.9
.

The line bundle
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]

is invariant with respect to the dynamical and
qKZ
connections.
More precisely,

\operatorname{(i)}

if
s({\boldsymbol{z}})
is a local section of
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\times\{\lambda\}
, then

\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)s({\boldsymbol{z}})
,
i=1,2
, also are local sections of
\mathcal{L}
over

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\times\{\lambda\}
;

\operatorname{(ii)}

if
s({\boldsymbol{z}})
is a local section of
\mathcal{L}
over

(\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\cap\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda+2))\times\{\lambda\}
, then

\tau s({\boldsymbol{z}})
, is a local section of
\mathcal{L}
over

(\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\cap\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda+2))\times\{\lambda+2\}
.

Proof.

Let
({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)\in\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\times\{\lambda\}
.
Let
c({\boldsymbol{z}})
be a scalar analytic function at
{\boldsymbol{a}}
.
Consider the local section
c({\boldsymbol{z}})\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
of
\mathcal{L}
at
({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)\big(c({\boldsymbol{z}})\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,cH_{i}\mathcal{I}+c\frac{\partial\mathcal{I}}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,cH_{i}\mathcal{I}+c\Big(\mathcal{I}^{(i)}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}_{i}\mathcal{I}\Big)+\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,cH_{i}\mathcal{I}+c\Big(H_{i}\mathcal{I}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}_{i}\mathcal{I}\Big)+\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{c}{2}\mathcal{I}_{i}+\frac{\partial c}{\partial z_{i}}\Big)\mathcal{I}\,.

Here we used Lemma
5.5
.
Clearly, the last expression is a local section of
\mathcal{L}
at
({\boldsymbol{a}},\lambda)
.
Part (i) is proved.

By definition of
\tau
, we have
\tau\big(c({\boldsymbol{z}})\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\big)=c({\boldsymbol{z}})K({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
.
We also have the equality

\displaystyle c({\boldsymbol{z}})K({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)=c({\boldsymbol{z}})\tilde{\mathcal{I}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2),

which holds on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}(\lambda)
,
by Lemma
5.5
. The vectors

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)

and

{\mathcal{I}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)

are proportional on
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}(\lambda)\cap\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}(\lambda+2)
, by Lemma
5.6
.
For the smaller set
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda)\cap\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(\lambda+2)
,
the initial vector vector
{\mathcal{I}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda)
and the resulting vector
{\mathcal{I}}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda+2)
are
both nonzero, by Lemmas
5.7
and
5.8
. This proves part (ii).
∎

5.7.
Special points

Lemma 5.10
.

The points
\big(0,1;1\big)
,
\big(1,0;1\big)
,

\big(1,1;1\big)
belong to

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(1)\times\{1\}\subset\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]
.

Proof.

Straightforward calculation shows that

T_{s}\big(0,1;1\big)=(-1)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}
,

I_{s,1}\big(0,1;1\big)=

(-1)^{(p^{s}-3)/2}
,

I_{s,2}\big(0,1;1\big)=(-1)^{(p^{s}-3)/2}\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}
.
Hence
\mathcal{I}\big(0,1;1\big)=\big(-1,\frac{1}{2}\big)
.
Similarly we obtain
\mathcal{I}\big(1,0;1\big)=\big(\frac{1}{2},-1\big)
,

\mathcal{I}\big(1,1;1\big)=\big(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\big)
.
These vectors are nonzero modulo
p
.
∎

By Lemma
5.10
, the analytic vector-function
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{z}};1)
is nonzero at

(0,1;1)
, and its values generate the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over a neighborhood of
(0,1)
in

\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(1)
. Over a neighborhood of
(0,1)
,
the same line bundle can be defined differently. Consider the family of
elliptic curves
X({\boldsymbol{z}})
defined by the equation

y^{2}=t(t-z_{1})(t-z_{2})
. If the parameter
(z_{1},z_{2})
is close to
(0,1)
the curve

X({\boldsymbol{z}})
has a vanishing cycle denoted by
C_{0,1}
. The vector-function

(5.9)

\displaystyle I^{(C_{0,1})}({\boldsymbol{z}};1)\ :=\ \int_{C_{0,1}}\Big(\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})y}\,,\ \frac{1}{(t-z_{2})y}\Big)dt

is holomorphic at
(0,1)
, solves dynamical equations (
2.2
)
for
\lambda=1
, and
I^{(C)}(0,1;1)\neq 0
.
The values of
I^{(C)}\big({\boldsymbol{z}};1\big)
generate a line bundle denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{0,1}
over a neighborhood of
(0,1)
. The line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{0,1}
is invariant
with respect to the dynamical connection. The dynamical connection for
\lambda=1

does not have other invariant proper nontrivial subbundles near
(0,1)
since other
solutions of equations (
2.2
) for
\lambda=1
at (0,1) include
\log z_{1}
.
Hence our line bundle
\mathcal{L}
coincides with the line bundle

\mathcal{L}_{0,1}

Similarly, the elliptic curve
X({\boldsymbol{z}})
has a vanishing cycle denoted by
C_{1,0}

(z_{1},z_{2})
is close to
(1,0)
. The values of the nonzero vector-function

\displaystyle I^{(C_{1,0})}({\boldsymbol{z}};1)\ :=\ \int_{C_{1,0}}\Big(\frac{1}{(t-z_{1})y}\,,\ \frac{1}{(t-z_{2})y}\Big)dt

generate a line bundle denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{1,0}

(1,0)

\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}_{1,0}

(1,0)
in
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(1)

X({\boldsymbol{z}})

(z_{1},z_{2})

(1,1)

\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}_{1,1}

in
\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}_{*}(1)

Thus our global line bundle

5.8.
Monodromy

For an odd integer
\lambda
, the differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)
,
i=1,2
, define a flat dynamical
connection on the trivial bundle

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
The flat sections of the connection have the form

\displaystyle I^{(C)}({\boldsymbol{z}};\lambda,\mu)=\int_{C}t^{-\lambda/2}(t-z_{1})^{-1/2}(t-z_{2})^{-1/2}\Big(\frac{1}{t-z_{1}},\frac{1}{t-z_{2}}\Big)dt,

see (
1.1
). The monodromy of this connection does not depend on the choice of the odd integer
\lambda
and is isomorphic
to the monodromy of the Gauss-Manin connection on the bundle with fibers being the first homology groups
of elliptic curves
X({\boldsymbol{z}})
of the family defined by the equation

y^{2}=t(t-z_{1})(t-z_{2})
. It is classically known that this monodromy is irreducible. Hence the dynamical connection
defined by
\mathcal{D}_{i}(\lambda)
,
i=1,2
, over the field of complex numbers has no invariant line
subbundles. Thus the presence of our line subbundle

\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}^{(m)}[\Lambda]
, invariant with respect to the dynamical and
qKZ
connections,
is a specific
p
-adic feature.

Abstract
. In this short note, we study the variation of orbital integrals, as traces on the group algebra G G , under the deformation groupoid. We show that orbital integrals are continuous under the deformation. And we prove that the pairing between orbital integrals and K K -theory element of C r ∗ ​ ( G ) C^{*}_{r}(G) stays constant with respect to the deformation for regular group elements, but vary at singular elements.

The Connes tangent groupoid was applied
[

to study the

and formulate a new approach to the Connes-Kasparov conjecture. More precisely, let

At

\varphi_{t}(k,v)=k\exp_{G}(tv),\ (k_{1},v_{1},t)\cdot_{t}(k_{2},v_{2},t)=\varphi^{-1}_{t}\big(\varphi_{t}(k_{1},v_{1})\cdot\varphi_{t}(k_{2},v_{2})\big).

In this article, we study variations of traces on

Let

In this paper, we focus on the classical orbital integrals

2.
Cartan Motion Group

Let
K
be a connected compact Lie group and
\mathfrak{p}
is an even dimensional Eculid-ean space on which
K
acts. For simplicity, we assume that the
K
-action on
\mathfrak{p}
is spin so that we have the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{spin}(\mathfrak{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces SO(\mathfrak{p})}

Let
S_{\mathfrak{p}}=S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}\oplus S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded spin representation of
\mathrm{Cliff}(\mathfrak{p})
. In addition, we assume that the
K
-fixed part of
\mathfrak{p}
is trivial.

Definition 2.1
.

The semidirect product
K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
is the group whose underlying set is the Cartesian product
K\times\mathfrak{p}
, equipped with the product law

(k_{1},v_{1})\cdot_{0}(k_{2},v_{2})=\left(k_{1}k_{2},k_{2}^{-1}\cdot v_{1}+v_{2}\right),\quad k_{1},k_{2}\in K,\quad\text{and}\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{p},

which is called the
Cartan motion group
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
of
K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
, denoted by
C^{*}_{r}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
, is the completion of the convolution algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
in the norm obtained from the left regular representation of
C_{c}^{\infty}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
as bounded convolution operators on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
.

Fix the Haar measure on
K
so that the volume of
K
equals one. Together with a
K
-invariant measure on vector space
\mathfrak{p}
, we obtain a Haar measure on
K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
. If
f
is a smooth, compactly supported function on
K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
, we define its
Fourier transform
, a smooth function from
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
into the smooth functions on
K\times K
by the formula

\widehat{f}(\varphi)(k_{1},k_{2})=\int_{\mathfrak{p}}f(k_{1}k_{2}^{-1},v)\varphi(k_{2}^{-1}\cdot v)\;dv.

Here
\varphi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},k_{1},k_{2}\in K,v\in\mathfrak{p}
and
e
is the identity element in
K
.

For a fixed
\varphi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
, we shall think of
\widehat{f}(\varphi)
as an integral kernel and hence as a compact operator on
L^{2}(K)
. The Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
is therefore a function from
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
into
\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)
, where
\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)
denotes the space of compact operators on
L^{2}(K)
. The function is equivariant for the natural action of
K
on
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
and the conjugacy action of
K
on
\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)
induced from the right regular representation of
K
on
L^{2}(K)
. We define

\displaystyle C_{0}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\text{continuous functions from $\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}$ into $\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)$, vanishing at infinity}\right\}

and

C_{0}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)\right)^{K}=\left\{\text{$K$-equivariant functions in $C_{0}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)\right)$}\right\}.

The Fourier transform provides a concrete description of the (reduced) group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The Fourier transform
f\mapsto\widehat{f}
extends to a
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

C_{r}^{*}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})\cong C_{0}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)\right)^{K}

Proof.

See
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
. ∎

We study the
orbital integrals
on Cartan motion group. Let
T\subseteq K
be the maximal torus of
K
and
x\in T
be a regular element. In particular,
x
gives an endomorphism from
\mathfrak{p}
to itself such that
\det_{\mathfrak{p}}(\id-x)\neq 0
.
We write
\mathcal{O}_{x}
the conjugacy class of
(x,0)\in K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
.

Definition 2.4
.

For any
f\in C_{c}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
, we define the orbital integral

\tau_{x}(f)\colon=\int_{(k,v)\in\mathcal{O}_{x}}f(k,v)\;dkdv.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
x
is regular in
T
, then the orbital integral associated to
x
for the Cartan motion group equals

\displaystyle\tau_{x}(f)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\id-x)}\cdot\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{p}}f(k\cdot x\cdot k^{-1},v)\;dkdv.

Proof.

For any
(k,v)\in K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
, we have that

\displaystyle(k,v)\cdot_{0}(x,0)\cdot_{0}(k,v)^{-1}=

\displaystyle(k,v)\cdot_{0}(x,0)\cdot_{0}(k^{-1},-k\cdot v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k\cdot x,x^{-1}\cdot v)\cdot_{0}(k^{-1},-k\cdot v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k\cdot x\cdot k^{-1},kx^{-1}\cdot v-k\cdot v).

The lemma follows immediately from change of variable

v\mapsto kx^{-1}\cdot v-k\cdot v=kx^{-1}(\id-x)\cdot v.

∎

For any finite dimensional
K
-representation
E
, we denote by
\chi_{E}
the character of
E
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The determinant function
\det_{\mathfrak{p}}(\id-x)
has the following formula,

\mathrm{det}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\id-x)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\left(\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}}(x)-\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}}(x)\right)^{2},

where
\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\pm}}
is the character of
S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\pm}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be the Lie algebra of
T
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
be a fixed set of positive roots for the
T
-action on
\mathfrak{p}
. Suppose that
x=e^{H}
with
H\in\mathfrak{t}
. We compute that

\displaystyle\text{det}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\text{id}-e^{H})=

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}\left(1-e^{\alpha(H)}\right)\cdot\left(1-e^{-\alpha(H)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}\left(e^{\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\alpha(H)}{2}}\right)^{2}.

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional
K
-representation and
x
be a regular element in
T
. Suppose that
g(z)
is a
K
-invariant Schwartz function on
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
and
f\in C_{0}(K\ltimes\mathfrak{p})
is the inverse Fourier transform of

g(z)\cdot\id_{E}\in C_{0}\left(\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)\right)^{K},\quad z\in\widehat{\mathfrak{p}},

where by the Peter-Weyl theorem
\id_{E}
is an element of
\mathbb{K}\left(L^{2}(K)\right)
.
Then

\tau_{x}(f)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\frac{\chi_{E}(x)}{\left(\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}}(x)-\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}}(x)\right)^{2}}\cdot g(0).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\tau_{x}(f)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{det}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\id-x)}\cdot\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{p}}f(k\cdot x\cdot k^{-1},v)\;dkdv.

For any regular
z\in\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
, it determines a
K\ltimes\mathfrak{p}
-representation on
L^{2}(K)
given by

\left(\pi_{z}(x,v)s\right)(u)=e^{i\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{u}z,v\rangle}\cdot s(x^{-1}u),\quad u\in K,

where
s\in L^{2}(K)
. By the Fourier transform, we have that

\displaystyle f(k\cdot x\cdot k^{-1},v)=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}}g(z)\cdot\tr\left(\pi_{z}(kxk^{-1},v)\circ\id_{E}\right)\;dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{E}(x)\cdot\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}}\int_{K}g(z)\cdot e^{i\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{u}z,v\rangle}\;dudz

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tau_{x}(f)=

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{E}(x)}{\text{det}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\text{id}-x)}\cdot\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{p}}\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}}\int_{K}g(z)\cdot e^{i\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{u}z,v\rangle}\;dudzdkdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\frac{\chi_{V}(x)}{\left(\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}}(x)-\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}}(x)\right)^{2}}\cdot\int_{\mathfrak{p}}\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}}g(z)\cdot e^{i\langle z,v\rangle}\;dzdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\frac{\chi_{V}(x)}{\left(\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}}(x)-\chi_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}}(x)\right)^{2}}\cdot g(0).

∎

3.
Deformation to the Cartan motion groups

In this section, we study the limit of the orbital integral under the deformation to the Cartan motion groups. Let
G
be a connected, linear, real reductive Lie group and
K
be its maximal compact subgroup. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and
K
, and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
be the Cartan decomposition. Let
dg
be the standard Haar measure on
G
. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be the maximal abelian sub-algebra in
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
the positive Weyl chamber.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following identity holds

\int_{G}f(g)dg=\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(k\exp(v)k^{\prime}\right)\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\sinh(\alpha(v))\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime},

where
\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})
is the set of restricted roots of
\mathfrak{a}
-action on
\mathfrak{p}
and
n_{\alpha}
is the multiplicity.

Proof.

See
[
19
, Proposition 5. 28]
. ∎

For every non-zero real number
t
, we define a group
G_{t}
by using the global diffeomorphism
\varphi_{t}\colon K\times\mathfrak{p}\to G
given by

(k,v)\mapsto k\exp_{G}(tv).

We define a family of groups

\mathcal{G}=\begin{cases}G_{t},&t>0,\\
&\\
G_{0}=K\ltimes\mathfrak{p},&t=0.\end{cases}

The bijection

K\times\mathfrak{p}\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{G}

defined by the formula

(k,v,t)\mapsto\begin{cases}(k,v,0),&t=0,\\
&\\
\left(k\exp(tv),t\right),&t>0,\end{cases}

is a diffeomorphism. As a result, every smooth function
f
on
\mathcal{G}
has the following form

\begin{cases}f(k,v,0)=F(k,v,0),\\
&\\
f(k\exp(tv),t)=F(k,v,t),&t>0,\end{cases}

for some smooth function
F
on
K\times\mathfrak{p}\times[0,1]
.

Definition 3.2
.

For every positive real number
t
, we define the Haar measure
d_{t}g
on
G_{t}
by the following formula

\displaystyle\int_{G_{t}}f(g,t)\;d_{t}g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{\dim\mathfrak{p}-\dim\mathfrak{a}}}\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(k\exp(tv)k^{\prime},t\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\sinh(\alpha(tv))\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
f
is a smooth function on
\mathcal{G}
with compact support and
x\in K
is a regular element. Then

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{G_{t}}f(g\cdot x\cdot g^{-1},t)\;d_{t}g=\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{p}}f\left(kxk^{-1},w-\text{Ad}_{kxk^{-1}}w,0\right)\;dkdw.

Proof.

By Definition
3.2
of Haar measure on
G_{t}
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{G_{t}}f(g\cdot x\cdot g^{-1},t)\;d_{t}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(k^{\prime}\exp(tv)kxk^{-1}\exp(-tv)k^{\prime-1},t\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\frac{\sinh(\alpha(tv))}{t}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(\exp(t\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)k^{\prime}kxk^{-1}\exp(-tv)k^{\prime-1},t\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\frac{\sinh(\alpha(tv))}{t}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(\exp(t\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)\exp(-t\Ad_{k^{\prime}kxk^{-1}k^{\prime-1}}\circ Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)k^{\prime}kxk^{-1}k^{\prime-1},t\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\frac{\sinh(\alpha(tv))}{t}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(\exp(t\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)\exp(-t\Ad_{kxk^{-1}}\circ Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)kxk^{-1},t\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\frac{\sinh(\alpha(tv))}{t}\right)^{n_{\alpha}}\;dkdvdk^{\prime},

where in the last equation we have changed
k^{\prime}k
to
k
. Let
w=\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v\in\mathfrak{p}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}f\left(\exp(t\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)\exp(-t\Ad_{kxk^{-1}}\circ Ad_{k^{\prime}}v)kxk^{-1},t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(kxk^{-1},w-\Ad_{kxk^{-1}}w,0\right).

Moreover, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\sinh(\alpha(tv))}{t}=\alpha(v).

Hence, by the compact support assumption on
f
, we can commute the integral with the limit with respect to
t
and compute

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{G_{t}}f(g\cdot x\cdot g^{-1},t)\;d_{t}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\int_{K}f\left(kxk^{-1},\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v-\Ad_{kxk^{-1}}\circ\Ad_{k^{\prime}}v,0\right)\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\alpha(v)\right)^{n_{\alpha}}dkdvdk^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{p}}f\left(kxk^{-1},w-\Ad_{kxk^{-1}}w,0\right)\;dwdk,

where the last equation follows from the fact that the term

\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{a})}\left(\alpha(v)\right)^{n_{\alpha}}

is the Jacobian of the map
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\times K\to\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

As we assume in Section
2
that the

\mathfrak{p}

Lemma 3.4
.

We have the following

\lim_{t\to 0}\kappa_{t}^{s}(k\exp(tv))=\kappa_{0}^{s}(k,v).

Proof.

It is proved in
[
5
, Theorem 2.48]
that the heat kernel on a compact manifold is smooth with respect to the parameter

, indexing a smooth family of Riemannian metrics. The same proof extends to

with analogous estimates. See
[
14
, Lemma 2.2, Theorem 2.2]
.
∎

We define a function
\Phi\colon\mathcal{G}\to C
by

\begin{cases}\Phi(k,v,0)=s^{-\dim G/2}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{-|v|^{2}}{16s}\right),\\
&\\
\Phi(k\exp(tv),t)=s^{-\dim G/2}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{-|v|^{2}}{16s}\right),&t>0.\end{cases}

By the estimate in
[
7
]
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for all
t\in[0,1]
,

(3.1)

\|\kappa_{t}^{s}(g)\|\leq C\cdot\Phi(g),\quad g\in G_{t}.

By the same computation in Theorem
3.3
, as
\Phi(g)
is independent of
t
, the integral

\int_{G_{t}}f(g\cdot x\cdot g^{-1},t)\;d_{t}g

is uniformly bounded for all
t\in[0,1]
. Hence, the following theorem
follows from the dominated convergence theorem:

Theorem 3.5
.

For any

K

We have defined the deformation groupoid

\{G_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}

C^{*}
-algebra of each

C^{*}

Let
E

K

and a family of Dirac operators

acting on

\left[L^{2}(G_{t})\otimes S_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes E\right]^{K}.

We denote by
D^{E}_{t,\pm}

\left[L^{2}(G_{t})\otimes S^{\pm}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes E\right]^{K}.

We consider the following Connes-Moscovici projection
[

P^{E}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-D^{E}_{t,-}D^{E}_{t,+}}&e^{\frac{-D^{E}_{t,-}D^{E}_{t,+}}{2}}\cdot\frac{(1-e^{-D^{E}_{t,-}D^{E}_{t,+}})}{D^{E}_{t,-}D^{E}_{t,+}}\cdot D^{E}_{t,-}\\
e^{-\frac{D^{E}_{t,+}D^{E}_{t,-}}{2}}\cdot D^{E}_{t,+}&1-e^{-D^{E}_{t,+}D^{E}_{t,-}}\end{pmatrix}

which is an idempotent and

[P^{E}_{t}]-\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\

defines a generator in

Theorem 4.1

If

0&1\end{pmatrix}

Proof I:

Theorem 4.5
.

For the
L^{2}
-trace, we have that

\tau_{e}\left([P^{E}_{t}]\right)=t^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\tau_{e}\left([P^{E}]\right).

Proof.

We follow the approach in
[
10
]
. First fix a volume form

\omega\in\Lambda^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{*}.

For any top degree form
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{*}
, we define
\lambda([\mathfrak{p}])\in\mathbb{C}
by

\lambda=\lambda([\mathfrak{p}])\cdot\omega.

Then we identify the highest weight
\mu
of
E
as an element in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and define

\beta_{\mu}\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{p}^{*}

by the following

\beta_{\mu}(X,Y)=\mu\left([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\right),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}.

By
[
10
, Proposition 7.1 A]
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tau_{e}\left([P^{E}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{formal degree of the discrete series representation determined by $E$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\cdot 2^{n}}\left(\Lambda^{n}\beta_{\mu}\right)\left([\mathfrak{p}]\right).

where
n=\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}
.

The family of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
can be identified with
\mathfrak{g}
by the following map: for any
X=X_{\mathfrak{k}}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, we define

X^{t}=X_{\mathfrak{k}}+\frac{1}{t}\cdot X_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{t}.

One can check that

(4.2)

\begin{cases}[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}_{t}}=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}&X,Y\in\mathfrak{k},\\
[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}_{t}}=t^{2}\cdot[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}&X,Y\in\mathfrak{p},\\
[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}_{t}}=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}&X\in\mathfrak{k},Y\in\mathfrak{p}.\\
\end{cases}

Applying the formula (
4.1
) to
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
, we obtain the formula of
\beta^{t}_{\mu}
from (
4.2
)

\beta_{\mu}^{t}=t^{2}\cdot\beta_{\mu}.

Therefore, we conclude

\tau_{e}\left([P^{E}_{t}]\right)=t^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\tau_{e}\left([P^{E}]\right).

∎

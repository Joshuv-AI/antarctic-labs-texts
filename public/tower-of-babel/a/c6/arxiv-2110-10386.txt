A uniform version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence for extremal Kähler metrics on polarized toric manifolds
Yasufumi Nitta, Shunsuke Saito

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to solve a uniform version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture for polarized toric manifolds. Also, we show a combinatorial sufficient condition for uniform relative K-polystability.

Primary 53C25,Secondary 32Q26,14M25

1.
Introduction

Let
(X,L)
be an
n
-dimensional polarized algebraic manifold. We denote
\mathcal{H}(X,L)
by the set of all Kähler metrics of
X
in
c_{1}(L)
. A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)
is called a
Calabi’s extremal Kähler metric
if it is a critical point of the Calabi functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(X,L)\ni\alpha\mapsto\int_{X}(s(\alpha)-\overline{s})^{2}\,\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}\in\mathbf{R},

where
s(\alpha)
is a scalar curvature of
\alpha
and
\overline{s}=-n(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1})/(L^{n})
is its average.
Calabi showed in
[
14
]
that this condition is equivalent to that
\operatorname{grad}_{\omega}^{(1,0)}(s(\omega)-\overline{s})
is a holomorphic vector field on
X
. Extremal Kähler metrics have been widely studied as canonical Kähler metrics containing constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics and in particular Kähler-Einstein metrics. Especially, the existence problem of extremal Kähler metrics is known as the
Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture
, and is one of the central problem in Kähler geometry.

The Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture predicts that the existence of an extremal Kähler metric is equivalent to the stability of
(X,L)
in some sense of geometric invariant theory. Székelyhidi introduced in
[
73
]
the notion of
relative K-polystability
as a candidate of such a stability condition.
Roughly speaking, relative K-polystability requires the positivity of the
relative Donaldson-Futaki invariant

DF_{V}>0
for any nontrivial test object. Here
V
is the extremal Kähler vector field of
(X,L)
(
[
40
]
), and test object is a special kind of degeneration of
(X,L)
, which is called a
test configuration
.

On the other hand, calculations by Apostolov-Calderbank-Gauduchon-Tønnesen-Friedman
[
4
]
suggest that relative K-polystability might not be sufficient to ensure the existence of a extremal Kähler metric, and leads to an expectation that we need to strengthen the notion of relative K-polystability.
For strengthenings, the following two approaches are known:

(a)

Requiring that
DF_{V}
is bounded from below by a multiple of some ‘norm’ of test configurations (
[
72
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
12
,
13
]
).

(b)

Testing
DF_{V}>0
for more general test objects than test configurations (
[
76
]
,
[
63
]
).

In this paper, we will show the equivalence of both approaches for polarized toric manifolds. As a consequence, we will solve a uniform version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture for polarized toric manifolds.

An
n
-dimensional polarized toric manifold
(X,L)
corresponds to an
n
-dimensional integral Delzant polytope
P\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
which is written by an intersection of half spaces

(1.1)

\displaystyle P=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
r
is the number of facets of
P
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
,
d_{j}\in\mathbf{Z}
and each
\lambda_{j}
is a primitive vector.
Then, any toric Kähler metric representing
c_{1}(L)
corresponds to a smooth strictly convex function on
P^{\circ}
which satisfies the
Guillemin’s boundary condition
(see Section
4.2
).
Also, any toric test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
for
(X,L)
corresponds to a rational piecewise affine function
f
on
P
, and the relative Donaldson-Futaki invariant
DF_{V}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
can be written as

\displaystyle L_{V}(f)=\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma-\int_{P}(\overline{s}+V)f\,dx,

where
\sigma
is the Borel measure on
\partial P
which is the multiple of the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure by
|\lambda_{j}|^{-1}
on each facet
F_{j}=\{x\in P\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}=0\}
(see
[
80
]
).

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all strictly convex functions on
P^{\circ}
which satisfy the Guillemin’s boundary condition.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{PL}^{\mathbf{Q}}
be the set of all rational piecewise affine functions on
P
. As mentioned above, we can regard
\mathcal{C}_{PL}^{\mathbf{Q}}
as the set of all toric test configurations for
(X,L)
.
Also, we define
\mathcal{C}_{PL}
to be the set of all piecewise affine functions on
P
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
be the set of all continuous convex functions on
P
which is smooth in the interior.
Further, let
P^{\ast}
be the union of relative interiors of faces of
P
up to codimension one, and
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
be the set of all continuous convex functions on
P^{\ast}
which are integrable on
\partial P
with respect to the measure
\sigma
.
We fix a point
x_{0}\in P
in the interior, and denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}
the set of all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
satisfying the
normalized condition

\inf_{P^{\ast}}f=f(x_{0})=0
.
Also, set
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}
for any
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}_{*}
.
Finally, for each
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
we denote the
J
-norm
by
\|f\|_{J}
(see Section
5.4
).

Under the notations above, our first main result is the following equivalence.

Theorem 1.0.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be one of the spaces
\mathcal{E}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
,
\mathcal{C}_{PL}
,
\mathcal{C}_{PL}^{\mathbf{Q}}
,

or

.
Then the followings are equivalent.

(b)_{\mathcal{F}}

There exists a

such that

for any

(J)_{\mathcal{F}}

There exists a

(\mathrm{K})_{\mathcal{C}_{\ast}}

We say that

.

(The toric Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence)
.

Let
(X,L)
be an
n
-dimensional polarized toric manifold.
Let

be a maximal algebraic torus of

, and

(1)

(X,L)
admits an
S
-invariant extremal Kähler metric.

(2)

(X,L)

(3)

The relative K-energy of

is

-coercive.

The implications (1)
\Rightarrow

and
(b)_{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}

Corollary 1.0.3
.

Let

,

(2)

We mention the very recent papers

As a simple application of Theorem

Corollary 1.0.4
.

Let
(X_{1},L_{1})
and
(X_{2},L_{2})
be polarized toric manifolds, and set

and

Since the product metric of extremal Kähler metrics is also an extremal Kähler metric,

is uniformly relatively K-polystable by Theorem

Next, we give a combinatorial sufficient condition for unirofm relative K-polystability.

.

Let

\displaystyle V=0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{s}<\frac{n+1}{d_{x_{0}}},

\displaystyle V\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{s}+\max_{P}V\leq\frac{n+1}{d_{x_{0}}}.

In case that

\displaystyle V=0

2.2.
Extremal Kähler metrics

In this section, we recall the definition of extremal Kähler metrics.
Let
(X,L)
be an
n
-dimensional polarized manifold, and fix a maximal algebraic torus
T
of
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
the set of all
S
-invariant Kähler metrics
\omega
such that
\omega\in c_{1}(L)
.
By setting
\overline{s}=-n(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1})/(L^{n})
, we define
\Phi\colon\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}\to\mathbf{R}
by

\displaystyle\Phi(\omega)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{X}(S(\omega)-\overline{s})^{2}\omega^{n}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{\omega}(X)}\int_{X}(s(\omega)-\overline{s})^{2}\omega^{n},

where
\mathrm{vol}_{\omega}(X)=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
and
\overline{s}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{X}s(\omega)\omega^{n}
.
Note that
\mathrm{vol}_{\omega}(X)
and
\overline{s}
is independent of choice of
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
since
\mathrm{vol}_{\omega}(X)=(L^{n})=\langle c_{1}(L)^{n},[X]\rangle
and
\overline{s}=-n(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1})/(L^{n})
.
We call
\Phi
the Calabi functional.

Definition 2.2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
is called an extremal Kähler metric if
\omega
is a critical point of
\Phi
.

Kähler-Einstein metrics are extremal metrics, and (more generally) constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics in
\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
are typical examples of extremal Kähler metrics.
The following fact is well-known.

Proposition 2.2.2

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
is an extremal Kähler metric if and only if
\mathrm{grad}_{g}(s(\omega)-\overline{s})
is a real holomorphic vector field on
X
.

We fix
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
.
Set

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(X,\omega,\mathbf{R})_{0}^{S}=\{f\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})\mid\int_{X}f\omega^{n}=0\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}(X,\omega)_{0}=\{u\in C^{\infty}(X,\omega,\mathbf{R})_{0}^{S}\mid 4\pi J\mathrm{grad}_{g}u\in\mathfrak{s}\}.

Conversely, for each
W\in\mathfrak{s}
there exists unique
u_{\omega}^{W}\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{S}
such that

\displaystyle i_{W}\omega=-du_{\omega}^{W},\quad\int_{X}u_{\omega}^{W}\omega^{n}=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle-du_{\omega}^{W}=g(JJ\mathrm{grad}_{g}u_{\omega}^{W},\cdot)=i_{2\pi J\mathrm{grad}_{g}u_{\omega}^{W}}\omega

and
2\pi J\mathrm{grad}_{g}u_{\omega}^{W}=W\in\mathfrak{s}
, which implies
u_{\omega}^{W}\in\mathfrak{s}(X,\omega)_{0}
.
Hence the correspondence
\mathfrak{s}\ni W\mapsto u_{\omega}^{W}\in\mathfrak{s}(X,\omega)_{0}
gives a Lie algebra isomorphism.
The following is also ovbious.

Proposition 2.2.3
.

A Kähler metric
\omega\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
is extremal if and only if
s(\omega)-\overline{s}\in\mathfrak{s}(X,\omega)_{0}
.

The Futaki invariant and the extremal Kähler vector field

2.4.
Riemannian geometry of the space of Kähler potentials

In

n

\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
.
By the well-known
dd^{c}
-lemma,
\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}
can be identified the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{S}=\mathcal{H}(X,\omega_{0})^{S}\coloneqq\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{S}\mid\omega_{\varphi}\coloneqq\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\varphi>0\}.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{S}
, the tangent space
T_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}^{S}
at
\varphi
can be identified with
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{S}
.
Then the natural Riemannian metric on
T_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}^{S}
is given by

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\varphi}\coloneqq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{X}uv\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

Let
\{\varphi^{t}\}
be a smooth path in
\mathcal{H}^{S}
, and
\{u^{t}\}
be a smooth vector filed of
\mathcal{H}^{S}
along the path
\{\varphi^{t}\}
, namely,
u^{t}\in T_{\varphi^{t}}\mathcal{H}^{S}=C^{\infty}(X,\mathbf{R})^{S}
for each
t
.
Then the Levi-Civita connection is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\varphi}^{t}}u^{t}=\dot{u}^{t}-\frac{1}{2}\langle d\dot{\varphi}^{t},du^{t}\rangle_{g_{\varphi^{t}}}.

This immidiately implies that
t\mapsto\varphi^{t}
is a geodesic if and only if
\nabla_{\dot{\varphi}^{t}}\dot{\varphi}^{t}=0
, or equivalently

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ddot{\varphi}^{t}-\frac{1}{2}|d\dot{\varphi}^{t}|_{g_{\varphi^{t}}}^{2}=0.

As discovered by Semmes
[
69
]
and Donaldson
[
33
]
, the above equation can be understood as a complex Monge-Ampère equation as follows.
For each
T\in(0,\infty]
, let
A=\{w\in\mathbf{C}\mid e^{-T/2}\leq|w|\leq 1\}
.
Here we propose
A=\{w\in\mathbf{C}\mid 0<|w|\leq 1\}
if
T=\infty
.
By setting
w=\exp(-t/2+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\theta)
, we have
t=-\log|w|^{2}
and

\displaystyle e^{-T/2}\leq|w|\leq 1\iff 0\leq t\leq T.

Let
\overline{X}=X\times A
.
We regard
\overline{X}
as a complex manifold with boundary.
Let
\pi\colon\overline{X}\to X
be the natural projection.
For each smooth path
\{\varphi^{t}\}
in
\mathcal{H}^{S}
, we define
\Phi\colon\overline{X}\to\mathbf{R}
by

\displaystyle\Phi(x,w)\coloneqq\varphi^{-\log|w|^{2}}(x).

Theorem 2.4.1

(
[
69
]
)
.

We denote the
d
and
d^{c}
-operator on
\overline{X}
by
\overline{d}
and
\overline{d}^{c}
, respectively.
Then

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}\omega_{0}+\overline{d}\,\overline{d}^{c}\Phi)^{n+1}=(n+1)\left(\ddot{\varphi}^{t}-\frac{1}{2}|d\dot{\varphi}^{t}|_{g_{\varphi^{t}}}^{2}\right)\pi^{*}\omega_{\varphi^{t}}^{n}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{dw\wedge d\overline{w}}{|w|^{2}}.

In particular,
\{\varphi^{t}\}
is a geodesic if and only if
\Phi
satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}\omega_{0}+\overline{d}\,\overline{d}^{c}\Phi)^{n+1}=0.

Unfortunately, the Dirichlet problem of the equation (
2.2
) does not usually have a smooth solution (see
[
53
]
).
This means that in general one cannot find a smooth geodesic segment connecting two points in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{S}
.
On the other hand, Chen
[
18
]
proved that the Dirichlet problem of (
2.2
) always has a unique solution in a weak sense (
[
8
]
).
After successive refinement in
[
11
]
and
[
26
]
, it is proved that the weak solution has
C^{1,1}
regularity.

\displaystyle H(\varphi)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{X}\log\left(\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

The property (P2) is obtained by the compactness theorem in
[
10
, Theorem 2.17]
and the lower semicontinuity of

.
The property (P3) is due to
[

, Theorem 3.6]
.
The property (P4) is already showed in Proposition

.
The property (P6) can be omitted (see Remark 4.8 of
[

]
).
The property (P7) is easily proved.

We need to check the property (P5).
Let
\mathfrak{aut}(X,V)=\{W\in\mathfrak{aut}(X)\mid L_{V}W=0\}
.
Then
\mathfrak{aut}(X,V)
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{aut}(X)
.
We denote by

the connected Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)_{0}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{aut}(X,V)
.
Let
\omega_{i}
,
i=1,2
be two
S
-invariant extremal Kähler metrics in
\mathcal{H}(X,L)^{S}

\displaystyle K_{i}=\mathrm{Isom}(X,\omega_{i})_{0}.

By a theorem of Calabi

]

\mathrm{Aut}(X,V)_{0}
is a reductive and each

\mathrm{Aut}(X,V)_{0}
.
Since
\omega_{i}
is
S
-invariant, we have

and that

is a maximal compact torus of both
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
.
Hence by
[

K_{2}=\tau K_{1}\tau^{-1}
for some

By Berman-Berndttson’s uniqueness theorem of extremal Kähler metrics, we can find

(see the proof of

Then we have

in

Furthermore, by

Hence there exists

By setting

and

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=f^{*}\omega_{2}=(k_{1}\tau_{1})^{*}\omega_{2}=\tau_{1}^{*}k_{1}^{*}\omega_{2}=\tau_{1}^{*}\omega_{2}.

3.1.
Test configurations

Let
(X,L)

n

Definition 3.1.1
.

A test configuration for
(X,L)
of exponent
r

(1)

a scheme
\mathcal{X}
with a
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-action,

(2)

a
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-equivariant flat and proper morphism of schemes

\mathbf{G}_{m}
acts on
\mathbf{A}^{1}
by multiplication,

(3)

a
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-linearlized
\pi
-very ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
\mathcal{X}
, and

(4)

an isomorphism
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1})\cong(X,L^{r})

Here
\mathcal{X}_{t}

\pi
over
t\in\mathbf{A}^{1}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}

A test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is called product if
\mathcal{X}
is isomorphic to the fiber product of
X
and
\mathbf{A}^{1}
over
\mathbf{A}^{1}

\mathbf{G}_{m}
acts only on the second factor.
A test configuration

\mathcal{X}
is a normal variety.
For an algebraic subgroup
G

G

with a lifted
G
-action on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
which preserves each fiber, commutes with the fiber
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-action of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
, and coincides with
G
-action when actiong on
(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1})\cong(X,L^{r})
.

For our purpose, it is convenient to compactify test configurations.
Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
be a test configuration for
(X,L)
of exponent
r
.
The
\mathbf{G}_{m}
-action on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
and the isomorphism

induces an equivariant trivialization
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})|_{\mathbf{G}_{m}}\cong(X\times\mathbf{G}_{m},p_{1}^{\ast}L^{r})

is the projection to the first factor, and
\mathbf{G}_{m}

Then we can compactify

by gluing it with the product
(X\times(\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{0\}),p_{1}^{\ast}L^{r})
along their respective open sets
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0}

The resulting

\mathbf{G}_{m}
-equivariant flat family over
\mathbf{P}^{1}
with fibers isomorphic to
(X,L^{r})
except over 0.
We call
(\overline{\mathcal{X}},\overline{\mathcal{L}})

the canonical compactification
of the test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
.
Note that the action of
\mathbf{G}_{m}
on the
\infty
-fiber

Example 3.1.2
.

Since

for any sufficiently divisible

Let

be the centralizer of a subset

in
\mathrm{GL}(H^{0}(X,L^{m}))

For any morphism

we define

as the Zariski closure of the set

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathbf{G}_{m}}\lambda^{\vee}(\tau)(\Phi_{m}(X))\times\{\tau\}

Example 3.1.3

The set of test configurations for

admits two natural operations: translations and scalings.

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
be a test configuration for
(X,L)
.

(1)

Let

(2)

Associated to a test configuration, the Donaldson-Futaki invariant is defined as follows.
Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
be a test configuration for
(X,L)
of exponent
r

By the flatness of

\displaystyle N_{rm}\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{C}}H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0}^{m})=\dim_{\mathbf{C}}H^{0}(X,L^{rm})

for sufficiently large

Moreover, the asymptotic Riemann-Roch theorem tells us that

\displaystyle N_{m}=a_{0}m^{n}+a_{1}m^{n-1}+O(m^{n-2}),

where

\displaystyle a_{0}=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!},\quad a_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1})}{(n-1)!}.

Note that

\displaystyle\overline{s}=-n\frac{(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1})}{(L^{n})}=-2\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}.

Since

Let

\displaystyle w_{m}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{m}}\lambda_{i}^{(m)}=b_{0}m^{n+1}+b_{1}m^{n}+O(m^{n-1})

for sufficiently large

divisible by

We then consider the coeffients

\displaystyle\frac{w_{m}}{mN_{m}}=F_{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})+F_{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})m^{-1}+O(m^{-2}),

\displaystyle F_{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\frac{b_{0}}{a_{0}},\quad F_{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\frac{a_{0}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{0}}{a_{0}^{2}}.

.

When the test configuration

\displaystyle w_{rm}

\displaystyle=\chi(\overline{\mathcal{X}},\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{m})-\chi(X,L^{rm})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{n+1})}{r^{n+1}(n+1)!}(rm)^{n+1}-\frac{1}{2r^{n}n!}\left((K_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{n})+2r^{n}(L^{n})\right)(rm)^{n}

(2)

left-continuous:
F^{\lambda}R_{m}=\bigcap_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}F^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m}
,

(3)

multiplicative:
F^{\lambda}R_{m}\cdot F^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m^{\prime}}\subset F^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}R_{m+m^{\prime}}
, and

(4)

linearly bounded: there exist
e_{-},e_{+}\in\mathbf{Z}
such that
F^{me_{-}}R_{m}=R_{m}
and
F^{me_{+}}R_{m}=\{0\}
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbf{Z}
-filtration if
F^{\lambda}R_{m}=F^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}R_{m}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
and
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
A filtration
FR
is
T
-equivariant if
F^{\lambda}R_{m}
is
T
-invariant subspace of
R_{m}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
.

Definition 3.4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F^{\lambda}R_{m}\}
be a
\mathbf{Z}
-filtration of
R
.

(1)

The Rees algebra of
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbf{C}[t]
-algebra defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rees}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}}t^{-\lambda}F^{\lambda}R_{m}.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is finitely generated if its Rees algebra is a finitely generated graded
\mathbf{C}[t]
-algebra.

(2)

The associated graded algebra of
\mathcal{F}
is a graded
\mathbf{C}
-algebra defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Gr}_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}/t\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}}F^{\lambda}R_{m}/F^{\lambda+1}R_{m}.

Given a finitely generated
\mathbf{Z}
-filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{F^{\lambda}R_{m}\}
, one can construct a test configuration for
(X,L)
as follows.
Let
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\mathrm{Rees}(\mathcal{F})
be the Rees algebra of the filtration
FR
.
Then
\mathcal{R}
is a torsion free
\mathbf{C}[t]
-algebra.
Since
\mathbf{C}[t]
is a principal ideal domain,
\mathcal{R}
is flat over
\mathbf{C}[t]
.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\Proj\mathcal{R},\quad\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(1),

and
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbf{A}^{1}
be the structure morphism.
Then
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is a flat family over
\mathbf{A}^{1}
.
Further, for any

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{a}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\Spec(\mathbf{C}[t]/(t-a)\mathbf{C}[t])

\displaystyle=\Proj(\mathcal{R}/(t-a)\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Proj R&a\neq 0,\\
\Proj\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{R}&a=0.\end{cases}

Hence
(\mathcal{X}_{a},\mathcal{L}_{a})=(X,L)
for any

and

is a test configuration for
(X,L)
.

(Test configurations)

Example 3.4.4

(Translations and scalings)
.

Let

3.5.
Limit measures

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized manifold and
\mathcal{F}
a filtration of
R=R(X,L)
.
For each
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
, we define a Borel probability measure
\nu_{m}
on
\mathbf{R}
by

\displaystyle\nu_{m}\coloneqq-\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{N_{m}}\dim F^{m\lambda}R_{m}\right),

where the derivative is taken in the sense of distributions.
We call it
the normalized weight measure
.
By the linearly boundedness of
\mathcal{F}
,
\nu_{m}
has uniformly bounded support.
To describe its limit, to each
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
, we define a graded subalgebra
R^{(\lambda)}
of
R(X,L)
by

\displaystyle R^{(\lambda)}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}F^{m\lambda}R_{m}.

The volume
of
R^{(\lambda)}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(R^{(\lambda)})\coloneqq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{N_{m}}\dim F^{m\lambda}R_{m}.

Theorem 3.5.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

The sequence of normalized weight measures
\{\nu_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
converges weakly to the probability measure

\displaystyle\mathrm{DH}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq-\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathrm{vol}(R^{(\lambda)}).

We call
\mathrm{DH}(\mathcal{F})

the Duistermaat-Heckman measure
of the filtration
\mathcal{F}
.

Example 3.5.2

(Test configurations)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
be a test configuration for
(X,L)
of exponent
1
and
\mathcal{F}
the filtration of
R
as in Example
3.4.4
.
By
[
68
, Lemma 8.5]
, the normalized weight measure is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{N_{m}}\dim F^{m\lambda}R_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}}\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0}^{m})_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda/m}

for any

.
Hence the limit coincides with the Duistermaat-Heckman measure:

\displaystyle\mathrm{DH}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{DH}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}).

4.
Convex Preliminaries

4.1.
Polarized toric manifolds

In this subsection, we briefly review the definition and construction of polarized toric manifolds.
See
[
42
]
for details.

A
2n
-dimensional symplectic toric manifold is a
2n
-dimensional compact connected symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
endowed with an effective Hamiltonian action of an
n
-dimensional compact torus
S=(S^{1})^{n}
and the moment map
\mu\colon X\to M_{\mathbf{R}}
.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a
2n
-dimensional symplectic toric manifold.
According to the convexity theorem of Atiyah
[
6
]
and Guillemin-Sternberg
[
44
]
, the image of the moment map
P\coloneqq\mu(X)\subset M_{\mathbf{R}}
is a convex polytope obtained as the convex hull of the image of the fixed points of the
S
-action.
We call
P
the
moment polytope
.
Further, since
(X,\omega)
is toric,
P
satisfies the following special conditions, usually called the
Delzant condition
:

(1)

There are exactly
n
edges meeting at each vertex.

(2)

Each edge meeting at the vertex
v\in P
is of the form
v+tu_{i}
,
t\geq 0
, where
u_{i}\in N
is primitive.

(3)

For each vertex, the corresponding
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
generate the lattice of
N_{\mathbf{R}}
over
\mathbf{Z}
.

A convex polytope satisfiying the conditions above is called a
Delzant polytope
.
In
[
31
]
, Delzant showed that there is a one to one correspondence between isomorphism classes of symplectic toric manifolds and Delzant polytopes.
More precisely, for each Delzant polytope
P
, one can construct in a canonical way a symplectic toric manifold in such a way that
P
may be identified with the moment polytope.
This construction is known as the
Delzant construction
.
Let
P
be a Delzant polytope and
(X_{P},\omega_{P})
the corresponding symplectic toric manifold with the moment map
\mu_{P}\colon X_{P}\to M_{\mathbf{R}}
.
By the Delzant construction, there is an
S
-invariant complex structure
J_{P}
compatible with
\omega_{P}
.
In particular,
(X_{P},\omega_{P},J_{P})
is a Kähler manifold.
Since the
S
-action preserves the complex structure
J_{P}
, it can be canonically extended to an action of
T\coloneqq S^{\mathbf{C}}=(\mathbf{G}_{m})^{n}
preserving
J_{P}
(
[
45
, Theorem 4.4]
).
Then
X_{P}
contains
T
as an open dense orbit, and hence
X_{P}
has a structure of a nonsingular toric variety with the fan associated to the polytope
P
(
[
54
, Lemma 9.2]
).

Let

(4.1)

\displaystyle P=\{x\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\}

be the facet representaion of
P
.
Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the natural pairing between
N_{\mathbf{R}}
and
M_{\mathbf{R}}
,
r
is the number of facets of
P
,
\lambda_{j}\in N
,
d_{j}\in\mathbf{R}
, and each
\lambda_{j}
is primitive.
Let
P^{\circ}
denote the interior of
P
.
Then
X_{P}^{\circ}\coloneqq\mu_{P}^{-1}(P^{\circ})
is a dense open subset of
X_{P}
where the
S
-action is free.
This coincides with the open dense orbit of the
T
-action described above.
Also, for any
k
-dimensional face
F
of
P
,
\mu_{P}^{-1}(F)
is an
T
-invariant
k
-dimensional connected complex submanifold of
X_{P}
.
In particular, if
F
is a facet of
P
then
\mu_{P}^{-1}(F)
is a
T
-invariant prime divisor of
X_{P}
.
For each
j=1,\ldots,r
, let
F_{j}
denote the facet of
P
defined by

\displaystyle F_{j}=\{x\in P\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}=0\},

and
D_{j}\coloneqq\mu_{P}^{-1}(F_{j})
.
By setting
c_{j}
the Poincare dual of
D_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle[\omega_{P}]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}c_{j}

as a de Rham cohomology class.
If the Delzant polytope
P
is
integral
, that is, each vertex of
P
belongs to
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, then
d_{j}\in\mathbf{Z}
(
j=1,\ldots,r
) and hence
[\omega_{P}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z})
.
Hence, in this case there exists a
T
-equivariant holomorphic line bundle
L_{P}
over
X_{P}
which satisfies
c_{1}(L_{P})=[\omega_{P}]
.
By the Kodaira embedding theorem,
L_{P}
is an ample line bundle over
X_{P}
.
We call
(X_{P},L_{P})
a
polarized toric manifold associated to
P
.

4.2.
Symplectic potentials

In this subsection, we quickly review differential-geometric aspects of toric Kähler manifolds.
For details, see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
43
]
.

Let
P\subset M_{\mathbf{R}}
be an
n
-dimensional integral Delzant polytope, and
(X_{P},L_{P})
the polarized toric manifold corresponding to
P
.
We choose an
S
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in c_{1}(L_{P})
.
Since
X_{P}^{\circ}
is holomorphically isomorphic to
T
, there is an
S
-invariant smooth function
\phi\colon T\to\mathbf{R}
so that

\displaystyle\omega=dd^{c}\phi

on
X_{P}^{\circ}
(
[
43
, Theorem 4.3]
).
Through the identification
X_{P}^{\circ}\cong(\mathbf{C}^{\times})^{n}\cong\mathbf{R}^{n}\times S
, we can regard
\phi
as a smooth function defined on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Remark 4.2.1
.

Let
\tau=(\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{n})
,
\tau^{i}=\exp((-1/2)y^{i}+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\theta^{i})
(
i=1,\ldots,n
) denote the coordinate of
T
.
Then, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\tau^{i}}{\tau^{i}}=-\frac{1}{2}dy^{i}+2\pi\sqrt{-1}d\theta^{i},\quad\frac{d\bar{\tau}^{i}}{\bar{\tau}^{i}}=-\frac{1}{2}dy^{i}-2\pi\sqrt{-1}d\theta^{i}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\tau^{i}\wedge d\bar{\tau}^{i}}{|\tau^{i}|^{2}}=dy^{i}\wedge d\theta^{i}.

Further, since

\displaystyle\tau^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau^{i}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{4\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{i}},\quad\bar{\tau}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\tau}^{i}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{4\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{i}},

we obtain

\displaystyle dd^{c}\phi=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial\tau^{i}\partial\bar{\tau}^{j}}d\tau^{i}\wedge d\bar{\tau}^{i}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}\frac{d\tau^{i}}{\tau^{i}}\wedge\frac{d\bar{\tau}^{i}}{\bar{\tau}^{j}}

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(T,\mathbf{R})^{S}
.
In particular,
dd^{c}\phi
is a Kähler form on
T
if and only if
\phi
is a strictly convex function on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

By using the moment map, one can describe
\phi
in terms of a convex function on
P
, which is called a symplectic potential.
Indeed, up to an additive constant, the derivative
\nabla\phi
coincides with the moment map for the Kähler metric
\omega
.
Hence we may assume that
\nabla\phi
precisely coincides with the moment map by adding a suitable linear function.
Since
\phi
is strictly convex, the moment map
\nabla\phi
is a diffeomorphism from
\mathbf{R}^{n}
onto
P^{\circ}
.
Now we introduce the coordinate

\displaystyle x=(\nabla\phi)(y),

and define the function
u\colon P^{\circ}\to\mathbf{R}
as the Legendre dual of
\phi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle u(x)+\phi(y)=\langle x,y\rangle.

u
is called the
symplectic potential
of the Kähler metric
\omega
.

Example 4.2.2

(
[
43
]
)
.

Let
\ell_{j}(x)\coloneqq\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,r
) and
\ell_{\infty}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ell_{j}
.
Then, by setting
\phi_{P}\coloneqq\mu_{P}^{\ast}(\ell_{\infty}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\ell_{j})
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{P}=dd^{c}\phi_{P}

on
X_{P}^{\circ}
.
The symplectic potential of the Kähler metric
\omega_{P}
is given by

\displaystyle u_{P}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ell_{j}\log\ell_{j},

usually called the Guillemin potential.

By the theory of Guillemin
[
43
]
and Abreu
[
1
]
, there is the following correspondence for
S
-invariant Kähler metrics in
c_{1}(L_{P})
and symplectic potentials.
Let
u
be a strictly convex function on
P^{\circ}
.
The function
u
is said to satisfy the
Guillemin’s boundary condition
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

u-u_{P}\in C^{\infty}(P)
;

(2)

For any face
F
of
P
,
u|_{F^{\circ}}
is a strictly convex function on
F^{\circ}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the set of all strictly convex functions on
P^{\circ}
which satisfy the Guillemin’s boundary condition.

Theorem 4.2.3

(
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
43
]
)
.

Every
S
-invariant Kähler metric in the class
c_{1}(L_{P})
has a symplectic potential
u
in
\mathcal{S}
.
Conversely, every function belongs to
\mathcal{S}
is a symplectic potential for some
S
-invariant Kähler metric in
c_{1}(L_{P})
.

For later use, for each
u\in\mathcal{S}
we denote
\varphi_{u}\in\mathcal{H}(X_{P},\omega_{P})^{S}
by the Kähler potential corresponding to
u
, which is determined by
\varphi_{u}|_{X_{P}^{\circ}}=\phi-\phi_{P}
, where
\phi
is the Legendre dual of
u
.
By using the correspodence in Theorem
4.2.3
, the scalar curvature of an
S
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega\in c_{1}(L_{P})
is given by derivatives of the corresponding symplectic potential
u
with respect to the symplectic coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

Theorem 4.2.4

(Abreu
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\omega
be an
S
-invariant Kähler metric in the class
c_{1}(L_{P})
and
u
the symplectic potential of
\omega
.
Then the scalar curvature
s(\omega)
of
\omega
is given by

\displaystyle s(\omega)=s(u)\coloneqq-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}u^{ij}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

where
u^{ij}
is the inverse of the Hessian
(u_{ij})
of

:

\displaystyle(u_{ij})=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right).

Furthermore, by using Donaldson’s integration by parts formula (
[
34
]
), the average of the scalar curvature
\bar{s}
is obtained as follows.
Let us recall the Borel measure
\sigma
on
\partial P
defined by

\displaystyle\sigma(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{|A\cap F_{j}|_{n-1}}{|\lambda_{j}|},

where
|\cdot|_{n-1}
be the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
This measure arises naturally as the subleading coefficient of the Ehrhart polynomial of an integral polytope

\displaystyle E_{P}(m)\coloneqq\#(mP\cap M)=\mathrm{vol}(P)m^{n}+\frac{\sigma(\partial P)}{2}m^{n-1}+O(m^{n-2}).

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
denote the set of all continuous convex functions on
P
which are smooth in the interior.

Theorem 4.2.5

(
[
34
, Lemma 3.3.5]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
.
Then
u^{ij}f_{ij}
is integrable on
P
and

\displaystyle\int_{P}u^{ij}f_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial P}fd\sigma+\int_{P}f(u^{ij})_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial P}fd\sigma-\int_{P}fs(u)\,dx.

Corollary 4.2.6
.

The average of the scalar curvature
\overline{s}
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{s}=\frac{\sigma(\partial P)}{\mathrm{vol}(P)}.

4.3.
Toric test configurations

In this subsection we explain the construction of toric test configurations.
For this, we first recall the algebraic construction of toric varieties following
[
27
, Chapter 7]
(see also
[
81
]
).
Let
P\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional integral (possibly unbounded) polyhedron.
Then
P
has a unique facet representaion

\displaystyle P=\{x\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\},

where
\lambda_{j}\in N
,
d_{j}\in\mathbf{Z}
and
\lambda_{j}
is primitive.
Also, it can be written as a Minkowski sum

\displaystyle P=Q+C,

where

is an integral polytope and

[
81

).
The cone part of

is determined by

\displaystyle C=\{x\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\}.

(
[
81

).
Following
[
81
]
, we call
C
the
recession cone
of
P
.
Let
C(P)
denote the
cone of

P
defined by

\displaystyle C(P)=\{(x,\rho)\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\times\mathbf{R}\mid\rho\geq 0,\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+\rho d_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\}.

Then it is easy to check that

\displaystyle C(P)\cap(M_{\mathbf{R}}\times\{0\})=C\times\{0\},

and
\rho P
is the slice of
C(P)
at height
\rho
for any
\rho>0
.
Let
S_{P}\coloneqq C(P)\cap(M\times\mathbf{Z})
.
Also, we let
\sigma\coloneqq C\times\{0\}
and
S_{\sigma}\coloneqq\sigma\cap(M\times\mathbf{Z})
.
From Gordan’s lemma,
S_{P}
and
S_{\sigma}
are affine semigroups.
Let
R\coloneqq\mathbf{C}[S_{P}]
be the semigroup ring of
S_{P}
.
Then
R
is a finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-algebra.
The character associated to
(\alpha,m)\in M\times\mathbf{Z}
is written
\chi^{\alpha}s^{m}
, and
R
is graded by height, i.e.
\deg(\chi^{\alpha}s^{m})=m
.
Consequently, we obtain a graded
\mathbf{C}
-algebra

\displaystyle R=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}R_{m},\quad R_{0}=\mathbf{C}[S_{\sigma}].

The inclusion
R_{0}\hookrightarrow R

R_{0}
-algebra on
R
.
Let
U_{P}\coloneqq\Spec R_{0}
,
X_{P}\coloneqq\Proj R

Then

is an affine toric variety,
X_{P}
is a projective toric variety, and
\pi_{P}
is a projective toric morphism.
For each

F_{j}
denote the facet of
P

F_{j}=\{x\in P\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}=0\}
.
Associated to
F_{j}

T
-invariant prime divisor

of
X_{P}
.
Then,

\displaystyle D_{P}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}D_{j}

is a
\pi_{P}
-ample Cartier divisor of
X_{P}
(
[
27
, Example 7.2.8]
).
An important fact is that if
P
is an integral Delzant polytope then
(X_{P},L_{P})\cong(X_{P},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))
.

Now let
P\subset M_{\mathbf{R}}
be an integral Delzant polytope, and identify
(X_{P},L_{P})
and
(X_{P},\mathcal{O}_{X_{P}}(D_{P}))
constructed above.
Recall that a function
f\colon P\to\mathbf{R}
is called
rational piecewise affine convex
if

is a convex function of the form

(4.2)

\displaystyle f=\max\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}

with each
\ell_{j}
an affine function having rational coefficients.
Given a rational piecewise affine convex function
f
, we can construct a
T
-equivariant test configuration

for

as follows.
Choose an integer
L
so that
L>\max_{P}f
, we define a rational convex polyhedron

by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\{(x,y)\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\times\mathbf{R}\mid x\in P,\ f(x)-L\leq y\}.

By replacing

,
L
,
\mathcal{P}
by
kf
,
kL
,
k\mathcal{P}
for suitable

is an integral polyhedron.
Then the recession cone of
\mathcal{P}
is given by

, and the cone of

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{P})=(\mathcal{C}\times\{0\})\cup\left(\bigcup_{\rho>0}\rho\mathcal{P}\times\{\rho\}\right).

Let

Then

Let
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\mathbf{C}[S_{\mathcal{P}}]

Then
\mathcal{R}
is a finitely generated
\mathbf{C}
-algebra.
The character associated to

Consequently, we have the graded

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}_{m},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{m}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{C}[S_{\sigma}]=\mathbf{C}[t]&m=0,\\
\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{C}}\left\{\chi^{\alpha}t^{\lambda}s^{m}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha\in mP\cap M,\lambda\in\mathbf{Z},\\

The inclusion

\mathbf{C}[t]

\mathcal{R}

Note also that
\mathcal{R}

\mathbf{C}[t]

By setting
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{P}}

defined above, we have a polarized scheme

\displaystyle(\mathcal{X}_{f},\mathcal{L}_{f})=(\Proj\mathcal{R},\mathcal{O}_{\Proj\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{P}})).

Then one can check that
\mathcal{O}_{\Proj\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{P}})=\mathcal{O}_{\Proj\mathcal{R}}(1)

Let
\pi_{f}\colon\mathcal{X}_{f}\to\mathbf{A}^{1}

Then

is a projective morphism and hence proper.
By construction,

-ample line bundle over

\mathcal{X}_{f}

We claim

.
To see this, it is sufficient to show that

is the Rees algebra of a finitely generated

-filtration of

Let

be a
\mathbf{Z}
-filtration of

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}R_{0}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{C}&\lambda\leq 0,\\

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}R_{m}=\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{C}}\{\chi^{\alpha}s^{m}\mid\alpha\in mP\cap M,\ \lambda\leq m(L-f(\alpha/m))\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rees}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}}t^{-\lambda}F^{\lambda}R_{m}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}}\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{C}}\left\{\chi^{\alpha}t^{\lambda}s^{m}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha\in mP\cap M,\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\{(x,y)\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\times\mathbf{R}\mid x\in P,\ f(x)-L\leq y\leq 0\}.

4.4.
Energy functionals on polarized toric manifolds

By using Duistermaat-Heckman theorem and Legendre dual functions, we can write down energy functionals in terms of convex functions.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

Let
\{u^{t}\}\subset\mathcal{S}
be a smooth path, and
\{\phi^{t}\}
be their Legendre duals.
Then

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{t}(x)=-\dot{\phi}^{t}(\nabla u^{t}(x))

for any
x\in P^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Since
\phi^{s}
is a strictly convex function,
\nabla u^{s}(x)
is a unique minimizer of
y\mapsto\phi^{s}(y)-\langle y,x\rangle
.
By setting
y^{s}=\nabla u^{s}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}(\phi^{t}(y^{s})-\langle y^{s},x\rangle)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}\nabla u^{s}(x).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}u^{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}(\langle y^{s},x\rangle-\phi^{s}(y^{s}))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}(\langle y^{s},x\rangle-\phi^{t}(y^{s}))+\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}(\phi^{t}(y^{s})-\phi^{s}(y^{s}))

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\phi^{t},\dot{y}^{t}\rangle-\dot{\phi}^{t}(y^{t})-\langle\nabla\phi^{t},\dot{y}^{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\dot{\phi}^{t}(y^{t}),

as required.
∎

Proposition 4.4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}(X_{P},\omega_{P})^{S}
, and
u\in\mathcal{S}
be the symplectic potential of
\omega_{\varphi}
.
Then

\displaystyle E(\varphi)=-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P}(u-u_{P})\,dx.

Proof.

Let
\{u^{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{S}
be a smooth path so that
u^{0}=u_{P}
and
u^{1}=u
.
Let
\varphi^{t}\coloneqq\varphi_{u^{t}}\in\mathcal{H}(X_{P},\omega_{P})^{S}
for each
t\in[0,1]
.
Note that
\varphi^{0}=0
and
\varphi^{1}=\varphi
.
Then we have

\displaystyle E(\varphi)

4.5.

In this subsection, we give convex analytic expression of non-Archimedean functionals for toric test configurations.
Let

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P}\rho(L-f)\,dx.

5.1.
Spaces of convex functions

Let
P\subset M_{\mathbf{R}}
be an
n
-dimensional integral Delzant polytope defined by

\displaystyle P=\{x\in M_{\mathbf{R}}\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,\ldots,r)\},

where
\lambda_{j}\in N
,
d_{j}\in\mathbf{Z}
,
r
is the number of the facets, and each
\lambda_{j}
is primitive.
Throughout this section, we assume the origin 0 lies in the interior of
P
.
Then
d_{j}>0
for any
j=1,\ldots,r
.
Let
\mathrm{CVX}(P)
denote the set of all lower semicontinuous convex functions
f\colon P\to(-\infty,+\infty]
such that
f\not\equiv+\infty
.
Note that every function in
\mathrm{CVX}(P)
is bounded from below since
P
is compact.
For each subset
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathrm{CVX}(P)
, set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{F}\mid\inf_{P}f=f(0)=0\}.

If
\mathcal{F}
is closed under addition of affine functions, then for any function
f\in\mathcal{F}
there exists an affine function
\ell
such that
f+\ell\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
by the supporting hyperplane theorem.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{CVX}(P)\cap L^{1}(P)
.
By the toric pluripotential theory
[
24
, Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.6, Proposition 3.9]
,
\mathcal{E}_{1}
can be identified with the metric completion of the space
(\mathcal{S},d_{1})
.
For each
j=1,\ldots,r
, let
F_{j}
denote the facet of
P
defined by

\displaystyle F_{j}=\{x\in P\mid\langle\lambda_{j},x\rangle+d_{j}=0\},

and define a subset
P^{\ast}
of
P
by

\displaystyle P^{\ast}=P^{\circ}\cup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}F_{j}^{\circ}\right).

Here
F_{j}^{\circ}
denotes the relative interior of
F_{j}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
be the set of all elements of
\mathrm{CVX}(P)
which are continuous on
P^{\ast}
and integrable on
\partial P
.
Note that the integral of any function in
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
over the boundary makes sense since the measure
\sigma
is supported on the facets of
P
.
A relation between
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1.1
.

\mathcal{C}_{\ast}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap L^{1}(\partial P)
.

We explain a proof of Proposition
5.1.1
below.
The inclusion
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap L^{1}(\partial P)
is obtained from the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1.2
.

Let
d=\max\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}\}
.
If
f
is a nonnegative function in
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{P}f\,dx\leq\frac{d}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sigma(\partial P)}{n}f(0)+\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma\right).

In particular, for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{P}f\,dx\leq\frac{d}{n+1}\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma.

Proof.

For each
j=1,\ldots,r
, let
C(F_{j})=\{t\zeta\mid t\in[0,1],\ \zeta\in F_{j}\}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle P=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}C(F_{j}).

Also, by convexity of
f
we have

\displaystyle\int_{C(F_{j})}f\,dx

\displaystyle\leq d_{j}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}\,dt\int_{F_{j}}((1-t)f(0)+tf)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=d_{j}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)t^{n-1}\,dt\int_{F_{j}}f(0)\,d\sigma+d_{j}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}\,dt\int_{F_{j}}f\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{j}}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sigma(F_{j})}{n}f(0)+\int_{F_{j}}f\,d\sigma\right).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{P}f\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{C(F_{j})}f\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{d_{j}}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sigma(F_{j})}{n}f(0)+\int_{F_{j}}f\,d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{n+1}\left(\frac{\sigma(\partial P)}{n}f(0)+\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma\right).

The latter claim is now obvious.
∎

Corollary 5.1.3
.

Every function in
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
is integrable on
P
.
In particular, we have
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap L^{1}(\partial P)

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
.
Then there is an affine function
\ell
so that
\tilde{f}\coloneqq f+\ell\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{*}
.
By Proposition
5.1.2

\displaystyle\int_{P}\tilde{f}\,dx\leq\frac{d}{n+1}\int_{\partial P}\tilde{f}\,d\sigma<\infty

and hence
\tilde{f}
is integrable on
P
.
Since

is integrable on
P
,
f=\tilde{f}-\ell
is also integrable on
P
.
∎

Let us show the converse inclusion.
For the purpose, we use the following facts from the Donldson’s work
[
34
]
.
Let

For each

\displaystyle D_{x}(f)\coloneqq\sup\{|\lambda|\mid f(y)\geq\langle\lambda,x\rangle+f(y)\ (y\in\Omega)\}.

Proposition 5.1.4

(
[

, Lemma 5.2.3]

There is a constant

such that if a nonnegative convex function

on
\Omega

\displaystyle D_{x}(f)\leq\kappa d_{x}^{-(n+1)}\int_{\Omega}f\,dy

for any
x\in\Omega
.
Here

is the distance from

to the boundary of
\Omega

Proposition 5.1.5

(
[
34
, Lemma 5.2.4]
)
.

For any convex function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
and any two points

,

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\max\{D_{x}(f),D_{y}(f)\}|x-y|.

Let
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap L^{1}(\partial P)
.
By adding a suitable affine function, we may assume
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{1}}
.
Since
f
is integrable over

,

on
P^{\circ}
.
Continuity of

on
P^{\circ}
is obvious by the convexity.
Let
F\in\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}\}
.

Lemma 5.1.6
.

For each
\zeta\in F
we have
{\displaystyle f(\zeta)=\lim_{t\to 1}f(t\zeta)}
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in F
and
s,t\in[0,1]
.
If
s<t
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(t\zeta)-f(s\zeta)}{t-s}\geq\frac{f(t\zeta)-f(0)}{t-0}\geq 0

by the convexity of
f
.
Hence the function
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto f(t\zeta)\in\mathbf{R}
is monotonically non-decreasing.
Further, since

\displaystyle f(t\zeta)\leq(1-t)f(0)+tf(\zeta)=tf(\zeta)

for any

, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 1}f(t\zeta)\leq f(\zeta)\leq\liminf_{\xi\to\zeta}f(\xi)

by the lower semicontinuity of

.
We claim that
\liminf_{\xi\to\zeta}f(\xi)\leq\lim_{t\to 1}f(t\zeta)
.
Let
\delta>0
and

.
Then

and

\displaystyle\inf_{B(\zeta,\delta)\cap P}f\leq f(t\zeta).

Hence we have

\displaystyle\inf_{B(\zeta,\delta)\cap P}f\leq\lim_{t\to 1}f(t\zeta)

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\xi\to\zeta}f(\xi)=\sup_{\delta>0}\inf_{B(\zeta,\delta)\cap P}f\leq\lim_{t\to 1}f(t\zeta),

as required.
∎

For each
\eta\in(0,1]
, define a Borel measure
\sigma_{\eta}
on
\eta(\partial P)
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\eta}(A)\coloneqq\eta^{n-1}\sigma(\eta^{-1}A).

By setting
C\coloneqq\int_{\partial P}f(\xi)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\int_{\eta(\partial P)}f(\xi)\,d\sigma_{\eta}=\eta^{n-1}\int_{\partial P}f(\eta\xi)\,d\sigma\leq\eta^{n}\int_{\partial P}f(\xi)\,d\sigma=\eta^{n}C.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
F^{\circ}
.
We write
F
as

\displaystyle F=\{x\in P\mid\langle\lambda,x\rangle+d=0\}.

Regarding
f
as a nonnegative convex function on
\eta F^{\circ}
, by Proposition
5.1.4
there is a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle D_{\zeta}(f)\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{\zeta}^{-n}\int_{\eta F}f(\xi)\,d\sigma_{\eta}\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{\zeta}^{-n}\eta^{n}C

for any
\zeta\in\eta K
.
Here
d_{\zeta}
is the distance from
\zeta
to the boundary
\partial(\eta F^{\circ})
.
Also, by setting
d_{\eta K}
with the distance between
\eta K
and
\partial(\eta F^{\circ})
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\zeta}(f)\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{\zeta}^{-n}\eta^{n}C\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{\eta K}^{-n}\eta^{n}C=|\lambda|\kappa d_{K}^{-n}C.

Hence, by Proposition
5.1.5
we obtain

\displaystyle|f(\zeta^{\prime})-f(\zeta^{\prime\prime})|\leq\max\{D_{\zeta^{\prime}}(f),D_{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}(f)\}|\zeta^{\prime}-\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{K}^{-n}C|\zeta^{\prime}-\zeta^{\prime\prime}|

for any
\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}\in\eta K
.
Let
\zeta\in F^{\circ}
, and

\displaystyle K\coloneqq\overline{B(\zeta,\delta|\zeta|)}\cap\partial P

for each
\delta>0
.
By choosing
\delta
to be sufficiently small, we may assume that
K\subset F^{\circ}
.
Set
C(K)\coloneqq\{t\xi\mid t\geq 0,\xi\in K\}
.
Then
C(K)
is a closed neighborhood of
\zeta
in
M_{\mathbf{R}}
.
Let
x\in C(K)\cap P^{\circ}
, and
H_{x}
be the hyperplane of
M_{\mathbf{R}}
which contains
x
and is parallel to the hyperplane containing
F
.
Then
H_{x}
intersects the line
\mathbf{R}\zeta
at a single point
\{y\}
.
Define

by

.
Then the function
x\mapsto t(x)
is continuous and

(as
x\to\zeta

Also, since

we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|\lambda|\kappa d_{K}^{-n}C|x-y|.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle|f(\zeta)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\zeta)-f(y)|+|f(y)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\zeta)-f(t(x)\zeta)|+|\lambda|\kappa d_{K}^{-n}C|y-x|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\zeta)-f(t(x)\zeta)|+|\lambda|\kappa d_{K}^{-n}C|t(x)\zeta-x|

5.2.
Approximations and compactness of convex functions

We collect various approximation and compactness results for convex functions, which is crucial for our proof of Theorem

Proposition 5.2.1
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative functions in
\mathcal{C}_{*}
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{*}
.
Suppose that
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and

satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges locally uniformly to
f
on
P^{\circ}
.

(ii)

{\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}}\int_{\partial P}f_{i}\,d\sigma<\infty}

Then
{\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}=0}
.

Proof.

By assumption, there is a constant
C>0
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(0)+\sup_{i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}}f_{i}(0)+\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma+\sup_{i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}}\int_{\partial P}f_{i}\,d\sigma\leq C.\end{split}

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Then for each
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
we have

\displaystyle\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}=\int_{P\setminus\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx+\int_{\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx.

By the convexity of
f_{i}
and
f
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{P\setminus\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{P\setminus\eta P}(f+f_{i})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\int_{\eta}^{1}(1-t)t^{n-1}\,dt\int_{F_{j}}(f(0)+f_{i}(0))\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\int_{\eta}^{1}t^{n}\,dt\int_{F_{j}}(f+f_{i})\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq 2Cd\sigma(\partial P)\int_{\eta}^{1}(1-t)t^{n-1}\,dt+2Cd\int_{\eta}^{1}t^{n}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2Cd(\sigma(\partial P)+1)\int_{\eta}^{1}t^{n-1}\,dt

\displaystyle=2Cd(\sigma(\partial P)+1)\frac{1-\eta^{n}}{n}.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, choose
\eta\in(0,1)
so that

\displaystyle 2Cd(\sigma(\partial P)+1)\frac{1-\eta^{n}}{n}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
f
on
\eta P
, there exists
N\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
such that for any integer

\displaystyle\int_{\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, for any integer

\displaystyle\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}

\displaystyle=\int_{P\setminus\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx+\int_{\eta P}|f-f_{i}|\,dx<\varepsilon,

as required.
∎

For any
j\in\{1,,\ldots,m\}
, choose a sequence
\{\ell_{j}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of rational affine functions which converges pointwise to
\ell_{j}
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and define a rational piecewise affine function
f^{(i)}
by

\displaystyle f^{(i)}(x)=\max\{\ell_{1}^{(i)}(x),\ldots,\ell_{m}^{(i)}(x)\}.

Then
\{f^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges locally uniformly to
f
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
In particular,
\{f^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
f
on
P
.
∎

5.3.
The non-Archimedean K-energy

We can define
L(f)
and
L_{V}(f)
for any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, taking value in
(-\infty,\infty]
.
By using these, we can get the following characterization of
\mathcal{C}_{\ast}
.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\ast}=\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid L(f)<\infty\}=\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}\mid L_{V}(f)<\infty\}.

Proof.

By Proposition

, for each

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}_{\ast}\iff\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma<\infty\iff L(f)<\infty\iff L_{V}(f)<\infty,

∎

5.4.
The J-norm

For any convex function
f
in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
, define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{J}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\text{$\xi\colon$affine}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P}(f+\xi)\,dx-\inf_{P}(f+\xi)\right).

We call
\|f\|_{J}
the
J-norm
of
f
.
The followings are the basic properties on the J-norm.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

(1)

\|f+\xi\|_{J}=\|f\|_{J}
for any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\xi\in N_{\mathbf{R}}
.

(2)

\|f\|_{J}\geq 0
for any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, and
\|f\|_{J}=0
if and only if
f
is affine.

(3)

There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{J}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(P)}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{J}

for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
.

(4)

If a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
converges uniformly to
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f_{i}\|_{J}=\|f\|_{J}.

Proof.

The claim (1) is clear, and (2) is easily deduced from (1) and (3).
Let us show the claim (3).
The first inequality is obvious.
For the latter inequality, it is sufficient to show that there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{J}\geq\kappa

for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
with
\|f\|_{L^{1}(P)}=1
.
For contradiction, suppose that the conclusion does not hold.
Then there is a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
such that

(i)

\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}=1
for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
, and

(ii)

{\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f_{i}\|_{J}=0}
.

By condition (ii), there is a sequence of affine functions
\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that

(iii)

{\displaystyle\inf_{P}(f_{i}+\ell_{i})=0}
for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
, and

(iv)

{\displaystyle\int_{P}(f_{i}+\ell_{i})\,dx=0}
.

By conditions (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv) and
[
34
, Corollary 5.2.5]
, we may assume both
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{f_{i}+\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converge locally uniformly on
P^{\circ}
.
Then the sequence
\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges locally uniformly on
P^{\circ}
.
Moreover, since every
\ell_{i}
is affine, the limit
\ell
is also affine and the convergence
\ell_{i}\to\ell
is uniform.
Let

\displaystyle f(x)\coloneqq\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}(x),\quad\ell(x)\coloneqq\lim_{i\to\infty}\ell_{i}(x)

for each
x\in P^{\circ}
.
By (iv) above,
f+\ell=0
on
P^{\circ}
and hence
f
is affine on
P^{\circ}
.
Moreover, since
\inf_{P^{\circ}}f=f(0)=0
we obtain

\displaystyle f=-\ell=0.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 1=\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}\leq\|f_{i}+\ell_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}+\|-\ell_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}\to 0\quad(\text{as $i\to\infty$}),

which is contradiction.

Finally, we show the claim (4).
For each
\varepsilon>0
, choose
N\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{P}|f-f_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4\mathrm{vol}(P)}

for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq N}
.
Then for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq N}
and an affine function
\ell
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{P}(f+\ell)\,dx-\mathrm{vol}(P)\inf_{P}(f+\ell)\right)-\left(\int_{P}(f_{i}+\ell)\,dx-\mathrm{vol}(P)\inf_{P}(f_{i}+\ell)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{P}(f+\ell)\,dx-\int_{P}(f+\ell)\,dx\right|+\mathrm{vol}(P)\left|\inf_{P}(f+\ell)-\inf_{P}(f_{i}+\ell)\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{P}(f-f_{i})\,dx\right|+\mathrm{vol}(P)\sup_{P}|f-f_{i}|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By choosing
\ell
so that

\displaystyle\int_{P}(f+\ell)\,dx-\mathrm{vol}(P)\inf_{P}(f+\ell)-\|f\|_{J}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

we obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f_{i}\|_{J}&\leq\int_{P}(f_{i}+\ell)\,dx-\mathrm{vol}(P)\inf_{P}(f_{i}+\ell)\\
&<\int_{P}(f+\ell)\,dx-\mathrm{vol}(P)\inf_{P}(f+\ell)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\\
&<\|f\|_{J}+\varepsilon.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{J}<\|f_{i}\|_{J}+\varepsilon.

By (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain
|\|f\|_{J}-\|f_{i}\|_{J}|<\varepsilon
, as required.
∎

Similar to the J-norm,
the reduced
L^{1}
-norm
of a convex function
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,T}\coloneqq\inf_{\text{$\xi\colon$affine}}\int_{P}|(f+\xi)-(\overline{f+\xi})|\,dx.

The similar claims as (1), (2), (3) of Proposition
5.4.1
are valid for the
J
-norm.
In particular, the reduced
L^{1}
-norm and the
J
-norm are equivalent in the following sense.

Proposition 5.4.2
.

There are constants
C_{2},C_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{2}\|f\|_{1,T}\leq\|f\|_{J}\leq C_{3}\|f\|_{1,T}

for any
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

An advantage of the reduced
L^{1}
-norm is the following
L^{1}
-continuity.

Proposition 5.4.3
.

If a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
converges to
f\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
in
L^{1}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f_{i}\|_{1,T}=\|f\|_{1,T}.

Proof.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, choose
N\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
so that

\displaystyle\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq N}
.
Then for any
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq N}
and an affine function
\ell
we have

\displaystyle|\|f+\ell\|_{L^{1}(P)}-\|f_{i}+\ell\|_{L^{1}(P)}|\leq\|f-f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(P)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By choosing
\ell
so that

\displaystyle|\|f+\ell\|_{L^{1}(P)}-\|f\|_{1,T}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

we obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|f_{i}\|_{1,T}\leq\|f_{i}+\ell\|_{L^{1}(P)}<\|f+\ell\|_{L^{1}(P)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\|f\|_{1,T}+\varepsilon.

Similarly, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,T}<\|f_{i}\|_{1,T}+\varepsilon.

By (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain
|\|f\|_{T,1}-\|f_{i}\|_{T,1}|<\varepsilon
, as required.
∎

It is not known whether the similar result as Proposition
5.4.3
are valid for the
J
-norm or not.

Proof of Theorem

Finally in this section, we prove Theorem

.
By the implication
(b)_{\mathcal{C}_{PL}^{\mathbf{Q}}}\Rightarrow(J)_{\mathcal{C}_{PL}^{\mathbf{Q}}}

First suppose that

\displaystyle L_{V}(f)\geq\delta\int_{\partial P}f\,d\sigma\geq 0

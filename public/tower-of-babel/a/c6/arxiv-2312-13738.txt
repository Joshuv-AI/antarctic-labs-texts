Real forms of minimal SL 2 \SL_{2} -threefolds Thanks: The IMB receives support from the EIPHI Graduate School (contract ANR-17-EURE-0002).
Lucas Moulin

Abstract
. We complete the classification of the real forms of almost homogeneous SL 2 \SL_{2} -threefolds. More precisely, we use the Luna-Vust theory to determine the real forms of minimal smooth complete SL 2 \SL_{2} -varieties containing an orbit isomorphic to SL 2 / H \SL_{2}/H , where H H is a finite cyclic subgroup of SL 2 \SL_{2} . Moreover, we study the rationality and the set of real points of those varieties.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. A classical problem in algebraic geometry is to determine the real forms of
X
, i.e. the real algebraic varieties
W
such that
W_{\mathbb{C}}:=W\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\simeq X
. This problem is usually divided in two parts: whether
X
admits a real form or not, and if so, determine how many there are (up to isomorphism).

In the present article, we work on a slightly different problem. We are interested in algebraic varieties endowed with an algebraic group action, and thus we only consider real forms which are compatible with this algebraic group action. More precisely, let
G
be a complex reductive algebraic group and let
F
be a real algebraic group such that
G\simeq F_{\mathbb{C}}
as complex algebraic groups. Now let
X
be a complex algebraic
G
-variety. We say that
W
is a
real F-form
of
X
if
W_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq X
as complex algebraic
G
-varieties. When the real algebraic group
F
is clear from the context, we will just refer to real
F
-forms as real forms.

An important invariant of
G
-varieties is the complexity. For
B\subset G
a Borel subgroup, the
complexity
of a complex algebraic
G
-variety is defined as the maximal codimension of a
B
-orbit. Note that this does not depend on the choice of
B
. Forms over any perfect base field of complexity-zero varieties (more commonly known as spherical varieties) have already been classified by Huruguen
[
Hur11
]
, Wedhorn
[
Wed18
]
, and Borovoi-Gagliardi
[
BG21
]
. For
T
a torus, forms of complexity-one
T
-varieties have also been studied by Langlois in
[
Lan15
]
and by Gillard in
[
Gil22
]
.

Another invariant of a homogeneous space
G/H
is the rank, which we denote rk
(G/H)
. Suppose that rk
(G/H)\leq 1
. Then, Panyushev proved in
[
Pan95
]
that either
G/H
is a spherical homogeneous space or
G/H
is obtained from a homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefold by parabolic induction. In this sense, almost homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefolds are therefore the easiest case of complexity-one almost homogeneous varieties. Thus, let us now suppose that
G=\SL_{2}
and that
X
is a complex almost homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefold, i.e. an
\SL_{2}
-variety with a dense open orbit
X_{0}=\SL_{2}/H
, where
H
is a finite subgroup of
\SL_{2}
. Note that, since a Borel subgroup of
\SL_{2}
is of codimension 1, these varieties are of complexity one. In this setting, the Luna-Vust theory provides a combinatorial description of all the
G
-equivariant embeddings
X_{0}\hookrightarrow X
for a given
X_{0}
, up to
G
-equivariant isomorphisms (see
[
LV83
, § 9]
for details or §
2.2
for an overview).

More specifically, we will study the real forms of minimal smooth completions of
G/H
. Here we call a
minimal smooth completion
of
G/H
a smooth complete almost homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefold, with open orbit
G/H
, such that any
\SL_{2}
-equivariant birational morphism
X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
, with
X^{\prime}
smooth, is an isomorphism. To simplify, we will also refer to a minimal smooth completion as a
model
. Those varieties are well-known, they were first studied by Umemura in
[
Ume88
]
as the connected component of their automorphism groups are maximal connected algebraic subgroups of the Cremona group Bir
(\mathbb{P}^{3})
, and the projective ones were then classified by Nakano in
[
Nak89
]
as an application of Mori theory. They arise naturally in many ways in algebraic geometry, and the list includes Fano varieties such as
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, the smooth quadric
Q_{3}
and the degree five del Pezzo surface
Y_{5}
, as well as Umemura
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles and the projectivization of Schwarzenberger bundles (see
[
Ume88
]
and
[
BFT23
]
). From the Luna-Vust theory point of view, the full classification and the description of the corresponding combinatorial data were obtained by Moser-Jauslin
[
MJ87
]
when
H\subset\{\pm Id\}
and by Bousquet
[
Bou00
]
in the general case.

Using a generalization of the Luna-Vust theory over arbitrary perfect fields, Moser-Jauslin and Terpereau determined in
[
MJT
]
the real forms of the minimal smooth completions of
\SL_{2}/H
when
H
is a non-cyclic finite subgroup. In their case, there is always a unique smooth completion which has one or two real
F
-forms for a fixed
F
. Here we will use a similar approach to determine the real forms of
\SL_{2}/H
when
H
is a cyclic subgroup of order
n
. However, in this case, a few new difficulties arise compared to the non-cyclic case since the automorphism group
\Aut^{G}(X)
can be infinite and there are many more cases to consider. Also the underlying complete
G
-varieties are not all projective, so we have to investigate more carefully the effectiveness of the Galois descent.

For a cyclic subgroup

of cardinal

1.2.
Our results

In this paper, we determine the equivariant real forms of all these models using the Luna-Vust theory. The group
\SL_{2}
has two real forms up to isomorphism, namely
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
and
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
. Their set of real points are
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\SU_{2}(\mathbb{C})
respectively. Therefore, we classify the equivariant real forms for these two groups. Moreover we determine which real forms (in the classical sense) appear as an equivariant real form. Finally, we also determine which equivariant real forms are rational and whether they have real points. Recall that a rational variety always has real points. In this case we show that the converse is true. The main result of this article is the following (see Section
5
for the proof):

.

Note that for

there are two real forms in total, but only the rational one is equivariant under an action of a real form of

.

1.3.
Structure of the article

Section
2
contains preliminary results necessary for the rest of the paper. In Section
2.1
, we review real group structures on reductive algebraic groups as well as real structures on varieties endowed with an algebraic group action. We also explain how they correspond to real forms. Then, in Section
2.2
we see how Luna-Vust theory specializes to classify the equivariant embeddings of homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefolds, and how a generalization of this theory can be used to classify real forms. In Section
3
, we explain how to compute the equivariant automorphism groups of equivariant embeddings of homogeneous spaces using the combinatorial data of the Luna-Vust theory. We then compute those groups for all the minimal smooth completions of
\SL_{2}/H
, where
H
is a cyclic subgroup. In Section
4
, we determine all the real forms of the
\SL_{2}
-threefolds listed previously. Finally, Section
5
, we study the rationality and the real locus of the real forms we obtained previously, and then prove the
Main Theorem
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Real structures and real forms

We start by recalling the notions of real forms and real structures on a given complex algebraic group
G
and then on a
G
-variety, and how the notions are related. More details can be found in
[
MJT22
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a complex algebraic group.

(i)

A
real form
of
G
is a pair (
F
,
\Theta
) with
F
a real algebraic group and
\Theta:G\rightarrow F_{\mathbb{C}}=F\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
an isomorphism of algebraic groups. (To simplify notation we will drop the isomorphism
\Theta
and simply write that
F
is a form of
G
).

(ii)

A
real group structure

\sigma
on
G
is a scheme involution on
G
such that the diagram

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma^{*}\ =\ \mathrm{Spec}(z\mapsto\overline{z})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})}

commutes and

\iota_{G}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\iota_{G}\text{ and }m_{G}\circ(\sigma\times\sigma)=\sigma\circ m_{G},

where
\iota_{G}:G\rightarrow G
is the inverse morphism and
m_{G}:G\times G\rightarrow G
is the multiplication morphism.

(iii)

Two real group structures
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are equivalent if there exists a group automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(G)
such that
\sigma^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\sigma\circ\varphi^{-1}
.

(iv)

If
G
is a complex algebraic group with a real structure
\sigma
, then the set of fixed point
G(\mathbb{C})^{\sigma}
is called the
real locus
of
(G,\sigma)
.

There is a correspondence between real group structures on
G
and real forms of
G
given as follows (see
[
FSS98
, § 1.4]
).

\bullet
If
\sigma
is a real group structure on
G
, one associates the real algebraic group
G_{0}:=G/\langle\sigma\rangle
and the isomorphism
\Theta
given by
(q,f)
, where
q:G\rightarrow G_{0}
is the quotient morphism and
f:G\rightarrow\text{Spec($\mathbb{C}$)}
is the structure morphism.

\bullet
To a real form
F
of
G
, one associates the real group structure given by
\sigma:G\simeq F_{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{Id\times Spec(z\mapsto\bar{z})}F_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq G
.

Moreover, two real forms of
G
are isomorphic (as real algebraic groups) if and only if the corresponding real group structures are equivalent.

Example 2.2
.

For
G=\SL_{2}
, let
\sigma_{s}
be the real group structure defined by
\sigma_{s}(g)=\overline{g}
where
\overline{g}
is the complex conjugate of
g
and let
\sigma_{c}
be the real group structure defined by
\sigma_{c}(g)=\prescript{t}{}{\sigma}_{s}(g)^{-1}
. Note that
\sigma_{c}(g)=e\sigma_{s}(g)e^{-1}
for all
g\in G
, where
e=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}
. The real loci of
\sigma_{s}
and
\sigma_{c}
are respectively
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\SU(2)
, and the corresponding real forms are respectively
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
and
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
. Also, any real group structure on
G
is strongly equivalent to either
\sigma_{s}
or
\sigma_{c}
.

We now define real forms and equivariant real structures for a
G
-variety.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a complex algebraic group with a real structure, and let

be a
G
-variety. We denote by
F=G/\langle\sigma\rangle
the corresponding real form of
G
.

\bullet
An

of

\bullet
A
(G,\sigma)
-equivariant real structure
on

\mu
on

such that the diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma^{*}\ =\ \mathrm{Spec}(z\mapsto\overline{z})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C})}

commutes, and such that

\forall g\in G(\mathbb{C}),\forall x\in X(\mathbb{C}),\ \mu(g\cdot x)=\sigma(g)\cdot\mu(x).

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

2.2.
Luna-Vust Theory for equivariant embeddings of
\bm{\SL_{2}/H}

We now recall how the Luna-Vust theory specializes to classify the equivariant embeddings of homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefolds. This section is inspired by
[
MJT
, Appendix B]
. For more information on the Luna-Vust theory in the general case, see
[
LV83
]
and
[
Tim11
, § 3]
.

This description was given by Luna-Vust
[
LV83
, § 9]
for equivariant embeddings of
\SL_{2}
, and by Moser-Jauslin
[
MJ87
]
and Bousquet
[
Bou00
]
for equivariant embeddings of
\SL_{2}/H
.

\bullet

The colors.
We start by describing the set of colors of
G/H
where

\bullet

The invariant valuations.

\mathcal{V}^{G}\simeq\{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times([-1,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})\}/\sim

. For

where

\bullet

The colored data.
Now let

be a
G
-variety in the

-birational class of

, and let

-equivariant birational map. The pair

is called a

-model of

. In the general theory, we associate to each

-orbit

a pair

\displaystyle\bullet\>

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{G}_{Y}=\{\nu_{D}\in\mathcal{V}^{G}(X)\>|\text{ $Y\subset D$, where $D$ is a $G$-stable prime divisor of $X$}\}

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{V}^{G}(X)\simeq\mathcal{V}^{G};\text{ and}

\displaystyle\bullet\>

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{B}_{Y}=\{D\in\mathcal{D}^{B}(X)\>|\>Y\subset D\}\subset\mathcal{D}^{B}(X)\simeq\mathcal{D}^{B}.

Such a pair is called the colored data of the
G
-orbit

, and the sets of all those pairs is called the colored equipment of

.

We give a description of all possible colored data in our particular case, i.e. where
X
is
G
-equivariant embedding of
G/H
with
H\subset G=\SL_{2}
a finite subgroup. We call the facet of an orbit the set of valuations in

which dominate the local ring of the closure of the given orbit.

The (non-open)

-orbits are divided into the six following types:

\bullet

Type
\mathcal{C}(j,r)
(
2
-dimensional orbit):
j\in\mathbb{P}^{1}/H
and

.

For this case,
\mathcal{V}_{Y}^{G}=\{\nu(j,r)\}
is a single element of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
,

\mathcal{D}_{Y}^{B}=\varnothing
, and the facet of
Y
is
\{\nu(j,r)\}
.

\bullet

Type
\mathcal{A}_{N}(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N},r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})
(orbit isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
):
j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}
are

distinct elements of
\mathbb{P}^{1}/H
, and
r_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}\cap]-1,b(j_{i})]
for each

.

Suppose also that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+r_{i}}\geq 1
. Then
\mathcal{V}_{Y}^{G}=\{\nu(j_{1},r_{1}),\ldots,\nu(j_{N},r_{N})\}
and
\mathcal{D}_{Y}^{B}=\mathbb{P}^{1}/H\setminus\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\}
. The facet of

is

\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\{\nu(j_{i},s_{i})\ |\ -1<s_{i}<r_{i}\}\cup\{\nu(j,s)\ |\ j\not\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\},-1<s\leq b(j)\}.

\bullet

Type

\bullet

Type

\bullet

Type

and

\bullet

Type
\mathcal{B}_{0}(j,r)
(fixed point):

, and

. Then
\mathcal{V}_{Y}^{G}=\{\nu(j,r))\}
and

The facets of the colored data of a

. Therefore, we can now represent the colored equipment of equivariant embeddings of homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-threefolds. We represent the set
\mathcal{V}^{G}(G/H)
by a skeleton diagram, and we represent each orbit by its facet. Since the facets for orbits of types
\mathcal{B}_{-}
,

and

are the same, we differentiate them with a sign

for many examples of such diagrams.

\bullet

Luna-Vust over the field of real numbers.
Finally, we present how the Luna-Vust theory allows us to find real forms of equivariant embeddings of
\SL_{2}/H
. To do this, Moser-Jauslin and Terpereau defined the following action of the Galois group
\Gamma
on the colored equipment of
G/H
in
[
MJT
, § 4.3]
, generalizing the action defined by Huruguen
[
Hur11
]
in the case of spherical varieties.

Let
\sigma\in\{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{c}\}
and let
\mu
be a
(G,\sigma)
-equivariant real structure on
G/H
. By Proposition
2.4
, we have
\mu(gH)=\sigma(g)tH
for a certain
t\in G
. Then the action of the Galois group
\Gamma
on the set of colors
\mathcal{D}^{B}(G/H)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}/H
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\alpha:\beta]H\mapsto[\overline{\alpha}:\overline{\beta}]tH&\text{if $\sigma=\sigma_{s}$; and}\\[1.0pt]
[\alpha:\beta]H\mapsto[-\overline{\beta}:\overline{\alpha}]tH&\text{if $\sigma=\sigma_{c}$}\end{array}\right.

and the
\Gamma
-action on
\mathcal{V}^{G}(G/H)
is defined by

\forall j\in\mathbb{P}^{1}/H,\forall\nu(j,r)\in\mathcal{V}^{G}(G/H),\gamma\cdot\nu(j,r)=\nu(\gamma\cdot j,r).

Note that this action preserves the type of the
G
-orbits. It can be visualised on the skeleton diagrams where it corresponds to a permutation of the spokes.

We can now give the following result that determines when an equivariant real structure on a homogeneous space
X_{0}
extends to an effective equivariant real structure on an equivariant embedding of
X_{0}
, using the previously defined
\Gamma
-action on the colored equipment of that embedding.

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
MJT
, Theorem 4.7]
)

Let

be a finite subgroup of

, let

be a real group structure on

corresponding to the real form

of

, and
let

be a

-equivariant real structure on

Let

be a

-equivariant embedding of

.
Then

extends to an effective

-equivariant real structure

on

-form of

through the map

, if and only if

(1)

the

and

induced by

Equivariant automorphism groups

In this section, we first see how we can use the Luna-Vust theory to compute the equivariant automorphism groups of equivariant embeddings of homogeneous spaces. Then, we apply to the particular case of the minimal smooth completions of

listed in Section

.

Let
k
be an algebraic closed field,

a reductive group over

,

an algebraic subgroup of
G
and
B\subset G
a Borel subgroup of
G
. We denote by

the field
k(G)^{H}=k(G/H)
.

We have an isomorphism (see
[

, Proposition 1.8]
)

\displaystyle N_{G}(H)/H

\displaystyle\longrightarrow Aut^{G}(G/H)

\displaystyle nH

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\varphi_{n}:kH\mapsto kn^{-1}H).

We consider two group actions on
G/H
:

\bullet

The action of
A=Aut^{G}(G/H)\cong N_{G}(H)/H

\bullet

The action of
G
by left multiplication.

Those actions induce actions of
A
and
G
on

. Indeed, for

,

and
g\in G
, we define:

\bullet

(g\cdot f)(x)=f(g^{-1}x)
and,

\bullet

(\varphi_{n}(f))(kH)=f(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(kH))=f(knH).

We can now define an action of

on
\mathcal{V}^{G}
and on
\mathcal{D}^{B}
.

\bullet

Action of

on
\mathcal{V}^{G}
: If

, and if

, then
((\varphi_{n}\nu)(f))=\nu(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(f)).
Since

acts on

-stable geometric valuation on

.

\bullet

Action of

on

: Again, since

acts on the left and the action of

is induced by a right action of

, the action of

on

induces an action on

-stable divisors.

Let
X_{0}=G/H
. We can now determine when a
G
-equivariant automorphism of

extends to an automorphism on a

-model of

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,\psi)
be a

-model of

, and
\varphi\in A
. Let

be the colored data of

. Then, the automorphism

extends to an automorphism of

if and only if
\mathbb{F}(X,\psi)
is preserved by the action of

on

and

\forall(\mathcal{V}^{G}_{Y},\mathcal{D}^{B}_{Y})\in\mathbb{F}(X,\psi),(\varphi(\mathcal{V}^{G}_{Y}),\varphi(\mathcal{D}^{B}_{Y}))\in\mathbb{F}(X,\psi).

For the proof, we will need the following result that gives a combinatorial description of the

-charts of

. We recall that a

[

is a bijection between the

-charts of

and the pairs

satisfying conditions
(C)
,
(F)
, and
(W)
(See
[

).

The converse map sends the pair
(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R})
to
\mathrm{Spec}(A[\mathcal{W},\mathcal{R}])
, where

. Let

5.
Proof of the Main Theorem

We can now use the previous results to prove the main theorem. In this section, we always consider real structures up to equivalence and real forms up to isomorphism. We say that a real form (in the classical sense)
Y
of a complex variety is equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
(resp of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
) when there is an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
(resp of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
) on
Y
such that
Y_{\mathbb{C}}
is an almost homogeneous
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
-threefold.

We recall that the correspondence between the geometric description and the description from the Luna-Vust point of view is given in the
Appendix
.

To begin with, note that all the geometrical descriptions given in Section
1.1
are valid over any field of characteristic zero. In particular, all the minimal
\SL_{2}
-threefolds have a trivial real form given by the same description. Furthermore, it is easily checked, by a case by case study, that those real forms are all rational.

We first prove the following lemma, which helps us determine when a real form has no real points. Recall that a rational variety always has a real point (in fact a dense subset of real points).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth almost homogeneous
\SL_{2}
-variety. If the intersection of
X(\mathbb{R})
with the open orbit of
X
is empty, then
X(\mathbb{R})=\varnothing
.

Proof.

By
[
Man20
, Corollary 2.2.10]
, the real locus of a smooth real variety is either dense or empty. Hence, either
X(\mathbb{R})
intersects the open orbit of
X
or
X(\mathbb{R})=\varnothing
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

For
H
a finite subgroup of
\SL_{2}
and
\sigma\in\{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{c}\}
, the list of all
(G,\sigma)
-equivariant real structures on
\SL_{2}/H
, as well as the corresponding real loci, can be found in
[
MJT
, Appendix C]
. We can therefore use the previous lemma to determine if
X
has a real point for all minimal smooth completions
X
of
\SL_{2}/H
.

We now split the proof of the
Main Theorem
into a case by case study, starting with the Umemura
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles
X_{k}(n,m)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{k}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
X_{k}(n,m)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{k}
always has a unique real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form, which is rational. Moreover, it has no real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form when
k
is odd, and a unique real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form, which has no real points, when
k
is even. Finally, all real forms of
X_{k}(n,m)
are equivariant under an action of either
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
or
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

We first suppose that
k
is odd. Then, by Propositions
4.1
and
4.4
, there is only one
(G,\sigma_{s})
-equivariant real structure on
X_{k}(n,m)
and no
(G,\sigma_{c})
-equivariant real structure. Hence, when
k
is odd,
X_{k}(n,m)
has one real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form which must be the trivial one and is therefore rational.

On the other hand when
k
is even, by Propositions
4.3
and
4.5
, we have one
(G,\sigma)
-equivariant real structure for each
\sigma\in\{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{c}\}
. For
\sigma_{c}
, the real locus of the open orbit is empty by
[
MJT
, Appendix C]
. Therefore by Lemma
5.1
, when
k
is even,
X_{k}(n,m)
has a rational real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form and a real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form with no real points.
Finally, there are no other real forms by
[
TZ24
, Proposition 7.1]
and its proof.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

The decomposable
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
Z(1-k)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
has a unique real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form, which is rational, for all
k
. Moreover, it has no real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form when
k
is odd, and a unique real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form, which has no real points, when
k
is even. Finally, all real forms of
Z(1-k)
are equivariant under an action of either
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
or
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the case of the Umemura
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles. The non-equivariant real forms are in
[
TZ24
, Proposition 5.3 and Remark 5.4]
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

For
n\geq 2
, the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
W(n+1)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
has a unique real form. This real form is rational and is equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
, but not under an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.
On the other hand, the variety
W(1)
has three real forms :

-

The trivial real form which is rational and is equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

-

A rational real form which is equivariant under an action of both
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
and
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
.

-

A real form with no real points which is equivariant under an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 2
, there is, by Proposition
4.1
, only one
(G,\sigma_{s})
-equivariant real structure on
W(n+1)
and no
(G,\sigma_{c})
-equivariant real structure. Hence,
W(n+1)
only has one real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form, which is the trivial one and is in particular rational. By
[
TZ24
, Corollary 7.2]
, there are no other real forms for
W(n+1)
.

The variety
W(1)
can also be defined as the complex flag variety
\PGL_{3,\mathbb{C}}/B
where
B
is a Borel subgroup
\PGL_{3,\mathbb{C}}
. By
[
TZ24
, Proposition 8.1]
, this variety has three real forms, two rational ones and one with no real points. We only obtained one real structure with an empty real locus which therefore corresponds to the unique real form with no real points. For the other real structures, the
\Gamma
-action given by the real structure defined on the open orbit by
g\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)
acts trivially on the combinatorial data, whereas the other two real structures with a non-empty real locus do not. Therefore the real structure given by
g\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)
corresponds to the trivial real form and the other two real structures correspond to the nontrivial real form.
∎

Let us now look at the three isolated models, namely
\mathbb{P}^{3}\simeq\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\oplus R_{1})
,
\mathbb{P}(R_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(R_{1})
and
Q_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}\simeq\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\oplus R_{1}\oplus R_{0})
.

Proposition 5.6
.

The variety
\mathbb{P}^{3}\simeq\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\oplus R_{1})
has one rational real form and one real form with no real points. Those two varieties are both equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
and an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\{\sigma_{s},\sigma_{c}\}
, by Proposition
4.1
there are two
(G,\sigma)
-equivariant real structures, with one of them having an empty real locus. However, it is known that
\mathbb{P}^{3}
has two real forms, one is rational while the other has no real points. Therefore the real
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-form with no real points corresponds to the same real form as the real
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-form with no real points. The same can be said for the rational real form of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

The variety
\mathbb{P}(R_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(R_{1})
has two real forms. One is rational and is equivariant under an action of both
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
and
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
, while the other one has no real points and is not equivariant under an action of a real form of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

In this case there is one
(G,\sigma_{s})
-equivariant real structure given by
gH\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)\begin{bmatrix}e^{-i\pi x}&0\\
0&e^{i\pi x}\end{bmatrix}H
. On the open orbit, this real structure is equivalent to
gH\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)H
which has a non-empty real locus. Therefore this real structure corresponds to a real form with real points. Similarly, there is one
(G,\sigma_{c})
-equivariant real structure which corresponds to a real form with real points. Since
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\simeq\mathbb{P}(R_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(R_{1})
has only one real form with real points, which is rational, the previous two equivariant real structures correspond to the same rational real form. Moreover,
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
has a unique real form with no real points, which is therefore not equivariant under an action of a real form of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
Q^{r,s}
the smooth real quadric hypersurface of equation
\{x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{r}^{2}-x_{r+1}^{2}-...-x_{r+s}^{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{r+s}
.

Proposition 5.8
.

The complex quadric
Q_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
has three real forms :

-

Q^{5,0}
, which has no real points, and is equivariant both under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
and of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

-

Q^{4,1}
, which is rational, and is equivariant under an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
.

-

Q^{3,2}
, which is rational, and is equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

It is classical that
Q_{3}
has three real forms:
Q^{3,2}
and
Q^{4,1}
, which are both rational, and
Q^{5,0}
, which has no real points (see e.g.
[
TZ24
, Proposition 10.2]
). The equivariant real structures on
Q_{3}
are given in Proposition
4.3
(model (a2)). The real structures corresponding to
g\mapsto\sigma_{c}(g)eH
and to
g\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)eH
have an empty real locus and therefore correspond to the real form
Q^{5,0}
. Moreover, by
[
Nak89
, Lemma 1.19]
, the real form
Q^{4,1}
is not equivariant under any action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
. Hence, the real structure
g\mapsto\sigma_{s}(g)H
corresponds to the real form
Q^{3,2}
. Furthermore, the group
\text{PSU}_{2}
cannot be embedded into
\SO(3,2)
, which is the automorphism group of
Q^{3,2}
. Therefore, the real structure given by
g\mapsto\sigma_{c}(g)H
corresponds to the real form
Q^{4,1}
.
∎

We now consider the decomposable
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles
Y(a,b)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let

be two integers such that
a>b,a+b\leq 0

-

\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
, while the other two have no real points and are not equivariant under an action of a real form of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
.

-

If
a+b\neq 0
and
a+b
is even, then
Y(a,b)
has three real forms. The trivial one, which is equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
, and two without real points: one is equivariant under an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
, while the other one is not equivariant under an action of a real form of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
.

-

If
a+b=0
, then
Y(a,b)
has five real forms. Two that are equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
, a rational one and one without real points. Two that are equivariant under an action of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
but not of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
, a rational one and one without real points. And one without real points that is not equivariant under an action of a real form of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

We use
[
TZ24
, Propositions 5.7 and 5.12, and Remark 5.8 ]
and its proof to determine all the real forms of

and which ones are rational or without real points. Then, similarly as in the previous cases, we deduce which ones are equivariant under an action of
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
(respectively of
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
) from the results of Section
4
.
∎

It remains to treat the case of the models

with

even. The variety
Y_{0}(2)
corresponds to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\simeq\mathbb{P}(R_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(R_{1})\times\mathbb{P}(R_{1})

Proposition 5.10
.

Let

be an even integer. Then
Y_{0}(k)
has one rational
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-real form, one rational
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-real form, one
\SL_{2,\mathbb{R}}
-real form with no real points and one
\Spin_{3,\mathbb{R}}
-real form with no real points. Moreover,

has no other real form.

Those varieties appear as

in

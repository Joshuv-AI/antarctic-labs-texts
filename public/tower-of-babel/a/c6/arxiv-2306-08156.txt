Orthosymplectic Superinstanton Counting and Brane Dynamics
Taro Kimura and Yilu Shao

Abstract
We extend the study of superinstantons presented in [ KP19 ] to include orthosymplectic supergroup gauge theories, B n 0 | n 1 B_{n_{0}|n_{1}} , C n C_{n} , and D n 0 | n 1 D_{n_{0}|n_{1}} . We utilize equivariant localization to obtain the LMNS contour integral formula for the instanton partition function, and we investigate the Seiberg–Witten geometries associated with these theories. We also explore the brane configurations involving positive and negative branes together with O-planes that realize the orthosymplectic supergroup theories.



1
Introduction

Supergroups are symmetries of graded vector spaces with both commuting and anticommuting variables, and is naturally used in studying supersymmetry
[
DeW12
]
as transformation groups on supermanifolds. They are widely used in the study of disordered systems
[
Efe12
,
Weg16
]
such as the Parisi-Sourlas supersymmetry
[
PS79
]
. Conformal field theories with supergroups as superconformal symmetries also appear frequently in both string theory and condensed matter physics (see
[
QS13
]
for a review).

Although typically regarded as a global symmetry, supergroups can also be treated as a local gauge symmetry, leading to a
non–unitary
theory with negative energy spectrums and particles that break the spin-statistic theorem. While the perturbation theory of a
\mathrm{U}(n_{0}|n_{1})
theory cannot be distinguished from those of a
\mathrm{U}(n_{0}-n_{1}|0)=\mathrm{U}(n_{0}-n_{1})
theory
[
AGM91
,
Yos92
,
BVW99
,
Vaf01
,
DHJV18
]
, the nonperturbative dynamics of the former are markedly different due to the presence of the unstable vacua. As a result, proper treatment of the nonperturbative aspects of these theories is crucial. Much progress has been made in understanding their nonperturbative dynamics, ranging from the brane construction using
nagative branes

[
Vaf01
,
OT06
,
DHJV18
,
Nek20
,
NP19
]
, non–unitary holography
[
Vaf14
]
and supergroup Chern–Simons theory as the boundary condition of
\mathcal{N}=4

S
-duality
[
MW15
]
. It is also discovered that negative branes will lead to dynamical space-time signature change
[
DHJV18
]
which gives an explanation of the dualities mentioned in
[
Hul98
]
implying possible background-independence of M-theory. In the recent work
[
MSS22
,
SST23
]
the importance of “negative” instantons is also mentioned. For an overview of previous studies on supergroup gauge theory and supermatrix model, see a recent review
[
Kim23
]
.

In this paper, we focus on anti-selfdual Yang–Mills connections in supergroup gauge theories with
B_{n_{0}|n_{1}}
,
C_{n}
, and
D_{n_{0}|n_{1}}
type supergauge groups (or orthosymplectic), which are natural generalizations of the
A_{n_{0}|n_{1}}
type (or unitary) superinstantons studied in previous work
[
KP19
]
.

Our main objective is to derive the super-analog of the Losev-Moore-Nekrasov-Shatashvili (LMNS) contour integral formula using equivariant localization. We also investigate the
\widehat{A}_{1}
quiver realizations of orthosymplectic supergroup gauge theories and consider the Seiberg–Witten geometries
[
SW94b
,
SW94a
]
associated with these theories. Through instanton calculus and brane dynamics, we have confirmed their consistency. Additionally, we have established the Hanany-Witten brane constructions in type IIA string theory for both supergroup gauge theories and supergroup quiver gauge theories that employ orthosymplectic supergroups as gauge symmetries. To achieve this, we have utilized a combination of negative branes and orientifold planes. We have also explored the gauging process and the one-to-many correspondence
[
KS20
]
associated with non–uniqueness of the simple root decomposition of the super root system. Then we considered the inclusion of ON
-
planes to realize supergroup quiver gauge theories of
D
type.

The paper is organized as follows: In section
2
, we review the ADHM construction as well as the equivariant localization of unitary supergroup gauge theories performed in
[
KP19
]
. Then, in section
3

NS04

MW04
]
and then derive the corresponding Seiberg–Witten geometries. In section
5

2.1
Supergroup Gauge Theory

We consider an Yang–Mills theory with gauge supergroup
G=G_{0}|G_{1}
which has the corresponding Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\operatorname{Lie}G

A

\mathfrak{g}

and denote the corresponding curvature

G

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{YM}}[A]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{str}(F\wedge\star F)

(2.1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{g^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{tr}_{0}(F\wedge\star F)-\left(-\frac{1}{g^{2}}\right)\int_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{tr}_{1}(F\wedge\star F).

with

Evidences discovered about the structure of a supergroup (see sec.

G_{0}\times G_{1}
with the off-diagonal blocks (fermionic part) transforming under the bifundamental representation. Thus a supergroup gauge theory with
G=G_{0}|G_{1}
can be realized by a cyclic
\widehat{A}_{1}
quiver gauge theory:

G_{1}
G_{0}
G_{0}|G_{1}
g^{2}
g^{2}
-g^{2}

(2.2)

with a unphysical coupling constant
g^{2}_{1}=-g^{2}

[
DHJV18

General Notations

Throughout this paper, the notations are compatible with
[
Kim21
]

X

\operatorname{ch}\mathbf{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathbf{E}}\mathrm{e}^{x_{i}}.

(2.3)

The corresponding index functor can be written as

\mathbb{I}[\mathbf{E}]=\prod_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{E}}\left[x_{i}\right]

(2.4)

where the bracket symbol
[\ldots]
is defined by

[x]=\begin{cases}x&(\text{4d, cohomology})\\
1-\mathrm{e}^{-x}&(\text{5d, K-theory})\\
\theta\left(\mathrm{e}^{-x};p\right)&(\text{6d, elliptic})\end{cases}

(2.5)

for 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
theory on
\mathcal{S}
, 5d
\mathcal{N}=1
theory on
\mathcal{S}\times S^{1}
and 6d
\mathcal{N}=(1,0)
theory on
\mathcal{S}\times T^{2}
.

The Adams operations is defined by

\operatorname{ch}\mathbf{E}^{[p]}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathbf{E}}\mathrm{e}^{px_{i}}.

(2.6)

The character of the dual bundle
\mathbf{E}^{\vee}
is defined by

\ch\mathbf{E}^{\vee}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\mathbf{E}}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{i}}.

(2.7)

In terms of Adams operation we write
\mathbf{E}^{\vee}=\mathbf{E}^{[-1]}

We also denote the alternating sum of anti-symmetrizations of the bundle

\wedge_{y}\mathbf{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}(-y)^{i}\wedge^{i}\mathbf{X}

(2.8)

and use the shorthand notation
\wedge\mathbf{X}=\wedge_{1}\mathbf{X}

2.2.2
ADHM Construction

In a supergroup gauge theory, the anti-selfdual Yang–Mills connection is called the
superinstantons
. The instanton number
k
is promoted to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
(k_{0}|k_{1})
as well as the gauge group rank.

For a general quiver gauge theory defined over quiver
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
, we denote the gauge nodes
i\in\Gamma_{0}
, and edges (bifundamental hypermultiplets) by
\Gamma_{1}
. We assign to every node a supergroup
\mathrm{U}(n_{0}|n_{1})
. Consider the
(k_{0}|k_{1})
-instanton moduli space on
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and we introduce the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded supervector space

\displaystyle N=\mathbb{C}^{n_{0}|n_{1}}=\mathbb{C}_{0}^{n_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{1}^{n_{1}}=:N_{0}\oplus N_{1}

(2.9)

\displaystyle K=\mathbb{C}^{k_{0}|k_{1}}=\mathbb{C}_{0}^{k_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{1}^{k_{1}}=:K_{0}\oplus K_{1}

Here we have two instanton numbers
k_{0,1}
corresponding to
G_{0,1}
gauge nodes. The total instanton charge is given by
\mathsf{k}=\operatorname{sdim}K=k_{0}-k_{1}
because negative instantons carry negative topological charges. And it is worth noting that because of the effect of these negative-instantons
[
KP19
, sec 3.1.4]
there are infinite contributions at any sector of fixed instanton number. For example the
\mathsf{k}=1
sector may have
\mathsf{k}=k_{0}-k_{1}=1-0=2-1=3-2=...
. So any dualities between supergroup gauge theories can only be seen non-perturbatively.

Framing and Instanton Bundles

We define the graded framing and instanton bundles over the superinstanton moduli space as follows:

\mathbf{N}=\left(\mathbf{N}_{i}\right)_{i\in\Gamma_{0}},\quad\mathbf{K}=\left(\mathbf{K}_{i}\right)_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}

(2.10)

with

\mathbf{N}_{i}=\mathbf{N}_{i}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{i}^{1},\quad\mathbf{K}_{i}=\mathbf{K}_{i}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{K}_{i}^{1}

(2.11)

which are further decomposed as

\mathbf{N}_{i}^{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}}\mathbf{N}_{i,\alpha}^{\sigma},\quad\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}}\mathbf{K}_{i,\alpha}^{\sigma}

(2.12)

Their automorphism groups are

\mathrm{GL}(N)=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}\mathrm{GL}\left(n_{i,0}|n_{i,1}\right),\quad\mathrm{GL}(K)=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}\mathrm{GL}\left(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1}\right)

(2.13)

Their equivariant characters are given by summing over the Cartan tori respectively

\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i,\alpha}^{\sigma}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{a}_{i,\alpha}^{\sigma}},\qquad\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i,\alpha}^{\sigma}=\mathrm{e}^{\phi_{i,a}^{\sigma}}

(2.14)

In which the parameters are

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\mathsf{a}_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{i,n_{i,0}}^{0},\mathsf{a}_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{i,n_{i,1}}^{1}\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{N_{i}}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{N_{i}},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\phi_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\phi_{i,k_{i,0}}^{0},\phi_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\phi_{i,k_{i,1}}^{1}\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{K_{i}}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{K_{i}}

therefore the supercharacters are

\operatorname{sch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i}=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i}^{0}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i}^{1},\quad\operatorname{sch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i}=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i}^{0}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i}^{1}

(2.16)

Spacetime Bundle

The fiber of the cotangent bundle at the fixed point
o\in\mathcal{S}
is given by

\mathbf{Q}=T_{o}^{*}\mathcal{S}=\mathbf{Q}_{1}\oplus\mathbf{Q}_{2}

(2.17)

with the character

\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{i}=\mathrm{e}^{\epsilon_{i}}=q_{i}\quad(i=1,2).

(2.18)

in which
\epsilon_{1,2}
are the equivariant parameters on
\Omega
-background. We also denote

\wedge\mathbf{Q}_{1,2}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\wedge^{k}\mathbf{Q}_{1,2},\quad\wedge\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\wedge^{k}\mathbf{Q},

(2.19)

with characters

\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\wedge\mathbf{Q}_{1,2}=1-q_{1,2},\quad\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\wedge\mathbf{Q}=\left(1-q_{1}\right)\left(1-q_{2}\right)

(2.20)

and
\epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}
. We also define
\wedge^{2}\mathbf{Q}=\operatorname{det}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}_{1}\mathbf{Q}_{2}
, with

\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\wedge^{2}\mathbf{Q}=q_{1}q_{2}=:q

(2.21)

Observable Bundle

We define the observable bundle by pullback at the fixed point

\mathbf{Y}_{o}=i_{o}^{*}\mathbf{Y}_{\mathcal{S}}=\left(\mathbf{N}_{i}-\wedge\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i}\right)_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}.

(2.22)

It also has a graded structure
\mathbf{Y}_{i}=\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{1}
.

2.2.3
Equivariant Index Formula

In analogy to the ordinary
\mathrm{U}(n)
gauge theory case, the vector multiplet and the hyper- and half-hypermultiplet bundles can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{i}=\frac{\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{\vee}\mathbf{Y}_{i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}}\,,

(2.23a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f}}=-\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{i}^{\vee}\mathbf{Y}_{i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}}\,,\qquad\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af}}=-\frac{\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{\vee}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}}\,.

(2.23b)

The
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\sigma}
s are the matter bundles, whose fibers are the space of virtual zero modes of the Dirac operator in the instanton background. The corresponding characters are

\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\sigma}=\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{f}}}\mathrm{e}^{m_{i,f}^{\sigma}},\quad\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{i}^{\sigma}=\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{af}}}\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{m}_{i,f}^{\sigma}}

(2.24a)

\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_{e}=\mathrm{e}^{m_{e}}=:\mu_{e}

(2.24b)

Expanding the above formula gives

\displaystyle\sch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\text{inst}}

\displaystyle=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}_{i,0}^{\text{inst}}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}_{i,1}^{\text{inst}}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=0,1}(-1)^{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}_{i,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{inst}}\,,

(2.25a)

\displaystyle\sch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{e}^{\text{inst}}

\displaystyle=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{e,0}^{\text{inst}}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{e,1}^{\text{inst}}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=0,1}(-1)^{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{e,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{inst}}\,

(2.25b)

\displaystyle\sch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{(a)f,inst}}

\displaystyle=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{i,0}^{\text{(a)f,inst}}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{i,1}^{\text{(a)f,inst}}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=0,1}(-1)^{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{i,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{(a)f,inst}}

(2.25c)

where the components are

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{i,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{inst}}

\displaystyle=-\det\mathbf{Q}^{\vee}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\sigma\vee}\mathbf{N}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}-\mathbf{N}^{\sigma\vee}_{i}\mathbf{K}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}+\wedge\mathbf{Q}^{\vee}\cdot\mathbf{K}^{\sigma\vee}_{i}\mathbf{K}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}

(2.26a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{i,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{f,inst}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{M}^{\sigma\vee}_{i}\mathbf{K}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}

(2.26b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{i,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{af,inst}}

\displaystyle=\det\mathbf{Q^{\vee}}\cdot\mathbf{K}^{\sigma\vee}_{i}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{i}

(2.26c)

By taking the supercharacter of
\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\text{inst }}
we get the contour integral formula or the
Losev–Moore–Nekrasov–Shatashvili (LMNS) formula

[
LNS99
,
LNS98
,
MNS00
]
of instanton part of the instanton partition function .

Z_{n,k}^{\text{inst }}=\frac{1}{k_{0}!k_{1}!}\frac{\left[-\epsilon_{12}\right]^{k_{0}+k_{1}}}{\left[-\epsilon_{1,2}\right]^{k_{0}+k_{1}}}\oint_{\mathrm{JK}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=0,1\\
a=1,\ldots,k_{\sigma}\end{subarray}}\frac{d\phi_{a}^{\sigma}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\prod_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=0,1}z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{vec}}z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{f}}z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{af}}

(2.27)

with

\displaystyle z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{vec}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}P_{\sigma}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma})^{-1}\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}+\epsilon_{12})^{-1}\prod_{a\neq b}^{k_{\sigma}}\mathscr{S}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}-\phi_{b}^{\sigma})^{-1}&(\sigma=\sigma^{\prime})\\
\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma^{\prime}}}P_{\sigma}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma^{\prime}})\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}\widetilde{P}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}+\epsilon_{12})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\ldots,k_{\sigma}\\
b=1,\ldots,k_{\sigma^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\mathscr{S}(\phi_{b}^{\sigma^{\prime}}-\phi_{a}^{\sigma})&(\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime})\end{cases}

(2.28a)

\displaystyle z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{f}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}P^{\text{f}}_{\sigma}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma})\\
\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}P^{\text{f}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma})^{-1}\end{cases}\qquad z_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{af}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}\widetilde{P}^{\text{af}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}+\epsilon_{12})&(\sigma=\sigma^{\prime})\\
\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k_{\sigma}}\widetilde{P}^{\text{af}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}+\epsilon_{12})^{-1}&(\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime})\end{cases}

(2.28b)

with the gauge and matter polynomials

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}(\phi)=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{\sigma}}[\phi-\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}]\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{\sigma}}[-\phi+\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}]\,,

(2.29a)

\displaystyle P^{\text{f}}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n^{\text{f}}_{\sigma}}[\phi-m_{f}^{\sigma}]\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{\text{af}}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n^{\text{af}}_{\sigma}}[-\phi+\widetilde{m}_{f}^{\sigma}]\,.

(2.29b)

and the
\mathscr{S}
function

\mathscr{S}(\phi)=\frac{\left[\phi-\epsilon_{1}\right]\left[\phi-\epsilon_{2}\right]}{[\phi]\left[\phi-\epsilon_{12}\right]}.

(2.30)

Notice that the contour integral formula (
2.27
) is actually the same as an
\widehat{A}_{1}
quiver gauge theory. These results first appears in
[

, eq. (3.18)]
and the above is modified from

2.3
The Seiberg–Witten Curve

For ordinary pure
\mathrm{U}(n)
super Yang–Mills, the Seiberg–Witten curve is given by

y+\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{y}=\operatorname{det}(x-\phi),

(2.31)

in which
\phi=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})
is the adjoint complex scalar field in the
\mathcal{N}=2
vector multiplet with its vacuum expectation values (or the Coulomb branch parameters)
\mathsf{a}_{i}
.
\mathfrak{q}
is the instanton counting parameter, for definition see (
4.19
). For the
\mathrm{U}(n_{0}|n_{1})
theory, we simply replace the determinant with the superdeterminant

y+\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{y}=\operatorname{sdet}(x-\phi)

(2.32)

with

\operatorname{sdet}(x-\phi)=\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{0}}\left(x-\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}^{0}\right)}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{1}}\left(x-\mathsf{a}_{\alpha}^{1}\right)}

(2.33)

since we can also diagonalize the matrix as

\phi=\operatorname{diag}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})=\operatorname{diag}(\mathsf{a}^{0}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}^{0}_{n_{0}},\mathsf{a}^{1}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}^{1}_{n_{1}})\;.

(2.34)

Finally we get the corresponding Seiberg–Witten curve

y+\frac{q}{y}=\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{0}}\left(x-\mathsf{a}^{0}_{\alpha}\right)}{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n_{1}}\left(x-\mathsf{a}^{1}_{\alpha}\right)}.

(2.35)

This result
[
DHJV18
, eq. (8.4)]
can be derived by brane constructions with negative branes as well as instanton calculus.

3
Orthosymplectic Superinstantons

In this section we begin to discuss the orthosymplectic supergroup gauge theories. We also note that at the level of partition function, the
C_{n}
supergroup makes no difference with the
D_{n_{0}|n_{1}}
supergroup with
n_{0}=1
and
n_{1}=n-1
.

3.1
Total Framing, Instanton and Observable Bundles

Since orthosymplectic supergroups are “real” groups, we define its “total” framing and instanton bundles by only taking the real part, i.e.

\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{N}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{i}^{\vee}\right),\quad\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{K}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\vee}\right).

(3.1)

and are graded

\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}=\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{1},\quad\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}=\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{1}.

(3.2)

The parameters are

\displaystyle\mathrm{a}_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\pm\mathrm{a}_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\pm\mathrm{a}_{i,n_{i,0}}^{0},(0),\pm\mathrm{a}_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\pm\mathrm{a}_{i,n_{i,1}}^{1}\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{N_{i}}.

(3.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\pm\phi_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\pm\phi_{i,k_{i,0}}^{0},\pm\phi_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\pm\phi_{i,k_{i,1}}^{1},(0)\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{K_{i}}.

So their supercharacters are

\displaystyle\sch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}

\displaystyle=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{0}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{1}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathsf{a}^{0}_{\alpha}}+\chi_{n}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathsf{a}^{1}_{\alpha}}

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\sch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}

\displaystyle=\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{0}-\ch_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{1}=\sum_{a=1}^{k_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\phi^{0}_{a}}-\sum_{a=1}^{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\phi^{1}_{a}}-\chi_{k}.

(3.4b)

Here
\chi_{n}=0,1
acts as

\displaystyle\chi_{n}=1,\ G=\OSp(2n_{0}+1|n_{1})

\displaystyle\leadsto B_{n_{0}|n_{1}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\chi_{n}=0,\ G=\OSp(2n_{0}|n_{1})

\displaystyle\leadsto D_{n_{0}|n_{1}}

and we denote
k_{1}=2\xi+\chi_{k}
, with
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\chi_{k}\in\{0,1\}
indicates the parity of
k_{1}
.

The total observable bundle is defined by

\mathbf{Y}_{i}=\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}-\wedge\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}

(3.6)

which is graded by

\mathbf{Y}_{i}=\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{0}\oplus\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{1}

(3.7)

as well.

3.2
Vector Multiplet

For gauge group
G_{i}=\OSp(2n_{0}+\chi_{n}|n_{1})
, contributions from the vector multiplet can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{2}-\mathbf{Y}_{i}^{[2]}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}})

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\wedge\mathbf{Q})}(\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{2}-\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{[2]}-2\wedge\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}\,\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}+(\wedge\mathbf{Q})^{2}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{2}+(\wedge\mathbf{Q})^{[2]}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{[2]})

where the perturbative part is

\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\rm pert}=\frac{1}{2\left(\wedge\mathbf{Q}\right)}\left(\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{2}-\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{[2]}\right)

(3.9)

and the remaining is the instanton part

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\rm inst}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\wedge\mathbf{Q})}\left[-2(\wedge\mathbf{Q})\cdot\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}\,\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}+(\wedge\mathbf{Q})^{2}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{2}+(\wedge\mathbf{Q})^{[2]}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{[2]}\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left[-\mathbf{N}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{\sigma}\,\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}(\wedge\mathbf{Q})\cdot(\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{\sigma}\,\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{\sigma^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{2}(1+(\wedge\mathbf{Q}_{1,2}))\cdot(\mathbf{K}_{i,\mathrm{tot}}^{\sigma})^{[2]}\right].

We also remark here that in the case of a quiver gauge theory, the contributions from the vector multiplet of the neighbouring nodes differ with a sign, i.e.

\mathbf{V}^{\rm SpO}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\mathbf{Y}_{j}^{2}+\mathbf{Y}_{j}^{[2]}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}})

(3.11)

due to the fact that in order not to violate the global symmetry, the neighbouring node of an orthosymplectic (
\OSp

This can also be seen from the brane construction in sec.

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\pm\mathsf{a}_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\pm\mathsf{a}_{i,n_{i,0}}^{0},\pm\mathsf{a}_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\pm\mathsf{a}_{i,n_{i,1}}^{1},(0)\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{N_{i}}.

(3.12)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\pm\phi_{i,1}^{0},\ldots,\pm\phi_{i,k_{i,0}}^{0},(0),\pm\phi_{i,1}^{1},\ldots,\pm\phi_{i,k_{i,1}}^{1}\right)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathsf{T}_{K_{i}}.

3.3
Fundamental and Bifundamental (Half-)Hypermultiplets

More generally, for a quiver gauge theory corresponding to quiver
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
with ortheosymplectic gauge nodes, we can have (anti-)fundamental hypermultiplets and bifundamental half-hypermultiplets
[
HKS11a
,
Zha19
]
.

3.3.1
Fundamental Hypermultiplets

In order to be consistent with global symmetries, the (anti-)fundamental hypermultiplet associated to an orthosymplectic (
\OSp
) gauge node should have an SpO type flavor symmetry. For a(n) (anti-)fundamental hypermultiplet with flavor symmetry
\text{SpO}(n^{\mathrm{f},0}_{i}|2n^{\mathrm{f},1}_{i}+\chi_{f})
connected to the
i
-th node, we assign the total matter bundles
[
KP19
,
Kim21
]
by

\left(\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\right)_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{M}_{i}+\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\vee}\right),\quad\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\right)_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{i}+\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{i}^{\vee}\right)

(3.23)

with graded mass parameter
m_{i,f}^{\sigma}
or
\tilde{m}^{\sigma}_{i,f}
, and
\chi_{\mathrm{(a)f}}=0,1
depending on the parity. The supercharacters associated with the flavor symmetry are

\displaystyle\operatorname{sch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{tot},i}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{tot},i}^{0}-\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{tot},i}^{1}=\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{f}}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm m_{i,f}^{0}}-\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{f}}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm m_{i,f}^{1}}-\chi_{\mathrm{f}},

(3.24a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{sch}_{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}^{0}-\operatorname{ch}_{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}^{1}=\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{af}}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\tilde{m}_{i,f}^{0}}-\sum_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{af}}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\tilde{m}_{i,f}^{1}}-\chi_{\mathrm{af}}.

(3.24b)

Then the (anti-)fundamental hypermultiplet contributions to the (extended) tangent bundle of the instanton moduli space can be written as

\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f}}=-\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\,\mathbf{Y}_{i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}}:=\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f,pert}}+\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f,inst}},\quad\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af}}=-\frac{\mathbf{Y}_{i}\,\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}}:=\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af,pert}}+\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af,pert}}

(3.25)

By expanding (
3.25
) we can extract the perturbative and instanton contributions

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f,pert}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\,\mathbf{N}_{{\rm tot},i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}},\hskip 13.50008pt\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f,inst}}={\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\,{\mathbf{K}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}

(3.26a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af,pert}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathbf{N}_{{\rm tot},i}\,{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}}{\wedge\mathbf{Q}},\quad\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af,inst}}={\mathbf{K}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}\,\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\mathrm{tot},i}.

(3.26b)

Recall (
3.4
), we can now write the contributions of the fundamental hypermultiplets to the instanton partition function as

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{\text{f,inst}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}\left[\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{f,inst}}\right]

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{i,0}}\frac{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{f}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}\pm m_{i,f}^{0}\right]}{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{f}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}\pm m_{i,f}^{1}\right]\left([\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}]\right)^{\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a=1}^{k_{i,1}}\frac{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{f}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}\pm m_{i,f}^{1}\right]\left([\pm m_{i,f}^{1}]\right)^{\chi_{k}}([0])^{\chi_{\mathrm{f}}\chi_{k}}}{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{f}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}\pm m_{i,f}^{0}\right]\left([\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}]\right)^{\chi_{\mathrm{f}}}\left([\pm m_{i,f}^{0}]\right)^{\chi_{k}}},

and

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{\text{af,inst}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}\left[\mathbf{H}_{i}^{\text{af,inst}}\right]

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{i,0}}\frac{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{af}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}\pm m_{i,f}^{0}\right]}{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{af}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}\pm m_{i,f}^{1}\right]\left([\pm\phi_{i,a}^{0}]\right)^{\chi_{\mathrm{af}}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a=1}^{k_{i,1}}\frac{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,1}^{\mathrm{af}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}\pm m_{i,f}^{1}\right]\left([\pm m_{i,f}^{1}]\right)^{\chi_{k}}([0])^{\chi_{\mathrm{af}}\chi_{k}}}{\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,0}^{\mathrm{af}}}\left[\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}\pm m_{i,f}^{0}\right]\left([\pm\phi_{i,a}^{1}]\right)^{\chi_{\mathrm{af}}}\left([\pm m_{i,f}^{0}]\right)^{\chi_{k}}},.

which are the superanalogs of
[
Zha19
, eq. (42)]
. Here we found that when we set
\chi_{\mathrm{(a)f},k}=1
the instanton partition function vanishes due to the fermonic zero modes. Similiar phenomenon can be seen if we consider a symplectic gauge node and an orthogonal flavor node, and take one of the mass parameters to zero (corresponds to
\chi_{\mathrm{(a)f}}=1
).

We can also define the supermatter polynomials similiar to (
2.29
) by

P_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{f}}(\phi)=\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{f}}}\left[\pm\phi\pm m_{i,f}^{\sigma}\right],\quad\widetilde{P}_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{af}}(\phi)=\prod_{f=1}^{n_{i,\sigma}^{\mathrm{af}}}\left[\pm\phi\pm\widetilde{m}_{i,f}^{\sigma}\right]

(3.29)

So in terms of supermatter polynomials we can write the fundamental hypermultiplets as

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{\text{f,inst}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left\{P_{i,\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{f}}(\phi_{i,a}^{\sigma})\right\}^{\Pi(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})}

(3.30a)

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{\text{af,inst}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left\{\widetilde{P}_{i,\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{af}}(\phi_{i,a}^{\sigma})\right\}^{\Pi(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})}.

(3.30b)

with
\Pi(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=(-1)^{-(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})}
.

The contributions from massless bifundamental half-hypermultiplet between
i
-th and
j
-th nodes, i.e. the edge

is

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\text{pert }}_{e}

3.4
Quiver Gauge Theory

Let
\Gamma(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
be a quiver with gauge nodes
G_{i}
of orthosymplectic supergroups. We can formally write down the instanton partition function by combining the ingredients from section
3
. The instanton partition function for instanton number
(k_{0}|k_{1})
reads:

Z^{\text{inst.}}_{(k_{0}|k_{1})}=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}\frac{1}{\left|W_{i}\right|}\oint_{\mathsf{T}_{K}}\prod_{a,\sigma}\frac{d\phi^{\sigma}_{i,a}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}Z_{i}^{\text{vec,inst.}}Z_{i}^{\text{f,inst.}}Z_{i}^{\text{af,inst.}}\left(\prod_{e\in\Gamma_{1}}Z^{\text{bf,inst.}}_{e}\right).

(3.34)

where
\left|W_{i}\right|
is the order of the Weyl group of
G^{\vee}_{i}
. For the
\OSp
case (
i\in\Gamma_{0}^{\circ}
) with instanton number
(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1}=2\xi_{i}+\chi_{i,k})
we have
G_{i}^{\vee}=\mathrm{SpO}(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1})
and

\absolutevalue{W_i(\mathrm{SpO}(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1}))}=2^{k_{i,0}}(k_{i,0})!2^{\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}!

(3.35)

For the SpO case (
i\in\Gamma_{0}^{\bullet}
) with instanton number
(k_{i,0}=2\zeta_{i}+\chi_{i,k}|k_{i,1})
we have
G_{i}^{\vee}=\OSp(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1})
and

\absolutevalue{W_i(\OSp(k_{i,0}|k_{i,1}))}=2^{\zeta_{i}}\zeta_{i}!2^{k_{i,1}}(k_{i,1})!

(3.36)

The gauge nodes of the quiver theory should be alternating OSp and SpO nodes, as we have claimed before. This can also be seen from the brane constructions in sec.
5.3
.

n^{f}
fundamental matter multiplets, its Seiberg–Witten curve can be written as

y+\frac{P(x)\Lambda^{2n-n^{\mathrm{f}}}}{y}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n}(x-\mathsf{a}_{\alpha})

(4.11)

with the matter polynomial

P(x)=\prod_{f=1}^{n^{\mathrm{f}}}\left(x-m_{f}\right).

(4.12)

Now we perform a change of variable

, the curve (
4.11

y+\frac{\Lambda^{2n-n^{\mathrm{f}}}}{y}=\frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n}(x-\mathsf{a}_{\alpha})}{\sqrt{P(x)}}

(4.13)

which has similar form as (
2.35
). The Seiberg–Witten curve of a
\mathrm{SU}(n_{0}|n_{1})
supergroup gauge theory is the same as an
\SU(n_{0})
SQCD (super quantum chromodynamics) with
2n_{1}
flavors with pairwisely identified Coulomb branch parameters
[
DHJV18
]
. Thus an
\mathrm{SU}(n^{\mathrm{f}}_{0}|n^{\mathrm{f}}_{1})
flavor group will generate a matter polynomial of the form

P(x)=\frac{\prod_{f=1}^{n^{\mathrm{f}}_{0}}\left(x-m_{f}\right)}{\prod_{f=1}^{n^{\mathrm{f}}_{1}}\left(x-m_{f}\right)}.

(4.14)

For the superflavor group
\mathrm{SU}(n^{\mathrm{f}}_{0}|n^{\mathrm{f}}_{1})
we also define
n^{\mathrm{f}}=n^{\mathrm{f}}_{0}-n^{\mathrm{f}}_{1}
.

In our case, we can think of the
C_{n}
gauge theory as gauging a
\SU(2n)
gauge theory with massless
\SU(4^{-})
flavors. The
\SU(4^{-})
node gives the contribution

\sqrt{P(x)}=\frac{1}{\left(\prod_{f=1}^{4}(x-m_{f})\right)^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{4}}}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}

(4.15)

combining with the curve of
\SU(2n)
theory and rename the variables we get

y+\frac{\Lambda^{4n+4}}{y}=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{n}\left(x^{2}-\mathsf{a}_{l}^{2}\right)+2/x^{2}}{1/x^{2}}=x^{2}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\left(x^{2}-\mathsf{a}_{l}^{2}\right)+2

which is exactly (

(see

]
also) there is an extra

It is stated in

Here based on the observation of (

And the rule is that we generate the fundamental character by iWeyl reflection
[
NP23
]
. That is, starting with
y_{i}
, and continuously replacing the numerator by the following rule

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle\rightarrow y_{0}\cdot\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{0}^{2}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}=\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{0}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}

(4.18)

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow y_{1}\cdot\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{y_{1}^{2}}\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{y_{1}}\mathfrak{q}_{1}

where
\mathfrak{q}_{i}

\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{2n_{i}-2\sum_{j}n_{j}}

(4.19)

with
i,j
neighboring nodes. For the superconformal case,
b_{i}=0
. The coupling constant becomes a dimensionless parameter
\lambda_{i}
.

In the case of
\widehat{A}_{1}
quiver, we have

y_{0}+\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{0}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}+...

(4.20)

which goes on infinitely.

4.2.2
Supergroup Case

However, the series is finite if we are considering the
\widehat{A}_{1}
quiver gauge associated to a supergroup gauge theory. As is seen in (
2.1
), the coupling constant satisfies
\tau_{1}=-\tau_{0}
, or in exponential form,
\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\mathfrak{q}_{0}^{-1}
. We notice that under the rule (
4.18
), the following

\displaystyle\frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}\cdot\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{0}^{2}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}=\frac{y_{1}}{y_{0}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\frac{y_{1}}{y_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{y_{1}}{y_{0}}\cdot\frac{y_{0}^{2}}{y_{1}^{2}}\mathfrak{q}_{1}=\frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}\mathfrak{q}_{0}^{-1}

forms an involution. The affine character is

\chi(y_{0,1}):\ \frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}+\frac{y_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{0}}{y_{0}}.

(4.22)

Thus the Seiberg–Witten curve for a general supergroup gauge theory takes the form

\frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}+\frac{y_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{0}}{y_{0}}=\frac{\mathsf{T}_{0}(x)}{\mathsf{T}_{1}(x)}.

(4.23)

with
\mathsf{T}_{i}
the gauge polynomials. Let
y=y_{0}/y_{1}
and
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{0}
, we have the more familiar expression

y+\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{y}=\frac{\mathsf{T}_{0}(x)}{\mathsf{T}_{1}(x)}.

(4.24)

This result is rigorously derived from instanton calculus in
[
NP23
, eq. 7.81]
including also the case when
\mathfrak{q}_{0}\neq\mathfrak{q}_{1}
.

6
Outlooks

In this study, we presented the superinstanton counting for orthosymplectic supergroup gauge theories, derived the corresponding Seiberg–Witten geometry, and provided brane constructions for these theories. While we partially solved some of the problems raised in
[
KP19
, sec. 8.1]
, there is still much to be explored.

ADHM Constructions

Supersymmetric instantons can be constructed systematically using ADHM construction, and the instanton partition function is given by the equivariant localization on the instanton moduli space determined by the ADHM equations. However, we did not present the explicit mathematical construction of the superinstanton moduli space in this paper. A rigorous mathematical definition of the superinstanton moduli space (as well as for the unitary supergroup case) will be of both mathematical and physical interest.

Exceptional Supergroups

The generalization of supergroup gauge theories with exceptional supergroups such as
G_{3}
or
F_{4}
may also have physical implications.

Relations with Integrable Systems

In
[

Appendix A
Supermathematics

A.1
Super Linear Algebra

We will give a very brief introduction on supergroups first, following
[
Kim21
]
. For more about supergroups and its mathematical meanings, we refer the readers to
[
FSS96
,
DeW12
,
Var04
,
Ber87
]
.

A
supervector space
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space consists of both commuting and anti-commuting variables

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}.

(A.1)

Its
superdimension

\operatorname{sdim}V=\sum_{\sigma=0,1}(-1)^{\sigma}\operatorname{dim}V_{\sigma}=\operatorname{dim}V_{0}-\operatorname{dim}V_{1}

(A.2)

can be negative.

A linear map
M
between supervector space
V
and
W
can be written in terms of a
supermatrix

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}M_{00}&M_{01}\\
M_{10}&M_{11}\end{array}\right)

(A.3)

with
M_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{Hom}\left(V_{\sigma},W_{\sigma^{\prime}}\right)
. The corresponding
superdeterminant
(or
Berezinian
) is

\operatorname{sdet}M=\frac{\operatorname{det}\left(M_{00}-M_{10}M_{11}^{-1}M_{01}\right)}{\operatorname{det}M_{11}}.

(A.4)

And the
supertrace
is

\operatorname{str}M=\operatorname{tr}_{0}M-\operatorname{tr}_{1}M=\operatorname{tr}M_{00}-\operatorname{tr}M_{11}.

(A.5)

Also, we define the
supertranspositon
of a super matrix by

M^{\text{st }}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}M_{00}&M_{01}\\
M_{10}&M_{11}\end{array}\right)^{\text{st }}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{00}^{\mathrm{t}}&M_{01}^{\mathrm{t}}\\
-M_{10}^{\mathrm{t}}&M_{11}^{\mathrm{t}}\end{array}\right)

(A.6)

where
A^{\mathrm{t}}
denotes the ordinary transpose operation. We can check that
\left(M_{1}M_{2}\right)^{\text{st }}=M_{2}^{\text{st }}M_{1}^{\text{st }}
, but
\left(M^{\text{st }}\right)^{\text{st }}\neq M
in general.

The Hermitian conjugation of a supermatrix is defined as follows,

M^{\dagger}=\bar{M}^{\mathrm{st}}

(A.7)

which satisfies
\left(M^{\dagger}\right)^{\dagger}=M
.

A.2.1

Like the ordinary classification of semi-simple Lie groups (algebras), we can also classify classical supergroups (superalgebras). The results are roughly given by the following table

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\hline\cr\hline\cr\text{Name}&\text{Alternative name}&\text{Bosonic subgroup}\\
\hline\cr A_{n|m}&\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|m+1)&A_{n}\oplus A_{m}\oplus T_{1}\\
A_{n|n}&\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)&A_{n}\oplus A_{n}\\

\hline\cr F_{4}&-&A_{1}\oplus B_{3}\\

(A.8)

A.2.2
Unitary Supergroup

We define the squared norm

|\Psi|^{2}=\operatorname{str}\left(\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}\right).

(A.9)

for
\Psi\in\mathbb{C}^{n|m}
. Then the
A
-type (
unitary supergroup
)
\mathrm{U}(n|m)
is defined as the isometry group with respect to the supervector space
\mathbb{C}^{n|m}
such that

|\Psi|^{2}=|U\Psi|^{2},\quad U^{\dagger}=U^{-1}

(A.10)

Thus we have

\mathrm{U}(n|m)=\left\{U\in\mathrm{GL}\left(\mathbb{C}^{n|m}\right)\mid U^{\dagger}=U^{-1}\right\}

(A.11)

The bosonic subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(n|m)
is given by
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(m)
, and the fermonic part is given by
\mathbf{n}\times\bar{\mathbf{m}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{n}}\times\mathbf{m}
bifundamental representations with the dimension
2nm
.

A.2.3
Orthosymplectic Supergroup

Consider the real supervector space
\mathbb{R}^{n|2m}
. The squared norm is defined by:

|\Psi|^{2}=\Psi^{\mathrm{t}}\Omega\Psi=\operatorname{str}\left(\Omega\Psi\Psi^{\mathrm{t}}\right)

(A.12)

with the skew-symmetric form

\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{1}_{n}&0\\
0&\mathbb{1}_{m}\otimes\mathbf{j}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathbf{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)

(A.13)

Then the isometry group of
\mathbb{R}^{n|2m}
is given by

|\Psi|^{2}=|O\Psi|^{2},\quad O^{\text{st }}\Omega O=\Omega.

(A.14)

Thus we have

\operatorname{OSp}(n|m)=\left\{O\in\mathrm{GL}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n|2m}\right)\mid O^{\mathrm{st}}\Omega O=\Omega\right\}

(A.15)

The bosonic part of
\OSp(n|m)
is thus given by
\mathrm{O}(n)\times\mathrm{Sp}(m)
, and the fermonic part is given by
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{2m}
bifundamental representation with the dimension
2nm
.

A.2.4
Superalgebras

Superalgebra is also graded

\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{A}_{1}

(A.16)

with the supercommutator

[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{|a||b|}ba

(A.17)

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
. Here we denote the parity of an element
x\in V_{\sigma}
by
|x|=\sigma
for
\sigma=0,1
, which is called even/bosonic for
\sigma=0
, and odd/fermionic for
\sigma=1
.
The supercommutator also satisfies the super version of Jacobi identity

[a,[b,c]]=[[a,b],c]+(-1)^{|a||b|}[b,[a,c]].

(A.18)

For example we denote

\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m}=\left\{a\in\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{A}_{1},\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{A}_{0}=n,\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{A}_{1}=m|\operatorname{str}a=0\right\},

(A.19)

which corresponds to the
A
-type supergroup
A_{n-1|m-1}
.

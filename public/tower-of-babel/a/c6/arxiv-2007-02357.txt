Existence and uniqueness of some Cauchy Type Problems in fractional q q -difference calculus
L.E. Persson, S. Shaimardan, N.S. Tokmagambetov

Abstract
. In this paper we derive a sufficient condition for the existence of a unique solution of a Cauchy type q q -fractional problem (involving the fractional q q -derivative of Riemann-Liouville type) for some nonlinear differential equations. The key technique is to first prove that this Cauchy type q q -fractional problem is equivalent to a corresponding Volterra q q -integral equation. Moreover, we define the q q -analogue of the Hilfer fractional derivative or composite fractional derivative operator and prove some similar new equivalence, existence and uniqueness results as above. Finally, some examples are presented to illustrate our main results in cases where we can even give concrete formulas for these unique solutions.

Preliminaries

First we recall some elements of
q
-calculus, for more information see e.g. the books
[
23
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
29
]
. Throughout this paper, we assume that
0<q<1
and
0\leq a<b<\infty
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then a
q
-real number
[\alpha]_{q}
is defined by

[\alpha]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{\alpha}}{1-q},

where
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{q\rightarrow 1}\frac{1-q^{\alpha}}{1-q}=\alpha
.

We introduce for
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}=1,\;(a;q)_{n}=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n}\left(1-q^{k}a\right),\;(q;a)_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a,q)^{n}_{q},\;and\;(a;q)_{\alpha}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\alpha}a;q)_{\infty}}.

The
q
-analogue of the power function
(a-b)_{q}^{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle(a-b)_{q}^{\alpha}{:=}a^{\alpha}\frac{(\frac{a}{b};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\alpha}\frac{a}{b};q)_{\infty}}.

Notice that
(a-b)_{q}^{\alpha}=a^{\alpha}(\frac{a}{b};q)_{\alpha}
.

The
q
-analogue of the binomial coefficients
[n]_{q}!
are defined by

[n]_{q}!:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{1,\mathrm{\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;if\;n}=\mathrm{0,}}\\
{[1]_{q}\times[2]_{q}\times\cdots\times[n]_{q},\mathrm{\;if\;n}\in\mathrm{N,\;\;}}\end{array}\right.

The gamma function
\Gamma_{q}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{q}(x):=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{x};q)_{\infty}}(1-q)^{1-x},\;\;\;

for any
x>0
. Moreover, it yields that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{q}(x)[x]_{q}=\Gamma_{q}(x+1).

The
q
-analogue differential operator
D_{q}f(x)
is

\displaystyle D_{q}f(x):=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{x(1-q)},

and the
q
-derivatives
D^{n}_{q}(f(x))
of higher order are defined inductively as follows:

\displaystyle D_{q}^{0}(f(x)):=f(x),\;\;\;D^{n}_{q}(f(x)):=D_{q}\left(D_{q}^{n-1}f(x)\right),(n=1,2,3,\dots)

Notice that

(2.2)

\displaystyle D_{q}\left[(x-b)_{q}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha]_{q}(x-b)_{q}^{\alpha-1}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle D_{q}\left[(a-x)_{q}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\alpha]_{q}(a-qx)_{q}^{\alpha-1}.

The
q
-integral (or Jackson integral)
\int\limits_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x
is defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{q}x:=(1-q)a\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m}f(aq^{m})

for
a=0
and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x=\int\limits_{0}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x-\int\limits_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{q}x,

for
0<a<b
. Notice that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}D_{q}f(x)d_{q}x=f(b)-f(a)

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{a}^{x}f(x)g(t)d_{q}td_{q}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}\int\limits_{qt}^{b}f(x)g(t)d_{q}xd_{q}t.

Moreover, the multiple
q
-integral
\left(I^{n}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)
is

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left(I^{n}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{x}\int\limits_{a}^{t}\int\limits_{a}^{t_{n-1}}\dots\int\limits_{a}^{t_{2}}d_{q}t_{1}d_{q}t_{2}{\dots}d_{q}t_{n-1}d_{q}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(n)}\int\limits_{a}^{x}(x-qt)_{q}^{n-1}f(t)d_{q}t.

Definition 2.1
.

The Riemann-Liouville
q
-fractional integrals
I^{\alpha}_{a+}f
of order
\alpha>0

are defined by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f\right)(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)}\int\limits_{a}^{x}(x-qt)_{q}^{\alpha-1}f(t)d_{q}t.

Definition 2.2
.

The Riemann-Liouville fractional
q
-derivative
D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f
of order
\alpha>0
is defined by

\displaystyle\left(D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f\right)(x):=\left(D^{[\alpha]}_{q,a+}I^{[\alpha]-\alpha}_{q,a+}f\right)(x).

Notice that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}(t-a)_{q}^{\lambda}\right)(x)=\frac{\Gamma_{q}(\lambda+1)}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+\lambda+1)}(x-a)_{q}^{\alpha+\lambda},

for
\lambda\in(-1,\infty)
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
we define the space
L^{p}_{q}=L^{p}_{q}[a,b]
by

\displaystyle L^{p}_{q}[a,b]:=\left\{f:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}|f(x)|^{p}d_{q}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\}.

Definition 2.4
.

A function

is called

-absolutely continuous

\exists\varphi\in L^{1}_{q}[a,b]

\displaystyle f(x)=f(a)+\int\limits_{a}^{x}\varphi(t)d_{q}t,

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[n-1]_{q}!}\int\limits_{a}^{x}(x-qt)_{a}^{n-1}\varphi(t)d_{t}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}(x-a)_{q}^{k},

We also need the following result of independent interest:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

and

For

is as usual defined by

), (

) and (

), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f\|_{L^{p}_{q}[a,b]}

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\int\limits_{a}^{b}\left[\int\limits_{a}^{x}(x-qt)_{q}^{\alpha-1}\left|f(t)\right|d_{q}t\right]^{p}d_{q}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{k}\left[D_{q,a+}^{\alpha-m}(t-a)_{q}^{\alpha-k}\right](x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(\alpha-m+1\right)}+\left[D_{q,a+}^{\alpha-m}I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{k}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(m-k+1\right)}(x-a)_{q}^{m-k}+\left[I^{m}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right](x).

Finally, by taking the limit of (
3.3
) when
x\rightarrow a+
, we obtain the relations in
(
3.2
). The proof is complete.

∎

3.2.
Existence and uniqueness of a unique global solution to the Cauchy type
q
-fractional problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) in
L^{1}_{\alpha,q}[a,b]

.

In this subsection we give conditions for a unique global solution to the Cauchy type
problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) in the space
L^{1}_{\alpha,q}[a,b]
defined for
\alpha>0
by

\displaystyle L^{1}_{\alpha,q}[a,b]:=\left\{y\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]:D_{q,a+}^{\alpha}\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]\right\}.

The proof of the following existence and uniqueness theorem depends heavily on Theorem
3.1
and the
Banach fixed point Theorem (see e.g.
[
33
]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n-1<\alpha\leq n;n\in\mathbb{N}
and
G\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open set and
f(.,.):[a,b]\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
f(x,y(x))\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
for any
y\in G
and
satisfies a Lipschitz condition in the following form:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left|f(x,y_{1}(x))-f(x,y_{2}(x))\right|\leq C\left|y_{1}(x)-y_{2}(x)\right|,

where
C>0
. Then there exists a unique solution
y(x)\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
to the Cauchy type problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.1
, it is sufficient to study the existence of a unique solution

y(x)\in L[a,b]
to the
q
-integral equation (
3.3
).
Consequently, the equation (
3.3
) can be written in the operator form
y=Fy
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left(Fy\right)(x):=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right](x),

where
y_{0}:=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{k}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(\alpha-k+1\right)}(x-a)_{q}^{\alpha-k}
.

Let
[a,\xi]\subset[a,b]
be such that it holds that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\omega:=C\frac{\left(a-q\xi\right)_{q}^{\alpha}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(\alpha+1\right)}\leq 1.

From (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and Lemma
2.6
it follows that

\displaystyle\|Fy_{1}-Fy_{2}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]}\leq C\|I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}\left(y_{1}-y_{2}\right)\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]}\leq\omega\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]}.

Hence, according to the Banach fixed point theorem (see e.g.
[
33
]
), there exists a unique solution
y^{\prime}\in L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]
such that
Ty^{\prime}=y^{\prime}
.

Moreover, in view of this theorem, the solution
y^{\prime}
is obtained as a limit of a convergent sequence
\left\{\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x)\right\}
in the space
L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]
, i.e. that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow 0}\|F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}-y^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]}=0,

where
y_{0}
is any function in
L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]
.

If at least one
b_{k}\neq 0
in the initial condition
(
3.2
), then we can take
y^{\prime}_{0}=y_{0}
. By (
3.10
), the sequence
\left\{\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x)\right\}
is defined by

\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x):=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,F^{m-1}y^{\prime}_{0}(t))\right](x),

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\left(y_{m}(x):=F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x)
. Then

y_{m}(x)=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y_{m-1}(t))\right](x)
and

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow 0}\|y_{m}-y^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi]}=0.

This means that we actually applied the method of successive approximations to
find a unique solution
y^{\prime}(x)
to the integral equation (
3.3
). Thus,
there exists a unique solution
y(x)=y^{\prime}(x)\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
to the equation (
3.5
) and hence to the Cauchy type problem
(
3.1
)-(
3.2
).
This fact completes the proof of Theorem 3.2.

∎

In this section we define the

]
,
[

]
).
Moreover, the existence and uniqueness theorems for nonlinear fractional
q
-differential equations with Hilfer fractional

-derivatives types will be proved, which are the

Definition 4.1
.

We define the Hilfer fractional
q
-derivative
\mathcal{D}_{q,a+}^{\alpha,\beta}f

of order
0<\alpha<1
and type
0\leq\beta\leq 1
with respect to

by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}D_{q}\left(I_{q,a+}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}f\right)\right)(x).

Note that in case when

the generalized fractional
q
-derivative (
4.1

q

2.2

q

\left({}_{c}D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)

Definition 4.2
.

Let
n-1<\alpha\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. We define the generalized fractional
q
-derivative
D_{q,a+}^{\alpha,\beta}f
as follows:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(n-\alpha)}D^{n}_{q}\left(I_{q,a+}^{(1-\beta)(n-\alpha)}f\right)\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(n-\alpha)}D_{q,a+}^{\alpha+\beta n-\alpha\beta}f\right)(x).

For the proof of our main results in this section we also need two lemmas of independent interest.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
n=[\alpha]
and

. Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}D_{q}^{k}f(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\alpha+1\right)}\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k}+\int\limits_{a}^{k}\frac{\left(x-qt\right)_{q}^{n-\alpha-1}D_{q}^{k}f(t)}{\Gamma_{q}(n-\alpha)}d_{q}t

for all
x\in[a,b]
.

Proof.

Since
f\in AC^{n}_{q}[a,b]
we have that

(4.4)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}D_{q}^{k}f(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k+1\right)}\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k}+I^{n}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}f(x),

for all
x\in[a,b]
. By using Definition
2.2
, (
2.2
) and (
2.10
) we get that

(4.5)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}\left[\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(D^{\alpha}_{q}I^{n-\alpha}_{q,a+}\left(t-a\right)_{q}^{k}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{q}(k+1)}{\Gamma_{q}(k+n-\alpha+1)}\left(D^{n}_{q}\left(t-a\right)_{q}^{k+n-\alpha}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{q}(k+1)}{\Gamma_{q}(k-\alpha+1)}\left(t-a\right)_{q}^{k-\alpha}.

Applying the operator
D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}
to both sides of (
4.4
) and using (
4.5
) and Lemma
2.3
b) we have that

\displaystyle\left(D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}D_{q}^{k}f(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k+1\right)}\left(D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}\left(t-a\right)_{q}^{k}\right)(x)+D^{\alpha}_{q,a+}\left(I^{n}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}D_{q}^{k}f(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\alpha+1\right)}\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k-\alpha}+\left(I^{n-\alpha}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}f\right)(x),

∎

so (
4.3
) follows from (
2.9
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
y\in L_{q}^{1}(a,b)
,
n-1<\alpha\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,
\gamma=(n-\alpha)(1-\beta)
and

. Then the following equality holds:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(x)-y_{q,\alpha,\beta}(x),

where

y_{q,\alpha,\beta}(x):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(x-a)_{q}^{k-\gamma}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\gamma+1\right)}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a+}\left(D_{q}^{k}I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}y\right)(x).

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
a) and Definition
2.2
it follows that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}I^{\beta(n-\alpha)}_{q,a+}D^{\alpha+\beta n-\alpha\beta}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{\alpha+\beta(n-\alpha)}_{q,a+}D^{\alpha+\beta(n-\alpha)}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{n-\gamma}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}y\right)(x).

We define
\widetilde{y}:=I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}y\in AC_{q}^{n}[a,b]
. Then, according to Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
2.3
b) (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we find that

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(D_{q,a+}^{\gamma}\widetilde{y}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}\left(D_{q}^{k}\widetilde{y}\right)(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\gamma+1\right)}\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k-\gamma}+\left(I^{n-\gamma}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}\widetilde{y}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a}\left(D_{q}^{k}I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}\right)(x)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\gamma+1\right)}\left(x-a\right)_{q}^{k-\gamma}+\left(I^{n-\gamma}_{q,a+}D_{q}^{n}I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle y_{q,\alpha,\beta}(x)+\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\right)(x),

which completes the proof.

4.2.
The existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Cauchy type
q
-fractional problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in

.

In this subsection we give conditions for a unique global solution to the Cauchy type

1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the space
L^{1}_{\alpha,\beta,q}[a,b]
defined for
\alpha>0
by

\displaystyle L^{1}_{\alpha,\beta,q}[a,b]:=\left\{y\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]:D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]\right\}.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
a>0
,
G\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open set and
f(.,.):[a,b]\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
f(x,y(x))\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
for any

satisfying the condition (

,

,

to the Cauchy type problem (

)-(
1.2
).

Proof.

We begin to prove the existence of a unique solution
y\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
. According to Theorem
4.5
, it is sufficient to prove the existence of a unique solution
y\in L_{q}^{1}[a,b]
to the nonlinear Volterra integral equation (
4.8

4.8

y=Fy
such that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left(Fy\right)(x):=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right](x),

where
y_{0}:=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{k}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\gamma+1\right)}(x-a)_{q}^{k-\gamma}

Let
[a,\xi_{1}]\subset[a,b]

(4.12)

\displaystyle\omega:=C\frac{\left(a-q\xi_{1}\right)_{q}^{\alpha}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(\alpha+1\right)}\leq 1.

First we prove the following: If

L^{1}_{q}[a,\xi]

(Ty)(x)\in L^{1}_{q}[a,\xi_{1}]

From (

\displaystyle\|Fy_{1}-Fy_{2}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]}\leq C\|I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}\left(y_{1}-y_{2}\right)\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]}\leq\omega\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]},

and the proof of our first claim is done. Since

is Banach space we are according to the Banach fixed point theorem (see e.g.
[
33
]
), there exists a unique solution
y^{\prime}\in L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]
such that
Ty^{\prime}=y^{\prime}
.

Moreover, in view of this theorem, the solution
y^{\prime}
is obtained as a limit of a convergent sequence
\left\{\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x)\right\}

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow 0}\|F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}-y^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]}=0,

in the space
L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]
, where
y_{0}
is any function in
L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]
.

If at least one
b_{k}\neq 0
in the initial condition
(
1.2
), then we can take

. By (

), the sequence
\left\{\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x)\right\}

\left(F^{m}y^{\prime}_{0}\right)(x):=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,F^{m-1}y^{\prime}_{0}(t))\right](x),

for

. Let

. Then

y_{m}(x)=y_{0}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y_{m-1}(t))\right](x)

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow 0}\|y_{m}-y^{\prime}\|_{L_{q}^{1}[a,\xi_{1}]}=0.

y^{\prime}(x)
to the integral
equation (
4.8
) on
[a,\xi_{1}]
. Next we consider the interval
[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]
, where
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}+h_{1}
,
h_{1}>0
is such that
\xi_{2}<\infty
. Rewrite the equation (

(4.13)

\displaystyle y(x)=y_{0}(x)+\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right)(\xi_{1})+\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,\xi_{1}+}f(t,y(t))\right)(x).

\displaystyle\left(Fy\right)(x):=y_{10}+\left[I^{\alpha}_{q,\xi_{1}+}f(t,y(t))\right](x),

)-(

belongs to the space

y\in L^{1}_{q}[a,b]
is a limit of the sequence
\left\{y_{m}\right\}\in L^{1}_{q}[a,b]

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{m}-y\|_{L^{1}_{q}[a,b]}=0,

with the choice of certain

. Since

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y_{m}-D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\|_{L^{1}_{q}[a,b]}=\|f\left(x,y_{m}\right)-f\left(x,y\right)\|_{L^{1}_{q}[a,b]}\leq A\|y_{m}-y\|_{L^{1}_{q}[a,b]},

by (
4.15
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y_{m}-D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}y\|_{L^{1}_{q}[a,b]}=0,

and hence
D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,a+}\in L^{1}_{q}[a,b]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
G
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}
and

f(.,.):(a,b]\times G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
f(x,y(x))\in L_{q}^{1}(a,b)
for any

. If

0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,
\gamma=(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)
,

, then
y(t)
satisfies a.e. the relations (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) if and only if,
y(x)
satisfies a.e. the integral equation

(4.16)

\displaystyle y(x):=b\frac{(x-a)_{q}^{k-\gamma}}{\Gamma_{q}\left(k-\gamma+1\right)}+\left(I^{\alpha}_{q,a+}f(t,y(t))\right)(x).

a>0

Example 5.1
.

Let
\lambda,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
-2\alpha-\gamma+1>0
. Then we consider

f(x,y(x)):=\lambda\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}y^{2}(x),

and assume that
n-1<\alpha\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,
\gamma=(n-\alpha)(1-\beta)
and
I^{\gamma}_{q,a+}y\in AC_{q}^{n}[a,b]
.

Moreover, we choose
G
so that

(x,y)\in[qa,b]\times G:=\left\{qa\leq x\leq b;\left|y(x)\right|<M<\infty\right\}.

We note that
f(x,y(x))
satisfies (
3.8
) since, for
-2\alpha-\gamma+1>0
,

x\in[qa,b]
and
y_{1};y_{2}\in G
,

\displaystyle\left|f(x,y_{1}(x))-f(x,y_{2}(x))\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\left|\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}y^{2}_{1}(x)\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}y^{2}_{2}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{(b-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(a-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}\left|y_{1}(x)+y_{2}(x)\right|\left|y_{1}(x)-y_{2}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{(b-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(a-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}2M\left|y_{1}(x)-y_{2}(x)\right|,

which proves that
f
satisfies the Lipschitz condition (
3.8
) in Theorem
4.6
.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{qa}^{b}\left|f(x,y(x))\right|d_{q}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{qa}^{b}\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}\left|y(x)\right|^{2}d_{q}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{(b-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(a-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}M^{2},

which means that
f(x,y(x))\in L_{q}^{1}[qa,b]
. We can conclude that, according to Theorem
4.6
, the equation

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,qa+}y\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}y^{2}(x),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a+}\left(I^{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}_{q,a+}y\right)(x)=0,

has a unique solution. Note also that in this case, according to Lemma
2.3
the condition (
1.2
) with
b_{k}=0
,
k=0,1,..n-1
coincides with (
5.2
).

Moreover, in this special case
2\alpha+\gamma<1
we claim that this unique solution is given by

(5.3)

\displaystyle y(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-2\alpha-\gamma\right)}\left(x-qa\right)^{-\alpha-\gamma}_{q}.

In fact, by applying the operator
D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,qa+}
and using in order (
4.2
), (
2.10
), (
2.2
), (
2.1
) and (
2.10
), we get that

\displaystyle D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,qa+}\left[\left(t-qa\right)^{-\alpha-\gamma}_{q}\right](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}D_{q}I_{q,a+}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}(t-qa)_{q}^{-\alpha-\gamma}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}\left(D_{q}\right)(t-qa)_{q}^{1-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma}\right)(x)}{\frac{\Gamma_{q}{(2-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}{\Gamma_{q}{(1-\alpha-\gamma)}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}(t-qa)_{q}^{-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma}\right)(x)}{\frac{\Gamma_{q}{(2-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}{\left[1-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma\right]_{q}\Gamma_{q}{(1-\alpha-\gamma)}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(I_{q,a+}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}(t-qa)_{q}^{-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma}\right)(x)}{\frac{\Gamma_{q}{(1-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}{\Gamma_{q}{(1-\alpha-\gamma)}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\Gamma_{q}{(1-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}{\Gamma_{q}{(1+\beta-\alpha\beta-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}(x-qa)_{q}^{\beta-\alpha\beta-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma}}{\frac{\Gamma_{q}{(1-\alpha-\gamma)}}{\Gamma_{q}{(1-2\alpha-\beta+\alpha\beta-\gamma)}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-2\alpha-\gamma\right)}\left(x-qa\right)^{-2\alpha-\gamma}_{q},

for
2\alpha+\gamma<1
. Therefore,

(5.4)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha,\beta}_{q,qa+}\left[y(x)\right]=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-2\alpha-\gamma\right)}\right]^{2}\left(x-qa\right)^{-2\alpha-\gamma}_{q},

Hence, by using the well-known formulas

(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}(x-qa)_{q}^{-\alpha-\gamma}=1\;\;\text{and}\;\;(x-qa)_{q}^{-\alpha-\gamma}=(x-qa)_{q}^{-2\alpha-\gamma}(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma}a)_{q}^{\alpha},

in q-analysis (see [
[
23
]
, formula (3.7)]) we have that

(5.5)

\displaystyle f(x,y(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}y^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-2\alpha-\gamma\right)}\right]^{2}\times\frac{(x-q^{-\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha+\gamma}}{(x-q^{-2\alpha-\gamma+1}a)_{q}^{\alpha}}\left[(x-qa)_{q}^{-\alpha-\gamma}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)}{\Gamma_{q}\left(1-2\alpha-\gamma\right)}\right]^{2}(x-qa)_{q}^{-2\alpha-\gamma}=D_{q,qa+}^{\alpha,\beta}\left[y(x)\right],

i.e.

defined by (

) satisfies (
5.1
).

Now, by combining (

) and using that (

) and

, we see that

defined by (

5.1
)-(
5.2
). The proof of our claim is complete.

Abstract
We use the ultrafilter-convergence axiomatics for topological spaces to motivate in detail a gentle categorical introduction, first to Barr’s 𝐒𝐞𝐭 {\bf Set} -based relational T T -algebras, and then to Burroni’s T T -preorders internal to a category 𝒞 {\cal C} , here called T T -spaces in 𝒞 {\cal C} , for a monad T T on 𝒞 {\cal C} that substitutes the ultrafilter monad on 𝐒𝐞𝐭 {\bf Set} . Within these settings one finds not only the notions of compactness and Hausdorff separation, originally due to Manes, but also that of complete regularity. Based on a somewhat hidden result by Burroni, the main theorem of this paper establishes an external fibrational characterization of the category of completely regular T T -spaces with its reflexive subcategory of compact Hausdorff T T -spaces, under modest assumptions on 𝒞 {\cal C} and T T .

Introduction

The axiomatization of convergence was a key motivator for the development of the notions of metric and topology, as pursued primarily by Frechét and Hausdorff in the early twentieth century. The 1967 thesis by Manes
[
23
]
(see also
[
24
]
) characterized compact Hausdorff spaces as the Eilenberg-Moore algebras with respect to the ultrafilter monad
{\cal U}
on the category
{\bf Set}
of sets. In this setting, the algebraic structure of a compact Hausdorff space
X
is given by a single (generalized and infinitary) operation
{\cal U}X\to X
on its underlying set which assigns to every ultrafilter on
X
its limit, subject to just two equational axioms that are reminiscent of the unity and associativity axioms for monoids.

Replacing the mapping
{\cal U}X\to X
by a mere relation between ultrafilters and points, Barr
[
4
]
showed that, with the appropriate relational relaxation of the two equational axioms, one obtains an axiomatization of all topological spaces, with the ultrafilter convergence axioms now generalizing the reflexivity and transitivity conditions of preorders. He thereby reinforced a sentiment that is already apparent in Hausdorff’s book
[
13
]
, namely that topological spaces are generalized preordered sets which, in today’s parlance, appear as Alexandroff spaces.

Burroni
[
7
]
took Barr’s relational
T
-algebras for a monad
T
from the category
{\bf Set}
to an arbitrary base category
{\cal C}
with pullbacks, without any a-priori restrictions on the monad
T
. His
T
-
categories
in
{\cal C}
cover simultaneously categories internal to
{\cal C}
(when
T
is the identity monad) and
T
-preorders internal to
{\cal C}
, which give an equivalent description of Barr’s relational
T
-algebras in the case
{\cal C}={\bf Set}
. We call these
T
-preorders
T
-
spaces
in this paper, in order to emphasize their topological relevance, which reaches far beyond the order-theoretic perspective.

Without any reference to neighbourhood systems, or to open or closed sets, in the largely expository Section 2 we strictly follow the Manes-Barr ultrafilter-convergence axioms to give the notions of compactness, Hausdorff separation and complete regularity, and establish the reflectivity of Hausdorff compactness and complete regularity in this setting. The point of this presentation becomes clear in Section 3 since, in the preceding section, we actually never use any details regarding the ultrafilter monad, other than the general monad conditions. This means that, without any loss in mathematical content,
{\cal U}
may be replaced by any
{\bf Set}
-monad
T
so that, in effect, topological spaces may be traded for Barr’s relational
T
-algebras. Other than the case
T={\cal U}
, we consider in some detail the case that
T
is the power-set monad, in order to augment (and, in one instance, rectify) its treatment in
[
23
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
28
]
.

Minimizing Burroni’s categorical apparatus, we describe in a topology-oriented notation the decisive step of replacing
{\bf Set}
by an abstract category
{\cal C}
in Section 4. In order to present the category
T
-
Spa
({\cal C})
of
T
-spaces in
{\cal C}
as a
topological
(
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
) and, hence, “
{\bf Top}
-like” category over
{\cal C}
, we assume
{\cal C}
to be complete and well-powered and to have a (regular epi, mono)-factorization system, and impose the preservation of regular epimorphisms by
T
as the only condition beyond the monad axioms. (This condition comes for free in the case
{\cal C}={\bf Set}
, Choice granted.) Requiring only modest knowledge of basic category theory, we establish the Tychonoff Theorem and the existence of the
\mathrm{\check{C}}
ech-Stone reflection in this setting. Its presence is helpful (but not indispensable) for the introduction of the notion of complete regularity at this level.

Without reference to the case
{\cal C}={\bf Set}
and
T={\cal U}
, and under a notation which suggests that he regarded just the identity monad as his role model, Burroni
[
7
]
characterized the category of completely regular
T
-spaces as a universal (Grothendieck-) fibrational extension of the monadic category of compact Hausdorff
T
-spaces over
{\cal C}
. Our Theorem
5.4
gives a detailed analysis, extension and proof of this result, highlighting in particular its 2-categorical nature.

We end this paper with some comparative remarks about some related works.

.

We call a

6
Some remarks on related work

For
{\cal C}={\bf Set}
, the structure of a
T
-space

as presented in Section 3 is given by a relation

C\subseteq TX\times X
; equivalently, by a map

. Monoidal topology
[
15
]
, as initiated in the papers
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
29
]
, extends Barr’s relational
T
-algebras and, hence, Burroni’s
T
-orders at the level of sets, in two ways. On one hand, one replaces the Boolean algebra
\mathsf{2}
by a quantale
\mathsf{V}
,
i.e.
, by a complete lattice
\mathsf{V}
that comes with a monoid structure whose multiplication preserves arbitrary joins in each variable. This permits in particular the inclusion of metrically enriched structures into the setting (as initiated by Lawvere
[
19
]
). Following Lowen’s
[
21
]
and other authors’ works, these have enjoyed much attention in recent years.

On the other hand, for the transitivity axiom (T) of
Definition
2.3
, the monoidal topology setting allows for considerable flexibility of how the relation
C
gets extended to a relation
\hat{C}\subseteq TTX\times TX
; instead of prescribing the so-called Barr-extension of the monad
T
, one considers a fairly general lax-functorial extension of
T
from mappings to
\mathsf{V}
-valued relations of sets.
Among many other applications, this additional flexibility led to the unexpected result
[
15
]
that the same two-axiom presentation of topological spaces in terms of ultrafilter convergence remains valid if one replaces ultrafilters by filters, under an appropriate lax extension of the filter monad.

Briefly then, the setting of monoidal topology extends the Barr-Burroni setting considerably, in the case
{\cal C}={\bf Set}
. However, it does not allow for an easy extension to other base categories. Put in more categorical terms, monoidal topology leans on enriched categorical methods (with the quantale
\mathsf{V}
being treated as a “thin” monoidal-closed category), while the general Burroni setting extends the theory of small categories and preorders internal to a category
{\cal C}
, using an arbitrary monad on
{\cal C}
.

A large portion of Ralph Kopperman’s work, pursued over several decades, concerns generalized metric structures and their applications in topology and computer science; see, for example,
[
17
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
6
]
. It is therefore closely related to the monoidal-topology setting, but there is a significant conceptual difference, as he normally considers the “value recipient”
\mathsf{V}
to vary with, and be part of, the structure of the space-like objects that are under investigation.

Nonlocal correlation in L-functions
Gordon Chavez

Abstract
. We identify a nonlocal correlation structure in L-functions. This structure involves very long and infinite-range correlations between values of logarithmic L-functions, where the correlation strongly depends upon the presence of a multiplicative relationship between the two points in question on the complex plane. This correlation sharply jumps if the two points share a multiplicative relationship and takes much lower values otherwise, demonstrating a previously undescribed type of long-range order in L-functions. We leverage this correlation structure to provide a novel set of identities that relate the distribution of Möbius function solutions, sums over the non-divisors of the integers, and the polylogarithms.

\displaystyle\chi(n)\begin{cases}=0;&\textnormal{gcd}(n,M)>1\\

In this paper we show that, along with their chaotic fluctuations, there is also significant long-range statistical order in many of these functions.

Below we describe very long and infinite-range correlations between values of logarithmic principal L-functions, where the correlation strongly depends upon the presence of a multiplicative relationship between the two points in question on the complex plane. In particular, we show that if

2.
Preliminaries

The prime zeta function
\mathcal{P}\left(s\right)
is a closely related function to
\zeta(s)
that has the expressions

(2.1)

\mathcal{P}\left(s\right)=\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{s}}

The expression (
2.2

(2.3)

\sum_{m|n}\mu(m)=0

for all

and the Möbius inversion-derived analytic continuation (
2.2
) is convergent everywhere in
\textnormal{Re}(s)>0
except at the points

, which arise from the simple pole
\zeta(1)
, and at the points

where
\zeta\left(ns\right)=0
.

We will generalize (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) for the consideration of L-functions

, where
\chi
is a given Dirichlet character modulo
M
. We may generalize (

) by defining the prime L-function with the series

(2.4)

\mathcal{P}_{\chi}\left(s\right)=\sum_{p}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}

where
\chi(p)=e^{-i\theta_{p}}
for primes

such that

and
\chi(p)=0
for primes
p
such that
p|M
. The case

gives results for the zeta function and is represented by (

)-(
2.2
). Below we will consider the case of principal characters

, which, by (

) have the properties

(2.5)

\chi_{0}(p)=\begin{cases}1\hskip 7.11317pt;\hskip 7.11317ptp\nmid M\\
0\hskip 7.11317pt;\hskip 7.11317ptp|M\end{cases}

and

(2.6)

\chi_{0}^{n}=\chi_{0}

for any

. We next note that one may take the logarithm of

) relating L-functions

\mathcal{P}_{\chi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log L\left(ns,\chi^{n}\right)

For principal characters

Before proceeding we lastly make a few notes about the polylogarithm (

Additionally, a function related to the polylogarithm will repeatedly appear that has the form

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(ks)}{k^{2}}.

For

\displaystyle E\left\{\log\left|L\left(\sigma+i\alpha t,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\log\left|L\left(\sigma^{\prime}+i\beta t,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right\}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(k\left(\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}\right)\right)}{k^{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta|\alpha\\
\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(l\left(\beta\sigma+\alpha\sigma^{\prime}\right)\right)}{l^{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta\nmid\alpha\end{cases}

where the lower-right result is achieved by simply noting
\beta|k
implies
k=l\beta
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

We next note that (
3.15
) may be written using the analytic continuation (
2.7
), which extends (
3.15
)’s domain of definition to
\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}>0
with
kj\left(\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}\right)\neq 1
for any
k,j\in\mathbb{N}
in the case of
\beta|\alpha
and
kj\left(\beta\sigma+\alpha\sigma^{\prime}\right)\neq 1
for any
k,j\in\mathbb{N}
in the case of
\beta\nmid\alpha
. This proves (
3.9
) and its constraints on
\sigma
. We then note that (
3.15
) may be written using the prime sum (
2.4
), giving

\displaystyle E\left\{\log\left|L\left(\sigma+i\alpha t,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\log\left|L\left(\sigma^{\prime}+i\beta t,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right\}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum_{p\nmid M}\frac{1}{p^{k\left(\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}\right)}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta|\alpha\\
\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l^{2}}\sum_{p\nmid M}\frac{1}{p^{l\left(\beta\sigma+\alpha\sigma^{\prime}\right)}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta\nmid\alpha\end{cases}

The domain of convergence for (
2.4
) implies that (
3.16
) is convergent for
\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}>1
in the case of
\beta|\alpha
and
\beta\sigma+\alpha\sigma^{\prime}>1
in the case of
\beta\nmid\alpha
. We then lastly note that, by (
2.10
), (
3.15
) is equivalent to (
3.10
).
∎

3.2.1.
Nonlocal diagonal correlation

We first apply Theorem
3.1
to describe
\log\left|L\left(s,\chi_{0}\right)\right|
’s nonlocal diagonal covariance function, which we denote by

)-(
3.10

\displaystyle R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)=E\left\{\log\left|L\left(\alpha\left(\sigma+it\right),\chi_{0}\right)\right|\log\left|L\left(\beta\left(\sigma+it\right),\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2k\alpha\sigma)}{k^{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta|\alpha,\hskip 11.9501pt\sigma>0,\hskip 2.84544pt\sigma\neq\frac{1}{2j\alpha},j\in\mathbb{N}\\
\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2k\alpha\beta\sigma)}{k^{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta\nmid\alpha,\hskip 7.11317pt\sigma>0,\hskip 2.84544pt\sigma\neq\frac{1}{2j\alpha\beta},j\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}\sum_{p\nmid M}\textnormal{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2\alpha\sigma}}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta|\alpha,\hskip 11.9501pt\sigma>\frac{1}{2\alpha}\\
\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta}\sum_{p\nmid M}\textnormal{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2\alpha\beta\sigma}}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta\nmid\alpha,\hskip 7.11317pt\sigma>\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta}\end{cases}

It is clear that (

) has much richer structure than
\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(s)
’s corresponding diagonal covariance function (
3.7
). From (

(3.18)

R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{\alpha}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\alpha,\sigma)\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta|\alpha\\

and

\rho_{1}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)=\frac{R_{1}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)}{\sqrt{R_{1}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\alpha,\alpha,\sigma)R_{1}^{\textnormal{diag}}(\beta,\beta,\sigma)}}

for

3.18

\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\frac{1}{2^{2\alpha\beta\sigma+1}}\hskip 2.84544pt;&\beta\nmid\alpha\end{cases}

4.

In this section we will leverage the above results to prove Theorem

and the log-L-function

4.1.
Gaps between prime-L-functions and log-L-functions

We have seen that while
\log\left|L\left(s,\chi_{0}\right)\right|
displays nonlocal diagonal correlation,
\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(s)
does not. We will apply this fact to study the statistical behavior of the difference between
\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(s)
and
\log\left|L\left(s,\chi_{0}\right)\right|
, i.e.,

(4.1)

\varepsilon_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)=\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)-\log\left|L\left(\sigma+it,\chi_{0}\right)\right|.

The difference (
4.1
) contains the contributions to
\log\left|L\left(\sigma+it,\chi_{0}\right)\right|
that give rise to the nonlocal correlations described in Section
3
. Along with the definition (
4.1
), we will additionally consider the representation

(4.2)

\varepsilon_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log\left|L\left(n\left(\sigma+it\right),\chi_{0}\right)\right|,

which immediately follows from the analytic continuation (
2.7
) and (
2.6
). We will apply (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) to produce two different representations for the mean square of
\varepsilon_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)
using Theorem
3.1
’s covariance function result.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
t
uniformly distributed in
[a,b]
with
b-a\rightarrow\infty
and all
\sigma>1/4
,

(4.3)

E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)

and

\displaystyle E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{2}}\left(-\left(\mu(n)+2\right)R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\nmid n\\
m<n\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(nm,nm,\sigma)\right)

Proof.

We first note from (
3.7
) that

(4.5)

E\left\{\left(\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)\right)^{2}\right\}=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2\sigma)}{2}.

We then square (
4.1
) and apply the expectation, noting (
4.5
) as well as (
3.9
) with
n=m=1
to write

\displaystyle E\left\{\varepsilon_{\chi_{0}}^{2}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2\sigma)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2k\sigma)}{k^{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle-2E\left\{\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)\log\left|L\left(\sigma+it,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right\}.

We consider
\sigma>1
so we may apply the Euler product and Taylor expansion for
\log\left|L\left(s,\chi_{0}\right)\right|
with (
2.6
) to write

(4.7)

\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)\log\left|L\left(\sigma+it,\chi_{0}\right)\right|=\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(k\left(\sigma+it\right)\right)}{k}.

We then note that (
4.7
) has the absolute upper bound

(4.8)

\mathcal{P}(\sigma)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}(k\sigma)}{k}<\infty.

The finite bound (
4.8
) shows that (
4.7
) satisfies the condition (
3.2
). We may therefore apply the expectation to (
4.7
) term-by-term. We then note from (
3.7
) that the only term with nonvanishing expected value is the term corresponding to
k=1
, which gives

(4.9)

E\left\{\textnormal{Re}\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma+it\right)\log\left|L\left(\sigma+it,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right\}=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2\sigma)}{2}.

Applying (
4.9
) in (
4.6
) and cancelling terms then gives

(4.10)

E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2k\sigma)}{k^{2}}.

We then use Möbius inversion to show that, for any
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.11)

\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2\sigma)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{v}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2kn\sigma)}{k^{v}}.

We expand the right-hand side of (
4.11

(4.12)

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2k\sigma)}{k^{v}}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{v}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{0}}(2kn\sigma)}{k^{v}}.

Dividing (
4.12
) by 2, setting

, comparing to (
4.10

3.17

4.3
).

We next consider (
4.2
) and take the square, giving the series expression

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{n^{2}}\left(\log\left|L\left(n(\sigma+it),\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)^{2}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\sum_{2\leq m<n}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\left(\log\left|L\left(n(\sigma+it),\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)\left(\log\left|L\left(m(\sigma+it),\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right).

We note from the Euler product and Taylor expansion that, for any

\displaystyle\log\left|L\left(n\left(\sigma+it\right),\chi_{0}\right)\right|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{p\nmid M}\frac{\cos\left(nt\log p\right)}{p^{kn\sigma}}

and, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

(4.15)

\log\left|L\left(n\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\sim\frac{1}{p^{n\sigma}},

where
p
is the minimum prime such that
p\nmid M
. Hence the sum of the absolute values of the summands of (
4.13
)’s first series has the upper bound

(4.16)

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\log\left|L\left(n\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)^{2}<\infty,

while the sum of the absolute values of the summands of (
4.13
)’s second series has the upper bound

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{2\leq m<n}\frac{1}{m}\left(\log\left|L\left(n\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)\left(\log\left|L\left(m\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle<2\left(\log\left|L\left(\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\log\left|L\left(n\sigma,\chi_{0}\right)\right|<\infty.

The finite bounds (
4.16
)-(
4.17

4.13
) satisfies the condition (
3.2
). We may therefore apply the expectation to (
4.13
) term-by term. We do so and write

(4.18)

\displaystyle E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{n^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{2\leq m<n}\frac{\mu(n)\mu(m)}{nm}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,m,\sigma).

We expand the second summation to give

\displaystyle E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{n^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)

(4.19)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m|n\\
2\leq m<n\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,m,\sigma)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\nmid n\\
m<n\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,m,\sigma)\right).

We then apply (
3.18
) in (
4.19
) and simplify to give the result

\displaystyle E\left\{\varepsilon^{2}_{\chi_{0}}\left(\sigma,t\right)\right\}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{n^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)

(4.20)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{2}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m|n\\
2\leq m<n\end{subarray}}\mu(m)R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(n,n,\sigma)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\nmid n\\
m<n\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}R_{\chi_{0}}^{\textnormal{diag}}(nm,nm,\sigma)\right)

We next gather the series in (

Appendix A
On statistical independence

The characteristic function of a random variable
X
is defined

(A.1)

\varphi_{X}(\lambda)=E\left\{e^{i\lambda X}\right\}.

We suppose
t
is uniformly distributed in
[a,b]
with
b-a\rightarrow\infty
and consider the following sum over some set of primes
p
:

(A.2)

X(t)=\sum_{p}a_{p}e^{-i\left(\alpha_{p}t\log p+\theta_{p}\right)},

where
a_{p},\theta_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}
. We substitute (
A.2
)’s real part into (
A.1
) to write

(A.3)

\varphi_{X(t)}(\lambda)=E\left\{\prod_{p}\exp\left(i\lambda a_{p}\cos\left(\alpha_{p}t\log p+\theta_{p}\right)\right)\right\}.

We then expand (
A.3
) using the Bessel function identity

(A.4)

e^{ix\cos y}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}i^{n}J_{n}\left(x\right)e^{iny}

where
J_{n}(.)
is the
n
th-order Bessel function of the first kind. This gives

(A.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi_{X(t)}\left(\lambda\right)=E\left\{\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...}\left(i^{n_{1}+n_{2}+...}J_{n_{1}}\left(\lambda a_{p_{1}}\right)J_{n_{2}}\left(\lambda a_{p_{2}}\right)...\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\times e^{i\left(n_{1}\theta_{p_{1}}+n_{2}\theta_{p_{2}}+...\right)}e^{it\left(n_{1}\alpha_{p_{1}}\log p_{1}+n_{2}\alpha_{p_{2}}\log p_{2}+...\right)}\right)\right\}\end{split}

The exponential terms on (
A.5
)’s far right-hand side are unit circle rotations with
t
. Therefore taking the expected value will cause all terms to vanish except those for which

(A.6)

n_{1}\alpha_{p_{1}}\log p_{1}+n_{2}\alpha_{p_{2}}\log p_{2}+n_{3}\alpha_{p_{3}}\log p_{3}+...=0.

However, by unique-prime-factorization, the
\log p
’s are linearly independent over the rational numbers. Therefore the only solution to (
A.6
) is given by

(A.7)

n_{1}=n_{2}=n_{3}=...=0.

This simplifies (
A.5
) to give

(A.8)

\varphi_{X(t)}\left(\lambda\right)=\prod_{p}J_{0}\left(\lambda a_{p}\right).

We next note from (
A.1
) and (
A.11
) that the characteristic function for a single summand in (
A.2
)’s real part,
a_{p}\cos(\alpha_{p}t\log p+\theta_{p})
, is given by

(A.9)

\varphi_{p}\left(\lambda\right)=J_{0}\left(\lambda a_{p}\right).

Therefore, by (
A.8
) and (
A.9
),

(A.10)

\varphi_{X(t)}\left(\lambda\right)=\prod_{p}\varphi_{p}\left(\lambda\right).

This shows that (
A.2
)’s summands are independent. Essentially equivalent reasoning using the identity

(A.11)

e^{ix\sin\phi}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{n}\left(x\right)e^{in\phi}

gives equivalent results for (
A.2
)’s imaginary part. This completes the proof sketch. For further details and complete proof, apply results in
[
6
]
(pg. 36, Cor. 5.2 pg. 38, Eqn. 6.4 pg. 41, Cor. 6.7 pg. 43, Cor. 6.8 pg. 44).

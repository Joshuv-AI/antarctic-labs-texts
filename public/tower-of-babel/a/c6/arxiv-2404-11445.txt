Multi-modalities and non-commutativity/associativity in functorial linear logic: a case study
Carlos Olarte, Elaine Pimentel

Abstract




1
Introduction

Similar to modal connectives, the exponential
\mathop{!}
in intuitionistic linear logic (
\mathsf{ILL}
) is not
canonical

[
3
]
, in the sense that if
i\not=j
then

\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\not\equiv\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}F
.
Intuitively, this means that we can mark the exponential with
labels
taken from a set
I
organized in a pre-order
\preceq
(
i.e.
, reflexive and transitive), obtaining (possibly infinitely-many) exponentials (
\mathop{!}^{i}

for
i\in I
).
Also as in multi-modal systems, the pre-order determines the provability relation:
for a general formula
F
,
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{b}$}F

implies

\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{a}$}F
iff
a\preceq b
.

The algebraic structure of subexponentials, combined with their intrinsic structural property allow for the proposal of rich linear logic based frameworks. This opened a venue for proposing different multi-modal substructural logical systems, that encountered a number of different applications.

There are, however, two main differences between multi-modalities in normal modal logics and subexponentials in linear logic.

i. substructural behaviour.
Subexponentials carry the possibility of
having different structural behaviors;

ii. nature of modalities.

Normal modal logics start from the weakest version, assuming only axiom
\mathsf{K}
,
then extensions are considered, by adding other axioms. Exponentials in linear
logic“take for granted” the behaviors expressed by axioms

\mathsf{T}
and
\mathsf{4}
.

Regarding (i), originally
[
11
]
, subexponentials could assume only weakening and contraction axioms, while in
[
5
,
6
]
, non-commutative systems allowing commutative subexponentials were presented

\mathsf{C}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\otimes\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\qquad\mathsf{W}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}1\qquad\mathsf{E}:\;\;(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F)\otimes G\equiv G\otimes(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F)

Finally, in
[
2
]
associativity was added to the scene, where exponentials could
assume or not the axioms

\mathsf{A1}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\otimes(G\otimes H)\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\otimes G)\otimes H\qquad\mathsf{A2}:\;\;(G\otimes H)\otimes\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}G\otimes(H\otimes\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F)

Concerning (ii), Guerrini et al.
[
4
]
unified the modal and

\mathsf{LL}
approaches, with the exponentials assuming only the linear
version of
\mathsf{K}
, with the possibility of adding modal extensions to it. This discussion was brought to multi-modal case
in
[
8
]
, where
subexponentials consider not only the structural axioms
for contraction and weakening, but also the subexponential version of axioms

\{\mathsf{K},\mathsf{4},\mathsf{D},\mathsf{T}\}
:

\mathsf{K}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}(F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}G)\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}G\qquad\mathsf{D}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}\hbox{$\mathord{?}^{i}$}F\qquad\mathsf{T}:\;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}F\qquad\mathsf{4}\>;\;\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F

In this work, we intend to join these two studies. This means that
\mathord{?}^{i}
can behave classically or not, model associative and commutative systems or not,
but also with exponential behaviors different from those in
\mathsf{LL}
. Hence, by
assigning different modal axioms one obtains, in a modular way, a class of
different substructural modal logics.

2
Non-commutative, non-associative linear logic (
\mathsf{acLL}_{\Sigma}
)

In the following, we will briefly describe the system
\mathsf{acLL}_{\Sigma}
. For more details, please refer to
[
2
]
.

Connectives.

First of all, non-commutativity implies that the left residual
\mathrel{\mbox{$\circ\!-$}}
should be added to the language of (propositional intuitionistic) linear logic with subexponentials (
\mathsf{SELL}
), which contains the connectives
\otimes,\mathbin{\&},\oplus,1,0,\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ},\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{l}$},\hbox{$\mathord{?}^{l}$}
. The subexponentials are organized as follows.

Definition 1
(Signature)

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathsf{C},\mathsf{W},\mathsf{A1},\mathsf{A2},\mathsf{E}\}
. A

subexponential signature
is given
by a triple
\Sigma=(I,\preccurlyeq,f)
, where
I
is a set of indices,
(I,\preccurlyeq)
is a pre-order, and
f
is a mapping from
I
to

2^{\mathcal{A}}
. Finally, every signature
\Sigma
is assumed to be upwardly closed w.r.t.
\preceq
, that is, if
i\preceq j
then
f(i)\subseteq f(j)
for all
i,j\in I
.

Contexts and rules.

Losing commutativity implies that contexts should be handled by lists instead of multisets, and the lack of associativity forces tree-shaped contexts.
Rules then act deeply in these structures. For example, the promotion and exchange rules are

\Gamma\Rightarrow\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\Gamma^{{\uparrow\!\!({i})}}\Rightarrow F\qquad\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{e}$}\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{(\Delta_{2},\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{e}$}\Delta_{1})\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G\qquad\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{(\Delta_{1},\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{e}$}\Delta_{2})\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{e}$}\Delta_{2},\Delta_{1})\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G

where
{\uparrow\!\!({i})}
denotes the
upset
of the index
i
,
i.e.
, the set
\{j\in I:i\preccurlyeq j\}
.

Example 1

Let
\Gamma=(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}A,(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}B,\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{k}$}C))
be represented below left,
i\preceq j
but
i\not\preceq k
, and
\mathsf{W}\in f(k)
. Then
\Gamma^{{\uparrow\!\!({i})}}=(\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}A,\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}B)
is depicted below right
,
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}A
,
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}B
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{k}$}C
,
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}A
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}B

Observe that, if
\mathsf{W}\notin f(k)
, then
\Gamma^{{\uparrow\!\!({i})}}
cannot be built. In this case, any derivation of
\Gamma\Rightarrow\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}(A\otimes B)
cannot start with an application of the promotion rule
\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}R
(similarly to how promotion in
\mathsf{ILL}
cannot be applied in the presence of non-classical contexts). In this case, if
A,B
are atomic, this sequent would not be provable.

3
Linear nested systems

In this section we present a brief introduction to
linear nested systems. For further details, refer to (
[
7
,
9
]
).

One of the main problems of using sequent systems as a framework is that sequents are often not adequate for expressing modal behaviors.
In order to propose a better formulation,
we need a tighter control of formulas in the context, something that sequents do not provide. Hence the need for extending the notion of sequent systems.

Definition 2
(LNS)

The set
\mathsf{LNS}
of
linear nested sequents
is given recursively by:

(i) if
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is a sequent then
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta\in\mathsf{LNS}

(ii) if
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is a sequent and
\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{LNS}

then
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{LNS}
.

We call each sequent
in a linear nested sequent a
component
and slightly abuse
notation, abbreviating “linear nested sequent” to
\mathsf{LNS}
.
We shall denote by
\mathsf{LNS}_{\mathcal{L}}
a linear nested sequent system
for a logic
\mathcal{L}
.

In words, a linear nested sequent is simply a finite list of sequents that matches exactly the
history
of a backward proof search in an ordinary sequent
calculus (
[
7
]
).

A further advantage of this framework is that it is often possible to restrict the list of sequents in a
\mathsf{LNS}
to the last 2 components, that we call
active
.

Definition 3
(End-active)

An application of a linear nested sequent rule is
end-active

if the rightmost components of the premises are active and the only
active components (in premise and conclusion) are the two rightmost
ones. The
end-active variant
of a
\mathsf{LNS}
calculus is the
calculus with the rules restricted to end-active applications.

3.1
\mathsf{LNS}
for multi-modal
\mathsf{LL}

In the quest for locality,
[
4
]

proposed 2-sequents systems for
\mathsf{LL}
variants, with separate rules for the exponentials. In
[
8
]
this work was revisited, establishing a lighter notation and extending the discussion to multi-modalities.

\mathsf{LNS}_{\mathsf{LL}}
(
[
8
]
) is an end-active, linear nested system for linear logic.
In this system, the promotion rule is split into the following local rules:

{\cal E}\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\Gamma\Rightarrow!F\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\Rightarrow F\qquad\Gamma,\mathop{!}F\Rightarrow H\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\Delta\Rightarrow G\Gamma\Rightarrow H\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\Delta,\mathop{!}F\Rightarrow G

Observe that no checking must be done in the context in order to apply the
\mathop{!}L
rule: The only checking is in the
\mathop{!}R
rule, where
{\cal E}
should be the empty sequent or an empty list of components.

More precisely, applying the
\mathop{!}
rule enables the creation of the
future history
, in which the banged formula should be proved.
The
intended interpretation of a
\mathsf{LNS}
in
\mathsf{LL}
is

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\iota(\Gamma\Rightarrow F)&:=&\bigotimes\Gamma\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}F\\
\iota(\Gamma\Rightarrow F\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/\!}\mathcal{H})&:=&\bigotimes(\Gamma\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}F)\bindnasrepma\;\mathop{!}\iota(\mathcal{H})\end{array}

The notion of signatures is then enhanced. We say that
\Sigma=(I,\preccurlyeq,f)
is a
functorial signature

if it is defined over
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathsf{C},\mathsf{W},\mathsf{A1},\mathsf{A2},\mathsf{E},\mathsf{K},\mathsf{4},\mathsf{T},\mathsf{D}\}
and
\mathsf{K}\in f(i),\;\forall i\in I
. If, moreover,
\mathsf{A1},\mathsf{A2}\in f(i),\;\forall i\in I
, then the functorial signature is called
associative
.

The associative case.

In the presence of associativity, contexts are lists of formulas, and the tree structure is not needed.
Given an associative signature
\Sigma
,
we introduce nesting operators
\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/^{i}\!}
and their
unfinished versions
\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}
for every
i\in I
, and change the interpretation so that they are interpreted by
the corresponding modality:

\displaystyle\iota(\Gamma\Rightarrow F)

\displaystyle:=\bigotimes\Gamma\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}F

\displaystyle\iota(\Gamma\Rightarrow F\mathbin{\!/\mkern-5.0mu/^{i}\!}\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle:=\iota(\Gamma\Rightarrow F\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\mathcal{H}):=(\bigotimes\Gamma\mathbin{-\hskip-1.99168pt\circ}F)\;\bindnasrepma\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}\,\iota(\mathcal{H})

The operators
\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}

indicate that the standard sequent rule for the modality indexed by
i

has been partially processed as shown below.

The exponential rules for the
end-active linear nested system
\mathsf{LNS}_{(I,\preccurlyeq,F)}
is given by the following rules

\Gamma,\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}F\Rightarrow G\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\Delta\Rightarrow H\Gamma\Rightarrow G\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}F,\Delta\Rightarrow H\qquad\Gamma\Rightarrow\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\Gamma\Rightarrow\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\Rightarrow F\qquad\mathcal{E}\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\Gamma\Rightarrow F\Gamma\Rightarrow F

\Gamma,\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}F\Rightarrow G\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\Delta\Rightarrow H\Gamma\Rightarrow G\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{j}$}F,\Delta\Rightarrow H\quad\Gamma,\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\Rightarrow G\Gamma\Rightarrow G\mathbin{\!\backslash\mkern-5.0mu\backslash^{i}\!}F\Rightarrow

\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{\hbox{$\mathop{!}^{i}$}F\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G\Gamma\mathopen{}\left\{F\right\}\mathclose{}\Rightarrow G

Note that the order of formulas in the context is respected in the application of the rules.

3.2
The non-associative case

Since
\mathsf{LNS}
do not preserve tree structures, the system just proposed is not adequate for the non-associative setting. In this case, we propose a skeleton that keeps track of the shape of the structure as follows.

Given a structure
\Gamma
, we represent by
\Gamma^{\circ}
its underlying tree structure, where the leaves of
\Gamma
are substituted by empty contexts
\{\;\}
(holes).
\Gamma^{\circ}\{\}
will then represent
\Gamma^{\circ}
with a specific position highlighted, and the usual context substitution can be applied. That is, given an underlying structure
\Gamma^{\circ}
, we write
\Gamma^{\circ}\mathopen{}\left\{F\right\}\mathclose{}
for the context where some specific hole
\Gamma^{\circ}\mathopen{}\left\{\mkern 3.0mu\right\}\mathclose{}
has been replaced by
F
. If
\Gamma^{\circ}=\varnothing
the hole is removed.

\mathsf{LNS}

4
Conclusion

In this ongoing work, we propose a modular system for dealing with multi-modal
\mathsf{LL}
systems, where substructural and modal axioms are taken into account.

The motivation for that is twofold: (1) to expand the role of
\mathsf{LL}
as a framework for reasoning about systems, as in
[
10
,
13
,
15
]
and, mainly, (2) extending the computational interpretation of subexponentials
[
12
,
14
]
also to the non-associative/commutative case. We intend to pursue these directions in the near future.

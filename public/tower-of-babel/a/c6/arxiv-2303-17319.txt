1.
Introduction

The study of reproducing kernels
in several complex variables plays an important role in
several fields of mathematics.
The Szegő kernel is is the reproducing kernel
of the space of square integrable CR functions on a CR manifold.
Our starting point is a fundamental result by
Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand
[
5
, (1.5) Théorème]
,
showing that the Szegő kernel of a compact strictly pseudoconvex
CR manifold of dimension greater or equal to five
is a complex Fourier integral operator (see (
1.7
)).
This result has many applications in complex geometry and
geometric quantization.
It can be used to produce many smooth
CR functions and is related to a global CR embedding
theorem by Boutet de Monvel
[
3
]
,
cf. also
[
8
, §12]
, stating that
for a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR
manifold
X
of dimension greater or equal to five
there are global smooth CR functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}

such that the map

(1.1)

\Phi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N},\quad x\in X\rightarrow(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{N}(x))\in\mathbb{C}^{N},

is an embedding. Boutet de Monvel’s embedding theorem
is of very general nature.
For the study of specific CR geometry problems,
such as the stability of CR structures or the existence of spherical CR embeddings,
we need to refine the embedding theorem,
and find adapted embeddings (
1.1
).
In Kähler geometry
similar problems have been well studied using semi-classical
Bergman kernel asymptotic
expansions
[
6
,
9
,
30
,
41
,
47
]

and the Kodaira embedding theorem. Therefore, we believe it is
important to consider a semiclassical version of the
Boutet de Monvel and Sjöstrand’s result and a Kodaira-type embedding
theorem for strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds.
In this paper, we achieve these semi-classical theorems
by studying the weighted spectral projections
of first-order elliptic self-adjoint Toeplitz operators involving packages of eigenvalues
indexed by a semi-classical parameter
k
drifting to the right on
the real axis when
k\to+\infty
, cf. Theorem

1.1
.
In the following we will formulate the most important results.

Let
(X,T^{1,0}X)
be an orientable
compact strictly pseudoconvex Cauchy–Riemann (CR) manifold
of hypersurface type and of dimension
2n+1
,
n\geq 1
,
where
T^{1,0}X
denotes the CR structure of
X
.
Let
J\in\End(HX)
be the complex structure map on the
associated Levi distribution

HX={\rm Re\,}(T^{1,0}X)\subset TX
.
Let
\xi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{*}X)
be a contact form on
X

such that the Levi form

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}d\xi(\cdot,J\cdot)

is positive definite and
dV_{\xi}:=\frac{2^{-n}}{n!}\xi\wedge\left(d\xi\right)^{n}

be the volume form induced by
\xi
. Let
\xi_{x}:=\xi(x)
,
x\in X
.
Let
dV
be any volume form on
X
and consider

L^{2}(X)=L^{2}(X,dV)
the associated space of
of square integrable functions.

Let
\overline{\partial}_{b}
be the tangential Cauchy–Riemann
operator on
X
. We assume that the Kohn Laplacian

\Box_{b}^{(0)}=\overline{\partial}^{*}_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}
has closed range in

L^{2}
. We recall that in this case, according to Boutet de Monvel’s proof

[
2
,
3
]
and Kohn’s argument
[
34
]
,
for all compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds,
the closed range condition is equivalent to the CR embeddability of

X
into some
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. We also recall that the closed range condition holds
for any compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold when

n\geq 2
, cf.
[
3
]
, or when
n=1
and
X

admits a transversal CR
\mathbb{R}
-action, cf.
[
38
,
42
]
.

We denote the space of square integrable CR functions by

(1.2)

H^{0}_{b}(X):=\{u\in L^{2}(X):\overline{\partial}_{b}u=0\},

where
\overline{\partial}_{b}u
is defined in the sense of currents.
It equals
{\rm Ker}~\Box_{b}^{(0)}
and is a closed subspace of

L^{2}(X)
. The Szegő projection is the
orthogonal projection
\Pi:L^{2}(X)\to H^{0}_{b}(X)
,
and the associated Schwartz kernel
\Pi(x,y)\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times X)

is called the Szegő kernel. If
\Box_{b}^{(0)}
has
closed range in
L^{2}
, then
\Pi
maps
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

into
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
, cf.
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
for example.

Let
P\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(X)
be a first-order formally
self-adjoint classical pseudodifferential operator and let

T_{P}:=\Pi P\Pi:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
be the Toeplitz operator
associated to
P
. If the principal symbol
\sigma_{P}
of
P

restricted to the symplectic cone

(1.3)

\Sigma:=\{(x,t\xi_{x}):x\in X,~t>0\}\subset T^{*}X

is everywhere positive, we say that
T_{P}
is elliptic.
Then
T_{P}
has a self-adjoint
L^{2}
-extension,
cf. Theorem
3.3
, and
the spectrum
\Spec(T_{P})\subset\mathbb{R}
of
T_{P}

consists only of isolated
eigenvalues, is bounded from below
and has only
+\infty
as a point of accumulation.
Moreover, for every

\lambda\in\Spec(T_{P})\setminus\{0\}
, the eigenspace

\Ker(T_{P}-\lambda I)

is a finite dimensional subspace of
H^{0}_{b}(X)\cap\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

(see Theorem
3.4
).

The main analytic object of this paper is a spectral operator
and its Schwartz kernel.
We consider a function
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with

\supp\chi\subset(0,+\infty)
and define for
k>0
the function

(1.4)

\chi_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\,,\quad\chi_{k}(t)=\chi\big(k^{-1}t\big).

Then
\chi_{k}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,
\supp\chi_{k}=k\supp\chi\subset(0,+\infty)
.
We will study the spectral operator
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
constructed
by the functional calculus. The kernel of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})

is given by

(1.5)

\chi_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\chi_{k}(\lambda_{j})f_{j}(x)\overline{f_{j}}(y)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X\times X),

where
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{j}\leq\ldots\,

is the sequence of non-zero eigenvalues of
T_{P}
arranged in
increasing order and counted with multiplicity,
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{+\infty}
is an orthonormal system
for
H^{0}_{b}(X)\cap\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

consisting of eigenfunctions of
T_{P}
corresponding
to the sequence of eigenvalues

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{+\infty}
.
The range of the operator
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
contains
the eigenspaces of
T_{P}
corresponding to eigenvalues
in the set
k\supp\chi
, which drifts to the right and increases as

k\to+\infty
.
We consider a function
\chi
with support in
(0,+\infty)

in order to avoid the zero eigenvalue of
T_{P}
,
which has infinite multiplicity and whose
corresponding eigenfunctions are not necessarily CR.

Our first result describes the kernel
of the spectral operator
\chi_{k}(T_{P})

as a semi-classical Fourier integral modulo
k
-negligible
smooth kernels. To state it, we recall
Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand’s fundamental theorem
[
5
]

(see also
[
24
,
32
]
) about the structure of the
Szegő kernel, cf. Theorem
2.10
.
For any coordinate patch
(D,x)
on
X
,
there is a smooth function
\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
with

(1.6)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Im}\varphi(x,y)\geq 0,\\
&\varphi(x,y)=0~\text{if and only if}~y=x,\\
&d_{x}\varphi(x,x)=-d_{y}\varphi(x,x)=\xi(x),\end{split}

such that we have on
D\times D
, the Szegő projector
can be approximated by a Fourier integral operator

(1.7)

\Pi(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{it\varphi(x,y)}s(x,y,t)dt+F(x,y),

where
F(x,y)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D)
and
s(x,y,t)\in S^{n}_{\operatorname{cl}}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})

is a classical Hörmander symbol satisfying
s(x,y,t)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}s_{j}(x,y)t^{n-j}
in

S^{n}_{1,0}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and

(1.8)

s_{0}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,T^{1,0}X)
be an orientable
compact strictly pseudoconvex Cauchy–Riemann manifold
of dimension
2n+1
,
n\geq 1
,
such that the Kohn Laplacian on
X
has closed range in

L^{2}(X)
. Let
\xi
be be a contact form on
X
such that the Levi form

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}d\xi(\cdot,J\cdot)
is positive definite.
Let
(D,x)
be any coordinate patch and let

\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
be the phase function satisfying
(
1.6
) and (
1.7
).
Then for any formally self-adjoint first order pseudodifferential operator

P\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(X)
with

\sigma_{P}(\xi)>0
on
X
,
and for any
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,+\infty))
,
\chi\not\equiv 0
,
the Schwartz kernel of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
,

\chi_{k}(\lambda):=\chi\left(k^{-1}\lambda\right)
,
can be represented for
k
large by

(1.9)

\chi_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{ikt\varphi(x,y)}{A}(x,y,t,k)dt+O\left(k^{-\infty}\right)~\text{on}~D\times D,

where
{A}(x,y,t,k)\in S^{n+1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(1;D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

(1.10)

\begin{split}&{A}(x,y,t,k)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}{A}_{j}(x,y,t)k^{n+1-j}~\mathrm{in}~S^{n+1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(1;D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\\
&A_{j}(x,y,t)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}),~j=0,1,2,\ldots,\\
&{A}_{0}(x,x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x)\,\chi(\sigma_{P}(\xi_{x})t)\,t^{n}\not\equiv 0,\end{split}

and for some compact interval
I\Subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(1.11)

\begin{split}\supp_{t}A(x,y,t,k),~\supp_{t}A_{j}(x,y,t)\subset I,\ \ j=0,1,2,\ldots~.\end{split}

Moreover, for any
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

such that
\supp(\tau_{1})\cap\supp(\tau_{2})=\emptyset
,
we have

(1.12)

\tau_{1}\chi_{k}(T_{P})\tau_{2}=O\left(k^{-\infty}\right).

The representation (
1.9
)
of the kernel of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})

can be viewed as a semi-classical version of the theorem of
Boutet de Monvel and Sjöstrand. It
shows that
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
is a semi-classical Fourier integral
operator with complex phase and
with canonical relation generated by the phase
\varphi(x,y)t
.
The integral in (
1.9
)
is a smooth kernel, because
t
runs in the bounded interval

I
. The term
O(k^{-\infty})
denotes
k
-negligible
smooth kernels (cf. Definition
2.2
). We refer to
(
2.12
) for the definition of
the space
S^{n+1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(1;D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
.

It was shown in
[
4
, Lemma 12.2]

that there is a classical elliptic pseudodifferential operator

Q
on
X
so that

(1.13)

Q\Pi\equiv\Pi Q,\ \ T_{P}\equiv\Pi Q\Pi.

From (
1.13
), we can check that

(1.14)

\chi_{k}(T_{P})=\Pi\circ\chi_{k}(Q)\circ\Pi+O(k^{-\infty}).

Although it is possible to investigate how
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
behaves for
large values of
k
by
using to (
1.13
) and (
1.14
),
we adopt an alternative method in this article.
Namely, we develop a calculus for
z
-dependent Fourier integral
operators with complex phase in order to obtain an expansion of

(z-T_{P})^{-1}\Pi
, cf. Theorem
3.12
,
and we use the Helffer–Sjöstrand’s
formula (cf.
[
10
, §2]
or
[
11
, §8]
)
to reduce the study of

\chi_{k}(T_{P})
to the asymptotic expansion of

(1.15)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\chi}_{k}}{\partial\overline{z}}(z-T_{P})^{-1}\Pi\,\frac{dz\wedge d\overline{z}}{2\pi i},

as
k\to+\infty
, where
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}

is an almost analytic extension of
\chi_{k}
.
We refer to Section
2.3

for further details about the strategy of proving
Theorem
1.1
,
and to Theorems
4.11
and

4.12
for the proof.
A spectral asymptotics result in
the case of an arbitrary function
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
will be given in

4.13
.

Although it is possible to study the expansion of

(z-T_{P})^{-1}\Pi
by using the calculus of
Hermite type Fourier integral operators in

[
4
, §2-11]
, it is unclear how to get the
phase function (
1.6
) and
the semi-classical symbol (
1.10
)
to describe
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
as in (

) by using such kind of calculus.
The calculus used in this paper not only
gives us an expansion formula for the operator

(

suitable to explore CR geometry.

We will later explore the application of this to CR embedding problems.
Additionally, (

of CR manifolds, building on the works of
[
27
,
29
]
.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

In the situation of Theorem
1.1
,
the kernel of the operator
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
has the asymptotic expansion on the diagonal

(1.16)

\chi_{k}(T_{P})(x,x)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{A}_{j}(x)k^{n+1-j}~\text{in}~S^{n+1}_{\rm loc}(1;X),

where
\mathcal{A}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
and

(1.17)

\mathcal{A}_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}(x)}{\sigma_{P}(\xi_{x})^{n+1}dV(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\chi(t)t^{n}dt.

The trace of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})
has the asymptotics as
k\to+\infty
,

(1.18)

\operatorname{Tr}\chi_{k}(T_{P})=\int_{X}\chi_{k}(T_{P})(x,x)dV=\frac{k^{n+1}}{2\pi^{n+1}}\int_{X}\frac{dV_{\xi}(x)}{\sigma_{P}(\xi_{x})^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\chi(t)t^{n}dt+O(k^{n})\,.

1.18
) in geometric terms.

dV_{\Sigma}
the symplectic volume form on
\Sigma\subset T^{*}X
.

\int_{X}\frac{dV_{\xi}(x)}{\sigma_{P}(\xi_{x})^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\chi(t)t^{n}dt=\int_{\Sigma}\chi(\sigma_{P}|_{\Sigma})dV_{\Sigma}\,,

(1.20)

\operatorname{Tr}\chi_{k}(T_{P})=\frac{k^{n+1}}{2\pi^{n+1}}\int_{\Sigma}\chi(\sigma_{P}|_{\Sigma})dV_{\Sigma}+O(k^{n})\,,\quad k\to+\infty\,.

The first one concerns a Szegő type limit theorem for scaled spectral measures.
Szegő limit theorems give the weak
asymptotics of the spectral measure of a family of operators with
respect to the parameter of the family, see
[

.
Szegő first obtained such a result for
truncated infinite Toeplitz matrices, as the size of the truncation tends to infinity.
Let

and

be as in Theorem

Let

be the sequence of non-zero eigenvalues
of

The counting spectral measure of

is

\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\delta\left(t-\lambda_{j}\right)
,
where
\delta
denotes the Dirac measure at zero.
By the Weyl law for
T_{P}
(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 13.1]
),
the spectrum counting function

has the asymptotics

1

[
4
]

N(k)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Sigma_{1})}{2\pi^{n+1}}k^{n+1}+O(k^{n}),\quad k\to+\infty,

where

\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma
where
\sigma_{P}\leq 1
.
Note that

It follows that

for all

. Let

We will now examine to which extend the map

preserves

and

\omega_{\beta}
for

Theorem 1.5
.

Consider the situation in Theorem

and

F_{k}
as in (

).
Choose the volume form and the pseudodifferential operator to be

is the Reeb vector field determined by

Then we have that in

-topology as

\begin{split}&|k^{-1}dF_{k}\mathcal{T}|^{2}=C^{\prime}_{\chi}+O(k^{-1}),\\

&|k^{-1}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda(k)}\circ F_{k}-k^{-1}dF_{k}\mathcal{T}|^{2}=O(k^{-1}),\end{split}

on

Theorem 1.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem

and with

as in (

in the

-topology on

.

Corollary 1.7
.

In the situation of Theorem
1.6

with

as in Theorem

we have

(1.31)

F_{k}^{*}d\omega_{\lambda(k)}=d\xi+O(k^{-1})\,,\quad k\to+\infty,

in the

-topology on

.

We note that in the situation of

,

induces a Levi form on any sphere

of positive radius centered in zero.
Furthermore, the Levi form on

with respect to

(see Definition

Hence, Corollary

can be seen as a

who proved that the

under the Kodaira map for large tensor powers of a positive

the line bundle curvature.
We also point out that
(
1.30

(

are invariant under scaling of the map
F_{k}

by constant factors and that the leading terms are independent
of the choice of
\chi
. Moreover, Theorem
1.6

holds for any choice of pseudodifferential operator

satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.6
indicates
that the CR and contact structures on
X
are closely related
to those of spheres in
\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
via the map
F_{k}
when
k

becomes large. We will show now that under the right choices of the
operator
T_{P}
we can achieve that
F_{k}(X)
is actually lying
in a sphere up to a small perturbation when
k
becomes large.
We note that the study of the embeddability of
of strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds in spheres is a
important topic in CR geometry and complex analysis.
Fornaess
[
13
]
obtained
the embeddability in boundaries of strictly convex domains
in complex Euclidean space
for a large class of strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds,.
It was shown by Faran
[
12
]
(see also Forstneric
[
14
]
)
that there exist real analytic strictly pseudoconvex hypersurfaces
in the complex Euclidean space which are not CR-embedabble into
finite dimensional spheres. However, Lempert
[
37
]

(see also
[
36
]
) proved that every compact,
real analytic, strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface in a complex
Euclidean space can be CR embedded into an infinite dimensional sphere.
Forstneric
[
14
]
and Løw
[
40
]

independently proved that any bounded strictly pseudoconvex
domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary can be properly and
holomorphically embedded into some finite dimensional unit ball
and in addition proved that such an embedding can be chosen
to be continuous up to the boundary.
Theorem
1.8
provides an embedding
result for strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds into arbitrarily small
perturbations of spheres, preserving the Reeb vector field asymptotically.

Let
S^{2N-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the sphere of radius
one centered in zero. Given a smooth function

f\colon S^{2N-1}\to(-1,\infty)
we define the perturbed sphere

S^{2N-1}(f)
by

(1.32)

S^{2N-1}(f):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\colon|z|=1+f\Big(\frac{z}{|z|}\Big)\right\}.

The projection
z\mapsto z/|z|
defines a
diffeomorphism between
S^{2N-1}(f)
and
S^{2N-1}
.
Furthermore,
S^{2N-1}(f)
carries the induced CR structure
as a real hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Given
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
,
we define a
1
-form
\alpha_{f,\beta}
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
by

(1.33)

\alpha_{f,\beta}=-\frac{i(\partial-\overline{\partial})\rho(z)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\beta_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}},

where
\rho
is the defining function for
S^{2N-1}(f)

given by
\rho(z)=|z|-f\left(z/|z|\right)-1
.
It turns out that the pullback of
\alpha_{f,\beta}
to

S^{2N-1}(f)
via the inclusion map is a non-vanishing real

1
-form annihilating the CR structure
T^{1,0}S^{2N-1}(f)

of
S^{2N-1}(f)
. We will show now that any compact strictly
pseudoconvex CR manifold as in Theorem
1.1

can be CR embedded in a perturbed sphere where the perturbation
can be chosen arbitrary small. This phenomenon can be seen as an
almost spherical embedding.

Theorem 1.8
.

Consider the situation in Theorem
1.1

and denote by
\mathcal{T}
the Reeb vector field with
respect to
\xi
determined by
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}d\xi=0

and
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=1
.
Fix any Hermitian metric and a family of
\mathscr{C}^{m}
-norms,

m\geq 0
, on
X
. For any
\varepsilon>0
and any
m\in\mathbb{N}

there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a perturbed sphere
S^{2N-1}(f)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}

where
f\colon S^{2N-1}\to(-1,\infty)
is smooth,
\beta\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}

and a CR embedding
F\colon X\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
with the following properties:

(i)

F(X)\subset S^{2N-1}(f)
.

(ii)

\sup_{S^{2N-1}}|f|\leq\varepsilon
.

(iii)

There exists an open neighborhood
U
of

\{\widetilde{F}(x)\colon x\in X\}
in

S^{2N-1}
, where
\widetilde{F}(x):=F(x)/|F(x)|
for

,
such that
\sup_{U}|df|\leq\varepsilon
.

(iv)

\|f\circ\widetilde{F}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{m}(X)}\leq\varepsilon
,

(v)

|F^{*}\alpha_{f,\beta}-\xi|\leq\varepsilon
.

(vi)

F_{*}\mathcal{T}
is transversal to

T^{1,0}S^{2N-1}(f)\oplus T^{0,1}S^{2N-1}(f)
at any point of
F(X)
.

(vii)

We have

\Big\|1-\frac{|F_{*}\mathcal{T}|}{|\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\circ F|}\Big\|_{\mathscr{C}^{m}(X)}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\Bigl\|1-\frac{\langle F_{*}\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\circ F\rangle}{|F_{*}\mathcal{T}||\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\circ F|}\Bigr\|_{\mathscr{C}^{m}(X)}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\|F^{*}\omega_{\beta}-\xi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{m}(X)}\leq\varepsilon.

Note that the choice of
\varepsilon>0
may have an effect on the
dimension
N
.
As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.8
,
any strictly pseudoconvex, orientable, compact CR manifold

of dimension greater or equal to five can be embedded in a
perturbed sphere.

It was shown in
[
44
]

that any compact Sasakian manifold admits a CR-embedding into a
Sasakian manifold which is diffeomorphic to a sphere.
If in addition the first
de Rham cohomology group of
X
vanishes, it was
proved in
[
46
]
that
X
can be CR-embedded into a sphere with
a transversally deformed Sasakian structure.
See also the related result
[
39
]
in Sasakian geometry.
We note that Theorem
1.8

can be also stated as a result on CR embeddings into spheres
with non-standard CR structures.
We refer to Theorem
6.5
for a precise statement.

\begin{split}&T_{P}(x,y)\equiv T_{\varphi}(x,y)~\text{on}~X\times D,\\

Example 5.2
.

Associated with the contact form
\xi
one has the
so-called Reeb vector field
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(\xi)

uniquely defined by the equations

\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=1

\iota_{\mathcal{T}}d\xi=0

[

It follows that

which is preserved under the flow of

. Hence we find that the operator

-inner product induced by
the volume form

.
We can apply Theorem
1.1
with
P:=-i\mathcal{T}

to study the spectral asymptotics of
T_{P}=\Pi(-i\mathcal{T})\Pi
,
which is in this case directly linked to the dynamics of the Reeb flow for CR functions.
Furthermore, it turns out that in this case the leading coefficient
\mathcal{A}_{0}(x)

in the asymptotic expansion of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})(x,x)
is constant on
X
and equals

\frac{k^{n+1}}{2\pi^{n+1}}\int\chi(t)t^{n}dt
. We note that
\alpha_{P}=\xi

holds in that case.
In this set-up,
with the additional assumption that the flow of
\mathcal{T}

preserves the CR structure, the asymptotic expansion of
\chi_{k}(T_{P})

on the diagonal is due to
Herrmann–Hsiao–Li
[
21
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Example 5.3

A particular case of Example

Let

The connection

In this case,
\xi(-i\partial_{\vartheta})=1

Theorem 5.5
.

In the situation of Example
5.3

(5.10)

\begin{split}&\chi_{k}(T_{\vartheta})(x,x)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}a_{j}(x)k^{n+1-j}~\text{in}~S^{n+1}_{\rm loc}(1;X),\end{split}

\displaystyle a_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi(t)t^{n-j}dt\right)b_{j}(\pi_{M}(x))
and

are given in (
5.7
),

Proof.

When
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\Pi\circ\chi_{k}(T_{\vartheta})=\chi_{k}(T_{\vartheta})
. Also, when
k>0
is large, by (

) and

(5.11)

\left|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi\left(\frac{m}{k}\right)B_{m}(\pi_{M}(x))-\sum_{j=0}^{N}k^{n+1-j}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}k^{-1}\chi\left(\frac{m}{k}\right)\left(\frac{m}{k}\right)^{n-j}b_{j}(\pi_{M}(x))\right|\\

By the Poisson summation formula,

[

In the right-hand side of the above equation, when

Finally, we give an explicit calculation for the spectrum of

\rho([z])=y_{1}^{2}+\ldots+y_{n}^{2}

it is enough to prove the statement
when
v
is a smooth CR function. Note that we have a Lie group action on
X

by the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
given by
[t]\circ[z]=[z+t]
.
Hence by Fourier transform we can write
v
as the
L^{2}
converging sum

v=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}v_{m}
where
v_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, are smooth functions given by

(5.48)

v_{m}([z])=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{[t]\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}v([z+t])e^{-i\langle m,t\rangle}d\mathbb{T}^{n}([t]).

Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action is CR as the restriction of a holomorphic action on
M
. It follows that
v_{m}
is a smooth CR function since
v
is smooth and CR. Furthermore, we can diffeomorphically identify
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times S^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}
and
X
via the mapping
([x],y)\mapsto[x+iy]
which we will denote by
F
. It follows that
v_{m}
can be written as
v_{m}=f_{m}s_{m}
for some smooth function
f_{m}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
with
\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial x_{j}}=0
,
1\leq j\leq n
. We will show that
f_{m}
is constant. Therefore, take a point
[x+iy]\in X
and consider the decomposition
T_{[x+iy]}X=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
where
W_{1}=dF(T_{[x]}\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
W_{2}=dF(T_{y}S^{n-1}_{\varepsilon})
. We have
\mathcal{X}(f_{m})=0
for all
\mathcal{X}\in W_{1}
. So we need to show
Y(f_{m})=0
for all
Y\in W_{2}
. We note that
J(W_{2})\subset W_{1}
with
T\perp J(W_{2})
where
J
is the defining endomorphism for the CR structure. It follows that for
Y\in W_{2}
we have
Z:=Y-iJY\in T^{1,0}X
. Since
v_{m}
and
s_{m}
are smooth CR functions we find at
[x+iy]
that

(5.49)

0=\overline{Z}(v_{m})=\overline{Z}(f_{m})s_{m}=Y(f_{m})\widetilde{s}_{m}

holds.
We have that
\widetilde{s}_{m}
is nowhere zero and hence
V(f_{m})=0
for all
V\in T_{[x+iy]}X
.
We conclude that
v=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}s_{m}
for some numbers
\{a_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\subset\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.10
.

From Lemma
5.14
and the first part of Lemma
5.13
it follows that the set
\{s_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
forms an orthonormal basis of
H_{b}^{0}(X)
. Hence we find
Af=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(iTf,s_{m})s_{m}
for any smooth CR function
f
. Choosing
f=s_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the claim follows from the second part of Lemma
5.13
.
∎

6.
Semi-classical CR embeddings and their applications

6.1.
CR Kodaira embedding

We are going to prove Theorem
1.4
.
Let
(X,T^{1,0}X)
be a compact orientable strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold as in Theorem
1.1
. Denote by
\xi
a contact form and by
\mathcal{T}
the respective Reeb vector field. Let
T_{P}
ba a Toeplitz operator as in Theorem
1.1
. We denote by
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots
its positive eigenvalues counting multiplicities and let
f_{1},f_{2}\ldots
be a respective orthonormal system of eigenfunctions in
H^{0}_{b}(X)
. From now on let
\chi\in\mathscr{C}_{c}^{\infty}((\delta_{1},\delta_{2}))
,
\chi\not\equiv 0
, be a cut-off function for some
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
and we put
\eta(t):=|\chi|^{2}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In the situation and notation in Theorem
1.1
, we notice that the local asymptotic expansion

(6.1)

\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{ikt\varphi(x,y)}A(x,y,t,k)dt+O\left(k^{-\infty}\right)

holds and

(6.2)

{A}_{0}(x,x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x)\,|\chi(\sigma_{P}(\xi_{x})t)|^{2}t^{n}

satisfies
\int_{0}^{+\infty}A_{0}(x,x,t)dt\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D,]0,\infty[)
.

Setting
N_{k}=\#\{j\in\mathbb{N}:0<\lambda_{j}\leq\delta_{2}k\}
, we define

(6.3)

G_{k}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}},\quad G_{k}(x)=\left(\chi(\lambda_{1}k^{-1})f_{1},\ldots,\chi(\lambda_{N_{k}}k^{-1})f_{N_{k}}\right).

We will to show that
G_{k}
is a CR embedding for all sufficiently large
k
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
k_{0}>0
such that
G_{k}(X)\subset\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}\setminus\{0\}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Proof.

We have
|G_{k}(x)|^{2}=\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,x)
. From Theorem
1.1
using
\eta\geq 0
,
\eta\not\equiv 0
, we find that there exists
C,k_{0}>0
such that
|G_{k}|^{2}\geq C
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists
k_{0}>0
such that
G_{k}
is an immersion for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Proof.

Put
H=\{H_{x}\}_{x\in X}
,
H_{x}:=\text{Re}(T^{1,0}_{x}X)
, and
define an isomorphism
L_{k}\colon TX\to TX
by
L_{k}(\mathcal{T})=\frac{1}{k}\mathcal{T}
and
L_{k}(V)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}V
for
V\in H
. Denote by
h
the positive symmetric bilinearform on
H
induced by the Levi-form. Extend
h
to a symmetric bilinearform on
TX
by
h(\mathcal{T},\cdot)\equiv 0
. Moreover, let
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle_{X}
be a smooth metric on
\mathbb{C}TX
as in Section
2.2
. We will show that there exists positive smooth functions
a,b\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

(6.4)

k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}})=a\xi\otimes\xi+bh+O(k^{-\frac{1}{2}})

where
g_{\text{eucl}}
denotes the Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
induced by its standard Hermitian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
More precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(6.5)

\left|k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(F^{*}_{k}g_{\text{eucl}})(V,W)-a\xi(V)\xi(W)-bh(V,W)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{k}}|V|_{X}|W|_{X}

for all
k\geq 1
,
V,W\in T_{p}X
,
p\in X
.
From this statement it follows that the pullback of the
Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
under
G_{k}
becomes
a Riemannian metric on
X
when
k
is large enough.
Hence
G_{k}
is an immersion for all sufficiently large
k
.

Let us show that (
6.5
) holds.
With Theorem
1.1
and
Lemma
4.14
we have

(6.6)

\begin{split}&k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}})(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})\\
&=k^{-n-2}\mathrm{Re}\left((d_{x}\otimes d_{y}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)|_{x=y})(\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{T})\right)\\
&=a+O(k^{-1})\end{split}

for some positive smooth function
a\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Hence there exists
C_{1}>0
with

(6.7)

|k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}})(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})-a|\leq C_{1}k^{-1},\>\>\text{for all $k\geq 1$.}

From Lemma
4.14
we also have for
V\in H_{x}
that

(6.8)

k^{-n-1}(d_{x}\otimes d_{y}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)|_{x=y})(\mathcal{T}\otimes V)=O(k).

We conclude that there exist
C_{2}>0
such that

(6.9)

|k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}})(\mathcal{T},V)|\leq C_{2}k^{-\frac{1}{2}}|V|_{X}

holds for all
V\in H
and all
k\geq 1
.
We define a symmetric bilinear form on

H
by
R_{k}=\{(R_{k})_{x}\}_{x\in X}
,
(R_{k})_{x}\colon H_{x}\times H_{x}\to\mathbb{C}
,

(6.10)

(R_{k})_{x}(V_{1},V_{2}):=k^{-n-1}G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}}(V_{1},V_{2})=k^{-n-1}\text{Re}\langle V_{1}G_{k}(x),V_{2}G_{k}(x)\rangle.

From Theorem
1.1
we find bilinear forms

r_{j}=\{(r_{j})_{x}\}_{x\in X}
,
(r_{j})_{x}\colon H_{x}\times H_{x}\to\mathbb{R}
,
j=0,1
,
and a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

(6.11)

|R_{k}(V_{1},V_{2})-k^{2}r_{0}(V_{1},V_{2})-k^{1}r_{1}(V_{1},V_{2})|\leq C_{3}|V_{1}|_{X}|V_{2}|_{X}

holds for all
k\geq 1
,
x\in X
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in H_{x}
. From Lemma
4.14
we obtain
r_{0}\equiv 0
. So we will calculate
r_{1}
. Given
x\in X
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in H_{x}
we can find smooth sections
Z_{j}\in\Gamma(U,T^{1,0}X)
with
2\text{Re}(Z_{j})_{x}=V_{j}
,
j=1,2
, defined in an open neighborhood
U
around
x
.
At
x\in X
, since
G_{k}
is a CR map, we find

(6.12)

\begin{split}\langle(\chi(k^{-1}\lambda_{1})V_{1}f_{1},\ldots,&\chi(k^{-1}\lambda_{N_{k}})V_{1}f_{N_{k}})),(\chi(k^{-1}\lambda_{1})V_{2}f_{1},\ldots,\chi(k^{-1}\lambda_{N_{k}})V_{2}f_{N_{k}}))\rangle\\
&=:\langle V_{1}G_{k},V_{2}G_{k}\rangle=\langle Z_{1}G_{k},Z_{2}G_{k}\rangle\\
&=\overline{Z_{2}}(Z_{1}\langle G_{k},G_{k}\rangle)-\langle\overline{Z_{2}}(Z_{1}G_{k}),G_{k}\rangle\\
&=\overline{Z_{2}}(Z_{1}\langle G_{k},G_{k}\rangle)+\langle[Z_{1},\overline{Z_{2}}]G_{k},G_{k}\rangle.\end{split}

We have

(6.13)

k^{-n-1}\overline{Z_{2}}(Z_{1}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,x))=O(1)

and

(6.14)

k^{-n-1}(d_{x}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)|_{x=y})([Z_{1},\overline{Z_{2}}])\\
-i\left(\frac{k}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{\sigma_{P}(\xi)^{n+2}dV}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{n+1}\eta(t)dt\right)\xi([Z_{1},\overline{Z}_{2}])=O(1).

We find, with
\xi([Z_{1},\overline{Z}_{2}])=-2i\mathcal{L}(Z_{1},Z_{2})
, that
r_{1}(V_{1},V_{2})=bh(V_{1},V_{2})
for some positive smooth function
b\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence we conclude that there exist a constant
C_{4}>0
such that

(6.15)

|k^{-n-1}L_{k}^{*}(G_{k}^{*}g_{\text{eucl}})(V_{1},V_{2})-bh(V_{1},V_{2})|\leq C_{4}k^{-1}|V_{1}|_{X}|V_{2}|_{X}

holds for all
k\geq 1
and all
V_{1},V_{2}\in H_{x}
,
x\in X
. The statement follows.
∎

We are going to show now that
G_{k}
is injective for all sufficiently large
k
. Given a point
p\in X
we fix coordinates
(x,y)
around
(p,p)\in X\times X
centered in the origin as in Theorem
2.15
.
We write
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}),~z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
,
y^{\prime}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2n}),~w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
, where
z_{j}=x_{2j-1}+ix_{2j},~w_{j}=y_{2j-1}+y_{2j}
. By Theorem
2.15
, for any phase function
\varphi\in{\Ph}(\Pi,\xi,D)
, we have

(6.16)

\begin{split}\varphi(x,y)=&x_{2n+1}-y_{2n+1}+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}|z_{j}-w_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}i\mu_{j}(\overline{z}_{j}w_{j}-z_{j}\overline{w}_{j})\\
+&\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(d_{j}(z_{j}x_{2n+1}-w_{j}y_{2n+1})+\overline{d}_{j}(\overline{z}_{j}x_{2n+1}-\overline{w}_{j}y_{2n+1})\right)\\
+&(x_{2n+1}-y_{2n+1})f_{\varphi}(x,y)+O\left(|(x,y)|^{3}\right).\end{split}

Furthermore, for this coordinates there exists a constant
C>0
with
\mathrm{Im}\varphi(x,y)\geq C|z-w|^{2}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

There exists
k_{0}>0
such that
\widetilde{G}_{k}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}\setminus\{0\}
,
\widetilde{G}_{k}(x)=\frac{G_{k}(x)}{|G_{k}(x)|}
is well-defined and injective for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\widetilde{G_{k}}
is well-defined for all sufficiently large
k>0
follows immediately from Lemma
6.1
.
We prove the injectivity statement by contradiction following
the methods in
[
28
, Theorem 4.8]
.
Suppose we can find sequences of integers
\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
sqeunces of points
\{x_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{y_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
X
with

\lim_{j\to+\infty}k_{j}=+\infty
such that
x_{k_{j}}\neq y_{k_{j}}

and
\widetilde{G}_{k_{j}}(x_{k_{j}})=\widetilde{G}_{k_{j}}(y_{k_{j}})

for each
j\geq 1
. For simplicity, we just use the index

k
instead of
k_{j}
. Because
X
is compact, we may assume
by passing to a subsequence that
x_{k}\to p\in X
and

y_{k}\to q\in X
as
k\to+\infty
.
Since
\langle G_{k}(x),G_{k}(y)\rangle=\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,y)
and
|G_{k}(x)|=\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x,x)}
, here we have

(6.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})}{\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},x_{k})}\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})}}=\frac{|G_{k}(x_{k})|^{2}}{|G_{k}(x_{k})||G_{k}(x_{k})|}=1

for all sufficiently large
k>0
.
From (
1.12
) in
Theorem
1.1

and the above equation we conclude that
p=q
must hold.
We consider several cases for the convergent speed of

x_{k}
and
y_{k}
to
p
.
Denote
x_{k}=(z^{k},x^{k}_{2n+1})
and
y_{k}=(w^{k},y^{k}_{2n+1})
in local coordinates around
p
as above when
k>0
is large enough. We identify
p
as
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, and we let
\mathscr{M}\geq 0
be a constant with

(6.18)

k|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}\geq\mathscr{M}

for infinitely many sufficiently large
k>0
.

Case I:
\mathscr{M}>0
.
We find
by (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.1
and Fatou lemma that for some subsequence we have

(6.19)

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|&\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\int e^{-kt\operatorname{Im}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}|{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)|dt\\
&\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\int e^{-Ck|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}t}|{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)|dt\\
&=\int e^{-C\mathscr{M}t}{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt<\int{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt.\end{split}

However, we also have

(6.20)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},x_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})=\int{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt>0,

and hence

(6.21)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|}{\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},x_{k})}\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})}}<1

which contradicts (
6.17
). So, we must have

(6.22)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}=0.

Now, take a subsequence such that

(6.23)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|=\mathscr{N}

for some
\mathscr{N}\in[0,+\infty]
.

Case II:
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}=0
and
\mathscr{N}\in(0,+\infty]
.
From Taylor’s formula, (
1.6
) and
\partial_{x}\mathrm{Im}\varphi(x,x)=0
we have

(6.24)

\begin{split}|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})-\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|&\leq C_{1}|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}\\
|\mathrm{Re}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})-\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|&\leq C_{2}|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}\end{split}

and

(6.25)

|x^{k}_{2n+1}-y^{k}_{2n+1}|\leq C_{3}(|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|+|z^{k}-w^{k}|)

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
independent of
k>0
.
Since
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle=0

we find

(6.26)

\begin{split}\left|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})\right|&\geq\left|\mathrm{Re}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})\right|\geq c(|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|-|z_{k}-w_{k}|^{2})\\
\left|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})\right|&\leq\frac{1}{c}(|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|+|z_{k}-w_{k}|^{2})\end{split}

when
k>0
is large enough for some constant
c>0
.
Suppose that
\mathscr{N}=\infty
.
Since we have
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}=0
it follows from (
6.26
) that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k\left|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})\right|=\infty
.
We obtain

(6.27)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|\\
=&\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\left|\int e^{ikt\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)dt\right|\\
\leq&\limsup_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}\int\left|e^{ikt\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}\frac{1}{ik\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}\frac{d}{dt}{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)\right|dt\\
=&\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int e^{-kt\operatorname{Im}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}\frac{1}{k|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})|}\left|\frac{d}{dt}{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)\right|k^{-n-1}dt\\
\leq&\int\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\left(e^{-Ctk|z^{k}-w^{k}|^{2}}\frac{1}{k|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})|}\left|\frac{d}{dt}{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)\right|k^{-n-1}\right)dt\\
=&0.\end{split}

With (
6.20
) we find

(6.28)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|}{\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},x_{k})}\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})}}=0,

which contradicts (
6.17
).

If
\mathscr{N}\in(0,+\infty)
, then by (
6.26
) we have that
k|\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})|
is bounded. Furthermore, we have that
k|\mathrm{Re}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})|
is bounded from below by a positive constant when

\mathrm{Im}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})\geq 0
. Hence, by passing to a subsequence we can assume
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})=a+ib
with

and

.
With
A_{0}(0,0,t)\geq 0
and
A_{0}(0,0,\cdot)\not\equiv 0
we conclude

(6.29)

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-n-1}|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|\\
=&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left|\int e^{itk\operatorname{Re}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}e^{-tk\operatorname{Im}\varphi(x_{k},y_{k})}k^{-n-1}{A}(x_{k},y_{k},t,k)dt\right|\\
=&\left|\int e^{ita}e^{-tb}{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt\right|<\left|\int e^{-tb}{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt\right|\\
\leq&\int{A}_{0}(0,0,t)dt.\end{split}

With (
6.20
) it follows that

(6.30)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},y_{k})|}{\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(x_{k},x_{k})}\sqrt{\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})}}<1

which contradicts (
6.17
).
So we may write

(6.31)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|=0.

Case III:
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|z^{k}-w_{k}|^{2}=0
and
\lim_{k\to+\infty}k|\langle\partial_{x}\varphi(y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle|=0
.
Define

(6.32)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{F}_{k}(u):=|\eta_{k}(T_{P})(ux_{k}+(1-u)y_{k},y_{k})|^{2}\\
&\mathscr{G}_{k}(u):=\eta_{k}(T_{P})(y_{k},y_{k})\eta_{k}(T_{P})(ux_{k}+(1-u)y_{k},ux_{k}+(1-u)y_{k}),\\
&\mathscr{H}_{k}(u):=\frac{\mathscr{F}_{k}(u)}{\mathscr{G}_{k}(u)}.\end{split}

From (
6.17
) and basic inequalities,

(6.33)

0\leq\mathscr{H}_{k}(u)\leq 1.

Moreover, because
\mathscr{H}_{k}(0)=\mathscr{H}_{k}(1)=1
,
we can find a
u_{k}\in(0,1)
such that

(6.34)

\mathscr{H}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})\geq 0.

Applying the exterior differential on both sides

For the proof of Theorem
6.5
we need two results from linear algebra.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
E
be a finite dimensional complex Hermitian vector space and let
V,W\subset E
two subspaces of equal dimension. Assume that there exists
0\leq\delta<1
such that

(6.68)

\displaystyle|\langle v,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta|v||w|\text{ for all }v\in V,w\in W^{\perp},

(6.69)

\displaystyle|\langle v,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta|v||w|\text{ for all }v\in V^{\perp},w\in W.

Then, for the Grassmannian distance between
V
and
W
we have
\operatorname{dist}(V,W)\leq\sqrt{2\delta}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{V}
and
P_{W}
denote the orthogonal projections to
V
and
W
respectively. Given
a\in E
we find

(6.70)

\begin{split}&|(P_{V}-P_{W})a|^{2}\\
=&\langle P_{V}a,a\rangle+\langle P_{W}a,a\rangle-\langle P_{V}a,P_{W}a\rangle-\langle P_{W}a,P_{V}a\rangle\\
=&\langle P_{V}a,(1-P_{W})a\rangle+\langle P_{W}a,(1-P_{V})a\rangle.\end{split}

It follows from the assumptions that
|(P_{V}-P_{W})a|^{2}\leq 2\delta|a|^{2}
and hence

(6.71)

\operatorname{dist}(V,W):=\|P_{V}-P_{W}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\sqrt{2\delta}.

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional complex Euclidean space with the standard Hermitian metric and let
A,D\in\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{C})
two matrices with
m<n
. Assume that there exists
c,\varepsilon>0
such that
|A^{*}v|\geq c|v|
for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
\|D\|_{\text{op}}\leq\varepsilon
with
c>2\varepsilon
. Put
V={\rm Ker}~A
and
W={\rm Ker}~(A+D)
. We have

(6.72)

\displaystyle|\langle v,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{c-2\varepsilon}|v||w|\text{ for all }v\in V,w\in W^{\perp},

(6.73)

\displaystyle|\langle v,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{c-2\varepsilon}|v||w|\text{ for all }v\in V^{\perp},w\in W.

Proof.

Put
B=A+D
and let
v\in V
and
w\in W^{\perp}
be arbitrary. We have
Av=0
and
w\in\operatorname{ran}B^{*}
. Hence, there exists
b\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
w=A^{*}b+D^{*}b
. We obtain

(6.74)

|\langle v,w\rangle|=|\langle v,A^{*}b\rangle|+|\langle v,D^{*}b\rangle|\leq|v||D^{*}b|\leq\varepsilon|v||b|.

Then the first inequality follows from

|w|\geq|A^{*}b|-|D^{*}b|\geq(c-\varepsilon)|b|
. The second inequality follows similarly using
A=B+(-D)
with
|B^{*}v|\geq(c-\varepsilon)|v|
for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
∎

The first claim in Theorem

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
S^{2N+1}(f)
be a perturbed sphere where

\operatorname{dist}(C^{1,0}_{p},T^{1,0}_{p}S^{2N+1})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}}

Proof.

Since
H
is a diffeomorphism between

and

\mathcal{C}^{1,0}
defines a CR structure on
S^{2N+1}
.
Given

is in element of

(6.76)

\displaystyle d\overline{w}_{j}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\,\,\text{ for all }j=0,\ldots,N

(6.77)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}\overline{w}_{j}dw_{j}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|w|d(f\circ H)v.

H^{-1}(z)=(1+f(z))z

v\in\mathbb{C}T_{z}S^{2N+1}

\begin{split}\left(d\overline{z}_{j}+\frac{\overline{z}_{j}df_{z}}{1+f(z)}\right)(v)&=0,\,\,\text{ for all }j=0,\ldots,N\\

(6.79)

6.78

Persistent nodal magnon-photon polariton in ferromagnetic heterostructures
Zhuolun Qiu, Xi-Han Zhou, Hanchen Wang, Guang Yang, Tao Yu

Abstract
Exceptional points with coalescence of eigenvalues and eigenvectors are spectral singularities in the parameter space, achieving which often needs fine-tuning of parameters in quantum systems. We predict a persistent realization of nodal magnon-photon polariton, i.e. , a polariton of long wavelength without any gap splitting in a thin ferromagnetic insulator film sandwiched by two normal metals, which persistently exists when the ferromagnet is sufficiently thick ∼ 100 \sim 100 nm due to the joint effect of dissipation and dissipative coupling. We perform the model calculation beyond the perturbation theory using a classical approach, develop a quantum scheme able to account for the Ohmic dissipation, and find ultrastrong coupling with coupling strength comparable to the bare magnon frequency. Via revealing a simple conversion relation we extend this formalism to superconductors and predict the gap opened by the ultrastrong coupling strongly depends on the direction of polariton propagation. Our findings may help search for robust non-Hermitian topological phases in magnonic and spintronic devices.

The electric field obeys Maxwell’s equations

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf E}({\bf r},t)=-\frac{\partial{\bf B}({\bf r},t)}{\partial t},

(1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf H}({\bf r},t)={\bf J}({\bf r},t)+\epsilon_{r}\dfrac{\partial{\bf E}({\bf r},t)}{\partial t},

(1b)

where

is the dielectric constant depending on the materials:

{\bf J}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle\text{in FI}:~~\nabla^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)+k^{2}_{0}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)=-i\omega\mu_{0}\nabla\times{\bf M}({\bf r},t),

In the NM, the electric field drives the electric current according to Ohm’s Law

The electric field in the NM is governed by

The interaction between magnon and photon in the ferromagnetic heterostructure can be conveniently understood using the quantum approach. However, the Ohmic dissipation in the NM
|
FI
|
NM heterostructure presents a challenge that requires a different quantum scheme from Hermitian conventions.

In the long wavelength limit, the eigenfrequency of the magnon modes is captured by Kittel’s formula
[
71
]

\omega_{N}=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+N_{xx}M_{s})(H_{0}+N_{yy}M_{s})},

(15)

where
N_{xx}
and
N_{yy}
are the “demagnetization factors”. Without the NMs,
N_{xx}=1
and
N_{yy}=0
are real numbers for the magnetic films. Nevertheless, the NMs reflect and dissipate the magnetic stray field, rendering the demagnetization factors to be complex numbers. According to Eq. (
11
), when
k\rightarrow 0
(refer to Appendix
A
for explicit derivation of the renormalized demagnetization factor)

N_{xx}=1,~~~N_{yy}={d_{F}}/({\delta+d_{F}}).

(16)

So
N_{xx}
is a constant, while
N_{yy}
depends on the collective-mode frequency
\omega
via
\delta
. By substitution into Kittel’s formula (
15
), the eigenfrequency of the magnon mode in the NM
|
FI
|
NM is strongly renormalized to be

\omega_{N}=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})\left(H_{0}+\frac{d_{F}}{\delta+d_{F}}M_{s}\right)},

(17)

which is a complex number and cannot be described by the Gilbert phenomenology, different from that of the NM
|
FM bilayer
[
30
]
. Microwave absorption experiments should conveniently measure ferromagnetic resonance (FMR) with significant frequency shift and broadening.

Combining the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation (
12
) and the normalization condition
\int d\mathbf{r}\left({M}_{x}{M}_{y}^{*}-{M}_{x}^{*}{M}_{y}\right)=-i/2
for the magnon modes
[
72
,
73
]
, we find the normalized amplitudes

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{x}=i\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{N}}{8d_{F}\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}+M_{s})}},

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{y}=-\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}+M_{s})}{8d_{F}\omega_{N}}},

with which we expand the magnetization operators as

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{x}

\displaystyle=-i\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar M_{s}\omega_{N}}{4d_{F}\mu_{0}(H_{0}+M_{s})}}\left(e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}-e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{y}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\mu_{0}\gamma^{2}(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}{4d_{F}\omega_{N}}}\left(e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}+e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\right),

(18)

where
\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{m}_{\bf k}
are the creation and annihilation operators of magnon. In this ansatz,
\hat{M}_{x,y}
are no longer Hermitian operators since
\omega_{N}
is not purely real, different from the conventional quantization scheme.

To derive the
bare
photon modes in the heterostructure, we construct an alternative NM
|
non-magnetic-insulator(I)
|
NM heterostructure, in which the thickness of the non-magnetic insulator is also set to be
2d_{F}
. The electric fields are governed by

\displaystyle\text{in I}:~~~\nabla^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)+k_{0}^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)=0,

\displaystyle\text{in NMs}:~~~\nabla^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)+k_{n}^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)=0.

(19)

Given the isotropy in the propagation
y
-
z
plane, without losing generality, we consider the wave propagation along the
\hat{\bf z}
-direction. We assume the plane-wave solution
{\bf E}=\tilde{\bf E}(x)e^{ikz-i\omega t}
, where the amplitudes

\displaystyle\text{In I},~~\tilde{\bf E}(x)=\boldsymbol{\cal E}_{0}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+\boldsymbol{\cal E}^{\prime}_{0}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\text{In NM}~(x>d_{F}),~~\tilde{\bf E}(x)=\boldsymbol{\cal E}_{1}e^{i{\cal B}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\text{In NM}~(x<-d_{F}),~~\tilde{\bf E}(x)=\boldsymbol{\cal E}_{2}e^{-i{\cal B}_{k}x},

in which
{\cal A}_{k}=\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}
and
{\cal B}_{k}=\sqrt{k_{n}^{2}-k^{2}}
. According to Faraday’s Law, the magnetic field
H_{y}=1/(i\omega\mu_{0})(\partial_{z}E_{x}-\partial_{x}E_{z})
.
{E}_{z}
and
\partial_{x}E_{x}
at the interfaces are continuous, leading to the secular equation for the components
\{{\cal E}_{0x},{\cal E}^{\prime}_{0x}\}
:

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{F}}&(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{F}}\\
(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{F}}&(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{F}}\end{matrix}\right|=0,

i.e.,

i(R_{k}^{2}+1)\sin(2{\cal A}_kd_{F})=2R_{k}\cos(2{\cal A}_kd_{F}).

Since the wavelength
2\pi/|{\bf k}|
is much larger than the insulator thickness
d_{F}

\sin(2{\cal A}_kd_{F})\approx 2{\cal A}_{k}d_{F}
and
\cos(2{\cal A}_kd_{F})\approx 1

\omega\mu_{0}\sigma_{c}\gg\omega^{2}\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}
for the NMs, such that

R_{k}=\frac{({\cal A}_{k}^{2}+k^{2}){\cal B}_{k}}{{\cal A}_{k}({\cal B}_{k}^{2}+k^{2})}\approx-i\frac{k_{0}^{2}}{{\cal A}_{k}}\delta\ll 1.

Accordingly, Eq. (
20

k^{2}=\Omega_{n}^{2}\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm I}\left(1+{\delta}/{d_{F}}\right),

where
\epsilon_{\rm I}
is the dielectric constant of the insulator, from which

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}=\sqrt{d_{F}/(d_{F}+\delta)\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm I}}|{\bf k}|

is the dispersion of photon modes in the NM
|
I
|
NM heterostructure, which is no longer purely real since
\delta
contains the imaginary component. They correspond to the TM modes with
E_{y}=H_{x}=H_{z}=0

\{E_{z},E_{x},H_{y}\}
.

When

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{x,k}(x)

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{0x}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{z,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\cal A}_{k}}{k}\left({\cal E}_{0x}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}-{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{y,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\omega\epsilon_{\rm I}}}{k}\left({\cal E}_{0x}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x}\right),

where

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}=ik\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{d_{F}+\delta}},

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k_{n}^{2}-k^{2}}=i\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{\delta}{d_{F}+\delta}k^{2}}\approx\frac{i}{\delta}.

Accordingly, the fields in the insulator are simplified as

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{x,k}(x)

\displaystyle\approx{\cal E}_{0x}(e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x})\approx 2{\cal E}_{0x},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{z,k}(x)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{{\cal A}_{k}}{k}{\cal E}_{0x}(e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}-e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x})\approx i2\frac{{\cal A}_{k}^{2}}{k}{\cal E}_{0x}x,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{y,k}(x)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{{\omega\epsilon_{\rm I}}}{k}{\cal E}_{0x}(e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x})\approx 2\frac{{\omega\epsilon_{\rm I}}}{k}{\cal E}_{0x}.

Thereby,
H_{y}\equiv H_{0y}
and

are uniform across the thin insulator layer.
On the other hand, when
x>d_{F}
and
x<-d_{F}

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{x,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{\rm I}}{\epsilon_{0}+i\sigma_{c}/\omega}\tilde{E}_{x,k}(\pm d_{F}^{\mp})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{F})},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{z,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{z,k}(\pm d_{F})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{F})},

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{y,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}_{y,k}(\pm d_{F})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{F})},

(23)

where “

and

H_{y}\approx H_{0y}e^{-{\rm sgn}(x)(x-{\rm sgn}(x)d_{F})/\delta}
outside of the insulator.

The electric field can be either in-plane

or out-of-plane

|
I
|

across the thickness of the heterostructure and total current

per unit length along the
\hat{\bf y}
-direction.
From the spatial profile of the electric fields in Fig.
2
(a) and (b), the voltage is only contributed by the out-of-plane
x

V=\int{\rm d}xE_{x}=2E_{0x}d_{F}

follows Ampère’s circuital Law, for which we select an integration route traversing the insulator in the

-

\hat{\bf y}
-direction is set to the unit length and extends to infinite along the
\hat{\bf x}

The dynamics of the voltage and current are not independent but interplay with each other by the electromagnetic dynamics.
An integration over Maxwell’s equation
\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t
leads to

\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}{\rm d}x\partial_{z}E_{x}=-\mu_{0}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}{\rm d}x\dot{H}_{y},

(24)

where from Eq. (
23

\int_{-\infty}^{-d_{F}}{\rm d}xH_{y}=\int_{d_{F}}^{+\infty}{\rm d}xH_{y}=H_{0y}\delta

24

\displaystyle 2\partial_{z}E_{0x}d_{F}=-2\mu_{0}\dot{H}_{0y}d_{F}(1+\delta/d_{F}).

(25)

Substituting
E_{0x}
and
H_{0y}
with
V
and
I
, Eq. (
25
) becomes

\partial_{z}V=-2\mu_{0}d_{F}(1+{\delta}/{d_{F}})\dot{I}.

(26)

Comparing Eq. (
26

\partial_{z}V=-L\dot{I}
, we find the effective inductance

\displaystyle L=2\mu_{0}d_{F}(1+\delta/d_{F}),

(27)

which is not purely real since
\delta
is a complex number. Additionally, substituting
E_{0x}
and
H_{0y}
with
V
and
I
to Maxwell’s equation
\partial_{z}H_{0y}=-\epsilon_{\rm I}\dot{E}_{0x}
yields
\partial_{z}I=-\epsilon_{\rm I}/(2d_{F})\dot{V}
. Comparing again with the circuit equation
\partial_{z}I=-C\dot{V}
, we find the effective capacitance

\displaystyle C=\epsilon_{\rm I}/(2d_{F}).

(28)

With the effective inductance (
27
) and capacitance (
28
), we are now allowed to express the Hamiltonian of NM
|
I
|
NM heterostructure to the effective one

\hat{H}=\int{\rm d}z~\frac{1}{2}(L\hat{I}^{2}+C\hat{V}^{2}).

(29)

In the wave-vector space,
{\bf k}=k\hat{\bf z}
, and

\hat{H}=\int{\rm d}k~\frac{1}{2}\left(L\hat{I}_{k}\hat{I}_{-k}+\frac{CL^{2}}{k^{2}}\dot{\hat{I}}_{k}\dot{\hat{I}}_{-k}\right),

(30)

where
\hat{I}_{k}(t)=\int{\rm d}z/\sqrt{2\pi}\hat{I}(z,t)e^{-ikz}
.

We then define the canonical conjugate variables:
\hat{q}_{k}=\hat{I}_{k}
represents the generalized coordinate and
\hat{\pi}_{k}=CL^{2}\dot{\hat{I}}_{k}/k^{2}
represents the canonical momenta. With them, we express the effective Hamiltonian (
30

\hat{H}=\int{\rm d}k\left(\frac{m_{k}}{2}\Omega_{n}^{2}\hat{q}_{k}\hat{q}_{-k}+\frac{1}{2m_{k}}\hat{\pi}_{k}\hat{\pi}_{-k}\right),

(31)

where
m_{k}=CL^{2}/k^{2}
.

We now define the annihilation and creation operators of photon modes as

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m_{k}\hbar\Omega_{n}}}(m_{k}\Omega_{n}\hat{q}_{k}+i\hat{\pi}_{-k}),

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{k}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m_{k}\hbar\Omega_{n}}}(m_{k}\Omega_{n}\hat{q}_{-k}+i\hat{\pi}_{k}),

(32)

which obey the bosonic commutation relation
[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{p}_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta(k-k^{\prime})
. Inversely,

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_{k}\Omega_{n}}}(\hat{p}_{k}+\hat{p}_{-k}^{\dagger}),

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{k}=i\sqrt{\frac{m_{k}\hbar\Omega_{n}}{2}}\left(\hat{p}_{k}^{\dagger}-\hat{p}_{-k}\right).

We note again in this quantization scheme that
\hat{q}_{k}
and
\hat{\pi}_{k}
are no longer Hermitian since the photon dispersion
\Omega_{n}
is not purely real due to the Ohmic dissipation.
This allows us to express the quantized magnetic field
\hat{\bf H}
regarding the photon creation and annihilation operators, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\bf H}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}_{y}\hat{\bf y}=\int\frac{{\rm d}k}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\hat{q}_{k}e^{ikz}\hat{\bf y}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}k}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_{k}\Omega_{n}}}\left(\hat{p}_{k}e^{ikz}+\hat{p}_{k}^{\dagger}e^{-ikz}\right)\hat{\bf y}.

(33)

For the waves propagating with arbitrary wave vector
{\bf k}
, we express their magnetic field operators by extending Eq. (
33
) as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{y}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}~\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_{k}\Omega_{n}}}\cos\theta_{\bf k}\left(e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{z}

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}~\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_{k}\Omega_{n}}}\sin\theta_{\bf k}\left(e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\right).

(34)

In terms of the magnetization operator
\hat{\bf M}
(
18
) and the magnetic-field operator
\hat{\bf H}
(
34
),
the Zeeman interaction describes the interaction between the magnon and photon

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{0}\int d{\bf r}\hat{\bf M}\cdot\hat{\bf H}=-\mu_{0}\int{\rm d}{\bf r}\hat{M}_{y}\hat{H}_{y}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bf k}~g_{\bf k}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}+\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}^{\dagger}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}\right),

where the coupling constant

g_{\bf k}/\hbar=\cos\theta_{\bf k}\frac{\hbar\gamma}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}{2m_{k}\Omega_{n}\omega_{N}d_{F}}}

(35)

strongly depends on the propagation direction of the collective modes. Crucially, the coupling

is dissipative
[
2

79
,
80
]
in that
\Omega_{n}
and

The total Hamiltonian of the system

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bf k}\left(\hbar\omega_{N}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{m}_{\bf k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\hbar\Omega_{n}\left(\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+g_{\bf k}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}+\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}^{\dagger}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}\right)\right)

The coalescence of the two modes is different from the level attraction phenomena

IV.1
Classical approach

As an extension of the above formalism, we consider the SC

|

Since
1/\lambda^{2}\gg k_{0}^{2}
when
\omega\lesssim 100
GHz,
{\cal B}_{k}\approx i/\lambda

|{\cal A}_{k}|\sim k

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{x}=-\dfrac{1-k_{0}^{2}d_{F}\lambda}{1-{\cal A}_{k}^{2}d_{F}\lambda}{\cal M}_{x}\approx-{\cal M}_{x},

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{y}=-\dfrac{d_{F}}{k_{0}^{2}\lambda+{\cal A}_{k}^{2}d_{F}}\left(k_{0}^{2}-\frac{d_{F}}{\lambda+d_{F}}k_{y}^{2}\right){\cal M}_{y}.

This implies that the out-of-plane

is continuous across the SC
|
FI interfaces. The SC then mainly modulates the in-plane magnetic field along the

On the other hand, the electric field inside the FI according to Eqs. (

\displaystyle{\tilde{E}}_{x}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega\mu_{0}k_{z}d_{F}}{{\cal A}_{k}^{2}d_{F}+k_{0}^{2}\lambda}{\cal M}_{y},

\displaystyle{\tilde{E}}_{y}

\displaystyle=-\omega\mu_{0}\lambda d_{F}k_{z}{\cal M}_{x}-i\frac{\lambda}{d_{F}+\lambda}\frac{\omega\mu_{0}d_{F}}{{\cal A}_{k}^{2}d_{F}+k_{0}^{2}\lambda}k_{y}k_{z}{\cal M}_{y}x,

\displaystyle{\tilde{E}}_{z}

\displaystyle=\omega\mu_{0}\lambda d_{F}k_{y}{\cal M}_{x}-i\omega\mu_{0}\dfrac{(k_{0}^{2}-\frac{d_{F}}{\lambda+d_{F}}k_{y}^{2})\lambda}{k_{0}^{2}\lambda+{\cal A}_{k}^{2}d_{F}}{\cal M}_{y}x.

Combining with the linearized Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation (

k^{2}+K_{\omega}\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}k_{y}^{2}=k_{0}^{2}\left(1+K_{\omega}+\frac{\lambda}{d_{F}}\right).

(43)

We note that replacing

\omega^{2}_{u(l)}({\bf k})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)+\omega_{S}^{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)-\omega_{S}^{2}\right)^{2}+4\Delta_{\bf k}^{2}}\right),

(44)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}(k)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{d_{F}}/({d_{F}+\lambda})}ck,

\displaystyle\omega_{S}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})(H_{0}+M_{s}d_{F}/(d_{F}+\lambda))},

(45)

are, respectively, the frequency of the Swihart mode
[

]
between two SCs (refer to Appendix

) and the “renormalized” FMR frequency by the SCs
[

,

]
, and

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}_{\bf k}=\cos^{2}\theta_{\bf k}\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)\mu_{0}^{2}\gamma^{2}M_{s}(H_{0}+M_{s})\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}

(46)

relates to the anisotropic coupling between the Swihart and Kittel modes. Here
c=1/{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm FI}}}
is the light velocity in the FI.

The maximal value of
\Delta_{\bf k}^{2}
appears when
\cos^{2}\theta_{\bf k}=1
, i.e., the modes propagating parallel to
{\bf M}_{s}
, resulting in the largest splitting. While when the collective modes propagate perpendicular to the external magnetic field or
{\bf M}_{s}
,
\cos^{2}\theta_{\bf k}=0
, the Swihart mode and Kittel mode decouples with

\omega_{u}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\omega_{S},~~~~~~k_{y}\leq k_{c}\\
&\Omega_{s}(k_{y}),~k_{y}>k_{c}\end{aligned}\right.,~\omega_{l}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Omega_{s}(k_{y}),~k_{y}\leq k_{c}\\
&\omega_{S},~~~~~~~k_{y}>k_{c}\end{aligned}\right.,

(47)

where
k_{c}=\sqrt{1+\lambda/d_{F}}\omega_{S}/c
is the crossing point of the two modes.
These features in the coupling between
the two modes can be understood from the Zeeman interaction
-\mu_{0}{M}_{y}{H}_{y}
.
When
\mathbf{k}
is perpendicular to
{\bf M}_{\mathrm{s}}
, substituting
\Omega_{s}
into Eq. (
41
) yields
H_{y}=0
. Consequently, the Zeeman interaction between the magnon and photon modes vanishes. On the other hand, when
\mathbf{k}
is parallel to
{\bf M}_{\mathrm{s}}
,
k_{y}=0
, and
H_{y}
reaches its maximum according to Eq. (
41
). As a result, the Zeeman interaction between the two modes is maximized in the bulk volume configuration.

When
k\rightarrow 0
, the frequencies of the two branches are reduced to

\displaystyle\omega_{u}

\displaystyle=\omega_{S},

\displaystyle\omega_{l}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}(d_{F}+\lambda)+M_{s}d_{F}\sin^{2}\theta_{\bf k}}{H_{0}(d_{F}+\lambda)+M_{s}d_{F}}\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}}ck\rightarrow 0.

(48)

Accordingly,
\omega_{u}(k\rightarrow 0)
is the shifted FMR
[

,
49
]
, while
\omega_{l}(k\rightarrow 0)
renders the Swihart photon modes anisotropic and reduces the group velocity.
On the other hand, when
k
is large, i.e.,

,

\displaystyle\omega_{u}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}(c^{2}k^{2}+\mu_{0}^{2}\gamma^{2}(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}\cos^{2}\theta_{\bf k})}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}}ck=\Omega_{s}(k),

\displaystyle\omega_{l}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})\left(H_{0}+\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}M_{s}\sin^{2}\theta_{\bf k}\right)}.

(49)

Thereby,
\omega_{u}
is switched to the Swihart mode, while
\omega_{l}
becomes the shifted FMR.

IV.2
Quantum approach

In the SC
|
FI
|
SC Josephson junction, the photon modes are well confined between two SC layers, acting as an ultrathin microwave waveguide without introducing dissipation. Such photon modes are described by the Swihart mode
[
46
]
.
In the long wavelength limit, similar to the NM case (
15
), the eigenfrequency of magnon is also given by Kittel’s formula
[
71
]

\omega_{S}=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+N_{xx}M_{s})(H_{0}+N_{yy}M_{s})},

(50)

but the demagnetization factors are renormalized
to be
N_{xx}=1
are
N_{yy}=d_{F}/(d_{F}+\lambda)
by the SCs, as shown explicitly in Appendix
A
. The interaction between magnon and Swihart photon is described by the Zeeman interaction

\hat{H}_{\rm int}=-\mu_{0}\int{\rm d}{\bf r}~\hat{\bf M}\cdot\hat{\bf H}_{\rm Sw},

where
\hat{\bf M}
is the magnetization operator for the magnon mode and
\hat{\bf H}_{\rm Sw}
is the magnetic-field operator for the Swihart mode.

Combining the Landau-Lifshitz equation and normalization relation
\int d\mathbf{r}\left({M}_{x}{M}_{y}^{*}-{M}_{x}^{*}{M}_{y}\right)=-i/2

[
72
,
73
]
, we find the normalized amplitudes

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{x}=i\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{S}}{8d_{F}\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}+M_{s})}},

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{y}=-\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}+M_{s})}{8d_{F}\omega_{S}}}.

The magnetization is then quantized as

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{x}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}\left(-i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar M_{s}\omega_{S}}{4d_{F}\mu_{0}(H_{0}+M_{s})}}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{y}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\mu_{0}\gamma^{2}(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}{4d_{F}\omega_{S}}}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{m}_{\bf k}+{\rm H.c.}\right).

(51)

According to Appendix
B
, the magnetic field is quantized according to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{{\rm Sw},y}

\displaystyle\approx\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}~\left(\frac{\Omega_{s}}{2k}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm FI}\hbar\Omega_{s}}{d_{F}}}\cos\theta_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{{\rm Sw},z}

\displaystyle\approx\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{2\pi}~\left(-\frac{\Omega_{s}}{2k}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\rm FI}\hbar\Omega_{s}}{d_{F}}}\sin\theta_{\bf k}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

(52)

where
\hat{p}_{\bf k}
is the annihilation operator of Swihart photons.
These quantizations lead to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{0}\int d{\bf r}\hat{M}_{y}\hat{H}_{{\rm Sw},y}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bf k}~g_{\bf k}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}+\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}^{\dagger}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}\right),

where the coupling constant

g_{\bf k}/\hbar=\cos\theta_{\bf k}\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma\Omega_{s}}{2|{\bf k}|}\sqrt{\frac{(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}\Omega_{s}\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm FI}}{\omega_{S}}}

(53)

strongly depends on the propagation direction of the collective modes.
The total Hamiltonian of the system reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bf k}~\left(\hbar\omega_{S}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{m}_{\bf k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\hbar\Omega_{s}(k)\left(\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+g_{\bf k}\left(\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}+\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf k}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}^{\dagger}-\hat{m}_{\bf k}\hat{p}_{\bf-k}\right)\right),

(54)

which goes beyond the perturbation theory.

After the Bogoliubov transformation on the Hamiltonian (
54
), the eigenfrequency of the collective modes is governed by the characteristic equation

\omega^{4}({\bf k})-\omega^{2}\left(\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)+\omega_{S}^{2}\right)+\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)\omega_{S}^{2}-4\Omega_{s}(k)\omega_{S}{g_{\bf k}^{2}}/{\hbar^{2}}=0.

(55)

Substitution of
\Omega_{s}(k)
(
45
) and
g_{\bf k}
(
53
) into Eq. (
55
) yields

\displaystyle\omega^{4}-\omega^{2}\left(\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}\frac{k^{2}}{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm FI}}+\omega_{S}^{2}\right)+\omega_{S}^{2}\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}\frac{k^{2}}{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm FI}}

\displaystyle-\mu_{0}^{2}\gamma^{2}(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}\left(\frac{d_{F}}{d_{F}+\lambda}\right)^{2}\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm FI}}=0,

(56)

which is exactly the same as Eq. (
44
) derived by the classical approach.

The solution of Eq. (
55
) is

\omega_{u(l)}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)+\omega_{S}^{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\Omega_{s}^{2}(k)-\omega_{S}^{2}\right)^{2}+16\Omega_{s}(k)\omega_{S}\frac{g_{\bf k}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\right).

(57)

When
\Omega_{s}(k)=\omega_{S}
, the magnon and Swihart modes cross with each other, and the solutions at the crossing point
\omega^{*}_{u(l)}=\sqrt{\omega_{S}^{2}\pm 2\omega_{S}g_{\bf k}/\hbar}
defines the anti-crossing gap as

\displaystyle\Delta\omega

\displaystyle=\omega^{*}_{u}-\omega^{*}_{l}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\omega_{S}^{2}+2\omega_{S}g_{\bf k}/\hbar}-\sqrt{\omega_{S}^{2}-2\omega_{S}g_{\bf k}/\hbar}.

(58)

When
g_{\bf k}/\hbar\ll\omega_{S}
,
\Delta\omega\approx 2g_{\bf k}/\hbar
is the same as the solution obtained by the perturbation theory
[
12
]
, which, however, breaks down when
g_{\bf k}/\hbar
is comparable to the bare magnon frequency
\omega_{S}
. We also note that the stable solution only exists when
2g_{\bf k}/\hbar<\omega_{S}
. Because
2g_{\bf k}/\hbar=\cos\theta_{\bf k}\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}d_{F}/(d_{F}+\lambda)}<\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})(H_{0}+M_{s}d_{F}/(d_{F}+\lambda))}=\omega_{S}
, we confirm that the solution of (
56
) is always stable.

The gap
\Delta\omega
approaches a constant when
d_{F}\gg\lambda
. In this limit the frequency of the Swihart mode
\Omega_{s}\rightarrow c|{\bf k}|
, the “renormalized” Kittel frequency
\omega_{S}\rightarrow\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}+M_{s})
, and the coupling constant
g_{\bf k}/\hbar\rightarrow\cos\theta_{\bf k}\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}/2
. Accordingly,

\displaystyle\Delta\omega|_{d_{F}\gg\lambda}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})+\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})-\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s})M_{s}}}\right)

(59)

is free of geometry parameters.

V
Discussion and conclusion

In conclusion, we develop a non-perturbation theory using both classical and quantum approaches to investigate the collective magnon-photon modes in the ferromagnetic heterostructures composed of ferromagnetic insulators sandwiched by either normal metals or superconductors, thereby representing either dissipative or non-dissipative states of electrons. We develop a non-Hermitian quantization scheme with non-Hermitian magnetization and magnetic/electric-field operators to account for the Ohmic dissipation, due to which the coupling between magnon and photon is dissipative. A simple conversion relation between the two electronic states is revealed by finding most formalisms with normal metals can be extended to those with superconductors by simply replacing the skin depth
\delta
with London’s penetration depth
\lambda
, although the former is non-Hermitian and the latter is Hermitian. Our formalism is “phenomenological” in the sense that the thermal effect and interactions between magnons and other quasiparticles are accounted for by the phenomenological saturation magnetization, Gilbert damping, penetration depth, and electric conductivity that may depend on the thermal fluctuation and various interactions in different materials, which we take typical values from the experiments.

In the NM
|
FI
|
NM heterostructure, we predict the persistent realization of non-Hermitian nodal magnon-photon polariton of long wavelength, where the coupling strength is comparable to the bare magnon frequency.
Remarkably, the exceptional points at which the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are coalescent persist over a wide range of wave vectors when the ferromagnetic layer is sufficiently thick
\sim 100
nm, without the need for fine-tuning of parameters. The dissipationless nature of the superconductors eliminates the eddy-current-induced damping present in the normal metal case. In the SC
|
FI
|
SC Josephson junction, we find that magnon-photon coupling exhibits strong anisotropy. The coupling is ultrastrong when the electromagnetic field propagates along the direction of the saturation magnetization but vanishes when they are normal to each other due to the chirality of the dipolar stray field.

Exceptional points hold the potential application in the enhancement of sensitivity in magnonic devices, as recently observed in the enhanced magnonic frequency combs
[
55
]
. Our results suggest that the common ferromagnetic heterostructures can host persistent EPs and anisotropic ultrastrong magnon-photon coupling, which may enable improved sensitivity and performance of the magnonic devices
[
61
,
62
]
. The predicted persistent nodal polariton and strongly anisotropic coupling provide new opportunities to explore the synergies between magnonics and superconducting electronics
[
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
,
5
,
67
,
88
,
89
,
90
]
.

Appendix B
Swihart mode and its quantization

In this appendix, we first review the electromagnetic modes in the SC
|
I
|
SC heterostructure. The thickness of the middle non-magnetic insulator is
2d_{I}
. Swihart found that the superconductor strongly modulates the distribution of the electromagnetic field, significantly slowing down the speed of electromagnetic waves, which is now known as the Swihart mode
[
46
]
.

According to Maxwell’s equations
[
70
]
, in different regions, the electric field obeys

\displaystyle\text{in I}:

\displaystyle~~~\nabla^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)+k^{2}_{0}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)=0,

\displaystyle\text{in SC}:

\displaystyle~~~\nabla^{2}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)+k^{2}_{s}\mathbf{E}({\bf r},t)=0,

(64)

where
k_{0}=\omega\sqrt{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm I}}
. Our system is isotropic in the
y
-
z
plane, so we consider the electromagnetic waves propagating along the
\bf\hat{z}
-direction with wavevector
k
without losing generality. We assume the formal solution
{\bf E}(x,z,t)=\tilde{\bf E}(x)e^{ikz-i\omega t}+{\rm H.c.}
, where the amplitudes

\displaystyle\text{in I}:~~{\tilde{\bf E}}(x)={\boldsymbol{\cal E}}_{0}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+{\boldsymbol{\cal E}}_{0}^{\prime}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\text{in SC~``1"}~(x>d_{I}):~~{\tilde{\bf E}}(x)={\boldsymbol{\cal E}}_{1}e^{i{\cal B}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\text{in SC~``2"}~(x<-d_{I}):~~{\tilde{\bf E}}(x)={\boldsymbol{\cal E}}_{2}e^{-i{\cal B}_{k}x},

where
{\cal A}_{k}=\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}
and
{\cal B}_{k}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}-1/\lambda^{2}-k^{2}}
.
In the non-magnetic insulator, the magnetic field is governed by Faraday’s Law, i.e.,
i\mu_{0}\omega{\bf H}({\bf r},t)=\nabla\times{\bf E}({\bf r},t)
, leading to

\displaystyle H_{x}=1/(i\omega\mu_{0})(\partial_{y}E_{z}-\partial_{z}E_{y}),

\displaystyle H_{y}=1/(i\omega\mu_{0})(\partial_{z}E_{x}-\partial_{x}E_{z}),

\displaystyle H_{z}=1/(i\omega\mu_{0})(\partial_{x}E_{y}-\partial_{y}E_{x}).

(65)

The boundary conditions are already described in the main text, with which we find the amplitudes
\{{\cal E}_{0x},{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime},{\cal E}_{0y},{\cal E}_{0y}^{\prime}\}
obey the matrix equation

\left(\begin{matrix}(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&0&0\\
(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&0&0\\
0&0&({\cal A}_{k}-{\cal B}_{k})e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&-({\cal A}_{k}+{\cal B}_{k})e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\\
0&0&({\cal A}_{k}+{\cal B}_{k})e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&-({\cal A}_{k}-{\cal B}_{k})e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}{\cal E}_{0x}\\
{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime}\\
{\cal E}_{0y}\\
{\cal E}_{0y}^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right)=0.

(66)

This implies that the
x
- and
y
-components of the electric fields decouple, forming two distinct modes.
The non-zero solutions require the determinant of the coefficient matrix to equal zero.

The first mode is the TE mode with
E_{x}=E_{z}=H_{y}=0
. It has three components
\{H_{z},H_{x},E_{y}\}
. From the secular equation

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}({\cal A}_{k}-{\cal B}_{k})e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&-({\cal A}_{k}+{\cal B}_{k})e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\\
({\cal A}_{k}+{\cal B}_{k})e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&-({\cal A}_{k}-{\cal B}_{k})e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\\
\end{matrix}\right|=0,

we find its dispersion relation

\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm I}}}\sqrt{k^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{d_{F}\lambda}\right)}.

(67)

which holds a large cut-off frequency and hence mismatches with the Kittel frequency in the magnetic film.

The second one is the TM mode with
E_{y}=H_{x}=H_{z}=0
. This mode has three components
\{E_{z},E_{x},H_{y}\}
. From the secular equation

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\\
(R_{k}+1)e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}&(R_{k}-1)e^{i{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}\end{matrix}\right|=0,

we find the Swihart-mode frequency
[
46
]

\Omega_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{d_{I}}{d_{I}+\lambda}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}\epsilon_{\rm I}}}|{\bf k}|.

(68)

Next, we quantize the Swihart mode. The Hamiltonian associated with the Swihart mode of the SC
|
I
|
SC Josephson junction includes the energy of electromagnetic field as well as the supercurrents
[
91
]
:

\hat{H}_{\rm Sw}=\int{\rm d}{\bf r}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{r}}{2}\hat{\bf E}^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\hat{\bf H}^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\lambda^{2}\hat{\bf J}_{\rm s}^{2}\right),

(69)

where
{\bf J}_{\rm s}
is the super-current flowing in the superconductor. We expand the electromagnetic field and supercurrent in terms of the photon operators
\hat{p}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\bf E}

\displaystyle=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{{\rm d}k}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\tilde{\bf E}_{k}(x)e^{ikz}\hat{p}_{k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\bf H}

\displaystyle=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{{\rm d}k}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\tilde{\bf H}_{k}(x)e^{ikz}\hat{p}_{k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\bf J}_{s}

\displaystyle=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{{\rm d}k}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\tilde{\bf J}_{s,k}(x)e^{ikz}\hat{p}_{k}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

(70)

where
\tilde{\bf E}_{k}(x)
,
\tilde{\bf H}_{k}(x)
, and
\tilde{\bf J}_{s,k}(x)
are the amplitudes. The photon creation and annihilation operators obey the commutation relation
[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{p}^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}}]=i\hbar\delta(k-k^{\prime})

When
-d_{I}<x<d_{I}
, the electromagnetic field is derived from Eq. (
64
) and the boundary conditions as

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{x,k}(x)

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{0x}e^{i{\cal A}_{k}x}+{\cal E}_{0x}^{\prime}e^{-i{\cal A}_{k}x},

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{s,k}(x)

\displaystyle=0.

(71)

They are incorporated into the electromagnetic field at
x>d_{I}
and
x<-d_{I}
after applying the continuity conditions for the
E_{z}
and
H_{y}
at the interfaces:

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{x,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{\rm I}}{\epsilon_{0}+i\sigma_{s}/\omega}\tilde{E}_{x,k}(\pm d_{I}^{\mp})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{I})},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{z,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{z,k}(\pm d_{I})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{I})},

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{y,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}_{y,k}(\pm d_{I})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{I})},

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{s,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s}\tilde{E}_{z,k}(x)=\frac{i}{\omega\mu_{0}\lambda^{2}}\tilde{E}_{z,k}(\pm d_{I})e^{\pm i{\cal B}_{k}(x\mp d_{I})}.

Here with the dispersion of the Swihart mode (
68

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}=ik\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{d_{I}+\lambda}},

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k_{s}^{2}-k^{2}}=i\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{d_{I}+\lambda}k^{2}}.

When the wavelength of the photon is much larger than the scale of the system, i.e.,

{\sin 2{\cal A}_{k}d_{I}}/({2{\cal A}_{k}})\approx d_{I}

Then with relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x[{\epsilon_{r}}\left(\tilde{E}_{x,k}^{(*)}\tilde{E}_{x,-k}^{(*)}+\tilde{E}_{z,k}^{(*)}\tilde{E}_{z,-k}^{(*)}\right)

and by substitution of Eqs. (

Random approximation of convex bodies in Hausdorff metric
Joscha Prochno, Carsten Schütt, Mathias Sonnleitner *, Elisabeth M. Werner

Abstract
While there is extensive literature on approximation, deterministic as well as random, of general convex bodies K K in the symmetric difference metric, or other metrics arising from intrinsic volumes, very little is known for corresponding random results in the Hausdorff distance when the approximant K n K_{n} is given by the convex hull of n n independent random points chosen uniformly on the boundary or in the interior of K K . When K K is a polygon and the points are chosen on its boundary, we determine the exact limiting behavior of the expected Hausdorff distance between a polygon as n → ∞ n\to\infty . From this we derive the behavior of the asymptotic constant for a regular polygon in the number of vertices.

Introduction

A convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be approximated arbitrarily well by polytopes in the symmetric difference metric or the Hausdorff metric. This can be achieved in many ways. For instance,
by taking the convex hull of sufficiently many random points sampled inside the convex body
K
or on its boundary
\partial K
. In this paper we investigate how much a convex body
K
differs from the convex hull
K_{n}
of
n\in\mathbb{N}
points chosen at random from
K
. More specifically, we are interested in the order of magnitude of the following two quantities

\mathbb{P}(\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})\geq t),\quad t>0,\hskip 56.9055pt\mbox{and}\hskip 56.9055pt\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})],

where the points spanning the polytope
K_{n}
are chosen uniformly at random either from
K
or from the boundary of
K
, with respect to the normalized Hausdorff measure of appropriate dimension. We refer to the end of this section for the definitions of the relevant notions.

There is a vast amount of literature on these problems when the error of approximation is measured by the expectation of the symmetric difference metric
d_{S}
: for points chosen uniformly at random inside the body, A. Rényi and R. Sulanke studied this question in dimension
2

[
13
,
14
]
;
I. Bárány proved asymptotics involving the Gauß-Kronecker curvature for smooth convex bodies in arbitrary dimension
[
2
]
; C. Schütt generalized this to arbitrary convex bodies in
[
16
]
. In
[
3
]
, I. Bárány and Ch. Buchta determined the asymptotic behavior of the expected symmetric difference metric for convex polytopes in arbitrary dimensions.

More recently, M. Reitzner
[
11
]
and C. Schütt and E. M. Werner
[
17
]
proved asymptotics for
\mathbb{E}[d_{S}(K,K_{n})]
for random points chosen from the boundary of a sufficiently smooth convex body. In
[
12
]
these three authors extended these results to the case when the convex bodies are polytopes.

The extensive literature on approximation of general convex bodies, deterministic as well as random, mostly involves the symmetric difference metric, or other metrics coming from intrinsic volumes. For smooth convex bodies the asymptotic constants are often given as integrals of curvature(s) of
K
over its boundary and for polytopes combinatorial quantities appear. For more information, we refer to the survey
[
10
]
.
However, very little is known for corresponding results in the Hausdorff distance and, for the reader’s convenience, we provide a short overview.

S. Glasauer and R. Schneider
[
7
]
considered the case of a convex body with
C^{3}_{+}
-boundary, that is three times continuously differentiable boundary with everywhere positive curvature, and a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
with density
h>0
of class
C^{1}
on the boundary. For random points sampled according to this density they proved the asymptotics

\hskip-14.22636pt\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{\frac{2}{d-1}}\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(B_{2}^{d-1})}\max_{x\in\partial K}\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{K}(x)}}{h(x)}\right)^{\frac{2}{d-1}},

(1)

where
\kappa_{K}
is the Gauß-Kronecker curvature,
B_{2}^{d-1}
the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit ball and
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}
denotes convergence in probability as
n\to\infty
. The case
d=2
was already obtained by R. Schneider
[
15
]
.

L. Dümbgen and G. Walther
[
6
]
showed upper bounds for the Hausdorff distance. More precisely, they proved that if
K_{n}
is the convex hull of random points uniformly distributed on the boundary
\partial K
of an arbitrary convex body
K
, then with probability one

\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})\leq C\Big(\frac{\log n}{n}\Big)^{\alpha}

for some constant
C>0
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\alpha=\frac{1}{d-1}
. The exponent
\frac{1}{d-1}
can be improved to
\frac{2}{d-1}
if the body is smooth in the following sense: at each boundary point, there is a unique normal and this normal is Lipschitz-continuous on
\partial K
. The latter includes the case of
C^{3}_{+}
-boundary, see, e.g., G. Walther
[
18
, Thm. 1]
. In
[
6
]
, a result for points sampled uniformly inside
K
was proven, where
\alpha=\frac{1}{d}
in general and
\alpha=\frac{2}{d+1}
for smooth bodies.

A direct consequence of the convergence in (
1
) and the bounds in
[
6
]
is the following,
which we only state for the case of the uniform distribution on
\partial K
. We prove it in Section
2
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
K
be of class
C^{3}_{+}
and, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K_{n}
be the convex hull of
n
independent random points uniformly distributed on
\partial K
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{\frac{2}{d-1}}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})]=c_{K},

where
c_{K}\in(0,\infty)
is the limiting constant in (
1
) for
h\equiv\mathrm{vol}_{d-1}(\partial K)^{-1}
.

In the case when points are chosen independently and uniformly at random within a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we are only aware of exact asymptotic results in dimension two. However, for higher-dimensional smooth convex bodies there is work in progress due to P. Calka and J. Yukich. H. Bräker, T. Hsing and N.H. Bingham
[

obtained a formula for the limiting distribution function for smooth convex bodies
K

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Q

\mathbb{R}^{2}

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{M}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{n}\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\leq t)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1-p_{i}(t)),\qquad t>0,

\int_{\alpha_{i}-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}h_{i}(t,\alpha){\rm d}\alpha&\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\alpha_{i}<\pi,\end{array}\right.

(2)

with

h_{i}(t,\alpha)=\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\operatorname{vol}_{2}(Q)}\left(\tan\alpha+\tan(\alpha_{i}-\alpha)\right)\right)\frac{t^{2}}{2\operatorname{vol}_{2}(Q)}\tan^{2}\alpha.

Note that the limiting distribution is a product of distribution functions, one for each vertex. Below, we use tail bounds to show that the convergence in distribution extends also to convergence in expectation. Let us note that V.E. Brunel
[
5

provides concentration inequalities for random polytopes in Hausdorff distance, but an additional log-factor appears in the resulting bounds on the expectation.

The only result we could find in the literature on the behavior of

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})]\asymp n^{-1/d},

where

n

Proposition 2
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex polytope and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Q_{n}
be the convex hull of
n
points sampled independently and uniformly from the boundary of
Q
. Then

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]\asymp n^{-1/(d-1)}.

I. Bárány’s result gives the dependence on the number of chosen points but nothing is known about the precise asymptotic behavior.

In this paper we consider a polygon
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and determine the exact constants in
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]
as the number of points chosen on the boundary
\partial Q
tends to infinity. We will mainly follow the approach of
[
4
]
, which is based on Poisson processes, and provide the necessary arguments to achieve convergence in expectation.

Corollary 3
.

If
Q

are as in Theorem

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{M}\geq\pi/2

In the case when all angles are equal,
Q

Corollary 4
.

Let
Q_{M}^{\rm reg}
be a regular polygon with
M\geq 4
vertices and
Q_{M,n}
the convex hull of
n
points independently and uniformly sampled from the boundary of
Q_{M}^{\rm reg}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q_{M}^{\rm reg},Q_{M,n})]\asymp\left|\partial Q_{M}^{\rm reg}\right|\,\frac{\log M}{M}.

Proof.

Let
\beta
be the exterior angle of
Q_{M}^{\rm reg}
. Then
\beta=\frac{2\pi}{M}
and thus
\alpha=\pi-\beta=\pi(1-\frac{2}{M})\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
, as
M\geq 4
. Hence we
can apply Corollary
3
.
Corollary

yields for a regular

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]
is up to absolute constants equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots i_{k}\leq M}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{k}}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}{M\choose k}\frac{\sin\alpha_{M}}{k},

where

\sin(\alpha_{M})=\sin(\pi(1-2/M))\asymp\frac{2\pi}{M}.

By formula
[

\sum_{k=1}^{M}\binom{M}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{1}{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{1}{k}\asymp\log M,

which completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with a remark on the log-factor in Corollary
4
and a remark on higher moments.

Remark 1
.

The logarithm appearing in Corollary
4
can be heuristically derived from the asymptotic independence of the random variables

Z_{i}:=n\,\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},K_{n}),\quad i\in\{1,\dots,M\},

which is crucial in the proof of Theorem
1
below. Assuming that these random variables are in fact independent, we can use Lemma
5
below to write

n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q_{M}^{\rm reg},Q_{M,n})]=\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq i\leq M}Z_{i}\big]

as a maximum of independent random variables. Bounds appearing in the proof of Corollary
3
allow us to conclude that the tails
\mathbb{P}(M\cdot Z_{i}\geq r)
decay exponentially in
r
. This yields that
M\mathbb{E}\big(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}Z_{i}\big)
is of order
\log M
, which is the statement of Corollary
4
. To give an idea for this implication, note that by a substitution we have

\displaystyle M\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{1\leq i\leq M}Z_{i}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}Z_{i}\geq r/M\Big){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=(\log M)\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}\big(1-\mathbb{P}(M\cdot Z_{i}\geq x\log M)\big){\rm d}x,

and we can insert the tail bounds to derive the stated asymptotics.

Remark 2
.

We can also establish asymptotics for arbitrary moments. Let
\gamma>0
be a real number. Modifying the first steps of the proof of Theorem
1
and using that

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})^{\gamma}]=\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma-1}\mathbb{P}(\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\geq r){\rm d}r

will give, with the notation of Theorem

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\gamma}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})^{\gamma}]=\gamma\,\left|\partial\,Q\right|^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma-1}\Bigg(1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}\Big(1-r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y\Big)\Bigg){\rm d}r.

Notation.
The Euclidean ball with center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and radius
\rho>0
is denoted by
B_{2}^{d}(x,\rho)
. We write in short
B_{2}^{d}:=B_{2}^{d}(0,1)
for the unit ball. For a set
A
, its
d

\mathrm{vol}_{d}(A)
or
|A|
, its diameter is
\mathrm{diam}(A)
. The distance of the set
A
to another set
B
is

\mathrm{dist}(A,B)=\inf\{\|x-y\|:x\in A,y\in B\}
. In particular,
\mathrm{dist}(x,K)=\inf_{y\in K}\|x-y\|
.

The symmetric difference metric between two convex bodies
C
and
K
, i.e., compact, convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with nonempty interior, is

d_{S}(C,K)=\operatorname{vol}_{d}(C\setminus K)+\operatorname{vol}_{d}(K\setminus C)

and the Hausdorff metric between two convex bodies
C
and
K
is

\delta_{H}(C,K)=\inf\{\rho\geq 0\colon C\subseteq K+B_{2}^{d}(0,\rho)\hskip 2.84526pt\mbox{and}\hskip 2.84526ptK\subseteq C+B_{2}^{d}(0,\rho)\},

where
C+K=\{x+y\colon x\in C\hskip 2.84526pt\mbox{and}\hskip 2.84526pty\in K\}
is their Minkowski sum. The convex hull of points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a convex polytope denoted by
[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

To do so, we need additional lemmas.

An important observation is the fact that for a convex polytope the Hausdorff distance to a convex subset is attained at the vertices and asymptotically, as
n\to\infty
, “everything happens at the vertices”.

Lemma 5
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex polytope with vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{M}
. If
K\subset Q
is a convex, nonempty subset, then

\delta_{H}(Q,K)=\max_{1\leq i\leq M}\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},K).

This lemma is known, see, e.g.,
[
5
, Lem. 2]
, and follows from the fact that the convex continuous function
\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,K)
attains its maximum on
Q
at a vertex.

The following lemma will be useful for geometric computations.

Lemma 6
.

Let
D
be a triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with sides of lengths
a,b,c
and angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
between the sides
b
and
c
. Then the height
h
with respect to the side
a
equals

h=\frac{bc\sin\alpha}{\sqrt{b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos\alpha}}.

(6)

Proof.

Formula (
6
) follows from elementary trigonometry. The area
|D|
of
D
equals

\frac{1}{2}a\cdot h=|D|=\frac{1}{2}bc\sin\alpha.

Using the law of cosine,
a^{2}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos\alpha
, we obtain

h=\frac{bc}{a}\sin\alpha=\frac{bc\sin\alpha}{\sqrt{b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos\alpha}},

which proves the desired formula.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma

we deduce the function

in Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

After a possible rescaling we can assume that
|\partial Q|=1
. We adapt the proof of

in order to first prove the distributional convergence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\leq r)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1-q_{i}(r)),\quad r>0,

(8)

where

q_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad r\mapsto r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y,

with
\ell_{\alpha}
and
h(y,\alpha)
as in Theorem
1
. Recall that
Q_{n}
is the convex hull of
n
independently and uniformly distributed points

\partial Q
.

First, we show how (
8
) implies the convergence in expectation which is claimed in Theorem
1
. We have the tail bound (see also the proof of (
17
) of Proposition
2
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})>r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}(n\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})>r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}(\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}\cap B_{2}^{2}(v_{i},r/n)=\emptyset)

\displaystyle\leq Me^{-cr},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
c=2/|\partial Q|
. Thus, for
p>1
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]^{p}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\geq r^{1/p}){\rm d}r\leq M\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-cr^{1/p}}{\rm d}r<\infty,

i.e., the sequence
(n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly integrable and

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]=\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1-q_{i}(r)){\rm d}r.

This extends to arbitrary moments.

We show (
8

[
4

. We refer to
[
9
]
for more information on (Poisson) point processes. We begin with some preparation. Let

L=\frac{\min_{1\leq i\leq M}\|v_{i}-v_{i+1}\|_{2}}{2},

where we set

. Then the ball
B_{2}^{2}(v_{i},L),1\leq i\leq M,
only intersects the edges of
Q
which are incident to

.

For
1\leq i\leq M
, define the point process
\xi_{n,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{n(X_{j}-v_{i})\cap B_{2}^{2}(0,Ln)}
regarded as a random element in the space
\mathcal{N}_{i}
of locally finite counting measures on the boundary of a cone
C_{i}
with apex at the origin and angle
\alpha_{i}
. When
1\leq i\leq M
, we can write
C_{i}
as the convex hull of two rays spanned by unit vectors
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We endow
\mathcal{N}_{i}
,

As

, we have

(\xi_{n,1},\dots,\xi_{n,M})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm d}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{M}),

(9)

where
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{M}
are independent Poisson processes on
\partial C_{1},\dots,\partial C_{M}
, respectively, and intensity measure being Lebesgue measure. Thus, for every Borel subset
B\subset\partial C_{i}
, the distribution of the number of points
\xi_{i}(B)
of
\xi_{i}
in

is a Poisson random variable with parameter

, and for any choice of disjoint Borel sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\subset\partial C_{i}
the random variables
\xi_{i}(B_{1}),\dots,\xi_{i}(B_{m})

\xi_{j}(A_{1}),\dots,\xi_{j}(A_{m})
, whenever

.

For convenience of readers unfamiliar with Poisson processes, we provide a brief justification of (
9
). Choose bounded measurable sets
A_{i}\subset\partial C_{i}
and integers
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for each
i
. Then (
9
) is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n,1}(A_{1})=k_{1},\dots,\xi_{n,M}(A_{M})=k_{M})=\prod_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{i}(A_{i})=k_{i}),

(10)

where
\xi_{i}(A_{i})
is Poisson distributed with parameter
|A_{i}|
. For
n
large enough such that the sets
v_{i}+\frac{1}{n}A_{i},i=1,\dots,M,
are disjoint and part of the polygon
Q
which has
|\partial Q|=1
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{M}k_{i}<n
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\{j\colon X_{j}\in v_{1}+\frac{1}{n}A_{1}\}|=k_{1},\dots,|\{j\colon X_{j}\in v_{M}+\frac{1}{n}A_{M}\}|=k_{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{M}!(n-\sum_{i=1}^{M}k_{i})!}\Big(\frac{|A_{1}|}{n}\Big)^{k_{1}}\cdots\Big(\frac{|A_{M}|}{n}\Big)^{k_{M}}\Big(1-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M}|A_{i}|}{n}\Big)^{n-\sum_{i=1}^{M}k_{i}}.

Taking the limit, we arrive at

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n,1}(A_{1})=k_{1},\dots,\xi_{n,M}(A_{M})=k_{M})=\frac{1}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{M}!}|A_{1}|^{k_{1}}\cdots|A_{M}|^{k_{M}}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{M}|A_{i}|}.

This proves (
10
), and thus (
9
).

We continue the proof of Theorem
1
. By Lemma
5
we have

n\,\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})=\max_{1\leq i\leq M}n\,\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n}).

(11)

Provided that for all
1\leq i\leq M
the point process
\xi_{n,i}
has nonempty intersection with both rays forming
\partial C_{i}
, we have

n\,\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})=\max_{1\leq i\leq M}n\,\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})=\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{n,i}),

where
f_{i}
continuously maps any locally finite counting measure
\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
to the smallest distance between the origin and the convex hull of the points of
\eta
. In fact, the probability that for some
1\leq i\leq M
the process
\xi_{n,i}
does not intersect one of the rays forming
\partial C_{i}
is given by

\mathbb{P}(\exists i\colon X_{j}\not\in[v_{i},v_{i}+La_{i}]\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,n\lor X_{j}\not\in[v_{i},v_{i}+Lb_{i}]\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,n),

which, by a union bound, is bounded by
2M(1-L)^{n}
as
|\partial Q|=1
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}(n\,\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\neq\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{n,i}))\leq 2M(1-L)^{n}\to 0.

(12)

By the continuous mapping theorem it follows from (
9
) that

\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{n,i})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm d}\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{i}).

Combined with
11
, we get

n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm d}\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{i}).

If we can show that, for
i=1,\dots,M
,

\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r)=q_{i}(r),\quad r>0,

(13)

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(n\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\leq r)=\mathbb{P}(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}f_{i}(\xi_{i})\leq r)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1-q_{i}(r)),

i.e., (
8
) and thus the conclusion holds.

We show (
13
) for
1\leq i\leq M
and
r>0
. Define the random variable
T_{i}=\inf\{y>0\colon\xi_{i}([0,ya_{i}])\neq 0\}
, i.e., the distance to the origin of the closest point to the origin belonging to
\xi_{i}
and being on one ray of
\partial C_{i}
.

Observe that for any
R>0
, by the law of total probability and Lemma
7
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r,T_{i}\leq R)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r|T_{i}=y){\rm d}\mathbb{P}(T_{i}\leq y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}r}^{R}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{i}([0,rh(y/r,\alpha_{i})a_{i}])=0){\rm d}\mathbb{P}(T_{i}\leq y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}r}^{R}e^{-rh(y/r,\alpha_{i})-y}{\rm d}y,

since
\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r|T_{i}=y)
is the probability that the points of
\xi_{i}
can be separated from the origin by a line passing through
ya_{i}
and having distance
r
from the origin, conditional on the fact that one point lies in
ya_{i}
. Further, note that the distribution of
T_{i}
has Lebesgue density

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{P}(T_{i}\leq y)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{i}([0,ya_{i}])\neq 0)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(1-\mathbb{P}(\xi_{i}([0,ya_{i}])=0))=e^{-y},\quad y>0.

Letting
R\to\infty
gives

\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(f_{i}(\xi_{i})>r,T_{i}\leq R)=\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}r}^{\infty}e^{-rh(y/r,\alpha_{i})-y}{\rm d}y=q_{i}(r).

This completes the proof.

∎

Remark 3
.

Note that a similar approach as in the proof of Theorem
1
can be used to show the convergence in expectation

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]=\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1-p_{i}(r)){\rm d}r,

where
Q_{n}
is the convex hull of
n
independent random points distributed uniformly inside
Q
and
p_{i}
is as in (
2
).

Proof of Corollary
3
.

With notation as in Theorem
1
, it holds by (
3
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]=\left|\partial\,Q\right|\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}\Big(1-r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y\Big){\rm d}r.

As by assumption
\alpha_{i}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
for all
i
, we have that
\ell_{\alpha_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq M
. Since the function
h\geq 0
, for all
1\leq i\leq M
,

r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y\leq r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r\cdot y}{\rm d}y=e^{-r\cdot\ell_{\alpha_{i}}},

and hence

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{M}\Big(1-r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y\Big)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{M}\big(1-e^{-r\cdot\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}\big)

\displaystyle=1-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\exp(-r(\ell_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\ell_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}))\right).

Since
\ell_{\alpha_{i}}=\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,M
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]

\displaystyle\leq\left|\partial\,Q\right|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\exp(-r(\ell_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\ell_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}))\right){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\left|\partial\,Q\right|\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\frac{1}{\ell_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\ell_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}}

\displaystyle=\left|\partial\,Q\right|\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{k}}}}.

Now we treat the inverse inequality. For
y\geq\frac{2}{\sin\alpha}
we have, for any
\alpha\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
,

\frac{y^{2}}{2}\sin^{2}\alpha\leq y^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha-1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle h(y,\alpha)=y\frac{\sin(\alpha)\sqrt{y^{2}-1}-\cos(\alpha)}{y^{2}\sin^{2}(\alpha)-1}\leq y\frac{\sin(\alpha)y-\cos\alpha}{y^{2}\sin^{2}(\alpha)-1}\leq\frac{2}{\sin\alpha}-\frac{2\cos\alpha}{y\sin^{2}\alpha}\leq\frac{3}{\sin\alpha}.

It follows that

r\int_{\ell_{\alpha}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha))}{\rm d}y\geq r\int_{2\ell_{\alpha}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+\frac{3}{\sin\alpha})}{\rm d}y=re^{-r\frac{3}{\sin\alpha}}\int_{2\ell_{\alpha}}^{\infty}e^{-ry}{\rm d}y=e^{-r\frac{5}{\sin\alpha}}.

We complete the proof with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]=\left|\partial\,Q\right|\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}\big(1-r\int_{\ell_{\alpha_{i}}}^{\infty}e^{-r(y+h(y,\alpha_{i}))}{\rm d}y\big){\rm d}r

\displaystyle\geq\left|\partial\,Q\right|\int_{0}^{\infty}1-\prod_{i=1}^{M}\big(1-e^{-r\frac{5}{\sin\alpha_{i}}}\big){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\left|\partial\,Q\right|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\exp\left(-5r\left(\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{k}}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\left|\partial\,Q\right|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq M}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\sin\alpha_{i_{k}}}}\right).

∎

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Define for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the random variable

X_{n}=\Big(\frac{n}{\log n}\Big)^{2/(d-1)}\delta_{H}(K,K_{n}).

We deduce from tail bounds given in
[
6
]
that
X_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}c_{K}
implies
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]\to c_{K}
.

As
K
is
C^{3}_{+}
, there is in particular at each boundary point
x\in\partial K
a unique normal
N(x)
and there exists
\ell>0
such that

|N(x)-N(y)|\leq\ell|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\partial K
, see
[
6
]
.
Then, for
w>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>\ell\,w)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})>\ell\,w\Big(\frac{\log n}{n}\Big)^{2/(d-1)}\Big)=\mathbb{P}(\delta_{H}(K,K_{n})>\ell\delta_{n}^{2}),

where we set
\delta_{n}=\sqrt{w}\Big(\frac{\log n}{n}\Big)^{1/(d-1)}
. Thus, by
[
6
, Thm. 1(b)]
, we have

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>\ell\,w)\leq\mathbb{P}(\partial K\not\subset B(Z_{n},\delta_{n})),

where
Z_{n}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
and
B(Z_{n},\delta_{n})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{2}^{2}(X_{i},\delta_{n})
is a union of random Euclidean balls. From the proof of
[
6
, Lem. 1]
, we get

\mathbb{P}(\partial K\not\subset B(Z_{n},\delta_{n}))\leq(4R+\alpha)^{d}(\delta_{n}/2)^{-d}\exp(-\alpha n(\delta_{n}/2)^{d-1})

for some constants
R,\alpha>0
and both,
w
and
n
sufficiently large. This yields
c,C>0
independent of
w
and
n
such that

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\geq\ell\,w)\leq Cw^{-d/2}\Big(\frac{n}{\log n}\Big)^{d/(d-1)}\exp(-cw^{(d-1)/2}\log n).

(14)

Pick
S>\max\{c_{K},\ell\}
. Then

\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\geq w){\rm d}w=\int_{0}^{S}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\geq w){\rm d}w+\ell^{-1}\int_{S\,\ell^{-1}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>\ell\,w){\rm d}w.

(15)

Since
X_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}c_{K}
, by the Dominated Convergence theorem, the first summand satisfies

\int_{0}^{S}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\geq w){\rm d}w\to\int_{0}^{S}\mathbb{P}(c_{K}\geq w){\rm d}w=c_{K}.

Using (
14
), we will show that the second summand in (
15
) tends to zero. It satisfies

\ell^{-1}\int_{S\,\ell^{-1}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>\ell\,w){\rm d}w\leq C\Big(\frac{n}{\log n}\Big)^{d/(d-1)}\ell^{-1}\int_{S\,\ell^{-1}}^{\infty}w^{-d/2}\exp(-cw^{(d-1)/2}\log n){\rm d}w.

Since
L:=S\,\ell^{-1}>1
, we estimate the integral from above using
w^{-d/2}\leq 1
for
w\geq L
. Then it remains to bound, for
\alpha=\frac{d-1}{2}
and
c_{n}=c\log n
,

\int_{L}^{\infty}e^{-c_{n}w^{\alpha}}{\rm d}w=c_{n}^{-1/\alpha}\int_{c_{n}^{1/\alpha}L}^{\infty}e^{-w^{\alpha}}{\rm d}w.

The tail bound

\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-u^{\alpha}}{\rm d}u\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{u^{\alpha-1}}{t^{\alpha-1}}e^{-u^{\alpha}}{\rm d}u=\frac{e^{-t^{\alpha}}}{\alpha t^{\alpha-1}},\quad t>0,

gives

\int_{L}^{\infty}e^{-c_{n}w^{\alpha}}{\rm d}w\lesssim n^{-cL^{\alpha}},

which goes to zero faster than any fixed polynomial if
S
is chosen large enough. Thus, the second summand in (
15
) tends to zero and the proof is complete.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{M}
be the vertices of
Q
and
Q_{n}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are sampled independently at random from
\partial Q
. Then Lemma
5
implies

n^{1/(d-1)}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}\big){\rm d}r,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
. If
rn^{-1/(d-1)}\geq\mathrm{diam}(Q)
the integrand vanishes. Otherwise, a union bound gives

\mathbb{P}\big(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}\big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}).

For all
i=1,\dots,M
, if
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}
, then

B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},rn^{-1/(d-1)})\cap\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}=\emptyset.

Further, there exists a constant
c=c(Q)\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
i=1,\dots,M
and for all
\varrho\in(0,\mathrm{diam}(Q)]
,

\mathbb{P}(X_{1}\in B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},\varrho))\geq c\,\varrho^{d-1}.

(16)

Therefore, for all
i=1,\dots,M
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}\big)\leq(1-cr^{d-1}/n)^{n}\leq e^{-cr^{d-1}}.

(17)

Summing over
i=1,\dots,M
and integrating over
r\in(0,\infty)
gives an upper bound, independent of
n
, on the quantity
n^{1/(d-1)}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]
.

For the lower bound we note that for every
i=1,\dots,M
there exists
c_{i}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\varrho>0
and all point sets
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset\partial Q
, we have

B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},c_{i}\varrho)\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\emptyset\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])\geq\varrho.

(18)

Before we prove this relation, we use it to complete the proof of the lower bound. Let
C=\max_{1\leq i\leq M}c_{i}
and let
R>0
be small enough such that the balls
B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},C\,R),1\leq i\leq M,
are pairwise disjoint. Let
r\in(0,R]
. Then (
18
) implies that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},C\,r\,n^{-1/(d-1)})=\emptyset)

\displaystyle=(1-c\,C^{d-1}r^{d-1}/n)^{n},

which is at least
\frac{1}{2}
provided that
R
is small enough.
Then we have, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

n^{1/(d-1)}\mathbb{E}[\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})]\geq\int_{0}^{R}\mathbb{P}(\delta_{H}(Q,Q_{n})\geq rn^{-1/(d-1)}){\rm d}r\geq R/2.

This completes the lower bound since
R
depends only on
C
and thus only on
Q
.

It remains to show (
18
). Fix
1\leq i\leq M
and
\varrho>0
. We will find
c_{i}>0
such that for any point set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset\partial Q
with
B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},\varrho)\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\emptyset
, we have

\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])\geq c_{i}^{-1}\varrho.

The distance
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])
becomes minimal if
A=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\cap\partial B^{2}_{2}(v_{i},\varrho)
contains at least two points, say
x
and
y
. Then
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])
is equal to the minimal distance between
v_{i}
and the line connecting
x
and
y
. A brief calculation using Lemma
6
shows that, for any
x,y\in A
, this distance is equal to

\rho\,\frac{\sqrt{1+\cos(\alpha_{i})}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha_{i}=\angle(x,v_{i},y)\in(0,\pi),

(19)

thus concluding the proof.
∎

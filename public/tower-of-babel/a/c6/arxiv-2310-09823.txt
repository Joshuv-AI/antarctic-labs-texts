Abstract
. We consider the elliptic Ginibre matrices in the orthogonal symmetry class that interpolates between the real Ginibre ensemble and the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble. We obtain the finite size corrections of the real eigenvalue densities in both the global and edge scaling regimes, as well as in both the strong and weak non-Hermiticity regimes. Our results extend and provide the rate of convergence to the previous recent findings in the aforementioned limits. In particular, in the Hermitian limit, our results recover the finite size corrections of the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble established by Forrester, Frankel and Garoni.



1.
Introduction and main results

By their very nature, elliptic random matrices were introduced to interpolate between Hermitian and non-Hermitian random matrix theories, see
[
21
, Section 2.3]
and
[
22
, Sections 2.8 and 5.5]
for recent reviews.
One of the simplest ways to define the model in the orthogonal symmetry class is by starting with the real Ginibre matrix (denoted GinOE)
X
, an
N\times N
matrix whose elements are given by independent real Gaussian random variables with mean
0
and variance
1/N
.
As expected from its fundamental structure, the Ginibre matrix serves as a cornerstone in non-Hermitian random matrix theory.
To introduce the elliptic random matrix model, one usually makes use of a parameter
\tau\equiv\tau_{N}\in[0,1]
.
This parameter determines the Hermiticity of the model and may depend on the matrix dimension
N
.
Then the one-parameter generalisation of the GinOE, known as the elliptic GinOE, is defined by

(1.1)

X^{(\tau)}:=\sqrt{\frac{1+\tau}{2}}S_{+}+\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau}{2}}S_{-},\qquad S_{\pm}=\frac{X+X^{T}}{\sqrt{2}}.

Notice here that for
\tau=0,
the matrix
X^{(\tau)}
recovers the GinOE matrix, whereas for
\tau=1
, it recovers the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE), cf.
[
33
, Chapter 1]
.
Let us mention that the terminology
elliptic
stems from the fact that the eigenvalues of
X^{(\tau)}
tend to uniformly occupy the ellipse

(1.2)

\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\Big(\frac{x}{1+\tau}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{y}{1-\tau}\Big)^{2}\leq 1\Big\},

which is known as the elliptic law, see e.g.
[
67
,
7
,
17
]
.
We refer to
[
4
,
19
,
66
,
70
,
69
,
64
,
46
,
51
,
35
,
57
,
18
]
and references therein for recent work on the elliptic Ginibre ensembles.

Compared to their complex or quaternion counterparts, a characteristic feature of real random matrices is their non-trivial probability of having real eigenvalues. This arises because the characteristic polynomial of the real matrix model consists of real coefficients, resulting in eigenvalues that are either purely real or form complex conjugate pairs, see e.g.
[
68
,
5
,
1
,
32
,
62
,
75
,
65
,
39
,
76
,
85
,
54
,
28
]
for related models.
From a statistical physics point of view, this behaviour resembles that of two-species particle systems, such as a two-component plasma in the plane.
In particular, the statistics of real eigenvalues enjoy surprising connections to other fields, such as annihilating Brownian motions
[
84
,
53
]
and diffusion processes
[
34
,
73
]
, and also find applications for instance in the context of the equilibrium counting
[
47
]
, so-called mermaid states in certain topologically protected quantum dots
[
11
]
.

In this work, we shall study real eigenvalues of the elliptic GinOE focusing on finite size effects in scaling limits.
This in turn relates to the question of the speed of convergence to universal laws exhibited by the elliptic GinOE real eigenvalues.
For this purpose, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}
the (random) number of real eigenvalues.
The basic statistical information of real eigenvalues is encoded in the
1
-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which is defined by its characterizing property: for a test function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(1.3)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\tau}}f(x_{j})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\textbf{R}_{N}(x)\,dx.

In particular, by letting
f\equiv 1
, it gives rise to the expected number of real eigenvalues

(1.4)

E_{N,\tau}:=\mathbb{E}\mathcal{N}_{\tau}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)\,dx.

Then the normalised density
\rho_{N}\equiv\rho_{N,\tau}
of real eigenvalues is defined by

(1.5)

\rho_{N}(x):=\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau}}\mathbf{R}_{N}(x).

In the study of the large-
N
asymptotics of the elliptic Ginibre matrices, the following two distinct regimes emerge.

•

Strong non-Hermiticity
. Here,
\tau
is fixed in the interval
[0,1)
. In particular if
\tau=0
, it coincides with the GinOE.

•

Weak non-Hermiticity
. Here,
\tau\uparrow 1
with a proper speed. In this regime, one again needs to distinguish two different scales: for a fixed parameter
\alpha\in[0,\infty),

–

Bulk scaling
:

(1.6)

\tau\equiv\tau_{N}=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{N},

–

Edge scaling
:

(1.7)

\tau\equiv\tau_{N}=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{N^{1/3}}.

In particular, if
\tau=1
(i.e.
\alpha=0
), it corresponds to the GOE.

The weakly non-Hermitian regime was introduced by Fyodorov, Khoruzhenko and Sommers
[
48
,
50
,
49
]
, where they studied the critical transition of the elliptic GinUE statistics in the bulk scaling (
1.6
).
On the one hand, the edge scaling (
1.7
) has been employed in
[
12
,
6
,
20
]
to derive non-Hermitian extensions of the Airy point processes.
We also refer to
[
3
,
8
,
16
,
24
]
and references therein for further recent work on the elliptic Ginibre ensembles at weak non-Hermiticity.

Turning back to the real eigenvalue statistics, the recent work
[
23
]
obtained the full asymptotic expansion of the expected number
E_{N,\tau}
of real eigenvalues.
From now on, we shall focus on the case where the matrix dimension
N
is even. The odd
N
case should be treated separately, as the analysis is usually more involved, see e.g.
[
77
,
38
]
.

In the strongly non-Hermitian regime where
\tau\in[0,1)
is fixed, it was established in
[
23
, Proposition 2.1]
that

(1.8)

E_{N,\tau}=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

as
N\to\infty
.
The leading order asymptotic behaviour of (
1.8
) for the GinOE case (
\tau=0
) was first obtained in the celebrated work
[
30
]
of Edelman, Kostlan and Shub.
For general
\tau\in[0,1)
fixed, this leading order behaviour was then later derived by Forrester and Nagao
[
43
]
.
From the formula (
1.8
), one can observe that
E_{N,\tau}
is an increasing function of
\tau
.
This behaviour is intuitively clear, as increasing
\tau
brings the model closer to a symmetric matrix.
Let us also mention that the behaviour (
1.8
) indeed holds as long as
1-\tau\gg 1/N
.
We stress that while the leading order asymptotic
O(\sqrt{N})
term in (
1.8
) depends on
\tau
, the subleading order
1/2
does not depend on
\tau
.
This fact is closely related to the universal edge scaling limit, cf. Remark
1.6
.

The asymptotic behaviour of
E_{N,\tau}
in the bulk scaling weak non-Hermiticity regime (
1.6
) exhibits different and seemingly more complicated behaviour.
In this case, it was shown in
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
that

(1.9)

E_{N,\tau}=Nc(\alpha)+c_{0}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}+O(N^{-1}),

as
N\to\infty
, where

(1.10)

\displaystyle c(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})+I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})],

(1.11)

\displaystyle c_{0}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})+\alpha^{2}I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})].

Here,
I_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind,
see e.g.
[
71
, Chapter 10]
.

Unlike the precise asymptotic expansion of the expected number of real eigenvalues, for general
\tau\in[0,1]
, the asymptotic behaviour of the 1-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}
is available in the literature only for the leading order.
(See however Remarks
1.4
and
1.7
for the GinOE case
\tau=0
and the GOE case
\tau=1
respectively.)
Nevertheless, the precise asymptotic behaviour, often referred to as the
finite size correction
in random matrix theory, encompasses important statistical properties such as the counting statistics
[
9
,
26
,
25
,
10
,
2
,
29
,
80
,
79
]
, which also carries physical implications.
On the one hand, for the Hermitian case, there have been extensive works on the finite size corrections of eigenvalue distributions, see e.g.
[
72
,
13
,
36
,
52
,
44
,
86
,
37
,
45
]
and references therein.
Furthermore, these have applications in various contexts, including the statistics of the critical zeros of the Riemann zeta function
[
40
]
and the distribution of the longest increasing subsequence
[
41
,
14
]
.

In our first result, we obtain the finite size correction of the global eigenvalue densities for both strong and weak non-Hermiticities.

Theorem 1.1

(
Finite size correction of the
1
-point functions
)
.

Let
N
be an even integer. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have the following.

(i)

(Strong non-Hermiticity)
Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed. Then for any
x\in(-1-\tau,1+\tau)
, we have

(1.12)

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{\rm s}(x)N^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(e^{-\epsilon N}),\qquad\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{\rm s}(x):=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some
\epsilon>0.

(ii)

(Weak non-Hermiticity)
Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.
Then for any
x\in(-2,2)
, we have

(1.13)

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{\rm w}(x)\,N+\mathbf{R}_{(1)}^{\rm w}(x)+O(N^{-1}),

where

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{\rm w}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}),

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{(1)}^{\rm w}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\alpha}{8\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})-\frac{3\alpha^{2}x^{2}+4-4\alpha^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(x^{2}-4)}.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
together with (
1.5
), (
1.8
) and (
1.9
), we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2

(
Finite size correction of the normalised densities
)
.

Let
N
be an even integer. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have the following.

(i)

(Strong non-Hermiticity)
Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed. Then for any
x\in(-1-\tau,1+\tau)
, we have

(1.16)

\rho_{N}(x)=\rho_{(0)}^{\rm s}(x)+\rho_{(1)}^{\rm s}(x)\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1}),

where

(1.17)

\rho_{(0)}^{\rm s}(x):=\frac{1}{2(1+\tau)},\qquad\rho_{(1)}^{\rm s}(x):=-\Big(\frac{\pi(1-\tau)}{32(1+\tau)^{3}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(ii)

(Weak non-Hermiticity)
Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.
Then for any
x\in(-2,2)
, we have

(1.18)

\rho_{N}(x)=\rho_{(0)}^{\rm w}(x)+\rho_{(1)}^{\rm w}(x)\,N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}),

where

(1.19)

\displaystyle\rho_{(0)}^{\rm w}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}),

(1.20)

\displaystyle\rho_{(1)}^{\rm w}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{c(\alpha)^{2}}\frac{c(\alpha)\alpha^{2}-4c_{0}(\alpha)-2}{8\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})-\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{3\alpha^{2}x^{2}+4-4\alpha^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(x^{2}-4)}.

Here
c(\alpha)
and
c_{0}(\alpha)
are given by (
1.10
) and (
1.11
).

Remark 1.3

(
Leading order asymptotic of the global densities
)
.

We stress that the leading order of the density at strong non-Hermiticity (i.e. the
1/(2(1+\tau))
part in (
1.16
)) was obtained in
[
43
, Section 6.2]
.
Note that the uniform density is quite natural given the elliptic law (
1.2
).
We refer to
[
82
]
for the “square root” relation between the complex and real eigenvalue densities in a more general context.

On the other hand, the leading order of the density at weak non-Hermiticity (i.e. the
\rho_{(0)}
part of (
1.18
)) was shown in
[
23
, Theorem 2.8]
.
This formula was indeed predicted by Efetov
[
31
]
some time ago now, utilizing the supersymmetry method in the context of directed quantum chaos.
A notable feature of this one-parameter family of densities is that it interpolates the uniform density (cf. (
1.17
) with
\tau=1
) and the Wigner’s semi-circle law:

(1.21)

\rho_{(0)}^{\rm w}(x)\sim\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}&\textup{ as }\alpha\to\infty;\\
\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}&\textup{ as }\alpha\to 0.\end{cases}

Note that, by (
1.3
), these leading order asymptotic behaviours yield the leading order asymptotic behaviours of the expected number of real eigenvalues given in (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).
For the weakly non-Hermitian regime, one also needs the integral representation

(1.22)

c(\alpha)=\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha\sqrt{1-s^{2}})\,ds

of the function
c(\alpha)
in (
1.10
), see e.g.
[
23
,
24
]
.

Remark 1.4

(
Global density of the GinOE
)
.

For the GinOE case when
\tau=0
, it can be shown that the
1
-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}
satisfies the asymptotic behaviour

(1.23)

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)\Big|_{\tau=0}=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{(-1,1)}(x)+\frac{1}{4}\Big(\delta(x-1)+\delta(x+1)\Big)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

in the sense of distribution, see
[
22
, pp. 33–34]
.
In connection with the formula (
1.12
) for
\tau=0
, one can observe that there are no additional polynomial order contributions from the bulk of the spectrum.
The asymptotic formula (
1.23
) follows from the perfect screening of the charge density in the Coulomb gas picture of the GinOE as well as the edge scaling limit (
1.29
) below.
This in turn implies that the
O(1)
-term of the formula (
1.23
) remains valid for general value of
\tau\in[0,1)
at strong non-Hermiticity.
Note also that by (
1.3
), the expansion (
1.23
) indeed gives rise to the
1/2
in the
O(1)
-term of the expansion (
1.8
) for
\tau=0
.
This formula can also be used to derive the large-
N
expansion of the moment generating function, which satisfies a linear differential equation recently found in
[
18
, Corollary 1.5]
.
We also refer to
[
55
,
60
]
for the rate of convergence of the empirical measure of the GinOE.

Next, we investigate the edge scaling limits of the real eigenvalue densities.
It is worth recalling here that, due to the elliptic law (
1.2
), the edge of real eigenvalues is located at
\pm(1+\tau).

Furthermore, by the symmetry
x\mapsto-x
, it suffices to consider the right-most edge
1+\tau.

To describe the edge scaling limits at weak non-Hermiticity, it is convenient to use the notation

(1.24)

\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(x)\coloneqq e^{\frac{\alpha^{6}}{12}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}x}\operatorname{Ai}(x+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{4}),

where

(1.25)

\operatorname{Ai}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\Big(\frac{t^{3}}{3}+xt\Big)\,dt

is the Airy function, see e.g.
[
71
, Chapter 9]
.
Furthermore, we define the rescaling densities.

•

Strong non-Hermiticity
. For a fixed
\tau\in[0,1)
, define

(1.26)

R_{N}^{\rm s}(\xi):=\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}}\mathbf{R}_{N}\Big(1+\tau+\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}}\,\xi\Big).

•

Weak non-Hermiticity
. For
\tau_{N}=1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{N^{1/3}}
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, define

(1.27)

R_{N}^{\rm w}(\xi):=\frac{1}{N^{2/3}}\mathbf{R}_{N}\Big(1+\tau+\frac{\xi}{N^{2/3}}\Big).

We mention that the rescaling orders are chosen according to the typical eigenvalue spacings.
Then we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5

(
Finite size correction of the edge scaling densities
)
.

Let
N
be an even integer. Then we have the following.

(i)

(Strong non-Hermiticity)
Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(1.28)

R_{N}^{\rm s}(\xi)=R_{(0)}^{\rm s}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{\rm s}(\xi)\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1}),

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, where

(1.29)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{\rm s}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\Big(1-\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}\xi)+\frac{e^{-\xi^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\operatorname{erf}(\xi))\Big),

(1.30)

\displaystyle R_{(1)}^{\rm s}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}{12\pi(1-\tau)^{2}}e^{-2\xi^{2}}\Big((1+\tau)\xi^{2}-3\Big)\Big(1+e^{\xi^{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\xi\big(1+\operatorname{erf}(\xi)\big)\Big).

(ii)

(Weak non-Hermiticity)

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N^{\frac{1}{3}}
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
. Then as
N\to\infty,
we have

(1.31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N}^{\rm w}(\xi)=R_{(0)}^{\rm w}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{\rm w}(\xi)\,N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}),\end{split}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
for any
\epsilon>0
, where

(1.32)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{\rm w}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)\bigg(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\bigg),

(1.33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{(1)}^{\rm w}(\xi)&:=-\frac{\alpha^{6}+2\alpha^{2}\xi+2}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)^{2}+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\alpha^{4}t+\alpha^{2}}{2}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)\,t\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\bigg)\,dt\\
&\quad+\bigg(\frac{\alpha^{4}\xi+2\alpha^{2}}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\xi+\alpha^{6}+2}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)\bigg)\bigg(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\bigg).\end{split}

Remark 1.6

(
Edge scaling limits and universality
)
.

The leading order edge scaling limit (
1.29
) was initially derived for the GinOE case (
\tau=0
) in
[
42
,
15
]
and subsequently extended to encompass general values of
\tau\in[0,1)
in
[
43
]
.
It is worth noting that the function
R_{(0)}^{\text{ s }}
remains invariant regardless of the chosen
\tau
, illustrating the universality principle in random matrix theory. We refer the reader to
[
63
]
for a general review on the universality and also
[
27
,
58
]
and references therein for universality of non-Hermitian random matrices.
In contrast to the leading order, the sub-leading correction term
R_{(1)}^{\rm{s}}
does exhibit dependence on
\tau
, making it non-universal.
For the elliptic GinUE and GinSE models, the subleading corrections were obtained in
[
64
]
and
[
19
]
respectively. In those works, a similar dependence on
\tau
is observed.

For the weakly non-Hermitian regime, the leading order edge scaling limit (
1.32
) was obtained in
[
6
]
, where the authors introduced the non-Hermitian extension of the classical Airy point process.
In this case, the leading order already depends on
\tau
(or
\alpha
). One may interpret that in this case, the choice of
\alpha
determines the intrinsic geometric property—the curvature of the ellipse (
1.2
) that reveals a critical transition—and thus determines the universality class.

Remark 1.7

(
Finite size corrections of the GOE
)
.

Let us discuss some immediate consequences of Theorems
1.1
and
1.5
for the GOE case.
For the case
\tau=1
(i.e.
\alpha=0
), by Theorem
1.1
(ii) with
\alpha=0
, we have

(1.34)

\rho_{N}(x)\Big|_{\alpha=0}=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}\,N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}),

where we have used the asymptotic behaviour of the error function

\operatorname{erf}(x)\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,x,\qquad(x\to 0),

see e.g.
[
71
, Eq.(7.6.1)]
.
This coincides with the finite size correction of the GOE density towards the Wigner’s semi-circle law established in
[
36
, Proposition 5]
.
We also refer to
[
59
]
for the resolvent approach to derive (
1.34
).

For the edge scaling density, it follows from Theorem
1.5
(ii) with
\alpha=0
that

(1.35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N}(\xi)\Big|_{\alpha=0}&=R_{(0)}^{\rm w}(\xi)\Big|_{\alpha=0}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{d}{d\xi}R_{(0)}^{\rm w}(\xi)\Big|_{\alpha=0}\Big)N^{-1/3}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon})\\
&=\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(\xi)^{2}-\xi\,\operatorname{Ai}(\xi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Ai}(\xi)\bigg(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}(t)\,dt\bigg)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{4}\bigg[\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(\xi)\bigg(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}(t)\,dt\bigg)-\operatorname{Ai}(\xi)^{2}\bigg]N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}).\end{split}

The fact that the correction in (
1.35
) can be written as a derivative implies that there is a re-centering of
\xi
on the left hand side, specifically
\xi\mapsto\xi-1/(2N^{1/3})
, which
eliminates the leading correction on the right hand side.
This is in keeping with the optimal rate of convergence being
O(N^{-2/3})

for edge scaling of the GOE
[
61
]
.

We mention that in contrast to the above, the correction term in (
1.35
) does not entirely coincide with
[
36
, Proposition 9]
.
Indeed, as confirmed by the authors, there is a minor typo in
[
36
, Proposition 9]
that arises from a typo in the Plancherel-Rotach asymptotic formula in
[
36
, Eq.(3.14)]
, where
(2N)^{1/2}
should be corrected to
(2N+1)^{1/2}
.
With this correction, the resulting formula coincides with our formula (
1.35
).

Remark 1.8

(
Further expansions
)
.

We note that the methods employed for our main results can also be extended to derive more precise expansions in Theorems
1.1
and
1.5
.
To achieve this, more detailed asymptotic behaviours of the Hermite polynomials (Plancherel-Rotach formulas) are required.
We mention that in some of our analysis, we have already crucially utilized a recent work
[
74
]
on such behaviours to derive the first subleading corrections.
On one hand, in Theorem
1.1
, it is also possible to deduce the asymptotic behaviour of the
1
-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}
outside its support (i.e.
x\not\in(-1-\tau,1+\tau)
), unveiling the exponential decays.

Organisation of the paper

The rest of this paper is organised as follows.
In Section

In Section

.
In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some standard facts on the real eigenvalue density of the elliptic GinOE.
We then recall the Plancherel-Rotach asymptotic formulas of the Hermite polynomials that will be crucially used in the later analysis.
Unless otherwise stated, we assume that
N
is an even integer and
\tau\in(0,1]
.

It is well known that the elliptic GinOE eigenvalues form a Pfaffian point process, and its
2\times 2
matrix-valued kernel can be expressed in terms of the associated skew-orthogonal polynomials, see e.g.
[
22
, Section 2.8]
.
Furthermore, in
[
43
]
, the associated skew-orthogonal polynomial is constructed in terms of the classical Hermite polynomial

H_{k}(x):=(-1)^{k}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d}{dx}e^{-x^{2}}.

As a result, it was shown in
[
43
, Section 6.2]
that after some manipulations, the
1
-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}
can be written as

(2.1)

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)+\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x),

where

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}x^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau)}x^{2}}H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau)}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du.\end{split}

See also
[
18
, Appendix A]
.
We first discuss the
\tau=0
case.

Remark 2.1
.

For the GinOE case
\tau=0
, the 1-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N}
was obtained in
[
30
, Corollary 4.3]
without the use of the skew-orthogonal polynomial formalism.
In this case, the Hermite polynomials in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are replaced by the monomials, which yields the expression

(2.4)

\mathbf{R}_{N}(x)\Big|_{\tau=0}=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma(N-1,Nx^{2})}{\Gamma(N-1)}+\frac{(2N)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}{2\,\Gamma(N-1)}|x|^{n-1}e^{-\frac{N}{2}x^{2}}\gamma(\tfrac{N-1}{2},\tfrac{N}{2}x^{2})\Big),

where
\gamma(s,x):=\int_{0}^{x}t^{s-1}e^{-t}\,dt
is the lower incomplete gamma function.
Let us mention that the expression (
2.4
) holds not only for the even integer
N
but also for the odd integer
N
.
Due to the expression (
2.4
), the analysis of
\mathbf{R}_{N}
is straightforward and follows from the uniform asymptotic behaviours of the incomplete gamma function
[
83
, Section 11.2.4]
.

From the above discussion, one can realize that the analysis of
\mathbf{R}_{N}
highly relies on whether one can find an effective way to analyse the summation in (
2.2
).
For the GOE case when
\tau=1
, this can be achieved using the classical Christoffel-Darboux formula

\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{1}{2^{k}\,k!}H_{k}(x)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(H_{N-2}(x)H_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)-H_{N-2}^{\prime}(x)H_{N-1}(x)\Big).

This allows to derive the large-
N
behaviour of the
1
-point function for the GOE, see
[
36
]
.
In contrast, for general
\tau\in(0,1)
, the Christoffel-Darboux formula cannot be applied.
Instead, an effective way to analyse
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}
was found in
[
23
, Section 4]
through the observation

(2.5)

\Big(\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)\Big)^{\prime}=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x).

This follows from the generalised Christoffel-Darboux formula introduced by Lee and Riser in
[
64
]
.
Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(
Integral representation of
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}
)
.

The function
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}
in (
2.2
) has an integral representation

(2.6)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x_{0})-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}\int_{x_{0}}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du,

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.

One can make use of the expression (
2.6
) to avoid dealing with the summation in (
2.2
). Indeed, analysing such a summation becomes particularly involved when considering the precise asymptotic expansion.
On the other hand, additional difficulties arise from the need to analyse the extra integral in (
2.6
).
As discussed later, this in turn requires analysing certain oscillatory integrals.
Furthermore, one needs to derive the asymptotic value of the initial term
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x_{0})
by examining the summation (
2.2
).
To address this, we will choose a suitable value for
x_{0}
according to the situation under consideration.

After these preparations, one can expect that the asymptotic behaviours of the Hermite polynomials will play a key role in the later analysis.
These are known as the Plancherel-Rotach formulas, see e.g.
[
74
,
81
]
and references therein.
To describe the Plancherel-Rotach formulas in various regimes, it is convenient to use the following notations:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle:=x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}-\arccos(x);

(2.8)

\displaystyle\theta_{m}(x)

\displaystyle:=(m+\frac{1}{2})\arccos(x)-\frac{\pi}{4};

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sigma(x)

\displaystyle:=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}.

Lemma 2.3

(
The Plancherel-Rotach formula for the oscillatory regime
)
.

Let
N
and
m
be integers, and
x\in(-1,1)
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{N+m}(\sqrt{2N}x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}(1-x^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}(N+m)!e^{\frac{N}{2}}2^{\frac{N+m}{2}}N^{-\frac{N+m+1}{2}}e^{Nx^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(h_{(0)}^{\rm osc}(x)+h_{(1)}^{\rm osc}(x)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big),\end{split}

where

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{(0)}^{\rm osc}(x)&:=\cos(N\phi(x)-\theta_{m}(x)),\\
h_{(1)}^{\rm osc}(x)&:=A_{m}(x)\cos(N\phi(x)-\theta_{m}(x))+B_{m}(x)\sin(N\phi(x)-\theta_{m}(x)).\end{split}

Here,
\phi
and
\theta_{m}
are given in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{m}(x)&:=\frac{(6m^{2}+6m+1)x^{2}-6m^{2}-12m-4}{24(1-x^{2})},\\
B_{m}(x)&:=-\frac{(12m^{2}+12m+2)x^{3}-(12m^{2}+12m-3)x}{48(1-x^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.\end{split}

Lemma 2.4

(
The Plancherel-Rotach formula for the critical regime
)
.

Let
N
and
m
be integers, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.13)

e^{-Nx^{2}}H_{N+m}(\sqrt{2N}x)=\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}(N!)^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{12}}\Big(\operatorname{Ai}(\xi)-(m+\tfrac{1}{2})\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}})\Big),

for
x=1+2^{-1}N^{-\frac{2}{3}}\xi
, where
\operatorname{Ai}
is the Airy function.

Lemma 2.5

(
The Plancherel-Rotach formula for the exponential regime
)
.

Let
N
and
m
be integers, and
x\in(1,\infty)
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.14)

H_{N+m}(\sqrt{2N}x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}(x^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}}2^{\frac{N+m}{2}}N^{\frac{N+m-1}{2}}\sigma(x)^{N+m+\frac{1}{2}}e^{Nx/\sigma(x)}\Big(1+O(N^{-1})\Big),

where
\sigma
is given in (
2.9
).

Note that in the analysis of (
2.1
), we shall use the asymptotic behaviours of the Hermite polynomial of the form
H_{N+m}(\sqrt{2N}\frac{x}{2\sqrt{\tau}}).

On the one hand, notice here that the foci of the ellipse (
1.2
) are located at
\pm 2\sqrt{\tau}
.
Therefore, depending on the position of
x
with respect to the foci, we need to apply the Plancherel-Rotach formula in different regimes:

•

if
|x|<2\sqrt{\tau}
, it corresponds to the oscillatory regime, Lemma
2.3
, cf. Region I in Figure

•

if

, it corresponds to the critical regime, Lemma
2.4
, cf. Region II in Figure

;

•

if
|x|>2\sqrt{\tau}
, it corresponds to the exponential regime, Lemma
2.5
, cf. Region III in Figure

.

(Let us mention that the line segment connecting two foci coincides with the limiting zero set of the planar Hermite polynomial known as the limiting skeleton.)
Note also that in the weakly non-Hermitian regime where
\tau\uparrow 1
, the focus
2\sqrt{\tau}
becomes close to the right-most point

3.1.
Strong non-Hermiticity

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.1
(i).
We assume throughout this subsection that
\tau\in(0,1)
is fixed.
For
\tau=0
, see Remark
2.1
.

We begin with the analysis of the large-
N
behaviour of
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)
.
For this purpose, recall that the regularized hypergeometric function
{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is defined by

(3.1)

{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(a,b;c;z):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(c+s)\ s!}z^{s},\qquad(|z|<1)

and by the analytic continuation for
|z|\geq 1
, see e.g.
[
71
, Chapter 15]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
be fixed.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.2)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(e^{-\epsilon N}),

for some
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

By using (
2.2
), (
3.1
) and the Hermite number

H_{k}(0)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1-k}{2})}2^{k},

we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)&=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(0)^{2}=\Big(\frac{N}{2}\pi\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(2\tau)^{k}}{k!}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{1-k}{2})^{2}}\\
&=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{N}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{N+2}{2};\tau^{2}).\end{split}

We now apply the Euler integral formula
[
71
, Eq.(15.6.1)]
: for
c>b>0
,

(3.4)

{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}}{(1-zt)^{a}}\,dt.

This gives rise to

(3.5)

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{N+2}{2};\tau^{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\frac{N-1}{2}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\tau^{2}t}\,dt.

Then it follows from

0\leq\frac{t^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}{1-\tau^{2}t}\leq\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}},\qquad t\in[0,1],

that

0\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{N+2}{2};\tau^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{2}{1-\tau^{2}}.

Since
\tau\in(0,1)
, one can observe that the second term in the last expression of (
3.3
) decays exponentially as
N\to\infty
, and the proof is complete.
∎

It remains to show the exponential decay of the integrals in (
2.3
) and (
2.6
) as
N\to\infty
.
We first prove the exponential convergence of
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x\in[0,1+\tau)
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.6)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)+O(e^{-\epsilon N}),

for some constant
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

We first prove the lemma for
x\in[0,r_{\tau}]
, where

(3.7)

r_{\tau}:=\frac{2\sqrt{\tau}+\lambda_{\tau}}{2},\qquad\lambda_{\tau}:=\Big(-\frac{2\tau(1+\tau)\log\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We note here that
2\sqrt{\tau}<\lambda_{\tau}<1+\tau
, cf. Figure
3
.
The main ingredient of the proof is the inequality for the Hermite polynomials given in
[
56
, Eq.(8.954)]
.
It says that there is a constant
C>0
satisfying

(3.8)

\Big|H_{N+m}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)\Big|\leq C\Big((N+m)!\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{Nx^{2}}{4\tau}},\qquad(N\to\infty),

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

It follows from (
2.6
) and (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)-\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)\big|\leq\widetilde{C}N^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{N\omega_{0}(u)}\,du,

for some constant
\widetilde{C}>0
, where

(3.9)

\omega_{0}(u):=\log\tau+\frac{1-\tau}{2\tau(1+\tau)}u^{2}.

Note that
\omega_{0}(u)
has two zeros
\pm\lambda_{\tau}
.
Hence, it is negative and strictly increasing in
[0,r_{\tau}]
.
Therefore, we have

\int_{0}^{x}e^{N\omega_{0}(u)}\,du\leq xe^{N\omega_{0}(x)}=O(e^{-\epsilon N}),

for some constant
\epsilon>0
.
Therefore, the lemma is proved for
x\in[0,r_{\tau}]
.

Next, we prove the lemma for
x\in[r_{\tau},1+\tau)
.
By applying (
2.14
) to (
2.6
), we have

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(r_{\tau})+\widehat{C}\int_{r_{\tau}}^{x}f(u)e^{N\omega_{1}(u)}\,du\ \Big(1+O(N^{-1})\Big),

for some constant
\widehat{C}>0
, where

f(u):=(u^{2}-4\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u+\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau})^{-2},

and

(3.10)

\omega_{1}(u):=\frac{(1-\tau)u^{2}-(1+\tau)u\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau}}{2\tau(1+\tau)}+2\log\Big(\frac{u+\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau}}{2}\Big).

Notice that

\omega_{1}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{(1-\tau)u-(1+\tau)\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau}}{\tau(1+\tau)}.

Therefore, we have
\omega_{1}^{\prime}(1+\tau)=0
.
Moreover, since

\omega_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)=\frac{(1-\tau)\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau}-(1+\tau){u}}{\tau(1+\tau)\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau}}<0,\qquad u\in(2\sqrt{\tau},1+\tau],

the function
\omega_{1}(u)
is increasing on
(2\sqrt{\tau},1+\tau]
.
Furthermore, the function
f(u)
is decreasing on
u\in(2\sqrt{\tau},1+\tau)
, which implies that
f(u)
is bounded by
f(r_{\tau})
for any
u\in[r_{\tau},1+\tau)
.
Thus, we have

\int_{r_{\tau}}^{x}f(u)e^{N\omega_{1}(u)}\,du\leq(x-r_{\tau})\,f(r_{\tau})e^{N\omega_{1}(x)}\,du=O(e^{-\epsilon N}),

for some constant
\epsilon>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

It remains to prove the exponential decay of
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x\in[0,1+\tau)
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.11)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x)=O(e^{-\epsilon N})

for some constant
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

Recall that
r_{\tau}
and
\lambda_{\tau}
are defined in (
3.7
).
We first prove the lemma for
x\in[0,r_{\tau}]
.
It follows from (
2.3
) and (
3.8
) that

|\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x)|\leq CN^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{0}(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{0}(u)}\,du

for some constant
C>0
, where
\omega_{0}(u)
is given in (
3.9
).
Similar to the above, one can observe that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{0}(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{0}(u)}\,du\leq xe^{N\omega_{0}(x)}=O(e^{-\epsilon N}),

for any
x\in[0,r_{\tau}]
.
Thus, the lemma is proved for
x\in[0,r_{\tau}]
.

Next, suppose
x\in[r_{\tau},1+\tau)
.
By applying (
2.14
) to (
2.3
), we have

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{N}^{2}(r_{\tau})+\widetilde{C}\,(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-4\tau})g(x)e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{1}(x)}\int_{r_{\tau}}^{x}g(u)e^{\frac{N}{2}\omega_{1}(u)}\,du\ \Big(1+O(N^{-1})\Big),

for some constant
\widetilde{C}>0
, where
\omega_{1}(u)
is given in (
3.10
) and

g(u):=(u^{2}-4\tau)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(u+\sqrt{u^{2}-4\tau})^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Then the rest of the proof proceeds in the same manner as the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.2.
Weak non-Hermiticity

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.1
(ii).
As before, we first compute the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.12)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)=\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)N+\frac{\alpha^{2}-1}{4\pi}e^{-\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{8\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)+O(N^{-1}).

Proof.

By
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)=a_{N}-b_{N}
, where

a_{N}:=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad b_{N}:=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\tau^{N}}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})}{\pi}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{\tau^{2}-1}).

Notice here that

(3.13)

a_{N}=\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}N+\frac{\alpha}{8\sqrt{\pi}}+O(N^{-1}).

To analyse
b_{N}
, we make use of
[
71
, Eq.(15.8.3)]
and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})}{\pi}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{\tau^{2}-1})&=\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{N}{2};\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}})\\
&\quad-\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\frac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{3}{2};\tfrac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}).\end{split}

By using (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{3}{2};\tfrac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}t\Big)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}\,dt.

Note here that

\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}t\Big)^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}=e^{\alpha^{2}t}\Big(1+\big(\alpha^{4}t^{2}+\tfrac{(2-\alpha^{2})\alpha^{2}}{2}t\big)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{3}{2};\tfrac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}t}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Big(1+\big(\alpha^{4}t^{2}+\tfrac{(2-\alpha^{2})\alpha^{2}}{2}t\big)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}}}{\alpha}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)+\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}-1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}+\frac{2\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{2}+1}{4\alpha}e^{\alpha^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)\Big)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}).

On the other hand, by
[
71
, Eq.(15.4.6)]
and the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle{}_{2}\mathbf{F}_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{N}{2};\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{2}(2N-\alpha^{2})}{N^{2}}\Big)^{-\frac{N}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{\alpha^{2}}\Big(1+\frac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big).

Combining all of the above, we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle b_{N}=\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}(1-\operatorname{erf}(\alpha))N+\Big(\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{4\pi}e^{-\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{8\sqrt{\pi}}(1-\operatorname{erf}(\alpha))\Big)+O(N^{-1}).

Then the lemma follows from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

with fixed

.
Then as

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-\frac{Nx^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}H_{N+m}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)&=\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}(1-\frac{x^{2}}{4})^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{\frac{N}{2}}(N+m)!2^{\frac{N+m}{2}}N^{-\frac{N+m+1}{2}}\\

for any

k_{(1)}^{(m)}(x)&:=C_{m}(\tfrac{x}{2})\cos(N\phi(\tfrac{x}{2})-\widetilde{\theta}_{m}(\tfrac{x}{2}))+D_{m}(\tfrac{x}{2})\sin(N\phi(\tfrac{x}{2})-\widetilde{\theta}_{m}(\tfrac{x}{2})).\end{split}

Here,
\phi
and
\widetilde{\theta}_{m}
are given in (

3.15

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{m}(x)&:=A_{m}(x)+\frac{(3\alpha^{2}-1)\alpha^{2}x^{2}-3\alpha^{4}x^{4}}{4(1-x^{2})},\\
D_{m}(x)&:=B_{m}(x)-\frac{(3\alpha^{2}+2m+1)\alpha^{2}x-4\alpha^{4}x^{3}}{4\sqrt{1-x^{2}}},\end{split}

where
A_{m}
and

We observe from (

) and (

involve certain oscillatory integrals.
To analyse such integrals, we shall utilize the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f
and
\psi
be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-functions on a neighborhood of an interval
[a,b]
.
Suppose
\psi
has no critical point in
[a,b]
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.20)

\int_{a}^{b}f(u)e^{iN\psi(u)}du=i\Big(\frac{f(a)}{\psi^{\prime}(a)}e^{iN\psi(a)}-\frac{f(b)}{\psi^{\prime}(b)}e^{iN\psi(b)}\Big)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}).

Proof.

Since
\psi^{\prime}
has no zero in
[a,b]
, it follows from integration by parts that

\int_{a}^{b}f(u)e^{iN\psi(u)}\,du=i\bigg[\Big(\frac{f(a)}{\psi^{\prime}(a)}e^{iN\psi(a)}-\frac{f(b)}{\psi^{\prime}(b)}e^{iN\psi(b)}\Big)+\int_{a}^{b}\Big(\frac{f(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\Big)^{\prime}e^{iN\psi(u)}\,du\bigg]N^{-1}.

Again, by integration by parts, we have

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\Big(\frac{f(u)}{\psi^{\prime}(u)}\Big)^{\prime}e^{iN\psi(u)}\,du\Big|\leq\bigg(2\Big\|\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{f}{\psi^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}\Big\|_{\infty}+x\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{f}{\psi^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}\Big)^{\prime}\Big\|_{\infty}\bigg)N^{-1}=O(N^{-1}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to compute the finite size corrections for
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{j}(x)

We begin with
\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(3.21)

\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{1}(x)N+\mathbf{R}_{(1)}^{1}(x)+O(N^{-1}),

for any

, where

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{(0)}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\alpha}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}),

(3.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{R}_{(1)}^{1}(x)&=\frac{\alpha}{8\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})-\frac{3\alpha^{2}x^{2}-4\alpha^{2}+8}{8\pi\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(x^{2}-4)}\\
&\quad-\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}x^{2}}}{\pi(4-x^{2})}\sin(2N\phi(\tfrac{x}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{x}{2}))+\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-\alpha^{2}}.\end{split}

Here,
\phi
and
\Theta
are given in (
2.7
) and (
3.16
).

Proof.

Note that by (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)-\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)&=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\alpha^{2}}2^{-N+\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+\tfrac{4\alpha^{2}-\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big)\frac{N}{(N-2)!}\\
&\quad\times\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du.\end{split}

Using (
3.17
) and Stirling’s formula

(3.24)

N!=\sqrt{2\pi N}\Big(\frac{N}{e}\Big)^{N}\Big(1+\frac{1}{12}N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big),

we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)-\mathbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)&=-Ne^{-\alpha^{2}}\Big(1+(\tfrac{4\alpha^{2}-\alpha^{4}}{2}+\tfrac{1}{12})N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big)\\
&\quad\times\Big(K_{(0)}(x)+K_{(1)}(x)N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\Big),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle K_{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}u^{2}}}{\pi(1-\frac{u^{2}}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}}k_{(0)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(0)}^{(-1)}(u)\,du,

\displaystyle K_{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}u^{2}}}{\pi(1-\frac{u^{2}}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(k_{(1)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(0)}^{(-1)}(u)+k_{(0)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(1)}^{(-1)}(u)\Big)\,du.

Here,
k_{(j)}^{(m)}
’s are defined in (
3.18
).

We first evaluate
K_{(0)}(x)
.
By the definition (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle k_{(0)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(0)}^{(-1)}(u)=\frac{u}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{u}{2})).

Therefore, we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{(0)}(x)&=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{u\,e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}u^{2}}}{4\pi(1-\frac{u^{2}}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,du+\operatorname{Re}\Big[\int_{0}^{x}f(\tfrac{u}{2})e^{i2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})}\,du\Big]\\
&=\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\alpha}\Big(\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)-\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})\Big)+\operatorname{Re}\Big[\int_{0}^{x}f(\tfrac{u}{2})e^{i2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})}\,du\Big],\end{split}

where

f(u):=\frac{1}{2\pi(1-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\exp\Big(\alpha^{2}u^{2}-i\Theta(u)\Big).

We now apply (
3.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Re}\Big[\int_{0}^{x}f(\tfrac{u}{2})e^{i2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})}\,du\Big]&=\operatorname{Im}\Big[\Big(\frac{f(\frac{x}{2})}{\phi^{\prime}(\frac{x}{2})}e^{i2N\phi(\frac{x}{2})}-\frac{f(0)}{\phi^{\prime}(0)}e^{i2N\phi(0)}\Big)\Big]N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})\\
&=\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}x^{2}}}{\pi(4-x^{2})}\sin(2N\phi(\tfrac{x}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{x}{2}))N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}).\end{split}

Putting them together, we have

(3.27)

K_{(0)}(x)=\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\alpha}\Big(\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)-\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})\Big)+\frac{e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}x^{2}}}{\pi(4-x^{2})}\sin(2N\phi(\tfrac{x}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{x}{2}))N^{-1}+O(N^{-2}).

We now turn into evaluation of
K_{(1)}(x)
.
By the definition (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle k_{(1)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(0)}^{(-1)}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{-2}(\tfrac{u}{2})}{2}\Big[\cos(2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{u}{2}))+\frac{u}{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{D_{-2}(\tfrac{u}{2})}{2}\Big[\sin(2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{u}{2}))+\Big(1-\frac{u^{2}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle k_{(0)}^{(-2)}(u)k_{(1)}^{(-1)}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{-1}(\tfrac{u}{2})}{2}\Big[\cos(2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{u}{2}))+\frac{u}{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{D_{-1}(\tfrac{u}{2})}{2}\Big[\sin(2N\phi(\tfrac{u}{2})-\Theta(\tfrac{u}{2}))-\Big(1-\frac{u^{2}}{4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

Then, by using (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), after some computations, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{(1)}(x)&=\frac{6\alpha^{4}-21\alpha^{2}-1}{24\sqrt{\pi}\alpha}e^{\alpha^{2}}\Big(\operatorname{erf}(\alpha)-\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{3\alpha^{2}x^{2}-4\alpha^{2}+8}{8\pi\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}x^{2}}{4}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}-2}{4\pi}+O(N^{-1}).\end{split}

Substituting (
3.27
) and (
3.28
) into (

), the conclusion follows.

4.1.

In this subsection, we prove Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
be fixed. Then as

, we have

(4.9)

e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau)}x^{2}}H_{N+m}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)=\frac{e^{\frac{m}{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\tau}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{\frac{N+m}{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}}\Big(1+h_{(1)}^{\rm exp}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1})\Big)

.

The error bound of

in (

) was obtained in
[

, Theorem 2.1]

Furthermore, it was shown that if

after the integration.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
be fixed. Then as
N\to\infty

R_{N}^{1}(\xi)=R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{1}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1}),

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, where

(4.12)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\Big(1-\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}\xi)\Big),

Proof.

By (
4.2
), it is enough to compute the asymptotic behaviours of
S_{N}
and
T_{N}^{1}(\xi)

Note that by Stirling’s formula (

), we have

S_{N}=\Big(\frac{1-\tau}{2\pi(1+\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{3}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{e\tau}{2N}\Big)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1+O(N^{-1})\Big).

On the other hand, by (

) and Remark

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{N}^{1}(\xi)&=\Big(\frac{2(1+\tau)}{\pi(1-\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{3}{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}\\
&\quad\times\int_{\xi}^{\infty}e^{-2t^{2}}\bigg(1+\Big(\frac{2}{3}\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{3}{2}}t^{3}-\frac{3-\tau}{1-\tau}\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}t\Big)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1})\bigg)\,dt.\end{split}

Using the Gaussian integral

\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{3}e^{-a^{2}t^{2}}dt=\frac{a^{2}x^{2}+1}{2a^{4}}e^{-a^{2}x^{2}},\qquad(a>0),

Next, we compute the asymptotic behaviours of

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
be fixed. Then as

R_{N}^{2}(\xi)=R_{(0)}^{2}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{2}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1}),

for

, where

(4.17)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\xi^{2}}\Big(1+\operatorname{erf}(\xi)\Big),

(4.18)

\displaystyle R_{(1)}^{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}{12\pi(1-\tau)^{2}}e^{-2\xi^{2}}\bigg(\Big((1+\tau)\xi^{3}-3\xi\Big)\sqrt{\pi}e^{\xi^{2}}\Big(1+\operatorname{erf}(\xi)\Big)-(1+\tau)\xi^{2}-4\tau+5\bigg).

Proof.

We begin with (
4.6
).
Making use of Stirling’s formula (
3.24
), we have

\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1+\tau)}{2N}}\frac{(N-2)!}{(N/2-1)!}\tau^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}=\frac{\tau\sqrt{\pi(1+\tau)}}{2}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1+O(N^{-1})\Big).

By using (
4.9
) and Remark
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2}(\xi)&=\frac{\tau\sqrt{1+\tau}}{2}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\\
&\quad\times\int_{\xi}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}\bigg(1+\Big(\frac{1}{3}\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{3}{2}}t^{3}+\frac{\tau-2}{1-\tau}\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}t\Big)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1})\bigg)\,dt.\end{split}

Then, by the Gaussian integral (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2}(\xi)&=\frac{\tau\sqrt{\pi(1+\tau)}}{2}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\\
&\quad\times\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\operatorname{erf}(\xi)\Big)+\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau}}{6\sqrt{\pi}(1-\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big((1+\tau)\xi^{2}+4\tau-5\Big)e^{-\xi^{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1})\bigg).\end{split}

Putting them together in (
4.6
), we have

(4.19)

T_{N}^{2}(\xi)=\Big(\frac{2\pi(1+\tau)}{1-\tau}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{3}{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e\tau}\Big)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\xi^{2}}\Big(R_{(0)}^{2}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{2}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+O(N^{-1})\Big).

Combining this with (
4.14
), the lemma follows.
∎

4.2.
Weak non-Hermiticity

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.5
(ii).
Throughout this subsection, let us write

(4.20)

x=1+\tau+\frac{\xi}{N^{2/3}}.

For the later purpose, we state a generalised version of Lemma
2.4
. See e.g.
[
78
, Eqs.(3.3) and (3.5)]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N^{\frac{1}{3}}
with fixed
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-\frac{Nx^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}}H_{N+m}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)&=\exp\Big(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{4}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{6}\Big)(2N)^{\frac{m}{2}}\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}(N!)^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{12}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\big(\mathcal{A}_{m}(\xi)\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\mathcal{B}_{m}(\xi)\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)\big)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon})\Big),\end{split}

uniformly for
\xi\in[-N^{\delta},N^{\delta}]
, where
\delta\in[0,2/3)
,
\epsilon>2\delta
and

(4.22)

\mathcal{A}_{m}(t):=\frac{2\alpha^{4}t+\alpha^{8}+(4m+2)\alpha^{2}}{8},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{m}(t):=\frac{2\alpha^{2}t+\alpha^{6}-4m-2}{4}.

As before, we compute the asymptotic behaviours of
R_{N}^{1}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N^{\frac{1}{3}}
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.23)

R_{N}^{1}(\xi)=R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)+R_{(1)}^{1}(\xi)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}),

for any
\epsilon>0
, where

(4.24)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\,dt,

(4.25)

\displaystyle R_{(1)}^{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha^{2}\xi+\alpha^{6}+4}{4}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)^{2}+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{4}t+\alpha^{2}}{2}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}\,dt.

Proof.

Note that as
N\to\infty,

(4.26)

\tau^{N}=\Big(1-\frac{\alpha^{8}}{4}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}})\Big)\exp\Big(-\alpha^{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}-\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}-\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{3}\Big).

Then it follows from (
4.3
) and (
3.24
) that

(4.27)

S_{N}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Big(\frac{e}{2N}\Big)^{N}N^{\frac{11}{6}}\Big(1+(2\alpha^{2}-\tfrac{\alpha^{8}}{4})N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}})\Big)\exp\Big(-\alpha^{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}-\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}-\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{3}\Big).

Next, we analyse the asymptotic behaviour of
T_{N}^{1}(\xi)
.
Let us write

(4.28)

r_{N}:=1+\tau+\frac{N^{\delta}}{N^{2/3}},

for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
Then we divide

T_{N}^{1}(\xi)=T_{N}^{1,1}(\xi)+T_{N}^{1,2}(\xi),

where

\displaystyle T_{N}^{1,1}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{x}^{r_{N}}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du,

\displaystyle T_{N}^{1,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{r_{N}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du.

Using (
4.21
) and the Airy differential equation
\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime\prime}(x)=x\operatorname{Ai}(x)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{N}^{1,1}(\xi)&=\exp\Big(\alpha^{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{3}\Big)(2N)^{-\frac{3}{2}}2^{N+1}(N!)N^{-5/6}\\
&\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\xi}^{N^{\delta}}\Big(\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}+\big(a_{1}(t)\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)^{2}+a_{2}(t)\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\big)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}\Big)\,dt+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+3\delta})\bigg),\end{split}

where

a_{1}(t):=\frac{2\alpha^{4}t+\alpha^{8}-4\alpha^{2}}{4},\qquad a_{2}(t):=\frac{2\alpha^{2}t+\alpha^{6}+4}{2}.

By the asymptotic formula of the Airy function
[
71
, Eq.(9.7.5)]
,

\operatorname{Ai}(x)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}},\qquad(x\to+\infty),

we have

\int_{N^{\delta}}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\,dt=O(e^{-N^{\delta}}).

Using this together with (
3.24
), after some computations, we obtain

(4.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{N}^{1,1}(\xi)&=\sqrt{\pi}\Big(\frac{2N}{e}\Big)^{N}N^{-\frac{11}{6}}\exp\Big(\alpha^{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{3}\Big)\\
&\quad\times\Big(R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)+\Big(R_{(1)}^{1}(\xi)-(2\alpha^{2}-\tfrac{\alpha^{8}}{4})R_{(0)}^{1}(\xi)\Big)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+4\delta})\Big).\end{split}

By
[
74
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have that for
x>1
,

H_{N+m}(\sqrt{2N}x)\leq\frac{1}{(x^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}}N^{\frac{N+m-1}{2}}2^{\frac{N+m}{2}}\sigma(x)^{N+m+\frac{3}{2}}e^{Nx/\sigma(x)}.

This in turn implies that for
x>r_{N}
,

(4.30)

e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}x^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}x)\leq\frac{1}{(\frac{x^{2}}{4\tau}-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-N}N^{N-\frac{5}{2}}2^{N-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\Big(2N\omega(\tfrac{x}{2\sqrt{\tau}})\Big),

where

\omega(x):=\log(\sigma(x))+\frac{x}{\sigma(x)}-\frac{2\tau}{1+\tau}x^{2}.

Notice here that for a sufficiently large
N
and
x\in[1+\tau,\infty)
, we have

(4.31)

\omega^{\prime}(x)=-2\sigma(x)+\frac{2(1-\tau)}{1+\tau}x=-2\sigma(x)+\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{N^{1/3}}\frac{1}{1+\tau}x<-x.

Furthermore, we have

(4.32)

\omega(r_{N})=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{\alpha^{4}}{4}N^{-\frac{2}{3}}-N^{-1+\frac{3}{2}\delta}+O(N^{-1+\frac{1}{2}\delta}).

On the other hand, by (
4.31
) and (
4.32
), we deduce

(4.33)

\exp\Big(2N\omega(\tfrac{x}{2\sqrt{\tau}})\Big)\leq\exp\Big(\alpha^{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{3}\Big)\exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}N^{\frac{3}{2}\delta}-Nx^{2}\Big).

Combining (
4.30
) and (
4.33
), we obtain

(4.34)

T_{N}^{1,2}(\xi)=T_{N}^{1,1}(\xi)\cdot O(e^{-N^{\delta}}).

Now the lemma follows from (
4.2
), (
4.27
) and (
4.29
).
∎

It remains to show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\tau=1-\alpha^{2}/N^{\frac{1}{3}}
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.35)

R_{N}^{2}(x)=R_{(0)}^{2}(x)+R_{(1)}^{2}(x)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}),

for any
\epsilon>0
, where

(4.36)

\displaystyle R_{(0)}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)\Big(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\Big),

(4.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{(1)}^{2}(x)&=\Big(\frac{\alpha^{4}\xi+2\alpha^{2}}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\xi+\alpha^{6}+2}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)\Big)\Big(1-\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\Big)\\
&\quad+\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)\Big(\frac{2\alpha^{2}\xi+\alpha^{6}+6}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{8}\int_{\xi}^{\infty}t\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)\,dt\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

It follows from (
3.24
) and (
4.26
) that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1+\tau)}{2N}}\frac{(N-2)!}{(N/2-1)!}\tau^{1-\tfrac{N}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}\exp\Big(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{4}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{6}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(1+\frac{\alpha^{8}-10\alpha^{2}}{8}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}})\Big).

We decompose
\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2}
as

\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2}(\xi)=\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,1}(\xi)+\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,2}(\xi),

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,1}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{x}^{r_{N}}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau)}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du,

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,1}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{r_{N}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau)}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}u)\,du.

Here,
r_{N}
is given in (
4.28
).
Then a similar analysis, akin to the one presented in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, establishes that for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,1}(\xi)&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}\exp\Big(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{4}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{6}\Big)\\
&\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\Big(\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)+\Big(\tfrac{2\alpha^{4}t+\alpha^{8}-6\alpha^{2}}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(t)+\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}t+\alpha^{6}+6}{4}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t)\Big)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}\Big)\,dt+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}+2\delta})\bigg),\end{split}

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2,1}(\xi)\cdot O(e^{-N^{\delta}}).

Using (
4.21
), it follows from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{N}^{2}(\xi)&=\exp\Big(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2}N^{\frac{2}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{4}}{4}N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{\alpha^{6}}{6}\Big)\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{2N}{e}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\Big(\frac{2\alpha^{4}\xi+\alpha^{8}-2\alpha^{2}}{8}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}(\xi)+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\xi+\alpha^{6}+2}{4}\operatorname{Ai}_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\xi)\Big)N^{-\frac{1}{3}}+O(N^{-\frac{2}{3}})\Big)\\
&\quad\times\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\pi(1+\tau)}{2N}}\frac{(N-2)!}{(N/2-1)!}\tau^{1-\tfrac{N}{2}}-\widetilde{T}_{N}^{2}(\xi)\Big).\end{split}

Combining this with (
4.2
) and (
4.27
), we obtain the desired asymptotic behaviour.
∎

2-prime hyperideals
Mahdi Anbarloei

Abstract
. Multiplicative hyperrings are an important class of algebraic hyperstructures which generalize rings further to allow multiple output values for the multiplication operation. Let R R be a commutative multiplicative hyperring. A proper hyperideal I I of R R is called 2-prime if x ∘ y ⊆ I x\circ y\subseteq I for some x , y ∈ R x,y\in R , then x 2 ⊆ I x^{2}\subseteq I or y 2 ⊆ I y^{2}\subseteq I . The 2-prime hyperideals are a generalization of prime hyperideals. In this paper, we aim to study 2-prime hyperideals and give some results. Moreover, we investigate δ \delta -2-primary hyperideals which are an expansion of 2-prime hyperideals.

A hyperoperation ”
\circ
” on nonempty set
G

\circ

G

(G,\circ)
is called hypergroupoid. The hyperoperation on

X\circ Y=\cup_{x\in X,y\in Y}x\circ y,\ \ X\circ g=X\circ\{g\}.

A hypergroupoid
(G,\circ)
is called a semihypergroup if for all
x,y,z\in G
,
(x\circ y)\circ z=x\circ(y\circ z)
, which is associative. A semihypergroup is said to be a hypergroup if
g\circ G=G=G\circ g
for all
g\in G
. A nonempty subset
H
of a semihypergroup
(G,\circ)
is called a
subhypergroup if for all
x\in H

. A commutative hypergroup
(G,\circ)
is canonical if

(i)

there exists
e\in G
with

, for every
x\in G
.

(ii)

for every
x\in G
there exists a unique
x^{-1}\in G
with
e\in x\circ x^{-1}
.

(iii)

x\in y\circ z
implies
y\in x\circ z^{-1}
.

A nonempty set
R
with two hyperoperations
"+"
and
"\circ"
is called a
hyperring if
(R,+)
is a canonical hypergroup ,
(R,\circ)
is a semihypergroup with

r\circ 0=0\circ r=0

r\in R
and the hyperoperation
"\circ"
is distributive with respect to

x\circ(y+z)=x\circ y+x\circ z

3.
2-prime hyperideals

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I
be a proper hyperideal of
R
. We say that
I
is 2-prime if for
all
x,y\in R
,
x\circ y\subseteq R
implies
x^{2}\subseteq I
or
y^{2}\subseteq I
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{Z},+,.)
be the ring of integers. In the multiplicative hyperring
(\mathbb{Z}_{A},+,\circ)
with
A=\{5,7\}
and the hyperoperation
a\circ b=\{a.r.b\ |\ r\in A\}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{A}
, the principal hyperideal
3\mathbb{Z}
is 2-prime.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
I
be a hyperideal of
R
. Then

(1)

If
I
is a 2-prime hyperideal, then
\sqrt{I}
is a prime hyperideal.

(2)

If
P
is a prime hyperideal of
R
, then
P^{2}
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R
.

(3)

Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be two multiplicative hyperrings such that
f:R_{1}\longrightarrow R_{2}
is a good homomorphism. If
I_{2}
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R_{2}
, then
f^{-1}(I_{2})
is a 2-prime hyperideals of
R_{1}
.

(4)

Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be two multiplicative hyperrings such that
f:R_{1}\longrightarrow R_{2}
is a good epimorphism. If
I_{1}
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R_{1}
with
Kerf\subseteq I_{1}
, then
f(I_{1})
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R_{2}
.

(5)

If
I
is a 2-prime hyperideal and
I_{1},I_{2}
are two subsets of
R
with
I_{1}\circ I_{2}\subseteq I
, then

\bigcup_{x\in I_{1}}x^{2}\subseteq I
or
\bigcup_{y\in I_{2}}y^{2}\subseteq I
.

Proof.

(1) Let the hyperideal
I
be 2-prime. If
x\circ y\subseteq\sqrt{I}
for
x,y\in R
then for some positive integer
n
,
x^{n}\circ y^{n}\subseteq I
. Since hyperideal
I
is 2-prime, we have
x^{2n}\subseteq I
or
y^{2n}\subseteq I
. Thus we get
x\in\sqrt{I}
or
y\in\sqrt{I}
which means hyperideal
\sqrt{I}
is prime.

(2) Since
P^{2}\subseteq P
, then we are done.

(3) Let
x\circ y\subseteq f^{-1}(I_{2})
for
x,y\in R_{1}
. Hence
f(x\circ y)\subseteq I_{2}
. Since the hyperideal
I_{2}
is 2-prime, then
f^{2}(x)=f(x^{2})\subseteq I_{2}
or
f^{2}(y)=f(y^{2})\subseteq I_{2}
. Thus
x^{2}\subseteq f^{-1}(I_{2})
or
y^{2}\subseteq f^{-1}(I_{2})
i.e., the hyperideal
f^{-1}(I_{2})
is 2-prime.

(4) Let for some elements
x_{2},y_{2}\in R_{2}
,
x_{2}\circ y_{2}\subseteq f(I_{1})
. Since
f
is an epimorphism, there exist
x_{1},y_{1}\in R_{1}
such that
f(x_{1})=x_{2},f(y_{1})=y_{2}
and so
f(x_{1}\circ y_{1})=x_{2}\circ y_{2}\subseteq f(I_{1})
. Now take any
u\in x_{1}\circ y_{1}
. Then we get
f(u)\in f(x_{1}\circ y_{1})\subseteq f(I_{1})
and so
f(u)=f(w)
for some

. This implies that
f(u-w)=0
, that is,
u-w\in Kerf\subseteq I_{1}
and so
u\in I_{1}
. Since
I_{1}
is a
{\bf C}
-hyperideal of
R_{1}
, then we conclude that
x_{1}\circ y_{1}\subseteq I_{1}
. Since
I_{1}
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R_{1}
then
x_{1}^{2}\subseteq I_{1}
which implies
x_{2}^{2}=f(x_{1}^{2})\subseteq f(I_{1})
or
y_{1}^{2}\subseteq I_{1}
which implies
y_{2}^{2}=f(y_{1}^{2})\subseteq f(I_{1})
.Consequently,
f(I_{1})
is a 2-prime hyperideal of
R_{2}
.

(5) Let
\bigcup_{x\in I_{1}}x^{2}\nsubseteq I

\bigcup_{y\in I_{2}}y^{2}\nsubseteq I

y_{0}^{2}\nsubseteq I

I
is 2-prime, then
x_{0}\circ y_{0}\nsubseteq I
which is a contradiction.
∎

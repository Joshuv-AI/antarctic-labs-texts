Abstract
. The Shafarevich conjecture/problem is about the finiteness of isomorphism classes of a family of varieties defined over a number field with good reduction outside a finite collection of places. For K3 surfaces, such a finiteness result was proved by Y. She. For hyper-Kähler varieties, which are higher-dimensional analogues of K3 surfaces, Y. André proved the Shafarevich conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties of a given dimension and admitting a very ample polarization of bounded degree. In this paper, we provide a unification of both results by proving the (unpolarized) Shafarevich conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties in a given deformation type. We also discuss the cohomological generalization of the Shafarevich conjecture by replacing the good reduction condition by the unramifiedness of the cohomology, where our results are subject to certain necessary assumption on the faithfulness of the action of the automorphism group on cohomology. In a similar fashion, generalizing a result of Orr and Skorobogatov on K3 surfaces, we prove the finiteness of geometric isomorphism classes of hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type in a given deformation type defined over a number field with bounded degree. A key to our approach to these results is a uniform Kuga–Satake map, inspired by She’s work, and we study its arithmetic properties, which are of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of places of
K
. The classical Hermite–Minkowski theorem says that there are only finitely many extensions of
K
with a fixed degree that is unramified outside
S
. The geometric generalization is the so-called
Shafarevich question
: given a family
\mathcal{M}
of smooth projective varieties defined over
K
, we ask the following:

Question 1.1

(Finiteness of varieties)
.

Is the set
\mathrm{Shaf}_{\mathcal{M}}(K,S)
of
K
-isomorphism classes of varieties in
\mathcal{M}
defined over
K
and with good reduction outside
S
finite?

As the notation indicates, such question can be traced back to a famous conjecture of Shafarevich
[
72
]
for the family of smooth curves of a given genus (
\geq 2
), which plays an important role in Faltings’ proof of the Mordell conjecture in
[
21
]
. Such Shafarevich-type question has been investigated in various situations, where results fall into two categories: polarized versus unpolarized.

For
polarized varieties
, i.e. varieties equipped with an ample line bundle, the question has an affirmative answer in a number of cases:

•

(Faltings
[
21
]
) Curves of a fixed genus
g\geq 2
, i.e.
\mathrm{Shaf}_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}(K,S)
is a finite set.

•

(Faltings
[
21
]
) Abelian varieties of a fixed dimension
g
and admitting a polarization of a given degree
d
, i.e.
\mathrm{Shaf}_{\mathcal{A}_{g,d}}(K,S)
is a finite set.

•

(Scholl
[
71
]
) del Pezzo surfaces.

•

(André
[
5
]
) K3 surfaces admitting a polarization of a given degree.

•

(André
[
5
]
) Hyper-Kähler varieties (with
b_{2}>3
) of given dimension with a very ample polarization of bounded degree; and similarly for more general “K3-type” varieties such as cubic fourfolds.

•

(Javanpeykar–Loughran
[
33
]
) Flag varieties.

•

(Javanpeykar
[
31
]
) Canonically polarized surfaces fibered over a curve.

•

(Javanpeykar–Loughran
[
34
]
,
[
35
]
) Certain varieties “controlled” by abelian varieties (via e.g. intermediate Jacobian): complete intersections of Hodge level
\leq 1
, prime Fano threefolds of index 2, sextic surfaces etc.

These results can often be reinterpreted as the finiteness of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
-points in certain moduli spaces; see works of Javanpeykar and his coauthors
[
36
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
for related studies from this point of view of arithmetic hyperbolicity. More recently, Lawrence and Sawin
[
46
]
proved some analogous finiteness result for hypersurfaces in abelian varieties based on the techniques in
[
47
]
.

The
unpolarized
Shafarevich conjecture is a much stronger statement which bypasses the restriction on polarizations (e.g. degree or natural embedding) in the finiteness statements in the above polarized version. The first example is Zarhin’s result
[
83
]
which gives a positive answer to Question
1.1
for abelian varieties of a given dimension
g
, i.e. the following set is finite:

\bigcup_{d}\mathrm{Shaf}_{\mathcal{A}_{g,d}}(K,S),

generalizing the aforementioned theorem of Faltings.
As for K3 surfaces, Y. She
[
74
]
established the unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture, strengthening André’s result:

Theorem 1.2

(She)
.

The following set is finite:

\mathrm{Shaf}_{\rm K3}(K,S)=\left\{X~\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a K3 surface over $K$}&\\
\hbox{\rm having good reduction outside $S$}&\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{K}.

The analogue of Zarhin’s trick for K3 surfaces also has its origin in Charles’ proof of the Tate conjecture
[
19
]
, as well as in Orr–Skorobogatov
[
66
]
.

More recently, the third author
[
76
]
proved an even stronger version of Theorem
1.2
by releasing the good reduction condition to a condition on unramified places of the second cohomology as Galois modules, hence establishing the so-called
cohomological (unpolarized) Shafarevich conjecture
for K3 surfaces.
He also established the analogous results for bielliptic surfaces
[
78
]
, Enriques surfaces
[
77
]
, and proper hyperbolic polycurves
[
61
]
(the last one, jointly with Nagamachi, generalizes
[
31
]
).

1.2.
Finiteness of hyper-Kähler varieties with bounded bad reduction

In this paper, we aim to generalize She’s result (Theorem
1.2
) to higher-dimensional analogues of K3 surfaces, which are hyper-Kähler varieties (see Section
2
for generalities on this type of varieties). We propose the following conjecture. As before, we fix a number field
K
and a finite set of places
S
. A smooth projective
K
-variety is called hyper-Kähler if the associated complex variety is hyper-Kähler (for an/any embedding of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
); see Section
2
.

Conjecture 1.3

(Unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties)
.

Given a positive integer
n
, there are only finitely many
K
-isomorphism classes of
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties defined over
K
and having good reduction outside
S
.

In this generality, Conjecture
1.3
seems out of reach in view of the topological difficulty that even the finiteness of deformation classes of complex hyper-Kähler manifolds in a given dimension is unknown. It is therefore natural to restrict ourselves to a given
deformation type
. Let
M
be a deformation type of complex hyper-Kähler manifolds (e.g.
M
can be
K3^{[n]}
,
\mathrm{Kum}_{n}
,
OG_{6}
, or
OG_{10}
). We say a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
is of deformation type
M
, if
X\times_{K}\Spec(\mathbb{C})
is so for some embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
; see Section
2.2
.

The following
Shafarevich set
is the central object of study in this paper:

(1.1)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(K,S)=\left\{X~\bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety over $K$ of deformation type $M$}\\
\hbox{\rm having good reduction outside $S$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{K}.

Generalizing the cohomological Shafarevich conjecture for K3 surfaces proposed by the third author in
[
76
]
, let us also consider the following
cohomological Shafarevich set
, which is larger than (
1.1
):

(1.2)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(K,S)=\left\{X~\bigg|\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety over $K$ of deformation type $M$ }&\\
\hbox{\rm with $\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ unramified outside $S$}&\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{K}.

Here, we say a
{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-module is unramified at a place
v
if the action of the inertia group
\mathrm{I}_{\bar{v}}
is trivial, where
\bar{v}
is any extension to
\overline{K}
of the valuation
v
.

Compared to the case of K3 surfaces, a new feature of higher-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties that causes substantial difficulties is the existence of non-isomorphic birational transformations among them. Birational isomorphisms between hyper-Kähler varieties are isomorphisms in codimension one and hence preserve (see Proposition
3.3
) the cohomological Shafarevich condition in (
1.2
) on the unramifiedness of the second cohomology. In particular,
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(K,S)
contains the entire
K
-birational class of any of its members. This motivates us to consider the so-called
Shafarevich set with essentially good reduction
, denoted by
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\ess}(K,S)
; see Definition
3.4
for the precise definition. Roughly speaking, a hyper-Kähler variety
X
defined over
K
has essentially good reduction at a place
v
if after a finite extension
K^{\prime}/K
that is unramified at
v
, we have a
K^{\prime}
-birational model of
X_{K^{\prime}}
that has good reduction.

The above three Shafarevich sets are related as follows:

(1.3)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(K,S)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\ess}(K,S)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(K,S).

The first main result of the paper is the following, which gives a positive answer to Conjecture
1.3
for all hyper-Kähler varieties,
upon fixing the deformation type
. The statement is in a more general setting: we allow finitely generated field extension
F
of
\mathbb{Q}
instead of just a number field
K
, the role of the ring of
S
-integers in
K
is played by a finitely generated normal domain
R
with fraction field
F
, and the good reduction condition is stipulated only for prime ideals of height 1. See Section
3
for the precise definitions of various Shafarevich sets. In the general notation, the previous
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(K,S)
corresponds to
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(K,\mathcal{O}_{K,S})
.

Theorem 1.4

(Unpolarized Shafarevich conjecture in a deformation class)
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra which is a normal integral domain with fraction field
F
. Let
M
be a deformation type of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}\neq 3
.
Then there are only finitely many
F
-birational isomorphism classes in
\mathrm{Shaf}^{\ess}_{M}(F,R)
.

If
b_{2}\geq 5
, then
\mathrm{Shaf}^{\ess}_{M}(F,R)
, and hence
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)
, is a finite set.

The finiteness of deformation classes of hyper-Kähler manifolds in a given dimension is unknown. Nevertheless, Huybrechts
[
29
]
proved such a finiteness result upon fixing the Beauville–Bogomolov form. This has been strengthened by Kamenova
[
39
]
in the following form:

Theorem 1.5

(Huyberchts, Kamenova)
.

There are only finitely many deformation classes of complex hyper-Kähler manifolds, with given Fujiki constant and given discriminant of the Beauville–Bogomolov form.

The definitions of the Fujiki constant and the discriminant, which are natural deformation invariants for hyper-Kähler varieties, are recalled in Section
2
.
Combining Theorem
1.5
with Theorem
1.4
, we obtain the following result in the direction of Conjecture
1.3
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of places of
K
.
For any
n,\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{Q}
, there are only finitely many
K
-isomorphism classes of
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties defined over
K
, with Fujiki constant
c
, discriminant of Beauville–Bogomolov form
\Delta
,
b_{2}\geq 5
, and with good reduction outside
S
.

The gap between Theorem
1.6
and Conjecture
1.3
can be resolved if there are only finitely many possibilities for Fujiki constants and discriminants of the Beauville–Bogomolov forms of hyper-Kähler manifolds in a given dimension.

Another new feature of hyper-Kähler varieties in dimension
>2
is that the action of the automorphism group on the second cohomology is not necessarily faithful (see Example
2.10
), which by
twisting

[
13
]
leads to the failure of the naive version of the cohomological generalization of the Shafarevich conjecture for (polarized or unpolarized) hyper-Kähler varieties in general, see Section
8.1
for counter-examples. On the other hand, we have the following result, where
(i)
says that passing to geometric isomorphism classes (hence ignoring the effect of twists) recovers the finiteness, and
(ii)
says that the nonfaithfulness of the action of automorphism group is the “only” obstruction to finiteness.

Theorem 1.7

(Cohomological Shafarevich conjecture)
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra which is a normal integral domain with fraction field
F
. Let
M
be a deformation type of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}\neq 3
.

(i)

There are only finitely many geometrically birational isomorphism classes in
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
.

If
b_{2}\geq 5
, then there are only finitely many geometric isomorphism classes in
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
.

(ii)

Suppose that for any hyper-Kähler variety in the deformation type
M
, the automorphism group acts faithfully on the second cohomology. Then there are only finitely many
F
-birational isomorphism classes in
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
.

If
b_{2}\geq 5
, then
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
is a finite set.

In fact, to remedy the nonfaithfulness of the action of the automorphism group on the second cohomology, we investigate in Section
8.2
a more general version of the cohomological Shafarevich conjecture by taking into account of (the unramifiedness of) cohomology groups of degree other than 2, and Theorem
1.7
is proved in the general form of Theorem
8.3
. As a consequence, we deduce in Corollary
8.4
that the full-degree cohomological Shafarevich conjecture holds for all hyper-Kähler varieties of known deformation type.

1.3.
Geometric finiteness of hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.4
is the construction of a
uniform Kuga–Satake map
(
[
66
]
,
[
74
]
, see Section
5.5
) and its arithmetic properties,
which allows us to show, as an intermediate step towards Theorem
1.4
, that there are only finitely many isometry classes of (geometric) Picard lattices of hyper-Kähler varieties arising from
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\ess}(F,R)
(see Theorem
7.4
).

More generally, we propose the following conjecture, inspired by yet another conjecture of Shafarevich formulated in
[
73
]
.

Conjecture 1.8

(Finiteness of geometric Picard lattices)
.

Let
d
be an integer and
M
a deformation type of hyper-Kähler manifolds. Then there are only finitely many isometry classes in the following set of lattices:

(1.4)

\bigg\{{\rm Pic}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\bigg|~\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$~is hyper-K\"{a}hler over a number field $K$ of degree $\leq d$ }&\\
\hbox{\rm$X_{\mathbb{C}}:=X\times_{K}\Spec(\mathbb{C})$ is of deformation type $M$ for some $K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}$}&\end{array}\bigg\}.

Here the Picard group is equipped with the restriction of the Beauville–Bogomolov quadratic form.

As a special case, if we fix the number field
K
in (
1.4
), the finiteness of isometry classes in (
1.4
) can be regarded as a strengthening of the Bombieri–Lang conjecture for moduli spaces of lattice-polarized hyper-Kähler varieties, which predicts that the
K
-rational points in a moduli space
\mathscr{F}
of lattice-polarized hyper-Kähler varieties are contained in the Noether–Lefschetz loci if
\mathscr{F}
is of “sufficiently” general type.

One could also speculate the more ambitious form without the restriction on deformation type. In the case of K3 surfaces, Conjecture
1.8
has been confirmed for K3 surfaces of CM type by Orr–Skorobogatov in
[
66
, Corollary B.1]
. In fact, they proved the following stronger result
[
66
, Theorem B]
:

Theorem 1.9

(Orr–Skorobogatov)
.

There are only finitely many geometric isomorphism classes of K3 surfaces of CM type which can be defined over a number field of a given degree.

As another application of the uniform Kuga–Satake construction, our second main result generalizes Theorem
1.9
to hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
M
a fixed deformation type of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}\geq 4
. Then there are only finitely many geometrically birational equivalence classes of hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type and of deformation type
M
which can be defined over a number field of degree
\leq d
.
When
b_{2}\geq 5
, there are only finitely many geometric isomorphism classes of hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type and of deformation type
M
which can be defined over a number field of degree
\leq d
.

Note that (geometrically) birational transformations preserve the (geometric) Picard lattice. In particular, Conjecture
1.8
holds for hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type:

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
M
a fixed deformation type of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}\geq 4
. There are only finitely many
isometry classes of the geometric Picard lattices of hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type and of deformation type
M
which can be defined over a number field of degree
\leq d
.

As a consequence, we get the uniform boundedness of Brauer groups for hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type.

Corollary 1.12
.

Fix a deformation type
M
of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}\geq 4
.
For any positive integer
d
, there exists a constant
N
such that

|\Br(X)/\Br_{0}(X)|<N
and
|\Br(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})^{{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/F)}|<N

for any hyper-Kähler variety
X
of CM type, of deformation type
M
, and defined over a number field
F
of degree at most
d
.

2.
Generalities on hyper-Kähler varieties

Hyper-Kähler varieties are higher-dimensional analogues of

K3
surfaces. They play a significant role in algebraic geometry since they form one type of building blocks of varieties with vanishing first Chern class, by the Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem (
[
7
,
10
]
).
We collect in this section some generalities on those varieties. See
[
7
,
28
,
25
]
for basic results and examples over the field of complex numbers.

Firstly, we fix the definition: a
hyper-Kähler
(or
irreducible symplectic
) variety over a field
k
of characteristic
0
is a geometrically connected smooth projective
k
-variety
X
such that it is geometrically simply connected
\pi_{1}^{\et}(X_{\bar{k}})=1
, and
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
is spanned by a nowhere degenerate algebraic
2-form. Note that the 2-form is automatically closed (hence symplectic) by the degeneration of the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence. Moreover,
\pi_{1}^{\et}(X_{\bar{k}})=1
implies that
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})=0
by étale Hurewicz theorem. Using Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem, it is easy to see that
X
is hyper-Kähler if and only if
X\times_{k}\Spec\mathbb{C}
is a complex hyper-Kähler manifold in the sense of
[
7
,
28
]
, for any embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
; see
[
9
, Lemma 3.1.3]
.

2.1.
Beauville–Bogomolov form

For a complex hyper-Kähler variety
X
(i.e.
k=\mathbb{C}
),
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
carries an integral, primitive quadratic form
q_{X}
, called the Beauville–Bogomolov (BB) form, which satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

q_{X}
is non-degenerate and of signature
(3,b_{2}(X)-3)
.

(2)

There exists a positive rational number
c_{X}
, called the
Fujiki constant
, such that
q_{X}^{n}(\alpha)=c_{X}\int_{X}\alpha^{2n}
for all classes
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, where
2n=\dim(X)
.

(3)

The Hodge decomposition
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{H}^{2,0}(X)\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X)
is orthogonal with respect to
q_{X}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.1
.

All known examples of complex hyper-Kähler manifolds have an even BB form, i.e.
\Lambda_{M}
is an even lattice. The evenness of
\Lambda_{M}
in general is unknown. Let us mention that there are examples of hyper-Kähler
orbifolds
with odd BB form
[
40
]
.

By Bogomolov–Tian–Todorov
[
11
]
, the deformation space of
X
is unobstructed and its smooth deformations remain hyper-Kähler. The property
(2)
ensures the deformation invariance of the Fujiki constant
c_{X}
and the lattice
\Lambda_{X}:=(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),q_{X})
. From now on, we assume that
k
is finitely generated.

For a hyper-Kähler variety
X
defined over a field
k
of characteristic zero, upon fixing an embedding
\iota\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, Artin’s comparison isomorphism

(2.1)

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X\times_{k,\iota}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}(1))\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))

allows us to transport the BB form
q_{X}
on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X\times_{\iota}\Spec\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Z}(1))
to a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-quadratic form:

q\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))\times\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}.

where
\bar{k}
denotes the algebraic closure of
k
in
\mathbb{C}
.
As shown in
[
9
, Lemma 4.2.1]
, this
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-quadratic form is independent of the choice of the embedding, which is called the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-BB form
of
X
. Similarly, the Fujiki constant
c_{X}
is defined to be the Fujiki constant of
X\times_{\iota}\Spec\mathbb{C}
, which is also independent of
\iota
.

2.2.
Deformation type and
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type

Definition 2.2

(Deformation type)
.

Let
M
be a fixed complex hyper-Kähler manifold.
Given an embedding
\iota\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we say that a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
k
is of (complex)
deformation type

M
with respect to
\iota
if the complex variety
X_{\mathbb{C}}:=X\times_{k,\iota}\Spec(\mathbb{C})
is deformation equivalent to
M
. We say a hyper-Kähler variety
X
is of deformation type
M
if it is so for some embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

So far, only a few deformation types have been discovered: in each even dimension
2n
, there are the
K3^{[n]}
-type and the generalized Kummer type
\operatorname{Kum}_{n}
constructed by Beauville
[
7
]
, and O’Grady constructed in
[
64
]
and
[
63
]
two other sporadic examples in dimension 6 and 10, called
OG_{6}
-type and
OG_{10}
-type respectively. Clearly, a hyper-Kähler variety can have at most finitely many deformation types. It is an interesting open question whether the deformation type is actually independent of the embedding
\iota\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

If
X
is of deformation type
M
, denoting
\Lambda_{M}
the lattice realized by the BB form on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and
c_{M}
its Fujiki constant, then as we mentioned above in Section
2.1
,
\Lambda_{M}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-BB form of
X
, and
c_{M}
is the Fujiki constant of
X
, regardless of the embedding
\iota
.

In
[
24
]
, the authors consider the moduli problem of polarized hyper-Kähler manifolds of fixed numerical type. This motivates us to introduce the following weaker notion of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-
numerical type
of hyper-Kähler varieties over general fields, which is better suited for our treatment when dealing with moduli spaces and period maps.

Definition 2.3

(
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type)
.

We fix a positive integer
n
.
Let
k
,
k^{\prime}
be fields of characteristic
0
, and
X
(resp.
X^{\prime}
) a
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler variety over
k
(resp.
k^{\prime}
).
We say that
X
and
X^{\prime}
are
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerically equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

g\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X^{\prime}_{\overline{k^{\prime}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

such that

g
is isometric with respect to the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-BB forms, and
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same Fujiki constant.

Remark 2.4
.

A
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type is the union of finitely many deformation types.
Indeed, if
X
and
Y
are of the same deformation type (i.e.
X_{\mathbb{C}}
and
Y_{\mathbb{C}}
are deformation equivalent), then by the definitions of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-BB form and Fujiki constant,
X
and
Y
clearly have the same
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type. Conversely, since there are only finitely many possible isometry classes of
\mathbb{Z}
-lattices of bounded rank and discriminant, fixing a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type
N
leaves only finitely many possibilities for the BB lattice of
X_{\mathbb{C}}
for any
X
of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type
N
. By Huybrechts
[
29
, Theorem 4.3]
, there are only finitely many possibilities for the deformation types.

2.3.
Polarization in family

Let
T
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme. A
family
of (projective) hyper-Kähler varieties over
T
means a proper smooth morphism of algebraic spaces
\mathfrak{X}\to T
such that every geometric fiber is a projective hyper-Kähler variety.

Given such a family of projective hyper-Kähler varieties

f\colon\mathfrak{X}\to T,

the relative Picard functor
{\rm Pic}_{X/T}
is represented by a separated algebraic space over
T
, denoted by the same notation. In particular, if
X
is a hyper-Kähler variety over a field
k
of characteristic zero, then the relative Picard functor
{\rm Pic}_{X/k}
is represented by a separated, smooth,
0
-dimensional scheme over
k
(cf.
[
14
, §8.4, Theorem 1]
). Let
G_{k}:={\rm Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
be the absolute Galois group of
k
.
Then the group
{\rm Pic}_{X/k}(k)
is identified with the subgroup

{\rm Pic}_{X/k}(\bar{k})^{G_{k}}\subset{\rm Pic}_{X/k}(\bar{k})={\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})
, hence is torsion free (since
{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})
is so). Denote
{\rm Pic}_{X/k}(k)
by
{\rm Pic}_{X}
in the sequel.

Definition 2.5

(cf.
[
5
,
69
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\to T
be a family of hyper-Kähler varieties over a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme
T
.
A
polarization
on
\mathfrak{X}/T
is a global section
\mathcal{H}\in{\rm Pic}_{\mathfrak{X}/T}(T)
, such that for every geometric point
t
of
T
, the fiber
\mathcal{H}_{t}\in{\rm Pic}_{\mathfrak{X}_{t}}
is an ample line bundle on
\mathfrak{X}_{t}
.

By the Leray–Serre spectral sequence and Hilbert’s Theorem 90, we get an exact sequence

0\to{\rm Pic}(X)\to{\rm Pic}_{X}\cong{\rm Pic}_{X/k}(\bar{k})^{G_{k}}\xrightarrow{\delta}\Br(k).

Thus
{\rm Pic}(X)\subset{\rm Pic}_{X}
as a finite index subgroup (see also
[
5
, Lemma 2.3.1]
). For each prime
\ell
, the
\ell
-adic cycle class map defines an embedding

(2.2)

c_{1}^{\ell}\colon{\rm Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))

whose image lies in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})^{G_{k}}
.
Putting all prime
l
together, we get

(2.3)

c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}(X)\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))^{G_{k}}.

The Kummer sequence induces the following short exact sequence: for any
n
,

(2.4)

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{G}_{m})/n\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mu_{n})\to\Br(X_{\bar{k}})[n]\to 0.

Passing to the projective limit, we obtain an embedding

(2.5)

{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)),

which is the first Chern class map for
X_{\bar{k}}
. Therefore, we can extend (
2.2
) to a homomorphism (keeping the same notation):

(2.6)

c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)),

whose image lies in the
G_{k}
-invariant part.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a number field
k
with
b_{2}>3
. Then the first Chern class map (
2.6
) induces an isomorphism:

c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))^{G_{k}}.

Proof.

When
b_{2}(X)>3
, André
[
5
, Theorem 1.6.1 (2)]
proved the Tate conjecture for divisors on polarized hyper-Kähler varieties over number fields, i.e., for each prime
\ell
, the first Chern class map induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-vector spaces:

c_{1}^{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\colon{\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{G_{k}}.

Hence
\rk({\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=\rk\left(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))^{G_{k}}\right)
. To conclude, it suffices to show that
{\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is saturated in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))
via
c_{1}
.

To this end, by the exponential sequence, the cokernel of the (complex) first Chern class map

(2.7)

c_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\colon{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})\cong{\rm Pic}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})

lies in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathbb{C}}})
, which is torsion-free. Thus
{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
is saturated.
The complex and the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
first Chern class maps (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) are compatible in the sense that the comparison isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

identifies the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-sublattices

(2.8)

c_{1}^{\ell}({\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=c_{1}({\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}}))\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}.

Therefore,
{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))
is saturated.
Since
{\rm Pic}_{X}\subseteq{\rm Pic}(X_{\bar{k}})
is also saturated by definition,
{\rm Pic}_{X}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{k}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))
is saturated.
∎

2.4.
Automorphisms and birational self-maps

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over a finitely generated field
k
of characteristic zero. For any field extension
k^{\prime}/k
, denote by
\Aut(X_{k^{\prime}})
the group of
k^{\prime}
-automorphisms of
X_{k^{\prime}}
and
{\rm Bir}(X_{k^{\prime}})
the group of
k^{\prime}
-birational self-maps. The group
\Aut(X_{k^{\prime}})
is discrete.
For an embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the action of
\Aut(X_{\mathbb{C}})
on the second cohomology preserves the Beauville–Bogomolov form. Define

(2.9)

\Aut_{0}(X_{\mathbb{C}}):=\ker\left(\Aut(X_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z}))\right);

and its subgroup of automorphisms acting trivially on the whole cohomology:

(2.10)

\Aut_{00}(X_{\mathbb{C}}):=\ker\left(\Aut(X_{\mathbb{C}})\to{\rm GL}(\mathrm{H}^{*}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}))\right).

Theorem 2.7

(Huybrechts
[
28
, Proposition 9.1]
, Hassett–Tschinkel
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

The group
\Aut_{0}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
is a finite group and it is deformation invariant.

Similarly, we have the following result concerning
\Aut_{00}
in the projective setting. The result is probably known to experts, but we cannot locate a reference.

Proposition 2.8
.

The group
\Aut_{00}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
is invariant along a polarized deformation family of hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Proof.

We are in the complex setting and drop the subscript
\mathbb{C}
here.
The first step is similar to Hassett–Tschinkel
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
: given a complex hyper-Kähler manifold
X
, the group
\Aut_{00}(X)
acts equivariantly on the universal deformation over the Kuranishi space
\mathfrak{X}\to\operatorname{Def}(X)
. Since the action on

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,T_{X})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\subset\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})

is the identity, the action is the identity on
\operatorname{Def}(X)
, i.e. it is a fiberwise action. This shows that for any family of hyper-Kähler manifolds
\mathfrak{X}\to B
, the group scheme map
\pi\colon\Aut_{00}(\mathfrak{X}/B)\to B
is a local homeomorphism. We are reduced to show the universal closedness of
\pi
for a projective family
\mathfrak{X}/B
; in other words, given a family of automorphisms
\phi\in\Aut_{0}(\mathfrak{X}/B)
, if
\phi_{t}\in\Aut_{00}(X_{t})
for all
t\neq 0
, then the specialization
\phi_{0}\in\Aut_{0}(X_{0})
acts trivially on the whole cohomology ring. To this end, we equip the family
\mathfrak{X}/B
with a relatively ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, then
\mathcal{L}
and
\phi^{*}(\mathcal{L})
makes
\phi
a polarized isomorphism over
B
. By Matsusaka–Mumford
[
56
, Corollary 1]
, the specialization (as cycles) of the graph
\Gamma_{\phi_{t}}
is the graph
\Gamma_{\phi_{0}}
. As
[\Gamma_{\phi_{t}}]=[\Delta_{X_{t}}]
for all
t\neq 0
, we have that
[\Gamma_{\phi_{0}}]=[\Delta_{X_{0}}]
, i.e.
\phi_{0}
acts trivially on
\mathrm{H}^{*}(X_{0},\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

We do not know whether
\Aut_{00}
is a deformation invariant for hyper-Kähler manifolds. The above argument only shows that for a family of hyper-Kähler manifolds,
\Aut_{00}
forms an open subgroup scheme of the locally constant group scheme
\Aut_{0}
. It could happen that very general non-projective hyper-Kähler manifolds have less automorphisms acting trivially on the whole cohomology.

Example 2.10
.

The groups
\Aut_{0}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\Aut_{00}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
for complex hyper-Kähler varieties
X_{\mathbb{C}}
of known deformation types have been worked out:

(1)

K3^{[n]}
-type:
\Aut_{0}
is trivial by Beauville
[
6
, Proposition 10]
.

(2)

Generalized Kummer type
\operatorname{Kum}_{n}
:

\Aut_{0}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)\mathbb{Z})^{4}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

by Boissière, Nieper-Wisskirchen, and Sarti
[
12
, Corollary 5]
. Oguiso proved in
[
65
, Theorem 1.3]

that
\Aut_{00}
vanishes.

(3)

OG_{6}
-type: Mongardi and Wandel proved that
\Aut_{0}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{8}
in
[
60
, Theorem 5.2]
, and that
\Aut_{00}
vanishes in
[
60
, Remark 6.9]
. Note that they showed the vanishing of
\Aut_{00}
for crepant resolutions of the projective
OG_{6}
singular moduli spaces. But by Proposition
2.8
,
[
59
, Example 3.18]
and the density of projective members in the moduli space, we can conclude the vanishing for all
OG_{6}
-type manifolds; cf.
[
65
, Section 4]
.

(4)

OG_{10}
-type:
\Aut_{0}
is trivial by Mongardi and Wandel
[
60
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Moreover, we can consider the automorphism group of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties. For a polarized pair
(X,H)
, we define

\Aut(X_{k^{\prime}},H_{k^{\prime}})=\{f\in\Aut(X_{k^{\prime}})|~f^{\ast}H=H\},

which is clearly finite. Similarly, one could also consider the group
{\rm Bir}(X_{k^{\prime}},H_{k^{\prime}})
of
k^{\prime}
-birational self maps of
(X_{k^{\prime}},H_{k^{\prime}})
. However, by a result of Fujiki
[
23
, Thm 4.4]
, any birational map preserving an ample class is an isomorphism, i.e.
{\rm Bir}(X_{k^{\prime}},H_{k^{\prime}})=\Aut(X_{k^{\prime}},H_{k^{\prime}})
.

2.5.
Wall divisors and ample cone

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
k
of a given deformation type.
Let
N^{1}(X)={\rm Pic}(X)
be the Néron–Severi lattice of
X
, equipped with the Beauville–Bogomolov form. Inside the
Néron–Severi space

N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes\mathbb{R},

we have the positive cone
{\rm Pos}(X)
defined as the connected component of
\{v\in N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}~|~v^{2}>0\}
containg the ample classes, and the nef cone
{\rm Nef}(X)
defined as the closure of the ample cone
{\rm Amp}(X)
.
The
birational ample cone
, denoted by
{\rm BA}(X)
, is defined as the union

\bigcup\limits_{f}f^{\ast}{\rm Amp}(Y),

where
f
runs through all
k
-birational isomorphisms from
X
to other hyper-Kähler varieties over
k
. It is known that the closure of
{\rm BA}(X)
is the movable cone
\overline{{\rm Mov}}(X)
(see
[
27
,
79
]
).

Definition 2.11
.

(
[
2
]
,
[
58
]
)
Let
D
be a divisor class on a hyper-Kähler variety
X_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
D
is called a
wall divisor
or a
monodromy birationally minimal
(MBM) class if
q(D)<0
and

\Phi(D^{\perp})\cap{\rm BA}(X_{\mathbb{C}})=\emptyset,

for any
Hodge monodromy operator
\Phi
. We denote the set of wall divisors on
X_{\mathbb{C}}
by
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Remark 2.12
.

There are other equivalent definitions for wall divisors/MBM classes. In
[
2
]
, the MBM classes are defined as certain classes in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
for all
X
in a given deformation type and the collection of MBM classes is monodromy invariant. The notion in Definition
2.11
corresponds to those MBM classes that are of Hodge type
(1,1)
on
X
. See
[
2
]
for more details.

The collection of wall divisors are deformation invariant where they stay of
(1,1)
-type. Almost by construction, we have the following connection between wall divisors and the ample cone of hyper-Kähler varieties.

Lemma 2.13

(cf.
[
2
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
58
]
)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety and
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
be the collection of wall divisors of
X_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})^{\perp}
be the union of all orthogonal complement to all
D\in\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then the ample cone
{\rm Amp}(X)
is a connected component of
\mathrm{Pos}(X)\backslash\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})^{\perp}
.

Proof.

For complex hyper-Kähler varieties, the result is a reformulation of the definition of wall divisors. Now for
X
defined over
k
, the Néron–Severi space
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is the Galois-invariant space of
N^{1}(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{R}}
. Clearly, a divisor is ample on
X
if and only if it is ample when pulled back to
X_{\mathbb{C}}
. Therefore,
{\rm Amp}(X)
is the intersection of
{\rm Amp}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
with
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
{\rm Amp}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
is a connected component of the complement of
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})^{\perp}
in
N^{1}(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbb{R}}
, and it is convex,
{\rm Amp}(X)
must also be a connected component of the complement of
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})^{\perp}
inside
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

In
[
3
]
, Verbitsky and Amerik proved the following boundedness results on wall divisor classes and confirmed the Kawamata–Morrison cone conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties (see also
[
4
]
).

Theorem 2.14

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.5, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth hyper-Kähler variety with
b_{2}\geq 5
over a field
k
of characteristic zero. Then the Beauville–Bogomolov squares of the elements in
\mathcal{W}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
are bounded (from below).

Using Markman and Yoshioka’s argument in
[
53
, Corollary 2.5]
and the movable cone conjecture for complex hyper-Kähler varieties (proved by Markman
[
52
]
), the boundedness of squares of wall divisors above allow Amerik and Verbitsky to conclude the finiteness of the set of birational hyper-Kähler models of a given hyper-Kähler variety over the complex numbers. Over an arbitrary field of characteristic zero, using the method of Bright–Logan–van Luijk
[
15
]
for K3 surfaces, the third author
[
79
, Theorem 4.2.7]
generalized Markman’s work
[
52
]
and deduced in the similar way the finiteness of birational hyper-Kähler models:

Theorem 2.15

(Finiteness of birational models)
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0. Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety defined over
k
with
b_{2}\geq 5
.
Then there are only finitely many
k
-isomorphism classes of hyper-Kähler varieties defined over
k
which are
k
-birational to
X
.

3.
Various Shafarevich sets of hyper-Kähler varieties

In this section, we first define in a more general setting of the Shafarevich sets (
1.1
), (
1.2
) mentioned in the introduction, and then introduce the notion of essentially good reduction and the corresponding Shafarevich set. Throughout the section, let
R
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra which is a normal integral domain with fraction field
F
. Fix positive integers
n
and
d
. Let
M
be a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type or a deformation type of
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties (Section
2.2
); these two choices do not make much difference thanks to Remark
2.4
, and we will make use of both.

3.1.
Polarized and unpolarized Shafarevich sets and their cohomological versions

The Shafarevich set (
1.1
) is generalized as follows:

Definition 3.1

(Polarized and unpolarized Shafarevich sets)
.

We define the following
Shafarevich set
and
polarized Shafarevich set
:

(3.1)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)=\left\{X\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$X$ has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F};

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}(F,R)=\left\{(X,H)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$(X,H)$ is a polarized hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{$(H)^{2n}=d$ ,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$X$ has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F}.

Here,
X
has
good reduction
at
\mathfrak{p}
means there is a regular algebraic space
\mathfrak{X}
which admits a smooth and proper morphism to the spectrum of the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
\mathfrak{X}_{F}\cong X
as
F
-schemes.

Following
[
76
]
, we define the following
cohomological Shafarevich set
and its polarized version:

Definition 3.2

(Cohomological Shafarevich sets)
.

Let notation be as above.

(3.2)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\textrm{hom}}(F,R)=\left\{X~\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$ for some $\ell\neq\operatorname{char}(k(\mathfrak{p}))$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F};

(3.3)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}^{\textrm{hom}}(F,R)=\left\{(X,H)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$(X,H)$ is a polarized hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{$(H)^{2n}=d$ ,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$ for some $\ell\neq\operatorname{char}(k(\mathfrak{p}))$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F}.

Here,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is
unramified
at
\mathfrak{p}
if the inertia group acts on it trivially. For
n=1
(K3 surfaces), the unramifiedness condition in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) is independent of the choice of
\ell\neq\operatorname{char}(R/\mathfrak{p})
, see
[
76
, Section 5]
. Unfortunately, we do not know the
\ell
-independence of unramifiedness in higher dimensions. Therefore, we also consider the following slightly more restrictive version of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
): for a prime number
\ell\in R^{\times}
:

(3.4)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}(F,R)=\left\{X~\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F};

(3.5)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}(F,R)=\left\{(X,H)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$(X,H)$ is a polarized hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{$(H)^{2n}=d$ ,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F}.

Note that the unpolarized Shafarevich sets
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)
admits a natural surjection (forgetting the polarization) from the union of polarized Shafarevich sets:

(3.6)

\coprod\limits_{d}\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}(F,R)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)

Similarly for the cohomological Shafarevich sets
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}
and
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}
.

By the smooth and proper base change theorems (see
[
1
, Théorème XII.5.1]
in the scheme setting and
[
49
]
for the more general setting), having good reduction implies the unramifiedness. Therefore,

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R);

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R).

The difference between
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)
and
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
is partially explained by the following observation:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be hyper-Kähler varieties defined over
F
. Suppose that
X
and
Y
are birationally isomorphic over
F
. Then
X\in\mathrm{Shaf}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{M}(F,R)
if and only if
Y\in\mathrm{Shaf}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{M}(F,R)
.

Proof.

For any height 1 prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, let
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the completion of
F
at
\mathfrak{p}
.
The birational isomorphism between
X
and
Y
is an isomorphism in codimension 1, hence we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(Y_{\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))

as
{\rm Gal}(\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/F_{\mathfrak{p}})
-modules for all
\ell
and
\mathfrak{p}
. It follows that
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(Y_{\bar{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

In other words, the unramifiedness condition can not distinguish between birationally isomorphic members in
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R)
. This suggests our approach towards the Shafarevich conjecture: on one hand, we have the finiteness of isomorphism classes within a birational isomorphism class (this is guaranteed by
[
79
]
, recalled above as Theorem
2.15
); on the other hand, we study the finiteness of birational isomorphism classes in various Shafarevich sets, which is the main goal of the paper, accomplished in Section
7
).

3.2.
Shafarevich sets of hyper-Kähler varieties with essentially good reduction

To take into account of the birational transformations, we introduce the following notion of
essentially good reduction
of hyper-Kähler varieties, which roughly means having good reduction after birational transformations over an unramified base change.

Definition 3.4

(Essentially good reduction)
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
F
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a height
1
prime ideal of
\Spec R
.
We say
X
has
essentially good reduction
at
\mathfrak{p}
if
there exists a finite étale extension
S
of the completion
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to the maximal ideal, such that there exists a smooth proper algebraic space
\mathcal{Y}
over
S
whose generic fiber
\mathcal{Y}_{\Frac(S)}
is a hyper-Kähler variety that is
\Frac(S)
-birationally isomorphic to
X_{\Frac(S)}
.

Remark 3.5
.

The requirement on base change is meaningful: already in dimension 2, there are examples of K3 surfaces over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which does not admit good reduction, but admits good reduction after a finite unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(see
[
48
, Theorem 1.6]
).

Definition 3.6

(Essential Shafarevich sets)
.

Let notation be as above.

(3.7)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\ess}(F,R)=\left\{X~\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$X$ is a hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$X$ has essentially good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F}

(3.8)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}^{\ess}(F,R)=\left\{(X,H)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$(X,H)$ is a polarized hyper-K\"{a}hler variety of type $M$ over $F$,}&\\
\hbox{$(H)^{2n}=d$ ,}&\\
\hbox{\rm for any height 1 prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R$, }&\\
\hbox{\rm$X$ has essentially good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$}\end{array}\right\}/\cong_{F}

By definition, we have

(3.9)

\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{\ess}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}(F,R)\subset\mathrm{Shaf}_{M}^{{\rm hom}}(F,R),

and similarly for polarized Shafarevich sets.
The difference between the middle two sets is partially explained in Section
8
.

4.
Moduli of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties

In this section, we review the moduli theory of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties. A good reference is Bindt’s recent PhD thesis
[
9
]
.

4.1.
Moduli stacks of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties

Given positive integers
n
and
d
, consider the groupoid fibration
\mathscr{F}_{d}\to(\mathtt{Sch}/\mathbb{Q})
of
2n
-dimensional polarized hyper-Kähler varieties of degree
d
. Let us write it in the form of a moduli functor

\mathscr{F}_{d}\colon(\mathtt{Sch}/\mathbb{Q})^{op}\to\mathtt{Groupoids}

defined as follows: for any
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme
T
,

(4.1)

\mathscr{F}_{d}(T)=\left\{(f\colon\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow T,\xi)~\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbox{\rm$\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow T$ is a family of $2n$-dimensional hyper-K\"{a}hler varieties,}\\
\hbox{\rm$\xi\in{\rm Pic}_{\mathfrak{X}/T}(T)$ is a polarization with $(\xi_{t}^{2n})=d$ for all $t\in T(\bar{k})$}\end{array}\right\}

(see Section
2.3
for the notion of polarization), and for any morphism
T_{1}\to T_{2}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-schemes, the functor
\mathscr{F}_{d}(T_{2})\to\mathscr{F}_{d}(T_{1})
is given by pulling back families.
Here, an isomorphism between
(\mathfrak{X}_{1}\to T,\xi_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{X}_{2}\to T,\xi_{2})
in the groupoid
\mathscr{F}_{d}(T)
is a Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

together with an isomorphism
\psi^{*}(\xi_{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}\xi_{1}
in the groupoid
{\rm Pic}_{\mathfrak{X}_{1}/T}(T)
.

Proposition 4.1

(Bindt)
.

The moduli functor
\mathscr{F}_{d}
is represented by a separated smooth Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
, still denoted by
\mathscr{F}_{d}
.

Proof.

[
9
, Theorem 3.3.2, Lemma 3.3.9]
shows that
\mathscr{F}_{d}
is a separated smooth Deligne–Mumford stack locally of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
. To show that it is of finite type, applying Kollár–Matsusaka
[
44
]
, since
(\xi^{2n})=d
is fixed, there are only finitely many possibilities for the Hilbert polynomial. Hence
\mathscr{F}_{d}
is of finite type.
∎

We shall introduce the moduli stack
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
of
oriented
polarized hyper-Kähler spaces of degree
d
, as a finite covering of
\mathscr{F}_{d}
.

Definition 4.2

(Orientation)
.

Let
T
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme, and let
f\colon\mathfrak{X}\to T
be a family of hyper-Kähler varieties with
b_{2}>3
(Section
2.3
). An
orientation
on
\mathfrak{X}/T
is an isomorphism of sheaves over
S_{\et}
:

(4.2)

\omega\colon\underline{\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\det R^{2}_{\et}f_{\ast}\mu_{4}.

Such a pair
(\mathfrak{X}/S,\omega)
is called a
family of oriented hyper-Kähler varieties
over
T
.

The following observation in
[
9
, Lemma 4.3.2]
shows that the “orientation” defined here is equivalent to that in Taelman
[
75
]
, at least over a normal base.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
S
be a normal scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
. For any family of oriented hyper-Kähler varieties
(f\colon\mathfrak{X}\to S,\omega)
there are unique isomorphisms of lisse
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-local systems

\omega_{\et}\colon\underline{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\det R^{2}_{\et}f_{*}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)

on
S
and

\omega_{\operatorname{an}}\colon\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\det R_{\operatorname{an}}^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}(1)

on
S_{\mathbb{C}}
,
whose reductions on
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
is
\omega
and
\omega_{\et}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}=\omega_{\operatorname{an}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We denote by
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
the moduli stack of families of oriented polarized hyper-Kähler varieties of degree
d
. Clearly, the natural forgetful functor
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}\to\mathscr{F}_{d}
is finite and étale of degree
2
by Lemma
4.3
.
Thus the stack
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
, by Proposition
4.1
.

Remark 4.4
.

For K3 surfaces, the moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{d}
can be defined over
\mathbb{Z}
and is smooth over
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2d}]
. It is natural to ask whether the same assertion holds for higher dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties. This is known to be true for some families, such as the Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds. However, this is a rather difficult problem in general because the deformation theory and even the “right” definition of hyper-Kähler varieties over mixed characteristic fields are unclear at present, see Yang
[
82
]
.

Different from the case of K3 surfaces, the moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{d}
is not necessarily (geometrically) connected.
In practice, as there are only finitely many connected components, we may work with a connected component of the moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{d}
or
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
each time.

Remark 4.5
.

It is also natural to consider the moduli stack of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties of degree
d
of a given deformation type, which occurs as a union of some geometric connected components of
\mathscr{F}_{d}
. However, we do not know whether such stack is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, as it is unclear to us whether the hyper-Kähler varieties in a connected component of
\mathscr{F}_{d}
are deformation equivalent. Instead, one can consider the moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{M,d}
of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties of a given
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-numerical type
M
(Section
2.2
), this will be a substack of
\mathscr{F}_{d}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
as
M
is stable under the
{\rm Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-action. In this way, we can view
\mathrm{Shaf}_{M,d}(F,R)
as a subset of isomorphism classes in
\mathscr{F}_{M,d}(F)
.

4.2.
Level structures

Let
\mathscr{F}_{d}^{{\dagger}}
be a (scheme-theoretic) connected component of
\mathscr{F}_{d}
. Choose a point
(X_{0},H_{0})\in\mathscr{F}_{d}^{{\dagger}}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
\Lambda
be the BB lattice of
X_{0}
. Let
\Lambda_{h}
be the orthogonal complement of
h=c_{1}(H_{0})
in
\Lambda
.
For any geometric point
(X,H)
in
\mathscr{F}_{d}^{{\dagger}}(\mathbb{C})
, there is an isomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-lattices

\phi\colon\Lambda\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

such that
\phi(h)=c_{1}(H)
by the connectedness of
\mathscr{F}_{d}^{\dagger}
(cf.
[
9
, Lemma 4.5.1]
).

Following the work in
[
69
]
and
[
8
]
, we write
G={\rm SO}(\Lambda_{h,{\mathbb{Q}}})
and let
K\to G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be a continuous group homomorphism from a profinite group, with compact open image and finite kernel. We say that
K\to G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, or simply
K
, is
admissible
if the image of every element of
K
can be viewed as an isometry of
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{A}_{f}
fixing
h
and stabilizing
\Lambda_{h}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Recall that there is an injection

(4.3)

K_{h}\coloneqq\left\{g\in{\rm SO}(\Lambda)(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\;|\;g(h)=h\right\}\rightarrow G(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

By definition,
K_{h}
is admissible, and
K
is admissible if and only if its image in
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
lies in
K_{h}
.

Consider
\mathcal{J}_{h}
, the sheafification of the
presheaf of sets on
(\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}^{{\dagger}}_{d})_{\et}
, with sections over a connected
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme
T
given by

(4.4)

(f\colon\mathfrak{X}\to T,\xi;\omega)\rightsquigarrow\left\{\alpha\colon\Lambda\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(\mathfrak{X}_{\bar{t}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))\;\middle|\;\begin{matrix}\text{$\alpha$ is an isometry}\\
\text{such that $\alpha(h)=\widehat{c}_{1}(\xi_{\bar{t}})$}\\
\text{and $\det(\alpha)=\omega_{\et}$}.\end{matrix}\right\}^{\pi_{1}^{\et}(T,\bar{t})}

Here
\widehat{c}_{1}
is the product of all
\ell
-adic first Chern class maps and the action of
\pi^{\et}_{1}(T,\bar{t})
is via the target of
\alpha
.

By construction, any admissible subgroup
K
naturally acts on
\mathcal{J}_{h}(T)
(via the source of each
\alpha
). Denote
K\backslash\mathcal{J}_{h}
for the quotient of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
by the left-action of
K
, which is well-defined in the category of étale sheaves on
(\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d})_{\et}^{{\dagger}}
.

Definition 4.6
.

A
K
-level structure
on a polarized family of hyper-Kähler varieties
f\colon\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow T
is a section over
T
of the étale sheaf
K\backslash\mathcal{J}_{h}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K}
be the moduli stack of oriented polarized hyper-Kähler varieties of degree
d
with a
K
-level structure. It can be viewed as a finite étale cover of the original moduli stack (cf.
[
50
, Proposition 3.11]
), i.e.,

Proposition 4.7
.

The stack
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K}^{{\dagger}}
is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack and the forgetful map

\pi_{d,K}\colon\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K}^{{\dagger}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}^{{\dagger}}

is finite and étale.

For any field
F
of characteristic zero, the objects in
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K}^{{\dagger}}(F)
are those
(X,H,\omega;\alpha)
such that

•

(X,H,\omega)
is an oriented polarized family of hyper-Kähler varieties over
F
, and

•

\alpha
is a
K
-level structure
\alpha\colon\Lambda\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\bar{F}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))
satisfying

\alpha(\rho)({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F))\subseteq K,

where
\rho\colon{\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F)\to\Aut{(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1))}
is the Galois representation, and
\alpha(\rho)(\sigma)=\alpha^{-1}\circ\rho(\sigma)\circ\alpha
for any
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F)
.

In this paper, we mainly consider the following level structures on hyper-Kähler varieties.

Definition 4.8
.

(1)

The
full-level-
m
structure
is defined by the admissible group

K_{h,m}\coloneqq\left\{g\in{\rm SO}(\Lambda)(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\big|g(h)=h,g\equiv 1~\bmod m\right\}\to G(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

(2)

As in
[
70
]
, we define the
spin level structures
as follows:
Let
{\rm Cl}^{+}(\Lambda_{h})
be the even Clifford algebra of
\Lambda_{h,\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let

{\rm GSpin}(\Lambda_{h})(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})={\rm GSpin}(\Lambda_{h})(\mathbb{A}_{f})\cap{\rm Cl}^{+}(\Lambda_{h}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}),

and

K_{m}^{\operatorname{sp}}=\left\{g\in{\rm GSpin}(\Lambda_{h})(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\big|g\equiv 1~\bmod m\right\}.

Recall that one has an adjoint representation

\ad\colon{\rm GSpin}(\Lambda_{h})\to G

defined by
\ad(x)=(v\mapsto xvx^{-1})
. The spin level-
m
structure is the map

K_{m}^{\operatorname{sp}}\xrightarrow{\ad}G(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

We set
K^{\ad}_{m}\subseteq G(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
to be the image
K_{m}^{\operatorname{sp}}
under the adjoint representation: It is an open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
.

For the following usage, we denote by
K^{\ad}_{L,m}
and
K^{\operatorname{sp}}_{L,m}
the corresponding level structures for a different lattice
L
.

It could happen that the action of the automorphism group of a hyper-Kähler variety is not faithful on the second cohomology (Example
2.10
), hence
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K}
is not represented by a scheme (but only by a stack) even when
K
is very small, but we have the following remedy.

Corollary 4.9
.

If the automorphism group of a very general member of hyper-Kähler varieties parametrized by
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
is trivial, then
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K_{h,m}}^{{\dagger}}
is represented by a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme for
m\geq 3
, still denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{d,K_{h,m}}^{{\dagger}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any geometric point
(X,\xi,\omega;\alpha)
in
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d,K^{\ad}_{m}}^{{\dagger}}(\mathbb{C})
has only trivial automorphism. Under our assumption, this follows from the fact the automorphism group of
X
acts faithfully on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}(1))
and any finite order automorphism of the pair
(\mathrm{P}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}(1)),\alpha)
is trivial for
m\geq 3
(cf.
[
69
, Lemma 1.5.12]
).
∎

5.
Arithmetic period map and uniform Kuga–Satake

A standard approach to study the moduli space
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
introduced in the previous section is via the period map, which serves in a canonical way as a connection between
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{d}
and the Shimura varieties of orthogonal type.

5.1.
Orthogonal Shimura varieties

We first recall the notion of Shimura varieties attached to a lattice. Let
L
be the lattice over
\mathbb{Z}
of signature
(2,n)
with
n\geq 1
and let
G={\rm SO}(L_{\mathbb{Q}})
be the special orthogonal group scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
associated with
L_{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Consider the pair
(G,D)
, where
D
is the space of oriented negative definite planes in
L_{\mathbb{R}}
:

D={\rm SO}(2,n)/{\rm SO}(2)\times{\rm SO}(n)

where
{\rm SO}(2,n)={\rm SO}(L)(\mathbb{R})
.
To any admissible level structure
K\to G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, one can associate a Shimura stack
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)
, which is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{Q}
. When
K
is neat,
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)
is a smooth quasi-projective variety. Its complex points can be identified with the double coset quotient stack:

(5.1)

\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)(\mathbb{C})=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash D\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K.

The pair
(G,D)
is called the Shimura data.
It is well-known that the reflex field of the Shimura datumn
(G,D)
is equal to
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)
be the canonical model of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)(\mathbb{C})
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
75
]
and
[
9
]
, the notation
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}[G,D]
is used for Shimura stacks in order to distinguish from the classical Shimura varieties. For ease of notation, we will use the classical notation to denote the Shimura stack.

For simplicity, we denote by
K_{L}
the
discriminant kernel
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, which is the largest subgroup of
G(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
that acts trivially on the discriminant of
L
. Its
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-component is just the image of
{\rm GSpin}(L)(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for
p\geq 3
.
In particular, we have

K^{\ad}_{L,m}\subseteq K_{L}

as compact open subgroup for
m\geq 3
,
where
K^{\ad}_{L,m}
is defined in Definition
4.8
.
We write

\mathrm{Sh}(L)=\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{L}}(G,D)

for the orthogonal Shimura stack with level
K_{L}
, and

\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(L)=\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,D)

for any open compact subgroup
K\subset K_{L}
.
If
L
contains a hyperbolic lattice, the Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}(L)(\mathbb{C})=\Gamma_{L}\backslash D
is irreducible and
\Gamma_{L}
is the largest subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Z})
that acts trivially on the discriminant (cf.
[
50
, (4.1)]
). For any
m\geq 3
, there is a finite cover

\mathrm{Sh}_{K^{\ad}_{L,m}}(L)\to\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{L}}(L)

of degree
[K_{L}:K^{\ad}_{L,m}]
.

Let
\underline{L}
be the local system on
\mathrm{Sh}(L)(\mathbb{C})
attached to the tautological representation
L
of
\operatorname{O}(L)
. The non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
L
gives rise to an injective map of local systems

\underline{L}\rightarrow\underline{L}^{\vee}.

The finite local system
\underline{L}^{\vee}/\underline{L}
with its
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
-valued quadratic form is canonically isomorphic to the locally constant sheaf
\underline{\disc(L)}
over
\mathrm{Sh}(L)(\mathbb{C})
.

The Shimura stack
\mathrm{Sh}(L)
has the following modular description (cf.
[
57
, §3]
or
[
9
, Lemma 4.4.4]
).

Proposition 5.2
.

The groupoid
\mathrm{Sh}(L)(S)
is the solution of the moduli problem of classifying tuples
(H,q,\xi,\omega,\alpha_{K_{L}})
, where

•

H
is a variation of
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures over
S
whose fibers are Hodge lattices of K3-type with signature
(3,m)
;

•

q\colon H\times H\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is a bilinear form of variation of Hodge structures;

•

\xi
is a global section of
H
of type
(0,0)
such that
q(\xi,\xi)>0
;

•

\omega\colon\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\to\det(H)
is an isomorphism of local systems;

•

\alpha_{K_{L}}
is a
K_{L}
-level structure on
(H,q,\xi)
;

such that for any point
s\in S
there is a rational Hodge isometry
\beta_{s}\colon H_{s}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}
such that
\beta_{s}(\xi_{s})=h
and preserving the determinant.

5.2.
Integral model of Shimura varieties.

Keep the same notation as in the previous section. We recall some results on the existence of integral canonical models of the orthogonal Shimura varieties
\mathrm{Sh}(L)
.

We refer to
[
51
, Definition 4.2]
for the definition of (smooth) integral canonical model. Among the requirements of this definition, the most important one for our usage is the smooth extension property. For the convenience of readers, we record it here.

Definition 5.3
.

A (pro)-scheme
\mathscr{S}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is with
(smooth) extension property
if for any regular and locally-healthy (formally smooth) scheme
\mathscr{X}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, any morphism
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathscr{S}
can be extended to
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{S}
.

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose the discriminant group
\disc(L\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{N}])
is cyclic. Then the Shimura varieties
\mathrm{Sh}(L)
admits a canonical regular integral models
\mathscr{S}_{L}
over
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2N}]
. Moreover, for any neat
K\subset K_{L}
such that the
p
-primary component
K_{p}=K_{L,p}
for some
p\nmid 2N
, there is a finite extension

\mathscr{S}_{L,K}\to\mathscr{S}_{L}.

étale on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

Proof.

For any
p\nmid 2N
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-lattice
L\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is with cyclic discriminant group. Let
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be the

6.

As an intermediate step towards the Shafarevich conjecture for polarized hyper-Kähler varieties, we prove, in Proposition

below, the finiteness of twists/models of polarized hyper-Kähler varieties admitting essentially good reductions.
We will make use of results in Appendix

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

The relative canonical sheaf

\mathcal{X}_{s}
is a principal Cartier divisor of
\mathcal{X}
.
Therefore, the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is a smooth proper algebraic space over
k(s)
with a trivial canonical sheaf.
Suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{X}_{s}
were ruled.
Take a normal projective variety
V
over

\mathcal{X}_{s}
.
By using Chow’s lemma and taking elimination of indeterminacies,

over

We define a Weil divisor

We also put

8.
Cohomological Shafarevich conjecture

In this section, we give examples where
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M}(F,S)
, even
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M,d}(F,S)
is infinite. Hence the naive cohomological generalization of the Shafarevich conjecture fails in general. We propose a remedy by taking into account of cohomology of degrees other than 2.

8.1.
Failure of the (second) cohomological Shafarevich conjecture

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety defined over a number field
F
and
H
is a polarization of degree
d
. There is a bijection between the set (assuming nonempty) of all
F
-forms of
X_{\bar{F}}
and the Galois cohomology

(8.1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F),\Aut(X_{\bar{F}})),

via the construction of twisting
[
13
]
. Since
\Aut(X_{\bar{F}})
is finitely generated by Cattaneo–Fu
[
17
]
, up to replacing
F
by a finite extension, we can assume that
{\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F)
acts trivially on
\Aut(X_{\bar{F}})
.

Recall from Section
2.4
that

\Aut_{0}(X)\coloneqq\ker\left(\Aut(X)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))\right).

As a result,
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F),\Aut_{0}(X_{\bar{F}}))
is identified with a subset of (
8.1
). If
X
is in
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M,d}(F,S)
, then twisting by distinct elements in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F),\Aut_{0}(X_{\bar{F}}))
gives rise to different
F
-forms of
(X_{\bar{F}},H_{\bar{F}})
(resp.
X_{\bar{F}}
), while the unramifiedness condition on the second cohomology is preserved by construction, i.e. all those
F
-forms are in
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M,d}(F,S)
(resp.
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M}(F,S)
). If
\Aut_{0}(X_{\overline{F}})
is nontrivial, the cohomology

\mathrm{H}^{1}({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F),\Aut_{0}(X_{\bar{F}}))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}({\rm Gal}(\bar{F}/F),\Aut_{0}(X_{\bar{F}}))

is infinite in general, giving rise to infinitely many elements in
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M,d}(F,S)
.

Let us give some concrete examples.
Let
X
be a
2n
-dimensional generalized Kummer type hyper-Kähler variety. As before, by using
[
17
]
, we may assume that
\Aut(X_{\overline{F}})=\Aut(X)
. By
[
12
, Corollary 5]
,
\Aut_{0}(X)

is isomorphic to the semi-direct product
(\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)\mathbb{Z})^{4}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. As there are infinitely many degree 2 extensions of
F
, we get infinitely many homomorphisms

{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

hence infinitely many elements in
\operatorname{Hom}({\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F),\Aut_{0}(X))
. Similarly, for
OG_{6}
-type hyper-Kähler varieties
X
,
\Aut_{0}(X)
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\times 8}
by
[
60
, Theorem 5.2]
, we get infinitely many elements in
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}}_{M,d}(F,S)
.

We have obtained the following result:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\ell
be any prime number, and
S
the set of places consisting of places
v
with
v\mid\ell
and ramified places of the
{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
-module

.

(1)

The following subset of
\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}_{M,d}(F,S)
is infinite:

(8.2)

\left\{Y\in\mathrm{Shaf}^{{\rm hom}_{\ell}}_{M,d}(F,S)\left|\begin{array}[]{l}Y_{\overline{F}}\cong X_{\overline{F}}\end{array}\right.\right\}/{\cong_{F}}

(2)

Let
T
be any finite set of finite places of
F
.
Then for almost all
X
in (
8.2
) ,
there exists a finite place

(depending on

) such that

does not admit essentially good reduction at

though
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{F}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is unramified at

.
Moreover, enlarging

if necessary, we may assume that

admits potentially good reduction at

.

The second point above says that the analogue of
[

, Theorem 1.3 (ii)]
(see also
[
76
, Introduction]
) does not hold for higher-dimensional hyper-Kähler varieties when
\Aut_{0}
is nontrivial.

8.2.

We have seen that the non-faithfulness of the action of automorphisms on

leads to infiniteness of the cohomological Shafarevich sets. We provide a remedy by taking into account more cohomological degrees other than 2. Throughout this section, let

Define the following group

(resp.

9.
Finiteness of CM type hyper-Kähler varieties

Recall that a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
is called of
CM type
if the Mumford–Tate group
\mathrm{MT}(T(X))
is abelian, where
T(X)\subset\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is the transcendental (rational) cohomology. The following results allows us to study hyper-Kähler varieties of CM type via abelian varieties of CM type.

Lemma 9.1
.

The Kuga–Satake variety of a complex hyper-Kähler variety
X
is of CM type if and only if
X
is of CM type. In particular, the point

\Psi^{{\rm KS}}_{d,\mathbf{K}_{4}}(X,H,\omega;\alpha)\in\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime\prime}_{4}}({\rm GSp}(W),\Omega^{\pm})(\mathbb{C})

is a CM-point if
X
is of CM type.

Proof.

A proof for the statement for K3 surfaces can be found in for example
[
30
, Proposition 9.3]
, which works for general Hodge structures of K3 type. We sketch it here for the convenience of readers.

Let
A
be the (uniform) Kuga–Satake abelian variety of
X
. Let
\MT(A)
be the Mumford–Tate group of
\mathrm{H}_{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})
. The Hodge structure of
\mathrm{H}_{1}(A,\mathbb{Q})
factors through
\widetilde{h}
in the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm GSpin}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\Sigma,\mathbb{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ad}

the Mumford–Tate group is contained in
{\rm GSpin}(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The commutativity of the diagram implies that
\MT(T(X))
is contained in
\ad(\MT(A))
.

If
A
is of CM type, then
\MT(A)
is abelian, and so is its image in
G_{\Sigma}
under
\ad
. Therefore
\MT(T(X))
is also abelian. Conversely, we note that
\widetilde{h}(\mathbb{S})\subseteq\ad^{-1}(\MT(T(X))_{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus
\MT(A)
is a subgroup of
\ad^{-1}(\MT(T(X))
, which are both solvable if
\MT(T(X))
is abelian. Since
\MT(A)
is reductive,
\MT(A)
is abelian if
\MT(T(X))
is abelian.
∎

Appendix A
Matsusaka–Mumford theorem for algebraic spaces

The Matsusaka–Mumford theorem
[
56
]

states that a birational map between two algebraic varieties
over the fraction field of a discrete valuation ring
specializes to a birational map between the special fibers
if special fibers are non-ruled.
In this appendix, we extend this theorem to
birational maps between algebraic spaces which are not necessarily schemes and study the specialization homomorphism.
(Such birational maps appear naturally in the study of
hyper-Kähler varieties. See
[
48
, Section 4.4]
.)
The result should be known by experts but we could not find an appropriate reference.

We recall that for an integral and separated algebraic space
X
, there exists an open subspace which is a scheme and contains a generic point of
X
. Moreover, there exists a largest such open subspace, called the
schematic locus
of
X
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional integral separated algebraic space of finite type over a field
k
.
We say
X
is ruled if the schematic locus
X^{\prime}
of
X
is ruled, i.e.
X^{\prime}
is
k
-birationally isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\times Y
for some scheme
Y
of dimension
n-1
over
k
.

In the sequel, let
R
be a discrete valuation ring,
K=\Frac R
its fraction field,
s
the closed point of
\Spec R
, and
k(s)
the residue field.

Theorem A.2

(Matsusaka–Mumford for algebraic spaces)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be integral smooth proper algebraic spaces over
R
.
Suppose that the special fiber
\mathcal{Y}_{s}
is not ruled.
Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}_{\eta}\overset{\simeq}{\dashrightarrow}\mathcal{Y}_{\eta}

be a birational map between the generic fibers.
Then there exist closed algebraic subspaces

V\subset\mathcal{X}
,
W\subset\mathcal{Y}

with special fibers
V_{s},W_{s}
satisfying
V_{s}\neq\mathcal{X}_{s}
,
W_{s}\neq\mathcal{Y}_{s}
,
such that

f
extends to an isomorphism over
R

\widetilde{f}\colon\mathcal{X}\setminus V\overset{\simeq}{\to}\mathcal{Y}\setminus W.

Proof.

The same proof as in
[
56
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
54
, Theorem 5.4]
) works, by using the notion of generic point and henselian local rings of decent algebraic spaces.
∎

We thank Tetsushi Ito for his help with the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}

be an integral proper smooth algebraic space over
R
.
Suppose that the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is not ruled.
Let
{\rm Bir}(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})
be the group of
F
-birational automorphisms
of the generic fiber
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
.
Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
{\rm Bir}(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})
.

(1)

There exists a proper closed subspaces
V\subset\mathcal{X}

with
V_{s}\neq\mathcal{X}_{s}
and a homomorphism

\psi\colon G\to\Aut_{R}(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)

such that
\psi(f)|_{\mathcal{X}_{\eta}}=f
for any
f\in G
.

(2)

Let
\overline{\psi}
be the specialization morphism defined as the composition

G\overset{\psi}{\to}\Aut_{R}(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)\to\Aut((\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s})

given by
\overline{\psi}(f):=\psi(f)|_{(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}}
.
If the characteristic of the residue field of
R
is
0
,
the map
\overline{\psi}
is injective.
If the characteristic of the residue field of
R
is
p>0
,
the kernel of
\overline{\psi}
is a
p
-group.

Proof.

(1) We put
G=\{f_{1}=\mathrm{id},\ldots,f_{r}\}
.
Let
V_{f_{i}},W_{f_{i}}\subset\mathcal{X}
be proper closed subspaces
associated with
f_{i}
given by Theorem
A.2
(i.e.
f_{i}
extends to an isomorphism
\widetilde{f}_{i}\colon\mathcal{X}\setminus V_{f_{i}}\simeq\mathcal{X}\setminus W_{f_{i}}
).
Here, we put
V_{f_{1}}=\emptyset
.
We put
V^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{f_{i}}
,
and

V:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{f}_{i}^{-1}(V^{\prime}).

First, since
|\widetilde{f}_{i}^{-1}(V^{\prime})\cup V_{f_{i}}|
is a closed subset of
|\mathcal{X}|
, the subset
V
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, since we have

|\widetilde{f}_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{f}_{j}^{-1}(V^{\prime}))|\subset|\widetilde{(f_{j}\circ f_{i})}^{-1}(V^{\prime})\cup V_{f_{j}\circ f_{i}}|,

we have
\widetilde{f}_{i}^{-1}(V)\subset V
.
Since
V^{\prime}\subset V
, the morphism
\widetilde{f}_{i}
restricts to an
R
-morphism

f_{i}^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{X}\setminus V\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\setminus V.

Since
(f_{i})^{\prime}\circ(f_{i}^{-1})^{\prime}=(f_{i}^{-1})^{\prime}\circ(f_{i})^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}
,

f_{i}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism, and
V
satisfies the desired condition.

(2)
Take an automorphism
f\in\ker\overline{\psi}
.
Let
\ord(f)
be the order of
f
.
Assume that
\ord(f)
is invertible in the residue field of
R
.
We shall show that
f
is the identity.

Since
f
acts trivially on
(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}
,
it fixes every closed point
x\in(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}
.
Let
k(s)
be the residue field of
R
.
We can take a
\overline{k(s)}
-valued point
x
of
(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}
, such that
x
is contained in the schematic locus of
(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}
.
It is enough to show that the action of
f
on the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}
is trivial.
Let
\pi
be a uniformizer of
R
.
Since
\pi
is not invertible in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}
,
we have
\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\pi^{n}\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}=(0)
by Krull’s intersection theorem; see
[
55
, Theorem 8.10]
.
Therefore, it is enough to show that
the action of
f
on

\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}/\pi^{n}\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}

is trivial for every
n\geq 1
.
We shall prove the assertion by induction on
n
.
We put
A_{n}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}/\pi^{n}\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}
.
When
n=1
, it follows because
f
acts trivially on
the special fiber
(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)_{s}
.
If the assertion holds for some
n\geq 1
, the exact sequence

0\to\pi^{n}A_{n+1}\to A_{n+1}\to A_{n}\to 0

shows that, for an element
a\in A_{n+1}
,
we have
f^{\ast}(a)=a+\pi^{n}b
for some
b\in A_{n+1}

(here the action of
f
on
A_{n+1}
is denoted by
f^{\ast}
).
Since
\pi^{n+1}=0
in
A_{n+1}
,
we have
(f^{\ast})^{r}(a)=a+r\pi^{n}b
for every
r\geq 1
.
In particular, we have

(f^{\ast})^{\ord(f)}(a)=a+\ord(f)\pi^{n}b=a.

Since
\pi^{n}A_{n+1}
is a vector space over
k(x)

and
\ord(f)
is invertible in the residue field,
we have
\pi^{n}b=0
.
Thus, the action of
f
on
A_{n+1}
is trivial.
By induction on
n
, the assertion is proved.
∎

Remark A.4
.

If
\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})
is infinite,
a homomorphism

\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})\to\Aut(\mathcal{X}\setminus V)

may not exist for any choice of
V\subset\mathcal{X}_{s}
.
The problem is that there might be infinitely many closed subsets
that should be removed from
\mathcal{X}

if we apply Lemma
A.2
.
On the other hand, there is a well-defined specialization homomorphism

{\rm Bir}(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})\to{\rm Bir}(\mathcal{X}_{s}),

where
{\rm Bir}(-)

denotes the group of birational automorphisms;
see
[
45
, p. 191-192, Exercises 1.17]
.

Remark A.5
.

There does not exist a specialization map

\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})\to\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{s})

in general.
The problem is that an automorphism of the generic fiber
does not always extend to an automorphism of the integral model.
Such an extension exists for abelian varieties (
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.3]
), but fails in general for
for K3 surfaces (
[
48
, Example 5.4]
).
However, since any birational map between K3 surfaces can be extended to an automorphism, there still exists a map
\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{\eta})\to\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{s})
for K3 surfaces.
On the other hand, suppose that there exists a polarization on
\mathcal{X}
(this does not hold in general, see
[
54
, Example 5.2]
), then by Matsusaka–Mumford
[
56
, Theorem 2]
,
there always exists a specialization map

\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{\eta},\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{X}_{\eta}})\to\Aut(\mathcal{X}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}).

provided that the special fiber is non-ruled.
This map is used in André’s proof of the Shafarevich
conjecture for (very) polarized hyper-Kähler varieties;
see the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 9.3.1]
.

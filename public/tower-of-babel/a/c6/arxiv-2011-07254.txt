From spectral cluster to uniform resolvent estimates on compact manifolds
Jean-Claude Cuenin

Abstract
. It is well known that uniform resolvent estimates imply spectral cluster estimates. We show that the converse is also true in some cases. In particular, Sogge’s universal spectral cluster estimates for the Laplace–Beltrami operator on closed Riemannian manifolds directly imply uniform resolvent estimates outside a parabolic region, without any reference to parametrices. The method is purely functional analytic and takes full advantage of the known spectral cluster bounds. This yields new resolvent estimates for manifolds with boundary or with low-regularity metrics, among other examples. Moreover, we show that the resolvent estimates are stable under perturbations and use this to establish uniform Sobolev and spectral cluster inequalities for Schrödinger operators with singular potentials.

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2}))^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\sigma(q)-1},

\displaystyle\sigma(q)=\max\bigl(\,n(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})-\tfrac{1}{2},\,\tfrac{n-1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{q})\,\bigr).

The paper

provides a fairly complete study of the mapping properties of the Hadamard parametrix for the resolvent and the corresponding remainder term, using oscillatory integral theorems. Only a special case of these estimates is needed to prove (

avoids oscillatory integral theorems and only uses stationary phase to prove (

[

]
universal spectral cluster bounds are used to handle a “global term”. However, the proof still uses the short-time Hadamard parametrix for the wave propagator
\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})
to handle a “local term”. The bounds for the latter are independent of the global geometry of the manifold. One of our main results, Theorem
2.1
(or rather its generalization, Theorem

), is close to
[
6

L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}
resolvent bounds for

to a larger region

, are equivalent to

spectral cluster bounds in shrinking frequency windows
[\lambda,\lambda+\epsilon(\lambda)]
.
The first difference between this and our result is that we are in a sense concerned with the opposite of improved bounds. Namely, we consider metrics of low regularity and seek bounds on the resolvent without losses compared to the smooth case. In fact, we will show how the two approaches can be combined to get improved resolvent bounds from improved spectral cluster bounds, without any a priori assumption on the smoothness of the metric.
Of course, it is unreasonable to expect improved estimates for metrics of such low regularity as considered here, but the point is that all the geometric information is now encoded in the spectral cluster bound.
The second difference is that we completely avoid parametrix estimates and, again, solely rely on the spectral cluster bounds (or the equivalent quasimode bounds). In the case of improved estimates, we replace the step in
[
6
]
involving the Hadamard parametrix by a more abstract argument. As an application, we convert the recent spectral cluster bounds of Canzani and Galkowski
[
8
]
to improved resolvent bounds under pure dynamical assumptions on the manifold.
The Hadamard parametrix construction breaks down for metrics with limited smoothness.
Smith
[
40
,
42
]
and Tataru
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
used wave packet techniques to construct parametrices for regularized wave operators
and established Strichartz and square function estimates for the variable-coefficient wave equation with rough coefficients. In contrast to the smooth case, one cannot hope for asymptotic expansions of the fundamental solution modulo smoothing operators since the errors in the parametrix are too large. However, for the sake of proving estimates, they can be considered as forcing terms, and the equation can be solved iteratively. The wave packet parametrices require roughly two derivatives on the coefficients as this guarantees uniqueness of the bicharacteristic flow. For metrics with
C^{1,1}
coefficients one could prove the parametrix bounds for the resolvent estimates concurrently with those for the spectral cluster bounds.
This is essentially the approach of Koch and Tataru
[
31
, Theorem 2.5]
. The difference between the
TT^{*}
version of the spectral cluster bound (or quasimode bound) and the resolvent is merely a causal factor
\mathbf{1}_{t\geq 0}
, where

denotes one of the spatial coordinates (see the proof of Lemma
4.13
). The dispersive (
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
) estimate
[
31
, Proposition 7.4]
is insensitive to this factor, and the spectral cluster and resolvent estimates both follow from this.
However, this strategy fails to prove resolvent analogues of the spectral cluster bounds of Smith and Sogge
[
47
]
or Smith, Koch and Tataru
[
29
]
. In those cases, the metric is only Lipschitz, and good parametrix bounds hold only on small spatial scales. Highly nontrivial energy propagation estimates are needed to prove estimates on the unit scale. Following the arguments of these papers carefully, one could perhaps prove resolvent estimates directly. However, the preceding discussion should make it clear that this is not completely straightforward.
Theorem
2.1
has the advantage that it treats the spectral cluster estimates as a black box and thus yields several new resolvent estimates in one stroke.

A simple argument involving the Phragmén–Lindelöf maximum principle (see e.g.
[

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+z)^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q}(M)}\lesssim_{\delta}|z|^{\sigma(q)-1/2}

. Here we take the branch of the square root with
\mathrm{Im}\,\sqrt{\cdot}>0
. After possibly multiplying the metric by an innocuous factor, we may always assume that
\delta=1
, in accordance with (
2

is decreasing in

Spectral cluster estimates (

, see

. In the low-regularity setting that we will consider here,

, there may be losses in the exponents of (

\sigma(q)
may have to be replaced by
\gamma(q)>\sigma(q)
. We will refer to spectral cluster estimates with exponent as in the smooth case, i.e.

, as ‘‘perfect” estimates.

This terminology is borrowed from a seminar talk of Daniel Tataru.

For the resolvent, we distinguish two types of perfect estimates. The first we call estimates with “perfect exponents”. This means that the exponent is the same as in (
5
) (and hence the same as for elliptic estimates), but the region
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
for which the estimate holds may be smaller than the “perfect region”
\Omega=\{\mathrm{Im}\,\sqrt{z}\geq 1\}
. In the second type the region
\Omega
is perfect, but the exponent may be larger than in (
5
). If both the exponent and the region is perfect, we simply say that we have perfect resolvent estimates. Note that, by Sobolev embedding, if we have perfect exponents for
q_{1}
, then the same is true for all
q_{2}\geq q_{1}
. We will consider regions of the form
\Omega_{\rho}=\{z=(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\mu)^{2}:\,\lambda\geq 1,\,|\mu|\gtrsim\lambda^{\rho}\}
, disregarding the elliptic region (the outside of the union of a disk around the origin and a cone in the direction of the positive
z
-axis) in which (
5
) trivially holds. Hence the relevant range for
\rho
is
0\leq\rho\leq 1
, and the perfect region corresponds to
\rho=0
.

When proving (
2
) in a coordinate patch of the manifold, the crucial region in phase space is
|x|\lesssim 1
,
|\xi|\approx\lambda
. Away from this region, one has stronger elliptic estimates, and the inequality follows by Sobolev embedding. For the arguments concerning elliptic estimates we refer to the proof of
[
32
, Corollary 5]
or that of
[
41
, Theorem 7]
but we will discuss these briefly in Subsection
4.3
. In the crucial phase space region, the difference between spectral cluster and resolvent estimates is at most logarithmic in the frequency
\lambda
. In fact, this holds in a rather abstract setting, see Proposition
3.3
. Similar abstract considerations (see e.g.
[
38
, Theorem XIII.25]
or
[
18
, Corollary 3.3]
) show that the spectral cluster bound (
4
) is equivalent to the analogue of (
2
) for the imaginary part of the resolvent. Hence, any difference between (
2
) and (
4
) can only come from the real part. The logarithmic loss incurred by the abstract bounds hints at an inefficiency in detecting cancellations.
This is most clearly seen in the constant coefficient case, where the distributional formula

\displaystyle(|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)^{2})^{-1}=\pv(|\xi|^{2}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}+\mathrm{i}\pi\delta(|\xi|^{2}-\lambda^{2})

reveals cancellations of the real part. More generally, in the case where the operator has only absolutely continuous spectrum, Guillarmou and Hassell
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
showed that the cancellation effects can be exploited by an integration by parts argument. This requires stronger pointwise bounds on the spectral measure and all its derivatives of order
\leq n/2
. The cancellation is related to the causal structure of the resolvent mentioned before.

Most modern proofs of spectral cluster and resolvent estimates rely on wave equation techniques.
One popular approach is via square function estimates for the wave equation
[
37
,
42
,
2
,
9
,
43
]
, another one is via Strichartz estimates for a half wave equation
[
31
,
7
,
34
,
30
]
. The latter is purely stationary, i.e. all the variables are spatial. Locally in phase space, one can single out a distinguished direction that plays the role of a fictional time. In fact, the papers listed under the first approach also use this phase space localization.
While square function estimates are of independent interest, the second approach seems more natural for a purely stationary problem. More importantly, the homogeneous Strichartz estimates (corresponding to spectral cluster bounds) can easily be upgraded to inhomogeneous estimates (corresponding to resolvent bounds) by invoking the Christ–Kiselev lemma
[
10
,
46
,
51
]
. For square function estimates, the passage from homogeneous to inhomogeneous estimates is more involved, as the recent work of Smith
[
43
]
shows.

2.
Main results

2.1.
From spectral cluster to resolvent bounds

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary and with Lipschitz metric
g
. Assume that
2\leq q\leq 2n/(n-2)
if
n\geq 3
and
2\leq q<\infty
if
n=2
, and that the spectral cluster estimate

(6)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{\gamma(q)}

holds for some
\gamma(q)\in[\sigma(q),\sigma(q)+1/2]
. Then we have the uniform resolvent estimate

(7)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\gamma(q)-1}.

Remark 2.2
.

The restrictions on
\gamma(q)
are natural since,
by Sobolev embedding, the bounds trivially hold for
\gamma(q)=\sigma(q)+1/2
. On the other hand, we cannot have better bounds than in the smooth case.

Remark 2.3
.

We actually prove a more general result (Theorem
4.1
) that allows us to 1) obtain resolvent estimates with perfect exponents in a an intermediate region
\Omega_{\rho}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, even when perfect spectral cluster estimates are not available and 2) convert improved spectral cluster bounds to improved resolvent bounds (in the sense discussed in the introduction).

Remark 2.4
.

We do not touch here on operator-theoretic considerations related to the definition of
\Delta_{g}
and domain issues. In the context of low-regularity metrics, it is advantageous to define the operator by quadratic form methods, see e.g.
[
13
, Chapter 6]
. Thus, we implicitly assume that
\Delta_{g}
is in divergence form and that the resolvent equation
\Delta_{g}u+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2}u=F
is interpreted in a weak sense (see Subsection
4.5
for more details). This point of view is also taken in
[
41
,
29
,
30
]
.

2.2.
Stability under perturbations

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that the conditions of Theorem

are satisfied with perfect exponent
\gamma(q)=\sigma(q)
and that

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+V+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\sigma(q)-1}

, then

Remark 2.6
.

The above also follows from recent results by Blair, Huang, Sire and Sogge

. However, for the exponents

and the self-dual estimates considered here, we can relax the assumptions in the abstract Theorem 2.1 of

. Instead of assuming the quasimode

, we only need to assume one of them (since they are equivalent).

Remark 2.7
.

Since

. We will subsequently consider critically singular potentials

Remark 2.8
.

The stability holds in greater generality, not just for potential perturbations, see Proposition

. Similar results may be found in
[
31
,
32
]
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Perfect spectral cluster estimates (i.e. (
6

\gamma(q)=\sigma(q)

2.4.
Manifolds with boundary

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, subject to either Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. Assume that
2\leq q\leq 2n/(n-2)
if
n\geq 3
and
2\leq q<\infty
if
n=2
.

(i)

We have the estimates

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\lambda^{1/3})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\sigma(q)-1}.

(ii)

If
n=2,3,4
and
q\geq 2(n+2)/(n-1)
, then

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\sigma(q)-1}.

(iii)

If
M
has strictly concave boundary and the boundary conditions are Dirichlet, then

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\sigma(q)-1}\ln\langle\lambda\rangle.

These estimates are stable under perturbations by real-valued potentials
V\in L^{n/2}(M)
.

Remark 2.17
.

(ii)
implies that the uniform Sobolev inequality (
1
) holds for manifolds with boundary in dimensions
n=2,3,4
. The proof is based on a deep result by Smith and Sogge
[
47
]
, who proved that perfect spectral cluster estimates hold in the stated range. For
n=2
their result is best possible. The conjectured range for perfect spectral cluster estimates in higher dimensions is
q\geq(6n+4)/(3n-4)
. If true, this would imply the uniform Sobolev inequality in all dimensions
n\geq 3
. The higher dimensional estimates of
[
47
]
are still good enough to obtain this in dimensions
n=3,4
. Their results also yield spectral cluster bounds for
n\geq 5
,
q\geq 4
, but these do not imply uniform Sobolev estimates since
2n/(n-2)<4
.

Remark 2.18
.

The estimates for manifolds with boundary are closely related to those for manifolds with Lipschitz metrics.
The reason is that one can double the manifold and reflect the metric across the boundary in an odd (for Dirichlet boundary conditions) or even (for Neumann boundary conditions) way. This produces a manifold without boundary, but with Lipschitz metric, which accounts for the simple bound in
(i)
.

Remark 2.19
.

The fact that the bounds in
[
47
]
were proved by reducing to a manifold without boundary is important here since Theorem
2.1
does not directly apply to manifolds with boundary. Such a generalization would imply perfect resolvent estimates for manifolds with strictly concave boundaries, based on the corresponding spectral cluster bounds of Grieser
[
19
]
(in two dimensions) or those of Smith and Sogge
[
45
]
(in any dimension). Microlocally, the proof of Theorem
2.1
does not work at “glancing” points of
T^{*}(\partial M)
, see
[
23
, Chapter 24]
. It would be interesting to prove a version of Theorem
2.1
for manifolds with boundary. However, the abstract bound (
31
) holds irrespective of boundaries and yield a weaker bound with a logarithmic loss in
(iii)

Remark 2.20
.

The Lipschitz singularity produced by the doubling procedure is of a special type, which is why the bounds of
[
47
]
yield uniform Sobolev inequalities in higher dimensions, while those of
[
30
]
do not.
Incidentally, the proof in
[
47
]
yields the conjectured results for the special Lipschitz singularities considered there. This explains the similarity of the conjectured estimates for Lipschitz metrics and the actual estimates in
(ii)
for manifolds with boundary.

2.5.
Improved estimates

Following Canzani–Galkowski

\mathcal{C}_{{}_{x}}^{r,t}:=\big\{\gamma(t):\,\gamma\in\Xi,\,\gamma(0)=x,\,\exists\,n-1\text{ conjugate points to }x\text{ in }\gamma(t-r,t+r)\big\},

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplace–Beltrami operator on an

Remark 2.22
.

For spectral clusters, Canzani-Galkowski

Remark 2.23
.

Since

Remark 2.24
.

As before, one could interpolate with the trivial

Remark 3.6

Theorem 4.1

Remark 4.2

for the exponent
q=2^{*}
. The proof uses the Hadamard parametrix for the wave equation, which is not available under our limited smoothness assumptions. We therefore include the argument for improved bounds, avoiding the Hadamard parametrix and extending the result of
[
6
]
to all
2\leq q\leq 2^{*}
.

We briefly outline the plan of the proof. We need to prove that if
u
satisfies the resolvent equation

(34)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})u=F,

and (
32
) holds, then

(35)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\langle\lambda\rangle^{2\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q}(M)}.

Subsections
4.2
–
4.9
are devoted to the proof of the case
\epsilon\geq 1
. Subsection
4.10
deals with the proof for
\lambda^{-1}\leq\epsilon<1
.

4.2.
Reductions

Until the end of Subsection
4.9
we will consider the case
\epsilon\geq 1
. Note that, by Sobolev embedding, the estimate is trivial for
\lambda\leq 1
, so we will always assume
\lambda\geq 1
in the following.
We will perform a series of reductions:

(i)

Reduction to the case
\epsilon\leq\lambda
and to spectrally localized solutions (
u=\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u
) via elliptic estimates;

(ii)

First application of the spectral cluster bound;

(iii)

Localization and reduction to an equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
;

(iv)

Symbol smoothing and factorization;

(v)

Reduction to a first order hyperbolic (half wave) equation;

(vi)

Reduction of the inhomogeneous to a homogeneous estimate (second application of the spectral cluster bound).

4.3.
Elliptic estimates

As already pointed out in the introduction, stronger estimates than (
33
) hold in the elliptic regime, that is when

(a)

either
\epsilon\geq\lambda
,

(b)

or
u=\Pi_{>2\lambda}u
.

The following proposition thus takes care of the first reduction step.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary and with Lipschitz metric. Assume that
u
satisfies (
34
) and that either (a) or (b) hold. Then, for
2\leq q\leq 2^{*}
,
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

(36)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}u\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{2n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}},

(37)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}u\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Remark 4.4
.

Since
\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\geq 1
and
\gamma(q)\geq n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{2n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\leq\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1}.

Proof.

Taking inner product in
L^{2}(M)
of both sides of (
34
) against
u
yields

(38)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}-(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}=-\langle F,u\rangle_{L^{2}(M)},

where
\nabla_{g}
is the gradient with respect to the metric
g
.
Taking real and imaginary parts, we obtain

(39)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|F\|_{H^{-1}},

(40)

\displaystyle 2\epsilon\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|F\|_{H^{-1}},

where
\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}:=\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
H^{-1}
is the dual space. In case (a), adding (
39
) and (
40
), we get

(41)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim|\langle F,u\rangle|\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|F\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim c\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+c^{-1}\|F\|_{H^{-1}}^{2},

where
c>0
is arbitrary. Choosing
c
sufficiently small, we may absorb the first term on the right of (
42
) in the left hand side, so that

(42)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|F\|_{H^{-1}},

In case (b), since
\Pi_{>2\lambda}u=u
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}=\langle\Delta_{g}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\|\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\geq 4\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

This together with (
39
) yields (
41
) and hence (
42
).
Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(M)\subset L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(M)
, the dual embedding
L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(M)\subset H^{-1}(M)
and (
42
) yield

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|F\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}},

while (
41
) also yields

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim|\langle F,u\rangle|\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|F\|_{L^{2}},

hence
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\|F\|_{L^{2}}
. Interpolation between the
L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\to L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}
and the
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bound yields (
36
) for
q
in the stated range
since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}=\frac{(1-\theta)(n-2)}{2n}+\frac{\theta}{2}\iff\theta=-n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})+1.

By duality, (
37
) is equivalent to

(43)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}.

By (
41
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim|\langle F,u\rangle|\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}},

which together with (
36
) gives

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{2n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}^{2}

and hence proves (
43
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Since we were only working with quadratic forms, it would have been sufficient to assume that the coefficients of the metric are bounded measurable functions.

4.4.
First application of the spectral cluster bound

Given the elliptic estimate
(
36
),
it remains to prove

(44)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1}\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(M)}.

The next lemma shows how the spectral cluster bound (
32
) is used to control various errors that will appear later when we localize in position and/or frequency.

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume that the spectral cluster estimate (
32
) holds. Also assume that any solution of the equation

(45)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})u=F+G

on
M
satisfies the bound

(46)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla_{g}u\|_{L^{2}}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}).

Then (
44
) holds.

Proof.

By linearity it suffices to prove this separately when
G=0
or
F=0
.
By the implication
a)\implies b)
in Corollary
3.5
, (
32
) implies the
L^{2}\to L^{q}
bound

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}.

This is still valid if we replace
\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon
by
\lambda-\mathrm{i}\epsilon
. Hence,
by duality (note that
\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}
commutes with the resolvent), we have the
L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{2}
bound

(47)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}.

The latter implies that

(48)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(M)}.

Due to the spectral localization, we also have

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla_{g}\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

The last two displays together with (
46
) yield

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla_{g}\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}.

This proves the claim when
G=0
. The case
F=0
follows immediately from the quasimode estimate (
29
) (using the implication
a)\implies c)
in Corollary
3.5
).
∎

Note that we can absorb
(2\mathrm{i}\lambda\epsilon-\epsilon^{2})u
into the driving term
G
on the right hand side of (
45
) without changing the estimate. We will thus consider the equation

(49)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2})u=F+G,

and we need to prove (
46
), given (
32
).

4.5.
Localization and reduction to an equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

We cover
M
by finitely many charts,
M=\bigcup_{j=1}^{K}U_{j}
, and consider a partition of unity
\phi_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,K
, subordinate to this cover. By the triangle inequality, it would thus suffice to prove (
35
) for
\phi_{j}u
.
If
u
satisfies the resolvent equation (
45
), then
\phi u
satisfies

(50)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2})(\phi u)=\phi(F+G)+[\Delta_{g},\phi]u.

Since all the terms are supported in a fixed chart
U_{j}
, we may write
\Delta_{g}
in local coordinates,

(51)

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}=|g|^{-1/2}\partial_{i}g^{ij}|g|^{1/2}\partial_{j},

where
g^{ij}:=(g^{-1})_{ij}
,
|g|:=|\det g|
and repeated indices are summed over.
We set
\rho=|g|^{1/2}
,
a^{ij}=|g|^{1/2}g^{ij}
, and we will work in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\rm d}x)
. Note that
\rho
and
(a^{ij})
are bounded from above and below by a positive constant (the latter in the sense of quadratic forms). Then (
50
) can be written as

(52)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}(\phi u))+\lambda^{2}\rho\phi u=\rho\phi(F+G)+\partial_{i}(a^{ij}u\partial_{j}\phi)+a^{ij}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}u.

At this point we drop the spectral localization and consider
u
as a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with compact support;
without loss of generality, we assume that it is supported in the unit cube
Q=[0,1]^{n}
. By shrinking coordinate patches and rescaling, if necessary, we can always assume that
g^{ij}
is globally defined and close to the Euclidean metric in the sense that

(53)

\displaystyle\|g^{ij}-\delta^{ij}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\ll 1,

Then we also have

(54)

\displaystyle\|\rho-1\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}+\|a^{ij}-\delta^{ij}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\ll 1.

Here and in the following (until the end of Subsection
4.9
) all norms are taken over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
unless stated otherwise.
In particular, there exists
\kappa
close to
1
such for all
x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

(55)

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\kappa|\xi|^{2}.

Note that the localization has produced an
additional error term (the second term on the right of (
52
); the third term can be absorbed into
G
)
of the form
\partial_{i}G^{i}
, where
G^{i}=a^{ij}u\partial_{j}\phi
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|G^{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. We will include this into the driving term, that is, we will replace
F+G
by
F+G+\partial_{i}G^{i}
below. We will write
\vec{G}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
(so that
\partial_{i}G^{i}=\Div\vec{G}
) and
\|\vec{G}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|G^{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Thus (
46
) would be a consequence of the following global estimate.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume that
a^{ij}
,
\rho
are globally defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and satisfy (
54
). If

(56)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}\rho u=F+G+\Div\vec{G},

holds on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
then

(57)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\vec{G}\|_{L^{2}}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}),

provided that the same estimate holds when
F=0
.

Remark 4.8
.

(i)
With reference to (
52
), we would replace
F
by
\rho\phi F
,
G
by
\rho\phi G+a^{ij}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}u
. This does not change the estimate (recall that
\epsilon\geq 1)
.

(ii)
The estimate with
F=0
implies the spectral cluster bound (
32
), see
[
41
, Cor. 5]
). Here, we will use the converse. Although it is rather obvious that the steps of the reduction can be reversed,
we will elaborate on this point in Subsection
4.8
.

(iii)
Since in the following we will use the letter
a
for symbols, to avoid confusion, we will again write
g^{ij}
instead of
a^{ij}
.

4.6.
Symbol smoothing and factorization

We replace
g^{ij},\rho
by smooth functions
g^{ij}_{\lambda},\rho_{\lambda}
, which are obtained by smoothly truncating to frequencies
|\xi|\ll\lambda
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
g_{\lambda}^{ij}:=\varphi(D)g^{ij},\rho_{\lambda}(\xi):=\varphi(D)\rho
, where
\varphi
is a bump function adapted to
|\xi|\ll\lambda
. Then

\displaystyle\|\rho-\rho_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Lip}},

\displaystyle\|g^{ij}-g^{ij}_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|g^{ij}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

Proof.

We only prove this for
\rho
, the proof for
g^{ij}
is identical.
Since
\varphi(0)=1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x)-\rho_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\varphi^{\vee}(x-y)(\rho(x)-\rho(y)){\rm d}y,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Thus,

(58)

\displaystyle|\rho(x)-\rho_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|(x-y)\varphi^{\vee}(x-y)|{\rm d}y.

By assumption,
\varphi(\xi)=\varphi_{0}(\xi/c\lambda)
for some bump function
\varphi_{0}
on the unit scale and some
c\ll 1
. Therefore,
\varphi^{\vee}(x)=(c\lambda)^{n}\varphi_{0}^{\vee}(c\lambda x)
. A change of variables thus yields the claimed bound.
∎

By Lemma
4.9
we have for all
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\displaystyle\|(g^{ij}-g^{ij}_{\lambda})\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}

which means that the smoothing error
\partial_{i}(g^{ij}-g^{ij}_{\lambda})\partial_{j}u

may be absorbed into the forcing term
\vec{G}
.
It thus suffices to prove (
57
) whenever

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(g_{\lambda}^{ij}\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}\rho_{\lambda}u=F+G+\Div\vec{G}.

To proceed, we split
u=u_{\ll\lambda}+u_{\lambda}+u_{\gg\lambda}
, where the terms are frequency-localized to
|\xi|\ll\lambda
,
|\xi|\approx\lambda
and
|\xi|\gg\lambda
, respectively. The estimates for
u_{\ll\lambda}
and
u_{\gg\lambda}
follow from integration by parts and Sobolev embedding, similarly as in Proposition
4.3
, and they do not require the spectral cluster bound (see also the proof of
[
41
, Cor. 5]
).

It remains to consider
u_{\lambda}:=\Gamma(D)u
, where
\Gamma
is a bump function adapted to
|\xi|\approx\lambda
. This satisfies

(59)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(g_{\lambda}^{ij}\partial_{j}u_{\lambda})+\lambda^{2}\rho_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}=\Gamma(D)(F+G+\Div\vec{G})+\partial_{i}([g_{\lambda}^{ij},\Gamma(D)]\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}[\rho_{\lambda},\Gamma(D)]u.

Similarly as in Lemma
4.9
one easily proves

(60)

\displaystyle\|[g_{\lambda}^{ij},\Gamma(D)]\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},\quad\lambda^{2}\|[\rho_{\lambda},\Gamma(D)]u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence the commutator errors can be absorbed into
\vec{G}
and
G
, respectively, without changing the right hand side of (
57
). Moreover, due to the frequency truncation of
g^{ij}_{\lambda}
and
\rho_{\lambda}
, all terms in (
59
) are localized to frequencies
|\xi|\approx\lambda
. For instance,

\displaystyle\supp(\widehat{\rho_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}})=\supp(\widehat{\rho_{\lambda}}\ast\widehat{u_{\lambda}})\subset\supp(\widehat{\rho_{\lambda}})+\supp(\widehat{u_{\lambda}}).

Hence, choosing the implicit constant in the truncation
|\xi|\ll\lambda
(see Lemma
4.9
) sufficiently small ensures that
\rho_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}
is still supported in
|\xi|\approx\lambda
. Due to this frequency localization and since
\epsilon\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|\Gamma(D)(G+\Div\vec{G})+\partial_{i}([g_{\lambda}^{ij},\Gamma(D)]\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}[\rho_{\lambda},\Gamma(D)]u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}}+\|\vec{G}\|_{L^{2}}.

We may thus treat all the terms on the right of (
59
) (except
F_{\lambda}:=\Gamma(D)F
) the same and call this collection of terms
G
again.
Note also that if (
53
), (
54
) hold, then by the triangle inequality we also have

(61)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\lambda}-1\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}+\|g_{\lambda}^{ij}-\delta^{ij}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\ll 1.

For instance, by Lemma
4.9
,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\lambda}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\rho-1\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{\lambda}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\ll 1

for
\lambda\gg 1
(which we may always assume), and
\|\nabla\rho_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\ll 1
. By a partition of unity, we may actually assume that
\Gamma
localizes to the conic region
|\xi^{\prime}|\ll\xi_{1}\approx\lambda
, where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
It will thus suffice to prove the following version of Proposition
4.7
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Assume that
g_{\lambda}^{ij}
,
\rho_{\lambda}
, globally defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, are truncated to frequencies
|\xi|\ll\lambda
and satisfy (
61
).
If

(62)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(g_{\lambda}^{ij}\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}\rho_{\lambda}u=F+G

holds as an equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
u
,
F
,
G
are Fourier supported in
|\xi^{\prime}|\ll\xi_{1}\approx\lambda
, then

(63)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}),

provided that the same estimate holds when
F=0
.

To proceed, we relabel
x_{1}=t
and use coordinates
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
instead of
(x_{1},x^{\prime})
. Dual coordinates are denoted by
(\tau,\xi)
.
We will use paradifferential calculus to reduce (
63
) to a first order equation.
For this, we will work with the following symbol classes (cf.
[
30
]
).

Definition 4.11

(Symbol classes)
.

Let
a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)
be a family of symbols in
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, depending on parameters
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lambda\geq 1
. For
\kappa\in[0,1]
, we say
a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)\in S_{\lambda,\lambda^{\kappa}}
if, for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
,

(64)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t,x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda^{-|\beta|+\kappa|\alpha|}.

We say
a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)\in C^{k}S_{\lambda,\lambda^{\kappa}}
if the stronger bound

(65)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t,x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda^{-|\beta|+\kappa(|\alpha|-k)_{+}}

holds, where
x_{+}=\max(x,0)
.

We will only need the case
k=1
,
\kappa=1
and
d=n-1
. Symbols
will initially be defined in the phase space region
B_{\lambda}=\{|(t,x)|\leq 1
,
|\xi|\ll\tau\approx\lambda\}
and extended globally (consistently with the symbol class).
We sometimes suppress the dependence on
\lambda
, i.e. we write
a(t,x,\xi)
instead of
a(t,x,\xi,\lambda)
.
As remarked in
[
30
]
, since
a\in S_{\lambda,\lambda}
is simply a bounded family of zero order symbols in the Hörmander class
S^{0}_{0,0}
, rescaled by
(t,x,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda t,\lambda x,\xi/\lambda)
, the Kohn-Nirenberg quantization
a(t,x,D_{x})
and the Weyl
quantization
a^{w}(t,x,D_{x})
are
L^{2}
bounded (with uniform bounds in
t
and
\lambda
)
[
26
]
. We will use the Weyl quantization here.
The asymptotic laws for composition and adjoint for symbols
a,b\in C^{1}S_{\lambda,\lambda}
hold to first order, viz.

(66)

\begin{split}&a^{w}(t,x,D_{x})b^{w}(t,x,D_{x})=(ab)^{w}(t,x,D_{x})+\lambda^{-1}R_{1},\\
&a^{w}(t,x,D_{x})^{*}=(\overline{a})^{w}(t,x,D_{x})+\lambda^{-1}R_{2},\end{split}

where the errors

R_{1},R_{2}
are quantizations of symbols in
S_{\lambda,\lambda}
and hence bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Since
g
is pointwise close to the flat metric (recall (
53
)) and
\lambda\gg 1
, Lemma
4.9
shows that
g_{\lambda}^{11}(x)>0
for
x\in Q
. We may then factorize

(67)

\displaystyle-\langle(\tau,\xi),g_{\lambda}^{-1}(t,x)(\tau,\xi)\rangle+\lambda^{2}=g_{\lambda}^{11}(t,x)(\tau+\widetilde{a}(t,\xi^{\prime},\lambda))(\tau-a(t,\xi^{\prime},\lambda))

on
B_{\lambda}
,
where
\widetilde{a},a>0
are symbols in
\lambda C^{1}S_{\lambda,\lambda}
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Euclidean inner product and
g_{\lambda}^{-1}
is the matrix with entries
g_{\lambda}^{ij}
.
We smoothly truncate the
(t,x)
Fourier transform of
a
to frequencies
|(\tau,\xi)|\ll\lambda
. The resulting symbol
a_{\lambda}
is again in
\lambda C^{1}S_{\lambda,\lambda}
, and the difference
a-a_{\lambda}
is in
S_{\lambda,\lambda}
(the proof is similar to that of Lemma
4.9
and is omitted). We also extend
a_{\lambda}
globally so that
a_{\lambda}(t,x,\xi)=\lambda
for
|\xi|\gg\lambda
, while preserving the frequency localization in the
(t,x)
variables. The same procedure is applied to
\widetilde{a}
.

Since the norm in Proposition
4.10
is taken over the unit cube,
we can replace
u
by
\chi^{w}u
, where
\chi
is adapted to

B_{\lambda}
and
\chi^{w}=\chi^{w}(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})
. Indeed, if

\chi(t,x,\tau,\xi)=1
on the support of
\phi(t,x)\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)
, then

\|\phi u-\chi^{w}u\|_{H^{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\|u\|_{H^{-N}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for any
N>0
.
By Sobolev embedding, the error may thus be absorbed into the first term on the right of (
63
).
Moving forward, we may thus assume that
u
is microlocalized, i.e.

(68)

\displaystyle u=\chi^{w}u+\mathcal{O}_{H^{-N}\to H^{N}}(\lambda^{-N})u\quad\forall N>0.

First order calculus (
66
) and the factorization (
67
) then yield, with
D=(D_{t},D_{x})
,

(69)

\displaystyle-\langle D,g_{\lambda}^{-1}(t,x)D\rangle u+\lambda^{2}u=-g_{\lambda}^{11}(t,x)(D_{t}+\widetilde{a}_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u+\lambda Ru

for some
R=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}(1)
. Due to (
68
), the factor
g^{11}_{\lambda}(t,x)(D_{t}+\widetilde{a}_{\lambda}(t,x,D_{x}))
admits a parametrix
Q
with symbol in
\lambda^{-1}S_{\lambda,\lambda}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle-Qg_{\lambda}^{11}(t,x)(D_{t}+\widetilde{a}_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u=u+\lambda^{-1}R_{1}u

for some
R_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}(1)
. Hence, if
u
satisfies (
62
), then

\displaystyle(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u=Q(F+G)-\lambda^{-1}R_{1}(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u-\lambda QRu.

We write the right hand side as
F^{\prime}+G^{\prime}
, with
F^{\prime}=QF
and
\|G^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}}
. We will also
need that operators with
S_{\lambda,\lambda}
symbols are
L^{p}
bounded for
1<p<\infty
. Since we already know that they are
L^{2}
bounded, this follows from Calderón–Zygmund theory since their Schwartz kernels satisfy standard estimates. In fact, after rescaling
(t,x,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda t,\lambda x,\xi/\lambda)
, it suffices to estimate the kernel
K
of an operator corresponding to an
S_{0,0}
symbol, localized to frequencies
|\xi|\lesssim 1
; this satisfies the trivial bound
|K(x,y)|+|\nabla_{x,y}K(x,y)|\lesssim 1
.
As a consequence, we have
\|F^{\prime}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}
. We work again with
F,G
instead of
F^{\prime},G^{\prime}
. In other words, it suffices to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.12
.

Assume that
a_{\lambda}\in\lambda C^{1}S_{\lambda,\lambda}
has
(t,x)
-frequencies
|(\tau,\xi)|\ll\lambda
and
a_{\lambda}(t,x,\xi)=\lambda
for
|\xi|\gg\lambda
. If

u
satisfies

(70)

\displaystyle(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u=F+G,

on
[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
,
then we have

(71)

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}([0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\epsilon^{-1}\|G\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}),

provided that the same estimate holds for
F=0
.

4.7.
Microlocalized quasimode estimate

We will prove Proposition
4.12
by using the spectral cluster bound (
32
) (for the second time).
As explained in Remark
4.8
(ii), we
first need to convert the spectral cluster bound to a suitable quasimode bound (coresponding to the case
F=0
in Proposition
4.10
). First, by the implication
(a)\implies(c)
in Corollary
3.5
, the spectral cluster bound (
32
) implies the quasimode bound

(72)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2})u\|_{L^{2}(M)})

for spectrally localized functions, i.e.
\Pi_{\leq 2\lambda}u=u
. We can remove the assumption of spectral localization since we have the stronger elliptic estimate (
43
) for
\Pi_{>2\lambda}u
. After extending
g
globally (subject to (
53
)) like before, we may consider this as a problem on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Setting
\rho=|g|^{1/2}
,
a^{ij}=|g|^{1/2}g^{ij}
and relabelling
a^{ij}
to
g^{ij}
again, we can replace
(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2})u
by
\partial_{i}(g^{ij}\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}\rho u
, where we use that
\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
, due to (
54
). Therefore,

(73)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(\epsilon\lambda)^{-1}\|\partial_{i}(g^{ij}\partial_{j}u)+\lambda^{2}\rho u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})})

The key point is that this inequality is microlocalizable and stable under lower order perturbations (here we use again that
\epsilon\geq 1)
. After microlocalization to
B_{\lambda}
, we may thus again replace
g^{ij},\rho
by the smoothed functions
g_{\lambda}^{ij},\rho_{\lambda}
and use the first order calculus, in particular the factorization (
69
),
to obtain the microlocalized quasimode estimate

(74)

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\epsilon^{-1}\|(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}})

with norms over
(t,x)\in[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
and for all
u\in L^{2}_{t,x}
.

4.8.
From homogeneous to inhomogeneous estimates

We will use the Christ–Kiselev lemma to upgrade (
74
) to the stronger estimate (
71
). In view of the discussion after (
71
), it remains to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.13
.

Assume that the microlocalized quasimode estimate (
74
) holds for every bump function
\chi
supported in
B_{\lambda}=\{|(t,x)|\leq 1
,
|\xi|\ll\tau\approx\lambda\}
.
Let
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]\subset[0,1]
and assume that
\chi,\widetilde{\chi}
are supported in
B_{\lambda}
. Assume that

u
satisfies

(75)

\displaystyle(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))u=\widetilde{\chi}^{w}F+G,\quad u(t_{0})=u_{0},

on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. Then we have

(76)

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{1/2}\|G\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}),

with norms over
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. Moreover, we have the energy estimate

(77)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{1/2}\|G\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
t_{0}=0
(the symbol bounds on
a_{\lambda}
are uniform in
t
over a compact interval).
Let
S(t,s):L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
be the unitary propagator associated to the bounded self-adjoint operator
a_{\lambda}^{w}
; it satisfies

\displaystyle(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))S(t,s)u_{0}=0,\quad S(s,s)u_{0}=u_{0}

for
t,s\in[0,T]
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
. Observe the group property
S(t,r)S(r,s)=S(t,s)
and
S(t,s)^{*}=S(t,s)^{-1}=S(s,t)
.
Set
S(t)=S(t,0)
.
Applying (
74
) to
S(t)u_{0}(x)
we get

(78)

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}S(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{q}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\leq\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

where we used the unitarity of
S(t)
and Hölder in the last estimate (recalling that
t\in[0,T]
).
By duality, this implies that

(79)

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}S(s)^{*}\chi^{w}f(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}.

Indeed, for
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{x}
,
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\langle\int_{0}^{t}S(t)^{*}\chi^{w}f(t){\rm d}t,u_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}|\leq\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}\|\chi^{w}S(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{q}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Of course, this also holds when
\chi^{w}
is replaced by
\widetilde{\chi}^{w}
. Hence, if

f,g\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}
, then

\displaystyle|\langle\int_{0}^{T}\chi^{w}S(t)S(s)^{*}\widetilde{\chi}^{w}f(s){\rm d}s,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{t,x}}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\int_{0}^{T}S(s)^{*}\widetilde{\chi}^{w}f(s){\rm d}s,\int_{0}^{T}S(t)^{*}\chi^{w}g(t){\rm d}t\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\int_{0}^{T}S(s)^{*}\tilde{\chi}^{w}f(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\int_{0}^{T}S(t)^{*}\chi^{w}g(t){\rm d}t\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}T\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}.

By the group property, this implies that

(80)

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{T}\chi^{w}S(t,s)\tilde{\chi}^{w}F(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\lesssim\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}T\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}.

Thus, an application of the Christ–Kiselev lemma
[
10
]
, as formulated e.g. in
[
46
,
51
]
, yields

(81)

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}\chi^{w}S(t,s)\tilde{\chi}^{w}F(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\lesssim\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}T\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}}.

By unitarity of
S(t,s)
, (
78
) also implies

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\chi^{w}S(t,s)u_{0}\|_{L_{t,x}^{q}}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Minkowski’s and Hölder’s inequality then yield

(82)

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{s<t}\chi^{w}S(t,s)G(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\chi^{w}S(t,s)G(s)\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}{\rm d}s\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T\|G\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

Assume now that
u
satisfies (
75
). By Duhamel’s formula,

(83)

\displaystyle u(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)G(s){\rm d}s+\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)\widetilde{\chi}^{w}F(s){\rm d}s.

Hence (
76
) follows from (
82
), (
81
) and (
78
).
Further, (
83
) and unitarity of
S(t,s)
implies

(84)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|G(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}{\rm d}s+\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)\widetilde{\chi}^{w}F(s){\rm d}s\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

(85)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{1/2}\|G\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}T^{1/2}\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t,x}},

where we used (
79
) for the third term. In addition, we used that, by the group property,
S(t,s)=S(s,t)^{*}=(S(s)S(t)^{*})^{*}=S(t)S(s)^{*}
. This proves the energy estimate (
77
).
∎

Remark 4.14
.

The proof is similar to that of
[
31
, Prop. 4.8]
. The first difference is that we omitted the curvature condition (A3) in
[
31
]
and replaced it by the (black box) assumption that the spectral cluster bound (or the equivalent quasimode estimate (
74
)) holds. In contrast, the real principal type condition (A2) in
[
31
]
is crucial for the reduction to the first order equation (
70
).
In the context of Theorem
2.1
, the condition (A3) is of course also satisfied:
The fibers of the cosphere bundle
|\xi|_{g}=1
have everywhere nonzero Gaussian curvature. But this information is now encoded in the spectral cluster bound. The second difference is that
[
31
, Proposition 4.8]
applies only to symbols
a_{\lambda^{1/2}}
in the smaller class
C^{2}S_{\lambda,\lambda^{1/2}}
, at which one would arrive, after similar reductions as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, if one had started with a
C^{1,1}
metric. Lipschitz functions are well approximated by
C^{1,1}
functions on the
\lambda^{-1/3}
scale. On this scale the wave packet parametrix constructions work,
and one can prove the spectral cluster (homogeneous Strichartz) and resolvent (inhomogeneous Strichartz) bounds concurrently. These follow from dispersive (
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
) estimates on the regularized flow corresponding to
a_{\lambda^{1/2}}
. For such estimates, the causal factor
\mathbf{1}_{t\geq s}
is innocuous. On the other hand, the flow of
a_{\lambda}
in Lemma
4.13
is not regular enough for the dispersive estimates to hold. The third difference is that we use a microlocalization in the full phase space
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}_{t,x}^{n}
, whereas Koch and Tataru
[
31
]
only assume microlocalization in
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}_{x}
(hence their estimate is stronger). We could achieve the same if we assume that (
74
) holds under this weaker assumption. However, such an estimate does in general not follow from the spectral cluster estimate (
32
).

Proof of Proposition

in the case
\epsilon\geq 1
.

Let
\widetilde{\chi}

\widetilde{\chi}=1
on

.
First order calculus allows us to replace

in (

We also note that (

(86)

\displaystyle(D_{t}-a_{\lambda}^{w}(t,x,D_{x}))(\phi(t)u)=\phi(t)(\widetilde{\chi}^{w}F+G)-\mathrm{i}\phi^{\prime}(t)u

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Let
1=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\phi_{j}(t)

\epsilon^{-1}
and

Let
\psi_{j}

I_{j}

\psi_{j}(t)=1
on the support of
\phi_{j}

If
\phi

\psi_{j}

\chi_{1}^{w}F_{j}+G_{j}

, where
\chi_{1}

and equals

on the support of
\widetilde{\chi}

(87)

\displaystyle\|G_{j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2}(I_{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}+\epsilon\|u\|_{L^{2}(I_{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}+\lambda^{-N}\|F\|_{H^{-N}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|F_{j}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

where we used standard pseudodifferential calculus for the first estimate. In the following,

is arbitrary and my change from line to line (the constant of course depends on

4.13
(with

implies

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}(\psi_{j}u)\|_{L^{q}(I_{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}\epsilon^{-1}(\|G_{j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\delta\lambda^{\gamma(q)}\|F_{j}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}),

for all

(89)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{j}\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}(I_{j}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}^{q}

\displaystyle\|\chi^{w}u\|_{L^{q}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}^{q}

L^{q^{\prime}}\subset H^{-N}
and
\delta\epsilon^{-1}\geq\lambda^{-1/2}

.

4.10.
The case
\epsilon<1

We now turn to the case
\epsilon<1
.
In view of the elliptic estimates in Proposition
4.3
(case (b) applies to arbitrary
\epsilon
) it is sufficient to prove

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}/\lambda)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1}.

where
\chi(\tau)
is a bump function adapted to
|\tau|\leq 2
(here
\tau
is unrelated to the dual variable of
t
in the previous subsections). We partially follow the strategy of
[
6
]
and represent the resolvent operator as a cosine transform,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}f:=(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{2})^{-1}f=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}\ell\lambda-\ell\epsilon}\cos(\ell\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})f{\rm d}\ell.

We will also need the localized version

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}f=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(\ell){\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}\ell\lambda-\ell\epsilon}\cos(\ell\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})f{\rm d}\ell,

where
\rho
is a bump function adapted to
[-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}]
, with
\delta_{0}\ll 1
smaller than half the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
.
We will show that

(92)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}/\lambda)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1},

This is better than the right hand side of (
33
) since
\delta^{2}\epsilon^{-1}\geq 1
. for the proof we write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}f=(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{-1}\sum_{\pm}\psi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}\pm\lambda)f,

where
\psi
is the Fourier transform of
c\rho(\ell){\rm e}^{-\epsilon\ell}H(\ell)
; here
H
is the Heaviside function and
c
is a constant that will change from line to line.
Then

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}\psi(\tau)|=|\mathcal{F}(c\ell^{k}\rho(\ell){\rm e}^{-\epsilon\ell}H(\ell))|\leq c\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell^{k}\rho(\ell){\rm d}\ell,

which means that
\psi
is smooth on the unit scale (i.e. bounds independent of
\epsilon
).
Moreover,
since the Fourier transform of
{\rm e}^{-\epsilon\ell}H(\ell)
equals
c(\tau+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{-1}
, the convolution theorem yields that

\displaystyle\psi(\tau)=c\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\rho}(\sigma)}{(\tau-\sigma)+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}{\rm d}\sigma,

from which we will conclude that
\psi(\tau)
decays like
|\tau|^{-1}
.
Indeed, by the (easy part of) the Paley–Wiener theorem,
\widehat{\rho}
is an entire function with
|\widehat{\rho}(\sigma)|\lesssim\langle\sigma\rangle^{-N}{\rm e}^{\delta_{0}|\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma|}
. Consider the contour
\Gamma_{R}
with vertices
\tau-R
,
\tau+R
,
\tau+R+2\mathrm{i}
and
\tau-R+2\mathrm{i}
, transversed counterclockwise and with
R>|\tau|\gg 1
. The residue theorem yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\frac{\widehat{\rho}(\sigma)}{(\tau-\sigma)+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}{\rm d}\sigma=\widehat{\rho}(\tau+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\tau^{-N}).

The integrals over the vertical sides are
\mathcal{O}(R^{-1})
, while the integral over the (top) horizontal side yields the leading contribution
\mathcal{O}(|\tau|^{-1})
. This shows that
|\psi(\tau)|\lesssim|\tau|^{-1}
. More generally, since

\displaystyle\partial^{k}\psi(\tau)=c\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\rho}(\sigma)}{[(\tau-\sigma)+\mathrm{i}\epsilon]^{k+1}}{\rm d}\sigma,

the same argument (note that the residue vanishes for
k\geq 1
) yields the symbol type bounds
|\partial^{k}\psi(\tau)|\leq C_{k}\langle\tau\rangle^{-1-k}
.
Hence, if we set

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)^{-1}\sum_{\pm}\psi(\tau\pm\lambda)(\tau^{2}-(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})\chi(\tau/\lambda),

we have that
|\partial^{k}\eta(\tau)|\leq C_{k}\langle\tau\rangle^{-k}
. For instance,

\displaystyle|\eta(\tau)|\leq\lambda^{-1}\sum_{\pm}\langle\tau\pm\lambda\rangle\chi(\tau/\lambda)\lesssim 1

since
\tau\lesssim\lambda
on the support of
\chi(\tau/\lambda)
.
By standard multiplier theorems (e.g.
[
49
, Corollary 4.3.2]
), we have
\|\eta(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq C_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
. Thus, by the fist part of the proof (i.e. the resolvent estimate for
\epsilon=1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}/\lambda)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\leq\|\eta(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}/\lambda)\|_{L^{q}\to L^{q}}\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1}.

The global part
\mathfrak{S}-\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}
can be estimated exactly as in
[
6
]
. Since the argument there is only given for
q=2n/(n-2)
,
n\geq 3
, we provide some details and simplifications for the case considered here. By definition,
\mathfrak{S}-\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc}=\lambda^{-1}m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})
, where
m
is the multiplier

\displaystyle m(\tau)=\frac{\lambda}{\mathrm{i}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\rho(\ell)){\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}\ell\lambda-\ell\epsilon}\cos(\ell\tau){\rm d}\ell.

The main observation is that, since
1-\rho(\ell)
vanishes near the origin, there are no boundary terms and we can integrate by parts as many times as we please. Using Euler’s formula for the cosine function, we deduce that

\displaystyle|m(\tau)|\lesssim(1+|\tau-\lambda|)^{-N}+\epsilon^{-1}(1+\epsilon^{-1}|\tau-\lambda|)^{-N},

see
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
.
We now use Lemma
3.1
with
m_{i}(\tau)=\sqrt{m(\tau)\chi(\tau/\lambda)}
,
i=1,2
, and

\displaystyle M_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\lesssim\lambda}(1+|k-\lambda|)^{-N}\|\Pi_{[k,k+1]}\|^{2}_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{2}}+\epsilon^{-1}(1+\epsilon^{-1}|k-\epsilon^{-1}\lambda|)^{-N}\|\Pi_{[\epsilon k,\epsilon(k+1)]}\|^{2}_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}+\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)}.

This yields

\displaystyle\|(\mathfrak{S}-\mathfrak{S}_{\rm loc})\chi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}/\lambda)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}=\lambda^{-1}\|m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{2}\lambda^{2\gamma(q)-1},

which completes the proof in the case
\epsilon<1
.

5.
Stability under perturbations

We will show that the resolvent estimates are stable under perturbations
V=V(\lambda)
with the mapping properties

(93)

\displaystyle V:W_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2},2}\cap W_{\lambda}^{s(q),q}\to W_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2},2}+W_{\lambda}^{-s(q),q^{\prime}},

Here,
1-s(q)=n(1/2-1/q)
and
W_{\lambda}^{s,p}
are Sobolev spaces flattened at frequency
\lambda
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{\lambda}^{s,p}}=\|(\lambda^{2}-\Delta_{g})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}}.

We assume that we have perfect estimates for the exponent
q
under consideration, i.e.

(94)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{2\sigma(q)-1},

where we recall that
\sigma(q)=n(1/2-1/q)-1/2
for
q\geq q_{n}
. This makes the the estimates compatible with Sobolev embedding. Note that the spaces in (
93
) are isomorphic as
\lambda
varies, but we ask for uniform bounds in
\lambda
. More precisely, we will assume that

(95)

\displaystyle M(\Lambda):=\sup_{\lambda\geq\Lambda}\|V(\lambda)\|_{X(\lambda)\to X^{\prime}(\lambda)}<\infty

for some (and hence all)
\Lambda\geq 1
. Here we introduced the spaces

\displaystyle X(\lambda)=W_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2},2}\cap W_{\lambda}^{s(q),q},\quad X^{\prime}(\lambda)=W_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2},2}+W_{\lambda}^{-s(q),q^{\prime}}.

To prove the stability we first add in the
L^{2}
estimates in the
L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}
resolvent bound.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that (
94
) holds. Then we have

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}u\|_{\lambda^{-1/2}L^{2}\cap\lambda^{\sigma(q)-1/2}L^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\lambda^{1/2}L^{2}+\lambda^{-\sigma(q)+1/2}L^{q^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

The
L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}
bound holds by assumption. The
L^{2}\to L^{q}
bound is equivalent to the spectral cluster bound (implication
(a)\iff(b)
in Corollary
3.5
) and hence follows from the
L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}
bound (Proposition
3.3
). The
L^{q}\to L^{2}
bound then follows by duality. The
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bound follows from the spectral theorem.
∎

Next we add in the elliptic
W_{\lambda}^{-1,2}\to W_{\lambda}^{1,2}
estimates, similar to
[
32
, Theorem 7]
, to obtain an even stronger version.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that (
94
) holds. Then there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(96)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{X^{\prime}(\lambda)\to X(\lambda)}\leq C_{0}.

Proof.

This follows from the previous lemma together with the Sobolev embeddings

(97)

\displaystyle W_{\lambda}^{1,2}\subset W_{\lambda}^{s(q),q},\quad W_{\lambda}^{-s(q),q^{\prime}}\subset W_{\lambda}^{-1,2},

and the observation that for functions localized to frequency
\lesssim\lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{\lambda}^{s(q),q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\lambda^{-s(q)}L^{q}},\quad\|u\|_{\lambda^{s(q)}L^{q^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{W_{\lambda}^{-s(q),q^{\prime}}}.

Note again that
s(q)=-\sigma(q)+1/2
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The
L^{2}
based bounds allow one to handle gradient perturbations or perturbations of the metric, similarly to
[
32
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
11
]
.

We will assume that for some
c\in(0,1)
,

(98)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=\inf\{\Lambda\geq 1:\,M(\Lambda)\leq c\,C_{0}^{-1}\}<\infty.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that (
96
) holds and that
V=V(\lambda)
is a family of perturbations satisfying (
95
), (
98
). Then

(99)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{g}+V+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{X^{\prime}(\lambda)\to X(\lambda)}\leq C_{0}(1-c)^{-1}

for all
\lambda\geq\Lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
5.2
, the second resolvent formula

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g}+V+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}=(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}(\mathbf{1}+V(\Delta_{g}+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1})

and a Neumann series argument.
∎

The estimate (
99
) is effective in the sense that it is valid for a whole family of perturbations satisfying appropriate bounds. We are now turning our attention to non-effective bounds, i.e. we fix a perturbation
V
and allow the constants in the estimates depend on
V
itself. We assume that (
93
) holds and take norms for fixed
\lambda
, say
\lambda=1
. For simplicity we only consider zero order perturbations (potentials). That is, we assume that
V\in L^{p}(M)
with
1/p=1/q^{\prime}-1/q
. Since
M
is compact, we have the inclusion
L^{p_{2}}(M)\subset L^{p_{1}}(M)
for
p_{2}\geq p_{1}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

If
n=2
or
n=3
and
q<2^{*}
, then by Lemma
5.1
we have

\displaystyle M(\Lambda)\leq\Lambda^{2\sigma(q)-1}\|V\|_{L^{p}}.

If we fix
c=1/2
in the definition of
\Lambda_{0}
, as we do now, this implies that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\leq(2C_{0}\|V\|_{L^{p}})^{\frac{1}{1-2\sigma(q)}}.

Note that
1-2\sigma(q)>0
here. The claim then follows from Proposition
5.4
.

Now consider the case
n\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}
, so that
2\sigma(q)-1=0
. For
\alpha_{0}>0
we set

\displaystyle V_{>\alpha_{0}}=V\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in M:\,|V(x)|>\alpha_{0}\}},\quad V_{\leq\alpha_{0}}=V-V_{>\alpha_{0}}.

Since
V\in L^{n/2}
we may choose
\alpha_{0}
so large that
\|V_{>\alpha_{0}}\|_{L^{n/2}}\leq C_{0}^{-1}/4
. Since
\|V_{\leq\alpha_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\alpha_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\|V\|_{X(\lambda)\to X^{\prime}(\lambda)}\leq\|V_{>\alpha_{0}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\to L^{q}}+\lambda^{-1}\|V_{\leq\alpha_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}C_{0}^{-1}+\lambda^{-1}\alpha_{0},

which yields

\displaystyle M(\Lambda)\leq\tfrac{1}{4}C_{0}^{-1}+\Lambda^{-1}\alpha_{0},\quad\Lambda_{0}\leq 4\alpha_{0}C_{0}.

Thus, the claim again follows from Proposition
5.4
.
∎

6.
Applications

6.1.
From Tomas–Stein to Kenig–Ruiz–Sogge

In the remainder of this section we will consider applications of Theorem
2.1
. Ultimately, we will obtain new uniform resolvent estimates for compact manifolds with boundary or nonsmooth metrics. As a warm-up, we start by re-proving several known resolvent estimates, using the corresponding spectral cluster bound.

The prototype of a spectral cluster bound is the Tomas–Stein inequality, which can be stated as a spectral cluster bound for the Euclidean Laplacian
-\Delta
(see
[
49
, Chapter 5]
),

(100)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+\epsilon]}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to L^{q_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\epsilon^{1/2}\lambda^{1/q_{n}}

for
\lambda,\epsilon>0
. This also follows from the unit size spectral cluster bounds

(101)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to L^{q_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/q_{n}}

by scaling. We will show that (
101
) implies the resolvent bound

(102)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta+1+\mathrm{i}0)^{-1}\|_{L^{q_{n}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to L^{q_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim 1.

This is the Kenig–Ruiz–Sogge bound in the special case of self-dual exponents (
[
27
, Theorem 2.3]
) and with spectral parameter
|z|=1
. The general case can again be obtained by scaling. Moreover, all other bounds
2\leq q\leq 2^{*}
follow from (
102
) by Sobolev embedding.
The Tomas–Stein estimate (
101
) is of the form of the abstract spectral cluster bound (
32
) with
\delta=\epsilon=1
and
\gamma(q_{n})=\sigma(q_{n})=1/q_{n}
. Note that the exponent in (
102
) is
-2/(n+1)=2/q_{n}-1
. Therefore, Theorem
4.1
implies

(103)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta+(\lambda+\mathrm{i})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q_{n}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to L^{q_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}.

Rescaling
x\to\lambda^{-1}x
then yields

(104)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta+(1+\mathrm{i}\lambda^{-1})^{2})^{-1}\|_{L^{q_{n}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to L^{q_{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim 1.

Taking the limit
\lambda\to\infty
proves (
102
).

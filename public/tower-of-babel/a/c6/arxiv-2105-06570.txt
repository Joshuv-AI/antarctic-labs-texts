Simplified Kripke semantics for K45-like Gödel modal logics and its axiomatic extensions
Ricardo Oscar Rodriguez, Olim Frits Tuyt, Francesc Esteva, Lluís Godo

Abstract




0.1
Introduction

Possibilistic logic

[
9
,
10
]
is a well-known uncertainty logic to reason with graded (epistemic) beliefs on classical propositions by means of necessity and possiblity measures.
In this setting, epistemic states of an agent are represented by possibility distributions. If
W
is a set of classical evaluations or possible worlds, for a given propositional language, a
normalized possibility distribution
on
W
is a mapping
\pi:W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
, with
\sup_{w\in W}\pi(w)=1
. This map
\pi
ranks interpretations according to its plausibility level:
\pi(w)=0
means that
w
is rejected,
\pi(w)=1
means that
w
is fully plausible, while
\pi(w)<\pi(w^{\prime})
means that
w^{\prime}
is more plausible than
w
.
A possibility distribution
\pi
induces a pair of dual possibility and necessity measures on propositions, defined respectively as:

\Pi(\varphi)=\sup\{\pi(w)\mid w\in W,w(\varphi)=1\}

N(\varphi)=\inf\{1-\pi(w)\mid w\in W,w(\varphi)=0\}

N(\varphi)
measures to what degree
\varphi
can be considered certain given the given epistemic, while
\Pi(\varphi)
measures the degree in which
\varphi
is plausible or possible. Both measures are dual in the sense that
\Pi(\varphi)=1-N(\neg\varphi)
, so that the degree of possibility of a proposition
\varphi
equates the degree in which
\neg\varphi
is not certain.
If the normalized condition over possibility distribution is dropped, then we gain the ability to deal with inconsistency. In
[
11
]
, a possibility distribution which satisfies
\sup_{w\in W}\pi(w)<1
is called sub-normal. In this case, given a set
W
of classical interpretations, a degree of inconsistency can be defined in the following way:

inc(W)=1-\sup_{w\in W}\pi(w)

When the normalised possibility distribution
\pi
is
\{0,1\}
-valued, i.e. when
\pi
is the characteristic function of a subset
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq W
, then the structure
\langle W,\pi\rangle
, or better
\langle W,E\rangle
, can be seen in fact as a K45 frame. Indeed, it is folklore that modal logic K45, which is sound and complete w.r.t. the class of Kripke frames
\langle W,R\rangle
where
R
is a euclidean and transitive binary relation, also has a simplified semantics given by the subclass of frames
\langle W,E\rangle
, where
E
is
a non-empty subset of
W
(understanding
E
as its corresponding binary relation
R_{E}
defined as
R_{E}(w,w^{\prime})
iff
w^{\prime}\in E
).

When we go beyond the classical framework of Boolean algebras of events to many-valued frameworks, one has to come up with appropriate extensions
of the notion of necessity and possibility measures for many-valued events
[
8
]
.

In the particular context of Gödel fuzzy logic
[
14
]
, natural generalizations that we will consider in this paper are the following.
A possibility distribution is as before a function
\pi\colon W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
but now,
W
is a set of Gödel propositional interpretations that induces the
following generalized possibility and necessity measures over Gödel logic propositions:

\Pi(\varphi)=\sup_{w\in W}\{\min(\pi(w),w(\varphi))\}

N(\varphi)=\inf_{w\in W}\{\pi(w)\Rightarrow w(\varphi)\},

where
\Rightarrow
is Gödel implication, that is, for each
x,y\in[0,1]
,
x\Rightarrow y=1
if
x\leq y
,
x\Rightarrow y=y
, otherwise.
1
1

1

Strictly speaking, the possibility measure is indeed a generalization of the classical one, but the necessity measure is not, since
x\Rightarrow 0\neq 1-x
.

These expressions agree with the ones commonly used in many-valued modal Kripke frames
\langle W,R\rangle
to respectively evaluate modal formulas
\Diamond\varphi
and
\Box\varphi
(see for example
[
1
]
and references
therein) when the
[0,1]
-valued accessibility relation
R:W\times W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]

is replaced by a possibility distribution
\pi:W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
as
R(w,w^{\prime})=\pi(w^{\prime})
, for any
w,w^{\prime}\in W
.

Actually, modal extensions of Gödel fuzzy logic have been studied by Caicedo and Rodríguez
[
5
]
, providing sound and complete axiomatizations for different classes of

[0,1]
-valued Kripke models. These structures are triples
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
, where
W
is a set of worlds,
R
is a
[0,1]
-valued accessibility relation, as above, and
e:W\times Var\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
is such that, for every
w\in W
,
e(w,\cdot)
is a Gödel
[0,1]
-valued evaluation of
propositional variables (more details in next section) that extends to modal formulas as follows:

\displaystyle e(w,\Diamond\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{\min(R(w,w^{\prime}),e(w^{\prime},\varphi))\}

\displaystyle e(w,\Box\varphi)

\displaystyle=\inf_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{R(w,w^{\prime})\Rightarrow e(w^{\prime},\varphi)\}.

We will denote by
{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G})
the class of
[0,1]
-models
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
where
R
satisfies the following many-valued counterpart of the classical properties:

•

Transitivity
:
\forall w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in W
,
\min(R(w,w^{\prime}),R(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}))\leq R(w,w^{\prime\prime})

•

Euclidean
:
\forall w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in W
,
\min(R(w,w^{\prime}),R(w,w^{\prime\prime}))\leq R(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})

In this setting, the class
\Pi\mathcal{G}
of
possibilistic Kripke
models

\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
, where
\pi\colon W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
is a possibility distribution (not necessarily normalized) on the set of worlds
W
, can be considered as the subclass of
{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G})
models
\langle W,R,e\rangle
where
R
is independent of the world in its first argument, in the sense that
R(w,w^{\prime})=\pi(w^{\prime})
. Since
\Pi\mathcal{G}\subsetneq{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G})
, it follows that the set
Val({\cal K}45(\mathbf{G}))
of valid formulas in the class of

{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G})
is a subset of the set
Val(\Pi\mathcal{G})
of valid formulas in the class
\Pi\mathcal{G}
, i.e.
Val({\cal K}45(\mathbf{G}))\subseteq Val(\Pi\mathcal{G})
. The interesting question is therefore whether the converse inclusion
Val(\Pi\mathcal{G})\subseteq Val({\cal K}45(\mathbf{G}))
holds, and thus whether the class
\Pi\mathcal{G}
provides a simplified possibilistic semantics for the modal logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
.

In
[
2
]
the authors claim to provide a positive answer, not for the class
{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G}))
models but for the subclass of
{\cal KD}45(\mathbf{G}))
models, i.e. those
{\cal K}45(\mathbf{G}))

further satisfying the many-valued counterpart of seriality:

•

Seriality

\forall w\in W
,
\sup_{w^{\prime}\in W}R(w,w^{\prime})=1

Indeed, they prove that the logic
KD45(\mathbf{G})
, complete w.r.t. the
class of
{\cal KD}45(\mathbf{G}))
models, is also complete w.r.t. the class
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
of possibilistic models
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
, where
\pi:W\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
is a
normalized
possibility distribution on
W
.
Unfortunately, it has to be noted that the completeness proof in
[
2
]
has some flaws.
In this paper we provide a correct proof, not only for the completeness of
KD45(\mathbf{G})
w.r.t. to its corresponding class of possibilistic frames, but also for the weaker logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
accounting for partially inconsistent possibilistic Kripke frames.

This paper is organized as follows: Section

Section

0.2
Preliminaries on propositional and modal Gödel fuzzy logic

This section is devoted to preliminaries on the Gödel fuzzy logic G. We present their syntax and semantics, their main logical properties and the notation we use throughout the article.

The language of Gödel propositional logic
\mathcal{L}(V)
is built as usual from a countable set of propositional variables
V
, the constant
\bot
and the binary connectives
\land
(conjunction) and
\mathop{\rightarrow}
(implication).
Further connectives are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\top

is

\displaystyle\bot\rightarrow\bot

\displaystyle\varphi\vee\psi

is

\displaystyle((\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow\psi)\wedge((\psi\rightarrow\varphi)\rightarrow\varphi),

\displaystyle\lnot\varphi

is

\displaystyle\varphi\rightarrow\bot,

\displaystyle\varphi\equiv\psi

is

\displaystyle(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\wedge(\psi\rightarrow\varphi).

The following are the
axioms
of
G
:

(A1)

(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow((\psi\rightarrow\chi)\rightarrow(\varphi\rightarrow\chi))

(A2)

(\varphi\wedge\psi)\rightarrow\varphi

(A3)

(\varphi\wedge\psi)\rightarrow(\psi\wedge\varphi)

(A4a)

(\varphi\rightarrow(\psi\rightarrow\chi))\rightarrow((\varphi\wedge\psi)\rightarrow\chi)

(A4b)

((\varphi\wedge\psi)\rightarrow\chi)\rightarrow(\varphi\rightarrow(\psi\rightarrow\chi))

(A6)

\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\varphi\wedge\varphi)

(A7)

((\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow\chi)\rightarrow(((\psi\rightarrow\varphi)\rightarrow\chi)\rightarrow\chi)

(A8)

\bot\rightarrow\varphi

The
deduction rule
of
G
is modus ponens.

Gödel logic can be obtained in fact as the axiomatic extension of Hájek’s Basic Fuzzy Logic BL
[
14
]
(which is the logic of continuous t-norms and their residua) by means of the contraction axiom (A6)
\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\varphi\land\varphi)
. Since the unique idempotent continuous t-norm is the minimum, this yields that Gödel logic is strongly complete with respect to its standard fuzzy semantics that interprets formulas over the structure
[0,1]_{\mathrm{G}}:=\langle[0,1],\min,\Rightarrow_{\mathrm{G}},0,1\rangle
2
2

2

Called standard Gödel algebra.
, i.e. semantics defined by truth-evaluations
e
such that
e(\varphi\land\psi)=\min(e(\varphi),e(\psi))
,
e(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)=e(\varphi)\Rightarrow_{\mathrm{G}}e(\psi)
and
e(\bot)=0
.

Gödel logic can also be seen as the axiomatic extension of intuitionistic propositional logic by the prelinearity axiom
(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\lor(\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}\varphi)
. Its algebraic semantics is therefore given by the variety of prelinear Heyting algebras, also known as Gödel algebras. In fact, it is sound and complete in the following stronger sense, see
[
4
]
.

Proposition 0.2.1
.

i)

If
T\cup\{\varphi\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X),

then
T\vdash_{G}\varphi
implies
\inf v(T)\leq v(\varphi)

for any valuation
v:X\rightarrow[0,1]
.

ii)

If
T
is countable,
and
T\nvdash_{G}\varphi_{i_{1}}\vee..\vee\varphi_{i_{n}}

for each finite subset of a countable family
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
there is
an evaluation
v:\mathcal{L}(X)\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
v(\theta)=1
for all

\theta\in T
and
v(\varphi_{i})<1
for all
i\in I
.

We mention in passing that the algebraic semantics of Gödel algebra is is given by the variety of prelinear Heyting algebras, also known as Gödel algebras. A Gödel algebra is a structure
{\bf A}=\langle A,*,\Rightarrow,0,1\rangle
which is a (bounded, integral, commutative) residuated lattice satisfying the contraction equation

x*x=x,

and pre-linearity equation

(x\Rightarrow y)\lor(y\Rightarrow x)=1,

where
x\lor y=((x\Rightarrow y)\Rightarrow y)*((y\Rightarrow x)\Rightarrow x)
).

Let us consider a modal expansion of Gödel logic with two operators
\Box
and
\Diamond
. The set of formulas
\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(V)
is built as
\mathcal{L}(V)
(always assuming countability of the set of propositional variables
V
) but extending the set of operations with two unary symbols
\Box
and
\Diamond
. Whenever
V
is clear from the context we will simply write
\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}
.

In the style introduced by Fitting
[
12
,
13
]
and studied in the works mentioned in the introduction, we define the Gödel Modal Logic as arising from its semantic definition. This is given by enriching usual Kripke models with evaluations over the previous standard algebra, as in
[
4
,
5
]
and others. Formally:

Definition 0.2.1
.

A
Gödel-Kripke model
is a structure
\langle W,R,e\rangle
where
W
is a non-empty set of so-called worlds, and
R\colon W\times W\rightarrow[0,1]
and
e\colon V\times W\rightarrow[0,1]
are arbitrary mappings.

The evaluation
e
can be uniquely extended to a map with domain
W\times\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}
in such a way that it is a propositional Gödel homomorphism (for the propositional connectives) and where the modal operators are interpreted as infima and suprema, namely:

•

e(v,\bot)\coloneqq 0
,

•

e(v,\varphi\star\psi)\coloneqq e(v,\varphi)\star e(v,\psi)
for
\star\in\{\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow\}
,

•

e(v,\Box\varphi)\coloneqq\bigwedge_{w\in W}(R(v,w)\rightarrow e(w,\varphi)),

•

e(v,\Diamond\varphi)\coloneqq\bigvee_{w\in W}(R(v,w)\wedge e(w,\varphi)).

A formula
\varphi
is
valid
in a Gödel-Kripke model
\langle W,R,e\rangle
, if
e(w,\varphi)=1
for all
w\in W
. We will denote by
\cal G
the class of Gödel Kripke models and will say that a formula
\varphi
is
\cal G
-valid
, written
\models_{\mathcal{G}}\varphi
, if
\varphi
is valid in all Gödel Kripke models.

In their paper
[
5
]
Caicedo and Rodriguez define the logic
K(\mathbf{G})
as the smallest set of formulas containing some axiomatic version of Gödel-Dummet
propositional calculus, that is, Heyting calculus plus the prelinearity law, and the following additional axioms and rule:

(K_{\Box})

\Box(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}(\Box\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)

(K_{\Diamond})

\Diamond(\varphi\lor\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}(\Diamond\varphi\lor\Diamond\psi)

(F_{\Box})

\Box\top

(P)

\Box(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}(\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\psi)

(FS2)

(\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)

(Nec)

from
\varphi
infer
\Box\varphi

They denote deduction in this system as
\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}
and they show the following result:

Theorem 0.2.1

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}
. Then

\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}\varphi\text{ if and only if }\models_{\mathcal{G}}\varphi.

Proofs with assumptions are allowed with the restriction that
(Nec)
may be applied only when the premise is a
theorem.
This restriction allows for the following convenient reduction (see
[
4
]
).

Lemma 0.2.1
.

Let
ThK(\mathbf{G})
be the set of theorems of
K(\mathbf{G})
with no assumptions, then for any theory
T
and formula
\varphi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}:T\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
if and only if
T\cup ThK(G)\vdash_{\mathbf{G}}\varphi
where
\vdash_{\mathbf{G}}
denotes deduction in Gödel fuzzy logic G.

The following are some theorems of
K(\mathbf{G})
, see
[
5
]
:

\begin{array}[]{rlrl}T1.&\lnot\Diamond\theta\leftrightarrow\square\lnot\theta&\\
T2.&\lnot\lnot\square\theta\rightarrow\square\lnot\lnot\theta&\\
T3.&\Diamond\lnot\lnot\varphi\rightarrow\lnot\lnot\Diamond\varphi&\\
T4.&(\square\varphi\rightarrow\Diamond\psi)\vee\square((\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow\psi)\\
T5.&\Diamond(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}(\square\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\psi)\\
\end{array}

The first one is an axiom in Fitting’s systems in
[
12
]
, the next two were
introduced in
[
5
]
, the fourth one will be useful in our completeness
proof and is the only one depending on prelinearity. The last is known as
the first connecting axiom given by Fischer Servi.
In addition, it is interesting to notice that the following rule is derivable:

\begin{array}[]{rl}(Nec_{\Diamond})&\mbox{from }\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi\mbox{ infer }\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\psi\\
\end{array}

Indeed, if
\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi
, then
\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}\Box(\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)
by

(P)
and modus ponens we get
\vdash_{K(\mathbf{G})}\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\psi
.

0.3
The logic
K45(\mathbf{G})

The logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
is defined by adding to
K(\mathbf{G})
the following axioms:

(4_{\Box})

\Box\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\Box\varphi

(4_{\Diamond})

\Diamond\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\varphi

(5_{\Box})

\Diamond\Box\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\varphi

(5_{\Diamond})

\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\Diamond\varphi

KD45(\mathbf{G})

(D)

\Diamond\top

KT45(\mathbf{G})

T\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
iff
T\cup ThK45(\mathbf{G})\vdash_{G}\varphi
. By the deduction theorem of Gödel logic, the latter is equivalent to
ThK45(\mathbf{G})\vdash_{G}T^{\land}\mathop{\rightarrow}\varphi
, and by (i) again, this is equivalent to
\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}T^{\land}\mathop{\rightarrow}\varphi
.

\blacksquare

Remark 0.3.1
.

It is worth noting that for any valuation
v
such that
v(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
there is no formula
\varphi
such that
v(\Diamond\top)<v(\nabla\varphi)<1
with
\nabla\in\{\Box,\Diamond\}
because both formulae
(\Box\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\varphi)\vee\Box\varphi
and
\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\top
are in

ThK45(\mathbf{G})
.

0.4
Simplified Kripke semantics and completeness

In this section we will show that
K45(\mathbf{G})
is complete with respect to a class of simplified Kripke Gödel frames.

Definition 0.4.1
.

A possibilistic Kripke frame, or
\Pi
-frame, is a
structure
\langle W,\pi\rangle
where
W

is a non-empty
set of worlds
, and
\pi:W\rightarrow[0,1]
is a possibility distribution over
W
.

A possibilistic Gödel Kripke model,
\Pi G
-model
for short, is a triple
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
where
\langle W,\pi\rangle
is a
\Pi
-frame and
e:W\times Var\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
provides a Gödel evaluation of variables in each world.
For each
w\in W
,
e(w,-)
extends to arbitrary formulas in the usual way for the propositional connectives and for modal operators in the following way:

e(w,\Box\varphi):=\inf_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{\pi(w^{\prime})\Rightarrow e(w^{\prime},\varphi)\}

e(w,\Diamond\varphi):=\sup_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{\min(\pi(w^{\prime}),e(w^{\prime},\varphi))\}
.

If
\pi
is normalised, i.e. if
\sup_{w\in W}\pi(w)=1
, then
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
will be called a normalised possibilistic Gödel Kripke model, or
\Pi^{*}G
-model
. A formula
\varphi
is
valid
in a
\Pi G
-model
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
if
e(w,\varphi)=1
for all
w\in W
.

We will denote by
\Pi\cal G
the class of possibilistic Gödel Kripke models, and by
\Pi^{*}\cal G
the subclass of normalised models. We say that a formula
\varphi
is
\Pi\cal G
-valid
, written
\models_{\Pi\mathcal{G}}\varphi
, if
\varphi
is valid in all possibilistic Gödel Kripke models, and
\Pi^{*}\cal G
-valid
, written
\models_{\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\varphi
, if
\varphi
is valid in all normalised possibilistic Gödel Kripke model.

Observe that the evaluation of formulas beginning with a modal operator is in fact independent from the current world. Also note that the
e(\cdot,\Box\varphi)
and
e(\cdot,\Diamond\varphi)
are in fact generalisations for Gödel logic propositions of the necessity and possibility degrees of
\varphi
introduced in Section
0.1
for classical propositions, although now they are not dual (with respect to Gödel negation) any longer.

In the rest of this section we are going to show in detail a weak completeness proof of the logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
(resp.
KD45(\mathbf{G})
) with respect to the class
\Pi\mathcal{G}
(resp. the subclass
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
) of possibilistic Gödel Kripke models. In fact one can prove a slightly stronger result, namely completeness for deductions from finite theories.

We start with the case of
K45(\mathbf{G})
. In what follows, for any formula
\varphi
we denote by
Sub(\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}

the set of subformulas of
\varphi
and containing
the formula
\bot
. Moreover, let
X:=\{\square\theta,\Diamond\theta:\theta\in\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}\}
be the set of formulas in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}

beginning with a modal operator; then
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}(Var)=\mathcal{L(}Var\cup X)
. That is, any formula in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}(Var)
may be seen as a propositional Gödel formula built from the extended set
of propositional variables
Var\cup X
. In addition, for a given formula

\varphi
, let
\sim_{\varphi}
be the equivalence relation on
[0,1]^{Var\cup X}\times[0,1]^{Var\cup X}

defined as follows:

u\sim_{\varphi}w\mbox{ iff }\forall\psi\in Sub(\varphi):u(\Box\psi)=w(\Box\psi)\mbox{ and }u(\Diamond\psi)=w(\Diamond\psi).

Now, assume that a formula
\varphi
is not a theorem of
K45(\mathbf{G})
. Hence by completeness of Gödel calculus and Lemma
0.2.1
, there exists a Gödel valuation
v
such that
v(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
and
v(\varphi)<1
.
With the valuation
v
now fixed, we follow the usual canonical model construction, defining a canonical
\Pi G
-model
\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{\varphi}
in which we will show
\varphi
is not valid.

The
canonical model

\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{\varphi}=\langle W^{v}_{\varphi},\pi^{v}_{\varphi},e^{v}_{\varphi}\rangle
is defined
as follows:

•

W^{v}_{\varphi}
is the set
\{u\in[0,1]^{Var\cup X}\mid u\sim_{\varphi}v\mbox{ and }u(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1\}
.

•

\pi^{v}_{\varphi}(u)=\inf_{\psi\in Sub(\varphi)}\{\min(v(\Box\psi)\rightarrow u(\psi),u(\psi)\rightarrow v(\Diamond\psi))\}.

•

e^{v}_{\varphi}(u,p)=u(p)
for any
p\in Var
.

In this context, we call the elements of
\Delta_{\varphi}:=\{\square\theta,\Diamond\theta:\theta\in Sub(\varphi)\}
the
fixed points
of the Canonical Model.
Note that having
\nu(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
does not guarantee that
\nu
belongs to the canonical model because it may not take the appropriated values for the fixed points, i.e. it may be that
u\not\sim_{\varphi}\nu
. However, the next lemma shows how to, under certain conditions, transform such an evaluation into another belonging to the canonical model.

Lemma 0.4.1
.

Let
u\in W^{v}_{\varphi}
and let
\nu:{Var\cup X}\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
be a Gödel valuation. Define
\delta=\max\{u(\lambda):\nu(\lambda)<1\mbox{ and }\lambda\in\Delta_{\varphi}\}
and
\beta=\min\{u(\lambda):\nu(\lambda)=1\mbox{ and }\lambda\in\Delta_{\varphi}\}
. If
\nu
satisfies the following conditions:

a.

\nu(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
.

b.

For all
\lambda\in X
, we have
u(\lambda)>\delta\Rightarrow\nu(\lambda)=1
.

c.

For any
\psi,\phi\in\{\lambda:u(\lambda)\leq\delta\mbox{ and }\lambda\in X\}
:
u(\psi)<u(\phi)
implies
\nu(\psi)<\nu(\phi)
.

d.

For any
\psi,\phi\in\Delta_{\varphi}:u(\psi)\leq u(\phi)
implies
\nu(\psi)\leq\nu(\phi)
.

then, there exists a Gödel valuation
w\in W^{v}_{\varphi}
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
with
\delta+\varepsilon<\beta
, and for any formulae
\psi
and
\phi
, the following conditions hold:

1.

\nu(\psi)=1
implies
w(\psi)\geq\delta+\varepsilon
.

2.

\nu(\psi)<1
implies
w(\psi)<\delta+\varepsilon
.

3.

1\neq\nu(\psi)\leq\nu(\phi)
implies
w(\psi)\leq w(\phi)
.

4.

\nu(\psi)<\nu(\phi)
implies
w(\psi)<w(\phi)
.

First of all, notice that if
\nu
satisfies the conditions
c
and
d
, then necessarily
\delta<\beta
. Indeed, suppose
\delta\geq\beta
. Then there are at least two formulas
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
in
\Delta_{\varphi}
such that
\nu(\theta_{1})<1
,
\nu(\theta_{2})=1
and
\delta\geq u(\theta_{1})\geq u(\theta_{2})\geq\beta
. Note that the case
u(\theta_{1})>u(\theta_{2})
is not possible because it would violate condition
c
, and the case
u(\theta_{1})=u(\theta_{2})
is also imposible because it would then violate condition
d
.

Let
B=\{\nu(\lambda):\lambda\in\Delta_{\varphi},\nu(\lambda)<1\}\cup\{0\}=\{b_{0}=0<b_{1}<\ldots<b_{N}\}
. Obviously,
b_{N}<1
. For each
0\leq i\leq N
, pick
\lambda_{i}\in\Delta_{\varphi}
such that
\nu(\lambda_{i})=b_{i}
.
Define now a continuous strictly function
g:[0,1]\mapsto[0,\delta+\varepsilon)\cup\{1\}
such
that

g(1)=1

g(b_{i})=u(\lambda_{i})
for every
0\leq i\leq N

g[(b_{N},1)]=(\delta,\delta+\varepsilon)

Notice that
\delta=g(b_{N})
. In addition, define another continuous strictly increasing function
h:[0,1]\mapsto[\delta+\varepsilon,1]
such that

h(0)=\delta+\varepsilon

h[(0,\beta)]=(\delta+\varepsilon,\beta)

h(x)=x
, for
x\in[\beta,1]

Then we define the valuation
w:Var\cup X\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
as follows:

w(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(\nu(p)),&\mbox{if }\nu(p)<1,\\
h(u(p)),&\mbox{if }\nu(p)=1.\end{array}\right.

Next step is to prove that
w
satisfies the required Properties 1–4. And we are going to prove it by induction on the maximum of the complexity of both formulae
\psi,\phi
.

First, we consider the base case when both
\psi
and
\phi
belong to
Var\cup X
. Then

1.

By definition of
w
,
\nu(\psi)=1
implies
w(\psi)=h(u(\psi))\geq\delta+\varepsilon
. The same happens for
\phi
.

2.

By definition of
w
,
\nu(\psi)<1
implies
w(\psi)=g(\nu(\psi))<\delta+\varepsilon
. The same happens for
\phi
.

3.

Suppose
1\neq\nu(\psi)\leq\nu(\phi)
. Since
\nu(\psi)<1
, by definition of
w
, we have
w(\psi)=g(\nu(\psi))<\delta+\varepsilon
. Now, we analyse different cases.
If
\nu(\phi)<1
then
w(\phi)=g(\nu(\phi))\geq g(\nu(\psi))=w(\psi)
because
g
is strictly increasing. Otherwise, If
\nu(\phi)=1
then
w(\phi)=h(u(\phi))\geq\delta+\varepsilon>w(\psi)=g(\nu(\psi))
.

4.

Suppose
\nu(\psi)<\nu(\phi)
. In this case, the proof is similar to the previous one. If
\nu(\psi)<1
and
\nu(\phi)=1
then
w(\phi)=h(u(\phi))\geq\delta+\varepsilon>g(\nu(\psi))=w(\psi)
. On
the contrary, if both
\nu(\psi)<1
and
\nu(\phi)<1
then
w(\phi)=g(\nu(\phi))>g(\nu(\psi))=w(\psi)
because
g
is strictly increasing.
This case will become important when we try to prove that
w
satisfies the axioms of
K45(\mathbf{G})
.

In fact, in the case both formulae
\psi
and
\phi
belong to
Var\cup X
we can prove that two further conditions hold:

5.

If
\nu(\psi)=\nu(\phi)=1
and
u(\psi)\leq u(\phi)
then

w(\psi)\leq w(\phi)
.

6.

If
\nu(\psi)=\nu(\phi)=1
and
u(\psi)<u(\phi)
then
w(\psi)<w(\phi)
.

Indeed, by definition of
w
, we have
w(\psi)=h(u(\psi))
and
w(\phi)=h(u(\phi))
, and since
h
is strictly increasing,
u(\psi)\leq u(\phi)
(resp.
u(\psi)<u(\phi)
) implies
h(u(\psi))\leq h(u(\phi))
(resp.
h(u(\psi))<h(u(\phi))
), as desired. These properties will become important when proving that
w
satisfies the axioms of
K45(\mathbf{G})
.

Now, we consider the inductive step.

Claim
:
Induction hypothesis (IH): if Properties 1–4 are satisfied by formulas with complexity at most
n
, then these properties also hold for formulae with complexity at most
n+1
.

The proof of this claim is quite technical and it is moved to the appendix.

Finally, we prove that
w\in W^{v}_{\varphi}
, i.e. we prove that both
w\sim_{\varphi}v
and
w(ThK45({\cal G}))=1
.

(i)

By definition,
w\sim_{\varphi}v
iff
w(\Box\phi)=v(\Box\phi)
and
w(\Diamond\phi)=v(\Diamond\phi)
for all
\phi\in Sub(\varphi)
. Let
A\in\{\Box\phi,\Diamond\phi\}\subseteq\Delta_{\varphi}
.
By definition of
w
,
w(A)=g(\nu(A))
if
\nu(A)<1
, and
w(A)=h(u(A))
, otherwise. Since
A\in\Delta_{\varphi}
, if
\nu(A)<1
then, by construction (
u(A)\leq\delta
), we have
g(\nu(A))=u(A)\leq\delta
, and if
\nu(A)=1
, again by construction (
(u(A)\geq\beta
), we have
h(u(A))=u(A)
. So, we have proved that
w\sim_{\varphi}u
, but by hypothesis
u\sim_{\varphi}v
as well, thus
w\sim_{\varphi}v
as well.

(ii)

We first prove that all axioms of
K45(\mathbf{G})
are evaluated to 1 by
w
. The axioms of
\mathcal{G}
are evaluated to
1
by any Gödel valuation. As for the specific axioms of
K45(\mathbf{G})
(i.e. axioms
(4_{\Box}),(4_{\Diamond}),(5_{\Box}),(5_{\Diamond})
), observe that all these
axioms are of the form
\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi
for some
\phi,\psi\in X
. Then it is enough to prove that for any
\varphi,\psi\in X
, if
\nu(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)=1
then
w(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)=1
.
Indeed, if
\nu(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)=1
, then
\nu(\phi)\leq\nu(\psi)
and we have two possibilities:

–

if
1\neq\nu(\phi)\leq\nu(\psi)
, then by Property 3, we have
w(\phi)\leq w(\psi)
.

–

if
\nu(\phi)=\nu(\psi)=1
, then by definition of
w
,
w(\phi)=h(u(\phi))
and
w(\psi)=h(u(\psi))
. But since
u\in W^{v}_{\varphi}
,
we have
u(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)=1
, because we assume that
\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi\in K45(\mathbf{G})
with
\phi,\psi\in X
. Thus
u(\phi)\leq u(\psi)
and, by Property 5, we know
w(\phi)=h(u(\phi))\leq h(u(\psi))=w(\psi)
.

Finally, let us consider the axioms of
K(\mathbf{G})
(Section 2). We have to prove that
w(\chi)=1
for each of such axioms
\chi
, knowing by assumption that
\nu(\chi)=1
. The case of
(F_{\Box})
is easy since
(F_{\Box})\in X
, and then,
by definition,
w(F_{\Box})=h(u(F_{\Box}))=1
.
As for axiom
(K_{\Box})
, note that, using propositional reasoning, it can be equivalently expressed first as
(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\land\Box\phi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi
, and then as
(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)\lor(\Box\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)
. Therefore, if
\nu(K_{\Box})=1
, it means either
\nu(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=1
or
\nu(\Box\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=1
. But these two cases concern implications of formulas from
X
, and hence we are in the same situation above as with the specific axioms of

K45(\mathbf{G})
, and hence using the same reasoning, we can conclude that either
w(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=1
or
w(\Box\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=1
.

The case of axioms
(K_{\Diamond})
and
(P)
can be dealt in an analogous way, as they can be written as a disjunction of implications of formulas from
X
. So the case left is that of axiom
(FS2)
,
(\Diamond\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)
.

By hypothesis, we know
\nu(FS2)=1
, that is
\nu(\Diamond\phi)\Rightarrow\nu(\Box\psi)\leq\nu(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))
. According to that,
we have to prove
w(FS2)=1
is as well.
Notice that, since
\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)
is a tautology,
u(\Box(\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)))=\nu(\Box(\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)))=1
. Then, by definition,
w(\Box(\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)))=1
as well and because axiom (K) is valid for

, we have
w(\Box\psi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))=1
, i.e.
w(\Box\psi)\leq w(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))
.

Now, we consider the following cases:

–

Case
u(\Diamond\phi)\leq u(\Box\psi)
. Then
u(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))=1
which implies
\nu(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))=1
. Hence, by construction,
w(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))=h(u(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi)))=1
.

–

Case
u(\Diamond\phi)>u(\Box\psi)
. Here we distinguish three subcases:

*

u(\Box\psi)\leq\delta<u(\Diamond\phi)
: by Conditions
b
and
c
,
\nu(\Diamond\phi)=1
and
\nu(\Box\psi)<1
, respectively. Therefore, by Property 4,
w(\Box\psi)<w(\Diamond\phi)
and
thus
w(\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=w(\Box\psi)\leq w(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))
and hence
w(FS2)=1
.

*

\delta<u(\Box\psi)<u(\Diamond\phi)
: by Condition
b
,
1=\nu(\Diamond\phi)=\nu(\Box\psi)\leq\nu(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))
. Thus, by construction,
w(FS2)=(h(u(\Diamond\varphi))\Rightarrow h(u(\Box\psi)))\Rightarrow h(u(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))=1
.

*

u(\Box\psi)<u(\Diamond\phi)\leq\delta
: by Condition
c
,
\nu(\Box\psi)<\nu(\Diamond\phi)
, and by Property 4,
w(\Box\psi)<w(\Diamond\phi)
, and hence
w(\Diamond\varphi\mathop{\rightarrow}\Box\psi)=w(\Box\psi)\leq w(\Box(\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi))
, and hence
w(FS2)=1
.

So far, we have proved that
w(\phi)=1
if
\phi
is a
K45(\mathbf{G})
axiom. To conclude the proof, we need to extend this result to the rest of the formulas in
ThK45({\mathbf{G}})
. Recall that a formula
\phi
in the set
ThK45({\mathbf{G}})
is either an axiom of
K45(\mathbf{G})
or
\phi
can be proved from the axioms and rules of inference
K45(\mathbf{G})
. In the latter case, there is non-empty finite sequence of formulae
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{n}
, with
\phi_{n}=\psi
, and each
\phi_{i}
is either an axiom or it has been obtained by application of an inference rule on some of the preceding formulae
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{i-1}
. We proceed by induction on the length
n
of the sequence. The unique interesting case is when
\phi_{n}
is obtained by applying the (Nec) rule to some
\phi_{j}
with

, i.e.
\phi_{n}=\Box\phi_{j}
. It is clear that
\phi_{j}\in ThK45({\mathbf{G}})
, and by the inductive hypothesis,
w(\phi_{j})=1
. Of course,
\Box\phi_{j}\in ThK45({\mathbf{G}})
as well, and thus
\nu(\Box\xi)=1
too. But since
\Box\phi_{j}\in X
, by definition of

, we hace
w(\psi)=w(\Box\phi_{j})=h(u(\Box\phi_{j}))=1
.

Completeness will follow from the next truth-lemma.

Lemma 0.4.2

(Truth-lemma)
.

e^{v}_{\varphi}(u,\psi)=u(\psi)
for any
\psi\in Sub(\varphi)
and any
u\in W^{v}_{\varphi}
.

For simplicity, we will write
W^{c}
,
\pi^{c}
and
e^{c}
for
W^{v}_{\varphi},\pi^{v}_{\varphi}
and
e^{v}_{\varphi}
, respectively. We prove the identity by
induction on the complexity of the formulas in
Sub(\varphi)
, considered
now as elements
of
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}(Var)
. For
\bot
and the propositional
variables in
Sub(\varphi)
the equation holds by definition. The only non trivial
inductive steps are:
e^{c}(u,\Box\psi)=u(\Box\psi)
and
e^{c}(u,\Diamond\psi)=u(\Diamond\psi)
for
\Box\psi,\Diamond\psi\in Sub(\varphi).
By the inductive hypothesis we may assume that
e^{c}(u^{\prime},\psi)=u^{\prime}(\psi)
for every
u^{\prime}\in W^{c};
thus we must prove

\displaystyle\inf_{u^{\prime}\in W^{c}}\{\pi^{c}(u^{\prime})\Rightarrow u^{\prime}(\psi)\}=u(\Box\psi)

(1)

\displaystyle\sup_{u^{\prime}\in W^{c}}\{\min(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),u^{\prime}(\psi))\}=u(\Diamond\psi)

(2)

By definition,
\pi^{c}(u^{\prime})\leq(v(\Box\psi)\Rightarrow u^{\prime}(\psi))
and
\pi^{c}(u^{\prime})\leq(u^{\prime}(\psi)\Rightarrow v(\Diamond\psi))
for any
\psi\in Sub(\varphi)
and
u^{\prime}\in W;

therefore,
u(\Box\psi)=v(\Box\psi)\leq(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime})\Rightarrow u^{\prime}(\psi))
and
\min(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),u^{\prime}(\psi))\leq v(\Diamond\psi)=u(\Diamond\psi).
Taking infimum over
u^{\prime}
in the first
inequality and the supremum in the second we get

u(\Box\psi)\leq\inf_{u^{\prime}\in W^{c}}\{\pi^{c}(u^{\prime})\Rightarrow u^{\prime}(\psi)\},\ \sup_{u^{\prime}\in W^{c}}\{\min(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),u^{\prime}(\psi))\}\leq u(\Diamond\psi).

Hence, if
u(\Box\psi)=1
and
u(\Diamond\psi)=0
we directly obtain (
1
) and (
2
), respectively. Therefore, it only remains to prove
the next two claims for
\Box\psi,\Diamond\psi\in Sub(\varphi)
.

Claim 1
.
If
u(\Box\psi)=\alpha<1
then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a valuation
w\in W^{c}
such that
\pi^{c}(w)>w(\psi)
and
w(\psi)<\alpha+\varepsilon
, and thus
(\pi^{c}(w)\Rightarrow w(\psi))<\alpha+\varepsilon
.

Claim 2.
If
u(\Diamond\psi)=\alpha>0
then, for any small enough
\varepsilon>0,
there exists a valuation
w^{\prime}\in W^{c}
such that
\min(w^{\prime}(\psi),\pi^{c}(w^{\prime}))\geq\alpha-\varepsilon
.

The proofs of these two claims are rather involved and they can be found in the appendix.

\blacksquare

Theorem 0.4.1

(Weak completeness
K45(\mathbf{G})
)
.

For any formula
\varphi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}
,
\models_{\Pi\mathcal{G}}\varphi
iff
\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi.

One direction is soundness, and it is easy to check that the axioms are valid in the class
\Pi\mathcal{G}
of models. As for the other direction, assume
\not\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi.
Then
ThK45(\mathbf{G})\not\vdash_{G}\varphi
by Lemma
0.2.1
, and thus
there is, by Proposition
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
, a Gödel valuation
v:Var\cup X\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
v(\varphi)<v(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1.
Then
v
is a world of the canonical model
\mathfrak{M}_{\varphi}^{v}
and by Lemma
0.4.2
,
e_{\varphi}^{v}(v,\varphi)=v(\varphi)<1.
Thus
\not\models_{\Pi\cal G}\varphi
.

\blacksquare

In addition, it is also easy to generalize last proof for getting completeness for deductions from finite theories as it is shown by the next theorem:

Theorem 0.4.2

(Finite strong completeness
K45(\mathbf{G})
)
.

For any
finite theory
T
and formula
\varphi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}
, we have:
T\models_{\Pi\mathcal{G}}\varphi
iff
T\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
.

The proof is an easy adaptation of the one of weak completeness. We will only mention the main differences. If
T\not\vdash_{K45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
, by completeness of Gödel logic and Lemma
0.3.2
, there exists a Gödel valuation
v
such that
v(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
,
v(T)=1
and
v(\varphi)<1
. Now, in order to build a canonical model, we need to take into account not only
v
and
\varphi
but also
T
. To do that we have to follow the very same steps as before but replacing everywhere the set
Sub(\varphi)
of subformulas of
\varphi
by the larger set
Sub(T,\varphi)
of subformulas of
T\cup\{\varphi\}
, i.e.
Sub(T,\varphi)=\bigcup_{\psi\in T\cup\{\varphi\}}Sub(\psi)
. Let us denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{T,\varphi}=\langle W^{c},\pi^{c},e^{c}\rangle
the canonical model built accordingly, where
v\in W^{c}
. Note that
there is no need of any modification in neither Lemma
0.4.1
nor the Truth Lemma
0.4.2
(except for replacing
Sub(\varphi)
by
Sub(T,\varphi)
in its statement). Then the theorem follows by observing that
Lemma
0.4.2
guarantees that
e^{c}(v,\psi)=v(\psi)=1
for all
\psi\in T
while
e^{c}(v,\varphi)=v(\varphi)<1
. Therefore,
T\not\models_{\Pi\mathcal{G}}\varphi
.

\blacksquare

Actually, the proofs for weak and finite strong completeness of
K45(\mathbf{G})
with respect to the class of simplified possibilistic models
\Pi\mathcal{G}
easily generalize to completeness of
KD45(\mathbf{G})
, the axiomatic extension
K45(\mathbf{G})
with axiom

, with respect to the class of
normalized
possibilistic models
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
.

Corollary 0.4.1

(Finite strong completeness
KD45(\mathbf{G})
)
.

For any
finite theory
T
and formula
\varphi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}
, we have:
T\models_{\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\varphi
iff
T\vdash_{KD45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
.

Proof.

We only consider the proof of weak completeness, its extension to a proof
of finite strong completeness can be then devised as in Theorem
0.4.2
. Indeed, the proof only needs small adaptations to the one
for the case of
K45(\mathbf{G})
. To start with, for technical reasons that will become clear later, we will actually prove that
\not\vdash_{KD45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi^{\prime}
implies
\not\models_{\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\varphi^{\prime}
, where
\varphi^{\prime}=\Diamond\top\mathop{\rightarrow}\varphi
. Note that since
\Diamond\top
is an axiom of
KD45(\mathbf{G})
and is valid in the class of frames
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
, the former condition is indeed equivalent to prove that
\not\vdash_{KD45(\mathbf{G})}\varphi
implies
\not\models_{\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\varphi
.
Next, in the definition of the set of worlds
W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
of the canonical model
\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
, we need to replace the condition
u(ThK45(\mathbf{G}))=1
by
u(ThKD45(\mathbf{G}))=1
, i.e. we define

W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}=\{u\in[0,1]^{Var\cup X}\mid u\sim_{\varphi^{\prime}}v\mbox{ and }u(ThKD45(\mathbf{G}))=1\},

and analogously in the condition
\bf a.
of Lemma
0.4.1
. Then in the proof of this lemma (item (ii) after the
Claim
), one has to further check that
w(\Diamond\top)=1
. Observe that
\Diamond\top
is provably equivalent to
\square\top\mathop{\rightarrow}\Diamond\top
, but this formula is of the form
\phi\mathop{\rightarrow}\psi
for some
\phi,\psi\in X
, and hence it falls under the cases already considered in the proof. Thus Lemma
0.4.1
holds, and the same happens with Lemma
0.4.2
, that holds as well without any modification. Moreover, Lemma
0.4.2
allows us to
prove that the canonical model belongs in fact to the class
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
of normalized possibilistic models. Indeed, by (
2
) it follows that

\sup_{u^{\prime}\in W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}}\{\min(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),u^{\prime}(\psi))\}=u(\Diamond\psi)

for every
\Diamond\psi\in Sub(\varphi^{\prime})
, and since
\Diamond\top\in Sub(\varphi^{\prime})
and
u\in W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
(and hence
u(\Diamond\top)=1
), we finally have

1=u(\Diamond\top)=\sup_{u^{\prime}\in W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}}\{\min(\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),u^{\prime}(\top))\}=\sup_{u^{\prime}\in W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}}\pi^{c}(u^{\prime}),

in other words,
\pi^{c}
is normalized and thus
\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}\in\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
.
In summary, we have found a model
\mathfrak{M}^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}=\langle W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}},\pi^{c},e_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{v}\rangle\in\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
and a world
v\in W^{v}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
such that
e_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{v}(v,\varphi^{\prime})=e_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{v}(v,\varphi)<1
, and therefore
\not\models_{\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\varphi
.
∎

where we change condition

. But again this is easy to be verified, and the rest of the proof is working well. Then, we are are able to prove the following:

Theorem 0.4.3

(Weak completeness
KT45(\mathbf{G})
)
.

For any formula

in
\mathcal{L}_{\square\Diamond}
:

\models_{\Pi^{5}\mathcal{G}}\varphi\mbox{ iff }\vdash_{KT5(\mathbf{G})}\varphi

where

is the class of possibilistic frames

iff
\pi
is such that
\pi(w)=1
for every

. In other words,
\Pi^{5}\mathcal{G}
is in fact the class of universal models, the simplified semantics for
S5(\mathbf{G})
, a result that is well-known in the literature
[
7
]
.

0.5
Decidability

So far, we have shown that
Val(\Pi{\mathcal{G}})=ThK45(\mathbf{G})
, i.e. the set of valid formulas in
\Pi\mathcal{G}
, the class of all
\Pi G
-frames, coincides with the set of theorems of the logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
, or in other words, the logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
is sound and complete with respect to
\Pi G
-frames. It is natural to ask whether the logic

K45(\mathbf{G})
is decidable. Unfortunately, it is easy to check that under the possibilistic semantics the logic
K45(\mathbf{G})
does not satisfy the finite model property. Indeed, consider the formula

\square\lnot\lnot p\rightarrow\lnot\lnot\square p

where
p
is a propositional variable. This formula is not valid in the model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathbb{N},\pi,e\rangle,
where for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\pi(n)=1\mbox{ and }e(p,n)=\tfrac{1}{n+1}.

Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

e(\lnot\lnot p,n)=(\frac{1}{n+1}\Rightarrow 0)\Rightarrow 0=1,

e(\Box p,n)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{\pi(n)\Rightarrow e(p,n)\}=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{1{\Rightarrow}\frac{1}{n+1}\}=0,

e(\Box\neg\neg p,n)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{\pi(n)\Rightarrow e(\neg\neg p,n)\}=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{1{\Rightarrow}1\}=1,

and hence
e(\square\lnot\lnot p\rightarrow\lnot\lnot\square p,n)=0
.
However,
\Box\lnot\lnot p\rightarrow\lnot\lnot\Box p
is valid in
any
\Pi G
-model
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
where
W
is finite.

Nevertheless, in
[
6
]
, an alternative semantics was provided for
K(\mathbf{G})
which admits the finite model property. In this section, we are going to adapt
that semantics in order to obtain an equivalent class of
\Pi G
-models. For that, we are going to use the same strategy used in
[
6
]
, i.e. by limiting the truth-values of modal formulae to a finite number of possibilities. According to this idea, we propose the following adaptation of the original semantics given in
[
6
]
.

Definition 0.5.1
.

A
{\Pi GF}
-model is a quadruple
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,\pi,T,e\rangle
, where
\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
is a
\Pi G
-model and
T\in\mathcal{P}_{<\omega}(\left[0,1\right])
is a finite set of
truth values satisfying
\{0,1\}\subseteq T\subseteq[0,1]
.
The valuation
e
is extended to formulas using the same clauses for non-modal connectives as for
\Pi G
-models, and using the following revised clauses for modal connectives:

\displaystyle e(\Box\varphi,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{r\in T:r\leq\inf_{y\in W}\{\pi(y){\Rightarrow}\ e(\varphi,y)\}\right\}

\displaystyle e(\Diamond\varphi,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left\{r\in T:r\geq\sup_{y\in W}\{\min(\pi(y),e(\varphi,y))\}\right\}.

A formula
\varphi
is said to be
valid
in
\mathfrak{M}
if
e(\varphi,x)=1
for all
x\in W
. We will denote by
\Pi\mathcal{GF}
the class of all
\Pi GF
-models.

Notice that now the formula
\varphi=\Box\neg\neg p\mathop{\rightarrow}\neg\neg\Box p
has a finite

\Pi GF
-counter-model. Indeed, consider the
\Pi GF
-model
\mathfrak{M}_{0}=\langle W,\pi,T,e\rangle
with
W=\{a\}
,
\pi(a)=1
,
T=\{0,1\}
, and such that
e(p,a)=\frac{1}{2}
. Then we have:

•

e(\neg p,a)=0
,
\pi(a)\Rightarrow e(\neg\neg p,a)=1
,
and so
e(\Box\neg\neg p,a)=1
.

•

Further,
e(\Box p,a)=0
(since
\pi(a){\Rightarrow}\ e(p,a)=\frac{1}{2}
,
and
0
is the next smallest element of
T
);

•

Hence,
e(\neg\Box p,a)=1
and
e(\neg\neg\Box p,a)=0
.

•

Therefore,
1=e(\Box\neg\neg p,a)>e(\neg\neg\Box p,a)=0
and
\Box\neg\neg p\mathop{\rightarrow}\neg\neg\Box p
is not valid in
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
.

Next, we are going to prove that both semantics characterize the same logic. First we need the following lemmas to prove the main result.

Lemma 0.5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,\pi,T,e\rangle
be an
\Pi GF
-model. Given an
order-embedding
h\colon[0,1]\mathop{\rightarrow}[0,1]
satisfying
h(0)=0
,
h(1)=1
, and for any
t\in T:h(t)=t
, consider
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle\widehat{W},\widehat{\pi},\widehat{T},\widehat{e}\rangle
, with
\widehat{W}=W_{\mathfrak{M}}
,
\widehat{\pi}(x)=h(\pi(x))
,

\widehat{T}(x)=T(x)
, and
\widehat{e}(p,x)=h(e(p,x))
for all
x\in W

and
p\in\rm{Var}
. Then, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)
and
x\in W
:

\widehat{e}(\varphi,x)=h(e(\varphi,x))

It is a special case of part (c) of Lemma 1 in
[
6
]
.

\blacksquare

Now, we provide the key construction of a
\Pi G
-model taking the same truth values for formulae as a given
\Pi GF
-model.

Lemma 0.5.2
.

For any
\Pi GF
-model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,\pi,T,e\rangle
, there is a
\Pi G
-model
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle\widehat{W},\widehat{\pi},\widehat{e}\rangle
with
W\subseteq\widehat{W}
,
such that
\widehat{e}(\varphi,x)=e(\varphi,x)
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)
and
x\in W_{\mathfrak{M}}
.

We proceed similarly to the proof of Lemma 4 in
[
6
]
. Given a
\Pi GF
-model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,\pi,T,e\rangle
, we construct its associated
\Pi G
-model
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}

directly by taking infinitely many copies of
\mathfrak{M}
. Consider
T=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
with
0=\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{n}=1
and, using Lemma
0.5.1
, define a family of order-embeddings
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
from
[0,1]
into
[0,1
]
satisfying
h_{k}(0)=0
and
h_{k}(1)=1
, such that

h_{k}(\alpha_{i})

=

\alpha_{i}

for all
i\leq n-1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

h_{k}[(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1})]

=

(\alpha_{i},\min(\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{k},\alpha_{i+1}))

for all
i\leq n-1
and even
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

h_{k}[(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1})]

=

(\max(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}-\frac{1}{k}),\alpha_{i+1})

for all
i\leq n-1
and odd
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define a
\Pi G
-model

\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}_{k}=\langle\widehat{W}_{k},\widehat{\pi}_{k},\widehat{e}_{k}\rangle
such that
each
\widehat{W}_{k}
is a copy of
W
with distinct
worlds,
\widehat{\pi}_{k}=h_{k}(\pi)
and
\widehat{e}_{k}(\varphi,x^{k})=h_{k}(e(\varphi,x))
for each copy
x^{k}
of
x\in W
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)
. We also let

\widehat{W}_{0}=W
,
\widehat{\pi}_{0}=\pi
and
\widehat{e}_{0}=e
. Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle\widehat{W},\widehat{\pi},\widehat{e}\rangle
where

\widehat{W}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{W}_{k}\qquad{\rm and}\ \text{
for }\widehat{x}\in\widehat{W}_{k}:\qquad\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{x})=\widehat{\pi}_{k}(\widehat{x})\qquad;\qquad\widehat{e}(p,\widehat{x})=\widehat{e}_{k}(p,\widehat{x}).

Now, It then suffices to prove that
\widehat{e}(\varphi,x)=e(\varphi,x)
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)
and
x\in W
, proceeding by an induction on
\ell(\varphi)
. The base case
\ell(\varphi)=1
follows directly from the definition of
\widehat{e}
. For the inductive step, the cases for the
non-modal connectives follow easily using the induction hypothesis. Let us just consider the case
\varphi=\Box\psi
, the case
\varphi=\Diamond\psi
being very similar. There are two possibilities. Suppose first that

e(\Box\psi,x)=\max\{r\in T:r\leq\inf\{\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y):y\in W\}\}=1.

That means for all
y\in W\ :\ \pi(y)\leq e(\psi,y)
and hence, for
all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}
:
h_{k}(\pi(y))=\widehat{\pi}_{k}({y}^{k})=\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\leq h_{k}(e(\psi,y))=\widehat{e}_{k}(\psi,y^{k})=\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y})
.
It follows that

\widehat{e}(\Box\psi,\widehat{x})=\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}=1=e(\Box\psi,x).

Now suppose that
e(\Box\psi,x)=\alpha_{i}<1
for some
i\leq m-1
. Then
\pi(z)\Rightarrow e(\psi,z)\geq\alpha_{i}
for all
z\in W
, and thus,
(\star)
,
\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{z})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{z})\geq\alpha_{i}
for all
\widehat{z}\in\widehat{W}
, by construction using the order-embeddings
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
.

There are two subcases. First, suppose that there is at least one
y\in W
such that
\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y)=\alpha_{i}
; call it
y_{0}
. This means that
\pi(y_{0})>e(\psi,y_{0})=\alpha_{i}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}_{0})=\widehat{e}_{k}(\psi,{y}_{0}^{k})=h_{k}(e(\psi,y_{0}))=h_{k}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i}
. Since
\pi(y_{0})>\alpha_{i}
, also for all

k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y}_{0})=\widehat{\pi}_{k}({y}_{0}^{k})=h_{k}(\pi(y_{0}))>\alpha_{i}=\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}_{0})
, and hence, using
(\star)
,

\widehat{e}(\Box\psi,\widehat{x})=\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{z})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{z}):\widehat{z}\in\widehat{W}\}=\alpha_{i}=e(\Box\psi,x).

Now suppose that
\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y)>\alpha_{i}
for all
y\in W
. Since
e(\Box\psi,x)=\max\{r\in T:r\leq\inf\{\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y):y\in W\}\}=\alpha_{i}
, there is at least one
y\in W
such that
\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y)\in(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1})
; call it
y_{0}
. Then, by construction, for any

there is a

such that
\widehat{\pi}({y}^{k}_{0})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,{y}^{k}_{0})\in(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}+\epsilon)
.
Using
(\star)
, this ensures that

\widehat{e}(\Box\psi,\widehat{x})=\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(z)\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,z):z\in\widehat{W}\}=\alpha_{i}=e(\Box\psi,x)
.

\blacksquare

, we have the next corollary.

Corollary 0.5.1
.

.

The next lemma paves the way to prove in Theorem

that the logic

has the finite model property and offers a bound

Lemma 0.5.3
.

Let
\mathrm{\Sigma}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)

\Pi G
-model
\mathfrak{M}
, there is a finite
\Pi GF
-model
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}
with
\widehat{W}\subseteq W
, such that

\widehat{e}(\varphi,x)=e(\varphi,x)
for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}
and

|\widehat{W}|+|\widehat{T}|\leq 2|\mathrm{\Sigma}|
.

Let
\mathrm{\Sigma}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)
be a finite fragment,
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,\pi,e\rangle
a
\Pi G
-model. First, define
\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Box}
as the set of
all box-formulas in
\mathrm{\Sigma}
,
\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Diamond}
as the set of all diamond-formulas in
\mathrm{\Sigma}
, and
\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\rm{Var}}
as the set of all variables in
\mathrm{\Sigma}
. Let us also define
e_{x}[\mathrm{\Delta}]=\{e(\varphi,x):\varphi\in\mathrm{\Delta}\}
for any

and
\mathrm{\Delta}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)

e_{x}[\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Box}\cup\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Diamond}]\cup\{0,1\}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}

0=\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{n}=1
.

Next, we choose a finite number of
y\in W

\Box\psi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Box}

, and for each
\Diamond\psi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Diamond}

e(\Diamond\psi,x)=\alpha_{i}>0
, choose a

such that
e(\psi,y_{\Diamond\psi})>\alpha_{i-1}

\widehat{W}=\{x\}\cup\{y_{\Box\psi}\in W:\Box\psi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Box}\}\cup\{y_{\Diamond\psi}\in W:\Diamond\psi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Diamond}\}
. Clearly
\widehat{W}\subseteq W
is finite. We define
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle\widehat{W},\widehat{\pi},\widehat{T},\widehat{e}\rangle
where
\widehat{T}=e_{x}[\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Box}\cup\mathrm{\Sigma}_{\Diamond}]\cup\{0,1\}
, and both
\widehat{\pi}
and
\widehat{e}
are equal to
\pi
and
e
restricted to
\widehat{W}
, respectively. It then follows by

\ell(\varphi)
that
\widehat{e}(\varphi,x)=e(\varphi,x)
for all

and
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}
. The base case follows from the definition of
\widehat{e}
. For the inductive step, let
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Sigma}
be of the form
\varphi=\Box\psi
(the non-modal cases follow directly, using the induction hypothesis). We need to consider the same two cases it was considered in previous

0.5.2

\inf\{\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y):{y\in W}\}\leq\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}

because of
\widehat{W}\subseteq W
. Now suppose that
1=e(\Box\psi,x)
. Then, because

,

\displaystyle 1=e(\Box\psi,x)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y):{y\in W}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{r\in\widehat{T}:r\leq\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{e}(\Box\psi,x).

For the second case,
e(\Box\psi,x)=\inf\{\pi(y)\Rightarrow e(\psi,y):y\in W\}=\alpha_{i}<1
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. According to our choise, there exists a

such that
\alpha_{i}\leq\widehat{\pi}(y_{\Box\psi})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,y_{\Box\psi})<\alpha_{i+1}

\alpha_{i}\leq\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}\}\leq\widehat{\pi}(y_{\Box\psi})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,y_{\Box\psi})<\alpha_{i+1}

Thus

\widehat{e}(\Box\psi,x)=\max\{r\in\widehat{T}:r\leq\inf\{\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{y})\Rightarrow\widehat{e}(\psi,\widehat{y}):\widehat{y}\in\widehat{W}\}\}=\alpha_{i}

\blacksquare

.

Finally, we can state the main result of this section.

Theorem 0.5.1

(main)
.

For each
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box\Diamond}(Var)

iff

is valid in all (finite)

0.6
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the logic over Gödel fuzzy logic arising from many-valued Gödel Kripke models with possibilistic semantics, and have shown that it actually corresponds to a simplified semantics for the
logic
K45({\bf G})
, the extension of Caicedo and Rodriguez’s bi-modal Gödel logic with many-valued versions of the well-known modal axioms
4
and
5
. We have also considered the extension with the axiom
D
, the logic
KD45({\bf G})
, and have shown to be captured by normalised possibilistic Gödel Kripke models. In this way, we have obtained many-valued Gödel generalizations of the results reported by Pietruszczak in
[
15
]
about simplified semantics for several classical modal logics. We have also shown the decidability of those logics.

It is worth noticing that the truth-value of a formula
\Diamond\varphi

in a possibilistic Kripke model is indeed a proper generalization of the possibility measure of
\varphi
when
\varphi
is a classical proposition, however the semantics of
\Box\varphi
is not. This is due to the fact that the negation in Gödel logic is not involutive. Therefore, a first open problem we leave for further research is to consider to extension of the logic
K45({\bf G})
with an involutive negation and investigate its possibilistic semantics. Finally, we would like to study the connection between our simplified possiblistic semantics and the pseudomonadic algebras proposed in
[
3
]
.

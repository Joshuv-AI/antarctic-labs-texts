Charge algebra for non-abelian large gauge symmetries at O ⁡ ( r ) O(r)
Miguel Campiglia, Javier Peraza

Abstract
Asymptotic symmetries of gauge theories are known to encode infrared properties of radiative fields. In the context of tree-level Yang-Mills theory, the leading soft behavior of gluons is captured by large gauge symmetries with parameters that are O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) in the large r r expansion towards null infinity. This relation can be extended to subleading order provided one allows for large gauge symmetries with O ⁡ ( r ) O(r) gauge parameters. The latter, however, violate standard asymptotic field fall-offs and thus their interpretation has remained incomplete. We improve on this situation by presenting a relaxation of the standard asymptotic field behavior that is compatible with O ⁡ ( r ) O(r) gauge symmetries at linearized level. We show the extended space admits a symplectic structure on which O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) and O ⁡ ( r ) O(r) charges are well defined and such that their Poisson brackets reproduce the corresponding symmetry algebra.

The goal of the present paper is to improve on this situation. For definiteness we will work in the context of pure YM theory in four spacetime dimensions, although we expect the main ideas should apply to other settings.

As in the gravitational example, we would like to proceed by first identifying the appropriate “kinematical” fields that allow for

5
Outlook

The study of asymptotic symmetries in gauge and gravitational theories has proven to be a useful source of information on their infrared properties. The nature of these symmetries, however, depends crucially on the subtle problem of boundary conditions imposed on the fields. In this work, we have proposed an enlargement of the radiative phase space of classical Yang-Mills theory so that it can support linearized
O(r)
gauge symmetries. The extended space is parametrized by the standard asymptotic gauge field plus a “Goldstone mode” that transforms inhomogenously under linearized
O(r)
symmetries. We showed this extended space admits a symplectic structure such that the charges associated to the
O(r^{0})
and
O(r)
gauge symmetries are compatible with known tree-level soft gluon theorems, and such that their Poisson brackets reproduce the variation algebra.

There are several future directions that appear worth pursuing.

On the one hand, we hope our results are the first order approximation of a higher order symmetry algebra, at least within the tree-level theory. The next order would include linearized
O(r^{2})
gauge transformations and
O(r)
ones at second order. The former would be related to the (partial) sub-subleading soft gluon factorization
Hamada:2018vrw
. In this context, it would be important to make contact with the “celestial” 2d CFT approach to symmetries
Cheung:2016iub
;
Nande:2017dba
;
He:2015zea
;
Himwich:2019dug
;
Fan:2019emx
;
Kalyanapuram:2021tnl
;
Raclariu:2021zjz
;
Pasterski:2021rjz
, which naturally incorporates higher order factorization formulas satisfying a rich algebraic structure
Guevara:2021abz
;
Jiang:2021ovh
.

On the other hand, staying within the algebra at
O(r)
, it would be exciting if one could extend the analysis to include loop corrections to the soft gluon factorization formulas, see e.g.
Bern:2014oka
. It is interesting to note that the algebra consistency lead us to include higher order terms in the charges that would be sensitive to such loop effects. The problem however would be quite challenging, in particular due the appearance of infrared divergences. Here again it may prove useful to establish contact with the celestial CFT methods, which appear well suited for the incorporation of these effects
Albayrak:2020saa
;
Gonzalez:2020tpi
;
Gonzalez:2021dxw
;
Magnea:2021fvy
.

Much of the inspiration for the present work stems from the study of asymptotic symmetries in gravity. In this regard, it has been proposed
subsub
that asymptotic diffeomorphisms generated by certain
O(r)
sphere-vector fields are behind the sub-subleading soft graviton factorization
stromcach
. Perhaps a similar strategy as the one presented here could be used to identify an extended space supporting such singular transformations.

As illustrated below, the subleading coefficients are determined by recursively solving the residual gauge equation (
21
)

\square\Lambda+[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\nabla^{\mu}\Lambda]=r^{-1}(\partial^{2}_{r}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\partial^{a}\partial_{a})(r\Lambda)\\
+[\mathcal{A}_{r}-\mathcal{A}_{u},\partial_{r}\Lambda]-[\mathcal{A}_{r},\partial_{u}\Lambda]+r^{-2}q^{ab}[\mathcal{A}_{a},\partial_{b}\Lambda]=0,

(86)

where recall that
\partial_{a}
is used to denote the sphere covariant derivative and hence
\partial^{a}\partial_{a}
is the sphere laplacian.
Substituting (
84
) and (
71
) in (
86
) one finds

\square\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}+[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\nabla^{\mu}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}]=\frac{1}{r^{2}}(-2\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\ln}{\lambda}+D^{a}\partial_{a}\lambda)+O(\ln^{2}r/r^{3}).

(87)

Similarly for (
85
) one gets

\square\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}+[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\nabla^{\mu}\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}]=\frac{\ln r}{r}(-2\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0,\ln}{\varepsilon}-[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0,\ln}{A}_{u},\varepsilon])\\
+\frac{1}{r}(-2\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0}{\varepsilon}-2\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0,\ln}{\varepsilon}+(\partial^{a}\partial_{a}+2)\varepsilon+[A^{a},\partial_{a}\varepsilon])+O(\ln^{2}r/r^{2}).

(88)

The vanishing of the factors with a given
r
-dependence in (
87
) and (
88
) can be used to determine the subleading coefficients in terms of the respective leading coefficients
\lambda
and
\varepsilon
.

We finally describe the residual
O(r^{0})
gauge parameter in the extended space (
39
), which is required to compute the modified bracket (
23
) between
O(r^{0})
and
O(r)
gauge parameters. The residual gauge equation in the extended space is

\nabla^{\nu}\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}\tilde{\Lambda}=0

(89)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
is the
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mu}
–gauge covariant derivative, which in the parametrization (
39
) reads

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}=\mathcal{D}_{\mu}+[\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Lambda^{1}_{\phi},\cdot].

(90)

Since we are working to first order in
\phi
, it suffices we consider a corrected gauge parameter of the form

\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}=\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}+\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi},

(91)

where
\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}=O(\phi)
is the correction. Substituting (
91
) in (
89

\phi

\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}
must satisfy

\square\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}+[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\nabla^{\mu}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}]+[\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Lambda^{1}_{\phi},\nabla^{\mu}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}]=0.

(92)

This equation is of the same form as the original residual gauge equation (
86
) except that it now has a “source” term. Proceeding as in the earlier cases, one finds

\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}=\ln r\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0,\ln}{\lambda}_{\phi}+\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0}{\lambda}_{\phi}+o(1)

(93)

with the coefficients determined by the equations

\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0,\ln}{\lambda}_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}[\phi,\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\ln}{\lambda}],\quad\quad\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle 0}{\lambda}_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}[\phi,\partial_{u}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\ln}{\lambda}]+\frac{1}{2}[D^{a}\phi,\partial_{a}\lambda].

(94)

B.2
Commutators

Having understood the
O(r^{0})
gauge parameter in the extended space, we can now compute its modified bracket (
23
) with the
O(r)
gauge parameter:

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}]^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}]+\delta_{\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}-\delta_{\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}}\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon},

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon},\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}]+\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}-\delta_{\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}}\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon},

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{1}_{[\varepsilon,\lambda]},

(97)

In going from the first to second line, we used that
\delta_{\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}=0
and
\delta_{\Lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}=\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}
, as follows from (
41
), and dropped
O(\phi\varepsilon)

B.3
Bulk description of
O(r^{0})
parameters in the extended space

In the body of the paper we defined the extension of
O(r^{0})
large gauge transformations to
\Gamma^{\textrm{ext}}
by (
42

However, from the “bulk” description (

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{\lambda}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mu}:=\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}\tilde{\Lambda}^{0}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda,\phi}+[\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\Lambda^{1}_{\phi},\Lambda^{0}_{\lambda}]

where the second equality follows from evaluating

Appendix C
Types of corrections to asymptotic charges

Throughout the paper we have made reference to the link between asymptotic charges and soft gluon theorems. In this appendix we discuss some aspects of this relationship.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be a scattering amplitude involving
n
external gluons. Its dependence with the YM coupling
g
is of the form

\mathcal{S}_{n}=g^{n-2}(\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{tree}}+g^{2}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{1-loop}}+\cdots).

(102)

On the other hand, the dependence of the asymptotic charges on
g
can be obtained from the expressions given in the body of the paper (where we set
g=1
) by doing the replacements
A_{a}\to gA_{a}
,
\Lambda\to g\Lambda
and multiplying by an overall
g^{-2}
factor. One then finds that both
Q^{0,\text{rad}}_{\lambda}
and
Q_{Y}
depend on
g
according to

Q^{\textrm{tree}}=Q^{\textrm{soft}(1)}+gQ^{\textrm{hard}(2)},

(103)

where
Q^{\textrm{soft}(1)}
is linear in the gauge field and inserts a soft (
\omega\to 0
energy) gluon and
Q^{\textrm{hard}(2)}
is quadratic in the gauge field and preserves the number of external gluons. Schematically one has

[Q^{\textrm{soft}(1)},\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{tree}}]\sim g\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n+1_{s}},\quad[Q^{\textrm{hard}(2)},\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{tree}}]\sim\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{tree}},

(104)

where the power of
g
in the first equation comes from the extra coupling needed to get a non-trivial amplitude with
n+1_{s}
gluons. The “
s
” label indicates that the extra gluon is soft. The (leading or subleading) single-soft tree-level factorization theorem can then be written as

[Q^{\textrm{tree}},\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n}]\sim 0.

(105)

In our analysis we encountered two kinds of corrections the charges (
103
) may receive.

The first type of correction are “hard” terms that are of higher order in the gauge field. For instance

Q=Q^{\textrm{tree}}+g^{2}Q^{\textrm{hard}(3)}.

(106)

When acting on the S matrix this cubic correction would yield, to lowest order, a term of the form

[g^{2}Q^{\textrm{hard}(3)},\mathcal{S}_{n}]\sim g^{n}[Q^{\textrm{hard}(3)},\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{tree}}]\sim g^{n+1}\mathcal{S}_{n+1}^{\textrm{tree}},

(107)

where the
O(A^{3})
charge adds an external gluon (not necessarily soft), with the corresponding extra
O(g)
factor. Since the coupling power corresponding to (
105
) is
g^{n-1}
, the term (
107
) does not affect the tree-level Ward identity. At 1-loop however, the Ward identity would get contributions from (
107
) and from

[Q^{\textrm{tree}},g^{n}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{1-loop}}]\sim g^{n+1}(\mathcal{S}_{n+1_{s}}^{\textrm{1-loop}}+\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\textrm{1-loop}}).

(108)

Thus, in the case of a charge of the form (
106
), the would-be Ward identity would enforce the sum of (
107
) and (
108
) to vanish. The study of such potential identities is however well-beyond the scope of the present paper. Even to make sense of such expressions would require a treatment of infrared divergences that are not present in the tree-level case.

There is a second type of correction the charge may acquire, of the type

Q=Q^{\textrm{tree}}+gQ^{\textrm{soft}(2)},

(109)

which is quadratic in the soft part of the gauge field. As discussed in
cl
in the gravitational context, such kind of terms are non-trivial if at least one of the external states is soft. Schematically,

[Q^{\textrm{soft}(2)},\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}}]=0,\quad\text{but}\quad[Q^{\textrm{soft}(2)},\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}+1_{s}}]\sim\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}+1_{s}}.

(110)

Thus, a would-be Ward identity with charge (
109
) would lead to a relation of the type

0=[Q,\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}+1_{s}}]\sim g(\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}+2_{s}}+\mathcal{S}^{\textrm{tree}}_{n_{h}+1_{s}}).

(111)

Such kind of terms are thus sensitive to (tree-level) double-soft gluon emission. See
cl
;
anupam
;
distler
for related discussion of double-soft emission and asymptotic charges.

Appendix D
Covariant LPS charge

We start by expanding the covariant derivatives in (
50
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{u}(D_{a}F_{ru}+D^{b}F_{ba})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\partial_{u}F_{ru}+\partial^{b}\partial_{u}F_{ab}+\partial_{u}([A_{a},F_{ru}]+[A^{b},F_{ba}]).

(112)

Recalling the expressions for
F_{ru}
(
48
) and
F_{ab}
(
47

112

Q^{\textrm{soft}}_{Y}
plus the
J_{u}
part of
Q^{\textrm{hard}}_{Y}
plus an extra term coming from the non-abelian part of

Q^{\textrm{extra}}_{Y}:=\int dud^{2}xu\text{Tr}Y^{a}\partial_{u}\partial^{b}[A_{a},A_{b}].

(113)

The last two terms of (
112
) lead the
J_{a}
part of
Q^{\textrm{hard}}_{Y}
plus cubic terms plus quadratic “soft” terms given by

\displaystyle Q^{(2)}_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x\text{Tr}Y^{a}[E^{1}_{a},A^{+}_{a}]

(114)

\displaystyle Q^{(3)}_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dud^{2}xu\text{Tr}Y^{a}\partial_{u}\big([A_{a},\int^{\infty}_{u}J_{u^{\prime}}du^{\prime}]+[A^{b},[A_{b},A_{a}]]\big)

(115)

where

E^{1}_{a}(x):=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}duu\partial_{u}A_{a}(u,x).

(116)

Summarizing, the charge
Q^{\text{cov}}_{Y}
defined by (
50
) differs from the LPS charge

by the three terms:

Q^{\text{cov}}_{Y}-Q_{Y}=Q^{\textrm{extra}}_{Y}+Q^{(2)}_{Y}+Q^{(3)}_{Y}.

(117)

As explained in appendix
C
,
Q^{(2)}_{Y}
and
Q^{(3)}_{Y}
yield trivial single-insertion tree-level Ward identities. On the other hand
Q^{\textrm{extra}}_{Y}
produces non-trivial terms in the single Ward identity that are in conflict with the subleading soft gluon theorem. To see the form of these terms, let us evaluate the commutator
Q^{\textrm{extra}}_{Y}
with
\hat{A}_{a}(\omega,x)
. For the purposes of this evaluation, one can assume decaying
u

A_{a}(u,x)
and integrate by parts the

From the elementary commutator

[A^{\beta}_{b}(u^{\prime},x^{\prime}),\hat{A}^{\alpha}_{a}(\omega,x)]_{\textrm{op}}=-\delta^{\alpha\beta}q_{ab}\frac{e^{i\omega u}}{2\omega}\delta^{(2)}(x^{\prime},x)

(119)

\alpha

[Q_{Y}^{\textrm{extra}},\hat{A}_{a}(\omega,x)]_{\textrm{op}}=-\frac{1}{2\omega}[\partial_{a}Y_{b}-\partial_{b}Y_{a},\hat{A}^{b}(\omega,x)].

(120)

Thus, a tree-level Ward identity for

would include the above contribution in addition to the terms (

Appendix E
Comparison with the gravitational case

In the gravitational case, the standard radiative fall-off conditions on the spacetime metric take the form (focusing for simplicity on the angular components of the metric)

g_{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\to\infty}}{{=}}r^{2}q_{ab}+rC_{ab}+\cdots

(121)

where
q_{ab}
is the
u
-independent 2d metric on the celestial sphere and
C_{ab}=C_{ab}(u,x)
encodes gravitational radiation at null infinity. The latter plays the role of “free data” that determines the asymptotic metric components through Einstein equations (supplemented by gauge fixing conditions). Schematically,

\Gamma^{\textrm{rad}}_{\textrm{grav}}\approx\{C_{ab}(u,x)\}.

(122)

The non-trivial diffeomorphisms preserving (
121
) are asymptotic Lorentz transformations plus so-called supertranslations, generated by vector fields of the form

\xi^{0}_{f}=f(x)\partial_{u}+\cdots,

(123)

with arbitrary
f(x)
. These can be thought of as the analogous to the
O(r^{0})
large gauge transformations in the YM case. The analogy however breaks down at the level of the algebra, since supertranslations are abelian,

[\xi^{0}_{f},\xi^{0}_{f^{\prime}}]^{*}=0

(124)

(the bracket is now a modified vector field Lie bracket analogous to (
23
), see e.g.
Barnich:2013sxa
).

The above asymptotic symmetries can be enlarged to include superrotations, generated by vector fields with a leading non-trivial angular component

\xi^{1}_{Y}=Y^{a}(x)\partial_{a}+\cdots

(125)

with arbitrary
Y^{a}(x)
.
15
15

15

In the special case where
Y^{a}
is a (global) conformal Killing vector field of
q_{ab}
, these are not new symmetries but represent the generators of the asymptotic Lorentz group. Although we shall be phrasing the extension in terms of arbitrary sphere vector fields, the following discussion applies equally well to the case of local conformal Killing vector fields.
However, to allow for such transformations one needs to relax the form of the asymptotic metric (
121
). As in the YM case, one can write a linearized version of the extended space as:

\Gamma^{\textrm{ext}}_{\textrm{grav}}=\{\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{1}_{X}}g_{\mu\nu},\quad g_{\mu\nu}\in\Gamma^{\textrm{rad}}_{\textrm{grav}},\quad X^{a}\in\mathfrak{X}(S^{2})\}.

(126)

Of course, in the gravitational case we known how to “exponentiate” the linearized extension in order to obtain a full extended space. We will however continue the discussion within the linearized setting in order to make contact with the analysis in this paper.

The space (
126
) is then naturally parametrized by pairs

\Gamma^{\textrm{ext}}_{\textrm{grav}}\approx\{(C_{ab}(u,x),X^{a}(x))\},

(127)

where
C_{ab}
“generates” the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and
X^{a}
implements its superrotated version.
16
16

16

Strictly speaking,
X^{a}
should be considered modulo global conformal Killing vector fields on the celestial sphere. Other more commonly used labels for the “superrotation frame” are either the deformed 2d metric
\tilde{q}_{ab}
or the so-called Geroch/Liouville tensor
T_{ab}=-(D_{a}D_{b})^{\textrm{TF}}D_{c}X^{c}
, see e.g.
Barnich:2010eb
;
clnewsym
;
comperevacua
.

Eqs. (
126
), (
127
) are the gravitational versions of Eqs. (
39
), (
40
) in the YM case. The extended gravitational space now supports the action of superrotations (
125
), which in the parametrization (
127
) takes the form

\delta^{1}_{Y}C_{ab}=0,\quad\delta^{1}_{Y}X^{a}=Y^{a},

(128)

in parallel to Eq. (
41
). The trivial action on
C_{ab}
in (
128
) may appear at odds with the standard action of superrotations one encounters in the literature. The reason is that the definition of
C_{ab}
in (
127
) does not agree with the usual one. In order to compare with the more standard parametrization, let us look at the angular components of the full metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
in (
126
). These take the form

\tilde{g}_{ab}=r^{2}\tilde{q}_{ab}+r\tilde{C}_{ab}+\cdots,

(129)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{ab}+X\cdot q_{ab},

(130)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{ab}+X\cdot C_{ab}-u(D_{a}D_{b})^{\textrm{TF}}D_{c}X^{c},

(131)

(
TF
stands for trace-free part) with

\displaystyle X\cdot q_{ab}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}-D_{c}X^{c})q_{ab}

(132)

\displaystyle X\cdot C_{ab}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}+\frac{1}{2}D_{c}X^{c}(u\partial_{u}-1))C_{ab}.

(133)

In the standard parametrization one treats
\tilde{C}_{ab}
, rather than
C_{ab}
, as independent variable.
17
17

17

What we are calling
\tilde{C}_{ab}
is what usually is denoted as
C_{ab}
(e.g.
Barnich:2010eb
;
stromvirasoro
;
superboost
).
From (
131
) and (
128
) one finds

\delta^{1}_{Y}\tilde{C}_{ab}=Y\cdot\tilde{C}_{ab}-u(D_{a}D_{b})^{\textrm{TF}}D_{c}Y^{c},

(134)

which coincides with the standard superrotation action (recall we are working to linear order in
X^{a}
and
Y^{a}
and dropping
O(X^{2})
,
O(Y^{2})
and
O(XY)
terms). In this way, we can think of
C_{ab}
as a “dressed” version of the standard
\tilde{C}_{ab}
which neutralizes the action of superrotations.

There is yet another parametrization that is useful to consider, which consists in removing the
O(u)
part of
\tilde{C}_{ab}
,

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{ab}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{ab}-u(D_{a}D_{b})^{\textrm{TF}}D_{c}X^{c}

(135)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{ab}+X\cdot C_{ab}.

(136)

In this case the action of superrotations is purely homogeneous,

\delta^{1}_{Y}\hat{C}_{ab}=Y\cdot\hat{C}_{ab}.

(137)

Regardless of the parametrization being used, the algebra relation between supertranslations and superrotations is given by

[\xi^{0}_{f},\xi^{1}_{Y}]^{*}=-\xi^{0}_{Y\cdot f},

(138)

where

Y\cdot f:=(\mathcal{L}_{Y}-\frac{1}{2}D_{c}Y^{c})f.

(139)

The algebraic relation (
138

O(r^{0})
rather than
O(r)
symmetry.
Schematically,

[\xi^{0},\xi^{1}]\sim\xi^{0}\quad\text{vs.}\quad[\Lambda^{0},\Lambda^{1}]\sim\Lambda^{1}.

(140)

Let us now discuss charges. In the radiative space, supertranslations are generated by

P^{\text{rad}}_{f}=\int d^{2}xf(x)\mathcal{P}(x)

(141)

where
\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}du(N^{ab}N_{ab}-2D^{a}D^{b}N_{ab}),

(142)

\delta^{1}_{Y}P^{\textrm{ext}}_{f}=-P^{\textrm{ext}}_{Y\cdot f}.

(143)

This has the simple solution

\displaystyle P^{\textrm{ext}}_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\text{rad}}_{f}-P^{\text{rad}}_{X\cdot f},

(144)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}xf(\mathcal{P}+X\cdot\mathcal{P}),

(145)

X\cdot\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{L}_{X}+\frac{3}{2}D_{c}X^{c})\mathcal{P}.

(146)

Eq. (
144

59

\xi^{1}

In order to compare (

144

\hat{N}_{ab}\equiv\partial_{u}\hat{C}_{ab}

(147)

Substituting (

\mathcal{P}+X\cdot\mathcal{P}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}-\hat{N}^{0}_{ab}D^{a}D^{b}D_{c}X^{c},

(148)

\hat{\mathcal{P}}
is given by (

N_{ab}

\hat{N}_{ab}
and

\hat{N}^{0}_{ab}:=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}du\hat{N}_{ab}.

(149)

The RHS of Eq. (

T_{ab}=-(D_{a}D_{b})^{\textrm{TF}}D_{c}X^{c}

cp

One can similarly construct superrotation charges. The situation is again structurally different from the YM case due to (

To conclude, the differences between the YM and gravity cases may be summarized by saying that the notion of
“covariance” at null infinity in each theory is given by transformations associated to different radial/energy order: In YM the natural notion of covariance comes from
\Lambda^{0}
transformations, whereas in gravity it comes from
\xi^{1}
transformations.

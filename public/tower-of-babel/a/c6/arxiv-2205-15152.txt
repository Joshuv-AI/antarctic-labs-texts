Semiclassical spectrum of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator on an annulus
E. Lavigne Bon

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the semiclassical analysis of the spectrum of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator on an annulus. We assume that the magnetic field is strictly positive and radial. We give an explicit asymptotic expansion at the first order of the lowest eigenvalues of this operator in the semiclassical limit. In particular, we exhibit the Aharonov-Bohm effect that has been revealed, for constant magnetic field, in a recent paper by Helffer and Sundqvist.



1.
Introduction

This paper deals with the low-lying spectrum of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator. This operator is the Hamiltonian of the Pauli’s equation, which models non-relativistic spin-1/2 particules interacting with a magnetic field. For a particle of mass and electric charge equal to 1, in a pure magnetic field perpendicular to the domain, the Pauli’s equation reads

ih\partial_{t}\Psi=\mathcal{P}_{h}\,\Psi,

where
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the state of the system,
\Omega
a smooth bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
h>0
the semiclassical parameter. We associate to the magnetic field (which is assumed to be smooth), denoted by
B
, a magnetic vector potential
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
satisfying
B=\partial_{x}A_{2}-\partial_{y}A_{1}
.

The Dirichlet-Pauli operator acts as

\mathcal{P}_{h}=\left[\sigma\cdot\left(-ih\nabla-A\right)\right]^{2}\;\;\text{on}\;\;\,\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{2}\right),

with
\sigma=\left(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right)
, the Pauli matrices (Hermitian, unitary)

(1.1)

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

and
\sigma\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{v}}=\sigma_{1}\mathrm{v_{1}}+\sigma_{2}\mathrm{v_{2}}
for
\mathrm{\mathbf{v}}=\left(\mathrm{v_{1}},\mathrm{v_{2}}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Notice that to ensure its selfadjointness we assume that boundary carries Dirichlet conditions.
Let
\left(\lambda_{k}(h)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be the real non-decreasing sequence of eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
counting multiplicity.

The Dirichlet-Pauli operator has been the subject of many recent works. In particular, the paper
[
4
]
presents a new approach, for simply connected domains, leading to very accurate estimates of the low-lying spectrum. Helffer and Sundqvist
[
13
]
proved the exponential decay rate of the ground state in the case of a connected domain. They also given, in the same paper, numerical results describing the behavior of the first eigenvalues on an annulus with a constant magnetic field.

Here, we consider an annulus
\Omega
and we assume that the magnetic field is strictly positive and radial.
We give explicitly the first term of the asymptotic expansion for the first eigenvalues. Let us informally describe our main result. We prove in this paper that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\lambda_{k}(h)=\min_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}V\subset\mathbb{Z}\\
\#V=k\end{array}}\max_{m\in V}\,f\left(m-\frac{c_{0}}{h}\right)\,\sqrt{h}\;e^{\frac{2\phi_{\min}}{h}}\;(1+o_{h\rightarrow 0}(1))\,,

with
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi_{\min}
a strictly negative constant and
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a coercive function known explicitly according to the magnetic field and
\Omega
.

The prefactor in
\lambda_{k}(h)
encodes two kinds of gauge invariance. Of course, the standard magnetic gauge invariance (adding a gradient to the magnetic
potential) leaves
\phi_{\rm min},f
and
c_{0}
invariant. The presence
of the hole introduces another degree of freedom given by conjugating the operator by
e^{ip\,\mathrm{arg}}
(
p\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathrm{arg}
being the argument). The invariance by translation by an integer of the prefactor of
\lambda_{k}(h)
is reminiscent of this invariance (see Section
2.2
).

Note that the constant
c_{0}
depends mainly on the circulation of the magnetic potential on the interior boundary. When it does not vanish, we observe an oscillation of the eigenvalues in the semiclassical limit through the 1-periodicity of

\displaystyle d\longmapsto\min_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}V\subset\mathbb{Z}\\
\#V=k\end{array}}\max_{m\in V}\,f\left(m-d\right).

This influence of the circulation of the magnetic potential on the eigenvalues evokes the Aharonov-Bohm effect. It has already been mentioned in
[
13
]
, for more details on this phenomenon see the original reference
[
1
]
, for a new point of view see also
[
2
]
.

This result highlights some phenomena that should also appear in doubly connected case (without symetry) and in multiconnected domains.

1.1.
Hypothesis and definition of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an annulus centered at the origin with radius
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
. We let

(1.2)

\partial\Omega=\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\bigsqcup\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}},

with
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}=\mathcal{C}(0,\rho_{1})
and
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathcal{C}(0,\rho_{2})
.

Consider a magnetic field
B\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}\right)
. Despite the presence of a hole, there exists a regular vector potential denoted by
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
, which satisfies

(1.3)

B=\mathrm{rot}\left(A\right)=\partial_{x}A_{2}-\partial_{y}A_{1}.

Note however that two Pauli operators with two vector potentials associated with
B
are not necessarily unitarily equivalent (see Section
2
).

Assumption 1.1
.

The magnetic field is
radial
and
strictly positive
.

1.1.1.
The Dirichlet-Pauli operator

We are interested in the Dirichlet-Pauli operator
\left(\mathcal{P}_{h},\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}\right)\right)
defined for all
h>0
as

\mathcal{P}_{h}=\left[\sigma\cdot\left({\bf p}-A\right)\right]^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}|{\bf p}-A|^{2}-hB&0\\
0&|{\bf p}-A|^{2}+hB\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h}&0\\
0&\mathcal{L}^{+}_{h}\end{pmatrix},

acting on the domain

\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}\right)=\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{2}\right).

Here
{\bf p}=-ih\nabla
.
This operator is non-negative, self-adjoint with compact resolvent. By the spectral theorem, the spectrum of
\left(\mathcal{P}_{h},\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}\right)\right)
is real, discrete and can be written as a sequence tending to
+\infty
.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the behavior of the lowest eigenvalues in the semiclassical limit. Since
B>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
, it is enough to study the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h}
.

Notation 1
.

Let
\left(\lambda_{k}(h)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
denote the non-decreasing sequence of eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
.

By the min-max theorem, we have the following characterization

(1.4)

\lambda_{k}(h)=\underset{\underset{\mathrm{dim}V=k}{V\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{2})}}{\mathrm{inf}}\;\underset{u\in V\backslash\{0\}}{\mathrm{sup}}\;\frac{\left\|\sigma\cdot\left({\bf p}-A\right)u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}}{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}}.

1.1.2.
Scalar potential

The choice of
A
will play an important role. A particular choice is associated with the scalar potential,
\phi
being the unique solution in
H_{0}^{1}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}\right)
(cf.
[
9
, Theorem 6, p.326]
) of the Poisson equation

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\phi=B&\text{on}\;\Omega\\
\phi=0&\text{in}\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Since
B
is positive,
\phi
is subharmonic and satisfies

\underset{x\in\overline{\Omega}}{\mathrm{max}}\;\phi=\underset{x\in\partial\Omega}{\mathrm{max}}\;\phi=0\;,

by the maximum principle.
In particular, the minimum of
\phi
is negative and attained in
\Omega
. We note that the exterior normal derivative of
\phi
, denoted
\partial_{n}\phi
, is strictly positive on
\partial\Omega
from
[
9
, Hopf’s Lemma, p.330]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Assumption
1.1
on the magnetic field and the uniqueness of
\phi
ensures that
\phi
is radial and admits a unique circle of minimum centered at the origin and of radius
\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{min}}\in]\rho_{1},\rho_{2}[
. We note
\phi_{\min}
the minimum of
\phi
. In polar coordinates,
\phi
is the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}\phi^{\prime}(r)=B(r)&\text{on }\;]\rho_{1},\rho_{2}[\\
\phi\left(\rho_{1}\right)=\phi\left(\rho_{2}\right)=0\;.&\end{array}\right.

so that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(r_{\min})=B(r_{\min})\geqslant B_{0}=\inf\{B(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x\in\Omega\}
.

1.2.
Results and discussions

Some recent works have investigated the semiclassical limit of the bottom of the spectrum:

(1)

In the non-simply connected case, Helffer and Sundqvist have proved, in
[
13
]
, that if the magnetic field is positive, then

\lambda_{1}(h)=\exp{\left(\frac{2\phi_{\min}}{h}+o_{h\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)\right)}.

The techniques employed for the lower bound are mainly based on those used by Ekholm, Kovařík and Portmann in
[
8
]
. The main novelty of their proof is the combination of some gauge invariances and the Hodge-de Rham theory to control the oscillations induced by the circulation of the magnetic potential.

In
[
13
, Section 7]
, a numerical analysis of the smallest eigenvalues is realized in the case of a constant magnetic field on the annulus, by means of a finite difference method. It relies on a Fourier decomposition, parametrized by the circulation of the magnetic potential.

(2)

In the simply connected case, Barbaroux, Le Treust, Raymond and Stockmeyer have proved, in
[
4
]
, that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, there exist
C_{inf}(k),C_{sup}(k)>0
such that

C_{inf}(k)h^{1-k}e^{2\phi_{\min}/h}(1+o_{h\rightarrow 0}(1))\leqslant\lambda_{k}(h)\leqslant C_{sup}(k)h^{1-k}e^{2\phi_{\min}/h}(1+o_{h\rightarrow 0}(1))\,,

under the assumption that
B
is positive and that
\phi
has a unique minimum, which is non-degenerate.
The prefactors
C_{inf}(k)
and
C_{sup}(k)
, are given explicitly. Their strategy is based on the Riemann mapping theorem and the connection between the spectral analysis of Dirichlet-Pauli operator and Cauchy-Riemann operators. In particular, the lower bound, established by a holomorphic approximation result
[
4
, Proposition 5.4]
, is a consequence of the ellipticity of Cauchy-Riemann operators.

The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}\right)
be radial such that

B_{0}=\mathrm{inf}\{B(x),\;x\in\Omega\}>0,

and
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
be an associated vector potential.

Then, for all fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\lambda_{k}(h)=\alpha_{k}(h)\;\sqrt{h}\;e^{2\phi_{\min}/h}(1+o_{h\to 0}(1)),

where

\alpha_{k}(h)=\min_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}V\subset\mathbb{Z}\\
\#V=k\end{array}}\max_{m\in V}\,f\left(m-\frac{c_{0}}{h}\right)\;\text{and}\;\;c_{0}=\rho_{1}\partial_{r}\phi\left(\rho_{1}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}A\,,

with
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

f(m)=2\sqrt{\frac{B(r_{\min})}{\pi}}\left(\partial_{n}\phi\left(\rho_{1}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{r_{\min}}\right)^{2m+1}+\partial_{n}\phi\left(\rho_{2}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{r_{\min}}\right)^{2m+1}\right)\,.

Remark 1.4
.

We note that, unlike
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
, the decreasing behavior in
h
is the same for each of the eigenvalues. This comes from the fact that the scalar potential does not admit a unique non-degenerate minimum.

We give here a brief outline of the ideas to establish the main theorem.

i.

In Section
2
, we follow
[
13
]
to select a useful gauge. The explicit description of the Hodge-de Rham theory reveals the role of circulations when writing the magnetic potential, see Proposition
2.3
. In Section
3
, we make a fibration of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator by means of Fourier series:

\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{-}.

ii.

Techniques used in
[
4
]
and in Sections
5
and
6
share common features. First, we prove uniform ellipticity inequalities for the Dirac operator for fixed
m
. Then, we deduce a lower bound for
k\geqslant 2
of the
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k,m}(h)
of
\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{-}
. Finally, with the upper bound on
\lambda_{1,m}(h)
, we deduce a localization and a monomial approximation for the eigenfunctions associated to
\lambda_{1,m}(h)
.

iii.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on Proposition
4.1
:

(a)

The lowest eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h}
are, in the semiclassical limit, ground states of
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h,m}
, see Lemma
4.6
.

(b)

The sequence of renormalized eigenvalues,

f_{1,h}(m)=\frac{\lambda_{1,m}(h)}{\sqrt{h}\,e^{2\phi_{\min}/h}},

with
\phi_{\min}
defined in Remark
1.2
, converges uniformly (in
m
) on any compact of
\mathbb{R}
, when
h
tends to
0
. Moreover, it also verifies a property of weak coercivity with respect to
m
.

Finally, if
m(h)
is such that
\lambda_{1,m(h)}(h)=\lambda_{k}(h)
, the upper bound of Lemma
4.5
implies that
m(h)
is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
. The min-max formula on integers with the uniform convergence on any compact ends the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Choice of gauges on the annulus

When the domain is simply connected the vector potential
A
can be chosen, via gauge invariance, equal to
\nabla^{\perp}\phi
, where
\phi
is a solution of (
1.5
), modulo the gradient of a regular function
[
4
]
.

In this section we slightly revisit
[
13
]
by describing explicitly an equivalence class of magnetic potentials defined in such a way that the set of associated Pauli operators are unitarily equivalent.

2.1.
Selection of vector potential

Recall that
A
is fixed and satisfies (
1.3
).
By gauge invariance, we can choose a new magnetic potential
\mathbf{\check{A}}
such that

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{rot}(\mathbf{\check{A}})=B\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mathrm{div}({\bf\check{A}})=0&\text{on}\;\Omega,\\
{\bf\check{A}}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0&\text{in}\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In fact, consider
f
a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta f=\mathrm{div}(A)&\text{on}\;\Omega\\
\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{n}=-A\cdot\mathbf{n}&\text{in}\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

Such a solution exists but is not unique, see
[
3
, Theorem 5.2.18]
.

We can easily check that
\mathbf{\check{A}}=A+\nabla f
verify (
2.1
).

The unitary transformation to be carried out on the operator to make such a modification is the following

\sigma\cdot\left({\bf p}-\mathbf{\check{A}}\right)=\exp\left(i\frac{f}{h}\operatorname{Id}\right)\;\sigma\cdot\left({\bf p}-A\right)\;\exp\left(-i\frac{f}{h}\operatorname{Id}\right).

2.2.
A class of admissible vectors potential

Let us describe a family of vector potentials associated with
B
. To do so, we will need the following two lemmas, consequences of the Hodge de-Rham theory, whose proofs are provided in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
a vector potential satisfying (
2.1
) with
B=0
and

\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}F=0,

where
\partial\Omega_{int}
is defined in (
1.2
).

Then,
F=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\theta
the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\Delta\theta=0&\text{on}\;\Omega&&&\\
\theta=1&\text{in}\;\partial\Omega_{int}&\text{and}&\theta=0&\text{in}\;\partial\Omega_{ext}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\nabla^{\perp}\theta
verifies (
2.1
) with
B=0
and we have in polar coordinates, for all
(r,s)\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]\times[0,2\pi[
,

\nabla^{\perp}\theta(r,s)=\frac{1}{r\;\mathrm{ln}(\rho_{1}/\rho_{2})}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(s)\\
\cos(s)\end{pmatrix}\;.

Moreover

\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}\nabla^{\perp}\theta=\frac{2\pi}{\ln{\left(\rho_{1}/\rho_{2}\right)}},

with
\partial\Omega_{int}
defined in (
1.2
).

We have a family of vector potentials that give rise to unitarily equivalent operators.

Notation 2
.

We note
\mathrm{arg}(\cdot)
the principal value of the argument that lies within the interval
[0,2\pi[
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Recall that
\phi
is defined in (
1.5
) and
\theta
in Lemma
2.2
.

For all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h>0
, consider

{\bf A_{h,p}}=\nabla^{\perp}\phi\;+\;h\,\gamma_{h,p}\;\mathrm{ln}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)\,\nabla^{\perp}\theta\,,

with
\gamma_{h,p}=p+c_{0}/h
and
c_{0}=\rho_{1}\partial_{r}\phi\left(\rho_{1}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}A
.

Then, we have

\left[\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{h,p}}\right)\right]^{2}=e^{ip\,\mathrm{arg}}\,\left[\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{\check{A}}\right)\right]^{2}\,e^{-ip\,\mathrm{arg}}.

Proof.

(1)

Let us show that there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbf{\check{A}}=\nabla^{\perp}\phi+c\nabla^{\perp}\theta
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
to be determined, consider

F=\mathbf{\check{A}}-\nabla^{\perp}\phi-\alpha\nabla^{\perp}\theta.

The vector field
F
verifies (
2.1
). By linearity, we have
\mathrm{rot}\left(F\right)=\mathrm{div}\left(F\right)=0
. Moreover, since
\phi
and
\theta
are constant on each connected component of the boundary we have

\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\mathbf{n}=-\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t}\phi(\gamma(t))=0\;\;\;\text{and similarly}\;\;\;\nabla^{\perp}\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,

with
\gamma
a parametrization of a connected component of the boundary and
\mathbf{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}
the associated unit normal.

If we find
\alpha
such that
\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}F=0
, then by Lemma
2.1
we have
F=0
.

On the one hand we have for
(r,s)\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]\times[0,2\pi[

\nabla^{\perp}\phi(r,s)=\partial_{r}\phi(r)\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(s)\\
\cos(s)\end{pmatrix},

thus

\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}\nabla^{\perp}\phi=2\pi\rho_{1}\partial_{r}\phi(\rho_{1}).

On the other hand,

\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}\mathbf{\check{A}}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}A+\nabla f=\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}A\,.

Finally, the choice

\alpha=\ln{\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)}\left(\rho_{1}\partial_{r}\phi(\rho_{1})-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega_{int}}A\right),

gives the desired result.

(2)

Let us now take
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h>0
.

For
(x,y)\in\Omega
, the function
\mathrm{exp}(ip\,\mathrm{arg}(x,y))
is smooth. We can change the magnetic potential by conjugating with the unitary operator
\mathrm{exp}(ip\,\mathrm{arg})

Note that for all
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times[0,2\pi[

-ih\nabla=-ih\,\left(\begin{pmatrix}\cos{(s)}\\
\sin{(s)}\end{pmatrix}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin{(s)}\\

For
(x,y)\in\Omega
, we have, in polar coordinates, for all

,

-ih\nabla\,e^{ip\,\mathrm{arg}(x,y)}=\frac{hp}{r}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin{(s)}\\
\cos{(s)}\end{pmatrix}e^{ips}=hp\,\ln{\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)}\nabla^{\perp}\theta\,e^{ip\,\mathrm{arg}(x,y)},

where we used Lemma

Finally, we have

\left[\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{h,p}}\right)\right]^{2}=e^{ip\,\mathrm{arg}}\,\left[\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{\check{A}}\right)\right]^{2}\,e^{-ip\,\mathrm{arg}}

{\bf A_{h,p}}={\bf\check{A}}\,+\,ih\,e^{ip\mathrm{arg}}\,\nabla e^{-ip\mathrm{arg}}={\bf\check{A}}\,+\,hp\,\ln{\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\right)}\nabla^{\perp}\theta.

Taking

3.
Fibration of the Dirichlet-Pauli operator

In this section, we decompose the Dirichlet-Pauli operator, with potential
\mathbf{A_{h,p}}

Under the assumption that the magnetic field is radial, the Dirichlet-Pauli operator in polar coordinates, denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{h}}

Definition 3.1
.

Let
h\in]0,1]
and
m,p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We define the operator
\left(\mathcal{P}_{h,m},\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h,m}\right)\right)
on
L^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
as the operator acting as

(3.4)

\mathcal{P}_{h,m}=\left[-h^{2}\left(\partial_{rr}^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{4r^{2}}\right)\;+\;\left(h\;\frac{\left(m-\gamma_{h,p}\right)}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi(r)\right)^{2}\right]I_{2}\;-\;hB(r)\sigma_{3}\;,

with
\gamma_{h,p}
defined in Proposition

.

Moreover,
\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h,m}\right)=H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
. We also note
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h,m}
and
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{h,m}
, the operators given by

\mathcal{P}_{h,m}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}^{-}_{h,m}&0\\
0&\mathcal{L}^{+}_{h,m}\end{pmatrix}\,.

We give in the next lemma the connection between the spectrum of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
and that of
\mathcal{P}_{h,m}
for

. A proof of this result is given in Appendix

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumptions made in Section
1
, we have for all
h\in]0,1]
:

(3.5)

\mathrm{Sp}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}\right)=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Sp}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h,m}\right)},

with

given in Definition

is an eigenfunction of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{h}}
associated with the same eigenvalue, with

given in (

).

.

In the same way as in Lemma

3.1.
Extension of the definition of known operators

The definition of
\mathcal{P}_{h,m}
, see Definition
3.1
, naturally leads us to consider real moments (and not only integer moments). Indeed, in (
3.4
), the factor
m-\gamma_{h,p}
is no more an integer.

Section
3.1.1
is devoted to the study of some remarkable operators with real angular momentum.

3.1.1.
Real angular momentum operators

All the results of this section will be demonstrated in the case where the magnetic potential is equal to
\textbf{A}_{0}=\nabla^{\perp}\phi
. We will see in the next subsection that it is sufficient when considering real moments.

Let us define the magnetic gradient, magnetic Laplacian and magnetic Dirac operators appearing during the manipulation of the Pauli operator. This definition allows us to have a better overview of the operator
\mathcal{P}_{h,m}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
h\in]0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will show that by defining
\mathcal{P}_{h,m}
with the potential
\mathbf{A_{0}}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{-}=d_{h,m}d_{h,m}^{\times}\,,\;\;\;\mathcal{L}_{h,m+1}^{+}=d_{h,m}^{\times}d_{h,m},

where
d_{h,m}
given in Definition
3.5
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
.

We define
\left(\mathbf{p}_{h,m},\,\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathbf{p}_{h,m}\right)\right),\;\left(d_{h,m},\,\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}\right)\right),\;\left(\mathscr{M}_{h,m},\,\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathscr{M}_{h,m}\right)\right)
the operators on
L^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
acting as

\begin{split}\textbf{p}_{h,m}&=-ihe_{r}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)+e_{s}\left(\frac{hm}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)\,,\\
d_{h,m}&=-ih\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{m+1/2}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\,,\\
\mathscr{M}_{h,m}&=\textbf{p}_{h,m}^{\times}\cdot\textbf{p}_{h,m}=-h^{2}\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{4r^{2}}\right)+\left(\frac{hm}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)^{2}\,,\end{split}

where the family of vectors
(e_{r},e_{s})
constitutes a direct orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\textbf{p}_{h,m}^{\times}
is the formal adjoint of
\textbf{p}_{h,m}
.

Moreover,

\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(\textbf{p}_{h,m}\right)=H^{1}_{0}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\mathrm{Dom}\left(\mathscr{M}_{h,m}\right)=H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right).

The operators
\textbf{p}_{h,m},\,d_{h,m},\,\mathscr{M}_{h,m}
are actually related to each other as one can see in the following.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\textbf{A}_{0}=\nabla^{\perp}\phi
where
\phi
is the unique solution of the Poisson’s equation (
1.5
).

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
, we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}d^{\times}_{h,m}d_{h,m}&=\mathscr{M}_{h,m+1}+hB\,,\\
d_{h,m}d_{h,m}^{\times}&=\mathscr{M}_{h,m}-hB\,,\end{split}

where
d^{\times}_{h,m}
is the formal adjoint of
d_{h,m}
(in the distribution meaning).

When
m
is an integer, the operators
\textbf{p}_{h,m}
and
d_{h,m}
acting on the radial functions verify

(3.7)

\begin{split}\textbf{p}_{h,m}&=e^{-ims}r^{1/2}\left(\textbf{p}-\textbf{A}_{0}\right)r^{-1/2}e^{ims}\,,\\
\mathscr{M}_{h,m}&=e^{-ims}r^{1/2}\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}}\right|^{2}r^{-1/2}e^{ims}\,,\end{split}

and

(3.8)

\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{h,m}\\
d^{\times}_{h,m}&0\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-ims}&0\\
0&e^{-i(m+1)s}\end{pmatrix}r^{1/2}\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}})r^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}e^{ims}&0\\
0&e^{i(m+1)s}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

where
\textbf{p}=-ih\nabla=-ihe_{r}\partial_{r}-ihe_{s}\frac{\partial_{s}}{r}
is the momentum operator in polar coordinates
(e_{r}(s)=(\cos(s),\sin(s))
,
e_{s}(s)=(-\sin(s),\cos(s)))
,
\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}}\right|^{2}
is the magnetic Laplacian.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\right)
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
.

(i)

Let us start with (
3.6
). We have

\displaystyle d^{\times}_{h,m}d_{h,m}

\displaystyle\,u=-h^{2}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{m+1/2}{r}+\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{m+1/2}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\,u

\displaystyle=-h^{2}\left(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{m+1/2}{r^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{m+1/2}{r}\right)^{2}+2\frac{m+1/2}{r}\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)^{2}\right)u\,,

and then

\begin{split}d^{\times}_{h,m}d_{h,m}\,u&=-h^{2}\left(\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{(m+1)-1/2}{r^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{(m+1)-1/2}{r}\right)^{2}\right.\\
&\left.+2\frac{(m+1)-1/2}{r}\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)^{2}\right)u\\
&=\mathscr{M}_{h,m+1}u+h\left(\partial_{r}^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\phi\right)u\\
&=\left(\mathscr{M}_{h,m+1}+hB\right)u\,.\end{split}

In the same way,

\begin{split}d_{h,m}d^{\times}_{h,m}&\,u=-h^{2}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{m+1/2}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{m+1/2}{r}+\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\,u\\
&=-h^{2}\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{m+1/2}{r^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{m+1/2}{r}\right)^{2}+2\frac{m+1/2}{r}\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}-\left(\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)^{2}\right)u\\
&=\mathscr{M}_{h,m}u-h\left(\partial_{r}^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\phi\right)u\\
&=\left(\mathscr{M}_{h,m}-hB\right)u\,.\end{split}

(ii)

Let us then establish (
3.7
). On the one hand, we have

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &r^{1/2}\partial_{r}r^{-1/2}u=\partial_{r}u-\frac{1}{2r}u\,,\\
&r^{1/2}\partial^{2}_{r}r^{-1/2}u=\partial^{2}_{r}u-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}u+\frac{3}{4r^{2}}u\,,\end{aligned}\right.

on the other hand, we deduce from the radialness of
\phi
that

(3.10)

A_{0}=\nabla^{\perp}\phi=\left(e_{r}\partial_{r}+e_{s}\frac{\partial_{s}}{r}\right)^{\perp}\phi=e_{s}\partial_{r}\phi\,.

The magnetic gradient becomes

\begin{split}e^{-ims}r^{1/2}\left(\textbf{p}-\textbf{A}_{0}\right)r^{-1/2}e^{ims}v&=e^{-ims}r^{1/2}\left(-ih\left(e_{r}\partial_{r}+e_{s}\frac{\partial_{s}}{r}\right)-e_{s}\partial_{r}\phi\right)r^{-1/2}e^{ims}v\\
&=-ihe_{r}r^{1/2}\partial_{r}r^{-1/2}v+e_{s}\left(\frac{hm}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)v\\
&=\textbf{p}_{h,m}v\,.\end{split}

Then, the magnetic Laplacian satisfies

\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}}\right|^{2}u=-h^{2}\Delta\;u+2ihA_{0}\cdot\nabla\;u+\left\lvert A_{0}\right\rvert^{2}u\,.

In polar coordinates, we have

\begin{split}&r^{1/2}\Delta\;r^{-1/2}u=\left(\partial^{2}_{r}+\frac{1}{4r^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}_{s}}{r^{2}}\right)u\,,\\
&r^{1/2}A_{0}\cdot\nabla\;r^{-1/2}u=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\partial_{r}\phi\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\\
\frac{\partial_{s}}{r}\end{pmatrix}u=\partial_{r}\phi\frac{\partial_{s}}{r}u\,,\\
&r^{1/2}\left\lvert A_{0}\right\rvert^{2}r^{-1/2}u=\left(\partial_{r}\phi\right)^{2}u\,.\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}e^{-ims}r^{1/2}\left|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}}\right|^{2}r^{-1/2}e^{ims}u&=-h^{2}\left(\partial^{2}_{r}+\frac{1}{4r^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)u-2\frac{hm}{r}\partial_{r}\phi u+\left(\partial_{r}\phi\right)^{2}u\\
&=\mathscr{M}_{h,m}u\,.\end{split}

(iii)

Let us finish with (
3.8
). Using (
3.10
) and the properties of Pauli matrices, we have

\begin{split}\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}})&=\sigma\cdot\left(-ihe_{r}\partial_{r}+e_{s}\left\{\frac{-ih\partial_{s}}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right\}\right)\\
&=\sigma\cdot e_{r}\left(-ih\partial_{r}\operatorname{Id}_{2}+i\sigma_{3}\left\{\frac{-ih\partial_{s}}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right\}\right)\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-is}\\
e^{is}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-ih\partial_{r}+i\left\{\frac{-ih\partial_{s}}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right\}&0\\
0&-ih\partial_{r}-i\left\{\frac{-ih\partial_{s}}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right\}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ihe^{-is}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{-i\partial_{s}}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\\
-ihe^{is}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{-i\partial_{s}}{r}+\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)&0\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

From Equation (
3.9
), we have

\begin{split}r^{1/2}&\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{A_{0}})r^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}e^{ims}&0\\
0&e^{i(m+1)s}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ihe^{-is}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{-i\partial_{s}-1/2}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)\\
-ihe^{is}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{-i\partial_{s}+1/2}{r}+\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{h}\right)&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}e^{ims}\\
v_{2}e^{i(m+1)s}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}e^{ims}&0\\
0&e^{i(m+1)s}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{h,m}\\
d^{\times}_{h,m}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.\end{split}

∎

The following lemma will be important when studing the adjoint of
d_{h,m}
and to determine elliptic estimates.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
h\in]0,1]
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
. We have

\begin{split}\left\|d_{h,m}u\right\|^{2}&=h^{2}\left\|\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)u\right\|^{2}+\left\|\left(\frac{h(m+1)}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)u\right\|^{2}+h\left\|\sqrt{B}u\right\|^{2}\\
\left\|d^{\times}_{h,(m+1)}u\right\|^{2}&=h^{2}\left\|\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)u\right\|^{2}+\left\|\left(\frac{h(m+1)}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)u\right\|^{2}-h\left\|\sqrt{B}u\right\|^{2}\,,\end{split}

Proof.

Let
h\in]0,1]
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
. By using Proposition
3.6
, one easily checks that

\displaystyle\left\|d_{h,m}u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\textbf{p}_{h,m+1}u\right\|^{2}+h\left\|\sqrt{B}\,u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=h^{2}\left\|\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)u\right\|^{2}+\left\|\left(\frac{h(m+1)}{r}-\partial_{r}\phi\right)u\right\|^{2}+h\left\|\sqrt{B}u\right\|^{2}\,.

We proceed in the same way for
\left\|d^{\times}_{h,(m+1)}u\right\|^{2}
.
∎

Next proposition gives an explicit description of the kernel of the adjoint of
d_{h,m}
and of its orthogonal.

Proposition 3.8
.

(i)

The operator
\left(d_{h,m},\;\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}\right)\right)
is closed with closed range.

(ii)

The adjoint
\left(d_{h,m}^{*},\;\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)\right)
acts as
d_{h,m}^{\times}
on
\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)=H^{1}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)

and

\mathrm{ker}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)=\mathrm{Vect}\left(r\mapsto e^{-\phi/h}r^{m+1/2}\right).

(iii)

We have,
\mathrm{ker}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)=\left\{d_{h,m}w;\;\;w\in H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)\right\}
.

Proof.

(i)

Lemma
3.7
ensures the equivalence between the norm
H_{0}^{1}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
and the graph norm of
d_{h,m}
. Thus, the operator
\left(d_{h,m},\;\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}\right)\right)
is closed. The closed image property follows from the same lemma. Indeed, for any
u\in H_{0}^{1}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)

\left\|d_{h,m}u\right\|\geqslant\sqrt{2hB_{0}}\left\|u\right\|,

with
B_{0}=\inf_{x\in\Omega}B(x)>0
by assumption.

(ii)

By definition,

\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)\subset\left\{u\in L^{2};\;\;d_{h,m}^{\times}u\in L^{2}\right\}=H^{1}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right).

On the other hand, if
v\in H^{1}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
we have for all
w\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)

\left\langle v,d_{h,m}w\right\rangle=\left\langle d_{h,m}^{\times}v,w\right\rangle,

and
d_{h,m}^{\times}v\in L^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)
. Finally, we can extend by density this result to
\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}\right)
to obtain
v\in\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)
and
d_{h,m}^{*}=d_{h,m}^{\times}
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ker}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{2};\;\;d_{h,m}^{\times}u=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{e^{-\phi/h}v,v\in L^{2};\;\;\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{m+1/2}{r}\right)v=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vect}\left(r\mapsto e^{-\phi/h}r^{m+1/2}\right).

(iii)

The following equalities are consequences of Proposition
3.6
,

\begin{split}&\mathrm{ker}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)^{\perp}\cap\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)=\mathrm{Im}\left(d_{h,m}\right)\cap\mathrm{Dom}\left(d_{h,m}^{*}\right)\\
&=\left\{d_{h,m}w;\;\;w\in H_{0}^{1}\text{ and }d_{h,m}^{\times}d_{h,m}w=\left(\mathcal{M}_{h,m+1}+hB\right)w\in L^{2}\right\}\\
&=\left\{d_{h,m}w;\;\;w\in H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

∎

3.2.
Back to the Pauli operator and symmetry of the Dirac operator

Following the same idea as in Remark

and

that give us an explicit formula for the spectrum of

Definition 3.9
.

Let
h>0
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Consider
\left(\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{\pm},Dom\left(\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{\pm}\right)\right),
the self-adjoint operators acting as

\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{-}=d_{h,\widetilde{m}}d_{h,\widetilde{m}}^{\times}\,,\;\;\;\mathcal{L}_{h,m+1}^{+}=d_{h,\widetilde{m}}^{\times}d_{h,\widetilde{m}}\;\;and\;\;Dom\left(\mathcal{L}_{h,m}^{\pm}\right)=H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}\left([\rho_{1},\rho_{2}],\mathbb{C}\right),

where
d_{h,\widetilde{m}}
defined in Definition
3.5
, with
\widetilde{m}=m-\gamma_{h,p}
and
\gamma_{h,p}
defined in Proposition
2.3

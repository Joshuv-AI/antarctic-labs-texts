Tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions via Fock-Goncharov duality and a conjecture of Ringel
Peigen Cao, Antoine de St. Germain, Jiang-Hua Lu

Abstract
. We study tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions associated to symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrices in the framework of Fock-Goncharov duality in cluster algebras. In particular, we generalize and prove a conjecture of C. M. Ringel on cluster-additive functions associated to arbitrary Cartan matrices of finite type. For a Fock-Goncharov dual pair of positive spaces of finite type, we use tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions to explicitly express the Fock-Goncharov pairing between their tropical points and the bijections between global monomials on one and tropical points of the other.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction

Additive functions for a translation quiver

\Gamma=(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1},\tau)
, defined as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued functions on the
vertex set
\Gamma_{0}
and satisfying certain mesh-type conditions,
play an important role in the
representation theory of finite dimensional algebras
[
HPR80
,
Bob99
]
,
[
DG80
, p.52]
. When
\Gamma
is the repetition quiver of an acyclic valued quiver, the definition of additive functions can be formulated using the underlying Cartan matrix.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be any
r\times r
symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
). An
additive function associated to
A
is a map
d:[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

d(i,m)+d(i,m+1)=\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}(-a_{j,i})d(j,m)+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-a_{j,i})d(j,m+1),\;\;\;(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

(1.1)

Here and for the rest of the paper, we write
[1,r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
for any integer
r\geq 1
.

Motivated by the combinatorial features of cluster categories
[
BMR+06
]
and cluster-tilted algebras
[
BMR07
]
,
C. M. Ringel introduced in
[
Rin12
,
\lx@sectionsign
11]
the notion of
cluster-additive functions
. At roughly the same time, L. Guo introduced in
[
Guo13
]
(see also
[
Pro20
]
)
tropical friezes
in the context of
2-Calabi-Yau Hom-finite triangulated categories. We reformulate their definitions again using Cartan matrices as follows. For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, set
[a]_{+}={\mathrm{max}}(0,a)
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be any
r\times r
symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix.

1) A
cluster-additive function associated to
A
is a map

k:{[1,r]}\times{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

k(i,m)+k(i,m+1)=\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}(-a_{j,i})[k(j,m)]_{+}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-a_{j,i})[k(j,m+1)]_{+},\;\;\;(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

(1.2)

2) A
tropical frieze associated to
A
is a map

f:{[1,r]}\times{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

f(i,m)+f(i,m+1)=\left[\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}(-a_{j,i})f(j,m)+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-a_{j,i})f(j,m+1)\right]_{+},\;\;\;(i,m)\in[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

(1.3)

Let
C_{\rm add}(A)
, resp.
C_{\rm frieze}(A)
, be the set of all
cluster-additive functions, resp. tropical friezes, associated to
A
.
As (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are recursive with respect to the total order
on
[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}
given by

(i,m)<(i^{\prime},m^{\prime})\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{iff}\hskip 14.45377ptm<m^{\prime},\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;m=m^{\prime}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;i<i^{\prime},

(1.4)

one has bijections
C_{\rm add}(A)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
and
C_{\rm frieze}(A)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}

via evaluations on
[1,r]\times\{0\}
.

Suppose that
A
is of finite and simply-laced type, and identify
[1,r]\times{\mathbb{Z}}
with the
vertex set of the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the
derived category
D^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda
is the path algebra of the Dynkin quiver associated to
A
.
Ringel
[
Rin12
, p. 477]
conjectured a
periodicity property
of every

k\in C_{\rm add}(A)
, which amounts to saying that
k
can be regarded as a cluster-additive function on
the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the
cluster category
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits\Lambda
,
i.e., the orbital quotient of
D^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits\Lambda)
by its Frobenius isomorphism
[
BMR+06
]
.
For every (not necessarily basic) tilting module
T
of
\Lambda
, Ringel also constructed in
[
Rin12
,
\lx@sectionsign
10]

a function
d_{T}\in C_{\rm add}(A)
expressed via dimensions of indecomposable modules of the
cluster-tilted algebra of
T
, and gave a decomposition of
d_{T}
into
certain elementary cluster-additive functions called
cluster-hammock functions

(see §
6.6
). The main conjecture of Ringel in
[
Rin12
]
, stated in

[
Rin12
,
\lx@sectionsign
6]
, says that
every
k\in C_{\rm add}(A)
is a finite sum of
cluster-hammock functions
. Ringel’s conjecture is thus the cluster categorical analog of
Butler’s theorem
[
But81
]
that additive functions on the Auslander-Reiten quiver of a representation-finite
algebra are integral linear combinations of hammock functions.
Ringel’s conjecture on cluster-additive functions was proved by Ringel
[
Rin12
]
for type
A_{n}

and by Guo
[
Guo13
]
for all (finite and simply-laced) type. See Remark
7.10
for
Guo’s work
[
Guo13
]
on tropical friezes.

In this paper, we study tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions associated to

arbitrary symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrices
, and, more importantly, in contrast to the categorical approaches of Ringel
[
Rin12
]
and Guo

[
Guo13
]
,
we do so via tropical evaluations of functions on certain Fock-Goncharov dual pairs of positive spaces
[
FG09
]
,
thereby placing tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions in the framework of Fock-Goncharov duality in the theory of cluster algebras
[
FG09
,
GHKK18
]
.

To achieve the said goal, for a Fock-Goncharov dual pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{Y})
as defined in
[
FG09
]
(see Definition
2.4
),
instead of looking for parametrizations of vector space bases of the algebra of (global) functions on
\mathbb{A}
(resp. on
\mathbb{Y})
by
the set of tropical points of
\mathbb{Y}
(resp. of
\mathbb{A}
) as in the original conjectures stated in
[
FG09
]
,
we introduce a
correspondence
between (global) functions on
\mathbb{A}
(resp.
on
\mathbb{Y}
) and tropical points of
\mathbb{Y}
(resp. of
\mathbb{A})
via the notion of
admissible functions
. For
an arbitrary symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix
A
, we give two
realizations, of both tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions, via tropical points of one space and admissible functions on the other in a suitable Fock-Goncharov dual pair, and we show that
the two realizations coincide if and only if the tropical points and the admissible functions are in correspondence (see Theorem A in
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
and Theorem C in
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
). In particular, the values of
tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions are interpreted via denominator vectors of admissible functions.

The connections between tropical friezes, cluster-additive functions and Fock-Goncharov duality are in full
display when the Cartan matrix
A
is of
finite type
:
as a consequence of the validity of the Fock-Goncharov duality
for cluster algebras of finite type, we
formulate and prove Ringel’s conjecture on cluster-additive functions associated to a Cartan matrix
A

of any finite type

(i.e., not necessarily simply-laced as in the original conjecture of Ringel in
[
Rin12
]
),
and we determine, in Lie theoretical terms, the periodicity of all cluster-additive functions and tropical friezes
associated to such an
A
.
In the other direction, for any Fock-Goncharov dual pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{Y})
of finite type, we show that admissible functions
on
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are precisely all the global monomials
[
GHKK18
, Definition 0.1]
,
and we use tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions to
express, in formulas that can be
readily implemented by a computer program,
the bijection between tropical points of
\mathbb{Y}
(resp. of
\mathbb{A}
) and
global monomials on
\mathbb{A}
(resp. on
\mathbb{Y}
) as well as
the Fock-Goncharov pairing between tropical points of
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{Y}
.

We give more precise statements of the main results of the paper in

2
.

2.2.
Fock-Goncharov duality via admissible functions

Let
B
be any
r\times r
mutation matrix, not necessarily of the form

in (
2.5
),
and consider the Fock-Goncharov
dual pair
(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}},\mathbb{Y}_{B})
.

Following
[
GHKK18
, Definition 0.1]
, for
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
or
\mathbb{Y}_{B}

{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{X})
that are
Laurent monomials in some cluster of

on
\mathbb{X}

\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}

\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
defined
in
[
FZ02
]
(see Lemma

). By
[
GHKK18
]
(see Lemma

and Lemma-Definition

), global monomials on

\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
are in bijection with their
g
-vectors which lie in
\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})

\mathbb{Y}_{B}
are in bijection with their
g
-vectors which lie in
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})

V. V. Fock and A. B. Goncharov conjectured in
[
FG09

that
there is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{\rho}\}
of
{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})

\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and extending the
parametrization of cluster monomials on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
by their
g
-vectors, and a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{y_{\scriptstyle{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}\}

of
{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
, parameterized by
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and extending the parametrization of
global monomials on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
by their
g
-vectors, such that

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{\rho})={\rm val}_{\rho}(y_{{\scriptstyle{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}}),\hskip 14.45377pt\;\;\forall\;\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max}),\;\;\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max}).

(2.14)

The common integer value in (
2.14
) is the conjectured
Fock-Goncharov pairing
between the tropical points

\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.
The conjecture as stated does not hold in general
[
GHK15
]

{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
may have “too few” elements. In
[
GHKK18
]
the authors modified the

\theta_{\rho}\in{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
and
\theta_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\in{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})

\rho

\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and

in a certain subset of
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
, and it is conjectured in
[
GHKK18
, Remark 9.11]

that (
2.14
) holds
for
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{\rho}=\theta_{\rho}
and
y_{\scriptstyle{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}=\theta_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
. When

is skew-symmetric,
the conjecture in

[

has been proved in
[

]
(see Remark

While theta functions are important, their construction in

4.4

\rho

Similarly, for

), and we write

-admissible. An element in

Remark 3.1
.

5.
Fock-Goncharov pairing from the perspective of admissible functions

5.1.
A question

Motivated by
[
FG09
, Conjecture 4.3]
, we ask the following question
on a mutation matrix
B
in terms of the Fock-Goncharov dual pair
(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}},\mathbb{Y}_{B})
.

Question 5.1
.

What mutation matrices
B
have
Property
\mathfrak{P}
, in the sense that

{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\neq\emptyset
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})\neq\emptyset

for every
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and that

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\rho}(y)

(5.1)

for any
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
and
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
?

If the mutation matrix
B
has Property
\mathfrak{P}
, one then has a
well-defined
Fock-Goncharov pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\;\;\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathrm{max}}})\times\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathrm{max}}})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\;\;\langle\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\rho\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\rho}(y)

by taking any
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
and

y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
for
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.

Remark 5.2
.

Recall from
\lx@sectionsign
4.6
the theta functions given by the maps

\Theta(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}),\;\;\rho\longmapsto\theta_{\rho}\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt\Theta(\mathbb{Y}_{B})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B}),\;\;\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\longmapsto\theta_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}.

The
theta pairing conjecture
in
[
GHKK18
, Remark 9.11]
says that

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\theta_{\rho})={\rm val}_{\rho}(\theta_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}})
for all

\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\Theta(\mathbb{Y}_{B})\subset\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathrm{max}}})
and

\rho\in\Theta(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\subset\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathrm{max}}})
.
The theta pairing conjecture for skew-symmetric mutation matrices has recently been proved in
[
CMMM22
]
.

\diamond

We now discuss the values of the two expressions in (
5.1
). Following
[
GHKK18
, Definition 9.1 and Lemma 9.3]
, for

\rho=\{\rho_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}_{\rm row}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}}\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathrm{max}}})\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\{\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}_{\rm row}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}(\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathrm{max}}}),

(5.2)

we say that

\rho
is
optimized at
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
if
-\rho_{w}\geq 0
, and that
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is

optimized at
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
if
-\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{w}B_{w}^{T}\geq 0
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\rho
and
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
be as in (
5.2
), and let
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})

and
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
. Then

1)
{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\geq-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}
for every
t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
; if
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is
optimized at
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
, then
{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}
;

2)
{\rm val}_{\rho}(y)\geq-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}
for every
t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
; if
\rho
is optimized at
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
, then

{\rm val}_{\rho}(y)=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}
.

Proof.

1) Let
t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
be arbitrary and write

x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=({\bf x}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{-\rho_{t}^{T}}(1+L_{t})
as in (
4.2
), so
L_{t}
is a
sum of Laurent monomials of the form
({\bf x}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{B_{t}^{T}{\bf u}}
with
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
.
Then

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}+{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(1+L_{t})=-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}+{\rm max}\{0,{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(L_{t})\}\geq-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}.

Suppose that
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is optimized at
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
. Then
-{\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}B_{w}^{T}{\bf u}\geq 0
for every
monomial term
({\bf x}_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{B_{w}^{T}{\bf u}}
of
L_{w}
. Thus

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}+{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(1+L_{w})=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}.

One proves 2) similarly.
∎

The following special case of (
5.1
), which has been noted in
[
GHKK18
, Remark 9.11]
for theta functions
(see also
[
FG09
, 4 of Conjecture 4.3 and Theorem 5.2]
),
is key to our study in

\lx@sectionsign
6

of tropical friezes and cluster-additive functions.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})

and
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
, where
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathrm{max}}})

and
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}(\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathrm{max}}})
.
If
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is a global monomial on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
, or if
y
is a global monomial on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
, then

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\rho}(y)={\rm max}\{-\delta_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{t}^{T}:t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}\},

(5.3)

and the maximum is achieved at any
w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
such that either
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
or
y
is a Laurent monomial at
w
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is a global monomial on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
and
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
.
By Lemma
4.2
,
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=({\bf x}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{w})^{-\rho_{w}^{T}}
for some

w\in\mathbb{T}_{r}
with
-\rho_{w}\geq 0
. Then

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}({\bf x}_{w}^{-\rho_{w}^{T}})=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}
.
Since
\rho
is optimized at
w
, by Lemma
5.3
one has
{\rm val}_{\rho}(y)=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}={\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.

Suppose that
y
is a global monomial on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
and

x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
. By Lemma
4.3
,
y={\bf y}_{w}^{-(\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{T}}
for some

w\in{\mathbb{T}}_{r}
and
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is optimized at
w
. By Lemma
5.3
again,

{\rm val}_{\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=-\delta_{w}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rho_{w}^{T}={\rm val}_{\rho}(y)
.
∎

Recall from Remark
4.6
that when
B
has full rank,
any
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
is
\rho
-admissible for a unique
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.
We do not know whether or not such a uniqueness statement holds in general,
but we have the following consequence of Proposition
5.4
.

Corollary 5.5
.

For a mutation matrix
B
, if the semi-field
\mathbb{F}_{>0}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
has a set of generators consisting of global monomials on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
, then
every
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
is
\rho
-admissible for a unique
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.

Proof.

Let
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}
be a set of generators of

\mathbb{F}_{>0}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
consisting of global monomias on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
, and for

i\in[1,l]
, let
\delta_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
be the
g
-vector of
z_{i}
.
Suppose that

x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\cap{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}_{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
for
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})={\rm Hom}_{\rm sf}(\mathbb{F}_{>0}(\mathbb{Y}_{B}),\mathbb{Z}^{\rm max})
.
Then by Proposition
5.4
, one has

\rho(z_{i})={\rm val}_{\rho}(z_{i})={\rm val}_{\delta_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\rho^{\prime}}(z_{i})=\rho^{\prime}(z_{i})

for every
i\in[1,l]
. As
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}
generates

\mathbb{F}_{>0}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
, one has
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
.
∎

.

Under the condition on the mutation matrix

5.2.
Fock-Goncharov pairing in finite type

In this subsection, assuming that

is of finite type, i.e.,
\mathbb{A}_{B}
, equivalently,

\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
, has finitely many cluster variables, we prove in
Theorem
5.9
that admissible functions on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
and on

\mathbb{Y}_{B}
are precisely all the global monomials, and we recover in Corollary
5.10

the well-defined Fock-Goncharov pairing established in
[
FG09
,
\lx@sectionsign
5.1]
.

For any mutation matrix
B
, recall
that
{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
denotes the set of all cluster monomials on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})

that of all global monomials on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
.
Recall also that the
g
-vector of
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
is denoted as
\rho(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
,
and the
g
-vector of
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
is denoted as

\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}(y)\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.
It is well-known
[
HPS18
]
that the
g
-vector fan
of
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
(see Remark
4.9
) is complete if
B
is of finite type.

Lemma 5.8
.

When
B
is of finite type, one has bijections

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max}),\;\;x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\longmapsto\rho(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max}),\;\;y\longmapsto\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}(y).

Proof.

Recall from
\lx@sectionsign
4.2

that
\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{Y}_{B},{\mathbb{Z}})
is
the set of the
g
-vectors of all cluster monomials on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
.
As the
g
-vector fan of
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
is complete in this case,

\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{Y}_{B},{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
.
By (
4.13
), one also has

\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}},{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
, where
\Delta^{+}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}},{\mathbb{Z}})
is the set of the
g
-vectors of all global
monomials on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
. Lemma
5.8
now follows from Lemma
4.8
and Lemma-Definition
4.17
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

When the mutation matrix
B
is of finite type, one has

{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})={\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})={\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B}).

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\mathbb{Y}_{B}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
and assume that
z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
is
\rho
-admissible. Since
B
is of finite type and by Lemma
5.8
,
there exists a cluster monomial

x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
of
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
whose
g
-vector is
\rho
. It suffices to show
z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
.

Let
a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
and let
d_{a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\in\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm max})
be the
d
-tropical point of
a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
defined in
Lemma-Definition
3.7
. One has
{\rm val}_{d_{a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}(u^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,u^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}
for any
u^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathcal{U}^{+}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})\setminus\{0\}
.
By Lemma
5.8
, there exists a global monomial
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
whose
g
-vector is
d_{a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
.
Then by Proposition
5.4
, one has

{\rm val}_{d_{a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}(z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})={\rm val}_{\rho}(y)={\rm val}_{d_{a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
.
Hence,

(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}=(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}
for any
a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
.

Since
B
is of finite type, cluster monomials on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
form an atomic
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
(see
[
FT17
, Theorem 10.2]
). Thus the element
z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
admits a unique expression
z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{q}
, where each

x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is a cluster monomial on
\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}
. It follows that

(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}=(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}={\rm max}\{(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d},\,\ldots,\,(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x_{q}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}\},\hskip 14.45377pt\forall\;a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}}).

(5.4)

Thus
(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}\geq(a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\,\|\,x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})_{d}
for every
a^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
and every
j\in[1,q]
.
By Lemma
3.10
,
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=x_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
for each
j\in[1,q]
. It follows that
q=1
and
z^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
. Hence,
{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})={\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{A}_{B^{T}})
.

Assume now that
y\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
. By Proposition
4.16
,

\widetilde{p}_{\!{}_{B}}^{*}(y)\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B}^{\rm prin})
, where the map
\widetilde{p}_{\!{}_{B}}^{*}
is given in
(
4.12
). Let
\phi:{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B}^{\rm prin})\to{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{A}_{B})
be the coefficient specialization
which sends every cluster variable
u_{t;i}
of
\mathbb{A}_{B}^{\rm prin}
to the corresponding cluster variable
x_{t;i}
of
\mathbb{A}_{B}

and every frozen variable
p_{j}
to
1
. Then
\phi(\widetilde{p}_{\!{}_{B}}^{*}(y))\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{A}_{B})
, so

\phi(\widetilde{p}_{\!{}_{B}}^{*}(y))
is a cluster monomial on
\mathbb{A}_{B}
, which implies that
\widetilde{p}_{\!{}_{B}}^{*}(y)

is an extended cluster monomial on
\mathbb{A}_{B}^{\rm prin}
. By Proposition
4.16
again,

must be a global monomial on
\mathbb{Y}_{B}
. Thus
{\mathcal{U}}^{\rm admi}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})={\mathcal{U}}^{\rm mono}(\mathbb{Y}_{B})
.
∎

.

FG09
,

-vector

Proof.

This follows from Theorem

.

We give some remarks comparing our results with Guo’s in

In

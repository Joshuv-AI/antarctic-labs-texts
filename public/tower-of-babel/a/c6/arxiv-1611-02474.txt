Blowup solutions for a nonlinear heat equation involving a critical power nonlinear gradient term Thanks: H. Zaag is supported by the ERC Advanced Grant no. 291214, BLOWDISOL and by the ANR project ANAÉ ref. ANR-13-BS01-0010-03. —————– August 10, 2026


Abstract
. We consider the following exponential reaction-diffusion equation involving a nonlinear gradient term: ∂ t U = Δ ​ U + α ​ | ∇ U | 2 + e U , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ N × [ 0 , T ) , α > − 1 . \partial_{t}U=\Delta U+\alpha|\nabla U|^{2}+e^{U},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T),\quad\alpha>-1. We construct for this equation a solution which blows up in finite time T > 0 T>0 and satisfies some prescribed asymptotic behavior. We also show that the constructed solution and its gradient blow up in finite time T T simultaneously at the origin, and find precisely a description of its final blowup profile. It happens that the quadratic gradient term is critical in some senses, resulting in the change of the final blowup profile in comparison with the case α = 0 \alpha=0 . The proof of the construction inspired by the method of Merle and Zaag in 1997, relies on the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one, and uses the index theory to conclude. One of the major difficulties arising in the proof is that outside the blowup region , the spectrum of the linearized operator around the profile can never be made negative. Truly new ideas are needed to achieve the control of the outer part of the solution. Thanks to a geometrical interpretation of the parameters of the finite dimensional problem in terms of the blowup time and the blowup point, we obtain the stability of the constructed solution with respect to perturbations of the initial data.

1.
Introduction.

We are interested in the following nonlinear heat equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}U&=&\Delta U+\alpha|\nabla U|^{r}+e^{U},\\
U(0)&=&U_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
U(t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to U(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Delta
and
\nabla
stand for the Laplacian and the gradient in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 1
,

r=2\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha>-1.

Equation (
1.1
) can be viewed as the limiting case of the following critical splitting as
p\to+\infty
, which was introduced by Chipot and Weissler
[
CW89
]
:

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+\alpha|\nabla U|^{r}+|U|^{p-1}U,\quad\text{with}\;\;p>1\;\text{and}\;r=\frac{2p}{p+1}.

(1.2)

The Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) can be solved in several functional spaces
\mathcal{F}
, for example
\mathcal{F}=W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or in a special affine space
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}_{a}
for some positive constant
a
with

\mathcal{H}_{a}=\{u\in\psi+W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\text{with}\;\psi(x)=-\ln(1+a|x|^{2})\}.

(1.3)

In particular, the problem (
1.1
) has a unique classical solution
U(t)\in\mathcal{F}
defined on
[0,T)
with
T\leq+\infty
(see Remark
1.3
for more details). In the case
T=+\infty
, we have the global existence for
U(t)
; on the contrary, i.e
T<+\infty
, we say that the solution
U(t)
blows up in finite time
T
, namely

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\|U(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=+\infty,

or

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\|U(t)-\psi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=+\infty\;\;\text{in the second case.}

As for the case of equation (
1.1
), the value
r=2
is a critical exponent for different reasons (
r<2
and
r>2
correspond to the sub-critical and super-critical cases). One reason is that, when
r=2
, equation (
1.1
) is invariant under the following transformation:

\forall\lambda>0,\quad U_{\lambda}(x,t)=2\ln\lambda+U(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),

(1.4)

as for the equation without the gradient term, i.e
\alpha=0
. Recalling that equation (
1.2
) is invariant under the transformation

\forall\lambda>0,\quad U_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}U(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

(1.5)

Let us now sketch the main results for the case of the equation

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+e^{U}.

(1.6)

One of the first result for the finite-time blow up problem (
1.6
) is due to Friedman and McLoed
[
FM85
]
who proved the following upper and lower bounds for the blowup rate of
U
under some conditions on the initial data,

-c\leq U(0,t)+\ln(T-t)\leq C,

(1.7)

for some
c,C
positive (see also Berbenes and Eberly
[
BE89
]
for this estimate).

The study of the blowup behavior for solution (
1.6
) is done through the introduction of similarity variables

W_{a}(y,s)=U(x,t)+\ln(T-t),\quad y=\frac{x-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\ln(T-t),

(1.8)

where
a
may or not be a blowup point for
U
. From (
1.6
), we see that
W_{a}
satisfies the following equation: for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\ln T,+\infty)
,

\partial_{s}W_{a}=\Delta W_{a}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla W_{a}+e^{W_{a}}-1.

Since
s\to+\infty
as
t\to T
, the change of variables (
1.8
) converts any question about the blowup of
U
into one about the large time asymptotic of
W_{a}
.

According to Berbenes and Eberly
[
BE89
]
, we know that if
U(x,t)
is a solution of (
1.6
) which blows up at
x=a
and
t=T
, then

\lim_{t\to T}\big[U(a+y\sqrt{T-t},t)+\ln(T-t)\big]=\lim_{s\to+\infty}W_{a}(y,s)=0,

(1.9)

uniformly on compact sets
|y|\leq R
.

This estimate has been refined until the higher order by Bebernes and Briche
[
BB92
]
, Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV93
]
in one dimensional case. More precisely, they classified the behavior of
W_{a}
for
|y|
bounded, and showed that one of the following cases occurs:

\sup_{|y|\leq R}\left|W_{a}(y,s)+\frac{1}{4s}(y^{2}-2)\right|=o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right),

(1.10)

or

\sup_{|y|\leq R}|W_{a}(y,s)|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\mu s})\quad\text{for some $\mu>0$,}

(the exponential convergence has been refined up to order
1
in
[
HV93
]
). It is remarkable that a similar result can be extended to higher dimensional cases by using the technique of
[
Vel92
]
for the equation

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+|U|^{p-1}U.

(1.11)

When (
1.10
) occurs, the authors of
[
BB92
]
established the following blowup profile in the variable
z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
(which is the intermediate scale that separates the regular and singular parts)

\lim_{t\to T}\big[U(a+z\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|},t)+\ln(T-t)\big]=\lim_{s\to+\infty}W_{a}(z\sqrt{s},s)=\Phi_{0}(z),

(1.12)

uniformly on compacts in
z
, where

\Phi_{0}(z)=-\ln\left(1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{4}\right).

(1.13)

Note that (
1.12
) was formally obtained in
[
Dol89
]
,
[
Dol85
]
and
[
VGH91
]
by means of the method of matched asymptotic expansions. The asymptotic behavior (
1.12
) leads to the limiting profile in the
U(x,t)
variable, in the sense that
U(x,t)\to U^{*}_{0}(x)
when
t\to T
if
x\neq a
and
x
is in the neighborhood of
a
with

U^{*}_{0}(x)\sim-2\ln|x-a|+\ln|\ln|x-a||+\ln 8.

(1.14)

In
[
Bre90
]
and
[
Bre92
]
, Bressan proved the existence of solutions to (
1.6
) which blow up in finite time and verify the behavior (
1.12
). He also obtained the stability of such blowup behavior with respect to small perturbations of the the initial data. For more results related to equation (
1.6
), see Fila and Pulkkinen
[
FP08
]
,
[
Pul11
]
and the references therein.

By considering
\alpha\neq 0
and
r=2
in (
1.1
), we want to ask whether the nonlinear gradient term appearing in the equation affects the blowup profile of the case
\alpha=0
, i.e equation (
1.6
). Note that the case
r<2
can be considered as a perturbation of equation (
1.6
) because the nonlinear gradient term has a sub-critical size in the sense that in the similarity variables setting (
1.8
), equation (
1.1
) yields

\partial_{s}W_{a}=\Delta W_{a}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla W_{a}+\alpha e^{-\delta s}|\nabla W_{a}|^{r}+e^{W_{a}}-1,\quad\text{with}\;\;\delta=1-\frac{r}{2}.

(1.15)

This gives another explanation to the fact that problem (
1.1
) is critical when
r=2
. In fact, our problem is motivated by the work of Tayachi and Zaag
[
TZ16
]
treated for equation (
1.2
). In that paper, the authors construct for (
1.2
) a solution which blows up in finite time
T
only at the origin and give a sharp description of its blowup profile in the case where
\alpha>0
and
p>3
. The originality of
[
TZ16
]
lays in the fact that the constructed solution does not exist in the case of the standard nonlinear heat equation (
1.11
). In particular, their solution has a profile depending on the variable

z=\frac{x}{|\ln(T-t)|^{\mu}\sqrt{T-t}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mu=\frac{p+1}{2(p-1)}>\frac{1}{2},

(1.16)

which is different from the results for equation (
1.11
) by Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV93
]
,
[
Vel92
]
,
[
Vel93
]
, Bricmont and Kupianen
[
BK94
]
, where the blowup profiles depend on the reduced variables

z=\frac{x}{|\ln(T-t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{T-t}}\quad\text{or}\quad z=\frac{x}{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2m}}}\;\;\text{where $m\geq 2$ is an integer.}

This evidently shows the effect of the forcing gradient term in equation (
1.2
) with
\alpha>0
in the equation. It is worth to mention the work by Ebde and Zaag
[
EZ11
]
where the authors considered equation (
1.2
) in the case where
r
is sub-critical, i.e
r<\frac{2p}{p+1}
. They showed that the involved nonlinear gradient term in (
1.2
) does not affect to the final blowup profile, leading to the same result (construction and stability of a solution whose blowup profile depends on the variable
z=\frac{x}{|\ln(T-t)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{T-t}}
) for the standard nonlinear heat equation (
1.11
) obtained in
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
. For more results related to (
1.2
), see Galaktionov and Vázquez
[
GV93
]
,
[
GV96
]
, Snoussi, Tayachi and Weissler
[
ST07
]
, Chipot and Weissler
[
CW89
]
, Fila
[
Fil91
]
, Souplet, Tayachi and Weissler
[
STW96
]
,
[
Sou01
]
,
[
ST01
]
, Chlebík, Fila and Quittner
[
CFQ03
]
.

In this paper, we aim at constructing a solution for equation (
1.1
) and giving precisely the description of its blowup profile. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1

(
Existence of a blowup solution for equation (
1.1
) with the description of its profile
)
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
r=2
, there exists
T_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for each
T\in(0,T_{0}]
, equation (
1.1
) has a solution
U(x,t)
such that
U
and
\nabla U
blow up in finite time
T
at the origin simultaneously. Moreover,

(i)

For all
t\in[0,T)
,

\left\|(T-t)e^{U(x,t)}-e^{\Phi_{\alpha}\left(z\right)}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|}},

(1.17)

where
C
is some positive constant and

\Phi_{\alpha}(z)=-\ln\left(1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{4+4\alpha}\right),\quad z=\frac{x}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}}.

(1.18)

(ii)

The functions
e^{U}
and
\nabla U
blow up at the origin and only there.

(iii)

There exists a function
U^{*}_{\alpha}(x)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
U(x,t)\to U^{*}_{\alpha}(x)
and
\nabla U(x,t)\to\nabla U^{*}_{\alpha}(x)
as
t\to T
, uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, where

U^{*}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\ln\left(\frac{(8+8\alpha)|\ln|x||}{|x|^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as $|x|\to 0$,}

(1.19)

and

|\nabla U^{*}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}\quad\text{as $|x|\to 0$.}

(1.20)

Remark 1.2
.

Although our problem can be considered as the limiting case of (
1.2
) treated in
[
TZ16
]
, the construction is far from a simple adaptation of the method in
[
TZ16
]
. Compared to the paper
[
TZ16
]
, our work has the following major difficulty: the spectrum of the linearized operator around the profile outside the
blowup region
can never be made negative. This requires new ideas as far as the control of the outer component is concerned, and this is one of the main novelties of our approach. Note that the linearized operator in the power case (equation (
1.2
)) behaves as one with fully negative spectrum, which greatly simplifies the analysis in the outer region.

Remark 1.3
.

The initial data for which equation (
1.1
) has a solution blowing up in finite time
T
at the origin and verifying the behavior (
1.17
) and (
1.19
) is given by formulation (
3.12
) below. Note that the initial datum
U_{0}(x)
defined in (

\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Indeed, if we define

\partial_{t}\tilde{U}=\Delta\tilde{U}+2\alpha\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\tilde{U}+\alpha|\nabla\tilde{U}|^{2}+e^{\psi}e^{\tilde{U}}+\alpha|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\Delta\psi.

(1.21)

Since

1.21

for equation (
1.1

The derivation of the profile

.

From part

Remark 1.6
.

The results stated in Theorem
1.1
also hold for more general equations than (
1.1
), that is

\partial_{t}U=\Delta U+\alpha|\nabla U|^{2}+e^{U}+F(U,\nabla U),

where

is Lipschitz and satisfies

|F(u,v)|\leq C(1+e^{r_{1}u}+|v|^{r_{2}}),\quad r_{1}\in[0,1),\;\;r_{2}\in[0,2).

Indeed, the nonlinear term

can be considered as subcritical (in size) in comparison with the main nonlinear terms in the equation, therefore, a simple perturbation of our proof can be extended to this case without difficulties.

Our goal is to construct for equation (

) a solution

which blows up in finite time

and verifies the behaviors (

). The proof is performed in the framework of the similarity variables defined in (

), we may assume

in (

) and write

for simplicity of the notation. We recall from (

,
W
solves the equation

\partial_{s}W=\Delta W-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla W+\alpha|\nabla W|^{2}+e^{W}-1.

(1.23)

If we introduce

Z=e^{W},

then
Z
solves

\partial_{s}Z=\Delta Z-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla Z+(\alpha-1)\frac{|\nabla Z|^{2}}{Z}+Z^{2}-Z.

(1.24)

Constructing a solution for (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.17
) reduces to the construction of a solution for (
1.24
) such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}Q(y,s)&=e^{W(y,s)}-e^{\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)}\equiv Z(y,s)-\Gamma_{\alpha}(y,s)\to 0,\end{array}\quad\text{as}\;s\to+\infty,

(1.25)

where
Q
satisfies the equation

\partial_{s}Q=\big(\mathscr{L}+V(y,s)\big)Q+Q^{2}+(\alpha-1)\frac{|\nabla Q+\nabla\Gamma_{\alpha}|^{2}}{Q+\Gamma_{\alpha}}+R(y,s),

(1.26)

and

\mathscr{L}=\Delta-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla+1,\quad V=2(\Gamma_{\alpha}-1),\quad\|R(y,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{s}.

Satisfying such a property is guaranteed by a condition that
Q(s)
belongs to some set
\mathcal{V}_{A,K_{0}}(s)
which shrinks to
0
as
s\to+\infty
(see item
(i)
in Definition
3.1
below for an example). Let us insist on the fact that we do not work with equation (
1.23
), but with equation (
1.24
) (see explaination below). Since the linearization of equation (
1.24
) around the profile
e^{\Phi_{\alpha}}\equiv\Gamma_{\alpha}
gives
N+1
positive modes,
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
zero modes, and an infinite dimensional negative part, we can use the method of
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
which relies on two arguments:

-

The use of the bounding effect of the heat kernel to reduce the problem of the control of
Q(s)
in
\mathcal{V}_{A,K_{0}}(s)
to the control of its positive modes (in fact, we control a modified version of
Q
in
\mathcal{V}_{A,K_{0}}
; see Proposition
3.5
). Let us insist on the fact that if we linearize equation (
1.23
) around
\Phi_{\alpha}
, say that
q=W-\Phi_{\alpha}
, we could not able to control
q
outside the
blowup region
because in this region the linear part of the equation satisfied by
q
does not have a fully negative spectrum. In the contrary, the spectrum of the linear operator of the equation satisfied by
Q
is negative outside the
blowup region
, which makes the control of
Q
in that region easily.

-

The control of the
(N+1)
positive modes thanks to a topological argument based on index theory (see the arguments at page
3
).

(Note that the topological argument is also used in
[
Bre92
]
to solve the finite dimensional problem, however, the approach of the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one in our proof is totally different from that in
[
Bre92
]
).

Since the gradient term in the equation (
1.1
) in the critical case contributes to the change of the blowup profile and given the fact that the blowup profile
\Phi_{\alpha}
is different from the one considered in
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
, our proof truly requires crucial modifications of the methods of
[
BK94
]
and
[
MZ97b
]
and special arguments in order to handle the nonlinear gradient term. These modifications lay in the following places:

(i)

We no longer work with the linearization of equation (
1.23
) around
\Phi_{\alpha}
, but instead the equation (
1.26
) satisfied by
Q(y,s)
. Note that linearizing (
1.23
) around the profile
\Phi_{\alpha}
generates the linear operator
\mathscr{L}+\tilde{V}
which behaves similarly as in the power case (equation (
1.2
)) inside the
blowup region
, but there is a quite difference outside the
blowup region
. Indeed, Bricmont and Kupiainen’s approach for equation (
1.2
) gives that the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}+\tilde{V}
in the outer region is controlled by
-\frac{1}{p-1}+\epsilon
which can be made negative by taking
\epsilon
small enough. Whereas, the same estimate for equation (
1.1
) gives that the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}+\tilde{V}
is controlled by
0+\epsilon
, which can never be made negative. In order to overcome this difficulty, we no longer work with the linearization of equation (
1.23
) around
\Phi_{\alpha}
, but instead the equation (
1.26
) satisfied by
Q
. In fact, the spectrum of the linear part of equation (
1.26
) is fully negative in the outer region, which make it easy to control. However, this manner gives additionally a term of the form
\frac{|\nabla Q+\nabla\Gamma_{\alpha}|^{2}}{Q+\Gamma_{\alpha}}
(see (
1.26
)) which is needed new ideas to achieve the control.

(ii)

Defining the shrinking set
\mathcal{S}^{*}
(see Definition
3.1
below) to trap the solution. Note that our definition of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
is defferent from the one in
[
MZ97b
]
designed for the standard nonlinear heat equation (
1.2
) with
\alpha=0
. Note also that equation (
1.26
) is almost the same as in
[
MZ97b
]
, except for the nonlinear gradient term which causes serious difficulties in the analysis. In
[
MZ97b
]
, the authors introduced estimates of
Q
in the
blowup region

|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
, and in the regular region
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
. However, the estimates in the region
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
imply smallness of
Q
only, and do not allow any control of the nonlinear gradient term in this region. In other words, the analysis based on the method of
[
MZ97b
]
, that is to estimate the solution in the
z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
variable is not sufficient and must be improved. In particular, we introduce estimates of
Q
in three regions in a different variable scale, which follows the approach of
[
MZ97a
]
using for a finite time quenching problem of vortex reconnection with the boundary. More precisely, in the
blowup region
, i.e.
|x|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}
, we do an asymptotic analysis around the profile
\Gamma_{\alpha}
through equation (
1.26
). In the intermediate region, i.e.
\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\leq|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}
, we control the solution by using classical parabolic estimates through introducing a rescaled function of
U
(see (
3.4
) below). In the regular region, i.e.
|x|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}
, we estimate directly
U
. The estimates in the intermediate and regular regions allow us to control the nonlinear gradient term appearing in equation (
1.26
).

The derivation of the final profile
U_{\alpha}^{*}(x)
stated in part
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
uses the method of
[
Zaa98
]
and
[
Mer92
]
. In particular, we need to establish a new
no blowup under some threshold
criterion for a parabolic inequality with a nonlinear gradient term, whose proof follows ideas given in
[
GK89
]
(see Proposition
3.6
).

The organization of the rest of this paper is as follows:

- In Subsection
2.1
, we first explain formally how we obtain the profile
\Phi_{\alpha}
and give a suggestion for an appreciated profile to be linearized around. In Subsection
2.2
, we give a formulation of the problem in order to justify the formal argument.

- In Section
3
, we give all the arguments of the proof of part
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
assuming technical results, which are left to the next section.

- In Section
4
, we give the proof of the technical results used in the existence’s proof, that is the proof of Proposition
3.5
. We divide its proof in two subsections. In Subsection
4.1
, we derive
a priori estimates
of
U(t)
in the set
\mathcal{S}^{*}(t)
. In Subsection
4.2
, we show that all the estimates given in Definition
3.1
of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
can be improved, except for the positive modes, which concludes the proof of the reduction to a finite dimensional one.

- In Appendix
A
, we give properties of the shrinking set
\mathcal{S}^{*}(t)
to trap the solution as well as properties of the initial data corresponding to the blowup solution described in Theorem
1.1
. In Appendix

, we give the proof of a

criterion for a parabolic inequality with a nonlinear gradient term, which is an ingredient in the proof of the existence of the final blowup profile.

2.
Formulation of the problem.

2.1.
A formal analysis.

In this subsection, we use matching asymptotics to formally derive the blowup behavior of the solution to (
1.1
). More precisely, we will explain how to deduce the following behavior

U(x,t)+\ln(T-t)\sim\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;t\to T,

(2.1)

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is defined by (
1.18
).

In the similarity variables setting (
1.8
), justifying (
2.1
) is equivalent to showing that

W(y,s)\sim\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty,

where
W
satisfies (
1.23
). Let us rewrite the equation on
W
as follows:

\partial_{s}W=\mathscr{L}W+\alpha|\nabla W|^{2}+Q(W),

(2.2)

where

\mathscr{L}=\Delta-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla+1,

(2.3)

and

Q(W)=e^{W}-1-W.

Note that we have

\big|Q(W)-\frac{1}{2}W^{2}\big|\leq C|W|^{3}\quad\text{as}\;\;W\to 0.

The linear operator
\mathscr{L}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the weighted
L^{2}
space associated with the weight
\rho
defined by

\rho(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}.

It can be shown that the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}
is explicitly given by (see
[
FK92
]
for instance)

\text{spec}(\mathscr{L})=\left\{1-\frac{n}{2},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For
N=1
, all the eigenvalues are simple and the eigenfunctions are dilations of Hermite polynomials: the eigenvalue
1-\frac{n}{2}
corresponds to the following eigenfunction:

h_{n}(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\big[n/2\big]}\frac{n!}{i!(n-2i)!}(-1)^{i}y^{n-2i}.

(2.4)

The first three eigenfunctions are

h_{0}(y)=1,\quad h_{1}(y)=y,\quad h_{2}(y)=y^{2}-2.

Notice that
h_{n}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{n}(\xi)h_{m}(\xi)\rho(\xi)d\xi=2^{n}n!\delta_{n,m},\quad\mathscr{L}h_{n}=\big(1-\frac{n}{2}\big)h_{n}.

(2.5)

We also introduce
k_{n}(y)=\|h_{n}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}h_{n}(y)
. In higher dimensions, the eigenfuntions are formed by taking products of the polynomials
\{h_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, namely that for
n=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, the eigenfunction corresponding to
1-\frac{|n|}{2}
(
|n|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}
) is

h_{n}(y)=h_{n_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots h_{n_{N}}(y_{N}).

(2.6)

Since the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}
span the whole space
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can expand the solution
W
as follows:

W(y,s)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}^{\infty}W_{m}(s)h_{m}(y).

For simplicity, let us assume that the solution is radially symmetric in
y
. Since
h_{m}
with
m\geq 3
correspond to negative eigenvalues of
\mathscr{L}
, we may consider that

W(y,s)=W_{0}(s)+W_{2}(s)(|y|^{2}-2N),

(2.7)

with
W_{0}(s),W_{2}(s)\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
(for simplicity in the notation, we write
W_{2}(s)
instead of
W_{(2,0,\cdots,0)}(s)
).

Writing
\alpha|\nabla W|^{2}=4\alpha(h_{2}+2Nh_{0})W_{2}^{2}
, multiplying equation (
2.2
) by
k_{0}\rho
and
k_{2}\rho
respectively and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we derive the following ODE system for
W_{0}
and
W_{2}
:

\displaystyle W_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=W_{0}+\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{2}+N(4+8\alpha)W_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|W_{0}|^{3}+|W_{2}|^{3})

(2.8)

\displaystyle W_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0+W_{0}W_{2}+(4+4\alpha)W_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|W_{0}|^{3}+|W_{2}|^{3}).

(2.9)

Assuming that

|W_{0}(s)|=o(|W_{2}(s)|)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty,

(2.10)

we then rewrite equation (
2.9
) as follows:

W_{2}^{\prime}=(4+4\alpha)W_{2}^{2}+o(|W_{2}|^{2})\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty,

which yields

W_{2}(s)=-\frac{1}{(4+4\alpha)s}+o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

From equation (
2.8
) and the above formula of
W_{2}
, we have
W_{0}^{\prime}=W_{0}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)
, which gives

|W_{0}(s)|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

From the above formulas of
W_{0}
and
W_{2}
, we have by equation (
2.9
),

W_{2}^{\prime}=(4+4\alpha)W_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{3}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty,

hence,

W_{2}(s)=-\frac{1}{(4+4\alpha)s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

Such
W_{0}
and
W_{2}
are compatible with the assumption (
2.10
). Therefore, we write by (
2.7
),

W(y,s)=-\frac{1}{4+4\alpha}\frac{y^{2}}{s}+\frac{N}{(2+2\alpha)s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty,

(2.11)

in
L^{2}_{\rho}
, and also uniformly in compact sets by standard parabolic regularity.

Since (
2.11
) provides a relevant variable for blowup, namely
z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
, we then try to search formally solutions of (
1.23
) of the form

\tilde{W}(y,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{j}}w_{j}(z),\quad z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}},

(2.12)

and compare elements of order
\frac{1}{s^{j}}
. For
j=0
and
j=1
, we find that

-\frac{z}{2}\cdot\nabla w_{0}(z)+e^{w_{0}(z)}-1=0,

(2.13)

F(z):=\frac{z}{2}\cdot\nabla w_{1}(z)-e^{w_{0}(z)}w_{1}(z)-\frac{z}{2}\cdot\nabla w_{0}(z)-\Delta w_{0}(z)-\alpha|\nabla w_{0}(z)|^{2}=0.

(2.14)

Recalling that
\tilde{W}\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
on every compact set, we naturally impose the condition

w_{0}(0)=0.

Solving equation (
2.13
) with this condition, we then obtain by radial symmetry

w_{0}(z)=-\ln(1+c_{0}|z|^{2}),

for an integration constant
c_{0}
. Because we want a bounded solution, then it requires
c_{0}>0
. From the Taylor expansion for
|y|
bounded, we write

\tilde{W}(y,s)\sim-\ln\left(1+c_{0}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}\right)\sim-c_{0}\frac{|y|^{2}}{s}-\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{2}\frac{|y|^{4}}{s^{2}}+\cdots,

from which and (
2.11
), we find that the coefficient
c_{0}=\frac{1}{4+4\alpha}
. Thus,

w_{0}(z)=-\ln\left(1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{4+4\alpha}\right).

Substituting this formula into (
2.14
) and evaluating
F
at
z=0
, we obtain

w_{1}(0)=2Nc_{0}=\frac{N}{2+2\alpha},

which matches with the expansion (
2.11
).

In conclusion, the first term in the expansion (
2.12
) of
\tilde{W}
is precisely the profile function
\Phi_{\alpha}
as expected in (
2.1
).

2.2.
Transformation of the problem.

In this subsection, we set up the problem of constructing a solution
U
for equation (
1.1
) which blows up in finite time
T
only at the origin and satisfies (
1.17
). In particular, we want to prove for suitable initial data
U_{0}
of (
1.1
) that

\lim_{t\to T}\left\|(T-t)e^{U(z\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|},t)}-e^{\Phi_{\alpha}(z)}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0,

(2.15)

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is defined by (
1.18
). Or, in the self-similar setting (
1.8
), our aim is to construct initial data
W(s_{0})
such that the equation (
1.23
) has a solution
W(y,s)
defined for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[s_{0},+\infty)
and satisfies

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left\|e^{W(y,s)}-e^{\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)}\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

(2.16)

In the previous subsection, we formally obtain for
W(y,s)
an expansion of the form
\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{j}}w_{j}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})
with
w_{0}=\Phi_{\alpha}
and
w_{1}
satisfying (
2.14
). Hence, we will not linearize
W
around
\Phi_{\alpha}
, but we will study the difference
W(y,s)-w_{0}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})-\frac{1}{s}w_{1}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})
. Since the expression of
w_{1}
is too complicated, we study instead
W(y,s)-w_{0}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})-\frac{1}{s}w_{1}(0)
. Let us introduce

Q(y,s)=e^{W(y,s)}-\psi_{\alpha}(y,s)\equiv Z(y,s)-\psi_{\alpha}(y,s)\;\;\text{where}\;\;\psi_{\alpha}(y,s)=e^{\frac{N}{(2+2\alpha)s}}e^{\Phi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)},

(2.17)

where
\Phi_{\alpha}
is defined in (
1.18
). Then, we see from (
1.24
) that
Q
satisfies

\partial_{s}Q=\big(\mathscr{L}+V(y,s)\big)Q+Q^{2}+G(Q)+R(y,s),

(2.18)

where
\mathscr{L}
is defined by (
2.3
) and

\displaystyle V(y,s)

\displaystyle=2(\psi_{\alpha}(y,s)-1),

(2.19)

\displaystyle G(Q)

\displaystyle=(\alpha-1)\left[\frac{|\nabla Q+\nabla\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}}{Q+\psi_{\alpha}}-\frac{|\nabla\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\psi_{\alpha}}\right],

(2.20)

\displaystyle R(y,s)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}\psi_{\alpha}+\Delta\psi_{\alpha}-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla\psi_{\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha}^{2}-\psi_{\alpha}+(\alpha-1)\frac{|\nabla\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\psi_{\alpha}}.

(2.21)

(Note that the equation satisfied by
Q
is almost the same as in
[
MZ97b
]
, except the term
G(Q)
).

Satisfying (
2.16
) reduces to the construction of initial data
Q(y,s_{0})
such that the equation (
2.18
) has a solution
Q(y,s)
defined for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[s_{0},+\infty)
such that

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\|Q(s)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

(2.22)

Our analysis uses the Duhamel formulation of equation (
2.18
): for each
s\geq\sigma\geq s_{0}
, we have

Q(s)=\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma)Q(\sigma)+\int_{\sigma}^{s}\mathcal{K}(s,\tau)\left[Q^{2}(\tau)+G(Q(\tau))+R(\tau)\right]d\tau,

(2.23)

where
\mathcal{K}
is the fundamental solution of the linear operator
\mathscr{L}+V
defined for each
\sigma>0
and
s\geq\sigma
by

\partial_{s}\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma)=(\mathscr{L}+V)\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma),\quad\mathcal{K}(\sigma,\sigma)=\text{Identity}.

(2.24)

Since we want to construct for (
2.18
) a solution
Q
satisfying (
2.22
) and the fact that

\|R(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{s}
for
s
large, it is then reasonable to think that the dynamics of equation (
2.18
) are influenced by the linear part, namely
\mathscr{L}+V
. The properties of the self-adjoint operator
\mathscr{L}
are given in the previous subsection. In particular,
\mathscr{L}
is predominant on all the modes, except on the null modes where the terms
VQ
and
G(Q)
will play a crucial role. As for potential
V
, it has two fundamental properties which will strongly influence our strategy:

(i)

\|V(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
. In practice, the effect of
V
in the
blowup region

\{|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}\}
is regarded as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{L}
(except for the null mode).

(ii)

outside of the
blowup region
, we have the following property: for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
K_{\epsilon}>0
and
s_{\epsilon}
such that

|V(y,s)-(-1)|\leq\epsilon-1,\quad\forall s\geq s_{\epsilon},\;\forall|y|\geq K_{\epsilon}\sqrt{s},

which
1
is the largest eigenvalue of the operator
\mathscr{L}
. Thus, the spectrum of the linear operator
\mathscr{L}+V
is fully negative, hence, the control of
Q
in
L^{\infty}
ouside the
blowup region
will be done without difficulties. Note that linearizing equation (
1.23
) around
\Phi_{\alpha}
, say
q=W-\Phi_{\alpha}
, generates the linear operator
\mathscr{L}+\tilde{V}
whose spectrum in the outer region is fully positive. This is the major reason why we do not work with
q
, but with
Q=Z-\psi_{\alpha}
.

Since the behavior of the potential
V(y,s)
inside and outside of the
blowup region
is different, let us decompose
Q
as follows:

Q(y,s)=\chi(y,s)Q(y,s)+(1-\chi(y,s))Q(y,s)\equiv Q_{b}(y,s)+Q_{e}(y,s),

(2.25)

where

\chi(y,s)=\chi_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{K_{0}\sqrt{s}}\right),

(2.26)

and
\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,+\infty))
with
\text{supp}(\chi_{0})\subset[0,2]
and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
, and
K_{0}>0
is to be fixed large enough. Note that
supp(Q_{b}(s))\subset\mathbf{B}(0,2K_{0}\sqrt{s})
and
supp(Q_{e}(s))\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbf{B}(0,K_{0}\sqrt{s})
.

Since the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}
span the whole space
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, let us write

\displaystyle Q_{b}(y,s)

\displaystyle=Q_{0}(s)+Q_{1}(s)\cdot y+y^{T}Q_{2}(s)y-2tr(Q_{2}(s))+Q_{-}(y,s)

\displaystyle=Q_{0}(s)+Q_{1}(s)\cdot y+Q_{\bot}(y,s),

(2.27)

where

Q_{0}(s)=P_{0}(Q_{b})(y,s),\quad Q_{1}(s)\cdot y=P_{1}(Q_{b})(y,s),

Q_{-}(y,s)=P_{-}(Q_{b})(y,s)=\sum_{m\geq 3}P_{m}(Q_{b})(y,s),

Q_{\bot}(y,s)=P_{\bot}(Q_{b})(y,s)=\sum_{m\geq 2}P_{m}(Q_{b})(y,s),

and
P_{m}
is the projector on the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
1-\frac{m}{2}
defined by

P_{m}(Q_{b})(y,s)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N},|\beta|=m}\frac{h_{\beta}(y)}{\|h_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{\beta}(y)Q_{b}(y,s)\rho(y)dy,

(2.28)

where
h_{\beta}
is defined by (
2.6
), and
Q_{2}(s)
is a symmetric
(N\times N)
matrix defined by

Q_{2}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{b}(y,s)\mathcal{M}(y)\rho(y)dy,

(2.29)

and

\mathcal{M}(y)=\left\{\frac{1}{8}y_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{ij}\right\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

(2.30)

According to (
2.25
) and (
2.27
), we have

Q(y,s)=Q_{0}(s)+Q_{1}(s)\cdot y+y^{T}Q_{2}(s)y-2tr(Q_{2}(s))+Q_{-}(y,s)+Q_{e}(y,s),

(2.31)

and

Q(y,s)=Q_{0}(s)+Q_{1}(s)\cdot y+Q_{\bot}(y,s)+Q_{e}(y,s).

(2.32)

The reader should keep in mind that
Q_{m},\,m=0,1,2
,
Q_{-}
and
Q_{\bot}
are the coordinates of
Q_{b}
and not those of
Q
.

3.
Proof of the existence without technical details.

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

Lemma B.1
.

Let
r>0
and
\phi_{r}
be a smooth function supported on

, where

3.17
). Then, we have the following:

\displaystyle\|w_{r}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w(0)\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-s}}\right)\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}ds

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}^{2}\right)\|w_{r}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds,

(B.1)

\displaystyle\|g_{r}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g(0)\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-s}}\right)\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}ds+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\nabla u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}ds

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-s}}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\right)\|g_{r}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds.

(B.2)

Proof.

We only deal with (
B.1
) since (
B.2
) follows similarly. By the definition, we see that
w_{r}
satisfies the following equation:

\partial_{\tau}w_{r}-\Delta w_{r}=\big(e^{u}\Delta\phi_{r}-2\nabla\cdot(e^{u}\nabla\phi_{r})\big)+e^{u}\phi_{r}(\partial_{\tau}u-\Delta u-|\nabla u|^{2}),

hence, the semigroup representation formula for
w_{r}
gives

\displaystyle w_{r}(\tau)=e^{\tau\Delta}w_{r}(0)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\Delta}\big(e^{u}\Delta\phi_{r}-2\nabla\cdot(e^{u}\nabla\phi_{r})\big)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\Delta}e^{u}\phi_{r}(\partial_{\tau}u-\Delta u-|\nabla u|^{2})ds,

(B.3)

for
0<\tau<1
, where
e^{\theta\Delta}
is the semigroup associated with the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the following well known properties:

\|e^{\theta\Delta}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\|\nabla e^{\theta\Delta}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\forall\theta>0,\;f\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(B.4)

Using this regularity effect and the definition of
\phi_{r}
, the middle term in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\|e^{u}\Delta\phi_{r}-2\nabla\cdot(e^{u}\nabla\phi_{r})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-s}}\right)\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}ds.

3.17
), the last term is dominated by

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-s)\Delta}e^{u}\phi_{r}(1+e^{u}+|\nabla u|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\left\|e^{u(s)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}^{2}\right)\|w_{r}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}ds.

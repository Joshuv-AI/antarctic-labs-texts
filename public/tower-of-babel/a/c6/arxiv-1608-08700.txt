Abstract
We prove the Künneth formula for the irregular Hodge filtrations on the exponentially twisted de Rham and the Higgs cohomologies of smooth quasi-projective complex varieties. The method involves a careful comparison of the underlying chain complexes under a certain elimination of indeterminacy.

1
The main result

Let
U
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers, and
f\in\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{U})
a regular function. Attached to such a pair
(U,f)
and a non-negative integer
k
, one has the

k
-th de Rham cohomology
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U,f)
and
Higgs cohomology
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Hig}}^{k}(U,f)
, defined in Section
2
. The two spaces
\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{k}(U,f),\diamond\in\{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{Hig}\}
, are of the same finite dimension over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
1
, Remark 1.3.3]
), and are equipped with the decreasing
irregular Hodge filtrations

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{k}(U,f),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},

indexed by the ordered set
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers with finitely many jumps. In the following, we omit the adjective and just call them the
Hodge filtrations
. For the motivations and the basic properties of the Hodge filtration, including in particular the degeneration of the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence, see
[
1
,
3
]
and the references therein. We recall the construction in Section
2
.

Now consider two such pairs
(U_{i},f_{i})
,
i=1,2
. On the product
U:=U_{1}\times U_{2}
, consider the regular function
f
defined by
f\colon(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto f_{1}(x_{1})+f_{2}(x_{2})
. We call the pair
(U,f)
the
product
of the two
(U_{i},f_{i})
. For
\diamond\in\{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{Hig}\}
, there is the canonical map

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i+j=k}\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{i}(U_{1},f_{1})\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{j}(U_{2},f_{2})\to\mathrm{H}^{k}_{\diamond}(U,f)

(1)

induced by cup product. We equip the space on the left hand side with the product filtration, i.e., the
\lambda
-th filtration for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
is given by the subspace

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i+j=k}\left(\sum_{a+b=\lambda}F^{a}\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\diamond}(U_{1},f_{1})\otimes F^{b}\mathrm{H}^{j}_{\diamond}(U_{2},f_{2})\right),

where the inner sum is taken inside
\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{i}(U_{1},f_{1})\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{j}(U_{2},f_{2})
. In this article, we prove the following Künneth formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

With notations as above, the map (
1
) is an isomorphism of filtered spaces.

In particular, denoting
\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{\lambda}V
the
\lambda
-th graded piece of a filtered space
(V,F)
, one has the identification

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{\lambda}\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{k}(U,f)=\bigoplus_{r,s}\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{r}\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{s}(U_{1},f_{1})\otimes\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{\lambda-r}\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{k-s}(U_{2},f_{2}).

Recently different proofs of the Künneth formula in a more general setting appear in
[
8
, Theorem 3.39]
where the involved coefficients in the cohomology are allowed to be exponential twists of complex Hodge modules. One of the main ideas in that work is to enrich the de Rham and Higgs cohomologies into the Brieskorn lattice (see the last section Section
4
) or a twistor structure and treat the irregular Hodge filtration as a byproduct of the enrichment. The notion of a
V
-adapted trivializing lattice for a meromorphic connection on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of special type is introduced
[
8
, Section 3.2.b]
in order to obtain the Künneth formula for irregular Hodge filtrations. The methods depend on the deep theory of twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules mainly developed by Sabbah and Mochizuki (see
[
6
]
). On the other hand, our approach is much more elementary. We believe that the concrete filtered de Rham complex used here would be suitable for computations in some interesting examples of irregular Hodge structures in the future work.

The rest of the article begins in Section
2
with a brief of the construction of the Hodge filtration. We follow the approach of
[
11
]
by putting a filtration on the de Rham complex or the Higgs complex via a certain compactification of the pair
(U,f)
. Here we introduce the notion of a non-degenerate compactification, which is weaker than that of a good compactification used in
[
11
]
, but appears naturally in the later section (see also
[
7
, Section 4]
,
[
9
, Section 7.3]
,
[
5
]
). In order to compare the cohomologies with the filtrations of the summands
(U_{i},f_{i})
and of their product
(U,f)
, we construct a particular compactification of
(U,f)

from the fixed ones of
(U_{i},f_{i})
in Section
3
. The proof of the Künneth formula is obtained by a careful investigation of the relations between the involved complexes on the compactifications. In the last Section
4
, we remark that one can interpolate the two spaces
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Hig}}^{k}(U,f)
as the fibers of the
Kontsevich bundle
on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over
1
and
0
, respectively.
In fact, the fiber over
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
of the bundle is equal to
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U,f/c)
and hence the Künneth formula also holds true. However at
\infty
the situation is more complicated in this regard and the direct analogue of the Künneth formula does not hold in general.

2
The filtrations

In this section, we fix a pair
(U,f)
consisting of a smooth quasi-projective variety
U
over
\mathbb{C}
and a regular function
f\in\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{U})
.

2.1
The compactification

Let
X
be a projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
containing
U
such that the reduced subvariety
S:=X\setminus U
is a normal crossing divisor. Regard
f\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(*S))
as a rational function on
X
. Let
P
and
Z
be the pole divisor and the zero divisor of
f
on
X
, respectively, and let
{P}_{\mathrm{red}}
be the support of
P
.

{definition*}

(i)

We call
X
a
non-degenerate compactification
of
(U,f)
if there exists a neighborhood
V\subset X
of
P
such that
Z\cap V
is smooth and
Z+S
forms a reduced normal crossing divisor on
V
.

(ii)

We call
X
a
good compactification
of
(U,f)
if
f
indeed defines a morphism
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

If
X
is non-degenerate, analytically locally at a point of
P
, there exists a coordinate system

\displaystyle\{x_{1},\dots,x_{l},y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{r}\}

such that

\displaystyle S=(xy)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f=\frac{1}{x^{e}}\qquad\text{or}\qquad f=\frac{z_{1}}{x^{e}}

(2)

for some
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{l}
. (Here and afterwards, we use the standard multi-index convention.) If
X
is good, the second case of
f
in the above expression does not occur.

For example, consider the case where
U
is a complex torus and
f
a Laurent polynomial. Assume that
f
is non-degenerate with respect to its Newton polytope (for the definition, see
[
11
, Section 4]
). Then any toric smooth compactification compatible with the Newton polytope is a non-degenerate compactification of
(U,f)
by
[
11
, Proposition 4.3]
or
[
5
, Lemma 6.6]
. The non-degenerate compactification also appears in the considerations of rescaling
from a good compactification
[
9
, Section 7.3]
, and of Fourier transform
[
7
, Section 4]
. It is discussed in
[
5
]
where in this situation, the author calls the meromorphic function
f
on
X

non-degenerate along
S

[
5
, Definition 2.6]
. In this case, the author investigates the structures of the twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module associated with the meromorphic connection
(\mathscr{O}_{X}(*S),\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)
; it is shown in particular that if
S
equals the pole divisor of
f
, the resulting twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module is pure
[
5
, Lemma 2.10 and Corollary 3.12]
.

2.2
The filtered complexes

Fix a non-degenerate compactification
X
of
(U,f)
with boundary
S
. Regard
f
as a rational function on
X
and let
P=f^{*}(\infty)
be the pole divisor with multiplicities. We have
\mathrm{d}f\in\Gamma\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log S)(P)\big)
.

{definition*}

(i)

The
twisted de Rham complex
and the
Higgs complex
are the complexes

\displaystyle(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log S)(*P),\Theta)=\big[\mathscr{O}_{X}(*P)\!\xrightarrow{\Theta}\!\cdots\!\xrightarrow{\Theta}\!\Omega_{X}^{i}(\log S)(*P)\!\xrightarrow{\Theta}\!\Omega_{X}^{i+1}(\log S)(*P)\xrightarrow{\Theta}\!\cdots\big],

where
\Theta=\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f
and
\mathrm{d}f
, respectively. Here
\mathrm{d}f
sends a local section
\omega
to
\mathrm{d}f\wedge\omega
.

(ii)

We call the associated hypercohomology groups
\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(\log S)(*P),\Theta))
the
de Rham cohomology
and the
Higgs cohomology of
(U,f)
, and denote respectively by

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(U,f)=\mathrm{H}^{k}(U,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Hig}}^{k}(U,f)=\mathrm{H}^{k}(U,\mathrm{d}f).

(iii)

For an effective divisor
D
on
X
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
, let

\displaystyle\Omega_{X}^{i}(\log S)(\lfloor\mu D\rfloor)_{+}=\begin{cases}\Omega_{X}^{i}(\log S)(\lfloor\mu D\rfloor),&\text{if $\mu\geq 0$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
\Theta\in\{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}f\},\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
, let

\displaystyle F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)=F^{\lambda}(\Theta)=\big[\mathscr{O}_{X}(\lfloor-\lambda P\rfloor)_{+}\xrightarrow{\Theta}\cdots\xrightarrow{\Theta}\Omega_{X}^{i}(\log S)(\lfloor(i-\lambda)P\rfloor)_{+}\xrightarrow{\Theta}\cdots\big].

The subindex
X
in
F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)
will be omitted if the base variety is clear. The
Hodge filtrations
of the de Rham and the Higgs cohomologies are

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}\mathrm{H}^{k}(U,\Theta)=\mathrm{Image}\big\{\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,F^{\lambda}(\Theta))\to\mathrm{H}^{k}(U,\Theta)\big\}

(3)

induced by the inclusions of complexes.

(iv)

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
Kontsevich sheaf of differential
p
-forms
is the coherent subsheaf of
\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log S)(*P)

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)=\ker\big\{\Theta\colon\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log S)(\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor)\to\Omega_{X}^{p+1}(*S)/\Omega_{X}^{p+1}(\log S)(\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor)\big\},

and it forms the
Kontsevich complex

(\Omega_{f}^{\bullet}(\alpha),\Theta)
equipped with the filtration
(\Omega_{f}^{\bullet}(\alpha),\Theta)_{\bullet\geq p}
by direct truncation. We simply write
\Omega_{f}^{p}
for
\Omega_{f}^{p}(0)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

\mathrm{(i)}

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module
\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)
is locally free of rank
\binom{\dim X}{p}
with

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)=\Omega_{f}^{p}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{O}_{X}(\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor).

\mathrm{(ii)}

For
\Theta\in\{\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}f\}
the three inclusions

\displaystyle F^{0}(\Theta)\to F^{-\alpha}(\Theta),\qquad 0\leq\alpha,

\displaystyle(\Omega_{f}^{\bullet}(\alpha),\Theta)\to F^{-\alpha}(\Theta),\qquad 0\leq\alpha,

\displaystyle(\Omega_{f}^{\bullet}(\alpha),\Theta)_{\bullet\geq p}\to F^{-\alpha+p}(\Theta),\qquad 0\leq\alpha<1

are quasi-isomorphisms.

Proof 2.2
.

Both statements are local properties for coherent sheaves on
X
and we can restrict to the coordinates such that (
2
) holds.

(i) In case
f=\frac{1}{x^{e}}
, the local freeness and the quasi-isomorphisms are obtained in
[
1
, equation (1.3.1)]
,
[
3
, Lemma 2.12(a)]
and
[
11
, Proposition 1.3.]
,
[
1
, Proposition 1.4.2]
,
respectively. In fact, the methods are similar to the arguments below.

Consider the second case
f=\frac{z_{1}}{x^{e}}
so that

\displaystyle z_{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{f}=x^{e}\mathrm{d}f=\mathrm{d}z_{1}-z_{1}\sum e_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}.

In this chart,
the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module
\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log S)
is freely generated by

\displaystyle z_{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{f}\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p-1}\xi_{i},\qquad\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p-1}\subset\left\{\mathrm{d}z_{2},\dots,\mathrm{d}z_{r},\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{l}}{x_{l}},\frac{\mathrm{d}y_{1}}{y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\mathrm{d}y_{m}}{y_{m}}\right\}

(4)

and

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i},\qquad\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}\subset\left\{\mathrm{d}z_{2},\dots,\mathrm{d}z_{r},\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{l}}{x_{l}},\frac{\mathrm{d}y_{1}}{y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\mathrm{d}y_{m}}{y_{m}}\right\}.

(5)

The
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module
\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)
is indeed freely generated by

\displaystyle x^{-\lfloor\alpha e\rfloor}\omega_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{e-\lfloor\alpha e\rfloor}\omega_{2},

(6)

where
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
run through elements in (
4
) and (
5
), respectively.

(ii) Let
D
be a divisor supported on
{P}_{\mathrm{red}}
, and
E
an irreducible component of
{P}_{\mathrm{red}}
. Let

\displaystyle A^{p}_{k}(D)=\Omega^{p}(\log S)(D+pP+kE),\qquad p,k\in\mathbb{Z},\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}.

To obtain the quasi-isomorphisms, it suffices to show that the inclusion of complexes

\displaystyle(A_{k-1}^{\bullet}(D),\Theta)\to(A_{k}^{\bullet}(D),\Theta)

(7)

is a quasi-isomorphism for any
k,\alpha
and all choices of
D
(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 1.2]
). In fact, the first desired quasi-isomorphism then follows immediately. The last two quasi-isomorphisms can be derived by a decreasing induction (see the analogous statement
[
1
, Proposition 1.4.2]
in the case of good compactification and its proof, which works for both
\Theta=\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}f
).

To check the quasi-isomorphism (
7
), we may assume that
E=(x_{1})
in the local model. Consider local sections
h\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(D)
, and
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
in (
4
) and (
5
),
respectively. Then
\big\{h\cdot x^{-pe}x_{1}^{-k}\omega_{i}\big\}
generates
A_{k}^{p}(D)
and

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\

\displaystyle A_{k}^{p}(D)/A_{k-1}^{p}(D)\to A_{k}^{p+1}(D)/A_{k-1}^{p+1}(D),

\displaystyle\frac{h}{x^{pe}x_{1}^{k}}\cdot\begin{cases}\omega_{1},\\
\omega_{2}\end{cases}\mapsto\begin{cases}0,\\
\frac{h}{x^{(p+1)e}x_{1}^{k}}(x^{e}\mathrm{d}f)\wedge\omega_{2}.\end{cases}

Therefore the quotient
\big(A_{k}^{p}(D)/A_{k-1}^{p}(D),\Theta\big)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
of (
7
) is exact.

{remark*}

In a more functorial way, one can consider the
\mathscr{D}
-module
\mathscr{M}
on
X
attached to the meromorphic connection
(\mathscr{O}_{X}(*S),\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)
and define the
irregular Hodge filtration
on
\mathscr{M}
as given in
[
9
, Definition 5.1]
. By
[
9
, Lemma 9.17]
, the filtered complex
F^{\lambda}(\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)
defined above is quasi-isomorphic to the filtered de Rham complex associated with the filtered
\mathscr{D}
-module
\mathscr{M}
. When
X
is a good compactification, this is obtained in
[
1
, Proposition 1.7.4]
. Forgetting the filtrations, the quasi-isomorphisms of various de Rham complexes of
\mathscr{M}
are also derived in
[
5
, Lemmas 2.13 and 2.15]
in the case where
X
is non-degenerate, including the compactly supported counterpart.

2.3
The independence

{proposition*}

For
\diamond\in\{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{Hig}\}
, the space
\mathrm{H}_{\diamond}^{k}(U,f)
with the Hodge filtration is independent of the choice of the non-degenerate compactification of
(U,f)
. More precisely, if
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is a morphism between non-degenerate compactifications extending the identity on
U
, then there is a natural quasi-isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}F^{\lambda}_{X^{\prime}}(\Theta)\simeq F^{\lambda}_{X}(\Theta),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}.

(8)

Proof 2.3
.

The assertion for good compactifications is proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1.7]
, by comparing the degree
p
components
F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)^{p}
of
F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)
on various
X
for a fixed
p
(and hence the proof works for both
\Theta=\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f
and
\mathrm{d}f
).

In the following we show that by performing successively certain blowups, one can replace a non-degenerate compactification
X
(thus in the second case of (
2
)) by a good one and compare the involved chain complexes (cf.
[
11
, Section 4(
b
)]
). Let
\varpi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be the blowup along the intersection
\Xi
of
Z=(z_{1})
and the irreducible component
(x_{1})
of
P
with multiplicity
e_{1}
. Let
E
be the exceptional divisor,
\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{X}\setminus U
and
\widetilde{P}
the pole divisor of
\tilde{f}:=\varpi^{*}f
. We want to establish that
\mathrm{R}\varpi_{*}F_{\widetilde{X}}^{\lambda}(\Theta)
and
F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)
are canonically quasi-isomorphic.

In case
e_{1}>1
, it can be proved similarly as
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
. In more details, write
\lambda=-\alpha+p
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. On
\widetilde{X}
define the complex

\displaystyle R^{\lambda}(\Theta)=\big(\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{\bullet}\big(\log\widetilde{S}\big)(\lfloor(\alpha-p+\bullet)\big(\widetilde{P}+E)\rfloor\big)_{+},\Theta\big).

We have

\displaystyle\varpi^{*}F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta),F_{\widetilde{X}}^{\lambda}(\Theta)\subset R^{\lambda}(\Theta).

By
[
11
, Proposition 4.4(i)]
, each component of the complex
R^{\lambda}(\Theta)/\varpi^{*}F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)
is a direct sum of copies of
\mathscr{O}_{E/\Xi}(-1)
. Hence the adjunction
F_{X}^{\lambda}(\Theta)\to\mathrm{R}\varpi_{*}R^{\lambda}(\Theta)
is a quasi-isomorphism. On the other hand, there are increasing complexes
R^{\lambda}_{q}(\Theta)
on
\widetilde{X}
(those denoted by
R_{\lambda}(q)
in
[
11
, equation (27)]
, which is a complex under either
\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f
or
\mathrm{d}f
) with
R^{\lambda}_{-1}(\Theta)=F^{\lambda}_{\widetilde{X}}(\Theta)
and
R^{\lambda}_{\dim\widetilde{X}-p}(\Theta)=R^{\lambda}(\Theta)
such that
R^{\lambda}_{q}(\Theta)/R^{\lambda}_{q-1}(\Theta)

is quasi-isomorphic to the complex consisting of a direct sum of copies of
\mathscr{O}_{E/\Xi}(-1)
concentrated at degree
(p+q)
. In fact, in
[
11
, Lemma 4.5]
, we further introduce the complexes
(K_{\rho,\eta,\xi}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)
and prove that the inclusion
(K_{\rho,\eta,\xi}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)\subset(K_{\rho,\eta+1,\xi}^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)
is a quasi-isomorphism by showing the quotient complex is exact. Now
one notices that
K_{\rho,\eta,\xi}^{\bullet}
is indeed also stable under
\mathrm{d}f
; on the quotient
K_{\rho,\eta+1,\xi}^{\bullet}/K_{\rho,\eta,\xi}^{\bullet}
, one has the equality
\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f=\mathrm{d}f
of the differential maps
under the condition
e_{1}>1
. Therefore the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 4.4(ii)]
describing
R^{\lambda}_{q}(\Theta)/R^{\lambda}_{q-1}(\Theta)
goes through in both cases
\Theta=\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f
and
\Theta=\mathrm{d}f
. Hence one concludes that the inclusion
F_{\widetilde{X}}^{\lambda}(\Theta)\subset R^{\lambda}(\Theta)
induces a quasi-isomorphism
\mathrm{R}\varpi_{*}F_{\widetilde{X}}^{\lambda}(\Theta)\to\mathrm{R}\varpi_{*}R^{\lambda}(\Theta)
under the assumption
e_{1}>1
.

The following arguments work for any
e_{1}\geq 1
and simplify those in
[
11
, Section 4(
b
)]
. (Cf.,
[
3
, Lemma 2.12(b)]
.) By Proposition
2.1
, it suffices to show that for any
0\leq\alpha<1
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the inclusion relation
\varpi^{*}\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)\subset\Omega_{\tilde{f}}^{p}(\alpha)
on
\widetilde{X}
and it induces an isomorphism
\Omega_{f}^{p}(\alpha)\to\mathrm{R}\varpi_{*}\Omega_{\tilde{f}}^{p}(\alpha)
on
X
.

We first compute
\Omega_{\tilde{f}}^{p}(\alpha)
. Explicitly the blowup
\widetilde{X}
is defined by the equation

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&z_{1}\\
u&v\end{pmatrix}=0

in
X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
[u:v]
is the homogeneous coordinate on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. On the chart
v\neq 0
with local coordinates

\displaystyle\left\{\bar{u}=\frac{u}{v},x_{2},\dots,x_{l},y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{r}\right\},

the
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
-module
\Omega_{\tilde{f}}^{p}(\alpha)
is generated by the basis

\displaystyle\delta\frac{\mathrm{d}\tilde{f}}{\tilde{f}}\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{p-1}\xi_{i},\qquad\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\delta=z_{1}^{-\lfloor\alpha(e_{1}-1)\rfloor}\bar{u}^{-\lfloor\alpha e_{1}\rfloor}x_{2}^{-\lfloor\alpha e_{2}\rfloor}\cdots x_{l}^{-\lfloor\alpha e_{l}\rfloor},\\

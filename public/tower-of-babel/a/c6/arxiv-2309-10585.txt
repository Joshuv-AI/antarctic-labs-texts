Transfer of derived equivalences from subalgebras to endomorphism algebras II


Abstract
We investigate derived equivalences between subalgebras of some Φ \Phi -Auslander-Yoneda algebras from a class of n n -angles in weakly n n -angulated categories. The derived equivalences are obtained by transferring subalgebras induced by n n -angles to endomorphism algebras induced by approximation sequences. Then we extend our constructions [ 4 ] to n n -angle cases. Finally, we give an explicit example to illustrate our result.

1
Introduction

In the representation theory of algebras, derived equivalences have been
shown to preserve many homological invariants and provide new bridges between algebras and geometry. Rickard’s Morita theory for
derived categories states that two rings are derived equivalent if and only if there is
a tilting complex over one ring with endomorphism ring isomorphic to the other ring
[
19
,
20
]
.
However, in general, it is very difficult to describe the derived equivalence class of a given ring.
One idea is to study derived equivalent rings can be related by a sequence of such elementary derived equivalences. Many such kind of derived equivalent rings comes from mutations sequences of objects in categories, where approximations play a central role. This occurs in many aspects in algebra and geometry, such as mutations of tilting modules
[
9
]
, mutations of cluster tilting objects
[
5
]
, mutations of silting objects
[
1
]
, mutations of exceptional sequences
[
22
]
.

It is interesting to know whether the mutation sequences always give derived equivalent endomorphism rings.
In
[
12
]
, Hu and Xi got
derived equivalences by
\mathcal{D}
-split sequences via tilting modules. Indeed, they used some special approximation sequences to get
derived equivalences. In
[
13
]
, Hu and Xi constructed derived equivalences between
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebras
from given
\nu
-stable derived equivalences. Furthermore, Hu, Koenig and Xi
[
11
]
proved that certain
triangles with symmetric approximations and some conditions give rise to derived equivalences of quotient algebras of endomorphism algebras of objects in the sequences modulo some particularly defined ideals.
After that Chen
[
6
]
generalized their result to
n
-angles in
n
-angulated categories.
Chen and Hu
[
8
]
introduced
symmetric approximation sequences in additive categories and weakly
n
-angulated categories which include (higher) Auslander-Reiten sequences (triangles) and showed that such sequences always give rise to derived equivalences between the quotient rings of endomorphism rings of objects in the sequences modulo some ghost and coghost ideals. Recently, Pan
[
18
]
proved that symmetric approximation sequences in
n
-exangulated categories give rise to derived equivalences between quotient algebras of locally
\Phi
-Beilinson-Green algebras in the principal diagonals modulo some factorizable ghost and coghost ideals by the locally finite tilting family.

A natural question arises, how about derived equivalences between subalgebras?

In
[
7
]
, Chen constructed derived equivalences
between some subalgebras related to
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebras induced by exact sequences in abelian categories.
Thomas and Pan investigate derived equivalences between subalgebras of some
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebras from a class of triangles
in triangulated categories. The derived equivalences are obtained by transferring subalgebras induced by triangles to endomorphism algebras induced by approximation sequences

We extend in this paper Thomas-Pan’s main result
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
to a more general case and construct
derived equivalences between subalgebras of
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebras induced from
n
-angles in a weakly
n
-angulated category
(\mathcal{T},\Sigma)
(
n\geq 2
). In order to state our result precisely,
we first fix some notation. Let
\Phi
be an admissible set of
\mathbb{N}
and
X
an object in
\mathcal{T}
. The
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebra
of
X
is
{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X):=\bigoplus_{i\in\Phi}{\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,X[i])
. The matrix rings

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X)}&\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X,M)}\\
{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M,X)&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M)\end{pmatrix}

and

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(Y)}&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(Y,M)\\
\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M,Y)}&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M)\end{pmatrix}

are some subrings of
{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X\oplus M)
and
{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(Y\oplus M)
, respectively. For definitions of these rings, we refer the reader to Section 2.

Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\Sigma)
be a weakly
n
-angulated category (
n\geq 2
),
and let
M
be an object in
\mathcal{T}
. Let

X\xrightarrow{f}M_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}M_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}M_{n-2}\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma X

be an
n
–angle in
\mathcal{T}
with
M_{j}\in{\rm add\,}(M)
for all
j=1,\cdots,n-2
, assume that
{\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}(X))=0={\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y,\Sigma^{i}(M))
for
0\neq i\in\Phi
,
and
{\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma(X),Y)={\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma(X),M)={\rm Hom\,}_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma(M),Y)=0
.
Then the rings

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X)}&\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(X,M)}\\
{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M,X)&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M)\end{pmatrix}

and

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(Y)}&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(Y,M)\\
\widetilde{{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M,Y)}&{\rm E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}(M)\end{pmatrix}

are derived equivalent.

Remark 1.2
.

(1) By transferring subalgebras induced by
n
-angles to endomorphism algebras induced by approximation sequences,

Although the method is similar to that in

(2) We focus on derived equivalences between subalgebras which is different from derived equivalences between quotient algebras by Chen

(3) If

2.1
Conventions

We begin by briefly recalling some definitions and notations on
derived categories and derived equivalences.

Let
\mathscr{A}

\alpha\beta
, where

and

\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}
is a sequence of

in
\mathscr{A}
of the form:

\cdots\rightarrow X^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}X^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{i+1}}}{{\rightarrow}}X^{i+1}\rightarrow\cdots,

such that
d^{i}d^{i+1}=0
for all

. If
X^{\bullet}=(X^{i},d_{X}^{i})
and

Y^{\bullet}=(Y^{i},d_{Y}^{i})
are two complexes, then a morphism
f^{\bullet}:X^{\bullet}\rightarrow Y^{\bullet}
is a sequence of morphisms
f^{i}:X^{i}\rightarrow Y^{i}
of

\mathscr{A}
such that
d^{i}_{X}f^{i+1}=f^{i}d^{i}_{Y}
for all

i\in\mathbb{Z}
. The morphism
f^{\bullet}
is called a chain map between

X^{\bullet}
and
Y^{\bullet}
. The category of
complexes over
\mathscr{A}
with chain maps is denoted by

{\mathscr{C}}(\mathscr{A})
.
The homotopy category of complexes over
\mathscr{A}
is denoted by

{\mathscr{K}}(\mathscr{A})
. If
\mathscr{A}
is an abelian category, then we denote by
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{A})

the derived category of complexes over
\mathscr{A}
. It is
well known that, for an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
, the
categories
{\mathscr{K}}(\mathscr{A})
and
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{A})
are triangulated
categories. For basic results on triangulated categories, we refer
the reader to
[
17
]
.

Let
R
be a commutative artinian ring and let
A
be an Artin
R
-algebra.
Denote by
A
-Mod and
A
-mod the category of left
A
-modules and finitely generated left

A
-modules, respectively. The full subcategory of
A
-Mod and
A
-mod consisting of
projective modules is denoted by
A\mbox{{\rm-Proj}}
and
A\mbox{{\rm-proj}}
, respectively.
Let
{\mathscr{K}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
denote the
homotopy category of bounded complexes of
A
-modules and let

{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
denote the bounded derived category of A

, respectively.

The following theorem is a key ingredient of Morita theory on derived equivalences for module categories of
rings or algebras which was established by Rickard
[
19
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
19
, Theorem  6.4]

Let
A
and

be rings. The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
and
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
are equivalent
as triangulated categories.

(ii)

{\mathscr{K}}^{-}(A\mbox{{\rm-Proj}})
and
{\mathscr{K}}^{-}(B\mbox{{\rm-Proj}})
are equivalent as

(iii)

{\mathscr{K}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-Proj}})
and

are equivalent as

(iv)

{\mathscr{K}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-proj}})

are equivalent as

(v)

for some complex

in

satisfying

(a)

{\rm Hom\,}_{{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})}(T^{\bullet},T^{\bullet}[n])=0

for all

.

(b)

{\rm add\,}(T^{\bullet})
, the category of direct summands of
finite direct sums of copies of

as a triangulated category.

Remarks.
(1) The rings
A
and

are said to be derived equivalent if

and

satisfy the conditions of the above theorem.

(2) The complex
T^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{K}}^{b}(A\mbox{{\rm-proj}})

in Theorem
2.1
(v) which satisfies the conditions (a) and (b) is
called a
tilting complex
for

.

2.2
\Phi
-Auslander-Yoneda algebras

Let
\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{N}

Recall from

\mathbb{Z}

If

Recall that the
\Phi

13
]
as follows. Let

\Phi

.
For a triangulated

\mathcal{T}
with shift functor [1]

to
\mathcal{T}
. The
\Phi
-orbit category

is a category in which the

Steady-state thermodynamics for population dynamics in fluctuating environments with side information Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hideyuki Miyahara

Abstract
Steady-state thermodynamics (SST) is a relatively newly emerging subfield of physics, which deals with transitions between steady states. In this paper, we find an SST-like structure in population dynamics of organisms that can sense their fluctuating environments. As heat is divided into two parts in SST, we decompose population growth into two parts: housekeeping growth and excess growth. Then, we derive the Clausius equality and inequality for excess growth. Using numerical simulations, we demonstrate how the Clausius inequality behaves depending on the magnitude of noise and strategies that organisms employ. Finally, we discuss the novelty of our findings and compare them with a previous study.

Introduction

Thermodynamics is one of the most important subfields of physics and provides the foundation for other subfields in physics.
Furthermore, the last two decades have witnessed novel developments in thermodynamics: stochastic thermodynamics and steady-state thermodynamics (SST).
In stochastic thermodynamics, Jarzynski’s equality and Crooks’ theorem succeed in unifying the second law of thermodynamics and linear response theory
Jarzynski 1997a
;
Jarzynski 1997b
;
Crooks 1999
;
Crooks 1998

;
Sagawa and Ueda 2009
;
Sagawa and Ueda 2010
;
Sagawa 2017
;
Parrondo et al. 2015
;
Miyahara and Aihara 2018
.
On the other hand, in SST, which deals with transitions between non-equilibrium steady states, the Clausius equality and inequality for excess heat during transitions between non-equilibrium steady states were found
Oono and Paniconi 1998
;
Speck and Seifert 2005
;
Esposito and Van den
Broeck 2010
;
Chetrite and Gawȩdzki 2008
;
Sekimoto 1998
;
Sasa and Tasaki 2006
;
Komatsu et al. 2008
;
Komatsu and Nakagawa 2008
;
Sagawa and Hayakawa 2011
.

In theoretical biology, population dynamics, which involves the study of the dynamics of the number of individuals and organisms, is a particularly important topic.
In particular, population dynamics of organisms that switch their phenotypes stochastically has attracted much attention and has been investigated extensively because it is quite essential to understand how organisms adapt to and survive their fluctuating environments
Kussell and Leibler 2005
;
Leibler and Kussell 2010
;
Hermisson et al. 2002
;
Georgii and Baake 2003
;
Baake et al. 2005
;
Baake and Georgii 2007
;
Sughiyama et al. 2015
.
Recently, population dynamics and thermodynamics have been integrated, and interdisciplinary research between population dynamics and thermodynamics has witnessed some novel findings.
For example, FRs for population dynamics were found
Cover and Thomas 2012
;
Kobayashi and Sughiyama 2015
;
Kobayashi and Sughiyama 2017
;
Miyahara 2019
.
Furthermore, an SST-like structure was found for a biological system
Sughiyama and Kobayashi 2017
;
Levien et al. 2020
;
Kwon and Park 2021
.

In this paper, we construct an SST-like structure for population dynamics of organisms that can sense their environments.
As mentioned above, an SST-like structure for population dynamics was investigated in Ref.
Sughiyama and Kobayashi 2017
, but only a fixed transition matrix was considered.
Furthermore, this previous work assumed that organisms cannot sense their environment.
When we consider the adaption of organisms to their environments, the ability of sensing environments is critical and must be taken into account.
Then, we extend the work by introducing information on environments and derive new relations for population dynamics.

II
Prerequisites

In this section, we first define some variables that characterize population dynamics and some quantities that play a critical role in developing an SST-like structure for population dynamics.
We also define steady states and two types of population growth: housekeeping growth and excess growth.

II.1
System variables

We consider organisms with
n
phenotypes and assume that phenotype-switching and duplication occur at discrete time
\tau=1,2,\dots,t
.
To describe it mathematically, let us introduce three variables:
N_{\tau}(x)
,
y_{\tau}
, and
z_{\tau}
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
and
\tau=1,2,\dots,t
.
Here
N_{\tau}(x)
is the number of organisms of phenotype
x
at time
\tau
,
y_{\tau}
is the state of the environment at time
\tau
, and
z_{\tau}
represents information on
y_{\tau}
.
Furthermore, we assume that organisms do not know
y_{\tau}
but may utilize
z_{\tau}
as information on
y_{\tau}
.
In addition, we impose the following relations:
N_{\tau}(x)\geq 0
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
and
\tau=1,2,\dots,t
.
We also define
\vec{N}_{\tau}\coloneqq[N_{\tau}(1),N_{\tau}(2),\dots,N_{\tau}(n)]^{\intercal}
.

II.2
Dynamics and detailed balance condition

We now describe the system of interest that is composed of two terms: phenotype-switching and population growth.
We provide two mathematical expressions of the target system: the stochastic equation and its matrix form since they are both helpful.

II.2.1
Dynamics in the stochastic equation

In this paper, we consider the system governed by

\displaystyle N_{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=\mu_{y_{\tau}}(x)\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}T_{z_{\tau}}(x|x^{\prime})N_{\tau-1}(x^{\prime}),

(II.2.1)

where
T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})
represents the phenotype-switching rate from phenotype
x^{\prime}
to phenotype
x
in the case of
z
and
\mu_{y}(x)
is the duplication rate of organisms whose phenotype is
x
when the state of the environment is
y
.
Thus,
T_{z}(\cdot|\cdot)
satisfies
\sum_{x=1}^{n}T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})=1
.
Each organism changes its phenotype by
T_{z}(\cdot|\cdot)
in Eq. (
II.2.1
) typically to maximize its total population.

We assume that
T_{z}(\cdot|\cdot)
satisfies the detailed balance condition

\displaystyle T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})w_{z}^{T}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=T_{z}(x^{\prime}|x)w_{z}^{T}(x),

(II.2.2)

where
w_{z}^{T}(x)
is the stationary distribution induced by
T_{z}(\cdot|\cdot)
.
The detailed balance condition is originally an assumption widely used in thermodynamics; otherwise, we cannot define thermodynamic quantities of the system.
We also define

\displaystyle H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})\coloneqq\mu_{y}(x)T_{z}(x|x^{\prime}),

(II.2.3)

for
x,x^{\prime}=1,2,\dots,n
.

II.2.2
Dynamics in the matrix form

We provide a matrix expression of the dynamics defined in Sec.
II.2.1
.
That is, we rewrite Eq. (
II.2.1
) as

\displaystyle\vec{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\hat{M}_{y_{t}}\hat{T}_{z_{t}}\vec{N}_{t-1},

(II.2.4)

where
\hat{M}_{y}
and
\hat{T}_{z}
are the
n\times n
matrices given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{y}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}[\mu_{y}(1),\mu_{y}(2),\dots,\mu_{y}(n)],

(II.2.5)

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{z}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}T_{z}(1|1)&T_{z}(1|2)&\dots&T_{z}(1|n)\\
T_{z}(2|1)&T_{z}(2|2)&\dots&T_{z}(2|n)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
T_{z}(n|1)&T_{z}(n|2)&\dots&T_{z}(n|n)\end{bmatrix}.

(II.2.6)

From Eq. (
II.2.4
), we also have

\displaystyle\vec{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{M}_{y_{\tau}}\hat{T}_{z_{\tau}}\bigg]\vec{N}_{0}.

(II.2.7)

Similarly, the matrix representation of Eq. (
II.2.3
) is also introduced as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{y,z}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\hat{M}_{y}\hat{T}_{z}.

(II.2.8)

Note that
[\hat{H}_{y,z}]_{x,x^{\prime}}=H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})
where
[\cdot]_{x,x^{\prime}}
is an element in the
x
-th row and the
x^{\prime}
-th column of the matrix.

II.3
Population growth

Population growth is of great importance in population dynamics; we here introduce it by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\ln\frac{N_{t}}{N_{0}},

(II.3.1)

where
N_{\tau}\coloneqq\sum_{x=1}^{n}N_{\tau}(x)
is the total number of organisms at time
\tau
.
We also define
\phi_{\tau}\coloneqq\ln\frac{N_{\tau}}{N_{\tau-1}}
; so we have
\Phi_{0\to t}=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\phi_{\tau}
.

II.4
Steady states

We introduce steady states, which play a critical role in SST.
When
y_{\tau}
and
z_{\tau}
are fixed or vary sufficiently slowly,
\phi_{\tau}
reaches a steady state labeled by
y_{\tau}
and
z_{\tau}
.
The above situation corresponds to a quasi-static process in thermodynamics, and thus it is of great importance.

Let us denote the normalized steady state by
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq[v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(1),v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(2),\dots,v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(n)]^{\intercal}
.
In the case of
y_{\tau}=y
and
z_{\tau}=z
,
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}
satisfies

\displaystyle v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}\mu_{y}(x)\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime}),

(II.4.1)

where
\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}
is
\phi_{\tau}
in the case of the steady state labeled by
y
and
z
.
Note that
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}
is normalized such that
\|\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\|_{1}=1
, while
\vec{N}_{\tau}
is not; thus, at a steady state, we have
\vec{v}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}=\vec{N}_{\tau}/N_{\tau}
.
Mathematically, steady-state population growth per time step is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}

\displaystyle=\ln\sum_{x,x^{\prime}=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\mu_{y}(x)T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime}),

(II.4.2)

where
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq\big[u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(1),u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(2),\dots,u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(n)\big]
is the dual eigenvector of
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}
that satisfies
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)=1
.

II.5
Eigenstates

We define the left eigenvectors
\{\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\}_{x=1}^{n}
and right eigenvectors
\{\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\}_{x=1}^{n}
of
\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}
.
Then,
\{\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\}_{x=1}^{n}
and
\{\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\}_{x=1}^{n}
of
\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}
satisfy, respectively,

\displaystyle\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}

\displaystyle=\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x),

(II.5.1)

\displaystyle\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x),

(II.5.2)

where the elements of
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)
and
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)
are nonnegative,
\|\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\|_{1}=1
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
, and
\{\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\}_{x=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}
that satisfy
\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(1)\geq\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(2)\geq\dots\geq\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(n-1)\geq\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(n)
.
Furthermore, we impose

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{n}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)

\displaystyle=\hat{1}_{n},

(II.5.3)

for the normalization of
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
.
Here
\hat{1}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.
By definition, we have
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}=\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(1)
,
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}=\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(1)
, and
\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}=\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{eig}}(1)
.

II.6
Housekeeping growth and excess growth

In SST, heat is divided into two parts, that is, housekeeping and excess parts.
Similarly, we divide population growth into two parts:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{hk}}+\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}},

(II.6.1)

where housekeeping growth and excess growth are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{hk}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}},

(II.6.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Phi_{0\to t}-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{hk}}.

(II.6.3)

Thus, the main purpose of this paper is to consider the Clausius equality and inequality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
).

II.7
Forward and backward path probabilities

Path probabilities are useful for the derivation of the main results of this paper.
First we define the forward path probability as follows:

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{f}}(X_{0\to t}|Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}T_{z_{\tau}}(x_{\tau}|x_{\tau-1})\bigg]p_{\mathrm{ini}}(x_{0}),

(II.7.1)

where
X_{0\to t}\coloneqq\{x_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0}^{t}
and
Z_{0\to t}\coloneqq\{z_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0}^{t}
.
We also define the backward path probability as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\Big(\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\mu_{y_{\tau}}(x_{\tau})\Big)p_{\mathrm{f}}(X_{0\to t}|Z_{0\to t})}{\Big\langle\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\mu_{y_{\tau}}(x_{\tau})\Big\rangle_{p_{\mathrm{f}}(X_{0\to t}|Z_{0\to t})}},

(II.7.2)

where
Y_{0\to t}\coloneqq\{y_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0}^{t}
and
\langle f(x)\rangle_{g(x)}\coloneqq\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)g(x)
.
The backward path probability, Eq. (
II.7.2
), is also expressed as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle=\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}-\phi(\tau)\hat{1}_{n}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0}),

(II.7.3)

where

\displaystyle\vec{u}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{x-1},1,\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-x}],

(II.7.4)

\displaystyle\vec{v}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{x-1},1,\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-x}]^{\intercal}.

(II.7.5)

Our definitions of the forward and backward path probabilities are the same as those defined in Ref.
Kobayashi and Sughiyama 2015
;
Nozoe et al. 2017
;
Genthon and Lacoste 2020
.
The forward path probability is the probability of observing an organism whose history is
X_{0\to t}
when we randomly pick out an organism at time
0
and track its phenotype-switching in a time-forward manner.
When the tracked organism duplicates, we can randomly choose one of the daughters since the forward path probability does not depend on the choice.
On the other hand, the backward path probability is the probability of observing an organism of which history is
X_{0\to t}
when we randomly choose an organism at time
t
and track it back in a time-backward manner.
We utilize Eq. (
II.7.2
) to characterize the necessary condition for the second main result of this paper.

III
Main results

This section is the main part of this paper, in which we state the main claims.
The first one is the Clausius equality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
), and the second one is the Clausius inequality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
).

III.1
First main result

We state the first main result of this paper: the Clausius equality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
).
Let us consider a quasi-static process with Eq. (
II.2.1
); that is, we focus on the process in which the initial state is given by
\vec{N}_{0}=N_{0}\vec{v}_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}
and
\Delta y_{\tau}\coloneqq y_{\tau}-y_{\tau-1}
and
\Delta z_{\tau}\coloneqq z_{\tau}-z_{\tau-1}
are sufficiently small (
|\Delta y|,|\Delta z|\ll 1
) such that
\vec{N}_{\tau}/N_{\tau}=\vec{v}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}
for
\tau=0,1,2,\dots,t
.

If
w_{z}^{T}(x)
in Eq. (
II.2.2
) is independent of
z
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}w_{z}^{T}(x)

\displaystyle=0,

(III.1.1)

where
\nabla_{z}
is the derivative with respect to
z
, we obtain the Clausius equality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
):

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=S(y_{t},z_{t})-S(y_{0},z_{0}),

(III.1.2)

where the pseudo-entropy
S(y,z)
is given by
1
1

1

The pseudo-entropy (
III.1.3
) is not the entropy, but in our framework, it plays an important role similar to the entropy in thermodynamics; so we call it the pseudo-entropy.

\displaystyle S(y,z)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\ln\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)w_{z}^{T}(x).

(III.1.3)

Here, we provide an example in which Eq. (
III.1.1
) is satisfied.
Let us consider a system of two phenotypes.
If we set

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{z}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1-\omega_{z}&\omega_{z}\\
\omega_{z}&1-\omega_{z}\end{bmatrix},

(III.1.4)

where
[\hat{T}_{z}]_{x,x^{\prime}}\coloneqq T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})
, then the stationary distribution is given by
[w_{z}^{T}(1),w_{z}^{T}(2)]=[1/2,1/2]
for any
\omega_{z}
; thus, Eq. (
III.1.1
) is satisfied.
Equation (
III.1.4
) can be easily generalized to the case of
n
phenotypes.

We have assumed the quasi-static process for the above theorem.
Otherwise, this theorem does not hold since the eigenstate associated with the largest eigenvalue is not attainable.
The following theorem, however, holds even when the process is not quasi-static.

III.2
Second main result

Then we describe the second main result of this paper, that is, the Clausius inequality for excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
).
Let us assume that the initial state is given by
\vec{N}_{0}=N_{0}\vec{v}_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}
and
w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})
satisfies

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(III.2.1)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{b},\tau\to\tau^{\prime}}
is the expected value with respect to the backward path probability, that is,
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{b},\tau\to\tau^{\prime}}\coloneqq\langle\cdot\rangle_{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{\tau\to\tau^{\prime}}|\vec{Y}_{\tau+1\to\tau^{\prime}},\vec{Z}_{\tau+1\to\tau^{\prime}})}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle\leq S(y_{t},z_{t})-S(y_{0},z_{0}).

(III.2.2)

Note that, when Eq. (
III.1.1
) is satisfied, Eq. (
III.2.1
) is also satisfied.

IV
Derivation of the first main result

This section is devoted to the derivation of the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
).
We first give another expression of excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
), that is, the Berry-curvature-like expression, and then the detailed derivation of the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
), by using the Berry-curvature-like expression of excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
).

IV.1
Berry-curvature-like expression of excess growth

Before getting the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
), we show the Berry-curvature-like expression of
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
in a quasi-static process:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\bigg]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\bigg]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau},

(IV.1.1)

where
\nabla_{y}
is the derivative with respect to
y
.
The proof of Eq. (
IV.1.1
) is shown as follows.

Proof.

By using the path integral formulation, population growth, Eq. (
II.6.1
), can be represented as

\displaystyle e^{\Phi_{0\to t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\vec{v}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{\tau})\}_{x_{\tau},\tau},\\
\{\vec{u}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{\tau})\}_{x_{\tau},\tau}\end{subarray}}\vec{1}_{n}\vec{v}_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{t})\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}_{y_{\tau-1},z_{\tau-1}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]\vec{u}_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x_{0})\vec{N}_{0},

(IV.1.2)

where
\vec{1}_{n}=[\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{n}]
.
Note that
\vec{1}_{n}\vec{v}_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{eig}}(x)=1
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
.

In a quasi-static process, Eq. (
IV.1.2
) is dominated by the eigenstate that has the largest eigenvalue; thus, when initial state is given by
\vec{v}_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\Phi_{0\to t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{0}}\vec{1}_{n}\vec{v}_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}_{y_{\tau-1},z_{\tau-1}}^{\mathrm{st}}\bigg]\vec{u}_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}\vec{N}_{0}

(IV.1.3)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}_{y_{\tau-1},z_{\tau-1}}^{\mathrm{st}}.

(IV.1.4)

Due to the fact that, for
x=1,2,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)v_{y^{\prime},z^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta y}e^{-u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta z},

(IV.1.5)

each term in Eq. (
IV.1.4
) is computed as

\displaystyle\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}}\vec{v}_{y^{\prime},z^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{st}}

\displaystyle=e^{\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}e^{\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)v_{y^{\prime},z^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}

(IV.1.6)

\displaystyle=e^{\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}e^{-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta y}e^{-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta z}.

(IV.1.7)

Then, Eq. (
IV.1.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle e^{\Phi_{0\to t}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}e^{\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}}e^{-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}}e^{-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}}.

(IV.1.8)

By taking the logarithm of both sides of Eq. (
IV.1.8
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}.

(IV.1.9)

Since
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{hk}}=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}
, we have
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
, Eq. (
IV.1.1
).

∎

IV.2
Proof of the Clausius equality

In this subsection, we provide the proof of the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
).
The proof is given as follows.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mu_{y}(x)T_{z}(x^{\prime}|x).

(IV.2.1)

Eq. (
II.2.2
) leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}\tilde{H}_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{w_{z}^{T}(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}\mu_{y}(x)T_{z}(x^{\prime}|x)\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{w_{z}^{T}(x^{\prime})}

(IV.2.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}\mu_{y}(x)T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{w_{z}^{T}(x)}

(IV.2.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{\prime}=1}^{n}H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{w_{z}^{T}(x)}

(IV.2.4)

\displaystyle=e^{\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{w_{z}^{T}(x)}.

(IV.2.5)

Due to the assumption that
T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})
is ergodic and
H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})
is irreducible, and the Perron-Frobenius theorem, the largest left eigenvector is unique.
Thus, there exists
C_{y,z}
such that, for
x=1,2,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{y,z}\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{w_{z}^{T}(x)}.

(IV.2.6)

By using the fact that
\|\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\|_{1}=1
, we have

\displaystyle C_{y,z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}w_{z}^{T}(x)u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x).

(IV.2.7)

Furthermore, since
\vec{u}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{y,z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}w_{z}^{T}(x)[u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)]^{2}.

(IV.2.8)

By taking the derivative of Eq. (
IV.2.8
) with respect to
y
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{y}\ln C_{y,z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{w_{z}^{T}(x)u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{C_{y,z}}\nabla_{y}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

(IV.2.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

(IV.2.10)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x),

(IV.2.11)

where we have used Eq. (
IV.2.6
).
Next, by taking the derivation of Eq. (
IV.2.8
) with respect to
z
, we have almost the same expression:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{z}\ln C_{y,z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{w_{z}^{T}(x)u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{C_{y,z}}\nabla_{z}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{(u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x))^{2}}{C_{y,z}}\nabla_{z}w_{z}^{T}(x)

(IV.2.12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{(u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x))^{2}}{C_{y,z}}\nabla_{z}w_{z}^{T}(x)

(IV.2.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)

(IV.2.14)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z}v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x).

(IV.2.15)

In Eq. (
IV.2.14
), the second term vanishes due to the assumption in Eq. (
III.1.1
).
Thus, Eq. (
IV.1.1
) can be computed further as

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{y_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\bigg]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\sum_{x=1}^{n}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\nabla_{z_{\tau}}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)\bigg]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(IV.2.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln C_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}\bigg]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln C_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}\bigg]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(IV.2.17)

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\ln C_{y,z}\bigg]_{y=y_{0},z=z_{0}}^{y=y_{t},z=z_{t}}

(IV.2.18)

\displaystyle=\big[S_{y,z}\big]_{y=y_{0},z=z_{0}}^{y=y_{t},z=z_{t}}

(IV.2.19)

\displaystyle=S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}},

(IV.2.20)

where
S_{y,z}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\ln C_{y,z}
.
Therefore we have obtained the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
).

∎

V
Derivation of the second main result

This section shows the derivation of the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).
We first define two different path probabilities and a symmetrized divergence.
Then, we show the derivation of the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).

V.1
Path probabilities and symmetrized divergence

In this subsection, we introduce the two different path probabilities and the symmetrized divergence on the path probabilities.
These quantities are essential to prove the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).

V.1.1
Retrospective process and its dual process

Here, we introduce a Markov process that mimics the backward path probability for a fixed environment, called the retrospective process.
The backward path probability, Eq. (
II.7.2
), is also expressed as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle=e^{-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{\tau}}\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]v_{y_{0},z_{0}}(x_{0})

(V.1.1)

\displaystyle=\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}-\phi_{\tau}\hat{1}_{n}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]v_{y_{0},z_{0}}(x_{0}).

(V.1.2)

Note that we have used
p_{\mathrm{ini}}(x_{0})=v_{y_{0},z_{0}}(x_{0})
.
In a quasi-static process or the case where the changes of
y_{\tau}
and
z_{\tau}
are sufficiently small, we have

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle\approx p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t}),

(V.1.3)

where

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{Q}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]w_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{Q}(x_{0}),

(V.1.4)

and

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{y,z}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}Q_{y,z}(1|1)&Q_{y,z}(1|2)&\dots&Q_{y,z}(1|n)\\
Q_{y,z}(2|1)&Q_{y,z}(2|2)&\dots&Q_{y,z}(2|n)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{y,z}(n|1)&Q_{y,z}(n|2)&\dots&Q_{y,z}(3|n)\\
\end{bmatrix},

(V.1.5)

\displaystyle Q_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}.

(V.1.6)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{n}Q_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}H_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}

(V.1.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}e^{\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}

(V.1.8)

\displaystyle=1;

(V.1.9)

thus,
Q_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})
can be interpreted as a conditional probability.

We then consider the Markov process induced by

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}H_{y,z}(x^{\prime}|x)e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}.

(V.1.10)

Note that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{n}\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}H_{y,z}(x^{\prime}|x)e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}

(V.1.11)

\displaystyle=1.

(V.1.12)

From Eqs. (
V.1.6
) and (
V.1.10
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(x|x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}H_{y,z}(x^{\prime}|x)e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}

(V.1.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{y,z}^{Q}(x)}{w_{y,z}^{Q}(x^{\prime})}\bigg(\frac{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x^{\prime})}{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}H_{y,z}(x^{\prime}|x)e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}\bigg)

(V.1.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{y,z}^{Q}(x)}{w_{y,z}^{Q}(x^{\prime})}Q_{y,z}(x^{\prime}|x),

(V.1.15)

where

\displaystyle w_{y,z}^{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x).

(V.1.16)

We also note that
w_{y,z}^{Q}(x)
satisfies the condition of probability distribution:
\sum_{x=1}^{n}w_{y,z}^{Q}(x)=1
.

The process induced by Eq. (
V.1.10
), which we call the dual retrospective process, is given by

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau-1})e^{\ln\hat{\tilde{Q}}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau})\bigg]w_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{Q}(x_{t}),}

(V.1.17)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{Q}}_{y,z}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(1|1)&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(1|2)&\dots&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(1|n)\\
\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(2|1)&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(2|2)&\dots&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(2|n)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(n|1)&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(n|2)&\dots&\tilde{Q}_{y,z}(n|n)\\
\end{bmatrix},

(V.1.18)

and
X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}
,
Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}
, and
Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}
are the reversed processes of
X_{0\to t}
,
Y_{0\to t}
, and
Z_{0\to t}
, respectively.

V.1.2
Symmetrized divergence

We define the symmetrized divergence on the path probabilities by computing the logarithm of the ratios between path probabilities.
In the following subsection, we utilize it to prove the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).

From Eq. (
V.1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\vec{u}(x)e^{\ln\hat{Q}_{y,z}}\vec{v}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}\frac{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}{u_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau-1})}\vec{u}(x)e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}}\vec{v}(x^{\prime}).

(V.1.19)

Then, Eq. (
V.1.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle=\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{Q}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]v_{y_{0},z_{0}}(x_{0})

(V.1.20)

\displaystyle=e^{-\sum_{\tau}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}}\frac{\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}{\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau-1})}\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]w_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{Q}(x_{0}),

(V.1.21)

and

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}\bigg)\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}\bigg)\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\bigg(\ln\frac{u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})}{u_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}+\ln\frac{w_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{Q}(x_{0})}{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}\bigg)e^{\Phi_{0\to t}}p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t}).

(V.1.22)

Thus, the logarithm of the ratio between
p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})
and
p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})
is computed as

\displaystyle\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln\frac{u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})}{u_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}-\ln\frac{w_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{Q}(x_{0})}{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(V.1.23)

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau},

(V.1.24)

where we have used Eqs. (
II.6.2
) and (
II.6.3
).

Next, we consider the dual retrospective process.
From Eq. (
V.1.18
), we have

\displaystyle\vec{u}(x^{\prime})e^{\ln\hat{\tilde{Q}}_{y,z}}\vec{v}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}}\frac{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau-1})}{v_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)}\vec{u}(x)e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y,z}}\vec{v}(x^{\prime}).

(V.1.25)

Then, Eq. (
V.1.17
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})

\displaystyle=e^{-\sum_{\tau}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}}\frac{\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau-1})}{\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}\bigg[\prod_{\tau=1}^{t}\vec{u}(x_{\tau})e^{\ln\hat{H}_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}\vec{v}(x_{\tau-1})\bigg]w_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{Q}(x_{t}),

(V.1.26)

and then we have

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\phi_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}\bigg)\exp\bigg(\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}\bigg)\exp\bigg(\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\bigg(\ln\frac{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}{v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})}+\ln\frac{w_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{Q}(x_{t})}{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}\bigg)e^{\Phi_{0\to t}}p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t}).

(V.1.27)

Thus, the logarithm of the ratio between
p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})
and
p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})
is computed as

\displaystyle\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})}

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln\frac{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}{v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})}-\ln\frac{w_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{Q}(x_{t})}{v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(V.1.28)

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\big[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})\big]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}.

(V.1.29)

By using Eqs. (
V.1.24
) and (
V.1.29
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})}

\displaystyle\quad=\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{\big[p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(V.1.30)

\displaystyle\quad=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln\frac{u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}{v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}\bigg]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln\frac{u_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}{v_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{\tau})}\bigg]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(V.1.31)

\displaystyle\quad=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\nabla_{y_{\tau}}\ln\frac{C_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}{w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})}\bigg]\cdot\Delta y_{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\bigg[\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln\frac{C_{y_{\tau},z_{\tau}}}{w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})}\bigg]\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}

(V.1.32)

\displaystyle\quad=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\big[\ln C_{y_{t},z_{t}}-\ln C_{y_{0},z_{0}}\big]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}.

(V.1.33)

Then, we define the symmetrized divergence as

\displaystyle D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg\langle\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{\big[p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t},

(V.1.34)

and it is computed as

\displaystyle D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}X_{0\to t}\,p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{\big[p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(V.1.35)

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}+S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-\int\mathcal{D}X_{0\to t}\,p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})

(V.1.36)

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}+S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-\sum_{x_{t}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t},

(V.1.37)

where
\int\mathcal{D}X_{0\to t}\coloneqq\sum_{\{X_{0\to t}\}}
.
Note that we have used
v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})=\sum_{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{t-1}}p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})
and
v_{y_{0},z_{0}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{0})=\sum_{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}}p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})
since we are studying a transition between stationary states. Note that the non-negativity of the symmetrized divergence is shown in Appendix
A
.

V.2
Proof of the Clausius inequality

We define

\displaystyle\sigma_{0\to t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}+S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}.

(V.2.1)

Note that
\sigma_{0\to t}
is not thermodynamically the entropy production, but we call it the entropy production since its mathematical properties are similar to the thermodynamic entropy production.
In the rest of this section, we prove the following inequality:

\displaystyle\sigma_{0\to t}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(V.2.2)

Eq. (
V.2.2
) leads to the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).

Proof.

For any given process from
\tau=0
to
\tau=t
, we can define a new process from
\tau=0
to
\tau=t^{\prime}
such that it is identical to the given process from
\tau=0
to
\tau=t
and the duplication rate of the new process is constant (
\mu_{y_{\tau}}(x)=\mathrm{const.}
) for
t\leq\tau\leq t^{\prime}
.
In this proof, we use the new process.
Note that we can perform this technique for any process; so, this discussion is not a limitation.
To describe the new process, we also define
Y_{0\to t^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{y_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0}^{t^{\prime}}
,
Y_{t+1\to t^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{y_{\tau}\}_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}
,
Z_{0\to t^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{z_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0}^{t^{\prime}}
, and
Z_{t\to t^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{z_{\tau}\}_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}
.

From Eq. (
V.1.37
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_{x_{t}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t}.

(V.2.3)

Due to the assumption
\mu_{y_{t^{\prime}}}(x)=\mathrm{const.}
, we have
u_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x)=1
for
x=1,2,\dots,n
.
Then, we get
S_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}=0
and
\sum_{x_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})\ln u_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})=0

Sughiyama and Kobayashi 2017
; thus we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=S_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-D_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_{i_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})\ln u_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t^{\prime}}

(V.2.4)

\displaystyle=-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-D_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t^{\prime}}

(V.2.5)

\displaystyle\leq-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t^{\prime}},

(V.2.6)

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}

\displaystyle=S_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}-S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-D_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\sum_{i_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})\ln u_{y_{t^{\prime}},z_{t^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t^{\prime}})-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},t\to t^{\prime}}

(V.2.7)

\displaystyle=-S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-D_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sym}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},t\to t^{\prime}}

(V.2.8)

\displaystyle\leq-S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},t\to t^{\prime}}.

(V.2.9)

Next, let us denote
Y_{0\to t}
,
Y_{t+1\to t^{\prime}}
, and
Y_{1\to t^{\prime}}
that maximize
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
,
\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}
, and
\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}
, by
Y_{0\to t}^{*}
,
Y_{t+1\to t^{\prime}}^{*}
, and
Y_{1\to t^{\prime}}^{*}
, respectively.
We also denote the maximized values of
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
,
\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}
,
\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex}}
, and
D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}
by
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex},*}
,
\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*}
,
\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*}
, and
D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym},*}
, respectively.
In this case, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex},*}+\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*},

(V.2.10)

and, due to the Clausius equality, Eq. (
III.1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{t\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*}

\displaystyle=-S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=t+1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},t\to t^{\prime}},

(V.2.11)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ex},*}

\displaystyle=-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t^{\prime}}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t^{\prime}}.

(V.2.12)

Thus, Eq. (
V.2.10
) leads to

\displaystyle S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}-D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym},*}-\sum_{x_{t}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})-S_{y_{t},z_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}.

(V.2.13)

Then,

\displaystyle D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym},*}+\sum_{x_{t}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\geq 0.

(V.2.14)

By definition,
Y_{0\to t}^{*}
maximizes
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex}}
; thus, for any
Y_{0\to t}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle\geq D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym},*}.

(V.2.15)

By using Eqs. (
V.2.1
) and (
V.1.37
), we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{0\to t}

\displaystyle=D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}+\sum_{x_{t}=1}^{n}v_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})\ln u_{y_{t},z_{t}}^{\mathrm{st}}(x_{t})+\bigg\langle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\Big(\nabla_{z_{\tau}}\ln w_{z_{\tau}}^{T}(x_{\tau})\Big)\cdot\Delta z_{\tau}\bigg\rangle_{\mathrm{b},0\to t}.

(V.2.16)

Thus, Eqs. (
V.2.14
), (
V.2.15
), and (
III.2.1
) lead to Eq. (
V.2.2
).

VII
Discussions

This section discusses the findings of this paper.
We first explain the novelty of the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), by comparing it with a trivial bound.
Then we explain the gap between them by using perturbation theory on eigenvalues.
To clarify the novelty of our findings, we also mention the relationship between the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), and the Clausius inequality found in Ref.
Sughiyama and Kobayashi 2017
.
Finally, we explain that a similar relationship also holds for the expected values.

VII.1
Novelty of the Clausius inequality

In short, the novelty of the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), is that excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
), is bounded by the difference between the path-independent quantities, Eq. (
III.1.3
), which we call the pseudo-entropy, at the beginning and end of a given process.
We elaborate on the novelty of the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), by using Eq.(
VI.0.1
) because it provides us with a slightly different perspective.
Let us introduce
\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}\coloneqq\max_{x}\mu_{y}(x)
, which is the maximum duplication rate when
y
is given.
Since
\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}\leq\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}
,
we have a following trivial bound:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{max}}+S_{y_{t},z_{t}}-S_{y_{0},z_{0}}.

(VII.1.1)

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{max}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\mu_{y_{\tau}}^{\mathrm{max}}.

(VII.1.2)

If
\{y_{\tau}\}_{\tau=0,1,\dots,t}
are given, then Eq. (
VII.1.1
) becomes a good bound because organisms can always select the phenotype that has the largest duplication rate for given
y_{\tau}
.
However, in a practical setup, organisms cannot get
y_{\tau}
directly.
Thus, the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), provides us with a tighter bound than Eq. (
VII.1.1
) for a practical setup.

VII.2
Perturbation analysis of the Clausius equality and inequality

By using the perturbation theory on eigenvalues, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{y,z}^{\mathrm{st}}

\displaystyle\approx\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}+[\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}]^{\intercal}\hat{M}_{y}(\hat{T}_{z}-\hat{1}_{n})\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}

(VII.2.1)

\displaystyle=\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}+\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}[\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}]^{\intercal}(\hat{T}_{z}-\hat{1}_{n})\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}},

(VII.2.2)

where
\hat{1}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix and
\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}
satisfies
\hat{M}_{y}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}=\mu_{y}^{\mathrm{max}}\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}
and
\|\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}\|_{1}=\|\vec{v}_{y,z}^{\mathrm{max}}\|_{2}=1
.
Note that the above approximation is valid when
T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})\approx\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}
.
Here,
\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta function.

As we explained, the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
), is a bound when we adopt a
z
-dependent transition matrix
T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})
, and Eq. (
VII.1.1
) is a looser bound defined by the maximum duplication growth rate.
The gap between Eqs.(
VI.0.1
) and (
VII.1.1
) to the first-order perturbation is the summation of the second term of Eq. (
VII.2.2
) for
\tau=1,2,\dots,t
, and it clearly shows that, by considering the phenotype-switching rate described by
T_{z}(x|x^{\prime})
, we can obtain a tighter bound for the growth rate.

VII.3
Relation with the case without information

Until now, we have considered the case in which we can utilize information on environments.
Let us consider organisms that cannot sense their environments, that is, the case of a fixed transition matrix.
Then, the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex},\text{(w/o info)}}

\displaystyle\leq S_{y_{t},c}-S_{y_{0},c},

(VII.3.1)

where
\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{ex},\text{(w/o info)}}
is excess growth, Eq. (
II.6.3
), in the case of
C_{0\to t}\coloneqq\{c\}_{\tau=1}^{t}
and
c
is a constant that does not depend on
\tau
.
This result is equivalent with Ref.
Sughiyama and Kobayashi 2017
.

VII.4
Expectation of the Clausius inequality

The Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
) is the inequality for each realization of
{Y_{0\to t}}
and
{Z_{0\to t}}
; so its expected value is easily computed as

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{0\to t}\rangle_{p({Y_{0\to t}},{Z_{0\to t}})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\Phi_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{hk}}\rangle_{p({Y_{0\to t}},{Z_{0\to t}})}+\langle S_{y_{t},z_{t}}\rangle_{p(y_{t},z_{t})}-\langle S_{y_{0},z_{0}}\rangle_{p(y_{0},z_{0})}.

(VII.4.1)

Here
p(y_{\tau},z_{\tau})
is the distribution of
y_{\tau}
and
z_{\tau}
.
From the viewpoint of experimental verifications, Eq. (
VII.4.1
) may be more convenient than the Clausius inequality, Eq. (
III.2.2
).

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have constructed an SST structure with side information in population dynamics and derived novel relations, the Clausius equality and inequality.
First, we defined population growth of organisms and divided it into two parts: housekeeping growth and excess growth.
Then we showed that excess growth is bounded by the difference of path-independent quantities, which we call the pseudo-entropy, at the beginning and end.
We also performed numerical simulations to confirm our findings with several different setups.
Finally, we explained the relationship between our findings and previous ones and discussed their novelties.
To the best of our knowledge, this paper has found an SST structure with side information in interdisciplinary sciences for the first time.

Non-negativity of the symmetrized divergence

We show that
D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}
in Eq. (
V.1.34
) is non-negative.
The proof is as follows:

\displaystyle D_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}X_{0\to t}\,p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})}{\big[p_{\mathrm{r}}(X_{0\to t}|Y_{0\to t},Z_{0\to t})p_{\mathrm{dr}}(X_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}}|Y_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}},Z_{0\to t}^{\mathrm{r}})\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}}

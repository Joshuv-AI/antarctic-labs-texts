Integral representations for the Hartman–Watson density
Yuu Hariya

Abstract
This paper concerns the density of the Hartman--Watson law. Yor (1980) obtained an integral formula that gives a closed-form expression of the Hartman--Watson density. In this paper, based on Yor’s formula, we provide alternative integral representations for the density. As an immediate application, we recover in part a Dufresne’s result (2001) that exhibits remarkably simple representations for the laws of exponential additive functionals of Brownian motion. † † Mathematical Institute, Tohoku University, Aoba-ku, Sendai 980-8578, Japan † † E-mail: hariya@tohoku.ac.jp † † Key Words and Phrases : Brownian motion; exponential functional; Hartman–Watson law † † MSC 2010 Subject Classifications : Primary 60J65; Secondary 60J55, 60E10

Introduction

Let
B=\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion.
For every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by

B^{(\mu)}=\{B^{(\mu)}_{t}:=B_{t}+\mu t\}_{t\geq 0}
the Brownian motion
with constant drift
\mu
and set

\displaystyle A^{(\mu)}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{2B^{(\mu)}_{s}}ds,\quad t\geq 0;

when
\mu=0
, we simply write
A_{t}
for
A^{(\mu)}_{t}
.
This additive functional, together with the geometric Brownian motion

e^{B^{(\mu)}_{t}},\,t\geq 0
, plays an important role in a number of areas
such as option pricing in mathematical finance,
diffusion processes in random environments, probabilistic study of
Laplacians on hyperbolic spaces, and so on; see the detailed surveys

[
14
,
15
]
by Matsumoto–Yor and references therein.

In
[
19
]
, Yor proved that for every
t>0
, the joint law
of
B_{t}
and
A_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(B_{t}\in dx,\,A_{t}\in dv\right)=\frac{1}{v}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2v}\left(1+e^{2x}\right)\right\}\Theta(e^{x}/v,t)\,dxdv,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\,v>0,

(1.1)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(e^{B_{t}}\in du,\,A_{t}\in dv\right)=\frac{1}{uv}\exp\left(-\frac{1+u^{2}}{2v}\right)\Theta(u/v,t)\,dudv,\quad u,v>0,

(1.2)

where for every
r>0
, the function
\Theta(r,t),\,t>0
, is the
(unnormalized) density of the so-called Hartman–Watson distribution
(
[
7
]
) which is characterized by the Laplace transform

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t\right)\Theta(r,t)=I_{|\lambda|}(r),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

Here for every index
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
the function
I_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function
of the first kind of order
\nu
; see
[
10
, Section 5.7]

for the definition. By the Cameron–Martin relation, we see from
(
1.2
) that for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
, the law of

A^{(\mu)}_{t}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(A^{(\mu)}_{t}\in dv)}{dv}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2v}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}t\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u}\,u^{\mu}\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2v}\right)\Theta(u/v,t)

\displaystyle=v^{\mu-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2v}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}t\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{\mu-1}\exp\left(-\frac{v}{2}r^{2}\right)\Theta(r,t),\quad v>0,

(1.4)

where for the second line, we changed the variables with
u=vr,\,r>0
.

It is also proven by Yor
[
18
]
that the function
\Theta

admits the following integral representation: for every
r>0
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\Theta(r,t)=\frac{r}{\sqrt{2\pi^{3}t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}-y^{2}}{2t}\right)\exp\left(-r\cosh y\right)\sinh y\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{t}\right),

(1.5)

which may be rephrased conveniently as

\displaystyle\Theta(r,t)=\frac{r}{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{2t}\right)\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\left(-r\cosh B_{t}\right)\sinh B_{t}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{t}B_{t}\right)\right],

(1.6)

by the fact that the function
\mathbb{R}\ni y\mapsto\sinh y\sin(\pi y/t)
is
symmetric. We also refer to
[
3
, p. 82]
for (
1.5
),
and
[
3
, p. 175, formula 1.10.8]
for the joint law
(
1.1
).

The function
\Theta
as well as formula (
1.5
)
have continuously attracted researchers’ attention particularly from
the view point of evaluation of Asian options in the
Black–Scholes framework; see, e.g.,
[
16
,
4
,
2
,
11
]
and
references therein. Yor obtained (
1.5
) by inverting the
Laplace transform (
1.3
), reasoning of which is also
reproduced in
[
14
, Appendix A]
.
In
[
6
, Subsection A.3]
, we explain
(
1.5
) via

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r}\,e^{-r\cosh x}\Theta(r,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right),\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.7)

which relation is found in, e.g.,
[
12
, Proposition 4.5(i)]
and,
as was observed in
[
14
, Proposition 4.2]
, may be obtained
by integrating both sides of (
1.1
) with respect to

.

) (or (

)),

Theorem 1.1
.

For every

and

, it holds that

\displaystyle\Theta(r,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8t}\right)\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cosh B_{t}\cos(r\sinh B_{t})\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2t}B_{t}\right)\right]

(1.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8t}\right)\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cosh B_{t}\sin(r\sinh B_{t})\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2t}B_{t}\right)\right]

(1.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8t}\right)\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cosh B_{t}\cos\left(r\sinh B_{t}-\frac{\pi}{2t}B_{t}\right)\right].

(1.10)

More generally, we have for every
r>0
and
t>0

\displaystyle\Theta(r,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8t}\right)\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cosh B_{t}\cos(r\sinh B_{t}-\nu)\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2t}B_{t}-\nu\right)\right],

(1.11)

\nu\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary. Representations
(

), (

the case

Remark 1.2

Since the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi t}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{nB_{t}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{t}B_{t}\right)\right],

\displaystyle e^{-n^{2}t/2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{nB_{t}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{t}B_{t}\right)\right]

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

From now on, we fix
t>0
. This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let two real-valued functions
F
and
G
on
\mathbb{R}
be continuous
for simplicity and suppose that they are even functions and satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[|F(B_{t})|\right]<\infty

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[|G(B_{t})|\cosh B_{t}\right]<\infty.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

Under condition (
2.1
), the following three relations
are equivalent:

\displaystyle{\rm i}\

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{F(B_{t})\cosh B_{t}}{\cosh(2B_{t})+\cosh(2x)}\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)G(x)\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R};

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\rm ii}\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-r\cosh B_{t}}F(B_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[G(B_{t})\cosh B_{t}\cos(r\sinh B_{t})\right]\quad\text{for any }r\geq 0;

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\rm iii}\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{F(B_{t})}{\cosh B_{t}+\cosh x}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{G(B_{t})}{\cosh(x+B_{t})}\right]\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.4)

Since, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we only use the implication
from i to ii, we give a proof of it
below and postpone proofs of other implications to
the appendix.

Proof of
i

\Rightarrow

ii
in Lemma

.

For every
r\geq 0
, we integrate both sides of (
2.2
) multiplied by

\cosh x\cos(r\sinh x)
with respect to

.
Then the right-hand side turns into that of (
2.3
) in view of the
latter condition in (
2.1
). On the other hand, provided that we are
allowed to use Fubini’s theorem, the left-hand side turns into

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\mathbb{E}\!\left[F(B_{t})\cosh B_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\frac{\cosh x\cos(r\sinh x)}{\cosh(2B_{t})+\cosh(2x)}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-r\cosh B_{t}}F(B_{t})\right]

and

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\frac{\cosh x\cos(r\sinh x)}{\cosh(2b)+\cosh(2x)}=\frac{\pi}{2\cosh b}e^{-r\cosh b},

(2.5)

which follows readily by noting

\cosh(2b)+\cosh(2x)=2\left(\cosh^{2}b+\sinh^{2}x\right)
,
and changing the variables

. Usage of Fubini’s theorem
mentioned above is justified by taking

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\cosh x\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{|F(B_{t})|\cosh B_{t}}{\cosh(2B_{t})+\cosh(2x)}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[|F(B_{t})|\right],

(2.6)

which is assumed to be finite in (

∎

. A pair of functions

Proof of Theorem

First we prove (

.

Given

, we integrate both sides of (
2.3
) multiplied by

with respect to

. Then by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,e^{-r\cosh x}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[G(B_{t})\cosh B_{t}\cos(r\sinh B_{t})\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{G(B_{t})}{\cosh(x+B_{t})}\right].

(A.1)

By the latter condition in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[G(B_{t})\cosh B_{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,e^{-r\cosh x}\cos(r\sinh B_{t})\right]

(A.2)

On the other hand, by the symmetry of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[G(B_{t})\left\{\frac{1}{\cosh(x+B_{t})}+\frac{1}{\cosh(x-B_{t})}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[G(B_{t})\frac{\cosh x\cosh B_{t}}{\cosh(x+B_{t})\cosh(x-B_{t})}\right].

Noting the fact that

\begin{split}\cosh(x+y)\cosh(x-y)&=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\cosh(2x)+\cosh(2y)\right\}\\
&=\cosh^{2}x+\sinh^{2}y\end{split}

We turn to the proof of the implication from iii to i.
To this end, we prepare the following lemma:

Lemma A.1
.

For every
x,b\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dy}{\cosh(x+y)}\,\frac{1}{\cosh(2b)+\cosh(2y)}=\frac{\pi}{2\cosh b\left(\cosh b+\cosh x\right)}.

Proof.

We may assume
|x|\neq|b|
; validity in the case

|x|=|b|
is verified by passing to the limit.
By symmetrization and by the relation

\cosh(2b)+\cosh(2y)=2\left(\cosh^{2}b+\sinh^{2}y\right)
,
the left-hand side of the claimed identity is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\left\{\frac{1}{\cosh(x+y)}+\frac{1}{\cosh(x-y)}\right\}\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}b+\sinh^{2}y},

which is rewritten, due to relation (
A.3
), as

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh x}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\,\frac{\cosh y}{\left(\cosh^{2}x+\sinh^{2}y\right)\!\left(\cosh^{2}b+\sinh^{2}y\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh x}{2\left(\cosh^{2}b-\cosh^{2}x\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dz\left(\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}x+z^{2}}-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}b+z^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh x}{2\left(\cosh^{2}b-\cosh^{2}x\right)}\left(\frac{\pi}{\cosh x}-\frac{\pi}{\cosh b}\right),

where we changed the variables with
\sinh y=z
in the
second line.
Now the claimed identity follows.
∎

Abstract




William Kengne
and

Abstract
:
The explicit regularization and optimality of deep neural networks estimators from independent data have made considerable progress recently.
The study of such properties on dependent data is still a challenge.
In this paper, we carry out deep learning from strongly mixing observations, and deal with a broad class of loss functions.
We consider sparse-penalized regularization for deep neural network predictor.
For a general framework that includes, regression estimation, classification, time series prediction
\cdots
oracle inequalities for the expected excess risk are established, and upper bounds on the class of Hölder smooth functions and composition structured Hölder functions are provided.
For nonparametric autoregression with a Gaussian error and the Huber loss function, the upper bound of the
L_{2}
error established matches (up to a logarithmic factor) with the lower bound established in
[
24
]
which shows that, the sparse-penalized deep neural networks estimator proposed is optimal in the minimax sense.
For autoregression with the Laplace error and the Huber loss function, an upper and a lower bound of the
L_{2}
error on the class of composition Hölder functions are established.
These bounds match up to a logarithmic factor, that is, the proposed estimator attains the minimax optimal rate.
Based on the lower bound established in
[
2
]
, we show that the deep neural network estimator for the classification task with the logistic loss on strongly mixing observations is optimal in the minimax sense.

Keywords:
Deep neural networks, strong mixing, sparse-penalized regularization, minimax optimality, nonparametric regression, binary classification.

Our main contributions focus on the following issues:

1.

Oracle inequality and excess risk bounds of the SPDNN estimator
.
For a general framework, that includes regression and classification with a Lipschitz continuous loss function, oracle inequality for the expected excess risk of the SPDNN predictor is established.

•

Excess risk bounds on the class of Hölder functions
.
The expected excess risk bound, when the target function belongs to the class of Hölder smooth functions (defined in Subsection
4.2
, see (
4.3
)) with smoothness parameter
s
is derived.
Under the subexponential strong mixing condition, the convergence rate of this bound is of order
\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(n^{(\alpha)}\big)^{-\frac{\kappa s}{\kappa s+d}}\Big)\big(\log n^{(\alpha)}\big)^{3}
(where
n^{(\alpha)}
is given in (
4.2
)) for some constant
\kappa\geq 1
, and
d
is the dimension of the input space.
Moreover, when the data are exponentially strongly mixing, the convergence rate of the bound of the expected excess risk is
\mathcal{O}\Big(n^{-\frac{\kappa s}{\kappa s+d}}\big(\log(n)\big)^{5}\Big)
.
For nonparametric regression with a symmetric (around 0) error and the Huber loss, and the classification problem with the logistic loss, these rates are obtained with
\kappa=2
.

•

Excess risk bounds on the class of composition structured Hölder functions
.
Under the subexponential strong mixing condition, we show that, when the target function belongs to the class of composition structured Hölder functions (defined in Subsection
4.3
, see (
4.6
)), the convergence rate of the expected excess risk bound of the SPDNN estimator is of order
\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(\phi_{n^{(\alpha)}}^{\kappa/2}\lor\phi_{n^{(\alpha)}}\big)\big(\log\big(n^{(\alpha)}\big)\big)^{3}\Big)
, where
\phi_{n^{(\alpha)}}
is given in (
4.7
).
In this class, the convergence rate is
\mathcal{O}\Big(\big(\phi_{n}^{\kappa/2}\lor\phi_{n}\big)\big(\log(n)\big)^{5}\Big)
when dealing with exponentially strongly mixing observations.
As above, these rates can be applied with
\kappa=2
for nonparametric regression and classification.

2.

Minimax optimality in nonparametric autoregression and binary classification on the class of composition Hölder functions
.

•

Autoregression with a Gaussian error
. We deal with the Huber loss function. So, the upper bound of the expected
L_{2}
error of the predictor is of order
\mathcal{O}\Big(\phi_{n}\big(\log(n)\big)^{5}\Big)
, which matches (up to a logarithmic factor) with the lower bound established in
[
24
]
and shows that, the SPDNN estimator proposed is optimal in the minimax sense.

•

Autoregression with a Laplace error
. A lower bound of the
L_{2}
error on the class of composition Hölder functions is established.
For the learning task with the Huber loss, the upper bound of the expected
L_{2}
error of the estimator matches (up to a logarithmic factor) with the lower bound obtained, which shows that, the SPDNN predictor is minimax optimal.

•

Binary classification
. The problem of binary time series classification with the logistic loss is performed.
The upper bound of the expected excess risk of the predictor matches (up to a logarithmic factor) with the lower bound obtained in
[
2
]
, that is, the SPDNN estimator is optimal in the minimax sense.

\beta^{*}\coloneqq\beta_{i^{*}}
,
\beta^{**}\coloneqq\beta_{i^{*}}^{*}
and
t^{*}\coloneqq t_{i^{*}}
.
Moreover, set
B=\prod_{\ell=i^{*}+1}^{q}(\beta_{\ell}\land 1)
.
So, we also have
\beta^{**}=\beta^{*}B
.
Let
K\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\beta^{*}}(\mathbb{R},1)
with support
[0,1]
.
Define
m_{n}\coloneqq\lfloor\rho n^{1/(2\beta^{**}+t^{*})}\rfloor
and
h_{n}\coloneqq 1/m_{n}
where
\rho
is chosen such that
nh_{n}^{\beta^{**}+t^{*}}\leq\log(2)/(72\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}})
.
Set

\mathcal{U}_{n}\coloneqq\{(u_{1},\cdots,u_{t^{*}}):u_{i}\in\{0,h_{n},2h_{n},\cdots,(m_{n}-1)h_{n}\}\}.

For any
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{t^{*}})\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
,
define,

\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})\coloneqq h_{n}^{\beta^{*}}\prod_{j=1}^{t^{*}}K\big(\frac{x_{j}-u_{j}}{h_{n}}\big).

(6
.
38)

We have

\|\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{B}\|_{2}=\Big[\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{t^{*}}}\Big(h_{n}^{\beta^{*}B}\prod_{j=1}^{t^{*}}K^{B}(\frac{x_{j}-u_{j}}{h_{n}})\Big)^{2}dx_{1}\dots dx_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}=\Big[\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{t^{*}}}(h_{n}^{\beta^{*}B})^{2}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{t^{*}}K^{B}(\frac{x_{j}-u_{j}}{h_{n}})\Big)^{2}dx_{1}\dots dx_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}.

Let
z_{j}=\frac{x_{j}-u_{j}}{h_{n}}
for
j=1,\dots,t^{*}
.
Since
K
is supported on
[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{B}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big[\displaystyle\int_{\big[-\frac{u_{1}}{h_{n}},\frac{1-u_{1}}{h_{n}}\big]\times\cdots\times\big[-\frac{u_{t^{*}}}{h_{n}},\frac{1-u_{t^{*}}}{h_{n}}\big]}(h_{n}^{\beta^{*}B})^{2}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{t^{*}}K^{B}(z_{j})\Big)^{2}h_{n}dz_{1}\dots h_{n}dz_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=h_{n}^{\beta^{*}B+t^{*}/2}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{t^{*}}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{t^{*}}K^{B}(z_{j})\Big)^{2}dz_{1}\dots dz_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}=h_{n}^{\beta^{**}+t^{*}/2}\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}.

(6
.
39)

Also, one can easily get that, for all
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
with
\boldsymbol{u}\neq\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}
,

\int_{[0,1]^{t^{*}}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})dx_{1}\dots dx_{t^{*}}=0.

(6
.
40)

For any
\boldsymbol{W}=(\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{U}_{n}|}
, define for all
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{t^{*}})\in[0,1]^{t^{*}}
,

h_{\boldsymbol{W}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})^{B}.

(6
.
41)

From (
6.39
) and (
6.40
), we have (see also
[
32
]
) for all
\boldsymbol{W}=(\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}},\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{U}_{n}|}
,

\displaystyle\|h_{\boldsymbol{W}}-h_{\boldsymbol{W^{\prime}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big[\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t^{*}}}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})^{B}-\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\omega^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{u}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})^{B}\Big)^{2}dx_{1}\dots dx_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}(\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}}-\omega^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{u}})^{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t^{*}}}(\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{t^{*}})^{B})^{2}dx_{1}\dots dx_{t^{*}}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\text{Ham($\boldsymbol{W}$, $\boldsymbol{W^{\prime}}$)}}\|\psi_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{B}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\text{Ham($\boldsymbol{W}$, $\boldsymbol{W^{\prime}}$)}}h_{n}^{\beta^{**}+t^{*}/2}\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}

(6
.
42)

where
\text{Ham($\boldsymbol{W}$, $\boldsymbol{W^{\prime}}$)}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\textrm{\dsrom{1}}_{\omega_{\boldsymbol{u}}\neq\omega^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{u}}}
.
From Lemma 2.9 in
[
35
]
, see also the proof of Theorem 3 in
[
32
]
), there exists a subset
\mathcal{W}\subset\{0,1\}^{m_{n}^{t^{*}}}
satisfying
|\mathcal{W}|\geq 2^{m_{n}^{t^{*}}/8}
such that
\sqrt{\text{Ham($\boldsymbol{w}$, $\boldsymbol{w^{\prime}}$)}}\geq m_{n}^{t^{*}/2}/\sqrt{8}
for all
\boldsymbol{W},\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
with
\boldsymbol{W}\neq\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}
.
Recall that
m_{n}=1/h_{n}
.
According to (
6.42
), we have for all
\boldsymbol{W},\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
with
\boldsymbol{W}\neq\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}

\|h_{\boldsymbol{W}}-h_{\boldsymbol{W^{\prime}}}\|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}m_{n}^{t^{*}/2}h_{n}^{\beta^{**}+t^{*}/2}\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}h_{n}^{t^{*}/2}}h_{n}^{\beta^{**}+t^{*}/2}\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}h_{n}^{t^{*}/2}}h_{n}^{\beta^{**}}\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}.

(6

Let
\kappa=\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}/\sqrt{8\rho^{\beta^{**}}}

Recall,

\|K^{B}\|_{2}^{t^{*}}=\kappa\sqrt{8}\sqrt{\rho^{\beta^{**}}}\geq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{8}}{h_{n}^{\beta^{**}}}\sqrt{n^{-2\beta^{**}/(2\beta^{**}+t^{*})}}.

(6

6.6
Proof of Theorem
5.2

We will apply the Theorem 2.7 in
[
35
]
.
In this subsection, we set:

•

\|\cdot\|_{q}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}[0,1]^{d}}
for all
q\geq 1
;

•

For all
h^{*}\in\mathcal{M}(q,\mathbb{d},\mathbb{t},\boldsymbol{\beta},A)
,
P_{h^{*},n}
denotes the distribution of
\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}=(Y_{n},\dots,Y_{-d+1})
where,
Y_{i}
,
i=-d+1,\cdots,n
are generated from model (
5.1
);

•

The probability densities considered are with respect to the Lebesgue measure;

•

For two probability measures
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
,
KL(P_{1},P_{2})
represents the Kullback-Leibler divergence of
P_{1}
from
P_{2}
.

Consider model (
5.1
) with the error term that has a standard Laplace probability density.

By Lemma
5.1
, there is a constant
A>0
such that, for
n>0
, there exist an integer
M\geq 1
and function
h_{(0)},\dots,h_{(M)}\in\mathcal{G}(q,\mathbb{d},\mathbb{t},\boldsymbol{\beta},A)
such that

\|h_{(j)}-h_{(k)}\|_{2}\geq\kappa\sqrt{\phi_{n}}~\text{for all}~0\leq j<k\leq M,

and,

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}n\|h_{(j)}-h_{(0)}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{9}\log(M)

(6
.
47)

with
\kappa>0
, independent of
n
.
So,
h_{(0)}^{*}=h_{(0)}\textrm{\dsrom{1}}_{[0,1]^{d}},\dots,h_{(M)}^{*}=h_{(M)}\textrm{\dsrom{1}}_{[0,1]^{d}}\in\mathcal{M}(q,\mathbb{d},\mathbb{t},\boldsymbol{\beta},A)
and

\|h_{(j)}^{*}-h_{(k)}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\kappa^{2}\phi_{n}~\text{for all}~0\leq j<k\leq M.

Thus the first item of Theorem 2.7 in
[
35
]
hold.

Let us show the second item of Theorem 2.7 in
[
35
]
.

Let
P_{j}=P_{h_{(j)}^{*},n}
be the law of random vector
\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}=(Y_{n},\dots,Y_{-d+1})
, where
Y_{t}
with
(t=-d+1,\dots,n)
are generated from model (
5.1
) with standard Laplace error.
For
t>d
, let
X_{t}=(Y_{t-1},\dots,Y_{t-d})
.
For model (
5.1
), the conditional density of
Y_{t}
given
X_{t-d}
, is:

g\big(y_{t}-h^{*}(X_{t-d})\big)=\frac{1}{2}\exp(-|y_{t}-h^{*}(X_{t-d})|),

where
g(y)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|y|}
is the density of the standard Laplace distribution.
So, one can easily get,

\frac{dP_{j}}{dP_{0}}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{n})=\prod_{t=1}^{n}\frac{g(Y_{t}-h_{(j)}^{*}(Y_{t-1},\dots,Y_{t-d}))}{g(Y_{t}-h_{(0)}^{*}(Y_{t-1},\dots,Y_{t-d}))}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\log\frac{dP_{j}}{dP_{0}}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[-|Y_{t}-h_{(j)}^{*}(X_{t})|+|Y_{t}-h_{(0)}^{*}(X_{t})|]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|h_{(j)}^{*}(X_{t-d})-h_{(0)}^{*}(X_{t-d})|]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|h_{(j)}^{*}(X_{0})-h_{(0)}^{*}(X_{0})|]\leq n\|\widetilde{h}_{(j)}-\widetilde{h}_{(0)}\|_{1}.

Hence, the Kullback-Leibler divergence between of
P_{j}
from
P_{0}
satisfies,

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}KL(P_{j},P_{0})\leq n\|h_{(j)}^{*}-h_{(0)}^{*}\|_{1}.

Therefore, by using the Hölder inequality and (
6.47
), it holds that,

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}KL(P_{j},P_{0})\leq n\|h_{(j)}^{*}-h_{(0)}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{9}\log M.

So, the second attempt in Theorem 2.7 in
[
35
]
is satisfied.
Thus, one can find a positive constant
C_{\kappa}
such that

\underset{\widehat{h}_{n}}{\inf}~\underset{h^{*}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(q,\mathbb{d},\mathbb{t},\boldsymbol{\beta},A)}{\sup}\mathbb{E}[\|\widehat{h}_{n}-h^{*}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}]\geq C_{\kappa}\phi_{n}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

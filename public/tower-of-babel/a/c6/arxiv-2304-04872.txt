Idempotentization of Affine Schemes and Sheaves Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Félix Baril Boudreau ∗, Cristhian Garay López

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce the idempotentization process, which bears some philosophical and mathematical similarities with modern analytification and tropicalization. Idempotentization associates to any affine scheme an idempotent version of itself with respect to a fixed covering by distinguished affine open subschemes. Once this cover is fixed, we can functorially associate a Zariski sheaf of rings or modules to a sheaf of idempotent semiring or a sheaf of idempotent semimodules. We show that idempotentization is independent of the chosen cover and in the Noetherian case, the idempotentization of the structure sheaf recovers the global sections. Underlying our formalism is a combinatorial reflection of lattices of subobjects of ordered-theoretic objects seen as lattices coming from commutative algebra. This has topological consequences for the semiring of subtractive ideals of a commutative semiring S S : On one hand, it is a topological retract of the semiring of congruence relations of S S for the coarse lower topology. On the other hand, it is a topological retract of the semiring of ideals of S S for the coarse upper topology.

1.
Introduction

This article is the first step in exploring the idea of transforming algebraic geometry objects into idempotent semi-algebraic-geometry-like objects and studying their properties in an
idempotentized
framework. Our approach stems from exploiting correspondences between order-theoretic structures and idempotent algebraic structures and from considering the structure of certain non-Archimedean seminorms associated with commutative rings and modules over them. As a result, giving any affine scheme with a fixed covering by distinguished affine open subschemes, we
idempotentize
the scheme and functorially idempotentize any Zariski sheaf of rings or modules defined over it, with respect to that data.

L
, an element
c\in L
is said to be
compact
if for each set
\Lambda
, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, an element
a_{\lambda}\in L
satisfying
c\leq\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}
, there exists a finite subset
\Omega
of
\Lambda
such that
c\leq\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}
. A complete lattice
L
is
algebraic
if each of its elements is a least upper bound of compact elements.

Definition 2.14
.

A semiring
S
is
lattice-ordered
if it also has the structure of a lattice such that, for all
a,b\in S
: (1)
a+b=a\vee b
, and (2)
ab\leq a\wedge b
,and the partial order
\leq
is induced by the lattice structure on
S
.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
S
be a lattice-ordered semiring that is complete for the lattice structure. Let
S^{c}
be the subset of compact elements in
S
. If
S^{c}
is closed under products and contains the multiplicative identity element
1_{S}
of
S
, then
S^{c}
is a submonoid of
S
for this structure.

Lattice-ordered semiring morphisms are semiring morphisms (see
[
14
]
*p.239, Section 21).

Definition 2.16
.

We say that a complete lattice-ordered semiring
S
is an
algebraic lattice
if every element of
S
is the join of (possibly infinitely many) compact elements.

2.2.
Relevant Examples

The following examples of the theory of Subsection
2.1
will be needed later.

Example 2.17
.

On
\mathbb{N}
we define a lattice structure by setting, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\wedge b:=\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)
and
a\vee b:=\gcd(a,b)
. In this lattice, we have
a\leq b
precisely when
b
divides
a
. If
\cdot
denotes the integer multiplication on
\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathbb{N}^{\gcd}:=(\mathbb{N},\vee,\cdot)
is a lattice-ordered semiring (see
[
14
]
*21.9).

Example 2.18
.

If
R
is a commutative ring and
M
is an
R
-module, we let
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
be the set of all
R
-submodules of
M
. The set
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
can be endowed with a structure of commutative monoid as it is closed under finite sums of
R
-submodules and its identity element is the zero
R
-module. It can also be seen as a poset by defining the following partial order
\leq
. If
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
, then we write
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
if
N_{1}+N_{2}=N_{2}
. The pair
(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R),\leq)
is also a lattice if we define for any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
their greatest lower bound
N_{1}\vee N_{2}
and their least upper bound to be respectively the
R
-submodules
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
and
N_{1}+N_{2}
of
M
. This lattice is bounded because
0,M\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
are such that for any
N\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
,
N\vee M=N+M=M
and
N\wedge 0=N\cap 0=0
. Defining the greatest lower bound and the least upper bound on an arbitrary family
\{N_{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Id}(R)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
of elements of
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
as the
R
-submodules

\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}N_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}N_{\lambda}\text{ and }\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\Lambda}N_{\lambda}:=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}N_{\lambda},

implies that the lattice
(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R),\leq)
is complete. Finally, this complete lattice is algebraic because every
N\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
can be written as the sum of cyclic
R
-submodules
N=\sum_{n\in N}R\cdot n
.

Its subset
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
of compact elements is the set of finitely generated
R
-submodules of
M
. In particular,
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)=\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
precisely when
R
is Noetherian. In general, the set
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
has a structure of bounded lattice coming from the one of
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
. However, if
R
is not Noetherian, then there is an
N\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
not in
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
with an infinite set
\{n_{\lambda}\in N\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
of generators. So
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
is not complete, since
N=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}R\cdot n_{\lambda}=\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}R\cdot n_{\lambda}
.

Example 2.19
.

Specializing Example
2.18
to
M=R
, we write
\operatorname{Id}(R):=\mathrm{Mod}(R,R)
and
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}:=\mathrm{Mod}(R,R)^{c}
. Dedekind was the first to observe that the set
\operatorname{Id}(R)
together with addition and multiplication of ideals formed an additively-idempotent semiring (see
[
14
]
*p. 14, Example 1.4). This semiring is even simple since for any

we have
I+R=R
.

Example 2.20
.

If
R
is a Bézout domain, and
R^{\times}
is its group of units, then
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
coincides with the quotient
R/R^{\times}
and is a multiplicatively cancellative idempotent semiring. As
Frac(\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c})=Frac(R)/R^{\times}
,
Frac(\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c})^{\times}
is the
divisibility group
of
R
of
[
26
]
*p. 1.

Example 2.21
.

An integral domain
R
is
Prüfer
if every non-zero finitely generated ideal is invertible. Noetherian Prüfer domains are precisely the Dedekind domains, while Prüfer domains which are also GCD domains are the Bézout domains. The set
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
is a marked monoid with no nonzero zerodivisors. Hence, the semiring
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
has no nonzero zerodivisors. Moreover,
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
is multiplicatively cancellative if and only
R
is a Prüfer domain (see
[
14
]
*Example 4.36).

Example 2.22
.

The action of a ring
R
on an
R
-module
M
yields an action of
\operatorname{Id}(R)
on
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
, endowing
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
with a structure of
\operatorname{Id}(R)
-semimodule. Note that for all
I,J\in\operatorname{Id}(R)
, all
N\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
and all families
\{J_{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Id}(R)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
and
\{N_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, we have
\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}J_{\lambda}\right)N=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}J_{\lambda}N
,
J\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}N_{\lambda}\right)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}JN_{\lambda}
,
(IJ)N=I(JN)
,
RN=N
and
0_{R}N=0_{N}
. In the 1970’s, D. D. Anderson studied these objects and called them
fake rings
and
fake modules
in
[
1
]
. In particular, if
J\in\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
and
N\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
, then
JN\in\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
. With this multiplication,
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c}
is an
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodule.

There is a corresponding discussion with
k
-subsemimodules and
k
-ideals.

Example 2.23
.

Let
\mathrm{SMod}_{k}(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R))
be the set of
k
-subgroups of
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
. Note that each of these elements has a structure of
k
-
\operatorname{Id}(R)
-subsemimodule of
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
, but our results will not use this. Endow with similar operations as in Example

[

]
*p.332, Example 3.19 one can see that

is an algebraic lattice and that
\mathrm{SMod}_{k}(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R))^{c}
is the set of finitely generated

-subsemigroups of
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
. The last conclusion can be reworded by saying that
\mathrm{SMod}_{k}(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R))^{c}
is the set
\mathrm{SMod}_{k}(\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)^{c})

and write
\operatorname{Id}_{k}(\operatorname{Id}(R))
for
\mathrm{SMod}_{k}(\mathrm{Mod}(R,R))

as the

of the product ideal

2.3.
Non-Archimedean Seminorms

In this subsection, we define non-Archimedean seminorms over Abelian groups, rings and modules over normed rings and give some examples. As before, we write
a\leq b
to mean that
a+b=b
. Non-Archimedean seminorms on an Abelian group are defined as follows.

Definition 2.24
.

A
non-Archimedean seminorm
is a map
|\cdot|:A\xrightarrow{}{N}
from an Abelian group
(A,+,0)
to an idempotent monoid
{(N,+,0)}
that satisfies

(1)

(unit)
|0|=0

and

(2)

(subadditivity)
|a+b|\leq|a|+|b|
for all
a,b\in A
.

Such non-Archimedean seminorm
|\cdot|
is a
norm
if each
a\in A\smallsetminus\{0\}
satisfies
|a|\neq 0
and we call the triple
(A,|\cdot|,{N})
a
seminormed group
(respectively,
normed group
).

We now turn to rings. Inspired by the concept of non-Archimedean seminorm on a ring as in
[
5
]
*p. 11, we ask a non-Archimedean seminorm to be submultiplicative, which generalizes the multiplicative valuation of
[
12
]
*p.3989, Definition 2.5.1. If
S
is totally ordered, the latter is the definition of a
Krull valuation
.

Definition 2.25
.

A
non-Archimedean seminorm
is a map
|\cdot|:R\xrightarrow{}S
from a ring
R
to an idempotent semiring
S
such that

(1)

(unit)
|0|=0
and
|1|=1
,

(2)

(sign)
|-1|=1
,

(3)

(submultiplicativity)
|ab|\leq|a||b|
for all
a,b\in R
and

(4)

(subadditivity)
|a+b|\leq|a|+|b|
for all
a,b\in R
.

The non-Archimedean seminorm
|\cdot|
is
multiplicative
if
|ab|=|a||b|
holds for all
a,b\in R
, and it is a
norm
if each non-zero
a\in R
we have
|a|\neq 0
. We keep the term
valuation
for a multiplicative norm. We call the triple
(R,|\cdot|,S)
a
seminormed ring
(respectively,
normed ring
). Since only non-Archimedean seminorms are considered, we call them
seminorms
and denote them using lowercase Roman letters instead of bars
|\cdot|
.

Example 2.26
.

Let
v:R\to S
be a multiplicative seminorm. If
S
is an idempotent semifield, one can construct
\ell
-valuations (see
[
26
]
*p. 266). In particular, we have
v(-1)=1
since
(S\setminus\{0\},\times,1)
is torsion-free. More simply, if
R
is a field, then

is a valuation.

Definition 2.27
.

A seminorm
v:R\xrightarrow{}S
is
integral
if
v(a)\leq 1
for all
a\in R
.

Remark 2.28
.

If
S
is an idempotent semiring, then the set
S_{\leq 1}:=\{y\in S:y\leq 1\}
is a simple semiring and we let
i:S_{\leq 1}\to S
be the inclusion map. If
v:R\xrightarrow{}S
seminormed ring, then the subring
v^{-1}(S_{\leq 1})
of

is called the
seminorm ring
of

.
Note that

is integral if and only if it factors as

, where
v^{\prime}:R\to S_{\leq 1}
is a seminorm. From now on, if
v
is an integral seminorm, then we assume that

is a simple semiring. In particular
ba\leq a
for all
a,b\in S
as we have
ba+a=a(b+1)=a
.

Following
[
5
]
*p. 12, we consider norms defined on modules over normed rings.

Definition 2.29
.

Let
v:R\to S
be a normed ring, let

be an
R
-module and let

be an

-semimodule. A seminorm of Abelian groups

is a

if there is a nonzero

(1)

w(r\cdot m)\leq Cv(r)\cdot w(m)

for each

and

. The

if each

satisfies

.

3.
Universal Valuation and Universal Norm

3.1.
The Universal Valuation

If
f:R_{1}\xrightarrow{}R_{2}
is a ring morphism, we let
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)
be the map that sends an
I\in\operatorname{Id}^{c}(R_{1})
to
\langle f(I)\rangle\in\operatorname{Id}^{c}(R_{2})
. Since each
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
is an idempotent (even simple) semiring for the sum and product of ideals and as a result each
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)
is a semiring morphism, this yields a functor
\operatorname{Id}^{c}
from the category
\mathrm{Ring}
of commutative rings to the category
\mathrm{SRing}^{\text{idem}}
of idempotent semirings.

The so-called
universal valuation of
R
is the map
u_{R}:R\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
that sends an element
a\in R
to the principal ideal
\langle a\rangle:=R\cdot a
. Given a ring morphism
f:R_{1}\to R_{2}
one has the commutative diagram
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)\circ u_{R_{1}}=u_{R_{2}}\circ f
. As observed in
[
12
]
*p.3391, Proposition 2.5.4 and
[
24
]
*p.449, Example 3.16, the map
u_{R}
is an integral seminorm and it is moreover universal among the seminorms defined over
R
. More precisely, any seminorm that takes values in an idempotent semiring, or equivalently simple semiring because of Remark
2.28
, factors uniquely through
u_{R}
. For a ring
R
, there is a set-functor
\mathrm{isn}_{R}
from the category of simple semirings, that sends a simple semiring
S
to the set
\mathrm{isn}_{R}(S)
of integral seminorms
v:R\to S
. In the above two
loc. cit.
, the authors also deduce that
\mathrm{ins}_{R}
is represented by
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
f:R\to S
be a ring morphism. Let
J\in\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
with generating set
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}\}
. Then,
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)(\sum_{i=1}^{m}Ra_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Sf(a_{i})
. This map does not depend on the choice of generators for
J
. Indeed, if
\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}\}
is a second set of generators, then for each
1\leq j\leq n
there are
x_{1,j},\cdots,x_{m,j}\in R
such that
c_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i,j}a_{j}
. Then,
f(c_{j})
equals
\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(x_{i,j})f(a_{i})
and so
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)(\sum_{i=1}^{m}Ra_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}Sf(c_{j})
. By symmetry,
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(f)(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Rc_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Sf(a_{i})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
v:R\to S
is an integral seminorm, then the map
\hat{v}:\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}\to S
that sends
J=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Ra_{i}
, to
\sum_{i=1}^{m}v(a_{i})
is a semiring morphism.

Proof.

The map
\hat{v}
is well defined. Indeed, if
J=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Ra_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}Rb_{j}
, then for each
1\leq i\leq m
, there exist
c_{i,j}\in R
with
1\leq j\leq n
such that
a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}b_{j}
, and thus
v(a_{i})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(c_{i,j})v(b_{j})
. Since
S
is simple, we have
v(c_{i,j})v(b_{j})\leq v(b_{j})
for all
j
, so
v(a_{i})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(c_{i,j})v(b_{j})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(b_{j})
, hence
\sum_{i=1}^{m}v(a_{i})\leq m\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(b_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(b_{j})
. By symmetry,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(b_{j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}v(a_{i})
, and so
\hat{v}
is well defined.

The map
\hat{v}
is a semiring morphism. Indeed, it follows immediately from the definition of
\hat{v}
that
\hat{v}(0)=\langle 0\rangle
and
\hat{v}(1)=\langle 1\rangle
. Finally, if
I=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Ra_{i}
and
J=\sum_{j=1}^{n}Rb_{j}
, then
\hat{v}(\alpha+\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}v(a_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}v(b_{j})=\hat{v}(\alpha)+\hat{v}(\beta)
and
\hat{v}(\alpha\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}v(a_{i})v(b_{j})=\hat{v}(\alpha)\hat{v}(\beta)
.
∎

3.2.
The Universal Norm

In this subsection, we fix a ring
R
over which all modules are going to be defined. If
M
is an
R
-module, the set
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
of
R
-submodules of
M
is an additive idempotent monoid for the sum of
R
-submodules. We write
\mathrm{Mod}(M)
for
\mathrm{Mod}(M,R)
and
\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
for its submonoid of compact elements when there is no ambiguity. Given an
R
-module morphism
f:M_{1}\xrightarrow{}M_{2}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Mod}^{c}(f)
the map that sends an
N\in\mathrm{Mod}^{c}(M_{1})
to
\langle f(N)\rangle=f(N)

\mathrm{Mod}^{c}(M_{2})
. So if
N=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Rn_{i}
, then
f(N)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{Mod}^{c}(f)(Rn_{i})
. As mentioned in Example
2.22
, the action of
R
on
M
turns
\mathrm{Mod}(M)
into an idempotent
\operatorname{Id}(R)
-semimodule and
\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
into an idempotent
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodule. The map
\mathrm{Mod}(f)
then becomes an
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodule morphism and we thus have a functor

\mathrm{Mod}^{c}:R-\mathrm{Mod}\to\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}-\mathrm{SMod},

to the category
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}-\mathrm{SMod}
of
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodules. Similarly to Proposition
3.2
, we have the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
w:M\to N
be an
u_{R}
-seminorm.

(a)

The map
\hat{w}
that sends an element
T=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Rt_{i}
of
\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
to
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(t_{i})\in N
is a monoid morphism satisfying
\hat{w}(JT)\leq J\hat{w}(T)
for all
J\in\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
.

(b)

The map
\hat{w}
from (a) is an
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodule morphism if and only if
w
satisfies the following stronger version of (
1
): For all
r\in R
and
m\in M
, we have

(2)

w(rm)=u_{R}(r)w(m).

Proof.

(a)

The map
\hat{w}
is independent of the choice of generators for
T\in\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
. Indeed, if
T
has generating sets
\{t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\}
and
\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}\}
, then each
s_{j}
can be written as a sum
s_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{j,i}t_{i}
for some
c_{j,i}\in R
. Thus, there is a
C\in\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that

(3)

w(s_{j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(c_{j,i}t_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}Cu_{R}(c_{j,i})w(t_{i}).

Now,
Cu_{R}(c_{i,j})\leq R
in
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
and together with (
3
) this yield
\sum_{j=1}^{n}w(s_{j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(t_{i})
. Similarly, we obtain
\sum_{i=1}^{w}(t_{i})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w(s_{j})
.

The map
\hat{w}
is a monoid morphism. Indeed, we have
\hat{w}(0)=(0)
and for
T_{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
and
T_{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}
in
\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
, we have
\hat{w}(T_{a}+T_{b})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(a_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}w(b_{j})=\hat{w}(T_{a})+\hat{w}(T_{b})
.

Finally, for
J=\sum_{j=1}^{n}Rx_{j}\in\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
and
T=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Rt_{i}\in\mathrm{Mod}(M)^{c}
we have
\hat{w}(JT)=\sum_{i,j}w(x_{j}t_{i})
and
J\hat{w}(T)=\sum_{i,j}Rx_{j}w(t_{i})
. Thus,

\hat{w}(JT)=\sum_{i,j}w(x_{j}t_{i})\leq\sum_{i,j}CRx_{j}w(t_{i})\leq\sum_{i,j}Rx_{j}w(t_{i})=J\hat{w}(T),

which concludes the proof.

(b)

If (
2
) holds, then
\hat{w}(JT)=\hat{w}(\sum_{i,j}(x_{j})(t_{i}))=\sum_{i,j}\hat{w}(x_{j}t_{i})=J\hat{w}(T)
, and in particular
J\hat{w}(T)\leq\hat{w}(JT)
. Conversely, we recover (
2
) by applying
\hat{w}(JT)=J\hat{w}(T)
to
J=Rr
and
T=Rm
.∎

Next, we prove a universal seminorm analogue of
[
12
]
*Proposition 2.5.4 and
[

]
*p.449, Example 3.16 for the
u_{R}
-norm

(see Subsection

Proposition 3.4
.

The map
u_{M}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

Any
u_{R}
-seminorm on
M
factors uniquely through
u_{M}
.

(ii)

If
f:M_{1}\xrightarrow{}M_{2}
is an
R
-module morphism, then
u_{M_{2}}\circ f=\mathrm{Mod}^{c}(f)\circ u_{M_{1}}
.

(iii)

For an
R
-module

, the functor
\text{sn}_{u_{R}}
from the category of
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodules to the category of sets that sends a
\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c}
-semimodule

\text{sn}_{u_{R}}(N)=\{u_{R}-\text{seminorms }w:M\to N\}
is represented by

If

The following result is proven in

.

If
(A,\leq)
and

are ordered sets, then the following conditions about a map

are equivalent.

(1)

For any
y\in B
there is an
x\in A
such that
f^{-1}(B_{\leq^{\prime}y})=A_{\leq x}
.

(2)

The map
f
is order-preserving and there is an order-preserving map

such that
\operatorname{Id}_{A}\leq g\circ f
and

(respectively

) on an ordered set

The coarse upper topology coincides with the coarse lower topology for the inverse order of

.

Definition 3.26
.

Let

be a semiring. An equivalence relation

is said to be a

of

for all

of

4.
Idempotentization
of Sheaves and Schemes

4.1.
Summary

This section is about the idempotentization process and is structured as follows.
In Subsection
4.2
, we define functors
\Phi
that send a presheaf
\mathcal{F}
of rings or modules over a scheme
X
, to a presheaf
\Phi(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathbb{B}
-algebras or of
\operatorname{Id}^{c}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-semimodules over
X
. Then, in Subsection
4.3
, we put structures of idempotent semirings and semimodules on the stalks of
\Phi(\mathcal{F})
. Since
\Phi(\mathcal{F})
is not a sheaf in general, we sheafify it in Subsection
4.4
. For technical reasons, we now restrict to affine schemes
X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. In Subsection
4.5
, we use the homeomorphism
\widetilde{u_{R}}:\mathrm{Spec}(R)\to\mathrm{Spec}_{k}(\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c})
and push forward the sheafified presheaf
\Phi(\mathcal{F})^{\#}
to get a sheaf on
\mathrm{Spec}_{k}(\operatorname{Id}(R)^{c})
. This is the
R
-idempotentization of the presheaf
\mathcal{F}
. Specializing to Noetherian rings

, we show that in this context
\Phi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
is already a sheaf and that the
R
-idempotentization of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
preserves global sections. We also prove that
R
-idempotentization is functorial in
R
. We end this section by proving in Subsection
4.6
that idempotentization can be defined with respect to a covering by distinguished affine open subsets of a ring
R
and prove the resulting sheaves do not depend on the choice of covering of
X
.

The category of idempotent semirings is equivalent to the category
\mathbb{B}-\mathrm{Alg}
of
\mathbb{B}
-algebras. We denote by
\mathrm{SMod}^{\text{idem}}
the category of idempotent semimodules over arbitrary idempotent semirings. We write
PSh(X,\mathrm{Ring})
and
PSh(X,\mathbb{B}-\mathrm{Alg})
(respectively

and

) for the categories of presheaves (respectively sheaves) on
X
with values in
\mathrm{Ring}
and in
\mathbb{B}-\mathrm{Alg}
. We also write
PMod(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and

for the categories of presheaves and sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on
X
.

4.2.

Let

be the poset of open subsets of a topological space

with respect to set inclusion. All sheaves we consider are such with respect to a Zariski topology.

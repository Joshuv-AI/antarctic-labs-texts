Transmission phase evolution in fully screened and overscreened Kondo impurities
D. B. Karki

Abstract
We study the coherent properties of fully screened and overscreened Kondo effects based on the Nozieres local Fermi-liquid theory and Affleck-Ludwig boundary conformal-field-theory approach, respectively. Coherent transports through an SU( OPEN N ) N) generalization of fully screened and the multi- 𝒦 \mathcal{K} -channel overscreened Kondo impurities at and beyond the particle-hole (PH) symmetric point are thoroughly investigated. We report distinctive Fermi-liquid coefficients characterizing the finite temperature correction to the transmission phase shift and normalized visibility in fully screened Kondo regime, which can be measured with the existing experimental setups. Our work equally uncovers the significance of temperature correction to the transmission phase shift and visibility in non Fermi-liquid regime associated with the PH asymmetric overscreened Kondo effects with an arbitrary number of conduction channels 𝒦 \mathcal{K} . We propose viable roots of verifying our predictions in connection to the recent experiments, in particular with the experiments studying highly symmetric forms of fully screened Kondo effects and two-channel overscreened Kondo effects realized in quantum dot nano-structures.

II
AB-effect and coherent properties

The TPS is one of the characteristic feature of Kondo correlation which, however, can not be measured directly in conventional Kondo setup. The AB ring containing the quantum impurity in one of the arm has been suggested
Gerland et al. 2000
as an effective tool of obtaining the TPS information. The TPS has been shown to be easily extracted from the AB oscillations of the conductance as a function of the magnetic flux through the AB ring
Takada et al. 2014
;
Gerland et al. 2000
.

In a typical AB interferometer two different electronic paths are connected between source S and drain D. The quantum impurity under study is then placed in one of the path (let us say lower) and the remaining one (upper) is the reference path. The transport measurement of one path can be done independently of other by switching off the later
Carmi et al. 2012
. When an electron is send from source it suffers a path difference of
\Phi e/\hbar c+\theta_{\rm tp}
while reaching to the drain due to AB effect. Thus the interference between two paths depends on the magnetic flux
\Phi
enclosed within the AB ring and the TPS
\theta_{\rm tp}
which can be regarded as the relative phase between two paths. The source-drain conductance
G_{\rm SD}
reads
Carmi et al. 2012

G_{\rm SD}=G_{\rm d}+G_{\rm rf}+2\sqrt{\eta}\sqrt{G_{\rm d}G_{\rm rf}}\cos\left(\Phi e/\hbar c+\theta_{\rm tp}\right),

(6)

where
G_{\rm d}
is the conductance of the corresponding path with the QD (impurity of interest) and
G_{\rm rf}
is the conductance of reference path. The factor
\eta
is a constant characterizing the amount of coherent transport as compared to the total transport.

Following the setup proposed in Ref.
(
Carmi et al. 2012
)
, we now briefly describe the electron transport in open AB ring. The setup contains two external leads acting as the source and drain and two internal paths. While the upper path is the reference path, the lower path further contains two internal leads (the left and right leads) connecting the impurity of interest. The transmitted electron from the source to the left leads then propagate toward the impurity. These electrons get scattered off the impurity and propagate along the right lead which finally get transmitted out into the drain. Given that the source and drain are very weakly coupled to the internal leads
(
Carmi et al. 2012
)
, the strong electron interaction on the top of resonance scattering can be realized in the left lead-impurity-right lead system.

The low energy transport properties, such as the conductance, of the system mainly governed by the corresponding spectral function or the T-matrix. We connect the spectral function
\left[-\pi\nu\mathcal{T}^{\rm tot}_{r}(\varepsilon,T)\right]
and the TPS by defining the thermally and spin averaged function

Here

\mathcal{K}

In addition, the another quantity of paramount experimental interests is the normalized visibility

which accounts for the proportion of coherent transport over the total transport for given experimental setup. The Eq. (

\displaystyle+\frac{(\pi T)^{2}}{3}\Big[\sin 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha_{2}-\frac{N-1}{4}\phi_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\cos 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha^{2}_{1}+2(N-1)\phi^{2}_{1}\Big)\Big],

(21)

and

\displaystyle\pi\;{\rm Re}\mathcal{J}=\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\delta_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{(\pi T)^{2}}{3}\Big[\cos 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha_{2}-\frac{N-1}{4}\phi_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle-\sin 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha^{2}_{1}+2(N-1)\phi^{2}_{1}\Big)\Big].

(22)

The TPS then directly follows from Eqs. (
III.1
) and (
III.1
) with the definition
\theta_{\rm tp}{=}\arctan\left[{\rm Im}\mathcal{J}/{\rm Re}\mathcal{J}\right]
as presented in Eq. (
8
). For completeness, we explicitly write the mathematical expression of TPS

\theta_{\rm tp}=\tan^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{\sin^{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{(\pi T)^{2}}{3}\Big[\sin 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha_{2}-\frac{N-1}{4}\phi_{2}\Big)+\cos 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha^{2}_{1}+2(N-1)\phi^{2}_{1}\Big)\Big]}{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\delta_{0}+\frac{(\pi T)^{2}}{3}\Big[\cos 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha_{2}-\frac{N-1}{4}\phi_{2}\Big)-\sin 2\delta_{0}\Big(\alpha^{2}_{1}+2(N-1)\phi^{2}_{1}\Big)\Big]}\Bigg).

(23)

In the following subsections we discuss the TPS evolution in 1CK effects separately for different cases.

III.2
TPS and normalized visibility with exact SU(
N
) symmetry

In the limit of exact
SU(N)
symmetry various exact identities connection different FL coefficients are available. The connection of first and second generation of FL coefficients,
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
respectively, characterizing the elastic effects is given by
Noziéres 1974
;
Mora et al. 2009

\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha^{2}_{1}}=\frac{N-2}{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(1/N)\tan(\pi/N)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{N}\right)}\cot\left[\frac{m\pi}{N}\right].

(24)

Here the symbol
\Gamma(x)
defines the Euler’s gamma-function. Note that for the half-filled systems,
m{=}N/2
, the second generation of the FL-coefficient gets exactly nullified. In addition, the connections between the elastic FL-coefficients and those of inelastic one read
Mora 2009

\alpha_{1}=(N-1)\phi_{1},\;\;\;\alpha_{2}=\frac{N-1}{4}\phi_{2}.

(25)

In addition, without loss of generality we define the Kondo temperature of the
SU(N)
system by simple scaling relation

T_{\rm K}\equiv\frac{1}{\alpha}_{1}.

(26)

From Eqs. (
24
), (
25
) and (
26
), it is apparent that the low energy sector of
SU(N)
Kondo effects is fully described by the single energy scale
\alpha_{1}
or equivalently the Kondo temperature
T_{\rm K}
. By plugging in the Eqs. (
24
), (
25
) and (
26
) into Eq (
23
) followed by the simple mathematical simplification, we obtained the expression of TPS, whose low temperature expansion reads

\left.\theta_{\rm tp}\right|_{m,N}=\frac{m\pi}{N}+\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m)\left(\frac{\pi T}{T_{\rm K}}\right)^{2},

(27)

with the coefficient
\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m)
characterizing the finite temperature correction

\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m)=\frac{1}{3}\frac{N+1}{N-1}\cot\left(\frac{\pi m}{N}\right).

(28)

From the last Eq. (
27
) it is seen that the coefficient
\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m)
can be considered as distinctive FL coefficient characterizing SU(
N
) Kondo effects. Once
m
is changed to even value keeping
N
also even this numbers ultimately vanishes owing to have the exact PH symmetric situation where the
\pi/2
phase locked has been experimentally observed. For odd
N
, however, with any given numbers of electrons
m
the PH symmetric analog doesn’t simply exists and hence the TPS acquires the finite temperature correction. This finite temperature corrections in
SU(N)
Kondo effects are likely to be observed with the existing experimental setup
Takada et al. 2014
.

For case of
N{=}2m
the small PH symmetry breaking effects can be accounted for by considering the potential scattering phase shift
\delta_{\rm P}
such that
\delta_{0}=\frac{\pi}{2}\pm\delta_{\rm p}
for
\delta_{\rm P}\ll\delta_{0}

Affleck and Ludwig 1993

\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m=N/2)=\mp\frac{1}{3}\frac{N+1}{N-1}\tan\delta_{\rm p}.

(29)

From above discussion it is instructive to think of the coefficient
\mathscr{C}_{{\rm T},N}(m)
as the distinctive FL constant of generic SU(
N
) Kondo impurity, which could be of paramount importance for ongoing transport experiments in Kondo correlated systems. The Eq. (
29
) provides the qualitative idea of TPS evolution beyond PH symmetric point of the
SU(N{=}2m)
Kondo effects. In the following section, we present the microscopic calculations starting from the single level Anderson model for TPS in paradigmatic
SU(2)
Kondo effects beyond PH symmetric point.

From Eqs. (
III.1
) and (
III.1
) we get the expression for the normalized visibility in
SU(N)
Kondo regime

\left.\eta\right|_{m,N}=1-\frac{1}{3\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\pi}{N}\right)}\;\frac{N+1}{N-1}\;\left(\frac{\pi T}{T_{\rm K}}\right)^{2}.

(30)

At the zero temperature case of fully screened Kondo effects, the formation of Kondo singlet implies that all the particles that are scattered off the impurity
are scattered into single particle states. Consequently, all the conductance is carried by the coherent processes. At finite temperature the phase shift acquires the energy dependence and also the inelastic effects become prominent. Both of these processes result in the finite temperature reduction of visibility as expressed by Eq. (
30
).

III.3
TPS evolution beyond PH symmetric point in paradigmatic
SU(2)
Kondo effects

The
SU(2)
Kondo effects are one of the most intensely studied variant of
SU(N)
framework. From the previous section, it is seen that the PH symmetric point result in the
\pi/2
phase shift between outgoing and incoming electron states. However, the PH symmetric description is not always sufficient to analyze the real experiment. The single-impurity Anderson model
Anderson 1961
provides an straightforward way of proceeding with the qualitative description for the transport features beyond PH symmetric point of the
SU(2)
Kondo correlated systems.

The second generation of the FL coefficients
\alpha_{2}
and
\phi_{2}
(see previous section) incidentally vanish owing to have the PH symmetry. Therefore, the original theory of 1CK proposed by Nozieres
Noziéres 1974
contains two FL parameters namely
\alpha_{1}
and
\phi_{1}
. Beyond PH symmetric transport description, thus, involves the four FL parameters
\alpha_{1,2}
and
\phi_{1,2}
in addition the zero energy phase shift
\delta_{0}

Mora et al. 2015
. The general idea to proceed further would then be to make the connection of five FL parameters with the original parameters of the Anderson model. The generic single-impurity Anderson model is described by three parameters: the impurity energy level
\varepsilon_{\rm d}
, level hybridization
\mathcal{F}
and the Coulomb energy
U

Anderson 1961
.

The connection between the two set of parameters, the FL parameters and parameters of Anderson model, for some limiting cases has been investigated in the recent work
Mora et al. 2015
. The emergent of Kondo regime can be assured with fulfilling the condition
U\gg\mathcal{F}
and
-U+\mathcal{F}<\varepsilon_{\rm d}<-\mathcal{F}
. The latter condition can be polished to express it into the more symmetrical form

-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\mathcal{F}}-2\right)<\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varepsilon_{\rm d}+\frac{U}{2}\right)<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{U}{\mathcal{F}}-2\right).

(31)

In this limit, FL-coefficients can be expressed merely in terms of the spin susceptibility
\chi_{\rm s}
and its derivative
\chi^{\prime}_{\rm s}
with respect to
\varepsilon_{\rm d}

Mora et al. 2015

\alpha_{1}\simeq\phi_{1}\simeq\pi\chi_{\rm s},\;\;\alpha_{2}=\frac{3}{4}\phi_{2}\simeq-\pi\chi^{\prime}_{\rm s}.

(32)

The Eq. (
32
) provides the
\varepsilon_{\rm d}
,
\mathcal{F}
and
U
dependence of FL coefficients
\alpha_{1,2}
since the spin susceptibility
\chi_{\rm s}
can elegantly be expressed in terms of the original parameter of the Anderson model
Tsvelik and Wiegmann 1983
. The actual connections are given by
Mora et al. 2015

\overline{\alpha}_{1}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathscr{B}}}\exp\left[\pi\left(\frac{\mathscr{B}}{8}-\frac{1}{2\mathscr{B}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}}{\mathscr{B}}\right],

(33)

\overline{\alpha}_{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{\mathscr{A}}{\mathscr{B}^{3/2}}\exp\left[\pi\left(\frac{\mathscr{B}}{8}-\frac{1}{2\mathscr{B}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}}{\mathscr{B}}\right],

(34)

with the re-scaled parameters

\overline{\alpha}_{1}=\mathcal{F}\alpha_{1},\;\;\overline{\alpha}_{2}=\mathcal{F}^{2}\alpha_{2},\;\;\mathscr{A}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varepsilon_{\rm d}{+}\frac{U}{2}\right)\!,\;\;\mathscr{B}=\frac{U}{\mathcal{F}}.

Substitution of the Eq. (
32
) into Eq. (
23
) for
N{=}2
results in the TPS for
SU(2)
Kondo effects beyond PH symmetric point

\tan\theta_{\rm tp}=\frac{\sin^{2}\delta_{0}+\left(\pi\overline{T}\right)^{2}\Big(\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{1}\cos 2\delta_{0}+\frac{2}{9}\overline{\alpha}_{2}\sin 2\delta_{0}\Big)}{\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\delta_{0}-\left(\pi\overline{T}\right)^{2}\Big(\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{1}\sin 2\delta_{0}-\frac{2}{9}\overline{\alpha}_{2}\cos 2\delta_{0}\Big)},

(35)

with the re-scaled temperature
\overline{T}{=}T/\mathcal{F}
. The lowest order temperature correction for TPS then reads

\theta_{\rm tp}(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})=\delta_{0}+\left(\pi\overline{T}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{2}{9}\;\overline{\alpha}_{2}+\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{1}\cot\delta_{0}\right].

(36)

Therefore the finite temperature TPS depends on three parameters
\delta_{0}
,
\overline{\alpha}_{1}
and
\overline{\alpha}_{2}
. The connections of
\overline{\alpha}_{1}
and
\overline{\alpha}_{2}
to the original parameters of the Anderson model
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are provided in Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) respectively. The remaining parameter, the zero energy phase shift, further depends on
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
in rather non-trivial way, which can be obtained by solving the coupled equations
Tsvelik and Wiegmann 1983
;
Mora et al. 2015

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=

\displaystyle\Theta(q)\sqrt{2\mathscr{B}q}-\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{B}}}{2\pi^{3/2}}\times{\rm Re}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\int^{\infty}_{0}dy\;\frac{e^{-2iq\pi y}}{y^{3/2}}\times

\displaystyle\Bigg\{e^{-\pi y}\left(\frac{e}{iy}\right)^{iy}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+iy\right)-\sqrt{\pi}\Bigg\}\Bigg],

(37)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{0}(\mathscr{A};\mathscr{B})=\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\times{\rm Re}\Big[i\int^{\infty}_{0}dy\;\frac{e^{-2iq\pi y}}{y}e^{-\pi y}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{e}{iy}\right)^{iy}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+iy\right)\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{dx}{\pi}\frac{e^{i\pi yx^{2}/\mathscr{B}}}{1+\left(x+\frac{\mathscr{B}}{2}\right)^{2}}\Big]\Bigg).

(38)

In the last equations,
\Theta
stands for the unit step function and
q
is an arbitrary parameter. With all the specifications discussed above, the Eq. (
36
) provides the generic feature of finite temperature TPS evolution at and beyond the PH symmetric point for paradigmatic
SU(2)
Kondo correlated systems.

Before closing this subsection, we briefly discuss about the dependence of the normalized visibility on the microscopic parameters
\varepsilon_{\rm d}
,
\mathcal{F}
and
U
. Although the required relation can be directly read out from the Eq. (
35
), the more straightforward way of getting compact expression is to use the Eq. (
30
) for
N{=}2
in terms original parameters of the Anderson model which reads

\eta(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})=1-\frac{\overline{\alpha}_{1}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{\pi T}{\mathcal{F}}\right)^{2}.

(39)

IV
Overscreened Kondo effects

The ground state of a quantum impurity with an effective spin
\mathcal{S}
in the overscreened Kondo regime
2\mathcal{S}<\mathcal{K}
have a finite residual impurity spin of magnitude
|\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{K}/2|
. The situation for
\mathcal{K}
CK is, therefore, strikingly different as compared to the conventional 1CK. The zero temperature limit for conventional 1CK is completely described by the single particle scattering off the impurity
Noziéres 1974
. Nevertheless, the finite probability for the multi particle scattering off a impurity at zero temperature is one of the hallmark of overscreened Kondo effects
Nozieres and Blandin 1980
, which signifies the NFL correlation. This characteristics of an overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK results in the dramatic change of transport features over the corresponding 1CK effects. The common example includes the temperature dependence of the differential conductance acquires the fractional critical exponent that depends on the number of screening channels
\mathcal{K}

Affleck and Ludwig 1993
. In this section, we investigate the influences of NFL correlation on the TPS behavior of generic overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK using the seminal work of AL based on the BCFT
Affleck and Ludwig 1993
.

IV.1
Formulation of T-matrix

We consider an spin-1/2 overscreened Kondo impurity with
\mathcal{K}
conduction channels in the AL framework. In order to account for the effects of PH symmetry breaking, we explicitly consider the potential scattering on the top of Kondo interaction. The potential scattering is accounted for by considering the phase shift
\delta_{\rm P}
. The T-matrix of
\mathcal{K}
CK effects then reads
Affleck and Ludwig 1993

\displaystyle-\pi\nu\mathcal{T}(\varepsilon,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\Big[\left(1-e^{2i\delta_{\rm P}}\mathbb{S}\right)-e^{2i\delta_{\rm P}}Z\;\mathcal{I}\Big].

(40)

In Eq (
40
), the symbol
\mathbb{S}
stands for the S-matrix in the single particle sector of
\mathcal{K}
CK effects. The S-matrix is the function of the spin of the impurity
\mathcal{S}
, spin of conduction electrons
j
and number of conduction channels
\mathcal{K}

Affleck and Ludwig 1993

\mathbb{S}=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi(2j+1)(2\mathcal{S}+1)}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi(2j+1)}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi(2\mathcal{S}+1)}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right)}.

(41)

In the following, we focus on the particular case of
j,\mathcal{S}=1/2
and denote the S-matrix as

\left.\mathbb{S}\right|_{j,\mathcal{S}=1/2}\equiv\mathbb{S}=\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right)\Big/\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right),

(42)

and the general case of arbitrary impurity spin and spin of conduction electrons will be treated later.
The low energy physics of the problem is fundamentally controlled by the parameter
Z
which is defined in terms of the leading irrelevant operator (LIO)
\lambda
, number of orbital channels
\mathcal{K}
and the temperature
T
such that
Affleck and Ludwig 1993

\displaystyle Z=\mathscr{R}(\Delta)\lambda(\pi T)^{\Delta},\;\;\;\Delta\equiv\frac{2}{\mathcal{K}+2},

(43)

with the parameter
\mathscr{R}
defined by the relation

\mathscr{R}(\Delta)=3{\times}2^{\Delta}\sin\left(\pi\Delta\right){\sqrt{\!\frac{{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}{+}2}\right)}{\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}{+}2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{K}{+}2}\right)^{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathcal{K}{-}1}{\mathcal{K}{+}2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathcal{K}{+}1}{\mathcal{K}{+}2}\right)}}}.

In addition the energy and temperature dependent parameter
\mathcal{I}
in Eq (
40
) reads

\mathcal{I}\equiv\int^{1}_{0}du\left[\frac{u^{-\frac{i\varepsilon}{2\pi T}}(1-u)^{\Delta}}{\sqrt{u}}F(u,\Delta)-\mathscr{X}(u,\Delta)\right],

(44)

with the new definition

\mathscr{X}(u,\Delta)=\frac{\Gamma[1+2\Delta]}{\Gamma^{2}[1+\Delta]}u^{\Delta-1}(1-u)^{-(1+\Delta)},

(45)

and the hypergeometric functions
{}_{2}F_{1}(1+\Delta,1+\Delta;1;u)\equiv F(u,\Delta)

F(u,\Delta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{d\theta}{(u+1-2\sqrt{u}\cos\theta)^{1+\Delta}}.

(46)

We then extract the real and imaginary parts of the T-matrix from Eq (
40
), which cast into the compact form

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\left(-\pi\nu\mathcal{T}\right)

\displaystyle{=}\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\mathbb{S}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\right)\left[1-Q_{2}\mathcal{I}_{1}-Q_{3}\mathcal{I}_{2}\right],

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(-\pi\nu\mathcal{T}\right)

\displaystyle{=}\frac{1}{2}\left(1{-}\mathbb{S}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\right)\left[Q_{1}{-}Q_{2}\mathcal{I}_{2}{+}Q_{3}\mathcal{I}_{1}\right].

(47)

Here we defined the new symbols
Q_{j}

\displaystyle Q_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{S}\;\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}}{1-\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathbb{S}},

(48)

\displaystyle Q_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\;\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}}{1-\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathbb{S}},

(49)

\displaystyle Q_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\;\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}}{1-\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathbb{S}}.

(50)

In addition, the short-hand notations
\mathcal{I}_{1,2}
in Eq (
40
) depend on the temperature and energy for given value of
\Delta
in such a way that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle{\equiv}\!\int^{1}_{0}\!\!\!du\left[\!\cos\left[\!\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi T}\!\log(u)\right]\!\!\frac{(1{-}u)^{\Delta}F(u,\Delta)}{\sqrt{u}}{-}\mathscr{X}(u,\Delta)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle{\equiv}\int^{1}_{0}\!\!\!du\left[\sin\left[\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi T}\log(u)\right]\frac{(1-u)^{\Delta}F(u,\Delta)}{\sqrt{u}}\right].

(51)

IV.2
TPS evolution in overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK effects

In this subsection we proceed for the calculation of the thermally averaged values of the real and imaginary parts of the T-matrix needed for computing the the TPS for
\mathcal{K}
CK effects. To this end, the Eq. (
40
) provides the imaginary part of thermally averaged value of T-matrix

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4T}\sum_{\rm r=\uparrow,\downarrow}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{2\pi}\;\frac{{\rm Im}\left[-\pi\nu\mathcal{T}(\varepsilon,T)\right]}{\cosh^{2}(\varepsilon/2T)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(1-\mathbb{S}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\right)\left(1-B_{1}-B_{2}\right).

(52)

To obtain Eq (
IV.2
) we changed the order of integration between the energy
\varepsilon
and the variable
u
followed by defining the energy integrals
B_{1,2}
in terms of
\mathcal{I}_{1,2}

\displaystyle B_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{Q_{j+1}}{4T}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\varepsilon\frac{\mathcal{I}_{j}}{\cosh^{2}(\varepsilon/2T)},\;j=1,2.

(53)

By symmetry the integral
B_{2}
vanishes while
B_{1}
simplifies to

\displaystyle B_{1}=-Q_{2}\mathscr{P}(\Delta),

(54)

with the new definition

\mathscr{P}(\Delta)\equiv\int^{1}_{0}du\left[\frac{F(u)\log(u)}{(1-u)^{1-\Delta}}+\mathscr{X}(u,\Delta)\right].

(55)

To arrived at the last equation the exact integral related to the Fourier-transform of Fermi-function has been employed

\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{d\varepsilon}{\cosh^{2}(\varepsilon/2T)}\cos\left[\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi T}\ln u\right]=\frac{4T\sqrt{u}\log(u)}{u-1}.

This completes the calculation of the thermally averaged value for the imaginary part of the T-matrix. Similar procedures have then be used for the calculation of the thermally averaged real part of the T-matrix. To list the results, we have

\displaystyle\pi\;{\rm Im}\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[1-\mathbb{S}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}+Z\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathscr{P}(\Delta)\Big],

(56)

\displaystyle\pi\;{\rm Re}\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\mathbb{S}\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}-Z\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathscr{P}(\Delta)\Big].

(57)

The LIO
\lambda
in the expression of
Z
Eq. (
43
) depends on the Kondo temperature
T_{\rm K}
. In this work, without loss of generality, we define the Kondo temperature as given by

\lambda\equiv-\left(\frac{1}{T_{\rm K}}\right)^{\Delta}.

(58)

By plugging in the Eqs. (
56
)- (
58
) into Eq. (
8
), we obtain a compact form of TPS in overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK regime

\theta_{\rm tp}{=}{\tan^{-1}}\!\Bigg[\!\frac{1{-}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\frac{\cos\left(\pi\Delta\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\Delta/2\right)}{-}\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\mathscr{D}(\Delta)\!\left(\frac{\pi T}{T_{\rm K}}\right)^{\Delta}}{\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}\frac{\cos\left(\pi\Delta\right)}{\cos\left(\pi\Delta/2\right)}+\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}\;\mathscr{D}(\Delta)\!\left(\frac{\pi T}{T_{\rm K}}\right)^{\Delta}}\!\!\Bigg],

(59)

with the new definition

\mathscr{D}(\Delta)=\mathscr{R}(\Delta)\;\mathscr{P}(\Delta).

(60)

The Eq. (
59
) is the general expression providing the TPS evolution in overscreened Kondo regime. The function
\mathscr{P}(\Delta)
has recently been computed to be

\mathscr{P}(\Delta)=\frac{1}{\Delta(1+\Delta)}.

(61)

The Eqs. (
43
), (
59
) and (
61
) provide the complete evolution of TPS in overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK effects.

Of the particular experimental interest is the 2CK, where the single-particle
\mathbb{S}
-matrix vanishes resulting in the absence of single-particle scattering off a impurity at zero temperature. For this special case of 2CK,
\mathscr{D}(\Delta){=}4
and
\Delta{=}1/2
resulting in the greatly simplified form of TPS

\theta^{\rm 2CK}_{\rm tp}={\tan^{-1}}\!\Bigg[\!\frac{1-4\cos 2\delta_{\rm P}\sqrt{\pi T/T_{\rm K}}}{4\sin 2\delta_{\rm P}\sqrt{\pi T/T_{\rm K}}}\Bigg].

(62)

From the last Eq. (
62
), we recover the

). Beyond PH symmetric point, however, the TPS acquires the significant temperature correction from the corresponding zero temperature value of
\pi/2
. In addition the normalized visibility can be easily read out from the Eqs. (

) and (
57
).

In the previous subsections we considered the particular case of spin-1/2 impurity screened by
\mathcal{K}
channels of spin-1/2 conduction electrons. Therefore, before closing this subsection we briefly discuss the coherent properties of overscreened Kondo effects with an arbitrary spin
\mathcal{S}

) with

\mathbb{S}=\cos\left[\frac{\pi(2\mathcal{S}+1)}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]\Big/\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right].

(63)

In addition, consideration of general

\mathscr{R}(\Delta)=2^{\Delta+1}\mathcal{X}\;\sin(\pi\Delta),

(64)

with the numerical factor

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\equiv

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{\Gamma\left[\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]^{2}}{\Gamma\left[\frac{\mathcal{K}+1}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]\Gamma\left[\frac{\mathcal{K}-1}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]}\frac{\cos\left[\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]\;\sec\left[\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]-\mathbb{S}}{1+2\cos\left[\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal{K}+2}\right]}\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(65)

Of the particular interest, for this work, are the situation with

. As seen from the Eq. (

free from the spin amplitude

. The normalized visibility at the lowest order of temperature (with

) for these special cases reads

\displaystyle\eta^{\mathcal{K}=\!4\mathcal{S}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\Big[1{-}2^{\Delta{+}1}\mathcal{X}\sin(\pi\Delta)\mathscr{P}(\Delta)\!\left(\pi T/T_{\rm K}\right)^{\Delta}\!\Big].

(66)

Although the scattering amplitude
\mathbb{S}
gets vanishes, the finite temperature behavior of
\eta
therefore depends on the
\mathcal{S}
via the coefficient in front of the temperature in Eq. (

V
Conclusion

We develop a full-fledged theoretical framework to study the coherent properties of fully screened and overscreened Kondo effects by exploiting the AB ring. The developed formalism is equally applicable for the transport description at and beyond the PH symmetric point and therefore provides the complete evolution of TPS and normalized visibility in Kondo correlated systems. In the realm of 1CK, we extended the commonly studied TPS behavior in the conventional
SU(2)
Kondo regime to that in a high-symmetry
SU(N)
Kondo paradigm. We explored a distinctive FL coefficient characterizing the finite temperature correction to the TPS and normalized visibility that can be measured with the existing experimental setup
Takada et al. 2014
. In addition, we provided a general mathematical framework to deal with the TPS behavior beyond the PH symmetric point in the conventional
SU(2)
Kondo regime. Our work equally provides the complete picture of TPS evolution in overscreened
\mathcal{K}
CK effects. In particular, the constructed theoretical framework based on boundary conformal-field-theory shows the crucial role of finite temperature effects on the coherent properties in the NFL regime associated with the overscreened Kondo effects. In addition, the expression of the T matrices explored in this work could be equally useful for the study of thermoelectric transport through the Kondo impurities both in the FL and NFL regime. Although we focused our discussion on the original spin Kondo effects, the developments in this work are equally applicable for the exotic topological Kondo effects. In this work, we used the BCFT developments based on the
SU(2)_{\mathcal{K}}
current algebra for the explanation of TPS evolution in the overscreened Kondo regime; the exploration of similar physics for the
SU(N)_{\mathcal{K}}
appears a valid avenue for further research. Yet another very interesting topic for future investigation might be to unveil the TPS evolution at the charge-Kondo regime.

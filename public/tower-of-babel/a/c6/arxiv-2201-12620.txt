Introduction

Preamble.
The main purpose of this survey is to present a concise exposition of some applications of the theory of nonlinear spectral gaps which can serve as a roadmap for newcomers in the field and experts alike. Having as our main focus a result (Theorem
1
) of
Naor 2021
, we shall highlight some ideas which have played a pivotal role in recent developments and mention connections with classical geometric and algorithmic questions. The material of this paper is a mere expository repackaging of a selection of such developments and any difference in presentation is solely cosmetic.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
,
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
be two metric spaces and
D\in[1,\infty)
. We say that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
embeds into
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
with bi-Lipschitz distortion at most
D
if there exists a scaling factor
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
and a map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
such that

\forall\ x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\sigma d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leq d_{\mathcal{N}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\leq\sigma Dd_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y).

(1)

Following
Naor 2021
, we say that an
infinite

The

{theo}

[Average John]
There exists a universal constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that the
\tfrac{1}{2}

(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
admits an embedding into
\ell_{2}
with quadratic average distortion at most
C\sqrt{\log(\mathrm{dim}(X)+1)}

Theorem
1
is a metric counterpart of a classical theorem of
John 1948
, asserting that any finite-dimensional normed space embeds into
\ell_{2}
with bi-Lipschitz distortion at most
\sqrt{\mathrm{dim}(X)}
. This statement is famously optimal, e.g. for
X=\ell_{1}^{d}
or
X=\ell_{\infty}^{d}
, yet Naor’s theorem shows that an exponential improvement of the relevant distortion is possible if one relaxes the pointwise lower bound of the bi-Lipschitz condition
\eqref
eq:bi-lip to the averaged requirement
\eqref
eq:av-dist and replaces the normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
by its
\tfrac{1}{2}
-snowflake. Before explaining the ideas that come into the proof of Theorem
1
, it is worth pointing out that both of these modifications of John’s theorem are necessary in order to deduce bounds for the distortion which are subpolynomial on
\mathrm{dim}(X)
. In fact, the average John theorem is optimal in three distinct ways.

\bullet
If one is interested in bi-Lipschitz embeddings of snowflakes of normed spaces
X
into
\ell_{2}
in lieu of average distortion embeddings, then the relevant distortion has to depend polynomially on
\mathrm{dim}(X)
. Indeed, in
Naor 2021
, it is shown that the bi-Lipschitz distortion required to embed the
\theta
-snowflake of
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
into
\ell_{2}
is at least a constant multiple of
d^{\theta/2}
. The proof relies on metric cotype.

\bullet
The exponent
\tfrac{1}{2}
is the least amount of snowflaking that one needs to perform in order to obtain embeddings whose quadratic average distortion depends subpolynomially on
\mathrm{dim}(X)
. More specifically, in
Naor 2021
it is shown that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, the quadratic average distortion required to embed the
(\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)
-snowflake of
\ell_{1}^{d}
into
\ell_{2}
is at least a constant multiple of
d^{\varepsilon}
. The proof relies on Enflo type.

\bullet
Finally,
\sqrt{\log\mathrm{dim}(X)}
is the asymptotically optimal bound for the quadratic average distortion required to embed the
\tfrac{1}{2}
-snowflake of an arbitrary finite-dimensional space
X
into
\ell_{2}
. This will be further explained (for
X=\ell_{\infty}^{d}
) in Remark
6.1
below.

In the rest of the introduction, we shall describe the strategy of the proof of the average John theorem and introduce the necessary background.

1.1
Nonlinear spectral gaps

Let
\triangle^{n-1}=\{(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}=1\}
be the
n
-dimensional standard simplex. Consider a (row)-stochastic matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, that is, a matrix for which
(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{in})\in\triangle^{n-1}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Given a vector
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{n})\in\triangle^{n-1}
, we say that the matrix
A
is
\pi
-reversible if
\pi_{i}a_{ij}=\pi_{j}a_{ji}
for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. These objects admit a classical probabilistic interpretation. Consider the discrete-time homogeneous Markov chain
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on the state space
\{1,\ldots,n\}
with transition probabilities given by

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\mathbb{P}\{X_{t+1}=j\ |\ X_{t}=i\}=a_{ij},

(3)

where
t\geq 0
. If the transition matrix
A
is
\pi
-reversible, then
\pi
is also a stationary distribution for the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, that is, if
X_{0}
is distributed according to
\pi
then so is
X_{t}
for any
t\geq 1
. This is expressed algebraically by the matrix identity
\pi A=\pi
, where
\pi
is thought of as a row-vector. In the probabilistic framework above, reversibility simply means that the Markov process is invariant under time reversal in the sense that
(X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{T})
has the same joint distribution as
(X_{T},X_{T-1},\ldots,X_{0})
for any
T\in\mathbb{N}
.

Consider the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\pi)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\pi)})
whose (semi-)norm is given by

\forall\ x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\|x\|_{L_{2}(\pi)}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4)

Analytically, the stochastic matrix
A
is
\pi
-reversible if and only if it defines a self-adjoint contraction on
L_{2}(\pi)
with real eigenvalues which we shall denote by
1=\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(A)\geq-1
. The spectral gap of
A
is the algebraic quantity
1-\lambda_{2}(A)
which is known to encode important combinatorial properties of the matrix. It is a simple linear algebra exercise to show that the reciprocal
\gamma(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(1-\lambda_{2}(A))^{-1}
of the spectral gap is the least constant
\gamma\in(0,\infty]
for which the inequality

\forall\ x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\ell_{2},\qquad\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\leq\gamma\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

(5)

holds true. It is a well-known consequence of Cheeger’s inequality (see, e.g.,
Davidoff et al. 2003
) that upper bounds on
\gamma(A)
are equivalent to good expansion properties of the underlying weighted graph defined by
A
.

The above analytic characterization of a spectral gap as an optimal constant in a functional inequality was the starting point for the theory of
nonlinear spectral gaps
, of which Theorem
1
is the latest application. Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space and
p\in(0,\infty)
. If
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and
A
is a
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix, the spectral gap of
A
with respect to
d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p}
, denoted by
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
, is the least
\gamma\in(0,\infty]
such that

\forall\ x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{p}\leq\gamma\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{p}.

(6)

If the metric
d_{\mathcal{M}}
is inherited by a norm
\|\cdot\|
, we will denote
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
by
\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|^{p})
. As explained in
Mendel & Naor 2014
, unless
\mathcal{M}
is a singleton, if
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
is finite then
\lambda_{2}(A)
is bounded away from 1 by a positive quantity depending only on
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
. On the other hand, obtaining sensible upper bounds for
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
in terms of the usual spectral gap
1-\lambda_{2}(A)
is a notoriously hard task even for very structured metric spaces
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
. This difficulty reflects the fact that nonlinear spectral gap inequalities
\eqref
eq:nsg capture delicate interactions of spectral properties of the matrix
A
and geometric characteristics of the underlying metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
.

The study of nonlinear spectral gap inequalities
\eqref
eq:nsg has led to very fruitful investigations which have been impactful in various areas of mathematics and theoretical computer science such as metric geometry, geometric group theory, operator algebras, Alexandrov geometry and approximation algorithms. We refer, for instance, to the works of
Matousek 1997
,
Gromov 2003
,
Lafforgue 2008
;
Lafforgue 2009
,
Pisier 2010
,
Naor & Silberman 2011
,
Kondo 2012
,
Mendel & Naor 2013
;
Mendel & Naor 2014
;
Mendel & Naor 2015
,
Mimura 2015
,
Naor 2014
;
Naor 2017
;
Naor 2021
;
Andoni et al. 2018c
;
Andoni et al. 2018b
(see also Section
6
below for a high-level exposition of some of those). The pertinence of nonlinear spectral gaps to the study of average distortion embeddings into normed spaces and Theorem
1
stems from an important duality
principle which was discovered by
Naor 2014
and which we shall now describe.

1.3
Extrapolation

As explained above, the forthcoming proof of Theorem
1.2
does not rely on any sophisticated analytic machinery beyond elementary spectral properties of matrices. We will however use the following extrapolation principle for Poincaré inequalities.

Proposition 1
.

For every
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
there exist
c(p,q),C(p,q)\in(0,\infty)
such that the following conclusion holds. For every normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
, every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and every
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix
B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

c(p,q)\cdot\gamma(B,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{q})^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\gamma(B,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\leq C(p,q)\cdot\gamma(B,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{q}).

(10)

Proposition
1
is the vector-valued version (due to
Cheng 2016
;
de Laat & de la Salle 2021
) of the extrapolation principle for Poincaré inequalities
(
Matousek 1997
)
. In Section
3
, we shall also discuss a strengthening of Proposition
1
and its relation to a long-standing problem in the nonlinear theory of Banach spaces.

1.4
Historical discussion

Motivated by a classical theorem of
Ribe 1976
and kickstarted by
Bourgain 1986
, the
Ribe program
is a vast research program in metric geometry which aims to uncover deep structural analogies between the local theory of normed spaces and (nonlinear) metric spaces. In the nearly four decades that lapsed since Bourgain’s formalization of its objectives, the Ribe program has been an extraordinary source of surprising phenomena which arise when one studies metric spaces through the lens of Banach space theory and, vice versa, when one considers normed spaces as objects in the metric category. Numerous such key insights obtained in the last two decades originate in works of Naor and his collaborators. We refer to the surveys of
Kalton 2008
,
Naor 2012
;
Naor 2018
,
Ball 2013
,
Baudier & Johnson 2016
and
Godefroy 2017
and to the monograph of
Ostrovskii 2013
for a snapshot of some of these advances and their applications to other areas
of mathematics and theoretical computer science.

Theorem
1
is a prime example of a result conceptually belonging in the Ribe program for multiple reasons. Firstly, the statement of the theorem contains a highly nonlinear operation (snowflaking) performed on a norm and the desired embedding itself is not realized by a linear operator despite the fact that both the source and the target space are linear. Moreover, as already mentioned, the proof of Theorem
1
relies on the theory of nonlinear spectral gaps, a large part of which has been developed in the context of the Ribe program (see the discussion on expanders with respect to Banach spaces in Section
6.3
below). Finally, as discussed in
Naor 2021
, Naor’s initial interest in this research direction stemmed from a question regarding the embeddability of expanders into low-dimensional normed spaces raised by
Andoni et al. 2017
in the context of the approximate nearest neighbor search problem. A negative answer to this question (see Theorem
6.1
below) by
Naor 2017
;
Naor 2021
which follows easily from Theorem
1
shall be explained in detail in Section
6.1
. Theorem
6.1
also provides a new negative answer to an old question of
Johnson & Lindenstrauss 1984
who asked whether every
n
-point metric space admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding with constant distortion into a
d
-dimensional normed space, where
d=O(\log n)
. This question had previously been answered negatively by
Arias-de-Reyna & Rodr\’guez-Piazza 1992
for small distortions and
Matousek 1996
in general. Naor’s works provide a novel and more robust approach to this problem as they highlight a specific criterion (spectral gap) which implies the intrinsic high-dimensionality of the metric space at hand. Johnson and Lindenstrauss raised this question as a step towards finding a metric version of the aforementioned classical theorem of
John 1948
. A deep and impactful nonlinear John theorem was discovered via a completely different route in the influential work of
Bourgain 1985
. Quite surprisingly, Theorem
1
, which answers negatively the question of Johnson and Lindenstrauss, is itself a metric version of John’s theorem.

2
Duality and average distortion

In this section we present the proof of Naor’s duality Theorem
1.2
. Despite the fact that the theorem is stated for an
arbitrary
metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, the crux of the argument is the following special case in which
\mathcal{M}
is assumed to be
finite
. The general case follows by a (standard yet lengthy) discretization and compactness argument which can be found in
Naor 2021
. The finitary version stated below was proven in the case that
\pi
is the normalized counting measure in
Naor 2014
, where it is said that the argument is inspired by the proof of
Ball 1992
.

{theo}

[Naor’s duality – finitary version]
Suppose that
q,D\in[1,\infty)
,
\theta\in(0,1]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and fix
\pi\in\mathrm{int}(\triangle^{n-1})
. Let
\mathcal{M}=(\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a Banach space such that every
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})

\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta q})\leq D^{q}\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q}).

(11)

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a function
f=f_{\varepsilon}:(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta})\to\ell_{q}^{m}(Y)
which is
(D+\varepsilon)
-Lipschitz satisfying the condition

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}\|f(x_{i})-f(x_{j})\|_{\ell_{q}^{m}(Y)}^{q}\geq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{\theta q}.

(12)

Proof 2.1
.

It clearly suffices to assume that
\theta=1
as otherwise we can simply apply the same result to the
\theta
-snowflake of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be the class of all symmetric
n\times n
matrices
(c_{ij})
for which there exist
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in Y
, not all of which are equal, with

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad c_{ij}=\frac{\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\pi_{r}\pi_{s}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{r},x_{s})^{q}}{\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\pi_{r}\pi_{s}\|y_{r}-y_{s}\|_{Y}^{q}}\cdot\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|_{Y}^{q}.

(13)

Moreover, let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be the class of all symmetric
n\times n
matrices with nonnegative entries and vanishing diagonal and consider the convex hull
\mathcal{Q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{C}+\mathcal{P})
.

Let
T=(t_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
be the
n\times n
matrix with entries given by
t_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(D+\varepsilon)^{q}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{q}
for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We shall prove that
T\in\mathcal{Q}
. Suppose that this is not the case. Then, by the Hahn–Banach separation theorem, there exists a nonzero symmetric
n\times n
matrix
H=(h_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
with vanishing diagonal such that

\inf_{(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}h_{ij}b_{ij}\geq(D+\varepsilon)^{q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}h_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{q}.

Since
\mathcal{Q}
contains a translate of
\mathcal{P}
, choosing

whose only nonzero entries are those indexed by

and

, where

, we deduce that

for every

. Moreover, as
\pi_{i}\neq 0
for every

, we can define the parameter

\sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\sum_{r\neq i}h_{ir}\in(0,\infty)

(15)

and consider the matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
whose entries are given by

\forall\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad a_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\cases{}{h_{ij}}{\sigma\pi_{i}},&\mbox{if }i\neq j\\
1-\frac{1}{\sigma\pi_{i}}\sum_{r\neq i}h_{ir},\mbox{if }i=j.

(16)

By construction,
A
is a
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix as the choice of
\sigma
guarantees that its entries are nonnegative. Moreover, inequality
\eqref
eq:hb
can be equivalently rewritten as

\inf_{(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}b_{ij}\geq(D+\varepsilon)^{q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{q}.

(17)

Combining
\eqref

\inf_{(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}b_{ij}\geq\frac{(D+\varepsilon)^{q}}{\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{q}

(18)

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
, we have

\begin{split}\inf_{(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}b_{ij}&\leq\inf_{(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}b_{ij}\\
&=\inf_{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in Y}\frac{\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\pi_{r}\pi_{s}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{r},x_{s})^{q}}{\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\pi_{r}\pi_{s}\|y_{r}-y_{s}\|_{Y}^{q}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|_{Y}^{q}\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q})}\cdot\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\pi_{r}\pi_{s}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{r},x_{s})^{q}.\end{split}

(19)

Combining
\eqref

\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})\geq(D+\varepsilon)^{q}\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q})
which contradicts the assumption

T\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Since
T\in\mathcal{Q}
and all matrices in
\mathcal{P}
have nonnegative entries, we deduce that there exists

,

(20)

for every

. Consider the mapping

(21)

{rema}

, the
\theta
-snowflake of

admits an isometric embedding into

. Schoenberg’s theorem was later extended by

\theta
-snowflake of

admits an isometric embedding into

. Despite decades of attention, Question

, where

. In the forthcoming work of

,

and

do not admit a bi-Lipschitz embedding into

, where

\omega_{\theta,\eta}(t)=t^{\theta}\log^{\eta}(1+t)\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad\omega_{\theta,\eta}(t)=\frac{t^{\theta}}{1+\log^{\eta}(1+t)}.

(32)

We refer to

4.1
Nonlinear Rayleigh quotients

Fix
p\geq 1
, a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and a probability measure
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
. Let
L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})
be the metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{n},d_{L_{p}(\pi,\mathcal{M})})
whose metric is given by

d_{L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},y_{i})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(33)

where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\bm{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{M}^{n}
. Moreover, we shall use the ad hoc notation
L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})^{\dagger}
for the subset
L_{p}(\pi;M)\setminus\{(x,\ldots,x):\ x\in\mathcal{M}\}
of
L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a row-stochastic matrix that is
\pi
-stationary (in the sense that
\pi A=\pi
) and
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})^{\dagger}
. Following
Naor 2018
, we consider the corresponding nonlinear Rayleigh quotient given by

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{p}}{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{p}}.

(34)

By definition, if
A
is
\pi
-reversible, the nonlinear spectral gap
\eqref
eq:nsg satisfies

\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})=\sup_{\bm{x}\in L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})^{\dagger}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})}.

(35)

We will need the following properties of nonlinear Rayleigh quotients.

{lemm}

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space,
p\geq 1
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
. If
A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
are
\pi
-stationary stochastic matrices and
\bm{x}\in L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})^{\dagger}
, then we have

(i)

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})=\lambda\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};B,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
.

(ii)

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};\lambda A+(1-\lambda)\mathsf{Id}_{n},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})=\lambda\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
, where
\mathsf{Id}_{n}
is the identity matrix.

(iii)

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};AB,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};B,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

(iv)

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};B^{t},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})\leq t^{p}\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};B,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})
for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 4.1
.

The first property is evident from the definition
\eqref
eq:nrq and the second follows from (i) since
\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};\mathsf{Id}_{n},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})=0
. Moreover, (iv) follows by iterating (iii) so we are left to prove that. Notice that
AB
is
\pi
-stationary and the triangle inequality gives

\split\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}&\pi_{i}(AB)_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{j})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}b_{kj}\big(d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{k})+d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{k},x_{j})\big)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq\Big(\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ik}b_{kj}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{k})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Big(\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ik}b_{kj}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{k},x_{j})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
=\Big(\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ik}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},x_{k})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Big(\sum_{k,j=1}^{n}\pi_{k}b_{kj}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{k},x_{j})^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(36)

where in the last equality we used the stationarity of
A
in the form
\pi_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ik}
. The desired inequality (iii) follows from
\eqref
eq:iii after renormalizing.

(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})

{lemm}

Fix
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and let

be a
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix. For every Hilbert space

(37)

Proof 4.2
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
be the inner product of
\mathcal{H}
and notice that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\|x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-2\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{j}x_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=2\|\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}^{2},

(38)

since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}=0
. Moreover, since

is
\pi
-stationary and stochastic, we have

\split&\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(B^{2})_{ij}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(B^{2})_{ij}(\|x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\left\langle x_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{n}(B^{2})_{ij}x_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
=2\|\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\big\langle x_{i},\big((B^{2}\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})\bm{x}\big)_{i}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=2\|\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}^{2}-2\|(B\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}^{2},

where in the last equality we additionally used the
\pi
-reversibility of

eq:nrq of nonlinear Rayleigh quotients.

{lemm}

Let

be a normed space and

Proof 4.3
.

Without loss of generality, we can translate the components

of the vector

Therefore,

\|(B\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{X})\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;X)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:isomorph}}}{{\leq}}D\|(B\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:uselema}}}{{\leq}}\eta D\|\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;\mathcal{H})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:isomorph}}}{{\leq}}\eta D\|\bm{x}\|_{L_{2}(\pi;X)},

(43)

Since

(44)

On the other hand, using the triangle inequality we get

5
Beyond Hilbertian embeddings

Theorem
1
is a special case of a much more general embedding theorem proven by
Naor 2021
. As a matter of fact, a lot of the ideas required to prove this more general statement have already been used in the Hilbertian case. A key ingredient required to go beyond Theorem
1
is the notion of
Markov type
introduced by
Ball 1992
.

{defi}

A metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
has Markov type
p\in(0,\infty)
with constant
M\in(0,\infty)
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
, every
\pi
-reversible matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and every
\bm{x}\in L_{p}(\pi;\mathcal{M})
, we have

\forall\ t\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A^{t},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})\leq M^{p}t\ \mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p}).

(56)

The least such constant
M\in(0,\infty)
will be denoted by
\mathsf{M}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.

In
Ball 1992
, it was shown that any Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has
\mathsf{M}_{2}(\mathcal{H})=1
. Following
Ball et al. 1994
, we say that a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is
p
-uniformly smooth, where
p\in[1,2]
, if there exists a constant
S\in(0,\infty)
such that

\forall\ x,y\in X,\qquad\frac{\|x\|_{X}^{p}+\|y\|_{X}^{p}}{2}\leq\Big\|\frac{x+y}{2}\Big\|_{X}^{p}+S^{p}\Big\|\frac{x-y}{2}\Big\|_{X}^{p}.

(57)

The least such constant
S\in(0,\infty)
will be denoted by
\mathsf{S}_{p}(X)
. A deep theorem of
Naor et al. 2006
asserts that every
p
-uniformly smooth normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
has Markov type
p

\mathsf{M}_{p}(X)\lesssim\mathsf{S}_{p}(X).

(58)

An inspection of the proof of Theorem
1.2
reveals that the power of the norm in the estimate

eq:take-power with
\eqref
eq:mtype and using the extrapolation inequality

\split\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,&\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})=2\mathcal{R}\Big(\bm{x};\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2},\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:mtype}}}{{\gtrsim}}_{{}_{\mathsf{M}_{p}(X)}}\frac{1}{t}\mathcal{R}\Big(\bm{x};\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2}\Big)^{t},\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:extrapolation}}}{{\gtrsim}}\frac{1}{t}\mathcal{R}\Big(\bm{y};\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2}\Big)^{t},\|\cdot\|_{X}^{2}\Big).

(59)

Then, repeating the rest of the proof mutatis mutandis, we deduce the bound

\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\lesssim_{{}_{\mathsf{M}_{p}(X)}}\frac{\log(\mathrm{dim}(X)+1)}{1-\lambda_{2}(A)},

(60)

{theo}

\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and every
\pi
-reversible matrix

, we have

\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\lesssim_{{}_{\mathsf{M}_{p}(X),\mathsf{K}_{q}(Y)},p,q}\log(\mathsf{c}_{Y}(X)+1)\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q}).

(62)

Fix
\bm{x}\in L_{p}(\pi;X)^{\dagger}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}x_{i}=0
. Using Markov type and extrapolation as in

, there exists

with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}y_{i}=0
such that

\mathcal{R}(\bm{x};A,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\gtrsim_{{}_{\mathsf{M}_{p}(X)},p,q}\frac{1}{t}\mathcal{R}\Big(\bm{y};\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2}\Big)^{t},\|\cdot\|_{X}^{q}\Big).

(63)

Moreover, if
B_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(\tfrac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2})^{t}
, the argument of

eq:22 and
\eqref

\split\mathcal{R}(\bm{y};B_{t},\|\cdot\|_{X}^{q})\gtrsim_{q}&\frac{\|\bm{y}-(B_{t}\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{X})\bm{y}\|_{L_{q}(\pi;X)}^{q}}{\|\bm{y}\|^{q}_{L_{q}(\pi;X)}}\\

(64)

where

and moreover

\split\|B_{t}\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{Y}\|_{L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)\to L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)}=\Big\|\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2}\Big)^{t}&\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{Y}\Big\|_{L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)\to L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)}\\

(68)

Optimizing over

\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\lesssim_{{}_{\mathsf{M}_{p}(X)},p,q}\frac{\log(\mathsf{c}_{Y}(X)+1)}{\log\big(1/\|(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2})\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{Y}\|_{L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)\to L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)}\big)}

(69)

Observe that so far we have been very closely following the Hilbertian proof. Indeed, if
Y
is a Hilbert space and
q=2
, then the operator norm appearing in
\eqref
eq:66 is simply
\tfrac{1+\lambda_{2}(A)}{2}
and thus
\eqref
eq:estimate follows from
\eqref
eq:66 and John’s theorem which asserts that
\mathsf{c}_{\ell_{2}}(X)\leq\sqrt{\mathrm{dim}X}
. In the general (Banach space-valued) setting of Theorem
5
, we need a more robust argument to show that the operator norm
\big\|(\tfrac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2})\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{Y}\big\|_{L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)\to L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)}
is bounded away from 1 by a quantity which depends on the nonlinear spectral gap
\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q})
. To do this, we will leverage the
q
-uniform convexity of the normed space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
.

Fix a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and
q\in(0,\infty)
. If
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix, the
nonlinear absolute spectral gap
of
A
with respect to
d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q}
, denoted by
\gamma_{+}(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})
, is the least constant
\gamma_{+}\in(0,\infty]
such that

\forall\ x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\pi_{j}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},y_{j})^{q}\leq\gamma_{+}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{i}a_{ij}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i},y_{j})^{q}.

(70)

The terminology stems from the fact that
\gamma_{+}(A,|\cdot|^{2})=(1-\max_{i=2,\ldots,n}|\lambda_{i}(A)|)^{-1}
, where
1=\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(A)\geq-1
are the eigenvalues of
A
. Nonlinear spectral gaps and nonlinear absolute spectral gaps are related via the following inequalities.

{lemm}

Fix
q\in[1,\infty)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
. For every
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and every metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, we have

2\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})\leq\gamma_{+}\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q}\Big)\leq 2^{2q+1}\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q}).

(71)

The elementary proof of Lemma
5
can be found in
Naor 2014
. The pertinence of absolute spectral gaps in the ensuing discussion is that, in the case of uniformly convex spaces, they have a useful connection to vector-valued operator norms of adjacency matrices. This is manifested by the following proposition of
Mendel & Naor 2014
, whose proof relies on Pisier’s martingale cotype inequality for
q
-uniformly convex spaces
(
Pisier 1975
)
.

Proposition 3
.

Fix
q\in[2,\infty)
and let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a
q
-uniformly convex normed space. Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and every
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix
C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\|C\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{Y}\|_{L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)\to L_{q}^{{}^{0}}(\pi;Y)}\leq\Big(1-\frac{1}{(2^{q-1}-1)\mathsf{K}_{q}(Y)^{q}\gamma_{+}(C,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(72)

Proposition
3
is proven by
Mendel & Naor 2014
for the special case that
\pi
is the uniform measure on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
C
is a symmetric stochastic matrix. The proof of the general statement presented here is similar to this special case and we thus omit it. Plugging the bound
\eqref
eq:bound|| in
\eqref
eq:66 for
C=\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2}
, we finally deduce that

\split\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:66}\wedge\eqref{eq:bound||}}}{{\lesssim}}_{{}_{\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathsf{M}_{p}(X),\mathsf{K}_{q}(Y)},p,q}\log(\mathsf{c}_{Y}(X)&+1)\gamma_{+}\Big(\frac{A+\mathsf{Id}_{n}}{2},\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q}\Big)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:abs}}}{{\lesssim_{q}}}\log(\mathsf{c}_{Y}(X)+1)\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{Y}^{q}).\ \hskip 24.15253pt\Box

(73)

6
Geometric and algorithmic applications

In this final section, we present a selection of geometric and algorithmic applications of nonlinear spectral gaps (mostly without proofs) and related open questions.

6.1
Nonembeddability of expanders into low-dimensional normed spaces

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
d
-regular graph on the vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We shall denote by
A_{G}
the normalized adjacency matrix of
G
, that is, the
n\times n
symmetric stochastic matrix whose entries are given by
(A_{G})_{ij}=\frac{{\bf 1}_{\{i,j\}\in E}}{d}
, where
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A sequence
\{G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
d
-regular graphs with
|V_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
is called an expander graph sequence if
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}})<\infty
. The existence of regular expander graph sequences is a classical fact that can be proven via the probabilistic method (
Pinsker 1973
and
Bollob\’as 1988
), while deterministic constructions are notoriously more involved (see, e.g., the book of
Davidoff et al. 2003
). Embeddability properties of connected expanders viewed as metric spaces when equipped with the shortest path distance were first investigated by
Linial et al. 1995
who, among other results, showed that if an
n
-vertex
d
-regular expander embeds with quadratic average distortion
D
in a
k
-dimensional normed space, then
k\gtrsim(\log_{d}n)^{2}/D^{2}
. We shall now present the following (sharp) improvement of Linial, London and
Rabinovich’s result due to
Naor 2017
as a consequence of the average John theorem.

{theo}

For every
q\in[1,\infty)
, there exists
c(q)\in(0,\infty)
such that the following holds for every
\gamma,D\in[1,\infty)
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a
d
-regular connected graph on
n
vertices with
\gamma(A_{G})\leq\gamma
and let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a normed space such that
(G,d_{G})
admits an embedding into
X
with
q
-average distortion at most
D
. Then,

\mathrm{dim}(X)\geq n^{c(q)/\gamma D\log d}.

(74)

Proof 6.1
.

We shall first prove the case
q=1
. By the assumption, there exists a
D
-Lipschitz map
6
6

6

As is common, we shall identify the graph
G
with its vertex set
V
thus writing
f:G\to X
rather than
f:V\to X
. Moreover, we will always denote by
d_{G}
the shortest path distance on
V
.

f:(G,d_{G})\to(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
satisfying the average lower bound

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X}\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}d_{G}(u,v).

(75)

Let
k=\mathrm{dim}(X)
. Applying Theorem
1
for the measure
\mu=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V}\delta_{f(u)}
on
X
, we deduce that there exists a
O(\sqrt{\log k})
-Lipschitz function
h:(X,\|\cdot\|_{X}^{1/2})\to\ell_{2}
such that

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}\|h(f(u))-h(f(v))\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X}.

(76)

Since the graph
G
is a regular expander, inequality
\eqref
eq:spegap implies that

\split\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}\|h(f(u))-h(f(v))\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}&\leq\frac{2\gamma}{dn}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}\|h(f(a))-h(f(b))\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\\
\lesssim\frac{\gamma\log k}{dn}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}\|f(a)-f(b)\|_{X}\lesssim\gamma D\log k,

(77)

where in the last two inequalities we used the Lipschitz conditions for
h
and

. On the other hand, the graph
G
is
d

there exist at least
\frac{n}{2}
vertices

such that

. Hence, we have

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}d_{G}(u,v)\gtrsim\log_{d}n,

(78)

which, combined with

\log_{d}n\lesssim\gamma D\log k,

(79)

thus completing the proof of

. To address the general case

, we need a slight modification of this argument. It is a formal consequence of

and Theorem

, that for any
q\geq 1
, the
\tfrac{1}{2}
-snowflake of any finite-dimensional normed space

embeds into
\ell_{2}
with

. Considering a
O(\sqrt{\log k})

instead of the embedding

\eqref

.

A few historical comments are in order. Due to the existence of regular expander graph sequences, Theorem
6.1
implies that for arbitrarily large
n
, there exists an
n
-point metric space
(\mathcal{M}_{n},d_{\mathcal{M}_{n}})
such that if
\mathcal{M}_{n}
admits an embedding with bi-Lipschitz distortion
D
into a finite-dimensional normed space
X
, then
\mathrm{dim}(X)\geq n^{c/D}
for some universal constant
c\in(0,\infty)
. Therefore, Theorem
6.1
provides a negative answer to the question of
Johnson & Lindenstrauss 1984
discussed in Section
1.4
. A different negative answer to this question had been given in important work of
Matousek 1996
, who devised an ingenious random family of metric spaces and showed that they satisfy this property using input from real algebraic geometry. It is worth mentioning that a precursor of Theorem
6.1
is a result of
Lee et al. 2005
, who showed that if an
n
-vertex regular expander embeds in
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
with bi-Lipschitz distortion at most
D
,
then
d\geq n^{c/D}
for some universal constant
c\in(0,\infty)
.

Quantitatively, Theorem
6.1
provides a sharp relation between the dimension of the target space
X
, the number of vertices of
G
and the distortion
D
. Indeed, a classical theorem of
Johnson et al. 1987
asserts that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
D\geq 1
, any
n
-point metric space
\mathcal{M}
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion at most
D
into some
d
-dimensional normed space
X
, where
d\lesssim_{D}n^{C/D}
for some universal constant
C\in(0,\infty)
. This result was later refined by
Matousek 1992
, who showed that one can always take
X=\ell_{\infty}^{d}
as a target space in this statement.

{rema}

The optimality of Theorem
6.1
which follows from the works of
Johnson et al. 1987
and
Matousek 1992
immediately implies that the
O(\sqrt{\log\mathrm{dim}(X)})
upper bound for the average distortion in Theorem
1
is sharp. Indeed, suppose that the
\tfrac{1}{2}
-snowflake of
X=\ell_{\infty}^{d}
admitted an embedding into
\ell_{2}
with quadratic average distortion
o(\sqrt{\log d})
. Then, the proof of Theorem
6.1
would show that if an
n
-vertex expander
embeds with bi-Lipschitz distortion
D
in
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
, then

\log n=o(D\log d).

(80)

However, this inequality contradicts the embedding theorem of
Matousek 1992
.

Following the terminology of
Naor 2018
, we say that an infinite-dimensional Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
admits (quadratic) average dimension reduction with distortion
D\in(1,\infty)
if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
k_{n}=k_{n}^{D}(X)\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log k_{n}}{\log n}=0

(81)

such that the following condition holds. For any
n
points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, there exists a subspace
F=F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
X
with
\mathrm{dim}F\leq k_{n}
and points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in F
satisfying
\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|_{X}\leq D\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}
for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|_{X}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}^{2}.

(82)

As every finite metric space embeds isometrically in
\ell_{\infty}
, the aforementioned result of
Matousek 1996
(or Theorem
6.1
) implies that
\ell_{\infty}
does not admit average dimension reduction with any distortion
D>1
. The following tantalizing question remains open.

Question 6.2
.

Does
\ell_{1}
admit average dimension reduction with any distortion
D>1
?

We note that the bi-Lipschitz analogue of Question
6.2
is answered by a famous theorem of
Brinkman & Charikar 2005
(see also
Lee & Naor 2004
for a different influential proof) who showed that for arbitrarily large
n
and
D>1
there exists an
n
-point subset of
\ell_{1}
which does not admit a bi-Lipschitz embedding into any subspace of
\ell_{1}
of dimension at most
n^{c/D^{2}}
, where
c\in(0,\infty)
is a universal constant.

6.2
Average distortion embeddings of
\ell_{p}
into
\ell_{2}

In Theorem
1
, it was established that any finite-dimensional normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
admits an embedding into
\ell_{2}
with quadratic average distortion
O(\sqrt{\log(\mathrm{dim}(X)+1)})
via the nonlinear spectral gap inequality
\eqref
eq:estimate. As explained in Remark
6.1
, this estimate for the quadratic average distortion is asymptotically optimal yet, quite surprisingly, there exist many non-Hilbertian normed spaces which embed with constant quadratic average distortion in
\ell_{2}
. The following result is the main theorem of
Naor 2014
.

{theo}

There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
p\in(2,\infty)
, the normed space
\ell_{p}
admits an embedding into
\ell_{2}
with quadratic average distortion
Cp
.

Theorem
6.2
is established in
Naor 2014
via the nonlinear spectral gap inequality

\forall\ p>2,\qquad\gamma(A,\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{p}}^{2})\lesssim\frac{p^{2}}{1-\lambda_{2}(A)},

(83)

which holds for any
\pi\in\triangle^{n-1}
and any
\pi
-reversible stochastic matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, and the duality principle of Theorem
1.2
. Once again,
\eqref
eq:lp is proven in
Naor 2014
in the special case that
\pi
is the uniform measure on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
A
is a symmetric stochastic matrix. The proof of the more general statement presented here (which is equivalent to Theorem
6.2
) is identical. In
Naor 2014
, Theorem
6.2
and
\eqref
eq:lp were used to give new lower bounds for the
\ell_{p}
-distortion of random connected
d
-regular graphs, Ramanujan graphs and abelian Alon–Roichman graphs, improving earlier results of
Matousek 1997
. It is worth pointing out that
\eqref
eq:lp is no longer valid when
p\in[1,2)
.

6.3
Expanders with respect to Banach spaces

Combinatorial expanders are ubiquitous geometric objects whose metric structure is notoriously incompatible with Euclidean geometry. Nonlinear spectral gaps allow us to analyze non-Euclidean analogues of these exotic metrics. Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space. A sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
d
-regular graphs with
|G_{n}|\to\infty
is called an expander graph sequence with respect to
\mathcal{M}
if
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2})<\infty
. If such graphs exist, we say that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
admits a sequence of
d
-regular expanders. The following influential observation on the embeddability of expanders is essentially due to
Matousek 1997
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space and fix
\gamma,q\in(0,\infty)
. Suppose that
G=(V,E)
is a
d
-regular connected graph on
n
vertices with
\gamma(A_{G},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})\leq\gamma
. If
(G,d_{G})
embeds into
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
with
q
-average distortion at most
D
, then
D\gtrsim\frac{\log_{d}n}{\gamma^{1/q}}
.

Proof 6.3
.

By the assumption, there exists
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
and a
\sigma D
-Lipschitz map
f:(G,d_{G})\to(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
satisfying the average lower bound

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f(u),f(v)\big)^{q}\geq\frac{\sigma^{q}}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}d_{G}(u,v)^{q}\gtrsim\sigma^{q}(\log_{d}n)^{q},

(84)

where the second inequality follows from
\eqref
eq:x4 and Jensen’s inequality. On the other hand, by the definition
\eqref
eq:nsg of
\gamma(A,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{q})
, we have

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f(u),f(v)\big)^{q}\leq\frac{2\gamma}{dn}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f(a),f(b)\big)^{q}\leq\gamma\sigma^{q}D^{q},

(85)

where the last inequality follows from the Lipschitz condition for
f
. Rearranging, we deduce the desired lower bound for the
q
-average distortion
D
.

Deciding whether a given non-Euclidean metric space admits a sequence of expanders is a notoriously difficult problem in metric geometry, even when specified to normed spaces. By Proposition
4
, it is clear that if there exists a sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of regular expanders with respect to a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
, then
X
cannot contain subspaces uniformly isomorphic to
\{\ell_{\infty}^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
as it would then bi-Lipschitzly contain all finite metric spaces with uniform distortion. Normed spaces which do not uniformly contain
\{\ell_{\infty}^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
are said to have finite cotype in Banach space theory jargon
(
Maurey 2003
)
. Strikingly, this is the only known necessary condition for a normed space to admit an expander graph sequence and the following general question remains open.

Question 6.4
.

Is every combinatorial expander also an expander with respect to any normed space of finite cotype?

Such implications, asserting that a classical spectral gap implies a nonlinear spectral gap, are currently only known for substantially smaller classes of normed spaces from works of
Matousek 1997
,
Ozawa 2004
,
Pisier 2010
and
Naor & Silberman 2011
. It is worth mentioning that even the following question, which is formally weaker than Question
6.4
in view of Proposition
4
, remains open.

Question 6.5
.

Does there exist a sequence of finite metric spaces
\{(\mathcal{M}_{n},d_{\mathcal{M}_{n}})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that for any normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
of finite cotype, the bi-Lipschitz distortion required to embed
\mathcal{M}_{n}
into
X
satisfies
\mathsf{c}_{X}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\gtrsim_{X}\log|\mathcal{M}_{n}|
?

A positive answer to Question
6.5
would imply a striking dichotomy in the embeddability of finite metric spaces into infinite-dimensional normed spaces. If such a normed space
X
does not have finite cotype, then it bi-Lipschitzly contains every finite metric space with distortion
1+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0

(
Maurey 2003
)
. On the other hand, if
X
is an arbitrary infinite-dimensional space, then any finite metric space
\mathcal{M}
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
X
with distortion
O(\log|\mathcal{M}|)
by the theorems of
Dvoretzky 1961
and
Bourgain 1985
. A positive answer to Question
6.5
would imply that this bound is always optimal under the (necessary) assumption that
X
has finite cotype.

In regard to Question
6.4
, even the
existence
of a sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of regular graphs which are expanders with respect to any space of finite cotype remains unknown. The strongest available result in this direction is the following profound theorem of
Lafforgue 2008
, whose proof is an ingenious combination of algebraic and vector-valued harmonic analytic methods. We say that a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
has nontrivial type if
X
does not contain subspaces uniformly isomorphic to
\{\ell_{1}^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}

(
Maurey 2003
)
. Any space of nontrivial type has finite cotype, but the converse is not true (e.g. for
\ell_{1}
).

{theo}

There exists a sequence of regular graphs
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which is an expander graph sequence with respect to any normed space of non-trivial type.

Lafforgue’s graphs can be obtained as Cayley graphs of finite quotients of co-compact lattices in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
p
is a prime and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the field of
p
-adic rationals.

A completely different construction of a sequence of regular graphs which are expanders with respect to a large family of norms was presented in work of
Mendel & Naor 2014
. Theirs is a vector-valued adaptation of the zig-zag product construction of
Reingold et al. 2002
and the resulting graphs are expanders with respect to any normed space which admits an equivalent uniformly convex norm. Clearly any such space has nontrivial type but the converse is not true
(
Pisier 1975a
)
. While we will not outline the argument of
Mendel & Naor 2014
, it is worth pointing out that it consists of a novel construction of a base graph along with an adaptation of the zig-zag iteration of
Reingold et al. 2002
. The necessity of the uniform convexity assumption in this argument stems from this iteration procedure. On the other hand, the construction of the base graph (which was straightforward in the case of combinatorial expanders) has raised influential questions in vector-valued harmonic analysis that led to investigations of independent interest (
Mendel & Naor 2014
;
Eskenazis & Ivanisvili 2020
;
Eskenazis & Ivanisvili 2021
).

6.4
Expanders with respect to Alexandrov spaces

A complete geodesic metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is an Alexandrov space of nonpositive curvature (or a CAT(0) space) if for any quadruple of points
x,y,z,m\in\mathcal{M}
such that
m
is a metric midpoint of
x
and
y
, that is,
d_{\mathcal{M}}(m,x)=d_{\mathcal{M}}(m,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)
, we have

d_{\mathcal{M}}(z,m)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}(z,x)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}(z,y)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)^{2}.

(86)

If the reverse inequality holds true for any such quadruple
x,y,z,m\in\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is an Alexandrov space of nonnegative curvature. Alexandrov spaces of nonpositive (respectively nonnegative) curvature are (potentially singular) metric spaces which generalize Riemannian manifolds with nonpositive (resp. nonnegative) sectional curvature.

An argument of
Wang 1998
shows that any regular combinatorial expander is also an expander with respect to any Hilbert manifold with a CAT(0) Riemannian metric (see also
Naor & Silberman 2011
, Corollary 4.10
). The first systematic study of expanders with respect to (non-smooth) Alexandrov spaces of nonpositive curvature was undertaken by
Mendel & Naor 2015
, who showed the following theorem.

{theo}

There exists a CAT(0) space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and a sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of 3-regular graphs such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2})<\infty
, yet a random
d
-regular graph
{\bf G}
on
n
vertices satisfies
\gamma(A_{\bf G},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2})\gtrsim(\log_{d}n)^{2}
with probability
1-o_{n}(1)
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem
6.4
reveals a striking difference between nonlinear spectral gaps with respect to Alexandrov spaces of nonpositive curvature and classical spectral gaps, as a random
d
-regular graph on
n
-vertices is a combinatorial expander with probability
1-o_{n}(1)
as
n\to\infty
for any fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}

(
Bollob\’as 1988
)
. The following question remains open.

Question 6.6
.

Does every CAT(0) space admit a sequence of regular expanders? More ambitiously, does there exists a sequence of
O(1)
-regular graphs
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
|G_{n}|\to\infty
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2})<\infty
for every CAT(0) space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
?

A positive answer to the stronger statement in Question
6.6
would imply (in view of Proposition
4
) the existence of arbitrarily large finite metric spaces requiring logarithmic distortion to be embedded in any Alexandrov space of nonpositive curvature. The following question was asked by
Eskenazis et al. 2019
.

Question 6.7
.

Does there exist a sequence of finite metric spaces
\{(\mathcal{M}_{n},d_{\mathcal{M}_{n}})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that for any CAT(0) space
(\mathcal{N},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{N}})
, the bi-Lipschitz distortion required to embed
\mathcal{M}_{n}
into
\mathcal{N}
satisfies
\mathsf{c}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\gtrsim_{\mathcal{N}}\log|\mathcal{M}_{n}|
?

In the dual nonnegative curvature regime, the analogue of Question
6.6
was answered by
Andoni et al. 2018a
, who showed that there exists an Alexandrov space of nonnegative curvature which does not admit any sequence of regular expanders. Moreover, they asked the following dual to Question
6.7
.

Question 6.8
.

Does there exist an Alexandrov space of nonnegative curvature
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
such that any finite metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
embeds into
\mathcal{N}
with bi-Lipschitz distortion
\mathsf{c}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{M})\lesssim\sqrt{\log|\mathcal{M}|}
?

In their paper, they specifically asked Question
6.8
for the concrete Alexandrov space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which is the space of all Borel probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\|x\|_{\ell_{2}^{3}}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
equipped with the Wasserstein
\mathsf{W}_{2}
-distance.

6.5
Coarse non-universality

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
be two metric spaces and
\omega,\Omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
two moduli satisfying
\omega\leq\Omega
pointwise and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
. A mapping
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a coarse embedding with lower and upper moduli
\omega
and
\Omega
respectively if

\forall\ x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\omega\big(d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\big)\leq d_{\mathcal{N}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\leq\Omega\big(d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\big).

(87)

A family of metric spaces
\{(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha},d_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}})\}_{\alpha}
is said to embed equi-coarsely into a metric space
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
if there exist two moduli
\omega,\Omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
\omega\leq\Omega
pointwise and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and a family of coarse embeddings
\{f_{\alpha}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{N}\}_{\alpha}
with lower and upper moduli
\omega
and
\Omega
. The pertinence of nonlinear spectral gaps in coarse geometry stems from the
following influential observation of
Gromov 2000
;
Gromov 2003
.

Proposition 5
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,\infty)
and let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a metric space. Suppose that
\{G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of connected
d
-regular graphs with
|V_{n}|\to\infty
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})<\infty
. Then, the family of graphs
\{(G_{n},d_{G_{n}})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
equipped with their shortest path distances does not equi-coarsely embed into
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof 6.9
.

Let
\gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A_{G_{n}},d_{\mathcal{M}}^{p})<\infty
. Suppose that there exist two moduli
\omega,\Omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and mappings
f_{n}:V_{n}\to\mathcal{M}
with

\forall\ x,y\in V_{n},\qquad\omega\big(d_{G_{n}}(x,y)\big)\leq d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\leq\Omega\big(d_{G_{n}}(x,y)\big).

(88)

By definition of nonlinear spectral gaps, we have

\frac{1}{|V_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V_{n}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f_{n}(u),f_{n}(v)\big)^{p}\leq\frac{2\gamma}{d|V_{n}|}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E_{n}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f_{n}(a),f_{n}(b)\big)^{p}.

(89)

Moreover, using the upper modulus, we get

\frac{2\gamma}{d|V_{n}|}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E_{n}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f_{n}(a),f_{n}(b)\big)^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:coarse-assume}}}{{\leq}}\frac{2\gamma}{d|V_{n}|}\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E_{n}}\Omega(1)^{p}=\gamma\Omega(1)^{p}.

(90)

Finally, as each graph
G_{n}
is
d
-regular, for any
u\in V_{n}
there exist at least
\frac{|V_{n}|}{2}
vertices
v\in V_{n}
such that
d_{G_{n}}(u,v)\geq\lfloor\log_{d}(|V_{n}|/2)\rfloor
. Thus, the lower modulus gives

\frac{1}{|V_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V_{n}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(f_{n}(u),f_{n}(v)\big)^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:coarse-assume}}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{|V_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in V_{n}}\omega\big(d_{G_{n}}(u,v)\big)^{p}\geq\frac{\omega(\lfloor\log_{d}(|V_{n}|/2)\rfloor)^{p}}{2}.

Combining all the above, we deduce that

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\omega(\lfloor\log_{d}(|V_{n}|/2)\rfloor)^{p}\leq 2\gamma\Omega(1)^{p},

(91)

which clearly contradicts the coarse condition
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
.

An important consequence of Gromov’s observation is that if
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
admits a sequence of regular expanders
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, then there exists a metric space (e.g. the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{n\geq 1}(G_{n},d_{G_{n}})
) which does not admit a coarse embedding into
\mathcal{M}
. Consequently, the mere existence of combinatorial expanders implies that Hilbert spaces are not coarsely universal which is a well-known theorem of
Dranishnikov et al. 2002
. Moreover, Lafforgue’s Theorem
6.3
implies the existence of a metric space which does not admit a coarse embedding into any Banach space of non-trivial type. The coarse non-universality of this class was previously established in work of
Mendel & Naor 2008
by proving that Banach spaces of non-trivial type with cotype
q
have
sharp
metric cotype
q
. Understanding whether every Banach space of cotype
q
has sharp metric cotype
q
is the central open problem in the theory of metric cotype of Banach spaces; see
Giladi et al. 2011
for the best known results to date. If this was the case, then the following (currently open) question on coarse embeddings would have a negative answer.

Question 6.10
.

Does every separable metric space embed coarsely into some Banach space of finite cotype?

It follows from Proposition
5
that a negative answer to Question
6.10
would also be a consequence of the existence of a sequence of regular graphs
\{G_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which are expanders with respect to any normed space of finite cotype simultaneously, let alone from a positive answer to the much stronger Question
6.4
.

Despite the fact that Question
6.6
on the existence of expanders with respect to Alexandrov spaces of nonpositive curvature remains open, the coarse non-universality of this class was established by
Eskenazis et al. 2019
, thus answering a question raised by
Gromov 1993
. The main technical contribution of this work is the proof that every CAT(0) space
\mathcal{M}
has sharp metric cotype 2 which formally implies that
\ell_{q}
does not admit a coarse embedding in
\mathcal{M}
for any
q>2
. In contrast to this result, the very surprising fact that there exist coarsely universal Alexandrov spaces of nonnegative curvature was proven by
Andoni et al. 2018a
.

6.6
Approximate nearest neighbor search

Fix a parameter
c>1
. The
c
-Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search problem is defined as follows. Given an
n
-point dataset
\mathcal{P}
in some metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, we want to build a data structure
7
7

7

For the purposes of this survey, a data structure of size
M
is an array
A[1\ldots M]
of numbers (the “memory”) along with an algorithm which, given a point
q\in\mathcal{M}
, returns a point
\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{P}
.
that, given any query point
q\in\mathcal{M}
, returns a point
\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{P}
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,\widehat{p})\leq c\min_{p\in\mathcal{P}}d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,p)
. In practice, this problem can be reduced to its “decision version” (see
Har-Peled et al. 2012
), which is the
c
-Approximate
Near
Neighbor Search (
c
-ANN) problem at a
pre-fixed
distance scale
r>0
. In the
c
-ANN problem at scale
r
, we are again given an
n
-point dataset
\mathcal{P}
in some metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and we want to build a data structure that, given any query point
q\in\mathcal{M}
for which there exists a point
p^{\ast}\in\mathcal{P}
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,p^{\ast})\leq r
, returns a point
\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{P}
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,\widehat{p})\leq cr
. The main parameters to optimize are the
space
the data structure occupies and the
time
it takes to answer a query. A majority of the research conducted on this problem has focused on
d
-dimensional normed spaces rather than general metric spaces and moreover most of the algorithms in the literature are
randomized
in the sense that they return a random point
\widehat{p}
satisfying
d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,\widehat{p})\leq cr
with probability at least
1-\delta
for some pre-fixed confidence parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
.

The first approaches to the
c
-ANN problem for
d
-dimensional norms produced data-independent data structures, in which the memory cells accessed by the algorithm do not depend on the dataset
\mathcal{P}
but only on the query point
q
. In particular, building such data structures via (oblivious) metric dimension reduction has been used with great success for the Hilbert space
\ell_{2}^{d}
(
Indyk & Motwani 1999
;
Har-Peled et al. 2012
), the hypercube
\{-1,1\}^{d}
equipped with the Hamming distance
(
Kushilevitz et al. 2000
)
and spaces which (effectively) embed in them
(
Andoni et al. 2009
;
Nguy\˜\ˆen 2014
)
. While dimension reduction techniques yield data structures with polynomial space for these norms, these results are often far from practical due to the large degree of said polynomial. To overcome this barrier,
Indyk & Motwani 1999
introduced an influential technique called
Locality-Sensitive Hashing
(LSH) relying on (data-independent) randomized space partitions. Somewhat informally, a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over a family of partitions of
\mathcal{M}
is called
sensitive
at scale
r
up to error
c
if any two points at distance at most
r
are
\mathcal{D}
-likely to belong in the same cluster of the partition and any two points at distance at least
cr
are
\mathcal{D}
-unlikely to do so (where the implicit probabilities affect the space and time requirements of the data structure). As shown by
Indyk & Motwani 1999
, a (computationally efficient) sensitive distribution over random partitions can serve as a pre-filter for the dataset
\mathcal{P}
as the query point
q
is very likely to be indistinguishable from its near neighbors but is unlikely to collide with points
p
having
d_{\mathcal{M}}(q,p)>cr
. Using LSH, they were able to improve the space requirements over the existing
c
-ANN algorithms to almost linear for large enough accuracy parameters
c>1
. We refer to the thorough survey of
Andoni et al. 2018
for a detailed account of these and other contributions on the
c
-ANN problem and further references.

Despite these advances towards the
c
-ANN problem, researchers proved strong impossibility results
(
Motwani et al. 2007
;
O’Donnell et al. 2014
)
for the existence of data-independent data structures arising from LSH, thus creating the necessity for the development of efficient data-
dependent
algorithms. Historically, the first such result was proven by
Indyk 2001
for
\ell_{\infty}^{d}
. In recent years, this approach has gained a lot of momentum, especially in view of the works of
Andoni et al. 2009
for the Ulam metric,
Andoni et al. 2014
;
Andoni & Razenshteyn 2015
for
\ell_{2}^{d}
and
Andoni et al. 2017
for 1-symmetric norms. A breakthrough in this direction was presented in the work of
Andoni et al. 2018b
who showed the following theorem for
general

d
-dimensional normed spaces. It is worth emphasizing that their result does not a priori give any bound on the running time of the algorithm, it just restricts the number of memory locations the data structure is allowed to probe.

{theo}

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
X
be a
d
-dimensional normed space. There exists a randomized data structure for
O\big(\frac{\log d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)
-ANN over
X
with the following properties:

\bullet

The space used by the data structure is
n^{1+\varepsilon}\cdot d^{O(1)}
;

\bullet

The query procedure probes
n^{\varepsilon}\cdot d^{O(1)}
words in memory.

In order to prove Theorem
6.6
, the authors introduced a geometric parameter called the cutting modulus
\Xi(\mathcal{M},\varepsilon)
associated to a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and a parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, which governs the following data-dependent partitioning scheme: every finite dataset in
\mathcal{M}
either has a subset of proportional size (measured appropriately) which is contained in a ball of radius
\Xi(\mathcal{M},\varepsilon)
or admits a cut which is
\varepsilon
-sparse. Relying on this notion,
they were able to show the following general partitioning theorem.

{theo}

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a finite metric space and fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
. There exists a collection
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of
\mathcal{M}
with
\log|\mathcal{C}|=O\big(\log|\mathcal{M}|\log(\log|\mathcal{M}|/\varepsilon)\big)
such that for any
n
-point dataset
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{M}
, we have one of the following two properties:

\bullet

Either there exists
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
and
R\leq\Xi(\mathcal{M},\varepsilon)
such that
|\mathcal{P}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{0},R)|\geq\tfrac{n}{50}
, or

\bullet

There exists a subcollection
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
such that

\forall\ i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\qquad\frac{n}{50}\leq|S_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}|\leq\frac{49n}{50}

(92)

and for every
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leq 1
, we have

\#\big\{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:\ {\bf 1}_{S_{i}}(x)\neq{\bf 1}_{S_{i}}(y)\big\}\leq 50\varepsilon m.

(93)

Theorem
6.6
suggests a very natural LSH with approximation
O(\Xi(\mathcal{M},\varepsilon))
since, at each step of the algorithm, we either have a dense ball of radius
\Xi(\mathcal{M},\varepsilon)
or we have a collection of subsets with a distribution that decreases the size of the dataset and rarely splits the query from its nearby points in the dataset. The relevance of those results with the subject of this survey stems from the fact that Theorem
1.2
implies that if
X
is a normed space, then the cutting modulus satisfies

\Xi(X,\varepsilon)\lesssim\frac{\log(\mathrm{dim}(X)+1)}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

(94)

The main idea of the proof of
\eqref
eq:bound-cut is to apply
\eqref
eq:estimate to the adjacency matrices of geometric graphs associated to finite subsets of
X
. If such a graph does not have a subset of proportional size (with respect to the underlying stationary measure) contained in a ball of radius
\Omega(\log(\mathrm{dim}(X)))
, then the nonlinear spectral gap inequality
\eqref
eq:estimate implies that it also cannot have large (classical) spectral gap and thus admits a sparse cut by Cheeger’s inequality. A combination of Theorem
6.6
and
\eqref
eq:bound-cut implies Theorem
6.6
. We refer to the work of
Andoni et al. 2018b
for the precise definition of the cutting modulus and the proofs of these results.

In their follow-up work,
Andoni et al. 2018c
proved the existence of data structures for ANN over
d
-dimensional normed spaces with slightly worse (but still subpolynomial) approximation and reasonable bounds for the running time. Some of their results still rely on elements of the theory of nonlinear spectral gaps, whereas others use the existence of a remarkable uniform homeomorphism between spheres of Banach spaces which originates in the resolution of the distortion problem by
Odell & Schlumprecht 1994
.

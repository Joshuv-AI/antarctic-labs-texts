Regularity of the spatially homogenous fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation
Chao-Jiang Xu and Yan Xu

Abstract
. We study the Cauchy problem of the spatially homogenous fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation and show that the solution enjoys Gevrey regularity and decays estimation with an L 2 L^{2} initial datum for positive time.

1.
Introduction

The Cauchy problem of fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\left((1-\Delta_{v})^{s}+\langle v\rangle^{2s}\right)u=f(t,x,v),\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
u=u(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the density distribution function of particles at time
t\geq 0
, and
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
represent the position and velocity variables of particles, respectively, with
\gamma>-3
,
0<s\leq 1
.

Here,
s
and
\gamma
are parameters that lead to the classical Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation if
\gamma=0,s=1
and it is the leading term to linear Landau operator of Maxwellian case; if
s=1
, it is the leading term to linear Landau operator of the soft and hard potential case; if
0<s<1
, it is the leading term to linear Boltzmann operator of the soft and hard potential case.

The Kramers equation as a special Fokker-Planck equation was initially derived by Kramers
[
8
]
to describe the kinetics of a chemical reaction. Later, it turned out that it had more general applicability, e.g., to different fields such as supersonic conductors, Josephson tunneling junction, relaxation of dipoles, and second-order phase-locked loops. Kolmogorov
[
9
]
first studied this equation and gave an explicit formula for the fundamental solution, which implies the existence and smoothness of the solution. It was the main example for motivation to the general theory of H
\rm\ddot{o}
rmander
[
2
]
of hypoelliptic equations. The study of hypoelliptic equations often falls back on pseudodifferential operators, which has been addressed in H
\rm\ddot{o}
rmander’s classical results
[
2
,
3
]
. Mathematical analysis of the Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation is initially motivated by a trend to equilibrium for confining potentials
[
1
,
4
,
16
]
.

For the Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation, Desvillettes and Villani established the explicit decay of any polynomial order
t^{-1}
in
[
1
]
. Exponential decay was shown in
[
13
]
and an explicit rate was given in
[
6
]
. Spectral properties for some different Fokker-Planck equations have been discussed by Kolokoltsov
[
11
]
. The semiclassical resolvent estimates for the Kramers-Fokker-Planck operator have been studied in
[
7
]
. The phase space reduction of the one-dimensional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation was demonstrated in
[
10
]
. The Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation with potential has been studied in
[
14
]
. An optimal global-in-time
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate for solutions to the Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation with short-range potential has been given in
[
15
]
.

In this work, we consider the Cauchy problem of the spatially homogenous fractional Kramers-Fokker-Planck equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\left((1-\Delta_{v})^{s}+\langle v\rangle^{2s}\right)u=f(t,v),\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with
0<s<1
and
\gamma+2s>0
. Moreover, the operator
(1-\Delta)^{s}
, where for any
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
(1-\Delta)^{s}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is defined via the Fourier transform

\begin{split}(1-\Delta)^{s}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\mathcal{F}u\right),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{split}

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are Fourier transform and its inverse, respectively.

Recalling the definition of classes of symbols
S^{m}_{1,0}
, a
C^{\infty}
function
a(x,\xi)
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
belongs to the class
S^{m}_{1,0}
, if there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
, independent of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, such that

\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a(x,\xi)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\beta|},\quad\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. And the class
S^{m}_{1,0}
is a Fr
{\rm\acute{e}}
chet space with the semi-norm

|a|_{l}^{m}=\max_{|\alpha+\beta|=l}\sup_{x,\xi}(1+|\xi|)^{|\beta|-m}\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a(x,\xi)\right|.

So that the operator
(1-\Delta)^{s}
can be viewed as a pseudo-differential operator of symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}
.

We say that a
C^{\infty}
function
u\in\mathcal{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the Gevrey spaces, where
\sigma>0
, if there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

e^{c_{0}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

equivalently, for any multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C^{|\alpha|+1}(\alpha!)^{\sigma},

the constant
c_{0}=\frac{1}{C}
is the Gevrey radius. Then we introduced the Gelfand-Shilov space
S^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
\mu,\nu>0
and
\mu+\nu\geq 1
, the
C^{\infty}
function
u\in S^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, if there exist
c_{0},\tilde{c}_{0}>0
such that

e^{c_{0}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\qquad{\rm and}\qquad e^{\tilde{c}_{0}|v|^{\frac{1}{\nu}}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We study the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
), with
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
, and show that the smooth solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
initial datum enjoys the Gelfand-Shilov regularity. The main results read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
, for any
T>0
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
u
be the smooth solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) and

satisfies

\left\|\langle D\rangle^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq A^{k+1}k!,\quad t\in]0,T],\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(1.3)

then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any

, we have

\left\|\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}k}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\frac{C^{k+1}}{t^{k}}k!,\quad\forall t\in]0,T],

(1.4)

with
\tilde{s}=\min\{1/2,s\}

Theorem 1.2
.

For
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
, for any

and

be the solution of the Cauchy problem (

(1.5)

Remark: We have then proven that the solution of Cauchy problem (

2.
Estimations of Commutator and Interpolation

In the following, the notation
A\lesssim B
means there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, and the notation
[T_{1},T_{2}]
means
T_{1}T_{2}-T_{2}T_{1}
, which denotes the commutator. For simplicity, we denote the weighted Lebesgue spaces and the weighted Sobolev spaces

\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|f\|_{p,\gamma},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma}f\right\|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|f\|_{H^{m}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad m\in\mathbb{R},

with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, where we use the notation
\langle v\rangle=(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

For later use, we need the following estimation of the commutator for the operator
\langle D\rangle^{r}=(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}
with
r>0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, depends on
m
and
r
, such that

\left\|\left[(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}},\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{H^{r-1}_{m}}.

Proof.

Let
\langle v\rangle^{m}u=g
, then we have

\left[(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}},\langle v\rangle^{m}\right]u=(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}g-\langle v\rangle^{m}(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}\left(\langle v\rangle^{-m}g\right).

Since
(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}
can be viewed as a pseudo-differential operator of symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{r}{2}}
, it follows that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\langle v\rangle^{m}(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}\langle v\rangle^{-m}=\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|<N}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\langle v\rangle^{m}D_{v}^{\alpha}\langle v\rangle^{-m}a^{(\alpha)}(D_{v})+\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D_{v}),

where
a^{(\alpha)}(\xi)=\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\langle\xi\rangle^{r}
, and

r_{N}(v,\xi)=N\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\theta)^{N-1}}{\alpha!}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)d\theta,

with
r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)
is the oscillating integral, defined via

r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)=Os-\iint e^{-v^{\prime}\eta}D^{\alpha}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}a^{(\alpha)}(\xi+\theta\eta)\frac{dv^{\prime}d\eta}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

Using the identity

e^{-iv^{\prime}\eta}=\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}\left(1-\Delta_{\eta}\right)^{l^{\prime}}e^{-iv^{\prime}\eta}=\langle\eta\rangle^{-2l}\left(1-\Delta_{v^{\prime}}\right)^{l}e^{-iv^{\prime}\eta},

then from the fact
2l^{\prime}>|m|+N+|\beta|+3
,
2l>r+N+|\beta^{\prime}|+3
, integration by parts and using the Leibniz formula, we can deduce that

\begin{split}&D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}C_{\beta}^{\beta_{1}}D^{\beta_{1}}_{v}\langle v\rangle^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1-\Delta_{\eta})^{l^{\prime}}a^{(\alpha+\beta^{\prime})}(\xi+\theta\eta)G(v,\eta)\frac{d\eta}{\langle\eta\rangle^{2l}(2\pi)^{3}},\end{split}

where

G(v,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-iv^{\prime}\eta}(1-\Delta_{v^{\prime}})^{l}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D^{\alpha+\beta_{2}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}\right)dv^{\prime}.

By using the Leibniz formula and the fact
2l^{\prime}>|m|+N+|\beta|+3
, one gets

\begin{split}|G(v,\eta)|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}\left|(1-\Delta_{v^{\prime}})^{l}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D^{\alpha+\beta_{2}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}\right)\right|dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq c_{1}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq 2l}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}\left|\partial^{\sigma}_{v^{\prime}}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}}D^{\alpha+\beta_{2}}\langle v+v^{\prime}\rangle^{-m}\right)\right|dv^{\prime}\\
&\leq c_{2}\langle v\rangle^{-m-N-|\beta_{2}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{3}}\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-2l^{\prime}+|m|+N+|\beta_{2}|}dv^{\prime}\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\langle v\rangle^{-m-N-|\beta_{2}|},\end{split}

with the constant
\tilde{c}_{2}
depends on
m
.
Substituting it into
D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)\right)
, since
\theta\in]0,1[
, by using Peetre’s inequality, we have for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}&\left|D^{\beta}_{v}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\xi}\left(\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{\theta,\alpha}(v,\xi)\right)\right|\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-2l}\langle\xi+\theta\eta\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|}d\eta\\
&\leq c_{3}\langle\xi\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-2l+|r|+N+|\beta^{\prime}|}d\eta\leq\tilde{c}_{3}\langle\xi\rangle^{r-N-|\beta^{\prime}|},\end{split}

(2.1)

here we use the fact
2l>r+N+|\beta^{\prime}|+3
, and the constant
\tilde{c}_{3}
depends on
m
,
r
. So that we can obtain

\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D)\in\Psi_{1,0}^{r-N}.

And therefore,
\langle v\rangle^{m}r_{N}(v,D)(1-\Delta)^{\frac{N-r}{2}}\in\Psi_{1,0}^{0}
, then it follows that

\begin{split}\left\|[(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}},\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}]u\right\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{r-j}{2}}g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}r_{N}(\cdot,D)g\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{r-j}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{r-N}}\leq C_{1}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{r-1}_{m}},\end{split}

with
C_{1}
depends on
m
and
r
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
k,l>0
and
\delta>0
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0

\epsilon

\|u\|_{H^{k}_{l}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\epsilon\|u\|_{H^{k+\delta}_{l}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
, the key idea of the proof is for any
m>0
, the embedding
H^{m}_{l}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compact.
For any
\epsilon>0
, choose the constant
R>0
sufficiently large such that

(1+|v|^{2})^{-l}\leq\epsilon,\quad|v|\geq R/2.

(2.4)

Fixing a cutoff function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with the properties that
0\leq\phi(v)\leq 1
, and

\phi(v)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad v\in B_{R/2}(0),\\
&0,\quad v\in B^{c}_{R}(0),\end{aligned}\right.

here

and

Then by using the Plancherel Theorem and Young inequality, it follows that

\begin{split}&\|\phi u\|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}\left(\hat{\phi}*\hat{u}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq 2^{m}\left\|\left(\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}\hat{\phi}\right)*\left(\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}\hat{u}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{m}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u\|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Assume that
\{\langle v\rangle^{l}u_{j}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then by using the Sobolev inequality, one gets
\{u_{j}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since the embedding

) we can obtain

And therefore from

)
, we can conclude

\begin{split}\|u_{j^{\prime}}-u_{n^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&\leq\left\|\phi(u_{j^{\prime}}-u_{n^{\prime}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|(1-\phi)(u_{j^{\prime}}-u_{n^{\prime}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq\left\|\phi\langle\cdot\rangle^{l}(u_{j^{\prime}}-u_{n^{\prime}})\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(0))}+\|u_{j^{\prime}}-u_{n^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(|v|\geq R/2)}\\

3.
Energy Estimates

In this section, we study the energy estimates of the solution to the Cauchy problem (

Lemma 3.1
.

For
0<s<1
,
\gamma+2s>0
and
T>0
. Let
u
is the smooth solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). Assume
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

satisfies (
1.3
). Then there exists a constant
B_{0}>0
, depends on

and

, such that for any

\begin{split}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}d\tau\leq B_{0}.\end{split}

(3.1)

Proof.

Since
u
is the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}(1-\Delta)^{s}u,u\right)+\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=(f,u).

(3.2)

Noting that

\begin{split}&\left(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}(1-\Delta)^{s}u,u\right)\\
&=\left((1-\Delta)^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right),\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right)+\left(\left[\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2},(1-\Delta)^{s}\right]u,\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right),\end{split}

since
(1-\Delta)^{s}\in\Psi_{1,0}^{2s}
, from using the G
{\rm\mathring{a}}
rding inequality we can get that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
,

\left((1-\Delta)^{s}\left(\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right),\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-C_{0}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2}.

Firstly, for
0<s\leq 1/2
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
2.2
) we get

\begin{split}\left|\left(\left[\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2},(1-\Delta)^{s}\right]u,\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right)\right|\leq C_{2}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2},\end{split}

substituting it into (
3.2
), since
\gamma+2s>0
, from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2}+\left\|f(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

(3.3)

with
\tilde{C}_{0}
depends on
C_{0}
and
C_{2}
. For
\gamma>0
, since
s>0
, we have

\tilde{C}_{0}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2}\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{s/2}_{\gamma/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

then from Lemma

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(C_{4}\tau)^{k-j}C_{k}^{j}\left\|(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}})^{j}u\right\|_{2,\gamma/2+s}\right)^{2}d\tau\\

&\leq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\frac{(C_{4}T)^{k-j}B_{1}^{j+1}k!}{(k-j)!}+C_{4}TB_{1}^{k}k!\right)^{2}\leq\left((3+C_{4}T)B_{1}^{k}k!\right)^{2},\end{split}

similarly, taking
B_{1}\geq(\tilde{C}_{4}T)^{2}+1
, from (
4.3
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}\left(C_{5}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\tilde{C}_{4}\tau)^{k-j}C_{k}^{j}\left\|(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}})^{j}u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\right)^{2}d\tau\leq\left(C_{5}(3+\tilde{C}_{4}T)B_{1}^{k}k!\right)^{2}.

Then using (
1.3
) and (
4.3
), it follows that

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}d\tau\\
&\leq 2T(T^{k}A^{k+1}k!)^{2}+16\left(\tilde{C}_{6}+(C_{5})^{2}(3+\tilde{C}_{5}T)^{2}\right)(B_{1}^{k}k!)^{2},\end{split}

here
\tilde{C}_{5}=\max\{C_{4},\tilde{C}_{4}\}
. Finally, taking

B_{1}\geq\max\left\{B_{0},TA+1,(\tilde{C}_{5}T)^{2}+1,4\sqrt{\tilde{C}_{6}+(C_{5})^{2}(3+\tilde{C}_{5}T)^{2}+TA^{2}}+1,\right\},

we get for any
0<t\leq T
,

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle D\rangle^{2\tilde{s}}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}d\tau\leq\left(B_{1}^{k+1}k!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Next, we construct the following estimate to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
0<s<1
and
\gamma+2s\geq 0
. Let
u
be the solution of Cauchy problem (
1.1
),
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
e^{t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then there exists a constant
B_{2}>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<t\leq T
,

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{(L^{\infty}]0,T];L^{2})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}dt\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}dt\leq\left(B_{2}^{k+1}k!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(4.7)

Proof.

From (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}\\
&\quad+\left((1-\Delta)^{s}\left((t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s})^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right),\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right)\\
&=kt^{2k-1}\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left(\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}f,\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right)\\
&\quad+\left(\left[(1-\Delta)^{s},\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}\right]u,\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right)\\
&=kt^{2k-1}\left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+R_{1}+R_{2}.\end{split}

(4.8)

We show (
4.7
) holds by induction on the index
k
. For
k=0
, it is enough to take in (
3.1
). Assume
k\geq 1
and (
4.7
) is true for
0\leq m\leq k-1
,

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{m}u\right\|^{2}_{(L^{\infty}]0,T];L^{2})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{m}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}dt\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{m}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}dt\leq\left(B_{2}^{m+1}m!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(4.9)

Now, we prove that (
4.7
) holds true for
m=k
. By using the G
{\rm\mathring{a}}
rding inequality, it follows that

\begin{split}&\left((1-\Delta)^{s}\left((t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s})^{k}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}u\right),\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}\left(t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}-C_{0}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2},\end{split}

as the argument in the Lemma
3.1
, applying Lemma
2.4
if
\gamma>0
and applying Lemma
2.5
if
-2s<\gamma\leq 0
, respectively, we get

C_{0}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2}\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}+\tilde{C}_{0}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

For the term
R_{1}
, by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has

|R_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

For
R_{2}
, from the Plancherel theorem, as the arguments in (
4.5
), it follows that

\left\|\left[\langle D\rangle^{2s},\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2}\right]u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}(C_{7}t)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{j}u\right\|_{H^{2s-1}_{\gamma/2}}.

Hence, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it follows that for
0<s\leq 1/2

|R_{2}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}(C_{7}t)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{j}u\right\|_{2,\gamma/2}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|_{2,\gamma/2},

and for
1/2<s<1
, we have

|R_{2}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}(C_{7}t)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{j}u\right\|_{H^{2s-1}_{\gamma/2}}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{2s-1}_{\gamma/2}}.

Then for both two cases, using Lemma
2.4
for
\gamma>0
and using Lemma
2.5
for
-2s<\gamma\leq 0
, we obtain that for all
0<s<1

\begin{split}|R_{2}|&\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}+\tilde{C}_{7}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(C_{7}t)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{j}u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\right)^{2},\end{split}

with
\tilde{C}_{7}
depends on
\gamma
and
s
.

Plugging these results back into (
4.8
), one gets

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}+\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\\
&\leq 4kt\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)}\right)^{k-1}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}+C_{8}t^{2}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{(\gamma/2+s)}\right)^{k-1}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}\\
&\quad+2\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+4\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(C_{7}t)^{k-(j-1)}C_{k}^{j}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{j}u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\right)^{2},\end{split}

with
C_{8}=4(\tilde{C}_{0}+\tilde{C}_{7}+1)
. Integrating from 0 to
t
, using Minkowski inequality and (
4.9
), taking
B_{2}\geq(C_{7}T)^{3}+1
, we obtain for all
t\in[0,T]

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}d\tau\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}d\tau\\
&\leq\left(4kT+C_{8}T^{2}\right)\left(B_{2}^{k}(k-1)!\right)^{2}+4\left((4+(C_{7}T)^{2})B_{2}^{k}k!\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\tau\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}d\tau.\end{split}

Since
e^{t\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, by using Taylor Formula it follows that there exists a constant
\tilde{A}>0
such that

\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq t^{k}\tilde{A}^{k+1}k!,

and therefore taking

B_{2}\geq\max\left\{2\sqrt{T+C_{8}T^{2}+(4+(C_{7}T)^{2})^{2}+T\tilde{A}^{2}}+1,(C_{7}T)^{3}+1,B_{0},\tilde{A}T+1\right\},

we get for all
0<t\leq T
,

\begin{split}&\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{(L^{\infty}]0,T];L^{2})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}dt\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(t\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma/2+s}\right)^{k}u\right\|^{2}_{2,\gamma/2+s}dt\leq\left(B_{2}^{k+1}k!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

∎

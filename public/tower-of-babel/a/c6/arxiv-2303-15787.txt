A groupoid approach to the Wodzicki residue
Nathan Couchet, Robert Yuncken

Abstract
Originally, the noncommutative residue was studied in the 80’s by Wodzicki in his thesis [ 33 ] and Guillemin [ 18 ] . In this article we give a definition of the Wodzicki residue, using the langage of r r -fibered distributions from [ 24 ] , [ 30 ] , in the context of filtered manifolds. We show that this groupoidal residue behaves like a trace on the algebra of pseudodifferential operators on filtered manifolds and coincides with the usual residue Wodzicki in the case where the manifold is trivially filtered. Moreover, we show that the groupoidal residue coincides with Ponge’s definition [ 25 ] for contact and codimension 1 foliation Heisenberg manifolds and Dave-Haller’s definition for general filtered manifolds.



Keywords
: Wodzicki residue, pseudodifferential calculus, classical symbol, tangent groupoid, filtered manifold, non-commutative geometry.

Primary: 47G30; secondary: 22A22, 35S05, 58H05, 58J40.

Introduction.

One of the remarkable features of the theory of pseudodifferential operators is the noncommutative residue of Wodzicki, which he defined in 1984 in his thesis

Let us very briefly recall the groupoid approach to pseudodifferential operators, first observed by Debord-Skandalis
[

in 2014 and developped by van Erp and the second author some years later

\mathbb{T}M=M\times M\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\bigcup TM\times\{0\},

(1.6)

The bundle of osculating groups admits a family of automorphism

Using these dilations we obtain a smooth

]

Let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{s}(y,x,t)=(y,x,s^{-1}t)\penalty\ \penalty\ (x,y)\in M,\\
\alpha_{s}(x,\xi,0)=(x,\delta_{s}(\xi),0)\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in M,\penalty\ \xi\in\mathcal{T}_{H}M_{x}.\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

The key point in the groupoidal approach to pseudodifferential operators
[

is to consider

which are essentially homogeneous for the Debord-Skandalis action in the following sense. A

-fibered distribution on

from

to

-fibers,

\mathbbm{k}(x,t)\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(r^{-1}(x,t))

r
-proper. We say that a

-fibered distribution is properly supported if its support is also

-proper.

Recall that

denotes the proper smooth sections of the 1-density bundle tangent to the

-fibers, see

We say that a properly supported

. The set of essentially homogeneous distributions of this kind is denoted

One of the main theorems of the article

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\text{H\"{o}r}}^{m}(M)=\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{H}^{m}(M).

(1.9)

4.40
) by
\overline{k}(x,x-y)
. Then
\overline{P}=Op(\overline{k})\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{\text{H\"{o}r}}^{m-1}(M)
and using what we did just before,
Res_{x}^{W}(\overline{P})=Res_{x}(\overline{P})
for all
x\in M
. It suffices to apply Lemma
4.2
to the function
k_{0}\in\Psi\mathrm{hf}_{G}^{0}
to conclude, as we have
k(x,z)=\overline{k}(x,z)+\chi(z)k_{0}(x,z).

∎

5
The noncommutative residue on a filtered manifold

In his article
[
25
]
, Ponge defined a noncommutative residue that fits the context of a Heisenberg manifold.
This was generalized to arbitrary filtered manifolds by Dave and Haller in
[
10
]
.
In this section, we will show that their definitions coincide with the groupoidal residue of Definition
1.4
for pseudodifferential operators of order
\leq-d_{H}
.
Again, we will restrict our attention to scalar-valued operators to simplify notation, although one can easily generalise to vector bundles using (
1.13
).

Let us begin with Ponge’s noncommutative residue for a Heisenberg manifold.
Let
M
be a Heisenberg manifold of dimension
d+1
with hyperplane bundle
\mathcal{V}\leq TM
. The algebra of Heisenberg pseudodifferential operators of BG
[
3
]
is denoted
\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\bullet}(M)
.
It is shown in
[
9
]
that this coincides with the groupoidal calculus when
M=\mathbb{H}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbb{H}_{n}
being the
2n+1
dimensional Heisenberg group, or
M
is a contact manifold or a codimensional one foliation. That is:

\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{m}(M)=\mathbb{\Psi}_{H}^{m}(M),

(5.1)

for
M
in these cases, though we expect it to be true also for a general Heisenberg manifold.

Ponge’s noncommutative residue is defined as follows.
Let
\Big(X_{j}\Big)_{j\in\{0,...,d\}}
be a local
H
-frame of vector fields on an open subset
U\subset M
and
\Psi_{x}:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a privileged change of coordinates centered at
x
, see
[
25
, p 415 and Definitions 2.3 ,2.4]
. The latter assertion means that if
\Big(X_{j}\Big)_{j\in\{0,...,d\}}
is a local
H
-frame of vector fields, then we have
\Psi_{x}(x)=0
and
(\Psi_{x})_{*}X_{j}(x)=\partial_{j}|_{x}
. In this context, the noncommutative residue of Ponge of a Heisenberg pseudodifferential operator
P\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{-d_{H}}(U)
of degree
-d_{H}
on
U
, is defined as follows.

Let
p_{-d-2}
be the term of degree
-d_{H}=-(d+2)
from the asymptotic expansion of the symbol of
P\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{-d_{H}}(U)
, see
[
25
, 2.7 p409]
. Then set the noncommutative residue of
P
at
x
:

c_{P}(x)=\frac{|d\Psi_{x}|}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}p_{-(d+2)}(x,\xi)d\xi,

(5.2)

where
|d\Psi_{x}|
is the jacobian of
\Psi_{x}
, see
[
25
, Lemma 3.9]
.
The reader can compare this definition in contrast with the "non-graded" non commutative residue (
1.3
).

We will first give a direct proof of the equality of the groupoidal residue and Ponge’s residue on a contact manifold or foliation. Note that we will give a more general result afterwards in Theorem
5.3
by taking advantage of results of Dave-Haller
[
10
]
.

We begin with the case of the model groups
M=\mathbb{H}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, where
d=2n+m
. If
n=0
then
\mathbb{H}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
by convention. We equip
M
with the model vector fields
\overline{X}=(X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{d})
of
[
3
, chapter 1 p12-13]
, also see
[
9
, section 5.1]
, so that
(X_{0},...,X_{2n})
generate
\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
X_{0}
is central and
(X_{2n+1},...,X_{d})
are the usual vectors fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Recall that
\mathcal{E}_{r}^{\prime}(M\times M)
denotes the set of
r
-fibered distributions on the pair groupoid
M\times M
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given a model manifold
M=\mathbb{H}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
of homogeneous dimension
d_{H}=d+2
,
2n+m=d
, with the standard model structure as in
[
9
]
and
P\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{m}(M)
, with
m\leq-d_{H}
, then the residue of Ponge at
x
and our residue from Definition
1.4
coincide:

Res_{x}(P)=c_{P}(x).

(5.3)

Proof.

We will rely heavily on
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
. We denote
k\in\mathcal{E}_{r}^{\prime}(M\times M)
the kernel of
P
. Since the Heisenberg and groupoidal calculi coincide, see
[
9
, Theorem 5.16]
, there exists
\mathbbm{k}\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{vEY}^{-d_{H}}(\mathbb{T}_{H}M)
such that
\mathbbm{k}|_{t=1}=k
. Moreover we may suppose, see
[
30
, proposition 42]
that
\mathbbm{k}
is homogeneous on the nose of order
-d_{H}
outside
t\in[-1,1]
.
We pull this back via exponential coordinates, again as in
[
9
, section 5]
, yielding
\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}\in\mathcal{E}_{r}^{\prime}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
with the equality
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}_{*}\Big(\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}\Big)
. Note that in the case of model manifold, the exponential coordinate chart is globally define.

Next we must consider the symbol of
P
. By the definition of the Heisenberg calculus,
[
3
, Chapter 3, § 10]
,
P
is defined starting from a graded-polyhomogeneous function
f\in S_{phg,G}^{-d_{H}}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Letting
\overline{\sigma}(x,\xi)=(x,\sigma_{0}(x,\xi),...,\sigma_{d}(x,\xi))
be the coordinate transform
[
3
, Equations (10.14),(10.15)]
obtained from the symbols of the model vector fields
(X_{0},...,X_{d})
, Beals and Greiner define the
\mathcal{V}
-symbol associated to
f
by:

q(x,\xi)=\overline{\sigma}^{*}f(x,\xi).

(5.4)

See also
[
9
, Definition 5.5]
.
The symbol and kernel are related by fiberwise Fourier transform, after the abovementioned coordinate changes. Explicitly, we have:

f=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\tilde{\mathbbm{k}})|_{t=1},

(5.5)

where
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the fiberwise Fourier transform in the second variable. Extending this fiberwise Fourier transform to all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let us put:

u=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\tilde{\mathbbm{k}})\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}).

(5.6)

Note that the Debord-Skandalis action transforms under the Fourier transform as:

\mathcal{F}_{2}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{s*}\circ\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}=\beta_{s}^{*},

(5.7)

where
\beta_{s}:M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
are the dilations:

\beta_{s}(x,v,t)=(x,\delta_{s}(v),st),

(5.8)

see
[
9
, Proposition .13]
. The essential homogeneity of
\mathbbm{k}
and consequently of
\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}=(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}_{*})^{-1}(\mathbbm{k})
, therefore implies that
u\in\mathcal{H}\mathcal{S}_{G}^{m}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
where the homogeneity modulo Schwartz is with respect to the dilations
\beta_{s}
(thanks to the hypothesis on the homogeneity of
\mathbbm{k}
outside
t\in[-1,1]
), see the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 5.16]
.

Set
u_{0}=u|_{t=0}
. By
[
9
, Proposition 3.2]
we have
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\mathcal{S}_{G}^{-d_{H}}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Therefore by a well-known Lemma, eg
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
, outside a compact neighbourhood containing
0
of
\xi
we may write:

u_{0}=u_{0}^{\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime},

(5.9)

where
u_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{G}^{-d_{H}}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and
u_{0}^{\prime\prime}\in S_{G}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.

We may extend the homogeneous function
u_{0}^{\prime}
as a tempered distribution (not necessarily homogeneous), still denoted
u_{0}^{\prime}
. Indeed, we may first extend
u_{0}^{\prime}
to a distribution such as in
[
3
, section § 15]
,
[
26
, Lemma 3.1]
, or in
[
20
, Theorem 3.2.4]
,
and it is tempered because it has polynomial growth at infinity.
Therefore we can find
u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))=C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
such that the following holds everywhere:

u_{0}=u_{0}^{\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}.

(5.10)

We can compute for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
:

\displaystyle Res_{x}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\alpha_{s*}\mathbbm{k}-\mathbbm{k}\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\alpha_{s*}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}_{*}\Big(\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}\Big)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}_{*}\Big(\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}\Big)\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\tilde{\alpha}_{s*}\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}-\tilde{\mathbbm{k}}\Big)\circ\Big(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}\Big)^{-1}|_{(x,0,0)},

(5.13)

where we used in (
5.12
) the equality
\tilde{\alpha}_{s}=\Big(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}\Big)^{-1}\circ\alpha_{s}\circ\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}
,
see (
4.5
).
Also recall that
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{xp}^{\overline{X}}|_{M\times\{0\}\times\{0\}}=id_{M\times\{0\}\times\{0\}}
.
We next continue to compute in (
5.13
):

\displaystyle Res_{x}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\tilde{\alpha}_{s*}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(u)-\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(u)\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}u-u\Big)|_{(x,0,0)},

(5.15)

where we use the equality
\mathcal{F}_{2}\circ\tilde{\alpha}_{s*}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}=\beta_{s}^{*}
recalled earlier. Now we use (
5.10
):

\displaystyle Res_{x}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}(u_{0}^{\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})-(u_{0}^{\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}

(5.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}u_{0}^{\prime}-u_{0}^{\prime}+s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}(u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})-(u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}.

(5.17)

Since
u_{0}^{\prime\prime}
is Schwartz class in
\xi
and
u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is compactly supported in
\xi
, their fiberwise Fourier transforms are smooth.

Therefore
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(u_{0}^{\prime\prime})
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})
, can be evaluated at
(x,0,0)
and as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
, we get:

\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}(u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})-(u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+u_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime})\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}=0.

(5.18)

Hence:

Res_{x}(P)=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(s^{d_{H}}\beta_{s}^{*}u_{0}^{\prime}-u_{0}^{\prime}\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}.

(5.19)

Finally, we use
[
25
, Lemma 3.1 p 414 and Equation (3.2)]
which assert that we have for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
:

\beta_{s}^{*}u_{0}^{\prime}=s^{-d_{H}}u^{\prime}_{0}+s^{-d_{H}}\log(s)c_{0}(u_{0}^{\prime})\delta_{0},

(5.20)

where:

c_{0}(u_{0}^{\prime})=\int_{|\xi|=1}u_{0}^{\prime}(x,\xi)d\sigma(\xi).

(5.21)

Then, using (
5.20
), Equation (
5.19
) becomes:

\displaystyle Res_{x}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\Big(\log(s)c_{0}(u_{0}^{\prime})\delta_{0}\Big)|_{(x,0,0)}

(5.22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}\int_{|\xi|=1}u_{0}^{\prime}(x,\xi)d\sigma(\xi),

(5.23)

where the constant
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}
appears in the inverse Fourier transform formula.
Now, thanks to
[
3
, Equation (3.25) p 27]
we see that:

{}^{t}\Big(d\Psi_{x}\Big)\xi=\sigma(x,\xi),

(5.24)

where
\sigma
is defined in
[
9
, Equation (5.12)]
. The reader can compute the Jacobian of
\Psi_{x}
and see that it is triangular and unipotent and so has determinant equal to one, see also
[
8
]
.

According to
[
9
, the proof p8 of Theorem 1.12]
, the purely homogeneous component
u_{0}^{\prime}
of
u_{0}
is the first term in the asymptotic expansion of the polyhomogeneous function
f=u|_{t=1}
, see
[
9
, Equation (3.16)]
and the remarks which follow. Therefore:

u_{0}^{\prime}(x,\xi)=p_{-(d+2)}(x,\xi),

(5.25)

where
p_{-(d+2)}
is the term of degree
-(d+2)
in the asymptotic expansion of
f=u|_{t=1}
.
We have shown that :

Res_{x}(P)=\frac{|d\Psi_{x}|}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}\int_{|\xi|=1}p_{-(d+2)}(x,\xi)d\sigma(\xi).

(5.26)

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

If
M
is a contact manifold or a codimensional one foliation, then the groupoidal residue of Definition
1.4
agree with’s Ponge noncommutative residue for operators
P\in\mathbb{\Psi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{-d_{H}}(M)
, meaning that (
5.3
) still holds.

Proof.

Darboux’ Theorem for a contact manifold or Frobenius’ Theorem for a codimensional one foliation, implies that around any
x\in M
, there is a local coordinate system which identifies
M
with the model
space
\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
d=2n
, or
\mathbb{H}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since both Ponge’s residue and the groupoidal residue are independent of (priveleged) coordinates, the result follows.
∎

We conclude by proving the equality of the groupoidal residue with Dave and Haller’s noncommutative residue. Let
M
be a closed filtered manifold. Recall that in this context, the appropriate analogue of an elliptic operator is a Rockland operator, as follows. At each point
x\in M
, a differential operator
D
of filtered order
m
has a principal part
\sigma^{m}_{x}(D)
which is a left-invariant homogeneous differential operator on the osculating group
\mathcal{T}_{H}M_{x}
and so an element of the enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{t}_{H}M_{x})
. The operator is Rockland if for every
x\in M
and every non trivial irreducible unitary representation of
\mathcal{T}_{H}M_{x}
, the operator
\pi(\sigma^{m}_{x}(D))
is invertible.

Fix a strictly positive Rockland differential operator
D
on
M
of even order
r>0
with respect to the filtration. If
P\in\Psi^{m}_{H}(M)
is a pseudodifferential operator in the groupoidal calculus of
[
30
]
, then Dave and Haller
[
10
]
prove that the zeta function

\zeta_{P}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}(PD^{-z})

is well-defined for
\Re(z)>(n+k)/r
and admits a meromorphic extension with at most simple poles located at
(d_{H}+m-j)/r
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
. They then define a noncommutative residue
\tau
by

\tau(P)=r.\mathrm{res}_{z=0}(\zeta_{P}(z)).

(5.27)

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
M
be a closed filtered manifold and
P\in\Psi^{m}_{H}(M)
. If the order of
P
is
m\leq-d_{H}
, then the noncommutative residue (
5.27
) of Dave-Haller is equal to the integral of the groupoidal residue of Definition
1.4
,

\tau(P)=\int_{M}Res_{x}(P).

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(M\times M)
be the kernel of
P
. It suffices to consider the case when
m=-d_{H}
, since otherwise both residues are
0
. Thus there is
\mathbbm{k}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(\mathbb{T}_{H}M)
essentially homogeneous of order
m=-d_{H}
for the Debord-Skandalis action, such that
\mathbbm{k}|_{t=1}=k
. The restriction
k_{0}:=\mathbbm{k}|_{t=0}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}(\mathcal{T}_{H}M)
is a fibred distribution which is homogeneous of order
-d_{H}
modulo
C^{\infty}
, with respect to the pushforward of distributions by the graded dilations
\delta_{s}
. Specifically, it is the first term in the pseudo-homogeneous expansion of the kernel
k
, see
e.g.

[
12
, Remark 3.4]
. Interpreting
k_{0}
as a generalized function,
it is pseudohomogeneous function of degree
0
, with the degree shift of
d_{H}
as explained in Remark
2
. Explicitly, this means that

k_{0}(x,\xi)=f_{0}(x,\xi)+\log(|\xi|)p_{0}(x)

for some
f_{0}
homogeneous of order
0
and
p_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, see (
3.5
).

Now, according to Dave-Haller
[
10
, Corollary 6(d)]
,

\tau(P)=\int_{M}p_{0}(x)\,dx.

On the other hand, a direct calculation of the cocycle (
1.8
) shows that the groupoid residue at a point
x\in M
is given by

Res_{x}P=\frac{1}{\log(s)}\left(\log|\delta_{s}(\xi)|\,p_{0}(x)-\log|\xi|\,p_{0}(x)\right)|_{\xi=0}=p_{0}(x),

in an analogous fashion to the proof of Lemma
4.2
, see Equation (
4.21
) and following.
The result follows.
∎

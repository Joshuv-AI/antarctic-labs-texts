Random Polynomial Graphs for Random Turán Problems
Sam Spiro

Abstract
Bukh and Conlon used random polynomial graphs to give effective lower bounds on ex ⁡ ( n , 𝒯 ℓ ) \mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{T}^{\ell}) , where 𝒯 ℓ \mathcal{T}^{\ell} is the ℓ \ell th power of a balanced rooted tree T T . We extend their result to give effective lower bounds on ex ⁡ ( G n , p , 𝒯 ℓ ) \mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},\mathcal{T}^{\ell}) , which is the maximum number of edges in a 𝒯 ℓ \mathcal{T}^{\ell} -free subgraph of the random graph G n , p G_{n,p} . Analogous bounds for generalized Turán numbers in random graphs are also proven.



Keywords:
Turán numbers; Random graphs; Generalized Turán problems; Extremal combinatorics.

Given a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}

The bound

[

m(H)=\max\left\{\frac{e(H^{\prime})}{v(H^{\prime})}:H^{\prime}\subseteq H\right\},

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
H
be a graph with
v

e

(T,R)

\rho(T)\geq\frac{b}{a}

\ell_{0}

\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, we have for all

with

and

that a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},H,\mathcal{T}^{\ell})=\Omega\left(p^{e-\frac{a}{b}\cdot e}n^{v-\frac{a}{b}\cdot e}\right).

We note that it is easy to show by a first moment argument that
\mathcal{N}_{H}(G_{n,p})=0
with high probability if

, so it is reasonable to only consider

with
p^{m(H)}n\to\infty
.

2
Key Propositions

In this section we state without proof the tools we need in order to prove Theorem
1.3
. These results are somewhat general, and we have hope that they will find applications outside of the present work.

We begin with a generalization of Theorem
1.1
. To state this result; given a graph
F
and a set
R\subsetneq V(F)
, we will say that the pair
(F,R)
is a
rooted graph
and refer to
R
as its set of
roots
. We define
\mathcal{F}^{\ell}
and
\rho(F)
exactly analogous to how these were defined for rooted trees.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H
be a graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, and let
\{(F_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(F_{t},R_{t})\}
be a set of rooted graphs such that
\rho(F_{i})\geq\frac{b}{a}
for all
i
with
a,b
positive integers. Then there exists some
\ell_{0}
depending only on
H
and
\{(F_{1},R_{1}),\ldots,(F_{t},R_{t})\}
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
,

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\ell})=\Omega\left(n^{v-\frac{a}{b}\cdot e}\right).

We emphasize that it is straightforward to derive Proposition
2.1
by using the approach of Ma, Yuan, and Zhang
[
31
]
, which itself is based heavily off of the random polynomial approach of Bukh and Conlon
[
7
]
. We include the details for completeness in Section
4
.

Our lasts two results are new and utilize the concept of local isomorphisms. For this definition, we adopt the convention
\phi(S):=\{\phi(x):x\in S\}
whenever
\phi
is a map and
S
is a set.

Definition 1
.

Given two rooted graphs
(F,R),(F^{\prime},R^{\prime})
, we say that a map
\phi:V(F)\to V(F^{\prime})
is a
local isomorphism
if

(a)

The map
\phi
is surjective with
\phi(R)\subseteq R^{\prime}
,

(b)

Every
e\in E(F)
has
\phi(e)\in E(F^{\prime})
(i.e.
\phi
is a homomorphism), and

(c)

Every
e,f\in E(F)
with
e\neq f
and
e\cap f\neq\emptyset
satisfy
\phi(e)\neq\phi(f)
.

Given a rooted graph
(F,R)
, we let
\mathcal{L}(F)
denote the set of rooted graphs
(F^{\prime},R^{\prime})
such that there exists a local isomorphism
\phi:V(F)\to V(F^{\prime})
, and given a set of rooted graphs
\mathcal{F}
, we define
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{L}(F)
.

Our main motivation for these definitions is the following result for graphs without roots, which allows us to transfer lower bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(m,H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}))
to lower bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(G,H,\mathcal{F})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G,H
be graphs, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of graphs. If
m
is an integer satisfying
m\geq 4|E(H)|\Delta
where
\Delta
is the maximum degree of
G
, then there exists a
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
which is
\mathcal{F}
-free such that

\mathcal{N}_{H}(G^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ex}(m,H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}))m^{-v(H)}\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H}(G).

We anticipate that Proposition
2.2
will be quite useful in obtaining lower bounds for generalized relative Turán numbers. With this result in hand, we see that to prove Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to prove effective lower bounds on
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell}))
. We will do this by utilizing Proposition
2.1
together with the following result, which says that the rooted density of a forest can only increase after taking local isomorphisms.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
(T,R_{T})
is a rooted forest and
(F,R_{F})\in\mathcal{L}(T)
, then
\rho(T)\leq\rho(F)
.

We will only apply Proposition
2.3
when
T
is a tree, but its proof is somewhat more natural in the setting of forests.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We postpone proving the results of the previous section for the moment, and instead show how these propositions quickly imply our main result. We begin with a deterministic result

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H
be a graph with
v
vertices and
e
edges, and let
(T,R_{T})
be a rooted tree such that
\rho(T)\geq\frac{b}{a}
with
a,b
positive integers. Then there exists some
\ell_{0}
depending only on
H
and
(T,R_{T})
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
,

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell}))=\Omega\left(n^{v-\frac{a}{b}\cdot e}\right).

For this proof and throughout the paper, given a graph
G
and a set of vertices
S
, we define
G[S]
to be the induced subgraph of
G
with vertex set
S
.

Proof.

Let
c
denote the number of rooted graphs that are in
\mathcal{L}(T)
up to isomorphism, and observe that
c<\infty
because local isomorphisms are in particular surjective homomorphisms. Let
\ell_{*}:=\left\lfloor\ell/c\right\rfloor
, noting that we can take
\ell_{*}
to be arbitrarily large by taking
\ell
to be arbitrarily large.

We claim that for every
F\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})
, there exists some rooted graph
F_{*}\in\mathcal{L}(T)
such that
F
contains an element of
\mathcal{F}_{*}^{\ell_{*}}
as a subgraph. Assuming this claim, we see that for a graph to be
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})
-free, it suffices for it to be
\bigcup_{F_{*}\in\mathcal{L}(T)}\mathcal{F}_{*}^{\ell_{*}}
-free. Note that
\rho(F_{*})\geq\frac{b}{a}
for all
F_{*}\in\mathcal{L}(T)
by Proposition
2.3
, so by Proposition
2.1
we have for
\ell
sufficiently large that

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell}))\geq\mathrm{ex}(n,H,\bigcup_{F_{*}\in\mathcal{L}(T)}\mathcal{F}_{*}^{\ell_{*}})=\Omega\left(n^{v-\frac{a}{b}\cdot e}\right).

It remains to prove this claim.

By definition of the set
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}
, if
F\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})
, then there exists a local isomorphism
\phi:V(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}T_{i})\to V(F)
where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
are
\ell
distinct copies of
T
which agree on
R_{T}
. Define the induced subgraph
F_{i}=F[\phi(V(T_{i}))]
for all
i
. Note that the restriction
\phi:V(T_{i})\to V(F_{i})
is a local isomorphism from
(T_{i},R_{T})
to
(F_{i},\phi(R))
because
\phi
is a local isomorphism. By the pigeonhole principle and the definition of
\ell_{*}
, there exists some
(F_{*},R_{*})\in\mathcal{L}(T)
such that at least
\ell_{*}
different integers
i
have
(F_{i},\phi(R))
isomorphic to
(F_{*},R_{*})
. Let
I
denote the set of these
i
. By definition,
\bigcup_{i\in I}F_{i}
is an element of
\mathcal{F}_{*}^{\ell_{*}}
and also a subgraph of
F
, so we conclude the claim and hence the result.
∎

We can now prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
A
denote the event that
\mathcal{N}_{H}(G_{n,p})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}_{H}(G_{n,p})]=\Theta(p^{e}n^{v})
and that

has maximum degree at most

Proof of Proposition
2.1

We again emphasize that our proof of Proposition
2.1
is nearly identical to that of Ma, Yuan, and Zhang
[
31
]
and of Bukh and Conlon
[
7
]
.
We begin with a simple combinatorial observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every rooted graph
(F,R)
and integer
\ell\geq 1
, we have that every
K\in\mathcal{F}^{\ell}
satisfies

e(K)\geq\rho(F)(v(K)-|R|)

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
\ell
. If
\ell=1
then
K=F
, so the claimed inequality is equivalent to having

\frac{e(F)}{v(F)-|R|}\geq\rho(F):=\min_{S\subseteq V(F)\setminus R}\frac{e_{S}}{|S|},

where we recall that
e_{S}
is the number of edges of
F
incident to a vertex of
S
. This inequality is trivially true, proving the base case.

Assume now that we proven the result up to some value
\ell>1
, and let
K\in\mathcal{F}^{\ell}
. This means
K
can be written as the union of
\ell
labeled copies of
F
, say
F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}
, such that each of these agree on the set of roots
R
. Let
K^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{i<\ell}F_{i}
and let
S
be the set of vertices in
F_{\ell}
which are not in
K^{\prime}
, noting that necessarily
S\subseteq V(F)\setminus R
. By definition of
\rho(F)
, we must have
e_{S}\geq\rho(F)|S|
. Using this and our inductive hypothesis gives

e(K)=e(K^{\prime})+e_{S}\geq\rho(F)(v(K^{\prime})-|R|)+\rho(F)|S|=\rho(F)(v(K)-|R|),

giving the result.
∎

We now introduce some algebraic definitions and lemmas. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q

\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathbf{X}^{1},\mathbf{X}^{2}]
with
\mathbf{X}^{j}=(X^{j}_{1},\ldots,X_{b}^{j})
to denote the set of polynomials with variables
X_{i}^{j}
which have coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Given such a polynomial
f
and

, we define
f(x^{1},x^{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, by replacing each
X_{i}^{j}
symbol in
f
with
x_{i}^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

We say that a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathbf{X}^{1},\mathbf{X}^{2}]
has degree at most
d
in
\mathbf{X}^{j}
if each of its monomials with respect to
\mathbf{X}^{j}
, say
(X^{j}_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(X_{b}^{j})^{\alpha_{b}}
, satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{b}\alpha_{i}\leq d
. We say that such a polynomial is

if
f(x^{1},x^{2})=f(x^{2},x^{1})
for all
x^{1},x^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{d,2b}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathbf{X}^{1},\mathbf{X}^{2}]
denote the set of all pseudo-symmetric polynomials in
2b
variables which have degree at most
d
in
\mathbf{X}^{j}
for all
j
.

Lemma 4.2

(

Let

, and if

(

.

Let

be varieties over

of complexity at most

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If

, which follows from every such
F
containing a cycle.
, proving the lower bound for this range.

For
p\ll n^{-1/m_{2}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})}
we use a somewhat more careful argument based off of
[
33
]
. Define
F^{\prime}
and
X_{F}
as above. Let
\omega_{F}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[e(G_{n,p})]}{\mathbb{E}[X_{F}]}
, and by similar reasoning as above we have
\omega_{F}\to\infty
. Choose
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)
any function such that
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\varepsilon\omega_{F}\to\infty
for all
F
. By Markov’s inequality,

\Pr\left[X_{F}\geq\varepsilon p{n\choose 2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon\omega_{F}}\to 0.

Thus a.a.s.
X_{F}=o(p{n\choose 2})=o(\mathbb{E}[e(G_{n,p})])
. By the Chernoff bound,
e(G_{n,p})\geq(1-o(1))p{n\choose 2}
a.a.s. for
p\gg n^{-2}
. Thus by deleting an edge from each copy counted by some
X_{F}
, we obtain a
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}
-free subgraph with
(1-o(1))p{n\choose 2}
edges a.a.s.
∎

We say a rooted tree
(T,R)
is
balanced
if
\rho(T)=\frac{e(T)}{v(T)-|R|}
. It is easy to show
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{T}^{\ell})=O(n^{2-1/\rho(T)})
whenever
(T,R)
is balanced (see
[
7
]
), and in particular the lower bound of Corollary
6.1
is tight at
p=1
for balanced trees. It is natural to ask if this lower bound is tight for all
p
, possibly up to some extra logarithmic factors.

Question 6.2
.

Which balanced trees
T
are such that for all
\ell
, there exists a constant
c=c(\ell)
such that for all
p
we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},\mathcal{T}^{\ell})=O_{\ell}\left(\max\left\{p^{1-\frac{1}{\rho(T)}}n^{2-\frac{1}{\rho(T)}},n^{2-\frac{1}{m_{2}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})}}(\log n)^{c}\right\}\right).

In fact, this upper bound might hold even if one only forbids a single element of
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}
. To this end, given a rooted graph
(F,R)
, we let
F^{\ell}
denote the element of
\mathcal{F}^{\ell}
with the maximum number of vertices, i.e. the graph consisting of
\ell
copies of
F
which agree only at vertices of
R
.

Question 6.3
.

Which balanced trees
T
are such that for all
\ell
, there exists a constant
c=c(\ell)
such that for all
p
we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},T^{\ell})=O_{\ell}\left(\max\left\{p^{1-\frac{1}{\rho(T)}}n^{2-\frac{1}{\rho(T)}},n^{2-\frac{1}{m_{2}(T^{\ell})}}(\log n)^{c}\right\}\right).

Question
6.3
can be viewed as a significant generalization of a conjecture of Bukh and Conlon
[
7
]
, which asserts that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{\ell})=\Theta(n^{2-1/\rho(T)})
if
T
is balanced and
\ell
is sufficiently large. This conjecture of Bukh and Conlon is unsolved despite receiving a massive amount of attention, and as such it seems hopeless to answer Question
6.3
in general. However, it is plausible that Question
6.3
could be solved for trees
T
which are known to satisfy Bukh and Conlon’s conjecture. There are many such
T
, see for example
[
12
,
13
,
25
,
26
,
28
,
29
]
.

The only positive answers to Questions
6.2
and
6.3
that we are aware of come from work of Morris and Saxton
[
32
]
who gave a positive answer to Question
6.3
when
T
is a star and
R
is its set of leaves. They also gave a positive answer to Question
6.3
when
T
is a path and
R
is its set of leaves, but they did this only for
\ell=2
. In forthcoming work with McKinley, we extend this result of Morris and Saxton to give a positive answer to Question
6.3
for all
\ell
when
T
is a path.

The proofs of the results mentioned above all rely on the method of hypergraph containers together with a form of “balanced supersaturation” (which roughly say that if
G
has
e(G)\gg n^{2-1/\rho(T)}
, then
G
has about as many copies of
T^{\ell}
as one would expect in a random graph, and that these copies are “spread out” in
G
). It is possible that some of the current proofs showing
\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{\ell})=O(n^{2-1/\rho(T)})
for various graphs
T
can be extended to give supersaturation, but we suspect that balanced supersaturation will be difficult. Balanced supersaturation might be easier to prove for the family
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}
since here supersaturation is very easy to prove, but we are unaware of how to show that these copies are sufficiently spread out.

In addition to the difficulties mentioned above, extra complications may arise whenever we do not have
m_{2}(T^{\ell})=\frac{e(T^{\ell})-1}{v(T^{\ell})-2}
. This can happen even for relatively simple
T
. For example, let
T
be the tree obtained by subdividing an edge of
K_{1,3}
, and let
R
be its set of leaves. Note that
(T,R)
is a balanced tree. Because
T^{\ell}
contains
K_{2,\ell}
as a subgraph, we have for
\ell\geq 2
that

m_{2}(T^{\ell})\geq\frac{e(K_{2,\ell})-1}{v(K_{2,\ell})-2}=\frac{2\ell-1}{\ell}>\frac{4\ell-1}{2\ell+1}=\frac{e(T^{\ell})-1}{v(T^{\ell})-2}.

Perhaps the reason this phenomenon occurs is because
(T,R)
is balanced but not “strictly balanced” in the sense that
\rho(T)=\frac{e_{S}}{|S|}
for some
S\subsetneq V(T)\setminus R
, and it is possible that the problem is easiest for trees which do not exhibit this behavior.

Generalized Turán problems in random graphs.
Because Question
6.3
is unlikely to be solved in general, it might be more tractable to look at generalized Turán problems in random graphs for trees
T
where positive answers to Questions
6.2
or
6.3
are known. For example, we ask the following.

Question 6.4
.

What is the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},K_{a,b},K_{s,t})
when
t
is sufficiently large in terms of
a,b,s
?

Note that Theorem
1.3
, together with the fact that
\rho(K_{1,s})=s
when
R
is the set of leaves, gives
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},K_{a,b},K_{s,t})=\Omega(p^{ab-ab/s}n^{a+b-ab/s})
a.a.s., though a better bound for small
p
can be obtained by a deletion argument. When
p=1
this bound was shown to be tight by Ma, Yuan, and Zhang
[
31
]
for all
a,b,s
. Morris and Saxton
[
32
]
gave a (relatively) easy argument showing that when
a=b=1
, the lower bound from Theorem
1.3
together with a deletion argument establishes the correct bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},K_{a,b},K_{s,t})
. It is plausible that an adaptation of these approaches could be used to give tight results for all
a,b,s,p
.

Hypergraphs.
For ease of presentation we only stated our results in terms of graphs, but everything we did easily extends to
r
-uniform hypergraphs. We briefly discuss how this works.

The notions of rooted hypergraphs, densities, local isomorphisms, and so forth, are defined exactly analogous to how these are defined in the setting of graphs. Here the hypothesis of Proposition
2.2
must change to have
m\gg\Delta_{i}^{1/(r-i)}
for all
i
, where
\Delta_{i}
is the maximum
i
-degree of the
r
-graph
G
, and the current proof can easily be adapted to this new setting using an approach similar to that of
[
40
, Proposition 2.1]
. The statement and proof of Proposition
2.1
is essentially word for word the same as it is currently written.

The only minor complication arises with Proposition
2.3
, and in particular what it means for a hypergraph to be a tree. For our current proof to go through, we define an
r
-uniform forest to be an
r
-graph such that one can order its edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
in such a way that
|e_{i}\cap\bigcup_{j<i}e_{j}|\leq 1
for all
i
. Given this, one can prove an analog of Lemma
5.1
by letting
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-1}\in e_{m}
be the vertices of degree 1 of some minimal counterexample
T
, then arguing that
\phi:V(T-\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-1}\})\to V(F)
is still a local isomorphism, and then arguing more or less as the proof is currently written.

In total this approach will show
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},H,\mathcal{T}^{\ell})=\Omega(n^{v-\frac{a}{b}e})
when
T
is an
r
-uniform tree and
\ell
is sufficiently large. Unfortunately this bound is very weak in general. Indeed, if
T
is a loose path with three edges and
R
is a set of two leaves, then this bound gives
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{T}^{\ell})=\Omega(n^{r-\frac{3r-4}{3}})=\Omega(n^{4/3})
. However, it is easy to show
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{T}^{\ell})\geq\mathrm{ex}(n,T)=\Omega(n^{r-1})
by simply taking every edge containing a given vertex. To give a lower bound which is actually effective, one should instead prove

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},H,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell}))=\Omega(n^{v-\frac{a}{b}e}),

where now we forbid all of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})
instead of just
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}
.
Such a bound can easily be shown using the current argument after noting that the
G^{\prime}
obtained from Proposition
2.2
is in fact
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
-free (this follows from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})
, which follows from the fact that the composition of local isomorphisms is again a local isomorphism). If
T
is a loose path on three edges, then
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell})
contains the set of Berge theta graphs with paths of length 3, and from this one can conclude
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^{\ell}))=O(n^{4/3})
, matching the lower bound given by this approach. In particular, this approach recovers the lower bounds for the Turán number of berge theta graphs obtained by He and Tait
[
23
]
.

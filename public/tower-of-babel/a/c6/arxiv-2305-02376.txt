Abstract
. In this article, we establish the Wong-Zakai approximation result for a class of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with fully local monotone coefficients perturbed by a multiplicative Wiener noise. This class of SPDEs encompasses various fluid dynamic models and also includes quasi-linear SPDEs, the convection-diffusion equation, the Cahn-Hilliard equation, and the two-dimensional liquid crystal model. It has been established that the class of SPDEs in question is well-posed, however, the existence of a unique solution to the associated approximating system cannot be inferred from the solvability of the original system. We employ a Faedo-Galerkin approximation method, compactness arguments, and Prokhorov’s and Skorokhod’s representation theorems to ensure the existence of a probabilistically weak solution for the approximating system. Furthermore, we also demonstrate that the solution is pathwise unique. Moreover, the classical Yamada-Watanabe theorem allows us to conclude the existence of a probabilistically strong solution (analytically weak solution) for the approximating system. Subsequently, we establish the Wong-Zakai approximation result for a class of SPDEs with fully local monotone coefficients. We utilize the Wong-Zakai approximation to establish the topological support of the distribution of solutions to the SPDEs with fully local monotone coefficients. Finally, we explore the physically relevant stochastic fluid dynamics models that are covered by this work’s functional framework.

Introduction

1.1.
The model and literature

Approximating solutions to stochastic differential equations (SDEs) can be achieved through various methods, including the Euler scheme, the Crank-Nicholson scheme, and the Wong-Zakai approximation, etc., see the works
[
23
,
24

59

Due to the continuous yet nondifferentiable nature of Brownian motion, there is a need for an approximation that can capture its properties in some way.
In 1965, E. Wong and M. Zakai proposed a novel scheme for approximating the solution of an SDE perturbed by a one-dimensional Brownian motion. This approach involved replacing the Brownian motion with a suitable smooth approximation and making a drift correction in the original SDE (see
[
59
]
). This method is now commonly known as the
Wong-Zakai approximation
. The extension of the Wong-Zakai approximation to the multi-dimensional case was achieved in
[
60
]
, building upon the original work of
[
59
]
. Moreover, the Wong-Zakai approximation has received widespread attention in infinite-dimensional cases as well, as evidenced by the works
[
13
,
21
,
56
,
57
,
58
]
, etc., which discuss its application to stochastic (partial) differential equations driven by infinite-dimensional noise. Notably, the author in
[
58
]
provided an extensive overview of the Wong-Zakai approximation for various types of SDEs, including SDEs with finite-dimensional state and noise, SDEs with infinite-dimensional state and finite-dimensional noise, and SDEs with infinite-dimensional state and noise, etc.
Furthermore,
[
58
]
(and
[
57
]
) also introduced two new types of correction terms in the Wong-Zakai approximation.

The Wong-Zakai approximation studied for two-dimensional hydrodynamical models, such as the magnetic Bénard problem, Navier-Stokes equations, and magneto-hydrodynamic equations, in
[
11
]
. In
[

1.2.
Difficulties and approaches

As discussed already, the existence of
a unique probabilistically strong solution
to the system (
1.1
) with fully local monotone coefficients (see Hypothesis
2.1
below) has been established in a recent paper
[
52
]
. The unique solution for the approximating system (see (
2.6
) below) cannot be concluded from the solvability of the original system, as it requires estimating the drift parts
\mathrm{A}
and
\sigma\dot{\mathrm{W}}^{m}
in (
2.6
) separately. This is due to the fact that the approximating system is considered as a pathwise deterministic system (the diffusion coefficient is
0
).
The solvability of the system that approximates SPDEs with locally monotone coefficients relies on compactness and monotonicity criteria. However, the aforementioned approach is not immediately applicable for SPDEs with fully local monotone coefficients. We demonstrate the existence of a unique, probabilistically strong solution of the system (
2.6
) using compactness principles combined with techniques from pseudo-monotone operator theory.

It is noteworthy that we take into account a general stochastic system (shown in (
2.16
)) in our analysis, which encompasses the system (
2.6
) and thereby allows us to demonstrate the topological support of the distribution corresponding to the solution of (
1.1
). The proof of solvability result of the system (
2.16
) is based on a standard Faedo-Galerkin approximation technique. We first derive uniform moment estimates for the Galerkin approximating solutions (as displayed in Lemma
3.1
), and then establish the tightness of the laws of these solutions in appropriate spaces (as mentioned in Lemma
3.2
). Utilizing Prokhorov’s theorem (see
[
3
, Section 5]
) and Skorokhod’s representation theorem (see
[
5
, Theorem C.1]
) along with the pseudo-monotonicity of the mapping

, we further show that system (
2.16
) admits a probabilistically weak solution. Further, we demonstrate pathwise uniqueness of the solutions to the system (
2.16
) (as presented in Theorem
3.8
). Finally, the classical Yamada-Watanabe theorem (
[

) guarantees the existence of a unique probabilistically strong solution to the system (
2.16

Note that the approximating system does not contain any diffusion term (see (
2.6
) below), which leads to difficulty in estimating the difference between the solutions of the original and the approximating systems while proving the Wong-Zakai approximation result for the system (
1.1
). We use the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\lfloor\frac{t}{\varpi}\rfloor}\int_{l\varpi}^{(l+1)\varpi\land t}
for
t\in[0,T],

where
\varpi=\frac{T}{2^{m}}

\mathrm{W}(\cdot)
(see (

which implies

For

(2.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}}_{m}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H},\ \text{ by }\ \widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}}_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{D}\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}),\ \ \text{ for all }\ \ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{H}.

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathbf{Y}^{m}(t)&=\mathrm{A}(t,\mathbf{Y}^{m}(t))\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mathbf{Y}^{m}(t))\dot{\mathrm{W}}^{m}(t)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}}_{m}(\mathbf{Y}^{m}(t))\mathrm{d}t,\\

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\boldsymbol{g}}^{m_{0}}\ll\bar{\mathbb{P}}
, which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big\{\|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{Z}_{\boldsymbol{g}}\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\mu\big\}=\bar{\mathbb{P}}\big\{\omega:\|\mathcal{A}_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}\circ\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{0}(\omega),\omega)-\mathbf{Z}_{\boldsymbol{g}}\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\mu\big\}>0.

Thus,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathbb{P}\circ\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\big)\supset\bar{\mathcal{L}}.

Combining (
5.9
) and (
5.13
), we obtain the required result, that is,
\mathrm{supp}\big(\mathbb{P}\circ\mathbf{Y}^{-1}\big)=\bar{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

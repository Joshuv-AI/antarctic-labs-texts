The Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann/Landau system with polynomial perturbation near Maxwellian
Chuqi Cao, Dingqun Deng, Xingyu Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann system without angular cutoff and the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau/Boltzmann system with polynomial perturbation F = μ + f F=\mu+f near global Maxwellian. In particular, we prove the global existence, uniqueness and large time behavior for solutions in a polynomial weighted space H x , v N ​ ( ⟨ v ⟩ k ) H^{N}_{x,v}(\langle v\rangle^{k}) . The method is based on Duhamel’s principle with the crucial time-decay analysis on the particle distribution f f and the electromagnetic field ( E , B ) (E,B) .

1.
Introduction

The Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann equation reads

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}F_{+}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{+}+(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{+}=Q(F_{+},F_{+})+Q(F_{-},F_{+}),\\
\partial_{t}F_{-}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{-}-(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{-}=Q(F_{+},F_{-})+Q(F_{-},F_{-}),\\
F_{\pm}(0,x,v)=F_{0,\pm}(x,v),\quad E(0,x)=E_{0}(x),\quad B(0,x)=B_{0}(x)\end{cases}

where
E,B
satisfies

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}E-\nabla_{x}\times B=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(F_{+}-F_{-})dv,\quad\partial_{t}B+\nabla_{x}\times E=0,\\
&\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(F_{+}-F_{-})dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
F(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the distribution function of colliding particles at time
t>0
and position
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
, moving with at velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
[E(t,x),B(t,x)]
is the self-consistent, spatially periodic electromagnetic field coupled to
F(t,x,v)
.

1.0.1.
Boltzmann collision operator

The Boltzmann collision operator
Q
is a bilinear operator which acts only on the velocity variable
v
given by

Q(G,F)(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)(G^{\prime}_{*}F^{\prime}-G_{*}F)d\sigma dv_{*}.

Let us give some explanations on the collision operator.

(1)

We use the standard shorthand
F=F(v),G_{*}=G(v_{*}),F^{\prime}=F(v^{\prime}),G^{\prime}_{*}=G(v^{\prime}_{*})
, where
v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*}
are given by

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad v_{*}^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

This representation follows the parametrization of the set of solutions of the physical law of elastic collision:

v+v_{*}=v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*},\quad|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}=|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}.

(2)

The nonnegative function
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
in the collision operator is called the Boltzmann collision kernel. It is assumed depending only on
|v-v_{*}|
and the deviation angle
\theta
through

\cos\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma.

(3)

In the present work, our
basic assumptions on the kernel
B
can be concluded as follows:

\mathbf{(A1).}

The Boltzmann kernel
B
takes the form

B(v-v_{*},\sigma)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}b(\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma)
,
where
b
is a nonnegative function.

\mathbf{(A2).}

The angular function
b(\cos\theta)
is not locally integrable and it satisfies

\mathcal{K}\theta^{-1-2s}\leq\sin\theta b(\cos\theta)\leq\mathcal{K}^{-1}\theta^{-1-2s},~\mbox{with}~0<s<1,~\mathcal{K}>0.

\mathbf{(A3).}

The parameter
\gamma
and
s
satisfy the condition
-3<\gamma\leq 1
and
s\geq 1/2.

\mathbf{(A4).}

Without lose of generality, we may assume that
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
is supported in the set
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
, i.e.
\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma\geq 0
, for otherwise
B
can be replaced by its symmetrized form:

\overline{B}(v-v_{*},\sigma)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\big(b(\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma)+b(\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot(-\sigma))\big)\mathrm{1}_{\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma\geq 0},

where
\mathrm{1}_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
.

1.0.2.
Landau collision operator

We also consider (
1.1
) as the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equation, and
Q
as the Landau operator

(1.3)

\displaystyle Q(g,f)(v)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{v}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi(v-v_{*})(g(v_{*})\nabla_{v}f(v)-f(v)\nabla_{v_{*}}g(v_{*}))dv_{*},

\displaystyle\text{with}\,\,\Phi(u)=|u|^{\gamma+2}\left(I-\frac{u\otimes u}{|u|^{2}}\right),\,\,\gamma\in[-3,1].

The Landau operator (
1.3
) can be equivalently written in the form

Q(g,f)(v)=\partial_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}a_{ij}(v-v_{*})(g_{*}\partial_{j}f-f\partial_{j}g_{*})dv_{*},

where we use the convention of summation of repeated indices, and the derivatives are in the velocity variable
\partial_{i}=\partial_{v_{i}}
. Hereafter we use the shorthand notations
g_{*}=g(v_{*}),f=f(v),\partial_{j}g_{*}=\partial_{v_{*j}}g(v_{*}),\partial_{j}f=\partial_{v_{j}}f(v)
,
and

a_{ij}(v)=|v|^{\gamma+2}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{|v|^{2}}\right),\quad-3\leq\gamma\leq 1.

Then we define

\displaystyle b_{i}(v)=\partial_{j}a_{ij}(v)=-2|v|^{\gamma}v_{i},

\displaystyle c(v)=\partial_{ij}a_{ij}(v)=-2(\gamma+3)|v|^{\gamma},\quad\text{ when }-3<\gamma\leq 1,

\displaystyle c(v)=-8\pi\delta_{0},\quad\text{ when }\gamma=-3,

Then we have
Q(g,f)=\partial_{i}\big((a_{ij}*g)\partial_{j}f-(b_{i}*g)f\big)=(a_{ij}*g)\partial_{ij}f-(c*g)f.

Remark 1.1
.

We will call the cases
\gamma\geq 0
and
\gamma<0
hard potential and soft potential respectively.

1.1.
Basic properties and the perturbation equation

We recall some basic facts on the Boltzmann/Landau equation.

\bullet

Conservation Law.
Formally if
F
is the solution to (
1.1
) with the initial data
F_{0}
, then it enjoys the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, that is,

(1.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{+}dvdx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{-}dvdx=0,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(F_{+}+F_{-})dvdx=0,\,\,

(1.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}(F_{+}+F_{-})dvdx+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|E(t,x)|^{2}+|B(t,x)|^{2}dx=0.

Taking the inner product of (
1.1
) with 1 over
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}
, we get the continuity equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{\pm}dv+\nabla_{x}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vF_{\pm}dv=0,

For simplicity, we assume the initial data
F_{0}
is normalized such that the equilibrium associated with the equation (
1.1
) will be the standard Gaussian function, i.e.

\mu(v)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-|v|^{2}/2},

which enjoys the same mass, momentum, and energy as
F_{0}
.

\bullet

Perturbation Equation.
In the perturbation framework, let
f=[f_{+},f_{-}]
be the perturbation such that

F_{\pm}=\mu+f_{\pm}.

Then the equation (
1.1
) becomes

(1.6)

\partial_{t}f_{\pm}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\pm}\pm(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\pm}\mp(E\cdot v)\mu=Q(f_{\pm}+f_{\mp},\mu)+Q(2\mu+f_{\pm}+f_{\mp},f_{\pm})

where
E,B
satisfies

(1.7)

\partial_{t}E-\nabla_{x}\times B=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(f_{+}-f_{-})dv:=G,\quad\partial_{t}B+\nabla_{x}\cdot E=0,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0,

and we define the linearized operators
L=[L_{+},L_{-}],\mathcal{L}=[\mathcal{L}_{+},\mathcal{L}_{-}]
by

L_{\pm}f=2Q(\mu,f_{\pm})+Q(f_{+}+f_{\mp},\mu),\quad\mathcal{L}f=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f\mp E\cdot v\mu+Lf

and the nonlinear collision operator
\Gamma=[\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}]
by

\Gamma_{\pm}(f,g)=Q(g_{\pm}+g_{\mp},f_{\pm}).

By assuming
F_{0}
have the same mass, total momentum and total energy as
\mu
, the conservation law (
1.4
) turns to

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\pm}dvdx=0,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(f_{+}+f_{-})dvdx=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}(f_{+}+f_{-})dvdx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|E(t,x)|^{2}+|B(t,x)|^{2}dx=0.

Then the continuity equation is

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\pm}(v)dv+\nabla_{x}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf_{\pm}(v)dv=0.

and the kernel of
L
is

\ker(L)=\text{span}\{[1,0]\mu,[0,1]\mu,[1,1]v_{1}\mu,[1,1]v_{2}\mu,[1,1]v_{3}\mu,[1,1]|v|^{2}\mu\},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then we define the projection onto
\ker(L)
by

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{\pm}f

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\pm}dv\right)\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{i}(f_{+}+f_{-})dv\right)v_{i}\mu+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{6}(f_{+}+f_{-})dv\right)(|v|^{2}-3)\mu

(1.9)

\displaystyle=:\Big(a_{\pm}(t,x)+v\cdot b(t,x)+(|v|^{2}-3)c(t,x)\Big)\mu,

or equivalently by

\bm{P}f=\Big(a_{+}(t,x)[1,0]+a_{-}(t,x)[0,1]+v\cdot b(t,x)[1,1]+(|v|^{2}-3)c(t,x)[1,1]\Big)\mu.

\|S_{\mathbf{L}}(t)f_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}(\mu^{-1/2})}\leq e^{-\lambda t}\|f_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}(\mu^{-1/2})}^{2}
. Next, for some

\displaystyle A_{\pm}=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}+L_{\pm}-M\chi_{R},\quad K_{1}=M\chi_{R},\quad B_{\pm}=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}+L_{\pm},\quad K_{2}=\pm\mu v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi,

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm}(L_{\pm}f,f_{\pm})_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\leq-C\|f\|_{H^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{k}}^{2}+C_{k}\|f\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2},

\mathbf{L}

((f,g))_{k}:=(f,g)_{L^{2}_{k}}+\eta\int_{0}^{+\infty}(S_{\mathbf{L}}(\tau)f,S_{\mathbf{L}}(\tau)g)_{L^{2}_{v}}d\tau.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(S_{\mathbf{L}}(\tau)\mathbf{L}f,S_{\mathbf{L}}(\tau)f)d\tau=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|S_{\mathbf{L}}(\tau)f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau=-\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

Therefore, it is important to note that the linear operator
\mathbf{L}
can exhibit non-negativity properties within an appropriate function space. The estimate of
\mathbf{L}
within this specific function space empowers us to establish global well-posedness by combining the nonlinear estimates.

In the study presented in
[
7
]
, the amalgamation of findings from the VPB/VPL system and the application of the semigroup method enabled us to establish the global well-posedness and elucidate the long-term behavior of the VPL/VPB system. Building upon this achievement, the objective of this paper is to extend our previous results to the VMB/VML system. However, a fundamental distinction arises between the Poisson equation and the Maxwell equation. Specifically, in the Maxwell equation, there is dissipation of the electric and magnetic fields, denoted as
E
and
B
respectively. Consequently, when computing the time derivative of the energy of the system
(f,E,B)
, the order of the
x
derivative in
E
and
B
is consistently one less than that in
f
.

For instance, regarding the macroscopic estimate, the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann equation exhibits an inequality of the form:

\partial_{t}({\|}f{\|}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+{\|}\nabla_{x}\phi{\|}_{H^{2}_{x}})+\lambda{\|}\textbf{P}f{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{V}}+\lambda{\|}\nabla_{x}\phi{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\|}(\textbf{I}-\textbf{P})f{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{10}}+{\|}\nabla_{x}\phi{\|}^{4}_{L^{2}_{x}}

In contrast, for the Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann equation, the corresponding macroscopic estimate becomes:

\partial_{t}({\|}f{\|}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+{\|}E,B{\|}_{H^{2}_{x}})+\lambda{\|}\textbf{P}f{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}++\lambda{\|}E,B{\|}^{2}_{H^{1}_{x}}\lesssim{\|}(\textbf{I}-\textbf{P})f{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{10}}+{\|}E,B{\|}^{4}_{L^{2}_{x}}

thus, when exclusively examining the evolution behavior of the coordinate
f
, there is no loss of derivatives in the spatial variable
x
, and the outcome closely resembles that of the Poisson equation. However, when considering the collective evolution behavior of the entire system
(f,E,B)
, meticulous adjustment of the
x
derivatives becomes imperative due to the dissipation inherent in
E
and
B
. Indeed, our main theorem illustrates that achieving global existence necessitates a sacrifice in regularity of order 1. Additionally, to prove polynomial decay, further losses in
x
regularity are inevitable. It is noteworthy to highlight another distinction from the VPB/VPL system: in contrast, the large-time asymptotic behavior of the system under consideration consistently exhibits polynomial decay, even in cases involving hard potential, as opposed to the exponential decay observed in the VPB/VPL system.

Another noteworthy aspect pertains to the choice of the weight function. It is essential to recall that the polynomial perturbation can be viewed as a change of variable, given by
F=\mu+\langle v\rangle^{k}f_{\pm}
, where
k>0
is a constant. In the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (VPB) equation, when addressing the additional term
\pm k\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\frac{v}{\langle v\rangle}f
, one can adopt the approach presented in
[
46
]
,
[
7
]
, introducing the corresponding weight function
e^{\frac{\pm k\phi}{\langle v\rangle}}
to absorb the extra term. However, in the In the Vlasov-Maxwell-Boltzmann (VMB) equation, due to the dissipation of
E
and
B
, this method is no longer effective. Consequently, we opt for the careful selection of the weight function
\langle v\rangle^{(k,\alpha,\beta)}
, and in the case of a soft potential, the weight function even needs to be time-dependent, a detail that will be elaborated upon later.

1.4.1.
About the choice of the weight function for soft potential case
\gamma+2s<0

When
\gamma+2s<0
, the challenge arises from the omission of the term equivalent to
{\|}f{\|}_{L^{2}_{v}}
in the computation of
\partial_{t}E_{K,k}
. This omission is attributed to the inequality
{\|}f{\|}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\lesssim{\|}f{\|}_{L^{2}_{v}}
. To elaborate, the inherent property
{\|}f{\|}_{H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}\lesssim{\|}f{\|}_{L^{2}_{v}}
leads to the absence of the crucial
{\|}f{\|}_{L^{2}_{v}}
term in the calculation of
\partial_{t}E_{K,k}
. This omission introduces additional complexities into the proof. To overcome this challenge, we propose a solution by introducing a time-dependent weight function. Specifically, for any
k
and
\delta>0
, we define
\mathsf{w}(v,t):=\langle v\rangle v^{k(1+(1+t)^{-\delta})}
. It is noteworthy that, due to our choice of
\langle v\rangle
, we ensure
\log\langle v\rangle\gtrsim\log 2>0
. Through direct calculation, we establish that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf{w}^{2}=-\frac{2\delta k}{(1+t)^{1+\delta}}\log\langle v\rangle\mathsf{w}^{2}\lesssim-(1+t)^{-1-\delta}\mathsf{w}^{2}.

This modification introduces an extra term
(1+t)^{-1-\delta}F_{K,k}
in the analysis of the convergence of the energy
E_{K,k}
, allowing us to demonstrate the polynomial decay of
E_{K,k}
. As we will illustrate later in the proof, the choice of
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
proves to be adequate for this purpose.

1.4.2.
About the assumption
s\geq 1/2

Similar to the Poisson case, the assumption
s\geq 1/2
proves to be essential. Let
\bm{g}=(f_{\pm},E,B)
. We need to study the following linear operator

(1.17)

L_{1}(\bm{g})=(-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\pm}\pm E\cdot v\mu+L_{\pm}f,\nabla_{x}\times B-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(f_{+}-f_{-})dv,-\nabla_{x}\times E).

In a broad sense, when employing the semigroup method to analyze the Vlasov-Maxwell term
(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f
, it becomes imperative to manage the term

\int_{0}^{\infty}(S_{L_{1}}(\tau)[(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f],S_{L_{1}}f)d\tau

given the non-commutative nature of the operators
S_{L_{1}}(t)
and
\nabla_{v}
, integration by parts with respect to
\nabla_{v}
is not permissible. Therefore, we must rely on an upper bound to restrict this term. Specifically, the control of the term
{\|}E\cdot\nabla_{v}f{\|}_{H^{-s}_{12}}\sim{\|}E\cdot f{\|}_{H^{1-s}_{12}}
by
{\|}E{\|}_{H^{2}_{x}}{\|}f{\|}_{H^{s}_{12}}
necessitates the condition
1-s\leq s
, which translates to
s\geq 1/2
.

The exploration of the Boltzmann/Landau equation extends beyond the perturbation analysis based on the Maxwellian distribution. Another intriguing avenue of research revolves around the global existence and regularity of the solution in the vicinity of the vacuum state, denoted as
f_{\text{vac}}=0
. In contrast to the perturbation analysis centered on the Maxwellian distribution, investigations into perturbations near the vacuum state are notably scarce.

In the context of the Landau equation, J. Luk established results for moderately soft potentials within the range
-2<\gamma<0
in
[
56
]
. S. Chaturvedi, in a separate work
[
12
]
, demonstrated the existence of solutions for hard potentials within the interval
0\leq\gamma<1
. Addressing the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation, S. Chaturvedi extended the analysis to moderately soft potentials with

in
[
13
]
. Y. Guo, in
[
43
]
, successfully tackled the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann equation with cutoff. Notwithstanding these achievements, the question of stability near vacuum for the Boltzmann/Landau equation, particularly with very soft potentials, remains an unresolved challenge.

However, by using weighted energy estimates and carefully studying null structure in the nonlinearity of the Boltzmann/Landau equation, it is reasonable to believe that the global stability and regularity of the non-cutoff Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann equation and the Vlasov-Poisson-Landau equation with moderately soft potentials can be established. These analytical tools will contribute to advancing our understanding of stability phenomena in kinetic equations. We leave it as a future work.

\|a\|\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}a\|,\quad\|b\|\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}b\|,\quad\|c\|\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}c\|+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}cdx\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}c\|+\|E\|^{2}+\|B\|^{2},\quad\|B\|\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}B\|

for
K=2
, we define the energy
E_{2,int}
as

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}(\int{((\bm{I}_{\pm}-\bm{P}_{\pm})\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{c})_{L^{2}_{v}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}cdx}+\sum_{i,j}\int{((\bm{I}_{\pm}-\bm{P}_{\pm})\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{ij})_{L^{2}_{v}}\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}b_{i}dx}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int{(\partial^{\alpha}b+((I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{a})_{L^{2}_{v}})\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{+}+a_{-})dx})\mp\lambda\int{(((I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{a})_{L^{2}_{v}})\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{+}-a_{-})dx})

\displaystyle-\kappa(E,G)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa(\nabla_{x}E,\nabla_{x}G)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa^{2}(\nabla_{x}\times B,E)_{L^{2}_{x}}

for
K=3
, we define the energy

as

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 3}(\int{((\bm{I}_{\pm}-\bm{P}_{\pm})\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{c})_{L^{2}_{v}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}cdx}+\sum_{i,j}\int{((\bm{I}_{\pm}-\bm{P}_{\pm})\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{ij})_{L^{2}_{v}}\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}b_{i}dx}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int{(\partial^{\alpha}b+((I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{a})_{L^{2}_{v}})\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{+}+a_{-})dx})\mp\lambda\int{(((I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f,\xi_{a})_{L^{2}_{v}})\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{+}-a_{-})dx})

\displaystyle-\kappa(E,G)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa(\nabla_{x}E,\nabla_{x}G)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa(\nabla_{x}^{2}E,\nabla_{x}^{2}G)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa^{2}(\nabla_{x}\times B,E)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\kappa^{2}(\nabla_{x}\times(\nabla_{x}\times B),\nabla_{x}\times E)_{L^{2}_{x}}

so for
K=2
, we choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough, take linear combination
{\eqref{uni11}}+{\eqref{uni15}}+{\eqref{aplus4}}+{\eqref{aplus5}}+\kappa\times{\eqref{equationE7}}
and (

\partial_{t}E_{2,int}(t)+\lambda{\|}a_{\pm},b,c{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}+\lambda{\|}E,B{\|}^{2}_{H^{1}_{x}}\lesssim{\|}(\bm{I}-\bm{P})f{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{D}}+{\|}(g,\xi)_{L^{2}_{v}}{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}+{\|}E,B{\|}^{4}_{L^{2}_{x}}

for
K=3
, we choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough, take linear combination
{\eqref{uni11}}+{\eqref{uni15}}+{\eqref{aplus4}}+{\eqref{aplus5}}+\kappa\times{\eqref{equationE8}}
and (
3.36
) to obtain

\partial_{t}E_{3,int}(t)+\lambda{\|}a_{\pm},b,c{\|}^{2}_{H^{3}_{x}}+\lambda{\|}E,B{\|}^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}\lesssim{\|}(\bm{I}-\bm{P})f{\|}^{2}_{H^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{D}}+{\|}(g,\xi)_{L^{2}_{v}}{\|}^{2}_{H^{3}_{x}}+{\|}E,B{\|}^{4}_{L^{2}_{x}}

and the lemma is proved.

4.
Semigroup of the linearized operator

In this Section, we will derive the estimate on the semigroup generated by the linearized operator
L_{1}
given by (
1.17
).
To take into account both the particle distribution and the electromagnetic field, we consider the equation

(4.1)

\partial_{t}\bm{g}=L_{1}\bm{g},

where we have denoted

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{g}&=(f_{+},f_{-},E,B),\\
L_{1}(\bm{g})&=\Big(-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{+}+E\cdot v\mu+L_{+}f,\,-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{-}-E\cdot v\mu+L_{-}f,\\
&\qquad\quad\nabla_{x}\times B-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(f_{+}-f_{-})dv,\,-\nabla_{x}\times E\Big),\end{aligned}

and denote by
\bm{g}=S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g}_{0}
the solution of the equation

(4.3)

\partial_{t}\bm{g}=L_{1}\bm{g},\quad\bm{g}(0)=\bm{g}_{0},\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{g}_{E}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})(v)dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{g}_{B}=0.

In the following, we’ll use
\bm{g}_{f},\bm{g}_{E},\bm{g}_{B}
to denote the variables of
\bm{g}
, and simply write
[f,E,B]=[\bm{g}_{f},\bm{g}_{E},\bm{g}_{B}]
in the same line accordingly when there’s no confusion. Recall also that we use the norm
\|\bm{g}\|^{2}_{H^{k}_{x}H^{m}_{k}}=\|f\|^{2}_{H^{k}_{x}H^{m}_{k}}+\|[E,B]\|^{2}_{H^{k}_{x}}
.
For the estimate of semigroup
S_{L_{1}}(t)
generated by
L_{1}
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\geq k_{0}
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Then for any suitable function
\bm{g}
, we have large-time behavior:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-1/2}\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{H^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{4}},

and we have a stronger result about integrability and regularizing behavior:

(4.5)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial^{\alpha}S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\min\Big\{\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{H^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{k+10}},\,\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{H^{3}_{x}H^{-s}_{k-\gamma/2}}\Big\}.

moreover, for the
f
coordinate, we have a better estimate:

(4.6)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial^{\alpha}(S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g})_{f}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}H^{-s}_{k-\gamma/2}}.

Proof.

Note that the operators
\partial_{x}
and
L_{1}
are commutative, so we only consider the case
\alpha=0
. The proof below also works if
H^{2}_{x}
is replaced by
H^{k+2}_{x}
and
H^{3}_{x}
is replaced by
H^{k+3}_{x}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For the function
\bm{g}
defined in (
4.2
), an arbitrary weight function
m
and the projection
\bm{P}
given in (
1.1
), we denote
m(\bm{I}-\bm{P})g:=(m(\bm{I}-\bm{P})f_{\pm},E,B)
, in other words, the weight function
m
and the projection
\bm{P}
act only on the variable
f
.
Note also that for polynomial weight, we choose
k\geq k_{0}
while for exponential weight, one can choose arbitrary
k\geq 0
below.

First, we define linear operators
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
, which split
L_{1}
. Let
M>0
be a large constant to be chosen,
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be the smooth truncation function satisfying
1_{[-1,1]}\leq\chi\leq 1_{[-2,2]}
and we denote
\chi_{R}(\cdot):=\chi(\cdot/R)
for
R>0
. Then we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\bm{g}:

\displaystyle=\big(-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{+}+L_{+}f-M\chi_{R}f_{+},\,-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{-}+L_{-}f-M\chi_{R}f_{-},\,\nabla_{x}\times B-E,\,-\nabla_{x}\times E-B\big),

\displaystyle K_{1}\bm{g}:

\displaystyle=\big(M\chi_{R}f_{+},M\chi_{R}f_{-},0,0\big),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\bm{g}:

\displaystyle=\big(-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{+}+L_{+}f,\,-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{-}+L_{-}f,\,\nabla_{x}\times B-E,\,-\nabla_{x}\times E-B\big),

\displaystyle K_{2}\bm{g}:

\displaystyle=\Big(E\cdot v\mu,\,-E\cdot v\mu,\,-E-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(f_{+}-f_{-})dv,\,B\Big),

which give
\mathcal{A}+K_{1}=\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}+K_{2}=L_{1}
. Next, we denote by
S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)g_{0}
the solution to the equation

(4.7)

\partial_{t}\bm{g}=\mathcal{A}\bm{g},\quad\bm{g}(0)=\bm{g}_{0},\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})(v)dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0,

and denote by
S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)g_{0}
the solution to the equation

(4.8)

\partial_{t}\bm{g}=\mathcal{B}\bm{g},\quad\bm{g}(0)=\bm{g}_{0},\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})(v)dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0.

Thus, from the definition of the semigroup, we have

(4.9)

\partial_{t}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}-\mathcal{A}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}=0,\quad\partial_{t}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\bm{g}-\mathcal{B}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\bm{g}=0,\quad\partial_{t}S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g}-\mathcal{L}_{1}S_{L_{1}}(t)\bm{g}=0,

and the Duhamel’s principle implies

(4.10)

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)=S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)+\int^{t}_{0}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t-s)K_{1}S_{\mathcal{A}}(s)\,ds,\quad S_{L_{1}}(t)=S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)+\int^{t}_{0}S_{L_{1}}(t-s)K_{1}S_{\mathcal{B}}(s)\,ds.

Step 1: Estimate of
S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)
.

First, after direct computation by choosing
M>0
sufficiently large and using (
2.12
), there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\bm{g},\bm{g})_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pm}(L_{\pm}f-M\chi_{R}f_{\pm},f_{\pm})_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}+(\nabla_{x}\times B,E)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\|E\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}-(\nabla_{x}\times E,B)_{L^{2}_{x}}-\|B\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}-\|E\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}-\|B\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq-\lambda\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}.

Thus, if
\gamma\geq 0
, from the
L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}
energy estimate of (
4.9
)
1
,

(4.12)

\|S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim e^{-\lambda t}\|\bm{g}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}};

if

from interpolation
{\|}f{\|}_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq{\|}f\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}{\|}^{\frac{2}{3}}_{L^{2}_{v}}{\|}f\langle v\rangle^{6}{\|}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{L^{2}_{v}}

(\mathcal{A}\bm{g},\bm{g})_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\leq-\lambda\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+6}}^{-1}\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{3}.

After solving the ODE of
L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}
energy estimate of (
4.9
)
1
, i.e.

\partial_{t}\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}\leq-2\lambda\big(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+6}}\big)^{-1}\|\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{3},

we have

(4.13)

\|S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-2}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|S_{\mathcal{A}}(s)\bm{g}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+6}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-2}\|\bm{g}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+6}}.

Therefore, the integrability can be obtained from (
4.12
) and (
4.13

\alpha\geq 0

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial^{\alpha}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\,ds\lesssim\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k+6}}.

Next, for the regularizing effect, we perform the duality argument of
S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)
. Consider the dual
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}
of
\mathcal{A}_{l}=\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{A}(\langle v\rangle^{-l}f)
for any

\bm{h}=\langle v\rangle^{2k-l}\bm{g}

only. Then
\big(\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}\bm{g},\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bm{g}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}=\big(\langle v\rangle^{l}\bm{g},\mathcal{A}(\langle v\rangle^{2k-l}\bm{g})\big)_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}=\big(\langle v\rangle^{2l-2k}\bm{h},\mathcal{A}\bm{h}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.

Take the
L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}
inner product of
\partial_{t}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}-\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}=0

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\big(\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g},\langle v\rangle^{2k}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}=0.

Hence for
l-k\geq k_{0}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\langle v\rangle^{l-k}\bm{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\big(\langle v\rangle^{2l-2k}\bm{h},\mathcal{A}\bm{h}\big)_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}=0,
where
\bm{h}=\langle v\rangle^{2k-l}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}
.
Integrating over
t

4

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lambda\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\langle v\rangle^{l-k}\bm{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}^{2}\,dt\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{l-k}\bm{h}|_{t=0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\lambda\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{l}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}^{2}\,dt\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bm{g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.

Observe that if
\partial_{t}\bm{g}=\mathcal{A}\bm{g}

\partial_{t}\langle v\rangle^{l}\bm{g}=\mathcal{A}_{l}\langle v\rangle^{l}\bm{g}
.
Thus,
\langle v\rangle^{l}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}=S_{\mathcal{A}_{l}}(\langle v\rangle^{l}\bm{g})
.
Moreover, by duality, we have
(S_{\mathcal{A}_{l}}\bm{g}_{1},\bm{g}_{2})_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}=(\bm{g}_{1},S_{\mathcal{A}_{l}^{*}}\bm{g}_{2})_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}
for any vector-valued functions
\bm{g}_{1},\bm{g}_{2}
given in (
4.2
). Therefore,
there exists a vector-valued function (given in (
4.2
)) sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}
in Schwartz space such that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\leq 1
, we have from (
4.15

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\big(\langle v\rangle^{2k}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g},\varphi_{n}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\big|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}\big|\big(\langle v\rangle^{2k}\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g},S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{2k}}(t)\varphi_{n}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\big|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-s}_{-\gamma/2}}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}S_{\mathcal{A}^{*}_{2k}}(t)\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{\gamma/2}}^{2}\,dt

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{k-\gamma/2}\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-s}_{v}},

for any
k\geq k_{0}
. So, noting that
\partial^{\alpha}
commutes with

(4.17)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial^{\alpha}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}dt\lesssim\|\partial^{\alpha}\bm{g}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-s}_{k-\gamma/2}}^{2}.

Step 2: Estimate of
S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)
.

Next, we compute
\mathcal{B}
and consider linearized equation

(4.18)

\leq-\lambda\|f\|_{H^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}-\|E\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}-\|B\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{x}}+C\|f\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}\leq-\lambda\|\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}H^{s}_{k+\gamma/2}}^{2}+C\|f\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}\bm{g},\bm{g})_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\mu^{-1/2})}

where we used (

Similar to the analysis of

\|\mu^{-1/2}S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-2}\|\mu^{-1/2}\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{6}}\,\,\text{for}\,\,-3\leq\gamma<0.

For operator

Thus, from Duhamel’s principle (

\displaystyle\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(t-s)K_{1}S_{\mathcal{A}}(s)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}\,ds

For the second term, using Minkowski’s inequality twice we easily compute that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{B}}(s)K_{1}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t-s)\bm{g}\,ds\Big\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|S_{\mathcal{B}}(s)K_{1}S_{\mathcal{A}}(t-s)\bm{g}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{k}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle(\text{because of {\eqref{equationa3}}, {\eqref{equationa4}}})\lesssim

{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}g{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}^{2}_{K,k}
may not be equivalent to
E_{{K+1},k}(t)
because of the loss of one-order derivative, but it is larger than
E_{K,k}(t)
.
Recall from (
3.1
) and (
1.17
) that the solution
\bm{g}
to VMB/VML system (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) can be written as the solution to

\partial_{t}\bm{g}=L_{1}\bm{g}+N\bm{g},\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot E=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(f_{+}(v)-f_{-}(v)\big)\,dv,\quad\nabla_{x}\cdot B=0,\quad\bm{g}(0)=\bm{g}_{0},

where
N(\bm{g})=((E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f+Q(f_{+},f_{+})+Q(f_{-},f_{+}),\,-(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f+Q(f_{-},f_{-})+Q(f_{+},f_{-}),0,0)
.
Note that
N(\bm{g})_{E,B}=(0,0)
.
Thus, if
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we can easily compute that for any
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(S_{L_{1}}(\tau)N\bm{g},S_{L_{1}}(\tau)\bm{g})_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}((S_{L_{1}}(\tau)Ng)_{f},(S_{L_{1}}(\tau)g)_{f})_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}d\tau

\displaystyle(\text{because of}\,\,{\eqref{equationl10}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Ng\|_{H^{K}_{x}H^{-s}_{10-\gamma/2}}\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}H^{-s}_{10-\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(E+v\times B)\cdot\nabla_{v}f+Q(f,f)\|_{H^{K}_{x}H^{-s}_{10-\gamma/2}}\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{12}}

\displaystyle(\text{because of}\,\,{\eqref{equationbols1}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{12}}\|f\|_{H^{6}_{x}L^{2}_{12}}(\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}H^{s}_{12}}+\|E\|_{H^{K}_{x}})

(5.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2\eta}\lambda D_{K,k}+C\eta E_{6,k}(\|[E,B]\|_{H^{K}_{x}}^{2}+\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}).

The above proof relies on the crucial observation that the coordinates
E
and
B
of
N(g)
are both set to 0. Since the dissipation is exclusively in
E
and
B
and not in
f
, there is no loss in the
x
derivatives for

. This feature greatly simplifies the proof. So we deduce from (
5
), (
5.12
), (
5
) that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}\bm{g}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{K,k}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}D_{K,k}(f)+\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-C-\eta^{2}CE_{6,k}\right)(\|[E,B]\|_{H^{K}_{x}}^{2}+\|f\|_{H^{K}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2})\leq 0.

Therefore, if we take
\eta\gg C
large enough and
E_{6,k}\ll\frac{1}{C^{3}}
small enough, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
, such that
\frac{\eta}{2}-C-\eta^{2}CE_{6,k}\geq\lambda_{0}
. Then we have that for
2\leq K\leq 6
,

(5.15)

\frac{d}{dt}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}\bm{g}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{K,k}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}G_{K,k}\leq 0.

Choose
K=6
in (
5.15
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}\bm{g}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{6,k}^{2}\leq 0
.
This implies that for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, we have

E_{6,k}(t)\leq{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}\bm{g}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}^{2}\leq E_{6,k}(0)+\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}(S_{L_{1}}(\tau)\bm{g}_{0},S_{L_{1}}(\tau)\bm{g}_{0})_{H^{6}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}d\tau\lesssim E_{7,k}(0).

Thus if we assume the initial data
E_{7,k}(0)\leq\epsilon_{0}
is small, we are able to close the first argument
E_{6,k}(t)\leq\epsilon/2
in the
a priori
assumption (
5.3
).

Finally, we come to prove the convergence. For any
k\geq\frac{9}{2}\geq-\frac{3\gamma}{2}
, Take
K=2
in (
5.15
), we obtain

(5.16)

\frac{d}{dt}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}f{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}G_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}(f)\leq 0.

Next, from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, we have

(5.17)

\|[E,B]\|_{H^{3}_{x}}\lesssim\|[E,B]\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{H^{6}_{x}}\|[E,B]\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{H^{2}_{x}}\leq E_{6}^{\frac{1}{4}}D_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{k}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{\frac{3}{4}}
. Next, using the

, we obtain from (
5.17
) that

{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}f{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}^{2}\lesssim E_{3,k-\frac{9}{2}}(f)\lesssim E_{6,k}^{\frac{1}{4}}(f)G_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(f)\lesssim G_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}(f).

Together with (

), and recalling that

\frac{d}{dt}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}f{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{2,k-\frac{9}{2}}^{2}+\lambda_{1}{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}f{{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}}_{2,k-3}^{\frac{8}{3}}\leq 0.

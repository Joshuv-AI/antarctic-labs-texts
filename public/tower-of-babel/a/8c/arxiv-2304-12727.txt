On forward-backward SDE approaches to continuous-time minimum variance estimation
Jin Won Kim and Sebastian Reich

Abstract
. The work of Kalman and Bucy has established a duality between filtering and optimal estimation in the context of time-continuous linear systems. This duality has recently been extended to time-continuous nonlinear systems in terms of an optimization problem constrained by a backward stochastic partial differential equation. Here we revisit this problem from the perspective of appropriate forward-backward stochastic differential equations. This approach sheds new light on the estimation problem and provides a unifying perspective. It is also demonstrated that certain formulations of the estimation problem lead to deterministic formulations similar to the linear Gaussian case as originally investigated by Kalman and Bucy. Finally, optimal control of partially observed diffusion processes is discussed as an application of the proposed estimators.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we revisit the problem of estimation in the context of continuous time filtering problems
[
11
,
1
]
.
Our interest has been triggered by recent progress on the topic
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, which has extending the dual optimal control perspective for linear problems, as originally exposed by
[
12
,
13
,
11
]
,
to nonlinear filtering problems. The key idea is to consider an estimator in the form of

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{T}[f]=\mu[{\rm Y}_{0}]-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},

where
f(x)
is the function which the conditional expectation value is to be estimated of. Furthermore,
\mu
is the density of states at initial time
t=0
and
Z_{0:T}
is the observation path, which are both given. The function
{\rm Y}_{0}(x)
and the control
\mathcal{U}_{0:T}
are to be determined such that
\pi_{T}[f]=\mathcal{S}_{T}[f]
. Here
\pi_{T}
denotes the filtering distribution at final time
t=T
. As proposed in the work of
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, the function
{\rm Y}_{t}(x)
, and the control
\mathcal{U}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, satisfy the backward stochastic partial differential equation (BSPDE)

(2)

-{\rm d}{\rm Y}_{t}=\mathcal{L}^{x}\,{\rm Y}_{t}{\rm d}t-{\rm V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}+(\mathcal{U}_{t}+{\rm V}_{t})^{\rm T}h\,{\rm d}t,\quad{\rm Y}_{T}=f.

The minimum variance property of the estimator is then expressed in terms of an appropriate cost function which determined the optimal
\mathcal{U}_{0:T}
uniquely. Here
\mathcal{L}^{x}
denotes the generator of the underlying signal process and
h(x)
is the forward map in the observation process.

We address the same estimation problem from the perspective of forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) instead
[
7
]
. Our approach allows for a unified perspective on the following four scenarios:

(i)

estimating
\pi_{T}[f]
using an estimator of the form (
1
),

(ii)

estimating
\sigma_{T}[f]
using an estimator of the form (
1
), where
\sigma_{t}
denotes the non-normalized filtering distribution satisfying the Zakai equation
[
11
,
1
]
,

(iii)

estimating
\pi_{T}[f]
using the innovation

(3)

I_{t}=Z_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\pi_{s}[h]{\rm d}s

in the estimator (
1
) instead of the observations
Z_{t}
,

(iv)

estimating
\sigma_{T}[f]
using the induced observation error

(4)

W_{t}=Z_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s}){\rm d}s

instead of the observations
Z_{t}
directly.

Our FBSDE approach allows for a transparent definition of minimum variance estimation in all four cases. It is found that (ii)-(iv) lead to deterministic backward Kolmogorov equations
[
20
,
7
]
in the unknown
{\rm Y}_{t}(x)
instead of the BSPDE formulation (
2
), which arises from (i). Furthermore, while (ii) and (iii) lead to the standard backward Kolmogorov equation, (iv) introduces an additional drift term in the spirit of Feynman–Kac formulations
[
7
]
. By strictly following a FBSDE approach, a numerical treatment also becomes feasible following established computational methods for FBSDEs
[
8
]
. This aspect will be explored in a future publication.

We emphasize that we assume throughout this paper that the observable
f(x)
is a deterministic function independent of the data
Z_{0:T}
. On the contrary, applications of estimators of the form (
1
) to the problem of filter stability have been discussed in
[
14
,
17
]
. In this context, the terminal condition in (
2
) becomes data dependent and the analysis of the resulting FBSDEs becomes more involved. Such an extension is left to future work. Another possible extension concerns estimators for more general conditional expectation values such as

(5)

\mathcal{V}_{T}:=\int_{0}^{T}\pi_{t}[c_{t}]{\rm d}t+\pi_{T}[f]

with given functions
c_{t}(x)
,
t\in[0,T]
, and
f(x)
. In this paper, we briefly discuss an application of (
5
) in the context of optimal control of partially observed diffusion processes
[
3
,
5
]
.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section
2
provides the necessary background on time-continuous nonlinear filtering and introduces two sets of forward SDEs (FSDEs), which will be play an essential role in deriving the optimal estimators for scenarios (i)-(iv). The estimators for the conditional expectation values are discussed for all four scenarios separately in Section
3
. Emphasis is put on a unified framework using appropriate FBSDEs. It is demonstrated that the FBSDE become equivalent to a backward Kolmogorov equation under scenarios (ii) and (iii). The FBSDE perspective also leads to novel formulations of the stochastic optimal estimation problem (i) while scenario (iv) results in a backward PDE formulation in the spirit of Feynman–Kac. Section
4
provides an application of (
5
) to problems from optimal control of partially observed diffusion processes
[
3
,
5
]
.
Our paper ends with some conclusions.

2.
The continuous time filtering problem

The joint measure over paths
(X_{0:T},Z_{0:T})
in state variable
x
and observed variable
z
is determined by the FSDE

(6a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad X_{0}\sim\mu,

(6b)

\displaystyle{\rm d}Z_{t}

\displaystyle=h(X_{t}){\rm d}t+{\rm d}W_{t},\qquad\,\,Z_{0}=0,

for time
t\in[0,T]
. Here
(B_{t},W_{t})
are independent Brownian motions.
We denote the induced path measure by
\mathbb{P}
and the expectation value of a function
g(x,z)
by
\mathbb{E}[g(X_{t},Z_{t})]
. We are interested in the
conditional path measure
X_{t}|Z_{0:t}
and denotes its density at time
t
, by
\pi_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]

[
11
,
1
]
. Recall that the filtering distribution
\pi_{t}
satisfies the Kushner–Stratonovitch (KS) equation of nonlinear filtering
[
11
,
1
]
.

It is also well-known that the innovation process
(
3
) behaves like Brownian motion independent of
B_{t}
under the path measure
\mathbb{P}
and we introduce the FSDE

(7a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad\qquad\qquad X_{0}\sim\mu,

(7b)

\displaystyle{\rm d}D_{t}

\displaystyle=D_{t}\,(h(X_{t})-\pi_{t}[h])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},\quad\qquad\,D_{0}=1,

which are driven by the independent Brownian processes
(B_{t},I_{t})
. We denote the path measure induced by the FSDE (
7
) by
\mathbb{P}^{\ast}
and its expectation operator by
\mathbb{E}^{\ast}
. It holds that

(8)

\pi_{t}[f]=\mathbb{E}^{\ast}[D_{t}f(X_{t})|I_{0:t}].

We also introduce the shorthand notation

(9)

\mathbb{E}^{\ast}_{I}[g(X_{t},D_{t})]=\mathbb{E}^{\ast}[g(X_{t},D_{t})|I_{0:t}],

for any suitable function
g(x,d)
.

The SDE (
7b
) is of mean-field type, which becomes more transparent when rewritten in the form

(10)

{\rm d}D_{t}=D_{t}\,(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}.

Hence, alternatively, we consider the change of measure

(11)

\frac{{\rm d}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}{{\rm d}\mathbb{P}}(X_{t})=\tilde{D}_{t}^{-1},

where

(12)

{\rm d}\tilde{D}_{t}=\tilde{D}_{t}\,h(X_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},\qquad\tilde{D}_{0}=1.

We recall that this change of measure arises from an application of Girsanov’s theorem
[
20
]

and that
Z_{t}
behaves like Brownian motion independent of
B_{t}
under the new path measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}

[
1
]
. We, hence, introduce a second pair of FSDEs

(13a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad\qquad\qquad X_{0}\sim\mu,

(13b)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{D}_{t}

\displaystyle=\tilde{D}_{t}\,h(X_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{D}_{0}=1,

which are now driven by the independent Brownian processes
(B_{t},Z_{t})
. The induced path measure is denoted by
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}
and its associated expectation operator by
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}
.

Furthermore, one can introduce the non-normalized filtering density

\sigma_{t}
, which satisfies

(14)

\sigma_{t}[f]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}[\tilde{D}_{t}f(X_{t})|Z_{0:t}]

for fixed observation process
Z_{0:t}
. Again we introduce the shorthand notation

(15)

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}[g(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}[g(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})|Z_{0:t}].

Recall that
\sigma_{t}
satisfies the Zakai equation of nonlinear filtering
[
11
,
1
]
and that

(16)

\pi_{t}[f]=\frac{\sigma_{t}[f]}{\sigma_{t}[1]}.

3.
FBSDE estimators for conditional expectation values

We wish to construct estimators for the conditional expectation values
\pi_{T}[f]
and

\sigma_{T}[f]
, respectively, of the following form:

(17a)

\displaystyle S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]

\displaystyle:=\tilde{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},

(17b)

\displaystyle S_{T}^{\pi}[f]

\displaystyle:=\breve{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}\breve{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},

(17c)

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]

\displaystyle:=\hat{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}\hat{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},

(17d)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{T}^{\sigma}[f]

\displaystyle:=\bar{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}\bar{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}W_{t}.

The required random variables
\tilde{Y}_{0}
,
\breve{Y}_{0}
,
\hat{Y}_{0}
,
\bar{Y}_{t}
and controls
\tilde{U}_{0:T}
,
\breve{U}_{0:T}
,
\hat{U}_{0:T}
,
\bar{U}_{t}
,
respectively, are chosen such that

(18a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}[f],

(18b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[S_{T}^{\pi}[f]]

\displaystyle=\pi_{T}[f],

(18c)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]]

\displaystyle=\pi_{T}[f],

(18d)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[\bar{S}_{t}^{\sigma}[f]]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}[f]

for any suitable observable
f(x)
.

We derive appropriate FBSDE formulations for each of the four estimators in the following subsections. See
[
7
,
4
]
for an introduction to BSDEs and FBSDEs.

3.1.
Observation-based estimator I

We start with the estimator (
17a
) and introduce the BSDE

(19)

{\rm d}\tilde{Y}_{t}=\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},\qquad\quad\tilde{Y}_{T}=\tilde{D}_{T}f(X_{T}),

where
(X_{T},\tilde{D}_{T})
is defined by the FSDE (
13
).

We note that

(20a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}\left[\left(\tilde{D}_{T}f(X_{T})-S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}\left[\left(\tilde{D}_{T}f(X_{T})-\tilde{Y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(20b)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\tilde{U}_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(20c)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{U}_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right]+\tilde{M}_{T},

where
\tilde{M}_{T}
is a martingale term which vanishes under expectations with respect to observations
Z_{0:T}
. The desired control
\tilde{U}_{0:T}
and its associated estimator (
17a
) are therefore chosen to minimize the cost functional

(21)

\tilde{J}_{T}(\tilde{U}_{0:T}):=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{U}_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right].

The cost functional (
21
) is minimized for

(22)

\tilde{U}_{t}=-\tilde{V}_{t}.

This control leads to an estimator (
17a
) of minimum variance as expressed by
\tilde{J}_{T}
. Furthermore, we immediately obtain from (
19
) and (
17a
) that the mean estimation error satisfies

(23)

\sigma_{T}[f]-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\tilde{U}_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}\right].

Using (
22
), this implies (
18a
) for the estimator (
17a
) as desired.

In order to gain a better insight into the solution structure of the FBSDEs (
13
) and (
19
) and to find an explicit explicit expression for the optimal control
\tilde{U}_{0:T}
, we introduce the
notations
\tilde{Y}_{s}^{t,x,d}
,
\tilde{V}_{s}^{t,x,d}
,
\tilde{Q}_{s}^{t,x,d}
, and
\tilde{U}_{s}^{t,x,d}
for solutions of the FBSDE considered over the restricted time interval
s\in[t,T]
with initial condition

X_{t}=x
and
\tilde{D}_{t}=d
. Equation (
19
) now implies

(24)

\tilde{Y}_{t}^{t,x,d}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\tilde{Y}_{t+\tau}^{t,x,d}\right]

for
\tau\in(0,T-t]
, where
X_{s}^{t,x}
denotes solutions of the forward SDE (
13a
) with
X_{t}=x
for
s\geq t
and similarly for
\tilde{D}_{s}^{t,x,d}
. Here
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}
denotes now expectation with respect to the Brownian noise
B_{t:t+\tau}
in (
13a
) and the observations
Z_{t:t+\tau}
in (
13b
) under the path measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}

for fixed initial conditions
X_{t}=x
and
\tilde{D}_{t}=d
. In particular, we can set
t+\tau=T
and use

\tilde{Y}_{T}^{t,x,d}=\tilde{D}_{T}^{t,x,d}f(X_{T}^{t,x})
.

Furthermore, let us denote the generator of the FSDEs (
13
) by
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
, which acts on functions
g(x,d)
, that is,

(25)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\,g(x,d)=b(x)^{\rm T}\nabla_{x}g(x,d)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla_{x}^{2}g(x,d)+\frac{d^{2}}{2}h(x)^{\rm T}h(x)\,\partial_{d}^{2}g(x,d).

Then, under appropriate smoothness assumptions, the deterministic function

(26)

\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d):=\tilde{Y}_{t}^{t,x,d}

satisfies the backward Kolmogorov equation

(27)

-\partial_{t}\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d)=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\,\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d),\qquad\tilde{y}_{T}(x,d)=d\,f(x),

since

(28a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{y}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}\tilde{y}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\tilde{y}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})){\rm d}t

(28b)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\sigma\nabla_{x}\tilde{y}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t}){\rm d}B_{t}+\partial_{d}\tilde{y}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})\tilde{D}_{t}h(X_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}

(28c)

\displaystyle={\rm d}\tilde{Y}_{t}=\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\tilde{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}.

Hence one also obtains

(29a)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{t}^{t,x,d}

\displaystyle=\sigma\nabla_{x}\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d),

(29b)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{t}^{t,x,d}

\displaystyle=d\,\partial_{d}\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d)\,h(x),

and we can define the deterministic control function

(30)

\tilde{u}_{t}(x,d):=-d\,\partial_{d}\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d)\,h(x)

and minimum variance control
\tilde{U}_{t}=\tilde{u}_{t}(X_{t},\tilde{D}_{t})
.

To simplify further, we make the
ansatz

\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d)=d\,y_{t}(x)
and find that this assumption implies

(31)

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\,\tilde{y}_{t}(x,d)=d\,\mathcal{L}^{x}\,y_{t}(x),

where
\mathcal{L}^{x}
denotes the generator of (
7a
).
Hence
y_{t}(x)
has to satisfy the backward Kolmogorov equation

(32)

-\partial_{t}y_{t}(x)=\mathcal{L}^{x}y_{t}(x),\qquad y_{T}(x)=f(x).

Furthermore,

(33)

u_{t}(x,d)=-d\,y_{t}(x)\,h(x)

and (
17a
) becomes

(34)

S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]=y_{0}(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{D}_{t}\,y_{t}(X_{t})\,h(X_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}.

A closely related estimator can be found in
[
14
]
(Section 2.3.3), where it has been used to derive Zakai’s equation. The difference is that
[
14
]
considers directly the averaged estimator (
40
) not relying on a FBSDE formulation.

Furthermore, one can derive a BSDE representation for
y_{t}(x)
directly. Upon introducing
Y_{t}
such that
\tilde{Y}_{t}=\tilde{D}_{t}Y_{t}
and utilizing (
13b
), (
6b
), and (
19
), one obtains the BSDE

(35)

{\rm d}Y_{t}=Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+V_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}W_{t},\qquad Y_{T}=f(X_{T}),

along the FSDEs (
6
).
Please be aware that
(Q_{t},V_{t})
are not the same as in the BSDE (
19
). In fact, since

(36a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{Y}_{t}

\displaystyle=\tilde{D}_{t}{\rm d}Y_{t}+Y_{t}\tilde{D}_{t}h(X_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}+\tilde{D}_{t}h(X_{t})^{\rm T}V_{t}{\rm d}t

(36b)

\displaystyle=\tilde{D}_{t}Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\tilde{D}_{t}\left(V_{t}+Y_{t}h(X_{t})\right)^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},

one finds that
\tilde{Q}_{t}=\tilde{D}_{t}Q_{t}
and
\tilde{V}_{t}=\tilde{D}_{t}V_{t}+\tilde{D}_{t}Y_{t}h(X_{t})
. Furthermore, since
Y_{t}
does not depend on
Z_{t:T}
,
V_{t}\equiv 0
and we obtain

(37)

{\rm d}Y_{t}=Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}

in line with the backward Kolmogorov equation (
32
). Let us summarize our findings in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The estimator (
17a
) becomes unbiased for
\tilde{Y}_{0}=Y_{0}

with
Y_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, defined by the FBSDEs

(38a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\quad X_{0}\sim\mu,

(38b)

\displaystyle{\rm d}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad\qquad\,\,\,Y_{T}=f(X_{T}).

The control
\tilde{U}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, is provided by

(39)

\tilde{U}_{t}=-\tilde{D}_{t}Y_{t}h(X_{t}).

We end this section with a brief discussion related to the problem of filter stability. Let us introduce the abbreviation

(40)

\mathcal{S}_{T}^{\sigma}[f]:=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[S_{T}^{\sigma}[f]]=\mu[Y_{0}]-\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}[\tilde{U}_{t}]{\rm d}Z_{t}

The optimal choice
\tilde{U}_{t}=-\tilde{V}_{t}
leads to

(41)

\tilde{Y}_{T}-\mathcal{S}_{T}^{\sigma}[f]=\tilde{Y}_{0}-\mu[\tilde{Y}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\tilde{V}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[\tilde{V}_{t}]){\rm d}Z_{t}

from which we can recover

(42)

\mathcal{S}_{T}^{\sigma}[f]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[\tilde{Y}_{T}]=\sigma_{T}[f]

(unbiasedness) as well as

(43a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\left(\tilde{Y}_{T}-\sigma_{T}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mu\left[\left(\tilde{Y}_{0}-\mu[\tilde{Y}_{0}]\right)^{2}\right]

(43b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{V}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}^{\ast}[\tilde{V}_{t}]\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right]

(variance propagation). The last identity gives rise to the differential equation

(44)

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\left(\tilde{Y}_{t}-\sigma_{t}[f]\right)^{2}\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{V}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}[\tilde{V}_{t}]\|^{2}\right]

and the variance of
\tilde{Y}_{t}
is non-increasing as time goes backward from
t=T
to
t=0
.
The expression on the right hand side of (
44
) can be viewed as a (non-stationary) Dirichlet form and
carré du champ
operator
[
2
]
, respectively, associated with the FSDE (
13
). Furthermore, under an assumed Poincaré-type inequality

(45)

c\,\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\left(\tilde{Y}_{t}-\sigma_{t}[f]\right)^{2}\right]\leq\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\left[\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{V}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}[\tilde{V}_{t}]\|^{2}\right],

c>0
, the decay becomes exponential. We note that

(46)

\|\tilde{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{V}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}_{Z}[\tilde{V}_{t}]\|^{2}=\sigma^{2}\|\tilde{D}_{t}\nabla_{x}y_{t}(X_{t})\|^{2}+\|\tilde{D}_{t}y_{t}(X_{t})h(X_{t})-\sigma_{t}[y_{t}h]\|^{2},

which provides an explicit expression for the decay of variance via (
44
). It would be of interest to relate these considerations to the problem of filter stability. See the closely related work
[
17
]
. An obvious observation is that an exponential decay in the variance of
Y_{t}
implies an exponential decay of variance in
\tilde{Y}_{t}
.

3.2.
Innovation-based estimator

We now turn to the estimator (
17b
). The required BSDE for
\breve{Y}_{t}
is given by

(47)

{\rm d}\breve{Y}_{t}=\breve{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\breve{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},\quad\,\,\,\,\breve{Y}_{T}=D_{T}f(X_{T}),

along solutions of the FSDE (
7
). We note again that

(48a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(D_{T}f(X_{T})-S_{T}^{\pi}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(D_{T}f(X_{T})-\breve{Y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\breve{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(48b)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\breve{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\breve{U}_{t}+\breve{V}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(48c)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\breve{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\breve{U}_{t}+\breve{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right]+\breve{M}_{T},

where
\breve{M}_{T}
is a martingale term which vanishes under expectation with respect to
I_{0:T}
. The control
\breve{U}_{t}
in (
17b
) is chosen to minimize the cost functional

(49)

\mathcal{J}_{T}(\breve{U}_{0:T}):=\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\breve{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\breve{U}_{t}+\breve{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right],

which is again minimized by
\breve{U}_{t}=-\breve{V}_{t}
and the associated estimator (
17b
) is of minimum variance.

The time-dependent generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
associated with the FSDE (
7
) is provided by

(50)

\mathcal{L}_{t}\,g=b^{\rm T}\nabla_{x}g+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla_{x}^{2}g+\frac{d^{2}}{2}\|h-\pi_{t}[h]\|^{2}\,\partial_{d}^{2}g.

Let the function
\breve{y}_{t}(x,d)
satisfy the associated BSPDE

(51)

-{\rm d}_{t}\breve{y}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}\breve{y}_{t}\,{\rm d}t-\breve{\mathcal{V}}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},\qquad\breve{y}_{T}(x,d)=d\,f(x),

where the
\breve{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
term is needed to make
\breve{y}_{t}(x,d)
adapted to the forward process. However, as shown below,
\partial_{d}^{2}\breve{y}_{t}=0
and we may conclude that
\breve{\mathcal{V}}_{t}\equiv 0
. Then Itô’s formula implies

(52a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\breve{y}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t})

\displaystyle=({\rm d}_{t}\breve{y}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t})+\mathcal{L}_{t}\breve{y}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t}){\rm d}t)

(52b)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\sigma\nabla_{x}\breve{y}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t}){\rm d}B_{t}+D_{t}\partial_{d}\breve{y}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t})(h(X_{t})-\pi_{t}[h])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}

(52c)

\displaystyle=\breve{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\breve{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}={\rm d}\breve{Y}_{t}.

Hence, we find that
\breve{q}_{t}(x,d)=\sigma\nabla_{x}\breve{y}_{t}(x,d)
as well as

(53)

\breve{v}_{t}(x,d)=d\,\partial_{d}\breve{y}_{t}(x,d)\,(h(x)-\pi_{t}[h]).

Again, we make the
ansatz

\breve{y}_{t}(x,d)=d\,y_{t}(x)
and find that
y_{t}(x)
has to satisfy the previously stated backward Kolmogorov equation (
32
). Therefore, we also find that
\breve{Q}_{t}=\breve{q}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t})
with

\breve{q}_{t}(x,d):=d\,\sigma\,\nabla_{x}y_{t}(x)
as well as
\breve{V}_{t}=\breve{v}_{t}(X_{t},D_{t})
with
\breve{v}_{t}(x,d)=d\,v_{t}(x)
where

(54)

v_{t}(x)=y_{t}(x)\,(h(x)-\pi_{t}[h]).

The optimal control is given by

(55)

\breve{U}_{t}=-D_{t}\,y_{t}(X_{t})\,(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})])

and (
17b
) becomes

(56)

S_{T}^{\pi}[f]=y_{0}(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}D_{t}\,y_{t}(X_{t})\,(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}.

Alternatively, using the
ansatz

\breve{Y}_{t}=D_{t}Y_{t}
, one can reformulate the BSDE (
47
) as a BSDE (
35
) along the FSDE (
6
) in the new variable
Y_{t}
. The two BSDEs are now related via
\breve{Q}_{t}=D_{t}Q_{t}
as well as

(57)

\breve{V}_{t}=D_{t}V_{t}+D_{t}Y_{t}\left(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]\right)

and one finds again that
V_{t}\equiv 0
. Hence the BSDE (
35
) reduces to (
37
), which corresponds to the backward Kolmogorov equation (
32
). Let us summarize our findings in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

The estimator (
17b
) becomes unbiased for
\breve{Y}_{0}=Y_{0}

with
Y_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, defined by the FBSDEs (
38
) and
the control
\breve{U}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, is provided by

(58)

\breve{U}_{t}=-D_{t}Y_{t}(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]).

The considerations on filter stability, as put forward in Section
3.1
, extend naturally to the innovation-based estimator (
17b
). In particular, (
44
) becomes

(59a)

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\left(\breve{Y}_{t}-\pi_{t}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

(59b)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\sigma^{2}\|D_{t}\nabla_{x}y_{t}(X_{t})\|^{2}+\|D_{t}y_{t}(X_{t})(h(X_{t})-\pi_{t}[h])-\pi_{t}[y_{t}(h-\pi_{t}[h])]\|^{2}\right]

with
\breve{Y}_{t}=D_{t}\,y_{t}(X_{t})
and an appropriate Poincaré inequality would again establish an exponential decay of variance.

Example 3.1
.

The linear-Gaussian case is a special case of the model (
6
) where
b(x)=A^{\rm T}x
,
h(x)=H^{\rm T}x
for some

and
H\in\Re^{n\times m}
, and
\mu
is Gaussian with mean
m_{0}
and covariance matrix
\Sigma_{0}
. Fix
\bar{f}\in\Re^{n}
and consider a linear function

. In this case, the solution to the backward Kolmogorov equation is also a linear function
y_{t}(x)=\bar{y}_{t}^{\rm T}x
with

-\frac{{\rm d}\bar{y}_{t}}{{\rm d}t}=A\bar{y}_{t},\quad\bar{y}_{T}=\bar{f}.

Upon introducing the filtered state covariance matrix

(61)

\Sigma_{t}:=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}X_{t}X_{t}^{\rm T}]-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}X_{t}]\,\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}X_{t}]^{\rm T},

the optimal control (
55

(62)

\mathbb{E}_{I}^{*}[U_{t}]=-H^{\rm T}\Sigma_{t}\bar{y}_{t}

and the estimator (

(63a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[S_{T}^{\pi}[f]]

(63b)

\displaystyle=\bar{y}_{0}^{\rm T}m_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\bar{y}_{t}^{\rm T}\Sigma_{t}H{\rm d}I_{t}

(63c)

\displaystyle=\bar{f}^{\rm T}e^{TA^{\rm T}}m_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\bar{f}^{\rm T}e^{(T-t)A^{\rm T}}\Sigma_{t}H{\rm d}I_{t}

Define the filtered mean

(64)

m_{t}:=e^{tA^{\rm T}}m_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A^{\rm T}}\Sigma_{s}H{\rm d}I_{s},

and observe that
\mathcal{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]=\bar{f}^{\rm T}m_{T}
. The Kalman–Bucy filter is obtained by differentiate (

) with respect to
t
:

(65)

{\rm d}m_{t}=A^{\rm T}m_{t}{\rm d}t+\Sigma_{t}H{\rm d}I_{t}.

Please note that, contrary to (
60
), the BSPDE formulations (
2
) leads to the controlled backward Kolmogorov equation

(66)

-\frac{{\rm d}\bar{y}_{t}}{{\rm d}t}=A\bar{y}_{t}+H\bar{u}_{t},\quad\bar{y}_{T}=\bar{f}

instead
[

]
. We explore this connection further in the following subsection.

3.3.
Observation-based estimator II

We now state a BSDE required to define the estimator (
17c
). The key observation is again that the backward process
\hat{Y}_{t}
is chosen such that

(67)

D_{T}f(X_{T})-\hat{S}^{\pi}_{T}[f]=\hat{Y}_{T}-\hat{Y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t}

becomes independent of the innovation process
I_{0:T}
under an optimal choice of the control
\hat{U}_{0:T}
. These considerations lead immediately to the BSDE

(68)

{\rm d}\hat{Y}_{t}=\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\hat{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}-\hat{U}_{t}^{\rm T}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]{\rm d}t,\qquad\hat{Y}_{T}=D_{T}f(X_{T}),

along the FSDE (
7
) since then

(69)

D_{T}f(X_{T})-\hat{S}^{\pi}_{T}[f]=\int_{0}^{T}\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{U}_{t}+\hat{V}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}

and the desired control is provided by
\hat{U}_{t}=-\hat{V}_{t}
. The same conclusion can be drawn from
the associated cost function

(70)

\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{T}(\hat{U}_{0:T}):=\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\hat{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\hat{U}_{t}+\hat{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right],

which arises from

(71a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(D_{T}f(X_{T})-\hat{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(D_{T}f(X_{T})-Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{U}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(71b)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{U}_{t}+\hat{V}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

(71c)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\hat{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\hat{U}_{t}+\hat{V}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right]+\hat{M}_{T}

and we have again demonstrated the minimum variance property of the estimator (
17c
) up to the martingale term
\hat{M}_{T}
.

However, contrary to the previous two estimators, the BSDE (
68
) does no longer lead to a standard backward Kolmogorov-type PDE. This is due to the appearance of the

(72)

\hat{U}_{t}^{\rm T}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]=-\hat{V}_{t}^{\rm T}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]

drift term in (
68
). Let us summarize our findings in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

The estimator (
17c
) becomes unbiased for
\hat{Y}_{0}

defined by the FBSDEs

(73a)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,X_{0}\sim\mu,

(73b)

\displaystyle{\rm d}D_{t}

\displaystyle=D_{t}\,(h(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},\qquad\qquad\,D_{0}=1,

(73c)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\hat{Y}_{t}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\hat{V}_{t}^{\rm T}({\rm d}I_{t}+\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}h(X_{t})]{\rm d}t),\qquad\hat{Y}_{T}=D_{T}f(X_{T}).

The control
\hat{U}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, is provided by
\hat{U}_{t}=-\hat{V}_{t}
.

Let us again discuss some implications of the proposed estimator and
introduce the abbreviation

(74)

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}^{\pi}[f]:=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{S}_{T}^{\pi}[f]]=\mu[\hat{Y_{0}}]-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{U}_{t}]^{\rm T}{\rm d}Z_{t},

which implies

(75a)

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{T}-\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}^{\pi}[f]

\displaystyle=\hat{Y}_{0}-\mu[\hat{Y}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}

(75b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{V}_{t}+\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{U}_{t}])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{U}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{U}_{t}])^{\rm T}\pi_{t}[h]{\rm d}t

The choice
\hat{U}_{t}=-\hat{V}_{t}
leads to

(76)

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}^{\pi}[f]=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{Y}_{T}]=\pi_{T}[f]

as well as

(77a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\left(\hat{Y}_{T}-\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}^{\pi}[f]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mu\left[\left(\hat{Y}_{0}-\mu[\hat{Y}_{0}]\right)^{2}\right]

(77b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\hat{Q}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\hat{V}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{V}_{t}]\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right]

(77c)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\mathbb{E}^{\ast}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{V}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{V}_{t}])^{\rm T}\pi_{t}[h]{\rm d}t\right)^{2}\right].

Furthermore, if one applies the averaged control

(78)

\hat{U}_{t}=-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{V}_{t}]

in the BSDE (
68
) directly, then (
75
) turns into

(79)

\hat{Y}_{T}-\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{T}^{\pi}[f]=\hat{Y}_{0}-\mu[\hat{Y}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{V}_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\hat{V}_{t}])^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}.

Hence the integral term (
77c
) vanishes and the resulting (
77
) reveals the more common minimum variance/optimal control aspect of the averaged control (
78
). We also note that the resulting estimator (
17c
) remains unbiased under the averaged control (
78
).

Let us finally connect the discussion so far to the work
of
[
14
,
16
]
, which is based on the estimator (
1
)
and the BSPDE (
2

is not Brownian motion with respect to the path measure

{\rm d}Y_{t}=Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+V_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}W_{t}-\mathcal{U}_{t}^{\rm T}h(X_{t})\,{\rm d}t,\qquad Y_{T}=f(X_{T}).

along the FSDE (
6
). In a next step, we obtain

(81)

Y_{T}-\mathcal{S}_{T}=Y_{0}-\mu[Y_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}(V_{t}+\mathcal{U}_{t})^{\rm T}{\rm d}W_{t}

and

(82)

\mathbb{E}\left[(Y_{T}-\mathcal{S}_{T})^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[(Y_{0}-\mu[Y_{0}])^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|Q_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}+\mathcal{U}_{t}\|^{2}\right){\rm d}t\right].

Please note that the optimal control is
not
provided by

. In fact, we introduce

(83)

\check{Y}_{t}:=D_{t}Y_{t}

and, using (

(84)

{\rm d}\check{Y}_{t}=\check{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\check{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}-D_{t}\,\mathcal{U}_{t}^{\rm T}h(X_{t}){\rm d}t,\qquad\check{Y}_{T}=D_{T}f(X_{T}),

with

and
\check{Q}_{t}

(85)

and
\check{Q}_{t}=D_{t}Q_{t}
, respectively.
Note that the BSDE (

(86)

\pi_{T}[f]=\mu[\check{Y}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\ast}_{I}[\check{V}_{t}]^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{U}_{t}^{\rm T}\pi_{t}[h]{\rm d}t

and we can characterize the dependence of the estimation error on

(87)

\mathcal{S}_{T}-\pi_{T}[f]=-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{U}_{t}+\mathbb{E}^{\ast}_{I}\left[\check{V}_{t}\right]\right)^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}.

The error becomes zero (unbiased estimator) for

(88)

\mathcal{U}_{t}=-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\check{V}_{t}]=-\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[D_{t}V_{t}+D_{t}Y_{t}(h(X_{t})-\pi_{t}[h])],

which also provides the minimizer of (

We finally discuss the rather unconventional estimator (

\bar{U}_{t}=-\bar{V}_{t}

{\rm d}Y_{t}=Q_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+V_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}W_{t}-(V_{t}+Y_{t}h(X_{t}))^{\rm T}h(X_{t}){\rm d}t,\qquad Y_{T}=f(X_{T}).

.

17d
) becomes unbiased for
\bar{Y}_{0}

, where

4.
Application: Optimal control of partially observed diffusion

In this section, we discuss an application of the estimator (
17b
)
to partially observed stochastic optimal control problems
[
3
,
5
]
. More specifically, consider the controlled diffusion process

(100)

{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},\alpha_{t}){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{t}

subject to minimising the cost function

(101)

\mathcal{V}_{T}(\alpha_{0:T})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}c_{t}(X_{t},\alpha_{t}){\rm d}t+f(X_{T})\right]

for given
c_{t}(x,\alpha)
over all admissible controls
\alpha_{0:T}
.
Since we wish to condition the control on the available data, we introduce the separated cost function

(102)

\mathcal{V}_{T}(\alpha_{0:T}|I_{0:T})=\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}\left[\int_{0}^{T}D_{t}c_{t}(X_{t},\alpha_{t}){\rm d}t+D_{T}f(X_{T})\right].

Note that (
102
) corresponds to the conditional expectation value (
5
) for fixed control.

Example 4.1
.

A standard example is provided by adding an additive control, that is,

(103)

b(x,\alpha)=\tilde{b}(x)+G\alpha,

where
G
denotes an appropriate matrix and
\tilde{b}
a control independent drift, and applying a quadratic cost function
c_{t}(x,\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}
.
\Box

Upon extending the techniques developed in Section
3.2
, we construct an estimator of the form

(104)

\mathcal{V}_{T}(\alpha_{0:T}|I_{0:T})=\mu[\breve{Y}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{\ast}[\breve{V}_{t}]^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t},

where
(\breve{Y}_{t},\breve{V}_{t})
satisfy an appropriate generalization of the BSDE (
47
), that is,

(105)

{\rm d}\breve{Y}_{t}=\breve{Q}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}B_{t}+\breve{V}_{t}^{\rm T}{\rm d}I_{t}-D_{t}c_{t}(X_{t},\alpha_{t}){\rm d}t,\qquad\breve{Y}_{T}=D_{T}f(X_{T}),

along the FSDE (
7
) with the drift term in (
7a
) now being given by
b(X_{t},\alpha_{t})
.
Note that
\mu[\breve{Y}_{0}]=\mathcal{V}_{T}(\alpha_{0:T})
.

Following the weak formulation of stochastic optimal control
[
4
,
7
]
, we introduce

(106)

\bar{B}_{t}=B_{t}+\sigma^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s},\alpha_{s}){\rm d}s

and recall that
\bar{B}_{t}
is Brownian motion under a modified probability measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}
according to Girsanov’s theorem
[
20
]
. Hence the FSDEs
(
7a
)-(
7b

(107a)

5.
Conclusions

Building on the previous work
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
on optimal estimation for nonlinear filtering problems, we tackled the problem in this paper from a FBSDE approach, as also widely used in the context of optimal control problems
[
7
]
. Our approach sheds new light on the underlying structure of minimum variance estimation by carefully selecting the set of FBSDEs. In particular, we have strictly followed the classical FBSDE framework by only allowing for Brownian noise in the FBSDE formulations. We have also demonstrated that the two estimation formulations for
\sigma_{T}[f]
lead, in fact, to a deterministic control law. This fact had been previously utilized in
[
14
]
in the context of scenario (ii) for deriving Zakai’s equation; but its full implications only emerge in the context minimum variance estimation, its extension to the estimation of
\pi_{T}[f]
, and applications to stochastic optimal control. A further application could include the study of filter stability using (
98
).

It will also be of interest to explore numerical approximations of the proposed estimators and control laws based, for example, on
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
and their relation to mean-field filtering equations
[
24
,
6
,
21
]
. Finally, the semi-group approach to partially observed optimal control problems, as discussed in
[
19
]
, allows one to avoid the BSPDE formulations (
109

2

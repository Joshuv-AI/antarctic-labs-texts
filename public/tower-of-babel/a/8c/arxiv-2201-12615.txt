Abstract
. In the present paper, the Ising model with mixed spin-(1,1/2) is considered on the second order Cayley tree. A construction of splitting Gibbs measures corresponding the model is given which allows to establish the existence of the phase transition (non-uniqueness of Gibbs measures). We point out that, in the phase transition region, the considered model has three translation-invariant Gibbs measures in the ferromagnetic and anti-ferromagnetic regimes, while the classical Ising model does not possesses such Gibbs measures in the anti-ferromagnetic regime. It turns out that the considered model, like the Ising model, exhibits a disordered Gibbs measure. Therefore, non-extremity and extremity of such disordered Gibbs measures is investigated by means of tree-indexed Markov chains. Keywords : the mixed spin-(1,1/2) Ising model, Gibbs measures, phase transition, disorder phase.



1.
Introduction

In the last decades, the Ising model has been one of the most
intensively studied model which used to describe critical
behaviours of certain systems in natural sciences. Many
interesting results have been observed in the phase transition
theory by means of exactly solvable Ising models
[
1
]
.
Recently, several extension have been carried out in the a mixed
spin Ising model to describe a wide range of systems
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Because it
is the lowest mixed-spin system, the mixed spin-1 and spin-1/2
system is an excellent candidate for studying mixed-spins. Higher
spins have higher critical temperatures, as is generally known,
i.e. spin-1 is a higher spin than spin-1/2. In addition, unlike
the spin-1/2 system, the spin-1 system exhibits first-order phase
transitions for acceptable parameters
[
11
,
17
]
. In

[
2
]
, Silva and Salinas performed some exact
calculations for a Curie-Weiss or mean-field version of the
mixed-spin Ising Hamiltonian on the Bethe lattice. In

[
3
]
, it has been studied the entropy and
isothermal entropy change of the mixed spin-(1,1/2) Ising model on
the Bethe lattice by means of the exact recursion relations for
the coordination numbers
q=3,4
and 6. In the present situation, there
are plenty of works devoted such types of investigations. All
existing works related to mixed spin Ising models deal with
physical approach from the physics perspective. Mostly, it has been
employed the exact iterative relations technique/the
renormalization group theory, Monte Carlo simulations,
Green-function technique, the mean-field theory and a path
probability method (see
[
9
]
, for related references).

On the other hand, it is essential to investigated the mentioned
models from a rigorous mathematical perspective. In this regard,
statistical mechanical approach is adequate, since it predicts the
relation between the observable macroscopic properties of the
system given only the knowledge of the microscopic interactions
between components. This can be explained by mathematical
framework. In this scheme, Gibbs measures are one of the central
objects of equilibrium statistical mechanics
[
8
]
.
Therefore, one of the main problems of statistical physics is to
describe all Gibbs measures corresponding to the given
Hamiltonian. To the best knowledge of the authors, the mixed spin
Ising models on the Cayley tree (the same as Bathe lattice

[
24
]
) are not well studied from the measure-theoretical point
of view. Therefore, one of the main aims of the present paper is
to develop a measure-theoretic approach (i.e. Gibbs measure
formalism) and rigorously establish the phase transition for the
Ising model with mixed spin on the Cayley trees.

In the rigorous approach, the Ising model on the Cayley tree
always has a disordered phase which corresponds to a Gibbs measure
having no external effect. In
[
7
,
10
]
, it was
established that the disordered Gibbs measure, in the
ferromagnetic Ising model on the Cayley tree, is extreme, for

T\geq T^{SG}_{c}
, where
T^{SG}_{c}
is the critical temperature of
the spin glass model, and it is not extreme for
T<T^{SG}_{c}
. We
point out that the extremality of the disordered phase of lattice
models is important in the theory of information flows

[
15
,
18
,
19
]
. Therefore, there are several works
devoted to the extremality problem of Gibbs measures for variety
of models on trees (see

[
13
,
14
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
).

In present paper, we consider the Ising
model with mixed spin-(1,1/2) on the second order Cayley tree. We construct Gibbs measures
corresponding the model, which allows us to establish the
existence of the phase transition (non-uniqueness of Gibbs
measures).

The main result of this paper is the following one (see Fig
1
):

.

Assume that
J\neq 0
and
\theta=\exp\{J\beta/2\}

(i)

If

, where

are given by (

(ii)

There exists

Interestingly, in the phase transition region of the considered model has a totally different phase diagram comparing to the Ising model. Indeed, the considered model possess three splitting translation-invariant Gibbs measures in both the ferromagnetic and
anti-ferromagnetic regimes, whereas the classical Ising
model does not have translation-invariant Gibbs measures in the
anti-ferromagnetic regime (i.e.
\theta<1

[

,
8
]
.
Moreover, it turns out that the considered model, like the Ising model, exhibits a
disordered Gibbs measure.

The proof of this theorem will be given in forthcoming sections.

3.
Splitting Translation invariant Gibbs measures (TIGMs)

In this section, we deal with the existence of splitting
translation-invariant Gibbs measures (STIGMs) corresponding to the
Ising model with mixed spin-1 and spin-1/2 by analyzing the the
equations (
2.4
)-(
2.6
). Note that the
vector valued functions

\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}=\{\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{-\frac{1}{2}}(x),\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\}

and
\boldsymbol{h}=\{\boldsymbol{h}_{-1}(x),\boldsymbol{h}_{0}(x),\boldsymbol{h}_{1}(x)\}

are considered as
translation-invariant
if

\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{i}(x)=\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{i}(y)
and

\boldsymbol{h}_{j}(x)=\boldsymbol{h}_{j}(y)
for all

, where

and
j\in\{-1,01\}
(see

[
26
]
for details). Then the corresponding measure is called
splitting translation invariant Gibbs measure
.
In what follows, we restrict ourselves to the tree of order two (i.e.
k=2
).

For the sake of convenience, let us denote:
\boldsymbol{h}_{j}:=\boldsymbol{h}_{j}(x)
for all
x\in\Gamma_{+}^{2}
,
j\in\Phi
,
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{i}:=\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{i}(x)
,
x\in\Gamma_{-}^{2}
,

.

Assuming

and

), (
2.5
), (
2.6
)
we obtain

(3.1)

\exp(U_{1})=\left(\frac{\theta^{2}+\exp(V)}{\theta(1+\exp(V))}\right)^{2},

(3.2)

\exp(U_{2})=\left(\frac{1+\theta^{2}\exp(V)}{\theta(1+\exp(V)}\right)^{2}.

(3.3)

\exp(V)=\left(\frac{\exp(U_{1})+\theta^{2}\exp(U_{2})+\theta}{\theta^{2}\exp(U_{1})+\exp(U_{2})+\theta}\right)^{2},

where
\theta=e^{{\frac{J\beta}{2}}}
.

Now, let us consider substitutions as
X=\exp(U_{1})
,

and

, then the last equations
reduce to

(3.4)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\theta^{2}+Z}{\theta(1+Z)}\right)^{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\theta^{2}Z}{\theta(1+Z)}\right)^{2},

(3.6)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{X+\theta^{2}Y+\theta}{\theta^{2}X+Y+\theta}\right)^{2}.

By Theorem
2.1

We notice that the (

The corresponding STIGM

Remark 3.1
.

We notice that for symmetric models (interactions) the disordered Gibbs measure corresponds (in most cases)
to the zero solution of the recurrent equations. However, if the model is not symmetric, then
corresponding recurrent equation has no zero solution. In that case, the disordered
measure is defined as a translation-invariant Gibbs measure which corresponds
to the free boundary condition. Therefore, in some literatures (see for example

[
18
]
) the disordered measure is called free measure.

4.

In this section, we are going to investigate stability of the fixed point

6.
Extremality of the Disordered Phase by Tree-indexed Markov chains

In this section, we are going to prove the second part of Theorem
1.1
. The extremality of the disordered phase of the Ising model with mixed spin
(1,1/2)
is investigated by means of tree-indexed Markov chains. Let us recall tree-indexed Markov chains. Suppose that we are given a tree with vertices set
V
, a probability measure
\nu
and a transition matrix
\mathbb{P}=(P_{ij})_{i,j\in\Phi}
on the single-site space which is here a finite set
\Phi=\{1,2,\dots,q\}
. We can obtain a tree-indexed Markov chain
X:V\rightarrow\Phi
by choosing
X(x^{0})
according to
\nu
and choosing
X{\nu},
for each vertex
v\neq x^{0}
, using the transition probabilities given the value of its parent, independently of everything else (see
[
8
, Definition 12.2]
for details).

Let us define the entries of the transition probabilities matrices as follows:

\mathbb{P}=(P_{ij})
as

P_{ij}=\frac{\exp(ij\beta J+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{j})}{\sum\limits_{u=\mp\frac{1}{2}}\exp(iu\beta J+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{u})},

where
i\in\{-1,0,1\}
and
j\in\{-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
.

Assume that

Z=\exp(\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{\widetilde{h}}_{-\frac{1}{2}})
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\theta^{2}Z}{1+\theta^{2}Z}&\frac{1}{1+\theta^{2}Z}\\
\frac{Z}{1+Z}&\frac{1}{1+Z}\\
\frac{Z}{\theta^{2}+Z}&\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta^{2}+Z}\end{array}\right).

Similarly, we define the entries of the transition probabilities
matrix
\mathbb{Q}=(Q_{ij})
by

Q_{ij}=\frac{\exp(ij\beta J+\boldsymbol{h}_{j})}{\sum\limits_{\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}}\exp(i\ell\beta J+\boldsymbol{h}_{\ell})},

where
i\in\{-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
and
j\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

Assuming
X=\exp(\boldsymbol{h}_{-1}-\boldsymbol{h}_{0})
and

Y=\exp(\boldsymbol{h}_{1}-\boldsymbol{h}_{0})
, then one finds

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\theta^{2}X}{\theta^{2}X+\theta+Y}&\frac{\theta}{\theta^{2}X+\theta+Y}&\frac{Y}{\theta^{2}X+\theta+Y}\\
\frac{X}{X+\theta+\theta^{2}Y}&\frac{\theta}{X+\theta+\theta^{2}Y}&\frac{\theta^{2}Y}{X+\theta+\theta^{2}Y}\end{array}\right).

Therefore, from (
6
) and (
6
), a
STIGM corresponding to a vector
v=(X,Y,Z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(which is the solution to system of the equations
(
3.4
)-(
3.6
)) is a tree-indexed Markov
chain with states
\{-1,0,1\}
and transition probabilities
matrix is obtained as follows:

\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{P}\mathbb{Q}=\frac{1}{(X+\theta+Y\theta^{2})}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{X\left(1+\theta^{4}\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(1+Z\theta^{2}\right)}&\frac{\theta\left(1+\theta^{2}\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(1+Z\theta^{2}\right)}&\frac{Y\theta^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(1+Z\theta^{2}\right)}\\
\frac{X\left(1+\theta^{2}\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{(1+Z)}&\frac{\theta\left(1+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{(1+Z)}&\frac{Y\left(\theta^{2}+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{(1+Z)}\\
\frac{X\theta^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(Z+\theta^{2}\right)}&\frac{\theta\left(\theta^{2}+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(Z+\theta^{2}\right)}&\frac{Y\left(\theta^{4}+\sqrt{Z^{3}}\right)}{\left(Z+\theta^{2}\right)}\end{array}\right).

Note that the matrices
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{H}

are stochastic ones. Noticing that the disordered phase corresponds to

Z=1
and
X=Y=\left(\frac{1+\theta^{2}}{2\theta}\right)^{2}
, then the corresponding matrix
\mathbb{H}
reduces to

(6.8)

\mathbb{H}=\frac{1}{1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)\left(1+\theta^{4}\right)&4\theta^{3}&2\theta^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)\\
\frac{\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)^{3}}{2}&4\theta^{3}&\frac{\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)^{3}}{2}\\
2\theta^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)&4\theta^{3}&\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)\left(1+\theta^{4}\right)\end{array}\right).

In order to investigate the criterion of extremality of a STISGM,
let us now give the definitions of quantities
\kappa
and

\gamma
given in the Ref.
[
16
]
. Let

\mu^{s}_{\mathcal{T}_{x}}
denote the (finite-volume) Gibbs measure
in which the parent of
x
has its spin fixed to
s
and the
configuration on the bottom boundary of
\mathcal{T}_{x}
(i.e.,
on
\partial\mathcal{T}_{x}\setminus\{\text{parent of}\ x\}
) is
specified by
\mathcal{T}
(see

[
16
,
18
,
29
]
).

For two measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{1}
on
\Omega
,
||\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}||_{x}
denotes the variation distance between the projections of

\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{1}
onto the spin at
x
, i.e.

||\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}||_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\Phi}\left|\mu_{1}(\sigma(x)=i)-\mu_{2}(\sigma(x)=i)\right|.

Let
\eta^{x,s}
be the configuration
\eta
with the spin at
x

set to
s
.

Definition 6.1
.

[
16
, Definition 3.1]

For a collection of Gibbs distributions

\{\mu^{s}_{\mathcal{T}_{x}}\}
, define the quantities

\kappa\equiv\kappa(\{\mu^{s}_{\mathcal{T}_{x}}\})
and

\gamma\equiv\gamma(\{\mu^{s}_{\mathcal{T}_{x}}\})
by

(1)

\kappa=\sup\limits_{z\in\Gamma^{k}}\max\limits_{z,s,s^{\prime}}\|\mu_{T_{z}}^{s}-\mu_{T_{z}}^{s^{\prime}}\|_{z}

(2)

\gamma=\sup\limits_{A\subset\Gamma^{k}}\max_{z,s,s^{\prime}}\|\mu_{A}^{\eta^{y,s}}-\mu_{A}^{\eta^{y,s^{\prime}}}\|_{z}
, where the maximum is taken over all boundary conditions
\eta
, all sites
y\in\partial A
, all neighbors

x\in A
of
y
, and all spins
s,s^{\prime}\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

It is known
[
16
, Theorem 9.3]
that a sufficient condition for extremality of the translation-invariant Gibbs measure is the following
inequality:

(6.9)

k\kappa\gamma<1.

Note that
\kappa
has the particularly simple form
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}\max\limits_{i,j}\left\{\sum\limits_{l=1}^{3}\left|H_{i,l}-H_{j,l}\right|\right\}
.

From the Eq. (
6.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6})}\max\left[\left\{(-1+\theta)^{2}(1+\theta)^{2},2(-1+\theta)^{2}(1+\theta)^{2},(-1+\theta)^{2}(1+\theta)^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}}{(1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6})},

here we have used that
k=2
.

From the definition
6.1
, it is easy that

\gamma=\kappa
. So, the extramality condition (
6.9
) reduces to

2\kappa\gamma-1=2\left(\frac{\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}}{(1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6})}\right)^{2}-1<0.

One can see that
1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6}=(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}
. Therefore,

(6.10)

\displaystyle 2\kappa\gamma-1

\displaystyle=2

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}\right)^{2}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}-1\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}+1\right)<0.

It is obvious that (
6.10
) is equivalent to

(6.11)

\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}-1<0.

From (
6.11
), one gets

\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)-\left(4\theta^{3}+\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)^{3}\right)<0.

After some long and tedious algebraic operations, the last one reduces to

(-1+\sqrt{2})+\left(-3-\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{2}-4\theta^{3}+\left(-3-\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{4}+\left(-1+\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{6}<0.

and after some algebraic operations, finally we arrive at

(6.12)

(\sqrt{2}-1)\vartheta^{3}-(4\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}-3)\vartheta-4<0,

where
\vartheta=\theta+\frac{1}{\theta}.

Using Cardano’s formula and Mathematica
[
30
]
, the solution of (
6.12
) is
\vartheta<\vartheta_{crt}
, where

\vartheta_{crt}\approx 3.634
. Therefore, from
\theta+\frac{1}{\theta}<3.634
, one finds

(6.13)

f(\theta)=\theta^{2}-3.634\theta+1<0.

So, the solution of the last inequality is
\theta\in(\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2})
, where

(6.14)

\tilde{\theta}_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\vartheta_{crt}\pm\sqrt{\vartheta_{crt}^{2}-4}\big).

Note that

\tilde{\theta}_{1}\approx 0.299934,\tilde{\theta}_{2}\approx 3.33407
.

Consequently, we get the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\theta\in(\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2})
, then the disordered phase is extreme.

Now, it is natural to ask: if
\theta\notin(\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2})
, is the disordered phase extreme?
To respond the raised question, we are going to determine the regions of the
parameters in which the disordered phase is not extreme in the set
of all Gibbs measures (including the non-translation invariant
ones). It is known that a sufficient condition (Kesten-Stigum
condition
[
12
]
) for non-extremality of a
Gibbs measure
\mu
corresponding to the matrix
\mathbb{H}
on a
Cayley tree of order
k\geq 1
is that
k|\lambda^{2}_{\max}|>1
,
where
|\lambda_{\max}|
is the second largest (in absolute value)
eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}
given in (
6.8
). Furthermore,
we are going to employ this condition to determine the
non-extremity of the disordered phase.

One can show that the set of the eigenvalues of the stochastic

\mathbb{H}
given in (
6.8
) are the following ones:

\left\{0,\frac{\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6}},1\right\}.

Due to
1+3\theta^{2}+4\theta^{3}+3\theta^{4}+\theta^{6}=(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}
, the above condition can be rewritten as

(6.15)

\displaystyle V(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda^{2}_{\max}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}\right)^{2}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}-1\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}+1\right)>0.

Obviously (
6.15
) is equivalent to

(6.16)

\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{(1+\theta^{2})^{3}+4\theta^{3}}-1>0.

From (
6.16
), one finds

\sqrt{2}\left(-1+\theta^{2}\right)^{2}\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)-\left(4\theta^{3}+\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)^{3}\right)>0.

After some long and tedious algebraic operations, we get

(-1+\sqrt{2})+\left(-3-\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{2}-4\theta^{3}+\left(-3-\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{4}+\left(-1+\sqrt{2}\right)\theta^{6}>0.

Hence, by denotiing
\vartheta=\theta+\frac{1}{\theta}
the last one is reduced to

(6.17)

(\sqrt{2}-1)\vartheta^{3}-(4\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}-3)\vartheta-4>0.

One can observe that (
6.17
) and (
6.12
) are complementary inequalities, therefore, the solution of (
6.17
) (in terms of
\theta
) is
(0,\tilde{\theta}_{1})\cup(\theta_{2},\infty)
.
Now, keeping in mind Theorem
6.2
we obtain the following important result.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
J\neq 0
and consider the Ising model with mixed spin
(1,1/2)
on a Cayley tree of order two. Then There exist
\tilde{\theta}_{1}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{2}
(see (
6.14
)) such that the disordered phase
\mu_{0}
is extreme if and only if

\theta\in(\tilde{\theta}_{1},\tilde{\theta}_{2});

where
\theta=\exp\{J\beta/2\}
.

Remark 6.1
.

We notice that, for the Ising model on the Cayley tree, the
disordered phase is extreme, if and only if
\exp\{J\beta\}<1/\sqrt{k}

[
7
,
10
]
.
On the other hand, the Ising model with mixed spin
(1,1/2)
also has like Potts kind of behavior. However, for the Potts model
on the Cayley tree the extremality of the disordered phase has been investigated in
[
14
,
18
,
28
]
. It turns out that there are two critical values
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
such that if
\theta<\theta_{1}
then the disordered phase is extreme, if
\theta>\theta_{2}
then it is not extreme, but in the region
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
its the extremality is still an open problem. We stress that in the considered setting,
the extremity condition is similar to the Ising model.

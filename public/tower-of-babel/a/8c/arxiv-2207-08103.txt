Pre- ( n + 𝟐 ) \bm{(n+2)} -angulated categories ★ † † footnotetext: ★ Panyue Zhou was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 11901190).
Jing He, Panyue Zhou, Xingjia Zhou

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the notion of pre- ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories as higher dimensional analogues of pre-triangulated categories defined by Beligiannis-Reiten. We first show that the idempotent completion of a pre- ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category admits a unique structure of pre- ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category. Let ( 𝒞 , 𝔼 , 𝔰 ) (\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s}) be an n n -exangulated category and 𝒳 \mathscr{X} be a strongly functorially finite subcategory of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . We then show that the quotient category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is a pre- ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category. These results allow to construct several examples of pre- ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories. Moreover, we also give a necessary and sufficient condition for the quotient 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} to be an ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category. Keywords: n n -exangulated categories; ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories; n n -exact categories; idempotent completion; quotient categories 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10

Introduction

Triangulated categories were introduced in the mid 1960’s by Verdier
[
V
]

Having their origins in algebraic geometry and algebraic topology, triangulated categories have by now become indispensable in many different areas of mathematics.
Geiss, Keller and Oppermann
[
GKO
]
introduced
a new type of categories, called
(n+2)
-angulated categories, which generalize triangulated categories: the classical triangulated categories are the special case
n=1
.
Jasso
[
J
]
introduced
n
-abelian and
n
-exact categories, these are analogs of abelian and
exact categories from the point of view of higher homological algebra.
The case
n=1
corresponds to the usual concepts of abelian and exact categories. Later, Lin
[
L2
]
introduced right
(n+2)
-angulated categories, the right triangulated categories in the sense of Assem, Beligiannis and Marmaridis
[
ABM
]
are the special case
n=1
.

Extriangulated categories were recently introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
by extracting those properties of
{\rm Ext}^{1}
on exact categories and on triangulated categories that seem relevant from the point of view of cotorsion pairs. In particular, triangulated categories and exact categories are extriangulated categories. There are many examples of extriangulated categories which are neither triangulated categories nor exact categories, see
see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
,
ZhZ
,
NP1
]
.
Recently, Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN1
]
introduced the notion of
n
-exangulated categories. It should be noted that the case
n=1
corresponds to extriangulated categories. As typical examples we have that
n
-exact and
(n+2)
-angulated categories are
n
-exangulated, see
[
HLN1
, Proposition 4.5 and Proposition 4.34]
. However, there are some other examples of
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
(n+2)
-angulated nor
n
-exact, see
[
HLN1
,
HLN2
,
LZ
,
HZZ
,
HZZ1
,
HHZ
,
KMS
]
.

Beligiannis and Reiten
[
BR
]
introduced the notion of pre-triangulated categories
which provides a common generalization of abelian,
triangulated and stable categories.
Based on Beligiannis-Reiten’s idea, we introduce the notion of
pre-
(n+2)
-angulated categories, see Definition
3.1
.
Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
4.4
for more details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma^{n},\Omega^{n},\Theta,\Phi,\varepsilon,\eta)
be a pre-
(n+2)
-angulated category.
Then its idempotent completion
(\widetilde{\mathscr{C}},\widetilde{\Sigma^{n}},\widetilde{\Omega^{n}},\widetilde{\Theta},\widetilde{\Phi},\widetilde{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\eta})
admits a unique structure of pre-
(n+2)
-angulated category.

Our second result shows
how to equip quotient categories with a pre-
(n+2)
-angulated structure as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
5.4
for more details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category and

\mathscr{X}
be a strongly functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
.
Then the quotient category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is a pre-
(n+2)
-angulated category.

As an application of Theorem
1.2
, we show
how to equip quotient categories with an
(n+2)
-angulated structure.
This recovers and gives a short proof to a result of Zhou
[

, Theorem 5.7]

Theorem 1.3
.

(see Theorem
6.5
for more details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma^{n},\Theta)
be an
(n+2)
-angulated category with
a Serre functor
\mathbb{S}
, and
\mathscr{X}
be a strongly functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
.
Then the pre-
(n+2)
-angulated category

is
(n+2)
-angulated if and only

.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review basic concepts and results on
n
-exangulated categories.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category and

\mathbb{E}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ab}~~\mbox{($\mathsf{Ab}$ is the category of abelian groups)}

an additive bifunctor.
For any pair of objects
A,C\in\mathscr{C}
, an element
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called an
\mathbb{E}
-extension
or simply an
extension
. We also write such
\delta
as
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
when we indicate
A
and
C
. The zero element
{}_{A}0_{C}=0\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called the
split
\mathbb{E}
-extension
. For any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
, let
\delta\oplus\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})
be the
element corresponding to
(\delta,0,0,{\delta}{{}^{\prime}})
through the natural isomorphism
\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})\simeq\mathbb{E}(C,A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A^{\prime})
.

For any
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},C)
,
\mathbb{E}(C,a)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{E}(c,A)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)
are simply denoted by
a_{\ast}\delta
and
c^{\ast}\delta
, respectively.

Let
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
be any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions. A
morphism

(a,c)\colon\delta\to{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
of extensions is a pair of morphisms
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C,C^{\prime})
in
\mathscr{C}
, satisfying the equality

a_{\ast}\delta=c^{\ast}{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category and
n
be any positive integer.

Definition 2.1
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.7]

Let
\bf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
whose components are zero in the degrees outside of
\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}
. Namely, an object in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
is a complex
X_{\bullet}=\{X_{i},d^{X}_{i}\}
of the form

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{0}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{n-1}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{n}}X_{n+1}.

We write a morphism
f_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
simply
f_{\bullet}=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})
, only indicating the terms of degrees
0,\ldots,n+1
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.11]

By Yoneda lemma, any extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
induces natural transformations

\delta_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(-,C)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(-,A)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta^{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,-)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(C,-).

For any
X\in\mathscr{C}
, these
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}
are given as follows.

(1)

(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(X,C)\to\mathbb{E}(X,A)\ :\ f\mapsto f^{\ast}\delta
.

(2)

\delta^{\sharp}_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to\mathbb{E}(C,X)\ :\ g\mapsto g_{\ast}\delta
.

We simply denote
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}(g)
by
\delta_{\sharp}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}(g)
, respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.9]

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},n
be as before. Define a category
\AE:=\AE^{n+2}_{(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E})}
as follows.

(1)

A object in
\AE^{n+2}_{(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E})}
is a pair
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
of
X_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}

and
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},X_{0})
satisfying

(d_{0}^{X})_{\ast}\delta=0~~\textrm{and}~~(d^{X}_{n})^{\ast}\delta=0.

We call such a pair an
\mathbb{E}
-attached
complex of length
n+2
. We also denote it by

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-2}^{X}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}.

(2)

For such pairs
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
and
\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle
, a morphism
f_{\bullet}\colon\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle\to\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle
is
defined to be a morphism
f_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}(X_{\bullet},Y_{\bullet})
satisfying
(f_{0})_{\ast}\delta=(f_{n+1})^{\ast}\rho
.

We use the same composition and the identities as in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.13]

An
n
-exangle
is a pair
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
of
X_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}

and
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},X_{0})
which satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

The following sequence of functors
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Ab}
is exact.

\mathscr{C}(-,X_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{0})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{n})}\mathscr{C}(-,X_{n+1})\xrightarrow{~\delta_{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(-,X_{0})

(2)

The following sequence of functors
\mathscr{C}\to\mathsf{Ab}
is exact.

\mathscr{C}(X_{n+1},-)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{n},\ -)}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{0},\ -)}\mathscr{C}(X_{0},-)\xrightarrow{~\delta^{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},-)

In particular any
n
-exangle is an object in
\AE
.
A
morphism of
n
-exangles
simply means a morphism in
\AE
. Thus
n
-exangles form a full subcategory of
\AE
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.22]

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a correspondence which associates a homotopic equivalence class
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[{}_{A}{X_{\bullet}}_{C}]
to each extension
\delta={}_{A}\delta_{C}
. Such
\mathfrak{s}
is called a
realization
of
\mathbb{E}
if it satisfies the following condition for any
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
and any
\mathfrak{s}(\rho)=[Y_{\bullet}]
.

(R0)

For any morphism of extensions
(a,c)\colon\delta\to\rho
, there exists a morphism
f_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}(X_{\bullet},Y_{\bullet})
of the form
f_{\bullet}=(a,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n},c)
. Such
f_{\bullet}
is called a
lift
of
(a,c)
.

In such a case, we simply say that “
X_{\bullet}
realizes
\delta
” whenever they satisfy
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
.

Moreover, a realization
\mathfrak{s}
of
\mathbb{E}
is said to be
exact
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(R1)

For any
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
, the pair
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
is an
n
-exangle.

(R2)

For any
A\in\mathscr{C}
, the zero element
{}_{A}0_{0}=0\in\mathbb{E}(0,A)
satisfies

\mathfrak{s}({}_{A}0_{0})=[A\overset{\mathrm{id}_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A\to 0\to\cdots\to 0\to 0].

Dually,
\mathfrak{s}({}_{0}0_{A})=[0\to 0\to\cdots\to 0\to A\overset{\mathrm{id}_{A}}{\longrightarrow}A]
holds for any
A\in\mathscr{C}
.

Note that the above condition (R1) does not depend on representatives of the class
[X_{\bullet}]
.

Definition 2.6
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.23]

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be an exact realization of
\mathbb{E}
.

(1)

An
n
-exangle
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
is called an
\mathfrak{s}
-
distinguished

n
-exangle if it satisfies
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
. We often simply say

n
-exangle
when
\mathfrak{s}
is clear from the context.

(2)

An object

is called an
\mathfrak{s}
-conflation
or simply a
conflation
if it realizes some extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},X_{0})
.

(3)

A morphism
f
in
\mathscr{C}
is called an
\mathfrak{s}
-inflation
or simply an
inflation
if it admits some conflation
X_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
satisfying
d_{0}^{X}=f
.

(4)

A morphism
g
in
\mathscr{C}
is called an
\mathfrak{s}
-deflation
or simply a
deflation
if it admits some conflation
X_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
satisfying
d_{n}^{X}=g
.

Definition 2.7
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.27]

For a morphism
f_{\bullet}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}(X_{\bullet},Y_{\bullet})
satisfying
f_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
for some
A=X_{0}=Y_{0}
, its
mapping cone

M_{{}_{\bullet}}^{f}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
is defined to be the complex

X_{1}\xrightarrow{d^{M_{f}}_{0}}X_{2}\oplus Y_{1}\xrightarrow{d^{M_{f}}_{1}}X_{3}\oplus Y_{2}\xrightarrow{d^{M_{f}}_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d^{M_{f}}_{n-1}}X_{n+1}\oplus Y_{n}\xrightarrow{d^{M_{f}}_{n}}Y_{n+1}

where
d^{M_{f}}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}-d^{X}_{1}\\
f_{1}\end{bmatrix},

d^{M_{f}}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}-d^{X}_{i+1}&0\\
f_{i+1}&d^{Y}_{i}\end{bmatrix}\ (1\leq i\leq n-1),

d^{M_{f}}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{n+1}&d^{Y}_{n}\end{bmatrix}
.

The mapping cocone
is defined dually, for morphisms
h_{\bullet}
in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
satisfying
h_{n+1}=\mathrm{id}
.

Definition 2.8
.

[
HLN1
, Definition 2.32]

An
n
-exangulated category
is a triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
of additive category
\mathscr{C}
, additive bifunctor
\mathbb{E}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathscr{C}\to\mathsf{Ab}
, and its exact realization
\mathfrak{s}
, satisfying the following conditions.

(EA1)

Let

\mathscr{C}
. If both

and

. Dually, if
f
and
g

.

(EA2)

For

and

, let
{}_{A}\langle X_{\bullet},c^{\ast}\rho\rangle_{C}
and
{}_{A}\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle_{D}

-exangles. Then

has a
good lift

-exangle
\langle M^{f}_{{}_{\bullet}},(d^{X}_{0})_{\ast}\rho\rangle

(EA2
op
)

Dual of (EA2).

Note that in the case

, a triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is a

-exangulated category if and only if it is an extriangulated category, see

6.1
Auslander-Reiten

-angles and Serre functors

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma^{n},\Theta)
be an
(n+2)
-angulated category. We denote by

the Jacobson radical of

. Namely, it is given by the formula

{\rm rad}_{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)=\{g\colon X\to Y~|~{\rm Id_{\emph{X}}-}hg~\mbox{is invertible for any}~h\colon Y\to X\},

for all objects

and

Next, we recall some terminology for the Auslander–Reiten
theory. Let

be a morphism. One says that

is
right almost split
if

is not a split epimorphism and every non-split epimorphism

factors through
f
.
In a dual manner, one defines

to be

.

.

[

, Definition 3.8]
and
[

be a

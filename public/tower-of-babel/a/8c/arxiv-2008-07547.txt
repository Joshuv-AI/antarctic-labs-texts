Universal spectral form factor for many-body localization
Abhishodh Prakash, J. H. Pixley, Manas Kulkarni

Abstract
We theoretically study correlations present deep in the spectrum of many-body-localized systems. An exact analytical expression for the spectral form factor of Poisson spectra can be obtained and is shown to agree well with numerical results on two models exhibiting many-body-localization: a disordered quantum spin chain and a phenomenological l l -bit model based on the existence of local integrals of motion. We also identify a universal regime that is insensitive to the global density of states as well as spectral edge effects.



Understanding how thermal equilibrium may, or may not emerge in isolated many-body quantum systems remains a central question in quantum statistical mechanics.
Thermal
systems which are said to exhibit
quantum chaos
satisfy the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH)
Srednicki 1994
;
Srednicki 1999
whose subsystems equilibriate under their own dynamics.
In addition to being highly entangled, i.e. satisfying a “volume law” scaling with subsystem size, the eigenspectra of these systems exhibit long range repulsions that are captured by random matrix theory and produce universal features in their correlations measured in their
spectral form factor
(SFF, defined below in
eqs.

4
and
5
) such as the linear ramp
Haake 2010
;
Cotler et al. 2017
;
Gharibyan et al. 2018
;
Liu 2018
;
Chen and Ludwig 2018
;
Bertini et al. 2018
;
Kos et al. 2018
(as shown in
fig.

1
). In the presence of strong quenched randomness or quasiperiodicity, quantum systems can become
many body localized
(MBL)
Nandkishore and Huse 2015
;
Abanin et al. 2019
;
Gopalakrishnan and Parameswaran 2020
;
Deng et al. 2017
where ETH is violated. In contrast to chaotic systems, MBL is characterized by eigenstates with short-range “area law” entanglement and an absence of level repulsion. Recent experiments on ultra-cold atomic gases
Schreiber et al. 2015
;
Choi et al. 2016
;
Lukin et al. 2019
, trapped ions
Smith et al. 2016
, superconducting qubits
Roushan et al. 2017
;
Xu et al. 2018
and nuclear spins
Wei et al. 2018
have provided evidence for the existence of the MBL phase.

Instabilities to MBL have been argued to arise in high dimensions
De Roeck and Huveneers 2017
and in the presence of certain symmetries
Potter and Vasseur 2016
. More recently however, the very existence of the MBL phase has been challenged based on a finite size scaling analysis of the linear ramp of the SFF on approach to the MBL transition from the chaotic side
Šuntajs et al. 2020a
. A critique of this work was subsequently presented
Abanin et al. 2021
pointing out the intricacies involved in finite sized calculations and conclusions drawn from them, while further studies have highlighted the difficulty in studying the MBL transition in finite size numerics
Sierant et al. 2020
;
Panda et al. 2020
. Recently, the authors of Ref.
Šuntajs et al. 2020a
pointed out that their claim of the absence of MBL is due to their choice of scaling function, which instead should follow a Kosteritz-Thouless “like” scaling form as they demonstrate in Ref.
Šuntajs et al. 2020b
consistent with recent theories of the MBL transition
Goremykina et al. 2019
;
Dumitrescu et al. 2019
;
Morningstar et al. 2020
.
Irrespective of the question of validity of the finite-size numerics in the vicinity of the MBL
transition
, the question of how to characterize the MBL
phase
using the SFF alone, is undoubtedly worthy of further examination.
If the MBL phase indeed exists, it is conceivable that its SFF has its own universal features to which any putative system exhibiting MBL should be compared. However, apart from a few hints
Chan et al. 2018
, the existence of such a form and an understanding of its features has been lacking thus far.

In this Letter, we investigate the spectral correlations in MBL systems. We show that the SFF for MBL systems can be calculated deep in the spectrum due to their convergence with
Poisson levels
for which we can derive an exact analytical expression with a finite number of levels
Riser et al. 2020
, (plotted as a solid line in
fig.

1
).
We determine the validity of this expression by comparing it with numerical simulations of a phenomenological
l
-bit model
Serbyn et al. 2013
;
Huse et al. 2014
as well as a microscopic disordered many-body Hamiltonian. In both cases, by focusing on states in the middle of the many body spectra where the many-body density of states is nearly flat, we find excellent agreement between the exact expression and the numerical results. In the limit of an infinite number of levels, to leading order, our results reduce to the general expectation of integrable systems due to Berry and Tabor
Berry and Tabor 1977
;
Marklof 2001
. However, we show that the leading correction to the SFF beyond that of Berry and Tabor is universal in an intermediate power-law scaling regime and is robust to changes in the global density of states as well as spectral edge effects. Our results provide further support for the existence of the MBL phase in one dimension.

Models for many-body localization
: To make a detailed comparison with the properties of the MBL phase, we consider two different models. The first is a quantum spin-chain with quenched disorder whose Hamiltonian is

H=\sum_{i}J_{1}(S^{x}_{i}S^{x}_{i+1}+S^{y}_{i}S^{y}_{i+1}+\Delta S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{i+1})+w_{i}S^{z}_{i}\\
+\sum_{i}J_{2}(S^{x}_{i}S^{x}_{i+2}+S^{y}_{i}S^{y}_{i+2}+\Delta S^{z}_{i}S^{z}_{i+2}).

(1)

S^{\alpha}
are spin operators that can be written in terms of Pauli matrices as
S^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{\alpha}
and the random couplings
w_{i}
are drawn from a uniform distribution
[-W,W]
.
Variants of this model have been previously studied
Šuntajs et al. 2020a
;
Khemani et al. 2017a
;
Khemani et al. 2017b
and are known to have a thermal phase at weak disorder and an MBL phase at strong disorder.
Following Ref
Šuntajs et al. 2020a
, we set
J_{1}=J_{2}=1.0
and
\Delta=0.55
.

Deep in the MBL phase, any local Hamiltonian such as
eq.

1
can be described by a complete set of emergent local integrals of motion
Serbyn et al. 2013
;
Huse et al. 2014
. This means that there should exist a finite depth unitary circuit
U
that can recast
H
into a diagonal form,
UHU^{\dagger}=H_{lbit}
:

H_{lbit}=\sum_{i}J^{(1)}_{i}\kappa^{z}_{i}+\sum_{i,j}J^{(2)}_{ij}\kappa^{z}_{i}\kappa^{z}_{j}+\sum_{i,j,k}J^{(3)}_{ijk}\kappa^{z}_{i}\kappa^{z}_{j}\kappa^{z}_{k}+\ldots

(2)

where
\kappa^{z}_{i}
are the so called
l
-bit Pauli operators with localized support on the Hilbert space near site
i
, whose eigenvalues represent the locally conserved quantities and the magnitudes of
J^{m}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}
fall off exponentially with distance.
The second model we consider is a truncated version of the above phenomenological
l
-bit model
eq.

2
where we retain only up to 10 spin nearest neighbor interactions with all couplings drawn from a uniform distribution
J^{(1\ldots 10)}\in[-1,1]
.

Characterizing spectral correlations of quantum systems
: A popular diagnostic used to distinguish MBL and chaotic systems via their spectral correlations is the
adjacent gap ratio
(
r
)
Oganesyan and Huse 2007
. This is defined in terms of successive gaps
\delta_{i}=E_{i+1}-E_{i}
of an ordered energy spectrum
\{E_{i}\}
as follows:

r_{i}=\frac{\mathrm{min}(\delta_{i},\delta_{i+1})}{\mathrm{max}(\delta_{i},\delta_{i+1})}.

(3)

For chaotic systems, the value of
\langle r\rangle
(where
\langle\dots\rangle
denotes averaging over samples and energy) can be computed from an appropriate random matrix ensemble. For example, the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE), appropriate for systems with time-reversal symmetry gives
\langle r\rangle\approx 0.53
, while Poisson levels, applicable for MBL systems gives
\langle r\rangle=2\ln(2)-1\approx 0.39

Oganesyan and Huse 2007
. As shown in the inset of
fig.

1
, by tracking
\langle r\rangle
, we can see that the Hamiltonian of
eq.

1
supports a thermal phase for small
W
and an MBL phase for large
W
with the critical disorder strength somewhere near
W_{c}\approx 7.3
where the curves for different system sizes cross, consistent with previous work
Šuntajs et al. 2020a
.

The adjacent gap ratio captures the repulsion of neighboring levels, and thus only probes
local
spectral correlations. It does not measure long-range spectral correlations, which have important and useful information.
A more
comprehensive
diagnostic
is the spectral form factor (SFF)
Haake 2010
, which is the main tool of analysis in this Letter and is defined in terms of eigenvalues
\{E_{i}\}
as follows:

K(\tau,N)=\langle\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}e^{i\tau(E_{m}-E_{n})}\rangle

(4)

where
N
is the total number of eigenvalues in consideration. Also useful is the connected SFF defined as

K_{c}(\tau,N)=\langle\sum_{m,n=1}^{N}e^{i\tau(E_{m}-E_{n})}\rangle-|\langle\sum_{m=1}^{N}e^{i\tau E_{m}}\rangle|^{2}.

(5)

The information about long-range correlations is contained in the form of
K(\tau,N)
interpolating the early and late
\tau
values of
N^{2}
and
N
respectively (
0

N
for
K_{c}(\tau,N)

\langle r\rangle

1

Cotler et al. 2017
;

;
Chen and Ludwig 2018

Bertini et al. 2018

As we increase the disorder strength, as shown in

Deep in the MBL phase (i.e. where

SFF for Poisson levels
:

The single-particle spectrum of the Anderson insulator
Anderson 1958
deep in the localized phase can simply be described by a set of uncorrelated random numbers (the values of random chemical potentials). In this case, on scales smaller than the single-particle bandwidth, the spectrum looks like a
Poisson process

Mehta 2004
;
Haake 2010
. For example, the distribution of the level spacings is exponential
P(\delta)=\frac{1}{\mu}\exp\left(-\frac{\delta}{\mu}\right)
. Consistent with the hypothesis of emergent integrability in localized systems, this is identical to the distribution of level spacings in point particle systems with integrable classical trajectories conjectured by Berry and Tabor
Berry and Tabor 1977
;
Marklof 2001
and has been verified in several systems
Casati et al. 1985
;
Marklof 1998
.

The many body levels of the Anderson insulator on the other hand is a weighted sum of the single particle eigenvalues. For a system of size
L
, the
\sim\mathcal{O}(L)
random numbers present in the Hamiltonian are used to generate
\sim 2^{L}
many-body eigenvalues and are no longer completely uncorrelated. How the spectrum further changes in the presence of interactions for MBL systems is less obvious. However, extensive work
Serbyn and Moore 2016
;
Oganesyan and Huse 2007
;
Sierant and Zakrzewski 2019
;
Buijsman et al. 2019
;
Sierant and Zakrzewski 2020
has provided evidence that the Poisson nature continues to persist in the many-body levels of MBL systems on energy scales smaller than the many-body bandwidth
Oganesyan and Huse 2007
. To compute the SFF, we need more information than local statistics such as the level spacing distribution - we need the joint distribution of eigenvalues
P(E_{n},n;E_{m},m)
i.e. the likelihood of the
n^{th}

E_{n}
when the
m^{th}
level is
E_{m}
. For Poisson process, this is

Conclusion
:
In this Letter, we have derived an exact expression for the spectral form factor of Poisson levels and identified a universal regime. We have shown that this describes the SFF in the many body localized phase well through a detailed comparison with numerical results on two separate physical models. The analytic expression of the spectral form factor obtained here is expected to apply to any integrable many-body quantum system. In particular, we conjecture that in the SFF of integrable models, the universal power-law correction should be observed as a
refined
version of the Berry-Tabor conjecture.

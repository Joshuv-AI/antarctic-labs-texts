Concurrence for well-formed CAFs: Naive Semantics
Rafael Kiesel, Anna Rapberger

Abstract
In the area of claim-based reasoning in abstract argumentation, a claim-based semantics is said to be concurrent in a given framework if all its variants yield the same extensions. In this note, we show that the concurrence problem with respect to naive semantics is 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{coNP}} -hard for well-formed CAFs. This solves a problem that has been left open in [ 2 ] .



1
Introduction

Claim-augmented argumentation frameworks (CAFs)

[
4
]
extend
abstract argumentation frameworks (AFs)

[
1
]
by a function that assigns a claim to each argument.
Formally, CAFs correspond to directed labeled graphs where the nodes correspond to arguments, the labels correspond to the arguments claims, and the arcs indicate (unidirectional) conflicts between them.
As with arguments in AFs, the acceptance status of claims is decided via
argumentation semantics
. There are often several possibilities to lift AF semantics to claim-level
[
3
]
;
considering
naive semantics
which is based on maximization of conflict-free sets gives rise to two claim-based variants:
The so-called
inherited
variant, which performs maximization on argument-level, and the
claim-level semantics
, which performs maximization over claim-sets.
Deciding whether two different variants of a semantics yield the same claim-based outcome for a given CAF (the so-called
concurrence problem
) can be computationally challenging:
As shown in
[
2
]
, the concurrence problem is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete with respect to naive semantics.

In this note, we show that deciding concurrence with respect to naive semantics is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-hard even for
well-formed CAFs
, an important sub-class of CAFs that impose restrictions on the attack relation.
Well-formed CAFs satisfy a quite natural behavior of conflicts: A CAF is well-formed if arguments with the same claims attack the same arguments.
This closes the complexity gap in
[
2
]
for the concurrence problem.

3
The Concurrence Problem for Naive Semantics

An interesting problem that arises when considering different variants of semantics for CAFs is the so-called
concurrence problem
.

\textit{Con}_{\sigma}^{\Delta}
,
\Delta\in\{\mathit{CAF},\mathit{wf}\}

Input:

A CAF
\mathcal{F}
(if
\Delta=\mathit{CAF}
)/a well-formed CAF
\mathcal{F}
(if
\Delta=\mathit{wf}
)

Output:

true
iff
\sigma\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{\mathit{cl-\sigma}}(\mathcal{F})

For naive semantics, the problem can be formulated as follows: Given a CAF
\mathcal{F}
, is it the case that maximization on argument-level yields the same accepted sets as maximization on claim-level?

In
[
2
]
it has been shown that
\textit{Con}_{\sigma}^{\mathit{CAF}}
is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete. Since concurrence for well-formed CAFs with respect to naive semantics is a special case of CAFs we obtain upper bounds for
\textit{Con}_{\sigma}^{\mathit{wf}}
, that is,
\textit{Con}_{\sigma}^{\mathit{wf}}
is in
{\mathsf{coNP}}
.

We restate the following proposition
[
2
]
.

Proposition 1
.

For a CAF
\mathcal{F}=(A,R,\mathit{cl})
,

\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{\mathit{cl-naive}}(\mathcal{F})
if and only if
naive\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
is incomparable.

Thus it suffices to verify incomparability of
\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
.
An
{\mathsf{NP}}
procedure for the complementary problem is by a standard guess and check procedure:
Guess
E,G\subseteq A
and check (i)
E,G\in\sigma((A,R))
and (ii)
\mathit{cl}(E)\subset\mathit{cl}(G)
.
The former can be checked in time polynomial in the number of arguments in
\mathcal{F}
.

Next we show that verifying incomparability of
\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
is
{\mathsf{NP}}
-hard even if
\mathcal{F}
is well-formed. We will first define the base reduction, which we slightly extend to obtain the the reduction for the well-formed case.

Reduction 1
.

Let
\varphi
be given by a set of clauses

C=\{cl_{1},\dots,cl_{n}\}
over

X
.
We construct

with

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle X\cup\bar{X}\cup C\cup\{\varphi\}\cup\{a_{1},a_{2}\},\text{ with }\bar{X}=\{\bar{x}\mid x\in X\},

\displaystyle R=

\displaystyle\{(x,cl)\mid cl\in C,x\in cl\}\cup\{(\bar{x},cl)\mid cl\in C,\neg x\in cl\}

\displaystyle\cup\{(x,\bar{x}),(\bar{x},x)\mid x\in X\}\cup\{(cl_{i},\varphi)\mid i\leq n\}\cup\{(\varphi,a_{2})\},

and
\mathit{cl}(x)=x
,
\mathit{cl}(\bar{x})=\bar{x},\mathit{cl}(cl_{i})=cl_{i},\mathit{cl}(\varphi)=\varphi
and
\mathit{cl}(a_{i})=a
.

We take the smallest sub-CAF that is not concurrent.
This corresponds to

Proposition 2
.

\textit{Con}_{\mathit{na}}^{\mathit{wf}}
is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-hard.

Proof.

For hardness, we present a reduction from UNSAT: Let
\varphi
be given by a set of clauses
C=\{cl_{1},\dots,cl_{n}\}
over literals in
X
. W.l.o.g. we can assume that
\varphi
does not contain tautological clauses, i.e.,
there is no
cl_{i}
,
i\leq n
with
x,\bar{x}\in cl_{i}
for any
x\in X
. Let
(A,R,\mathit{cl})
be defined as in Reduction
1
.
We will show
\varphi
is unsatisfiable iff
\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
is incomparable.

First asssume
\varphi
is satisfiable and consider a model
M
of
\varphi
.
Let
E=M\cup\{\bar{x}\mid x\notin M\}\cup\{\varphi,a_{1}\}
. Clearly,
E
is conflict-free; moreover, as
M
satisfies each clause
cl_{i}
there is either
x\in cl_{i}
with
x\in M

or
\bar{x}\in cl_{i}
with
x\notin M
, thus
E
attacks each
cl_{i}
. Since, also
\varphi
and
a_{1}
are in
E
we the only argument left to consider is
a_{2}
, which is however attacked by
\varphi
and can therefore not be included, while preserving conflict-freeness. We can conclude that
E
is a subset-maximal conflict-free set.
Moreover,
E^{\prime}=M\cup\{\bar{x}\mid x\notin M\}\cup\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
is also a subset-maximal conflict-free set, since still every
cl_{i}
is attacked and
\varphi
attacks
a_{2}
.
It follows that
\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
is not incomparable since

\mathit{cl}(E)=M\cup\{\bar{x}\mid x\notin M\}\cup\{\varphi,a\}
is a strict superset of
M\cup\{\bar{x}\mid x\notin M\}\cup\{a\}=\mathit{cl}(E^{\prime})
and both are contained in
\mathit{na}\!_{c}(\mathcal{F})
.

Now assume that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable. Let
E
be a subset-maximal conflict-free set. If
\varphi\in E
it follows that none of the
cl_{i}
are in
E
. Therefore, it holds that for each
x
exactly one of
x
and
\bar{x}
is in
E
. Furthermore, it always holds that
a_{1}
is in
E
. This means that
\mathit{cl}(E)=\{\varphi,a\}\cup\{x\mid x\in X^{\prime}\}\cup\{\bar{x}\mid x\not\in X^{\prime}\}
for some subset
X^{\prime}
of
X
.

Case I: Assume there is a naive extension
E^{\prime}
such that
\mathit{cl}(E)\subsetneq\mathit{cl}(E^{\prime})
. Then
E^{\prime}
must contain
\mathit{cl}_{i}
for some

,
x
for some
x\not\in X^{\prime}
or
\bar{x}
for some
x\in X^{\prime}
. This is not possible, since this would imply
E\subsetneq E^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction to the assumption that
E
and
E^{\prime}
are naive extensions.

Case II: Assume there is a naive extension
E^{\prime}
that contains
\varphi
such that
\mathit{cl}(E^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathit{cl}(E)
. Then there must be some
x
with
x\in X^{\prime}
or
\bar{x}
with

that is not contained

. This is not possible, since this would imply
E^{\prime}\subsetneq E

E
and
E^{\prime}
are naive extensions.

Case III: Assume there is a naive extension
E^{\prime}
that does not contain
\varphi
such that
\mathit{cl}(E^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathit{cl}(E)
. This is impossible, as
\varphi
is unsatisfiable, which entails that there is a clause

that is not satisfied by

Next we show that the same holds for any naive extension

that does not contain

E

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(A\setminus\{a_{1},a_{2},\varphi\},R\setminus\{(\varphi,a_{2})\}\cup\{(cl_{i},\varphi)\mid i=1,\dots,n\},\mathit{cl})

∎

4
Conclusion

We see that even for well formed CAFs the concurrence problem is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete, as it is the case for general CAFs. This shows that while well formedness is an interesting property that only allows a fragment of the CAFs that might be deemed more reasonable, it does not lead to CAFs that are simpler, at least with respect to the concurrence problem.

Abstract
. We prove a criterion for Benjamini-Schramm convergence of periodic orbits of Lie groups. This general observation is then applied to homogeneous spaces and the space of translation surfaces.



1.
Benjamini-Schramm convergence

Let
H\subset{\rm{SL}}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-compact semisimple group.
Even though
H\subset{\rm{SL}}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
, we will write
e

for the identity element in
H
. The notation
I
(for the identity matrix)
will only be used when the vector space structure of the space of matrices is relevant.

Let
\|\;\|
denote the maximum norm on
{\rm Mat}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the standard basis, and put

B^{H}(e,R)=\{h\in H:\|h-I\|<R\text{ and }\|h^{-1}-I\|<R\}.

We also equip
H
with the right invariant Riemannian metric induced by the killing form,
and let
B^{H}_{\rm Rie}(e,r)
denote the ball of radius

r
centered at the identity with respect to this metric. Then for every
R
, there exists
r>0
so that

B^{H}_{\rm Rie}(e,r)\subset B^{H}(e,R).

Let
r(R)
denote
1/2
the supremum of all such
r
, then
B^{H}_{\rm Rie}(e,r(R))\subset B^{H}(e,R)
and
r(R)\to\infty
as
R\to\infty
; indeed it is not difficult to see that
r(R)\geq C\log R
where
C>0
depends on the embedding
H\subset{\rm{SL}}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\Delta\subset H
be a discrete subgroup.
The injectivity radius of
y\in H/\Delta
is define as the supremum over all
r>0
so that
the map
h\mapsto hy
is injective on
B^{H}_{\rm Rie}(e,r)
.

Let
\Delta_{n}\subset H
be a sequence of lattices in
H
.
The sequence
\{H/\Delta_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

Benjamini-Schramm
converges to
H
if
for every
r>0
we have

\mu_{n}\Bigl(\{y\in H/\Delta_{n}:\text{injectivity radius of $y$}<r\}\Bigr)\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}

where
\mu_{n}
denote the
H
-invariant probability measure on
H/\Delta_{n}
for every
n
.

Throughout, we assume that
H
acts continuously on
X
preserving the measure
\mu
;
also assume that
{\rm Stab}_{H}(x)
is discrete for every
x\in X
.

An orbit
Hx\subset X
is called
periodic
if
Hx\subset X
is a closed subset
and
{\rm Stab}_{H}(x)
is a lattice in
H
.

For a periodic orbit
Hx
, let
\mu_{Hx}
denote the pushforward
of the
H
-invariant probability measure of
H/{\rm Stab}_{H}(x)
to
Hx
.

1.1 Proposition
.

Let
\{Hx_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of periodic orbits in
X
satisfying that

(1.1)

\mu_{Hx_{n}}\to\mu\quad\text{ as $n\to\infty$.}

Assume further that for every
R>0
there exists a continuous function

f_{R}:X\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

f_{R}(x)>0
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

(2)

if
f_{R}(x)>0
for some
x\in X
, then
{\rm Stab}_{H}(x)\cap B^{H}(e,R)=\{e\}
.

Then
H/{\rm Stab}_{H}(x_{n})
Benjamini-Schramm converges to
H
.

Proof.

Let
R>0
.
Let
Y=Hx\subset X
be a periodic orbit, and put
\Delta={\rm Stab}(x)
.
The map
h\Delta\mapsto hx
is a homeomorphism from
H/\Delta
onto
Y
.
Let
h\Delta\in H/\Delta
, and write
y=hx\in Y
. Suppose now that
h_{1}h\Delta=h_{2}h\Delta

for some
h_{1},h_{2}\in B^{H}(e,R)
.
Then
\|h_{2}^{-1}h_{1}-I\|<NR^{2}
and

h_{2}^{-1}h_{1}\in h\Delta h^{-1}={\rm Stab}_{H}(y).

This and the assumption (2) in the proposition imply that

(1.2)

If
f_{NR^{2}}(y)>0
, then the injectivity radius of
h\Delta
is at least
r(R)
;

recall that
B^{H}_{\rm Rie}(e,r(R))\subset B^{H}(e,R)
.

Let now
\varepsilon>0
. In view of our assumption (1) in the proposition,
there exists a compact subset
K_{\varepsilon}\subset X
so that

\mu(K_{\varepsilon})>1-\varepsilon
and
f_{NR^{2}}(x)>0
for all
x\in K_{\varepsilon}
.

Since
f
is continuous and
K_{\varepsilon}
is compact, there exists some
\delta>0
so that

f_{2R}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})
, where
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})

denotes a finite open covering of the set
K_{\varepsilon}
with balls of radius
\delta
centered at points in
K_{\varepsilon}
.

Since
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})
is an open set and
\mu_{Hx_{n}}\to\mu
, we conclude that

\liminf_{n}\mu_{Hx_{n}}\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\geq\mu\bigl(\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

This and the fact that
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(K_{\varepsilon})\subset\{y\in Hx_{n}:f_{NR^{2}}(y)>0\}
imply:
there exists some
n_{0}
so that

\mu_{Hx_{n}}\Bigl(\{y\in Hx_{n}:f_{NR^{2}}(y)>0\}\Bigr)>1-2\varepsilon\quad\text{for all $n>n_{0}$ }.

In consequence, using (
1.2
) we deduce that

\mu_{H/{\rm Stab}(x_{n})}\Bigl(\{y\in H/{\rm Stab}(x_{n}):\text{injectivity radius of $y$ is $<r(R)$}\}\Bigr)<2\varepsilon

for all
n>n_{0}
. Since
r(R)\to\infty
as
R\to\infty
, the claim follows.
∎

2.3 Lemma
.

Let the notation and the assumptions be as in Theorem
2.1
.
Then for
\mu_{X}
-a.e.
x\in X
we have

{\rm Stab}(x)\cap H=\{e\}

Proof.

Let
\bf H
denote the Zariski closure of
H
in
\bf G
.
Since
H
is a connected semisimple Lie group, it has finite index in the group
H^{\prime}:={\bf H}(\mathbb{R})\cap G
.

By Chevalley’s theorem, there exists a finite dimensional (real) representation
(\rho,W)

of
\bf G
and a vector
w\in W
so that
{\bf H}=\{g\in{\bf G}:gw=w\}
. In particular, we conclude that

(2.2)

H^{\prime}=G\cap{\bf H}=\{g\in G:gw=w\}.

Let now
x=g_{0}\Gamma
. Then
{\rm Stab}(x)=g_{0}\Gamma g_{0}^{-1}
, and

H\cap g_{0}\Gamma g_{0}^{-1}
is nontrivial if and only if there exists some
e\neq\gamma\in\Gamma

so that
\gamma\in g_{0}^{-1}Hg_{0}
. Since
H\subset H^{\prime}
, we conclude that
\gamma g_{0}^{-1}w=g_{0}^{-1}w
. Hence,

g_{0}^{-1}\in{\bf F}_{\gamma}=\{g\in{\bf G}:\gamma gw=gw\}.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the set
{\bf F}_{\gamma}
is an algebraic variety defined over
\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover,
G={\bf G}(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}
is Zariski dense in
{\bf G}
. These and the fact that
\Gamma
is countable imply that
unless there exists some
e\neq\delta\in\Gamma
so that

\delta gw=gw\quad
for all
g\in G
,

the lemma holds — indeed in that case
G\setminus\Bigl(\cup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\bf F}_{\gamma}\Bigr)
is a conull subset of
G
,
and for every
g
in this set we have
H\cap{\rm Stab}(g\Gamma)=\{e\}
.

Assume now to the contrary that
G=\{g\in G:\delta gw=gw\}
for some nontrivial

\delta\in\Gamma
. Then by (
2.2
) we have
\delta\in gH^{\prime}g^{-1}
for all
g\in G
, hence,

\delta\in\bigcap_{g\in G}gH^{\prime}g^{-1}.

Since
[H^{\prime}:H]<\infty
, there exists some
n
so that
\delta^{n}\in gHg^{-1}
for all
g\in G
. That is,

\delta^{n}\in\cap_{g\in G}\,gHg^{-1}
. However,
\Gamma
is torsion free and
\cap_{g\in G}\,gHg^{-1}
is a finite group.
This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We may and will assume that
G\subset{\rm{SL}}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
for some
N
.
As before, for all subgroups
L\subset G
and all
R>0
, let

where
\|\;\|
denotes the maximum norm on
{\rm{SL}}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the standard basis.

Recall that
\mu_{X}
denotes the
G
-invariant probability measure on
X
.
First note that by a theorem of Mozes and Shah
[
17
]
and our
assumptions (1) and (2) in the theorem, we have

\mu_{Hx_{n}}\to\mu_{X}\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Let
{\rm dist}
denote the right invariant Riemannian metric on

induced using the killing form.
Let

, and put

Define

.
In particular, by Lemma

-a.e.

Altogether, we deduce that

3.
The space of Abelian differentials

Let
g\geq 2
, and let
\mathcal{T}_{g}
denote the Teichmüller space of complex structure on a compact Riemann
surface of genus
g
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g}
the corresponding moduli space, i.e., the quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
by
the mapping class group,
\MC_{g}
.

As it is well-known,
\MC_{g}
is not torsion free, however, it has subgroups of finite index which are torsion free — indeed
the kernel of the natural map from
\MC_{g}
to
{\rm{Sp}}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
is torsion free.

We fix, once and for all, a covering map

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}

which corresponds to a torsion free finite index subgroup of
\MC_{g}
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
be an isometric immersion for the Teichmüller metric.
Typically,
f(\mathbb{H}^{2})
is dense in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, however, there are situations where
f(\mathbb{H}^{2})
is an algebraic curve
in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. In the latter case, the stabilizer
\Delta
of
f
is a lattice in
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
, and we obtain a
Teichmüller curve

f:V=\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Delta\to\mathcal{M}_{g}.

For every
g\geq 2
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
contains a dense family of Teichmüller curves
which arise as branched cover of flat tori.
There are also examples of infinite families of
primitive
Teichmüller curves, i.e., Teichmüller curves which do not arise as a branched cover of flat tori, in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
when
g=2,3,4
,
[
15
,
16
]
.

3.1 Theorem
.

Let
\{V_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an infinite family of Techimüller curves in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
For every
n
, let
\widehat{V}_{n}\to V_{n}
be a lift of
V_{n}
to
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
Then
\{\widehat{V}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
Benjamini-Schramm converges to

.

and

to highlight a unifying theme between the homogeneous setting and the setting at hand.

For every

Form a vector bundle over the Teichmüller space

Let

There is a natural action of

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in{\rm GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

(3.1)

h\cdot\omega=\begin{pmatrix}i\\

c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{R}(\omega)\\

We let
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}

.

For every

\sum\alpha_{i}=2g-2

Let

We can define a map

\Phi(M,\omega)=\left(\int_{\gamma_{1}}\omega,\dots,\int_{\gamma_{k}}w\right)

The map

Alternatively,

The area of a translation surface is given by

The

3.2.
Affine measures and manifolds

For a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)
we write

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{E}=\{(M,t\omega):(M,\omega)\in\mathcal{E},t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{H}(\alpha).

An ergodic
{\rm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant probability measure
\nu
on

\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)
is called
affine
if the following hold:

(i)

The support
\mathcal{M}
of
\nu
is an

immersed submanifold
of

\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)
, i.e., there exists a manifold
\mathcal{N}
and a proper continuous
map
f:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)
so that
\mathcal{M}=f(\mathcal{N})
. The self-intersection set of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e., the set of
points of
\mathcal{M}
which do not have a
unique preimage under
f
, is a closed subset of
\mathcal{M}
of

\nu
-measure
0
. Furthermore, each point in
\mathcal{N}
has a
neighborhood
U
such
that locally
\mathbb{R}f(U)
is given by a complex linear subspace defined
over
\mathbb{R}
in the period coordinates.

(ii)

Let
\bar{\nu}
be the measure supported on
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}
so that
d\bar{\nu}=d\nu da
. Then each point in
\mathcal{N}
has a
neighborhood
U
such that the restriction of
\bar{\nu}
to
\mathbb{R}f(U)
is an affine linear measure in the period
coordinates on
\mathbb{R}f(U)
, i.e., it is (up to normalization) the
restriction of the Lebesgue measure to the subspace
\mathbb{R}f(U)
.

A suborbifold
\mathcal{M}
for which there exists a measure

\nu
such that the pair
(\mathcal{M},\nu)

satisfies (i) and (ii) is said to be
affine invariant submanifold
.

We sometimes write
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}
to indicate the affine invariant measure
\nu
on affine invariant submanifold
\mathcal{M}
.

Note that in particular, any affine invariant submanifold is a closed
subset of
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)
which is invariant under the action of
{\rm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
and which in period coordinates is an affine subspace.
We also consider the entire stratum
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\alpha)

to be an (improper) affine invariant submanifold.

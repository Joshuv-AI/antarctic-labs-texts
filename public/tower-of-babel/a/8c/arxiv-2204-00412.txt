Multiplicative complements I.
Anett Kocsis, Dávid Matolcsi, Csaba Sándor, György Tőtős

Abstract
In this paper, we study how dense a multiplicative basis of order h h for ℤ + \mathbb{Z}^{+}\! can be, improving on earlier results. Upon introducing the notion of a multiplicative complement , we present some tight density bounds.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
denote the set of positive integers. For
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
and
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
, the
multiplicative representation function

S_{A,h}(n)

of order
h
counts the ordered representations of
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
as a product of
h
elements of
A
; that is, we define

S_{A,h}(n)=|\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):a_{i}\in A\text{ and }a_{1}\cdots a_{h}=n\}|.

We say that
A
is a
multiplicative basis of order
h
for
\mathbb{Z}^{+}\mkern-1.0mu
if all positive integers can be written in the form
a_{1}\cdots a_{h}
for some
a_{i}\in A
, which we may also express as having
S_{A,h}(n)\geq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. We denote the set of multiplicative bases like
A
by
\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
. The
counting function

A(n)
now counts the elements of
A
that are less than or equal to
n
; that is, we write
A(n)=|A\cap\{1,2,\ldots,n\}|
. It is easy to see that for
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, the prime numbers are necessarily members of
A
, and hence

A(n)\geq\pi(n)=(1+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n}.

In 1938, Raikov proved the following density bounds.

Theorem 1

(
[
6
, Th. 1]
)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Then:

1.

For all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}
.

2.

There exists
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
with
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}<\infty
.

Note that
\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)=(1+o(1))h
as
h\to\infty
. In 2018, Pach and Sándor improved upon these inequalities.

Theorem 2

(
[
4
, Th. 3]
)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Then:

1.

For all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}\geq\frac{\sqrt{6}}{e\pi}
.

2.

There exists
C>0
such that for each
h\geq 2
, one can find
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
with

\mkern-100.0mu\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}=C.

The following theorem provides an even better lower bound.

Theorem 3
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. For all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}\geq\frac{\sqrt[h]{h!}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}
.

Let us note that for
h\geq 2
, the sequence
\frac{\sqrt[h]{h!}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}
is decreasing in
h
, and its limit is
\frac{1}{e}
. Consequently:

Corollary 4
.

With
h\geq 2
, for all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}>\frac{1}{e}
.

For
A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
, we define the
common multiplicative representation function
as

S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(n)=|\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):a_{i}\in A_{i}\text{ and }a_{1}\cdots a_{h}=n\}|

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Raikov’s theorem may now be generalised to these functions as follows.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Pick
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{h}\in(0,1)
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h}\tau_{i}=1
, and assume that

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}<\infty

for
1\leq i\leq h
. Then, we have

\left(\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(n)}{x}\right)\;\prod_{i=1}^{h}\;\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}\;\leq\;\prod_{i=1}^{h}\;\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}.

Let
A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
for
1\leq i\leq h
. We shall refer to the
h
-tuple
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})
as
a multiplicative complement of order
h
if all positive integers can be written in the form
a_{1}\cdots a_{h}
with
a_{i}\in A_{i}
, which we may also express as having
S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(n)\geq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. We denote the set of multiplicative complements of order
h
by
\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
. The following is then a direct consequence of the above theorem.

Corollary 6
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
and
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
. Pick
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{h}\in(0,1)
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h}\tau_{i}=1
and

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}<\infty

for
1\leq i\leq h
. Then, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{h}\;\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}\;\leq\;\prod_{i=1}^{h}\;\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}.

This inequality is sharp.

Theorem 7
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Pick
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{h}\in(0,1)
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}\in(0,\infty)
with
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h}\tau_{i}=1
and
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{h}a_{i}=1
. Then, there exists
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}=\frac{a_{i}}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}

for all
1\leq i\leq h
.

A simple corollary to these theorems may now be formulated.

Corollary 8
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Then:

1.

For all
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\max\{A_{1}(x),\ldots,A_{h}(x)\}\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}
.

2.

There is
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
for which
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\max\{A_{1}(x),\ldots,A_{h}(x)\}\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}
.

It is easy to see that if
(A_{1},\dots A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
, then we necessarily have
A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{h}\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, and hence:

Corollary 9
.

With
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exists
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
such that

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}=\frac{h}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)}.

One can readily check that
\frac{h}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{h})}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{h})}
and
\displaystyle\min_{h\geq 2}\Gamma\Big(1+\frac{1}{h}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}
. This yields the next statement.

Corollary 10
.

With
h\geq 2
, there exists
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
such that

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

In 2018, Pach and Sándor also proved density bounds for the limit inferior.

Theorem 11

(
[
4
, Th. 4]
)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Then:

1.

For all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\frac{x}{\log x}}>1
.

2.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}
with
\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\frac{x}{\log x}}\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

For multiplicative complements, we may formulate the analogue as follows.

Theorem 12
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
. Then:

1.

For all
(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
, we have
\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\max\{A_{1}(x),\ldots,A_{h}(x)\}}{\frac{x}{\log x}}>\frac{1}{h}
.

2.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
with
\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\max\{A_{1}(x),\ldots,A_{h}(x)\}}{\frac{x}{\log x}}\leq\frac{1}{h}+\varepsilon

Let us, finally, propose some problems for further research. Note that for
h\geq 2
, we have

\mkern 14.0mu\frac{1}{e}<\frac{\sqrt[h]{h!}}{h\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{h}\right)}\leq\inf_{A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}}\bigg\{\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\frac{x}{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}}\bigg\}\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{h}\right)}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

.

Is it true that for all
h\geq 2

A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{h}

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}x}{x}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{h}\right)}\,?

As answering this question seems to be hard, we shall simplify it. Note that for
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{2}

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}}.

Problem 2
.

Is it true that there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{2}
, we have

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}+\delta\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}}\,?

Picking now
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{2}

S_{A,2}(n)\geq 2
holds as long as

is not a perfect square, and so
\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A,2}(n)\geq x-\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A,2}(n)}{x}\geq 2.

Thus, according to Theorem
5
, we can further reduce Problem
2
to the following.

Problem 3
.

Is it true that there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
A\in\mathop{\rm MB}\nolimits_{2}

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A,2}(n)}{x}\geq 2+\delta_{0}\,?

Concluding, note how Theorem

\liminf

\limsup

the analogue is:

Problem 4

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\in\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h}\tau_{i}=1

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}<\infty

for
1\leq i\leq h

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A_{1},\dots,A_{h}}(n)}{x}<\infty\,?

Proofs

In what follows, for
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we write
A[s]=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{a^{s}}
, where
s>1
.

The proof of Theorem
3
and that of Theorem
5
are based on the next lemma, coming from Raikov’s paper yet not explicitly stated there.

Lemma 1
.

Choose
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
and
\tau\in(0,1)
such that
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau}x}{x}<\infty
. Then,

\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\tau}A[s]\leq\Gamma(\tau)\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau}x}{x}.

Proof.

We know that for all
s>1
, we have

s\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}dx=\sum_{a\in A}s\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s+1}}dx=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{a^{s}}=A[s].

Pick now
a>\alpha
, where

\mkern-4.0mu\alpha=\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau}x}{x}.

Then, there exists
x_{0}>1
such that for all
x\geq x_{0}
, we have

\frac{A(x)}{x}\leq\frac{a}{\log^{1-\tau}x}.

Consequently,

A[s]\leq s\int_{1}^{x_{0}}\frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}dx+sa\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{s}\log^{1-\tau}x}dx.

Changing variable and writing
t=(s-1)\log x
in the rightmost integral, we get

A[s]\leq s\int_{1}^{x_{0}}\frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}dx+\frac{s}{(s-1)^{\tau}}a\int_{(s-1)\log x_{0}}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!t^{\tau-1}e^{-t}dt.

Introducing the gamma function, we may now rewrite the previous inequality as

(s-1)^{\tau}A[s]\leq s(s-1)^{\tau}\!\!\int_{1}^{x_{0}}\frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}dx-sa\!\!\int_{0}^{(s-1)\log x_{0}}\!\!\!t^{\tau-1}e^{-t}dt+\Gamma(\tau)sa.

Since
s\searrow 1
, we have

s(s-1)^{\tau}\!\!\int_{1}^{x_{0}}\frac{A(x)}{x^{s+1}}dx\to 0,\quad sa\!\!\int_{0}^{(s-1)\log x_{0}}\!\!\!t^{\tau-1}e^{-t}dt\to 0\;\text{ and }\;\Gamma(\tau)sa\to\Gamma(\tau)a,

and thus

\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\tau}A[s]\leq\Gamma(\tau)a.

This proves the desired inequality.
∎

The following lemma improves on Raikov’s result.

Lemma 2
.

For a multiplicative basis
A
of order
h
for
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\frac{1}{h}}A[s]\geq\sqrt[h]{h!}\,.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\mkern 20.0mu\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\frac{1}{h}}A[s]=\alpha<\infty.

Set
B=A\mkern 1.0mu\cup\mkern 1.0mu\{a^{2}\mkern 1.0mu:\mkern 1.0mua\in A\}
. Then,

\frac{1}{h!}A[s]^{h}+B[s]^{h-1}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\left(\frac{S_{A,h}(n)}{h!}+S_{B,h-1}(n)\right).

Given that
A
is a multiplicative basis of order
h
for
\mathbb{Z}^{+}\!
, we can write
n=a_{1}\cdots a_{h}
for some
a_{i}\in A
. If
S_{B,h-1}(n)=0
, then
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for all
i\,\neq\,j
, meaning that
S_{A,h}(n)\geq h!
. That is, for all
n\geq 1
, we have

\frac{S_{A,h}}{h!}+S_{B,h-1}(n)\geq 1.

Consequently, for all
s>1
, we get

\frac{1}{h!}A[s]^{h}+B[s]^{h-1}\geq\zeta(s).

Note that
B[s]\leq A[s]+A[2s]
for any
s>1
, and hence

(s-1)B[s]^{h-1}\leq(s-1)A[2s]^{h-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}\binom{h-1}{j}\left((s-1)^{\frac{1}{j}}A[s]\right)^{j}A[2s]^{h-1-j}.

Clearly,
A[2s]\to A[2]\leq\zeta(2)<\infty
as
s\searrow 1
. Since
\alpha<\infty
, for all
1\leq j\leq h-1
, we get

\lim_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\frac{1}{j}}A[s]=0,

and so

\lim_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)B[s]^{h-1}=0.

Therefore, we can conclude that

\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)\frac{1}{h!}A[s]^{h}\geq\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)\zeta(s)=1,

which then completes the proof.
∎

Proof of theorem
3
.

Using Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
, for a multiplicative basis
A
of order
h
, we have

\limsup_{x\to\infty}A(x)\frac{\log^{1-\frac{1}{h}}}{x}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{h})}\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\frac{1}{h}}A[s]\geq\frac{\sqrt[h]{h!}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{h})}.

Proof of theorem
5
.

We prove by contradiction. Suppose that

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A_{1},\dots,A_{h}}(n)}{x}>\prod_{i=1}^{h}\left(\Gamma(\tau_{i})\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}\right).

Pick
G
satisfying

\prod_{i=1}^{h}\left(\Gamma(\tau_{i})\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}\right)<G<\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(n)}{x}.

We can find
n_{0}>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, we have

b_{n}=\sum_{k\leq n}S_{A_{1},\dots,A_{h}}(k)\geq G\cdot n.

Pick
n>n_{0}
and
s>1
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(k)}{k^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{b_{k}-b_{k-1}}{k^{s}}=\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{b_{k}}{k}\left(\frac{k}{k^{s}}-\frac{k}{(k+1)^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq G\mkern-4.0mu\sum_{n_{0}\leq k<n}\mkern-4.0mu\left(\frac{1}{k^{s-1}}-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{s-1}}+\frac{1}{(k+1)^{s}}\right)\geq G\mkern-8.0mu\sum_{n_{0}<k\leq n}\frac{1}{k^{s}}.

It follows that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{A_{1},\dots,A_{h}}(k)}{k^{s}}\geq G\cdot\zeta(s)-G\sum_{k\leq n_{0}}\frac{1}{k^{s}}.

Note that
\displaystyle G\sum_{k\leq n_{0}}\frac{1}{k^{s}}
tends to
G\sum_{k\leq n_{0}}\frac{1}{k}
as
s\searrow 1
, which expression is finite, and thus

\liminf_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{A_{1},\ldots,A_{h}}(k)}{k^{s}}\geq\liminf_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)G\zeta(s)=G.

From

\mkern-18.0mu\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{A_{1},l\dots,A_{h}}(k)}{k^{s}}=\prod_{i=1}^{h}A_{i}[s]

and using
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h}\tau_{i}=1
, we get

G\leq\liminf_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)\prod_{i=1}^{h}A_{i}[s]=\liminf_{s\searrow 1}\prod_{i=1}^{h}\left((s-1)^{\tau_{i}}A_{i}[s]\right)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{h}\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\tau_{i}}A_{i}[s].

Using now Lemma
1
, we get

\limsup_{s\searrow 1}(s-1)^{\tau_{i}}A_{i}[s]\leq\Gamma(\tau_{i})\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}.

Putting it all together, it follows that

G\leq\prod_{i=1}^{h}\left(\Gamma(\tau_{i})\limsup_{x\to\infty}A_{i}(x)\frac{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}{x}\right)\mkern-2.0mu,

which, however, contradicts the choice of
G
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7
.

We prove by showing that the set
P
of prime numbers may be written as

\mkern-10.0muP=P_{1}\cup\dots\cup P_{h}

(2.1)

with
P_{i}\cap P_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
and with the partitions
P_{i}
subject to

P_{i}(x)=\tau_{i}\frac{x}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\Big)

(2.2)

and

\lim_{x\to\infty}\bigg(\prod_{{p\leq x,\;p\in P_{i}}}\frac{p}{p-1}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{p-1}{p}\bigg)^{\tau_{i}}=a_{i}.

(2.3)

In order to see why the existence of such a partition indeed implies the theorem, we shall invoke the Wirsing–Odoni theorem (see
[
3
, Pr. 4]
).

Theorem
.

(Wirsing–Odoni)

Let
f
be a multiplicative function. Assume that there exist constants
u
,
v
such that
0\leq f(p^{k})<uk^{v}
for all primes
p
and all positive integers
k
. Assume further that there exist real numbers
\xi>0
and
1<r<2
such that

\sum\limits_{x\geq p\text{ prime}}f(p)=\xi\frac{x}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{x}{\log^{r}x}\Big)

as
x\to\infty
. Then, the product

C_{f}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\xi)}\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\Big(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+\frac{f(p^{3})}{p^{3}}+\cdots\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\xi}

over the primes is convergent (and positive), and

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}f(n)=C_{f}\frac{x}{\log^{1-\xi}x}+O\Big(\frac{x}{\log^{r-\xi}x}\Big)

as
x\to\infty
.

Back to the proof, introduce

A_{i}=\{n:\textrm{each prime factor of $n$ belongs to the set $P_{i}$}\}

for
1\leq i\leq h
. We wish to apply the Wirsing–Odoni theorem to the multiplicative function defined as
f(p^{k})=1
if
p\in P_{i}
and
f(p^{k})=0
if
p\notin P_{i}
with
p
a prime. It follows that

\sum_{p\leq x}f(p)=P_{i}(x)=\tau_{i}\frac{x}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\Big),

and by condition (
2.3
), also

C_{f}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}\prod_{p\textrm{ prime}}\Big(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+\frac{f(p^{3})}{p^{3}}+\ldots\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\tau_{i}}=\frac{a_{i}}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}.

According to the Wirsing–Odoni theorem, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=A_{i}(x)=\Big(\frac{a_{i}}{\Gamma(\tau_{i})}+o(1)\Big)\frac{x}{\log^{1-\tau_{i}}x}.

As
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{h})\mkern-1.0mu\in\mkern-1.0mu\mathop{\rm MC}\nolimits_{h}
, we win.

The existence of a suitable partition subject to conditions (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) is shown via the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
P=\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots\}
be the set of prime numbers with
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots
, and pick
0<\kappa\leq 1
. Consider now
Q\subseteq P
subject to

Q(x)=\kappa\frac{x}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\Big)

and such that there exists

p\mkern 1.0muv_{1}\in A_{j}^{i+1}
,
v_{2}\in A_{k}^{i+1}
.

•

If
y/x<p
and
y/N\leq p
, then
p\in F_{3}^{1}\cup F_{3}^{2}\cup\ldots\cup F_{3}^{h}
, so there exists
1\leq j\leq h
such that
p\in A_{j}^{i+1}
and
v=m/p\leq y/p\leq N
. Consequently, there exists
1\leq k\leq h
,
k\neq j
such that
v\in A_{k}^{i+1}
since
[N]\subseteq A_{1}^{i+1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{h}^{i+1}
. In particular,
m
can be written as
m=p\mkern 1.0muv
.

•

If
y/x<p<y/N
, then there exists
j
with
N\leq j\leq x-1
and
y/(j+1)<p\leq y/j
, so we have
v\leq\frac{y}{p}<x
. By the induction hypothesis, we can find
v_{1}\in A_{j}^{i}
,
v_{2}\in A_{k}^{i}
for
1\leq j\neq k\leq h
such that
v=v_{1}v_{2}
. As before, without loss of generality, we may assume that
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
, so we have
v_{1}\leq\sqrt{v}\leq\sqrt{y/p}
. Then,
pv_{1}\in F_{2}\subset(A_{j}^{i+1}\cap A_{k}^{i+1})
and
v_{2}\in A_{k}^{i}\subset A_{k}^{i+1}
, and hence
m
can be written as
m=(pv_{1})v_{2}
with
pv_{1}\in A_{j}^{i+1}
,
v_{2}\in A_{k}^{i+1}
.

This shows that condition (
1
) is also met.

We now prove that for all
1\leq j\leq h
, condition (
2
) is met for
A_{j}^{i+1}
and
y=n_{i+1}
. If
x^{4}<y
, then

|A_{j}^{i}\cup F_{0}|=|A_{j}^{i}\cup\{i:x<i\leq y^{2/3}x\}|\leq y^{2/3}x<y^{11/12}<\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\,\frac{y}{\log y}

for
y
large enough. Moreover, if
x^{4}<y
and
x\geq N>\frac{256}{\varepsilon^{2}}
, then

\displaystyle|F_{1}|

\displaystyle=|\{pv:y^{2/3}<p\leq y/x,\;p\text{ is a prime},\;v<\sqrt{x}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\pi(y/x)\sqrt{x}\leq 2\frac{y/x}{\log(y/x)}\sqrt{x}=2\frac{y}{\log y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\log x}{\log y}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\,\frac{y}{\log y}.

If
y>x^{200/\varepsilon}
, then

\displaystyle|F_{2}|

\displaystyle=|\{pv:y/x<p\leq y/N,\;p\text{ is a prime},\;v\leq\sqrt{y/p}\}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{pv:\exists j:N\leq j\leq x-1,\;y/(j+1)<p\leq y/j,\;p\text{ is a prime},\;v\leq\sqrt{j+1}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=N}^{x-1}\bigg(\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j}\Big)-\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j+1}\Big)\bigg)\sqrt{j+1}.

Note that with
x
fixed and as
y\to\infty
, we have
\pi\big(\frac{y}{j}\big)=\frac{y}{j\log y}+o\big(\frac{y}{j(\log y)^{1.5}}\big)
, yielding

\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j}\Big)-\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j+1}\Big)=\frac{1}{j(j+1)}\frac{y}{\log y}+o\Big(\frac{y}{j(\log y)^{1.5}}\Big)

(for instance, it suffices to take
y=x^{x}
). Hence, we may write

\sum\limits_{j=N}^{x-1}\bigg(\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j}\Big)-\pi\Big(\frac{y}{j+1}\Big)\bigg)\sqrt{j+1}=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j=N}^{x-1}\frac{1}{j\sqrt{j+1}}\bigg)\frac{y}{\log y}+o\Big(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{\log y}}\Big)\frac{y}{\log y},

where

\sum\limits_{j=N}^{x-1}\frac{1}{j\sqrt{j+1}}<\int_{\frac{256}{\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{3/2}}dx=\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.

In particular, we have

|\{pv:y/x<p\leq y/N,\;p\text{ is a prime},\;v\leq\sqrt{y/p}\}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\,\frac{y}{\log y}

for
y
large enough relative to
x
. This accounts now for the last term in the decomposition of
A_{j}^{i+1}
as

|F_{3}^{j}|=|\{p:y/N<p\leq y,\;p\text{ is a prime},\;\pi(p)\equiv j\;{\rm mod}\;h\}|\leq 1+\frac{\pi(y)}{h}\leq\left(\frac{1}{h}+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\right)\frac{y}{\log y}

for
y
sufficiently large. Putting this all together, for all
1\leq j\leq h
and for
y
sufficiently large, we get

|A_{j}^{i+1}|\leq|A_{j}^{i}|+|F_{0}|+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|+|F_{3}^{j}|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{h}+\varepsilon\Big)\frac{y}{\log y},

completing the proof.
∎

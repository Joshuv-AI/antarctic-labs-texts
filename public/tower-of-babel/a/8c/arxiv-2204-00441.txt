Hochschild homology of mod- p p motivic cohomology over algebraically closed fields
Bjørn Ian Dundas, Michael A. Hill, Kyle Ormsby, Paul Arne Østvær

Abstract
We perform Hochschild homology calculations in the algebro-geometric setting of motives. The motivic Hochschild homology coefficient ring contains torsion classes which arise from the mod- p p motivic Steenrod algebra and from generating functions on the natural numbers with finite non-empty support. Under the Betti realization, we recover Bökstedt’s calculation of the topological Hochschild homology of finite prime fields.

Tor spectral sequence for motivic Hochschild homology

A motivic spectrum is cellular if it belongs to the smallest full subcategory of the
stable motivic homotopy category, which is closed under homotopy colimits and contains
the motivic spheres
S^{p,q}
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
,
see
[
9
, §2.8]
.
The cellularity assumption is central in motivic homotopy theory,
see, e.g.,

[
28
, §2.3]
.
It is, moreover, needed for running the motivic Tor spectral sequence.

We begin by relating the integral Tor spectral sequence to the bar construction.
Our setup is a map of motivic ring spectra
\mathscr{R}\rightarrow\mathscr{Q}
and an
\mathscr{Q}
-
\mathscr{R}
bimodule
\mathscr{M}
.
We assume that
\mathscr{R}
is a commutative motivic ring spectrum and
\mathcal{A}=\mathscr{Q}\wedge\mathscr{R}
is a cofibrant
\mathscr{R}
-algebra.
Then the derived smash product
\mathscr{M}\wedge_{\mathcal{A}}\mathscr{Q}
in (
8
) is the realization of the
simplicial object

\{[s]\mapsto\mathscr{M}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}^{s}}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{Q}\}.

The skeletal filtration yields the
E^{1}
page of the Tor spectral sequence,
which — if
\pi_{\star}\mathcal{A}
is flat over
\pi_{\star}\mathscr{R}
—
takes the form

E^{1}_{s,\star}=B_{s}(\pi_{\star}\mathscr{M},\pi_{\star}\mathcal{A},\pi_{\star}\mathscr{Q})=\pi_{\star}\mathscr{M}\otimes_{\pi_{\star}\mathscr{R}}\pi_{\star}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes_{\pi_{\star}\mathscr{R}}^{s}}\otimes_{\pi_{\star}\mathscr{R}}\pi_{\star}\mathscr{Q}.

It is conventional to denote the generators of the bar complex by
[m_{0}|a_{1}|\dots|a_{s}|n_{s+1}]
.
When
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{R}
we abbreviate
[1|a_{1}|\dots|a_{s}|1]
to
[a_{1}|\dots|a_{s}]
.
The homology of
E^{1}_{s,\star}
computes the
E^{2}
page of the Tor spectral sequence (
15
).
We recall the
d^{1}
differential is given by the alternating sum of the face maps

d_{i}[m_{0}|a_{1}|\dots|a_{s}|n_{s+1}]=\begin{cases}[m_{0}\cdot a_{1}|a_{2}|\dots|a_{s}|n_{s+1}]&i=0\\
[m_{0}|a_{1}|\dots|a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1}|\dots|a_{s}|n_{s+1}]&0<i<s\\
[m_{0}|a_{1}|\dots|a_{s-1}|a_{s}\cdot n_{s+1}]&i=s.\end{cases}

If
\mathcal{A}
is commutative
and the modules
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{Q}
are commutative
\mathcal{A}
-algebras,
then the skeletal filtration of the simplicial object

\{[s]\mapsto\mathscr{M}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{A}^{\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}^{s}}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{Q}\}
is isomorphic to
(\mathscr{M}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{Q})\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}(S^{1}\otimes\mathcal{A})

in the category of commutative
\mathscr{R}
-algebras
. This is
isomorphic to the more Hochschild-homology-looking
\{[s]\mapsto(\mathscr{M}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{Q})\wedge\mathscr{Q}^{\wedge s}\}
, and the filtration arises from the simplicial circle.
Hence the Tor spectral sequence (
15
) is a
\pi_{\star}\mathscr{R}
-algebra spectral sequence with the
multiplicative structure on the
E^{1}
page given by the shuffle product introduced by Eilenberg-Mac Lane

[
11
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
\mathscr{M}
,
\mathscr{R}
, and
\mathscr{S}
are cellular motivic spectra.
Then
the skeletal filtration of the simplicial circle gives rise to a strongly convergent trigraded Tor spectral sequence

E^{2}_{h,t,w}=\mathbf{Tor}^{\pi_{\star}(\mathscr{S}\wedge\mathscr{R}^{\text{op}})}_{h,t,w}(\pi_{\star}(\mathscr{M}),\pi_{\star}(\mathscr{S}))\Rightarrow\mathbf{MHH}_{h+t,w}(\mathscr{R};\mathscr{M}).

(15)

Here,

h
is the homological grading on the torsion product and
(t,w)
is the internal grading
for the bigraded motivic homotopy groups in topological degree
t
and weight
w
.
The differentials are of the form

d^{r}\colon E^{r}_{\ast,\ast,\ast}\rightarrow E^{r}_{\ast-r,\ast+r-1,\ast}.

If
\mathscr{R}
is commutative and
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{M}
are commutative
\mathscr{R}
-algebras, then the Tor spectral sequence is a spectral sequence of
\mathscr{R}
-algebras. The pinch map on the circle induces the Hopf-algebra structure on the torsion groups on the
E^{2}
page. If
E^{2}
,…,
E^{r}
are all flat over
\mathscr{R}_{\star}
for
2\leq r
, then the
E^{r}
page inherits an
\mathscr{R}
-Hopf algebra structure; in particular, the
r
-th differential
d^{r}\colon E^{r}\to E^{r}
satisfies the “co-Leibniz” rule in the sense that it commutes with the coproduct
\psi\colon E^{r}\to E^{r}\otimes E^{r}
.

Proof.

This follows from (
8
),

[
1
, §4]
,

[
9
, Proposition 7.7]
,

[
25
, §2]
.
∎

The suspension map
\sigma\colon\mathscr{R}\wedge S^{1}\wedge\mathscr{R}\to S^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R}
has a simple interpretation under
the isomorphism

S^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R}\cong\{[s]\mapsto(\mathscr{R}\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}\mathscr{R})\wedge(\mathscr{R}\wedge\mathscr{R})^{\wedge_{\mathscr{R}}s}\}

of the Tor-interpretation: it is the map from
S^{1}\wedge(\mathscr{R}\wedge\mathscr{R})=\{[s]\mapsto\bigvee_{\{1,\dots,s\}}(\mathscr{R}\wedge\mathscr{R})\}
sending the
i
th summand to the inclusion on the
i
th factor (and units elsewhere). In particular, if
x\in\pi_{d,w}(\mathscr{R}\wedge\mathscr{R})
, then
\sigma_{*}x\in\pi_{d+1,w}(S^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R})
is the class represented by
[x]\in E^{1}_{1,d,w}
.

The Hopkins-Morel equivalence shown by Hoyois
[
16
, Proposition 8.1]
implies the cellularity assumption
in
Proposition

2.4
holds for
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{S}=\mathbf{M}\mathbb{F}_{p}
since the base scheme
F
is a field of exponential
characteristic
\mathsf{e}(F)\neq p
.
In this case,
we have the Tor spectral sequence

E^{2}_{h,t,w}=\mathbf{Tor}^{\mathcal{A}_{\star}}_{h,t,w}(\mathbb{M}_{\star},\mathbb{M}_{\star})\Rightarrow\mathbf{MHH}_{h+t,w}(\mathbf{M}\mathbb{F}_{p}).

(16)

Remark 2.5
.

By
Remark

2.5
and (
16
) it follows that,
for algebraically closed fields,

\mathbf{MHH}_{\star}(\mathbf{M}\mathbb{F}_{p})
is independent of the exponential characteristic
\neq p
.

2.4
Torsion products

For reference we record some basic facts about the structure of
\operatorname{Tor}
in simple situations.
If
(\mathsf{x}_{i})
is a basis for an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space,
we denote the corresponding divided power, exterior, and symmetric algebras by
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})
,

\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})
, and
\text{S}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})
,
respectively.
Let
\gamma_{n}\mathsf{x}
denote the
n
th divided power of a class
\mathsf{x}
in degree
d
.
Then the graded divided power algebra
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})
is generated by elements
\gamma_{p^{j}}\mathsf{x}
in degree

dp^{j}
subject to the relations
\gamma_{0}\mathsf{x}=1
,

\gamma_{1}\mathsf{x}=\mathsf{x}
, and

\gamma_{m}\mathsf{x}\cdot\gamma_{n}\mathsf{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m+n\\[4.30554pt]
m\end{array}\right)\gamma_{m+n}\mathsf{x}.

The symmetric algebra functor
\text{S}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(-)
is left adjoint to the forgetful functor from

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-modules,
and the symmetric algebra
\text{S}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})
is canonically isomorphic to a polynomial ring.
We let
\text{P}^{h}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})
denote the height
h
truncated polynomial ring
\text{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})/\mathsf{x}^{h}
.
With these definitions,
an exercise in binomial coefficients shows there is an isomorphism of algebras

\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})=\mathbb{F}_{p}\{\gamma_{p^{j}}\mathsf{x}|j\geq 0\}\cong\bigotimes_{j\geq 0}\text{P}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\gamma_{p^{j}}\mathsf{x}).

(17)

We shall make repeatedly use of the following torsion product computations,
see
[
25
, §6]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

For the symmetric algebra
\text{S}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})
on a generator
\mathsf{x}
in even degree
d
,
there is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-bialgebra isomorphism

\mathbf{Tor}^{\text{S}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})}_{\ast}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma\mathsf{x}).

Here,

\sigma\mathsf{x}
in degree
(1,d)
is a coalgebra primitive represented in the bar complex by
[\mathsf{x}]
.

(ii)

For the exterior algebra
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})
on a generator
\mathsf{x}
in odd degree
d
,
there is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-bialgebra isomorphism

\mathbf{Tor}^{\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathsf{x})}_{\ast}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma\mathsf{x}).

Here,
\gamma_{j}\sigma\mathsf{x}
in degree
(j,dj)
is represented in the bar complex by
[\mathsf{x}|\mathsf{x}|\dots|\mathsf{x}]
and has coproduct

\psi\gamma_{k}\sigma\mathsf{x}=\underset{i+j=k}{\sum}\gamma_{i}\sigma\mathsf{x}\otimes\gamma_{j}\sigma\mathsf{x}.

Remark 2.7
.

As an example, let us reconstruct a direct proof of the equation

\tau\sigma\xi_{i+1}=\rho\sigma\tau_{i+1}\in\mathbf{MHH}_{\star}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
Lemma

2.3
. Consider the
E^{1}
page

E^{1}_{s,\star}=\mathcal{A}_{\star}^{\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{\star}}s}\cong\mathbb{M}_{\star}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{\star}}\mathcal{A}_{\star}^{\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{\star}}s}\otimes_{\mathbb{M}_{\star}}\mathbb{M}_{\star}

of the spectral sequence for
\mathbf{MHH}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\star}=\pi_{\star}(\mathbf{M}\mathbb{F}_{2}\wedge\mathbf{M}\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the dual Steenrod algebra. Then

d^{1}[\tau_{i}|\tau_{i}]=[\tau^{2}_{i}]=\tau[\xi_{i+1}]+\rho[\tau_{i+1}]+\rho[\tau_{0}\xi_{i+1}]

and
d^{1}[\tau_{0}|\xi_{i+1}]=[\tau_{0}\xi_{i+1}]
, so that
\tau[\xi_{i+1}]+\rho[\tau_{i+1}]
is a boundary.
Hence
\tau\sigma\xi_{i+1}=\rho\sigma\tau_{i+1}
.

With the notation
\gamma_{j}\sigma\tau_{i}=[\tau_{i}|\dots|\tau_{i}]\in E^{1}_{j,\star}
and
\sigma x=[x]
the shuffle product yields an explicit formula for the
d^{1}
differentials

\displaystyle d^{1}\gamma_{j+2}\sigma\tau_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{j-1}[\tau_{i}|\dots|\tau_{i}^{2}|\dots|\tau_{i}]=[\tau_{i}^{2}]\cdot[\tau_{i}\dots|\tau_{i}]=(\tau[\xi_{i+1}]+\rho[\tau_{i+1}]+\rho[\tau_{0}\xi_{i+1}])\cdot[\tau_{i}\dots|\tau_{i}]

\displaystyle=(\tau\sigma\xi_{i+1}+\rho\sigma\tau_{i+1}+\rho\sigma(\tau_{0}\xi_{i+1}))\cdot\gamma_{j}\sigma\tau_{i}.

When the ground field contains a square root of
-1
, so that
\rho=0
, we get the formula

d^{1}\gamma_{j+2}\sigma\tau_{i}=\tau\sigma\xi_{i+1}\cdot\gamma_{j}\sigma\tau_{i}.

Conversely, for odd primes
p
we can use
Lemma

2.3
to
deduce
differentials by a simple weight count — simplifying the corresponding topological argument.

Lemma

2.6
tells us that

E^{2}=\mathbb{M}_{\star}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\bigotimes_{i\geq}\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma\xi_{i+1})\otimes\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\sigma\tau_{i}).

We know that
\tau^{p-1}\sigma\xi_{i+1}
has to be hit by a differential. When the ground field is algebraically closed,
\mathbb{M}_{\star}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\tau]
with
\tau\in\mathbb{M}_{0,-1}
. In this case the source of the differential hitting
\tau^{p-1}\sigma\xi_{i+1}
must come from linear combinations of monomials in
\sigma\xi_{i}
s and
\gamma_{j}\sigma\tau_{i}
s of total degree
2p^{i+1}
and weight at least
p^{i+1}-p
. A quick count shows that the only monomial with sufficient weight is
\gamma_{p}\sigma\tau_{i}
and so we have the equation
(described up to a unit in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
)

d^{p-1}\gamma_{p}\sigma\tau_{i}\overset{\cdot}{=}\tau^{p-1}\sigma\xi_{i+1}.

2.5
A Bockstein type complex

We end the section by doing an entirely algebraic exercise which will be needed later on.
Let
p
be any prime and consider the commutative differential graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
(C,D)
, where

C=\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\bar{\mu}_{i})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\bar{\lambda}_{i+1})

and
D\colon C\to C
is the derivation generated by
D(\gamma_{j+p}\bar{\mu}_{i})=\bar{\lambda}_{i+1}\gamma_{j}\bar{\mu}_{i}
.
As before,

B^{D}=\mathrm{im}D
,
Z^{D}=\ker D
and
H^{D}=Z^{D}/B^{D}
— the aim of this subsection is to calculate these.
In the application
C
will be the mod-
\tau^{p-1}
motivic Hochschild homology of
\mathbb{F}_{p}

(the reader may recognize it as
\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{rig}}_{\star}}_{*}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
) and
D
will be derived from a Bockstein.

We first fix some notation.
For each non-empty finite set of natural numbers
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
we choose an element
t_{S}\in S
with the property that
t_{S\cup T}\in\{t_{S},t_{T}\}
.
The minimum,
t_{S}=\min S
, is a good choice, but there are many others.
Down the road, such a choice amounts to a particular choice of basis,
and there is no reason to prefer one over the other,
except that in concrete examples,
some can be more convenient.
If the function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\circlearrowleft
has finite non-empty support,
we write
t_{f}=t_{\mathrm{supp}f}
.
For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\delta_{j}\colon\mathbb{N}\circlearrowleft
be the function with
\mathrm{supp}\,\delta_{j}=\{j\}
and
\delta_{j}(j)=1
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
J
denote the set of pairs
(S,f)
,
where the function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\circlearrowleft
has finite support and
S\subseteq\mathrm{supp}f
.
The subset
K\subseteq J
consists of the pairs
(S,f)
,
where the support of
f
is non-empty and
S
does not contain
t_{f}
.

Definition 2.9
.

For
(S,f)\in J
,
we set

\chi_{S,f}:=\left(\prod_{m\in S}\bar{\lambda}_{m+1}\gamma_{pf(m)-p}\bar{\mu}_{m}\right)\left(\prod_{n\not\in S}\gamma_{pf(n)}\bar{\mu}_{n}\right)\in C.

(18)

In particular,

\chi_{\emptyset,0}=1
,

\chi_{\emptyset,p^{j}\delta_{n}}=\gamma_{p^{j+1}}\bar{\mu}_{n}
and
\chi_{\{m\},\delta_{m}}=\lambda_{m+1}
.

We note that

D\chi_{S,f}=\sum_{n\in\mathrm{supp}(f)-S}\chi_{S\cup\{n\},f}

since
D\gamma_{n}\bar{\mu}_{i}=\bar{\lambda}_{i+1}\gamma_{n-p}\bar{\mu}_{i}
.
Next we construct sub-complexes of
(C,D)
.

Definition 2.10
.

If
f\colon\mathbb{N}\circlearrowleft
has finite support,
the
associated
f
-cube
is the sub-complex

(C^{f},D):=(\bigoplus_{S\subseteq\mathrm{supp}f}\mathbb{F}_{p}\{\chi_{S,f}\},D)\subseteq(C,D).

If
f=0
, then
C^{f}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Furthermore, let
Z^{f}=\ker D\cap C^{f}
,
B^{D}=\mathrm{im}D\cap C^{f}
and
H^{f}=Z^{f}/B^{f}
.

Note that if
f=0
, then
Z^{f}=H^{f}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Recall the number
t_{f}\in\mathrm{supp}f
chosen once and for all (whenever
\mathrm{supp}f
is non-empty) just before
Definition
2.8
.

We analyze the multiplicative structure.

Definition 2.12
.

For functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{N}\circlearrowleft
with finite support and non-empty finite sets
S,T\subseteq\mathbb{N}

define
K_{S,T,f,g}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
by

K_{S,T,f,g}=\left(\prod_{s\in S}\binom{fs-1+gs}{fs-1}\right)\left(\prod_{t\in T}\binom{ft+gt-1}{ft}\right)\left(\prod_{c\notin S\cup T}\binom{fc+gc}{fc}\right)

if
(S,f),(T,g)\in J
and
S\cap T=\emptyset
, and set
K_{S,T,f,g}=0
otherwise.
Moreover,
we define

\epsilon_{u,S,T,f,g}=K_{S\cup\{u\},T\cup\{t_{f+g}\},f+g}+K_{S\cup\{t_{f+g}\},T\cup\{u\},f+g}.

Note that when
(S,f),(T,g)\in J
and
S\cap T=\emptyset
,
each factor in the formula
K_{S,T,f,g}
is

unless the index is in

\mathrm{supp}\,f\cap\mathrm{supp}\,g=\mathrm{supp}(f\cdot g)
,
and so we can restrict to these factors to simplify the calculation.
We will need
\epsilon_{u,S,T,f,g}
only in the case when
u\in\mathrm{supp}(f+g)
,

u\not=t_{f+g}
and
u\notin S\cup T
.

The following lemma,
a consequence of the defining relations among divided power generators of
C
,
explains the relevance of these numbers.

Lemma 2.13
.

For
(S,f),(T,g)\in J
we have

\chi_{S,f}\chi_{T,g}=K_{S,T,f,g}\cdot\chi_{S\cup T,f+g}

and if

(A-A)(A-A)=\{(a_{1}-a_{2})(a_{3}-a_{4}):a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in A\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}

“large”? There is a number of results in the literature that address this type of question (in fact over finite fields of not necessarily prime order).

Theorem 1
.

Let
p

A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set in the finite field with
p

B,C,D

(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\text{ with }a,a^{\prime}\in A,\dots,d,d^{\prime}\in D

is
~\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(p^{2/3}|A|^{16/3})
.

Hence if
|A|=\Omega(p^{5/8})
, then the number of solutions is
O(|A|^{8}/p)
and consequently

|(A\pm A)(A\pm A)|>\frac{p}{2}.

(A\pm A)(A\pm A)
is used as shorthand for three different sets like

(A-A)(A+A)=\{(a_{1}-a_{2})(a_{3}+a_{4}):a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in A\}.

Theorem 2

(Rudnev)
.

Let

A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
and

. Suppose that

\sum_{x\in X}E_{+}(A,xA)=O(E_{+}(A)^{1/2}(|A|^{3/2}|X|^{3/4}+|A||X|))=O(|A|^{3}|X|^{3/4}+|A|^{5/2}|X|).

By virtue of Bourgain’s Theorem C, where it is further assumed that
|X|\leq|A|
and the exponent of

in the right hand side is

, one could prove a weaker version of Theorem
1
where the hypothesis would be

for some absolute positive constant

[

2
Additive energies of a set and its dilates

We begin by defining the additive energy of a set and its dilate and listing some of its properties, which will be instrumental in the proof of Theorem
1
.

We use standard notation on set operations. For sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
we denote by
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}

their
sum set
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
we denote by
\xi A=\{\xi a:a\in A\}
the
dilate
of
A
by
\xi
.

We also use
representation functions
of the following kind

r_{A+BA}(x)=\text{ number of solutions to $x=a+ba^{\prime}$ with $a,a^{\prime}\in A$ and $b\in B$}.

Note that, say, the dilate sum
A+\xi A
is the support of the function
r_{A+\xi A}
.

Definition
.

Let
0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The
additive energy
of
A
and
\xi A
is defined in the following equivalent ways

\displaystyle E_{+}(A,\xi A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r_{A\pm\xi A}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\text{number of solutions to $(a_{1}-a_{2})=\xi(a_{3}-a_{4})$ with the $a_{i}\in A$}

\displaystyle=|A|^{2}+\text{number of solutions to $\frac{a_{1}-a_{2}}{a_{3}-a_{4}}=\xi$ with the $a_{i}\in A$}.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the identity
\sum_{x}r_{A+\xi A}(x)=|A|^{2}
(each ordered pair
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A\times A
contributes 1 to the sum) imply

E_{+}(A,\xi A)\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{x}r_{A+\xi A}(x)\right)^{2}}{|\mathrm{supp}(r_{A+\xi A})|}\geq\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}.

(1)

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on an exact expression for the sum of the
E_{+}(A,\xi A)
over all
0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
due to Bourgain, Katz, and Tao
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3

(Bourgain , Katz, Tao)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The following identity holds.

\sum_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{+}(A,\xi A)=|A|^{4}+p|A|^{2}-2|A|^{3}.

Hence for every set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
we have the following inequality

\sum_{\xi\in S}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)\leq p|A|^{2}.

Proof.

The first statement is Lemma 1 in
[
5
]
. The second follows by the Cauchy-Schwarz lower bound on
E_{+}(A,\xi A)
stated in (
1
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in S}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{CS}}{\leq}\sum_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{p-1}{p}|A|^{4}

\displaystyle=|A|^{4}+p|A|^{2}-2|A|^{3}-|A|^{4}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}

\displaystyle\leq p|A|^{2}.

∎

The Bourgain-Katz-Tao calculation implies that
E_{+}(A,\xi A)=\Omega(|A|^{3})
for
O(p/|A|)
many
\xi
. We deduce stronger quantitative bounds from Theorem
2
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p
be a prime,
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and
K=O(\max\{p/|A|,|A|^{1/2}\})
. The number of
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
such that
E_{+}(A,\xi A)>|A|^{3}/K
is
O(K^{4})
.

Proof.

Let
X
be the set of
\xi
in question.

We first prove that
|A|^{2}|X|=O(p^{2}),
as is required in the statement of Theorem
2
. The
K=O(p/|A|)
condition implies that
E_{+}(A,\xi A)=\Omega(|A|^{4}/p)
and so Lemma
3
implies that
|X|=O(p|A|^{2}/(|A|^{3}/K))=O(Kp/|A|)
. Therefore
|A|^{2}|X|=O(Kp|A|)=O(p^{2})
.

Next we use the
K=O(|A|^{1/2})
condition to prove that
|X|<|A|^{2}
. Suppose not. Then the
|A|^{5/2}|X|
term would dominate in the conclusion of Theorem
2
and we would get

\frac{|X||A|^{3}}{K}<\sum_{\xi\in X}E_{+}(A,\xi A)=O(|A|^{5/2}|X|\},

which would imply
K=\Omega(|A|^{1/2})
. So we must have
|X|\leq|A|^{2}
and so the
|A|^{3}|X|^{3/4}
term dominates in the conclusion of Theorem
2
. This implies

|X|\frac{|A|^{3}}{K}\leq\sum_{\xi\in X}E_{+}(A,\xi A)=O(|X|^{3/4}|A|^{3}).

It follows that
|X|=O(K^{4})
.
∎

Note that the
K=O(p/|A|)
condition is not restrictive, as the proof implies.

3
Sketch of the proof of Theorem
1

Before giving a detailed proof of Theorem
1
, we present a quick overview of the argument when
A=B=C=D
. The intention is to illustrate why we can go bellow the
p^{2/3}
threshold for
(A-A)(A-A)
. There naturally is considerable overlap between this section and the subsequent, where a detailed proof is given.

(A-A)(A-A)
is the support of the function
r:=r_{(A-A)(A-A)}
, which counts the number of representations of
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
of the form
(a_{1}-a_{2})(a_{3}-a_{4})
with
a_{1},\dots,a_{4}\in A
. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

|(A-A)(A-A)|\geq\frac{|A|^{8}}{\sum_{x}r(x)^{2}}.

The heuristic that random sets should minimise the second moment of
r
suggests that
\sum r(x)^{2}\geq|A|^{8}/p
. Theorem
1
follows from the fact that
|A|^{8}/p
is the correct order of magnitude for
\sum r(x)^{2}
provided that
|A|=\Omega(p^{5/8})
.

To see why this is the case, we observe that, roughly speaking,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{i}\in A}|\{(a_{1}-a_{2})(a_{3}-a_{4})=(a_{5}-a_{6})(a_{7}-a_{8})\}|

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{a_{i}\in A}\left|\left\{\frac{a_{1}-a_{2}}{a_{7}-a_{8}}=\frac{a_{5}-a_{6}}{a_{3}-a_{4}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}.

To bound
\sum E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}
, we express each
E_{+}(A,\xi A)=|A|^{4}/p+E_{\xi}
for some
0\leq E_{\xi}\leq|A|^{3}
. Then

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}\simeq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}+\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{\xi}^{2}.

The first sum on the right side is about
|A|^{8}/p
. Our task is to bound the second sum. Let us begin with a trivial bound.

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{\xi}^{2}\leq|A|^{3}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{\xi}\overset{\text{Lemma~\ref{BKT}}}{\leq}p|A|^{5}.

Noting that

p|A|^{5}\leq\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}\iff|A|^{3}\geq p^{2},

we observe that
|(A-A)(A-A)|=\Omega(p)
when
|A|=\Omega(p^{2/3})
.

This simple argument is sharp only when a positive proportion of the sum

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{\xi}\overset{\text{Lemma~\ref{BKT}}}{\simeq}p|A|^{2}

comes from the values of
\xi
where
E_{\xi}=\Omega(|A|^{3})
.

If this were the case, then there would be
\Omega(p/|A|)
values of
\xi
for which
E_{+}(A,\xi A)\simeq E_{\xi}=\Omega(|A|^{3})
. This would contradict Lemma
4
. This means that
\displaystyle\sum E_{\xi}^{2}
is considerably smaller than
p|A|^{5}
, which in turn allows one to go below the
p^{2/3}
threshold.

4
An explicit Theorem C

Let us now prove Theorem
2
. For notational brevity, we express sets of solutions to equations like
(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})
by

\{(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\}.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}E_{+}(A,xA)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{i}\in A,x\in X}|\{a_{1}-a_{2}=x(a_{3}-a_{4})\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\sum_{a_{i}\in A,x\in X}|\{a_{1}-a_{2}=t=x(a_{3}-a_{4})\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r_{A-A}(t)r_{X(A-A)}(t)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{C-S}}{\leq}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r_{A-A}(t)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r_{X(A-A)}(t)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=E_{+}(A)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\sum_{a_{i}\in A,x_{j}\in X}|\{x_{1}(a_{1}-a_{2})=t=x_{2}(a_{3}-a_{4})\}|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=E_{+}(A)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{a_{i}\in A,x_{j}\in X}|\{x_{1}(a_{1}-a_{2})=x_{2}(a_{3}-a_{4})\}|\right)^{1/2}.

We apply Theorem 19 in
[
1
]
to bound the bracketed term under the hypothesis
|A|^{2}|X|=O(p^{2})
. In the notation of
[
1
]
, we apply Theorem 19 (or the discussion above it) to
B=X
and
C=A
. So
P=A\times X
and hence
|L|=|A||X|
. We note that
|L||A|=|A|^{2}|X|=O(p^{2})
. Therefore the number of solutions to
x_{1}(a_{1}-a_{2})=x_{2}(a_{3}-a_{4})
with the
a_{i}\in A
and
x_{j}\in X
is

O(|A|^{3}|X|^{3/2}+|A|^{2}|X|(|A|+|X|))=O(|A|^{3}|X|^{3/2}+|A|^{2}|X|^{2}).

The theorem follows by the inequalities
(m+n)^{1/2}\leq m^{1/2}+n^{1/2}
and
E_{+}(A)\leq|A|^{3}
.
∎

Murphy communicated to us the following analogous result for
multiplicative energies
, which, like Theorem
2
, is analogous, albeit weaker, to a result of Murphy, Roche-Newton, and Shkredov from
[
13
]
. We define the multiplicative energy of
A
and its translate
A+\xi
as follows.

E_{\times}(A,A+\xi)=\text{ number of solutions to }a_{1}(a_{2}+\xi)=a_{3}(a_{4}+\xi)\text{ with the }a_{i}\in A.

The multiplicative energy of
A
is defined by
E_{\times}(A)=E_{\times}(A,A)
and is at most
|A|^{3}
.

Theorem 5

(Murphy)
.

Let
p
be a prime and
A,X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Suppose that
|A|^{2}|X|=O(p^{2})
. The following inequality is true.

\sum_{x\in X}E_{\times}(A,A+x)=O(E_{\times}(A)^{1/2}(|A|^{3/2}|X|^{3/4}+|X||A|))=O(|X|^{3/4}|A|^{3}+|X||A|^{5/2}).

5
Bringing additive energies into the calculation

To prove Theorem
1
we express
(A-B)(C-D)
as the support of the representation function
r:=r_{(A-B)(C-D)}
and bound the second moment of
r
, which equals the number of solutions to the given equation. The rest follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

The proof is split in two parts: The first where we reduce the question to bounding
\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}
; and the second where via Theorem
2
and Lemma
3
we bound the sum
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}
. In this section we complete the first step.

Recall that
B,C,D
are translates of non-zero dilates of
A
and that for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
we take

r(x)=\text{ number of solutions to $(a-b)(c-d)=x$ with $a\in A,\dots,d\in D$}.

(2)

Lemma 6
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
B,C,D
be translates of non-zero dilates of
A
. The number of solutions to

(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\text{ with }a,a^{\prime}\in A,\dots,d,d^{\prime}\in D

is
\displaystyle O(|A|^{6})+\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}
.

Proof.

We denote the set of solutions to
(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})
by

\{(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\}.

The number of solutions where
(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})=0
is
O(|A|^{6})
. From now on we consider non-zero solutions. To denote this we put a
*
over sums. We have the following string of inequalities.

\displaystyle\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}|\{(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\neq 0\}|

\displaystyle~=\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}^{*}|\{(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\}|

\displaystyle~=\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{a-b}{a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}}=\frac{c-d}{c^{\prime}-d^{\prime}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle~=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{a-b}{a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}}=\xi=\frac{c-d}{c^{\prime}-d^{\prime}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle~=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{a-b}{a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}}=\xi\right\}\right|\right)\left(\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{c-d}{c^{\prime}-d^{\prime}}=\xi\right\}\right|\right).

(3)

For
\xi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{a-b}{a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}}=\xi\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}|\{a-\xi a^{\prime}=b-\xi b^{\prime}\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}^{*}|\{a-\xi a^{\prime}=x=b-\xi b^{\prime}\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x}r_{A-\xi A}(x)r_{B-\xi B}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(\sum_{x}r_{A-\xi A}(x)^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{x}r_{B-\xi B}(x)^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{E_{+}(A,\xi A)E_{+}(B,\xi B)}

\displaystyle=E_{+}(A,\xi A).

In the last step we used the fact that if
B=c+\lambda A
, then
E_{+}(A)=E_{+}(B)
.

We similarly have

\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}^{*}\left|\left\{\frac{c-d}{c^{\prime}-d^{\prime}}=\xi\right\}\right|\leq E_{+}(A,\xi A).

So inequality (
5
) becomes

\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{b,b^{\prime}\in B}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{d,d^{\prime}\in D}|\{(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\neq 0\}\leq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}.

The lemma follows by adding the “
\xi=0
term”.
∎

We continue with some more rearranging.

Lemma 7
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The following identity holds.

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}\leq\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+2|A|^{6}+\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}-2\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)+(p-1)\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lemma~\ref{BKT}}}{=}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}-\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}-2|A|^{6}+4\frac{|A|^{7}}{p}-\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\xi\neq 0}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}-2|A|^{6}-\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}.\hskip 43.05542pt\qed

6
The sum of the squares of additive energies

The next and more substantial step is to use Lemma
4
to, essentially, bound the sum of the squares of the additive energies.

Proposition 8
.

Let
p
be a prime and
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The following inequality holds.

\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}=O(p^{2/3}|A|^{16/3}+p|A|^{9/2}).

Proof.

We split the calculation in three parts. Let
K=O(\max\{p/|A|,|A|^{1/2})
be a parameter to be determined later.

For “very small”
E_{+}(A,\xi A)
we use Lemma
3
.

\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)\leq|A|^{5/2}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq|A|^{5/2}\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)\overset{\text{Lem.\,\ref{BKT}}}{=}p|A|^{9/2}.

(4)

Similarly, for “small”
E_{+}(A,\xi A)
we have

\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)\leq\frac{|A|^{3}}{K}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{p|A|^{5}}{K}.

(5)

For “large”
E_{+}(A,A\xi)
we define an integer
k
via
2^{k-1}<K\leq 2^{k}
and consider sets
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
defined by

X_{i}=\left\{\xi\neq 0:\frac{|A|^{3}}{2^{i}}<E_{+}(A,\xi A)\leq\frac{|A|^{3}}{2^{i-1}}\right\}.

By Lemma
4
we conclude
|X_{i}|=O(2^{4i})
. So

\displaystyle\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)>\tfrac{|A|^{3}}{K}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)>\tfrac{|A|^{3}}{K}}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\xi\in X_{i}}E_{+}(A,\xi A)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|X_{i}|\frac{|A|^{6}}{2^{2i}}

\displaystyle=O\left(|A|^{6}\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{2i}\right)

\displaystyle=O(2^{2k}|A|^{6})

\displaystyle=O(K^{2}|A|^{6}).

(6)

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\neq 0}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)\leq|A|^{5/2}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 21.52771pt+\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)\leq\tfrac{|A|^{3}}{K}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 21.52771pt+\sum_{E_{+}(A,\xi A)>\tfrac{|A|^{3}}{K}}\left(E_{+}(A,\xi A)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{vsmall},\eqref{small},\eqref{large}}{=}O\left(p|A|^{9/2}+\frac{p|A|^{5}}{K}+|A|^{6}K^{2}\right).

To optimise
K
we equate the two expressions containing it.

\frac{p|A|^{5}}{K}=|A|^{6}K^{2}\iff K^{3}=\frac{p}{|A|}\iff K=\left(\frac{p}{|A|}\right)^{1/3},

crucially noting that the above value is indeed
O(\sqrt{p/|A|})
. We have already assumed that
K=O(|A|^{1/2})
.

Taking
K=(p/|A|)^{1/3}
produces the stated upper bound.
∎

7
Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
1

We are finally in position to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Lemmata
6
and
7
, the number of solutions to

(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\text{ with }a,a^{\prime}\in A,\dots,d,d^{\prime}\in D

is
\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(p^{2/3}|A|^{16/3}+p|A|^{9/2}+|A|^{6})=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(p^{2/3}|A|^{16/3}+p|A|^{9/2})
.

When
|A|=\Omega(p^{5/8})
, the first term dominates the second and third.

For the final conclusion we note that
(A-B)(C-D)
is the support of the function
r
defined in (
2
) on p.
2
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

|(A-B)(C-D)|\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r(x)\right)^{2}}{\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r(x)^{2}}=\frac{|A|^{8}}{\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}r(x)^{2}}

because each ordered quadruple
(a,b,c,d)\in A\times B\times C\times D
contributes 1 to the sum
\sum_{x}r(x)
.

In the
|A|=\Omega(p^{5/8})
range, the sum
\sum_{x}r(x)^{2}=O(|A|^{8}/p)
because it is nothing other than the number of solutions to

(a-b)(c-d)=(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})(c^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\text{ with }a,a^{\prime}\in A,\dots,d,d^{\prime}\in D.

Yau and Souplet-Zhang type gradient estimates on Riemannian manifolds with boundary under Dirichlet boundary condition
Keita Kunikawa, Yohei Sakurai

Abstract
. In this paper, on Riemannian manifolds with boundary, we establish a Yau type gradient estimate and Liouville theorem for harmonic functions under Dirichlet boundary condition. Under a similar setting, we also formulate a Souplet-Zhang type gradient estimate and Liouville theorem for ancient solutions to the heat equation.

Primary 53C20; Secondly 31C05, 35K05, 35B40, 58J35

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Elliptic and parabolic gradient estimates

In geometric analysis,
one of the main problems is to estimate the gradient of solutions to elliptic or parabolic equations on Riemannian manifolds under curvature bounds.
Yau
[
14
]
has established the following gradient estimate for harmonic functions (see
[
14
]
, and also
[
2
]
):

Theorem 1.1

(
[
14
]
,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold (without boundary).
For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
.
For
x_{0}\in M
,
let
u:B_{R}(x_{0})\to(0,\infty)
be a positive harmonic function.
Then we have

\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\sqrt{K}\right)

on
B_{R/2}(x_{0})
,
where
C_{n}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
.

Inspired by Theorem
1.1
and the work of Hamilton
[
3
]
,
Souplet-Zhang
[
13
]
have obtained the following parabolic version (see
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
):

Theorem 1.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold (without boundary).
For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
.
For
x_{0}\in M
,
let
u
be a positive solution to the heat equation on

Q_{R,T}(x_{0}):=B_{R}(x_{0})\times[-T,0].

For
A>0
, we assume
u<A
.
Then we have

\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\sqrt{K}\right)\left(1+\log\frac{A}{u}\right)

on
Q_{R/2,T/4}(x_{0})
.

1.2.
Gradient estimates on manifolds with boundary

The aim of this article is to formulate analogues of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
on Riemannian manifolds with (smooth) boundary under Dirichlet boundary condition.
Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
.
For
R>0
,
we denote by
B_{R}(\partial M)
the
R
-neighborhood of
\partial M
.
We also denote by
H_{\partial M}
the infimum of the mean curvature over
\partial M
,
where the mean curvature is defined as the trace of the shape operator for the unit inner normal vector on
\partial M
.
For harmonic functions with Dirichlet boundary condition (i.e., it is constant on the boundary),
we prove the following gradient estimate on a neighborhood of the boundary under a lower Ricci curvature bound, and a lower mean curvature bound for the boundary:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
and
H_{\partial M}\geq-(n-1)\sqrt{K}
.
Let
u:B_{R}(\partial M)\to(0,\infty)
be a positive harmonic function with Dirichlet boundary condition.
We assume that its derivative
u_{\nu}
in the direction of the outward unit normal vector
\nu
is non-negative over
\partial M
.
Then we have

\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\sqrt{K}\right)

on
B_{R/2}(\partial M)
.

Further,
for solutions to the heat equation with Dirichlet boundary condition,
we obtain the following gradient estimate under the non-negativity of mean curvature on the boundary:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
.
Let
u
be a positive solution to the heat equation on

Q_{R,T}(\partial M):=B_{R}(\partial M)\times[-T,0].

For
A>0
,
let us assume
u<A
.
We further assume that
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition
(
i.e.,
u(\cdot,t)|_{\partial M}
is constant for each fixed
t\in[-T,0]
)
, and
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
over
\partial M\times[-T,0]
.
Then we have

\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\sqrt{K}\right)\left(1+\log\frac{A}{u}\right)

on
Q_{R/2,T/4}(\partial M)
.

1.3.
Liouville theorems

Theorem
1.3
together with the standard argument leads to the following Liouville theorem:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
We assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
.
Let
u:M\to(0,\infty)
be a positive harmonic function with Dirichlet boundary condition.
We assume
u_{\nu}\geq 0
over
\partial M
.
Then
u
is constant.

Remark 1.6
.

Without the assumption
u_{\nu}\geq 0
,
this type of Liouville theorem does not hold.
Actually,
if
M=[0,\infty)
and
u(x)=1+x
,
then it is a non-trivial positive harmonic function.

Theorem
1.4
together with a similar argument of the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
tells us the following:

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
We assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
.
Then we have the following:

(1)

Let
u:M\times(-\infty,0]\to(0,\infty)
be a positive ancient solution to the heat equation with Dirichlet boundary condition,
and
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
over
\partial M\times(-\infty,0]
.
If

u(x,t)=\exp\left[o\left(\rho_{\partial M}(x)+\sqrt{|t|}\right)\right]

near infinity, then
u
must be constant.
Here
\rho_{\partial M}(x)
denotes the Riemannian distance from the boundary;

(2)

let
u:M\times(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}
be an ancient solution to the heat equation with Dirichlet boundary condition,
and
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
over
\partial M\times(-\infty,0]
.
If

u(x,t)=o\left(\rho_{\partial M}(x)+\sqrt{|t|}\right)

near infinity, then
u
is constant.

Remark 1.8
.

Due to the splitting theorem by Kasue
[
5
, Theorem C]
,
under the same setting as in Corollaries
1.5
and
1.7
,
if
M
is non-compact,
then it is isometric to
[0,\infty)\times\partial M
.

2.
Preliminaries

Here,
let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold with boundary.

2.1.
Laplacian comparison on manifolds with boundary

We first recall a Laplacian comparison theorem for the distance function from the boundary.
The distance function from the boundary
\rho_{\partial M}:M\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\rho_{\partial M}:=d(\cdot,\partial M),

which is smooth outside of the cut locus for the boundary
\mathrm{Cut}\,\partial M
(see e.g.,
[
11
, Section 3]
for the basics of the cut locus for the boundary).

For
K,\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
we denote by
s_{K,\Lambda}(t)
the unique solution to the Jacobi equation
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)+K\varphi(t)=0
with initial conditions
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=-\Lambda
.
We have the following Laplacian comparison theorem (see
[
4
, Corollary 2.44]
):

Theorem 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For
K,\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)K
and
H_{\partial M}\geq(n-1)\Lambda
.
Then we have

\Delta\rho_{\partial M}\leq(n-1)\frac{s^{\prime}_{K,\Lambda}(\rho_{\partial M})}{s_{K,\Lambda}(\rho_{\partial M})}

outside of
\mathrm{Cut}\,\partial M
.

Remark 2.2
.

For
K\geq 0
,
we see
s_{-K,-\sqrt{K}}(t)=e^{\sqrt{K}t}
;
in particular,

\frac{s^{\prime}_{-K,-\sqrt{K}}(t)}{s_{-K,-\sqrt{K}}(t)}=\sqrt{K}.

On the other hand,
we possess
s_{-K,0}(t)=\cosh\sqrt{K}t
;
in particular,

\frac{s^{\prime}_{-K,0}(t)}{s_{-K,0}(t)}=\sqrt{K}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{K}t}{\cosh\sqrt{K}t}\leq\sqrt{K}.

2.2.
Reilly type formula

We next recall the following useful formula,
which is used in the standard proof of the so-called Reilly formula (see e.g.,
[
10
]
,
[
7
, Chapter 8]
):

Proposition 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

For all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\right)_{\nu}=2\,\varphi_{\nu}\left[\Delta\varphi-\Delta_{\partial M}(\varphi|_{\partial M})-\varphi_{\nu}H\right]

\displaystyle+2\,g_{\partial M}\left(\nabla_{\partial M}(\varphi|_{\partial M}),\nabla_{\partial M}\varphi_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle-2\,{\rm I\hskip-0.09995ptI}\left(\nabla_{\partial M}(\varphi|_{\partial M}),\nabla_{\partial M}(\varphi|_{\partial M})\right),

where
H
and
{\rm I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
are the mean curvature and second fundamental form,
respectively.

2.3.
Harmonic functions

We recall the following estimate for a harmonic function

\Delta u=0,

which is a consequence of Bochner formula and refined Kato inequality (see e.g.,
[
12
, (3.9)]
):

Proposition 2.4
.

For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
.
Let
u:M\to(0,\infty)
denote a positive harmonic function.
We set

(2.1)

\phi:=\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}.

Then we have

\Delta\phi\geq-(n-1)K\phi-\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}\frac{g(\nabla\phi,\nabla u)}{u}+\frac{1}{n-1}\phi^{3}.

2.4.
Heat equation

We collect some properties of a positive solution to the heat equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u

We possess the following (see e.g.,
[
13
]
,
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
):

Lemma 2.5
.

We set

(2.2)

f:=\log u.

Then we have

(\Delta-\partial_{t})f=-\|\nabla f\|^{2}.

We also have the following (see e.g.,
[
13
]
,
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
):

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f
be defined as
(\ref{eq:log heat})
.
We assume
\sup f<1
,
and set

(2.3)

w:=\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{(1-f)^{2}}.

Then we have

\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)w-\frac{2fg(\nabla w,\nabla f)}{1-f}\geq 2(1-f)w^{2}+\frac{2\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{(1-f)^{2}}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section,
we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
and
u
be as in Theorem
1.3
.
We define a function
F:B_{R}(\partial M)\to\mathbb{R}
by

F:=(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})\phi,

where
\phi
is defined as (
2.1
).
Assume that
F
achieves its maximum at
\bar{x}\in B_{R}(\partial M)
.

We first consider the case where
\bar{x}\in B_{R}(\partial M)\setminus\partial M
.
Due to the well-known argument by Calabi
[
1
]
,
we may assume that
\bar{x}
does not belong to
\mathrm{Cut}\,\partial M
(see e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
for the explicit construction of a barrier function).
At
\bar{x}
,
it holds that

\nabla F=0,\quad\Delta F\leq 0,

and hence

\frac{\nabla\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}=\frac{\nabla\phi}{\phi},\quad-\frac{\Delta\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}+\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}-\frac{2g(\nabla\rho^{2}_{\partial M},\nabla\phi)}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})\phi}\leq 0.

It follows that

(3.1)

\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}-\frac{\Delta\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}-\frac{2\|\nabla\rho^{2}_{\partial M}\|^{2}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})^{2}}\leq 0.

By virtue of Theorem
2.1
,

(3.2)

\Delta\rho^{2}_{\partial M}=2+2\rho_{\partial M}\Delta\rho_{\partial M}\leq 2+2(n-1)\sqrt{K}\rho_{\partial M}\leq C_{n,1}(1+\sqrt{K}\rho_{\partial M}),

where
C_{n,1}:=2(n-1)
.
Since
\|\nabla\rho^{2}_{\partial M}\|^{2}=4\rho^{2}_{\partial M}
,
Proposition
2.4
, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) yield

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}-\frac{C_{n,1}(1+\sqrt{K}\rho_{\partial M})}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}-\frac{8\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-(n-1)K-\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}\frac{g(\nabla\phi,\nabla u)}{\phi u}+\frac{1}{n-1}\phi^{2}-\frac{C_{n,1}(1+\sqrt{K}\rho_{\partial M})}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}-\frac{8\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})^{2}}.

Using

\frac{g(\nabla\phi,\nabla u)}{\phi u}=\frac{2\rho_{\partial M}g(\nabla\rho_{\partial M},\nabla u)}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})u}\leq\frac{2\rho_{\partial M}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M}}\phi,

we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F^{2}}{n-1}-\frac{4(n-2)\rho_{\partial M}F}{n-1}-C_{n,1}(1+\sqrt{K}\rho_{\partial M})(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})-8\rho^{2}_{\partial M}-(n-1)K(R^{2}-\rho^{2}_{\partial M})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F^{2}}{n-1}-2C_{n,2}RF-C_{n,1}(1+\sqrt{K}R)R^{2}-8R^{2}-(n-1)KR^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F^{2}}{n-1}-2C_{n,2}RF-C_{n,3}(1+\sqrt{K}R)^{2}R^{2},

where

C_{n,2}:=2(n-2)(n-1)^{-1},\quad C_{n,3}:=\max\left\{C_{n,1}+8,\frac{C_{n,1}}{2},n-1\right\}.

Therefore,

F(\bar{x})\leq(n-1)\left[C_{n,2}R+\sqrt{C^{2}_{n,2}R^{2}+\frac{C_{n,3}}{n-1}R^{2}(1+\sqrt{K}R)^{2}}\right]\leq C_{n,4}R(1+\sqrt{K}R),

where

C_{n,4}:=(n-1)\left(2C_{n,2}+\sqrt{\frac{C_{n,3}}{n-1}}\right).

On the ball we have

\frac{3R^{2}}{4}\sup_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n,4}R(1+\sqrt{K}R).

We arrive at the desired inequality.

We next observe the case where
\bar{x}\in\partial M
.
In this case,
we refer to the argument of the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 2]
.
At
\bar{x}
,
it holds that

F_{\nu}\geq 0,

and hence

\phi_{\nu}=\frac{F_{\nu}}{R^{2}}\geq 0;

in particular,
(\phi^{2})_{\nu}\geq 0
.
Here we notice that
the Dirichlet boundary condition for
u
and the assumption
u_{\nu}\geq 0
yield
\|\nabla u\|=u_{\nu}
.
Since
\phi=\|\nabla\log u\|
, and
\log u
also satisfies Dirichlet boundary condition,
Proposition
2.3
implies

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(\phi^{2})_{\nu}=\left(\|\nabla\log u\|^{2}\right)_{\nu}=2(\log u)_{\nu}\left(\Delta\log u-(\log u)_{\nu}H\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}\left(-\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}H\right)=2\frac{u^{2}_{\nu}}{u^{2}}\left(-\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}-H\right).

Therefore,

\phi(\bar{x})=\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}\leq-H\leq(n-1)\sqrt{K}.

It follows that

F(\bar{x})=R^{2}\phi(\bar{x})\leq(n-1)\sqrt{K}R^{2}\leq C_{n,5}R(1+\sqrt{K}R),

where
C_{n,5}:=n-1
.
Thus,

\frac{3R^{2}}{4}\sup_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{u}\leq C_{n,5}R(1+\sqrt{K}R).

We complete the proof.

\Box

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In the present section,
we prove Theorem
1.4
.
Let
(M,g)
denote an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.

4.1.
Cutoff argument

In this subsection,
we make preparations for the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
To do so,
we first recall the following elementary fact:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then there is a smooth function
\psi:[0,\infty)\times(-\infty,0]\to[0,1]
,
and a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
depending only on
\alpha
such that the following hold:

(1)

\psi\equiv 1
on
[0,R/2]\times[-T/4,0]
, and
\psi\equiv 0
on
[R,\infty)\times(-\infty,-T/2]
;

(2)

\partial_{r}\psi\leq 0
on
[0,\infty)\times(-\infty,0]
, and
\partial_{r}\psi\equiv 0
on
[0,R/2]\times(-\infty,0]
;

(3)

we have

\frac{|\partial_{r}\psi|}{\psi^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{R},\quad\frac{|\partial^{2}_{r}\psi|}{\psi^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{R^{2}},\quad\frac{|\partial_{t}\psi|}{\psi^{1/2}}\leq\frac{C}{T},

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Using Lemma
4.1
,
we show the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

For
K\geq 0
,
we assume
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
.
Let
u
denote a positive solution to the heat equation on
Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
.
We assume
u<1
.
We define
f
and
w
as
(\ref{eq:log heat})
and
(\ref{eq:gradient log heat})
on
Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
,
respectively.
We also take a function
\psi:[0,\infty)\times(-\infty,0]\to[0,1]
in Lemma
4.1
with
\alpha=3/4
,
and define

(4.1)

\psi(x,t):=\psi(\rho_{\partial M}(x),t).

Then we have

(4.2)

(\psi w)^{2}\leq\frac{C_{n,1}}{R^{4}}+\frac{\widetilde{C}}{T^{2}}+C_{n,2}K^{2}+2\Phi

at every
(x,t)\in Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
with
x\notin\partial M\cup\mathrm{Cut}\,\partial M
,
where for the universal constants
C_{3/4},C>0
given in Lemma
4.1
,
we put

\displaystyle C_{n,1}

\displaystyle:=20C^{2}_{3/4}\left(\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{8}+\frac{1}{4}+C^{2}_{3/4}+\frac{27C^{2}_{3/4}}{160}\right),\quad\widetilde{C}:=5C^{2},

\displaystyle C_{n,2}

\displaystyle:=20(n-1)^{2}\left(1+\frac{C^{2}_{3/4}}{8}\right),

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle:=(\Delta-\partial_{t})(\psi w)-\frac{2g\left(\nabla\psi,\nabla(\psi w)\right)}{\psi}-\frac{2fg(\nabla(\psi w),\nabla f)}{1-f}.

Proof.

Notice that

u<1
implies
f<0
,
and hence
w
is well-defined.
By direct computations and Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)w-\frac{2\psi fg(\nabla w,\nabla f)}{1-f}+w\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)\psi-\frac{2w\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}}{\psi}-\frac{2wfg(\nabla\psi,\nabla f)}{1-f}

\displaystyle\geq 2(1-f)\psi w^{2}+\frac{2\psi\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{(1-f)^{2}}+w\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)\psi-\frac{2w\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}}{\psi}-\frac{2wfg(\nabla\psi,\nabla f)}{1-f}.

It follows that

(4.3)

2(1-f)\psi w^{2}\leq\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}+\Psi_{3}+\Psi_{4}+\Phi

for

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{2\psi\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{(1-f)^{2}},\quad\Psi_{2}:=-w\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)\psi,

\displaystyle\Psi_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2w\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}}{\psi},\quad\Psi_{4}:=\frac{2wfg(\nabla\psi,\nabla f)}{1-f}.

We estimate
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3},\Psi_{4}
from above.
The following Young inequality plays an essential role in the estimates:
For all
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
a,b\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.4)

ab\leq\frac{\varepsilon a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{q}}{\varepsilon^{q/p}q}.

The inequality

(4.5)

\frac{\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}}{\psi^{3/2}}\leq\frac{C^{2}_{3/4}}{R^{2}}

is also useful,
which follows from Lemma
4.1
.
We first deduce an upper bound of
\Psi_{1}
.
By the assumption for the Ricci curvature,
the Young inequality (
4.4
) with
p,q=2
,
and
\psi\leq 1
,

(4.6)

\Psi_{1}=-\frac{2\psi\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{(1-f)^{2}}\leq 2(n-1)K\psi w\leq\varepsilon\psi^{2}w^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}K^{2}}{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}K^{2}}{\varepsilon}.

We next give an upper bound of
\Psi_{2}
.
Due to Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle=-w\left(\Delta-\partial_{t}\right)\psi=-w\left(\partial_{r}\psi\Delta\rho_{\partial M}+\partial^{2}_{r}\psi-\partial_{t}\psi\right)

\displaystyle\leq w|\partial_{r}\psi|\Delta\rho_{\partial M}+w|\partial^{2}_{r}\psi|+w\,|\partial_{t}\psi|

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\sqrt{K}w|\partial_{r}\psi|+w|\partial^{2}_{r}\psi|+w\,|\partial_{t}\psi|.

From the Young inequality (
4.4
) with
p,q=2
,
Lemma
4.1
,
and
\psi\leq 1
,
we derive

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}K}{4}\frac{|\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}}{\varepsilon\psi}\right)+\left(\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{|\partial^{2}_{r}\psi|^{2}}{\varepsilon\psi}\right)+\left(\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{|\partial_{t}\psi|^{2}}{\varepsilon\psi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{C^{2}_{3/4}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{\psi^{1/2}}{R^{4}}+\frac{C^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{1}{T^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}C^{2}_{3/4}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{K}{R^{2}}\psi^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{C^{2}_{3/4}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{1}{R^{4}}+\frac{C^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{1}{T^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}C^{2}_{3/4}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{K}{R^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon\psi w^{2}+\frac{C^{2}_{3/4}}{4\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{R^{4}}+\frac{C^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\frac{1}{T^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}C^{2}_{3/4}}{8\varepsilon}K^{2},

where we used the inequality of arithmetic-geometric means in the last inequality for the
(K/R^{2})
-term.
We next study an upper bound of
\Psi_{3}
.
By the Young inequality (
4.4
) with
p,q=2
,
and (
4.5
),

(4.8)

(\ref{eq:log heat}),(\ref{eq:gradient log heat})

\log(1-f)

2.3

\displaystyle=-f\frac{\|\nabla f\|^{2}}{(1-f)^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}f}{1-f}=-f\frac{f^{2}_{\nu}}{(1-f)^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}f}{1-f}=-f(\log(1-f))^{2}_{\nu}-\frac{\partial_{t}f}{1-f}.

-fw^{1/2}\leq-H\leq 0

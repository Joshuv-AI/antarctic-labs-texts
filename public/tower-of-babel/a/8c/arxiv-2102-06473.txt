Abstract

1.
Introduction

In 2008, Brundan and Kleshchev
[
1
]
gave an explicit isomorphism between blocks of cyclotomic affine Hecke algebras of symmetric groups, or the corresponding degenerated cyclotomic affine Hecke algebras, and cyclotomic KLR algebras of type A. On the other hand, in 1989, Lusztig showed that there is a natural isomorphism between affine Hecke algebras and their graded versions
[
5
]
. Motivated by Lusztig’s work, we introduced the semi-rationalization extensions of affine Hecke algebras in
[
4
]
, where an isomorphism between direct sums of blocks of cyclotomic affine Hecke algebra of all types and their cyclotomic semi-rationalization algebras was given.

The aim of this paper is to give a shorter proof of Brundan and Kleshchev isomorphism using the machinery of our general result.

Main result
(Theorem
5.6
)
The cyclotomic KLR algebra of type A and blocks of the cyclotomic
(
degenerate
)
affine Hecke algebra of a symmetric group can be realized as the same quotient of an algebra.

It can be illustrated by the following diagram of algebras.

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
\mathcal{L}
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})
\cong
\cong
\cong

The algebra
\mathcal{L}
, called the Lusztig algebra, is some extension of a degenerate affine Hecke algebra or a non-degenerate affine Hecke algebra. This algebra contains all corresponding rational functions, which allow us to construct the KLR-basis in a unified way either in the degenerate case or in the non-degenerate case. The algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
, called the semi-rationalization algebra, can be viewed as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
only containing part of the rational functions. The semi-rationalization algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
has three bases: the first one is the KLR basis by which we construct an isomorphism between the cyclotomic semi-rationalization algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
and the cyclotomic KLR algebra
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
; the second one is the degenerate Bernstein-Zelevinski basis by which we give an isomorphism between
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
and the cyclotomic degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
; the third one is the non-degenerate Bernstein-Zelevinski basis by which we give an isomorphism between
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
and the cyclotomic non-degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Demazure operator

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group with basic transpositions
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n-1}
. Let
\Bbbk
be a field. Then
S_{n}
acts on the left on the polynomial ring
\Bbbk[X]
and on the Laurent polynomial ring
\Bbbk[X^{\pm 1}]
in
X:=X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
by permuting variables. Using the
S_{n}
-action above, the
Demazure operators
are defined as
\partial_{r}
on
\Bbbk[x]
for all
1\leq r<n
as

\partial_{r}(f)=\frac{\sigma_{r}(f)-f}{X_{r}-X_{r+1}}.

It is well-known that the Demazure operators satisfy the Leibniz rule

\partial_{r}(fg)=\partial_{r}(f)g+\sigma_{r}(f)\partial_{r}(g),

for all
f,g\in\Bbbk[X]
and for
1\leq r<n
, and the relations

\sigma_{r}(\partial_{r}(f))=\partial_{r}(f),\quad\partial_{r}(\sigma_{r}(f))=-\partial_{r}(f).

Let
\Bbbk(X)
be the corresponding rational function field, then the
S_{n}
-action on
\Bbbk[X]
above can be extended to an action
w\colon\tfrac{f}{g}\mapsto\tfrac{w(f)}{w(g)}
of
S_{n}
on
\Bbbk(X)
(by the field automorphism). This means that the action of the Demazure operators on
\Bbbk[X]
also extends to operators on
\Bbbk(X)
.

Let
F
be the quotient field of the subalgebra

Z:=\Bbbk[X]^{S_{n}}=\{f\in\Bbbk[X]|w(f)=f\ \mbox{for every}\ w\in S_{n}\}

of
S_{n}
-invariants. By
[
5
, 3.12 (a)]
, there is a natural
\Bbbk
-algebra isomorphism

(2.1)

\Bbbk[X]\otimes_{Z}F\to\Bbbk(X),f\otimes g\mapsto fg.

A_{\infty}
if

or

if

:

A_{\infty}:
\cdots
-2
-1

\cdots

0
1
2

For
1\leq r<n
, we define the map
\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s}=i_{\sigma_{r}(s)}
.
Then
S_{n}
acts on the set of
n
-tuples
\text{\boldmath$i$}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in I^{n}
by the place permutation

3.
Affine Hecke algebras and their rationalizations

3.1.
The degenerate affine Hecke algebra

Following
[
3
]
, the
degenerate affine Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}
of
S_{n}
is defined to be the associated unital
\Bbbk
-algebra with generators
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},T_{1},\cdots,T_{n-1}\}
subject to the following relations for all admissible indices:

(3.1)

\displaystyle X_{r}X_{s}

\displaystyle=X_{s}X_{r};

(3.2)

\displaystyle T_{r}X_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}(X_{s})T_{r}+\partial_{r}(X_{s});

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad T_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle=1;

(3.4)

\displaystyle T_{r}T_{s}

\displaystyle=T_{s}T_{r}\hskip 123.48494pt\text{if $|r-s|>2$};

(3.5)

\displaystyle T_{r}T_{r+1}T_{r}

\displaystyle=T_{r+1}T_{r}T_{r+1};

For
w=\sigma_{r_{1}}\sigma_{r_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{r_{m}}\in S_{n}
a reduced expression we put
T_{w}:=T_{r_{1}}T_{r_{2}}\cdots T_{r_{m}}
. Then
T_{w}
is a well-defined element in
\mathcal{H}
and the algebra
\mathcal{H}
is a free
\Bbbk[X]
-module with the basis
\{T_{w}\ |\ w\in S_{n}\}
.

3.2.
The non-degenerate affine Hecke algebra

Assume
1\neq q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. We define the
affine Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}_{q}
to be the associated unital
\Bbbk
-algebra with generators
\{X_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,X_{n}^{\pm 1},T_{1},\cdots,T_{n-1}\}
subject to the following relations for all admissible indices:

(3.6)

\displaystyle X_{r}^{\pm 1}X_{s}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=X_{s}^{\pm 1}X_{r}^{\pm 1},\quad X_{r}X_{r}^{-1}=X_{r}^{-1}X_{r}=1;

(3.7)

\displaystyle T_{r}X_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}(X_{s})T_{r}+(q-1)X_{r+1}\partial_{r}(X_{s});

(3.8)

\displaystyle T_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q-1)T_{r}+q;

(3.9)

\displaystyle T_{r}T_{s}

\displaystyle=T_{s}T_{r}\hskip 123.48494pt\text{if $|r-s|>2$};

(3.10)

\displaystyle T_{r}T_{r+1}T_{r}

\displaystyle=T_{r+1}T_{r}T_{r+1};

Similar to the degenerate case,
\mathcal{H}_{q}
is a free
\Bbbk[X^{\pm 1}]
-module with the basis
\{T_{w}\ |\ w\in S_{n}\}
.

3.3.
The rationalization

By
[
5
, Proposition 3.11]
, the center of
\mathcal{H}
is the commutative algebra
Z
of
S_{n}
-invariants. Thus
\mathcal{H}
can be seen as a
Z
-subalgebra (identified with the subspace
\mathcal{H}\otimes 1
) of the
F
-algebra
\mathcal{H}_{F}:=\mathcal{H}\otimes_{Z}F
.

Similarly, the non-degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q}
also can be seen as a
Z
-subalgebra (identified with the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{q}\otimes 1
) of the
F
-algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q,F}:=\mathcal{H}_{q}\otimes_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{F}.
For any
1\leq r<n
and
f\in\Bbbk(X)
, as a consequence of
[
5
, 3.12 (d)]
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle T_{r}f=\begin{cases}\sigma_{r}(f)T_{r}+\partial_{r}(f)&\text{if $f\in\mathcal{H}_{F}$},\\
\sigma_{r}(f)T_{r}+(q-1)X_{r+1}\partial_{r}(f)&\text{if $f\in\mathcal{H}_{q,F}$}.\end{cases}

In light of the Bernstein and Zelevinski basis of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{q}
, there are decompositions, see
[
5
, 3.12(c)]
:

(3.12)

\mathcal{H}_{F}(\mbox{resp.},\mathcal{H}_{q,F})=\oplus_{w\in S_{n}}T_{w}\Bbbk(x)=\oplus_{w\in S_{n}}\Bbbk(x)T_{w}.

3.4.
Intertwining elements

For
1\leq r<n
, we define the
intertwining element

\kappa_{r}
as follows:

\kappa_{r}:=\begin{cases}T_{r}+\tfrac{1}{X_{r}-X_{r+1}}&\text{in $\mathcal{H}_{F}$},\\
T_{r}+\tfrac{(q-1)X_{r+1}}{X_{r}-X_{r+1}}&\text{ in $\mathcal{H}_{q,F}$}.\end{cases}

The elements
\kappa_{r}
have the following properties
[
5
, Proposition 5.2]
:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}f

\displaystyle=\sigma_{r}(f)\kappa_{r}\hskip 42.67912pt\text{$\forall f\in\Bbbk(X)$};

(3.14)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-\tfrac{1}{(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{2}}&\text{in $\mathcal{H}_{F}$},\\
q-\tfrac{(q-1)^{2}X_{r}X_{r+1}}{(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{2}}&\text{ in $\mathcal{H}_{q,F}$};\end{cases}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}\kappa_{s}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{s}\kappa_{r}\hskip 56.9055pt\text{if $s\neq r,r+1$};

(3.16)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}\kappa_{r+1}\kappa_{r}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{r+1}\kappa_{r}\kappa_{r+1}.

If
w=\sigma_{r_{1}}\sigma_{r_{2}}\cdots\sigma_{r_{m}}
is a reduced expression in
S_{n}
, then we can define
\kappa_{w}:=\kappa_{r_{1}}\kappa_{r_{2}}\cdots\kappa_{r_{m}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{F}
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}_{q,F}
). It is also a well-defined element by the braid relations (
3.15
) and (
3.16
). By the decompositions (
3.12
), we know that
\{\kappa_{w}\ |\ w\in S_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{H}_{F}
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}_{q,F}
) as free
\Bbbk(X)
-module.

4.
The Lusztig extensions of affine Hecke algebras

From now on, we fix
I=\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}

e=\mathrm{char}\Bbbk
for the degenerate affine Hecke algebras, and

and setting

4.1.
The Lusztig extensions

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the unital
\Bbbk
-algebra with basis
\{\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})|\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Multiplication is given by

(4.1)

\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})=\delta^{\text{\boldmath$i$}}_{\text{\boldmath$j$}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

The

of
\mathcal{H}

is the
\Bbbk

\mathcal{L}
which is equal as
\Bbbk

\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{H}_{F}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{E}=\oplus_{w\in S_{n},\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}\kappa_{w}\Bbbk(X)\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})

of the rationalization algebra
\mathcal{H}_{F}
and the semi-simple algebra
\mathcal{E}
. Multiplication is defined so that
\mathcal{H}_{F}
(identified with the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{F}\otimes 1
) and

\mathcal{E}
(identified with the subspace

) are subalgebras of
\mathcal{L}
, and in addition

(4.2)

\displaystyle X_{r}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})X_{r},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))\kappa_{r},

for all

and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}

The

\mathcal{L}_{q}
of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
with respect to
\mathcal{E}
is defined similarly. We have the following relations

(4.4)

T_{r}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}\epsilon(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+\tfrac{1}{X_{r}-X_{r+1}}(\epsilon(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$}))&\text{in $\mathcal{L}$},\\

4.2.
The Brundan-Kleshchev auxiliary elements

Recall that

Then
y_{r}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})y_{r}

Let
\Bbbk[y]
be the polynomial ring with

Lemma 4.6

\Bbbk(X)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{E}=\Bbbk(y)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{E}

\Bbbk[X]

\mathcal{L}

Combining (

Imitate the proof above, we can prove the non-degenerate case.

and
f^{-1}(y)=f(0)^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{m}g(y)^{l}
in
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Thus the homomorphism
\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
is surjective and induces a surjective homomorphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Let
\tilde{F}
be the localization of the commutative ring
\Bbbk[y]^{S_{n}}
of
S_{n}
-invariants in
\Bbbk[y]
with respect to
\{f\in\Bbbk[y]^{S_{n}}\ |\ f(0)\neq 0\}
. Similar to the proof of (
2.1
), we have
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\Bbbk[y]^{S_{n}}}\tilde{F}
by Theorem
4.24
. Since the elements
y_{r}
are nilpotent in

\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
, similar to the proof above, we know that if
f(y)\in K[y]^{S_{n}}
with
f(0)\neq 0
, then it is a unit in
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Thus the homomorphism
K[y]^{S_{n}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\Lambda)
induces a morphism
\pi_{2}\colon\tilde{F}\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Therefore we have an induced algebra homomorphism
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. The homomorphism
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
induces an algebra homomorphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. It is easy to check that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are two-sided inverses. So
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\cong\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

5.
The Brundan-Kleshchev isomorphism

5.1.
Cyclotomic degenerate affine Hecke algebras

Recall that the
cyclotomic degenerate affine Hecke algebra
is defined as

\mathcal{H}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{H}/\langle\prod_{i\in I}(X_{1}-i)^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle.

By
[
1
, Subsection 3.1]
, there is a system
\{e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\}
of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
such that
1=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and

e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)=\{h\in\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\ |\ (X_{r}-i_{r})^{m}h=0\ \mbox{for all}\ 1\leq r\leq n\ \mbox{and}\ m\gg 0\}.

It is easy to check that
X_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})X_{r}
for all
1\leq r\leq n
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
, and the element
f(X)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(X)\in\Bbbk[X]
is a unit in
e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
if and only if
f(\text{\boldmath$i$})\neq 0
. In this case, we write
f(X)^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
for the inverse.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
1\leq r<n
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}
, we have that

(5.2)

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}&\text{if $i_{r+1}=i_{r}$},\\
e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{-1}(e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-e(\text{\boldmath$i$}))&\text{if $i_{r+1}\neq i_{r}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
1\leq s\leq n
, the element

(\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-\sigma(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}(X_{s}-i_{s})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})&\text{if $s\neq r,r+1$,}\\
(X_{r+1}-i_{r+1})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})&\text{if $s=r,$}\\
(X_{r}-i_{r})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})&\text{if $s=r+1.$}\end{cases}

is nilpotent in
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. Similarly, we can show that

\partial_{r}((X_{s}-i_{s})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=0
by the binomial theorem for an integer
m\gg 0
whenever
i_{r}=i_{r+1}
. Therefore, if
i_{r}=i_{r+1}
, we get from (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle(X_{s}-i_{s})^{m}T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=T_{r}(\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-i_{s})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+{\partial}_{r}((X_{s}-i_{s})^{m})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=0

whenever
m\gg 0
. Thus
T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
and then
T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}.

If
i_{r+1}\neq i_{r}
, as a result of (
3.11
), it holds that

\displaystyle(X_{s}-\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})^{m}[T_{r}(X_{r}-X_{r+1})+1]e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=[T_{r}(X_{r}-X_{r+1})+1](\sigma_{r}(X_{s})-\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})_{s})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})

\displaystyle=0

whenever
m\gg 0
. Therefore

\displaystyle T_{r}(X_{r}-X_{r+1})e(\text{\boldmath$i$})+e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))[T_{r}(X_{r}-X_{r+1})+1]

Then right-multiplying by
(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
, we obtain

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Similarly, we can prove that

e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})-(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{-1}e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$})).

Therefore

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+(X_{r}-X_{r+1})^{-1}(e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-e(\text{\boldmath$i$})).

∎

5.2.
Cyclotomic non-degenerate affine Hecke algebras

Recall that the
non-degenerate cyclotomic affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
is

\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{H}_{q}/\langle\prod_{i\in I}(X_{1}-q^{i})^{\Lambda_{i}}\rangle.

Similar to
[
1
, Subsection 4.1]
, there is a system
\{e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\ |\ \text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}\}
of mutually orthogonal idempotents in
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
such that
1=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
and

\displaystyle e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)=\{h\in\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)\ |\ (X_{r}-q^{i_{r}})^{m}h=0\ \mbox{for all}\ 1\leq r\leq n\ \mbox{and}\ m\gg 0\}

.
Accordingly, it holds that
X_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=e(\text{\boldmath$i$})X_{r}
for all
1\leq r\leq n
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
, and the element
f(X)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(X)\in\Bbbk[X]
is a unit in
e(\text{\boldmath$i$})\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
if and only if
f(q^{\text{\boldmath$i$}})\neq 0
. We write
f(X)^{-1}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
for the inverse in this case.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we can show that

Lemma 5.3
.

For
1\leq r<n
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}
, we have

(5.4)

T_{r}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})=\begin{cases}e(\text{\boldmath$i$})T_{r}&\text{if $i_{r}=i_{r+1}$},\\
e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))T_{r}+\tfrac{(1-q)X_{r+1}}{X_{r+1}-X_{r}}(e(\sigma_{r}(\text{\boldmath$i$}))-e(\text{\boldmath$i$}))&\text{if $i_{r}\neq i_{r+1}$}.\end{cases}

Let

e(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}e(\text{\boldmath$i$}).

Then
e(\mathcal{C})
is a primitive central idempotent by (
5.2
) and (
5.4
).

5.3.
The BK isomorphism

By Lemma
4.27
, Theorem (
4.24
) and
5.1
, (
5.4
)there is a homomorphism

\rho\colon\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})(\mbox{resp}.\ \mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C}))

sending the generators
X_{r},T_{r}
to the same named elements, and
f^{-1}(X)\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(\text{\boldmath$i$})\neq 0
(resp.
f^{-1}(X)\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})
with
f(q^{\text{\boldmath$i$}})\neq 0
) to
f^{-1}(X)e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
. We have the following important result.

Lemma 5.5
.

\rho
is an algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

We only prove the claim for degenerate case since the proof of non-degenerate case is similar. Apparently,
\rho
is surjective, so we only need to construct a left-inverse of
\rho
. By Lemma
4.27
, there is a homomorphism

\tau\colon\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)

sending the generators
X_{r},T_{r}
to the same named elements. Let
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{n}
. If
\text{\boldmath$i$}\neq\text{\boldmath$j$}
, then there is some
1\leq r\leq n
such that
j_{r}\neq i_{r}
. We claim that
\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0
. In fact, by the construction of
e(\text{\boldmath$j$})
, there is an integer
m\gg 0
such that
(X_{r}-j_{r})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$j$})=0
. Thus we have

(X_{r}-j_{r})^{m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau((X_{r}-j_{r})^{m}e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0.

The assumption
j_{r}\neq i_{r}
implies that the element
(X_{r}-j_{r})^{-1}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\in\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Thus we get

\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=(X_{r}-j_{r})^{-m}(X_{r}-j_{r})^{m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=(X_{r}-j_{r})^{-m}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})0=0.

Therefore, if
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{n}\setminus\mathcal{C}
we have
\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=0.
Therefore, if
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in\mathcal{C}
, we obtain

\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in\mathcal{C}}\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$i$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$j$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\sum_{\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{n}}\tau(e(\text{\boldmath$i$}))=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$})\tau(1)=\epsilon(\text{\boldmath$j$}).

These show that
\tau|_{\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})}\colon\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)e(\mathcal{C})\to\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
is an algebra homomorphism. It is easy to check that
\tau\rho
is the identity on each generator of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda)
. Thus
\rho
is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

The cyclotomic KLR algebra of type A and blocks of the cyclotomic
(

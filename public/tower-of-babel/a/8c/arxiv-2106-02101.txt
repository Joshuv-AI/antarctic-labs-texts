The Weyl problem for unbounded convex domains in ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} Thanks: Partially supported by FNR project O20/14766753.
Jean-Marc Schlenker

Abstract
. Let K ⊂ ℍ 3 K\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3} be a convex subset in ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} with smooth, strictly convex boundary. The induced metric on ∂ K \partial K then has curvature K > − 1 K>-1 . It was proved by Alexandrov that if K K is bounded, then it is uniquely determined by the induced metric on the boundary, and any smooth metric with curvature K > − 1 K>-1 can be obtained. We propose here an extension of the existence part of this result to unbounded convex domains in ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} . The induced metric on ∂ K \partial K is then clearly not sufficient to determine K K . However one can consider a richer data on the boundary including the ideal boundary of K K . Specifically, we consider the data composed of the conformal structure on the full boundary of K K (in the Poincaré model of ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} ), together with the induced metric on ∂ K \partial K . We show that a wide range of “reasonable” data of this type, satisfying mild curvature conditions, can be realized on the boundary of a convex subset in ℍ 3 {\mathbb{H}}^{3} . Keywords: Weyl problem, hyperbolic geometry, convex domain, isometric embedding.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
The classical Weyl problem in Euclidean and hyperbolic space

The study of smooth or polyhedral convex surfaces in the 3-dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
has a long history. One of the first “modern” results on this theme was the proof by Legendre
[
LegII
]
and Cauchy
[
Cau13
]
of the rigidity of convex polyhedra: if two polyhedra have the same combinatorics and corresponding faces are isometric, then they are congruent.

Alexandrov
[
Ale05
]
later realized that Cauchy’s rigidity result is related to a much deeper statement. The induced metric on the boundary of a convex polyhedron in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is an Euclidean metric with cone singularities with cone angle less than
2\pi
at the vertices, and Alexandrov proved that each metric of this type on the sphere can be realized as the induced metric on the boundary of a unique polyhedron. This result was later extended by Alexandrov
[
Ale05
]
to bounded convex subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, thus solving the “Weyl problem”, proposed by H. Weyl in 1915 and for which substantial progress had already been made by H. Lewy
[
Lew35
]
and others.

Theorem 1.1

(Lewy, Alexandrov)
.

Any smooth metric of positive curvature on the sphere is induced on the boundary of a unique smooth strictly convex subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Alexandrov and Pogorelov also extended Theorem
1.1
to hyperbolic space.

Theorem 1.2

(Alexandrov, Pogorelov)
.

Any smooth metric of curvature
K>-1
on the sphere is induced on the boundary of a unique convex subset of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
with smooth boundary.

Versions of Theorem
1.2
also hold for ideal polyhedra
[
Riv92
]
and for hyperideal polyhedra
[
Sch98a
]
(see also
[
Sch98b
]
for a result on convex domains bounded by disks, as well as a broad generalization by Luo and Wu
[
LW19
]
). The existence of those results hints at the possibility of a wider extension to more general unbounded convex domains in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
as considered here.

1.2.
The boundary data on unbounded convex subsets

Before stating our main result, we define a notion of “boundary data” that is adapted to complete convex subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. In the special case of bounded convex subsets, it coincides with the induced metric in the usual sense.

We consider a convex subsets
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, which a priori is unbounded. Let
\partial_{0}\Omega=\partial\rho(\Omega)
, where
\rho:{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is the Poincaré model of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. So
\partial_{0}\Omega
is the “full” boundary of
\Omega
, including its metric boundary in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
but also its ideal boundary.

We now define a notion of boundary data for a convex subset of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. When this subset is bounded, it’s just the induced metric on the boundary. For an unbounded convex domain
\Omega
, however, a boundary data records both the induced metric on the metric boundary of
\Omega
and the conformal structure on the “full” boundary
\partial_{0}\Omega
, coming from the induced metric on the metric boundary but also from the conformal structure at infinity on the ideal boundary.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be a convex subset. A boundary data for
\Omega
is a pair
(U,h)
, where
U\subset{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
is an open subset,
h
is a complete, conformal metric on
U
, such that there exists a homeomorphism
\gamma:\partial_{0}\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
such that:

•

\gamma
is conformal on the interior of
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
,

•

\gamma\circ\rho(\partial\Omega)=U
, and
\gamma\circ\rho
is an isometry from
\partial\Omega
equipped with its induced metric to
(U,h)
.

The hyperbolic Gauss formula shows that
h
has curvature
K\geq-1
. Note that
\partial_{0}\Omega=\partial\Omega\cup\partial_{\infty}\Omega
, and a homeomorphism
\gamma
as above defines a conformal structure on
\partial\Omega
and on
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
, by pull-back by
\gamma
of the conformal structure on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
.

We do not consider here whether a boundary data (and homeomorphism
\gamma
) is always unique, see
[
Sch20
, §1.4]
for partial results in this direction. We note however that one always exists under conditions that cover the hypothesis of Theorem
1.7
below. The statement uses the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
h
be a complete, smooth, conformal metric on a surface
S
of curvature
K
. We will say that
h
has bounded derivatives if for each
k\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that the
k
-th derivatives of
K
, measured with respect to
h
, are bounded by
C_{k}
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be a convex subset such that
\partial\Omega
is a disjoint union of topological disks, and that the induced metric on
\partial\Omega
has

(1)

curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,1/\epsilon]
, for some
\epsilon>0
,

(2)

bounded derivatives,

(3)

injectivity radius bounded from below by
\epsilon
.

Then there exists a boundary data for
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\delta:\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to\partial_{0}\Omega
be the closest-point projection – in other terms,
\delta
is the identity on
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
, while it projects each point of
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\setminus\partial_{\infty}\Omega
to a point of
\partial\Omega
.

It is proved in Lemma
4.1
that, under the hypothesis considered here,
\partial\Omega
has principal curvatures in
[1/k_{0},k_{0}]
, for some
k_{0}>1
.

As a consequence,
\delta
is
K_{0}
-quasi-conformal, for a constant
K_{0}
depending only on
k_{0}
. Applying the measurable Riemann mapping theorem
[
AB60
]
to the Beltrami differential of
\delta
on
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\setminus\partial_{\infty}\Omega
, we obtain a quasi-conformal homeomorphism
q:\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
which is conformal on
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
, and has the same Beltrami differential as
\delta
on
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\setminus\partial_{\infty}\Omega
.

The homeomorphism
\delta\circ q^{-1}:{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\to\partial_{0}\Omega
is then conformal by construction. The pair
((\delta\circ q^{-1})^{-1}(\partial\Omega),(\delta\circ q^{-1})^{*}I)
is therefore a boundary data for
\Omega
, where
I
denotes the induced metric on
\partial\Omega
.
∎

Our main motivation is the following question, appearing as Question
W^{\Omega}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
in
[
Sch20
]
, reformulated here in line with Definition
1.3
.

Question 1.6
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
be an open subset, and let
h
be a complete metric of curvature
K\geq-1
on
U
. Is there a unique convex subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
such that
(U,h)
is a boundary data for
\Omega
?

As pointed out in
[
Sch20
]
, the answer to this question in such a general setting might be negative, and additional assumptions might be necessary. We provide here an existence result under stronger curvature conditions, and under a mild regularity asumption on the boundary of
U
together with a topological hypothesis on
U
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
k_{0}>0
,
U\subset{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
be an open subset which is a disjoint union of
k_{0}
-quasidisks, let
\epsilon>0
, and let
h
be a smooth, complete metric or curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,-\epsilon]
on
U
with bounded derivatives. There exists a convex subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
such that the boundary data of
\Omega
is
(U,h)
.

A
quasidisk
in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
is the image of a disk by a quasiconformal map from
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
to
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
.

1.3.
A dual question

Specifically in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, there is also a
dual
Weyl problem, concerning the third fundamental form on the boundary of convex bodies, rather than their induced metrics. This third fundamental form is in fact the induced metric on the boundary of a dual convex body in the de Sitter space.

For ideal polyhedra, the third fundamental form is encoded in the (exterior) dihedral angles, so that the dual Weyl problem corresponds to determining which families of dihedral angles can be obtained, see
[
And71
,
Riv96
]
. For bounded polyhedron, it corresponds to the “dual metric” considered by Hodgson and Rivin
[
HR93
]
, while a result is also known for smooth metrics
[
Sch96
]
.

Question
1.6
has an analog concerning the extension of this “dual” Weyl problem to unbounded convex domains with a prescribed data at infinity that includes the third fundamental form, rather than the induced metric, on
\partial\Omega
, see
[
Sch20
]
. We do not consider this dual problem here. It appears plausible that the methods developed here to prove Theorem
1.7
could also apply, in some form, to prove a dual statement, but a different regularization procedure appears to be needed to obtain the necessary estimates.

1.4.
Related results and motivations

A list of related results as well as some background information can be found
[
Sch20
]
, we only list here a few results and conjectures in direct relation with Theorem
1.7
.

Convex subsets with a quasicircle at infinity

One of the simplest cases that can be considered is when
U
is the complement of a quasi-circle in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, and
h
is a complete metric of constant curvature
K\in(-1,0)
on
U
. The corresponding special cases of Theorem
1.7
(together with a dual result with the induced metric replaced by the third fundamental form) is the main result of
[
BDMS21
]
. (Note that the result of
[
BDMS21
]
on the induced metrics also covers the case
K=-1
, where the convex subset
\Omega
is the convex hull of its ideal boundary.) Recently, this existence result was extended to metrics of variable curvature by Abderrahim Mesbah
[
Mes24
]
.

The statements of Theorem A and Theorem B in
[
BDMS21
]
appear superficially quite different from the statement of Theorem
1.7
, since it concerns the possibility to prescribe the “gluing map” at infinity between the induced metrics on the two connected components of the boundary of a domain, with induced metric of constant curvature, which has as boundary at infinity a quasicircle. A moment of reflection shows however that Theorem B of
[
BDMS21
]
is the special case of Theorem
1.7
when
U
is the complement of a quasicircle in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
and
h
has constant curvature
K\in(-1,0)
, with the choice of a gluing map equivalent, through conformal welding, to the choice of the quasicircle which is the complement of
U
in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
.

Convex subsets in quasifuchsian manifolds

The main motivation for this result from
[
BDMS21
]
is a conjecture made by Thurston concerning the induced metric and the measured bending lamination on the convex cores of geometrically finite hyperbolic manifolds. We recall those conjectures in the quasifuchsian case for simplicity. A
quasifuchsian manifold
is a complete hyperbolic manifold, homeomorphic to
S\times{\mathbb{R}}
, where
S
is a closed surface of genus at least
2
, containing a compact, non-empty, geodesically convex subset. A quasifuchsian manifold
M
contains a smallest non-empty convex subset, its
convex core

C(M)
, which is either a totally geodesic surface (in the “Fuchsian” case) or a closed domain with boundary the disjoint union of two surfaces, each homeomorphic to
S
, with an induced metric of constant curvature
-1
, pleated along a measured laminations. Thurston thus stated two conjectures:

(1)

any pair of hyperbolic metrics on
S
, considered up to isotopy, can be uniquely realized as the induced metrics on the boundary of a quasifuchsian manifold,

(2)

any pair of measured laminations on
S
that fill together, such that no leaf has weight larger than
\pi
, can be realized uniquely as the measured bending lamination on the convex core of a quasifuchsian manifold.

The existence part of both statements is known, for (1) it is a consequence of results of Labourie
[
Lab92
]
or of Epstein–Marden
[
EM86
]
, while for (2) it is proved (for geometrically finite hyperbolic manifolds) by Bonahon–Otal
[
BO04
]
. Uniqueness was recently obtained for (2)
[
DS24
]
, while it remains open for (1).

Existence and uniqueness results are known for larger geodesically convex subsets (containing the convex core) with smooth boundary. The induced metric then has curvature
K>-1
, while the measured bending lamination is replaced by the third fundamental form of the boundary, which has curvature
K<1
and closed, contractible geodesics of length
L>2\pi
. It is proved in
[
Lab92
]
(for the existence) and
[
Sch06
]
(for the uniqueness) that, if
h_{-},h_{+}
are two smooth metrics of curvature
K>-1
on a closed surface
S
of genus at least
2
, there exists a unique hyperbolic metric with convex boundary on
S\times[0,1]
such that the induced metrics on the two connected components of the boundary are
h_{-}
and
h_{+}
.

Convex domains in convex co-compact hyperbolic manifolds

The two Thurston conjectures above can in fact be stated for convex co-compact hyperbolic manifolds, and in this more general setting the existence has been proved for both conjecture, and are due to the authors mentioned above in the quasifuchsian setting.

Moreover, if
M
is a closed manifold with non-empty boundary which admits a convex co-compact hyperbolic manifold on its interior, then, again by
[
Lab92
,
Sch06
]
, any metric of curvature
K>-1
on the boundary of
M
can be uniquely realized as the induced metric of a convex hyperbolic metric
g
on
M
.

Theorem
1.7
can be seen as a (partial) generalization of the existence part of this result. Indeed, given such a hyperbolic metric
g
on
M
, the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
, equipped with the lifted metric
\tilde{g}
, is isometric to the interior of a convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, with
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
equal to the limit set
\Lambda
of
M
.
\Omega
is then invariant under the action of a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}M\to PSL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
. The existence result from
[
Lab92
]
then implies the existence of a convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
with a prescribed metric of curvature
K>-1
on each boundary component (equal to the lift of a metric on the corresponding boundary component of
\partial M
). Moreover, in
\partial_{0}\Omega
equipped with the underlying conformal structure, each connected component of
\partial\Omega
corresponds to a quasidisk. So this result from
[
Lab92
]
implies a special case of Theorem
1.7
, which moreover is invariant under the representation
\rho
(without the asumption that the curvature is negative). Theorem
1.7
can be considered as extending the existence result of
[
Lab92
]
without
the need for a group action. However Theorem
1.7
is more general since it allows for situations where
U
is not dense in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
.

Convex domains complete on one side in quasifuchsian manifolds

One such situation can be found by considering a closed, geodesically convex, subset
\Omega
in a quasifuchsian manifold
M
, assuming that
\Omega
is complete “on one side”, that is,
\Omega
is homeomorphic to
S\times[0,\infty)
, where
S
is again a closed surface of genus at least
2
and
\Omega
is complete on the side corresponding to
\infty
, while its metric boundary corresponds to
S\times\{0\}
, which is a smooth, locally strictly convex surface
\partial\Omega
in
M
. The induced metric on
\partial\Omega
then has curvature
K>-1
.

One can then ask whether given any conformal structure
c
on
S
, and any metric
h
of curvature
K>-1
on
S
(both considered up to isotopy) there is a unique quasifuchsian metric on
S\times{\mathbb{R}}
, containing a unique geodesically convex subset
\Omega
complete on one side, such that the conformal structure on the complete boundary is
c
, while the induced metric on the other boundary is
h
. This case is considered in
[
CS22
]
, where an existence and uniqueness result is obtained.

Lifting to the universal cover of
\Omega
, this translates as another instance of Theorem
1.7
, where
U
is now a disk in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
(corresponding to the universal cover of
\partial\Omega
) while the interior of the complement of
U
corresponds to the universal cover of
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
. An existence result in this setting is proved in
[
CS22
]
, by methods which are quite different from those considered here.

1.5.
Questions not considered here

The Weyl problem described in Section
1.1
can also be stated in terms of isometric immersions of locally convex surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
or
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. This point of view leads to different extensions to unbounded locally convex surfaces in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, see
[
Sch20
, Section 5]
. We do not consider questions arising from this other point of view here.

It should be noted that the curvature hypothesis in Theorem
1.7
are quite restrictive. On one hand, one could consider metrics of curvature
K\geq-1
, including the special case of
K=-1
, corresponding to the case where
\partial\Omega
is a locally convex pleated surface, and
\Omega
is the convex hull of its asymptotic boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
. On the other hand, one could consider metrics with positive curvature at some points – this is excluded here for “technical” reasons. The hypothesis of bounded derivatives is also probably too restrictive, and could be weakened with additional efforts.

We also do not consider here the uniqueness in Theorem
1.7
, since different methods and tools are necessary.

1.6.
Outline of the proofs and content of the paper

Section
2
contains a number of well-known constructions and results that are needed at various places in the paper. Section
3
shows how a given boundary data
(U,h)
can be “approximated”, in a suitable sense, by a sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of Riemannian metrics on
S^{2}
which are equal to
h
on increasing domains in
U
, and have constant positive curvature (going to
0
as
n\to\infty
) on open subsets containing the complement of
U
. The Alexandrov theorem can be applied to those metrics, yielding a sequence
(\Omega_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of convex subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, and the remaining part of the paper is focused on proving that
\Omega_{n}\to\Omega
, where
\Omega
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.7
.

Section
4
provides a key estimate on the principal curvatures of surfaces under bounds on the curvature of the induced metrics, following well-understoods arguments that need to be adapted to our context.

Section
5
then contains the proof of the main results. One key idea of the proof of Theorem
1.7
is that, thanks to the bounds on the principal curvatures in Section
4
, the Gauss maps of the surfaces
\partial\Omega_{n}
can be considered as uniformly quasi-conformal diffeomorphisms from
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
to
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
. This sequence therefore has a subsequence converging to a quasiconformal homeomorphism, while, for each connected component of
U
, the corresponding sequence of surfaces in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
(suitably normalized by isometries) has a subsequence converging on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
to a limit surface. The relative positions of those limit surfaces is controlled by the Gauss maps of the
\partial\Omega_{n}
, leading to the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

2.
Background

This section contains two elementary results, used below, recalled here for the reader’s convenience and to avoid ambiguities in notations.

2.1.
Conformal metrics on surfaces

Consider a surface
S
equipped with a smooth Riemannian metric
h
. The following lemma indicates how the curvature changes under a conformal change of metrics.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(S,h)
be a surface equipped with a Riemannian metric, let
u:S\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function, and let
\bar{h}=e^{2u}h
. The curvature of
\bar{h}
is equal to

\bar{K}=e^{-2u}(\Delta_{h}u+K)~,

where
K
is the curvature of
h
.

Note that in this formula and elsewhere, the Laplacian of
h
is defined as

\Delta_{h}u=-\mbox{tr}(Ddu)~,

where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection of
h
. In other terms,
\Delta_{h}
is a positive operator on closed surfaces.

We refer to
[
Bes87
, Theorem 1.159]
for a proof.

2.2.
Surfaces in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be a smooth, oriented surface. We define its
shape operator

B
as

Bx=\nabla_{x}n~,

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
,
n
is the unit oriented normal to
S
, and
x
is any tangent vector to
S
. We then define the
second fundamental form
of
S
as

I\hskip-2.84544ptI(x,y)=I(Bx,y)~,

where
I
denotes the induced metric on
S
(also classically known as the first fundamental form) and
x,y
are two tangent vectors at the same point, and the
third fundamental form
of
S
as

I\hskip-2.84544ptI\hskip-2.84544ptI(x,y)=I(Bx,By)~.

The second fundamental form
I\hskip-2.84544ptI
is a symmetric bilinear form on
TS
, and it is positive semi-definite when
S
is locally convex, with unit normal oriented towards the concave side. We will say that
S
is
strictly convex
if
I\hskip-2.84544ptI
is positive definite.

The shape operator
B
of a smooth surface
S
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
satisfies the
Gauss-Codazzi
equation:

\det(B)=K-1~,

where
K
is the curvature of the induced metric
I
, and, for any two vector fields
x,y
tangent to
S
,

(\nabla_{x}B)(y)-(\nabla_{y}B)(x)=0~.

3.
Approximation by metrics on the sphere

In this section we define a sequence of conformal metrics on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, that “approximate” in a suitable sense a metric
h
defined on an open subset
U
, as seen in the statement of Theorem
1.7
. We then show that this sequence of approximating metrics has suitable bounds on curvatures. This will allow us in Section
5
to prove that one can apply the Alexandrov Theorem to those metrics and obtain a sequence of convex domains converging (after extraction of a subsequence) to a non-compact domain having the required boundary data.

3.1.
Bounds on conformal factors

The main goal of this section is to obtain a bound on the gradient of the conformal factor on each connected component of
U
. We consider a fixed spherical metric
c_{1}
on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, and a connected component
U_{0}
of
U\subset{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, equipped with a complete Riemannian metric
h
which can be written as

h=e^{2u}c_{1}~,

where
u:U_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
c_{1}
is a spherical metric on
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, it is the visual metric associated to a point
m_{1}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. Recall that given a point
m\in{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, the visual metric
c_{m}
on
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
associated to
m
is the spherical metric such that the distance between to ideal points
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is the angle between the geodesic rays from
m
to
\xi
and
\eta
. To simplify a bit the argument, we will assume that
m_{1}\in CH({\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U_{0})
, where
CH
denotes the hyperbolic convex hull.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
h
has curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,-\epsilon]
, where
\epsilon>0
. Assume moreover that
U_{0}
is a
k
-quasi-disk. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
k
, such that for all
m\in U_{0}
,
\|du(m)\|_{h}\leq C
.

The proof is based on comparisons between four conformal metrics on
U_{0}
. In addition to
h
and
c_{1}
, we will consider the hyperbolic metric
h_{-1}
(conformal to
h
) on
U_{0}
, and the Thurston metric
h_{Th}
on
U_{0}
.

Recall that the Thurston metric is defined as follows: for all
m\in U_{0}
, we let
D
be a maximal disk containing
m
and contained in
U_{0}
, and let
h_{Th}
at
m
be equal to the complete hyperbolic metric on
D
at
m
. This metric
h_{Th}
then has curvature in
[-1,0]
, see e.g.
[
KP94
]
.

We will denote by
v,w,x:U_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
the functions such that

h=e^{2v}h_{-1}~,~~h_{-1}=e^{2w}h_{Th}~,~~h_{Th}=e^{2x}c_{1}~.

The proof of Lemma
3.1
will directly follows from the following three lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
h_{-1}
be the complete hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
h
on

The proof of Lemma

requires a simple proposition concerning the comparison between visual metrics at two different points in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
m_{0},m_{1}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be at distance
\delta>0
, and let
y:\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
c_{m_{1}}=e^{2y}c_{m_{0}}
.
Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be such that the angle at
m_{0}
between the segment ending at
m_{1}
and the geodesic ray ending at
\xi
is
\theta
. Then

(1)

y(\xi)=-\log(\cosh(\delta)-\cos(\theta)\sinh(\delta))~.

As a consequence, if
\partial_{\theta}
is the vector at
\xi
corresponding to varying
\theta
while keeping
m_{0},m_{1}
and the plane containing
m_{0},m_{1}
and
\xi
fixed, then

(2)

dy(\partial_{\theta})=-\frac{\sin(\theta)}{\rm{cotanh}(\delta)-\cos(\theta)}~.

Proof.

The first point is a consequence of a standard hyperbolic geometry formula relating the values at
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
of the Busemann function associated to
\xi
. Namely, if
m(t)
is the point at distance
t
from
m_{0}
along the ray
[m_{0},\xi)
, and if
l(t)=d(m_{1},m(t))
then the triangle formula can be written as:

\cosh(l(t))=\cosh(t)\cosh(\delta)-\cos(\theta)\sinh(t)\sinh(\delta)~.

As
t\to\infty
, it follows that

e^{l(t)-t}\sim\cosh(\delta)-\cos(\theta)\sinh(\delta)~,

and the first point follows.

The second point follows from the first by a direct computation.
∎

The Thurston metric has a simple relation to the visual metric, stated for the reader’s convenience in the next proposition. We denote by
\Sigma_{0}
the pleated locally convex surface facing
U_{0}
, defined as:

\Sigma_{0}=\partial CH({\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U_{0})~,

where

denotes the hyperbolic convex hull.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\xi\in U_{0}
, and let
m
be the closest-point projection of
\xi
on
\Sigma_{0}
. Then

Proof.

Let
P
be the support plane of

at

orthogonal to the direction of the geodesic ray from

to
\xi
. This plane

faces a disk in
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
(with the same boundary) which is a maximal disk contained in

and containing
\xi
. By construction,

is equal at

to the push-forward of the induced metric on

by the normal exponential map, considered as a map from

to

.

However a simple computation (for instance done in the Poincaré model of

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

To obtain the required comparison, we choose a point
\xi\in U_{0}
and let

be the closest-point projection of

at first-order at

to

at

3.2.
Construction of approximating metrics

We can now define rather explicitly the sequence of approximating metrics that will be used in the proof. The construction uses the choice of a “cut-off” function.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists a smooth function
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that:

(1)

\phi(x)=x
for
x\leq 0
,

(2)

\phi(x)=1
for
x\geq 2
,

(3)

\phi^{\prime}(x)\in[0,1]
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in[0,2]
,

(4)

\phi^{\prime}(x)\leq e^{2(\phi(x)-x)}
for all
x\in[0,2]
.

Proof.

Note first that
f(x)=x
is a solution of the differential equation

f^{\prime}(x)\leq e^{2(f(x)-x)}

over
[0,1]
. As a consequence, one can find a function
\phi
satisfying the conditions above over
(-\infty,1-\epsilon)
and arbitrarily close to
f(x)=x
, for any
\epsilon>0
. Taking
\epsilon
small enough, one can then “bridge” this function to
f(x)=1
for
x\geq 1+\epsilon
while still satisfying the conditions of the lemma.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

We choose a function
\phi
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.7
.
For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define
\phi_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\phi_{n}(x)=n+\phi(x-n)~.

For the next definition, we need to remark that
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
has at least 2 points, because
U
admits a complete, conformal metric of negative curvature. Therefore the convex hull of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
contains a point
m_{1}
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(U,h)
be a boundary data on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, let
c_{1}=c_{m_{1}}
be the visual metric of a point
m_{1}\in CH({\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U)
, and let
u:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
h=e^{2u}c_{1}
. We define a sequence of conformal metrics
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
by

\displaystyle h_{n}=e^{2\phi_{n}(u)}c_{1}

on
U

\displaystyle h_{n}=e^{2(n+1)}c_{1}

\displaystyle\mbox{on ${\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U$}~.

Note that
h_{n}
is a smooth metric on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
since, by definition of
\phi
,
\phi_{n}(u)
is equal to
n+1
in a neighborhood of the boundary of
U
. (It follows from the fact that
h
is complete on
U
together with Lemma
3.1
(for from Lemma
3.2
) that
u\to\infty
on
\partial U
.)

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume that
K\in[-1+\epsilon,-\epsilon]
with derivatives uniformly bounded (with respect to
h
), that
u
is bounded from below, and that
\|du\|_{h}
is bounded from above, on
U
.
There exists a constant
K_{max}>0
(depending on those bounds) such that the curvature
K_{n}
of
h_{n}
is:

•

equal to
K
on the set of points
x\in U
where
u(x)\leq n
,

•

in the interval
[-1+\epsilon,K_{max}]
on the set of points
x\in U
where
n\leq u(x)\leq n+2
,

•

equal to
e^{-2(n+1)}
on the set of points
x\in U
where
u(x)\geq n+2
and on the complement of
U
.

Moreover, the metrics
h_{n}
have bounded derivatives, for constants
C_{k}
which do not depend on
n
.

Proof.

In the region of
U
where
u(x)\leq n
,
h_{n}=h
, and therefore
K_{n}=K
, the curvature of
h
. On the region of
U
where
u(x)\geq n+2
, and on the complement of
U
,
h_{n}=e^{2(n+1)}c_{1}
, and it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
K_{n}=e^{-2(n+1)}
.

We now focus on the region where
n\leq u(x)\leq n+2
.
The conformal transformation of curvature (Lemma
2.1
) also shows that

K_{n}=e^{-2\phi_{n}(u)}(\Delta_{c_{1}}\phi_{n}(u))+1)~.

However

d(\phi_{n}(u))=\phi^{\prime}_{n}(u)du~,

so that

Dd(\phi_{n}\circ u)=\phi^{\prime}_{n}(u)Ddu+(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ u)du\otimes du~,

and therefore

\Delta_{c_{1}}(\phi_{n}\circ u)=(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u)\Delta_{c_{1}}u-(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ u)\|du\|^{2}_{c_{1}}~.

As a consequence, and since
\|du\|^{2}_{c_{1}}=e^{2u}\|du\|^{2}_{h}
:

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\phi_{n}\circ u}((\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u)\Delta_{c_{1}}u-(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ u)\|du\|^{2}_{c_{1}}+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u)e^{2(u-\phi_{n}\circ u)}e^{-2u}(\Delta_{c_{1}}u+1)+e^{-2\phi_{n}\circ u}(1-(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u))-e^{2(u-\phi_{n}\circ u)}(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ u)\|du\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u)e^{2(u-\phi_{n}\circ u)}K+e^{-2\phi_{n}\circ u}(1-(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u))-e^{2(u-\phi_{n}\circ u)}(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ u)\|du\|^{2}_{h}~.

By the hypothesis,
K\in[-1+\epsilon,-\epsilon]
, and by point (4) in the choice of
\phi
,
0\leq(\phi^{\prime}_{n}\circ u)e^{2(u-\phi_{n}\circ u)}\leq 1
, so the first term is in
[-1+\epsilon,0]
. By point (3) in the choice of
\phi
, the second term is non-negative, and it is uniformly bounded because by construction,
e^{-2\phi_{n}\circ u}
is bounded from above. Finally, the third term is also non-negative by point (3) in the choice of
\phi
and uniformly bounded by the fact that
u-\phi_{n}\circ u
is bounded from above and the uniform bound on
\|du\|_{h}
. The first part of the result follows.

For the second part, note that the metric
c_{1}
can be written as
c_{1}=e^{-2u}h
. It thus follows from Lemma
2.1
that

1=e^{2u}(-\Delta_{h}u+K)~,

so that

\Delta_{h}u=e^{-2u}+K~.

Elliptic regularity and a standard bootstrapping argument, together with the hypothesis that
\|du\|_{h}
is uniformly bounded, then shows that all derivatives of
u
are uniformly bounded with respect to
h
.

We can then deduce from those estimates on
u
that similar estimates also apply to
\phi_{n}(u)
, that is, its derivatives are uniformly bounded with respect to
h_{n}
. Indeed, this is clear in the region where
u\leq n
, and also in the region where
u\geq n+2
. In the region where
n\leq u\leq n+2
, it follows from the definition of
\phi_{n}
(and from the uniform bound on
\|du_{n}\|
) that the derivatives of
\phi_{n}(u)
are uniformly bounded with respect to
h
. Since
e^{-4}h\leq h_{n}\leq h
when
n\leq u\leq n+1
, the derivatives of
\phi_{n}(u)
are also uniformly bounded with respect to
h_{n}
. Lemma
2.1
then shows that the derivatives of
K_{n}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
h_{n}
.
∎

3.3.
A lower bound on the injectivity radius

In addition to the upper bound on the curvature, the next section will make an essential use of a lower bound on the injectivity radius of the metrics
h_{n}
, as stated in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

There exists
l_{0}>0
such that the injectivity radius of
h_{n}
is bounded from below by
l_{0}
.

We introduce additional notations for the proof, namely a decomposition of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
in three subsets:

•

U_{u\leq n}
is the set of points
x\in U
where
u(x)\leq n
,

•

U_{n<u\leq n+1}
is the set of points
x\in U
where
n<u(x)\leq n+1
,

•

U_{u\leq n+1}=U_{u\leq n}\cup U_{n<u\leq n+1}
,

•

U_{n+1<u}
is the union of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
with the set of points
x\in U
where
n+1<u(x)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.11
.

We consider a short geodesic
\gamma
of length
l
for the metric
h_{n}
, and will show that
l
cannot be smaller than
l_{0}
, for a value of
l_{0}
that will be determined.

The proof will consider two cases, with a similar argument used for a different background metric in each case. In each case we will use that any disk
\Omega
with
\partial\Omega=\gamma
must have total curvature
\pi
(by the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem) and thus, since
h_{n}
has curvature
K\leq K_{max}
, area bounded from below:
A_{h_{n}}(\Omega)\geq\pi/K_{max}
.

First case:
\gamma\cap U_{u\leq n}=\emptyset
.

In this case
u\geq n
on
\gamma
, and we compare
h_{n}
to the metric
e^{2n}c_{1}
. We have
h_{n}\geq e^{2n}c_{1}
on
\gamma
, so that
L_{c_{1}}(\gamma)\leq e^{-n}l
. However we also have
h_{n}\leq e^{2(n+1)}c_{1}
on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
and therefore, for each of the disks
\Omega
bounded by
\gamma
,
A_{c_{1}}(\Omega)\geq e^{-2(n+1)}\pi/K_{max}
. For
l
small enough, this contradicts the spherical isoperimetric inegality.

Second case:
\gamma\cap U_{u\leq n}\neq\emptyset
.

We first notice that there exists a constant
l_{1}>0
(depending on the constant
C
in Lemma
3.1
) such that, if
L_{h_{n}}(\gamma)\leq l_{1}
, then
L_{h}(\gamma)\leq 2l_{1}
and
u\leq n+2
on
\gamma
. Indeed,
\|du\|_{h}\leq C
, and since

h=e^{2u}c_{1}=e^{2u-2\phi_{n}(u)}h_{n}\leq e^{2u-2n}h_{n}~,

we have

\|du\|_{h_{n}}\leq e^{u-n}\|du\|_{h}\leq e^{u-n}C~.

It follows from this equation, and from the fact that
u\leq n
at a point of
\gamma
, that if
\gamma
is short enough, then
e^{u-n}\leq 2
on
\gamma
, and therefore
L_{h}(\gamma)\leq 2L_{h_{n}}(\gamma)\leq 2l
.

It then follows that
\gamma\subset U
, and therefore
\gamma
is contained in a connected component
U_{0}
of
U
. Since
U
is a disjoint union of quasidisks, this connected component is topologically a disk, and therefore
\gamma
bounds a topological disk
\Omega\subset U_{0}
. Since
h\geq h_{n}
on
U_{0}
,
A_{h}(\Omega)\geq A_{h_{n}}(\Omega)\geq\pi/K_{max}
. So for
l
small enough, we again have a contradiction with the isoperimetric inequality, this time for
(U_{0},h)
.
∎

4.1.

The first point of this section is to find a uniform bound on the principal curvatures on the boundaries of the domains

The proof uses a result of Labourie

Lab89
, Théorème D]
which we recall here for the reader’s convenience (restricting to the special case where the target space is
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
).

Theorem 4.2

(Labourie)
.

Let
f_{n}:S\to{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
be a sequence of immersions of a surface
S
such that the pullback
f_{n}^{*}(h)
of the hyperbolic metric
h
converges smoothly to a metric
g_{0}
.
If the integral of the mean curvature is uniformly bounded, then a subsequence of
f_{n}
converges smoothly to an isometric immersion

f_{\infty}
such that
f_{\infty}^{*}(h)=g_{0}
.

Note that Theorem
4.2
is local: no assumption of compactness of
S
or completeness of
f_{n}^{*}(h)
is needed.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We proceed by contradiction and assume that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all
n
, there exists a convex subset
\Omega_{n}
such that the induced metric on
\partial\Omega_{n}
has curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,1/\epsilon]
and injectivity radius bounded from below by
\epsilon
, while the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega_{n}
at a point
x_{n}
are not in
[1/n,n]
.

We apply Theorem
4.2
to a sequence of parameterizations
\rho_{n}:{\mathbb{D}}\to\partial\Omega_{n}
of the disk of center
x_{n}
and radius
\epsilon/2
, which are indeed topological disks by the asumption on the injectivity radius. Since the induced metrics on the
\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
have curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,1/\epsilon]
, there exists a subsequence such that the induced metrics converge to a limit
g_{0}
. Since the
\Omega_{n}
have bounded curvature, we can choose the
\rho_{n}
so that this convergence is
C^{\infty}
. Therefore, by Theorem
4.2
, after extracting another subsequence, there exists a sequence of isometries
(\psi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
such that

(1)

either the integral mean curvature on
\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
tends to infinity,

(2)

or the
\psi_{n}\circ\rho_{n}:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
converge smoothly to a smooth limit
\rho_{\infty}:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
.

Clearly the second hypothesis is excluded by the hypothesis on the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega_{n}
at
x_{n}
, so it is sufficient to exclude also the first case.

Let
\Omega_{n}^{1}
be the
1
-neighborhood of
\Omega_{n}
:

\Omega_{n}^{1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{3}~|~d(x,\Omega_{n})\leq 1\}~,

and let
\rho:\partial\Omega_{n}^{1}\to\partial\Omega_{n}
be the closest-point projection. Since
\Omega_{n}
is convex and smooth,
\Omega_{n}^{1}
is also convex, and
\rho
is a diffeomorphism. A well-known “tube” formula indicates that
\rho
contracts the area of
\partial\Omega_{n}^{1}
at a point
x\in\partial\Omega_{n}^{1}
by a factor
\cosh(1)^{2}+\cosh(1)\sinh(1)\mbox{tr}(B)+\sinh(1)^{2}\det(B)
, where
B
denotes the shape operator of
\Omega_{n}
at
\rho(x)
. As a consequence, the area of
\rho^{-1}(\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}}))
is equal to

A(\rho^{-1}(\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})))=\int_{\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})}\cosh(t)^{2}+\cosh(t)\sinh(t)\mbox{tr}(B)+\sinh(t)^{2}\det(B)da_{I}~.

Let
B(x_{n},2)
be the ball of center
x_{n}
and radius
2
. The projection from

(\partial B(x_{n},2))\cap({\mathbb{H}}^{3}\setminus\Omega^{1}_{n})

to
\partial\Omega^{1}_{n}
is contracting, and its image contains
\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
. As a consequence, the area of
\rho^{-1}\circ\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is at most equal to the area of
\partial B(x_{n},2)
, so that

\int_{\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})}\cosh(1)^{2}+\cosh(1)\sinh(1)\mbox{tr}(B)+\sinh(1)^{2}\det(B)da_{I}\leq 4\pi\sinh(2)^{2}~.

Since all three terms are non-negative, this simplies in particular that the integral of the mean curvature is bounded on
\rho_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, and the first alternative above is impossible. This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.2.
Curvature bounds in the almost-flat regions

In the proof of Theorem
1.7
it will be necessary to show that the conformal structure on the boundary at infinity of the convex set
\Omega_{\infty}
which is the limit of the
\Omega_{n}
is identified – under the limit map from
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
to
\overline{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
– with the conformal structure at infinity of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
. This will be based on the fact that the regions in the
\partial\Omega_{n}
which are the image of the complement of
U
are (asymptotically) umbilic, with principal curvatures going to
1
as
n\to\infty
. This section is focused on proving this result.

Specifically, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
k_{0}>0
, there exists
R>0
and
K_{0}>0
such that if
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is a convex domain and
x\in\partial\Omega
such that the injectivity radius of
(\partial\Omega,I)
at
x
is at least
R
, with curvature in
[-K_{0},K_{0}]
on the ball of center
x
and radius
R
, then the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
at
x
are in
[1-k_{0},1+k_{0}]
.

The proof uses the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
. Assume that
(\partial\Omega,I)
is flat, and that its injectivity radius at a point
x\in\partial\Omega
is infinite. Then
\Omega
is a horosphere.

Proof.

This follows directly from a result of Volkov and Vladimirova
[
VV71
]
who proved that an isometric immersion of the Euclidean plane in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is either a parameterization of a horosphere, or a multiple cover of a cylinder.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
k_{0}>0
such that no such
R
and
K_{0}
exist. Then for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}
there exists a convex subset
\Omega_{n}
and a point
x_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{n}
such that the injectivity radius of
\partial\Omega_{n}
at
x_{n}
is at least
n
, and the curvature of
\partial\Omega_{n}
on the ball of center
x_{n}
and radius
n
is in
[-1/n,1/n]
. However one of the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega_{n}
at
x_{n}
is not in
[1-k_{0},1+k_{0}]
.

For each
n
, let
\phi_{n}\in\rm{Isom}({\mathbb{H}}^{3})
be an isometry sending
x_{n}
to
x_{0}
(a fixed point). We can extract from
(\phi_{n}(\Omega_{n}))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence of convex subset
\Omega^{\prime}_{n}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
which converges in the Hausdorff topology on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
to a limit
\Omega_{\infty}
(see
[
NQS79
]
and references therein for topological properties of the space of convex subsets of a linear space).

Then
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{\infty}
by construction. Moreover
\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{n}\to\partial\Omega_{\infty}
, and the induced metric on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{n}
converges to the induced metric on
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
(see
[
BBI01
, Section 10.2]
). As a consequence,
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
is intrisically flat, with infinite injectivity radius since the injectivity radius of
\partial\Omega_{n}
at
x_{n}
is at least
n
. It follows from Proposition
4.4
that it is a horosphere. This contradicts the hypothesis that the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{n}
are not in
[1-k_{0},1+k_{0}]
at
x_{0}
.
∎

5.
Proofs of the main results

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.7
.

5.1.
Convergence of the Gauss maps

We consider the sequence of convex domains
\Omega_{n}
and conformal diffeomorphisms
\phi_{n}:{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\to\partial\Omega_{n}
described in Lemma
3.12
.

For each
n
, we denote by
G_{n}:\partial\Omega_{n}\to\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
the hyperbolic Gauss map of
\partial\Omega_{n}
, that is, the map sending
x\in\partial\Omega_{n}
to the endpoint at infinity of the geodesic ray starting from
x
directed by the exterior normal of
\Omega
at
x
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a constant
K_{G}>1
such that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}:{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\to\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is
K_{G}
-quasi-conformal.

Proof.

Let
x\in\partial\Omega_{n}
, for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We can identify
T_{x}\partial\Omega_{n}
to
T_{G_{n}(x)}\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
by the exponential map at
x
,
\exp_{x}:T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, using the natural identification of
T_{x}\partial\Omega_{n}
with the normal in
T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
of the unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{n}
at
x
. Under this identification, the differential of
G_{n}
at
x
is equal to
Id+B
, where
Id
is the identity, and
B
the shape operator of
\partial\Omega_{n}
.

Since the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega_{n}
are uniformly bounded, the differentials of the Gauss map
G_{n}
are uniformly bounded. Since the
\phi_{n}
are conformal, it follows that the maps
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
are uniformly quasi-conformal.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

After extracting a subsequence and composing with a sequence of Möbius transformations
u_{n}
, the sequence
(G_{n}\circ\phi_{n})_{n}
converges
C^{0}
to a
K_{G}
-quasi-conformal homeomorphism
\psi_{\infty}:{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\to\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
.

Proof.

This follows from the compactness of the space of
K
-quasi-conformal homeomorphisms from
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
to
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, see e.g.
[
Ahl66
]
.
∎

Note that the Möbius transformations appearing in this corollary are boundary values of hyperbolic isometries. If
u\in PSL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
is a hyperbolic isometry, and
\partial u
is its boundary action, considered as a Möbius transformation, then composing
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
on the left by a Möbius transformation
\partial u
is equivalent to composing
\phi_{n}
on the left by
u
and then composing with its Gauss map.

We will assume in the last section below that the
\phi_{n}
have already been composed on the left by a sequence of Möbius transformations, so that
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\to\psi_{\infty}
.

5.2.
Convergence of boundary data

Recall that we denote by
\rho:{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\to B(0,1)
the Poincaré model of hyperbolic space. We consider the sequence of maps
(\phi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
considered above.

Lemma 5.3
.

After extracting a subsequence,

(1)

the sequence of restrictions of the
\phi_{n}
to
U
converge
C^{\infty}
to an isometric embedding
\phi_{\infty}
of
(U,h)
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
,

(2)

the sequence
(\rho\circ\phi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to a map
\Phi_{\infty}:{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\to B(0,1)
which is conformal on
U
and on the interior of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
,

(3)

\phi_{\infty}(U)
is the boundary in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
of a convex subset
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
U_{0}
be a connected component of
U
. It follows from the arguments already used in Section
4.1
that there exists a sequence
(u^{0}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of hyperbolic isometries such that the sequence of restrictions of the
u^{0}_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
to
U_{0}
converges
C^{\infty}
(after extracting a subsequence) to a limit
\phi_{0,\infty}
. It follows that the Gauss maps of the
u^{0}_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
converge to the Gauss map of
\phi_{0,\infty}
on
U_{0}
, that is,

\partial u_{n}^{0}\circ(G_{n}\circ\phi_{n})\to G_{0,\infty}\circ\phi_{0,\infty}~,

where
G_{0,\infty}
denotes the Gauss map of
\phi_{0,\infty}
.

However we already know by Corollary
5.2
that the
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
converge to a limit
\psi_{\infty}
, so that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial u_{n}^{0}\circ\psi_{\infty}=G_{0,\infty}\circ\phi_{0,\infty}~.

As a consequence,
(u_{n}^{0})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to a hyperbolic isometry
u^{0}
. So, to obtain the convergence of
u_{n}^{0}\circ\phi_{n}\circ\phi_{0,\infty}
above, it is sufficient to take
u_{n}^{0}
constant and equal to
u^{0}
. This means that after replacing the
\phi_{n}
by
u^{0}\circ\phi_{n}
, the sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges
C^{\infty}
(without normalization by the sequence
(u^{0}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
). This proves the first point.

The second point is less obvious than it might appear. We already know that on
U
,
\phi_{n}\to\phi_{\infty}
, where
\phi_{\infty}
is an isometry from
(U,h)
to
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
and is therefore conformal. However the conformality of the limit map is less clear on
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
. This is because the Poincaré model of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
does not preserve convexity, so that the
\rho(\Omega)
are not necessarily convex, and a sequence of conformal embeddings might well converge to a limit which is not conformal. This could conceivably happen at parts of
\partial\Omega_{n}
converging to infinity in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
.

To avoid this issue for regions converging to
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, we note that for all
x\in{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
,
(\phi_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has the same limit in
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
as
(G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Let
x\in{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
be contained in the interior of the complement of
U
, then Lemma
4.3
shows that the principal curvatures of
\phi_{n}
at
x
converge to
1
as
n\to\infty
. It follows that the pull-back by
G_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
of the conformal metric at infinity is quasi-conformal to
h_{n}
in the neighborhood of
x
, with quasiconformal constant going to
1
as
n\to\infty
. So
\phi_{\infty}
is conformal on
U
and on the interior of
{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\setminus U
.

The third point follows from the construction, since
\phi_{\infty}(U)=\partial\Omega_{\infty}
, where
\Omega_{\infty}
is the Hausdorff limit (on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
) of the
\Omega_{n}
, which are convex subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
(U,h)
be a boundary data, with
U\subset{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
a disjoint union of
k_{0}
-quasidisks, and let
h
be a complete metric of curvature
K\in[-1+\epsilon,-\epsilon]
on
U
. We define a sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of approximating metrics following Definition
3.9
, and those metrics have curvature
K_{n}\in[-1+\epsilon,K_{max}]
by Lemma
3.10
. (Note that the constant
K_{max}
does not depend on the connected component of
U
since each is a
k_{0}
-quasidisk, and the curvature estimates on
h_{n}
are valid for all.)

The Alexandrov Theorem (Theorem
1.2
) therefore realizes each of the
h_{n}
as the induced metric on the boundary of a convex subset
\Omega_{n}\subset{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, as see in Section
3.4
.

Lemma
5.3
then shows that, after normalizing by a sequence of isometries and extracting a subsequence,
(\Omega_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to a convex subset
\Omega_{\infty}
realizing the boundary data
(U,h)
.
∎

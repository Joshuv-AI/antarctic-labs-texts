Abelian surfaces over finite fields containing no curves of genus 3 3 or less
Elena Berardini, Alejandro Giangreco Maidana, Stefano Marseglia

Abstract
. We study abelian surfaces defined over finite fields which do not contain any possibly singular curve of genus less than or equal to 3 3 . Firstly, we complete and expand the characterisation of isogeny classes of abelian surfaces with no curves of genus up to 2 2 initiated by the first author et al. in previous work. Secondly, we show that, for simple abelian surfaces, containing a curve of genus 3 3 is equivalent to admitting a polarisation of degree 4 4 . Thanks to this result, we can use existing algorithms to check which isomorphism classes in the isogeny classes containing no genus 2 2 curves have a polarisation of degree 4 4 . Thirdly, we characterise isogeny classes of abelian surfaces with no curves of genus ≤ 2 \leq 2 , containing no abelian surface with a polarisation of degree 4 4 . Finally, we describe the absolutely irreducible genus 3 3 curves lying on abelian surfaces containing no curves of genus less than or equal to 2 2 , and show that their number of rational points is far from the Serre–Weil bound.

Introduction

Studying the minimal genus of algebraic curves lying on an abelian variety is a classical question
[
1
,
8
,
4
]
.
In this paper, we focus our attention on abelian surfaces.
Over an algebraically closed field, every abelian surface is isogenous to a principally polarised one
[
22
, Cor. 1, p. 234]
, that is, to the Jacobian of a smooth genus 2 curve or to the product of two elliptic curves.
A well-known consequence of this fact is that every abelian surface over an algebraically closed field contains a (possibly singular) irreducible curve of geometric genus
2
or
1
; see also Lemma
1.1
below. On another note, one can also study when an abelian surface contains curves of a fixed genus. Over the complex numbers, this question has been treated in
[
5
]
for genus
3
curves and in
[
7
]
for hyperelliptic curves of genus
4
. Over non-algebraically closed fields, the situation is more complicated. For example, it is no longer true that any abelian surface is isogenous to a principally polarised one.

From now on, we direct our attention to the case of finite fields. Isogeny classes in which no abelian surface admits a principal polarisation are classified in
[
15
]
. Furthermore, even if an isogeny class is principally polarisable, it might not contain a Jacobian surface nor the product of two elliptic curves
[
16
]
. Indeed, by a theorem of Weil
[
16
, Thm. 1.3]
, we have one more option among principally polarisable abelian surfaces, that is, Weil restrictions of elliptic curves defined over a quadratic extension of the finite field. To sum up, studying the minimal genus of algebraic curves lying over an abelian surface defined over a finite field is a hard question, and it is in fact open in its full generality.

Besides the intrinsic theoretical interest, this question was raised in the context of coding theory. The first author and her co-authors studied in
[
2
]
the so-called algebraic geometry codes constructed from a rational divisor on an abelian surface defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. They showed that the minimum distance of such codes respects, under some hypotheses, a lower bound that is somewhat proportional to the minimum genus of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-irreducible curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
lying on the surface
[
2
, Thm. 3.3 and Rmk. 3.6]
. Since codes with large minimum distance are desirable, this led to the question on how to find abelian surfaces containing no curves of low genus.

Since

It follows that

(4)

\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(G)=\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(\alpha_{p}^{n})\cdot\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(G^{\prime})=p^{n}\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(G^{\prime}).

Let
M_{p}
be
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
with the trivial
R
-module structure and
M^{\prime}
be a finite
R
-module such that
\epsilon([G^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{C}})=[M^{\prime}]_{R}
.
By Theorem
4.3
, we have

[M]_{R}=n[M_{p}]_{R}\oplus[M^{\prime}]_{R}.

Hence

(5)

|M|=|M_{p}|^{n}\cdot|M^{\prime}|=p^{n}|M^{\prime}|.

Since, by construction of
M^{\prime}
, we have that
\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(G^{\prime})=|M^{\prime}|
, by combining Equations (
4
) and (
5
) it follows that
\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(G)=|M|
.
∎

Since
R
is a locally free
R^{+}
-module, the association
M\mapsto M\otimes_{R^{+}}R
induces a well defined homomorphism
t_{R/R^{+}}:G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R^{+}})\to G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
S
be an order in
K
such that
S=\overline{S}
and that
S
is locally free of rank
2
over
S^{+}=S\cap K^{+}
.
Then
Z(S)=t_{S/S^{+}}(G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}}))
.
Moreover, if
M\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S}
is such that
[M]_{S}\in Z(S)
then
[M]_{S}=t_{S/S^{+}}(P^{\prime})
for some effective
P^{\prime}\in G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}})
.

Proof.

Let
P^{\prime}
be an element of
G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}})
.
Write

P^{\prime}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}n_{\mathfrak{n}}[S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}]_{S^{+}},

where
\mathfrak{n}
runs over the maximal ideals of
S^{+}
.
Then

t_{S/S^{+}}(P^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}n_{\mathfrak{n}}[S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}\otimes_{S^{+}}S]_{S}.

We have a natural
S
-linear isomorphism
S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}\otimes_{S^{+}}S\simeq S/\mathfrak{n}S
.
So

N_{S/S^{+}}([S/\mathfrak{n}S]_{S})=2[S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}]_{S^{+}}

because
S
is locally free of rank
2
over
S^{+}
.
Hence
N_{S/S^{+}}([S/\mathfrak{n}S]_{S})\in 2G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}})
.
Since
S=\overline{S}
and
\mathfrak{n}S=\overline{\mathfrak{n}S}
, by Lemma
4.1
, we get that
[S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}\otimes_{S^{+}}S]_{S}=[S/\mathfrak{n}S]_{S}
is symmetric.
This concludes the proof that

t_{S/S^{+}}(G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}}))\subseteq Z(S).

Now we prove the reverse inclusion.
Let
P
be a symmetric element of
G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S})
.
Write

P=\sum_{\mathfrak{N}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}[S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S},

where the sum is taken over all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{N}
of
S
.
Since
P
is symmetric we must have
n_{\mathfrak{N}}=n_{\overline{\mathfrak{N}}}
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
S
.
Write

P=\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\neq\overline{\mathfrak{N}}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}\oplus S/\overline{\mathfrak{N}}]_{S}\right)+\sum_{\mathfrak{N}=\overline{\mathfrak{N}}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S}\right).

Fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
S
and set
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{N}\cap S^{+}=\overline{\mathfrak{N}}\cap S^{+}
.

Assume first that
\mathfrak{N}\neq\overline{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Then
\mathfrak{n}S\subseteq\mathfrak{N}\overline{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Hence we have a surjective map

S/\mathfrak{n}S\twoheadrightarrow S/\mathfrak{N}\overline{\mathfrak{N}}\simeq S/\mathfrak{N}\times S/\overline{\mathfrak{N}}.

Since
S/\mathfrak{n}S
is a
2
-dimensional vector space over
S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}
, we get that
S/\mathfrak{N}\simeq S/\overline{\mathfrak{N}}\simeq S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}
.
This means that
\mathfrak{n}S=\mathfrak{N}\overline{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Therefore

[S/\mathfrak{N}\oplus S/\overline{\mathfrak{N}}]_{S}=[S/\mathfrak{N}\overline{\mathfrak{N}}]_{S}=[S/\mathfrak{n}S]_{S}=t_{S/S^{+}}([S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}]_{S^{+}}).

Now consider the case
\mathfrak{N}=\overline{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Observe that the residue field extension
S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}\to S/\mathfrak{N}
is of degree
1
or
2
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{2}
) be the set of maximal ideals
\mathfrak{N}
of
S
such that
\mathfrak{N}=\overline{\mathfrak{N}}
and the degree is
1
(resp.
2
).
If
\mathfrak{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
then
\mathfrak{N}=\mathfrak{n}S
, since
S/\mathfrak{n}S
is has dimension
2
over
S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}
.
Hence

[S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S}=[S/\mathfrak{n}S]_{S}=t_{S/S^{+}}([S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}]_{S^{+}}).

By the first part of the proof, we get that
P=\overline{P}\in Z(S)
if and only if

\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S}\right)=P-\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\neq\overline{\mathfrak{N}}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}\oplus S/\overline{\mathfrak{N}}]_{S}\right)-\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{2}}n_{\mathfrak{N}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S}\right)

is in
Z(S)
as well.
This happens precisely when
n_{\mathfrak{N}}N_{S/S^{+}}\left([S/\mathfrak{N}]_{S}\right)
is in
2G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}})
for every
\mathfrak{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
, that is, when each
n_{\mathfrak{N}}
is even, since
S/\mathfrak{N}\simeq S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}
is a simple
S^{+}
-module.
Since, for
\mathfrak{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
, we have
S
-linear isomorphisms

t_{S/S^{+}}([S^{+}/\mathfrak{n}]_{S^{+}})\simeq S/\mathfrak{n}S\simeq(S/\mathfrak{N})^{2},

we conclude the proof that
Z(S)\subseteq t_{S/S^{+}}(G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{S^{+}}))
by additivity of
t_{S/S^{+}}
.
∎

Recall that the order
R=\mathbb{Z}[\pi,q/\pi]
is locally free of rank
2
over
R^{+}=\mathbb{Z}[\pi+q/\pi]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\ell
be a rational prime.
The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There exists a finite
R
-module
M
of order
|M|=\ell^{2}
such that the class of
M
in
G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R})
is of the form
t_{R/R^{+}}(P^{\prime})
for some effective
P^{\prime}
in
G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R^{+}})
.

(2)

There exists a finite
R
-module
M
of order
|M|=\ell^{2}
such that
[M]_{R}
is in
Z(R)
.

(3)

f^{+}(t)\bmod\ell
has a linear factor.

If any of the equivalent statements hold then
[M]_{R}=t_{R/R^{+}}([R^{+}/\mathfrak{l}]_{R^{+}})
where
\mathfrak{l}
is the unique maximal ideal of
R^{+}
above
\ell
corresponding to any linear factor of
f^{+}(t)\bmod\ell
.

Proof.

The equivalence of
1
and
2
is an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.6
.

Let
P^{\prime}\in G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R^{+}})
be effective and non-zero.
Then there are positive integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
and maximal
R^{+}
-ideals
\mathfrak{n}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{n}_{r}
such that

P^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{i}].

Hence

t_{R/R^{+}}(P^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{i}\otimes_{R^{+}}R].

Since
R
is locally free of rank
2
over
R^{+}
, we get that

|R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{i}\otimes_{R^{+}}R|=|R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{i}|^{2}.

If
M
is a finite
R
-module whose image in
G(\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{R})
is
t_{R/R^{+}}(P^{\prime})
for
P^{\prime}
as above then

|M|=\prod_{i=1}^{r}|R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{i}|^{2n_{i}}.

We now show that
1
implies
3
.
So, assume also that
M
as above satisfies
|M|=\ell^{2}
for a rational prime
\ell
.
Then
r=1
,
n_{1}=1
and
R^{+}/\mathfrak{n}_{1}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
.
This implies that
f^{+}(t)\bmod\ell
has a linear factor (cf.
[
26
, Thm. 8.2]
).

Finally, assume that
3
holds.
Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the corresponding maximal ideal of
R^{+}
.
Then we see that
R^{+}/\mathfrak{l}\otimes_{R^{+}}R
is an
R
-module of order
\ell^{2}
, as in
1
.
∎

The following result completely describes
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
for the maximal order
\mathcal{O}
of
K
.

Proposition 4.8
.

If
K/K^{+}
is ramified at a finite prime then
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})=0
.
Otherwise, the Artin map induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})\simeq\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/K^{+})
.

Proof.

This is part of
[
14
, Prop. 6.2]
.
∎

Define

H(R)=\frac{Z(R)}{B(R)}.

where

.

We have an exact sequence

H(\mathcal{O})\overset{(N,-\chi)}{\longrightarrow}H(R)\oplus\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})\overset{\psi\oplus i^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{B}(R)\to 0.

Proof.

This is a special case of

, Prop. 6.4]
.

.

5.
Polarisations of degree
4
on abelian surfaces

In Section
2
, we showed that an abelian surface with Weil polynomial
f\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
contains an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-irreducible curve of arithmetic genus
3
if and only if it admits a polarisation with kernel of order
4
.
We now apply the results developed in Section
4
to characterise isogeny classes defined by a polynomial in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
not containing abelian surfaces with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-irreducible curve of genus
3
lying on.

In this section, we assume that
f(t)\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
.
As before, we denote the corresponding isogeny class by
\mathcal{C}
and set
K=\mathbb{Q}[x]/f(t)=\mathbb{Q}(\pi)
and
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\pi+q/\pi)
.
We fix also
R=\mathbb{Z}[\pi,q/\pi]\subset K
and
R^{+}=\mathbb{Z}[\pi+q/\pi]\subset K
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f(t)\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}
.
Then
2
is inert in
K^{+}
if and only if there is no attainable
R
-module of order
4
.

Proof.

If
f^{+}(t)\bmod 2
is irreducible then
2
is inert in
K^{+}
and there is no attainable
R
-module of order
4
by Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
4.10
.
So, we assume that
f^{+}(t)\bmod 2
has a linear factor for the rest of the proof.
Hence, again by Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
4.10
, if there is an attainable
R
-module
M
of order
4
then we must have

[M]_{R}=[R^{+}/\mathfrak{l}\otimes_{R^{+}}R]_{R}=[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R},

where
\mathfrak{l}
is a maximal ideal of
R^{+}
such that
R^{+}/\mathfrak{l}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
By Lemma
3.3
,
K/K^{+}
is unramified.
So, the Artin map induces an isomorphism
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/K^{+})\simeq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
by Proposition
4.8
.
Let
\alpha
be the non-zero element of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
, which corresponds to the Artin symbol of any maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}^{+}
that stays inert in
K
.
By Theorem
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
,
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is non-zero and in
i^{*}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O}))
.
Hence
I_{\mathcal{C}}=i^{*}(\alpha)
.
Let
z_{\mathfrak{l}}
be the image of
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
in
\mathcal{B}(R)
.
Note that

z_{\mathfrak{l}}=(\Psi\oplus i^{*})((x_{\mathfrak{l}},0)),

where
x_{\mathfrak{l}}
is the image of
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
in
H(R)
.
We obtain that
z_{\mathfrak{l}}-I_{\mathcal{C}}=(\Psi\oplus i^{*})((x_{\mathfrak{l}},\alpha))
.
Hence there is an
R
-module
M
of order
4
which is attainable if and only if

(x_{\mathfrak{l}},\alpha)\in\ker((\Psi\oplus i^{*}))=(N,-\chi)(H(\mathcal{O})),

where the equality holds by Proposition
4.9
.

Assume that
2
is inert in
K^{+}
and write
\mathfrak{m}=2\mathcal{O}^{+}
.
By Proposition
3.5
, we see that
\mathfrak{m}
splits in
K
, say
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}=\mathfrak{M}\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
.
A preimage of
x_{\mathfrak{l}}
in
H(\mathcal{O})
via
N:H(\mathcal{O})\to H(R)
must be an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear combination of the images
y_{\mathfrak{M}}
and
y_{\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
of
\mathfrak{M}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
in
H(\mathcal{O})
.
Since
\mathfrak{m}
is split in
K
, then the Artin symbols of
\mathfrak{M}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
are trivial in
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/K^{+})\simeq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
.
Hence any
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear combination of the images
y_{\mathfrak{M}}
and
y_{\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
won’t be mapped by
\chi:H(\mathcal{O})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
to
\alpha
.
This means that
(x_{\mathfrak{l}},\alpha)
is not in
(N,-\chi)(H(\mathcal{O}))
.
Hence, there is no
R
-module
M
of order
4
which is attainable.

Assume now that
2
is not inert in
K^{+}
.
Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
containing
\mathfrak{l}R
.
Denote the image of
[\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}]_{\mathcal{O}}
in
H(\mathcal{O})
by
y_{\mathfrak{M}}
.
By Proposition
3.5
, we have that
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}
for a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathcal{O}^{+}
.
This implies that
\chi(y_{\mathfrak{M}})=\alpha
.
So, to prove that
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is attainable, we are left to show that
N(y_{\mathfrak{M}})=x_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
We first consider the case that
\mathfrak{l}R
is a maximal ideal of
R
.
Note that
\mathfrak{l}R=\mathfrak{M}\cap R
and we have an isomorphism
R/\mathfrak{l}R\simeq\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}
.
Hence
N(y_{\mathfrak{M}})=x_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
Therefore,
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is attainable.
Now we consider the case when
\mathfrak{l}R
is not a maximal ideal of
R
.
Set
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{M}\cap R
.
The natural surjective map
R/\mathfrak{l}R\twoheadrightarrow R/\mathfrak{L}
is not an isomorphism.
Since
\mathfrak{L}=\overline{\mathfrak{L}}
and
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}\in Z(R)
we get that

[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}=2[R/\mathfrak{L}]_{R}.

Moreover, also
[\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}]_{R}=2[R/\mathfrak{L}]_{R}
.
Hence, again,
N(y_{\mathfrak{M}})=x_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
Therefore, as before,
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is attainable.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
f(t)\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
.
Write
2\mathcal{O}^{+}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and let
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{m}\cap R^{+}
be the unique maximal ideal of
R^{+}
above
2
.
Then there is an
R
-module
M
of order
4
which is attainable
if and only if
\mathfrak{l}R
is not a maximal ideal of
R
.

Proof.

Observe that
f(t)\bmod 2
is a square of a linear factor.
Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the corresponding maximal ideal of
R^{+}
.
By Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
4.10
, if there is an attainable
R
-module
M
of order
4
then we must have

[M]_{R}=[R^{+}/\mathfrak{l}\otimes_{R^{+}}R]_{R}=[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}.

Since
f(t)\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
, the obstruction element
I_{\mathcal{C}}
is trivial by Theorem
4.10
.
Hence, there is an
R
-module
M
of order
4
which is attainable if and only if the image
z_{\mathfrak{l}}
of
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
in
\mathcal{B}(R)
is trivial.
Note that

z_{\mathfrak{l}}=(\Psi\oplus i^{*})((x_{\mathfrak{l}},0)),

where
x_{\mathfrak{l}}
is the image of
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
in
H(R)
.
Also, the
R
-module
R/\mathfrak{l}R
has length at most
2
and that it has length
1
if and only if
\mathfrak{l}R
is a maximal ideal of
R
.

If the length of
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is
2
then
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}=[R/\mathfrak{n}]_{R}+[R/\overline{\mathfrak{n}}]_{R}
for a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
R
, with possibly
\mathfrak{n}=\overline{\mathfrak{n}}
, and so, by Lemma
4.1
,
[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
is in
B(R)
.
Hence,
x_{\mathfrak{l}}
is trivial in
H(R)
and so
z_{\mathfrak{l}}=I_{\mathcal{C}}
in
\mathcal{B}(R)
.
This means that
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is an attainable
R
-module of order
4
.

To conclude the proof, we need to show that if the length of
R/\mathfrak{l}R
is
1
, that is,
\mathfrak{l}R
is a maximal
R
-ideal, then
(x_{\mathfrak{l}},0)
is not in

\ker((\Psi\oplus i^{*}))=(N,-\chi)(H(\mathcal{O})),

where the equality holds by Proposition
4.9
.
So, assume that
\mathfrak{l}R
is a maximal ideal of
R
.
Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
above
\mathfrak{l}R
.
Since we have an inclusion of residue field
R/\mathfrak{l}R\to\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}
and
2
is not inert in
\mathcal{O}

by Proposition
3.5
, then

R/\mathfrak{l}R\simeq\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{4}.

This implies that
[\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}]_{R}=[R/\mathfrak{l}R]_{R}
.
If we denote the image of
[\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}]_{\mathcal{O}}
in
H(\mathcal{O})
by
y_{\mathfrak{M}}
, we get
N(y_{\mathfrak{M}})=x_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
By Proposition
3.5
, since
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{4}
, we get that
\mathfrak{m}
is inert in
K
.
This means that the Artin symbol
\left(\frac{K/K^{+}}{\mathfrak{m}}\right)
is a generator of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K/K^{+})
.
We now claim that
K/K^{+}
is unramified.
If not, then by Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we would get that there is unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathcal{O}^{+}
above
2
which ramifies in
\mathcal{O}
, say
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}=\mathfrak{M}^{2}
.
For such
\mathfrak{M}
we would have
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{M}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Since we know that every maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
above
\mathfrak{l}
has residue field isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, we deduce that
K/K^{+}
is unramified.
Then, by Proposition
4.8
, we get that
\chi(y_{\mathfrak{M}})\neq 0
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
.
So
(x_{\mathfrak{l}},0)
is not in the image of
(N,-\chi)
and, therefore, there is no
R
-module of order
4
which is attainable.
∎

In Theorem
5.7
, we wrap-up the discussion above.
Moreover, in certain cases with

ordinary, we show that if there is an abelian variety with polarisation with kernel of order

To do so, we use results from
[

]
.
The construction of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
in
[

]
is slightly different form the one in
[

]
that we have presented above, but certainly equivalent in view of Proposition
4.8
and
[

.

Definition 5.3
.

A finite
R
-module
M
is
strongly attainable

if there exists an abelian variety

in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(B)
is the maximal order of

with a polarisation
\varphi:B\to B^{\vee}
such that

.

Clearly strongly attainable implies attainable, while the contrary does not hold in general.
A characterisation of strongly attainable
R
-modules is given in
[
13
, Prop. 5.7]
, repeated below as Proposition

6.
Computing isomorphism classes admitting a polarisation of degree
4

In
[
19
]
, it is described how to compute the isomorphism classes of abelian varieties over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
belonging to an ordinary isogeny class
\mathcal{C}
determined by an irreducible Weil polynomial

.
We summarise here the results that are relevant for us; see
[
19
, Cor. 4.4, Thm. 5.4]

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[t]/(f(t))=\mathbb{Q}[\pi]
where
\pi
denotes the class of

in
K
and set

.
There is an equivalence between the category of abelian varieties in
\mathcal{C}
(with
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-morphisms) and the category of fractional

-ideals in
K
(with
R
-linear morphisms).
Hence, every overorder

of

occurs as the endomorphism ring of an abelian variety in
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover, the functor inducing the equivalence is compatible with duality and allows to describe polarisations as

-linear morphisms.
For example, if

then
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(A^{\vee})=\overline{S}
.
In
[
19
, Sec. 6]
, the equivalence is used to produce an algorithm to compute the abelian varieties in

together with their polarisations (of a fixed degree) up to polarised isomorphism.
We will use this algorithm in the next examples.

Remark 6.1
.

An analogous description of polarisations exists also for simple almost-ordinary abelian varieties over any finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of odd characteristic; see
[
23
]
.
This result does not apply to our case since none of the polynomials in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}
is almost-ordinary by Lemma
1.12
.
A similar description also exists for abelian varieties over prime fields whose Weil polynomial does not have repeated complex roots, but only for polarisations of degree

; see
[
6
]
.

Example 6.2
.

Consider the isogeny class of abelian surfaces over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with label

determined by the Weil polynomial
f(t)=t^{4}-t^{2}+4
.
According to the LMFDB, the class contains a Jacobian.
Moreover, one computes that the order

has

,

and

, where

is the maximal order of the number field

.
One observe that

Hence, each abelian surface

with endomorphism ring

cannot be isomorphic to its dual, which has endomorphism ring

-irreducible curve of arithmetic genus

In Theorem

7.
Genus
3
curves lying on abelian surfaces

In this final section, we switch our attention from abelian surfaces to curves, and gather information on absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3
curves lying on simple abelian surfaces. The following lemma characterises such curves as bielliptic.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
q
be the power of an odd prime. Let
C
be an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3
curve lying on a simple abelian surface
A
, both defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then there exists an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, with
n\leq 4
, such that
C
is a double
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-cover of
E
.

Proof.

Here we mainly follow
[
5
, Sec. 1.2]
, with some minor modifications since we are working over finite fields instead of over
\mathbb{C}
.
By
Theorem

2.2
, the curve
C
induces a polarisation
\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}:A\to A^{\vee}
of degree
4
, where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(C)
is the ample line bundle defined by
C
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the base locus of
\mathcal{L}
, that is,
\mathcal{B}=\{P\in A\,\mid\,s(P)=0\text{ for any section }s\in H^{0}(A,\mathcal{L})\}

[
10
, II, Lem. 7.8]
. Fix some
P_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
. Since
\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}=\{P\in A\,\mid\,t_{P}^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}\}

[
21
, Ch. I, Sec. 8]
we have, for any and
P\in\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
,
s(P_{0}+P)=t_{P}^{*}s(P_{0})=s(P_{0})=0
. Thus
P_{0}+\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. Since we are in characteristic different from
2
,
\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
has order equal to
4
, the degree of
\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{B}
contains at most
C.C=4
points. Hence,
P_{0}+\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\mathcal{B}
coincide as sets of geometric points. Consider now the involution
\iota:A\to A
given by
P\mapsto-P+2P_{0}
. Since the points in
\ker\lambda_{\mathcal{L}}
are
2
-torsion points, the points in
\mathcal{B}
are fixed points of
\iota
. Furthermore, we have
\iota^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}
, and in particular
\iota C=C

[
5
, Prop. 1.6]
. Then one can lift
\iota
to
C
as an involution, and since
C
is smooth,
\iota
has exactly four fixed points on
C
, namely the points in
\mathcal{B}

[
5
, 1.7]
. Then, by the genus formula, the quotient
E=C/\iota
is an elliptic curve, that is,
C
is a double cover of
E
. Note that
\iota
is not necessarily an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational map: this will depend on whether
\mathcal{B}
contains a rational point or not, that is, whether one can take
P_{0}
to be rational. However, the fact that
\mathcal{B}
is rational as a set implies that it is a (necessarily disjoint) union of orbits under the action of the Frobenius endomorphism. A point is of degree
n
if the size of its orbit is
n
. As the size of
\mathcal{B}
is
4
, the degree of its points is at most
4
. Hence the point
P_{0}
defining the involution
\iota
will have degree at most
4
. So
\iota
and
E
are defined over an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree at most
4
.
∎

For the rest of the section, we fix an abelian surface
A
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with Weil polynomial in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}\sqcup\{(t^{2}-2)^{2},(t^{2}-3)^{2}\}
, that is, which is simple and not isogenous to a Jacobian, or equivalently, not containing absolutely irreducible curves of geometric genus

,

or

by Theorem

. We suppose that there is an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3

defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that lies on

, and we want to study that curve.

We recall that absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3
curves are either hyperelliptic curves or plane quartics. The next lemma shows that, under some conditions, hyperelliptic genus
3
curves cannot lie on our abelian surfaces.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
q
be the power of an odd prime. Let
C
be an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3
curve lying on an abelian surface

defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which is simple and not isogenous to a Jacobian. Suppose further that the elliptic curve doubly covered by
C
from Lemma

is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
C
is hyperelliptic. We mainly follow the reasoning in the introduction of
[
24
]
. By Lemma

and our hypothesis,

is also bielliptic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, it is of the form

, with

. Here

is a polynomial of degree
4
. In this case, the double cover is given by the involution
(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)
and the elliptic curve is given by

. Furthermore, quotienting

by the hyperelliptic involution

gives the genus

. Finally, we have

. On the other hand, as

lies on

, we know by
[

, Prop. 2]

that

is an isogeny factor of

, that is, there exists an elliptic curve

such that
\mathop{\rm Jac}\nolimits(C)\sim A\times E^{\prime}
. Since

is simple we necessarily have

, and hence
\mathop{\rm Jac}\nolimits(F)\sim A
. The Jacobian of

can be simple or split in the product of two elliptic curves. In both cases, this leads to a contradiction.
∎

Hence, we are left with the case in which

is a bielliptic plane quartic. In this case, using results from
[

]
, we get the following result.

of degree

Proof.

By Lemma

we know that
C
cannot be hyperelliptic. Applying Lemma
7.1
and using the hypothesis of the proposition,
C
is the double cover of an elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, we can assume that the involution giving the double cover is
(x:y:z)\mapsto(x:-y:z)
, and therefore that
C
can be written in the form
y^{4}-h(x,z)y^{2}+r(x,z)=0
, where
h
is a homogenous degree
2
polynomial, and
r
is homogenous of degree
4
. Let us suppose that the polynomial
r
has two quadratic factor
f
and
g
and that the polynomial
h(x,z)
is not a linear combination of
f
and
g
. Then, by
[
24
, Thm. 1.1]
we can explicitly construct an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
2
curve
F
such that
\mathop{\rm Jac}\nolimits(C)\sim\mathop{\rm Jac}\nolimits(F)\times E
. Then, the curve
C
cannot lie on
A
, since otherwise
A
would be isogenous to
\mathop{\rm Jac}\nolimits(F)
by Lemma
7.1
. Finally, one of the two conditions in the proposition must hold.
∎

Finally, we present bounds on the number of rational points that an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
3
curve lying on an abelian surface with Weil polynomial in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{npp}}\sqcup\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\mathrm{Wres}}\sqcup\{(t^{2}-2)^{2},(t^{2}-3)^{2}\}
can have. To start with, we recall and extend a result from
[
9
]
on the number of rational points on curves over abelian surfaces.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian surface defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with trace
-a
, that is, with Weil polynomial
t^{4}+at^{3}+bt^{2}+qat+q^{2}
. Let
C
be an absolutely irreducible curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, of arithmetic genus
p_{a}(C)=p_{a}
, lying on
A
. Then

|\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})-(q+1+a)|\leq|p_{a}-2|\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Proof.

The upper bound on
\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is
[
9
, Thm. 4]
.
The proof can be adapted to obtain also the lower bound on
\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In what follows, we borrow the notation from the proof of
[
9
, Thm. 4]
.

If
p_{a}=1
, then we know that the curve
C
is elliptic, of trace say
-e
. Write
a=e+x_{2}
for some integer
-\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor\leq x_{2}\leq\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor
. Hence,

\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1+e=q+1+a-x_{2}\geq q+1+a-\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Suppose now
p_{a}\geq 2
.
By
[
3
, Prop. 2.3]
, we know that
\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})-\#\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\geq g-p_{a}
, where
\tilde{C}
is the normalisation of
C
and
g
is its genus. Following the reasoning in the proof of
[
9
, Thm. 4, Eq. (5)]
, there exist real numbers
x_{3},\dots,x_{g}
such that

\#\tilde{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1+a+\sum_{i=3}^{g}x_{i}\,\text{ and }\,\sum_{i=3}^{g}x_{i}\geq-(g-2)\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Using that
g-p_{a}\leq 0
, we get the following series of inequalities:

\displaystyle\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})

\displaystyle\geq q+1+a+\sum_{i=3}^{g}x_{i}+g-p_{a}

\displaystyle\geq q+1+a-(g-2)\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+g-p_{a}

\displaystyle\geq q+1+a-(g-2)\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor+(g-p_{a})\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor

\displaystyle=q+1+a-(p_{a}-2)\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor,

and the statement follows.
∎

Note that in
[
9
, Thm. 4]
the author needs the hypothesis
a\geq-q
, which is however only used to prove the result for curves which are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-irreducible but not absolutely irreducible. For such curves, the number of rational points cannot exceed
p_{a}-1
, but we cannot prove a lower bound different from the trivial one.

From Proposition
7.4
, we deduce the following facts. First, recall that a Weil restriction has always zero trace. Thus, if
C
is an absolutely irreducible curve of arithmetic genus
3
lying on a Weil restriction, then

(6)

q+1-\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor\leq\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq q+1+\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Indeed, in this case,
C
has the same number of rational points of the elliptic curve of which it is the double cover.

Secondly, we know that the trace of an abelian surface which does not admit a principal polarisation respects
a^{2}=q-b
. Then, if
C
is an absolutely irreducible curve of arithmetic genus
3
on such a surface, we have

q+1-\sqrt{q-b}-\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor\leq\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq q+1+\sqrt{q-b}+\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

A sloppy estimation, using that
|b|\leq 2q
and hence
\sqrt{q-b}\leq\sqrt{3q}\leq 2\sqrt{q}
, gives us

(7)

q+1-2\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor\leq\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq q+1+2\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

In particular, we see that an absolutely irreducible curve of genus
3
lying on a simple abelian surface not isogenous to the Jacobian of an absolutely irreducible smooth genus
2
curve is far from reaching the Serre–Weil bound which states
|\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q})-(q+1)|\leq 3\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor
.

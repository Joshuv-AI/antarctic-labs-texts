Spectral analysis and best decay rate of the wave propagator on the tadpole graph
Kaïs Ammari, Rachid Assel, Mouez Dimassi

Abstract
. We consider the damped wave semigroup on the tadpole graph ℛ {\mathcal{R}} . We first give a meticulous spectral analysis, followed by a judicious decomposition of the resolvent’s kernel. As a consequence, and by showing that the generalized eigenfunctions form a Riesz basis of some subspace of the energy space ℋ \mathcal{H} , we establish the exponential decay of the corresponding energy, with the optimal decay rate dictated by the spectral abscissa of the relevant operator.

Introduction

Over the past few years, there has been a burgeoning interest in various physical models encompassing multi-link flexible structures. These structures comprise either finitely or infinitely many interconnected flexible elements, such as strings, beams, plates, and shells. A wealth of literature has emerged on this subject, underscoring its significance. Notable references include

Tadpole graph

A tadpole graph is a term commonly used in mathematics and graph theory. It refers to a specific type of graph that resembles the shape of a tadpole, hence the name. In a tadpole graph, you have a main cycle (the body of the tadpole) with additional nodes (the tail) attached to it. A tadpole graph is a quantum graph with a simple geometry, which has been extensively researched in the past decade (see
[
1
]
and references therein) and has numerous practical applications. Dispersive effects for the free Schrödinger equation on the tadpole

The damped wave equation was intensively studied in the case of compact graph, where mostly controllability, observability and stabilization of the problem are studied, see e.g.
[

9
]

13

In the present study, we consider the damped wave equation on a non-compact tadpole graph (with Kirchhoff condition at the single vertex incorporating a damping
term
-\alpha u_{t}
see (
2.1

{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
, and the asymptotic behavior of
e^{t{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}}
as

For

\alpha=0

\alpha\in]1,3[\cup]3,+\infty[
a discrete spectrum arises in the left half complex plan. Second, we explore an application of our analysis by investigating the best decay rate of the corresponding stabilization problem.

3.2 Proposition
.

The operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
with domain
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

For all
U=(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})

(3.4)

\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}U,U\rangle=-2i\Im\sum_{k=1}^{2}\langle u^{\prime}_{k},v^{\prime}_{k}\rangle-\alpha|v_{1}(0)|^{2}.

(2)

({\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}))

(3)

{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}^{*})={\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}}_{-\alpha}),\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{*}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-I\\

\langle{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{\alpha}U,U\rangle=2i\Im\sum_{k=1}^{2}\langle u^{\prime}_{k},v^{\prime}_{k}\rangle-\alpha|v_{1}(0)|^{2}.

Proof.

For

\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}U,U\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Big\{\langle v^{\prime}_{k},u_{k}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle u_{k}^{\prime\prime},v_{k}\rangle\Big\}=-2i\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Im\langle u^{\prime}_{k},v^{\prime}_{k}\rangle-\Big(u^{\prime}_{2}(0)+u^{\prime}_{1}(0)-u^{\prime}_{2}(L)\Big)v_{1}(0),

which yields (
3.4
) by (
Aα

Let

{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})

(resp.

(resp.
(f,g)
) in

Since

Applying (

Letting

(3.6)

\langle(f,g),(x,y)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Big(\langle f_{k}^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{k}\rangle+\langle g_{k},y_{k}\rangle\Big)=-2i\Im\sum_{k=1}^{2}\langle x^{\prime}_{k},y^{\prime}_{k}\rangle-\alpha|f(0)|^{2}.

Combining this with the fact that

Now we prove (3).
Let

\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Big\{\langle v^{\prime}_{k},\omega_{k}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle u_{k}^{(2)},\Omega_{k}\rangle\Big\}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Big\{\langle u^{\prime}_{k},f_{k}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle v_{k},g_{k}\rangle\Big\},

(3.13)

By definition of

\langle{\mathbb{G}}_{\alpha}(z)w,\phi\rangle=-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\langle w^{\prime}_{k},\phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(R_{k})}-\alpha z{w_{1}(0)}\overline{\phi_{1}(0)}-z^{2}\langle w,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R})}

=\langle h+\nu\delta,\phi\rangle=\langle h,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R})}+\nu\overline{\phi_{1}(0)}.

Applying the above equality to

with

Hence that

4.
Riesz basis of the stabilization subspace

In this section, it is proved that the generalized eigenfunctions of the dissipative operator
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
associated to the eigenvalues in
\Sigma_{p,\alpha},\alpha\in]0,1[\cup]3,+\infty[,
form a Riesz basis of the subspace of
\mathcal{H}
which they span (denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
).
To this end, we recall that a sequence
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Riesz basis in a Hilbert space
V
if there exist a Hilbert space
\tilde{V},
an orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\tilde{V}
and an isomorphism
\Theta:\tilde{V}\longrightarrow V
such that
\Theta e_{n}=\Psi_{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.
Our strategy is to prove that the Gram matrix
(\langle\Psi_{n},\Psi_{m}\rangle)_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded bijective operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).
This is equivalent to prove the existence of two positive constants
A
and
B
such that

A\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{n}c_{n}\Psi_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq B\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{2}

for every sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

We start by computing the normalized eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
z_{n}(\alpha).

We assume throughout this section that
\alpha\in]1,3[.
The case when
\alpha\in]3,+\infty[
can be studied by the same approach.

So let
n
and
\alpha\in]1,3[
be fixed. In order to simplify the writings, we put
\omega_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{L}\ln\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{3-\alpha}\right)
and
\nu=\frac{2\pi}{L}.
By this doing we obtain
z_{n}(\alpha)=-\omega_{\alpha}+in\nu.

In vertue of (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), the eigenfunction associated to
z_{n}(\alpha)
is of the form given by

(4.44)

\Psi_{n}=C_{n}(u_{1,n},u_{2,n},z_{n}(\alpha)u_{1,n},z_{n}(\alpha)u_{2,n})

with

u_{1,n}(x)=e^{-\omega_{\alpha}x}e^{in\nu},\quad\text{for}\;x\in[0,+\infty[

and

u_{2,n}(x)=\frac{3-\alpha}{4}e^{-\omega_{\alpha}x}e^{in\nu}+\frac{1+\alpha}{4}e^{\omega_{\alpha}x}e^{-in\nu},\quad\text{for}\;x\in[0,L].

where
C_{n}
is a constant such that
\|\Psi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1.

4.1 Remark
.

Some useful symmetry properties of the eigenvalues and the eigenfunctions are below.

•

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\;z_{-n}(\alpha)=\overline{z_{n}(\alpha)}.

•

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\;u_{1,-n}(x)=\overline{u_{1,n}(x)}.

•

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\;u_{2,-n}(x)=\overline{u_{2,n}(x)}.

4.2 Proposition
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
C_{n}>0
defined by

C_{n}^{-2}=\left(\omega_{\alpha}+\left(\frac{\nu}{\omega_{\alpha}}\right)^{2}n^{2}\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{3-\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(e^{2\omega_{\alpha}L}-1)+\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(1-e^{-2\omega_{\alpha}L})\right).

Then, the eigenfunction
\Psi_{n}
is normalized in
\mathcal{H},
i.e.
\|\Psi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1.

Moreover, there exists
\tilde{C}>0
such that, for all large
n\neq m\in\mathbb{Z},
we have

(4.45)

\left|\langle\Psi_{n},\Psi_{m}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\tilde{C}}{|n-m|}\right).

Proof.

First, a straightforward calculus gives

\|u_{1,n}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\omega_{\alpha}},\;\|u_{1,n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}^{2}+\nu^{2}n^{2}}{2\omega_{\alpha}},

and

\|u_{2,n}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\omega_{\alpha}}\left\{\left(\frac{3-\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(e^{2\omega_{\alpha}L}-1)+\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(1-e^{-2\omega_{\alpha}L})\right\},

and

\|u_{2,n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}=(\omega_{\alpha}^{2}+\nu^{2}n^{2})\|u_{2,n}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}.

Thus the normalizing constant
C_{n}
can be obtained.
Now, let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
and assume
n\neq m.
One can compute the inner products
\langle u_{1,n},u_{1,m}\rangle,\langle u^{\prime}_{1,n},u^{\prime}_{1,m}\rangle,\langle u_{2,n},u_{2,m}\rangle
and
\langle u^{\prime}_{1,n},u^{\prime}_{2,m}\rangle
explicitly since all of the functions are given. We obtain

(4.46)

\langle\Psi_{n},\Psi_{m}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\left(1+K\right)\frac{C_{n}C_{m}z_{n}\bar{z}_{m}}{\omega_{\alpha}+i(n-m)\nu}&if&n\neq-m\\
\left(1+K-\frac{(1+\alpha)(3-\alpha)L}{8}\right)\frac{C_{n}^{2}|z_{n}|^{2}}{\omega_{\alpha}+i\nu}&if&n=-m\end{array}\right.

where we have set

K=\left(\frac{3-\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(e^{2\omega_{\alpha}L}-1)+\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}(1-e^{-2\omega_{\alpha}L}).

Recalling that
C_{n}
behaves like
{\mathcal{O}}(|n|^{-1})
and that
|z_{n}|
is of order
|n|
for large
n
, we obtain (
4.46
).
∎

4.3 Theorem
.

[
Riesz basis for the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
]

The sequence of generalized eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha},\alpha\in]1,3[\cup]3,+\infty[,
forms a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}.

Proof.

We assume that
1<\alpha<3
and we recall that
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}=\overline{{\rm{Span}}\left\{\Psi_{n};n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}}.

•

The sequence
\left\{\Psi_{n};n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}.

•

We consider the map
\Theta:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
defined by

\Theta((c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\Psi_{n}.

We claim that
\Theta
is an isomorphism. Let
N\in\mathbb{N},p\in\mathbb{N}.
We have

(4.47)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=N}^{N+p}c_{n}\Psi_{n}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{n=N}^{N+p}|c_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=N}^{N+p}\sum_{k=j+1}^{N+p}2\Re\left(c_{j}\bar{c}_{k}\langle\Psi_{j},\Psi_{k}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\tilde{C}\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}|c_{m}|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{|c_{n+m}|}{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c_{n}|^{2},\;\text{for some constant}\;A>0.

As a consequence of the properties given in Remark
4.1
, the same can be done for negative values of
N\in\mathbb{Z}.
Thus, the series
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\Psi_{n}
converges in
\mathcal{H}
and there exists
\tilde{K}>0
such that
\|\Theta((c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\tilde{K}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n}|^{2}.
Since
\Theta
is linear, this proves that
\Theta
is continuous.

•

The map
\Theta
is one to one.

•

Moreover, using again (
4.47
), we see for
N
large enough

\left\|\sum_{n=N}^{+\infty}c_{n}\Psi_{n}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{n=N}^{+\infty}|c_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=N}^{+\infty}|c_{n}|^{2}.

This is also because the series
\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n^{2}}
converges. Now, we fix such integer
N.
We will prove the continuity of
\Theta^{-1}
by contradiction. We assume that there exists a normalized sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}),
and a subsequence
(c_{n}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}^{k}\Psi_{n}\right\|=0.

We set
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}^{k}\Psi_{n}=\tilde{\varphi}_{k}+\psi_{k}
with

\varphi_{k}=\sum_{|n|\leq N-1}c_{n}^{k}\Psi_{n}\quad and\quad\psi_{k}=\sum_{|n|\geq N}c_{n}^{k}\Psi_{n}.

Up to an extraction of a subsequence we may assume that
(\varphi_{k})
converges in
\mathcal{H}
to some
\varphi
when
k
tends to infinity. Therefore we have

\|\varphi_{k}+\psi_{k}\|^{2}=\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}+2\Re(\langle P\varphi_{k},\psi_{k}\rangle)+\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}\geq\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}-\|P\varphi_{k}\|^{2}

where
P
is the orthogonal projection on
{\rm{Span}}\{\varphi_{m};|m|\geq N\}.
For
k\to+\infty
we have
\|\varphi_{k}\|\to\|\varphi\|
and
\|P\varphi_{k}\|\to\|P\varphi\|.
This yields
\varphi=P\varphi
and consequently
\varphi=0.

This is impossible since

0<\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=N}^{+\infty}|c_{n}|^{2}\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}^{k}\Psi_{n}\right\|=0.

This ends the proof.

∎

5.
Energy estimate

The Lumer-Phillips theorem combined with Theorem
3.5
implies that the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha},\alpha\geq 0,
generates a
C_{0}
semigroup of contractions on
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
(e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}})_{t\geq 0}
. So, system (
2.1
) is well-posed in
\mathcal{H}
(and in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}))
. More precisely, we have the following corollary.

5.1 Corollary
.

For all
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}
, the wave system (
2.2
)
admits a unique mild solution
(u,u^{\prime})\in C([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}).
Moreover, if
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
then (
2.2
) admits a unique strong solution
(u,u^{\prime})\in C([0,+\infty);\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}))
and satisfies the energy identity (
2.3
).

Now, using the Riesz basis constructed in the latest section, the energy is proved to decrease exponentially to a non-vanishing value depending on the initial datum. The decay rate is explicitly given at the end of Theorem
5.2
below since
\alpha\in]1,3[\cup]3,+\infty[.

5.2 Theorem
.

(
Energy estimate
)

We assume that
\alpha\in]1,3[\cup]3,+\infty[
. Let

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
u^{\prime}(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\,t\geq 0,

be the energy,
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
(respectively
\mathcal{H}_{p}
) be the subspace of
\mathcal{H}
spanned by the
\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda,\cdot)
’s (resp.
\psi^{p}(\omega,\cdot)
’s), which are the normalized (in
\mathcal{H}
) eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda
in
\Sigma_{p,\alpha}
(resp.
\Sigma_{p}
). We have :

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{H}_{p}
.

(2)

Let
(u_{0},u_{1})
in
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{p}
be the initial condition of the boundary value problem (
2.2
) and
(u_{0}^{p},u_{1}^{p})
its orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{p}
.

Then
(u(t),u^{\prime}(t))
decreases exponentially, in
\mathcal{H}
, to
(u_{0}^{p},u_{1}^{p})

when
t
tends to
+\infty
. More precisely

(5.48)

E(t)=E^{p}(t)+E^{p,\alpha}(t)=E^{p}(0)+E^{p,\alpha}(t)\leq E^{p}(0)+e^{-2\omega t}E^{p,\alpha}(0)

where

E^{p}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}^{p}\\
u_{1}^{p}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\,E^{p,\alpha}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\,\left\|e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}-u_{0}^{p}\\
u_{1}-u_{1}^{p}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\,t\geq 0,

and

-\omega:=\sup_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p,\alpha}}\Re(\lambda)<0.

Proof.

(1)

Above all, it is easy to see that if
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)=\lambda\psi^{p}(\lambda,.),
then
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\alpha}\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)=-\lambda\psi^{p}(\lambda,.).

Now, to prove that
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
, it suffices to check that any generalized
eigenfunction
\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)
of
\mathcal{H}_{p,\alpha}
is orthogonal to any eigenfunction
\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)

of
\mathcal{H}_{p}.

First we assume that
\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)
is an eigenfunction, i.e

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)=\lambda^{\prime}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.).

Therefore, since
\lambda
is purely imaginary,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda^{\prime}\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=&\left\langle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=&\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\alpha}\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=&-\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=&\lambda\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.\end{array}

Consequently
\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

Secondly, we assume that
\lambda^{\prime}
is not simple. Let
\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)
be an associated generalized eigenfunction of order
p\geq 2,
in the sense that

(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}-\lambda^{\prime})^{p}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)=0,\;(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}-\lambda^{\prime})^{p-1}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.)\neq 0.

Setting
\psi=(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}-\lambda^{\prime})\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),
then
\psi
is a generalized eigenfunction associated to
\lambda^{\prime}
of order
p-1,
so arguing by iteration with respect to the order
p
we can assume that
\left\langle\psi,\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

Therefore

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda^{\prime}\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=&\left\langle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{-}(\lambda^{\prime},.)+\psi,\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=&\left\langle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\\
&=&\lambda\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\end{array}

as previously. Consequently
\left\langle\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda^{\prime},.),\psi^{p}(\lambda,.)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

(2)

The purely point spectrum of the dissipative operator
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is the union of
\Sigma_{p}
set of the purely imaginary eigenvalues and
\Sigma_{p,\alpha}
set of the other eigenvalues.

The initial conditions
(u_{0},u_{1})
are written as a sum of two terms:

U_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p}}U^{p}_{0}(\lambda)\psi^{p}(\lambda,\cdot)+\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p,\alpha}}U^{p,\alpha}_{0}(\lambda)\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda,\cdot)

where
\psi^{p}(\lambda,\cdot)
(respectively
\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda,\cdot)
) is a normalized (in
\mathcal{H}
) eigenfunction of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda
in
\Sigma_{p}
(resp.
\Sigma_{p,\alpha}
). Note that the sum takes into account the multiplicities of the eigenvalues here.

Thus the solution of the boundary value problem (
2.2
) is:

U(t):=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
u^{\prime}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p}}U^{p}_{0}(\lambda)e^{\lambda t}\psi^{p}(\lambda,\cdot)+\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p,\alpha}}U^{p,\alpha}_{0}(\lambda)e^{\lambda t}\psi^{p,\alpha}(\lambda,\cdot).

The energy
E(t)=E^{p}(t)+E^{p,\alpha}(t)
with

E^{p}(t):=1/2\,\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p}}\left|U^{p}_{0}(\lambda)\right|^{2}|e^{\lambda t}|^{2},

E^{p,\alpha}(t):=1/2\,\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{p}}\left|U^{p,\alpha}_{0}(\lambda)\right|^{2}|e^{\lambda t}|^{2}.

Now, since
\Sigma_{p}
contains only purely imaginary eigenvalues,
|e^{2\lambda t}|=1
, for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{p}
and any
t>0
. Thus
E^{p}(t)=E^{p}(0)
for any
t>0
.

The real part of
\lambda
is a non-positive real number if
\lambda
is such that
\lambda\in\Sigma_{p,\alpha}
. This real part is proved to be equal to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{1}{L}\ln\Big(\frac{3-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\Big),\hbox{ if }\,\alpha\in]1,3[,\\
\frac{1}{L}\ln\Big(\frac{\alpha-3}{1+\alpha}\Big)\hbox{ if }\alpha>3,\end{array}\right.

when
\lambda\in\Sigma_{p,\alpha}
.

It holds
E^{p,\alpha}(t)\leq e^{-2\omega t}E^{p,\alpha}(0)
. Thus
E^{p,\alpha}(t)
decreases exponentially to
0
when
t
tends to
+\infty
and the total energy
E(t)
decreases exponentially to
E^{p}(0)
when
t
tends to
+\infty
.

∎

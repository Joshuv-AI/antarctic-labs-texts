Excluding Affine Configurations over a Finite Field

Abstract

Generic solutions

It is implicit in
[

Main results

We show that under certain conditions, the system
(\star)
is temperate. Our main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

The balanced system
(\star)
is temperate if the coefficient matrix
A
is tame.

Here, the matrix
A
is called
tame
if every system implied by
(\star)
that has
m^{\prime}
independent equations, uses at least
2m^{\prime}+1

(\star)
implies the equation

, then without loss of generality,

has the following form:

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|cc}1&-1&0\cdots 0\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{c}0\\[-4.30554pt]
\vdots\\
0\end{array}&\lx@intercol\hfil B\hfil\lx@intercol\end{array}\right).

Then
(\star)
is temperate if and only if the system with coefficient matrix
B
is temperate. Hence, we may assume that
(\star)
does not imply an equation of the form

.

The systems that were shown to be moderate or temperate in

,
15

16

have tame coefficient matrices. Hence these results are implied by Theorem

Over the fields

Theorem B
.

The balanced system
(\star)
is temperate if

The number of solutions of affine rank

Theorems

Let

Outline paper and proof

In Section
2
, we set up our notation and preliminaries.

In Section
3
, we introduce

matrix. This allows us to restrict the proof of Theorem

to the case

), there are two disjoint affine bases of

. This is a crucial part of the proof of Theorem

, and the place where the tameness of

In Section
4
we show a supersaturation result for solutions of a given affine rank. If for some

forces solutions of affine rank
\geq r
, then for
\delta^{\prime}<\delta
the condition
|S|\geq q^{(1-\delta^{\prime})n}
forces

to have
\Omega(q^{r-c\delta^{\prime}}n)

\geq r
(for some constant

In Section

we prove our main theorem: Theorem

In Section

In Section

Finally, in Section

. We have
r_{A}(U)=|U|
if and only if the columns of
A
indexed by
U
are linearly independent. We say that
U
is a
basis
of
A
if
|U|=r_{A}(U)=\rank(A)
. Note that any set
I\subseteq[k]
with
r_{A}(I)=|I|
is a subset of a basis.

The pair
M=([k],\mathcal{I})
where
\mathcal{I}=\{I\subseteq[k]:r_{A}(I)=|I|\}
is a linear matroid with collection of independent sets
\mathcal{I}
and rank function
r_{A}
. The following proposition follows directly by applying the matroid union theorem to two copies of
M
(see for instance Corollary 42.1b in
[
24
]
). It gives a min-max formula for the maximum size of the union of two sets of linearly independent columns of the matrix
A
.

5
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
. Then

\max\{|I\cup J|:I,J\subseteq[k],\ r_{A}(I)=I,\ r_{A}(J)=J\}\ =\ \min\{k-|U|+2r_{A}(U):U\subseteq[k]\}.

Affine rank and generic solutions

Given vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, their
affine rank

\arank\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
is the maximum number of affinely independent vectors in
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
. In other words,

\arank\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}=\rank(X),\text{ where }X=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
x_{1}&\cdots&x_{k}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}^{(n+1)\times k}.

For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
we will also denote
\arank(x)=\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\arank\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
.

6
.

For
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in(\mathbb{F}^{n})^{k}
, let

\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\{(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}:\mu_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}x_{k}=0,\quad\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}=0\}.

So the elements of
\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
correspond to the affine relations between
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.

7
.

If
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, we say that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is a
generic
solution if
\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\rowspace(A)
.

8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Then

\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})+\dim(\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))=k.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{(n+1)\times k}
be the matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
x_{1}&\cdots&x_{k}\end{pmatrix}.

For
I\subseteq[k]
the vectors
x_{i},\ i\in I
are affinely independent if and only if the columns of
A
indexed by
I
are linearly independent. So
\rank(A)=\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
. Since
\ker(A)=\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, the result follows from the rank-nullity theorem.
∎

9
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be a row-balanced matrix of rank
m
. Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
. Then
\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\leq k-m
. Equality holds if and only if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is a generic solution of
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Proof.

Since
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, the row space of
A
is contained in
\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
.
Therefore we have
m=\rank(A)\leq\dim(\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))
, so it follows from Lemma
8
that

\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=k-\dim(\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))\leq k-m.

Clearly we have equality if and only if the row space of
A
is equal to
\Ann_{\bal}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, which is equivalent to
x
being a generic solution.
∎

10
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be a balanced matrix of rank
m
. Then
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
has a generic solution in
\mathbb{F}^{k-m-1}
.

Proof.

By permuting the
k
columns of
A
, and performing elementary row-operations, we may assume that
A
is of the form
A=\begin{pmatrix}I&B\end{pmatrix}
. Let
z_{m+1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{k-m-1}
be affinely independent (e.g.
z_{k}=0
,
z_{m+i}=e_{i}
for
i\in[k-m-1]
) and set
z_{i}=-\sum_{j\in[k-m]}B_{ij}z_{j}
for
i\in[m]
. Then
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
and
\arank(z)=k-m
. So by Lemma
9
,
z
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.
∎

11
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be a row-balanced matrix of rank
m
. Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
such that
\arank(x)=k-m
. Let
U\subseteq[k]
have size
|U|=m
. Then

\text{$U$ is a basis of $A$}\iff\arank\{x_{i}:i\in[k]\setminus U\}=k-m.

Proof.

Let
r=r_{A}(U)
be the rank of the submatrix induced by the columns in
U
. By applying row operations to
A
(and permuting columns in
U
) we may assume that this submatrix is equal to

\begin{pmatrix}I_{r}&B\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

where
I_{r}
is the
r\times r
identity matrix.

If
U
is a basis of
A
, then
r=m
and the system
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
expresses for every
j\in U
the vector
x_{j}
as an affine combination of
\{x_{i}:i\in[k]\setminus U\}
. So
\arank\{x_{i}:i\in[k]\setminus U\}=\arank\{x_{i}:i\in[k]\}=k-m
.

If
U
is not a basis of
A
, then
r<m
and hence the equation
a_{m1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{mk}x_{k}
gives an affine relation between the vectors in
\{x_{i}:i\in[k]\setminus U\}
as
a_{mj}=0
for all
j\in U
. So
\arank\{x_{j}:j\in[k]\setminus U\}<k-m
.
∎

3
Tame matrices

To show that a system
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
is temperate, we will rely on the slice rank method not only for the system
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
itself, but also for implied systems. For this reason, we want the number of variables in an implied system to be more than twice the number of equations. If this is the case, we say that
A
is
tame
.

12
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be a matrix of rank
m
. We say that
A
is
tame
if, by elementary row-operations and column permutations, we cannot bring
A
into the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A^{\prime}&0\\
B&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{m^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}}
and
k^{\prime}\leq 2m^{\prime}
.

13
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be a matrix of rank
m
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

A
is tame.

(ii)

For all
U\subsetneq[k]
we have
2r_{A}(U)\geq 2m+1-k+|U|
.

(iii)

For all
i\in[k]
the set
[k]\setminus\{i\}
contains two disjoint bases of
A
.

Proof.

\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}
. Assume (ii) holds, and suppose that
A
is not tame. Using elementary row-operations and permuting columns, we can bring
A
into the form
\tilde{A}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A^{\prime}&0\\
B&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{m^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}}
with
k^{\prime}\leq 2m^{\prime}
. Since
r_{A}
is preserved under elementary row-operations, the matrix
\tilde{A}
also satisfies (ii). However, taking
U=\{k^{\prime}+1,\ldots,k\}
we obtain a contradiction. Indeed, since
C
has rank at most
m-m^{\prime}
, we have
r_{\tilde{A}}(U)\leq m-m^{\prime}
and therefore

2r_{\tilde{A}}(U)\leq 2m-2m^{\prime}\leq 2m-k^{\prime}=2m-k+|U|.

\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(ii)}
. Suppose that (ii) does not hold and let
U\subsetneq[k]
be such that
2r_{A}(U)\leq 2m-k+|U|
. Set
k^{\prime}=k-|U|
and
m^{\prime}=m-r_{A}(U)
. Then

k^{\prime}=k-|U|\leq 2m-2r_{A}(U)=2m^{\prime}.

By permuting columns of
A
, we may assume that
U=\{k^{\prime}+1,\ldots,k\}
. By performing elementary row-operations, we can bring
A
into the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A^{\prime}&0\\
B&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
C
is a
|U|\times r_{A}(U)
matrix. Then
A^{\prime}
is an
m^{\prime}\times k
matrix with
k^{\prime}\leq 2m^{\prime}
.

\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iii)}
. Let
i\in[k]
. For every
U\subseteq[k]\setminus\{i\}
we have
2r_{A}(U)\geq 2m+1-k+|U|
, or equivalently,

(k-1)-|U|+2r_{A}(U)\geq 2m.

By Proposition
5
there exist
I,J\subseteq[k]\setminus\{i\}
such that
|I\cup J|=2m
and
r_{A}(I)=|I|
and
r_{A}(J)=|J|
. Hence,
I
and
J
are two disjoint bases of
A
.

\textup{(iii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(ii)}
. Let
U\subsetneq[k]
and let
i\in[k]\setminus U
. Let
B,B^{\prime}\subseteq[k]\setminus\{i\}
be two disjoint bases. Then

|U\cap(B\cup B^{\prime})|\geq|U|-(k-1-|B\cup B^{\prime}|)=|U|-k+1+2m.

It follows that

2r_{A}(U)\geq 2\max\{|U\cap B|,|U\cap B^{\prime}|\}\geq|U\cap B|+|U\cap B^{\prime}|\geq|U|-k+1+2m.

∎

14
.

Note that Lemma
13
gives a good characterization of tame matrices in the following sense: if a matrix is not tame, this can be certified by giving a set
U\subsetneq[k]
that violates the inequality in (ii), and if a matrix is tame, this can be certified by giving two disjoint bases contained in
[k]\setminus\{i\}
for every
i\in[k]
as in (iii). In fact, since finding a maximum size set that is the union of two independent sets can be done in polynomial time by the matroid union algorithm, we can check in polynomial time if a given matrix is tame by checking (iii).

15
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{(m+1)\times(2m+1)}
have rank
m+1
, and let
A^{\prime}
be obtained by deleting the last row of
A
. Suppose that
A^{\prime}
is tame. Then, there are bases
B_{1},B_{2}
of
A
such that
B_{1}\cup B_{2}=[2m+1]
.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq[2m+1]
. If
|U|=2m+1
, then
r_{A}(U)=m+1
. If
|U|=2\ell+1
for some
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
, we have
r_{A}(U)\geq r_{A^{\prime}}(U)\geq\ell+1
by Lemma
13
since
A^{\prime}
is tame. If
|U|=2\ell+2
and
u\in U
, we have
r_{A}(U)\geq r_{A}(U\setminus\{u\})\geq\ell+1
. So we see that

2r_{A}(U)\geq|U|\quad\text{for all $U\subseteq[2m+1]$}.

It now follows by Proposition
5
that there are
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[2m+1]
with
r_{A}(I_{1})=|I_{1}|
,
r_{A}(I_{2})=|I_{2}|
, and
|I_{1}\cup I_{2}|=2m+1
. Extending for
i=1,2
the set
I_{i}
to a basis
B_{i}
of
A
completes the proof.
∎

The following proposition will be of key importance in the proof of Theorem
A
.

16
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times(2m+1)}
be a balanced, tame matrix of rank
m
. Let
x
be a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
with
\arank(x)=r\leq m
. Then there exist disjoint subsets
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq[2m+1]
of size
r
, such that
\arank\{x_{i}:i\in I_{1}\}=\arank\{x_{i}:i\in I_{2}\}=r
.

Proof.

Let
m^{\prime}=2m+1-r\geq m+1
. We can extend
A
with additional rows to obtain a balanced matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{m^{\prime}\times(2m+1)}
of rank
m^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}x^{\mathsf{T}}=0
(extend the rows of
A
to a basis of
\Ann_{\bal}(x)
.) Note that we add
m+1-r\geq 1
rows in total.

Let
A^{\prime\prime}
be the submatrix of
A^{\prime}
consisting of the first
m+1
rows (so
A
with one row added). By Lemma
15
there exist subsets
J_{1},J_{2}\subseteq[2m+1]
of size
m+1
such that
J_{1}\cup J_{2}=[2m+1]
and
r_{A^{\prime\prime}}(J_{1})=r_{A^{\prime\prime}}(J_{2})=m+1
. Clearly, we then also have:
r_{A^{\prime}}(J_{1})=r_{A^{\prime}}(J_{2})=m+1
. It follows that there are bases
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of
A^{\prime}
such that
B_{1}\cup B_{2}=[2m+1]
. Since
|B_{1}|=|B_{2}|=2m+1-r
, it follows that
I_{1}:=[2m+1]\setminus B_{1}
and
I_{2}:=[2m+1]\setminus B_{2}
are disjoint sets of size
r
. By Lemma
11
,
\arank\{x_{i}:i\in I_{1}\}=r=\arank\{x_{i}:i\in I_{2}\}
.
∎

Extending tame matrices

In this subsection we show the following proposition.

17
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be tame and row-balanced with
k>2m+1
. Then
A
can be extended to a tame, row-balanced matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{m^{\prime}\times(2m^{\prime}+1)}
such that if
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k^{\prime}})
is a generic solution to
A^{\prime}(x^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, then
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Before proving the proposition, we need a number of lemmas.

18
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times k}
be tame. Suppose that
k\geq 2m+2
. Then there exist
i\in[k-1]
such that for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
the matrix

A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
\alpha e_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}+\beta e_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&\gamma\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}^{(m+1)\times(k+1)}

is tame.

Proof.

For
U\subseteq[k-1]
define
f(U)=2r_{A}(U)-2m-1+k-|U|
. Since
A
is tame, it follows by Lemma
13
that
f(U)\geq 0
for every
U\subseteq[k-1]
. Let

\mathcal{T}=\{U\subseteq[k-1]:f(U)=0\}.

Note that
[k-1]\in\mathcal{T}
since
r_{A}([k-1])\leq m
. So
\mathcal{T}
is nonempty.

Claim
.

Let
U,V\in\mathcal{T}
. Then
U\cup V,U\cap V\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof of claim.

By the submodularity of
r_{A}
we have
f(U\cap V)+f(U\cup V)\leq f(U)+f(V)=0
. Since
f(U\cap V)\geq 0
and
f(U\cup V)\geq 0
, we have
f(U\cap V)=f(U\cup V)=0
.

\scriptstyle\blacksquare

Let
I\subseteq[k-1]
be the unique inclusionwise minimal subset of
\mathcal{T}
. Since
\emptyset\not\in\mathcal{T}
as
k>2m+1
, the set
I
is nonempty. Let
i\in I
. So
i\in U
for all
U\in\mathcal{T}
. We will show that for this choice of
i
the matrix
A^{\prime}
is tame for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
U\subsetneq[k+1]
be arbitrary. By Lemma
13
, it suffices to show that

2r_{A^{\prime}}(U)\geq 2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|.

Suppose that for some
j\in\{i,k,k+1\}
we have
j\in U
. If
k+1\in U
we choose
j=k+1
. Note that
U\setminus\{j\}\subsetneq[k]
. Since
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\neq 0
, we have
r_{A^{\prime}}(U)\geq 1+r_{A}(U\setminus\{j\})
. Since
A
is tame, we obtain

\displaystyle 2r_{A^{\prime}}(U)

\displaystyle\geq 2+2r_{A}(U\setminus\{j\})

\displaystyle\geq 2+2m+1-k+|U\setminus\{j\}|

\displaystyle=2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|

as desired.

Therefore, we may assume that
U\cap\{i,k,k+1\}=\emptyset
. So
U\subseteq[k-1]
and
U\not\in\mathcal{T}
. It follows that
f(U)\geq 1
, and therefore

\displaystyle 2r_{A^{\prime}}(U)

\displaystyle\geq 2r_{A}(U)

\displaystyle\geq 1+(2m+1-k+|U|)

\displaystyle=2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|.

∎

Note that if
\mathbb{F}\neq\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we can choose elements
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
. Hence, if
A
is row-balanced, we can take
A^{\prime}
to be row-balanced as well. For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
this does not work. In fact the row-balanced tame matrix
\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1&1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{1\times 4}
cannot be extended to a row-balanced tame matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2\times 5}
. For this reason, we need the following lemma.

19
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m\times k}
be tame. Suppose that
k\geq 2m+2
. Then there exists
i\in[k-1]
such that the matrix
A^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{(m+3)\times(k+5)}
given by

A^{\prime\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0&0&0&0\\
e_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}+e_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&0&1&0&0\\
e_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1&0&1&0\\
e_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&1&1&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

is tame.

Note that if
A
has rank
m
and is row-balanced, then
A^{\prime\prime}
has rank
m+3
and is row balanced.

Proof.

Let
i
be as in the conclusion of Lemma
18
. So the submatrix

A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
e_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}+e_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&1\end{pmatrix}

of
A^{\prime\prime}
induced by the first
k+1
columns and
m+1
rows is tame. We will use the shorthand notations
r^{\prime}=r_{A^{\prime}}
and
r^{\prime\prime}=r_{A^{\prime\prime}}
. By Lemma
13
we know that

2r^{\prime}(U)\geq 2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|\quad\text{for all $U\subsetneq[k+1]$}.

Let
U\subsetneq[k+5]
be arbitrary. To show that
A^{\prime\prime}
is tame, it suffices to show that

2r^{\prime\prime}(U)\geq 2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|

since
2(m+3)+1-(k+5)=2(m+1)+1-(k+1)
.

By permuting the first
k-1
columns, we may assume without loss of generality that
i=k-1
. Let
U_{1}=U\cap[k-2]
and
U_{2}=U\setminus U_{1}=U\cap\{k-1,\ldots,k+5\}
. First consider the case that
|U_{2}|\leq 6
. Note that the submatrix of
A^{\prime\prime}
induced by the last three rows and the last seven columns represent all
7
vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
. Since any set of
t\leq 6
vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
spans a subspace of dimension at least
t/2
, we see that
r^{\prime\prime}(U)\geq r^{\prime}(U_{1})+|U_{2}|/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle 2r^{\prime\prime}(U)

\displaystyle\geq 2r^{\prime}(U_{1})+|U_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq 2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|

\displaystyle=2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|.

Now consider the remaining case that
|U_{2}|=7
. Since
k+4
and
k+5
belong to
U
, we have
r^{\prime\prime}(U)\geq 2+r^{\prime}(U\cap[k+1])
. Since
U
does not contain
[k+1]
(as
U\not=[k+5])
, we have

\displaystyle 2r^{\prime\prime}(U)

\displaystyle\geq 2r^{\prime}(U\cap[k+1])+4

\displaystyle\geq 2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U\cap[k+1]|+4

\displaystyle=2(m+1)+1-(k+1)+|U|.

∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
17
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
A
can be extended to a row-balanced, tame
k^{\prime}\times m^{\prime}
matrix
A^{\prime}
such that the following two properties hold:

•

k^{\prime}-2m^{\prime}=(k-2m)-1
,

•

if
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k^{\prime}})
satisfies
A^{\prime}(x^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}=0
and
\arank\{x^{\prime}\}=k^{\prime}-m^{\prime}
, then
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
satisfies
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
and
\arank(x)=k-m
.

Indeed, repeating this step
k-2m-1
times results in a
(k-m-1)\times(2(k-m-1)+1)
matrix with the required properties. Note that we used the characterization of generic solutions from Lemma
9
.

Let us first consider the case that
\mathbb{F}\neq\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
. Let
m^{\prime}=m+1
and
k^{\prime}=k+1
. By Lemma
18
, there exists
i\in[k-1]
such that the matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{m^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}}
given by

A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
\alpha e_{i}+\beta e_{k}&\gamma\end{pmatrix}

is tame. Since
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
, the matrix
A^{\prime}
is balanced. Now suppose
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1})
satisfies
A^{\prime}(x^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}=0
and
\arank(x^{\prime})=(k+1)-(m+1)
. Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
. Clearly,
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
. Since
\gamma\neq 0
, the vector
x_{k+1}
is an affine combination of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
, so
\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\arank(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1})=k-m
as required.

Now let us consider the case that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. By Lemma
19
, we obtain the tame, row-balanced matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}}
with
m^{\prime}=m+3
and
k^{\prime}=k+5
given by

A^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0&0&0&0\\
e_{i}+e_{k}&1&0&1&0&0\\
e_{i}&1&1&0&1&0\\

In this section we prove a supersaturation result (Proposition

For

the
Gaussian binomial coefficient

{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{d}}_{q}
is given by

{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{d}}_{q}=\begin{cases}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{1-q^{n-i}}{1-q^{d-i}}&\text{if }d\leq n\\
0&\text{if }d>n\end{cases}.

The following fact is well-known.

20
.

Let
k\leq d\leq n
be nonnegative integers. Then the number of
d
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
containing a given
k
-dimensional linear subspace is
{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-k}{d-k}}_{q}
.

Proof.

Let

be a
k
-dimensional linear subspace and let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
be a basis of

d-k

such that

are linearly independent vectors in

(q^{n}-q^{k})(q^{n}-q^{k+1})\cdots(q^{n}-q^{d-1})
. Indeed, for

arbitrarily in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\mathrm{span}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{i-1})
. Thus, we obtain a
d

W:=\mathrm{span}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})
containing
V
.

Each subspace

of dimension

is thus obtained

times, since there are that many ways to extend

to a basis

of

. We conclude that the number of
d
-dimensional subspaces containing

is equal to

\frac{(q^{n}-q^{k})\cdots(q^{n}-q^{d-1})}{(q^{d}-q^{k})\cdots(q^{d}-q^{d-1})}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-k}{d-k}}_{q}.

Note that Lemma

We will need the following bounds on the Gaussian binomial coefficients.

21

Let

q^{d(n-d)}\leq{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{d}}_{q}\leq 4q^{d(n-d)}.

The lower bound follows directly from the fact that for all

\frac{q^{n-i}-1}{q^{d-i}-1}\geq\frac{q^{n-i}}{q^{d-i}}=q^{n-d}.

The upper bound follows from the fact that for all

\frac{q^{n-i}-1}{q^{d-i}-1}\leq q^{n-d}(1+\frac{1}{q^{d-i}-1})\leq q^{n-d}(1+\frac{1}{2^{d-i}-1})

and the fact that

\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(1+\frac{1}{2^{d-i}-1})\ =\ 2\prod_{i=2}^{d}(1+\frac{1}{2^{i}-1})\ \leq\ 2e^{\sum_{i=2}^{d}\frac{1}{2^{i}-1}}\ \leq\ 2e^{\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\frac{1}{2^{i}}}\ \leq\ 2e^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq 4.

have size
|S|\geq C_{t}q^{(1-\delta_{t})n}
. We define

m=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta_{t-1}}{2+\delta_{1}+\delta_{t-1}}n\right\rfloor,\qquad k=\left\lceil\frac{2+\delta_{1}}{2+\delta_{1}+\delta_{t-1}}n\right\rceil=n-m.

By the choice of
n_{t}
we have
m\geq n_{1}
and
k\geq n_{t-1}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
we denote
S_{x}:=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}:(x,y)\in S\}
. Let
T=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}:|S_{x}|\geq C_{1}q^{(1-\delta_{1})m}\}
. We have

|S|=\sum_{x\in T}|S_{x}|+\sum_{x\not\in T}|S_{x}|\leq|T|q^{m}+C_{1}q^{k+(1-\delta_{1})m}.

Since
\delta_{1}m+1\geq\delta_{t}n
, we have
(1-\delta_{t})n+1\geq k+(1-\delta_{1})m
. Similarly, we have
(1-\delta_{t})n+1\geq 3m+(1-\delta_{t-1})k
. It follows that

|S|\geq C_{t}q^{(1-\delta_{t})n}\geq C_{1}q^{k+(1-\delta_{1})m}+C_{t-1}q^{3m+(1-\delta_{t-1})k}.

Hence, it follows that

|T|\geq\frac{|S|-C_{1}q^{k+(1-\delta_{1})m}}{q^{m}}\geq q^{2m}C_{t-1}q^{(1-\delta_{t-1})k}.

For every
x\in T
, there is a line
\ell
contained in
S_{x}
. Since the number of lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
is less than
q^{2m}
, there is a subset
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
of size
|T^{\prime}|\geq|T|q^{-2m}
and a line
\ell\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
such that
\ell\subseteq S_{x}
for all
x\in T^{\prime}
. Since
|T^{\prime}|\geq C_{t-1}q^{(1-\delta_{t-1})k}
there is a (
t-1
)-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq T^{\prime}
. Hence
\{(x,y):x\in V,y\in\ell\}
is a
t
-dimensional subspace contained in
S
.
∎

We restate Theorem B.

28
.

Let
q\in\{2,3\}
and let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times k}
be a row-balanced matrix of rank
m
. Then
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
is temperate.

Proof.

Let
d=k-m-1
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
contains a generic solution
y
to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
by Lemma
10
. So
y
has affine rank
m-k
.

Now let
n\geq n_{d}
and let
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
have size
|S|\geq C_{d}q^{(1-\delta_{d})n}
. Then
S
contains a
d
-dimensional affine subspace
V=\{b_{0}+\alpha_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}b_{d}:\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
. Setting
x_{i}=b_{0}+y_{i1}b_{1}+\cdots+y_{id}b_{d}
, we obtain a solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
where
x
has affine rank
k-m
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in S
.
∎

7
Affine copies

Given a balanced matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times k}
of rank
m
, there is a generic solution
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k-m-1}
by Lemma
10
. Conversely, if
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r})^{k}
, then
z
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, where
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times k}
has rows that are a basis of
\Ann_{\bal}(z)
. Instead of saying that
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
has a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, we can equivalently say that
S
contains an affine copy of
z
. Thus, we can reformulate Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
and obtain Corollary C.

29
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We say that
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
is an
affine copy
of
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
if there is an injective linear map
L:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and a vector
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that
x_{i}=L(z_{i})+a
for all
i\in[k]
.

Note that if
x
is an affine copy of
z
, then any translate of
x
is an affine copy of any translate of
z
.

30
.

Let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r})^{k}
. Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times k}
be such that the rows form a basis of
\Ann_{\bal}(z)
. Let
n\geq r
and let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{k}
. Then
x
is an affine copy of
z
if and only if
x
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Proof.

By translating, we may assume without loss of generality that
z_{k}=0
and
x_{k}=0
. Note that
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Suppose that
x
is an affine copy of
z
. Then
x_{i}=L(z_{i})
for all
i\in[k]
, where
L:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is an injective linear map. It is easy to verify that
\Ann_{\bal}(x)=\Ann_{\bal}(z)
. So
x
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
x
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
. Define
L:\spn\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1}\}\to\spn\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}
by

L(\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i}z_{i}):=\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}.

This is a well-defined injective linear map, since

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i}z_{i}=0

\displaystyle\iff(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},-\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i})\in\Ann_{\bal}(z)

\displaystyle\iff(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-1},-\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i})\in\Ann_{\bal}(x)

\displaystyle\iff\sum_{i\in[k-1]}\lambda_{i}x_{i}=0.

Since
n\geq r
, we can extend
L
to an injective linear map
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. So
x
is an affine copy of
z
.
∎

We can now prove Corollary
D
as a direct consequence of Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
.

Proof of Corollary
D
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times k}
be a matrix such that the rows form a basis of
\Ann_{\bal}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
. So
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a generic solution to
Ax^{\mathsf{T}}=0
. If
q\not\in\{2,3\}
, then the premise of Corollary
D
implies by Lemma
11
that for every
i\in[k]
the matrix
A
has two disjoint bases in
[k]\setminus\{i\}
. Hence, by Lemma
13
, the matrix
A
is tame.

By Lemma
30
, it follows directly from Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
that there is a
\delta>0
such that any subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
without affine copy of
z
has size
|S|=O(q^{(1-\delta)n})
.
∎

8
Concluding remarks

An advantage of considering generic solutions over
(\star)
-shapes, is that they can be used to obtain not just one, but many solutions given a sufficiently large set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. In particular, we obtain Corollary
C
which we restate here.

31
.

Suppose that the coefficient matrix
A
of
(\star)
is tame. Then there exist
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that for all positive
\delta^{\prime}<\delta
the following holds: if
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
has size
|S|\geq q^{(1-\delta^{\prime})n}
, then
S
has
\Omega(q^{(k-m)n-C\delta^{\prime}n})
solutions to
(\star)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
5
and Proposition
22
.
∎

We conclude with an open problem for which we think both a positive and a negative answer would be very interesting.

32
.

Is it true that
(\star)
is temperate for all balanced matrices
A
?

A positive answer would be a very strong result, since we do not even know if the system corresponding to
4
-term progressions is temperate. As a final note, we remark that the situation where
A
is tame, corresponds to a strict subset of systems of linear forms of Cauchy-Schwarz complexity
1
(see:
[
13
]
). Perhaps this class would be a good candidate for proving a generalisation.

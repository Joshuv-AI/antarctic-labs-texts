Operational relevance of resource theories of quantum measurements
Michał Oszmaniec, Tanmoy Biswas

Abstract
For any resource theory it is essential to identify tasks for which resource objects offer advantage over free objects. We show that this identification can always be accomplished for resource theories of quantum measurements in which free objects form a convex subset of measurements on a given Hilbert space. To this aim we prove that every resourceful measurement offers advantage for some quantum state discrimination task. Moreover, we give an operational interpretation of robustness, which quantifies the minimal amount of noise that must be added to a measurement to make it free. Specifically, we show that this geometric quantity is related to the maximal relative advantage that a resourceful measurement offers in a class of minimal-error state discrimination (MESD) problems. Finally, we apply our results to two classes of free measurements: incoherent measurements (measurements that are diagonal in the fixed basis) and separable measurements (measurements whose effects are separable operators). For both of these scenarios we find, in the asymptotic setting in which the dimension or the number of particles increase to infinity, the maximal relative advantage that resourceful measurements offer for state discrimination tasks.

2
Notation and main concepts

Throughout the paper we will be interested in POVMs on finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\approx\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Such generalized measurements (POVMs) can be understood as tuples
\mathbf{M}=\left(M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\right)
of non-negative operators on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}=\mathbbm{1}
, where
n
is the number of outcomes and
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. The operators
M_{i}
are called the effects of POVM
\mathbf{M}
. According to Born’s rule, when a POVM
\mathbf{M}
is measured on a quantum state
\rho
the probability of obtaining the outcome
i
is given by
p_{i}=\tr\left(M_{i}\rho\right)
. We denote the set of POVMs on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
n
outcomes by
\mathcal{P}\left(d,n\right)
. This set has a natural convex structure
[
46
]
: given two POVMs
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{P}\left(d,n\right)
, their convex combination
p\mathbf{M}+(1-p)\mathbf{N}
is the POVM with
i
-th effect given by
\left[p\mathbf{M}+\left(1-p\right)\mathbf{N}\right]_{i}\coloneqq pM_{i}+(1-p)N_{i}
. The operation of taking convex combinations of measurements can be operationally realized as performing POVMs
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
with certain probabilities and then combining the outcomes.

Our operational interpretation of
resourcefullness
of quantum measurements will be based on the task of minimal-error state discrimination
[
11
,
12
]
. The purpose of this task is to optimally distinguish quantum states states generated by the ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{q_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, where
\{q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a probability distribution and
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a collection of quantum states. The success probability of identifying the states generated by
\mathcal{E}
via a measurement
\mathbf{M}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)
is given by

p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{M})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\tr(M_{i}\rho_{i})\ .

(1)

One is often interested in choosing the measurement
\mathbf{M}
, such that
p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{M})
is maximized. However, if only a restricted class of measurements
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(d,n)
is allowed, the maximum
p_{\mathrm{succ}}
may not be achieved.

We now give a minimal formulation of a resource theory of measurements, under the assumption of convexity. In our treatment we will focus on a set of quantum measurements

Justification of assumptions:
The convexity of
\mathcal{F}
can be justified by the fact that, as argued before, convex combination of measurements can be realised by a purely classical process. The compactness of
\mathcal{F}
is a technical assumption that allows us to formally state our results. However, in basically any physically interesting case the class of free measurements
\mathcal{F}
can be viewed as a set of convex combinations (convex hull) of a compact set of "primitive" measurements
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{F}
contains (a) classical post-processing i.e. operation of the form
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{M}^{\prime}

M^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j}q(i|j)M_{i}
, for some stochastic matrix
\mathcal{Q}_{ij}=q(i|j)

\mathcal{F}

For every set of free measurements
\mathcal{F}

\mathbf{M}
to make it free. In analogy to previous works

18

17
]
we will call this quantifier

1
):

R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}):=\min\bigg\{s|\ \exists\mathbf{N}\text{ such that }\frac{\mathbf{M}+s\mathbf{N}}{1+s}\in\mathcal{F}\bigg\}\ .

(2)

The robustness enjoys a number of natural properties as stated by the following proposition.

Proposition 1

(Properties of robustness)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(d,n)
be a closed convex set of free measurements. The robustness

satisfies the following properties: (i)
Faithfulness:
R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\geq 0
and
R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})>0
iff
\mathbf{M}\notin\mathcal{F}
, (ii) Convexity:
R_{\mathcal{F}}(p\mathbf{M}_{1}+(1-p)\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\leq pR_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
, (iii) Monotonicity under free operations:
R_{\mathcal{F}}\left[\varphi(\mathbf{M})\right]\leq R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})
for all free operations
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}
.

The proof of the above follows directly form the definitions of the concepts involved and we defer it to the Appendix.

Main results

In this part we give our main results concerning the operational interpretation of resourceful measurements for a class of quantum state discrimination tasks.

Theorem 1

(Minimal-error state discrimination

can certify resource character of measurements)

For any resourceful POVM

We begin the proof by noting that

R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})+1=\sum_{i}\tr(M_{i}\sigma_{i})
, where
\sigma_{i}
cannot be classically distinguished. Since for any
\rho
we have
\tr(M_{i}\rho)\leq\tr(M_{i})
we obtain
R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})+1\leq\sum_{i}\tr(M_{i})=d
. Moreover, if
n\geq d
this bound can be easily saturated for projective measurements in the Fourier basis
|\tilde{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{i2\pi jk}{d}\right)|k\rangle
=with ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\{\frac{1}{d},|\tilde{j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^{d}
.

For
n<d
, we will use (
10
) to get the missing upper bound. We first note that since operators
M_{a}
are upper bounded by
\mathbbm{1}
we have
\tr(Z_{a}M_{a})\leq\tr(Z_{a})=1
. Therefore , from (
10
) we can conclude that
R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})=\sum_{a}\tr(Z_{a}M_{a})\leq\sum_{a}\tr(Z_{a})=n
, hence
R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})\leq n-1
.
Combining this with the previous case we conclude
R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})=\min\{d,n\}-1
.

Just like in the case
n\geq d
we will proof the saturation of this bound using Fourier transform of the original basis
|\tilde{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{\frac{i2\pi jk}{d}}|k\rangle
. To prove the equality for the case
n<d
, we choose an ensemble
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}
uniformly distributed over first
n
orthonormal states from ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{0}
,
i.e
,
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}=\{\frac{1}{n},|\tilde{j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^{n}
and an
n
outcome POVM
\tilde{\mathbf{M}}:=\bigg\{\{|\tilde{j}\rangle\langle\tilde{j}|\}_{i=1}^{n-1},\mathbb{I}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|\tilde{j}\rangle\langle\tilde{j}|\bigg\}
. For this particular case, again RHS of (
11
) reduce to
n-1
. Combining these two cases we obtain
\max_{\mathbf{M}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)}R_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbf{M})=\min\{d,n\}-1\
.

Now we will show that for
n\geq d
any POVM for which (
11
) holds, have the effects directly proportional to
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
where
|\psi\rangle=(1/\sqrt{d})\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{exp}(i\theta_{j})|j\rangle
. This follows from the simple observation that for
n\geq d
, maximization of (
10
)
\forall a
,
\tr(Z_{a}M_{a})=\tr(M_{a})
requires that
Z_{a}=P_{\mathrm{Supp}(M_{a})}+B_{a}
, where
\tr(B_{a}P_{\mathrm{Supp}(M_{a})})=0
. Moreover, to satisfy the constraint of (
10
),
P_{\mathrm{Supp}(M_{a})}
has to be one dimensional. This implies only feasible solution will be

\displaystyle M_{a}=\alpha_{a}\Pi_{a}\;\;\;\;Z_{a}=\Pi_{a},

(13)

where
\Pi_{a}
is rank one projector with trace 1. Then to be a valid POVM,

\sum_{a}\alpha_{a}\Pi_{a}=\mathbbm{1}\Rightarrow\sum_{a}\alpha_{a}\langle i|\Pi_{a}|i\rangle=1.

(14)

Combining (
14

\langle i|\Pi_{a}|i\rangle=\frac{1}{d}
for every

. This leads to the conclusion that the POVM

|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
where
|\psi\rangle=(1/\sqrt{d})\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{exp}(i\theta_{j})|j\rangle

∎

4.2
Separable measurements

We now turn our attention to the measurements that can be performed on a composite quantum systems. In what follows we will study the maximal advantage that entangled measurements can offer for MESD over so-called separable measurements on multiparticle quantum systems. Separable measurements contain the set of LOCC measurements and are relatively easy to characterize. For this reason the maximal advantage with respect to separable measurements is in general smaller then the advantage with respect to LOCC measurements.

Consider a biparticle quantum system
\mathcal{H}_{AB}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. We define a POVM
\mathbf{F}
as separable if all its effects
\{F_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
are separable operators i.e.
F_{i}=\sum_{k}Q^{k}_{i}\otimes\tilde{Q}^{k}_{i}
where
\{Q^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\tilde{Q}^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are positive operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively. The above definition can be easily extended to system of multiple qubits
\mathcal{H}_{N}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}
. We denote the set of biparticle separable measurements by
\mathrm{Sep}(AB)
and separable multiqubit measurements by
\mathrm{Sep}(N)
. Recall that we made the dependence on number of outcomes
n
implicit. In what follows we will assume that
n
is fixed and equals at least the dimension of the relevant Hilbert space (
d_{A}d_{B}
and
2^{N}
respectively). It easily follows that
\mathrm{Sep}(AB)
and
\mathrm{Sep}(N)
are convex compact subsets of
\mathcal{P}(d,n)
for suitable Hilbert spaces. The following Lemma gives the comprehensive answer to the question about the maximal advantage for MESD that entangled measurements can offer over separable measurements in both bipartite and multiparty scenario.

Proposition 4

(Maximal advantage of entangled meaurements over separable measurements)
.

Let
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
denote the dimensions of local spaces in the biparticle Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}
. Then, the following inequalities hold

\min\{d_{A},d_{B}\}-1\leq\max_{\mathbf{M}}R_{\mathrm{Sep}(AB)}(\mathbf{M})\leq\min\{d_{A},d_{B}\}\ ,

(15)

where the maximization is over all measurements on
\mathcal{H}_{AB}
.

Moreover, the maximal robustness of entangled qubit measurement increases exponentially with the size of the system. Specifically, for sufficiently large
N
we have

c\frac{2^{N}}{8N^{2}}-1\leq\max_{\mathbf{M}}R_{\mathrm{Sep}(N)}(\mathbf{M})\leq 2^{\frac{3}{2}N-1}-1\ ,

(16)

where the maximization is over all measurements on
\mathcal{H}_{N}
and
c>0.7
.

Sketch of the proof.

The upper bounds in (
15
) and (
16
) follow from application of explicit simulation strategy based on (global) depolarizing map
\Phi_{t}
. This map, transforms effects of a measurement
\mathbf{M}
as follows:
\Phi_{t}(M_{i})=tM_{i}+(1-t)\frac{\tr(M_{i})}{d}\mathbbm{1}
. It is easy to check that the resulting operators still form a POVM on a total space. Because this map preserves traces of the effects we can write
\Phi_{t}(M_{i})=\tr(\mathbf{M}_{i})\left(\Phi_{t}(\rho_{i})\right)
, where
\rho_{i}=M_{i}/\tr(M_{i})
is a quantum state. To get an upper bound on the robustness of
\mathbf{M}
it that suffices to find lower bond on
t_{\ast}
defined as the number for which
all states

\rho_{i}
constructed from the effects of
M_{i}
are separable. To find the upper bound we can directly use the results from entanglement theory. Concretely, the problem of how much "white noise" can be added to a quantum state before it becomes separable quantum states has been studied before. Specifically the maximal value of "random robustness" (minimal
s=1/t-1
such that
\Phi_{t}(\rho)
is separable) equals
\min\{d_{A},d_{B}\}-1
in the biparticle scenario
[
19
]
and can be upper bounded by
2^{\frac{3}{2}N-1}-1
for the multipartice case
[
54
]
. To obtain the lower bounds we use characterization of the robustness MESD given in Eq.(
5
) by showing advantage of entangled measurements over separable ones for particular uniform ensambles of quantum states. for biparticle case we consider exnamble consisting of orthogonal maximally states
[
29
]
, while for multiparticle case we consider ensemble consisting of
2^{N}
iid random quantum states taken from the Haar measure on
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes N}

[
55
]
(see Appendix for details).
∎

Appendix A
Proofs of general results

For the convenience of the reader we first recall the definition of robustness for general convex and compact subset of free measurements
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(d,n)

R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}):=\min\bigg\{s|\ \exists\mathbf{N}\text{ such that }\frac{\mathbf{M}+s\mathbf{N}}{1+s}\in\mathcal{F}\bigg\}\ .

(17)

Proposition 1

(Properties of robustness)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(d,n)
be a compact convex set of free measurements. The robustness
R_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the following properties: (i)
Faithfulness:
R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\geq 0
and
R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})>0
iff
\mathbf{M}\notin\mathcal{F}
, (ii) Convexity:
R_{\mathcal{F}}(p\mathbf{M}+(1-p)\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\leq pR_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
, (iii) Monotonicity under free operations:
R_{\mathcal{F}}\left[\varphi(\mathbf{M})\right]\leq R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})
for all free operations
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

(i) Faithfulness
. This property follows directly from the definition given in Eq.(
17
).

(ii) Convexity
. From the definition of the robustness (
17
) it follows that for two POVMs
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
there exist POVMs
\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}

\displaystyle=(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}))\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\mathbf{N}\ ,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime}))\tilde{\mathbf{M}^{\prime}}-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\ ,

(19)

where
\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
. We therefore get that for all
p\in[0,1]

\displaystyle p\mathbf{M}+(1-p)\mathbf{M}^{\prime}=p[(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}))\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\mathbf{N}]

(20)

\displaystyle+(1-p)[(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime}))\mathbf{M}^{\prime}-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\mathbf{N}^{\prime}]\ .

After defining

\displaystyle\tilde{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle pR_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\ ,

(21)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{N}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{pR_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\mathbf{N}+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\mathbf{N}^{\prime}}{\tilde{r}}\ ,

(22)

T

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{p(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\tilde{\mathbf{M}})+(1-p)(\tilde{\mathbf{M}^{\prime}}+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\tilde{\mathbf{M}^{\prime}})}{1+\tilde{r}}\ ,

(23)

we note that
\tilde{N}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)
, while
\textbf{T}\in\mathcal{F}
. We finally observe that

p\mathbf{M}+(1-p)\mathbf{M}^{\prime}=(1+\tilde{r})\textbf{T}-\tilde{r}\tilde{\mathbf{N}}\ ,

(24)

which directly implies
\tilde{r}=pR_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\geq R_{\mathcal{F}}(p\mathbf{M}+(1-p)\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
.

(iii) Monotonicity under free operations
. From the definition we have that for every
\mathbf{M}
there exist
\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)
and
\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

\mathbf{M}=(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}))\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\mathbf{N}\ .

(25)

Applying
\varphi
to both sides of the above equality we obtain

\varphi(\mathbf{M})=(1+R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M}))\varphi(\tilde{\mathbf{M}})-R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})\varphi(\mathbf{N})\ .

(26)

Since, by assumption,
\varphi(\tilde{\mathbf{M}})\in\mathcal{F}
, equation (
26
) directly implies
R_{\mathcal{F}}\left[\varphi(\mathbf{M})\right]\leq R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})
.
∎

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}

\in

\mathcal{P}(d,n)
and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(d,n)
be a compact convex set of free measurements. Then, the generalized robustness
R_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{M})
can be expressed as the following optimisation problem.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{maximize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tr(Z_{i}M_{i})-1

(27)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle Z_{i}\geq 0,\;i=1,\ldots,n\ ,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}Tr(Z_{i}N_{i})\leq 1\;\forall\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{F}\ .

Proof.

Our proof starategy is the following. The problem in (
27
) belongs to the class of semi-infinite optimisation problems
[
47
]
. We will compute its dual and argue that there is no duality gap. Finally, the dual problem will turn out to be equivalent to the original definition of robustness from (
17
). After simple algebraic manipulations we obtain that (
27
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle-\min_{\mathbf{X}}\bigg\{\sum_{i}\tr(X_{i}M_{i})-1:\forall{i},\;X_{i}\leq 0,\ \forall\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{F}\ \ \sum_{i}Tr(X_{i}N_{i})\geq-1\bigg\}

(28)

\displaystyle=-\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}\bigg\{\sum_{i}\tr(\tilde{X}_{i}M_{i}):\forall{i},\;X_{i}\leq\frac{\mathbb{I}}{d}\ \forall\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{F}\ \ \sum_{i}Tr(\tilde{X}_{i}N_{i})\geq 0\bigg\}\ .

The
Lagrangian
corresponding to the last problem in (
28
) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X},\textbf{G},\{k(\mathbf{N})\})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\tr(\tilde{X}_{i}M_{i})+\tr(X_{i}-\frac{I}{d})G_{i}-\int d\mathbf{N}k(\mathbf{N})\tr(N_{i}X_{i})\bigg)\ ,

(29)

where
\textbf{G}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
and
k(\mathbf{N})
are lagrange multipliers which satisfy
G_{i}\geq 0

\forall{i}
and
k\mathbf{N})\geq 0
for almost all
\mathbf{N}\in\mathcal{F}
. To construct the dual problem to (
29
) we minimize
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X},\textbf{G},\{k(\mathbf{N})\})
over primal variables
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
. Since the
Lagrangian
is affine function of
\tilde{\textbf{X}}
, we obtain minimum equal to
\infty
unless
\textbf{G}+\mathbf{M}=\int k(\mathbf{N})\mathbf{N}d\mathbf{N}
. If the condition holds then we obtain
-\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tr(G_{i})
. Hence we see that the dual of the problem (
27
) can be written as

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tr(G_{i})

(30)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\textbf{G}+\mathbf{M}=\int k(\mathbf{N})\mathbf{N}d\mathbf{N}\ ,

\displaystyle G_{i}\geq 0\;\forall{i}\ ,

\displaystyle k(\mathbf{N})\geq 0\;\;\;\forall{\mathbf{N}}\in\mathcal{F}\ .

We note that the primal problem (
27
) posseses a strictly feasible points (for example
\mathbf{Z}=(\frac{\lambda\mathbbm{1}}{d},\ldots,\frac{\lambda\mathbbm{1}}{d}
, for
\lambda\in(0,1)
), which follows from the assumption of the compactness of
\mathcal{F}
and the form of the constrains in (
27
). Finally, we conclude the proof by noting that
\textbf{G}+\mathbf{M}=\int k(\mathbf{N})\mathbf{N}d\mathbf{N}
iff
\textbf{G}=s\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
where
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(d,n)
and
s=\sum_{i}\frac{\tr G_{i}}{d}
.
∎

Proposition 4

(Maximal advantage of entangled measurements over separable measurements)
.

Let
d_{A}
and

denote the dimensions of local spaces in the biparticle Hilbert space

Proof of lower bounds in Eq.(

) and Eq.(

Our strategy to prove the lower bounds is to construct ensembles

for which the relative advantage

\frac{p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{M})}{{\max_{\mathbf{N}\in Sep(\mathcal{H}_{A}:\mathcal{H}_{B})}}p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E},\mathbf{N})}

of some entangled measurement

(i) Biparticle case

\mathbf{M}_{0}
whose effects are of the form

where we have used the separability of operators

we conclude the proof by observing that

R_{\mathrm{Sep}(AB)}(\mathbf{M}_{0})\geq\frac{p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathbf{M}_{0})}{\max{N\in\mathrm{Sep}(AB)}p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathbf{N})}-1\geq D-1\ .

(42)

(ii)

Multiparticle case
We proceed analogously to the biparticle case. Consider a random uniform ensemble of
M=2^{N}
Haar random, independant and identically distributed pure states in
\mathcal{H}_{N}
,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}=\left\{{\frac{1}{2^{N}},\Psi_{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{2^{N}}\ .

(43)

The above ensemble is random as states composing it are independent random pure states i.e.
\Psi_{i}=U_{i}\Psi_{0}U^{\dagger}_{i}
, for
U_{i}
distributed according to Haar measure on the group
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{N})
. In
[
55
]
it was shown that in the limit of large
N
, for typical realizations of the ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}
the so-called pretty good measurement
\mathbf{M}_{PGM}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}})
is capable to to distinguish states from
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}
with success probability bounded away form zero

p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}},\mathbf{M}_{PGM}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}))\geq 0.7\ .

(44)

Importantly, since states
\Psi_{i}
are Haar random, they are typically highly entangled and this causes them to be indistinguishable by separable measurements. To show this we will use a result that can be found in the proof of Theorem 2 form
[
65
]
, which states that for Haar random pure state
\Psi
with probability
P\leq\mathrm{exp}(-N^{2})

\max_{\phi\in\mathrm{SEP}}\tr(\phi\Psi)\geq\frac{8N^{2}}{2^{N}}\ ,

(45)

where the maximum is over all separable pure states on
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. Now, since states
\Psi_{i}
are iid Haar-random, by the union bound, we get that with probability
q\geq 1-2^{N}\mathrm{exp}(-N^{2})
, in the limit of large dimensions

\forall_{i}\ \max_{\phi\in\mathrm{SEP}}\tr(\phi\Psi_{i})\leq\frac{8N^{2}}{2^{N}}\ .

(46)

The above bound (which holds with high probability as
N\rightarrow\infty
) can be the used to upper bound the success probability of discrimination of states from the the ensemble. Note that for every separable effect
N_{i}
we have
\tr(\Psi_{i}N_{i})\leq\tr(N_{i})\max_{\phi\mathrm{SEP}}\tr(\phi\Psi_{i})\leq\tr(N_{i})\frac{8N^{2}}{2^{N}}
. Using this we obtain

p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}},\mathbf{N})\leq\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{N}}\tr(N_{i})\frac{2^{N}}{8N^{2}}=\frac{8N^{2}}{2^{N}}\ .

(47)

Finally, combining (
44
), (
47
), and the operational characterisation of
R_{\mathrm{Sep}(N)}
we obtain the desired lower bound by realizing that there exist a realization of a random ensemble
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}
such that

\max_{\mathbf{M}}R_{\mathrm{Sep}(N)}(\mathbf{M})\geq\frac{p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}},\mathbf{M}_{PGM}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}}))}{\max_{\mathbf{N}\in\mathrm{Sep}(N)}p_{\mathrm{succ}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Haar}},\mathbf{N})}-1\geq c\frac{2^{N}}{8N^{2}}-1\ .

(48)

∎

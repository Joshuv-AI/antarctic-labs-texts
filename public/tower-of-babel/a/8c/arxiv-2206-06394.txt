Stable anisotropic minimal hypersurfaces in 𝐑 4 \mathbf{R}^{4}
Otis Chodosh, Chao Li

Abstract
. We show that a complete, two-sided, stable immersed anisotropic minimal hypersurface in 𝐑 4 \mathbf{R}^{4} has intrinsic cubic volume growth, provided the parametric elliptic integral is C 2 C^{2} -close to the area functional. We also obtain an interior volume upper bound for stable anisotropic minimal hypersurfaces in the unit ball. We can estimate the constants explicitly in all of our results. In particular, this paper gives an alternative proof of our recent stable Bernstein theorem for minimal hypersurfaces in 𝐑 4 \mathbf{R}^{4} . The new proof is more closely related to techniques from the study of strictly positive scalar curvature.

By plugging the position vector field into (

), we obtain the following isoperimetric type inequality.

Corollary 15
.

Suppose
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary, and the image of
\partial M
is contained in
B_{\mathbf{R}^{n+1}}(0,\rho)
for some
\rho>0
. Then

|M|\leq\frac{\rho\|\Phi\|_{C^{1}(S^{n})}}{n\cdot\min_{\nu\in S^{n}}\Phi(\nu)}|\partial M|.

Proof.

Recall that
r(x)=\dist_{\mathbf{R}^{n+1}}(x,0)
. Plug
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}e_{i}
, the position vector field in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
, into (
3
). Then
\Div_{M}X=n
, and

D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu=\sum_{i}(D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}x_{i})(e_{i}\cdot\nu)=\sum_{i}(D\Phi(\nu)^{T}\cdot e_{i})(e_{i}\cdot\nu)=D\Phi(\nu)^{T}\cdot\nu=0.

On the other hand,
|X(x)|\leq r(x)
. Thus, we find (using
\nu,\eta
orthonormal)

\int_{M}n\Phi(\nu)\leq\int_{\partial M}\|\Phi\|_{C^{1}(S^{n})}|X|\leq\rho\|\Phi\|_{C^{1}(S^{n})}|\partial M|.

This completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma generalizes the traced Gauss equation
R=-|A|^{2}
(valid for minimal hypersurfaces) to the case of
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary hypersurfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
, under the assumption that
D^{2}\Phi(\nu)
is sufficiently pinched.

Lemma 16
.

Suppose
\Phi
satisfies (
1
) and
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
is
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary. Then at each point on
M
, the induced scalar curvature satisfies
R\leq 0
and

(4)

-R\leq|A|^{2}\leq-c_{0}R,

where

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1.09

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationarity can be written as
\tr_{M}(\Psi(\nu)S_{M})=0
. Diagonalizing
A_{M}
at a given point, write
k_{i}
for the principal curvatures of
M
and
e_{i}
for corresponding principal directions. Thus,
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationarity can be written as

0=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}k_{i}

where
a_{i}=D^{2}\Phi(\nu)[e_{i},e_{i}]
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}
. Note that the pinching assumption (
1
) yields

1\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}\leq\sqrt{2}.

We have
|A|^{2}=\sum k_{i}^{2}
,
R=2\sum_{i<j}k_{i}k_{j}
. Writing
k_{3}=-\frac{a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}}{a_{3}}
, we have

|A|^{2}=Q_{1}(k_{1},k_{2}):=\frac{a_{1}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{1}^{2}+\frac{2a_{1}a_{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{1}k_{2}+\frac{a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{2}^{2},

-R=Q_{2}(k_{1},k_{2}):=\frac{2a_{1}}{a_{3}}k_{1}^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3})}{a_{3}}k_{1}k_{2}+\frac{2a_{2}}{a_{3}}k_{2}^{2}.

By the Gauss equation, we have
R+|A|^{2}=H^{2}\geq 0
, and hence
|A|^{2}\geq-R
. Moreover, whenever
(a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3})^{2}<4a_{1}a_{2}
(which is guaranteed by, for instance,
a_{3}<4a_{1}
),
Q_{2}
is a positive definite quadratic form, and hence
-R
is nonnegative. Given that
\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}},\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
, (
4
) follows from Appendix
B
.
∎

3.2.
Second variation

Suppose now that
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable.
In Section
A.2
we derive the following second variation formula.

(5)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbf{\Phi}(M_{t})=\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla u,\Psi(\nu)\nabla u\right\rangle-\tr_{M}\left(\Psi(\nu)S_{M}^{2}\right)u^{2},

where
u\nu
is the variation vector field. Note that stability and (
5
) implies that

(6)

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}-\Lambda|A|^{2}u^{2}\geq 0

for all
u\in C_{c}^{1}(M\setminus\partial M)
. Here,
\Lambda
depends on ellipticity of
\mathbf{\Phi}
. It is important to observe that if
\Phi
satisfies (
1
) then
\Lambda\geq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and in particular

(7)

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}u^{2}\geq 0

for all

.

3.3.
Sobolev inequality and its consequences

In this section, we assume that
n\geq 3
,
M^{n}
is a two-sided
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable hypersurface immersed in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
, where
\mathbf{\Phi}
is a general anisotropic elliptic integral. The Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality
[
31
]
implies that for any
f\in C_{c}^{1}(M)
,

C_{n}\left(\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla f|+|fH|.

(See also
[
5
]
.)

Replacing
f
by
f^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}
, we find:

(8)

C_{n}\left(\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\int_{M}\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}|f|^{\frac{n}{n-2}}|\nabla f|+|f|^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}|H|.

By the Hölder inequality,

\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}|H|\leq\left(\int_{M}f^{2}H^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

The
\Phi
-stability inequality implies

\int_{M}f^{2}H^{2}\leq n\int_{M}f^{2}|A|^{2}\leq C(\Phi)\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}.

Now we use the Hölder inequality on the first term of the right hand of (
8
) and conclude the following Sobolev inequality:

(9)

\left(\int_{M}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C(n,\Phi)\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}.

Corollary 17
.

Suppose
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable. Assume that
B_{M}(p,\rho)\subset M
has compact closure. Then,

|B_{M}(p,\rho/2)|\geq C(n,\Phi)\rho^{n}.

Proof.

For any
u\in C^{1}(M)
such that
u\geq 0
and
\Delta u\geq 0
, the Sobolev inequality (
9
) and the standard Moser iteration implies that, for any
\theta\in(0,1)
,
s>0
,

\sup_{B_{M}(p,\theta\rho)}u\leq C(n,\theta,\Phi,s)\left(\rho^{-n}\int_{B_{M}(p,\rho)}u^{s}\right)^{1/s}.

The result follows by taking
u=1
,
s=1
and
\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 18
.

The use of Sobolev inequality for volume lower bound was first used by Allard
[
2
, 7.5]
.

Corollary 19
.

Suppose
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is two-sided complete,
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable, and
K
is a compact subset of
M
. Then each unbounded component of
M\setminus K
has infinte volume.

Proof.

Let
E
be an unbounded component of
M\setminus K
. Suppose the contrary, that
|E|<V<\infty
. Choose
\rho
such that
C(n,\Phi)\rho^{n}>V
. By completeness, there exists
p\in E
such that
d_{M}(p,\partial E)>\rho
. Then we have

V>|E|>|B_{M}(p,\rho)|>C(n,\Phi)\rho^{n}>V,

a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Combining (
9
) and Corollary
19
, the same argument as used by Cao–Shen–Zhu
[
6
]
implies the following result:

Corollary 20
.

If
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is complete, two-sided
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable immersion with at least two-ends, then there is a bounded non-constant harmonic function on

with finite Dirichlet energy.

4.
One-endedness

Through this section we assume that
n=3
,
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
is
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable. By analyzing harmonic functions on
M
, we will show that
M
has only one end, if
\mathbf{\Phi}
satisfies (
1
) (following
[
44
,
6
]
).

Lemma 21
.

Suppose that
M^{3}
is a complete, two-sided,
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stationary and stable immersion in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
, and
u
is a harmonic function on
M
. Then

(10)

(\Lambda-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\int_{M}\varphi^{2}|A|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\varphi^{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2},

for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(M)
. Here
\Lambda=\Lambda(\Phi)
is the constant in (
6
).

Proof.

Fix
p\in M
. Let
k_{i}
be the principal curvatures,
e_{i}
be the corresponding orthonormal principal directions diagonalizing
A_{M}
.

We first show that for any immersed hypersurface
M^{3}
in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
, equipped with the induced metric,
p\in M
, and any unit vector
v\in T_{p}M
, we have

\Ric(v,v)\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}.

Write
v=\sum y_{i}e_{i}
. Then
\sum y_{i}^{2}=1
. By the Gauss equation, we have

\Ric(e_{i},e_{j})=\sum_{k}\textrm{Rm}(e_{i},e_{k},e_{k},e_{j})=\sum_{k}\left(A(e_{k},e_{k})A(e_{i},e_{j})-A(e_{i},e_{k})A(e_{j},e_{k})\right),

and thus
\Ric(e_{i},e_{j})=0
when
i\neq j
, and
\Ric(e_{i},e_{i})=\sum_{j\neq i}A(e_{i},e_{i})A(e_{j},e_{j})
. Therefore,

\Ric(v,v)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}A(e_{j},e_{j})A(e_{i},e_{i})y_{i}^{2}=k_{1}(k_{2}+k_{3})y_{1}^{2}+k_{2}(k_{3}+k_{1})y_{2}^{2}+k_{3}(k_{1}+k_{2})y_{3}^{2}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz and the AM-GM inequality,

k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}\geq k_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k_{2}+k_{3})^{2}\geq-\sqrt{2}k_{1}(k_{2}+k_{3})\\
\Rightarrow\quad k_{1}(k_{2}+k_{3})\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{2}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}.

Similarly,

k_{2}(k_{3}+k_{1})\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2},\quad k_{3}(k_{1}+k_{2})\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}.

Therefore,

(11)

\Ric(v,v)\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}\sum_{i}y_{i}^{2}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}.

Applying this to
\nabla u
, we conclude that:

\Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A_{M}|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since
M
is
\Phi
-stable, (
6
) yields

\int_{M}\Lambda|A|^{2}\varphi^{2}\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(M).

Replacing
\varphi
by
|\nabla u|\varphi
, we have:

(12)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}|A|^{2}&\leq\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\int_{M}\left(\varphi|\nabla u|\left\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla|\nabla u|\right\rangle+\varphi^{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\right)\\
&=\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\int_{M}\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|\Delta|\nabla u|.\end{split}

By the improved Kato inequality,

|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\geq\frac{3}{8}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}.

Combined with the Bochner formula and (
11
), we have:

(13)

\begin{split}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}&=2\Ric_{M}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\\
&\geq-\sqrt{2}|A|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}|^{2}.\end{split}

Thus,

(14)

\Delta|\nabla u|\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A|^{2}|\nabla u|+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{-1}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

(
10
) follows from (
12
) and (
14
).

∎

Proposition 22
.

Suppose
\Phi
satisfies (
1
). Then any complete, two-sided,
\Phi
-stable immersion
M^{3}
in
\mathbf{R}^{4}
has only one end.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary, that
M
has at least two ends. Then Corollary
20
implies that
M
admits a nontrivial harmonic function
u
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C<\infty
. For
\rho>0
, take
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M)
such that
\varphi|_{B_{M}(0,\rho)}=1
,
\varphi|_{B_{M}(0,2\rho)}=0
, and
|\nabla\varphi|\leq\tfrac{2}{\rho}
. Then (
10
) implies that

\int_{B_{M}(0,\rho)}(\Lambda-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})|A|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\rho^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{4C}{\rho^{2}}.

Here
\Lambda\geq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
by (
1
). Sending
\rho\to\infty
, we conclude that

|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\equiv 0.

In particular, this implies that
|\nabla u|
is a constant. Since
u
is nonconstant, we have that
|\nabla u|>0
. However, this implies that

\int_{M}1=\frac{1}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty,

contradicting Corollary
19
.
∎

5.
A conformal deformation of metrics

Take
M^{3}\to\mathbf{R}^{4}
to be
\mathbf{\Phi}
-stable, where
\Phi
satisfies (
1
). In this section we carry out the conformal deformation technique used by Gulliver-Lawson
[
26
]
on
M
.

Consider the function
r(x)=\dist_{\mathbf{R}^{n+1}}(0,x)
on
M
, and the position vector field
\vec{X}
. Then
\Delta\vec{X}=\vec{H}
. Thus,
\Delta(r^{2})=\Delta(\sum x_{i}^{2})=2\vec{X}\cdot\Delta X+2|\nabla\vec{X}|^{2}=2\vec{X}\cdot\vec{H}+2n
. We find:

\Delta r=\frac{n}{r}+H(\hat{x}\cdot\nu)-\frac{|\nabla r|^{2}}{r},

here
\hat{x}=\frac{\vec{X}}{|\vec{X}|}
is the normalized position vector.

Suppose that
w>0
is a smooth function on
M^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. On
M\setminus\{0\}
, define
\tilde{g}=w^{2}g
. For
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
,
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M\setminus\{0\})
consider the quadratic form

\mathcal{Q}_{w}(\varphi)=\int_{M}\left(|\tilde{\nabla}\varphi|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{R}-\lambda)\varphi^{2}\right)d\tilde{\mu},

where
\tilde{\nabla}
,
\tilde{R}
,
\tilde{\mu}
are the gradient, the scalar curvature and the volume form with respect to
\tilde{g}
, respectively. One relates the geometric quantities in
g
and
\tilde{g}
as follows:

|\nabla\varphi|_{g}^{2}=w^{2}|\tilde{\nabla}\varphi|_{\tilde{g}}^{2},\quad d\mu=w^{-n}d\tilde{\mu}.

Moreover, we have

w^{2}\tilde{R}=R-2(n-1)\Delta\log w-(n-1)(n-2)|\nabla\log w|^{2}.

Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{w}(\varphi):=\mathcal{Q}_{w}(w^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\varphi)
. We compute:

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{w}(\varphi)\\
&=\int_{M}\left(w^{-2}|\nabla(w^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\varphi)|_{g}^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{R}-\lambda)w^{2-n}\varphi^{2}\right)w^{n}d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(w^{n-2}|w^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\nabla\varphi-\tfrac{n-2}{2}\varphi w^{-\frac{n}{2}}\nabla w|_{g}^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}\tilde{R}-w^{2}\lambda)\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi-\tfrac{n-2}{2}\varphi\nabla\log w|_{g}^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}\tilde{R}-w^{2}\lambda)\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|_{g}^{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}\left\langle\nabla(\varphi^{2}),\nabla\log w\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}|\nabla\log w|_{g}^{2}\varphi^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}\tilde{R}-w^{2}\lambda)\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|_{g}^{2}+\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\Delta\log w+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}|\nabla\log w|_{g}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}\tilde{R}-w^{2}\lambda\right)\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|_{g}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\varphi^{2}-\left(\frac{n}{2}\left(\Delta\log w+\tfrac{(n-2)}{2}|\nabla\log w|_{g}^{2}\right)+w^{2}\lambda\right)\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu.\end{split}

We now choose
w=r^{-1}
on
M\setminus\{0\}
. Note that (dropping the
g
subscript on the norm of the gradient)

\begin{split}\Delta\log w+\frac{n-2}{2}|\nabla\log w|^{2}&=-\frac{\Delta r}{r}+\frac{n}{2}\frac{|\nabla r|^{2}}{r^{2}}\\
&=-\frac{n}{r^{2}}-\frac{H(\hat{x}\cdot\nu)}{r}+\frac{n+2}{2}\frac{|\nabla r|^{2}}{r^{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(15)

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{w}(\varphi)=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\varphi^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}\left(n+rH(\hat{x}\cdot\nu)-\tfrac{n+2}{2}|\nabla r|^{2}\right)-\lambda\right)r^{-2}\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&\geq\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\varphi^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}\left(n-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta r^{2}H^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\beta}-\tfrac{n+2}{2}|\nabla r|^{2}\right)-\lambda\right)r^{-2}\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}R-\tfrac{n}{4}\beta H^{2})\varphi^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}\left(n-\tfrac{1}{2\beta}-\tfrac{n+2}{2}|\nabla r|^{2}\right)-\lambda\right)r^{-2}\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu,\end{split}

for
\beta>0
to be chosen later.

By the Gauss equation and Lemma
16
,

H^{2}=|A|^{2}+R\leq(1-c_{0})R.

Combining with
|\nabla r|\leq 1
, we have

(16)

\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{w}(\varphi)\geq\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{n}{4}\beta(c_{0}-1))R\varphi^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{2}(\tfrac{n-2}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\beta})-\lambda\right)r^{-2}\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu.

On the other hand, (
7
) and (
4
) imply that for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M)
,

\int_{M}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}R\varphi^{2}\right)d\mu\geq 0.

Note that
R\leq 0
. Thus, by choosing

\beta=\frac{4(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{2})}{n(c_{0}-1)},\quad\lambda=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{1}{2\beta}\right)=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n(c_{0}-1)}{8(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{2})}\right),

we have that
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{w}(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M\setminus\{0\})
. We summarize these in the following Proposition.

Proposition 23
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
,
(M^{n},g)
is an immersed hypersurface in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
\Lambda,c_{0}\in\mathbf{R}
, such that:

\int_{M}(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\Lambda R\varphi^{2})dV_{M}\geq 0,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(M),

\Lambda>\tfrac{1}{2},\quad c_{0}\geq 1,\quad|A|^{2}\leq-c_{0}R_{M}.

Then the conformally deformed manifold
(M\setminus\{0\},\tilde{g}=r^{-1}g)
satisfies

\lambda_{1}(-\tilde{\Delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{R})\geq\lambda,

where
\lambda=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n(c_{0}-1)}{8(\Lambda-\frac{1}{2})}\right)
.

6.
Volume estimates

We first recall a diameter bound for warped
\mu
-bubbles in
3
-manifolds satisfying
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+\tfrac{1}{2}R)\geq\lambda>0
.

Lemma 24

(Warped
\mu
-bubble area and diameter bound)
.

Let
(N^{3},g)
be a
3
-manifold with compact connected boundary satisfying

(17)

\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+\tfrac{1}{2}R)\geq\lambda>0.

Suppose there exists
p\in N
such that
d_{N}(p,\partial N)\geq\frac{5\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
. Then there exists a connected open set
\Omega
containing
\partial N
,
\Omega\subset B_{\frac{5\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}(\partial N)
, such that each connected component of
\partial\Omega\setminus\partial N
is a
2
-sphere with area at most
\tfrac{8\pi}{\lambda}
and intrinsic diameter at most
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

This is an application of estimates for the warped
\mu
-bubbles (see, e.g.
[
8
, Section 3]
). Since
N
satisfies (
17
), there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(N)
,
u>0
in
\mathring{N}
, such that

(18)

\Delta_{N}u\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}(2\lambda-R_{N})u.

Take
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M)
to be a smoothing of
d_{N}(\cdot,\partial N)
such that
|\Lip(\varphi_{0})|\leq 2
, and
\varphi_{0}=0
on
\partial N
. Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
such that
\varepsilon,\frac{4}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\pi+2\varepsilon
are regular values of
\varphi_{0}
. Define

\varphi=\frac{\varphi_{0}-\varepsilon}{\frac{4}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}-\frac{\pi}{2},

\Omega_{1}=\{x\in N:-\tfrac{\pi}{2}<\varphi<\tfrac{\pi}{2}\}
, and
\Omega_{0}=\{x\in N:-\tfrac{\pi}{2}<\varphi\leq 0\}
. We have that
|\Lip(\varphi)|<\tfrac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}
. In
\Omega_{1}
, define
h(x)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\tan(\varphi(x))
. By a direct computation, we have

(19)

\lambda+h^{2}-2|\nabla h|\geq 0.

Minimize

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial\Omega}ud\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})hud\mathcal{H}^{3},

among Caccioppoli sets
\Omega
in
\Omega_{1}
with
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{0}
is compactly contained in
\Omega_{1}
. By
[
8
, Proposition 12]
, a minimizer
\tilde{\Omega}
exists and has regular boundary. We take
\Omega
to be the connected component of
\{x\in N:0\leq\varphi_{0}(x)\leq\varepsilon\}\cup\tilde{\Omega}
that contains
\partial N
(in other words, we disregard any component of
\tilde{\Omega}
that is disjoint from
\partial N
). We verify that
\Omega
satisfies the conclusions of Lemma
24
.

Indeed, for any connected component
\Sigma
of
\partial\Omega\cap\Omega_{1}
, the stability of
\mathcal{A}

Lemma 25
.

Below,

(1)

\tilde{g}
-unit speed, connecting

Appendix A
First and second variation

We derive first and second variations of
\mathbf{\Phi}
with emphasis on our geometric applications (see also
[
16
, Appendix A]
and
[
53
, Section 2]
). For
M^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
a two-sided immersion, set

\mathbf{\Phi}(M):=\int_{M}\Phi(\nu)

for
\Phi:\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\to(0,\infty)
an elliptic integrand.

A.1.
First variation

Consider a
1
-parameter family of surfaces
M_{t}
with normal speed at
t=0
given by
u\nu
(with
u\in C^{1}_{c}(M\setminus\partial M)
). Recall that
\dot{\nu}=-\nabla u
. We find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbf{\Phi}(M_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(Hu\Phi(\nu)-D_{\nabla u}\Phi(\nu)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(H\Phi(\nu)+\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu)^{T})\right)u

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(H\Phi(\nu)+\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu)-(D_{\nu}\Phi(\nu))\nu)\right)u

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(H\Phi(\nu)+\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu))-(D_{\nu}\Phi(\nu))H\right)u.

Now, we note that we have that
D\Phi(\nu)\cdot\nu=\Phi(\nu)
by Euler theorem for homogeneous functions. Thus, we find that

(21)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbf{\Phi}(M_{t})=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu))u.

Thus,

(22)

H_{\Phi}=\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu)).

vanishes if and only if
M
is a critical point of
\mathbf{\Phi}
. Let us rewrite this as follows (with
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
a local orthonormal frame for
M
):

\displaystyle\Div_{M}(D\Phi(\nu))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{e_{i}}D\Phi(\nu))\cdot e_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}D^{2}\Phi(\nu)[D_{e_{i}}\nu,e_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}D^{2}\Phi(\nu)[S_{\Sigma}(e_{i}),e_{i}],

for
S_{M}
the shape operator of
M
. Let us define
\Psi(\nu):T\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\to T\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
by
\Psi(\nu):X\mapsto D^{2}\Phi(\nu)[X,\cdot]
. (This is just the
(1,1)
-tensor associated to
D^{2}\Phi(\nu)
via the Euclidean metric.)

Then, we find

(23)

H_{\Phi}=\tr_{M}(\Psi(\nu)S_{M})

Note that for
\Phi(\nu)=|\nu|
, we have

D\Phi(\nu)=|\nu|^{-1}\nu,\Psi(\nu)=|\nu|^{-1}\Id-|\nu|^{-3}\nu\otimes\nu^{\flat}

so in particular, when
|\nu|=1
, we find
\Psi(\nu)|_{T_{p}\Sigma}=\Id_{T_{p}\Sigma}
. Thus, this recovers the usual mean curvature.

A.2.
Second variation

Recall the tube formula:

\dot{S}=-\nabla^{2}u-S^{2}u

(where we are regarding
\nabla^{2}u
as a
(1,1)
-tensor via
g_{M}
). Note also that the trace of a
(1,1)
-tensor is independent of the metric. Thus, we find

\dot{H}_{\Phi}=\tr_{M}(-\Psi(\nu)\nabla^{2}u-\Psi(\nu)S_{M}^{2}u+\Psi(\nu)^{\prime}S_{M})

Note that

\Psi(\nu)^{\prime}=-(D_{\nabla u}\Psi)(\nu)

Hence,

\dot{H}_{\Phi}=\tr_{M}(-\Psi(\nu)\nabla^{2}u-\Psi(\nu)S_{M}^{2}u-(D_{\nabla u}\Psi)(\nu)S_{M}).

Integration on
M
gives

(24)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbf{\Phi}(M_{t})=\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla u,\Psi(\nu)\nabla u\right\rangle-\tr_{M}\left(\Psi(\nu)S_{M}^{2}\right)u^{2}.

Thus, stability implies that

(25)

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}-\Lambda|A|^{2}u^{2}\geq 0,\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{1}(M\setminus\partial M).

Here
\Lambda
depends on ellipticity of
\Phi
. In particular, if
\Phi
satisfies (
1
), then (
5
) implies that for
\Phi
-stable surfaces
M
, we have

(26)

\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|A_{M}|^{2}u^{2}\geq 0,\quad\forall u\in C_{c}^{1}(M\setminus\partial M).

Note that when
\Phi(X)=|X|
, we have seen that
\Psi(Y)=|Y|^{-1}\Id-|Y|^{-3}Y\otimes Y^{\flat}
. Hence,

D_{X}\Psi(\nu)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\Psi(\nu+X)=2(X\cdot\nu)\Id-X\otimes\nu^{\flat}-\nu\otimes X^{\flat}

In particular,
D_{\nabla u}\Psi(\nu)|_{T_{p}M}=0
. Thus, we recover the standard second variation formula in this case.

A.3.
First variation through vector fields

We also deduce first variation formula of
\mathbf{\Phi}
through variations that are not necessarily normal to
M
. We compute as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Phi(\nu)\Div_{M}X

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Phi(\nu)\Div_{M}X^{T}+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(\Phi(\nu)X^{T})-\nabla(\Phi(\nu))\cdot X^{T}+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(\Phi(\nu)X^{T})-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}\nu\cdot X^{T}+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(\Phi(\nu)X^{T})-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X^{T}\cdot\nu+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(\Phi(\nu)X^{T})+D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}(X\cdot\nu)-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Div_{M}(\Phi(\nu)X^{T})+\Div((X\cdot\nu)D\Phi(\nu)^{T})-(X\cdot\nu)\Div_{\Sigma}D\Phi(\nu)^{T}

\displaystyle\qquad-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-(X\cdot\nu)\Div_{M}D\Phi(\nu)^{T}-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu+\Phi(\nu)(X\cdot\nu)H

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\partial M}\Phi(\nu)X\cdot\eta+(X\cdot\nu)D\Phi(\nu)\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-(X\cdot\nu)\Div_{M}D\Phi(\nu)-D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu+\int_{\partial M}\Phi(\nu)X\cdot\eta+(X\cdot\nu)D\Phi(\nu)\cdot\eta.

Thus, we find that if
H_{\Phi}=0
, then

(27)

\int_{M}\Phi(\nu)\Div_{M}X+D_{D\Phi(\nu)^{T}}X\cdot\nu=\int_{\partial M}\Phi(\nu)X\cdot\eta+(X\cdot\nu)D\Phi(\nu)\cdot\eta.

Appendix B
Some computations for quadratic forms

In this section we explicitly compute the constant
c_{0}
appeared in Lemma
16
. The approach is elementary.

Lemma 27
.

Let
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}
be positive constants such that
\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}}\leq\sqrt{2}
.
Consider quadratic forms

Q_{1}(k_{1},k_{2})=\frac{a_{1}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{1}^{2}+\frac{2a_{1}a_{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{1}k_{2}+\frac{a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}{a_{3}^{2}}k_{2}^{2},

Q_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})=\frac{2a_{1}}{a_{3}}k_{1}^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+a_{2}-a_{3})}{a_{3}}k_{1}k_{2}+\frac{2a_{2}}{a_{3}}k_{2}^{2}.

Then we have
Q_{1}\leq c_{0}Q_{2}
, where

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\approx 1.09.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=\frac{a_{1}}{a_{3}}
,
\beta=\frac{a_{2}}{a_{3}}
, with
2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
. Then

(28)

Q_{1}(k_{1},k_{2})=(1+\alpha^{2})k_{1}^{2}+2\alpha\beta k_{1}k_{2}+(1+\beta^{2})k_{2}^{2}\\
=(1+\alpha^{2})\left(k_{1}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{1+\alpha^{2}}k_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1+\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}k_{2}^{2}.

Under the substitution
x=k_{1}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{1+\alpha^{2}}k_{2}
,
y=k_{2}
, we have
k_{1}+k_{2}+(-\alpha k_{1}-\beta k_{2})=(1-\alpha)x+\frac{1-\beta-\alpha\beta+\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}y
.
Thus, by Cauchy-Schwartz,

\displaystyle(Q_{1}-Q_{2})(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=(k_{1}+k_{2}-\alpha k_{1}-\beta k_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=\left((1-\alpha)x+\frac{1-\beta-\alpha\beta+\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}y\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left((1+\alpha^{2})x^{2}+\frac{1+\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}y^{2}\right)=c_{1}Q_{1}(k_{1},k_{2}),

where
c_{1}=\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}+\left(\frac{1-\beta-\alpha\beta+\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}
. This gives
Q_{1}\leq\frac{1}{1-c_{1}}Q_{2}
. Using
2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
, we have:

c_{1}\leq\frac{(1-2^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}}{1+\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\frac{1-2^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{1+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}}{2}=\frac{3}{2}-\sqrt{2}.

The result follows.
∎

Globalization of Partial Actions of Ordered Groupoids on Rings
Wesley G. Lautenschlaeger and Thaísa Tamusiunas

Abstract
. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition to the existence of an ordered globalization of a partial ordered action of an ordered groupoid on a ring and we also present criteria to obtain uniqueness. Furthermore, we apply those results to obtain a Morita context and to show that an inverse semigroup partial action has a globalization (unique up to isomorphism) if, and only if, it is unital.

Primary. 20L05, 18B40, 20N02. Secondary. 16S35, 20M18

Our main goal in this work is to analyze the existence and uniqueness of ordered globalizations of partial ordered actions of ordered groupoids on rings. In Section 2 we provide a background about partial ordered actions of ordered groupoids on rings and fix some notations. In Section 3 we prove that a partial ordered action of an ordered groupoid on a ring has a globalization if, and only if, it is unital. In Section 4 we define strong partial ordered actions, pseudoassociative groupoids and minimal globalizations, showing that a unital strong partial ordered action of a pseudoassociative groupoid on a ring always has a unique minimal globalization. Finally, in Section 5 we present two applications: we construct a Morita context between the crossed products of an ordered groupoid acting partially on a ring and its globalization and we apply our results in the case of inverse semigroups acting on rings via the Ehresmann-Schein-Nambooripad (ESN) Theorem
[

Throughout, rings and algebras are associative and unital unless otherwise stated.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall that a
groupoid

\mathcal{G}
is a small category in which every morphism is an isomorphism. We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{0}
the set of objects of
\mathcal{G}
. Observe that
\text{id}:\mathcal{G}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
, given by
\text{id}(x)=\text{id}_{x}
, is an injective map and whence we identify
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. Given
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the
domain
and the
range
of
g
will be denoted by
d(g)
and
r(g)
, respectively. Hence,
d(g)=g^{-1}g
and
r(g)=gg^{-1}
. For all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
, we write
\exists gh
whenever the product
gh
is defined. We denote
\mathcal{G}_{2}=\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}:\exists gh\}
.

We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
ordered
if there is a partial order
\leq
on
\mathcal{G}
such that:

(OG1)

If
g\leq h
then
g^{-1}\leq h^{-1}
;

(OG2)

For all
g,h,k,\ell\in\mathcal{G}
such that
g\leq h
,
k\leq\ell
,
\exists gk
and
\exists h\ell
, we have
gk\leq h\ell
;

(OG3)

Given
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
such that
e\leq d(g)
, there is a unique element
(g|e)\in\mathcal{G}
which satisfies
(g|e)\leq g
and
d(g|e)=e
.

(OG3*)

Given
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
such that
e\leq r(g)
, there is a unique element
(e|g)\in\mathcal{G}
which satisfies
(e|g)\leq g
and
r(e|g)=e
.

In a partially ordered set
P
, we say that
z\in P
is the
meet
of
x\in P
and
y\in P
if
z\leq x
,
z\leq y
and if
w\in P
is such that
w\leq x,y
then
w\leq z
. We denote
z=x\wedge y
. A poset where every pair of elements has a meet is called a
meet semilattice
. If
\mathcal{G}
is such that
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a meet semilattice with respect to
\leq
, we say that
\mathcal{G}
is an
inductive
groupoid.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ordered groupoid and
A
a ring. We say that
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a partial action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
if
A_{r(g)}
is an ideal of
A
,
A_{g}
is an ideal of
A_{r(g)}
,
\alpha_{g}:A_{g^{-1}}\to A_{g}
is an isomorphism of rings, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and

(P1)

\alpha_{e}=\text{Id}_{A_{e}}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, and
A\subseteq\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
;

(P2)

\alpha_{h}^{-1}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})\subset A_{(gh)^{-1}}
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
;

(P3)

\alpha_{g}\circ\alpha_{h}(a)=\alpha_{gh}(a)
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2},a\in\alpha_{h}^{-1}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})
.

The condition
A\subseteq\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
is required to recover the concept of partial group actions on rings, once if
G
is a group with identity element
1_{G}
acting partially on a ring
A
via
\alpha
, then
A_{1_{G}}=A
and
\alpha_{1_{G}}=\text{Id}_{A}
. Similar conditions have already appeared in some other papers, such as
[
9
]
(on the notion of a “split”
\mathcal{G}
-set) and in (P4) of
[
23
]
.

By
[
4
, Lemma 1.1]
, it is also true that:

(i)

\alpha_{g}^{-1}=\alpha_{g^{-1}}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

(ii)

\alpha_{g}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})=A_{g}\cap A_{gh}
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

We say that a partial action
\alpha
is
unital
if each
A_{g}
is a unital ring with central and idempotent identity
1_{g}
. Clearly,
A_{g}=A1_{g}=1_{g}A
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. We say that
\alpha
is
preunital
if
A_{e}
is a unital ring with central and idempotent identity
1_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

Furthermore,
\alpha
is said to be a
partial ordered action
, or simply
P.O. action
, if

(PO)

If
g\leq h
, then
A_{g}\subseteq A_{h}
and
\alpha_{g}=\alpha_{h}|_{A_{g^{-1}}}
.

Two P.O. actions
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
and
\gamma=(C_{g},\gamma_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
C
are
isomorphic
(we denote by
\alpha\simeq\gamma
) if there is a collection of ring isomorphisms
\{\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to C_{e}:e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}\}
such that

(i)

\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})=C_{g}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
,

(ii)

\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}(a)=\gamma_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}(a)
, for all
a\in A_{g^{-1}}
.

A global action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
is a partial action such that
A_{g}=A_{r(g)}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Ordered global actions and unital global actions are defined analogously. Of course a global action is unital if and only if it is preunital.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ordered groupoid and
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
a P.O. action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. We define the
partial skew groupoid ring

A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
by
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}=\bigoplus_{g\in\mathcal{G}}A_{g}\delta_{g}
, where each
\delta_{g}
is a symbol. We consider the usual sum and the product is given by

\displaystyle(a_{g}\delta_{g})(b_{h}\delta_{h})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{g}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a_{g})b_{h})\delta_{gh},\text{ if }\exists gh,\\
0,\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

linearly extended. We have that
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
is a ring but it is not necessarily associative nor unital. A condition to ensure the associativity of
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
is to ask

\alpha
to be a unital partial action (c.f.
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
12
, Corollary 3.2]
). Also, by
[
3
, Propostion 3.3]
, if
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is finite, then
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
is unital with identity
1_{A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}1_{e}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ordered groupoid and
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
a P.O. action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
such that
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
is associative. Define the ideal
N
of
A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}
as
N=\langle a\delta_{g}-a\delta_{h}:g\leq h,a\in A_{g}\subseteq A_{h}\rangle
. We define the
partial skew ordered groupoid ring

A\ltimes_{\alpha}^{o}\mathcal{G}
as
A\ltimes_{\alpha}^{o}\mathcal{G}=\frac{A\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}}{N}
.

3.
Globalization

From now on, assume that all actions and partial actions are preunital, unless stated otherwise.

Let
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be an ordered action of an ordered groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
B
. Let
A
be a unital ideal of
B
. We shall construct a P.O. action
\alpha
of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
by restricting
\beta
. Consider, for each
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,
A_{e}:=A\cap B_{e}
, which is an ideal of
A
. Now, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, define
A_{g}:=A_{r(g)}\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})
, which is an ideal of
A_{r(g)}
and also define
\alpha_{g}=\beta_{g}|_{A_{g^{-1}}}
, which is a ring isomorphism.

Observe that if
e\leq f
are elements of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, then
B_{e}\subseteq B_{f}
, which implies
A_{e}=A\cap B_{e}\subseteq A\cap B_{f}=A_{f}
. Now, if
g\leq h
, then

\displaystyle A_{g}

\displaystyle=A_{r(g)}\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})\subseteq A_{r(h)}\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})

\displaystyle=A_{r(h)}\cap\beta_{h}(A_{d(g)})\subseteq A_{r(h)}\cap\beta_{h}(A_{d(h)})=A_{h},

and given arbitrary
a\in A_{g^{-1}}
,
\alpha_{h}(a)=\beta_{h}(a)=\beta_{g}(a)=\alpha_{g}(a)
, so
\alpha_{g}=\alpha_{h}|_{A_{g^{-1}}}
. Hence
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a P.O. action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
. We say that
\alpha
is the
standard restriction
of
\beta
to the ideal
A
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{s,s^{-1},r(s),d(s),e\}
with
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\{r(s),d(s),e\}
,
e\leq s,

and\leq s^{-1}
,

,

Let

\displaystyle B_{d(s)}=B_{s^{-1}}=Re_{1}\oplus Re_{2},\quad B_{r(s)}=B_{s}=Re_{2}\oplus Re_{3},\quad B_{e}=Re_{2}

Let

of

Remark 3.2
.

Every P.O. action
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
obtained by a standard restriction of an ordered action
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring

, where

is an ideal of
B
, satisfies the following condition: if

, then

. In fact,

\displaystyle A_{e}\cap A_{g}

\displaystyle=A_{e}\cap A_{r(g)}\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})=A_{e}\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})

\displaystyle=\beta_{g}(\beta_{g^{-1}}(A_{e}))\cap\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)})=\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)}\cap\beta_{g^{-1}}(A_{e}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))=\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)}\cap B_{r(g^{-1}|e)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{g}(A_{d(g)}\cap B_{d(e|g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))=\beta_{g}(A\cap B_{d(g)}\cap B_{d(e|g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{g}(A\cap B_{d(e|g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))=\beta_{g}(A_{d(e|g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{(e|g)}(A_{d(e|g)}\cap\beta_{(g^{-1}|e)}(A_{e}))=A_{e}\cap\beta_{(e|g)}(A_{d(e|g)})=A_{(e|g)}.

\beta
, which generalizes the standard one. Let
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be an ordered global action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring

. Consider a unital ideal

of

is an ideal of

, for all

. Consider

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a preunital P.O. action of an ordered groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. A unital ordered global action
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring

is said to be a
globalization
of

if for all

there is
an injective homomorphism of rings
\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to B_{e}
such that

(i)

\varphi_{e}(A_{e})
is an ideal of
B_{e}
;

(ii)

\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})=\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{r(g)})\cap\beta_{g}(\varphi_{d(g)}(A_{d(g)}))
, for all

;

(iii)

\beta_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}(a)=\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}(a)
, for all

and

(iv)

B_{g}=\sum_{r(h)\leq r(g)}\beta_{h}\left(\varphi_{d(h)}(A_{d(h)})\right)
.

Now we shall prove that a P.O. action has a globalization if, and only if, it is unital.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a preunital P.O. action of an ordered groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. Then
\alpha
has a globalization
\beta
if and only if
\alpha
is unital.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Once
\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})=\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{r(g)})\cap\beta_{g}(\varphi_{d(g)}(A_{d(g)}))
and
A_{r(g)}
and
A_{d(g)}
are unital, the assertion clearly holds.

(
\Leftarrow
): Assume that every
A_{g}
is unital with identity
1_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G},A)
be the ring of all maps
\mathcal{G}\to A
and define
\mathcal{G}_{g}=\{h\in\mathcal{G}:r(h)\leq r(g)\}
.

Take
F_{g}=\{f\in\mathcal{F}:f(h)=0,\text{ for all }h\notin\mathcal{G}_{g}\}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{g}=\mathcal{G}_{r(g)}
, it follows that
F_{g}=F_{r(g)}
. If
h\leq g
, then
r(h)\leq r(g)
, so
F_{h}\subseteq F_{g}
. Clearly
F_{g}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{F}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

Now fix the notation
f(h):=f|_{h}
, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
h\in\mathcal{G}
.

For
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
f\in F_{g^{-1}}
, define
\gamma_{g}:F_{g^{-1}}\to F_{g}
by

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}(f)|_{h}=\begin{cases}f((g^{-1}|r(h))h),\text{ if }h\in\mathcal{G}_{g},\\
0,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Notice that if
h\in\mathcal{G}_{g}
, then
r(h)\leq r(g)=d(g^{-1})
, and hence the restriction
(g^{-1}|r(h))
is defined, as well as the product
(g^{-1}|r(h))h
. Moreover,
r((g^{-1}|r(h))h)=r(g^{-1}|r(h))\leq r(g^{-1})
, from where it follows that
\gamma_{g}
is well defined. It is easy to see that
\gamma_{g}
is a ring homomorphism.

For
g\in\mathcal{G}
,
f\in F_{g^{-1}}
and
h\in\mathcal{G}_{g^{-1}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\gamma_{g^{-1}}\circ\gamma_{g}(f)|_{h}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{g}(f)|_{(g|r(h))h}=f((g^{-1}|r(g|r(h)))(g|r(h))h)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}f((g^{-1}g|r(h))h)=f((d(g)|r(h))h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{=}}f(r(h)h)=f(h),

where the equality (
*
) follows by
[
20
, Proposition 4.1.3]
and the equality (
**
) follows since
r(h)\leq d(g)
. Similarly
\gamma_{g}\circ\gamma_{g^{-1}}(f)|_{h}=f(h)
. Thus we have that
\gamma_{g}
is a ring isomorphism.

Observe that
\gamma_{e}=I_{F_{e}}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}

4.
Uniqueness

In this section we will give a sufficient condition for a P.O. action to have a unique globalization. In order to achieve this goal, we introduce the notion of a minimal globalization.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}

\mathcal{G}

\alpha

(iv’)

B_{g}=\sum_{r(h)=r(g)}\beta_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(A_{d(h)}))
, for all

,

holds.

Moreover, we introduce a specific type of P.O. action. Given an ordered groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we define the
pseudoproduct

*
as

\displaystyle g*h=\begin{cases}(g|d(g)\wedge r(h))(d(g)\wedge r(h)|h),\text{ if }d(g)\wedge r(h)\text{ is defined},\\
\text{undefined, otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a P.O. action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. We say that
\alpha
is a

[
16

(PS)

\alpha_{g}\circ\alpha_{h}=\alpha_{g*h}\circ\text{Id}_{A_{h^{-1}}}

g,h\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\exists g*h
.

Remark 4.3
.

Since (PS) holds, notice that
\text{dom}(\alpha_{g}\circ\alpha_{h})=\text{dom}(\alpha_{g*h}\circ\text{Id}_{A_{h^{-1}}})
, for all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\exists g*h
. But this is the same as saying that
\alpha_{g}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})=A_{g}\cap A_{g*h}
.

In the case where the action is global, we have that strong global ordered actions are precisely global ordered actions. In the case where
\mathcal{G}
is trivially ordered with the equality, the concepts of strong P.O. action and partial action coincide.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a strong P.O. action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring

. The following statements are true:

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Proof.

(i): If

.

Now

.

(ii): Notice that

by the uniqueness of restrictions. Hence

(iii): From (PS) it follows that

5.
Applications

We conclude by presenting two applications of the results of the previous sections. We first show that the partial skew ordered groupoid ring is Morita equivalent to the skew ordered groupoid ring relative to its globalization. Afterwards, as a consequence of the ESN Theorem, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a partial action of an inverse semigroup to have a (unique) minimal globalization.

5.1.
A Morita Context

To simplify the notation, in this subsection we will assume that
\varphi_{e}=\iota_{e}:A_{e}\to B_{e}

.

A

(ii)

On physical measures of multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields
Sylvain Crovisier, Xiaodong Wang, Dawei Yang, Jinhua Zhang

Abstract
Bonatti and da Luz have introduced the class of multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields to characterize systems whose periodic orbits and singularities do not bifurcate under perturbation (called star vector fields). In this paper, we study the Sinaï-Ruelle-Bowen measures for multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields: in a C 1 C^{1} open and C 1 C^{1} dense subset of multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields, each C ∞ C^{\infty} one admits finitely many physical measures whose basins cover a full Lebesgue measure subset of the manifold. Similar results are also obtained for C 1 C^{1} generic multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields.



1
Introduction

One aim in differentiable dynamical systems is to describe the behavior of most orbits of typical systems. From the ergodic viewpoint, this has been realized by Sinaï-Ruelle-Bowen
[
S
,
Bo
,
BoR
,
R1
]
for uniformly hyperbolic systems. In the case of hyperbolic vector fields, it has been proved by Bowen and Ruelle
[
BoR
]
that the forward orbit of Lebesgue almost every point in the manifold will converge to an ergodic measure
which belongs to a finite family of (physical) measures and which admits rich geometric and dynamical properties. Such measures are nowadays called Sinaï-Ruelle-Bowen (SRB) measures.

It is expected that such theory can be extended for large classes of systems beyond uniform hyperbolicity.
For instance this has been established for some sets of non-hyperbolic parameters with positive Lebesgue measure
inside the Hénon family. This also has led to many important progresses in the study of partially hyperbolic systems. However there is still no complete theory.

In the case of vector fields, Morales, Pacifico and Pujals
[
MPP
]
have defined the notion of “singular hyperbolicity” to characterize robust transitivity in dimension
3
and singular dynamics modeled on the Lorenz attractor. Singular hyperbolicity is still a form of partial hyperbolicity allowing the existence of singularities, i.e., points where the vector field vanishes. More recently, Bonatti and da Luz
[
BdL
]
have introduced the “multi-singular hyperbolicity” to describe a robust class of vector fields satisfying a strong stability property (the star property): bifurcation phenomena cannot occur on periodic orbits nor on singularities for any system
C^{1}
close. The star property has played an important role in the exploration of the structural stability conjecture of Palis and Smale.
It has been shown that the star property coincides with the uniform hyperbolicity in the case of diffeomorphisms
and non-singular vector fields
[
L2
,
M1
,
M2
,
H
,
GW
]
.

The dynamics of general star vector field is still striking due to the existence of singularities.
There are many examples of such systems which are not uniformly hyperbolic:
for instance, some Cherry flows
[
Ch
,
PdM
]
in dimension
2
and the famous Lorenz attractors
[
Lo
]

in dimension
3
(which supports robust chaotic non-hyperbolic dynamics).
In dimension 4, Shi, Gan and Wen
[
SGW
]
have proved that
C^{1}
generic star vector fields are singular hyperbolic.
In dimension
5
, da Luz
[
dL
]
has constructed a
C^{1}
open set of star vector fields which are not singular hyperbolic. From
[
BdL
]
it turns out that multi-singular hyperbolicity characterizes star vector fields for a
C^{1}
open and dense set of systems. The multi-singular hyperbolicity is specific to singular vector fields (i.e. has no counterpart for diffeomorphisms) and is not covered by partial hyperbolicity.

In this paper, we study the ergodic theory of multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields: we will prove that for a large set among star vector fields, there are finitely many physical SRB measures whose basins cover a set with full Lebesgue measure in the manifold.

Let
M
be a
C^{\infty}
closed Riemannian manifold. Denote by
{\cal X}^{r}(M)
(
r\geq 1
) the space of
C^{r}
vector fields on
M
.
As mentioned previously, a
C^{1}
vector field
X
is said to be
star
if there exists a
C^{1}
neighborhood
{\cal U}
of
X
such that, for any
Y\in{\cal U}
, all the critical elements (i.e. periodic orbits or singularities) of the flow generated by
Y
are hyperbolic. We denote by
{\cal X}^{*}(M)
the set of multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields on
M
endowed with
C^{1}
topology.
On a
C^{1}
open and dense subset of
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
, the star property is equivalent
[
BdL
]
to the multi-singular hyperbolicity, whose precise definition is stated in Section
2.2
below. In this paper we study the dynamics for a
C^{1}
open and dense set of vector fields in
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
, hence we do not have to distinguish star and multi-singular hyperbolic systems.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the flow generated by a vector field
X
.
A
\varphi
-invariant probability measure is said to be
physical
if it attracts a set with positive Lebesgue measure, i.e.,

{\rm Leb}\big(\operatorname{Basin}(\mu)\big)>0
, where

\operatorname{Basin}(\mu)=\bigg\{x\in M:\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\varphi_{s}(x)}\operatorname{d}s=\mu\bigg\}.

This is related to ergodic measures satisfying variational and geometric properties.
An ergodic measure
\mu
is said to be a
Sinaï-Ruelle-Bowen measure
(abbr. SRB measure) if it has at least one positive Lyapunov exponent and if its metric entropy equals the sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents. Note that SRB measures are defined in
C^{1}
-scenario, and this allows us to work on SRB measures in
C^{1}
-generic setting.
A classical theorem by Ledrappier and Young
[
LeYo
]
states that for
C^{2}
systems, the SRB property is equivalent to the absolute continuity of the conditional measures along the unstable manifolds which is usually taken as the definition of SRB measures for
C^{2}
systems.
For any
C^{2}
vector field, an ergodic hyperbolic SRB measure is physical
[
Le
, Théorème 4.9]
.

Theorem A
.

There exists a
C^{1}
open and dense subset
{\cal U}
of
{\cal X}^{*}(M)
such that for any
C^{\infty}
vector field
Y\in{\cal U}
, there exist
finitely many ergodic physical measures
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k}
such that

\operatorname{Leb}\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Basin}(\mu_{i})\big)=\operatorname{Leb}(M).

This property extends to
C^{1}
generic multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields.

Theorem B
.

There exists a dense G
δ
subset
{\cal G}
of
{\cal X}^{*}(M)
such that each
X\in{\cal G}
has finitely many ergodic physical measures
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{k}
such that

\operatorname{Leb}\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Basin}(\mu_{i})\big)=\operatorname{Leb}(M).

Beyond uniform hyperbolicity, the
C^{1}
generic ergodic theory was established for partially hyperbolic volume-preserving diffeomorphisms by Avila-Bochi-Wilkinson
[
ABW
]
, Avila-Bochi
[
AB
]
and Avila-Crovisier-Wilkinson
[
ACW
]
.
For dissipative cases, there are several results concerning the existence adnd finiteness of
physical measures for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with (1) mostly contracting/expanding center or (2) with one-dimensional center, among which we would like to mention that the results in

[
CYZ
,
HYY
]
are obtained via variational approach as in our paper.
Using SRB measures, our result characterizes a large class of dissipative vector fields including the famous Lorenz attractors and some others.

In Theorems
A
and
B

we have more information.
For any vector field
X
in the open set
{\cal U}
provided by Theorem
A
,
there exist finitely many attractors
\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{k}
, which are sinks or
singular hyperbolic attractors.
If moreover
X
is
C^{\infty}
or belongs to the dense G
δ
subset
{\cal G}

given by Theorem
B
,
the support of each ergodic physical measure
\mu_{i}
coincides with one of these attractors
\Lambda_{i}

and each attractor supports exactly one physical measure.
Moreover when
\Lambda_{i}
is not a sink,
\mu_{i}
is an SRB measure.
See the more detailed statement in Theorem
6.1
below.

Let us notice that in the particular case of
C^{2}
singular hyperbolic vector fields,
any system admits a finite collection of physical measures whose basins cover a set with full Lebesgue measure,
see
[
APPV
,
LeYa
,
CYZ
,
A
]
.

Let us discuss the main ingredients of our proofs.
There are several ways to build SRB measures for differentiable systems.
We follow a variational approach, which goes back to
[
CQ
,
Q
]

and which is well-adapted to systems admitting a dominated splitting, see
[
CCE
,
CaY
,
LeYa
,
CYZ
,
HYY
]
;
when there is no dominated splitting, results have been obtained for some smooth systems
using Yomdin theory
[
B1
,
B2
,
BCS
]
. Singular hyperbolic vector fields admit a dominated splitting,
which allowed
[
LeYa
,
CYZ
]
to build SRB measures in this case. For multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields,
there is no dominated splitting in general and we had to face a new difficulty.
In
[
B1
]
, the variational approach combined with Yomdin theory (for
C^{\infty}
vector fields) produces measures whose entropy
is linked with the (upper) Lyapunov exponents of points in a set with positive Lebesgue measure:
without dominated splitting, this is not enough to guarantee that these measures are SRB.
Under multi-singular hyperbolicity, one can blow-up each singularity and obtain a dominated splitting over a bundle
related to the initial tangent bundle, which allows us to check Pesin entropy formula.
This gives the following result for multi-singular hyperbolic sets.
For an invariant compact set
\Lambda
, its basin is defined as:

\operatorname{Basin}(\Lambda)=\big\{x\in M:\omega(x)\subset\Lambda\big\}.

Theorem C
.

Let
X\in{\cal X}^{\infty}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a multi-singular hyperbolic set which does not contain any sink.
If
\operatorname{Leb}\big(\operatorname{Basin}(\Lambda)\big)>0
, then there is an SRB measure supported on
\Lambda
.

This is the starting point for proving Theorems
A
and
B
.
Their proofs use the robustness of singular hyperbolic attractors (proved in
[
CY
]
)
and a characterization of attractors in the multi-singular hyperbolic setting
using bi-Pliss points (see Theorem
6.4
).

2
Fundamental properties of multi-singular hyperbolicity

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional closed manifold endowed with a Riemannian metric.
The set of transverse intersections between two submanifolds
V,W
will be denoted by
V\pitchfork W
.
Given a
C^{1}
vector field
X
on
M
, we denote by
(\varphi^{X}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
the flow generated by
X
and
(D\varphi_{t}^{X})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
the tangent flow. When there is no ambiguity, we will drop the index
X
for simplicity.
The
singular set
is
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X):=\{x:X(x)=0\}
.
A point
x
is
periodic
if it is non singular and satisfies
\varphi_{t}(x)=x
for some
t>0
.
A point
x
is a
critical point
if it is either a singularity or a periodic point.
The orbit of a critical point is a
critical element
.

For each point
x\in M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
, one can define the normal space at
x
,

{\cal N}^{X}(x)={\cal N}(x):=\{v\in T_{x}M:v\bot X(x)\}.

This gives a bundle
{\cal N}
over the set
M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
that is called the
normal bundle
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a
C^{1}
vector field
X
, the
linear Poincaré flow

(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is defined on
{\cal N}
by

\Psi_{t}(v)=D\varphi_{t}(v)-\frac{<D\varphi_{t}(v),X(\varphi_{t}(x))>}{\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|^{2}}\cdot X(\varphi_{t}(x))\textrm{~~ for any $x\in M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)$ and $v\in{\cal N}(x)$}.

In other terms, the linear Poincaré flow is defined as follow: for any vector in the normal bundle, one first sends it by the tangent flow and then projects it back to the normal bundle.

2.1
Chain recurrence classes

Conley’s theory
[
Co
]
decomposes the dynamics as follows.
One says that
x
is
chain attainable
from
y
(and one denotes
y\dashv x
),
if there exists
T>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0

there are
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}
with
k\geq 1
and
t_{i}>T
for
0\leq i<k
satisfying:

•

x_{0}=y
and
x_{k}=x
;

•

\operatorname{d}(\varphi_{t_{i}}(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<{\varepsilon}
for
i=0,\cdots,k-1.

A point
x
is
chain recurrent
if
x
is chain attainable from itself. We denote by
{\cal R}(X)
the set of chain recurrent points. One defines a relation
\sim
on
{\cal R}(X)
as follows

x\sim y\;\Leftrightarrow\;(y\dashv x~\text{ and }~x\dashv y).

This is a closed equivalence relation. For each point
x\in{\cal R}(X)
, the equivalence class containing
x
is called the
chain recurrence class
of
x
and will be denoted by
C(x)
.

The basin of an invariant compact set
\Lambda
is defined as

\operatorname{Basin}(\Lambda)=\big\{x\in M:\omega(x)\subset\Lambda\big\}.

An invariant compact set
\Lambda
is
attracting

if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Lambda
such that
\varphi_{t}(\overline{U})\subset U
for some
t>0

and
\Lambda=\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t}(U)
.
In this case, one can replace
U
by a neighborhood which is
trapping

i.e. which satisfies
\varphi_{t}(\overline{U})\subset{\rm Int}(U),\forall t>0
, see
[
Co
, Section 5.B]
.
An invariant compact set
\Lambda
is an
attractor
if
it is an attracting transitive set.

An invariant compact set
\Lambda
is
Lyapunov stable
if
for any neighborhood
U
of
\Lambda
, there exists a smaller neighborhood
V
of
\Lambda
such that

\varphi_{t}(V)\subset U
for any
t\geq 0
.

A
quasi-attractor
is a chain recurrence class which admits arbitrarily small trapping neighborhoods.

By
[
BC
, Proposition 1.7]
, for any vector field
X
in a dense G
δ
subset of
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
, a chain recurrence class
C
of
X
is a quasi-attractor if and only if
C
is Lyapunov stable; moreover it also gives
the following property (see also
[
MP
, Theorem A]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a dense G
δ
subset
{\cal G}
of
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
and for any
X\in{\cal G}
, there exists a dense G
δ
subset
{\cal R}_{X}\subset M
such that for any
x\in{\cal R}_{X}
, the limit set
\omega(x)
is a quasi-attractor.

The following result states a local version of this property.
It follows easily from previous works and its proof will be given in the appendix.

Theorem 2.3
.

There exists a dense G
δ
subset
{\cal G}\subset{\cal X}^{1}(M)
with the following property.
For any
X\in{\cal G}
and any hyperbolic critical element
\gamma
of
X
,
there is a dense G
δ
subset
R_{\gamma}
of
W^{u}(\gamma)
such that the
\omega
-limit set of any point
x\in R_{\gamma}
is a quasi-attractor.

2.2
Multi-singular hyperbolicity

We first recall the notions of hyperbolicity and singular hyperbolicity.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
. A
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant compact set
\Lambda
is
hyperbolic
if there exist a
(D\varphi_{t})
-invariant continuous splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E^{s}\oplus\mathbb{R}\cdot X\oplus E^{u}
and two constants
C>0,\lambda>0
satisfying the following properties.

•

E^{s}
is
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-
contracted
:

\|D\varphi_{t}|_{E^{s}(x)}\|\leq C\cdot e^{-\lambda t}~~\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$ and $t\geq 0$};

•

E^{u}
is
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-
expanded
:

\|D\varphi_{-t}|_{E^{u}(x)}\|\leq C\cdot e^{-\lambda t}~~\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$ and $t\geq 0$}.

Remark 2.5
.

1.

The sub-bundles
E^{s}
,
\mathbb{R}\cdot X
and
E^{u}
are allowed to be trivial. For instance, when
\Lambda
is reduced to a singularity
\sigma
(i.e.
X(\sigma)=0
), then the splitting is
T_{\sigma}M=E^{s}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{u}_{\sigma}
. When
\Lambda
is reduced to a critical element (a singularity or a periodic orbit)
such that
E^{s}=\{0\}
(resp.
E^{u}=\{0\}
), then
\Lambda
is a source (resp. sink).

2.

In the hyperbolic splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E^{s}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X\oplus E^{u}
, the dimension
\operatorname{dim}(E^{s})
is called the
index
of
\Lambda
.

In their study of robustly transitive singular sets, Morales, Pacifico and Pujals
[
MPP
]
introduced the notion of
singular hyperbolicity
which very well describes the structure of Lorenz attractors.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
. A
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant compact set
\Lambda
is
positively singular hyperbolic
if there exist a
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant splitting
T_{\Lambda}M=E^{ss}\oplus E^{cu}
, and two numbers
C>1
and
\lambda>0
satisfying the following properties.

•

Contraction of
E^{ss}
:
\|D\varphi_{t}|_{E^{ss}(x)}\|\leq C\cdot e^{-\lambda t}
for all
x\in\Lambda
and
t\geq 0
.

•

Domination:
\|D\varphi_{t}|_{E^{ss}(x)}\|\cdot\|D\varphi_{-t}|_{E^{cu}(\varphi_{t}(x))}\|\leq C\cdot e^{-\lambda t}
for all
x\in\Lambda
and
t\geq 0
.

•

Sectional expansion of
E^{cu}
: for any
x\in\Lambda
and any 2-dimensional linear subspace
P\subset E^{cu}(x)
, one has

|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{t}|_{P})|\geq C^{-1}\cdot e^{\lambda t}~\textrm{ for $t\geq 0$}.

An invariant compact set is
negatively singular hyperbolic
if it is positively singular hyperbolic with respect to
-X
. An invariant compact set is
singular hyperbolic
if it is positively or negatively singular hyperbolic.
A vector field is singular hyperbolic if each of its chain recurrence class is either singular hyperbolic or a hyperbolic singularity.

The notion of Lorenz like singularity plays an important role.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
.
A singularity
\sigma
is
Lorenz like
, if
\sigma
admits a
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
with
\operatorname{dim}E^{c}_{\sigma}=1
such that
the largest Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\sigma}^{ss}
along
E_{\sigma}^{ss}
, the smallest Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\sigma}^{uu}
along
E_{\sigma}^{uu}
and the center Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\sigma}^{c}
along
E^{c}
satisfy

0<|\lambda_{\sigma}^{c}|<\min\{-\lambda_{\sigma}^{ss},\lambda_{\sigma}^{uu}\}.

Remark 2.8
.

Every Lorenz like singularity
\sigma
is hyperbolic
and admits a strong stable manifold
W^{ss}(\sigma)
tangent to
E^{ss}_{\sigma}
and a strong unstable manifold

W^{uu}(\sigma)
tangent to
E^{uu}_{\sigma}
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and let
\Lambda
be an invariant compact set for the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Given
\eta,T>0
, a
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
is an
(\eta,T)
-dominated splitting for the linear Poincaré flow
if
the spaces
{\cal E}(x)
(resp.
{\cal F}
) have the same dimension and

\|\Psi_{T}|_{{\cal E}(x)}\|\cdot\|\Psi_{-T}|_{{\cal F}(\varphi_{T}(x))}\|\leq e^{-\eta}\textrm{ for any $x\in\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)$}.

One says that an invariant compact set
\Lambda
admits a dominated splitting for the linear Poincaré flow if it admits an
(\eta,T)
-dominated splitting for some
\eta,T>0
. Similarly, one can define the dominated splitting for the tangent flow
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

The dominated splitting of the linear Poincaré flow may not be robust due to the existence of singularities.
This leads to the notion of
singular domination
defined in
[
CdLYZ
, Definition 1.1]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be an invariant compact set of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. One says that
\Lambda
admits a
singular domination
if
there exists a splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
such that:

•

the splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
is dominated for the linear Poincaré flow
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
;

•

each
\sigma\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{Sing}(X)
satisfies one of the following properties:

–

either
\sigma
admits a dominated splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus F_{\sigma}
for
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, where
E^{ss}_{\sigma}
is uniformly contracting with
\operatorname{dim}(E^{ss}_{\sigma})=\operatorname{dim}({\cal E})
and
W^{ss}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda=\{\sigma\}
;

–

or
\sigma
admits a dominated splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
for
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, where
E^{uu}_{\sigma}
is uniformly expanding with
\operatorname{dim}(E^{uu}_{\sigma})=\operatorname{dim}({\cal F})
and
W^{uu}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda=\{\sigma\}
.

Recall that when
\operatorname{dim}(M)\leq 4
generic star vector fields are singular hyperbolic
[
MPP
,
SGW
]
, while when
\operatorname{dim}(M)\geq 5
da Luz
[
dL
]
constructed generic star vector fields that are not singular hyperbolic. To characterize star vector fields, Bonatti-da Luz
[
BdL
]
introduced the important notion of
multi-singular hyperbolicity
and proved that star is equivalent to multi-singular hyperbolic open and densely.
Later on,
[
CdLYZ
]
gave an alternative definition which is equivalent to the one given by Bonatti-da Luz under a mild condition (see Theorems C and D in
[
CdLYZ
]
) and which is based on the notion of singular domination.
This is the definition that we will use here.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
X\in{\cal X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be an invariant compact set of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. One says that
\Lambda
is
multi-singular hyperbolic
if either it is a hyperbolic singularity or periodic orbit, or if:

•

\Lambda
admits a singular domination
{\cal N}^{s}\oplus{\cal N}^{u}
with
\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{s})>0
and
\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{u})>0
;

•

each
\sigma\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{Sing}(X)
is Lorenz like with a splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
satisfying
\operatorname{dim}E^{ss}_{\sigma}=\operatorname{dim}{\cal N}^{s}
and
\operatorname{dim}E^{uu}_{\sigma}=\operatorname{dim}{\cal N}^{u}
;

•

there exist a compact isolating neighborhood
V
of
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)\cap\Lambda
and
\eta,T>0
such that for any
x\in\Lambda
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

x,\varphi_{t}(x)\notin V
and
t>T
, one has

~\|\Psi_{-t}|_{{\cal N}^{u}(\varphi_{t}(x))}\|<e^{-\eta t}\textrm{ and }\|\Psi_{t}|_{{\cal N}^{s}(x)}\|<e^{-\eta t}.

(1)

Lemma 2.11
.

A quasi attractor
\Lambda
which is multi-singular hyperbolic and not a sink, is singular hyperbolic.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda
is not a sink, it is not reduced to a singularity or to a periodic orbit.
Let us consider a singularity
\sigma
in
\Lambda
. By Definitions
2.7
and
2.10

it is Lorenz-like, hyperbolic and admits a splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
with
\operatorname{dim}(E^{c}_{\sigma})=1
,

\operatorname{dim}(E^{ss}_{\sigma})=\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{s})
and
\operatorname{dim}(E^{uu}_{\sigma})=\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{u})
,
where
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)}={\cal N}^{s}\oplus{\cal N}^{u}

is the multi-singular hyperbolic splitting on
\Lambda
.
Since
\Lambda
is chain-transitive and not reduced to
\sigma
,
both sets
W^{s}(\sigma)
and
W^{u}(\sigma)

intersect
\Lambda\setminus\{\sigma\}

(we say that
\sigma
is active).

We claim that the center Lyapunov exponent of
\sigma
is negative.
Otherwise
W^{s}(\sigma)
has dimension
\operatorname{dim}(E^{ss}_{\sigma})
,
so
W^{ss}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda\neq\{\sigma\}
.
Definition
2.9
then implies that

W^{uu}(\sigma)\cap\Lambda=\{\sigma\}
.
But this is a contradiction since
\Lambda
is a quasi-attractor.

Thus
\Lambda
only contains Lorenz-like singularities with negative center Lyapunov exponent. In other words, all singularities in
\Lambda
have the same index.
Then one can conclude by
[
CdLYZ
, Theorem C]
.
∎

It is not difficult to see that a hyperbolic set is always singular hyperbolic and a singular hyperbolic set is always multi-singular hyperbolic. The differences appear when the set admits singularities.
One has the following result from
[
BdL
]
and
[
CdLYZ
, Theorem C]
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X\in{\cal X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a multi-singular hyperbolic set.
If
\Lambda\cap\operatorname{Sing}(X)=\emptyset
, then
\Lambda
is hyperbolic.

A
C^{1}
vector field
X
is called
multi-singular hyperbolic
if each chain recurrence class is a multi-singular hyperbolic set. Denote by
{\cal X}^{*}(M)
the space of
C^{1}
multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields, endowed the induced topology from
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
.

2.3
Properties of hyperbolic vector fields

Let
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
be two (non-singular) hyperbolic periodic orbits of
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
. One says that
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are
homoclinically related
if the stable manifold of one periodic orbit has non-empty transverse intersection with the unstable manifold of the other, and vice versa. The
homoclinic class
of a hyperbolic periodic orbit
\gamma
is defined as

H(\gamma)=\overline{\bigcup\{\gamma^{\prime}:\textrm{$\gamma^{\prime}$ is homoclinically related to $\gamma$}\}}.

It is classical that
H(\gamma)
is a transitive set (see for instance
[
N1
]
).

One summarizes the following well-known result (see for instance
[
W2
]
):

Proposition 2.13
.

Given
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
,
each hyperbolic singularity-free chain recurrence class is a homoclinic class and all the periodic orbits contained inside are homoclinically related.

An attractor contains the unstable manifolds of its hyperbolic periodic points. Conversely, one has the following:

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a hyperbolic chain recurrence class which is singularity-free.
If
\Lambda
contains the unstable manifold of a hyperbolic periodic orbit
\gamma
, then
\Lambda
is an attractor.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.13
,
\Lambda=H(\gamma)
, and as a consequence
\Lambda
is transitive.
By definition,
H(\gamma)\subset\overline{W^{u}(\gamma)}
, and by the assumption
\overline{W^{u}(\gamma)}\subset\Lambda
, one has
\Lambda=H(\gamma)=\overline{W^{u}(\gamma)}
.

Since
\Lambda
is hyperbolic and coincides with
\overline{W^{u}(\gamma)}
,
the shadowing lemma implies that for any two small neighborhoods
U,V
of
\Lambda

there exists
T>0
such that
\varphi_{t}(U)\subset V
for any
t>T
.
This implies that
\Lambda
is attracting, hence is an attractor.
∎

The following is a classical result proved in
[
BoR
, Theorem 5.6]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

If
X
is a
C^{2}
vector field, then each hyperbolic singularity-free chain recurrence class
\Lambda

is either an attractor or satisfies
{\rm Leb}(\{x:\omega(x)\subset\Lambda\})=0
.

Remark 2.16
.

One can provide a proof of this proposition in another way: by Proposition
2.13
, the set
\Lambda
is a hyperbolic homoclinic class hence it is transitive; if
\operatorname{Leb}(\{x:\omega(x)\subset\Lambda\})>0
, by Theorem F in
[
CYZ
]
and Theorem A in
[
LeYo
]
, there exists a point
x\in\Lambda
such that the unstable manifold
W^{u}(x)
of
x
is contained in
\Lambda
. Since
\Lambda
is hyperbolic and transitive, the set
\Lambda
is saturated by unstable manifolds, hence it is an attractor.

2.4
Number of chain recurrence classes

Proposition 2.17
.

Multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields have at most countably many chain recurrence classes.

Proof.

Since the singularities are hyperbolic, there exist at most finitely many singularities; thus, there are only finitely many chain recurrence classes containing singularities.
Any singularity-free chain recurrence class is hyperbolic by Proposition
2.12
, hence contains a hyperbolic periodic orbit. Since a vector field has at most countably many hyperbolic periodic orbits, it has at most countably many singularity-free chain recurrence classes.
∎

Recall that every multi-singular hyperbolic vector field satisfies the star condition: no non-hyperbolic critical element can be created by
C^{1}
small perturbations
[
BdL
]
.
For star vector fields, S. Liao
[
L3
]
proved the finiteness of the set of sinks (see also
[
YZ
, Theorem A]
). Thus one has the following theorem.

Theorem 2.18
.

Multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields have at most finitely many sinks.

2.5
Lyapunov exponents and hyperbolic measures

Given an invariant measure, Oseledec theorem
[
O
]
associates at almost every point
x

some numbers
\lambda_{i}(x)
called
Lyapunov exponents of
x
.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
X
be a
C^{1}
vector field and
\mu
be an invariant measure. There is a
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant measurable set
\Gamma
with
\mu(\Gamma)=1
, such that for any
x\in\Gamma
, there are
k=k(x)
numbers
\lambda_{1}(x)<\lambda_{2}(x)<\cdots<\lambda_{k}(x)
and a splitting
T_{x}M=E_{1}(x)\oplus E_{2}(x)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{k}(x)

satisfying:

•

the splitting is invariant:

D\varphi_{t}(E_{i}(x))=E_{i}(\varphi_{t}(x))
for any
x\in\Gamma
, any
1\leq i\leq k
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
;

•

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|D\varphi_{t}(v)\|=\lambda_{i}(x)
, for any
x\in\Gamma
,
1\leq i\leq k
and any unit vector
v\in E_{i}(x)
.

If
\mu
is ergodic, then
k
and
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k}
are constant
\mu
-almost everywhere.

An invariant measure is
regular
if
\mu(\operatorname{Sing}(X))=0
. A regular ergodic measure is said to be
hyperbolic
if it admits only one zero Lyapunov exponent (along the direction
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X
).

The
index

\operatorname{Ind}(\mu)
of a hyperbolic ergodic measure
\mu
is defined as the number of its negative Lyapunov exponents (counting the multiplicities), i.e. the dimension of the space
\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda_{i}<0}E_{i}
.
The index
\operatorname{Ind}(\gamma)
of a hyperbolic critical element
\gamma

is defined as the index of the unique invariant measure supported on
\gamma
.
This coincides with the index of hyperbolic sets defined in Remark
2.5
.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and let
\mu
be an invariant measure of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
For
\mu
-almost every point
x
,
the
sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents
is defined as

\sum\lambda^{+}(x):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\max(\lambda_{i}(x),0)\cdot\operatorname{dim}E_{i}(x).

If
\mu
is ergodic,
\sum\lambda^{+}(x)
is constant
\mu
-almost everywhere:
one sets
\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\sum\lambda^{+}(x)
.

Under a dominated splitting, the following property holds.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and let
\mu
be a regular ergodic measure of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

satisfying:

•

there is a dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}

for the linear Poincaré flow;

•

the number of positive Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
equals
\operatorname{dim}{\cal F}
.

Then

\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{1}|_{{\cal F}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X})|{\rm d}\mu=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(\Psi_{1}|_{{\cal F}})|{\rm d}\mu.

Proof.

The direct sum of all bundles with positive Lyapunov exponents are contained in
{\cal F}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X
. Since the flow direction provides zero Lyapunov exponents,

\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{1}|_{{\cal F}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X})|{\rm d}\mu.

On the other hand, by the definition of the linear Poincaré flow
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,

\int\log|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{1}|_{{\cal F}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X})|{\rm d}\mu=\int\left(\log|\operatorname{det}(\Psi_{1}|_{{\cal F}})|+\log\|D\varphi_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}\cdot X}\|\right){\rm d}\mu.

Using again
\displaystyle\int\log\|D\varphi_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}\cdot X}\|{\rm d}\mu=0
, one gets

\int\log|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{1}|_{{\cal F}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X})|{\rm d}\mu=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(\Psi_{1}|_{{\cal F}})|{\rm d}\mu.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the special case of a multi-singular hyperbolic set, Proposition
2.21
gives:

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a multi-singular hyperbolic set with the splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{s}\oplus{\cal N}^{u}
. Then any regular ergodic measure
\mu
supported on
\Lambda
is hyperbolic and

\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(\Psi_{1}|_{{\cal N}^{u}})|\operatorname{d}\mu.

(2)

For a positively singular hyperbolic set
\Lambda
,
(
2
) becomes:

\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)=\int\log|\operatorname{det}(D\varphi_{1}|_{E^{cu}})|{\rm d}\mu.

2.6
Invariant manifolds and homoclinic classes

Given a point
x
, its stable set is defined to be

W^{s}(x)=\big\{y\in M:~\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{t}(y))=0\big\}.

This stable set is not enough in the study of flow due to the existence of shears. One has to define the stable set of an orbit: given a point
x
, define

W^{s}({\rm Orb}(x))=\big\{y\in M:~\exists\text{~a homeomorphism~}\theta:~{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}~\textrm{s.t.}~\lim_{t\to+\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(y))=0\big\}.

When
X
is
C^{2}
and
\mu
is a hyperbolic measure,
Pesin theory associates to
\mu
-almost every point
x

a stable manifold which is tangent to the sum of the Oseledets spaces
E_{i}(x)

associated to negative Lyapunov exponents and which coincides with the stable set
W^{s}(x)
;
moreover the stable set of the orbit
\gamma
of
x
is the union of stable sets of points in
\gamma
:

W^{s}(\gamma)=\bigcup_{y\in\gamma}W^{s}(y).

This applies in particular to hyperbolic periodic orbits.
Analogously, one defines the unstable set or manifold of points
W^{u}(x)
and orbits
W^{u}(\gamma)
.

We say that a hyperbolic ergodic measure
\mu
and a hyperbolic periodic orbit
\gamma
are

homoclinically related
if for
\mu
-almost every point
x
,
the stable manifold
W^{s}(x)
has non-empty transverse intersections with the unstable manifold of
\gamma
,
and the unstable manifold
W^{u}(x)
has non-empty transverse intersections with the stable manifold of
\gamma
.

The next result is the flow version of the classical result of Katok
[
K
]
whose proof can be found in
[
LL
]
(see also
[
SGW
, Theorem 5.6]
under star condition). In
[
LL
]
, the authors carefully combine Pesin theory with Liao’s theory on scaled tubular neighborhoods
due to a lack of uniform continuity caused by the singularities. One can also employee
[
PYY1
, Section 2.3]
and Liao’s shadowing lemma proven by Gan
[
G
]
to prove the following result.

Theorem 2.23
.

Let
\mu
be a regular hyperbolic ergodic measure of a
C^{2}
vector field
X
.
Then there is a hyperbolic periodic orbit
\gamma
such that

•

\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)\subset H(\gamma)
.

•

\mu
is homoclinically related with
\gamma
; in particular, for
\mu
-almost every point
x
, the iterates
\varphi_{t}(W^{u}(x))
accumulate on
W^{u}(\gamma)
when
t\to+\infty
.

2.7
Metric entropy and SRB measures

Given a diffeomorphism
f
on
M
and an
f
-invariant measure
\mu
,
one associates the Kolmogorov-Sinaï metric entropy
{\rm h}_{\mu}(f)
.
Given the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
M
and a
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant measure
\mu
, the metric entropy of the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is defined as the entropy
{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})
of the time one map
\varphi_{1}
. Indeed, one has that
{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{t})=|t|\cdot{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})
for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
CFS
, §6,Theorem 3]
).

We recall Ruelle’s inequality
[
R2
]
.

Theorem 2.24
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{1}(M)
and assume that
\mu
is an invariant measure of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
Then,

{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})\leq\int\sum\lambda^{+}(x)\operatorname{d}\mu.

An ergodic measure
\mu
is
SRB
if it has at least one positive Lyapunov exponent
and
{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})=\sum\lambda^{+}(\mu)
.
A general invariant measure is SRB if almost all its ergodic components are SRB.

Ledrappier and Young have shown
[
LeYo
, Theorem A]
that the above definition of an SRB measure
is equivalent to another one, which is defined more geometrically and is usually taken as the definition
of an SRB measure. Here we state the version for vector fields.

Theorem 2.25
.

Let
X
be a
C^{2}
vector field and
\mu
be an ergodic measure exhibiting positive Lyapunov exponents. Then
\mu
is SRB if and only if
\mu
admits positive Lyapunov exponents and the conditional measures of
\mu
along Pesin unstable manifolds are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.

The following consequence is similar to
[
T
, Proposition D]
.

Corollary 2.26
.

Given a
C^{2}
vector field
X
and a hyperbolic SRB measure
\mu
, there is a hyperbolic periodic orbit
\gamma
such that

W^{u}(\gamma)\subset\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a hyperbolic periodic orbit given by Theorem
2.23

and
x
be a typical point of
\mu
. Since
\mu
is SRB, by Theorem
2.25
, Lebesgue almost every point in the unstable manifold
W^{u}(x)
of
x
is typical for
\mu
. This implies that
W^{u}(x)\subset\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)
. By Theorem
2.23
,
W^{u}(\gamma)
is accumulated by
\varphi_{t}(W^{u}(x))
when
t\to\infty
. Thus,
W^{u}(\gamma)
is contained in
\operatorname{Supp}(\mu)
.
∎

2.8
Singular hyperbolic attractors

An invariant compact set
\Lambda
is
robustly transitive
for
X

if it admits a neighborhood
U
such that
\Lambda=\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi^{X}_{t}(U)

and for any vector field
Y
that is
C^{1}
close to
X
, the set

\Lambda^{Y}=\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi^{Y}_{t}(U)
is transitive for
Y
.

Theorem 2.27

(
[
CY
]
)
.

There exists an open and dense subset
{\cal U}
of
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
such that for any
X\in{\cal U}
,
if
\Lambda
is a singular hyperbolic Lyapunov stable chain recurrence class, then
\Lambda
is a robustly transitive attractor; moreover
\Lambda=H(\gamma)
for any periodic orbit
\gamma\subset\Lambda
.

Remark 2.28
.

A singular hyperbolic attractor is always positively singular hyperbolic, see for instance
[
CY
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Under singular hyperbolicity,
[
PYY1
]
shows that the entropy is upper semi-continuous:

Theorem 2.29
.

Let
\Lambda
be a singular hyperbolic attractor of a
C^{1}
vector field
X
. Assume that
\{X_{n}\}
is a sequence of vector fields such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{n}=X
in
{\cal X}^{1}(M)
and that each
X_{n}
has an invariant measure
\mu_{n}
such that
\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}
is supported on
\Lambda
. Then,

{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1}^{X})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\rm h}_{\mu_{n}}(\varphi_{1}^{X_{n}}).

Theorem
2.29
is based on
[
PYY1
, Theorem A]
which asserts that if
\Lambda
is a singular hyperbolic set for a
C^{1}
vector field, then there is
\delta>0
such that on a neighborhood of
\Lambda
, the dynamics is robustly

[

]
, a robust uniform entropy expansiveness implies the upper semi-continuity of the entropy as in Theorem

.

3.1
Pliss points

For a periodic orbit
\gamma

\pi(\gamma)
its period.
Liao has proved the following fundamental property of star vector fields (with singularities or not).
We will apply it to multi-singular hyperbolic vector fields.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
L1
]
)
.

For any
X\in{\cal X}^{*}(M)
, there exist
\eta,T>0
and a
C^{1}
neighborhood
{\cal U}
of
X
with the following properties. For any

\gamma
of

(2\eta,T)

\prod_{i=0}^{[\pi(\gamma)/T]-1}\|\Psi^{Y}_{T}|_{{\cal N}^{s}(\varphi^{Y}_{iT}(p))}\|\leq e^{-\eta\pi(\gamma)}\textrm{\>~and~\>}\prod_{i=0}^{[\pi(\gamma)/T]-1}\|\Psi^{Y}_{-T}|_{{\cal N}^{u}(\varphi^{Y}_{-iT}(p))}\|\leq e^{-\eta\pi(\gamma)}

for each

One also considers a
rescaled linear Poincaré flow
(\Psi_{t}^{*})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

x

v\in{\cal N}_{x}
and

\Psi_{t}^{*}(v)=\frac{\Psi_{t}(v)}{\|D\varphi_{t}|_{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x)}\|}.

also holds for the rescaled linear Poincaré flow

At each singularity

4.4
Dominated splittings of the extended linear Poincaré flow

Let
\Lambda
be a
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
invariant compact set and
\widehat{\Lambda}\subset\widehat{M}

its blow-up. A
(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant splitting
\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}

if there are
\eta,T>0
such that

\|\widehat{\Psi}_{T}|_{\widehat{\cal E}({L})}\|\cdot\|\widehat{\Psi}_{-T}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{T}({L}))}\|\leq e^{-\eta},~~~\textrm{ for any ${L}\in\widehat{\Lambda}$}.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\Lambda\subset M
has a dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
for
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, if the singularities in
\Lambda
are hyperbolic and if any singularity
\sigma\in{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)\cap\Lambda

satisfies
\operatorname{dim}(E^{s}_{i}(\sigma))\geq\operatorname{dim}({\cal E})
or
\operatorname{dim}(E^{u}_{j}(\sigma))\geq\operatorname{dim}({\cal F})
, where

T_{\sigma}M=E^{s}_{i}(\sigma)\oplus E^{c}(\sigma)\oplus E^{u}_{j}(\sigma)
is the splitting
introduced in (
3
),
then the blow-up
\widehat{\Lambda}\subset\widehat{M}
admits a dominated splitting
\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}
for
(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
such that

\Theta(\widehat{\cal E}({\widehat{x}}))={\cal E}(x)\;\text{ and }\;\Theta(\widehat{\cal F}({\widehat{x}}))={\cal F}(x)\;\;\text{ for any $x\in\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)$}.

(4)

Remark 4.6
.

If
\Lambda
is multi-singular hyperbolic and if
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)}={\cal N}^{s}\oplus{\cal N}^{u}

is the associated dominated splitting, then for any singularity
\sigma\in\Lambda
,
either
E^{ss}(\sigma)
or
E^{uu}(\sigma)
is escaping, and

\operatorname{dim}(E^{ss}(\sigma))=\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{s})
,
\operatorname{dim}(E^{uu}(\sigma))=\operatorname{dim}({\cal N}^{u})
.
So the Lemma can be applied and gives a dominated splitting

\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\cal N}^{s}\oplus\widehat{\cal N}^{u}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Since
\Theta
is an isometry between fibers of

\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{M}\setminus\theta^{-1}({\operatorname{Sing}}(X))}
and
{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X))}
,
the dominated splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}

induces a dominated splitting

\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{M}\setminus\theta^{-1}({\operatorname{Sing}}(X))}=\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}

satisfying (
4
). This dominated splitting extends to the closure
of
\big\{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x):~x\in\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)\big\}
, so by definition of

\widehat{\Lambda}
, it remains to show that there exists a dominated splitting
over each compact set
\big\{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot v:~v\in E^{c}(\sigma)\setminus\{0\}\big\}\subset\widehat{M}
for each
\sigma\in\Lambda\cap{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
. We now fix such a singularity
\sigma
.
We can assume
\operatorname{dim}(E^{c}(\sigma))>0
otherwise the set
\big\{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot v:~v\in E^{c}(\sigma)\setminus\{0\}\big\}

is empty and there is nothing to prove.

We will consider the case
\operatorname{dim}E_{j}^{u}(\sigma)\geq\operatorname{dim}({\cal F})
;
the other case
\operatorname{dim}E_{i}^{s}(\sigma)\geq\operatorname{dim}({\cal E})
can be treated similarly.
Since
E^{c}(\sigma)
is non-trivial and
\sigma
is hyperbolic,
\sigma
is accumulated by points of
\Lambda

along some direction in
E^{c}(\sigma)
.
Then, the argument in
[
GY

(using
\operatorname{dim}E_{j}^{u}(\sigma)\geq\operatorname{dim}({\cal F})
)
implies that the Oseledets splitting at
\sigma

where
\operatorname{dim}E^{uu}(\sigma)=\operatorname{dim}({\cal F})
and the moduli of the eigenvalues associated to

E^{wu}(\sigma)
are smaller than the moduli of the eigenvalues associated to

E^{uu}(\sigma)
. Possibly
\operatorname{dim}(E^{wu}(\sigma))=0

Now we consider any line
L\subset E^{c}(\sigma)

\widehat{\cal F}(L)
to be the orthogonal space of
L
in
L\oplus E^{uu}(\sigma)

Since
E^{uu}(\sigma)

\widehat{\cal F}(L^{\prime})=\widehat{\Psi}_{t}(\widehat{\cal F}(L))
. We define in a similar way

\widehat{\cal E}(L)
to be the orthogonal space of
L

E^{ss}_{i}(\sigma)\oplus E^{c}(\sigma)\oplus E^{wu}(\sigma)

We also have

\widehat{\cal E}(L^{\prime})=\widehat{\Psi}_{t}(\widehat{\cal E}(L))
.

Now we check the domination. We have the following estimates:

•

Let us write
E^{cs}(\sigma):=E^{ss}_{i}(\sigma)\oplus E^{c}(\sigma)\oplus E^{wu}(\sigma)

Since
\widehat{\cal E}(L)
is contained in the invariant space

the map
\widehat{\Psi}_{t}|_{\widehat{\cal E}(L)}
is obtained from

by an orthogonal projection and

\|\widehat{\Psi}_{t}|_{\widehat{\cal E}(L)}\|\leq\|D\varphi_{t}|_{E^{cs}(\sigma)}\|

for any

•

Since the angle between

and

there exists

such that
for any

and any

\|\widehat{\Psi}_{t}|_{\widehat{\cal E}(L)}\|\cdot{\|\widehat{\Psi}_{-t}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{t}(L))}\|}\leq C\cdot{\|D\varphi_{t}|_{E^{cs}(\sigma)}\|}\cdot{\|D\varphi_{-t}|_{E^{uu}(\sigma)}\|}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\eta t},

for some

and any

This proves that the decomposition

\widehat{\cal N}=\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}
is dominated above the set

\theta^{-1}(\sigma)\cap\widehat{\Lambda}
.

.

4.5
Dynamics at Lorenz like singularities

We recall that the blow-up of a multi-singular hyperbolic set
\Lambda

admits a dominated splitting for
(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, see Lemma
4.5
and Remark
4.6
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\sigma
be a singularity in a multi-singular hyperbolic set
\Lambda
with the splitting
{\cal N}_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}={\cal N}^{s}\oplus{\cal N}^{u}

and let
L\in\theta^{-1}(\sigma)\cap\widehat{\Lambda}
. Then

•

either
L\oplus\widetilde{\Theta}(\widehat{\cal N}^{u}_{L})=E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
,

•

or
L\subset E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}(\widehat{\cal N}^{u}_{L})\cap(E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

We first claim that
L\subset(E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma})\cup(E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma})
.
Indeed if this is not the case, then there exists a sequence
x_{n}\to\sigma
in
\Lambda\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)

such that
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x_{n})\to L
. This implies that
x_{n}
accumulates
\sigma
along a direction
not included in
(E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma})\cup(E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma})
.
Using the dominated splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}

one deduces that
x_{n}
have iterates converging to some point in
W^{ss}(\sigma)

and other iterates converging to some point in
W^{uu}(\sigma)
.
This contradicts the definition of singular domination on
\Lambda

(Definition
2.9
).

In the case where
L\subset E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
,
let
{\cal F}^{s}_{L}
be the orthogonal complement to
E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
inside
T_{\sigma}M

and let
{\cal F}^{u}_{L}
be the orthogonal complement to
L
inside
E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
.
After identification by
\widehat{\Theta}
, we get a decomposition
{\cal F}^{s}_{L}\oplus{\cal F}^{u}_{L}

of
\widehat{\cal N}_{L}
which is mapped by
\widehat{\Psi}_{t}

to the corresponding decomposition of
\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\varphi}_{t}(L)}
for each
t
since

E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
is invariant.
This shows that
{\cal F}^{s}_{L}=\widehat{\Theta}({\cal N}^{s}_{L})
and
{\cal F}^{u}_{L}=\widehat{\Theta}({\cal N}^{u}_{L})
.
The first item of the lemma holds.

In the case where
L\subset E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}
, the same argument shows that

\widehat{\Theta}({\cal N}^{u}_{L})
is the orthogonal complement of
E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}
,
hence the second item holds.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\sigma
be a Lorenz like singularity with its splitting
T_{\sigma}M=E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{uu}_{\sigma}
,
let
L\subset T_{\sigma}M
be a
1
-dimensional space and

be a subspace with dimension

\operatorname{dim}(E^{uu})

If one of the following setting is satisfied:

•

and
\sigma
does not have index
d-1
,

•

or
L\subset E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}(F)\cap(E^{ss}_{\sigma}\oplus E^{c}_{\sigma})=\{0\}

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\big|{\rm Det}(\widetilde{\Psi}_{t}|_{F})\big|>0.

In the first case,

, the definition of

In the second case,

along

∎

Recall that a singularity

Lemma 4.12

Let

be a multi-singular hyperbolic set for some

Proof.

∎

5
SRB measures on multi-singular hyperbolic sets

In this section, we prove Theorem
C
and discuss some consequences on SRB measures.

5.1
Growth rate of volumes

To a homeomorphism
f
of
M
and
x\in M
, one associates the set
{\cal M}_{x}(f)
of measures which
are the accumulation points of the sequence
\big\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{i}(x)}\big\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Given a continuous flow
\varphi=(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
one associates the set
{\cal M}_{x}(\varphi)
of accumulation points of
the arc
\big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\varphi_{s}(x)}\operatorname{d}s\big)_{t>0}
when
t
tends to infinity.

Using Yomdin theory, Burguet has obtained
[
B1
]
the following
result relating the growth rate of the volume and the entropy for
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism on
M
. Then for Lebesgue almost every point
x\in M
, there is a measure
\mu\in{\cal M}_{x}(f)
such that for any
1\leq k\leq d
,

{\rm h}_{\mu}(f)\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\wedge^{k}Df^{n}(x)\|.

This immediately extends to vector fields, and it helps us to exclude the negative Lyapunov exponents for the lift of
\delta_{\sigma}
which comes from the flow direction.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the flow defined by a
C^{\infty}
vector field
X
over
M
. Then for Lebesgue almost every point
x\in M
, there is a measure
\mu\in{\cal M}_{x}(\varphi)
such that for any
1\leq k\leq d
,

{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})\geq\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|\wedge^{k}D\varphi_{t}(x)\|.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1}\in{\cal M}_{x}(\varphi_{1})
given by Theorem
5.1
.
Then the measure
\mu=\int_{0}^{1}(\varphi_{s})_{*}\mu_{1}{\rm d}s
belongs to
{\cal M}_{x}(\varphi)

and satisfies
h_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})=h_{\mu_{1}}(\varphi_{1})
.
The result then follows from

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|\wedge^{k}D\varphi_{t}(x)\|=\limsup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\wedge^{k}D\varphi_{n}(x)\|.

∎

Since the linear Poincaré flow is the projection of the tangent flow to the normal bundle, one gets the following corollary.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X\in{\cal X}^{\infty}(M)
defining the flows
\varphi=(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
and
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Then for Lebesgue almost every
x\in M\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)
, there is a measure
\mu\in{\cal M}_{x}(\varphi)
such that for any
1\leq k\leq d-1
,

{\rm h}_{\mu}(\varphi_{1})\geq\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|\wedge^{k}\Psi_{t}(x)\|.

The growth of the volume in the blow-up is related to invariant measures as follows.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let us consider an invariant compact set
\widehat{\Lambda}\subset\widehat{M}
for
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

which has a dominated splitting
\widehat{\cal N}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\cal E}\oplus\widehat{\cal F}
for
(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then there exists a neighborhood
\widehat{U}
of
\widehat{\Lambda}
in
\widehat{M}
such that for any point
L\in\widehat{M}
whose forward orbit
is contained in
\widehat{U}
and which satisfies
\omega(L)\subset\widehat{\Lambda}

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{t}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}(L)}\big|=\sup_{\widehat{\nu}\in{\cal M}_{L}(\widehat{\varphi})}\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\nu}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
, it suffices to prove

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{s}(L))}\big|{\rm d}s=\sup_{\widehat{\nu}\in{\cal M}_{L}(\widehat{\varphi})}\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\nu}.

One first proves the inequality “
\leq
”. Let
T_{i}\to+\infty
be a sequence realizing the limsup in the left hand side. Taking a subsequence if necessary, one gets a limit measure

\widehat{\nu}=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T_{i}}\int_{0}^{T_{i}}\delta_{\widehat{\varphi}_{s}(L)}{\rm d}s.

One concludes by computing:

\displaystyle\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{T_{i}}\int_{0}^{T_{i}}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{s}({L}))}\big|{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{s}({L}))}\big|{\rm d}s.

Now we prove the inequality“
\geq
”. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\widehat{\mu}\in{\cal M}_{L}(\widehat{\varphi})
such that

\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\mu}>\sup_{\widehat{\nu}\in{\cal M}_{L}(\widehat{\varphi})}\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\nu}-\varepsilon.

There exists a sequence
S_{i}\to+\infty
satisfying

\displaystyle\int\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{{\widehat{\cal F}}}\big|{\rm d}\widehat{\mu}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\int_{0}^{S_{i}}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{s}({L}))}\big|{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\log\big|{\rm Det}\widehat{\Psi}_{1}|_{\widehat{\cal F}(\widehat{\varphi}_{s}({L}))}\big|{\rm d}s.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, one concludes.
∎

In this section, we first discuss the uniqueness of physical measures on singular hyperbolic attractors.
We then give a criterion for an accumulation of chain recurrence classes to be an attractor.
At last, we prove the following statement which clearly implies Theorems
A
and
B

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists a
C^{1}
open and dense subset

of
{\cal X}^{*}(M)
and a dense G

6.1
Physical measures for generic singular hyperbolic attractors

for

Note that Qiu

Theorem 6.2

Let

For any invariant measure

Existence and accumulation of attractors

We now give a new criterion for the existence of attractors, based on invariant manifolds of bi-Pliss points.

.

.

admits at most finitely many sinks and finitely many quasi-attractors with singularities. Hence

has infinitely many quasi-attractors

Let

be the subset

\bigcap_{i}U_{m_{i}}=\{x\}
. By the fact that
x\in R_{\gamma}^{n}=W_{n}(\gamma)\cup(W^{u}(\gamma)\setminus\overline{W_{n}(\gamma)})
, we have that
x\in W_{n}(\gamma)
. This implies
\operatorname{Orb}^{+}(x,\varphi^{Y})\cap U_{n}\neq\emptyset
and thus
\operatorname{Orb}^{+}(x,\varphi^{Y})\cap V\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Conley’s theory shows that for any
x\in M
, there exists a quasi-attractor
\Lambda
of
Y
such that
x\dashv y

for any
y\in\Lambda
(note that for
C^{1}
generic vector fields it can be deduced from Theorem
2.2
.)
It remains to show that if
x\in R_{\gamma}
then
\omega(x)=\Lambda
.

By the claim above
\Lambda\subset\omega(x)
. But since
\Lambda
is a quasi-attractor, it admits arbitrarily small
trapping neighborhoods, hence
\omega(x)=\Lambda
.
∎

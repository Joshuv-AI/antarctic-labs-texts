A Fast Algorithm for Denumerants with Three Variables
Feihu Liu, Guoce Xin, [2mm] School of Mathematical Sciences, [-0.8ex] Capital Normal University, Beijing, 100048, P.R. China

Abstract
Let a , b , c a,b,c be distinct positive integers such that a < b < c a<b<c and gcd ⁡ ( a , b , c ) = 1 \gcd(a,b,c)=1 . For any non-negative integer n n , the denumerant function d ⁡ ( n , a , b , c ) d(n;a,b,c) denotes the number of solutions of the equation a ​ x 1 + b ​ x 2 + c ​ x 3 = n ax_{1}+bx_{2}+cx_{3}=n in non-negative integers x 1 , x 2 , x 3 x_{1},x_{2},x_{3} . We present an algorithm that computes d ⁡ ( n , a , b , c ) d(n;a,b,c) with a time complexity of O ⁡ ( log ⁡ b ) O(\log b) .

1
Introduction

Let
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{k}\}
be a nonempty set of positive integers. The
restricted partition function of the set

A
, denoted
p_{A}(n)
, counts the number of solutions in non-negative integers
x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, to the equation

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=n.

When
\gcd(A)=1
, the function
p_{A}(n)
is referred to as the
denumerant
, denoted as
d(n;a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
. This concept was introduced by Sylvester
[
13
]
. A related problem is to determine the largest non-negative integer
F(A)
for which
d(F(A);a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=0
. This integer is known as the
Frobenius number
of
A
.

For the case
k=2
, Sylvester
[
14
]
derived the formula for the Frobenius number
F(A)=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}
. Sertöz
[
12
]
and Tripathi
[
15
]
independently obtained an explicit formula for
d(n;a_{1},a_{2})
. However, Curtis
[
5
]
demonstrated that for
k\geq 3
, the Frobenius number cannot be expressed by closed formulas of a certain type.

For
k=3
, let
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
. Greenberg
[
7
]
proposed a fast algorithm for computing
F(A)
with a runtime complexity of
O(\log a_{1})
.
In 1953, Popoviciu
[
11
]
developed an algorithm with a time complexity of
O(a_{3}\log a_{3})
for calculating
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
when
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are pairwise coprime positive integers.
In 1995, Lisonëk
[
9
]
presented an arithmetic procedure to compute
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
in time complexity of
O(a_{1}a_{2}\log a_{2})
when
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are pairwise coprime positive integers (under certain conditions, this complexity can be reduced to
O(a_{1}a_{2})
).
In 2003, Brown, Chou, and Shiue
[
4
]
introduced an
O(a_{1}a_{2}\log a_{3})
algorithm for computing
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.
In 2018, Aguiló-Gost and Llena
[
2
]
proposed an
O(a_{2}+\log a_{3})
algorithm to compute
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.
In 2020, Binner
[
3
]
developed an algorithm for computing
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
with a runtime of
O(\log a_{3})
. Some relevant references can be found in
[
1
,
8
,
10
]
.

In this paper, we combine the constant term method mentioned in
[
16
]
with a key transformation technique in
[
18
]
to devise a fast algorithm for calculating
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
. The complexity of our algorithm is
O(\log a_{2})
with
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we provide a brief introduction to the constant term method.
Section 3 outlines the necessary reduction operations for our algorithm.
Section 4 presents our algorithm and a specific example.
Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{P}
denote the set of all complex numbers, all integers, all non-negative integers, and all positive integers, respectively.

2
A Brief Introduction to the Constant Term Method

In this section, we introduce two basic tools in the study of constant term theory.
To maintain brevity, we only work over the field
\mathbb{C}((\lambda))
of Laurent series in
\lambda
.
The constant term operator
\mathrm{CT}_{\lambda}
acts by

\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}c_{i}\lambda^{i}=c_{0},

where
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and the coefficients
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Subsequently, the denumerant is expressed as

\displaystyle d(n;a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{k})=\sum_{x_{i}\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\lambda^{x_{1}a_{1}+x_{2}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k}a_{k}-n}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{a_{1}})(1-\lambda^{a_{2}})\cdots(1-\lambda^{a_{k}})}.

For the theory for constant terms in several variables, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
.

The basic problem in constant term theory is how to efficiently extract the constant term of

\displaystyle E(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{L(\lambda)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})},

(1)

where
L(\lambda)
is a Laurent polynomial, the variables
z_{i}
are independent of
\lambda
, and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{P}
for all
i
.
In the general setting, we need to clarify the series expansion of
(1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})^{-1}
in a field of iterated Laurent series.
The situation in
\mathbb{C}((\lambda))
is simple. For a given positive integer
k
and complex variable
z
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z\lambda^{k}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}z^{j}\lambda^{jk};\ \ \ \frac{1}{1-z\lambda^{-k}}=\frac{-z^{-1}\lambda^{k}}{1-z^{-1}\lambda^{k}}=-\sum_{j\geq 0}z^{-(j+1)}\lambda^{(j+1)k}.

Our investigation relies on the well-known partial fraction decompositions.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
E(\lambda)
be defined as in (
1
), where
z_{i}
are complex parameters and the denominator factors are
coprime to each other. Then
E(\lambda)
has a partial fraction decomposition given by

E(\lambda)=P(\lambda)+\dfrac{p(\lambda)}{\lambda^{m}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{A_{i}(\lambda)}{1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}},

(2)

where
P(\lambda),p(\lambda)
, and the
A_{i}(\lambda)
’s are all polynomials,
\deg p(\lambda)<m
, and
\deg A_{i}(\lambda)<a_{i}
for all
i
.

Furthermore, the polynomial
A_{i}(\lambda)
is uniquely characterized by

A_{i}(\lambda)\equiv E(\lambda)\cdot(1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})\ \mod\langle 1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}\rangle,\ \ \deg_{\lambda}A_{i}(\lambda)<a_{i},

(3)

where
\langle 1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}\rangle
denotes the ideal generated by
1-z_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}
.

The following corollary is straightforward but important.

Corollary 2.2
.

Follow the notation in Proposition
2.1
. We obtain

\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{\lambda}E(\lambda)=P(0)+A_{1}(0)+A_{2}(0)+\cdots+A_{k}(0).

(4)

Moreover, if
E(\lambda)
is a proper fraction in
\lambda
, that is, the degree of the numerator is less than the degree in the denominator,
then
P(\lambda)=0
; if
\lim_{\lambda=0}E(\lambda)
exists, then
p(\lambda)=0
. If both conditions hold, then

E(0)=A_{1}(0)+A_{2}(0)+\cdots+A_{k}(0).

It is important to note that in the multivariate setting, certain terms
A_{i}(0)
may be omitted from (
4
).
For the basic building block
A_{s}(0)
, we introduce the notation

\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\underline{\frac{1}{1-z_{s}\lambda^{a_{s}}}}E(\lambda)\cdot(1-z_{s}\lambda^{a_{s}})=A_{s}(0).

One can think that only the single underlined factor of the denominator contributes when taking the constant term in
\lambda
.
Define the
type
of
\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{\lambda}E(\lambda)
as
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
and the
type
of
\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}_{\lambda}E(\lambda)
with
1-z_{s}\lambda^{a_{s}}
underlined
as
(\mathrm{\textcircled{s}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
. The
index
of the latter constant term is defined as
a_{s}
.

We require an additional tool, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
18
]
,The key transformation)
.

Let
\gcd(u,a)=1
.
For any rational function
F(\lambda)
that is meaningful when modulo
1-z\lambda^{a}
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\underline{\frac{1}{1-z\lambda^{a}}}F(\lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\underline{\frac{1}{1-z^{1/u}\lambda^{a}}}F(\lambda^{u}),

where
u
is referred to as a
multiplier
.

For denumerant
d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
, we need to introduce slack variables
z_{i}
to apply Proposition
2.1
. We have

\displaystyle d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow 1}\left\{\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a_{1}}z_{1})}(1-\lambda^{a_{2}}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{a_{3}}z_{3})}+\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{a_{1}}z_{1})\underline{(1-\lambda^{a_{2}}z_{2})}(1-\lambda^{a_{3}}z_{3})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{a_{1}}z_{1})(1-\lambda^{a_{2}}z_{2})\underline{(1-\lambda^{a_{3}}z_{3})}}\right\}

or (simply written as)

\displaystyle d(n;a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle=\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow 1}\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a_{1}}z_{1})(1-\lambda^{a_{2}}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{a_{3}}z_{3})}}.

(5)

The flow chart of our algorithm is as follows.

\displaystyle(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\downarrow

\displaystyle(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{11},1,a_{31})\longrightarrow(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{12},1,a_{32})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1(p-1)},1,a_{3(p-1)})\longrightarrow{\boxed{(\textcircled{1};a_{1p},1,a_{3p})}}

(6)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\downarrow\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\downarrow\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\downarrow

\displaystyle{\boxed{(\textcircled{2};a_{11},1,a_{31})}}\ \ \ \ \ {\boxed{(\textcircled{2};a_{12},1,a_{32})}}\ \ \ \ \ \cdots\ \ \ \ \ {\boxed{(\textcircled{2};a_{1(p-1)},1,a_{3(p-1)})}}

Our algorithm is based on the following observations of constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.
If
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2})=1
, then a constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
can be transformed into
a constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{11},1,a_{31})
by using Theorem
2.3
, where
a_{11}=a_{1}
.

A constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1i},1,a_{3i})
, for
1\leq i\leq p-1
, can be expressed as the sum of two distinct constant terms:
One is of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{2}};a_{1i},1,a_{3i})
and hence simplifies to a rational function;
The other is of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{3}};a_{1i}^{\prime},1,a_{3i}^{\prime})
and can be written as a constant term of
type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1(i+1)},1,a_{3(i+1)})
with index
a_{1(i+1)}=a_{3i}^{\prime}\leq a_{1i}/2
.

In the flow chart (
2
),
a_{1p}=0
or
1
with
p\leq\log a_{1}
. When
a_{1p}=0
, the constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1p},1,a_{3p})
is
0
(see Subsection
3.4
). When
a_{1p}=1
, the constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1p},1,a_{3p})
is a single rational function.

3
Several Reductions

Let
a,b,c
be positive integers such that
a<b<c
and
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
. We derive several reductions needed in our algorithms.

3.1
Reduction to the Case
\gcd(a,b)=1

Though not used in our algorithm, we can reduce
(a,b,c)
to the pairwise coprime case by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F(\lambda)
be a formal power series in
\lambda
with coefficients in the field
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g,c\in\mathbb{P}
. If
\gcd(g,c)=1
, then

\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\left(F(\lambda^{g})\cdot\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{c})}\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\left(F(\lambda)\cdot\frac{\lambda^{\frac{-n+tc}{g}}}{(1-\lambda^{c})}\right),

where
c^{\prime}c\equiv 1\pmod{g}
,
t\equiv nc^{\prime}\pmod{g}
, and
0\leq t<g
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda^{g})\cdot\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{c})}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda^{g})\cdot\lambda^{-n}\cdot(1+\lambda^{c}+\cdots+\lambda^{(g-1)c})}{(1-\lambda^{cg})}.

By
\gcd(c,g)=1
and
-n+tc\equiv 0\pmod{g}
, we deduce that
t\equiv c^{-1}n\pmod{g}
, leading to

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda^{g})\cdot\lambda^{-n}\cdot(1+\lambda^{c}+\cdots+\lambda^{(g-1)c})}{(1-\lambda^{cg})}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda^{g})\cdot\lambda^{-n}\cdot\lambda^{tc}}{(1-\lambda^{cg})}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{\frac{-n+tc}{g}}}{(1-\lambda^{c})}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Suppose

\displaystyle g_{1}=\gcd(b,c),\ \ \ a_{1}a\equiv 1\mod g_{1},\ \ \ k\equiv na_{1}\mod g_{1},\ \ 0\leq k<g_{1},

\displaystyle g_{2}=\gcd(a,c),\ \ \ b_{2}b\equiv 1\mod g_{2},\ \ \ j\equiv nb_{2}\mod g_{2},\ \ 0\leq j<g_{2},

\displaystyle g_{3}=\gcd(a,b),\ \ \ c_{3}c\equiv 1\mod g_{3},\ \ \ i\equiv nc_{3}\mod g_{3},\ \ 0\leq i<g_{3}.

By applying Lemma
3.1
iteratively, we obtain

\displaystyle d(n;a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{a})(1-\lambda^{b})(1-\lambda^{c})}=d\left(\frac{n-ic}{g_{3}};\frac{a}{g_{3}},\frac{b}{g_{3}},c\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{n-ic-jb}{g_{2}g_{3}};\frac{a}{g_{2}g_{3}},\frac{b}{g_{3}},\frac{c}{g_{2}}\right)=d\left(\frac{n-ic-jb-ka}{g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}};\frac{a}{g_{2}g_{3}},\frac{b}{g_{1}g_{3}},\frac{c}{g_{1}g_{2}}\right).

(8)

3.2
Reduction to Two Contribution Term

For (
5
), we consider the contribution of
(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})
to the constant term. There exists
s\in\mathbb{P}
such that
0<sc-n\leq c
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})\underline{(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}(\lambda^{c}z_{3})^{s}}{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})\underline{(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{-\lambda^{sc-n}z_{3}^{s}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})}(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})},

where the last equation follows from Corollary
2.2
.

Hence, we derive

\displaystyle d(n;a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow 1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}-\lambda^{sc-n}z_{3}^{s}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})}(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})}+\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{-n}-\lambda^{sc-n}z_{3}^{s}}{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})\underline{(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})}(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow 1}(T(a)+T(b)).

(9)

For the reduction from
(a,b)
to
(a/g_{3},b/g_{3})
, it is sufficient to perform the extended gcd computation once.

\gcd(a,b)=g_{3}\Rightarrow au+bv=g_{3}\Leftrightarrow ua/g_{3}+vb/g_{3}=1,\text{where }u,v\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

(10)

In the subsequent subsections, we assume that
a:=\frac{a}{g_{3}}
and
b:=\frac{b}{g_{3}}
, or simply
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

3.3
Reduction to the Case
b=1

If
\gcd(a,b)=1
, then we can reduce a constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a,b,c)
to a constant term of type

(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a,1,c^{\prime})
. This handles the constant term
T(a)
in (
3.2
). The constant term
T(b)
is similar.

Consider a constant term of the following form:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})}(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})},

where
r_{1},r_{2}
are integers and
\mathbf{z^{m_{i}}}=z_{1}^{m_{i1}}z_{2}^{m_{i2}}z_{3}^{m_{i3}}
,
m_{ij}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Applying (
10
), we find
u,v
such that

.
It follows that
\gcd(a,v)=1

2.3
applies to yield

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1})}(1-\lambda^{b}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{c}z_{3})}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1}^{\frac{1}{v}})}(1-\lambda^{bv}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{cv}z_{3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1}^{\frac{1}{v}})}(1-\lambda^{1-au}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{cv}z_{3})}=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}v}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}z_{1}^{\frac{1}{v}})}(1-\lambda z_{1}^{\frac{u}{v}}z_{2})(1-\lambda^{cv}z_{3})}.

3.4
A Euclid Style Recursive Operation

We aim to establish a recursion for
T(a)
and
T(b)
. For this, we relax to consider the following constant term:

\displaystyle F(\underline{\lambda^{a}\mathbf{z^{\alpha}}},\lambda\mathbf{z^{\gamma}},\lambda^{c}\mathbf{z^{\beta}},\lambda^{r_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}},\lambda^{r_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}})=\mathop{\mathrm{CT}}\limits_{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}-\lambda^{r_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}}{\underline{(1-\lambda^{a}\mathbf{z^{\alpha}})}(1-\lambda\mathbf{z^{\gamma}})(1-\lambda^{c}\mathbf{z^{\beta}})},

(12)

where
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
.
The base cases are: i) if
a=0
, then the constant term is just
0
;
ii) if
a=1
, then the constant term becomes (according to (
3

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m_{1}}-r_{1}\alpha}-\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m_{2}}-r_{2}\alpha}}{(1-\mathbf{z}^{\gamma-\alpha})(1-\mathbf{z}^{\beta-c\alpha})}.

We require the following two elementary operations.

The

\mathrm{rem}^{\star}(\lambda^{m},1-\mathbf{z^{h}}\lambda^{a},\lambda)
and the

\mathrm{srem}^{\star}(\lambda^{m},1-\mathbf{z^{h}}\lambda^{a},\lambda)

\displaystyle\mathrm{rem}^{\star}(\lambda^{m},1-\mathbf{z^{h}}\lambda^{a},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{h}\cdot(-\ell)}\lambda^{r},\ \ \text{where}\ m=\ell a+r,\ 0<r\leq a;

\displaystyle\mathrm{srem}^{\star}(\lambda^{m},1-\mathbf{z^{h}}\lambda^{a},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{h}\cdot(-\ell)}\lambda^{r},\ \ \text{where}\ m=\ell a+r,\ -\frac{a}{2}<r\leq\frac{a}{2}.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have the following recursion:

\displaystyle F(\underline{\lambda^{a}\mathbf{z^{\alpha}}},\lambda\mathbf{z^{\gamma}},\lambda^{c}\mathbf{z^{\beta}},\lambda^{r_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}},\lambda^{r_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m_{2}}^{\prime}-t_{2}\gamma}-\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m_{1}}^{\prime}-t_{1}\gamma}}{(1-\mathbf{z}^{\alpha-a\gamma})(1-\mathbf{z}^{\beta_{2}-c_{2}\gamma})}+F(\underline{\lambda^{c_{2}}\mathbf{z^{\beta_{2}}}},\lambda\mathbf{z^{\gamma}},\lambda^{a}\mathbf{z^{\alpha}},\lambda^{t_{2}}\mathbf{z^{m_{2}}}^{\prime},\lambda^{t_{1}}\mathbf{z^{m_{1}}}^{\prime}),

(13)

where the new parameters are determined through several elementary operations.

Let us delay the proof of this lemma.
The first term in (
3.2
) corresponds to the constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{2}};a_{11},1,a_{31})
within the flow chart (
2
). By

, the second term in (

) corresponds to the constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{12},1,a_{32})
with index

in the flow chart (
2
). Finally, by iteratively applying Lemma

at most

steps, we can derive a summation formula for rational functions only involving

.

Proof.

Let
\lambda^{c_{1}}\mathbf{z}^{\beta_{1}}=\mathrm{srem}^{\star}(\lambda^{c},1-\mathbf{z^{\alpha}}\lambda^{a},\lambda)\cdot\mathbf{z}^{\beta}
.
If
0\leq c_{1}\leq\frac{a}{2}
, then

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

1
correctly computes

Proof.

In step 1, the primary task is the computation of
u,v
, which costs
O(\log a)
. Subsequently, we arrive at the
\gcd(a,b)=1

Step 2 takes several ring operations to reach the constant term

Step 3 performs at most
\log a

3.2
, each reducing the index of the constant term by half. Each iteration step takes

ring operations since the two operations

Step 4 is similar to Step 3. It takes
O(\log b)

Now, we arrive at the summation in (
14
).

Step 5 takes
O(\log a+\log b)

O(1)
ring operations.

In total, the algorithm cost
O(\log b)
ring operations.

5
Concluding Remark

The constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{s}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
corresponds to a denumerant simplicial cone, as detailed in
[
18
]
.
A basic problem is to write such a constant term as the sum of a
N
rational functions, so that
N
is small in some sense.
Barvinok’s algorithm in computational geometry applies to show that
N
can be bounded by a polynomial in
\log a_{s}

when
k
is fixed. In algebraic combinatorics, it is conjectured in
[
18
]
that
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{s}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
is bounded by a polynomial in
\log a_{s}
for a suitable strategy
h
on the choices of the valid multiplier. Here,
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{s}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
denotes the number of terms obtained by repeated application of Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.2
according to
h
.

A partial result is presented in
[
18
]
for the case where
a_{1}\leq 13
, where the optimal strategy
h
is specified, and the value of
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},1,\dots,1)
is explicitly determined to be bounded by a polynomial in
k
.

Lemma
3.2
is equivalent to giving a simple strategy
h
so that
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{s}};a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\leq\log a_{s}
:
First transform to
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},1)
and then use the fact
f^{1}(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},1)\leq\log a_{1}
, where
h=1
means to always choose
1
as the valid multiplier.
The fact naturally extends to general length
k
as

f^{1}(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},a_{2},1,\dots,1)\leq(k-2)\log a_{1}
.

Extending the above idea gives the following partial, but stronger, result:

Proposition 5.1
.

If one application of Theorem
2.3

results in a constant term of type
(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
such that
for an
\ell
we have
|a_{i}-a_{i^{\prime}}|\leq 13
for
2\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq\ell
, and
a_{j}\leq 13
for
j>\ell
,
then there exists a strategy
h
such that
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\leq p(k)\log a_{1}
for some polynomial
p(k)
.

Sketched proof.

The case
\ell=2
follows the same idea as in the flow chart, except that
the outputs are constant terms handled by the partial result.

For
\ell>2
, the recursion yields

f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{1}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{i}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})+\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{k}f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{j}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k}).

Since
a_{j}\leq 13
, each
f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{j}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
is bounded by a polynomial in
k
.
Since
|a_{i}-a_{i^{\prime}}|\leq 13
for
2\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq\ell
, one more application of Corollary
2.2
yields

f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{i}};a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{i}};b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1},a_{i},b_{i+1},\dots,b_{k})=\sum_{j\neq i}f^{h}(\mathrm{\textcircled{j}};b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1},a_{i},b_{i+1},\dots,b_{k}),

where
b_{j}\leq 13
for all
j\neq 1,i
. This is just the
\ell=2
case of the proposition.
∎

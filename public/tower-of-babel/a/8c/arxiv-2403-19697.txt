Abstract non-scalar Volterra difference equations
Marko Kostić

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the abstract non-scalar Volterra difference equations. We employ the Poisson like transforms to connect the solutions of the abstract non-scalar Volterra integro-differential equations and the abstract non-scalar Volterra difference equations. We also investigate the existence, uniqueness and almost periodicity of solutions to the abstract multi-term fractional difference equations with Weyl fractional derivatives.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
k:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
,
k\neq 0
, a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is a solution of the abstract degenerate Volterra difference equation (

and a strongly continuous operator family

(i)

Then
W(0)f(0)=k(0)Cu(0).

(ii)

If

then we have

for all

In particular, if

is injective, then there exists at most one solution of (

).

Proof.

The given assumptions imply

W(0)B(0)u(0)=k(0)Cu(0)+W(0)A(0)u(0)\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ B(0)f(0)=f(0)+A(0)u(0).

This simply yields

To prove (ii) with

observe first that

W(1)Bu(0)=k(1)Cu(0)+W(1)A(0)u(0)+W(0)A(1)u(0)\mbox{ and }Bu(0)=f(0)+A(0)u(0).

Hence,

(2.4)

\displaystyle W(1)f(0)=k(1)Cu(0)+W(0)A(1)u(0).

Similarly, we have

W(0)Bu(1)=k(0)Cu(1)+W(0)A(0)u(1)\mbox{ and }Bu(1)=f(1)+A(1)u(0)+A(0)u(1).

Hence,

(2.5)

\displaystyle W(0)f(1)=k(0)Cu(1)-W(0)A(1)u(0).

Keeping in mind (

), we get that
(kC\ast_{0}u)(1)=(W\ast_{0}f)(1).
Considering the terms

W(v-1)Bu(1),...,
and
W(0)Bu(v),
a similar line of reasoning gives that
(kC\ast_{0}u)(v)=(W\ast_{0}f)(v)
for all
v\in{\mathbb{N}}.
It is clear that, if
f\equiv 0
,
k(0)\neq 0
and
C
is injective, then
(kC\ast_{0}u)(v)=0
for all
v\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0};
inductively, it readily follows that

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
k:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},

Suppose, further, that
(i)
or
(ii)
holds, where:

(i)

is a bounded sequence,
k\in l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}:Y)

(ii)

Then
u(\cdot)
is bounded,

\displaystyle B(v)u(v)=(k\circ Cf)(v)+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v}A(v-l)u(l),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

It is clear that the sequence

is well-defined,
u(\cdot)
is well-defined and bounded if (i) holds as well as that

if (b) holds. The functional equality (S1) and the closedness of the linear operator

in combination with the assumptions
\sum_{v=0}^{+\infty}\|S(v)_{\mid Y}\|_{L(Y)}<+\infty

simply imply the following:

\displaystyle Bu(v)-(k\circ Cf)(v)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v}BS(v-l)f(l)-(k\circ Cf)(v)

\displaystyle=-(k\circ Cf)(v)+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v}\Bigl[k(v-l)Cf(l)+\bigl[A\ast_{0}S\bigr](v-l)f(l)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{+\infty}A(s)\sum_{r=0}^{+\infty}S(r)f(v-s-r)=\sum_{s=0}^{+\infty}A(s)u(v-s)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v}A(v-l)u(l),\ v\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where we have also used the Fubini theorem in the last line of computation.

The main result of this section reads as follows:

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that

Define

\displaystyle BS_{a,w}(v)y=k_{a,\omega}(v)Cy+a\omega^{v}\bigl(A_{a,w}\ast_{0}U\bigr)(v)y-\omega^{v}(-1)^{v}\bigl(A_{a,w}\ast_{0}U\bigr)(v-1)y.

4.
The abstract multi-term fractional difference equations with Weyl fractional derivatives

Given a one-dimensional sequence
(u_{k})
in
X
, the Euler forward difference operator
\Delta
is defined by
\Delta u_{k}:=u_{k+1}-u_{k}.
The operator
\Delta^{m}
is defined inductively; then, for every integer
m\geq 1,
we have:

\displaystyle\Delta^{m}u_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j}\binom{m}{j}u_{k+j}.

If
u:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\rightarrow X
is a given sequence and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)\setminus{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
then we
define the fractional integral
\Delta^{-\alpha}u:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\rightarrow X
by

\Delta^{-\alpha}u(v):=\sum_{j=0}^{v}k^{\alpha}(v-j)u(j),\quad v\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Set
m:=\lceil\alpha\rceil.
Then the Riemann-Liouville fractional difference operator of
order
\alpha>0,

\Delta^{\alpha}
shortly,
is defined by

\Delta^{\alpha}u(v):=\Bigl[\Delta^{m}\bigl(\Delta^{-(m-\alpha)}u\bigr)\Bigr](v),\quad v\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Further on,
if
\alpha>0,

m=\lceil\alpha\rceil
and
u:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow X
satisfies
\sum_{v=-\infty}^{\infty}\|u(v)\|\cdot(1+|v|)^{m-\alpha-1}<+\infty,
then we define the Weyl fractional derivative

\displaystyle\Bigl[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u\Bigr](v):=\Bigl[\Delta^{m}\Bigl(\Delta^{-(m-\alpha)}_{W}u\Bigr)\Bigr](v),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where

\Bigl(\Delta^{-(m-\alpha)}_{W}u\Bigr)(v):=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v}k^{m-\alpha}(v-l)u(l),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Hence,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u\Bigr](v)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v+j}(-1)^{m-j}\binom{m}{j}k^{m-\alpha}(v+j-l)u(l),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Due to
[
1
, Remark 2.4]
, the assumption
\sum_{v=-\infty}^{\infty}\|u(v)\|\cdot(1+|v|)^{m-\alpha-1}<+\infty
implies

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u\Bigr](v)=\Bigl[\Delta^{m}\Bigl(\Delta^{-(m-\alpha)}_{W}u\Bigr)\Bigr](v)=\Bigl[\Delta^{-(m-\alpha)}_{W}\Bigl(\Delta^{m}u\Bigr)\Bigr](v),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}};

furthermore, it can be simply proved that, for every real number
a,
the sequence
h(\cdot)=u(\cdot+a)
satisfies
\sum_{v=-\infty}^{\infty}\|h(v)\|\cdot(1+|v|)^{m-\alpha-1}<+\infty
as well as that (cf. (
4.1
)):

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}h\Bigr](v)=\Bigl[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u\Bigr](v+a),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

In the sequel, we also write
\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u(v)
in place of
[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u](v).

If
A
is a closed linear operator, then
we must distinguish the terms
A(\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u)
and
\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}(Au)
for
\alpha>0.

For example, if
u(v)\equiv x\notin D(A),
then we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}u(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v+j}(-1)^{m-j}\binom{m}{j}k^{m-\alpha}(v+j-l)x

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j}\binom{m}{j}\Biggl[\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}k^{m-\alpha}(s)x\Biggr]=0,\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}},

but the term
[\Delta^{\alpha}_{W}(Au)](v)
is not well-defined for any
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Define now
T_{i,L}^{W}u(v):=A_{i}\Delta_{W}^{\alpha_{i}}u(v)
, if
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, and
T_{i,R}^{W}u(v):=\Delta_{C}^{\alpha_{i}}A_{i}u(v)
, if
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
.
We assume that, for every
v\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
,
T_{i}^{W}u(v)
denotes either
T_{i,L}^{W}u(v)
or
T_{i,R}^{W}u(v)
.
Of concern is the following abstract multi-term fractional difference equation without initial conditions:

(4.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{W}u(v+v_{i})=f(v),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow X
is a given sequence. A
strong solution of problem (
4.5
) is any sequence
u:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow X
such that the term
T_{i}^{W}u(\cdot+v_{i})
is well-defined for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{n}
as well as that (
4.5
) identically holds for any
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Set now
v_{min}:=\min\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}
and
y(v):=u(v+v_{min}),

v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Then it is clear that the problem (
4.5
)
is equivalent with the probem (cf. (
4.3

\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{W}y\bigl(v+[v_{i}-v_{min}]\bigr)=f(v),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}},

as well as that

for all

Therefore, in the analysis of the existence and uniqueness of solutions of the problem (
4.5
),
we may assume without loss of generality that
v_{1}\geq 0,\ ...,\ v_{n}\geq 0

Due to (

is equivalent with

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}A_{i}\Biggl[\sum_{j=0}^{m_{i}}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{v+j}(-1)^{m_{i}-j}\binom{m_{i}}{j}k^{m_{i}-\alpha}(v+j-l)u(l)\Biggr]

We cannot expect the uniqueness of (periodic/almost periodic) strong solutions of (

5.
Conclusions and final remarks

In this paper, we have examined
the abstract non-scalar Volterra difference equations. We have employed the Poisson like transforms and, as a special case of our analysis, we have investigated the existence, uniqueness and almost periodicity to the abstract multi-term fractional difference equations with Weyl fractional derivatives.

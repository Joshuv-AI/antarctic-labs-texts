Higher weight spectra of ternary codes associated to the quadratic Veronese 3 3 -fold
Krishna Kaipa, Puspendu Pradhan

Abstract
. The problem studied in this work is to determine the higher weight spectra of the Projective Reed-Muller codes associated to the Veronese 3 3 -fold 𝒱 \mathcal{V} in P ​ G ​ ( 9 , q ) PG(9,q) , which is the image of the quadratic Veronese embedding of P ​ G ​ ( 3 , q ) PG(3,q) in P ​ G ​ ( 9 , q ) PG(9,q) . We reduce the problem to the following combinatorial problem in finite geometry: For each subset S S of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} , determine the dimension of the linear subspace of P ​ G ​ ( 9 , q ) PG(9,q) generated by S S . We develop a systematic method to solve the latter problem. We implement the method for q = 3 q=3 , and use it to obtain the higher weight spectra of the associated code. The case of a general finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} will be treated in a future work.

Introduction

Let

denote the
n

on
PG(n,q)

\imath(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{0}^{2},x_{0}x_{1}\dots,x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}^{2}),

for some fixed ordering of the
K
monomials of degree
2
in the variables
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}

Let

and let

denote the matrix of size

\mathbb{G}_{n}=\left[\begin{matrix}v_{1}&\dots&v_{N}\end{matrix}\right],

\imath(P_{j})

(
[
5

is a quadratic form

and the weight of a codeword is the number of points of

not lying on the corresponding quadric hypersurface defined by the vanishing of

is a linear subspace of

generated by some
r
linearly independent quadratic forms

. The weight of this subcode is the number of points of

which do not lie on the common zero set of

. Equivalently,

correspond to codimension

\mathcal{L}
of
PG(K-1,q)
, with the weight of the subcode being the number of points of
\mathcal{V}_{n}
not lying on
\mathcal{L}
. The
r
-th generalized weight enumerator (in short GWE) of

is the polynomial

W^{(r)}(Z)=\sum_{\mathcal{L}\subset PG(K-1,q)\text{ of codim $r$}}Z^{\#\mathcal{V}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{i}A_{i}^{(r)}Z^{i},

where
A_{i}^{(r)}
is the number of codimension
r
linear subspaces of
PG(K-1,q)
which intersect
\mathcal{V}_{n}
in

N-i
points.

We also explain the algebraic-geometric significance of the polynomial
Z^{N}W^{(r)}(\frac{1}{Z})
. It gives the answer to the following geometric problem:

Problem
.

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{r}{j}_{q}=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(q^{r}-q^{i})}{|GL_{j}(q)|}=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(q^{r}-1)(q^{r-1}-1)\dots(q^{r-j+1}-1)}{(q^{j}-1)(q^{j-1}-1)\dots(q-1)},

3.
Approach to the Problem

Let
C
be a linear
[N,K,d]_{q}
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
N
, dimension
K
and minimum distance
d
with generator matrix
M_{K\times N}
. For
J\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
, let
r_{J}
be the rank of the submatrix of
M_{K\times N}
on the columns indexed by
J
.
The higher weight spectrum of
C
can be obtained just by knowing
r_{J}
for each
J\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
. To see this, consider for each
1\leq j\leq N
, the polynomials
B_{j}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
defined by

(4)

\displaystyle B_{j}(T)=\sum_{J}(T^{K-r_{J}}-1),

where the sum runs over all subsets
J\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
of size
j
. We define
B_{0}(T)=(T^{K}-1)
. The
extended weight enumerator
polynomial (in short EWE) is the polynomial
W_{C}(Z;T)\in\mathbb{Z}[Z,T]
defined by

\displaystyle W(Z;T)=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=0}^{N}B_{j}(T)(1-Z)^{j}Z^{N-j}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+(T^{K}-1)Z^{N}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}B_{j}(T)(1-Z)^{j}Z^{N-j}.

Let
A_{j}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
be polynomials defined by

(6)

\displaystyle W(Z;T)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{j}(T)Z^{j}.

The polynomial
W(Z;q^{m})
is the weight enumerator polynomial of the extended code
C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
(see
[
4
, Proposition 5.23]
). Thus,
A_{j}(q^{m})
is the number of weight
j
codewords of
C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.
The
r
-th generalized weight enumerator polynomial of
C
can be obtained from extended weight enumerator polynomial:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 5.7]

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
0\leq r\leq K
. Then the
r
-th generalized weight enumerator polynomial of
C
can be obtained from the extended weight enumerator polynomial of
C
by:

W^{(r)}(Z)=\frac{1}{|GL_{r}(q)|}\sum_{j=0}^{r}{r\brack j}_{q}(-1)^{r-j}q^{\tbinom{r-j}{2}}W(Z;q^{j}).

From (
4
), (
5
) and Theorem
3.1
, it follows that determining the quantities
r_{J}
for all
J\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
allows us to obtain the
r
-th generalized weight enumerator polynomial of
C
.
It will be convenient to introduce the numbers
B_{j,i}
defined to be the number of
J\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
|J|=j
and rank
(G_{J})=i
. In terms of
B_{j,i}
we can write

B_{j}(T)=\sum_{i\geq 1}B_{j,i}(T^{K-i}-1).

We note that in the polynomial
B_{j}(T)
the quantities
B_{j,K}
are multiplied by
(T^{K-K}-1)=0
. Therefore, there is no need to determine the quantities
B_{j,K}
. In other words, we may re-define

(7)

B_{j}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}B_{j,i}(T^{K-i}-1).

By
[
4
, Theorem 5.4]
, we have
A_{0}(T)=1
, and for
j>0
:

(8)

A_{j}(T)=\sum_{i=N-j}^{N-d}(-1)^{N+j+i}\textstyle\tbinom{i}{N-j}B_{i}(T).

It will be useful to note that :

(9)

B_{j,j}=\textstyle\tbinom{N}{j}-\sum_{i<j}B_{j,i},\quad\text{ if $j\leq K$}.

Although, in the expression (
7
) the quantities
B_{j,K}
were not required, for later use we note that

(10)

B_{j,K}=\textstyle\tbinom{N}{j}-\sum_{i<K}B_{j,i},\quad\text{ if $j>K$.}

Lemma 3.3
.

Given a rank
r
configuration
S
, there is a unique maximal rank
r
configuration
\bar{S}\supset S
. We have
\bar{S}=V(I_{2}(S))
.

Proof.

Let
S^{\prime}=V(I_{2}(S))
. We must show that i)
S^{\prime}
has rank
r
, and ii) any rank
r
configuration
T
containing
S
, is contained in
S^{\prime}
.

As for i), it suffices to show
I_{2}(S^{\prime})=I_{2}(S)
. Since
S^{\prime}\supset S
, we have
I_{2}(S^{\prime})\subset I_{2}(S)
. On the other hand, if
f\in I_{2}(S)
then by definition of
S^{\prime}
, it follows that
f
vanishes on
S^{\prime}
, and hence
f\in I_{2}(S^{\prime})
, i.e.
I_{2}(S)\subset I_{2}(S^{\prime})
. Thus,
I_{2}(S^{\prime})=I_{2}(S)
.

As for ii), if
T\supset S
has rank
r
, then the fact that
\text{rank}(T)=\text{rank}(S)=r
implies
I_{2}(S)=I_{2}(T)
, and hence
T\subset V(I_{2}(T))=V(I_{2}(S))=S^{\prime}
.
We define
\bar{S}=V(I_{2}(S))
.
∎

The next lemma allows us to build
\mathcal{C}_{r}
’s by adding points to each
\mathcal{C}_{r-1}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
S
is a
\mathcal{C}_{r}
, and
T\subset S
then
\bar{T}\subset S
. In particular,
S
contains a
\mathcal{C}_{r-1}
configuration
T
.

Proof.

Since
T\subset S
, we get
I_{2}(S)\subset I_{2}(T)
, and hence
\bar{T}=V(I_{2}(T))\subset V(I_{2}(S))=S
.

We delete points of
S
one at a time until the rank drops by one. If
S^{\prime}
is the resulting rank
(r-1)
configuration, then
T=\overline{S^{\prime}}
is a
\mathcal{C}_{r-1}
configuration contained in
S
. ∎

In order to determine the rank
r
configurations of a given size
j
, it suffices to look for size
j
and rank
r
subsets of each
\mathcal{C}_{r}
. There are two steps in our approach.

Step 1
\rangle

Determine the set
\mathfrak{C}_{r}
of all
\mathcal{C}_{r}
’s. i.e., the collection of all maximal rank
r
configurations. We recursively construct all
\mathcal{C}_{r}
configurations by adding points to
\mathcal{C}_{r-1}
configurations. We will also obtain a decomposition,

\mathfrak{C}_{r}=\cup_{\alpha\in\{a,b,c,\dots\}}\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}.

Each of the parts
\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}
will be a class of maximal configurations consisting of a union of
G
-orbits of
\mathcal{C}_{r}
’s. We will also determine the size of each class
\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}
.

Step 2
\rangle

For each class
M_{r\alpha}
and each integer
j>r
, we will determine

B_{j,r}(\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha})
, which is the number of rank
r
configurations of size
j
, contained in a configuration
T
representing the class
\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}
.

From Step 1 and Step 2, we calculate
B_{j,r}
for
j>r
by:

(12)

B_{j,r}=\sum_{\alpha\in\{a,b,c,\dots\}}|\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}|\cdot B_{j,r}(\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}),\qquad j>r.

The quantity
B_{r,r}
is determined by the identity

B_{r,r}=\tbinom{N}{r}-\sum_{i<r}B_{r,i}.

We also give a systematic procedure to
determine the quantities
B_{j,r}(\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha})
recursively with respect to
r
:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
T
be a
\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha}
configuration. For each
i<r
, let

\mathfrak{C}_{i}(T)
denote the collection of all
\mathcal{C}_{i}
configurations that are contained in
T
, and that have at least
(r+1)
points. We can write

\mathfrak{C}_{i}(T)=\cup_{\beta\in\{a,b,c,\dots\}}\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)
where
\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)
denotes the class of configurations
\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}
that are contained in
T
and have at least
r+1
points.
We have

(13)

B_{j,r}(\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha})=\tbinom{|T|}{j}-\sum_{i<r}\sum_{\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)\in\mathfrak{C}_{i}(T)}|\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)|\cdot B_{j,i}(\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)).

Proof.

In order to obtain
B_{j,r}(\mathcal{M}_{r\alpha})
, we must subtract from
\tbinom{|T|}{j}
, the number of
j
point configurations
S\subset T
having rank
at most
r-1
. If
S\subset T
is a
j
point configuration of rank
i<r
, then by Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.3
,
\bar{S}
is the unique
\mathcal{M}_{i\beta}(T)
configuration containing
S
. Thus, the second term in the right hand side of (
13
) exactly counts all the
j
-point configurations in
T
that have rank strictly less than
r
.

∎

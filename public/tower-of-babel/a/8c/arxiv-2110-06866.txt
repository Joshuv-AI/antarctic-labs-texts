Appendix A
Practical Implementation of BLR and MarBLR

In this manuscript, we implement MarBLR using a Laplace approximation of the logistic posterior and perform Kalman filtering with collapsing
(
Gordon and Smith 1990
;
West and Harrison 1997
)
.
Because BLR corresponds to MarBLR with
\alpha=0
and
\delta^{2}=0
, we use this same procedure to perform approximate Bayesian inference.
The Kalman filtering approach is simple and computationally efficient; We describe the steps below.
We note that for the special case of BLR, one can also perform posterior inference by sampling Polya-Gamma latent variables
(
Polson et al. 2013
)
.
This would allow one to perform full Bayesian inference but is significantly more costly in terms of computation time.

We make predictions and update the posterior using the following recursive procedure.
The process is initialized with the Gaussian prior for
\theta_{1}
with mean
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
and posterior covariance
\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}
.
Let
D^{(t)}
denote the observations up to and including time
t
.

Prediction step.
At time
t
, let the approximation for
\theta_{t-1}|W_{t-1}=w_{t-1},D^{(t-1)}
be the Gaussian distribution with mean
\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}}
and covariance
\hat{\Sigma}_{t-1,w_{t-1}}
.
We also assume
\Pr(W_{t-1}=w_{t-1}|D^{(t-1)})
is known.
We generate predictions at time
t
using the posterior distribution
\theta_{t}|D^{(t-1)}
, which is a mixture of the distributions

\displaystyle\theta_{t}|W_{t}=w_{t},W_{t-1}=w_{t-1},D^{(t-1)}\sim N\left(\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}},(1+\delta^{2}w_{t})\hat{\Sigma}_{t-1,w_{t-1}}\right)

(1)

for
w_{t},w_{t=1}\in\{0,1\}
with weights by
\Pr(W_{t}=w_{t}|W_{t-1}=w_{t-1})\Pr(W_{t-1}=w_{t-1}|D^{(t-1)})
. Recall that
\Pr(W_{t}=1|W_{t-1}=w_{t-1})=\alpha
in the MarBLR prior.
We predict that
Y=1
for a subject
x
using the posterior mean of
\Pr(Y=1|X=x)
.

Update step.
Next, we observe a new batch of labeled observations and update the posterior.
That is, we must perform inference for
\theta_{t}|D^{(t)}
, which is a mixture of the distributions
\theta_{t}|W_{t}=w_{t},W_{t-1}=w_{t-1},D^{(t)}
with probability weights
\Pr(W_{t}=w_{t},W_{t-1}=w_{t-1}|D^{(t)})
for
w_{t},w_{t-1}\in\{0,1\}
.
Let
\tilde{\ell}_{t}(\theta,w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}|z_{t,i},\theta\right)+\log p(\theta\mid w,D^{(t-1)})
.
We approximate the distribution
\theta_{t}|W_{t}=w_{t},W_{t-1}=w_{t-1},D^{(t)}
using a Gaussian distribution with its mean computed using a Newton update

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},w_{t-1}}=\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}}-\left[\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\tilde{\ell}_{t}\left(\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}},w_{t-1}\right)\right]^{-1}\nabla_{\theta}\tilde{\ell}_{t}\left(\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}},w_{t-1}\right)

(2)

and its covariance as

\hat{\Sigma}_{t,w_{t},w_{t-1}}=\left[\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\tilde{\ell}_{t}(\hat{\theta}_{t-1,w_{t-1}},w_{t-1})\right]^{-1}.

The probability
\Pr(W_{t}=w_{t},W_{t-1}=w_{t-1}\mid D^{(t)})
, which is proportional to

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\theta_{t}}p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot},\theta_{t}\right)p\left(\theta_{t}\mid w_{t},w_{t-1},D^{(t-1)}\right)d\theta_{t}\right]\Pr(W_{t}|W_{t-1})\Pr(W_{t-1}|D^{(t-1)}),

(3)

is approximated using a Laplace approximation for the integral in (
3
), i.e.

\displaystyle 2\pi^{d/2}\left|\left\{\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\tilde{\ell}(\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},w_{t-1}})\right\}^{-1}\right|^{1/2}p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot},\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},w_{t-1}}\right)p\left(\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},w_{t-1}}\mid D^{(t-1)}\right).

(4)

Let
\hat{q}_{w_{t},w_{t-1}}
denote the estimated probability.
Finally, we approximate the posterior distribution
\theta_{t}|W_{t}=w_{t},D^{(t)}
using a single Gaussian distribution by moment-matching
(
West and Harrison 1997
;
Orguner and Demırekler 2007
)
with mean and covariance

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t}}=\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{w_{t},0}+\hat{q}_{w_{t},1}}\left(\hat{q}_{w_{t},0}\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},0}+\hat{q}_{w_{t},1}\hat{\theta}_{t,w_{t},1}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{t,w_{t}}=\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{w_{t},0}+\hat{q}_{w_{t},1}}\left(\hat{q}_{w_{t},0}\hat{\Sigma}_{t,w_{t},0}+\hat{q}_{w_{t},1}\hat{\Sigma}_{t,w_{t},1}\right).

(6)

Appendix B
Online model revision for batched data

In certain settings, it is more convenient and practical for the data stream to be observed in batches of size
n>1
.
Here we discuss the necessary modifications to our framework for analyzing the performance on an online model reviser for batched data.
We denote a batch of observations as
\{(x_{t,i},y_{t,i}):i=1,....,n\}
and use the notation
a_{t,\cdot}
to denote the sequence
(a_{t,1},...,a_{t,n})
.

We extend the online model reviser to output a probability distribution over all possible outcomes for a batch of observations, i.e.
\hat{A}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{X}^{n}\mapsto\Delta_{2^{n}}
where
\Delta_{2^{n}}
is the probability simplex over all possible outcomes
(y_{t,1},...,y_{t,n})
.
The loss of the online model reviser over the entire time period is then defined as the average negative log likelihood

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{nT}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot},\hat{A}_{t}\left(\hat{f}_{t}(x_{t,\cdot}),x_{t,\cdot}\right)\right).

(7)

This theoretical framework allows predictions from the online model reviser to depend on all unlabeled observations
\{x_{t,i}:i=1,...,n\}
.
By defining regret with respect to (
7
), we are able to derive Type I and II regret bounds for the batched setting.
This is necessary for analyzing BLR and MarBLR because outcomes are not independent given the observations up to time
t
in the Bayesian framework.
In particular, the outcomes are correlated because of the shared (latent) revision parameter
\theta_{t}
.

Appendix C
Type I and II Regret bounds

C.1
Notation and assumptions

We suppose there are
n
observations at time points
t=1,...,T
for some
T\geq 2
.
Consider any sequence of revision parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{T}\}
, where
\theta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
t=1,2,\ldots,T
,
with unique values at times
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},...,\tau_{s}\}
, where
\tau_{1}=1<\tau_{2}<\ldots<\tau_{s}\leq T
.
In other words,
\{\theta_{\tau_{1}},\theta_{\tau_{2}},\ldots,\theta_{\tau_{s}}\}
denotes the sequence of values that the sequence
\boldsymbol{\theta}
shifted over.
Henceforth, we use
|\boldsymbol{\tau}|
(rather than
s
) to indicate the number of times in
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
For ease of notation, we use the convention
\tau_{|\tau|+1}:=T+1
. Note that the variable
\tau_{|\tau|+1}
is not part of the sequence
\boldsymbol{\tau}
and is used purely to simplify the notation.
We use
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{locked}}:=\{\tau_{1}\}=\{1\}
to denote the shift times in the edge case of “locked” sequences
\boldsymbol{\theta}
that do not shift over time.
Let
D^{(t)}
denote all the data observed up to time
t
.

The cumulative negative log-likelihood when using Bayesian inference at each time point is

L_{\mathrm{BF}}=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot},D^{(t-1)}\right),

where
p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot},D^{(t-1)}\right)
is the posterior distribution at time
t
.
The cumulative negative log-likelihoods for MarBLR and BLR are denoted by
L_{\mathrm{MarBLR}}
and
L_{\mathrm{BLR}}
, respectively, and are special cases of
L_{\mathrm{BF}}
for their specific choice of priors.
The MarBLR prior
p_{0}
over
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is defined using a Gaussian random walk with a homogeneous transition matrix as follows.
Given
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and some shift probability
\alpha\in[0,1]
, let

\theta_{1}\sim N(\theta_{\mathrm{init}},\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}),\quad W_{1}=1,

(8)

and for
t=2,3,\ldots,T
let

\displaystyle\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=\theta_{t-1}+\beta_{t}W_{t}

(9)

\displaystyle W_{t}

\displaystyle\sim\Bernoulli(\alpha)

\displaystyle\beta_{t}

\displaystyle\sim N(0,\delta^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}).

Note that
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\left\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\right\}
can be regarded as the indices at which the sequence
\left\{W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{T}\right\}
is
1
-valued. In particular, having
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{locked}}
implies that
W_{1}=1
and
W_{t}=0
for all
t>1
.
The BLR prior is a special case where
\delta^{2}=\alpha=0
.

Type I regret compares BLR and MarBLR to locking the original revision parameters at its initial value
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
, i.e.
\theta_{t}=\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
for all
t\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,T\right\}
.
The cumulative negative log-likelihood of the locked initial model is given by

L_{\mathrm{locked}}=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot};\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right).

Type II
\boldsymbol{\tau}
-regret compares BLR and MarBLR to the best sequence of parameters in retrospect for update times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, denoted
\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}
for
j=1,...,|\tau|
.
Its cumulative negative log-likelihood is defined as

L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot};\tilde{\theta}_{t}\right)

where
\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}
for
j=1,...,|\tau|
satisfy

\left.\nabla\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}}=0,\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|.

(10)

In addition, we introduce the notion of a distribution over the sequences

\boldsymbol{\theta}
. For such a distribution

Q
, its expected negative log-likelihood is given by

L_{Q}=E_{Q}\left[-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot};\theta_{t}\right)\right].

Given mean and variance parameters
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{t})_{t\in\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=(\Sigma_{t})_{t\in\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, we define
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\mu},\Sigma}
to be the distribution over
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with shift times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
where
\theta_{\tau_{j}}
for
j=1,...,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|
are jointly independent and normally distributed per

\theta_{\tau_{j}}\sim N\left(\mu_{\tau_{j}},\Sigma_{j}\right).

(11)

Some results in the following sections rely on the assumption that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w^{2}}\log p\left(y|z^{\top}\theta=w\right)\right|\leq c,

(12)

for all
y
and
w
.
This always holds for logistic regression with
c\leq 1
.

C.2
Useful Results

Consider the prior distribution
p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
over sequences
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.
Let
p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
be its marginal distribution over shift times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
and
p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)
be the conditional distribution over sequences
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with shift times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.

Lemma 1

(Variational bound)
.

Consider any prior distribution
p_{0}
over sequences
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.
Given any
\boldsymbol{\tau}
and any distribution
Q
, it holds that

L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{Q}\leq\E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim Q}\left[\KL\left(Q(\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right)\right]+\KL\left(Q(\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right).

Proof.

First, we can reexpress the cumulative negative log-likelihood of the Bayesian dynamical model by chaining the conditional probabilities as follows:

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot},D^{(t-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\right).

Similarly, the cumulative negative log-likelihood of any sequence of calibration parameters can be written as

-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p\left(y_{t,\cdot}\mid z_{t,\cdot};\theta_{t}\right)=-\log p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T};\boldsymbol{\theta}\right).

Thus, the difference in the cumulative negative log-likelihood between the Bayesian dynamical model and any sequence of parameters is given by

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{Q}

\displaystyle=\E_{Q}\left[\log\frac{p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T};\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)}{p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\right)}\right].

By Bayes’ Rule, the posterior distribution
p_{T}
over
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with respect to the Bayesian dynamical model satisfies

p_{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)=\frac{p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T};\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)}{p\left((y_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\mid(z_{t,\cdot})_{t=1,...,T}\right)}.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{Q}

\displaystyle=\E_{Q}\left[\log\frac{p_{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)}{p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)}\right]

(13)

\displaystyle=\E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim Q}\left[\E_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim Q(\cdot|\boldsymbol{\tau})}\left[\log\frac{p_{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)}{p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)}\right]\right]+\E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim Q}\left[\log\frac{p_{T}(\boldsymbol{\tau})}{p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\tau})}\right].

Moreover, because the KL divergence is always positive, it holds that

\displaystyle\E_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim Q(\cdot|\boldsymbol{\tau})}\left[\log\frac{p_{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)}{p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\KL\left(Q(\cdot|\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right)-\KL\left(Q(\cdot|\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right)

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\KL\left(Q(\cdot|\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right).

Likewise,

\displaystyle E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim Q}\left[\log\frac{p_{T}(\boldsymbol{\tau})}{p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\tau})}\right]\leq\KL\left(Q(\boldsymbol{\tau})\mid\mid p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\tau})\right).

(15)

Finally, by combining equations (
13
) and (
14
) we arrive at the conclusion of this theorem.
∎

C.3
Type I regret results for MarBLR

Let the distribution
p_{0}
be the MarBLR prior as defined per (
8
) and (
9
).
For a given
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, let
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}
be a Gaussian random walk with expected shifts
\beta_{j}
at known shift times
\tau_{j}
for
j=1,...,|\tau|
.
That is,

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\tau_{j-1}}\sim N\left(\beta_{j},\nu^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\right).

(16)

and

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau_{1}}\sim N\left(\mu_{1},\epsilon_{1}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\right).

(17)

We begin with simplifying the KL divergence term in Lemma
1
.

Lemma 2
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, consider the Gaussian random walk
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}
.
We have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\KL\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right)=&\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}d+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-\frac{d|\tau|}{2}\\
&+d(|\tau|-1)\log\frac{\delta}{\nu}-d\log\epsilon_{1}\\
&+\frac{d\nu^{2}(|\tau|-1)}{2\delta^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{|\tau|}\beta_{j}^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\beta_{j}.\end{split}

(18)

Proof.

For ease of notation, let
\Theta_{J}
be the space over sequences

\left(\theta_{1},...,\theta_{J}\right)
. Given the known times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, there is a one-to-one mapping from sequences
in
\Theta_{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}
to sequences in
\Theta_{T}
with unique values
at times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
Let
Q^{\mathrm{sub}}
be the probability distribution over
\Theta_{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}

as defined by
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}
. Likewise, let
p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}

be the PDF over
\Theta_{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}
as defined by the conditional
prior distribution
p_{0}\left(\cdot\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)
.

We have that

\displaystyle\KL\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\cdots\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{1})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{1})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{1})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{|\tau|-1}\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j},{\theta}_{j+1})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j+1}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j}\end{split}

(19)

The first term in (
19
) is the KL divergence of two multivariate Normal distributions,
N(\mu_{1},\epsilon_{1}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})
and
N(\theta_{\mathrm{init}},\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})
, and can be shown to be equal to

\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}d+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-d\log\epsilon_{1}-\frac{d}{2}.

(20)

Also, for
j=1,...,|\tau|-1
, we have that each summand in the second term in (
19
) is equal to

\displaystyle\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j},{\theta}_{j+1})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j+1}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\log\frac{\delta}{\nu}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j},{\theta}_{j+1})\left({\theta}_{j+1}-{\theta}_{j}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left({\theta}_{j+1}-{\theta}_{j}\right)\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j+1}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j}-\frac{d}{2}.

(21)

By the definition of
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}
, we have that
\Delta_{j+1}=\theta_{\tau_{j+1}}-\theta_{\tau_{j}}\sim N\left(\beta_{j+1},\nu^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\right)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j},{\theta}_{j+1})\left({\theta}_{j+1}-{\theta}_{j}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left({\theta}_{j+1}-{\theta}_{j}\right)\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j+1}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j}=

\displaystyle d\nu^{2}+\beta_{j+1}^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\beta_{j+1}.

Plugging in the above result into (
21
), we have that

\displaystyle\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j},{\theta}_{j+1})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{j+1}\mid{\theta}_{j})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j+1}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{j}=

\displaystyle d\log\frac{\delta}{\nu}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\left(d\nu^{2}+\beta_{j+1}^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\beta_{j+1}\right)-\frac{d}{2}.

(22)

Combining the results (
19
), (
20
) and (
22
), we attain the desired conclusion.
∎

To bound the Type I regret for MarBLR, we compare the regret via the intermediary
Q

over

Lemma 3
.

Let the distribution
p_{0}
be defined as above.
Let distribution

over

have the same distribution over

, with

distributed

Let

We have that

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{Q}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}d-\frac{d\xi}{2}+d(\xi-1)\log\frac{\delta}{\nu}-d\log\epsilon_{1}+\frac{d\nu^{2}(\xi-1)}{2\delta^{2}}.

(24)

Proof.

Next we bound

Lemma 4
.

Assume that there is a
c>0
that bounds the second derivative as in (
12
).
Assume that there is an
R
such that
\frac{1}{n(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\preceq R^{2}I
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|\}
.
Let
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}
be the zero-centered Gaussian random walk with
\mu_{1}=\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
.
Then it holds that

L_{Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}^{(W)}}-L_{\mathrm{locked}}\leq\frac{cnR^{2}}{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})\left(T\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\nu^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{|\tau|}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})(j-1)\right).

Proof.

We use a Taylor expansion. For
j=1,...,|\tau|
, there is some
\theta_{mid}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta_{\tau_{j}}\right)=&-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-\left.\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{init}}}^{\top}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\\
&\ -\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\left.\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\theta_{mid}}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right).\end{split}

(25)

Note that

\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\log p\left(y\mid z;\theta\right)\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w^{2}}\log p\left(y|w\right)\left(z^{\top}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\right)^{2},

where
w=z^{\top}\theta
is the predicted logit. Using equation (
12
) it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\left.\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\theta_{mid}}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\right|

(26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{t,i}^{\top}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\right)^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right).

(28)

Because the expected value of
\theta
with respect to
Q
is
\theta_{\mathrm{init}}
, we have the following after taking the expectation of equation (
25
) combined with equation (
26
):

\displaystyle L_{Q}

\displaystyle=\E_{Q}\left[-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathrm{locked}}+\sum_{j=1}^{|\tau|}\frac{c}{2}\E_{Q}\left[\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\right].

Assuming there exists some
R^{2}
that satisfies the lemma assumptions, the following holds after taking the expectation with respect to
Q
:

\displaystyle\E_{Q}\left[\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})nR^{2}\E_{Q}\|\theta_{\tau_{j}}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})nR^{2}\left(\epsilon_{1}^{2}+(j-1)\nu^{2}\right)\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}).

After summing over
j=1,...,|\tau|
, we reach our desired result.
∎

We combine the two prior lemmas to obtain the following bound on the Type I error for MarBLR.

Theorem 5

(Type I regret for MarBLR)
.

Let
\xi=\E_{p_{0}}|\boldsymbol{\tau}|
denote the expected number of shift times be denoted.
The Type I regret for MarBLR is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{MarBLR}}-L_{\mathrm{locked}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{cnR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{d\alpha(T-1)}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta^{2}cnR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}{2d}\right).

Proof.

First, note that under the MarBLR prior
p_{0}
over shift times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
as defined previously, we have that

\E_{p_{0}}\left[\sum_{j=2}^{|\tau|}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})(j-1)\right]=\E_{p_{0}}\left[\sum_{t=2}^{T}W_{t}(T+1-t)\right]=\frac{\alpha}{2}T(T-1),

and
\xi=\E_{p_{0}}|\boldsymbol{\tau}|=\alpha(T-1)+1
.

Thus, summing the upper bounds from Lemmas
3
and
4
and taking expectations with respect to
\boldsymbol{\tau}\sim p_{0}
, we have that

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{\mathrm{locked}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}d-\frac{d\alpha(T-1)}{2}-\frac{d}{2}+d\alpha(T-1)\log\frac{\delta}{\nu}-d\log\epsilon_{1}+\frac{d\alpha(T-1)}{2\delta^{2}}\nu^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle+\frac{cnR^{2}T}{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})\left(\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}(T-1)\nu^{2}\right).

(30)

We minimize the upper bound by selecting

\epsilon_{1}^{2}=\frac{d}{d+cnR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}

and

\nu^{2}=\frac{d}{\frac{d}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{c}{2}nR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}

to obtain the upper bound

\frac{d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{cnR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}{d}\right)+\frac{d\alpha(T-1)}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta^{2}cnR^{2}T\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})}{2d}\right).

∎

Lemma 6
.

Assume that the second derivative is bounded by a constant

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{Q}}-L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{cn\sum_{j=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}R_{j}^{2}\left(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j}\right)}{2}\epsilon^{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})+\frac{cn}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}R_{j}^{2}\left(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j}\right)\left\|\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{locked}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{2}.

Because

4

\E_{\tilde{Q}}\left[-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right]\leq-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\\

\tilde{Q}

\displaystyle\E_{\tilde{Q}}\left[\left(\theta_{1}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{1}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\right]

.

Lemma 8
.

Consider the distribution
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}}
as defined above, and the MarBLR prior
p_{0}
as defined per (
8
) and (
9
).
For any
\boldsymbol{\tau}
,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
and
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle KL\left(Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}}\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\tau\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-d\log\epsilon_{1}-\frac{d}{2}|\boldsymbol{\tau}|+(|\boldsymbol{\tau}|-1)d\log\delta

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=2}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\left[\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\left(d\left(\epsilon_{t-1}^{2}+\epsilon_{t}^{2}\right)+\left(\mu_{t}-\mu_{t-1}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\mu_{t}-\mu_{t-1}\right)\right)-d\log\epsilon_{t}\right].

Proof.

We define
\Theta_{J}
and
p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}
as in Lemma
2
.
We define
Q^{\mathrm{sub}}
as the distribution over
\Theta_{J}
as defined by
Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}}

We have that

\displaystyle KL\left(Q_{\tau,(\mu_{t})_{t\in\tau}}\mid\mid p_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\tau\right)\right)

\displaystyle=KL\left(Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\mid\mid p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\cdots\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\sum_{t=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{t}\right)}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{t}\mid\theta_{t-1}\right)}d\theta_{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\cdots d\theta_{1},

(33)

because
\theta_{t}
in

are jointly independent and
\theta_{t}
in

\theta_{t-1}

As such,

\displaystyle KL\left(Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\mid\mid p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)}d\theta_{1}

(34)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=2}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{t-1},\theta_{t}\right)\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{t}\right)}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{t}\mid\theta_{t-1}\right)}d\theta_{t}d\theta_{t-1}.

and

, and can be shown to be equal to

\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}\left(\theta_{1}\right)}d\theta_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}d+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-d\log\epsilon_{1}-\frac{d}{2}.

(36)

Next each term in the summation of (

) is equal to

\displaystyle\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t-1},{\theta}_{t})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t}\mid{\theta}_{t-1})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\log\frac{\delta}{\epsilon_{t}}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t-1},{\theta}_{t})\left({\theta}_{t}-{\theta}_{t-1}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left({\theta}_{t}-{\theta}_{t-1}\right)\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t-1}-\frac{d}{2}.

(37)

We note that under

it holds that

(\theta_{t}-\theta_{t-1})\sim N\left(\mu_{t}-\mu_{t-1},(\epsilon_{t-1}^{2}+\epsilon_{t}^{2})\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\right).

Therefore, (
37
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\int\int Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t-1},{\theta}_{t})\log\frac{Q^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t})}{p_{0}^{\mathrm{sub}}({\theta}_{t}\mid{\theta}_{t-1})}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t}\mathrm{d}{\theta}_{t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\log\frac{\delta}{\epsilon_{t}}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\left(d\left(\epsilon_{t-1}^{2}+\epsilon_{t}^{2}\right)+(\mu_{t}-\mu_{t-1})^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}(\mu_{t}-\mu_{t-1})\right)-\frac{d}{2}.

(38)

Plugging (

) gives us the desired result.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose there is a constant
c
that bounds the second derivative as in (
12
).
Assume that there is an
R
such that
\frac{1}{n(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\preceq R^{2}I
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|\}
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle L_{Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}}}-L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}cnR^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})\left(\epsilon_{k(j)}^{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})+\left\|\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{2}\right)

where
k(j):=\max\{k:\tau^{\prime}_{k}\leq\tau_{j}\}
.

Proof.

For the ease of notation denote
\tilde{Q}:=Q_{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}}
.
It holds that

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{Q}}-L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}\left(E_{\tilde{Q}}\left[\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta_{t}\right)+\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\right]\right)

Recall that for any sequence
\boldsymbol{\theta}
drawn from
\tilde{Q}
, for any
j=1,\ldots,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|
, the parameters
\theta_{t}
are constant over
t=\tau^{\prime}_{j},\ldots,\tau^{\prime}_{j+1}-1
.
Taking a Taylor expansion, there exists some
\theta_{mid}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\middle|z_{t,i};\theta\right)&=-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\middle|z_{t,i};\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\\
&\quad\quad-\left.\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\middle|z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}}^{\top}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)^{\top}\left.\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\middle|z_{t,i};\theta\right)\right|_{\theta=\theta_{mid}}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right).\end{split}

(39)

Since
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}
is a subsequence of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
, for
\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\tilde{Q}
we have that
\theta_{t}=\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}
for all
t=\tau_{j},....,\tau_{j+1}-1
, where
k(j):=\max\{k:\tau^{\prime}_{k}\leq\tau_{j}\}
. Thus, we can use the above decomposition to evaluate (
39
) with
\theta_{t}=\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k_{j}}}
in place of
\theta
.

By the definition of
\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}
, the gradient in the expression above is zero, so the second term is equal to zero. Because we assumed the second derivative was bounded by
c
as in (
12
), the expression simplifies to the bound

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\theta_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p\left(y_{t,i}\mid z_{t,i};\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{2}\left(\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right).

Assuming there exists some
R^{2}
that satisfies the lemma assumptions, it follows that

\displaystyle\E_{\tilde{Q}}\left[\left(\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\right)\left(\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})nR^{2}\E_{\tilde{Q}}\left\|\theta_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})nR^{2}\left(\epsilon_{k(j)}^{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})+\left\|\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{2}\right).

We finish the proof by summing over
j
.
∎

We combine the results to get the following bound.

Theorem 10

(Type II regret for MarBLR)
.

Suppose there is a constant
c
that bounds the second derivative as in (
12
).
Assume that there is an
R
such that
\frac{1}{n(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})}\sum_{t=\tau_{j}}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t,i}z_{t,i}^{\top}\preceq R^{2}I
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,|\boldsymbol{\tau}|\}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}
be any subsequence of the sequence of shift times
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{MarBLR}}-L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)+\frac{d}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{cnR^{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})\left(\tau^{\prime}_{2}-\tau^{\prime}_{1}\right)}{d}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=2}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}|}\left[\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}}\right)+d\log\left(\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{cnR^{2}\Tr(\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}})\left(\tau^{\prime}_{j+1}-\tau^{\prime}_{j}\right)}{d}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\log p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime})+(|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}|-1)d\log\delta+\frac{1}{2}cnR^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}|}(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j})\left\|\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{k(j)}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{2}.

Proof.

By combining Lemmas
1
,
8
and

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{BF}}-L_{\mathrm{Dyn},\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{2}\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}-\theta_{\mathrm{init}}\right)-d\log\epsilon_{1}-\frac{d}{2}|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}|+(|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}|-1)d\log\delta

(40)

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=2}^{|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\prime}|}\left[\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\left(d\left(\epsilon_{t-1}^{2}+\epsilon_{t}^{2}\right)+\left(\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}}\right)^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{-1}\left(\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t}}-\tilde{\theta}_{\tau^{\prime}_{t-1}}\right)\right)-d\log\epsilon_{t}\right]

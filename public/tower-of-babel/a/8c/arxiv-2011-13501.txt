Abstract
. We study the stabilization and the wellposedness of solutions of the wave equation with subcritical semilinearities and locally distributed nonlinear dissipation. The novelty of this paper is that we deal with the difficulty that the main equation does not have good nonlinear structure amenable to a direct proof of a priori bounds and a desirable observability inequality. It is well known that observability inequalities play a critical role in characterizing the long time behaviour of solutions of evolution equations, which is the main goal of this study. In order to address this, we truncate the nonlinearities, and thereby construct approximate solutions for which it is possible to obtain a priori bounds and prove the essential observability inequality. The treatment of these approximate solutions is still a challenging task and requires the use of Strichartz estimates and some microlocal analysis tools such as microlocal defect measures. We include an appendix on the latter topic here to make the article self contained and supplement details to proofs of some of the theorems which can be already be found in the lecture notes of [ 7 ] . Once we establish essential observability properties for the approximate solutions, it is not difficult to prove that the solution of the original problem also possesses a similar feature via a delicate passage to limit. In the last part of the paper, we establish various decay rate estimates for different growth conditions on the nonlinear dissipative effect. We in particular generalize the known results on the subject to a considerably larger class of dissipative effects.

1.
Introduction

In this article, our purpose is to study the wellposedness and decay properties of solutions of the wave equation with subcritical semilinearities and locally distributed nonlinear dissipation. More precisely, we consider the initial boundary value problem given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+f(u)+a(x)g(\partial_{t}u)=0&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u=0&~\hbox{ on }~\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x)&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) is a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
f
and
g
are real valued functions satisfying Assumption
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
below, respectively:

Assumption 1.1
.

f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with

(1.2)

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\ \ \ |f^{(j)}(s)|\leq k_{0}(1+|s|)^{p-j},j=1,2,\forall s\in\mathbb{R},

for some constant
k_{0}>0
, where

(1.3)

1\leq p<\frac{n+2}{n-2}\hbox{ if }n\geq 3\hbox{ and }p\geq 1\hbox{ if }n=1,2.

In addition,

(1.4)

\displaystyle 0\leq F(s)\leq f(s)s,\forall s\in\mathbb{R},

where
F(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\lambda}f(s)\,ds
.

Remark 1.1
.

(
1.2
) in particular gives

(1.5)

\displaystyle|f(r)-f(s)|\leq c\left(1+|s|^{p-1}+|r|^{p-1}\right)|r-s|,\forall s,r\in\mathbb{R}

for some
c>0
.

Assumption 1.2
.

The feedback function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous, monotone increasing and also satisfies
the following:

(1.6)

\displaystyle g(s)s>0\hbox{ for }s\neq 0,\hbox{ and }\tilde{m}s^{r+1}\leq g\left(s\right)s\leq\tilde{M}s^{r+1}\hbox{ for }\left|s\right|>1,

where
\tilde{m}
and
\tilde{M}
are positive constants. In addition, we assume there is a continuous, concave and strictly increasing function
h_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying

(1.7)

h_{0}(g(s)s)\geq s^{2}+g(s)^{2},~|s|\leq 1.

It is not difficult to construct such a function by using (
1.6
).

In what follows, we will write
a(s)\lesssim b(s)
in the sense
a(s)\leq Cb(s)
, where
C>0
is a constant. Moreover,
a(s)\sim b(s)
is used whenever
a(s)\lesssim b(s)
and
b(s)\lesssim a(s)
. We will classify the nonlinear feedbacks of various growth rates by using the notion of the polynomial order at infinity:

Definition 1.1
.

A monotone increasing map
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
g(0)=0
, is of the order
r:=\mathcal{O}(g)\geq 0
at infinity if

(1.8)

\begin{split}|s|^{r+1}\sim g(s)s&\quad\text{for}\quad|s|\geq 1.\end{split}

If the order
r
is bigger than, less than, or equal to
1
we say the map
f
is superlinear, sublinear, or linearly bounded at infinity, respectively.

We in addition assume that the damping coefficient satisfies the following:

Assumption 1.3
.

a(x)
is nonnegative and belongs to
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. Moreover,
a(x)\geq a_{0}>0
on a neighborhood
\omega
of the entire boundary
\partial\Omega
.

We associate the following energy functional with problem (
1.1
):

(1.9)

\displaystyle E_{u}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}+|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}F(u(x,t))\,dx,

where
F
is the antiderivative of
f
vanishing at zero.

In order to obtain the stabilization of the nonlinear energy
E_{u}(t)
associated with problem (
1.1
), two ingredients are essential: (A): the energy identity:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\qquad E_{u}(t_{2})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)g(\partial_{t}u(x,t))\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,dxdt=E_{u}(t_{1})

for all
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<+\infty
and
(B): the observability inequality: there exists some constant
C=C(\|\{u_{0},u_{1}\}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)})>0
so that

(1.11)

\displaystyle E_{u}(0)\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|g(\partial_{t}u)|^{2}\right)\,dxdt

holds true for all
T\geq T_{0}
(where
T_{0}
is a certain time for which the so called geometric control condition holds). Moreover, for the given geometry we take the following granted.

Assumption 1.4
.

Given any
T\geq T_{0}
, the unique solution

u\in C(]0,T[;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}(]0,T[,H^{-1}(\Omega))

that satisfies

(1.12)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+V(x,t)u=0&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=0&~\hbox{ on }~\omega,\\
\end{cases}

where
V\in L^{\infty}(]0,T[,L^{\infty}(\Omega))
, is the trivial one.

The approach based on the ingredients (A) and (B) is a well known strategy in stabilization theory; see for example
[
10
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
49
]
and references therein. These papers work directly on the original problem (
1.1
) and use a

as a crucial ingredient for proving the observability inequality. However, the direct approach has a challenge that was explained in
[
22
]
as follows:

At first one argues by contradiction that the observability fails and thereby obtains a sequence of (suitably normalized) solutions violating the desired inequality. Then, via passage to the limit, one gets a function
u
in the energy space, which solves

(1.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+f(u)=0~\hbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,T)

with
\partial_{t}u|_{\omega}\equiv 0
. The goal is to show that (
1.13
) has a unique solution and it is zero because this would mean that the only solution of (
1.1
) without damping is the zero solution, which is absurd. To this end, one can differentiate in time, set
w=\partial_{t}u
and consider the problem

(1.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}w-\Delta w+f^{\prime}(u)w=0~\hbox{in}~\Omega\times(0,T)

with
w|_{\omega}\equiv 0
. The next step is then to apply a unique continuation argument to the above problem that has a lower order potential to deduce that
w\equiv 0
. This would imply that
u
is time independent, and therefore
u
becomes the solution of a stationary semilinear elliptic problem whose solution is certainly zero thanks to right sign of the nonlinearity. Some unique continuation results assuming rather strong integrability conditions for the potential are available in the literature
[
23
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
46
]
,
[
48
]
. Part of these results are valid only locally while others work globally. However, none of them directly applies to the given class of nonlinearities because of the less restrictive assumptions on the power index.

The difficulty explained above with the direct approach was treated in
[
22
]
by unraveling hidden regularity properties associated with the potential
f^{\prime}(u)
in the case the domain is
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}
. To handle the same difficulty on a general domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we first introduce a sequence of truncated problems rather than directly working on the original problem (
1.1
):

(1.15)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{k}-\Delta u_{k}+f_{k}(u_{k})+a(x)g(\partial_{t}u_{k})=0&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u_{k}=0&~\hbox{ on }~\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u_{k}(x,0)=u_{0,k}(x),~\partial_{t}u_{k}(x,0)=u_{1,k}(x)&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f_{k}
is defined in (
2.3
) and
\{u_{0,k},u_{1,k}\}
is a sequence of initial regular data that approaches
\{u_{0},u_{1}\}
in the phase space. Such truncations were previously used within the context of stabilization of nonlinear wave equations
[
33
]
or the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
[
41
]
,
[
12
]
. Once we show that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{k}
possesses a strong regularity, then we can exploit additional regularity properties of the nonlinear term
f_{k}(u)
by utilizing Strichartz estimates that are valid for solutions of the subcritical wave equation. Next, the ellipticity
on the domain
\omega\times(0,T)
and the property of propagation of singularities provide a desirable regularity for
u_{k}
and integrability for
f_{k}^{\prime}(u_{k})
. Only then, one can utilize the unique continuation results cited above. Since the sequence of problems (
1.15
) converges to the original problem (
1.1
), it is sufficient to obtain the energy identity and the observability inequality for each truncated problem because the same properties as well as the decay remain valid for the original problem via a delicate passage to the limit. An advantage of the present approach is that we do not need to consider the well known due to Gérard
[
25
]
, which, roughly speaking, guarantees that the semilinear (subcritical) solution is asymptotically close to the solution of the undamped linear wave equation. This work uses the above approach to deal with a larger class of dissipative effects, thus substantially generalizing the recent results of
[
10
]
.

The observability of the truncated problem is the novel ingredient here for which we trigger some microlocal analysis tools such as microlocal defect measures. Indeed, Hörmander
[
28
]
and Duistermaat and Hörmander
[
29
]
described the propagation of singularities to a partial differential equation

is a union of integral curves of the Hamiltonian of

of the principal symbol

of a differential operator

2.
Wellposedness

2.1.
Existence of solutions in the energy space

This section is devoted to the proof of the wellposedness of solutions to problem

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+f(u)+a(x)g(\partial_{t}u)=0&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u=0&~\hbox{ on }~\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x)&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega.\end{cases}

We shall first consider the following sequence of approximate problems with truncated nonlinearities:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{k}-\Delta u_{k}+f_{k}(u_{k})+a(x)g(\partial_{t}u_{k})=0&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u_{k}=0&~\hbox{ on }~\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u_{k}(x,0)=u_{0,k}(x),~\partial_{t}u_{k}(x,0)=u_{1,k}(x)&~\hbox{ in }~\Omega,\end{cases}

where,
\{u_{0,k},u_{1,k}\}
is a sequence that will be defined later, and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.3)

f_{k}(s):=\begin{cases}f(s),&|s|\leq k,\\
f(k),&s>k,\\

We shall start this section with some auxiliary results whose proofs are easy.

)

2.2.
Abstract framework and energy functionals

We consider the weak phase space

\mathcal{H}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega),

which is endowed with the inner product

\langle(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}\,dx.

To study Hadamard’s wellposedness for problem (
2.2
), we write it in an abstract operator theoretic form. Setting
W_{k}(t)=(u_{k},\partial_{t}u_{k})
, the system (
2.2
) can be reformulated as the following Cauchy problem in
\mathcal{H}
:

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial W_{k}}{\partial t}(t)+\mathbb{A}W_{k}(t)+\mathbb{B}_{k}(W_{k}(t))={}0\\
W(0)={}(u_{0,k},u_{1,k}),\end{cases}

where the unbounded operator
\mathbb{A}:\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is given by

(2.7)

\mathbb{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-I\\
-\Delta&a(x)g(\cdot)\end{array}\right),

that is,

(2.8)

\mathbb{A}(u,v)=\left(-v,-\Delta u+a(x)g(v)\right),

with domain

(2.9)

\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{A})=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}:\begin{array}[]{c}v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\

and
\mathbb{B}_{k}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is the nonlinear operator

(2.10)

\mathbb{B}_{k}(u,v)=\left(0,f_{k}(u)\right).

Employing standard arguments of the nonlinear semigroup

Theorem 2.3

(Wellposedness)
.

If

If

\mathcal{O}(g)=1

\mathcal{O}(g)\neq 1

Remark 2.1

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|a(\cdot)g(\partial_{t}u_{k}(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|a(x)g(\partial_{t}u_{k}(x,t))|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

In this section, we abuse notation and set

Note that in Theorem

, the truncation is not necessary, since the non-linearity grows according to Sobolev’s immersion.

As in equation (6.7) of

{\bf D}_{a}^{b}\big(g(s);u_{t}\big):=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\Omega}a(x)g(u_{t})u_{t}\,dQ_{T}.

The main result of the literature, which allows the explicit derivation of decay rates, is the following:

Corollary 4.1

(Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
, Lemma 8.2 in
[
17
]
, Corollary 3.1.1 in
[
18
]
)
.

Suppose Assumption
4.1
holds. Let
T_{0}>0
be given by (
3.46
) or Theorem
4.1
. Let
h
and
q
be the functions defined in
(
4.1
)
and
(
4.3
)
, respectively. Then,

(4.6)

E_{u}(t)\leq S\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)\hbox{ for all }t\geq T_{0},

with
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}S(t)=0
, where
S
is the solution to the following nonlinear ODE:

(4.7)

S_{t}+q(S)=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

where

(4.8)

K=\left[C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))\right]

if
g
is linearly bounded near infinity,

(4.9)

K=\left[C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))\cdot D_{0}^{\frac{p_{0}(r-1)}{p_{0}-r-1}}\right]

if
g
is superlinear near infinity, and

(4.10)

K=\left[C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))\cdot D_{0}^{\frac{p_{0}(1-\theta)}{p_{0}-r-1}}\right]

if
g
is sublinear near infinity.

Proof.

In what follows, we proceed as in Lasiecka and
Tataru’s work
[
33
]
(see Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.3
of the referred paper) adapted to our context. Let

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(x,t\right)\in Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T):\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|>1\text{ a.e. }\right\},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{T}\backslash\Sigma_{\alpha}.

The proof will require the following inequalities:

I)

Damping near the origin
. From (
1.7
) and the fact that
h
is concave
and increasing,
a(x)\leq\|a\|_{\infty}+1,

and
\frac{a(x)}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}<a(x),
we deduce that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}a(x)\left(\left[g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\right]^{2}+\left(\partial_{t}u\right)^{2}\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}a(x)h_{0}\left(g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\frac{a(x)}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}h_{0}\left(g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}(1+\|a\|_{\infty})h_{0}\left(\frac{a(x)}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}(1+\|a\|_{\infty})h_{0}\left(a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}.

Then, by Jensen’s inequality,

(4.12)

\displaystyle(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}h_{0}\left(a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\operatorname{meas}\left(Q_{T}\right)h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\int_{Q_{T}}a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\,dQ\right)\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\operatorname{meas}\left(Q_{T}\right)r\left(\int_{Q_{T}}a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\,dQ\right),

where
r\left(s\right)=h_{0}\left(\frac{s}{\operatorname{meas}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)
.

(4.13)

\int_{\Sigma_{\beta}}a(x)\left(\left[g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\right]^{2}+\left(\partial_{t}u\right)^{2}\right)d\Sigma_{\beta}\leq(1+\|a\|_{\infty})\operatorname{meas}\left(Q_{T}\right)r\left(\int_{Q_{T}}a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\,dQ\right).

II)

Linearly-bounded damping at infinity
. If
\mathcal{O}(g)=1
according to Definition (
1.1
), then, from hypothesis (
1.6
), we obtain

(4.14)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)\left(|g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)|^{2}+|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\right)d\Sigma_{\alpha}\leq\left(\tilde{m}^{-1}+\tilde{M}\right)\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)g\left(\partial_{t}u\right)\partial_{t}u\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}.

III)

Superlinear damping at infinity
.
Suppose
\mathcal{O}(g)=r>1
. From (
1.6
) we obtain
|g(s)|>\tilde{m}|s|
for
|s|>1
with
\tilde{m}>0
independent of
s
, and we trivially estimate

(4.15)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|u_{t}|^{2}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{m}^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{2}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}.

Next, for
\lambda\in]0,1[,

(4.16)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{2}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}=\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{2\lambda}|g(u_{t})|^{2(1-\lambda)}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}=:J_{1}.\\

Choose any
p_{0}>2r
and estimate
J_{1}
using Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents
\frac{p_{0}}{2\lambda r}
and
\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda r}
(splitting
a(x)
as
a(x)^{2\lambda r/{p_{0}}}\cdot a(x)^{(p_{0}-2\lambda r)/{p_{0}}}
):

(4.17)

J_{1}\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{p_{0}/r}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\right)^{2\lambda r/{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda r}}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-2\lambda r}{p_{0}}}.

Note that
\mathcal{O}(g)=r
implies

(4.18)

g(s)s\sim s^{r+1}\sim g(s)^{(r+1)/r},\quad|s|>1.

Thus, for
|g(u_{t})|^{\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda r}}
to be equivalent to the dissipation integrand
g(u_{t})u_{t},
we solve

\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda r}=\frac{1+r}{r}\implies\lambda=\frac{p_{0}(r-1)}{2r(p_{0}-r-1)}.

With this choice of
\lambda,
we combine (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) to conclude

(4.19)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)(|u_{t}|^{2}+|g(u_{t})|^{2})\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\lesssim C_{a}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p_{0}}(\Omega))}^{\frac{p_{0}(r-1)}{p_{0}-r-1}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}a(x)g(u_{t})u_{t}\,dQ_{T}\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-2\mathcal{O}(g)}{p_{0}-1-\mathcal{O}(g)}}

where
C_{a}=(\sup a(x))^{\frac{2\lambda r}{p_{0}}}
.

IV)

Sublinear damping at infinity
.
Assume
\mathcal{O}(g)=r<1
in view of Definition
1.1
. From (
1.6
) we obtain
\tilde{M}|s|>|g(s)|
for
|s|>1
with
\tilde{M}>0
independent of
s
:

(4.20)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})|^{2}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\leq\tilde{M}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|g(u_{t})||u_{t}|\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}=\tilde{M}\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)g(u_{t})u_{t}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}.

For
\lambda\in]0,1[,

(4.21)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|u_{t}|\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}=\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|u_{t}|^{2\lambda}|u_{t}|^{2(1-\lambda)}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}=:J_{2}.

Let
p_{0}>2
and estimate
J_{2}
using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{p_{0}}{2\lambda}
and
\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda}
(splitting
a(x)
as
a(x)^{2\lambda/p_{0}}\cdot a(x)^{(p_{0}-2\lambda)/p_{0}}
):

(4.22)

J_{2}\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|u_{t}|^{p_{0}}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\right)^{2\lambda/{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)|u_{t}|^{\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda}}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-2\lambda}{p_{0}}}

The value of
\lambda\in]0,1[
is chosen so that

(4.23)

|u_{t}|^{\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda}}=u_{t}^{r+1}\sim g(u_{t})u_{t}\quad\text{for}\quad|u_{t}|>1,

namely,

\frac{2(1-\lambda)p_{0}}{p_{0}-2\lambda}=1+r\implies\lambda=\frac{p_{0}(1-r)}{2(p_{0}-1-r)}.

Combining (
4.20
)-(
4.22
):

(4.24)

\int_{\Sigma_{\alpha}}a(x)(|u_{t}|^{2}+|g(u_{t})|^{2})\,d\Sigma_{\alpha}\lesssim C_{a}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p_{0}}(\Omega))}^{\frac{p_{0}(1-r)}{p_{0}-r-1}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}}a(x)g(u_{t})u_{t}\,dQ_{T}\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-2}{p_{0}-1-\mathcal{O}(g)}}

where
C_{a}=(\sup a(x))^{\frac{2\lambda}{p_{0}}}
.

We combine the above estimates, observability inequality (
3.46
), and (
4.4
) from Theorem
4.1
with inequality (
4.13
) and with either (
4.14
), (
4.19
), or (
4.24
), depending on whether
\mathcal{O}(g)=1
,
\mathcal{O}(g)>1
, or
\mathcal{O}(g)<1
, respectively. Using definition (
4.5
) and relabeling constants, we obtain

E_{u}(0)\leq K\left(I({\bf D}_{0}^{T}\big(g(s);u_{t}\big))+h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T})}{\bf D}_{0}^{T}\big(g(s);u_{t}\big)\right)+h_{1}({\bf D}_{0}^{T}\big(g(s);u_{t}\big))\right),

where
h_{1}
is given by (
4.2
) and
K=C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))
if
g
is linearly bounded near infinity,
K=C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))\cdot D_{0}^{\frac{p_{0}(r-1)}{p_{0}-r-1}}

if
g
is superlinear near infinity, and
K=C(T,a,\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T}))\cdot D_{0}^{\frac{p_{0}(1-\theta)}{p_{0}-r-1}}
if
g
is sublinear near infinity.

In all cases there exists a constant
K>0
and a function
h
such that

E_{u}(0)\leq K(I+h)({\bf D}_{0}^{T}\big(g(s);u_{t}\big)).

By using the energy identity, we obtain

(I+h)^{-1}(K^{-1}E_{u}(T))\leq E_{u}(0)-E_{u}(T).

To finish the proof of the decay, we invoke the
following result due to I. Lasiecka and Tataru

[
33
]
:

Lemma A
:
Let
p
be a
positive, increasing function such that
p(0)=0
. Since

p
is
increasing, we can define an increasing function
q,

q(x)=x-(I+p)^{-1}\left(x\right).
Consider a sequence
s_{m}
of
positive numbers that satisfies

s_{m+1}+p(s_{m+1})\leq s_{m}.

Then
s_{m}\leq S(m)
, where
S(t)
is
a solution
of the differential equation

\frac{d}{dt}S(t)+q(S(t))=0,\text{ \ }S(0)=s_{0}.

Moreover, if
p(x)>0
for
x>0
, then
\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}

S(t)=0.

The semigroup property of
U(t)=(u(t),u_{t}(t))
yields

(4.25)

E_{u}((m+1)T)+(I+h)^{-1}(K^{-1}E_{u}((m+1)T))\leq E_{u}(mT)

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Applying Lemma A with
s_{m}=E_{u}(mT)
thus results in

(4.26)

E_{u}(mT)\leq S(m),\text{ \ \ }m=0,1,....

Finally, using the dissipativity of
E_{u}(t)
, we have for
t=mT+\tau,

0\leq\tau\leq T,

(4.27)

E_{u}(t)\leq E_{u}(mT)\leq S(m)=S\left(\frac{t-\tau}{T}\right)\leq S\left(\frac{t}{T}-1\right)\text{ \ \ for \ }t>T_{0}\text{.}

Above, we use the fact that
S(\cdot)
is dissipative. The
proof of decay for
E_{u}
is now complete.
∎

The algorithm for computations of decay rates given by Corollary
4.1
is very general and
provides explicit decay rates without any restrictions on the growth of the dissipation
g
at the
origin. Indeed, as shown in
[
33
]
, this algorithm gives exponential decay rates for the damping
that is bounded from below by a linear function and algebraic decay rates for polynomially
decaying dissipation at the origin. We shall illustrate below how other cases can be treated as
well. By specializing a bit further the class of nonlinear dissipation, we will be able to obtain
an explicit description of the decay rates. The obtained decay rates are optimal, since they are the
same as these optimal rates derived in
[
1
]
for the model that does not account for the sources. In
addition, we will be able to obtain decay rates for nondifferentiable dissipation, such as fractional
powers.

Next, we present the result that allows the derivation of the explicit decay rates.

Corollary 4.2

(Lasiecka-Toundykov, Corollary 1 in
[
34
]
)
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Corollary
4.1
are satisfied and that the function
h
in (
4.1
) can be expressed as
h=h_{b}+h_{s}
where
h_{b}
and
h_{s}
are concave, monotone increasing, zero at the origin, and, in addition,
h_{s}=o(h_{b}).
The latter means that
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}h_{s}(x)/h_{b}(x)=0
and
h_{b}
has no upper linear bound on
[0,1)
. Then, given any positive
\gamma<1
, there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(\gamma)\geq T
such that the following energy estimate holds:

(4.28)

E_{u}(t)\leq\bar{S}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right),\hbox{ for all }t\geq 2t_{0},

where
\bar{S}
is the solution of the following nonlinear ODE:

(4.29)

\bar{S}_{t}+h_{b}^{-1}\left(\gamma K^{-1}\bar{S}\right)=0,\quad\bar{S}(0)=E_{u}(0),

with
K
and
T_{0}
as in Corollary
4.1
.

Proof.

Assumptions on
h
imply that
q\geq h_{b}^{-1}\circ(\gamma K^{-1}I)
near the origin. Indeed, writing
\mathcal{F}=K(I+h)
we obtain

(4.30)

\displaystyle q={}

\displaystyle I-(I+\mathcal{F}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left[(I+\mathcal{F}^{-1})\circ(I+\mathcal{F}^{-1})^{-1}\right]-(I+\mathcal{F}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ(I+\mathcal{F}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ(\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}+\mathcal{F}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ\left[(K(I+h))\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}+\mathcal{F}^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ[K\mathcal{F}^{-1}+Kh\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}+\mathcal{F}^{-1}]^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[(Kh+(K+1)I)\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{F}\circ(Kh+(K+1)I)^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(Kh_{b}+Kh_{s}+(K+1)I)^{-1}.

Note that
Kh_{s}+(K+1)I=o(h_{b})
since
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{Kh_{s}(x)+(K+1)x}{h_{b}(x)}=0
. Thus,
q=(Kh_{b}+o(h_{b}))^{-1}
. Moreover, if
w=o(h_{b})
then, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\frac{o(h_{b})(x)}{Kh_{b}(x)}=\frac{w(x)}{Kh_{b}(x)}<\varepsilon
whenever
x\in(0,\delta)
. Therefore,
o(h_{b})(x)<(1+\varepsilon)Kh_{b}(x)-Kh_{b}(x)
for all
x\in(0,\delta)
, which implies that
(1+\varepsilon)Kh_{b}\geq Kh_{b}+o(h_{b})
in
[0,\delta)
. Consequently, if we let
\gamma=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}
, then
h_{b}^{-1}\circ\gamma K^{-1}I\leq(Kh_{b}+o(h_{b}))^{-1}=q
in
[0,\delta)
.

Let
\ell:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be given by
\ell=(I+h^{-1})\circ(K^{-1}I)
. From Corollary
4.1
we know
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}S(t)=0
. Thus, there exists
t_{0}\geq T_{0}
such that
S(t_{0})<\delta
. Define
s_{m}=E_{u}(mt_{0})
. Let us show that

(4.31)

E_{u}(t)\leq\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right),\hbox{ for all }t\geq 2t_{0}.

where

(4.32)

\tilde{S}_{t}+h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}(\tilde{S}(t)))=0,\quad\tilde{S}(0)=E_{u}(t_{0}).

To this end, it is sufficient to prove that

(4.33)

s_{m+1}\leq\tilde{S}(m),\hbox{ for all }m=0,1,2,\dots.

Indeed, since every
t\geq 2t_{0}
can be written as
t=(m+1)t_{0}+\tau
with
m\geq 1
, it follows that
E_{u}(t)\leq E_{u}((m+1)t_{0})\leq E_{u}(mt_{0})\leq\tilde{S}(m+1)=\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}}\right)\leq\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)
.

For
m=0
, estimate (
4.33
) is clear. Assume
s_{m+1}\leq\tilde{S}(m)
. From (
4.3
) and (
4.25
) we deduce

(4.34)

s_{m+2}+\ell(s_{m+2})\leq s_{m+1},\hbox{ for all }m\geq 1.

Inequality (
4.34
) is equivalent to

(I+\ell)s_{m+2}\leq s_{m+1}

and since
(I+\ell)^{-1}
is monotone increasing,
s_{m+2}\leq(I+\ell)^{-1}s_{m+1}=s_{m+1}-q(s_{m+1})
, hence

(4.35)

s_{m+2}\leq s_{m+1}-q(s_{m+1}).

Integrating equation (
4.32
) from
m
to
m+1
yields

\tilde{S}(m+1)-\tilde{S}(m)=-\int_{m}^{m+1}h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}(\tilde{S}(t)))\,dt.

Therefore,

(4.36)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(m+1){}

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}(m)-\int_{m}^{m+1}h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}(\tilde{S}(t)))\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{S}(m)-h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}(\tilde{S}(m)))

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{S}(m)-q(\tilde{S}(m))

\displaystyle=(I+\ell)^{-1}(\tilde{S}(m))

\displaystyle\geq(I+\ell)^{-1}s_{m+1}

\displaystyle=s_{m+1}-q(s_{m+1})

\displaystyle\geq s_{m+2}.

If we consider the equation

(4.37)

\bar{S}_{t}+h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}(\bar{S}(t)))=0,\bar{S}(0)=E_{u}(0),

then
\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\leq\bar{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)
for all
t\geq 2t_{0}
.

Indeed, note that
\tilde{S}(t)=G^{-1}(-t)
where
G(S)=\int_{E_{u}(t_{0})}^{S}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau
and
\bar{S}(t)=\bar{G}^{-1}(-t)
where
\bar{G}(S)=\int_{E_{u}(0)}^{S}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau
. Thus,

(4.38)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{u}(t_{0})}^{\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau{}=

\displaystyle G\left(\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\right)=-\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right).

Since
\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\geq 0
,
\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\leq\tilde{S}(0)=E_{u}(t_{0})\leq E_{u}(0)
. From (
4.38
) we obtain

(4.39)

\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1=\int_{\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)}^{E_{u}(t_{0})}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau\leq\int_{\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)}^{E_{u}(0)}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau.

Inequality (
4.39
) yields

(4.40)

\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\leq-\int_{E_{u}(0)}^{\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)}\frac{1}{h_{b}^{-1}(\gamma K^{-1}\tau)}\,d\tau=-\bar{G}\left(\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\right).

Therefore, (
4.40
) implies

(4.41)

\tilde{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\leq\bar{G}^{-1}\left(-\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right)\right)=\bar{S}\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}-1\right),\hbox{ for all }t\geq 2t_{0}.

Combining (
4.31
) and (
4.41
), the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We emphasize that the Corollaries
4.1
and
4.2
hold for solutions of system (
1.1
) with subcritical source terms and if
\mathcal{O}(g)=1
.

Corollary 4.3

(Corollary 2 in
[
34
]
)
.

Under hypothesis of Corollary
4.1
, suppose that damping is linearly bounded at infinity.

a)

If
g
is sublinear at the origin, i.e.,
m_{0}x^{\theta+1}\leq g(x)x\leq M_{0}x^{\theta+1}
with
\theta\in(0,1)
for
|x|<1
, the auxiliary function
h_{0}
may be defined as
h_{0}(x)=cx^{\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta}}
, where

. Then ODE (

) can be replaced with

S_{t}+\frac{1}{c^{\frac{1+\theta}{2\theta}}}(\gamma K^{-1}S(t))^{\frac{1+\theta}{2\theta}}=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

and energy decay (
4.6
) holds for all
t\geq 2t_{0}
with sufficiently large threshold value
t_{0}>0
.

b)

If
g
is superlinear at the origin, i.e.,
m_{0}x^{r+1}\leq g(x)x\leq M_{0}x^{r+1}
with
r>1
for
|x|<1
and the function
x\mapsto\sqrt{x}g(\sqrt{x})
is convex and superlinear (no lower linear bound with positive slope) on interval
[0,\delta)
, for some
\delta>0
, then ODE (
4.7
) can be replaced with

(4.43)

S_{t}+\sqrt{\gamma K^{-1}}S^{\frac{1}{2}}g[(\gamma K^{-1}S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

and energy estimate (
4.6
) holds whenever
t\geq 2t_{0}
for a sufficiently large
t_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Replace the sentence “Corollary 1” by “Corollary
4.2
” in the proof of Corollary 2 in
[
34
]
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

By integrating the differential equation (
4.43
) we obtain
S(t)=G^{-1}\left(-\frac{\tilde{C}}{2}t,E_{u}(0)\right)
where

G(y,E_{u}(0))=\int_{\sqrt{\tilde{C}E_{u}(0)}}^{\sqrt{\tilde{C}y}}\frac{1}{g(u)}du

with

5.
Computing the decay rates

Examples in this section illustrate the above results. We shall account for various configurations that include sub and superlinear damping, both at infinity and the origin. We emphasize that the examples found here are adapted from (
[
15
]
, section 8) and Examples 3.2 and 3.3 in
[
18
]
.

5.1.
Linearly bounded case

Throughout this section assume that
mx^{2}\leq g(x)x\leq Mx^{2}
for all
|x|\geq 1
. In the examples below, the constant
T_{0}
comes from Lemma
3.43
and the constant
K
comes from (
4.8
).

Example 5.1

(Linearly bounded damping at the origin)
.

In this case one obtains exponential decay rates. Suppose
m_{0}x^{2}\leq g(x)x\leq M_{0}x^{2}
for all
|x|<1
. Then
h_{0}(x)=(M_{0}+m_{0}^{-1})x
, function
q
in (
4.3
) can be computed explicitly, and ODE (
4.7
) is

S_{t}+\tilde{C}S=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

where
\tilde{C}=\frac{K^{-1}\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T})}{\operatorname{meas}(Q_{T})(2+K^{-1})+M_{0}+m_{0}^{-1}}
. This gives us
S(t)=E_{u}(0)e^{-\tilde{C}t}
and
E_{u}(t)\leq E_{u}(0)e^{\left(-\tilde{C}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)\right)}
for all
t\geq T_{0}
.

Example 5.2

(Superlinear polynomial damping near the origin)
.

Suppose
m_{0}x^{p+1}\leq g(x)x\leq M_{0}x^{p+1}
for all
|x|<1
and some
p>1
. Since the function
s\mapsto\sqrt{s}g(\sqrt{s})=s^{(p+1)/2}
is convex for
p\geq 1
, By Corollary
4.3
item b), the energy decay is determined by the solution of

S_{t}+\tilde{C}S^{\frac{p+1}{2}}=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

where
\tilde{C}=(\gamma K^{-1})^{\frac{p+1}{2}}
. This equation can be integrated directly, of course. However, for sake of illustrating the general formula, we find

G(y,E_{u}(0))=\int_{\sqrt{E_{u}(0)}}^{\sqrt{y}}u^{-p}du=\frac{1}{1-p}[y^{\frac{-p+1}{2}}-E_{u}(0)^{\frac{-p+1}{2}}].

From here,
G^{-1}(t)=[E_{u}(0)^{\frac{-p+1}{2}}+t(1-p)]^{\frac{2}{-p+1}}
. Thus,

E_{u}(t)\leq S\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)=G^{-1}\left(-\frac{\tilde{C}}{2}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)\right)=\left[E_{u}(0)^{\frac{-p+1}{2}}+\frac{(p-1)\tilde{C}}{2}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)\right]^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

for all
t\geq T_{0}
.

Example 5.3

(Sublinear damping at the origin)
.

We consider
g(s)=s^{\theta}
for all
|s|<1
and some
\theta\in(0,1)
. By Corollary
4.3
item a), the energy decay is determined by the solution of

S_{t}+\tilde{C}S^{\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta}}=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

where
\tilde{C}=\left(\frac{\gamma K^{-1}}{c}\right)^{\frac{1+\theta}{2\theta}}
with
c=\frac{2(M_{0}^{2}+1)}{m_{0}^{\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta}}}
.
In this case,

G(s,E_{u}(0))=\frac{\sqrt{S}^{-\frac{1}{\theta}+1}}{-\frac{1}{\theta}+1}-\frac{\sqrt{E_{u}(0)}^{-\frac{1}{\theta}+1}}{-\frac{1}{\theta}+1}.

Therefore,
G^{-1}(t)=\left[\left(-\frac{1}{\theta}+1\right)t+\sqrt{E_{u}(0)}^{-\frac{1}{\theta}+1}\right]^{\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}}
. Thus,

E_{u}(t)\leq\left[\left(\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta}\right)\frac{\tilde{C}}{2}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)+E_{u}(0)^{\frac{-1+\theta}{2\theta}}\right]^{-\frac{2\theta}{1-\theta}}\hbox{ for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Example 5.4

(Exponential damping at the origin)
.

We take
g(s)=s^{3}e^{-1/s^{2}}
for
s
at the origin. Since the function
h^{-1}(s)=g(\sqrt{s})\sqrt{s}=s^{2}e^{-1/s}
is convex on
[0,\delta)
for some small
\delta>0
, we solve

S_{t}+\tilde{C}^{2}S^{2}e^{-(\tilde{C}S)^{-1}}=S_{t}+\sqrt{\tilde{C}S}g[(\tilde{C}S)^{\frac{1}{2}}]=0,

where
\tilde{C}=\gamma K^{-1}
. In this case,
G(y,E_{u}(0))=-\frac{1}{2}[e^{1/(\tilde{C}y)}-e^{1/(\tilde{C}E_{u}(0))}]
and
G^{-1}(t,E_{u}(0))=\tilde{C}^{-1}[\ln(e^{1/(\tilde{C}E_{u}(0))}-2t)]^{-1}
. Hence,

E_{u}(t)\leq\tilde{C}^{-1}\left[\ln\left(\tilde{C}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)+e^{1/(\tilde{C}E_{u}(0))}\right)\right]^{-1}\hbox{ for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Example 5.5
.

We consider
g(s)=s|s|e^{-1/|s|}
for
s
near zero. Since the function
g(\sqrt{s})\sqrt{s}=s^{3/2}e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}}
is convex on
[0,\delta)
for some small
\delta>0
we are led to differential equation

S_{t}+(\tilde{C}S)^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}S}}}=0,\quad S(0)=E_{u}(0),

where
\tilde{C}=\gamma K^{-1}
. In this case
S(t)=\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\ln^{-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{C}t+2e^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}E_{u}(0)}}})\right)
. Therefore,

E_{u}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\frac{1}{\ln^{2}\left(e^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}E_{u}(0)}}}+\frac{\tilde{C}}{2}\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}-1\right)\right)}\hbox{ for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Appendix A
Preliminaries on microlocal analysis

In this section, we supplement details to proofs of some of the theorems on pseudo-differential operators and microlocal defect measures, whose original versions in French can be found in the elegant lecture notes of Burq and Gérard
[
7
]
.

A.1.
Pseudo-differential operators

Let
\Omega
be an open and nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. A differential operator on
\Omega
is a linear map
P:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
of the form

(A.1)

\displaystyle Pu(x):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u(x),~(\partial^{\alpha}:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}})

where
a_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
are complex valued functions. The greatest integer
m
such that the functions
a_{\alpha}
,
|\alpha|=m
are not all zero is called the order of
P
.
The map
p:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, defined by

\displaystyle p(x,\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\,(i\xi)^{\alpha},

is called the symbol of
P
.

We observe that
P
is characterized by the identity

(A.2)

\displaystyle P(e_{\xi})(x)=p(x,\xi)\,e_{\xi}(x),~~\hbox{ where }~e_{\xi}(\cdot)=e^{i\,\left<(\cdot),\xi\right>}=e^{i\,(\cdot)\cdot\xi}.

Adopting to the notation

\displaystyle D=\frac{1}{i}\partial,\ D_{j}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{j}\hbox{ and }D^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{i^{|\alpha|}}\partial^{\alpha},

the operator
P
can be rewritten as

(A.3)

\displaystyle P=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)i^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}=p(x,D).

The formula (
A.2
) can be generalized as follows: for all
(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all
u\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
,

(A.4)

\displaystyle P(ue_{\xi})=p(x,D)(ue_{\xi})=e_{\xi}\,p(x,\xi+D)(u)=e_{\xi}\,\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\frac{\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)}{\alpha!}D^{\alpha}u,

where the previous sum is finite once
p
is a polynomial in the variable
\xi
.

If
P
is a differential operator of order
m

and symbol
p
, then the principal symbol of order
m
, denoted by
\sigma_{m}(P)
, is the homogeneous part of degree
m
in
\xi
of the polynomial function
p(x,\xi)
, namely

(A.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(P)(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}(x)(i\xi)^{\alpha}.

Definition A.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then a symbol of order at most
m
in
\Omega
is said to be a function
a:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of class
C^{\infty}
, with support in
K\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
, such that for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}
with

(A.6)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,K}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{m-|\beta|}.

We shall denote the vectorial space of all symbols of order at most
m
in
\Omega
by
\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m}(\Omega)
.

Proposition A.1
.

If
a\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m}(\Omega)
, the formula

(A.7)

\displaystyle Au(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)\,d\xi

defines, for all
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, an element
Au
of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

The formula (
A.7
) defines a linear map
A:C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\longrightarrow C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, which is called the pseudo-differential operator of order
m
and symbol
a
. We will often denote the map
A
by
a(x,D)
.

The set of all pseudo-differential operators of order
m
on
\Omega
will be denoted by
\Psi^{m}_{c}(\Omega)
.

Definition A.2
.

An operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{c}(\Omega)
is essentially homogeneous if there exists a function
a_{m}=a_{m}(x,\xi)
with
\operatorname{{supp}}a_{m}\subset K\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
, homogeneous of order
m
in
\xi
and smooth except at
\xi=0
and a function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
being zero near
0
and
1
in the infinity such that

(A.8)

\displaystyle a(x,\xi)=a_{m}(x,\xi)\chi(\xi)+r(x,\xi),

for some
r\in\mathcal{S}^{m-1}_{c}(\Omega)
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}_{c}(\Omega)
be essentially homogeneous. Then, for all
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
, and
x\in\Omega
,

(A.9)

\displaystyle t^{-m}e^{-i(tx)\cdot\xi}A(ue_{t\xi})(x)\rightarrow a_{m}(x,\xi)u(x),~\hbox{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.

Definition A.3
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
A.2
, we say that
A
admits a principal symbol of order
m
. The function
a_{m}
characterized by (
A.9
) is called the principal symbol of order
m
of
A
and is denoted by
\sigma_{m}(A)
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
A
be a pseudo-differential operator of symbol
a\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m}(\Omega)
with
\operatorname{{supp}}a\subset K\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy

in a neighborhood of

. Then, there exists a pseudo-differential operator

on

\displaystyle\left(A(\chi u),v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(u,A_{\chi}^{\ast}v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In addition,
A_{\chi}^{\ast}
admits a symbol
a_{\chi}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m}(\Omega)
verifying, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(A.10)

\displaystyle a_{\chi}^{\ast}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\frac{1}{\alpha!}D_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\overline{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m-N-1}(\Omega).

In particular, if
A
admits a principal symbol of order

, it is the same of

, and

(A.11)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(A_{\chi}^{\ast})=\overline{\sigma_{m}(A)}.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
A
and

be pseudo-differential operators of symbols

,
b\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{n}(\Omega)
, respectively. Then, the composition

is a pseudo-differential operator admitting a symbol

which satisfies

(A.12)

\displaystyle a\#b-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}aD_{x}^{\alpha}b\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m+n-N-1}(\Omega),

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

In addition, if
A
admits a principal symbol of order
m
and
B
admits a principal symbol of order
n
, then
AB
admits a principal symbol of order
m+n
and

admits a principal symbol of order

, given by

(A.13)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m+n}(AB)=\sigma_{m}(A)\sigma_{n}(B),

(A.14)

\displaystyle\sigma_{m+n-1}([A,B])=\frac{1}{i}\left\{\sigma_{m}(A),\sigma_{n}(B)\right\}.

Definition A.4
.

For any compact set
K
contained in
\Omega
and for all

,

shall denote the space of distributions with compact support in

whose extensions by zero belong to

, i.e.,

\displaystyle H_{K}^{s}(\Omega)

.
We equip

\displaystyle H_{K}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H_{comp}^{s}(\Omega)

Theorem A.3
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{m}(\Omega)
and let

If

.

Let

Theorem A.4

(
A G
\rm\overset{\circ}{a}

Let

. Then, for all

\displaystyle Re\,(Av,v)_{L^{2}}\geq-\delta\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-C_{\delta}\|v\|_{H^{-1/2}}^{2},~\hbox{ for all }v\in L_{comp}^{2}.

A.2.
Microlocal defect measures

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{K}|u_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx<+\infty,

for any compact set
K
contained in
\Omega
.

We shall say that
u_{n}
converges weakly to
u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
if one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{n}(x)f(x)\,dx\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\Omega}u(x)f(x)\,dx

for each
f\in L_{comp}^{2}(\Omega)
.

We describe the loss of strong convergence of the sequence
u_{n}
to
0
in
L^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
, by means of a positive Radon measure on
\Omega\times S^{d-1}
, that is,
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
if, and only if,
\mu=0
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L_{loc}^{2}(\Omega)
weakly converging to
0
. Let
A
be a pseudo-differential operator of order
0
on
\Omega
that admits a principal symbol
\sigma_{0}(A)
of order
0
. If
\sigma_{0}(A)\geq 0
, then one has

\displaystyle Im(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})_{L^{2}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~\hbox{ and }~\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}Re(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})_{L^{2}}\geq 0.

Proof.

First, note that since

is bounded in

and converges weakly to

\displaystyle\|\chi u_{n}\|_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Indeed, recall that

\displaystyle\|\chi u_{n}\|_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-1/2}|\widehat{\chi u_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi u_{n}}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(x)u_{n}(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx

tends to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\chi(x)u_{n}(x)e^{-ix\xi}}dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\operatorname{{supp}}(\chi)}{\chi(x)u_{n}(x)e^{-ix\xi}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{\operatorname{{supp}}(\chi)}1dx<\infty.

Applying the dominated convergence theorem, we have, for all

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{|\xi|\leq R}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-1/2}|\widehat{\chi u_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{|\xi|\leq R}|\widehat{\chi u_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

On the other hand, the Plancherel theorem yields

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{|\xi|>R}|\widehat{\chi u_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-1/2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{R}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

so,

which proves (

is arbitrary. Applying (
A.15
) and (
A.16
) to

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}[-\delta\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}]+\liminf_{n\rightarrow 0}[-C_{\delta}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{H^{-1/2}}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\delta\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In addition,

which proves the lemma since

Theorem A.5
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L_{loc}^{2}(\Omega)
weakly converging to
0
. Then, there exists a subsequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}}
and a positive Radon measure
\mu
on

such that for any essentially homogeneous pseudo-differential operators

with principal symbol
\sigma_{0}(A)
, one has

(A.18)

\displaystyle\left(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\Omega\times S^{n-1}}\sigma_{0}(A)(x,\xi)\,d\mu

for all

such that
\chi=1
in
\pi_{x}(\operatorname{{supp}}(\sigma_{0}(A)))
.

Proof.

From the assumption of the theorem, one has

(A.19)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|A(\chi u_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(K,\chi)\,\underset{(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times S^{d-1}}{\max}|\sigma_{0}(A)|.

Indeed, first observe that since
A\in\Psi_{c}^{0}(\Omega)
, it maps
H_{comp}^{s}(\Omega)
into

for all

and some compact set

A
maps
L_{comp}^{2}(\Omega)
continuously into

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(A(\chi u_{n}))

.

Now, from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A(\chi u_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A(\chi u_{n}),A(\chi u_{n}))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{\chi}^{\ast}A(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})\|\chi u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The principal symbol of the operator

is

. Hence,

Then,

Observe that

weakly in

. Indeed, let

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{n}(\chi g)dx\rightarrow 0,

From Remark

,

, and hence

\displaystyle(r(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\hbox{ as }n\to\infty.

On the other hand, since

in

\displaystyle(b(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}b(x,D)(\chi u_{n})(x)u_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-((M^{2}\chi I-b(x,D))(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})+((M^{2}\chi I)\chi u_{n},\chi u_{n}).

We estimate the term
\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|A(\chi u_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|A(\chi u_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}(A_{\chi}^{\ast}A(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}Re(A_{\chi}^{\ast}A(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}Re(b(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})+\limsup_{n\to\infty}Re(r(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}Re-(M^{2}\chi I-b(x,D)(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})

\displaystyle+\limsup_{n\to\infty}((M^{2}\chi I)\chi u_{n},\chi u_{n})

\displaystyle-\liminf_{n\to\infty}Re((M^{2}\chi I-b(x,D))(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})

\displaystyle M^{2}\max_{x\in\Omega}\chi(x)\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\chi u_{n}\|^{2},

.

Therefore, from the properties of

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|A(\chi u_{n})\|\leq C(\chi,K)\underset{(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times S^{d-1}}{\max}|\sigma_{0}(A)|.

) we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})|\leq C\underset{(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times S^{d-1}}{\max}|\sigma_{0}(A)|.

of

Moreover, there is

such that for all

\displaystyle|(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})-(A_{i_{0}}(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})|

Then,

(A(\chi u_{n}),\chi u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\tilde{L}(\sigma_{0}(A)).

does not depend on the choice of

. Indeed, let

Theorem A.6

Theorem A.7
.

\mu(N)=0

. Let

such that

. By continuity, there is an open neighborhood

of

\displaystyle=

We will call the vector field defined in

We finish this section by examining the case of the wave equation in an inhomogeneous medium:

\displaystyle P(x,D)u=-\rho(x)\partial_{t}^{2}u+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}(K(x)\partial_{x_{j}}u).

whose principal symbol is given by

(A.37)

\displaystyle p(t,x,\tau,\xi)=-\rho(x)\tau^{2}+\xi^{\top}\cdot K(x)\cdot\xi,\hbox{ where }\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{d}),

,

,

,

for

.

Let’s describe the bicharacteristics of

. From Lemma

the bicharacteristics do not change if we multiply

by a non-zero function. So we can study the Hamiltonian curves of

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\tau^{2}+\xi^{\top}\cdot\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\cdot\xi\right).

Proposition A.3
.

Up to a change of variables, the bicharacteristics of (
A.38

\displaystyle s\mapsto\left(s,y(s),\tau,-\tau\left(\frac{K(y(s))}{\rho(y(s))}\right)^{-1}\dot{y}(s)\right),

on

, parameterized by the arc length.

Proof.

Let’s define a curve

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\alpha:&I&\longrightarrow&\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\\
&s&\mapsto&(t(s),x(s),\tau(s),\xi(s))\end{array}

such that

(A.39)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{t}(s)&=\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial\tau}(\alpha(s)),\\
\dot{x}_{j}(s)&=\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial\xi_{j}}(\alpha(s)),j=1,\cdots,d,\\
\dot{\tau}(s)&=-\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial t}(\alpha(s)),\\
\dot{\xi}_{j}(s)&=-\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial x_{j}}(\alpha(s)),~j=1,\cdots,d.\end{aligned}\right.

From (
A.38
) and
\eqref{1.38}_{1}
, we have

\dot{t}(s)=-\tau(s).

Also, from (
A.38
) and
\eqref{1.38}_{2}
it follows that

(A.40)

\dot{x}(s)=\frac{K(x(s))}{\rho(x(s))}\cdot\xi(s).

Equation (
A.38

ensure that

\dot{\tau}(s)=-\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial t}(\alpha(s))=0.

Finally, from

\dot{\xi}_{j}(s)=-\frac{\partial\tilde{p}}{\partial x_{j}}(\alpha(s))=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{k_{ij}(x(s))}{\rho(x(s))}\right)\xi_{i}(s)\cdot\xi_{j}(s).

Thus, the sought after curve must satisfy the equations

(A.41)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{t}(s)=-\tau(s),\\
&\dot{x}(s)=\frac{K(x(s))}{\rho(x(s))}\cdot\xi(s),\\
&\dot{\tau}(s)=0,\\
&\dot{\xi}(s)=-\frac{1}{2}{(\xi(s))}^{\top}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{K(x(s))}{\rho(x(s))}\right)\cdot\xi(s)\end{aligned}\right.

Introducing the matrix

A.41

\xi(s)=G(x(s))\dot{x}(s).

Let

be defined by

so that its derivative is given by

So,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{k}}(x)

\displaystyle=d\left(f\circ\frac{K}{\rho}\right)_{x}(e_{k})

\displaystyle=d(f)_{\left(\frac{K}{\rho}\right)(x)}\circ d\left(\frac{K}{\rho}\right)_{x}(e_{k})

\displaystyle=d(f)_{\left(\frac{K}{\rho}\right)(x)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-1}.

That is,

(A.43)

Therefore, from

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\frac{k_{ij}(x(s)}{\rho(x)})\right)\xi_{i}(s)\cdot\xi_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{\top}G(x)\cdot\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\cdot\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{k}}(x)\cdot\frac{K(x)}{\rho(x)}\cdot G(x)\dot{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{\top}\cdot\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{k}}(x)\cdot\dot{x}.

Thus, from (

(A.45)

Since

Continuity at a boundary point of solutions to quasilinear elliptic equations with generalized Orlicz growth and non-logarithmic conditions
Oleksandr V. Hadzhy, Mykhailo V. Voitovych

Abstract
We consider the Dirichlet problem for quasilinear elliptic equations with Musielak-Orlicz ( p , q ) (p,q) -growth and non-logarithmic conditions on the coefficients. A sufficient Wiener-type condition for the regularity of a boundary point is established. Keywords: nonlinear elliptic equations; Musielak-Orlicz growth; non-logarithmic condition; boundary regularity; Wiener condition. MSC (2020) : 35B65, 35D30, 35J60.

3
A sufficient condition for the regularity of a boundary point:
proof of Theorem
2.4

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,G}(\Omega)
,
and let
u
be a bounded weak solution of Eq. (
1.1
) satisfying the condition

u-f\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We need to prove that
\lim\limits_{\Omega\ni x\rightarrow x_{0}}u=f(x_{0})
.
This equality will be established if we show that

f(x_{0})\leqslant\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\essinf\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u

and

\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\esssup\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\leqslant f(x_{0})
.
The proves of the both inequalities are completely similar and we will prove only the second one.
We argue by contradiction and assume that

L:=\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\esssup\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u>f(x_{0}).

(3.1)

Let
f(x_{0})<l<L
, then
\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}M_{l}(\rho)=L-l>0
and
M_{l}(\rho)

is bounded away from zero for sufficiently small
\rho
: there exist constants

a,\rho_{\ast}\in(0,1)
such that

M_{l}(\rho)>a>0\ \ \text{for all}\ \rho\in(0,\rho_{\ast}).

(3.2)

Let us prove the following inequality:

\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)\leqslant a^{1-q}\Lambda(\gamma,3n,\rho)\rho^{n-1}g\left(x_{0},\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\right)

(3.3)

for arbitrary fixed
\rho\in(0,\rho_{\ast})
.
To do this, we fix a function

\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\rho/16}(x_{0}))
such that

0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1
,
\eta=1
in
B_{3\rho/64}(x_{0})
and

|\nabla\eta|\leqslant 64/\rho
.
We set

v_{l,\rho}=\frac{u_{l,\rho}\eta}{M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{l,\rho/32}=\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\cap\overline{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u_{l}(x)=0\}}.

It is clear that
\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega\subset E_{l,\rho/32}
and

v_{l,\rho}\in\mathfrak{M}(E_{l,\rho/32},B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
,
therefore by Definition
2.3
the following inequality holds:

\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)\leqslant\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(E_{l,\rho/32},B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)\leqslant\int_{B_{\rho/16}(x_{0})}G(x,|\nabla v_{l,\rho}|)\,dx.

(3.4)

The integral in (
3.4

\int_{\Omega_{l,\rho/16}}G(x,|\nabla v_{l,\rho}|)\,dx\leqslant a^{1-q}\Lambda(\gamma,3n,\rho)\rho^{n-1}g\bigg(x_{0},\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\bigg),

, then by condition (

) and
(

) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{l,\rho/16}}G(x,|\nabla v_{l,\rho}|)\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{l,\rho/16}}g\bigg(x,\frac{|\nabla(u_{l,\rho}\eta)|}{M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho}\bigg)\frac{|\nabla(u_{l,\rho}\eta)|}{M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\Lambda(\gamma,3n,\rho)(M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho)^{1-p}\rho^{n-1}g\bigg(x_{0},\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\bigg)

) and
(

), we have on the set

and on the set

\displaystyle g\bigg(x,\frac{|\nabla(u_{l,\rho}\eta)|}{M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho}\bigg)\leqslant g\bigg(x,\frac{b_{0}}{M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}(M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho)^{1-q}g(x,b_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}(M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho)^{1-q}g\bigg(x,\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma e^{\lambda(\rho)}(M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho)^{1-q}g\bigg(x_{0},\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\bigg).

) in the following form:

\displaystyle{\rm w}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}_{x_{0}}\bigg(\Lambda(-\gamma,3n,\rho)\,\frac{\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)}{\rho^{n-1}}\bigg),

\displaystyle=g^{-1}_{x_{0}}\bigg(a^{q-1}\Lambda(-\gamma,3n,\rho)\,\frac{\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)}{\rho^{n-1}}\bigg),

and using (

), we derive the following:

g^{-1}_{x_{0}}\bigg(\Lambda(-\gamma,3n,\rho)\,\frac{\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(\overline{B}_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)}{\rho^{n-1}}\bigg)\leqslant a^{-\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\,\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}.

It is readily verified that

\int_{0}\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\,d\rho<+\infty,

and therefore the previous inequality gives

\int_{0}g^{-1}_{x_{0}}\bigg(\Lambda(-\gamma,3n,\rho)\,\frac{\textrm{Cap}_{G}\big(B_{\rho/32}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,B_{\rho}(x_{0})\big)}{\rho^{n-1}}\bigg)\,d\rho<+\infty,

Consequently, hypothesis (

The inequality

The proof is complete.

In this section we prove Theorem

.
We assume that all the hypotheses and notation of this theorem
are in force. For definiteness, we also assume that

in (

).
In the case where

, the proof is completely similar.
We need some inequalities and several lemmas.
First, we note simple analogues of Young’s inequality:

g(x,a)\,b\leqslant\varepsilon\,g(x,a)\,a+g(x,b/\varepsilon)\,b\ \ \text{if}\ \varepsilon,a,b>0,\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

(4.1)

In fact, if

which proves inequality (

Next, we set

\mathcal{G}(x,{\rm w})=\int_{0}^{{\rm w}}g(x,{\rm v})\,d{\rm v}\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\in\Omega,\ {\rm w}>0.

(4.2)

The following inequalities hold:

\mathcal{G}(x,{\rm w})\geqslant\gamma\,G(x,{\rm w})\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\overline{\Omega},\ {\rm w}\geqslant 2b_{0},

(4.3)

G(x,{\rm w})\geqslant p\,\mathcal{G}(x,{\rm w})\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\overline{\Omega},\ {\rm w}>0.

(4.4)

Indeed, if
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
{\rm w}\geqslant 2b_{0}
, then

1.2
), (
2.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

\mathcal{G}(x,{\rm w})=\int_{0}^{{\rm w}}g(x,{\rm v})\,d{\rm v}\geqslant\int_{b_{0}}^{{\rm w}}g(x,{\rm v})\,d{\rm v}\geqslant\frac{g(x,{\rm w})}{c_{1}{\rm w}^{\,q-1}}\int_{b_{0}}^{{\rm w}}{\rm v}^{\,q-1}d{\rm v}\geqslant\frac{1-2^{-q}}{c_{1}q}\,G(x,{\rm w}),

which implies (
4.3
).
Now, let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
{\rm w}>0
be arbitrary,
then by (

), (

) and (
4.2
) we obtain

\mathcal{G}(x,{\rm w})=\int_{0}^{{\rm w}}g(x,{\rm v})\,d{\rm v}\leqslant\frac{g(x,{\rm w})}{{\rm w}^{p-1}}\int_{0}^{{\rm w}}{\rm v}^{\,p-1}\,d{\rm v}=\frac{1}{p}\,g(x,{\rm w}){\rm w}=\frac{1}{p}\,G(x,{\rm w}),

which yields (
4.4

The rest of the lemmas in this section are successive stages in the proof of

[

adapted to Eq.(

\displaystyle\leqslant\Lambda(\gamma,3n,\rho)(M_{l}(\rho)+2b_{0}\rho)\rho^{n-1}g\left(x_{0},\frac{M_{l}(\rho)-M_{l}(\rho/4)+2b_{0}\rho}{\rho}\right).

High-dimensional sparse trigonometric approximation in the uniform norm and consequences for sampling recovery
Moritz Moeller, Serhii Stasyuk, Tino Ullrich

Abstract
Recent findings by Jahn, T. Ullrich, Voigtlaender [ 10 ] relate non-linear sampling numbers for the square norm and quantities involving trigonometric best m − m- term approximation errors in the uniform norm. Here we establish new results for sparse trigonometric approximation with respect to the high-dimensional setting, where the influence of the dimension d d has to be controlled. In particular, we focus on best m − m- term trigonometric approximation for (unweighted) Wiener classes in L q L_{q} and give precise constants. Our main results are approximation guarantees where the number of terms m m scales at most quadratic in the inverse accuracy 1 / ε 1/\varepsilon . Providing a refined version of the classical Nikol’skij inequality we are able to extrapolate the L q L_{q} -result to L ∞ L_{\infty} while limiting the influence of the dimension to a d \sqrt{d} -factor and an additonal log \log -term in the size of the (rectangular) spectrum. This has consequences for the tractable sampling recovery via ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -minimization of functions belonging to certain Besov classes with bounded mixed smoothness. This complements polynomial tractability results recently given by Krieg [ 12 ] .

2
Trigonometric best
m
-term widths and Wiener spaces

For an integrable
d
-variate periodic function
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
we define the Fourier coefficient with respect to the multi-indexed frequency
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as usual by

\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\exp(-2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}\,.

An important role in this paper will be played by trigonometric polynomials. For a subset
Q\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the linear set

\mathcal{T}(Q):=\Big\{\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in Q}c_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot)\penalty\ :\penalty\ c_{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{C},\mathbf{k}\in Q\Big\}\,.

Let us single out the special case

\mathcal{T}\big([-\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}]\big):=\mathcal{T}(\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}[-N_{j},N_{j}])\,,

where
Q=\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}[-N_{j},N_{j}]
represents an (anisotropic) cuboid . The spaces of
\mathcal{T}(Q)
are linear subspaces of
{\rm span\,}\mathcal{T}^{d}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{d}
denotes the dictionary consisting of all complex exponentials, i.e.,

\mathcal{T}^{d}:=\Big\{\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\Big\}\,.

Let us now comment on non-linear approximation with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{d}
and replace the above linear spaces by the non-linear space

\Sigma_{m}:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}e_{j}(\cdot)\penalty\ :\penalty\ e_{1}(\cdot),...,e_{m}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{T}^{d};c_{1},...,c_{m}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}\subset{\rm span\,}\mathcal{T}^{d}\,.

(2.1)

We denote by
\sigma_{m}(f;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{q}
the best approximation error
\|f-s\|_{L_{q}}
using a trigonometric
m
-term sum

s=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in\Lambda}c_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})\in\Sigma_{m}

with an appropriate
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Having a function class
\mathbf{F}
(unit ball of some quasi-Banach space of multivariate functions) we define

\sigma_{m}(\mathbf{F};\mathcal{D})_{Y}:=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathbf{F}}\sigma_{m}(f;\mathcal{D})_{Y}

(2.2)

and call it best
m
-term width of
\mathbf{F}
in
Y
with respect to the dictionary
\mathcal{D}
. Quantities of this type have been already considered and studied by Pietsch
[
21
]
in the 1980s. Nonlinear approximation gained a lot of interest when wavelets entered image and signal processing, see DeVore
[
7
]
for references and historical remarks. During the paper there might occur a slight inaccuracy in notation. We sometimes consider vector spaces
\mathbf{F}
and
\sup
over
\|f\|_{\mathbf{F}}\leq 1
in (
2.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
0<\theta\leq\infty
. The space
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\penalty\ :\penalty\ \|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}:=\Big(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{\theta}\Big)^{1/\theta}<\infty\Big\}\,,

with the usual modifications in the case where
\theta=\infty
. The quantity
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}
is a norm on
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
if
\theta\geq 1
and a quasi-norm if
\theta<1
. In case
\theta=1
the space
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{1}
is called Wiener algebra.

The following statement is a direct consequence of a result known as
Stechkin’s lemma
although Stechkin never stated it in this form, see
[
4
, Section 7.4]
for historical comments and further references.
Let the Fourier coefficients
(\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}_{j}))_{j}
of a function
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be arranged such that

|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}_{1})|\geq|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}_{2})|\geq|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}_{3})|\geq...

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<\theta<\gamma\leq\infty
. Then it holds

\sigma_{m}(f;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}}=\Big(\sum\limits_{j=m+1}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{\gamma}\Big)^{1/\gamma}\leq(m+1)^{-(1/\theta-1/\gamma)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}

(2.3)

for all
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
. As usual, if
\gamma=\infty
the sum in the middle is replaced by the
\sup
over
j
.

For results in this direction for weighted Wiener classes we refer to the recent work by V. K. Nguyen and V. D. Nguyen
[
18
]
. Here will be interested in the situation where the target space is
L_{q}
,
2\leq q\leq\infty

3
Nikol’skij’s inequality revisited

Nikol’skij’s inequality has been first mentioned in
[
19
, Eq. 2.4]
. For the multivariate trigonometric version we refer to
[
4
, Thm. 2.4.5]
and the references therein. It essentially states

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq C_{d}\,\|f\|_{L_{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.1)

for
f\in T([-\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}])
. With the classical proof one may observe that the constant
C_{d}
is usually exponentially growing in
d
. We will sharpen this inequality what concerns the
d
-dependence. In the below version of the Nikol’skij inequality the constant is less than
3
if
q
is chosen as
d\log(\#Q)
. As in Jahn, Ullrich, Voigtlaender
[
10
]
we rely on mapping properties of specifically tailored de la vallée Poussin operators to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1

(Nikol’skij’s inequality)
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
then for

f\in\mathcal{T}\big([-\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}]\big)\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\big(\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}[-N_{j},N_{j}]\big),

it holds

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq e^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\,\|f\|_{L_{q}}(2d+2)^{\frac{d}{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

(3.2)

In particular, for
f\in\mathcal{T}([-N,N]^{d})
it holds

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq e^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\|f\|_{L_{q}}\left((2d+2)N\right)^{\frac{d}{q}}.

Proof.

We use the mapping properties of a modified de la Valleé Poussin operator (see
[
10
, Sect. 3.1]
)

V_{\mathbf{N}}(f)(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\,v_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})

(3.3)

with weights
v_{\mathbf{k}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}v_{k_{j}}
, where

v_{k_{j}}=\begin{cases}1,&|k_{j}|\leq N_{j},\\
\frac{(2d+1)N_{j}-|k_{j}|}{2dN_{j}}\,,&N_{j}<|k_{j}|\leq(2d+1)N_{j},\\
0,&|k_{j}|>(2d+1)N_{j}\,.\end{cases}

(3.4)

Let us first estimate
\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{\infty}}
. Afterwards, we will take care of showing
\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{\infty}\to L_{\infty}}\leq e
and apply an interpolation argument. With a straight forward calculation we find

\displaystyle\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|f\|_{L_{2}}\leq 1}\|V_{\mathbf{N}}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|f\|_{L_{2}}\leq 1}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})v_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{\infty}}=\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in[-(2d+1)\mathbf{N},(2d+1)\mathbf{N}]}|v_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in[-(2d+1)\mathbf{N},(2d+1)\mathbf{N}]}\prod_{j=1}^{d}v_{k_{j}}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{d}(2d+2)N_{j}\Big)^{1/2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=(2d+2)^{\frac{d}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(3.8)

From
[
10
, Sect. 3.1]
we obtain additionally

\begin{split}\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{\infty}\to L_{\infty}}&\leq\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}v_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{k_{j}}\exp(2\pi ikx)\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T})}\\
&\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\Big(\frac{2(d+1)N_{j}}{2dN_{j}}+\frac{N_{j}}{2dN_{j}}\Big)=\Big(1+\frac{1}{d}\Big)^{d}\leq e.\end{split}

(3.9)

Now we use a standard interpolation of operators argument to get results for
2<q<\infty
.
The interpolation parameter
\theta
now has to be chosen in such a way that
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{\infty}
which yields
\theta=1-\frac{2}{q}
. This gives

\displaystyle\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{q}\to L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{\infty}}^{1-\theta}\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{\infty}\to L_{\infty}}^{\theta}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{\infty}}^{\frac{2}{q}}\|V_{\mathbf{N}}\|_{L_{\infty}\to L_{\infty}}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq e^{1-\frac{2}{q}}(2d+2)^{\frac{d}{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}^{\frac{1}{q}},

(3.12)

which concludes the proof.
∎

4
Sparse trigonometric approximation in
L_{q}
,
2\leq q\leq\infty

Lemma 4.1

(Bernstein inequality,
[
9
, Corollary 7.31]
)
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{J}
be independent mean-zero random variables with uniform bound
\big|X_{j}\big|<B
and controlled second moments
\sigma^{2}=\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}\big|X_{j}\big|^{2}
. Then it holds for all
s\geq 0

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}X_{j}\Big|>s\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+Bs/3}\Big).

(4.1)

Lemma
4.1
also holds for complex-valued random variables with slight modifications in the constants.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the same conditions as in Lemma
4.1
it holds for independent mean-zero complex valued random variables
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{J}
the bound,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}Z_{j}\Big|>s\Big)\leq 4\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}/8}{\sigma^{2}+Bs/6}\Big).

(4.2)

Proof.

We consider that the absolute value of a sum of complex random variables
Z_{j}=X_{j}+iY_{j}
can be estimated from above as by the sum of absolute values. From there follows this bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}Z_{j}\Big|>s\Big)&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}X_{j}\Big|+\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}iY_{j}\Big|>s\Big)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}X_{j}\Big|>\frac{s}{2}\cup\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}Y_{j}\Big|>\frac{s}{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}X_{j}\Big|>\frac{s}{2}\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{J}Y_{j}\Big|>\frac{s}{2}\Big)\\
&\leq 4\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}/8}{\sigma^{2}+Bs/6}\Big).\end{split}

(4.3)

Where in the last line we used that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{J}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{J}
are families of real-valued independent random variables that fulfil the requirements of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The following is a specific formulation of “tails to moments” type, adapted to our formulation of the Bernstein inequality. General formulations can be found in
[
9
, Section 7.2]
.

Corollary 4.3

(Tails to moments)
.

Let
X
be a random variable with bounded tails of the form

\mathbb{P}\Big(\big|X\big|>s\Big)\leq\beta\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+Bs}\Big).

(4.4)

Then for
p\geq 1
the
p
-th moment of
X
is bounded by

\mathbb{E}\big(|X|^{p}\big)^{1/p}\leq\beta^{1/p}\sqrt{p}\sigma+2\beta^{1/p}pB.

(4.5)

Proof.

We first consider a different representation of the
p
-th moment

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(|X|^{p}\big)&=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\big|X\big|>s\big)s^{p-1}ds\\
&\leq p\int_{0}^{\sigma^{2}/B}\beta\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)s^{p-1}ds+p\int_{\sigma^{2}/B}^{\infty}\beta\exp\Big(-\frac{s}{2B}\Big)s^{p-1}ds.\\
\end{split}

(4.6)

We can see that these two summands are very similar. Indeed we can write them in a unified way by introducing an additional variable
\gamma
such that
\gamma=2
describes the first and
\gamma=1
, the second term and denoting by
\kappa_{2}=\sigma
and
\kappa_{1}=B
. We can now substitute
u=\frac{s^{\gamma}}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\begin{split}p\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{\gamma}}{2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}}\Big)s^{p-1}ds&=p\beta\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{u\gamma}{2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}}\Big)(\gamma u)^{\frac{p-1}{\gamma}}(\gamma u)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}}du\\
&=p\beta\gamma^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{u\gamma}{2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}}\Big)u^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}du\,.\end{split}

(4.7)

A standard change of variable
t=\frac{u\gamma}{{2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}}}
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}p\beta\gamma^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{u\gamma}{2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}}\Big)u^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}du&=p\beta\gamma^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)\big(t2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}\big)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}dt\\
&=p\beta\gamma^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}\big(2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}\big)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t)t^{\frac{p}{\gamma}-1}dt\\
&=\beta\big(2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}\big)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\frac{p}{\gamma}\Gamma\Big(\frac{p}{\gamma}\Big)\\
&\leq\beta\big(2\kappa_{\gamma}^{\gamma}\big)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\Big(\frac{p}{\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\,,\end{split}

(4.8)

where we used that
x\Gamma(x)\leq x^{x}
, see e.g.
[
13
, Lem. 4.9]
.
Now taking the
p
-th root yields the assertion

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(|X|^{p}\big)^{1/p}&\leq\sum_{\gamma=1}^{2}\beta^{1/p}(2p/\gamma)^{1/{\gamma}}\kappa_{\gamma}\\
&\leq\beta^{1/p}\sqrt{p}\kappa_{2}+\beta^{1/p}2p\kappa_{1}\\
&=\beta^{1/p}\sqrt{p}\sigma+2\beta^{1/p}pB.\end{split}

(4.9)

∎

First we show that every function from the (classical) Wiener Algebra can be well approximated in the
L_{q}
norm by trigonometric polynomials. We use the random choice of coefficients proposed in
[
14
, Lem. 2]
. Our proof is rather elementary and allows for tracking the involved constants.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
0<\theta\leq 1
then it holds

\sigma_{4m}(\mathcal{A_{\theta}};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{q}}\leq C\,\sqrt{q}m^{1/2-1/\theta}\,,

(4.10)

where
C=26+\frac{2}{3}<27
.

Proof.

Step 1.
We first consider the approximation of a trigonometric polynomial
t\in{\rm span\,}\mathcal{T}^{d}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
by a trigonometric polynomial
s\in\Sigma_{4m}
with at most
4m
non-zero frequencies. Let
t
be given as follows

t(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in[-N,N]^{d}}\hat{t}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}),

(4.11)

where
J
is the number of non-zero frequencies of
t
and
(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j}))_{j=1}^{J}
is a non-increasing rearrangement of absolute values of its Fourier coefficients. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the trigonometric polynomial
t_{n}
as follows

t_{n}:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\,.

(4.12)

It represents the straight-forward approximation of
t
where the
n
largest coefficients are used.
For our second step we follow a similar strategy as was done in
[
14
, Lem. 2]
. Consider a family of independent discrete complex-valued random variables
v_{j}
for
j=2m+1,\ldots,J
which are constructed as follows

v_{j}=\begin{cases}\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})p_{j}^{-1},&\text{with probability}\,\,p_{j}\,,\\
0,&\text{with probability}\,\,1-p_{j}\,,\\
\end{cases}

(4.13)

where

p_{j}=m|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{-1}
.
From Lemma
2.2
it follows that the
p_{j}
are actually probabilities, i.e., that
0\leq p_{j}\leq 1
.
Now we can analyze an
L_{q}
-approximation of
t-t_{2m}
by
\sum_{j}v_{j}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}^{q}&\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{q}}^{q}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big|^{q}d\mathbf{x}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}q\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big|>s\Big)s^{q-1}dsd\mathbf{x}\,.\end{split}

(4.14)

We are now ready to apply the Bernstein inequality Corollary
4.2
to
X_{j}=(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})
. First note that for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have
\mathbb{E}X_{j}=0
and
\big|X_{j}\big|\leq|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|p_{j}^{-1}
.
We also need a bound on the moments of the
X_{j}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big|(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\big|^{q}&=\mathbb{E}\big|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j}\big|^{q}\\
&=|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{q}(p_{j}^{-1}-1)^{q}p_{j}+|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{q}(1-p_{j})\\
&=|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{q}\big|(1-p_{j})^{q}p_{j}^{1-q}+1-p_{j}\big|\\
&\leq 2|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{q}p_{j}^{1-q}.\end{split}

(4.15)

In particular, for the second moment we get
\mathbb{E}\big|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j}\big|^{2}\leq 2|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{2}p_{j}^{-1}
.

We can use Lemma
4.1
or Corollary
4.2
, the Bernstein inequality, with

B=\sup_{j}|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})p_{j}^{-1}\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})|\leq m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}},

and

\sigma^{2}=2\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|^{2}p_{j}^{-1}\leq 2m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|\leq 2m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{2}\,.

We have

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}(\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})-v_{j})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})\Big|>s\Big)\leq 4\exp\Big(-\frac{s^{2}/8}{2m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{2}+m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}s/6}\Big).

(4.16)

Using Corollary
4.3
with
\beta=4
,
\sigma=4m^{-1/2}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
and
B=4/3\,m^{-1}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
we obtain for (
4.14
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}&\leq 4^{1/q}4m^{-1/2}\sqrt{q}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}+2\cdot 4^{1/q}4/3m^{-1}q\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\leq 8m^{-1/2}\sqrt{q}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}+\frac{16}{3}m^{-1}q\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\leq(8+\frac{16}{3})\sqrt{q}m^{-1/2}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\,,\\
\end{split}

(4.17)

where we used that
q\geq 2
and
m\geq q
.
The expected number of non-zero coefficients
c_{m}
is

\mathbb{E}c_{m}=\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}p_{j}=\sum_{j=2m+1}^{J}m|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k}_{j})|\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{-1}\leq m.

(4.18)

Since both random variables
c_{m}
and
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}
are non-negative we can apply Markov’s inequality to see that for any
\delta>0

\mathbb{P}\big(c_{m}\geq(2+\delta)\mathbb{E}c_{m}\big)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}c_{m}}{(2+\delta)\mathbb{E}c_{m}}<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.19)

This means
\mathbb{P}\big(c_{m}>2\mathbb{E}c_{m})<\frac{1}{2}
and analogously
\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}>2\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}})<\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, the probability for both these events (at the same time) is strictly less than one. Hence, there is a realization such that
c_{m}\leq 2\mathbb{E}c_{m}
and
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L_{q}}
.
This implies the existence of
s\in\Sigma_{2m}
with

\|t-t_{2m}-s\|_{L_{q}}\leq 2\Big(8+\frac{16}{3}\Big)\sqrt{\frac{q}{m}}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\,

(4.20)

if
m\geq q
. Note that always
\|t-t_{m}\|_{L_{q}}\leq\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. So there is always
\tilde{s}\in\Sigma_{4m}
such that

\|t-\tilde{s}\|_{L_{q}}\leq\Big(16+\frac{32}{3}\Big)\sqrt{\frac{q}{m}}\|t-t_{m}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\leq\Big(16+\frac{32}{3}\Big)\sqrt{q}m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\,,

where in the last step we again applied Lemma
2.2
. This proves (
4.10
) for
\mathcal{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\cap{\rm span\,}\mathcal{T}^{d}
.

Step 2.
Let see argue that this also holds for all function
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
. For each
f
with
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\leq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N>N_{0}

\|f-f_{N}\|_{L_{q}}\leq\|f-f_{N}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}<\varepsilon\,.

(4.21)

Here
f_{N}
is defined as above as the trigonometric polynomial with the largest coefficients.
This implies the existence of
s=s(\varepsilon,N)\in\Sigma_{4m}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f-s\|_{L_{q}}&\leq\|f-f_{N}\|_{L_{q}}+\|f_{N}-s\|_{L_{q}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon+C\,\sqrt{q}\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|f_{N}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon+C\,\sqrt{q}\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\,.\end{split}

(4.22)

Since the second summand is neither depending on
\varepsilon
nor on
N
we can pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
and obtain

\sigma_{4m}(f;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{q}}\leq C\,\sqrt{q}\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\,.

The desired result now follows after taking
\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\leq 1}
on both sides.
∎

While this result does not hold for
q=\infty
(the right hand side would be
\infty
anyways) we can still recover a result for trigonometric polynomials in that regime.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
0<\theta\leq 1
. For a trigonometric polynomial
t\in\mathcal{T}\big(Q\big)
with
Q=\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}[A_{j},B_{j}]
and integers
A_{i}<B_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
,
it holds,

\sigma_{4m}(t;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}\leq C_{1}\sqrt{d}\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\big(\log{\#Q}\big)^{1/2}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}},

(4.23)

with an absolute constant
C_{1}<\sqrt{2}e^{5/2}C\leq 460
and
{\#Q}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(B_{j}-A_{j}+1)
.

Proof.

Let consider the polynomial

\widetilde{t}(\mathbf{x})=t(\mathbf{x})\cdot\exp(-2\pi i(\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{A})/2\cdot\mathbf{x})

instead of
t(\cdot)
. Assume without loss of generality, that we may divide by
2
(otherwise add a
1
in the respective component). Clearly, we have
\sigma_{n}(t;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}=\sigma_{n}(\widetilde{t};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}
and the spectrum of
\widetilde{t}(\cdot)
is contained in
[-(B_{1}-A_{1})/2,(B_{1}-A_{1})/2]\times...\times[-(B_{d}-A_{d})/2,(B_{d}-A_{d})/2]
. Let us put
N_{j}=\lfloor(B_{j}-A_{j}+1)/2\rfloor
,
j=1,...,d
.
We will use the sharpened Nikol’skij inequality in Theorem
3.1
together with the
L_{q}
-result in Theorem
4.4
.
We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{4m}(\widetilde{t};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{t}-s\|_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{1-2/q}(2d+2)^{d/q}2^{-d/q}{\#Q}^{1/q}\|t-s\|_{L_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{1-2/q}\,\sqrt{q}m^{1/2-1/\theta}(d+1)^{d/q}{\#Q}^{1/q}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}},

where we applied Theorem
4.4
and noted that
s\in\mathcal{T}\big([-\mathbf{N},\mathbf{N}]\big)\cap\Sigma_{4m}
in the proof of Theorem
4.4
. Now we can choose
q=2d\log({\#Q})
and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{4m}(t;\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}&\leq Ce\,\sqrt{{2d\log({\#Q})}}m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}(d+1)^{\frac{d}{2d\log({\#Q})}}{\#Q}^{\frac{1}{2d\log({\#Q})}}\\
&\leq Ce^{1+1/(2d)}\sqrt{d}(d+1)^{\frac{1}{2\log({\#Q})}}\log({\#Q})^{1/2}m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\\
&\leq C\sqrt{2}e^{2+1/(2d)}\sqrt{d}\log({\#Q})^{1/2}m^{1/2-1/\theta}\|t\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\,.\end{split}

(4.24)

We used that
d<2\log({\#Q})
and have therefore

(d+1)^{1/(2\log({\#Q}))}\leq e^{\frac{\log(d+1)}{d}}\leq e.

(4.25)

∎

5
Application – Besov spaces with mixed smoothness

The results in the previous section may be applied to Besov spaces with dominating mixed smoothness as considered for instance in
[
4
, Page 40, (3.3.3)]
. Consequences for weighted Wiener classes of several types will be given in the forthcoming paper
[
16
]
. For Besov spaces with mixed smoothness we give both, bounds for best
m
-term widths and (non-linear) sampling numbers via (
1.2
). Let us fix an equivalent quasi-norm and start from the segmentation
\mathbb{Z}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}I_{n}

with
I_{0}=\{0\}
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}

I_{n}=\big\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\penalty\ :\penalty\ 2^{n-1}\leq|k|<2^{n}\big\}\,.

This gives
\#I_{n}=2^{n}
.
For
\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
we write

I_{\mathbf{k}}=I_{k_{1}}\times\ldots\times I_{k_{d}},

(5.1)

each of these blocks contains

\#I_{\mathbf{k}}=2^{|\mathbf{k}|_{1}}

(5.2)

points. This also immediately induces a segmentation of
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
into cuboids (
5.1
), that is
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}}I_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Definition 5.1
.

For
1<p<\infty
,
0<\theta<\infty
and
r\geq 0
we define the (periodic) Besov space
\mathbf{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}
with mixed smoothness as follows

\mathbf{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}\coloneqq\Big\{f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\penalty\ \|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}}<\infty\Big\}\,,

(5.3)

with the (quasi-)norm

\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}}\coloneqq\bigg(\sum_{\mathbf{l}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}}2^{|\mathbf{l}|_{1}r\theta}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{l}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{p}^{\theta}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

(5.4)

and the typical modification if
\theta=\infty
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
(p,\theta)\in\{(p,\theta):\,2\leq p<\infty
,
0<\theta\leq 1\}\setminus(2,1)
. Then it holds

\sigma_{4m}(\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}\leq C_{2}\,d\,\Big(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{-1/2}\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{d}{p}\Big)^{1/2},\Big(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\}\,m^{-(1/\theta-1/2)}\log(dm)^{1/2}\,,

(5.5)

where
C_{2}=C_{1}+3e<468
, and
C_{1}
is the constant from Theorem
4.5
.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}
we start by projecting on the space of trigonometric polynomials

\mathcal{T}([-N,N]^{d})
with the projection operator

P_{N}f=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in[-N,N]^{d}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\,.

(5.6)

Obviously it holds
P_{N}f\in\mathcal{T}([-N,N]^{d})
. The projection now allows for the following

\begin{split}\sigma_{4m}\big(\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta};\mathcal{T}^{d}\big)_{L_{\infty}}&=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}}\leq 1}\sigma_{4m}(f)_{L_{\infty}}\\
&=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{4m}}\|f-s\|_{L_{\infty}}\\
&\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}}\leq 1}\Big(\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{4m}}\|P_{N}f-s\|_{L_{\infty}}+\|f-P_{N}f\|_{L_{\infty}}\Big).\\
\end{split}

(5.7)

We take care of the first summand. By Hölder’s inequality it is easy to see that the space
\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}
is embedded into
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
with norm
1
(independently of the dimension) if
2\leq p<\infty
and
0<\theta\leq 1
. This gives

\begin{split}\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{4m}}\|P_{N}f-s\|_{L_{\infty}}&\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{4m}}\|P_{N}f-s\|_{L_{\infty}}\\
&\leq C_{1}\,d\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\log(2N)^{1/2}\,,\end{split}

(5.8)

where the last inequality follows from Theorem
4.5
.

It remains to estimate
\|f-P_{N}f\|_{\infty}
. Here the condition
(p,\theta)\in\{(p,\theta):\,2\leq p<\infty
,
0<\theta\leq 1\}\setminus(2,1)
will play an important role. Let us assume that
N=2^{\ell}
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
will be specified later. It clearly holds

\begin{split}\|f-P_{N}f\|_{\infty}^{\theta}\leq\sum\limits_{|\mathbf{j}|_{\infty}\geq\ell}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{j}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{\infty}}^{\theta}.\end{split}

(5.9)

Here we use our refined version of Nikol’skij’s inequality from Theorem
3.1
. We obtain

\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{j}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{\infty}}^{\theta}\leq e^{\theta-2\theta/p}\big((d+1)^{d}2^{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}}\big)^{\theta/p}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{j}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{p}}^{\theta}\,,

which implies

\begin{split}\|f-P_{N}f\|_{L_{\infty}}&\leq e^{1-2/p}(d+1)^{d/p}\bigg(\sum\limits_{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}\geq\ell}2^{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}\theta/p}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{j}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{p}}^{\theta}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\\
&\leq e^{1-2/p}((d+1)^{d/p}\Big(\sup\limits_{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}\geq\ell}2^{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}(1/p-(1/\theta-1/2))}\Big)\\
&\times\bigg(\sum\limits_{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}\geq\ell}2^{|\mathbf{j}|_{1}(1/\theta-1/2)\theta}\Big\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{\mathbf{j}}}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\exp(2\pi i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{L_{p}}^{\theta}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\\
&\leq e^{1-2/p}(d+1)^{d/p}N^{-(1/\theta-1/2-1/p)}\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta}}\,.\end{split}

(5.10)

Choosing
l=\log_{2}(N)
such that
(d+1)^{d/p}N^{-(1/\theta-1/2-1/p)}\leq m^{1/2-1/\theta}
we obtain

\big((d+1)^{d/p}m^{1/\theta-1/2}\big)^{(1/\theta-1/2-1/p)^{-1}}\leq N\leq 2\,\big((d+1)^{d/p}m^{1/\theta-1/2}\big)^{(1/\theta-1/2-1/p)^{-1}}\,.

(5.11)

Together with (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) we see that

\sigma_{4m}(\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}\leq C_{2}\,d\,\Big(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{-1/2}\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{d}{p}\Big)^{1/2},\Big(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\}\,m^{1/2-1/\theta}\log(dm)^{1/2}\,.

∎

This bound has an immediate consequence for Besov spaces with mixed smoothness via (
1.2
).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
(p,\theta)\in\{(p,\theta):\,2\leq p<\infty
,
0<\theta\leq 1\}\setminus(2,1)
. Then there are absolute constants
C>0
and a constant
c=c(p,\theta)>0
such that

\varrho_{cd^{2}(\log^{2}d)m(\log^{3}m)}(\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta})_{L_{2}}\leq Cd^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{(2-\theta)p}{(2-\theta)p-2\theta}}m^{-(1/\theta-1/2)}(\log(dm))^{1/2}\,.

Proof.

We employ (
1.2
) and use Theorem
5.2
to estimate
\sigma_{4m}(\mathbf{B}^{1/\theta-1/2}_{p,\theta};\mathcal{T}^{d})_{L_{\infty}}\,.
In order to estimate

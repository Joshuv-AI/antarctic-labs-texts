The (Co)homology of the Deligne-Mumford Moduli Spaces of Marked Rational Curves
Aleksey Zinger

Abstract
This informal note collects key results and open problems on the (co)homology of the Deligne-Mumford moduli spaces of real marked rational curves. The open problems are both of topological nature, aiming to investigate the (co)homology of these spaces further, and of algebraic nature, aiming to describe the structure of the homology of these spaces in operad-like terms.



\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell}=
Deligne-Mumford moduli space of (complex) rational curves
with
\ell
marked points; smooth projective variety of complex dimension
\ell\!-\!3
.
An element of this space is the equivalence class of stable tuples

\mathcal{C}\equiv\big(\Sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\big),

where
\Sigma
is a connected, possibly nodal, Riemann surface and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\!\in\!\Sigma

are distinct smooth points.

\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,\ell}=
Deligne-Mumford moduli space of real rational curves
with
k
real marked points and
\ell
conjugate pairs of marked points;
compact smooth real analytic variety of dimension
k\!+\!2\ell\!-\!3
;
orientable if
k\!=\!0
or
k\!+\!2\ell\!\leq\!4
.
An element of this space is the equivalence class of stable tuples

\mathcal{C}\equiv\big(\Sigma,\sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},(z_{1}^{+},z_{1}^{-}),\ldots,(z_{\ell}^{+},z_{\ell}^{-})\!\big),

where
\Sigma
is a connected, possibly nodal, Riemann surface,

\sigma
is an anti-holomorphic involution on
\Sigma
and

z_{1},\ldots,z_{k},z_{1}^{+},z_{1}^{-},\ldots,z_{\ell}^{+},z_{\ell}^{-}\in\Sigma

are distinct smooth points such that
\sigma(z_{i}^{+})\!=\!z_{i}^{-}
for
i\!=\!1,\ldots,\ell
.

[\ell]\!=\!\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.

H_{*}(\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell};\mathbb{Q}),&\hbox{if}~k\!+\!\ell\!\geq\!2;\end{cases}\qquad\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k)=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell).

Similarly to the previous cases, there are node-identifying immersions

\begin{split}\iota_{k\ell k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}i}^{\mathbb{R}}\!:\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell}\!\times\!\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime}+1,\ell^{\prime}}&\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+k^{\prime},\ell+\ell^{\prime}}\qquad\hbox{and}\\
\iota_{k\ell\ell^{\prime}i}^{+}\!:\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell}\!\times\!\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell^{\prime}+1}&\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell+\ell^{\prime}-1},\end{split}

with
k\!+\!\ell\!\geq\!2
in both cases,

i\!\in\![k\!+\!1]
and
k^{\prime}\!+\!\ell^{\prime}\!\geq\!2
in the first case,
and
i\!\in\![\ell]
and
\ell^{\prime}\!\geq\!2
in the last case.
They induce homomorphisms

\begin{split}\iota_{k\ell k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}i*}^{\mathbb{R}}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\!\approx\!H_{*}\big(\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell}\!\times\!\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k^{\prime}+1,\ell^{\prime}};\mathbb{Q}\big)&\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(k\!+\!k^{\prime}\!-\!1,\ell\!+\!\ell^{\prime}\big)\quad\hbox{and}\\
\iota_{k\ell\ell^{\prime}i*}^{+}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(\ell^{\prime})\!\approx\!H_{*}\big(\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1,\ell}\!\times\!\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell^{\prime}+1};\mathbb{Q}\big)&\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(k,\ell\!+\!\ell^{\prime}\!-\!1\big).\end{split}

We define the homomorphisms

\displaystyle\iota_{k\ell 10i*}^{\mathbb{R}}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,0)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(k,\ell),\qquad\iota_{10k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}1*}^{\mathbb{R}}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,0)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\qquad\iota_{k\ell 1i*}^{+}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(k,\ell)

similarly to (
1
).
These bilinear maps induce multilinear maps

\begin{split}\circ\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(m_{1},\ell_{1}^{\prime})\!\times\!\ldots\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(m_{k},\ell_{k}^{\prime})&\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(m_{1}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!m_{k},\ell\!+\!\ell_{1}^{\prime}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!\ell_{k}^{\prime}\big)\qquad\hbox{and}\\
\circ\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(m_{1})\!\times\!\ldots\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(m_{\ell})&\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(k,m_{1}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!m_{\ell}\big)\end{split}

similarly to (
2
).
The last two maps determine an operad
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}

of graded vector spaces and a right module structure on
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k,\ell)\!)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}

over the operad
(\!(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(\ell)\!)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}},\circ)
for each
k\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

As suggested by A. Voronov, the above structures can be combined into an object resembling
a bicolored operad; see
[
6
, Section 1.1]
.
For
n\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(n)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(n)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+\ell=n\\
k\geq 0,\ell\geq 1\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k\!+\!1,\ell\!-\!1)\,.

For each fixed
n\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, auxiliary grading is provided by
k\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
above,
with the elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(n)
being of the auxiliary degree 0.
For
x\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(n)
, let
|x|\!=\!n
and

|x|_{+}=\begin{cases}n,&\hbox{if}~x\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(n);\\
\ell\!-\!1,&\hbox{if}~x\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k\!+\!1,\ell\!-\!1).\end{cases}

We take the set of colors to be
\mathcal{C}\!\equiv\!\{+,\mathbb{R}\}
and define

\textnormal{out}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C},\qquad\textnormal{out}(x)=\begin{cases}+,&\hbox{if}~x\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(n);\\
\mathbb{R},&\hbox{if}~x\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(k\!+\!1,\ell\!-\!1).\end{cases}

For
i\!\in\![n]
, define

\textnormal{in}_{i}\!:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C},\qquad\textnormal{in}_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}+,&\hbox{if}~i\!\leq\!|x|_{+};\\
\mathbb{R},&\hbox{if}~i\!>\!|x|_{+}.\end{cases}

Let
1_{+}\!=\!1\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(1)
and
1_{\mathbb{R}}\!=\!1\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(1,0)
.
The above auxiliary grading is preserved by the maps

\circ_{i}\!:\big\{(x,y)\!\in\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(m)\!\times\!\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(n)\!:\textnormal{in}_{i}(x)\!=\!\textnormal{out}(y)\!\big\}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}(m\!+\!n\!-\!i),\quad i\!\in\![m],

induced by the homomorphisms
\iota_{k\ell k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}i*}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\iota_{k\ell\ell^{\prime}i*}^{+}

defined in the previous paragraph.
These maps satisfy a modification of
[
6
, (1.1.4a)]
,

x\!\circ_{i}\!(y\!\circ_{j}\!z)=\begin{cases}(x\!\circ_{i}\!y)\!\circ_{j+i-1}\!z,&\hbox{if}~\textnormal{out}(y)\!=\!\textnormal{out}(z);\\
(x\!\circ_{i}\!y)\!\circ_{j+|x|_{+}}\!z,&\hbox{if}~\textnormal{out}(y)\!\neq\!\textnormal{out}(z);\end{cases}\quad\hbox{if}~1\leq i\leq|x|,\,1\leq j\leq|y|.

(3)

These maps also satisfy a modification of
[
6
, (1.1.4b)]
,

(x\!\circ_{i}\!y)\!\circ_{j+|y|-1}\!z=\begin{cases}(x\!\circ_{j}\!z)\!\circ_{i}\!y,&\hbox{if}~\textnormal{out}(y)\!=\!+;\\
(x\!\circ_{j}\!z)\!\circ_{i+|z|_{+}}\!y,&\hbox{if}~\textnormal{out}(y)\!=\!\mathbb{R};\end{cases}\quad\hbox{if}~1\leq i<j\leq|x|.

(4)

The identities (
3
) and (
4
) hold whenever both sides are defined.
They agree with
[
6
, (1.1.4a)]
and
[
6
, (1.1.4b)]

whenever
\textnormal{out}(y)\!=\!\textnormal{out}(x)
and
\textnormal{out}(y)\!=\!\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
The identity
[
6
, (1.1.4c)]
holds without any modifications.

A natural question is to describe the above algebraic structures explicitly
and perhaps to find a more succinct way of presenting them.

Gravitational radiation and dynamical degrees of freedom in DGP gravity
D.V. Gal’tsov

Abstract
The paper is devoted to the study of gravitational radiation in the DGP model of gravity. It includes a detailed analysis of dynamical degrees of freedom of tensionless DGP and their comparison with other related theories. We develop a procedure for constructing the effective energy-momentum tensor of the gravitational field on the brane from the non-local effective action of the DGP model based on the Noether method used to determine the conservation laws corresponding to the symmetries of the action. Using it we construct the effective energy-momentum tensor of the dynamical degrees of freedom of DGP gravity on the brane and obtain the effective four-dimensional gravitational radiation energy flux. We then obtain an analog of the quadrupole formula for the effective gravitational radiation power of an arbitrary non-relativistic source on the brane. We also discuss the possibility of detecting the effect of leakage of gravitational waves into the extra dimension resulting from the metastable character of the effective graviton on the brane. In particular, in accordance with the infrared transparency of the bulk of DGP model, we find that the frequency of gravitational-wave signal controls the effective crossover radius, beyond which the leakage of gravitational waves manifests itself.

Introduction

Over the past two decades, many modifications of the general theory of relativity (GR) have been developed
[
1
,
2
]
. Among them, an important class are theories of gravity with extra space-time dimensions, motivated mainly by the development of string theory, which is currently the main model of quantum gravity and requires the existence of extra dimensions for its self-consistency

In particular, braneworld models have been widely developed, aimed at solving certain problems of high-energy physics and cosmology

As a result, the production of light Kaluza-Klein gravitons can significantly change astrophysical processes
[
17
]
, opening up the possibility of experimental study of extra dimensions using astrophysical and cosmological observations.

Recently, the actively developing gravitational-wave astronomy has become one of the most promising tools for the experimental search and study of extra dimensions
[

,
19

. Extra spacetime dimensions may manifest themselves through additional polarizations of gravitational waves

,

,

, differences in the propagation of gravitational-wave and counterpart electromagnetic signals from binary neutron star mergers

, additional contributions to the gravitational field source in Einstein’s equations

In this paper, we consider the process of gravitational radiation and the effect of gravitational wave leakage into an extra dimension in the framework of the DGP gravity model with one infinite extra spacelike dimension. The general DGP action may contain a Nambu-Goto term for the brane, which is equivalent to the four-dimensional cosmological constant term leading to the de Sitter background solution of the theory. In principle, the DGP model contains two branches of solutions that differ in the choice of boundary conditions in the bulk
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
: the normal branch corresponds to the radiation boundary conditions and admits a metastable effective graviton quasi-localized on the brane, while the self-accelerated branch corresponds to the opposite boundary condition and admits the de Sitter cosmological solution for the vacuum brane
[

]
. However, in the latter case the graviton contains a ghost degree of freedom

,
41
,
37
,
42
,
43

[
44

, admitting the modification of laws of gravitation at cosmological scales. In what follows, we study the gravitational-wave effects in this branch of DGP model. For this purpose we can use the simpler model without Nambu-Goto term.

Since the DGP graviton effective action on the brane
[
45
,
40
]
lacks gauge symmetry, unlike in general relativity where it is commonly used to extract the dynamical degrees of freedom of the gravitational field transporting radiation to infinity, we also investigate the dynamical degrees of freedom of DGP gravity. Based on an analogy with massive Fierz-Pauli gravity, we construct a non-local effective action of the dynamical degrees of freedom of the DGP graviton on the brane and the corresponding brane-localized effective stress-energy tensor. Since we consider the DGP model with a tensionless brane, in this paper we study the spectrum of gravitational perturbations on the background of a flat brane embedded in a five-dimensional Minkowski bulk, while most of the literature has considered the case of a de Sitter vacuum brane (see, e.g.,
[

The metastable character of effective DGP-graviton on the brane should lead to the leakage of gravitational waves into the extra dimension at cosmological scales
[

]
, which manifests in a faster, compared to GR, attenuation of the amplitude of gravitational waves with distance from the source. In particular, based on the behavior of the Newtonian potential in the DGP model
[

]
, in Ref.

]
an empirical formula qualitatively describing the intensity of leakage of gravitational waves into the extra dimension was proposed

h_{\times/+}\propto\frac{1}{d_{L}(1+\left(d_{L}/r_{c}\right)^{n/2})^{1/n}},

This effect opens up the way to experimentally test the DGP model by use of joint observations of gravitational-wave and counterpart electromagnetic signals from the mergers of binary neutron stars

47

The first constraints on the number of spacetime dimensions and the parameters of the DGP model have already been obtained based on the Eq. (

Note that the process of gravitational radiation in the DGP model was recently studied in Ref.

However, being based on the momentum space calculations, this approach does not capture information about the structure of gravitational field in the wave zone and, as a consequence, the leakage of gravitational waves into the extra dimension. Moreover, this approach also obscures the study of non-local tail signals in gravitational radiation

We obtain an analog of the quadrupole formula for the effective four-dimensional gravitational radiation power of an arbitrary non-relativistic source on the brane. To construct the effective energy-momentum tensor of the gravitational field on the brane from the non-local effective action of DGP model

we give a generalization of the Noether method to the case of such non-local theories. 49

This paper is organised as follows. In section

2
DGP model of gravity

The DGP model of gravity
[
11
,
12
]
is a braneworld model with the dynamical 3-brane
\Sigma
embedded into the five-dimensional bulk
\cal M
with one infinite extra spacelike dimension. All matter fields and particles are assumed to be localised on
\Sigma
and only gravity propagates through the extra dimension. The dynamics of gravitational field is determined by the five-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert action in the bulk
\cal M
, as well as by the additional four-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert term induced on the brane
\Sigma
due to the interaction of bulk gravitons with the quantum loops of matter particles on the brane
[
11
]
.

2.1
Non-linear theory

Let us start with briefly recalling the geometrical formulation of full non-linear DGP gravity model (see, e.g.,
[
64
,
65
]

We cover

The brane

Linearized theory

(4+1)
-foliated metric as

H_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu},\quad H_{\mu y}=n_{\mu},\quad H_{yy}=2n.

(2.32)

Thus, the linear equations of motion of DGP gravity take the form
[
65
]

\displaystyle{{}_{5}}\square\big(h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\big)=0,\quad y\neq 0,

(2.33)

\displaystyle M_{4}^{2}\!\left.\big({{}_{4}}\square h_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h\big)\right|_{y=0}+M_{5}^{3}\big[\partial_{y}h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{y}h\big]_{y=0}=-2{T_{\rm m}}_{\alpha\beta},

(2.34)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\beta}h,\quad n=\frac{1}{2}h,\quad n^{\mu}=0.

(2.35)

Here,
{{}_{5/4}}\square
denotes the five/four-dimensional d’Alembert operators in Minkowski spacetime, correspondingly. We split Eq. (
2.28
) into the bulk equation of motion (
2.33
) and the junction condition on the brane (
2.34
), and the last equation in (
2.35
) fixes the residual gauge symmetry (
2.30
) with transformation parameters satisfying the condition
{{}_{5}}\square\xi_{\mu}=0
. Also, the energy-momentum tensor of the brane localised matter have to be conserved
\partial^{\alpha}{T_{\rm m}}_{\alpha\beta}=0
for the self-consistency of the obtained equations.

Imposing the radiation boundary condition on the gravitational field in the bulk (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
)

h_{\mu\nu}\propto\int d\omega\,e^{-i\omega t\pm ik(\omega)y}\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}(\omega),\quad y\gtrless 0,

(2.36)

we find from the Eq. (
2.33
) the following expression for the jump of the induced metric derivative on the brane

\big[\partial_{y}h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{y}h\big]_{y=0}=2\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}\left.\big(h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\big)\right|_{y=0}.

(2.37)

As a result, the junction condition (
2.34
), being the effective four-dimensional equation of motion of the gravitational field on the brane, takes the form

\left.\big({{}_{4}}\square h_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h\big)\right|_{y=0}+M_{c}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}\left.\big(h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\big)\right|_{y=0}=-\frac{2}{M_{4}^{2}}{T_{\rm m}}_{\alpha\beta},\quad M_{c}=\frac{2M_{5}^{3}}{M_{4}^{2}}.

(2.38)

Note that the presence of a strong coupling regime in the DGP model
[
45
,
73
,
40
]
imposes constraints on the validity range of the linear approximation. Namely, the further calculations are valid only for the processes of emission of gravitational waves by systems of gravitating objects separated from each other by a distance
r\gg r_{\rm sc}\sim(M_{c}^{2}M_{4})^{-1/3}
and moving with characteristic frequencies
\omega\ll m_{\rm sc}\sim(M_{c}^{2}M_{4})^{1/3}
.

3
Effective theory on the brane

To calculate the effective four-dimensional gravitational radiation power of matter on the brane, one needs to construct the effective energy-momentum tensor (EMT) of the gravitational field on the brane. One way to construct it is to project and localise on the brane the full five-dimensional EMT of the gravitational field in the bulk, by analogy with the effective EMT of the scalar DGP model obtained in Ref.
[
39
]
. However, in this section we develop a procedure for constructing the effective EMT of the gravitational field from the non-local effective action of the DGP model on the brane based on the Noether method.

3.1
Effective energy-momentum tensor of DGP scalar

First, we illustrate our method for constructing the effective EMT of the field on the brane within the framework of scalar field analog of DGP model. Due to its simplicity, it is easy to additionally verify the proposed method by calculating the brane localized part of the full five-dimensional Hamiltonian of the scalar field in the bulk.

3.1.1
Effective action on the brane

For the reader’s convenience, we first briefly recall the method for constructing the effective action of scalar field DGP model on the brane
[
45
]
.

Let us rewrite the action of the scalar field DGP model
[
11
]
as

S=M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,dy\left((\partial_{\alpha}\varphi)^{2}-(\partial_{y}\varphi)^{2}\right)+M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\,dy\,\delta(y)\,(\partial_{\alpha}\varphi)^{2},\quad\eta_{\mu\nu}\propto(+--\,-).

(3.1)

Fourier transforming the scalar field over the coordinates on the brane, we obtain the solution to the corresponding equation of motion in the bulk

{{}_{5}}\square\varphi(x;y)=0,\quad y\neq 0,

(3.2)

satisfying the radiation boundary condition in the bulk as
[
45
,
39
]

\varphi(x;y)=\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\tilde{\varphi}(p)e^{-ipx}e^{\sqrt{-p^{2}}|y|}=e^{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}|y|}\psi(x),\quad\psi(x)=\varphi(x;0).

(3.3)

To construct the effective action of the scalar field
\psi(x)
on the brane, we substitute Eq. (
3.3
) into the five-dimensional action (
3.1
) and compute the integral over the extra dimension.

Using the Eq. (
3.3
) and the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma, one finds that the parts of the bulk action (
3.1
) to the left and right of the brane have the same form

M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\pm}}dy\left(\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\partial_{\alpha}\varphi-\partial_{y}\varphi\partial_{y}\varphi\right)=M_{5}^{3}\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\sqrt{-p^{2}}\tilde{\varphi}(p)\tilde{\varphi}(-p).

(3.4)

Combining them and substituting the
\delta
-function into the momentum integral, we rewrite the bulk part of the action (
3.1
) in the coordinate space as

\displaystyle M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,dy\left((\partial_{\alpha}\varphi)^{2}-(\partial_{y}\varphi)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2M_{5}^{3}\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k\,\delta^{(4)}(k+p)\sqrt{-p^{2}}\tilde{\varphi}(p)\tilde{\varphi}(k)

\displaystyle=2M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi.

(3.5)

Note that, due to the definition of the square root of d’Alembertian based on the field’s Fourier transform, one can <<integrate by parts>> the integrands containing it without changing the sign

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\,A(x)\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,B(x)=\int d^{4}x\,B(x)\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,A(x).

(3.6)

Thus, the effective action of the DGP scalar field on the brane takes the form
[
45
]

S_{\rm eff}=M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\left(\partial^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\alpha}\psi+M_{c}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi\right).

(3.7)

Correspondingly, the effective equation of motion of the field on the brane is found as
[
45
,
39
]

\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}}{\delta\psi}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square\psi-M_{c}\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi=0.

(3.8)

Here, when varying the action we integrated by parts the square root of d’Alembertian without changing the sign (
3.6
).

3.1.2
Effective energy-momentum tensor on the brane

The effective EMT of the DGP scalar field on the brane cannot be constructed from the action (
3.7
) using the standard formula for the canonical EMT (see, e.g.,
[
74
,
75
]
), since the effective Lagrangian contains not only the first derivatives of the field but also a non-local mass term

{\cal L}_{\rm eff}=M_{4}^{2}\partial^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\alpha}\psi+2M_{5}^{3}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi.

(3.9)

We construct it by the Noether method used to obtain the conservation laws corresponding to the symmetries of the action
[
74
,
75
]
. In particular, in the standard local theory the conservation of EMT corresponds to the invariance of the action with respect to the global shifts of coordinates.

We infinitesimally shift coordinates in the action (
3.7
), temporarily assuming the transformation parameters to depend on initial coordinates

x^{\mu}\to x^{\prime\mu}=x^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}(x),\quad\psi(x)\to\psi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\psi(x),\quad|\xi^{\mu}|\ll 1.

(3.10)

The field derivatives and the transformation Jacobian are expanded in
\xi
up to the first order contributions as

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}),\quad\det\left(\frac{\partial x^{\prime\alpha}}{\partial x^{\beta}}\right)=1+\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square^{\prime}}=\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}-\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}{{}_{4}}\square\xi^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}).

(3.12)

As a result, the action (
3.7
) receives an infinitesimal increment, which up to the boundary terms is written as

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}^{\prime}[\psi^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=S_{\rm eff}[\psi]+\delta S_{\rm eff}[\psi]+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}[\psi]

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\bigg(-2M_{4}^{2}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi+M_{4}^{2}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}\partial^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\alpha}\psi+2M_{5}^{3}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi

\displaystyle-M_{5}^{3}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\psi+M_{5}^{3}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}\psi\bigg).

(3.14)

We remove the derivatives from
\xi^{\alpha}
by integrating by parts and after that assume the transformation parameters to be constants

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\bigg(2M_{4}^{2}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi-\eta_{\alpha\beta}M_{4}^{2}\partial^{\sigma}\psi\partial_{\sigma}\psi-\eta_{\alpha\beta}2M_{5}^{3}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi

\displaystyle+M_{5}^{3}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\psi-M_{5}^{3}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}\psi\bigg),\quad\xi^{\alpha}={\rm Const}.

(3.15)

Here
\overset{\to}{=}
denotes the result of integration by parts up to the boundary terms discarded in the action. In the limit
M_{5}\to 0
, we obtain in Eq. (
3.1.2
) under the 4-divergence operator the canonical EMT of the massless scalar field, being conserved on shell.

In the case of DGP model
M_{5}\neq 0
, we can eliminate some terms under the divergence operator in the Eq. (
3.1.2
). Integrating by parts, we find that the divergence of the non-local part of the action increment is zero off shell

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\bigg(-\eta_{\alpha\beta}2M_{5}^{3}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi+M_{5}^{3}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\psi-M_{5}^{3}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\frac{1}{\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}\psi\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\left(-2M_{5}^{3}\partial_{\beta}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\psi-2M_{5}^{3}\psi\sqrt{{{}_{4}}\square}\,\partial_{\beta}\psi\right)\overset{\to}{=}0.

(3.16)

Thus, the increment of the effective action of DGP scalar takes the form

\delta S_{\rm eff}=\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\bigg(2M_{4}^{2}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi-\eta_{\alpha\beta}M_{4}^{2}\partial^{\sigma}\psi\partial_{\sigma}\psi\bigg).

(3.17)

The tensor under the divergence operator, which does not vanish off shell when the divergence acts on it, is the effective EMT of the DGP scalar on the brane

T_{\alpha\beta}=2M_{4}^{2}\left(\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}\psi\partial_{\sigma}\psi\right).

(3.18)

The EMT (
3.18
) coincides with the effective EMT obtained in Ref.
[
39
]
by projecting the canonical EMT of the DGP scalar in the bulk on the brane and extracting from it the part localized on the brane. Unlike the local theory of a massless scalar field
M_{5}=0
, the obtained effective EMT (
3.18
) is not conserved on the equation of motion (
3.8
)

\partial^{\alpha}T_{\alpha\beta}=2M_{4}^{2}{{}_{4}}\square\,\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi\neq 0

(3.19)

in accordance with the metastable character of the effective graviton on the brane
[
36
]
.

Note that the effective EMT on the brane (
3.18
) could be obtained from the effective Lagrangian (
3.9
) using the standard formula for the canonical EMT
[
74
,
75
]
by neglecting the non-local mass term. Further, an analogous result will also be obtained for the effective EMT of DGP gravity.

3.1.3
Brane localized part of the five-dimensional Hamiltonian

To verify the obtained expression for the effective EMT of the field on the brane (
3.18
), we calculate the Hamiltonian of the five-dimensional theory (
3.1
) and extract from it the part localized on the brane. It should coincide with the
T^{00}
component of the effective EMT, since they both give the energy density of the DGP scalar localized on the brane.

From the action (
3.1
) we find the conjugate momentum of the field in the bulk

\pi=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\varphi)}=2M_{5}^{3}\partial_{0}\varphi+2M_{4}^{2}\delta(y)\partial_{0}\varphi.

(3.20)

Accordingly, the DGP scalar field’s Hamiltonian in the bulk is written as

{\cal H}=M_{5}^{3}\left((\partial_{0}\varphi)^{2}+(\mathbf{\nabla}\varphi)^{2}+(\partial_{y}\varphi)^{2}\right)+M_{4}^{2}\delta(y)\left((\partial_{0}\varphi)^{2}+(\mathbf{\nabla}\varphi)^{2}\right).

(3.21)

Integrating it over a small interval around the brane
y\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
,
\epsilon\to+0
by use of Eq. (
3.3
), we find the brane localized part of the Hamiltonian

{\cal H}_{\rm br}=\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}dy\,{\cal H}=M_{4}^{2}\left((\partial_{0}\psi)^{2}+(\mathbf{\nabla}\psi)^{2}\right).

(3.22)

Obviously, the
T^{00}
component of the effective EMT of the DGP scalar (
3.18
) coincides with the obtained brane localised part of five-dimensional Hamiltonian. Thus, Eq. (
3.18
) provides the correct expression for the effective EMT of the DGP scalar field on the brane.

3.2
Effective energy-momentum tensor of DGP gravity

Now we turn to calculating the effective EMT of the DGP gravity on the brane from the quadratic effective action of DGP graviton
[
45
,
64
,
65
]

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\Big(-\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}+2\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\alpha}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}-2\partial^{\alpha}h\partial^{\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h

\displaystyle-M_{c}\big(h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h-h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}\big)\Big).

(3.23)

One can easily show that the equations of motion resulting from this action coincide with Eq. (
2.38
), obtained by linearising the equations of motion of gravitational field in the bulk. Note also that the action (
3.2
) is the Fierz-Pauli massive gravity action
[
76
,
64
,
65
]
up to replacing the non-local mass with the standard one

-M_{c}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}\leftrightarrow m^{2}.

(3.24)

In particular, due to the lack of gauge symmetry, the effective DGP graviton on the brane has five dynamical degrees of freedom, by analogy with massive graviton.

We obtain the effective EMT of DGP graviton on the brane from the effective action (
3.2
) by use of the Noether method. For this, we infinitesimally shift the coordinates and accordingly transform the gravitational field as

x^{\mu}\to x^{\prime\mu}=x^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}(x),\quad h_{\mu\nu}(x)\to h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=h_{\mu\nu}(x),\quad|\xi^{\mu}|\ll 1.

(3.25)

The transformations of field derivatives and non-local mass terms are given by Eqs. (
3.11
) and (
3.12
). Let us consider separately the increments received by the local and non-local parts of the effective action under this transformations.

We start with the non-local part of the effective action

S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}=\frac{1}{4}M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\left(h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}\,h_{\mu\nu}-h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}\,h\right),

(3.26)

as the increment of local part is expected to yield the canonical EMT of a massless graviton. Up to the first order contributions, we find the increment of the non-local part of effective action (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle{S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}}^{\prime}[h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}]=S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}[h_{\mu\nu}]+\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}[h_{\mu\nu}]+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}),

(3.27)

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}=\frac{1}{4}M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\left(h^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-h\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h+\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\quad-\left.\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h+\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}\partial^{\beta}\xi^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h\partial_{\beta}\partial^{\beta}\xi^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}h\right).

(3.28)

Integrating by parts we remove the derivatives from the local shifts of coordinates
\xi^{\alpha}
and then assume them to be global

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{4}M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}h\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}+\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h\right),\quad\xi^{\alpha}={\rm Const}.

(3.29)

Due to the
\xi^{\alpha}
being constant, we can transform the first two terms in Eq. (
3.2
) to the bilinear form in field derivatives and combine them with the next two terms

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{4}M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\bigg(\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\alpha}h\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h\bigg).

(3.30)

Finally, integrating by parts we find that the 4-divergence participating in the increment of the non-local part of effective action vanishes off shell

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm nl}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{4}M_{5}^{3}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\bigg({{}_{4}}\square h^{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-{{}_{4}}\square h^{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-{{}_{4}}\square h\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h

\displaystyle+{{}_{4}}\square h\frac{1}{\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}}\partial_{\beta}h-\partial_{\beta}h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\beta}h^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\beta}h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h

\displaystyle-\partial_{\beta}h\sqrt{-{{}_{4}}\square}h\bigg)=0.

(3.31)

Thus, the non-local part of effective action (
3.2
) does not contribute to the effective EMT of DGP graviton on the brane, by analogy with the effective EMT of the DGP scalar (
3.18
).

Now we consider the local part of effective action (
3.2
)

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}=\frac{1}{8}M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\left(-\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}+2\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\alpha}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}-2\partial^{\alpha}h\partial^{\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h\right).

(3.32)

After the local shift of coordinate (
3.25
) it is rewritten as

\displaystyle{S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}}^{\prime}[h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}]=S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}[h_{\mu\nu}]+\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}[h_{\mu\nu}]+{\cal O}(\xi^{2}),

(3.33)

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}=\frac{1}{2}M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\,\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}h\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\beta}h\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\alpha}\right.

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h\partial_{\beta}h-\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}h\partial^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h\right).

(3.34)

Integrating by parts we remove derivatives from
\xi^{\alpha}
, in what follows assuming them constant, and combine the third and fourth terms in the increment of the action

\displaystyle\delta S_{\rm eff}^{\rm loc}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{2}M_{4}^{2}\int d^{4}x\,\xi^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\beta}h\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h\partial_{\beta}h\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}h\partial^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h\right).

(3.35)

Given the mostly positive signature of Minkowski metric, the tensor under the 4-divergence is equal to the effective EMT of DGP graviton on the brane with the opposite sign

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}M_{4}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\beta}h\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha}h\partial_{\beta}h\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}h\partial^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h\right).

(3.36)

By analogy with the DGP scalar, the obtained effective EMT coincides with the one calculated from the effective Lagrangian (
3.2
) by use of the standard formula for the canonical EMT neglecting the non-local terms.

Note that, in accordance with the equivalence principle, only the energy density of gravitational field averaged over the region of spacetime with a size
\cal S
of order of several gravitational field wavelengths centered on the observation point
O_{\cal S}(x)
has a physical meaning
[
77
,
78
,
79
]

\langle T_{\alpha\beta}(x)\rangle=\frac{1}{{\cal S}^{4}}\int_{O_{\cal S}(x)}d^{4}x^{\prime}\,T_{\alpha\beta}(x^{\prime}),\quad{\cal S}>\lambdabar.

(3.37)

In this case, one can integrate by parts under the averaging sign and neglect the boundary terms of order
\lambdabar/{\cal S}
choosing a sufficiently large averaging region. As a result, given the Eqs. (
2.38
), the averaged effective EMT of DGP gravity on the brane can be rewritten as

\left\langle T_{\alpha\beta}\right\rangle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{4}M_{4}^{2}\left\langle\partial_{\alpha}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\alpha}h\partial_{\beta}h-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h\right\rangle.

(3.38)

4
Dynamical degrees of freedom of tensionless DGP gravity

As mentioned above, the effective action of DGP graviton on the brane (
3.2
) does not have a gauge symmetry, by analogy with the Fierz-Pauli massive gravity theory
[
76
]
(see, also,
[
64
,
65
]
). Therefore, the effective DGP graviton on the brane has more degrees of freedom compared to the massless graviton. Some of them are non-dynamical and, thus, do not carry the gravitational radiation from the field source to infinity. However, their contribution into the effective EMT (
3.38
) can not be eliminated by an appropriate gauge choice, in contrast with massless gravity
[
80
,
79
]
, due to the lack of gauge symmetry in the effective action (
3.2
). Moreover, taking into account their contribution to the gravitational radiation leads to the non-physical results. As a result, one has to separately construct the effective EMT of dynamical degrees of freedom of DGP graviton to calculate the gravitational radiation in DGP model.

We construct the effective EMT of the dynamical degrees of freedom of DGP graviton from the dynamical part of effective action (
3.2
). For this, one has to decouple the contributions of dynamical (dDOF) and non-dynamical (ndDOF) degrees of freedom into the effective action, which is a non-trivial task in the case of DGP gravity.

Further, we define the dDOF as the degrees of freedom whose equations of motion contain the second time derivatives and for which there are no constraint equations rewriting their first time derivatives in terms of other degrees of freedom.

4.1
Dynamical DOFs of electrodynamics

Let us illustrate the main steps of our approach within a simple example of electrodynamics. Here, the contribution of ndDOF into the EMT is automatically eliminated, in contrast with gravity. Therefore, one can calculate the electromagnetic radiation without fixing a unitary gauge.

4.1.1
Counting DOFs

Consider the free electromagnetic field with the equation of motion
[
81
,
82
]

\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0,\quad F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu},\quad\eta_{\mu\nu}\propto(+--\,-).

(4.1)

To determine the dDOF of electromagnetic field, we perform its scalar-vector (SV) decomposition
[
80
]

A_{\mu}=\left\{A_{0},\partial_{i}\varphi+A_{i}^{\rm t}\right\},\quad\partial_{i}A_{i}^{\rm t}=0,\quad i,j=\overline{1,3},

(4.2)

where the four degrees of freedom of electromagnetic field are divided into two scalar, with respect to the spatial coordinates transformations, degrees of freedom
A_{0}
and
\varphi
and two vector degrees of freedom
A_{i}^{\rm t}
. Correspondingly, the Eq. (
4.1
) is rewritten as

-\Delta A_{0}+\partial_{0}\Delta\varphi=0,\quad-\partial_{0}^{2}\partial_{i}\varphi-\partial_{0}^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\Delta A_{i}^{\rm t}+\partial_{i}\partial_{0}A_{0}=0,\quad\Delta\equiv\partial_{i}\partial_{i}.

(4.3)

Here, only
\varphi
and
A_{i}^{\rm t}
have second time derivatives. However, due to the gauge symmetry of electromagnetic field

A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_{0}\to A_{0}+\partial_{0}\alpha,\quad\varphi\to\varphi+\alpha,\quad A_{i}^{\rm t}\to A_{i}^{\rm t}

(4.4)

the field
\varphi
is the Stuckelberg field and is not a dDOF. In particular, in the unitary gauge
\varphi=0
the equations of motion take the form

\Delta A_{0}=0,\quad-\partial_{0}^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\Delta A_{i}^{\rm t}+\partial_{i}\partial_{0}A_{0}=0.

(4.5)

Thus, the electromagnetic field has only two dDOF
A_{i}^{\rm t}

[
80
]
.

One can also demonstrate it without fixing the unitary gauge. For this, we solve the first equation in (
4.3
) taking into account the boundary condition
A_{0},\varphi\xrightarrow{r\to\infty}0

\Delta\left(\partial_{0}\varphi-A_{0}\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_{0}=\partial_{0}\varphi.

(4.6)

Substituting the solution into the second equation in (
4.3
), we obtain the following system of equations of motion

\partial_{0}^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\Delta A_{i}^{\rm t}=0,\quad A_{0}=\partial_{0}\varphi.

(4.7)

Analogously, here only
A_{i}^{\rm t}
has the second time derivative and, thus, is dDOF, while
A_{0}
and
\varphi
are ndDOF.

Note that the gauge transformations (
4.4
) include only the ndDOF
A_{0}
and
\varphi
, while
A_{i}^{\rm t}
remains unaffected.

4.1.2
Dynamical DOFs contribution to the EMT

Let us demonstrate that the on shell ndDOF of electromagnetic field do not contribute into the symmetrized gauge-invariant EMT
[
81
,
82
,
75
]

T_{\alpha\beta}=F_{\alpha\mu}F^{\mu}_{\,\cdot\,\beta}+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}.

(4.8)

Substituting into it the SV decomposition of electromagnetic field (
4.2
) and rewriting it by components using the Eqs. (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}-\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\right)\left(\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}-\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\right),\quad T_{0k}=\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\left(\partial_{k}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\partial_{i}A_{k}^{\rm t}\right),

(4.9)

\displaystyle T_{kl}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{0}A_{k}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{l}^{\rm t}+\left(\partial_{k}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\partial_{i}A_{k}^{\rm t}\right)\left(\partial_{l}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\partial_{i}A_{l}^{\rm t}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{kl}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{kl}\left(\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}-\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\right)\left(\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}-\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\right).

(4.10)

Thus, all the components of symmetrized EMT depend only on the dDOF
A_{i}^{\rm t}
. Given the gauge invariance of symmetrized EMT, it makes it possible to compute the electromagnetic radiation without fixing the unitary gauge
\varphi=0
and/or separately extracting the EMT of dDOF of electromagnetic field.

Note that we can discard some of the terms in the EMT components, as they contain the derivatives of transverse vector along itself
A_{j}^{\rm t}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}
, which have to vanish. This can be easily understood using the example of a plane monochromatic wave

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\rm t}=\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{-ikx}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k),\quad\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k)=\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k_{0})\delta^{(4)}(k-k_{0}),

(4.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}A_{i}^{\rm t}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k_{0})k_{0i}=0.

(4.12)

In this case, for the derivative of transverse vector along itself we obtain

A_{j}^{\rm t}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}=\int\frac{d^{4}kd^{4}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{8}}\,e^{-ix(k+k^{\prime})}\tilde{A}_{j}^{\rm t}(k^{\prime})ik_{j}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k)=\frac{e^{-2ik_{0}x}}{(2\pi)^{8}}ik_{0j}\tilde{A}_{j}^{\rm t}(k_{0})\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}(k_{0})=0.

(4.13)

Thus, the symmetrized EMT of electromagnetic field is written as

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t},

(4.14)

\displaystyle T_{0k}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{k}A_{i}^{\rm t},

(4.15)

\displaystyle T_{kl}

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{l}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{kl}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{kl}\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}\partial_{i}A_{j}^{\rm t}.

(4.16)

Here, some terms in the
(kl)
-components of EMT have been discarded, due to the Eqs. (
4.7
) and the corresponding dispersion relation

{{}_{4}}\square A_{i}^{\rm t}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k_{0\mu}^{2}=0.

(4.17)

From this one easily finds that the symmetrized EMT of electromagnetic field can be rewritten in terms of dDOF
A_{i}^{\rm t}
as

T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\nu}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\alpha}A_{i}^{\rm t}.

(4.18)

It has the structure of the canonical EMT of a set of massless <<scalar fields>>
\left\{A_{i}^{\rm t}\right\}
. Further, we demonstrate that the EMT of both massless gravity and DGP gravity also has an analogous structure.

4.1.3
Action and EMT of dynamical DOFs

Finally, we extract from the action of free electromagnetic field
[
81
,
82
,
75
]

S=-\frac{1}{4}\int d^{4}x\,F_{\mu\nu}^{2}

(4.19)

the part corresponding to the dDOF and obtain from it the canonical EMT of dDOF of electromagnetic field coinciding with the Eq. (
4.18
).

Integrating by parts and taking into account the transversality of the dDOF
A_{i}^{\rm t}
, we rewrite the action of electromagnetic field as

S\overset{\to}{=}\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}A_{0}\partial_{i}A_{0}-\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{i}A_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\right).

(4.20)

Here, the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom are decoupled and we can consider the actions for them separately

\displaystyle S=S_{\rm d}[A_{i}^{\rm t}]+S_{\rm nd}[\varphi,A_{0}],

(4.21)

\displaystyle S_{\rm d}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\partial^{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t},

(4.22)

\displaystyle S_{\rm nd}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\big(\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi+\partial_{i}A_{0}\partial_{i}A_{0}-2\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{i}A_{0}\big).

(4.23)

One can demonstrate that the non-dynamical part of the action is gauge-invariant (
4.4
). Also, it is easy to show that the obtained actions yield the equations of motion (
4.7
).

We obtain the canonical EMT of dDOF of electromagnetic field from the action (
4.22
) using the standard formula
[
81
,
82
,
75
]

{T_{\rm d}}^{\mu}_{\,\cdot\,\nu}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{\rm d}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t})}\partial_{\nu}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}{\cal L}_{\rm d},\quad{\cal L}_{\rm d}=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t}.

(4.24)

As a result, we find the EMT of dDOF of electromagnetic field
A_{i}^{\rm t}
coinciding with the symmetrized EMT (
4.18
)

{T_{\rm d}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\nu}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\alpha}A_{i}^{\rm t}.

(4.25)

Therefore, in electrodynamics there are two equivalent ways of computing the electromagnetic radiation:

•

By splitting the degrees of freedom.
One has to extract the action of dDOF of electromagnetic field and obtain the corresponding equations of motion and the canonical EMT. Next, one has to solve the equations of motion for the dDOF and calculate the radiation energy flux carried by them.

•

Without splitting the degrees of freedom.
One has to construct the symmetrized EMT of electromagnetic field and solve the equations of motion without splitting the dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom. After this, one computes the corresponding radiation energy flux, in which the contributions of ndDOF mutually cancel out.

Further, we demonstrate that in the case of gravity the second method for calculating the gravitational radiation is not available (at least when using the Isaacson EMT).

4.2
Dynamical DOFs of gravity

Let us demonstrate that in the case of massless gravity Isaacson EMT, being gauge invariant, contains contributions from the ndDOF of gravitational field, in contrast with electrodynamics. Therefore, to correctly compute the gravitational radiation one has either to fix the unitary gauge to use the Isaacson EMT or to construct the canonical EMT of dDOF of gravitational field.

4.2.1
Isaacson energy-momentum tensor of gravitational field

In accordance with the Isaacson approach
[
77
,
78
]
(see, also,
[
79
]
), in GR the EMT of gravitational field is constructed from the quadratic part of expansion of Einstein tensor in metric perturbations

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left\langle{{}_{(2)}}G_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle,\quad G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R,\quad g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},\quad|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll 1,

(4.26)

where
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is some background metric, and
{{}_{(2)}}G_{\mu\nu}
is the quadratic part of Einstein tensor expansion in
h_{\mu\nu}
. Here, the averaging (
3.37
) guarantees the gauge-invariance of EMT.

Therefore, on the Minkowski space background
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\propto(-++\,+)
the Isaacson EMT of gravitational field is written as

t_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left\langle{{}_{(2)}}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}{{}_{(2)}}R-\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}{{}_{(1)}}R\right\rangle,

(4.27)

where
{{}_{(1)}}R
,
{{}_{(2)}}R
, and
{{}_{(2)}}R_{\mu\nu}
denote the Ricci tensor and scalar expansions up to the first and second order contributions in
h_{\mu\nu}
, correspondingly. Integrating by parts under the averaging operator, we obtain the Isaacson EMT in the following form
[
77
,
78
,
79
]

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left\langle\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\mu}h\partial^{\beta}h_{\beta\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\partial_{\nu}h\partial^{\beta}h_{\mu\beta}+\frac{3}{4}\partial^{\beta}h\partial_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\beta}h_{\beta\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\beta\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\mu}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{8}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h\partial_{\sigma}h+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\sigma\beta}-\frac{1}{8}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h^{\alpha\beta}\right\rangle.

(4.28)

Note that until this point we assumed that the metric perturbations satisfy the general linear equation of motion (see, e.g.,
[
79
]
)

\partial^{\sigma}\partial_{\mu}h_{\sigma\nu}+\partial^{\sigma}\partial_{\nu}h_{\sigma\mu}-{{}_{4}}\square h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}h-\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\eta_{\mu\nu}{{}_{4}}\square h=0.

(4.29)

We simplify the equation of motion by fixing the Lorentz gauge

\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\nu}h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square\left(h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}h\right)=0.

(4.30)

Correspondingly, the EMT (
4.2.1
) is also simplified significantly given the Eq. (
4.30
)

t_{\mu\nu}\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left\langle\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}h\right\rangle.

(4.31)

The obtained Isaacson EMT is invariant under the residual gauge transformations
[
79
]

h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu},\quad{{}_{4}}\square\xi_{\mu}=0

(4.32)

up to the 4-divergence, which can be neglected under the averaging operator

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{32\pi G}\bigg\langle\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}h+2\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}+2\partial_{\nu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}

\displaystyle-\partial_{\nu}h\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}+2\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}+2\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\beta}\xi_{\alpha}-2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\gamma}\xi^{\gamma}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{32\pi G}\bigg\langle\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}h+\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}+\partial_{\nu}h\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}-\partial_{\mu}h\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}

\displaystyle-\partial_{\nu}h\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}-2\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\nu}{{}_{4}}\square\xi_{\beta}+2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}-2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\gamma}\xi^{\gamma}\bigg\rangle=t_{\mu\nu}.

(4.33)

Thus, one can expect that the gravitational radiation energy flux calculated using the Isaacson EMT both in the Lorentz gauge (
4.31
) preserving the part of ndDOF’s contributions and in the transverse-traceless (unitary) gauge eliminating all the ndDOF’s contributions should be the same. However, the EMT (
4.31
) does not lead to the correct quadrupole formula for the gravitational radiation power of a non-relativistic source without fixing the transverse-traceless gauge. In particular, in Appendix
A
we demonstrate that this EMT yields a non-physical negative result for the gravitational radiation power of a non-relativistic harmonically oscillating point mass. Below, we demonstrate that this is due to the remaining contributions of ndDOF of gravitational field into the Isaacson EMT (
4.31
).

4.2.2
Counting DOFs

To introduce our notation, in this section we determine the dDOF of massless gravitational field. For this, we perform its scalar-vector-tensor (SVT) decomposition
[
83
,
84
,
85
,
80
]

\displaystyle h_{00}=-2\Phi,\quad h_{0i}=\partial_{i}B+S_{i},\quad h_{ij}=-2\delta_{ij}\Psi+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E+\partial_{i}F_{j}+\partial_{j}F_{i}+h_{ij}^{\rm tt},

(4.34)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}S_{i}=0,\quad\partial_{i}F_{i}=0,\quad\partial_{i}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0,\quad h_{ii}^{\rm tt}=0.

(4.35)

As a result, the linearized Einstein equations (
4.29
) are rewritten as

\displaystyle 2\Delta\Psi=0,\quad 4\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\Psi+\Delta\left(\partial_{0}F_{i}-S_{i}\right)=0,

(4.36)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}^{2}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}-\Delta h_{ij}^{\rm tt}+2\delta_{ij}\left(\Delta\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)+2\partial_{0}^{2}\Psi-\partial_{0}^{2}\Delta E+\partial_{0}\Delta B\right)-2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left(\Phi-\Psi\right)-2\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}B

\displaystyle\quad-\partial_{0}\left(\partial_{i}S_{j}+\partial_{j}S_{i}\right)+2\partial_{0}^{2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E+\partial_{0}^{2}\left(\partial_{i}F_{j}+\partial_{j}F_{i}\right)=0.

(4.37)

We partially solve these equations to obtain the constraints equations for the gravitational field components. From the first equation in (
4.36
), given the boundary condition
h_{\mu\nu}\xrightarrow{r\to\infty}0
, we find

\Delta\Psi=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Psi=0.

(4.38)

Substituting this solution into the second equation in (
4.36
), we arrive at

\Delta\left(\partial_{0}F_{i}-S_{i}\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\partial_{0}F_{i}=S_{i}.

(4.39)

Using Eqs. (
4.38
) and (
4.39
) we find the trace of Eq. (
4.37
) in the form

\Delta\left(\Phi-\partial_{0}^{2}E+\partial_{0}B\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi+\partial_{0}B=\partial_{0}^{2}E.

(4.40)

Finally, substituting Eqs. (
4.38
), (
4.39
), and (
4.40
) into the Eq. (
4.37
), we rewrite the system of gravitational field equations of motion as

\partial_{0}^{2}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}-\Delta h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0,\quad\Psi=0,\quad\partial_{0}F_{i}=S_{i},\quad\Phi+\partial_{0}B=\partial_{0}^{2}E.

(4.41)

Here, only the fields
h_{ij}^{\rm tt}
and
E
contain the second time derivatives. However,
E
is not a dDOF, due to the gauge symmetry of gravitational field
[
79
]

h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu},\quad\xi_{\mu}=\left\{\xi_{0},\partial_{i}\xi+\xi_{i}^{\rm t}\right\},\quad\partial_{i}\xi_{i}^{\rm t}=0,

(4.42)

which in SVT decomposition (
4.34
) is written as

\displaystyle\Phi\to\Phi-\partial_{0}\xi_{0},\quad B\to B+\xi_{0}+\partial_{0}\xi,\quad S_{i}\to S_{i}+\partial_{0}\xi_{i}^{\rm t},

(4.43)

\displaystyle\Psi\to\Psi,\quad E\to E+\xi,\quad F_{i}\to F_{i}+\xi_{i}^{\rm t},\quad h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\to h_{ij}^{\rm tt}.

(4.44)

Thus,
E
is the Stuckelberg field and is not a dDOF. In particular, in the unitary gauge
B=E=F_{i}=0
all the fields except
h_{ij}^{\rm tt}
vanish on the equations of motion (
4.41
), while the latter satisfies the free wave equation. Therefore, the massless gravitational field has two dDOF
h_{ij}^{\rm tt}
, as expected
[

]
.

4.2.3
Non-dynamical DOFs contribution into the Isaacson EMT

Substituting the SVT expansion of gravitational field (
4.34
) into the Eq. (
4.31
), integrating by parts, and using the equations of motion (
4.41
), we arrive at the following expression for the Isaacson EMT of gravitational field

\displaystyle t_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left\langle\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}+2\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Phi-2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{i}B\partial_{\nu}\partial_{i}B-2\partial_{\mu}S_{i}\partial_{\nu}S_{i}+2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{i}F_{j}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{i}F_{j}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial_{\nu}\Delta E-2\partial_{\mu}\Delta E\partial_{\nu}\Phi+2\partial_{\mu}\Delta E\partial_{\nu}\Delta E\right\rangle.

(4.45)

Here, we have used the Isaacson EMT in the Lorentz gauge (
4.30
), which in the SVT decomposition (
4.34
) is written as

\partial_{0}\Phi+\Delta B+3\partial_{0}\Psi-\partial_{0}\Delta E=0,\quad-\partial_{0}S_{i}+\Delta F_{i}=0,\quad-\partial_{0}B+\Psi+\Delta E-\Phi=0.

(4.46)

Thus, outside the unitary gauge the contributions of ndDOF are preserved in the Isaacson EMT (
4.31
), vanishing only in the transverse-traceless (unitary) gauge on the equations of motion (
4.41
)

B=E=F_{i}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left\langle\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\right\rangle.

(4.47)

As a result, in contrast with the electrodynamics, Isaacson gauge-invariant EMT generally preserves the contributions of ndDOF of gravitational field, leading to the need to either calculate gravitational radiation in the unitary gauge or to separately extract the EMT of dDOF.

4.2.4
Action and EMT of dynamical DOFs

Let us extract the dynamical part of the action of massless gravitational field. We start with the quadratic approximation of the Einstein-Hilbert action
[
79
]

S=-\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\left(\partial^{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}+2\partial_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}h-2\partial_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\sigma\nu}-\partial^{\mu}h\partial_{\mu}h\right).

(4.48)

Substituting the Eqs. (
4.34
) and (
4.35
) into it, we obtain

\displaystyle S=S_{\rm d}[h_{ij}^{\rm tt}]+S_{\rm nd}[\Phi,B,S_{i},\Psi,E,F_{i}],

(4.49)

\displaystyle S_{\rm d}=-\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\partial^{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt},

(4.50)

\displaystyle S_{\rm nd}=\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\big(16\partial_{0}\Psi\partial_{0}\Delta E-24\partial_{0}\Psi\partial_{0}\Psi+2\partial_{0}\partial_{i}F_{j}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}F_{j}+2\partial_{k}S_{i}\partial_{k}S_{i}

\displaystyle\quad-16\Delta B\partial_{0}\Psi-4\partial_{0}S_{i}\Delta F_{i}+8\partial_{i}\Psi\partial_{i}\Psi-16\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{i}\Psi\big).

(4.51)

Therefore, in massless gravity the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom of gravitational field into the action are automatically decoupled, by analogy with electrodynamics (
4.21
–
4.23
) and, thus, one can extract the dynamical part of the action. Note also that the gauge symmetry (
4.43
–
4.44
) is entirely contained in the non-dynamical part of the action, while the dynamical part is not affected by the gauge transformations, by analogy with electrodynamics. One easily finds that the action (
4.49
) yields the equations of motion

{{}_{4}}\square h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0,\quad\Delta\Psi=0,\quad\partial_{0}B+\Phi=\partial_{0}^{2}E,\quad S_{i}=\partial_{0}F_{i},

(4.52)

coinciding with the Eq. (
4.41
) obtained by linearizing the Einstein equations.

Finally, we construct the canonical EMT of the dDOF of massless gravitational field
[
81
,
82
,
75
]

{T_{\rm d}}^{\mu}_{\,\cdot\,\nu}=-\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{\rm d}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt})}\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}+\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}{\cal L}_{\rm d},\quad{\cal L}_{\rm d}=-\frac{1}{64\pi G}\partial^{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}.

(4.53)

Here, the canonical EMT is defined with the opposite sign, compared to the Eq. (
4.24
), due to the use of Minkowski metric with the mostly positive signature. As a result, the canonical EMT of the dDOF of massless gravitational field is written as

{T_{\rm d}}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left(\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\alpha}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\right).

(4.54)

By analogy with the EMT of electromagnetic field (
4.25
), it also has the structure of the EMT of a set of <<scalar fields>>
\big\{h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\big\}
. After averaging (
3.37
), the obtained EMT coincides on the equations of motion (
4.52
) with the Isaacson EMT in the unitary gauge (
4.47
)

{{}_{4}}\square h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\langle{T_{\rm d}}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle\overset{\to}{=}\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left\langle\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}\right\rangle.

(4.55)

Thus, in massless gravity, besides using the Isaacson EMT in the unitary gauge, the gravitational radiation can be equivalently computed as the energy flux through a distant surface enclosing the radiation source determined by the canonical EMT of dDOF of gravitational field (
4.55
).

4.3
Dynamical DOFs of massive electrodynamics

In contrast with electrodynamics and massless gravity, the effective action of DGP gravity (
3.2
) does not have a gauge symmetry, obscuring the extraction of dynamical part of the action. Therefore, before proceeding to the construction of the effective EMT of dDOF of DGP gravity, we first consider a couple of simple examples of gauge-noninvariant theories. Let us start with the massive electrodynamics.

The action of free massive electrodynamics has the form
[
64
,
65
]

S=\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}A_{\mu}^{2}\right),\quad\eta_{\mu\nu}\propto(+--\,-),

(4.56)

and the corresponding equations of motion are written as

\partial^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\mu}+m^{2}A_{\mu}=0.

(4.57)

Acting on it by the 4-divergence, we obtain the constraint equation
\partial^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0
for the components of the vector field. Using it, we rewrite the Eq. (
4.57
) as the system of equations

{{}_{4}}\square A_{\mu}+m^{2}A_{\mu}=0,\quad\partial^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0.

(4.58)

As is demonstrated below, to decouple the dynamical and non-dynamical parts of the action (
4.56
) one has to introduce the gauge symmetry into the action by use of the Stuckelberg change of field variables
[
64
,
65
]
and then fix it by imposing such a gauge condition that in terms of the new field variables the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom into the action are decoupled.

4.3.1
Counting DOFs

To determine the dDOF of massive vector field, we perform its SV decomposition in accordance with the Eq. (
4.2
). As a result, the equations of motion (
4.58
) are rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{0}^{2}A_{0}-\Delta A_{0}+m^{2}A_{0}=0,

(4.59)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}^{2}\partial_{i}\varphi+\partial_{0}^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\Delta\partial_{i}\varphi-\Delta A_{i}^{\rm tt}+m^{2}\partial_{i}\varphi+m^{2}A_{i}^{\rm tt}=0,

(4.60)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}A_{0}=\Delta\varphi.

(4.61)

Acting by the 3-divergence on the Eq. (
4.60
), we split it into the equations of motion for
\varphi
and
A_{i}^{\rm t}

\Delta\left({{}_{4}}\square\varphi+m^{2}\varphi\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square\varphi+m^{2}\varphi=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square A_{i}^{\rm t}+m^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}=0.

(4.62)

Finally, substituting the constraint equation (
4.61
) into the Eq. (
4.59
), we arrive at the following system of equations of motion for the massive vector field components

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Delta\varphi-\Delta A_{0}+m^{2}A_{0}=0,\quad\partial_{0}A_{0}=\Delta\varphi,

(4.63)

\displaystyle{{}_{4}}\square\varphi+m^{2}\varphi=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square A_{i}^{\rm t}+m^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}=0.

(4.64)

Thus, the massive vector field has three dDOF
\varphi
and
A_{i}^{\rm t}
, in contrast with massless electrodynamics, where
\varphi
is the Stuckelberg field (
4.4
).

4.3.2
Action and EMT of dynamical DOFs

Decomposing the action of massive electrodynamics (
4.56
) by use of Eq. (
4.2
), we find that the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom are not decoupled

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{0}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}A_{i}^{\rm t}A_{i}^{\rm t}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{i}\varphi\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}A_{0}\partial_{i}A_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}A_{0}A_{0}-\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{i}A_{0}\right).

(4.65)

Here, the last term in the action (
4.3.2
) contains the interaction of dynamical degree of freedom
\varphi
and non-dynamical
A_{0}
. Analogous term is also present in the action of massless electrodynamics (
4.20
), however, in this case
\varphi
is a non-dynamical degree of freedom.

We decouple the contributions of dDOF and ndDOF into the action using the Stuckelberg method
[
64
,
65
]
, introducing the gauge symmetry into the action by the appropriate change of field variables. Namely, we make the following change of field variables

A_{\mu}=\tilde{A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\phi,

(4.66)

after which the action (
4.56
) is rewritten as

S=\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{2}+m^{2}\tilde{A}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi\right).

(4.67)

Here, we obtained the kinetic interaction of fields
\tilde{A}_{\mu}
and
\phi
, and the action acquired the gauge symmetry

\tilde{A}_{\mu}\to\tilde{A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha,\quad\phi\to\phi-\alpha.

(4.68)

The equations of motion corresponding to the obtained action have the form

\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\mu}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}+m\partial_{\mu}\varphi=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\varphi+m\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=0,

(4.69)

where we have rescaled the scalar field as
\phi=\varphi/m
(note that here
\varphi
does not coincide with the longitudinal part of the vector field in Eq. (
4.2
)).

We fix the gauge symmetry (
4.68
) by imposing the gauge condition similar to the Lorentz gauge in massless electrodynamics, which decouples the equations of motion (
4.69
)
[
64
,
65
]

\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=m\varphi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square\tilde{A}_{\mu}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\varphi+m^{2}\varphi=0.

(4.70)

Adding to the action (
4.67
) the corresponding gauge-fixing term

S_{\rm gf}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left(\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}-m\varphi\right)^{2}

(4.71)

and integrating by parts, we rewrite the action (
4.67
) as

S+S_{\rm gf}\overset{\to}{=}\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\mu}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}\right).

(4.72)

As a result, we have decoupled the
\tilde{A}_{\mu}
and
\varphi
contributions into the action, obtaining in both cases the canonical kinetic and mass terms in the action. The obtained action still has the residual gauge symmetry

\tilde{A}_{\mu}\to\tilde{A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha,\quad\varphi\to\varphi-m\alpha,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\alpha+m^{2}\alpha=0.

(4.73)

As the field
\varphi
also satisfies the free massive wave equation, we completely fix the gauge symmetry by imposing the unitary gauge condition
\varphi=0
. Note that one can fix this gauge even in the presence of a conserved vector current as the source of massive vector field
[
64
,
65
]
. Thus, in the unitary gauge the action of massive electrodynamics takes the form

S+S_{\rm gf}=\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{2}\right).

(4.74)

By fixing the unitary gauge, we have returned to the original field variables
\tilde{A}_{\mu}=A_{\mu}
satisfying, by virtue of Eq. (
4.70
), the system of equations of motion (
4.58
)

{{}_{4}}\square\tilde{A}_{\mu}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=0,\quad\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=0.

(4.75)

As a result of this change of field variables, the dDOF and ndDOF contributions into the action (
4.74
) are decoupled. Performing the SV decomposition of the vector field (
4.2
)

\tilde{A}_{\mu}=\big\{\tilde{A}_{0},\partial_{i}\tilde{A}+\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\big\},\quad\partial_{i}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}=0,

(4.76)

we rewrite the action (
4.74
) as

\displaystyle S+S_{\rm gf}

\displaystyle\overset{\to}{=}\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{0}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\right),\quad\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}=\partial_{i}\tilde{A}.

(4.77)

From the corresponding equations of motion

{{}_{4}}\square\tilde{A}_{0}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{0}=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}+m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}=0

(4.78)

and gauge condition
\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}=0
we find that the dynamical degrees of freedom of massive electrodynamics are
\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}
and
\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}
. Therefore, the action of massive electrodynamics (
4.74
) is splitted into the dynamical and non-dynamical parts as

\displaystyle S+S_{\rm gf}=S_{\rm d}[\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l},\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}]+S_{\rm nd}[\tilde{A}_{0}],

(4.79)

\displaystyle S_{\rm d}=\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\right),

(4.80)

\displaystyle S_{\rm nd}=\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{0}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{0}^{2}\right).

(4.81)

Note that the non-dynamical part enters the action with the opposite sign –
\tilde{A}_{0}
is the ghost degree of freedom. However, it poses no danger here, being non-dynamical and not interacting with other degrees of freedom.

Finally, from the action (
4.80
) we find the canonical EMT of dynamical degrees of freedom of massive electrodynamics

\displaystyle{T_{\rm d}}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}+\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}m^{2}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}.

(4.82)

By analogy with massless electrodynamics (
4.25
), it has the structure of the EMT of a set of massive <<scalar fields>>
\{\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l},\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\}
. In accordance with the presence of the third dynamical degree of freedom
\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}
in the massive vector field, in the massless limit it does not transform into the EMT of massless electrodynamics (
4.25
)

\displaystyle{T_{\rm d}}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{m\to 0}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm l}+\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}

\displaystyle\neq\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{i}^{\rm t}.

(4.83)

Thus, to decouple the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom into the action of a theory without gauge symmetry, such as massive electrodynamics and massive gravity, or DGP gravity, one can use the Stuckelberg trick, introducing the gauge symmetry into the action by the appropriate change of field variables and then fixing it by imposing the decoupling gauge condition.

4.4
Dynamical DOFs of massive gravity

Let us now construct the canonical EMT of dDOF of massive gravity. As the effective action of DGP gravity (
3.2
) coincides with the action of massive gravity up to replacing the non-local mass by the rigid one (
3.24
), the EMT of dDOF of DGP gravity will be constructed by analogy with the derivations presented in this section.

The action of free Fierz-Pauli massive gravity is written as
[
76
,
64
,
65
]

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\big(-\partial^{\sigma}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\alpha\beta}+2\partial^{\alpha}h_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h^{\mu\beta}-2\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}h

\displaystyle+\partial^{\alpha}h\partial_{\alpha}h+m^{2}(h^{2}-h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta})\big),\quad\eta_{\mu\nu}\propto(-++\,+).

(4.84)

We obtain the corresponding equation of motion of the massive graviton in the form

{{}_{4}}\square h_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\sigma\beta}-\partial_{\beta}\partial^{\sigma}h_{\alpha\sigma}+\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h+\eta_{\alpha\beta}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}{{}_{4}}\square h-m^{2}\left(h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\right)=0.

(4.85)

By analogy with massive electrodynamics, we reduced it to the simpler form, obtaining from it the constraint equations for the components of gravitational field. Acting on the equation (
4.85
) by the 4-divergence, we find the first constraint equation

\partial^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\beta}h.

(4.86)

Substituting it into the Eq. (
4.85
), we arrive at the system of equations of motion

{{}_{4}}\square h_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h-m^{2}\left(h_{\alpha\beta}-\eta_{\alpha\beta}h\right)=0,\quad\partial^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\beta}h.

(4.87)

Calculating the trace of the obtained equation, we find the second constraint equation

h=0.

(4.88)

As a result, we rewrite the equation of motion of the massive gravitational field (
4.85
) as the free massive wave equation and two constraint equations
[
64
,
65
]

{{}_{4}}\square h_{\alpha\beta}-m^{2}h_{\alpha\beta}=0,\quad\partial^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}=0,\quad h=0.

(4.89)

4.4.1
Counting DOFs

Substituting the SVT decomposition (
4.34
–
4.35
) into the Eq. (
4.89
), we obtain the equations of motion of massive gravity in the form

\displaystyle{{}_{4}}\square\Phi-m^{2}\Phi=0,

(4.90)

\displaystyle{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{i}B+{{}_{4}}\square S_{i}-m^{2}\partial_{i}B-m^{2}S_{i}=0,

(4.91)

\displaystyle-2\delta_{ij}{{}_{4}}\square\Psi+2{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E+{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{i}F_{j}+{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{j}F_{i}+{{}_{4}}\square h_{ij}^{\rm tt}+2\delta_{ij}m^{2}\Psi-2m^{2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E

\displaystyle\quad-m^{2}\partial_{i}F_{j}-m^{2}\partial_{j}F_{i}-m^{2}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0,

(4.92)

while the constraint equations for the components of gravitational field are written as

\displaystyle 2\partial_{0}\Phi+\Delta B=0,

(4.93)

\displaystyle-\partial_{0}\partial_{i}B-\partial_{0}S_{i}-2\partial_{i}\Psi+2\Delta\partial_{i}E+\Delta F_{i}=0,

(4.94)

\displaystyle\Phi-3\Psi+\Delta E=0.

(4.95)

We decouple the equations of motion by use of the constraint equations. Acting on the Eq. (
4.91
) with 3-divergence, we decouple the equations of motion for
B
and
S_{i}

\Delta\left({{}_{4}}\square B-m^{2}B\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square B-m^{2}B=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square S_{i}-m^{2}S_{i}=0.

(4.96)

Calculating the trace of the Eq. (
4.92
), we obtain

\frac{1}{3}\Delta\left({{}_{4}}\square E-m^{2}E\right)={{}_{4}}\square\Psi-m^{2}\Psi.

(4.97)

Substituting the obtained relation back into the Eq. (
4.92
), we arrive at the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}\delta_{ij}\Delta\left({{}_{4}}\square E-m^{2}E\right)+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left({{}_{4}}\square E-m^{2}E\right)+{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{i}F_{j}+{{}_{4}}\square\partial_{j}F_{i}+{{}_{4}}\square h_{ij}^{\rm tt}

\displaystyle\quad-m^{2}\partial_{i}F_{j}-m^{2}\partial_{j}F_{i}-m^{2}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0.

(4.98)

Acting on it twice by the 3-divergence, we decouple the equations of motion for
\Psi
and
E

\Delta^{2}\left({{}_{4}}\square E-m^{2}E\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square E-m^{2}E=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square\Psi-m^{2}\Psi=0.

(4.99)

Finally, given the obtained equations of motion, acting on the Eq. (
4.92
) by the 3-divergence, we decouple the equations of motion for
F_{i}
and
h_{ij}^{\rm tt}

\Delta\left({{}_{4}}\square F_{j}-m^{2}F_{j}\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{{}_{4}}\square F_{j}-m^{2}F_{j}=0,\quad{{}_{4}}\square h_{ij}^{\rm tt}-m^{2}h_{ij}^{\rm tt}=0.

(4.100)

Thus, all the components of massive gravitational field satisfy the free massive wave equations.

Now we determine the dDOF of massive graviton by use of the constraint equations. For this, we split the Eq. (
4.94
) into two constraint equations, acting on it by the 3-divergence

\Delta\left(-\partial_{0}B-2\Psi+2\Delta E\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-\partial_{0}B-2\Psi+2\Delta E=0,\quad-\partial_{0}S_{i}+\Delta F_{i}=0.

(4.101)

Given the Eq. (
4.93
), we find that
\Phi
,
B
and
S_{i}
are ndDOF. To obtain another constraint equation, we act by the time derivative on the Eq. (
4.95
). Then from the system of equations

\partial_{0}\Phi-3\partial_{0}\Psi+\partial_{0}\Delta E=0,\quad 2\partial_{0}\Phi+\Delta B=0,\quad-\partial_{0}B-2\Psi+2\Delta E=0,

(4.102)

we find the following expressions for the first time derivatives of the fields
\Psi
and
E

\partial_{0}\Psi=-\frac{1}{4}m^{2}B,\quad\partial_{0}\Delta E=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}^{2}B-\frac{1}{4}m^{2}B.

(4.103)

Therefore,
\Psi
is also the ndDOF, in contrast with
E
, as substituting the expression for its time derivative into the equation of motion increases its order in time derivatives. As a result, the massive gravitational field has five dDOF
E
,
F_{i}
and
h_{ij}^{\rm tt}
, as expected
[
64
,
65
]
.

4.4.2
Action and EMT of dynamical DOFs

By analogy with massive electrodynamics, substituting the SVT decomposition of the massive gravitational field (
4.34
) into the action (
4.84
), we do not decouple the contributions of dynamical and non-dynamical degrees of freedom into the action. One can see this from the decomposition of the action of massless gravity (
4.49
), which is also contained in the decomposition of the massive gravity action. There, the interaction term
\propto\partial_{0}\Psi\partial_{0}\Delta E
is present in the non-dynamical part of the action (
4.51
). However, in massive gravity the field
E
is dDOF and does not allow one to directly split the action (
4.84
) into the dynamical and non-dynamical parts.

To split the action (
4.84
) into the dynamical and non-dynamical parts, we use the Stuckelberg method
[
64
,
65
]
, by analogy with massive electrodynamics. However, in this case, to decouple the contributions of dDOF and ndDOF into the action, we need to make three changes of field variables. The first one is written as

h_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\tilde{A}_{\mu}.

(4.104)

After this field variables change the action of massive gravity (
4.84
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\Big(-\partial^{\sigma}\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}+2\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{h}^{\mu\beta}-2\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{h}+\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{h}

\displaystyle+m^{2}\big(\tilde{h}^{2}-\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}\big)-m^{2}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}^{2}+4m^{2}\big(\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{\alpha}\tilde{h}-\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{A}_{\beta}\big)\Big)

(4.105)

and acquires the gauge symmetry

\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}\to\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}+\partial_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}\xi_{\alpha},\quad\tilde{A}_{\alpha}\to\tilde{A}_{\alpha}-\xi_{\alpha}.

(4.106)

Making the second change of field variables

\tilde{A}_{\mu}=\bar{A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\phi,

(4.107)

we arrive at the action (
4.4.2
) in the form

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\,\Big(-\partial^{\sigma}\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}+2\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{h}^{\mu\beta}-2\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{h}+\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{h}

\displaystyle+m^{2}\big(\tilde{h}^{2}-\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}\big)-m^{2}\bar{F}_{\alpha\beta}^{2}+4m^{2}\big(\partial^{\alpha}\bar{A}_{\alpha}\tilde{h}-\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\bar{A}_{\beta}\big)

\displaystyle+4m^{2}\big(\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\tilde{h}-\tilde{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\phi\big)\Big)

(4.108)

introducing into it the second independent gauge symmetry

\bar{A}_{\mu}\to\bar{A}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha,\quad\phi\to\phi-\alpha.

(4.109)

Finally, to eliminate the interaction between
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi
, we make the third change of variables

\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}=\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}+m^{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\phi,

(4.110)

after which the action of massive gravity is written as
[
64
,
65
]

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64\pi G}\int d^{4}x\Big(-\partial^{\sigma}\bar{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\sigma}\bar{h}_{\alpha\beta}+2\partial^{\alpha}\bar{h}_{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\bar{h}^{\mu\beta}-2\partial_{\alpha}\bar{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\bar{h}+\partial^{\alpha}\bar{h}\partial_{\alpha}\bar{h}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ij,kl}^{\rm tt}=P_{ik}P_{jl}-\frac{1}{2}P_{ij}P_{kl},\quad n_{i}\Lambda_{ij,kl}^{\rm tt}=0,\quad\Lambda_{ii,kl}^{\rm tt}=\Lambda_{ij,kk}^{\rm tt}=0,

(5.10)

\displaystyle n_{i}=x_{i}/r,\quad P_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-n_{i}n_{j}.

(5.11)

(5.12)

\displaystyle G_{4}(x|\mu)=\frac{\theta(t)}{2\pi}\left[\delta(x^{2})J_{0}(\mu\sqrt{x^{2}})-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\theta(x^{2})}{\sqrt{x^{2}}}\mu J_{1}(\mu\sqrt{x^{2}})\right],\quad\rho(\mu)=\frac{M_{c}^{2}}{\mu^{2}+M_{c}^{2}},

\displaystyle\psi_{ij}

5.3
Quadrupole formula in DGP gravity

Having extracted the emitted parts of dDOF of DGP gravity, we now calculate the effective gravitational radiation power of an arbitrary non-relativistic source on the brane.

6
Conclusions

The aim of this paper was to study the process of gravitational radiation in the DGP gravity model within the framework of classical field theory. Considering small perturbations of the gravitational field on the background of a flat tensionless brane embedded in the five-dimensional Minkowski bulk, we obtained an analogue of the quadrupole formula for the effective power of gravitational radiation of an arbitrary non-relativistic source on the brane (
5.33
). Also, based on the obtained expression for the gravitational field on the brane in the wave zone (
5.25
) and (
5.30
), we estimated the parameters of the Deffayet-Menou formula (
1.1
)
[
47
]
. It is shown that the parameter
n
, which characterizes the steepness of the transition of the asymptotics of the gravitational field from the four-dimensional to the five-dimensional regime, has the value
n=1
, while the crossover radius of the DGP model
R_{c}
should be replaced in this formula by the effective crossover radius
R_{\rm eff}=2\bar{\omega}R_{c}
(where
\bar{\omega}=\omega/M_{c}
), depending on the frequency of the gravitational-wave signal
\omega
.

Since the effective quadratic action of DGP gravity on the brane (
3.2
) is a non-local theory, we generalized Noether’s method for constructing the stress-energy tensor to the case of such non-local theories. As a result, it was shown that the effective stress-energy tensor of DGP gravity on the brane (
3.38
) can be calculated from the effective non-local action (
3.2
) using the standard formula for the canonical stress-energy tensor
[
81
,
82
,
75
]
, neglecting non-local mass terms in the action. The proposed method for constructing the effective energy-momentum tensor was additionally verified within the scalar-field analogue of the DGP model by calculating the brane-localized part of its full five-dimensional Hamiltonian (
3.22
). Based on the proposed procedure, the effective energy-momentum tensor of the dynamical degrees of freedom of DGP gravity (
4.5
) was constructed, determining the effective energy flux of gravitational radiation on the brane.

The problem of gravitational radiation in the DGP model was recently discussed in
[
51
]
using the effective field theory approach to radiation problems
[
52
,
53
]
. However, this analysis, based on momentum space calculations, does not provide information about the structure of the gravitational field in the wave zone and the role of the metastable nature of the effective graviton on the brane in the formation and leakage of gravitational radiation into the extra dimension. Our approach is aimed precisely at these features of gravitational radiation in the DGP model and obtaining explicit predictions for future GW experiments. In particular, we tested the constraints on the intensity of gravitational wave leakage into the extra dimension and the possibility of its detection by modern and future gravitational wave observatories obtained in
[
39
]
within the framework of the scalar-field analogue of the DGP model.

Appendix A
Gravitational radiation of oscillating mass

In GR, the linear equation of motion of the gravitational field in the Lorentz gauge on the Minkowski space background has the form
[
79
]

{{}_{4}}\square\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=-16\pi G{T_{\rm m}}_{\mu\nu},\quad\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}h,\quad\partial^{\mu}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=0,

(A.1)

while the Isaacson EMT (
4.31
) is written in terms of the reduced metric perturbations as

\bar{h}=-h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{32\pi G}\left\langle\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}\bar{h}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}\partial_{\nu}\bar{h}\right\rangle.

(A.2)

The gravitational field of the particle of mass
m
moving along a fixed worldline
z^{\mu}(\tau)
parameterized by its proper time is found as (see, e.g.,
[
63
]
)

{T_{\rm m}}_{\mu\nu}=m\int d\tau\,v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\,\delta^{(4)}(x-z),\quad v^{\mu}=\frac{dz^{\mu}}{d\tau}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{4Gm}{\hat{\rho}}\hat{v}_{\mu}\hat{v}_{\nu},\quad\hat{\rho}=\hat{v}^{\alpha}(x_{\alpha}-\hat{z}_{\alpha}),

(A.3)

where the retarded proper time
\hat{\tau}
is defined as the solution of the equation

(x_{\alpha}-\hat{z}_{\alpha})^{2}=0,\quad x^{0}\geq\hat{z}^{0},

(A.4)

and all the hatted quantities correspond to this moment of proper time. Note also that the Lorentz-invariant distance
\hat{\rho}
coincides with the spatial distance from the observation point to the particle in the Lorentz frame comoving with the particle at the time
\hat{\tau}
and is equivalent to it far away from the particle
[
63
]
. Thus, the long-range part of the gravitational field derivative, which determines the gravitational radiation energy flux through the distant 2-surface enclosing the particle is found as
[
63
]

\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{4Gm}{\hat{\rho}}\hat{c}_{\mu}\left[\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{v}_{\beta}+\hat{v}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\beta}+(\hat{a}\hat{c})\hat{v}_{\alpha}\hat{v}_{\beta}\right],\quad a^{\mu}=\frac{d^{2}z^{\mu}}{d\tau^{2}},\quad x^{\mu}-\hat{z}^{\mu}=\hat{\rho}\hat{c}^{\mu},\quad\hat{c}_{\mu}^{2}=0.

(A.5)

In the non-relativistic limit
|\mathbf{v}|\ll 1
, at the distance from the particle’s region of motion
|\mathbf{x}|\gg|\mathbf{z}|
the components of the emitted part of the gravitational field are written as

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}_{00}=-\frac{4Gm}{r}c_{\mu}n_{k}\!\left.a_{k}\right|_{t_{\rm r}},\quad c^{\mu}=\left\{1,n^{i}\right\},\quad n^{i}=x^{i}/r,\quad t_{\rm r}=t-r,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}_{0i}=\frac{4Gm}{r}c_{\mu}\!\left.a_{i}\right|_{t_{\rm r}},\quad\partial_{\mu}\bar{h}_{ij}=-\frac{4Gm}{r}c_{\mu}\!\left.\left(\bar{a}_{i}\bar{v}_{j}+\bar{v}_{i}\bar{a}_{j}\right)\right|_{t_{\rm r}},

(A.7)

Note that in the non-relativistic limit the different components of the gravitational field have different orders of smallness.

Substituting the expansions (
A.6
–
A.7
) into the Eq. (
A.2
), in the non-relativistic limit we find the following expression for the gravitational radiation energy flux of the particle

\frac{dW}{d\Omega_{2}}=t^{0i}n^{i}r^{2}=\frac{Gm^{2}}{2\pi}\!\left.\left\langle\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{n}\bar{\mathbf{a}})^{2}-2\bar{\mathbf{a}}^{2}\right\rangle\right|_{t_{\rm r}}.

(A.8)

Note that we have obtained the contribution of a lower order of smallness compared to the quadrupole formula
[
79
]
. This is due to the Lorentz gauge preserving the ndDOF of gravitational field, which provide this contribution into the gravitational radiation energy flux. In particular, the gravitational radiation power of an oscillating point mass
\mathbf{z}(t)=\left\{0,0,R_{0}\cos\omega_{0}t\right\}
, calculated by use of the obtained
incorrect
formula (
A.8
), turns out to be negative

W=-\frac{11}{6}Gm^{2}R_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}^{4}<0,

(A.9)

due to the contribution of the ndDOF of gravitational field. Thus, to correctly calculate the gravitational radiation without fixing the transverse-traceless (unitary) gauge, one has to separately construct the EMT of the dDOF of gravitational field.

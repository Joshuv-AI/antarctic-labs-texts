A new approach to the operator formalism for Gromov-Witten invariants of the cap and tube
Ajith Urundolil Kumaran, Longting Wu

Abstract
. Based on Johnson’s operator formula for the equivariant Gromov-Witten theory of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} -orbifolds, we give a new approach to the operator formalism by Okounkov and Pandharipande regarding the ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -equivariant Gromov-Witten theory of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} relative to one or two points. We also extend their operator formalism in the non-equivariant specialization to the case where we allow negative contact orders.

Let

Under the guidance of this new perspective, the relative GW-theory has made great progress. For example, in

1.2.
Basic setup

1.2.1.
The Chen-Ruan cohomology of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}

First, we have an obvious
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
such that
0
and
\infty
are fixed points. It naturally induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the inertia stack
I\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
. The fixed locus of the corresponding coarse moduli
\underline{I}\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
consist of
r
copies of
0
and
s
copies of
\infty
.

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Chen-Ruan orbifold cohomology
H^{*}_{CR,\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\mathbb{C})
plays an important role in the definition of the equivariant orbifold invariants of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
. As a vector space, it is equal to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant cohomology
H^{*}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\underline{I}\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\mathbb{C})
. But
H^{*}_{CR,\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\mathbb{C})
has a shifted grading and deformed cup product which are not relevant in this paper. We recommend the readers to see
[
2
]
for an introduction to Chen-Ruan cohomology.

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant cohomology of a point
H^{*}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(pt,\mathbb{C})
is equal to
\mathbb{C}[t]
where
t
is the first Chern class of the standard representation of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. So
H^{*}_{CR,\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\mathbb{C})
is canonically a
\mathbb{C}[t]
-module. The Atiyah-Bott localization formula tells us that after localizing the appropriate element of
\mathbb{C}[t]
,
H^{*}_{CR,\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s})
has a basis given by the classes
\boldsymbol{0}_{i/r}
,
\boldsymbol{\infty}_{j/s}
(
0\leq i\leq r-1
,
0\leq j\leq s-1
) where
\boldsymbol{0}_{i/r}
is the Poincaré dual of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point
0
in the component of
\underline{I}\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
with age
\frac{i}{r}
, and
\boldsymbol{\infty}_{j/s}
is the Poincaré dual of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed point
\infty
in the component of
\underline{I}\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
with age
\frac{j}{s}
.

1.2.2.
Orbifold GW-invariants of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},d)
be the moduli space of stable maps to
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
with genus
g,m
markings and degree
d
. The natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},d)
. The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant orbifold GW-invariant can be defined via an integration over the equivariant virtual cycle of the moduli space:

(1)

\left\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\dots\tau_{k_{m}}(\gamma_{m})\right\rangle_{g,d}^{\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\,\mathbb{C}^{*}}\coloneqq\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},d)]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bar{\psi}_{i}^{k_{i}}ev_{i}^{*}(\gamma_{i})

where
ev_{i}
denotes the evaluation map of the
i
-th marking which allows us to pull back the equivariant Chen-Ruan cohomology classes
\gamma_{i}\in H^{*}_{CR,\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s})
,
\bar{\psi}_{i}
are psi-classes pulled back from the corresponding psi-classes of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)
via the morphism
f:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathcal{C}_{r,s},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)
which forgets the orbifold structure. We add a bullet to denote the corresponding disconnected invariant, that is,
\langle\dots\rangle^{\bullet}
.

Remark 1.1
.

We notice that the above notation (
1
) does not specify the age information of each marking. Such age information can be specified via the choosing of
\gamma_{i}
. For example, if we choose
\gamma_{i}
to be
\boldsymbol{0}_{\mu_{i}/r}
(
\mu_{i}>0
), then we know that the
i
-th marking is mapped to
0
with age
\frac{\mu_{i}}{r}
.

1.2.3.
\tau
function

The
\tau
function encodes all the disconnected GW-invariants of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
. It is defined as

(2)

\tau=\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{d\geq 0}u^{2g-2}q^{d}\left\langle\exp\left(\sum_{k,i}x_{k}(i)\tau_{k}(\boldsymbol{0}_{i/r})+\sum_{k,j}x_{k}^{*}(j)\tau_{k}(\boldsymbol{\infty}_{j/s})\right)\right\rangle_{g,d}^{\bullet\,\mathcal{C}_{r,s},\,\mathbb{C}^{*}}.

Here the formal variables
\{x_{k}(i)\}
,
\{x_{k}^{*}(j)\}
are introduced so as to keep track of insertions
\tau_{k}(\boldsymbol{0}_{i/r})
,
\tau_{k}(\boldsymbol{\infty}_{j/s})
respectively.

1.2.4.
Johnson’s operator formula

Using the virtual localization and orbifold ELSV formula, Johnson expressed the
\tau
function as a vacuum expectation on the infinite wedge space:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
,
13
]
)
.

(3)

\tau=\left\langle e^{\sum_{k,i}x_{k}(i)\boldsymbol{A}_{i/r}[k]}e^{\frac{t\alpha_{r}}{ur}}\left(\frac{q}{t^{1/r}(-t)^{1/s}}\right)^{H}e^{\frac{-t\alpha_{-s}}{us}}e^{\sum_{k,j}x^{*}_{k}(j)\boldsymbol{A}^{*}_{j/s}[k]}\right\rangle

An explanation of the infinite wedge space and operators appearing in the above formula will be given in Section
3
.

1.2.5.
GW-invariants of the cap and tube

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}/0,d,\vec{\mu}_{0})
be the moduli space which parametrizes relative stable maps from genus
g,n
-pointed curves to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d
with monodromy at
0
given by an ordered partition
\vec{\mu}_{0}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{\rho})
of
d
. The GW-invariants of the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)
can be defined as integrals against the virtual cycle as before:

\left\langle\vec{\mu}_{0}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\right.\right\rangle\coloneqq\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}/0,d,\vec{\mu}_{0})]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}ev_{i}^{*}(\gamma_{i})

where
\gamma_{i}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. As before, we add a bullet or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
to denote the corresponding disconnected or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant invariant. Here we omit the degree
d
and genus
g
in the bracket notation, since
d
can be determined from
\vec{\mu}_{0}
and
g
can be determined from the dimension constraint.

For the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)
, we write the monodromy data on the right side of the bracket:

\left\langle\left.\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\right|\vec{\mu}_{\infty}\right\rangle.

As for the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0\cup\infty)
, we write the monodromy data of
0
on the left and write that of
\infty
on the right:

\left\langle\vec{\mu}_{0}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\right|\vec{\mu}_{\infty}\right\rangle.

According to
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
22
]
, we can redefine GW-invariants of the cap and tube as certain limits of orbifold GW-invariants of
\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
(see Section
2
). A little more precisely, by replacing each multiplicity
\mu_{i}\in\vec{\mu}_{0}
(resp.
\mu^{\prime}_{i}\in\vec{\mu}_{\infty}
) with an insertion
0_{\mu_{i}/r}
(resp.
\infty_{\mu^{\prime}_{i/s}}
) which stands for the identity element of the twisted sector over
0
(resp.
\infty
) with age
\mu_{i}/r
(resp.
\mu^{\prime}_{i}/s

\mathcal{C}_{r,s}
. For example, the GW-invariant of the tube above corresponds to an orbifold invariant

\left\langle\prod_{i}\tau_{0}(0_{\mu_{i}/r})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\prod_{i}\tau_{0}(\infty_{\mu^{\prime}_{i}/s})\right\rangle^{\mathcal{C}_{r,s}}_{g,d}.

Here
\gamma_{i}
should be seen as cohomology classes in the untwisted sector. For the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)
(resp.
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)

\mathcal{C}_{r,s=1}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{r=1,s}
).

For sufficiently large

r,s
and the constant term matches with the relative GW-invariant we defined above. Note that for the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)
(resp.
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)
), the polynomial will only depend on
r
(resp.
s
).

In
[
6
,
7
]
, relative GW-invariants were generalized to include negative contact using the orbifold perspective. Specializing to the previous cap or tube case, negative contact
\mu_{i}\in\vec{\mu}_{0}
(resp.
\mu^{\prime}_{i}\in\vec{\mu}_{\infty}

0_{\frac{r+\mu_{i}}{r}}
(resp.
\infty_{\frac{s+\mu_{i}^{\prime}}{s}}

1.3.
Main results

The orbifold perspective gives us a new approach to the operator formalism for GW-invariants of the cap and tube. For technical reasons, we only consider the following two cases.

(I) In the first case, we consider equivariant invariants without negative contact.
We always treat the cap case carefully since the tube case follows by a similar analysis. So we want to compute the following equivariant GW-invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)

\left\langle\vec{\mu}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\boldsymbol{0})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{l_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\infty})\right.\right\rangle^{\bullet,\mathbb{C}^{*}}

G(\vec{\mu}|z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},w_{1},\cdots,w_{m}).

The orbifold perspective leads us to consider the following orbifold invariants:

(4)

\left\langle\prod_{i}\tau_{0}(\boldsymbol{0}_{\mu_{i}/r})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\boldsymbol{0}_{0/r})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{l_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\infty}_{0/1})\right\rangle^{\bullet,\mathcal{C}_{r,s=1},\mathbb{C}^{*}}.

2.
Relative GW-Theory vs. Orbifold GW-theory

2.1.
General Theory

In this section we recall the basic notions of relative GW-theory with possibly negative contact orders. The content and notation in this section is largely based on
[
6
,
7
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A topological type
\Gamma
is a tuple
(g,n,\beta,\rho,\vec{\mu})
where
g,n
are non-negative integers,
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is a curve class and
\vec{\mu}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{*})^{\rho}
is a partition (we allow negative integers) of the intersection number
\int_{\beta}D
.

Let
X_{D,r}
be the
r
-th root stack along the divisor
D
. We use
\underline{I}(X_{D,r})
to denote the coarse moduli space of the inertia stack of
X_{D,r}
. It has the following form:

(6)

\underline{I}(X_{D,r})\cong X\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r-1}D.

We label the components of this inertia stack by
\frac{i}{r}
for
i=0,...,r-1
. These fractional integers are called ages. The age
\frac{0}{r}
is reserved for the component isomorphic to
X
. The other components are called twisted sectors. A topological type
\Gamma=(g,n,\beta,\rho,\vec{\mu})
determines a moduli space of stable maps into
X_{D,r}
in the following way:

•

g,\beta
correspond to genus and curve class.

•

n
is the number of markings without orbifold structure.

•

\rho
is the number of markings with orbifold structure.

•

When
\mu_{i}>0
, the evaluation map of the corresponding marking lands on the twisted sector with age
\frac{\mu_{i}}{r}
.

•

When
\mu_{i}<0
, the evaluation map of the corresponding marking lands on the twisted sector with age
\frac{r+\mu_{i}}{r}
.

Note that for this to make sense we need
r>\max_{1\leq i\leq\rho}\lvert\mu_{i}\rvert
. We denote this moduli space by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}(X_{D,r})
. The domain curves of these stable maps are connected. If we also allow disconnected domain curves we will write
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bullet}_{\Gamma}(X_{D,r})

3.
Orbifold GW-invariants via the infinite wedge

In this section, we want to give a brief introduction of Johnson’s operator formula in the half-infinity wedge space for the

The half-infinity wedge space is deeply related with the representation theory of the symmetric group. In his PhD thesis

As these numbers are naturally connected with the character theory of the symmetric group, Johnson was able to express the equivariant orbifold potential as an operator expectation on the half-infinity wedge space. We now give a short introduction to the half-infinity wedge space. This section is based on

Let

\mathcal{J}_{1},...,\mathcal{J}_{t}
be the connected components of
\mathcal{J}
ordered by the smallest vertex each component contains. This induces a partition of
\{1,...,n\}
. We call this partition
P
the combinatorial type of
\mathcal{J}
.

Let
P=P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{t}
be such a partition. It naturally induces a partition of the operators
(\mathcal{O}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{n})
. We use
\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}
to denote the part corresponding to
P_{i}
. We have the following important lemma about the set
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{n})
.

Lemma A.16
.

Let
\mathcal{J}_{1},...,\mathcal{J}_{t}
be the connected components of an interaction diagram
\mathcal{J}
. Then

(55)

\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{n})\iff\mathcal{J}_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}})\quad\forall i.

Proof.

Let us first show
"\Rightarrow"
: By Lemma
A.11
, we only need to show that each
\mathcal{J}_{i}
is valid. Since
\mathcal{J}_{i}
is connected, we know that at the end of the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
, there is only one vertex. And this vertex corresponds to an operator with
r
-energy 0 since
\mathcal{J}
is valid. We now show each step in the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
is valid. If we go through every step of the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J})
and we ignore every step which does not use a vertex from
\mathcal{J}_{i}
we exactly isolate the steps of
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
in the right order. So we assume that we are at an arbitrary step in the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
. As a valid step in
L(\mathcal{J})
, we know that it is a step in
L(\mathcal{J})
that satisfies (b) of Definition
A.8
. So it must also be a step in
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
satisfying (b). By Remark
A.9
, this arbitrary step is a valid step for
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
.

Next, we show
"\Leftarrow"
: Because each connected component
\mathcal{J}_{i}
of
\mathcal{J}
is valid, we know that at the end of the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J})
, each vertex must correspond to an operator with zero
r
-energy. We are left to show each step in the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J})
is valid. Let us assume we are at an arbitrary step in the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J})
. Let us call our interaction diagram at this step
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
. The connected components
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,...,t
are the connected components of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
ordered by the smallest vertex each component contains. Let
v
be the smallest vertex of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
which has exactly one outgoing edge and no incoming edges. Let
v_{i}
be the smallest vertex with the same property for
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,...,t
. It is clear that we have:

(56)

v=\min\{v_{1},...,v_{t}\}.

We know that each
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{i}
is valid for the appropriate operators for
i=1,...,t
. This means the first step of the algorithm of
L(\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{i})
satisfies (b) of Definition
A.8
for
i=1,...,t
. This means that
v_{i}
is the smallest vertex of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{i}
whose corresponding operator has positive
r
-energy for
i=1,...,t
. By Equation (
56
), we know that the first step of
L(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})
also satisfies (b). By Remark
A.9
, we know that each step of
\mathcal{J}
is valid.
∎

Note that we could easily express
L(\mathcal{J})
in terms of those connected ones
L(\mathcal{J}_{i})
:

(57)

L(\mathcal{J})=L(\mathcal{J}_{1})\cdot...\cdot L(\mathcal{J}_{t}).

We are ready to prove Lemma
4.2
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

By the analysis in the beginning of this Appendix, we know that for sufficiently large
r
,

(58)

\langle L_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{A}_{a_{i}}(z_{i},u,t)L_{2}\rangle=\sum_{\sum m_{i}=0}\langle L_{1}F_{n}(R_{m_{1},a_{1}},\cdots,R_{m_{n},a_{n}})L_{2}\rangle.

So we only need to analysis the operator

\sum_{\sum m_{i}=0}F_{n}(R_{m_{1},a_{1}},\cdots,R_{m_{n},a_{n}})

By (
54
), it equals to

(59)

\sum_{\sum m_{i}=0}\;\sum_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{K}(R_{m_{1},a_{1}},\cdots,R_{m_{n},a_{n}})}L(\mathcal{J}).

Each interaction diagram
\mathcal{J}
with a fixed combinatorial type
P=P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{l(P)}
can be decomposed into connected components
\mathcal{J}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{J}_{l(P)}
. It naturally induces two partitions:

\vec{m}_{P_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\vec{m}_{P_{l(P)}}=\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{n}\},\quad\vec{a}_{P_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\vec{a}_{P_{l(P)}}=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}

and a partition of operators

R_{\vec{m}_{P_{1}},\vec{a}_{P_{1}}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{\vec{m}_{P_{l(P)}},\vec{a}_{P_{l(P)}}}=\{R_{m_{1},a_{1}},\cdots,R_{m_{n},a_{n}}\}.

Then we may further write (
59
) as

\sum_{\sum m_{i}=0}\;\sum_{P=P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{l(P)}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(R_{\vec{m}_{P_{i}},\vec{a}_{P_{i}}})\atop\mathcal{J}_{i}\,\mathrm{connected}}L(\mathcal{J}_{1})\cdots L(\mathcal{J}_{l(P)}).

Here we have used the equation (
57
) and Lemma
A.16
. By exchanging the summation order, it can be further written as

\sum_{P=P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{l(P)}}\prod_{i=1}^{l(P)}\left(\sum_{|\vec{m}_{P_{i}}|=0}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(R_{\vec{m}_{P_{i}},\vec{a}_{P_{i}}})\atop\mathcal{J}_{i}\,\mathrm{connected}}L(\mathcal{J}_{i})\right).

Each factor in the product equals to

\sum_{|\vec{m}_{P_{i}}|=0}G_{l(P_{i})}(R_{\vec{m}_{P_{i}},\vec{a}_{P_{i}}})

by Definition
A.12
. By Remark
A.14
, it can be written in the following form:

f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}(z_{P_{i}},u,t,r)\mathcal{E}_{|\vec{a}_{P_{i}}|}(u|z_{P_{i}}|)

where
f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}(z_{P_{i}},u,t,r)
is a function indexed by the ordered set
\vec{a}_{P_{i}}
.

From the above discussion, we may conclude that

\langle L_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{A}_{\vec{a}}L_{2}\rangle=\sum_{P}\left\langle L_{1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l(P)}f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}(z_{P_{i}},u,t,r)\mathcal{E}_{|\vec{a}_{P_{i}}|}(u|z_{P_{i}}|)\right)L_{2}\right\rangle

for sufficiently large
r
and these functions
f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}
can be determined by the equation

(60)

f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}(z_{P_{i}},u,t,r)\mathcal{E}_{|\vec{a}_{P_{i}}|}(u|z_{P_{i}}|)=\sum_{|\vec{m}_{P_{i}}|=0}G_{l(P_{i})}(R_{\vec{m}_{P_{i}},\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}).

Obviously, these functions
f_{\vec{a}_{P_{i}}}
do not depend on the operators
L_{1},L_{2}
but only on the ordered set
\vec{a}_{P_{i}}
.

∎

Appendix B
A computation using WDVV equation

In this appendix, we will use the WDVV equation in
[
6
, Proposition 7.5]
to calculate relative invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0\cup\infty)
of a certain type. Such invariants will be used in the computation of Section
4
.

To state the WDVV equation in our case, we need some preparation. First, we choose the basis
\{1,H\}
for
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
where
H
is the hyperplane class. As for
0\cup\infty
, we use
\boldsymbol{0}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\infty}
) to denote the identity class of
H^{*}(0)
(resp.
H^{*}(\infty)
). Let
\mathfrak{H}_{0}=H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
and
\mathfrak{H}_{i}=H^{*}(0\cup\infty)=H^{*}(0)\oplus H^{*}(\infty)

for
i\neq 0
. The space of primary insertions for relative invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0\cup\infty)
is

\mathfrak{H}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{H}_{i}.

For an element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{H}_{i}
, we denote by
[\alpha]_{i}
the image in
\mathfrak{H}
via the obvious embedding. There is a natural basis for
\mathfrak{H}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{0,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[1]_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{0,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[H]_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{i,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\boldsymbol{0}]_{i},\;i\neq 0,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{i,\infty}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{i},\;i\neq 0

and a natural pairing:

([\alpha]_{i},[\beta]_{j})=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }i+j\neq 0,\\
\int_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\alpha\cup\beta,\text{ if }i=j=0,\\
\int_{0\cup\infty}\alpha\cup\beta,\text{ if }i+j=0,\;i,j\neq 0.\end{cases}

Let
\{\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee}\}
be the dual basis of
\{\tilde{T}_{i,k}\}
under the above pairing. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{0,0}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[H]_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{0,1}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[1]_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{i,0}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\boldsymbol{0}]_{-i},\;i\neq 0,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{i,\infty}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-i},\;i\neq 0.

Let
[\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},\cdots,[\alpha_{n}]_{i_{n}}\in\{\tilde{T}_{i,k}\}
. We define

(61)

I_{d}([\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},[\alpha_{2}]_{i_{2}},\cdots,[\alpha_{n}]_{i_{n}})

to be the following genus zero relative invariants

(62)

\left\langle\vec{\mu}_{0}\left|\prod_{s\in S}\tau_{0}(\alpha_{s})\right|\vec{\mu}_{\infty}\right\rangle_{g=0,d}

where the contact orders and the set of inner markings can be naturally read from (
61
):

\vec{\mu}_{0}=\{i_{s}|\alpha_{s}=\boldsymbol{0}\},\quad\vec{\mu}_{\infty}=\{i_{s}|\alpha_{s}=\boldsymbol{\infty}\},\quad S=\{s|i_{s}=0\}.

Note that
the express (
62
) makes sense only if
\vec{\mu}_{0}
and
\vec{\mu}_{\infty}
are two partitions of
d
. So if this is not the case, we simply set
I_{d}([\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},[\alpha_{2}]_{i_{2}},\cdots,[\alpha_{n}]_{i_{n}})=0
. Another constraint comes from the dimension constraint of (
62
). So if the degree summation of all the insertions in (
62
) does not match with the virtual dimension of the corresponding genus zero relative moduli space, then we also have
I_{d}([\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},[\alpha_{2}]_{i_{2}},\cdots,[\alpha_{n}]_{i_{n}})=0
.

We are ready to state the WDVV equation.

Proposition B.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

\displaystyle\sum I_{d_{1}}([\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},[\alpha_{2}]_{i_{2}},\prod_{j\in S_{1}}[\alpha_{j}]_{i_{j}},\tilde{T}_{i,k})I_{d_{2}}(\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee},[\alpha_{3}]_{i_{3}},[\alpha_{4}]_{i_{4}},\prod_{j\in S_{2}}[\alpha_{j}]_{i_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum I_{d_{1}}([\alpha_{1}]_{i_{1}},[\alpha_{3}]_{i_{3}},\prod_{j\in S_{1}}[\alpha_{j}]_{i_{j}},\tilde{T}_{i,k})I_{d_{2}}(\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee},[\alpha_{2}]_{i_{2}},[\alpha_{4}]_{i_{4}},\prod_{j\in S_{2}}[\alpha_{j}]_{i_{j}}),

where each sum takes over all
d_{1}+d_{2}=d
, all indices
i,k
of basis, and
S_{1},S_{2}
disjoint sets with
S_{1}\cup S_{2}=\{5,...,n\}
. Also, the
\prod
symbol makes each factor as a separate insertion, instead of multiplying them up.

Next, using the above WDVV equation, we can compute relative invariants in the following form:

(63)

\left\langle\vec{\mu}_{0}|\emptyset|\vec{\mu}_{\infty}\right\rangle

where
\vec{\mu}_{0}=(a,b_{1},...,b_{n})
and
\vec{\mu}_{\infty}=(d)
are two partitions of
d>0
such that
b_{1},...,b_{n}
are negative. We can rewrite (
63
) as

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{n}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d}).

We have the following explicit formula.

Proposition B.2
.

(64)

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{n}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d})=d^{n-1}.

Proof.

We will prove it by induction on
n
. If
n=0
, then
a=d
. In this case, the corresponding genus zero relative moduli space has only one point with automorphism group
\mu_{d}
. So

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{d},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d})=d^{-1}.

Let us assume that (
64
) holds for all
n\leq m
. We need to show that (
64
) also holds for
n=m+1
. We will
use the WDVV equation of Proposition
B.1
with the following
m+4
insertions:

[\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d},[H]_{0},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{k+1}},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{2}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{m}}.

In this case, the WDVV equation becomes

\displaystyle\sum I_{d_{1}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d},\prod_{j\in S_{1}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}},\tilde{T}_{i,k})I_{d_{2}}(\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee},[H]_{0},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{m+1}},\prod_{j\in S_{2}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum I_{d_{1}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[H]_{0},\prod_{j\in S_{1}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}},\tilde{T}_{i,k})I_{d_{2}}(\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{m+1}},\prod_{j\in S_{2}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}})

where each sum takes over all
d_{1}+d_{2}=d-b_{m+1}
, all indices
i,k
of basis, and
S_{1},S_{2}
disjoint sets with
S_{1}\cup S_{2}=\{1,...,m\}
. Let us first analyse the LHS of the equation.

If
S_{2}
is not empty, then in order to make the total sum of contact orders over
0
in
I_{d_{2}}
non-negative,
\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee}
must be of the form
[\boldsymbol{0}]_{c}
with
c>0
. Then using the fact that the total sum of contact orders over
0
and
\infty
are the same, we may deduce that
c=-\sum_{j\in S_{2}}b_{j}-b_{m+1}
. So
\tilde{T}_{i,k}=[\boldsymbol{0}]_{-c}
and
d_{1}=d
,
d_{2}=-b_{m+1}
. Then by induction and divisor equation, we have

\displaystyle I_{d_{1}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d},\prod_{j\in S_{1}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{-c})

\displaystyle=d^{|S_{1}|},

\displaystyle I_{d_{2}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{c},[H]_{0},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{m+1}},\prod_{j\in S_{2}}[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{j}})

\displaystyle=(-b_{m+1})^{|S_{2}|}

where
|S_{1}|
and
|S_{2}|
are the numbers of elements in
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. The different ways of choosing the sets
S_{1},S_{2}
give the combinatorial number
m\choose|S_{2}|
. So when
S_{2}
is not empty, the total contribution to the LHS of WDVV equation is

\sum_{|S_{2}|>0}{m\choose|S_{2}|}d^{m-|S_{2}|}(-b_{m+1})^{|S_{2}|}.

Here we have used the fact that
|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|=m
.

If
S_{2}
is empty, we could similarly deduce that
\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee}
must be
[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{b_{m+1}}
or
[\boldsymbol{0}]_{-b_{m+1}}
. If
\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee}=[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{b_{m+1}}
, then
I_{d_{2}}=0
by dimension reason. If
\tilde{T}_{i,k}^{\vee}=[\boldsymbol{0}]_{-b_{m+1}}
, then the corresponding term on the LHS of WDVV equation becomes

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{m+1}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d})I_{d_{m+1}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{-b_{m+1}},[H]_{0},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{m+1}}).

By divisor equation, the second term
I_{d_{m+1}}
in the above product is
1
.
So we may conclude that the total sum on the LHS of WDVV equation is

(65)

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{m+1}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d})+\sum_{|S_{2}|>0}{m\choose|S_{2}|}d^{m-|S_{2}|}(-b_{m+1})^{|S_{2}|}.

A similar analysis to the RHS of WDVV equation also shows that
only one term survives in the total summation:

I_{d-b_{m+1}}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[H]_{0},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{m}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d-b_{m+1}})I_{0}([\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-d+b_{m+1}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{-b_{m+1}}).

The second term in the above product equals to
1
since there is only one negative insertion (see
[
6
, Example 5.5]
). As for the first term, it equals to

(66)

(d-b_{m+1})^{m}

by induction and divisor equation. Now LHS=RHS becomes (
65
)=(
66
). So

I_{d}([\boldsymbol{0}]_{a},[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{1}},\cdots,[\boldsymbol{0}]_{b_{m+1}},[\boldsymbol{\infty}]_{d})=d^{m}.

∎

Abstract
. This is a continuation of a previous study initiated by one of us on nonlocal vertex bialgebras and smash product nonlocal vertex algebras. In this paper, we study a notion of right H H -comodule nonlocal vertex algebra for a nonlocal vertex bialgebra H H and give a construction of deformations of vertex algebras with a right H H -comodule nonlocal vertex algebra structure and a compatible H H -module nonlocal vertex algebra structure. We also give a construction of ϕ \phi -coordinated quasi modules for smash product nonlocal vertex algebras. As an example, we give a family of quantum vertex algebras by deforming the vertex algebras associated to non-degenerate even lattices.

1.
Introduction

Vertex algebras (see
[
B1
]
,
[
FLM
]
) are analogues and
generalizations of commutative associative algebras,
while nonlocal vertex algebras (see
[
BK
]
,
[
Li2
]
; cf.
[
Li4
]
)
are analogues of noncommutative associative algebras.
The (weak) quantum vertex algebras
[
Li4
]
, which are certain variations of quantum vertex operator algebras
in the sense of Etingof-Kazhdan
[
EK
]
, are a special family of nonlocal vertex algebras.
To a certain extent, the notion of nonlocal vertex algebra plays the same role in the general vertex algebraic theory
as the notion of associative algebra does in the classical algebraic theory.
On the other hand, quantum vertex (operator) algebras, which from various viewpoints
are analogues of quantum groups (algebras), form a perfect category.

In
[
Li8
]
, certain vertex-algebra analogues of Hopf algebras were introduced and studied.
Specifically, notions of nonlocal vertex bialgebra and
(left)
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra for a nonlocal vertex bialgebra
H
were introduced, and then
the smash product nonlocal vertex algebra
V\sharp H
of an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
V
with
H
was constructed.
As an application, a new construction of the lattice vertex algebras
and their modules was given.

In vertex algebra theory, among the most important notions are those of modules and
\sigma
-twisted modules
for a vertex algebra
V
with
\sigma
a finite order automorphism.
A notion of quasi module, generalizing that of module, was introduced in
[
Li3
]

in order to associate vertex algebras to a certain family of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras.
Indeed, with this notion vertex algebras can be associated to a wide variety of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras.
(From a certain point of view, the notion of quasi module also generalizes that of twisted module (see
[
Li7
]
).)
Furthermore, in order to associate quantum vertex algebras to algebras such as quantum affine algebras,
a theory of
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for nonlocal vertex algebras (including quantum vertex algebras)
was developed in
[
Li9
,
Li10
]
, where weak quantum vertex algebras were associated to quantum affine algebras
conceptually
.

In the aforementioned notion of
\phi
-coordinated module, the symbol
\phi
refers to
an associate of the
1
-dimensional additive formal group (law),
which by definition is
F(x,y):=x+y
(an element of
{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
) and which satisfies

F(0,x)=x=F(x,0),\quad F(x,F(y,x))=F(F(x,y),z).

This very formal group law (implicitly) plays an important role in the theory of vertex algebras and their modules.
In contrast, an associate of
F(x,y)
is a formal series
\phi(x,z)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]
, satisfying the condition

\phi(x,0)=x,\ \ \ \phi(\phi(x,y),z)=\phi(x,y+z).

It was proved therein that for any
p(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))
, the formal series
\phi(x,z)
defined by

\phi(x,z)=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}x
is an associate and every associate is of this form.
The essence of
[
Li9
]
is that a theory of
\phi
-coordinated modules
for a general nonlocal vertex algebra is attached to each associate
\phi(x,z)
,
where the usual theory of modules becomes a special case with
\phi

taken to be the formal group law
itself.

The importance lies in the fact that a wide variety of (quantum associative) algebras
can be associated to
\phi
-coordinated (quasi) modules
for some (weak) quantum vertex algebras by choosing a suitable associate
\phi
.

What is a
\phi
-coordinated quasi module?
Let
\phi(x,z)
be an associate of
F(x,y)
.
Note that in the definition of modules for an associative algebra and for a (nonlocal) vertex algebra,
the key ingredient is associativity. For a general nonlocal vertex algebra
V
,
the main defining property of a
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module
W
with vertex operator map
Y_{W}(\cdot,x)

(the representation morphism) is that for any
u,v\in V
, there exists a nonzero polynomial
q(x_{1},x_{2})
such that

q(x_{1},x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})\in\textnormal{{Hom}}(W,W((x_{1},x_{2}))),

\left(q(x_{1},x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})\right)|_{x_{1}=\phi(x_{2},z)}=q(\phi(x_{2},z),x_{2})Y_{W}(Y(u,z)v,x_{2}).

The usual modules including the adjoint module are simply
\phi
-coordinated (quasi) modules with
\phi(x,z)=F(x,z)=x+z
.
In practice, for some algebras, their modules of highest weight type cannot be associated with vertex algebras
in terms of usual modules, but can be viewed as
\phi
-coordinated (quasi) modules for some vertex algebras or
quantum vertex algebras with some
\phi
.

In this paper, we develop the theory of smash product nonlocal vertex algebras further in several directions,
with an ultimate goal to construct certain desired quantum vertex (operator) algebras.
Among the main results, we first introduce a notion
of right
H
-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra for a nonlocal vertex bialgebra
H

and then using a compatible right
H
-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra
structure on an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
V
, we construct a deformed nonlocal vertex algebra structure on
V
.
We show that under certain conditions, the deformed nonlocal vertex algebras are quantum vertex algebras.
In another direction (representation aspect),
we construct
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for smash product nonlocal vertex algebras and
for the aforementioned deformed nonlocal vertex algebras.
We also apply the general results to the vertex algebras associated to non-degenerate even lattices,
to obtain a family of quantum vertex algebras.

Now, we give a more detailed description of the contents.
Recall that a nonlocal vertex bialgebra is simply a nonlocal vertex algebra
V

equipped with a classical coalgebra structure on
V
such that the comultiplication
\Delta
and the counit
\epsilon
are
homomorphisms of nonlocal vertex algebras. For a nonlocal vertex bialgebra
H
, an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
is a nonlocal vertex algebra
V
equipped with a module structure
Y_{V}^{H}(\cdot,x)

for
H
viewed as a nonlocal vertex algebra such that

\displaystyle Y_{V}^{H}(h,x)v\in V\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((x)),

\displaystyle Y_{V}^{H}(h,x){\bf 1}_{V}=\epsilon(h){\bf 1}_{V},

\displaystyle Y_{V}^{H}(h,x)Y(u,z)v=\sum Y\left(Y_{V}^{H}(h_{(1)},x-z)u,z\right)Y_{V}^{H}(h_{(2)},x)v

for
h\in H,\ u,v\in V
. (The first condition is technical, while the other two are analogues of the classical counterparts.)
Given an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
V
, we have a
smash product nonlocal vertex algebra
V\sharp H
, where
V\sharp H=V\otimes H
as a vector space and

Y^{\sharp}(u\otimes h,x)(v\otimes k)=\sum Y(u,x)Y_{V}^{H}(h_{(1)},x)v\otimes Y(h_{(2)},x)k

for
u,v\in V,\ h,k\in H
.

Let
H
be a nonlocal vertex bialgebra. A
right
H
-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra
is defined to be a nonlocal vertex algebra
V

equipped with a comodue structure
\rho:V\rightarrow V\otimes H
for
H
viewed as a coalgebra such that

\rho
is a homomorphism of nonlocal vertex algebras. Let
V
be an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra.
We say a right
H
-comodule structure
\rho:V\rightarrow V\otimes H
is
compatible
with the (left)
H
-module structure
if
\rho
is an
H
-module homomorphism with
H
acting only on the first factor of
V\otimes H
.
Assuming that
\rho:V\rightarrow V\otimes H
is a compatible right
H
-comodule structure on an

H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
V
, we construct a new nonlocal vertex algebra
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y_{V}^{H}}^{\rho}(V)

with
V
as the underlying space, where the vertex operator map, denoted here by
Y_{new}(\cdot,x)
, is given by

Y_{new}(u,x)v=\sum Y(u_{(1)},x)Y_{V}^{H}(u_{(2)},x)v

for
u,v\in V
, where
\rho(u)=\sum u_{(1)}\otimes u_{(2)}\in V\otimes H
in the Sweedler notation.
(Recall that
Y_{V}^{H}(\cdot,x)
denotes the vertex operator map for
H
on the module
V
.)
Furthermore, we show that
{\mathcal{D}}_{Y_{V}^{H}}^{\rho}(V)
is a quantum vertex algebra,
assuming that
H
is cocommutative,
V
is a vertex algebra, and
Y_{V}^{H}(\cdot,x)
is invertible
with respect to convolution (plus some technical condition).

In the theory of vertex algebras, an important family consists of
the vertex algebras
V_{L}
associated to non-degenerate even lattices
L
,
which are rooted in the vertex operator realization of affine Kac-Moody algebras.
These structurally simple vertex algebras
are often used to construct or study more complicated vertex operator algebras (see
[
FLM
]
for example).
Recall that
V_{L}=S(\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]
as a vector space,
where
\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-}={\mathfrak{h}}\otimes t^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[t^{-1}]
with
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}L
is an abelian Lie algebra and

{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]
is the
\varepsilon
-twisted group algebra of
L
with
\varepsilon
a certain particular
2
-cocycle of
L
.
Set
B_{L}=S(\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[L]
equipped with the tensor product bialgebra structure, where
{\mathbb{C}}[L]
is the ordinary group algebra.
Use a natural derivation on
B_{L}
and the Borcherds construction to make
B_{L}
a commutative vertex algebra.
In fact,
B_{L}
is a vertex bialgebra, which was exploited in
[
Li8
]
.

In this paper, we explore the vertex bialgebra
B_{L}
furthermore. We prove that
there exists a natural right
B_{L}
-comodule vertex algebra structure
\rho:V_{L}\rightarrow V_{L}\otimes B_{L}

on the vertex algebra
V_{L}
. In fact, with
V_{L}
identified with
B_{L}
as a vector space,
\rho
is simply the comultiplication

\Delta:B_{L}\otimes B_{L}\rightarrow B_{L}
. (It was proved in
[
Li8
]
that with canonical identifications,

\Delta
is a vertex algebra embedding of

into
B_{L,\varepsilon}\sharp B_{L}
.)
On the other hand, for any linear map
\eta(\cdot,x):{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes x{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
,
we give a compatible
B_{L}
-module vertex algebra structure

on
V_{L}
. Consequently, we obtain a family of quantum vertex algebras

(with
V_{L}
as the underlying vector space).

In a sequel, we shall use
\hbar
-adic versions of the results obtained in this paper
to construct certain quantum vertex operator algebras (over
{\mathbb{C}}[[\hbar]]
)
in the sense of Etingof-Kazhdan by deforming vertex algebras
V_{L}
, where

\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for these quantum vertex operator algebras
are associated to highest weight modules for twisted quantum affine algebras.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we first recall the basic notions and results about

For a ring

Definition 2.12

The notion of homomorphism of nonlocal vertex bialgebras is defined in the obvious way:
For nonlocal vertex bialgebras (

a nonlocal vertex bialgebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes f)\circ\Delta,\ \ \quad\varepsilon^{\prime}\circ f=\varepsilon.

In other words, a nonlocal vertex bialgebra homomorphism is both
a nonlocal vertex algebra homomorphism and a coalgebra homomorphism.

Definition 2.13

(2.18)

\displaystyle Y_{V}^{H}(h,x_{1})Y(u,x_{2})v=\sum Y(Y_{V}^{H}(h_{(1)},x_{1}-x_{2})u,x_{2})Y_{V}^{H}(h_{(2)},x_{1})v

Notice that for

\displaystyle\sum u_{(1),(1)}\otimes u_{(2)}\otimes u_{(1),(2)}=\sum u_{(1)}\otimes u_{(2),(1)}\otimes u_{(2),(2)}.

Second, we show that (

3.

Throughout this section, we assume that

By coassociativity we have

\displaystyle P_{23}(\Delta\otimes\Delta)\Delta=(\Delta\otimes\Delta)\Delta.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra.
Denote by

-module nonlocal vertex algebra

Example 3.2

Let
(V,\rho)
be an
H
-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra. Define
a linear map

\displaystyle Y_{M}^{\varepsilon}(h,x)v=\varepsilon(h)v\ \ \ \mbox{ for }h\in H,\ v\in V.

From definition,

Proposition 3.3

As the main result of this section, we have:

Theorem 3.6

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(u,x)v=\ e^{x{\mathcal{D}}}\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(-x){\mathcal{S}}(-x)(v\otimes u).

It then follows from

{\mathcal{S}}^{21}(x)=R_{12}{\mathcal{S}}(x)R_{12}

\displaystyle\sum u_{(1),(2)}\otimes u_{(2)}\otimes u_{(1),(1)}=\sum u_{(2)(1)}\otimes u_{(2),(2)}\otimes u_{(1)},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{21}(-x){\mathcal{S}}(x)(v\otimes u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{\mathcal{S}}^{21}(-x)\left(Y_{M}(u_{(2)},-x)v_{(1)}\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},x)u_{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}\left(\left(Y_{M}(u_{(2)},-x)v_{(1)}\right)_{(2)},x\right)\left(Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},x)u_{(1)}\right)_{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varepsilon(u_{(2)})v_{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(v_{(2)})u_{(1)}=\sum\varepsilon(v_{(2)})v_{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(u_{(2)})u_{(1)}=v\otimes u.

is unitary.

Furthermore, for

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(x)\left(\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(u,z)v\otimes w\right)

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(z)\otimes 1\right){\mathcal{S}}^{23}(x){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z)(u\otimes v\otimes w)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{12}(x){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z){\mathcal{S}}^{23}(z)(u\otimes v\otimes w)

\displaystyle\sum{\mathcal{S}}^{12}(x){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z)\left(u\otimes Y_{M}(w_{(2)},-z)v_{(1)}\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},z)w_{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x+z)Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},z)w_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y_{M}\left(\left(Y_{M}(w_{(2)},-z)v_{(1)}\right)_{(2)},-x\right)\left(Y_{M}\left(w_{(1),(2)},-x-z\right)u_{(1)}\right)_{(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}\left(\left(Y_{M}\left(w_{(1),(2)},-x-z\right)u_{(1)}\right)_{(2)},x\right)\left(Y_{M}(w_{(2)},-z)v_{(1)}\right)_{(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x+z)Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},z)w_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y_{M}\left(v_{(1),(2)},-x\right)Y_{M}\left(w_{(1),(2)},-x-z\right)u_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}\left(u_{(1),(2)},x\right)Y_{M}(w_{(2)},-z)v_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x+z)Y_{M}^{-1}(v_{(2)},z)w_{(1),(1)}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{23}(z){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z){\mathcal{S}}^{12}(x)(u\otimes v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y_{M}\left(w_{(2)},-x-z\right)Y_{M}(v_{(2)},-x)u_{(1),(1)}\otimes Y_{M}\left(w_{(1),(2)},-z\right)Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x)v_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}\left(v_{(1),(2)},z\right)Y_{M}^{-1}\left(u_{(1),(2)},x+z\right)w_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y_{M}(v_{(2)},-x)Y_{M}\left(w_{(2)},-x-z\right)u_{(1),(1)}\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x)Y_{M}\left(w_{(1),(2)},-z\right)v_{(1),(1)}

\displaystyle\quad\otimes Y_{M}^{-1}\left(u_{(1),(2)},x+z\right)Y_{M}^{-1}\left(v_{(1),(2)},z\right)w_{(1),(1)},

where for the last equality we are also using the commutativity for
(Y_{M}^{\pm 1},Y_{M}^{\pm 1})
and
(Y_{M}^{\pm 1},Y_{M}^{\mp 1})
.
Note that with
H
cocommutative we have

P_{12}(\rho\otimes 1)\rho(v)=P_{12}P_{23}(\rho\otimes 1)\rho(v),

which states

\sum v_{(1),(2)}\otimes v_{(1),(1)}\otimes v_{(2)}=\sum v_{(2)}\otimes v_{(1),(1)}\otimes v_{(1),(2)}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum u_{(1),(1)}\otimes u_{(1),(2)}\otimes u_{(2)}=\sum u_{(1),(1)}\otimes u_{(2)}\otimes u_{(1),(2)},

\displaystyle\sum w_{(1),(2)}\otimes w_{(2)}\otimes w_{(1),(1)}=\sum w_{(2)}\otimes w_{(1),(2)}\otimes w_{(1),(1)}.

Then we conclude

{\mathcal{S}}^{12}(x){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z){\mathcal{S}}^{23}(z)(u\otimes v\otimes w)={\mathcal{S}}^{23}(z){\mathcal{S}}^{13}(x+z){\mathcal{S}}^{12}(x)(u\otimes v\otimes w).

Last, the shift condition can be proved straightforwardly.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)
with
{\mathcal{S}}(x)
is a quantum vertex algebra.
∎

The following is a result about the generating subsets of
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)
:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be an invertible element of

.
Suppose that

and

are generating subspaces of

and

as nonlocal vertex algebras, respectively, such that

Then

is also a generating subset of
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)
.

Proof.

Let
X
consist of all subspaces

of

such that

\displaystyle Y_{M}^{-1}(T,x)U\subset U\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((x)).

Denote by

the sum of all such subspaces. It is clear that

is the (unique) largest in

.
We see that
S
and
{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}
are in

, so that
{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}+S\subset K
.
Let
K^{2}
denote the linear span of
u_{n}v
in
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)
for
u,v\in K,\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For
u,v\in K
and
t\in T
, from the first part of the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we see that

\displaystyle Y_{M}^{-1}(t,x)\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(u,z)v=\sum\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(Y_{M}^{-1}(t_{(1)},x-z)u,z)Y_{M}^{-1}(t_{(2)},x)v.

It follows that
K^{2}
is in

. As

is the largest, we have

.
Thus

is a nonlocal vertex subalgebra of
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)
with

.

Now, let
H^{\prime}
be the maximal subspace of
H
such that

\displaystyle Y_{M}^{-1}(H^{\prime},x)K\subset K\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((x)).

We have
T+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}\subset H^{\prime}
. Note that for

, we have

\displaystyle Y_{M}^{-1}(Y(h,z)k,x)V^{\prime}=Y_{M}^{-1}(h,x+z)Y_{M}^{-1}(k,x)K\subset K\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]].

It follows that

is a nonlocal vertex subalgebra of

. Thus

.

Let
V^{\prime\prime}
be any subspace of

such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle Y_{M}(H,x)V^{\prime\prime}\subset V^{\prime\prime}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((x))\quad\textnormal{{and}}\quad\rho(V^{\prime\prime})\subset V^{\prime\prime}\otimes H.

That is,

is an

-submodule and a sub-comodule.
For

, we have

\displaystyle\rho\left(\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(V)(u,z)v\right)=Y^{\sharp}(\rho(u),z)\rho(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y(u_{(1)},z)Y_{M}(u_{(2),(1)},z)v_{(1)}\otimes Y(u_{(2),(2)},z)v_{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum Y(u_{(1),(1)},z)Y_{M}(u_{(1),(2)},z)v_{(1)}\otimes Y(u_{(2)},z)v_{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(u_{(1)},z)v_{(1)}\otimes Y(u_{(2)},z)v_{(2)}.

It follows that (
3.9
) holds for the nonlocal vertex subalgebra of

.

.

From Lemma

, for

\displaystyle Y(u,x)v=\sum\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{M}}^{\rho}(Y)(u_{(1)},x)Y_{M}^{-1}(u_{(2)},x)v\in K[[x,x^{-1}]].

As

.
Therefore,

.
∎

4.
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for smash product nonlocal vertex algebras

In this section, we first recall from
[
Li9
]
the basic notions and results on

\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for nonlocal vertex algebras and
then study (equivariant)
\phi
-coordinated modules for smash product nonlocal vertex algebras.

We begin with formal group.
A
one-dimensional formal group (law)
over
{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
H
]
)
is a formal power series
F(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
such that

F(0,y)=y,\ \ F(x,0)=x,\ \ F(F(x,y),z)=F(x,F(y,z)).

A particular example is the
additive formal group

F_{a}(x,y):=x+y
.

Let
F(x,y)
be a one-dimensional formal group over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
An
associate
of
F(x,y)
(see
[
Li9
]
) is a formal series

\phi(x,z)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]
, satisfying the condition that

\displaystyle\phi(x,0)=x,\ \ \ \ \phi(\phi(x,y),z)=\phi(x,F(y,z)).

The following result was obtained therein:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))
.
Set

\displaystyle\phi(x,z)=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}x=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\left(p(x)\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}x\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]].

Then
\phi(x,z)
is an associate of
F_{a}(x,y)
.
On the other hand, every associate of
F_{a}(x,y)
is of this form with
p(x)
uniquely determined.

Example 4.2
.

Taking
p(x)=1
in Proposition
4.1
, we get

\phi(x,z)=x+z=F_{a}(x,z)
(the formal group itself), and taking
p(x)=x
we get

\phi(x,z)=xe^{z}
. More generally, for
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, from
[
FHL
]
we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{r}(x,z):=e^{zx^{r+1}\frac{d}{dx}}x=\begin{cases}x\left(1-rzx^{r}\right)^{\frac{-1}{r}}&\ \ \mbox{ if }r\neq 0\\
xe^{z}&\ \ \mbox{ if }r=0.\end{cases}

From now on, we shall
always
assume that
\phi(x,z)
is an associate of
F_{a}(x,y)

with
\phi(x,z)\neq x
, or equivalently,

\phi(x,z)=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}x
with
p(x)\neq 0
.

From
[
Li9
]
, for
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
,
f(\phi(x,z),x)
exists in
{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]
and
the correspondence

f(x_{1},x)\mapsto f(\phi(x,z),x)
gives a ring embedding

\iota_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}:\ {\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x))\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]\subset{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z)).

Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}_{\ast}((x_{1},x_{2}))
the fraction field of
{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
.
Then
\iota_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}
naturally extends to a field embedding

(4.2)

\displaystyle\iota_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}^{*}:\ {\mathbb{C}}_{\ast}((x_{1},x))\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z)).

As a convention, for
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\ast}((x_{1},x_{2}))
, we write

(4.3)

\displaystyle F(\phi(x,z),x)=\iota_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}^{*}(F(x_{1},x))\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z)).

For
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
, by definition

f(\phi(x,z_{1}),\phi(x,z_{2}))=\left(e^{z_{1}p(x_{1})\partial_{x_{1}}}e^{z_{2}p(x_{2})\partial_{x_{2}}}f(x_{1},x_{2})\right)|_{x_{1}=x_{2}=x},

which exists in
{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
, and
the correspondence

f(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto f(\phi(x,z_{1}),\phi(x,z_{2}))

is a ring embedding of
{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
into
{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z_{1},z_{2}]]
(see
[
JKLT
]
).
Then this ring embedding gives rise to a field embedding

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}((x))_{*}((z_{1},z_{2})),

where
{\mathbb{C}}((x))_{*}((z_{1},z_{2}))
denotes the fraction field of
{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z_{1},z_{2}))
. As a convention,
for
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
, we
view
F(\phi(x,z_{1}),\phi(x,z_{2}))
as an element of
{\mathbb{C}}((x))_{*}((z_{1},z_{2}))
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
V
be a nonlocal vertex algebra.
A
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module
is a vector space
W
equipped with a linear map

\displaystyle Y_{W}(\cdot,x):

\displaystyle V\rightarrow(\textnormal{{End}}W)[[x,x^{-1}]]

\displaystyle v\mapsto Y_{W}(v,x),

satisfying the conditions that

Y_{W}(u,x)w\in W((x))\ \ \ \textnormal{{for }}u\in V,\ w\in W,

Y_{W}({\bf 1},x)=1_{W}\ \ (\textnormal{{the identity operator on }}W),

and that for any
u,v\in V
, there exists
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))^{\times}
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})\in\textnormal{{Hom}}(W,W((x_{1},x_{2}))),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left(f(x_{1},x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})\right)|_{x_{1}=\phi(x_{2},z)}=f(\phi(x_{2},z),x_{2})Y_{W}(Y(u,z)v,x_{2}).

The following notion was introduced in
[
JKLT
]
(cf.
[
Li10
]
):

Definition 4.4
.

Let
V
be a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra and let
\chi_{\phi}
be a linear character of

such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\phi(x,\chi(g)z)=\chi_{\phi}(g)\phi(\chi_{\phi}(g)^{-1}x,z)\ \ \mbox{ for }g\in G.

A
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi

is a
\phi
-coordinated quasi

-module

(W,Y_{W})
satisfying the conditions that

(4.8)

\displaystyle Y_{W}(R(g)v,x)=Y_{W}(v,\chi_{\phi}(g)^{-1}x)\ \ \ \ \mbox{ for }g\in G,\ v\in V

and that for

, there exists

(4.9)

\displaystyle q(x_{1}/x_{2})Y_{W}(u,x_{1})Y_{W}(v,x_{2})\in\textnormal{{Hom}}(W,W((x_{1},x_{2}))),

where

by

for

.

Remark 4.5
.

It was proved (see loc. cit.) that the condition (

) is equivalent to

\displaystyle p(\chi_{\phi}(g)x)=\chi(g)^{-1}\chi_{\phi}(g)p(x)\ \ \mbox{ for }g\in G,

where

.
In case

with

)
is equivalent to

.

The following is a straightforward analogue of Lemma
2.11

for
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-modules:

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the setting of Definition
4.4
, assume that all the conditions hold except
(
4.8
), and instead assume

(4.11)

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\phi}(R(g)v,x)=Y_{W}^{\phi}(v,\chi_{\phi}(g)^{-1}x)\quad{for}\ g\in G,\ v\in S,

where

is a generating subset of
V
.
Then

is a
(G,\chi_{\phi})
-equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi
V
-module.

Next, we study
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra structures
on smash product nonlocal vertex algebra
V\sharp H
and
equivariant
\phi
-coordinated quasi modules for
V\sharp H
.
For the rest of this section, we fix a group
G
with a linear character
\chi
.
We first formulate the following notion:

Definition 4.7
.

A
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex bialgebra

H
is a nonlocal vertex bialgebra
and a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra on which

acts as an automorphism group with

viewed as a coalgebra.

The following two propositions can be proved straightforwardly:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
H
be a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex bialgebra.
Suppose that
V
is a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra and an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
with the
H
-module structure denoted by
Y_{V}^{H}(\cdot,x)
such that

(4.12)

\displaystyle R_{V}(g)Y_{V}^{H}(h,x)v=Y_{V}^{H}(R_{H}(g)h,\chi(g)x)R_{V}(g)v\quad{for}\ g\in G,\ h\in H,\ v\in V,

where

and

denote the representations of

on

and

, respectively.
Then

is a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra with

.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
H,V
be given as in Proposition

.
In addition, assume that

is a right

-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra whose right

-comodule structure

\rho:V\rightarrow V\otimes H
is compatible with

and is a

-module homomorphism.
Then
\mathfrak{D}_{Y_{V}^{H}}^{\rho}(V)
is a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra
with the same representation

of

on

.

is a homomorphism of

-module nonlocal vertex algebras
from

to

.

Remark 4.10
.

Let
H
be a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex bialgebra and let
V
be a
(G,\chi)
-module nonlocal vertex algebra.
As a convention,
we shall always assume that the condition (
4.12
) holds
for an
H
-module nonlocal vertex algebra structure
Y_{V}^{H}
on
V
and that

\rho
is also a
G
-module homomorphism for an
H
-comodule nonlocal vertex algebra structure
\rho
.

Recall that
{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
denotes the fraction field of
{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
and that
for
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
,
F(\phi(x,z),x)
was defined as an element of
{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z))
.
Here, we interpret this definition in a different way.
For
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))
, we alternatively define
f(\phi(x,z),x)
in two steps via substitutions

{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))\overset{s_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]\overset{s_{x_{2}=x}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]\subset{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z)),

where
s_{x_{1}=\phi(x,z)}
and
s_{x_{2}=x}
denote the indicated substitution maps.
The composition of the two substitutions was proved to be one-to-one.
But, note that the substitution
{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]\overset{s_{x_{2}=x}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}((x))[[z]]
is
not
one-to-one.
Set

(4.13)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]^{o}=\{f(x,x_{2},z)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]\ |\ f(x,x,z)\neq 0\}.

Note that
f(\phi(x,z),x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]^{o}
for
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}((x_{1},x_{2}))^{\times}
. Then set

(4.14)

\displaystyle\Lambda(x,x_{2},z)=\left\{\frac{f}{g}\ |\ f\in{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]],\ g\in{\mathbb{C}}((x,x_{2}))[[z]]^{o}\right\}.

The substitution map
s_{x_{2}=x}
extends uniquely to a ring embedding

(4.15)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{x_{2}=x}:\ \Lambda(x,x_{2},z)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}((x))((z)).

Now, for any
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
, we have
F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})\in\Lambda(x,x_{2},z)

and

(4.16)

\displaystyle F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})|_{x_{2}=x}:=\tilde{s}_{x_{2}=x}(F(\phi(x,z),x_{2}))=F(\phi(x,z),x).

We have the following result:

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\phi(x,z)=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}x
with
p(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))^{\times}
and let
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
. Then

(4.17)

\displaystyle F(\phi(x_{1},z),x_{2})=F(x_{1},\phi(x_{2},-z))

holds in
{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))[[z]]
if and only if

(4.18)

\displaystyle p(x_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}F(x_{1},x_{2})=-p(x_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}F(x_{1},x_{2}).

Furthermore, assuming either one of the two equivalent conditions, we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle F(\phi(x,z),x)=f(z),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\iota_{x,z_{1},z_{2}}F(\phi(x,z_{1}),\phi(x,z_{2}))=f(z_{1}-z_{2})

for some
f(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))
.

Proof.

From definition we have

F(\phi(x_{1},z),x_{2})=e^{zp(x_{1})\partial_{x_{1}}}F(x_{1},x_{2})\ \mbox{ and }\ F(x_{1},\phi(x_{2},-z))=e^{-zp(x_{2})\partial_{x_{2}}}F(x_{1},x_{2}).

This immediately confirms the first assertion. For the second assertion, assuming

F(\phi(x_{1},z),x_{2})=F(x_{1},\phi(x_{2},-z))
, we have

\displaystyle p(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(\phi(x,z),x)

\displaystyle=\left(p(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})+p(x_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})\right)|_{x_{2}=x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})+p(x_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}F(x,\phi(x_{2},-z))\right)|_{x_{2}=x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}F(x,\phi(x_{2},-z))\right)|_{x_{2}=x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(F(\phi(x,z),x_{2})-F(x,\phi(x_{2},-z))\right)|_{x_{2}=x}

\displaystyle=0.

As
p(x)\neq 0
, we get
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(\phi(x,z),x)=0
. Thus

F(\phi(x,z),x)\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))
. Setting
f(z)=F(\phi(x,z),x)
, using (
4.17
) we have

\displaystyle F(\phi(x,z_{1}),\phi(x,z_{2}))=F(\phi(\phi(x,z_{1}),-z_{2}),x)=F(\phi(x,z_{1}-z_{2}),x)=f(z_{1}-z_{2}),

where we are using obvious expansion conventions.
This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.12
.

Let
\phi(x,z)=e^{zp(x)\frac{d}{dx}}x
with
p(x)\in{\mathbb{C}}((x))^{\times}
. Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}_{\phi}((x_{1},x_{2}))
the set of
all
F(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}_{*}((x_{1},x_{2}))
such that (
4.18

5.

In this section, we use the vertex bialgebra

5.1.
Vertex algebra

and associative algebra

We first recall the lattice vertex algebra

(see
[

]

[

]

Let

symmetric
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued bilinear form
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
such that
{\langle}\alpha,\alpha{\rangle}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
for
\alpha\in L
.
We assume that
L
is nondegenerate in the obvious sense.
Set

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}L

and extend
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
to a symmetric
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
View
{\mathfrak{h}}
as an abelian Lie algebra with
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
as a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form.
Then we have an affine Lie algebra
\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}
, where

\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}={\mathfrak{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t,t^{-1}]+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf k}

as a vector space, and where
{\bf k}
is central and

(5.2)

\displaystyle[\alpha(m),\beta(n)]=m\delta_{m+n,0}{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}{\bf k}

for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathfrak{h}},\ m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\alpha(m)
denoting
\alpha\otimes t^{m}
. Set

(5.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\pm}={\mathfrak{h}}\otimes t^{\pm 1}{\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}],

which are abelian Lie subalgebras. Identify
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes t^{0}
.
Furthermore, set

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\prime}=\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{+}+\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-}+{\mathbb{C}}{\bf k},

which is a Heisenberg algebra. Then
\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}=\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
, which is a direct sum of Lie algebras.

Let
\varepsilon:L\times L\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
be a 2-cocycle such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)\varepsilon(\beta,\alpha)^{-1}=(-1)^{{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}},\ \ \ \varepsilon(\alpha,0)=1=\varepsilon(0,\alpha)\quad\textnormal{{ for }}\alpha,\beta\in L.

Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]
the
\varepsilon
-twisted group algebra of
L
,
which by definition has a designated basis
\{e_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in L\}
with relations

(5.5)

\displaystyle e_{\alpha}\cdot e_{\beta}=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)e_{\alpha+\beta}\quad\textnormal{{ for }}\alpha,\beta\in L.

Make
{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]
an
\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}
-module by letting
\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{\prime}

act trivially and letting
{\mathfrak{h}}

(={\mathfrak{h}}(0))
act by

(5.6)

\displaystyle h(0)e_{\beta}={\langle}h,\beta{\rangle}e_{\beta}\ \ \mbox{ for }h\in{\mathfrak{h}},\ \beta\in L.

Note that
S(\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-})
is naturally an
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module of level
1
.
Set

(5.7)

\displaystyle V_{L}=S(\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{-})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L],

the tensor product of
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-modules, which is an
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module of level
1
.
Set

{{\bf 1}}=1\otimes e_{0}\in V_{L}.

Identify
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]
as subspaces of

via the correspondence

a\mapsto a(-1)\otimes 1\ (a\in{\mathfrak{h}})\quad\textnormal{{and}}\quad e_{\alpha}\mapsto 1\otimes e_{\alpha}\ (\alpha\in L).

For
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, set

(5.8)

\displaystyle h(z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}h(n)z^{-n-1}.

On the other hand, for
\alpha\in L
set

(5.9)

\displaystyle E^{\pm}(\alpha,z)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{\alpha(\pm n)}{\pm n}z^{\mp n}\right)

on
V_{L}
. For
\alpha\in L
, define a linear operator

z^{\alpha}:\ {\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L]\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{\varepsilon}[L][z,z^{-1}]

(5.10)

\displaystyle z^{\alpha}\cdot e_{\beta}=z^{{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}}e_{\beta}\ \ \ \mbox{ for }\beta\in L.

Then there exists a vertex algebra structure on
V_{L}
, which is uniquely determined by
the conditions that
{{\bf 1}}
is the vacuum vector and that
(h\in{\mathfrak{h}},\,\alpha\in L)

(5.11)

\displaystyle Y(h,z)=h(z),\quad Y(e_{\alpha},z)=E^{-}(-\alpha,z)E^{+}(-\alpha,z)e_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}.

Next, we recall from
[
LL

the associative algebra

By definition,

with unit

where

\displaystyle h[z]=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}h[n]z^{-n-1},\ \ \ \ e_{\alpha}[z]=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e_{\alpha}[n]z^{-n-1}.

The relations are

\displaystyle\textnormal{{(AL1) }}\ \ \ \ e_{0}[z]=1,

\displaystyle\textnormal{{(AL2) }}\ \ \ \ \left[h[z_{1}],h^{\prime}[z_{2}]\right]={\langle}h,h^{\prime}{\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right),

\displaystyle\textnormal{{(AL3) }}\ \ \ \ \left[h[z_{1}],e_{\alpha}[z_{2}]\right]={\langle}\alpha,h{\rangle}e_{\alpha}[z_{2}]z_{1}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right),

\displaystyle\textnormal{{(AL4) }}\ \ \ \ \left[e_{\alpha}[z_{1}],e_{\beta}[z_{2}]\right]=0\ \textnormal{{ if }}{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\textnormal{{(AL5) }}\ \ \ \ (z_{1}-z_{2})^{-{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}}\left[e_{\alpha}[z_{1}],e_{\beta}[z_{2}]\right]=0\ \textnormal{{ if }}{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}<0,

\displaystyle\Phi(f(a,x))e_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}}e_{\beta}\otimes x{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\quad\textnormal{{and}}\quad\Phi(f(a,x))b\in{\mathbb{C}}{{\bf 1}}\otimes x{\mathbb{C}}[[x]].

It follows that

(5.42)

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}\Phi(f^{\prime}(a,x))b=0=\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}\Phi(f^{\prime}(a,x))\beta=\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}\exp\left(\Phi(f(a,x))\right)b

and

\displaystyle\exp\left(\Phi(f(a,x))\right)e_{\beta}\in e_{\beta}+{\mathbb{C}}e_{\beta}\otimes x{\mathbb{C}}[[x]].

Then we get that

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}Y^{f}(a,x)b=\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}Y(a,x)b,\quad\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}Y^{f}(a,x)e_{\beta}=\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}Y(a,x)e_{\beta},

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{x}x^{-1}Y^{f}(\alpha,x)e_{\alpha}=\partial e_{\alpha}+{\langle}f^{\prime}(\alpha,0),\alpha{\rangle}e_{\alpha},

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Sing}}_{x}x^{{\langle}\alpha,\beta{\rangle}}Y^{f}(e_{\alpha},x)e_{\beta}=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)e_{\alpha+\beta}.

Combining these with Lemma
2.6
, Definition

and relations (

), (

), (
5.41
), we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
G
be an automorphism group of
V_{L}
such that
G({\mathfrak{h}})={\mathfrak{h}}
and let
\chi
be a linear character of
G
.
Assume
\eta(\cdot,x):{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes x{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
is a linear map satisfying the condition

(5.43)

\displaystyle(\mu\otimes 1)\eta(h,x)=\eta(\mu(h),\chi(\mu)x)\ \ \ \mbox{ for }\mu\in G,\ h\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

Then
V_{L}^{\eta}
is a
(G,\chi)
-module quantum vertex algebra with
R
defined by

R(g)=\chi(g)^{L(0)}g
for
g\in G
.

Proof.

Recall that for
v\in V_{L},\ f(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}((t))
,

\Phi(v\otimes f(t))(z)=\text{Res}_{x_{1}}Y(v,x_{1})f(z-x_{1}).

For
\mu\in G
, set
R_{\mu}=\chi(\mu)^{L(0)}\mu
.
For
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mu}\Phi(\eta(h,t))(z)=\

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{x_{1}}R_{\mu}Y(\eta(h,z-x_{1}),x_{1})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{x_{1}}\chi(\mu)Y((\mu\otimes 1)\eta(h,z-x_{1}),\chi(\mu)x_{1})R_{\mu}\mu

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{x_{1}}\chi(\mu)Y(\eta(\mu(h),\chi(\mu)(z-x_{1})),\chi(\mu)x_{1})R_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{z_{1}}Y(\eta(\mu(h),\chi(\mu)z-z_{1}),z_{1})R_{\mu}

(5.44)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\Phi(\eta(\mu(h),t))(\chi(\mu)z)R_{\mu}.

Then for
\alpha\in L
we get

\displaystyle R_{\mu}Y^{\eta}(e_{\alpha},z)R_{\mu}^{-1}=\

\displaystyle R_{\mu}Y(e_{\alpha},z)\exp(\Phi(\eta(\alpha,t))(z))R_{\mu}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle Y(R_{\mu}e_{\alpha},\chi(\mu)z)\exp\left(R_{\mu}\Phi(\eta(\alpha,t))(z)R_{\mu}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\chi(\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}{\langle}\alpha,\alpha{\rangle}}Y(\mu e_{\alpha},\chi(\mu)z)\exp\left(\Phi(\eta(\mu(\alpha),t)(\chi(\mu)z)\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu,\alpha}\chi(\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}{\langle}\mu(\alpha),\mu(\alpha){\rangle}}Y(e_{\mu(\alpha)},\chi(\mu)z)\exp\left(\Phi(\eta(\mu(\alpha),t))(\chi(\mu)z)\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu,\alpha}\chi(\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}{\langle}\mu(\alpha),\mu(\alpha){\rangle}}Y^{\eta}(e_{\mu(\alpha)},\chi(\mu)z)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle Y^{\eta}(R_{\mu}e_{\alpha},\chi(\mu)z),

where
\mu(e_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\mu,\alpha}e_{\mu(\alpha)}
with
\lambda_{\mu,\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Note that (
5.43
) implies

(\mu\otimes 1)\eta^{\prime}(h,x)=\chi(\mu)\eta^{\prime}(\mu(h),\chi(\mu)x).

Following the argument in (
5.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\mu}\Phi(\eta^{\prime}(h,t))(z)R_{\mu}^{-1}=\chi(\mu)\Phi(\eta^{\prime}(\mu(h),t))(\chi(\mu)z)=\Phi(\eta^{\prime}(R_{\mu}(h),t))(\chi(\mu)z).

Using this we get

R_{\mu}Y^{\eta}(h,x)R_{\mu}^{-1}=R_{\mu}\left(Y(h,x)+\Phi(\eta^{\prime}(h,t))(x)\right)R_{\mu}^{-1}=Y^{\eta}(R_{\mu}h,\chi(\mu)x).

Note that
\{e_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in L\}\cup{\mathfrak{h}}
is a generating subset of
V_{L}^{\eta}
as a nonlocal vertex algebra.
By using weak associativity (or by Lemma
2.11
) it is straightforward to show that

R_{\mu}Y^{\eta}(v,x)R_{\mu}^{-1}=Y^{\eta}(R_{\mu}v,\chi(\mu)x)\ \ \ \mbox{ for all }\mu\in G,\ v\in V_{L}.

Therefore,
V_{L}^{\eta}
is a
(G,\chi)
-module quantum vertex algebra as desired.
∎

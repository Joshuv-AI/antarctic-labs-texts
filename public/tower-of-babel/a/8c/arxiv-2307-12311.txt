A new upper bound on Ruzsa’s number on the Erdős–Turán conjecture
Yuchen Ding and Lilu Zhao

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the Ruzsa number R m R_{m} is bounded by 192 192 for any positive integer m m , which improve the prior bound R m ≤ 288 R_{m}\leq 288 given by Y.–G. Chen in 2008.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B13, 11B34.

1.
Introduction

For any positive integer
m
, let
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
be the set of residue classes mod
m
(we represented
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
for simplicity below). For any subsets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, let
\sigma_{A,B}(n)
be the number of solutions to the equation
n=x+y~(x\in A,y\in B)
. Let
\sigma_{A}(n)=\sigma_{A,A}(n)
.
The Ruzsa number
R_{m}
is defined to be the least positive integer
r
so that there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}
with

1\leq\sigma_{A}(n)\leq r\quad(~\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}).

Our aim is to prove the following improvement of Chen’s bound
R_{m}\leq 288

[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any positive integer
m
we have
R_{m}\leq 192
.

Chen believed that
R_{m}\leq 20
for any positive integer
m
(private communications).

Below, we briefly mention the motivation and history of Ruzsa’s number.

A subset
A
of the natural numbers is called an asymptotic basis if
\sigma_{A}(n)\geq 1
for all sufficiently large integers
n
.
In a cute article involving the Sidon set, Erdős and Turán
[
5
]
posed a well–known conjecture which states that if
A
is an asymptotic basis, then the representation function
\sigma_{A}(n)
cannot be bounded. People pay much attention to the Erdős–Turán conjecture. In 2003, Grekos, Haddad, Helou and Pihko
[
9
]
showed that for an asymptotic basis
A
, the representation function
\sigma_{A}(n)
is not less than
6
for infinitely many
n
. It was then improved to
\sigma_{A}(n)\geq 8
infinitely many often by Borwein, Choi and Chu
[
1
]
. In another approach, Sándor
[
10
]
proved that if

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{A}(n)=K

for some given constant
K
, then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{A}(n)\leq K-2\sqrt{K}+1.

Chen
[
3
]
constructed an asymptotic basis
A
of the natural numbers which satisfies that the set of
n
with
\sigma_{A}(n)=2
has density
1
. For more information on the Erdős–Turán conjecture, one can also refer to the nice books of Halberstam and Roth
[
6
]
, Tao and Vu
[
14
]
.

Motivated by Erdős’ question, Ruzsa
[
8
]
constructed an asymptotic basis
A
of natural numbers which has a bounded square mean value, i.e.,

\sum_{n\leq N}\sigma_{A}^{2}(n)\ll N.

In his argument, Ruzsa actually proved that there is a given constant
C
such that

R_{m}\leq C
for any positive integer
m
, which is surely a nontrivial result due to the consistent bound of all integers
m
. Using Ruzsa’s construction, Tang and Chen
[
12
]
proved that
R_{m}\leq 768
for all sufficiently large
m
. Shortly after, they further
[
13
]
showed that
R_{m}\leq 5120
for any natural number
m
. In a previously beautiful work, Chen
[
2
]
improved the bound to
R_{m}\leq 288
for any natural number
m
. However, as Chen noted that he cannot improve the trivial lower bound
R_{m}\geq 3
for
m\neq 1,3
. Recently, Sándor and Yang
[
11
]
give the nontrivial lower bound of
R_{m}
. They proved that
R_{m}\geq 6
for
m\geq 36
. Moreover, Sándor and Yang classified all the numbers
m
with
R_{m}=k
for
1\leq k\leq 5
(see the Appendix of their article). In
[
4
]
, a related problem of the Ruzsa number was investigated by Chen and Sun.

Proofs

Before presenting the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we list a few lemmas to be used later.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
2

Let
p
be a prime. Then
R_{2p^{2}}\leq 48
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
m\leq 4356
, we have
R_{m}\leq 132
.

Proof.

For
m\leq 4356
, let

A=\{0,1,2,\cdots,66,2\cdot 66,3\cdot 66,\cdots,66\cdot 66\}.

Then
\sigma_{A}(n)\geq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
by the Euclidean division. It is clear that

\sigma_{A}(n)\leq|A|\leq 132.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

[
2
, Lemma 4]

For any real number
x\geq 33
, there exists at least one prime in the interval
\left(x,\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}\right]
.

Proof.

Let
\pi(x)
be the number of primes not exceeding
x
. We know, from the results of Panaitopol
[
7
]
, that

\pi(x)<\frac{x}{\log x-1-(\log x)^{-0.5}}\quad(x\geq 6)

and

\pi(x)<\frac{x}{\log x-1+(\log x)^{-0.5}}\quad(x\geq 59).

We first show that

\pi\left(\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}\right)-\pi(x)>\frac{\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}}{\log\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}-1+\left(\log\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{-0.5}}-\frac{x}{\log x-1-(\log x)^{-0.5}}\geq 0

for
x>1242
. By simple rearrangements of the inequality, it suffices to prove that

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\log x\geq 1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\log\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}+(\log x)^{-0.5}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(\log\frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{-0.5}.

Checking by the computer, we know that it does satisfy for
x=1242
, and hence for
x>1242
.
For
33\leq x\leq 1242
, our lemma can again be easily checked via help of the computer.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be two positive integers with
\frac{3}{2}m_{1}\leq m_{2}<2m_{1}
. Then

R_{m_{2}}\leq 4R_{m_{1}}.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Chen
[
2
, Lemma 3]
with some refinements. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}
be the one satisfying that

1\leq\sigma_{A}(n)\leq R_{m_{1}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}
. We make the provision that if
a\in A
, then
0\leq a\leq m_{1}-1
. Suppose that
m_{2}=m_{1}+r
, then we have
\frac{1}{2}m_{1}\leq r<m_{1}
. Let

B=A\cup\{a+r:a\in A\}.

We first claim that
\sigma_{B}(n)\geq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
. In fact, for
0\leq n\leq m_{1}-1
, there exist
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
such that
n\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{1})
, which means that

n=a_{1}+a_{2}\quad\text{or}\quad n+m_{1}=a_{1}+a_{2}

since
a_{1}+a_{2}\leq 2m_{1}-2<2m_{1}
.
If
n=a_{1}+a_{2}
, then
n\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{2})
. Otherwise, we have
n+m_{1}=a_{1}+a_{2}
, then
n+m_{2}=a_{1}+(a_{2}+r)
, from which we deduce that
n\equiv a_{1}+(a_{2}+r)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{2})
. Thus, we have shown that
\sigma_{B}(n)\geq 1
for any
0\leq n\leq m_{1}-1
. We now consider the case
m_{1}\leq n\leq m_{2}-1
. In this case, we have

0<m_{1}-r\leq n-r\leq m_{1}-1.

Hence, there exist
a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime}\in A
such that
n-r\equiv a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{1})
, which implies that

n-r=a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}\quad\text{or}\quad n-r+m_{1}=a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}

again by the fact that
a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}<2m_{1}
. If
n-r=a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}
, then
n\equiv a_{1}^{\prime}+(a_{2}^{\prime}+r)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{2})
. So, we can now assume that
n-r+m_{1}=a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}
, from which we deduce that
n+m_{2}=(a_{1}^{\prime}+r)+(a_{2}^{\prime}+r)
, i.e.,
n\equiv(a_{1}^{\prime}+r)+(a_{2}^{\prime}+r)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{2})
. Therefore, we have
\sigma_{B}(n)\geq 1
for any
m_{1}\leq n\leq m_{2}-1
too.

In the following, we prove that
\sigma_{B}(n)\leq 4R_{m_{1}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
with
0\leq n\leq m_{2}-1
, we can assume that

n\equiv b_{1}+b_{2}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m_{2}),\quad(b_{1},b_{2}\in B).

(2.1)

From Eq. (
2.1
) we have

n+km_{2}=b_{1}+b_{2},\quad b_{1},b_{2}\in B

(2.2)

for some nonnegative integers
k
. Since
b_{1}+b_{2}\leq 2m_{2}-2<2m_{2}
, we know that
k=0
or
1
. Thus, we can conclude that

\sigma_{B}(n)\leq\#\{(b_{1},b_{2}):n=b_{1}+b_{2}\}+\#\{(b_{1},b_{2}):n+m_{2}=b_{1}+b_{2}\},

(2.3)

where the
b^{\prime}s
belong to
B
and
a^{\prime}s
belong to
A
, respectively, from now on. By the definition of
B
we have

\displaystyle\#\{(b_{1},b_{2}):n=b_{1}+b_{2}\}\leq

\displaystyle\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n=a_{1}+a_{2}\}+2\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n-r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}

\displaystyle+\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n-2r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle\#

\displaystyle\{(b_{1},b_{2}):n+m_{2}=b_{1}+b_{2}\}\leq\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{2}=a_{1}+a_{2}\}

\displaystyle+2\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{2}-r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}+\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{2}-2r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}

\displaystyle+2\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{1}=a_{1}+a_{2}\}+\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{1}-r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}.

(2.5)

Below, we shall illustrate that there are some disjoint solutions in Eqs. (

Case I.

\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n-2r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}\neq 0
. In this case, we have
n\geq 2r\geq m_{1}.

It then follows that

n+m_{1}+r>n+m_{1}\geq 2m_{1},

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{1}=a_{1}+a_{2}\}=\#\{(a_{1},a_{2}):n+m_{1}+r=a_{1}+a_{2}\}=0

(2.6)

since

Hence, by Eqs (

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
2
, Lemma 3]
, Chen showed that
R_{m_{1}}\leq 6R_{m_{2}}
under the condition that

m_{1}<m_{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}m_{1}.

The improvement of the upper bound on the Ruzsa number comes from our refined relation between
R_{m_{1}}
and
R_{m_{2}}
. The idea for this refined relation relies on the following two points. Firstly, we make the observation that if we use the basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}
to generate a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
, then the parameter
k
in Eq. (
2.2
) can only be 0 or 1. Whereas, if we use the basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
to generate a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}
, like the ones taken by Chen, then the corresponding parameter
k
in Eq. (
2.2
) could be 0 or 1 or 2, which makes
relation between
R_{m_{1}}
and
R_{m_{2}}
less clean and involved.
Basing on this new construction, we introduce a new correlation of
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
(i.e.,
\frac{3}{2}m_{1}\leq m_{2}<2m_{1}
) to form some disjoint solutions to
\sigma_{B}(n)
which certainly would be the key ingredient of our note.

Now, let’s turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
2.2
, we only need to consider
m>4356
.
By this condition we shall have
\sqrt{m/4}>33
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a prime
p
such that

\sqrt{\frac{m}{4}}<p\leq\sqrt{\frac{m}{3}}.

In other words, we have

\frac{3}{2}\cdot 2p^{2}=3p^{2}\leq m<4p^{2}=2\cdot 2p^{2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
and
2.4
it follows that

R_{m}\leq 4R_{2p^{2}}\leq 192.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3.
Some related conjectures

In
[
2
]
, Chen asked that whether there are infinitely many odd (resp. even) numbers in the sequence
\{R_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
. He called it a basic problem on Ruzsa’s number. To end up this note, we pose a few conjectures which seems to be of some interests.

Conjecture 3.1
.

For any positive integer
m
, we have
\left|R_{m+1}-R_{m}\right|\leq 1.

For
m\leq 34
, Conjecture
3.1
is true by the table in the Appendix of the article of Sándor and Yang
[
11
]
.
Perhaps, this conjecture is not really difficult.

Conjecture 3.2

(Chen)
.

We have
R_{m+1}=R_{m}
for infinitely many positive integers
m
.

Comparing with Conjecture
3.1
, it might be somewhat easier to reach Conjecture
3.2
.

From now on, let
\mathcal{H}_{m}
be the set of all subsets
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that

\sigma_{A}(n)\geq 1

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Define the following minimal mean value amount

\ell_{m}=\min_{A\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}}\left\{m^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\sigma_{A}(n)\right\}.

Conjecture 3.3
.

We have
\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{m}\geq 3.

The philosophy leading to Conjecture
3.3
is that we believe that for most integers
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
there are at least two essentially different representations, providing that
\sigma_{A}(n)\geq 1

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.
This can be viewed as an analogue of the Erdős–Turán conjecture in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.
It is clear that we have

\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{m}\geq 2

from Lemma 2.2 of
[
11
]
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{m}\leq 192.

The authors think that any improvement of the bounds involving
\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{m}
and
\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ell_{m}
above would be of interest. Actually, Conjecture
3.3
could be interpreted to the following more simple form: Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{m}
be a subset such that
A+A=\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then

\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A|}{\sqrt{m}}\geq\sqrt{3}.

For positive integer
m
, let
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{m}
be the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
with
1\leq\sigma_{A}(n)\leq R_{m}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.
Let

\mathcal{K}_{m}=\min_{A\in\mathcal{H}^{*}_{m}}\{\#A\}.

Conjecture 3.4
.

We have
\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{K}_{m}/\sqrt{3m}\geq 1.

Conjecture
3.4
is slightly weaker than Conjecture
3.3
. One may expect that
\ell_{m}
is ‘always’ attained for some
A\in\mathcal{H}^{*}_{m}
. That is,

Conjecture 3.5
.

We have
\mathcal{K}_{m}^{2}=m\ell_{m}
for sufficiently large
m
.

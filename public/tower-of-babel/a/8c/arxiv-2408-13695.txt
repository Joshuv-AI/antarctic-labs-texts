The Lang-Trotter conjecture on average for genus- 2 2 curves with Klein- 4 4 reduced automorphism group
Chihiro Ando

Abstract
For an elliptic curve E E over ℚ \mathbb{Q} without complex multiplication, Lang and Trotter conjecture # ⁡ { p < X ∣ E ​ has a supersingular reduction at ​ p } ∼ c ​ X log ⁡ X \#\{p<X\mid E\text{ has a supersingular reduction at }p\}\sim\frac{c\sqrt{X}}{\log X} as X → ∞ X\rightarrow\infty , where c > 0 c>0 is a constant depending only on E E . Fourvy and Murty obtained an average estimation related to the Lang-Trotter conjecture, called the Lang-Trotter conjecture on average. We considered the Lang-Trotter conjecture for curves of genus 2, and obtained a similar result to the Lang-Trotter conjecture on average for the family of curves C λ : y 2 = x ⁡ ( x − 1 ) ​ ( x + 1 ) ​ ( x − λ ) ​ ( x − 1 / λ ) C_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-{\lambda})(x-1/\lambda) . Such curves are characterized as curves of genus two with reduced automorphism group containing the Klein 4 4 -group.

Introduction

Let
E_{a,b}
be the elliptic curve defined by the Weierstrass equation

y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b

for
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
{\pi}_{0}(X,a,b)
be the number of primes
p<X
such that
E_{a,b}
has a supersingular reduction at
p
. If
E_{a,b}
has complex multiplication, Deuring
[
8
]
showed that

{\pi}_{0}(X,a,b)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{X}{\log X}.

If
E
does not have complex multiplication, Lang and Trotter
[
12
]
conjecture the existence of a constant
c
depending only on
E_{a,b}
such that

{\pi}_{0}(X,a,b)\sim\frac{c\sqrt{X}}{\log X}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
. Although it is still an open question, a weaker result due to Elkies
[
6
]
says that there are infinitely many supersingular primes. In addition, Fourvy and Murty obtained an average estimation related to the Lang-Trotter conjecture. They showed for
A,B>X^{1+\epsilon}
,

\frac{1}{AB}\sum_{\lvert a\rvert\leq A}\sum_{\lvert b\rvert\leq B}{\pi}_{0}(X,a,b)\sim\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X},

where
a,b
run through integers. They used the fact that the number of supersingular elliptic curves
E_{a,b}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(
p\neq 2,3
) with
0\leq a,b<p
is equal to

\frac{p}{2}\cdot\left(h(-4p)+h(-p)\right)+O(p),

where
h(-p)
and
h(-4p)
are the class numbers of primitive quadratic forms with discriminant
-p
and
-4p
respectively.
Moreover, they also proved that the average estimation is true if
A,B>X^{1/2+\epsilon}
and
AB>X^{3/2+\epsilon}
.
This result is called
the Lang-Trotter Conjecture on average
.
Let
a_{p}(E):=p+1-\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then, Fourvy and Murty’s result is the case of
a_{p}(E)=0
. David and Pappalardi
[
4
]
generalized this result to the case that
a_{p}(E)=r
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, and Baier
[
3
]
improved the error terms in their results.

In this paper, we extend the Lang-Trotter conjecture on average to curves of genus 2. Curves of genus 2 are classified into 7 types (cf Igusa
[
10
]
, see Section
2.2
for a review). In particular, we focus on one of them: the genus-2 curves defined by the following equation:

C_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-{\lambda})\left(x-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right).

These genus-two curves are characterized as having the reduced automorphism group
{\rm RA}(C_{\lambda})
containing the Klein 4-group
V_{4}
. For general
\lambda
, it is known that
{\rm RA}(C_{\lambda})
is isomorphic to
V_{4}
.
Here the
reduced automorphism group
of hyperelliptic curve
C
is the quotient of the automorphism group of
C
by the central subgroup generated by the hyperelliptic involution.
Let
p
be a prime such that
p\geq 5
. We consider whether
C_{\lambda}
has a superspecial reduction at
p
or not.
Here, a curve
C
over a field
K
is
supersupecial
if its Jacobian variety is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves over
\overline{K}
, the algebraic closure.
First, for a fixed
p
, let

{\psi}_{p}:=\#\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,\pm 1\}\mid C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\}.

Next, for a fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
and a curve
C_{\lambda}
, let

{\phi}_{\lambda}(X):=\#\{p<X\mid C_{\lambda}\text{ has a superspecial reduction at }p\}.

We determined
\psi_{p}
and used it to compute
\sum_{\lvert\lambda\rvert\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)
for
N\geq X^{1+\epsilon}
. The first theorem determines
\psi_{p}
in terms of class numbers
h(-p)
and
h(-4p)
.

Theorem A
.

Let
p
be prime with
p\geq 5
. Then we have

(1)

{\psi}_{p}=h(-4p)
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
,

(2)

{\psi}_{p}=6h(-p)-2
if
p\equiv 3\bmod 8
,

(3)

{\psi}_{p}=2h(-p)-2
if
p\equiv 7\bmod 8
.

The proof uses the result of Ibukiyama, Katsura and Oort
[
9
, §1.2]
that
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if two elliptic curves

E_{\Lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)\left(x+(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}\right)

(1)

and

E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}}:y^{2}=x(x-1)\left(x+(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}\right)

(2)

are supersingular. We shall make a one-to-one correspondence between a pair of superspecal curves
\{C_{\lambda},C_{-\lambda}\}
to a pair of supersingular elliptic curves
\{E_{\Lambda},E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}}\}
, and determine the value of
{\psi}_{p}
, see Section
4
. This enables us to reduce the problem to that on elliptic curves.
However,
Theorem
A
does not immediately follow from known results for elliptic curves. The difficulties arise from the fact that the description as in (
1
) and (
2
) requires the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and that the set of their
j
-invariants is a proper subset of the set of supersingular
j
-invariants since the elliptic curves are in a specific form.
Thus, we needed elaborate arguments to obtain Theorem
A
, see Section
3
. As for the enumeration of superspecial curves in a specific form, this result is reminiscent of the result by Auer and Top
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]
on the enumeration of supersingular elliptic curves in the Legendre form.

In the second theorem, we computed
\sum_{\lvert\lambda\rvert\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)
using the result of Theorem A.

Theorem B
.

Let
X,\epsilon
be positive numbers, and
N
be a positive number which satisfies
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
. Then, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\lvert\lambda\rvert\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)\sim\frac{3\pi}{2}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

We used the idea from Fourvy and Murty. First of all, we changed the order of taking sum in the following way.

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<X\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{p<X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|\leq N\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1

(3)

If
\lambda\equiv{\lambda}^{\prime}\bmod p
, then
C_{\lambda}
and
C_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
are the same curve in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and in particular, if one is superspecial, the other is also superspecial. Hence, for every
p\geq 5
, we describe
the inner sum of the right hand side of (
3
) in terms of
{\psi}_{p}
.
From the result of Theorem
A
, we can express
\sum_{|\lambda|\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)
with the class numbers, and we can calculate it by using the class number formula.

In addition to Theorem
B
, we have the similar result for
\lambda
running through rational numbers whose height is not exceeding a
positive number
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
. Here, for
\lambda=b/a
with coprime integers
a,b
, the
height
of
\lambda
is defined to be

\text{max}\{|a|,|b|\}
, denoted by
0pt(\lambda)
.

Theorem C
.

Let
X,\epsilon
be positive numbers, and
N
be a positive number which satisfies
N>X^{1+\epsilon}
. Then,

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)\sim\frac{9}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

For each
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we calculate the number of
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
0pt(\lambda)\leq N
and
\lambda=t\bmod p
. Then we use the result of Theorem B to deduce Theorem C.

Here is an outline of this paper. In section 2, we recall some properties of supersingular elliptic curves and genus-two curves, which we will use in the proof of Theorem

. In addition, we also recall an analytic result that we will use in the proof of Theorem
B
. In Section 3, we express the number of supersingular elliptic curves in a specific form with the class numbers

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some known results on supersingular elliptic curves and their relations with the class numbers. We also review some properties of genus-two curves. Moreover, we recall an analytic result for the Legendre symbol.

2.1
Supersingular Legendre elliptic curves

We review some results in
[
2
]
by Auer and Top about elliptic curves and we describe relations between supersingular elliptic curves and class numbers.

Let
E_{\lambda}
be a Legendre elliptic curve
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We assume
j(E_{\lambda})\neq 0
.
We determine which Legendre elliptic curves are isomorphic to
E_{\lambda}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We say an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is Legendre isomorphic if
E
is isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to a Legendre elliptic curve
E_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Proposition 2.1

(Auer and Top
[
2
, Lemma 3.1.]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime satisfying
p>3
and
p\bmod 4=3
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with j-invariant
j(E)\neq 0
. If
E
is Legendre isomorphic, then there are exactly 3 values of
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}
such that
E\cong E_{\lambda}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. More precisely speaking, we have

•

E_{-1}\cong E_{2}\cong E_{1/2}
;

•

if
\lambda\neq-1,2,1/2
, then
E_{\lambda}\ncong E_{1-\lambda},E_{1/\lambda}\ncong E_{1-1/\lambda},E_{1/(\lambda-1)}\ncong E_{\lambda/(\lambda-1)}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and

E_{\lambda}\cong E_{1/\lambda}\cong E_{1/(1-\lambda)}\
if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
and
\lambda-1\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
,

E_{\lambda}\cong E_{1/\lambda}\cong E_{\lambda/(\lambda-1)}\

if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
and
\lambda-1\notin{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
,

E_{\lambda}\cong E_{1-1/\lambda}\cong E_{1/(1-\lambda)}\
if
\lambda\notin{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
and
\lambda-1\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
,

E_{\lambda}\cong E_{1-1/\lambda}\cong E_{\lambda/(\lambda-1)}\

if
\lambda\notin{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
and
\lambda-1\notin{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
.

The next lemma tells us when
\lambda
and
\lambda-1
belong to
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{2}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Auer and Top
[
2
, Lemma 2.1.]
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}
. Then,

(\lambda,0)\in[2]E(\mathbb{F}_{p})\text{ if and only if }\lambda,\lambda-1\in\left({\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{*}\right)^{2}.

The next proposition says if
E_{\lambda}
is supersingular, then
\lambda
is in a specific form.

Proposition 2.3

(Auer and Top
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.]
)
.

Let
p\geq 3
be a prime and
q
be a power of
p
. Let
E_{\lambda}
be a supersingular elliptic curve with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}
. Then
-\lambda\in\left({{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}}^{*}\right)^{8}
.

We also review the enumeration of supersingular elliptic curves up to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-isomorphism by the class numbers. Let
p>3
be a prime. It is known that for a supersingular elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the endomorphism ring
{\textrm{End}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]
or
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-p}}{2}\right]
. Moreover, it is known that if
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
which satisfy

{\textrm{End}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]
is equal to
h(-4p)
, and if
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
which satisfy
{\textrm{End}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{-p}}{2}\right]
is equal to
h(-p)
(cf. Delfs and Galbraith
[
5
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.7]
).

By the fact described above and Proposition
2.1
, we have the folloing Auer and Top’s result.
Let
T_{p}:=\{s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}\mid E_{s}\text{ is supersingular}\}
.

Proposition 2.4

(Auer and Top
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.]
)
.

Let
p>3
be a prime satisfying
p\bmod 4=3
. Then
\#T_{p}=3h(-p)
.

We can also deduce the next lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

The number of the set of
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p}

such that
y^{2}=x(x-\mu)(x-\overline{\mu})
is supersingular is
(p-1)h(-4p)
.

Proof.

Having the same discussion with Auer and Top
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.]
, we see that if an elliptic curve
E:y^{2}=x(x-\mu)(x-\overline{\mu})
with
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p}
is supersingualr, the endomorphism ring of
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies
{\text{End}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]
, and conversely, if a supersingular elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
has an endomorphism ring over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfying
{\text{End}}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-p}]
, then
E
can be given by an equation
y^{2}=x(x-\mu)(x-\overline{\mu})
with
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Since two elliptic curves
E_{1}:y^{2}=x(x-\mu)(x-\overline{\mu})
and
E_{2}:y^{2}=x(x-\nu)(x-\overline{\nu})
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if and only if
\mu=u^{2}\nu
or
\mu=u^{2}\overline{\nu}
for some
u\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{*}
, we conclude that
the number of the set of
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p}

such that
y^{2}=x(x-\mu)(x-\overline{\mu})
is supersingular is

2\cdot\#\left({\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{*}\right)^{2}\cdot h(-4p)=(p-1)h(-4p)
.
∎

2.2
Genus-2 curves

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
\neq 2
and
\overline{K}
the algebraic closure of
K
. We review some facts on genus-two curves. Igusa
[
10
]
classified curves of genus two into 7 types as follows:

(0)

y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-{\lambda}_{1})(x-{\lambda}_{2})(x-{\lambda}_{3})

(1)

y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)(x-\mu)\left(x-\lambda\frac{1-\mu}{1-\lambda}\right)

(2)

y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)\left(x-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)\left(x-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\right)

(3)

y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-\lambda)\left(x-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)

(4)

(p\neq 3,5)\ y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)

(5)

y^{2}=x(x^{2}-1)(x^{2}+1)

(6)

(p\neq 5)\ y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-1-\zeta)(x-1-\zeta-{\zeta}^{2})(x-1-\zeta-{\zeta}^{2}-{\zeta}^{3})
, where
\zeta
is a primitive fifth root of unity

We focus on a genus-2 curves of type (3):

C_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x+1)(x-{\lambda})\left(x-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)

and recall some properties shown by Ibukibara, Katsura and Oort in
[
9
]
.

For each
\lambda\in K
, we choose a square root (in
\overline{K}
) of
\lambda^{2}-1
, fix it and write it as
\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}
.
For the curve
C_{\lambda}
, we consider two elliptic curves:

E_{\Lambda(\lambda)}:Y^{2}=X(X-1)(X+(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2})
,

E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)}:Y^{2}=X(X-1)(X+(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2})
,

where we denote
\Lambda(\lambda):=-(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}
and
{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda):=-(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}
.

Then we have morphisms of degree two from
C_{\lambda}
to the elliptic curves.

Proposition 2.6

(Ibukiyama, Katsura and Oort
[
9
, §1.2]
)
.

The morphism
f_{1}:C_{\lambda}\longrightarrow E_{\Lambda(\lambda)}
sending
(x,y)
to

\left(-\left(\lambda x+\frac{({\lambda}^{2}-1)x}{x-\lambda}\right),\frac{\epsilon{\lambda}^{\frac{3}{2}}(x-(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1}))}{(x-\lambda)^{2}}y\right),

and the morphism
f_{2}:C_{\lambda}\longrightarrow E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)}
sending
(x,y)
to

\left(-\left(\lambda x+\frac{({\lambda}^{2}-1)x}{x-\lambda}\right),\frac{\epsilon{\lambda}^{\frac{3}{2}}(x-(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1}))}{(x-\lambda)^{2}}y\right)

are well-defined and are of degree two,
where
\epsilon
is a square root of
-1

and
{\lambda}^{3/2}
is a square root of
\lambda^{3}
.

Assume that the characteristic of
K
is greater than
2
.
The next proposition enables us to reduce the problem on the superspeciality of
C_{\lambda}
to that on
E_{\Lambda(\lambda)}
and
E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)}
.

Proposition 2.7

(Ibukiyama, Katsura and Oort
[
9
, Lemma 1.1 (ii), Proposition 1.3 (ii), (iii)]
)
.

The curve
C_{\lambda}
is superspecial if and only if the two associated elliptic cuves
E_{\Lambda(\lambda)}
and
E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)}
are supersingular.

2.3
An analytic result

We review a result on a sum of the Legendre symbols weighted by the Mangoldt function
\Lambda(n)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

S(D,X):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq D}\left|\sum_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|,

where the star on the summation indicates that
d
is not square. Then for every
C>0
and
3\leq D\leq X^{\frac{49}{50}}
, we have

S(D,X)\ll XD(\log X)^{-C}.

In addition, we also have

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq D}\left|\ \sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|\ll XD(\log X)^{-C},

where the star on the summation indicates that
d
is non square integer and the prime indicates that
n\equiv 3\bmod 4
.

Moreover, we have for
3\leq 2D\leq X^{\frac{49}{50}}

\sideset{}{{}^{**}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq D}\left|\ \sideset{}{{}^{\prime\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|\ll 2XD(\log X)^{-C}.

where the double star on the summation indicates that we sum over integers
d
such that
d
and
d/2
are not square and the double prime indicates that
n\equiv 3\bmod 8
. We have the same result if we sum over
n\equiv 7\bmod 8
.

Proof.

For the first part of the lemma, see Jutila
[
11
, Lemma 8]
.
For the second part of the lemma, see Fouvy and Murty
[
7
, Lemma 6]
. We will prove the last part of the lemma. We see

\displaystyle\left|\ \sideset{}{{}^{\prime\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{2d}{n}\right)\right|.

Since

\sideset{}{{}^{**}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq D}\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{2d}{n}\right)\right|\leq\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq 2D}\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|,

we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{**}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq D}\left|\ \sideset{}{{}^{\prime\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|\leq\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0\leq d\leq 2D}\left|\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{3\leq n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)\right|\ll 2XD(\log X)^{-C}.

∎

3
The number of supersingular elliptic curves and the class numbers

In this section, we express the number of supersingular elliptic curves in a specific form with the class numbers
h(-p),h(-4p)
.
We consider the following sets related to supersingular elliptic curves, which we will use in the proof of Theorem A.
We use the notation in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
, for example
\Lambda(\lambda):=-(\lambda-\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}
and
{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda):=-(\lambda+\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}
, and
E_{s}
denotes the Legendre elliptic curve given by the equation
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-s)
.

S_{p}:=\{{\mu}^{p-1}\mid\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p},E_{{\mu}^{p-1}}\text{ is supersingular}\}
,

T_{p}:=\{s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}\mid E_{s}\text{ is supersingular}\}
,

T^{\prime}_{p}:=T_{p}\smallsetminus\{-1\}
,

U_{p}:=\{-t^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}\mid t\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\ E_{-t^{2}}\text{ is supersingular}\}
,

U^{\prime}_{p}:=U_{p}\smallsetminus\{-1\}
,

{\Theta}_{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-(\lambda\pm\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1})^{2}\end{array}\middle|\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\smallsetminus\{0,\ \pm 1\},\\
\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}-1}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p},\\
E_{\Lambda(\lambda)},E_{{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)}\text{ are supersingular}\end{array}\right\}
.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
\overline{\mu}
be the conjugate of
\mu
over

-(\mu-\overline{\mu})^{2}/\mu\overline{\mu}

Proof.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\left(X^{2}+2(\mu+\overline{\mu}\right)X+\left(\mu-\overline{\mu})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\left(X-\left(-(\mu+\overline{\mu})+2\sqrt{\mu\overline{\mu}}\right)\right)\left(X-\left(-(\mu+\overline{\mu})-2\sqrt{\mu\overline{\mu}}\right)\right).

Proof of Theorem C

We will prove Theorem C in Introduction. To show Theorem C, We will calculate the number of
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
whose height is not exceeding
N
and satisfying
\lambda=t\bmod p
for each
t=0,1,\ldots,p-1
.

We take a positive
\lambda=a/b\in\mathbb{Q}
with positive coprime integers
a,b
. Assume
0pt(\lambda)\leq N
. Then we have
0\leq a\leq N
and
1\leq b\leq N
. It is easy to see that
a/b=t\bmod p
if and only if
b
is not divisible by
p
and
a-bt=kp
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We fix
b
that is not divisible by
p
and we will calculate the number of positive irreducible fractions
\lambda=a/b>0
satisfying
\lambda=t\bmod p
.

If
b=1
, then it is equal to
N/p+O(1)
. If
b\geq 2
, then we have
k\neq 0
since if
a
and
b
are relatively prime. Since
a
satisfies
0\leq a\leq N
, we have
-bt/p\leq k\leq(N-bt)/p
. It is easy to see that
a
and
b
are relatively prime if and only if
k
and
b
are relatively prime. Then we count the number of such
k
, which is

\sum_{d\mid b}\mu(d)\frac{N}{pd}+(\text{ error terms }).

Here,
\mu
is the Möbius function, and the error terms grow no faster than the number of divisors of
b
. If we denote the number of divisors of
b
by
d(b)
, we see that
\sum_{b\leq N}d(b)\sim N\log N
.

Then we find that
\#\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\mid\lambda>0,\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N,\ \lambda=t\bmod p\}
is

\frac{N}{p}+\frac{N}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq b\leq N\\
p\nmid b\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}+O(N\log N).

We will calculate the sum of
\varphi(b)/b
. We know that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq b\leq N\\
p\nmid b\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}=\sum_{2\leq b\leq N}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq b\leq N\\
p\mid b\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}.

We use Abel’s identity and the fact that
\sum_{n\leq x}\varphi(n)=\frac{3}{{\pi}^{2}}x^{2}+O(x\log x)
for
x>1
to find that

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq b\leq N}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{b\leq N}\varphi(b)}{N}-1+\int_{1}^{N}\frac{\sum_{b\leq T}\varphi(b)}{T^{2}}dT

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{6}{{\pi}^{2}}N+O((\log N)^{2})

and that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq b\leq N\\
p\mid b\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(b)}{b}=\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{N}{p}}\frac{\varphi(lp)}{lp}\leq\sum_{1\leq l\leq\frac{N}{p}}\frac{\varphi(l)}{l}=\frac{6}{{\pi}^{2}}\frac{N}{p}+O((\log N)^{2}).

Then we have

\#\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\mid 0pt(\lambda)\leq N,\ \lambda=t\bmod p\}=\frac{12}{{\pi}^{2}}\frac{N^{2}}{p}+o(N^{2})+O\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2}}\right).

From the calculation above, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{ht}(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p<X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ht}(\lambda)\leq N\\
C_{\lambda}\text{ is superspecial}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p<X}\left(\frac{12}{{\pi}^{2}}\frac{N^{2}}{p}+o(N^{2})+O\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\right){\psi}_{p}.

A calculation shows that the error terms are ultimately negligible compared to
N^{2}\sqrt{X}/\log X
. Thus, from the result of Theorem A, we find that

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{9}{\pi}\frac{N^{2}\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{N^{2}\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

Hence we have

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q},\ 0pt(\lambda)\leq N}{\phi}_{\lambda}(X)=\frac{9}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}+o\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}\right).

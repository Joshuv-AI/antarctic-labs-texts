K ​ 3 K3 Elliptic Genus and an Umbral Moonshine Module
Vassilis Anagiannis, Miranda C. N. Cheng, Sarah M. Harrison

Abstract
Umbral moonshine connects the symmetry groups of the 23 Niemeier lattices with 23 sets of distinguished mock modular forms. The 23 cases of umbral moonshine have a uniform relation to symmetries of K ​ 3 K3 string theories. Moreover, a supersymmetric vertex operator algebra with Conway sporadic symmetry also enjoys a close relation to the K ​ 3 K3 elliptic genus. Inspired by the above two relations between moonshine and K ​ 3 K3 string theory, we construct a chiral CFT by orbifolding the free theory of 24 chiral fermions and two pairs of fermionic and bosonic ghosts. In this paper we mainly focus on the case of umbral moonshine corresponding to the Niemeier lattice with root system given by 6 copies of D 4 D_{4} root system. This CFT then leads to the construction of an infinite-dimensional graded module for the umbral group G D 4 ⊕ 6 G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}} whose graded characters coincide with the umbral moonshine functions. We also comment on how one can recover all umbral moonshine functions corresponding to the Niemeier root systems A 5 ⊕ 4 ​ D 4 A_{5}^{\oplus 4}D_{4} , A 7 ⊕ 2 ​ D 5 ⊕ 2 A_{7}^{\oplus 2}D_{5}^{\oplus 2} , A 11 ​ D 7 ​ E 6 A_{11}D_{7}E_{6} , A 17 ​ E 7 A_{17}E_{7} , and D 10 ​ E 7 ⊕ 2 D_{10}E_{7}^{\oplus 2} .

The moonshine phenomenon, the study of which began with the discovery of monstrous moonshine
[
1

Recent years have seen a surge of interest in moonshine, initiated by the observation

It was soon realised that this

Recall that there are 24 inequivalent positive-definite, even unimodular lattices with rank 24. Among them, the Leech lattice
\Lambda_{\text{Leech}}

For each of the 23

More explicitly, the main conjecture of umbral moonshine states that there exists an naturally-defined

-graded

-module

K^{X}_{r}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\\
D=-r^{2}\rm{mod}~4m\end{subarray}}K^{X}_{r,D}

H^{X}_{g,r}(\tau)=-2\,q^{-{1\over 4m}}\,\delta_{r^{2},1(4m)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\\
D=-r^{2}\rm{mod}~4m\end{subarray}}q^{{D\over 4m}}{\rm{Tr}}_{K^{X}_{r,D}}(g)~

The other components are then determined by the property that, when combined with the index

The existence of the umbral moonshine modules

To the best of our knowledge, a uniform construction of all 23 umbral moonshine modules, which is clearly desirable given the uniform construction of the moonshine relation itself, is not yet in sight.

2.1
Umbral Twining Genera

For each case of umbral moonshine corresponding to the Niemeier lattice
N^{X}
there is a natural way to attach a weak Jacobi form of weight zero and index one to each
g\in G^{X}

[
15
]
.
This weak Jacobi form
\phi^{X}_{g}
constitutes two parts: one is the (twined) elliptic genus of the SCFT describing the surface singularities corresponding to the root system
X
, and the second comes from the contribution of the umbral moonshine mock modular forms.

We start by reviewing the first part, the singularity elliptic genus.
The type of singularities a
K3
surface can develop are given by the so-called du Val or Kleinein singularities, which admit an ADE classification. These singularities are isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a finite subgroup of
SU(2)
as in the McKay correspondence.
Let
m
denote the Coxeter number of the corresponding root system.
Recall that
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal minimal models (which have spectral flow symmetries) also admit an ADE classification
[
23
]
, and the central charge
c:=3\hat{c}
is given by

\hat{c}=1-{2\over m}.

(2.1)

The classification of these supersymmetric minimal models stems from the classification of modular invariant combinations of left- and right-moving characters of affine
{\mathfrak{s}l}_{2}
, given in terms of
a
2m\times 2m
matrix, which we denote by
\Omega^{\Phi}
for the minimal model corresponding to the simply-laced root system
\Phi
. The explicit expression for
\Omega^{\Phi}
can be found in
[
23
]
.
In terms of these matrices, the elliptic genus of the super minimal model is given by
[
24
]

Z_{\text{minimal}}^{\Phi}(\tau,\zeta)=\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}/2m\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{\Phi}_{r,r^{\prime}}\tilde{\chi}^{r}_{r^{\prime}}(\tau,\zeta)={\text{Tr}(\Omega^{\Phi}\cdot\tilde{\chi})},

(2.2)

where
\tilde{\chi}^{r}_{s}(\tau,\zeta)

, are corresponding minimal model characters.

In
[
16

\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
was proposed to
be given by an
{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
–orbifold of the corresponding supersymmetric minimal model tensored with a non-compact CFT, and
takes the form

\left({\cal N}=2~\text{minimal }\otimes{\cal N}=2~\left(\frac{\operatorname{\textsl{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}{U(1)}\right)_{m}~{\text{coset}}\right)/{{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}},

(2.3)

where the second factor,
the
\left(\frac{\operatorname{\textsl{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}{U(1)}\right)_{m}

describes the geometry of a semi-infinite cigar
[
25

and has central charge

\hat{c}=1+\frac{2}{m}.

The spectrum of the theory contains a discrete part as well as a continuous part; the latter exists due to the fact that the theory is non-compact and gives a non-holomorphic (in the
\tau

[
17

Both mathematically and physically, there is a well-defined way to isolate the holomorphic part of the elliptic genus, which we denote by

In the above equation, we make use of the Ramond character graded by

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}q^{a^{2}}y^{2a}\,Z_{\rm minimal}^{\Phi}(\tau,\zeta+a\tau+b)Z_{L_{m}}(\tau,\zeta+a\tau+b).

with the same Coxeter number, we write

Now we discuss the second part of the weak Jacobi form

It is shown in

2.2
Conway Twining Genera

In

The Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond sectors of the original theory before orbifolding split into

It was then shown that

Given a fixed

is non-vanishing if and only if it fixes exactly a
4
-plane. In the latter case,
D_{g}
is determined up to a sign by the eigenvalues of
g
, since we are free to exchange what we call
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}^{-1}
. As a result, for exactly four-plane preserving elements there are in fact two choices of
\phi_{g}
depending on the choice of the sign of
D_{g}
, and we define

\begin{split}\phi_{\epsilon,g}(\tau,\zeta)&:=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)^{2}}\frac{\eta_{-g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{-g}(\tau)}-\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)^{2}}\frac{\eta_{g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{g}(\tau)}\right.\\
&~~~~~~~~~~\left.-\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)^{2}}C_{-g}\eta_{-g}(\tau)-\epsilon~\frac{\theta_{1}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{6}}\left|D_{g}\right|\eta_{g}(\tau)\right].\end{split}

(2.14)

where
\epsilon=\pm 1
encodes the sign ambiguity of
D_{g}
.
In all cases, it was shown that

\phi_{g}
are Jacobi forms of weight
0
and index
1

that fixes at least a 4-plane. As discussed in §

K3

3
The Chiral CFT

In this section we present the construction of a chiral CFT
{\cal T}
, by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-orbifolding a free theory consisting of 12 complex chiral fermions, 2 fermionic and 2 bosonic ghost systems.
Its symmetries accommodate the umbral group we are interested in, and its twined partition functions reproduce (among others) the weak Jacobi forms
\phi_{g}
reviewed in §
2
.

3.1
The Fermions

The first ingredient to build our chiral theory
{\cal T}
is 24 real chiral fermions
\tilde{\psi}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\psi}_{24}
, similar to the starting point of the Conway module discussed in §
2.2
.
Equivalently, this theory, which we call
\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
, is given by 12 complex chiral fermions,
\psi_{i}^{\pm}:={1\over\sqrt{2}}(\tilde{\psi}_{i}\pm i\tilde{\psi}_{i+12})
, with the action

\displaystyle S_{\psi}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^{2}z\sum_{i=1}^{12}\left(\psi^{+}_{i}\bar{\partial}\psi^{-}_{i}+\psi^{-}_{i}\bar{\partial}\psi^{+}_{i}\right).

(3.1)

Their OPEs take the form

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)\psi^{\pm}_{j}(z^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{O}(z-z^{\prime}),~~~\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)\psi^{\mp}_{j}(z^{\prime})\sim\frac{\delta_{ij}}{z-z^{\prime}}.

(3.2)

The associated Viraroso operator is given by

\displaystyle L^{\psi}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L^{\psi}_{n}z^{-n-2}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}\mathrel{\left(\psi^{+}_{i}\partial\psi^{-}_{i}+\psi^{-}_{i}\partial\psi^{+}_{i}\right)}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}},

with respect to which
\psi^{\pm}_{i}
are holomorphic primary fields with weight
1/2
.
The open dots denote the regular part of the associated expression; we refer to this as the canonical ordering. In terms of modes, it means that the annihilators are always put to the right.
By expanding the fields in modes,

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)=\sum_{r}\psi^{\pm}_{i,r}~z^{-r-\frac{1}{2}},

(3.7)

the OPEs lead to the standard anti-commutation relations

\displaystyle\left\{\psi^{\pm}_{i,r}\psi^{\pm}_{j,s}\right\}=0~,~~~\left\{\psi^{\pm}_{i,r}\psi^{\mp}_{j,s}\right\}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{r+s,0}.

(3.8)

The
{\operatorname{\textsl{SL}}}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
-invariant vacuum
|0\rangle
satisfies the usual highest weight condition

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i,r}|0\rangle=0~~~\forall~r>0.

(3.9)

Note that here the canonical ordering coincides with the normal ordering corresponding to the above canonical vacuum, where the positive modes are annihilators.

To compute the characters of the theory,
consider the conformal mapping from the complex plane to the cylinder given by
z=e^{w}
.
We denote the Virasoro zero mode on the cylinder by

\displaystyle L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}:=L^{\psi}_{0}-\frac{c_{\psi}}{24}

(3.10)

where
c_{\psi}=12
is the central charge.
Consider general boundary conditions parametrized by
\rho

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i}(w+2\pi i)=e^{\mp 2\pi i\rho}\psi^{\pm}_{i}(w).

(3.11)

The periodic (
P
,
\rho=0
) and anti-periodic (
A
,
\rho=1/2
) cases correspond to the usual Ramond and NS sectors. Note that the
\psi^{\pm}_{i}
must acquire opposite phases as in (
3.11

The
A
sector Hilbert space
\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{A}}
is built by acting on the ground state
|0\rangle
with the creation operators
\psi^{\pm}_{i,r}
with
r\leq-1/2
, and its character is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{\psi}^{\text{A}}(\tau):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{A}}}\left[q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=q^{-1/2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{12}(1+q^{n-1/2})^{2}=\left(\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}.

(3.12)

We also define an operator
(-1)^{F}
which has the property that it anticommutes with all the fermionic modes, squares to the identity, and acts trivially on
|0\rangle
.
Note that
(-1)^{F}
commutes with the Virasoro operator and hence we can define the following character,

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{A}}(\tau):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{A}}}\left[(-1)^{F}q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=q^{-1/2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{12}(1-q^{n-1/2})^{2}=\left(\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}.

(3.13)

In the
P
sector
\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{P}}
, the ground states form a
2^{12}
-dimensional representation of the
24
-dimensional Clifford algebra. Explicitly, a basis can be given by the mononomials

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i_{1},0}\cdots\psi^{-}_{i_{k},0}|s\rangle,

(3.14)

where we single out
|s\rangle
to be annihilated by all the
\psi^{+}_{i,0}
and we require that
(-1)^{F}
acts trivially on
|s\rangle
.
The conformal weight of the
P
ground states is equal to
\frac{24}{16}=\frac{3}{2}
, as each of the complex fermions (along with its complex conjugate) contributes
\frac{2}{16}
due to the presence of twist fields that interpolate between the
A
and
P
sectors.
Putting things together, we obtain the following
P
sector characters.

\displaystyle\chi_{\psi}^{\text{P}}(\tau):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{P}}}\left[q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=2^{12}q\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{12}(1+q^{n})^{2}=\left(\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{P}}(\tau):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\text{P}}\left[(-1)^{F}q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=\left(\frac{\theta_{1}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}=0.

(3.16)

The latter vanishes because half of the ground states have
-1
eigenvalue under
(-1)^{F}
, while the rest have
+1
.

Later we will consider an orbifold of
{\cal T}_{\psi}
by a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
generated by
\xi
, which acts on the fermions by

\displaystyle\xi\psi^{\pm}_{i}=-\psi^{\pm}_{i},

(3.17)

and trivially on the
A
ground state.
Note that any state with an odd (resp. even) number of excitations is an eigenstate of
\xi
with eigenvalue
-1
(resp.
+1
), and hence
\xi
acts in exactly the same way as
(-1)^{F}
on the quantum states in both
A
and
P
sectors.
Therefore we conclude that

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{A}}}\left[\xi~q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{A}}(\tau)=\left(\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{P}}}\left[\xi~q^{L^{\psi}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right]=\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{P}}(\tau)=0.

(3.19)

The Ghosts

The next ingredients we need are the fermionic and bosonic ghost systems (see

They are described by the action

\displaystyle S_{\text{gh}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z~\mathbf{b}\bar{\partial}\mathbf{c},

where
\mathbf{b}

The OPEs between the ghost fields have the form

(3.25)

(3.26)

Consequently, the canonical ordering does not generally coincide with the usual normal ordering.
As before, we consider the

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(w+2\pi i)=e^{-2\pi i\rho}\mathbf{b}(w),~~~\mathbf{c}(w+2\pi i)=e^{2\pi i\rho}\mathbf{c}(w),

The natural ground states on the cylinder are defined as the states annihilated by all positive modes,
{\bf b}_{r},\mathbf{c}_{r}

\displaystyle J=-\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}\mathrel{\mathbf{b}\mathbf{c}}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}.

(3.32)

Next we derive the characters of the ghost systems, defined by

\displaystyle\chi_{a}^{\text{S}}(\tau,\zeta):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\text{S}}\left[y^{J_{\text{cyl},0}}~q^{L^{\text{gh}}_{\text{cyl},0}}\right],

(3.33)

where

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{F}}^{\text{A}}(\tau,\zeta)=q^{-1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+yq^{n-1/2}\right)\left(1+y^{-1}q^{n-1/2}\right)=\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta)}{\eta(\tau)}.

(3.34)

Similarly, by accounting for all possible states in the Fock space created by the negative integral modes of the ghost fields

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{F}}^{\text{P}}(\tau,\zeta)=q^{1/12}\left(y^{1/2}+y^{-1/2}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+yq^{n}\right)\left(1+y^{-1}q^{n}\right)=\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta)}{\eta(\tau)}.

(3.35)

For the bosonic ghost system, the

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{B}}^{\text{A}}(\tau,\zeta)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-yq^{n-1/2}\right)^{-1}\left(1-y^{-1}q^{n-1/2}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\eta(\tau)}{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta)},

(3.37)

3.3
The Orbifold Theory

After describing the basic ingredients, we now put them together and construct the chiral CFT that will reproduce the K3 elliptic genus and its twinings. Let
\cal{T}_{\text{B}}
denote the theory of 2 copies of the bosonic ghost system and the theory of 2 copies of the fermionic ghost system. We will later see that
{\cal T}
can be equipped with an
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal symmetry for certain choices of
h_{\text{B}}
and
h_{\text{F}}
.
We will let
h_{\text{B}}=\frac{1}{2}
and
h_{\text{F}}=1
, corresponding to the total central charge
c_{\mathcal{T}}=-4-2+12=6
.

We want to consider a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
orbifold of the theory

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\text{free}}=\mathcal{T}_{\text{B}}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\text{F}}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\psi},

(3.40)

where

\mathcal{T}=\left(\mathcal{T}_{\text{B},\text{A}}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\text{F},\text{A}}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{A}}^{0}\right)\oplus\left(\mathcal{T}_{\text{B},\text{P}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\text{F},\text{P}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{\psi,\text{P}}^{1}\right).

(3.41)

Introducing chemical potentials

&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}-\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}-\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{1}(\tau,\zeta_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}\right].\end{split}

(3.43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textbf{EG}(\tau,\zeta;K3)=Z\left(\tau,\frac{1}{2},\zeta\right)=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}}\left[(-1)^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}~y^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}~q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}-\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}-\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^{12}\right].\end{split}

, precisely the superconformal symmetry of
K3
non-linear sigma models.

If the conformal weights of a pairs of
bc-\beta\gamma
ghost system satisfy
h_{\text{F}}=h_{\text{B}}+\tfrac{1}{2}
, there exists an
\mathcal{N}=1
current of weight
3/2
. In our case with two pairs of
bc-\beta\gamma
ghosts, it is given by

G=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial\beta_{j}c_{j}+\frac{2h_{\text{F}}-1}{2}\partial(\beta_{j}c_{j})-2b_{j}\gamma_{j}\right).

(3.45)

To enhance this to
\mathcal{N}=4
, we need an
SU(2)
subalgebra generated by
J_{i}
with
i=1,2,3
. One can show that such currents are given by

J_{1}=\frac{i}{2}(\beta_{1}\gamma_{2}-\beta_{2}\gamma_{1})~,~~~J_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{1}\gamma_{2}+\beta_{2}\gamma_{1})~,~~~J_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}\mathrel{\beta_{1}\gamma_{1}-\beta_{2}\gamma_{2}}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}.

(3.46)

From now on we will choose

h_{\text{F}}=1~,~~h_{\text{B}}=\frac{1}{2},

(3.47)

so that
SU(2)
cuurent algebra is given by

J_{i}(z)J_{j}(w)\sim\frac{\delta_{ij}~k_{\text{gh}}/2}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{i\epsilon_{ijk}J_{k}(w)}{z-w}.

(3.48)

with
k_{\text{gh}}=-1
.
Acting with the generators
J_{i}
on

(with
h_{\text{F}}=1
) to construct the rest of the supercurrents, we get

\begin{split}J_{i}(z)G(w)\sim-\frac{i}{2}\frac{1}{z-w}G_{i}(w).\end{split}

(3.49)

One can check that, together with the Virasoro field

L^{\text{gh}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}}\mathrel{-b_{i}\partial c_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\partial\beta_{i}\gamma_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{i}\partial\gamma_{i}}\mathrel{\vbox{\halign{#\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\kern 3.01385pt\vbox{\hbox{\circle{1.5}}}\kern 0.86108pt$\cr}}},

(3.50)

the fields
G
,
G_{i}
and
J_{i}
indeed form an
\mathcal{N}=4
SCA with central charge
c_{\text{gh}}=-6
and level
k_{\text{gh}}=-1
. As in
[
40
]
we can further define

G_{1}^{\pm}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(G\pm iG_{3}),~~G_{2}^{\pm}:=\pm\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(G_{1}\pm iG_{2}),

(3.51)

which transform in the representation
\mathbf{2}+\bar{\mathbf{2}}
of
SU(2)
, and reproduce the standard small
\mathcal{N}=4
SCA.

We note that the supercurrents, as well as the
J_{i}
, survive the orbifold, since they are all bilinears in the ghost fields.
In the remaining part of the paper we will however not make use of this superconformal symmetry. In particular, we have used the grading by the ghost
U(1)
-currents instead of the
R
-current in defining the (twined) partition function (
3.42
) and in §
3.4
.
Especially, different from the construction in
[
39
]
, where the grading is with respect to a
U(1)
current built from a choice of four real fermions, the symmetry groups of our theory
{\cal T}
are not
restricted to
be 4-plane preserving subgroups of
\textsl{Co}_{0}
.
The choice of the central charge of the ghost systems is in any case immaterial for the construction of our module, since the relevant characters do not depend on it.

3.4
The Twined Characters

As discussed in the previous subsection,
\mathcal{T}
has a manifest
\text{Spin}(24)
symmetry. We can thus consider the twined partition function

\displaystyle Z_{g}\left(\tau,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\right):=\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}}\left[g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right],

(3.52)

by an element
g\in\text{Spin}(24)
, which has a manifest action on
\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
(cf. §
2.2
) and acts trivially on the ghost systems.
We now specialize to the case
g\in\textsl{Co}_{0}<\text{Spin}(24)
, i.e. to elements that preserve the
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal structure of
\mathcal{T}_{\psi}
. Specifically, the relevant characters are twined as follows

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{A}}^{\text{free}}}\left[g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]=\frac{\eta_{-g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{-g}(\tau)}\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{3}\left(\tau,\zeta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}},

(3.53)

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{A}}^{\text{free}}}\left[\xi~g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]=-\frac{\eta_{g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{g}(\tau)}\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{4}\left(\tau,\zeta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}},

(3.54)

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{P}}^{\text{free}}}\left[g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]=-C_{-g}\eta_{-g}(\tau)\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{2}\left(\tau,\zeta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}},

(3.55)

\displaystyle\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{P}}^{\text{free}}}\left[\xi~g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]=q\nu\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{12}\left(1-\lambda_{i}q^{n}\right)\left(1-\lambda_{i}^{-1}q^{n-1}\right)\frac{\theta_{1}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\theta_{1}\left(\tau,\zeta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}.

(3.56)

where the factors
\theta_{i}(\tau,\zeta_{2})^{2}/\theta_{i}(\tau,\zeta_{1}-1/2)^{2}
originate from the ghosts contribution.

In order to make contact with K3 and the umbral module discussed in the next section, we further specialize to a subgroup
G
of
\textsl{Co}_{0}
, such that each
g\in G
generates a 4-plane preserving subgroup of
\textsl{Co}_{0}
.
Note that by requiring that
g\in G
is
4
-plane preserving does not imply in general that
G
is
4
-plane preserving. For instance, in the case
G\cong 3.S_{6}
that is of special interest for us, different
g\in 3.S_{6}
do not in general fix the same
4
-plane, and thus
3.S_{6}
does not preserve a
4
-plane.

Finally, we specialise the fugacities of the ghost currents to the values

\zeta_{2}=\zeta
and
\zeta_{1}=1/2
.
Note that care has to be taken when taking the
\zeta_{1}\to 1/2
limit in (
3.56
).
On the one hand, the degeneracy of A ground states in
{\cal T}_{\psi}
and the fact that at least two of the twelve pairs of
g
-eigenvalues are given by unity leads to a zero in the numerator. On the other hand, the infinite degeneracy of bosonic ghost ground states requires regularisation when taking
\zeta_{1}\to 1/2
. As a result, we regularise the partition function by introducing an adiabatic shift in boundary condition given by a small positive parameter
\eta
. We consider the boundary conditions
\rho=0+\eta
and
\rho=1/2+\eta
as in (
3.11
), (
3.27
), and compute the
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
limit of the partition function
Z^{\eta}_{g}\left(\tau,\frac{1}{2},\zeta\right)
with the regulator
\eta
present. This is straightforward for all the terms besides (
3.56
), which receives the following contributions

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{\chi}_{\text{B}}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau,1/2)^{2}=q^{-2/12}(1-q^{\eta})^{-2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{n+\eta}\right)^{-2}\left(1-q^{n-\eta}\right)^{-2},\\
&\tilde{\chi}_{\text{F}}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}=-q^{2/12}\left(iy^{1/2}q^{\delta}-iy^{-1/2}\right)^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-yq^{n+\eta}\right)^{2}\left(1-y^{-1}q^{n-\eta}\right)^{2},\\
&\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau)=q\nu\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{n-\eta}\right)^{2}\left(1-q^{n-1+\eta}\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{10}\left(1-\lambda_{i}q^{n-\eta}\right)\left(1-\lambda_{i}^{-1}q^{n-1+\eta}\right).\end{split}

(3.57)

We see that, upon multiplying the above expressions, the potentially problematic factors
(1-q^{\eta})^{\pm 2}
drop out and we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\operatorname{{tr}}_{\mathcal{T}_{\text{P}}^{\text{free}}}\left[\xi~g~y_{1}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{B}}}y_{2}^{J_{\text{cyl},0}^{\text{F}}}q^{L_{0}^{\text{tot}}-\frac{6}{24}}\right]&=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\tilde{\chi}_{\text{B}}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau,1/2)^{2}~\tilde{\chi}_{\text{F}}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}~\tilde{\chi}_{\psi}^{\text{P},\eta}(\tau)\\
&=\frac{\theta_{1}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{6}}D_{g}\eta_{g}.\end{split}

(3.58)

Putting everything together, we finally get

\begin{split}\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}Z_{g}^{\eta}\left(\tau,\frac{1}{2},\zeta\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\theta_{3}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{3}(\tau,0)^{2}}\frac{\eta_{-g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{-g}(\tau)}-\frac{\theta_{4}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{4}(\tau,0)^{2}}\frac{\eta_{g}(\tau/2)}{\eta_{g}(\tau)}\right.\\
&~~~~~~~~~~\left.-\frac{\theta_{2}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\theta_{2}(\tau,0)^{2}}C_{-g}\eta_{-g}(\tau)-\frac{\theta_{1}(\tau,\zeta)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{6}}D_{g}\eta_{g}(\tau)\right].\end{split}

(3.59)

We observe that this agrees with
\phi_{\epsilon,g}
as defined in (
2.11
) and (
2.14
), the Conway twining graded by a
k=1

U(1)
current. In particular, note that the same sign ambiguity in
D_{g}
in the twining of the Conway CFT described in §
2.2
is also present here, leading to the sign
\epsilon
in the definition of the twining functions.
As mentioned before, a crucial difference is that the
U(1)
grading in the
{\cal T}
is preserved by the
G
-action since it is constructed out of the ghost fields which the group acts trivially on.

4
The Module for
D_{4}^{\oplus 6}
Umbral Moonshine

The goal of the section is to explain how the ingredients in the previous sections lead to a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded infinite dimensional vector space admitting a
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
–action that underlies the
D_{4}^{\oplus 6}
case of umbral moonshine. In particular, we will describe
how the umbral mock modular forms
H^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}_{g}
for all elements
g
of the umbral group
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
are recovered from the twined partition functions of the chiral CFT
{\cal T}
. In §
4.1
we describe an explicit construction of the group. In §
4.2
we explain the action of the group on the BPS states of 6 copies of the CFT describing a singularity of
D_{4}
type.
In §
4.3
we combine the ingredients and give expressions for

in terms of these physical ingredients.

4.1
The Group

For completeness, in this subsection we describe a concrete realization of the group
3.S_{6}
, following
[
45
]
.
The hexacode is the unique three-dimensional code of length 6 over
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
that is Hermitian and self-dual.
It is the glue code of the Niemeier lattice
N^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
with root system
D_{4}^{\oplus 6}

[
46
]
, and for this reason it plays a significant role in the case of umbral moonshine corresponding to
N^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
.
Moreover, this code also plays an important role in the construction of the largest Mathieu group
M_{24}
.
Its automorphism is given by
3.A_{6}
, which can be explicitly constructed in the following way.
Write
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}=\{0,1,\omega,\bar{\omega}\}
with

\omega^{2}=\bar{\omega},~~\bar{\omega}^{2}=\omega,~~\omega^{3}=1.

The triple cover of the alternating group
A_{6}
can be generated by the permutations
(1,2)(3,4)
,
(1,2)(5,6)
,
(3,4)(5,6)
,
(1,3,5)(2,4,6)
,
(1,3)(2,4)
, as well as the composition of the permutation and multiplication
(1,2,3){\rm diag}(1,1,1,1,\bar{\omega},\omega)
. This group acts on the 6 coordinates and in particular induces all even permutations. It also contains the element corresponding to scalar multiplication by
\omega
and by
\bar{\omega}
. Hence we have constructed a group with centre
3\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/3
and we will call
z
the generator of the center corresponding to
{\rm diag}(\omega,\omega,\omega,\omega,\omega,\omega)
, the scalar multiplication by
\omega
.

The group
3.A_{6}
can be enlarged to
3.S_{6}
by adjoining an extra generator which acts on a vector in
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}^{6}
by permuting the last two coordinates followed by a complex conjugation:
\omega\leftrightarrow\bar{\omega}
. This group is often referred to as the semi-automorphism group of the hexacode, since it leaves the code invariant but does not act linearly on it. For this reason, the group
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}:={\rm Aut}(N^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}})/({\rm Weyl}({D_{4}}))^{\otimes 6}\cong 3.S_{6}
is the umbral group corresponding to the corresponding case of umbral moonshine.

From the above description, we can define a representation for the group
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
given by the group homomorphism
\epsilon:G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}\to\{1,-1\}
, given by
\epsilon_{g}=1
(-1) when
g
induces an even (odd) permutation on the 6 coordinates. In the notation in Table
1
, this is given by the irreducible character
\chi_{2}
.
This representation will play an important role in describing the umbral module in what follows.

More generally, the action of
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}^{6}
determines the umbral moonshine module for the
{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
case of umbral moonshine.
For later use we will now describe this action in more detail. Writing the natural basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}^{6}
as given by
{\bf e}_{0}^{i}
,
{\bf e}_{1}^{i}
,
{\bf e}_{\omega}^{i}
and
{\bf e}_{\bar{\omega}}^{i}
for
i=1,\dots,6
, we obtain a 24-dimensional permutation representation of
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
. The corresponding 24-dimensional cycle shape is denoted by
\widetilde{\Pi}_{g}
in Table
2
. Furthermore, from the above construction of
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
it is clear that the action of
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
does not mix
{\bf e}_{0}^{i}
with
{\bf e}_{1}^{i}
,
{\bf e}_{\omega}^{i}
and
{\bf e}_{\bar{\omega}}^{i}
and hence we arrive at a six-dimensional representation of
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
. The corresponding 6-dimensional cycle shape is denoted by
\bar{\Pi}_{g}
in Table
2
, and the corresponding character denoted by
\bar{\chi}
. One has
\bar{\chi}=\chi_{1}+\chi_{3}
in terms of the irreducible representations (cf. Table
1
). Alternatively, one might think of the 6-dimensional representation as spanned by the 6 vectors of the form
{\bf e}_{1}^{i}+{\bf e}_{\omega}^{i}+{\bf e}_{\bar{\omega}}^{i}
.
Similarly, we also define another character
\chi
by
\chi_{g}=\bar{\chi}_{g}\epsilon_{g}
.
One has
\chi=\chi_{2}+\chi_{4}
in terms of the irreducible representations. Finally, we have the 12-dimensional representation with basis
{\bf e}_{1}^{i}-{\bf e}_{\omega}^{i}
and
{\bf e}_{1}^{i}-{\bf e}_{\bar{\omega}}^{i}
for
i=1,\dots,6
. We denote the corresponding character by
\check{\chi}
, given by
\check{\chi}=\chi_{14}
in terms of the irreducible characters.

One can translate the above description of the group action on the hexacode into an action on the root systems
D_{4}^{\oplus 6}
in a straightforward way. First one identifies each copy of
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
with a copy of
D_{4}
and identifies
{\bf e}_{0}
with the central node of the dynkin diagram and
{\bf e}_{1}
,
{\bf e}_{\omega}
and
{\bf e}_{\bar{\omega}}
with the three nodes connected to the central node.

4.2
The Singularities

As reviewed in §
2.1

5
Discussion

In this paper we construct a module for the
D_{4}^{\oplus 6}
case of umbral moonshine. This is the first time that the module is constructed for a case of umbral moonshine with a sizeable umbral finite group (with
|G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}|\sim 10^{3}
, this group is larger than the cases of umbral moonshine where modules have been constructed previously in
[
12
,
13
,
7
]
, where the groups have order dividing 24).
This is also the first construction of the umbral module which utilises the connection to symmetries of K3 string theory.
At the same time, there are clearly important open questions remaining. In the following we discuss a few of them.

•

Note that our construction naturally leads to a super module for
G^{D_{4}^{\oplus 6}}
. However, apart from the virtual representation corresponding to the leading polar term
(cf. (
1.1
)), the umbral module is known to constitute of the even part. It would be nice to make this positivity manifest.

•

What is the physical or geometric meaning of the chiral CFT
{\cal T}
?
The relation between the Conway CFT, which is closely related to
{\cal T}
, and a specific K3 sigma model has been elucidated in
[
39
,
47
,
48
]
. It would be interesting to understand the physical role played by the ghost systems.

•

An obvious question is whether one can employ a similar construction for the other cases of umbral moonshine. Note that the chiral CFT
{\cal T}
has
Spin(24)
symmetry which preserves the fermionic and bosonic
U(1)
ghost currents. It is hence possible to define the regularised twined partition function
\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}Z_{g}^{\eta}(\tau,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
(cf. §
3.4
) for any element of any of the 23 umbral groups. To make contact with weak Jacobi forms of the type of K3 elliptic genus, one has to specialise the fugacity to
\zeta_{1}={1\over 2}
. However, this leads to a finite answer only when taking
\eta\to 0^{+}
if
g
is 4-plane preserving. To construct umbral moonshine modules for cases where not all group elements are 4-plane preserving (
X=A_{1}^{\oplus 24}
,
A_{2}^{\oplus 12}
, and
A_{4}^{\oplus 6}
), one needs a construction that works with the two-elliptic-variable functions
Z_{g}(\tau,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
directly.

•

Note that the contribution of the vector-valued umbral moonshine mock modular forms
(H^{X}_{g,r})
to the twined partition function of the theory
{\cal T}
is basically given by a single
q
-series
{1\over 2\pi i}{\partial\over\partial\omega}\Psi^{X}_{e}(\tau,w)
. See (
2.8
).
What allows us to recover from it the individual components
H^{X}_{g,r}
of the mock modular forms is the following two facts. First, there are just two independent components in the case
X=D_{4}^{\oplus 6}
, which can be taken to be
H^{X}_{g,1}
and
H^{X}_{g,3}
.
Second, the representations underlying the 1st resp. 3rd component have the feature that they factor through
S_{6}
resp. are faithful representations. As a result, it is possible to use the projection operator to isolate the contributions from the two independent components from the twined partition function of
{\cal T}
. A similar projection property also holds for other 14 cases of umbral moonshine (cf. Conjecture 6.3 in
[
6
]
).

In view of this, another challenge when attempting to generalise the current construction to other cases of umbral moonshine is how to disentangle the contributions from different components of the vector-valued umbral moonshine mock modular forms
(H^{X}_{g,r})
in the twined partition functions for the cases of
X
with many independent components.
Recall that an important feature of umbral moonshine is the “multiplicative relations” relating
H^{X^{\prime}}_{g^{\prime}}
and
H^{X}_{g}
for specific pairs of Niemeier root systems
(X,X^{\prime})
and group elements
g\in G^{X}
and
g^{\prime}\in G^{X^{\prime}}
(cf. §5.3 of
[
6
]
). As we will see in more detail below, these relations together with the projection property enable us to disentangle different components in the vector-valued functions
H^{X}_{g}
in various cases.

Finally, we point out that for the cases that
g
is a 4-plane preserving group element of a umbral group
G^{X}
,
many mock modular forms
H^{X}_{g}
for many different
X
and
g
can be obtained in a similar way as discussed in §
4
.

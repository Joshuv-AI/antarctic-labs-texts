Ilaria Colazzo, Alan Koch, Isabel Martin-Lyons, Paul J. Truman

Abstract
. Skew bracoids have been shown to have applications in Hopf-Galois theory. We show that a certain family of skew bracoids correspond bijectively with left cancellative semibraces. A consequence of this correspondence is that skew bracoids in this family can be used to obtain and study solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation; we study this process and the resulting solutions. We give numerous examples of skew bracoids satisfying our hypothesis, drawing upon a variety of constructions in the literature.

1.
Introduction

Skew braces, introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin in
[
8
]
, provide an algebraic framework for studying bijective nondegenerate solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation (see
[
6
]
or Section
4
for more details on the set-theoretic YBE). A skew brace is a triple
(G,\star,\cdot)
in which
(G,\star)
and
(G,\cdot)
are groups and the compatibility relation

x\cdot(y\star z)=(x\cdot y)\star x^{-\star}\star(x\cdot z)

(1)

holds for all
x,y,z\in G
(here
x^{-\star}
denotes the inverse of
x
with respect to the binary operation
\star
). Thanks to the importance of the Yang–Baxter equation in theoretical physics and statistical mechanics
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
, skew braces have been intensively studied, revealing connections with numerous other topics including braids, knots, regular subgroups of permutation groups, and Hopf-Galois theory. This has led to a fruitful network of ideas, which has motivated the development of numerous generalizations and variants of skew braces. For example, in
[
3
]
left
semibraces
are introduced; these are triples
(G,+,\cdot)
in which
(G,\cdot)
is a group,
(G,+)
is a left cancellative semigroup, and the binary operations are connected via a relation similar to (
1
) (see Section
3
for more details). Left semibraces yield
left nondegenerate
solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation. In
[
15
]
two of the authors introduce
skew bracoids
; these consist of two groups
(G,\cdot)
and
(N,\star)
, together with a transitive action of the former on the latter that interacts with the binary operation on
N
in a manner analogous to (
1
). Skew bracoids can be used to broaden the connection between skew braces and Hopf-Galois theory
[
15
, Section 5]
.

In this paper we show that a certain large family of skew bracoids correspond bijectively with semibraces (Theorem
3.5
). This correspondence enriches the study of both objects: it shows that many skew bracoids yield solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation, it connects semibraces with Hopf-Galois theory, and it shows that results concerning the classification or description of either object can be reinterpreted in terms of the other.

In Section
2
we recall some fundamental definitions and results concerning skew bracoids, specify the family of skew bracoids we will study (Definition
2.1
), establish a number of equivalent characterizations (Proposition
2.2
), and use these to give a variety of examples. We also exhibit a family of skew bracoids that do not satisfy our hypothesis. In Section
3
we prove several technical lemmas concerning skew bracoids satisfying Definition
2.1
, and use these to establish our main result (Theorem
3.5
).

As noted above, an interesting consequence of the correspondence established in Theorem
3.5
is that it allows us to obtain a solution of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation from certain skew bracoids by passing through the corresponding semibrace. In Section
4
we show how this solution may be obtained directly from the skew bracoid, and study properties of this construction.

A (left)

viz

For brevity, we shall refer to skew braces simply as

In this section we specify and characterize a large family of bracoids; in subsquent sections we will connect bracoids in this family with semibraces and with set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation.

We say that a bracoid

Definition 2.1

We shall say that a bracoid

is essentially a brace.

To justify our choice of terminology we note that if

Example 2.3

and so
\rho_{e}=\mathrm{id}
. Now by Lemma
3.2
, for all
x\in G
we have

\rho_{x}\rho_{x^{-1}}=\rho_{x^{-1}}\rho_{x}=\rho_{e}=\mathrm{id},

so
\rho_{x}
is bijective with inverse
\rho_{x^{-1}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For
x,y,z\in G
we have

\lambda_{x}(yz)=\lambda_{x}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}(z).

Proof.

By repeated application of (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(yz)\rho_{yz}(x)=\;

\displaystyle xyz

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(y)\rho_{y}(x)z

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}(z)\rho_{z}(\rho_{y}(x))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}(z)\rho_{yz}(x);

the result follows immediately.
∎

With these results to hand, we can state and prove our main result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a group with an exact factorization
HS
. There is a bijection between

1)

binary operations
\star
on
H
and transitive actions
\odot
of
(G,\cdot)
on
H
such that
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is a left bracoid containing a brace and with
\Stab_{G}(e)=S
;

2)

binary operations
+
on
G
such that
(G,+,\cdot)
is a left semibrace in which
G+e=H
and
E=S
.

Proof.

First suppose that
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is a left bracoid containing a brace and with
\Stab(e)=S
. For
x,y\in G
define

x+y=y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x).

We claim that
(G,+,\cdot)
is a left semibrace. First we show that
+
is associative on
G
. For
x,y,z\in G
we have

\displaystyle x+(y+z)=\;

\displaystyle x+z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)\lambda_{(z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y))^{-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(z^{-1})y^{-1}}(x),

(by (
8
))

and

\displaystyle(x+y)+z=\;

\displaystyle y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x)+z

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(z^{-1})}(\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x))

(by Corollary
3.4
)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)\lambda_{\rho_{y}(z^{-1})y^{-1}}(x).

( by Lemma
3.1
)

Thus
+
is associative, and so
(G,+)
is a semigroup.

Next we show that
(G,+)
is left cancellative. For
x,y,z\in G
we have

\displaystyle x+y=x+z

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x)=z\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle x\rho_{x}(y^{-1})^{-1}=x\rho_{x}(z^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(y^{-1})=\rho_{x}(z^{-1})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle y=z.

( by Corollary
3.3
)

Thus
(G,+)
is left cancellative.

Finally we show that the left semibrace relation is satisfied. For
x,y,z\in G
we have

\displaystyle xy+x(x^{-1}+z)=\;

\displaystyle xy+xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x^{-1})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x^{-1})\lambda_{(xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x^{-1}))^{-1}}(xy)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x^{-1})\lambda_{\rho_{x^{-1}}(z^{-1})}(xy)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(x^{-1}xy)

(by Corollary
3.4
)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle xz\lambda_{z^{-1}}(y)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle x(y+z).

Thus
(G,+,\cdot)
is indeed a left semibrace.

For the remaining claims, we note that for
x\in G
we have
x+e=e\lambda_{e^{-1}}(x)=\lambda_{e}(x)
; thus
G+e=\lambda_{e}(G)=H
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle x+x=x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle x\lambda_{x^{-1}}(x)=x

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\lambda_{x^{-1}}(x)=e

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\lambda_{x^{-1}}(x)\odot e=e

(note
\lambda_{x^{-1}}(x)\in H
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\gamma_{x^{-1}}(x\odot e)=e

(relationship between
\lambda
and
\gamma
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle x\odot e=e

(
\gamma_{x}\in\Aut(H,\star)
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle x\in S.

Thus
E=S
.

Conversely, suppose that
+
is a binary operation on
G
such that
(G,+,\cdot)
is a left semibrace in which
G+e=H
and
E=S
. For
h,k\in H
define

h\star k=k+h;

then
(H,\star)
is the opposite group to
(H,+)
. In addition, for
x\in G
and
h\in H
define
x\odot h=xh+e
. We claim that
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is a bracoid containing a brace such that
\Stab(e)=S
. It is straightforward to verify that
\odot
is a transitive left action of
(G,\cdot)
on
H
; it remains to show that the bracoid relation (
2
) is satisfied. By the definition of the binary operation
\star
, it suffices to show that

x\odot(h+k)=(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+(x\odot k)

(14)

for all
x\in G
and
h,k\in H
. We shall use repeatedly the fact that since
e\in E
we have
e+y=y
for all
y\in G
, along with the semibrace relation (
6
). Beginning with the right hand side of (
14
) we have:

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+(x\odot k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+(xk+e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+(x(e+k)+e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+xe+\mathcal{L}_{x}(k)+e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+xe+e+\mathcal{L}_{x}(k)+e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\odot h)-(x\odot e)+(x\odot e)+\mathcal{L}_{x}(k)+e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle xh+\mathcal{L}_{x}(k)+e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(h+k)+e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\odot(h+k).

Hence (
14
) holds, and so
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is indeed a left bracoid.

For the remaining claims: first we note that
x\in S
if and only if
x+e=e
, which occurs if and only if
x\in E
; second, for
h\in H
we have
h\odot e=h+e=h
, so the action of
H
on itself is transitive, and so
(H,+,\cdot)
is a brace.

The constructions described above are mutually inverse: if
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is a bracoid as in the statement of the theorem and
(G,+,\cdot)
is the corresponding semibrace, then the bracoid corresponding to
(G,+,\cdot)
is
(G,\cdot,H,\hat{\star},\hat{\odot})
, where for all
h,k\in H
and
x\in G
we have
h\;\hat{\star}\;k=k+h
and
x\;\hat{\odot}\;h=xh+e
. Since
k+h=h\star k
, we quickly see that
\hat{\star}
coincides with
\star
. Since
\hat{\odot}
and
\odot
are both transitive actions of
(G,\cdot)
on
H
, they coincide if and only if
x\;\hat{\odot}\;e=x\odot e
for all
x\in G
. Writing
x=hs
, with
h\in H
and
s\in S
, we have:

x\;\hat{\odot}\;e=\;hs\;\hat{\odot}\;e=h+e=hs\odot e=x\odot e.

Thus the bracoid corresponding to
(G,+,\cdot)
is the original bracoid
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
.

Conversely, if
(G,+,\cdot)
is a semibrace as in the statement of the theorem and
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is the corresponding bracoid, then the semibrace corresponding to
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is
(G,\hat{+},\cdot)
, where
x\;\hat{+}\;y=y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x)
. We have

\displaystyle x\;\hat{+}\;y=\;

\displaystyle y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y\gamma_{y^{-1}}(x\odot e)

(by (
7
))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y((y^{-1}\odot e)^{-1}\star(y^{-1}\odot(x\odot e))

(by the definition of
\gamma_{y^{-1}}
)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y(y^{-1}\odot((y\odot e)\star(x\odot e)))

(by (
2
))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y(y^{-1}\odot((y+e)\star(x+e)))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y(y^{-1}\odot(x+e+y+e))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle y(y^{-1}(x+y+e)+e)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle yy^{-1}(x+y+e)+y(y^{-1}+e)

(by (
6
))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle x+y+y(y^{-1}+e)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle x+y(e+e)

(again by (
6
))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle x+y.

Thus the semibrace corresponding to
(G,\cdot,H,\star,\odot)
is the original semibrace
(G,+,\cdot)
.

Hence we obtain the bijection given in the statement of the theorem.
∎

4.
Solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation

A
solution of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation
on a nonempty set
G
(hereafter, simply a
solution
on
G
) is a map
r:G\times G\rightarrow G\times G
such that

(r\times\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\times r)

as functions on
G\times G\times G
. We say that solution is
bijective
if
r
is a bijective function, and
involutive
if
r^{2}=\mathrm{id}
. For
x,y\in G
we write

r(x,y)=(\lambda_{x}(y),\rho_{y}(x));

we say that a solution is
left nondegenerate
if
\lambda_{x}
is bijective for each
x\in G
,
right nondegenerate
if
\rho_{y}
is bijective for each
y\in G
, and
nondegenerate
if it is both left and right nondegenerate.

As stated in Section
1
, braces yield bijective nondegenerate solutions: if
(G,\star,\cdot)
is a brace and we set

\lambda_{x}(y)=x^{-\star}\star(xy)\mbox{ and }\rho_{y}(x)=\lambda_{x}(y)^{-1}xy

(15)

then
r(x,y)=(\lambda_{x}(y),\rho_{y}(x))
is such a solution
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
.

In
[
3
, Theorem 9]
it is shown that if
(G,+,\cdot)
is a left semibrace and we set

\mathcal{L}_{x}(y)=x(x^{-1}+y)\mbox{ and }\mathcal{R}_{y}(x)=\mathcal{L}_{x}(y)^{-1}xy

(16)

then
r(x,y)=(\mathcal{L}_{x}(y),\mathcal{R}_{y}(x))
is a left nondegenerate solution. The structure of a solution arising in this way is further explored in
[
4
]
, using the notion of the
matched product
of solutions
[
4
, Definition 1 and Theorem 2]
. Recall that we have a decomposition
(G,+)=(H,+)\oplus(E,+)
where
H=G+e
and
E
is the set of idempotents with respect to
+
. The solution
r(x,y)
restricts to each of
H
and
E
. In fact,
(H,+,\cdot)
is a left skew brace and
(E,+,\cdot)
is a trivial left semibrace, and the solution arising from the original semibrace
(G,+,\cdot)
is isomorphic to the matched product of the solution arising from
(H,+,\cdot)
and the solution arising from
(E,+,\cdot)

[
4
, Theorem 9 and Theorem 10]
.

Applying the correspondence obtained in Theorem
3.5
, we can obtain a solution from a bracoid containing a brace by passing through the corresponding semibrace. We recall the functions
\lambda,\rho
defined in (
7
) and (
8
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(G,H)
be a bracoid containing a brace. Then the function
r:G\times G\rightarrow G\times G
defined by

r(x,y)=(\rho_{x^{-1}}(y^{-1})^{-1},\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x^{-1})^{-1})

is a left nondegenerate solution.

Proof.

Let
(G,+,\cdot)
be the semibrace corresponding to
(G,H)
via Theorem
3.5
. Then for
x,y\in G
we have
x+y=y\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x)
. We rewrite the solution arising from the semibrace
(G,+,\cdot)
in terms of
\lambda
and
\rho
.

\displaystyle r(x,y)=(\mathcal{L}_{x}(y),\mathcal{R}_{y}(x))=\;

\displaystyle(x(x^{-1}+y),\mathcal{L}_{x}(y)^{-1}xy)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(xy\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x^{-1}),(xy\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x^{-1}))^{-1}xy)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(\rho_{x^{-1}}(y^{-1})^{-1},\lambda_{y^{-1}}(x^{-1})^{-1}).

∎

The large number of inverses appearing in the formula for the solution in Proposition
4.1
makes it rather unwieldy. By applying some standard techniques we can relate this left nondegenerate solution to a right nondegenerate solution, which more closely resembles the solution obtained from a skew brace in (
15
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(G,H)
be a bracoid containing a brace. Then the function
\tilde{r}:G\times G\rightarrow G\times G
defined by

\tilde{r}(x,y)=(\lambda_{x}(y),\rho_{y}(x))

is a right nondegenerate solution.

Proof.

Define maps
\iota,\tau:G\times G\rightarrow G\times G
by

\iota(x,y)=(x^{-1},y^{-1})\mbox{ and }\tau(x,y)=(y,x).

It is clear that
\iota,\tau
are both self-inverse bijections. It is known (see
[
6
]
, for example) that conjugating a left nondegenerate solution by
\iota
yields another left nondegenerate solution, and that conjugating a left nondegenerate solution by
\tau
yields a right nondegenerate solution. Taking
r(x,y)
as in Proposition
4.1
we have:

\displaystyle\tau\iota r\iota\tau(x,y)=\;

\displaystyle\tau\iota r(y^{-1},x^{-1})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\tau\iota(\rho_{y}(x)^{-1},\lambda_{x}(y)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(\lambda_{x}(y),\rho_{y}(x)).

Hence
\tilde{r}(x,y)
is a right nondegenerate solution.
∎

Since the solution obtained from the bracoid in Proposition
4.1
is the same as the solution arising from corresponding semibrace, and the solution obtained in Proposition
4.2
is closely related to it, they each restrict to the subgroups
H
and
S
of
G
, and each is isomorphic to a suitable matched product of those restrictions.

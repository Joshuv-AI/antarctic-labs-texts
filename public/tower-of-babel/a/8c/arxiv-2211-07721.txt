DIRECTED HEREDITARY SPECIES AND DECOMPOSITION SPACES
ALEX CEBRIAN, WILSON FORERO

Abstract
We introduce the notion of directed hereditary species and show that they have associated monoidal decomposition spaces, comodule bialgebras, and operadic categories. The notion subsumes Schmitt’s hereditary species, Gálvez–Kock–Tonks directed restrictions species, and a directed version of Carlier’s construction of monoidal decomposition spaces and comodule bialgebras. In addition to all the examples of Schmitt, Gálvez–Kock–Tonks and Carlier, the new construction covers also the Fauvet–Foissy–Manchon comodule bialgebra of finite topological spaces, the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees, and the Faà di Bruno comodule bialgebra of linear trees.

2.8

Species

The theory of species was introduced by Joyal
[
39
]
as a combinatorial theory of formal power series. A species is a functor from the category of finite sets and bijections to the category of sets. Usual types of species include graphs, trees, endomorphisms, permutations, etc. Schmitt
[
49
]
extended the notion of species in such a way that the combinatorial structures are classified according to their functorial properties. Let
\mathbb{S}_{p}
be the category of finite sets and partial surjections: a
hereditary species

[
49
]
is a functor
H\colon\mathbb{S}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
. If partial surjections are portrayed as spans

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

we can say that

is contravariant in injections and covariant in surjections.

We introduce the new notion of
connected directed hereditary species
to cover examples related to the Fauvet–Foissy–Manchon comodule bialgebra of finite topologies and admissible maps; and the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees. But it does not cover Schmitt hereditary species. For such species, we need the non-connected case, which will be defined in Section
9
.

Definition 2.9
.

A
connected directed hereditary species
is a functor
H\colon\mathbb{K}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbf{Grpd}
.

Note that

is covariant in contractions and contravariant in convex maps.
An element of

is called a
H
-structure on the finite poset

.

2.10
.

Coalgebras from directed connected hereditary species

As in the case of Schmitt hereditary species, connected directed hereditary species give rise to coalgebras. Let

be a poset and

an

The comultiplication of

where the sum ranges over isomorphism classes of contractions

is the restriction of

for every

in

Definition 4.1
.

[
25
, §2.2]

Let
T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
be an identity-on-objects monotone map between two preorders. We define

to be the preorder with the same objects as

and
T^{\prime}
, and the preorder is defined by closing the relation
\mathcal{R}
by transitivity:

x\mathcal{R}y\Longleftrightarrow(x\leq_{T}y\;\text{or}\;y\leq_{T^{\prime}}x).

In other words, we obtain

by inverting those arrows of
T
that also belong to
T^{\prime}
, and then closing by transitivity. Later in this section, we will use the following more categorical characterization of quotients.

The underlying set of a preorder

will be denoted as
\underline{T}
, and
\overline{T}
denotes the discrete preorder of connected components of
T
. We have a natural map
\operatorname{comp}\colon T\to\overline{T}
that sends each object to its corresponding connected component.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be an identity-on-objects monotone map between two preorders. Then

is the pushout in category of preorders
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\prime}/T^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T/T^{\prime}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

Proof.

Since the maps are identity-on-objects then
\underline{T/T^{\prime}}=\underline{T}
. This means that at the level of sets the square is a pushout. Consider the preorder given by closing the relation
\mathcal{R}
by transitivity:

x\mathcal{R}y\Longleftrightarrow(x\leq_{T}y\;\text{or}\;y\leq_{T^{\prime}}x).

It is straightforward to see that the diagram commutes at the level of preorders. In case that we have another preorder

in

that made the square commutes, the monotonicity of the maps force that

is equal to the transitive closure of

.

and

.

A

it consists of the following data, subject to some axioms: a set of objects;
two different classes of morphisms (horizontal and vertical); and squares, which are connected by various source, target, identity, and composition maps.

Lemma 5.12

The category of finite preorders and admissible maps has chosen initial objects given by the groupoid finite preorders.

Proof.

Given a finite preorder

Lemma 5.13

Running example, part III: Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees

The connected directed hereditary species
H_{\operatorname{CEM}}
of trees, described in §
2.11
, induces a comodule bialgebra. The comultiplication
\Delta_{H_{\operatorname{CEM}}}
is explained in Remark
2.18
. The second comultiplication is given by the directed restriction species of trees
R_{\operatorname{BCK}}
(§

). By Proposition
7.8
, this is a comodule bialgebra usually known as the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees
[
10
]
.

On the other hand, Kock
[
40
, §5.4]
showed another presentation of the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees related to the reduced Baez–Dolan construction of the terminal operad. In the operadic setting, operadic trees have an input edge (the leaf edge) and an output edge (the root edge), and there is a tree without nodes (where leaf=root). To relate operadic trees and combinatorial trees, we have to forget all decorations and shave off leaf edges and root edge. This construction is known as the
core
of an operadic tree.

Since the trees involved in the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra are combinatorial trees, it is not possible to realise this bialgebra as the incidence bialgebra of an operad, but this can be solved using the core construction. Kock
[
40
, Proposition 5.4.7]
proved that taking core of the incidence comodule bialgebra of the reduced Baez–Dolan construction of the terminal operad, we obtain the Calaque–Ebrahimi-Fard–Manchon comodule bialgebra of rooted trees. Note that our approach to this comodule bialgebra is more straightforward since it follows from the connected directed hereditary species
H_{\operatorname{CEM}}
of trees.

Example 7.11

(

8
Connected directed hereditary species and operadic categories

The goal of this section is to construct a functor from the category of connected directed hereditary species to the category of operadic categories.

Operadic categories were introduced by Batanin and Markl
[
6
]

and used to prove the duoidal Deligne conjecture. An operadic
category is a kind of combinatorial structure whose ‘algebras’
are operads of various kinds depending on the operadic category.
For example,
\Delta
is an operadic category and its operads
are nonsymmetric operads.

An operadic category
\mathcal{C}
has chosen local terminal objects (that is,
in each connected component there is a chosen terminal object),
a cardinality functor
|-|:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{FinSet}

to the standard skeleton of the category of
finite sets, and a notion of fibre: this is an assignment that
for each morphism
F\colon Y\to X

in the operadic category, and each
i\in|{X}|
gives a new
object denoted
f^{-1}(i)
, but this is abstract and does not
have to be a fibre in the usual sense of the word. For example,
it is not necessarily a subobject of
Y
. These data are subject to
many axioms, which can be formulated in various ways

[
6
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
32
]
. The Carlier proof
[
11
]
that hereditary
species induce operadic categories consisted in checking the whole
list of axioms.

Garner, Kock, and Weber
[
32
]
observed that the
chosen-local-terminals structure amounts precisely to be a coalgebra
for the upper decalage comonad, and went on to give a characterisation
of operadic categories in terms of a certain modified decalage
comononad.

Recently Batanin, Kock, and Weber
[
4
]
have
found a more conceptual characterisation, where all the axioms end up
formulated as simplicial identities. Their discovery is that just as
the chosen-local-terminals structure amounts to an extra top degeneracy
map, the fibre structures amount to an extra top face map, except
that this extra top face map lives in the Kleisli category for the
free-symmetric-monoidal-category monad. To understand this point of view, we will introduce a few concepts.

8.1
.

The lt-nerve

Recall that

We now work with posets whose underlying sets are ordinals and strict pullbacks. These assumptions are necessary to follow the work of Batanin and Markl

on operadic categories.

Moreover, we work only with

Let

and the stability of discrete opfibrations under pullback that

Combining this with the fact that

has a terminal object (the poset with one element), it follows that the set
H[1]
of

-structures of the poset with one element is a set of chosen local terminal objects in

as a consequence of Lemma

. ∎

Let

8.6

Half decalage

Let

denote the subcategory of pseudosimplicial groupoids that the unique pseudo-simplicial identities involve the top face maps. Given a pseudo simplicial space

\mathbbe{\Delta}^{\operatorname{t}}

throws away the top face maps but keeps the top

A

-presheaf

is a
\mathbbe{\Delta}^{\operatorname{t}}
-Segal space
if the simplicial groupoid obtained after eliminating

is Segal. Since we are working in this section with

-valued species, we have to modify the definition of

to
\mathbf{H}=\mathsf{S}\operatorname{N}^{\operatorname{lt}}\mathbb{H}

Lemma 8.7
.

Let

be a connected directed hereditary species. Then

is a

Since

is a category, its nerve

is a Segal space

, and therefore
\mathsf{S}\operatorname{N}\mathbb{H}
is a Segal space, as

preserves pullbacks and hence Segal objects. This means that
\mathsf{S}\operatorname{N}^{\operatorname{lt}}\mathbb{H}
is a
\mathbbe{\Delta}^{\operatorname{t}}
-Segal space.
∎

Connected directed hereditary species constitute a new family of examples of operadic categories. In fact, the connected directed hereditary species associated to the Fauvet–Foissy–Manchon comodule bialgebra of finite topologies and admissible maps; and the connected directed hereditary species

9
Directed hereditary species as monoidal decomposition spaces, comodule bialgebras and operadic categories

Schmitt hereditary species are not connected directed hereditary species, as the fibres along a surjection between discrete posets are not necessarily connected. To cover these examples, in this section, we introduce the notion of collapse, which allows for non-connected fibres. This leads to the notion of (not-necessarily-connected) directed hereditary species. Furthermore, each directed hereditary species induces a decomposition space (
9.17
), a comodule bialgebra (
9.20
), and a operadic category (
9.22
).

9.1

Partially reflecting maps

Definition 9.2

A map of posets

.

In the category of posets, collapse maps are stable under pullback.

Proof.

Let

and

be posets. Let

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{Q}}

The partial order

on

In case

Directed hereditary species as operadic categories

We now work with
\mathbf{Set}
-valued species as in the classical theory and posets whose underlying sets are ordinals. These are necessary to ensure that given a directed hereditary species

is a discrete opfibration and have precise constructions in the context of operadic categories. For a directed hereditary species
H
, the category
\mathbb{H}
is the pullback of the Grothendieck construction
\int H\to\mathbb{D}_{p}
along the inclusion
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}_{p}
.

Lemma 9.23
.

Let
H\colon\mathbb{D}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
be a directed hereditary species. Then
\mathbb{H}
is a category with local terminal objects.

Proof.

Since
H
is a presheaf with values in
\mathbf{Set}
, we have that
\int H\to\mathbb{D}_{p}
is a discrete opfibration. This implies by the construction of
\mathbb{H}
and the stability of discrete opfibration under pullback that
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D}
is a discrete opfibration.
Combining this with the fact that
\mathbb{D}
has a terminal object (the poset with one element), it follows that the set
H[1]
of
H
-structures of the poset with one element is the set of local terminal objects in
\mathbb{H}
by Lemma
8.2
. ∎

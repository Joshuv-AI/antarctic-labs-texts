New Philosopher Inequalities for Online Bayesian Matching, via Pivotal Sampling
Mark Braverman, Mahsa Derakhshan, Tristan Pollner, Amin Saberi, David Wajc

Abstract
We study the polynomial-time approximability of the optimal online stochastic bipartite matching algorithm, initiated by Papadimitriou et al. (EC’21). Here, nodes on one side of the graph are given upfront, while at each time t t , an online node and its edge weights are drawn from a time-dependent distribution. The optimal algorithm is 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} -hard to approximate within some universal constant. We refer to this optimal algorithm, which requires time to think (compute), as a philosopher , and refer to polynomial-time online approximations of the above as philosopher inequalities . The best known philosopher inequality for online matching yields a 0.652 0.652 -approximation. In contrast, the best possible prophet inequality, or approximation of the optimum offline solution, is 0.5 0.5 . Our main results are a 0.678 0.678 -approximate algorithm and a 0.685 0.685 -approximation for a vertex-weighted special case. Notably, both bounds exceed the 0.666 0.666 -approximation of the offline optimum obtained by Tang, Wu, and Wu (STOC’22) for the vertex-weighted problem. Building on our algorithms and the recent black-box reduction of Banihashem et al. (SODA’24), we provide polytime (pricing-based) truthful mechanisms which 0.678 0.678 -approximate the social welfare of the optimal online allocation for bipartite matching markets. Our online allocation algorithm relies on the classic pivotal sampling algorithm (Srinivasan FOCS’01, Gandhi et al. J.ACM’06), along with careful discarding to obtain strong negative correlations between offline nodes, while matching using the highest-value edges. Consequently, the analysis boils down to examining the distribution of a weighted sum X X of negatively correlated Bernoulli variables, specifically lower bounding its mass below a threshold, 𝔼 ⁡ [ min ⁡ ( 1 , X ) ] \mathbb{E}[\min(1,X)] , of possible independent interest. Interestingly, our bound relies on an imaginary invocation of pivotal sampling.

(

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a polynomial-time algorithm that on set

(P3)

Negative cylinder dependence (NCD):
For all

\Pr\left[\bigwedge_{i\in I}(X_{i}=1)\right]\leq\prod_{i\in I}\Pr[X_{i}=1]\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\Pr\left[\bigwedge_{i\in I}(X_{i}=0)\right]\leq\prod_{i\in I}\Pr[X_{i}=0].

Algorithmically, at each time

(Q1)

Assign/discard each available offline node to

(Q2)

Get

(Q3)

Obtain strong negative correlations between offline nodes’ availability, specifically NCD.

By Properties

In what follows, we let

Independent Coin Bound.

Let
S
denote the random set of all
i
such that
X_{i}=1
; by the union bound, for any realization of
S

\min\left(1,\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}\right)\geq 1-\prod_{i\in S}(1-c_{i}).

Thus, by imagining that every

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\min(1,X)]\geq\mathbb{E}_{S}\left[1-\prod_{i\in S}(1-c_{i})\right]=1-\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i}(1-c_{i}\cdot X_{i})\right]\geq 1-\prod_{i}(1-c_{i}\cdot\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]).

(3)

Here, the final step follows from non-trivial calculations which crucially use that
\{X_{i}\}
are NCD. Using that
1-c_{i}\cdot\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\leq\exp(-\mathbb{E}[c_{i}\cdot X_{i}])

Bucketing Bound.

The first inequality of the independent coin bound of
Equation

3

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\min(1,X)]\geq 1-\prod_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\left(1-\sum_{i\in B}c_{i}\cdot\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\right).

(4)

This bound is similar to the bound obtained by

Fractional Bucketing Bound.

The bucketing bound of
Equation

4
unfortunately can also be quite lossy, due to integrality issues. For example, if

for every
i

Our most novel bound on

In particular, for any time-step

In particular, using this generalized pivotal sampling, we can modify the

Appendix H
A Truthful Mechanism for Matching Markets

In this section, we show our algorithm has implications for Bayesian mechanism design in matching markets. Consider a setting
n
offline items
I
and
T
buyers who have unit-demand valuations over these items.
13
13

13

We recall a
unit-demand valuation

v(\cdot)
is one such that
v(S)=\max_{T\subseteq S,|T|\leq 1}v(T)

Each buyer
t\in[T]
samples his valuation function

from a known distribution
\mathcal{D}_{t}
; at time

.

Note that if the realization of each valuation is revealed to us, this is equivalent to the setting considered earlier, and
Algorithm

4
will in polynomial-time achieve a
0.678
-approximation to the social welfare achievable by the optimum online algorithm. In this section, we argue that this guarantee extends to the case where buyers are strategic agents, and must not truthfully reveal their valuation. There is an extensive line of work studying this setting, and generalizations thereof, showing that we can achieve a (tight)
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
-approximation to the social welfare of the allocation of optimum offline. This work is the first to explicitly study the question against the online benchmark.

We achieve our guarantee by using an (adaptive) pricing-based mechanism, which is easily observed to be dominant-strategy incentive-compatible (DSIC). The prices are set via a recent result of
[
BHK+24
]

Definition H.1
.

A matching mechanism is

t

t

t

\pi_{i^{*}}

Observation H.1
.

A pricing-based mechanism is DSIC.

BHK+24
]

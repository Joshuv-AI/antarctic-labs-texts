The term

In literature, second order semipositone problems have been widely studied. In the seminal paper

5.1
).
Now, for every
\varphi\in D_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\varphi\geq 0
we show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)f_{j}(u_{j})\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)f_{0}(u)\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z},\quad\hbox{as $j\to\infty$}.

We split

\lvert f_{j}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u)\rvert\leq\lvert f_{j}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u_{j})\rvert+\lvert f_{0}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u)\rvert.

By the continuity of
K_{0}^{\prime}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)\lvert f_{0}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u)\rvert\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\to 0,\quad\hbox{as $j\to\infty$}.

Further,
\lvert f_{j}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u_{j})\rvert\leq a_{j}
. Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)\lvert f_{j}(a_{j})-f_{0}(u)\rvert\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\\
\leq a_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)\lvert f_{0}(u_{j})-f_{0}(u)\rvert\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\to 0\quad\hbox{as}\penalty\ j\to\infty.

From the weak formulation

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u_{j}(z)\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)f_{j}(u_{j})\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z},\forall\varphi\in D_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\varphi\geq 0.

Taking the limit as
j\to\infty
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u(z)\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)f(u)\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z},\forall\varphi\in D_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\varphi\geq 0.

Now, for any
\varphi\in D_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we write
\varphi=\varphi^{+}-\varphi^{-}
. We see that the above identity holds for both
\varphi^{+}
and
\varphi^{-}
, so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u(z)\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(z)f(u)\varphi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z},\quad\forall\varphi\in D_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(30)

that is,
u
is a weak solution of (
29
).
With the same argument of Lemma
5.1
, we obtain
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
p\in[2^{*}_{\gamma},\infty]
and
u
is a continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

The function
u
is positive.

Proof.

Taking the test function
\varphi=u^{-}=\min\left\{u,0\right\}
in (
30
) we find that
\lVert u^{-}\rVert_{\gamma}=0
, so
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Let
\Sigma=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be the degenerate set for the Grushin operator.
If
\Omega
is any bounded domain contained in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
, then
\Delta_{\gamma}
is a uniformly elliptic operator on
\Omega
. Hence
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and either
u\equiv 0
on
\Omega
or
u>0
in
\Omega
. In the former case, it follows from
[
26
, Proposition 2.5]
that
u\equiv 0
on the connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
which contains
\Omega
. Therefore
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
.

Now, it is clear that
\Sigma
has empty interior, and it follows from
[
25
, Theorem 9, Chapter 1]
that
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Recalling that
u
is a continuous function, we conclude that
u\equiv 0
on
\Sigma
. To this stage the function
u
might be zero in (the interior of) some connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
and strictly positive in (the interior of) some other component. However this is again impossible, since
[
26
, Proposition 2.5]
applies again on any ball which meets two such components across
\Sigma
. Hence either
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, or
u>0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The former is not admissible due to the Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

When
\gamma
is an even integer, a very elegant proof of the positivity of
u
can be devised. We follow an idea of
[
6
]
. When
\gamma
is an integer, it is known that
-\Delta_{\gamma}
satisfies the Hörmander condition. Recalling that
g>0
and
f_{0}\geq 0
, we see that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{\gamma}u\nabla_{\gamma}\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}z}\geq 0
for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi\geq 0
. Hence
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(-\Delta_{\gamma}\varphi)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\geq 0
for every such
\varphi
. Recalling that
u
is a continuous function, it follows that
u
is a viscosity subsolution of the operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}
. The strong maximum principle proved in
[
4
, Corollary 1.4]
yields that either
u\equiv 0
or
u>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The first option, again, is not admissible due to the Lemma
5.3
.

Now we are going to show that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{\infty}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
. We essentially follow
[
1
,
7
]
, although a few important differences will arise.

For
z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, we denote by

d(z)=\left(\lvert x\rvert^{2\left(\gamma+1\right)}+(\gamma+1)^{2}\lvert y\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\left(\gamma+1\right)}}

(31)

the homogeneous norm associated to
\Delta_{\gamma}
. Note that this norm is homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the anisotropic dilatation

\delta_{\lambda}(x,y)=(\lambda x,\lambda^{\gamma+1}y),\quad\lambda>0.

and, for
\gamma=0
, it reduces to the usual Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The function

\Gamma(z)=\frac{C}{d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}

is a fundamental solution for the Grushin operator with singularity at
z=0
, see
[
5
]
or
[
16
, Appendix B]
.

The constant
C
is a suitable positive constant that depends on
m,\ell
and
\gamma
. Precisely,

C^{-1}=(N_{\gamma}-2)\int\limits_{\{z\mid d(z)=1\}}\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2\gamma}}{\left(\lvert x\rvert^{2\left(1+2\gamma\right)}+\left(1+\gamma\right)^{2}\lvert y\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}S,

where
S
denotes the surface measure of the manifold
\left\{z\mid d(z)=1\right\}
.

We recall the following definition.

Definition 6.4
.

A function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is radially symmetric (radial for short) if there exists a function
v\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

u(z)=v(r)

where
r=d(z)
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For
R>0
we set
B_{R}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid d(\xi)<R\right\}
.

Let us consider a spherical shell
\Omega=B_{R_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}}}
, with
0\leq R_{1}<R_{2}\leq+\infty
. If
u
is a radial function, by
[
15
, Eq. (2.4)]
we get the formula

\int_{\Omega}u(d(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi=s_{N}\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}\rho^{N_{\gamma}-1}u(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho,

(32)

where
s_{N}
is an explicit constant that depends only on
N
.

Proposition 6.5
.

\lVert u_{j}-u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell})}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Sect. 3.1]
and recalling that
u
is a continuous function, we have the representation formula

u(z)=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\quad\hbox{for a.e. $z\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$}.

(33)

Moreover, since
u_{j}
is a solution of (
SP
), we also have

u_{j}(z)=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(\xi)f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\quad\hbox{for a.e. $z\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$}.

(34)

Using (
33
) and (
34
) we estimate
\lvert u-u_{j}\rvert
as follows: for every
z\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
,

\lvert u(z)-u_{j}(z)\rvert\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right).

(35)

Take
\delta>1
. Applying the Hölder’s inequality with the conjugate pairs
(\delta,\tilde{\delta})
we estimate the first integral of (
35
) as

\int_{B_{1}}g(\xi)\frac{\left|f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\right|}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\left(\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{d(z-\xi)^{(N_{\gamma}-2)\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\times\\
\left(\int_{B_{1}}g(\xi)^{\tilde{\delta}}\left|f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\right|^{\tilde{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\delta}}}.

(36)

We compute, using (
32
)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{d(z-\xi)^{(N_{\gamma}-2)\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=s_{N}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho^{N_{\gamma}-1}}{\rho^{(N_{\gamma}-2)\delta}}d\rho\leq C(N_{\gamma}).

Moreover, the second term in the inequality (
36
) converges to zero. In fact, proceeding as in Lemma
5.1
,
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p},\,p\in[2^{*}_{\gamma},\infty)
. Then we can apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Next, the second integral of (
36
) has the following bound:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}g(\xi)\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Again, by the generalized dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(\xi))-f_{0}(u(\xi))\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=0.

Therefore, (
35
) yields
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{\infty}(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
as
j\to\infty
.
∎

The immediate corollary concludes the proof of the first statement of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 6.6
.

u_{j}\geq 0
a.e. in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
for
j
large enough.

Let us now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 6.7
.

If
(f3)
and
(g1)
are satisfied then
u_{j}>0
a.e. in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Proof.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, since
f_{j}
is locally Lipschitz
(f3)
and
0\leq u_{j},u\leq C
, we have

\lvert f_{j}(u_{j}(z))-f_{0}(u(z))\rvert\leq M\lvert u_{j}(z)-u(z)\rvert,

for some
M>0
. For
z\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, using (
33
) and (
34
), we get

\lvert u_{j}(z)-u(z)\rvert\leq C\left(M\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(\xi)\frac{\lvert u_{j}(\xi)-u(\xi)\rvert}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+a_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(\xi)}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right).

Since
g
satisfies
(g1)
, from the above inequality and the previous theorem we get

\lvert u_{j}(z)-u(z)\rvert\leq C\left(M\lVert u_{j}-u\rVert_{\infty}+a_{n}\right)\frac{C(g)}{d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}.

Hence

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}\lvert u_{j}(z)-u(z)\rvert\to 0\penalty\ \hbox{as $j\to\infty$}.

(37)

We claim that

\lim_{d(z)\to+\infty}d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}u(z)>0.

(38)

Using (
33
) it follows that

\lim_{d(z)\to+\infty}d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}u(z)=C\lim_{d(z)\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
\geq C\int_{B_{R}}\frac{g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

(39)

for any
R>0
. For any
R>0
there exists
z\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
such that
d(z)>2R+1
. Hence

d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}\geq\lvert d(z)-d(\xi)\rvert^{N_{\gamma}-2}\geq\lvert d(z)-R\rvert^{N_{\gamma}-2}\geq 2^{2-N_{\gamma}}\left(1+d(z)\right)^{N_{\gamma}-2},

for every
\xi\in B_{R}
. Using the above estimate, for
\xi\in B_{R}
we get

\frac{g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\leq 2^{N_{\gamma}-2}g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi)).

Furthermore,

\frac{g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\to g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))

a.e. in
B_{R}
, as
d(z)\to\infty
. Therefore, the Dominated Convergence Theorem yields

\lim_{d(z)\to+\infty}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{g(\xi)f(u(\xi))d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}{d(z-\xi)^{N_{\gamma}-2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{B_{R}}g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

So, from (
39
) we conclude that

\lim_{d(z)\to+\infty}d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}u(z)\geq C\int_{B_{R}}g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Letting
R\to\infty
and applying the Fatou’s lemma we get

\lim_{d(z)\to\infty}d(z)^{N_{\gamma}-2}u(z)\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(\xi)f_{0}(u(\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

So (
38
) follows by (
33
) and Proposition
6.2
.

Therefore, by (
37
) there exists
j_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
R>>1
such that for
j\geq j_{2},\,u_{j}>0
a.e. on
(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\setminus B_{R}
. Moreover, since
u
is continuous, there exists
\eta>0
such that
u>\eta
on
\overline{B_{R}}
. Therefore, from Proposition
6.5
, there exists
j_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
j\geq j_{3},\,u_{j}>0
a.e. on
\overline{B_{R}}\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
. Thus, by choosing
j_{4}=\max\left\{j_{2},j_{3}\right\}
, we see that for
j\geq j_{4},\,u_{j}>0
a.e. on
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

The validity of the previous argument relies heavily on the representation formula (
33
), which is typically derived from the existence of a Green function associated to the differential operator. For linear differential operators with constant coefficients, the existence of a fundamental solution allows one to construct (continuous) solutions via convolution. by exploiting the invariance of the operator under translations. The Grushin operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
is a differential operator with variable coefficients, and in particular it is invariant under translations only in the subspace
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. So, even if a fundamental solution with singularity at a fixed point is available, it is not trivial to use it in order to derive an explicit Green function. As we have seen, it was proved in
[
18
]
that (
33
) holds true at points of the form
(0,y)
at which the weak solution
u
is continuous. It is interesting to observe that the authors of
[
17
]
proved the equivalence of a representation formula like (
33
) and of a Liouville-type result for the operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
, but only for
C^{2}
solutions. However a full regularity theory for
\Delta_{\gamma}
is not available, this approach remains unapplicable.

Furthermore, the existence of an
approximate
Green function for
\Delta_{\gamma}
was proved in
[
29
]
and exploited in
[
2
]
. Unfortunately the known properties of it are too weak for our purposes, and would lead to replace (
g
1
) with a completely implicit assumption.

Halfspace depth for general measures: The ray basis theorem and its consequences
Petra Laketa, Stanislav Nagy

Abstract
. The halfspace depth is a prominent tool of nonparametric multivariate analysis. The upper level sets of the depth, termed the trimmed regions of a measure, serve as a natural generalization of the quantiles and inter-quantile regions to higher-dimensional spaces. The smallest non-empty trimmed region, coined the halfspace median of a measure, generalizes the median. We focus on the (inverse) ray basis theorem for the halfspace depth, a crucial theoretical result that characterizes the halfspace median by a covering property. First, a novel elementary proof of that statement is provided, under minimal assumptions on the underlying measure. The proof applies not only to the median, but also to other trimmed regions. Motivated by the technical development of the amended ray basis theorem, we specify connections between the trimmed regions, floating bodies, and additional equi-affine convex sets related to the depth. As a consequence, minimal conditions for the strict monotonicity of the depth are obtained. Applications to the computation of the depth and robust estimation are outlined.



1.
Introduction: Characterization of the halfspace median

The lack of natural ordering of the points in multidimensional spaces makes the nonparametric analysis of multivariate data challenging. The depth introduces a data-dependent ordering of the sample points, in the direction from the most central observations being those that attain high depth values, to the peripheral ones with low depth
[
13
,
32
,
34
,
4
]
. A definition of depth-based central regions of the data, which are the regions where the depth exceeds given thresholds, ensues naturally. The smallest non-empty depth region is often termed the depth median set of the data. The depth medians provide convenient robust location estimators, well studied in the literature.

We consider the seminal halfspace (or Tukey) depth, and the general setup of finite Borel (not necessarily probability) measures. Write
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
for the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The set of all closed halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}
. Elements of
\mathcal{H}
can be represented as

H_{x,u}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\leq\left\langle y,u\right\rangle\right\}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
a point in the boundary hyperplane and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
the inner unit normal of
H_{x,u}
. We write
\mathcal{H}(x)=\left\{H_{x,u}\colon u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right\}
for all halfspaces whose boundary passes through
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The collection of all finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. The halfspace depth was first considered by
Tukey, 1975
and later substantially elaborated on by
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
. The
halfspace depth
of a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to (w.r.t.) a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is defined as

(1)

D(x;\mu)=\inf_{H\in\mathcal{H}(x)}\mu(H)=\inf_{H\in\mathcal{H},x\in H}\mu(H).

The two expressions for the depth are easily seen to be equivalent. Our principal interest lies in the concept of the
depth-trimmed regions
(also called
central regions
) of
\mu
, defined for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
by
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon D\left(x;\mu\right)\geq\alpha\right\}
. It is a simple observation that the regions
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
are always closed convex sets, non-increasing in
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
in the sense of set inclusion. For
\alpha\leq 0
we have
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
; for all
\alpha
large enough
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\emptyset
. The supremum of all
\alpha
such that
D_{\alpha}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
is denoted by
\alpha^{*}(\mu)
. We call the set
D_{\alpha^{*}}(\mu)
the
(halfspace) median set
of
\mu
, and denote it by
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
. The median set is always non-empty and compact. Elements of
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
are called
(halfspace) medians
of
\mu
. Any halfspace
H\in\mathcal{H}(x)
that satisfies
D(x;\mu)=\mu(H)
is called a
minimizing halfspace
of
\mu
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The so-called ray basis theorem provides a convenient characterization of a halfspace depth median of a measure
\mu
in terms of its minimizing halfspaces. In its simplest form, the theorem asserts that for measures
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with continuous and positive density, a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a halfspace median of
\mu
if and only if the collection of its minimizing halfspaces covers the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. That result was first proved by
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
, and later extended in
Rousseeuw and Ruts, 1999
. For the special case of uniform measures on convex bodies, such a characterization of the deepest point relates to an early observation of
Grünbaum, 1963
from convex geometry. For details and additional discussion about the ray basis theorem, its history and relevance in both statistics and geometry see
[
20
, Section 4.3.1]
and
[
22
]
.

Our initial goal is to revisit the ray basis theorem, and consolidate its statement by extending it to any depth region
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
of a general measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, under minimal assumptions.

Our paper is concluded with three applications of our results in Section

is not necessarily a single point set. Especially for empirical measures

, that is measures corresponding to datasets, the median set is frequently full-dimensional. In Section

we propose to single out the collection of those medians that satisfy an additional covering property. We obtain a smaller collection of covering medians of

. An algorithm for finding covering medians is given. In Section

we obtain a consequence of the general ray basis theorem regarding the structure of the central regions

for

also in the case when they are not full-dimensional, in a spirit similar to that lately employed in

. Finally, in Section

Dyckerhoff, 2017

\mu
, it ensures the almost sure uniform convergence of the depth upper level sets estimated from the data towards their population counterparts. As such, sufficient conditions for strict monotonicity find applications in the estimation of the depth-trimmed regions

from data in multivariate statistics. The proofs of all theoretical results are provided in an extensive appendix accompanying the paper.

1.1.
Preliminaries and notation

In the proofs of our results, we use tools from measure theory, as well as from the theory of convex sets. Our general reference to the concepts used from convexity theory is
[
25
]
. We now set the most important notations used in the paper, and state a preliminary observation about the halfspace depth that will be useful later. For a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
its interior, closure and boundary are denoted by
\intrOp\left(A\right)
,
\clOp\left(A\right)
and
\bdOp\left(A\right)
, respectively. Denote by
\affOp\left(A\right)
and
\convOp\left(A\right)
the affine hull and the convex hull of

. The sets
\relintOp\left(A\right)
,
\relclOp\left(A\right)
and
\relbdOp\left(A\right)
represent the (relative) interior, closure and boundary of
A
in the space
\affOp\left(A\right)
, and
\dim A=\dim\left(\affOp\left(A\right)\right)
is the dimension of
A
. The complement to
A
is
A^{\mathsf{c}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus A
. For sets
A
and
B
we write
A\subset B
if
A\subseteq B
and
A\neq B
. We say that
H\in\mathcal{H}
is a touching halfspace of a non-empty convex set

if
H\cap\clOp\left(A\right)\neq\emptyset
and
\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap A=\emptyset
. The collection of all touching halfspaces to
A
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}(A)
. We also define
\mathcal{H}(\emptyset)=\mathcal{H}
. In this notation,
\mathcal{H}(x)
is the same as
\mathcal{H}(\{x\})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Well-behaved measures.
We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is
smooth
if
\mu(\bdOp\left(H\right))=0
for each
H\in\mathcal{H}
. We call
\mu

smooth at
a convex set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\mu(\bdOp\left(H\right))=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}(A)
, and smooth at a point

if it is smooth at
\left\{x\right\}
. Smoothness of
\mu
at a point is a condition stronger than
\mu
being atom-less; yet, it is still weaker than smoothness in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A measure
\mu
is said to have
contiguous support
if the support of
\mu
cannot be separated by a slab between two parallel hyperplanes of non-empty interior with zero
\mu
-mass. Finally,
\mu
is said to have
contiguous support at
a convex set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
if

(3)

for each
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(A)
and
H^{\prime}\subset H\in\mathcal{H}
,
\bdOp\left(H\right)\cap\intrOp\left(A\right)\neq\emptyset
implies
\mu(H^{\prime})<\mu(H)
.

Note that the condition (
3
) is void if
\intrOp\left(A\right)=\emptyset
, and therefore it is enough to consider
A
full-dimensional. In that case, (
3
) means that any shift
H\supset H^{\prime}
of a touching halfspace
H^{\prime}
of
A
has
\mu
-mass larger than
H^{\prime}
. It is satisfied if, for instance, the set
A
is a subset of the support of
\mu
. An absolutely continuous measure is smooth (at any
A
convex). A measure with connected support has contiguous support (at any convex subset
A
of the support of
\mu
).

Minimizing halfspaces.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
recall that
H\in\mathcal{H}(x)
is a minimizing halfspace of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
at
x
if
D(x;\mu)=\mu(H)
. In general, the set of minimizing halfspaces of
\mu
at a point may be empty. It is guaranteed to be non-empty if, for instance, the measure
\mu
is smooth at
x
. Our first observation that will prove to be useful in the sequel is that it is always possible to find
H\in\mathcal{H}(x)
with the property
\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq D\left(x;\mu\right)
. We call such a halfspace

a

of
\mu
at
x
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
there exists a generalized minimizing halfspace of
\mu
at any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
\mu
is smooth at
x
, then there exists a minimizing halfspace of
\mu
at
x
.

Additional notations.
In addition to the upper level set of the halfspace depth
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon D\left(x;\mu\right)\geq\alpha\right\}
, we also consider the set

U_{\alpha}(\mu)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon D(x;\mu)>\alpha\right\}=\bigcup_{\beta>\alpha}D_{\beta}(\mu).

It is convex, but not always closed. As will be seen later in the paper, the set

is also not open in general. Both

and

A face of a convex set

and

in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

We write

for the relatively open (that is, not containing

) line segment between distinct point

2.
The general ray basis theorem

The standard ray basis theorem, as dubbed by
Rousseeuw and Ruts, 1999
, asserts that under certain conditions on a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, the halfspace median
x
of
\mu
is characterized by the fact that
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be covered by minimizing halfspaces of
x
. The formal statement of the theorem is given below.

Theorem 2

(
[
24
, Propositions 8 and 12]
)
.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, the following holds true:

(i)

If for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(4)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(x)\colon\mu(H)=D(x;\mu)\right\},

then
x
is a median of
\mu
, i.e.
D(x;\mu)=\alpha^{*}(\mu)
.

(ii)

If
x
is a median of
\mu
and, in addition,
\mu
is absolutely continuous with a density that is continuous and positive in an open convex set, then (
4
) is true for
x
.

The proof of the direct part
(i)
of Theorem
2
is simple: since
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is covered by halfspaces of
\mu
-mass exactly
D(x;\mu)
, each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
must be contained in such a halfspace
H_{y}
, and consequently
D(y;\mu)\leq\mu(H_{y})=D(x;\mu)
, as follows directly from the definition of the halfspace depth (
1
). The proof of the so-called
inverse ray basis theorem
, stated in part
(ii)
, is slightly more involved, and technical. Our aim is to obtain a result similar to Theorem
2
for any depth region
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, under minimal conditions. We prove that for well-behaved measures it is possible to cover all points outside
\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
with closed halfspaces
H
whose
\mu
-mass is bounded from above by
\alpha
. For measures that may not be smooth the appropriate formulation of the latter condition turns out to be
\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha
. In the following lemma we introduce a condition that will play an important role in the formulation of the extended ray basis theorem. We treat not only the depth-trimmed regions
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, but also
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
, as well as their interiors.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
S\in\{D_{\alpha}(\mu),U_{\alpha}(\mu),\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right),\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\}
. Then

(5)

S^{\mathsf{c}}\subseteq\left(\intrOp\left(S\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}\subseteq\bigcup\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\mbox{ and }\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S=\emptyset\}.

Application of Lemma
3
to the median set
S=\intrOp\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)
gives a slightly weaker version of the standard inverse ray basis theorem. Suppose that the measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
satisfies the conditions of part
(ii)
of Theorem
2
. First, it is not difficult to observe that for such
\mu
our auxiliary Lemma
1
about the existence of a generalized minimizing halfspace ensures that the median set
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
cannot be full-dimensional.

Because the median set is always convex, it is not full-dimensional if and only if its interior is empty. Therefore, for any

gives that the whole space can be covered by halfspaces

(i)

of

\alpha^{*}(\mu)

(ii)

without a particular connection to any fixed point

Our intention is now to extend the results of Lemma

Definition
.

For a convex set

The collection

Lemma 4
.

Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and a bounded convex set

satisfying (
5
) with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. For any

there exists

such that

and

S^{\mathsf{c}}\subseteq(\intrOp\left(S\right))^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(S)\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\right\}.

Lemma
4
is technical, but presents an important intermediate result. It provides multiple consequences that we explore in the sequel. Lemma
3
allows us to apply Lemma
4
to the sets
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
,
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
or their interiors, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. This is the foundation for the most general statement of the ray basis theorem that can be devised for general measures. To obtain finer results, it is necessary to impose additional assumptions. In the literature on the halfspace depth, two typical assumptions are the smoothness of
\mu
, and the contiguity of its support. We require these conditions only locally, at the considered depth regions. Application of Lemma
4

S=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
yields the following generalization of the inverse ray basis theorem.

Theorem 5

(
General inverse ray basis theorem
)
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\notin\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
and
F\in\mathfrak{F}(x,D_{\alpha}(\mu))
there exists
H(x,F)\in\mathcal{H}(D_{\alpha}(\mu),F)
such that
x\in H(x,F)
and
\mu(\intrOp\left(H(x,F)\right))\leq\alpha
.
In particular,

(6)

\left(\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(D_{\alpha}(\mu))\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\right\}.

Additionally, if

(i)

\mu
is smooth at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, then
\mu(H(x,F))=\alpha
and

\left(\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(D_{\alpha}(\mu))\colon\mu(H)=\alpha\right\}.

(ii)

\mu
has contiguous support at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, then

\left(\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\right\}.

(iii)

\mu
is smooth at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
and has contiguous support at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, then

\left(\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(H)=\alpha\right\}.

Note that if
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
fails to be full-dimensional, the left hand sides in the formulas in Theorem

, and the theorem therefore gives conditions under which the whole sample space can be covered by halfspaces of limited
\mu

contained in a hyperplane, also the condition from part
(ii)
of Theorem

Before moving to the discussion about the relevance of Theorem
5

as another corollary of Lemma
4

Theorem 6
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\notin\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)

\mu(\intrOp\left(H(x,F)\right))\leq\alpha

In particular,

(7)

\left(\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\right\}.

Additionally, if

Comparison of Theorem

and

— for

. We postpone this discussion into Section

If

where it was noted that for

is always a closed set. Our first consequence of the general ray basis theorem is the following observation concerning the dimensionality of the median set. It presents a refinement of

Corollary 7
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

(i)

If
\mu
has contiguous support at

, then
\dim\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)\neq d
.

(ii)

If
\mu

Without the assumptions of smoothness and contiguous support, the median set may be of any dimension. Consider, for instance
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
that gives mass

Corollary 8

(
Inverse ray basis theorem for the median
)
.

Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is smooth at

\left(\intrOp\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)\right)^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(D^{*}\left(\mu\right))\colon\mu(H)=\alpha^{*}(\mu)\right\}.

If, in addition,
\mu
has contiguous support at

4
) holds true for any

The assumptions of Corollary

are weaker than those in Theorem

(i)

instead of the existence of the density

(ii)

Remark

(Uniqueness of the median)
.

In Corollary
7
we show that under the assumptions of both contiguous support and smoothness of
\mu
at
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
, the median set cannot be of dimension
d
, or
d-1
. In particular, for
d=1
and
2
it follows that the median must be unique. It is tempting to claim that for a smooth measure
\mu
with convex support (part
(ii)
of Theorem
2
), the median set must consist of a single point, compare with
[
18
, Proposition 7]
and the discussion in
[
26
, Section 3]
. Surprisingly, it turns out that there exist probability distributions with a density that is smooth and positive in a convex set in dimension
d>2
that fail to possess a unique halfspace median. The appropriate conditions for the uniqueness of the halfspace median in higher dimensions turn out to be not only smoothness and contiguous support, but, quite surprisingly, also a certain integrability assumption. For a detailed discussion on the problem of the uniqueness of the halfspace median we refer to
[
23
]
. For that reason, one has to be careful when interpreting the inverse ray basis theorem in Corollary
8
. Suppose that a measure
\mu
is smooth with contiguous support. Then for any point
x\in D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
it is true that the sample space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is covered by minimizing halfspaces of
\mu
at
x
. That, however, does not mean that the median of
\mu
must be unique. Especially in higher dimension, the non-trivial median set may still lie in the boundary of all the halfspaces from the covering system.

We continue by giving examples that demonstrate that the assumptions of Corollary
8
are difficult to be weakened. In our first example we show that without local smoothness, even in the case when
x
is the unique median of
\mu
, it may fail to satisfy the covering condition (
4
).

3.
Depth regions and floating body

Formula (
2
) allows us to write any depth region
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
as an intersection of closed halfspaces whose complements have
\mu
-mass smaller than
\alpha
. Another important affine equivariant set is the
floating body
of
\mu
corresponding to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=\bigcap\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(H^{\mathsf{c}})\leq\alpha\right\},

as defined in
[
2
, Section 5]
. According to the discussion in
[
20
]
, the floating bodies are of great interest in both geometry and probability theory. For the special case of
\mu
a uniform distribution on a full-dimensional convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the floating body has a compelling mechanical interpretation — the set
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
can be described as the part of the convex solid
K
of (volumetric mass) density
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{*}(\mu))
that never submerges beneath the surface of water of unit density, when fully rotated on the surface. The history of the research on floating bodies goes well into the 19th century. In statistics, a construction equivalent to the floating body of a measure is much more recent, and sometimes referred to as the multivariate trimming
[
21
,
17
]
. Being intersections of closed sets, both
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
and
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
are closed. In what follows we use the results of Section
2
, and precise the connections between the depth regions
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
,
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
, the floating body
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
, and a further set that turns out to be of interest in our analysis

U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\bigcap\left\{\intrOp\left(H\right)\colon H\in\mathcal{H},\ \mu(H^{\mathsf{c}})\leq\alpha\right\}.

We demonstrate that this last region is an upper level set of a function closely related to the halfspace depth

D^{\circ}(x;\mu)=\inf_{x\in H}\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right)),

considered in, e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 1]
. Note also that
(U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu))^{\mathsf{c}}=\bigcup\{H\in\mathcal{H}\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha\}
already appeared in Theorem
6
, meaning that
U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
must be an open set. The following theorem comprehensively covers the inter-relations between all these affine constructions, and generalizes several results that can be found scattered in the relevant literature on multivariate trimming concepts
[
26
,
17
,
18
,
3
,
20
]
.

Theorem 9
.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

(8)

\displaystyle U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\subseteq\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\subseteq U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)\subseteq D_{\alpha}(\mu).

Additionally,

(i)

U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon D^{\circ}(x;\mu)>\alpha\right\}
.

(ii)

if
\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\neq\emptyset
, then
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
.

(iii)

if
\mu
has contiguous support at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, then
\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
.

(iv)

if for each
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(D_{\alpha}(\mu))
we have that
H^{\prime}\subset H\in\mathcal{H}
implies
\mu(H^{\prime})<\mu(H)
, then
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
.

(v)

if
\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\neq\emptyset
and
\mu
has contiguous support at
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, then
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
.

An application of Theorem
9
to the estimation of the depth regions
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
from datasets is given in Section
4.3
. The condition of non-empty interior of
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
that figures in parts
(ii)
and
(v)
of the previous theorem is not restrictive, as shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 10
.

If for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a point
x\in U_{\alpha}(\mu)
such that
\mu
is smooth at
x
, then
\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\neq\emptyset
.

Combining Lemma
10
and Theorems
6
and
9
we get that for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with a density and
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{*}(\mu))
the set
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
is open and
U_{\alpha}(\mu)\subset\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
. If the support of
\mu
is, in addition, contiguous, we can also write
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
. In the simplest situation of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with a density that is positive in the convex support of
\mu
, we can therefore write
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, and the floating bodies completely coincide with the central regions of the depth. The last situation is common in the literature on floating bodies in geometry, where all the above definitions are used interchangeably. In the general setup of the depth and (probability) measures, it is however necessary to differentiate between them.

Our statement of Theorem
9
is strict — none of the inclusions can be reversed, in general. Each strict upper level set
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
of a smooth measure
\mu
is open (Theorem
6
), and thus strictly smaller than
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
. It is easy to construct a measure without contiguous support that violates
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
, see e.g.
[
20
, Figure 7]
. Even for measures with contiguous support, equality
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
from part
(iv)
of Theorem
9
can fail; consider
\mu
being the Dirac measure at the origin
o\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\alpha=1
. In that case,
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)=\emptyset
, while
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\{o\}
. In the following example we show that in the case when
\intrOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=\emptyset
, the halfspace depth may fail to satisfy
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=D_{\alpha}(\mu)
(the so-called strict monotonicity property), even under the assumption of contiguous support. The example demonstrates that also the remaining set inclusions in Theorem
9
cannot be reversed for general measures without local assumptions.

Example 3
.

Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
given by a mixture of the uniform distribution on the unit disc
C=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon\left\|x\right\|\leq 1\right\}
with mass
1/4
, and two atoms located at
m=\left(0,1\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
and
z=\left(0,1/2\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
with masses
1/2
and
1/4
, respectively. Point
m
is certainly the unique halfspace median of
\mu
, with
D\left(m;\mu\right)=\alpha^{*}(\mu)=1/2
. It is also easy to see that the halfspace depth of
\mu
at the point
o=\left(0,0\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
equals
1/8
. Because of the two large atoms of
\mu
, all points in the open line segment
L(o,z)
have also depth
1/8
. Similarly, all points in
L(z,m)
have the same depth as
z
, that is
D\left(z;\mu\right)=1/8+1/4=3/8
. Because for any
y\notin L[o,m]
certainly
D\left(y;\mu\right)<1/8
, we have for
\alpha=1/8
that
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=L[o,m]
, while
\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=L[z,m]
, meaning that the strict monotonicity property of the halfspace depth is violated, see also the left hand panel of Figure
3
. In addition, it is easy to verify that
U_{\alpha}^{FB}(\mu)
is equal to
D_{\alpha}(\mu)\supset\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
, while the set
U^{\circ}_{\alpha}(\mu)
from (
8
) is empty. We conclude that, in general, one cannot write
U_{\alpha}(\mu)
as a simple intersection of halfspaces, as possible for
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
in (
2
).

4.
Applications: Refined medians, computation, and consistency

4.1.
Covering halfspace median

Our first application of the ray basis theorems from Section
2
is motivated by another refinement of the main equality (
6
) applied to
\alpha=\alpha^{*}(\mu)
: For any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
there exists a point
x\in D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
that allows us to cover the whole space with halfspaces
H\in\mathcal{H}(x)
whose interior has
\mu
-mass at most

\gamma^{*}(\mu)=\inf\{\alpha>0\colon\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)=\emptyset\}=\sup\{\alpha>0\colon\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)\neq\emptyset\}.

Observe that
\gamma^{*}(\mu)\leq\alpha^{*}(\mu)
. Therefore, the following claim is stronger than the inverse ray basis theorem not only

(i)

because it asserts the existence of such a special point in
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
, but also in the sense that

(ii)

the masses of the halfspaces that cover
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are ensured to be at most
\gamma^{*}(\mu)
, and not just
\alpha^{*}(\mu)
.

Theorem 11
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
there exists a point
x\in D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
, such that

(9)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(x)\colon\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\gamma^{*}(\mu)\right\}.

Suppose that
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
is not a single point set. It is certainly of interest to single out the subset
C^{*}\left(\mu\right)
of
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
of those points that satisfy the additional covering property (
9
) characteristic to centrally located points. We call such medians the
covering halfspace medians
(or simply
covering medians
) of the measure
\mu
. The concept of covering medians is interesting especially in the situation when the median set is full-dimensional, as frequently happens with data generated from a smooth distribution. In that situation, the relatively large median set typically reduces to a smaller subset of the most centrally located points being the covering medians.

Example 4
.

For an empirical measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
with atoms at points

,

,
c=\left(-5,0\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
,
d=\left(1,3\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
,
e=\left(-1,3\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
and

, the median set
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
is equal to the triangle determined by points
d
,
e
and
f
, while
C^{*}\left(\mu\right)
is a smaller triangle determined by the lines
l(a,e)
,
l(b,f)
and
l(c,d)
, see the right hand panel of Figure
3
.

The covering medians present a genuine refinement of the halfspace medians. They can be shown to satisfy an array of properties expected from well-behaved location estimators, such as

existence as proved in Theorem

;

(ii)

The covering medians are also intimately connected with the robustness properties of the halfspace median and the depth. All these results will be presented elsewhere in an appropriate context. Here we mention only several basic observations closely linked to Theorem
11
. The first one demonstrates that unlike the standard halfspace median set, the set of the covering medians does not have to be convex.

.

The proof of Theorem

allows us to devise a simple algorithm for finding the covering medians of
\mu
listed as Algorithm

Search for a covering halfspace median of a measure
\mu

Let

. Denote by

4.2.
Trimmed regions for atomic measures

Our second application of the generalized ray basis theorem is a necessary condition on the depth regions

of atomic measures.

Corollary 13
.

For
\mu
an atomic measure with finitely many atoms,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\notin\intrOp\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
and
F\in\mathfrak{F}(x,D_{\alpha}(\mu))

and
\mu(\intrOp\left(H(x,F)\right))<\alpha
. Moreover, each face

of the convex polytope

of dimension

is contained in the convex hull of at least

atoms located in a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A special case of this result in the situation when

is full-dimensional was used in the derivation of a fast algorithm for the computation of the depth regions of datasets in
[
15
]
. The present general version may find applications in the computation of the halfspace depth of datasets in the situation when
\dim\left(D_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)<d
. For example, suppose that for given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
corresponding to a dataset the algorithm from
[
15
]
fails to find an interior point of the region
D_{\alpha}(\mu)
. The reason may be twofold: either

(i)

D_{\alpha}(\mu)
is an empty set, or

(ii)

it is less than full-dimensional.

Corollary

asserts that in the latter case, the region

must be contained in a hyperplane spanned by

data points. Thus, the search for

may continue in the intersection of data-determined hyperplanes, and the last known non-empty region

\beta<\alpha
.

4.3.
Estimation of central regions

Part
(v)
of Theorem
9
yields an important equality. According to
Dyckerhoff, 2017
the halfspace depth is
strictly monotone
at
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
if for all
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{*}(\mu))
we can write
D_{\alpha}(\mu)=\clOp\left(U_{\alpha}(\mu)\right)
. Strict monotonicity is a crucial assumption that ensures the sample version consistency of the halfspace depth-trimmed regions
[
8
, Example 4.2]
. As a consequence of Lemma
10
we obtain a condition ensuring the strict monotonicity of the halfspace depth — for a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
to have a strictly monotone depth it is enough to assume contiguous support and smoothness at a single point of the median set. In particular, under these very mild conditions, it is possible to guarantee the almost sure uniform convergence of the sample depth trimmed regions to their population counterparts. To precise this, we need to consider a topology on the space of compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A natural choice is that given by the Hausdorff distance
[
25
, Section 1.8]
. For
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact the Hausdorff distance of
K
and
L
is given by

\delta_{H}(K,L)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in L}\left|x-y\right|,\sup_{x\in L}\inf_{y\in K}\left|x-y\right|\right\}.

Corollary 14

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

and any

converging weakly to

This result follows directly from
[

and our previous discussion. The
General assumption
in

is satisfied thanks to our Lemma

. In those references analogous consistency results are proved under more restrictive conditions.

Appendix A
Proofs of the theoretical results

We begin with a lemma collecting several properties of the convergence of halfspaces.

Lemma 15
.

Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, a sequence of closed halfspaces
\{H_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The following claims hold true:

(i)

If there is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
x_{n}\in\bdOp\left(H_{n}\right)
and
x_{n}\rightarrow x

\{H_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
converging to a closed halfspace

.

(ii)

If

and
\mu(\intrOp\left(H_{n}\right))\leq\alpha
for each
n
, then
\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha
.

(iii)

If

,
\mu(\intrOp\left(H_{n}\right))\leq\alpha
for each

,

and
\mu(\bdOp\left(H\right))=0
, then
\mu(H)=\alpha
.

(iv)

If
H_{n}\rightarrow H
and
x\notin\intrOp\left(H_{n}\right)
for each
n
, then
x\notin\intrOp\left(H\right)
.

Proof.

For part
(i)
, denote
H_{n}=H_{x_{n},v_{n}}
. The set of unit vectors
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is bounded, meaning that
\left\{v_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
contains a convergent subsequence
\{v_{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, whose limit point we denote by
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We obtain that
H_{n_{k}}\rightarrow H_{x,v}=H\in\mathcal{H}(x)
as
k\to\infty
as desired. Part
(ii)
is a consequence of the Fatou lemma
[
6
, Lemma 4.3.3]
and the fact that
\intrOp\left(H\right)\subseteq{\lim\inf}_{k\to\infty}\intrOp\left(H_{n_{k}}\right)
. Under the assumptions of part
(iii)
there exists
x\in D_{\alpha}(\mu)\cap H
. For this point we can directly write by part
(ii)
of this lemma
\alpha\leq D\left(x;\mu\right)\leq\mu(H)=\mu(\intrOp\left(H\right))\leq\alpha
. To prove
(iv)
, assume that
x\in\intrOp\left(H\right)
. Then
x\in\intrOp\left(H_{n}\right)
starting from some index, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 16
.

If
H\in\mathcal{H}
and

is a convex set such that
\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S=\emptyset
and
\relintOp\left(S\right)\cap H\neq\emptyset
, then
\affOp\left(S\right)\subseteq\bdOp\left(H\right)
.

Proof.

Consider
x\in\relintOp\left(S\right)\cap H
. Since
x\in\relintOp\left(S\right)
, there exists a ball
B_{x}
centred at
x
in space
\affOp\left(S\right)
so that
B_{x}\subset S
. Since
\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S=\emptyset
we get

and
B_{x}\subset\bdOp\left(H\right)
, meaning that
\affOp\left(S\right)=\affOp\left(B_{x}\right)\subseteq\bdOp\left(H\right)
.
∎

Lemma 17
.

For

non-empty convex and

the collection

contains a non-empty set.

Proof.

In the case that

, there is a face

of
\bdOp\left(A\right)
containing

in its relative interior
[

contains a non-empty set. Otherwise, let

guarantees that there exists a touching halfspace

of

Proof of Theorem

[
25

is an intersection of a larger collection of halfspaces than the depth region

Inclusion

Suppose that there exists

such that

\mu(H_{y,v}^{\mathsf{c}})\leq\alpha

Part
(i)

Pick

and note that

. We reached a contradiction, since for such a halfspace

must be contained in

Part
(i)
: Inclusion

Under the considered condition of

A.11.
Proof of Theorem

We distinguish two cases according to whether the median set
D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
is full-dimensional, or not.

Case (i):
\intrOp\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)=\emptyset
.

We write
a=\gamma^{*}(\mu)
and start with
U_{a}(\mu)=\bigcup_{\beta>a}D_{\beta}(\mu)
. Since
\intrOp\left(D_{\beta}(\mu)\right)=\emptyset
for each
\beta>a
and sets

are nested, we conclude that also
\intrOp\left(U_{a}(\mu)\right)=\emptyset

applied with

can be covered by halfspaces

such that

for each such

. Since
\affOp\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)\subseteq\affOp\left(U_{a}(\mu)\right)

Case (ii):
\intrOp\left(D^{*}\left(\mu\right)\right)\neq\emptyset
.

In this case,
\gamma^{*}(\mu)=\alpha^{*}(\mu)
. Denote
S_{0}=D^{*}\left(\mu\right)
. This is a full-dimensional convex set, and for its barycentre

and
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
the uniform probability distribution on
S_{0}
we know that

, due to a result of Grünbaum, see
[
20
, Theorem 3]
. Let
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}(x_{0})
be a generalized minimizing halfspace of
\mu
at
x_{0}
in the sense of Lemma
1
, i.e.
\mu\left(\intrOp\left(H_{0}\right)\right)\leq\alpha^{*}(\mu)
. Writing
\vol(A)
for the
d
-dimensional volume of a measurable set
A
, this implies
\nu_{0}(H_{0})=\vol(S_{0}\cap H_{0})/\vol(S_{0})\geq D(x_{0};\nu_{0})>\mathrm{e}^{-1}
. Denote
S_{1}=\clOp\left(S_{0}\setminus H_{0}\right)

\vol(S_{1})=\vol(S_{0})(1-\nu_{0}(H_{0}))<\vol(S_{0})(1-\mathrm{e}^{-1})
. We iterate the previous procedure and for

\mu
at

as

. Then

, and consequently
\intrOp\left(S\right)=\emptyset
.

5
) of our main Lemma

5

, because

By the induction step,
S_{k}^{\mathsf{c}}
is covered by halfspaces
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
\mu\left(\intrOp\left(H\right)\right)\leq\alpha^{*}(\mu)
and
\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S_{k+1}\subseteq\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S_{k}=\emptyset
. Adding to that collection the halfspace

that also satisfies
\mu\left(\intrOp\left(H_{k}\right)\right)\leq\alpha^{*}(\mu)
and
\intrOp\left(H_{k}\right)\cap S_{k+1}=\emptyset
, we obtain a covering of
S_{k+1}^{\mathsf{c}}
with the desired property, i.e. also
S_{k+1}
satisfies (
5
). We finally show that also
S
satisfies (
5
). Take any
x\notin S
and the first index
k=0,1,2,\dots
such that
x\notin S_{k}
. There exists
H\in\mathcal{H}
with

,

\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S_{k}=\emptyset
. Since

, it follows that
\intrOp\left(H\right)\cap S=\emptyset
. Lemma
4
can now be applied to the set
S
to give the desired result, as follows by
\intrOp\left(S\right)=\emptyset
.

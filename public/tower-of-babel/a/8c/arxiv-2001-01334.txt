Infinitely many Lagrangian fillings
Roger Casals, Honghao Gao

Abstract
. We prove that all maximal-tb positive Legendrian torus links ( n , m ) (n,m) in the standard contact 3-sphere, except for ( 2 , m ) (2,m) , ( 3 , 3 ) , ( 3 , 4 ) (3,3),(3,4) and ( 3 , 5 ) (3,5) , admit infinitely many Lagrangian fillings in the standard symplectic 4-ball. This is proven by constructing infinite order Lagrangian concordances which induce faithful actions of the modular group PSL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) \operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) and the mapping class group M 0 , 4 M_{0,4} into the coordinate rings of algebraic varieties associated to Legendrian links. In particular, our results imply that there exist Lagrangian concordance monoids with subgroups of exponential-growth, and yield Stein surfaces homotopic to a 2-sphere with infinitely many distinct exact Lagrangian surfaces of higher-genus. We also show that there exist infinitely many satellite and hyperbolic knots with Legendrian representatives admitting infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings.

1.
Introduction

We show that essentially all maximal-tb positive Legendrian torus links in the standard contact 3-sphere remarkably admit infinitely many non-Hamiltonian isotopic exact Lagrangian fillings in the standard symplectic
4
-ball. Heretofore, the existence of Legendrian links with infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings remained open.

In fact, the faithful
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
representation in our Theorem
1.1
allows us to obtain several consequences. We present new results for Lagrangian concordance monoids, including the first known example of a Lagrangian concordance of infinite order, the existence of an exponential-growth subgroup in the fundamental group of the space of Legendrian links isotopic to
\Lambda(3,6)
, and the existence of Weinstein 4-manifolds homotopic to the 2-sphere with infinitely many non-Hamiltonian isotopic exact Lagrangian surfaces of higher-genus in the same smooth isotopy class. In addition, we construct infinitely many instances of both satellite and hyperbolic knots in the 3-sphere with Legendrian representatives with infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings in the standard symplectic 4-ball.

1.2.
Main Results

Let
\Lambda(n,m)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
be the maximal-tb positive Legendrian
(n,m)
-torus link,
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, as depicted in Figure
1
. Positive Legendrian torus knots are Legendrian simple positive braids
[
26
]
, and thus are uniquely determined by their Thurston-Bennequin invariants and their rotation numbers. These Legendrian links can be obtained by considering the positive braids
\beta=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot\sigma_{n-1})^{m+n}
in
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
and satelliting the zero section
S^{1}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
to the standard Legendrian unknot
\Lambda(1,1)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Let
\mathcal{L}(n,m)
be the space of Legendrian links isotopic to the maximal-tb Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(n,m)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
, with base point an arbitrary but fixed maximal-tb Legendrian representative.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))
be the moduli space of framed sheaves associated to the Legendrian link
\Lambda(n,m)
, as we shall introduce in Section
3
. This
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))
is an algebraic variety
[
64
]
, and in our case it will be a quasi-projective subvariety of the projective Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(n,n+m)
. Since
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))
is a Legendrian isotopy invariant
[
39
,
64
,
65
]
, it defines a monodromy representation

\Gamma:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(n,m))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))),

into the space of algebraic automorphisms of the algebraic variety
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))
. In turn, by pull-back, we obtain a representation

\Gamma^{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(n,m))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))]),

into the automorphisms of the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))]
of
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))
. In particular, a set of based loops
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}:S^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(n,m)
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
, gives rise to a monodromy representation

\Gamma^{*}:\langle[C_{1}],\ldots,[C_{r}]\rangle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(n,m))]).

of the subgroup
\langle[C_{1}],\ldots,[C_{r}]\rangle\leq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(n,m))
generated by the homotopy classes of the based maps
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}:S^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(n,m)
. The first result we present is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(3,6)
be the space of Legendrian links isotopic to the maximal-tb Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(3,6)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Then there exist two based loops
A,B:S^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(3,6)
, and a regular function
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(3,6))]
such that the monodromy representation

\Gamma^{*}:\langle[A],[B]\rangle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(3,6))])

restricts to a faithful modular representation

\Gamma^{*}|_{\mathcal{O}(\Delta)}:\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O}(\Delta))

along the orbit
\mathcal{O}(\Delta)
of the function
\Delta
.
\Box

In Theorem
1.1
, we choose the base point for the space
\mathcal{L}(3,6)
to be the Legendrian link in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
whose front projection is depicted in Figure
1
, under an arbitrary but fixed choice of Darboux chart
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. In the statement of Theorem
1.1
,
\langle[A],[B]\rangle\subseteq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(3,6))
denotes the subgroup generated by the homotopy classes
[A],[B]\in\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(3,6))
, with concatenation of based loops as its group operation. The modular group
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
shall appear geometrically as the free product
\mathbb{Z}_{3}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, with the factor
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
generated by the restriction of
[A]
and the factor
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
generated by the restriction of
[B]
, and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(3,6))])
denotes the group of (cluster) automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(3,6))]
. Theorem
1.1
is remarkable in that
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is an infinite group and thus provides the first result of its kind in the study of
3
-dimensional Legendrian links.

Remark 1.2
.

The reason for the choice of the Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(3,6)
is that it is the geometric source of the extended root system
E_{8}^{(1,1)}
. Indeed, it can be understood as the maximal-tb Legendrian approximation of the transverse link of the unimodal
J_{10}
singularity
[
2
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
shall clarify how the
E_{8}^{(1,1)}
algebraic structure arises from
\Lambda(3,6)
.
\Box

We show that the Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(4,4)
also exhibits a noteworthy symmetry:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(4,4)
be the space of Legendrian links isotopic to the maximal-tb Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(4,4)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Then there exist three based loops
\Xi_{i}:S^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(4,4)
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, and a subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(4,4))]
such that the monodromy representation

\Gamma^{*}:\langle[\Xi_{1}],[\Xi_{2}],[\Xi_{3}]\rangle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(4,4))])

restricts to a faithful representation

\Gamma^{*}|_{\mathcal{O}(F)}:M_{0,4}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{O}(F))

of the mapping class group
M_{0,4}
along the orbit
\mathcal{O}(F)
.
\Box

The mapping class group
M_{0,4}
of the four-punctured 2-sphere contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
with finite index and it is thus infinite. In Theorem
1.3
, the base point for the space
\mathcal{L}(4,4)
is chosen to be the Legendrian link in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
with front projection as depicted in Figure
1
, also under an arbitrary but fixed choice of Darboux chart
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. The subgroup
\langle[\Xi_{1}],[\Xi_{2}],[\Xi_{3}]\rangle\subseteq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(4,4))
has loop concatenation as its group operation.

Remark 1.4
.

The two groups
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
and
M_{0,4}
featured in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are akin to each other in that there are two group isomorhisms
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\cong B_{3}/Z(B_{3})
and
M_{0,4}\cong B^{s}_{4}/Z(B^{s}_{4})
, where
B_{3}
denotes the braid group in 3-strands,
B^{s}_{4}
denotes the spherical braid group in 4-strands, and
Z(B_{3})
and
Z(B^{s}_{4})
denote their respective centers.
\Box

Let us now state implications of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, all of which are new results in low-dimensional contact and symplectic topology.

1.3.
Lagrangian Fillings

Consider the subset

\mathcal{H}:=\{(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}:n\leq m,3\leq n,6\leq m\}\cup\{(4,4),(4,5),(5,5)\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}.

The first consequence of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
is:

Corollary 1.5
.

The Legendrian torus link
\Lambda(n,m)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
,
(n,m)\in\mathcal{H}
, admits infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings in the standard symplectic
4
-ball
(D^{4},\omega_{\text{st}})
.
\Box

For each
\Lambda(n,m)
, these infinitely many exact Lagrangian fillings are smoothly isotopic and not Hamiltonian isotopic. Note that both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
are needed in order to cover all
\Lambda(n,m)
for
(n,m)\in\mathcal{H}
. That said, Theorem
1.1
suffices in order to conclude Corollary
1.5
for
(n,m)\in(\mathcal{H}\setminus\{(4,4),(4,5),(5,5)\})
and thus Theorem
1.3
is included to achieve Corollary
1.5
for
(n,m)=(4,4),(4,5)
and
(5,5)
. It should be noted that the article
[
64
]
succeeded in constructing
finitely
many Lagrangian fillings of the maximal-tb Legendrian
(n,m)
-torus link, as many as maximal pairwise weakly separated
n
-element subsets
[
50
,
58
]
of
[1,n+m]
, a finite number which is bounded above by
nm+1
. Corollary
1.5
implies that these finitely many exact Lagrangian fillings do not exhaust all possible, actually infinitely many, exact Lagrangian fillings.

Every knot
K\subseteq S^{3}
is either a torus knot, a satellite knot or a hyperbolic knot, as proven in
[
66
, Theorem 2.3]
by W.P. Thurston. Let us consider a Legendrian representative
\Lambda_{K}\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
of the smooth type
K\subseteq S^{3}
and denote by
l(\Lambda_{K})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
the number of orientable exact Lagrangian fillings
L\subseteq(D^{4},\omega_{\text{st}})
of the Legendrian knot
\Lambda_{K}\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
, up to a Hamiltonian isotopy. Consider the smooth invariant

\overline{l}(K):=\sup\{l(\Lambda_{K}):\Lambda_{K}\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})\mbox{ is a Legendrian representative of }K\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

for a smooth knot
K\subseteq S^{3}
. To our knowledge, there is no hitherto known instance of a non-trivial knot
K\subseteq S^{3}
for which
\overline{l}(K)
is known and non-vanishing. In addition, there are non-trivial knots
K\subseteq S^{3}
for which the invariant
\overline{l}(K)
vanishes. For instance,
\overline{l}(m(8_{19}))=0
is known to vanish since the Kauffman upper bound is not sharp
[
36
,
54
]
. We shall now use Theorem
1.1
to show that
\overline{l}(K)
is actually infinite for infinitely many knots within each of the three Thurston classes:

Corollary 1.6
.

The equality
\overline{l}(K)=\infty
holds for infinitely many torus knots, infinitely many satellite knots and infinitely many hyperbolic knots
K\subseteq S^{3}
in the
3
-sphere.

The satellite knots in Corollary
1.6
can be chosen to be cable knots, and the hyperbolic knots we will exhibit are also well-beloved
[
7
,
38
]
. For instance, we will show that
\overline{l}(K)=\infty
already for
K=k(4_{3})
, one of the simplest hyperbolic non-2-bridge knots
[
11
]
.

1.4.
Lagrangian Concordances

Now, let
\mathbb{L}(n,m)
be the monoid of exact Lagrangian concordances, up to Hamiltonian isotopy, for the Legendrian link
\Lambda(n,m)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
readily imply:

Corollary 1.7
.

There exists subgroups
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{L}(3,6)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{L}(4,4)
such that the group
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is a factor of
\Gamma
, and
M_{0,4}
is a factor of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
\Box

By definition, the groups
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
in Corollary
1.7
are the subgroups generated by the exact Lagrangian concordances obtained by graphing the Legendrian loops in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
. Corollary
1.7
emphasizes the relevance of Lagrangian concordances in the study of Legendrian knots. In particular, the existence of Lagrangian concordances of infinite order is a new result which itself provides a genuinely useful perspective for the study of Lagrangian fillings. Indeed, there is no a priori reason for the infinite Lagrangian fillings in Corollary
1.5
to be describable in terms of a finite number of Lagrangian concordances. The present results show that this is the case. Similarly,

Corollary 1.8
.

There exists subgroups
\Gamma\subseteq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(3,6))
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(4,4))
such that the group
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
is a factor of
\Gamma
, and
M_{0,4}
is a factor of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
\Box

Corollary
1.7
and Corollary
1.8
are the first instances in contact topology of infinite order elements in the concordance monoid
\mathbb{L}(\Lambda)
, and the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda))
, for a Legendrian
\Lambda\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Both
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
and
M_{0,4}
contain free groups of any countable rank as subgroups, and thus many infinite order elements exist in
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(3,6))
and
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(4,4))
. In fact,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are exponential-growth subgroups of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(3,6))
and
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}(4,4))
.

Remark 1.9
.

Corollary
1.8
stands in contrast with A. Hatcher’s work
[
40
, Theorem 1]
in the smooth category. Indeed, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{K}_{(n,m)})
of the space
\mathscr{K}_{(n,m)}
of smooth knots in
S^{3}
isotopic to the
(n,m)
-torus knot is the finite Abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
\Box

1.5.
Stein surfaces

Finally, let
M(n,m)
be the Stein surface obtained by a attaching Weinstein
2
-handle
[
17
,
69
]
to
(D^{4},\lambda_{\text{st}})
along each of the components of the Legendrian link
\Lambda(n,m)\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
. For
\gcd(n,m)=1
, the Weinstein 4-manifold
M(n,m)
is homotopy equivalent to the 2-sphere. Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
imply the existence of infinitely many Lagrangian surfaces in the following Stein surfaces:

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
(n,m)\in\mathcal{H}
,
\gcd(n,m)=1
and
M(n,m)
the Weinstein 4-manifold obtained by attaching a Weinstein 2-handle to
(D^{4},\omega_{\text{st}})
along
\Lambda(n,m)
. Then
M(n,m)
contains infinitely many smoothly isotopic closed exact Lagrangian surfaces of genus
\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(m-1)
which are not Hamiltonian isotopic.
\Box

To our knowledge, Corollary
1.10
presents the first known Stein surfaces homotopic to the
2
-sphere with infinitely many non-Hamiltonian isotopic exact Lagrangian surfaces of higher genus in the same smooth isotopy class.

Infinitely many distinct Lagrangian 2-spheres were known to exist in
A_{k}
-Milnor fibres
[
61
, Theorem 5.10]
,
k\geq 3
, and infinitely many exact Lagrangian tori were known to exist in certain Stein surfaces by using either of the articles
[
45
,
63
,
67
,
68
]
. (These infinite families of genus
0
and
1
are presently not known to come from infinitely many Lagrangian fillings of a Legendrian link, nor are the ambient Weinstein 4-manifolds homotopic to the
2
-sphere.)

In Corollary
1.10
, the 1-dimensional intersection form of the Weinstein 4-manifold
M(n,m)
is positive definite and equals
(nm-n-m-1)
, since
tb(\Lambda(n,m))=nm-n-m
. In consequence,
M(n,m)
does not admit any Lagrangian surface of genus strictly less than
\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(m-1)
. Thus, the genus in Corollary
1.10
is sharp.

Remark 1.11
.

The Lagrangian 2-spheres in
[
61
]
differ by a composition of symplectic Dehn twists
[
3
,
60
]
. This is not the case for the exact Lagrangian higher-genus surfaces in Corollary
1.10
since, by the paragraph above, the Weinstein 4-manifolds
M(n,m)
,
(n,m)\in\mathcal{H}
, do not contain embedded Lagrangian 2-spheres.
\Box

2.
The geometric construction

In this section we construct the Legendrian loops in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
associated to the Legendrian links
\Lambda(3,6)
and
\Lambda(4,8)
. This construction is one of the central geometric contributions of the article. This section also serves to setup the elements of contact geometry that we shall need
[
24
,
37
]
.

The Legendrian loops
\Sigma_{1},\delta^{2},\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2}
and
\Xi_{3}
that we construct can be equivalently considered as exact Lagrangian concordances in the symplectization
(S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}(t),d(e^{t}\alpha_{\text{st}}))
with no critical points with respect to the projection onto the
\mathbb{R}
-factor
[
17
,
37
]
. These Lagrangian concordances are obtained by graphing concatenations of the Legendrian isotopies described in Subsection
2.2
.

2.1.
The standard contact 3-space

The Legendrian links
\Lambda\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
in this article will be considered inside the standard contact 3-space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
, considered as a standard Darboux chart within the contact 3-sphere
(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})

[
2
,
4
]
.

In discussing Lagrangian fillings, the inclusion
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
will be composed with the inclusion
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})\subseteq(S^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
given by the one-point compactification. In this identification, the Lagrangian fillings of a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
will be exact Lagrangian surfaces in
(D^{4},\lambda_{\text{st}})
considered up to Hamiltonian isotopy.

The constructions in this article give rise to contact geometric objects in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
, including Legendrian links and contact isotopies. Nevertheless, it is enlightening to focus on a small neighborhood of the standard Legendrian unknot
\Lambda_{un}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
which is contactomorphic to
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
, and work in the solid torus
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
. Thus, in this article, Legendrian links
\Lambda\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
and compactly supported contact isotopies in
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\in\operatorname{Cont}(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
shall implicitly be understood as Legendrian links
\Lambda\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
and contact isotopies
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\in\operatorname{Cont}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
by satelliting the zero section
S^{1}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
to
\Lambda_{un}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
.

2.2.
Legendrian Loops

By definition, a Legendrian loop in
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
is a Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
such that
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{1}
. Let
(\theta,p_{\theta},z)\in S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
be global coordinates in
J^{1}S^{1}
and
\xi_{\text{st}}=\ker{(dz-p_{\theta}d\theta)}
. The description of our Legendrian loops shall use the front projection

(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})\longrightarrow S^{1}\times\mathbb{R},\quad(\theta,p_{\theta},z)\longmapsto(\theta,z),

which is indeed a valid front as the fibers are Legendrians. By definition, the Legendrian
\Lambda(\mathscr{B})\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
associated to a positive Legendrian braid
\mathscr{B}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
is the image of
\mathscr{B}
under the operation of satelliting the zero section
S^{1}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
along the standard Legendrian unknot.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a geometric positive braid
\mathscr{B}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
, which is a Legendrian link, can be encoded algebraically by a positive expression
\beta
, i.e. a positive braid word, of an element
[\beta]\in B_{k}
of the
k
-stranded braid group

B_{k}:=\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k-1}|\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\mbox{ for }j\neq i\pm 1,1\leq i,j\leq k-1\rangle,

where
\sigma_{i}
are the standard Artin generators. The choice of representation
\beta
, i.e. braid word, for the element
[\beta]\in B_{k}
is not unique, as one might use the word relations in
B_{k}
to obtain different representations
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
such that
[\beta_{1}]=[\beta_{2}]
in
B_{k}
. Given a positive braid word
\beta
, we denote by
\mathscr{B}(\beta)\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
the Legendrian link associated to
\beta
, and denote by
\Lambda(\beta)\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\text{st}})
the Legendrian link
\Lambda(\mathscr{B}(\beta))
.

Notice that the geometric braid
\mathscr{B}\subseteq(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
has a front in
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we fix a basepoint
\theta_{0}\in S^{1}
and require that a braid word
\beta
representing
\mathscr{B}
has the form:

\beta=\prod_{j=1}^{l}\sigma_{i_{j}},\quad 1\leq i_{j}\leq k-1,

where
\sigma_{i_{1}}
is the first crossing in the front diagram of
\mathscr{B}
on the right of the vertical line
\{\theta_{0}\}\times\mathbb{R}\subseteq S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
and the crossings are read from left to right.

In this article, we shall construct Legendrian loops by performing Legendrian isotopies which primarily consist of Reidemeister moves in the front. In particular, the three central operations that we use are:

(i)

Reidemeister III Moves
. In terms of the given braid word presentation
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}(\beta)
, the Reidemeister III move consists in applying the relation
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
. We shall refer to a Legendrian isotopy which implements the substitution

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\longmapsto\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}

as an ascending Reidemeister III Move, and denote it by R3
a
. Similarly, to a Legendrian isotopy which implements the substitution

\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\longmapsto\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

as a descending Reidemeister III Move, and denote it by R3
d
. Thus, either R3 Reidemeister move is understood as a Legendrian isotopy.

(ii)

Cyclic Permutation
. Consider a braid
\mathscr{B}(\beta)
represented by

\beta=\prod_{j=1}^{l}\sigma_{i_{j}},\quad 1\leq i_{j}\leq k-1.

By definition, a cyclic shift
\delta
is a Legendrian isotopy
\{\psi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
which brings the geometric braid
\mathscr{B}(\beta)
to
\psi_{1}(\mathscr{B}(\beta))
such that the braid word
\psi_{1}(\beta)
for the latter is

\psi_{1}(\beta)=\left(\prod_{j=2}^{l}\sigma_{i_{j}}\right)\sigma_{i_{1}},\quad 1\leq i_{j}\leq k-1.

Note that this braid word for
\psi_{1}(\beta)
is read with respect to the fixed basepoint
\theta_{0}
. Explicitly, this Legendrian isotopy can be geometrically visualized by rotating
\mathscr{B}(\beta)
to the left by an appropriate angle while keeping the zero section fixed.

Since we study Legendrian braids in
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
, rather than
(J^{1}[0,1],\xi_{\text{st}})
, two braid words
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
which differ by a cyclic permutation yield Legendrian isotopic
\Lambda(\beta_{1})
and
\Lambda(\beta_{2})
. Hence, the operations above produce Legendrian isotopies.

(iii)

Commutation
. The third move
\gamma
is just implementing the commutation relation in the braid group
B_{k}
. It is described as follows:

\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}\sigma_{i_{j}}\right)\sigma_{i_{p}}\sigma_{i_{p+1}}\left(\prod_{j=p+2}^{l}\sigma_{i_{j}}\right)\longmapsto\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}\sigma_{i_{j}}\right)\sigma_{i_{p+1}}\sigma_{i_{p}}\left(\prod_{j=p+2}^{l}\sigma_{i_{j}}\right),

with indices
1\leq i_{j}\leq k-1,1\leq p\leq l-1
and
i_{p+1}\neq i_{p}\pm 1
. This move can be realized by a compactly supported Legendrian isotopy in
(J^{1}S^{1},\xi_{\text{st}})
which we also refer to as
\gamma
, which is the greek letter for
c
, standing for commutation.

which geometrically represents the Legendrian torus link

\delta
exactly
(n-1)

for
\Lambda(n,m)
. This is the Legendrian loop studied in

42
]
where it is shown to be a non-trivial Legendrian loop. We shall provide our own alternative proof of this non-triviality.

which arise as

3.1.
The Broué-Deligne-Michel Description

In order to directly compute with the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
and construct the representations in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, we require a more explicit description of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
. This description is available due to the work
[
65
]
, which proves that
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
is isomorphic to a classical moduli BS(
\beta
), modulo the gauge action, associated to a braid
\beta
by M. Broué-J. Michel
[
10
]
and Deligne
[
19
]
.

Let
G=\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{C})
and
B\subseteq G
the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices. The quotient
G/B
is the flag variety, whose points parametrize complete flags
V^{\bullet}
of vector subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. The Bruhat decomposition

\displaystyle G/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}BwB/B

implies that the relative position of a pair of flags
(V_{1}^{\bullet},V_{2}^{\bullet})
is determined by an element
s\in S_{k}=\mbox{Weyl}{(G)}
of the Weyl group, in this case a permutation in the symmetric group. Consider the Artin generators
\sigma_{i}\in B_{k}
,
1\leq i\leq k-1
, and denote by
\overline{\sigma_{i}}
the image of
\sigma_{i}
under the projection
B_{k}\longrightarrow S_{k}
from the braid group to the
A_{k-1}
-Coxeter group
S_{k}
. Given a flag
V^{\bullet}
and a permutation
s\in S_{k}
, let
S_{s}(V^{\bullet})
be the set of flags in relative
s
-position with respect to
V^{\bullet}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\beta
be a positive braid word

\beta=\prod_{j=1}^{l(\beta)}\sigma_{i_{j}},\quad 1\leq i_{j}\leq k-1,

and consider the subset

\mbox{BS}(\beta):=\{(V^{\bullet}_{1},\ldots,V^{\bullet}_{l(\beta)})\in(G/B)^{l(\beta)}:V^{\bullet}_{m+1}\in S_{\overline{\sigma}_{i_{m}}}(V^{\bullet}_{m}),1\leq m\leq l(\beta)\}\subseteq(G/B)^{l(\beta)},

where the index
1\leq m\leq l(\beta)
is understood cyclically modulo
l(\beta)
, i.e. the condition for
m=l(\beta)
reads
V^{\bullet}_{1}\in S_{\overline{\sigma}_{i_{l(\beta)}}}(V^{\bullet}_{l(\beta)})
. By definition, BS(
\beta
) is said to be the open Bott-Samelson variety associated to
\beta
.
\Box

For each
\beta
, the group
G
acts on the open Bott-Samelson variety BS(
\beta
) diagonally on the left, given that the flag variety
G/B
is given by the
B
-action on the right. The article
[
65
, Section 6]
identifies
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
with the quotient
G\backslash
BS(
\beta
). It is a consequence of this identification that our moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
can be described as follows.

Choose a set of points
\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{l(\beta)}\}\in S^{1}
such that the vertical lines
\{\theta_{m}\}\times\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq m\leq l(\beta)
do not intersect the front
\beta\subseteq S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
at a crossing and there exists a unique crossing of
\beta
between
\{\theta_{m}\}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\{\theta_{m+1}\}\times\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq i\leq l(\beta)
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
is the moduli space given by associating a complete flag
V^{\bullet}_{m}
along each vertical line
\{\theta_{m}\}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
V^{\bullet}_{0}=V^{\bullet}_{l(\beta)}
and two flags
V^{\bullet}_{m}
and
V^{\bullet}_{m+1}
differ only and exactly in their
i_{m+1}
-dimensional subspaces for all
1\leq m\leq l(\beta)
, modulo the gauge group action of
\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{C})
. This description in terms of BS(
\beta
) will be used in Sections
4
and
5
.

3.2.
Moduli of Framed Sheaves

In the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
we shall need a framed enhancement
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
of the Bott-Samelson varieties
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
. In precise terms, the points of
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
are given by the
l(\beta)
-tuples of flags
[(V^{\bullet}_{1},\ldots,V^{\bullet}_{l(\beta)})]\in\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\Lambda(\beta))
equipped with trivializations
\tau
for the stalks at a specified set of points. In this case, we choose the set of points such that the set contains exactly one point for each region where the constructible sheaf has a 1-dimensional stalk. Given that they are in bijection, we will interchangeably speak of these points or the open strata in the front diagram that contain them, these open strata shall also be referred to as regions. Hence, in the language of Bott-Samelson varieties, the trivialization
\tau
consists of a series of isomorphisms

V^{(1)}_{m}\cong\mathbb{C},\quad 1\leq m\leq l(\beta).

In our context, the moduli spaces of framed sheaves
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
are algebraic varieties
[
65
]
. It should be emphasized that the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
depends on the choice of trivialization
\tau
. In our choice above,
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
shall depend on the choice of braid word
\beta
. Indeed, the length of the tuple is precisely
l(\beta)
. Nevertheless, the article
[
64
]
shows that a Legendrian isotopy generates an equivalence of moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
of framed sheaves, with the trivialization, and its region, being pushed forward under the isotopy. Thus, in studying the action of a Legendrian loop on
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
we identify the moduli spaces of framed sheaves along the Legendrian isotopy and compare the action at the canonically identified endpoints of the Legendrian loop.

Explicitly, let
\{\Psi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a Legendrian loop based at the identity, i.e.
\Psi_{0}=\mbox{Id}
. By
[
64
, Section 2]
, there is a canonical isomorphism between the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau)
and
\mathcal{M}(\Psi_{t}(\Lambda),(\Psi_{t})_{*}\tau)
for all
t\in[0,1]
. By virtue of being a Legendrian loop,
\Psi_{1}=\mbox{Id}
and thus we obtain an algebraic automorphism
\Psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau))
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau)
. This automorphism is to be understood as the monodromy of the Legendrian loop
\{\Psi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, in line with T. Kálmán’s
[
42
, Section 3]
monodromy invariant. The automorphism
\Psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau))
in turn induces an automorphism
\Psi^{*}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau)])
in the coordinate ring of regular functions on
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,\tau)
.

In Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
the focus will be on two moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda(\beta),\tau)
for the two braid words
\beta=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{9}
and
\beta=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3})^{8}
and a chosen trivialization
\tau
.

This concludes the proof of Theorem

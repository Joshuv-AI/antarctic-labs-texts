The defect b b -theorem under bulk RG flows
Tom Shachar a, Ritam Sinha b , and, Michael Smolkin a

Abstract
It is known that for RG flows confined to a two-dimensional defect, where the bulk maintains its conformal nature, the coefficient of the Euler density in the defect’s Weyl anomaly (termed b b ) cannot increase as the flow progresses from the ultraviolet to the infrared, a principle known as the b b -theorem. In this paper, we investigate whether this theorem still holds when the bulk, instead of being critical, also undergoes an RG flow. To address this question, we examine two distinct and perturbatively tractable examples. Our analysis reveals that a straightforward extension of the b b -theorem to these cases of RG flows fails.

Introduction

The renormalization group (RG) flow serves as a foundational framework for identifying the degrees of freedom governing low-energy phenomena. Its core premise lies in simplifying theories by disregarding their microscopic details while preserving their low-energy physics. 2
RG flow in the presence of 2D defect

In this section we derive perturbative beta functions in the presence of two-dimensional defect. In the next section, we use these results to analyze the effect of bulk RG flows on the defect
b
-theorem.

Consider a
d
-dimensional Euclidean defect CFT with a set of local operators
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
, each having scaling dimensions
\Delta_{i}=2-\epsilon
and
\Delta_{\alpha}=d-\epsilon
, respectively. For simplicity, we assume that the 2-point functions of these operators have unit amplitude. Their OPE coefficients are denoted as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{i}(x)\mathcal{O}_{j}(0)\sim{C_{ij}^{k}\over|x|^{\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{k}}}\mathcal{O}_{k}(0)+\ldots~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}(x)\mathcal{O}_{\beta}(0)\sim{C_{\alpha\beta}^{\nu}\over|x|^{\Delta_{\alpha}+\Delta_{\beta}-\Delta_{\nu}}}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}(0)+\ldots~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{i}(0)\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}(x)\sim{C_{i\alpha}^{j}\over|x|^{\Delta_{\alpha}+\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{j}}}\mathcal{O}_{j}(0)+\ldots~,

(1)

We use these operators to initiate an RG flow in both the bulk and the two-dimensional defect by deforming the CFT as follows

\displaystyle S=S_{\text{CFT}}+\delta S=S_{\text{CFT}}+g^{i}_{0}\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\gamma}\,\mathcal{O}_{i}+\lambda^{\alpha}_{0}\int\,d^{d}x~\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}~,

(2)

where
\left\{\sigma_{a}\right\}_{a=1,2}
and
\left\{x^{\mu}\right\}_{\mu=1,\ldots,d}
denote the coordinates that parametrize the two-dimensional defect and the bulk, respectively, while
\gamma_{ab}
represents the induced metric. Assuming that
\epsilon\ll 1
, operators with Greek indices represent weakly relevant deformations in the bulk, while operators with Latin indices correspond to weakly relevant deformations in the two-dimensional defect. By construction, the CFT without a defect and with the bulk coupling
g_{0}^{i}
tuned to zero, serves as a UV fixed point of the flow, whereas the IR end of the flow is located in its vicinity. Hence, the couplings remain weak throughout the flow, and one can perturbatively expand around the UV CFT,

\displaystyle e^{-\delta S}=1-\delta S+{\lambda^{\alpha}_{0}\lambda^{\beta}_{0}\over 2}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{\beta}+{g^{i}_{0}g^{j}_{0}\over 2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{O}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{j}+g^{i}_{0}\lambda^{\alpha}_{0}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{O}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}~.

(3)

where we introduced a shorthand notation

\int_{\mathcal{D}}=\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\gamma}~,\quad\int_{\mathcal{B}}=\int d^{d}x~.

(4)

To obtain the defect and the bulk at a specific scale
\mu
, we integrate out distances within the range
0\leq\ell\leq\mu^{-1}
. This calculation essentially involves excluding a small ball with a radius of
\mu^{-1}
around the operators on the right-hand side of the expression above. The resulting relation between the dimensionless couplings at scale
\mu
and their bare counterparts is given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{\nu}\mu^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\nu}_{0}-{\pi^{d\over 2}\over\Gamma\big({d\over 2}\big)}{\mu^{-\epsilon}\over\epsilon}\,C^{\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\,\lambda_{0}^{\alpha}\,\lambda_{0}^{\beta}+\ldots~,

\displaystyle g^{i}\mu^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i}_{0}-\pi C^{i}_{jk}\,g_{0}^{j}\,g_{0}^{k}\,{\mu^{-\epsilon}\over\epsilon}-{2\pi^{d\over 2}\over\Gamma\big({d\over 2}\big)}\,{\mu^{-\epsilon}\over\epsilon}\,C^{i}_{j\alpha}\,g^{j}_{0}\,\lambda_{0}^{\alpha}\,+\ldots~.

(5)

Hence, the flow of the couplings follows the pattern

\displaystyle\beta^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu{dg^{i}\over d\mu}=-\epsilon g^{i}+\pi C^{i}_{jk}g^{j}g^{k}+{2\pi^{d\over 2}\over\Gamma\big({d\over 2}\big)}C^{i}_{j\alpha}\,g^{j}\lambda^{\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(g^{3})~,

\displaystyle\beta^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu{d\lambda^{\nu}\over d\mu}=-\epsilon\lambda^{\nu}+{\pi^{d\over 2}\over\Gamma\big({d\over 2}\big)}C^{\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{3})~.

(6)

3
O(N)
vector model with a defect

In this section, we delve into a specific example of the general setup introduced in the previous section. We focus on evaluating the fixed points within the two-dimensional coupling space and analyze the RG flows connecting them. Our examination reveals that when the flow extends beyond the defect, implying that the bulk is not conformally invariant and undergoes changes along the RG flow, the naive extension of the
b
-theorem is compromised.

Consider a free massless
O(N)
vector field
\phi
in
d=4-\epsilon
dimensions. This CFT can be perturbed by a weakly relevant
O(N)
symmetric quartic interaction in the bulk and an
O(N)
symmetric quadratic interaction on a two-dimensional spherical defect,

\displaystyle S=\int_{\mathcal{B}}~\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\lambda\mu^{\epsilon}\phi^{4}\Big)+g\mu^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}~\phi^{2}~,

(7)

Neither the two-point function of
\phi^{4}
nor that of
\phi^{2}
is normalized.
2
2

2

For brevity, we suppress the flavor index of the vector field
\phi
,
e.g.,

\phi^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\phi_{a}\phi_{a}
, and similarly for
\phi^{4}=(\phi^{2})^{2}
. The exact expressions for the two-point functions of the free theory are as follows,

\langle\phi_{a}(x)\phi_{c}(0)\rangle={\delta_{ac}\,C_{\phi}\over|x|^{d-2}}~,\quad\langle\phi^{2}(x)\phi^{2}(0)\rangle={2NC_{\phi}^{2}\over|x|^{2(d-2)}}~,\quad\langle\phi^{4}(x)\phi^{4}(0)\rangle={8N(N+2)C_{\phi}^{4}\over|x|^{4(d-2)}}~.

(8)

However, for the purpose of using (
6
), we only need to consider the relevant OPE coefficients, which can be obtained using Wick contractions,

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(x)\phi^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C_{\mathcal{D}}\over|x|^{d-2}}~\phi^{2}(0)+\ldots~,

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(x)\phi^{4}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C_{\mathcal{BD}}\over|x|^{2(d-2)}}~\phi^{2}(0)+\ldots~,

(9)

\displaystyle\phi^{4}(x)\phi^{4}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C_{\mathcal{B}}\over|x|^{2(d-2)}}~\phi^{4}(0)+\ldots~,

with

C_{\mathcal{D}}=4C_{\phi}\,,~~~~C_{\mathcal{BD}}=4(N+2)C_{\phi}^{2}\,,~~~C_{\mathcal{B}}=8(N+8)C_{\phi}^{2}\,,~~~C_{\phi}={\Gamma\left({d-2\over 2}\right)\over 4\pi^{d\over 2}}~.

(10)

Hence, the beta functions in this model take the form,

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon\lambda+\pi^{2}C_{\mathcal{B}}\lambda^{2}~,

\displaystyle\beta_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon g+\pi C_{\mathcal{D}}g^{2}+2\pi^{2}C_{\mathcal{BD}}\lambda g~.

(11)

In total, there are four fixed points of the flow:

(g_{*},\lambda_{*})=\{(0,0),(0,\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}),(g_{\text{\tiny GD}},0),(g_{\text{\tiny{WF}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}})\}~~,

(12)

where

\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}=\frac{2\pi^{2}\,\epsilon}{N+8}~,\quad g_{\text{\tiny G}}=\epsilon\pi~,\quad g_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}=\frac{6\pi\epsilon}{N+8}~.

(13)

The first two fixed points with
g_{*}=0
correspond to the Gaussian and Wilson-Fisher (WF) CFTs without defects. The two remaining fixed points in the two-dimensional
(g,\lambda)
-plane represent DCFTs with non-vanishing defects. For example, the point
(g_{\text{\tiny G}},0)
describes a non-trivial defect in a free field theory
[
70
]
, which we will refer to as the Gaussian DCFT (dG). Similarly,
(g_{\text{\tiny{WF}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}})
describes a non-trivial conformal defect embedded into the WF CFT bulk
[
73
,
74
,
75
]
, which we will refer to as dWF for brevity.

By analyzing the matrix of derivatives of the beta functions in (
11
), one can show that the trivial defect in the Gaussian and WF CFTs is infrared repulsive under the
\phi^{2}
deformation. Additionally, the dG has an unstable direction triggered by turning on the relevant
\phi^{4}
deformation, which causes the dG to flow towards dWF, as shown in Fig.
1
. In the remaining part of this section, we demonstrate that along this RG flow, the
b
-anomaly increases, thus violating the naive generalization of the
b
-theorem for 2D surface defects in the presence of bulk RG flows.

To determine the value of the anomaly constant
b
for dWF, we examine the RG trajectory of (
11
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}
. In this scenario,
\beta_{\lambda}=0
throughout the flow, indicating that the bulk remains intact and is represented by the WF CFT. In contrast, the defect changes: it is trivial in the UV but is characterized by
g_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}\neq 0
in the IR. This RG trajectory corresponds to a horizontal line connecting the WF fixed point on the
\lambda
-axis with dWF in Fig.
1
. Therefore, for this specific RG trajectory, we can use the general result for the increment of
b
, as obtained in
[
70
]
through conformal perturbation theory. In our context, it takes the form,

b_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}=-{N\over 8}\bar{\epsilon}^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\epsilon}^{4})~,

(14)

where
\bar{\epsilon}=2-\Delta_{\phi^{2}}
, and
\Delta_{\phi^{2}}
represents the scaling dimension of
\phi^{2}
at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point,
i.e.,

\bar{\epsilon}=-{{\partial}\beta_{g}\over{\partial}g}\bigg|_{g=0,~\lambda=\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}}={6\,\epsilon\over N+8}~.

(15)

In Appendix
A
, we perform a direct calculation of the partition function to independently verify the above value of
b_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}
. Similarly
[
70
]
,

b_{\text{\tiny{G}}}=-{N\over 8}\epsilon^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})~.

(16)

Hence, for all
N
, we obtain

b_{\text{\tiny{WF}}}-b_{\text{\tiny{G}}}=\frac{N\left(\epsilon^{3}-\bar{\epsilon}^{3}\right)}{8}>0~,

(17)

leading to a violation of the naive generalization of the
b
-theorem under the RG flow from the dG to dWF.

4
Gross-Neveu-Yukawa model with a defect

Consider now the same two-dimensional spherical defect as in the previous section, but this time embedded in the
d=4-\epsilon
dimensional bulk described by a single bosonic field,
\phi
, coupled to
N_{\text{F}}
Dirac fields,

\displaystyle S=\int_{\mathcal{B}}~\Big(\bar{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\eta\,\mu^{\epsilon\over 2}\bar{\psi}\psi\phi+\frac{\lambda\mu^{\epsilon}}{4!}\phi^{4}\Big)+g\mu^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\phi^{2}~.

(18)

This action describes the
U(N_{\text{\tiny F}})
invariant Gross-Neveu-Yukawa (GNY) model with a two-dimensional spherical defect. To maintain brevity, we suppress the flavor index of the vector field
\psi
.

The RG flow in this model occurs in the three-dimensional space of couplings
(\eta,\lambda,g)
. The associated beta functions are derived in Appendix
B
, while here we present the final expressions

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon\lambda+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\Big(\,\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}+N\lambda\eta^{2}-6N\eta^{4}\Big)+\ldots~,

\displaystyle\beta_{\eta^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon\eta^{2}+\frac{N+6}{16\pi^{2}}\eta^{4}+\ldots~,

(19)

\displaystyle\beta_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon g+\frac{g^{2}}{\pi}+\frac{g}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\lambda+N\eta^{2}\right)+\ldots~,

where ellipses denote higher-order corrections in the small coupling constants. Here,
N=\text{Tr}(\mathbb{I}_{4\times 4})N_{\text{\tiny F}}=4N_{\text{\tiny F}}
represents the total number of fermionic degrees of freedom in four dimensions. Notably, the RG flow equations (
19
) cannot be recovered using (
6
) because conformal perturbation theory in Section
3
is restricted to quadratic order in the coupling constants, where the knowledge of OPE coefficients is enough to recover the structure of the beta functions. In contrast, for consistency, the beta functions (
19
) must necessarily include quartic corrections in
\eta
.

The two-dimensional plane
\eta=0
encompasses all the fixed points previously discussed in section
3
. In addition to these, there exist two new fixed points:
(\eta_{*},\lambda_{*},g_{*})=(\eta_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},g_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}})
and
(\eta_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},0)
, with and without defect, respectively, where

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{N+6}}~,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{384\pi^{2}N\epsilon}{\left(N+6\right)\left(N-6+\sqrt{N^{2}+132N+36}\right)}~,

(20)

\displaystyle g_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\epsilon\bigg(1-\frac{6+5N+\sqrt{N^{2}+132N+36}}{6\left(N+6\right)}\,\bigg)~.

The fixed point
(\eta_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},0)
belongs to the
g=0
plane accommodating all possible RG flows without a defect. As shown in Fig.
2
, this fixed point is an IR stable attractor of the RG trajectories restricted to this plane.

Analysis of the
3\times 3
matrix of derivatives of the beta functions (
19
) leads to the conclusion that the previously IR attractive fixed point
(0,\lambda_{\text{\tiny{WF}}},g_{\text{\tiny{WF}}})
becomes unstable under Yukawa deformation. Similarly, the critical GNY model without a defect becomes IR repulsive when introducing the
\phi^{2}
deformation onto the two-dimensional defect surface. Furthermore, as depicted in Fig.
3
, the DCFT characterized by
(\eta_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},\lambda_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}},g_{\text{\tiny{GNY}}})
, denoted by dGNY in the figure, stands as the only IR stable fixed point in the three-dimensional coupling space of the GNY model with the type of defect studied in this section.

Discussion

In this paper, we discussed a violation of the naive generalisation of the
b
-theorem, to the case of simultaneous bulk and defect RG flows, in a quantum system defined on a
d
-dimensional flat bulk coupled to a
2d
defect. We demonstrated said violation in two distinct models namely, the
O(N)
vector model and the Gross-Neveu-Yukawa (GNY) model, each coupled to a two-dimensional spherical defect. The computation of the
b
-anomaly at the various fixed points in each of these models was done using conformal perturbation theory, and the result was verified from an independent computation of the free energy in each system.

The failure to generalise the
b
-theorem to the case of simultaneous bulk-defect RG flows is reminiscent of a similar violation of the
g
-theorem
[
71
]
, which characterises RG flows on
1d
boundaries of
2d
critical systems
3
3

3

The
g
-theorem has recently been shown to hold for
1d
defects as well
[
66
]
. It should be straightforward to show a violation of the defect
g
-theorem using arguments similar to
[
71
]
.
. In case of
1d
boundaries/defects, the
g
-function is interpreted as the
impurity entropy
, that can either increase or decrease under bulk interactions. Such an interpretation provides a clear physical understanding underlying the failure to generalise the
g
-theorem to include bulk RG flows. Finding a similar understanding in the context of the
b
-theorem remains one of the interesting open problems.

Another challenging open question that follows from such a violation, is regarding the existence and construction of an altogether different class of
C
- functions for defect QFTs, that follow from the partition function, and that again display monotonicity properties under simultaneous bulk and defect RG flows.

A completely independent, alternate construction of the
C
-functions

g
-theorem in
1+1

[
65
]
and later generalised to boundaries in higher spacetime dimensions in
[
48
]
. The proof was finally extended to defects of arbitrary co-dimension in
[

]
. For other works on defect RG flows using quantum information methods, see
[

,
78

79

for boundaries and defects was provided using information theoretic techniques (such as relative entropy, entanglement entropy and the Quantum Null Energy Condition) in

Appendix A
Computation of the

-anomaly

Another method of calculating the
b
-anomaly is based on evaluating the free energy. For a two-dimensional spherical conformal defect embedded in flat space, the free energy is given by,

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\text{\tiny CFT}}-\frac{b}{3}\log\left(\mu R\right)+\ldots~.

Here,
\mathcal{F}_{\text{\tiny CFT}}
represents the free energy of the ambient CFT without the defect,
R

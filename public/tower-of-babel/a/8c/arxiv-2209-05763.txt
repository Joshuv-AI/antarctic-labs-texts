The density property for vector bundles
Riccardo Ugolini, Jörg Winkelmann

Abstract
. We prove that holomorphic vector bundles over Stein manifolds with the density property also satisfy the density property, provided that the total space is holomorphically flexible. We apply this result to provide a new class of Stein manifolds with the density property.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 32M18; Secondary: 32Q28, 32M25

1.
Introduction

The class of Stein manifolds with the density property is at the center of the Andersén-Lempert Theory. The latter began when Andersén and Lempert first described the group of holomorphic automorphisms of complex affine spaces
{\mathbb{C}}^{n},\ n>1

[
AL92
]
. Later, Varolin generalized their ideas and introduced the density property:

Definition 1.1

(
[
Var01
]
)
.

A complex manifold
X
has the
density property
if the Lie algebra generated by complete holomorphic vector fields (those whose flows are defined for all complex times) is dense in the Lie algebra of all holomorphic vector fields in the compact-open topology.

Note that the flow of a complete vector field at a fixed time
t\in{\mathbb{C}}
defines an automorphism of
X
. Under the additional condition that
X
is Stein, the density property hence guarantees the existence of a large number of automorphisms. This has many striking consequences; we refer the interested reader to the monograph
[
For17
]
or the more recent survey
[
FK22
]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2

(Main theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Stein manifold with density property,
\pi:E\to X
a holomorphic
vector bundle.

Assume that there exists an automorphism

\phi
of the total space
E
and points
p,q\in E
such that

\pi(p)=\pi(q)
, but
\pi(\phi(p))\neq\pi(\phi(q))
.

Then
E
satisfies the density property.

For the proof, we distinguish two cases:
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(E)\geq 2
is
dealt with in Theorem
9.1
and Theorem

10.1
takes care of the case
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(E)=1
.

The first application of Theorem
1.2
concerns a specific class of
flexible
manifolds.

Definition 1.3
.

A complex algebraic manifold
X
is flexible at a point
p\in X
if the tangent space
T_{p}X
is spanned by the tangent vectors to the orbits
Hp
of
unipotent one-parameter algebraic subgroups
H\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}_{alg}(X)
of the group of algebraic automorphisms. We say that
X
is flexible if it is flexible at all of its points.

These one-parameter subgroups induce algebraic
actions by the additive group of
{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e., they correspond to

locally nilpotent derivations
(LNDs). In other words:
X
is flexible
at
p
if for every
v\in T_{p}X

there exists an algebraic vector field
\Theta
such that the
induced derivation
\theta
of the function ring
{\mathbb{C}}[X]
satisfies

\forall f\in{\mathbb{C}}[X]:\exists n:\underbrace{\theta(\ldots(\theta(}_{\text{$n$-times}}f))\ldots)=0

These LNDs proved useful in many aspects of the Andersén-Lempert theory; more information can be found in the monograph
[
Fre17
]
.

Denoting by
\mathop{\mathrm{SAut}}(X)
the group generated by all unipotent
one-parameter subgroups of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}_{alg}(X)
,
the main result about flexible affine manifolds is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

[
AFK+13
, Theorem 0.1]

Let
X
be an affine algebraic manifold of dimension greater than one. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is flexible

(2)

the group
\mathop{\mathrm{SAut}}(X)
acts transitively

(3)

the group
\mathop{\mathrm{SAut}}(X)
acts
n
-transitively for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

An action is
n
-transitive if any ordered
n
-tuple of distinct points can be sent to any other such
n
-tuple by the action.
Condition
(iii)

implies that the automorphism group of a
flexible manifold is infinite dimensional.

An easy example of a flexible variety is
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.

In view of Theorem
1.4
, we have the
Corollary of our main Theorem:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X
be a Stein manifold with density property,

E\to X
a holomorphic vector bundle such that the total space
E
is a flexible affine variety.

Then
E
satisfies the density property.

Proof.

The automorphism group of the complex manifold
E
acts

2
-transitively on
E
, because
E
is flexible.
Hence we may choose points
p,q\in E
with
\pi(p)=\pi(q)

and an automorphism
\phi
of
E
such that
\pi(\phi(p))\neq\pi(\phi(q))
. Now we may invoke Theorem
1.2
.

∎

This raises the question how to check whether
E
is flexible.
It is therefore useful to know that flexibility of the base manifold

X
implies the flexibility of the total space
E
.

Theorem 1.6

(=Theorem
11.4
)
.

Let
E\to X
be an algebraic vector bundle over a flexible smooth
affine variety
X
.

Then
E
is a flexible affine variety.

The proof of the main theorem for vector bundles of rank greater than one
contains two main ingredients: Proposition
6.7
gives that all vector fields on
E
that are compatible with the projection map are limits of Lie combinations of complete vector fields, while Theorem
6.8
grants that there is no intermediate closed Lie algebra between the Lie algebra of vector fields compatible with the projection map and the Lie algebra of all vector fields. The fact that
E
is flexible is used to guarantee the existence of a complete vector field which is not compatible with the projection map.

The case of line bundles is handled separately in Section
10
. The main difference is that the fiber does not satisfy the density property, hence a new approach is required when approximating vertical vector fields.

Using Theorem
1.2
, we are able to provide a class of Stein manifolds with the density property.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible affine variety with only constant invertible functions. Assume a reductive algebraic group
G
acts on
X
with an open orbit and that the unique closed orbit is biholomorphic to neither the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
nor a torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then
X
has the density property.

With the exception on the condition about the unique closed orbit, the hypotheses are the same as in
[
GS19
, Theorem 2]
where the authors prove that such manifolds are flexible.

The fact that there is a unique closed orbit is a consequence of Luna’s slice Theorem
(
[
Lun73
]
, see also
[
Sha94
, Theorem 6.7]
).

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

Luna’s slice Theorem gives that
X
is
G
-biholomorphic to
G\times_{H}\mathbb{C}^{N}
for a reductive subgroup
H\subset G
acting linearly on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, the projection
G\times_{H}\mathbb{C}^{N}\to G/H
onto the first factor is a holomorphic vector bundle. Since
G
and
H
are reductive,
G/H
is affine and biholomorphic to the unique closed orbit of the
G
-action on
X
. The work of Donzelli, Dvorsky, and Kaliman
[
DDK10
]
guarantees that
G/H
has the density property; the conclusion follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Manifolds equipped with a Lie group action admitting an open orbit are called
quasi-homogeneous

(or
almost-homogeneous
); many quasi-homogeneous affine varieties were classified by Popov
[
Pop73a
,
Pop73b
]
and Popov and Vinberg
[
VP72
]
.

If we require
G
to be semisimple instead of reductive, the result holds without additional hypotheses and is a corollary of the following
result:

Theorem 1.8
.

[
AFK+13
, Theorem 5.6]

Suppose that a connected semisimple algebraic group
G
acts on a smooth irreducible affine variety
X
with an open orbit.

Then
X
is homogeneous with respect to a connected affine algebraic group
\tilde{G}\supset G
without nontrivial characters.

For the sake of completeness, we explicitly state the result for semisimple groups; we do not claim any contribution to it.

Corollary 1.9

(Arzhantsev, Flenner, Kaliman, Kutzschebauch, Zaidenberg)
.

Let
X
be an affine manifold of dimension greater than one and not isomorphic to a torus
\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that a connected semisimple algebraic group
G
acts on
X
algebraically with an open orbit.

Then
X
satisfies the density property.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.8
,
X
is a homogeneous space.
Since a semisimple algebraic group acts with an open orbit,

X
is neither one-dimensional nor a torus.
Hence it satisfies the density property thanks to
[
KK17
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

Example 1.10
.

Let

Fact 1.11
.

Let
X
,
Y
be complete vector fields on a manifold
M
.

Then
[X,Y]
can be approximated by
linear combinations of complete vector fields.

Proof.

For the benefit of the reader we indicate a proof although this is well
known.
Let
\phi_{t}
be the flow associated to
X
. Then

[X,Y]={\mathcal{L}}_{X}(Y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{t}^{*}Y-Y}{t}

where
{\mathcal{L}}
denotes the Lie derivative.
Both
\frac{1}{t}\phi_{t}^{*}Y
and
\frac{1}{t}Y

are complete for every
t\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
M
be a manifold,
D
the space of all vector fields and

C
the vector space generated by all complete vector fields.

Then the closure of
C
in
D
is a Lie subalgebra.

2.
Connections and horizontal vector fields

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle.

To find complete vector fields on
E
, we will lift complete vector fields on
X
along the projection
\pi:E\to X
. In order to do so, we need a holomorphic connection.

A holomorphic connection
\nabla

on a holomorphic vector bundle is a rule to derive
holomorphic sections.
It defines a map
\nabla:\Gamma(U,E)\to\Omega^{1}(U)\otimes\Gamma(U,E)

for open subsets
U\subset X
.
In local coordinates
\nabla
evaluates to

\nabla\sigma\simeq d\sigma+A\sigma

with the “
connection form”

A
being a section
in
End(E)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}
.

A connection yields a direct sum decomposition of the tangent
bundle
T_{E}=H\oplus V
where
H
is the vector bundle of

horizontal

vector fields and
V
the bundle of
vertical
vector fields.
(such a direct sum decomposition is also known as

“Ehresmann connection”
).
A vector field
v
on
X
may be lifted (with respect to the given
connection) to a “horizontal vector field”
\tilde{v}
.
In local coordinates (
x_{i}
coordinates on the base,
y_{j}
linear
coordinates on the fiber) we have

(1)

v=\sum_{i}f_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\ \mapsto\ \tilde{v}=\left(\sum_{i}f_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)+\sum_{i,j,k}A_{i,j}^{k}f_{k}(x)y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

For details on the theory of connections,
see e.g.
[
Ati57
]
,
[
Wel08
]
.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
X
be a manifold,
E
a vector bundle over
X
with a
connection
\nabla
.

Then every vector field on the manifold
E
decomposes as
the sum of a horizontal (with respect to
\nabla
) and a
vertical vector field.

Proof.

This follows from the direct sum decomposition
T_{E}=H\oplus V
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Every holomorphic vector bundle
E
over a Stein manifold
X
admits a holomorphic connection.

Proof.

The obstruction to the existence of a holomorphic connection on
E
is given by a cohomology class in
H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}\otimes End(E))
. The sheaf
\Omega^{1}\otimes End(E)
is coherent and
X
is Stein, hence Cartan’s Theorem B guarantees that the cohomology group is trivial and there is no obstruction to the existence of a holomorphic connection.
∎

Now that we can lift vector fields, we need to check that completeness is preserved.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle equipped with a holomorphic connection.

Then for every complete vector field on
X
the lift to
E
which is horizontal with respect to the connection is complete, too.

Proof.

Let
V=\sum_{i}f_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
be (the local expression for) a complete vector field on the base with
associated flow
\Phi(x,t)
, i.e.,
\Phi^{\prime}(x,t)=f(\Phi(x,t))
.

Locally, on some neighborhood
U\subset X
we may trivialize
E
such that

\nabla(\sigma)=d\sigma+\alpha\sigma

where
\alpha
is a holomorphic function on
U
with
values in
Mat(n\times n,\mathbb{C})
. The horizontal lift of
V
is given by Equation (
1
) above.

Hence, we are looking for a function
H:U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{r}
such that

H_{i}^{\prime}(x,s,t)=\sum_{i,j,k}\alpha^{i}_{jk}(\Phi(x,t))v_{j}(\Phi(x,t))H_{k}(x,s,t)

But this is a
linear
differential equation. Thus there is a global
solution (global on the fiber).

∎

We conclude this section with a lemma which will be used later.
Loosely speaking, a
\pi
-compatible vector field is one that maps
\pi
-fibers to
\pi
-fibers; see Section
4
for more details.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle equipped with a holomorphic connection over a Stein manifold with the density property
X
.

Then every horizontal vector field on
E

which is
\pi
-compatible
can be approximated by linear combinations of complete vector fields.

Proof.

A
\pi
-compatible horizontal vector field on
E
is the lift of a vector field
V
on
X
. Because of the density property and Fact
1.11
,
V
can be approximated by a linear combination of complete vector fields. Since lifting a vector field is a linear operation, the same linear combination of the lifts of these vector fields will approximate our original vector field on
E
.
∎

3.
The Euler vector field

Let
E\to X
be a complex vector bundle. Then there is a natural

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
E
with a corresponding vector field,
the “
Euler vector field”

\Theta
.

In local coordinates: Let
(x_{i})_{i}
be coordinates on the
base space
X
and let
(y_{j})_{j}
be fiberwise linear coordinates
on
E
.
Then

\Theta=\sum_{j}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

The vector bundle structure is completely determined by the Euler vector field:
The zero-section coincides with the vanishing locus of
\Theta
,

\pi(p)=\pi(q)
for
p,q\in E
iff the closures of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-orbits
through
p
and
q
intersect.
A function
\lambda
on
E
is fiber wise linear if and only
if
\Theta\lambda=\lambda
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with a holomorphic connection

\nabla
and let
\Theta
be the Euler vector field.

Then
[\Theta,V]=0
for every horizontal vector field
V
.

Proof.

This follows from an easy calculation using (
1
).
∎

4.
Vector fields (in)compatible with the vector bundle structure

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
a holomorphic vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
.

•

An automorphism
\phi
of the complex manifold
E
is called

“compatible with the bundle structure”
,
or simply “
\pi
-compatible”
if
\phi
maps
\pi
-fibers to
\pi
-fibers
(In other words: if

\forall p,q\in E:\pi(p)=\pi(q)\implies\pi(\phi(p))=\pi(\phi(q))
.)

•

A vector field
v
on
E
(not necessarily complete)
is called “
\pi
-compatible” if for every

p,q\in E
with
\pi(p)=\pi(q)
we have
\pi_{*}(v_{p})=\pi_{*}(v_{q})
.

In the above setup an automorphism or a vector field
is called
incompatible
if it is not compatible.

If
X
is smooth and therefore normal, being “compatible”
is equivalent to the existence of an automorphism
\psi
of
X
such that
the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

is commutative.

Note that we do not require the map
\phi
to be fiberwise linear.
Hence the notion “
\pi
-compatible automorphism
”
is weaker than the notion
of a vector bundle automorphism.

For a vector field
v
on
E

“
\pi
-compatibility”
is equivalent to the existence of a vector field

w
on
X
with
\pi_{*}(v_{p})=w_{\pi(p)}\ \forall p\in E
.

If a
\pi
-compatible vector field is also complete, the associated
flow consists of
\pi
-compatible automorphisms.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
v
be a
\pi
-compatible vector field
on
E
and let
f
be a holomorphic function
on
E
.
Assume that
v
is not everywhere tangent to the
\pi
-fibers (i.e.,
\pi_{*}v\not\equiv 0
).

Then
fv
is
\pi
-compatible if and only if
f
is constant
along the
\pi
-fibers.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is not constant along the
\pi
-fibers.

The set
\Omega_{1}
of
p\in E
where
f
is not constant along
the
\pi
-fiber is open and dense
in
E
.
The set
\Omega_{2}
of points where
v
is not tangent to the
\pi
-fiber is
also open and dense.

Hence the intersection of these two sets
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}

is still dense and in
particular not empty. Let
p
be a point in
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
.
Choose
q\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}

with
\pi(p)=\pi(q)
, but
f(p)\neq f(q)
.
(This is possible, since
p\in\Omega_{1}
.)
By construction, we have
\pi_{*}(v_{p})\neq 0
and

\pi_{*}(v_{p})=\pi_{*}(v_{p}q)
.

We obtain

\pi_{*}(fv_{p})=f(p)\underbrace{\pi_{*}(v_{p})}_{\neq 0}\neq f(q)\pi_{*}(v_{p})=f(q)\pi_{*}(v_{p})=\pi_{*}(fv_{q}).

Thus
fv
can not be
\pi
-compatible unless
f
is constant along the

\pi
-fibers.

The opposite direction is obvious.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
V
be a complete vector field on
E
. Then the associated

•

consists of vector bundle automorphisms if
[\Theta,V]=0
.

•

consists of automorphisms
\phi
which stabilize each fiber
(i.e.
\pi\circ\phi=\pi
) and act fiberwise
as translations if
[\Theta,V]=-V
.

Proof.

This follows easily from explicit calculations in local

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
E\to X
be a vector bundle over a Stein manifold
X
.

If there exists a
\pi
-incompatible automorphism
\phi
, there also
exists a
\pi
-incompatible complete vector field
v
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
denote the Euler vector field (see §

).
If
\phi_{*}\Theta
is
\pi
-incompatible, we are done.
Thus we may assume that
\phi_{*}\Theta
is
\pi
-compatible.
Let
F
be a
\pi
-fiber such that
\phi^{-1}(F)
is not a fiber.
Then we choose a holomorphic function
f
on
X
such that

is not constant along
\phi^{-1}(F)
. This implies that

is not constant on

. Now

implies that
\phi_{*}\left((f\circ\pi)\Theta\right)

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be an algebraic vector bundle and assume
that

Then there

\pi
-incompatible complete holomorphic vector field

.

The automorphism group of every flexible smooth affine variety

which is contained in the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module generated by the

(\sigma_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

must vanish on almost all integers, hence this
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module does not
equal
\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{C}})
, although it is dense.

Proof.

Fix a Runge exhaustion
U_{k}
, i.e., we choose
holomorphically convex open subsets
U_{k}

of
X
such that
U_{k}
is relatively compact in
U_{k+1}
,

U_{k}
is Runge in
X
and
X
equals the union of all
U_{k}
.

(For example, if
\rho
is a strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion
function, we may choose
U_{k}=\{x\in X:\rho(x)<k\}
.)

Fix
p\in X
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Choose
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}

within the given family such that

\sigma_{1}(p),\ldots,\sigma_{r}(p)
generate
E_{p}
.
Then these sections generate
E
outside an analytic subset
S_{1}

of codimension at least
1
.

Now we may proceed by recursion: Choosing one point on each irreducible
component of
S_{1}\cap U_{k}
and then enough sections such that they generate
at these points. Since
U_{k}
is relatively compact,
there are only finitely many such irreducible components.
Hence finitely many additional sections are enough.
In this way we may increase
the codimension of the singular set
step by step, finally verifying:
For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
a finite subfamily
\{\sigma_{j}:j\in I_{k}\}
such that these
sections generate
E_{p}
for every
p\in U_{k}
.

Let
N_{k}=\#I_{k}
. Let
I_{k}=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{N_{k}}\}
.
We consider the sheaf morphism
\mathcal{O}^{N_{k}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
given by

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k}}f_{j}\sigma_{j}

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
denote the kernel sheaf.
For every
k
we obtain a long exact
sequence

\to\Gamma(U_{k},\mathcal{O}^{N_{k}})\longrightarrow\Gamma(U_{k},E)\longrightarrow H^{1}(U_{k},{\mathcal{K}})\to\ldots

Since
U_{k}
is Stein
and
{\mathcal{K}}
is coherent,
the cohomology group
H^{1}(U_{k},{\mathcal{K}})

must be trivial. Thus

\Gamma(U_{k},\mathcal{O}^{N})\to\Gamma(U_{k},E)
is surjective.
Hence every section of
E|_{U_{k}}
is contained in the

\mathcal{O}_{U_{k}}
-module generated by the
\sigma_{i}
.

Let
s\in\Gamma(X,E)
. For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the section
s

is contained in the
\mathcal{O}_{U_{k}}
-module generated by the
\sigma_{j}
.

Fix a hermitian metric on
E
.
Since
U_{k}
is Runge in
X
, we may find
s_{k}\in\Gamma(X,E)

such that

||s-s_{k}||_{\overline{U_{k-1}}}\leq\frac{1}{k}

Then
s=\lim_{k\to\infty}s_{k}
for the topology of locally uniform
convergence.
Therefore

\Gamma(X,E)
equals the
closure of the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module generated by the
\sigma_{j}
.
∎

Theorem 5.5

(Peter-Weyl-Theorem)
.

Let
V
be a Fréchet space and let
G
be a reductive group
acting linearly and continuously on
V
.
An element
v\in V
is called
G
-finite if the
G
-orbit through
v

spans a finite-dimensional vector subspace of
V
.

Then the set of all
G
-finite elements is dense in
V
.

Proof.

See
[
PW27
]
for the original reference pertaining only to compact groups or the textbook
[
Sha94
]
for the statements about reductive groups.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a vector bundle,
\Theta
the Euler vector field
and let
H
be a closed subspace of
\Gamma(E,TE)
which is invariant
under

ad(\Theta):v\mapsto[\Theta,v].

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let

H_{k}=\{v\in H:[\Theta,v]=kv\}.

Then

\oplus_{k\geq-1}H_{k}

is dense in

.

Proof.

This follows from the Peter-Weyl-Theorem,
because
H_{k}
equals the set of

for which
the natural
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action operates as
\lambda:v\to\lambda^{k}v
.
∎

6.
Vertical vector fields

As mentioned in the introduction, we will prove that vector fields preserving the projection can be approximated by Lie combinations of complete vector fields. We already proved that horizontal vector fields preserving the projection can be approximated by Lie combinations of complete vector field. We will now show the same result for vertical vector fields. These aspects will be put together in Proposition
6.7
.

Remark 6.1
.

If a vector field
v
on a manifold can be approximated
by Lie combinations of complete vector fields, it can already
be approximated by linear combinations. This holds, because

[v,w]=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\phi_{t}^{*}w-w}{t}

if
v
is complete with flow
\phi_{t}
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle
of rank
r
over a Stein manifold
X
.

An
“admissible family”
(or “admissible frame”)
is a family
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r};f)

such that

•

Every
\sigma_{j}
is a section in
E
.

•

\sigma_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\sigma_{r}
is not identically zero.

•

f
is a non-constant holomorphic function on
X
.

•

f\left(\sigma_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\sigma_{r}\right)^{-1}
is a
holomorphic section of
(\det E)^{*}
.

We observe that by the last condition
\sigma_{1}(x),\ldots,\sigma_{r}(x)

is a vector space basis of the fiber
E_{x}=\pi^{-1}(x)\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{r}

for every
x\in X
with

f(x)\neq 0
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle
of rank
r
over a Stein manifold
X
.

Let
S
be a countable subset of
X
. Then there exists
an admissible family (in the sense of Definition
6.2
)
with

f(x)\neq 0\ \forall x\in S

Proof.

The space of global holomorphic sections
V=\Gamma(X,E^{r})

is a Fréchet vector space with respect to the topology of locally uniform
convergence. Hence its topology is completely metrizable and
V

satisfies the Baire property. Thus the intersection of countably many open
dense subsets is still dense and in particular not empty.

For each
x\in S
we consider the set
W_{x}
of those

(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in V

for which
\sigma_{1}(x),\ldots,\sigma_{r}(x)

forms a basis of the vector space
E_{x}
.

Then each
W_{x}
is open and dense in
V
, implying:

\cap_{x\in S}W_{x}\neq\{\}

We choose an element

(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in\cap_{x\in S}W_{x}

Consider the set
C
of all
x
in which these sections fail to
generate, i.e., the zero-locus of

\sigma_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\sigma_{r}
.
This is a closed analytic set which does not intersect
S
.
Taking multiplicities into account, we may regard
C
as a divisor.
Let
F
denote the space of those holomorphic
functions on
X
which vanish on each irreducible component
of
C
with at least the same multiplicity
as
\sigma_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\sigma_{r}
, i.e.,
div(F)\geq C
as divisors.

For each
x\in S
, the set
\{f\in F:f(x)\neq 0\}
is open and dense.
Thus there is an element
f\in F
with

\forall x\in S:f(x)\neq 0
.

By construction,
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r};f)
is an admissible
family.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

There are finitely many admissible families

A_{k}=(\sigma_{1}^{k},\ldots,\sigma^{(k)}_{r};f^{(k)})

such that

\forall x\in X:\exists k:f^{(k)}(x)\neq 0

Proof.

We start with one admissible family
A_{1}
.

Given finitely many admissible families
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
, let

Z_{k}
denote the common zero locus of the
f^{(k)}
.
We choose a countable set
\Sigma_{k}
which intersects every irreducible
component of
Z_{k}
and apply Lemma
6.3
with
S=\Sigma_{k}
.
This yields another family
A_{k+1}
such that
\dim Z_{k+1}<\dim Z_{k}
.

Thus we may proceed recursively until we found enough families to arrive
at an empty common zero locus.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a vector bundle of rank
r
and let

(\sigma_{r},\ldots,\sigma_{r};f)
be an admissible family.

Then there exists a continuous linear operator
\Xi
from the
space of vector fields on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
to the space of vertical vector fields
on
E
such that:

(1)

\Xi
preserves the natural grading (induced by the natural

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action).

(2)

For every
x\in X
with
f(x)\neq 0
the evaluated
map
v\mapsto\Xi(v)(x)
equals the map induced by a linear
isomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and
E_{x}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.

(3)

\Xi(v)
is complete if
v
is complete.

Proof.

We define
X^{*}=\{x\in X:f(x)\neq 0\}
and

\Phi:X^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\to E
as

\Phi(x;t)=\frac{1}{f(x)}\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}\sigma_{j}(x)

and
\Phi_{x}(t)=\Phi(x,t)
.

For a vector field

v=\sum_{k=1}^{r}h_{k}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}

we obtain a vertical vector field on
E|_{X^{*}}
as

\displaystyle(D\Phi_{x})(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r}h_{k}(t)(D\Phi_{x})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}h_{k}(t)(D\Phi_{x})_{k,l}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{l}}\right)\text{ with $s=(D\Phi_{x})(t)$}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}h_{k}(\Phi_{x}^{-1}(s))(D\Phi_{x})_{k,l}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{l}}\right)

We need some linear algebra: For a
r\times r
-matrix
S
Cramer’s rule states

S^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det(S)}S^{\#}
. If
f
is a scalar, we deduce

(S\frac{1}{f})^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det(S\frac{1}{f})}(S\frac{1}{f})^{\#}=\frac{1}{f^{-r}\det(S)}f^{1-r}(S)^{\#}=\frac{f}{\det(S)}S^{\#}

In our setting we have
\Phi_{x}=\frac{1}{f(x)}S(x)

where
S(x)
is the
r\times r
-matrix
formed by the
\sigma_{j}(x)
and we may deduce that

x\mapsto\Phi_{x}^{-1}
which a priori is defined only on
X^{*}

actually extends holomorphically to all of
X
.

On the other hand, by construction
x\mapsto f(x)\Phi_{x}
is holomorphic
on the whole
X
.

Therefore

(2)

\Xi(v)=\tilde{v}=\left(f(x)\right)^{m}(D\Phi_{x})(v)

defines a global vertical holomorphic vector field on
E
for
m\geq 1
.

Immediate from the construction we have properties
(1)
and
(2)
.
Property
(3)
holds on
X^{*}
, because
\Phi_{x}
is an isomorphism
where
f(x)\neq 0
.

Taking
m\geq 2
in equation (
2
)
the vector field
\tilde{v}
will vanish
on all fibers
E_{x}
with
f(x)=0
and therefore trivially be complete.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a Stein manifold
X
.

If
r=rank(E)\geq 2
,
then every vertical vector field on
E
can be
approximated by Lie combinations of complete vertical vector fields.

Proof.

Let
v
be a vertical vector field on
E
. Due to the Peter-Weyl-theorem
(Theorem
5.5
) we may approximate
v
by finite sums
of homogeneous vector fields. Hence it suffices to consider the case
where
v
is homogeneous, i.e.,
[E,v]=kv
for some degree
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Vertical vector fields of a fixed degree
k
can be regarded as
sections in a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module sheaf on
X
.

Lemma
6.4
gives us finitely many admissible
families

A_{k}=(\sigma_{1}^{n},\ldots,\sigma^{(n)}_{r};f^{(n)}):\quad\forall x\in X:\exists k:f^{(n)}(x)\neq 0

With Lemma
6.5
it follows that for every fixed
k

there are finitely many vertical
vector fields
w_{\alpha}
on
E
such that

(1)

Every
w_{\alpha}
is induced by a map
\Xi
associated to
an admissible family as
described in Lemma
6.5
.

(2)

For every
x
the vector space of vector fields
of degree
k
on
E_{x}
is spanned by the
w_{\alpha}
.

By Andersen-Lempert theory every vector field on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
can be
approximated by linear combinations of complete vector fields
(taking into account
6.1
).

Thus for every
w_{\alpha}
we can find a vector field
u
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and a
sequence of sums of complete vector fields
u_{n}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}

with

w_{\alpha}=\Xi(u)\text{ and }\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}=u

Therefore
w_{\alpha}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Xi(u_{n})
. Note that the vertical vector
fields
\Xi(u_{n})
are complete by Lemma
6.5
, (3).

Thus every
w_{\alpha}
is approximable. On the other hand, since
X
is Stein,
the vertical vector fields of fixed degree
k
form a coherent sheaf and
this sheaf is everywhere generated by the
w_{\alpha}
,
we know that every vertical vector field of degree
k
may be
represented as a finite
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear combination of the
w_{\alpha}
.

Since we discuss
vertical
vector fields on
E
, finite

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear combinations of complete resp. approximable
vertical vector fields are still complete resp. approximable.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.7

Let

be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
\geq 2
over a Stein manifold

Then every
\pi
-compatible vector field on

Proof.

A
\pi
-compatible vector field

on

can be decomposed as the sum of a vertical vector field

and a

The conclusion is obtained by applying Proposition

∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle
over a Stein manifold
X
.

Let
L
be the Lie algebra of all holomorphic vector fields on the manifold

E
and let
P
denote the subalgebra of
\pi
-preserving vector fields.

Then there does not exist any intermediate closed Lie algebra, i.e., if

H
is a closed Lie subalgebra of
L
with
P\subset H
, then either

H=P
or
H=L
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in X
. In local coordinates near
p
, vector fields on
E
may be
written as

(3)

v=\sum_{\alpha,k}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}+\sum_{j}g_{j}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
are local coordinates on the base
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})
local coordinates in the
fiber direction with
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
being a
multi-index. As usual,
|\alpha|=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}
.

Note that
v\in P
if and only if

f_{\alpha,k}\equiv 0
for
|\alpha|>0
.

Thus any element
w
in
P
may locally be written in the form

(4)

w=\sum_{k}f_{0,k}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}+\sum_{j}g_{j}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

Consequently,
elements in
L/P
may be represented as

(5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
\alpha\neq 0\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

Assume that
H\neq P
.
We consider
the adjoint action of the Euler vector field

\Theta=\sum_{j}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

For
v
as in (
3
) we have

[\Theta,v]=\sum_{\alpha,k}|\alpha|f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}+\sum_{j}(\theta(g_{j})-g_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

Hence
[\Theta,v]\in P
for
v\in P
and we obtain an induced
action
ad(\Theta)
on
L/P
via
v\mapsto[\Theta,v]
.
This action is given as

ad(\Theta)(w)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
\alpha\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\alpha|f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\text{ for }w=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
\alpha\neq 0\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

Note that
\Theta
is complete and yields a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
L/P

given as

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\ni\lambda:w\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
\alpha\neq 0\end{subarray}}\lambda^{|\alpha|}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

with
w
as in (
5
).

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is a reductive group and
L
is a Fréchet space.
Hence we may apply the Peter-Weyl Theorem (Theorem
5.5
).
This theorem implies that, given a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action
\mu
on a Fréchet space

V
, the subspace
\oplus_{d\in{\mathbb{Z}}}V_{d}
is dense in
V
where

V_{d}=\{v\in V:\mu(\lambda)(v)=\lambda^{d}v\}.

Since
L
is a Fréchet space and
P
and
H
are closed invariant subspaces,
the quotient space
H/P
is a Fréchet space on which
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts.

It follows that if we take
w\in H/P
and write it as in (
5
), we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=c}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\in H/P\ \forall c\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In other words, if
H\neq P
, then

H/P
contains a non-zero vector field
w
which is homogeneous in the

y
-graduation, i.e.,
\exists m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
:

H\ni w=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
|\alpha|=m\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\quad\text{ modulo $P$}

The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action induced by the Euler vector field yields
gradings on
L
,
H
,
P
and the algebra of vertical vector fields

V
.

For any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
the vector spaces
L_{d}
,
P_{d}
and
V_{d}
can be realized
as spaces of global section on
X
of coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{L}}_{d}
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
,
{\mathcal{V}}_{d}
, e.g., for any open subset
U\subset X
, we define

{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(U)
as the set of all vector fields
w
on
\pi^{-1}(U)\subset E

which are in the form (

) and satisfy
[\Theta,w]=dw
.

Similarily the quotient space
(L/P)_{d}=L_{d}/P_{d}
arises as space of
global sections
for a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
(
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
).

Using this notions,
H\neq P
implies that
there exists a number
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that
(L/P)_{d}
has non-zero intersection with
H/P
.

Let us now consider vector fields which locally may be written as

u=\sum_{j}c_{j}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

The family of these vector fields define a coherent sheaf

\mathcal{A}={\mathcal{V}}_{1}
on
X
. Since
X
is Stein, this sheaf is spanned
by global sections. Fix a non-zero such vector field
u
.

The adjoint action
ad(u):v\mapsto[u,v]
preserves
P
and
defines a morphism of the graded Lie algebra
L/P
of degree
-1
,
i.e.,

ad(u):(L/P)_{k+1}\mapsto(L/P)_{k}

Observe that
u\in P
, because it is a vertical vector field.
Therefore

ad(u):(H/P)\mapsto(H/P)

Let

v=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
|\alpha|=d\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

represent a non-zero homogeneous element of
H/P
.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is spanned by global sections, there is a vector
field
u\in P
as above with

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
|\alpha|=d\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)u\left(y^{\alpha}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\not\equiv 0

It follows:
If
H/P
contains a non-zero homogeneous element of
degree

, it also contains a non-zero homogeneous element of
degree
d
.

By repeated application of this argument we deduce that

H/P
contains a non-zero homogeneous element of degree
1
.

In other words, for the grading of
L/P=\oplus_{d}(L/P)_{d}

defined by the eigenspace
decomposition with respect to
ad(\Theta)
, we have

H/P
has non-zero intersection with
(L/P)_{1}
.

Next we claim:
There is such a homogeneous element
v
of degree
1

in
H/P
which may locally be written as

v=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
|\alpha|=1\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

such that there exists an index
\alpha
(with
|\alpha|=1
)
such that
f_{\alpha,k}(p)\neq 0
.

For any
v
as in the claim we call the minimum of the vanishing orders
in
p

of the components
f_{\alpha,k}
(
|\alpha|=1
) simply the

of
v
.
Thus our goal is to show that
H
contains an element
v
of degree
1
and
order
0
.
Let
N
denote the minimal number
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
for which
H
contains an
element of degree
1
and order
N
and fix such an element

.

Next let
w
be a vector field on
X
which does not vanish in
p
.
In appropriately chosen local coordinates near
p
on
X
we have

w=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}

and

v=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,k\\
|\alpha|=1\end{subarray}}f_{\alpha,k}(x)y^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}=\sum_{j,k}f_{j,k}(x)y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

with

.

Next let
\tilde{w}
be the lift of

with respect to some holomorphic

. Then

\tilde{w}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sum_{j,l}A_{j,l}(x)y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{l}}

where the

are holomorphic functions which depend on the

As a horizontal vector field,
\tilde{w}\in P
.
Hence
[\tilde{w},v]\in H
. Now

[\tilde{w},v]=\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial f_{j,k}}{\partial{x_{1}}}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\text{ $+$ terms vanishing of order $\geq N$}

By the minimality of

along the integral curve of

-direction).

But

was arbitrary vector field on
X
(with
w(p)\neq 0
).
Hence we obtain:
If
N>0
, then for every integral curve of every

to this curve vanishes of order at least
N+1
in
p
.

The vanishing order of a holomorphic function equals the minimum of
the vanishing order of the restriction to every such integral curve.
Hence the assumption

yields a contradiction to the
minimality assumption.
This proves the claim, i.e.,

admits an element

of degree

and order

.

Next claim:

defines a sub
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module sheaf

:
Indeed, if

(note that

).

,

.

Thus we have

H\ni v=\sum_{j,k}f_{j,k}(x)y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

\exists j,k:f_{j,k}=1
.
Wlog

.

Fix

Since

a global vector field

0&\text{ else. }\\
\end{cases}

We lift

In other words, there is (for every

Recall that

in

The present section and the one immediately following it establish a few facts needed in the proof of the line bundle case.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
V,W,Y
be vector fields on a manifold, and
\phi
a function.

Assume that
V\phi=0
,
W\phi=0
,
[W,Y]=0
and
[W,V]=V
.

Define

.

Then
W\zeta=\zeta

and
\zeta W
is contained in the Lie algebra generated
by
\phi W
,
Y
,
\phi Y
and
V

Proof.

\displaystyle\phantom{=}[\phi W,[Y,V]]-[\phi Y,V]

\displaystyle=-[Y,[V,\phi W]]-[V,[\phi W,Y]]-\phi[Y,V]+\underbrace{(V\phi)}_{=0}Y

\displaystyle=-[Y,\phi\underbrace{[V,W]}_{=-V}]-[Y,\underbrace{(V\phi)}_{=0}W]-[V,\phi\underbrace{[W,Y]}_{=0}]+[V,(Y\phi)W]-\phi[Y,V]

\displaystyle=[Y,\phi V]-0-0+(VY\phi)W+(Y\phi)\underbrace{[V,W]}_{=-V}-\phi[Y,V]

\displaystyle=\hbox{\pagecolor{yellow}$\phi[Y,V]$}+\hbox{\pagecolor{pink}$(Y\phi)V$}+(VY\phi)W-\hbox{\pagecolor{pink}$(Y\phi)V$}-\hbox{\pagecolor{yellow}$\phi[Y,V]$}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(VY\phi)}_{\zeta}W

\displaystyle=\zeta W

Thus
\zeta W
is in the Lie algebra generated by

\phi W
,
Y
,
\phi Y
and
V
.

On the other hand

\displaystyle W\zeta

\displaystyle=WVY\phi

\displaystyle=WY\underbrace{V\phi}_{=0}+W[V,Y]\phi

\displaystyle=0+[V,Y]\underbrace{W\phi}_{=0}+[W,[V,Y]]\phi

\displaystyle=-\left([V,\underbrace{[Y,W]}_{=0}]+[Y,\underbrace{[W,V]}_{=V}]\right)\phi\quad\text{\sl by Jacobi-Identity}

\displaystyle=-[Y,V]\phi=-\left(Y\underbrace{V\phi}_{=0}-VY\phi\right)=\zeta

∎

Let
\pi:E\to X

We decompose the space
D

\forall k,m:\forall v\in D_{k},w\in D_{m}:[v,w]\in D_{k+m}

For

A vector field

Now for every function

11.
Flexibility

This Section aims to prove that the total space of an algebraic vector bundle over a flexible affine variety is flexible, too.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
E\to B
be an algebraic vector bundle
over a smooth affine

k
-variety
B
.

Let
v
be a locally nilpotent derivation of
k[B]
.

Then the vector bundle
E
is invariant under the associated flow,
i.e.,

\mu_{t}^{*}E\simeq E

where
\mu_{t}
denotes the automorphism of
B
corresponding to the

k
-algebra automorphism

f\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}v^{n}(f)}{n!}

Proof.

In
[

]
(see also
[

]
)
Lindel proved (partially) the Bass-Quillen conjecture.
Lindel’s result implies that, given a smooth affine variety

and

,
every
vector bundle on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\times V
is given as a pull-back of a vector bundle
on
V
via the projection map
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\times V\to V
.

Let
\mu:G_{a}\times B\to B
be the map defining the group action
induced by
v
, i.e.,
\mu(t,x)=\mu_{t}(x)

12.
Tame sets

The notion of
tame set
was first introduced by Rosay and Rudin
[
RR88
]
as they were studying the properties of the group of holomorphic automorphisms of complex Euclidean spaces.

Definition 12.1

(
[
RR88
]
,Def. 3.3)
.

Let
e_{1}
be the first standard basis vector of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A set
A\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called
tame

(in the sense of Rosay and Rudin) if there exists an
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
F(A)=\mathbb{N}\cdot e_{1}
.
It is very tame if we can choose
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
to be volume preserving.

In recent years, this notion was generalized to arbitrary complex manifolds. The definition of
strongly tame set
is due to Andrist and the first named author
[
AU19
]
;
weakly tame sets
were introduced by the second named author
[
Win19
]
.

Definition 12.2

(Andrist, Ugolini)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
G\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)
be a subgroup of its group of holomorphic automorphisms. We call a closed discrete infinite set
A\subset X
a
strongly
G
-tame set
if for every injective mapping
f\colon A\to A
there exists a holomorphic automorphism
F\in G
such that
F|_{A}=f
. If
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)
, we only say
strongly tame set
.

Definition 12.3

(Winkelmann)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold.
An infinite discrete
subset
D
is called
weakly tame

if for every exhaustion function
\rho\colon X\to\mathbb{R}

and every function
\zeta\colon D\to\mathbb{R}

there exists an automorphism
\Phi
of
X

such that
\rho(\Phi(x))\geq\zeta(x)
for all
x\in D
.

Any strongly tame set is automatically weakly tame, hence their names. When
X={\mathbb{C}}^{n},\ n>1
, both notions coincide with the classical definition by Rosay and Rudin.

It is clear that the existence of a tame set (strong or weak) requires the group of holomorphic automorphisms to be somewhat large. In this section, we provide existence results for total spaces of holomorphic vector bundles.

Proposition 12.4
.

Let
E\to B
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a Stein manifold
B
. Then
E
admits a weakly tame set.

See
[
Win19
]
, Proposition 8.4 for a closely related result.

Proof.

Choose a closed discrete sequence
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset E
such that
\pi(a_{n})\neq\pi(a_{m})
for
n\neq m
,
a_{n}\neq 0\in E_{\pi(a_{n})}
, and such that
\pi(A)
is also closed and discrete in
B
.

Fix an exhaustion function
\rho
. Given a function
\zeta\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
, we can find a holomorphic function
h
on
B
such that
\rho(e^{h(\pi(a_{n}))}\cdot a_{n})>\zeta(a_{n})
, because
A
is a discrete subset in the
Stein manifold
B
.

Let
\Theta
be the Euler vector field, then the time-1 flow
\Phi
of the vector field
h\Theta
is such that
\rho(\Phi(x))\geq\zeta(x)
for all
x\in A
i.e.
A
is weakly tame.
∎

As we saw, the existence of a weakly tame set in the total space of a vector bundle over a Stein manifold does not require any additional hypothesis. The picture is quite different for strongly tame sets.

Given a complex manifold

Let

Proof.

Let

Let

is
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(B)^{0}
-tame, there exists an automorphism

such that
\Phi(\pi(a_{n}))=\pi(f(a_{n}))

\Phi
induces a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}

Abstract
Time-dependent studies of inclusive charm decays to multibody self-conjugate final states can be used to determine the indirect C ​ P CP -violating observable A Γ A_{\Gamma} and the mixing observable y C ​ P y_{CP} , provided that the fractional C ​ P CP -even content of the final state, F + F_{+} , is known. This approach can yield significantly improved sensitivity compared with the conventional method that relies on decays to C ​ P CP eigenstates. In particular, D → π + ​ π − ​ π 0 D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0} appears to be an especially powerful channel, given its relatively large branching fraction and the high value of F + F_{+} that has recently been measured at charm threshold.

Introducing self-conjugate multibody decays and the
CP
-even fraction
F_{+}

The
CP
content of an inclusive self-conjugate multibody decay, for example
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, can be measured with a sample of coherently produced
D{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}
pairs at the
\psi(3770)
resonance, such as that collected by the CLEO-c and BESIII experiments. A double-tag technique is employed in which one
D
meson is reconstructed in the signal decay of interest, and the other in its decay to a
CP
eigenstate. In such an event, and neglecting any
CP
violation, the quantum numbers of the
\psi(3770)
meson means that the
CP
eigenvalue of the signal decay is fixed. The
CP
-even fraction of the signal decay is given by
F_{+}=N^{+}/(N^{+}+N^{-})
, where
N^{+}
(
N^{-}
) designates the number of decays tagged as
CP
-even (-odd), after correction for detector inefficiencies and the specific branching fractions of the
CP
eigenstate tags employed. In this manner
F_{+}
has been measured for the decay
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
and found to be
0.973\pm 0.017
, indicating the mode to be almost fully
CP
even
MINAKSHI
;
FOURPI
.

Although
CP
violation is neglected in the currently available measurements of
F_{+}
this assumption introduces negligible bias in the result.
Both the Standard Model and theories of New Physics expect direct
CP
violation in charm decays to be
\leq 10^{-3}

THEORY2
, a
prediction which is compatible with existing experimental results
PDG
. Any effects will therefore be small alongside the measurement precision attainable with the CLEO-c and current BESIII data sets. Furthermore, the double-tag analyses performed at these experiments have no sensitivity to indirect
CP
violation at leading order in
(x,y)
, as the
D{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}
system is produced at rest. For the specific case of
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, a recent time-integrated high precision analysis by LHCb has revealed no evidence of any direct
CP
-violating effects
PIPIPI0
.

There is a simple relationship between
F_{+}
and the parameters that describe the intensity and strong-phase variation over the phase space of the decay.
The amplitude of a multibody decay such as
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
is dependent on the final-state kinematics, which can be uniquely defined by
the Dalitz plot coordinates
s_{12}=m^{2}(\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
and
s_{13}=m^{2}(\pi^{-}\pi^{0})
.
The amplitude of a
D^{0}
decay to a specific final state is given by
\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(s_{12},s_{13})=a_{12,13}e^{i\delta_{12,13}}
, where the integral of
|\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(s_{12},s_{13})|^{2}
over the full Dalitz plot is normalised to unity.
Consider the situation where the Dalitz plot is divided into two bins by the line
s_{12}=s_{13}
.
The bin for which
s_{12}>s_{13}
is labelled
-1
and the opposite bin is labelled
+1
.
The parameter
K_{i}
(
{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptK}{}}_{i}
) is the flavour-tagged fractional intensity, being the proportion of decays to fall in bin
i
in the case that the mother particle is known to be a
D^{0}
(
{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}{}^{0}
) meson:

K_{i}\equiv\int_{i}|a_{12,13}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{12}\,\mathrm{d}s_{13}.

(7)

The parameter
c_{i}
is the cosine of the strong-phase difference between
D^{0}
and
{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}{}^{0}
decays averaged in bin
i
and weighted by the absolute decay rate:

c_{i}\equiv\int_{i}\frac{a_{12,13}\bar{a}_{12,13}\cos(\delta_{12,13}-\bar{\delta}_{12,13})}{\sqrt{K_{i}{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptK}{}}_{i}}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{12}\,\mathrm{d}s_{13}.

(8)

A parameter
s_{i}
is defined in an analogous manner for the sine of the strong-phase difference.

The
CP
-tagged populations of these bins,
N_{i}^{\pm}
, normalised by the corresponding single
CP
-tag yields, is given by
ANTONBONDAR

\displaystyle N_{i}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{D}\Big(K_{i}\,\pm\,2c_{i}\sqrt{K_{i}{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptK}{}}_{i}}\,+\,{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptK}{}}_{i}\Big).

(9)

Here
h_{D}
is a normalisation factor independent of bin number and
CP
tag.
When there is no direct
CP
violation in the decay
\mathcal{A}_{{{\kern 1.63885pt\overline{\kern-1.63885ptD}{}}{}^{0}}}(s_{12},s_{13})=\bar{a}_{12,13}e^{i\bar{\delta}_{12,13}}\equiv a_{13,12}e^{i\delta_{13,12}}
and so

\displaystyle{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptK}{}}_{i}={K_{-i}},

\displaystyle c_{i}=c_{-i}\;{\rm and}\;s_{i}=-s_{-i}.

(10)

Under this assumption, and the identities
N^{\pm}=\sum_{i}N_{i}^{\pm}
, and
\sum_{i}K_{i}=1
, it follows that in the two-bin case

F_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\,+\,2c_{1}\sqrt{K_{1}K_{-1}}\right).

(11)

Measurements with inclusive self-conjugate multibody decays

Now consider, for an incoherently produced
D
meson, the time dependence of a self-conjugate multibody decay.
The time evolution of the
D^{0}
to the point
(s_{12},s_{13})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(t,s_{12},s_{13})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{12,13}e^{i\delta_{12,13}}g_{+}(t)

(12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r_{CP}e^{i\phi_{CP}}a_{13,12}e^{i\delta_{13,12}}g_{-}(t),

where
g_{\pm}(t)=\frac{1}{2}[e^{-i(M_{1}-i\Gamma_{1}/2)t}\pm e^{-i(M_{2}-i\Gamma_{2}/2)t}]
.
Ignoring terms of
\mathcal{O}(x^{2},y^{2},xy)
or higher, the rate of decay to that point is proportional to

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(t,s_{12},s_{13})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\Gamma t}\bigg\{a_{12,13}^{2}-a_{12,13}a_{13,12}r_{CP}\Gamma t\times

\displaystyle\Big[y\cos(\delta_{12,13}-\delta_{13,12}-\phi_{CP})+

\displaystyle x\sin(\delta_{12,13}-\delta_{13,12}-\phi_{CP})\Big]\bigg\}.

Integrating this over the two bins of the full Dalitz plot leads to the time-dependent decay probability

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(D^{0}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{+1}|\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(t,s_{12},s_{13})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{12}\,\mathrm{d}s_{13}+

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}|\mathcal{A}_{D^{0}}(t,s_{12},s_{13})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{12}\,\mathrm{d}s_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm exp}{(-\Gamma t)}\Big[1-r_{CP}(2F_{+}-1)\times

\displaystyle(y\cos\phi_{CP}-x\sin\phi_{CP})\Gamma t\Big],

where use is made of the definitions of
c_{i},s_{i}
and the relations given in Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
).
Hence the width of the decay is approximated at first order in
x
and

by

\hat{\Gamma}\approx\Gamma\Big[1+r_{CP}(2F_{+}-1)\times(y\cos\phi_{CP}-x\sin\phi_{CP})\Big].

(16)

The time evolution for the
{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}{}^{0}
decay to the point
(s_{12},s_{13})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{{{\kern 1.63885pt\overline{\kern-1.63885ptD}{}}{}^{0}}}(t,s_{12},s_{13})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{CP}}e^{-i\phi_{CP}}a_{12,13}e^{i\delta_{12,13}}g_{-}(t)

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle a_{13,12}e^{i\delta_{13,12}}g_{+}(t),

and thus the width of the
{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}{}^{0}
decay is approximated by

\hat{\bar{\Gamma}}\approx\Gamma\Big[1+\frac{1}{r_{CP}}(2F_{+}-1)\times(y\cos\phi_{CP}+x\sin\phi_{CP})\Big].

(18)

Defining
A_{\Gamma}^{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{\hat{\Gamma}-\hat{\bar{\Gamma}}}{\hat{\Gamma}+\hat{\bar{\Gamma}}}
and

\displaystyle A_{\Gamma}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2F_{+}-1)y\cos\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}-\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right)-

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2F_{+}-1)x\sin\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}+\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right),

\displaystyle y_{CP}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2F_{+}-1)y\cos\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}+\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right)-

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2F_{+}-1)x\sin\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}-\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right).

These expressions contain an additional dilution factor of
(2F_{+}-1)
in comparison to the
CP

5

1

Expressions (

In this case the relations in Eq. (
10

D^{0}
and

With the inclusion of direct

\displaystyle A_{\Gamma}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[(2F_{+}^{\prime}-1)y\cos\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}-\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right)-

\displaystyle(2F_{+}^{\prime}-1)x\sin\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}+\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right)+

\displaystyle y\Delta\sin\phi_{CP}\left(r_{CP}-\frac{1}{r_{CP}}\right)+

The Belle collaboration has reported a model-dependent analysis of the mode

through comparing the

;

with

decays as long as

exists from the charm threshold it is possible to obtain this information from a comparison of a measurement of

obtained from

.

In summary, inclusive measurements of the time evolution of mutibody self-conjugate charm decays offer the possibility to obtain significantly improved sensitivity to
CP
violation and mixing in the
D^{0}{{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}{}^{0}}
system. The observables
A_{\Gamma}^{\rm eff}
and
y^{\rm eff}_{CP}
are simply related to those of the
CP
eigenstate case,
A_{\Gamma}
and
y_{CP}
, by a dilution factor
(2F_{+}-1)
, where
F_{+}
is the fractional
CP
-even content of the decay. This parameter may be measured in coherently produced
D{\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}}
decays at the
\psi(3770)
. One of the modes for which
F_{+}
is known,
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, has the potential to yield a more precise determination of
A_{\Gamma}
and
y_{CP}
than is possible with
CP
eigenstate decays, and another,
D\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, also offers good sensitivity. Other promising channels exist with relatively high branching fractions and should also be exploited, provided that analyses at the
\psi(3770)
show them to be dominated by a single
CP
eigenstate. Alternatively, measurements of
y_{CP}^{\rm eff}
using these latter channels will allow their
CP
content to be determined, which is valuable input for the programme to measure the unitarity angle
\gamma
. First results using this class of decays are eagerly awaited.

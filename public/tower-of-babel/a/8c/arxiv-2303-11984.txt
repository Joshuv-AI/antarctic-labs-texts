On the Homotopy Type of the Polyhedral Join over the Independence Complex of a Forest
Kengo Okura

Abstract
. We consider a certain class of simplicial complexes which includes the independence complexes of forests. We show that if a simplicial complex K K belongs to this class, then the polyhedral join 𝒵 K ∗ ​ ( X ¯ , ∅ ) \mathcal{Z}^{*}_{K}(\underline{X},\emptyset) is homotopy equivalent to a wedge sum of CW complexes of the form Σ r X i 1 ∗ X i 2 ∗ ⋯ ∗ X i k \Sigma^{r}X_{i_{1}}*X_{i_{2}}*\cdots*X_{i_{k}} , where X ¯ \underline{X} is a family { X i } i ∈ V ⁡ ( K ) \{X_{i}\}_{i\in V(K)} of CW complexes and Σ \Sigma denotes the unreduced suspension. This result is applied to study the homotopy type of the independence complex of the lexicographic product G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] of a graph H H over a forest G G . We denote by L m L_{m} a tree on m m vertices with no branches. Proposition 2.3

0
-simplex.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
K
be a non-empty s-vertex-decomposable simplicial complex. If
K
is not connected, then

\displaystyle K=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)\sqcup\langle\{v\}\rangle,

for some
v,w\in V(K)
.

Proof.

We first observe that
K
is not a simplex since
K
is not connected. So, there exists
v,w
such that
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
,
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
are s-vertex-decomposable, and
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)
. Here we have

\displaystyle K=\mathrm{st}_{K}(v)\cup_{\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)}\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(v)\cup_{\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)}\mathrm{st}_{K}(w).

Thus, if
K
is not connected, then
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
must be empty since both
\mathrm{st}_{K}(v)
and
\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)
are connected. Therefore, we get the desired conclusion since
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)=\emptyset
implies that
\mathrm{st}_{K}(v)=\langle\{v\}\rangle
.
∎

Recall from
[
5
, Definition 11.1]
that a simplicial complex
K
is called
vertex decomposable
if

•

K
is either a simplex or
\{\emptyset\}
, or

•

there exists a vertex
v
of
K
such that

–

\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
,
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
are vertex-decomposable, and

–

no maximal simplex of
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
is maximal in
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
.

We show that s-vertex-decomposability is a stronger version of vertex-decomposability.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex and
v,w
be vertices of
K
such that
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)
. Then we have

(1)

v\neq w
,

(2)

\mathrm{lk}_{K}(w)=\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)\cap\mathrm{dl}_{K}(w)
,

(3)

\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)\subset\mathrm{lk}_{K}(w)
.

Proof.

We get
v\neq w
from
v\notin\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)\ni w
. We also obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{lk}_{K}(w)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)\cap\mathrm{dl}_{K}(w)=\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)\cap\mathrm{dl}_{K}(w).

Thus, in order to prove (3), it remains to show that
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)\subset\mathrm{dl}_{K}(w)
. We have
\{v,w\}\notin K
since
v\notin\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)
. Thus, if
\sigma\in\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
contains
w
, then it implies
\{v,w\}\subset\sigma\cup\{v\}\in K
, a contradiction. So,
w\notin\sigma
for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex. If
K
is s-vertex-decomposable, then
K
is vertex-decomposable.

Proof.

Let
K
be a s-vertex-decomposable simplicial complex. We prove the proposition by induction on
\#\left(V(K)\right)
.
If
\#\left(V(K)\right)=0
, then
K=\{\emptyset\}
, which is vertex-decomposable.

For a non-negative integer
n
, suppose that if
K^{\prime}
is s-vertex-decomposable simplicial complex such that
\#\left(V(K^{\prime})\right)<n
, then
K^{\prime}
is vertex-decomposable. Consider
K
with
\#\left(V(K)\right)=n
.
If
K=\Delta^{n-1}
, then
K
is vertex-decomposable. Assume that there exist vertices
v,w
of
K
such that

•

\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
,
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
are s-vertex-decomposable, and

•

\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)
.

\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
and
\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)
are vertex-decomposable by the assumption of the induction. Furthermore, for any maximal simplex
\sigma\in\mathrm{lk}_{K}(v)

\displaystyle\sigma\subsetneq\sigma\cup\{w\}\in\mathrm{st}_{K}(w)=\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v).

The above inclusion follows from Lemma

\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

(1) directly follows from the definition of polyhedral joins.

We prove (2). First, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{K}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=(A_{1}*\cdots*A_{m})\cap(B_{1}*\cdots*B_{m})

\displaystyle\left(\begin{aligned} &A_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{i}&&(i\in\sigma)\\
&\emptyset&&(i\notin\sigma)\end{aligned}\right.\\
&B_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{i}&&(i\in\tau)\\
&\emptyset&&(i\notin\tau)\end{aligned}\right.\end{aligned}\right)

\displaystyle\ \vee\left(\bigvee_{k,r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}}\left({\bigvee}_{\binom{k+1}{n-2k-3r+2}\binom{k+r}{r}}\Sigma^{r}X^{*(k+1)}\right)\right)

5.
The Independence Complex of the Lexicographic Product over a Forest

The lexicographic product
{G}[{H}]
of
H
over
G
, which is called
composition
by Harary
[
10
]
, is defined as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G,H
be graphs. The
lexicographic product

{G}[{H}]
of
H
over
G
is a graph defined by

\displaystyle V({G}[{H}])=V(G)\times V(H),

\displaystyle E({G}[{H}])=\left\{(u_{1},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2})\ \middle|\ \begin{aligned} &u_{1}u_{2}\in E(G)\\
&\text{ or}\\
&u_{1}=u_{2},v_{1}v_{2}\in E(H)\end{aligned}\right\}.

The independence complex of

and the polyhedral join are related in the following way.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G,H
be graphs. Then we have

\displaystyle I({G}[{H}])=\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),\emptyset).

Proof.

Follows from
[
15
, Example 25, Proposition 27]
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\{(K_{i},L_{i})\}_{i\in[m]}
be a family of pairs of simplicial complexes and their subcomplexes.
For a family

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}(\underline{X})\cong\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{K}}(\underline{X}),\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{L}}(\underline{X})),

Proof.

Using Proposition

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}(\underline{X},\emptyset)^{*\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{S\in M}\left(\bigcup_{\sigma\in(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}}(\underline{X},\emptyset)^{*\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{S\in M}\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{S\in M}\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{J_{1}*\cdots*J_{m}}(\underline{X})

\displaystyle\left(J_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &K_{i}&&(i\in S)\\
&L_{i}&&(i\notin S)\end{aligned}\right.\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigcup_{S\in M}\left(\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{J_{1}}(\underline{X})*\cdots*\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{J_{m}}(\underline{X})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{S\in M}(\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{K}}(\underline{X}),\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{L}}(\underline{X}))^{*S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{K}}(\underline{X}),\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{\underline{L}}(\underline{X})).

∎

Let

∎

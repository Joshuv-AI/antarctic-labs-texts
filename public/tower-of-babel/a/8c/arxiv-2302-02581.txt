The Mouse Set Theorem Just Past Projective
Mitch Rudominer

Abstract
. We identify a particular mouse, M ld M^{\text{ld}} , the minimal ladder mouse, that sits in the mouse order just past M n ♯ M_{n}^{\sharp} for all n n , and we show that ℝ ∩ M ld = Q ω + 1 \mathbb{R}\cap M^{\text{ld}}=Q_{\omega+1} , the set of reals that are Δ ω + 1 1 \Delta^{1}_{\omega+1} in a countable ordinal. Thus Q ω + 1 Q_{\omega+1} is a mouse set. This is analogous to the fact that ℝ ∩ M 1 ♯ = Q 3 \mathbb{R}\cap M^{\sharp}_{1}=Q_{3} where M 1 ♯ M^{\sharp}_{1} is the the sharp for the minimal inner model with a Woodin cardinal, and Q 3 Q_{3} is the set of reals that are Δ 3 1 \Delta^{1}_{3} in a countable ordinal. More generally ℝ ∩ M 2 ​ n + 1 ♯ = Q 2 ​ n + 3 \mathbb{R}\cap M^{\sharp}_{2n+1}=Q_{2n+3} . The mouse M ld M^{\text{ld}} and the set Q ω + 1 Q_{\omega+1} compose the next natural pair to consider in this series of results. Thus we are proving the mouse set theorem just past projective. Some of this is not new. ℝ ∩ M ld ⊆ Q ω + 1 \mathbb{R}\cap M^{\text{ld}}\subseteq Q_{\omega+1} was known in the 1990’s. But Q ω + 1 ⊆ M ld Q_{\omega+1}\subseteq M^{\text{ld}} was open until Woodin found a proof in 2018. The main goal of this paper is to give Woodin’s proof.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we write
\mathbb{R}
to mean
{}^{\omega}{\omega}
and we call elements of
{}^{\omega}{\omega}

In the 1990’s Martin, Steel and Woodin proved that, assuming large cardinals or determinacy,

the set of reals that are

in a countable ordinal,
where

is the standard, minimal proper-class inner model with
n
Woodin cardinals (including the case

For even

,

\Sigma^{1}_{n+2}

For odd

,

, the largest countable
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}

set closed downwards under
\Delta^{1}_{n+2}
degrees. See
[

]
.

Recall the definition of

-small (Definition 1.1 from
[
Ste95
]
.) A premouse

is

-small above

iff whenever

is the critical point of an extender on the

-sequence, and

We say that M is

-small iff
M
is
n
-small above
0
.

In the above-mentioned theorems, if one wishes to work with countable mice rather than proper class models, one may substitute

for

is the least sound, iterable mouse
that is not

-small.

In
[
Rud95
]
and
[
Rud97
]
we explored an extension of this theory
to projective-like pointclasses beyond the projective and achieved partial results.
But even at the very first step past projective the full mouse set theorem was left open.

Let
\Sigma^{1}_{\omega}

be the pointclass consisting of recursive unions of Projective sets. Consider
the projective hierarchy built over
\Sigma^{1}_{\omega}
:

\Pi^{1}_{\omega}=\neg\Sigma^{1}_{\omega}
,

\Sigma^{1}_{\omega+1}=\exists^{\mathbb{R}}\Pi^{1}_{\omega}
, etc.
By
[
Ste83
]
,
\Sigma^{1}_{\omega+n}=\Powerset(\mathbb{R})\cap\Sigma_{n+1}(J_{2}(\mathbb{R}))

and we get a Second Periodicity Theorem:
\Sigma^{1}_{\omega+2n}
and
\Pi^{1}_{\omega+2n+1}
are scaled pointclasses.

\Sigma^{1}_{\omega}
is not closed under
\forall^{\omega}
and so is not a perfect analog of
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
.
But
\Pi^{1}_{\omega+1}
is closed under
\exists^{\omega}
and is a good analog of
\Pi^{1}_{1}
or
\Pi^{1}_{3}
.
Let

be the set of reals that are
\Delta^{1}_{\omega+1}
in a countable ordinal.

In
[
Rud95
]
, we defined the minimal ladder mouse

and showed that

\mathbb{R}\cap M^{\text{ld}}\subseteq Q_{\omega+1}
. (

is the minimal iterable mouse
M
such
that for each

there is a cardinal
\delta_{n}
of
M
and an initial segment

such that
\delta_{n}
is Woodin in

and

is not

-small above
\delta_{n}

But the other direction,

, remained open until
Woodin found a proof in the summer of 2018. Woodin transmitted the proof to Steel who fleshed it out
and extended it in some hand-written notes
[
Ste18
]
.
The author refined the relevant sections of those notes into this paper.
Woodin and Steel have elected to not appear as co-authors of this paper,
but the mathematical provenance of the ideas contained in the paper should be clear from this paragraph.

No new technology is involved in the proof and it could have been discovered in the 1990’s. The main ingredients
of the proof are stationary tower forcing, genericity iterations, and a lemma stating that
all small generic extensions of a sufficiently correct, countable, transitive model of Set Theory
meet the same equivalence classes of a thin, sufficiently
definable equivalence
relation. (See Lemma
5.2
.)

One of the key technical themes of the paper is that, even though
the
\delta_{n}
of
M^{\text{ld}}
are only Woodin over an initial segment

of
M^{\text{ld}}
as opposed to all of
M^{\text{ld}}
, because they are
cardinals in
M^{\text{ld}}
we can recover some of the same properties
that we would have if
\left\langle\delta_{n}\right\rangle
were an
\omega
-sequence
of Woodins in
M^{\text{ld}}
. A good example of this theme is Lemma

5.5
which gives us a filter

for the countable
stationary tower of
M^{\text{ld}}
such that for all
n
,

is
\mathbb{Q}_{<\delta_{n}}^{M}
-generic over
P_{n}
, and this implies
that the ultrapower
\Ult(M^{\text{ld}},G)
has some of the same properties as an
ultrapower by the stationary tower up to the supremum
of
\omega
Woodin cardinals.

Beyond
\ZFC
, there are two necessary hypotheses for this paper:

(1)

that determinacy holds for the pointclasses we consider (roughly,
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{2}(J_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
),

(2)

and that the mice we consider exist and are sufficiently iterable.

We will not attempt to articulate optimal hypotheses. Instead, for the official hypothesis for this paper we will
use:

“there exists
\omega
Woodin cardinals with a measurable cardinal above them all.” If we don’t explicitly state otherwise, this is the assumption of all

This large cardinal hypothesis implies

(see

[

But the reader should keep in

is not really

\varphi(y_{1})=\varphi(y_{2})
, and
\varphi^{M[g_{1}]}(y_{1})=\varphi^{M[g_{2}]}(y_{2})
.

Furthermore
\sigma^{M[g_{1}]}=\sigma^{M[g_{2}]}
where

\sigma^{M[g_{i}]}:(\boldsymbol{\delta}^{1}_{\omega})^{M[g_{i}]}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\delta}^{1}_{\omega}

is given by Definition
4.6
.

Let
h
be a
\Pi^{1}_{\omega}
code for a subset
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then
(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{1}]}=(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{2}]}

and
\sigma^{M[g_{1}],h,x}=\sigma^{M[g_{2}],h,x}
where

\sigma^{M[g_{i}],h,x}:(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{i}]}\rightarrow(T^{h}(x))^{V}

is the tree embedding from
Definition
4.6
.

Proof.

All of this follows easily from Corollary
5.3
and its proof.

Let
y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g_{1}]
be as in the statement of the Lemma. The proof of
Corollary
5.3
gives us a

y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g_{2}]
such that
\varphi(y_{1})=\varphi(y_{2})
.

Since this is true for all projective norms
\varphi^{\prime}

we have that

\ran(\sigma^{M[g_{1}]})=\ran(\sigma^{M[g_{2}]})

and since
\sigma^{M[g_{i}]}
is the inverse of the transitive collapse of its range, we have that

\sigma^{M[g_{1}]}=\sigma^{M[g_{2}]}
and so
\varphi^{M[g_{1}]}(y_{1})=\varphi^{M[g_{2}]}(y_{2})
.

Suppose
(s,u)\in(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{1}]}
. Let
n=\length(s)=\length(u)
. Then there is

y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g_{1}]
extending
s
such that
(\forall i<n)\,(y_{1},x)\in G^{i}_{h(i)}
and

u(i)=(\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y_{1},x))^{M[g_{1}]}
.

By Corollary
5.3
, relativized to
x
, there is a
y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g_{2}]
such that

•

y_{2}\!\!\upharpoonright\!n=y_{1}\!\!\upharpoonright\!n
,

•

(y_{2},x)\in\bigcap\limits_{i<n}G^{i}_{h(i)}
, and

•

for
i<n
,
\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y_{2},x)=\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y_{1},x)
.

So then for
i<n
,
(\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y_{2},x))^{M[g_{2}]}=(\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y_{1},x))^{M[g_{1}]}
.
So
(s,u)\in(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{2}]}
. We have shown that
(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{1}]}\subseteq(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{2}]}

and the same argument shows that

(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{2}]}\subseteq(T^{h}(x))^{M[g_{1}]}
.

That
\sigma^{M[g_{1}],h,x}=\sigma^{M[g_{2}],h,x}
now follows immediately.
∎

Definition 7.15
.

Let
M
be a countable transitive model of

\ZFC-\text{ Replacement }+\text{ ``}\exists\text{ cofinally many inaccessible cardinals.'' }

Suppose that every set-generic extension of
M
is projectively correct.

Let
h
be a
\Pi^{1}_{\omega}
code for a subset
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let

.

Let
\delta
be a cardinal of

. Then we define

•

X^{M,\delta}=\ran(\sigma^{M[g]})

•

\bar{T}^{h,x,M,\delta}=\ran(\sigma^{M[g],h,x})

where
g
is any

\Coll(\delta,\omega)
-generic over
M
.

The definitions makes sense because by Lemma
7.14
,
applied to a rank initial segment of
M
,

\sigma^{M[g]}
and
\sigma^{M[g],h,x}
don’t depend on the choice of
g
.

X^{M,\delta}\subset\boldsymbol{\delta}^{1}_{\omega}
, namely

X^{M,\delta}=\big\{\varphi(y)\,\mid\,\varphi
is a projective norm and
y\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g]
for
some
g\subset\Coll(\delta,\omega)
generic over
M\big\}
.

\bar{T}^{h,x,M,\delta}\subset(T^{h}(x))^{V}
, namely

\bar{T}^{h,x,M,\delta}=\big\{(s,u)\in(T^{h}(x))^{V}\,\mid\,\exists g\,\Coll(\delta,\omega)
-generic over
M
and
\exists y\in\mathbb{R}\cap M[g]
s.t.

y
extends
s
and
(\forall i<\length(u))\,(y,x)\in G^{i}_{h(i)}
and

u(i)=\varphi^{i_{0}}_{h(i_{0}),i_{1}}(y,x)\big\}
.

If
\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
then

and

\bar{T}^{h,x,M,\delta_{1}}\subset\bar{T}^{h,x,M,\delta_{2}}

Let

Let
\sigma^{M,\infty}
be the inverse of the transitive collapse of

.

Let

Very roughly speaking

is “the version of

in the symmetric collapse
over

that satisfies the hypotheses of Definition

Lemma 7.16
.

Fix

be such that

Let
n

,
\delta<\delta_{n}
and

There is a

\Coll(\delta_{n},\omega)
-generic over

\Coll(\delta_{n},\omega)

-generic over

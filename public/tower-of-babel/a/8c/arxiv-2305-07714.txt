The Perron solution for elliptic equations
without the maximum principle

Abstract




Abstract

In this article we consider the Dirichlet problem
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
with respect to a
second-order elliptic differential operator in divergence form.
We do not assume a divergence condition as in the
pioneering work by Stampacchia, but merely assume that
0
is not
a Dirichlet eigenvalue.
The purpose of this article is to define and investigate a solution
of the Dirichlet problem, which we call Perron solution,
in a setting where no maximum principle is available.
We characterise this solution in different ways: by approximating the domain
by smooth domains from the interior, by variational properties,
by the pointwise boundary behaviour at regular boundary points and
by using the approximative trace.
We also investigate for which boundary data the Perron solution
has finite energy.
Finally we show that the Perron solution is obtained as an

H^{1}_{0}
-perturbation of a continuous function on
\overline{\Omega}
.
This is new even for the Laplacian and solves an open problem.

May 2023.

MSC (2020): 31B25, 35J25, 46E35, 31C25.

Keywords: Dirichlet problem, Perron solution, approximative trace, regular points,
domain approximation.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded set with boundary
\Gamma
.
The Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian asks to find a function

u
such that

\Delta u=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi

for a given
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
This is a classical and well-studied problem.
The domain
\Omega
is called
Wiener regular
if for all

\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
there exists a classical solution

u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}(\Omega)
.
While this is a weak regularity condition that can be elegantly characterised,
it is not satisfied for arbitrary
\Omega
.

A generalised solution for the Dirichlet problem was introduced by
Perron
[
Per
]

and further developed in particular by Wiener
[
Wie1
]
,

[
Wie2
]
,
[
Wie3
]
, Brelot
[
Bre
]
and
Keldyš
[
Kel1
]
,
[
Kel2
]
.
For this Perron solution several different but equivalent descriptions
were obtained.
Given
\varphi
, the Perron solution is unique and, if possible,
classical, but in general the boundary condition
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi

will not be satisfied at every boundary point.
Perron’s construction uses a supremum of subsolutions and infimum of
supersolutions, and critically depends on the maximum principle
(see
[
GT
]
Section 2.8).

In this paper we define and study a generalised solution for the Dirichlet
problem with continuous boundary data
\varphi
on an arbitrary bounded,
open
\Omega
and for a general second-order elliptic operator with
lower-order coefficients.
We only assume a straightforward spectral condition.
As the maximum principle does not need to hold for such an operator,
Perron’s construction cannot be used.
In
[
AE2
]
the authors considered the aforementioned operators,
but only on Wiener regular domains, where it was possible to obtain a
classical solution, that is a harmonic
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})

with
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
.

We give four equivalent descriptions of our generalised solution.
Since these generalise different characterisations of the Perron solution
in a more classical setting, it is justified to continue to use the
name Perron solution.
Our results extend work by Stampacchia
[
Sta
]
,
since we are not dependent on a maximum principle and therefore do not
need a divergence condition on the lower-order coefficients.
The four equivalent descriptions are based on the following
concepts, for simplicity expressed here for the Laplacian:

1.

A continuous solution operator
T\colon C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)
via
H^{1}
-extensions and an
L_{\infty}
-estimate.

2.

Domain approximation from the interior.

3.

Proper limit behaviour at regular boundary points.

4.

Existence of a function
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})

with
\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi

such that
\Delta\Phi\in(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.
Then the Perron solution is the unique harmonic function
u
on
\Omega

such that
u-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The fourth description is completely new,
even in the special case of the Laplacian, where it solves an open problem.

Moreover, we characterise those
\varphi
for which one obtains solutions
with finite energy, i.e. when
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, which turns out
to be independent of the chosen elliptic operator.
Finally, we investigate under which conditions it suffices to consider
a trace in
H^{1}(\Omega)
with respect to the
(d-1)
-dimensional
Hausdorff measure on
\Gamma
in order to uniquely determine solutions.

In order to precisely state the main theorems of this paper, we have to introduce some notation,
which we keep throughout the paper.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be open and bounded with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
and
d\geq 2
.
For all
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
let
a_{kl}\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}

be a bounded measurable function and suppose that there exists a

\mu\in(0,1]
such that

\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}a_{kl}(x)\,\xi_{k}\,\overline{\xi_{l}}\geq\mu\,|\xi|^{2}

for all
x\in\Omega
and
\xi\in\mathds{C}^{d}
.
Further, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
let
b_{k},c_{0}\colon\Omega\to\mathds{C}

and
c_{k}\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}

be bounded and measurable.
Define the map
{\cal A}\colon H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\to{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
by

\langle{\cal A}u,v\rangle_{{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times{\cal D}(\Omega)}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}c_{k}\,(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{v}+\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{v}

for all
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Here we denote by
{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
the antidual of

{\cal D}(\Omega)=C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the space of all test functions.
A function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathds{C}
is called
{\cal A}
-harmonic

if
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
{\cal A}u=0
.
Throughout this paper we assume that
0
is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue,
that is, if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is such that
{\cal A}u=0
, then
u=0
.

This completes the list of conditions that we assume throughout the paper.
Recall that the set of all Dirichlet eigenvalues is discrete.
Stampacchia’s divergence condition implies that all Dirichlet eigenvalues
lie in the open right half-plane, whereas under our assumption there may be
Dirichlet eigenvalues in the left half-plane.
Obviously the latter does occur for certain choices of the coefficients.
Moreover, the above assumption is natural and in some sense best
possible since otherwise solutions can be perturbed with a non-trivial

{\cal A}
-harmonic element of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and there is no hope for
unique solvability.

Given
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to\mathds{C}
we study the Dirichlet problem

(D_{\varphi})\quad\mbox{Find an ${\cal A}$-harmonic function $u$ such that ``$u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi$'',}

where we will give different meanings to “
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
”.
The investigation of how solutions may attain the given boundary data

\varphi
is the purpose of this paper.

Given a function
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
,
a
classical solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})

is an
{\cal A}
-harmonic
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

such that
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
.
We emphasise that a classical solution is defined on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Define

{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):{\cal A}u=0\},

the space of all
{\cal A}
-harmonic functions in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Note that
{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then

H^{1}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\oplus{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega),

(1)

see Lemma
2.1
(b)
.
As in Stampacchia
[
Sta
]
we use (
1
) to define
a weak solution.
The difficulty in this paper is to show an
L_{\infty}
-estimate
for this solution without Stampacchia’s divergence condition

[
Sta
]
(9.2).

The first main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a unique bounded linear map
T\colon C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)

such that the function
T\varphi
is
{\cal A}
-harmonic for all
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)

and
\Phi|_{\Omega}-T(\Phi|_{\Gamma})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for all

\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
.

We prove Theorem
1.1
as a special case of Theorem
2.11
.
We write
T^{\Omega}
instead of
T
if we want to make precise the
underlying domain.
Given
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
we call
T\varphi
the

Perron solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
Note that
T\varphi
is a function on
\Omega
, not on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Next we describe several properties of the operator
T
which justify our terminology.
If
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\ldots\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
are open and bounded, then
we write
\Omega_{n}\uparrow\Omega
if
\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega
for all
n\in\mathds{N}

and for all compact
K\subset\Omega
there exists an
N\in\mathds{N}
such that

K\subset\Omega_{n}
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
with
n\geq N
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(a)

Let
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\ldots\subset\mathds{R}^{d}

be open and bounded with
\Omega_{n}\uparrow\Omega
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{\Omega_{n}}(\Phi|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})=T(\Phi|_{\Gamma})

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
.

(b)

Let
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and suppose that
{\cal A}u=0
.
Then
T(u|_{\Gamma})=u|_{\Omega}
.

This theorem is proved in Propositions
2.15
and
2.16
.
Theorem
1.2
allows us to give an alternative description of
T
.
First suppose that
\Omega
is Wiener regular.
Then by
[
AE2
]
Theorem 1.1 (or Corollary
3.4
in this paper)
for every
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
there exists a unique classical solution

u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
Theorem
1.2
(b)
shows that
T\varphi=u|_{\Omega}
.
In particular, classical solutions are unique, which is
not obvious without the maximum principle.
Now suppose that
\Omega
is arbitrary.
Then we may choose Wiener regular
(or even
C^{\infty}
-domains)
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\ldots
such that
\Omega_{n}\uparrow\Omega
.
Given
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
, take as
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})

an extension of
\varphi
and define
\varphi_{n}=\Phi|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then Theorem
1.2
(a)
shows that

T\varphi=\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{\Omega_{n}}\varphi_{n}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
.
For the Laplacian, that is if
a_{kl}=\delta_{kl}
and

b_{k}=c_{k}=c_{0}=0
, Keldyš
[
Kel2
]
Theorem II showed that
this limit coincides with the solution defined by Perron, justifying
our terminology.
Hervé
[
Her
]
generalised this result to
purely second-order differential operators
(which trivially verify Stampacchia’s divergence condition).

We next give a ‘Sobolev’-description of the Perron solution, which is
new even for the Laplacian.
By
H^{-1}(\Omega)
we denote the antidual of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Since
0
is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue by assumption, it follows
that
{\cal A}|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
is bijective (see Lemma
2.1
(a)
).
In general, a function
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
does not
have an extension
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)

(just take
\Omega
Lipschitz and
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\setminus H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
).
But the following extension is possible.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then there exists a
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
such that

\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
and
{\cal A}\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Hence there exists a unique
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

{\cal A}v={\cal A}\Phi
.
It follows that
T\varphi=\Phi|_{\Omega}-v
.

For the Laplacian it was proved
before in
[
AD1
]
that
T(\varphi)=\Phi|_{\Omega}-v
whenever
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
,

\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,

\Delta\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
as distribution,
\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi

and
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is such that

\Delta v=\Delta\Phi
.
But it remained an open question whether such an extension
\Phi
exists for all

\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
The above theorem gives a positive answer even for arbitrary elliptic operators.
As a consequence, for the Laplacian the Perron solution is always an

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-perturbation of a suitable extension

\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})
of
\varphi
.
(Remark. The extra condition
\Phi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
in Theorem
1.3

is needed if merely
a_{kl},b_{k}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.
If
a_{kl},b_{k}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, as is the case for the Laplacian, then this
extra condition is not needed.
We come back to this in Proposition
3.15
.)

As an immediate consequence of the last statement in
Theorem
1.3
one can characterise when

T\varphi
has finite energy.
It is remarkable that this property does not depend on the elliptic operator.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
if and only if there exists a

\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
.

Next we turn to regular points as in the paper
[
LSW
]

of Littman, Stampacchia and Weinberger.
Let
z\in\Gamma
.
We say that
z
is
{\cal A}
-regular
if

\lim_{x\to z}(T\varphi)(x)=\varphi(z)

for all
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
We say that
\Omega
is
{\cal A}
-regular
if
z
is
{\cal A}
-regular for all
z\in\Gamma
.
Therefore
\Omega
is
{\cal A}
-regular if and only if for each
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)

the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
has a unique classical solution.
If
-{\cal A}
is the Laplacian, then we simply say that

z
is
regular
if
z
is
(-\Delta)
-regular.

Extending Stampacchia’s result to our more general situation, we shall show in
Theorem
3.2
that a point
z\in\Gamma
is
{\cal A}
-regular if and only if it is regular.
Hence this notion of regularity is independent of the operator.

Even if
\Omega
is not Wiener regular, we can characterise the Perron
solution by the pointwise behaviour at the boundary.
Recall that a subset
P\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
is called a
polar set

if
\mathop{\rm cap}P=0
.
Given an assertion
Q(z)
for all
z\in\Gamma
, then we say that

Q(z)
holds for
quasi every

z\in\Gamma
if there exists a polar set
P\subset\Gamma

such that
Q(z)
is true for all
z\in\Gamma\setminus P
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and
u\in C_{b}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

u=T\varphi
.

(ii)

{\cal A}u=0
and
\lim_{x\to z}u(x)=\varphi(z)
for quasi every
z\in\Gamma
.

The implication
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
is well known
for the Laplacian, but it seems to be new for elliptic operators,
even in the situation considered by Stampacchia.
We prove Theorem
1.5
in Corollary
3.12
.

Finally we wish to consider the notion of approximative trace and
variational solution of the Dirichlet problem.
For this we provide
\Gamma
with the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure,
denoted by
\sigma
, and we assume for the remainder of the introduction
that
\sigma(\Gamma)<\infty
.
Variational solutions are based on the decomposition (
1
).
Let
w=v+u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\oplus{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
.
If in addition
w\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, then by our definition

T\varphi=u
is the Perron solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
,
where
\varphi=w|_{\Gamma}
is the restriction of
w
to the boundary.
Now we want to replace the restriction by a very weak notion of a trace.
Given
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma)
, then
we say that
\varphi
is an
approximative trace of
u
, or shortly,
a
trace of
u
if
there are
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.
We define

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)=\{\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma):\mbox{there exists a $u\in H^{1}(\Omega)$ such that
$\varphi$ is a trace of $u$}\},

the space of all traces.
Clearly
0
is a trace of every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For the remainder of this introduction we conversely suppose that
if
0
is a trace of
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, that is

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):0\mbox{ is a trace of }u\}.

(2)

Condition (
2
) is satisfied if
\Omega
has a continuous boundary
in the sense of graphs (see
[
Sau1
]
Corollary 7.48).

Now given
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
, there exists a
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\varphi
is a trace of
w
.
Decompose

w=v+u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\oplus{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega).

Since
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the function
\varphi
is also a trace of
u
.
In virtue of Condition (
2
), the function
u
is the unique

{\cal A}
-harmonic function in
H^{1}(\Omega)
having
\varphi
as a trace.
We call
u

the variational solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
Therefore for each
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
there exists a unique variational
solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
Even though the coupling between
w
and a trace
\varphi
is
very weak, the link between variational and Perron solutions is
as strong as it can be.
Our main result on the variational solution is the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume Condition (
2
).
Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.

(a)

Suppose in addition
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
and let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the variational
solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
Then
T\varphi=u
.

(b)

If
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then
\varphi
is a trace of
T\varphi
.
Consequently,
T\varphi
is the variational
solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
and
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
depends heavily on the previous results
on possible extensions of
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and it is given
in Theorems
5.6
and
5.7
.

We end this selection of highlights of the paper with a comment on the
coefficients.

Remark 1.7
.

Throughout this paper we assume that the
c_{k}
are real valued.
All results are also valid if the
c_{k}
are complex valued with

\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
The reason is as follows.
Define the map
\widetilde{\cal A}\colon H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\to{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
by

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\cal A}u,v\rangle_{{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times{\cal D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}(b_{k}-i\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k})\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}

\displaystyle{}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}(\mathop{\rm Re}c_{k})\,(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{v}+\int_{\Omega}(c_{0}-i\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k})\,u\,\overline{v}

for all
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Since

\int_{\Omega}(\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k})(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{v}=-\int_{\Omega}(\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k})\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Im}c_{k})\,u\,\overline{v}

for all
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
,
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
it follows that
{\cal A}=\widetilde{\cal A}
.
Now the coefficients of
\widetilde{\cal A}
satisfy the conditions on the
coefficients in the paper.

We do not know whether one can allow
c_{k}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
complex
valued throughout the paper.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we consider the Perron solution and prove
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
A few parts are based on techniques developed in
[

]
, but here more
delicate arguments are needed to control the constants, since in

(a)
a sequence of
domains is used.
In Section
3
we consider the
{\cal A}
-regular points
and show that they are independent of the elliptic operator.
We also give in Theorem

a sufficient condition
(in terms of the pointwise behaviour at the boundary)
for a function to be in

.
As one of the many consequences this gives
Theorem
1.5
in Corollary
3.12
.
In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.3
via a delicate

Finally in Section
5
we consider the approximative trace,
the variational solution of the Dirichlet problem, its possible uniqueness
(that is a characterisation of Condition (
2
))
and prove Theorem
1.6
.

2
The Dirichlet problem with continuous data

In this section we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
The proof requires some preparation.
Throughout this section we adopt the notation and assumptions as in
the first part of the introduction.
We first consider
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)

of the form
\varphi=\Phi|_{\Gamma}
with
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then we define an operator

T_{0}\colon\{\Phi|_{\Gamma}:\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\}\to C_{b}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega)

with range in the
{\cal A}
-harmonic functions
and show that it is bounded with respect to the
C(\Gamma)
-norm and the

C_{b}(\Omega)
-norm.
Next the Stone–Weierstraß theorem gives a continuous extension

T\colon C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)
.
We use a technique of
Stampacchia in order to show that
T\varphi
is
{\cal A}
-harmonic.
That gives Theorem
1.1
.

Define the form
\gothic{a}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}(u,v)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}c_{k}\,(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{v}+\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{v}.

(3)

Let
A^{D}
be the operator in
L_{2}(\Omega)
associated with the
form
\gothic{a}|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
,
that is

\displaystyle\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\Phi|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})-T(\Phi|_{\Gamma})\|_{C(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\Phi|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})-T^{\Omega_{n}}(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})\|_{C(K)}+\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})-T(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega})\|_{C(K)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\|T(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega})-T(\Phi|_{\Gamma})\|_{C(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\Phi|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}-\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})\|_{C_{b}(\Omega_{n})}+\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})-T(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega})\|_{C(K)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\|T(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\Gamma}-\Phi|_{\Gamma})\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{c}\,\varepsilon+\|T^{\Omega_{n}}(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega_{n}})-T(\widetilde{\Phi}|_{\partial\Omega})\|_{C(K)}+\hat{c}\,\varepsilon.

and using the first case

.

.

.

There exist open and bounded

for all

.

(a)
and

for all

.

\Omega_{1}\subset\mathds{R}^{d}

\mathop{\rm supp}u_{n}\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\overline{\Omega_{1}}\subset\Omega
.
Then
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})
.
By assumption there exists
M_{1}>0
such that

|\langle{\cal A}u,v\rangle|\leq M_{1}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
for all
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{1}}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}v}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{1}}c_{k}(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{v}+\int_{\Omega_{1}}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{v}\Big|

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}\,\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

for all
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
.
Since
u|_{\Omega_{1}}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
it follows that (
16
)
is valid for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1})
and in particular for

.
Therefore

\Big|\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}c_{k}(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{u_{n}}+\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{u_{n}}\Big|\leq M_{1}\,\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Note that

\|u_{n}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}.

Secondly

\partial_{k}u_{n}=\Big((\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u-2(\mathop{\rm Re}u)(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi^{2}\,\partial_{k}\chi_{n}+2(\mathop{\rm Re}u)(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi\,\partial_{k}\chi\Big)\,\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}u_{n}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+2\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\,(\|\partial_{k}\chi_{n}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{k}\chi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)})

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 3\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}^{2}+12(1+\|\nabla\chi\|_{2}^{2})\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}^{2}

and

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}+4(1+\|\nabla\chi\|_{2})\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}.

We wish to estimate
\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}^{2}
.

Clearly

\displaystyle M_{1}\,\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}c_{k}(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{u_{n}}+\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{u_{n}}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}u)\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}+\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}c_{k}(\partial_{k}u)\,\overline{u_{n}}+\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\overline{u_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}-2\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi^{2}\,\partial_{k}\chi_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+2\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi\,\partial_{k}\chi

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\,u\,\Big((\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,\mathop{\rm Re}u-\delta\,\mathds{1}\Big)^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}+I_{6}\geq I_{1}-|I_{2}|-|I_{3}|-|I_{4}|-|I_{5}|-|I_{6}|.

We estimate the six terms separately.

Ellipticity gives

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|-2\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi^{2}\,\partial_{k}\chi_{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{2}\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}\,\|\nabla\chi_{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{2}\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2},

where
M_{2}=2\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\|a_{kl}\|_{\infty}
.
Similarly

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|2\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,a_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi\,\partial_{k}\chi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{2}\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla\chi\|_{2}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}

and

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}b_{k}\,u\,\overline{\partial_{k}u_{n}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{3}\,\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,\Big(\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}+2(1+\|\nabla\chi\|_{2})\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{3}\,\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}+2M_{3}\,(1+\|\nabla\chi\|_{2})\,\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)},

for all

\displaystyle|I_{5}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}c_{k}\,(\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u)\,\Big((\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\,\mathop{\rm Re}u-\delta\,\mathds{1}\Big)^{+}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}|c_{k}|\,|\partial_{k}\mathop{\rm Re}u|\,\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,\Big((\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})^{2}\,\chi^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}u-\delta\,\mathds{1}\Big)^{+}

\displaystyle\mu\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle M_{5}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}+M_{6}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\,\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\,M_{5}^{2}+M_{6},

M_{5}=2M_{1}+M_{2}\,(1+\|\nabla\chi\|_{2})\,\|u\|_{C_{b}(\Omega)}+(M_{3}+M_{4})\,\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\|\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u\|_{2}^{2}\leq M_{7},

(17)

First note that

|(\mathds{1}_{[\chi^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}u-\delta>0]}\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u)(x)|^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}|(\mathds{1}_{[u_{n}>0]}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u)(x)|^{2}

for almost every
x\in\Omega

(\chi^{2}\,\mathop{\rm Re}u)(x)-\delta\leq 0

18

18

\int_{\Omega}|\mathds{1}_{[\chi^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}u-\delta>0]}\,\chi\,\nabla\mathop{\rm Re}u|^{2}\leq M_{7}.

Alternatively, returning to the estimate (

We emphasise that this bound is uniform in
\delta
and

The sequence

Passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists a

4
Approximation with
C(\overline{\Omega})
-functions

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
.
In particular, we show that for all
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
the Perron solution

T\varphi
is an element of
H^{1}(\Omega)
if and only if
\varphi

has an extension in
C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
.
The main tool is the next theorem, which is even valid for
H^{1}_{\rm loc}
-functions
instead of
H^{1}
-functions.

Throughout this section we adopt the notation and assumptions as in
the first part of the introduction.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and
u\in C_{b}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

be such that
{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and

\lim_{x\to z}u(x)=\varphi(z)
for quasi every
z\in\Gamma
.
Then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

such that
\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
,

u-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\|u-\Phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon
.

The proof requires a delicate induction procedure.
It relies on the next lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and
w\in C_{b}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

be both real valued.
Let
z_{0}\in\Gamma
and
r_{0},\beta,\delta>0
.
Suppose that

\displaystyle{\cal A}w\in H^{-1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to z}w(x)=\varphi(z)\quad\mbox{for quasi every }z\in\Gamma,\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle|\varphi(z)-\varphi(z_{0})|\leq\beta\quad\mbox{for all }z\in\Gamma\cap B(z_{0},4r_{0}).

one has

T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

The property
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is independent of the
elliptic operator.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

T^{-\Delta}\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.

Condition
(ii)
in Corollary
4.4
is independent of the operator.
∎

One might wonder whether the property
{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
for a
function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
depends on the operator.
We show in Example
4.7
that this is the case, in general.
Nevertheless, one has the following characterisation for
{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Suppose that
\lim_{x\to z}u(x)=\varphi(z)
for quasi every
z\in\Gamma
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

u-T\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(iii)

For all
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with

\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
and
{\cal A}\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega)

one has
u-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
’.
Let
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and suppose that

\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
and
{\cal A}\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Then
{\cal A}(u-\Phi)\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and

\lim_{x\to z}(u-\Phi)(x)=0
for quasi every
z\in\Gamma
.
Hence Corollary
3.11
implies that
u-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

‘
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
By Theorem
1.3
there exists a

\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
such that

\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
and
{\cal A}\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Note that
T\varphi
satisfies the same assumptions as the function
u

by Corollary
3.12
.
Then the implication
(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
with

u
replaced by
T\varphi
gives
T\varphi-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Consequently
u-T\varphi=(u-\Phi)-(T\varphi-\Phi)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
Since
u=(u-T\varphi)+T\varphi
one deduces that

{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
∎

We give an example of an open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{2}
,
an elliptic operator and a function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

such that
\Delta u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, but
{\cal A}u\not\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.

Example 4.7
.

For all
n\in\mathds{N}
define
I_{n}=(2^{-n},2^{-n}+2\cdot 4^{-n})
.
Then the sets
\overline{I_{n}}
are
pairwise disjoint.
Set
\Omega_{1}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}
.
Define
w\colon\overline{\Omega_{1}}\to\mathds{C}
by

w(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }x=0,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{n}\,4^{n}(x-2^{-n})&\mbox{if }n\in\mathds{N}\mbox{ and }x\in\overline{I_{n}}.\end{array}\right.

Then
w\in C(\overline{\Omega_{1}})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{1})

and
\Delta_{1}w=0
, where
\Delta_{1}
is the Laplacian on
\Omega_{1}
.
Next for all
n\in\mathds{N}
let
v_{n}\colon I_{n}\to\mathds{C}
be the tent on
I_{n}

with height
4^{-n}
.
So

v_{n}(x)=4^{-n}-|x-(2^{-n}+4^{-n})|.

Then
v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(I_{n})
and

\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(I_{n})}^{2}=\frac{2}{3}\cdot 4^{-3n}+2\cdot 4^{-n}
.
Hence

v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{1}).

Next define
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}\subset\mathds{R}^{2}
.
Then
\Omega
is open and bounded.
Consider
u=w\otimes w\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
Then
\Delta u=0\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, where
\Delta
is the Laplacian on
\Omega
.
In order to define the coefficients of the operator
{\cal A}
it is
convenient to split the interval
I_{n}
in the left part

I_{n}^{l}=(2^{-n},2^{-n}+4^{-n}]
and right part

I_{n}^{r}=(2^{-n}+4^{-n},2^{-n}+2\cdot 4^{-n})
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Define
\alpha\colon\Omega_{1}\to[1,2]
by

\alpha(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\mbox{if }n\in\mathds{N}\mbox{ and }x\in I_{n}^{l},\\[5.0pt]
1&\mbox{if }n\in\mathds{N}\mbox{ and }x\in I_{n}^{r}.\end{array}\right.

For all
k,l\in\{1,2\}
define
a_{kl}\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}
by

a_{kl}=(\alpha\otimes\alpha)\,\delta_{kl}
.
Moreover, set
b_{k}=c_{k}=0
and
c_{0}=0
, and let
{\cal A}
be the
corresponding elliptic operator.

Now suppose that
{\cal A}u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Then there is an
M>0
such that

|\langle{\cal A}u,\tau\rangle_{{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times{\cal D}(\Omega)}|\leq M\,\|\tau\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for all
\tau\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Let
N\in\mathds{N}
.
For all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
let
f_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I_{n})
.
Then
f=\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
and

\displaystyle 2\Big|\Big(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\alpha\,w^{\prime}\,\overline{f^{\prime}}\Big)\Big(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\alpha\,w\,\overline{f}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{k,l=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{kl}\,(\partial_{k}(w\otimes w))\,\overline{\partial_{l}(f\otimes f)}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle{\cal A}u,f\otimes f\rangle_{{\cal D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times{\cal D}(\Omega)}|\leq M\,\|f\otimes f\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Hence by density, approximating
v_{n}
by elements in
C_{c}^{\infty}(I_{n})
,
one deduces that

2\Big|\Big(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha\,w^{\prime}\,\overline{v_{n}^{\prime}}\Big)\Big(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha\,w\,\overline{v_{n}}\Big)\Big|\leq M\,\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}\Big)\otimes\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}\Big)\Big\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq M\,\|v\otimes v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

But

\int_{I_{n}}\alpha\,w^{\prime}\,v_{n}^{\prime}=\int_{I_{n}^{l}}2\cdot{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}\,4^{n}\cdot 1+\int_{I_{n}^{r}}1\cdot{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}\,4^{n}\cdot(-1)=\frac{1}{n}

and
\int_{I_{n}}\alpha\,w\,v_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Therefore

2\Big(\int_{I_{1}}\alpha\,w\,v_{1}\Big)\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\leq M\,\|v\otimes v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<\infty

for all
N\in\mathds{N}
.
This is a contradiction.

5
Dirichlet problem with the approximative trace

Adopt the notation and assumptions as in the first part of the introduction.
In addition we provide
\Gamma
with the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure,
denoted by
\sigma
.
Throughout this section we assume that
\sigma(\Gamma)<\infty
.
Instead of data in
C(\Gamma)
we wish to consider data in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.

We recall from the introduction the notion of a trace.
Given
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma)
we say that

\varphi
is an
approximative trace
, or simply
\varphi
is a
trace

of
u
, or
u
has
trace

\varphi
, if there are

u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\lim u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\lim u_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.
If
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then the set

\{\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma):\varphi\mbox{ is a trace of }u\}

of all traces of
u
may be empty, and also it may contain two and hence infinitely many elements.
If
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, then for each

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
there is a unique
\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma)

such that
\varphi
is a trace of
u
.
Of course, we are mainly interested in more general domains.
Set

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)=\{\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma):\mbox{there exists a $u\in H^{1}(\Omega)$ such that
$\varphi$ is a trace of $u$}\}.

Recall from Lemma
2.1
(b)
that

H^{1}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\oplus{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega).

Hence for all
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
there are
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

u\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
w=v+u
.
Consequently, for all
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
there exists a
u\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)

such that
\varphi
is a trace of
u
.
We call
u
a
variational solution of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.
In general there are several such variational solutions.

We next consider uniqueness of the variational solution.
We introduce

V(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):0\mbox{ is a trace of }u\},

the space of all
H^{1}(\Omega)
functions with vanishing approximative trace.
Clearly
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\subset V(\Omega)
.

Remark 5.1
.

By
[
Sau1
]
Corollary 7.39 one has

V(\Omega)\subset\{u|_{\Omega}:u\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})\mbox{ and }u=0\mbox{ a.e.\ on }\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\}.

Consequently
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is stable for the
Dirichlet problem, see
[
AD2
]
Theorem 4.7.
In particular, this is the case if
\Omega
has a continuous boundary
in the sense of graphs, see
[
AD2
]
Proposition 2.2.

The characterisation for the uniqueness of the variational solution
is as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

For all
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
there is a unique variational solution
of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.

(ii)

V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(iii)

V(\Omega)\cap{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)=\{0\}
.

Proof
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
Suppose
(i)
.
Let
w\in V(\Omega)
.
Using Lemma
2.1
(b)
we can write
w=v+u

with
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u\in{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)
.
Then
0
is a trace of
u
and
u
is
{\cal A}
-harmonic.
Clearly also
0\in L_{2}(\Gamma)
is a trace of
0\in H^{1}(\Omega)

and
0
is
{\cal A}
-harmonic.
Since the variational solution is unique, one deduces that
u=0
.
Then
w=v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
’.

V(\Omega)\cap{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)=\{0\}
.

‘
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
Let
\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma)
and
u_{1},u_{2}\in{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)

be such that
\varphi
is a trace of both
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
.
Then
0
is a trace of
u_{1}-u_{2}
.
So
u_{1}-u_{2}\in V(\Omega)
and
u_{1}-u_{2}\in{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)
.
So
u_{1}-u_{2}\in V(\Omega)\cap{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)=\{0\}
and
u_{1}=u_{2}
.
∎

It follows from Daners
[
Dan1
]
Proposition 3.3
that there exists a measurable set
\Gamma_{s}\subset\Gamma
such that

\{\varphi\in L_{2}(\Gamma):\varphi\mbox{ is a trace of }0\}=L_{2}(\Gamma_{s}).

For an elementary proof, see
[
AE1
]
Lemma 4.14.
We define
\Gamma_{r}=\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{s}
.
We say that
the trace on
\Omega
is unique
if every
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)

has at most one (approximative) trace.
So the trace on
\Omega
is unique if and only if
\sigma(\Gamma_{s})=0
.
The trace is unique if
\Omega
has a continuous boundary (in the sense of graphs).
If
d=2
then it suffices that
\Omega
is connected.
See
[
Sau2
]
for these two and other results on the trace.

We next give an example of a bounded connected open set
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{2}
that is
Wiener regular, on which the trace is unique, but where uniqueness of
a variational solution fails.
We even construct an (
{\cal A}
-harmonic) function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)\cap V(\Omega)

such that
u\not\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Hence for every
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
and
u_{0}\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)

such that
\varphi
is a trace of
u_{0}
, we obtain that also
u_{0}+c\,u\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)

and
\varphi
is a trace of
u_{0}+c\,u
for all
c\in\mathds{C}
.
In particular, for each
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)

there are infinitely many variational solutions
of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
{\cal C}\subset\mathds{R}
be the usual middle third Cantor set and set
S={\cal C}\times\{0\}
.
Define
\Omega=B(0,2)\setminus S
.
Clearly
S
is compact, so
\Omega
is open and bounded in
\mathds{R}^{2}
.
It is not hard to see that
\Omega
is also connected.
Obviously
\sigma(S)=0
.

We recall that if
E\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
and
a\in\mathds{R}^{d}
, then the set
E
is
called
thin at
a
if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{\rm cap}(E\cap B(a,r))}{r^{d-2}}\,\frac{dr}{r}<\infty.

If
U\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
is open, then a point
a\in\partial U
is
a regular point for
U
if and only if
U^{\rm c}
is not thin at
a

by the theorem on page 143 in
[
Wie2
]
.
It follows from
[
Tsu
]
Theorem 1 that every element of
S
is a
regular point for
\Omega
.
Hence every element of
\Gamma
is a regular point for
\Omega
and
\Omega
is
Wiener regular.
In particular
\mathop{\rm cap}(B(a,r)\setminus\Omega)>0
for all
a\in S
and
r\in(0,1)
.
Since
B(a,r)\setminus\Omega=B(a,r)\cap S
one establishes that
\mathop{\rm cap}S>0
.

Define
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
by

\varphi(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }z\in S,\\[5.0pt]
0&\mbox{if }z\in\partial B(0,2).\end{array}\right.

Since
\Omega
is Wiener regular, there exists a unique classical solution
of the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\varphi
.
Let
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
be the unique
{\cal A}
-harmonic function
such that
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
.
So
u|_{\Omega}=T\varphi
.
We next show that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Let
w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,2))
be such that
w|_{B(0,1)}=\mathds{1}
.
Then
\varphi=w|_{\Gamma}

and
T\varphi=T(w|_{\Gamma})\in H^{1}(\Omega)
by Theorem
1.1
.
Therefore
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, so by definition

u|_{\Gamma}
is a trace of
u
.
Since
\sigma(S)=0
one deduces that
u|_{\Gamma}=\varphi=0

\sigma
-almost everywhere.
So
0
is a trace of
u
and
u\in V(\Omega)
.
Because
\mathop{\rm cap}S>0
, the function
u
does not vanish quasi-everywhere
on
\Gamma
and hence
u\not\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
So in this example
V(\Omega)\neq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Note that
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)\cap V(\Omega)
,
but
u\not\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u\not\in C_{0}(\Omega)
.

Note that
\|T\varphi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}>0

and
\|\varphi\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}=0
.
Hence there does not exist a
c>0
such that

\|T\psi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|\psi\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}
for all

\psi\in C(\Gamma)
.
In particular, the map
T\colon C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)

does not extend to a bounded linear map from
L_{2}(\Gamma)
into
L_{2}(\Omega)
.

The example shows that the classical solution (and in particular
the Perron solution) is a much finer notion than the
variational solution for a given continuous approximative trace.
In the example one has
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
and
the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
has a unique classical solution
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.
But since
\varphi=0

\sigma
-a.e. on
\Gamma
, the zero-function
is another variational solution.
The problem does not occur if
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Suppose
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, that is for all
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
the variational solution
of the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
is unique.
Define the map
\gamma\colon{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)\to{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
by

\gamma(\varphi)=u,

where
u\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
is the unique element
with trace
\varphi
.

We next wish to prove that
\gamma
maps bounded functions in
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)

into bounded functions.
We need a lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Gamma)
.
Then there exist
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

and
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\lim u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\lim u_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
and

\|u_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Proof
.

First suppose that
\varphi
is real valued.
There are
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

and
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\lim u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\lim u_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.
Taking the real part we may assume that
u_{n}
and
u
are
real valued for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Define
v_{n}=(-\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)})\vee u_{n}\wedge\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then
v_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Moreover,

\lim v_{n}=(-\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)})\vee u\wedge\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)

and
\lim v_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.
Clearly
\|v_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

If
\varphi
is general, then write
\varphi=\mathop{\rm Re}\varphi+i\mathop{\rm Im}\varphi

and apply the above to
\mathop{\rm Re}\varphi
and
\mathop{\rm Im}\varphi
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Gamma)
.
Then
\gamma(\varphi)\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
and

\|\gamma(\varphi)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2c\,\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
,
where
c=\|T\|_{C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)}
.

Proof
.

By Lemma
5.4
there are
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

and
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\lim w_{n}=w
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\lim w_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
and

\|w_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
For all
n\in\mathds{N}
write
w_{n}=v_{n}+u_{n}
and
w=v+u
,
where
v_{n},v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{n},u\in{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Since
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
{\cal H}_{\cal A}^{1}(\Omega)
are closed in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
it follows from the closed graph theorem that
\lim u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

We know that
\gamma(\varphi)=u
.
Then
u_{n}=T(w_{n}|_{\Gamma})
for all
w\in\mathds{N}
by Theorem
1.1
.
So

\|u_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|w_{n}|_{\Gamma}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 2c\,\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Now
\lim u_{n}=u
in
H^{1}(\Omega)

and hence also in
L_{2}(\Omega)
.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary we may assume that

\lim u_{n}=u
almost everywhere.
Hence

\|\gamma(\varphi)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2c\,\|\varphi\|_{L_{\infty}(\Gamma)}
as required.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then
T\varphi=\gamma(\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.

Clearly
T(\Phi|_{\Gamma})=\gamma(\Phi|_{\Gamma})
for all
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

by Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.
By Stone–Weierstraß there exist
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2},\ldots\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\lim\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
C(\Gamma)
.
Then
T\varphi=\lim T(\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma})
in
C_{b}(\Omega)
and in particular pointwise
on
\Omega
.
If
n\in\mathds{N}
, then

\|\gamma(\varphi)-\gamma(\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma})\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2c\,\|\varphi-\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma}\|_{C(\Gamma)}

by Proposition
5.5
, where
c=\|T\|_{C(\Gamma)\to C_{b}(\Omega)}
.
Hence
\lim\gamma(\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma})=\gamma(\varphi)
almost everywhere
on
\Omega
.
So
\lim T(\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma})=\gamma(\varphi)
almost everywhere
on
\Omega
.
Therefore
T\varphi=\gamma(\varphi)
almost everywhere.
∎

One would expect that if
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and

T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
then
\varphi
is a trace of
T\varphi
.
The proof relies on Theorem
4.1
, whose proof
was quite involved and depended on capacity methods.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Suppose that
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then
\varphi
is a trace of
T\varphi
.

Proof
.

It follows from Corollary
3.12
and the choice
u=T\varphi
in Theorem
4.1

that for all
n\in\mathds{N}
there exists a
\Phi_{n}\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

such that
\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
,
T\varphi-\Phi_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

and in addition
\|T\varphi-\Phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Since
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
also
\Phi_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
So
\Phi_{n}\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Hence
\lim\Phi_{n}=T\varphi
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
obviously
\lim\Phi_{n}|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\Gamma)
.
Therefore
\varphi
is a trace of
T\varphi
.
∎

Theorems
5.6
and
5.7

of those
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
for which the Perron solution
T\varphi
is
an element of

.

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose that
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
It follows from Theorem
5.7
that
\varphi
is a trace of
T\varphi
.
Hence
\varphi\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
By assumption
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.
Hence
T\varphi=\gamma(\varphi)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
by Theorem
5.6
.
∎

The next example shows that the condition
V(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in Theorem
5.8

cannot be omitted.

Example 5.9
.

Let
\Omega
be the Wiener regular bounded open set as in Example
5.3
.
We shall show that there exists a
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)

such that
\varphi|_{\partial B(0,2)}=0
and
T\varphi\not\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence
\varphi=0
almost everywhere on
\Gamma
,
so
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

Set
d_{0}=\frac{\log 2}{\log 3}
.
Recall that
S={\cal C}\times\{0\}
.
Then
S
is a
d_{0}
-set in the sense of Jonsson–Wallin by

[
JW
]
Example II.2.
Fix
s\in(0,\frac{d_{0}}{2})
.
Since
s<1\wedge d_{0}
it follows from

[
CC
]
Theorem 3.2 that there exists a Weierstraß-type function
\psi\in C(S)
such that

\dim_{H}G(\psi)=d_{0}+1-s
,
where
\dim_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension and
G(\psi)
is the graph of
\psi
.
Define
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
by

\varphi(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi(z)&\mbox{if }z\in S,\\[5.0pt]
0&\mbox{if }z\in\partial B(0,2).\end{array}\right.

Since
\Omega
is Wiener regular, it follows from Corollary
3.4

that the Dirichlet problem
(D_{\varphi})
has a (unique) classical
solution
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
Then
T\varphi=u|_{\Omega}

and obviously
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)\cap{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\Omega)
.

Now suppose that
T\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}(B(0,2))

by
[
BW2
]
Proposition 3.6, since
\sigma(S)<\infty
.
Because every element in
H^{1}(B(0,2))
can be extended
to an element of
H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{2})=B^{22}_{1}(\mathds{R}^{2})
, a Besov space,
it follows from
[
JW
]
Theorem VII.1 that

\psi=u|_{S}\in B^{22}_{d_{0}/2}(S)
.
Then
[
CC
]
Theorem 3.3 implies that

\dim_{H}G(\psi)\leq d_{0}+1-\frac{d_{0}}{2}
.
But
\dim_{H}G(\psi)=d_{0}+1-s
.
So
\frac{d_{0}}{2}\leq s
, which is a contradiction.
Hence
T\varphi\not\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

If
\Omega
is Wiener regular, then it follows from
Biegert–Warma
[
BW1
]
Theorem 3.2(c)
\Rightarrow
(b) and the
Wiener condition (
12
) that
each
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
vanishes
(pointwise) on
\Gamma
.
This is no longer true if
u\in V(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
.
In fact, in Example
5.3
the set
\Omega
is Wiener regular,
but nevertheless there exists a
u\in V(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})

such that
u|_{\Gamma}\neq 0
in
C(\Gamma)
.
Next we show that any such
u
vanishes
\sigma
-a.e. on
\Gamma
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
u\in V(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
.
Then
u|_{\Gamma}=0

\sigma
-a.e.

Proof
.

Write
\varphi=u|_{\Gamma}\in C(\Gamma)
.
Recall that
\Gamma_{r}
and
\Gamma_{s}
are Borel measurable subsets of
\Gamma
.
Then
\varphi|_{\Gamma_{r}}=0

\sigma
-a.e. by construction/definition.
Define

Z=\{z\in\Gamma_{s}:\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega\cap B(z,r)|}{r^{d}}>0\}.

Then
\sigma(Z)=0
by
[
Sau2
]
Theorem 4.15.
We shall show that
\varphi(z)=0
for all
z\in\Gamma_{s}\setminus Z
.

Let
z\in\Gamma_{s}\setminus Z
and let
\delta>0
.
We first prove that
\sigma(\Gamma_{r}\cap B(z,\delta))>0
.
Indeed, suppose that
\sigma(\Gamma_{r}\cap B(z,\delta))=0
.
By
[
AFP
]
(3.43) there exists a
\gamma>0

such that

\Big(|\Omega\cap B(z,\delta)|\wedge|\Omega^{\rm c}\cap B(z,\delta)|\Big)^{(d-1)/d}\leq\gamma\,P(\Omega,B(z,\delta)),

(25)

where
P(\Omega,B(z,\delta))
denotes the perimeter of
\Omega
in
B(z,\delta)
.
Now

P(\Omega,B(z,\delta))=\sigma((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap B(z,\delta))

by
[
Fed
]
Theorem 4.5.11 and
[
AFP
]
(3.62), where

\partial^{*}\Omega
is the measure theoretic boundary of
\Omega
.
Note that

\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega=\{p\in\mathds{R}^{d}:\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega\cap B(p,r)|}{r^{d}}=0\mbox{ or}\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega^{\rm c}\cap B(p,r)|}{r^{d}}=0\}.

Clearly
\partial^{*}\Omega\subset\Gamma
.
Moreover,
\Gamma_{s}\setminus Z\subset\Gamma\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega
.
Hence
\partial^{*}\Omega\subset\Gamma_{r}\cup Z
.
Then

P(\Omega,B(z,\delta))=\sigma((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap B(z,\delta))\leq\sigma(\Gamma_{r}\cap B(z,\delta))+\sigma(Z)=0.

On the other hand,
|\Omega\cap B(z,\delta)|>0
since
z\in\partial\Omega

and
\Omega
is open.
Also
\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega\cap B(z,r)|}{r^{d}}=0

since
z\in\Gamma_{s}\setminus Z
.
Then
\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega\cap B(z,r)|}{r^{d}}=0

and consequently

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|\Omega^{\rm c}\cap B(z,r)|}{|B(z,r)|}=1
.
So
|\Omega^{\rm c}\cap B(z,\delta)|>0
.
This contradicts (
25
).

Let
z\in\Gamma_{s}\setminus Z
.
We proved that
\sigma(\Gamma_{r}\cap B(z,\delta))>0

for all
\delta>0
.
Since
\varphi|_{\Gamma_{r}}=0

\sigma
-a.e. and
\varphi
is continuous,
one deduces that
\varphi(z)=0
.
∎

We conclude with a sufficient condition which implies
for a Perron solution to vanish.

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose that
\sigma(\Gamma\cap B(z,\delta))>0
for all
z\in\Gamma

and
\delta>0
.
Let
\varphi\in C(\Gamma)
and suppose that
T\varphi\in V(\Omega)
.
Then
\varphi=0
and
T\varphi=0
.

Proof
.

It follows from Corollary
3.12
and Theorem
4.1

that there exists a
\Phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)

such that
\Phi|_{\Gamma}=\varphi
and
T\varphi-\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then
\Phi\in V(\Omega)
.
By Proposition
5.10
there exists a measurable set

N\subset\Gamma
such that
\sigma(N)=0
and

\Phi(z)=0
for all
z\in\Gamma\setminus N
.
Let
z\in\Gamma
.
If
n\in\mathds{N}
, then

\sigma((\Gamma\setminus N)\cap B(z,\frac{1}{n}))>0
, hence there
exists a
z_{n}\in(\Gamma\setminus N)\cap B(z,\frac{1}{n})
.
Then
\varphi(z)=\lim\varphi(z_{n})=0
.
∎

By the next lemma, the first condition in the previous proposition is satisfied if

is regular in topology, that is if
\overline{\Omega}\raisebox{6.10834pt}{\makebox[0.0pt]{\hskip-4.70001pt$\scriptstyle\circ$}}=\Omega

is

Lemma 5.12
.

Suppose
\Omega
is regular in topology.
Then
\sigma(\Gamma\cap B(z,\delta))>0
for all
z\in\Gamma
and
\delta>0
.

Proof
.

Let
z\in\Gamma
and
\delta>0
.
Then
|\Omega\cap B(z,\delta)|>0

|\Omega^{c}\cap B(z,\delta)|>0
.
Hence by (

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

0<P(\Omega,B(z,\delta))=\sigma((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap B(z,\delta))\leq\sigma(\Gamma\cap B(z,\delta))

as required.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

If
\Omega
is regular in topology, then

C(\overline{\Omega})\cap{\cal H}^{1}_{\cal A}(\Omega)\cap V(\Omega)=\{0\}.

Proof

2.16
.

5.12

.

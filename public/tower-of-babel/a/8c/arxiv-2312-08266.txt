Spin relaxation rate for baryons in thermal pion gas Preprint: KEK-TH-2565, J-PARC-TH-0294
Yoshimasa Hidaka, Masaru Hongo, Mikhail A. Stephanov, Ho-Ung Yee

Abstract
We study the relaxation dynamics of the spin polarization of baryons (nucleon and Λ \Lambda -baryon), in a thermal pion gas as a simple model of the hadronic phase of the QCD plasma produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions. For this purpose, we formulate the quantum kinetic theory for the spin density matrix of baryons in the leading order of the gradient expansion. Considering the baryon-pion elastic scattering processes as the dominant interaction between baryons and thermal pions, we compute the spin relaxation rate of nucleons and Λ \Lambda -baryons in a pion gas up to temperature 200 MeV. In the case of nucleons, we evaluate the spin relaxation rate in the s s -channel resonance approximation, based on the known experimental data on Δ \Delta -resonances. We also estimate the spin relaxation rate for Λ \Lambda -baryons, based on experimental inputs and theoretical models for the low-energy Λ \Lambda π \pi scattering, including the chiral perturbation theory.

Obviously, the key quantity in such out-of-equilibrium time-evolution is the relaxation rate of spin polarization toward its equilibrium value.
The spin polarization in general is not a conserved quantity, and thus, gives one of the non-hydrodynamic modes not amenable to a hydrodynamics description.
In the specific case where spin polarization shows much slower relaxation than other non-hydrodynamic modes, a quasi-hydrodynamics or Hydro+ description

The spin hydrodynamics, in this case, is characterized by a new transport coefficient, called rotational viscosity, which corresponds to the spin relaxation rate
\gamma_{S}

In Ref.

\alpha_{s}

It behaves as
\gamma_{S}\sim\alpha_{s}^{2}\log(1/\alpha_{s})\times({T^{3}/M_{q}^{2}})

M_{q}(\gg T)

T

The QCD plasma in the hadronic phase at low temperature and vanishing baryon chemical potential is mostly a gas of weakly interacting pions, described by chiral perturbation theory

;

;

The chiral perturbation theory, however, becomes inadequate around

f

As a simple model for the hadronic phase in this work, we will therefore approximate the plasma by a thermal pion gas without heavy use of the chiral perturbation theory.
We note that our approach works well for a low-temperature phase, while it eventually breaks down as the temperature becomes closer to the crossover temperature
T_{c}\sim 200
MeV from the hadronic to quark-gluon plasma phases.

The baryon density in this temperature range is dilute, and thus, we will neglect inter-baryon interactions in the plasma.
In other words, we treat each baryon independent of other constituents of the plasma, except thermal pions surrounding it.
The dominant baryon-pion interaction in this regime is the elastic two-body scattering of the baryon with background pions
Leutwyler and Smilga 1990
.
This motivates us to formulate the quantum kinetic theory of baryons interacting with the surrounding pions.
Based on this picture, we will construct the leading-order collision operator in the quantum kinetic theory of the spin density matrix, which allows us to compute the spin relaxation rate from the spin-flipping scattering processes.

Let us briefly summarize our picture of the relevant degrees of freedom and processes in the following.
At a sufficiently low temperature, the baryon-pion scattering amplitude is describable in the chiral perturbation theory.
Indeed, there exists a well-developed chiral perturbation theory including nucleons (see, e.g., Ref.
Stefan Scherer 2011

Above

The nucleon-pion scattering amplitudes in this energy range have been experimentally measured with a good precision a while ago—indeed, this was how the
\Delta
-baryon resonance was discovered.
Thus, instead of relying on the chiral perturbation theory, we can directly utilize these experimental data to evaluate the spin-flipping amplitudes that are needed to compute the spin relaxation rate.

On the other hand, experimental data on the

We will thus resort to a reasonable modeling, taking into account the known hadron spectrum including nearby strange baryons as well as the
s

Specifically, we consider two most relevant strange baryons, i.e.,

The former should be considered as a bound state of

Fortunately for us, the width of

II.1
Spin density matrix for baryons

Let us consider a baryon in the low-temperature pion gas.
In the limit where the baryon density is dilute, we can neglect baryon-baryon interactions compared to the leading-order baryon-pion interactions.
In this case, we can introduce a notion of the density matrix operator
\hat{\rho}
for one baryon defined in the Hilbert space of the single baryon state.
Expanding this baryon density operator in the basis of momentum and spin eigenstates
\{|\bm{p},s\rangle\}
with
s=\pm 1/2
, we can, in general, express
\hat{\rho}
as

\hat{\rho}=\int_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}|\bm{p}_{1},s_{1}\rangle\rho({\bm{p}_{1}},s_{1};{\bm{p}_{2}},s_{2})\langle{\bm{p}_{2}},s_{2}|,

(1)

in terms of a function
\rho({\bm{p}_{1}},s_{1};{\bm{p}_{2}},s_{2})
.
Here, we introduced a shorthand notation for the momentum integral as

\int_{\bm{p}_{1},\cdots\bm{p}_{n}}\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdots\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p_{n}}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

(2)

Equivalently, we can represent the same information in the mixed position-momentum basis by performing a Wigner transform as

\rho(\bm{x},\bm{p})\equiv\int_{\bm{p}_{r}}\rho(\bm{p}+{\bm{p}}_{r}/2,\bm{p}-{\bm{p}}_{r}/2)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{p}_{r}\cdot\bm{x}},

(3)

where we have omitted the spin variables for simplicity.

Since we consider the single baryon dynamics in the approximately uniform pion gas,
\bm{x}
-dependence of the Wigner transformed density matrix
\rho(\bm{x},\bm{p})
is assumed to be smooth enough.
Then, the baryon-pion interactions, which give rise to spin relaxation of baryons, happen at a length scale that is much shorter than the scale of variation in
\bm{x}
of the density matrix.
As a result, we can neglect
\bm{x}
-dependence in
\rho(\bm{x},\bm{p})
in the leading order of the gradient expansion.
This amounts to assuming that the density matrix is approximately diagonal in the momentum space

\rho({\bm{p}_{1}},s_{1};{\bm{p}_{2}},s_{2})\simeq\rho(\bm{p}_{1};s_{1},s_{2})(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{p}_{2}).

(4)

We here note that the
2\times 2
spin density matrix

\rho(\bm{p};s,s^{\prime})\equiv[\rho^{2\times 2}(\bm{p})]_{s,s^{\prime}}

serves as the distribution function in momentum space with matrix indices
s
and
s^{\prime}
.
When it is summed over the two spin states, the result should be identified as the probability distribution function in the ordinary kinetic theory
Lifshitz and Pitaevskii 1981
as
n(\bm{p})=\sum_{s}\rho(\bm{p};s,s)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{2\times 2}(\bm{p})\right)
.
Similarly, one can obtain the spin distribution in momentum space by computing the expectation value of the spin operator given by
\bm{S}(\bm{p})=({\hbar/2})\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{\sigma}\rho^{2\times 2}(\bm{p})\right)
, where
\bm{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices.

1

1

In this definition of the spin distribution we write
\hbar
explicitly.
We will, however, use the unit system with
\hbar=1
in the reminder of the paper.

Suppose that the baryon is put into a thermal bath of pion gas.
Neglecting other baryons and mesons, we introduce the total density operator at the initial time as

\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\hat{\rho}\otimes\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}
.
Here,
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}
denotes the equilibrium density operator of the thermal pion gas.
The time evolution of the total density matrix is given by

\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}(t+\Delta t)=\hat{U}(\Delta t)\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)
, where
\hat{U}(\Delta t)
is the unitary time-evolution operator generated by the total Hamiltonian, including interactions between baryons and pions.

We now perform the average over the pions to obtain the effective time-evolution equation for the baryon density matrix
\hat{\rho}
.
The initial state for the pions is given by the thermal density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}
.
We assume that the final states of the pions after interactions with the baryon do not matter for the time evolution of the baryon, due to fast thermal scrambling of the pions.
Therefore, we take a sum over all possible final states of pions after interactions.
Let us denote this thermal average and the summation over final states by a symbol

\langle\cdots\rangle_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}
.

To see what the above procedure means, we now evaluate

\mathrm{Tr}_{\pi}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}(t+\Delta t)=\langle\hat{U}(\Delta t)\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)\rangle_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi},

(5)

after expanding
\hat{U}(\Delta t)
and
\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)
in power series of the pion operators
\hat{\pi}(x)
.
Here,
\mathrm{Tr}_{\pi}
denotes the trace over all possible final states of the pion in the background.
Then, we are led to compute the thermal average in the pion sector

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\pi}\left(\hat{\pi}(x_{1})\hat{\pi}(x_{2})\cdots\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}\hat{\pi}(y_{1})\hat{\pi}(y_{2})\cdots\right),

(6)

where
\hat{\pi}(x_{i})
are the pion operator from
\hat{U}(\Delta t)
, and
\hat{\pi}(y_{i})
are those from
\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)
.
One can find that this is equivalent to the correlation functions in the Schwinger-Keldysh contour
Schwinger 1961
;
Keldysh 1964
with an initial thermal density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\pi}\left(\hat{\pi}(x_{1})\hat{\pi}(x_{2})\cdots\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\pi}\hat{\pi}(y_{1})\hat{\pi}(y_{2})\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\pi_{1}(x_{1})\pi_{1}(x_{2})\cdots\pi_{2}(y_{1})\pi_{2}(y_{2})\cdots\right\rangle_{\mathrm{SK}},

(7)

where
\pi_{1,2}(x)
are the pion fields placed in the forward or backward time contours, respectively.
In other words, we can consider the pion operators appearing in
\hat{U}(\Delta t)
and
\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)
as the fields in the forward and backward Schwinger-Keldysh contours, respectively, and use their thermal correlation functions in the Schwinger-Keldysh contour
Li and Yee 2019
.

II.2
Dynamics of the nucleon spin in a pion gas

The leading order interaction that we consider in this work is the two-body baryon-pion scattering shown in Fig.
1
.
For nucleons, it has been known that this is the dominant process of nucleon-pion interactions for temperatures up to
150
MeV
Leutwyler and Smilga 1990
.
In the chiral perturbation theory, there are tree-level diagrams composed of
NN\pi
and
NN\pi\pi
vertices, but the chiral perturbation theory is no longer justified for temperatures above
100
MeV.
In addition, the contribution from the
s
-channel
\Delta
-baryon resonance is significant for this temperature range.
Many state-of-the-art models of nucleon-pion scattering
Jenkins and Manohar 1991
;
Hemmert et al. 1998
;
Fettes and Meissner 2001
include higher baryon resonances, as well as the loop corrections.
The purpose of these models is to use the existing experimental measurements of nucleon-pion scattering cross sections, which in turn can be used to obtain the scattering amplitudes, to constrain the model parameters.

Figure 1:

The nucleon-pion scattering that dominantly controls in-medium properties of nucleons up to
T=150\,
MeV.

For these reasons, let us first express the evolution equation of the spin density matrix in terms of the nucleon-pion scattering amplitudes in general, which is model-independent.
On the one hand, we may then use the scattering amplitudes that are determined from the experiments, without need to resort to a model calculation.
The existing nucleon-pion scattering data can provide the necessary nucleon-pion scattering amplitudes for the pion gas of temperature up to about
200
MeV
Leutwyler and Smilga 1990
.
Moreover, any model computation of scattering amplitudes, including chiral perturbation theory, can be used in our formulation of the time evolution equation of the nucleon spin density matrix.

In the following, we assume that the nucleon density matrix is diagonal in the isospin space, and introduce the density matrix for a single nucleon in the isospin space as

\hat{\rho}_{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\rho_{p}}&0\\
0&\hat{\rho_{n}}\end{array}\right),

(8)

where
\hat{\rho}_{p}
and
\hat{\rho}_{n}
are the density matrices for the isospin
I_{z}=\pm 1/2
states, i.e., proton and neutron, respectively.
To describe the effects of the nucleon-pion scattering in the time evolution of the density matrix, we need to expand the unitary time evolution operators,
\hat{U}(\Delta t)
and
\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\Delta t)
, to at least fourth order of the nucleon-pion interaction Hamiltonian, and compute the thermal correlation functions of the pion operators in Gaussian factorization approximation.
In the limit
\Delta t\gg\tau
, where
\tau
is the correlation time of the scattering process, we obtain a first-order time evolution equation for the nucleon density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{N}(t)
, which is expressed in terms of the nucleon-pion scattering amplitude and its complex conjugate.
It is also possible to take a different route to obtain the same result from the collision term of the Kadanoff-Baym equation as shown in Appendix
A
.

Suppose that the scattering amplitude of a process
N^{s}_{\alpha}(\bm{p})+\pi^{a}(\bm{k})\to N^{s^{\prime}}_{\beta}(\bm{p}^{\prime})+\pi^{b}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
is represented by a matrix,
[\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta\alpha}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})]_{s^{\prime},s}=\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta\alpha}(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},s,\bm{k})
, in spin space with
s,s^{\prime}={\pm 1/2}
.
Here,
\alpha,\beta=p,n
and
a,b=1,2,3
specify the nucleon and pion isospin states, respectively.
We assume that the pion gas is isospin symmetric.
We then find that the time evolution of the spin density matrix for the proton is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{p}^{2\times 2}(\bm{p})}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{a,b,\beta}\mathcal{T}_{p\beta}^{ba}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})\rho_{\beta}^{2\times 2}(\bm{p}^{\prime})\big[\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{p\beta}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})\big]^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\qquad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big](2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}+\bm{k}-\bm{p}^{\prime}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\delta(E_{\bm{p}}+\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle-\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}(\bm{p})\rho_{p}^{2\times 2}(\bm{p}),

(9)

where
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{\bm{k}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}
and
E_{\bm{p}}={\bm{p}^{2}/(2M_{N})}
are the energy dispersion relations of the pions and the nucleons, respectively.
Note that
\rho_{\alpha}(\bm{p})
and
\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta\alpha}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})
are
2\times 2
matrices in the spin space.
We also introduced the spin and isospin-independent “thermal damping rate”

\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}(\bm{p})
given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}(\bm{p})=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{a,b,\beta}\left|\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta p}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})\right|^{2}n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\big](2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}+\bm{k}-\bm{p}^{\prime}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\delta(E_{\bm{p}}+\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}),

(10)

which is the total scattering rate of a nucleon of momentum
\bm{p}
interacting with thermal pions.
The pictorial representation of each contribution is given in Fig.
2
.
The first two terms arise from the “cross diagram”, and the damping rate comes from the self-energy terms (see, e.g., Ref.
Li and Yee 2019
).
The time evolution equation for
\rho_{n}
is the same, with the replacement
p\leftrightarrow n
in all terms.
Simply speaking, the cross diagram is a product of scattering amplitude and its complex conjugate, but with different initial and final states depending on the components of the density matrix.
On the other hand, the total self-energy term is simply the absolute square of the scattering amplitude, which represents the usual scattering rate of the nucleon-pion scattering.
The self-energy term describes the loss of probability from the initial states due to scattering processes, while the cross diagram restores the total probability to make sure that the time evolution is unitary.
This structure is ubiquitous in the evolution of open quantum systems, and has a form of the Lindblad equation
Lindblad 1976
.

Figure 2:
The cross diagram (a), and the self-energy diagrams, (b) and (c), for the nucleon-pion scattering in the time evolution of nucleon spin density matrix. The blob represents the nucleon-pion scattering amplitude or its complex conjugate.

The normalization of the
\mathcal{T}
-matrix elements in the above expression is conventional in the context of nonrelativistic scattering theory, which is related to the nonrelativistic scattering amplitudes as follows.
The thermal damping rate is equal to the total scattering rate of a nucleon with all thermal pions in the background.
In the heavy nucleon mass limit, the velocity of a nucleon is negligibly small, the final state pion energy is equal to the initial one, and the cross-section depends only on the pion energy.
In this limit, we have a simplified formula

\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}(\bm{p})=\int_{\bm{k}}\sigma_{\rm tot}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})v_{\bm{k}}n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big],

(11)

where
v_{\bm{k}}={|{\bm{k}}|/\epsilon_{\bm{k}}}
is the pion velocity, and
v_{\bm{k}}n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})
is the flux of incoming pions with momentum
\bm{k}
.
The
\sigma_{\rm tot}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})
is the total cross section for pion energy
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}
with all possible isospin states, i.e.,

\sigma_{\rm tot}=\sum_{a}\sigma_{p\pi^{a}\to p\pi^{a}}+\sigma_{p\pi^{0}\to n\pi^{+}}+\sigma_{p\pi^{-}\to n\pi^{0}}.

(12)

Comparing with the expression (
10
), we find

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm tot}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle={2\pi\over v_{\bm{k}}^{2}}\int_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{ab,\beta}\left|\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta p}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})\right|^{2}\delta(|\bm{k}^{\prime}|-|\bm{k}|)

\displaystyle={\epsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}\over(2\pi)^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\prime}\sum_{a,b,\beta}\left|\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta p}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\prime}\sum_{a,b,\beta}{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{ba}_{\beta p}\over\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\prime}}.

(13)

Here,
{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{ba}_{\beta p}/\mathrm{d}\Omega^{\prime}}
is the differential cross section with fixed spin and isospin states (
\int\mathrm{d}\Omega
denotes the angular integration).
From the relation
{\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\Omega}=|f(\Omega)|^{2}
, where
f
is the nonrelativistic scattering amplitude related to the conventionally reported experimental data, we find the normalization relation

f(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k})=-{\epsilon_{\bm{k}}\over(2\pi)}\mathcal{T}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}).

(14)

Another conventional normalization is the relativistic amplitude given by

\mathcal{M}=-(2M_{N})(2\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\mathcal{T}=(8\pi)M_{N}f.

(15)

As mentioned above, the nucleon velocity is negligible in the heavy nucleon mass limit,
v\sim\sqrt{T/M_{N}}\ll 1
, where we used
E_{\bm{p}}={\bm{p}^{2}/(2M_{N})}\sim T
with temperature
T
.
The change of nucleon energy is thus small compared to the pion energy, i.e.,
\Delta E_{N}\sim vk\ll k
.
In this limit, the scattering amplitudes and the evolution equation (
9
) become independent of the nucleon momentum, which allows us to express the evolution equation in terms of the total densities

n_{\alpha}=\int_{\bm{p}}n_{\alpha}(\bm{p})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{S}_{\alpha}=\int_{\bm{p}}\bm{S}_{\alpha}(\bm{p})

(16)

only, where
\alpha=p,n
.
Furthermore, the scattering amplitude is constrained by symmetry.
The parity, rotational, and isospin symmetry dictates the form of the scattering amplitude as
Fettes and Meissner 2001
;
Matsinos 1997

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{ba}_{\beta\alpha}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\delta^{ba}\delta_{\beta\alpha}\big[{\bf 1}_{2\times 2}g^{+}+\mathrm{i}\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{k}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})h^{+}\big]

\displaystyle+\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{bac}(\tau^{c})_{\beta\alpha}\big[{\bf 1}_{2\times 2}g^{-}+\mathrm{i}\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{k}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})h^{-}\big],

(17)

where
g^{\pm}
and
h^{\pm}
are functions of
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}=\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}
and the angle
\theta^{\prime}
between
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
, and
({\bf 1}_{2\times 2},\bm{\sigma})
are the matrices in spin space with indices
s^{\prime},s
, and
\tau^{c}
is the Pauli matrix in isospin space.
The first term is the charge-conserving amplitude, and the second term is the charge-exchanging amplitude.
Similarly,
g^{\pm}
and
h^{\pm}
are the spin conserving and spin flipping amplitudes, respectively.

In terms of
(g^{\pm},h^{\pm})
, the time evolution equation (
9

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{p}^{2\times 2}}{\partial t}=

and spin densiy
\bm{S}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{\Lambda}^{2\times 2}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{a,b}\mathcal{T}^{ba}(\bm{k};\bm{k}^{\prime})\rho_{\Lambda}^{2\times 2}\big[\mathcal{T}^{ba}(\bm{k};\bm{k}^{\prime})\big]^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\quad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle-\widetilde{\gamma}_{\Lambda}\rho_{\Lambda}^{2\times 2},

where the thermal damping rate

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{a,b}\left|\mathcal{T}^{ba}(\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{k})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}).

(28)

Multiplying Eq. (

or

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=4\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}|h|^{2}|\bm{k}\times\bm{k}^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}).

(30)

Although it is not of our main interest, the thermal damping rate is expressed as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}_{\Lambda}

III.1
The spin relaxation rate of nucleons in

However, instead of theoretically evaluating the scattering amplitude, we are going to use the experimental data on the resonant scattering amplitude.

We first express the scattering amplitude assuming that the

-baryon resonance gives a dominant contribution.
Since

-baryons are spin and isospin
3/2

Here we note that the amplitude for the
I=3/2

(g_{3/2},h_{3/2})=(g^{+}-g^{-},h^{+}-h^{-})

I=1/2

(g_{1/2},h_{1/2})=(g^{+}+2g^{-},h^{+}+2h^{-})

2
2

2

This can be seen by observing that the isospin exchange operator
\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{bac}(\tau^{c})_{\beta\alpha}=-2(I_{\pi}^{c}I_{N}^{c})^{ba}_{\beta\alpha}

I_{\pi}^{c}
and
I_{N}^{c}
are the isovector components of the pion and nuleon isospin operators respectively (
I_{\pi}=1

11/4-I(I+1)
for total isospin
I

Since the dominant resonance contribution appears only in the

(g_{3/2},h_{3/2})\approx-3(g^{-},h^{-})\approx(3/2)(g^{+},h^{+})

Then, the spin-damping rate for nucleons is expressed in terms of the

\displaystyle\gamma_{N_{s}}

\displaystyle\approx{8\over 3}\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}|h_{3/2}|^{2}|\bm{k}\times\bm{k}^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}).

(32)

Similarly, the nucleon flipping and the thermal damping rates are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}

\displaystyle\approx{4\over 9}\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(|g_{3/2}|^{2}+|h_{3/2}|^{2}|\bm{k}\times\bm{k}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}

\displaystyle\approx 2\int_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(|g_{3/2}|^{2}+|h_{3/2}|^{2}|\bm{k}\times\bm{k}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})\big]2\pi\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}).

(33)

The amplitude for the nucleon-pion scattering has been measured in experiments
Hohler et al. 1977
, which is available in the form of scattering phase shift
\delta(\epsilon_{\bm{k}})

Koch and Pietarinen 1980
.
Thus, we need to relate the resonance phase shift measured in experiments with the amplitudes
(g_{3/2},h_{3/2})

First of all, rotational symmetry allows us to choose the incoming pion momentum to be
\bm{k}=k\hat{\bm{z}}

is expressed in spherical coordinates system as
\bm{k}^{\prime}=k(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kz}\chi_{s}(1/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{i}^{l}(2l+1)j_{l}(kr)P_{l}(\cos\theta)\chi_{s}(1/2),

(34)

with the spherical Bessel function

, and the spin wave function
\chi_{s}(s^{\prime})=\langle s|s_{z}=s^{\prime}\rangle

The resonance appears in the total angular momentum

We focus on the

Noting

(l,m)

(35)

The state

\displaystyle|l=1,m=0\rangle|s_{z}=1/2\rangle

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\over 3}|J=3/2,J_{z}=1/2\rangle-\sqrt{1\over 3}|J=1/2,J_{z}=1/2\rangle.

Note that only the

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathrm{scatt}}

\displaystyle=f(\Omega){\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kr}\over r}

\displaystyle\equiv{3\over 2\mathrm{i}k}\sqrt{4\pi\over 3}\sqrt{2\over 3}(\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\delta(k)}-1)

where

\displaystyle|J=3/2,J_{z}=1/2\rangle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1\over 3}|l=1,m=1,s_{z}=-1/2\rangle.

\displaystyle f(1/2,1/2)

IV
Discussion

We find that the spin relaxation rate of baryons in the hadronic phase around temperature
T=150
MeV is between
30
MeV and
50
MeV, corresponding to a relaxation time
\tau\simeq 4\sim 7
fm/c.
This time scale is comparable to the time scale of the plasma evolution in heavy-ion collisions, which indicates that an initial out-of-equilibrium spin polarization in the hadronic phase will remain so for a significant fraction of the time spent by the plasma before freezeout.
This finding clearly demonstrates the necessity of including relaxation dynamics of spin polarization in any reliable theoretical prediction or phenomenological simulation for the spin polarization of observed hadrons.

In our analysis, we have used the properties of baryons and their interaction with pions as in the vacuum, without considering possible thermal corrections at finite temperature.
For example, the mass and width of baryon resonances in a finite temperature plasma may significantly be different from their value in the vacuum.
While for mesons such corrections have been studied in some detail previously
Sommerer et al. 1995
, we are not aware of a similar study for the baryon resonances in the temperature range we are interested in.
This, among many other possible improvements in our treatment, can be a subject of future study.

2

Computing these tree graphs, we find the relativistic scattering amplitude given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda\pi}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ab}\frac{4\bar{m}D^{2}}{3f_{\pi}^{2}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[1+\bar{m}\Delta m\left(\frac{1}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\frac{1}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\bar{m}}{2}\gamma^{\mu}(k_{\mu}+k^{\prime}_{\mu})\left(\frac{1}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{1}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)\right]u(p)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ab}\frac{4\bar{m}D^{2}}{3f_{\pi}^{2}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\bigg[1+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\left(\frac{s-u}{4m_{\Lambda}}-\mathrm{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\frac{k_{\mu}k^{\prime}_{\nu}}{2m_{\Lambda}}\right)\left(-\frac{\bar{m}}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\frac{\bar{m}}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)\bigg]u(p),

(64)

where we introduced
\Delta m\equiv m_{\Sigma}-m_{\Lambda}
,
2\bar{m}\equiv m_{\Sigma}+m_{\Lambda}
,
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{i}(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu})/2
,
Mandelstam variables

s\equiv(p^{0}+k^{0})^{2}-(\bm{p}+\bm{k})^{2}
and
u\equiv(p^{0}-k^{\prime 0})^{2}-(\bm{p}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}
,
and the wave function for the
\Lambda
-baryon
u(k)
(
\bar{u}(k)=u^{\dagger}(k)\gamma^{0}
).
To derive the second line, we used the Gordon decomposition

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[{p^{\mu}+p^{\prime\mu}}+\mathrm{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime}_{\nu}-p_{\nu})\right]u(p)/(2m_{N})

as well as the energy-momentum conservation
p+k=p^{\prime}+k^{\prime}
.
Note that we parametrize the
S
-matrix element as

\langle\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime}|(S-1)|\bm{p},\bm{k}\rangle=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+k-p^{\prime}-k^{\prime})\mathrm{i}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda\pi}
.

To proceed further, let us take the center-of-mass frame, in which

\bm{k}+\bm{p}=\bm{0}=\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}
and
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}+E_{\bm{k}}=\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}+E_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}
, and thus,
|\bm{k}|=|\bm{k}^{\prime}|
is satisified.
Note that two Mandelstam variables are simplified to be

s=(E_{\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{\bm{k}})^{2}
and

u=(E_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}})^{2}-2\bm{k}^{2}(1+\cos\theta)

in the center-of-mass frame.
Moreover, considering the heavy-baryon limit, we approximate the
\Lambda
-baryon wave function as

u(p)\simeq(\sqrt{2m_{\Lambda}}\chi,0)^{t}
with the two-component (or nonrelativistic) spinor
\chi^{(s)}
, which results in

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda\pi}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{i}\delta_{ab}\frac{8m_{\Lambda}\bar{m}D^{2}}{3f_{\pi}^{2}}\chi^{\dagger}\bigg[1+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{\bar{m}}{4m_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{s-u}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{s-u}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.0pt-\mathrm{i}\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{k}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})\frac{\bar{m}}{2m_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{1}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{1}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)\bigg]\chi.

(65)

Then, recalling Eq. (
15
), we find the
\mathcal{T}
-matrix for the
\Lambda\pi
scattering predicted from the chiral perturbation as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda\pi}

\displaystyle\simeq-\delta_{ab}\frac{2\bar{m}D^{2}}{3f_{\pi}^{2}\epsilon_{\bm{k}}}\bigg[1+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}+\frac{\bar{m}\Delta m}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{\bar{m}}{4m_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{s-u}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{s-u}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 64.0pt-\mathrm{i}\bm{\sigma}\cdot(\bm{k}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})\frac{\bar{m}}{2m_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{1}{s-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}-\frac{1}{u-m_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right)\bigg].

(66)

By matching this result with Eq. (
26
), we find the low-momentum expansion of the
\mathcal{T}
-matrix (
45
) in the main text.

1.
Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
K
, the Mordell-Weil Theorem states that the set of
K
-rational points
E(K)
of
E
is a finitely generated abelian group. It was long believed that the torsion subgroup of
E(K)
,
E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}
, is not only finite but rather it is uniformly bounded by an integer that merely depends on the degree of the number field
K
. Mazur,
[
19
]
, proved that this is indeed the case when
K
is the rational field
{\mathbb{Q}}
giving an exhaustive list of
15
possibilities for
E({\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{tors}}
. Kenku, Kamienny and Momose,
[
9
,
12
]
, established such an exhaustive list for
E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}
when
K
is a quadratic field. Later on, the uniform boundedness of
E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}
was proved by Merel,
[
20
]
, for any number field
K
of fixed degree. Although the result is exciting, the uniform bound introduced is far from being sharp leaving the mathematical community with the challenge of finding a list of all possible finite abelian groups that may occur as torsion subgroups of elliptic curves over a number field of a given fixed degree. Recently, such a list was presented for cubic fields,
[
4
]
.

Writing
{\mathfrak{p}}
for a prime ideal in the ring of integers of
K
with a corresponding residue field
k_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
of characteristic
p>2
, we set
\widetilde{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be the reduction of
E
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}
. It can be seen that
E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}
embeds in
\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})
for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of good reduction of
E
satisfying mild ramification conditions. This implies the divisibility of
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|
by
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
for every such
{\mathfrak{p}}
. One may pose a question on the existence of an elliptic curve over
K
together with an integer
m\geq 2
such that
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|
is divisible by
m
for all but finitely many primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
, or more generally, for a set of primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
of (natural) density
1
. Serre,
[
29
]
, answers this question revealing that such elliptic curves are the ones that are
K
-isogenous to elliptic curves with nontrivial
K
-rational torsion subgroups where
m
is the order of the torsion subgroup. In addition, Serre shows that if
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|\equiv a\mod m
for a set of primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
of density
1
, then
a
must be
0
and
m
must be the order of the torsion subgroup of a
K
-isogenous elliptic curve.
Consequently, Katz noticed that one may compute the greatest common divisor,
\gcd_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in S}|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|
, of
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|
where
{\mathfrak{p}}
runs over a set
S
of primes of good reduction of
E
of density one under mild conditions on the ramification indices of the primes,
[
10
]
.

The above discussion gives rise to the following questions. If
m\geq 2
is not a divisor of the order of the torsion subgroup of a
K
-isogenous elliptic curve to
E
, is it possible to determine all the possible congruence classes of
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|\mod m
for all primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
of good reduction of
E
? In addition, can one compute the density of primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
at which each congruence class modulo
m
occurs?

Fixing the number field
K
to be the rational field
{\mathbb{Q}}
and writing
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
for the finite field with
p
elements where
p
is a rational prime, the number of
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
-rational points
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
, where
p
is a prime of good reduction of
E
, satisfies the Hasse-Weil bound, namely,
\left||\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|-p-1\right|\leq 2\sqrt{p}
. Deuring,
[
5
]
, proved if
E
has complex multiplication, then
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|=p+1
for a set of primes of density
1/2
. In other words, he proved that the set of supersingular primes of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication has density
1/2
. Counting rational points of reductions of Elliptic curves with complex multiplication has received much attention, see for example
[
22
,
27
,
23
,
3
]
. Due to the arithmetic properties of complex multiplication, one may find an explicit description of supersingular primes using congruence classes modulo a fixed integer. For example, given a nonzero rational number
D
, the elliptic curve
E^{D}:y^{2}=x^{3}-6Dx^{2}-3D^{2}x
has complex multiplication by
\sqrt{-3}
, more precisely
\operatorname{End}(E^{D})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{-3}]
. The supersingular primes of the curve
E^{D}
are exactly the primes
p\equiv 2\mod 3
; whereas if
p\equiv 1\mod 3
, then
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|=p+1-2c\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)
where
p=c^{2}+3d^{2}
with
c\equiv\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\mod 3
and
\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
is the Legendre symbol modulo
p
,
[
25
]
. In particular,
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 3
if
p\equiv 2\mod 3
, while
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\text{ or }1\mod 3
if
p\equiv 1\mod 3
. In fact, taking into account the existence of a
2
-torsion point in
E^{D}({\mathbb{Q}})
, one sees that
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 6
if
p\equiv 5\mod 6
and
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\text{ or }4\mod 6
if
p\equiv 1\mod 6
. It follows that one may describe the congruence classes of
|\widetilde{E}^{D}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\mod 6
and the congruence classes modulo
6
of the primes
p
at which they occur, hence one may get estimates for the densities of these primes
p
using Dirichlet’s Theorem on primes in arithmetic progression. It is worth noting that one of the main tools to obtain the aforementioned expressions for the number of rational points on reductions of elliptic curves
E:\ y^{2}=f(x)
with complex multiplication is computing the character sum
\sum_{x\mod p}\left(\frac{f(x)}{p}\right)
. The reason is that the trace of Frobenius
a_{p}(E):=p+1-|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
of the curve
E
at
p
can be expressed in terms of the latter character sum.

Given an integer
m\geq 2
that is not a divisor of the order of the torsion subgroup of any elliptic curve in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogeny class of
E
, one knows that
E
has a torsion point of order
m
under base change to the division field
{\mathbb{Q}}(E[m])
, where
E[m]
is the group of
m
-torsion points on
E
. By considering the splitting behavior of the primes in the latter field, one can easily show that the primes
p
for which
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
is divisible by
m
is of positive density. In this work, we consider the case when
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|\equiv 0\mod m
for some quadratic field
K
. In
[
6
,
7
,
21
]
, the possible torsion subgroups of
E
that appear when the base is changed from
{\mathbb{Q}}
to a quadratic field
K
are listed. Using the arithmetic of quadratic fields, it can be seen that orders of reductions of
E
modulo primes of good reduction are closely linked to the torsion subgroups of certain quadratic twists of
E
. This enables us to obtain information about the congruence classes of
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
modulo
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
. In addition, we show the existence of an integer
N
that depends on
K
such that for primes
p
modulo
N
, one can determine explicitly the possibilities for
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
modulo
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
. Thus, given any
a
modulo
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
, an estimate for the density of primes
p
such that
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv a\mod|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
can be computed. The following result will be proved in the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})
, where
d
is a square free integer. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Set
M_{K}(E)=\sup\{|E^{\prime}(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|:E^{\prime}\textrm{ is ${\mathbb{Q}}$-isogenous to }E\}
. Assume that
M_{K}(E)>M_{\mathbb{Q}}(E)
.
If
p\nmid 2dM_{K}(E)
is a prime of good reduction of
E
, then

|\widetilde{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv\begin{cases}0\mod M_{K}(E)&\text{ if }\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)=1\\
2p+2\mod M_{\mathbb{Q}}(E^{d})&\text{ if }\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)=-1,\\
\end{cases}

where
E^{d}
is the quadratic twist of
E
by
d
.

The latter result provides families of elliptic curves
E
, with no complex multiplication, for which one can find integers
m
such that
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\mod m
are completely determined and the primes of occurrence
p
are explicitly known. Up to the knowledge of the authors, there are only few examples in the literature of pairs of elliptic curves with no complex multiplication together with integers
m
for which the latter information is available. For such an example see
[
13
, Theorem 2]
where the congruence classes
|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\mod 12
of the curve
E:\ y^{2}=x^{3}-12x-11
are given. We can recover the occurrences of these classes by noticing the growth of the torsion subgroup of a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogenous elliptic curve over the quadratic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{5})
.

For an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with a nontrivial torsion subgroup of order divisible by
N\geq 1
, the trace of Frobenius of
E
at a prime
p
of good reduction satisfies that
a_{p}(E)\equiv p+1\mod N
. Therefore, unless
N=p
, one sees that the trace of Frobenius
a_{p}(E)
can never be
1
due to a congruence obstruction modulo
N
. In fact, the Weak Lang-Trotter conjecture, see
[
15
]
and
[
11
, Conjecture 1.3]
, asserts that it is only congruence obstructions
that prevent an integer
a
from being a trace of Frobenius
a_{p}(E)
of
E
for infinitely many primes
p
of good reduction of
E
. In
[
2
]
, it was shown that if
E
has a rational
\ell
-isogeny,
\ell\neq 11
, the
number of primes
p
such that
a_{p}(E)\equiv r\mod\ell
is finite, for some
r
modulo
\ell
, if and only if
E
has rational

\ell
-torsion over the cyclotomic field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{\ell})
.
In our work, for elliptic curves
E
whose torsion subgroups grow over a quadratic field
K
, we provide other integers
N
, namely divisors of
|E(K)_{\operatorname{tors}}|
, such that congruences modulo
N
obstruct certain integers from being the trace of Frobenius
a_{p}(E)
for infinitely many primes
p
of good reduction.

Kubert,
[
14
]
, parametrized elliptic curves defined over the rational field
{\mathbb{Q}}
possessing nontrivial torsion subgroups. In other words, it was proved that any such elliptic curve is a rational specialization of an elliptic surface defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In
[
26
]
, elliptic curves with nontrivial torsion subgroups over quadratic fields were parametrized in a similar fashion. We present two explicit examples of elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
with trivial rational torsion in which a positive proportion of their smooth fibers satisfy the following property. The orders of the reductions of any of these fibers are divisible by a certain integer modulo primes lying in a set of primes of positive density strictly less than
1
.

3.
Local results over quadratic fields

In this section, we discuss the relation between the size of the reduction of an elliptic curve
E
modulo a rational prime
p
of good reduction of
E
and the size of the reduction of
E
modulo a prime that lies above
p

Let
K
be a number field of degree
d
with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
. Let
p
be a rational prime. If the prime ideal factorization of
p
in
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
is given by
p=\prod{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{e({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}|p)}
, we call
e({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}|p)
the ramification index of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
over
p
, and
f({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}|p)=[k_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}:{\mathbb{F}_{p}}]
the inertial degree of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
over
p
, where
\sum_{i}e({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}|p)f({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}|p)=d
. If

for some

, then

is said to ramify in
K
, otherwise

is said to be unramified in

,
[
17
, Chapter 3]
.

We will write

for the quadratic twist of

by

is an element in the base field.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a prime in
K
of good reduction of
E
over
K
. Assume that
{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p
where

is unramified in

. The following hold.

i)

If

, then
\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\cong\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})
.

ii)

If
f({\mathfrak{p}}|p)=2
, then
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|=|\widetilde{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\cdot|\widetilde{E}_{p}^{d}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
, where

is a nonsquare.

iii)

If
f({\mathfrak{p}}|p)=3
, then
|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|=|\widetilde{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\left(p^{2}-p+1+(p+1)|\widetilde{E}_{p}^{d}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|-|\widetilde{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\cdot|\widetilde{E}_{p}^{d}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\right)
, where

is a nonsquare.

Proof:

i) Since

|k_{{\mathfrak{p}}}|=p
, hence

.

For ii), if

, then

|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|=1+p^{2}-a_{\mathfrak{p}}(E)
, where

is the trace of Frobenius of

at
\mathfrak{p}
and
N(\mathfrak{p})=p^{2}
. If

{\mathbb{Q}}
has good reduction at a rational prime

a_{n+2}(E)=a_{1}(E)a_{n+1}(E)-pa_{n}(E),\text{ for all }n\geq 0

where

\displaystyle a_{\mathfrak{p}}(E)=(p+1-|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|)^{2}-2p=p^{2}+1-2(p+1)|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|+|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{E}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(k_{{\mathfrak{p}}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\left(2p+2-|\widetilde{E}_{p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\right).

We recall that for

A similar argument yields iii).

In general, if

E_{t}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
together with a set of primes
S
of positive density strictly less than
1
such that for all possible values of
t=t_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, one has
|\widetilde{E}_{t=t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|
is divisible by a fixed integer for all
p\in S
.
As an example, one can find the following family of elliptic curves,
[
13
, Theorem 1]
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and
t
be an integer such that
t(9t+4)\not\equiv 0\mod p
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve given by

E_{t}:y^{2}=x^{3}-(6t+3)x-(3t^{2}+6t+2).

Then
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 0\mod{3}
.

We recall that if
E_{\textbf{a}}
,
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6})
, is an elliptic curve defined by a Weierstrass equation
y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
with discriminant
\Delta_{\textbf{a}}
, then the division polynomial
\psi_{m}
,
m\geq 2
is odd, is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6},x]
of degree
(m^{2}-1)/2
, see
[
30
, Chapter III]
for the definition and explicit description of
\psi_{m}
. In addition, if
P=(x(P),y(P))\neq O_{E}
is a point in
E
, then
P
is a point in
E[n]
if and only if
\psi_{n}(x(P))=0
.

We will need the following standard proposition for our construction.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
p\neq 2
be a rational prime. Let
K
be a number field, with ring of integers
R
, and
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K
with
\operatorname{char}k_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 2,3
.

Fix
T\in R
. Let
\textbf{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4},A_{6})
be a
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-solution of the polynomial equation
G_{T}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6})\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}}
, where

G_{T}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6})=\psi_{p}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6},T)\in k_{\mathfrak{p}}[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{6}].

If the following two conditions hold

i)

\Delta_{\textbf{A}}\not\equiv 0\mod\mathfrak{p}
, and

ii)

z_{T}^{2}+A_{1}Tz_{T}+A_{3}z_{T}\equiv T^{3}+A_{2}T^{2}+A_{4}T+A_{6}\mod{\mathfrak{p}}
for some
z_{T}\in k_{\mathfrak{p}}
,

then the elliptic curve
E_{\textbf{A}}:y^{2}+A_{1}xy+A_{3}y=x^{3}+A_{2}x^{2}+A_{4}x+A_{6}
over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfies

|E_{\textbf{A}}(k_{\mathfrak{p}})|\equiv 0\mod{p}
.

Proof.

Condition i) guarantees that
E_{\textbf{A}}
is an elliptic curve over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, whereas condition ii) implies that
P_{T}=(T\mod{\mathfrak{p}},z_{T})\in E_{\textbf{A}}(k_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Finally, the fact that
G_{T}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4},A_{6})\equiv 0\mod\mathfrak{p}
asserts that
P_{T}
is a point of order
p
in
E_{\mathbf{A}}(k_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Consider the elliptic curve

\displaystyle E_{t}:y^{2}=f_{t}(x):=x^{3}-3(t^{2}+1)x^{2}+3x-1,\qquad\text{where }\Delta(E_{t})=-432\ t^{4}(9+4t^{2})\neq 0.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}
, one has

|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 0\mod 3
for all primes
p\neq 2,3
such that
\nu_{p}(t(9+4t^{2}))=0
.

Moreover, there are infinitely many rational values of
t
such that
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 6
for a set
S
of primes
p
of density at least
2/3
; and
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 12
for at least half of the primes in
S
.

Proof:

Although the elliptic curve
E_{t}
does not possess a torsion point of order
3
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
, the curve
E_{t}
and the curve
y^{2}+6t\,xy+2t(4t^{2}+9)y=x^{3}

are
3
-isogenous over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
, and the latter has a
3
-torsion point
(0,0)
.

The polynomial
f_{t}
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
. Moreover, it is easily seen that the discriminant of
f_{t}
is not a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
. It follows that the Galois group of
f_{t}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
is the symmetric group
S_{3}
on a set of three elements. Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem implies the existence of infinitely many rational values
t=t_{0}
for which
f_{t_{0}}
has Galois group
S_{3}
. In accordance with Chebotarev’s density theorem, the density of primes
p
for which
f_{t_{0}}
has at least one root modulo
p
is
2/3
. It follows that for each such
t=t_{0}
, the density of primes
p
such that either
|\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 6
, when
f_{t_{0}}
has exactly one root modulo
p
and hence
\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})
has exactly one nontrivial
2
-torsion point; or
|\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 12
, when
f_{t_{0}}
splits modulo
p
and hence
\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})
has full
2
-torsion, is of density at least
2/3
.

\Box

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
E_{t}
be the elliptic curve described by

\displaystyle E_{t}:y^{2}=g_{t}(x):=x^{3}-7t\ x^{2}+96t^{2}\ x+256\ t^{3},\qquad\text{where }\Delta(E_{t})=-121634816\ t^{6}\neq 0.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
and any prime
p\neq 2,3,29
with
\nu_{p}(t)=0
such that
\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)=1
, one has

|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 0\mod 5
.

Moreover, there are infinitely many rational values of
t
such that
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 10
for a set
S
of primes
p
of density at least
1/6
; and
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 20
for a positive proportion of the primes in
S
.

Proof:

The
5
-th division polynomial
\psi_{5}
of the elliptic curve
E_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{5}(x)=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle x\ (16\ t-x)(343597383680\ t^{10}+42949672960\ t^{9}\ x-5368709120\ t^{8}\ x^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 1929379840\ t^{7}\ x^{3}-56623104\ t^{6}\ x^{4}+33816576\ t^{5}\ x^{5}-835584\ t^{4}\ x^{6}+135936\ t^{3}\ x^{7}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 5776\ t^{2}\ x^{8}-60\ t\ x^{9}+5\ x^{10}).

Now one obtains
g_{t}(0)=2^{8}\ t^{3}
. Therefore, if
\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)=1
, then
(0,2^{4}tm_{t})\in\widetilde{E}_{t,p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})
where
t\equiv m_{t}^{2}\mod p
. We now conclude using Proposition
5.2
.

The polynomial
g_{t}
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
with Galois group
S_{3}
. Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem yields the existence of infinitely many rational values
t=t_{0}
for which
g_{t_{0}}
has Galois group
S_{3}
. According to Chebotarev’s density theorem, the density of primes
p
for which
g_{t_{0}}
has at least one root modulo
p
is
2/3
. Since the set of primes
p
for which
\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)=1
and hence
|\widetilde{E}_{t,p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|\equiv 0\mod 5
is
1/2
, one gets that the density of primes
p
such that either
|\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 10
or
|\widetilde{E}_{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})|\equiv 0\mod 20
is at least
1/6
.

\Box

Remark 5.5
.

The curve
E_{t}
in Theorem
5.4
does not possess a rational point of order
5
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
.
One also notices that
E_{t}
is the quadratic twist by
t
of the elliptic curve
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-7\ x^{2}+96\ x+256
. Therefore, for all quadratic twists of
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the order of the reduction of the twist is divisible by
5
modulo primes of density at least
1/2
.

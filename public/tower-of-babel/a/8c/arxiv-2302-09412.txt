Gromov-Witten and Welschinger invariants of del Pezzo varieties
NGUYEN Thi Ngoc Anh

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish formulas for computing genus- 0 0 Gromov-Witten and Welschinger invariants of some del Pezzo varieties of dimension three by comparing to that of dimension two. These formulas are generalizations of that given in three-dimensional projective space by E. Brugallé and P. Georgieva in [ BG16 ] .



1.
Introduction and statement of main results

In 2016, E. Brugallé and P. Georgieva in
[
BG16
]
proposed a method to compute the genus-
0
Gromov-Witten and Welschinger invariants of
\mathbb{C}P^{3}
. The idea comes from the obsevation of J. Kollár in
[
Kol15
]
about the properties of pencils of quadric surfaces in
\mathbb{C}P^{3}
.

In this paper, we generalize the results obtained in
[
BG16
]
for high degree del Pezzo varieties
X
. More precisely, we compare the genus-
0
Gromov-Witten (resp. Welschinger invariants) of three-dimensional (resp. real) del Pezzo varieties with that of (resp. real) non-singular surfaces which realize half of the first Chern class of
X
, i.e. (resp. real) non-singular surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
where
K_{X}
is the canonical divisor of
X
.
According to Proposition
2.2
, these surfaces are indeed del Pezzo surfaces.
The method used in this text restricts us to be concerned with three-dimensional del Pezzo varieties of degree among
\{6,7,8\}
. In 2018, A. Zahariuc also adapted the same strategy to study genus-
0
Gromov-Witten invariants in three-dimensional del Pezzo varieties of degree
2,3,4
and
5
(see
[
Zah18
]
). It is interesting if one can find out some relations between his formula and our formula in Theorem
1.3
. More general, we are looking forward to studying Gromov-Witten and Welschinger invariants’ relations between (real) two and three dimensional del Pezzo varieties of any degree.

We first introduce some notations and main results of this paper.

Some notations

In this text, the lower point
.
stands for intersection between two homology classes and the upper point
\cdot
stands for the cup product.

1.1.
Introduction and motivation

A Fano variety of dimension
n
, introduced by Gino Fano in 1930s, is a non-singular complex projective variety
X_{n}
of complex dimension
n
whose anti-canonical divisor
-K_{X_{n}}
is ample. The maximal positive integer
r>0
such that
-K_{X_{n}}=rH
, where
H
is a primitive divisor in
Pic(X_{n})
, is called
index of
X_{n}
and denoted by
ind(X_{n})
.

Non-singular
n
-dimensional Fano varieties are completely classified in 1980s by V. A. Iskovskikh (for
n=3
) and T. Fujita (for any
n
) (see

[
IPP+10
]
).

A
n
-dimensional Fano variety of index divisible by
(n-1)
is also called a
n
-dimensional del Pezzo variety
for any
n\geq 2
.
The degree of a
n
-dimensional del Pezzo variety
X_{n}
, denoted by
\deg(X_{n})
, is the integer given by
(\frac{1}{n-1})^{n}(-K_{X_{n}})^{n}
.
Note that the degree of a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety is no more than
8
(see Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 in
[
IPP+10
]
). Note also that every Fano surface is a del Pezzo surface.

We consider a rational curve realizing homology class
\beta\in H_{2}(X_{n};\mathbb{Z})
as
the image of a morphism
f:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\longrightarrow X_{n}
such that
f_{*}([\mathbb{C}P^{1}])=\beta
.

Suppose that
(n-1)
divides
\big(c_{1}(X_{n})\cdot\beta+n-3\big)
. We denote the integer
\frac{1}{n-1}\big(c_{1}(X_{n})\cdot\beta+n-3\big)
by
k_{\beta}
. One define the genus-
0
Gromov-Witten invariant as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

The
genus-
0
Gromov-Witten invariant of a
n
-dimensional del Pezzo variety
X_{n}
(
n\in\{2,3\}
) of homology class
\beta\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, denoted by
GW_{X_{n}}(\beta)
, is the number of irreducible rational curves representing homology class
\beta
and passing through a generic configuration
\underline{x}_{\beta}
of
k_{\beta}
points in
X_{n}
.

We denote by
(X_{n},\tau)
a real
n
-dimensional del Pezzo variety
X_{n}
equipped with an anti-holomorphic involution
\tau
, so-called a real structure. The fixed locus of this involution is the real part of
X_{n}
, denoted by
\mathbb{R}X_{n}
. An algebraic curve in
(X_{n},\tau)
is
real
if it is preserved by the real structure
\tau
. A configuration of points in
(X_{n},\tau)
is
real
if it is preserved by the real structure
\tau
.

Let
\beta\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(X_{n};\mathbb{Z})
be a
\tau
-anti-invariant class, i.e.
\beta
is such that
\tau_{*}(\beta)=-\beta
. Let
\underline{x}_{\beta}
be a real generic configuration of
k_{\beta}
points in
X_{n}
consisting of
r
real points and
l
pairs of complex conjugated points such that
k_{\beta}=r+2l
.

For each real rational curve in
(X_{n},\tau)
, we can associate to a sign
\pm 1
, called Welschinger’s sign (see
[
Wel05b
]
,
[
Wel05c
]
) such that when counting such curves with this sign, we obtain an invariant independant of the choice of real configurations. Such invariant is called Welschinger invariant and is known as the real counter part of the above Gromov-Witten invariant. We will proceed the precise definitions of Welchinger’s sign in Section
4
. This sign in particular depends on the
Pin^{\pm}
- structure on the real part of the variety whose dimension is three. In the following, we describe the definition of the genus-
0
Welschinger invariant.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X_{n},\tau)
be a real
n
-dimensional (
n\in\{2,3\}
) del Pezzo variety whose real part is
\mathbb{R}X_{n}
.
The
genus-
0
Welschinger invariant of
(X_{n},\tau)
of homology class
\beta\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(X_{n};\mathbb{Z})
is the number of irreducible rational curves counted with Welschinger’s sign, representing homology class
\beta
and passing through a real generic configuration
\underline{x}_{\beta}
of
k_{\beta}
points in
X_{n}
consisting of
l
pairs of complex conjugated points.

In particular, if
\mathbb{R}X_{3}
is orientable with orientation
\mathfrak{o}_{X_{3}}
and is equipped with a
Spin_{3}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}_{X_{3}}
, we denote this invariant by
W_{\mathbb{R}X_{3}}^{\mathfrak{s}_{X_{3}},\mathfrak{o}_{X_{3}}}(\beta,l)
.

When
n=2
, we denote this invariant simply by
W_{\mathbb{R}X_{2}}(\beta,l)
.

1.2.
Main results

Throughout the rest of this paper, unless otherwise stated, we denote by
X
a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety.

Let
\Sigma\subset X
be a non-singular surface in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
.

Let
\psi:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
be the map induced by the inclusion from
\Sigma
to
X
. We have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety such that
\ker(\psi)\cong\mathbb{Z}
where
\Sigma
and
\psi
are as described above. Let
S
be a generator of
\ker(\psi)
.
For every homology class
d\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, one has

GW_{X}(d)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{D\in\psi^{-1}(d)}(D.S)^{2}GW_{\Sigma}(D).

Theorem
1.3
is a generalization of Theorem 2 in
[
BG16
]
.

This theorem allows us to compute the genus-
0
Gromov-Witten invariants for almost all three-dimensional del Pezzo varieties of degree
6,7
and
8
.

Suppose now that
(X,\tau)
is a real three-dimensional del Pezzo variety equipped with a real structure
\tau
. Suppose also that
\mathbb{R}X
is orientable with orientation
\mathfrak{o}_{X}
and is equipped with a
Spin_{3}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}_{X}
. Let
(\Sigma,\tau|_{\Sigma})\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
be a non-singular real del Pezzo surface.

Let
\psi:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
be as in the Gromov-Witten case.

Let
g(D)=\frac{1}{2}(K_{\Sigma}.D+D^{2}+2)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\epsilon:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be a map given by

\epsilon(D)=\begin{cases}c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D\mod 2,\text{ if }\mathbb{R}f^{*}T\Sigma/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}=E^{+},\\
c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D-1\mod 2,\text{ if }\mathbb{R}f^{*}T\Sigma/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}=E^{-}\end{cases}

where
f:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\to\Sigma\subset X
is a real immersion such that
f_{*}[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]=D\in H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
, and
E^{\pm}
are two isotopy classes of the real part of a holomorphic line subbundle of degree
(c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D-2)
of the rank
2
holomorphic normal bundle
f^{*}TX/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}
(see Section
4.2
and Definition
4.9
).

Let
s^{\mathfrak{s}_{X}|_{\Sigma}}:H_{1}(\mathbb{R}\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}

be the quasi-quadratic enhancement in one-to-one correspondence to
the
Pin^{-}_{2}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}_{X|\Sigma}
over
\mathbb{R}\Sigma
, which is restricted from the
Spin_{3}
-structure
\mathfrak{s}_{X}
over
\mathbb{R}X
(see Proposition
4.14
and Equation (
3
)).

Let
\rho:H_{2}^{-\tau}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(\mathbb{R}\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be the homomorphism given by the homology class realized by the real part of the
\tau
-anti-invariant representative of
D
.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
((X,\tau),\mathfrak{o}_{X},\mathfrak{s}_{X})
be a real three-dimensional del Pezzo variety such that
\ker(\psi)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, where
(\mathfrak{o}_{X},\mathfrak{s}_{X})
are as described above. Let
S
be a generator of
\ker(\psi)
. Suppose
(\Sigma,\tau|_{\Sigma})\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is such that
S
is
\tau|_{\Sigma}
-anti-invariant. For every
d\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and
0\leq l<\frac{1}{2}k_{d}
,
one has

W^{\mathfrak{s}_{X},\mathfrak{o}_{X}}_{\mathbb{R}X}(d,l)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{D\in\psi^{-1}(d)}(-1)^{\epsilon(D)+g(D)+s^{\mathfrak{s}_{X}|_{\Sigma}}(\rho D)}|D.S|W_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(D,l).

Theorem
1.4
is a generalization of Theorem 1 in
[
BG16
]
.

As a result, this theorem allows us to compute the genus-
0
Welschinger invariants of almost all real three-dimensional del Pezzo varieties of degree
6,7
and
8
.

Since the Welschinger invariants count real curves with some sign
\pm 1
, in the case where these invariants vanish, it is not clear that there exists no such real curves. This gives rise to one of the optimal problems in real enumerative geometry: the sharpness of the Welschinger invariants, in which one shows that the lower bounds given by these invariants can be obtained. The first examples of vanishing genus-0 Welschinger invariants
in projective
3
-space was explored by G. Mikhalkin for rational curves of degree
4
and then by J. Kollár for any even degree, see
[
Kol15
]
. We extend Kollár’s results for some classes of del Pezzo varieties of dimension three, as shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
((X,\tau),\mathfrak{o}_{X},\mathfrak{s}_{X})
be a real three-dimensional del Pezzo variety as in Theorem
1.4
.

Let
d\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(X;\mathbb{Z})
such that
D.S
is even for any
D\in\psi^{-1}(d)
. Let
k_{d}
be the associated integer of
d
.

Then, there exists a real configuration of
k_{d}
points, consisting of at least one real point, through which no real irreducible rational curve of homology class
d
passes.

2.
Pencils of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a pencil of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
. Let
E
be its base locus. Let
\Sigma
be a general element of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
. Firstly, we prove that the base locus
E
is an elliptic curve (Proposition
2.1
) and
\Sigma
is a non-singular del Pezzo surface of the same degree as
\deg(X)
(Proposition
2.2
). Then, we explain how monodromy action relates to the enumerative problems. In the next subsection, we give the main ingredient to solve the
1
-dimensional problem
of Theorem
1.3
(Proposition
2.11
). In the last subsection, by analysing the properties of real pencils of surfaces, we give the main ingredient to solve the
1
-dimensional real problem
of Theorem
1.4
(Proposition
2.16
, Corollary
2.17
).

2.1.
Properties of pencils of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|

Proposition 2.1
.

If the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
is generic enough, then its base locus is a non-singular elliptic curve. Moreover, this curve realizes the homology class
\frac{1}{4}K^{2}_{X}
in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

By Bertini’s theorem (see
[
GH78
]
) and Theorem 1.2 in
[
Šok80
]
, a general element
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is a non-singular surface.

The base locus
E
of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
realizes the homology class
[\Sigma].[\Sigma]
, that is the self-intersection product of homology class
[\Sigma]\in H_{4}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
By the adjunction formula, we have:

K_{\Sigma}=(K_{X}+[\Sigma]).[\Sigma]=-[\Sigma]^{2}.

Thus, the curve
E
realizes the homology class
-K_{\Sigma}
, hence

E
realises the homology class
\frac{1}{4}K^{2}_{X}
in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Since two general elements in pencil have traversal intersections, the curve
E
is in fact a non-singular representative of the class
-K_{\Sigma}
. By the adjunction formula, we deduce easily that the genus of the curve
E
is
1
.

Hence, the proposition follows.
∎

Next proposition explains the interest of studying surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Every non-singular surface
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is a del Pezzo surface whose degree is equal to
\deg(X)
.

Proof.

By Corollary 1.11 in
[
Isk77
]
, every non-singular surface in
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is a del Pezzo surface.

Moreover, by definition, the degree of del Pezzo surface
\Sigma
is equal to
K_{\Sigma}^{2}
and degree of del Pezzo space
X
is equal to
\frac{1}{8}(-K_{X})^{3}
. The adjunction formula implies that
K_{\Sigma}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}K_{X}^{2}.[\Sigma]
.
Since
[\Sigma]=-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}
, we deduce that
\deg(\Sigma)=\deg(X)
.

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a non-singular elliptic curve in
X
realising the homology class
\frac{1}{4}K^{2}_{X}
in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and contained in a non-singular surface
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
.

Then,
E
is the base locus of a pencil of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
.

Proof.

By adjunction formula, the elliptic curve
E
realizes the anti-canonical class
-K_{\Sigma}\in H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
.
It is sufficient to prove that
E
is the complete intersection of
\Sigma
with another non-singular surface
\Sigma^{\prime}\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
. In other words, we prove that

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma})=H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}(-K_{\Sigma})).

It is evident that
H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}(-K_{\Sigma}))\subset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma})
as subspace. We then prove that these two vector spaces have the same dimension.
We consider the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(0)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow 0

which induces the long exact sequence of cohomology

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(0))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(0))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

We have the following facts (see
[
IPP+10
]
, Section 3.2).

•

Firstly, we have that
\dim(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(0)))=1
and
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(0))=0
.

•

Secondly, by Riemann-Roch theorem, we have

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}))=(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{3}+3-1=\deg(X)+2,

and

\dim H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}(-K_{\Sigma}))=(-K_{\Sigma})^{2}+2-1=\deg(\Sigma)+1.

Therefore, we deduce that

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma})=\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}))-1=\deg(X)+1.

Remark that
\deg(X)=\deg(\Sigma)
. As a consequence, we obtain the identification

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})|_{\Sigma})=H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}(-K_{\Sigma})).

Thus, the statement follows.

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

For every
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
non-singular, one has a surjective map

\psi:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

which is induced by the inclusion of
\Sigma
in
X
.

In particular, if
X\in\{\mathbb{C}P^{3},\mathbb{C}P^{3}\sharp\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{3}},\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}\}
then
\ker(\psi)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

One has the divisor
-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}
is ample. Hence, the statement follows by Lefschetz hyperplane theorem (see for example Theorem C.8 in
[
EH16
]
).

In particular,

•

if
X=\mathbb{C}P^{3}
then
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for any
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
non-singular;

•

if
X=\mathbb{C}P^{3}\sharp\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{3}}
then
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{3}
for any
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
non-singular;

•

if
X=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}

H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{3}

. Hence, if

.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
X
is a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety of
\deg(X)\in\{5,6,7,8\}
. There are exactly four singular surfaces in a generic pencil of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a generic pencil of surfaces in the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
whose base locus is an elliptic curve
E
. Let
N
be the number of singular elements in this pencil.

Let
\tilde{X}
be the blown-up of
X
along the elliptic curve
E
. We will compute
N
by comparing the Euler characteristic of
\tilde{X}
in two equivalent ways.

(1)

On the one hand, we have
\chi(\tilde{X})=\chi(X)
since the Euler characteristic of the blown-up locus is
\chi(E)=0
.

(2)

On the other hand, we consider
\tilde{X}
as blown-up of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
along its base locus
E
. The number
N
is then equal to the intersection numbers of the general projective line defined by
\mathcal{Q}
and the discriminant hypersurface
\Delta
in
\mathbb{C}P^{m}
(where
m=\dim(\mathbb{P}(\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}))))
). Let
\Sigma_{t_{1}},\Sigma_{t_{2}},\ldots,\Sigma_{t_{N}}
be
N
singular surfaces of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
. Then,
\tilde{X}-\{\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Sigma_{t_{i}}\}
is a fibration over
\mathbb{C}P^{1}-\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\}
, whose fibres are non-singular del Pezzo surfaces. Let
\Sigma
be one of such fibres.
We have

\chi(\tilde{X}-\{\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Sigma_{t_{i}}\})=\chi(\mathbb{C}P^{1}-\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{N}\})\times\chi(\Sigma).

2.2.

We consider the Lefschetz pencil

arises when we go along a path from

in the fibration

\displaystyle T_{{S_{i}}_{*}}:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\to H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto D+(D.S_{i})S_{i}.

or

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

By definition of a vanishing cycle

Lemma 2.12
.

The surfaces in the real pencil
\mathcal{Q}
containing a real rational curve in

Proof.

Let

be a real rational curve in

Remark 1

Proposition 2.13

Proof.

The sufficient condition is direct. Indeed, if

We prove the necessary condition by two steps.

In the first step, we prove that

Suppose that

. It is sufficient to prove that if

•

\tau|_{\Sigma_{t}}
-anti-invariant,

•

or

Indeed, we have

Moreover,

then there exists an integer

3.1.
Degeneration of configuration of points

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety
X
, if
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is a non-singular surface and
D\in\psi^{-1}(d)
then one has
k_{D}=k_{d}-1
.

Proof.

By the Poincaré duality
\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)=PD(-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})
and by hypothesis
\Sigma\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is non-singular, one has

k_{d}=\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)\cdot d=[\Sigma].d.

By the adjunction formula, by hypothesis

and by the Poincaré duality
c_{1}(\Sigma)=PD(-K_{\Sigma})
, one has

[\Sigma].d=-K_{\Sigma}.D=k_{D}+1.

Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

Let
\underline{x}_{d}
be a configuration of
k_{d}
distinct points in the base locus
E
of pencil
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\mathcal{C}(\underline{x}_{d})
be the set of

The following proposition generalizes Proposition 3 by J. Kollár in

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that the
k_{d}
points of

are in general position in

\mathcal{C}(\underline{x}_{d})

\mathcal{Q}

Proof.

Let
C_{d}
be a curve in the set
\mathcal{C}(\underline{x}_{d})

Claim 1:

Suppose that
C_{d}
is irreducible. Then, the whole curve
C_{d}
has to be contained in some non-singular surface of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

The first observation is that any point of
C_{d}\setminus\underline{x}_{d}
is contained in some surface, which is denoted by
\Sigma_{t}
, of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
. Then, the number of intersection points between

and
\Sigma_{t}
is
\sharp(C_{d}\cap\Sigma_{t})>\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)\cdot d
. By Bézout’s theorem, the whole curve

has to be contained in this surface.

We show that such surface
\Sigma_{t}
is non-singular if the chosen points of
\underline{x}_{d}
are generic enough. Indeed, suppose that the curve
C_{d}
(passing through the configuration
\underline{x}_{d}
in
E
) is contained in a singular surface
\Sigma_{sing}
of the pencil
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
rank(Pic(\Sigma_{sing}))=k-1
,

. Note that
rank(Pic(\Sigma_{sing}))=rank(Pic(\Sigma))-1
, where
\Sigma
is a non-singular surface in
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
(L_{1},L_{2}\ldots,L_{k})
be a basis of

, such that the vanishing cycle

. Let
(\tilde{L_{12}},\tilde{L_{3}}\ldots,\tilde{L_{k}})
be a basis of

. Consider a homomorphism given by

\displaystyle\gamma:H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})

\displaystyle\to H_{2}(\Sigma_{sing};\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle L_{1}-L_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto 0

\displaystyle L_{1}+L_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{L_{12}}

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{L_{i}},\forall i\in\{3,\ldots,k\}.

Suppose that the curve
C_{d}
realises homology class

in

.

in

.
If we fix

and move the last point (still in

), then we get a new configuration

, such that

. On the contrary, the divisor class

stays fixed. In other words, we can choose a new configuration

then the chosen configuration

, through which

passes, is not generic. Moreover, there are only finitely many singular surfaces in the pencil

(see Lemma

can not be contained.

∎

passes though exactly

.

Suppose that there exists a component of

.

Let

is an effective divisor class then there exists only countably many decompositions of

into a sum of effective divisors.

If the configuration
\underline{x}_{d}
is generic then the curve

is irreducible.

We prove this claim by contradiction. Fix an integer

is determined by

until we get a new configuration

in

[\underline{x}_{d_{1}}^{\prime}]\neq[\underline{x}_{d_{1}}]
in

, and the effective divisor

. As a result, if the curve
C_{d}
is reducible, then we can only choose the configurations, through which

passes, in a measure zero subset, hence such configurations are not generic.

∎

The last claim finishes our proof of this proposition.

3.2.1.
Moduli spaces of stable maps and balanced rational curves

We consider the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
of irreducible genus-
0

k_{d}
-pointed stable maps

f:(\mathbb{C}P^{1};p_{1},\ldots,p_{k_{d}})\to X

of homology class
d\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
the subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
consisting of simple maps. And, we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
the compactification (due to M. Kontsevich) of
\mathcal{M}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
, and its corresponding total evaluation map
ev_{X}
.

By the genericity of the configuration of points
\underline{x}_{d}=f(\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k_{d}}\})
in

, the invariant

can be identified with the number of elements
[(\mathbb{C}P^{1};p_{1},\ldots,p_{k_{d}});f]
in
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
, which are regular points of
ev_{X}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A rational curve parametrized by
f:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\to X
is
balanced
if
f
is balanced, i.e.
f
is an immersion and the normal bundle
f^{*}TX/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}
is isomorphic to the direct sum of two holomorphic line bundles of the same degree.

Lemma 3.4
.

(Lemma 1.2
[
Wel05c
]
)
An element
[(\mathbb{C}P^{1};p_{1},\ldots,p_{k_{d}});f]
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k_{d}}(X,d)
is a regular point of the map
ev_{X}
if and only if
f
is balanced.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\to X
be an immersion such that
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\Sigma
. If
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is not a complete intersection in
X
, then
f
is balanced.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{f}=f^{*}TX/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}=f^{*}T\Sigma/T\mathbb{C}P^{1}
be normal bundles.
We consider the following short exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles over
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{f}\longrightarrow f^{*}TX/T\Sigma\longrightarrow 0.\;(*)

Claim 1.
The map
f
is balanced if and only if the short exact sequence
(*)
does not split.

Suppose that
f_{*}[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]=D\in H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
.
We have seen that the rank
2
normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{f}
has degree
c_{1}(X)\cdot d-2=2k_{d}-2
where
d=\psi(D)\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. By definition, we have
f
is balanced if the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{d}-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{d}-1)
. We also have an isomorphism

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D-2).

This implies that the normal line bundle
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}
has degree
c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D-2=k_{d}-2
. Hence, the normal line bundle
f^{*}TX/T\Sigma
has degree
(2k_{d}-2)-(k_{d}-2)=k_{d}
. Therefore, the map
f
is balanced implies that the short exact sequence
(*)
does not split. Conversely, if the short exact sequence
(*)
does not split then
f
is balanced. Indeed, we suppose that
\mathcal{N}_{f}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{2})
such that
k_{1}>k_{2}
, i.e.
k_{1}>c_{1}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f})
. This implies the null-morphism
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{1})\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}
. Then, there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{1})\to\mathcal{N}_{f}/\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{f}/\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{f}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}(k_{d})
. Hence, the short exact sequence
(*)
splits (see page 252
[
GH83
]
).

Claim 2.
The exact sequence
(*)
splits if and only if
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is a complete intersection in
X
.

An idea of a proof is using the first-order deformation of the surface
\Sigma
in a pencil
\mathcal{Q}
generated by an elliptic curve
E
which realizes the homology class
\frac{1}{4}K^{2}_{X}
; and considering the divisor classes restricted on
E
by the first-order deformation of
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
in
\Sigma
.
By extending a result
in the proof of
Theorem
(4.f.3)
in
[
GH83
]
, the following two statements hold.

(1)

If the exact sequence
(*)
splits then the section of
\mathcal{N}_{f}
which vanishes at
D.E
points in the intersection
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\cap E
implies that restricted divisor class
[f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})]|_{E}
is constant.

(2)

If
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is not a complete intersection with
\Sigma
in
X
, then the restricted divisor class
[f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})]|_{E}
in
Pic(E)
must vary as we deform
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
. Indeed, let
f_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
be a first-deformation of
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
in the pencil, i.e.
f_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})\subset\Sigma_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{Q}
. One has

2[f_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})]|_{E,\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\sim\phi(\overline{D})-(D.S)S|_{E,\Sigma_{\epsilon}}=\phi(\overline{D})-(D.S)\phi(\overline{L})+2(D.S)L|_{E,\Sigma_{\epsilon}}.

By Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.8
and the fact that
L|_{E,\Sigma_{\epsilon}}
is not a constant as we deform
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
, the statement follows.

The proposition follows by the sufficient condition of
Claim 1
and necessary condition of
Claim 2
.
In other words, if
f(\mathbb{C}P^{1})
is not a complete intersection, then the exact sequence
(*)
does not split, and hence
f
is balanced.

∎

Remark 3

If an irreducible curve

in

Corollary 3.7
.

be a configuration of points as in Proposition

.
Then, every non-singular surface

in the pencil

, which contains a curve of class

, contains exactly

Proof.

We consider the moduli space of stable maps

and its corresponding total evaluation map

. Proposition

imply that for every map

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Regular values of the total evaluation map

contained in

form a dense open subset

.
In particular, if

then the cardinality of the set

.

Proof.

Let
\underline{x}_{d}
be a configuration of

distinct points on

for which the conclusion of Proposition

holds, i.e. every curve in

4.
The sign problems

4.1.
Welschinger’s signs of real curves on real del Pezzo surfaces

Let
(\Sigma,\tau)
be a real del Pezzo surface.
Let
D\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
and let
k_{D}=c_{1}(X)\cdot D-1
be its associated integer.
Let
C
be an irreducible real rational curve in
X
representing the homology class
D
and nodal, i.e. having only ordinary real nodes as singularities.
By adjunction formula, the total number of complex nodes of
C
, which consists of real nodes and pairs of complex conjugated nodes, is equal to

g(D)=\frac{1}{2}(-c_{1}(\Sigma)\cdot D+D^{2}+2).

A real node of
C
is either hyperbolic or elliptic in the following sense.

Definition 4.1
.

A
hyperbolic real node
of a curve is a real point whose local equation of the curve is given by
x^{2}-y^{2}=0
over
\mathbb{R}
(i.e. it is the intersection of two real branches).
An
elliptic real node
(or an isolated real node) of a curve is a real point whose local equation of the curve is given by
x^{2}+y^{2}=0
over
\mathbb{R}
(i.e. it is the intersection of two complex conjugated branches).

Let
\delta_{E}(C)
be the numbers of elliptic real nodes of
C
. Let
\delta_{H}(C)
be the numbers of hyperbolic real nodes of
C
. And let
\delta_{\mathbb{C}}(C)
be the numbers of pairs of complex conjugated nodes of
C
. One has

\delta_{E}(C)+\delta_{H}(C)+2\delta_{\mathbb{C}}(C)=g(D).

Definition 4.2
.

The
Welschinger’s sign
of an irreducible real rational curve
C
in
(\Sigma,\tau)
, denoted by
s_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(C)
, is defined as

s_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(C):=(-1)^{\delta_{E}(C)}.

Let
\underline{x}_{D}
be a real generic configuration of
k_{D}
points consisting of
r
real points and
l
pairs of complex conjugated points in
X
, such that
r+2l=k_{D}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}(\underline{x}_{D})
be a set of real rational curves in
\Sigma
representing the homology class
D
and passing through
\underline{x}_{D}
.
One has moreover, the genericity of
\underline{x}_{D}
implies that every real curve in
\mathcal{R}(\underline{x}_{D})
is nodal and irreducible.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(\Sigma,\tau)
be a real del Pezzo surface and
D\in H_{2}^{-\tau}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
. We associate each real curve
C\in\mathcal{R}(\underline{x}_{D})
to its Welschinger’s sign
s_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(C)
.

The
Welschinger invariant
of
(\Sigma,\tau)
of homology class
D
is defined as

W_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(D,l):=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{R}(\underline{x}_{D})}s_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(C).

Remark 4
.

If either
D\in H_{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\setminus H_{2}^{-\tau}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
or
k_{D}\leq 0
, then the corresponding Welschinger invariants
W_{\mathbb{R}\Sigma}(D,l)
vanish.

Theorem 4.4
.

(Welschinger
[
Wel05b
]
, Brugallé
[
Bru21
]
)
The Welschinger invariants in Definition
4.3
do not depend on the choice of a real generic configuration of points, provided its number of real points (or equivalently, its number of complex conjugated points).

Scattering for the magnetic Zakharov system in 3 dimensions
Xiaohong Wang, Lijia Han

Abstract




Abstract.
We consider the global existence and scattering for solutions of magnetic Zakharov system in three-dimensional space. When the initial datas are small, we prove the existence of smooth global solutions and scattering results, by combining the space–time resonance method, weighted Sobolev space and dispersive estimates. Moreover, the decay rates for the solutions are also obtained.

Introduction

In this paper, we
study the Cauchy problem for the magnetic Zakharov system (MZ)
given by

\left\{\!\!\begin{array}[]{lc}i\mathcal{E}_{t}+\Delta\mathcal{E}-n\mathcal{E}+i\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{B}=0,&\\
\alpha^{-2}n_{tt}-\Delta n=\Delta|\mathcal{E}|^{2},&\\
\beta^{-2}\mathcal{B}_{tt}+(\Delta^{2}-\Delta)\mathcal{B}=i\Delta\nabla\times(\nabla\times(\mathcal{E}\times\overline{\mathcal{E}})).\\
\end{array}\right.

where,
\alpha>0

\beta>0

electron. The function

Magnetic Zakharov equation is a system coupled by Zakharov equation and the equation describing the self generated magnetic field. The self generated magnetic field
was first found in 1971 in studying the laser plasma (see

years, with the development of laser plasma, astronomy plasma and the strong turbulence theory related

attention from researchers.

3.1
Space-time resonance set

It becomes clear that the properties of
\phi(\xi,\eta),\rho(\xi,\eta),\psi(\xi,\eta),\gamma(\xi,\eta)
will provide a key to understand the scattering of the system (

): this is the idea of space–time resonance. According to the concept of space–time resonance set in
[

]
, the space–time resonance set about

is

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\phi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\phi_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}\phi_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\}=\left\{2\xi-2|\eta|\pm|\alpha|=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\mathcal{T}_{\phi_{\pm}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{\pm}}\right\}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\eta=0,|\xi|=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\right\}.

The space–time resonance set about
\rho_{\pm}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\rho_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\rho_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rho_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}\rho_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\}=\left\{2\xi-2|\eta|\mp\beta\sqrt{{\eta}^{2}+1}\mp\beta|\eta|\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{{\eta}^{2}+1}}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rho_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\mathcal{T}_{\rho_{\pm}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\rho_{\pm}}\right\}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\eta=0,|\xi|=\frac{1}{2}\beta\right\}.

The space–time resonance set about
\psi_{\pm}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\psi_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\psi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}\psi_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\}=\left\{2\xi=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\psi_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{\pm}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\psi_{\pm}}\right\}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\eta=0,\xi=0\right\}.

The space–time resonance set about
\gamma_{\pm}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\gamma_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\gamma_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}\gamma_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\}=\left\{2\xi=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\gamma_{\pm}}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{\pm}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\gamma_{\pm}}\right\}=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\eta=0,\xi=0\right\}.

We define the resolution space
X
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|(\mathcal{E}=e^{-it\Delta}f,N_{\pm}=e^{{\mp}it|\alpha\nabla|}g_{\pm},M_{\pm}=e^{{\mp}it\beta(\Delta^{2}-\Delta)^{1/2}}h_{\pm})\big\|_{X}=\\

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta}F_{\pm},e^{{\mp}it|\alpha\nabla|}G_{\pm},e^{{\mp}it\beta(\Delta^{2}-\Delta)^{1/2}}H_{\pm}\big\|_{X}\lesssim\|(\mathcal{E},N_{\pm},M_{\pm})\|_{X}^{2},

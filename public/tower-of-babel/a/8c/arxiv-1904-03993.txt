One Parameter Family of Jordanian Twists
Daniel MELJANAC † 1 {}^{{\dagger}^{1}}, Stjepan MELJANAC † 2 {}^{{\dagger}^{2}}, Zoran ŠKODA † 3 {}^{{\dagger}^{3}} and Rina ŠTRAJN † 4 {}^{{}^{{\dagger}^{4}}}

Abstract
We propose an explicit generalization of the Jordanian twist proposed in r r -symmetrized form by Giaquinto and Zhang. It is proved that this generalization satisfies the 2-cocycle condition. We present explicit formulas for the corresponding star product and twisted coproduct. Finally, we show that our generalization coincides with the twist obtained from the simple Jordanian twist by twisting by a 1-cochain.

Introduction

Drinfeld twists of Hopf algebras
[
7
]
provide a systematic way of producing new examples in noncommutative geometry. Given a Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
with a coproduct
\Delta_{0}
, a counit
\epsilon_{0}
, and an element
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
satisfying a 2-cocycle condition and a normalization (counitality) condition
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
27
]
, often called a Drinfeld twist, one defines a new coproduct,
\Delta^{\mathcal{F}}(-)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Delta_{0}(-)\mathcal{F}
, which is coassociative due to the 2-cocycle condition. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}
as an algebra, together with the new coproduct
\Delta^{\mathcal{F}}
becomes a new
twisted
Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{F}}
. Along with a Hopf algebra, many associated constructions like its representations, comodules, module algebras and so on, are twisted as well, using standard formulas involving the twist
\mathcal{F}
. The systematic nature of the twisting procedure makes it suitable for finding new physical models with the Hopf algebra covariance built in.

In 1989, a new construction of a deformation 2-cocycle is proposed by Coll, Gerstenhaber and Giaquinto in
[
4
]
. Their construction starts with a
k
-algebra
A
with multiplication
\mu_{A}
and two derivations
\phi,\psi\colon A\to A
satisfying
[\phi,\psi]=\lambda\psi
for some
\lambda\in k
. The action of the 2-dimensional Lie algebra
L
generated by
\phi
and
\psi
on
A
by derivations extends to a unique action
\triangleright
of the Hopf algebra
U(L)
on
A
making it a
U(L)
-module algebra. They prove
[
4
,
12
]
that
\mu_{A}\circ(\phi\otimes\psi)
is a Hochschild 2-cocycle which may be integrated to yield a formal deformation of
A
. The deformed multiplication on
A[[t]]
is given by
\mu_{A}\circ(1\otimes 1+t\phi\otimes 1)^{1\otimes\psi}\!\circ(\triangleright\otimes\triangleright)
for
\lambda=1
. This formula involves the element

\displaystyle(1\otimes 1+t\phi\otimes 1)^{1\otimes\psi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\phi^{n}\otimes\binom{\psi}{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(1\otimes 1+t\phi\otimes 1)^{1\otimes\psi}}{}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\phi^{n}\otimes\psi(\psi-1)\cdots(\psi-n+1)\in(U(L)\otimes U(L))[[t]],

which is a (Drinfeld) 2-cocycle twist for the Hopf algebra
U(L)[[t]]
. They provide several examples. Their construction is reanalyzed in detail in
[
12
]
and the 2-cocycle twist has been rediscovered in
[
35
]
. Algebras obtained by variants of their method are now often called Jordanian deformations. Most studied examples are a Jordanian deformation of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
(and its dual) and more general Jordanian quantum groups, leading to the corresponding Jordanian classical
r
-matrices and quantum
R
-matrices (some of which were known before, e.g.,
[
26
, Example 1, due D. Gurevich]
and
[
5
, Section 2.2]
). Regarding that
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
can be embedded into Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
, it is not surprising that more elaborate versions of Jordanian twists are used to obtain new deformations of Yangians
[
20
,
23
,
37
]
, with applications to integrable models, chain models in particular
[
21
]
. A comprehensive study of a related class of classical
r
-matrices can be found in
[
19
]
.

Here we present another approach to new Jordanian deformations. Closer to the setup of our paper, consider the universal enveloping algebra of the 2-dimensional solvable Lie algebra with generators
H
and
E
with
[H,E]=E
. Define

\displaystyle H^{\langle m\rangle}=H(H+1)\cdots(H+m-1),\qquad H^{\langle 0\rangle}=1.

2
Generalization of the Giaquinto–Zhang twist

We define the generalized Jordanian twist
\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}

via an explicit expansion,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{k+l}\left((u-1)P\right)^{k}\binom{D}{l}\otimes(uP)^{l}\binom{D}{k}.

(2.1)

The twist
\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}
interpolates between
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{-1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}
. For
u\rightarrow 0
one can easily see
[
2
]
that (
2.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}^{-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-1}{\kappa}\right)^{m}P^{m}\otimes\binom{D}{m}={\rm e}^{\ln(1-\frac{1}{\kappa}P)\otimes D}.

For
u=1

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{m}\binom{D}{m}\otimes P^{m}={\rm e}^{D\otimes\ln(1+\frac{1}{\kappa}P)}.

For
u=\frac{1}{2}
this reduces to the twist introduced in
[
10
]
, where

\displaystyle t=\frac{1}{2\kappa},\qquad E=P,\qquad\text{and}\qquad H=-D.

2.1
2-cocycle condition

Theorem 2.1
.

For arbitrary
u
, twists
\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}
satisfy the
2
-cocycle condition given by

\displaystyle\big((\Delta_{0}\otimes 1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{{\rm GZ},u}\big)\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{{\rm GZ},u}\otimes 1\big)=\big((1\otimes\Delta_{0})\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{{\rm GZ},u}\big)\big(1\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{{\rm GZ},u}\big).

(2.2)

Proof 2.2
.

If we write

\displaystyle f_{n}:=\sum_{k+l=n}\left((u-1)P\right)^{k}\binom{D}{l}\otimes(uP)^{l}\binom{D}{k}

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{{\rm GZ},u}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{n}f_{n}

with
f_{n}
not depending on
\kappa
and
f_{0}=1\otimes 1
.
In terms of
f_{i}
, the 2-cocycle condition becomes a sequence of equations for all
n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left((\Delta_{0}\otimes 1)f_{i}\right)(f_{n-i}\otimes 1)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left((1\otimes\Delta_{0})f_{i}\right)(1\otimes f_{n-i}).

In the first order in
1/\kappa
,

\displaystyle f_{1}=(u-1)P\otimes D+uD\otimes P,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes 1)f_{1}+f_{1}\otimes 1=(1\otimes\Delta_{0})f_{1}+1\otimes f_{1},

and in the second order,

\displaystyle f_{2}=(u-1)^{2}P^{2}\otimes\binom{D}{2}+(u-1)uPD\otimes PD+u^{2}\binom{D}{2}\otimes P^{2},

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes 1)f_{2}+((\Delta_{0}\otimes 1)f_{1})(f_{1}\otimes 1)+f_{2}\otimes 1=(1\otimes\Delta_{0})f_{2}+((1\otimes\Delta_{0})f_{1})(1\otimes f_{1})+1\otimes f_{2}.

For general order
n
, it should hold that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},l_{1},l_{2}=0\\
k_{1}+k_{2}=k,\,l_{1}+l_{2}=l,\,k+l=n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left[\Delta_{0}\left(P^{k_{1}}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\right)\otimes P^{l_{1}}\binom{D}{k_{1}}\right]\left[P^{k_{2}}\binom{D}{l_{2}}\otimes P^{l_{2}}\binom{D}{k_{2}}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},l_{1},l_{2}=0\\
k_{1}+k_{2}=k,\,l_{1}+l_{2}=l,\,k+l=n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left[P^{k_{1}}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{0}\left(P^{l_{1}}\binom{D}{k_{1}}\right)\right]\left[1\otimes P^{k_{2}}\binom{D}{l_{2}}\otimes P^{l_{2}}\binom{D}{k_{2}}\right].

This can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{l}\left[\Delta_{0}\left(P^{k_{1}}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\right)\otimes P^{l_{1}}\binom{D}{k_{1}}\right]\left[P^{k-k_{1}}\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes P^{l-l_{1}}\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{l}\left[P^{k_{1}}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\otimes\Delta_{0}\left(P^{l_{1}}\binom{D}{k_{1}}\right)\right]\left[1\otimes P^{k-k_{1}}\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes P^{l-l_{1}}\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\right],

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{l}\left[\Delta_{0}\big(P^{k_{1}}\big)\big(P^{k-k_{1}}\otimes P^{l_{2}-l_{1}}\big)\binom{(D-k+k_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+l_{1})}{l_{1}}\otimes P^{l_{1}}\binom{D}{k_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\left[\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\otimes 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{k}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{l}\big[P^{k_{1}}\otimes\big(\Delta_{0}\big(P^{l_{1}}\big)\big(P^{k-k_{1}}\otimes P^{l-l_{1}}\big)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\left[\binom{D}{l_{1}}\otimes\binom{(D-k+k_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+l_{1})}{k_{1}}\left(\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\right)\right].

Let us compare the terms of type
P^{A}\otimes P^{B}\otimes P^{C}
with
A+B+C=k+l=n
on both sides. We see only the terms with
C=l_{1}
on the left-hand side and only the terms with
A=k_{1}
on the right-hand side. We also need to take into account
\Delta_{0}\big(P^{k_{1}}\big)=\sum\limits_{a=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{a}P^{k_{1}-a}\otimes P^{a}
on the left-hand side and
\Delta_{0}\big(P^{l_{1}}\big)=\sum\limits_{b=0}^{l_{1}}\binom{l_{1}}{b}P^{b}\otimes P^{l_{1}-b}
on the right-hand side to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}=0\\
k-a=A\end{subarray}}^{k}\sum_{a=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{a}\binom{(D-k+k_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+C)}{C}\left(\binom{D}{l-C}\otimes\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\right)\otimes\binom{D}{k_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}=0\\
l-b=C\end{subarray}}^{l}\sum_{b=0}^{l_{1}}\binom{l_{1}}{b}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\otimes\binom{(D-k+A)\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+l_{1})}{A}\left(\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes\binom{D}{k-A}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k-a=A\\
k_{1}=0\end{subarray}}^{k}\binom{k_{1}}{k-A}\binom{(D-k+k_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+C)}{C}\left(\binom{D}{l-C}\otimes\binom{D}{k-k_{1}}\right)\otimes\binom{D}{k_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l-b=C\\
l_{1}=0\end{subarray}}^{l}\binom{l_{1}}{l-C}\binom{D}{l_{1}}\otimes\binom{(D-k+A)\otimes 1+1\otimes(D-l+l_{1})}{A}\left(\binom{D}{l-l_{1}}\otimes\binom{D}{k-A}\right)

for every
k,l\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
, and all
A\leq k
,
C\leq l
.

In terms of the new variables

\displaystyle x=D\otimes 1\otimes 1,\qquad y=1\otimes D\otimes 1,\qquad z=1\otimes 1\otimes D,

and taking into account that
k+l=n
, we reduce the 2-cocycle condition to the identity

\displaystyle\binom{x}{l-C}\sum_{k_{1}=k-A}^{k}\binom{k_{1}}{k-A}\binom{x+y-k-l+k_{1}+C}{C}\binom{y}{k-k_{1}}\binom{z}{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\binom{z}{k-A}\sum_{l_{1}=l-C}^{l}\binom{l_{1}}{l-C}\binom{y+z-k-l+l_{1}+A}{A}\binom{x}{l_{1}}\binom{y}{l-l_{1}}

(2.3)

for all
A\leq k
and
C\leq l
. This is restated as (
A.1
), and then proved, in Appendix
A
.

For
C=0
the identity (
2.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=k-A}^{k}\binom{k_{1}}{k-A}\binom{y}{k-k_{1}}\binom{z}{k_{1}}=\binom{z}{k-A}\binom{y+z-k+A}{A}.

2.2
Star product

We now introduce an action
\triangleright
of
P
and
D
on the space of formal power series in variables
x_{\mu}
, where
\mu=0,1,\ldots,n
, by formulas

\displaystyle(P\triangleright f)(x)=-{\rm i}v_{\mu}\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x_{\mu}},\qquad(D\triangleright f)(x)=x_{\mu}\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x_{\mu}},

where the constants
v_{\mu}
are such that
v^{2}\in\{-1,0,1\}
and the Einstein summation rule is understood. We also denote
x=(x_{\mu})
and
\partial_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}
.

A star product
*
is then defined as

\displaystyle f*g=m\big(\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes\triangleright)(f\otimes g)\big)

for all formal power series
f
,
g
in
x_{\mu}

[
30
]
. In particular, for
f={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}
and
g={\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}
,

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}*{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}=m\big(\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes\triangleright)\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\otimes{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\big)\big)=:{\rm e}^{\mathcal{A}(u;k,q,x)},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle kx=k_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad qx=q_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}

are elements of the Minkowski space-time algebra, the function
\mathcal{A}
is implicitly defined by (
2.4
) and
m
denotes the multiplication map on usual functions. Using the actions of
P
and
D
on
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}
, it follows that

\displaystyle P\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}=(v\cdot k){\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx},\qquad P\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}=(v\cdot q){\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx},

where
(v\cdot k)=v_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
,
(v\cdot q)=v_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}
.
For
j<l
,

\displaystyle\left.\left(P^{j}\binom{D}{l}\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right)\right|_{x=0}=0,

\displaystyle\left.\left(P^{n}\binom{D}{n}\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right)\right|_{x=0}=(v\cdot k)^{n},

\displaystyle\left.\left(P^{n+1}\binom{D}{n}\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right)\right|_{x=0}=(n+1)(v\cdot k)^{n+1}.

The following identities hold

\displaystyle\binom{D}{n}\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}=\frac{({\rm i}kx)^{n}}{n!}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx},\qquad n\in\mathbf{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\left.\left(P^{j}\binom{D}{l}\triangleright{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right)\right|_{x=0}=\left.\left(P^{j}\triangleright\frac{({\rm i}kx)^{l}}{l!}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right)\right|_{x=0}=\binom{j}{l}(v_{\alpha}k_{\alpha})^{j}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}*{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\big)\big|_{x=0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}\right)^{n}(v\cdot k)^{n}(v\cdot q)^{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}* {\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\big)\big|_{x=0}}{}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}(v\cdot k)(v\cdot q)}={\rm e}^{\mathcal{A}(u;k,q,x)}\left.\right|_{x=0}.

(2.5)

We now calculate the partial derivatives of the star product,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}*{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\big)\big|_{x=0}=\sum_{j,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{j+l}\left\{{\rm i}k_{\mu}((u-1)P)^{j}\triangleright\left[\frac{(v\cdot k)^{l}}{l!}+\frac{(v\cdot k)^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}\right]{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right\}

\displaystyle{}\times\left\{(uP)^{l}\triangleright\frac{(v\cdot q)^{j}}{j!}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\right\}\bigg|_{x=0}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{j+l}\left.\left\{((u-1)P)^{j}\triangleright\frac{(v\cdot k)^{l}}{l!}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}\right\}\left\{{\rm i}q_{\mu}(vP)^{l}\triangleright\left[\frac{(v\cdot q)^{j}}{j!}+\frac{(v\cdot q)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\right]\right\}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle={\rm i}(k_{\mu}+q_{\mu})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}\right)^{n}(v\cdot k)^{n}(v\cdot q)^{n}+{\rm i}(k_{\mu}+q_{\mu})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}\right)^{n}n(v\cdot k)^{n}

\displaystyle+(v\cdot q)^{n}+{\rm i}\left[k_{\mu}\frac{u}{\kappa}(v\cdot q)+q_{\mu}\frac{(u-1)}{\kappa}(v\cdot k)\right]\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}\right)^{n}(n+1)(v\cdot k)^{n}(v\cdot q)^{n}

\displaystyle={\rm i}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}(k,q)\frac{1}{1-\frac{u(u-1)}{\kappa^{2}}(v\cdot k)(v\cdot q)}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx}*{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qx}\big)\big|_{x=0}={\rm i}\left(k_{\mu}\left(1+\frac{u}{\kappa}(v\cdot q)\right)+q_{\mu}\left(1+\frac{u-1}{\kappa}(v\cdot k)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\binom{D}{k}P=P\binom{D-1}{k}.

We calculate the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{{\rm GZ},u}^{-1}\left(p_{\mu}\otimes\left(1+\frac{u}{\kappa}P\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{u-1}{\kappa}P\right)^{k}\binom{D}{l}\otimes\left(\frac{uP}{\kappa}\right)^{l}\binom{D}{k}\right)\left(p_{\mu}\otimes 1+\frac{u}{\kappa}p_{\mu}\otimes P\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{(u-1)P}{\kappa}\right)^{k}\binom{D}{l}\otimes\left(\frac{uP}{\kappa}\right)^{l}\binom{D}{k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\quad{}+\left(\frac{(u-1)P}{\kappa}\right)^{k}\binom{D}{l}\otimes\left(\frac{uP}{\kappa}\right)^{l+1}\binom{D-1}{k}\right)(p_{\mu}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\quad{}-\left(\frac{(u-1)P}{\kappa}\right)^{k+1}\binom{D}{l}\otimes\left(\frac{uP}{\kappa}\right)^{l+1}\binom{D-1}{k}\right)(p_{\mu}\otimes 1).

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\left(\frac{(u-1)P}{\kappa}\right)^{k}l\binom{D}{l-1}\otimes\left(\frac{uP}{\kappa}\right)^{l}\frac{k}{D}\binom{D}{k}

3

Another construction for a generalized Jordanian twist is possible

[

is evidently obtained from

by an inner automorphism. Regarding that cohomologous twists give isomorphic mathematical objects, one sometimes thinks of these twists as gauge equivalent.

If

is a simple Jordanian twist, and

[

4.2

In the following proposition we state the conditions under which the two twists are equal, along with a simple proof.

.

Appendix

Lemma A.1
.

If
x
,
y
,
z
are mutually commuting variables, and
k
,
l
,
A
,
C
with
A\leq k
,
C\leq l
nonnegative integers, then

\displaystyle\binom{x}{l-C}\sum_{k_{1}=k-A}^{k}\binom{k_{1}}{k-A}\binom{z}{k_{1}}\binom{x+y-k-l+k_{1}+C}{C}\binom{y}{k-k_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\binom{z}{k-A}\sum_{l_{1}=l-C}^{l}\binom{l_{1}}{l-C}\binom{x}{l_{1}}\binom{y+z-k-l+l_{1}+A}{A}\binom{y}{l-l_{1}}.

(A.1)

Proof A.2
.

To make the proof more transparent, we make a change of summation indices

\displaystyle i=k_{1}-k+A,\qquad j=l_{1}-l+C,

hence
k_{1}=i+k-A
and
l_{1}=j+l-C
, to restate equation (
A.1
) as

\displaystyle\binom{x}{l-C}\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{i+k-A}{k-A}\binom{z}{i+k-A}\binom{x+y-l+i-A+C}{C}\binom{y}{A-i}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\binom{z}{k-A}\sum_{j=0}^{C}\binom{j+l-C}{l-C}\binom{x}{j+l-C}\binom{y+z-l+j-C+A}{A}\binom{y}{C-j}.

(A.2)

We remind the reader of the simple identity

\displaystyle\binom{r}{s}\binom{w}{r}=\binom{w}{s}\binom{w-s}{r-s},

which we apply in (
A.2
) for
w=z
on the left and for
w=x
on the right, to obtain an equivalent statement,

\displaystyle\binom{x}{l-C}\binom{z}{k-A}\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{z-k+A}{i}\binom{x+y-l+i-A+C}{C}\binom{y}{A-i}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\binom{z}{k-A}\binom{x}{l-C}\sum_{j=0}^{C}\binom{x}{j+l-C}\binom{y+z-k+j-C+A}{A}\binom{y}{C-j}.

(A.3)

We expand

\displaystyle\binom{x+y-l+i-A+C}{C}=\sum_{j=0}^{C}\binom{x-l+C}{j}\binom{y+i-A}{C-j}

on the left-hand side, and

\displaystyle\binom{y+z-k+j-C+A}{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom{z-k+A}{i}\binom{y+j-C}{A-i}

on the right-hand side of (
A.3
). Now both sides involve double summation over
i
and
j
. For each fixed pair
(i,j)
, compare the corresponding summands on the two sides. The factors involving
x
and
z
are identical on both sides. It remains to check that the factors involving
y
agree. Indeed, by definition,

\displaystyle\binom{y+i-A}{C-j}\binom{y}{A-i}=\frac{y(y-1)\cdots(y+i-A+j-C+1)}{(C-j)!(A-i)!}=\binom{y+j-C}{A-i}\binom{y}{C-j}.\!\!\!\!

∎

Chemical mapping of temperate sub-Neptune atmospheres: Constraining the deep-interior H 2 ​ O \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} / H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} ratio from the atmospheric CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} / CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} ratio
Jeehyun Yang, Renyu Hu

Abstract
Understanding the planetary envelope composition of sub-Neptune-type exoplanets is challenging due to the inherent degeneracy in their interior composition scenarios. Particularly, the planetary envelope’s H 2 ​ O \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} / H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} ratio, which can also be expressed as the O/H ratio, provides crucial insights into its original location relative to the ice line during planetary formation. Using self-consistent radiative transfer modeling and a rate-based automatic chemical network generator combined with 1D photochemical kinetic-transport atmospheric modeling, we investigate various atmospheric scenarios of temperate sub-Neptunes, ranging from H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} -dominated to H 2 ​ O \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} -dominated atmospheres with equilibrium temperatures ( T eq ) of 250–400 K. This study includes examples such as K2-18 b ( T eq = 255 K), LP 791-18 c ( T eq = 324 K), and TOI-270 d ( T eq = 354 K). Our models indicate that the atmospheric CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} / CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} ratio can be used to infer the deep-interior H 2 ​ O \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} / H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} ratio. Applying this method to recent JWST observations, our findings suggest K2-18 b likely has an interior that is 50% highly enriched in water, exceeding the water content in a 100 × \times Z ⊙ \odot scenario and suggesting a planetary formation mechanism involving substantial accretion of ices. In contrast, our model suggests that approximately 25% of TOI-270 d’s interior is composed of H 2 ​ O \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} , which aligns with the conventional metallicity framework with a metallicity higher than 100 × \times Z ⊙ \odot . Furthermore, our models identify carbonyl sulfide (OCS) and sulfur dioxide ( SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} ) as strong indicators for temperate sub-Neptunes with at least 10% of their interior composed of water. These results provide a method to delineate the internal composition and formation mechanisms of temperate sub-Neptunes ( T eq < ∼ T_{\rm eq}<\sim 500 K) via atmospheric characterization through transmission spectroscopy.

This characterization might help distinguish between different pathways of sub-Neptune formation. For example, ‘

Based on the elemental scenarios outlined in Section

After the models had converged and reached the steady state, we computed the synthetic transmission spectra of K2-18 b and TOI-270 d based on the molecular mixing ratio profiles, using the transmission spectra generation module of EPACRIS

III.4
Discussions

In this study, we propose measuring the abundances of
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

\mu

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
to
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
ratio of temperate sub-Neptunes. Additionally, we suggest that other species such as CO,
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, OCS, and

(see Section

) could provide further constraints on the envelope composition despite uncertainties in the deep-interior temperature. Notably, the inference of the envelope

As shown in Figure B

The equilibrium temperature of LP791-18 c falls between these two different regimes, presenting possible degenerate scenarios. Therefore, by performing chemical mapping of such temperate sub-Neptunes (e.g., LP791-18 c), we can cover a broad range of temperate sub-Neptunes with equilibrium temperatures (

IV
Conclusions

In this study, using self-consistent radiative transfer modeling
[
56
]
and a rate-based automatic chemical network generator combined with 1D photochemical kinetic-transport atmospheric modeling
[
74
]
, we have extensively investigated various atmospheric scenarios of temperate sub-Neptunes with equilibrium temperatures (
T
eq
) ranging from 250 to 400 K. We introduce a new framework that utilizes the atmospheric
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
/
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
ratio to gauge the bulk envelope
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
/
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
ratio of temperate sub-Neptunes, providing new insights into their formation locations relative to the ice-line. Furthermore, our models suggest that any potential detection of OCS and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
serves as strong indicators of at least a 10% water-rich envelope in temperate sub-Neptunes.

Benchmarking with recent JWST observations of two well-known temperate sub-Neptunes, K2-18b
[
39
]
and TOI-270d
[
4
]
, our modeling results suggest the following: The most probable scenario for TOI-270d’s envelope is

4
]
. Furthermore, our synthesized transmission spectra suggest the potential detection of carbonyl sulfide (OCS) at approximately 4.8–4.9

While TOI-270 d can be explained by conventional multipliers of solar metallicity, yielding approximately 230

Serge Bouc, Ivo Dell’Ambrogio, Rubén Martos

Abstract
. In equivariant topology, Greenlees and May used Mackey functors to show that, rationally, the stable homotopy category of G G -spectra over a finite group G G splits as a product of simpler module categories. We extend the algebraic part (also independently proved by Thévenaz and Webb) of this classical result to Mackey modules over an arbitrary Green functor, and use the case of the complex representation ring Green functor to obtain an algebraic model of the rational equivariant Kasparov category of G G -cell algebras.

1.
Introduction and results

Let
G
be a finite group, which we fix throughout the article.

In equivariant stable homotopy theory, a well-known result of Greenlees–May
[
GM95
, App. A]
states that the stable homotopy category of rational
G
-spectra splits as a product of module categories

(1.1)

\SH(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\underset{\text{Cl}(H),\ H\leq G}{\prod}\mathbb{Q}[W_{G}(H)]\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{Z}},

one for each
G
-conjugacy class
\mathrm{Cl}(H)
of subgroups
H\leq G
. Here
W_{G}(H)=N_{G}(H)/H
denotes the Weyl group of
H
in
G
, and the corresponding factor is the semisimple abelian category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded modules over the rational group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[W_{G}(H)]
(the decoration
(\ldots)^{\mathbb{Z}}
indicates graded objects).
This provides a canonical decomposition and an explicit algebraic model for rational equivariant cohomology theories and is thus of fundamental importance in equivariant topology.

The main result of this article is a generalization of the algebraic side of the story to general Green functors
[
Bou97
]
.
If
R
is any Green functor for
G

4.3
) that the construction of Brauer quotients defines a functor

(1.3)

\Br^{R}\colon R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey(G)\longrightarrow\underset{\mathrm{Cl}(H),\ H\leq G}{\prod}\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\,\textsf{-}\Mod

from the category of Mackey
R

i.e.
Mackey functors equipped with a left

-action) to a product of module categories over certain skew group rings
\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)

\overline{R}(H)
is the Brauer quotient of

W_{G}(H)
on it.
We prove:

A Theorem

(
cf.

4.5
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
R
a Green functor for
G
, and
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
a subring of the rationals containing
|G|^{-1}
.
Denote by
R_{Q}
the Green functor obtained by tensoring

Then (

) induces an equivalence

R_{Q}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey(G)\simeq\underset{\mathrm{Cl}(H),\ H\leq G}{\prod}Q\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\,\textsf{-}\Mod.

By taking
R
to be the Burnside ring Green functor, one recovers as a special case the Greenlees–May / Thévenaz–Webb rational splitting for plain Mackey functors.

It is known that the Greenlees-May splitting (
1.1

G
-spectra on the left-hand side and the usual product of group representations on the right-hand side; see

On the algebraic side, this is essentially contained in

B Theorem

(

5.1
)
.

For any commutative Green functor

for

C Theorem

(

Section

For any finite group

\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq{\prod_{\mathrm{Cl}(H),\,H\leq G\;\textrm{cyclic}}}\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{|H|})\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\,\textsf{-}\Mod^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\aleph_{1}}

For the right-hand side, we choose a cyclic subgroup

In particular

).

2.
Skew group rings

Suppose a finite group
W
acts on a ring
S
by ring automorphisms, say via the group homomorphism
\alpha\colon W\to\Aut(S)
,
w\mapsto\alpha_{w}
.
The
skew group ring
for this action, denoted

S\rtimes_{\alpha}W,

is defined to be the free
S
-module
\bigoplus_{w\in W}Sw
on the set
W
, equipped with the multiplication defined by additively extending the rule

(sw)(s^{\prime}w^{\prime}):=(s\alpha_{w}(s^{\prime}))(ww^{\prime})

in both variables.
Note that
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
is a unital and associative ring but
not
an
S
-algebra, indeed its multiplication is not
S
-bilinear.

2.1 Remark
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is any commutative ring, there is a unique extension of
c
to a
W
-action
\tilde{c}
on the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\mathbb{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S
, and there is an evident isomorphism

(\mathbb{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S)\rtimes_{\tilde{c}}W\cong\mathbb{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(S\rtimes_{c}W)

of
\mathbb{K}
-algebras. Accordingly, we may safely omit parentheses when extending scalars in a group ring,
e.g.
as in the statement of our main theorems with
\mathbb{K}=Q
.

2.2 Remark
.

The abelian category

S\rtimes_{\alpha}W\,\textsf{-}\Mod

of left modules over the ring
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
admits the following alternative description.
An object
V=(V,\varphi)
is determined by an
S
-module
V
equipped with a
W
-action
\varphi
on the underlying abelian group of
V
, which we will write as a set of abelian group automorphisms

\varphi_{w}\colon V\overset{\sim}{\to}V\quad(w\in W)

satisfying
\varphi_{w}\varphi_{w^{\prime}}=\varphi_{ww^{\prime}}
and
\varphi_{e}=\id_{V}
.
Moreover, the
S
-action and the
W
-action are compatible in the sense that

(2.3)

\varphi_{w}(s\cdot v)=\alpha_{w}(s)\cdot\varphi_{w}(v)

for all
w\in W
,
s\in S
and
v\in V
.
These two actions are obtained by restricting the given
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
-action along the inclusions
S\to S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
,
s\mapsto se
, and
W\to S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
,
w\mapsto 1_{S}w
.
Conversely, two such actions satisfying the above compatibility extend to a unique left
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
-action on the abelian group
V
. A morphism
\xi\colon(V,\varphi)\to(V^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime})
of left modules is the same as an
S
-linear map
V\to V^{\prime}
which is equivariant for the group actions:
\varphi^{\prime}_{w}\circ\xi=\xi\circ\varphi_{w}
for all
w\in W
.

2.4 Example
.

If the action
\alpha
is trivial,
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
is the usual group algebra
S[W]
.

The classical Maschke theorem for group algebras also holds for skew group rings:

2.5 Lemma

(
[
Mon80
, Corollary 0.2.(1)]
)
.

If
S
is a semisimple Artinian ring (
e.g.
a field) containing
|W|^{-1}
, then
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
is also a semisimple Artinian ring.
In particular, all objects in the abelian category
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W\,\textsf{-}\Mod
are projective.
∎

2.6 Remark
.

When the ring
S
is commutative,
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W\,\textsf{-}\Mod
admits a symmetric monoidal structure where
(V,\varphi)\otimes(V^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}):=(V\otimes_{S}V^{\prime},\varphi\otimes\varphi^{\prime})
for the diagonal action
(\varphi\otimes\varphi^{\prime})_{w}=\varphi_{w}\otimes\varphi_{w}
(
w\in W
), and where

f\otimes f^{\prime}:=f\otimes_{S}f^{\prime}
on morphisms. The associative, unit and symmetry isomorphisms are those of the underlying
S
-modules.
The tensor unit object
\mathbbm{1}
is provided by
(S,\alpha)
.

3.
Mackey modules over a Green functor

A
Mackey functor

M
for the finite group
G
consists of an abelian group
M(H)
for every subgroup
H\leq G
together with three families of group homomorphisms

\displaystyle\res^{H}_{K}\colon M(H)\to M(K),\quad\ind^{H}_{K}\colon M(K)\to M(H),\quad c_{g,H}\colon M(H)\to M({}^{g}H)

for all subgroups
K\leq H\leq G
and elements
g\in G
,
respectively called
restriction
,
induction
and
conjugation
maps; these are subject to certain relations.
(We use the standard notations
{}^{g}H=gHg^{-1}
and
H^{g}=g^{-1}Hg
for the conjugates of a subgroup
H\leq G
by an element
g\in G
.)
Mackey functors (for
G
) form an abelian category
\Mackey:=\Mackey_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)
, where a morphism
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
is a family of homomorphisms
f_{H}\colon M(H)\to M^{\prime}(H)
for all
H\leq G
, commuting with all restriction, induction and conjugation maps.

A
Green functor
is a Mackey functor
R
for which each
R(H)
is an (associative and unital) ring, restriction and conjugation maps are ring maps, and which satisfies the two
Frobenius relations

\ind_{K}^{H}(\res_{K}^{H}(r)\cdot r^{\prime})=r\cdot\ind_{K}^{H}(r^{\prime}),\quad\ind_{K}^{H}(r\cdot\res_{K}^{H}(r^{\prime}))=\ind_{K}^{H}(r)\cdot r^{\prime}.

A (left)
Mackey module
over the Green functor
R
is a Mackey functor
M
such that
M(H)
is endowed with the structure of a (left)
R(H)
-module (
H\leq G
), whose structure maps satisfy the analogs of the above Frobenius relations, and whose restriction and conjugation maps commute with the ring actions in the evident way (see
[
Bou97
, §1.1 and §2.1]
for all details on the axioms).

Mackey
R
-modules form an abelian category

R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey:=R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)

where a morphism
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
is a morphism of the underlying Mackey functors such that each
f_{H}\colon M(H)\to M^{\prime}(H)
is an
R(H)
-linear map.

3.1 Example
.

The first example of a Green functor is the
Burnside ring
Green functor, which in this article we denote by
B
.
It is commutative and acts uniquely on all Mackey functors, so that
B\,\textsf{-}\Mackey=\Mackey
.
See
[
Bou97
, §2.4]
.

3.2 Recollection
.

Besides the above
subgroups picture
of Mackey functors, the alternative
G
-set (or Dress) picture
is often very useful.
According to the latter, a Mackey functor is a pair
M=(M^{*},M_{*})
of functors
M^{*}\colon G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Ab
and
M\colon G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set}\to\Ab
(a “bifunctor”) agreeing on objects,
M^{*}(X)=M_{*}(X)=:M(X)
for all finite
G
-sets
X
, and which send disjoint unions to direct sums and satisfy a base-change condition for pullbacks.
A morphism
M\to M^{\prime}
is a transformation
\{f_{X}\colon M(X)\to M^{\prime}(X)\}_{X}
which is natural for both functorialities.
The equivalence of the two definitions takes a bifunctor
(M^{*},M_{*})
and yields a Mackey functor for the subgroups picture by setting
M(H):=M(G/H)
for all subgroups
H\leq G
as well as
\res^{H}_{K}:=M^{*}(G/K\twoheadrightarrow G/H)
,
\ind^{G}_{K}:=M_{*}(G/K\twoheadrightarrow G/H)
, and
c_{g,H}:=M^{*}(G/{{}^{g}H}\overset{\sim}{\to}G/H)
(here
G/K\twoheadrightarrow G/H
is the quotient map and
G/{}^{g}H\overset{\sim}{\to}G/H
is the isomorphism
\gamma{}^{g}H\to\gamma gH
).
Conversely, a subgroups-picture Mackey functor extends essentially uniquely to a bifunctor
(M^{*},M_{*})
via the same formulas, by decomposing
G
-sets into orbits. See
[
Bou97
, §1.1.2]
.

3.3 Recollection
.

If the Green functor
R
is commutative,
R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey
admits a symmetric monoidal structure.
We briefly recall its definition from
[
Bou97
, §6.6]
in terms of the
G
-set picture (
3.2
).
Given two
R
-modules
M,N
, the value at
X\in G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set}
of their tensor product
M\otimes_{R}N
is

\big(M\otimes_{R}N\big)(X):=\left(\bigoplus_{\theta:Y\to X}M(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N(Y)\right)/\mathcal{J},

where the sum is taken over all maps
\theta
of finite
G
-sets, and
\mathcal{J}
is the subgroup generated by the following three sets of relations:

(3.4)

\displaystyle M_{*}(f)(m)\otimes n^{\prime}-m\otimes

\displaystyle N^{*}(f)(n^{\prime}),\quad M^{*}(f)(m^{\prime})\otimes n-m^{\prime}\otimes M_{*}(f)(m^{\prime}),

(3.5)

\displaystyle(r\cdot m)\otimes n-m\otimes(r\cdot n)

for all
m\in M(Y)
,
n\in N(Y)
,
m^{\prime}\in M(Y^{\prime})
,
n^{\prime}\in N(Y^{\prime})
,
r\in R(Y)
, and all
G
-set maps
f\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
with
\theta^{\prime}f=\theta
.
Here
M^{*}
and
M_{*}
are the two functorialities of
M
, extending the restriction & conjugation maps and the induction maps of the subgroup picture, respectively.

If we only quotient out the relations (
3.4
), we get the value at
X
of the tensor product
M\otimes_{B}N
(denoted
M\hat{\otimes}N
in
[
Bou97
]
) of the underlying plain Mackey functors,
i.e.
the special case when
R
is the Burnside ring
B
.
The structure maps of the Mackey functor
M\otimes_{R}N
are inherited via the quotient map
M\otimes_{B}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
from those of
M\otimes_{B}N
, as described in
[
Bou97
, Prop. 1.6.2]
.

The tensor unit is
R
, and the unitality, associativity and symmetry isomorphisms of the tensor structure are inherited from those of
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}
in the obvious way.

4.
Brauer quotients of Mackey modules

In order to precisely state
A
, we still need to define Brauer quotients and the rationalization of a Green functor.
Slightly more generally:

4.1 Notation
.

For any subring
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
and any Green functor
R
, we write
R_{Q}
for the
Q
-linearization of
R
, that is the Green functor defined by
R_{Q}(H):=Q\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R(H)
for all
H\leq G
, and similarly for the structural maps.
The module category
R_{Q}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey
can be identified with the full subcategory of
R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey
containing all
Q
-local
(or
Q
-linear
) Mackey modules,
i.e.
those taking values in
Q
-modules.
(If necessary, recall that every subring
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
is a localization of
\mathbb{Z}
.)
The
rationalization
of
R
is the Green functor
R_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the case when
Q=\mathbb{Q}
.

4.2 Definition

(
[
Thé95
, §54]
)
.

For any Mackey functor
M
for
G
and subgroup
H\leq G
, the
Brauer quotient
of
M
at
H
is the quotient of abelian groups

\overline{M}(H):=M(H)/\left(\sum_{H^{\prime}\lneq H}\ind_{H^{\prime}}^{H}(M(H^{\prime}))\right)

obtained by killing all elements induced from proper subgroups of
H
.

4.3 Remarks
.

Let
M
be a Mackey functor and
H\leq G
a subgroup. In a series of successive easy observations, we will turn the Brauer quotient construction into a suitable functor
\Br_{H}
.

(a)

For
g\in N_{G}(H)
, the conjugation map
c_{g}:=c_{g,H}\colon M(H)\to M({}^{g}H)
is an automorphism of
M(H)=M({}^{g}H)
.
By an axiom of Mackey functors,
c_{g}=\id
whenever
g\in H
.
We thus obtain an action of
W_{G}(H)
on
M(H)
.
By another axiom of Mackey functors, namely the commutation relations

c_{g}\ind^{H}_{H^{\prime}}=\ind^{{}^{g}H}_{{}^{g}H^{\prime}}c_{g}\quad(\textrm{for }g\in G,H^{\prime}\leq H\leq G),

this
W_{G}(H)
-action descends on the Brauer quotient
\overline{M}(H)
.
We call this action (both on
M(H)
and
\overline{M}(H)
) the
conjugation action
and still denote it by
c
.

(b)

For a Green functor
R
, the subgroup
\sum_{H^{\prime}<H}\ind_{H^{\prime}}^{H}(R(H))\subseteq R(H)
is an ideal thanks to the (left) Frobenius relation.
Therefore
\overline{R}(H)
is a ring.

(c)

By definition, if
M=R
is a Green functor the conjugation action on
R(H)
is by ring automorphisms. This descends to
\overline{R}(H)
by part (b), hence we may construct the skew group ring

\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)

as in §
2
.

(d)

If
M
is a Mackey module over the Green functor
R
, the Brauer quotient
\overline{M}(H)
becomes a left
\overline{R}(H)
-module.
This follows similarly to (b) and using the left Frobenius axiom for Mackey modules.

(e)

Altogether, when
M
is a Mackey module over
R
, the conjugation action of (a) and the
\overline{R}(H)
-action of (d) turn
\overline{M}(H)
into a left
\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)
-module.
Indeed, this follows immediately from the Mackey module axiom

c_{g}(r\cdot m)=c_{g}(r)\cdot c_{g}(m)

(for
g\in G
,
r\in R(H)
,
m\in M(H)
) together with
2.2
.

(f)

Suppose
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
is a morphism of Mackey
R
-modules.
Since the components of
f
commute with the induction maps of
M
and
M^{\prime}
,
f_{H}
descends to a homomorphism
\overline{f_{H}}\colon\overline{M}(H)\to\overline{M^{\prime}}(H)
, which is
\overline{R}(H)
-linear because

-linear by definition.
Since the components of

4.4 Definition
.

Write

\Br_{H}\colon R\,\textsf{-}\Mackey\longrightarrow\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\,\textsf{-}\Mod

for the functor resulting from

, defined by

on objects and

on morphisms. We can now state:

4.5 Theorem
.

For any finite group

and any Green functor

for

for any subring

containing

(see

).

4.6 Remark
.

(

5.
Comparing the tensor structures

We now consider the tensor structures on our categories.

5.1 Theorem
.

Suppose
R
is a commutative Green functor for
G
.
Then for each
H\leq G
the Brauer quotient functor
\Br_{H}
of
4.4
is strong symmetric monoidal with respect to the tensor structures of

and
2.6

Therefore the functor

of

is symmetric monoidal (where its target category is equipped with the product tensor structure), and it induces an equivalence

R_{Q}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey\simeq{\underset{\mathrm{Cl}(H),\ H\leq G}{\prod}Q\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\overline{R}(H)\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\,\textsf{-}\Mod}

Proof.

As with
4.5
, we are essentially going to reduce the proof to the case of the Burnside Green functor

.
As mentioned in the introduction, in this case the result is known to be true by experts but we nonetheless provide a (sketch) of proof for completeness.

We need to find a symmetric monoidal structure on

For the second one, consider the following commutative diagram of abelian groups:

(5.3)

By one of the main results of
[
Del14
, §4]
, we know that the usual equivariant K-theory groups
k^{G}(A)(H):=K^{H}_{0}(A)\oplus K^{H}_{1}(A)
of each object
A\in\mathsf{KK}^{G}
can be naturally upgraded to form a Mackey module
k^{G}(A)
over the Green functor
\mathrm{R}^{G}
, as in
Section

3
, which moreover is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded and countable.
(Alternatively, the same conclusion can also be deduced from the Green 2-functor structure of
G\mapsto\mathsf{KK}^{G}
via Hom-decategorification; see
[
Del22
, Example 12.20]
.)
After restricting to
G
-cell algebras, this defines a functor

k^{G}\colon\mathsf{Cell}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathsf{KK}^{G}\longrightarrow\mathrm{R}^{G}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\aleph_{1}}

into the category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded countable Mackey
\mathrm{R}^{G}
-modules.
As a homological functor into an abelian category, it can be used to do relative homological algebra in
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
and set up spectral sequences.
This is the content of
[
Del14
]
.
By restricting further to rational
G
-cell algebras, we obtain a (simpler) homological functor

(6.3)

k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\colon\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\aleph_{1}}

into the category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded countable Mackey modules over
\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}

Proof of
C
.

First notice that the abelian category on the right-hand side is semisimple by
2.5
, since the coefficient ring in each skew group ring is a field, and because the graded countable modules in a semisimple category of modules again form a semisimple abelian category.

We claim that (
6.3
) is a tensor equivalence; by composing it with the tensor equivalence of
6.2
(in the graded countable variant as in
4.6
), we then get the desired result.

To prove the claim, we may for instance set up relative homological algebra as in
[
Del14
]
except that we use the homological functor
k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
instead of
k^{G}
.
It follows by rationalizing both sides of
[
Del14
, Thm. 4.9]
, together with
[
Del14
, Prop. 5.6]
, that (
6.3
) is the universal
\mathcal{I}
-exact stable homological functor for the homological ideal
\mathcal{I}:=\ker(k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of morphisms in
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
The associated UCT spectral sequence
[
Del14
, Thm. 5.12]
collapses on the first page for all
A,B\in\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, which implies that (
6.3
) is a fully faithful functor.
It is also essentially surjective, as the objects
k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}(G/H))
for
H\leq G
generate the whole category of Mackey modules through direct sums and retracts (again by semisimplicity, and because they are projective generators).
Thus (
6.3
) is an equivalence.

To see that is it a symmetric monoidal functor, note that the (rational analogue) of
[
Del14
, Lemma 5.15]
yields for every subgroup
H\leq G
a canonical isomorphism

k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}(G/H))\otimes_{\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(B)\overset{\sim}{\to}k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\big(\mathrm{C}(G/H)\otimes B\big)

in
\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,\textsf{-}\Mackey^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\aleph_{1}}
, natural in
B\in\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Using the semisimplicity of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we can easily extend these to an isomorphism

k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)\otimes_{\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}}k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(B)\overset{\sim}{\to}k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\big(A\otimes B\big)

natural in
A,B\in\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
This provides the required monoidal structure on (
6.3
), where the unit component

\mathrm{R}^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\overset{\sim}{\to}k^{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C})
is the usual identification of the representation ring with the equivariant K-theory of a point.
∎

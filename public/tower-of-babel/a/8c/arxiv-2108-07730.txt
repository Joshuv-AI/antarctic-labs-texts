On the role of the subsidiary fields in the momentum and angular momentum in covariantly quantized QED and QCD
Elliot Leader

Abstract
The covariant quantization of QED and QCD requires the introduction of subsidiary gauge-fixing and ghost fields and it is crucial to understand the role of these in the energy-momentum tensor, and in the momentum and angular momentum operators. These issues were studied in Leader 2011 , and key results from this study were utilized in the major review of angular momentum in Leader and Lorce 2013 . Damski Damski 2021 has rightly criticized as incorrect the derivation of certain equations in Leader 2011 . We show, however, that the key results of Leader 2011 which are utilized in Leader and Lorce 2013 are unaffected by Damski’s criticism.

I
Introduction

It is well known that the covariant quantization of QED and QCD i.e. in which the photon vector potential
A^{\mu}(x)
and the gluon vector potential
A^{\mu}_{a}
transform as genuine Lorentz 4-vectors, is a non-trivial task
Lautrup 1967
;
Nakanishi 1966
;
Nakanishi 1972
;
Kugo and Ojima 1979
involving the introduction of a scalar gauge-fixing field (Gf) in QED and both a gauge-fixing field and Faddeev-Popov ghosts fields (Gf+Gh) in QCD. In both QED and QCD the expressions for the linear and angular momentum
operators
include terms involving all these fields.

As explained in detail in
Leader 2011
it is necessary to work in an indefinite-metric space i.e. one in which the “length” or norm of a vector can be either positive or negative and the definition of the “physical states” with positive norm has to specified with care. It is also necessary to specify the condition for an operator to represent a physically measurable quantity i.e. to be an “observable”.

The situation is further complicated by the fact that it is possible to deal with different versions of the energy-momentum tensor
t^{\mu\nu}
, of which the two most important are the Canonical version
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}
which follows from Noether’s theorem and the Bellinfante version
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}
which is symmetric under
(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)
and which differs from
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}
by a divergence term, as will be spelled out in detail below. Both these versions of the energy-momentum tensor are conserved quantities. Based on these one can define, in the standard way, the momentum operators
P^{\mu}_{can}
and
P^{\mu}_{bel}
and the angular momentum operators
J^{i}_{can}
and
J^{i}_{bel}
. Because the energy-momentum tensors are related by a divergence one can show that for the matrix elements between any normalizable physical states,
\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}\,|\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{can}\,|\Psi\rangle
and
\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{bel}\,|\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{can}\,|\Psi\rangle
.

Of key physical interest in QCD is the question of the fraction of the momentum and angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons in a hadron, with analogous questions about electrons and photons in QED. Clearly to answer these questions one has to know the contributions of the subsidiary fields to the physical matrix elements of the above operators. In all papers dealing with the QCD case it is
assumed, without comment
, that the contribution from the subsidiary fields is zero. A proof of this was given in
Leader 2011
, which was based on the demonstration that for physical matrix elements in QED
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=0
and for QCD
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0
.

Damski
Damski 2021
has pointed out that the proof for both the Canonical and Bellinfante cases in QED is wrong, because it assumes that the physical states alone form a complete set and thus uses
1=\sum|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|
, which is an incorrect “resolution of the identity”, because it leaves out the states of negative norm. Although he does not comment on QCD, Damski’s argument shows that the proof that
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0
in
Leader 2011
is also incorrect.

This criticism seems to imply that the conclusion reached in
Leader 2011
that the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the momentum and angular momentum is false, but as will be shown, this implication is wrong.

We shall show the following:

a) while, indeed, for the Canonical case

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0

(1)

on the contrary, for the Bellinfante case, as claimed in
Leader 2011

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(2)

b) despite Eq. (
1
) one has

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0

(3)

and obviously an analogous result for the Bellinfante case as a consequence of (
2
),
which are key results utilized in the major review
Leader and Lorce 2013
of the ”Angular Momentum Controversy”.

c) despite Eq, (
1
) it turns out that the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the physical matrix elements of either the Canonical or Bellinfante versions of the momentum or angular momentum. This crucial result, as mentioned above, is normally assumed without comment in papers on QCD.

II
Physical matrix elements of the Bellinfante energy-momentum tensor

We shall show, as claimed in
Leader 2011
, that the physical matrix elements of the gauge-fixing and ghost
contributions
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)
in QCD and the gauge-fixing contribution
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)
in QED,
vanish. The proof given for the QED case in
Leader 2011
, as pointed out by Damski
Damski 2021
, is incorrect, but the result is actually true .
Surprisingly it turns out that the QCD case is simpler to deal with than the QED case , which can be derived as
a special case.

II.1
Quantum Chromodynamics

The pure quark-gluon Lagrangian
{\cal{L}}_{qG}
is

{\cal{L}}_{qG}=-\frac{1}{4}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}^{l}[\delta_{lm}\,i\,(\overrightarrow{\not\!{\partial}}-\overleftarrow{\not\!{\partial}})-2\,gt^{a}_{lm}\,\not{\negthickspace}{A}^{a}]\psi^{m}.

(4)

In order to quantize the theory covariantly one has to introduce both a gauge-fixing field
B(x)
and Fadeev-Popov anti-commuting fermionic ghost fields
c(x),\,\bar{c}(x)
. The Kugo-Ojima Lagrangian
Kugo and Ojima 1978
for the covariantly quantized theory is then

{\cal{L}}={\cal{L}}_{qG}+{\cal{L}}_{Gf+Gh}

(5)

where

{\cal{L}}_{Gf+Gh}=-i(\partial^{\mu}\bar{c}^{a})D^{ab}_{\mu}c_{b}-(\partial^{\mu}B^{a})A^{a}_{\mu}+\frac{\textsf{a}}{2}B^{a}B^{a}.

(6)

The physical states
|\Psi\rangle
are defined by the subsidiary conditions

Q_{B}|\Psi\rangle=0

(7)

Q_{c}|\Psi\rangle=0

(8)

where the conserved, hermitian charge
Q_{B}
is given by

Q_{B}=\int d^{3}x[B^{a}{\overleftrightarrow{\partial}}_{0}c^{a}-gB^{a}f_{abc}A^{b}_{0}c^{c}-i(g/2)(\partial_{0}\bar{c}^{a})f_{abc}c^{b}c^{c}].

(9)

and the conserved charge
Q_{c}

Q_{c}=\int d^{3}x[\bar{c}^{a}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{0}c^{a}-g\bar{c}^{a}f_{abc}A^{b}_{0}c^{c}]

(10)

“measures” the
ghost number

i[Q_{c},\phi]=N\phi

(11)

where
N=1
for
\phi=c,\,\,-1\,\,\textrm{for}\,\,\phi=\bar{c}\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,0
for all other fields.

The Bellinfante energy-momentum tensor is

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}=t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)+t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)

(12)

where

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)=\frac{i}{4}[\bar{\psi}_{l}\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}\psi_{l}+(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)]-G^{\mu\beta}_{a}G^{\nu}_{a\beta}-g^{\mu\nu}{\cal{L}}_{qG}

(13)

and the gauge-fixing and ghost terms are given by

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)=-(A^{\mu}_{a}\partial^{\nu}B_{a}+A^{\nu}_{a}\partial^{\mu}B_{a})-i[(\partial^{\mu}\bar{c}_{a})D^{\nu}_{ab}c_{b}+(\partial^{\nu}\bar{c}_{a})D^{\mu}_{ab}c_{b}]-g^{\mu\nu}{\cal{L}}_{Gf+Gh}.

(14)

This can be rewritten
Kugo and Ojima 1979
as an anti-commutator with
Q_{B}

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)=-\{Q_{B},\,\big((\partial^{\mu}\bar{c}_{a})A^{\nu}_{a}+(\partial^{\nu}\bar{c}_{a})A^{\mu}_{a}+g^{\mu\nu}[\frac{\texttt{a}}{2}\bar{c}_{a}B_{a}-(\partial^{\rho}\bar{c}_{a})A_{\rho}^{a}]\big)\}.

(15)

It follows from Eqs. (
7
) and (
15
) that
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)
does not contribute to physical matrix elements i.e.

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0

(16)

so that

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,QCD}\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle

(17)

and hence that the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the QCD expressions for either
P^{\mu}_{bel}
or
\bm{J}_{bel}
i.e.

\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle\equiv\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,t^{0\mu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle=\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,t^{0\mu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle

(18)

and

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ijk}\ \int d^{3}x\,\langle\Phi|\,x^{j}t^{0k}_{bel}\,-x^{k}t^{0j}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\,\epsilon^{ijk}\,\int d^{3}x\,\langle\Phi|\,x^{j}t^{0k}_{bel}(qG)\,-x^{k}t^{0j}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle.

(19)

II.2
Quantum Electrodynamics

The most general covariantly quantized version of QED is given by the Lautrup-Nakanishi Lagrangian density
Lautrup 1967
;
Nakanishi 1966
, which is a combination of the Classical Lagrangian (
Clas
) and a Gauge Fixing part (
Gf
)

{\cal{L}}={\cal{L}}_{Clas}+{\cal{L}}_{Gf}

(20)

where

{\cal{L}}_{Clas}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,[\bar{\psi}(i\not\!{\partial}-m+e\not\negthickspace{A})\psi+\textrm{h.c.}]

(21)

and

{\cal{L}}_{Gf}=-\partial_{\mu}B(x).A^{\mu}(x)+\frac{\textsf{a}}{2}B^{2}(x)

(22)

where
B(x)
is the gauge-fixing field and the parameter
a
determines the structure of the photon propagator and is irrelevant for the present discussion
1
1

1

The case
\textsf{a}=1
corresponds to the Gupta-Bleuler approach (see e.g.
Jauch and Rohrlich 1955
) based on the Fermi Lagrangian. Note also that in order to conform to the conventions used in the QCD case, the expression for
{\cal{L}}_{Gf}
differs from Nakanishi-Lautrup by a 4-divergence.

The
physical
states
|\Phi\rangle
of the theory are defined to satisfy

B^{(+)}(x)|\Phi\rangle=0

(23)

where

B(x)=B^{(+)}(x)+B^{(-)}(x)

(24)

with
B^{(\pm)}(x)
the positive/negative frequency parts of
B(x)
.

For the conserved Bellinfante density one finds,

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}=\theta^{\mu\nu}_{bel}+t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)

(25)

where
\theta^{\mu\nu}_{bel}
, which is referred to as the classical energy momentum tensor density, is

\theta^{\mu\nu}_{bel}=\frac{i}{4}\,\bar{\psi}(\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu})\,\psi-F^{\mu\beta}F^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\beta}-g^{\mu\nu}{\cal{L}}_{Clas}

(26)

where
\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}=\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}-2ieA^{\nu}
,
and

t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)=-A^{\nu}\,\partial^{\mu}B-A^{\mu}\,\partial^{\nu}B-g^{\mu\nu}{\cal{L}}_{Gf}

(27)

As explained in Kugo-Ojima
Kugo and Ojima 1978
the QED expressions can be obtained from the QCD case by putting the structure constants to zero and suppressing the colour group labels, in whch case the gauge-fixing field
B
and the ghost fields
c
and
\bar{c}
become free fields. The expression Eq. (
9

then becomes, in terms of creation and annihilation operators

Q_{B}=i\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{k}}[c_{k}^{\dagger}B_{k}-B_{k}^{\dagger}c_{k}].

(28)

Because the Fadeev-Popov ghosts are here free, the state vector space
\mathcal{V}
can be decomposed into a direct product

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{phys}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{FP}
where
\mathcal{V}_{phys}
is the usual QED physical state vector space. Moreover the ghosts are redundant, so that one can work in the sector containing neither
c
nor
\bar{c}
ghosts i.e.
\mathcal{V}_{phys}\otimes|0\rangle_{FP}
. Hence the physical states in QED can be taken to be
|\Phi\rangle\otimes|0\rangle_{FP}
. Using this and (
28

7

23

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,QED}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=_{FP}\hskip-2.84544pt\langle 0|\otimes\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,Free}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle\otimes|0\rangle_{FP}=0

(29)

where
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,Free}(Gf+Gh)
is
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,QCD}(Gf+Gh)
in which the structure constants are put to zero and the gauge-fixing and ghost fields are free. Thus

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel,QED}\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,\theta^{\mu\nu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle

(30)

and hence, as in QCD, the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the QED expressions for the physical matrix elements of either
P^{\mu}_{bel}
or
\bm{J}_{bel}

II.3
QED: direct study of subsidiary fields

The argument showing that the gauge fixing field in QED does not contribute to the physical expectation value of the
Bellinfante version of the energy-momentum tensor and hence does not contribute to the expectation values of the
Bellinfante
versions of the momentum and angular momentum, based on the QCD case, is rather abstract, so we here show that the
concrete expression for the gauge fixing contribution to the Bellinfante angular momentum, given in Damski’s paper
Damski 2021
, which deals only with the free electromagnetic case,
actually
vanishes i.e. we shall show directly that

\langle\Phi|\,\mathbf{J}_{bel}(\textrm{Gf})\,|\Phi\rangle=0

(31)

where, in Damski’s notation

J^{i}_{bel}(\textrm{Gf})=J^{i}_{\textrm{div}}+J^{i}_{\xi}.

(32)

Consider

\langle\Phi|J^{i}_{bel}(\textrm{Gf})|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|J^{i}_{\textrm{div}}+J^{i}_{\xi}|\Phi\rangle=\int d^{3}z\epsilon^{imn}\langle\Phi|z^{m}\mathcal{I}_{n}(z)-z^{n}\mathcal{I}_{m}(z)|{\Phi}\rangle

(33)

with, following Damski,

\mathcal{I}_{n}(z)=A_{n}\partial_{j}F_{0j}+(\partial\cdot A)\partial_{n}A_{0}.

(34)

Defining

B(z)\equiv-\partial\cdot A

(35)

and using the fact that in Damski the fields are free, one obtains

\mathcal{I}_{n}(z)=A_{n}\partial_{0}B-B\partial_{n}A_{0}.

(36)

We split the fields into their positive and negative frequency parts

B(z)=B^{(+)}+B^{(-)}\qquad B^{(-)}=[B^{(+)}]^{\dagger}\quad\textrm{and}\quad A_{\mu}(z)=A_{\mu}^{(+)}+A_{\mu}^{(-)}\qquad A_{\mu}^{(-)}=[A_{\mu}^{(+)}]^{\dagger}

(37)

with

A_{\mu}^{(+)}(z)=\int[dk^{\prime}]\sum_{\sigma=0}^{\sigma=3}\epsilon_{\mu}(\textbf{k}^{\prime},\sigma)c_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma}e^{-ik^{\prime}\cdot z}

(38)

where the
\epsilon_{\mu}
are polarization vectors and the
c_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma}
are annihilation operators and we
use the shorthand

[dk^{\prime}]\equiv\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\sqrt{2\omega_{k^{\prime}}}}.

(39)

In Damski’s notation one has

B^{(+)}(z)=-i\int[dk]\omega_{k}L_{\mathbf{k}}\omega e^{-ik\cdot z}

(40)

where

L_{\mathbf{k}}=c_{\mathbf{k}3}-c_{\mathbf{k}0}.

(41)

The physical states, as usual, are defined to satisfy

B^{(+)}(z)|\Phi\rangle=0\qquad\langle\Phi|B^{(-)}(z)=0.

(42)

Using these and the fact that the commutators
[B^{(+)},A_{\mu}^{(+)}]=[B^{(-)},A_{\mu}^{(-)}]=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|\mathcal{I}_{n}(z)|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|A_{n}\partial_{0}B^{(-)}-B^{(+)}\partial_{n}A_{0}|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi|[A^{(+)}_{n},\partial_{0}B^{(-)}]-[B^{(+)},\partial_{n}A^{(-)}_{0}]|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi|\Phi\rangle\{[A^{(+)}_{n},\partial_{0}B^{(-)}]-[B^{(+)},\partial_{n}A^{(-)}_{0}]\}

(43)

the last step following since the commutators are c-numbers.

The relevant commutators and polarization vectors are

[L_{\bm{k}},c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}0}]=-[c_{\bm{k}0},c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}0}]=\delta^{3}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\qquad\quad[L_{\bm{k}},c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}3}]=[c_{\bm{k}3},c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}3}]=\delta^{3}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})

(44)

and

\epsilon^{\mu}(\bm{k},0)=(1,\bm{0})\qquad\qquad\epsilon^{\mu}(\bm{k},3)=(0,\bm{k}/\omega_{k}).

(45)

Substituting Eqs. (
38
,
40
,
44
, and
45
) into Eq. (
II.3
) , we find, after some labour,
that

[A^{(+)}_{n},\partial_{0}B^{(-)}]-[B^{(+)},\partial_{n}A^{(-)}_{0}]=0

(46)

and thus that in QED

\langle\Phi|J^{i}_{bel,QED}(\textrm{Gf})|\Phi\rangle=0

(47)

in agreement with the more general derivation based on the QCD case.

II.4
Bellinfante summary

To summarize, despite the incorrect proof for the QED case given in
Leader 2011
, the physical matrix elements of the gauge-fixing and ghost contributions to the Bellinfante version of the energy-momentum tensor, in both QED and QCD, actually do vanish. Hence the only contributions to the momentum
P^{\mu}_{bel}
and angular momentum
\bm{J}_{bel}
are from photons and electrons in the QED case and from quarks and gluons in QCD.

This latter property was thus correctly utilized in the review paper
Leader and Lorce 2013
. Also, in writing down the most general structure for the matrix elements of
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle
in
Leader and Lorce 2013
, use was made of the claim that

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(48)

This follows because the total
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}
is a conserved operator and, via (
17
),

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(49)

Note also that this justifies the results in several papers in the literature, e.g.
Ji
Ji 1997
;
Ji 1997
;
Ji 1998
, Jaffe and Manohar
Jaffe and Manohar 1990
, Bakker, Leader and Trueman (BLT)
Bakker et al. 2004
and Wakamatsu
Wakamatsu 2010
;
Wakamatsu 2011
, where the general structure of the physical matrix elements of
t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)
(or its QED analogue) is derived under the
unstated assumption
that Eq. (
49
) holds.

III
Physical matrix elements of the Canonical energy-momentum tensor

In
Leader 2011
it was claimed that the physical matrix elements of the gauge-fixing and ghost
contributions
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)
in QCD and the gauge-fixing contribution
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)
in QED,
vanish. The result given for the QED case in
Leader 2011
, as pointed out by Damski
Damski 2021
, is wrong because of an incorrect use of the ”resolution of the identity” for a space with an indefinite metric, and, although not discussed by Damski, also the QCD result is wrong for the same reason.

Thus, in contrast to the Bellinfante case and contrary to the claims made in
Leader 2011
, in QCD

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0

(50)

and in QED

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0.

(51)

We shall analyze the consequences of these for the QCD case. Completely analogous arguments hold for the case of QED.

There are two questions which have to be answered. Given (
50
):

1) Is the analysis of the general structure of the physical matrix elements of the quark-gluon
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)
given in
Leader and Lorce 2013
correct?

2) Do the subsidiary fields contribute to the physical matrix elements of the Canonical momentum
P^{\mu}_{can}
and angular momentum
\bm{J}_{can}
?

III.1
Structure of the physical matrix elements of the quark-gluon
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)

The Bellinfante and Canonical energy-momentum tensors differ from each other by a divergence term of the following form:

t^{\mu\nu}_{can}=t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}-\partial_{\lambda}G^{\lambda\mu\nu},

(52)

where the so-called
superpotential
reads

G^{\lambda\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(M^{\lambda\mu\nu}_{\text{spin}}+M^{\mu\nu\lambda}_{\text{spin}}+M^{\nu\mu\lambda}_{\text{spin}}\right),

(53)

and, crucially, is antisymmetric w.r.t. its first two indices

G^{\lambda\mu\nu}=-G^{\mu\lambda\nu}.

(54)

The
M_{spin}
involves a sum over all fields and is given in terms of the Lagrangian by

M_{\text{spin}}^{\mu\nu\rho}(x)=-i\sum_{allfields}\,\frac{\partial\cal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{r})}\,(\Sigma^{\nu\rho})^{\phantom{r}s}_{r}\phi_{s}(x).

(55)

where
(\Sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\phantom{r}s}_{r}=-(\Sigma^{\nu\mu})^{\phantom{r}s}_{r}
is an operator related to the spin of the field. For example, for particles with the most common spins, one has

spin-
0
particle

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\phantom{r}s}_{r}

\displaystyle=0,

(56)

spin-
1/2
Dirac particle

\displaystyle\psi_{r}(x)

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\phantom{r}s}_{r}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\sigma^{\mu\nu}\right)^{\phantom{r}s}_{r},

(57)

spin-
1
particle

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=i\left(\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\,g^{\nu\beta}-\delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}\,g^{\mu\beta}\right).

(58)

Examination of the structure of the Lagrangians in Eqs. (
4
,
6
) shows that since
{\cal{L}}_{qG}
does not contain any gauge-fixing or ghost fields and
{\cal{L}}_{Gf+Gh}
does not contain any derivatives of
A^{\mu}_{a}
we may write

M_{\text{spin}}^{\mu\nu\rho}(x)=M_{\text{spin}}^{\mu\nu\rho}(x)|_{qG}+M_{\text{spin}}^{\mu\nu\rho}(x)|_{Gf+Gh}

(59)

and thus in Eq. (
52
)

G^{\lambda\mu\nu}=G^{\lambda\mu\nu}|_{qG}+G^{\lambda\mu\nu}|_{Gf+Gh}

(60)

so that separately

t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)=t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(qG)-\partial_{\lambda}G^{\lambda\mu\nu}|_{qG}

(61)

and

t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)=t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)-\partial_{\lambda}G^{\lambda\mu\nu}|_{Gf+Gh}.

(62)

Hence,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\lambda}G^{\lambda\mu\nu}|_{Gf+Gh}

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\quad\textrm{via}\,\,(\ref{Gsym})

and thus for the physical matrix elements, using (
16
),

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(64)

Finally, then, since the total
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}
is a conserved operator, we obtain the key result

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle=0

(65)

and the analysis of the general structure of the physical matrix elements of the quark-gluon
t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)
given in
Leader and Lorce 2013
, which relied on this property, is correct.

III.2
Contribution of the subsidiary fields to the physical matrix elements of the Canonical momentum
P^{\mu}_{can}
and angular momentum
\bm{J}_{can}

From Eqs. (

) and (
61
)

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,t^{0\mu}_{bel}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,t^{0\mu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle+\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,\partial_{\lambda}G^{\lambda 0\nu}|_{qG}\,|\Phi\rangle.

By the antisymmetry property (
54
) the last term is actually a 3-dimensional divergence
\partial_{i}G^{i0\nu}|_{qG}
, yielding a surface term at infinity, which, as always, is assumed to vanish. Thus

\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle=\int d^{3}x\langle\Phi|\,t^{0\mu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle.

(67)

But

\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{bel}\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle

(68)

so that, indeed,

\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,P^{\mu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle

(69)

and the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the physical matrix elements of
P^{\mu}_{can}
.
A similar argument shows that

\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle=\langle\Phi|\,J^{i}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle

(70)

and the subsidiary fields also do not contribute to the physical matrix elements of
J^{i}_{can}
.

III.3
Canonical summary

To summarize, despite the incorrect proof given in
Leader 2011
concerning the physical matrix elements of the gauge-fixing contributions in QED and the gauge-fixing and ghost contributions in QCD to the Canonical version of the energy-momentum tensor, the essential property used in writing down the most general structure for the QCD matrix elements
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle
and the QED matrix elements
\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle
in
Leader and Lorce 2013
, namely, that

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(qG)\,|\Phi\rangle=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{can}\,|\Phi\rangle=0

(71)

is correct.

Moreover, despite the incorrect derivation in
Leader 2011
, the only contributions to the physical matrix elements of the momentum
P^{\mu}_{can}
and angular momentum
\bm{J}_{can}
are from photons and electrons in the QED case and from quarks and gluons in QCD. This latter property was thus correctly utilized in the review paper
Leader and Lorce 2013
.

IV
Conclusions

Damski’s criticism
Damski 2021
of the
proof
given in
Leader 2011
for certain properties of the physical matrix elements of the energy-momentum tensor
t^{\mu\nu}
in QED is valid, and it is also applicable to QCD, so that, contrary to the assertions in
Leader 2011
, for the Canonical case

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0\quad\emph{and}\quad\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle\neq 0.

(72)

Nonetheless the following crucial features for QED and QCD, utilized in the angular momentum review
Leader and Lorce 2013
, are in fact correct:

a) for the Bellinfante case, as claimed in
Leader 2011

\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=0\quad\emph{and}\quad\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{bel}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(73)

b) despite Eq. (
72
) one has for the Canonical case,

\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf)\,|\Phi\rangle=0\quad\emph{and}\quad\partial_{\mu}\,\langle\Phi^{\prime}|\,t^{\mu\nu}_{can}(Gf+Gh)\,|\Phi\rangle=0.

(74)

and obviously an analogous result for the Bellinfante case as a consequence of (
73
).

c) despite Eq. (
72
) the subsidiary fields do not contribute to the physical matrix elements of either the Canonical or Bellinfante versions of the momentum or angular momentum, a result which is normally
assumed without comment
in papers on QCD.

Hamiltonian Structure of the Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Equations with Polarization and Magnetization
Alain J. Brizard a

Abstract
The Hamiltonian formulation of guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, which contain dipole contributions to the guiding-center polarization and magnetization, is presented in terms of a guiding-center Hamiltonian functional that is derived from the exact guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy conservation law, and an antisymmetric functional bracket that satisfies the Jacobi property. Exact energy-momentum and angular momentum conservation laws are expressed in Hamiltonian form and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell entropy functional is shown to be a Casimir functional.



I
Introduction

The investigation of the noncanonical Hamiltonian structure of important dissipation’s plasma dynamical systems began with the equations of ideal magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
[
1
]
and the Vlasov-Maxwell equations
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. These structures have led to the development of advanced structure-preserving algorithms
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
that allow for sophisticated numerical methods for solving dissipationless plasma equations that possess Hamiltonian and Lagrangian structures.

Based on the asymptotic separation of orbital time scales in a magnetically-confined plasma
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations have played a crucial role in the analysis of the long-time behavior of magnetized plasmas
[
14
,
15
,
13
]
. On the one hand, through the introduction of adiabatic invariants, constructed by standard Lie-transform perturbation methods
[
16
]
associated with widely-separated orbital time scales, fast orbital time scales are asymptotically removed from the Vlasov kinetic equation to produce a reduced Vlasov equation. On the other hand, this dynamical reduction introduces important polarization and magnetization effects into the reduced Maxwell equations
[
15
]
. In addition, the variational structure of these reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations allows the derivation of exact energy-momentum conservation laws through the application of the Noether method
[
15
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is to explore the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations. One approach is to proceed by Lie-transform methods
[
17
]
on the Hamiltonian structure of the Vlasov-Maxwell equations
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. A more direct approach, however, is to construct the Hamiltonian structure from the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations themselves. (The construction of the Hamiltonian structure for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations followed by Morrison
[
3
]
is reviewed in App.
A
.) This direct approach yielded the Hamiltonian structure for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations
[
18
]
in the simplest case when the guiding-center polarization appears only as a correction to the guiding-center magnetization
[
19
]
. A direct approach also yielded the Hamiltonian structure for the gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell theory
[
20
]
, which was made possible only because the gyrokinetic equations were expressed solely in terms of the perturbed electromagnetic fields
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
, instead of the standard gyrokinetic representation
[
14
]
involving the perturbed electromagnetic potentials
(\Phi_{1},{\bf A}_{1})
.

In order to take into account the full guiding-center polarization, it is a standard procedure
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
to describe particle motion in a local frame moving with the
E\times B
velocity, expressed as
{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}/B
(in terms of the electric field
{\bf E}
and the magnetic field
{\bf B}\equiv B\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
). In this moving frame, a generic expression for the guiding-center Lagrangian is expressed as
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]

L_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}+P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;-\;\left(e\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gc}\right),

(1)

where
({\bf X},P_{\|})
denote the guiding-center position and guiding-center parallel kinetic momentum, while the guiding-center
symplectic
momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}
and the guiding-center kinetic energy
K_{\rm gc}
may depend on the electric field
{\bf E}
. Hence, in this
symplectic
polarization representation, each term separately contributes to guiding-center polarization, which is defined in terms of the partial derivative of Eq. (
1
) with respect to
{\bf E}
:

\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}\;\equiv\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}\;-\;\partial K_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}.

(2)

In addition, we note that, despite the fact that the electromagnetic potentials
(\Phi,{\bf A})
appear in the guiding-center Lagrangian (
1
), the guiding-center equations of motion derived from it are gauge invariant since Eq. (
1
) transforms as
L_{\rm gc}\rightarrow L_{\rm gc}+(e/c)\,d\chi/dt
under the gauge transformation
(\Phi,{\bf A})\rightarrow(\Phi-c^{-1}\partial\chi/\partial t,\;{\bf A}+\nabla\chi)
, and the guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equations are invariant under the addition of an exact time derivative to the Lagrangian
L_{\rm gc}
.

In the present paper, we adopt the alternate
Hamiltonian
polarization representation in which the guiding-center polarization is introduced explicitly only through the guiding-center kinetic energy
K_{\rm gc}
, i.e., Eq. (
2
) simplifies to
\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}\equiv-\,\partial K_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}
when
\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E}\equiv 0
. These dual polarization representations are analogous to the Hamiltonian and symplectic representations of gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell theory
[
25
,
26
,
14
]
, in which the perturbed magnetic field, respectively, appears either in the gyrocenter Hamiltonian only or in both the gyrocenter Hamiltonian and the gyrocenter Poisson bracket
[
25
,
26
]
. We note that, in the gyrocenter and guiding-center symplectic representations, the inductive electric field and the polarization drift velocity appear explicitly in their respective Euler-Lagrange equations, while these terms are absent in the Hamiltonian representations. Hence, because of the simplicity of the Hamiltonian polarization representation, we will explore the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
1
) with
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\equiv 0
used for further simplicity, and leave the symplectic polarization case to future work.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations are presented, with the guiding-center polarization and magnetization defined in terms of derivatives of the guiding-center Lagrangian with respect to electric and magnetic fields, respectively. In Sec.
III
, the energy-momentum conservation laws of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, which are derived by Noether method, are presented. In addition, we show that the angular momentum conservation law follows from the explicit symmetry of the guiding-center stress tensor. In Sec.
IV
, we construct the functional bracket for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, following a direct procedure adopted for other reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations
[
18
,
20
]
. The form of the new guiding-center functional bracket will guarantee the Jacobi property.

II
Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations describe the time evolution of the guiding-center Vlasov phase-space density
F_{\rm gc}={\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\,F
, which is defined in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov function
F({\bf X},P_{\|},J,t)
and the guiding-center phase-space Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}
on a
4+1
reduced guiding-center phase space
({\bf X},P_{\|};J)
, where
J\equiv\mu B/\Omega=\mu\,(mc/e)
denotes the guiding-center gyroaction adiabatic invariant, and the Maxwell fields
({\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B})
, where the guiding-center displacement field
{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\bf E}+4\pi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}
is defined in terms of the guiding-center polarization
\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}
, while
{\bf B}
denotes the magnetic field. We note here that, in contrast to gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell theory
[
14
]
, the electric and magnetic fields are not separated into background (unperturbed) and fluctuating (perturbed) fields, where only the fluctuating fields are considered as variational fields.

As discussed by Morrison
[
27
]
, the functional bracket for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations considered here will act on arbitrary functionals
{\mathcal{F}}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]
and, in the Hamiltonian representation adopted here, the electric field
{\bf E}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]
will be shown to be expressed as a simple functional of the guiding-center fields
(F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B})
. Lastly, we will show that the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(F_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}\dot{\bf X}\right)\;-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial P_{\|}}\left(F_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}\dot{P}_{\|}\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\;-\;4\pi\;{\bf J}_{\rm gc},

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E},

(5)

can be expressed in Hamiltonian form

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}\;\equiv\;\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\right]_{\rm gc},

(6)

where
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}
denotes the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian functional and
[\;,\;]_{\rm gc}
denotes the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional bracket. In Eq. (
3
), the guiding-center equations of motion
(\dot{\bf X},\dot{P}_{\|})
will be derived (see App.
B
for details) from the guiding-center Lagrangian

\displaystyle L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}\;+\;P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;-\;\left(e\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gc}\right).

(7)

In Eq. (
4
), on the other hand, the guiding-center electromagnetic fields

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf E}\;+\;4\pi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc},

(8)

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf B}\;-\;4\pi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc},

(9)

are defined in terms of the guiding-center polarization and magnetization

\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc}\right)\;\equiv\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(-\;\partial K_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf E},\frac{}{}\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial{\bf B}\right),

(10)

where the guiding-center polarization is defined in the Hamiltonian representation, and we use the notation
\int_{P}(\cdots)=\sum\,\int(\cdots)\,2\pi dP_{\|}dJ
, which includes a summation over charged-particle species and excludes the guiding-center Jacobian

{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}
since it is included in the definition of the guiding-center Vlasov density
F_{\rm gc}
. The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
3
)-(
5
) are complemented by the remaining Maxwell equations

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\,\varrho_{\rm gc},

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(12)

where the guiding-center charge and current densities

(\varrho_{\rm gc},{\bf J}_{\rm gc})\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(e,\frac{}{}e\,\dot{\bf X}\right),

(13)

satisfy the charge conservation law

\frac{\partial\varrho_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf J}_{\rm gc}=0,

(14)

which can be obtained directly from Eqs. (
4
) and (
11
).

II.1
Guiding-center Hamilton equations

According to the Hamiltonian polarization representation adopted here, the guiding-center kinetic energy appearing in the guiding-center Lagrangian (
7
) is expressed as
[
13
]

K_{\rm gc}\;=\;\mu\,B\;+\;\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\;-\;\frac{m}{2}\,|{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}/B|^{2},

(15)

which includes a ponderomotive contribution associated with the
E\times B
velocity. The details of the guiding-center transformation leading to Eq. (
15
) are presented in App.
B
.

The Euler-Lagrange equations derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
7
) yield the guiding-center Hamilton equations of motion in reduced guiding-center phase space
({\bf X},P_{\|})
, which include the guiding-center velocity

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{\rm gc}}{\partial P_{\|}}\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\left(e\,{\bf E}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\nabla K_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB_{\|}^{*}}

(16)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\{{\bf X},\;K_{\rm gc}\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\;{\bf X}\}_{\rm gc},

and the guiding-center parallel force equation

\displaystyle\dot{P}_{\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(e\,{\bf E}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\nabla K_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}

(17)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\{P_{\|},\;K_{\rm gc}\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\;P_{\|}\}_{\rm gc},

where
\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}=P_{\|}/m
defines the parallel guiding-center velocity and the modified electric and magnetic fields are defined as

\displaystyle{\bf E}^{*}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf E}-\frac{P_{\|}}{e}\;\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial t}={\bf E}-\frac{P_{\|}}{eB}\,\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t},

(18)

\displaystyle{\bf B}^{*}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf B}\;+\;\frac{cP_{\|}}{e}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}.

(19)

In accordance with standard guiding-center theory
[
28
]
, we use the standard guiding-center ordering
{\bf E}={\bf E}_{\bot}+\epsilon\,E_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
, which holds that the parallel electric field is small compared with its components perpendicular to the magnetic field.

The reduced guiding-center Poisson bracket introduced in Eqs. (
16
)-(
17
) is defined as

\displaystyle\{F,\;G\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\nabla F\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial P_{\|}}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial P_{\|}}\,\nabla G\right)

(20)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla F\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla G,

where
F
and
G
are arbitrary functions of the reduced guiding-center phase-space coordinates
Z^{\alpha}=({\bf X},P_{\|})
. The guiding-center Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}=(e/c)\,B_{\|}^{*}
, on the other hand, where
B_{\|}^{*}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}
, satisfies the guiding-center Liouville equation

\frac{\partial{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}\dot{\bf X}\right)\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial P_{\|}}\left({\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}\dot{P}_{\|}\right)\;=\;0,

(21)

where
(\dot{\bf X},\dot{P}_{\|})
are given by Eqs. (
16
)-(
17
). As noted in App.
A
, the extended guiding-center Poisson bracket (
108
) is not needed in what follows in order to guarantee the antisymmetry of the Hamiltonian functional bracket (
65
).

We note that the reduced guiding-center Poisson bracket (
20
) can be expressed in divergence form as

\{F,G\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}F\;\left\{Z^{\alpha},\;G\right\}_{\rm gc}\right),

(22)

and that it automatically satisfies the Jacobi identity expressed in terms of the Jacobiator

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}[F,G,K]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\{F,\frac{}{}\{G,K\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\left\{G,\frac{}{}\{K,F\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}

(23)

\displaystyle+\;\left\{K,\frac{}{}\{F,G\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;0,

which holds for any three arbitrary functions
(F,G,K)
and follows from the condition
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}=\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}\equiv 0
.

II.2
Guiding-center polarization and magnetization

According to Eq. (
10
), the guiding-center polarization
\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}
and magnetization
\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc}
can be derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
7
) by considering the electric and magnetic variations

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf E}L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\delta_{\bf E}K_{\rm gc},

(24)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf B}L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf B}(P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}-\delta_{\bf B}K_{\rm gc},

(25)

where the guiding-center velocity
\dot{\bf X}
is kept constant. Here, the variation of the guiding-center symplectic momentum

\delta_{\bf B}(P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}})\;=\;(P_{\|}/B)\;\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}

yields the moving electric-dipole contribution to the guiding-center magnetization

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf B}(P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{P_{\|}}{B}\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}=\left(\frac{e}{c}\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}

(26)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,P_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}/mc\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B},

where

\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\equiv(e\widehat{{\sf b}}/\Omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\dot{\bf X}

(27)

denotes the guiding-center electric dipole moment
[
29
,
30
,
19
]
and we introduced the symmetric dyadic tensor

\mathbb{P}_{\|}\;=\;\frac{P_{\|}}{m\Omega}\;\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right).

(28)

In Eq. (
26
), and in what follows, we will use the dyadic double-dot product notation
{\bf a}\,{\bf b}\;\mbox{\boldmath$:$}\;\mathbb{W}={\bf b}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{W}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf a}=b_{i}\,W^{ij}\,a_{j}
, where
({\bf a},{\bf b})
is an arbitrary pair of vectors and
\mathbb{W}
is an arbitrary second-rank tensor, and summation over repeated indices is assumed.

Next, the variations of the guiding-center kinetic energy are

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf E}K_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\delta{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\right)\;\equiv\;-\;\delta{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E},

(29)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf B}K_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}\;-\;\delta{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta}{\delta\bf B}\left[\frac{mc^{2}}{2\,B^{2}}{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\right]

(30)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\delta{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}.

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E}
denotes the electric-dipole moment and the intrinsic guiding-center magnetic dipole is defined as

\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;-\,\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\frac{\bf E}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right),

(31)

which includes the standard magnetization
-\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
as well as contributions from the electric-dipole moment
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E}
.

Using the definitions (
24
)-(
25
), we, therefore, find

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf E}L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E},

(32)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bf B}L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{P_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}}{mc}\right),

(33)

so that the guiding-center polarization is defined as

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E},

(34)

while the guiding-center magnetization is defined as

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\times\frac{P_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}}{mc}\right).

(35)

Finally we note that the particle charge density
\varrho
and the particle current density
{\bf J}
are now expressed in guiding-center form as

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho_{\rm gc}\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc},

(36)

\displaystyle{\bf J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}/\partial t\;+\;c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc},

(37)

which guarantees the guiding-center charge conservation law (
14
).

III
Exact Energy-Momentum Conservation Laws

Using the Noether method
[
31
]
, the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
3
)-(
5
) can be shown to possess exact energy-momentum conservation laws
[
15
]
. We note that these conservation laws are exact since the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Lagrangian and Hamiltonian are assumed independent of external electromagnetic fields. In what follows, we will also assume vanishing contributions at spatial boundaries, so that energy-momentum sources are omitted.

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy conservation law

\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}/\partial t\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\;=\;0

(38)

is expressed in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}+\frac{\bf E}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}-|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}+{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}+\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}+|{\bf B}|^{2}\right),

and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density-flux

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\dot{\bf X}\,K_{\rm gc}\;+\;\frac{c\,{\bf E}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}.

(40)

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}
will be used in Eq. (
48
) to define the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\equiv\int_{X}{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}
.

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum conservation law

\partial{\bf P}_{\rm gc}/\partial t\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}\;=\;0

(41)

is expressed in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum density

{\bf P}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\bf B}{4\pi c},

(42)

and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell stress tensor is

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\dot{\bf X}\,P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\left({\bf D}_{\rm gc}\,{\bf E}+{\bf B}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\mathbb{I}}{4\pi}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}-|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)+{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\right],

which can be written in symmetric dyadic form as

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{m}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\mu B\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;+\;P_{\|}\;\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\;\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}+\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\right]

(44)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{4\pi}\;\left[\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}\;+\frac{}{}|{\bf B}^{2}|\right)\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}\;-\;\left({\bf E}{\bf E}\;+\frac{}{}{\bf B}{\bf B}\right)\right]\;+\;\chi_{\rm gc}\left[|{\bf E}_{\bot}|^{2}\;\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;+\;E_{\|}^{2}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;{\bf E}\,{\bf E}\right].

Here, we introduced the classical guiding-center susceptibility tensor
\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\chi$}_{\rm gc}}\equiv\chi_{\rm gc}\,\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)
, where
[
10
,
11
]

\chi_{\rm gc}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]\;\equiv\;\frac{mc^{2}}{B^{2}}\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{c^{2}}{4\pi\,v_{\rm A}^{2}}

(45)

is a functional of the magnetic field
{\bf B}
as well as a functional of the guiding-center Vlasov phase-space density
F_{\rm gc}
. Because of this symmetry property, we are also guaranteed that the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell toroidal angular momentum density

P_{{\rm gc}\varphi}\;\equiv\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,P_{\|}\,b_{\varphi}\;+\;{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\bf B}{4\pi c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}.

(46)

satisfies the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell toroidal angular momentum conservation law

\frac{\partial P_{{\rm gc}\varphi}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)\;=\;\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}^{\top}\;\mbox{\boldmath$:$}\;\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)\;\equiv\;0,

(47)

which follows from the dyadic symmetry of the guiding-center stress tensor (
44
), and the antisymmetry of the dyadic tensor
\nabla(\partial_{\varphi}{\bf X})
, i.e., the dyadic tensor identity
({\bf V}{\bf W}+{\bf W}{\bf V})\;\mbox{\boldmath$:$}\;\nabla(\partial_{\varphi}{\bf X})\equiv 0
follows from the vector identity
{\bf W}\,{\bf V}\;\mbox{\boldmath$:$}\;\nabla(\partial_{\varphi}{\bf X})=\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,({\bf V}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf W})
, which holds for arbitrary vectors
({\bf V},{\bf W})
.

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy conservation law (
38
) yields the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}+\int_{X}\left[\frac{\bf E}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}-|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\right]

(48)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\,\left(\mu B+\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\right)\;+\;\int_{X}\left[\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{8\pi}\;+\;\frac{{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{8\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}\right)^{-1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\right],

where the guiding-center permittivity tensor
\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}
is defined through the relation

{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\;=\;\left[\mathbb{I}\;+\;4\pi\;\chi_{\rm gc}\,\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E},

(49)

which is inverted

{\bf E}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]\;\equiv\;\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}^{-1}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}

(50)

in order to obtain the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional (
48
).

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum and angular momentum conservation laws (
41
) and (
47
), on the other hand, yield the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum and angular momentum functionals

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\int_{X}\left(\frac{{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}}{4\pi\;c}\right).

(51)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;P_{\|}\,b_{\varphi}\;+\;\int_{X}\left(\frac{{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}}{4\pi\;c}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}.

(52)

The three guiding-center functionals (
48
) and (
51
)-(
52
) will play an important role in constructing the Hamiltonian formulation of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
).

IV
Hamiltonian Formulation of the Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Equations

We note that the conserved guiding-center functionals
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm gc}=({\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc},\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi})
, defined in Eqs. (
48
) and (
51
)-(
52
), are assumed to satisfy the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell functional conservation law

[{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm gc},\;{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}\;=\;0,

(53)

While the conservation of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}
is automatically satisfied by the antisymmetry of the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional bracket
[\;,\;]_{\rm gc}
, the remaining conservation laws present constraints on the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket.

In addition, the guiding-center entropy functional

{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]\;\equiv\;-\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\ln\left(F_{\rm gc}/{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\right),

(54)

must be a Casimir functional of the guiding-center Hamiltonian functional bracket
[\;,\;]_{\rm gc}
, i.e.,
\left[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gc}=0
must vanish for all functionals
{\mathcal{K}}
.

IV.1
Bracket construction

In order to construct the Hamiltonian structure for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (

), (
4
), and (

), we now follow the same procedure used to construct the Hamiltonian functional bracket
[\;,\;]
for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations (reviewed in App.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\mu\,B+\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{\bf E}{8\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}

\displaystyle\left(\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}\right)^{-1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}/4\pi\;=\;{\bf E}/4\pi,

(57)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}+\frac{\delta}{\delta\bf B}\left[\frac{{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{8\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\overleftrightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\rm gc}}\right)^{-1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\right]+\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}\;-\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc},

where we used the identity (
50
) in obtaining Eq. (
56

\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}
is defined in Eq. (
30

where we used the definition (

from which Eq. (
6

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;+\;4\pi e\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}-\frac{\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right\}_{\rm gc}\right)

where
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are arbitrary functionals of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell fields

) [see also Eq. (

and the modified electric field

to the guiding-center Hamiltonian dynamics in reduced guiding-center phase space
({\bf X},P_{\|})
. The same separation of the particle equations of motion was responsible for the antisymmetric bracket derived by Morrison
[

,
3

and Marsden and Weinstein
[

for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations (see Eqs. (

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (

, except that functional derivatives

[

and assumed in Refs.
[

[

]

IV.2
Jacobi property

The proof of the Jacobi property

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

(66)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gc}\right]_{\rm gc}+\left[{\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{F}}]_{\rm gc}\right]_{\rm gc}+\left[{\mathcal{K}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gc}\right]_{\rm gc}

for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
65
), which must hold for any guiding-center functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
, is, therefore, identical to the proof presented in Ref.
[
33
]
.

The Jacobi property (
66
), which is inherited from the Jacobi property (
23
), can thus be expressed as a series in powers of
(4\pi e)
up to third order
[
33
]
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;{\sf Jac}[f,g,k]\;+\;4\pi e\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(F_{i}^{\star}\;{\sf Jac}[X^{i};g,k]+\leftturn\right)

(67)

\displaystyle+(4\pi e)^{2}\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(F_{i}^{\star}\,G_{j}^{\star}\;{\sf Jac}[X^{i},X^{j};k]+\leftturn\right)\;+\;(4\pi e)^{3}\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(F_{i}^{\star}\,G_{j}^{\star}\,K_{\ell}^{\star}\frac{}{}{\sf Jac}[X^{i},X^{j},X^{\ell}]\right),

where we use the notation
(f,g,k)\equiv(\delta{\mathcal{F}}/\delta F_{\rm gc},\delta{\mathcal{G}}/\delta F_{\rm gc},\delta{\mathcal{K}}/\delta F_{\rm gc})
and
({\bf F}^{\star},{\bf G}^{\star},{\bf K}^{\star})\equiv(\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{F}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc},\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{G}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc},\delta^{\star}{\mathcal{K}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc})
, the guiding-center Jacobiator
{\sf Jac}[\;,\;,\;]
is defined in Eq. (
23
). In addition, the
i^{th}
-component of the guiding-center position

{\bf X}
is denoted as
X^{i}
, summation over repeated indices is assumed, and the symbol
\leftturn
denotes a cyclic permutation of the functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
. In Eq. (
67
), it is, therefore, clear that the Jacobi property of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
65
) is inherited from the Jacobi property (
23
) of the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
20
), since each term in Eq. (
67
) vanishes identically because of this latter property, i.e.,
{\sf Jac}[F,G,K]=0
for all functions
(F,G,K)
. Hence, the Jacobi property for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
65
) holds under the condition
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}=0
.

With this same replacement
({\bf E}\rightarrow{\bf D}_{\rm gc})
, we can easily see that the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum functional (
51
) is a Hamiltonian functional invariant, while the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell entropy functional (
54
) is a Casimir invariant of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
65
), with the explicit proofs presented in
[
18
]
.

IV.3
Hamiltonian conservation laws

First, we rearrange terms in Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}

73
):

\displaystyle 4\pi c\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}-\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf B}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)\;=\;\left(\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}-\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial\varphi}\;+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla K_{\rm gc}+\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial\varphi}+\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{E}\right]\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\;\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{8\pi}\right),

\displaystyle-\,4\pi c\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}}{\delta\bf B}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\bf E}{4\pi}\right)\;+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;e\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf E}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf E}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial\varphi}+\widehat{\sf z}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf E}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{E}\;+\;e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right]+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\frac{{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{4\pi}\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\;-\;\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\;\left(\frac{|{\bf E}|^{2}}{8\pi}\right)\right],

where we used the definitions (
63
) and

\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla K_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{\partial K_{\rm gc}}{\partial\varphi}\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm E}\;-\;\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc},

which follows from Eqs. (
29
)-(
30
). Hence, after omitting exact spatial divergences, Eq. (
73
) becomes the guiding-center Hamiltonian conservation law

\left[{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\right]_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;0,

(74)

which proves that the guiding-center angular momentum functional (
52
) is a functional invariant.

V
Summary and Future Work

In the present work, we showed that the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
3
)-(
5
), which were previously derived from a variational principle
[
20
]
, can be expressed in Hamiltonian form (
6
) in terms of the Hamiltonian functional (
48
) and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
65
). The present guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian structure generalizes our previous work
[
18
]
, which allows a more consistent treatment of guiding-center polarization and magnetization.

We remind the Reader that we adopted a
Hamiltonian
polarization representation, here, in which the guiding-center polarization is introduced explicitly through the guiding-center Hamiltonian. In the alternate
symplectic
polarization representation
[
24
]
, the guiding-center polarization effects appear in both the symplectic (Poisson-bracket) structure as well as the guiding-center Hamiltonian, which implies that the polarization drift now appears as a guiding-center drift. This fact increases the complexity of the task of finding the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations in the
symplectic
polarization representation, which will be left for future work.

In future work, we will also extend the work on the metriplectic foundations of gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell–Landau theory
[
34
]
to the metriplectic foundations of guiding-center Vlasov–Maxwell–Landau theory to explore neoclassical transport in general magnetic geometry.

Appendix A
Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian Theory

In this Appendix, the Hamiltonian structure for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations, which was originally discovered by Morrison
[
3
]
& Marsden and Weinstein
[
4
]
, is presented using a direct construction that will be used in Sec.
IV
to construct the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations. In particular, we will show that the Hamiltonian structure for the Vlasov-Maxwell equations cannot be based on an extended Hamiltonian description of charged-particle dynamics in eight-dimensional phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p};t,w)
that includes the time-energy canonically-conjugate coordinates
(t,w)
.

A.1
Hamiltonian Particle Motion in Extended Phase Space

The equations of motion for a charged particle (of mass
m
and charge
e
) can be expressed in Hamiltonian form

dz^{a}/d\tau\;=\;\{z^{a},H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}

(75)

in extended phase space
z^{a}=({\bf x},{\bf p};t,w)
, where
{\bf p}
denotes the kinetic momentum,
(t,w)
denote the time-energy canonical coordinates, and
\tau
is the Hamiltonian orbit parameter. Here, the extended Hamiltonian is defined as

H_{\rm e}\;\equiv\;K\;+\;e\,\Phi\;-\;w,

(76)

where
K\equiv|{\bf p}|^{2}/2m
denotes the kinetic energy, and the extended (noncanonical) Poisson bracket is defined as

\displaystyle\{F,\;G\}_{\rm e}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial w}\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial w}\right)

(77)

\displaystyle+\;\left(\nabla_{\rm e}F\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bf p}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla_{\rm e}G\right)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bf p}\right),

where
(F,G)
are arbitrary functions on extended phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p};t,w)
, and the extended gradient
\nabla_{\rm e}
is defined as

\nabla_{\rm e}\;\equiv\;\nabla\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial w},

so that, using Eq. (
76
), we obtain
\nabla_{\rm e}H_{\rm e}\equiv-e\,{\bf E}
.

The equations of motion (
75
) can thus be explicitly expressed in extended Hamiltonian form as

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf x}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf x},\;H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}\;=\;\frac{\partial H_{\rm e}}{\partial\bf p}\;=\;\frac{\bf p}{m},

(78)

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf p}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf p},\;H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}\;=\;-\,\nabla_{\rm e}H_{\rm e}\;+\;\frac{\partial H_{\rm e}}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{d{\bf x}}{d\tau}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B},

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{t,\;H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}\;=\;-\;\frac{\partial H_{\rm e}}{\partial w}\;=\;1,

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{dw}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{w,\;H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}\;=\;\frac{\partial H_{\rm e}}{\partial t}\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial H_{\rm e}}{\partial\bf p}

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf x}}{d\tau},

where, according to Eq. (
80
), the Hamiltonian orbit parameter
\tau
is simply related to the time coordinate
t
. In addition, Eq. (
81
) implies that, without any explicit time dependence
(\partial/\partial t\equiv 0)
, the energy coordinate
w
is a constant of the motion. We note that, in accordance with the physical interpretation of the Vlasov equation, the Vlasov distribution
f({\bf x},{\bf p},t)
satisfies the extended Vlasov equation

\displaystyle\frac{df}{d\tau}({\bf x},{\bf p},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f,\;H_{\rm e}\}_{\rm e}

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{d\tau}\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\;+\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{d\tau}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla f\;+\;\frac{d{\bf p}}{d\tau}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\;\equiv\;0,

i.e., the Vlasov distribution
f({\bf x},{\bf p},t)
is constant along particle orbits in extended phase space.

A.2
Hamiltonian Particle Motion in Regular Phase Space

In their original works, Morrison
[
2
,
3
]
& Marsden and Weinstein
[
4
]
considered regular (noncanonical) phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p})
, for which the equations of motion (
78
)-(
79
) cannot be expressed in simple Hamiltonian form (
75
). However, by using the regular (noncanonical) Poisson bracket on arbitrary functions
(f,g)
on phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p})
:

\{f,\;g\}\;\equiv\;\left(\nabla f\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bf p}\;-\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla g\right)\;+\;\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bf p}\right),

(83)

the equations of motion (
78
)-(
79
) can instead be written as

\displaystyle\frac{dz^{\alpha}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{z^{\alpha},\;K\right\}\;+\;e\,{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\;z^{\alpha}\right\}

(84)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\{z^{\alpha},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;+\;4\pi\,e\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}z^{\alpha}\right\}.

Here, the contributions from the kinetic energy
K
and the electric field
{\bf E}
are separately expressed in terms of functional derivatives of the Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}[f,{\bf E},{\bf B}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int f({\bf x},{\bf p},t)\;K({\bf p})\;d^{3}{\bf x}\,d^{3}{\bf p}

(85)

\displaystyle+\;\int\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}({\bf x},t)\;+\frac{}{}|{\bf B}|^{2}({\bf x},t)\right)\frac{d^{3}{\bf x}}{8\pi},

where summation over charged particle species is implied wherever appropriate. Hence, the regular Vlasov equation for
f({\bf x},{\bf p},t)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}({\bf x},{\bf p},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla f\;-\;\frac{d{\bf p}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}

(86)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\left\{f,\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;-\;4\pi\,e\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}f\right\},

and the Maxwell equations for
{\bf E}({\bf x},t)
and
{\bf B}({\bf x},t)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}\;-\;4\pi\,e\int f\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\;d^{3}{\bf p}

(87)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 4\pi\,c\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf B}\;-\;4\pi\,e\;\int f\;\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;d^{3}{\bf p},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}\;\equiv\;-\;4\pi\,c\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E},

(88)

can all be expressed in terms of functional derivatives of the Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian functional (
85
) with respect to
(f,{\bf E},{\bf B})
:
\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta f=K
,
\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta{\bf E}={\bf E}/4\pi
, and
\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta{\bf B}={\bf B}/4\pi
.

A.3
Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian Theory

We now directly construct the Hamiltonian structure of the Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
86
)-(
88
). For this purpose, we consider the time evolution of an arbitrary Vlasov-Maxwell functional
{\mathcal{F}}[f,{\bf E},{\bf B}]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\;d^{3}{\bf x}\,d^{3}{\bf p}\;+\;\int\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}\;+\;\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\right)d^{3}{\bf x}

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\;\left(\left\{f,\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;+\;4\pi\,e\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}f\right\}\right)d^{3}{\bf x}\,d^{3}{\bf p}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(4\pi\,c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf B}-4\pi\,e\int f\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;d^{3}{\bf p}\right)d^{3}{\bf x}-4\pi c\,\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\bf E}\;d^{3}{\bf x},

which can thus be expressed in Hamiltonian form in terms of functional derivatives of the Vlasov-Maxwell Hamiltonian functional (
85
):

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}\;\equiv\;\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}\right],

(90)

where the antisymmetric Vlasov-Maxwell functional bracket
[
3
,
4
]
is defined as

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int f\;\left\{\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta f}\right\}\;d^{3}{\bf x}\,d^{3}{\bf p}+4\pi\,e\int f\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right\}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta f}\right\}\right)\;d^{3}{\bf x}\,d^{3}{\bf p}

(91)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi c\int\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf B}\;-\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\right)d^{3}{\bf x}.

In addition, the Vlasov-Maxwell functional bracket (
91
) satisfies the Jacobi property

\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{G}},\;{\mathcal{H}}]\,\right]\;+\;\left[{\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{H}},\;{\mathcal{F}}]\,\right]\;+\;\left[{\mathcal{H}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{F}},\;{\mathcal{G}}]\,\right]\;\equiv 0,

which holds for arbitrary Vlasov-Maxwell functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{H}})
provided the magnetic field
{\bf B}
satisfies the divergenceless condition
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}=0
(see App. B of Ref.
[
27
]
for a proof of the Jacobi property for the Hamiltonian bracket (
91
)). This condition is inherited from the condition for which the noncanonical Poisson bracket (
83
) satisfies the Jacobi property
\{f,\,\{g,\,h\}\}+\{g,\,\{h,\,f\}\}+\{h,\,\{f,\,g\}\}=0
.

A.4
Antisymmetry of the Vlasov-Maxwell Bracket

We now note that the antisymmetry property of the Vlasov-Maxwell functional bracket (
91
) depends crucially on the expressions (
84
) and (
86
), where the contributions from the kinetic energy
K\equiv\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta f
and the electric field
{\bf E}\equiv 4\pi\,\delta{\mathcal{H}}/\delta{\bf E}
must be explicitly separated. Hence, even though the particle dynamics has a simple Hamiltonian form (
75
) in eight-dimensional extended phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p};t,w)
, the Hamiltonian formulation of the Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
86
)-(
88
) is expressed in terms of a Hamiltonian functional (
85
) and a Vlasov-Maxwell functional bracket (
91
), whose antisymmetric structure requires considering particle Hamiltonian dynamics in regular phase space
({\bf x},{\bf p})
, defined in Eq. (
84
).

Appendix B
Guiding-center Phase-space Transformation

w
and time

as conjugate canonical coordinates
[

, defined in a frame moving with the

Z^{\alpha}\equiv({\bf X},P_{\|},J,\zeta;W,t)
, in terms of which we obtain the guiding-center Lagrangian (

), with the guiding-center kinetic energy (

), as they appear in Ref.
[

. In this drifting frame, the perpendicular particle kinetic momentum

is assumed to be explicitly dependent on the local gyroangle
\zeta_{0}
, while
J_{0}
is the lowest-order gyroaction that is canonically conjugate to
\zeta_{0}
.

B.1
Guiding-center transformation in extended phase space

We begin with the extended Poincaré-Cartan one-form in the local drifting frame
[
11
]

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}\;+\;\epsilon\,\left(p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf p}_{\rm E}\;+\frac{}{}{\bf q}_{\bot}\right)\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf x}-w\,{\sf d}t

(92)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,\gamma_{1},

while the extended phase-space Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,\Phi\;-\;w\;+\;\frac{\epsilon}{2m}\left(p_{\|}^{2}+|{\bf p}_{E}|^{2}+|{\bf q}_{\bot}|^{2}+2\,{\bf p}_{E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle H_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,H_{1}.

Here, the dimensionless ordering parameter
\epsilon
is introduced through the mass renormalization
m\rightarrow\epsilon\,m

[
23
,
35
]
, which is meant to represent the mass-over-charge ratio

used as an expansion parameter in standard guiding-center theory
[

.

We now proceed with the transformation to the extended guiding-center phase-space coordinates
Z^{\alpha}\equiv z^{\alpha}+\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\alpha}+\cdots
, where
\Gamma_{{\rm gc}0}=(e/c)\,{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}-W\,{\sf d}t

at lowest order in
\epsilon

[
11
]

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\rm gc}1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf p}_{\rm E}\;+\frac{}{}{\bf q}_{\bot}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;-\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,G_{1}^{\bf X}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}

(94)

\displaystyle+\;\left(G_{1}^{W}\;+\;\frac{e}{c}\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,G_{1}^{\bf X}\right){\sf d}t,

\displaystyle H_{{\rm gc}1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\;+\;\mu\,B\;+\;\frac{|{\bf p}_{E}|^{2}}{2m}\;+\;\frac{{\bf p}_{E}}{m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}

\displaystyle-\;e\,G_{1}^{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Phi\;+\;G_{1}^{W},

where the first-order components

and

are primarily chosen to eliminate the gyroangle
\zeta

for the guiding-center magnetic moment. In our earlier work

kinetic momentum

in the guiding-center Poincaré-Cartan one-form

from the right side of Eq. (

\Gamma_{{\rm gc}1}\;\equiv\;P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}

(96)

and we find the first-order components

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bf p}_{\rm E}\;+\frac{}{}{\bf q}_{\bot}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB}\;\equiv\;-\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm E}\;+\frac{}{}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\bot}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{e}{c}\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,G_{1}^{\bf X}\;=\;\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm E}\;+\frac{}{}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\bot}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}.

\displaystyle H_{{\rm gc}1}

B.2
Guiding-center dynamics in extended phase space

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{\rm gc}}{\partial P_{\|}}\;=\;P_{\|}/m,

(102)

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

(103)

\displaystyle\frac{dw}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(e\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gc}\right)\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial{\bf A}^{*}}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{d\tau},

Next, from the extended Euler-Lagrange equations (

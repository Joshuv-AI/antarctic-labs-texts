Connections and L ∞ L_{\infty} liftings of semiregularity maps
Emma Lepri, Marco Manetti

Abstract
. Let ℰ ∗ \mathcal{E}^{*} be a finite complex of locally free sheaves on a complex manifold X X . We prove that to every connection of type ( 1 , 0 ) (1,0) on ℰ ∗ \mathcal{E}^{*} it is canonically associated an L ∞ L_{\infty} morphism g : A X 0 , ∗ ​ ( ℋ ​ o ​ m 𝒪 X ∗ ​ ( ℰ ∗ , ℰ ∗ ) ) ↝ A X ∗ , ∗ A X ≥ 2 , ∗ ​ [ 2 ] g\colon A^{0,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}[2] that lifts the 1-component of the Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map. 1.
Introduction

In the remarkable paper
[
7
]
, Buchweitz and Flenner generalise the classical Severi-Kodaira-Bloch’s semiregularity map to any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of locally finite projective dimension on an arbitrary complex space
X
. Their description involves exterior powers of the cotangent complex and when
X
is a smooth complex manifold it
reduces to a map

\sigma\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\prod_{q\geq 0}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q})\,

defined in terms of the Atiyah class
\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})
of
\mathcal{F}
, see also
[
21
]
.
More precisely, the Yoneda pairing

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{i}_{X})\times\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{j}_{X})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{i+j}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{i+j}_{X}),\qquad(a,b)\mapsto a\circ b,

allows to define the exponential of the opposite of the Atiyah class

\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F}))\in\prod_{q\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{q}_{X})\,.

Since
X
is assumed smooth, every coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
has locally finite projective dimension
and then there are defined the trace maps

\operatorname{Tr}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{i}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{j}_{X})\to H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}_{X}),\qquad i,j\geq 0\,.

It is worth recalling that, as proved by Atiyah for vector bundles and by Illusie in the general case
[
3
,
18
]
, when
X
is a projective manifold, then with respect to the Hodge decomposition in cohomology,
the trace of the above exponential gives the Chern character of
\mathcal{F}
, cf.
[
7
, p.137]
:

\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Tr}(\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})))\,.

The Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map is defined by the formula:

\sigma=\sum_{q\geq 0}\sigma_{q}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\prod_{q\geq 0}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q}),\qquad\sigma(c)=\operatorname{Tr}(\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F}))\circ c)\,.

The name semiregularity is motivated by the fact that when
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
for a locally complete intersection
Z
of codimension
q
, then the map
\sigma_{q-1}
is the same as the classical semiregularity map defined by Bloch
[
6
]
.

The semiregularity map is important both in the variational Hodge conjecture and in deformation theory: we refer to
[
6
]
for a discussion about the first subject,
while for the second we recall that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})

is the obstruction space for the functor of deformations of
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover, a classical result by Mukai and Artamkin
[
2
,
11
,
17
]
, asserts that the
0
th component of the semiregularity map

\sigma_{0}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}),\qquad\sigma_{0}(c)=\operatorname{Tr}(c),

annihilates obstructions; in other words the kernel of the trace map
\operatorname{Tr}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is an obstruction space for
\mathcal{F}
.

One of the main results of
[
7
]
is that if the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of
X
degenerates at
E_{1}
, then every curvilinear (and also semitrivial, see
[
25
]
) obstruction is annihilated
by the semiregularity map. However Buchweitz and Flenner left unanswered the question of whether the semiregularity map annihilates every obstruction.

As suggested in
[
7
]
, and then clarified in
[
10
,
16
,
28
]
, the correct way to interpret the semiregularity map is as the obstruction map of a morphism of deformation theories, with target in a product of (formal) intermediate Jacobians: this implies in particular that from the point of view of deformation theory it is more appropriate, for every
q
, to consider the composition

\tau_{q}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sigma_{q}}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q})=H^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q}[q])\xrightarrow{i_{q}}\mathbb{H}^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq q}[2q]),

where
\Omega^{\leq q}_{X}=(\oplus_{i=0}^{q}\Omega_{X}^{i}[-i],\partial)
is the truncated holomorphic de Rham complex and
i_{q}
is induced by the inclusion of complexes
\Omega_{X}^{q}[q]\subset\Omega_{X}^{\leq q}[2q]
.
Clearly, when the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of
X
degenerates at
E_{1}
the second map is injective and therefore
\tau_{q}
and
\sigma_{q}
have the same kernel.

Finally, the paper
[
28
]
by Pridham contains a proof that the maps
\tau_{q}
annihilate every obstruction, for every coherent sheaf on a smooth manifold, while the analogous result for the classical Bloch semiregularity map was previously proved, under some mild additional assumption, in

[
16
]
.
Pridham works in the framework of homotopy homogeneous functors in the category of simplicial commutative algebras in order to construct a morphism of deformation theories inducing the semiregularity map.

The aim of this paper is to construct, by elementary methods, an explicit morphism of deformation theories
lifting
\tau_{1}
in the framework of differential graded Lie algebras.
We expect that this explicit approach should also work for the lifting of
\tau_{q}
,
q>1
, although this appears, at this moment, extremely complicated from the computational point of view.

Roughly speaking, by a nowadays well established theory, the category of deformation problems over a field of characteristic
0
is equivalent to the homotopy category of DG-Lie algebras over the same field: two DG-Lie algebras are homotopy equivalent if they are quasi-isomorphic or, equivalently, if they have isomorphic
L_{\infty}
minimal models, and
every morphism in the homotopy category
can be represented by an
L_{\infty}
morphism. The passage from homotopy classes of DG-Lie algebras to the standard, and more geometric, description of deformation problems is given by taking solutions of
the Maurer-Cartan equation modulus gauge action, see e.g.
[
25
,
26
]
and references therein.

It is well known that the deformation theory of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is controlled by
R\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
(see e.g.
[
9
,
14
,
27
]
), considered as an element
in the homotopy category of differential graded Lie algebras: if
\mathcal{F}
admits a finite locally free resolution (e.g. if
X
is a smooth projective manifold)

(1.1)

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-n}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{E}^{0}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0

then a representative of
R\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
is given by the Dolbeault complex

A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
.

Recall that the quasi-isomorphic complexes
\mathcal{E}^{*}
and
\mathcal{F}
have the same Atiyah class and that
the hypercohomology of
\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2]
is computed by the truncated de Rham complex
A_{X}^{*,*}/A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}[2]
. As in the case of locally free sheaves (see e.g.
[
1
,
3
]
), the Atiyah class of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
can be computed by using connections of type
(1,0)
: we describe this construction in Section
2
.

Then the main results of this paper are:

Theorem 1.1

(=Corollary
4.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a complex manifold
X
admitting a finite locally free resolution
\mathcal{E}^{*}
as in
(
1.1
). Then the choice of a connection of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{i}
for every
i
gives an explicit lifting of

\tau_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

to an
L_{\infty}
morphism

g\colon A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\frac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}}[2]\,.

In the above theorem, by the term lifting we mean that
the linear component

g_{1}\colon A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\to\frac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}}[2]\,

is a morphism of complexes inducing
\tau_{1}
in cohomology. A brief review of
L_{\infty}
morphisms between differential graded Lie algebras will be given in Section
3
.

Since
X
is smooth by assumption, according to Hilbert’s syzygy theorem (see e.g.
[
19
, V.3.11]
), if a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
admits a locally free resolution, then it also admits a finite locally free resolution. The following result is an almost immediate consequence of the above theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(=Corollary
4.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a complex manifold
X
admitting a locally free resolution. Then every obstruction to the deformations of
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the kernel of the map

\tau_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{H}^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2]).

If the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of
X
degenerates at
E_{1}
, then
every obstruction to the deformations of
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the kernel of the map

\sigma_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to{H}^{3}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}),\qquad\sigma_{1}(a)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})\circ a).

It is worth pointing out that our approach is almost entirely algebraic and that
the above corollary holds also over every smooth separated scheme of finite type over a field of characteristic 0
[
22
]
: a proof of this purely algebraic analogous result is outlined in Section
5
.

1.1.
Notation

If
V=\oplus V^{i}
is either a graded vector space or a graded sheaf, we denote by
\overline{v}
the degree of a homogeneous element
v\in V
. For every integer
p
the symbol
[p]
denotes the shift functor, defined by
V[p]^{i}=V^{p+i}
.

For a complex manifold
X
, its de Rham complex is denoted by
(A^{*,*}_{X},d=\partial+\overline{\partial})
and the subcomplexes of the Hodge filtrations by
A^{\geq p,*}_{X}
.
The holomorphic de Rham complex of
X
is denoted by
\Omega_{X}^{*}=(\oplus_{i\geq 0}\Omega_{X}^{i}[-i],\partial)
.

2.
Connections and Atiyah classes

The theory of connections of type
(1,0)
on holomorphic vector bundles
[
1
,
3
,
19
]
extends without difficulty to every complex of locally free sheaves. For simplicity of exposition we consider here only the case of finite complexes, which is completely sufficient for our applications.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let

\mathcal{E}^{*}\colon\qquad 0\to\mathcal{E}^{p}\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\mathcal{E}^{p+1}\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\cdots\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\mathcal{E}^{q}\to 0,\qquad\quad p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\delta^{2}=0,

be a fixed finite complex of locally free sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. We denote by

{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
the graded sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
:

{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})=\bigoplus_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}),\qquad{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})=\prod_{j}{{\mathcal{H}}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{j},\mathcal{E}^{i+j})\,.

Then
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
is a sheaf of locally free DG-Lie algebras over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
with the bracket equal to the graded commutator

[f,g]=fg-(-1)^{\overline{f}\,\overline{g}}gf

and the differential given by

f\mapsto[\delta,f]=\delta f-(-1)^{\overline{f}}f\delta\,.

For every
a,b,r

denote by
\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})\simeq\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{r}
the sheaf of differential forms of type
(a,b)
with coefficients in
\mathcal{E}^{r}
, and by

\overline{\partial}\colon\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})\to\mathcal{A}^{a,b+1}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r}),\qquad\overline{\partial}(\phi\cdot e)=\overline{\partial}(\phi)\cdot e

the Dolbeault differential.

We consider

\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})=\bigoplus_{a,b,r}\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})

as a graded sheaf on
X
, where the elements of
\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})
have degree
a+b+r
.
Unless otherwise specified, when we write
\phi\cdot e\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
we mean that
\phi
is a differential form and
e
is a holomorphic section of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
, or a germ of one.
Sometimes, for notational simplicity, whenever
\psi\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
and

h\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
we denote by
\psi\cdot h\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
their product, namely the bilinear extension of
\psi\cdot(\phi\cdot e)=(\psi\wedge\phi)\cdot e
,
\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
,
e\in\mathcal{E}^{*}
.

In accordance with the Koszul sign rule, the differential
\delta
can be extended to a differential

\delta\colon\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})\to\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r+1}),\qquad\delta(\phi\cdot e)=(-1)^{\overline{\phi}}\,\phi\cdot\delta(e)\,.

We have that
\overline{\partial}^{2}=\delta^{2}=0
and
[\overline{\partial},\delta]=\overline{\partial}\delta+\delta\overline{\partial}=0
, so that

\overline{\partial}+\delta
is a differential in
\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
.

Therefore the space of
\mathbb{C}
-linear morphisms of sheaves

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))

carries a natural structure of differential graded associative algebra: the product is given by composition and the differential is the graded commutator with
\overline{\partial}+\delta
.

Denoting by
A^{a,b}_{X}(-)
the global sections of
\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(-)
we have two differential graded subalgebras

A^{0,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\subset A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\subset\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})),

where for

\omega,\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X},\quad f\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}),\quad e\in\mathcal{E}^{*}

one has that

(\omega\cdot f)(\eta\cdot e)=(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}(\omega\wedge\eta)\cdot f(e)\,,

so that the elements of
A^{a,b}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
have degree
a+b+n
.
For every
a\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
we have

(2.1)

\overline{\partial}a=[\overline{\partial},a]

where the bracket on the right is intended in the DG-Lie algebra
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))
. In fact, for
\omega,\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
,
f\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
and
e\in\mathcal{E}^{*}
we have:

\displaystyle[\overline{\partial},\omega\cdot f](\eta\cdot e)

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}((-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}\omega\wedge\eta\cdot f(e))-(-1)^{\overline{\omega}+\overline{f}}(\omega\cdot f)(\overline{\partial}(\eta)\cdot e)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}\overline{\partial}(\omega)\wedge\eta\cdot f(e)+(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}+\overline{\omega}}\omega\wedge\overline{\partial}(\eta)\cdot f(e)-(-1)^{\overline{\omega}+\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}\omega\wedge\overline{\partial}(\eta)\cdot f(e)

\displaystyle=(\overline{\partial}(\omega)\cdot f)(\eta\cdot e).

The composition product in
A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
works in the following way:

\omega,\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X},\quad f,g\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})

(\omega\cdot f)(\eta\cdot g)=(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}(\omega\wedge\eta)\cdot fg\,,

and the commutator is

[\omega\cdot f,\eta\cdot g]=(\omega\cdot f)(\eta\cdot g)-(-1)^{(\overline{\omega}+\overline{f})(\overline{\eta}+\overline{g})}(\eta\cdot g)(\omega\cdot f)=(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{\eta}}(\omega\wedge\eta)\cdot[f,g]\,.

The above commutator and the differential
[\delta+\overline{\partial},-]=[\delta,-]+\overline{\partial}
give

A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
a structure of DG-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{*}
be a finite complex of locally free sheaves on a complex manifold
X
.
A connection of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is an element
D
of

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))
such that:

(1)

D(\phi\cdot s)=d\phi\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{\phi}}\phi\cdot D(s)
, with
s\in\mathcal{E}^{r}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
;

(2)

if
s\in\mathcal{E}^{r}
is a holomorphic section then
D(s)\in\mathcal{A}^{1,0}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})
.

The second condition is equivalent to
D=D^{1,0}+\overline{\partial}
, with

D^{1,0}\colon\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})\to\mathcal{A}^{a+1,b}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{r})
for every
a,b,r
.

Basically it is the same as giving a connection which is compatible with the holomorphic structure on every vector bundle of the complex. In particular, connections of type
(1,0)
always exist.

From now on, let
D=D^{1,0}+\overline{\partial}
be a fixed connection of type
(1,0)
on a fixed finite complex
\mathcal{E}^{*}

of locally free sheaves. Denote by

\nabla=[D-\overline{\partial},-]=[D^{1,0},-]\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}),\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))

the adjoint operator of the
(1,0)
-component of the connection
D
.

As in the classical case, the adjoint operator
[D,-]
is a connection of type
(1,0)
in the complex of locally free sheaves
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
. We need this fact only in the weaker form given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

In the above situation,
for every
h\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
we have

[D,h],\,\nabla(h)\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\,.

More precisely, if
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{a,b}_{X}
and
g\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
, then

\nabla(\eta\cdot g)\in A^{a+1,b}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
.

Proof.

Since every
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is locally free, we can describe
A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
as the set of morphisms of graded sheaves
h\colon\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
that are

\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
linear, i.e.,
h(f\cdot s)=(-1)^{\overline{h}\,\overline{f}}f\cdot h(s)
,
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
,
s\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
.

Thus, for every
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
and
s\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
we have

\begin{split}[D,h](fs)&=(-1)^{\overline{h}\,\overline{f}}D(fh(s))-(-1)^{\overline{h}}h(df\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{f}}f\cdot D(s))\\
&=(-1)^{\overline{h}\,\overline{f}}(df\cdot h(s)+(-1)^{\overline{f}}fD(h(s)))-(-1)^{\overline{h}+\overline{h}(\overline{f}+1)}df\cdot h(s)-(-1)^{\overline{h}+\overline{f}+\overline{h}\overline{f}}f\cdot h(D(s))\\
&=(-1)^{(\overline{h}+1)\overline{f}}f\cdot[D,h](s).\end{split}

This proves that
[D,h]\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
. According to (
2.1
) we also have

\nabla(h)=[D,h]-\overline{\partial}(a)\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\,.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

In the above notation, define

u=[D,\overline{\partial}+\delta]=[\overline{\partial}+\delta,D]=\nabla(\overline{\partial}+\delta)\,,

where the last equality follows by
[\overline{\partial},\overline{\partial}+\delta]=0
. Then

u\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
, and more precisely

u\in A^{1,1}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\oplus A^{1,0}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\,.

Proof.

The element
u\colon\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
is a morphism of graded sheaves of even degree and we need to show that it is
\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
-linear. By Lemma
2.2

we have
[D,\delta]\in A^{1,0}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
and we only need to prove that
[D,\overline{\partial}]
is
\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
-linear.

For
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X},s\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
we have:

\begin{split}[D,\overline{\partial}](fs)&=D(\overline{\partial}f\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{f}}f\cdot\overline{\partial}s)+\overline{\partial}(df\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{f}}fD(s))\\[3.0pt]
&=d(\overline{\partial}f)\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{f}+1}\overline{\partial}f\cdot D(s)+(-1)^{\overline{f}}df\cdot\overline{\partial}s+fD(\overline{\partial}(s))\\
&\quad+\overline{\partial}(df)\cdot s+(-1)^{\overline{f}+1}df\cdot\overline{\partial}s+(-1)^{\overline{f}}\overline{\partial}f\cdot D(s)+f\overline{\partial}(D(s))\\
&=f[D,\overline{\partial}](s).\end{split}

Therefore
[D,\overline{\partial}]\in A^{1,1}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
.
∎

Definition 2.4

(Atiyah class)
.

The Atiyah cocycle
u\in A^{1,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
of the connection
D
of type
(1,0)
as above is defined by the formula

u=[D,\overline{\partial}+\delta]=[\overline{\partial}+\delta,D]=\nabla(\overline{\partial}+\delta)\,.

The fact that
[\overline{\partial}+\delta,u]=\overline{\partial}(u)+[\delta,u]=0
, i.e., that
u
is a cocycle
follows immediately by the Jacobi identity:

[\overline{\partial}+\delta,u]=[\overline{\partial}+\delta,[\overline{\partial}+\delta,D]]=\frac{1}{2}[[\overline{\partial}+\delta,\overline{\partial}+\delta],D]=0\,.

The Atiyah class of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is the cohomology class of the Atiyah cocycle

\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{E}^{*})=[u]\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(A^{1,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})))=\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\Omega^{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{*})\,.

The Atiyah class does not depend on the choice of the connection of type
(1,0)
: two such connections
D,D^{\prime}
differ by
a\in A^{1,0}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
, so that

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=[D^{\prime},\overline{\partial}+\delta]=[D+a,\overline{\partial}+\delta]=[D,\overline{\partial}+\delta]+[a,\overline{\partial}+\delta]=u+[\delta,a]+\overline{\partial}a,

and
u
and
u^{\prime}
represent the same cohomology class.

It is straightforward to verify that the above definition of
\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
is completely equivalent to the one
given in standard literature, especially
[
14
, Section 10.1]
and
[
7
]
: therefore the Atiyah class is a homotopy invariant and depends only on the isomorphism class of the complex
\mathcal{E}^{*}
in the derived category of bounded complexes of locally free sheaves.

In particular, if
X
is smooth projective and
\mathcal{E}^{*}\to\mathcal{F}
is a finite locally free resolution of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, then the Atiyah class of
\mathcal{F}
is properly defined as
\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
and depends only on the class of
\mathcal{F}
in the bounded derived category of
X
.

Lemma 2.5
.

In the above setup,
for every
a\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
we have:

[\delta+\overline{\partial},\nabla](a)=[u,a],\qquad[\nabla(\overline{\partial}),a]=\nabla(\overline{\partial}a)+\overline{\partial}\nabla(a)\,.

In particular, if
a
is closed, then
[u,a]
is exact.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle[u,a]=[[D^{1,0},\delta+\overline{\partial}],a]=[D^{1,0},[\delta+\overline{\partial},a]]+[\delta+\overline{\partial},[D^{1,0},a]]=[\delta+\overline{\partial},\nabla](a),

while for the last equality, by (
2.1
),

[\nabla(\overline{\partial}),a]=[[D^{1,0},\overline{\partial}],a]=[D^{1,0},[\overline{\partial},a]]+[\overline{\partial},[D^{1,0},a]]=[D-\overline{\partial},\overline{\partial}a]+\overline{\partial}[D^{1,0},a]=\nabla(\overline{\partial}a)+\overline{\partial}\nabla(a).

∎

The trace operator
\operatorname{Tr}\colon{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
can be extended to

\operatorname{Tr}\colon A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\to A^{*,*}_{X},\quad\operatorname{Tr}(\omega\cdot f)=\omega\operatorname{Tr}(f).

If we consider the DG-Lie algebra structure on
A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
given above, with differential
[\delta,-]+\overline{\partial}
, and endow
A^{*,*}_{X}
with trivial bracket and differential
\overline{\partial}
, then the trace operator is a morphism of DG-Lie algebras, so that

\operatorname{Tr}([\delta,a]+\overline{\partial}a)=\overline{\partial}\operatorname{Tr}(a).

Lemma 2.6
.

In the above setup, for every

h\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
we have
\operatorname{Tr}([D,h])=d\operatorname{Tr}(h)
.

Proof.

By linearity it is sufficient to consider the case

h=\eta\cdot g
, with
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{*,*}_{X}
and
g\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
.
It is clear that it is enough to consider
g
of degree
0
, and by linearity we may assume
g
concentrated in one degree, i.e.,
g=g_{l}\colon\mathcal{E}^{l}\to\mathcal{E}^{l}
. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
be a local basis of holomorphic sections for
\mathcal{E}^{l}
, and let

g(e_{i})=\sum_{j}a_{ij}e_{j},\quad D(e_{i})=\sum_{j}\omega_{ij}e_{j},\quad\operatorname{Tr}(g)=(-1)^{l}\sum_{i}a_{ii}.

Then

d\operatorname{Tr}(\eta\cdot g)=d(\eta\operatorname{Tr}(g))=d\eta\operatorname{Tr}(g)+(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta d\operatorname{Tr}(g),

\displaystyle(\nabla+\overline{\partial})(\eta\cdot g)(e_{i})=D(\sum_{j}\eta a_{ij}e_{j})-(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}(\eta\cdot g)(\sum_{j}\omega_{ij}e_{j})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j}d(\eta)a_{ij}e_{j}+\sum_{j}(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta\wedge d(a_{ij})e_{j}+\sum_{j}(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta a_{ij}\wedge D(e_{j})-(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\sum_{j}\eta\wedge\omega_{ij}g(e_{j})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k}d(\eta)a_{ik}e_{k}+\sum_{k}(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta\wedge d(a_{ik})e_{k}+\sum_{j,k}(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta a_{ij}\wedge\omega_{jk}e_{k}-(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\sum_{j,k}\eta\wedge\omega_{ij}a_{jk}e_{k}\,.

Therefore

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}((\nabla+\overline{\partial})(\eta\cdot g))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sum_{i}\left(d\eta a_{ii}+(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta d(a_{ii})+\sum_{j}\left((-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta\wedge\omega_{ji}a_{ij}-(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta\wedge\omega_{ij}a_{ji}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l}\sum_{i}\left(d\eta a_{ii}+(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta d(a_{ii})\right)=d\eta\operatorname{Tr}(g)+(-1)^{\overline{\eta}}\eta d\operatorname{Tr}(g).

∎

Remark 2.7
.

It is useful to note that since the trace
is a morphism of differential graded Lie algebras,

\operatorname{Tr}([D,h])=\operatorname{Tr}((\nabla+\overline{\partial})(h))=d\operatorname{Tr}(h)
of Lemma
2.6
is equivalent to

\operatorname{Tr}(\nabla(h))=\partial\operatorname{Tr}(h).

3.
From connections and cyclic forms to
L_{\infty}
morphisms

As in the previous section, let

\mathcal{E}^{*}\colon\qquad 0\to\mathcal{E}^{p}\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\mathcal{E}^{p+1}\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\cdots\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\mathcal{E}^{q}\to 0

be a fixed finite complex of locally free sheaves on a complex manifold
X
.

Definition 3.1
.

By a cyclic (bilinear) form on the sheaf of DG-Lie algebras

{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
we mean a
graded symmetric
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-bilinear product of degree
0

{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\times{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{\langle-,-\rangle}\mathcal{O}_{X},

such that

\langle f,[g,h]\rangle=\langle[f,g],h\rangle\qquad\forall\;f,g,h\,.

Equivalently, for every
f,g,h
we have

\langle[f,g],h\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{g}}\langle g,[f,h]\rangle=0

i.e.,
\langle-,-\rangle
is invariant under the adjoint action. In particular

(3.1)

\langle[\delta,g],h\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{g}}\langle g,[\delta,h]\rangle=0\,.

Notice that (
3.1
) is equivalent to the fact that the bilinear form

\langle-,-\rangle
is closed in the dual of
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})^{\odot 2}
.

Every cyclic form on
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
has a natural extension

A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))^{\odot 2}\xrightarrow{\langle-,-\rangle}A^{*,*}_{X},\qquad\langle\phi f,\psi g\rangle=(-1)^{\overline{f}\,\overline{\psi}}\phi\wedge\psi\langle f,g\rangle,

and it is immediate to check that, for
f,g\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
:

(3.2)

\overline{\partial}\langle f,g\rangle=\langle\overline{\partial}f,g\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{f}}\langle f,\overline{\partial}g\rangle,

and then
\langle-,-\rangle
is
\overline{\partial}+[\delta,-]
closed.
Cyclic forms have received a lot of attention in several recent papers; for instance cyclic forms that are nondegenerate in cohomology play a central role in the proof of the formality conjecture for polystable sheaves on projective surfaces with torsion canonical bundles, given in
[
5
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

We shall say that a connection
D
of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is compatible with the cyclic form
\langle-,-\rangle
if

\langle[D,f],g\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{f}}\langle f,[D,g]\rangle=d\langle f,g\rangle\,,

or equivalently if

\langle\nabla(f),g\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{f}}\langle f,\nabla(g)\rangle=\partial\langle f,g\rangle\,.

for every
f,g\in A^{*,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
.

Example 3.3
.

According to Lemma
2.6
and Remark
2.7
, for every
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
the form

\langle f,g\rangle=a\operatorname{Tr}(fg)+b\operatorname{Tr}(f)\operatorname{Tr}(g)

is a cyclic form of degree
0
compatible with every connection of type (1,0).

We assume that the reader is familiar with the notion and basic properties of DG-Lie algebras and

L_{\infty}
morphisms between them, see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
20
,
26
]
and references therein. For the reader’s convenience and to fix the sign convention, we only recall here the definition of an

L_{\infty}
morphism of DG-Lie algebras in the version that we use for
explicit computations.

Let
V
be a graded vector space over a field of characteristic 0.
Given
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}

homogeneous vectors of
V
and a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we denote by

\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=\pm 1
the antisymmetric Koszul sign, defined by the relation

v_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(n)}=\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\,v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\,

in the
n
th exterior power
V^{\wedge n}
. We shall simply write
\chi(\sigma)
instead of

\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
when the vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
are clear from the context.
For instance, if
\sigma
is the transposition exchanging 1 and 2 we have

\chi(\sigma)=-(-1)^{\overline{v_{1}}\,\overline{v_{2}}}
.
Notice that if every
v_{i}
has odd degree, then

\chi(\sigma)=1
for every
\sigma
.

Because of the universal property of wedge powers, we shall constantly interpret every linear map

V^{\wedge p}\to W
as a graded skew-symmetric
p
-linear map
V\times\cdots\times V\to W
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(V,\delta,[-,-])
and
(L,d,\{-,-\})
be DG-Lie algebras over the same field. An

L_{\infty}
morphism
g\colon V\rightsquigarrow L
is a sequence of linear maps

g_{n}\colon V^{\wedge n}\to L
,
n\geq 1
, with
g_{n}
of degree
1-n
such that
g_{1}
is a morphism of complexes, while for every

n\geq 2
and every

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
homogeneous we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}&\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt\sigma\in S(p,n-p)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\chi(\sigma)(-1)^{(1-n+p)(|v_{\sigma(1)}|+\cdots+|v_{\sigma(p)}|-p)}\left\{g_{p}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(p)}),\vphantom{\sum}g_{n-p}(v_{\sigma(p+1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)})\right\}\\
&+dg_{n}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S(1,n-1)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n}(\delta(v_{\sigma(1)}),v_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(-1)^{n-2}\sum_{\sigma\in S(2,n-2)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n-1}([v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)}],v_{\sigma(3)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}).\end{split}

Notice that the morphism of complexes
g_{1}
factors to a morphism
g_{1}\colon H^{*}(V)\to H^{*}(L)
, and the above condition for
n=2
, which is equivalent to

g_{1}([v_{1},v_{2}])-\{g_{1}(v_{1}),g_{1}(v_{2})\}=dg_{2}(v_{1},v_{2})+g_{2}(\delta v_{1},v_{2})+(-1)^{\overline{v_{1}}}g_{2}(v_{1},\delta v_{2}),

tells us that
g_{1}
is a Lie morphism up to homotopy. In particular, the map
g_{1}\colon H^{*}(V)\to H^{*}(L)

is a morphism of graded Lie algebras.

Conversely, given a morphism of graded Lie algebras
\tau\colon H^{*}(V)\to H^{*}(L)
we shall say that an

L_{\infty}
morphism
g\colon V\rightsquigarrow L
lifts
\tau
if
g_{1}
induces
\tau
in cohomology.

In this paper we deal with
L_{\infty}
morphisms where the target
L
is an abelian DG-Lie algebra: this means that
\{-,-\}=0
and the above definition reduces to:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(V,\delta,[-,-])
be a DG-Lie algebra and
(L,d)
an abelian DG-Lie algebra. An
L_{\infty}
morphism
g\colon V\rightsquigarrow L
is a sequence of maps

g_{n}\colon V^{\wedge n}\to L
,
n\geq 1
, with
g_{n}
of degree
1-n
such that the following conditions

C_{n}
,
n=1,2,3,\ldots
, are satisfied:

C_{1}
:

g_{1}\delta=dg_{1}
;

C_{n},\;n\geq 2
:

for every

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
homogeneous we have

\begin{split}dg_{n}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})&=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S(1,n-1)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n}(\delta v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)})\\
&\quad+(-1)^{n-2}\sum_{\sigma\in S(2,n-2)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n-1}([v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)}],v_{\sigma(3)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}).\end{split}

Notice that if
g_{n}=0
for every
n\geq N
then
C_{n}
is trivially satisfied for every

n>N
.

We are now ready to prove the main result of this paper.
In the following we consider
the shifted quotient
\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}[2]
of the de Rham complex by the
2
nd subcomplex of the Hodge filtration as a DG-Lie algebra with trivial bracket.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{*}
be a finite complex of locally free sheaves on a complex manifold
X
and let
\langle-,-\rangle
be a cyclic form of degree
0
on
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
which is compatible with a connection
D
of type
(1,0)
. Then
there is an
L_{\infty}
morphism between DG-Lie algebras over the field
\mathbb{C}

g\colon A^{0,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}[2]

with components

\begin{split}g_{1}(f)&=\langle u,f\rangle=\langle f,u\rangle\in A^{1,*}_{X}[2],\\[4.0pt]
g_{2}(f,g)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\nabla(f),g\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{g}}\langle\nabla(g),f\rangle\right)\in A^{1,*}_{X}[2],\\[4.0pt]
g_{3}(f,g,h)&=-\frac{1}{2}\langle f,[g,h]\rangle\in A^{0,*}_{X}[2],\end{split}

and
g_{n}=0
for every
n>3
. As in the notation above,
u=\nabla(\overline{\partial}+\delta)
is the Atiyah cocycle of the connection
D
.

Notice that the definition of
g
only involves the DG-Lie structure of

A^{0,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
and not the associative composition product.

Proof.

Since the theorem gives explicit formulas for the components
g_{n}
, the proof reduces to a straightforward computation. Since
g_{n}=0
for every
n\geq 4

we need to check the conditions
C_{n}
of Definition
3.5
for
n=1,2,3,4
.
For
C_{1}
we have to prove that

dg_{1}(a)=g_{1}([\delta,a]+\overline{\partial}a).

This follows from the fact that
[\delta,u]+\overline{\partial}u=0
and that on the subcomplex
A^{1,*}_{X}\subseteq\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}
we have
d=\overline{\partial}
:

\displaystyle g_{1}([\delta,a]+\overline{\partial}a)

\displaystyle=\langle u,[\delta,a]+\overline{\partial}a\rangle=-\langle[\delta,u],a\rangle+\overline{\partial}\langle u,a\rangle-\langle\overline{\partial}u,a\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle[\delta,u]+\overline{\partial}u,a\rangle+\overline{\partial}\langle u,a\rangle=\overline{\partial}\langle u,a\rangle=dg_{1}(a).

The condition
C_{2}
is

g_{2}([\delta,a_{1}]+\overline{\partial}a_{1},a_{2})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}g_{2}(a_{1},[\delta,a_{2}]+\overline{\partial}a_{2})=g_{1}([a_{1},a_{2}])-dg_{2}(a_{1},a_{2}).

On the left hand side we have

\displaystyle g_{2}([\delta,a_{1}]+

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}a_{1},a_{2})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}g_{2}(a_{1},[\delta,a_{2}]+\overline{\partial}a_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle\nabla([\delta,a_{1}]),a_{2}\rangle+\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),[\delta,a_{1}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle\Big)+\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\Big(\langle\nabla(a_{1}),[\delta,a_{2}]\rangle+\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla([\delta,a_{2}]),a_{1}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle[\nabla(\delta),a_{1}]),a_{2}\rangle-\langle[\delta,\nabla(a_{1})]),a_{2}\rangle+\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),[\delta,a_{1}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),[\delta,a_{2}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle[\nabla(\delta),a_{2}]),a_{1}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle[\delta,\nabla(a_{2})]),a_{1}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(\delta),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle\Big).

Using Lemma
2.5
, the right hand side is:

\displaystyle g_{1}([a_{1},a_{2}])

\displaystyle-dg_{2}(a_{1},a_{2})=\langle\nabla(\delta)+\nabla(\overline{\partial}),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(\delta),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle[\nabla(\overline{\partial}),a_{1}],a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle[\nabla(\overline{\partial}),a_{2}],a_{1}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(\delta),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle+\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle+\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\overline{\partial}\nabla(a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(\delta),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\Big((-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),\overline{\partial}a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),\overline{\partial}a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{1}),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(\overline{\partial}a_{2}),a_{1}\rangle\Big),

and this proves
C_{2}
. For
C_{3}
we need to check that

\displaystyle dg_{3}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle=g_{3}([\delta,a_{1}]+\overline{\partial}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}g_{3}([\delta,a_{2}]+\overline{\partial}a_{2},a_{1},a_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})}g_{3}([\delta,a_{3}]+\overline{\partial}a_{3},a_{1},a_{2})-g_{2}([a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}g_{2}([a_{1},a_{3}],a_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}g_{2}([a_{2},a_{3}],a_{1}).

Using the compatibility of the connection and the cyclic form, the terms involving
g_{2}
can be expanded as:

\displaystyle-g_{2}([a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}g_{2}([a_{1},a_{3}],a_{2})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}g_{2}([a_{2},a_{3}],a_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle\nabla([a_{1},a_{2}]),a_{3}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})}\langle\nabla(a_{3}),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\big(\langle\nabla([a_{1},a_{3}]),a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),[a_{1},a_{3}]\rangle\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\big(\langle\nabla([a_{2},a_{3}]),a_{1}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle\nabla(a_{1}),[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle[\nabla(a_{1}),a_{2}],a_{3}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle[a_{1},\nabla(a_{2})],a_{3}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})}\langle\nabla(a_{3}),[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle[\nabla(a_{1}),a_{3}],a_{2}\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}+\overline{a_{1}}}\langle[a_{1},\nabla(a_{3})],a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}\langle\nabla(a_{2}),[a_{1},a_{3}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle[\nabla(a_{2}),a_{3}],a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle[a_{2},\nabla(a_{3})],a_{1}\rangle-\langle\nabla(a_{1}),[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle\Big)=-\frac{1}{2}\partial\langle a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle g_{3}([\delta,a_{1}]+\overline{\partial}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}}g_{3}([\delta,a_{2}]+\overline{\partial}a_{2},a_{1},a_{3})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})}g_{3}([\delta,a_{3}]+\overline{\partial}a_{3},a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle[\delta,a_{1}],[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle a_{1},[[\delta,a_{2}],a_{3}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle a_{1},[a_{2},[\delta,a_{3}]]\rangle+\langle\overline{\partial}a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}}\langle a_{1},[\overline{\partial}a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}}}\langle a_{1},[a_{2},\overline{\partial}a_{3}]\rangle\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\partial}\langle a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle

so that we obtain

dg_{3}(a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}])=-\frac{1}{2}d\langle a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\partial}\langle a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\partial\langle a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle.

Lastly, the condition
C_{4}
is

\displaystyle g_{3}([a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3},a_{4})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}g_{3}([a_{1},a_{3}],a_{2},a_{4})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}g_{3}([a_{1},a_{4}],a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}g_{3}([a_{2},a_{3}],a_{1},a_{4})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}\ \overline{a_{4}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{4}}}g_{3}([a_{2},a_{4}],a_{1},a_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})(\overline{a_{3}}+\overline{a_{4}})}g_{3}([a_{3},a_{4}],a_{1},a_{2})=0.

We have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\langle[a_{1},a_{2}],[a_{3},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\frac{1}{2}\langle[a_{1},a_{3}],[a_{2},a_{4}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\frac{1}{2}\langle[a_{1},a_{4}],[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\frac{1}{2}\langle[a_{2},a_{3}],[a_{1},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}\ \overline{a_{4}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{4}}}\frac{1}{2}\langle[a_{2},a_{4}],[a_{1},a_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})(\overline{a_{3}}+\overline{a_{4}})}\frac{1}{2}\langle[a_{3},a_{4}],[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[a_{1},a_{2}],[a_{3},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle[a_{1},a_{3}],[a_{2},a_{4}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle[a_{1},a_{4}],[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1},[a_{2},[a_{3},a_{4}]]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle a_{1},[a_{3},[a_{2},a_{4}]]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle a_{1},[a_{4},[a_{2},a_{3}]]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1},[a_{2},[a_{3},a_{4}]]-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}[a_{3},[a_{2},a_{4}]]-[[a_{2},a_{3}],a_{4}]\rangle=0\,.

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{*}
be a finite complex of locally free sheaves on a complex manifold
X
. Then
every connection
D
of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
gives an

L_{\infty}
morphism between DG-Lie algebras on the field
\mathbb{C}

g\colon A^{0,*}_{X}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}[2]

with components

\begin{split}g_{1}(f)&=-\operatorname{Tr}(uf)\in A^{1,*}_{X}[2]\\[4.0pt]
g_{2}(f,g)&=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\nabla(f)g-(-1)^{\overline{f}\overline{g}}\nabla(g)f\right)\in A^{1,*}_{X}[2]\\[4.0pt]
g_{3}(f,g,h)&=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(f[g,h])\in A^{0,*}_{X}[2],\end{split}

and
g_{n}=0
for every
n>3
.

Proof.

Use the cyclic form
\langle f,g\rangle=-\operatorname{Tr}(fg)
in Theorem
3.6
.∎

Remark 3.8
.

The homotopy class of the
L_{\infty}
morphism
g
of Corollary
3.7
depends on the choice of the connection. This also holds for complex analytic connections, that is for connections where the Atiyah cocycle vanishes
u=0
and therefore
g_{1}=0
. This implies that

g_{2}
factors to a bilinear graded skewsymmetric map in cohomology

g_{2}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\times\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathbb{H}^{i+j+1}\left(\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}\right)

that depends only on the homotopy class of
g
.

In order to see that the above maps depend on the connection it is sufficient to consider the example of a trivial bundle of rank
2
over an elliptic curve
X
.
In this case, since
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is trivial, every complex analytic connection is of type
D=d+\theta
, where

\theta
is a
2\times 2
matrix with values in
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
and then

\nabla=\overline{\partial}+[\theta,-]
. Similarly
\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
is identified with the Lie algebra
M_{2,2}(\mathbb{C})
of

2\times 2
matrices with constant coefficients and therefore

g_{2}(a,b)=-\frac{1}{2}([\theta,a]b-[\theta,b]a)\in H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X})=\mathbb{H}^{1}\left(\dfrac{A^{*,*}_{X}}{A^{\geq 2,*}_{X}}\right)\,.

If
dz
is a generator of
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
and
\theta=Cdz
, with
C\in M_{2,2}(\mathbb{C})
,
the conclusion follows by observing that the rank of the bilinear map

M_{2,2}(\mathbb{C})\times M_{2,2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},\quad(A,B)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}([C,A]B-[C,B]A)=\operatorname{Tr}(C[A,B]),

is equal to
0
when
C
is a multiple of the identity and is 2 otherwise.

4.
Semiregularity and deformations of coherent sheaves

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a complex manifold
X
equipped with a finite locally free resolution

(4.1)

0\to\mathcal{E}^{-n}\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\cdots\xrightarrow{\,\delta\,}\mathcal{E}^{0}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0\,.

Then the deformation theory of
\mathcal{F}
is controlled by the DG-Lie algebra

A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
defined in the previous sections for arbitrary finite complexes of locally free sheaves. This means that, over a local Artin
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
, the deformations of
\mathcal{F}
over
A
are determined by solutions of the Maurer-Cartan equations

\overline{\partial}x+[\delta,x]+\frac{1}{2}[x,x]=0,\qquad x\in\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{X}^{0,i}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{1-i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A},

with
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
the maximal ideal of
A
. Moreover two solutions of the Maurer-Cartan equation give isomorphic deformations if and only if they are gauge equivalent: when
\mathcal{F}
is locally free this fact is nowadays standard
[
12
,
26
]
and extends quite easily to the general case, see e.g.
[
5
,
9
,
27
]
.

The resolution (
4.1
) can also be used for an explicit description of the (modified) semiregularity map

\tau=\sum_{p\geq 0}\tau_{p}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq p}[2p]),\qquad\tau(a)=\operatorname{Tr}(\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F}))\circ a)\,.

Since the quasi-isomorphic complexes
\mathcal{E}^{*}
and
\mathcal{F}
have the same Atiyah class we have

\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})=[u]
, where
u\in A_{X}^{1,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))
is the Atiyah cocycle of a connection of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
. Moreover
the hypercohomology of
\Omega_{X}^{\leq p}[2p]
is computed by the truncated de Rham complex
A_{X}^{*,*}/A_{X}^{>p,*}[2p]
, and then every component
\tau_{p}
is induced in cohomology by the morphism of complexes

A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\to\frac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{>p,*}}[2p],\qquad f\mapsto\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!}\operatorname{Tr}(u^{p}f)\,.

Then, Corollary
3.7
immediately gives the following theorem.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a complex manifold
X
equipped with a finite locally free resolution
\mathcal{E}^{*}
. Then every connection of type
(1,0)
on the resolution
\mathcal{E}^{*}
gives a lifting of

\tau_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

to an
L_{\infty}
morphism

g\colon A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\frac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{>1,*}}[2]\,.

Now the application to deformation theory of Corollary
4.1
follows by a completely standard argument, the same used and explained, for instance, in
[
15
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
26
]
: every
L_{\infty}
morphism

g\colon V\rightsquigarrow L
of DG-Lie algebras induces a morphism of deformation functors
\operatorname{Def}_{V}\to\operatorname{Def}_{L}
(here
\operatorname{Def}
means the functor of Maurer-Cartan solution modulus gauge action) such that
the induced map in cohomology commutes with obstruction maps. If
L
is abelian, then every obstruction in
\operatorname{Def}_{L}
is trivial, hence every obstruction in
\operatorname{Def}_{V}
belongs to the kernel of
g_{1}\colon H^{2}(V)\to H^{2}(L)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on a complex manifold
X
admitting a locally free resolution. Then every obstruction to the deformations of
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the kernel of the map

\tau_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{H}^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2]).

If the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of
X
degenerates at
E_{1}
, then
every obstruction to the deformations of
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the kernel of the map

\sigma_{1}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to{H}^{3}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}),\qquad\sigma_{1}(a)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})\circ a).

Proof.

By the syzygy theorem it is not restrictive to assume that
\mathcal{F}
admits a finite locally free resolution

\mathcal{E}^{*}
.
According to Corollary
4.1
the map
\tau_{1}
lifts to
an
L_{\infty}
morphism

g\colon A_{X}^{0,*}({{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\frac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}}[2]\,

and we have that the linear component
g_{1}
commutes with obstruction maps of the associated deformation functors.
By construction the DG-Lie algebra

\dfrac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}}[2]
has trivial bracket and hence every obstruction of the associated deformation functor is trivial.

If the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence of
X
degenerates at
E_{1}
then the inclusion
of complexes
A^{1,*}_{X}[2]\subset\dfrac{A_{X}^{*,*}}{A_{X}^{\geq 2,*}}[2]
is injective in cohomology

H^{3}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

and the maps
\sigma,\tau
have the same kernel.
∎

5.
Outline of the analogous algebraic construction

Since the proof of the main theorem is mostly algebraic, it is not surprising the same can be slightly modified in order to have an algebraic analogue, valid on a smooth separated scheme
X
of finite type over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic 0, provided that an algebraic representative for
R\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
is given. Here we give only a sketch of a possible algebraic proof, a more detailed description is given in the paper by the first author
[
22
]
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{*}
be a finite complex of locally free sheaves on
X
and let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}
be an open
affine cover of
X
. For simplicity of exposition we assume that
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is a complex of free sheaves on every
U_{i}
, although this additional assumption is unnecessary and it can be easily removed.

Thus, according to
[
9
]
, a possible DG-Lie algebra representing
R\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
is
the totalization
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))

of the cosimplicial DG-Lie algebra of Čech cochains in
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*})
with respect to the open cover
\mathcal{U}
, see also
[
27
]
: here we follow the notation of
[
17
,
26
]
, where the reader can also find a complete and explicit definition of the totalization functor

\operatorname{Tot}\colon\{\text{cosimplicial DG-vector spaces}\}\to\{\text{DG-vector spaces}\}\,

together with its main properties. Here we only recall that
\operatorname{Tot}
preserves possible multiplicative structures, hence transforms cosimplicial DG-Lie algebras (resp.: cosimplicial abelian DG-Lie algebras) into
DG-Lie algebras (resp.: abelian DG-Lie algebras).

Denote by
\Omega=\Omega_{X/\mathbb{K}}
the sheaf of Kähler differentials.
For every coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}
we denote by
{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M})\simeq{{\mathcal{H}}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\Omega,\mathcal{M})
the sheaf of
\mathbb{K}
-linear
derivations
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}
, considered as a complex concentrated in degree
0
.
Following
[
17
]
we define the graded sheaf

\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{(f,\alpha)\in{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{*})\times{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M})\mid f(ax)=af(x)+\alpha(a)\otimes x,\;\forall x\in\mathcal{E}^{*},\,a\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\}.

The same argument of
[
17
]
shows that
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a finite complex of coherent sheaves and there exists a short exact sequence of complexes

0\to{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{f\mapsto(f,0)}\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}\xrightarrow{(f,\alpha)\mapsto\alpha}{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M})\to 0\,.

Then, (a germ of) an algebraic connection on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
may be conveniently defined as an element of
\mathcal{J}^{0}_{\Omega}
mapped onto the universal derivation
d\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega
.
Clearly, since the map
\mathcal{J}^{0}_{\Omega}\to{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\Omega)
is generally not surjective on global sections, a global algebraic connection on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
does not necessarily exist.

However, a global algebraic connection always exists in the totalization of
\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\Omega}
with respect to the affine open cover
\mathcal{U}
.
In fact, the exact sequence of coherent sheaves

0\to{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\Omega\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{*})\xrightarrow{f\mapsto(f,0)}\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{(f,\alpha)\mapsto\alpha}{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\Omega)\to 0\,

gives a short exact sequence of the corresponding cosimplicial complexes of Čech cochains in the open affine cover
\mathcal{U}
; since
\operatorname{Tot}
is an exact functor (see e.g.
[
8
,
26
]
) we get an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\Omega\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{*}))\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{*})\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\Omega))\to 0\,.

In view of the natural inclusion of global sections into the totalization, the universal derivation
d\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega
belong to
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\Omega))
and we may define a
connection of type
(1,0)
on
\mathcal{E}^{*}
as an element

D\in\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\Omega})
mapped onto
d
.

Now everything works, mutatis mutandis, as in the previous sections: consider the complex

\mathcal{O}_{X}[2]\xrightarrow{d}\Omega[1]
as a sheaf of abelian DG-Lie algebras and
define the Atiyah cocycle

u\in\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\Omega\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{*}))

as the differential of
D
. Denote
by

\nabla=[D,-]\colon\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\to\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\Omega\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{E}^{*}))

the adjoint of
D
and use the same formulas of Theorem
3.6
in order to define
an
L_{\infty}
morphism

g\colon\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E}^{*},\mathcal{E}^{*}))\rightsquigarrow\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}[2]\xrightarrow{d}\Omega[1])\,.

Finally, by Whitney’s integration theorem
[
13
,
26
]
the cohomology
of
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}[2]\xrightarrow{d}\Omega[1])
is the same as the hypercohomology of

\mathcal{O}_{X}[2]\xrightarrow{d}\Omega[1]
and
the linear component
g_{1}
induces in cohomology the first component
\tau_{1}
of the (modified) semiregularity map.

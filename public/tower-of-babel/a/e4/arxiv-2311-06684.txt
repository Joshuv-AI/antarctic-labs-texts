Contraction-based Tracking Control of Electromechanical Systems
Najmeh Javanmardi, Pablo Borja, Jacquelien M. A. Scherpen ✉

Abstract
This paper addresses the trajectory-tracking problem for a class of electromechanical systems. To this end, the dynamics of the plants are modeled in the so-called port-Hamiltonian framework. Then, the notion of contraction is exploited to design the desired closed-loop dynamics and the corresponding tracking controller. Notably, the proposed control design method does not require solving partial differential equations or changing the coordinates of the plant, which permits preserving the physical interpretation of the controller. The applicability of the proposed approach is illustrated in several electromechanical systems via simulations.

1
Introduction

Electromechanical (EM) technology has been increasingly used in numerous applications, e.g., electric drives, motors, micro and nanoelectromechanical systems (MEMS/NEMS), microphones, and switches
dean2009applications
;
zhang2014electrostatic
. EM systems consist of two subsystems—one mechanical and one electrical—coupled through nonlinear dynamics. Concerning modeling approaches for EM systems, the so-called port-Hamiltonian (pH) framework (see
GEO
;
VANJEL
) has proven suitable to represent the multi-domain and nonlinear nature of these systems.

Due to the nonlinear couplings between the mechanical and electrical subsystems, inherent instability issues may arise in EM systems. Such issues result in operational limitations on stabilization problems, e.g., pull-in instability in MEMS devices
zhang2014electrostatic
. In the literature, operational range enlargement (e.g.,
zhang2015continuous
) and the stabilization (e.g.,
borovic2004control
;
chu1994analysis
;
maithripala2005control
;
nadal
;
nunna2015constructive
;
rodriguez2003stabilization
) and trajectory-tracking problems (e.g.,
OWUSU
;
rodriguez2021exponential
;
sun
;
zhang2015continuous
;
Zhu
) for EM systems have been studied by adopting diverse control approaches. Among such strategies, energy-shaping methods have proven suitable for controlling EM systems while preserving the physical interpretation of the closed-loop system. Notably, energy-shaping control methods focus on modifying the energy of the closed-loop system to guarantee the stability of the desired equilibrium or trajectory. In this regard, the interconnection and damping assignment passivity-based control (IDA-PBC) methodology
ortega2002interconnection
is the most versatile energy shaping method in terms of the plants that can be stabilized, as it has been shown in a wide range of applications, e.g.,
ortega2001putting
;
rodriguez2003stabilization
. As part of the IDA-PBC approach, it is essential to solve a set of partial differential equations (PDEs), referred to as matching equations. However, finding the solution to these PDEs remains the most significant bottleneck in IDA-PBC.

In
yaghmaei2017trajectory
, the authors propose an IDA-PBC version to solve the trajectory-tracking problem for a class of pH systems. Such an approach is named timed interconnection and damping assignment passivity-based control (tIDA-PBC). This method is based on the notion of contractive pH systems. In particular, in exploiting the fact that the trajectories of contractive systems converge exponentially to each other. Consequently, the proposed tracking method ensures that the trajectories of the closed-loop system converge exponentially to the desired trajectories. An application of this approach is given in the recent paper
rodriguez2021exponential
, where the author implements the tIDA-PBC method in a permanent magnet synchronous motor.

The main contribution of this work consists in proposing a contraction-based method to solve the trajectory-tracking problem for a large class of EM systems. Additionally, the proposed control design approach does not require solving PDEs or any change of coordinates. We stress that methods for controlling EM systems without solving PDEs—e.g.,
BOR
;
nunna2015constructive
—are only suitable for stabilization purposes. On the other hand, methods that solve the trajectory-tracking problem for EM systems—e.g.,
rodriguez2021exponential
—require solving PDEs, which may hamper their implementation.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section
2
introduces the class of EM systems considered in this paper and provides the problem formulation. The main result of this work, i.e., the control approach to solving the trajectory-tracking problem for EM systems, is given in Section
3
. The applicability of the proposed control method is illustrated in Section
4
. Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

, for

.

.

2
Modeling and Problem Formulation

In this section, we present a pH model suitable for representing a broad range of EM systems. This model consists of a mechanical subsystem and an electrical subsystem. The states of the mechanical part are represented by the generalized positions
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}}
and momenta
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}}
, and the state of the electrical subsystem is given by
x_{\tt e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}}
. To simplify the notation, we denote the state vector of the EM system as

\eta=\begin{bmatrix}q\\
p\\
x_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}.

(1)

The dynamics of numerous EM systems can be represented in the pH framework as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\begin{bmatrix}\dot{q}\\
\dot{p}\\
\dot{x}_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}&=&\begin{bmatrix}0&\quad I&\quad 0\\
-I&\quad-R_{\tt m}&\quad 0\\
0&\quad 0&\quad J_{\tt e}-R_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{{\nabla_{{q}}}{H(\eta)}}\\
{{\nabla_{{p}}}{H(\eta)}}\\
{{\nabla_{{x}_{\tt e}}}{H(\eta)}}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
G_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}u,\\[19.91684pt]
H(\eta)&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{p^{\top}}M^{-1}p+V(q)+\frac{1}{2}(x_{\tt e}-\mu(q))^{\top}\Psi(q)(x_{\tt e}-\mu(q)),\end{array}

(2)

where
R_{\tt m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}\times n_{\tt m}}
is the damping matrix of the mechanical subsystem, which is positive definite;
J_{\tt e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}\times n_{\tt e}}
is the interconnection matrix of the electrical subsystem, which is skew-symmetric;
R_{\tt e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}\times n_{\tt e}}
is the damping matrix of the electrical subsystem, which is positive semi-definite;
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}\times n_{\tt m}}
denotes the mass inertia matrix, which is positive definite;
G_{\tt e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}\times n_{\tt e}}
is the full-rank input matrix;
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}}
is the input vector such that the product
G_{\tt e}u
denotes voltage or current. Moreover, the Hamiltonian
H(\eta)
describes the total energy of the system. The first term of the Hamiltonian defines the mechanical kinetic energy. The second term i.e.,
{V}:{\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}}}\to\mathbb{R}
, provides the mechanical potential energy. The last term of the Hamiltonian describes the coupling energy of the system, represented by
{\mu}:{\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}}
, where
{\Psi}:{\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}\times n_{\tt e}}
represents a capacitance or inductance matrix, and it is positive definite. Note that the third term of the Hamiltonian represents the electrical energy stored in
capacitors or inductors, where the capacitance or inductance depends on the displacement
q
.
We emphasize that the
possible values for
q
are determined based on physical
limitations in EM systems.

2.1
Problem formulation

The objective of this work is to the trajectory-tracking problem for the class of EM systems characterized by (
2
). To this end, we propose the notion of
contractive pH systems
for a class of EM systems.
Remarkably, contraction ensures that all the trajectories of a system converge exponentially together as time tends to infinity. Therefore, we propose a contractive pH system as the target dynamics. However, to guarantee that the trajectories of the closed-loop system converge to the desired one, we need to ensure that the desired trajectory is a feasible one. Hence, to formulate the control problem, we first need to introduce the following definition.

Definition 1

Given an open set
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\eta^{\star}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n_{\tt m}+n_{\tt e}}
is said to be a feasible trajectory of the system (
2
) if there exists
u^{\star}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}}
such that the pair
(\eta^{\star},u^{\star})
is a solution of (
2
) for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
.

Given Definition
1
and the concept of contractive pH systems, the control problem boils down to designing a controller such that the closed-loop system is a contractive pH system and the desired trajectory
\eta^{\star}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}q^{\star}&p^{\star}&x^{\star}_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
is a feasible trajectory of the closed-loop system.

3
Control Design Approach

In this section, a static controller is proposed to track the desired trajectory,
\eta^{\star}
, of the system (
2
). To this end, we assume that
\eta^{\star}
is a feasible trajectory. Moreover, because some results might be local depending on the plant, we define an open subset
D_{\tt 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n_{\tt m}+n_{\tt e}}
containing the desired trajectory.

The following assumption characterizes the class of EM considered in this work.

Assumption 1

Given (
2
), the potential energy satisfies the following

\nabla^{2}V(q)\succ 0,\quad\forall\;(q,p,x_{\tt e})\in D_{\tt 0}.

Remark 1

A broad range of EM systems, especially motors, MEMS, and NEMS, satisfy Assumption
1
. Intuitively, systems that are not affected by gravity comply with this condition.

The following lemma is useful for designing suitable target dynamics—i.e., contractive pH systems—which is the first step to solving the trajectory-tracking problem in the context of this work. In the following lemma, we adapt the results of Theorem 4 in
yaghmaei2017trajectory
to EM systems studied in this paper.

Lemma 1

Consider the following system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\begin{bmatrix}\dot{q}\\
\dot{p}\\
\dot{x}_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}&=&\begin{bmatrix}0&\quad I&\quad 0\\
-I&\quad-R_{\tt m}&\quad 0\\
0&\quad 0&\quad J_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{{\nabla_{{q}}}{H_{d}(\eta,t)}}\\
{{\nabla_{{p}}}{H_{d}(\eta,t)}}\\
{{\nabla_{{x}_{\tt e}}}{H_{d}(\eta,t)}}\end{bmatrix},\\[19.91684pt]
H_{d}(\eta,t)&=&H(\eta)+\Theta(x_{\tt e},t),\end{array}

(3)

where
\bar{R}_{\tt e}
is positive definite and
{\Theta}:{\mathbb{R}^{n_{\tt e}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice differentiable function. Suppose that
\Theta
and
\bar{R}_{\tt{e}}
can be chosen such that

\bullet

The following inequality holds

\beta_{1}I\prec\nabla_{\eta}^{2}H(\eta)+\nabla_{\eta}^{2}\Theta(x_{\tt e},t)\prec\beta_{2}I,\quad\eta\in D_{0}

(4)

where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
.

\bullet

The matrix

N=\begin{bmatrix}P&\left(1-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)PP^{\top}\\
-(1-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}+\varepsilon)I&-P^{\top}\end{bmatrix},

(5)

where

P\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}0&\quad I&\quad 0\\
-I&\quad-R_{\tt m}&\quad 0\\
0&\quad 0&\quad J_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e}\end{bmatrix},

(6)

has no eigenvalues on the imaginary axis for a positive constant
\varepsilon
.

Hence, the system (
3
) is contractive on the open subset
D_{0}
.

Proof

It follows from
(
yaghmaei2017trajectory
, Theorem 4)
that

P
is designed as a Hurwitz matrix. For this purpose, we redefine
P
in (
6
) as follows

P=\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}&\quad 0_{2n_{\tt m}\times n_{\tt e}}\\[5.69054pt]
0_{n_{\tt e}\times 2n_{\tt m}}&F_{2}\end{bmatrix},

(7)

where

F_{1}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}0_{n_{\tt m}\times n_{\tt m}}&\quad I\\[5.69054pt]
-I&\quad-R_{\tt m}\end{bmatrix},

(8)

and
F_{2}\triangleq J_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e}
. Therefore,
P
is Hurwitz if and only if
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are Hurwitz. Since
F_{2}\prec 0
, this matrix is Hurwitz. To prove
F_{1}
is Hurwitz, consider

S(\lambda)\triangleq\lambda I-F_{1}.

Therefore, the eigenvalues of
F_{1}
are determined by the following equation:

\textnormal{eig}(F_{1})=\det\big(S(\lambda)\big)=0.

According to the Schur complement, we have

\textnormal{eig}(F_{1})=\det\left(I\lambda\right){\det\left(I\lambda+R_{\tt m}+I\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)}=\det\left(I\lambda\frac{1}{\lambda}\right){\det(I\lambda^{2}+R_{\tt m}\lambda+I)}=0.

Furthermore, there exists an orthogonal matrix
U_{\tt m}
and a positive definite matrix
\Sigma_{\tt m}\triangleq\mbox{diag}(\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{{n_{\tt m}}})
, where
\sigma_{i}>0
,
i\kern-3.00003pt\in\kern-3.00003pt\{1,...,n_{\tt m}\}
, such that
\Sigma_{\tt m}=U_{\tt m}^{\top}R_{\tt m}U_{\tt m}
.
Therefore,

\textnormal{eig}(F_{1})={\det(I\lambda^{2}+R_{\tt m}\lambda+I)}=\det(\lambda^{2}I+\Sigma_{\tt m}\lambda+I)=\prod_{\tt i=1}^{n_{\tt m}}(\lambda^{2}+\sigma_{i}\lambda+1).

(9)

Because
\sigma_{i}>0
, the eigenvalues of (
9
) are negative, implying that
F_{1}
is Hurwitz. Accordingly,
P
defined in (
6
) is Hurwitz. Besides, conditions (
4
) and (
5
) follow immediately from the results reported in
(
yaghmaei2017trajectory
, Theorem 4)
. Hence, (
3
) is contractive.
\hfill\square

The following proposition provides a static tracking controller for the EM systems characterized by (
2
). To this end, based on Lemma
1
, we propose a contractive closed-loop system such that
\eta^{\star}
is a feasible trajectory of the closed-loop system. Hence, the
convergence property of the contractive systems ensures that all the trajectories of the closed-loop system converge exponentially over time, solving the trajectory-tracking problem.

Proposition 1

Consider the EM system (
2
) satisfying Assumption
1
and the feasible trajectory
\eta^{\star}
.
Suppose that there exist positive definite matrices
\bar{R}_{\tt e}
and
K_{\tt c}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
1
for
\Theta=\frac{1}{2}(x_{\tt e}-\alpha(t))^{\top}K_{\tt c}(x_{\tt e}-\alpha(t))
, where

\displaystyle\alpha(t)\triangleq{K_{\tt c}}^{-1}{\Psi}(q^{\star})(x^{\star}_{\tt e}-\mu(q^{\star}))+x_{\tt e}^{\star}-K_{\tt c}^{-1}(J_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e})^{-1}\dot{x}_{\tt e}^{\star}.

(10)

The static feedback controller

\displaystyle u=G_{\tt e}^{\dagger}\bigg((R_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e}){\Psi(q)(x_{\tt e}-\mu(q))}+(J_{\tt e}-\bar{R}_{\tt e})K_{\tt c}(x_{\tt e}-\alpha(t))\bigg)

(11)

guarantees that the trajectories of the closed-loop system converge exponentially to
\eta^{\star}
.

Proof

Applying the controller (
10
), the closed-loop system reduces to (
3
). Because the conditions of Lemma
1
are satisfied by
\bar{R}_{\tt e}
and
K_{\tt c}
, the closed-loop system (
3
) is contractive. In addition,
\alpha(t)
in (
10
) ensures that
\eta^{\star}(t)
is a feasible trajectory of the closed-loop system. Thereby, all trajectories of the closed-loop system exponentially converge to
\eta^{\star}(t)
due to the convergence property in contractive systems.
\hfill\square

Remark 2

The controller (
10
) solves the regulation problem if
\eta^{\star}
is chosen constant. Moreover, in such a case,
\eta^{\star}
is an exponentially stable equilibrium point for the closed-loop system.

5
Concluding Remarks

Contraction is a powerful tool that can be exploited for trajectory-tracking purposes. In this paper, we have shown how the trajectory problem for a class of systems can be solved by proposing a contractive closed-loop system while guaranteeing the desired trajectory is feasible. Moreover, the target dynamics are designed constructively so that no PDEs need to be solved to formulate the controller that achieves the desired closed-loop system.

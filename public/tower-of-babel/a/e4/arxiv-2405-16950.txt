The m m -step solvable anabelian geometry of mixed-characteristic local fields Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC2020): 11S20, 11S31, 11S15, 11F80
Hyeon, Seung-Hyeon

Abstract
Let K K be a mixed-characteristic local field, and let m m be an integer ≥ 0 \geq 0 . We shall denote by K m / K K^{m}/K the maximal m m -step solvable extension of K K , and write G K m G_{K}^{m} for the maximal m m -step solvable quotient of the absolute Galois group G K G_{K} of K K ; we will regard G K G_{K} and its quotients as filtered profinite groups by equipping them with the respective ramification filtrations (in upper numbering). It is known from the previous result due to Mochizuki that the isomorphism class of K K is determined by the isomorphism class of the filtered profinite group G K G_{K} . In this paper, we prove (with some mono-anabelian results) that the isomorphism class of K K is determined by the isomorphism class of the maximal metabelian quotient G K 2 G_{K}^{2} as a filtered profinite group, and further that K m / K K^{m}/K is determined functorially by the filtered profinite group G K m + 2 G_{K}^{m+2} (resp. G K m + 3 G_{K}^{m+3} ) for m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 (resp. m = 0 , 1 m=0,1 ).

Introduction

Let
K_{\circ}
(resp.
K_{\bullet}
) be a mixed-characteristic local field, that is, a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p_{K_{\circ}}}
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}_{p_{K_{\bullet}}}
) for some prime number

(resp.

For each
\square\in\left\{\circ,\bullet\right\}

of
K_{\square}

of

as a filtered profinite group by the ramification groups in upper numbering.
Suppose that we are given a field isomorphism

(cf., e.g., §

We can choose an isomorphism

G_{K_{\circ}}\xrightarrow{\cong}G_{K_{\bullet}};\quad\sigma\mapsto\theta\circ\sigma\circ\theta^{-1}

of profinite groups.
One can check that the above isomorphism respects the filtration by using the fact that
f
preserves the
p
-adic valuation. We denote by

\eta(f)\in\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{filt}}(G_{K_{\circ}},G_{K_{\bullet}})\coloneqq\Inn(G_{K_{\bullet}})\backslash\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathrm{filt}}(G_{K_{\circ}},G_{K_{\bullet}})

the equivalence class of the above isomorphism modulo inner automorphisms of

(i.e., the

(resp.

profinite groups.
We see that

does not depend upon the choice of the extension

Theorem 1.1

(Mochizuki
[
12
]
)
.

The map
\eta
is a bijection. Equivalently, for an isomorphism

\alpha\colon G_{K_{\circ}}\xrightarrow{\cong}G_{K_{\bullet}}

of
filtered
profinite groups, there exists a unique isomorphism

\theta\colon{K_{\circ}^{\mathrm{alg}}}\to{K_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
such that

\alpha(\sigma)=\theta\circ\sigma\circ\theta^{-1}

for every
\sigma\in G_{K_{\circ}}
. In particular, we have an isomorphism
\theta|_{K_{\circ}}\colon K_{\circ}\to K_{\bullet}
.

\diamond

The above result can be considered as one form of the
Grothendieck Conjecture
for mixed-characteristic local fields:
It is implied by the above theorem that the isomorphism class of a given mixed-characteristic local field

can be determined

from the isomorphism class of its absolute Galois group

(as a filtered profinite group).

[

]

]

Ramification groups in upper numbering
.
For a mixed-characteristic local field
K
and any Galois extension
F/K
contained in
{K^{\mathrm{alg}}}
, the Galois group
G=\Gal(F/K)
is a profinite group equipped with the filtration defined by the

(cf.
[

, Chap. IV, §3]
);
we denote by
G(v)
the
v
th
ramification group for a real number

.
The upper numbering is compatible with quotients:
If

(G/N)(v)=G(v)N/N

(1)

for all
v\geq 0
(cf.
loc. cit.
).
Therefore, given a fundamental system

of neighborhoods of identity consisting of open normal subgroups of

, we have a natural isomorphism

G(v)\xrightarrow{\cong}\varprojlim_{N\in\mathscr{N}}(G/N)(v)

(2)

Note that

\displaystyle G(0)

\displaystyle=\Gal(F/(F\cap K^{\mathrm{un}})),

\displaystyle\overline{\bigcup_{v>0}G(v)}

\displaystyle=\Gal(F/(F\cap K^{\mathrm{tame}})),

i.e.,
G(0)
(resp.
G(0+)\coloneqq\overline{\bigcup_{v>0}G(v)}
) is precisely the
inertia subgroup
(resp.
wild inertia subgroup
) of
G
. (See also
loc. cit.
, Exercise 1.)

Suppose that

is a finite Galois subextension of

.
We set
H\coloneqq\Gal(F/L)
, so that
G/H=\Gal(L/K)
.
We define the function
\phi=\phi_{L/K}\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
as the inverse function of

\psi(v)=\psi_{L/K}(v)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{v}\left((G/H):(G/H)(w)\right)\,dw.

It is clear from (

) that

, and

.
Suppose that

Lemma 2.1
.

For a real number

,

, and

\diamond

We prove the following lemma for later use.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be integers
\geq 0
.

(1)

Let
\varGamma
be a profinite group,

an open subgroup of

containing

If we denote by
\tilde{H}
the inverse image of
H
under the natural surjection
\varGamma\twoheadrightarrow\varGamma^{m+n}
, then the natural surjection
\tilde{H}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow H^{n}
is injective.

(2)

Let
k
be a field. For a finite extension

, we have

G_{l}^{n}=\Gal(k^{m+n}/l)^{n}

if
l
is contained in
k^{m}
.
In particular, if
G
is a profinite group of
\mathrm{MLF}^{m+n}
-type (i.e.,
G=\varGamma^{m+n}
for some profinite group
\varGamma
of
\mathrm{MLF}
-type), and
H
is an open subgroup of
G
containing
G^{[m]}
, then
H^{n}
is a profinite group of
\mathrm{MLF}^{n}
-type.

\diamond

Proof.

(1)

Since the natural surjection
\tilde{H}\twoheadrightarrow H=\tilde{H}/\varGamma^{[m+n]}
induces an isomorphism

\tilde{H}^{[n]}\varGamma^{[m+n]}/\varGamma^{[m+n]}=\tilde{H}^{[n]}/(\tilde{H}^{[n]}\cap\varGamma^{[m+n]})\xrightarrow{\cong}(\tilde{H}/\varGamma^{[m+n]})^{[n]},

we have a natural isomorphism
\tilde{H}/\tilde{H}^{[n]}\varGamma^{[m+n]}\to H^{n}
.
It follows from the hypothesis that
\tilde{H}\supseteq\varGamma^{[m]}
(and that
\tilde{H}^{[n]}\supseteq\varGamma^{[m+n]}
), and hence the assertion holds.

(2)

Apply (1) to the case
\varGamma=G_{k}
,
H=\Gal(k^{m+n}/l)
.

∎

Remark
.

(1)

If
G
is a profinite group of
\mathrm{MLF}
-type,
G
is
prosolvable

[
19
, Chap. IV, Corollary 5 of Proposition 7]
; hence

\bigcap_{m\geq 0}G^{[m]}=\{1\}.

However,
G
itself is
not solvable
, i.e.,
G^{[m]}\neq\{1\}
for every
m\geq 0
.
This can be seen from the fact that, for every prime number
p
, the wild inertia subgroup of
G_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
is isomorphic to a free pro-
p
group of countably infinite rank
[
15
, Proposition 7.5.1]
, which is not solvable.
Therefore, the sequence
\left\{G^{[m]}\right\}_{m\geq 0}
is strictly decreasing.

(2)

Let
G
be a profinite group of
\mathrm{MLF}^{m}
-type for some integer
m\geq 0
.
If we denote by
m(G)
the minimal integer
n
such that
G^{[n]}=\{1\}
, then
m=m(G)
.
In other words,
m(G)
—which is group-theoretically determined from the profinite group
G
—is the only integer

for which
G
is a profinite group of
\mathrm{MLF}^{m}
-type: Assume that

for some mixed-characteristic local field

and an integer

.
Then obviously

, and it is clear from (1) that

if

. Thus
m(G)
equals

by definition.

\diamond

(5)

Lemma A.2

Data-Driven Robust Optimization for Energy-Aware Collision Avoidance of Electric Vehicles
Simran Kumari, Ashish R. Hota, Siddhartha Mukhopadhyay

Abstract
In this paper, we simultaneously tackle the problem of energy optimal and safe navigation of electric vehicles in a data-driven robust optimization framework. We consider a dynamic model of the electric vehicle which includes kinematic variables in both inertial and body coordinate systems in order to capture both longitudinal and lateral motion as well as state-of-energy of the battery. We leverage past data of obstacle motion to construct a future occupancy set with probabilistic guarantees, and formulate robust collision avoidance constraints with respect to such an occupancy set using convex programming duality. Consequently, we present the finite horizon optimal control problem subject to robust collision avoidance constraints while penalizing resulting energy consumption. Finally, we show the effectiveness of the proposed approach in reducing energy consumption and ensuring safe navigation via extensive simulations involving curved roads and multiple obstacles.



I
Introduction

Electric vehicles (EVs) have seen significant growth in the past few years being a cleaner alternative to vehicles with internal combustion engines. Advanced Driver Assist Systems (ADAS) for EVs need to fulfil two primary objectives. First, it is essential for the ADAS to consider energy efficient driving in order to be able to extend the driving range in the face of battery storage limitations. The second objective is to ensure safe collision-free operation of the vehicle in uncertain environment of roads with dynamic obstacles represented by nearby vehicles. In this work, we propose a data-driven robust receding horizon optimization framework for energy efficient and collision-free navigation of EVs.

Several past works have explored the problem of energy-efficient driving in EVs, and proposed eco-driving

While a few recent works consider energy efficiency as well as safety aspects under Ecological Driver Assist System (EDAS)
[
5

Complementary to the above line of work, there exists a substantial body of literature on path planning and collision avoidance for (autonomous) vehicles

Several recent papers have tackled the problem of safe navigation in presence of dynamic obstacles using data-driven or stochastic optimization and MPC techniques. The work
[

]
utilizes scenario MPC for trajectory planning during lane change operation initiated by the driver. Similarly,

[

However, to the best of our knowledge, there has not been any major work that incorporates the effect of energy consumption in the safe trajectory planning problem. This is particularly important in the context of EVs since collision avoidance often involves lateral motion (lane changing), which has a significant impact on energy consumption, and therefore on the driving range of EVs
[
7
,
8
]
.

In this work, we aim to address the above research gap by addressing the problems of energy-aware and safe navigation simultaneously. We consider a dynamic model of the EV that captures both longitudinal and lateral motion as well as the dynamics of stored energy level in the battery. In a departure from most past works that capture vehicle dynamics using a bicycle model and assume vehicle motion along a straight road, we include the vehicle positions in both the inertial and the body frames as state variables to capture motion along curved roads. Instead of adopting a model-based approach to predict future trajectory of the obstacles, we adopt a data-driven approach where past data on the motion of surrounding obstacles is used to construct a polyhedral occupancy sets for each obstacle over a finite prediction horizon. These sets are constructed using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). The analysis in
[
21
,
22
]
provides finite-sample guarantees on these occupancy (uncertainty) sets containing the future positions of the obstacle with high probability. We then derive robust collision avoidance constraints which guarantee that the EV will not intersect with the future occupancy sets over the entire prediction horizon. Finally, a finite horizon constrained optimization is presented which minimizes control efforts, distance from a specified destination point, and energy consumption from the battery, while satisfying constraints on robust collision avoidance, and bounds on states and control inputs. This problem is solved repeatedly in a receding horizon manner similar to MPC.

We evaluate the proposed approach via simulations on two navigation scenarios, including motion on a curved road and multiple obstacles. Our results show that under the proposed controller, the EV is able to reach the destination while avoiding obstacles, and consumes significantly less amount of energy compared to an energy-unaware scheme (where the penalty on energy consumption is removed from the cost function) with a modest increase in travel time.

Energy-Aware Safe Navigation Framework

We tackle the problem of energy-aware safe navigation as an optimal control problem with robust collision avoidance constraints. We now formulate the constrained optimal control problem for energy-aware and safe navigation of the EV. The goal of the EV is to reach a desired destination whose coordinates (

IV
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a data-driven robust optimization framework for energy-aware collision avoidance of EVs in scenarios which consist of static as well as dynamic obstacles. Simulations performed on a variety of realistic scenarios illustrate that the proposed formulation achieves a significant reduction in energy consumption with a minimal increase in travel time compared to an energy-unaware scheme.

The primary assumption underlying the proposed formulation is that the orientation of the obstacles are assumed to remain unchanged throughout the prediction horizon. It is further assumed that battery power consumption is a function of motor torque and angular velocity, and power loss at the inverter is negligible. In addition to relaxing the above assumptions, there are several possible directions in which the present work can be strengthened. These include (i) handling model uncertainty or mismatch between the nominal and high fidelity EV models, (ii) formulating the collision avoidance constraints in terms of chance constraints or conditional-value-at-risk, and (iii) implementing the proposed approach in real-time hardware-in-loop simulation environments.

The m -connecting imset and factorization for ADMG models
Bryan Andrews, Gregory F. Cooper, Thomas S. Richardson, Peter Spirtes

Abstract
Directed acyclic graph (DAG) models have become widely studied and applied in statistics and machine learning—indeed, their simplicity facilitates efficient procedures for learning and inference. Unfortunately, these models are not closed under marginalization, making them poorly equipped to handle systems with latent confounding. Acyclic directed mixed graph (ADMG) models characterize margins of DAG models, making them far better suited to handle such systems. However, ADMG models have not seen wide-spread use due to their complexity and a shortage of statistical tools for their analysis. In this paper, we introduce the m -connecting imset which provides an alternative representation for the independence models induced by ADMGs. Furthermore, we define the m -connecting factorization criterion for ADMG models, characterized by a single equation, and prove its equivalence to the global Markov property. The m -connecting imset and factorization criterion provide two new statistical tools for learning and inference with ADMG models. We demonstrate the usefulness of these tools by formulating and evaluating a consistent scoring criterion with a closed form solution.



Keywords:
ADMG models, imsetal conditional independence, factorization criterion

Introduction

Directed acyclic graph (DAG) models, also known as Bayesian networks, have become widely studied and applied in statistics and machine learning—indeed, their simplicity facilitates efficient procedures for learning and inference
(
Bishop, 2006
;
Darwiche, 2009
;
Koller and Friedman, 2009
)
. Furthermore, DAG models allow for causal inference under a few additional assumptions
(
Spirtes et al., 2000
;
Pearl, 2009
)
. These models describe probability distributions that obey the conditional independence relations represented in a DAG, and may be characterized by a well-known recursive factorization. Moreover, multinomial and Gaussian DAG models form curved exponential families with known dimension
(
Geiger et al., 2001
)
. While variables may be added to DAG models to account for latent confounding, doing so leads to several challenges
(
Richardson and Spirtes, 2002
)
:

\bullet

these models are not always identifiable;

\bullet

inference may be sensitive to assumptions made about the latent variables;

\bullet

these models are not curved exponential families
Geiger et al., 2001
;

\bullet

distributions associated with one of these models may be difficult to characterize
Settimi and Smith, 1998
;
Settimi and Smith, 2000
.

Alternatively, we could consider models that describe probability distributions obeying the conditional independence relations represented in margins of DAGs. In this paradigm, we treat latent confounding as marginalized variables, thereby making no assumptions about the latent variables. However, DAG models, are not closed under marginalization. As a result, several authors have explored more general families of graphical models that are closed under marginalization
(
Richardson and Spirtes, 2002
;
Sadeghi, 2013
)
.

Figure 1:
Graphical representations for a system with latent confounding: (
i
) a DAG with an explicitly defined latent variable; (
ii
) an ADMG for the induced marginal independence model.

In this work, we focus on acyclic directed mixed graph (ADMG) models due to their simple and intuitive derivation as latent projections of DAGs
(
Verma and Pearl, 1990
;
Evans, 2018b
)
. Figure
1
illustrates a DAG with a latent variable and the corresponding ADMG. Notably, ADMGs generalize other families of graphical models that are closed under marginalization, such as directed (maximal) ancestral graphs
(
Sadeghi, 2013
)
. Accordingly, the results of this paper directly apply to those subfamilies.

ADMG models describe probability distributions that obey the conditional independence relations represented in an ADMG. Auspiciously, multinomial and Gaussian ADMG models form curved exponential families with known dimension
(
Richardson and Spirtes, 2002
;
Evans and Richardson, 2014
)
. However, these models have not seen wide-spread use due to their complexity and a shortage of statistical tools for their analysis. Similarly,
Studený, 2005
introduced structural imsets as a non-graphical representation of conditional independence which, while capable of representing all probabilistic independence models, have not seen wide-spread use. Developments to the imsetal representation of conditional independence have been largely theoretical, except for in constrained scenarios, such as the restriction to DAG independence models—indeed, several methods use imsets to learn DAGs
(
Studený, 2008
;
Studený and Cussens, 2017
;
Studený and Haws, 2014
)
. A recent innovation in the space of imsetal independence models introduced the characteristic imset as a more natural representation of DAG independence models
(
Hemmecke et al., 2012
)
.

In this paper we introduce the
m
-connecting imset, which is analogous to the characteristic imset for DAG models, in order to expand the tools available for analysis of ADMG models. Indeed, we show how the
m
-connecting imset admits a novel factorization criterion, characterized by a single equation, that is equivalent to the global Markov property—we call this new criterion the
m
-connecting factorization. Using the
m
-connecting factorization, we formulate a consistent scoring criterion with a closed-form solution for curved exponential families whose independence models are described by ADMGs. We investigate asymptotic properties of our score and compare its ability to recover correct independence models against the well-known FCI algorithm and two of its variants

This paper details the methods developed in a recent PhD thesis
(
Andrews, 2022
)
and is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces and discusses concepts relevant to ADMGs, imsets, and independence models. Section
3
introduces the
m
-connecting imset and factorization and proves the equivalence of the
m
-connecting factorization to the global Markov property for ADMG models. Section
4
formulates a consistent scoring criterion for ADMG curved exponential families and investigates its ability to recover correct independence models against the well-known FCI algorithm and two of its variants. Section
5
provides a discussion and states our conclusions. For readability, all proofs are postponed until the appendix.

2.2
Partially Ordered Sets

Throughout this paper,

Definition

A

A=B

transitivity

A\leq B
and
B\leq C

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\leq C
.

Definition

(

)

A

with a partial order

A\leq B

\leq
is a
total order

Two simple examples of partially ordered sets used throughout this paper are

(

)

2.3
Möbius Inversion

Two useful functions for analyzing posets that will be fundamental to deriving the
m
-connecting factorization are the
zeta function
and
Möbius function
. The zeta function
\zeta_{\mathsf{P}}:\mathsf{P}\times\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is defined:

\zeta_{\mathsf{P}}(B,A)=\begin{cases}\mkern 18.0mu1\mkern 36.0mu&B\subseteq A;\\
\mkern 18.0mu0\mkern 36.0mu&B\not\subseteq A,\end{cases}

and the Möbius function
\mu_{\mathsf{P}}\mkern 0.5mu:\mathsf{P}\times\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is defined:

\mu_{\mathsf{P}}(B,A)=\begin{cases}\mkern 6.0mu(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}\mkern 36.0mu&B\subseteq A;\\
\mkern 18.0mu0\mkern 36.0mu&B\not\subseteq A.\end{cases}

The posets we consider are finite and may be thought of as matrices. Abusing notation, we interpret
\zeta_{\mathsf{P}}
and
\mu_{\mathsf{P}}
as matrices where the first and second arguments act as the row and column indices, respectively. Under this interpretation, the Möbius function is the inverse of the zeta function in the sense that
\mu_{\mathsf{P}}\mkern 0.5mu=\zeta_{\mathsf{P}}^{-1}
.

2.4
Independence Models

Conditional independence is often used synonymously with probabilistic conditional independence—conditional independence statements that are represented in a probability measure. We use this term more generally to include graphical and imsetal conditional independence—conditional independence statements that are represented in an ADMG and an imset, respectively.

Definition

(
disjoint triple
)
.

If
A,B,C\subseteq V
are disjoint, then
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle
is the corresponding
disjoint triple
. The set of all disjoint triples is defined:

\mathcal{T}(V)\equiv\{\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\;:\;A,B,C\subseteq V\;\text{and}\;A\cap B=A\cap C=B\cap C=\varnothing\}.

Definition

(
independence statement
)
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a mathematical object over
V
and
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
, then the statement
“
A
and
B
are independent given
C
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
”
is an
independence statement
. In this case, we say
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle
is represented in
\mathcal{O}
and write
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
.

Definition

(
independence model
)
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a mathematical object over
V
, then the
independence model
induced by
\mathcal{O}
is defined:

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O})\equiv\left\{\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)\;:\;A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]\right\}.

Several families of independence models have been axiomatically characterized. The independence model
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O})
is called a
semi-graphoid
whenever conditions (
i - v
) hold for every collection of disjoint sets
A,B,C,D\subseteq V
:

i.

triviality

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp\varnothing\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
;

ii.

symmetry

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

B\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp A\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
;

iii.

decomposition

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp BD\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp D\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
;

iv.

weak union

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp BD\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid CD\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
;

v.

contraction

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid CD\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
and
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp D\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp BD\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
.

Furthermore,
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O})
is called a
graphoid
whenever conditions (
i - vi
) hold for every collection of disjoint sets
A,B,C,D\subseteq V
:

vi.

intersection

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid CD\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
and
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp D\mid BC\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp BD\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O})
is called a
compositional graphoid
whenever conditions (
i - vii
) hold for every collection of disjoint sets
A,B,C,D\subseteq V
:

vii.

composition

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
and
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp D\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp BD\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{O}\mkern 0.5mu]
.

2.5
Probabilistic Conditional Independence

Throughout this paper, we consider a collection of random variables
X
on a measurable space
(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{X})
indexed by
V
, and a probability measure
P:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,1]
having density
f:\mathsf{X}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
dominated by a
\sigma
-finite product measure
\mu
. If for
P
-almost all
x_{BC}\in\mathsf{X}_{BC}
:

P(X_{A}\in\Omega\mid X_{BC}=x_{BC})=P(X_{A}\in\Omega\mid X_{C}=x_{C})\mkern 18.0mu\text{for all}\;\Omega\in\mathcal{X}_{A}

or, alternatively, for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathsf{X}
:

f_{ABC}(x)f_{C}(x)=f_{AC}(x)f_{BC}(x),

then we say
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle
is represented in
P
and write
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5muP\mkern 0.5mu]
. The independence model induced by
P
is denoted by
\mathcal{I}(P)
. Moreover, the independence model induced by a probability measure is a semi-graphoid.

Proposition 2.3

(
Studený, 2005
)
.

If
P
is a probability measure, then
\mathcal{I}(P)
is a semi-graphoid.

In this paper, we are interested in the ADMG models that describe
positive measures
.

Definition

(
positive measure
)
.

If probability measure
P
has density
f:\mathsf{X}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, then
P
is a positive measure.

Notably, any independence model induced by a positive measure is a graphoid.

Proposition 2.4

(
Pearl, 1988
)
.

If
P
is a positive measure, then
\mathcal{I}(P)
is a graphoid.

2.6
Graphical Conditional Independence

Conditional independence in an ADMG is characterized by
m-connecting
paths and the
m-separation
criterion, which naturally extend
d-connecting
paths and the
d-separation
criterion from DAGs to ADMGs
(
Richardson and Spirtes, 2002
;
Richardson, 2003
)
.

Definition

(
m-connecting path
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG and
\langle a,b\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
such that a path
\pi
exists between
a
and
b
where:

i.

every non-collider on
\pi
is not a member of
C
;

ii.

every collider on
\pi
is an ancestor of some
c\in C
;

then
\pi
is an
m-connecting path
between
a
and
b
relative to
C
.

Definition

(
m-separation
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG and
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
such that no
m
-connecting path exists between
a
and
b
relative to
C
for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
, then
A
and
B
are
m-separated
by
C
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG and
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
. If
A
and
B
are
m
-separated by
C
, then we say
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle
is represented in
\mathcal{G}
and write
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{G}\mkern 0.5mu]
. The independence model induced by
\mathcal{G}
is denoted
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})
. Moreover, the independence model induced by an ADMG is a compositional graphoid.

Proposition 2.5

(
Sadeghi and Lauritzen, 2014
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is an ADMG, then
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})
is a compositional graphoid.

Notably, multiple ADMGs can induce the same independence model. This phenomenon is called
Markov equivalence
, and a complete set of Markov equivalent ADMGs is called a
Markov equivalence class
(MEC).

2.6.1
Graphical Markov Properties

ADMGs and probability measures are linked by conditional independence using the
global Markov property
.

Definition

(
global Markov property
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG and
P
is a probability measure where:

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{G}\mkern 0.5mu]\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0muA\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5muP\mkern 0.5mu]\mkern 18.0mu\text{for all}\mkern 18.0mu\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V),

or
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(P)
, then
P
satisfies the
global Markov property
for
\mathcal{G}
.

However, many of the conditional independence statements characterized by the global Markov property are redundant—implied by the semi-graphoid axioms. Accordingly, the global Markov property can be simplified to a more succinct property using a consistent order and a few additional concepts. Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG. If
b\in V
, then:

\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)\equiv\textup{col}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)\setminus b\mkern 72.0mu\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)\equiv\textup{col}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)

are the
Markov boundary
and
closure
of
b
, respectively. Together, they have the following property.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG. If
b\in V
, then
:

b\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp V\setminus\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)\mid\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{G}\mkern 0.5mu].

In other words,
\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)
renders
b
independent of the vertices not in
\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)
. Moreover, these sets are the smallest sets with this property and lead directly to the
ordered local Markov property
.

Definition

(
ordered local Markov property
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
. If
P
is a probability measure where:

b\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp A\setminus\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{A}}(b)\mid\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}_{A}}(b)\;[\mkern 0.5muP\mkern 0.5mu]\mkern 18.0mu\text{for all}\mkern 18.0muA\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})\;\text{and}\;b=\lceil A\rceil_{\leq},

then
P
satisfies the
ordered local Markov property
for
\mathcal{G}
and
\leq
.

Auspiciously, the global and ordered local Markov properties are equivalent.

Theorem 2.1

(
Richardson, 2003
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
and
P
is a probability measure, then the following are equivalent
:

i.

P
satisfies the global Markov property for
\mathcal{G}
;

ii.

P
satisfies the ordered local Markov property for
\mathcal{G}
and
\leq
.

2.6.2
Factorization

The global Markov property for DAGs can be characterized by a well-known
recursive factorization
that encodes the independence statements represented in a DAG algebraically. If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is a DAG and
P
is a probability measure, then the following are equivalent:

i.

f(x)=\prod_{v\in V}f_{v\mid\textup{pa}_{\mathcal{G}}(v)}(x)\mkern 18.0mu
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathcal{X}
;

ii.

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{G}\mkern 0.5mu]\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0muA\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5muP\mkern 0.5mu]\mkern 18.0mu
for all
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
.

Richardson, 2009
developed a similar factorization for ADMGs, however, in order to state his factorization, we first must review a few additional concepts. At a high level, Richardson’s factorization utilizes a function to partition the variables into sets called
heads
. The heads are then used to construct one or more equations. In these equations marginal densities over ancestral sets are set equal to the products of conditional densities of heads conditioned on accompanying sets called
tails
.

Definition

(
head
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG and
H\subseteq V

2.7
Integer-valued Multisets

Definition

(
integer-valued multiset
)
.

An
integer-valued multiset
,

for short, is an integer-valued function
u
:
\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. Alternatively,
u
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{P}(V)}
.

A simple example of an imset is the
identifier
. The identifier for a set
A\subseteq V
is defined:

\delta_{A}(S)\equiv\begin{cases}\mkern 18.0mu1&\mkern 36.0muS=A;\\
\mkern 18.0mu0&\mkern 36.0muS\neq A.\end{cases}

More generally, the identifier for a set of sets
\mathsf{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V)
is defined:

\delta_{\mathsf{A}}(S)\equiv\begin{cases}\mkern 18.0mu1&\mkern 36.0muS\in\mathsf{A};\\
\mkern 18.0mu0&\mkern 36.0muS\not\in\mathsf{A}.\end{cases}

The identifier imset may be used to construct
elementary imsets
which are the fundamental building block of the imsetal representation of conditional independence.

Definition

(
elementary imset
)
.

If
\langle a,b\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
, then the corresponding
elementary imset

u_{\langle a,b\mid C\rangle}:\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an imset defined:

u_{\langle a,b\mid C\rangle}\equiv\delta_{ab\mkern 0.5muC}+\delta_{C}-\delta_{a\mkern 0.5muC}-\delta_{b\mkern 0.5muC}.

\begin{array}[]{c @{\hspace{10mm}} rccccccccl}S\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&&\varnothing&\{a\}&\{b\}&\{c\}&\{a,b\}&\{a,c\}&\{b,c\}&\{a,b,c\}&\\
\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt\\
u_{\langle a,b\mid c\rangle}(S)\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&\Big[&0&0&0&1&0&\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$-$}1&\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$-$}1&1&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\Big]^{\top}\\
\end{array}

u_{\langle a,b\mid c\rangle}
.

Figure

. The structure of an elementary imset imitates the definition of probabilistic conditional independence when the independent sets are singletons. This is not a coincidence, in fact, elementary imsets were designed to represent statements of conditional independence in this manner. Elementary imsets may be used to construct other types of imsets, such as
semi-elementary imsets
and

.

Definition

(
semi-elementary imset
)
.

If
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
, then the corresponding
semi-elementary imset

is defined:

u_{\langle A,B\mid C\rangle}\equiv\delta_{ABC}+\delta_{C}-\delta_{AC}-\delta_{BC}.

Semi-elementary imsets may be constructed as linear combinations of elementary imsets with non-negative integer coefficients.

Proposition 2.7

(
Studený, 2005
)
.

Every semi-elementary imset is a linear combination of elementary imsets with non-negative integer coefficients.

Similarly, structural imsets may be constructed as linear combinations of elementary imsets with non-negative rational coefficients.

Definition

(
structural imset
)
.

If
u:\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an imset where:

u=\sum_{\langle a,b\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)}k_{a,b\mid C}\,u_{\langle a,b\mid C\rangle}\mkern 18.0mu\text{for some}\;k_{a,b\mid C}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+},

then

is a
structural imset
over
V
. Denote the set of all structural imsets over
V
by
\mathcal{S}(V)
.

Conditional independence in structural imsets may be characterized by subtraction of semi-elementary imsets. If there exists
k\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
such that
u-k\,u_{\langle A,B\mid C\rangle}
is a structural imset, then we say
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle
is represented in
u
and write
A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5muu\mkern 0.5mu]
. The independence model induced by
u
is denoted
\mathcal{I}(u)
. Moreover, the independence models induced by structural imsets are semi-graphoids.

Proposition 2.8

(
Studený, 2005
)
.

Let
u
be an imset. If
u\in\mathcal{S}(V)
, then
\mathcal{I}(u)
is a semi-graphoid.

Studený, 2005
linked structural imsets and probability measures by defining a factorization that uses the density of a probability measure to algebraically imply the conditional independence statements represented in a structural imset. Studený defined his factorization for probability measures with finite multiinformation; see
(
Studený, 2005
)
. Below, we restate Studený’s factorization for positive measures.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

be an imset and

be a positive measure. If

for all

.

Developments to the imsetal representation of conditional independence have been largely theoretical, except for in restricted scenarios, such as the restriction to DAG independence models—indeed, several methods use imsets to learn DAGs

Studený, 2008

Furthermore, the values of a characteristic imset may be read directly off of a DAG.

Proposition 2.9

Let

In this section, we introduce the

Definition

(

)
.

m
-connecting path between
a
and
b
relative to
C
for all
\langle a,b\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
where

.

The m-connecting imset naturally generalizes the characteristic imset in the following sense:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG and
S\subseteq V
. If
A=\textup{an}_{\mathcal{G}}(S)
and
B=\textup{bar}_{\mathcal{G}}(S)
, then

i.

ii.

m_{\mathcal{G}}(S)=1\mkern 18.0mu\Leftrightarrow\mkern 18.0muS\subseteq\textup{col}_{\mathcal{G}_{A}}(B)
.

The (non-empty) sets for which the m-connecting imset is 1 are called

and have been used to parameterize ADMG models
(
Evans and Richardson, 2014
)
, characterize margins of discrete DAG models
(
Evans, 2018a
)

(
Hu and Evans, 2020
)
.

(

)
.

\mathcal{G}=(V,E)

(
Hu and Evans, 2020
)

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)

C

(

3.1
Probabilistic Terms

To facilitate the forthcoming discussion, we define several new terms. We define a function
\phi_{A}:\mathcal{X}_{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as a linear combination of log density terms motivated by the Möbius inversion:

\log f_{A}(x)=\sum_{B\subseteq A}\phi_{B}(x)\mkern 72.0mu\phi_{A}(x)=\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A\setminus B|}\log f_{B}(x)\\
.

Defining functional vectors
h_{\mathsf{P}}(x):\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{P}(V)|}
and
\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x):\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{P}(V)|}

\underset{S\in\mathcal{P}(V)}{[h_{\mathsf{P}}(x)]_{S}}\equiv\log f_{S}(x)\mkern 72.0mu\underset{S\in\mathcal{P}(V)}{[\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)]_{S}}\equiv\phi_{S}(x),

we equivalently have:

h_{\mathsf{P}}=\zeta_{\mathsf{P}}^{\top}\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)\mkern 72.0mu\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)=\mu_{\mathsf{P}}^{\top}h_{\mathsf{P}}(x).

We accept the convention
\phi_{\varnothing}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. The expectation of
\phi_{A}(x)
with respect to
P
has been previously studied in the field of information theory by several researchers, including
McGill, 1954
, who coined the term
interaction information
. Accordingly, we call
\phi_{A}(x)
the
interaction information rate
.

We define the following shorthand for indicators of sets of sets and their corresponding
\phi
terms. Let

(A\neq\varnothing)
be disjoint sets:

\delta_{A\mid B}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq AB\\
A\subseteq S\end{subarray}}\delta_{S}\mkern 72.0mu\phi_{A\mid B}(x)\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq AB\\
A\subseteq S\end{subarray}}\phi_{S}(x).

Similar to above, we call
\phi_{A\mid B}(x)
the
conditional interaction information rate
. Another case is when the set of sets corresponds to a semi-elementary imset transformed by the Möbius inversion. Let
A,B,C\subseteq V

(AB\neq\varnothing)
be disjoint sets:

\delta_{A,B\mid C}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq ABC\\
S\not\subseteq AC\\
S\not\subseteq BC\end{subarray}}\delta_{S}\mkern 72.0mu\phi_{A,B\mid C}(x)\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq ABC\\
S\not\subseteq AC\\
S\not\subseteq BC\end{subarray}}\phi_{S}(x).

The expectation of
\phi_{A,B\mid C}(x)
with respect to
P
is the well-known information theoretic concept of
conditional mutual information
. Accordingly, we call
\phi_{A,B\mid C}(x)
the
mutual information rate
. The conditional mutual information rate and its corresponding imset have the following properties.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
P
be a positive measure

\displaystyle\phi_{A\mid B}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq AB\\

\displaystyle\phi_{A,B\mid C}(x)=\log\frac{f_{ABC}(x)f_{C}(x)}{f_{AC}(x)f_{BC}(x)}

The

Vertices in the density terms are conditioned on their Markov boundaries in the subgraph induced by their preceding vertices. Additionally, if a density term implies extraneous dependencies, then the term is reweighted by a ratio equaling 1 if and only if the extra dependencies are false in the probability measure.

i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq},e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}=\textsc{NIE}(\mathcal{G},\leq)
,
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})
, and
b=\lceil A\rceil_{\leq}
, then
:

b\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp A\setminus\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{A}}(b)\mid\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}_{A}}(b)\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mu_{\mathsf{P}}\mkern 0.5mui_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}\mkern 0.5mu].

Accordingly, we get the first of the following two results.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG,
\leq
be a total order consistent with
\mathcal{G}
. If
i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq},e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}=\textsc{NIE}(\mathcal{G},\leq)
, then
:

i.

\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\mathsf{P}}\mkern 0.5mui_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq})=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})
;

ii.

\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\mathsf{P}}\mkern 0.5mue_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})
.

Corollary 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{G}
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
, then
:

i.

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(P)\mkern 18.0mu\Leftrightarrow\mkern 18.0mu\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)^{\top}i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}=0\mkern 18.0mu
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathcal{X}
;

ii.

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(P)\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)^{\top}e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}=0\mkern 18.0mu
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Combining all the results above, we now state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
. If
P
is a positive measure, then the following are equivalent
:

i.

\log f(x)=\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})}\phi_{M}(x)-\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)^{\top}e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}\mkern 18.0mu
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathcal{X}
;

ii.

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5mu\mathcal{G}\mkern 0.5mu]

\mkern 18.0mu\Rightarrow\mkern 18.0mu

A\perp\mkern-9.0mu\perp B\mid C\;[\mkern 0.5muP\mkern 0.5mu]

for all
\langle A,B\mid C\rangle\in\mathcal{T}(V)
.

Alternatively, the adjusted sum over the parameterizing sets may be computed as an adjusted sum over conditional densities. Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
. If
R=\textup{pre}^{\leq}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)
where context clarifies
b\in V
, then:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})}\phi_{M}(x)-\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)^{\top}e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{P}(V)}[m_{\mathcal{G}}(S)-e_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(S)]\,\phi_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{R}}(b)\\
b\in S\end{subarray}}[1-i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(S)]\,\phi_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in V}\bigg[\log f_{b\mid\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}_{R}}(b)}(x)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\textup{cl}_{\mathcal{G}_{R}}(b)\\
b\in S\end{subarray}}i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(S)\,\phi_{S}(x)\bigg].

This alternative formulation of the
m
-connecting factorization in conjunction with the notion of a
dominating DAG
gives a nice intuition.

Definition

(
dominating DAG
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
. If
R=\textup{pre}^{\leq}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)
where context clarifies
b\in V
, then the corresponding
dominating DAG
is defined:

\textup{dom}(\mathcal{G},\leq)\equiv(V,\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{E}(V)\;:\;a\in\textup{mb}_{\mathcal{G}_{R}}(b)\}).

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG with consistent order
\leq
and dominating DAG
\mathcal{D}=\textup{dom}(\mathcal{G},\leq)
, then:

\log f(x)=\sum_{b\in V}\bigg[\log f_{b\mid\textup{pa}_{\mathcal{D}}(b)}(x)-\sum_{S\subseteq\textup{pa}_{\mathcal{D}}(b)}i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(bS)\,\phi_{bS}(x)\bigg].

Using this formulation, the
m
-connecting factorization becomes the well-known recursive factorization for the dominating DAG with a few additional conditional mutual information rate terms to adjust for any superfluous dependencies induced by
\mathcal{D}
.

4
Consistent Scoring Criterion

In this section, we discuss an application of the
m
-connecting factorization. In particular, we formulate a consistent scoring criterion with a closed-form solution for many curved exponential families, such as Gaussian and multinomial ADMG models. We investigate asymptotic properties of the score and compare its ability to recover correct independence models against the well-known FCI algorithm and two of its variants

Definition

(
exponential family
)
.

An
exponential family
is a family of probability measures that admit densities with respect to
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
:

f(x\mid\theta)=\exp\left[\theta^{\top}t(x)-\psi(\theta)\right]\mkern 18.0mu\textup{for all}\;\theta\in\Theta\;\textup{where}

\bullet

\Theta\equiv\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{|t(x)|}\,:\,\int_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\exp\left[\theta^{\top}t(x)\right]\textup{d}\mu(x)<\infty\}
is the
natural parameter space
;

\bullet

t(x)
is the
sufficient statistic
;

\bullet

\psi(\theta)\equiv\int_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\exp\left[\theta^{\top}t(x)\right]\textup{d}\mu(x)
is the
cumulant function
.

Let
P_{\theta}
denote a member of some curved exponential family parameterized by
\theta
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be an ADMG. We are interested in exponential families restricted to positive measures whose conditional independence relations are represented in
\mathcal{G}
. We denote the general parameter space constrained by
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})
:

\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\equiv\{\theta\in\Theta\;:\;\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathcal{I}(P_{\theta})\}.

When
\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}
is a smooth manifold, the family of probability measures
\{P_{\theta}\;:\;\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\}
is a
curved exponential family
; see
Kass and Vos, 1997
.

Proposition 4.1

(
Richardson and Spirtes, 2002
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG and
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{G}}(\mathcal{G})\equiv\{P_{\theta}\;:\;\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\}
is a family of Gaussian ADMG models, then
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{G}}(\mathcal{G})
is a curved exponential family with dimension
:

|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|=|V|+|\{M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})\;:\;|M|\in\{1,2\}\}|.

Proposition 4.2

(
Evans and Richardson, 2014
)
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is an ADMG and
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{M}}(\mathcal{G})\equiv\{P_{\theta}\;:\;\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\}
is a family of multinomial ADMG models, then
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{M}}(\mathcal{G})
is a curved exponential family with dimension
:

|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G})}|\mathcal{X}_{\textup{tail}_{\mathcal{G}}(H)}|\prod_{h\in H}(|\mathcal{X}_{h}|-1)

where the convention
|\mathcal{X}_{\varnothing}|=1
is accepted.

Let
X
be a collection of random variables indexed by
V
with positive measure
P_{\theta}
that admits density
f(x\mid\theta)
with respect to dominating
\sigma
-finite product measure
\mu
. Throughout this section, let
P_{\theta}
belong to a curved exponential family with parameter space
\Theta
and
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\overset{iid}{\sim}f(x\mid\theta)
. The log-likelihood for parameter estimate
\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
is defined:

\ell(\hat{\theta}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta}).

Moreover, the maximum log-likelihood estimate of the parameters is defined:

\hat{\theta}_{n}^{\text{mle}}\equiv\argmax_{\hat{\theta}\in\Theta}\ell(\hat{\theta}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})

and the maximum log-likelihood estimate of the parameters constrained by
\mathcal{G}
is defined:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathcal{G},n}^{\text{mle}}\equiv\argmax\limits_{\hat{\theta}\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}}\ell(\hat{\theta}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n}).

Let
\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
be an estimate of the parameters. If
T=\textup{tail}_{\mathcal{G}}(H)
and
R=\textup{pre}^{\leq}_{\mathcal{G}}(b)
where context clarifies
H\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G})
and
b\in V
, then we approximate the log-likelihood constrained by
\mathcal{G}
using
\hat{\theta}
and the
m
-connecting factorization as:

\displaystyle\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{G}}(\hat{\theta}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{M}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{H\mid T}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G})}\sum_{S\subseteq H}(-1)^{|H\setminus S|}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{ST}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G}_{R})\\
b\in H\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq H\\
b\not\in S\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|H\setminus S|-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{b\mid ST}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})

Similarly, if
\mathcal{D}=\textup{dom}(\mathcal{G},\leq)
, then the log-likelihood constrained by
\mathcal{G}
and
\leq
is approximated using
\hat{\theta}
and the
m
-connecting factorization as:

\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(\hat{\theta}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\equiv\sum_{b\in V}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{b\mid\textup{pa}_{\mathcal{D}}(b)}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})\,-\sum_{S\subseteq\textup{pa}_{\mathcal{D}}(b)}i_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(bS)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{bS}(x^{i}\mid\hat{\theta})\bigg].

4.1
Bayesian Information Criterion

The Bayesian information criterion (BIC) score provides a computationally efficient
O_{p}(1)
approximation for the log marginal likelihood of an exponential family.
Schwarz, 1978
originally formulated the BIC score for models defined by affine transformations of their natural parameter space which was later extended to curved exponential families by

Haughton, 1988
. The BIC score of a model has a simple formulation using the model’s maximum likelihood estimate and dimension:

\text{BIC}(\mathcal{G},x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\equiv\ell(\hat{\theta}_{\mathcal{G},n}^{\text{mle}}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})-\frac{|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|}{2}\log(n).

Notably, a positive measure
P_{\theta}
satisfies the global Markov property with respect to an ADMG
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}
. Moreover, the BIC score is a consistent score for model selection.

Proposition 4.3

(Proposition 1.2
Haughton, 1988
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
be ADMGs. Furthermore, let
X
be a collection of random variables indexed by
V
with positive measure
P_{\theta}
that admits density
f(x\mid\theta)
with respect to dominating
\sigma
-finite product measure
\mu
. Let
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\overset{iid}{\sim}f(x\mid\theta)
. If either
\theta\in(\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\setminus\Theta_{\mathcal{G}})
or
\theta\in(\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\cap\,\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}})
and
|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|<|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|
, then
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\textup{Pr}(\textup{BIC}(\mathcal{G},x^{1},\dots,x^{n})<\textup{BIC}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},x^{1},\dots,x^{n}))=1.

The BIC score has a closed-form solution for many curved exponential families, such as Gaussian and multinomial DAG models. Unfortunately, this is not always the case when the parameter space is constrained by the independence model induced by an ADMG. We develop an approximation for the BIC score, called the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}
score, that has a closed-form solution in both cases. Let
\mathcal{D}=\textup{dom}(\mathcal{G},\leq)
, we define the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}
score using the approximate maximum log-likelihood characterized by the
m
-connecting factorization:

\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}(\mathcal{G},\leq,x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\equiv\begin{cases}\mkern 18.0mu\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{G}}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathcal{D},n}^{\text{mle}}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})-\frac{|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|}{2}\log(n)\mkern 36.0mu&$|M|\leq 5$ for all $M\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})$\\
\mkern 18.0mu\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\leq}(\hat{\theta}_{\mathcal{D},n}^{\text{mle}}\mid x^{1},\dots,x^{n})-\frac{|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|}{2}\log(n)\mkern 36.0mu&otherwise\end{cases}

which has nice asymptotic properties and simplifies to BIC if
\mathcal{G}
is a DAG.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
be ADMGs and
\leq
and
\leq^{\prime}
be total orders consistent with
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, respectively. Furthermore, let
X
be a collection of random variables indexed by
V
with positive measure
P_{\theta}
that admits density
f(x\mid\theta)
with respect to dominating
\sigma
-finite product measure
\mu
. Let
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\overset{iid}{\sim}f(x\mid\theta)
.

If and
\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\setminus\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
, then:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\left|\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}(\mathcal{G},\leq,x^{1},\dots,x^{n})-\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\leq^{\prime},x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\right|=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}\left[\phi_{\mathsf{P}}(x)^{\top}i_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}^{\leq}\right].

If
x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\overset{iid}{\sim}f(x\mid\theta)
and
\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}\cap\,\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
with
|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|<|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|
, then:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\left|\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}(\mathcal{G},\leq,x^{1},\dots,x^{n})-\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\leq^{\prime},x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\right|=\frac{|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|-|\Theta_{\mathcal{G}}|}{2}.

The
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}
is a consistent score for model selection; see Appendix

and
4.2

.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)

be ADMGs and

4.2
Empirical Evaluation

While the BIC score is a consistent scoring criterion, it does not always have a closed-form solution for ADMG models. In particular, the calculation of the maximum log-likelihood requires the development of model specific optimizers. Such optimizers have been developed for Gaussian and multinomial ADMG models by
Drton and Richardson, 2004
;
Drton et al., 2009
and
Evans and Richardson, 2014
, respectively. In contrast, the approximate maximum log-likelihood calculation developed in this paper uses the maximum likelihood estimate of the parameters for a dominating DAG model. Accordingly, the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}
score has a closed form solution for many curved exponential families, such as Gaussian and multinomial ADMG models.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we introduce the
m
-connecting imset which provides an alternative representation for ADMG independence models. Furthermore, we define the
m
-connecting factorization criterion for ADMG models and prove its equivalence to the global Markov property. The
m
-connecting imset and factorization criterion provide two new statistical tools for learning and inference with ADMG models. We demonstrate the usefulness of these tools by formulating and evaluating a consistent scoring criterion for Gaussian and multinomial ADMG models with a closed form. Unlike existing methods for scoring ADMG models, our proposed approach does not require the development of a distribution specific optimizer. Consequently, we do not solve for parameters in a non-convex space.

In the past five years, there has been an influx of methods capable of learning ADMGs by optimizing a score, such as the methods of
Chen et al., 2021
;
Nowzohour et al., 2017
;
Triantafillou and Tsamardinos, 2016
;
Tsirlis et al., 2018
—these methods rely on the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{ICF}}
score. One could both improve the efficiency of these algorithms and allow them to be extended to more general distributions by replacing the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{ICF}}
score with the
\textup{BIC}_{\textup{MF}}
score. Additionally, since our score is formulated using the
m
-connecting imset, one could also explore a geometric approach for learning ADMGs, similar to existing DAG learning methods
Studený and Haws, 2014
;
Studený and Cussens, 2017
;
Linusson et al., 2021
.

Appendix C
Factorization of Graphs with Five Vertices

The graphs illustrated below are called maximally informative partial ancestral graphs (PAGs). Intuitively, a maximally informative PAG graphically represents the Markov equivalence class of a MAG (or an ADMG). A maximally informative PAG is not a mixed graph, but a graph that summarizes a set of mixed graphs. In addition to the standard set of edges used by mixed graphs, maximally informative PAGs also include edges with circle edge marks to denote ambiguity—the edge mark varies among the summarized graphs.

In what follows, we enumerate all MECs (up to vertex relabeling) for ADMGs with 5 vertices. Each MEC is represented graphically using a PAG and the corresponding m-connecting factorization is written next to the PAG. By expanding the conditional interaction information rates we can ensure that the corresponding sets are disjoint—this means that there is no need for an adjustment term in the factorization.

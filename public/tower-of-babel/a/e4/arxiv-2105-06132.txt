Abstract
The motive associated to the second Symanzik polynomial of the double-box two-loop Feynman graph with generic masses and momenta is shown to be an elliptic curve.

Introduction

Cubic hypersurfaces in algebraic geometry are sort of analogous to the baby bear’s porridge in the tale of Goldilocks and the three bears. Hypersurfaces of degrees one and two are too cold and uninteresting, while degrees four and higher are too hot to handle. Degree three is just right. The author thanks Dirk Kreimer and Pierre Vanhove for explaining the importance for physics of two-loop Feynman graphs whose amplitudes are (mixed, or relative) periods of certain singular cubic hypersurfaces. In particular, the paper
[
6
]
suggests the central role the singularities of the second Symanzik hypersurface play. The basic setup of iterated subdivision of the two-loop sunset grew out of collaboration with Vanhove
[
1
]
. The kite graph (written
(2,1,2)
in the notation explained in Appendix
A
) also leads to an elliptic curve. The situation for the kite is more elementary since one can fall back on the classical theory of cubic threefolds with isolated double points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. Vanhove has been able to calculate the
j
-invariant for the resulting elliptic curve. A similar calculation for the double box looks difficult. The author was also influenced by unpublished work of Matt Kerr classifying Feynman motives associated to a number of other two-loop diagrams.

This paper focuses on what is perhaps the most striking example, the massive second Symanzik motive associated to the double box graph, see Figure

The massive second Symanzik in our case is a (singular) cubic hypersurface

The study of motives associated to two-loop graphs involves a lot of detailed algebraic geometry. The author can only hope he has gotten the details right! Another approach, which might yield more information in this case about the elliptic curve, could be based on the study of linear spaces contained in the second Symanzik variety. For example, projecting from a double point on the variety realizes a singular cubic hypersurface of dimension

Proposition 6.1
.

With notation as above, we have
F^{4}H^{5}(Z,{\mathbb{C}})=(0).

Proof.

Again from
[
3
, Chapter II, Proposition 3.13]
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}^{1}\big(W,\Omega^{5}_{W}(Z)\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{6}_{W}(2Z)\big)\cong F^{5}H^{6}(W-Z,{\mathbb{C}})\cong F^{4}H^{5}(Z,{\mathbb{C}}).

It will suffice to show

\displaystyle H^{0}\big(W,\Omega^{6}_{W}(2Z)\big)=0=H^{1}\big(W,\Omega^{5}_{W}(Z)\big).

Z
is birational with the cubic
X_{3,1,3}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{6}
, and a section of
\Omega^{6}_{W}
with only a pole of order
2
along
Z
would give rise to a section of
\Omega^{6}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}
with only a pole of order
2
along
X_{3,1,3}
. Since
\Omega^{6}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-7)
there are no such sections.

It remains to show
H^{1}\big(W,\Omega^{5}_{W}(Z)\big)=(0)
. By duality, it suffices to show
H^{5}\big(W,\Omega^{1}_{W}(-Z)\big)

=(0)
. Recall
W
is obtained from
{\mathbb{P}}^{6}
by blowing up
C\amalg C^{\prime}\amalg{\mathcal{S}}
. The exceptional divisors are
E
,
E^{\prime}
,
\coprod_{i}{\mathbb{P}}^{5}\subset W
. Define
\Xi=\Omega^{1}_{W}/\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}
. We claim

\displaystyle\Xi\cong\Omega^{1}_{E/C}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{E^{\prime}/C^{\prime}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{5}}.

Indeed there is a natural surjective map just by restricting
1
-forms from
W
to the exceptional divisors. Consider the local structure along
E
. Locally
C\colon z_{1}=\cdots=z_{5}=0
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{6}
. Locally upstairs, we have
E\colon z_{1}=0
and
\Omega^{1}_{W}=\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}+\sum{\mathcal{O}}_{W}{\rm d}(z_{i}/z_{1})
. Note

\displaystyle z_{1}{\rm d}(z_{i}/z_{1})={\rm d}z_{i}-(z_{i}/z_{1}){\rm d}z_{1}\in\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}.

This implies that
\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}\supset{\mathcal{O}}_{W}(-E)\Omega^{1}_{W}
, so
\Omega^{1}_{W}|_{E}\twoheadrightarrow\Xi
. Also the conormal bundle
N^{\vee}(E/W)={\mathcal{O}}_{W}(-E)/{\mathcal{O}}_{W}(-2E)
of
E\subset W
lies in
\Omega^{1}_{W}|_{E}
. Similarly, the conormal bundle
N^{\vee}_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}
lies in
\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}|_{C}
, and the pullback
\pi^{*}N^{\vee}_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}\to N^{\vee}(E/W)
is surjective. It follows that

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}_{E}=\big(\Omega^{1}_{W}|_{E}\big)/N^{\vee}(E/W)\twoheadrightarrow\Xi.

The proof of (
6.1
) is now straightforward.

We have the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-Z)\to\Omega^{1}_{W}(-Z)\to\Xi(-Z)\to 0.

(6.1)

In the Picard group of
W
we have
-Z=-\pi^{*}(X_{3,1,3})+2E+2E^{\prime}+2\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}
. We compute the direct images

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}\big(\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-Z)\big)=\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-3)\otimes{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}\bigg({\mathcal{O}}_{W}\bigg(2E+2E^{\prime}+2\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}\bigg)\!\bigg).

(6.2)

The calculus of intersection theory on blowups
[
4
, Section II.7]
, yields
E\cdot E=[{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)]
, the dual of the tautological relatively ample bundle on the projective bundle
E
over
C
(and similarly for
E^{\prime}
and the
{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}
). Applying
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}
to the exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\Big(E+E^{\prime}+\sum{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}\Big)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\Big(2E+2E^{\prime}+2\sum{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}\Big)

\displaystyle\phantom{0}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-2)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}(-2)\oplus\bigoplus_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{5}}(-2)\to 0

and the similar sequence

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\Big(E+E^{\prime}+\sum{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}\Big)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}(-1)+\sum{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}}(-1)\to 0

and recalling that for a projective space bundle
{\mathbb{P}}\xrightarrow{\pi}S
with fibre
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
we have
{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(-n)=(0)
for

, we can see that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}\bigg({\mathcal{O}}_{W}\bigg(2E+2E^{\prime}+2\sum_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathbb{P}}^{5}_{s}\bigg)\!\bigg)\cong{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{W}).

(6.3)

We want to use these results to prove
H^{5}\big(W,\Omega_{W}^{1}(-Z)\big)=(0)
. From (
6.1
), it will suffice to show

\displaystyle H^{5}\big(W,\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-Z)\big)=(0)=H^{5}(W,\Xi(-Z)).

From (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) we get

\displaystyle H^{5}\big(W,\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-Z)\big)=H^{5}\big({\mathbb{P}}^{6},\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-3)\otimes{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\big).

(6.4)

We claim
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{W})\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}
, i.e.,

for

. This can be checked locally on
{\mathbb{P}}^{6}
, so for notational simplicity we assume

is

[
4

for

and

\big(

\displaystyle R^{i}\pi_{*}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{W}(-nE)\big)\to R^{i}\pi_{*}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{W}(-(n-1)E)\big)\to R^{i}\pi_{*}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{E}(n-1)\big).

and

.

) we get

\displaystyle H^{5}\big(W,\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-Z)\big)=H^{5}\big({\mathbb{P}}^{6},\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{6}}(-3)\big).

Appendix A
The symanzik polynomials

Let
(m,1,n)
denote the
2
-loop graph obtained by subdividing the
3
edges of the sunset graph
(1,1,1)
into
m
(resp. 1, resp.
n
) edges. We associate edge variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
(resp.
x_{m+1}
, resp.
x_{m+2},\dots,x_{m+n+1}
) to the subdivided edges in the evident way. The first Symanzik polynomial is the quadratic polynomial

\displaystyle\phi_{m,1,n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},x_{m+1},x_{m+2},\dots,x_{m+n+1})=\sum x_{i}x_{j}

with the sum being taken over all pairs
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
such that cutting edges
i
and
j
renders the graph a tree. One checks

\displaystyle\phi_{m,1,n}=x_{m+1}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}+x_{m+2}+\cdots+x_{m+n+1})+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{j=m+2}^{m+n+1}x_{j}\bigg).

The (massless) second Symanzik polynomial
\psi_{m,1,n}
has degree
3
in the edge variables. The coefficients are quadratic in the momentum flows
w_{\mu}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{D}
where
\mu
runs over the vertices of the graph. A monomial
x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}
appears in
\psi_{m,1,n}
if cutting the edges
i
,
j
,
k
in
(m,1,n)
reduces the graph to a
2
-tree. Here
2
-tree means a subgraph with no loops and exactly
2
connected components. (Connected components may be isolated vertices.) The coefficient of
x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}
in
\psi_{m,1,n}
is computed as follows. Let
C
be one of the two connected components of the cut graph
(m,1,n)-\{i,j,k\}
. The coefficient of
x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}
in
\psi_{m,1,n}
is

\displaystyle c_{i,j,k}=\bigg(\sum_{\mu\in\text{vert}(C)}w_{\mu}\bigg)^{2}.

Here all vertex momenta are oriented to point inward, and the square refers to the quadratic form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{D}
. Because the sum of graph momenta vanishes, switching the choice of connected component
C
does not change
c_{i,j,k}
.

A reminder: the geometric results in this paper are predicated on the coefficients
c_{i,j,k}
being sufficiently generic. In particular, for the
(3,1,3)
double box case, when
D=1
the
c_{i,j,k}
are never sufficiently generic. They are sufficiently generic if
D=4
and the momentum vectors
w_{\mu}
have generic entries.

Finally, the massive second Symanzik is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{m,1,n}:=\Big(\sum m_{i}^{2}x_{i}\Big)\phi_{m,1,n}+\psi_{m,1,n}.

Proposition A.1
.

For generic values of masses
m_{i}
and momentum vectors, the massive second Symanzik can be written

\displaystyle\Psi_{m,1,n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+n+1})=Q(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})(x_{m+1}+\cdots+x_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Psi_{m,1,n}(x_1,\ldots,x_{m+n+1})=}{}+Q^{\prime}(x_{m+2},\dots,x_{m+n+1})(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m+1})+x_{m+1}{\mathcal{A}}.

Here
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are homogeneous of degree
2
, and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,m+1}&a_{1,m+2}&\cdots&a_{1,m+n+1}\\
a_{2,m+1}&\cdots&\cdots&a_{2,m+n+1}\\
a_{m,m+1}&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&a_{m+1,2}&\cdots&a_{m+1,m+n+1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{m+1}\\
x_{m+2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m+n+1}\end{pmatrix}\!.

(A.1)

The proof of the proposition is straightforward using a computer. The author will leave a program on his web page in the Publications folder.

Note that the terms in
{\mathcal{A}}
have generic coefficients. They are linear in
x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1}
and also in
x_{m+1},\dots,x_{m+n+1}
and in
x_{m+1}
. The quadrics
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
have generic coefficients.

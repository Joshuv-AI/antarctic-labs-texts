Conditional algebras
Sergio Celani, Rafał Gruszczyński and Paula Menchón

Abstract
. Drawing on the classic paper by Chellas, (1975) , we propose a general algebraic framework for studying a binary operation of conditional that models universal features of the “if …, then …” connective as strictly related to the unary modal necessity operator. To this end, we introduce a variety of conditional algebras , and we develop its duality and canonical extensions theory. MSC: Primary 06E25, Secondary 03G05 Keywords: conditionals, Boolean algebras, Boolean algebras with operators, conditional algebras, modal algebras, pseudo-subordination algebras, binary operators



1.
Introduction

In the paper “Basic conditional logic”
(
1975
)
, Brian Chellas put forward a family of propositional systems whose goal was to capture the properties of a binary connective
\rightarrowtail
, the so-called
conditional
.
1
1

1

Chellas used the ‘
\Rightarrow
’ symbol. As we reserve this one for our meta-implication connective, we have chosen ‘
\rightarrowtail
’ instead.
The presentation and the analysis were based on a simple and appealing idea: if-then sentences are closely involved with relative necessity. Since a sentence with the form “if
p
, then
q
” conveys dependency of the content of its consequent
q
on the content of the antecedent
p
, it may be interpreted in the following way:
q
must be true, provided that
p
is true, or—to put it differently—
q
obtains in all those states in which
p
holds true. Thus, on the most elementary level, the conditional conceived as an operator is strictly related to the well-known necessity operator.

The three basic axioms of Chellas’s—that result from the above-mentioned idea—were

(CN)

\displaystyle p\rightarrowtail\top\,,

(CM)

\displaystyle(p\rightarrowtail(q\wedge r))\rightarrow((p\rightarrowtail q)\wedge(p\rightarrowtail r))\,,

(CC)

\displaystyle((p\rightarrowtail q)\wedge(p\rightarrowtail r))\rightarrow(p\rightarrowtail(q\wedge r))\,,

and the system based on them (and the classical logic) was named
normal conditional logic
. A special kind of frame semantics (with a function
f\colon W\times\mathcal{P}(W)\to\mathcal{P}(W)
in lieu of the routine ternary relation in
W^{3}
) was developed, and the standard metatheorems connecting it with the logic were proven.
2
2

2

Further analysis of the Chellas approach to conditionals was carried out by
Segerberg, (1989)
.

It is easy to see that each axiom has its counterpart in which
\rightarrowtail
is replaced with the family of relative necessity operators
[p]
, one for each variable
p
. Thinking of
p\rightarrowtail q
as
[p]q
(
q
is necessary relative to
p
), instead of the three axioms, we have infinitely many of them as instances of the following axioms schemata:

\displaystyle[\alpha]\top\,,

\displaystyle[\alpha](q\wedge r)\rightarrow([\alpha]q\wedge[\alpha]r)\,,

\displaystyle([\alpha]q\wedge[\alpha]r)\rightarrow[\alpha](q\wedge r)\,.

where
\alpha
marks the place in which we can put any propositional variable from the language. These explain the choice of the three axioms for the conditional logic.

The algebraic semantics for Chellas’s logic (and related systems) was provided by
Nute, (1980)
in the form of Boolean algebras with a binary operator
\rightarrowtriangle
interpreting
\rightarrowtail
.
3
3

3

Originally, Nute used ‘
\ast
’ instead of our ‘
\rightarrowtriangle
’.
He defined classes of algebras satisfying various conditions put upon
\rightarrowtriangle
, of which the variety of normal regular algebras satisfying conditions:

(C1)

\displaystyle a\rightarrowtriangle 1=1\,,

(C2)

\displaystyle(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\wedge(a\rightarrowtriangle c)=a\rightarrowtriangle(b\wedge c)\,,

corresponds directly to Chellas’s normal conditional logic. Among others, Nute proved the completeness theorem for this logic and normal regular algebras.
4
4

4

A similar algebraic semantics for the basic conditional logic of Chellas, but based on Heyting algebras, was presented in
(
Weiss,, 2018
)
.

In this paper, we aim to focus our attention on and delve into the study of Boolean algebras expanded with the
\rightarrowtriangle
operator that meets (
C1
) and (
C2
), and which in light of Nute’s results are algebraic models of Chellas’s normal conditional logic. The consequence of (
C2
) is that, in the second coordinate,
\rightarrowtriangle
is isotone, which opens the possibility of utilizing the algebraic tools developed in
(
Celani,, 2009
;
Menchón,, 2018
;
Celani and Menchón,, 2019
)
for monotonic operators. To be able to do this, we must ensure full monotonicity, which can easily be obtained by adopting the third condition

(C3)

(a\vee b)\rightarrowtriangle c\leq(a\rightarrowtriangle c)\wedge(b\rightarrowtriangle c)\,,

that, in the event, is equivalent to
\rightarrowtriangle
being antitone in the first coordinate. Our goals justify the choice, but the condition is also the counterpart of the following axiom considered by Chellas

(CM
′
)

((p\vee q)\rightarrowtail r)\rightarrow((p\rightarrowtail r)\wedge(q\rightarrowtail r))\,,

being one of the axioms considered for conditionals.
5
5

5

The algebraic counterpart of (
CM′
) is missing from Nute’s study of conditional logic, yet his completeness theorem extends to the normal conditional logic with (
CM′
) and the variety of conditional algebras. Let us also observe, after
(
Egré and Rott,, 2021
, Section 3.5)
, that the propositional version of this axiom (referred to as Simplification of Disjunctive
Antecedents) is “a bone of contention between theorists”. Although considered intuitively valid by many logicians, it was shown to entail monotonicity of conditionals, which is considered as one of the three paradigmatic invalidities of conditional logic (next to transitivity and contraposition). However, the monotonicity can be obtained by means of a certain rule of inference called LLE in
(
Egré and Rott,, 2021
, Section 3.3)
. The rule has been abandoned by some scholars in preference for the axiom (see e.g.,
Nute,, 1980
;
Fine,, 2012
;
Ciardelli et al.,, 2018
;
Santorio,, 2018
), but some other contemporary treatments of the logic of conditionals do not mention SDA at all (see
Unterhuber and Schurz,, 2014
;
Weiss,, 2019
). In our environment, there is no risk of obtaining the undesired monotonicity, and our hereby presented work may be considered as a contribution to the algebraic analysis of the very basic conditional logic validating SDA.

Thus, we adopt the following

Definition 1.1
.

\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle
is a
conditional
algebra if
A
is a Boolean algebra and
\rightarrowtriangle
is a binary operation on
A
that satisfies (
C1
), (
C2
), and (
C3
). The
\rightarrowtriangle
operation will be called the

. The class of conditional algebras is a variety, which we will denote by means of
\mathsf{CA}
.

\mathord{\dashv}

In the sequel, we begin with the development of the theory of canonical extensions of conditional algebras. In particular, we prove that
\mathsf{CA}
is closed under such extensions. Then, we introduce the notion of a
multimodal antitone algebra
, which is a Boolean algebra
A
with a family of unary necessity operators
\Box_{a}
(indexed by the elements of a subalgebra of

) corresponding to
\rightarrowtriangle
in the same way as Chellas’s
[p]
’s correspond to
\rightarrowtail
. We prove that conditional algebras and multi-modal antitone algebras are term equivalent, and we investigate the relation between their canonical extensions. Having finished this, we develop topological and categorical dualities for conditional algebras, and we characterize their subalgebras and congruences.

From a purely algebraic point of view,
\mathsf{CA}
is a generalization of some well-known varieties, such as:

(1)

pseudo-contact algebras
(
Düntsch and Vakarelov,, 2007
)
, and their equivalent counterparts subordination algebras
(
Bezhanishvili et al.,, 2017
)
,

(2)

pseudo-subordination algebras
(
Celani and Jansana,, 2022
)
,

(3)

strict-implication algebras and its subvariety of symmetric strict-implication algebras
(
Bezhanishvili et al.,, 2019
)
.

In the last section of the paper, we characterize these as subvarieties of

, using the tools developed in earlier sections.

2.
Conditional algebras and their canonical extensions

Lemma 2.1
.

In every conditional algebra
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle
:

(2.1)

If
a\leq b
, then
x\rightarrowtriangle a\leq x\rightarrowtriangle b
.

(2.2)

If
a\leq b
, then
b\rightarrowtriangle x\leq a\rightarrowtriangle x
.

(2.3)

\displaystyle(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\wedge(x\rightarrowtriangle y)\leq(a\wedge x)\rightarrowtriangle(b\wedge y)\,.

Proof.

(
2.1
) Let
a=a\wedge b
. Then by (
C2
)

x\rightarrowtriangle a=x\rightarrowtriangle(a\wedge b)=(x\rightarrowtriangle a)\wedge(x\rightarrowtriangle b)\,.

(
2.2
) This time, let
b=a\vee b
. Then applying (
C3
) we get

b\rightarrowtriangle x=(a\vee b)\rightarrowtriangle x\leq(a\rightarrowtriangle x)\wedge(b\rightarrowtriangle x)\,.

(
2.3
) Since
a\wedge x\leq a,x
we apply the previous point and (
C2
)

\displaystyle(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\wedge(x\rightarrowtriangle y)

\displaystyle{}\leq[(a\wedge x)\rightarrowtriangle b]\wedge[(a\wedge x)\rightarrowtriangle y]

\displaystyle{}=(a\wedge x)\rightarrowtriangle(b\wedge y)\,.\qed

Since—unlike, e.g., in the case of pseudo-subordination algebras—conditional operators are not in one-to-one correspondence with binary normal modal operators, it is necessary to employ a more expressive framework than ternary relations between ultrafilters for their representation. To build ultrafilter frames for conditional algebras we are going to need a ternary hybrid relation (i.e., involving both points and sets of points) associated with the conditional operator. Given
\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
, and
X,Y\subseteq A
we define the set

\displaystyle D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y)\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\{b\in A:(\exists a\in Y)\,a\rightarrowtriangle b\in X\}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\{\pi_{2}(\left\langle a,b\right\rangle):a\in Y\text{ and }a\rightarrowtriangle b\in X\}\,,

where
\pi_{2}
is the standard projection on the second coordinate. Intuitively,
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y)
is the set of all consequents of those conditionals in
X
whose antecedents are in
Y
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
.

(1)

If
Y_{1}\subseteq Y_{2}\subseteq A
, then
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y_{1})\subseteq D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y_{2})
, for any
X\subseteq A
.

(2)

If
Y
is upward closed, then
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{Y}(X)
is too, for any
X\subseteq A
.

(3)

D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\in\Fi(A)
, for all
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{H}\in\Fi(A)
.

Proof.

Ad 1. If
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y_{1})
, then by definition there exists
a\in Y_{1}
such that
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in X
. But
a\in Y_{2}
by the assumption and we get that
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{X}(Y_{1})
.

Ad 2. Let
a\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{Y}(X)
and
a\leq b
. By definition, in
X
there is a
c
such that
c\rightarrowtriangle a\in Y
. From (
2.1
) we get that
c\rightarrowtriangle a\leq c\rightarrowtriangle b
and since
Y
is upward closed,
c\rightarrowtriangle b\in Y
. In consequence
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{Y}(X)
, as required.

Ad 3. From the previous proposition, we get that
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})
is upward closed. Further,
1\in\mathscr{F}
and
1\rightarrowtriangle 1=1\in\mathscr{H}
.
Thus,
1\in D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})
.

To show that the set is closed under infima, take its elements
a
and
b
. So there are
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathscr{F}

such that
c_{1}\rightarrowtriangle a,c_{2}\rightarrowtriangle b\in\mathscr{H}
. By the fact that
\mathscr{H}
is
a filter and by (
2.3
) we get that:
c_{1}\wedge c_{2}\rightarrowtriangle a\wedge b\in\mathscr{H}
.
As
c_{1}\wedge c_{2}\in\mathscr{F}
,
a\wedge b\in D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})
.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

For a conditional algebra
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle
the
ultrafilter frame
of
\mathfrak{A}
is the structure
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Uf}}}(\mathfrak{A})\coloneqq\langle\Ul(A),T_{A}\rangle
,
with
T_{A}\subseteq\Ul(A)\times\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A))\times\Ul(A)

such that

(
\mathrm{df}\,T_{A}
)

T_{A}(u,Z,v)\quad\text{iff}\quad(\exists\mathscr{F}\in\Fi(A))(Z=\varphi(\mathscr{F})\wedge D_{u}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v)\,.

Thus,
T_{A}
holds between points and closed subsets of the Stone space of
A
.

\mathord{\dashv}

Given a set
Y\subseteq\Ul(A)
let

T_{A}(u,Y)\coloneqq\{v\in\Ul(A):T_{A}(u,Y,v)\}\,.

Since

(2.4)

\begin{split}T_{A}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}))&{}=\{v\in\Ul(A):D_{u}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v\}\\
&{}=\varphi(D_{u}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F}))\,,\end{split}

we get that

Proposition 2.4
.

T_{A}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}))
is always a closed subset of the Stone space of
A
.

The definition of the relation
T_{A}
is strictly related to and motivated by the techniques and results from
(
Celani,, 2009
;
Menchón,, 2018
;
Celani and Menchón,, 2019
)
. The papers deal with monotone operators that are modeled by hybrid relations
6
6

6

These are called
multirelations
in the aforementioned papers.
on its dual space, i.e., relations between ultrafilters and sets of ultrafilters. In particular, we get that, given a Boolean algebra with a binary operator
f
that is antitone in the first coordinate and isotone in the second, the dual relation
R_{f}
of
f
is a set of triples in
\Ul(A)\times\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A))\times\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A))
such that

\displaystyle R_{f}(u,Z,Y)\quad\text{iff}\quad(\exists\mathscr{F}\in\Fi(A))(\exists\mathscr{I}\in\Id(A))

\displaystyle\,(Z=\varphi(\mathscr{F})\ \text{and}\

\displaystyle Y=\varphi(\neg\mathscr{I})

\displaystyle\ \text{and}\ f^{-1}[u]\cap(\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{I})=\emptyset)

where:

(1)

\Id(A)
is the set of ideals of
A
,

(2)

\neg\mathscr{I}=\{\neg a:a\in\mathscr{I}\}
, and

(3)

f^{-1}[u]=\{\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\in A^{2}:f(a,b)\in u\}

Applying this to the particular case of the conditional operator, we first observe that

(\rightarrowtriangle)^{-1}[u]\cap(\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{I})=\emptyset\quad\text{iff}\quad D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})\cap\mathscr{I}=\emptyset\,.

Using Lemma
2.2
according to which
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})
is a filter, we obtain that

R_{\rightarrowtriangle}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}),\varphi(\neg\mathscr{I}))\quad\text{iff}\quad(\exists v\in\Ul(A))\,(D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v\ \text{and}\ v\in\varphi(\neg\mathscr{I}))\,.

In consequence, if
R_{\rightarrowtriangle}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}),\varphi(\neg\mathscr{I}))
, then there exists an ultrafilter
v
extending
\neg\mathscr{I}
such that
R_{\rightarrowtriangle}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}),\{v\})
.
So, replacing the singleton of

with

to the equivalent
T_{A}\subseteq\Ul(A)\times\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A))\times\Ul(A)

2.1.
Representation of conditional algebras

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a set, and let
\mathcal{F}_{X}
be any family of subsets of
X
.
\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\langle X,T\rangle
is a
ternary hybrid frame
(abbr.

) if

. As earlier, let

T(x,Z)\coloneqq\{y\in X:T(x,Z,y)\}.

\mathord{\dashv}

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\langle X,T\rangle
is a t-frame and
\mathcal{F}_{X}
is a family of subsets of

then the structure
\langle\mathcal{F}_{X},\rightarrowtriangle_{T}\rangle
such that

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\rightarrowtriangle_{T}}
)

U\rightarrowtriangle_{T}V\coloneqq\{x\in X:(\forall Z\in\mathcal{P}(U)\cap\mathcal{F}_{X})\,T(x,Z)\subseteq V\}

is a conditional algebra.

Proof.

(

) Immediate.

(

) Let
x\in(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T}V)\cap(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T}W)
,
Z\in\mathcal{P}(U)\cap\mathcal{F}_{X}
and

be such that

. By assumption we get that
y\in V
and
y\in W
, so
y\in V\cap W
. The converse is analogous.

(

) Let

,
Z\in\mathcal{P}(U)\cap\mathcal{F}_{X}
and

and, by assumption,

. The other inclusion is analogous.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Given a t-frame
\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\langle X,T\rangle
, its

is the structure
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Cm}}}(\mathfrak{X})\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{P}(X),\rightarrowtriangle_{T}\rangle

\mathord{\dashv}

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
. The full complex algebra of the ultrafilter frame
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Uf}}}(\mathfrak{A})
is

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Em}}}(\mathfrak{A})\coloneqq\operatorname{{\mathsf{Cm}}}(\operatorname{{\mathsf{Uf}}}(\mathfrak{A}))=\langle\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A)),\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{A}}\rangle\,.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
. Let
\mathscr{H}\in\Fi(A)

and let
a,b\in A
. Then
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in\mathscr{H}
if and only if for every filter

\mathscr{F}
such that
a\in\mathscr{F}
,
b\in D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})
.

Consequently,
a\rightarrowtriangle b\notin\mathscr{H}
if and only if there exist a filter
\mathscr{F}
and an ultrafilter

u\in\Ul(A)
such that
a\in\mathscr{F}
,
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq u

and
b\notin u
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in\mathscr{H}
and
a\in\mathscr{F}
, then
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{F})
by definition.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that for any filter
\mathscr{F}
such that
a\in\mathscr{F}
,
b\in D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})
. In particular,
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(\upop a)
, so there is
x\geq a
such that
x\rightarrowtriangle b\in\mathscr{H}
. Since by (
2.2
) it is the case that
x\rightarrowtriangle b\leq a\rightarrowtriangle b
, we have that
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in\mathscr{H}
.
∎

Theorem 2.10

(Representation Theorem)
.

For every
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
, the Stone mapping
\varphi\colon A\to\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A))
is an embedding of the algebra into
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Em}}}(\mathfrak{A})
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
u\in\varphi(a)\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{A}}\varphi(b)
, i.e.,
(\forall Z\subseteq\varphi(a))\,T_{A}(u,Z)\subseteq\varphi(b)
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a filter such that
a\in\mathscr{F}
. Thus
\varphi(\mathscr{F})\subseteq\varphi(a)
and so
T_{A}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}))\subseteq\varphi(b)
. By (
2.4
) we obtain that
\varphi(D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F}))\subseteq\varphi(b)
, which entails that
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})
. But this, according to Lemma
2.9
means that
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in u
.

(
\Leftarrow
) In this case assuming that
a\rightarrowtriangle b\in u
we have to show that

(\forall Z\subseteq\varphi(a))\,T_{A}(u,Z)\subseteq\varphi(b)\,.

If
Z\subseteq\varphi(a)
is not closed, then
T_{A}(u,Z)=\emptyset
. So assume
Z=\varphi(\mathscr{F})
. Since
a\in\mathscr{F}
by assumption, we have by Lemma
2.9
that
b\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})
. So
\varphi(D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F}))\subseteq\varphi(b)
, and
T_{A}(u,\varphi(\mathscr{F}))\subseteq\varphi(b)
by (
2.4
).
∎

2.2.
Canonical extensions of the conditional operator

We will show that the operator
\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{A}}
of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Em}}}(\mathfrak{A})
is indeed a
\pi
-extension of the
\rightarrowtriangle
of
\mathfrak{A}\in\mathsf{CA}
and in consequence
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Em}}}(\mathfrak{A})
is the canonical extension of
\mathfrak{A}
.
We will also prove that the
\pi
and
\sigma
-extensions of
\rightarrowtriangle
are different.

Let us recall that given a Boolean algebra
A
with an
n

A representation theorem for t-frames

In general, it is not possible to show that every t-frame

Take

6.
Categorical duality for conditional algebras

As is well known, Boolean homomorphisms correspond in a one-to-one manner to continuous functions of the dual spaces. If
h\colon A\to B
is a homomorphism of Boolean algebras, then the function
f_{h}\colon\Ul(B)\to\Ul(A)
given by

f_{h}(u)\coloneqq h^{-1}[u]

is a continuous function. On the other hand, if
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
is continuous function between Boolean spaces
\langle X_{1},\tau_{1}\rangle
and
\langle X_{2},\tau_{2}\rangle
, then the map
h_{f}\colon\clopen(\tau_{2})\to\clopen(\tau_{1})
such that

h_{f}(U)\coloneqq f^{-1}[U]

is a homomorphism of the Boolean algebras. We are going to make use of this correspondence to extend it to maps between conditional algebras and conditional spaces.

Definition 6.1
.

A homomorphism
h\colon A\to B
of conditional algebras is a homomorphism of Boolean algebras that also satisfies

(6.1)

h(a\rightarrowtriangle b)=h(a)\rightarrowtriangle h(b).

We will call such an
h
a
conditional
homomorphism.

\mathord{\dashv}

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle,\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\left\langle B,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
and
h\colon A\to B
be a Boolean homomorphism.
h
is a conditional homomorphism if and only if for all
u\in\Ul(B)
,
a,b\in A
the following equivalence holds

T_{B}(u,f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(a)])\subseteq f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(b)]\quad\text{iff}\quad T_{A}(f_{h}(u),\varphi(a))\subseteq\varphi(b).

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume that
h
is a homomorphism of conditional algebras. For the proof by contraposition, assume that
v\in\Ul(A)
is an element of
T_{A}(f_{h}(u),\varphi(a))
but
b\notin v
. Then,
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{f_{h}(u)}(\upop a)\subseteq v
, and
b\notin D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{f_{h}(u)}(\upop a)
, i.e.,
a\rightarrowtriangle b\notin h^{-1}[u]
. So,
h(a)\rightarrowtriangle h(b)=h(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\notin u
, and applying (
2.2
) we obtain that
h(b)\notin D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\upop h(a))
. It follows that there exists
w\in\Ul(B)
such that

w\in T_{B}(u,\varphi(h(a)))=T_{B}(u,f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(a)])

but
w\notin\varphi(h(b))=f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(b)]
, as required.

Analogously, let
v
be an ultrafilter of
B
such that

. Thus
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\upop h(a))\subseteq v
and

, since
f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(b)]=\varphi(h(b))
. Then,
h(a\rightarrowtriangle b)=h(a)\rightarrowtriangle h(b)\notin u
and we get
a\rightarrowtriangle b\notin h^{-1}[u]=f_{h}(u)

) it follows again that

, so there is an ultrafilter

that extends

T_{A}(f_{h}(u),\varphi(a))\nsubseteq\varphi(b)

(
\Leftarrow

and

be such that

and we get

. Thus,
T_{A}(f_{h}(u),\varphi(a))\subseteq\varphi(b)

T_{B}(u,f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(a)])\subseteq f_{h}^{-1}[\varphi(b)]
and it follows that

h(a)\rightarrowtriangle h(b)\in u

h(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\leq h(a)\rightarrowtriangle h(b)

Making use of Lemma

we introduce the following

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}_{1}\coloneqq\langle X_{1},\tau_{1},T_{1}\rangle
and
\mathfrak{X}_{2}\coloneqq\langle X_{2},\tau_{2},T_{2}\rangle
be conditional spaces. A continuous function

between Boolean spaces
\langle X_{1},\tau_{1}\rangle
and
\langle X_{2},\tau_{2}\rangle
is a

and

(
\mathrm{CF}
)

\displaystyle T_{1}(x,f^{-1}[U])\subseteq f^{-1}[V]\quad\text{iff}\quad T_{2}(f(x),U)\subseteq V.

\mathord{\dashv}

Observe that the identity function trivially satisfies (

CF

).

Lemma 6.4
.

Condition (

CF

) is equivalent to

h_{f}(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V)=h_{f}(U)\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{1}}h_{f}(V)

for all
U,V\in\clopen(\tau_{2})
, i.e., the function
h_{f}\colon\clopen(\tau_{2})\to\clopen(\tau_{1})
is a conditional homomorphism.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
f
satisfies (

CF

). Then we have
x\in h_{f}(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V)
iff
f(x)\in U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V
iff
T_{2}(f(x),U)\subseteq V
iff
T_{1}(x,f^{-1}[U])\subseteq f^{-1}[V]
iff

. Thus, the equality follows.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
h_{f}(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V)=h_{f}(U)\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{1}}h_{f}(V)
. Then,
T_{1}(x,f^{-1}[U])\subseteq f^{-1}[V]
iff
x\in h_{f}(U)\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{1}}h_{f}(V)
iff
x\in h_{f}(U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V)
iff
x\in f^{-1}[U\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{2}}V]
iff
T_{2}(f(x),U)\subseteq V
.
∎

Conditional algebras with conditional homomorphisms form a category in which the identity arrow is the identity homomorphism and the composition is the usual composition of functions. Also, conditional spaces with conditional functions form a category in which the identity arrow is given by the identity function and the composition is the usual composition of functions. We will denote these categories by
\mathbf{CA}
and
\mathbf{CS}
, respectively.

Notice that Theorem
5.4
and Lemma
6.2
allow to define a contravariant functor
G\colon\mathbf{CA}\to\mathbf{CS}
as follows:

\displaystyle\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle

\displaystyle\mapsto\langle\Ul(A),\tau_{s},T_{A}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\mapsto f_{h}.

Thanks to Proposition
2.6
, condition
(T2)
and Lemma
6.4
we can define a contravariant functor
H\colon\mathbf{CS}\to\mathbf{CA}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\langle X,\tau,T\rangle

\displaystyle\mapsto\langle\clopen(\tau),\rightarrowtriangle_{T}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{f}.

Theorem 6.5
.

The categories
\mathbf{CA}
and
\mathbf{CS}
are dually equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
. By Theorem
2.10
the map
\varphi\colon A\to\clopen(\tau_{s})
defines a natural isomorphism between

and
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{CA}}
. On the other hand, if
\langle X,\tau,T\rangle
is a conditional space, then from Theorem
5.8
we get that the map
\varepsilon\colon X\to\Ul(\clopen(\tau))
leads to a natural isomorphism between

and
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{CS}}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Characterization of conditional subalgebras

In this section, we present a characterization of the subalgebras of
conditional algebras.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
E
be an equivalence relation on a Boolean space

X
, end let
E(x)\coloneqq\{y\in X:E(x,y)\}
. A subset
U
of
X
is
closed under

E
if

x\in U
implies
E(x)\subseteq U
. An equivalence relation

on

if for each pair
\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\notin E

exists a clopen subset
U
of
X
that is closed under
E
and such that
x\in U

and
y\notin U
.

For

let

E(U)\coloneqq\{y\in X:(\exists x\in U)\,E(x,y)\}=\bigcup_{x\in U}E(x)\,.

If
A
is a Boolean algebra and
B
is a subalgebra of
A
,
then it is easy to show that the relation

E_{B}\coloneqq\left\{\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\in\Ul(A)\times\Ul(A):u\cap B=v\cap B\right\}

is a Boolean equivalence. Conversely, if
E
is a Boolean equivalence
of a Boolean space

then

is the domain of a subalgebra of the Boolean algebra
\clopen(\tau)
.
Moreover, the correspondence
B\mapsto E_{B}
is a dual isomorphism
between the lattice of domains of the subalgebras of
A
and the
lattice of the Boolean equivalence of the Stone space of
A
(see
Koppelber,, 1989
).

Our next aim is to prove that there is a bijective correspondence
between subalgebras of conditional algebras and certain Boolean
equivalences with an additional condition.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
E
be a Boolean equivalence on a space

\preceq_{E}
on
\closed(\tau)

as follows:

if and only if for every

and

Lemma 7.3

Let
A

(

(
\Leftarrow

and
u\cap B

Definition 7.4

8.
Congruences of conditional algebras

This section aims to study the congruences of conditional algebras. We use the categorical duality
developed in Section
6
in order to prove that for every conditional
algebra
\mathfrak{A}
there is a dual isomorphism between
the congruences of
A
and a family of closed subsets of
the dual space of
\mathfrak{A}
.

Let
\Cong(A)
be the lattice of congruences of an algebra
A
. For every
a\in A
and
\theta\in\Cong(A)
,
a/\theta
is an equivalence class of
a
with respect to
\theta
.
A/\theta
is the quotient algebra of
A
. According to a well-known folklore result on the Stone duality, for every closed subset
Y
of
\Ul(A)
, the set

\theta(Y)\coloneqq\{\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\in A\times A\colon Y\cap\varphi(a)=Y\cap\varphi(b)\}

is a congruence on
A
. Moreover, the assignment
Y\mapsto\theta(Y)

establishes a dual isomorphism between the lattice of the closed subsets of the Stone space
\Ul(A)
and the lattice
\Cong(A)
.

Let
\langle X,\tau,T\rangle
be a conditional space. For any pair of elements
x
and
y
we define

\mathbf{C}(x,y)\coloneqq\{Z\in\closed(\tau):T(x,Z,y)\}.

We will see that it is closed under intersection of chains. Let us consider a chain
\mathcal{C}
of members of
\mathbf{C}(x,y)
. Suppose
\bigcap\mathcal{C}\notin\mathbf{C}(x,y)
. Then, there exists
U\in\clopen(\tau)
such that
T^{c}(x,U,y)
and
\bigcap\mathcal{C}\subseteq U
. By compactness of
U
, we get that there exists
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C\subseteq U
. But by
(T3)
, it follows
T(x,U,y)
, a contradiction.

So, from the Kuratowski-Zorn lemma, we get that the set
\{X\setminus C:C\in\mathcal{C}\}
has maximal elements (with respect to set inclusion), provided it is non-empty. Dually,
\mathcal{C}
has minimal elements, provided it is non-empty. Therefore, we can introduce the following definition.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\langle X,\tau,T\rangle
be a conditional space and let
Y\in\closed(\tau)
. We say that
Y
is a
T
-closed set
if
for all
x,y\in X
, if
x\in Y
and
Z
is minimal in
\mathbf{C}(x,y)
, then
Z\subseteq Y
and
y\in Y
.

\mathord{\dashv}

In the proof of the next theorem, we will use the following standard result.

Lemma 8.2
.

If
Y
is a closed subset of the Stone space of
A
and
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\in\theta(Y)
, then there exists an
x\in\mathscr{F}_{Y}
such that
a\wedge x=b\wedge x
.

Proof.

If
Y
is closed, then
Y\cap\varphi(a)
is closed too, so there is a filter
\mathscr{F}_{1}
such that
Y\cap\varphi(a)=\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{1})
. Consider the filter
\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}
generated by
\mathscr{F}_{Y}\cup\{a\}
. We aim to show that
\mathscr{F}_{1}=\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}
.

We have that
\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{1})\subseteq Y
and
\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{1})\subseteq\varphi(a)
. Thus
\mathscr{F}_{Y}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{1}
and
a\in\mathscr{F}_{1}
. So
\mathscr{F}_{Y}\cup\{a\}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{1}
and
\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{1}
. For the other direction, assume that
\mathscr{F}_{1}\nsubseteq\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}
. Therefore there exists an ultrafilter
u
such that
\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}\subseteq u
and
\mathscr{F}_{1}\nsubseteq u
. But
u\in\varphi(a)\cap Y=\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{1})
and thus
u\in\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{1})
, a contradiction.

In an analogous way we demonstrate that the filter
\mathscr{F}_{2}
such that
Y\cap\varphi(b)=\varphi(\mathscr{F}_{2})
is equal to
\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{b}
, the filter generated by
\mathscr{F}_{Y}\cup\{b\}
.

Thus
\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}=\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{b}
. Since
b\in\mathscr{F}_{Y}^{a}
, there is

9.1.
Dual spaces of pseudo-subordination algebras

For a Boolean algebra
A
let

\closed_{+}(\tau_{s})\coloneqq\closed(\tau_{s})\setminus\{\emptyset\}\,.

By definition,

. For the condition (
C1∗
) we have the following

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle
be a conditional algebra. Then, (
C1∗
) holds in
\mathfrak{A}
iff
T_{A}\subseteq\Ul(A)\times\closed_{+}(\tau_{s})\times\Ul(A)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose that there exists
a\in A
such that
0\rightarrowtriangle a\neq 1
. So, there exists
u\in\Ul(A)
such that
0\rightarrowtriangle a\notin u
. In consequence,
a\notin D^{\to}_{u}(A)
, which means that
D^{\to}_{u}(A)
is a proper filter and as such can be extended to
v\in\Ul(A)
:
D^{\to}_{u}(A)\subseteq v
. As
\varphi(A)=\emptyset
, by definition, we get that
T_{A}(u,\emptyset,v)
.

(\Leftarrow)
Let
u\in\Ul(A)
. If
0\rightarrowtriangle a=1
for all
a\in A
, then
D^{\to}_{u}(A)=A
and therefore
T_{A}(u,\emptyset)=\varphi(D^{\to}_{u}(A))=\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let

\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}+\eqref{C3-reversed}
. Let
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{H}\in\Fi(A)
,
\mathscr{F}\neq A
and
u\in\Ul(A)
.
If
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq u
, then there exists
v\in\varphi(\mathscr{F})

such that
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(v)\subseteq u
.

Proof.

Let
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq u
. Consider the family

\mathbf{F}\coloneqq\{\mathscr{G}\in\Fi(A):D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}(\mathscr{G})\subseteq u\text{ and }\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{G}\}\,.

The family is non-empty, as
\mathscr{F}\in\mathbf{F}
, and—as it is routine to verify—for any non-empty chain
C\subseteq\mathbf{F}
,
\bigcup C\in\mathbf{F}
. Thus, by Kuratowski-Zorn Lemma
\mathbf{F}
has a maximal element
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
.

If
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}=A
then we can consider an arbitrary ultrafilter
v
extending
\mathscr{F}
. By monotonicity of
D_{\mathscr{H}}^{\rightarrowtriangle}
we have
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(v)\subseteq D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(A)\subseteq u
.

Let then
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}\subsetneq A
. We are going to show that
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
is prime, and so an ultrafilter. To this end, we assume that
a\vee b\in\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
. For the sake of contradiction, let
a\notin\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
and
b\notin\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
. Let us consider the filters
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{a}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{b}
generated by
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}\cup\{a\}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}\cup\{b\}
, respectively. Since
\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
is a proper subset of both and is maximal in
\mathbf{F}
, neither of the two filters is in
\mathbf{F}
, which means that

D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{a})\nsubseteq u\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{b})\nsubseteq u\,.

In consequence there exist
z_{1}\in\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{a}
,
z_{2}\in\mathscr{F}^{\ast}_{b}
and
x_{1},x_{2}\notin u
such
that

z_{1}\rightarrowtriangle x_{1},z_{2}\rightarrowtriangle x_{2}\in\mathscr{H}.

By construction, there are
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
such that
y_{1}\wedge a\leq z_{1}

and
y_{2}\wedge b\leq z_{2}
. Put
y\coloneqq y_{1}\wedge y_{2}
and
x\coloneqq x_{1}\vee x_{2}\notin u
. By (
2.2
) both
y\wedge a\rightarrowtriangle x
and
y\wedge b\rightarrowtriangle x
are in
\mathscr{H}
, and so by (
C3∗
)

(y\wedge a\rightarrowtriangle x)\wedge(y\wedge b\rightarrowtriangle x)\leq y\wedge(a\vee b)\rightarrowtriangle x\in\mathscr{H}.

Since
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{F}^{\ast})\subseteq u
and
y\wedge(a\vee b)\in\mathscr{F}^{\ast}
it follows that
x\in u
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
. If the relation
T_{A}
of its dual space
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})
satisfies

(\forall u,v\in\Ul(A))(\forall Y\neq\emptyset)\,(T_{A}(u,Y,v)\Rightarrow(\exists w\in Y)\,T_{A}(u,\{w\},v)

then

satisfies (

).

Proof.

Suppose that there exist
a,b,c\in A
such that
(a\rightarrowtriangle c)\wedge(b\rightarrowtriangle c)\nleq(a\vee b\rightarrowtriangle c)
. Then, there exists an ultrafilter
u
such that
(a\rightarrowtriangle c)\wedge(b\rightarrowtriangle c)\in u
but
(a\vee b\rightarrowtriangle c)\notin u
. Thus there exists
v\in\Ul(A)
such that
T(u,\varphi(a\vee b),v)
but
c\notin v
. Note that
a\vee b\neq 0
, so
\varphi(a\vee b)\neq\emptyset
. By assumption, there exists
w\in\varphi(a\vee b)
such that
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(w)\subseteq v
. But
c\in D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(w)
which is a contradiction.
∎

From lemmas
9.1
,
9.2
and
9.3
we get the dual spaces of pseudo-subordination algebras.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}
.
\mathfrak{A}
is a pseudo-subordination algebra iff the relation
T_{A}
of its dual space
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})
satisfies
:

if
T_{A}(u,Y,v)
, then there exists
w\in Y
such that
T_{A}(u,\{w\},v)
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume
\mathfrak{A}
is a pseudo-subordination algebra. If
T_{A}(u,Y,v)
, then, by equation (
C1∗
), for some proper filter
\mathscr{F}
,
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v
. Thus, there is an ultrafilter
w
such that
\mathscr{F}\subseteq w
and
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{u}(w)\subseteq v
, i.e.,
T_{A}(u,\{w\},v)
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
T_{A}
satisfies the condition. By Lemma
9.1
, we get that
\mathfrak{A}
meets the equation (
C1∗
), and by Lemma
9.3
, we get that
A
meets (
C3∗
). Thus,
\mathfrak{A}
is a pseudo-subordination algebra.
∎

The previous theorem guarantees that for a pseudo-subordination algebra
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle
, the relation
T_{A}
is actually induced by a ternary relation
S
on
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})
defined by

S(x,y,z)\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\longleftrightarrow}}\;T_{A}(x,\{y\},z).

Conversely,
T_{A}
can be recovered as

T_{A}(x,K,z)\text{ if and only if }K\in\closed(\tau_{s})\text{ and there is }y\in K\text{ such that }S(x,y,z).

9.2.
Correspondences

In this section we prove correspondences between the various axioms for
\rightarrowtriangle
presented in the previous subsection and first- and second-order properties of expanded Stone spaces of conditional algebras. To this end, we need the lemma below.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let

\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}

\mathscr{F},\mathscr{H}\in\Fi(A)

Assume that

\mathbf{F}\coloneqq\{\mathscr{G}\in\Fi(A):D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v\text{ and }\mathscr{H}\subseteq\mathscr{G}\}\,.

As
\mathscr{H}\in\mathbf{F}
, and
\mathbf{F}
is closed under unions of chains, by the Kuratowski-Zorn Lemma in
\mathbf{F}
there exists a maximal filter
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}

\mathscr{H}

\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
is proper, as
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{A}(\mathscr{F})=A\nsubseteq v

\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
is prime, assume that
a\vee b\in\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
but
a\notin\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
and
b\notin\mathscr{H}^{\ast}

Let us consider the filters
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{a}
and
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{b}
generated by
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}\cup\{a\}

and
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}\cup\{b\}
, respectively. Neither of them is in
\mathbf{F}
as they both extend
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
which is maximal in the set. In consequence
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{a}}(\mathscr{F})\nsubseteq v
and
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{b}}(\mathscr{F})\nsubseteq v
, so
there are
x_{1}\in\mathscr{F}
,
x_{2}\in\mathscr{F}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\notin v
such
that

x_{1}\rightarrowtriangle y_{1}\in\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{2}\rightarrowtriangle y_{2}\in\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{b}.

Take
y\coloneqq y_{1}\vee y_{2}\notin v
and
x\coloneqq x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\in\mathscr{F}

By Lemma

x\rightarrowtriangle y\in\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{a}\cap\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{b}

and
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}_{b}
there are

and

Then

and it is easy to see that

c\wedge(a\vee b)\leq x\rightarrowtriangle y\quad\text{and thus}\quad x\rightarrowtriangle y\in\mathscr{H}^{\ast}.

Since
D^{\rightarrowtriangle}_{\mathscr{H}^{\ast}}(\mathscr{F})\subseteq v
and

, we get

,
which is a contradiction. Thus,
\mathscr{H}^{\ast}
is prime, as required.

Let us say that a property
\Phi
of conditional algebras corresponds

if for every conditional

\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle

\mathfrak{A}
has

(in symbols:

(in symbols:
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})\models\Psi
). Of course, while speaking about a condition

for

\mathfrak{A}
satisfies, e.g.,

we mean that the closure of the formula holds in
\mathfrak{A}
. As all our constraints for
\rightarrowtriangle
are universal we will write—for example—
\mathfrak{A}\models a\rightarrowtriangle a=1
instead of
\mathfrak{A}\models(\forall a)\,a\rightarrowtriangle a=1
to save space and avoid notation cluttering. This will not lead to any ambiguity.

On the side of expanded spaces, we are interested in those properties of theirs that are expressible by means of
T_{A}

(1)

the expression ‘

(2)

‘

\forall u\in\Ul(A)
, and ‘
\forall Y

\forall Y\in\closed(\Ul(A))
(also with the possibility to use different letters).

Theorem 9.6

If
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle\in\mathsf{CA}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}\models a\rightarrowtriangle b\leq\neg b\rightarrowtriangle\neg a\hskip 9.24994pt

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{A}\models\neg(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\leq c\rightarrowtriangle\neg(a\rightarrowtriangle b)\hskip 9.24994pt&\text{iff}\hskip 9.24994pt\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})\models\forall uvw\forall Y\!Z(TuYv\,\&\,TuZw\Rightarrow TvZw)\,,\end{split}

Proof.

(

Definition 9.7
.

An equation
is
canonical
(i.e.,
\pi
-canonical
) for conditional algebras if whenever
it is valid in a conditional algebra
\mathfrak{A}\coloneqq\left\langle A,\rightarrowtriangle\right\rangle
, then it is valid
in the full complex algebra
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Em}}}(\mathfrak{A}))=\langle\mathcal{P}(\Ul(A)),\rightarrowtriangle_{T_{A}}\rangle

of its conditional space
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Es}}}(\mathfrak{A})\coloneqq\langle\Ul(A),\tau_{s},T_{A}\rangle
.

Lemma 9.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\langle X,\tau,T\rangle
be a conditional space. Let
x\in X
,
Y\in\closed(\tau)
and
W\subseteq X
. Then,
T(x,Y)\subseteq W
if and only if
x\in Y\rightarrowtriangle_{T}W
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
T(x,Y)\subseteq W
. Let
T(x,Z,y)
such that
Z\subseteq Y
. Let
U\in\clopen(\tau)
such that
Y\subseteq U
. Since
Z\subseteq U
, we get that
T(x,U,y)
. Thus, by condition
(T3)
,
T(x,Y,y)
and it follows
y\in W
.

(
\Leftarrow
)
x\in Y\rightarrowtriangle_{T}W
. Since
Y\subseteq Y
,
T(x,Y)\subseteq W
.
∎

Theorem 9.9
.

The following equivalences hold between any conditional space
\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\langle X,\tau,T\rangle
and its complex algebra
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Cm}}}(\mathfrak{X})=\left\langle\mathcal{P}(\Ul(X)),\rightarrowtriangle_{T}\right\rangle

(

T
at
\rightarrowtriangle

(T3
∗
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{{\mathsf{Cm}}}(\mathfrak{X})\models(a\rightarrowtriangle c)\wedge(b\rightarrowtriangle c)&{}\leq(a\vee b)\rightarrowtriangle c\quad\text{iff}\\
&\mathfrak{X}\models\forall xy\forall Y\neq\emptyset(TxYy\Rightarrow(\exists z\in Y)\,Tx\{z\}y)\end{split}

(T4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{{\mathsf{Cm}}}(\mathfrak{X})\models a\rightarrowtriangle b\leq c\rightarrowtriangle(a\rightarrowtriangle b)

\displaystyle{}\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{X}\models\forall xyz\forall YZ(TxYy\,\&\,TyZz\Rightarrow TxZz)

\displaystyle{}\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{X}\models\forall xy\forall Y(TxYy\Rightarrow(\exists z\in Y)\,Tx\{y\}z)

Summary and further work

We have shown that the techniques developed for monotonic operators in

Celani,, 2009

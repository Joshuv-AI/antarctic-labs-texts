Holographic superconductivity of a critical Fermi surface
Jörg Schmalian

Abstract
We derive holographic superconductivity from a Hamiltonian that describes pairing of two-dimensional electrons near a ferromagnetic quantum-critical point. At low energies the theory maps onto a four-dimensional gravity description with Lifshitz spacetime and dynamic scaling exponent z = 3 / 2 z=3/2 . The curved spacetime is due to powerlaw correlations of the critical normal state. The Lifshitz anisotropy is caused by phase-space constraints near the Fermi surface. The pairing instabilities obtained in Lifshitz space and from the Eliashberg formalism are found to be identical. We also formulate the holographic map for values of the dynamic scaling exponent 1 < z < ∞ 1<z<\infty . Our result provides an explicit realization of the holographic correspondence in two dimensions.

Appendix B
Analysis of the saddle point equations

B.1
Analysis in the normal state

Let us first analyze these equations in the normal state. We perform
the momentum integration as

\int_{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdots=\frac{1}{{\cal N}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdots=\rho_{F}\int\frac{d\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\int\frac{d\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2\pi}\cdots,

(29)

where
{\cal N}
is the number of lattice sites,
\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}}

is the energy, and
\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}
the direction of the
momentum
\boldsymbol{p}
.
\rho_{F}=\frac{1}{2\pi}m_{{\rm e}}a_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{F}

is the density of states multiplied by the Fermi energy
\varepsilon_{F}
,
where
a_{0}
is the lattice constant of the crystal and
m
e

the electron mass.
\rho_{F}
is a dimensionless quantity.

The repeating theme in the analysis is to assume and show in the end
that typical bosonic momenta are slower than fermionic ones which
implies that the fermionic self energy weakly depends on the momentum
perpendicular to the Fermi surface. Hence, we assume that
\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)\approx\Sigma_{\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\left(\epsilon\right)
,
independent on the deviation of
\boldsymbol{\left|p\right|}
from

p_{F}
, i.e. independent of
\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}}.
Then
it follows for the boson self energy:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi_{\boldsymbol{q}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)-\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2\pi}\frac{\left(\frac{\epsilon}{v_{F}q}\right)^{2}}{\left(\frac{\epsilon}{v_{F}q}\right)^{2}+\cos^{2}\left(\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}-\theta_{\boldsymbol{q}}\right)},

(30)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\Pi_{\boldsymbol{q}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)-\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\frac{\left|\epsilon\right|}{v_{F}q}.

If we introduce the renormalized correlation length

\xi=c/\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\Pi_{\boldsymbol{q}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)},

(31)

we obtain for the bosonic propagator

D_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{1}{c^{2}\left(\xi^{-2}+q^{2}\right)+\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\frac{\left|\epsilon\right|}{v_{F}q}}.

(32)

At the quantum-critical point we have
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{q}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)=\omega_{0}^{2}

such that
\xi\rightarrow\infty
and the boson becomes massless.

Now one can evaluate the fermionic self energy of the normal state:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu g^{2}\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int\frac{d\epsilon^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{D_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{i\epsilon^{\prime}-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}-\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}.

(33)

We focus on the critical point
\xi^{-1}=0
. Under the conditions
for the fermionic self energy outlined above, we can assume that
D_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right)

is dominated by the momentum dependence that is tangential to the
Fermi surface, expressed in terms of
\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}
and

\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
. Then we can perform the integral over

\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
using

\int\frac{d\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}}{i\epsilon^{\prime}-\Sigma_{\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}}=-i\pi{\rm sign\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)},

(34)

independent on the actual form of the self energy. This analysis also
illustrates that the precise form of the dispersion is not very important,
as long as it is sufficiently generic, i.e. without nested pieces
of the Fermi surface and the band width is large compared to the typical
excitation energy. The resulting angular integration is dominated
by small
\varphi=\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}-\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}

and yields for the self energy

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\lambda\frac{\varepsilon_{F}^{1/3}}{3}\int d\epsilon^{\prime}\frac{{\rm sign\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{1/3}}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\lambda{\rm sign}\left(\epsilon\right)\varepsilon_{F}^{1/3}\left|\epsilon\right|^{2/3},

with the dimensionless constant

\lambda=\frac{\mu}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}v_{F}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{F}^{3}c^{2}}\right)^{2/3}.

(36)

This result is indeed independent on momentum.

Eqn.(
30
) at
\xi^{-1}=0
and (
35
) are
the expressions for the bosonic and fermionic self energies given
in the main text.

B.2
Analysis in the superconducting state

Next we analyze the linearized gap equation for the anomalous self
energy

\hat{\Phi}\left(p\right)=-\mu g_{p}^{2}\int_{p^{\prime}}\hat{\sigma}^{z}\tilde{G}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\hat{G}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\hat{\sigma}^{z}D\left(p-p^{\prime}\right).

(37)

The Pauli principle implies
\Phi_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\left(p\right)=-\Phi_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}\left(-p\right).

We expand the anomalous self energy in spin space, using
p=\left(\boldsymbol{p},\epsilon\right)

for the momentum and energy parts of

:

(38)

For a system with inversion symmetry, the pairing function has well
defined parity. Hence, it is either odd in momentum
\Phi_{-\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)=-\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)

and even in energy
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(-\epsilon\right)=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)

or the other way around. In what follows we consider the even-frequency
option and find

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)=s_{\mu}\mu g_{p}^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\int\frac{d\epsilon^{\prime}}{2\pi}G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}^{\left(\mu\right)}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)D_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right),

(39)

where we used
\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr}\left(\sigma^{\mu}\sigma^{y}\sigma^{z}\sigma^{y}\sigma^{\nu}\sigma^{z}\right)=s_{\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}

with
s_{0}=s_{z}=-1
and
s_{x}=s_{y}=1
. Hence for even frequency
pairing and small momentum transfer we find triplet pairing
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(x\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)

and
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(y\right)}\left(\epsilon\right)
.
In a real crystalline lattice the degeneracy between these two states
may or may not be lifted. For our purposes, where we only analyze
the Gaussian part of the action, this effect is not particularly important
and we will assume that the degeneracy is lifted and consider only
one triplet pairing state which we call
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)
,
determined by the gap equation

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)=\mu g_{p}^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\int\frac{d\epsilon^{\prime}}{2\pi}G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)D_{\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right).

(40)

We again use that
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)
depends
only on the angle
\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}
and perform the integration
over
\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\int d\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)G_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\left(-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varepsilon_{F}^{2/3}\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{4/3}}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\lambda\varepsilon_{F}^{1/3}\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{2/3}}.

It follows with
\left(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\approx 2p_{F}^{2}\left(1-\cos\left(\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right)\right)

that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu g_{p}^{2}\rho_{F}}{2\varepsilon_{F}^{4/3}\lambda c^{2}p_{F}^{2}}\int\frac{d\epsilon^{\prime}}{\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{2/3}}

(42)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\Phi_{\theta^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\sqrt{2\left(1-\cos\left(\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right)\right)}}{\left(2-2\cos\left(\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right)\right)^{3/2}+\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}v_{F}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{F}^{3}c^{2}}\frac{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|}{\varepsilon_{F}}}.

To proceed we expand in harmonics

\Phi_{\theta}\left(\epsilon\right)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_{l}\left(\epsilon\right)e^{il\theta},

(43)

keeping in mind that only odd
l
are allowed as we are analyzing
odd-parity triplet pairing. This yields

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu g_{p}^{2}\rho_{F}}{2\varepsilon_{F}^{4/3}\lambda c^{2}p_{F}^{2}}\int d\epsilon^{\prime}\frac{d_{l}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{2/3}}\Phi_{l}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right),

(44)

where

d_{l}\left(\epsilon\right)=\int\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}\frac{\sqrt{2\left(1-\cos\varphi\right)}e^{il\varphi}}{\left(2-2\cos\varphi\right)^{3/2}+\frac{2g^{2}\rho_{F}v_{F}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{F}^{3}c^{2}}\frac{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|}{\varepsilon_{F}}}.

(45)

At small energies follows

d_{l}\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{F}^{3}c^{2}}{2g^{2}\rho_{F}v_{F}^{2}}\right)^{1/3}\frac{\varepsilon_{F}^{1/3}}{\left|\epsilon\right|^{1/3}}-\frac{\left|l\right|}{2}+{\cal O}\left(\kappa\frac{\left|\epsilon\right|}{\varepsilon_{F}}\right)^{1/3}.

(46)

The second term implies that the most attractive channel corresponds
to
l=\pm 1
, i.e.
p
-wave pairing. We pick this channel and determine
the frequency dependence of
\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)
from the
linearized gap equation

\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{1-\alpha}{3}\int d\epsilon^{\prime}\frac{\Phi\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{2/3}\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{1/3}}.

(47)

Following Ref.
Hauck2020
one makes a powerlaw ansatz
\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)\sim\left|\epsilon\right|^{\frac{1}{6}\pm i\beta}

and finds that superconductivity survives until
\alpha
reaches
a critical pair-breaking strength
\alpha_{*}\approx 0.879618
that
follows from

1=\tfrac{1}{3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{1-\alpha}{\left|1-x\right|^{1/3}\left|x\right|^{5/6}}.

(48)

Then the transition temperature vanishes as
T_{c}\sim\exp\left(-\frac{A}{\sqrt{\alpha_{*}-\alpha}}\right)
.
Right at
\alpha=\alpha^{*}
holds
\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)\propto\left|\epsilon\right|^{-\frac{1}{6}}\left(1+\frac{1}{6}\log\frac{\omega_{0}}{\left|\epsilon\right|}\right).

Appendix C
Gaussian pairing fluctuations

In this appendix we formulate the theory of Gaussian fluctuations
relative to the normal state. This lays the grounds for the derivation
of the holographic action. We keep the discussion rather general for
as long as it makes sense and specify the concrete interaction and
pairing state of our problem further below.

We expand the action Eq.(
5
) up to second order in pairing
terms, i.e.

\displaystyle S_{sc}/N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\left(\hat{F}^{\dagger}\otimes\hat{\Phi}+\hat{F}\otimes\hat{\Phi}^{\dagger}\right)

(49)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu g_{p}^{2}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int_{x,x^{\prime}}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{\gamma}_{s}\hat{F}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\hat{\gamma}_{s^{\prime}}^{T}\hat{F}^{\dagger}\left(x^{\prime},x\right)\right)D_{ss^{\prime}}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(\tilde{G}\otimes\hat{\Phi}^{\dagger}\otimes\hat{G}\otimes\hat{\Phi}\right).

After Fourier transformation to momentum and frequency coordinates,
where
k
refers to the variable conjugate to
(x+x^{\prime})/2
and
q

conjugate to
x-x^{\prime}
we obtain:

\displaystyle S_{sc}/N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{kq}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{F}^{\dagger}\left(k,q\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(k,q\right)+h.c.\right)

(50)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\mu g_{p}^{2}}{2}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\int_{kqq^{\prime}}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{\gamma_{s}}\hat{F}^{\dagger}\left(k,q\right)\hat{\gamma_{s^{\prime}}}^{T}\hat{F}\left(k,q^{\prime}\right)\right)D_{ss^{\prime}}\left(q-q^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{kq}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{G}\left(-\frac{k}{2}+q\right)\hat{\Phi}^{\dagger}\left(k,q\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(k,q\right)\hat{G}\left(-\frac{k}{2}-q\right)\right).

From the analysis of the gap equation we already know that the dependence
of the pairing function on the momentum
\boldsymbol{q}
and on the
energy
\epsilon
is rather different. Hence, at this point the usage
of combined space-time variables
q=\left(\boldsymbol{q},\epsilon\right)

or
k=\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega\right)
seizes to be efficient.
In what follows we expand the pairing functions with respect to some
complete set of functions
\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)}

that depend on
\boldsymbol{q}
and the spin structure

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}\left(k,q\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\sum_{L}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)},

\displaystyle\hat{F}\left(k,q\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\sum_{L}F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)}.

(51)

We insert this expansion and obtain

\displaystyle S_{{\rm sc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\left(\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)+\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\right)

(52)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu g_{p}^{2}\int F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)D_{LL^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}L^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}LL^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right),

where we introduced

D_{LL^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{q,}\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{\gamma}\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)\dagger}\hat{\gamma}^{T}\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}^{\left(L^{\prime}\right)}\right)D_{\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right),

(53)

as well as

\chi_{\boldsymbol{k},LL^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\int_{\boldsymbol{q}}{\rm tr}\left(\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)\dagger}\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L^{\prime}\right)}\right)G_{\frac{\boldsymbol{k}}{2}-\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\frac{\omega}{2}-\epsilon\right)G_{\frac{\boldsymbol{k}}{2}+\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\frac{\omega}{2}+\epsilon\right).

(54)

Now we are in a position to integrate out the
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}L}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

and obtain

\displaystyle S^{\left({\rm sc}\right)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\left(\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\right)_{LL^{\prime}}F_{\boldsymbol{k}L^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

(55)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu g_{p}^{2}\int F_{\boldsymbol{k}L}^{\dagger}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)D_{LL^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}L^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon^{\prime}\right).

This formulation of the problem is really only useful if we can find
a set of functions
\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\left(L\right)}

that simultaneously diagonalize the
\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}=0,LL^{\prime}}\left(\omega=0,\epsilon\right)

and
D_{LL^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right)
for all
\epsilon
. We can then
expand in this basis for small
\omega
and
\boldsymbol{k}
. To
proceed start from

\hat{\eta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\left(l,\alpha,n\right)}=i\hat{\sigma}^{y}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}\frac{e^{il\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}}{\sqrt{4\pi}}T_{n}\left(\frac{\left|p\right|-p_{F}}{p_{F}}\right).

(56)

Hence the index
L=\left(l,\alpha,n\right)
consists of the angular
momentum
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and the spin index of the Cooper pair
\alpha
,
with
\alpha=0
for a singlet and
\alpha\in\left\{x,y,z\right\}

for triplets. In addition, the
T_{n}\left(y\right)
is some complete
set of polynomials where
n
is the order of the polynomial. We use
the insight that we obtained from the solution of the linearized gap
equation. Here we found that only the leading polynomial
T_{0}\left(y\right)=1

in
y=\left(\left|p\right|-p_{F}\right)/p_{F}
contributes for momenta
near the Fermi surface. From now on we drop the index
n=0
. Now
we are in a position to evaluate the matrix elements. It follows

D_{\alpha l,\alpha^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right)=s_{\alpha}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}d_{l}\left(\epsilon\right),

(57)

with
d_{l}\left(\epsilon\right)
of Eq.(
46
).

We can generalize our approach and consider a self energy of the normal
state that behaves as

\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(\epsilon\right)=-i\lambda{\rm sign}\left(\epsilon\right)\varepsilon_{F}^{\gamma}\left|\epsilon\right|^{1-\gamma}

(58)

while the matrix of the pairing interaction is at leading order

D_{\alpha l,\alpha^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon\right)\sim\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\left|\epsilon\right|^{-\gamma}.

(59)

Then we have .

\displaystyle\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha l,\alpha^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{F}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}}}{\varepsilon_{F}}\frac{\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-i\theta_{\boldsymbol{p}}\left(l-l^{\prime}\right)}}{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{k}}+\Sigma\left(\frac{\omega}{2}+\epsilon\right)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{p}}+\Sigma\left(\frac{\omega}{2}-\epsilon\right)}.

(60)

For
l=l^{\prime}
and
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
follows

\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda\varepsilon_{F}^{1+\gamma}}\frac{\theta\left(\left|\epsilon\right|-\frac{\left|\omega\right|}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\left(\left|\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}\right|^{1-\gamma}+\left|\epsilon-\frac{\omega}{2}\right|^{1-\gamma}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{v_{F}k}{\lambda\varepsilon_{F}^{\gamma}}\right)^{2}}}.

(61)

Off diagonal elements in
l
and
l^{\prime}
only occur for finite momenta
and one can perform a small
k
expansion to include those. Expanding
for small
k
and
\omega
then yields the result given in Eq.(
11
).

Appendix D
The holographic map

In this appendix we give the details of the holographic map. To keep
the equations shorter we use, just like in the main text, a system
of units where energies are measured in units of
\varepsilon_{F}

and momenta in units of
p_{F}
.

The Gaussian theory of the pairing fluctuations theory takes the following
form

S_{{\rm sc}}=S_{{\rm sc},1}+S_{{\rm sc},2}

(62)

where

S_{{\rm sc},1}=\int_{\boldsymbol{k}\omega\epsilon}F_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

(63)

with

\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\left|\epsilon\right|^{1-\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{8}\left(\frac{\omega}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{\lambda^{2}\left|\epsilon\right|^{2-2\gamma}}\right),

(64)

as well as

S_{{\rm sc},2}=-\lambda_{p}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}\omega\epsilon\epsilon^{\prime}}\frac{F_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}},

(65)

with
\lambda_{p}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left(1-\gamma\right)\left(1-\alpha\right)
.
At the saddle point we obtain

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\lambda_{p}\int\frac{d\epsilon^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}}.

(66)

in terms of the anomalous self energy

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)=\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right).

(67)

Notice, the equation for
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

is nonlocal only with respect to the relative energy
\epsilon
.
For
\omega=0
and
\boldsymbol{k}=\boldsymbol{0}
this is the usual
linearized Eliashberg equation

\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{\lambda_{p}}{\lambda}\int d\epsilon^{\prime}\frac{\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{1-\gamma}}.

(68)

For
\gamma=1/3
this agrees with Eq.(
47
). At a superconducting
quantum-critical point, which is reached for a threshold strength
of the pairing interaction, one can make the power-law ansatz
\Phi\left(\epsilon\right)\sim\left|\epsilon\right|^{-\gamma/2}

which yields the condition

\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}=a_{\gamma}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{1}{\left|1-x\right|^{\gamma}\left|x\right|^{1-\gamma/2}}.

(69)

Below we will see that the same criterion also follows from the holographic
analysis at the Breitenlohner-Freedman bound.

D.1
On the sign of the
(\omega/\epsilon)^{2}
-term in
\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)

Before we perform the holographic map, we comment on the curious minus
sign in front of the
\frac{\gamma}{8}\left(\omega/\epsilon\right)^{2}

in
\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)
. At first
glance this looks like an instability towards large
\omega
configurations

F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)
. However, in a Gaussian
theory this depends on the appropriate source field or boundary conditions.
An external Josephson coupling

H_{J}=\int_{\boldsymbol{x},\tau}J_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x},\tau\right)\sum_{i}c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\left(\tau\right)c_{i\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\left(\tau\right)

(70)

couples to
\int d\epsilon F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right)
.
The bare susceptibility of relevance is

\check{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\int\frac{d\epsilon}{\pi}\chi_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\omega,\epsilon\right).

(71)

One easily sees that
\check{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\left(\omega\right)=\check{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)-a_{\omega}\left|\omega\right|^{\gamma}-a_{k}k^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}

is largest at
\omega=0
, where
a_{\omega}
and
\alpha_{k}
are
positive. Here
\check{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{0}}\left(0\right)=\frac{4\pi}{\gamma\lambda}

is finite.

The situation is somewhat similar to a Gaussian theory with action

S_{0}=\int_{\omega<\Lambda}d\omega\left(m^{2}+\omega^{2}\right)\phi_{\omega}\phi_{-\omega},

(72)

which is stable for
m^{2}>0
. Let us now introduce the new field

f_{\omega}=\phi_{\omega}\left(1+\omega^{2}/\Lambda^{2}\right)
.
At small momenta this theory becomes

S_{0}=\int_{\omega<\Lambda}d\omega\left(m^{2}-\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-1\right)\omega^{2}\right)f_{\omega}f_{-\omega},

(73)

which looks unstable for
m^{2}>\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{2}
. However,
large
\omega
fluctuations in
f_{\omega}
will be suppressed for
the original field
\phi_{\omega}
, which is simply the more reasonable
variable. The situation is very similar to our case where the scale

\Lambda
is played by
\epsilon
, see the cut off coefficient
\theta\left(\left|\epsilon\right|-\frac{\left|\omega\right|}{2}\right)

in Eq.(
61
).

D.2
Holographic map for
S_{{\rm sc}1}

We will first perform the map for the field

\psi\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega,\zeta\right)=c_{0}\zeta^{\frac{\left(d-1\right)\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}}F_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\omega,c_{1}/\zeta\right).

(74)

The additional denominator in Eq.(
12
) will be included
at the end. Inserting Eq.(
74
) into the action
S_{{\rm sc},1}

yields

S_{{\rm sc},1}=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}\omega\zeta}\zeta^{-\left(d-1\right)\left(1-\gamma\right)-2}\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-1}\left(\omega,c_{1}/\zeta\right)\left|\psi\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega,\zeta\right)\right|^{2}.

(75)

If we now use the explicit expression for
\chi^{-1}
we get

\displaystyle S_{{\rm sc},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda c_{1}^{2-\gamma}}{2\pi c_{0}^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}\omega\zeta}\zeta^{-d\left(1-\gamma\right)}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}-\frac{\gamma}{8}\left(\frac{\omega}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{k}{\lambda c_{1}^{1-\gamma}\zeta^{\gamma}}\right)^{2}\right)\left|\psi\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega,\zeta\right)\right|^{2}.

(76)

We fix the constant
c_{1}
such that the coefficient in front of

\omega^{2}
is
-\left(1-\gamma\right)^{d}
which yield

c_{1}^{\gamma}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi c_{0}^{2}}\frac{\gamma\left(1-\gamma\right)^{-d}}{8}.

(77)

Then we get

S_{{\rm sc},1}=\left(1-\gamma\right)^{d}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}\omega\zeta}\zeta^{-d\left(1-\gamma\right)}\left(\frac{m_{1}^{2}\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}-\omega^{2}+A\frac{k^{2}}{\zeta^{2\gamma}}\right)\left|\psi\left(\boldsymbol{k},\omega,\zeta\right)\right|^{2},

(78)

with
m_{1}^{2}=\frac{8c_{1}^{2}}{\gamma\left(1-\gamma\right)^{2}}

and
A=\frac{8c_{1}^{2\gamma}}{\gamma\lambda^{2}}.

D.3
Holographic map for
S_{{\rm sc}2}

Next we analyze
S_{{\rm sc},2}
. Let us drop all the irrelevant
coordinates and consider the action

{\cal S}_{F}=-\int\frac{d\epsilon d\epsilon}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\frac{F\left(\epsilon\right)F\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}}.

(79)

We perform a Mellin transform

F\left(\epsilon\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dwf_{w}\left|\epsilon\right|^{-iw-1+\gamma/2},

(80)

with inverse

\displaystyle f_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\epsilon}{4\pi}\left|\epsilon\right|^{iw-\gamma/2}F\left(\epsilon\right).

(81)

Then we obtain

{\cal S}_{F}=-\int\frac{dwdw^{\prime}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}f_{w}^{*}f_{w^{\prime}}R_{w,w^{\prime}}

(82)

with

R_{w,w^{\prime}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon d\epsilon^{\prime}\frac{\left|\epsilon\right|^{-iw-1+\gamma/2}\left|\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{iw^{\prime}-1+\gamma/2}}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}}.

(83)

We first perform the integration over
\epsilon^{\prime}
which yields

R_{w,w^{\prime}}=r_{w^{\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\epsilon}{\left|\epsilon\right|}\left|\epsilon\right|^{-i\left(w-w^{\prime}\right)},

(84)

with

\displaystyle r_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\left|x\right|^{iw}}{\left|1-x\right|^{\gamma}\left|x\right|^{1-\gamma/2}}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\cos\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\gamma\right)\left|\Gamma\left(1+iw-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{\left(2z-i\gamma\right)\pi}{4}\right)\right|^{2}}.

Performing the integration over
\epsilon
is straightforward and
yields

R_{w,w^{\prime}}=4\pi r_{w}\delta\left(w-w^{\prime}\right).

(86)

Hence, the Mellin transform diagonalizes the action
{\cal S}
. At
small
w
holds

r_{w}=a_{\gamma}-b_{\gamma}w^{2},

(87)

where the two positive coefficients are

\displaystyle a_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\Gamma\left(\gamma\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\right)\left(\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle b_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}\left(\pi^{2}-4\psi^{\left(1\right)}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{4}\right)\right)}{8\Gamma\left(\gamma\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\sin^{4}\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{4}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\right)}.

(88)

Thus, we obtain

{\cal S}_{F}=\int\frac{dw}{\pi}\left(-a+bw^{2}\right)\left|f_{w}\right|^{2}.

(89)

Alternatively, we will analyze a theory of the form

{\cal S}_{\psi}=\int d\zeta\zeta^{-d\left(1-\gamma\right)}\left|\partial_{\zeta}\psi\right|^{2},

(90)

which anticipates in the holographic action. To diagonalize the term
we perform a corresponding Mellin transform

\displaystyle\psi\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\varphi_{s}\zeta^{\frac{1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}-is},

(91)

with inverse

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{2\pi}\psi\left(\zeta\right)\zeta^{is-\frac{3+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}}.

(92)

Then holds

\partial_{\zeta}\psi=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\varphi_{s}\left(\frac{1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}-is\right)\zeta^{\frac{d\left(1-\gamma\right)-1}{2}-is},

(93)

and we get

{\cal S}_{\psi}=\int dsds^{\prime}\varphi_{s}^{*}\varphi_{s}\left(\frac{1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}+is\right)\left(\frac{1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}-is^{\prime}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\zeta^{i\left(s-s^{\prime}\right)}.

(94)

If we now use that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\zeta^{i\left(s-s^{\prime}\right)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\log\zeta e^{i\left(s-s^{\prime}\right)\log\zeta}=2\pi\delta\left(s-s^{\prime}\right),

(95)

we obtain

{\cal S}_{\psi}=2\pi\int ds\left(\frac{\left(1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)\right)^{2}}{4}+s^{2}\right)\left|\varphi_{s}\right|^{2}.

(96)

Hence, the two problems described by
{\cal S}_{F}
and
{\cal S}_{\psi}

are both diagonalized by a Mellin transform and then take a very similar
form. Notice, the expansion of
r_{w}
with respect to
w
is not
a gradient expansion in
\partial_{\zeta}\psi
. Instead, it is an
expansion for small
\left(\partial_{\zeta}-\frac{1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2\zeta}\right)\psi

which is the appropriate expansion for curved space. Here the coefficient

\frac{1+d\left(1+\gamma\right)}{2}
also determines the Breitenlohner-Freedman
bound for the mass.

The similarity in the action suggests to make the identification

\varphi_{s}=c_{0}f_{-s}.

(97)

The opposite sign is due to the fact that large
\zeta
and small

\epsilon
refer to low energies. We can perform the inverse Mellin
transform of this equation, which allows us to identity

\psi\left(\zeta\right)=c_{0}\zeta^{\frac{(d-1)\left(1-\gamma\right)}{2}}F\left(\frac{1}{\zeta}\right).

(98)

Hence, it follows

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}\int\frac{ds}{\pi}\left(-a_{\gamma}+b_{\gamma}s^{2}\right)\left|\varphi_{s}\right|^{2}

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{\gamma}}{2\pi^{2}c_{0}^{2}}{\cal S}_{\psi}-\frac{b_{\gamma}}{\pi c_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{a_{\gamma}}{b_{\gamma}}+\frac{\left(1+d\left(1-\gamma\right)\right)^{2}}{4}\right)\int ds\left|\varphi_{s}\right|^{2}.

To identify the last term, we use the inverse Mellin transform

\displaystyle\int ds\left|\varphi_{s}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\zeta\zeta^{-2-d\left(1-\gamma\right)}\left|\psi\right|^{2}.

(100)

Thus, we showed that

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\epsilon d\epsilon}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\frac{F\left(\epsilon\right)F\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)}{\left|\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma}}

Self duality in unconventional conformal supersymmetry
Pedro D. Alvarez, Cristóbal Corral, Jorge Zanelli

Abstract
In this work, we study (anti-)self duality conditions in unconventional conformal supersymmetry. We focus on a theory constructed in a Townsend-MacDowell-Mansouri form for an S ​ U ​ ( 2 , 2 | N ) SU(2,2|N) gauge connection with matter fields in the adjoint representation. We find bosonic solutions that correspond to analytic gravitational instantons with nontrivial torsion. These configurations can be regarded as the torsional generalization of the Taub-NUT/Bolt-AdS and Eguchi-Hanson metric and they are (anti-)self-dual with respect to a generalized dual operator. We explore their global properties and show that they saturate a BPS bound.

Introduction

In recent years, a family of supersymmetric theories in which spin-1/2 fermions are combined with the bosonic non-Abelian gauge fields as part of a connection for a graded Lie algebra, has been explored
Alvarez et al. 2012
;
Alvarez et al. 2014a
;
Alvarez et al. 2022

unconventional

Such models have a potential for a fermionic sector representing quarks and leptons and, in spite of being based on a local supersymmetric algebra, they contain no spin-3/2 gravitini.

In four dimensions, the resulting U-SUSY models can be seen as generalizations of Yang-Mills theory, where the dual operation (denoted by
\circledast

III
(anti-)Self-dual configurations

Since we are interested in (anti-)self-dual configurations, we focus on the Euclidean section of the theory by performing the analytic continuation of the Lorentzian time into the Euclidean one. This leads to an Euclidean superconformal group, whose properties have been discussed in Refs.

In what follows, we present a family of gravitational instantons with nontrivial torsion in unconventional conformal supersymmetric TMM theory; some of them being (anti-)self-dual in the sense of Eq. (
24
). They represent concrete solutions that exhibit the conditions discussed here.

IV
Gravitational instantons

In order to find (anti-)self-dual solutions to the field equations (
19a
) and (
19b
), we construct an ansatz for the vierbein, the “Lorentz”
SO(4)
connection and
\Lambda(x)
, based on the
U(1)
-fibration over Kähler manifolds. To this end, we consider the left-invariant Maurer-Cartan forms of
SU(2)
,
\sigma_{i}
with
i=1,2,3
; they are

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos\tau\,\text{d}\vartheta+\sin\tau\sin\vartheta\,\text{d}\varphi\,,

(27a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=-\sin\tau\,\text{d}\vartheta+\cos\tau\sin\vartheta\,\text{d}\varphi\,,

(27b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}

\displaystyle=\text{d}\tau+\cos\vartheta\,\text{d}\varphi\,.

(27c)

These
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued
1
-forms satisfy the condition
\text{d}\sigma_{i}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma^{j}\wedge\sigma^{k}
. Using them as building blocks, we consider a metric ansatz whose radial slices are conformally related to Berger’s sphere, that is,

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{d}r^{2}}{f(r)}+f(r)J^{2}(r)\sigma_{3}^{2}+h(r)\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}\right)\,,

(28)

where
\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}=\text{d}\vartheta^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta\text{d}\varphi^{2}
is the line element of the unit
2
-sphere. The metric (
28
) is locally invariant under the action of the isometry group
SU(2)\times U(1)
and possesses the relevant properties for analyzing different (anti-)self-dual configurations. Additionally, using the relation
g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{ab}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}
in Euclidean signature, we choose a vierbein basis as

\displaystyle e^{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{h(r)}\,\sigma_{1}\,,

\displaystyle e^{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{h(r)}\,\sigma_{2}\,,

\displaystyle e^{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{f(r)}\,J(r)\,\sigma_{3}\,,

\displaystyle e^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{d}r}{\sqrt{f(r)}}\,.

(29)

Analogously, demanding the Lorentz connection to remain invariant under the action of the isometry group of the metric, the connection satisfies

\displaystyle\omega^{12}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}(r)\,\sigma_{3}+\omega_{2}(r)\,e^{4}+\mathring{\omega}^{12}\,,

\displaystyle\omega^{13}

\displaystyle=\omega_{3}(r)\,\sigma_{1}-\omega_{4}(r)\,\sigma_{2}\,,

(30a)

\displaystyle\omega^{14}

\displaystyle=\omega_{5}(r)\,\sigma_{1}-\omega_{6}(r)\,\sigma_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\omega^{23}

\displaystyle=\omega_{4}(r)\,\sigma_{1}+\omega_{3}(r)\,\sigma_{2}\,,

(30b)

\displaystyle\omega^{24}

\displaystyle=\omega_{6}(r)\,\sigma_{1}+\omega_{5}(r)\,\sigma_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\omega^{34}

\displaystyle=\omega_{7}(r)\,\sigma_{3}+\omega_{8}(r)\,e^{4}\,,

(30c)

where
\omega_{i}(r)
, with
i=1,...,8
, are arbitrary functions of the radial coordinate only, to be determined from the field equations, and
\mathring{\omega}^{ab}
is the torsion-free Levi-Civita connection defined by
\text{d}e^{a}+\mathring{\omega}^{a}_{\ b}\wedge e^{b}=0
. Equivalently, the most general field
\Lambda(x)
compatible with the action of
SU(2)\times U(1)
isometry group implies that
\Lambda=\Lambda(r)
. Then, the field equations for the connection (
19a
) are solved by

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}=\omega_{6}=\omega_{8}=0\,,

\displaystyle\omega_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{J}{2}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle\omega_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(h\Lambda\right)^{\prime}}{2\Lambda}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\,,

\displaystyle\omega_{7}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(f\Lambda J^{2}\right)^{\prime}}{2\Lambda J}\,,

(31b)

where
f=f(r)
,
h=h(r)
,
J=J(r)
,
\Lambda=\Lambda(r)
, and prime denotes differentiation with respect to the radial coordinate
r
.

The form of these ansätze, together with the solution of the equation for the connection, allow the construction of two interesting families of gravitational instantons of the Taub-NUT and Eguchi-Hanson types possessing nontrivial torsion.

IV.1
Taub-NUT/Bolt-AdS-like solutions

The Taub-NUT metric, first found as a one-parameter extension of the Schwarzschild solution in vacuum general relativity
Taub 1951
;
Newman et al. 1963
, is usually interpreted as a gravitational dyon in the sense that the new parameter –the NUT charge– introduces a gravitomagnetic interaction between the background geometry and test particles following geodesics
Lynden-Bell and Nouri-Zonoz 1998
. The solution presents a topological defect –the Misner string
Misner 1963
–, which is the gravitational analog of the Dirac string in the magnetic monopole. The string can be rendered unobservable by performing an improper coordinate transformation that, in the Lorentzian signature, introduces closed time-like curves
Misner 1963
. Thus, the Taub-NUT metric is usually studied in the Euclidean continuation, which bears a close resemblance to instantons in Yang-Mills theory
Hawking 1977
;
Eguchi et al. 1978
;
Gibbons and Hawking 1979
;
Eguchi et al. 1980
. However, recent developments have led to consider the Lorentzian Taub-NUT metric again since it was shown that the Misner string is transparent to geodesic observers
Clément et al. 2015
, allowing an interpretation of the NUT charge as a genuine thermodynamic variable
Hennigar et al. 2019
;
Bordo et al. 2019a
;
Bordo et al. 2019b
;
Ballon Bordo et al. 2019
;
Durka 2022
;
Wu and Wu 2019
;
Chen and Jiang 2019
;
Ballon Bordo et al. 2020
.

The Taub-NUT solution with nontrivial torsion in the unconventional conformal supersymmetric model studied here is obtained by replacing the solutions (
31
) into (
19b
) alongside the radial-coordinate choice
h(r)=r^{2}-n^{2}
, with
n
being the NUT charge. Thus,
h(r)
represents the area coordinate with the origin at
r=n
. The hypersurfaces of constant
r
are conformally the Berger’s sphere. In general,
n\in\mathbb{R}
. However, in this work, we shall focus on the case
n\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

The remaining functions that solve the field equations are found to be
J(r)=2n
and

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{a\left[r\,b^{2}+3r^{2}(r+b)+bn^{2}\right]}{b\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}-\frac{r(r+b)^{2}}{b\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}+\frac{(r+b)^{3}\left(r\,b+n^{2}\right)}{b\ell^{2}\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}\,,

(32a)

\displaystyle\Lambda(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{(r+b)^{2}}\,,

(32b)

where
a
,
b
and
\ell
are integration constants. This is a four-parameter family of solutions with nontrivial torsion sourced by
\Lambda(x)
[see Eq. (
20
)]. Explicitly, the components of torsion associated to the solution in Eq. (
32
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle T^{a}=-\frac{\sqrt{f(r)}}{r+b}\;e^{a}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(33)

which vanishes at the zeroes of the metric function
f(r)
; the nuts and bolts cases are discussed below. On the other hand, the components of the Lorentz curvature
2
-form for this solution are

\displaystyle R^{12}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}-2\mathscr{B}(r)\,e^{3}\wedge e^{4}\,,

\displaystyle R^{13}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{3}-\mathscr{B}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(34a)

\displaystyle R^{14}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{4}+\mathscr{B}(r)e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\,,

\displaystyle R^{23}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{3}+\mathscr{B}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(34b)

\displaystyle R^{24}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{4}-\mathscr{B}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{3}\,,

\displaystyle R^{34}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}(r)\,e^{3}\wedge e^{4}-2\mathscr{B}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\,,

(34c)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}-n^{2}}\left[1-\frac{f\left(b^{2}r^{2}+3n^{2}r^{2}+8bn^{2}r+3b^{2}n^{2}+n^{4}\right)}{\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)(r+b)^{2}}\right]\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}(r)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{fn}{2\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}\right]^{\prime}\,,

(35b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(r)

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{f\left(br+n^{2}\right)}{2\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)(r+b)}\right]^{\prime}-\frac{f\left(b^{2}-n^{2}\right)}{2\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)(r+b)^{2}}\,,

(35c)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{r+b}-\frac{f}{(r+b)^{2}}\,.

(35d)

Additionally, the asymptotic behavior of the solution as
r\to\infty
is given by

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{r}{b\ell^{2}}\left(3a\ell^{2}-\ell^{2}+n^{2}+3b^{2}\right)\ +\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\left(3a\ell^{2}-2\ell^{2}+4n^{2}+3b^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r\,b\ell^{2}}\left[\ell^{2}\left(3an^{2}+(a-1)b^{2}-n^{2}\right)+b^{4}+n^{4}+6b^{2}n^{2}\right]+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle\Lambda(r)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{a}{r^{2}}-\frac{2ab}{r^{3}}+\frac{3ab^{2}}{r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})\,.

(36b)

The metric function exhibits the typical linear behavior in the radial coordinate as
r\to\infty
that appears in solutions to conformal gravity
Riegert 1984
;
Lu et al. 2012
;
Liu et al. 2013
;
Corral et al. 2021
, producing a space with weakened local AdS asymptotics. However, as shown by Maldacena in Ref.
Maldacena 2011
, one can impose Newmann boundary conditions on the Fefferman-Graham expansion to select only the Einstein modes of the theory, reducing four-dimensional conformal gravity to Einstein-AdS gravity (see also Refs.
Anastasiou and Olea 2016
;
Anastasiou et al. 2021
). In the case of the torsional Taub-NUT instanton presented here, the Riemannian curvature, i.e. the one constructed from the Levi-Civita connection
\mathring{\omega}^{a}_{\ b}
, possesses an asymptotically locally AdS behavior with curvature radius
\ell^{-2}
. The Lorentz curvature
2
-form is asymptotically locally flat rather than asymptotically locally AdS. This is due to the fact that the leading order in the asymptotic expansion of the torsion, i.e.,

\displaystyle T^{a}=-\left[\frac{1}{\ell}+\frac{3a\ell^{2}+b^{2}-\ell^{2}+n^{2}}{2b\ell\,r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})\right]e^{a}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(37)

is asymptotically constant. This constant in the contorsion contribution to the Lorentz curvature cancels the constant term from the Riemann curvature. Then, the fall-off of the Lorentz curvature
2
-form in Eq. (
34
) is encoded in the asymptotic expansion of its components as
r\to\infty
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{2}-b^{2}-3n^{2}}{\ell^{2}\,r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,

(38a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{n\left(\ell^{2}-n^{2}-3a\ell^{2}-3b^{2}\right)}{2b\ell^{2}r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,

(38b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{3a\ell^{2}+b^{2}-\ell^{2}+3n^{2}}{2\ell r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,

(38c)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{2}-b^{2}-3n^{2}}{\ell^{2}r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,.

(38d)

These configurations are therefore asymptotically locally Lorentz-flat solutions. Similar locally Lorentz-flat configurations are solutions of three-dimensional Einstein-AdS gravity
Alvarez et al. 2014b
, which can also be formulated as a Chern-Simons theory for the AdS group (for a review see
Zanelli 2005
;
Hassaine and Zanelli 2016
). On the other hand, asymptotically locally Lorentz-flat black holes have been reported in Ref.
Alvarez et al. 2023b
in four-dimensional unconventional conformal supersymmetric TMM theory.

There are two distinct topologies that can be obtained from the solution (
32
). They are characterized by the dimensionality of the set of fixed points of the Killing vector
\xi=\partial_{\tau}
. In particular, they correspond to nuts or bolts if either of the following conditions are met

Nut:

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle=0\,

and

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{f^{\prime}(r)}\bigg|_{r=n}\,,

(39)

Bolt:

\displaystyle f(r_{h})

\displaystyle=0\,

and

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{f^{\prime}(r)}\bigg|_{r=r_{h}}\,,

(40)

where
\beta_{\tau}
is the period of the Euclidean time, i.e.
\tau\sim\tau+\beta_{\tau}
and
r_{h}>n
is the bolt radius. The NUT condition (
39
) fixes one integration constant as

\displaystyle a=\frac{(b+n)[\ell^{2}-(b+n)^{2}]}{\ell^{2}(b+3n)}\equiv a_{\rm nut}\,.

(41)

Then, the metric function
f(r)
and the scalar
\Lambda(r)
become

\displaystyle f_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(r-n)(2r+b+n)}{(b+3n)(r+n)}+\frac{(r-n)^{2}\left[r\,b+3n(r+b)+n^{2}\right]}{\ell^{2}(b+3n)(r+n)}\,,

(42a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b+n)\left[\ell^{2}-(b+n)^{2}\right]}{\ell^{2}(b+3n)(r+b)^{2}}\,.

(42b)

Remarkably, this solution is locally self-dual in the sense of (
24
) and possesses nontrivial torsion sourced by
\Lambda(x)
. The absence of conical singularities at
r=n
implies that the Euclidean-time coordinate has to be identified as
0\leq\tau\leq 8\pi n
, rendering the topology near the origin that of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Depending on the region in the parameter space, the metric function
f_{n}(r)
could have a single root at
r=n
and, in other cases, a second one at
r=r_{+}>n
. This produces a compact self-dual Taub-NUT metric. The different behavior of the solution depending on the region in the parameter space are given in Figure
1
.

f(\rho)=0
, with
\rho\equiv r/n
. The black line corresponds to solutions with only one root and the white region corresponds to solutions with
f(r)>0
for
r>n
. The orange curve is a generic representative of the gray region (
\ell^{2}=n^{2}
,

), and the blue curve is a generic representative of the white region (

The Taub-bolt case, on the other hand, leads to an instanton with a horizon located at the largest root of the polynomial
f(r_{h})=0
. Indeed, the condition (
40
) implies that the following relation between the parameters must hold

\displaystyle a=\frac{\left[r_{h}(\ell^{2}-n^{2}-b^{2})-b\left(r_{h}^{2}+n^{2}\right)\right](r_{h}+b)^{2}}{\ell^{2}\left[n^{2}b+b^{2}r_{h}+3r_{h}^{2}(b+r_{h})\right]}\equiv a_{\rm bolt}\,.

(43)

This condition, in turn, implies that metric function
f(r)
and the scalar
\Lambda(r)
are

\displaystyle f_{b}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r(r+b)^{2}}{b\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}+\frac{(r+b)^{3}\left(r\,b+n^{2}\right)}{b\ell^{2}\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{h}+b)^{2}\left[r\,b^{2}+b\left(3r^{2}+n^{2}\right)+3r^{3}\right]\left[r_{h}b^{2}+b\left(r_{h}^{2}+n^{2}\right)+r_{h}\left(n^{2}-l^{2}\right)\right]}{b\ell^{2}\left(r^{2}-n^{2}\right)\left[n^{2}b+b^{2}r_{h}+3r_{h}^{2}(b+r_{h})\right]}\,,

(44a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{b}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left[r_{h}(\ell^{2}-n^{2}-b^{2})-b\left(r_{h}^{2}+n^{2}\right)\right](r_{h}+b)^{2}}{\ell^{2}(r+b)^{2}\left[n^{2}b+b^{2}r_{h}+3r_{h}^{2}(b+r_{h})\right]}\,,

(44b)

respectively. One can check that the condition
f_{b}(r_{h})=0
is satisfied. The absence of conical singularities at the bolt implies that
0\leq\tau\leq\beta_{\tau}
, where the period in Euclidean time is

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}=\frac{4\pi\ell^{2}\left[n^{2}b+b^{2}r_{h}+3r_{h}^{2}(r_{h}+b)\right]}{(r_{h}+b)\left[3r_{h}^{2}(r_{h}+b)+3r_{h}\left(\ell^{2}-n^{2}\right)+b\left(\ell^{2}-3n^{2}\right)\right]}\,.

(45)

The topology of the Taub-bolt instanton is different from that of Taub-NUT. The former connects a two-dimensional set of fixed points of the Killing vector
\partial_{\tau}
with a locally Lorentz-flat asymptotic region
r\to\infty
rather than a zero-dimensional set as in the case of NUT. The whole space has the topology of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
with a point removed and sent to infinity
Page 1978
. Even though this space is not locally self-dual as in the Taub-NUT case, one can check that it is asymptotically locally self-dual in the sense of Eq. (
24
).

IV.2
Eguchi-Hanson-like solutions

There is another anti-self-dual gravitational instanton in the space of solutions of this theory. In the context of Einstein gravity, this solution was first found by Eguchi and Hanson in
Eguchi and Hanson 1979
;
Eguchi and Hanson 1978
. For vanishing cosmological constant, it represents a Ricci-flat asymptotically locally Euclidean space with
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topology; the solution with nonvanishing cosmological term was studied in Ref.
Pedersen 1985
. The hypersurfaces of constant radial coordinate have the topology of
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{RP}^{3}
and its isometry group is
U(1)\times SU(2)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\simeq U(2)
. The Eguchi-Hanson (EH) metric was shown to be relevant in the resolution of orbifold singularities
Gibbons and Hawking 1978
;
Gibbons and Hawking 1979
;
Nitta and Uzawa 2021
and it has been used to construct higher-dimensional solutions with nontrivial topology in Einstein gravity and beyond
Clarkson and Mann 2006a
;
Clarkson and Mann 2006b
;
Ishihara et al. 2006
;
Chng et al. 2006
;
Wong and Mann 2012a
;
Wong and Mann 2012b
;
Hendi et al. 2012
;
Durgut et al. 2023
.

The generalized EH solution in the unconventional conformal supersymmetric theory studied here is found by choosing
h(r)=r^{2}/4
in Eq. (
28
). Then, we find that the field equations are solved by
J^{2}(r)=h(r)
and

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{a^{4}}{r^{4}}-\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{4pa^{4}\,r^{2}}{q^{2}}+pr^{4}\,,

(46a)

\displaystyle\Lambda(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\left(16pa^{12}-4a^{4}q^{4}-q^{8}\right)}{q^{2}\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}\,r^{2}\right)^{2}}\,.\,

(46b)

where
a
,
q
, and
p
are integration constants. This is a three-parameter family of solutions that can be regarded as a torsional extension of the metric found in Ref.
Corral et al. 2021
, in which the nontrivial components of the torsion for this solution are

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2q^{2}r\,\sqrt{f}}{2a^{4}+q^{2}r^{2}}\,e^{a}\wedge e^{4}\,.

(47)

Note that the torsion vanishes as
q\to 0
with
a\neq 0
. In this sense,
q
can be regarded as a torsional hair whose backreaction resembles that of the Maxwell field, as it happens in Poincaré gauge theories
Cembranos and Valcarcel 2017
;
Cembranos and Gigante Valcarcel 2018
;
Obukhov 2020
. Now the components of the Lorentz curvature
2
-form are

\displaystyle R^{12}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{W}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}-2\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{3}\wedge e^{4}\,,

\displaystyle R^{13}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{3}-\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(48a)

\displaystyle R^{14}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{4}+\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{3}\,,

\displaystyle R^{23}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{3}+\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(48b)

\displaystyle R^{24}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Y}(r)\,e^{2}\wedge e^{4}-\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{3}\,,

\displaystyle R^{34}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{Z}(r)\,e^{3}\wedge e^{4}-2\mathscr{X}(r)\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\,,

(48c)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{8fa^{4}q^{2}}{\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r^{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{4\left(f-1\right)}{r^{2}}\,,

(49a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r}\,,

(49b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{16fa^{4}q^{2}r-f^{\prime}\left(4a^{8}-q^{4}r^{4}\right)}{2r\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,

(49c)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left[f^{\prime}r\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r^{2}\right)^{2}\right]^{\prime}+2f^{\prime}\left(2a^{4}-q^{2}r^{2}\right)^{2}-8f^{\prime}q^{4}r^{4}-16fa^{4}q^{2}r}{2r\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r^{2}\right)^{2}}\,.

(49d)

If
p\neq 0
, this solution has a curvature singularity for
r\to\infty
, while its action and topological invariants diverge. This can be seen from the asymptotic expansion of the torsion
2
-form for large

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle=-\left[2\sqrt{p}\;r+\frac{q^{4}-4a^{8}p}{q^{4}\sqrt{p}\;r^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})\right]e^{a}\wedge e^{4}\,.

(50)

On the other hand, the fall-off of the Lorentz curvature
2
-form in this non-coordinate basis as
r\to\infty
is characterized by

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}(r)

\displaystyle=-4pr^{2}-\frac{8pa^{4}}{q^{2}}+\frac{4\left(2a^{4}q^{4}+q^{8}-8a^{12}p\right)}{q^{6}r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-6})\,,

(51a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}(r)

\displaystyle=2pr^{2}+\frac{4pa^{4}}{q^{2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-6})\,,

(51b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=2pr^{2}+\frac{4pa^{4}}{q^{2}}+\frac{8a^{4}q^{4}+q^{8}-32a^{12}p}{q^{6}r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-6})\,,

(51c)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}(r)

\displaystyle=-4pr^{2}-\frac{8pa^{4}}{q^{2}}+\frac{4\left(2a^{4}q^{4}+q^{8}-8a^{12}p\right)}{q^{6}r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-6})\,.

(51d)

Thus, if
p\neq 0
, this solution cannot be regarded as a gravitational instanton, whereas if
p=0
the solution becomes

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{a^{4}}{r^{4}}-\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}\,,

(52a)

\displaystyle\Lambda(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q^{2}\left(4a^{4}+q^{4}\right)}{\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}\,r^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,\,

(52b)

and it is anti-self dual in the sense of Eq. (
24
). This solution has been studied in the context of Einstein gravity in Refs.
Xiao 2004
;
Chen and Zhang 2021
;
Yang and Zhang 2023
. Indeed, the asymptotic behavior of the torsion in this case is given by

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{2}{r}-\frac{q^{4}+4a^{4}}{q^{2}r^{3}}-\frac{\left(q^{4}+4a^{4}\right)\left(q^{4}-8a^{4}\right)}{4q^{4}r^{5}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-7})\right]e^{a}\wedge e^{4}\,,

(53)

while the asymptotic expansion for the Lorentz curvature
2
-form can be read explicitly from Eq. (
51
) by taking
p=0
. The solution of Eq. (
52
) has a bolt at
r=r_{h}
defined by the largest real positive root of the polynomial
f(r_{h})=0
, that is,

\displaystyle r_{h}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{2}+\sqrt{q^{4}+4a^{2}}\right)\,.

(54)

This bolt exists for all
a,q\in\mathbb{R}
as the discriminant is always positive definite. The absence of conical singularities at the bolt, in turn, demands that the Euclidean-time coordinate has to be identified according to
0\leq\tau\leq\beta_{\tau}
, where

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}=\frac{8\pi}{r_{h}\,f^{\prime}|_{r=r_{h}}}=\frac{4\pi r_{h}^{4}}{2a^{4}+q^{2}r_{h}^{2}}\,.

(55)

In the vanishing torsion limit, i.e.
q\to 0
, the bolt is located at
r_{h}=a
and the period of the Euclidean time is
2\pi
, giving a topology of
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
as in the original Eguchi-Hanson metric
Eguchi and Hanson 1979
;
Eguchi and Hanson 1978
.

Additionally, if
p\to 0
and
q\to 0
while keeping
p/q^{2}\equiv-\Lambda_{0}/(24a^{4})
fixed, the solution presented in Eq. (
46
) is continuously connected to the Eguchi-Hanson metric of general relativity with cosmological constant found in Ref.
Pedersen 1985
, that is,

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{a^{4}}{r^{4}}-\frac{\Lambda_{0}\,r^{2}}{6}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;\Lambda(r)=\Lambda_{0}\,,

(56)

where
\Lambda_{0}
is an integration constant. Indeed, one can check using Eq. (
20
) that the torsion vanishes in this case. If
\Lambda_{0}\neq 0
, the hypersurfaces of constant radius have the topology of
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
with
k
an integer if
0\leq\tau\leq 4\pi/k
and

\displaystyle r_{h}=\sqrt{-\frac{2(k-2)}{\Lambda_{0}}}\,.

(57)

If
\Lambda_{0}>0
, the existence of a bolt demands that
k<2
. In such a case, the radial coordinate is bounded since it appears a cosmological horizon. On the contrary, if
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then
k>2
, and the radial coordinate is unbounded from above. The case
\Lambda_{0}=0
needs to be treated separately. In that case, the metric (
56
) becomes the standard EH metric of Ref.
Eguchi and Hanson 1979
;
Eguchi and Hanson 1978
with
r_{h}=a
and
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topology.

V
Global properties

In this section, we obtain the global properties of the solutions such as their Euclidean on-shell actions and topological invariants. The first is an important quantity that allows us to obtain the partition function of the system to first order in the saddle-point approximation through the relation
\ln\mathcal{Z}\approx-I_{E}
. Here, we only consider (anti-)self-dual instantons in the sense of Eq. (
24
) for the sake of simplicity, as the value of their Euclidean on-shell action is proportional to the Chern-Pontryagin index as we showed in Eq. (
26
). In the case of (anti-)self-dual Taub-NUT/AdS solution in Eq. (
42
), its action is

\displaystyle I_{\rm on-shell}=4\pi^{2}P_{1}[\mathcal{M}]=\frac{8\pi^{2}\left(\ell^{2}-4n^{2}\right)\left(b^{2}+6nb^{2}+17n^{2}\right)}{\ell^{4}(b+3n)^{2}}\,.

(58)

Note that if
\ell=2n
the Chern-Pontryagin index vanishes for the self-dual solution in Eq. (
42
). This is related to the fact that at this point in parameter space the solution becomes global (Euclidean) AdS with a nontrivial slicing
Johnson 2014a
;
Johnson 2014b
, which can be rendered as the ground state of the system. On the other hand, the Euclidean on-shell action of the torsional anti-self-dual EH instanton (
52
) is

\displaystyle I_{\rm on-shell}=-4\pi^{2}P_{1}[\mathcal{M}]=\frac{4\pi^{2}\left(q^{4}-4r_{h}^{2}q^{2}+6r_{h}^{4}\right)}{r_{h}^{2}\left(q^{2}-2r_{h}^{2}\right)}\,.

(59)

In the vanishing torsion limit, i.e.
q\to 0
, the action is equal to
12\pi^{2}
giving a Chern-Pontryagin index
P_{1}[\mathcal{M}]=-3
, a well-known result of the original EH space
Eguchi and Hanson 1979
;
Eguchi and Hanson 1978
.

The Euler characteristic of these configurations can be computed as
Eguchi et al. 1980

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{M}}\epsilon_{abcd}R^{ab}\wedge R^{cd}-2\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\epsilon_{abcd}\,\theta^{ab}\wedge\left(R^{cd}-\frac{2}{3}\,\theta^{a}_{\ c}\wedge\theta^{cd}\right)\right]\,,

(60)

where
\theta^{ab}=\omega^{ab}-\omega_{0}^{ab}
is the second fundamental form possessing only normal components to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
and
\omega_{0}^{ab}
denotes the Lorentz connection of the product space having only tangential components at the boundary. To compute the second fundamental form, we evaluate the torsional components at
r=r_{0}
with
r_{0}
constant, such that the full Lorentz connection
\omega_{0}^{ab}
has tangential components at the boundary. In the case of the novel torsional self-dual Taub-NUT/AdS of Eq. (
42
), the nontrivial components of
\theta^{ab}
are given by

\displaystyle\theta^{14}

\displaystyle=\frac{(br_{0}+n^{2})\Phi(r_{0})}{\ell\left(r_{0}^{2}-n^{2}\right)(b+r_{0})}\,e^{1}\,,

(61a)

\displaystyle\theta^{24}

\displaystyle=\frac{(br_{0}+n^{2})\Phi(r_{0})}{\ell\left(r_{0}^{2}-n^{2}\right)(b+r_{0})}\,e^{2}\,,

(61b)

\displaystyle\theta^{34}

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{(br_{0}+n^{2})\Phi(r_{0})}{\ell\left(r_{0}^{2}-n^{2}\right)(b+r_{0})}-\frac{\left(\ell^{2}-4n^{2}\right)(r_{0}-n)(b+r_{0})}{\ell(r_{0}+n)^{2}(3n+b)\Phi(r_{0})}\right]e^{3}\,,

(61c)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\Phi(r_{0})=\sqrt{\frac{(r_{0}-n)\left[b(r_{0}^{2}+\ell^{2})+2\ell^{2}r_{0}+n(3r_{0}^{2}+\ell^{2}+2br_{0})-n^{2}(2r_{0}+3b)-n^{3}\right]}{(r_{0}+n)(3n+b)}}\,.

(62)

Using this result, direct evaluation of the Euler characteristic for the self-dual Taub-NUT/AdS instanton yields

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{M})=1\,.

(63)

A similar procedure carried out for the Taub-bolt instanton gives
\chi(\mathcal{M})=2
. Then, the Euler characteristics of the Taub-NUT and Taub-bolt spaces differ by one unit, which reflects their different number of boundaries. Moreover, these values match those of the Riemannian case, showing that the affine properties of these spaces do not change the topological invariants of the Euler class.

In the case of the anti-self-dual EH solution, we follow the same strategy: we consider a product space of constant radial coordinate, i.e.
r=r_{0}
, such that the Lorentz connection has only tangential components at the boundary. Then, the nontrivial components of the second-fundamental form are

\displaystyle\theta^{14}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(2a^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{2}\right)\sqrt{r_{0}^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{2}-a^{4}}}{r_{0}^{3}\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r_{0}^{2}\right)}\,e^{1}\,,

(64a)

\displaystyle\theta^{24}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(2a^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{2}\right)\sqrt{r_{0}^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{2}-a^{4}}}{r_{0}^{3}\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r_{0}^{2}\right)}\,e^{2}\,,

(64b)

\displaystyle\theta^{34}

\displaystyle=\frac{2a^{8}+3a^{4}q^{2}r_{0}^{2}+2a^{4}r_{0}^{4}+2q^{4}r_{0}^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{6}}{r_{0}^{3}\left(2a^{4}+q^{2}r_{0}^{2}\right)\sqrt{r_{0}^{4}-q^{2}r_{0}^{2}-a^{4}}}\,e^{3}\,.

(64c)

Then, definition (
60
) yields the Euler characterstic of the torsional extension of the EH geometry as

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{M})=2\,.

(65)

Computing the index theorem for the Dirac operator is more involved. For non-compact manifolds, one needs to introduce the codimension-1 boundary integral of the Chern-Simons form that can be evaluated directly from
\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\theta^{a}_{\ b}\wedge R^{b}_{\ a}
. Additionally, one needs to include the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer
\eta
-invariant that measures the difference between the positive and negative eigenvalues of the tangential components of the Dirac operator
Atiyah et al. 1973
. In absence of torsion, one can use the Hitchin formula
Hitchin 1974
to compute the
\eta
-invariant directly. Nevertheless, in the presence of torsion, this formula does not apply and one needs to evaluate this invariant from its definition by using the Riemann-zeta function regularization procedure
Atiyah et al. 1973
. Moreover, the Dirac index receives corrections coming from the Nieh-Yan invariant
Hojman et al. 1980
;
Nelson 1980
;
Nieh and Yan 1982
, since it has been shown that this term contributes nontrivially to the axial anomaly
Chandia and Zanelli 1997
. This is certainly an interesting aspect that goes beyond the scope of this work and we plan to revisit it in the future.

VI
Summary

In this work we have considered the definition of (anti-)self duality brought in by unconventional supersymmetry. In the bosonic sector of the theory, this (anti-)self duality corresponds to conditions on the bosonic curvature components that saturate a BPS bound and, if the action is augmented by a topological term of the Pontryagin class, the moduli space of vacuum solutions of the theory is enlarged to include all the (anti-)self-dual configurations as ground states.

In the bosonic sector of the theory, we obtained explicit solutions of conformal unconventional supersymmetry that are (anti-)self dual. In particular, we focused on gravitational instantons constructed out of the
U(1)
-fibration of Einstein-Kähler manifolds that generalize the Taub-NUT/Bolt-AdS and Eguchi-Hanson spaces of general relativity including torsion. In both cases, the torsion is sourced by the compensating fields associated to the AdS boosts and special conformal transformations, but these fields are auxiliary and do not represent propagating deegrees of freedom. These solutions are shown to be continuously connected to their Einsteinian counterparts in the vanishing torsion limit.

Additionally, the global properties of these solutions such as the action and their topological invariants have been computed. In the bosonic sector, the action turns out to be proportional to the Chern-Pontryagin index of
SO(4)
that is explicitly computed; this is usually the case for instantons in Yang-Mills theory. Although the geometry of these solutions is different from that of their Riemannian counterparts as they include nontrivial torsion, the value of the Euler characteristic is insensitive to torsion, showing that the different affine properties of these spaces do not modify this aspect of their topology.

Interesting questions remain open. For instance, the contribution of these (anti-)self-dual configurations to the Dirac index is certainly of great interest. This could provide a connection between torsion and the axial anomaly as shown in
Chandia and Zanelli 1997
. Moreover, since the solutions presented here are non-compact, one would expect a boundary contribution to the topological invariants of the Pontryagin class. Although bulk contributions of torsion to the axial anomaly have been explored, the presence of boundaries implies that an extended analysis is needed. Finally, since these configurations saturate a BPS bound, it can be expected that they preserve a fraction of the supersymmetry only. We plan to study the existence of Killing spinors in these spaces to address this aspect in the future.

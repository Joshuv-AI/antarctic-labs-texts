1.
Introduction

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and
M
a finite Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
r
. Let
G(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}
be associated graded ring of
A
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
and
G(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}{\mathfrak{m}^{n}M}/{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M}
be associated graded module of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. Now
\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}
is irrelevant maximal ideal of
G(A)
we set depth
G(M)
=grade
(\mathcal{M},G(M))
. If
L
be an
A
-module then minimal number of generators of
L
is denoted by
\mu(L)
and its length is denoted by
\ell(L)
.

We know that Hilbert-Samuel function of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
is

H^{1}(M,n)=\ell({M}/{\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M})\ \text{for all}\ n\geq 0.

There exists a polynomial
P_{M}(z)
of degree
r
such that

H^{1}(M,n)=P_{M}(n)\ \text{for}\ n\gg 0.

This polynomial can be written as

P_{M}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(M)\binom{X+r-i}{r-i}

These coefficients
e_{i}(M)^{\prime}
s are integers and known as
Hilbert coefficients
of
M
.

We know that Hilbert series of
M
is formal power series

H_{M}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell(\mathfrak{m}^{n}M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)z^{n}

We can write

H_{M}(z)=\frac{h_{M}(z)}{(1-z)^{r}},\ \text{where}\ r=dimM

Here,
h_{M}(z)=h_{0}(M)+h_{1}(M)z+\ldots+h_{s}(M)z^{s}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
and
h_{M}(1)\neq 0
. This polynomial is know as
h-polynomial
of
M
.

If we set
f^{(i)}
to denote
i
th formal derivative of a polynomial
f
then it is easy to see that
e_{i}(M)=h_{M}^{(i)}(1)/i!
for
i=0,\ldots,r
. It is convenient to set
e_{i}(M)=h_{M}^{(i)}(1)/i!
for all
i\geq 0.

We know that if
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is Cohen-Macaulay with red
(A)\leq 2
then
G(A)
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
).

If
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module with red
(M)\leq 1
then
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
5
, Theorem 16]
), but if red
(M)=2
, then
G(M)
need not be Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
6
, Example 3.3]
).

Here we consider maximal Cohen-Macaulay (MCM) modules over a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
. We know that if
A
is a regular local ring then
M
is free, say
M\cong A^{s}
. This implies
G(M)\cong G(A)^{s}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

The next case is when
A
is a hypersurface ring.
For convenience we assume
A=Q/(f)
where
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
is a regular local ring with infinite residue field and
f\in\mathfrak{n}^{2}
.

If
f\in\mathfrak{n}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{3}
then
A
has minimal multiplicity. It follows that any MCM module
M
over
A
has minimal multiplicity. So
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

One of the cases of interest for us was when
f\in\mathfrak{n}^{3}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{4}
. Note in this case red
(A)=2
. So if
M
is any MCM
A
-module then red
(M)\leq 2
. In this case
G(M)
need not Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
6
, Example 3.3]
).

We know that if red
(M)\leq 1
then
M
has minimal multiplicity. This implies
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
2.14
). Furthermore, if red
(M)=0
then
M
is an Ulrich module (
an MCM module
M
is said to be Ulrich module if
e(M)=\mu(M)
).

Notice if
M
is an MCM module over
A
then projdim
{}_{Q}(M)=1
. So,
M
has a minimal presentation over
Q

0\rightarrow Q^{\mu(M)}\xrightarrow{\phi}Q^{\mu(M)}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0.

We investigate
G(M)
in terms of invariants of a minimal presentation of
M
over
Q
.

Set
i(M)=
max{
i|
all entries of
\phi
are in
\mathfrak{n}^{i}
}. Then
from
[
6
, Theorem 2]
we know that
e(M)\geq\mu(M)i(M)
for any MCM module
M
over a hypersurface ring; in that paper, it is given that if
e(M)=\mu(M)i(M)
then
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Here we consider the case when
e(M)=\mu(M)i(M)+1
and prove that:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
be a complete regular local ring of dimension
d+1
with infinite residue field. Let
g\in\mathfrak{n}^{i}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{i+1}
with
i\geq 2
. Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})=(Q/(g),\mathfrak{n}/(g))
and
M
be an MCM
A
-module. Now if

then depth

and
h_{M}(z)=\mu(M)(1+z+\ldots+z^{i(M)-1})+z^{s}
where

. Furthermore,

is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

.

If
\mu(M)=r
and
det(\phi)\in\mathfrak{n}^{r}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{r+1}
then we know that

(see
[
6
, Theorem 2]
). So

is an Ulrich module. This implies
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay. Here we consider the case when
det(\phi)\in\mathfrak{n}^{r+1}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{r+2}
and prove

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
({Q},\mathfrak{n})
be a complete regular local ring with infinite residue field of dimension
d+1
with
d\geq 0
. Let
M
be a
Q
-module with minimal presentation

0\rightarrow Q^{r}\xrightarrow{\phi}Q^{r}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

Now if
\phi=[a_{ij}]

where
a_{ij}\in\mathfrak{n}
with

, then depth

. In this case if

we can also prove that

(1)

is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

.

(2)

if and only if

.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be complete hypersurface ring of dimension

and
M
be an MCM

Now we consider the case when

and prove

.

Let

The next theorem deals with the case when

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be a complete hypersurface ring of dimension

with

be an MCM

-module with

5.
The case when

In this section we prove Theorem

has no free summand and prove:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete hypersurface ring of dimension
d

e(A)=3
and infinite residue field. Let
M
be an MCM
A
-module with no free summand. Now if
\mu(M)=3

Proof.

From
2.27
we can take
(A,\mathfrak{m})=(Q/(f),\mathfrak{n}/(f))
, where

(Q,\mathfrak{n})
is a regular local ring of dimension
d+1
and
f\in\mathfrak{n}^{3}\setminus\mathfrak{n}^{4}

Let dim
M\geq 1
and
0\rightarrow Q^{3}\xrightarrow{\phi}Q^{3}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
be a minimal presentation of
M
. Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be a maximal
\phi
-superficial sequence (see
2.23
). Set
M_{d}=M/\underline{x}M
and
(Q^{\prime},(y))=(Q/(\underline{x}),\mathfrak{n}/(\underline{x}))

Clearly,
Q^{\prime}

M_{d}\cong Q^{\prime}/(y^{a_{1}})\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y^{a_{2}})\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y^{a_{3}})

red_{(\underline{x})}(M)\leq 2

has no free summand (see Lemma

1\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{3}\leq 2

Case(1):

a_{1}=1,a_{2}=1,a_{3}=1

In this case

. This gives
h_{M_{d}}(z)=3
. So

e(M_{d})=\mu(M_{d})=3
. For dim
M\geq 1
we know that
e(M)=e(M_{d})
and
\mu(M)=\mu(M_{d})
. Also, notice that
i(M)=i(M_{d})=1
. So we have
e(M)=\mu(M)=3
this implies that
M
is an Ulrich module. So
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
h_{M}(z)=3
. (see
[
6
, Theorem 2]
).

Case(2):

a_{1}=1,a_{2}=1,a_{3}=2
.

In this case
M_{d}\cong Q^{\prime}/(y)\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y)\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y^{2})
. So we have
h_{M_{d}}(z)=3+z
.

This implies
v_{Q^{\prime}}(det(\phi\otimes Q^{\prime}))=v_{Q}(det(\phi))=4
.
Now for dim
M\geq 1

3.2
depth
G(M)\geq d-1
. Also, we have two cases.

First case when
h_{M}(z)=3+z
. In this case
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Second case when
h_{M}(z)=3+z^{2}
. In this case depth
G(M)=d-1
.

Case(3):

a_{1}=1,a_{2}=2,a_{3}=2
.

In this case
M_{d}\cong Q^{\prime}/(y)\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y^{2})\oplus Q^{\prime}/(y^{2})
. So
h_{M_{d}}(z)=3+2z.

We first consider the case when dim
M=3
because if dim
M\leq 2
there is nothing to prove.

Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
be a maximal
\phi
-superficial sequence. Set
M_{1}=M/x_{1}M
,
M_{2}=M/(x_{1},x_{2})M
,
M_{3}=M/(\underline{x})M
and
J=(x_{2},x_{3})
.

We first prove two claims:

Claim(1):

\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}
for all

Proof
Since
\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}M_{2}=x_{3}\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}
for all

\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}M_{2}:x_{3}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}
for all

.

So from
2.32
for all
i\geq 2
we have

0\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}M_{2}.

We also know that for
i\ggg 0
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}
. So it is clear that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}M_{2}
for all
i\geq 2
.

Claim(2):

\ell(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})\leq 1
.

Proof:
Now since
\mu(M_{2})=3
so
\ell(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})\leq 3
. If
\ell(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})=3
then
M_{2}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}
this implies that
\mathfrak{m}M_{2}=\mathfrak{m}\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}
. So
\mathfrak{m}M_{2}=0
and this is a contradiction. Now if possible assume that
\ell(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})=2
then
M_{2}=\langle m,l_{1},l_{2}\rangle
with
l_{1},l_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}\setminus\mathfrak{m}M_{2}
and
m\in M_{2}
. Now we have
\mathfrak{m}l_{i}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}
for
i=1,2.
If we set
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}/(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, then
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is principal ideal. We also know that
\ell(\mathfrak{m}M_{3})=\ell(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}M_{3})
and
\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{3}=(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})^{2}M_{3}=0
, this implies that
\ell(\mathfrak{m}M_{3})=1
. But we have
\ell(\mathfrak{m}M_{3})=2
(from the Hilbert series of
M_{3}
). So
\ell(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})\leq 1
.

Now from
2.32

0\rightarrow(\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}:x_{3})/\mathfrak{m}M_{2}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}M_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2}.

So from claim(2), we have
b_{1}(x_{3},M_{2})=\ell(\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}:x_{3}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2})\leq 1
.

Since dim
M_{2}=1
we can write

-polynomial of

h_{M_{2}}(z)=3+(\rho_{0}(M_{2})-\rho_{1}(M_{2}))z+\rho_{1}(M_{2})z^{2}

(see
2.15
). Since red
{}_{(\underline{x})}(M)\leq 2
,
\rho_{n}(M_{2})=0
for all
n\geq 2
. We have
\rho_{0}(M_{2})=\ell(\mathfrak{m}M_{2}/x_{3}M_{2})=e(M_{2})-\mu(M_{2})=2
and coefficients of

are non-negative [from
2.26
(1)].

From short exact sequence (see
2.29

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}:x_{3}/\mathfrak{m}M_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{2}/x_{3}\mathfrak{m}M_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{2}M_{3}/0\rightarrow 0

.

Subcase (ii):
When

.

From Claim(2) we have

. So in this case

2.7
).

Since dim
M_{1}=2
, from

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{n}M_{1}:J/\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}M_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{n}M_{1}:x_{2}/\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}M_{1}

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete hypersurface ring of dimension
d
with
e(A)=3
and infinite residue field. Let
M
be an MCM
A
-module with
\mu(M)=3
, then depth
G(M)\geq d-2
.

Proof.

We have two cases here.

First case when
M
has no free summand. In this case, from the above theorem depth
G(M)\geq d-2
.

Next case when
M
has free summand. In this case we can write
M\cong N\oplus A^{s}
for some
s\geq 1
and
N
has no free summand. We assume
N\neq 0
, otherwise
M
is free and
G(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Clearly,
N
is a MCM
A
-module (see
[
1
, Proposition 1.2.9]
). Notice that red
(N)\leq 2
, because red
(M)\leq 2
. Also
\mu(M)>\mu(N)
, so
\mu(N)\leq 2
.

If
\mu(N)=1
then we have a minimal presentation of
N
as
0\rightarrow Q\xrightarrow{a}Q\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
, where
a\in\mathfrak{n}
.
This implies
N\cong Q/(a)Q
. So,
G(N)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

If
\mu(N)=2
then depth
G(N)\geq d-1
(from Theorem
4.1
).

We know that (see
[
1
, Proposition 1.2.9]
)

depth
G(M)\geq
min{depth
G(N)
, depth
G(A)
}
=
depth
G(N)
.

So in this case depth
G(M)\geq d-1
.
∎

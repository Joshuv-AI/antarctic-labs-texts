Introduction

1.1  Basic definitions and notation

An
r
-uniform hypergraph (an
r
-graph
, for short), is a family of
r
-element subsets of a finite set.
We associate an
r
-graph
{\mathcal{H}}
with its edge set and call its vertex set
V({\mathcal{H}})
.
Often we take
V({\mathcal{H}})=[n]
, where
[n]:=\{1,2,3,\dots,n\}
.
Given an

-graph
{\mathcal{F}}
,
let

of
{\mathcal{F}}

{\rm{ex}}_{r}(n,{\mathcal{F}})

r
-graph on
n

{\mathcal{F}}
.

Since
a (graph) tree is connected and bipartite, it uniquely defines the parts in its bipartition. So, we say a tree

T

(s,t)
-tree
if one part of
V(T)
has
s

t

Let

, and let

be an
(s,t)
-tree
with parts

and
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{t}\}
.
Let

and

be pairwise disjoint sets, such that
|U_{i}|=a
and
|V_{j}|=b
for all

.
So

.
The
(a,b)
-blowup
of
T
, denoted by

, is the

r
-uniform hypergraph with edge set

{\mathcal{T}}(T,a,b):=\{U_{i}\cup V_{j}:u_{i}v_{j}\in E(T)\}.

The goal of this paper is to find the asymptotics of the Turán number for
(a,b)
-blowups of many trees of radius
2

\Delta
-systems method.
Earlier,

-blowups of different classes of trees and different pairs

Theorem 1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Suppose

,

,

,

.
Let

-free

This theorem asymptotically settles about a half of possible cases, but when

More is known on
(a,b)
-blowups of paths.

Let

denote the (graph) path with

The first edge of the path corresponds to

The case of

[

The case of

[

They showed that for fixed

For longer paths, the following was proved in
[

Theorem 2

(Theorem 1 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let

,

is even and

-blowups of trees of radius

1.2  New results, bushes and shadows

Since

has
{s}
disjoint edges
B_{i}\cup A_{i,1}
for
i=1,\ldots,{s}
, the example of the
r
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set

in which every edge intersects the set
[{s}-1]
shows that

{\rm{ex}}_{r}(n,{\mathcal{B}}_{{s},h}(a,b))\geq{n\choose r}-{n-{s}+1\choose r}\sim({s}-1){n\choose r-1}.

(1)

We will use the
\Delta
-systems approach to show that this is asymptotically correct in many cases.
For
a>b\geq 2
the asymptotic equality follows from Theorem
1
.
In this paper we deal with

considering shadows of hypergraphs.

For an

-graph
\mathcal{H}
the

-sets that lie in some edge of

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
a,b,{s},h
are positive integers,

.
Also suppose that in case of
(a,b)=(1,r-1)

.
Then there exists a

such that every

-vertex
r
-uniform family
\mathcal{H}

satisfying

|\mathcal{H}|>({s}-1)|\partial\mathcal{H}|+Cn^{r-2}

(2)

contains
the bush
{\mathcal{B}}_{{s},h}(a,b)
.

This implies that (
1
) is asymptotically exact in these cases as

are fixed and

.
Note that for
(a,b)=(1,r-1)
our proof works only for

.
In fact, in this case the theorem does not hold for

.
An example is this:
V({\mathcal{H}})=[n],A=[{s}-1]
and
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
, where

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
is the set of

-subsets of

with exactly one vertex in

and

.
This example has asymptotically

{\mathcal{B}}_{{s},2}(1,r-1)
.
By increasing

, we can get examples without

that have even more edges.

McLennan
[
9
]
proved that in the graph case (

),

. Vertex-disjoint unions of complete graphs

are extremal.
For the case
{s}=1
the restriction (
2
) is too strong,

. Even better bounds were proved in
[
5
]
. So we
suppose that
{s}\geq 2
,

.

Since each tree of diameter

.

Suppose

,

.
Let

.

such that every

-vertex

-graph

-systems approach to show that for

2
Definitions for the
\Delta
-system method and a lemma

A family of sets
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}
is a
q
-star
or a
\Delta
-system
or a
q
-sunflower with kernel
A
, if

F_{i}\cap F_{j}=A
for all
1\leq i<j\leq q
. The sets
F_{i}\setminus A
are called
petals
.

For a member
F
of a family
\mathcal{F}
, let the
intersection structure of
F
relative to
\mathcal{F}
be

\mathcal{I}(F,\mathcal{F})=\{F\cap F^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{F\}\}.

An
r
-uniform family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[n]\choose r}
is
r
-
partite
if there exists a partition

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
of the vertex set
[n]
such that
|F\cap X_{i}|=1
for each

F\in\mathcal{F}
and each
i\in[r]
.
For a partition
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
of
[n]
and a set
S\subseteq[n]
, the
pattern

\Pi(S)
is the set

\{i\in[r]:S\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
. Naturally, for a family
\mathcal{L}
of subsets of
[n]
,

\Pi(\mathcal{L})=\{\Pi(S):S\in\mathcal{L}\}\subseteq 2^{[r]}.

Lemma 6

(The intersection semilattice lemma (Füredi
[
6
]
))
.

For any positive integers
q
and
r
, there exists a positive constant
c(r,q)
such that every
family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[n]\choose r}
contains a subfamily
\mathcal{F}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
satisfying

1.
|\mathcal{F}^{*}|\geq c(r,q)|\mathcal{F}|
.

2.
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is
r
-partite, together with an
r
-partition
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
.

3. There exists a family
\mathcal{J}
of proper subsets of
[r]
such that
\Pi(\mathcal{I}(F,\mathcal{F}^{*}))=\mathcal{J}

holds for all
F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
.

4.
\mathcal{J}
is closed under intersection, i.e., for all
A,B\in\mathcal{J}
we have
A\cap B\in\mathcal{J}
, as
well.

5. For any
F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
and each
A\in\mathcal{I}(F,\mathcal{F}^{*})
, there is a
q
-star in
\mathcal{F}^{*}

containing
F
with kernel
A
.

Remark 1.

The proof of Lemma
6
in
[

]
yields that if
\mathcal{F}
itself is
r
-partite with an
r
-partition
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
, then the

-partition in the statement can be taken the same.

Remark 2.

By definition, if for some

none of the members of the family
\mathcal{J}
of proper subsets of
[r]
in

contains

, then for any two sets

, their intersections with

are distinct. It follows that if

, then

|\mathcal{F}^{*}|\leq\prod_{j\in M}|X_{j}|\leq\left(\frac{n-(r-m)}{m}\right)^{m}

Thus, if
|\mathcal{F}^{*}|>\left(\frac{n-r+m}{m}\right)^{m}
, then every
m
-element subset of
[r]
is contained in some

.

Call a family

of proper subsets of
[r]

m

(3)

For
k=0,1,\ldots,r
, define the family
\mathcal{J}^{(k)}
of proper subsets of

as follows. It contains

,

(b) all

-element subsets of

By definition, each
\mathcal{J}^{(k)}
is
(r-2)
-covering. Moreover,

-covering family of proper subsets of

Indeed, if an

-covering family
\mathcal{J}

6
Stability: Proof of Theorem
5

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform family not containing
a bush
{\mathcal{B}}_{{s},h}(a,b)
with

|\mathcal{H}|>({s}-1){n\choose{r-1}}-C_{0}n^{r-2}.

(7)

Define
C
,
m
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{0},\ldots,{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
as in Subsection
4.1
. By (
7
) and the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
,

|\widehat{\mathcal{H}}|\geq({s}-1){n\choose{r-1}}-(C+C_{0})n^{r-2}.

(8)

As in Subsection 4.2, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, the intersection structure
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
contains
\mathcal{J}^{(r-1)}
. So, again
for each hyperedge
E\in\mathcal{H}_{i}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
(
1\leq i\leq m
) there is an element
c(E)\in E
such that each proper subset of
E
containing
c(E)
is a kernel of an
{s}hr
-star in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
.

For
v\in[n]
, let
{\mathcal{H}}(v)=\{E\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}:v=c(E)\}
and
{\mathcal{G}}(v)=\{E\setminus\{v\}:E\in{\mathcal{H}}(v)\}
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\bigcup_{v\in[n]}{\mathcal{G}}(v)
.
By Claim
11
,

|{\mathcal{G}}(v)|=|\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(v)|
for each
v\in[n]
; in particular,
\sum_{v}|{\mathcal{G}}(v)|=|\widehat{\mathcal{H}}|
.
Furthermore, Claim
12
implies that

each
(r-2)
-subset
Y
of
[n]
is in the shadow of at most
{s}-1
families
{\mathcal{G}}(v)
.

(9)

Recall the Lovász form of the Kruskal-Katona Theorem:

Theorem 16
.

If
x
is a positive real,
1\leq k<n
,
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
and
|{\mathcal{F}}|={x\choose k}
, then

|\partial{\mathcal{F}}|\geq{x\choose k-1}
.

Here
{x\choose k}
is a non-negative real convex function defined as a degree
k

x(x-1)\dots(x-k+1)/k!
for
x\geq k-1
and
0
otherwise.

For every
j\in[n]
, there a real
x_{j}
such that
|{\mathcal{G}}(j)|={x_{j}\choose r-1}
.
Inequality (
8
) gives

\sum_{i}{x_{i}\choose r-1}\geq({s}-1){n\choose{r-1}}-(C+C_{0})n^{r-2},

(10)

and (
9
) and Lovász theorem give

({s}-1){n\choose r-2}\geq\sum_{i}|\partial{\mathcal{G}}(j)|\geq\sum_{i}{x_{i}\choose r-2}.

(11)

Let
b=3(r-1)!(C+C_{0})
.

Lemma 17
.

Suppose that
n\geq r^{2}
and
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\ldots\geq x_{n}
. Then
inequalities (
10
) and (
11
) concerning
n,r,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
imply

x_{1},\dots,x_{{s}-1}>n-b
.

Proof.

Pure college algebra. For brevity let
y:=x_{{s}}
.
The case
y\leq r-2
is obvious (in this case the left hand side of (
10
) has at most
{s}-1

nonzero terms), so from now on we suppose that
y>r-2
.
Multiply (
11
) by
(y-r+2)/(r-1)
and add it to (
10
).
Using
{x\choose r-1}=(x-r+2)/(r-1)\times{x\choose r-2}
(for all reals
x>r-2\geq 1
)
after rearrangements we get

\displaystyle(C+C_{0})n^{r-2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq{s}}\frac{y-x_{i}}{r-1}{x_{i}\choose r-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i\leq{s}-1}\frac{n-x_{i}}{r-1}{n\choose r-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i\leq{s}-1}\frac{x_{i}-y}{r-1}\left({n\choose r-2}-{x_{i}\choose r-2}\right).

The first and the third rows are non-negative. We obtain

(r-1)(C+C_{0})n^{r-2}/{n\choose r-2}\geq\sum_{1\leq i\leq{s}-1}(n-x_{i}).

So the left hand side is at most

.
∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof of the theorem. By Lemma

This completes the proof of Theorem
5

.

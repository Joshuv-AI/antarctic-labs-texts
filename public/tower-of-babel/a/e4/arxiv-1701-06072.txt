1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
R=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}R_{n}
be a Noetherian homogeneous ring with local base ring
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
. So
R_{0}
is a Noetherian ring and there are
finitely many elements
l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\in R_{1}
such that
R=R_{0}[l_{1},\dots,l_{r}]
. Let
R_{+}:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}R_{n}
denote
the irrelevant ideal of
R
and let
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\oplus R_{+}
denote the graded maximal ideal of
R
. Finally let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
and
N=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{n}
be
finitely generated graded
R
-modules.

Herzog introduced a generalization of local
cohomology so called generalized local cohomology, denoted by

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
which is isomorphic to

\underset{\underset{n}{\rightarrow}}{\mbox{lim}}{\rm{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(M/{{R_{+}}^{n}}M,N)
. We note that if
M=R
, then

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(R,N)=H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
is the usual local cohomology. As is
well known, the finiteness of the local cohomology modules have an
important role in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. Many
of mathematicians work on finiteness of local cohomology. One of
approaches in finiteness is Artinianess. Authors, in [BFT],
[BRS], [RS] and [S] studied Artinianess of the graded modules

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
,
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
and
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M))
. In the
recent paper we will study the Artinianess of Generalized graded
local cohomology. Here, we briefly mention some our results which
have been proved in this paper. Let
f
be the least non-negative
integer such that the graded module
H_{R_{+}}^{f}(M,N)
is not
finitely generated. We prove that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is Artinian for each
i\leq f
. We also
prove that if
M
is of finite projective dimension with
{\rm pd}_{R}M=n
and
c=c_{R_{+}}(N)
is the largest non-negative integer

i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)\neq 0
, then

H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)
is Artinian.
In particular if
c_{R_{+}}(M,N)
is the largest non-negative
integer
i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\neq 0
, then

c_{R_{+}}(M,N)\leq{\rm pd}_{R}M+c_{R_{+}}(N)
. We prove a similar
result for invariant
a_{R_{+}}(N)
which shows the largest
non-negative integer
i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
is not
Artinain. Moreover, we show that if
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is

R_{+}
-cofinite, then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is
Artinian. Finally we study Artinianess of generalized local
cohomology when
{\rm dim}(R_{0})\leq 1
. In this case, we prove
that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N),\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)),H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)),H_{R_{+}}^{j}(M,H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(N))
and
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
are Artinian for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

2.
The results

It should be noted that local flat morphism of local
Noetherian rings is faithfully flat. So, if
R^{\prime}_{0}
is flat over

R_{0}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0}
, then
R^{\prime}_{0}
is faithfully flat
over
R_{0}
. Moreover, it follows from [K, Theorem 1] that if

(R^{\prime}_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\prime})
is a faithfully flat local
R_{0}
-algebra, then
A

is a graded Artinian
R
-module if and only if

A^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A
is a graded Artinian module over

R^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}R
. In view of this argument, we have the
following proposition.

2.1 Proposition
.

Let
f:=f_{R_{+}}(M,N)
be the least non-negative integer such that

H_{R_{+}}^{f}(M,N)
is not finitely generated. Then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is Artinian for each
i\leq f
.

Proof.

We prove the assertion by induction on
i
. If
i=0
, then

\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N)
is finitely generated; and hence

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N))
is finitely generated.
Thus there exists some positive integer
n
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N))=0
and so the result
follows in this case. Now, suppose inductively that the result
has been proved for all values smaller than or equal to
i
with

i<f
and we prove it for
i+1
, where
i+1\leq f
. Consider the
exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N)\rightarrow N\rightarrow N/\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N)\rightarrow 0.
Application of the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,-)
to the above exact
sequence induces the following exact sequence

H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N/\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i+2}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))\hskip 14.22636pt{\ddagger}.
Set

K_{1}={\rm Ker}\phi
and
K_{2}={\rm Coker}\phi
. We note that for
each
i
, there is an isomorphism

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))\cong{\rm Ext}^{i}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))
and then this module is an

R_{+}
-torsion finitely generated graded
R
-module. Therefore

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are
R_{+}
-torsion finitely generated graded

R
-modules. Consider
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(K_{1})
and

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(K_{2})
. By the previous argument these
modules are finitely generated and
R_{+}
-torsion and then there
exists some positive integer number
n
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(K_{1})={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(K_{2})=0
and this implies that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(K_{1})
and

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(K_{2})
are Artinian. Now, in view of the
sequence
{\ddagger}
, we can conclude that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N))
is Artinian if and only if

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N/\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N))
is
Artinian. So we may assume that
\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N)=0
. Let

\mathbf{x}
be an indeterminate and let

R^{\prime}_{0}:=R_{0}[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R_{0}[\mathbf{x}]}
,

\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{m}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0},R^{\prime}=R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}R
,

M^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}M
, and
N^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}N
. Then by
the flat base change property of local cohomology, for each
i

we have
R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N))\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0}}(H_{(R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}R)_{+}}^{i+1}(R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}M,R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}N))

and using the above argument
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N))
is Artinian if and only if

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R^{\prime}_{0}}(H_{(R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}R)_{+}}^{i+1}(R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}M,R^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}N))

is Artinian. So we may assume that the residue field
k
of
R_{0}

is infinite. As
\Gamma_{R_{+}}(N)=0
, there exists an element

x\in R_{1}
which is a non-zerodivisor with respect to
N
and so
there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow N(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\rightarrow}}N\rightarrow N/xN\rightarrow 0
of graded
R
-modules. Application of the functor

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,-)
to this exact sequence induces the exact sequence

\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N/xN)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N)(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N).

If we
consider
U^{i}:=H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)/xH_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
, then we have the
exact sequence
0\rightarrow U^{i}\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N/xN)\rightarrow(0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i+1}(M,N)}x)(-1)\rightarrow 0.
Applying the functor

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(-)
to this sequence, we get the following
exact sequence

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N/xN))\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)}x)(-1)\rightarrow H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(U^{i})\hskip 14.22636pt\maltese.

We note that
U^{i}
is finitely
generated and
R_{+}
-torsion and so
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{i}(U^{i})\cong H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(U^{i})
is Artinian. On the other hand, one can easily
show that
f(M,N/xN)\geq f(M,N)-1
and so
i+1\leq f(M,N)
implies
that
i\leq f(M,N)-1\leq f(M,N/xN)
; and hence by induction
hypothesis
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N/xN))
is Artinian.
Therefore by the sequence
\maltese
, the module
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)(-1)}x)=(0:_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))(-1)}x)
is Artinian. Now, since
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is
x
-torsion, using Melkersson’s Lemma this
module is Artinain.
∎

2.2 Lemma
.

Let
0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M_{2}\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of
finitely generated graded
R
-modules. Then for any
R
-module

N
, there is the following long exact sequence

\dots\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(M_{1},N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M_{2},N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M_{1},N)\rightarrow\dots.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}:=0\rightarrow I^{0}\rightarrow I^{1}\rightarrow I^{2}\rightarrow\dots
be an
injective resolution of
N
. We note that for any finitely
generated
R
-module
M
and any
i
, there is an isomorphism

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)=H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{R}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})))

and by the basic properties of section functor each

\Gamma_{R_{+}}(I^{i})
in
\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})
is injective.
Thus there is an exact sequence of complexes

0\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{R}(M_{2},\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I}))\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{R}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I}))\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{R}(M_{1},\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I}))\rightarrow 0.

Now, by using a
basic theorem in homology theory, there is the following long
exact sequence of
R
-modules

\dots\rightarrow H^{i-1}({\rm Hom}_{R}(M_{1},\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})))\rightarrow H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{R}(M_{2},\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})))

\rightarrow H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{R}(M,\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})))\rightarrow H^{i}({\rm Hom}_{R}(M_{1},\Gamma_{R_{+}}(\mathcal{I})))\rightarrow\dots

and this
completes the proof.
∎

2.3 Theorem
.

Let
M
be of finite projective dimension with pd
{}_{R}(M)=n
and

c:=c_{R_{+}}(N)
be the largest positive integer
i
such that

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
is not zero. Then the following condition hold.

(i) The graded module
H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)/\mathfrak{m}_{0}H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)
is Artinian.

(ii) If
c_{R_{+}}(M,N)
is the
largest positive integer
i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is not
zero, then
c_{R_{+}}(M,N)\leq{\rm pd}_{R}M+c_{R_{+}}(N)=n+c
.

Proof.

(i) We proceed by induction on pd
{}_{R}(M)=n
. If
n=0
, the the
result is clear by [RS, Theorem 2.1]. Now, suppose inductively
that the result has been proved for all values smaller that
n>0

and so we prove this for
n
. Since pd
{}_{R}(M)=n
, there exists a
positive integer
t
and an exact sequence of graded modules
0\rightarrow K\rightarrow R^{t}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
such that pd
{}_{R}(K)=n-1
. In view of Lemma

2.2
, if we apply the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(-,N)
to this
exact sequence, we have the following exact sequence

H_{R_{+}}^{n+c-1}(K,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(R^{t},N).

We note that
n+c>c=c_{R_{+}}(N)
and so
H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(N)=0
. Now,
application of the functor
R_{0}/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}\otimes_{R}-
to the
above exact sequence induces the following epimorphisms

H_{R_{+}}^{n+c-1}(K,N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{n+c-1}(K,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{n+c}(M,N)\rightarrow 0.

By using induction hypothesis, the graded module
H_{R_{+}}^{n+c-1}(K,N)/{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}H_{R_{+}}^{n+c-1}(K,N)
is Artinian. Thus the result
follows by the above epimorphism. (ii) In this part, similar to (i),
we can apply an easy induction on pd
{}_{R}M=n
.
∎

2.4 Theorem
.

Let
M
be of finite projective dimension and let
a_{R_{+}}(M,N)

be the largest non-negative integer
i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)

is not Artinian.
Then we have the following conditions.

(i)
a_{R_{+}}(M,N)\leq{\rm pd}_{R}M+a_{R_{+}}(N)
, where
a_{R_{+}}(N)

is the largest non-negative integer
i
such that
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)

is not Artinian.

(ii)
H_{R_{+}}^{a+n}(M,N)/\mathfrak{m}_{0}H_{R_{+}}^{a+n}(M,N)
is Artinian,
where
a=a_{R_{+}}(N)
and pd
{}_{R}M=n
.

Proof.

(i) Let pd
{}_{R}M=n
and
a=a_{R_{+}}(N)
. We prove the assertion by
induction on pd
{}_{R}M=n
. If
n=0
, the result is clear. Suppose,
inductively that
n>0
and the result has been proved for all
values smaller than
n
and so we prove it for
n
. As pd
{}_{R}M=n
,
there exists a positive integer
t
and an exact sequence
0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow R^{t}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of
R
-modules such that pd
{}_{R}M_{1}=n-1
. In
view of Lemma
2.2
, if we apply the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(-,N)

to the above exact sequence, we get the following exact sequence
of
R
-modules
H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(M_{1},N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)^{t}.
Now, consider
i>a+n
. We note that

i-1>a+n-1={\rm pd}_{R}M_{1}+a_{R_{+}}(N)
and
i>a+n>a
. Thus, using
induction hypothesis,
H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(M_{1},N)
and
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)

are Artinian. Now, in view of the above exact sequence the result
follows. (ii) To prove this part, we again proceed by induction
on pd
{}_{R}M=n
. If
n=0
, then the result follows by [S, Theorem
2.3]. Now, suppose, inductively that
n>0
and the result has
been proved for all values smaller than
n
and so we prove it
for
n
. By a similar proof which mentioned in (i), there exists
an exact sequence of
R
-modules

H_{R_{+}}^{a+n-1}(M_{1},N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{a+n}(M,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{a+n}(N)^{t}.

Consider
X={\rm Im}(\alpha)
and
Y={\rm Im}(\beta)
. Since

a+n>a
, the module
Y/\mathfrak{m}_{0}Y
is Artinian. On the other
hand, by using induction hypothesis, one can easily see that

X/\mathfrak{m}_{0}X
is Artinian. Now, the result follows easily.

∎

2.5 Lemma
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is
R_{+}
-cofinite. Then

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is Artinian.

Proof.

Since
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is
R_{+}
-cofinite,
{\rm Hom}_{R}(R/R_{+},H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is finitely generated and

R_{+}
-torsion. Thus
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}({\rm Hom}_{R}(R/R_{+},H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)))\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}((0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)}R_{+}))=(0:_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))}R_{+})
is finitely generated and
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion. It implies that the last term is Artinian. On the other
hand, since
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is
R_{+}
-torsion,
it is Artinian.
∎

2.6 Proposition
.

Let
M
be of finite projective dimension and let
R_{+}
be a
principal graded ideal of
R
. Then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is Artinian for each
i
.

Proof.

As
R_{+}
is principal, using [DS, Theorem 2.8],
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is

R_{+}
-cofinite for each
i
. Now, the assertion follows by the
previous lemma.
∎

2.7 Lemma
.

Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated graded
R
-modules and
N

be
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion. Then
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)
is Artinian for all

i
.

Proof.

Since
N
is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion, there exists an injective
resolution
\mathcal{I}:=0\rightarrow I^{0}\rightarrow I^{1}\rightarrow\dots
such that each

I^{i}
is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion. By the definition of generalized
local cohomology there are the
following isomorphisms

H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\cong H^{i}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,\mathcal{I}))\cong H^{i}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(\mathcal{I})))\cong H^{i}({\rm Hom}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{I}))\cong H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M,N).
By the basic properties of generalized local
cohomology, the last term is Artinian; and hence the result
follows.
∎

2.9 Proposition
.

is Artinian. Thus
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)))
is Artinian and
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))=0
for each
i
and so (i) and (ii) are
clear in this case. Now, assume that dim
(R_{0})=1
. (i). By
applying the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,-)
to the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N)\rightarrow N\rightarrow N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N)\rightarrow 0
,
and applying the functor
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(-)
to the induced
exact functor, we can conclude that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is Artinian if and only if
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N)))
is Artinian and so
we may assume that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N)=0
. Now, let

x\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}
be a non-zerodivisor of
N
. Then there is an
exact sequence
0\rightarrow N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\rightarrow}}N\rightarrow N/xN\rightarrow 0
of graded

R
-modules. Application of the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,-)
to this
sequence induces the following exact sequence

H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(M,N/xN)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N).

We note that
N/xN
is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion and
so by Lemma
2.7
,
H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(M,N/xN)
is Artinian and
then
(0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)}x)
is Artinian. This implies that

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}((0:_{H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)}x)=(0:_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))}x)
is Artinian. Now, since
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is
x
-torsion, by using Melkesson’s Lemma,
it is Artinian. (ii). We proceed the assertion by induction on

i
. If
i=0
, then
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N))=H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N)).
We note that the last term is Artinian
because
\Gamma_{R_{+}}(M,N)
is finitely generated. Suppose,
inductively that the result has been proved for all values smaller
than
i
and so we prove it for
i
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}
be a
system of parameter of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. As
M
is finitely generated,
for some positive integer
t
there exists a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow R^{t}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of
R
-modules. In view of Lemma

2.2
, if we apply the functor
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(-,N)
to the above
exact sequence, we get the following exact sequence

H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(K,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N)\rightarrow H_{R_{+}}^{i}(R^{t},N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\rightarrow}}H_{R_{+}}^{i}(K,N).

Consider

A:={\rm Im}(\alpha)
,
B:={\rm Ker}(\beta)
and
C:={\rm Im}(\beta)
. Application of the functor
H_{yR}^{i}
to the above
exact sequence gives the epimorphism

H_{yR}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(K,N))\twoheadrightarrow H_{yR}^{1}(A)
, the
monomorphism
\Gamma_{yR}(C)\rightarrowtail\Gamma_{yR}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(K,N))
, and the exact sequence

\Gamma_{yR}(C)\rightarrow H_{yR}^{1}(B)\rightarrow H_{yR}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(R^{t},N))\hskip 8.5359pt({\ddagger}).
By using
induction hypotheses
H_{yR}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(K,N))=H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i-1}(K,N))
is Artinian and so is
H_{yR}^{1}(A)
. On
the other hand since, by (i), the module

\Gamma_{yR}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(K,N))
is Artinain, the module

\Gamma_{yR}(C)
is Artinian. Using [BFT, Theorem 2.5], the
module
H_{yR}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(R^{t},N))
is Artinian and then the
exact sequence
{\ddagger}
and the previous arguments imply that

H_{yR}^{1}(B)
is Artinian. Now, since both
H_{yR}^{1}(A)
and

H_{yR}^{1}(B)
are Artinian, one can easily deduce that

H_{yR}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))=H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(M,N))
is
Artinian.
∎

2.10 Proposition
.

Let dim
(R_{0})\leq 1
. Then
H_{R_{+}}^{p}(M,H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(N))
is
Artinian for each
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

If dim
(R_{0})=0
, then
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(N)=0
and so the result is
clear in this case. Now, assume that dim
(R_{0})=1
. By the
Grothendieck spectral sequence (see [R, Theorem 11.38]), for each

p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there is

E_{2}^{p,q}:=H_{R_{+}}^{p}(M,H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{q}(N))\underset{p}{\Longrightarrow}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{p+q}(M,N).

As dim
(R_{0})=1
, we have
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{q}(N)=0

for all
q>1
and then
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
for all
q\neq 0,1
. Thus we
can apply the dual of [W, Ex. 5.2.2] to get the following exact
sequence

E_{2}^{p+1,0}\rightarrow H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{p+1}(M,N)\rightarrow E_{2}^{p,1}\rightarrow E_{2}^{p+2,0}\rightarrow H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{p+2}(M,N).

It is easy to see that

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{p+1}(M,N)
and

E_{2}^{p+2,0}=H_{R_{+}}^{p+2}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N))=H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{p+2}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(N))
are Artinian. Thus the above
exact sequence implies that
E_{2}^{p,1}=H_{R_{+}}^{p}(M,H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(N))
is Artinian.
∎

2.11 Proposition
.

Let dim
(R_{0})\leq 1
. Then
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
is
Artinian for each
j,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

If dim
(R_{0})=0
, then each finitely generated
R
-module is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion. Thus
H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)
is Artinian and so is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-torsion. Then for each
j
, there is an isomorphism
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))\cong{\rm Ext}_{R}^{j}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
. One
can easily show that the last module is Artinian. Now, assume that
dim
(R_{0})=1
. We proceed by induction on
j
. If
j=0
, then we
have
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{0}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))={\rm Hom}_{R}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)))
. By using [BFT, Theorem 2.5], the module

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
is Artinian and so one can
easily show that
{\rm Hom}_{R}(M,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}(H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)))
is Artinian. Now, we assume that
j>0
and
the result has been proved for all values smaller than
j
and we
prove it for
j
. Since
M
is finitely generated, for some
positive integer
t
, there is an exact sequence
0\rightarrow K\rightarrow R^{t}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of
R
-module. In view of Lemma
2.2
, if we
apply the functor
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(-,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
to the above
exact sequence, we have the following exact sequence

H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j-1}(K,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))\rightarrow H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(M,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))\rightarrow H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(R^{t},H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N)).

We note that by induction hypotheses, the module
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j-1}(K,H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
is Artinian and
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{j}(R^{t},H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))=0
for all
j>1
and also
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}R}^{1}(R^{t},H_{R_{+}}^{i}(N))
is Artinian by [BFT, Theorem 2.5]. Now,
in view of the above exact sequence, we get our assertion.
∎

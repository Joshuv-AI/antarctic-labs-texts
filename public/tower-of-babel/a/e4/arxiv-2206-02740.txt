Abstract
. We study the relationship between transitivity and topological chaos for homeomorphisms of the two torus. We show that if a transitive homeomorphism of 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} is homotopic to the identity and has both a fixed point and a periodic point which is not fixed, then it has a topological horseshoe. We also show that if a transitive homeomorphims of 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} is homotopic to a Dehn twist, then either it is aperiodic or it has a topological horseshoe.



1.
Introduction

Three topological concepts that attempt to describe the complexities of dynamical systems are the notions of transitivity, of positive topological entropy, and of exponential growth of periodic points. While these three concepts are independent and there are several examples of systems possessing one of them but not the other two, it is a consequence of a result by Katok (see
[
Kat80
]
) that, for
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphisms of surfaces positive topological entropy implies the exponential growth of periodic points, since positive topological entropy implies the presence of horseshoes. On the other hand, transitivity is usually a “weaker” requirement, not implying the richness of dynamical behavior that positive topological entropy does.

But while in general transitivity does not imply that a system is topologically chaotic (i.e., has both positive topological entropy and also exponential growth of periodic points), there is evidence to suggest that, for surface homeomorphims, these concepts are related, and that in many cases the former implies the later. Indeed, it is a direct consequence of Corollary J of
[
LCT22
]
, improving on both a theorem by Handel (
[
Han92
]
) and previous work by the same authors (
[
LCT18
]
), that every transitive homeomorphism of the
2
-sphere with three distinct periodic orbits is topologically chaotic. Note that this is optimal in some ways. There are transitive
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
having only two fixed points, and every non-wandering homeomorphism of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
has at least two fixed points.

Our goal here is to try and extend the previous result, as best as possible, to different surfaces and in particular to
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Since this paper is concerned with continuous dynamics without any other smoothness requirement, let us introduce a purely topological concept of horseshoe, a variation of the one proposed by Kennedy-Yorke (
[
KY01
]
) that appeared in (
[
LCT22
]
).

Definition 1.1

(Topological Horseshoe)
.

Given
X
a Hausdorff locally compact topological space and
f:X\to X
a homeomorphism on
X
, we say that a compact subset
\Delta
of
X
is a
topological horseshoe for
f
if
\Delta
is invariant by a power
f^{q}
of
f
and if there exist a Hausdorff compact space
Y
, a continuous map
g:Y\to Y
, a integer
r\geq 2
and two continuous and surjective maps (factor maps)
\pi_{1}:Y\to\Delta
and
\pi_{2}:Y\to\Sigma
where
\Sigma=\{0,\cdots,r-1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that:

(1)

the factor map
\pi_{1}
is a semi-conjugation between
g
and
f^{q}|_{\Delta}
, where fibers of
\pi_{1}
are all finite with a uniform bound in their cardinality;

(2)

the factor map
\pi_{2}
is a semi-conjugation between
{g}
and the Bernoulli shift,
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
;

(3)

The preimage of every
n
-periodic sequence of
\Sigma
, by the factor map
\pi_{2}
, contains a
n
-periodic point of
{g}
.

It is an immediate consequence of Definition
1.1
that any homeomorphism of a compact metric space possessing a topological horseshoe has both strictly positive topological entropy, and also that some power of the map has exponential growth of periodic points, so that the system is topologically chaotic.

In this paper we investigate to what extent does transitivity imply the existence of a topological horseshoe for homeomorphisms of the
2
-torus.

There are of course examples of transitive torus homeomorphims without periodic points, like ergodic rotations, but there are even examples of transitive torus homeomorphisms that have positive topological entropy and no periodic points (
[
Ree81
]
). On the other hand, the same kind of restriction found for sphere homeomorphisms does not hold. One can build examples of
2
-torus homeomorphims with arbitrarily many fixed point that are transitive and smooth, and with zero entropy. This can be done by taking the time one map of a flow of the type
x\prime=\phi(x)v
, where
v
is a totally irrational vector, and
\phi:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to[0,1]
is null at a given finite set of points. The zeros of
\phi
will be fixed points of the dynamics and careful choice of
\phi
will also yield that the dynamic is transitive. Furthermore, a similar procedure could be done and one could obtain that the dynamics is even area-preserving, see
[
TAZ07
]
.

Further complicating the picture for torus homeomorphisms is that these do not all belong to the same isotopy class. The standard classification of isotopy classes of homeomorphims of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
says that, if
f
is in
\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, then
f
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

A power of
f
is isotopic to the identity,

(2)

A power of
f
is isotopic to a Dehn twist,

(3)

f
is isotopic to a linear Anosov homeomorphism.

It is a well known fact that any homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
that is isotopic to a linear Anosov map has a topological horseshoe. So we concentrate on the two models cases for the other situations.

For the homotopic to identity case we have the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
f
be a transitive homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and let
g
be a power of
f
such that
g
is isotopic to the identity. If
g
has both a fixed point and a non-fixed periodic point, then
f
has a topological horseshoe.

Note that Theorem A also holds if we just assume that
g
has two periodic points of different periods, instead of assuming it has a fixed. Indeed, let
z_{0},z_{1}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
be
q_{0},q_{1}
-periodic points, respectively, such
q_{0},q_{1}>1
with
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
. If
h:=g^{q_{0}}
then
h
satisfy the hypothesis of the Theorem A and so we get a horseshoe for
f
.

The previous theorem and examples dealt with homeomorphisms homotopic to the identity. For homeomorphisms homotopic to a Dehn twist case, we also have transitive examples without periodic points. For instance, a well known example is the map
(x,y)\to(x+y,y+\alpha)
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, which is a minimal homeomorphism. Our next result proves that a transitive homeomorphism homotopic to a Dehn twist is either aperiodic or it has a topological horseshoe.

Theorem B
.

Let
f
be a transitive homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and let
g
be a power of
f
such that
g
is isotopic to a Dehn twist. If
g
has a periodic point, then
f
has a topological horseshoe.

The main tools involved in this paper are Rotation Theory and Forcing Theory for Surface Homeomorphisms. The first one we discuss on Section
2
, as well as some other basic dynamical lemmas. In Section
3
we discuss some particular results from Forcing Theory, the main new tools developed recently that allow us to obtain the main results of this paper. Section
4
and
5
we present the proofs of Theorem A and Theorem B, respectively.

Let

be the space of homeomorphisms defined on

\alpha_{f}(z)=\bigcap_{k\leq 0}\overline{\left\{f^{n}(z)\;|\;n\leq k\right\}},\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\omega_{f}(z)=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\overline{\left\{f^{n}(z)\;|\;n\geq k\right\}}.

We say that a point
z\in S

or

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}(S)
. Take any integer
q\geq 1
and let
g=f^{q}
. If there is a point
z\in S
such that
\omega_{f}(z)=S
then

\bigcup_{r=0}^{q-1}\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))=S.

Proof.

Let
w\in S
be any point. As
\omega_{f}(z)=S
then
w\in\omega_{f}(z)
. So,
there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that
n_{k}\nearrow+\infty
and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}f^{n_{k}}(z)=w.

As
n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
r_{k}\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
such that
n_{k}=m_{k}q+r_{k}
. Then, we have

f^{n_{k}}(z)=f^{m_{k}q+r_{k}}(z)=g^{m_{k}}(f^{r_{k}}(z)),\;\mbox{for each}\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

By the Pigeonhole Principle there exists a subsequence
(r_{k_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
such that
r_{k_{i}}=r
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. So, if we consider the subsequence

\left(g^{m_{k_{i}}}(f^{r_{k_{i}}}(z))\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=\left(g^{m_{k_{i}}}(f^{r}(z))\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\left(g^{m_{k}}(f^{r_{k}}(z))\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}},

as a subsequence of a convergent sequence also converges to the same limit of it, we have

\lim_{i\to+\infty}g^{m_{k_{i}}}(f^{r}(z))=\lim_{k\to+\infty}g^{m_{k}}(f^{r_{k}}(z))=\lim_{k\to+\infty}f^{n_{k}}(z)=w.

Therefore, there exists some
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
such that
w\in\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))
, or equivalently,
w\in\bigcup_{r=0}^{q-1}\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))
as we wanted.
∎

The following definition is about Birkhoff recurrence classes.

Let

Rotation theory for 2-torus homeomorphism

Given a surface
S

\operatorname{Homeo}_{0}(S)
the subspace of
\operatorname{Homeo}(S)

Let

\check{\pi}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}

\mathbb{T}^{2}

Misiurewicz and Ziemian in
[

defined the rotation set of a lift
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of

\rho(\check{f})

\left(\frac{\check{f}^{n_{k}}(\check{z}_{k})-\check{z}_{k}}{n_{k}}\right)_{k\geq 1},

If a point

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\dfrac{\check{f}^{n}(\check{z})-\check{z}}{n}\;\;\mbox{exists,}

\check{z}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}(z)

\rho(z,\check{f})
is denoted as the limit above and it is called the rotation vector of

.

We will say that

for any
\check{z}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}(z)
.

\dfrac{Av}{||Av||}=\pm(1,0)\vskip 2.84544pt
, which is a horizontal segment, and by Lemma
2.7
, we have that
\rho(A\check{f}A^{-1})=A\rho(f)
.
∎

Let
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we say that the
deviations in the direction of some nonzero vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are uniformly bounded
, if there exists a real number
M>0
such that

\left|\operatorname{p}_{v}(\check{f}^{n}(\check{z})-\check{z})\right|\leq M,\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\forall\check{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\operatorname{p}_{v}(\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\frac{v}{||v||}\rangle
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. We say that
f
is
annular
if there is an integer
q\geq 1
and a lift
\check{g}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of
g=f^{q}
such that the deviations in the direction of some nonzero integer vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are uniformly bounded.

The next theorem, which we use often in this work, is due to Dávalos, improving on a similar result for non-wandering homeomorphisms by Guelman-Koropecki-Tal (
[
GKT14
]
):

Theorem 2.11

(Theorem A in
[
Dáv18
]
)
.

If
\rho(\check{f})
is a non-degenerate line segment with rational slope containing rational points, then
f
is annular in the direction perpendicular to
\rho(\check{f})
.

As a consequence of this theorem we have the following:

Corollary 2.12
.

If
\rho(\check{f})
is a non-degenerate line segment with rational slope containing the origin then there is a lift
\check{f}
of
f
which has uniformly bounded deviations in the direction perpendicular to
\rho(\check{f})
.

Proof.

Let
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
be a multiple of the direction of
\rho(\check{f})
and let
v^{\perp}=(-v_{2},v_{1})
.
By Theorem
2.11
as
\rho(\check{f})
is a non-degenerate line segment with rational slope containing the origin then there is an integer
q\geq 1
and a lift
\check{g}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
g=f^{q}
such that the deviations in the direction perpendicular to
\rho(\check{f})
are uniformly bounded, that is

\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{g}^{n}(\check{z})-\check{z})\right|\leq M,\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\forall\check{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

which, in particular, implies that
\rho(\check{g})
is a line segment in the direction of
v
.

The lift
\check{g}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of
g=f^{q}
can be considered as
\check{g}=\check{f}^{q}
. Otherwise there would be
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
such that
\check{g}=\check{f}^{q}+p
has uniformly bounded deviations in the direction perpendicular to the rotation set. But as, by assumption,
(0,0)\in\rho(\check{f})
, and since
\rho(\check{g})=q\rho(\check{f})+p
, we have that
p
is in
\rho(\check{g})
, which implies that in this case, we must have that
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\{\lambda v\;|\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

So, we have that
p=\lambda v
, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. And, as
\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\lambda v)=0
, there is no loss of generality supposing that
\check{g}=\check{f}^{q}
.

As for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
such that
n=mq+r
and as
\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
is finite we must have

\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{f}^{r}(\check{w})-\check{w})\right|\leq M_{0}
for some constant
M_{0}>0
, for all
r\in\{0,1\cdots,q-1\}
. So

\begin{array}[]{rl}\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{f}^{n}(\check{w})-\check{w})\right|=&\hskip-5.69046pt\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{f}^{mq}(\check{f}^{r}(\check{w}))-\check{w})\right|\\
\leq&\hskip-5.69046pt\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{g}^{m}(\check{f}^{r}(\check{w}))-\check{f}^{r}(\check{w}))\right|+\left|\operatorname{p}_{v^{\perp}}(\check{f}^{r}(\check{w})-\check{w})\right|\\
\leq&M+M_{0},\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\forall\check{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{array}

Which means that
\check{f}
has uniformly bounded deviations in the direction perpendicular to
\rho(\check{f})
.
∎

2.3.
Singular oriented foliations

Let
S
be an oriented surface. A
path
is a continuous map
\gamma:J\to S
where
J\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval. From now on, in absence of ambiguity, we will denoted the image of
\gamma
also as
\gamma
and call it by path. We will denote
\gamma^{-1}:-J\to S
the path defined by
\gamma^{-1}(t)=\gamma(-t)
. If
X
and
Y
are two disjoint subset of
S
we will say that a path
\gamma:[a,b]\to S

joins
X
to
Y
if
\gamma(a)\in X
and
\gamma(b)\in Y
. Moreover, if
\gamma:[a,b]\to S
and
\gamma^{\prime}:[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\to S
are two paths such that
\gamma(b)=\gamma^{\prime}(a^{\prime})
then we can concatenate the two paths and define the path
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}:[a,b+b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}]\to S

\gamma\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma(t)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in[a,b];\\
\gamma^{\prime}(t-b+a^{\prime})&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in[b,b+b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}]\;.\end{cases}

A path
\gamma:J\to S
is
proper
if
J
is open and the preimage of every compact subset of
S
is compact. If
S=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a
line
is an injective and proper path
\phi:J\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it inherits a natural orientation induced by the usual orientation on
\mathbb{R}
. We already know, by Jordan-Schoenflies Theorem, that the complement of a line
\phi
has two connected components. We will denote
R(\phi)
for the one that is on the right of
\phi
and
L(\phi)
for the other one that is on the left of it.

A path
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to S
such that
\gamma(t+1)=\gamma(t)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
lifts a continuous map
\Gamma:\mathbb{T}^{1}\to S
. We will say that
\Gamma
is a
loop
and
\gamma
is its
natural lift
. When the map
\Gamma
is also injective then
\Gamma
will be called
simple loop
. If
n\geq 1
, we denote
\Gamma^{n}
the loop lifted by the path
t\mapsto\gamma(nt)
, and we will say that
\Gamma^{n}
is a
multiple
of the loop
\Gamma
.

By a
singular oriented foliation
on an oriented surface
S
we mean a closed set
\operatorname{sing}({\mathcal{F}})
, called the
set of singularities
, together with a topological oriented foliation
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
on the complement of
\operatorname{sing}({\mathcal{F}})
, that we will denote by
\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}}):=S\backslash\operatorname{sing}({\mathcal{F}})
and call it
domain of
{\mathcal{F}}
.

If
\operatorname{sing}({\mathcal{F}})
is an empty set we will say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is a non-singular oriented foliation.

By Whitney’s Theorem, see
[
Whi33
]
and
[
Whi41
]
, any singular oriented foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
can be embedded in a
flow, which means that
{\mathcal{F}}
is the set of oriented orbits of some topological flow
\Phi:S\times\mathbb{R}\to S
, where the singularities of
{\mathcal{F}}
coincides with the set of the fixed points of
\Phi
. Thus we can define the
\omega
-limit and
\alpha
-limit sets of a leaf
\phi
as follows: if
\phi
is a leaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
z\in\phi
then

\alpha(\phi)=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\overline{\left\{\Phi(z,t)\;|\;t\leq n\right\}},\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\omega(\phi)=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\overline{\left\{\Phi(z,t)\;|\;t\geq n\right\}}.

We will denote
\phi^{-}_{z}
as the negative half-leaf and
\phi^{+}_{z}
as the positive half-leaf from the point
z
, which means that if
\phi_{z}=\Phi(z,0)
then

\phi^{-}_{z}=\left\{\Phi(z,t);\;t<0\right\},\;\mbox{and}\;\phi^{+}_{z}=\left\{\Phi(z,t);\;t>0\right\}.

If
\breve{S}
is the universal covering space of
S
and
{\breve{\pi}}:\breve{S}\to S
a universal covering projection then
{\mathcal{F}}
can be naturally lifted to a singular foliation
\breve{{\mathcal{F}}}
of
\breve{S}
such that
\operatorname{dom}(\breve{{\mathcal{F}}})={\breve{\pi}}^{-1}(\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}}))
.

We will denote
\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})
as the universal covering space of
\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})
,
\tilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})\to\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})
the universal covering projection and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
the non-singular oriented foliation of
\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})
lifted from
{\mathcal{F}}|_{\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})}
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a singular oriented foliation on an oriented surface
S
.

Definition 2.15
.

We will say that a path
\gamma:J\to\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})
is
positively transverse
to
{\mathcal{F}}
(in the whole text we will say just
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse) if for every
t_{0}\in J
there exist a neighborhood
W
of
\gamma(t_{0})
and an orientation preserving continuous map
h:W\to(0,1)^{2}
that maps the restricted foliation
{\mathcal{F}}|_{W}
onto the oriented foliation by vertical lines oriented downwards such that the first coordinate of the map
t\mapsto h(\gamma(t))
is strictly increasing in a neighborhood of
t_{0}
.

Note that an
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse path
\gamma
does not contain any singularity and each intersection of
\gamma
with a leaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
is topologically transverse and cross the leaf locally “from right to left”.

We need to define an equivalence relation between
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse paths.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
\gamma:J\to\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})
and
\gamma^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\to\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{F}})
be two
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse paths. We will say that
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are
{\mathcal{F}}
-
equivalent
if they can be lifted to
\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})
into paths
\tilde{\gamma}:J\to\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})
and
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\to\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}({\mathcal{F}})
that meet exactly the same leaves.

Definition 2.17
.

An
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse path
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to S
will be called
{\mathcal{F}}
-positively recurrent
if for every interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
there exists an interval
J_{0}\subset[t,+\infty)
such that
\gamma|_{J_{0}}
is equivalent to
\gamma|_{J}
. It will be called
{\mathcal{F}}
-negatively recurrent
if for every interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
there exists an interval
J_{0}\subset(-\infty,t]
such that
\gamma|_{J_{0}}
is equivalent to
\gamma|_{J}
. It is
{\mathcal{F}}
-bi-recurrent
if it is both
{\mathcal{F}}
-positively and
{\mathcal{F}}
-negatively recurrent.

3.
Forcing theory for surface homeomorphisms

Forcing theory for surface homeomorphisms is based on Brouwer theory and the famous Brouwer translation theorem. This theorem says that given
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
fixed point free, that is called
Brouwer’s homeomorphisms
, then for all
\check{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there is a line
\check{\phi}
such that
\check{z}\in\check{\phi}
and

\check{f}^{-1}(\check{\phi})\subset R(\check{\phi})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\check{f}(\check{\phi})\subset L(\check{\phi}),

this line is called
Brouwer’s line
.

To relate forcing theory with Brouwer’s theory we use a consequence of the Equivariant Foliation Theorem due to Le Calvez (see
[
LC05
]
). For do this we must introduce Maximal Isotopies.

3.1.
Maximal Isotopy, Equivariant Foliation

Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
be the space of identity isotopies of
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}_{0}(f)
. That is,
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
is the set of continuous paths defined on
[0,1]
joining the identity to
f
in the space
\operatorname{Homeo}(S)
, furnished with the
C^{0}
topology.

If
I=(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
, we define the
trajectory of the point
z\in S
along
I
as the path

I(z):t\mapsto I(z)(t)=f_{t}(z).

In general, we can define by concatenation
I^{n}(z)=\prod_{0\leq m<n}I(f^{m}(z))
for every integer
n\geq 1
. Furthermore, we can define

I^{\mathbb{N}}(z)=\prod_{m\geq 0}I(f^{m}(z)),\;I^{-\mathbb{N}}(z)=\prod_{m\leq 0}I(f^{m}(z)),\;I^{\mathbb{Z}}(x)=\prod_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}I(f^{m}(z)),

as the future, past and whole trajectory of a point
z\in S
along
I
, respectively.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}_{0}(S)
and
I\in{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
, we define the
fixed point set of
I
as the set
\operatorname{fix}(I)=\bigcap_{t\in[0,1]}\operatorname{fix}(f_{t}).
If
z\in\operatorname{fix}(I)
then
z
is called
fixed point for the isotopy
I
.

The complement set of
\operatorname{fix}(I)
in
S
,
S\backslash\operatorname{fix}(I)
will be called domain of
I
and will be denoted by
\operatorname{dom}(I)
, that is itself a surface.

We can define a partial order on
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
as follows: given
I^{\prime}
and
I
two elements of
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
, we say that
I^{\prime}\preceq I
if

(1)

\operatorname{fix}(I^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{fix}(I)
and

(2)

I
is homotopic to
I^{\prime}
relative to
\operatorname{fix}(I^{\prime})
.

Thus, we say that
I
is a maximal isotopy of
f
, if it is maximal for the partial order above.

An important result regarding existence of maximal isotopy is due Béguin, Crovisier and Le Roux in
[
BCLR20
]
:

Theorem 3.2

(Maximal Isotopy, see Corollary 1.3 in
[
BCLR20
]
)
.

Let
f:S\to S
be a homeomorphism isotopic to identity, then for every
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{f}
, there exists
I\in\mathcal{I}_{f}
such that
I^{\prime}\preceq I
and
I
is maximal for the partial order above.

An isotopy
I
is maximal if and only if, for every
z\in\operatorname{fix}(f)\setminus\operatorname{fix}(I)
, the closed curve
I(z)
is not contractible in
\operatorname{dom}(I)
. So, if
\tilde{I}=(\tilde{f}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is the identity isotopy that lifts
I|_{\operatorname{dom}(I)}
to the universal covering space
\widetilde{\operatorname{dom}}(I)
of
\operatorname{dom}(I)
then
\tilde{f}_{1}
has no fixed point, which implies that each connected component of
\tilde{S}
is homeomorphic to the plane. And therefore, as a consequence of the Equivariant Foliation Theorem, due to Le Calvez (see
[
LC05
]
), we have:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
f:S\to S
be a homeomorphism isotopic to identity of a surface
S
,
I=(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
a maximal identity isotopy on
S
such
f_{1}:=f
then there exists a singular oriented foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
S
such that
\operatorname{sing}({\mathcal{F}})=\operatorname{fix}(I)
and for all point
z\in\operatorname{dom}(I)
there is a positively
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse path
\gamma
that joins
z
to
f(z)
and is homotopic to
I(z)
on
\operatorname{dom}(I)
, with the endpoints fixed.

The foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfying the above conclusion is said to be
transverse to
I
.

3.2.
Transverse Trajectories and
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse intersection

Note that the path
\gamma
, given by Corollary
3.3
, is not uniquely defined.
We will write
I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)
for the set of paths
\gamma:[a,b]\to\operatorname{dom}(I)
defined on a compact interval
[a,b]
such that
\gamma(a)=z,\,\gamma(b)=f(z)
,
\gamma
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse and
\gamma
is homotopic to
I(z)
in
\operatorname{dom}(I)
, relative to the endpoints. We will also abuse the notation and use
I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)
for any representative in this class and call it the
transverse trajectory of

. As before, we can define by concatenation
I^{n}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\prod_{0\leq m<n}I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f^{m}(z))
for every integer

. Similarly we define

I^{\mathbb{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\prod_{m\geq 0}I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f^{m}(z)),\;I^{-\mathbb{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\prod_{m\leq 0}I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f^{m}(z)),\;I^{\mathbb{Z}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(x)=\prod_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f^{m}(z)),

and the last one will be called the

.

Our first goal is to show the existence of topological horseshoes for transitive homeomorphisms of

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

be topologically transitive with a non-empty fixed point set. If there is an integer

such that the power
g=f^{q}
of

\check{g}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
of

Before proving this proposition, let’s prove some useful results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Suppose that
h
admits a lift
\hat{h}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{A})
to the annulus. Moreover, suppose that

(1)

The map
\hat{h}
commutes with the integer vertical translations, that is,

\hat{h}(\hat{w}+(0,p))=\hat{h}(\hat{w})+(0,p),\;\forall p\in\mathbb{Z}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\hat{w}\in\mathbb{A};

(2)

There is a real number
M>0
such that for all
\hat{w}\in\mathbb{A}
we have

\left|\operatorname{p}_{2}(\hat{h}^{n}(\hat{w})-\hat{w})\right|\leq M,\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, if
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
are such that
w\in\omega_{h}(w^{\prime})
, then for every
\hat{w}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(w)
there exists some
\hat{w}^{\prime}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(w^{\prime})
such that
\hat{w}\in\omega_{\hat{h}}(\hat{w}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
be as in the hypothesis. Choose some
\hat{w}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(w)
, then there is a fundamental domain
\hat{D}=\mathbb{T}^{1}\times[d,d+1)
in
\mathbb{A}
that contains
\hat{w}
. Moreover, for all
\hat{u}\in\hat{D}
we have

(I)

d\leq\operatorname{p}_{2}(\hat{u})<d+1.

As
w\in\omega_{h}(w^{\prime})
we may assume, possibly by taking a subsequence, that

d_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(h^{m_{k}}(w^{\prime}),w)<1/k\,\,,\;\mbox{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

As
\hat{D}
is a fundamental domain, the above property and assumption (1) imply that there exist
\hat{w}^{\prime}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(w^{\prime})\cap\hat{D}
and a sequence of integers
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that

(II)

d_{\mathbb{A}}(\hat{h}^{m_{k}}(\hat{w}^{\prime})+(0,p_{k}),\hat{w})<1/k\,\,,\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

So, it follows from (
I
) and (
II
) that

(III)

d-\frac{1}{k}<\operatorname{p}_{2}(\hat{h}^{m_{k}}(\hat{w}^{\prime}))+p_{k}<d+1+\frac{1}{k},\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Furthermore, assumption (2) and inequality (
I
) applied to this
\hat{w}^{\prime}\in\hat{\pi}^{-1}(w^{\prime})\cap\hat{D}
give us that

(IV)

d-M<\operatorname{p}_{2}(\hat{h}^{m_{k}}(\hat{w}^{\prime}))<d+1+M.

Finally, (
III
) and (
IV
) imply, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, that

\begin{array}[]{rl}-d-1-M+d-\dfrac{1}{k}<p_{k}<-d+M+d+1+\dfrac{1}{k}\Rightarrow&|p_{k}|<M+1+\dfrac{1}{k}<M+2\end{array}

because we have that
k\in\mathbb{N}
and then
1/k\leq 1
.

This means that the discrete set
\{p_{k}\;|\;{k\in\mathbb{N}}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
is finite. Indeed the cardinality of
\{p_{k}\;|\;k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, namely
\#\{p_{k}\;|\;k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is less than or equal to
2\lfloor M+2\rfloor+1
, where
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the largest integer less than or equal to the positive real number
x
.

Then, by the Pigeonhole Principle, there is some fixed
p_{0}
in
\mathbb{Z}
and a subsequence
(p_{k_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
p_{k_{i}}\equiv p_{0}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

So,

d_{\mathbb{A}}(\hat{h}^{m_{k_{i}}}(\hat{w}^{\prime}+(0,p_{0})),\hat{w})<1/k_{i},\;\forall i\in\mathbb{N}

and this imply that
\hat{w}\in\omega_{h}(\hat{w}^{\prime}+(0,p_{0}))
.
∎

Theorem A
.

Let
f
be a transitive homeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and let
g
be a power of
f
such that
g
is isotopic to the identity. If
g
has both a fixed point and a non-fixed periodic point, then
f
has a topological horseshoe.

Proof.
Let
g=f^{q}\in\operatorname{Homeo}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
be a fixed point for
g
, that is
z_{0}\in\operatorname{fix}(g)
;
q_{1}>1
be the minimal period of a non-fixed periodic point
z_{1}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
for
g
, that is
z_{1}\in\operatorname{fix}(g^{q_{1}})\setminus\operatorname{fix}(g)
.

As
f
is transitive, let
z\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
be a point such that
\omega_{f}(z)=\alpha_{f}(z)=\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Then by Lemma
2.1
we have

\bigcup_{r=0}^{q-1}\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))=\bigcup_{r=0}^{q-1}\alpha_{g}(f^{-r}(z))=\mathbb{T}^{2}.

So, there are some
r,s\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
such that
z\in\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))
and
z\in\alpha_{g}(f^{-s}(z))
. But this imply that
z\in\omega_{g}(z)\cap\alpha_{g}(z)
. We will prove this fact for the
\omega
-limit, for
\alpha
-limit the proof is analogous.

Note that if
z\in\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))
then

f^{r}(z)\in\omega_{g}(f^{2r}(z))\Rightarrow\;\cdots\Rightarrow f^{(q-1)r}(z)\in\omega_{g}(f^{qr}(z)).

This last
\omega_{g}
-limit set is equal to
\omega_{g}(z)
, because
g=f^{q}
and it is
g
-invariant. So, we have

z\in\omega_{g}(f^{r}(z))\subset\omega_{g}(f^{2r}(z))\subset\cdots\subset\omega_{g}(z),

because is a fact that if
x\in\omega_{g}(y)
then
\omega_{g}(x)\subset\omega_{g}(y)
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{g}
be the set of identity isotopies for
g
, pick
I_{0}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{g}
such that
z_{0}\in\operatorname{fix}(I_{0})
and let
I\in{\mathcal{I}}_{g}
be a maximal isotopy for
g
such that
I_{0}\preceq I
.

Let
\check{I}=(\check{g}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be an identity isotopy that lifts
I
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\check{g}:=\check{g}_{1}
be the lift of
g
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by the isotopy
I
.

As
z_{1}\in\operatorname{fix}(g^{q_{1}})\setminus\operatorname{fix}(g)
with
q_{1}>1
we know that the trajectory of
z_{1}
by the isotopy
I
until order
q_{1}
,
I^{q_{1}}(z_{1})
, is a loop on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Then we have two possibilities for it:

(1)

I^{q_{1}}(z_{1})
is homotopic to zero on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
;

(2)

Or not, which means that
I^{q_{1}}(z_{1})
is an essential loop on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

We will proceed with the proof of the theorem considering these two cases separately. First we will show for case (2) and then for case (1).

4.1.
Essential loop on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

Suppose that
I^{q_{1}}(z_{1})
is an essential loop on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
then there exists
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
such that

\check{g}^{q_{1}}(\check{z}_{1})=\check{z}_{1}+(p_{1},p_{2}),\;\;\forall\check{z}_{1}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}(z_{1}).

Moreover,
z_{0}\in\operatorname{fix}(I)
implies
\check{g}(\check{z}_{0})=\check{z}_{0},
for any
\check{z}_{0}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}(z_{0}).

This implies that
\rho(z_{0},\check{g})=(0,0)
and
\rho(z_{1},\check{g})=\frac{1}{q_{1}}(p_{1},p_{2})
. And as
(p_{1},p_{2})\neq(0,0)
we must have that
\rho(\check{g})
has non-empty interior or it is a non-degenerate line segment of rational slope.

(1)

If
\operatorname{int}(\rho({\check{g}}))\neq\emptyset
then Theorem 1 due to Llibre and Mackay (
[
LM91
]
) together with the fact that all rational vectors in
\operatorname{int}(\rho({\check{g}}))
are realized, due to Franks
[
Fra89
]
, imply that
g
has a topological horseshoe.

(2)

If
\rho(\check{g})
is a segment of rational slope containing
(0,0)
and
\frac{1}{q_{1}}(p_{1},p_{2})
, and moreover these vectors are realized by
z_{0},z_{1}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, respectively, then Proposition
4.1
implies that
f
has a topological horseshoe.

4.2.
Loop homotopic to zero on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

Before proving this case, we present an easy result on closed transversal sections to topological flows on the plane that helps us to show that the set of leaves of an
{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse loop homotopic to zero in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is an inessential set that we prove next.

Definition 4.4

(see
[
KT14
]
)
.

An open subset
U
of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is

if every loop in

is homotopic to zero in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Otherwise,

is

. An arbitrary set

of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is called inessential if it has some inessential neighborhood. And, finally,

is
fully essential
if

is inessential.

It follows immediately from the definition that if
U\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
is open and connected then

is inessential if and only if, for every
\check{U}\subset\check{\pi}^{-1}(U)
,
\check{U}\cap\left(\check{U}+(p_{1},p_{2})\right)=\emptyset
for any
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
.

Let
\check{\mathcal{F}}
be a singular oriented foliation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
{\check{\Gamma}}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\to\operatorname{dom}(\check{\mathcal{F}})
be an
\check{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse simple loop on
\operatorname{dom}(\check{\mathcal{F}})
and
\check{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}\to\operatorname{dom}(\check{\mathcal{F}})
its natural lift. We will denote by
\operatorname{B}_{\operatorname{c}}({\check{\Gamma}})
and
\operatorname{UB}_{\operatorname{c}}({\check{\Gamma}})

, respectively.

Let

be the open topological annulus of the leaves that are crossed by
{\check{\Gamma}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see Proposition

). Similarly, we will denote

and

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\check{\Gamma}
be an
\check{\mathcal{F}}
-transverse simple loop in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\check{\gamma}
its natural lift and

\check{\Gamma}

\mathbb{R}^{2}

(2)

\check{\phi}\subset\check{U}
, one has that
\alpha(\check{\phi})\neq\emptyset
,
\alpha(\check{\phi})\subset\operatorname{B}_{\operatorname{c}}(\check{U})

Proof.

Fix, arbitrarily, a leaf
\check{\phi}

If

By Poincaré-Bendixson Theorem, as

is a compact set, neither
\omega(\check{\phi})\not\subset\check{U}
nor
\alpha(\check{\phi})\not\subset\check{U}
, because there is no singularities nor closed leaves contained in

. Therefore

Let

be any other leaf such that

and
\check{\gamma}(t^{\prime})=z^{\prime}
, where
\check{\gamma}
is the natural lift of
\check{\Gamma}
. By transversality of the path
\check{\Gamma}
we note that for every

is contained in

, then the same holds for every

sufficiently close to

. Since

is a compact interval, we get that

. Which implies, in an analogous way, that case (1) holds for leaf

.

Therefore we conclude that if

for some leaf

then case (1) holds for all other leaves contained in
\check{U}
.

The case (2) is shown in an analogous way, assuming that
\check{\phi}^{-}_{z}\subset\operatorname{B}_{\operatorname{c}}(\check{\Gamma})
and
\check{\phi}^{+}_{z}\subset\operatorname{UB}_{\operatorname{c}}(\check{\Gamma})
happens. ∎

5.2.
If
\rho_{V}(\hat{h})
is a non-degenerate compact interval of
\mathbb{R}

The existence of a horseshoe for this case is already known, althought we could not find it explicitly written in the literature. In any case it follows as a scholium of Theorem 3.1 due to Doeff and Misiurewicz in
[
DM97
]
together with the techniques developed by Llibre and Mackay in
[

]

is a

diffeomorphism has already been proved by Addas-Zanata in

AZ15
]
.

In the following we will give a new proof for the existence of a topological horseshoe for this case only using Forcing Theory and Rotation Theory.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\hat{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{A})
be isotopic to the identity and let
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be one of its lifts. We say that:

(1)

\hat{f}
satisfies the
infinity twist condition (ITC)
if for any lift
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the following property holds:

\operatorname{p}_{1}\circ\check{f}(\check{x},\check{y})\to\pm\infty\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;\check{y}\to\pm\infty.

(2)

(\hat{f},\check{f})
has a periodic orbit of rotation number
\frac{r}{s}
, if there exists
\hat{z}\in\mathbb{A}
such that
\hat{f}^{s}(\hat{z})=\hat{z}
and
\check{f}^{s}(\check{z})=\check{z}+(r,0)
for all
\check{z}\in\check{\tau}^{-1}(\hat{z})
.

Note that if
\hat{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{A})
is a lift of
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
homeomorphism isotopic to a Dehn twist map induced by the special matrix
A
then
\hat{f}
is isotopic to identity (as explained before) and satisfies ITC property. Indeed, if
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a lift of
\hat{f}
, then
\check{f}
is also a lift of
f
satisfying

\check{f}(\check{z}+(p_{1},p_{2}))=\check{f}(\check{z})+(p_{1}+mp_{2},p_{2}),

where
m\geq 1
is given by the Dehn Twist map.

Theorem 5.7

(Theorem

at
[
AZ05
]
)
.

Let
\hat{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{A})
be isotopic to the identity and let
\check{f}\in\operatorname{Homeo}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be some lift of
f
. Suppose
\hat{f}
satisfies the ITC property and there exist points

\begin{array}[]{cl}\operatorname{p}_{2}\circ\hat{f}^{n}(\hat{z}_{1})\to\mp\infty&\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\pm\infty\;\;\mbox{and}\\
\operatorname{p}_{2}\circ\hat{f}^{n}(\hat{z}_{2})\to\pm\infty&\;\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\pm\infty,\\
\end{array}

then for all rational numbers
\frac{r}{s}
,
(\hat{f},\check{f})
has at least two periodic orbits with rotation number equal to
\frac{r}{s}
.

\end{array}

Fractional derivatives are defined by means of analytical continuation of the Cauchy’s formula for the multiple integral of an

(2)

\frac{d^{\gamma}}{dt^{\gamma}}f(t)={{}^{C}}_{0}D^{\gamma}_{t}f(t)=I^{n-\gamma}\,_{0}D_{t}^{n}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f^{(n)}(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\gamma-n+1}}d\tau,

with

\displaystyle{{}^{C}}_{0}D_{t}^{\gamma}[f(t)+g(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{}^{C}}_{0}D_{t}^{\gamma}f(t)+{{}^{C}}_{0}D_{t}^{\gamma}g(t),

(4)

\displaystyle{{}^{C}}_{0}D_{t}^{\gamma}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad{where}\,\,c\,\,{is\,\,a\,\,constant}.

(5)

The Laplace transform of the function

Then, the Laplace transform of the Caputo fractional derivative

Appendix C
Equivalence between Lagrangian densities

The canonical Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{canonical}(q_{i},\Pi_{i},t)
(
32
) in gravitation theories is obtained from
the usual Lagrangian density
{\cal L}(q_{i},\dot{q}_{i},t)

(
29
), rewritten the velocities
\dot{q}_{i}
in term of the
momenta
\Pi_{i}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{q}_{i}}
to the
corresponding coordinate field
q_{i}
. With this procedure,
the canonical Lagrangian density appear directly written as a
Lagrangian density in constrained systems, where the Lagrangian
multiplier is the lapse function
N(t)
, being the corresponding
gauge parameter in this theory. This is equivalent to using the
canonical transformation where the hamiltonian density is
{\cal H}=\Pi_{j}\dot{q}^{j}-{\cal L}
. However, from this point of view,
in this expression the
{\cal H}
must be interpreted as
{\cal H}=N{\cal H}_{canonical}
where the lapse function
N
appears as a
lagrangian multiplier. In the following we realize this calculation,
employing the usual canonical transformation.
We have the momenta

\displaystyle\Pi_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{12}{N}e^{3\Omega}\dot{\Omega},\quad\rightarrow\quad\dot{\Omega}=\frac{N}{12}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\Omega},

(77)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{2\alpha}{N^{2\alpha-1}}e^{3\Omega}{\dot{\phi}}^{2\alpha-1},\quad\rightarrow\quad\dot{\phi}=-N\left[\frac{2^{\alpha}}{2\alpha}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\phi}\right]^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}},

(78)

substituting into the canonical transformation between the
Hamiltonian density and Lagrangian density

{\cal H}=\Pi_{j}\dot{q}^{j}-{\cal L}

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi_{\Omega}\dot{\Omega}+\Pi_{\phi}\dot{\phi}-e^{3\Omega}\left[6\frac{\dot{\Omega}^{2}}{N}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\dot{\phi}\right)^{2\alpha}N^{-2\alpha+1}\right]

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi_{\Omega}\left(\frac{N}{12}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\Omega}\right)+\Pi_{\phi}\left(-N\left[\frac{2^{\alpha}}{2\alpha}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\phi}\right]^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-e^{3\Omega}\left[\frac{6}{N}\left(\frac{N}{12}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\Omega},\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha}\left(-N\left[\frac{2^{\alpha}}{2\alpha}e^{-3\Omega}\Pi_{\phi}\right]^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\right)^{2\alpha}N^{-2\alpha+1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ne^{3\Omega}\Pi_{\Omega}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{24}\right)-Ne^{-\frac{3\Omega}{2\alpha-1}}\left(\frac{2^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\Pi_{\phi}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle+Ne^{-\frac{3\Omega}{2\alpha-1}}\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\frac{2^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\Pi_{\phi}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\frac{e^{-3\Omega}}{24}\Pi_{\Omega}^{2}-Ne^{-\frac{3\Omega}{2\alpha-1}}\left(\frac{2^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\left[\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\right]\Pi_{\phi}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\frac{e^{-3\Omega}}{24}\Pi_{\Omega}^{2}-Ne^{-\frac{3\Omega}{2\alpha-1}}\,\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}\left(\frac{2^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\Pi_{\phi}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}

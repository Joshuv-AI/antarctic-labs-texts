Abstract
. We give conditions for regularity of solutions of three dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations based on the pressure and on structure functions.



On the occasion of the centennial anniversary of O. A. Ladyzhenskaya

1.
Introduction

We consider solutions of incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, with smooth and localized initial
data, and discuss conditions in terms of pressure and structure functions that are easily accessible and guarantee that solutions which are smooth on a time interval
[0,T)
have smooth (and hence unique) extensions beyond
T
. The literature on regularity issues for Navier-Stokes equations is so extensive that we are not able to give here even the beginning of a survey. We mention just some minimal references in this short paper, with apologies to the many authors and works we knowingly or unknowingly leave out.

We discuss unforced Navier-Stokes equations

(1)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p=0,

with

(2)

\nabla\cdot u=0,

and

(3)

u(x,0)=u_{0}.

The kinematic viscosity
\nu
is a strictly positive constant,
u
is the velocity,
p
is the pressure. Most of this paper is concerned with solutions in the whole space, but there will be a few instances in which we refer to the bounded domain case. In that case the assumed boundary conditions are homogeneous Dirichlet,

(4)

u_{\left|\right.\,\partial\Omega}=0.

We maintain a sparing notation throughout the paper, omitting arguments and indices as often as we can.

We recall the local existence result for initial data (at time
T_{0}
) in
V
. The spaces
H
(mentioned below) and
V
are spaces of divergence-free vector fields which are completions of smooth compactly supported divergence free fields in the topologies of
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
. The norm in
V
is called the enstrophy. In the whole space it corresponds to the
\dot{H}^{1}
norm. Initial data with finite enstrophy lead to local strong solutions, that is unique solutions belonging to
L^{\infty}(T_{0},T_{0}+\tau;V)\cap L^{2}(T_{0},T_{0}+\tau;H^{2}\cap V)
for some
\tau>0
. Strong solutions are
C^{\infty}
smooth for
t>T_{0}
in smooth domains
[
5
]
. By ”conditions for regularity” for smooth solutions on a time interval
[0,T)
we mean conditions which guarantee
u(T)\in V
. These are global regularity conditions. We note here that we are not talking about
\epsilon
- regularity concepts (
[
11
]
) which are conditions on weak solutions in space-time cylinders, which imply pointwise local regularity inside a smaller cylinder. When assembled over space time, these conditions lead to partial regularity, and may lead to global regularity if additional assumptions are in place, (for instance a single potential first singularity at one point). In this paper we consider conditions which lead directly to persistence of regularity.

There are several well-known conditions for regularity. One of the simplest is

(5)

\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{V}^{4}dt\leq M_{V}<\infty.

From it, we have in a straightforward manner
[
5
]
that

(6)

\|u(t)\|_{V}^{2}\leq\|u(0)\|_{V}^{2}\exp{\left(C\nu^{-3}M_{V}\right)}

for all
0\leq t\leq T
. We adhere to the good practice that arguments of exponentials or logarithms should be nondimensional. Another easy to prove explicit condition is based on
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}
(see below, Theorem
5
).

The celebrated Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions
[
11
]
are

(7)

\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{p}dt\leq M_{p,q}<\infty,

with

(8)

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1,

and
3<q\leq\infty
. When
q=3
the condition is

(9)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{3}}\leq M_{3}<\infty,\quad t-a.e.\quad\text{on}\quad[0,T].

As it is very well-known, the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions imply regularity. The following is the explicit bound on the enstrophy.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with smooth boundary, let
q>3
and let
u
be a strong solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in
\Omega
on the interval
[0,T]
. There exists an absolute constant
C
such that

(10)

\|u(t)\|_{V}^{2}\leq\|u(0)\|_{V}^{2}\exp{\left[C\nu^{-\frac{q+3}{q-3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}ds\right]}.

holds for
0\leq t\leq T
. In particular, if (
7
) holds then

(11)

\|u(t)\|_{V}^{2}\leq\|u(0)\|_{V}^{2}\exp{\left[C\nu^{-\frac{q+3}{q-3}}M_{p,q}\right]}.

Proof.

Here is a brief proof. We recall that the Stokes operator is defined as

(12)

Au=-\mathbb{P}\Delta u,

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projector on divergence-free vector fields. We recall

(13)

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C|Au|_{H},

the fact that

(14)

\|u\|_{V}=\|u\|_{H^{1}({\Omega})},

and the notation

(15)

B(u,v)=\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla v).

We start with the enstrophy evolution

(16)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}_{V}+\nu|Au|^{2}_{H}=-(B(u,u),Au)_{H}\leq|B(u,u)|_{H}|Au|_{H}

Now, because
\mathbb{P}
is a projector, it follows that

(17)

|B(u,u)|_{H}\leq\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}.

A Hölder inequality with exponents
q,\frac{2q}{q-2},2
yields

(18)

|B(u,u)|_{H}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}},

and because
2<\frac{2q}{q-2}<6
, interpolation yields

(19)

\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{3}{q}}

Using the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,6}(\Omega)
, (
13
) and (
14
), we have

(20)

|B(u,u)|_{H}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|u\|_{V}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}\|Au\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{q}}

Thus, from(
16
) we have

(21)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}_{V}+\nu|Au|^{2}_{H}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}}\|u\|_{V}^{1-\frac{3}{q}}|Au|_{H}^{1+\frac{3}{q}}

and Young’s inequality with exponents
(\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{3}{q}))^{-1},(\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{3}{q})))^{-1}
yields

(22)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}_{V}+\nu|Au|^{2}_{H}\leq\frac{\nu}{2}|Au|^{2}_{H}+C\nu^{-\frac{q+3}{q-3}}\|u\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{q-3}}\|u\|_{V}^{2}.

The claimed inequality (
10
) follows by integrating the ODE inequality (
22
).
∎

Remark 1
.

The same result holds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with the same proof.

If

This condition is

\exists\delta>0,\forall t,\forall A,\quad|A|\leq\delta\Rightarrow\int_{A}|u(x,t)|^{3}dx\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{2C}\right)^{3}.

In the left hand side,
|A|
is the Lebesgue measure of

. In the right hand side,
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity and

is the constant in Morrey’s inequality,

(24)

\|u\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Remark 2
.

The condition (
23

\frac{\nu}{2C}
is fixed.

The proof (based on (
[

This paper is devoted to conditions based on pressure and on structure functions. There is a good motivation to seek conditions in terms of the pressure. In the absence of the pressure, the Navier-Stokes equations are Burgers equations in 3D and obey a maximum principle. This implies that the velocity is bounded, (if initially so), and the solutions are smooth for all time. The pressure in the Navier-Stokes equations is the only reason the equations are not local and the velocity magnitude is not a priori controlled. Conditions of regularity in terms of the pressure are known in
L^{2}

[
1
]
and one sided in
L^{\infty}

[
12
]
. We present in this paper an
L^{\frac{3}{2}}
condition, the analogue of the
q=3
condition (
23
) expressed in terms of only the pressure (Theorem
8
, Theorem
9
). We also give the analogues of the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin conditions (Theorem
10
).

The motivation to express conditions for regularity in terms of structure functions comes from experimental, numerical and theoretical turbulence studies. Structure functions are averages of moments of velocity increments. They obey remarkable and robust statistical relations. The relations need interpretation and then the may serve as reasonable hypothesis for the solutions of Navier-Stokes equations. One of the more widely verified relations is the ”four-fifths” law
[
8
]

(32)

\langle(\delta^{\parallel}_{\ell}u)^{3}\rangle=-\frac{4}{5}\epsilon|\ell|

where
\delta^{\parallel}_{\ell}(u)=(u(x+\ell)-u(x))\cdot\frac{\ell}{|\ell|}
is the longitudinal velocity increment and
\epsilon=-\langle\frac{dE}{dt}\rangle
is the rate of dissipation of energy, which in the case of unforced NSE
equals
\nu\langle|\nabla u|^{2}\rangle
. The four-fifths law is shown to hold for homogeneous and isotropic turbulence in the limit of time to infinity, followed by Reynolds number to infinity, followed by
\ell\to 0
, in this order. The Navier-Stokes solutions are assumed to be smooth. The braces
\langle\cdot\rangle
are expectations (ensemble average). Long time and space averages are usually assumed to realize them, and in numerical experiments these averages are used. The assumption of finite positive
\epsilon
is also made, in the limit of time to infinity, followed by Reynolds number to infinity, in this order. The Reynolds number is defined as

(33)

Re=\frac{UL}{\nu}

where
U
is a velocity scale, and
L
is a length scale. The classical K’41 Kolmogorov theory proposes scaling exponents
\zeta_{p}=\frac{p}{3}
for structure functions

(34)

\langle(\delta^{\parallel}_{\ell}u)^{p}\rangle=C_{p}(\epsilon|\ell|)^{\zeta_{p}}.

These relations are expected to hold in a range of scales,
|\ell|\in(\eta,L)
where
\eta
is the Kolmogorov dissipation scale,

(35)

\eta=\left(\frac{\nu^{3}}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

which is determined by the kinematic viscosity and energy dissipation rate, alone. Below the Kolmogorov dissipation scale, it is assumed that viscous effects dominate, with smooth behavior. The length scale
L
is the integral scale of turbulence.
Turbulence findings are average statements, they refer to typical long time behavior, and are asymptotic in Reynolds number. Interpreting them for the initial value problem for Navier-Stokes equations is challenging. It is however reasonable to expect that there are many solutions which give statistical weight to the turbulence laws and have properties that are consistent with them.

We give quantitative conditions in Theorem
11
, Theorem
12
. They involve a cutoff scale
r=r(t)
. We modify the structure function
S_{2}(x,r)
(see (
54
) below) to take into account a possibly non-universal viscous regularization below
r
. The regularity condition (
101
) requires
\int_{0}^{T}r(t)^{-4}dt<\infty
, a condition satisfied by the Kolmogorov length
r=\eta
. The condition requires in addition the smallness of
\int_{A}S_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}(x,r)dx\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{C}\right)^{3}
on sets of small enough measure,
|A|\leq\delta
.

Modern theories modify the scaling
\zeta_{p}=\frac{p}{3}
in (
34
) of the K’41 theory, reflecting experimental and numerical observation of intermittency. The turbulent signal is intermittent, that is, regions of high gradients of velocity are found to be sparse in both time and space. The connection between intermittency and regularity was explored in several mathematical works, (see for instance
[
10
]
and references therein) where assumptions of sparse behavior in physical space are used to deduce improved conditional regularity. In a different setting
[
2
]
, multifractal scaling exponents were connected to ratios of volume averages, and conditions for regularity were given on the basis of intermittency dimension.

In terms of the exponents, it is found numerically (see for instance
[
9
]
) that
\zeta_{2}>\frac{2}{3}
and, while
\zeta_{3}
remains close to 1,
\zeta_{p}
become smaller than
p/3
for large
p
, and perhaps even tends asymptotically to a constant, suggesting depletion of regularity, significantly below Hölder. We express conditions of regularity in terms of a Dini modulus of continuity which give regularity if logarithmic scaling is assumed (Theorem
13
). In Section
4.3
we consider a multifractal scenario where regularity still persists. In Section
4.4
we give a condition for regularity which requires small increments of velocity only in time dependent regions of high velocity and high gradients.

The proofs are based on observations concerning the pressure.

We consider solutions of

All terms in the right hand side of (

Thus, (

3.
Conditional regularity

Theorem 5
.

Assume

(57)

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{2}dt=N<\infty.

There exists an absolute constant
C
such that

(58)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\exp{\frac{CN}{\nu}}

Proof.

Remark 13

4.1.
A nearly selfsimilar example.

Theorem
11
(or rather, its proof) applies to functions which have small translation increments in
L^{3}
. We consider

(115)

where the leading term
V
satisfies

(116)

\|V(y+\cdot)-V(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}}\leq W(t)|y|^{s}

for some
s>0
.
In order to have nondimensional quantities, we write

(117)

W=UL^{1-s}.

The typical example is of the form

(118)

V(x,t)=U(t)P\left(\frac{x}{L(t)}\right)

where

is time independent and
\|P(\cdot+z)-P(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}}\leq C|z|^{s}
. We note that this condition is satisfied by many functions with slow decay which are not in

or even in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, such
as

,
\beta>0
. Of course, the condition is also satisfied on
B^{s}_{3,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

We have that (
116
) reads

(119)

\|V(y+\cdot)-V(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}}\leq UL\left(\frac{|y|}{L}\right)^{s},

and define

(120)

\frac{UL}{\nu}=Re(V).

We take
\epsilon>0
such that
s>\frac{1}{2}\epsilon
, and, writing
|y|^{-3}=|y|^{-1+\epsilon}|y|^{-2-\epsilon}
we use a Hölder inequality with exponents
3,\frac{3}{2}
to bound

(121)

S_{2}(x,r)^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{r}^{\frac{3\epsilon}{2}}\int_{|y|\leq{r}}|y|^{-3-\frac{3\epsilon}{2}}\left|V(y+x)-V(x)\right|^{3}dy\\

Integrating
dx
on
A
and switching the order of integration we deduce

(122)

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{A}S_{2}(x,r)^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{r}^{\frac{3\epsilon}{2}}\int_{|y|\leq{r}}|y|^{-3-\frac{3\epsilon}{2}}\int_{A}\left|V(y+x)-V(x)\right|^{3}dxdy\\
\leq C{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}W^{3}r^{3s}=C{\epsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(UL)^{3}\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{3s}.\end{array}

Assuming a bound on the Reynolds number of the profile,

(123)

and fixing

, we have

(124)

\int_{A}S_{2}(x,r)^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C_{s}\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{3s}(Re(V)^{3}\nu^{3}\leq C_{s}\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{3s}R^{3}\nu^{3}

The condition (
101
) is satisfied if

(125)

\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{s}R\leq C_{s}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(2C)^{-1}.

Remark 14
.

The condition (

In particular, if the leading term

4.2.
A Dini Condition

Theorem 13
.

Assume that
u
satisfies

(126)

\|\delta_{y}u\|_{L^{3}}\leq m(|y|)

where
\delta_{y}u(x,t)=u(x+y,t)-u(x,t)
, and where
0\leq m
is a time independent function satisfying

(127)

\int_{0}^{1}m^{2}(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}<\infty.

Then
u
satisfies (
101
) with
r
time independent, and consequently, smooth solutions of Navier-Stokes equations obeying (
126
) with (
127
) on
[0,T)
obey
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
with explicit bounds depending only on
m,\nu,T,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{3}},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that

(128)

\|S_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C\int_{|y|\leq 2r}\|\delta_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{3}}.

This inequality is proved by duality, integrating
S_{2}
against a test function in
L^{3}

(129)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}\phi dx=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|y|\leq 2r}\frac{dy}{|y|^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(x)|\delta_{y}u(x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\|\phi\|_{L^{3}}\int_{|y|\leq 2r}\|\delta_{y}u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\frac{dy}{|y|^{3}}.

From (
128
) and the assumed Dini condition, we deduce that the
\|S_{2}(\cdot,r)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{C}\right)^{2}

if
r
is chosen small enough so that

(130)

\int_{0}^{2r}m^{2}(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{C}\right)^{2}.

∎

Remark 15
.

Clearly
m(r)\sim\log^{-\alpha}(r^{-1})
with

is sufficient. As we remarked before, the smallness of the

increment does not imply that the function needs to be in

. We also remark that

can be allowed to depend on time, if

is uniformly integrable on
[0,1]
, or more generally, if denoting

(131)

I_{m(t)}(r)=\int_{0}^{2r}m^{2}(\rho,t)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

we have that the preimage of

under

, that is
r(t)=I_{m(t)}^{-1}\left(\left(\frac{\nu}{C}\right)^{2}\right)
, obeys

.

4.3.
Multifractal intermittent scenario

We consider the region

B_{U}(t)=\{x\left|\right.\;|u(x,t)|\geq U\}
defined before in (
76
). We take

U
time independent. We introduce a time independent length scale
L>0
, and we require

(132)

U^{2}\geq L^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}dx

so that

(133)

|B_{U}|\leq L^{3}.

We assume that the velocity increments

(134)

s_{2}(x,r)=\fint_{|y|=r}|u(x+y)-u(x)|^{2}dS(y)

obey bounds

(135)

s_{2}(x,r)\leq G^{2}\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{2\alpha(x)}

with
G>0
constant (with units of velocity),
L>0
as above, constant, (with units of length) and with
0<\alpha(x)\leq 1
. This upper bound is assumed to hold a.e. in
x\in B_{U}(t)
and for all
0<r<r_{0}
, where
0<r_{0}<L
is a fixed positive constant. Because

(136)

S_{2}(x,r)=\int_{0}^{2r}s_{2}(x,\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho},

we have that

(137)

S_{2}(x,r_{0})\leq CG^{2}\frac{1}{\alpha(x)}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{2\alpha(x)}

holds a.e in
x\in B_{U}
.
In multifractal turbulent intermittent scenarios, it is assumed that there is a spectrum of near-singularities of Hölder exponent
h
and that these are achieved on sets
\Sigma_{h}
of dimension
d(h)\leq 3
which occur randomly with probability
d\mu(h)
.

The dimension
d(h)
is implemented in the following manner. We take a region
V_{h}
around
\Sigma_{h}

and partition it in small disjoint cubes of size
\rho
with
\rho\leq r_{0}
. This region is a ”collar”of cross-section size
\rho
around the set
\Sigma_{h}\cap B_{U}
. The multifractal assumption is that the number of such cubes of
V_{h}
is of the order

N_{h}(\rho)=\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right)^{-d(h)}
. Assuming
\alpha(x)\geq h
to hold on each such cube, we have from
(
137
), on each cube

(138)

S_{2}(x,r_{0})\leq CG^{2}h^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{2h}.

Writing the volume of the cube as
L^{3}(\frac{\rho}{L})^{3}
, we have

(139)

\int_{B_{U}\cap V_{h}}S_{2}(x,r_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C(GL)^{3}\frac{1}{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{3h}\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right)^{3-d(h)}\leq C(GL)^{3}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{3-d(h)+3h}.

Above we used
\rho\leq{r_{0}}
.
Summing in
h
, remembering the frequency, we obtain

(140)

\int_{B_{U}\cap(\cup_{h}V_{h})}S_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}(x,r_{0})dx\leq C(GL)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{3-d(h)+3h}d\mu(h)

In the multifractal formalism, the structure function exponents are defined by

(141)

\zeta_{p}=\inf_{h}(3-d(h)+ph).

The inequality (
140
) above implies

(142)

\int_{B_{U}\cap(\cup_{h}V_{h})}S_{2}(x,r_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C_{\mu}(GL)^{3}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{\zeta_{3}}

where

(143)

C_{\mu}=C\int_{0}^{1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu(h)

is assumed to be finite. Introducing the Reynolds number based on
G
,

(144)

R_{G}=\frac{GL}{\nu}

and recalling that
B_{U}\setminus\cup_{h}V_{h}
was assumed to have measure zero, we have

(145)

\int_{B_{U}}S_{2}(x,r_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq\left[C_{\mu}R_{G}^{3}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{\zeta_{3}}\right]\nu^{3}

we see that the condition (
101
) is satisfied for
B_{U}
if

(146)

R_{G}^{3}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{L}\right)^{\zeta_{3}}\leq(C^{3}C_{\mu})^{-1}.

In classical turbulence theory
\zeta_{3}=1
. If
\zeta_{3}>0
, under the above scenario, it is enough to have
\frac{r_{0}}{L}
small enough in order to deduce that no singularities in finite time can occur.

. 1.
Introduction

In classical geometry, one can distinguish between euclidean space and hyperbolic space by measuring the rates at which geodesics diverge apart. In particular, in the zero curvature (euclidean) case the divergence is linear and in the negative curvature (hyperbolic) case the divergence is exponential. In
[
Gro93
]
Gromov discussed geodesic divergence for more general non-positively curved metric spaces and asked (6.B
2
(h), page 90) if such divergence is always linear or exponential. In
[
Ger94b
]
Gersten gave the first examples of
\CAT(0)
groups whose geodesic divergence is quadratic. In the same paper, Gersten defined divergence as a quasi-isometry invariant of geodesic metric spaces and of finitely generated groups.

Roughly speaking, Gersten’s notion of divergence is a function
\delta(r)
which measures the diameter of the sphere of radius
r
in the path-metric inherited from the complement of the open ball of radius
r
. In order to obtain a quasi-isometry invariant, one needs to consider the path-metric inherited from the complement of an open ball of radius
\rho r
for some
0<\rho\leq 1
and one works with equivalence classes of one-parameter families of functions
\{\delta_{\rho}(r)\}
. Precise definitions are given in section
2
.

In
[
Mac02
]
Macura studied divergence of free-by-cyclic groups with polynomially growing monodromies and gave the first example of a
\CAT(0)
group with cubic divergence and the first examples of finitely presented groups with polynomial divergence of arbitrary degree. Examples of
\CAT(0)
groups with polynomial divergence of arbitrary degree are provided in
[
Mac13
]
and
[
BD14
]
.

There has been intense study of divergence functions for particular families of finitely presented groups over the years. All these examples have divergence functions which are either polynomial or exponential. The first examples of finitely presented groups whose divergence is neither polynomial nor exponential appear in
[
BT
]
. The groups in
[
BT
]
make essential use of distorted infinite cyclic subgroups and so cannot be subgroups of
\CAT(0)
groups.
The research in the current paper was motivated by the following open question (paraphrased from
[
BD14
]
).

These subgroups provide further instances of the remarkable diversity of geometric behavior of subgroups of

There are two very useful properties of geodesics in negatively curved spaces. The first is that quasi-geodesics lie in bounded neighborhoods of geodesics between their endpoints; this was a critical ingredient in the proof of Mostow rigidity. This has been generalized to give the notions of Morse quasi-geodesic in a space and Morse element in a group. The second is the strong control on the diameter of images under nearest point projection onto geodesics. This has been generalized in
[
ABD
]
to give the notion of contracting quasi-geodesic in a metric space and contracting element in a group. These group theory generalizations are related as follows. Every contracting element is Morse. Moreover, these notions are equivalent in
\CAT(0)
and hyperbolic groups.
In Proposition

\CAT(0)

2.
Definitions and statement of the divergence results

Convention 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the collection of all functions from positive reals to positive reals. Let

and
g
be arbitrary elements of
\mathcal{M}
. We say that

is

f(x)\leq g(Ax)+Bx
for all

. We say that

is

to

f\preceq g
and
g\preceq f

One can define an equivalence relation between indexed families of functions as follows.
Let

\{\delta_{\rho}\}
is

by the family

, denoted
\{\delta_{\rho}\}\preceq\{\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}\}

such that
\delta_{L\rho}\preceq\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in(0,1]
. We say
\{\delta_{\rho}\}
is
equivalent
to
\{\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}\}
, denoted
\{\delta_{\rho}\}\sim\{\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}\}

\{\delta_{\rho}\}\preceq\{\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}\}
and
\{\delta^{\prime}_{\rho}\}\preceq\{\delta_{\rho}\}
. If

is

is equivalent to

Definition 2.2

(Group divergence)
.

Let
X
be a geodesic space with a fixed point
x_{0}
. For each
\rho\in(0,1]
we define a function
\delta_{\rho}\!:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
as follows. For each

\delta_{\rho}(r)=\sup d_{\rho r}(x_{1},x_{2})
where the supremum is taken over all

and

on the sphere

such that

. The family of functions

is the

of

Using Convention
2.1
one can show that up to equivalence the divergence of

and it is a quasi-isometry invariant (see

). The
divergence
of a finitely generated group

, denoted

\Gamma(G,S)
for some (any) finite generating set

.

We now recall the definition of quasi-geodesic divergence and undistorted cyclic subgroup divergence.

Definition 2.3

(Quasi-geodesic divergence)
.

Let
X
be a geodesic space and
\alpha\!:(-\infty,\infty)\to X
be an
(L,C)
–bi-infinite quasi-geodesic. The
divergence
of
\alpha
in

is the function
{\rm Div}_{\alpha}\!:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
defined as follows. Let

be an arbitrary number. If there is no path outside the open ball

connecting
\alpha(-r)
and

{\rm Div}_{\alpha}(r)=\infty

{\rm Div}_{\alpha}(r)
to be the infimum over the lengths of all paths outside the open ball
B(\alpha(0),r/L-C)
connecting
\alpha(-r)
and
\alpha(r)
.

Definition 2.4

(Cyclic subgroup divergence)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
\langle c\rangle
be an undistorted, infinite cyclic subgroup of

. Let

be a finite generating set of

that contains

The

of the cyclic subgroup
\langle c\rangle
in

Using Convention

We now review the concept of contracting quasi-geodesics and contracting elements in a finitely generated group.

Definition 2.5

(Contracting quasi-geodesic)

Let

Definition 2.6

(Contracting element)

Let

It is not hard to see that the notion of contracting quasi-geodesic is a quasi-isometry invariant. In particular, the concept contracting elements in a finitely generated group do not depend on the choice of finite generating set

Proposition 2.7

(See Proposition 5.3 in
[

)

Let
\alpha\!:(-\infty,\infty)\to X

(L,C)
–quasi-geodesic in a geodesic space

\alpha

–contracting quasi-geodesic. Then the divergence of

Definition 2.8

(Lipschitz equivalence of functions)
.

Let
f,g\!:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be two non-decreasing function. We say that
f
and
g
are

g(r)\leq Mf(Mr)\text{ and }f(r)\leq Mg(Mr).

Definition 2.9

(Distortion function)
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated group with a finite generating set
T
and let
F
be a subgroup of
H
with a generating set
R
. The
distortion function

\Dist_{F}^{H}\!:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is defined as follows:

\Dist_{F}^{H}(n)=\max\{\,{|g|_{R}}\mid{g\in F,|g|_{T}\leq n}\,\}\text{ for $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

We note that the function
\Dist_{F}^{H}
is non-decreasing. We can also consider the function
\Dist_{F}^{H}
as a non-decreasing function from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,\infty)
by linear interpolation over the open unit intervals.

The function
\Dist_{F}^{H}
is said to
admit an exponentially bounded sequence of certificates

(g_{m})
in

if there is a constant

such that the following hold:

(1)

|g_{m}|_{R}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty

(2)

|g_{m+1}|_{R}\leq C|g_{m}|_{R}
for each
m

(3)

\Dist_{F}^{H}\bigl(|g_{m}|_{T}/C\bigr)\leq C|g_{m}|_{R}
for each
m
.

The certificate elements

will be used to certify upper bounds on group divergence functions later on.

Up to Lipschitz equivalence, the distortion function
\Dist_{F}^{H}
does not depend on the choice of finite generating sets

and

. Furthermore, the fact that a distortion function admits an exponentially bounded sequence of certificates also does not depend on the choice of finite generating sets

and

.

Note that the function
\Dist_{F}^{H}(r)
grows at least linearly and so is Lipschitz equivalent to the monotonic increasing function
\Dist^{H}_{F}(r)+r
. Henceforth, we will always assume that

is a bijective function.

Lemma 3.2
.

The inclusion maps

be a group monomorphism taking each

5.1.

In

In this section we will make extensive use of the palindromic automorphism

In

]

on the fundamental group taking

to

,

to

, and

This lifts to an isometric involution

of the

–fold cyclic cover

of

corresponding to the subgroup

We will build new non-positively curved complexes by isometrically glueing simpler non-positively curved complexes along isometrically embedded (scaled) copies of

n
–fold cyclic covers

where

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

The distortion function

\varphi

representing an element of

. The sequence of elements

and provides a lower bound for the distortion function.

We now prove

admits an exponentially bounded sequence of palindromic certificates in

for each positive integer

\varphi

|a_{n+1}|_{R}\leq 3|a_{n}|_{R}\quad\text{ and }\quad|a_{n}|_{R}\geq 2^{n},

is a generating set of

|\varphi(a)|_{R}
and

are both bounded above by three. The second inequality can be proved by estimating the length of

in the free abelian group on

which is greater than

|a_{n}|_{T}=|\varphi^{n}(a)|_{T}=|t^{n}at^{-n}|_{T}\leq 2n+1\leq 3n\text{ for each }n\geq 1,

2^{|a_{n}|_{T}/3}\leq 2^{n}\leq|a_{n}|_{R}\,.

This implies that the exponential distortion function

Lemma 5.6
.

The upper bound for the distortion function is given in Corollary 9.14 of

We show that the same sequence

Remark 5.10
.

One can use Dehn functions to see that none of the groups
G_{m}
(
m\geq 1
) of Theorem
5.7
are
\CAT(0)
. We include a sketch of the argument here for readers with some familiarity with Dehn functions.

Let

denote a word in the generators of
G^{\prime}_{1}=H\ast_{F_{d}=F_{x\overline{y}}}(F_{x}\times F_{y}\times F_{z})
which represents the identity in
G^{\prime}_{1}\leq G_{1}\leq G_{m}
.

Claim 1.

{\rm Area}_{G_{m}}(w)={\rm Area}_{G^{\prime}_{1}}(w)
.

We prove this claim by showing that a minimal area van Kampen diagram for
w
over
G_{m}
cannot have any edges labelled by
e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and so is a van Kampen diagram over
G^{\prime}_{1}
.

We first show that a minimal area van Kampen diagram for
w
in
G_{m}
cannot involve any of the
s_{i}
for

. Assume to the contrary that there are such edge labels and let

be the maximum index of such edges. Because

is maximal, the notion of
s_{j}
–corridors are well defined for this van Kampen diagram. An
s_{j}
–corridor does not meet the boundary of the van Kampen diagram (because
s_{j}
does not appear in the word

) and so must form an annulus. An innermost such annulus contradicts minimality of the area because for
j\geq 2
each
s_{j}
conjugates a free group to another free group. The interior of an innermost
s_{j}
–annulus is just a finite tree. One can excise this

of strictly smaller area.

Next we show by contradiction that a minimal area van Kampen diagram for

cannot contain any edges labelled
s_{1}
. We have already shown that a minimal area van Kampen diagram does not contain any edges labelled
s_{j}
for
j\geq 2
. Therefore,
s_{1}
–corridors are well defined in this van Kampen diagram. As above,
s_{1}
–corridors can only form annuli. Because
s_{1}
commutes with all of
G^{\prime}_{1}
the inner and outer boundaries of
s_{1}
–annuli are identical words. We can excise such annuli and glue their inner boundaries to their outer boundaries to obtain a van Kampen diagram for
w
with smaller area.

Case

In this case take
(w_{n})
to be a sequence of snowflake words in

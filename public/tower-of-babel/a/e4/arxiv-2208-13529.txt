Group invariant solutions for the planar Schrödinger-Poisson system
Ganglong Zhou

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the following planar Schrödinger-Poisson system { − △ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u + ϕ ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) , in ℝ 2 , △ ​ ϕ = | u | p , in ℝ 2 , \begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+\phi{(x)}|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u),&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\ \triangle{\phi}=|u|^{p},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\end{cases} where p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 is a constant, V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) and f ⁡ ( x , t ) f(x,t) are continuous, mirror symmetric or rotationally periodic functions. By assuming that the nonlinearity f ⁡ ( x , t ) f(x,t) has critical exponential growth w.r.t. H 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}) , we obtain a nontrivial solution or a ground state solution of Nehari type to the above system. Our results extend previous works of Cao, Dai and Zhang [ 9 ] , Chen and Tang [ 16 ] . We handle more general nonlinearities f f with weaken constraint at infinity, and we assume only the (AR) type condition to take place of the monotonicity assumption (Theorem 1.1 ). We considered all the cases p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , and we show the existence of solutions with multiple types of symmetry. As in [ 16 ] , we adopt a version of mountain pass structure which provides a Cerami sequence (Lemma 3.1 ), with two innovative points. First, we make a key observation for the sign of a crucial part of the energy functional corresponding to the nonlocal term ϕ ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u \phi|u|^{p-2}u (Lemma 2.3 ), and secondly we adopt a new Moser type functions (Lemma 3.2 ) to ensure the boundedness and compactness of the Cerami sequence. Introduction

The present paper is concerned with the existence of solution to the planar Schrödinger-Poisson system

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+\phi{(x)}|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u),&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\
\triangle{\phi}=|u|^{p},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.\end{cases}

[formula]

where
p\geq 2
,
V,f
are continuous, mirror symmetric or rotationally periodic functions; and
f(x,t)
has exponential critical growth in the Trudinger-Moser sense (see
[
8
]
).

In last decades, considerable attentions have been paid for the following Schrödinger-Poisson system:

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+K(x)\phi{(x)}|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u),&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\\
\triangle{\phi}=K(x)|u|^{p},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.\end{cases}

[formula]

with various conditions on the parameters
p,N
and functions
V,K,f
. In three-dimensional case
N=3
, this kind of system arises in many contexts of physics, for example, in quantum mechanics
[
7
,
11
,
19
]
and semiconductor theory
[
5
,
6
,
21
,
23
]
. In
[
5
]
, the equation
(SP)
was introduced as a model describing solitary waves, for nonlinear stationary
equations of Schrödinger type interacting with an electrostatic field, where the unknown functions
u
and
\phi
denote wave function for particles and potential respectively. Mathematically speaking, for each
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the second equation in
(SP)
determines a unique solution
\phi_{u}
in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, i.e.
\phi_{u}:=\Gamma_{N}\ast|u|^{p}
, where

\Gamma_{N}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|,&\text{$N=2$},\\
\frac{1}{N(2-N)\omega_{N}}|x|^{2-N},&\text{$N\geq 3$},\end{cases}

and
\omega_{N}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. When
N\geq 3
, many minimization techniques were used to the system
(SP)
. We refer the readers to
[
4
,
12
,
25
,
29
]
and references therein.

In the following, let us focus on the two dimensional case. In 2008, Stubbe
[
27
]
considered the equation

-\triangle{u}+\lambda u+\phi{(x)}u=0,\;\;\triangle{\phi}=u^{2},\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},

[formula]

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. He set up a variational framework for
(S1)
with a subspace
Z
of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

Z:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(1+|x|)u^{2}dx<\infty\right\}.

He proved that there exists a unique radial ground state solution for any
\lambda\geq 0
. In addition, he proved
that there exists a negative number
\lambda^{\ast}
such that for any
\lambda\in(\lambda^{\ast},0)
there are two radial ground states with different
L^{2}
norms.

In 2016, Cigolani and Weth
[
17
]
considered the equation
(P)
with
p=2
and
f(x,u)=b|u|^{q-2}u
, that is

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+\phi{(x)}u=b|u|^{q-2}u,&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\
\triangle{\phi}=u^{2},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\end{cases}

[formula]

where
b\geq 0,q\geq 4
are constants and
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2},(0,\infty))
is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
periodic.
Using the concentration-compactness theory, they proved that the equation
(S2)
has a ground state
u\in X_{2}
and a solution sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset X_{2}
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(u_{n})=\infty
. Here

X_{2}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}+\ln(1+|x|)u^{2}\Big]dx<\infty\right\}

and
J
is the energy functional associated to
(S2)
.

In 2021, Cao et al.
[
9
]
generalized the above consideration for
p\geq 2
,

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+\phi{(x)}|u|^{p-2}u=b|u|^{q-2}u,&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\
\triangle{\phi}=|u|^{p},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\end{cases}

[formula]

where
b\geq 0,p\geq 2,q\geq 2p
are constants and
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2},(0,\infty))
is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
periodic. With similar method in
[
17
]
, they obtained the existence of a positive ground state solution of
(S3)
in
X_{p}
, where

X_{p}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}+\ln(1+|x|)|u|^{p}\Big]dx<\infty\right\}.

In 2020, Chen and Tang
[
16
]
considered the equation
(P)
with
p=2
, i.e.

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+\phi{(x)}u=f(x,u),&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\
\triangle{\phi}=u^{2},&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\end{cases}

[formula]

where
V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2},[0,\infty))
is axially symmetry and
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is of subcritical or critical exponential growth in the sense of Trudinger-Moser. More precisely, we say that
f(x,t)
has subcritical exponential growth at
t=\pm\infty
if it verifies

(F1’)

(subcritical case)
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|s|\leq A}|f(x,s)|<+\infty
for every
A>0
and

\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(x,t)|}{e^{\alpha t^{2}}}=0,\;\mbox{uniformly in}\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\;\mbox{for all}\;\alpha>0;

and
f(x,t)
is said having the critical exponential growth at
t=\pm\infty
if it verifies

(F1)

(critical case)
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|s|\leq A}|f(x,s)|<+\infty
for every
A>0,
and there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(x,t)|}{e^{\alpha t^{2}}}=0,\;\;\mbox{uniformly in}\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\;\mbox{for all}\;\alpha>\alpha_{0}

while

\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(x,t)|}{e^{\alpha t^{2}}}=+\infty,\;\mbox{uniformly in}\;\mathbb{R}^{2}\;\mbox{for all}\;\alpha<\alpha_{0}.

This notion of criticality was introduced by Adimurthi and Yadava
[
2
]
.

For the critical growth case, Chen and Tang
[
16
]
established the existence of a ground state solution for
(S4)
by assuming a series of technical conditions on
V
and
f
:

(V0)

V\in C(\mathbb{R},[0,\infty))
and
\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)>0
;

(CF1)

V(x):=V(x_{1},x_{2})=V(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
f(x,t):=f(x_{1},x_{2},t)=f(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,t)
for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
;

(CF2)

f(x,t)t>0
for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, and there exists
M_{0}>0
and
t_{0}>0
such that

F(x,t)\leq M_{0}|f(x,t)|,\;\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|t|\geq t_{0},

where
F(x,t):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)ds;

(CF3)

There exists
\kappa>0
,
\liminf_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{t^{2}F(x,t)}{e^{\alpha_{0}t^{2}}}\geq\kappa>\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}^{2}\rho^{2}}
uniformly on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\rho\in(0,1/2)
satisfies
\rho^{2}\max_{|x|\leq\rho}V(x)\leq 1
;

(CF4)

\frac{f(x,t)-V(x)t}{|t|^{3}}
is non-decreasing on
t\in(-\infty,0)
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Here we will prove the existence of a nontrivial solution to the equation
(P)
, not only for all
p\geq 2
, but also for much more general nonlinearities
f
and more general potentials
V
(see Remark
1.2
below).

To describe our main results, we introduce the following notations: Let us view
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
\mathbb{C}
, Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}},k\geq 2
, we say that
v\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
if
v(ze^{2\pi i/k})=v(z)
over
\mathbb{C}
. We define

E_{k,p}:=X_{p}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{k},\quad\mathcal{V}_{k,1}:=C(\mathbb{C})\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{k}

\mathcal{F}_{k,1}:=\{f\in C(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}):f(\cdot,t)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k},\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

We say that
v
is mirror symmetric, denoted by
v\in{\mathcal{M}}
if
v(\overline{z})=v(z)
in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let

T_{k,p}:=E_{k,p}\cap{\mathcal{M}},\quad\mathcal{V}_{k,2}:=\mathcal{V}_{k,1}\cap{\mathcal{M}},

\mathcal{F}_{k,2}:=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k,1}:f(\cdot,t)\in{\mathcal{M}},\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
, V and f satisfy (V0) and (F1). Assume that

(VF)

V\in\mathcal{V}_{k,1}\;\mbox{and}\;f\in\mathcal{F}_{k,1}
with
k\geq 4
or
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k,2}\;\mbox{and}\;f\in\mathcal{F}_{k,2}
with
k\geq 2
;

(F2)

f(x,t)t\geq 0
for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, and there exists
M_{0}>0
and
t_{0}>0
such that
F(x,t)\leq M_{0}|f(x,t)|
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
|t|\geq t_{0};

(F3)

There exists
q\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\liminf_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|t|^{q}F(x,t)}{e^{\alpha_{0}t^{2}}}=+\infty;

(F4)

(AR) type condition: There exist
\mu_{1}>2
,
\mu_{2}<\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}-1
such that

f(x,t)t\geq\mu_{1}F(x,t)-\mu_{2}V(x)t^{2},\;\;\forall\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(F5)

If
p=2
,
f(t)=o(t)
as
t\rightarrow 0
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{2};
and if
p>2
,
f(t)=O(t^{s_{0}})
with
s_{0}>1
as
t\rightarrow 0
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Then
(P)
has a nontrivial solution
\bar{u}
. Moreover,
\bar{u}\in E_{k,p}
if
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k,1}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k,1}
;
\bar{u}\in T_{k,p}
if
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k,2}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k,2}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Comparing to Chen and Tang
[
16
, Theorem 4]
, we have generalized or weaken all the assumptions (CF1)-(CF4), to (VF) and (F2)-(F4) respectively. More precisely,

•

(CF1) means just
V\in\mathcal{V}_{2,2}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{2,2}
, hence is a special case of (VF);

•

The condition (CF4) is a significant improvement of (F4) since the (AR) type condition does not require any monotonicity assumption;

•

The condition (F3) is also less restrictive than (CF3) for the behavior of
f
at infinity;

•

(F2) improves slightly (CF2) where
f(x,t)t>0
is replaced by
f(x,t)t\geq 0
;

Finally,
[
16
]
used implicitly the condition
f(t)=o(t)
with
p=2
in (F5), seeing the proof of Lemma 2.6 there.

With a similar condition to (CF4), we can obtain the least energy solution on the corresponding Nehari manifold.

.

Let
p\geq 2
,
V
and
f
satisfy (V0), (VF), (F1)-(F3) and (F5). Furthermore, we assume that

(F4’)

For any

,

is non-decreasing on

and

and

Comparing to the approach in

Preliminaries

In this section, we will give some preliminary definitions and basic facts. In the following, the letter
C
denotes generic positive
constants and
\|\cdot\|_{q}
denotes the standard norm in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})
.

The function space
X_{p}
is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{p}}:=\|u\|+\|u\|_{\ast},

(2.1)

where

\|u\|_{\ast}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(1+|x|)|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}};

(2.2)

while

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\Big]dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.3)

is induced by the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+V(x)uv)dx.

(2.4)

We will use the following bilinear functionals introduced by Stubbe
[
27
]
:

A_{1}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(1+|x-y|)u(x)v(y)dxdy;

(2.5)

A_{2}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)u(x)v(y)dxdy;

(2.6)

A_{0}(u,v):=A_{1}(u,v)-A_{2}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(|x-y|)u(x)v(y)dxdy.

(2.7)

By Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (see
[
20
]
), there exists
C>0
such that for any
u,v\in L^{4/3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

|A_{2}(u,v)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}u(x)v(y)dxdy\leq C\|u\|_{\frac{4}{3}}\|v\|_{\frac{4}{3}}.

(2.8)

Corresponding to (
2.5
)-(
2.7
), we define

I_{i}(u):=A_{i}(|u|^{p},|u|^{p}),\quad i=0,1,2.

(2.9)

The following bound for
I_{2}(u)
is a direct consequence of (
2.8
):

|I_{2}(u)|\leq C\|u\|_{\frac{4p}{3}}^{2p},\;\;\forall\;u\in L^{\frac{4p}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\forall\;p\geq 1.

(2.10)

We can write the associated functional of the equation
(P)
in the following form

\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4p\pi}I_{0}(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,u)dx

(2.11)

and the associated Nehari manifold of the functional (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{1}:=\{u\in E_{k,p}\backslash\{0\}:\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\},\\
\mathcal{N}_{2}:=\{u\in T_{k,p}\backslash\{0\}:\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\}.\end{split}

(2.12)

satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(e^{\alpha u^{2}}-1\right)dx<C(M,\alpha),

]

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that (V0),(F1),(F5) hold. Then
I_{i},\Phi\in C^{1}(X_{p},\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle I^{\prime}_{i}(u),v\rangle=2pA_{i}(|u|^{p},|u|^{p-2}uv),\quad i=1,2\\

Lemma 2.3
.

I_{0}(u)\leq 0,\;\;\forall\;u\in X_{p}.

Proof.

First, let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with
{\rm supp}(u)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)
. Let
\phi_{u}
be the Newton potential of
|u|^{p}
, i.e. the unique solution of
\Delta\phi=|u|^{p}
,
\phi\in D^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Then

2\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{u}\triangle\phi_{u}dx=I_{0}(u)=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}|u(x)|^{p}|u(y)|^{p}\ln(|x-y|)dxdy\leq 0.

Consider now
u\in{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
, take
R>0
such that
\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset B_{R}(0)
. Let
w(x)=u(2Rx)
, so
{\rm supp}(w)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)
and
\phi_{w}(x):=\frac{1}{4R^{2}}\phi_{u}(2Rx).
Hence

\frac{1}{2\pi}I_{0}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{u}\triangle\phi_{u}dx=16R^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{w}\triangle\phi_{w}dx\leq 0.

Now we conclude by density argument. Indeed, using standard argument, by cut-off and mollification, we can check readily that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is dense in
X_{p}
, that is for any
u\in X_{p}
, there exist
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{X_{p}}=0.
By the fact
I_{0}=I_{1}-I_{2}
and Lemma
2.2
, we conclude that
I_{0}(u)\leq 0.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume that (V0) and (F1) hold. Then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t\omega)=-\infty,\;\;\forall\;\omega\in X_{p}\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

For any
\omega\in X_{p}\backslash\{0\}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
m\{|\omega(x)|\geq\delta\}>0
. By Lemma

\displaystyle\Phi(t\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\omega\|^{2}+\frac{t^{4}}{4p\pi}I_{0}(\omega)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,t\omega)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\omega\|^{2}-C\int_{\{|\omega(x)|\geq\delta\}}e^{\alpha_{0}\delta^{2}t^{2}/2}dx\longrightarrow-\infty

as
|t|\to\infty
.
∎

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(1+|x-y|)|u(x)|^{p}|v(y)|^{p}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega_{2}}|v(y)|^{p}dy\int_{\Omega_{1}}\ln(1+|x-y|)|u(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega_{2}}|v(y)|^{p}dy\int_{\Omega_{1}}\ln(1+|x|)|u(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|v(y)|^{p}dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln(1+|x|)|u(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{k}\|u\|^{p}_{\ast}\|v\|^{p}_{p},\;\;\forall\;u,v\in E_{k,p}.

So we are done. ∎

Variational framework

In this section, we will quote a version of Mountain Pass Theorem and prepare the proof of Theorem
1.1

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y
be a real Banach space and
I\in C^{1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
. Let
S
be a closed subset of
Y
which disconnects
Y
into distinct connected
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. Suppose further that
I(0)=0
and

(i)
0\in Y_{1}
and there exists
\alpha>0
such that
I|_{S}\geq\alpha,

(ii) There is
e\in Y_{2}
such that
I(e)\leq 0.

Then
I
possess a
(Ce)_{c}
sequence with
c\geq\alpha>0
given by

c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\gamma(t)),

where

\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=0,\gamma(1)=e\}

and
(Ce)_{c}
sequence means a Cerami sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
such that

I(u_{n})\rightarrow{c},\;\|I^{\prime}(u_{n})\|_{Y^{\ast}}(1+\|u_{n}\|_{Y})\rightarrow 0.

The proof of the above Lemma can be found in
[
26
, Theorem 3]
. We state another key result which serves as a bridge between the mountain pass structure (see Lemma
3.3
) and Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that (V0),(F1) and (F3)-(F5) hold. Then there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\max_{t\geq 0}\Phi(t\omega_{n_{0}})<\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{0}},

(3.1)

where

\omega_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{\ln n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{q\ln{(\ln{n})}}{2\sqrt{2\pi\ln{n}}},&\text{$0\leq|x|\leq(\ln{n})^{q/2}/n$};\\
\frac{\ln(1/|x|)}{\sqrt{2\pi\ln n}},&\text{$(\ln{n})^{q/2}/n\leq|x|\leq 1$};\\
0,&\text{$|x|\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can fix
q\geq 2
. Direct computation yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}\leq&\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}dx+V_{1}\int_{B_{1}}\omega_{n}^{2}dx\\
=&1-\frac{q\ln{(\ln{n})}}{2\ln{n}}+V_{1}\delta_{n},\end{split}

(3.2)

where
V_{1}=\max_{x\in B_{1}}V(x)
and
\delta_{n}=O(\frac{1}{\ln{n}})
as
n\to\infty
.
By
(F3)
, there exists

such that

\frac{|t|^{q}F(x,t)}{e^{\alpha_{0}t^{2}}}\geq 1,\;\forall\;|t|\geq t_{0}.

(3.3)

There are three cases for the value of

.

Case (i):

. Then it follows from (
3.2

\displaystyle\Phi(t\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{t^{2p}}{2p}I_{0}(\omega_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,t\omega_{n})dx\leq\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t^{2}\leq\frac{7\pi}{4\alpha_{0}}

(3.4)

for large
n
.

Case (ii):
\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{\alpha_{0}}}\leq t\leq\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{\alpha_{0}}}
. Then
t\omega_{n}(x)\geq t_{0}
for

and large

. Then it follows from (
3.2
),(
3.3
) and Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(t\omega_{n})&=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{t^{2p}}{2p}I_{0}(\omega_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,t\omega_{n})dx\\
&\leq\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t^{2}-\frac{q\ln(\ln{n})}{4\ln{n}}t^{2}-\frac{2^{q/2}\pi^{1+q/2}(\ln{n})^{q}}{n^{2}t^{q}T_{n}^{q/2}}e^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2\pi}t^{2}T_{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t^{2}-\frac{q\ln(\ln{n})}{4\ln{n}}t^{2}-\frac{\alpha_{0}^{q/2}\pi(\ln{n})^{q}}{2^{q}n^{2}T_{n}^{q/2}}e^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2\pi}t^{2}T_{n}}=:\varphi_{n}(t),\end{split}

(3.5)

where

T_{n}:=\ln{n}-q\ln(\ln{n})+\frac{q^{2}\ln^{2}(\ln{n})}{4\ln{n}}.

Let
t_{n}>0
be the unique maximum of
\varphi_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

\displaystyle t_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{\alpha_{0}}\left[1+\frac{(q-1)\ln(\ln{n})}{2\ln{n}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ln{n}}\right)\right]

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(t)\leq\varphi_{n}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t_{n}^{2}-\frac{q\ln(\ln{n})}{4\ln{n}}t_{n}^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ln{n}}\right).

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
)-(
3.7

\displaystyle\Phi(t\omega_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{n}(t_{n})=\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{0}}-\frac{\ln(\ln{n})}{2\ln{n}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ln{n}}\right).

(3.8)

Case (iii):
t\geq\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{\alpha_{0}}}

t\omega_{n}(x)\geq t_{0}
for

for large

),(
3.3

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(t\omega_{n})&=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{t^{2p}}{2p}I_{0}(\omega_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,t\omega_{n})dx\\
&\leq\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t^{2}-\frac{2^{q/2}\pi^{1+q/2}(\ln{n})^{q}}{n^{2}t^{q}T_{n}^{q/2}}e^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2\pi}t^{2}T_{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{1+V_{1}\delta_{n}}{2}t^{2}-\frac{2^{q/2}\pi^{1+q/2}(\ln{n})^{q}}{t^{q}T_{n}^{q/2}}\exp\left[2\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{4\pi}t^{2}-1\right)T_{n}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{4\pi(1+V_{1}\delta_{n})}{\alpha_{0}}-\frac{\alpha_{0}^{q/2}\pi(\ln{n})^{q/2}}{2^{q}}n^{2}\\
&\leq 0\end{split}

(3.9)

for large

Combining the conclusions for cases (i)-(iii), the proof is completed.∎

Lemma 3.3

denotes the group of orthogonal transformations in

is a continuous map (see

Assume that

,

. Let

We claim that
\varphi^{\prime}(u)=0
in

. In fact, given any

, we define

\bar{v}=\frac{1}{\#(G)}\sum_{\tau\in G}\tau v,

where

denotes the cardinal of

. Therefore, one has

\displaystyle 0=\langle\varphi^{\prime}(u),\bar{v}\rangle=\frac{1}{\#(G)}\sum_{\tau\in G}\langle\varphi^{\prime}(u),\tau v\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\#(G)}\sum_{\tau\in G}\langle\varphi^{\prime}(u)\circ\tau^{-1},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\#(G)}\sum_{\tau\in G}\langle\varphi^{\prime}(u\circ\tau^{-1})\circ\tau^{-1},v\rangle

For the second line, we used the fact

. So we have
\varphi^{\prime}(u)=0
in
X_{p}
.

The two cases in Theorem

are direct consequence of the above discussion. Indeed, let

Further discussions

In this section, we discuss several open problems above the 2-D Schrödinger-Poisson system

\begin{cases}-\triangle{u}+V(x)u+K(x)\phi{(x)}|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u),&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$},\\

In this work, we considered mainly the case

1.1

[
22
]
. In other words, we consider the equation

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{c}(0)}\left|(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,\bar{u}))v\right|dx+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{c}}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\left|(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,\bar{u}))v\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(N_{c}+1)C\varepsilon\|v\|_{X}.

The continuity of Gateaux derivatives means that the functional is continuous differentiable.

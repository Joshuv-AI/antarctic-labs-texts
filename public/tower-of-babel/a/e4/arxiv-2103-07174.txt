Introduction

In this paper, we study the existence of normalized ground states for the following Kirchhoff equation:

-\left(a+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\right)\Delta u=\lambda u+|u|^{p-2}u+u^{5}\quad\ \text{for some}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.1)

with prescribed mass

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}=c^{2},

where
a,b>0
are constants and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
.
The weak solutions for the problem correspond to the critical points for the energy functional

E(u)=\frac{a}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{b}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{6}

on the constraint manifold

S(c)=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\Psi(u)=\frac{1}{2}c^{2}\},

where
\Psi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}
. Equation (
1.1
) is viewed as being nonlocal because of the appearance of the term
b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\Delta u
, which indicates that equation (
1.1
) is no longer a pointwise identity. The nonlocal term also results in lack of weak sequential continuity of the energy function associated to (
1.1
), even we remove the critical term
u^{5}
. If
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is replaced by a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then equation (
1.1
) describes the stationary state of the Kirchhoff type equation of the following type:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\left(a+b\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\right)\Delta u=f(x,u),&x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&x\in\partial{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.2)

which is presented by Kirchhoff in
[
8
]
. It is an extension of D’Alembert’s wave equation by considering the effects of the length of strings during vibrations.

Problem (
1.2
) has received much attention after Lions
[
10
]
proposed an abstract framework to deal with the problem. We refer the readers to
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
11
]
and the work
[
2
]
seems to be the first one studying the critical Kirchhoff problem.

More recently, normalized solutions for elliptic equations have attracted considerable attentions, e.g. see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and the references therein. The work
[
7
]
is the first paper to deal
with the existence of normalized solutions for a second order Schrödinger equation with a Sobolev sub-critical and
L^{2}
-supercritical nonlinearity and the papers
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
deal
with the existence of normalized solutions for the problem in bounded domains. When
b=0,
problem (
1.1
) becomes

-\Delta u=\lambda u+|u|^{p-2}u+u^{5},\quad\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

with prescribed mass

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}=c^{2},

which was recently investigated by Soave in
[
18
]
, where in case of
p\in(2,6)
the author studied the existence and properties of the ground states for the problem.

For Kirchhoff type problems with a prescribed mass, it is showed that
p=\frac{14}{3}
is the
L^{2}
-critical exponent for the minimization problem (
[
22
,
23
]
). The papers
[
9
,
22
,
24
]
consider the existence and properties of the
L^{2}
-subcritical constrained minimizers. In the case of
p\in(\frac{14}{3},6)
, the corresponding functional is unbounded from below on
S(c)
,
[
13
]
proved that there are infinitely many critical points by using a minimax procedure. However, few literature is concerned with normalized solutions for critical Kirchhoff problem. Inspired by
[
18
]
, in this paper we
attempt to study the critical Kirchhoff problem (
1.1
).

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a,b>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
. Then problem (
1.1
) has a couple of solutions
(u_{c},\lambda_{c})\in S(c)\times\mathbb{R}
for any
c>0
. Moreover,

E(u_{c})=\inf_{u\in V(c)}E(u),

(1.3)

where
V(c)
is the Pohozaev manifold defined in lemma
2.1
.

2
Preliminaries

To prove our theorem, we need some notations and useful preliminary results.

Throughout this paper, we denote
B_{r}(z)
the open ball of radius
r
with center at
z
, and
\|\cdot\|_{p}
the usual norm of space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
H=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with the usual norm of space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Generic positive constant is denoted by
C
,
C_{1}
, or
C_{2}
…, which may change from line to line. Let
\mathbb{H}=H\times\mathbb{R}
with the scalar product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{H}}+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

and the corresponding norm

\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}=\|\cdot\|^{2}_{H}+|\cdot|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}.

We denote the best constant of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

S=\inf_{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{6}},

(2.1)

where
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\nabla u\|_{2}.

In
[
20
]
, we know that
S
is achieved by

U_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\varepsilon^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(2.2)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter.

In this paper, we will find critical points of
E
on
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\bigcap S(c)
, where
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\triangleq\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):u(x)=u(|x|)\}
is a natural constraint.

The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev (GNS) inequality is crucial in our arguments: that is, there exists a best constant
C_{p}
depending on
p
such that for any
u\in H
,

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C_{p}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\beta_{p})p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\beta_{p}p},

(2.3)

where
\beta_{p}=\frac{3(p-2)}{2p}
.

As in
[
7
]
, we introduce the useful fiber map preserving the
L^{2}
-norm, that is,

H(u,s)\triangleq e^{\frac{3s}{2}}u(e^{s}x),\quad{\rm for\ a.e}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Define the auxiliary functional
I:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

I(u,s)=E(H(u,s))=\frac{a}{2}e^{2s}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{b}{4}e^{4s}\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-\frac{e^{p\beta_{p}s}}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}-\frac{e^{6s}}{6}\|u\|^{6}_{6}.

The Pohozaev identity plays an important role in our discussion. We give it in the following lemma; for more details, see
[
7
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u,\lambda)\in S(c)\times\mathbb{R}
be a weak solution of equation (
1.1
). Then u belongs to the set

V(c)\triangleq\{u\in H:P(u)=0\}

where

P(u)=a\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+b\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-\beta_{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}-\|u\|^{6}_{6}.

For any
u\in S(c)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
\Phi_{u}(s)\triangleq I(u,s)
. Then

\displaystyle(\Phi_{u})^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=ae^{2s}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+be^{4s}\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-\beta_{p}e^{p\beta_{p}s}\|u\|^{p}_{p}-e^{6s}\|u\|^{6}_{6}

\displaystyle=a\|\nabla H(u,s)\|^{2}_{2}+b\|\nabla H(u,s)\|^{4}_{2}-\beta_{p}\|H(u,s)\|^{p}_{p}-\|H(u,s)\|^{6}_{6}.

Therefore, we have

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
u\in S(c)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a critical point for
\Phi_{u}(s)
if and only if
H(s,u)\in V(c)
.

Remark 2.3
.

{\rm The\ map}\ (u,s)\in\mathbb{H}\mapsto H(u,s)\in H\quad{\rm is\ continuous};

(2.4)

see
[
4
, Lemma3.5]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
11
, Lemma 3.3]

For
t,s>0
, the following system

\begin{cases}x(t,s)=t-aS(t+s)^{\frac{1}{3}}=0\\
y(t,s)=s-bS^{2}(t+s)^{\frac{2}{3}}=0\end{cases}

has a unique solution
(t_{0},s_{0})
. Moreover, if
x(t,s)\geq 0
and
y(t,s)\geq 0
, then
t\geq t_{0}
,
t\geq t_{0}
.

3
Characterization of mountain pass level

As in
[
7
,
12
]
, we firstly prove that
I(u,s)
has the mountain pass geometry on
S(c)\times\mathbb{R}
in the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
a,b>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
. Let
u\in S(c)
be arbitrary fixed. Then

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla H(u,s)|^{2}\rightarrow 0,
and
I(u,s)\rightarrow 0^{+},
as
s\rightarrow-\infty;

(2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla H(u,s)|^{2}\rightarrow+\infty,
and
I(u,s)\rightarrow-\infty,
as
s\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

The proof is trivial from the facts

\|\nabla H(s,u)\|^{2}_{2}=e^{2s}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}

and

I(u,s)=E(H(u,s))=\frac{a}{2}e^{2s}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{b}{4}e^{4s}\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-\frac{e^{p\beta_{p}s}}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}-\frac{e^{6s}}{6}\|u\|^{6}_{6}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b,c>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
. Then there exists
K_{c}>0
such that

P(u),E(u)>0\ {\rm for\ all\ }u\in A_{c},\quad{\rm and}\quad 0<\sup_{u\in A_{c}}E(u)<\inf_{u\in B_{c}}E(u)

with

A_{c}=\{u\in S_{c}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq K_{c}\},\quad B_{c}=\{u\in S_{c}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}=2K_{c}\}.

Proof.

Let
K>0
be arbitrary fixed and suppose that
u,v\in S(c)
are such that
\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\leq K
and
\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}=2K.
Then, for
K>0
small enough, using (
2.3
) and
p\beta_{p}\geq 4
, there exist two constants
C_{1}

and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle P(u)

\displaystyle\geq a\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+b\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-C_{1}\|\nabla u\|^{p\beta_{p}}_{2}-C_{2}\|\nabla u\|^{6}_{2},

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{b}{4}\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}-C_{1}\|\nabla u\|^{p\beta_{p}}_{2}-C_{2}\|\nabla u\|^{6}_{2}

and

\displaystyle E(v)-E(u)

\displaystyle\geq E(v)-\frac{a}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{b}{4}\|\nabla u\|^{4}_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{aK}{2}+\frac{bK^{2}}{2}-C_{1}\|\nabla v\|^{p\beta_{p}}_{2}-C_{2}\|\nabla v\|^{6}_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{aK}{2}+\frac{bK^{2}}{2}-C_{1}K^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}-C_{2}K^{3}.

Therefore, by the above inequalities, it follows that there exists
K_{c}
small enough such that

P(u),E(u)>0\ {\rm for\ all\ }x\in A_{c},\quad{\rm and}\quad 0<\sup_{u\in A_{c}}E(u)<\inf_{u\in B_{c}}E(u)

with

A_{c}=\{u\in S_{c}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq K_{c}\},\quad B_{c}=\{u\in S_{c}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}=2K_{c}\}.

∎

Next, we give a characterization of mountain pass level for
I(u,s)
and
E(u)
.
E^{d}
denotes the set
\{u\in S_{c}:E(u)\leq d\}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under assumptions that
a,b>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
, let

\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}=\inf_{\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{\Gamma_{c}}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\widetilde{h}(t))

where

\widetilde{\Gamma_{c}}=\{\widetilde{h}\in C([0,1],S(c)\times\mathbb{R}):\widetilde{h}(0)\in(A_{c},0),\ \widetilde{h}(1)\in(E^{0},0)\},

and

\gamma_{c}=\inf_{h\in{\Gamma_{c}}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}E(h(t))

where

{\Gamma_{c}}=\{{h}\in C([0,1],S(c)):h(0)\in A_{c},\ h(1)\in E^{0}\}.

Then we have

\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}={\gamma}_{c}.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma_{c}\times\{0\}\subseteq\widetilde{\Gamma_{c}},
we have
\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}\leq{\gamma_{c}}
. So, it remains to prove that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}\geq{\gamma_{c}}
. For any
\widetilde{h}(t)=(\widetilde{h_{1}}(t),\widetilde{h_{2}}(t))\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{c}
, we set
h(t)=H(\widetilde{h_{1}}(t),\widetilde{h_{2}}(t))
. Then
h(t)\in\Gamma_{c}
and

\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\widetilde{h}(t))=\max_{t\in[0,1]}E(H(\widetilde{h_{1}}(t),\widetilde{h_{2}}(t))=\max_{t\in[0,1]}E(h(t)),

which shows that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}\geq{\gamma_{c}}
.
∎

In the following proposition, we give the existence of
(PS)_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}}
sequence for
I(u,s)
. Its proof is by a standard argument using the Ekeland’s Variational principle and constructing pseudo-gradient flow.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
7
, Proposition2.2]

Let
\{g_{n}\}\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}_{c}
be such that

\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(g_{n}(t))\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}+\frac{1}{n}.

Then there exists a sequence
\{(u_{n},s_{n})\}\subset S(c)\times\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

I(u_{n},s_{n})\in[{\gamma}_{c}-\frac{1}{n},\gamma_{c}+\frac{1}{n}]
;

(2)

\min_{t\in[0,1]}\|(u_{n},s_{n})-g_{n}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
;

(3)

\|I^{\prime}|_{S(c)\times\mathbb{R}}(u_{n},s_{n})\|\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}
i.e.

|\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n},s_{n}),z\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{{-1}}\times\mathbb{H}}|\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\|z\|_{\mathbb{H}}

for all

z\in\widetilde{T}_{(u_{n},s_{n})}\triangleq\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{H}:\langle u_{n},z_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Under the assumptions
a,b>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6
, there exists a sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset S(c)
such that

(1)

E(v_{n})\rightarrow\gamma_{c}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
;

(2)

P(v_{n})\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
;

(3)

E^{\prime}|_{S(c)}(v_{n})\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
i.e.

|\langle E^{\prime}(v_{n}),h\rangle_{{H}^{-1}\times H}|\rightarrow 0

uniformly for all
h
satisfying

\|h\|_{H}\leq 1\quad{\rm where}\quad h\in{T}_{v_{n}}\triangleq\{h\in{H}:\langle v_{n},h\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}={\gamma_{c}}
. Pick
\{g_{n}=((g_{n})_{1},0)\}\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}_{c}
such that

\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(g_{n}(t))\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{c}+\frac{1}{n}.

It follows from Proposition
3.4
that there exists a sequence
\{(u_{n},s_{n})\}\subset S(c)\times\mathbb{R}
such that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, one has

\displaystyle I(u_{n},s_{n})\rightarrow{\gamma}_{c},

(3.1)

\displaystyle s_{n}\rightarrow 0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}I(u_{n},s_{n})\rightarrow 0.

(3.3)

Let
v_{n}=H(u_{n},s_{n})
. Then
E(v_{n})=I(u_{n},s_{n})
and, by (
3.1
),
(1)
holds. For the proof of
(2)
, we notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}I(u_{n},s_{n})

\displaystyle=ae^{2s_{n}}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}+be^{4s_{n}}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{4}_{2}-e^{p\beta_{p}s_{n}}\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{p}-e^{6s_{n}}\|u_{n}\|^{6}_{6}

\displaystyle=a\|\nabla v_{n}\|^{2}_{2}+b\|\nabla v_{n}\|^{4}_{2}-\beta_{p}\|v_{n}\|^{p}_{p}-\|v_{n}\|^{6}_{6}

\displaystyle=P(v_{n}),

which implies
(2)

3.3
).

For the proof of
(3)
, let
h_{n}\in T_{v_{n}}
. We have

\displaystyle\langle E^{\prime}(v_{n}),h_{n}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H}

\displaystyle=a\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla v_{n}(x)\nabla h_{n}(x)+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}(x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla v_{n}(x)\nabla h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{n}(x)|^{p-{2}}v_{n}(x)h_{n}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v_{n}(x))^{5}h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=ae^{\frac{5s_{n}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)\nabla h_{n}(x)-e^{\frac{15s_{n}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x))^{5}h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+be^{\frac{15s_{n}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)\nabla h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle-e^{\frac{3(p-1)s_{n}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)|^{p-{2}}u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=ae^{2s_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}(x)e^{\frac{-5s_{n}}{2}}\nabla h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x)

\displaystyle+be^{4s_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}(x)e^{\frac{-5s_{n}}{2}}\nabla h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x)

\displaystyle-e^{p\beta_{p}s_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}(x)|^{p-{2}}u_{n}(x)e^{\frac{-3s_{n}}{2}}h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x)

\displaystyle-e^{6s_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u_{n}(x))^{5}e^{\frac{-3s_{n}}{2}}h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x).

Setting
\widehat{h}_{n}(x)=e^{\frac{-3s_{n}}{2}}h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x),
then

\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n},s_{n}),(\widehat{h}_{n},0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{{-1}}\times\mathbb{H}}=\langle E^{\prime}(v_{n}),h_{n}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\langle u_{n}(x),\widehat{h}_{n}(x)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{n}(x)e^{\frac{-3s_{n}}{2}}h_{n}(e^{-s_{n}}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{n}(e^{s_{n}}x)e^{\frac{3s_{n}}{2}}h_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{n}(x)h_{n}(x)=0.

So, we have that
(\widehat{h}_{n}(x),0)\in\widetilde{T}_{(u_{n},s_{n})}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|(\widehat{h}_{n}(x),0)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{h}_{n}(x)\|^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle=\|h_{n}(x)\|^{2}_{2}+e^{-2s_{n}}\|\nabla h_{n}(x)\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\widehat{h}_{n}(x)\|^{2}_{H},

where the last inequality holds by (

). Thus,

is proved.

In the following lemma, we give an upper bound estimate for the mountain pass level
\gamma_{c}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under assumptions
a,b>0
and
\frac{14}{3}\leq p<6

\gamma_{c}<\gamma^{*}_{c}\triangleq\frac{abS^{3}}{4}+\frac{b^{3}S^{6}}{24}+\frac{(4as+b^{2}S^{4})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{24}

Proof.

Let
\varphi(x)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2}(0))
be a radial cut-off function such that

and
\varphi(x)\equiv 1
on

. Then we take

(

defined in (

)) and

v_{\varepsilon}=c\frac{u_{\varepsilon}}{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}}\in S(c)\cap H_{r}^{1}.

We take
\varepsilon=1
and define

K_{1}\triangleq\|\nabla U_{1}\|^{2}_{2},\quad K_{2}\triangleq\|U_{1}\|^{6}_{6},\quad K_{3}\triangleq\|U_{1}\|^{p}_{p}.

According to

, Appendix A]
, we have

\begin{cases}K_{1}/K_{2}=S,\quad\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{2}=K_{1}+O(\varepsilon),\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{6}=K_{2}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\\
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{p}=\varepsilon^{3-\frac{p}{2}}\left(K_{3}+O(\varepsilon^{p-3})\right),\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{2}=O(\varepsilon^{2})+\omega\left(\int^{2}_{0}\varphi(r)dr\right)\varepsilon,\end{cases}

(3.4)

where

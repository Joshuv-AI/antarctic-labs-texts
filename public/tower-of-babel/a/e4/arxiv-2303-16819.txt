Uniform distribution of α ​ n \alpha n modulo one for a family of integer sequences
Andreas Weingartner

Abstract
. We show that the sequence ( α ​ n ) n ∈ ℬ (\alpha n)_{n\in\mathcal{B}} is uniformly distributed modulo 1, for every irrational α \alpha , provided ℬ \mathcal{B} belongs to a certain family of integer sequences, which includes the prime, almost prime, squarefree, practical, densely divisible and lexicographical numbers. We also give an estimate for the discrepancy if α \alpha has finite irrationality measure.

1.
Introduction

We say that a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers is uniformly distributed modulo
1
(u.d. mod 1) if

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|\{n\leq x:\{a_{n}\}\in[a,b]\}|}{x}=b-a

for all
0\leq a<b\leq 1
, where
\{u\}
denotes the fractional part of
u
.
Weyl’s criterion asserts that this is equivalent to

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}e(la_{n})=0,

for every fixed non-zero integer
l
, where
e(y):=e^{2\pi iy}
.

Let
\alpha
be any irrational real number.
Weyl’s criterion shows at once that the sequence
(\alpha n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is u.d. mod 1.
Vinogradov
[
11
, Ch. XI]
proved that the same holds for the sequence
(\alpha p)_{p\in\mathbb{P}}
, where
p
runs through the prime numbers.

We consider the following family of integer sequences. For each natural number
n
, let
I(n)
be an arbitrary interval of real
numbers, possibly empty, or the union of a bounded number (uniformly in
n
) of such intervals.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{I}
be the set of positive integers containing
n=1
and all those
n\geq 2
with prime factorization
n=p_{1}\cdots p_{k}
,
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\ldots\leq p_{k}
, which satisfy

p_{j}\in I\big(\prod_{1\leq i<j}p_{i}\big)\qquad(1\leq j\leq k).

This setting is more general than in
[
12
]
, where
I
was of the form
I(n)=[1,\theta(n)]
for some function
\theta(n)
.
If
I(1)=[1,\infty)
and
I(n)=\emptyset
for
n>1
, then
\mathcal{B}\setminus\{1\}
is the set of primes.
If
I(1)=I(p)=[1,\infty)
for primes
p
and
I(n)=\emptyset
for composite
n
, then
\mathcal{B}
is the set of integers with at most two prime factors,
counted with multiplicity. Similarly, one can obtain more general almost-primes.
Squarefree numbers can be generated with

, where
P(n)
denotes the largest prime factor of
n>1
and
P(1)=1
.
Integers whose divisors grow by factors of at most

[
7

or
t
-dense

[

This family also includes the

[

Define

Theorem 2

.

(Minor Arcs)

Let

leads to an estimate for the discrepancy of the sequence
(\{\alpha n\})_{n\in\mathcal{B}}

assuming the denominators of the continued fraction convergents

of
\alpha

The irrationality measure of an irrational number

\mu(\alpha)=1+\limsup_{j\geq 1}\frac{\log q_{j+1}}{\log q_{j}}

Every algebraic irrational number
\alpha

The following estimate for the discrepancy follows from Theorem

and the Erdős-Turán inequality (see Lemma

Theorem 4
.

Let
\kappa<\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}
and assume
\alpha
is irrational with finite irrationality measure. Then, for all

\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1}|B(x,y,\alpha)-yB(x)|\ll\frac{x}{\exp(\kappa\sqrt{\log x\log\log x})}.

In particular, the sequence
(\alpha n)_{n\in\mathcal{B}}
is u.d. mod 1
provided
B(x)
satisfies
B(x)\gg x\exp(-\kappa\sqrt{\log x\log\log x})
for some
\kappa<\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}
.

Derivation of Theorem
1
from Theorems

and

By Weyl’s criterion, we need to show that

T:=\frac{1}{B(x)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}e(l\alpha n)\to 0\quad(x\to\infty)

for every fixed non-zero integer

. It suffices to consider

, since the following is equally
valid if
\alpha
is replaced by
l\alpha
.
Let
(a_{j}/q_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be the sequence of continued fraction convergents of
\alpha
.
Then
q_{1}<q_{2}<\ldots
and

|\alpha-a_{j}/q_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{q_{j}q_{j+1}}\quad(j\geq 1).

Let

be sufficiently large and let
R=(\log x)^{A+1}
and
h=1
in Theorem
3
.
Define
Q=R^{12}L^{26}=L^{12A+38}
, where
L=\log x
.
If there is at least one

such that
Q<q_{j}\leq x/Q
,
Theorem
3
(with
q=q_{j}
) shows that we have
T\ll x(\log x)^{-A-1}/B(x)\ll 1/\log x
.

If there does not exist a
j
such that
Q<q_{j}\leq x/Q
, let
i
be such that

q_{i}\leq Q
and
q_{i+1}>x/Q
. We have
|\alpha-a_{i}/q_{i}|\leq 1/(q_{i}q_{i+1})\leq 1/q_{i+1}<Q/x
.
Theorem

, with

and

.

,

and
q_{i}\to\infty
.
Thus

as

.

5.
Lemmas for the major arcs

Let
P(n)
denote the largest prime factor of
n\geq 2
and put
P(1)=1
.
The following estimate follows from
[
1
, Eqs. (1.3), (1.4), (1.5)]
.

Lemma 2

(de Bruijn)
.

For
y\geq(\log x)^{2}
and
u=\frac{\log x}{\log y}\geq 1
, we have

\Psi(x,y):=\sum_{n\leq x\atop P(n)\leq y}1\ll x\exp(-u\log u).

A key ingredient for the major arcs is the Siegel-Walfisz theorem (see
[
5
, Thm. 6.9 and Cor. 11.21]
).

Lemma 3

(Siegel-Walfisz)
.

There exists a constant
c_{1}>0
such that the following holds. Let
A>0
. For
q\leq(\log x)^{A}
and
(a,q)=1
,

\pi(x,a,q):=\sum_{p\leq x\atop p\equiv a\bmod q}1=\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O_{A}(xe^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log x}}),

where
\pi(x):=\pi(x,0,1)
and
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function.

Lemma 4

(Ramanujan’s Sum)
.

Let
a,q,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
(a,q)=1
and
d|q
. Then

\sum_{n=1\atop(n,q)=d}^{q}e(na/q)=\mu(q/d),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

Proof.

Writing
n^{\prime}=n/d
and
q^{\prime}=q/d
we have
(a,q^{\prime})=1
and

\sum_{n=1\atop(n,q)=d}^{q}e(na/q)=\sum_{n^{\prime}=1\atop(n^{\prime},q^{\prime})=1}^{q^{\prime}}e(n^{\prime}a/q^{\prime})=\mu(q^{\prime})=\mu(q/d).

The last sum is called Ramanujan’s sum and is evaluated in
[
5
, Thm. 4.1]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2

If
n\in\mathcal{B}
,
n>1
, we write
n=mp
where
p=P(n)
and
m\in\mathcal{B}
.
We have

f(\alpha):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}(x)}e(\alpha n)=e(\alpha)+\sum_{m\in\mathcal{B}(x)}\sum_{p\in J(x,m)}e(\alpha pm)

where
J(x,m)
is the (possibly empty) union of a bounded number of intervals

J(x,m):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}:P(m)\leq y\leq x/m\}\cap I(m).

Define
g(x):=\log x\log\log x
.
If
E_{0}
denotes the contribution to
f(\alpha)
from primes
p\leq\exp(g(x)^{2/3})
, then

|E_{0}|\leq\Psi(x,\exp(g(x)^{2/3}))\ll x\exp(-g(x)^{1/3}/4),

by Lemma
2
.
Thus we may replace
J(x,m)
by

\tilde{J}(x,m):=J(x,m)\cap(\exp(g(x)^{2/3}),\infty).

Note that if
mp\equiv b\bmod q
where
(b,q)=d
and the prime
p
satisfies
p>q
, then
(m,q)=d
.
In this case we write
q^{\prime}=q/d
,
b^{\prime}=b/d
and
m^{\prime}=m/d
.
For

(r,q)=1
, let
\bar{r}
be such that
\bar{r}r\equiv 1\bmod q
.

We have

\begin{split}f(a/q+\beta)&=E_{0}+\sum_{d|q}\sum_{b=1\atop(b,q)=d}^{q}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop(m,q)=d}\sum_{p\in\tilde{J}(x,m)\atop mp\equiv b\bmod q}e(({\textstyle\frac{a}{q}}+\beta)pm)\\
&=E_{0}+\sum_{d|q}\sum_{b=1\atop(b,q)=d}^{q}e({\textstyle\frac{a}{q}}\,b)\sum_{m\in\mathcal{B}(x)\atop(m,q)=d}\sum_{p\in\tilde{J}(x,m)\atop p\equiv b^{\prime}\overline{m^{\prime}}\bmod q^{\prime}}e(\beta pm).\end{split}

Since
p\geq\exp(g(x)^{2/3})
, we have
q\leq(\log x)^{U}\ll(\log p)^{3U/2}
.
By the Siegel-Walfisz theorem, in the form of Lemma

, the sum over

where
E_{1}\ll\rho e^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log\rho}}
. Let

.
Integration by parts applied to the error term shows that

\sum_{p\in\tilde{J}(x,m)\atop p\equiv b^{\prime}\overline{m^{\prime}}\bmod q^{\prime}}e(\beta pm)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q^{\prime})}\sum_{p\in\tilde{J}(x,m)}e(\beta pm)+E_{2},

\ll q\sum_{m\leq x}\frac{x}{m}\exp(-c_{1}\sqrt[3]{g(x)})\ll x\exp(-2c\sqrt[3]{g(x)})=:E_{5}.

The contribution from

\ll q|\beta|x\sum_{m\leq x}\int_{\tilde{J}(x,m)}e^{-c_{1}\sqrt{\log\rho}}d\rho\leq q|\beta|x\sum_{m\leq x}\frac{x}{m}\exp(-c_{1}\sqrt[3]{g(x)})\ll E_{5},

since

and

.
Thus

The two inner sums are now independent of

We combine the two inner sums, writing

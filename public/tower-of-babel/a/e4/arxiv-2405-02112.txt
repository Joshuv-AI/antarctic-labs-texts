1.
Introduction

The study of determinants involving Legendre symbols is an active topic in algebra, number theory and combinatorics. Krattenthaler summarized many results on the evaluation of determinants (see
[
5
,
6
]
). Let
p
be an odd prime and
(\frac{\cdot}{p})
be the Legendre symbol. Carlitz
[
2
]
showed that

\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq p-1}=p^{(p-3)/2}.

In 2004, R. Chapman
[
3
]
studied several variants of Carlitz’s results. In particular, he posed seveal related conjectures. The most famous one is the “evil determinant conjecture” (see
[
1
,
4
]
for the motivation of this conjecture). When
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
we let
\varepsilon_{p}>1
and
h_{p}
denote the fundamental unit and class number
of the real quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
respectively. And we write

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{p}^{(2-(\frac{2}{p}))h_{p}}=a_{p}+b_{p}\sqrt{p}\

(1.1)

where
\ a_{p},b_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
The “evil determinant conjecture” says that

\displaystyle\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq\frac{p-1}{2}}=\begin{cases}-a_{p}&\mbox{if}\ p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4),\\
1&\mbox{if}\ p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4).\end{cases}

(1.2)

Chapman could not solve this problem for many years so that he named it “evil determinant conjecture”. This conjecture was later comfirmed by Vsemirnov
[
9
,
10
]
with sophisticated matrix decomposition.

In 2019, along this line, Zhi-Wei Sun
[
7
]
obtained many revelant results on this topic. For instance, if
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
is a prime and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, Sun showed that

\det\left[\left(\frac{i+dj}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq(p-1)/2}\equiv\bigg(\bigg(\frac{d}{p}\bigg)d\bigg)^{(p-1)/4}\frac{p-1}{2}!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p).

Also, Sun
[
7
]
posed some related conjectures for further study.
Let
x
be an indeterminate.
Define

C(x):=\det\left[x+\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq\frac{p-1}{2}}.

Recently Sun posed a conjecture on
C(x)
. In this paper we obtain the following theorem, which confirmed that conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
a_{p}
,
b_{p}
be as (
1.1
). Then

C(x)=\begin{cases}(\frac{2}{p})pb_{p}x-a_{p}&\mbox{if}\ p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4),\\
1&\mbox{if}\ p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4).\end{cases}

Note that when we take
x=0
the above result coincides with (
1.2
). Our idea to prove this result is mainly based on Vsemirnov’s decomposition
[
9
,
10
]
of
\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq(p-1)/2}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in section 2 and section 3.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Case
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)

We begin with a lemma we shall use in the proof.

Lemma 2.1

(The Matrix Determinant Lemma)
.

Let
H
be an
n\times n
matrix, and let
{\bf u},{\bf v}
be two
n
-dimensional column vectors. Then

\det(H+{\bf u}{\bf v}^{T})=\det H+{\bf v}^{T}{\rm adj}(H){\bf u},

where
{\rm adj}(H)
is the adjugate matrix of
H
and
{\bf v}^{T}
is the transpose of
{\bf v}
.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
8
]
.

Let
n=(p-1)/2
throuthout the proof. We first consider the case
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Case
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
.
Let
{\bf u}=(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}
be a column vector of length
n+1
and
{\bf v}=x\cdot{\bf u}
. By Lemma
2.1

\det(C(x))=\det(C)+x\cdot{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}.

In view of (
1.2
), it is sufficient to show that
{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=0
. For any matrix
A=[a_{ij}]_{0\leq i,j\leq m}
, we use
{\rm adj}(A)=[A_{ji}]_{0\leq i,j\leq m}
to denote its adjugate matrix.
Then
{\rm adj}(C)=[C_{ji}]_{0\leq i,j\leq\frac{p-1}{2}}
and

{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}C_{kl}=\sum_{k=0}^{(p-3)/4}\sum_{l=0}^{n}C_{kl}+C_{n-k\;n-l}.

If we let
M_{i,j}
denote the
(i,j)
minor of
C
(i.e., the determinant of the submatrix of
C
formed by deleting the
i
th row and
j
th column) then

C_{kl}=(-1)^{k+l}M_{k,l}=(-1)^{k+l}\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq k,j\neq l},

and

C_{n-k\;n-l}=(-1)^{n-k+n-l}M_{n-k,n-l}=(-1)^{k+l}\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq n-k,j\neq n-l}.

Note that

\displaystyle\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq n-k,j\neq n-l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{p-1}{4}\rfloor}\det\left[\left(\frac{n-j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq n-k,j\neq l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{p-1}{4}\rfloor}\cdot(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{p-1}{4}\rfloor}\det\left[\left(\frac{n-j-(n-i)}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq k,j\neq l}

\displaystyle=\det\left[-\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq k,j\neq l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq k,j\neq l}

\displaystyle=-\det\left[\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right]_{0\leq i,j\leq n\atop i\neq k,j\neq l}.

Hence

C_{kl}+C_{n-k\;n-l}=0

for any
0\leq k\leq(p-3)/4
and
0\leq l\leq n
. It follows that

{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
. ∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Case
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)

In this section we deal with the case
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
. We begin with the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
10
, Theorem 3]
)
.
Let
u_{i},v_{j}(1\leq i,j\leq m)
be complex numbers such that
u_{i}v_{j}\neq-1
. Then

\displaystyle\det\bigg[\frac{u_{i}+v_{j}}{1+u_{i}v_{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+u_{i})(1+v_{i})+(-1)^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-u_{i})(1-v_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\bigg((u_{i}-u_{j})(v_{j}-v_{i})\bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+u_{i}v_{j})^{-1}.

Using essentially the same method appeared in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 2]
, it is easy to get the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let notations be as above. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg)^{2}+\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)=(-1)^{n/2}\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}b_{p}p.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Case
p\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
.
By similar methods appeared in the proof of the case
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
, it is sufficient to show that

{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)pb_{p}.

(3.1)

For convenience, we introduce Vsemivnov’s notations
[
10
]
.
Let
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}
and define square matrices
U,V,D
of order
n+1
with

(i,j)
entry as follows:

\displaystyle u_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{-j-2i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{-2j-i}}{\zeta^{-i-j}+(\frac{i}{p})(\frac{j}{p})},\quad 0\leq i,j\leq n,

\displaystyle v_{ij}=

\displaystyle\zeta^{2ij},\quad 0\leq i,j\leq n,

\displaystyle d_{ii}=

\displaystyle\prod_{0\leq k\leq n\atop k\neq i}\frac{1}{\zeta^{2i}-\zeta^{2k}},\quad 0\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle d_{ij}=

\displaystyle 0,\quad 0\leq i\neq j\leq n.

Vsemivnov
[
10
, Theorem 2]
proved that

C=\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)\sqrt{p}\zeta^{(p-1)/4}\cdot VDUDV.

By this we have

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)^{n}(\sqrt{p})^{n}\zeta^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\cdot{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}U\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}(V)\cdot{\bf u}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{p})^{n}\zeta^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\cdot{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}U\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}(V)\cdot{\bf u}.

For any matrix
A
, we let
A^{(k)}
denote the matrix obtained from
A
via replacing all entries in the
(k+1)
-th row by
1
. Then

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V=

\displaystyle(1,1,\ldots,1)\cdot{\rm adj}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1,1,\ldots,1)\cdot[V_{ji}]_{0\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{0k},\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{1k},\ldots,\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{nk}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\det V^{(0)},\det V^{(1)},\ldots,\det V^{(n)}\bigg).

Since each element of the first row of
V
is
1
, we have
\det V^{(0)}=\det V
and
\det V^{(k)}=0
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Thus

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V=\det V\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0).

Recall that
D
is a diagonal matrix with
(i+1)
-th diagonal element
d_{ii}
. Hence

{\rm adj}D=\det D\cdot D^{-1}=\det D\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}\frac{1}{d_{00}}&&&\\
&\frac{1}{d_{11}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\frac{1}{d_{nn}}\\
\end{array}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V\cdot{\rm adj}D=\frac{\det V\cdot\det D}{d_{00}}\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0).

Note that
V
,
D
,
{\rm adj}V
and
{\rm adj}D
are symmetric matrices. Hence

{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}V\cdot{\bf u}=\frac{\det V\cdot\det D}{d_{00}}\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0)^{T}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}\cdot{\rm adj}V\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}U\cdot{\rm adj}D\cdot{\rm adj}(V)\cdot{\bf u}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}}{d_{00}^{2}}\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0)\cdot{\rm adj}U\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0)^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}}{d_{00}^{2}}\cdot(U_{00},U_{10},\ldots,U_{n0})\cdot(1,0,0,\ldots,0)^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}}{d_{00}^{2}}\cdot U_{00}.

By (
3
) we have

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=(\sqrt{p})^{n}\zeta^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\cdot\frac{(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}}{d_{00}^{2}}\cdot U_{00}.

Recall that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{00}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{0<k\leq n}(1-\zeta^{2k})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{2k})\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{2k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}\zeta^{2k}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{-2k})\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{2k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta^{n(n+1)}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{-2k})\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\zeta^{2k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta^{n(n+1)}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\zeta^{n(n+1)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=p^{\frac{p+3}{4}}\zeta^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}\cdot(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}\cdot U_{00}.

(3.3)

We next evaluate
U_{00}
. One can verify that

\displaystyle U_{00}

\displaystyle=\det[u_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{-j-2i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{-2j-i}}{\zeta^{-i-j}+(\frac{i}{p})(\frac{j}{p})}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{-i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{-j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{i+j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{i+j}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\frac{i}{p})(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{i+j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}\cdot\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{-\frac{p-1}{4}}\cdot\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

(3.4)

Take
m=n=(p-1)/2
and
u_{i}=v_{i}=(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}
in Lemma
3.1
and by Lemma
3.2
we deduce that

\displaystyle\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}\cdot(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+u_{i})(1+v_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-u_{i})(1-v_{i})\bigg)\cdot

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg((u_{i}-u_{j})(v_{j}-v_{i})\bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+u_{i}v_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n/2}\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}b_{p}p\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg((u_{i}-u_{j})(u_{j}-u_{i})\bigg)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+u_{i}u_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n/2+\binom{n}{2}}\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}b_{p}p\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+u_{i}u_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}b_{p}p\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+u_{i}u_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}b_{p}p\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}-\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}.

Define

f_{1}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}-\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)

and

f_{2}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg).

Then

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}-\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle=f_{1}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle=f_{1}^{2}\prod_{1\leq i=j\leq n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{j}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{j}\bigg(\frac{i}{p}\bigg)\zeta^{i}\bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle=f_{1}^{2}\ f_{2}^{-2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+\zeta^{2j})^{-1}.

By
[
10
, Corollary 2 (4.6)]
we see that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+\zeta^{2j})=\zeta^{n(n+1)/2}\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg).

(3.7)

Combining (
3
), (
3
) with (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\det\bigg[\frac{(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}+(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}{1+(\frac{i}{p})\zeta^{i}(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}}\bigg]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=pb_{p}\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}.

(3.8)

By (
3
) and (
3.8
) we have

\displaystyle U_{00}=\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)pb_{p}\cdot\zeta^{-\frac{p-1}{4}}\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}.

Combining (
3.3
) with the above equalities, we get

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{T}{\rm adj}(C){\bf u}=\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)pb_{p}\cdot\zeta^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{8}}\cdot p^{\frac{p+3}{4}}\cdot(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}.

(3.9)

By
[
10
, Eq. (4.10)]
one may verify that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\zeta^{(n+1)(p-1)/4}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{2}{p}\bigg)\sqrt{p}\bigg)^{n+2}\cdot(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}\cdot(\det G)^{-2}\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{-\frac{p^{2}-1}{8}}\cdot p^{\frac{p+3}{4}}\cdot(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}

where

(\det G)^{2}=\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\frac{j}{p})\zeta^{j}\bigg)^{2}=\zeta^{n(n+1)}=\zeta^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{4}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\zeta^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{8}}\cdot p^{\frac{p+3}{4}}\cdot(\det V)^{2}\cdot(\det D)^{2}\cdot f_{1}^{2}\cdot f_{2}^{-2}=\zeta^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{8}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{8}}=1.

(3.10)

The last equality follows from

\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{8}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{8}=p\cdot\frac{p-1}{4}\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p).

Now (
3.1
) follows from (

) and (

).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.∎

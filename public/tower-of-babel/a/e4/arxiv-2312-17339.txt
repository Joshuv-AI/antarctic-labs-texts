Orbifold del Pezzo surfaces in ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} format
Muhammad Imran Qureshi

Abstract
We construct two types of wellformed and quasismooth biregular models (infinite series) of rigid orbifold del Pezzo surfaces having their (sub) anti-canonical embeddings in ℙ 6 ​ ( w i ) \mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i}) . One type of model contains a family of rigid del Pezzo surfaces with a fixed Fano index and weights of ambient ℙ 6 ​ ( w i ) \mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i}) are parameterized by positive integers. In the other type models, weights of ℙ 6 ​ ( w i ) \mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i}) and Fano index, both are parameterized by the positive integers. The equations describing their images under (sub) anti-canonical embeddings are given in terms of the equations of the Segre embedding of ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} , which has codimension 4 in ℙ 7 \mathbb{P}^{7} . We also give a formula for the Hilbert series of a generic weighted ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} variety, a key tool in these constructions.

. All of our families of rigid odPs can be considered as weighted complete intersections of some weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
variety (defined in §
3
) or of some (weighted) projective cone(s) over it. The notation WPS gives the weights of ambient weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
containing our odPs, with powers of
w_{i}
representing the multiplicity of the given weight. The weights on
\mathbb{P}^{7}(w_{i})
containing a w
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
variety are determined by a choice of parameter
w
. The cube in the given table represents the weights of the ambient weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
variety. The bold numbers in the weight cube represent the degree(s) of quasilinear section(s) of
\mathbb{P}^{7}(w_{i})
with whom we take the intersection. If some numbers
a_{i}s
appear in
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
in the column WPS which are not in the weight cube, then our surface appears as a quasilinear section of some (weighted) projective cone(s) over given w
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
variety, with cone(s) having weights
a_{i}s
. The column
\mathcal{B},-K_{X}^{2}
and
h^{0}(-K)
lists the basket of rigid singularities, the anti-canonical degrees, and the first plurigenus of
X
, respectively.

The first key point of our computation is to prove the general Hilbert series formula for the general weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
variety, which enables to compute the anti-canonical divisor class of the ambient variety. Then we use an algorithmic method
[
Qur17
,
BKZ19
]
to search for candidate rigid del Pezzo surfaces by using the computer algebra system
MAGMA

[
BCP97
]
. We analyze these candidates by comparing the basket of orbifold points
and weights
to spot any pattern emerging among them which may lead to a biregular model (infinite series) of surfaces. Once such a model is identified, we use the strategy described in §
4
to prove the existence of wellformed and quasismooth models. The list of biregular models is non-exhaustive due to the non existence of termination conditions on the algorithmic approach used to compute candidate del Pezzo surfaces. However, our list is the complete classification up to certain computational bounds described in section
5.1
. In the Fano index 1 case, there are also wellformed and quasismooth families of rigid odPs which are not in any model, and they are listed as sporadic cases in section
5.2
.

1.2
Context and motivation

The classification of non singular del Pezzo surfaces is well understood and there are 10 deformation families.
An orbifold del Pezzo surface
X,
classically known as a log del Pezzo surface, is a del Pezzo surface with at worst isolated orbifold points. If
X
contains only
R
(rigid) singularities (See
2.1
) then it is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein(
qG
)-rigid. It is said to have a class TG if it can be deformed under a
qG
-degeneration to a normal toric del Pezzo surface.
Since all normal surfaces with orbifold points have partial smoothing to a surface containing only
R
singularities
[
KSB88
]
, our focus in this article is only on rigid del Pezzo surfaces.

Orbifold del Pezzo surfaces appear in various veins in algebraic geometry, for example classically in the minimal model program
[
KMM87
]
. More recently in the context of mirror symmetry
[
CCG+12
]
and
K
-stability questions through the existence of Kaehler-Einstein metrics
[
CS13
]
. In
[
CH17
]
,
Corti and Heuberger classified surfaces with
k\times\frac{1}{3}(1,1)
singularities by using primarily birational geometry. The classification with a single orbifold point of type
\frac{1}{r}(1,1)
has been given in
[
CP20
]
.
The study of the existence of orbifold Kahler–Einstein metrics was initiated in
[
JK01
]
and further investigated in
[
CS13
,
CPS10
,
KP15
]
.

One of the interesting problems is to construct the algebraic varieties embedded in some weighted projective space that can be described in terms of explicit equations. This allows us to use the properties of their graded rings to study their various geometric properties.
In
[
CS13
]
, a classification of index 1 del Pezzo hypersurfaces having a specific type of log-canonical threshold has been provided.
The ordered tuples
(d_{1},d_{2},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4},w_{5})
such that
X_{d_{1},d_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{5}),
is a wellformed and quasismooth weighted
complete intersection del Pezzo surface, have been classified by using combinatorial methods by Mayanskiy

[
May16
]
. The classification of low codimension del Pezzo surfaces with certain orbifold points appeared in
[
Qur20
]
.
The construction of biregular models of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces in non complete intersection case has been initiated in
[
Qur19
]
.
In the process, we provided new descriptions of some of the examples that already appeared in
[
CH17
]
. Our motivation is to produce an extensive testing field to study various phenomena like mirror symmetry and
K
-stability of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces.

In this article, our main emphasize is on the construction of del Pezzo surfaces. However, weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is one of the most easily described codimension 4 Gorenstein variety. The Hilbert series formula (
3.5
) and corresponding graded ring structure can be used to construct other interesting classes of polarized orbifolds, like Calabi–Yau 3-folds,
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano 3-folds and 3-folds of general type.

2
we recall basic definitions required in this article. We describe the notion of a weighted

Preliminaries and Notations

2.1
Preliminaries

An orbifold point of type
\frac{1}{r}(a,b)
is the quotient
\pi:\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{2}/\mu_{r}
given by

\mu_{r}\ni\epsilon:(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(\epsilon^{a}x_{1},\epsilon^{b}x_{2}).

It is called an isolated orbifold point if
r
is relatively prime to
a
and
b
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
AK14
]

Let
Q=\frac{1}{r}(a,b)
be an orbifold point and
d=\text{gcd}(a+b,r)
,
k=(a+b)/d
and
m=r/d
. Then we can present
Q
as
\frac{1}{dm}(1,mk-1)
and if
d<m
then it is called a (rigid)
R
-singularity.

Definition 2.2
.

[
Qur19
]

A
biregular model of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces
is an infinite series of families of del Pezzo surfaces satisfying the following conditions.

(i)

A family of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces exists for each parameter
r(n)
for all positive integers
n,
where
r
is a linear function of
n
.

(ii)

The embedding
\mathbb{P}(w_{i})
of each family of surfaces has at least one weight equal to
r
and The rest of the weights
w_{i}
and
(-K_{X})^{2}
are functions of
r
.

If the affine cone over of the surface is smooth outside the origin then we call it to be quasismooth. A wellformed surface does not contain any orbifold singularities in codimension 1.

Weighted

The formula allows us to compute the graded free resolution and orbifold canonical class of

Consider the Segre embedding of

\mathbb{P}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}_{u_{1},u_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{7}\left(s_{i}t_{j}u_{k}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{ijk}\right),1\leq i,j,k\leq 2.

Then the image of

2\times 2
minors of the cube

(3.1)

X_{111}
X_{121}
X_{221}
X_{211}
X_{112}
X_{122}
X_{222}
X_{212}

\mathbb{P}^{7}

It is well known that it is a Gorenstein variety of codimension 4 having a

[

]

We use the structure of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as a quotient of

by its group of upper triangular matrices

Let

is the product of the corresponding groups of upper triangular matrices. Then
G
has rank 6 character lattice
\Lambda=\Hom(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{6}

.
We choose a vector

\mu=(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},c_{1},c_{2})\in\Hom(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}),

for

Then we define weighted

(3.2)

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}=\left(C(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\setminus\{0\}\right)/\mathbb{C}^{\times}

where

X_{ijk}\mapsto\lambda^{a_{i}+b_{j}+c_{k}}X_{ijk},\;\;i,j,k\in\{1,2\}.

Thus we have the embedding

(3.3)

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\hookrightarrow w\mathbb{P}^{7}\left(a_{i}+b_{j}+c_{k}\right),i,j,k\in\{1,2\}

with image defined by
2\times 2

3.1

(3.4)

\deg\left(X_{ikj}\right)=a_{i}+b_{j}+c_{k},\;\;\forall i,j,k.

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
is wellformed then

K_{\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{O}(-l).

The first two terms in the numerator represent the
9\times 16
resolution.
For instance, the term
t^{a_{1}-a_{2}+s}=t^{(a_{1}+b_{1}+c_{1})+(a_{1}+b_{2}+c_{2})}
has the degree of the equation
X_{111}X_{122}-X_{112}X_{121}
.

Example 3.2
.

For
\mu=(0,0:0,0:1,1)
we get the straight
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{7}
with the canonical bundle
\mathcal{O}(-2)
which is a del Pezzo 3-fold of index 2. The hyperplane section of it gives the classical smooth del Pezzo surface of degree 6.

Example 3.3
.

For
\mu=(0,1:0,1:1,2)
we get the embedding

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{6}(1,2^{3},3^{3},4)

with the canonical bundle
\mathcal{O}(-5)
. The intersection with a generic quartic

X:=\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\cap(4)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,2^{3},3^{3})

is a family of orbifold del Pezzo surface of index 1 with 4 orbifold points of type
\frac{1}{3}(1,1)
. The toric construction for this surface was given in
[
CH17
]
.

4
Proofs of the results

We first describe the general strategy of proving the main theorem and list all the steps required in the proof of the main results. Then we describe the proof of main theorems in representative cases.

4.1
General strategy of proofs

For each model, and the sporadic examples of §
5.2
, the proofs are divided into several steps. We briefly discuss them here as the detailed explanation can be found in
[
Qur19
]
.

4.1.1
Computer search algorithm

This section recalls the algorithm from
[
Qur17
]
which we used to compute the list of candidate rigid del Pezzo surfaces. An important ingredient is the Riemann–Roch formula

[
BRZ13
]
which gives a formula for Hilbert series
P_{X}(t)
of
X
as the sum of a smooth part and an orbifold part.
It states that if an orbifold
X
has a collection
\mathcal{B}=\{k_{i}\times Q_{i}:m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}
of isolated orbifold points then;

(4.1)

P_{X}(t)=P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)+\sum_{Q_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}k_{i}P_{Q_{i}}(t),

where
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{smooth}}
gives the smooth part and represents the orbifold part of the Hilbert Series. Indeed if the variety is smooth then
P_{X}(t)=P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
. The algorithm provides a complete list of orbifolds having fixed Fano index, dimension, and canonical class
K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(kD)
in a given ambient space. Indeed, if
X
is a del Pezzo surface of index
I
then
k=-I.
We briefly recall the steps of our algorithm for the case of del Pezzo surfaces.

(i)

Calculate the Hilbert series and the canonical divisor class of the of
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
.

(ii)

Find all possible embeddings in
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
of index
I
, i.e.
K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(-I)
by enumerating weights and using the adjunction formula.

(iii)

For each embedding computed at step (ii), calculate the Hilbert series of
X
and the term representing its smooth part
P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
.

(iv)

Compute the list of all possible rigid orbifold points from the weights of the
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
that may lie on
X
.

(v)

Form all possible subsets of orbifold points and for each set
\mathcal{B}
, determine if the difference
P_{X}(t)-P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
can be equal to
\sum_{Q_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}k_{i}P_{Q_{i}}(t)
for some
k_{i}
.

(vi)

X
is a candidate rigid del Pezzo surface with a basket of singularities
\mathcal{B}
if the multiplicities
k_{i}
are non-negative.
Repeat steps (iii) to (vi) for each embedding computed at step (ii).

4.1.2
Pattern analysis

In this step, we analyze the data of candidate examples obtained by using a computer algorithm to spot a pattern among the candidates to form a model of del Pezzo surfaces. The key indicator of the pattern is usually the number of orbifold points and their multiplicities in the basket of orbifold points
\mathcal{B}
.

4.1.3
Proving existence

The next step is to prove the existence of such models as a complete intersection of some
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
or cone over it as a wellformed and quasismooth family of algebraic varieties. The existence of a family of del Pezzo surfaces with the given Hilbert series follows straightforwardly from the existence of ambient
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
. However, it does not guarantee that given del Pezzo surface has the same singularities as those predicted by the computer search algorithm of
4.1.1
or it is quasismooth. The singularities predicted by computer search may not necessarily lie on
X
. The crucial part of the existence is to show that del Pezzo orbifold
X
contains exactly the same orbifold points as suggested by the output in the computer search.
The wellformedness can be deduced as a consequence of the existence of correct orbifold points, as if
X
contains only point singularities then it avoids any orbifold locus of
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
having a dimension greater than or equal to one.

The proof of the quasismoothness requires an analysis of two different types of loci: orbifold loci and base loci.
The orbifold loci are restrictions of the singular strata of
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
to
X
and the base loci appear as loci of the linear systems of the successive intersection of weighted homogeneous forms while taking intersection with
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
. A general member in each family del Pezzo surface appearing in these models is quasismooth, outside of these loci, due to Bertini’s theorem.

4.1.4
Non existence of degeneration to a toric variety

Each family in our models of orbifold del Pezzo surfaces is locally
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein rigid. The first plurigenus
h^{0}(-K_{X})
(and all plurigenera
h^{0}(-mK))
are invariant under
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein deformations. Moreover
h^{0}(-K_{X})>0
for any toric Fano variety as the origin is always contained in the corresponding Fano polytope. Thus each family in our model which has
h^{0}(-K)=0
does not admit a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degeneration to a toric variety. In total, 5 out of 13 models have
h^{0}(-K_{X})=0
. These examples are of interest in the sense they can not be constructed using toric methods.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We provide a proof for one of the biregular models appearing in Theorem
1.1
. In all the index models, orbifold del Pezzo surfaces are obtained by using the intersection of some weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
variety with a form of degree
r
, which can be assigned to the same variable in the
weight cube and in the equations of
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
for each index model. Therefore we get the same base locus for each index model. Thus the below proof presents base locus calculations for all models appearing in Theorem
1.1

and hence of quasismoothness.

4.2.1
Biregular model
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{I}_{4}

This is an example of a model where the base locus is bigger than zero dimensional but geometrically it does not change for any family of the model, as described below. In this model, our parameter is
r=3n+8
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and the rest of the weights in terms of
r
are given as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lllll}p=r-3,&q=r-2,&s=r+2,&t=2r-3&m=2r-1.\end{array}

In this model each surface will have Fano index
r
, so both the set of ambient weights and Fano index will vary for each
n
. If we choose a parameter
w=(0,2:0,p:1,r)
, we get the embedding of weighted
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
3-fold,

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,3,q,r^{2},s,t,m)\text{ with }K_{\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{O}(-2r).

Then intersection of
\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}
with a generic weighted homogeneous form
H_{r}
of degree
r
:

X:=\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\cap(H_{r})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,3,q,r,s,t,m)=\mathbb{P}(a,b,c,d,e,f,g)

is a family of del Pezzo surfaces of Fano index
r
. The equations are presented by
2\times 2
minors of the weight cube:

a_{1}
c_{q}
H_{r}
b_{3}
d_{r}
f_{t}
g_{m}
e_{s}

There are two different cases we need to discuss for the orbifold strata of
X
:
r
is odd and
r
is even.

Index
r
is odd
:
If
X
has an odd Fano index then all the weights of the ambient
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
are odd. None of the weights share any common factor except
3,q
and
m
as
q
and
m
are multiples of
3
. Thus it is clear that they are all at isolated orbifold points if they lie on
X
. The weight
3
locus is

\mathrm{w}\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{P}(3,q,m):=\mathbb{V}(bc,bg,cg)\subset\mathbb{P}(b,c,g).

This locus is manifestly 3 coordinate points
b_{3},c_{q}
and
g_{m}
. The coordinate points
e_{s}
and
f_{t}
also lies on
X
. The coordinate point

does not lie on

.
The summary of tangent and local variables on each coordinate point is given as follows.

\begin{array}[]{cccll}P_{\mathrm{orb}}&X\cap P_{\mathrm{orb}}&\text{Tangent}|\text{local variables}&\text{Conditions on forms}&\textrm{type }\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr m&\text{coordinate pt }g_{m}&a,b,c,d\;|\;e,f&&\frac{1}{m}(s,t)\\
t&\text{coordinate pt }f_{t}&a,b,e,d\;|\;c,m&H_{r}=d+\cdots&\frac{1}{t}(2,q)\\

Therefore

Index

is even:
If the index

is even then the weights are

coming from the ambient
\mathbb{P}^{6}(w_{i})
. The rest of the orbifold strata is the same as in the case of an odd index. The weight two locus is

X\cap\mathbb{P}(q,r,s):=\mathbb{V}(d_{r}H_{r},c_{q}e_{s},H_{r}e_{s},d_{r}c_{q})\subset\mathbb{P}(c,d,e).

This is just a union of two coordinate points of weight

and

, which we already accounted for as isolated points. So we do not get any new orbifold points on this weight 2 locus.

Now we discuss the base locus of the linear system
|\mathcal{O}(r)|
. None of the weights except

\mathbb{P}(3,q,s,t,m)

(4.2)

Thus

is quasismooth beside the reduced part of the above locus and essentially this locus does not change for any value of parameter

by using the computer algebra suffices the quasismoothness of

. One can use the following
Magma

procedure MI4(n)
r:=3*n+8;

P<x1,x2,x3,x4,x5,x6,x8>:=ProjectiveSpace(Rationals(),[1,r,q,t,3,s,m]);
fr:=&+[ Random([1..5])*m : m in MonomialsOfWeightedDegree(CoordinateRing(P),r)];
Eq:=[-x6*fr+x5*x8,-x4*fr+x3*x8,-x4*x6+x2*x8,-x4*x5+x1*x8,-x4*x5+x2*fr,

SXred := ReducedSubscheme(JacobianSubrankScheme(X));
if Dimension(SXred) eq -1 then
printf "X is a quasismooth";
end if;
end procedure;

and

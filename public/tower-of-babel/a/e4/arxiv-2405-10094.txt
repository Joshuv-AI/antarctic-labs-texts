Decidability of Quasi-Dense Modal Logics
Piotr Ostropolski-Nalewaja, Tim S. Lyon

Abstract
. The decidability of axiomatic extensions of the modal logic 𝖪 \mathsf{K} with modal reduction principles , i.e. axioms of the form ◇ k ​ p → ◇ n ​ p \Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{n}p , has remained a long-standing open problem. In this paper, we make significant progress toward solving this problem and show that decidability holds for a large subclass of these logics, namely, for quasi-dense logics . Such logics are extensions of 𝖪 \mathsf{K} with with modal reduction axioms such that 0 < k < n 0<k<n (dubbed quasi-density axioms ). To prove decidability, we define novel proof systems for quasi-dense logics consisting of disjunctive existential rules, which are first-order formulae typically used to specify ontologies in the context of database theory. We show that such proof systems can be used to generate proofs and models of modal formulae, and provide an intricate model-theoretic argument showing that such generated models can be encoded as finite objects called templates . By enumerating templates of bound size, we obtain an ExpSpace decision procedure as a consequence.

1.
Introduction

Modal logic is a rich sub-discipline of mathematical logic, having important applications in numerous areas from computer science to legal theory. For example, temporal logics have been used in the verification of programs
(
2
)
, epistemic logics have found use in distributed systems
(
11
)
, and multi-agent logics have been used to analyze legal concepts
(
5
)
. Modal logics are typically obtained by extending propositional logic with a set of modalities, which prefix formulae and qualify the truth of a proposition. In this paper, we focus on a class of normal modal logics
(
4
)
, dubbed
quasi-dense (modal) logics
, whose language extends propositional classical logic with the possibility modality
\Diamond
and the necessity modality
\Box
.

Quasi-dense logics extend the minimal normal modal logic
\mathsf{K}
(cf. Blackburn et al.
(
4
)
) with a finite set of
quasi-dense axioms
of the form
\Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{n}p
such that
0<k<n
.
1
1

1

The name ‘quasi-dense’ is obtained from the fact that such axioms are canonical for a generalization of the density property. In particular, the axiom
\Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{n}p
is canonical for the following property: if a directed
R
path of length
k
exists from a world
w
to
u
in a Kripke model
M=(W,R,V)
, then a directed
R
path of length
n
exists from
w
to
u
as well. When
0<k<n
, this implies the existence of
n-1
points ‘between’
w
and
u
when an
R
path of length
k
exists, thus generalizing the usual density property.
This class of axioms forms a prominent subclass of the so-called
modal reduction principles

(
14
)
, which are modal axioms of the same form, though without the restriction that
k<n
. Logics extending
\mathsf{K}
with modal reduction principles were studied at least as far back as 1976 by van Bentham
(
14
)
. The decidability of such logics has remained a longstanding open problem (see
(
15
)
for a discussion), though partial solutions have been obtained.
For example, it is well-known that the decidability of
\mathsf{T}
,
\mathsf{K4}
,
\mathsf{S4}
, and
\mathsf{K}\oplus\Diamond p\rightarrow\Diamond\Diamond p
can be obtained via filtration techniques
(
4
)
and each logic
\mathsf{K}\oplus\Diamond^{n}p\rightarrow\Diamond p
is known to be decidable as well (cf.
(
13
)
). Notable however, is the result of Zakharyaschev
(
16
)
, who showed that any extension of
\mathsf{K4}
with modal reduction principles is decidable. In this paper, we obtain a new substantial result, showing all quasi-dense logics decidable via a novel model-theoretic argument. Thus, we provide additional tools for approaching the general problem and make further headway toward a complete solution.

Our approach to decidability is unique to the literature on modal logics in that we rely on tools from database theory to make our argument. In particular, we rely on
disjunctive existential rules
—normally used in ontology specification—which are first-order formulae of the form
\forall{\scalebox{0.9}{$\bar{x},\bar{y}$}}\;\beta(\bar{x},\bar{y})\to\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}\exists{\scalebox{0.9}{$\bar{z}_{i}$}}\;\alpha_{i}(\bar{y}_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
such that
\beta(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and each
\alpha_{i}(\bar{y}_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
are conjunctions of atomic formulae over constants and the variables
\bar{x},\bar{y}
and
\bar{y}_{i},\bar{z}_{i}
, respectively
(
7
)
. In the context of database theory, a finite collection
\mathcal{R}
of such rules constitutes an
ontology
, while a finite collection
\mathcal{D}
of first-order atoms over constants constitutes a
database
. Together,
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{R})
form a
knowledge base
, which may be queried to extract implicit information from the explicitly specified knowledge. The primary tool in querying a knowledge base
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{R})
is the so-called
(disjunctive) chase
, which is an algorithm that exhaustively applies rules from
\mathcal{R}
to
\mathcal{D}
, ultimately resulting in a selection of models of the knowledge base to which queries can be mapped
(
7
,
6
)
.

In this paper, we rely on the above tools, representing a modal formula
\varphi
as a database
\mathcal{D}_{\varphi}
and encoding a tableau calculus with a set
\mathcal{R}
of disjunctive existential rules. Running the disjunctive chase over
(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
simulates a tableau algorithm, effectively yielding a tableau proof or model of
\varphi
. We find this approach to be preferable to utilizing a standard tableau approach for a couple of reasons: first, this saves us from introducing the various notions required to specify a tableau calculus, which only forms a minor part of our overall decidability argument. Second, the tools used in the context of (disjunctive) existential rules are already perfectly suited for the intricate model-theoretic arguments we employ. To put it succinctly, this approach is more parsimonious than it would otherwise be.

We note that the product of the disjunctive chase, which we denote by
Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
, consists of a potentially infinite set of
instances
, i.e. potentially infinite sets of atomic first-order formulae, which intuitively correspond to maximal branches in a tableau. When all such instances contain a contradictory formula
\mathsf{contr}
,
Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
witnesses the unsatisfiability of
\varphi
, and when at least one such instance omits
\mathsf{contr}
, that instance can be transformed into a Kripke model witnessing the satisfiability of
\varphi
. Since
Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
can be infinitely large, the central issue that needs to be overcome to obtain decidability is the ‘finitization’ of
Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
. Indeed, we show that an instance
\mathcal{C}\in Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R})
is free of
\mathsf{contr}

iff
a finite encoding of the model
\mathcal{C}
exists, which we refer to as a
template
. As only a finite number of possible templates exist, we obtain a
ExpSpace
decision procedure for each quasi-dense logic as a corollary.

This paper is organized as follows: In
Section

2
, we define the language and semantics of quasi-dense logics, preliminary notions for disjunctive existential rules, and the various model-theoretic tools required for our decidability argument. In
Section

3
, we show that the disjunctive chase constitutes a sound and complete ‘proof system’ for quasi-dense logics. In
Section

4
, we employ an intricate model-theoretic argument showing a correspondence between models generated by the disjunctive chase and finite encodings of such models (i.e. templates), yielding a
ExpSpace
decision procedure for quasi-dense logics. Last, in
Section

5
, we share some concluding remarks and discuss future work.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Modal Logic

We let
\mathrm{Prop}:=\{p,q,r,\ldots\}
be a set of
propositional atoms
(which may be annotated), and define the
modal language

\mathcal{L}_{M}
to be the smallest set of
modal formulae
generated by the following grammar:

\varphi::=p\ |\ \neg p\ |\ \varphi\lor\varphi\ |\ \varphi\land\varphi\ |\ \Diamond\varphi\ |\ \Box\varphi

where
p
ranges over
\mathrm{Prop}
. The negation
\dot{\neg}\varphi
of a formula
\varphi
is defined recursively as shown below:

•

\dot{\neg}p:=\neg p
;

•

\dot{\neg}\neg p:=p
;

•

\dot{\neg}(\psi\lor\chi):=\dot{\neg}\psi\land\dot{\neg}\chi
;

•

\dot{\neg}(\psi\land\chi):=\dot{\neg}\psi\lor\dot{\neg}\chi
;

•

\dot{\neg}\Diamond\psi:=\Box\dot{\neg}\psi
;

•

\dot{\neg}\Box\psi:=\Diamond\dot{\neg}\psi
.

We define the
modal depth

d(\varphi)
of a modal formula
\varphi
recursively as follows: (1)
d(p)=d(\neg p)=0
, (2)
d(\psi\circ\chi)=\max\{d(\psi),d(\chi)\}
for
\circ\in\{\lor,\land\}
, and (3)
d(\triangledown\psi)=d(\psi)+1
for
\triangledown\in\{\Diamond,\Box\}
. Given a modal formula
\varphi
, define the set
\mathsf{ms}_{i}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
’s
depth
i
modal sub-formulae
as follows:
\psi\in\mathsf{ms}_{i}(\varphi)

iff

\psi
is a sub-formula of
\varphi
,
\psi
is of the form
\triangledown\chi
with
\triangledown\in\{\Diamond,\Box\}
, and
d(\varphi)-d(\psi)=i
.

Modal formulae are interpreted over special kinds of Kripke models, which we call
\mathcal{P}
-models
, defined below. We note that
\mathcal{P}
is taken to be a finite set of first-order formulae called
quasi-density properties
. A
quasi-density property
(or,
QDP
for short) is a first-order formula
p=\forall x,y(R^{k}(x,y)\rightarrow R^{n}(x,y))
such that
0<k<n
and where
R^{\ell}(x,y):=(x=y)
if
\ell=0
and
R^{\ell+1}:=\exists z(R^{\ell}(x,z)\land R(z,y))
. We also use
k\to k_{+}
to denote a QDP of the above form, where
n=k_{+}
suggests that
k_{+}
is larger than
k
. This notation will be used when
k
should be made explicit and is relevant to the discussion.

Definition 1 (
\mathcal{P}
-Model).

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a finite set of QDPs. A
\mathcal{P}
-frame
is defined to be an ordered pair
F(\mathcal{P}):=(W,R)
such that
W
is a non-empty set of points, called
worlds
, and the
accessibility relation

R\subseteq W\times W
satisfies every
p\in\mathcal{P}
. A
\mathcal{P}
-model
is defined to be a tuple
M(\mathcal{P})=(F(\mathcal{P}),V)
such that
F
is a
\mathcal{P}
-frame and
V:\mathrm{Prop}\to 2^{W}
is a
valuation function
mapping propositions to sets of worlds.

Definition 2 (Semantic Clauses).

Let
M(\mathcal{P})=(W,R,V)
be a
\mathcal{P}
-model. We define a forcing relation
\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}p

iff

w\in V(p)
;

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\neg p

iff

w\not\in V(p)
;

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi\lor\psi

iff

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
or
M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\psi
;

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi\land\psi

iff

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
and
M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\psi
;

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\Diamond\varphi

iff

\exists u\in W
,
R(w,u)
and
M(\mathcal{P}),u\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
;

•

M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\Box\varphi

iff

\forall u\!\in\!W
, if
R(w,u)
, then
M(\mathcal{P}),u\!\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\!\varphi
;

•

M(\mathcal{P})\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi

iff

\forall w\in W
,
M(\mathcal{P}),w\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
.

A modal formulae
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{M}
is
\mathcal{P}
-valid
, written
\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
,
iff
for all
\mathcal{P}
-models
M(\mathcal{P})
,
M(\mathcal{P})\Vdash_{\mathcal{P}}\varphi
. We define the modal logic
\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{M}
to be the smallest set of
\mathcal{P}
-valid formulae, and note that
\mathsf{L}(\emptyset)
is the well-known minimal, normal modal logic
\mathsf{K}
.

In this paper, we prove that each logic
\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
is decidable, that is, we provide a computable function
f
such that for any quasi-dense logic
\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
and modal formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{M}
,
f(\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P}),\varphi)=1
if
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
and
f(\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P}),\varphi)=0
if
\varphi\not\in\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
. We note that this provides a partial answer to a
\tilde{40}
year old problem, first discussed by Wolter and Zakharyaschev
(
15
, Problem 8)
. Therefore, the main theorem we demonstrate is the following:

Theorem 3.

Given a logic
\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
and a modal formula
\varphi
, it is decidable to check if
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{P})
.

From Modal Logic to Rules

For the remainder of the paper, let us fix a set

be two terms of

Note that if

is at depth smaller than

Enough groundwork has been laid to define the notion of a template. We encourage the reader to briefly skim through the rest of this section to understand how templates will be used to prove Lemma

Definition 14.

A

The core idea behind a template is that it serves as both a relaxation of the chase (as witnessed by

encode at least one element of the chase (see

\mathcal{C}\in Ch(\mathcal{D}_{\varphi},\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}\cup\mathcal{P})

Proof.

We argue that

In essence, the above observation tells us that if there exists a

(1)

5.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we provided a novel algorithm that uniformly decides quasi-dense logics – a substantial subclass of ‘modal reduction logics’ (i.e. logics axiomatized by extending
\mathsf{K}
with modal reduction principles). Our method relied on an established correspondence between members of the disjunctive chase and finite instances, called templates, which encode Kripke models witnessing the satisfiability of a modal formula. In the process, we provided a toolkit of model-theoretic operations which we aim to extend and adapt as required to approach the general decidability problem of modal reduction logics. We remark that our result is orthogonal to the other, notable class of modal reduction logics shown decidable by Zakharyaschev
(
16
)
, and thus we have taken a meaningful step forward and improved our understanding of this longstanding open problem.

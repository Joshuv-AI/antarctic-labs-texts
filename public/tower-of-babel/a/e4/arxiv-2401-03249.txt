Condition numbers for real eigenvalues of real elliptic ensemble: weak non-normality at the edge
Wojciech Tarnowski

Abstract
We calculate the asymptotics of the joint probability density function of the real eigenvalue and the square of the corresponding eigenvalue condition number for the real elliptic ensemble in the double scaling regime of almost Hermiticity and close to the edge of the spectrum. As a byproduct, we also calculate the one-parameter deformation of the Scorer’s function.

[

[

21

Much less, however, is known about the properties of their eigenvectors. Early studies followed the spirit of symmetric RMT and focused mostly on the localization properties
[
22
]
. Dropping the symmetry or, in general, normality (a matrix is normal if it commutes with its transpose), opens a new dimension for eigenvectors, because for each eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
there is not one, but two eigenvectors – left
\left<L_{i}\right|
and right
\left|R_{i}\right>
– satisfying their own eigenproblems:
X\left|R_{i}\right>=\lambda_{i}\left|R_{i}\right>
and
\left<L_{i}\right|X=\left<L_{i}\right|\lambda_{i}

\left<R_{i}|R_{j}\right>\neq\delta_{ij}\neq\left<L_{i}|L_{j}\right>
. Instead, left and right eigenvectors are normalized to
\left<L_{i}|R_{j}\right>=\delta_{ij}
, forming a biorthogonal set.

Traces of eigenvector non-orthogonality are observed in open quantum chaotic systems through the decay laws
[
23
]
, excess noise in open laser resonators
[
24
,
25
]
, resonance width shifts
[
26

[

[
33
,
34
,
35

[

37

[

39

[

[
45
,
46
]
and the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis
[
47

The study of eigenvector non-normality in RMT was initiated by Chalker and Mehlig
[
48

49

who introduced the matrix of overlaps
O_{ij}=\left<L_{i}|L_{j}\right>\left<R_{j}|R_{i}\right>

[

[
52

53

N=2
and turned the attention to their mean values by introducing the correlation functions

O_{1}(z)=\left<\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}O_{ii}\delta^{(2)}(z-\lambda_{i})\right>,\qquad O_{2}(z,w)=\left<\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l=1\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}^{N}O_{kl}\delta^{(2)}(z-\lambda_{k})\delta^{(2)}(w-\lambda_{l})\right>.

(1)

These objects are more tractable and became accessible with the use of large

Recently, Dubach and Bourgade
[

[
67

calculated the joint probability density function (jpdf) of the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvector self-overlap in the complex Ginibre ensemble. Fyodorov obtained also the jpdf

{\cal P}_{N}(z,t)=\left<\sum_{k}\delta(t+1-O_{ii})\delta(z-\lambda_{i})\right>

(2)

for the real eigenvalues in the real Ginibre, where the sum is performed over real eigenvalues and jpdf is normalized to the total number of real eigenvalues. Note that since
O_{ii}\geq 1
, it is more convenient to consider a shifted overlap
t=O_{ii}-1
. Subsequent results were also obtained for various other ensembles
[
69
,
70
,
72
,
71
]
and the eigenvector non-orthogonality was approached from other directions as well
[
73
]
.

For symmetric (in general normal) matrices the overlap matrix reduces to the identity matrix. It is therefore tempting to study eigenvector non-orthogonality at the transition between symmetric and asymmetric matrices. A natural model for such an analysis is the real elliptic ensemble. Elements of such matrices are Gaussian and distributed according to the measure

P(X)dX=C_{N}^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2(1-\tau^{2})}\textup{Tr}(XX^{T}-\tau X^{2})\right)dX,

(3)

where
dX=\prod_{ij=1}^{N}dx_{ij}
is the flat Lebesgue measure over its elements. The normalization constant reads
C_{N}=(2\pi)^{N^{2}/2}(1+\tau)^{N/2}(1-\tau^{2})^{N(N-1)/4}
. The parameter
\tau\in[0,1]
controls the correlation between elements on the opposite sides of the diagonal and provides continuous interpolation between the real Ginibre ensemble for
\tau=0
and the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) for
\tau=1
. In the limit
N\to\infty
for any fixed
0\leq\tau<1
the eigenvalue statistics fall into the bulk universality class of non-symmetric matrices. However, after tuning the rate of approaching symmetry so that the product
N(1-\tau)
is kept fixed one finds a new regime interpolating between GOE sine-kernel universality and non-Hermitian bulk universality. This regime was first studied for the complex elliptic ensemble and was dubbed
weak non-Hermiticity

[
74
,
75
,
76
]
(see
[
9
]
for the results in the real case).

Recently, an analogous regime was found at the level of jpdf (
2
) for the real eigenvalues of the real elliptic ensemble. The self-overlap transitions from
O_{ii}=1
at
\tau=1
to
O_{ii}\sim N
for fixed
\tau<1
. At the transition regime, called
weak non-normality
, the overlap is of order 1 with a non-trivial heavy-tailed density. The heavy-tailedness was found to be the most robust feature of self-overlap distribution, as it appears also for finite
N

[
48
]
and rank-1 perturbations of Hermitian matrices
[
28
]
.

The jpdf for the elliptic ensemble can be written in terms of the joint density
P_{N}^{\tau}(z,q)
of an eigenvalue
z
and rescaled and shifted overlap
q=(1-\tau)(O_{ii}-1)
. The jpdf (
2
) is recovered via
{\cal P}_{N}(z,t)=(1-\tau)^{-1}{\cal P}_{N}^{\tau}(z,\frac{t}{1-\tau})
and
{\cal P}_{N}^{\tau}
reads
[
72
, Theorem 2.1]

{\cal P}_{N}^{\tau}(z,q)=\frac{1}{2(1+\tau)\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2(1+\tau)}\left(1+\frac{q}{1+q}\right)}}{\sqrt{q(q+1)}}\left(\frac{q}{q+1+\tau}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}-1}Q_{N}(z,q,\tau),

(4)

where

Q_{N}(z,q,\tau)=\frac{(1+\tau-2z^{2})\tilde{P}_{N-1}(z)+z\tilde{R}_{N-1}(z)+\tau z\tilde{R}_{N-2}(z)}{1+q}+\frac{z^{2}\tilde{P}_{N-1}(z)}{(1+q)^{2}}+\frac{\tau^{2}(1+\tau)^{2}N\tilde{P}_{N-2}(z)}{(1+\tau+q)^{2}}\\
+\frac{(1+\tau)(1-\tau^{2})\tilde{S}_{N-2}(z)}{1+\tau+q}-\frac{\tau(1+\tau)z\tilde{R}_{N-2}(z)}{(1+q)(1+\tau+q)}

(5)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k!}((k+1)p_{k}^{2}(z)-kp_{k+1}(z)p_{k-1}(z)),

(6)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k!}((k+2)p_{k+1}(z)p_{k}(z)-kp_{k+2}(z)p_{k-1}(z)),

(7)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{N-k}{k!}((k+1)p_{k}^{2}(z)-kp_{k+1}(z)p_{k-1}(z)).

(8)

Here
p_{k}(z)=\tau^{k/2}\textup{He}_{k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)=\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{k/2}\mathrm{H}_{k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)
are the Hermite polynomials with the leading term
z^{k}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The presented form slightly differs from the one in
[
72
]
, hence we used tilded variables to distinguish from the original (non-tilded) notation. They are related as follows

P_{N}(z)=N!\tilde{P}_{N+1}(z),\qquad 2R_{N}(z)=N!\tilde{R}_{N+1}(z),\qquad NP_{N-1}(z)-T_{N-1}(z)=(N-1)!\tilde{S}_{N}(z).

2
Statement and discussion of the main results

The elliptic ensemble renders yet another weak non-Hermiticity regime. At
\tau=1
near the edge of the spectrum eigenvalue statistics are described by the Airy kernel, while at strong non-Hermiticity the kernel falls into the error function universality class. The interpolation between those two was found much later after the bulk weak non-Hermiticity
[
77
,
79
,
78
]
. The edge behavior is particularly interesting, since the rightmost (i.e. the one with the largest real part) eigenvalue plays an essential role in the stability of linear systems.
It is therefore natural to ask how stable the eigenvalues are at the edge of the spectrum. An insight into this problem can be obtained by analyzing the edge behavior of jpdf (
2
). Recent work
[
72
]
studied the limiting distribution of (
4
) at strong non-normality.
The main result of this work is complementing that calculation with the double scaling regime in which the spectral edge is probed on the scale
N^{-1/6}
and departure from symmetry on the scale
N^{-1/3}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\cal P}_{N}(z,t)
be defined as in (

is taken with respect to the real elliptic ensemble given by (

\displaystyle T_{3}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{\prime 2}(p)-\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(p)\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{\prime\prime}(p)\right]dp

(11)

\displaystyle T_{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{2}T_{3}+\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(p)dp

(12)

\displaystyle T_{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\zeta T_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(\zeta)+b^{2}\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(p)dp

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T_{3}+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(\zeta)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{\prime}(\zeta)-\zeta\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(p)dp.

(14)

[

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
{\cal P}^{w.e.}
be defined as in Theorem
2.1
. The following property holds

\rho_{b}(\zeta):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\zeta,t)dt=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(p)dp+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)\left(1-\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(p)dp\right).

(15)

It is clearly visible from (
10

\lim_{b\to 0}{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\zeta,t)=0
for any
t>0
, however
{\cal P}^{w.e.}
is singular at
t=0
. Corollary
2.2
shows that this singularity is integrable, and thus
{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\zeta,t)
tends to
\rho_{0}(\zeta)\delta(t)
as
b\to 0
, in agreement with the eigenvector orthogonality in the symmetric limit.

The first step in the proof of Corollary
2.2
is the change of integration variable
u=\frac{b^{2}}{t}
. This results in a non-trivial integral, which can be considered as one-parameter deformation of the Scorer’s function
[
81
]
(see also
[
82

). The evaluation of this integral is a result that deserves its own interest.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}
and define
\mathrm{Hi}_{b}(\zeta):=\pi^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\zeta u-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}u^{2}-\frac{1}{3}u^{3}\right)du
. The following result holds

\mathrm{Hi}_{b}(\zeta)=\mathrm{Bi}_{ib}(\zeta)\int_{-\infty}^{\zeta}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(t)dt-\mathrm{Ai}_{ib}(\zeta)\int_{-\infty}^{\zeta}\mathrm{Bi}_{b}(t)dt,

(16)

where
\mathrm{Bi}_{b}(\zeta):=e^{\frac{b^{2}\zeta}{2}+\frac{b^{6}}{12}}\mathrm{Bi}\left(\zeta+\frac{b^{4}}{4}\right)
and
\mathrm{Bi}
is the second solution of the Airy equation.

Interestingly, the scaling in Theorem
2.1
does not involve the variable
t
. This means that the self-overlap fluctuates around
1
on the scale
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Exactly the same scale is found at weak non-normality in the bulk
[
72
]
. This stays in contrast with the strong non-normality, where the self-overlap in the bulk grows linearly with the matrix size
[
48
,
41
,
67
]
, while at the edge its growth is slower (
\sim\sqrt{N}
)
[
59
,
67
,
72
]
. However, the two regimes of weak non-normality are achieved at different symmetry-braking scales. Weak non-normality in the bulk requires
1-\tau\sim N^{-1}
, while at the edge the departure from symmetry is larger, since
1-\tau\sim N^{-1/3}
.
Nevertheless, as presented in Fig.
1
, as one moves deeper into the bulk, the peak of the overlap distribution moves to the right and the right tail gets heavier. To account for the fact that
{\cal P}(z,t)
encodes also the density of eigenvalues, which changes with
\zeta
, we focused on the density of the overlap, conditioned on the eigenvalue, i.e.

\rho_{b}(t|\zeta)=\frac{{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\zeta,t)}{\rho_{b}(\zeta)},

(17)

where
\rho_{b}(\zeta)
is given by (
15
). Despite the fact that the matrix is very close to normal, eigenvectors are unlikely to be orthogonal. As shown in Fig.
1
, the conditional probability density is almost zero for small values of
t
. More precisely, it is exponentially suppressed by the factor
\exp(-b^{6}/t^{3})
.

Density of overlaps conditioned (see (
17
)) at various values of the corresponding eigenvalue with
b=0.6
. As
\zeta
moves deeper into the bulk, the density maxima move to the larger values, as well as the right tail of the distribution lifts up, a sign of increasing eigenvector non-orthogonality.

With the increasing non-normality, the distribution of the overlap moves towards larger values of
t
(see Fig.
2
), up to the strong non-normality regime. This is obtained from (
4
) by parameterizing
z=\sqrt{N}(1+\tau)+\delta\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}
and
t=\sigma\sqrt{N(1+\tau)/(1-\tau)}

[
72
]
. The limiting function can be recovered
from Theorem
2.1
by letting
b\to\infty
. The appropriate rescaling of
t
and
\sigma
can be deduced from the fact that in order to achieve
1-\tau=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the parameter
b
has to be of order
N^{1/6}
, which leads to the parameterization
\zeta=\sqrt{2}b\delta
and
t=\sqrt{2}b^{3}\sigma
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
{\cal P}^{w.e.}
be defined as in Theorem
2.1
. It admits the following limit

\lim_{b\to\infty}2b^{4}{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\sqrt{2}b\delta,\sqrt{2}b^{3}\sigma)=\frac{1}{4\pi\sigma^{2}}e^{\frac{\delta}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{4\sigma^{2}}}\left(e^{-2\delta^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-2\delta\right)\int\limits_{2\delta}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}dp\right).

(18)

This formula stays in full agreement with known results
[
67
,
72
]
. The prefactor
2b^{4}
is the Jacobian of the reparameterization.

Remark 2.5
.

The transition from symmetry to strong non-normality can be interpreted as a diffusion-like process. The deformed Airy function obeys diffusion equation in which
\eta=b^{2}/2
plays the role of time. More precisely, the function
\varphi(\zeta,\eta)=\exp(\zeta\eta+\frac{2}{3}\eta^{3})\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta+\eta^{2})
satisfies
\partial_{\eta}\varphi=\partial_{\zeta\zeta}\varphi
. This also explains the appearance of Gaussians in Corollary
2.4
.

Integral representations

Lemma 3.1
.

The functions
\tilde{P}_{N}
,

\displaystyle=

Proof.
We first notice that
\frac{d}{dx}p_{k}(x)=kp_{k-1}(x)

,
\tilde{R}_{N}
and
\tilde{S}_{N}

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x,y\to z}(\partial_{x}-\partial_{y})U_{N}(x,y),

(22)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x,y\to z}(\partial^{2}_{x}-\partial^{2}_{y})U_{N+1}(x,y),

(23)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x,y\to z}(\partial_{x}-\partial_{y})V_{N}(x,y).

(24)

The axuliary objects are defined as

U_{N}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{k!}p_{k+1}(x)p_{k}(y),\qquad\quad V_{N}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{N-k}{k!}p_{k+1}(x)p_{k}(y).

(25)

We use two standard integral representations of the Hermite polynomials

3.3
Strong non-normality limit

Proof of Corollary
2.4
.
From the right tail asymptotics of the Airy function
\mathrm{Ai}(x)\sim\frac{\exp(-\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2})}{2\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4}}
and its derivative
\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(x)\sim-\frac{x^{1/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\exp(-\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2})
we get the following

\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(b\delta\sqrt{2})=\frac{1}{b\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}}\right),\qquad\qquad\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}_{b}(b\delta\sqrt{2})=-\frac{\delta}{b^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{b^{3}}\right).

(46)

This in particular means that
T_{3}(b\delta\sqrt{2})=\mathcal{O}(b^{-1})
and since

\int_{b\delta\sqrt{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(p)dp=b\sqrt{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(bp\sqrt{2})dp=\frac{1}{2b\pi\sqrt{2}}\int_{2\delta}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}dp+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}}\right),

(47)

we also have
T_{2}(b\delta\sqrt{2})=\mathcal{O}(b)
. The exact asymptotics of
T_{3}
and
T_{2}
are not needed, since these terms are suppressed by
t^{3}
and
t^{2}
in the denominator in equation (
10
). In the second equality in (
47
) we used the asymptotics (
46
) under the integral, which can be justified by the dominated convergence theorem. This was followed by rescaling of the integration variable
p\to p/2
. Using this result, we calculate the asymptotics of the remaining terms

\displaystyle T_{1}(b\delta\sqrt{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2\pi\sqrt{2}}\int_{2\delta}^{\infty}e^{-p^{2}/2}dp+\mathcal{O}(1),

(48)

\displaystyle T_{0}(b\delta\sqrt{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-2\delta^{2}}-\frac{\delta}{2\pi}\int_{2\delta}^{\infty}e^{-p^{2}/2}dp+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{b}\right).

(49)

This result combined with taking remaining straightforward limits completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Integrating out the self-overlap

We start with two properties satisfied by the deformed Airy functions that are useful in manipulations of the formulas. They are straightforward to show, so we present them without a proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)=\exp(\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\zeta+\frac{1}{12}b^{6})\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta+\frac{b^{2}}{4})
and
\mathrm{Bi}_{b}(\zeta)=\exp(\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\zeta+\frac{1}{12}b^{6})\mathrm{Bi}(\zeta+\frac{b^{2}}{4})
, where
\mathrm{Ai}
and
\mathrm{Bi}
are the Airy functions. Let
F_{b}
and
G_{b}
be any combination of
\mathrm{Ai}_{b}
and
\mathrm{Bi}_{b}
. They satisfy the relations

\displaystyle F_{ib}(\zeta)G_{b}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{b}(\zeta)G_{ib}(\zeta)=F_{0}\left(\zeta+\frac{b^{4}}{4}\right)G_{0}\left(\zeta+\frac{b^{4}}{4}\right),

(50)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{ib}(\zeta)G_{b}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}}{2}F_{ib}(\zeta)G_{b}(\zeta)+F_{0}^{\prime}\left(\zeta+\frac{b^{4}}{4}\right)G_{0}\left(\zeta+\frac{b^{4}}{4}\right).

(51)

We are now ready to calculate the integral giving rise to the deformed Scorer’s function.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.
The exact form of the deformed Scorer’s function can be found by the variation of parameters method, since
\mathrm{Hi}_{b}
satisfies the inhomogeneous second order differential equation

\mathrm{Hi}_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)+b^{2}\mathrm{Hi}^{\prime}_{b}(\zeta)-\zeta\mathrm{Hi}_{b}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\pi},

(52)

which follows from
1=\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{2}+b^{2}u-\zeta)\exp(\zeta u-\frac{b^{2}u^{2}}{2}-\frac{u^{3}}{3})du
. Instead of proving that (
16
) matches the asymptotics, we observe that
\mathrm{Hi}_{b}
satisfies the (backward) diffusion equation in the variable
\eta=\frac{b^{2}}{2}
playing the role of time. That is, the function
h(\zeta,\eta)=\pi^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(\zeta u-\eta u^{2}-\frac{u^{3}}{3})du
satisfies equation
\partial_{\eta}h=-\partial_{\zeta\zeta}h
with the initial condition given by the Scorer’s function

h(\zeta,0)=\mathrm{Bi}(\zeta)\int_{-\infty}^{\zeta}\mathrm{Ai}(t)dt-\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta)\int_{-\infty}^{\zeta}\mathrm{Bi}(t)dt.

(53)

Trivially, (
16
) reduces to the above as
b\to 0
. Verification that (
16
) satisfies
\partial_{\eta}h=-\partial_{\zeta\zeta}h
is straightforward and uses Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
\mathrm{Ai}_{b}
and
\mathrm{Bi}_{b}
themselves satisfy diffusion equation, see Remark
2.5
.

∎

Proof of Corollary
2.2
.

To integrate out the
t
variable in (
10
), we first change the integration variable to
u=\frac{b^{2}}{t}
and rewrite the resulting integral as

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\cal P}^{w.e.}(\zeta,t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u^{3}}{3}-\frac{b^{2}u^{2}}{2}+\zeta u}\left[(u^{3}+b^{2}u^{2}-\zeta u-1)T_{3}(\zeta)+(u^{2}+b^{2}u-\zeta)\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}^{2}_{b}(p)dp\right.\\
\left.+\frac{1}{2}u\mathrm{Ai}^{2}_{b}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}^{2}(\zeta)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}_{b}(\zeta)\right]du.

(54)

The integrals can be expressed in terms of
\mathrm{Hi}_{b}
and its derivatives. However, we notice that the first parenthesis in the square bracket integrates out to 0, because it corresponds to the third order differential equation satisfied by
\mathrm{Hi}_{b}
, which can be obtained by differentiating both sides of (
52
). Analogously, the second parenthesis integrates to 1, by the virtue of (
52
). The fact that

\pi\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)\mathrm{Hi}^{\prime}_{b}(\zeta)+b^{2}\pi\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(\zeta)\mathrm{Hi}_{b}(\zeta)-\pi\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}_{b}(\zeta)\mathrm{Hi}_{b}(\zeta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\zeta}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(t)dt

can be verified by explicit calculations that involve the exact form of the deformed Scorer’s function (see Lemma
2.3
) and simple manipulations using Lemma
3.3
, followed by the use of the Wronskian
\mathrm{Ai}(x)\mathrm{Bi}^{\prime}(x)-\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{Bi}(x)=\pi^{-1}
. The final observation

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{b}(t)dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}(t)dt=1

(55)

completes the proof. The first equality follows from the fact that
\mathrm{Ai}_{b}
is a solution to the diffusion equation (see Remark
2.5
) and diffusion preserves probability, while the second equality is a known result.
∎

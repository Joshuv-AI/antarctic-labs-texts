Computation of the solution for the 2D acoustic pulse propagation
P.A. Bakhvalov

Abstract
We consider the 2D acoustic system with the Gaussian pulse as the initial data. This case was proposed at the first Workshop on benchmark problems in computational aeroacoustics, and it is commonly used for the verification of numerical methods. We construct an efficient algorithm to evaluate the exact solution for a given time t t and distance r r . For a precision ε \varepsilon , it takes c ​ ln ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) c\ln(1/\varepsilon) operations (the evaluation of a Bessel function counts as one operation) where c c does not depend on t t and r r . This becomes possible by using three different integral representations and an asymptotic series depending on t t and r r .



1
Introduction

The verification of a computational method or its program implementation implies the comparison of the numerical results with some reference data. Verification usually goes along with the progressive increase of the complexity and begins with simple cases
[
1
,
2
]
. At the first stage, it is favourable if the exact solution is available at any point of the computational domain. So cases with closed-form exact solutions are widely used. Integral form solutions are equally convenient provided that they may be computed precisely and efficiently.

The computational costs of the exact solution evaluation become more important for high-order finite-volume methods. In these schemes, one compares the numerical solution at a mesh cell with the cell average of the exact solution. Thus, to evaluate the average at one cell, one needs to evaluate the exact solution at several points, depending on the order of the scheme. A similar difficulty presents in some finite-element schemes where the numerical solutions are compared to the
L_{2}
-projection of the exact solution.

The first Workshop on benchmark problems in computational aeroacoustics
[
3
]
, which held in 1995, considered several verification cases. Cases 3.1 and 3.2 in
[
3
]
were the Cauchy problems for the linearized Euler equations with a uniform background flow. A general solution of these equations is a combination of acoustic, vortex, and entropy components, see
[
4
]
. In the coordinate system of the background flow, the vortex and entropy components are steady, and the acoustic one satisfies the acoustic system. In particular, for the tests in
[
3
]
, we come to the Cauchy problem

\frac{\partial p^{\prime}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{\prime}=0,\quad\frac{\partial\bm{u}^{\prime}}{\partial t}+\nabla p^{\prime}=0,

(1)

in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the initial data

p^{\prime}(0,\bm{r})=\exp(-\bm{r}^{2}/2),\quad\bm{u}^{\prime}(0,\bm{r})=0.

(2)

Here
p^{\prime}
and
\bm{u}^{\prime}
are the pressure and velocity pulsations, correspondingly. This benchmark problem is widely used
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

The solution of (
1
)–(
2
) is given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}p^{\prime}(t,r)\\
u_{r}^{\prime}(t,r)\end{array}\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega e^{-\omega^{2}/2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}J_{0}(r\omega)\cos(t\omega)\\
J_{1}(r\omega)\sin(t\omega)\end{array}\right)d\omega,

(3)

where
J_{j}(x)
are the Bessel functions of the first kind and index
j
.
In
[
3
,
4
]
it was suggested to apply a quadrature to (
3
). For
t,r\leqslant 20
, Gauss quadratures are fast and accurate. However, for bigger
t
or
r
they become inefficient.

Quadratures for integrals involving
J_{\nu}(r\omega)
,
r\gg 1
, were constructed in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, see also references therein. These methods are connected with the ones for integrals of the form
\int f(x)e^{i\omega g(x)}dx
,
\omega\gg 1
, which may be found in
[
12
,
13
]
. The expression (
3
) contains products of two different oscillatory kernels; the evaluation of such integrals is studied in
[
14
,
15
]
. All these methods are based on the continuation of the integrand to the complex domain and the proper choice of the integration path. They are well capable to evaluate the integral (
3
), but their computational costs is high. For instance, the evaluation of one oscillatory integral similar to (
3
) in
[
14
]
takes
0.016
seconds. The exact form of (
3
) does not help to simplify these methods, so for our purpose they take too much CPU time.

In this paper we are concerned with the algorithm to evaluate the solution of (
1
)–(
2
) for given
t,r\geqslant 0
with a requested absolute precision
\varepsilon
. The algorithm is designed for this particular problem and cannot be easily generalized to evaluate any other integral. In other words, we consider
p^{\prime}(t,r)
and
u_{r}^{\prime}(t,r)
defined by (
3
) as special functions and want to approximate it. Our method takes approximately
10^{-6}
seconds in the double precision and
10^{-4}
seconds in the double-double precision.

The idea of the algorithm is to use integral transforms and thus avoid the approximation of oscillatory integrals. The algorithm takes at most
c\ln(1/\varepsilon)
operations with
c
independent of
t
and
r
, provided that the evaluation of a Bessel function counts as one operation. We do not intend to get the relative precision
\varepsilon
, but this may be achieved by a small modification of our method. We also take precautions to avoid accumulating the arithmetic errors. The algorithm is implemented in the open-source ColESo library (Collection of Exact Solutions for verification of numerical algorithms for simulation of compressible flows)
[
16
]
. It is verified for the double precision and the double-double precision arithmetics.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section
2
gives an overview of the algorithm. In Section
3
we present the asymptotic series as
t\rightarrow\infty
for small

4
we prove the integral expressions we use. In Section
5
we study the accuracy of quadrature rules. Section
6
contains the results of the actual accuracy (i. e. in a finite precision arithmetics) and the computational time.

Let

H\!e_{n}(\omega)=(-1)^{n}e^{\omega^{2}/2}\frac{d^{n}}{d\omega^{n}}e^{-\omega^{2}/2}.

Since

J_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\cos(n\phi-x\sin\phi)d\phi,

Lemma 1
.

There holds
|\mathcal{I}_{n}(t)|\leqslant\sqrt{\pi/2}\ 2^{n/2}\ \Gamma(1+n/2)
.

Proof.

Using the representation

H\!e_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x+iy)^{n}\exp(-y^{2}/2)dy

we get

|\mathcal{I}_{n}(t)|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|H\!e_{n}(x)|\exp(-x^{2}/2)dx\leqslant

\leqslant\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x^{2}+y^{2}|^{n/2}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2}\right)dydx=

=\frac{2\pi}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}r^{n+1}\exp(-r^{2}/2)dr=\frac{2\pi}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}(\sqrt{2x})^{n}\exp(-x)dx.

Now the inequality to prove is by the definition of the Euler gamma function.
∎

For

\mathcal{I}_{n}(t)\approx-i^{n-1}\sum\limits_{l=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor}\frac{(2l-1)!!}{t^{2l-n+1}}.

Lemma 2

Let

R_{n,M}=\mathcal{I}_{n}(t)+i^{n-1}\sum\limits_{l=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor}\frac{(2l-1)!!}{t^{2l-n+1}}.

(-1)!!=1

|R_{n,M}(t)|\leqslant\sqrt{\pi/2}\frac{1}{t^{M-n}}2^{M/2}\Gamma(1+M/2).

(11)

Proof.

Integration by parts in (
9

\mathcal{I}_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{it}\left.H\!e_{n}(\omega)e^{-\omega^{2}/2}\exp(it\omega)\right|_{0}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{it}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(H\!e_{n}(\omega)e^{-\omega^{2}/2}\right)\exp(it\omega)d\omega=

=\frac{-1}{it}H\!e_{n}(0)-\frac{-1}{it}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}H\!e_{n+1}(\omega)e^{-\omega^{2}/2}\exp(it\omega)d\omega=\frac{1}{it}(\mathcal{I}_{n+1}(t)-H\!e_{n}(0)).

There holds

It remains to use the estimate given by Lemma

∎

Introducing the wave potential

\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial W}{\partial r}\right)=0,\quad t>0,\quad r>0,

5.1
General estimates

Lemma 7
.

Let
0<h\leqslant\pi

Let

Appendix A. Proof of Lemma
7

Put

g(z)=(f(z)+f(-z))/2.

By symmetry,

Q=h\sum\limits_{k=-n}^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{(kh)^{2}}{2}\right)g(kh).

Also put

J=\int\limits_{-L}^{L}f(x)\exp(-x^{2}/2)dx.

Let
\Gamma
be the counterclockwise oriented rectangular contour with the corners
\pm L\pm 2\pi i/h
. Let
\Gamma_{+}=\Gamma\cap\{\mathrm{Im}z>0\}
and
\Gamma_{-}=\Gamma\cap\{\mathrm{Im}z<0\}
.
Then by the Cauchy residue theorem

Q=\oint\limits_{\Gamma}g(z)\frac{\exp(-z^{2}/2)}{1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)}dz=\left(\int\limits_{\Gamma_{+}}+\int\limits_{\Gamma_{-}}\right)g(z)\frac{\exp(-z^{2}/2)}{1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)}dz.

Note that

\left(\int\limits_{\Gamma_{+}}-\int\limits_{\Gamma_{-}}\right)g(z)\frac{\exp(-z^{2}/2)}{1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)}dz=\int\limits_{\Gamma_{+}}g(z)\exp(-z^{2}/2)dz=-J.

The first identity in this chain is by the substitution
-z
for
z
, with the use of

\frac{1}{1-\exp(z)}+\frac{1}{1-\exp(-z)}=1.

Thus, the integration error is

Q-J=\int\limits_{\Gamma_{+}}G(z)dz,\quad G(z)=2g(z)\frac{\exp(-z^{2}/2)}{1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)}.

Let
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma_{-}
be the vertical segments of
\Gamma_{+}
, and
\gamma_{0}
be its horizontal segment.

First consider the integral over
\gamma_{0}
. Here
z=x+2\pi i/h
and

1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)=1-\exp(-2\pi ix/h)\exp(4\pi^{2}/h^{2}).

Then

\left|\int\limits_{\gamma_{0}}G(z)dz\right|\leqslant 2f_{0}\int\limits_{-L}^{L}\left|\frac{\exp(-x^{2}/2)\exp(2\pi^{2}/h^{2})}{\exp(4\pi^{2}/h^{2})-1}\right|dx\leqslant

\leqslant 2\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\exp(-4\pi^{2}/h^{2})}f_{0}\exp\left(-2\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}\right)\leqslant 5.2f_{0}\exp\left(-2\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}\right).

Here we used
h\leqslant\pi
, which is by assumption.

On vertical segments we have
z=\pm L+iy
,
0\leqslant y\leqslant 2\pi/h
, thus

1-\exp(-2\pi iz/h)=1-\exp(\mp 2\pi iL/h)\exp(2\pi y/h)=

=1-\exp(\mp 2\pi i(n+1/2))\exp(2\pi y/h)=1+\exp(2\pi y/h)

and

\left|\int\limits_{\gamma_{\pm}}G(z)dz\right|\leqslant 2f_{1}\exp(-L^{2}/2)\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi/h}\frac{\exp(y^{2}/2)}{1+\exp(2\pi y/h)}dy.

Using
y^{2}\leqslant(2\pi/h)y
and dropping unit in the denominator we get

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi/h}\frac{\exp(y^{2}/2)}{1+\exp(2\pi y/h)}dy\leqslant\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi y}{h}\right)dy=\frac{h}{\pi}.

Thus

\left|\int\limits_{\gamma_{\pm}}G(z)dz\right|\leqslant\frac{2h}{\pi}\exp(-L^{2}/2)f_{1}.

Combining the estimates and using
|I-Q|\leqslant|I-J|+|J-Q|
we get the statement of the lemma.

Abstract
. This paper is a complement of the modularity result of [ BHK+20 ] for the special case U ⁡ ( 1 , 1 ) U(1,1) not considered there. The main idea to embed a U ⁡ ( 1 , 1 ) U(1,1) Shimura curve to many U ⁡ ( n − 1 , 1 ) U(n-1,1) Shimura varieties for big n n , and prove a precise pullback formula of the generating series of arithmetic divisors. Afterwards, we use the modularity result of [ BHK+20 ] together with existence of non-vanishing of classical theta series at any given point in the upper half plane to prove the modulartiy result on U ⁡ ( 1 , 1 ) U(1,1) Shimura curves.

1.
Introduction

Modularity is a beautiful way to organize a sequence of objects (say numbers) with a lot of symmetry. A classical example is the theta function

(1.1)

\theta_{m}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{m}(n)q^{n},\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau},

where
r_{m}(n)
is the number of ways to express
n
as sum of
m
integers. The modularity of
\theta_{m}
implies in particular that if we know a few
r_{m}(n)
for “small”
n
, then we know all
r_{m}(n)
. Modularity has played an important role in number theory and other fields, see for example
[
HZ76
]
,
[
KM90
]
,
[
Kud97b
]
,
[
Zha21
]
,
[
BWR15
]
, and
[
BHK+20
]
.

In
[
BHK+20
]
, the authors proved the modularity of a generating series
of special divisors on a compactified integral model of a Shimura variety
associated to a unitary group (Krämer model for a unimodular lattice) of signature
(n-1,1)
for
n>2
, and left the special case
n=2
unresolved for technical reasons. This paper finishes the job for
n=2
by the so-called embedding trick. We now describe the main results in a little more detail. For undefined terms, see Section
2
for definition.

Let
\boldsymbol{k}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
be an imaginary quadratic field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and odd discriminant
-D
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a}
be two unimodular Hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
-lattices of signature
(1,0)
and
(n-1,1)
respectively. Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
be the associated integral model of the Shimura variety with toroidal compactification (see Section
2
). For each
m>0
, Kudla and Rapoport (
[
KR14
]
) constructed divisors
\mathcal{Z}(m)
in the open Shimura variety
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}
over
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. There are two Green functions for
Z(m)=\mathcal{Z}(m)(\mathbb{C})
,
\mathrm{Gr}_{B}(m)
constructed by J. Bruinier in his thesis (
[
Bru02
]
,
[
BF04
]
), and
\mathrm{Gr}_{K}(m,v)
constructed by S. Kudla with an extra parameter
v>0
(
[
Kud97a
]
). By studying their behavior at the boundary, one obtains two arithmetic divisors (
[
BHY15
]
,
[
How15
]
,
[
ES18
]
, see Sections
5
and
6
)

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{B}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m)=(\mathcal{Z}_{B}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m),\mathrm{Gr}_{B}(m)),\hbox{ and }\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m,v)=(\mathcal{Z}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m,v),\mathrm{Gr}_{K}(m,v))\in\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*}),

with
\mathcal{Z}_{i}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m)=\mathcal{Z}^{*}(m)+\hbox{ boundary components}
for

. Here
\mathcal{Z}^{*}(m)
is the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{Z}(m)
in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, and
\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
is the arithmetic Chow group with
\mathbb{C}
-coefficients and log-log singularities near the boundary (see
[
BGKK07
]
and
[
How15
]
). Actually,
\mathrm{Gr}_{K}(m,v)
is defined for all integers

and induces an arithmetic divisor (for all integers

)

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m,v)=(\mathcal{Z}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m),\mathrm{Gr}_{K}(m,v))\in\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*}).

One also defines arithmetic divisors
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{B}^{\mathrm{tot}}(0)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{tot}}_{K}(0,v)
in
\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
. Now we have two arithmetic theta functions with values in
\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{B}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m)q^{m}\in\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})[[q]],

(1.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(m,v)q^{m}\in\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})[[q,q^{-1}]].

Ehlen and Sankran proved in
[
ES18
, Theorem 1.4]
that
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)-\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
is a modular form for
\Gamma_{0}(D)
of weight
n
, character
\chi_{-D}^{n}
(where
\chi_{-D}(\cdot)
is the Kronecker symbol
\left(\frac{-D}{\cdot}\right)
) and with values in
\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
(see Section

for detail and some subtlety). In
[
BHK+20
]
, the authors proved the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)
for
n\geq 3
, and thus the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
for
n\geq 3
. In this paper, we prove the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
for
n=2
, which in turn implies the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)
. We record it as

Theorem 1.1
.

When
n=2
,
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)
and
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
are modular forms for
\Gamma_{0}(D)
of weight
2
, trivial character, and with values in

\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
.

We remark that the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}
could be used to prove a complement of the original Gross-Zagier formula (the case when every
p|N
is ramified in
\boldsymbol{k}
, in comparison to that every
p|N
is split in
\boldsymbol{k}
). Since
\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
is infinite dimensional, we refer to
[
ES18
, Definition 4.11]
for the precise meaning of the modularity. To prove the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
for
n=2
, we use the following embedding trick (see, for example,
[
Li23
]
,
[
HMP20
]
). Let
\Lambda
be a positive definite unimodular
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
-lattice of rank
m
. Replacing
\mathfrak{a}
by
\mathfrak{a}\obot\Lambda
, we obtain a Shimura variety

\mathcal{S}^{\diamond,*}
together with a canonical embedding:

(1.4)

\varphi_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{S}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\diamond,*},(A_{0},A,...)\mapsto(A_{0},A\times(A_{0}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Lambda),...).

It induces a homomorphism
\varphi_{\Lambda}^{*}:\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{\diamond,*})\rightarrow\widehat{\operatorname{CH}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{*})
. The following is the main technical theorem of this paper, which should be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let notations be as above. Then

\varphi_{\Lambda}^{*}(\widehat{\Theta}_{K}^{\diamond}(\tau))=\theta_{\Lambda}(\tau)\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau).

Here
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}^{\diamond}(\tau)
is the arithmetic theta function (with Kudla Green functions) associated to
\mathcal{S}^{\diamond,*}
, and

(1.5)

\theta_{\Lambda}(\tau)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}q^{(x,x)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}r_{\Lambda}(n)q^{n}

is a classical holomorphic modular form for
\Gamma_{0}(D)
of weight
m
and character
\chi_{-D}^{m}
.

By an analogue of a result of Yingkun Li
[
Li23
, Lemma 3.2]
, for every
\tau
, there is some
\Lambda
with
\theta_{\Lambda}(\tau)\neq 0
. So
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
is everywhere defined and the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
follows from the modularity result of
[
BHK+20
]
. Now Theorem
1.1
for
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)
follows from the modularity for
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
and the main result of
[
ES18
]
. Note that the analogue of Theorem
1.2
does not hold for
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}
.

The main work to prove Theorem
1.2
is to understand the pullback of special divisors on
\mathcal{S}^{\diamond,*}
that intersect with
\mathcal{S}^{*}
improperly and various subtleties about the boundary components. On the generic fiber, this pullback is controlled by the line bundle of modular form of weight
1
(descended from the tautological line bundle), which can be regarded as an analogue of the adjunction formula. The line bundle of modular form of weight
1
has two well-defined integral models:
\omega
considered in
[
BHK+20
]
and
\Omega
considered in
[
How19
]
. Although
\omega
is used in the definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{K}^{\mathrm{tot}}(0,v)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{B}^{\mathrm{tot}}(0)
in
[
BHK+20
]
, it turns out that
\Omega
is the one that controls the pullback of special divisors that intersect with
\mathcal{S}^{*}
improperly. So it is important to figure out their precise relation. We show in Section
3
(Theorem
3.2
):

(1.6)

\Omega=\omega\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Exc}})^{-1}.

We believe that this relation is of independent interest. It is discovered by
[
How19
]
that the line bundle
\Omega
controls the deformation theory of special divisors in the open Shimura variety
\mathcal{S}
. Another main technical point of this paper is to extend this observation to the toroidal compactification
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, see Theorem
4.4
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review basics on integral model, toroidal compactification and special divisors which are needed in this paper. In Section
3
, we prove the precise relation (
1.6
) between the two well-defined integral models of the line bundle of modular forms of weight
1
(Theorem
3.2
). In Section
4
, we extend the controlling deformation property of
\Omega
of Ben Howard to boundaries (Theorem
4.4
). Section
5
is dedicated to proving Theorem
5.5

1.2
without Green functions. In Section

, we deal with pullbacks of Green functions and metrics on line bundles, and finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In the first part of Section

[
Li23
]
) to prove the modularity of
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)
. In the second part of Section
7
, we describe two slightly different Green functions for special divisors, one defined by Bruinier in
[
BHK+20
]
and the other one defined by Ehlen and Sankaran in
[
ES18
]
. We show that
\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)
and
\widehat{\Theta}_{ES}(\tau)
are essentially the same (
7.21
), which will be used in the modularity of difference
\widehat{\Theta}_{K}(\tau)-\widehat{\Theta}_{B}(\tau)

2.1.
Unitary Shimura varieties

In this section, we review the theory of unitary Shimura variety following
[

]
. Let
W_{0}

\boldsymbol{k}
-vector spaces endowed with hermitian forms

and

of signatures
(1,0)

(n-1,1)

We assume the existence of

\quad\psi_{0}\left(w,w^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\boldsymbol{k}/\mathbb{Q}}H_{0}\left(\delta^{-1}w,w^{\prime}\right),\quad\psi\left(w,w^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\boldsymbol{k}/\mathbb{Q}}H\left(\delta^{-1}w,w^{\prime}\right).

be the subgroup of pairs for which the similitude factors are equal. We denote by
\nu:G\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
the common similitude character, and note that

Let

\mathcal{D}(W)=\{\text{negative definite }\mathbb{C}\text{-lines }y\subset W(\mathbb{R})\},

The symplectic forms (

\boldsymbol{k}
-conjugate-linear isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{k}\left(W_{0},W\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}\left(W,W_{0}\right),\ x\mapsto x^{\vee},

characterized by
\psi\left(xw_{0},w\right)=\psi_{0}\left(w_{0},x^{\vee}w\right)

\boldsymbol{k}
-vector space

(2.4)

(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}^{\vee}\circ x_{1}\in\operatorname{End}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(W_{0}\right)\cong\boldsymbol{k}.

Let

L\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0},\mathfrak{a}),

\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
-lattice of signature
(n-1,1)

G

\mathfrak{a}_{0}

\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle K=\left\{g\in G\left(\mathbb{A}_{f}\right):g\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}=\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}\text{ and }g\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}=\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}\right\}\subset G\left(\mathbb{A}_{f}\right),

In this subsection, we briefly review the theory of toroidal compatification of

A proper cusp label is an isomorphism class of pairs
\Phi=(\mathfrak{n},L_{\Phi})
in which
\mathfrak{n}
is a projective
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
-module of rank one, and
L_{\Phi}

\mathfrak{m}\subset L

(n-1,1)
and
\mathfrak{m}
is an isotropic direct summand of rank one. A normal decomposition of

is an
\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
-module direct sum decomposition

(2.10)

\displaystyle L=(\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{n})\obot L_{\Phi}

where
L_{\Phi}=(\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{n})^{\perp}
and
\mathfrak{n}
is an isotropic direct summand of rank one and can be identified as
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{O}_{\boldsymbol{k}})
. The hermitian form on
L
makes

\mathfrak{m}^{\perp}/\mathfrak{m}
into a unimodular hermitian lattice of signature

Proof.

from the exact sequence (

Now by Lemma

As a consequence,

Corollary 3.8

(s)=\sum_{p}\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{p},

∎

Doğa Gürgünoğlu 1 , Emil Björnson 1 , Gábor Fodor 1 2

Abstract
In this paper, we study the impact of pilot contamination in a system where two operators serve their respective users with the assistance of two wide-band reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), each belonging to a single operator. We consider one active user per operator and they use disjoint narrow frequency bands. Although each RIS is dedicated to a single operator, both users’ transmissions are reflected by both RISs. We show that this creates a new kind of pilot contamination effect when pilots are transmitted simultaneously. Since combating inter-operator pilot contamination in RIS-assisted networks would require long pilot signal sequences to maintain orthogonality among the users of different operators, we propose the orthogonal configurations of the RISs. Numerical results show that this approach completely eliminates pilot contamination, and significantly improves the performance in terms of channel estimation and equalization by removing the channel estimation bias.

I
Introduction

Pilot contamination is a key problem that frequently arises in wireless communication systems
[
1
]
. When multiple users use the same pilot sequences simultaneously in the same band, due to the limited channel coherence time, the
BS
(
BS
) cannot distinguish their channels, which typically results in poor channel estimates and extra beamformed interference towards pilot-sharing
UE
. Therefore, pilot contamination adversely affects the coherent reception of data, and methods to mitigate pilot contamination have been widely studied in the communication literature
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In recent years,
RIS
have arisen as a new technology component for 6G
[
5
]
. An
RIS
is a surface consisting of multiple reflecting elements that have sub-wavelength spacing and controllable reflection properties
[
6
]
.
This feature provides partial control of
the propagation environment that can lead to better services for users, especially when their serving
BS
is not in their
LOS
(
LOS
). By adjusting the impedances of the individual elements via a
RIS
controller, the elements are capable of adding desired phase shifts to the reflected signals, thereby forming reflected beams in desired directions that can significantly boost the
SINR
(
SINR
) at the receiver
[
5
]
.

On the other hand, the addition of
RIS
to existing systems introduces new design and operational challenges. For example, the length of the pilot signal required by a single
UE
is proportional to the number of
RIS
elements (e.g., tens or hundreds), because the
RIS
must change its configuration to explore all channel dimensions
[
7
,
8
]
.
In addition, passive
RIS
causes multiplicative path losses, which increases the large-scale fading loss between the transmitter and the receiver
[
9
]
. Active
RIS

[
10
]
, on the other hand, are less energy efficient, and due to the presence of amplifiers, it introduces additional noise. While the aforementioned problems caused by the
RIS
have been recognized
[
11
]
, pilot contamination caused by the presence of multiple
RIS
has not been studied in the literature. In this paper, however, we identify the creation of pilot contamination as another practical challenge: a
UE
that transmits pilots to its serving
BS
via multiple
RIS
, which change their configurations simultaneously, may cause a new kind of pilot contamination that has not been studied.

Wireless communication systems are governed by standards, and physical-layer specifications usually contain pre-defined sequences for pilot signals and codebooks for directional beamforming
[
12
]
. While the use
RIS
is not standardized yet, it is likely that the configuration sequences that facilitate the deployment of
RIS
while maintaining interoperability will be standardized. Consequently, when multiple cellular networks are deployed by different network operators in overlapping geographical areas, the
RIS
may adopt identical or overlapping pilot sequences and cause pilot contamination.

Due to the ability to change the environment’s propagation characteristics, deploying multiple
RIS
in a geographical area also implies that pilot contamination can occur due to a
UE
’s own pilot signal.
Since this phenomenon may exacerbate the pilot contamination problem, it is clear that pilot contamination due to the presence of
multiple
RIS
must be dealt with.

In this paper, we study the pilot contamination caused by the presence of multiple
RIS
by considering the uplink of a system consisting of two wide-band
RIS
, two single-antenna
UE
, and two co-located single-antenna
BS
, where the two
UE
are subscribed to different operators with non-overlapping narrow-band channels at different frequencies. Each
RIS
is dedicated to a single operator, but both
UE
signals are reflected from both
RIS
. In this scenario, although there is no interference between the two
UE
, both
RIS
affect both frequency bands. We propose the use of orthogonal
RIS
configuration sequences during pilot transmission to avoid pilot contamination. First, we describe the channel estimation procedure with a lack of information on channel statistics, i.e., when the channels are characterized by deterministic parameters. Assuming identical and orthogonal
RIS
configurations, we derive the statistics of the
ML
(
ML
) estimates and the effect of pilot contamination on channel estimation.
Then we analyze how it affects the data estimation process, and discuss how this effect can be mitigated.

III
Maximum Likelihood Channel Estimation

To estimate
\mathbf{g}_{k}
, which is
N

k

N

. Hence, both
\mathbf{B}_{1},\mathbf{B}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times N}
must have full column rank. Furthermore, we require that the

\mathbf{B}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{k}=L\mathbf{I}_{N}

ML

BS

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\mathbf{g}_{k}}f(\mathbf{y}_{pk};\mathbf{g}_{k})

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\mathbf{g}_{k}}\frac{1}{(\pi\sigma_{w}^{2})^{L}}\exp{\left(-\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{pk}-\sqrt{P_{p}}\mathbf{B}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}\mathbf{g}_{k}\|^{2}}{\sigma_{w}^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\mathbf{g}_{k}}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{pk}-\sqrt{P_{p}}\mathbf{B}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}\mathbf{g}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{p}}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{k})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{y}_{pk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L\sqrt{P_{p}}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{y}_{pk}.

1

Interestingly, the

and
\mathbf{r}_{k}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{q}_{k}}\mathbf{p_{k}}
. The resulting system model can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{p1}=\sqrt{P}_{p}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}&\mathbf{B}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{g}_{1}\\
\mathbf{r}_{1}\end{bmatrix}+\mathbf{w}_{p1},

(8a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{p2}=\sqrt{P}_{p}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{2}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{2}}&\mathbf{B}_{1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\mathbf{r}_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\mathbf{w}_{p2}.

(8b)

In (
8
), a known linear transformation is applied to the parameter vector of interest in the presence of additive noise. Consequently, the
ML
estimates for
UE
1’s unknown channels become

\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}\\
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{p}}}\begin{bmatrix}L\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}&\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}\\
\mathbf{B}_{2}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}&L\mathbf{I}_{N}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\\
\mathbf{B}_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{y}_{p1}.

(9)

Note that in this case, the total dimension of the unknown parameter vector is
2N
, hence, at least
2N
independent observations are required for the matrix inverse to exist. The structure in (
9
) applies to
UE
2 with alternated indices, and it gives the
ML
estimator, which is both unbiased and efficient, since (
8
) is a linear observation model with additive Gaussian noise
[
14
, Theorem 7.3]
. Hence, (
9
) is unbiased irrespective of other parameters such as
\sigma_{w}^{2}
,
L
,
P_{p}
, and it achieves the
CRLB
(
CRLB
), which provides a lower bound on the
MSE
(
MSE
) of any unbiased estimator
[
13
]
. It has to be noted that when
\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}
, (
9
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}\\
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L\sqrt{P_{p}}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0}&\mathbf{I}_{N}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\\
\mathbf{B}_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{y}_{p1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L\sqrt{P_{p}}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\\
\mathbf{B}_{2}^{H}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{y}_{p1}.

(10)

Note that the expression for
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
in (
III-B
) is the same as in (
5
). This shows that when
\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}
, the
MML
in (
5
) coincides with the
ML
estimator; that is, the misspecified model is sufficient when the configuration sequences are designed to alleviate pilot interference. The probability distribution of
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}
in this case can be expressed as

\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}\left(\mathbf{g}_{k},\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}}{LP_{p}}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}})^{-1}\right),

(11)

which shows that choosing the
RIS
configuration sequences such that
\mathbf{B}_{1}
and
\mathbf{B}_{2}
removes the bias from the
MML
estimator. However, the major setback of this approach is that the minimum number of observations required for this channel estimation procedure is
2N
instead of
N
, due to the fact that the
2N
-many
L
-dimensional columns must all be orthogonal to each other, for which
L\geq 2N
is required. Considering that the estimator bias in (
7
) does not vanish with increasing
L
, this is a necessary sacrifice. Hence, it has to be noted that the number of pilot transmissions increases linearly with the number of
RIS
deployed in proximity. In the next section, data signal transmission and its estimation will be analyzed.

III-C

MSE
During Channel Estimation

We consider the
MSE
as the channel estimation performance metric, which is the trace of the error covariance matrix that is derived in this section. In this derivation, we do not assume a particular choice of
\mathbf{B}_{1},\mathbf{B}_{2}
, but we utilize the basic assumption
\mathbf{B}_{k}\mathbf{B}_{k}^{H}=L\mathbf{I}_{N}
. Consequently, we use
\mathbf{b}_{k}
to denote the potential estimator bias. We can then compute the error covariance matrix as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{e,k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}-\mathbf{g}_{k})(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}-\mathbf{g}_{k})^{H}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k}^{H}+\frac{1}{LP_{p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-1}\mathbf{w}_{pk}\mathbf{w}_{pk}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-H}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k}^{H}+\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}}{LP_{p}}\left(\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{H}\right)^{-1}.

(12)

Consequently, the trace of the error covariance matrix becomes

\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{e,k})=\|\mathbf{b}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}}{LP_{p}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|h_{kn}|^{2}}

(13)

Note that for high
P_{p}
,
L
, and low
\sigma_{w}^{2}
, the second term in (
13
) vanishes, and the trace of the error covariance converges to
\|\mathbf{b}_{k}\|^{2}
which depend on the configuration of
\mathbf{B}_{1},\mathbf{B}_{2}
:

\|\mathbf{b}_{k}\|^{2}=\begin{cases}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{|r_{kn}|^{2}}{|h_{kn}|^{2}}&\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2},\\
0&\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}.\end{cases}

(14)

This result shows that configuring the
RIS
s such that
\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}
removes the asymptotic floor on the average
MSE
, which comes from the energy of the estimator bias. On the other hand, when the intended
RIS
-
BS
links
\mathbf{h}_{1},\mathbf{h}_{2}
are strong relative to the unintended and unknown overall link
\mathbf{r}_{k}
, the estimator bias will be weaker and the cost of choosing
\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2}
will be lower. Nevertheless, pilot contamination results in a fundamental error floor, even if the
RIS
are utilized in different bands. In the next section, we consider the estimation of data based on the channel estimation performed in this section and analyze the consequence of pilot contamination in this phase.

IV
Data Transmission

The channel estimation is followed by data transmission. We consider a data packet with a duration shorter than the channel coherence time, therefore, the channels acting on the transmitted data signals are the same as in the channel estimation part. Defining the data signal transmitted by the
k
th
UE
as
x_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
, we can express the received data as

\displaystyle y_{1}=\sqrt{P_{d}}(\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{1}\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{q}_{1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{2}\mathbf{p}_{1})x_{1}+w_{1},

(15a)

\displaystyle y_{2}=\sqrt{P_{d}}(\mathbf{h}_{2}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{2}\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{q}_{2}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{1}\mathbf{p}_{2})x_{2}+w_{2},

(15b)

where
w_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2})
denotes the receiver noise,
P_{d}
denotes the data transmission power, and the RIS configuration matrices
\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}
are selected based on the estimated channels to maximize the average channel gain as shown in
[
8
, Sec. II]
:

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{kn}=\arg(h_{kn})+\arg(\hat{g}_{kn}),

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}=\mathrm{diag}\left(e^{-j\hat{\phi}_{k1}},\dots,e^{-j\hat{\phi}_{kN}}\right).

(16)

However, since the
BS
are unaware of the unintended reflections and base their data estimation on channel estimates, they assume the following misspecified received data signal models:

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{1}=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{1}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}x_{1}+w_{1},

(17a)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{2}=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{2}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}x_{2}+w_{2}.

(17b)

Introducing the notation
m_{k}\triangleq\sqrt{P_{d}}(\mathbf{h}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}+\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k})
for
j,k\in\{1,2\},j\neq k
, and
\hat{m}_{k}\triangleq\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}
, (
15
) and (
17
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle y_{k}=m_{k}x_{k}+w_{k},\quad k=1,2,

(18a)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{k}=\hat{m}_{k}x_{k}+w_{k},\quad k=1,2.

(18b)

Based on the misspecified observation model in (
18b
), the
BS
estimate
x_{k}
by using the misspecified
MMSE
(
MMSE
) estimator

\hat{x}_{k}=\frac{\hat{m}_{k}^{*}}{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}}y_{k},\quad k=1,2.

(19)

In this section, we consider the
MSE
between
x_{k}
and
\hat{x}_{k}
as the performance metric for the data transmission. We derive the data estimation
MSE
for
UE

k
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|x_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}|^{2}\right]=1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\hat{x}_{k}|^{2}\right]-2\mathrm{Re}(\mathbb{E}[x_{k}\hat{x}_{k}^{*}])

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle-2\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\hat{m}_{k}m_{k}^{*}}{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})-2\mathrm{Re}(\hat{m}_{1}m_{1}^{*})(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})-2\mathrm{Re}(\hat{m}_{1}m_{1}^{*})(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})-\sigma_{w}^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}(\hat{m}_{k}m_{k}^{*}))}{(\hat{m}_{k}^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(\hat{m}_{k}^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|m_{k}-\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+2\sigma_{w}^{2}}{|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\hat{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right].

(20)

Defining
\epsilon_{k}\triangleq m_{k}-\hat{m}_{k}
, (
20
) can be rewritten as

\mathbb{E}[|x_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|\epsilon_{k}|^{2}+2\sigma_{w}^{2}}{|m_{k}-\epsilon_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}(|m_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|m_{k}-\epsilon_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}\right]

(21)

To examine the impact of pilot contamination on the data estimation performance more clearly, we now consider channel estimation at high
SNR
, so that the estimation error only comes from the estimator bias, i.e., pilot contamination. This happens when
L
or
P_{p}
is high and/or
\sigma_{w}^{2}
is low, which results in that the estimator covariances in (
7
) and (
11
) become zero. For notational convenience, we consider the case where
P_{p}
is arbitrarily large so that
\lim_{P_{p}\to\infty}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}=\mathbf{g}_{k}+\mathbf{b}_{k}
, where

\mathbf{b}_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{q}_{k}}\mathbf{p}_{k}&\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2},\\
\boldsymbol{0}&\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}.\end{cases}

(22)

IV-A

Data
MSE
with Channel Estimation at High
SNR

In (
21
),
\epsilon_{k}
and
m_{k}
are functions of
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
and
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}
, therefore as
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
and
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}
converge to their means,
\epsilon_{k}
and
m_{k}
become

\displaystyle\overline{m}_{k}=\sqrt{P_{d}}(\mathbf{h}_{k}^{T}\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{k}+\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}),

(23a)

\displaystyle\overline{\epsilon}_{k}=\sqrt{P_{d}}(\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}-\mathbf{h}_{k}^{T}\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\mathbf{b}_{k}),

(23b)

for
j,k\in\{1,2\}
and
j\neq k
.
\bar{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}
denotes the
RIS
configuration computed according to (
16
) with
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{k}=\mathbf{g}_{k}+\mathbf{b}_{k}
.

At high
SNR
, the MSE in (
21
) can be rewritten as

\text{MSE}=\frac{|\overline{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}+2\sigma_{w}^{2}}{|\overline{m}_{k}-\overline{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{w}^{2}(|\overline{m}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})}{(|\overline{m}_{k}-\overline{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}+\sigma_{w}^{2})^{2}}.

(24)

This is a practically achievable limit since RIS-aided systems require large pilot sequences over a narrow bandwidth, so the SNR might be larger than in the data transmission phase.

IV-B

Data
MSE
with Transmission at
High
SNR

In the previous subsection, we obtained the expression for data
MSE
when the channels are estimated at a high pilot
SNR
, while the data transmission is done at an arbitrary SNR. To study the case when also the data transmission a step further, we let
\sigma_{w}^{2}\to 0
, which results in the limit

\lim_{\sigma_{w}^{2}\to 0}\text{MSE}=\frac{|\overline{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}}{|\overline{m}_{k}-\overline{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}}.

(25)

Note that the resulting expression denotes the ratio between the estimated overall
SISO
(
SISO
) channel
\hat{m}_{k}
’s power and the mismatch parameter
\epsilon_{k}
’s power. Recall that
\mathbf{b}_{k}
depends on which
RIS
pilot sequence is utilized. For
\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2}
, we can obtain
\epsilon_{k}
as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}-\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{1}}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}\mathbf{p}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}-\sqrt{P_{d}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}\mathbf{p}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}-\sqrt{P_{d}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{k}\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}\mathbf{p}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}-\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k}\mathbf{p}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k})\mathbf{p}_{k}.

(26)

On the other hand,
\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=0
removes
\mathbf{b}_{k}
for
k\in\{1,2\}
. Consequently, we have

\epsilon_{k}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{k})\mathbf{p}_{k}&\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2},\\
\sqrt{P_{d}}\mathbf{q}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{j}\mathbf{p}_{k}&\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}.\end{cases}

(27)

It is possible to observe that the
\epsilon_{k}
corresponds to only the unintended reflection path itself when
\mathbf{B}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}
. On the other hand,
\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2}
yields an expression depending on the difference between the two
RIS
’ configurations during data transmission. Since each
RIS
is configured based on the channels of their respective users, it is highly unlikely that the configurations will be close. Moreover, it has to be noted that the
RIS
configuration of the non-serving
RIS
is different among the two cases since the channel estimates are also different.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the impact of pilot contamination in a system consisting of two wide-band
RIS
, two single-antenna
UE
, and two co-located single-antenna
BS
. We have demonstrated that the presence of multiple
RIS
in the same area causes pilot contamination, although the
UE
are subscribed to different operators and transmit over disjoint narrow frequency bands. To combat this new type of pilot contamination, we proposed the use of orthogonal
RIS
configurations during pilot transmission. In the numerical results, we have clearly shown that the proposed approach eliminates pilot contamination completely, and decreases data estimation
MSE
significantly. While increasing the number of pilots to configure
RIS
orthogonally alleviates pilot contamination, more efficient ways of dealing with this problem are needed in the future.

Cauchy problem for Dirac equations with Chern-Simons-Proca gauge field Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q41, 35Q55, 35Q40. Thanks: Keywords and phrases. Dirac equations, Chern-Simons-Proca field, global existence, scattering theory, space-time resonance.
Hyungjin Huh, Kiyeon Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem of Dirac equations with Chern-Simons-Proca(CSP) gauge field. We investigate global well-posedness and scattering theory for the solutions with small initial data. Main difficulties come from the fact that Strichartz estimate does not work and an absence of spinorial null structure which disturbs to show the global existence. To overcome these obstacles, we employ the space-time resonance argument introduced by Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah [ 16 , 17 , 18 ] , as well as various resonance functions derived from Dirac operators and the normal form approach. Our argument enables us to establish the global in time existence of solutions to Dirac equations with CSP gauge field. As a byproduct of our argument, we obtain the scattering results.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Dirac equations with Chern-Simons-Proca(CSP) field:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\gamma^{\mu}\textbf{D}_{\mu}\psi=m\psi,\\
\frac{1}{2}&\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}F_{\mu\nu}+{\lambda}A_{\rho}=-J^{\rho},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the spinor field is
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, the gauge fields are
A_{\mu}:{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and the covariant derivative is
\textbf{D}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-iA_{\mu}
. The curvature is defined by
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
.

\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
is the totally skew-symmetric tensor with
\epsilon^{012}=1
and
{\lambda}>0
is a Proca coupling constant. The Dirac gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
are defined by

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad\gamma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\gamma^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix}.

We also define
J^{\mu}=\left\langle\psi,\,{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi\right\rangle
with
{\alpha}^{\mu}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}
for
\mu=0,\,1,\,2
. The
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes a standard complex inner product. Greek indices mean the space-time components
\mu,\nu=0,\,1,\,2
and roman indices mean the spatial components
j=1,\,2
in the sequel. The mass parameter is
m\geq 0
. In this paper we only consider the massive case
m>0
.

Let us consider Lorenz gauge condition
\partial^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0
.
The static form of the second equations in (
1.1
) with above gauge condition can be described as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{1}A_{0}+{\lambda}A_{2}=J^{2},\\
&\partial_{2}A_{0}-{\lambda}A_{1}=-J^{1},\\
&F_{12}+{\lambda}A_{0}=-J^{0},\\
&\partial_{1}A_{1}+\partial_{2}A_{2}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Combining Dirac part in (
1.1
) and stationary gauge fields (
1.2
), we consider the following Cauchy problem for Dirac equations with Chern-Simons-Proca gauge fields:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} i\gamma^{\mu}\textbf{D}_{\mu}\psi&=m\psi,\\
({\lambda}^{2}-\Delta)A_{1}&={\lambda}J^{1}-\partial_{2}J^{0},\\
({\lambda}^{2}-\Delta)A_{2}&={\lambda}J^{2}+\partial_{1}J^{0},\\
({\lambda}^{2}-\Delta)A_{0}&=-{\lambda}J^{0}+\partial_{2}J^{1}-\partial_{1}J^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

with initial data
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)
.

Chern-Simons-Proca model is a gauge theory that combines the Chern-Simons term and the Proca term, which is a model of the electromagnetic interaction. The CSP-Dirac system describes the weak interaction of the Dirac fermions and intermediate vector Boson. The
(1+2)
dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theories give a useful description to fractional quantum Hall effect and anyonic superconductivity. Especially,
the Abelian Chern-Simons theories
[
11
,
20
]
have attracted much attention for both physicists and mathematicians.

The Proca term is a classical way of giving a mass to gauge fields. From the mathematical point of view, the Proca term plays an important role in proving a non-trivial solution to the equations (
1.3
).
In fact, if the Proca term vanishes,
\lambda=0
in (
1.3
), then we have Laplace operator
-\Delta
for gauge fields instead of Klein-Gordon operator
-\Delta+1
. Then the gauge fields are represented by

A=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{2}}J(y)dy,

which gives us difficulties in estimating
A_{\alpha}
.
The Proca term
(\lambda\neq 0)
is important in our analysis.
Several mathematical papers
[
2
,
12
,
19
,
38
]
have dealt with Maxwell-Proca equations. The Chern-Simons-Proca system has been studied in
[
15
,
22
,
34
]
(see also section 10.1 in
[
1
]
).

In this paper, we are concerned with the global well-posedness and linear scattering theory for the system (
1.3
). Since we consider our Cauchy problem based on the small initial data, the signs of nonlinearity are not relevant. Then we normalize
{\lambda}=1
and
m=1
for simplicity. Let us reformulate (
1.3
) by multiplying
\beta:=\gamma^{0}
.
Then we reduce our main equations (
1.3
) into

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D+m\beta)\psi=N_{1}(\psi,\psi,\psi)+N_{2}(\psi,\psi,\psi),

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle N_{1}(\psi,\phi,{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\Delta}\left\langle\phi,{\varphi}\right\rangle\psi-\frac{1}{1-\Delta}\left\langle\phi,{\alpha}^{1}{\varphi}\right\rangle{\alpha}^{1}\psi-\frac{1}{1-\Delta}\left\langle\phi,{\alpha}^{2}{\varphi}\right\rangle{\alpha}^{2}\psi,

\displaystyle N_{2}(\psi,\phi,{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{1}\left\langle\phi,{\alpha}^{2}{\varphi}\right\rangle-\partial_{2}\left\langle\phi,{\alpha}^{1}{\varphi}\right\rangle}{1-\Delta}\psi+\frac{\partial_{2}}{1-\Delta}\left\langle\phi,{\varphi}\right\rangle{\alpha}^{1}\psi-\frac{\partial_{1}}{1-\Delta}\left\langle\phi,{\varphi}\right\rangle{\alpha}^{2}\psi.

Here,
{\alpha}=({\alpha}^{1},{\alpha}^{2})
with
{\alpha}^{j}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{j}
and
D=-i\nabla
.

Remark 1.1
.

The equation (
1.4
) obeys mass conservation law. If a solution
\psi
is sufficiently smooth, then the mass
\|\psi(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}
is conserved, that is,
\|\psi(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}
for all
t
within an existence time interval.

There is a large amount of literature dealing with the problems of well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of small solutions of the Dirac equations with Hartree type nonlinearity. To better put (
1.4
) into context in relation with the global well-posedness and scattering theory, let us consider the following generalized Hartree type Dirac equations:

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D+m\beta)\psi=\left(V*\left\langle\psi,\Gamma_{1}\psi\right\rangle\right)\Gamma_{2}\psi\quad\mbox{on }\,{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d},

(1.5)

where
V
is potential and
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
are either of
\gamma^{\mu}
or suitable size identity matrix . We first mention the Coulomb potential
V(x)=|x|^{-1}
, corresponding to
{\lambda}=0
case in (
1.3
). In
d=3
,
[
7
,
14
]
has proved the (modified) scattering results for the case
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=I_{4}
. They exploited the spinorial null structure (
1.7
) and space-time non-resonance
[
33
]
. For the generalized Coulomb potential
V(x)=|x|^{-a}(1<a<\infty)
with
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=\beta
and
d=2
, Cho, Ozawa and the second author have shown the linear scattering theory for a small solution with low regularity in
[
9
]
. The Yukawa type potential
V=\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-2}
is related with our main equations. In
[
8
,
36
]
, the authors have studied the low regularity scattering results for Hartree-type Dirac equations in 2 dimension with small initial data in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and for
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=\beta
by utilizing the bilinear estimates based on Strichartz estimates for Klein-Gordon equations. We also refer to
[
37
,
39
]
for the same results for
H^{\varepsilon}
in
d=3
.

The nonlinear term of Dirac equations with Yukawa potential is considerably similar to our nonlinearity
N_{1}(\psi,\psi,\psi)
in (
1.4
). The significant difference is that the nonlinearity
\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-2}\left\langle\psi,\gamma^{0}\psi\right\rangle\gamma^{0}\psi
in
[
8
,
36
]
has the spinorial null structure, which enables to perform the bilinear estimates, which are first introduced by Klainerman and Machedon
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
for wave equations and developed by many mathematicians in
[
3
,
4
,
30
,
36
,
37
]
for Klein-Gordon type equations. In contrast to this fact, our nonlinearity in (
1.8
) has various interactions between the matrices
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
included in
\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-2}(|\psi|^{2})\psi
. These interactions disturb to prove global well-posedness by making the bilinear estimates as well as Strichartz estimates not working well. This obstacle comes from the fact that we cannot eliminate the resonance case between two spinors without Dirac matrix
\beta
even though we make use of the decay effect, arising in potential
\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-2}
.

In order to show the scattering phenomenon, we have to overcome these difficulties by employing other argumentation, at the expense of regularities or spatial weight. We describe the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\geq 50
. Suppose that the initial data
\psi_{0}
satisfy the condition

\displaystyle\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|x\psi_{0}\|_{H^{5}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

(1.6)

for sufficiently small
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
. Then there exists a unique global solution
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{s})
to (
1.4
), which scatters in
H^{5}(\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2}dx)
.

Our argument has not been confined to the structure of nonlinearity. We can apply our argument for (
1.5
) with arbitrary matrices
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}\in M_{2\times 2}({\mathbb{C}})
. For these reasons, it suffices to treat

\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-j}\left\langle\psi,{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi\right\rangle{\alpha}^{\nu}\psi,

for all
\mu
,
\nu=0,1,2
and
j=1,2
. Especially, we fix
j=1
.

Remark 1.3
.

The system (
1.1
) without Proca field
({\lambda}=0)
was considered as Chern-Simons-Dirac(CSD) equations. Since the singularity arising from potential disrupts to verify the global well-posedness and scattering theory even if we impose the static gauge fields condition, there is only local in time solution. For the local well-posedness results for the CSD system, we refer to
[
5
,
21
,
23
,
31
,
32
]
. However, when
V=|x|^{-1}
and
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=I_{2}
in (
1.5
), we expect that the argument in this paper can be applied to show the global well-posedness and modified scattering phenomenon with suitable phase correction
(
see
[
28
]
)
.

1.1.
Reformulation for Dirac equations

We use the representation of solution based on the massive Klein-Gordon equation. For the purpose of diagonalizing of (
1.4
), let us define the projection operators
\Pi_{\theta}(D)
by

\Pi_{\theta}(D):=\frac{1}{2}\left(I+\theta\frac{1}{\langle D\rangle}[\alpha\cdot D+\beta]\right),

where
\left<D\right>:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\langle\xi\rangle\mathcal{F}
and
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then we get

\displaystyle{\alpha}\cdot D+\beta=\langle D\rangle(\Pi_{+}(D)-\Pi_{-}(D)),

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\theta}(D)\Pi_{\theta}(D)=\Pi_{\theta}(D),\quad\Pi_{\theta}(D)\Pi_{-\theta}(D)=0.

(1.7)

We denote
\Pi_{\theta}(D)\psi
by
\psi_{\theta}
. Then the equation (
1.4
) becomes the following system of semi-relativistic Hartree equations:

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{t}+\theta\langle D\rangle)\psi_{\theta}=\Pi_{\theta}(D)[\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle\psi,{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi\right\rangle{\alpha}^{\nu}\psi]

(1.8)

with initial data
\psi_{\theta}(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0,\theta}:=\Pi_{\theta}(D)\psi_{0}
. The free solutions of (
1.8
) are
e^{-\theta it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\theta}
, where

e^{-\theta it\langle D\rangle}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{-\theta it\langle\xi\rangle}\mathcal{F}f)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi-\theta t\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Here
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are Fourier transform, its inverse, respectively. Note that
U(t)\psi_{0}
is also the free solution of (
1.4
), where

U(t):=\Pi_{+}(D)e^{-it\left\langle D\right\rangle}+\Pi_{-}(D)e^{it\left\langle D\right\rangle}.

Then, by Duhamel’s principle, the Cauchy problem (
1.8
) is equivalent to solve the integral equations:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi_{\theta}(t)&=e^{-\theta it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\theta}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+i\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\theta_{j}}\in\{\pm\}\\
j=1,2,3\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\theta i(t-t^{\prime})\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{\theta}(D)\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-1}\left[\left\langle\psi_{\theta_{2}}(t^{\prime}),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{\theta_{3}}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle\right]{\alpha}^{\nu}\psi_{\theta_{1}}(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

(1.9)

We call that the solution
\psi
scatters forward (or backward) in Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
if there exists
\psi^{l}\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H})
, linear solutions to
(-i\partial_{t}+\alpha\cdot D+\beta)\psi^{l}=0
, such that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)-\psi^{l}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\;\;\mbox{ as }\;\;t\to+\infty,\quad(-\infty\,\,\mbox{respectively}).

Equivalently,
\psi
is said to scatter forward (or backward) in
\mathcal{H}
if there exists
\psi_{\theta}^{l}:=e^{-\theta it\langle D\rangle}\varphi_{\theta}\;(\varphi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\theta}(t)-\psi_{\theta}^{l}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\;\;\mbox{ as }\;\;t\to+\infty,\quad(-\infty\,\,\mbox{respectively}).

By time reversal symmetry, it is enough to consider the forward scattering. In view of Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to prove that, for
\theta\in\{+,-\}
, there exists
\phi_{\theta}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\theta}(t)-e^{-\theta it\left\langle D\right\rangle}\phi_{\theta}\right\|_{H^{5}(\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2}dx)}\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\;\;t\to+\infty.

(1.10)

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

As we mentioned in known results, we cannot utilize the bilinear and Strichartz estimates via
U^{p}
–
V^{p}
spaces. For the purpose of obtaining the global well-posedness and showing the scattering phenomenon, we exploit the space-time resonance argument. This argument was first introduced by Germain, Masmoudi, and Shatah in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. It observes a time or space oscillation in the oscillatory integral which nonlinear terms of the main equation make. More precisely, we set an a priori assumption, which incorporates sufficient time decay to show scattering results at the expense of high regularity and weighted norm conditions. Indeed, we solve (
1.4
) in a function space, given by the norm for some
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=\left\{\psi:\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm\}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|xe^{\theta it\left\langle D\right\rangle}\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{H^{5}}\right)\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}\right\}.

In view of dispersive estimates for Klein-Gordon operator, we need the second order weight condition to obtain global decay
t^{-1}
in two dimensions. Since the decay effect of the nonlinearity is strong enough to show the linear scattering, we do not pursue the full decay here. However, we need to estimate decay of the weighted Sobolev space
W^{k,\infty}
for some regularity
k>0
that requires the initial data condition (see Proposition
2.1
). This fact enables us to consider only the first-order weighted condition. Then, using the assumption and decay property, we perform the weighted energy estimates. For these estimates, we invoke the Duhamel’s principle (
1.9
) with Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{{\theta_{0}}it\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\widehat{\psi_{\theta_{0}}}(t,\xi)&=\widehat{\psi_{0,{\theta_{0}}}}(\xi)+i\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\theta_{j}}\in\{\pm\}\\
j=1,2,3\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\left\langle\eta\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta_{2}}}(\sigma),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{3}}}(\eta+\sigma)\right\rangle\\
&\hskip 213.39566pt\times{\alpha}^{\nu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{1}}}(\xi-\eta)\,d\sigma d\eta ds,\end{aligned}

(1.11)

where a 4-tuple
\Theta=({\theta_{0}},{\theta_{1}},{\theta_{2}},{\theta_{3}})
and the resonant function

\displaystyle\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)={\theta_{0}}\left\langle x\right\rangle-{\theta_{1}}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle-{\theta_{2}}\left\langle\eta+\sigma\right\rangle+{\theta_{3}}\left\langle\sigma\right\rangle.

The main quantity that we shall handle is
xe^{\theta it\left\langle D\right\rangle}\psi_{\theta}
in
L^{2}
which corresponds to
(x-\theta it\left\langle D\right\rangle)\psi_{\theta}
in
L^{2}
. This operator cannot be commute with the equation, however, we may bound the weighted norm as follows. We apply
\nabla_{\xi}
to (
1.11
) via Fourier transform and then we obtain the worst term of the form

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}s\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)e^{is\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\left\langle\eta\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta_{2}}}(\sigma),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{3}}}(\eta+\sigma)\right\rangle{\alpha}^{\nu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{1}}}(\xi-\eta)\,d\sigma d\eta ds.

(1.12)

To bound (
1.12
), we need to recover the time growth
s
. To this end, we exploit the space-time resonance argument. Indeed, we estimate (
1.12
) by separating the space resonant case (
\nabla_{\eta}\phi_{\Theta}=0
or
\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}=0
), the time resonant case (
\phi_{\Theta}=0
), and the space-time resonant case satisfying both resonant cases. The first main proof of our paper is as follows:

(1)

The phase function
\phi_{\Theta}
does not exhibit any non-resonant case when
{\theta_{0}}={\theta_{1}},\,{\theta_{2}}={\theta_{3}}
. Fortunately, case
{\theta_{0}}={\theta_{1}}
has a strong null structure
\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\Big|_{\eta=0}=0
, sufficient to eliminate the resonant cases, i.e., it suffices only to handle the non-resonant case in terms of
\sigma
oscillation via Lemma
2.4
and we can establish to bound (
1.12
). On the other hand, there is no such null structure when
{\theta_{0}}\neq{\theta_{1}}
and however, the phase function is non-resonant in time, i.e., we have
|\phi_{\Theta}|\geq C
. Due to this time non-resonance, we may utilize the normal form approach. This approach leads us to treat the case in which the time derivatives fall on other spinors. To estimate these terms, we develop the bilinear estimates in which the time derivatives give the extra time decay effect (see Lemma
2.5
). This lemma also enables us to recover the time loss
s
. See the Section
4.1
.

The rest of the main proof is devoted to considering the resonant case
{\theta_{2}}\neq{\theta_{3}}
. In contrast, since there is no oscillation with respect to
\sigma
in this case, we cannot use the improved bilinear estimates (Lemma
2.4
). Thus, other approaches to obtain an additional time decay effect have to be employed. We give the second main observation of this paper:

(2)

We decompose the case by considering the frequency support relations
|\xi|
,
|\xi-\eta|,\,|\eta+\sigma|
, and
|\sigma|
to bound (
1.12
). There are two approaches to obtain an extra time decay. One approach proceeds using the time non-resonant case. For the time resonant case, we observe the space resonance cases in which there is no oscillation in
\eta
or
\sigma
,

\nabla_{\eta}\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)=0\quad\mbox{or}\quad\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)=0.

In this observation, since the extra decay effect accompanies the singularities
|\eta|^{-1}
or
|\sigma|^{-1}
, it is natural to employ the null structure which can eliminate these singularities. Among the sign relations, the case
\Theta=(+,-,-,+)
does not give any structure to recover the singularities, even though we consider the multiplier
\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\Theta}
. To avoid this difficulty, we add the
\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}
into the multiplier
\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\Theta}
. Then this new multiplier gives rise to a sufficient null structure

\left[\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\Theta}+\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}\right](\xi,\eta,\sigma)\Big|_{\sigma=0}=0

to reduce the singularity, arising from space resonance with respect to
\sigma
. Lastly, we estimate the remainder term with
\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}
by taking advantage of
[\nabla_{\sigma}\phi_{\Theta}]e^{is\phi_{\Theta}}=s^{-1}\nabla_{\sigma}(e^{is\phi_{\Theta}})
with integration by parts in
\sigma
. See the last case in section
4.2
for the details.

Remark 1.4
.

This argument has been applied to show a global well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of dispersive equations with various non-local differential operators including Dirac operator. We refer to
[
7
,
24
,
33
,

.

Remark 1.5

Regarding the global well-posedness and scattering results for the non-stationary Chern-Simons-Proca fields, we expect that we set appropriate a priori assumption by exploiting the space-time resonance argument. We treat this topic in the future work.

\textbf{A}\otimes\textbf{B}=(A_{i}B_{j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\cdots,n\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}
. We often denote
\textbf{A}\otimes\textbf{B}
simply by
A
B
. We also consider the product of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
by
xf=x\otimes f
. Moreover, we denote
\nabla f:=(\partial_{j}f_{i})_{ij}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
A priori assumption

As we mentioned in introduction, our proof of main theorem is based on the bootstrap argument. To perform that, we give a priori assumption as follows: For
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}}:=\|\psi_{+}\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{+}}+\|\psi_{-}\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{-}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1},

(2.1)

where, for
\theta\in\{+,-\}
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\theta}\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\theta}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|xe^{\theta it\left\langle D\right\rangle}\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{H^{5}}\right).

Under this assumption, we have the time decay effect.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\psi
satisfies a priori assumption (
2.1
) for given
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
T
. Then we have a decay

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(2.2)

for any
t\in[0,T]
and
0\leq k\leq 7
.

Remark 2.2
.

Regarding the time decay for linear Klein-Gordon equations, we may have the decay
t^{-1}
for sufficient smooth function
(
see
[
13
]
)
. It suffices to obtain the decay
t^{-\frac{1}{2}-{\varepsilon}}
to handle the cubic nonlinearity. In (
2.2
),
\frac{3}{4}
is not optimal and we may reduce the regularity assumption in Theorem
1.2
by optimizing the time decay. In this paper, we do not pursue optimality of the regularity
H^{s}(s\geq 50)
or
H^{5}(\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2}dx)
in (
2.1
). Our regularity condition is necessary to obtain
W^{k,\infty}
decay with the regularity condition
0\leq k\leq 7
.

Proof.

Note that we write

\psi_{\theta}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi,

where

\displaystyle\phi_{\theta}(\xi):={-\theta}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\xi\cdot\frac{x}{t}.

(2.3)

If
t\lesssim 1
, then by Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle|\left\langle D\right\rangle^{k}\psi_{\theta}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k-s}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\theta}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}}

for
0\leq k\leq 7
. Hence it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{4}}{\varepsilon}_{1},\end{aligned}

(2.4)

for
t\gg 1
. For the purpose of showing (
2.4
), we take a frequency decomposition of
\psi_{\theta}
such that

\psi_{\theta}(t,x)=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi.

Then we get

\displaystyle t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\left\langle D\right\rangle^{k}\psi_{\theta}(t,x)|\leq\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x),

where

I_{N}(t,x)=t^{\frac{3}{4}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\right|.

The proof is based on the stationary phase method. For this, we decompose the frequency support as

\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x)=\left(\sum_{N\leq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}+\sum_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}\right)I_{N}(t,x).

The low-frequency part can be estimated as

\sum_{N\leq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}I_{N}(t,x)\lesssim\sum_{N\leq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}t^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\rho_{N}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\frac{4}{3}}}\left\|\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{4}}\lesssim\|\left\langle x\right\rangle f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

For the high-frequency part, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}I_{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}t^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{1+k}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}t^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{8}N^{-s}\|f_{\theta}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{3}{4}}t^{(8-s)\frac{1}{s}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Let us now focus on the mid-frequency part. To prove this part, we use both the non-stationary and stationary
phase methods. From (
2.3
), we have

\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)=-\theta\frac{\xi}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\frac{x}{t}.

Then, when
|x|\geq t
, the phase
\phi_{\theta}
is non-stationary. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)|\gtrsim\left|\frac{|x|}{t}-\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\right|\gtrsim 1-\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\gtrsim\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{-2}.

(2.5)

On the other hand, the phase
\phi_{\theta}
could be stationary around

\xi_{0}
when
|x|<t
:

\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi_{0})=0\quad\mbox{where}\quad\xi_{0}=-\theta\frac{x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}.

We now set
|\xi_{0}|\sim N_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
N\nsim N_{0}
, then the phase is non-stationary and simple calculation yields that

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)\Big|\gtrsim\max\left(\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}},\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{\left\langle N_{0}\right\rangle^{3}}\right).

(2.6)

Let
\mathbf{p}_{\phi}=\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}|^{2}}
. Then we write the mid-frequency of

I_{N}(t,x)
by integration by parts as follows:

t^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{2}I_{N}^{j}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{N}^{1}(t,x)&=it^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\,d\xi,\\
I_{N}^{2}(t,x)&=it^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}(\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\,d\xi.\end{aligned}

(2.7)

By (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain the following bounds independent
of
N_{0}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}N^{-1},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5}N^{-2}.\end{aligned}

(2.8)

Using (
2.8
) and Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
(
2\leq p<\infty
), we see that

\displaystyle\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}N^{-1}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\left([{\Lambda}(\xi)]^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3+k}N^{-1}\left(N\|xf_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}+N^{1-2{\varepsilon}}\left\|\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}}}\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq 1}\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|+\sum_{1\leq N\leq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}+{\varepsilon}}\|\left\langle x\right\rangle f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}+t^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{8}{s}}\|\left\langle x\right\rangle f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

In a similar way to above, we also obtain

\displaystyle|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5}N^{-2}\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5+k}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\left\langle x\right\rangle f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}},

which leads us to

\sum_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{1}{s}}}|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

It remains to consider the stationary phase case,
N\sim N_{0}
. We
further decompose
|\xi-\xi_{0}|
into dyadic pieces
L
. Let
L_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}

satisfy that
\frac{L_{0}}{2}<t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq L_{0}
. Then we write

t^{\frac{3}{4}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq\sum_{L=L_{0}}^{2^{10}N}|J_{L}|,

where

\displaystyle J_{L}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &t^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\qquad\mbox{when }L=L_{0},\\
&t^{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\quad\qquad\mbox{when }L>L_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The case
L=L_{0}
is the stationary one. The fact
L_{0}\sim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and Sobolev embedding imply that

|J_{L_{0}}|\lesssim t^{\frac{3}{4}}L_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\|_{L_{\xi}^{4}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

The case
L>L_{0}
returns to non-stationary phase. By integration by parts, we write
J_{L}(t,x)
as in (
2.7
):

J_{L}(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{2}J_{L}^{j}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{L}^{1}(t,x)&=it^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\right)d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{2}(t,x)&=it^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}(\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\right)d\xi.\end{aligned}

Each
J_{L}^{j}
can be estimated similarly to
I_{N}^{j}
with the following phase
bounds:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}L^{-1},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5}L^{-2}.\end{aligned}

We omit details of the proof. This finishes the proof of time decay (
2.2
).
∎

2.2.
Useful estimates

Here we give several lemmas, useful in the sequel. We begin with introducing a multiplier lemma about pseudo-product operators, which will be repeatedly used in our main proof.

Lemma 2.3

(Coifman-Meyer operator estimates)
.

Assume that a multiplier
m
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\textbf{m}_{1}\|_{\rm CM}

\displaystyle:=\left\|\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{m}_{1}(\xi,\eta)e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{iy\cdot\eta}d\xi d\eta\right\|_{L_{x,y}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2+2})}<\infty,

\displaystyle\|\textbf{m}_{2}\|_{\rm CM}

\displaystyle:=\left\|\int\!\!\!\!\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{iy\cdot\eta}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}d\xi d\eta\right\|_{L_{x,y,z}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2+2+2})}<\infty.

Then, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{m}_{1}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{\psi}(\xi\pm\eta)\widehat{\phi}(\eta)d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\|\textbf{m}_{1}\|_{\rm CM}\|\psi\|_{L^{p}}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}.

Moreover, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\widehat{\psi}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\phi}(\eta\pm\sigma)\widehat{{\varphi}}(\sigma)\,d\sigma d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\|\textbf{m}_{2}\|_{\rm CM}\|\psi\|_{L^{p}}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}}\|{\varphi}\|_{L^{r}}.

These multiplier estimates lead us the following lemma by exploiting the space resonance approach.

Lemma 2.4

(Non-resonant estimates)
.

Assume that
\psi
satisfies a priori assumption (
2.1
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\left\langle\psi_{\theta}(t),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{\theta}(t)\right\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{7}{4}+\gamma}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}

(2.9)

for sufficiently small
0<\gamma\ll 1
and
\theta\in\{+,-\}
.

Proof.

Taking Fourier transform in the left-hand side of (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[P_{N}\left\langle\psi_{\theta}(t),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{\theta}(t)\right\rangle\right](\xi)=\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)=\theta(\left\langle\eta\right\rangle-\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle).

(2.11)

To extract an extra time decay in (
2.10
), we use the space resonance approach. To this end, we note that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\right|=\left|\frac{\eta}{\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\eta}{\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle}\right|\gtrsim\begin{cases}\frac{N}{\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{3}}&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\sim N_{2},\\
\frac{N_{1}}{\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle}&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\gg N_{2},\end{cases}

(2.12)

where
|\eta|\sim N_{1}
and
|\xi+\eta|\sim N_{2}
for the dyadic numbers
N_{1}
,
N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Here we may only consider that
N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}
due to a symmetry. Using a relation

e^{is{\varphi}_{\theta}}=-i\frac{1}{s}\frac{\nabla_{\eta}\varphi_{\theta}\cdot\nabla_{\eta}e^{is{\varphi}_{\theta}}}{|\nabla_{\eta}\varphi_{\theta}|^{2}},

the integration by parts in
\sigma
leads us that (
2.10
) is bounded by the following terms:

\displaystyle s^{-1}\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}\nabla_{\eta}m_{\theta,\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{1}}}(\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{2}}}(\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.13a)

\displaystyle s^{-1}\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}m_{\theta,\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\rho_{N_{1}}(\eta)\nabla_{\eta}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{2}}}(\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.13b)

\displaystyle s^{-1}\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{is\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}m_{\theta,\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{1}}}(\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\rho_{N_{2}}(\xi+\eta)\nabla_{\eta}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.13c)

plus a similar term, where

m_{\theta,\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\nabla_{\eta}\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}{|\nabla_{\eta}\varphi_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}}\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\eta)\rho_{N_{2}}(\xi+\eta).

By Lemma
2.3
with

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\eta}^{\ell}m_{\theta,\textbf{N}}\|_{\rm CM}\lesssim\begin{cases}N^{-1}N_{1}^{-\ell}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{11+2\ell}&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\sim N_{2},\\
N_{1}^{-1-\ell}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\gg N_{2},\end{cases}

for
\ell=0
,
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{2}}\|\eqref{eq:biliear1}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{2}}s^{-1}N^{-1}N_{1}^{-1}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{13}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-1}s^{-\frac{7}{4}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\gg N_{2}}\|\eqref{eq:biliear1}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\gg N_{2}}s^{-1}N_{1}^{-2}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{-1}s^{-\frac{7}{4}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{2}}\|\eqref{eq:biliear2}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{2}}s^{-1}N^{-1}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{11}\|P_{N_{1}}(xf_{\theta})\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-1}s^{-\frac{7}{4}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\gg N_{2}}\|\eqref{eq:biliear2}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\gg N_{2}}s^{-1}N^{-1}\left\langle N\right\rangle\|P_{N_{1}}(xf_{\theta})\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\theta,N_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-1}s^{-\frac{7}{4}+\gamma}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}.

Estimates for (
2.13c
) can be handled similarly. This finishes the proof of (
2.9
).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\psi
satisfies a priori assumption (
2.1
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}P_{N}f_{\theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{-5}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\partial_{t}\left\langle\psi_{\theta}(t),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{\theta}(t)\right\rangle\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{4}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

(2.15)

for sufficiently small
0<\gamma\ll 1
and
\theta\in\{+,-\}
.

Proof.

By Duhamel’s principle, recall that

\partial_{t}f_{\theta}(t)=C\Pi_{\theta}(D)\sum_{{\theta_{j}}\in\{\pm\},j=1,2,3}\left[\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle\psi_{\theta_{2}}(t),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{\theta_{3}}(t)\right\rangle\right]{\alpha}^{\nu}\psi_{\theta_{1}}(t).

Then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}P_{N}f_{\theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{-5}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\theta_{j}}\in\{\pm\},j=1,2,3\end{subarray}}\|\left\langle D\right\rangle^{-1}\left\langle\psi_{\theta_{2}}(t),{\alpha}^{\mu}\psi_{{\theta_{3}}}(t)\right\rangle\|_{H^{5}}\left\|\psi_{\theta_{1}}\right\|_{W^{5,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{-5}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

This finishes the proof of (
2.14
).

Let us consider (
2.15
). Since the time derivative falls on the phase function or spinors, the left-hand side of (
2.15
) is bounded by the
L^{2}
-norm of the following terms:

\displaystyle\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.16a)

\displaystyle\rho_{N}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}\left\langle\partial_{t}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.16b)

and symmetric term where time derivative
\partial_{t}
falls on the other spinor. Here
{\varphi}_{\theta}
is defined in (
2.11
). To estimate (
2.16a
) in
L^{2}
, we first localize
|\eta|
,
|\xi+\eta|
into the dyadic numbers
N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and we observe the phase function
{\varphi}_{\theta}
: by the symmetry, we may assume that
N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}
. Then we have

\left|{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\right|=\left|\left\langle\eta\right\rangle-\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle\right|\lesssim N

and

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\right|=\left|\frac{\eta}{\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\eta}{\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle}\right|\gtrsim\begin{cases}\frac{N}{\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{3}}&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\sim N_{2},\\
\frac{N_{1}}{\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle}&\mbox{ for }N_{1}\gg N_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.17)

From this observation, one can see that

e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}=-\frac{i}{t}\frac{\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\cdot\nabla_{\eta}e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}}{\left|\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\right|^{2}}.

Using the above relation, we perform the normal form transform to bound (
2.16a
) by the following terms:

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{N}(\xi)}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}\nabla_{\eta}m_{\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{1}}}(t,\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{2}}}(t,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.18a)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{N}(\xi)}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}m_{\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\rho_{N_{1}}(\eta)\nabla_{\eta}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{2}}}(t,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.18b)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{N}(\xi)}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{it{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}m_{\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)\left\langle\rho_{N_{1}}(\eta)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(t,\eta),{\alpha}^{\mu}\nabla_{\eta}\widehat{f_{\theta,N_{2}}}(t,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta,

(2.18c)

where

m_{\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta):=\frac{{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)}{|\nabla_{\eta}{\varphi}_{\theta}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}}\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\eta)\rho_{N_{2}}(\xi+\eta)

with
\textbf{N}=(N,N_{1},N_{2})
. Note that
f_{\theta_{1}}
and
f_{\theta_{2}}
are not symmetric from the support condition
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
. Let us consider
N\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}
. Then, we have the pointwise and Coifman-meyer bound

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\eta}m_{\textbf{N}}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{5}\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\|\nabla_{\eta}m_{\textbf{N}}\|_{\rm CM}\lesssim N_{1}^{-1}\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle^{11}.

(2.19)

Thus, for
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\leq\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}}
, we use Lemma
2.3
, Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{p}

(2\leq p<\infty)
, and Hölder inequality and, for
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\geq\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}}
, we utilize Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
with (
2.19

Proof of weighted estimates

This section is devoted to prove Proposition

. From the fact that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Theta}^{1}(s,\xi)

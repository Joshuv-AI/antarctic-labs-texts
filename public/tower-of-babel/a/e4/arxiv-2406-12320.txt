Viscosity-uniform error estimates for a semi-implicit Fourier method in Besov spaces
Xinyu Cheng, Zhaonan Luo, Sheng Wang

Abstract
. We prove error estimates for a semi-implicit Fourier–Galerkin method for two-dimensional incompressible flow on the torus, with viscosity 0 ≤ ν ≤ 1 0\leq\nu\leq 1 . The temporal error is first order in H s H^{s} for H s + 2 H^{s+2} initial data and integer s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 . For data in B 2 , 1 m B^{m}_{2,1} , with integer m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , the fully discrete error is of order τ + N − ( m − 2 ) \tau+N^{-(m-2)} in B 2 , 1 1 B^{1}_{2,1} and B ∞ , 1 0 B^{0}_{\infty,1} . These estimates hold on a common local interval, with constants and time-step thresholds independent of viscosity and Fourier resolution. For data in B 2 , 1 2 + ε B^{2+\varepsilon}_{2,1} , 0 < ε < 1 0<\varepsilon<1 , the error relative to Euler flow is bounded by C ⁡ ( τ ( 1 + ε ) / 2 + N − ε + ν ( 1 + ε ) / 2 ) C(\tau^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+N^{-\varepsilon}+\nu^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}) . The consistency bounds use viscosity-weighted regularity. Orthogonality of the Leray and Fourier projectors in the block energies gives the endpoint estimates, and stability with respect to the initial data yields the fractional rate. An L 2 L^{2} residual tolerance δ \delta contributes C ​ N ​ δ CN\delta to the endpoint error of computed iterates. We report refinement studies for smooth and fixed multiscale data, together with qualitative flow comparisons. An exactly solvable rough shear has fractional errors, up to logarithms, in B 2 , 1 1 B^{1}_{2,1} and first-order errors in B ∞ , 1 0 B^{0}_{\infty,1} .

1.
Introduction

Let
U^{\nu}
be the velocity of an incompressible fluid on

\mathbb{T}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}))^{2}
:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}U^{\nu}+U^{\nu}\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}+\nabla p^{\nu}=\nu\Delta U^{\nu},\qquad\nabla\cdot U^{\nu}=0,\qquad U^{\nu}(0)=u_{0}.

For
0\leq\nu\leq 1
, we approximate the velocity by a semi-implicit
Fourier method and write
V=U^{0}
for the Euler solution. We seek
error bounds in norms that control the uniform velocity error,
with constants bounded as
\nu\downarrow 0
. The continuous and
discrete solutions are compared on a common local interval
determined by the initial data.

Energy estimates are central to Leray’s construction of weak
solutions for viscous flow
[
22
]
. For two-dimensional Euler
flow, Yudovich
[
34
]
proved global well-posedness for bounded
vorticity. Marion and Temam
[
29
]
survey the continuous
theory, Galerkin approximation, and time discretization.
The error estimates below require bounds on the discrete velocity
in the Sobolev or Besov class of the continuous solution.

The time discretization is

(1.2)

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u^{n+1},\qquad u^{0}=u_{0},

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projector. The Fourier–Galerkin method
applies the orthogonal cutoff
\Pi_{N}
, retaining
|k|_{\infty}\leq N
,
to the nonlinear term and starts from
\Pi_{N}u_{0}
.
Each update is linear in the new velocity. Since
u^{n}
is divergence
free, periodicity gives

(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1},u^{n+1})=0
.
The resulting kinetic-energy identity holds for every time step.
At higher regularity, the same cancellation leaves commutator terms
in the differentiated and localized equations. We bound these terms
uniformly in viscosity on a common local interval. The step
restriction for the inner iteration is treated separately.

Chorin introduced the projection method and established convergence
estimates for discrete Navier–Stokes
approximations
[
8
,
9
]
. In the periodic setting, Guo and
Zou
[
13
]
analyzed nonlinear convergence of a Fourier spectral
projection method. He
[
16
]
studied
implicit–explicit Euler time discretization for smooth and
nonsmooth data. For finite elements, Heywood
and Rannacher related spatial error estimates to the regularity of
the solution near the initial time
[
17
]
and subsequently
proved second-order bounds for Crank–Nicolson time
discretization
[
18
]
. For the method studied here, the
regularity bounds used in both stability and consistency must
remain uniform as viscosity vanishes.

In low-regularity integrators, part of the evolution is incorporated
into the numerical step to reduce the differentiability required
by the consistency estimate; see Rousset and Schratz
[
31
]
. Energy stability and convergence have been proved
for an iterative low-regularity Cahn–Hilliard method
[
4
]
,
and optimal error estimates hold for a surface quasi-geostrophic
(SQG) integrator at endpoint regularity
[
5
]
.
For Navier–Stokes, Li, Ma, and Schratz
[
23
]

apply the viscous semigroup to the transporting velocity in a
semi-implicit exponential integrator. Their first-order
L^{2}

error bound is uniform in viscosity under uniform solution bounds
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,p})
,
p>2
. Under a uniform
H^{4}
solution
bound, Ma’s second-order exponential method
[
28
]
gives an

L^{2}
error estimate with a constant that remains bounded as
viscosity tends to zero. At fixed positive viscosity,
parabolic smoothing permits substantially rougher initial data:
Li, Ma, and Ueda
[
24
]
use graded time steps for a
semi-implicit finite element method with divergence-free
L^{2}

data, and Li, Rao, Zhang, and Zhou
[
25
]
obtain second-order
temporal and nearly second-order spatial bounds for an
implicit–explicit Runge–Kutta finite element method with critical

L^{2}
data. These latter estimates allow singular behaviour near
the initial time. We study the semi-implicit update
(
1.2
) in Besov norms that control the uniform
velocity error. Its first-order bounds for
B^{3}_{2,1}
data and
fractional bounds for
B^{2+\varepsilon}_{2,1}
data,
0<\varepsilon<1
,
hold through
t=0
on a common local interval, including at zero viscosity.

For the continuous inviscid limit, Masmoudi proved Sobolev convergence
and quantitative bounds for vortex-patch data
[
30
]
. Hmidi and Keraani treated viscous flows in
borderline Besov spaces
[
20
]
, while Guo, Li, and Yin established
local well-posedness in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
and an inviscid
limit
[
14
]
. For the SQG equations, quantitative inviscid
convergence in a lower-order Besov norm and convergence in the
topology of the initial data were established in
[
6
]
.
Uniform-in-time estimates for the
vanishing-damping limit of the inviscid Oldroyd–B model were
proved in
[
7
]
.
To estimate the discrete consistency defect, we use the paraproduct
and transport commutator estimates in
[
1
]
, together with
the fractional product and commutator inequalities in
[
27
]
.

This manuscript combines and revises the Sobolev and Besov analyses
of (
1.2
) presented in the original versions
of our earlier preprints
[
2
,
3
]
.
The endpoint error is measured in
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
, the sum of
the supremum norms of the dyadic blocks. It controls
L^{\infty}
;
in two dimensions,

(1.3)

B^{1}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}.

The main Besov error bounds can be summarized as follows.

Theorem

(Main results, informal)
.

\mathbb{T}^{2}

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u_{N}^{n}-V(t_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\bigl(\tau^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+N^{-\varepsilon}+\nu^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}\bigr).

Both bounds also hold in
B^{1}_{2,1}
. When the comparison is with

, the respective viscosity term is omitted. The constants,
common interval, and time-step threshold are independent of
\nu
and

.

By (

velocity error, including at zero viscosity. The precise statements are
given in Theorem
4.8
and
Corollaries

,
5.6
,
and
5.7
. Subsection

extends the first-order estimates to
B^{r}_{\infty,1}
for integer

.

We test each dyadic block in
L^{2}
and sum in
\ell^{1}
.
In these localized energy identities, the Leray projector drops
out and diffusion contributes a nonnegative term. The sharp
Fourier cutoff is also an
L^{2}
contraction on every block,
including those whose support crosses the cutoff boundary.
Its truncation error can thus be estimated in the stability norm
without a uniform
L^{\infty}
multiplier bound for
\Pi_{N}
.
We assume initial data in
B^{m}_{2,1}
, which contains

B^{m}_{\infty,1}
on the torus. The
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
error follows
from an
H^{1}
estimate; that Besov norm does not in general
control
L^{\infty}
.

For integers
s\geq 1
and
m\geq s+2
,

gives

(1.6)

\max_{t_{n}\leq T}\|u_{N}^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\bigl(\tau+N^{-(m-s-1)}\bigr);

see Theorem
3.1
.
For integers
s\geq 1
, the first-order
H^{s}
time estimates are proved
for
H^{s+2}
data. The separate
L^{2}
estimate assumes
H^{3}
initial data.
We control the viscous consistency defect by error dissipation in the
Sobolev estimate and by viscosity-weighted smoothing in the Besov estimate,
avoiding an unweighted bound on the second time derivative.
For fractional regularity, we smooth the initial data at frequency
R\simeq\tau^{-1/2}

and apply a first-order estimate whose dependence on the high
norm is linear. The unsmoothed low norm controls both the common
lifespan and the stability constants.
For computed Galerkin iterates, the effect of an inexact linear
solve is measured in
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
: under the assumptions of
Corollary
6.3
, an
L^{2}
residual
bounded by
\delta
in the equation divided by
\tau
adds

CN\delta
to the error in this endpoint norm.

The numerical experiments separate temporal, spatial, and
vanishing-viscosity errors. For low-regularity tests, the initial
datum is fixed and its projection changes with the resolution.
For the multiscale manufactured solution, the temporal error is
computed against the exact solution; for the unforced smooth flow,
we use an independent reference calculation. An exactly solvable transported shear measures
the spatial tail. For the rough lacunary shear, both norms in
(
1.3
) can be evaluated directly:
the temporal and viscosity errors have fractional rates, up to
logarithms, in
B^{1}_{2,1}
, while both errors are first order in

B^{0}_{\infty,1}
. This example leaves open the optimal uniform
rate in the endpoint norm. Qualitative comparisons include
double-shear roll-up at several viscosities.

2.
Preliminaries

We work on
\mathbb{T}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}))^{2}
with normalized
measure
(2\pi)^{-2}dx
. Our Fourier and Sobolev conventions are

z(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{z}(k)e^{ik\cdot x},\qquad\|z\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{z}(k)|^{2},\qquad\Lambda=(I-\Delta)^{1/2}.

The Leray projector
\mathbb{P}
has symbol
I-k\otimes k/|k|^{2}
for

k\neq 0
and
I
at
k=0
. The orthogonal projector
\Pi_{N}
retains the
modes
|k|_{\infty}\leq N
. The real divergence-free Fourier space is

X_{N}:=\bigl\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\Pi_{N}v=v,\quad\nabla\cdot v=0\bigr\}.

These projectors commute with derivatives and with the dyadic
multipliers introduced below.

Choose a smooth radial cutoff
\chi
with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
, equal to
one on
r\leq 1
and zero on
r\geq 2
, and define

\widehat{\Delta_{0}z}(k)=\chi(|k|)\widehat{z}(k),\qquad\widehat{\Delta_{j}z}(k)=\left[\chi(2^{-j}|k|)-\chi(2^{-j+1}|k|)\right]\widehat{z}(k),\quad j\geq 1.

These symbols form a partition of unity. The inhomogeneous Besov norm is

\|z\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|(2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}z\|_{p})_{j\geq 0}\right\|_{\ell^{q}}.

Here
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, and vector norms use the Euclidean magnitude.
Other admissible cutoffs give equivalent norms. The numerical cutoff
is specified in Section
7.1.1
.

For nonnegative quantities,
a\asymp b
means
cb\leq a\leq Cb
with
constants
c,C>0
independent of the variable or parameter in the
limit under consideration.

The two-dimensional Bernstein inequality and finite volume imply

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|z\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}},

\displaystyle\|z\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z\|_{H^{1}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|z\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}},

\displaystyle\|z\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|z\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}.

We use the inhomogeneous paraproduct and commutator estimates of

[
1
, Chapters 2 and 3]
. In the
\ell^{1}
Besov argument, the
algebra
B^{1}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
controls multiplication.
The
\ell^{2}
endpoint estimate follows from the
H^{1}
bound.

Write

H^{r}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):=\{z\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot z=0\}.

All initial velocities are assumed to be divergence free.
For
0\leq\nu\leq 1
, let
U^{\nu}
solve

(2.3)

\partial_{t}U^{\nu}+\mathbb{P}(U^{\nu}\cdot\nabla U^{\nu})=\nu\Delta U^{\nu},\qquad U^{\nu}(0)=u_{0},

and let
V
solve the corresponding Euler equation

(2.4)

\partial_{t}V+\mathbb{P}(V\cdot\nabla V)=0,\qquad V(0)=u_{0}.

The fully discrete Fourier–Galerkin approximation is

(2.5)

\frac{u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n}}{\tau}+\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u_{N}^{n+1},\qquad u_{N}^{0}=\Pi_{N}u_{0}.

Here
t_{n}=n\tau
,
M\tau=T
, and
0\leq n\leq M-1
.

The pure time-discrete scheme is

(2.6)

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u^{n+1},\qquad u^{0}=u_{0}.

The continuous equations and both schemes preserve the spatial mean
of
u_{0}
: the transport and diffusion terms integrate to zero by
periodicity and incompressibility, and
\Pi_{N}
retains the zero mode.

Proposition 2.1

(Unconditional kinetic-energy identity)
.

For any
N
,
\tau>0
, and
\nu\geq 0
, the Fourier scheme (
2.5
) satisfies, for
0\leq n\leq M-1
,

(2.7)

\|u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}+2\nu\tau\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Pair the equation with
2\tau u_{N}^{n+1}
. The projector drops from the inner product because the test field belongs to
X_{N}
. Periodicity and
\divergence u_{N}^{n}=0
give

(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1},u_{N}^{n+1})=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla|u_{N}^{n+1}|^{2}=0.

The polarization identity and integration by parts give (
2.7
). The same argument applies to the pure time-discrete equation.
∎

Identity (
2.7
) holds for every time step.
The regularity and error bounds below are local in time, with a step
threshold determined by solution norms.

3.
Sobolev estimates

All Sobolev indices in this section are nonnegative integers.

3.1.
Discrete regularity

We use the transport estimate

(3.1)

\left|\left\langle\Lambda^{m}(a\cdot\nabla z),\Lambda^{m}z\right\rangle\right|\leq C_{m}\|a\|_{H^{m}}\|z\|_{H^{m}}^{2},\qquad m>2,

when both
a
and
z
belong to
H^{m}_{\sigma}
.

For every
0\leq r\leq m
, Fourier truncation gives

(3.2)

\|(I-\Pi_{N})f\|_{H^{r}}\leq CN^{-(m-r)}\|f\|_{H^{m}}.

The constants are independent of
N
and
\nu
.

For fixed
u_{N}^{n}
, equation (
2.5
) is a linear problem in

X_{N}
. Its homogeneous equation gives

\|z\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\tau\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}=0,

by skew-symmetry of transport. The operator is therefore invertible.

Taking the
H^{m}
inner product of (
2.5
) with

u_{N}^{n+1}
and using (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}-\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\right)+\nu\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{m}\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{H^{m}}\|u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Let
X_{n}=\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{H^{m}}
. If

X_{n}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}
and

4C_{m}\tau\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}\leq 1/2
, then
(
3.3
) implies

X_{n+1}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-4C_{m}\tau\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}}X_{n}^{2}.

Put
A=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}
; the case
A=0
is immediate. Under the induction
hypothesis
X_{j}\leq 2A
for
0\leq j\leq n
, iteration of the preceding
one-step estimate gives

(3.4)

X_{n+1}^{2}\leq(1-4C_{m}\tau A)^{-(n+1)}A^{2}.

For
0\leq x\leq 1/2
,
-\log(1-x)\leq 2x
. Consequently,

(1-4C_{m}\tau A)^{-(n+1)}\leq\exp\bigl(8C_{m}(n+1)\tau A\bigr)\leq\exp(8C_{m}TA).

Choose

4C_{m}\tau A\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad 8C_{m}TA\leq\log 4.

Then (
3.4
) gives

X_{n+1}^{2}\leq 4A^{2}
, so the induction closes and

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}X_{n}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}.

Both restrictions depend only on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}
.

3.2.
Error estimates

For integers
s\geq 1
, the first-order
H^{s}
estimate assumes

m\geq s+2
. We split the consistency defect into convective and viscous
parts and absorb the
latter in the error dissipation. This avoids a uniform bound on

\partial_{tt}U^{\nu}
.
Throughout the paper, viscosity-weighted higher-norm terms are omitted
when
\nu=0
.

Theorem 3.1

(Sobolev error and inviscid comparison)
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
m\geq s+2
be integers, and let

u_{0}\in H^{m}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Suppose that
[0,T]
is a common
interval of existence on which

(3.5)

\sup_{0\leq\nu\leq 1}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{m}}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|V(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq K_{m}.

After decreasing
T
, if necessary, by an amount depending only on

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}
, there is

\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(T,K_{m})>0
, independent of
N
and
\nu
, such that
for
0<\tau\leq\tau_{0}
,
N\geq 2
, and
0\leq\nu\leq 1
,

(3.6)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u_{N}^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(\tau+N^{-(m-s-1)}\right).

The corresponding time-discrete solution satisfies

(3.7)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\tau.

In addition,

(3.8)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U^{\nu}(t)-V(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\nu,

and hence

(3.9)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u_{N}^{n}-V(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(\tau+N^{-(m-s-1)}+\nu\right).

All constants are independent of
\nu
,
\tau
, and
N
.
In particular,
s=1,m=3
gives the uniform estimate

\max_{n}\|u_{N}^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(\tau+N^{-1}).

Proof.

Continuous estimates.

The
H^{m}
energy identity and the divergence-free cancellation
give, for
m>2
,

(3.10)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|U^{\nu}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla U^{\nu}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C_{m}\|U^{\nu}\|_{H^{m}}^{3}.

Integrating this inequality and using
(
3.5
), as well as the equivalence between

\|\nabla z\|_{H^{m}}+\|z\|_{2}
and
\|z\|_{H^{m+1}}
on the torus,
yields

(3.11)

\sup_{0\leq\nu\leq 1}\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}\,dt\leq C(T,K_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}).

The
L^{2}
term contributes at most
\nu TK_{m}^{2}
.

For
s\geq 1
, multiplication by an
H^{s+1}
function is bounded on

H^{s}
, and the following non-endpoint estimates hold:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\Lambda^{s}(a\cdot\nabla z),\Lambda^{s}z\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{H^{s+2}}\|z\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|b\cdot\nabla c\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{H^{s}}\|c\|_{H^{s+2}},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|a\cdot\nabla h\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{H^{s+2}}\|h\|_{H^{s+1}}.

No divergence-free assumptions are needed. In (
3.12
),
the principal transport term is bounded by

\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla\cdot a\|_{\infty}\|z\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
and vanishes if

\nabla\cdot a=0
. The other terms and (
3.14
)
follow from the Leibniz rule and
H^{s+2}\hookrightarrow W^{s,\infty}
.
At
s=1
, the only additional
product in (
3.13
) is bounded by

\|b\,D^{2}c\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|b\|_{L^{4}}\|D^{2}c\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{1}}\|c\|_{H^{3}},

using the two-dimensional embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{4}
.
The other terms use
\|\nabla c\|_{\infty}\leq C\|c\|_{H^{3}}
.
For
s\geq 2
, the analogous undifferentiated factor is controlled by

H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, and all remaining Leibniz terms use

H^{s+2}\hookrightarrow W^{s,\infty}
.

Equation (
2.3
) and the algebra property
of
H^{s+1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, namely

\|U^{\nu}\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C\|U^{\nu}\|_{H^{s+1}}\|\nabla U^{\nu}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C\|U^{\nu}\|_{H^{s+2}}^{2},

imply

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{s+2}}^{4}\,dt+C\nu^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{s+3}}^{2}\,dt

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C(T,K_{m})+C\nu\left(\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}}^{2}\,dt\right)\leq C.

We used
m\geq s+2
and the weighted estimate
(
3.11
) for the highest derivative.

Consistency.

Define

(3.16)

\displaystyle q^{n+1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\bigl(U^{\nu}(t)-U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})\bigr)\,dt,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\eta_{c}^{n+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}\left[U^{\nu}(t_{n})\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}U^{\nu}(t)\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t)\,dt\right].

Averaging the continuous equation over
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
gives

(3.18)

\frac{U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})-U^{\nu}(t_{n})}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}\bigl(U^{\nu}(t_{n})\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})\bigr)-\nu\Delta U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})=\eta_{c}^{n+1}+\nu\Delta q^{n+1}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy–Schwarz,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|q^{n+1}\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\,dt.

For the convective defect, use

\displaystyle U^{\nu}(t_{n})\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})-U^{\nu}(t)\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigl(U^{\nu}(t_{n})-U^{\nu}(t)\bigr)\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})+U^{\nu}(t)\cdot\nabla\bigl(U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})-U^{\nu}(t)\bigr).

Estimates (
3.13
)–(
3.14
),
(
3.5
), and the fundamental theorem of calculus give

(3.20)

\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\tau\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\,dt.

Consequently, (
3.15
) gives the viscosity-independent bound

(3.21)

\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\left(\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{}+\nu\|q^{n+1}\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\right)\leq C\tau^{2}.

Galerkin error.

The discrete regularity estimate (
3.4
)
gives

(3.22)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{H^{m}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}},

with constants independent of
N
and
\nu
.

Define

\theta^{n}=u_{N}^{n}-\Pi_{N}U^{\nu}(t_{n}),\qquad\rho^{n}=(\Pi_{N}-I)U^{\nu}(t_{n}).

Projecting (
3.18
) and subtracting it from the
scheme gives

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{n+1}-\theta^{n}}{\tau}-\nu\Delta\theta^{n+1}+\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla\theta^{n+1})={}

\displaystyle-\Pi_{N}\eta_{c}^{n+1}-\nu\Delta\Pi_{N}q^{n+1}

\displaystyle-\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}\bigl((\theta^{n}+\rho^{n})\cdot\nabla U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})\bigr)

(3.23)

\displaystyle-\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}\bigl(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla\rho^{n+1}\bigr).

Take the
H^{s}
inner product with
\theta^{n+1}
. The viscous defect
is kept in divergence form:

(3.24)

\displaystyle\nu\left|\left\langle\Lambda^{s}\nabla q^{n+1},\Lambda^{s}\nabla\theta^{n+1}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\theta^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{}+C\nu\|q^{n+1}\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}.

The convective defect satisfies

\left|\langle\Lambda^{s}\eta_{c}^{n+1},\Lambda^{s}\theta^{n+1}\rangle\right|\leq C\|\theta^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{}+C\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

The remaining terms are estimated by
(
3.12
)–(
3.14
). Since

(3.25)

\displaystyle\|\rho^{n}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{m}N^{-(m-s)},\qquad\|\rho^{n}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq CK_{m}N^{-(m-s-1)},

multiplication by
2\tau
and Young’s inequality give

\displaystyle(1-C\tau)\|\theta^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\tau)\|\theta^{n}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{}+C\tau N^{-2(m-s-1)}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\quad+C\tau\left(\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{}+\nu\|q^{n+1}\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}\right).

Here (
3.24
) absorbs the viscous defect
into half of the dissipation.

Choose
C\tau_{0}\leq 1/2
, iterate
(
3.26
), and use

\theta^{0}=0
and (

). Discrete

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\theta^{n}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left(N^{-2(m-s-1)}+\tau^{2}\right).

Combining this with (

(
3.6
). For fixed

weak
H^{m}
and strong

Inviscid limit.

For

4.
Endpoint Besov estimates

We write
U=U^{\nu}
when the viscosity is fixed.
The endpoint estimates follow from localized
L^{2}
energies.
Orthogonality controls the sharp projector on each block, including
those whose supports cross the cutoff.

4.1.
Projection and regularity

Lemma 4.1

(Sharp projection in an
L^{2}
-based Besov space)
.

Let
\Pi_{N}
be the orthogonal Fourier projector onto

\{|k|_{\infty}\leq N\}
. For every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.1)

\|\Pi_{N}f\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}\leq\|f\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}.

If
m>s
, then

(4.2)

\|(I-\Pi_{N})f\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}\leq C_{m,s}N^{s-m}\|f\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}.

The constants are independent of
N
.

Proof.

The multipliers
\Pi_{N}
and
\Delta_{j}
commute, and
\Pi_{N}
is an
orthogonal contraction on
L^{2}
. This proves
(
4.1
) after multiplication by
2^{js}
and
summation in
j
. Moreover,

(I-\Pi_{N})\Delta_{j}=0
when
2^{j}\leq cN
, for a constant depending only
on the supports of the Littlewood–Paley cutoffs. On every remaining
block, including the transition blocks, we use only the
L^{2}

contraction property. Since
2^{j}\geq cN
there,

2^{js}\|(I-\Pi_{N})\Delta_{j}f\|_{2}\leq CN^{s-m}2^{jm}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{2}.

Summing this inequality proves (
4.2
).
∎

We use the critical product and commutator estimates

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\|g\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j}\|[\Delta_{j},a\cdot\nabla]b\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\|b\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}},\qquad\operatorname{div}a=0.

They follow from Bony’s decomposition and the critical embedding

B^{1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
. In particular,

(4.5)

\|a\cdot\nabla b\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\leq C\|a\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\|b\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}.

Lemma 4.2

(Continuous smoothing)
.

Let
U
be a smooth divergence-free solution of Navier–Stokes on

[0,T]
,
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. If

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\leq K_{3},

then

(4.6)

\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}}\,dt\leq C\bigl(\|U(0)\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+TK_{3}^{2}+T\|U(0)\|_{2}\bigr).

The constant is independent of
\nu
.
More generally, for every
s>2
, the bound

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}\leq K_{s}

implies

(4.7)

\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{s+2}_{2,1}}\,dt\leq C_{s,T,K_{s},\|U(0)\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}}.

Proof.

Apply
\Delta_{j}
to the equation and take the
L^{2}
inner product with

\Delta_{j}U
. The divergence-free transport term cancels, and the
localized Poincare inequality gives, for
j\geq 1
,

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta_{j}U\|_{2}+c\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}U\|_{2}\leq\|[\Delta_{j},U\cdot\nabla]U\|_{2}.

After multiplication by
2^{3j}
, summation, and use of the standard

B^{3}_{2,1}
commutator estimate, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+c\nu\|U\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}}\leq C\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}^{2}+C\nu\|\Delta_{0}U\|_{2}.

For the low block, the energy inequality gives

\|\Delta_{0}U(t)\|_{2}\leq C\|U(0)\|_{2}
. Integration proves
(
4.6
). The same calculation after multiplication
by
2^{js}
, using the standard
B^{s}_{2,1}
commutator estimate,
proves (
4.7
).
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Uniform local stability in
B^{m}_{2,1}
)
.

Let
m>2
and
A=\|u_{0}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}
. There are constants

c_{m},C_{m}>0
such that, if

(4.8)

T\leq\frac{c_{m}}{1+A},

then the continuous solution, the time-discrete solution
u^{n}
, and
the sharp Fourier–Galerkin solution
u_{N}^{n}
with initial value

\Pi_{N}u_{0}
satisfy

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}\,dt\leq C_{m}(A+T\|u_{0}\|_{2}),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\bigl(\|u^{n}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}+\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}\bigr)\leq C_{m}A,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\nu\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\bigl(\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}+\|u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}\bigr)\leq C_{m}(A+TA^{2}+T\|u_{0}\|_{2}).

All constants are independent of
\nu\in[0,1]
,
N
, and

\tau=T/M
.

Proof.

The case
A=0
is the zero solution; assume
A>0
.
The continuous estimate is the level-
m
version of the proof of
Lemma
4.2
. In particular,

(4.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\|U\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}+c_{m}\nu\|U\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}\leq C_{m}\|U\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}^{2}+C_{m}\nu\|\Delta_{0}U\|_{2},

where the last term is bounded by the
L^{2}
energy inequality. The
standard continuity bootstrap under (
4.8
)
proves (
4.9
).

For either discrete scheme, apply
\Delta_{j}
and pair the result with

\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}
. In the Fourier case

\Delta_{j}u_{N}^{n+1}\in X_{N}
, so self-adjointness gives

\langle\mathbb{P}\Pi_{N}\Delta_{j}F,\Delta_{j}u_{N}^{n+1}\rangle=\langle\Delta_{j}F,\Delta_{j}u_{N}^{n+1}\rangle.

Transport cancellation and the level-
m
commutator estimate give, for

w^{n}=u^{n}
or
w^{n}=u_{N}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|w^{n+1}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}+c_{m}\nu\tau\|w^{n+1}\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\|w^{n}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}+C_{m}\tau\|w^{n}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}\|w^{n+1}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+C_{m}\nu\tau\|\Delta_{0}w^{n+1}\|_{2}.

The last term is controlled by the discrete
L^{2}
energy inequality.
If
X_{n}=\|w^{n}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}
and
X_{n}\leq 2A
, then, after reducing

c_{m}
in (
4.8
),

X_{n+1}\leq(1-C_{m}\tau X_{n})^{-1}(X_{n}+C_{m}\nu\tau A)\leq(1+4C_{m}A\tau)X_{n}+C^{\prime}_{m}\tau A.

Here we used
\|\Delta_{0}w^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq A
. Discrete Gronwall and a
further reduction of
c_{m}
give
X_{n+1}<2A
, which closes
the bootstrap. Summing (
4.13
) then proves
(
4.11
). Finally,

\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}\leq A
follows from
Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark

(Approximation of the initial data)
.

The estimates hold first for smooth data and extend by
the usual Friedrichs approximation.
For the pure time-discrete scheme, the uniform Galerkin bound gives,
at each of the finitely many time levels, compact convergence in

H^{m-\epsilon}
with
0<\epsilon<m-2
. Passing to the limit yields the
update; blockwise convergence and Fatou’s lemma retain the
B^{m}_{2,1}

bound. With the previous field fixed, uniqueness follows from the

L^{2}
skew-symmetric energy estimate.

4.2.
Consistency

Set
U^{n}=U(t_{n})
and define the residual

(4.14)

\eta^{n+1}:=\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}(U^{n}\cdot\nabla U^{n+1})-\nu\Delta U^{n+1}.

Set

(4.15)

\displaystyle q^{n+1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\bigl(U(t)-U^{n+1}\bigr)\,dt,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\eta_{c}^{n+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}\left(U^{n}\cdot\nabla U^{n+1}-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}U(t)\cdot\nabla U(t)\,dt\right).

Time integration of the equation gives

(4.17)

\eta^{n+1}=\eta_{c}^{n+1}+\nu\Delta q^{n+1}.

Lemma 4.4

(Summed consistency)
.

Assume

(4.18)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}}\,dt\leq K.

Then

(4.19)

\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}+\nu\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\|q^{n+1}\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\leq C_{T,K}\tau.

The constant is uniform for
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.

Proof.

The equation and the product estimates imply

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,K},

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,K},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\nu\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,K}.

Indeed, the first two bounds follow from

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|U\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}^{2}+\nu\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|U\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+\nu\|U\|_{B^{4}_{2,1}},

and the elementary inequality

\|U\|_{B^{4}_{2,1}}\leq C(\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+\|U\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}})
.
Likewise,

\nu\|\partial_{t}U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\leq C\nu\|U\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\|U\|_{B^{4}_{2,1}}+\nu^{2}\|U\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}},

which proves (
4.22
).

For
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, the critical product law gives

\displaystyle\|U^{n}\cdot\nabla U^{n+1}-U(t)\cdot\nabla U(t)\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq CK\left(\int_{t_{n}}^{t}\|\partial_{t}U(s)\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\,ds+\int_{t}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U(s)\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\,ds\right).

Integrating first in
t
and then summing in
n
yields

\tau\sum_{n}\|\eta_{c}^{n+1}\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\leq CK\tau\left(\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{1}(0,T;B^{1}_{2,1})}+\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{1}(0,T;B^{2}_{2,1})}\right).

Finally, (
4.15
) and Fubini’s theorem give

\displaystyle\nu\tau\sum_{n}\|q^{n+1}\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\nu\sum_{n}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{t}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U(s)\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\nu\tau\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{1}(0,T;B^{3}_{2,1})}.

Equations (
4.20
)–(
4.22
)
complete the proof.
∎

4.3.
Time-discrete error

The
L^{2}
block estimates remove pressure and retain
frequency-localized dissipation. Direct endpoint testing has the
limitations described below.

Remark 4.5

(Diffusion and endpoint testing)
.

For a smooth vector field
w_{j}=\Delta_{j}w
and
2\leq p<\infty
,
integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta w_{j},|w_{j}|^{p-2}w_{j}\right\rangle={}

\displaystyle-\int|w_{j}|^{p-2}|\nabla w_{j}|^{2}\,dx

(4.23)

\displaystyle-(p-2)\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\int|w_{j}|^{p-4}(w_{j}\cdot\partial_{\ell}w_{j})^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

For a pressure-free transport–diffusion equation, this sign yields an

L^{p}
norm estimate that passes to
p=\infty
. For velocity equations,
the nonlinear test is generally not divergence free:

\left\langle\nabla p_{j},|w_{j}|^{p-2}w_{j}\right\rangle=-\left\langle p_{j},\operatorname{div}(|w_{j}|^{p-2}w_{j})\right\rangle.

Thus the pressure cancellation in an
L^{2}
test does not extend to this
test when
p\neq 2
.

Moreover, the positive constant in a frequency-localized coercive
inequality depends on
p
; see
[
26
]
. To see why it cannot stay
positive as
p\to\infty
, fix an integer
K\geq 1
and set

g(y)=\frac{3}{2}\cos y-\frac{1}{2}\cos^{3}y=\frac{9}{8}\cos y-\frac{1}{8}\cos(3y),\qquad f_{K}(x)=g(Kx_{1}).

This polynomial has frequencies
K
and
3K
. Near each point
y_{0}

where
|g(y_{0})|=1
,

1-|g(y)|\asymp|y-y_{0}|^{4},\qquad|g^{\prime}(y)|\asymp|y-y_{0}|^{3}.

With
z=p^{1/4}(y-y_{0})
, the contributions near these extrema give

(4.24)

\frac{-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\Delta f_{K})|f_{K}|^{p-2}f_{K}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|f_{K}|^{p}\,dx}=(p-1)\frac{\displaystyle\int|f_{K}|^{p-2}|\nabla f_{K}|^{2}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int|f_{K}|^{p}\,dx}\asymp K^{2}p^{-1/2}\longrightarrow 0.

Consequently, even on this fixed Fourier band, an estimate of the form

-\langle\Delta f,|f|^{p-2}f\rangle\geq cK^{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}

cannot hold with a positive constant independent of
p
. The example
concerns band-limited fields and does not require
f_{K}
to be the image
of a prescribed block operator.

Proposition 4.6

(
B^{1}_{2,1}
error and its endpoint consequence)
.

Let
u_{0}\in B^{3}_{2,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be divergence free, set

A=\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}
, and let
u^{n}
solve the time-discrete
scheme with
u^{0}=u_{0}
. Choose the common local interval as in
Lemma
4.3
. There is

\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(T,A)>0
such that, for
0<\tau\leq\tau_{0}
,

(4.25)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u^{n}-U(t_{n})\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}\leq C_{T,A}\tau.

Consequently, by blockwise Bernstein,

(4.26)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u^{n}-U(t_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C_{T,A}\tau.

Corollary 4.10

(Approximation in higher Besov norms)
.

Let
r\geq 0
be an integer, let each displayed regularity index
m
be
an integer, and let the initial velocity be divergence free.
On the common local interval, the following estimates hold uniformly for

\nu\in[0,1]

C\tau
.

\ell=r+1
. For a divergence-free transporting field

, the

bounds this by

and

5.1.

Lemma 5.2

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}

Proof.

Lemma 5.3

\displaystyle X_{n+1}+c_{m}\nu\tau\|z^{n+1}\|_{B^{m+2}_{2,1}}

Lemma 5.4

Lemma 5.5

(Tame first-order estimate)

\displaystyle\max_{n\tau\leq T_{*}}\|u_{g}^{n}-U_{g}(n\tau)\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}

Applying (

\displaystyle\|u_{g}^{n+1}\|_{B^{3}_{2,1}}+c\nu\tau\|u_{g}^{n+1}\|_{B^{5}_{2,1}}\leq{}

T
is

\left|\bigl(1+ic\tau_{j}k_{j}\bigr)^{-M_{j}}\right|=\bigl(1+c^{2}\tau_{j}\bigr)^{-T/(2\tau_{j})}\longrightarrow e^{-Tc^{2}/2}<1.

Since the exact multiplier has modulus one, their difference is bounded
below by a positive constant for large
j
. Block
j
therefore gives
the lower bound
C_{0}k_{j}A_{j}=C_{0}a\tau_{j}^{\alpha}/(j+1)^{2}
in

B^{1}_{2,1}
; the normalized
L^{2}
norm of a cosine is

1/\sqrt{2}
. For the inviscid comparison with
\nu_{j}=k_{j}^{-2}
,
the multiplier difference at this frequency has modulus
1-e^{-T}
,
which proves the second lower bound.

For the upper bounds, telescope the
M
one-step factors and use

|(1+ix)^{-1}-e^{-ix}|\leq Cx^{2}
. When
M\tau=T
, this gives

\left|(1+ic\tau k)^{-M}-e^{-ickT}\right|\leq C_{T,c}\min\{1,\tau k^{2}\}.

Likewise the continuous multiplier difference is bounded by

C_{T}\min\{1,\nu k^{2}\}
. For either parameter
q\in\{\tau,\nu\}
,
the corresponding
B^{1}_{2,1}
error is therefore bounded by

C\sum_{\ell\geq 1}k_{\ell}A_{\ell}\min\{1,qk_{\ell}^{2}\}.

For
0<q\leq 1/4
, choose

J=\lfloor\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{2}(1/q)\rfloor\geq 1
and split this sum at
J
.
Since
1-\varepsilon>0
and
1+\varepsilon>0
, the two geometric
tails with their polynomial weights satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 1}k_{\ell}A_{\ell}\min\{1,qk_{\ell}^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq aq\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}\frac{2^{(1-\varepsilon)\ell}}{(\ell+1)^{2}}+a\sum_{\ell>J}\frac{2^{-(1+\varepsilon)\ell}}{(\ell+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon,a}}{(J+1)^{2}}\left(q2^{(1-\varepsilon)J}+2^{-(1+\varepsilon)J}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon,a}\frac{q^{\alpha}}{(1+|\log q|)^{2}}.

For the growing sum, split at
J/2
: the denominators above that index
are comparable to
(J+1)^{2}
, and the exponential decay below it absorbs
the polynomial factor. The decreasing tail is bounded by its first
term. Taking
q=\tau_{j}
and
q=\nu_{j}
proves the upper bounds.

For
\beta>\alpha
, the ratio

2^{2j(\beta-\alpha)}/(j+1)^{2}
diverges, proving the assertion about
larger algebraic exponents.

In the endpoint norm the frequency weight is absent. Since

\sum_{j}k_{j}^{2}A_{j}<\infty
, the same multiplier bounds give

\|u^{M,0}-V(T)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C_{T,c}\tau\sum_{j}k_{j}^{2}A_{j},\qquad\|U^{\nu}(T)-V(T)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq T\nu\sum_{j}k_{j}^{2}A_{j}.

Here
M\tau=T
. The first estimate follows from telescoping and the
second from
1-e^{-y}\leq y
. These endpoint bounds are first order.
∎

For the same shear, the preceding proof gives first-order upper bounds
in
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
. The next proposition identifies their positive
leading constants, showing that both endpoint errors are asymptotically
first order. Its exact formulas are also used in the numerical
comparisons of Section
7.1.4
.

Proposition 5.9

(First-order endpoint asymptotics for the fixed shear)
.

For the datum of Proposition
5.8
, write

k_{j}=2^{j}
and
A_{j}=a2^{-(2+\varepsilon)j}/(j+1)^{2}
, and fix the same
positive time
T
. Let
V
and
U^{\nu}
be the continuous Euler and
Navier–Stokes velocities, respectively, and let
u^{n,0}
be the
inviscid time-discrete velocity. For
h>0
with
M=T/h\in\mathbb{N}
,
define

(5.32)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\rm time}(h)

\displaystyle=\|u^{M,0}-V(T)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}A_{j}\left|(1+ihck_{j})^{-T/h}-e^{-iTck_{j}}\right|,

(5.33)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{\rm visc}(h)

\displaystyle=\|U^{h}(T)-V(T)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}A_{j}(1-e^{-hTk_{j}^{2}}).

The viscosity formula is also defined for every
h>0
. Then

(5.34)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\downarrow 0\\
T/h\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{E_{0}^{\rm time}(h)}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{Tc^{2}}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}k_{j}^{2}A_{j}=:L_{0}^{\rm time}>0,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{E_{0}^{\rm visc}(h)}{h}

\displaystyle=T\sum_{j\geq 1}k_{j}^{2}A_{j}=:L_{0}^{\rm visc}>0.

Both endpoint errors are asymptotically first order for this datum.
These estimates do not determine the optimal uniform endpoint rate
for the full
B^{2+\varepsilon}_{\infty,1}
class.

Proof.

The multiplier formulas from the preceding proof and the fact that

\cos(k_{j}x_{1})
lies in block
j
give the exact sums
(
5.32
)–(
5.33
).
A real cosine with complex amplitude
z
has
L^{\infty}
norm
|z|
.
For each fixed
j
,

-\frac{T}{h}\log(1+ihck_{j})+iTck_{j}=-\frac{Tc^{2}k_{j}^{2}}{2}h+O_{j}(h^{2}),

and hence the time multiplier difference divided by
h
tends in
modulus to
Tc^{2}k_{j}^{2}/2
. Similarly,

(1-e^{-hTk_{j}^{2}})/h\to Tk_{j}^{2}
.
The time and viscosity multiplier differences are bounded by

C_{T,c}\min\{1,hk_{j}^{2}\}
and
\min\{1,hTk_{j}^{2}\}
, respectively.
After division by
h
, they are dominated by
C_{T,c}k_{j}^{2}
. Since

\sum_{j\geq 1}k_{j}^{2}A_{j}=a\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{2^{-\varepsilon j}}{(j+1)^{2}}<\infty\qquad(\varepsilon>0),

dominated convergence for the two nonnegative series proves
(
5.34
). The sums are strictly positive
because
a,T>0
and
c\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 5.10

(Inexact solution of the discrete equations)
.

The preceding estimates concern the exact discrete updates.
Corollary
6.3
extends them to computed
iterates by controlling the accumulated residual of the discrete equations.

6.
Iteration and residuals

Each Fourier step is a finite-dimensional linear problem. Write

Q_{N}=\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}
and work in the divergence-free Fourier space
X_{N}

defined above.

6.1.
Picard iteration

For fixed
u_{N}^{n}
, set
u^{(0)}=u_{N}^{n}
and solve

(6.1)

\frac{u^{(m+1)}-u_{N}^{n}}{\tau}+Q_{N}(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{(m)})=\nu\Delta u^{(m+1)}.

The diffusion solve is diagonal in Fourier space. With sufficient zero
padding, FFTs evaluate products without aliasing in the retained
coefficients. Since the nonlinear term has zero mean, every Picard
iterate satisfies
\widehat{u}^{(m)}(0)=\widehat{u}_{N}^{n}(0)
.

Theorem 6.1

(Picard convergence)
.

Suppose
\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq K
, with
K>0
.
For an absolute constant
C
, consecutive iterates satisfy, for
m\geq 1
,

(6.2)

\|u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}\|_{2}\leq q\|u^{(m)}-u^{(m-1)}\|_{2},

where one may take

(6.3)

q=\begin{cases}\min\{C\tau NK,(C\tau K^{2}/\nu)^{1/2}\},&\nu>0,\\
C\tau NK,&\nu=0.\end{cases}

If
q<1
, the iterates converge to the unique Galerkin update

u_{N}^{n+1}
. In particular, either of the conditions

(6.4)

\tau<c(NK)^{-1},\qquad\text{or}\qquad\nu>0\ \text{and}\ \tau<c\nu K^{-2}

is sufficient. A stopping rule

\|u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\rm it}
gives

(6.5)

\|u^{(m+1)}-u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{\rm it}}{1-q}.

If
K=0
, the initial guess is already the solution.

Proof.

Set
w^{(m+1)}=u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}
. Subtracting successive
equations in (
6.1
) gives

(6.6)

\tau^{-1}w^{(m+1)}+Q_{N}(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla w^{(m)})=\nu\Delta w^{(m+1)}.

The
L^{2}
test and the inverse inequality on
X_{N}
yield

\tau^{-1}\|w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq CKN\|w^{(m)}\|_{2}\|w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}.

For
\nu>0
, integration by parts instead gives

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\|w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K\|w^{(m)}\|_{2}\|\nabla w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla w^{(m+1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{CK^{2}}{\nu}\|w^{(m)}\|_{2}^{2}.

The two estimates give the contraction bounds. The fixed point solves
(
2.5
); uniqueness follows by the
L^{2}
test
of the difference of two solutions. Summing the geometric tail
of the iteration proves (
6.5
).
∎

6.2.
Residual estimates

Let
u_{N}^{n}
be the exact Galerkin trajectory and let

\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\in X_{N}
be computed states with

\widetilde{u}_{N}^{0}=u_{N}^{0}
. Define the residual by

(6.7)

r^{n+1}:=\frac{\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}}{\tau}+Q_{N}(\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n+1})-\nu\Delta\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n+1}.

This normalization includes division of the time-step equation by
\tau
.

Proposition 6.2

(Accumulated residual error)
.

Assume
N\geq 1
,
M\tau\leq T
, and

(6.8)

\max_{0\leq n<M}\|r^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq\delta,\qquad\max_{0\leq n<M}\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{\infty}\leq K_{*}.

Then, for every
\tau>0
,

(6.9)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-u_{N}^{n}\|_{2}\leq C(T,K_{*})\left(\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\|r^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C(T,K_{*})\delta.

Consequently,

(6.10)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\left(\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-u_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{1}_{2,1}}+\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-u_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\right)\leq C(T,K_{*})N\delta.

The constants are independent of
N
and
\nu\geq 0
.
Membership in
X_{N}
is the only regularity assumption on the
computed trajectory.

Proof.

For divergence-free
a\in X_{N}
, define
\mathcal{L}_{a}:X_{N}\to X_{N}
by

\mathcal{L}_{a}z=z+\tau Q_{N}(a\cdot\nabla z)-\nu\tau\Delta z
.
For
z\in X_{N}
, it satisfies

(\mathcal{L}_{a}z,z)=\|z\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\tau\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus

(6.11)

\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq 1.

For the accumulated error
e^{n}=\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-u_{N}^{n}
,
subtraction of the exact steps gives

\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\tau}+Q_{N}(\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla e^{n+1})-\nu\Delta e^{n+1}=r^{n+1}-Q_{N}(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}).

Applying (
6.11
) with

a=\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}
yields

\|e^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq(1+\tau K_{*})\|e^{n}\|_{2}+\tau\|r^{n+1}\|_{2}.

Iteration from
e^{0}=0
gives

\|e^{n}\|_{2}\leq e^{K_{*}T}\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|r^{j+1}\|_{2}\leq e^{K_{*}T}\sqrt{T}\left(\tau\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\|r^{j+1}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which proves (
6.9
).
For
s>0
and
z\in X_{N}
, finite overlap of the dyadic supports
and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

(6.12)

\|z\|_{B^{s}_{2,1}}\leq C_{s}\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq J_{N}}2^{2js}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq J_{N}}\|\Delta_{j}z\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{s}N^{s}\|z\|_{2},\qquad 2^{J_{N}}\leq CN.

Taking
s=1
and using

B^{1}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,1}
proves
(
6.10
).
∎

Corollary 6.3

(Computed endpoint approximation)
.

Let
m=2+\varepsilon
,
0<\varepsilon<1
, and

\alpha=(1+\varepsilon)/2
. Assume that
u_{0}\in B^{m}_{2,1}

is divergence free and fix
A\geq\max\{1,\|u_{0}\|_{B^{m}_{2,1}}\}
.
On the common interval
0<T\leq T_{*}(m,A)
of
Theorem
5.1
, let

\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\in X_{N}
,
0\leq n\leq M
, start from
\Pi_{N}u_{0}
,
where
M\tau\leq T
, and satisfy

\max_{0\leq n<M}\|r^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq\delta
.
There is a threshold
\tau_{\rm comp}>0
, depending only on

m,A,T
, such that for
0<\tau\leq\tau_{\rm comp}
,

N\geq 1
, and
0\leq\nu\leq 1
,

(6.13)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,A,T}(\tau^{\alpha}+N^{-\varepsilon}+N\delta),

(6.14)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}-V(t_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,A,T}(\tau^{\alpha}+N^{-\varepsilon}+\nu^{\alpha}+N\delta).

Both estimates also hold in
B^{1}_{2,1}
.
If in addition
N^{2}\delta\leq B
, with
B
fixed, then

(6.15)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\leq C_{m,A,T}(1+B).

Proof.

Lemma
5.3
bounds the exact Galerkin
trajectory in
B^{m}_{2,1}
by a constant depending only on
m,A
.
Since
m>2
, its Lipschitz norm is bounded by the same quantities.
Proposition
6.2
therefore applies
on the same time-step range as the exact Galerkin estimates.
Adding (
6.10
) to
Corollaries
5.6

and
5.7
proves the error bounds.
For the regularity bound, use (
6.12
)
with
s=2
:

\|\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}\leq\|u_{N}^{n}\|_{B^{2}_{2,1}}+CN^{2}\|e^{n}\|_{2}\leq C_{m,A,T}(1+N^{2}\delta).

∎

Remark 6.4

(Choice of stopping tolerance)
.

An algebraic residual

R^{n+1}=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}}\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n+1}-\widetilde{u}_{N}^{n}
satisfies
R^{n+1}=\tau r^{n+1}
.
Thus a PDE-residual tolerance
\delta
requires

\|R^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq\tau\delta
.
For example,
N\delta\lesssim\tau^{\alpha}+N^{-\varepsilon}

makes the solver term no larger than the temporal and spatial
terms in (
6.13
).
The condition
N^{2}\delta\leq B
is used only for
(
6.15
).
For Picard iteration, the contraction condition must hold in addition
to the time-step restrictions of the error estimate.

We examine temporal, spatial, and vanishing-viscosity errors for

For stable evaluation of the temporal multiplier, set

7.4.
Discussion

Both smooth tests show first-order convergence in time. The
manufactured solution contains several dyadic bands and provides
an exact reference. The unforced calculations compare viscous and
inviscid velocities from the same initial data. The changes caused
by refining the reference solution and the endpoint sampling grid
remain below the reported errors.

At fixed Fourier resolution, the projected rough datum is also
smooth. Its observed first-order time convergence is compatible
with a fractional estimate uniform over the full initial-data class.
For the infinite shear, the error sums include every frequency and
can be evaluated without a numerical reference solution. Modes near
the transition frequency produce the fractional scale, with its
logarithmic factor, in
B^{1}_{2,1}
. Removing the derivative weight
gives first-order convergence in
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
for the same datum.

The nonlinear spatial Cauchy differences depend on both resolutions
in the comparison. For the transported shear, the Fourier-tail error
can be computed directly. Its faster decay reflects the absence of
mode interactions and does not determine the optimal spatial rate
for nonlinear flow. In both sets of convergence tests, the implicit
solver residuals are well below the displayed errors. Their effect
is bounded by
CN\delta
as in
Proposition
6.2
. The same residual
estimate applies to the manufactured problem because its prescribed
force cancels when the exact and computed discrete equations are subtracted.

The flow comparisons concern finite-time evolution. For long-time
approximation, Hill and Süli studied the Navier–Stokes global
attractor
[
19
]
, and Wang proved uniform-in-time bounds and
convergence of stationary statistics for a second-order method on
a periodic domain
[
33
]
. Convergence of attractors or
stationary statistics is not covered by the local estimates proved here.

8.
Conclusion

The error estimates are uniform in viscosity on a local interval
fixed by the initial data. The consistency argument uses
viscosity-weighted higher derivatives, while dyadic
L^{2}
energy
estimates give the endpoint bound through

B^{1}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,1}
.
An
L^{2}
tolerance
\delta
for the PDE residual, with the discrete
equation divided by
\tau
, adds
CN\delta
to the endpoint error.

The optimal spatial and fractional rates in
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
remain
open for general nonlinear flow. The fixed-shear calculation distinguishes
the fractional scale in

B^{1}_{2,1}
from the first-order endpoint error of that example.
The limiting initial class
B^{2}_{2,1}
requires a separate
argument. The bounded-domain estimates assume uniform regularity
of the continuous family and, for finite elements, the approximation
properties stated in Appendix
B
.

Appendix A
No-slip boundaries

For projection methods, the treatment of pressure at the boundary
affects the velocity error. E and Liu analyzed the resulting
numerical boundary layers for time-discrete projection
methods
[
10
]
and fully discrete staggered-grid
methods
[
11
]
. Hou and Wetton proved second-order convergence
for a projection scheme with boundaries
[
21
]
, and Shirokoff
and Rosales obtained high accuracy near irregular no-slip boundaries
through a pressure-Poisson formulation
[
32
]
. The step below
is posed directly in the divergence-free space.

The estimate below requires uniform regularity of the no-slip
solutions; boundary layers may invalidate these bounds as

\nu\downarrow 0
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and set

V:=\{z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}:\nabla\cdot z=0\}.

We write
(\cdot,\cdot)
for the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product and

b(a,w,z):=\int_{\Omega}(a\cdot\nabla w)\cdot z\,dx.

If
a\in V
, then

(A.1)

b(a,z,z)=0\qquad(z\in V).

For
0<\nu\leq 1
, the no-slip semi-implicit step is: given
u^{n}\in V
,
find
u^{n+1}\in V
such that

(A.2)

\left(\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau},z\right)+b(u^{n},u^{n+1},z)+\nu(\nabla u^{n+1},\nabla z)=0\qquad(z\in V).

By (
A.1
), the bilinear form is coercive for each fixed

\nu>0
. The Lax–Milgram theorem gives a unique weak solution,
and the de Rham argument recovers the pressure up to an additive constant.

Theorem A.1

(Time error under uniform regularity)
.

For every
0<\nu\leq 1
, let
U^{\nu}
be a strong solution of the no-slip
Navier–Stokes equations in the weak form

(A.3)

(\partial_{t}U^{\nu},z)+b(U^{\nu},U^{\nu},z)+\nu(\nabla U^{\nu},\nabla z)=0\qquad(z\in V)

on
[0,T]
. Assume that

(A.4)

\sup_{0<\nu\leq 1}\left(\|\nabla U^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\partial_{tt}U^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\right)\leq K.

Let
u^{n}
be defined by (
A.2
), with

u^{0}=U^{\nu}(0)
and
t_{n}=n\tau
, where
M\tau=T
. There is

\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(K)>0
, independent of
\nu
, such that, if

0<\tau\leq\tau_{0}
, then

(A.5)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(T,K)\tau.

The constant in (
A.5
) is independent of

\nu\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
\partial_{t}U^{\nu}\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2})
, we use the continuous
L^{2}

representatives of
U^{\nu}
and
\partial_{t}U^{\nu}
at the time nodes.
The no-slip condition and uniform spatial Lipschitz bound imply, by
weak compactness along almost-everywhere time sequences, that

U^{\nu}(t)\in V
and
\|\nabla U^{\nu}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq K
for
every
t\in[0,T]
, with weak continuity in
H^{1}
.
Strong
L^{2}
continuity and the resulting uniform
L^{\infty}
bound
make
b(U^{\nu},U^{\nu},z)
continuous in time for each
z\in V
:
one may use
b(U^{\nu},U^{\nu},z)=-(U^{\nu}\otimes U^{\nu},\nabla z)
.
Each term in (
A.3
) therefore extends
continuously to every node.
Put
U^{n}=U^{\nu}(t_{n})
and define the consistency defect

(A.6)

\eta^{n+1}:=\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau}-\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})+(U^{n}-U^{n+1})\cdot\nabla U^{n+1}.

Equation (
A.3
), evaluated at
t_{n+1}
,
shows that

(A.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau},z\right)+b(U^{n},U^{n+1},z)+\nu(\nabla U^{n+1},\nabla z)=(\eta^{n+1},z).

The two pieces of (
A.6
) satisfy

(A.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau}-\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t_{n+1})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau}{3}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{tt}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\|(U^{n}-U^{n+1})\cdot\nabla U^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}\tau\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt.

Consequently,

(A.10)

\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\|\eta^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C(K^{2}+K^{4})\tau^{2}.

Let
e^{n}=u^{n}-U^{n}
. Subtracting
(
A.7
) from
(
A.2
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e^{n+1}-e^{n}}{\tau},z\right)+b(u^{n},e^{n+1},z)+b(e^{n},U^{n+1},z)+\nu(\nabla e^{n+1},\nabla z)=-(\eta^{n+1},z).

Taking
z=e^{n+1}
and using (
A.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|e^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|e^{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|e^{n+1}-e^{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\nu\|\nabla e^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\|e^{n}\|_{2}\|e^{n+1}\|_{2}+\|\eta^{n+1}\|_{2}\|e^{n+1}\|_{2}.

Young’s inequality implies

(A.11)

\bigl(1-(K+1)\tau\bigr)\|e^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+K\tau)\|e^{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\tau\|\eta^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Choose
\tau_{0}
so that
(K+1)\tau_{0}\leq 1/2
.
Discrete Gronwall,
e^{0}=0
, and (
A.10
) yield
(
A.5
).
∎

The estimate is conditional on (
A.4
)
and does not establish a no-slip inviscid limit. Higher-order
estimates require additional boundary regularity and compatibility;
on Lipschitz domains, the domains of powers of the Stokes operator
must be treated separately
[
12
]
.

Appendix B
Finite elements

We use the domain
\Omega
, space
V
, and trilinear form
b
of
Appendix
A
.
Let
V_{h}\subset V
be a conforming, pointwise divergence-free finite
element space. Consider

(B.1)

\left(\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}}{\tau},v_{h}\right)+b(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n+1},v_{h})+\nu(\nabla u_{h}^{n+1},\nabla v_{h})=0\qquad(v_{h}\in V_{h}).

The Ritz comparison below requires approximation and Lipschitz
bounds for the chosen space and mesh family.

Proposition B.1

(Finite element error)
.

Let
0<h\leq 1
, let
r\geq 2
, and let
V_{h}\subset V
be conforming and
pointwise divergence free. Define
R_{h}:V\to V_{h}
by

(B.2)

(\nabla R_{h}z,\nabla v_{h})=(\nabla z,\nabla v_{h})\qquad(v_{h}\in V_{h}).

Assume that, uniformly in
h
,

(B.3)

\|R_{h}z-z\|_{2}+h\|\nabla(R_{h}z-z)\|_{2}\leq C_{R}h^{r}\|z\|_{H^{r}}\qquad(z\in V\cap H^{r}(\Omega)^{2}).

Let
t_{n}=n\tau
with
M\tau=T
, and let
U^{\nu}
be strong no-slip
solutions of (
A.3
) on
[0,T]
.
Suppose that, uniformly for
0<\nu\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\|U^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{r})}+\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{r})}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{tt}U^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\nabla U^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq K_{U},

(B.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla R_{h}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K_{R}.

The constants
C_{R},K_{U},K_{R}
are assumed independent of
h
and
\nu
.
If
u_{h}^{0}=R_{h}U^{\nu}(0)
, then the finite-element scheme
(
B.1
) satisfies, for
\tau(K_{R}+1)\leq 1/2
,

(B.6)

\max_{0\leq n\leq M}\|u_{h}^{n}-U^{\nu}(t_{n})\|_{2}\leq C(T,C_{R},K_{U},K_{R})(\tau+h^{r-1}),

where the constant is independent of
\nu\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Write
U^{n}=U^{\nu}(t_{n})
, set
W_{h}^{n}=R_{h}U^{n}
, and put

\zeta^{n}=W_{h}^{n}-U^{n}
. The projection estimate gives

\|\zeta^{n}\|_{2}\leq C_{R}h^{r}K_{U}
, and
(
B.5
) bounds
\|\nabla W_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n+1}(v_{h})
denote the left-hand side of
(
B.1
) evaluated at
W_{h}^{n},W_{h}^{n+1}
. Subtracting
(
A.7
) and using the linearity of
R_{h}

gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n+1}(v_{h})={}

\displaystyle(\eta^{n+1},v_{h})+\bigl((R_{h}-I)D_{\tau}U^{n+1},v_{h}\bigr)

(B.7)

\displaystyle+b(W_{h}^{n},\zeta^{n+1},v_{h})+b(\zeta^{n},U^{n+1},v_{h}),

where
D_{\tau}U^{n+1}=(U^{n+1}-U^{n})/\tau
. Ritz orthogonality
(
B.2
) eliminates the viscous projection term:

\nu(\nabla\zeta^{n+1},\nabla v_{h})=0\qquad(v_{h}\in V_{h}).

Since
W_{h}^{n}
has zero trace,

\|W_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\nabla W_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Consequently

(B.8)

|\mathcal{R}_{n+1}(v_{h})|\leq\rho_{n+1}\|v_{h}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\rho_{n+1}:={}

\displaystyle\|\eta^{n+1}\|_{2}+\|(R_{h}-I)D_{\tau}U^{n+1}\|_{2}

(B.9)

\displaystyle+C_{\Omega}K_{R}\|\nabla\zeta^{n+1}\|_{2}+\|\nabla U^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\zeta^{n}\|_{2}.

The projection estimate and Cauchy–Schwarz in time show that

(B.10)

\displaystyle\|(R_{h}-I)D_{\tau}U^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{R}^{2}h^{2r}}{\tau}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\partial_{t}U^{\nu}(t)\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\,dt,

(B.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\zeta^{n+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}h^{r-1}\|U^{n+1}\|_{H^{r}},\qquad\|\zeta^{n}\|_{2}\leq C_{R}h^{r}\|U^{n}\|_{H^{r}}.

Combining these estimates with (
A.10
) gives

(B.12)

\tau\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\rho_{n+1}^{2}\leq C(T,C_{R},K_{U},K_{R})\bigl(\tau^{2}+h^{2(r-1)}\bigr).

Set
\theta^{n}=u_{h}^{n}-W_{h}^{n}
. Subtracting the residual equation from
(
B.1
) and testing with
\theta^{n+1}
, we use

b(u_{h}^{n},\theta^{n+1},\theta^{n+1})=0
by (
A.1
).
The remaining transport term is bounded by

K_{R}\|\theta^{n}\|_{2}\|\theta^{n+1}\|_{2}
. As in
(
A.11
),

\bigl(1-(K_{R}+1)\tau\bigr)\|\theta^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+K_{R}\tau)\|\theta^{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\tau\rho_{n+1}^{2}.

Since
\theta^{0}=0
, discrete Gronwall and
(
B.12
) give

\max_{n}\|\theta^{n}\|_{2}\leq C(\tau+h^{r-1})
.
Adding
\|\zeta^{n}\|_{2}\leq C_{R}h^{r}K_{U}
proves
(
B.6
).
∎

Remark B.2

(Projected Lipschitz bound)
.

A stable divergence-preserving interpolant gives a sufficient condition
for (
B.5
). On a quasi-uniform two-dimensional
mesh, suppose there is a divergence-preserving
I_{h}:V\cap H^{r}\to V_{h}

such that

\|\nabla I_{h}z\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{I}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad\|\nabla(z-I_{h}z)\|_{2}\leq C_{I}h^{r-1}\|z\|_{H^{r}}.

Ritz best approximation and the finite-element inverse inequality give

\displaystyle\|\nabla R_{h}z\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla I_{h}z\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{\rm inv}h^{-1}\|\nabla(R_{h}z-I_{h}z)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{I}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{\infty}}+C(C_{I},C_{\rm inv})h^{r-2}\|z\|_{H^{r}}.

Since
r\geq 2
and
h\leq 1
, the bound is uniform. These interpolation
properties and the Ritz approximation estimate
(
B.3
) must be verified for the chosen
element and mesh family; they do not follow from a Fortin operator
and discrete inf–sup stability alone.

Appendix C
H^{1}
dissipation

For the periodic Fourier scheme, let

Q_{N}:=\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}\Pi_{N}
, where
\Pi_{N}
is the square
cutoff
|k|_{\infty}\leq N
. Let

u_{N}^{n},u_{N}^{n+1}\in X_{N}
be divergence-free trigonometric polynomials
with this Fourier support satisfying

(C.1)

\frac{u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n}}{\tau}+Q_{N}(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{T}^{2}.

Proposition C.1

(
H^{1}
dissipation)
.

Assume

(C.2)

\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}\leq K.

There is an absolute
c>0
such that, whenever

(C.3)

\tau K^{2}\leq c\nu,

one has

(C.4)

\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n})\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\tau\|\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u_{N}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

A uniform
H^{s}
bound with
s>2
supplies the constant in
(
C.2
); no
H^{3}
estimate is needed.

Proof.

Let
d^{n+1}=u_{N}^{n+1}-u_{N}^{n}
. Testing
(
C.1
) with
-\tau\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla u_{N}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla d^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\nu\tau\|\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}

(C.5)

\displaystyle\qquad=\tau(u_{N}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1},\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}).

In two dimensions,

(C.6)

(w\cdot\nabla w,\Delta w)=0

for every smooth periodic divergence-free field
w
. Since

u_{N}^{n}=u_{N}^{n+1}-d^{n+1}
, the right-hand side of
(
C.5
) is

-\tau(d^{n+1}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1},\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}).

Integration by parts, using
\nabla\cdot d^{n+1}=0
, and the
two-dimensional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality give

\displaystyle|(d^{n+1}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1},\Delta u_{N}^{n+1})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla d^{n+1}\|_{2}\|\nabla u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CK\|\nabla d^{n+1}\|_{2}\|\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}.

Consequently,

\tau|(d^{n+1}\cdot\nabla u_{N}^{n+1},\Delta u_{N}^{n+1})|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla d^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+C\tau^{2}K^{2}\|\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Under (
C.3
), the last term is bounded by

\frac{1}{2}\nu\tau\|\Delta u_{N}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}
. Substitution into
(
C.5
), followed by multiplication by two,
proves (
C.4
).
∎

Remark C.2
.

When
K
is supplied by the local Sobolev bound, the condition is

\tau\leq c\nu/K^{2}
. This additional
H^{1}
monotonicity thus
requires positive viscosity unless the solution is spatially constant.
The kinetic-energy identity of Proposition
2.1

holds without this restriction.

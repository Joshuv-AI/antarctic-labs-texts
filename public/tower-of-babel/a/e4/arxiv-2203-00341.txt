Christensen–Evans theorem and extensions of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups
Melchior Wirth

Abstract
. In this note we prove a refined version of the Christensen–Evans theorem for generators of uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. We use this result to show the existence of GNS-symmetric extensions of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. In particular, this implies that the generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra can be written in the form specified by Alicki’s theorem.



1.
Introduction

Quantum Markov semigroups were originally introduced in mathematical physics to model the time evolution of certain open quantum systems and have since developed into an object of broad mathematical interest in pure and applied disciplines. Particular research effort has been spent on understanding the structure of their generators. For quantum Markov semigroups acting on matrix algebras, the results by Lindblad
[
Lin76
]
and Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan
[
GKS76
]
give a full description of the generators, which are now commonly called Lindblad operators. In the broader context of uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroups on von Neumann algebras, Christensen and Evans
[
CE79
]
showed that the generator is of the form

\mathcal{L}(x)=k^{\ast}x+xk-\Phi(x)

with a completely positive map
\Phi
.

In many situations however, the suitable category is not that of von Neumann algebras, but that of
W^{\ast}
-probability spaces, that is, von Neumann algebras with a fixed normal faithful state. In this situation, one is often interested in quantum Markov semigroups that satisfy a symmetry condition with respect to the reference state, such as GNS- or KMS-symmetry. Again, these conditions have a physical motivation in the description of open quantum systems whose environment is in thermal equilibrium. The structure of the generators of GNS- and KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras (and more generally the full algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space) has been intensively studied, too. In particular, Alicki’s theorem
[
Ali76
]
gives a full description of the generators of a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras in similar form to the Lindblad–Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan theorem. However, an analog of the Christensen–Evans theorem for GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on arbitrary von Neumann algebras seems to be missing so far.

Recent years have seen a renewed interest in the structure of the generators of quantum Markov semigroups in the study of noncommutative functional inequalities with applications in quantum information. In particular, Alicki’s theorem has been instrumental in the definition of a noncommutative analog of the
2
-Wasserstein distance by Carlen and Maas
[
CM17
]
; and the differential calculus developed there has found applications beyond
[
Bar17
,
GR21
]
. But to use Alicki’s theorem for a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on a finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra, it has to be assumed that it has a GNS-symmetric extensions to the full matrix algebra. While this can be shown for many examples of interest, the question whether this is always the case has been left open.

In this article we show that the generator of a uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup has a Christensen–Evans form in which the operator
k
and the completely positive map
\Phi
are compatible with GNS-symmetry, namely
k
is in the centralizer of the state (and positive) and
\Phi
is GNS-symmetric (
Theorem

4.16
).

Our proof relies on the theory of von Neumann bimodules and correspondences. We show that if a uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroup is GNS-symmetric, the associated GNS bimodule carries some extra structure (
Proposition

4.4
). This can be seen as a first step in extending the first-order differential calculus developed by Cipriani and Sauvageot
[
CS03
]
to quantum Markov semigroups that are GNS-symmetric with respect to a non-tracial state (or weight). A crucial step in the proof of the original Christensen–Evans theorem is that a derivation associated with the quantum Markov semigroup is inner. Likewise it will be central in our proof that in the case of a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup, the vector representing this derivation can be chosen compatible with the extra structure mentioned above.

As an application we show that a uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on a von Neumann subalgebra can be extended to a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on the full von Neumann algebra provided the subalgebra is the range of a normal conditional expectation (
Theorem

5.1
). In particular, Alicki’s theorem can be applied to any GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on a unital
\ast
-subalgebra of a matrix algebra (
Corollary

5.4
). In the appendix we give a short proof of the fact (originally due to Kadison and Ringrose
[
KR71
,
KR71a
]
) that every approximately finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra with separable predual is amenable, which might be of independent interest.

2.
GNS-symmetric maps

In this section we recall some definitions and prove two elementary facts about GNS-symmetric completely positive maps.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. We write
(\pi_{\varphi},L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\Omega_{\varphi})
for the cyclic representation associated with
\varphi
and
\sigma^{\varphi}
for the modular group. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
denote the set of analytic elements for
\sigma^{\varphi}
, that is, the set of all
x\in\mathcal{M}
for which
t\mapsto\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)
has an analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
. This continuation is denoted by
z\mapsto\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x)
. Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
is a weak
∗
dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
.

A bounded linear operator
P\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is called
GNS-symmetric
with respect to
\varphi
(or simply
\varphi
-symmetric
) if

\varphi(P(x)^{\ast}y)=\varphi(x^{\ast}P(y))

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
.

The main object of interest of this article are GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups, that is, weakly continuous semigroups of GNS symmetric unital completely positive maps on
\mathcal{M}
. It is customary to additionally assume that the individual maps are normal and commute with the modular group, but we will show that these two requirements are automatically satisfied.

For normality this is certainly known to experts, but as we could not find a precise reference, we give the short proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Every GNS-symmetric unital completely positive map is normal.

Proof.

Let
P
be a
\varphi
-symmetric unital completely positive map. By
[
Bla06
, Proposition III.2.2.2]
it suffices to show that
P
is strongly continuous on the unit ball of
\mathcal{M}
. By
[
Tak02
, Proposition III.5.3]
, on the unit ball the strong topology coincides with the topology induced by the norm

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty),\,\lVert x\rVert_{\varphi}=\varphi(x^{\ast}x)^{1/2}.

By the Kadison–Schwarz inequality and
\varphi
-symmetry,

\lVert P(x)\rVert_{\varphi}=\varphi(P(x)^{\ast}P(x))^{1/2}\leq\varphi(P(x^{\ast}x))^{1/2}=\varphi(x^{\ast}x)^{1/2}=\lVert x\rVert_{\varphi}.

Hence
P
is continuous with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\varphi}
.
∎

That GNS-symmetric unital completely positive maps commute with the modular group is also known for matrix algebras
[
Ali76
, Lemma 2]
,
[
CM17
, Lemma 2.5]
. Our proof in the infinite-dimensional case follows the same strategy, just that some care has to be taken of the domains.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
P
is a
\varphi
-symmetric unital completely positive map, then
P
commutes with the modular group
\sigma^{\varphi}
.

Proof.

As shown in the proof of the previous lemma, the map

\mathcal{M}\Omega_{\varphi}\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,x\Omega_{\varphi}\mapsto P(x)\Omega_{\varphi}

extends to a bounded linear operator
\tilde{P}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, and
\varphi
-symmetry of
P
implies that
\tilde{P}
is symmetric.

We consider the left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{M}\Omega_{\varphi}
as in
[
Tak03
, Example VI.1.3]
with modular operator
\Delta
, modular conjugation
J
and
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
. Since
P
is hermitian,
\tilde{P}
commutes with
S
.

Let

\mathfrak{A}_{0}=\left\{\xi\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D(\Delta^{n}):\Delta^{n}\xi\in\mathfrak{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

be the associated Tomita algebra.

In the following we will freely use the relations between
S
,
J
and
\Delta
as detailed in
[
Tak03
, Lemma VI.1.5]
. For
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{P}\Delta\xi,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta\xi,\tilde{P}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J\tilde{P}\eta,J\Delta\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{1/2}S\tilde{P}\eta,S\Delta^{1/2}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S\eta,\tilde{P}\Delta^{1/2}S\Delta^{1/2}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S\eta,S\tilde{P}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle JS\tilde{P}\xi,JS\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{1/2}\tilde{P}\xi,\Delta^{1/2}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{P}\xi,\Delta\eta\rangle.

Since
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
is a core for
\Delta
by
[
Tak03
, Theorem VI.2.2 (ii)]
, it follows that
\tilde{P}\xi\in D(\Delta)
and
\Delta\tilde{P}\xi=\tilde{P}\Delta\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
. Again since
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
is a core, this implies
\Delta\tilde{P}=\tilde{P}\Delta
and thus
\Delta^{it}\tilde{P}=\tilde{P}\Delta^{it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(P(x))\Omega_{\varphi}=\Delta^{it}(P(x)\Omega_{\varphi})=\Delta^{it}\tilde{P}(x\Omega_{\varphi})=\tilde{P}\Delta^{it}(x\Omega_{\varphi})=P(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))\Omega_{\varphi}.

As
\Omega_{\varphi}
is a separating vector for
\mathcal{M}
, this proves the claim.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Usually the extension of
P
from
\mathcal{M}
to the GNS Hilbert space
H
is not defined via the embedding
x\mapsto x\Omega_{\varphi}
as in this proof, but via the symmetric embedding
x\mapsto\Delta^{1/4}(x\Omega_{\varphi})
. However, commutation with the modular group as proven here implies that both extensions actually coincide.

3.
Von Neumann modules and correspondences

Throughout this article we use the language of von Neumann modules and correspondences, which we briefly recall now. As a detailed reference for von Neumann modules we refer to
[
Ske01
]
, while the necessary results on correspondences can be found in
[
Tak03
, Section IX.3]
.

Von Neumann modules are noncommutative analogs of
L^{\infty}
sections of a vector bundle, while correspondences are analogs of
L^{2}
sections. In the presence of a normal faithful state on the underlying von Neumann algebra, both carry the same information and one can go back and forth between these two notions (see
Proposition

3.1
), which will be useful later on.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra. A
pre-
C^{\ast}

A
-module
is a right
A
-module
F
with a sesquilinear map
(\cdot\mid\cdot)\colon F\times F\to A
such that

•

(\xi|\eta a)=(\xi|\eta)a
for all
\xi,\eta\in F
,
a\in A
,

•

(\xi|\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi\in F
,

•

(\xi|\xi)=0
implies
\xi=0
.

A
C^{\ast}

A
-module
is a pre-
C^{\ast}

A
-module
F
that is complete in the norm

\lVert\xi\rVert_{F}=\lVert(\xi|\xi)\rVert^{1/2}.

A
C^{\ast}
-module over
\mathbb{C}
is nothing but a Hilbert space.

A bounded linear operator
T
on a
C^{\ast}
-module
F
is called
adjointable
if there exists a bounded linear operator
T^{\ast}
on
F
such that

(T\xi|\eta)=(\xi|T^{\ast}\eta)

for all
\xi,\eta\in F
. Note that all adjointable operators are right module maps, that is,
T(\xi a)=(T\xi)a
for all
a\in A
,
\xi\in F
.

Let
A
,
B
and
C
be unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras. A
C^{\ast}

A
-
B
-module
is a
C^{\ast}
-bimodule together with an action of
A
by adjointable operators. In particular, a
C^{\ast}

\mathbb{C}
-
A
-bimodule is the same as a
C^{\ast}

A
-module and a
C^{\ast}

A
-
\mathbb{C}
-bimodule the same as a representation of
A
on a Hilbert space. In the case
A=B
we simply speak of
C^{\ast}

A
-bimodules.

The
tensor product

F\bar{\odot}G
of a
C^{\ast}

A
-
B
-module
F
and a
C^{\ast}

B
-
C
-module is the
C^{\ast}

A
-
C
-module obtained from the algebraic tensor product
F\otimes G
after separation and completion with respect to the
C
-valued inner product given by

(\xi\otimes\eta|\xi^{\prime}\otimes\eta^{\prime})=(\eta|(\xi|\xi^{\prime})\eta^{\prime})

and the actions given by

a(\xi\otimes\eta)=a\xi\otimes\eta,\,(\xi\otimes\eta)c=\xi\otimes\eta c.

If
A
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra of bounded operators on
H
and
F
is a
C^{\ast}

A
-module, we can embed
F
into
B(H,F\bar{\odot}H)
by the action

H\to F\bar{\odot}H,\,\zeta\mapsto\xi\otimes\zeta

for
\xi\in F
. If we refer to the strong topology on a
C^{\ast}
-module in the following, we always mean the strong topology in this embedding, where
H
is clear from the context (usually the standard form of a von Neumann algebra).

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra on
H
. A
von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-module
is a
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-module
F
that is strongly closed in
B(H,F\bar{\odot}H)
. The adjointable operators on a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-module form a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}(F)
.

If
\mathcal{N}
is another von Neumann algebra on
K
, then a
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-module is a
von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-module
if it is a von Neumann
\mathcal{N}
-module and the left action of
\mathcal{M}
on
F\bar{\odot}K
is normal.

Assume that
\psi
is a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{N}
. If
H=L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
, the Hilbert space
F\bar{\odot}H
carries not only a left action of
\mathcal{N}
, but also a right action given by

(\xi\otimes\eta)x=\xi\otimes\eta x

for
\xi\in F
,
\eta\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
and
x\in\mathcal{M}
. This makes
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
into an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondence
in the sense of Connes, that is, a Hilbert space with commuting representations of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\circ}
, the opposite
\ast
-algebra of
\mathcal{N}
.

Conversely, one can recover
F
from
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
. To prove this fact, we need some more terminology. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence. A vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is called
left-bounded
if there exists a constant
C(\xi)>0
such that

\lVert\xi y\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C(\xi)\lVert\Omega_{\psi}y\rVert_{2}

for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
. The set of all left-bounded vectors in denoted by
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
. By definition, for every
\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
the map

\Omega_{\psi}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H},\,\Omega_{\psi}x\mapsto\xi x

extends to a bounded right module map
L(\xi)
from
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
to
\mathcal{H}
. In particular, if
\xi,\eta\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
, then
L(\xi)^{\ast}L(\eta)
commutes with the right action of
\mathcal{N}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
, which implies
L(\xi)^{\ast}L(\eta)\in\mathcal{N}
. Conversely, if
T\colon L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)\to\mathcal{H}
is a bounded right module map, then it is not hard to see that
T\Omega_{\psi}
is left-bounded and
T=L(T\Omega_{\psi})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map

F\to F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi),\,\xi\mapsto\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi}

is a bijection onto
L_{\infty}((F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi))_{\mathcal{M}},\psi)
and

L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})^{\ast}L(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\psi})=(\xi|\zeta)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in F
.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondence, then
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
endowed with the actions

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\times L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\to L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi),\,(x,\xi)\mapsto x\xi,

\displaystyle L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\times\mathcal{N}\to L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi),\,(\xi,y)\mapsto L(\xi)y\Omega_{\psi}

and the
\mathcal{N}
-valued inner product
(\xi|\eta)=L(\xi)^{\ast}L(\eta)
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-module, and the map

L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\otimes\Omega_{\psi}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{H},\,\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi}x\mapsto\xi x

extends to a unitary from
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
to
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

If
y\in\mathcal{N}
and
\xi\in F
, then

\lVert(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})y\rVert^{2}=\langle\Omega_{\psi}y,(\xi|\xi)\Omega_{\psi}y\rangle_{2}\leq\lVert(\xi|\xi)\rVert\lVert\Omega_{\psi}y\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Thus
\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
is left-bounded. The equality
L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})^{\ast}L(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\psi})=(\xi|\zeta)
for
\xi,\zeta\in F
is straightforward.

To prove that the map
\xi\mapsto\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi}
is a surjection onto
L_{\infty}((F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi))_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
, let
\eta\in L_{\infty}((F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi))_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
and define the map

\Phi\colon F\to\mathcal{N},\,\xi\mapsto L(\eta)^{\ast}L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})

We have

\displaystyle\Phi(\xi x)(\Omega_{\psi}y)

\displaystyle=L(\eta)^{\ast}(\xi x\otimes\Omega_{\psi}y)

\displaystyle=L(\eta)^{\ast}(\xi\otimes x\Omega_{\psi}y)

\displaystyle=L(\eta)^{\ast}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi}\sigma^{\psi}_{i/2}(x)y)

\displaystyle=L(\eta)^{\ast}L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})\Omega_{\psi}\sigma^{\psi}_{i/2}(x)y

\displaystyle=\Phi(\xi)(x\Omega_{\psi}y)

for all
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\sigma^{\psi}}
,
y\in\mathcal{N}
and
\xi\in F
. By approximation, this implies that
\Phi
is a right module map.

Since von Neumann modules are self-dual
[
Ske01
, Theorem 3.2.11]
, there exists
\zeta\in F
such that

L(\eta)^{\ast}L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=(\zeta|\xi)=L(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

for all
\xi\in F
. Since the linear hull of elements of the form
L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\psi})x
with
\xi\in F
and
y\in\mathcal{N}
is dense in
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
, we have
L(\eta)=L(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\psi})
, which implies
\eta=\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\psi}
by applying both operators to
\Omega_{\psi}
.

For the converse, first note that whenever
\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
and
x,y\in\mathcal{N}
, then

\lVert(L(\xi)x\Omega_{\psi})y\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\lVert L(\xi)(x\Omega_{\psi}y)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\lVert L(\xi)\rVert\lVert x\rVert\lVert\Omega_{\psi}y\rVert_{2},

so that
L(\xi)x\Omega_{\psi}
is left-bounded and thus the right action is well-defined. It is not difficult to check that this makes
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
a
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-module and that
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)\cong\mathcal{H}
under the map above.

With this identification, the canonical embedding

L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\hookrightarrow B(L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi),L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi))

is just the map
\xi\mapsto L(\xi)
. As discussed above,
\{L(\xi)\mid\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)\}
is the set of all bounded right module maps from
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
to
\mathcal{H}
, which is clearly strongly closed in
B(L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi),\mathcal{H})
. Normality of the actions is easy to check, so that
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

As mentioned above, left-bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}
are in one-to-one correspondence with right module maps from
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
to
\mathcal{H}
. That these form a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-module has already been observed in
[
Sch02
, Section 1.6]
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondence and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is an
\mathcal{N}
-
\mathcal{R}
-correspondences, their
relative tensor product
or
Connes fusion tensor product

\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{H}_{2}
is the
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{R}
-correspondence obtained from
L_{\infty}((\mathcal{H}_{1})_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
after separation and completion with respect to

B(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})=\langle\eta_{1},L(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L(\xi_{2})\eta_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

The following identification is a straightforward consequence of the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-bimodules, then the map

\displaystyle F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\to(F_{1}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))\otimes_{\varphi}(F_{2}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)),

\displaystyle\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\mapsto(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})\otimes_{\varphi}(\eta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

extends to a unitary from
(F_{1}\bar{\odot}F_{2})\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
onto
(F_{1}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))\otimes_{\varphi}(F_{2}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))
.

Besides left-bounded vectors, there is the dual notion of right-bounded vectors. An element
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is called
right-bounded
if there exists a constant
C(\xi)>0
such that

\lVert x\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C(\xi)\lVert x\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert_{2}.

The set of all right-bounded vectors is denoted by
L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
. By definition, for every
\xi\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
the map

\mathcal{M}\Omega_{\varphi}\to\mathcal{H},\,x\Omega_{\varphi}\mapsto x\xi

extends to a bounded left module map
R(\xi)
from
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
to
\mathcal{H}
. Analogously to the case of left-bounded vectors, this implies
R(\xi)^{\ast}R(\eta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
for
\xi,\eta\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondences. By
[
Tak03
, Proposition IX.3.15]
, the semi-inner product form

B^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{2},\varphi)\to\mathbb{C},\,B^{\prime}(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})=\langle\xi_{1}\cdot JR(\eta_{1})^{\ast}R(\eta_{2})J,\xi_{2}\rangle

coincides with
B
on
L_{\infty}((\mathcal{H}_{1})_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)\otimes L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{2},\varphi)
. Thus the
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence obtained from
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{2},\varphi)
after separation and completion with respect to
B^{\prime}
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

4.
Christensen–Evans theorem for GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups

In this section we prove the first main result of this article, a refined version of the Christensen–Evans theorem
[
CE79
, Theorem 3.1]
for the generators of uniformly continuous GNS–symmetric quantum Markov semigroups (
Theorem

4.16
). The Christensen–Evans theorem asserts that the generator
\mathcal{L}
of a uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroup on a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is of the form

\mathcal{L}(x)=k^{\ast}x+xk-\Phi(x)

with
k\in\mathcal{M}
and a completely positive map
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
such that
\Phi(1)=k+k^{\ast}
.

We will show that if the given quantum Markov semigroup is
\varphi
-symmetric, then
k
can be chosen positive and in the centralizer of
\varphi
and
\Phi
can be chosen
\varphi
-symmetric.

Let us first recall the definition of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. A
quantum Markov semigroup
is a family
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of normal unital completely positive maps on
\mathcal{M}
such that

(i)

P_{0}=I
,

(ii)

P_{s}P_{t}=P_{s+t}
for
s,t\geq 0
,

(iii)

P_{t}(x)\to x
weakly as
t\searrow 0
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

A quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
is called
GNS-symmetric
if the individual maps
P_{t}
are GNS-symmetric for all
t\geq 0
. Note that in this case normality of the maps
P_{t}
is automatic by
Lemma

2.1
and furthermore every
P_{t}
commutes with the modular group
\sigma^{\varphi}
by
Proposition

2.2
.

If the convergence in (iii) is in norm for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
, then the quantum Markov semigroup is called
uniformly continuous
. In this case, the generator
\mathcal{L}
defined by

\mathcal{L}(x)=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{1}{t}(x-P_{t}(x))

is a normal bounded operator on
\mathcal{M}
. It is known that a normal bounded linear map
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{M}
generates a quantum Markov semigroup if and only if
\mathcal{L}(1)=0
and
\mathcal{L}
is conditionally negative definite, that is,

\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}x_{j}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(a_{j}^{\ast}a_{k})x_{k}\leq 0

whenever
a_{1},\dots,a_{n},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\sum_{j}a_{j}x_{j}=0
. Moreover, it is easy to see that the quantum Markov semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}
is GNS-symmetric if and only if
\mathcal{L}
is GNS-symmetric. The associated
carré du champ
is the
\mathcal{M}
-valued bilinear map

\Gamma\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M},\,\Gamma(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}y+x^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(y)-\mathcal{L}(x^{\ast}y).

We write
\Gamma(x)
for
\Gamma(x,x)
.

Using the conditional negative definiteness of the generator, one can associate a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule with a quantum Markov semigroup. This construction is known as the
GNS construction
for quantum Markov semigroups. Let us briefly sketch it.

On
\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}
define an
\mathcal{M}
-valued bilinear form
B
by

B(a\otimes x,b\otimes y)=-\frac{1}{2}x^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(a^{\ast}b)y.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is conditionally negative definite,
B
is positive semi-definite on the subspace
F_{0}=\{\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\mid\sum_{j}a_{j}x_{j}=0\}
. Let
F_{0}^{\prime}
be the
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-bimodule obtained from
F_{0}
after separation and completion with respect to
B
and write
(\cdot|\cdot)
for its
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner product. The strong closure
\overline{F_{0}^{\prime}}^{s}
of
F_{0}^{\prime}
inside
B(L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),F_{0}^{\prime}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule.

Define a derivation
\delta
on
\mathcal{M}
with values in
\overline{F_{0}^{\prime}}^{s}
by

\delta(x)=x\otimes 1-1\otimes x.

A direct calculation shows

(\delta(x)|\delta(y))=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}y+x^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(y)-\mathcal{L}(x^{\ast}y))=\Gamma(x,y).

The von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule
F
associated with
(P_{t})
is the strong closure of
\{x\delta(y)\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
inside
\overline{F_{0}^{\prime}}^{s}
.

The von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule
F
and the derivation
\delta
are essentially uniquely determined by
(P_{t})
. To show this, we will make use of the fact that von Neumann bimodules are dual spaces. More precisely, according to
[
Pas73
, Proposition 3.8]
,
F
is the dual space of
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}/\ker\iota
, where
\bar{F}
is the conjugate space of
F
and

\iota\colon\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}\to F^{\ast},\,\omega\otimes\zeta\mapsto\omega((\zeta|\,\cdot\,)),

with the duality given by

\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}/\ker\iota\times F\to\mathbb{C},\,(\psi+\iota,\xi)\mapsto\iota(\psi)(\xi).

In fact, the predual of a von Neumann module is unique according to
[
Sch02
, Theorem 2.6]
, which justifies to denote it by
F_{\ast}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
F^{\prime}
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule and
\delta^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{M}\to F^{\prime}
is a derivation such that
(\delta^{\prime}(x)|\delta^{\prime}(y))=\Gamma(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and
\{\delta^{\prime}(x)y\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is strongly dense in
F^{\prime}
, then there exists a unique weak
∗
continuous bimodule isomorphism
\alpha\colon F\to F^{\prime}
that satisfies

(\alpha(\xi)|\alpha(\zeta))=(\xi|\zeta)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in F
and
\alpha\circ\delta=\delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Uniqueness of the map
\alpha
is obvious from the density assumptions. Let us show existence. By construction,
\{\delta(x)y\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is strongly dense in
F
. Define

U\colon\{\delta(x)y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}\to F^{\prime}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,U(\delta(x)y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\delta^{\prime}(x)y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}.

Since

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{\prime}(a)x\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\delta^{\prime}(b)y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},(\delta^{\prime}(a)x|\delta^{\prime}(b)y)\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},x^{\ast}(\delta^{\prime}(a)|\delta^{\prime}(b))y\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},x^{\ast}\Gamma(a,b)y\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},x^{\ast}(\delta(a)|\delta(b))y\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a)x\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\delta(b)y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

for all
a,b,x,y\in\mathcal{M}
, the map
U
extends to a unitary from
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
to
F^{\prime}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

Taking into account that
x\delta(y)=\delta(xy)-\delta(x)y
, it is easy to check that
U
is a bimodule map. Hence
U
maps left-bounded vectors to left-bounded vectors and
L(U\eta)=UL(\eta)
for
\eta\in L_{\infty}((F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
.

By
Proposition

3.1
there exists a unique map
\alpha\colon F\to F^{\prime}
such that
\alpha(\xi)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=U(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})
for all
\xi\in F
. In particular,
\alpha
is a bijective bimodule map. Moreover,

\alpha(\delta(x))\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=U(\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\delta^{\prime}(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},

which implies
\alpha\circ\delta=\delta^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle(\alpha(\xi)|\alpha(\zeta))

\displaystyle=L(\alpha(\xi)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}L(\alpha(\zeta)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=L(U(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}))^{\ast}L(U(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}))

\displaystyle=L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}U^{\ast}UL(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=(\xi|\zeta).

It remains to show that
\alpha
is weak
∗
continuous. Recall the definition of
F_{\ast}
given above. Let
\iota_{F}
denote the canonical map from
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}
to
F^{\ast}
and write
\iota_{F^{\prime}}
for the analog map for
F^{\prime}
.

Since

\sum_{k}\lVert\omega_{k}\rVert\lVert\alpha^{-1}(\xi_{k})\rVert=\sum_{k}\lVert\omega_{k}\rVert\lVert\xi_{k}\rVert,

the map

\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes\bar{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes\bar{F},\,\omega\otimes\xi\mapsto\omega\otimes\alpha^{-1}(\xi)

extends to an isometry
\beta\colon\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}
. Moreover, as

\iota_{F}(\omega\otimes\alpha^{-1}(\xi))(\zeta)=\omega((\alpha^{-1}(\xi)|\zeta))=\omega((\xi|\alpha(\zeta)))=\iota_{F^{\prime}}(\omega\otimes\xi)(\alpha(\zeta)),

the map
\beta
maps
\ker\iota_{F^{\prime}}
to
\ker\iota_{F}
and the quotient map
\alpha_{\ast}\colon F^{\prime}_{\ast}\to F_{\ast}
is a predual of
\alpha
. Hence
\alpha
is weak
∗
continuous.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Assume that
\varphi
is a trace. By
[
CS03
]
, there exists an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence
\mathcal{H}
and a closable derivation
\partial\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}
such that
\varphi(\Gamma(x))=\lVert\delta(x)\rVert^{2}
. One can show that

\lVert\partial(x)y\rVert^{2}=\varphi(\Gamma(x)yy^{\ast}).

This implies

\lVert\partial(x)y\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\Gamma(x)\rVert\varphi(yy^{\ast}).

Hence
\partial(x)\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
and
L(\partial(x))^{\ast}L(\partial(x))=\Gamma(x)
. Note that since
\varphi
is a trace,
L(\eta)x\Omega_{\varphi}=\eta x
. If we view
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
as a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule as described in
Proposition

3.1
,
\partial
is thus a derivation with values in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
. Therefore
F
can be realized as a subbimodule of
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
and
\delta
as a corestriction of
\partial
.

Example 4.3

(Semigroups of Herz–Schur multipliers)
.

Let
G
be a discrete group. A conditionally negative definite length function on
G
is a map
\ell\colon G\to[0,\infty)
such that
\ell(e)=0
,
\ell(g^{-1})=\ell(g)
and

\sum_{g,h\in G}\bar{\alpha}_{g}\alpha_{h}\ell(g^{-1}h)\leq 0

for every
\alpha\in C_{c}(G)
with
\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}=0
. If
\ell
is a negative definite length function, the map
P_{t}
on
\mathbb{C}[G]
defined by
P_{t}\lambda_{g}=e^{-t\ell(g)}\lambda_{g}
extends to a normal unital completely positive map on the group von Neumann algebra
L(G)
, still denoted by
P_{t}
. The operators
P_{t}
form a
\tau
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup, where
\tau
is the trace on
L(G)
given by
\tau(x)=\langle\delta_{e},x\delta_{e}\rangle
.

For every conditionally negative definite length function
\ell
there exists a (real) Hilbert space
H
, an orthogonal representation
\pi
of
G
on
H
and a map
b\colon G\to H
satisfying the
1
-cocycle condition

b(gh)=b(g)+\pi(g)b(h)

such that
\lVert b(g)-b(h)\rVert^{2}=\ell(g^{-1}h)
.

In this case, the
L(G)
-
L(G)
-correspondence
\mathcal{H}
from
Remark

4.2
is a subcorrespondence of
\mathcal{H}=H_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\ell_{2}(G)
, where
H_{\mathbb{C}}
is an
L(G)
-
\mathbb{C}
-correspondence through the action of
L(G)
on
H_{\mathbb{C}}
induced by
\pi
. The derivation
\partial
is given by
\partial(\lambda_{g})=\delta_{g}\otimes b(g)

[
CS03
, Section 10.2]
.

The semigroup
(P_{t})
is uniformly continuous if and only if
\ell
is bounded. In this case, according to
Remark

4.2
the von Neumann
L(G)
-bimodule
F
can be realized as a subbimodule of
H_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes L(G)
with the standard inner product and right action and the left action given by
\lambda_{g}(\eta\otimes\lambda_{h})=\pi(g)\eta\otimes\lambda_{gh}
. The derivation
\delta
is then given by
\delta(\lambda_{g})=b(g)\otimes\lambda_{g}
.

So far, the construction of
F
and
\delta
works for arbitrary quantum Markov semigroups and does not use GNS-symmetry. We will next show that GNS-symmetry gives rise to some extra structure on
F
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(P_{t})
be a uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup. There exist a unique semigroup
(V_{t})
of weak
∗
continuous isometries of
F
and a unique anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}
on
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
such that

(a)

V_{t}(\delta(x))=\delta(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(b)

V_{t}(x\xi y)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)(V_{t}\xi)\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
,
\xi\in F
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(c)

\mathcal{J}(\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\delta(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
,

(d)

\mathcal{J}(x\eta y)=y^{\ast}(\mathcal{J}\eta)x^{\ast}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
,
\eta\in F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

Moreover, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
the map

F\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\to F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mapsto V_{t}\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

extends to a unitary
U_{t}
on
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and

(e)

\mathcal{J}\circ U_{t}=U_{t}\circ\mathcal{J}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since the linear hull of
\{x\delta(y)\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is strongly dense in
F
and
F\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
is dense in
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, uniqueness is clear.

Let us start with the existence of
V_{t}
. We will use that
\mathcal{L}
commutes with modular group by
Proposition

2.2
. Let
a_{j},x_{j},b_{k},y_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
with
\sum_{j}a_{j}x_{j}=\sum_{k}b_{k}y_{k}=0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As

\displaystyle\quad\;\left\langle\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(a_{j})\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x_{j})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\sum_{k}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(b_{k})\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y_{k})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x_{j}^{\ast})\mathcal{L}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k}))\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y_{k}))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x_{j}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k})y_{k}))

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\sum_{k}b_{k}\otimes y_{k}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

the map

\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mapsto\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(a_{j})\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x_{j})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

extends to a unitary
U_{t}
on
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and

U_{t}(x\eta y)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)(U_{t}\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
,
\eta\in F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
\eta\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
, then
U_{t}\eta\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
and
L(U_{t}\eta)=L(\eta)\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it}
.

Thus
U_{t}
restricts to a map from
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
to itself with
\lVert L(U_{t}\eta)\rVert=\lVert L(\eta)\rVert
. The existence of an isometry
V_{t}
on
F
with properties (a) and (b) as well as the relation
U_{t}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=V_{t}\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
follows from the identifications from
Proposition

3.1
. Note that we have

(V_{t}\xi|V_{t}\zeta)=L(U_{t}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}))^{\ast}L(U_{t}(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}))=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}L(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}L(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it}=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((\xi|\zeta)).

It just remains to show that
V_{t}
is weak
∗
continuous. The argument is as the one for the weak
∗
continuity of
\alpha
in the proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\lVert\omega_{k}\circ\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}\rVert\lVert V_{-t}\zeta_{k}\rVert=\sum_{k}\lVert\omega_{k}\rVert\lVert\zeta_{k}\rVert,

the map

\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes\bar{F}\to\mathcal{M}_{\ast}\otimes\bar{F},\,\omega\otimes\zeta\mapsto\omega\circ\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}\otimes V_{-t}\zeta

extends to an isometry
W_{t}
on
M_{\ast}\otimes_{\pi}\bar{F}
. Moreover, as

\iota(\omega\circ\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}\otimes V_{-t}\zeta)(\xi)=\omega(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(V_{-t}\zeta|\xi))=\omega((\zeta|V_{t}\xi))=\iota(\omega\otimes\zeta)(V_{t}\xi),

the map
W_{t}
leaves
\ker\iota
invariant and the quotient map
(V_{t})_{\ast}\colon F_{\ast}\to F_{\ast}
is a predual of
V_{t}
. Hence
V_{t}
is weak
∗
continuous.

Let us now come to the existence of
\mathcal{J}
. We will use
\varphi
-symmetry of
\mathcal{L}
and in particular
Proposition

2.2
.

Let
a_{j},x_{j},b_{k},y_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
with
\sum_{j}a_{j}x_{j}=\sum_{k}b_{k}y_{k}=0
.
Since

\displaystyle\quad\;\left\langle\sum_{k}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})^{\ast}\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(b_{k})^{\ast}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(a_{j})^{\ast}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(b_{k})\mathcal{L}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(a_{j})^{\ast})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\mathcal{L}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k}))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k})))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x_{j}^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(\mathcal{L}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k}))\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y_{k}))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(x_{j}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(a_{j}^{\ast}b_{k})y_{k})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},b_{k}\otimes y_{k}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

the map

\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mapsto-\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}(a_{j})^{\ast}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

extends to an anti-unitary operator
\mathcal{J}
on
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. Properties (c)–(e) as well as
\mathcal{J}^{2}=\mathcal{J}
are easy to check.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
\eta\in L_{\infty}((F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
and
x\in\mathcal{M}
, then

\lVert x\mathcal{J}\eta\rVert=\lVert\mathcal{J}(\eta x^{\ast})\rVert=\lVert\eta x^{\ast}\rVert\leq\lVert L(\eta)\rVert\lVert\Omega_{\varphi}x^{\ast}\rVert=\lVert L(\eta)\rVert\lVert x\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert.

Thus
\mathcal{J}
maps left-bounded vectors to right-bounded vectors (and vice versa).

Remark 4.6
.

If we write
L
and
R
for the left and right action of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
on

Remark 4.7
.

If
\varphi
is a trace, the map

\partial\colon\mathcal{M}\to F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,x\mapsto\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

satisfies
\partial(xy)=x\partial(y)+\partial(x)y
and
\partial(x^{\ast})=\mathcal{J}\partial(x)
. Thus the structure obtained in the previous proposition can be seen as a non-tracial version of Cipriani’s and Sauvageot’s first-order differential calculus
[
CS03
]
in the uniformly continuous case. Note that if
\varphi

V_{t}=\mathrm{id}
for all

, so that the group
(V_{t})
is a feature of the non-tracial case.

We record one observation from the proof of
Proposition

4.4
, as it will be needed later.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\xi,\zeta\in F
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((\xi|\zeta))=(V_{t}\xi|V_{t}\zeta).

In particular, if
\xi,\zeta
are invariant under
(V_{t})
and
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
, then
(\xi|x\zeta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
and

\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}((\xi|x\zeta))=(\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x)\zeta)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Since
(P_{t})
is uniformly bounded, the derivation
\delta\colon\mathcal{M}\to F
is bounded. It is a crucial step in the proof of the Christensen–Evans theorem to show that this implies that
\delta
is inner
[
CE79
, Theorem 2.1]
. For
\zeta\in F
we will write

\delta_{\zeta}\colon\mathcal{M}\to F,\,x\mapsto x\zeta-\zeta x.

We will show in two steps that GNS-symmetry of
(P_{t})
implies that
\delta
is of the form
\delta_{\xi}
with a vector
\xi\in F
that is invariant under the maps
V_{t}
and
\mathcal{J}
from
Proposition

4.4
. This will be instrumental in showing that the completely positive map in the Christensen–Evans theorem can be chosen GNS-symmetric.

Lemma 4.9
.

There exists
\xi^{\prime}\in F
such that
V_{t}\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta_{\xi^{\prime}}=\delta
.

Proof.

By
[
CE79
, Theorem 2.1]
there exists
\xi^{\prime\prime}\in F
such that
\delta_{\xi^{\prime\prime}}=\delta
. Let

C=\{\zeta\in F\mid\delta_{\zeta}=\delta,\,\lVert\zeta\rVert\leq\lVert\xi^{\prime\prime}\rVert\}.

This set is bounded and weak
∗
closed, hence weak
∗
compact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. Moreover it is convex.

If
\zeta\in C
, then

\delta_{V_{t}\zeta}(x)=xV_{t}\zeta-(V_{t}\zeta)x=V_{t}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}(x)\zeta-\zeta\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}(x))=V_{t}(\delta(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}))=\delta(x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
by
Proposition

4.4
(a). Moreover,
\lVert V_{t}\zeta\rVert=\lVert\zeta\rVert\leq\lVert\xi^{\prime\prime}\rVert
. Thus
V_{t}\zeta\in C
.

Since each
V_{t}
is weak
∗
continuous and
V_{s}V_{t}=V_{s+t}=V_{t}V_{s}
, we can apply the Markov–Kakutani fixed-point theorem to obtain
\xi^{\prime}\in C
with
V_{t}\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

In the light of
Lemma

4.8
,
V_{t}\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}
implies

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(\xi^{\prime}|x\xi^{\prime})=(\xi^{\prime}|\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)\xi^{\prime}).

The following result is a standard consequence of interpolation theory for noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces.

Lemma 4.11
.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}
one has

\varphi(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}x)\leq\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert\varphi(x^{\ast}x).

Proof.

The symmetric embedding of
\mathcal{M}
into
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is given by

j_{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,x\mapsto\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}(x\Omega_{\varphi}),

and the symmetric embedding of
\mathcal{M}
into
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
is
i_{\varphi}=j_{\varphi}^{\ast}Jj_{\varphi}
, where
J
is viewed as a linear map from
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
to
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)^{\ast}
, identified via the Riesz representation theorem with the conjugate space of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

By GNS-symmetry, the operator

\mathcal{L}_{\ast}\colon i_{\varphi}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\,\mathcal{L}_{\ast}(i_{\varphi}(x))=i_{\varphi}(\mathcal{L}(x))

satisfies
(\mathcal{L}_{\ast})^{\ast}\subset\mathcal{L}
. Hence
\mathcal{L}_{\ast}
is bounded with
\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\ast}\rVert=\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert
.

By interpolation theory for noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, this implies that

\mathcal{L}_{2}\colon j_{\varphi}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\,\mathcal{L}_{2}(j_{\varphi}(x))=j_{\varphi}(\mathcal{L}(x))

is bounded with the same norm. Since
\mathcal{L}
is GNS-symmetric, it follows that

\mathcal{L}_{2}(x\Omega_{\varphi})=\mathcal{L}_{2}(j_{\varphi}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/4}(x)\Omega_{\varphi}))=j_{\varphi}(\mathcal{L}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/4}(x)))=\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/4}(\mathcal{L}(x)))\Omega_{\varphi}=\mathcal{L}(x)\Omega_{\varphi}

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
, and thus by density for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Therefore

\varphi(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}x)=\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}(x\Omega_{\varphi}),x\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle_{2}\leq\lVert\mathcal{L}_{2}\rVert\lVert x\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert\varphi(x^{\ast}x).\qed

Lemma 4.12
.

There exists
\xi\in F
such
V_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
and
\delta_{i\xi}=\xi
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.9
there exists
\xi^{\prime}\in F
such that
V_{t}\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta_{\xi^{\prime}}=\delta
. Let
\mathcal{H}=F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and
\eta=\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}
. By
Proposition

3.1
we have
\eta\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
. We will show that
\eta\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
as well.

For
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
we have

\displaystyle x\eta=x\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}+\xi^{\prime}x\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}+\eta\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)

and hence

\displaystyle\lVert x\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\eta\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\lVert\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert L(\eta)\rVert\lVert\Omega_{\varphi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)\rVert_{2}+\langle\Omega_{\varphi},(\delta(x)|\delta(x))\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi^{\prime}\rVert\lVert x\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert_{2}+\varphi(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}x)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\xi^{\prime}\rVert+\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert)\lVert x\Omega_{\varphi}\rVert_{2}

by
Lemma

4.11
. Thus
\eta\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
and therefore
\mathcal{J}\eta\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
(see
Remark

4.5
).

By
Proposition

3.1
there exists
\zeta\in F
such that
\mathcal{J}\eta=\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
. We have

V_{t}\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=U_{t}\mathcal{J}\eta=\mathcal{J}(V_{t}\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\mathcal{J}(\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\mathcal{J}\eta=\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},

where we used
\mathcal{J}U_{t}=U_{t}\mathcal{J}
from
Proposition

4.4
(e). Hence
V_{t}\zeta=\zeta
.

Thus
\xi=\frac{1}{2i}(\xi^{\prime}-\zeta)
satisfies
V_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\frac{i}{2}(\mathcal{J}(\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})-\mathcal{J}(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}))=\frac{i}{2}(\zeta\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}-\xi^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

and

\displaystyle i(x\xi-\xi x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}(x\zeta-\zeta x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}(x\mathcal{J}\eta-(\mathcal{J}\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}(\eta x^{\ast}-\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast}\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}((\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast}\xi^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime}x^{\ast})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}(\delta(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\delta(x)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}

for
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
. Therefore
\delta_{i\xi}=\delta
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

By
Proposition

4.4
(b),
\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
implies

\langle a(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})x,b(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})y\rangle=\langle\mathcal{J}(b(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})y),\mathcal{J}(a(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})x)\rangle=\langle y^{\ast}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})b^{\ast},x^{\ast}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})a^{\ast}\rangle.

We have now gathered all the necessary ingredients to prove the main result of this section. However, before doing so, we want to show how the von Neumann bimodule
F
together with the extra structure described in
Proposition

4.4
can be lifted to a von Neumann algebra containing
\mathcal{M}
.

This construction is a special case of a result from
[
JRS
]
, building on the construction of operator-valued semicircular families in
[
Shl99
]
. Since the manuscript
[
JRS
]
is unpublished, we give a full proof here. Additionally, since we only deal with uniformly continuous semigroups, we can avoid the ultraproduct construction from
[
JRS
]
. Otherwise, the proof follows the same lines as the one in
[
JRS
]
, with some minor modifications.

One first observations is that the maps
(V_{t})
and
\mathcal{J}
take similar roles as the modular automorphism group and modular conjugation on a von Neumann algebra. In fact, this is more than just an analogy, as the following theorem shows. Note that if
\mathcal{N}_{1}\subset\mathcal{N}_{2}
is a von Neumann subalgebra and
\psi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{N}_{1}
such that
\sigma^{\psi}
leaves
\mathcal{N}_{1}
invariant, by Takesaki’s theorem
[
Tak03
, Theorem IX.4.2]
there exists a unique
\psi
-preserving conditional expectation
E
from
\mathcal{N}_{2}
to
\mathcal{N}_{1}
, which is normal. Endowed with the
\mathcal{N}_{1}
-valued inner product

(a|b)=E(a^{\ast}b)

this makes
\mathcal{N}_{2}
into a von Neumann
\mathcal{N}_{1}
-bimodule and

\mathcal{N}_{2}\otimes L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{1},\psi|_{\mathcal{N}_{1}})\to L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{2},\psi),\,x\otimes\eta\mapsto x\eta

extends to a unitary from
\mathcal{N}_{2}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{1},\psi|_{\mathcal{N}_{1}})
to
L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{2},\psi)
. In the statement of the following theorem we will identify these two spaces via this map.

Moreover, recall that the centralizer of a normal faithful state
\psi
on a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
is the set of all
x\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\sigma^{\psi}_{t}(x)=x
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, or, equivalently,
\psi(xy)=\psi(yx)
for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
.

Theorem 4.14
.

There exists a von Neumann algebra
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
containing
\mathcal{M}
as a von Neumann subalgebra with a normal faithful conditional expectation
E\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{M}
, a self-adjoint element
a
of the centralizer of
\varphi\circ E
with
E(a)=0
and a bimodule map
\alpha\colon F\to\hat{\mathcal{M}}
such that

(a)

E(\alpha(\zeta_{1})^{\ast}\alpha(\zeta_{2}))=(\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2})
for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in F
,

(b)

\alpha(V_{t}\zeta)=\sigma^{\varphi\circ E}_{t}(\alpha(\zeta))
for all
\zeta\in F
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(c)

(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathcal{J}\eta)=J_{\varphi\circ E}(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id})(\eta)
for all
\eta\in F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
,

(d)

\alpha(\delta(x))=i[x,a]
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}=F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})=L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}.

This is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence with the obvious actions.

By
Lemma

4.12
there exists
\xi\in F
such that
V_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
and
\delta_{i\xi}=\delta
. Let
\hat{\eta}=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
. By
Proposition

3.1
we have
\hat{\eta}\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
and since
\mathcal{J}\hat{\eta}=\hat{\eta}
also
\hat{\eta}\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
. Define

\displaystyle a(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x

\displaystyle=\check{\eta}x

\displaystyle a(\check{\eta})\eta

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta

for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
. Since
\hat{\eta}\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
, this map extends to a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, still denoted by
a(\hat{\eta})
. Let
s(\hat{\eta})=a(\hat{\eta})+a(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
s(\hat{\eta})
and the left action of
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
. Since
s(\check{\eta})
is a right module map and
\mathcal{M}
acts on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
from the left by right module maps, the von Neumann algebra
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
consists of right module maps. In particular,
b\Omega_{\varphi}\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
for every
b\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
. Thus

E\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to B(L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)),\,b\mapsto L(\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}bL(\Omega_{\varphi})=L(\Omega_{\varphi})^{\ast}L(b\Omega_{\varphi})

takes values in
\mathcal{M}
. Clearly
E
is completely positive, so that it is a conditional expectation. Let
\hat{\varphi}=\varphi\circ E=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},\cdot\;\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})}
.

We will show that
\Omega_{\varphi}
is cyclic and separating for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
. First note that
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})=\overline{\mathcal{M}\Omega_{\varphi}}\subset\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}
. Moreover, the linear hull of
\{x\delta(y)\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is by definition strongly dense in
F
, so that the linear hull of
\{x\delta(y)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
. Since

x\delta(y)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=i(xy\xi-x\xi y)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=i(xya(\check{\eta})-xa(\hat{\eta})y)\Omega_{\varphi},

it follows that
\mathcal{H}\subset\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}
.

From here on one can proceed by induction. Assume that
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}\subset\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}
. For
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
we have

(x\delta(y)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})\otimes_{\varphi}\eta=i(xya(\hat{\eta})-xa(\hat{\eta})y)\eta\in\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}.

By
Proposition

3.1
the linear hull of
\{x\delta(y)\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}
is strongly dense in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
. By the definition of the relative tensor product, this implies
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}\subset\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}
.

Finally, as elements with finitely many non-zero entries in the direct sum decomposition are dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, we conclude
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})\subset\overline{\hat{\mathcal{M}}\Omega_{\varphi}}
. In other words,
\Omega_{\varphi}
is cyclic for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
.

To show that
\Omega_{\varphi}
is separating for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, we prove that it is cyclic for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
. For that purpose define

\displaystyle b(\hat{\eta})x\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=x\hat{\eta}

\displaystyle b(\hat{\eta})\eta

\displaystyle=\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\hat{\eta}

for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes\varphi k}
. Since
\mathcal{J}\hat{\eta}=\hat{\eta}
and
\hat{\eta}
is left-bounded, it is also right bounded and
b(\hat{\eta})
extends to a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, still denoted by
b(\hat{\eta})
. Let
t(\hat{\eta})=b(\hat{\eta})+b(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}
.

Clearly,
b(\hat{\eta})
commutes with the left action of
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
. We will next show that
s(\hat{\eta})
commutes with
t(\hat{\eta})
. First note that

\displaystyle\langle b(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}\eta,x\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle_{2}=\langle\eta,x\hat{\eta}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle\hat{\eta}x^{\ast},\mathcal{J}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle\Omega_{\varphi}x^{\ast},a(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}\mathcal{J}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle Ja(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}\mathcal{J}\eta,x\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle_{2}

for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
and of course
a(\check{\eta})^{\ast}|_{\mathcal{H}}=L(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}
.

For
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
we have

\displaystyle t(\hat{\eta})s(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x

\displaystyle=t(\hat{\eta})\hat{\eta}x

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}x\otimes_{\varphi}\hat{\eta}+Ja(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}\mathcal{J}(\hat{\eta}x)

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}\otimes_{\varphi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\hat{\eta}+JL(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}L(x^{\ast}\hat{\eta})J\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=a(\hat{\eta})t(\hat{\eta})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\Omega_{\varphi}+\Omega_{\varphi}(x^{\ast}\xi|\xi)

\displaystyle=a(\hat{\eta})t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x+\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}((\xi|x\xi))\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=a(\hat{\eta})t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x+(\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\xi)\Omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=a(\hat{\eta})t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x+L(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\hat{\eta}

\displaystyle=a(\hat{\eta})t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x+a(\hat{\eta})^{\ast}t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}x,

where we used
Lemma

4.8
. The verification for commutation on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
is similar. Therefore,
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
contains
t(\hat{\eta})
and the right action of
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
. Hence
\Omega_{\varphi}
is cyclic for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
by an analog proof as for
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
.

Therefore
E
and consequently also
\hat{\varphi}
is faithful. If
b\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, then

\langle\Omega_{\varphi},s(\hat{\eta})b\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle=\langle\hat{\eta},b\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle=\langle t(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi},b\Omega\varphi\rangle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},t(\hat{\eta})b\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},bt(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},bs(\hat{\eta})\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

since
t(\hat{\eta})\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
. Thus
s(\hat{\eta})
is in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}
.

Define a map
\alpha
from the linear hull of
\{x\xi y\mid x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}\subset F
to
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
by
\alpha(x\xi y)=xs(\hat{\eta})y
. Then

\alpha(x\xi y)\Omega_{\varphi}=xs(\hat{\eta})y\Omega_{\varphi}=x\xi y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}.

Thus
\alpha
is well-defined and strongly continuous. It is easy to check that the extension to
F
satisfies properties (a)–(d).
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. For every uniformly continuous
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
with carré du champ
\Gamma
there exists a von Neumann algebra
\hat{\mathcal{M}}\supset\mathcal{M}
with a normal faithful conditional expectation
E\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{M}
and a self-adjoint element
a
in the centralizer of
\varphi\circ E
with
E(a)=0
such that

\Gamma(x,y)=E([x,a]^{\ast}[y,a]).

Now we come to the announced refinement of the Christensen–Evans theorem for generators of uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. If
(P_{t})
is a uniformly continuous
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup with generator
\mathcal{L}
, then there exists a
\varphi
-symmetric completely positive map
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
such that

\mathcal{L}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\Phi(1)x+x\Phi(1))-\Phi(x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Conversely, every operator of this form generates a uniformly continuous
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup.

Proof.

First assume that we are given a uniformly continuous
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup with generator
\mathcal{L}
. We will use the von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule with the extra structure constructed in this section. By
Lemma

4.12
there exists
\xi\in F
such that
V_{t}\xi=\xi
,
\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
and
\delta_{i\xi}=\delta
. By definition of
\delta
this means

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathcal{L}(x)^{\ast}y)

\displaystyle=\varphi((\delta(x)|\delta(y)))

\displaystyle=\varphi((x\xi|y\xi)+(\xi x|\xi y)-(x\xi|\xi y)-(\xi x|y\xi))

\displaystyle=\langle x\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},y\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle+\varphi(x^{\ast}(\xi|\xi)y)-\varphi((\xi|x\xi)^{\ast}y)-\langle\xi x\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},y\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}y^{\ast},\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}x^{\ast}\rangle+\varphi(x^{\ast}(\xi|\xi)y)-\varphi((\xi|x\xi)^{\ast}y)-\langle\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}y^{\ast},\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast}\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\varphi((\xi|\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x^{\ast}y)))+\varphi(x^{\ast}(\xi|\xi)y)-\varphi((\xi|x\xi)^{\ast}y)-\varphi((\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x^{\ast})\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y))

\displaystyle=\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}((\xi|\xi)x^{\ast}y))+\varphi(x^{\ast}(\xi|\xi)y)-\varphi((\xi|x\xi)^{\ast}y)-\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(\xi|x^{\ast}\xi y))

\displaystyle=\varphi((\xi|\xi)x^{\ast}y+x^{\ast}(\xi|\xi)y-2(\xi|x\xi)^{\ast}y)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
.

Thus

\mathcal{L}(x)=(\xi|\xi)x+x(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|x\xi).

Let
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M},\,x\mapsto 2(\xi|x\xi)
. Clearly, this map is completely positive. Moreover, if
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi(\Phi(x)^{\ast}y)

\displaystyle=2\varphi((x\xi|\xi y))

\displaystyle=2\langle x\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\xi y\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)^{\ast}\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x^{\ast})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\varphi((\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x^{\ast})))

\displaystyle=2\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}((\xi|y\xi)))

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{\ast}\Phi(y)).

Thus
\Phi
is
\varphi
-symmetric.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

Since the map
x\mapsto(\xi|x\xi)
is
\varphi
-symmetric, it commutes with
\sigma^{\varphi}
by
Proposition

2.2
. Thus

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((\xi|\xi))=(\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(1)\xi)=(\xi|\xi)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, that is,
(\xi|\xi)
is in the centralizer of
\varphi
.

Example 4.18
.

The simplest class of examples are quantum Markov semigroups with generators of the form
\mathcal{L}=I-\Phi
with unital completely positive
\Phi
. Evidently, such a quantum Markov semigroup is GNS-symmetric if and only
\Phi
is. Thus the generator is already in GNS-symmetric Christensen–Evans form.

In this case, the Fock space construction in the proof of
Theorem

4.14
is essentially that from
[
Shl99
]
, just that Shlyakhtenko starts with
\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
instead of the subspace
\{\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes x_{j}\mid\sum_{j}a_{j}x_{j}=0\}
in the definition of
F
, which allows to immediately write
\partial(x)=x\xi-\xi x
with
\xi=1\otimes 1
.

A special class of quantum Markov semigroups of this form are the generalized depolarizing (or dephasing) semigroups
P_{t}=e^{-t}I+(1-e^{-t})E
with a normal conditional expectation
E
, whose generator has the form
\mathcal{L}=I-E
.

Example 4.19
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\ell\colon G\to[0,\infty)
a conditionally negative definite length function. As discussed in
Example

4.3
, the operators
P_{t}
given by
P_{t}\lambda_{g}=e^{-t\ell(g)}\lambda_{g}
form a
\tau
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on the group algebra
L(G)
. Let

\xi=\frac{i}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}\delta(\lambda_{g})\lambda_{g^{-1}}.

A similar calculation as in
Theorem

A.1
shows that
\delta_{i\xi}=\delta
. Moreover,

\mathcal{J}(\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi})=-\frac{i}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}\delta(\lambda_{g^{-1}})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=-\frac{i}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}(\delta(\lambda_{g}\lambda_{g^{-1}})-\delta(\lambda_{g})\lambda_{g^{-1}})\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}=\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},

where we used that the modular group is trivial since
\tau
is a trace. Furthermore, the same fact also implies that
(V_{t})
is trivial, so that
V_{t}\xi=\xi
is automatic.

A direct calculation shows

(\xi|\lambda_{g}\xi)=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert^{2}}\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in G}K(h,h^{\prime})\lambda_{g}-\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{h\in G}K(h,g)\lambda_{g}=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert^{2}}\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in G}K(h,h^{\prime})\lambda_{g}-\frac{1}{2}\ell(g)\lambda_{g}

with
K(h,h^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(\ell(h)+\ell(h^{\prime})-\ell(h^{-1}h^{\prime}))
.

As shown in the proof of
Theorem

4.16
,

\mathcal{L}(\lambda_{g})=(\xi|\xi)\lambda_{g}+\lambda_{g}(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|\lambda_{g}\xi).

Indeed,

(\xi|\xi)\lambda_{g}+\lambda_{g}(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|\lambda_{g}\xi)=\frac{2}{\lvert G\rvert^{2}}\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}K(h,h^{\prime})\lambda_{g}-\frac{2}{\lvert G\rvert^{2}}\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in G}K(h,h^{\prime})\lambda_{g}+\ell(g)\lambda_{g}=\ell(g)\lambda_{g}.

Example 4.20
.

Let
X
be a finite set,
m
a probability measure on
X
of full support and
(P_{t})
a Markov semigroup on
C(X)
. (Completely) positive maps on
C(X)
are of the form

\Phi\colon C(X)\to C(X),\,\Phi f(x)=\sum_{y}Q(x,y)f(y)

with
Q(x,y)\geq 0
, and
\Phi
is (GNS-) symmetric if and only if

Q(x,y)m(x)=Q(y,x)m(y).

As a consequence of
Theorem

4.16
, the generators of symmetric Markov semigroups on
C(X)
are exactly the maps of the form

\mathcal{L}f(x)=\sum_{y}Q(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))

with weights
Q
satisfying the positivity and symmetry condition mentioned above.

Thus we recover the classical fact that generators of symmetric Markov semigroups on
C(X)
are exactly the graph Laplacians of undirected weighted graphs over
X
, or, equivalently, the generators of reversible Markov processes om
X
.

5.
Extensions of quantum Markov semigroups

In this section we use our version of the Christensen–Evans theorem for GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups to show that these can be extended from subalgebras to the whole algebra under natural conditions (
Theorem

5.1
). In particular, this implies that Alicki’s theorem is applicable to generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on arbitrary finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras (
Corollary

5.4
), which answers a question raised in
[
CM17
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(Extensions of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\hat{\mathcal{M}}
be an inclusion of von Neumann algebras and assume that there exists a normal conditional expectation
E
from
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
onto
\mathcal{M}
. Every uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
can be extended to a uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroup
(\hat{P}_{t})
on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
.

Moreover, if
\hat{\varphi}
is a normal faithful state on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
such that the modular group
\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}}
leaves
\mathcal{M}
invariant,
\varphi
is the restriction of
\hat{\varphi}
to
\mathcal{M}
and
(P_{t})
is
\varphi
-symmetric, then
(\hat{P}_{t})
can be chosen
\hat{\varphi}
-symmetric.

Proof.

By the Christensen–Evans theorem
[
CE79
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exists
k\in\mathcal{M}
and a completely positive map
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
with
\Phi(1)=k+k^{\ast}
such that the generator
\mathcal{L}
of
(P_{t})
satisfies

\mathcal{L}(x)=k^{\ast}x+xk-\Phi(x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Let

\hat{\mathcal{L}}\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to\hat{\mathcal{M}},\,\hat{\mathcal{L}}(x)=k^{\ast}x+xk-\Phi(E(x)).

Clearly
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
is an extension of
\mathcal{L}
and again of Christensen–Evans form, hence the generator of a quantum Markov semigroup on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
.

In the situation of the second paragraph,
k
can be chosen in the centralizer of
\sigma^{\varphi}
and
\Phi
can be chosen
\varphi
-symmetric by
Theorem

4.16
. Moreover,
E
can be chosen
\varphi
-symmetric as well by Takesaki’s theorem
[
Tak03
, Theorem IX.4.2]
. Then the operator
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
defined above generates a uniformly continuous
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The extension result for quantum Markov semigroups without symmetry assumptions is an immediate consequence of the original Christensen–Evans theorem and may have been known before, although we could not find it in the literature. In contrast, the existence of an extension preserving GNS-symmetry was raised as a question in
[
CM17
, Remark A.12]
. This construction of GNS-symmetric extensions might also prove useful to verify the geometric Ricci condition from
[
LJL20
, Section 3]
,
[
BGJ22
, Definition 3.23]
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
m
be a probability measure of full support on
\{1,\dots,n\}
and let
(P_{t})
be a symmetric Markov semigroup on
\ell_{\infty}(\{1,\dots,n\})
. By
Example

4.20
the generator
\mathcal{L}
of
(P_{t})
is of the form

\mathcal{L}f(j)=\sum_{j}Q(j,k)(f(j)-f(k))

with
Q\colon\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}\to[0,\infty)
and
Q(j,k)m(j)=Q(k,j)m(k)
.

View
\ell_{\infty}(\{1,\dots,n\})
as algebra of diagonal matrices inside
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and define a faithful state on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by
\varphi(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{jj}m(j)
. Let
B=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}Q(j,k)E_{jj}
, where
E_{jl}
denotes the usual matrix units. Then the extension of
(P_{t})
to a
\varphi
-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
provided by
Theorem

5.1
has generator
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
given by

\hat{\mathcal{L}}(A)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}Q(j,k)\left(\frac{E_{jj}A+AE_{jj}}{2}-A_{kk}E_{jj}\right).

Now we come to the announced application to Alicki’s theorem. A density matrix is a positive element
\sigma\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with trace
1
. If
\mathcal{M}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is a unital
\ast
-subalgebra, every state on
\mathcal{M}
is of the form
\varphi=\mathrm{tr}(\,\cdot\,\sigma)
for some density matrix
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
. The state
\varphi
is faithful if and only if
\sigma
has full rank.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
be a full-rank density matrix. A QMS
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
is said to satisfy the
\sigma
-detailed balance condition (DBC) if it is GNS-symmetric with respect to the state
\mathrm{tr}(\,\cdot\,\sigma)
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a unital
\ast
-subalgebra and
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
a full-rank density matrix. If
(P_{t})
is a quantum Markov semigroup on
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the
\sigma
-DBC, then it can be extended to a quantum Markov semigroup
(\hat{P}_{t})
on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying the
\sigma
-DBC.

In particular, there exists a finite set
\mathcal{J}
, real numbers
\omega_{j}
, positive numbers
c_{j}
and elements
v_{j}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for
j\in\mathcal{J}
with

(i)

\mathrm{tr}(v_{j})=0
for all
j\in\mathcal{J}
,

(ii)

\mathrm{tr}(v_{j}^{\ast}v_{k})=\delta_{jk}
for all
j,k\in\mathcal{J}
,

(iii)

\{v_{j}\mid j\in\mathcal{J}\}=\{v_{j}^{\ast}\mid j\in\mathcal{J}\}
,

(iv)

\sigma v_{j}\sigma^{-1}=e^{-\omega_{j}}v_{j}
for all
j\in\mathcal{J}

such that the generator
\mathcal{L}
of
(P_{t})
satisfies

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}c_{j}\left(e^{-\omega_{j}/2}v_{j}^{\ast}[v_{j},\cdot\,]-e^{\omega_{j}/2}[v_{j}^{\ast},\cdot\,]v_{j}\right).

Proof.

The extension part is a special case of
Theorem

5.1
, where the assumption on the existence of a conditional expectation is always satisfied in finite dimensions. The representation of the generator follows then from Alicki’s theorem
[
Ali76
, Theorem 3]
,
[
CM17
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

Appendix A
AFD von Neumann algebras are amenable

The hard part of the proof of the Christensen–Evans theorem is to show that every bounded derivation on a von Neumann algebra with values in a von Neumann bimodule is inner
[
CE79
, Theorem 2.1]
, and we also use this result in our proof of
Theorem

4.16
. In the case when the von Neumann algebra is finite-dimensional, which is of special interest to us for the application to Alicki’s theorem, we can give a considerably simpler proof of this result.

In fact, this proof covers not only finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras, but more generally approximately finite-dimensional ones, and not only derivations with values in a von Neumann bimodule, but more generally derivations with values in a dual Banach bimodule. Let us summarize the relevant terminology.

If
A
is a Banach algebra, a
Banach
A
-bimodule
is an
A
-bimodule
F
with a Banach norm such that

\lVert a\xi b\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert\lVert\xi\rVert\lVert b\rVert

for all
a,b\in A
and
\xi\in F
.

If
F
is the dual of a Banach space
F_{\ast}
and for every
a\in A
the maps
\xi\mapsto a\xi
and
\xi\mapsto\xi a
are weak
∗
continuous, then
F
is called a
dual
Banach
A
-bimodule. By
[
Pas73
, Proposition 3.8]
,
[
Ske01
, Theorem 3.2.11]
, every von Neumann bimodule is a dual Banach bimodule.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra and
F
a dual Banach
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule, a bounded derivation
\delta\colon\mathcal{M}\to F
is called
normal
if for every
\omega\in F_{\ast}
the map
a\mapsto\omega(\delta(a))
is weak
∗
continuous.

A von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is called
amenable
if every derivation on
\mathcal{M}
with values in a dual Banach
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule is inner. A von Neumann algebra with separable predual is called
approximately finite-dimensional
if it is the weak closure of an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras.

The following result is originally due to Kadison and Ringrose
[
KR71
,
KR71a
]
, but the proof given here seems simpler and only uses approximate finite-dimensionality directly.

Theorem A.1
.

Every approximately finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra with separable predual is amenable.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an approximately finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra and
(\mathcal{M}_{n})
an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
whose union is weakly dense in
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
denote be the group of unitary elements of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, respectively

Let
F
be a dual Banach
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule and
\delta\colon\mathcal{M}\to F
a normal derivation. As
\delta
is bounded, the sequence
(\xi_{n})
with

\xi_{n}=\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{n})}u^{\ast}\delta(u)\,du

is bounded in
F
. Here the integration is with respect to the normalized Haar measure on
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
. By the Banach-Alaoglu theorem there exists a weak
∗
accumulation point
\xi\in F
.

If
v\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
and
m\geq n
, then

\displaystyle v\xi_{m}-\xi_{m}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{m})}(vu^{\ast}\delta(u)-u^{\ast}\delta(u)v)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{m})}(vu^{\ast}\delta(u)+u^{\ast}u\delta(v)-u^{\ast}\delta(uv))\,du

\displaystyle=\delta(v)+\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{m})}vu^{\ast}\delta(u)\,du+\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{m})}u^{\ast}\delta(uv)\,du

\displaystyle=\delta(v),

where we used the substitution
u\mapsto uv^{\ast}
for the third summand in the last step. Hence
v\xi-\xi v=\delta(v)
.

By
[
Tak02
, Theorem II.4.11]
,
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M})
is the strong
∗
closure of
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
. As
\delta
is normal, this implies the identity
\delta(v)=v\xi-\xi v
for all
v\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M})
. Finally, as
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M})
spans
\mathcal{M}
, the same equality holds for arbitrary
x\in\mathcal{M}
instead of
v
.
∎

Remark A.2
.

Of course, if
\mathcal{M}
itself is finite-dimensional, there is no need for approximation and one can directly define
\xi=\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M})}u^{\ast}\delta(u)\,du
.

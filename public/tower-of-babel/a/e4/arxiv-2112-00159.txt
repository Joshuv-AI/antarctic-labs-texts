The permuton limit of strong-Baxter and semi-Baxter permutations is the skew Brownian permuton
Jacopo Borga

Abstract
We recently introduced a new universal family of permutons, depending on two parameters, called skew Brownian permuton . For some specific choices of the parameters, the skew Brownian permuton coincides with some previously studied permutons: the biased Brownian separable permuton and the Baxter permuton. The latter two permutons are degenerate cases of the skew Brownian permuton. In the present paper we prove the first convergence result towards a non-degenerate skew Brownian permuton. Specifically, we prove that strong-Baxter permutations converge in the permuton sense to the skew Brownian permuton for a non-degenerate choice of the two parameters. In order to do that, we develop a robust technique to prove convergence towards the skew Brownian permuton for various families of random constrained permutations. This technique relies on generating trees for permutations, allowing an encoding of permutations with multi-dimensional walks in cones. We apply this technique also to semi-Baxter permutations .

1.3
Outline of the strategy for the proofs and generality of our techniques

Despite the strategies to prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
are quite involved, in this section we try to give a brief idea of the main steps in these proofs. In addition we highlight the substantial differences with the proofs in
[
BM20
]
, where convergence in the permuton sense of Baxter permutations is established.

We start by briefly recalling how we proved in
[
BM20
]
that the scaling limit of Baxter permutations is the skew Brownian permuton
\bm{\mu}_{-1/2,1/2}
. The starting point consisted in using two bijections already available in the literature, one between Baxter permutations and bipolar orientations (
[
BBMF11
]
) and a second one between bipolar orientations and a family of two-dimensional random walks in the non-negative quadrant, called

(
[

To prove the convergence towards the skew Brownian permuton

5

5

Section

2.2

The last step in the proof was to transfer this coalescent-walk process convergence result to Baxter permutations using the bijection with tandem walks mentioned above.

A bijection between permutations encoded by a generating tree and multi-dimensional walks

We start by assuming that the children labels

There is a simple bijection between a family of permutations encoded by a generating tree and the set of paths in the tree from the root. We can associate to the endpoint of each path the permutation corresponding to that vertex. Encoding each path in the tree with the list of labels appearing on the path, every permutation of size

n

(k_{1}=\lambda,k_{2},\dots,k_{n})

k_{i}

(k_{i},k_{i+1}),

1\leq i\leq n-1,
is
consistent with the succession rule
in

13

1\leq i\leq n-1,
there exist
j=j({i})\in[1,h(k_{i})]
such that
k_{i+1}=e_{j}(k_{i})
. We denote by
\pw

Note that whenever
\mathcal{L}

\mathbb{Z}^{n}

n

2.2
Discrete coalescent-walk processes and permutations

Coalescent-walk processes
were introduced in
[
BM20
]
(see in particular Section 2.4) to study the scaling limit of Baxter permutations. We now recall their definition and their relations with permutations. Then in
Section

3.2
we show that there exists a natural coalescent-walk process encoding strong-Baxter permutations (and in
Section

5.1.2
we will do the same for semi-Baxter permutations).

Definition 2.4
.

Consider a (finite or infinite) interval
I
of
\mathbb{Z}
. A
coalescent-walk process
on
I
is a family of one-dimensional walks
\{(Z^{(t)}_{s})_{s\geq t,s\in I}\}_{t\in I}
such that:

•

Z^{(t)}_{t}=0
, for every
t\in I
;

•

for
t^{\prime}\geq t\in I
, if
Z^{(t)}_{k}\geq Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k}
(resp.
Z^{(t)}_{k}\leq Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k}
) then
Z^{(t)}_{k^{\prime}}\geq Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k^{\prime}}
(resp.
Z^{(t)}_{k^{\prime}}\leq Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k^{\prime}}
) for every
k^{\prime}\geq k
.

The set of coalescent-walk processes on some interval
I
is denoted by
\mathfrak{C}(I)
.

We highlight the following consequence of the definition above: if
Z^{(t)}_{k}=Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k}
for some time
k
, then
Z^{(t)}_{k^{\prime}}=Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k^{\prime}}
for all
k^{\prime}\geq k
.
If this is the case, we say that the walks
Z^{(t)}
and
Z^{(t^{\prime})}

coalesce
and we call
coalescent point
of
Z^{(t)}
and
Z^{(t^{\prime})}
the point
(s,Z^{(t)}_{s})
such that
s=\min\{k\geq\max\{t,t^{\prime}\}|Z^{(t)}_{k}=Z^{(t^{\prime})}_{k}\}
.

For a coalescent-walk process
Z=\{Z^{(t)}\}_{t\in I}\in\mathfrak{C}(I)
defined on a (finite or infinite) interval
I
, we define the following binary relation
\leq_{Z}
on
I
:

\begin{cases}i\leq_{Z}i,\\
i\leq_{Z}j,&\text{ if }i<j\text{ and }Z^{(i)}_{j}\leq 0,\\
j\leq_{Z}i,&\text{ if }i<j\text{ and }Z^{(i)}_{j}>0.\end{cases}

(15)

In
[
BM20
, Proposition 2.9]
it was shown that
\leq_{Z}
defines a total order on
I
. This total order allows to associate a permutation with a coalescent-walk process on the interval
[n]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
Z=\{Z^{(t)}\}_{i\in[n]}\in\mathfrak{C}([n])
be a coalescent-walk process on
[n]
. We denote by
\cpp(Z)
the only permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

\sigma(i)\leq\sigma(j)\iff i\leq_{Z}j.

(16)

As a consequence, for all
j\in[n]
,

\sigma(j)=\#\{i\in[n]|i\leq_{Z}j\}=\#\{i\in[j]:Z^{(i)}_{j}\leq 0\}+\#\{i\in[j+1,n]:Z^{(j)}_{i}>0\}.

In
[
BM20
, Proposition 2.11]
it was shown that pattern extraction in the permutation
\cpp(Z)
depends only on a finite number of walks. This is a key tool to prove permuton convergence results.

Proposition 2.6

(Proposition 2.11 in
[
BM20
]
)
.

Let
Z=\{Z^{(t)}\}_{t\in[n]}
be a coalescent-walk process and
\sigma=\cpp(Z)
the corresponding permutation of size
n
. We fix a set of indexes
I=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\}\subseteq[n]
. Then
\indperm_{I}(\sigma)=\pi
if and only if the following condition is satisfied:
for every
1\leq\ell<s\leq k,

Z^{(i_{\ell})}_{i_{s}}\geq 0\iff\pi(s)<\pi(\ell).

(17)

3.1
Succession rule for strong-Baxter permutations and the corresponding family of two-dimensional walks

We introduce some terminology.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a family of permutations
\mathcal{C},
a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
and an integer
m\in[n+1],
we say that
m
is an
active site
of
\sigma
if
\sigma^{*m}\in\mathcal{C}.
We denote by
\text{AS}(\sigma)
the set of active sites of
\sigma
.

We will adopt the following useful convention. Given a strong-Baxter permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{Sb}^{n}
with
x+1
active sites smaller than or equal to
\pi(n)
and
y+1
active sites greater than
\pi(n)
, we write

\text{AS}(\pi)=\{s_{-x}<\dots<s_{0}\}\cup\{s_{1}<\dots<s_{y+1}\},

where the first set corresponds to the
x+1
active sites smaller than or equal to
\pi(n)
and the second set corresponds to the
y+1
active sites greater than
\pi(n)
.

In
[
BGRR18
, Proposition 23]
it was shown that a generating tree for semi-Baxter permutations can be defined by the following succession rule
8
8

8

Note that the succession rule in our paper is obtained from the succession rule in
[
BGRR18
, Proposition 23]
by shifting all the labels by a factor
(-1,-1)
. This choice is more convenient for our purposes.
:

\begin{cases}\text{Root label}:(0,0)\\
(h,k)\to\begin{cases}(0,k),(1,k),\dots,(h-1,k),\\
(h,k+1),\\
(h+1,0),(h+1,1)\dots,(h+1,k),\end{cases}\quad\text{for all }h,k\geq 0.\end{cases}

(19)

In particular, the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}
-valued statistics that determines this succession rule is defined on every permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{Sb}
by

\Big(\#\{m\in\text{AS}(\sigma)|m\leq\sigma(n)\}-1\;,\;\#\{m\in\text{AS}(\sigma)|m>\sigma(n)\}-1\Big).

Section

2.1.2

\pw
between strong-Baxter permutations and the set of two-dimensional walks in the non-negative quadrant, starting at
(0,0)

I_{Sb}\coloneqq\{(-i,0):i\geq 1\}\cup\{(0,1)\}\cup\{(1,-i):i\geq 0\}.

Eq.

19
.

\mathcal{W}_{Sb}

\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}

\mathcal{W}_{Sb}

0,&\quad\text{if}\quad Z^{(t)}_{\ell}<0\text{ and }Z^{(t)}_{\ell}-(X_{\ell+1}-X_{\ell})\geq 0.\end{cases}

(24)

3.3
The diagram commutes

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.10
.

The diagram in

commutes.

Proof.

We show that
\pw^{-1}=\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}
. Fix

and let

. We are going to prove the following.

Claim.
Assume that

and
\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m]})=\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m]})
.
Then

\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})=\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]}).

Proof of the claim.
Since by assumption
\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m]})=\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m]})
it is enough to show that
\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})(m+1)=\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})(m+1)
. By
3.2
and assuming that

\text{AS}(\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]}))=\{s_{-x}<\dots<s_{0}\}\cup\{s_{1}<\dots<s_{y+1}\},

we have that
\pw^{-1}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})(m+1)=s_{0}
.

On the other hand, setting
\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})=\{Z^{(t)}\}_{t\in[m+1]}=Z
and using
3.9
, we have that

\FV(Z)=\{f_{-x}<\dots<f_{-1}<f_{0}=0<f_{1}<\dots<f_{y}\}=[-x,y].

Then by
2.5
and
3.7
we have that

\cpp\circ\wcp_{Sb}((W_{i})_{i\in[m+1]})(m+1)=\#\{t\in[m+1]:Z^{(t)}_{m+1}\leq 0\}=\sum_{\ell\leq 0}\mult(f_{\ell})=1+\sum_{\ell\leq-1}\mult(f_{\ell}).

Using

in

4
Probabilistic results for strong-Baxter permutations

The main goal of this section is to prove
1.5
. The proof of this result is divided in the following steps:

•

In
Section

4.1
we explain how to sample a uniform strong-Baxter permutation as a two-dimensional walk conditioned to stay in a cone.

•

Then, in
Section

4.2

•

In
Section

4.3

•

Finally, in

4.1
Sampling a uniform strong-Baxter permutation as a conditioned two-dimensional walk

Since the map
\pw:\mathcal{S}_{Sb}\to\mathcal{W}_{Sb}
is a size-preserving bijection and using the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{Sb}
, in order to sample a uniform strong-Baxter permutation of size
n
, it is enough to sample a uniform two-dimensional walk in the non-negative quadrant of size
n
, starting at
(0,0)
, with increments in
I_{Sb}
.

Consider the following probability measure on
I_{Sb}
(see also the left-hand side of
Fig.

7
):

\mu_{Sb}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(-i,0)}+\alpha\theta^{-1}\cdot\delta_{(0,1)}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{-1}\theta^{i}\cdot\delta_{(1,-i)},

(26)

where
\delta
denotes the delta-Dirac measure and
\alpha,\theta,\gamma
are the unique solutions of the following system of equations (the first equation guarantees that
\mu_{Sb}
is a probability measure; the second and the third equation that
\mu_{Sb}
is centered):

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\theta)}=\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^{2}},\\
\frac{1}{\theta}=\frac{\theta}{\gamma(1-\theta)^{2}},\\
\alpha=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\theta)}},\\
\alpha>0,\theta>0,\gamma>0,\alpha\theta^{-1}\leq 1.\end{cases}

(27)

With standard computations, we get that
\gamma
is the unique real root of the polynomial
-1+2\gamma-\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{3}
,
\theta=-7+18\gamma-14\gamma^{2}+11\gamma^{3}-3\gamma^{4}
, and
\alpha=\frac{36}{11}-\frac{83}{11}\gamma+\frac{61}{11}\gamma^{2}-4\gamma^{3}+\frac{12}{11}\gamma^{4}
.
With a computer one can estimate that
\theta\approx 0.43016
,
\gamma\approx 0.56984
, and
\alpha\approx 0.14861
.

Let
(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
be a random variable such that
\mathcal{L}aw(\bm{X},\bm{Y})=\mu_{Sb}
. With standard computations we have that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}]=\mathbb{E}[\bm{Y}]=0,

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}\bm{Y}]=-\frac{\alpha\theta}{\gamma(1-\theta)^{2}},

(28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}^{2}]=\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\theta)}+\frac{\gamma(1+\gamma)}{(1-\gamma^{3})}\right)\eqqcolon\sigma^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbb{E}[\bm{Y}^{2}]=\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\theta(1+\theta)}{\gamma(1-\theta^{3})}\right)\eqqcolon{\sigma^{\prime}}^{2}.

(29)

Therefore

\Var((\bm{X},\bm{Y}))=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\theta)}+\frac{\gamma(1+\gamma)}{(1-\gamma^{3})}&-\frac{\theta}{\gamma(1-\theta)^{2}}\\
-\frac{\theta}{\gamma(1-\theta)^{2}}&\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\theta(1+\theta)}{\gamma(1-\theta^{3})}\end{pmatrix},

(30)

and so

\rho=\Cor((\bm{X},\bm{Y}))=-\frac{\theta}{(1-\theta)^{2}\gamma\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(1-\theta)}+\frac{\gamma(1+\gamma)}{(1-\gamma^{3})}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\theta(1+\theta)}{\gamma(1-\theta^{3})}\right)}}\approx-0.21508.

(31)

Equivalently, with some standard computations, we obtain that
\rho
is the unique real solution of the polynomial
1+6\rho+8\rho^{2}+8\rho^{3}
.

We now denote by

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{Sb}}}\coloneqq\{(i,0):i\geq 1\}\cup\{(0,-1)\}\cup\{(-1,i):i\geq 0\},

(32)

i.e. the set of “reversed” increments (recall the definition of the set
I_{Sb}
in
Eq.

20
). We further denote by

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{Sb}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(i,0)}+\alpha\theta^{-1}\cdot\delta_{(0,-1)}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{-1}\theta^{i}\cdot\delta_{(-1,i)},

(33)

the “reversed” distribution on
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{Sb}}}
induced by
\mu_{Sb}
(see also the right-hand side of
Fig.

7
).

Figure 7:
Left:
Some of the increments in the set
I_{Sb}
are plotted together with the corresponding probability weights given by
\mu_{Sb}
.
Right:
Some increments in the set
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{Sb}}}
are plotted together with the corresponding probability weights given by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{Sb}}}

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define the following additional probability measure

\nu_{Sb}^{n}=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{Sb}}\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}\delta_{(h,\ell)},

(34)

where

\mathcal{L}_{Sb}^{n}\coloneqq\{\text{Labels at level $n$ in the generating tree for strong-Baxter permutations}\}

and the normalizing constant satisfies

Z_{n}=\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{Sb}}\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}.

Let
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\geq 1}
be a two-dimensional random walk with increments distributed as
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{Sb}}}
and starting probability
\nu_{Sb}^{n}(h,\ell)=\mathbb{P}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(1)=(h,\ell))
.
Denote by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}}}
the set of two-dimensional walks
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}
in the non-negative quadrant with increments in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{Sb}}}
and such that
x_{n}=(0,0)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Conditioning on the event
\left\{(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}}}\right\}
, the walk
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}
is a uniform walk in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}}}
.

Proof.

Fix
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}}}
. It is enough to show that
\mathbb{P}\left((\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}=(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\right)
is independent of the choice of
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}
. To do that, assume that
x_{1}=(h,\ell)
and recall that
x_{n}=(0,0)
. By definition of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{Sb}}}
and
\nu_{Sb}^{n}
we have that

\mathbb{P}\left((\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}=(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\right)=\nu_{Sb}^{n}(h,\ell)\cdot\alpha^{n-1}\gamma^{-h}\theta^{-\ell}=\frac{\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}}{Z_{n}}\cdot\alpha^{n-1}\gamma^{-h}\theta^{-\ell}=\frac{\alpha^{n-1}}{Z_{n}},

(35)

and this concludes the proof.
∎

Let
(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\geq 1}
be the reversed walk obtained from
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\geq 1}
. An easy consequence of
4.1
is the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Conditioning on the event
\left\{(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right\}
, the walk
(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}
is a uniform walk in
\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}
.

4.2
Scaling limit of the conditioned two-dimensional walks for strong-Baxter permutations

We define a rescaled version of the walk
(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\geq 1}=(\bm{X}_{n}(i),\bm{Y}_{n}(i))_{i\geq 1}
: for all
n\geq 1,
let
{\bm{\mathcal{W}}}_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the continuous function defined by linearly interpolating the points

{\bm{\mathcal{W}}}_{n}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)=\left(\frac{\bm{X}_{n}(k)}{\sigma\sqrt{n}},\frac{\bm{Y}_{n}(k)}{\sigma^{\prime}\sqrt{n}}\right),\quad\text{ for all }k\in[n],

(36)

where
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
were defined in
Eq.

28
.

All the spaces of continuous functions considered below are implicitly endowed with the
topology of uniform convergence on every compact set.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\rho
be the unique real solution of the polynomial

1+6\rho+8\rho^{2}+8\rho^{3}.

(37)

Conditioning on the event
\left\{(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right\}
, we have the following convergence in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

{\bm{\mathcal{W}}}_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{\rho},

(38)

where we recall that
{\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{\rho}=({\bm{\mathcal{E}}}_{\rho}(t))_{t\in[0,1]}
denotes a two-dimensional Brownian excursion of correlation
\rho
in the non-negative quadrant.

Proof.

An immediate consequence of
[
DW20
, Theorem 6]
is that, for every choice of
(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}
,
Eq.

38
holds conditioning on

\left\{(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right\}.

Therefore in order to prove the proposition it is enough to show that

\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)=1.

(39)

Note that

\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\middle|\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right)\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)}.

(40)

From
B.1
, there exist a constant
C>0
independent of
h,\ell,n
and a parameter
p>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\middle|\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right)\leq C(1+|(h,\ell)|^{p})n^{-p-1},\quad\text{for all}\quad n,h,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

(41)

where
|(h,\ell)|
denotes the Euclidean norm of the vector
(h,\ell)
.
Moreover, using the definition of the measure
\nu_{Sb}^{n}
in
Eq.

34
, we have that

\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{Sb}}\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\middle|\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right)\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}.

(42)

Noting that
(1,0)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{Sb}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and that
Z_{n}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\geq\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)\mathbb{P}\left((\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\middle|\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(1,0)\right)\geq C^{\prime}n^{-p-1},

(43)

where in the last inequality we used
B.2
.
Substituting in
Eq.

40
the bounds obtained in
Eqs.

41
and
43
, together with the trivial bound
\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\right)\leq C^{\prime\prime}\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}
, we can conclude that

\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\leq C^{\prime\prime\prime}(1+|(h,\ell)|^{p})\gamma^{h}\theta^{\ell}\quad\text{for all}\quad n,h,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(44)

Therefore for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that

\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus K_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\leq\varepsilon,\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(45)

As a consequence, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\\
\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{(h,\ell)\in K_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\\
\geq 1-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}\setminus K_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{W}_{n}(n)=(h,\ell)\middle|(\bm{W}_{n}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\mathcal{W}_{Sb}^{n}\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

(46)

This proves
Eq.

39
and concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.2.1
Sampling a uniform semi-Baxter permutation as a conditioned two-dimensional walk

We proceed as in
Section

4.1
.
Consider the following probability measure on
I_{sb}
(see also the left-hand side of
Fig.

13
):

\mu_{sb}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(-i,1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(i,-i+1)},

(117)

where
\alpha=\sqrt{5}-2
and
\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}
, and
\delta
denotes the delta-Dirac measure. Let
(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
be a random variable such that
\mathcal{L}aw(\bm{X},\bm{Y})=\mu_{sb}
. With standard computations it is easy to verify that:

\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}]=\mathbb{E}[\bm{Y}]=0,\quad\quad\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}^{2}]=2(2+\sqrt{5}),\quad\quad\mathbb{E}[\bm{Y}^{2}]=1+\sqrt{5},\quad\quad\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}\bm{Y}]=-(2+\sqrt{5}).

(118)

Therefore

\Var((\bm{X},\bm{Y}))=\begin{pmatrix}2(2+\sqrt{5})&-(2+\sqrt{5})\\
-(2+\sqrt{5})&1+\sqrt{5}\end{pmatrix}

(119)

and so
\rho=\Cor((\bm{X},\bm{Y}))=-\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{4}
.
We now denote by

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{sb}}}\coloneqq\{(i,-1):i\geq 0\}\cup\{(-i,i-1):i\geq 1\},

(120)

i.e. the set of “reversed” increments (recall the definition of the set
I_{sb}
in
Eq.

112
). We further denote by

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{sb}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(i,-1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha\gamma^{i}\cdot\delta_{(-i,i-1)},

(121)

the “reversed” distribution on
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{sb}}}
induced by
\mu_{sb}
(see also the right-hand side of
Fig.

13
).

For all

, we define the following additional probability measure

\nu_{sb}^{n}=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{sb}}\gamma^{2\ell+h}\delta_{(h,\ell)},

(122)

where

\mathcal{L}_{sb}^{n}\coloneqq\{\text{Labels at level $n$ in the generating tree for semi-Baxter permutations}\}

and the normalizing constant satisfies

Z_{n}=\sum_{(h,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}_{sb}}\gamma^{2\ell+h}.

Let
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\geq 1}
be a two-dimensional random walk with increments distributed as
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mu_{sb}}}
and starting probability
\nu_{sb}^{n}(h,\ell)=\mathbb{P}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(1)=(h,\ell))
.
Denote by

the set of two-dimensional walks

in the non-negative quadrant with increments in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{I_{sb}}}
and such that
x_{n}=(0,0)
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Conditioning on the event
\left\{(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{sb}^{n}}}\right\}
, the walk
(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}
is a uniform walk in
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{sb}^{n}}}
.

Proof.

Fix
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{sb}^{n}}}
. It is enough to show that
\mathbb{P}\left((\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}=(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\right)
is independent of the choice of
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}
. To do that, assume that the walk
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}
is formed by
k
increments of the form
(-i,i-1)
and
n-k-1
increments of the form
(i,-1)
. Moreover, assume that the sum of the
k
increments of the form
(-i,i-1)
is equal to
(-s,s-k)
and the sum of the
n-k-1
increments of the form
(i,-1)
is equal to
(q,-n+k+1)
. Under these assumptions we have that if
x_{1}=(h,\ell)
then
x_{n}=(h-s+q,\ell+s-n+1)
. Since
(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{W}_{sb}^{n}}}
, it must hold that
(h-s+q,\ell+s-n+1)=(0,0)
and so
s=n-1-\ell
and
q=n-1-\ell-h
, in particular
s+q=2n-2\ell-h-2
.

Under these assumptions,

\mathbb{P}\left((\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\bm{W}_{n}}}(i))_{i\in[n]}=(x_{i})_{i\in[n]}\right)=\nu_{sb}^{n}(h,\ell)\cdot\alpha^{n-1}\cdot\gamma^{s+q}=\frac{\gamma^{2\ell+h}}{Z_{n}}\cdot\alpha^{n-1}\cdot\gamma^{2n-2\ell-h-2}=\frac{\alpha^{n-1}\cdot\gamma^{2n-2}}{Z_{n}},

(123)

and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

We highlight a remarkable difference with the case of strong-Baxter permutations: the walks of the continuous coalescent-walk process for semi-Baxter permutations are classical Brownian motions. This was also the case of Baxter permutations (see
[
BM20
, Theorem 4.5 and Remark 4.3]
), but there is a substantial difference between the walks of the discrete coalescent-walk process associated with Baxter permutations and the ones associated with semi-Baxter permutations: in
[
BM20
, Proposition 3.3.]
we proved that the walks of the discrete coalescent-walk process associated with Baxter permutations are simple random walks with a specific step distribution that is centered. On the contrary the walks of the discrete coalescent-walk process associated with semi-Baxter permutations are not even martingales (this can be checked with a simple computation using
Eq.

117
and
5.1
). This will force us to first show that
\overline{{\bm{\mathcal{Z}}}}^{(u)}_{n}
converges in distribution to a skew Brownian motion (using some arguments similar to the ones already used for strong-Baxter permutations) and then to deduce that its parameter is
1/2
, concluding that it is actually a classical Brownian motion.

In simple words, we can summarize the discussion above as follows:

•

walks of the coalescent-walk process for Baxter permutations are symmetric both in the discrete and in the continuum limit;

•

walks of the coalescent-walk process for semi-Baxter permutations are not symmetric in the discrete case but they become symmetric in the continuum limit;

•

walks of the coalescent-walk process for strong-Baxter permutations are not symmetric both in the discrete and in the continuum limit.

Appendix C
Asymptotic estimates

Proposition C.1
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
and
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be two sequences of positive numbers such that for some
\alpha>1
:

•

a_{n}\sim a\cdot n^{-\alpha}
, for some
a>0
;

•

b_{n}\sim b\cdot n^{-\alpha}
, for some
b>0
;

Then as
n\to\infty
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\cdot b_{n-k}\sim n^{-\alpha}\left(a\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}+b\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\right).

Proof.

We split the sum in four parts as follows:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}b_{n-k}=t_{n}+u_{n}+v_{n}+w_{n}\quad
, where

t_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}b_{n-k},\quad u_{n}=\sum_{k=\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{k}b_{n-k},\quad v_{n}=\sum_{k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{n-\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}b_{n-k},\quad w_{n}=\sum_{k=n-\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor+1}^{n}a_{k}b_{n-k}.

(194)

By assumption, there exists
B>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
b_{n}\leq B\cdot n^{-\alpha}
. Then

u_{n}\leq B\sum_{k=\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{k}\cdot(n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor)^{-\alpha}=o(n^{-\alpha}).

Similarly,
v_{n}=o\left(n^{-\alpha}\right)
.
It remains to show that

t_{n}\sim n^{-\alpha}\cdot b\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k},\qquad w_{n}\sim n^{-\alpha}\cdot a\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}b_{k}.

(195)

We can write

t_{n}-n^{-\alpha}\cdot b\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}=x_{n}+y_{n}+z_{n}
, where

x_{n}=b\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}\left((n-k)^{-\alpha}-n^{-\alpha}\right),\quad y_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}\left(b_{n-k}-b(n-k)^{-\alpha}\right),\quad z_{n}=-n^{-\alpha}\cdot b\sum_{k=\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor+1}^{+\infty}a_{k}.

Obviously
\ z_{n}=o\left(n^{-\alpha}\right)
. For
x_{n}
note that

0\leq x_{n}\leq b\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}\left((n-\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor)^{-\alpha}-n^{-\alpha}\right)\leq n^{-\alpha}\left(\left(1-\frac{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}{n}\right)^{-\alpha}-1\right)b\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}.

Hence
\ x_{n}=o\left(n^{-\alpha}\right)
. Finally for
y_{n}
we have that

|y_{n}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}a_{k}(n-k)^{-\alpha}\left|(n-k)^{\alpha}b_{n-k}-b\right|\leq(n-n^{\alpha})^{-\alpha}\left(\sup_{\ell\geq n-\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor}|\ell^{\alpha}b_{\ell}-b|\right)\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}.

Therefore
\ y_{n}=o\left(n^{-\alpha}\right)
. This proves that

t_{n}\sim n^{-\alpha}\cdot b\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}.

Proof.

Since

Using that by assumption

Motivic virtual signed Euler characteristics and applications to Vafa-Witten invariants
Yunfeng Jiang

Abstract
. For any scheme M M with a perfect obstruction theory, Jiang and Thomas associate a scheme N N with symmetric perfect obstruction theory. The scheme N N is a cone over M M given by the dual of the obstruction sheaf of M M , and contains M M as its zero section. Locally N N is the critical locus of a regular function. In this note we prove that N N is a d d -critical scheme in the sense of Joyce. By assuming an orientation on N N there exists a global motive for N N locally given by the motive of vanishing cycles of the local regular function. We prove a motivic localization formula under the good and circle compact ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -action for N N . When taking Euler characteristic the weighted Euler characteristic of N N weighted by the Behrend function is the signed Euler characteristic of M M by motivic method. As applications we calculate the motivic generating series of the motivic Vafa-Witten invariants for K3 surfaces. This motivic series gives the result of the χ y \chi_{y} -genus for Vafa-Witten invariants of K3 surfaces, which is the same (at instanton branch) as the K-theoretical Vafa-Witten invariants of Thomas.

Motivic Vafa-Witten theory.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface. Motived by S-duality and Vafa-Witten’s equation in
[
38
]
, Tanaka-Thomas
[
34
]
,
[
35
]
have developed a theory counting stable and semistable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
where
E
is a torsion free rank
r
coherent sheaf on
S
, and
\phi:E\to E\otimes K_{S}
is a
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-linear morphism called the Higgs field. Let
N^{\perp}_{L}:=N_{L}^{\perp}(S,c)
represent the moduli space of Gieseker stable Higgs pairs with Chern class
c=(r,c_{1},c_{2})\in H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
, fixed determinant
\det(E)=L
and trace free
\phi
. In
[
34
]
, the moduli space
N^{\perp}_{L}
is proved to be isomorphic to the moduli space of stable two dimensional torsion sheaves on
X:=\Tot(K_{S})
supported on
S
. Since
X
is a Calabi-Yau threefold (not compact), the moduli space
N^{\perp}_{L}
admits a symmetric obstruction theory in
[
1
]
. Therefore there exists a virtual fundamental class
[N^{\perp}_{L}]^{\mbox{\tiny virt}}\in H_{0}(N^{\perp}_{L})
. The space
N^{\perp}_{L}
is not compact, but admits a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action scaling the Higgs field and the fixed locus is compact. The Vafa-Witten invariants are defined by virtual localization

\VW_{c}(S)=\int_{[(N^{\perp}_{L})^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}]^{\mbox{\tiny virt}}}\frac{1}{e(N^{\mbox{\tiny virt}})}

where
e(N^{\mbox{\tiny virt}})
is the Euler class of the virtual normal bundle. These invariants are called the
\SU(r)
-Vafa-Witten invariants.
On the other hand the following invariants

\vw_{c}(S)=\chi(N^{\perp}_{L},\nu_{N})

are defined as Behrend’s weighted Euler characteristic of
N^{\perp}_{L}
weighted by the Behrend function

\nu_{N}:N^{\perp}_{L}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. These two invariants
\VW(S)
and
\vw(S)
are not the same for general surfaces, especially for general type surfaces, but for surfaces with
K_{S}<0
and K3 surfaces,
\VW_{c}(S)=\vw_{c}(S)
, see
[
34
]
,
[
29
]
.

From a general theory in
[
23
]
, the moduli space
N^{\perp}_{L}
admits an orientation
K_{N^{\perp}_{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Let
\mathscr{MF}_{N^{\perp}_{L}}^{\phi}
be the global motive on
N^{\perp}_{L}
obtained by gluing the local motive of vanishing cycles of the local function. The motivic Vafa-Witten invariants are defined as:

Definition 1.2
.

\vw^{M}_{c}(S)=\int_{N^{\perp}_{L}}[\mathscr{MF}_{N^{\perp}_{L}}^{\phi}].

In
[
36
]
, R. Thomas defined the K-theoretical Vafa-Witten invariants using the virtual structure sheaf and an orientation of the moduli spaces. His invariants are the K-theoretical refinement of the invariants
\VW(S)
.
Our motivic Vafa-Witten invariants are only the motivic refinement of the invariants
\vw(S)
. They give the same refinement for K3 surfaces.

The locus
N\subset N^{\perp}_{L}
consisting of Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
such that the corresponding
E
is stable is a quasi-projective subscheme of
N^{\perp}_{L}
. The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed locus of
N
is just
M:=M_{L}(S,c)
, which is the moduli space of stable torsion free coherent sheaves with topological invariants
c
. This fixed component is the Instanton Branch of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed locus of
N_{L}^{\perp}
, see
[
34
]
.
The space
N\to M
is an affine cone over
M
, and is just the situation in Theorem
1.1
. Thus Theorem
1.1
gives a calculation of the motivic Vafa-Witten invariants.

Let
\mathscr{MF}_{N}^{\phi}:=\mathscr{MF}_{N^{\perp}_{L}}^{\phi}|_{N}
be the restriction of the global motive on
N
. In this paper we calculate the motivic Vafa-Witten invariants for K3 surfaces. We fix in
c_{0}=(r,c_{1},c_{2})
the rank
r
and
c_{1}
are coprime, so that the moduli space

M_{L}(S,c_{0})
is an irreducible smooth variety. We form the generating series

\vw^{M}_{r,c_{1}}(S)=\sum_{c_{2}}\vw^{M}_{(r,c_{1},c_{2})}(S)q^{c_{2}}.

We have the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective K3 surface. Fix the rank
r
and the first Chern class
c_{1}
such that they are coprime.
The motivic generating series of motivic Vafa-Witten invariants is given by:

\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{M}(S)=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}e^{\pi i\frac{r-1}{r}jc_{1}^{2}}\cdot\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}[S]}.

We prove Theorem
1.3
using Theorem
1.1
, and reduce the calculation to the general motive of
M
. From Yoshioka
[
39
]
,
M
is birational equivalent to the Hilbert scheme of points on
S
. Everything is reduced to the calculation of the motive of the Hilbert scheme of points on
S
, and then we use Göttsche’s result in
[
9
]
.

Recall that there is a ring homomorphism
e:K_{0}(\Var_{{\mathbb{C}}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}[u,v]
from the Grothendieck ring of varieties to
{\mathbb{Z}}[u,v]
given by the Hodge-Deligne polynomial of varieties. The
\chi_{y}
-genus is just

, i.e.,

. If we define

\vw^{\chi}_{r,c_{1}}(S)=\sum_{c_{2}}\vw^{\chi}_{(r,c_{1},c_{2})}(S)q^{c_{2}}

as the generating series of the

-genus of the Vafa-Witten invariants, where

\vw^{\chi}_{(r,c_{1},c_{2})}(S):=\chi_{y}(M_{L}(S,(r,c_{1},c_{2}))).

As a corollary of Theorem

we calculate the

Corollary 1.4

Let

be a smooth projective K3 surface. Fix the rank

and the first Chern class

Then the
\chi_{y}
-genus of the Vafa-Witten invariants is given by:

\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{\chi}(S)=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}e^{\pi i\frac{r-1}{r}jc_{1}^{2}}\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}},y)^{-1},

where

\widetilde{\Delta}(q,y):=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-q^{n}\right)^{20}\left(1-yq^{n}\right)^{2}\left(1-y^{-1}q^{n}\right)^{2}.

Thus we get similar result as in
[
36
]
where R. Thomas uses K-theoretical Vafa-Witten invariants to calculate the case of K3 surfaces, and also prove Conjecture 4.6 in
[
10
]
using motivic method.

Discussion.
Our motivic series of the Vafa-Witten invariants give the same result as in
[
36
]
for K3 surfaces, since for a K3 surface
S
,
\VW(S)=\vw(S)
. As pointed out in
[
34
]
, the big Vafa-Witten invariants
\VW(S)
are the correct invariants for the S-duality. It is especially interesting for general type surfaces.

Vafa-Witten actually predicted an S-transformation formula from the gauge group
\SU(r)
to its Langlands dual
\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
. In the case of K3 surfaces, the prime rank case is proved in
[
17
]
, and higher rank case is studied in
[
20
]
. The method we use is moduli space of optimal gerbe twisted sheaves on K3 surfaces. The K-theoretical and elliptic genus version of the S-duality conjecture for K3 surfaces are proved in
[
21
]
. The K-theoretical refinement
version of the S-duality implies an S-duality transformation for motivic Vafa-Witten invariants.

5.1.
Power structure on motivic ring

We recall the power structure on the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\Var_{\kappa})
following
[
12
]

Let
R
be a ring. A power structure on
R
is a map

(1+qR[\![q]\!])\times R\to 1+qR[\![q]\!])

by

(A(t),m)\mapsto(A(q))^{m}

which satisfies the properties:
A(q)^{0}=1
,
A(q)^{1}=A(q)
,
A(q)^{m+n}=A(q)^{m}A(q)^{n}
,
A(q)^{mn}=(A(q)^{m})^{n}
,

A(q)^{m}B(q)^{m}=(A(q)B(q))^{m}
, and
(1+q)^{m}=1+mq+O(q^{2})
.
From
[
12
]
, there exists a power structure on the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\Var_{{\mathbb{C}}})
, defining uniquely by the property
that for a variety
X

(1-q)^{-[X]}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[\Sym^{k}(X)]q^{k}

is the motivic zeta function.

Also from
[
12
]
, we have the exponential map

(5.1)

\Exp:q\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}[\![q]\!]\to 1+q\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}[\![q]\!]

by

\Exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[A_{n}]q^{n}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{-[A_{n}]}.

This equation satisfies the substitution rule:

(5.2)

\Exp(A(q))|_{q\mapsto(-{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{n}q}=\Exp\left(A\left((-{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{n}q\right)\right).

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
-Calculations for K3 surfaces

Let
S
be a smooth projective K3 surface. In this section we perform a calculation for the motivic invariants for
S
.
In this case
X=S\times{\mathbb{C}}
, and any
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed Higgs pair
(E,\phi)
has vanishing Higgs field
\phi=0
. Therefore the moduli space
N=N_{L}^{\perp}\to M
is a cone over the moduli space of stable sheaves
M
. We only have instanton branch.

Fix a topological invariant
c_{0}=(r,c_{1},c_{2})\in H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
(r,c_{1})=1
. Then semistablity coincides with stability and the moduli space
M=M_{L}(S,c_{0})
is an irreducible symplectic variety which is birational equivalent to the Hilbert scheme

\Hilb^{d}(S)
of point on
S
, see
[
39
]
. Here

d=1-\chi_{S}(c_{0},c_{0}).

Sine the moduli space
M=M_{L}(S,c_{0})
is smooth, its virtual dimension is the dimension

\vd=2-\chi_{S}(c_{0},c_{0}).

Thus

\int_{N}[\mathscr{MF}_{N}^{\phi}]={\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}\chi_{S}(c_{0},c_{0})-1}\int_{M}[M].

We calculated

d=1+r(c_{2}-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}.

Thus we write down the motivic generating series as:

(5.7)

\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{M}(S)=\sum_{c_{2}}[\mathscr{MF}_{N}^{\phi}]q^{c_{2}}=\sum_{c_{2}}{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{1}{2}\chi_{S}(c_{0},c_{0})-1}[\Hilb^{d}(S)]q^{c_{2}},

We calculate the motivic series (write
c_{2}
as integers
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
):

\displaystyle\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{M}(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{L}}^{-(1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1})}[\Hilb^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}(S)]q^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{L}}^{-(1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1})}[\Hilb^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}(S)]\cdot\left(q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}\left(q\right)^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\Hilb^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}(S)]\left({\mathbb{L}}^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}

Now using the exponential form, see
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\Hilb^{m}(S)]q^{m}=\Exp\left(\frac{[S]q}{1-{\mathbb{L}}q}\right)

and therefore

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{L}}^{m}[\Hilb^{m}(S)]q^{m}=\Exp\left(\frac{[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}q}{1-{\mathbb{L}}^{2}q}\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{M}(S)

\displaystyle=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\Hilb^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}(S)]\left({\mathbb{L}}^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{1+r(m-r)+\frac{1-r}{2}c^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}e^{\pi i\frac{r-1}{r}jc_{1}^{2}}\cdot\Exp\left(\frac{[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}\right).

We also can write it as the infinite product form. From (
5.1
), and since

\displaystyle\frac{[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}})^{n+1}\cdot(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}[S]\cdot(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}})^{n}.

Therefore

\Exp\left(\frac{[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}[S]}.

Then

\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{M}(S)=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}e^{\pi i\frac{r-1}{r}jc_{1}^{2}}\cdot\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}[S]}.

\square

5.4.
Proof of the
\chi_{y}
-genus in Corollary
1.4

From
[
12
]
, there exists a ring homomorphism

e:K_{0}(\Var_{{\mathbb{C}}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}[u,v]

given by sending each variety
X
to its Hodge-Deligne polynomial

X\mapsto e_{X}(u,v)=\sum_{i,j}h^{ij}(X)(-u)^{i}(-v)^{j}.

The
\chi_{y}
-genus is just
e_{X}(y,1)
.
The
\chi_{y}
-polynomial of the K3 surface
S
is
\chi_{y}(S)=2+20y+2y^{2}
.
For the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb^{m}(S)
, from the properties the ring homomorphism
in
[
12
, Theorem 2]

we have

\displaystyle\chi_{y}\left(\Exp\left(\frac{[S]\cdot{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\chi_{y}\left(\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-{\mathbb{L}}^{-1}[S]}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-y^{-1}(2+20y+2y^{2})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-20}\left(1-y\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-2}\left(1-y^{-1}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{n}\right)^{-2}.

We define

\widetilde{\Delta}(q,y):=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left(1-q^{n}\right)^{20}\left(1-yq^{n}\right)^{2}\left(1-y^{-1}q^{n}\right)^{2}

Then we get the
\chi_{y}
-genus of the Vafa-Witten invariants:

\vw_{r,c_{1}}^{\chi_{y}}(S)=q^{r-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1-r}{2r}c_{1}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}e^{\pi i\frac{r-1}{r}jc_{1}^{2}}\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{r}}q^{\frac{1}{r}},y)^{-1}.

Note that this result is similar to
[
36
, Theorem 5.48]
, where Thomas got similar results using K-theoretical Vafa-Witten invariants.

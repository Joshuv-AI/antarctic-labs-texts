A Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for higher rank groups
Yujiao Jiang, Guangshi Lü, Jesse Thorner, Zihao Wang

Abstract
. We establish a result of Bombieri–Vinogradov type for the Dirichlet coefficients at prime ideals of the standard L L -function associated to a self-dual cuspidal automorphic representation π \pi of GL n \operatorname{GL}_{n} over a number field F F which is not a quadratic twist of itself. Our result does not rely on any unproven progress towards the generalized Ramanujan conjecture or the nonexistence of Landau–Siegel zeros. In particular, when π \pi is fixed and not equal to a quadratic twist of itself, we prove the first unconditional Siegel-type lower bound for the twisted L L -values | L ⁡ ( 1 , π ⊗ χ ) | |L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)| in the χ \chi -aspect, where χ \chi is a primitive quadratic Hecke character over F F . Our result improves the levels of distribution in other works that relied on these unproven hypotheses. As applications, when n = 2 , 3 , 4 n=2,3,4 , we prove a GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} analogue of the Titchmarsh divisor problem and a nontrivial bound for a certain GL n × GL 2 \mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2} shifted convolution sum.

1.
Introduction

The distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions attracts a lot of attention among mathematicians. Let
a,q
be two integers such that
(a,q)=1
. We denote by
\pi(x)
the number of primes
p\leqslant x
and by
\pi(x;q,a)
the number of primes
p\leqslant x
satisfying
p\equiv a\,(\Mod{q})
. Dirichlet’s theorem indicates the following

\pi(x;q,a)\sim\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)},

where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function. Later, after Siegel’s result on the location of exceptional zero of Dirichlet
L
-functions, Walfisz proved that for all
\alpha>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
c_{\alpha}>0
such that if
q\leqslant(\log x)^{\alpha}
, then

\pi(x;q,a)=\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O\big(x\exp(-{c_{\alpha}}(\log x)^{1/2})\big).

When the modulus
q
gets larger, this problem becomes much more difficult. If the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) holds, then

\pi(x;q,a)=\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O\big(x^{\frac{1}{2}}\log qx\big)

holds for
q\geqslant x^{1/2-\varepsilon}
. However, such a hypothesis is very far from being proved.

The celebrated Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem in some sense shows that GRH holds on average. To be precise, let
A
be any positive real number, there exists
B=B(A)>0
such that for
Q\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log x)^{-B}
,

(1.1)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\max_{(a,q)=1}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\pi(y,q,a)-\frac{\pi(y)}{\varphi(q)}\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

This can be viewed as a fine substitute for the GRH in many applications. The theorem was originally proved using zero density estimates. After the work of Bombieri and Vinogradov, different proofs of this theorem are given by Gallagher
[
8
]
and Vaughan
[
33
]
.

There are a lot of higher-rank analogues of the classical Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem. Firstly, by means of Gallagher’s method, Grupp
[
10
]
obtained under a certain condition concerning Siegel’s zeros of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
automorphic
L
-functions,

\sum_{q\leqslant x^{2/9}(\log x)^{-B}}\max_{(a,q)=1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
n\equiv a\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\tau(m)m^{-\frac{11}{2}}\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
\Lambda(m)
is the von Mangoldt function and
\tau(m)
is the Ramanujan
\tau
-function. Later, Perelli
[
26
]
used the generalized Vaughan identity for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
automorphic
L
-functions and unconditionally proved the mean-value theorem with a level of distribution
2/5
instead of
2/9
.
Actually, Perelli’s approach still works for any holomorphic cusp form on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Recently, Acharya
[
1
]
and the first two authors
[
17
]
improved independently the level to
1/2
for any holomorphic or Maass cusp form on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. For any automorphic form
\pi
on higher-rank group
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
with
n\geqslant 3
, let
\lambda_{\pi}(m)
to be the
m
-th Dirichlet coefficient of the associated
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
, one can also show a result of Bombieri–Vinogradov type

(1.2)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\max_{(a,q)=1}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant x\\
m\equiv a\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\lambda_{\pi}(m)\Big|\ll_{A,\pi}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

For instance, the first two authors
[
17
]
established (
1.2
) with
Q=x^{\frac{2}{n+1}}(\log x)^{-B}
under the generalized Ramanujan conjecture (GRC) and a certain condition concerning Siegel’s zeros of the twisted
L
-functions
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
. Wong
[
34
]
showed (
1.2
) with
Q=x^{\min\{\frac{1}{n-2},\frac{1}{2}\}-\varepsilon}
under two similar conditions. The main tools of Jiang and Lü are the generalized Vaughan identity and the distribution of
\lambda_{\pi}(m)
in arithmetic progressions, while that of Wong is Gallagher’s technique as in
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we will explore further the possibility of Vaughan’s method and show an unconditional result for higher-rank groups in a number field. We refer the reader to Section 2 for the detailed introduction to the notation. Let
\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the ring of adeles over a number field
F
, and let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with unitary central character, normalized such that the central character is trivial on the diagonally embedded copy of the positive reals. Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
,
let
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
be the conductor of
\pi
,
L(s,\pi)
be the associated standard
L
-function, and
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient representation. We write
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
to be the
\mathfrak{n}
-th Dirichlet coefficient of
L(s,\pi)
, where
\mathfrak{n}
is an integral ideal in
F
.
Let
\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
to be the numerical norm.
As the classical Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem (
1.1
), we will consider estimates of large sieve type associated to
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
with certain congruence condition. We denote by
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
the narrow class group modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
h(\mathfrak{m})
be the cardinality of
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
and
\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}(1-\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-1})
.

Our arguments require that if
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, then
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
. This self-duality implies that
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\mathfrak{n}
. Also, we require that for all
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}
and all nontrivial primitive quadratic Hecke characters of
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
, we have
\pi\neq\pi\otimes\chi
. We let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
denote the set of all
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
satisfying these two hypotheses. We prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
. If
A>0
,
B=2^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}}(6A+12n+34)+2n-4
, and
\eta=\max\{2,\frac{n}{2}\}
, then

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
x\geqslant 2
and
Q=x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}
. The implied constant is ineffective.

Remark
.

1.

The congruence condition “
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
” is defined in Section
2.4
. This generalizes the usual notion of congruences on the integers to the integral ideals of
F
. In particular, if
F=\mathbb{Q}
, then the bound in Theorem
1.1
becomes

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\max_{\gcd(a,q)=1}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant y\\
p\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
p
(resp.
a
and
q
) are rational primes (resp. rational integers).

2.

If we adjust Theorem
1.1
so that we sum over
\mathfrak{m}
satisfying
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
, then we may obtain a similar result with the same level of distribution. Our result would then hold for all self-dual
\pi
since the condition
\pi\neq\pi\otimes\chi
automatically holds.

3.

The weight
\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}
is introduced by Huxley in
[
14
]
to cancel the contribution coming from the unit group (see (
2.11
)).

4.

Note that the analogue of Elliott-Halberstam conjecture will predict that
\eta=1+\varepsilon
, and the GRH for automorphic
L
-functions will trivially give that
\eta=2
. Since the arithmetic conductor of
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
might be quite large, it is hard to achieve any of them by our argument.

To handle the contribution when
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}
is smaller than a power of
\log x
, we need to prove an analogue of the Siegel–Walfisz theorem for the Dirichlet coefficients of
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)
. One of the novelties in our work which allows us to prove such a result without recourse to unproven hypotheses is a new Siegel-type lower bound for
|L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)|
when
\chi
is a primitive quadratic Hecke character and
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
(not necessarily self-dual) is not a quadratic twist of itself.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, and suppose that
\pi\neq\pi\otimes\nu
for all primitive quadratic Hecke characters
\nu
. Let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
be a primitive quadratic Hecke character. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
c_{\pi}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)>0
such that
|L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)|\geqslant c_{\pi}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}^{-\varepsilon}
.

Remark
.

Over
\mathbb{Q}
, Theorem
1.2
was claimed by Molteni in his PhD thesis, but there is a serious deficiency in his argument. Since this deficiency has occurred in several different papers (even before Molteni’s), we detail the deficiency and address it in Section
4
.

To handle the contribution when
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}
is larger than a power of
\log x
, we require a modification of Vaughan’s approach to the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem. The problem of estimating

\sideset{}{}{\sum}_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant X}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\Big|

is equivalent to that of handling

\sideset{}{}{\sum}_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

with a harmless error, where
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
is the coefficient of
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)
. We derive a generalized Vaughan identity, which gives an expression for
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
, and then apply it to decompose the above object into the Type I sum

(1.3)

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

and the Type II sum

(1.4)

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|{\underset{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}\leqslant y\atop\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})\Big|.

Note that (
1.4
) is actually bilinear form with
L,N
in suitable ranges, and
a(\mathfrak{n}),b(\mathfrak{n})
are arithmetic functions related to
\pi
.

In our setting, a strong bound for (
1.3
) is already new. Since it is useful in contexts beyond that of Theorem
1.1
, we state it as its own theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. If
A>0
,
B=2^{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]/4}(2A+16)+2n-5
, and
\eta=\max\{\frac{n}{2},2\}
, then

\sideset{}{}{\sum}_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|\ll_{\pi}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
x\geqslant 2
and
Q=x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}
.

Remark
.

The value of
\eta
in Theorem
1.1
is totally determined by the value of
\eta
in Theorem
1.3
. We improve previous results because we notice that more cancellation can be obtained by summing over the modulus, which is absent in the work of
[
17
,
33
]
. In fact, Vaughan
[
33
]
directly used the Pólya-Vinogradov inequality, and the first two authors
[
17
]
used the Voronoï formula on
{\rm GL}(n)
to treat the sum of
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
over a single arithmetic progression.

We estimate (
1.4
) through bilinear sum methods, proving a general result similar to
[
16
, Theorem 17.4]
. However, the important condition of
[
16
, Theorem 17.4]
is that one of these two arithmetic functions
a(\mathfrak{n})
and
b(\mathfrak{n})
satisfies a Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis. In our situation, we need to verify that both
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
and
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
satisfy a Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis. This hypothesis is straightforward to verify for
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
, and as mentioned above, we verify this hypothesis for
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
as a corollary of Theorem
1.2
. Note that there is a cumbersome cut-off condition in (
1.4
). To handle this, we adopt a trick of Vaughan in
[
33
]
.

Our upper bound for (
1.4
) involves the second moments of some arithmetic functions of length
x
, whose magnitudes need to be of order
O(x(\log x)^{c})
for some computable constant
c
. If GRC holds for
\pi
, then the desired upper bound follows from elementary estimate of divisor functions. In
[
17
]
, the first two authors bounded these arithmetic functions under Hypothesis H of Rudnick and Sarnak
[
27
]
. This mild conjecture is implied by GRC and is only known to hold for few cases. In order to circumvent this additional assumption, we instead bound them by some Dirichlet convolutions of
\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})
through the dual Pieri rule and a combinatorial lemma of Soundrarajan. The desired upper bound then follows from the Rankin–Selberg theory.

As in the classical case, Theorem
1.1
is a fruitful result. As an application, we will give one analogue of Titchmarsh’s divisor problem on
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with
2\leqslant n\leqslant 4
. Let
d(m)
be the usual divisor function. It is known that
d(m)
are Fourier coefficients of
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}E(z,s)
at
s=\frac{1}{2}
, where
E(z,s)
is the Eisenstein series for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Thus, the following result may be also viewed as the shifted convolution sum at primes for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant 4
, and fix
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
. If
x\geqslant 2
and
\pi
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)d(p-1)\ll_{\pi}\frac{x(\log\log x)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\log x}},

where the implied constant depends on
\pi
.

Remark
.

The case with
n=2
is known by the work of Acharya
[
1
]
. Under GRC, the first two authors
[
17
]
handled the cases with
n=2,3
and obtained a stronger upper bound than that in Corollary
1.4
.

If we use Theorem
1.3
instead of Theorem
1.1
, then the argument leading to Corollary
1.4
produces a corresponding shifted convolution bound over the integers.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant 4
, and fix
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
. If
x\geqslant 2
and
\pi
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then

\sum_{m\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(m)d(m-1)\ll_{\pi}x(\log\log x)^{\frac{3}{2}},

where the implied constant depends on
\pi
.

Finally, we show that Corollary
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
do in fact provide non-trivial estimates. We first recall an elementary result (see
[
17
, page 937]
, for example)

(1.5)

\sum_{q\leqslant x}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}\log x+O(1).

Assuming the Riemann hypothesis for all of the twisted
L
-functions
L(s,\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi))
as well as GRC, it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 5.15]
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\log p=\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}+O(x^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log qx)^{2}).

We average over the modulus
q\leqslant x^{1/3}
and obtain from (
1.5
) and partial summation that

\sum_{q\leqslant x^{1/3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\asymp x.

One can easily verify that
|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|\gg|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}
under GRC, so the above estimate gives

\sum_{p\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|d(p-1)\gg\sum_{p\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}d(p-1)\gg\sum_{q\leqslant x^{1/3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\gg x.

This means that there exists some cancellation in the sequence
\{\lambda_{\pi}(p)d(p-1)\}
, where
p
runs over all primes.

For Corollary
1.5
, we argue as follows. Firstly, we recall an interesting result in
[
32
]
: “
Let a multiplicative function
f(m)\geqslant 0
satisfy the following conditions: (i)
f(m)\geqslant 0
;
f(p^{r})\leqslant A^{r}
for some
A>0
; (ii)
f(m)\ll m^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
; (iii)
\sum_{p\leqslant x}f(p)\log p\geqslant\alpha x
with some
\alpha>0
, then one has the asymptotic formula

\sum_{m\leqslant x}f(m)d(m-1)=C_{f}\sum_{m\leqslant x}f(m)\log x(1+o(1))

for some constant
C_{f}
depending on
f
.
” Next, suppose that GRC holds, we then obtain from
[
18
, p. 595]
that

(1.6)

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|\log p\geqslant\Big(\frac{1}{m}+o(1)\Big)x,

and

(1.7)

\sum_{m\leqslant x}\left|\lambda_{\pi}(m)\right|\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}.

One can easily check that with the help of (
1.6
), the above conditions (i)-(iii) hold for
f(m)=|\lambda_{\pi}(m)|
under GRC. Hence, we could get

(1.8)

\sum_{m\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(m)|d(m-1)=C\sum_{m\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(m)|\log x(1+o(1))

for some constant
C
depending on
\pi
. Combining (
1.7
) with (
1.8
), we have

\sum_{m\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(m)|d(m-1)\gg x(\log x)^{\frac{1}{n}},

which implies that Corollary
1.5
do give a non-trivial upper bound.

2.
Properties of
L
-functions

2.1.
Conventions

For a parameter
\delta
, we use the notation
f\ll_{\delta}g
or
f=O_{\delta}(g)
to denote that there exists a constant
c\geqslant 0
, depending at most on
\delta
and
\pi
, such that such that
|f|\leqslant cg
in a range that will be clear in context.

2.2.
Automorphic
L
-functions

Let
F
be a number field with discriminant
D_{F}
and
d=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be
the ring of integers in
F
. For each place
v
of
F
, denote by
F_{v}
the completion of

F
with respect to
v
and by
O_{v}
the local ring of integers. The prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}
and the nonarchimedean places
v
are in bijective correspondence. So we may write
\mathfrak{p}
interchangeably with nonarchimedean places. Each
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
is a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
such that
\pi_{v}
is unramified for almost all finite places
v
. Let
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
be the conductor of
\pi
, which has the property that
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is ramified if and only if
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
.

For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, the standard local
L
-function is defined in terms of Satake parameters
A_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=\{\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p}),\dots,\alpha_{n,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
by

(2.1)

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1},

where
\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
is the absolute norm over
\mathbb{Q}
. For
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
, we have
\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. However, it might be the case that
\alpha_{i,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=0
for some
j
when
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
. The standard (finite)
L
-function is defined to be

(2.2)

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>1
, where the product is over all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
and the sum is over all integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}
. We can see that
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
is multiplicative, that is
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n}_{1}\mathfrak{n}_{2})=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n}_{1})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n}_{2})
for coprime integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. We can also write
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
in terms of Satake parameters

\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})=\sum_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}=k}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,\pi}^{m_{j}}(\mathfrak{p})

and extend it to all integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}
by multiplicativity. Taking logarithmic derivatives in (
2.2
), we can see that for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>1
,

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}^{ks}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}},

where

\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\;\;\text{ if }\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{p}^{k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
&0\,\;\;\;\quad\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

and
a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{k}
. We set
a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})=0
if
\mathfrak{n}
is not a prime ideal power. Note that
a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
. We write
\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
to be the coefficients of Dirichlet series
L(s,\pi)^{-1}
, namely

(2.3)

L(s,\pi)^{-1}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>1
. Then it can be is given by

(2.4)

\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}|\mathfrak{n}$ for some prime $\mathfrak{p}$,}\\
\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}\|\mathfrak{n}\\
\ell\leqslant n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\sum\limits_{1\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{\ell}\leqslant n}\alpha_{j_{1},\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\cdots\alpha_{j_{\ell},\pi}(\mathfrak{p})&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
is multiplicative.

Now suppose
v
is an archimedean place of
F
(denoted
v|\infty
), so
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Denote
\Gamma(s)
to be the usual gamma function and define

\Gamma_{v}(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\text{ if }F_{v}=\mathbb{R},\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\text{ if }F_{v}=\mathbb{C}.\end{array}\right.

For each archimedean place
v
, there exists
n
Langlands parameters
\mu_{1,\pi}(v),\ldots,\mu_{n,\pi}(v)
from which we define

L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)).

If we denote

L_{\infty}(s,\pi)=\prod_{v|\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),

then for nontrivial
\pi
, the complete
L
-function defined by

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}L(s,\pi)L_{\infty}(s,\pi)

extends to an entire function of order 1 and is bounded in the vertical strip.
Luo, Rudnick, and Sarnak
[
21
]
and Müller and Speh
[
25
]
proved that there exists
\theta_{n}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}]
such that we have the uniform bounds

(2.5)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})|\leqslant{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta_{n}}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))\leqslant\theta_{n}.

The generalized Ramanujan conjecture (GRC) predicts that
\theta_{n}=0
.

We denote by
\widetilde{\pi}
the contragradient representation of
\pi
which is also an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation with unitary central character. One can show that
\mathfrak{q}_{\widetilde{\pi}}=\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
. We can also define the
L
-function associated to
\widetilde{\pi}
in the same fashion. We have the equalities of sets
\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})}\}_{j=1}^{n}=\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p})\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{\overline{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)}\}_{j=1}^{n}=\{\mu_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}_{j=1}^{n}
. There exists a complex number
\varepsilon(\pi)
of modulus 1 such that

\Lambda(s,\pi)=\varepsilon(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}).

Now we define the analytic conductor of
\pi
. We write
q(\pi):=D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
for the arithmetic conductor, and the analytic conductor is defined by

C(\pi,t):=q(\pi)\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(3+|it+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)|^{d_{v}}):=q(\pi)q_{\infty}(\pi,t),

where
d_{v}=1
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
and
d_{v}=2
if
F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
This is an important parameter to describe
L(s,\pi)
. For example, the convexity bound, the zero-free region and second moment estimates can be described in terms of analytic conductor (see sections below).

2.3.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi=\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}=\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. The Rankin–Selberg
L
-function at a finite place
\mathfrak{p}
is defined to be

(2.6)

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1},

where
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})
are suitable complex numbers. For a finite place
\mathfrak{p}
such that either
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
or
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
is unramified, we have the equality of sets
\{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}=\{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\}
. We also define the (finite) Rankin–Selberg
L
-function to be

(2.7)

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>1
, where the product is over all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
and the sum is over nonzero integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}
.

For each archimedean place
v
, the local
L
-factor at
v
is

L(s,\pi_{v}\times\pi_{v}^{\prime})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v))

for suitable complex numbers
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
. Define

L_{\infty}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{v|\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}\times\pi_{v}^{\prime}).

When
v
is a place such that both
\pi_{v}
and
\pi_{v}^{\prime}
are unramified, then we have the equality of sets
\{\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)\}=\{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)+\mu_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(v)\}
.
By our normalization of the central characters, we have
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
has a pole at
s=1
with order
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=1
if and only if
\pi^{\prime}\simeq\widetilde{\pi}
, and
r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=0
otherwise. We can also associate an arithmetic conductor
q(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
to
\pi\times\pi^{\prime}
, so the complete Rankin–Selberg
L
-function is defined by

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}):=(s(s-1))^{r_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}q(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\frac{s}{2}}L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})L_{\infty}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}).

It is entire of order 1 and satisfies the following functional equation

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\varepsilon(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}),

where
\varepsilon(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is a complex number of modulus 1. It follows from the explicit description of the numbers
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
in
[
30
]
and
[
30
, Appendix]
yields the bounds

(2.8)

|\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})|\leqslant{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}^{\theta_{n}+\theta_{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v))\leqslant\theta_{n}+\theta_{n^{\prime}}.

We also define the analytic conductor
C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)
by

C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)=q(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\prod_{v|\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}(3+|it+\mu_{i,j,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)|^{d_{v}}):=q(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})q_{\infty}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)

for
d_{v}
as above. An important inequality about conductors (see
[
3
]
) is

(2.9)

C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)\ll C(\pi,0)^{n^{\prime}}C(\pi^{\prime},0)^{n}(3+|t|)^{nn^{\prime}d}.

We are especially interested in the case where
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
. In this case the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
has non-negative Dirichlet coefficients
\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})
(see Lemma
5.2
for instance). Moreover,
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
extends to the complex plane with a simple pole at
s=1
. Hence, it follows from a standard Tauberian argument that

(2.10)

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})\sim x\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})\ll x.

2.4.
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists

Let
F
be a number field. By a modulus
m
of
F
, we mean a function

m:\{\text{all places of }F\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

such that

(1)

for all nonarchimedean places
v
, we have
m(v)\geqslant 0
, with
m(v)=0
for all but finitely many
v
.

(2)

if
v
is a real archimedean place, then
m(v)=0
or 1.

(3)

if
v
is a complex archimedean palce, then
m(v)=0
.

For a modulus
m
, we write

U_{m}(v):=\begin{cases}(F_{v}^{\times})^{m(v)+1}&\mbox{if $v$ is archimedean,}\\
1+v^{m(v)}&\mbox{if $v$ is nonarchimedean and $m(v)\neq 0$,}\\
O_{v}^{\times}&\mbox{if $v$ is nonarchimedean and $m(v)=0$.}\end{cases}

Thus, in each case,
U_{m}(v)
is a neighbourhood of 1 in
F_{v}^{\times}
. Note that
m(v)=0
for all but finitely many nonarchimedean
v
, so

U_{m}:=\prod_{v}U_{m}(v)

is an open subset of the idele group
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
, where the product is over all places
v
of
F
. For any modulus
m
, we can define the ray class group modulo
m
to be
\mathrm{Cl}(m):=\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times}U_{m}
. By a narrow class group modulo an integral ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, we mean that it is defined by the modulus

m_{\mathfrak{a}}(v):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{ord}_{v}(\mathfrak{a})&\mbox{if $v$ is nonarchimedean},\\
1&\mbox{if $v$ is real archimedean,}\\
0&\mbox{if $v$ is complex archimedean.}\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{ord}_{v}
is the additive valuation with respect to
v
. We define
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{a}):=\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times}U_{m_{\mathfrak{a}}}
, which is a finite group, and whose cardinality is denoted by
h(\mathfrak{a})
. Later we may also use
\mathfrak{a}
to denote this modulus for the simplicity of notation. If
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
, one can use the map
\mathfrak{b}\mapsto\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{b})}\times\prod_{v|\infty}1\mod F^{\times}U_{m_{\mathfrak{a}}}
to projects
\mathfrak{b}
to
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{a})
, where
\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is any fixed choice of uniformizer in
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
. So by “
\mathfrak{b}\equiv\mathfrak{c}
in
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{a})
”, we mean that both
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{c}
are coprime with
\mathfrak{a}
and they have the same image under this map.

One may also define the ray class group in terms of ideals. Let
J_{F}
be the group of fractional ideals in
F
. If
S
is a finite set of prime ideals in
F
, we denote by
J_{F}^{S}
the subgroup of
J
generated by the prime ideals not in
S
. Define

F^{S}=\{x\in F:(x)\in J_{F}^{S}\}=\{x\in F:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=0\text{ for all finite }\mathfrak{p}\in S\}.

Given a modulus
m
, we denote by
F_{m,1}
the set consisting of elements
a\in F^{\times}
satisfying

\Big\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{ord}_{v}(a-1)&\geqslant m(v)&&\,\,\text{all nonarchimedean $v$ with $m(v)>0$,}\\
a_{v}&>0&&\text{ all real archimedean $v$ with $m(v)>0$,}\end{aligned}

where
a_{v}
is the image of
a
in
F_{v}
.
If
S(m)=\{\text{prime ideals }\mathfrak{p}\colon m(\mathfrak{p})>0\}
, then the ray class group modulo
m
can also be defined by
J^{S(m)}/F_{m,1}
. By
[
22
, Theorem 1.7, Chapter 5]
, we have the following exact sequence

(2.11)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\cap F_{m,1})\rightarrow F^{S(m)}/F_{m,1}\rightarrow\mathrm{Cl}(m)\rightarrow\mathrm{Cl}_{F}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathrm{Cl}_{F}
is the class group of
F
. Moreover, we have the following isomorphism

F^{S(m)}/F_{m,1}\simeq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v|\infty\text{ real}\\
m(v)>0\end{subarray}}\{\pm 1\}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\\
m(\mathfrak{p})>0\end{subarray}}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}^{m(\mathfrak{p})})^{\times}.

As a result, if we define
\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}(1-\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}})
to be Euler’s totient function in
F
, then

h(\mathfrak{m})=h\cdot 2^{r}\cdot\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\cap F_{m_{\mathfrak{m}},1})|^{-1},

where
h
is the class number of
F
and
r
is the number of real embeddings of
F
. One can show that
\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\gg\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}/\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}
.

For any character
\chi
on
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{a})
, there is a unitary Hecke character which is also denoted by
\chi=\prod_{v}\chi_{v}
such that
\chi(\mathfrak{p})=\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})
if
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\chi}
. One can see that the conductor of
\chi
divides
\mathfrak{a}
. We say that
\chi
is primitive modulo
\mathfrak{a}
if
\mathfrak{q}_{\chi}=\mathfrak{a}
. Now, for any
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, one has
\pi\otimes\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. By
[
5
]
, the standard
L
-function associated with
\pi\otimes\chi
equals

L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}.

For a prime
\mathfrak{p}\nmid(\mathfrak{q}_{\pi},\mathfrak{q}_{\chi})
, we have
\{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\}=\{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\alpha_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})\}
. Recall that
\alpha_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p})=\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})
for any uniformizer
\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
if
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\chi}
.
We set
\chi(\mathfrak{p})=0
for
\mathfrak{p|\mathfrak{q}_{\chi}}
, then by discussion above we have

(2.12)

\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\quad\text{when }(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\chi})=\mathcal{O}_{F},

and when
\chi
is primitive, we have for
\textup{Re}(s)>1
the identity

(2.13)

\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\chi(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)^{-1}=L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q}_{\chi}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big({1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}}\Big).

3.
Preliminary reductions and a generalized Vaughan identity

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
,
\eta=\max\{2,\frac{n}{2}\}
,
A>0
, and
B=2^{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]/4}(6a+12n+54)
. If
x\geqslant 3
and
Q=x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}
, then

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

Assuming Theorem
3.1
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We will argue that Theorem
3.1
implies that

(3.1)

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\Big|\ll_{A}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

The desired result will then follow by partial summation. To see that Theorem
3.1
implies (
3.1
), note that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal, then
a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{p})=\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}
. To estimate the contribution from higher powers of prime ideals, we observe that by (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\ll_{\varepsilon}y^{\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}1.

So the contribution of these terms to the average in Theorem
3.1
is

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}y^{\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}1\ll\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\max_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leqslant x}x^{\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant x\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}\max_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant x\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{p}^{k})=(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}1,

by the convention that
\sum_{\mathfrak{b}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}1=0
if
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})\neq\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We now argue that

(3.2)

\max_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leqslant x}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant x\\
k\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q\\
\mathfrak{p}^{k}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\\
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{p}^{k})=(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}1\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{\varepsilon}.

Once we establish this, it follows from the prime ideal theorem that

(3.3)

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{p}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}\Big|\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}.\hskip-2.84526pt

Since
x^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}\ll_{A}x(\log x)^{-A}
, this finishes the passage from powers of prime to prime ideals.

In order to prove (
3.2
), we begin with the fact that if
\mathfrak{b}\equiv\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
with
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{b})=(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
and
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leqslant x
, then there exists
\omega\in F^{\times}
such that
\omega
is totally positive,
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}
implies
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\omega-1)\geqslant\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})
, and
(\omega)=\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
. Let
h
be the class number of
F
, then
\omega^{h}=ba^{-1}
for some nonzero
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
with
\mathfrak{b}^{h}=(b)
and
\mathfrak{a}^{h}=(a)
. Since
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=0
for
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}
,
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(b-a)=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(b-a)-\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\omega^{h}-1)=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\omega-1)+\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\omega^{h-1}+\cdots+1)\geqslant\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})
if
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}
. Hence
\mathfrak{m}|(b-a)
. Recall that if
a\in F^{\times}
, then
\prod_{v}|a|_{v}=1
. As a result,
\prod_{v|\infty}|a|_{v}\leqslant x^{h}
and
\prod_{v|\infty}|b|_{v}\leqslant x^{h}
. All of the conjugates of an algebraic integer are algebraic integers, so their absolute values have a uniform lower bound depending only on
F
. We can see from this fact that
|a|_{v},|b|_{v}\ll x^{h}
for all
v|\infty
. Hence
N(b-a)=\prod_{v|\infty}|b-a|_{v}\ll x^{h[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
. One can check that there are at most
\tau_{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}(m)
integral ideals with norm
m
, where
\tau_{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}(m)
is the
m
-th Dirichlet coefficient of
\zeta(s)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
. Since
\tau_{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}(m)\ll_{\varepsilon}m^{\varepsilon}
, the innermost sum can be bounded by
\tau_{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}(\mathrm{N}(b-a))
, which is therefore
\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{\varepsilon}
, as desired.
∎

M(s)=\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant X}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}},\qquad N(s)=\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant Y}\frac{\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X\geqslant 1
and
Y\geqslant 1
. If
\mathfrak{n}
is an integral ideal with
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}>Y
, then
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{n=ab}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant X\end{subarray}}\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{a})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}-{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{n=abc}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant X,\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant Y\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{a})\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})

\displaystyle+{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{n=abc}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}>X,\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}>Y\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{a})\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c}).

Proof.

If
\textup{Re}(s)>1
, then we have the identity

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(s,\pi)M(s)+L(s,\pi)M(s)N(s)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)+N(s)\Big)\Big(1-L(s,\pi)M(s)\Big)-N(s).

Once we identify the coefficients of
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{-s}
on each side, we obtain the desired result.
∎

We apply Lemma

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant X^{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{bc=n}\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{X<\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}<y/X\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{bd=n}\\

4.
Zero-free regions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. We let
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
denote the trivial representation, whose
L
-function is the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{F}(s)
. Recall that
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}_{n}
is the subset consisting of
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
such that
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
and
\pi\neq\pi\otimes\chi
for all nontrivial quadratic primitive Hecke characters
\chi
. In this section, we prove a zero-free region for
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
which is comparable to that of Dirichlet characters, including the first unconditional
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}
-aspect bound on a possible Landau–Siegel zero. We then use this zero-free region along with standard contour integration techniques to prove an analogue of the Siegel–Walfisz theorem for the Dirichlet coefficients
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{p})
. We now present the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Q\geqslant 3
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{\flat}
. There exists a constant
c_{\pi}>0
, depending effectively on
\pi
, such that for all primitive Hecke characters
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
with
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}\leqslant Q
with at most one exception, the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
is nonzero in the region

\textup{Re}(s)\geqslant 1-\frac{c_{\pi}}{\log(Q(3+|\textup{Im}(s)|))}.

If the exceptional character
\chi_{1}
exists, then

•

\chi_{1}
is quadratic.

•

L(s,\chi_{1})
has exactly one zero
\beta_{1}
in this region, and
\beta_{1}
is both real and simple.

•

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
c_{\pi}(\varepsilon)>0
such that
\beta_{1}\leqslant 1-c_{\pi}(\varepsilon)Q^{-\varepsilon}
.

4.1.
Preliminaries for the zero-free region

We begin with a standard zero-free region.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, and let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
be a primitive Hecke character. There exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}(\pi)>0
such that
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)\neq 0
in the region

\textup{Re}(s)\geqslant 1-\frac{\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}}{\log(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}(3+|\textup{Im}(s)|))}

with the possible exception of one real zero
\beta_{1}<1
when
\pi\otimes\chi
is self-dual. When
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
, the exceptional zero can only exist when
\chi
is primitive, nontrivial, and quadratic.

Proof.

When
|\textup{Im}(s)|\neq 0
or
\pi\otimes\chi
is not self-dual, then the result follows from
[
12
, Theroem A.1]
with
\pi
(respectively
\pi^{\prime}
) therein replaced by
\pi\otimes\chi
(respectively
\mathbbm{1}
). When
\textup{Im}(s)=0
and
\pi\otimes\chi
is self-dual, then by
[
12
, Theorem A.1]
, there exists at most one zero
\beta_{1}<1
in the stated region, while the nonvanishing of
L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)
follows from
[
19
, Theorem A.1]
. If
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
, then
\pi\otimes\chi
is self-dual if and only if
\chi
is real and primitive. When
\pi=\widetilde{\pi}
and
\chi
is trivial, then by
[
19
, Theorem A.1]
, there exists effectively computable constant
=\Cr{brumleyzfrtriv}(\pi)>0
such that if
1-\Cr{brumleyzfrtriv}\leqslant s<1
, then
L(s,\pi)\neq 0
. This exhausts all cases once
\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}
is made suitably large (in an effective manner depending at most on
\pi
).
∎

Next, we quantify the idea that exceptional zeros are rare.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. Among the primitive quadratic Hecke characters
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
with
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}\leqslant Q
, at most one, say
\chi_{1}
, has the property that
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
has a real zero
\beta_{1}
in the interval

1-\frac{\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}}{\log Q}\leqslant s<1.

Proof.

This follows from
[
11
, Theorem A]
. We may take
\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}
to be the same as in Lemma
4.2
once
\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}
is made suitably small (in a manner that depends at most on
\pi
).
∎

4.2.
Preceding literature

Siegel proved that if
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q})
is a primitive nontrivial quadratic Dirichlet character, then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
c(\varepsilon)>0
such that
L(1,\chi)>c(\varepsilon)q^{-\varepsilon}
. All known proofs except for one by Bombieri
[
2
, Théorème 15]
use the fact that if
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q})
and
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q^{\prime}})
are distinct primitive nontrivial quadratic Dirichlet characters, and
\chi^{\prime\prime}
is he primitive Dirichlet character that induces
\chi\chi^{\prime}
, then there exists a Dirichlet series
F(s)
, depending explicitly on
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
, such that
F(s)
has

(i)

a pole of odd order
r\geqslant 1
at
s=1
,

(ii)

nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients, and

(iii)

an analytic continuation to suitable region past
\textup{Re}(s)=1
(e.g.,
\mathbb{C}-\{1\}
),

(iv)

and a residue at
s=1
that has
L(1,\chi)
as a factor with integral multiplicity at least one.

To study
L(1,\chi)
, the most natural choice of
F(s)
is
\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi^{\prime})L(s,\chi^{\prime\prime})
. A pole of odd order
r
at
s=1
is important; under the above hypotheses, the residue
R_{F}
of
F(s)
at
s=1
satisfies
R_{F}>0
, and as
s\to 1
along the real line, we have

(4.1)

F(s)\sim\frac{R_{F}}{s-1}.

If
0<\varepsilon<1
and there exists
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q^{\prime}})
such that
L(s_{0},\chi^{\prime})=0
for some
s_{0}\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
, then
F(s_{0})\leqslant 0
. On the other hand, if no such
\chi^{\prime}
exists, then by (
4.1
), we have that
F(s_{0})\leqslant 0
for some
s_{0}\in(1-\varepsilon/2,1)
. Therefore, for all
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
s_{0}\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
and
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,q^{\prime}})
, both depending
only on
\varepsilon
, such that
F(s_{0})\leqslant 0
. Davenport’s book
[
6
, Ch. 20]
is a standard source; it gives Estermann’s proof, which requires this argument as a key step.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, and let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
and
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}})
be distinct nontrivial primitive quadratic Hecke characters. Let
\psi
be the primitive character that induces
\chi^{\prime}\chi
(whose conductor necessarily divides
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
). The possible existence of the exceptional real zero of
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)
in Lemma
4.2
was eliminated by Hoffstein and Ramakrishnan
[
11
, Theorem B]
under the assumption of automorphy for certain Rankin–Selberg convolutions depending on
\pi
. When their automorphy hypothesis is not known to be satisfied, it is unclear how to construct a Dirichlet series
L(s)
depending on
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
with nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients, an analytic continuation, a pole of odd order at
s=1
, and a residue at
s=1
that has
L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)
as a factor with integral multiplicity at least one.

If we allow for a pole of
even
order at
s=1
, then we can construct an
L(s)
satisfying properties (ii)-(iv) above. Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, and suppose that
\pi\neq\pi\otimes\nu
for all primitive nontrivial quadratic Hecke characters over
F
. Define
\Pi=\mathbbm{1}\boxplus\pi
and
(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})_{\chi}=\chi\boxplus\pi\otimes\chi\boxplus\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi\boxplus\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi)
. Consider the representation

(4.2)

\Pi^{\star}=(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})\boxplus(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})_{\chi}\boxplus(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})_{\chi^{\prime}}\boxplus(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})_{\psi}

along with its
L
-function

(4.3)

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi^{\star})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{F}(s)L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi^{\prime})L(s,\psi)L(s,\pi)L(s,\widetilde{\pi})L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)L(s,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi)

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi\otimes\chi^{\prime})L(s,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi^{\prime})L(s,\pi\otimes\psi)L(s,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\psi)L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})L(s,\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi^{\prime}))L(s,\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\psi)).

Our twist hypothesis for
\pi
ensures that
L(s,\Pi^{\star})
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}-\{1\}
with a pole of order two at
s=1
. This auxiliary
L
-function was suggested by Molteni
[
23
, p. 141]
in a special case, with (
4.3
) providing a natural generalization. Instead of providing full details for how to prove a Siegel-type lower bound for
|L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)|
using
L(s,\Pi^{\star})
, Molteni references a “standard approach to Siegel-type theorems” in a paper by Golubeva and Fomenko
[
9
]
. However, in
[
9
, pp. 87-88]
, Golubeva and Fomenko only say that Estermann’s proof of Siegel’s theorem (the version in Davenport
[
6
, Ch. 20]
) applies to (
4.2
) (with
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
) “after fairly tedious calculations.”

As stated above, (
4.3
) satisfies properties (ii)-(iv), but not (i), since
L(s,\Pi^{\star})
has a pole of order two at
s=1
. In this situation, a Siegel-type lower bound does
not
follow from a direct generalization of the arguments in
[
6
, Ch. 20]
, or any other argument that proves Siegel’s theorem using the above auxiliary
L
-function
\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi^{\prime})L(s,\chi^{\prime\prime})
. Since
L(s,\Pi^{\star})
has a pole of order 2 at
s=1
and nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients, it follows that there exists a constant
R_{\Pi^{\star}}>0
such that as
s\to 1
along the reals, we have

L(s,\Pi^{\star})\sim\frac{R_{\Pi^{\star}}}{(s-1)^{2}}.

Consequently, there exists
s^{\prime}<1
such that
L(s,\Pi^{\star})>0
for all
s\in[s^{\prime},1)
. Therefore, it is no longer true that for all
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
s_{0}\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
and
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}})
such that
L(s_{0},\Pi^{\star})\leqslant 0
. An identical error can also be found in
[
15
]
.

In summary, a separate approach is needed in order to produce a lower bound for
|L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)|
when
\varepsilon
is so small that
L(s_{0},\Pi^{\star})>0
for all
s_{0}\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
. Such an approach is provided by the following lemma. This leads to a correction and substantial generalization of the works in
[
23
,
9
,
15
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
be a primitive quadratic Hecke character, and let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. If
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi)\neq 0
in the region

\textup{Re}(s)\geqslant 1-\frac{\Cr{ZFR_{c}onst}}{\log(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}(3+|\textup{Im}(s)|))},

then there exist constants
=\Cr{XLi1}(\pi)>0
and
=\Cr{XLi2}(\pi)>0
such that

|L(1,\pi\otimes\chi)|\geqslant\Cr{XLi1}\exp(-\Cr{XLi2}\sqrt{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}}).

Proof.

This follows from work of Li
[
20
, Corollary 7]
. While the proofs in
[
20
]
are performed over
F=\mathbb{Q}
, an extension over number fields follows
mutatis mutandis
.
∎

4.3.
An extension of Siegel’s theorem

Let
\Pi=\pi\boxplus\mathbbm{1}
, and define the numbers
a_{\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})
by the Dirichlet series identity

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{k\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}=\log L(s,\Pi\times\widetilde{\pi}),\qquad\textup{Re}(s)>1.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}})
and
\chi^{\prime}\,(\mathrm{mod}{\,\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}})
be primitive quadratic Hecke characters, and let
\psi
be the primitive Hecke character inducing
\chi^{\prime}\chi
. Let
\Pi=\mathbbm{1}\boxplus\pi
, and recall the definition of
\Pi^{\star}
in (
4.2
). There exists an entire function
\mathcal{H}(s)=\mathcal{H}_{\pi}(s,\chi,\chi^{\prime})
such that

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{k\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{ks}}(1+\chi(\mathfrak{p}^{k}))(1+\chi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p}^{k}))=\log(L(s,\Pi^{\star})\mathcal{H}(s)).

The Dirichlet coefficients
\lambda^{\star}(\mathfrak{n})
of
L(s,\Pi^{\star})\mathcal{H}(s)
are nonnegative, and
\lambda^{\star}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=1
. We have the bounds
|\mathcal{H}(1)|,|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(1)|\ll_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q})^{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Finally, if
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
|\mathcal{H}(\frac{1}{2}+it)|\ll(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q})^{3n^{2}/2}
.

Proof.

We determine
\mathcal{H}(s)
explicitly using the local calculations in
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}}

\displaystyle\Big\{\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi)}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\cdot\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\chi^{\prime})}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\cdot\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\psi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\psi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\psi)}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big]\Big\}.

The claimed bounds of
|\mathcal{H}(1)|
,
|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(1)|
, and
|\mathcal{H}(\frac{1}{2}+it)|
follow from (
2.5
) and (
2.8
) along with the bound
\#\{\mathfrak{p}\colon\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n}\}\ll(\log\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})^{-1}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}
.

The nonnegativity of
a_{\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})
follows from the proof of
[

Let
b_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
be defined as in Lemma
5.3
. Then we have

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}b_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})^{2}\ll x(\log x)^{4n+3},\qquad\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\ll x,\qquad\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\ll x(\log x)^{n}.

Proof.

In order to prove three upper bound estimates in a unified way, we introduce an arithmetic function
f(\mathfrak{n})
satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

0\leqslant f(\mathfrak{n})\ll\underbrace{(\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}*\cdots*\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}})}_{\text{$k$ terms}}(\mathfrak{n})
for all
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
and some
k\geqslant 1
;

(2)

f(\mathfrak{n})\ll\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{\delta}
for any integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}
and some
\delta<1
.

It is clear that the generating series of
\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})*\cdots*\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})
is exactly
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})^{k}
. Using the analytic properties of the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
and the Tauberian theorem, we find that there exists a constant
=\Cr{leading}(\pi,k)>0
such that

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\underbrace{(\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}*\cdots*\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}})}_{\text{$k$ terms}}(\mathfrak{n})\sim\Cr{leading}x(\log x)^{k-1}.

Now, it follows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}f(\mathfrak{n})\ll x(\log x)^{k-1}.

Moreover, for any
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F},
one has

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\mathfrak{n}_{2}
with
\mathfrak{n}_{1}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}^{\infty}
and
(\mathfrak{n}_{2},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
, where
\mathfrak{n}_{1}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}^{\infty}
means that
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n}_{1}
implies
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
for any
\mathfrak{p}
. Thus, we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}f(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}_{1}\leqslant x\\
\mathfrak{n}_{1}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}^{\infty}\end{subarray}}f(\mathfrak{n}_{1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}_{2}\leqslant x/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}_{1}\\
(\mathfrak{n}_{2},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}f(\mathfrak{n}_{2})

\displaystyle\ll x(\log x)^{k-1}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{1-\delta}}\Big)\Big)\ll x(\log x)^{k-1}.

By (
2.5
), it is easy to see that
b_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})^{2},|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}
and
d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}
are all
\ll\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{2\theta_{n}+\varepsilon}
. Note that
2\theta_{n}\leqslant 1-\frac{2}{n^{2}+1}
. Together this with Lemma
5.3
, we can take
f(\mathfrak{n})
to be
b_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})^{2},|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}
and
d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}
with
k=4(n+1),1
and
n+1
, respectively. Thus, the estimate (
5.10
) yields this lemma.
∎

6.
Large-sieve type estimates

In this section we obtain large sieve estimates for Dirichlet polynomials and
L
-functions. The main tool is the large sieve inequality for number fields introduced by Huxley
[
13
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
c(\mathfrak{n})
be any complex coefficients and define the Dirichlet polynomial to be

D(s,\chi)=\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\frac{c(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}.

Then we have

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}}|D(s,\chi)|^{2}\ll(Q^{2}+x)\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\Big|\frac{c(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{s}}\Big|^{2},

where the second sum is over all primitive narrow ideal class character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
and the implied constant depends on the number field.

To obtain large sieve inequality for
L
-functions, we use the approximation functional equation to approximate the
L
-functions in the critical strip by Dirichlet series. We state the approximate functional equation as in
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X>0
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
. Then for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s\in(0,1)
, we have

L(s,\pi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}V_{s}\Big(\frac{X\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{\sqrt{q(\pi)}}\Big)+\varepsilon(s,\pi)\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})}}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{1-s}}V_{1-s}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{X\sqrt{q(\pi)}}\Big),

where

\varepsilon(s,\pi)=\varepsilon(\pi)q(\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\frac{L_{\infty}(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})}{\L_{\infty}(s,\pi)}.

Moreover, for any
A>0
,
V_{s}(y)
is a function satisfying the following estimate

V_{s}(y)\ll_{A}\Big(1+\frac{y}{\sqrt{q_{\infty}(\pi,\operatorname{Im}s)}}\Big)^{-A}.

We shall use these two lemmata to deduce the second moments of twisted automorphic
L
-functions.

Proposition 6.3
.

For any real number
t
and
Q>0
, we have

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi)|^{2}\ll(Q^{2}+Q^{\frac{n}{2}}(3+|t|)^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{2}})(\log Q(3+|t|))^{2},

where the second sum is over all primitive narrow ideal class character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. The implied constant depends on
F
and
\pi

Proof.

By
q(\pi\otimes\chi)\sim Q_{1}
, we mean that
Q_{1}<q(\pi\otimes\chi)\leqslant 2Q_{1}
. Since
\chi
is a character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q

2.9
) that
q(\pi\otimes\chi)\ll Q^{n}
. As a result,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}\end{subarray}}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi)|^{2}\ll(\log Q)\max_{Q_{1}\ll Q^{n}}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}\\
q(\pi\otimes\chi)\sim Q_{1}\end{subarray}}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi)|^{2}.

Since

and

2.13
) and Lemma
6.2

\displaystyle|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\Big|\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1/2+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{X\sqrt{q(\pi\otimes\chi)}}\Big)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big|\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1/2-it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}-it}\Big(\frac{X\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{\sqrt{q(\pi\otimes\chi)}}\Big)\Big|^{2}:=|D_{1}(X)|^{2}+|D_{2}(X)|^{2}

for all

. As a result,

(6.2)

\displaystyle|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X_{1}^{-1}}^{2X_{1}^{-1}}\Big|\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1/2-it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}-it}\bigg(\frac{X\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{\sqrt{Q_{1}}}\bigg)\Big|^{2}\frac{dX}{X}.

For any
A>0
, we can see that

when

2.5
) and the relation (
2.12

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant({Q(3+|t|)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}})^{\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1/2+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{X\sqrt{Q_{1}}}\bigg)\\

Accordingly, the contribution from the first term on the right-hand side of (

(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}^{1/2+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{X\sqrt{Q_{1}}}\bigg)\Big|^{2}\frac{dX}{X}+Q^{2}\\

+(\log QT)\max_{\frac{n}{2}\leqslant\frac{\log M}{\log(Q(3+|t|)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]})}\leqslant\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon}\int_{X_{1}}^{2X_{1}}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}\\

7.
Type I sums: Proof of Theorem
1.3

For technical convenience, one usually works with the weighted sum

(7.1)

\psi_{\rho}(y,\pi,\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big(1-\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{y}\Big)^{\rho},

where
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
,
\rho\geqslant 0
.
We want to prove a Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem with the smooth weight for
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
. To be precise, we have

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\eta=\max\{2,\frac{n}{2}\}
,
\rho=\lfloor\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}\rfloor+1
, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the usual floor function. If
A
is any positive number, then we have

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\psi_{\rho}(y,\pi,\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{a})\Big|\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}},

where
Q=x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}
with
B=A+2n+3
.

Proof.

Detecting the congruence condition in (
7.1
) by the multiplicative characters

\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})},
we obtain the identity

(7.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big(1-\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{y}\Big)^{\rho}=\frac{1}{h(\mathfrak{m})}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}}\overline{\chi}(\mathfrak{a})\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big(1-\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{y}\Big)^{\rho}.

We shall treat the innermost sum on the right-hand side of (
7.2
) by the technique of standard contour integration, which could give a direct link between the summation associated to an arithmetic function and the corresponding Dirichlet series. If
\rho
is any positive integer and
c>0,
then we have the Mellin inversion formula

(7.3)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{x^{s}}{s(s+1)\cdots(s+\rho)}ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\rho!}(1-\frac{1}{x})^{\rho}&\hbox{if $x\geqslant 1$},\\
0&\hbox{if $0\leqslant x\leqslant 1$.}\end{array}\right.

Then it follows from (
7.3
) that

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big(1-\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{y}\Big)^{\rho}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1+\varepsilon)}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(\rho+1+s)}\Big(\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}\Big)y^{s}ds.

If
\chi
is induced by a primitive character
\chi_{1}(\text{mod}\;\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}),
then
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}|\mathfrak{m}
. We further get from (
2.13
) that

(7.4)

\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}=\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)^{-1}\Big)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\\
=L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big).

Due to the estimate (
2.5
), for any
\varepsilon>0,

\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi\otimes\chi}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\Big)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Big)\ll d_{F}(\mathfrak{m})^{2n}

at
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s=1/2
.
Applying the analytic properties of
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})
and then moving the line of integration to
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=1/2
. Thus, by the residue theorem, it is bounded by

d_{F}(\mathfrak{m})^{2n}y^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{(1/2)}|L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})|\frac{|ds|}{|s|^{\rho+1}}.

Gathering these estimates, we then have

(7.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big(1-\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}{y}\Big)^{\rho}\Big|

\displaystyle\ll x^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{(1/2)}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{d_{F}(\mathfrak{m})^{2n}}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\sum_{\mathfrak{m^{\prime}}|\mathfrak{m}}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi_{1}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m^{\prime}})}}|L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})|\frac{|ds|}{|s|^{\rho+1}}

where the trivial inequalities
d_{F}(\mathfrak{bc})\leqslant d_{F}(\mathfrak{b})d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})
and
\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{bc})\geqslant\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{b})\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{c})
. are used in the last step.
By Proposition
6.3
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the elementary estimate
\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})^{k}\ll x(\log x)^{2^{k}-1}
for any positive integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m^{\prime}}\leqslant Q/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}}\frac{d_{F}(\mathfrak{m^{\prime}})^{2n}}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m^{\prime}})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi_{1}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m^{\prime}})}}|L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})|

A bilinear form

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{a(\mathfrak{l})\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{b(\mathfrak{n})\}
be two sequences of complex numbers with
\mathcal{B}
satisfying the following Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis: For
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
and
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant(\log N)^{A}
for any
A>0
,

(8.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\ll\frac{(\mathcal{B}(N)N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\log N)^{9A}}.

Then we have

(8.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\Big|{\underset{\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\Big(Q+\sqrt{L+N}+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{(\log N)^{A}}\Big)(\log Q)^{2}\mathcal{A}(L)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(N)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

\mathcal{A}(L)=\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2},\quad\mathcal{B}(N)=\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2},

and the implied constant depends only on
A
.

Proof.

Using the orthogonality of multiplicative characters, we see that the left-hand side in (
8.2
) is bounded by

(8.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}}\Big|\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}a(\mathfrak{l})\chi(\mathfrak{l})\Big|\Big|\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{cr}\leqslant Q}\frac{1}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{c})\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{r})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{r})}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L\\
(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}a(\mathfrak{l})\chi(\mathfrak{l})\Big|\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big|.

Fix
\mathfrak{c}
and split the sum over
\mathfrak{r}
into dyadic segments
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}\sim R_{1}
. Then apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the dyadic segment to get

\leqslant\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}\sim R_{1}}\frac{1}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{r})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{r})}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L\\
(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}a(\mathfrak{l})\chi(\mathfrak{l})\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}\sim R_{1}}\frac{1}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{r})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{r})}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

By Lemma
6.1
, the above is

\displaystyle\ll\Big(R_{1}+\frac{L}{R_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(R_{1}+\frac{N}{R_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\Big(R_{1}+\sqrt{L+N}+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{R_{1}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Summing this over
R\leqslant R_{1}\leqslant Q/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}
, we get

(8.4)

\Big(\frac{Q}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}}+\sqrt{L+N}\log Q+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{R}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It remains to estimate the contribution of the primitive characters
\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{r})}
with
1\leqslant\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}\leqslant R.
For each of these we appeal to the condition (
8.1
).

First, we define Möbius function
\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{a})
for
F
by

\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{a})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}_{F},\\
(-1)^{r}&\mathfrak{a}\text{ is a product of }r\text{ distinct prime ideals, }\\
0&\mathfrak{a}\text{ is divided by square of a prime ideal. }\end{array}\right.

It is easy to see that
\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{a})
is a multiplicative function over the ideals which satisfies

\sum_{\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{a}}\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{o})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}_{F},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We detect the coprimality condition
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}
by this formula and get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{c}}\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{o})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{n}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{c}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{o}\leqslant K\end{subarray}}\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{o})\sum_{\mathfrak{l}|\mathfrak{o}}\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{l})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{l})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{c}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{o}>K\end{subarray}}\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{o})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{n}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle:=W_{1}+W_{2}.

where
K
will be chosen later. Next we estimate the innermost sum in
W_{1}
by splitting into classes in
\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
, and for each class we apply the Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis (
8.1
). Consequently, we have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{l})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{rl})}\chi(\mathfrak{h})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{h}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{rl})\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\ll\frac{h(\mathfrak{rl})N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\log N)^{9A}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we choose
\mathfrak{h}
to be integral representatives. This further yields

W_{1}\ll\frac{N\mathfrak{r}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\log N)^{A}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{o}|\mathfrak{c}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{o}\leqslant K\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}|\mathfrak{o}}|\mu_{F}(\mathfrak{l})|N\mathfrak{l}\ll\frac{RKN^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})}{(\log N)^{9A}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then we treat the sum
W_{2}
and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality getting

W_{2}\ll\Big(\frac{N}{K}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{F}(\mathfrak{c}).

Adding these two estimates for
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
and then choosing
K=(\log N)^{6A}
, we obtain

(8.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\ll\frac{RN^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})}{(\log N)^{3A}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, we use the trivial bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L\\
(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}a(\mathfrak{l})\chi(\mathfrak{l})\ll L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining this with the estimate (
8.5
) together, we obtain

(8.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{r}\leqslant R}\frac{1}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{r})}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{r})}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L\\
(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}a(\mathfrak{l})\chi(\mathfrak{l})\Big|\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N\\
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{c})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{R^{2}\sqrt{LN}d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})}{(\log N)^{3A}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Summing (
8.6
) and (
8.4
) over
\mathfrak{c}
, we infer the following bound for our original sum in (
8.3

\Big(Q+\sqrt{L+N}+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{R}+\frac{R^{2}\sqrt{LN}}{(\log N)^{3A}}\Big)(\log Q)^{2}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}|a(\mathfrak{l})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|b(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Choosing

∎

Lemma 8.2
.

With the notation and conditions as in Lemma
8.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|{\underset{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}\leqslant y\atop\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\Big(Q+\sqrt{L+N}+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{(\log N)^{A}}\Big)(\log xLN)(\log Q)^{2}\mathcal{A}(L)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(N)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

We follow the trick of Vaughan
[
33
, Lemma 2]
. If
\gamma>0
, then

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\beta\alpha}\frac{\sin\gamma\alpha}{\alpha}d\alpha=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $0\leqslant\beta<\gamma$,}\\
0&\mbox{if $\beta>\gamma$}\end{cases}=:\delta(\beta)

via the product-to-sum identities of trigonometric functions and the identity
e^{i\beta\alpha}=\cos\beta\alpha+i\sin\beta\alpha
. By integration by parts, we get

\int_{A}^{\infty}\frac{\sin Y\alpha}{\alpha}d\alpha\ll\frac{1}{YA}

for any
Y>0
and
A>0.
Thus, using the product-to-sum identities of trigonometric functions again, we have

(8.7)

\delta(\beta)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-A}^{A}e^{i\beta\alpha}\frac{\sin\gamma\alpha}{\alpha}d\alpha+O\Big(\frac{1}{A|\gamma-\beta|}\Big).

Taking
\gamma=\log(\lfloor y\rfloor+\frac{1}{2})
and
\beta=\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}
, we obtain the equality

\delta(\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}\leqslant y,\\
0&\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}>y.\end{array}\right.

Then it follows from (
8.7
) that

\delta(\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-A}^{A}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln})^{i\alpha}\frac{\sin\gamma\alpha}{\alpha}d\alpha+O\Big(\frac{y}{A}\Big).

Hence

{\underset{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln}\leqslant y\atop\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})={\underset{\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})\delta(\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{ln})\\
=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-A}^{A}{\underset{\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l})^{i\alpha}b(\mathfrak{n})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})^{i\alpha}\frac{\sin\gamma\alpha}{\alpha}d\alpha+O\Big(\frac{y}{A}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}|a(\mathfrak{l})b(\mathfrak{n})|\Big).

The error term here is manageable if we take
A=xLN
. For
y\leqslant x
, the integral is

\ll\int_{-A}^{A}\Big|{\underset{\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l})^{i\alpha}b(\mathfrak{n})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})^{i\alpha}\Big|\min\Big(\log x,\frac{1}{|\alpha|}\Big)d\alpha.

Summing the integrand over modulus
\mathfrak{m}
and applying Lemma
8.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|{\underset{\mathfrak{ln}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l}\leqslant L}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant N}}}a(\mathfrak{l})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{l})^{i\alpha}b(\mathfrak{n})(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})^{i\alpha}\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\Big(Q+\sqrt{L+N}+\frac{\sqrt{LN}}{(\log N)^{A}}\Big)(\log Q)^{2}\mathcal{A}(L)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(N)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\int_{-A}^{A}\min(\log x,|\alpha|^{-1})d\alpha\ll\log xLN
trivially, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

It remains to estimate the averages

for
i=1,2,3,4
, where the terms
S_{i}
are given by (3.4)-(3.7). To begin, we define

\alpha_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{bc=n}\\
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant X,~\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant X\end{subarray}}\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c}),\quad\beta_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{bd=n}\\
\mathfrak{b}>X\end{subarray}}\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{d}).

By inequalities in Section
5
, we can estimate the second moments of
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n}),\alpha_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
and
\beta_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
. Firstly, we get from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
5.2
) that

(9.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}|\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})\Big)\ll x.

Secondly, by the bound (
2.5
) and the definitions of
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})
and
a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ll\log x}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant x}(k+1)a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leqslant n^{2}+1}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{k}\leqslant x}(k+1)a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}+x^{1-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{n})+x^{1-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}+\varepsilon}\ll x.

This further implies that

(9.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}|\alpha_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\sum_{\mathfrak{bc=n}}|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})|^{2}d_{F}(\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant x}d_{F}(\mathfrak{b})|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})|^{2}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant x/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}}d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi\otimes\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{c})\ll x(\log x)^{n+2},

by inserting Lemma
5.4
, partial summation and the trivial fact
d_{F}(\mathfrak{bc})\leqslant d_{F}(\mathfrak{b})d_{F}(\mathfrak{c})
.
Thirdly, we immediately obtain from Lemma
5.4
that

(9.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}|\beta_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\ll x(\log x)^{4n+3}.

9.1.
The average for
S_{1}

To estimate the contribution from
S_{1},
we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
9.1
) to obtain

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{1}|\ll QX^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant X}|\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll QX.

9.2.
The average for
S_{2}

We split
S_{2}
in the following way:

S_{2}=\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant H}+\sum_{H<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant X}\Big)\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant y/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\\
\mathfrak{c}\equiv\mathfrak{ab^{-1}}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}=:S_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}+S_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},

where
H<X
. We deduce from partial summation and Theorem
1.3
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant H}|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})|\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{ab},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant y/\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\\
\mathfrak{c}\equiv\mathfrak{ab^{-1}}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\log x\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant H}|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})|\Big)\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{c}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})\Big|\ll\frac{Hx}{(\log x)^{A}}.

We use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Lemma
5.4
in the last step, and emphasize that
Q\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}
with
\eta=\max\{\frac{n}{2},2\}
,
B=2^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}}(2A+16)+2n-4
.
In order to estimate the contribution of
S_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},
splitting the range of summation over
\mathfrak{b}
into intervals of the form
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\sim M
with
H<M\leqslant X
, we get

(9.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}|

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)\max_{H\leqslant M\leqslant X}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|{\underset{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{bc}\leqslant y\atop\mathfrak{bc}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\sim M}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\leqslant x/M}}}\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\Big|.

In order to apply Lemma
8.2
, we need to verify the sequence
\{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\}
satisfies the Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis. So we have to estimate the sum of type
\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})
for any
\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}
. We choose a function
h
supported on
[0,x+Y]
, such that
h(z)=1
if
Y\leqslant z\leqslant x
and
h^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}Y^{-j}
for all
j\geqslant 0
. Here, the parameter
Y
will be chosen later subject to
1\leqslant Y\leqslant x
. By partial integration, the Mellin transform of
h
satisfies

\hat{h}(s)=\int_{0}^{x+Y}h(z)z^{s-1}dz\ll\frac{Y}{x^{1-\sigma}}\cdot\Big(\frac{x}{|s|Y}\Big)^{j}

for any
j\geqslant 1
and
1/2\leqslant\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s\leqslant 2
. Moreover, we derive from (
2.10
), Lemma
5.2
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\sum_{x<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x+Y}|\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|\ll Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x+Y}|\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll(xY)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for
0<Y\leqslant x
. Consequently, we have that

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})h(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})+O((xY)^{\frac{1}{2}}).

By Mellin’s inverse transform, we can write

(9.5)

\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})h(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\hat{h}(s)\Big(\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})}{{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}}^{s}}\Big)ds.

We know from (
7.4
) that the Dirichlet series appearing above equals
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})
except for some Euler factors, where
\chi_{1}(\text{mod}\;\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
is a primitive character induced by
\chi
with
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}|\mathfrak{m}
.
The convexity bound for
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})
gives

L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})\ll({\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(3+|t|)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]})^{\frac{n}{4}+\varepsilon}.

Moving the vertical line of integration in (
9.5
) to
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s=1/2
,
we obtain by Cauchy’s theorem and (
7.4
) that

\sum_{\mathfrak{n}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})h(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n})\ll d_{F}(\mathfrak{m})^{2n}\int_{(1/2)}|\hat{h}(s)L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{1})|ds\\
\ll\int_{0}^{x/Y}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1+t}({\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(3+t)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]})^{\frac{n}{4}}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}(3+t))^{\varepsilon}dt\\
+\int_{x/Y}^{\infty}\frac{Y}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\Big(\frac{x}{tY}\Big)^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}+2}({\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}t^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]})^{\frac{n}{4}}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}t)^{\varepsilon}dt\ll\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}^{\frac{n}{4}}\Big(\frac{x}{Y}\Big)^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Gathering the above results we arrive at

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\ll\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}^{\frac{n}{4}}\Big(\frac{x}{Y}\Big)^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2}}+(xY)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We choose
Y=({\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}x^{d})^{\frac{n}{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]+2}}
, thus obtaining

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\ll\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}^{\frac{n}{2n[F:\mathbb{Q}]+4}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]+1}{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]+2}+\varepsilon}.

Since the orthogonality of characters yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\ll\max_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}}\Big|\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\chi(\mathfrak{n})\Big|\leqslant x^{1-\varepsilon}

for any
\mathfrak{m}
with
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant x^{\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon}
, the sequence
\{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\}
satisfies the Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis, and so does
\{\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\}
by partial summation. Thus, we can apply Lemma
8.2
to the sum on the last line of (
9.4
) and then get from the second estimate in Lemma
5.4

\displaystyle\ll(\log xQ)^{4}\max_{H\leqslant M\leqslant X}\Big(Q+\sqrt{M+\frac{x}{M}}+\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x/M)^{A}}\Big)\sqrt{\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{b}\sim M}|\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{b})|^{2}\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{c}\sim x/M}|\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})\log\mathfrak{c}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll(\log xQ)^{5}\Big(Q\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{Xx}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{H}}+\frac{x}{(\log x/X)^{A}}\Big).

To sum up, the contribution from
S_{2}
is

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{2}|\ll\frac{Hx}{(\log x)^{A}}+(\log xQ)^{5}\Big(Q\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{Xx}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{H}}+\frac{x}{(\log x/X)^{A}}\Big).

9.3.
The average for
S_{3}
.

The treatment is similar to that of
S_{2}
. If we use (
9.2
), then

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{3}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{Hx}{(\log x)^{A-\frac{n}{2}-1}}+(\log xQ)^{\frac{n}{2}+5}\Big(Q\sqrt{x}+X\sqrt{x}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{H}}+\frac{x}{(\log x/X^{2})^{A}}\Big).

9.4.
The average for
S_{4}

This contribution is of the same form as
S_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
except the coefficients
\beta_{F,\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
instead of
\mu_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})
and
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})
instead of
\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\log\mathfrak{n}
. Since the Siegel–Walfisz condition of
\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{c})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{c})
follows from Corollary
4.8
, the estimates (
9.1
) and (
9.3
) give that

\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant Q}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}|S_{4}|\ll(\log xQ)^{2n+6}\Big(Q\sqrt{x}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{X}}+\frac{x}{(\log X)^{A}}\Big).

9.5.
Finishing the proof

Finally, we collect all of our estimates for the averages of
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}
, and
S_{4}
, taking
H=(\log x)^{\frac{2}{3}(A+4)}
and
X=x^{\frac{1}{3}}
, to arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{m}\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{\eta}}(\log x)^{-B}}\frac{h(\mathfrak{m})}{\varphi_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\max_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{O}_{F}}\max_{y\leqslant x}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{n}\leqslant y\\
\mathfrak{n}\equiv\mathfrak{a}\text{ in }\mathrm{Cl}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{F}(\mathfrak{n})a_{\pi}(\mathfrak{n})\Big|\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\frac{A}{3}-2n-6}},

where
\eta=\max\{\frac{n}{2},2\},B=2^{\frac{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}{4}}(2A+16)+2n-4
. After changing parameter
\frac{A}{3}-2n-6\mapsto A
and inserting the corresponding estimate into (
3.3
), Theorem
1.1
follows.

10.
An arithmetic application: Proof of Corollary
1.4

By the definition of divisor function, we have

d(m)=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\mid m\\
r<\sqrt{m}\end{subarray}}1+\delta_{\square}(m),\qquad\delta_{\square}(m):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }m\text{ is a perfect square,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Hence, we deduce that

(10.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)d(p-1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|(p-1)\\
r<\sqrt{p-1}\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\delta_{\square}(p-1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{r<\sqrt{x-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)+O(x^{\frac{3}{4}}).

Here the error term comes from

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\delta_{\square}(p-1)\ll\Big(\sum_{p\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{p\leqslant x}\delta_{\square}(p-1)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll x^{\frac{3}{4}}

by using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the Rankin–Selberg theory.

For
r\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log x)^{-B}
, the result in Theorem
1.1
produces a small enough estimate. Then we have

(10.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{r\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log x)^{-B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\ll\sum_{r\leqslant x^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log x)^{-B}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\Big|\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

For the contribution of the terms
\sqrt{x}(\log x)^{-B}<r<\sqrt{x-1},
the Brun-Titchmarsh inequality is used in general. However, it is not suitable for the automorphic context, since GRC remains open. By using the orthogonality relation of additive characters, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{q|r}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\,(\Mod{q})}e\Big(\frac{-a}{q}\Big)\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)e\Big(\frac{ap}{q}\Big).

Thus,

(10.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<r<\sqrt{x-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l<\sqrt{x-1}}\frac{1}{l}\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{l(\log x)^{B}}<q<\frac{\sqrt{x-1}}{l}}\frac{1}{q}\;\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\,(\Mod{q})}e\Big(\frac{-a}{q}\Big)\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)e\Big(\frac{ap}{q}\Big):=T_{1}+T_{2},

where
T_{1}
denotes the contribution of the terms
l\leqslant(\log x)^{B}
and
T_{2}
denotes the contribution of the other terms. First we treat the sum
T_{1}
. Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the additive large sieve inequality (e.g.
[
16
, Theorem 7.11]
), we have

(10.4)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{l\leqslant(\log x)^{B}}\frac{1}{l}\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{l(\log x)^{B}}<q<\frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{l}}\frac{1}{q}\;\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\,(\Mod{q})}\Big|\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)e\Big(\frac{ap}{q}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{l\leqslant(\log x)^{B}}\frac{1}{l}\Big(\sum_{q<\frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{l}}\;\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\,(\Mod{q})}\Big|\sum_{p\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(p)e\Big(\frac{ap}{q}\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{l(\log x)^{B}}<q<\frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{l}}\frac{1}{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{p\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log\log x)^{\frac{3}{2}}\ll\frac{x(\log\log x)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\log x}},

where we use the fact that
|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}\ll a_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(p)
and the estimate (
5.2
) in the last step.
Next, we treat the sum
T_{2}
. Interchanging the order of summation and applying the formula for the Ramanujan sum

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\,(\Mod{q})}e\Big(\frac{(p-1)a}{q}\Big)=\sum_{d|(p-1,q)}d\mu\Big(\frac{q}{d}\Big),

we obtain from Theorem
1.1
that

(10.5)

T_{2}=\sum_{(\log x)^{B}<l<\sqrt{x-1}}\frac{1}{l}\sum_{m<\frac{\sqrt{x-1}}{l}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{ml(\log x)^{B}}<q<\frac{\sqrt{x-1}}{ml}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\\
\ll(\log x)^{2}\sum_{q<\frac{\sqrt{x-1}}{(\log x)^{B}}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{q})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\Big|\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}.

Assembling these estimates in (
10.2
)–(
10.5
) yields

\sum_{r<\sqrt{x-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p\equiv 1\,(\Mod{r})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)\ll\frac{x(\log\log x)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\log x}}.

Inserting this into (
10.1
), this corollary follows.

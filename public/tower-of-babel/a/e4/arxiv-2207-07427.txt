Weak limits of entropy regularized Optimal Transport: potentials, plans and divergences
Alberto González-Sanz, Jean-Michel Loubes, Jonathan Niles-Weed

Abstract
This work deals with the asymptotic distribution of both potentials and couplings of entropic regularized optimal transport for compactly supported probabilities in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We first provide the central limit theorem of the Sinkhorn potentials—the solutions of the dual problem—as a Gaussian process in 𝒞 s ​ ( Ω ) \mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega) . Then we obtain the weak limits of the couplings—the solutions of the primal problem—evaluated on integrable functions, proving a conjecture of Harchaoui, Liu, and Pal (2020). Finally we consider the weak limit of the entropic Sinkhorn divergence between P P and Q Q under both assumptions H 0 : P = Q H_{0}:\ {\rm P}={\rm Q} or H 1 : P ≠ Q H_{1}:\ {\rm P}\neq{\rm Q} . Under H 0 H_{0} the limit is a quadratic form applied to a Gaussian process in a Sobolev space, while under H 1 H_{1} , the limit is Gaussian. We provide also a different characterisation of the limit under H 0 H_{0} in terms of an infinite sum of an i.i.d. sequence of standard Gaussian random variables. Such results enable statistical inference based on entropic regularized optimal transport.

Introduction

Optimal transport has proven its effectiveness as a powerful tool in statistical data analysis. Formulated as a minimization problem, it reads

\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}d\pi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

where
\Pi(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})
denotes the set of couplings between the probabilities
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{Q}
. Optimal transport provides a notion of discrepancy between distributions
[
37
, Chapter 7]
useful for testing similarity between probabilities
[
8
,
9
,
24
,
13
]
and making inference. For this, it is necessary to know the weak limit of
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q}_{m})
when the empirical measures
\mathrm{P}_{n}
and
\mathrm{Q}_{m}
are used in the place of the population ones,
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{Q}
. Unfortunately, in general dimension—with the exception of perhaps a few simplified cases
[
10
,
30
]
—the limit is unknown. Moreover, the rate is slower than the usual parametric rate
{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

[
38
,
18
]
. This motivates the use of regularization methods for optimal transport, since they are not affected by the curse of dimension, such as entropic regularization
[
5
]
, or simplified versions, such as sliced optimal transport
[
33
]
. Regularized optimal transport is now used for many practical applications such as domain adaptation
[
4
]
, counterfactual explanations
[
6
]
, music transcription
[
17
]
, diffeomorphic registration
[
15
,
7
]
and measure colocalization in super-resolution images
[
30
]
.

Thought weak limits are known for the optimal value in the regularized optimal transport problem, less is known about the distributional limits of the optimizers themselves. In this paper we provide the limits of the empirical solutions of the primal problem (plans/couplings), the dual problem (potentials), and the celebrated Sinkhorn divergence
[
20
]
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. The entropic regularized optimal transport cost between two probability measures
\mathrm{P},{\rm Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
is defined as the solution to the optimization problem

S_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}d\pi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\epsilon H(\pi\mid\mathrm{P}\otimes\mathrm{Q}),

(1)

where the relative entropy between two probability measures
\alpha
and
\beta
is written as
H(\alpha\mid\beta)=\int\mathop{\rm log}\nolimits(\frac{d\alpha}{d\beta}(x))d\alpha(x)
if
\alpha
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\beta
,
\alpha\ll\beta
, and
+\infty
otherwise. We denote the solution of (
1
) by
\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}
.
This problem can also be written in its dual formulation

S_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},{\rm Q})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L_{1}(\mathrm{P})\\
g\in L_{1}({\rm Q})\end{subarray}}\int f(\mathbf{x})d\mathrm{P}(\mathbf{x})+\int g(\mathbf{y})d\mathrm{Q}(\mathbf{y})-\epsilon\int\int e^{\frac{f(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}}{\epsilon}}d\mathrm{P}(\mathbf{x})d\mathrm{Q}(\mathbf{y})+\epsilon.

(2)

There exists a unique pair
(f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}},g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}})
of solutions of the above optimization problem such that

\int g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{x})d\mathrm{Q}(\mathbf{y})=0.

(3)

Moreover, it holds that
\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}=\xi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}d(\mathrm{P}\otimes\mathrm{Q})
with

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=e^{\frac{f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{x})+g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}}{\epsilon}}.

(4)

It can be shown that this optimality condition implies the unique extension of the solutions of (
4
) to the space
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
, given by the relations

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}&=-\epsilon\mathop{\rm log}\nolimits\left(\int e^{\frac{g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}}{\epsilon}}d\mathrm{Q}(\mathbf{y})\right)\quad{\rm and}\\
g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}&=-\epsilon\mathop{\rm log}\nolimits\left(\int e^{\frac{f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{x})-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\cdot\|^{2}}{\epsilon}}d\mathrm{P}(\mathbf{x})\right).\end{split}

(5)

These relations have an important consequence—the optimization class can be reduced from
L_{1}({\rm Q})
to
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)
, with, moreover, uniformly (for all
\mathrm{P},{\rm Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
) bounded derivatives, see
[
19
,
31
]
. Since the class
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)
, with an appropriate choice of
s
, is uniformly Donsker (eg. Section 2.7.1. in
[
36
]
), one can obtain the bound

\sqrt{n}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mid S_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})-S_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},{\rm Q})\mid\right]\leq C_{\Omega},

where the constant
C_{\Omega}
depends polynomially on
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)
, see
[
31
]
. Moreover, since the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[S_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})]
towards its population counterpart occurs at a faster rate (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
) and the fluctuations are asymptotically Gaussian (
[
14
,
31
,
11
]
), the weak limit

\sqrt{n}(S_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})-S_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},{\rm Q}))\xrightarrow{w}N(0,\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mathrm{P}}(f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbf{X})))

(6)

holds. Though (
6
) gives a weak limit for
S_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})
, it does not give information about the coupling
\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}
or the optimal dual variables
(f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}},g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}})
.

For statistical inference, obtaining the asymptotic behaviour of optimal regularized couplings themselves, i.e., the limits of

\sqrt{\frac{n\,m}{n+m}}\int\eta\,(d\pi_{\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q}_{m}}-d\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}),\quad\text{where $\eta\in L^{\infty}(\mathrm{P}\otimes\mathrm{Q})$,}

(7)

is highly desirable. Indeed, in many of the previously cited applications, the optimal coupling itself is the object of interest.
Obtaining the limit of (
7

With regards to the limit of (
7

[
28
]
studied a modified regularization procedure inspired by Schrodinger’s lazy gas experiment giving rise to a different empirical estimator

(8)

where the variance

\lambda\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mathrm{P}}\left((1-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Q}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{P}})^{-1}\big(\eta_{\mathbf{x}}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Q}}\eta_{\mathbf{y}}\big)(\mathbf{X})\right)\\
+(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathrm{Q}}\left((1-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{P}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Q}})^{-1}\big(\eta_{\mathbf{y}}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{P}}\eta_{\mathbf{x}}\big)(\mathbf{Y})\right),

see section
2
for the precise definitions of the operators

and section
3
for the ones of

[

conjectured that the distributional limit (

proved that (

The regularized transport cost is easier to compute than the usual optimal transport cost but is unsuitable for two-sample testing, since
S_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm P})\neq 0
. In
[
20
]
, the authors proposes to remedy this deficiency by defining the quadratic Sinkhorn divergence:

This definition satisfies several atractive properties: it is symmetric in
\mathrm{P},{\rm Q}
and

, with

if and only if
\mathrm{P}={\rm Q}

The weak limit of the empirical Sinkhorn’s divergence, described in Theorem

, has different rates depending on the hypotheses

or
H_{1}:\ {\rm P}\neq{\rm Q}

4
Weak limit of the Divergences

Recall that, for probabilities
{\rm P},{\rm Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, the quadratic Sinkhorn divergence
[
20
]
is defined as

D_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm Q})=S_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm Q})-\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm P})+S_{\epsilon}({\rm Q},{\rm Q})\right).

The correction terms in the definition of the Sinkhorn divergence are designed to debias the entropic optimal transport distance so that it becomes a
bona fide
discrepancy measure (i.e.,
D_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm Q})=0
if and only if
{\rm P}={\rm Q}
).
This distance is of great interest for statistical applications.
In this section, we study its limit distribution.
Theorem
4.4
provides limits of the quantity

a_{n}\left(D_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})-D_{\epsilon}({\rm P}_{n},{\rm Q})\right),

where the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
depends on the hypothesis
H_{0}:\ {\rm P}={\rm Q}
or
H_{1}:\ {\rm P}\neq{\rm Q}
. In the latter case, the limit can be established by means of the
[
14
]
’s technique based on Efron-Stein inequality—see also
[
31
,
11
]
for reproduction and improvement of this argument—with common rate
a_{n}=\sqrt{n}
. Since the proof under
H_{1}
does not have any technical inovation, we omit the details.
The case
{\rm P}={\rm Q}
, however, has the faster rate
a_{n}=n
and the limit depends on the
\mathrm{P}
-Brownian bridge
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{P}}
in
(\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega))^{\prime}
.
More precisely, the limit in the one sample case will be the action of the random operator
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{P}}
on the random function
(1-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{P}}^{2})^{-1}{\bf i}_{\xi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{P}}}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{P}})
.
To state our result, we define the bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\nu,\mu)

\displaystyle\mapsto\nu((1-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{P}}^{2})^{-1}{\bf i}_{\xi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{P}}}(\mu)),

where
\mathcal{S}
is defined in (
28
).

\mathbb{M}
can be seen as the quadratic approximation of the Sinkhorn divergence under
H_{0}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a compact set,
{\rm P},{\rm Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
and
{\rm P}_{n}
(resp.
\mathrm{Q}_{m}
) be the empirical measure of the i.i.d. sample
\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{n}
(resp.
\mathbf{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{Y}_{m}
) distributed as
\rm P
(resp.
\mathrm{Q}
). Then, if
m=m(n)\to\infty
and
\frac{m}{n+m}\to\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have the following limits.

•

Under
H_{0}:\ {\rm P}={\rm Q},

D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q}_{m})=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,\mathbb{M}(\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{Q}_{m},\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{Q}_{m})+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{n+m}{n\,m}\right)

and

D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{P})=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,\mathbb{M}(\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{P},\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{P})+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
.

•

Under
H_{1}:\ {\rm P}\neq{\rm Q},

\sqrt{\frac{n\,m}{n+m}}(D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q}_{m})-D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}))=\int\psi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}d(\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{P})+\int\psi_{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{P}}d(\mathrm{Q}_{m}-\mathrm{Q})\\
+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n+m}{n\,m}}\right),

and
\sqrt{n}(D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q})-D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}))=\int\psi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}d(\mathrm{P}_{n}-\mathrm{P})+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
\psi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}=f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}-\frac{1}{2}(f_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{P}}+g_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{P}})
and
\psi_{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{P}}=f_{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{P}}-\frac{1}{2}(f_{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{Q}}+g_{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{Q}})
.

Using Theorem
4.1
to prove that
n\,D_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{m})\xrightarrow{w}\mathbb{M}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{P}},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{P}})
is trivial due to the continuity of all of the operators involving
\mathbb{M}
. However, characterizing in terms of known random variables is not immediate. We would like to use the following result.

Lemma 4.2

(
26
, Theorem 6.32
)
.

A Gaussian random variable
\mathbb{G}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
can be represented as the a.s. limit as

in
\mathcal{H}
of the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}N_{i}e_{i}
, where
\{N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is an i.d.d. sequence of

and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore,
\|\mathbb{G}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{2}\chi(1)_{i}
where
\{\chi(1)_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.d.d sequence of Chi-squares with one degree of freedom.

However,
(\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega))^{\prime}
is not Hilbertian or separable, and this is not immediately deduced. To write everything in terms of a inner product in a Hilbert space we use the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

The operator
\mathbb{M}

is bilinear, definite positive and bounded.
As a consequence, the space

is a separable Hilbert space, where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{M}}=\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathbb{M}}

Proof.

where

Set

from where we deduce the symmetry.

Auxiliary results of Hölder spaces

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval,
F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(I)
and
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
, with
g(\Omega)\subset I
. Then the operator

\displaystyle\delta_{F}:\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto F(g),

is Fréchet differentiable in
g
with derivative

D\delta_{F}(g)h=F^{\prime}(g)h.
Moreover, setting
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
, the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto fg,

is Fréchet differentiable in
g
with derivative

h\to fh.

Proof.

To verify the first claim, i.e.

\|F(g+h)-F(g)-F^{\prime}(g)h\|_{\alpha}=o(\|h\|_{\alpha}),

we prove the bound for each derivative in a direction
v
by a recursive argument on
a=|v|
. Note that the first case, the uniform norm, is trivial; set
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and apply Taylor’s theorem to obtain

\rvert F(g(\mathbf{x})+h(\mathbf{x}))-F(g(\mathbf{x}))-F^{\prime}(g(\mathbf{x}))h(\mathbf{x})\rvert\leq\rvert F^{\prime\prime}(g(\mathbf{x}))h(\mathbf{x})^{2}\rvert\leq\sup_{I}\rvert F^{\prime\prime}\rvert\cdot\|h\|_{s}.

Suppose that the result holds for
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given a derivative in a direction
b
with
\rvert b\rvert=a+1
, there exists a decomposition
D_{b}=D_{c}D_{d}
with
\rvert d\rvert=1
and
\rvert c\rvert=a
. Therefore, the chain rule yields

\displaystyle D_{b}(F(g+h)-F(g)-F^{\prime}(g)h)

\displaystyle=D_{c}(F^{\prime}(g+h)(D_{d}g+D_{d}h)-F^{\prime}(g))D_{d}g-F^{\prime\prime}(g)D_{d}gh-F^{\prime}(g)D_{g}h

\displaystyle=D_{c}\left((F^{\prime}(g+h)-F^{\prime}(g)-F^{\prime\prime}(g)h)D_{d}g+(F^{\prime}(g+h)-F^{\prime}(g))D_{d}h\right).

Since the function
F^{\prime}
still satisfies the assumptions of the theorem we obtain, by induction hypothesis and Lemma
A.2
, the limits

\|D_{c}((F^{\prime}(g+h)-F^{\prime}(g)-F^{\prime\prime}(g)h)D_{d}g)\|_{\infty}\\
\leq C\|D_{c}(F^{\prime}(g+h)(D_{d}g+D_{d}h)-F^{\prime}(g))\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{s}=o(\|h\|_{s})

and

\|D_{c}((F^{\prime}(g+h)-F^{\prime}(g))D_{d}h)\|_{\infty}\leq C\|D_{c}(F^{\prime}(g+h)-F^{\prime}(g))\|_{\infty}\|h\|_{s}=o(\|h\|_{s}),

which finish the proof of the first claim.
For the second one the relation

f(g+h)-fg-fh=0
and Lemma
A.2
conclude.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)
then there exists a constant
C
depending on
\Omega
,
s
and
d
such that

\|f\,g\|_{s}\leq C\|f\|_{s}\|g\|_{s}.

Proof.

The proof is direct consequence of the multivariate Leibniz rule, i.e.

\|D_{a}(f\,g)\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{\rvert\alpha\rvert\leq a}{a\choose\alpha}\|D_{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{a-\alpha}g\|_{\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{s}\|g\|_{s}.

∎

B
Refined convergence rates of regularized potentials

The aim of this section is to refine the results of
[
12
]
so that any errors that arise in the linearizations vanish rapidly enough.
The proofs lack interest; they are merely calculations and Taylor expansions of the exponential function.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a compact set,
{\rm P},{\rm Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, and its empirical versions
\mathrm{P}_{n},\mathrm{Q}_{m}
, with
\frac{n}{m+n}\to\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}-e^{h_{*}}(h_{n,m}-h_{*})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}=o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n+m}{n\,m}}\right),\quad\text{and}

(41)

\displaystyle\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n+m}{n\,m}}\right).

(42)

Proof.

Since the functions
h_{n,m}
and
h_{n,m}
are uniformly bounded, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, Lemma
A.1
applied to the exponential gives

\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}-e^{h_{*}}(h_{n,m}-h_{*})\|_{s}=o_{\mathbb{P}}(\|h_{*}-h_{n,m}\|_{s})

and, using Theorem 4.5 in
[
12
]
, we obtain (
41
). To prove (
42
) we apply the inverse triangle inequality to (
41
);

\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}-e^{h_{*}}(h_{n,m}-h_{*})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\\
\geq\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}-\|e^{h_{*}}(h_{n,m}-h_{*})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}.

Then we apply Lemma
A.2
and (
41
) to obtain

\|e^{h_{n,m}}-e^{h_{*}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\leq C\|(h_{n,m}-h_{*})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega\times\Omega)}+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n+m}{n\,m}}\right).

Theorem 4.5 in
[
12
]
concludes.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a compact set and
\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, then

\left\rvert\frac{1}{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}({\rm P},{\rm Q})-\int hd\mathrm{P}d\mathrm{Q}-\frac{1}{2}\int(h-h_{*})^{2}d\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{6}\|h-h_{*}\|_{\infty}^{3}e^{\|h-h_{*}\|_{\infty}},

for all

, with
f,g\in\mathcal{C}(\Omega)
and
\int e^{{h}}\mathbb{C}\,d({\rm P}\otimes{\rm Q})=1
.

Proof.

The application of Taylor’s theorem to the exponential gives

\rvert e^{x}-1-x-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{6}\rvert x^{3}\rvert e^{\rvert x\rvert}.

(43)

Since also
\int e^{h_{*}}\mathbb{C}\,d({\rm P}\otimes{\rm Q})=1
, (
5
) yields

\frac{1}{\epsilon}S_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})=\int h_{*}d\mathrm{P}\mathrm{Q}\\
-\int\left(\,e^{{h_{*}}}-e^{h}\right)\mathbb{C}\,d({\rm P}\otimes{\rm Q}).

We can decompose

-\int\left(\,e^{{h_{*}}}-e^{h}\right)\mathbb{C}\,d({\rm P}\otimes{\rm Q})=\int\left(\,e^{h-{h_{*}}}-1\right)e^{h_{*}}\mathbb{C}\,d({\rm P}\otimes{\rm Q})

and, finally, (
43
) implies

\displaystyle\left\rvert\frac{1}{\epsilon}S_{1}(\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q})-\int h_{*}d(\mathrm{P}\otimes\mathrm{Q})-\int\left(h-h_{*}+\frac{1}{2}(h-h_{*})^{2}\right)d\pi_{\mathrm{P},\mathrm{Q}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{6}\|h-h_{*}\|_{\infty}^{3}e^{\|h-h_{*}\|_{\infty}}.

Since (
5

∎

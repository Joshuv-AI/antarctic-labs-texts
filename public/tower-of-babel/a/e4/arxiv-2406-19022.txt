Introduction

Let
\mathbb{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
with the uniform probability measure.
For a permutation
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
let
\prec_{\pi}
denote the partial order on
[n]

given by

if both
i<j
and
\pi(i)<\pi(j)
.
Let
X_{\pi}
denote the order complex of the poset
([n],\prec_{\pi})
, i.e. the simplicial complex
on the vertex set
[n]
whose
m
-simplices are all
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{m}\}\subset[n]

such that
i_{0}<\cdots<i_{m}
and
\pi(i_{0})<\cdots<\pi(i_{m})
.
The following result had been proved in an equivalent formulation by Przytycki and Silvero
[
3
]
, and independently but later by Chacholski, Levi and Meshulam
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
,
2
]
)
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
the complex
X_{\pi}
is either contractible or is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres

X_{\pi}\simeq S^{k_{1}}\vee\cdots\vee S^{k_{m}}.

(1)

Conversely, for any
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\geq 0
there exist an
n\geq 1
and
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
such that (
1
) holds.

Example 1.2
.

Let

\pi=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1234567\\
3254176\end{array}\right).

Then
X_{\pi}
depicted in Figure 1 is homotopy equivalent to

.

for

Let
r\geq 0
. Recall that a nonempty space

is
r
-connected if for all
0\leq i\leq r
,
any continuous map from the
i
-sphere

to

can be extended continuously to the
(i+1)
-th ball.
By convention, any nonempty space is
(-1)
-connected, while the empty set is not
(-1)
-connected.
Note that
X_{\pi}
is

-connected if either
X_{\pi}
is contractible, or if

is strictly less than all
k_{i}
’s appearing
in the decomposition (
1
). In this paper we study the connectivity of
X_{\pi}
for a random
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
.
Let

p_{r}(n):={\rm Pr}\left[\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}:X_{\pi}\text{~is~not~}r\text{-connected}\right].

It is easy to check (see Claim
4.2
) that
p_{0}(n)=\frac{2+o(1)}{n}
.
A much deeper result due to
Winkler
[
5
]
asserts that asymptotically almost surely the
1
-skeleton of
X_{\pi}
has diameter
3
.
Let

and for

let

.
Our main result is the following

.

Let

and

. Then

(i)

p_{r}(n)\leq 40^{r+1}\frac{(\log 3n)^{2r}}{n}.

(ii)

p_{r}(n)\geq\frac{1}{r!}\frac{(\log n)^{r}}{n}-c_{r}\frac{(\log n)^{r-1}}{n}.

For the proof of the upper bound it will be convenient to use an equivalent model for random permutation complexes (see e.g.
[
5
]
). Let
\leq
be the standard partial order on
{\twelvebb R}^{2}
, i.e.
q=(a,b)\leq q^{\prime}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})

if
a\leq a^{\prime}
and
b\leq b^{\prime}
. For
n\geq 1
and an
n
-tuple
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in({\twelvebb R}^{2})^{n}
let

Y_{q}
denote the order complex of
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}
, i.e. the simplicial complex on the vertex set
[n]
whose
m
-simplices are the subsets
\{i_{0},\ldots,i_{m}\}
such that

q_{i_{0}}<\cdots<q_{i_{m}}
. Let
\Omega_{n}=([0,1]^{2})^{n}
denote the probability space of
n
-tuples
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})

where the
q_{i}
’s are picked independently and uniformly from
[0,1]^{2}
. Let

denote the subspace of all

For

As

2
A Sufficient Condition for
r
-Connectivity

Let
K
be a finite simplicial complex and let
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be a family of subcomplexes
of
K
such that
\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}K_{j}=K
. For a nonempty
J\subset[m]
let
K_{J}=\bigcap_{j\in J}K_{j}
.
The
nerve

N(\mathcal{K})
of
\mathcal{K}
is the simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]

whose simplices are all
J\subset[m]
such that
K_{J}\neq\emptyset
.
As usual, let
\pi_{0}(K)
denote the set of connected components of
K
and for
i\geq 1
let
\pi_{i}(K)
denote the
i
-th homotopy group of
K
.
We shall need Björner’s version of the nerve theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 0
. Assume that
K_{J}
is either empty or
(r-|J|+1)
-connected for all
\emptyset\neq J\subset[m]
such that

|J|\leq r+1
. Then

\pi_{i}(K)\cong\pi_{i}(N(\mathcal{K}))
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.
In particular,
K
is
r
-connected iff
N(\mathcal{K})
is
r
-connected.

Fix
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\Omega_{n}^{\prime}
where
q_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})
and let

M(q)=\left\{i\in[n]:q_{i}\text{~is~a~minimal~element~of~}\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}\right\}.

For a subset
S\subset[n]
let

Y_{q}[S]=\{\sigma\in Y_{q}:\sigma\subset S\}
denote the induced subcomplex of
Y_{q}
on the vertex set
S
.
For
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
let
q_{i}\vee q_{j}=(\max\{a_{i},a_{j}\},\max\{b_{i},b_{j}\})
and
let

Y_{q,ij}=Y_{q}\left[\left\{\ell:q_{\ell}\geq q_{i}\vee q_{j}\right\}\right].

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
. If
Y_{q,ij}
is
(r-1)
-connected for all distinct
i,j\in M(q)
then

Y_{q}
is
r
-connected.

Proof.
Let
K=Y_{q}
and for
i\in M(q)
let

K_{i}=Y_{q}\left[\{j:q_{j}\geq q_{i}\}\right]
.
Then
K=\bigcup_{i\in M(q)}K_{i}
.
Let
\emptyset\neq J\subset M(q)
such that
|J|\leq r+1
. If
J=\{j\}
is a singleton, then

K_{J}=K_{j}
is a cone with apex
j
, hence contractible and therefore
\ell
-connected for all
\ell
.
Suppose on the other hand that
|J|\geq 2
. Let
i,j\in J
satisfy

a_{i}=\max\{a_{t}:t\in M(q)\}
and
b_{j}=\max\{b_{t}:t\in M(q)\}
. Then

K_{J}=Y_{q,ij}
. By assumption
K_{J}
is
(r-1)
-connected
and thus
(r-|J|+1)
-connected. Therefore
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in M(q)}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
.
As
K_{J}\neq\emptyset
for all
J\subset M(q)
, it follows that the nerve
N(\{K_{i}\}_{i\in M(q)})
is a simplex and hence
r
-connected. Therefore

K=Y_{q}
is
r
-connected as well.

\Box

3
A Key Estimate

For
k\geq 1
let
Q_{k}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in{\twelvebb R}^{k}:x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leq 1\}

denote the
k
-simplex in
{\twelvebb R}^{k}
and let
\Delta_{k}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1})\in{\twelvebb R}^{k+1}:x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}x_{i}=1\}

denote the
k
-simplex in
{\twelvebb R}^{k+1}
. Recall (see e.g. Exercise 13, Chapter 10 in
[
4
]
) that for any integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k+1}\geq 0

\begin{split}&\int_{x\in\Delta_{k}}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k+1}^{\alpha_{k+1}}dx=\sqrt{k+1}\,\int_{x\in Q_{k}}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i})^{\alpha_{k+1}}dx\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{k+1}\,\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{k+1}!}{(k+\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k+1})!}.\end{split}

(2)

In particular, the
k
-dimensional volume of
\Delta_{k}

is
{\rm vol}_{k}(\Delta_{k})=\sqrt{k+1}\,{\rm vol}_{k}(Q_{k})=\frac{\sqrt{k+1}}{k!}
.
For
p\geq 1
let
A_{k,p}=\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})\in{\twelvebb Z}^{p}:\alpha_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}=k\}
. For
k,\ell\geq 0
define

I(k,\ell)={\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-2}\int_{(x,y)\in\Delta_{2}^{2}}\left(x_{1}y_{3}+x_{2}(y_{2}+y_{3})+x_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2})\right)^{k}(x_{3}y_{3})^{\ell}dx\,dy.

The main result of this section is the following estimate.

Proposition 3.1
.

I(k,\ell)\leq\frac{40}{k+2}\binom{k+\ell+2}{2}^{-1}\binom{k+\ell}{k}^{-1}(1+\log(k+1)).

(3)

The proof of Proposition
3.1
depends on some preliminary observations.
For integers
N,t\geq 0
such that
N\geq 2t
let

F(N,t)=\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+t}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}.

Claim 3.2
.

F(N,t)\leq 1+\min\left\{t,\frac{N}{t}\right\}.

Proof.
The bound
F(N,t)\leq 1+t
is clear.
For the other inequality observe that if
0\leq i\leq t
then

\begin{split}&\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+t}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}=\frac{\left(\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i\right)!\left(\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor-i\right)!}{\left(\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+t\right)!\left(\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor-t\right)!}\\
&=\prod_{j=0}^{t-i-1}\frac{\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor-i-j}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+t-j}\leq\left(\frac{\frac{N}{2}-i}{\frac{N}{2}+t}\right)^{t-i}=\left(1-\frac{t+i}{\frac{N}{2}+t}\right)^{t-i}\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{(t+i)(t-i)}{\frac{N}{2}+t}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{N}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{i^{2}}{N}\right).\end{split}

(4)

Moreover,

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\exp\left(\frac{i^{2}}{N}\right)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(\frac{x^{2}}{N}\right)dx=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2j}}{N^{j}j!}\right)dx\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N^{j}j!}\int_{0}^{t}x^{2j}dx=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2j+1}}{N^{j}j!(2j+1)}\\
&\leq\frac{N}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2(j+1)}}{N^{j+1}(j+1)!}=\frac{N}{t}\left(\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{N}\right)-1\right).\end{split}

(5)

Combining (
4
) and (
5
) it follows that

\begin{split}F(N,t)&=1+\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}\\
&\leq 1+\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{N}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\exp\left(\frac{i^{2}}{N}\right)\\
&\leq 1+\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{N}\right)\cdot\frac{N}{t}\left(\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}}{N}\right)-1\right)<1+\frac{N}{t}.\end{split}

\Box

Corollary 3.3
.

For any
\ell,j\geq 0

\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+j}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}\leq\frac{10(\ell+j+1)}{j+1}.

(6)

Proof.
Write
N=2\ell+j
. Then

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil-\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil-1}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil-\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}+\sum_{i=\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil}^{j}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil-\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil}\binom{N}{\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor+i}^{-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}\\
&=2\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}-\frac{1+(-1)^{N}}{2}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil}^{-1}.\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}&\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+j}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}=\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}\\
&\leq 2\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\binom{N}{\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil+i}^{-1}=2F(N,\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor)\\
&\leq 2+2\min\left\{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor,\frac{N}{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\right\}=2+2\min\left\{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor,\frac{2\ell+j}{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\right\}\\
&\leq\frac{10(\ell+j+1)}{j+1}.\end{split}

\Box

Claim 3.4
.

For integers
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\geq 0

{\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-1}\int_{y\in\Delta_{2}}y_{3}^{\beta_{1}}(y_{2}+y_{3})^{\beta_{2}}(y_{1}+y_{2})^{\beta_{3}}dy\leq\frac{2\beta_{1}!\beta_{3}!}{(\beta_{2}+1)(\beta_{1}+\beta_{3}+1)!}.

(7)

Proof.

Applying a change of variables
\phi:\{(z_{1},z_{2}):0\leq z_{1}\leq z_{2}\leq 1\}\rightarrow\Delta_{2}

given by
\phi(z_{1},z_{2})=(1-z_{2},z_{2}-z_{1},z_{1})
with Jacobian
|J_{\phi}(z_{1},z_{2})|=\sqrt{3}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&{\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-1}\int_{y\in\Delta_{2}}y_{3}^{\beta_{1}}(y_{2}+y_{3})^{\beta_{2}}(y_{1}+y_{2})^{\beta_{3}}dy\\
&=\sqrt{3}\,{\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-1}\int_{0\leq z_{1}\leq z_{2}\leq 1}z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}z_{2}^{\beta_{2}}(1-z_{1})^{\beta_{3}}dz_{1}\,dz_{2}\\
&=2\int_{z_{1}=0}^{1}z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}(1-z_{1})^{\beta_{3}}\left(\int_{z_{2}=z_{1}}^{1}z_{2}^{\beta_{2}}dz_{2}\right)dz_{1}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{\beta_{2}+1}\int_{z_{1}=0}^{1}z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}(1-z_{1})^{\beta_{3}}dz_{1}\\
&=\frac{2\beta_{1}!\beta_{3}!}{(\beta_{2}+1)(\beta_{1}+\beta_{3}+1)!}.\end{split}

\Box

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

\begin{split}&I(k,\ell)={\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-2}\int_{(x,y)\in\Delta_{2}^{2}}\left(x_{1}y_{3}+x_{2}(y_{2}+y_{3})+x_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2})\right)^{k}(x_{3}y_{3})^{\ell}dx\,dy\\
&=\sum_{\alpha\in A_{k,3}}\binom{k}{\alpha_{1};\alpha_{2};\alpha_{3}}{\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-2}\int_{(x,y)\in\Delta_{2}^{2}}(x_{1}y_{3})^{\alpha_{1}}(x_{2}(y_{2}+y_{3}))^{\alpha_{2}}(x_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2}))^{\alpha_{3}}(x_{3}y_{3})^{\ell}dx\,dy\\
&=\sum_{\alpha\in A_{k,3}}\frac{k!}{\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\alpha_{3}!}\left({\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-1}\int_{x\in\Delta_{2}}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}x_{3}^{\alpha_{3}+\ell}dx\right)\left({\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-1}\int_{y\in\Delta_{2}}y_{3}^{\alpha_{1}+\ell}(y_{2}+y_{3})^{\alpha_{2}}(y_{1}+y_{2})^{\alpha_{3}}dy\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\alpha\in A_{k,3}}\frac{k!}{\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\alpha_{3}!}\cdot\left(\frac{2\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!(\alpha_{3}+\ell)!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2(\alpha_{1}+\ell)!\alpha_{3}!}{(\alpha_{2}+1)(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3}+\ell+1)!}\right)\\
&=\frac{4k!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{\alpha\in A_{k,3}}\frac{(\alpha_{1}+\ell)!(\alpha_{3}+\ell)!}{(\alpha_{2}+1)(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3}+\ell+1)!}\\
&=\frac{4k!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{(k-j+1)(j+\ell+1)!}\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3})\in A_{j,2}}(\alpha_{1}+\ell)!(\alpha_{3}+\ell)!\\
&=\frac{4k!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(2\ell+j)!}{(k-j+1)(j+\ell+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}\\
&=\frac{4k!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\ell!(\ell+j)!}{(k-j+1)(j+\ell+1)!}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+j}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{2\ell+j}{\ell+i}^{-1}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\frac{4k!\ell!}{(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{(k-j+1)(j+\ell+1)}\cdot\frac{10(\ell+j+1)}{j+1}\\
&=\frac{40k!\ell!}{(k+2)(k+\ell+2)!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{k-j+1}+\frac{1}{j+1}\right)\\
&=\frac{40}{k+2}\binom{k+\ell+2}{2}^{-1}\binom{k+\ell}{k}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\frac{1}{j}\\
&\leq\frac{40}{k+2}\binom{k+\ell+2}{2}^{-1}\binom{k+\ell}{k}^{-1}(1+\log(k+1)),\end{split}

where (a) follows from (
2
) and (

\Box

4
The Upper Bound

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
(i). We first obtain a recursive upper bound on

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
n\geq 3
. Then

p_{r}(n)\leq 20\left(1+\log n\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\frac{p_{r-1}(n-2-k)}{k+2}\right).\\

(8)

Proof.
For
1\leq i<j\leq n
and a subset
D\subset[n]\setminus\{i,j\}
let

R_{i,j,D}
denote the set of all
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\Omega_{n}

(C1)

i,j\in M(q)
.

(C2)

\{t\in[n]\setminus\{i,j\}:q_{t}\ngeq q_{i}\vee q_{j}\}=D
.

Fix
c=(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in[0,1]^{4}
such that

and

,
and let

\begin{split}E_{-}(c)&=[0,a_{1}]\times[0,b_{1}]\cup[0,a_{2}]\times[0,b_{2}]\setminus\{(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\},\\
E_{+}(c)&=[a_{2},1]\times[b_{1},1],\\
E(c)&=[0,1]^{2}\setminus\left(E_{-}(c)\cup E_{+}(c)\right).\end{split}

Figure 2:
E_{-}(c),E(c),E_{+}(c)

Suppose now that
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
satisfies

, see Figure 2.
Then

for

,

Writing

\begin{split}x&=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(a_{1},a_{2}-a_{1},1-a_{2}),\\
y&=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=(b_{2},b_{1}-b_{2},1-b_{1}),\end{split}

it follows that

\begin{split}&{\rm Pr}\left[~q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in R_{i,j,D}~|~q_{i}=(a_{1},b_{1}),q_{j}=(a_{2},b_{2})\right]\\
&={\rm vol}_{2}\left(E(c)\right)^{|D|}{\rm vol}_{2}\left(E_{+}(c)\right)^{n-2-|D|}\\
&=\left(a_{1}(1-b_{1})+(a_{2}-a_{1})(1-b_{2})+(1-a_{2})b_{1}\right)^{|D|}\left((1-a_{2})(1-b_{1})\right)^{n-2-|D|}\\
&=\left(x_{1}y_{3}+x_{2}(y_{2}+y_{3})+x_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2})\right)^{|D|}\left(x_{3}y_{3}\right)^{n-2-|D|}.\end{split}

(9)

Combining (
9
) and (
3
) and noting that the probability that
q_{i}
and
q_{j}
are incomparable is
\frac{1}{2}

we obtain

\begin{split}&{\rm Pr}\left[R_{i,j,D}\right]=\frac{1}{2}{\rm vol}_{2}(\Delta_{2})^{-2}\int_{(x,y)\in\Delta_{2}^{2}}\left(x_{1}y_{3}+x_{2}(y_{2}+y_{3})+x_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2})\right)^{|D|}\left(x_{3}y_{3}\right)^{n-2-|D|}dx\,dy\\
&=\frac{1}{2}I(|D|,n-2-|D|)\leq\frac{20}{|D|+2}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\binom{n-2}{|D|}^{-1}\left(1+\log(|D|+1)\right).\end{split}

Proposition
2.2
implies that if
Y_{q}
is not
r
-connected then there exist
1\leq i<j\leq n
and

D\subset[n]\setminus\{i,j\}
such that

q\in R_{i,j,D}
and the induced complex
Y_{q}\left[[n]\setminus(D\cup\{i,j\})\right]
is not
(r-1)
-connected.
Therefore by the union bound

\begin{split}&p_{r}(n)\leq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{D\subset[n]\setminus\{i,j\}}{\rm Pr}\left[q\in R_{i,j,D}:Y_{q}\left[[n]\setminus(D\cup\{i,j\})\right]\text{~is~not~}(r-1)\text{-connected}\right]\\
&=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{D\subset[n]\setminus\{i,j\}}{\rm Pr}\left[R_{i,j,D}\right]p_{r-1}(n-2-|D|)\\
&\leq\binom{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-2}{k}\left(\frac{20}{k+2}\binom{n}{2}^{-1}\binom{n-2}{k}^{-1}\left(1+\log n\right)\right)\cdot p_{r-1}(n-2-k)\\
&=20\left(1+\log n\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\frac{p_{r-1}(n-2-k)}{k+2}\\
&=20\left(1+\log n\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\frac{p_{r-1}(n-2-k)}{k+2}\right).\end{split}

\Box

We also need the following observation.

Claim 4.2
.

(i)
\frac{1}{n}\leq p_{0}(n)\leq\frac{4}{n}
for
n\geq 2
. (ii)
p_{0}(n)=\frac{2+o(1)}{n}
.

Proof.

X_{\pi}
is disconnected iff there exists a
1\leq k\leq n-1

such that
1\leq\pi(i)\leq n-k
for all
k+1\leq i\leq n
.
It follows that for
n\geq 5

\begin{split}p_{0}(n)&\leq\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k!(n-k)!\leq\frac{2}{n}+\frac{4}{n(n-1)}+(n-5)\binom{n}{3}^{-1}\\
&\leq\min\left\{\frac{4}{n},\frac{2}{n}+\frac{10}{n(n-1)}\right\}.\end{split}

This proves the upper bounds in (i) and (ii). For the lower bounds note that if
n\geq 2
then

{\rm Pr}[X_{\pi}{\rm~is~disconnected~}]\geq{\rm Pr}[\pi(1)=n{\rm~or~}\pi(n)=1]=\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{n(n-1)}.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(i).
We argue by induction on
r
. The induction base
r=0
follows from Claim
4.2
(i).
Assume now that
r\geq 1
.
Applying (

) and the induction hypothesis, it follows that

\begin{split}&p_{r}(n)\leq 20\left(1+\log n\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\frac{p_{r-1}(n-2-k)}{k+2}\right)\\
&\leq 20\log 3n\left(\frac{1}{n}+40^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\frac{\log(3(n-k-2))^{2(r-1)}}{(k+2)(n-k-2)}\right)\\
&\leq 20\log 3n\left(\frac{1}{n}+40^{r}(\log 3n)^{2(r-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\frac{1}{(k+2)(n-k-2)}\right)\\
&=20\log 3n\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{40^{r}(\log 3n)^{2(r-1)}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\left(\frac{1}{k+2}+\frac{1}{n-k-2}\right)\right)\\
&=20\log 3n\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{40^{r}(\log 3n)^{2(r-1)}}{n}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\frac{1}{k}\right)\right)\\

The proof of Theorem

(ii) depends on a homotopy decomposition result for permutation complexes given in

.
For
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
and a subset

, let
\psi_{A}(\pi)\in\mathbb{S}_{|A|}
denote the permutation pattern of
\pi

restricted to
A
, i.e. if

where

\psi_{A}(\pi)=\sigma^{-1}

where

\pi(a_{\sigma(1)})<\cdots<\pi(a_{\sigma(k)})
.
For
1\leq t\leq n
define

\pi_{t}^{\prime}=\psi_{[n]\setminus\{t\}}(\pi)\in\mathbb{S}_{n-1}~~~,~~~\pi_{t}^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{[n]\setminus[t]}(\pi)\in\mathbb{S}_{n-t}.

When

, we view
\pi_{t}^{\prime\prime}
as the empty permutation and define
X_{\pi_{t}^{\prime\prime}}=\emptyset
.

Let
\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
and let
i=\pi^{-1}(1),j=\pi^{-1}(2)
.
As usual, let
\Sigma X=S^{0}*X
denote the suspension of a complex

.

(

]
)
.

Let

.

Proposition 5.2
.

For

and

p_{r}(n)\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(1+\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i-1)p_{r-1}(n-i)\right).

(10)

Proof.
Fix

(ii)

\begin{split}&{\rm Pr}\left[\pi\in\mathbb{S}_{n}:\pi^{-1}(1)=i,\pi^{-1}(2)=j,X_{\pi}\text{~is~not~}r\text{-connected}\right]\\

Summing (

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(ii).
We argue by induction on

follows from Claim

(i).
Assume now that

) and the induction hypothesis we obtain

\begin{split}p_{r}(n)&\geq\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(i-1)p_{r-1}(n-i)\\
&=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-1-i)p_{r-1}(i)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-1-i)\left(\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\frac{(\log i)^{r-1}}{i}-c_{r-1}\frac{(\log i)^{r-2}}{i}\right)\\

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we studied the topological connectivity of the order complex
Y_{q}
of a sequence of
n
random points
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})

in the unit square. More generally, let
Y_{d,q}
denote the order complex determined by sequence of
n
points

q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\Omega_{d,n}=\left([0,1]^{d}\right)^{n}
. Our work suggests a number of directions for further research.

•

One natural problem is to close the gap between the lower and upper bounds in Theorem
1.3
. It seems likely
that the lower bound
p_{r}(n)=\Omega\left(\frac{(\log n)^{r}}{n}\right)
is closer to the truth.

•

Theorem
1.1
asserts that
Y_{2,q}=Y_{q}
is a wedge of spheres. Is there a simple
characterization of the homotopy type of
Y_{d,q}
for general
d
?

•

Let
p_{d,r}(n)
denote the probability that
Y_{d,q}
is
r
-connected for a random
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\Omega_{d,n}
.
It would be interesting to extend Theorem
1.3
to general
d
.
Our proof of the upper bound
p_{2,r}(n)=p_{r}(n)=O\left(\frac{(\log n)^{2r}}{n}\right)
does not use very specific properties of two dimensional posets (such as Theorem
1.1
and the homotopy decomposition in Proposition
5.1
), and may thus be
relevant to higher dimensions as well.

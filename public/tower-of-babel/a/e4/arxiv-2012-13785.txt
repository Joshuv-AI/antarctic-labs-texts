Many-body entanglement in fermion systems
N. Gigena, M. Di Tullio, R. Rossignoli

Abstract
We discuss a general bipartite-like representation and Schmidt decomposition of an arbitrary pure state of N N indistinguishable fermions, based on states of M < N M<N and ( N CLOSE (N - OPEN M ) M) fermions. It is directly connected with the reduced M M - and ( N CLOSE (N - OPEN M ) M) -body density matrices (DMs), which have the same spectrum in such states. The concept of M M -body entanglement emerges naturally in this scenario, generalizing that of one-body entanglement. Rigorous majorization relations satisfied by the normalized M M -body DM are then derived, which imply that the associated entropy will not increase, on average, under a class of operations which have these DMs as post-measurement states. Moreover, such entropy is an upper bound to the bipartite entanglement entropy generated by a class of operations which map the original state to a bipartite state of M M and N − M N-M effectively distinguishable fermions. Analytic evaluation of the spectrum of M M -body DMs in some strongly correlated fermionic states is also provided.



I
introduction

A remarkable feature of quantum mechanics is the existence of correlations between quantum systems that cannot be emulated by their classical counterpart. Entanglement is the most celebrated manifestation of such correlations and it has been object of intense research in quantum physics, particularly within the field of quantum information theory
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
. Particle indistinguishability is another fundamental feature of quantum mechanics, lying at the heart of condensed matter physics and quantum field theories. An interesting problem combining these two fundamental concepts is that of the study and quantification of correlations between indistinguishable particles, a topic that has received increasing attention in the last years
Benatti et al. 2020
. Indistinguishability poses a nontrivial difficulty in the study of quantum correlations, because the notion of entanglement is intimately connected with that of local operations, and the latter are possible only if the constituents of the system can be distinguished. Different approaches to this problem have been considered, like mode entanglement
Zanardi 2002
;
Shi 2003
;
Friis et al. 2013
, extensions based on correlations between observables
Barnum et al. 2004
;
Zanardi et al. 2004
;
Sasaki et al. 2011
;
Balachandran et al. 2013
;
Benatti et al. 2014

and entanglement beyond symmetrization

Schliemann et al. 2001a
;
Schliemann et al. 2001b
;
Eckert et al. 2002
;
Ghirardi et al. 2002
;
Paškauskas and You 2001
;
Wiseman and Vaccaro 2003
;
Iemini and Vianna 2013
;
Oszmaniec and Kuś 2013
;
Sárosi and Lévay 2014
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
;
Majtey et al. 2016
, which is independent of the choice of single particle (sp) basis. The relation between these forms of entanglement has been analysed by different authors

Benatti et al. 2020
;
Friis et al. 2013
;
Wiseman and Vaccaro 2003
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
;
Dasenbrook et al. 2016
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2017
;

;
Di Tullio et al. 2018
;
Di Tullio et al. 2019
;

;

;
Debarba et al. 2020
as well as the question regarding whether symmetrization correlations should be addressed as entanglement

;

;
Compagno et al. 2018
;

;

In this paper we explore the generalization of the bipartite formulation devised in
Gigena et al. 2020
for the notion of one-body entanglement, a measure of correlations beyond (anti)symmetrization introduced in
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
. We start by considering operators on the fermion Fock space of the system creating general
M
-fermion states, which are used to show that a general
N
-fermion state can be always written as a bipartite-like state of
M<N
and
N-M
fermions. This bipartite representation is connected with the definition of the
M
and
N-M
-body reduced density matrices (DMs)
Carlson and Keller 1961
;
Ando 1963
;
Amosov and Filippov 2017
in a way that closely resembles the case of bipartite states of distinguishable constituents. The ensuing
(M,N-M)
Schmidt-like decomposition of the state determines the diagonal form of these DMs, entailing they share the same non-zero eigenvalues. Pushing forward this analogy we propose to link the correlations between
M
and
N-M
-body observables, which we call
M
-body entanglement
, to the mixedness of the
M
-body DM, as formalized by eigenvalue majorization. We introduce a family of entropic measures of such correlations and show that there exists a class of operations not increasing the amount of these correlations in any
N
-fermion state. Finally, we prove the existence of a family of quantum maps converting
N
-fermion states into bipartite states of effectively distinguishable
M
and
N-M
-fermions, in the sense of occupying orthogonal sp subspaces. Conversion by means of any of these maps is such that the entanglement entropy of the bipartite target state is bounded from above by the
M
-body entropy, assigning the latter a clear operational meaning. Explicit examples of
M
-body DMs and their eigenvalues in some physical states are also provided.

III
M
-body Entanglement

III.1
Mixedness of the
M
-body DM

The Schmidt-like decomposition (
23
) of an
N
-fermion pure state, and the fact that the positive numbers
\lambda^{(M)}_{\nu}
represent the non-zero eigenvalues of both the
M
and
(N-M)
-body DM’s, naturally lead to a notion of
M
-body entanglement based on the spread of these eigenvalues. It characterizes the correlations between
M
and
(N-M)
-body observables in an
N
-fermion state. More precisely, given
two pure states
|\Psi\rangle
,
|\Phi\rangle
of
N
fermions, we will say that
|\Psi\rangle
is
not less
(M,N-M)
entangled
, or simply
not less
M
-body entangled
than
|\Phi\rangle
, if
\rho^{(M)}_{\Psi}
is
more mixed than
(or equally mixed as)
\rho^{(M)}_{\Phi}
, i.e., if their eigenvalues satisfy the majorization relation

\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{\Psi}^{(M)})\prec\bm{\lambda}({\rho}_{\Phi}^{(M)})\,,

(45)

where
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)})
denotes the spectrum of
\rho^{(M)}
, sorted in decreasing order. Explicitly, Eq. (
45
) means that all inequalities
Bhatia 1997
;
Marshall et al. 2011

\sum_{\nu=1}^{j}\lambda_{\nu}(\rho^{(M)}_{\Psi})\leq\sum_{\nu=1}^{j}\lambda_{\nu}(\rho^{(M)}_{\Phi})\,,\;\;j=1,\ldots,{\textstyle\binom{D}{M}}-1\,,

(46)

are to be satisfied by the sorted eigenvalues
\lambda_{\nu}
of
\rho^{(M)}_{\Psi}
and
\rho^{(M)}_{\Phi}
,
with equality for
j=\binom{D}{M}
, implying that those of
\rho_{\Psi}^{(M)}
are more spread out than those of
{\rho}_{\Phi}^{(M)}
. Of course,
one may likewise employ the partner DM
\rho^{(N-M)}
in (
45
)–(
46
) since they share the same non-zero eigenvalues.
For
M=1
we recover the concept of one-body entanglement, determined by the SPDM
\rho^{(1)}

Gigena et al. 2020
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
.

Eq. (
45
) is analogous to that satisfied by local reduced states in systems of distinguishable components (where it warrants that
|\Psi\rangle
can be converted to
|\Phi\rangle
by LOCC operations
Nielsen 1999
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
;
Nielsen and Vidal 2001
). Notice, however, that majorization provides a partial order, entailing that two states may be uncomparable according to previous criterion.

For example, in an
N
-fermion SD,
\rho^{(M)}
presents just
\binom{N}{M}
non-zero eigenvalues equal to
1
, while in the GHZ-like state (
43
), all
2\binom{N}{M}
non-zero eigenvalues of
\rho^{(M)}
are equal to
1/2
for
M<N
, implying

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{\Psi_{D/2}})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{\rm SD})

(47)

for
1\leq M\leq N-1
. Then
the state (
43
) is more entangled than a SD at
any

M
-body level (
1\leq M\leq N-1
).

However, in the pair condensate
|\Psi_{2k}\rangle
of Eq. (
36
) with
2\leq k\leq D/2-1
,
while clearly
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(1)}_{\Psi_{2k}})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(1)}_{\rm SD})

(Eq. (
38
)), neither
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(2)}_{\Psi_{2k}})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(2)}_{\rm SD})
nor
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(2)}_{\rm SD})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(2)}_{\Psi_{2k}})
are fulfilled, since the largest eigenvalue of
\rho^{(2)}_{\Psi_{2k}}
is greater than
1
whereas remaining
\binom{D}{2}-1
eigenvalues are non-zero and lower than
1

(Eqs. (
39
)–(
40
)), with
\binom{D}{2}>\binom{N}{2}
for
D>N
.
Hence, SDs no longer provide the least mixed two-body DM. The same occurs with the three-body DM when its largest eigenvalue in the state (
36
) exceeds
1
(Eq. (
41
)), in which case
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(3)}_{\Psi_{2k}})\nprec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(3)}_{\rm SD})
and
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(3)}_{\rm SD})\nprec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(3)}_{\Psi_{2k}})
.

III.2
M
-body entropies

Associated with previous definition
(
45
), we may first consider the
M
-body entropies

\displaystyle E_{f}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{f}(\rho_{\Psi}^{(M)})=S_{f}(\rho_{\Psi}^{(N-M)})\,,

(48a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}f[\lambda_{\nu}(\rho^{(M)}_{\Psi})]\,,

(48b)

where
S_{f}(\rho)={\rm Tr}f(\rho)
is a trace-form entropy
Canosa and Rossignoli 2002
;
Rossignoli and Canosa 1999
, with
f:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

a strictly concave non-negative function complying with
f(0)=0
. These entropies will satisfy

E_{f}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)\geq E_{f}^{(M)}(|\Phi\rangle)

(49)

whenever Eq. (
45
) is fulfilled
Bhatia 1997
;
Marshall et al. 2011
;
Rossignoli and Canosa 2002
.

Eq. (
48
) is particularly suitable for defining a one-body entanglement entropy
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
;
Gigena et al. 2020
since
\lambda^{(1)}_{\nu}\in[0,1]
and standard entropic measures can be employed. For
M\geq 2

it is possible to employ measures such as the bosonic-like entropy, obtained for

f(\lambda)=-\lambda\log\lambda+(1+\lambda)\log(1+\lambda)

(such that
\sum_{\nu}f(\lambda_{\nu})
represents the von Neumann entropy of independent bosons in the grand canonical ensemble with average occupation numbers
\lambda_{\nu}

Rossignoli and Canosa 1999
), which is just an example of an increasing concave function of
\lambda
satisfying
f(0)=0
.

A second possibility, strongly motivated by the majorization relations derived in the next subsections, is to consider the entropy of the normalized densities

\rho_{n}^{(M)}=\rho^{(M)}/{\textstyle\binom{N}{M}}\,,

(50)

which have eigenvalues
\lambda^{(M)}_{\nu}/\binom{N}{M}\in[0,1]

and satisfy
{\rm Tr}[\rho_{n}^{(M)}]=1
. In this case we may employ any entropic measure
S_{f}(\rho)={\rm Tr}\,f(\rho)
intended for standard probabilities and define an associated
M
-body entropy as

\displaystyle E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{f}(\rho_{n\Psi}^{(M)})=S_{f}(\rho_{n\Psi}^{(N-M)})\,.

(51)

In particular, the von Neumann entropy
S(\rho)=-{\rm Tr}\rho\log_{2}\rho
leads to

S(\rho^{(M)}_{n})=S(\rho^{(M)})/\binom{N}{M}+\log_{2}\binom{N}{M}
.
Other Schur-concave functions
Bhatia 1997
of
\rho_{n}^{(M)}
can also be used.
Since at fixed
N
Eq. (
45
) is fully equivalent to

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n\Psi})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n\Phi})\,,

(52)

as all eigenvalues are just rescaled by the same factor, it will also imply
E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)\geq E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Phi\rangle)
. And any pair of
N
-fermion states states uncomparable with (
45
) will remain uncomparable with (
52
), and viceversa.

On the other hand, Eq. (
52
) can also be used to compare the mixedness of reduced DMs
\rho^{(M)}_{n}
for states with distinct
N
, as
\rho^{(M)}_{n}
has fixed trace, implying

\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{n\Psi}^{(M)})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{n\Phi}^{(M)})\Rightarrow E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)\geq E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Phi\rangle)\,.

(53)

We finally remark that the converse of Eq. (
53
)
does not hold in general: Only if the entropic inequality
E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)\geq E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Phi\rangle)
holds for
all
concave functions
f
(and not just a particular choice) it can be ensured that
\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{n\Psi}^{(M)})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{n\Phi}^{(M)})

Rossignoli and Canosa 2002
. And this implies
\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{\Psi}^{(M)})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{\Phi}^{(M)})
only when
\rho_{\Psi}^{(M)}
and
\rho_{\Phi}^{(M)}
have the same trace, i.e.
|\Psi\rangle
and
|\Phi\rangle
the same fermion number.
For states with distinct
N
Eq. (
45
) is to be replaced by (
52
).

III.3
Operations not increasing the
M
-body entropy

Let us now determine the basic operations which do not increase the
M
-body entropy (
51
).
We first note that one-body unitary transformations

|\Psi\rangle\rightarrow{\cal U}|\Psi\rangle\,,\;\;{\cal U}=\exp[-i\sum_{i,j}H_{ij}c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{j}]\,,

(54)

where
H^{\dagger}=H
, lead to unitary transformations of all
M
-body DMs, thus leaving their eigenvalues and hence
M
-body entanglement unchanged:
Since
{\cal U}^{\dagger}c_{i}{\cal U}=\sum_{j}U_{ij}c_{j}
with
U=\exp[-iH]
, then
\rho^{(1)}\rightarrow U\rho^{(1)}U^{\dagger}
and
\rho^{(M)}\rightarrow U^{(M)}\rho^{(M)}U^{(M){\dagger}}
,
with
U^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha\alpha}^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}U_{\alpha_{1}\alpha^{\prime}_{i_{1}}}\ldots U_{\alpha_{m}\alpha^{\prime}_{i_{m}}}
and
\epsilon
the fully antisymmetric tensor.

We now show, for both pure states
|\Psi\rangle
with fixed fermion number
N
and also mixed states

\rho=\sum_{i}q_{i}|\Psi_{i}\rangle\langle\Psi_{i}|\,,

(55)

with definite
N
(
q_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}q_{i}=1
and
\hat{N}|\Psi_{i}\rangle=N|\Psi_{i}\rangle

\forall

), the following theorem:

Theorem 1.
The quantum operation described by the Kraus operators

{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}=\frac{c_{j}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\;\;j=1,\ldots,D

(56)

which satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{D}{{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}}^{\dagger}{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}=\hat{N}/N=\mathbbm{1}_{N}
within the subspace of states with definite fermion number
N
, does not increase, on average, the mixedness of the normalized
M
-body DMs
\rho^{(M)}_{n}=\rho^{(M)}/\binom{N}{M}

for
1\leq M\leq N-1
, and implies the majorization relation

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\prec\sum_{j}p_{j}\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{jn})\,,

(57)

between the spectrum of the initial and post-measurement normalized
M
-body DM’s.

In (
57
)
\rho^{(M)}_{jn}=\rho^{(M)}_{j}/\binom{N-1}{M}
are the normalized
M
-body DMs determined by the post-selected states

\displaystyle\rho_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{j}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{j}^{(1)}\rho{{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}}^{\dagger}=\frac{c_{j}\rho c^{\dagger}_{j}}{\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\rangle}\,,

(58)

while
p_{j}
is the probability of outcome

\displaystyle p_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,[\rho\,{{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}}^{\dagger}{\cal M}_{j}^{(1)}]=\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\rangle/N\,,

(59)

with

.
This measurement, with
D

j

\rho_{j}

Notice that Eq. (
57

S_{f}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\geq S_{f}[\sum_{j}p_{j}\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{jn})]\geq\sum_{j}p_{j}S_{f}(\rho^{(M)}_{jn})\,,

for any Schur-concave function of

of the normalized

.

-body entropy (

E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)\geq\sum_{j}p_{j}E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi_{j}\rangle)\,,

where
|\Psi_{j}\rangle={\cal M}_{j}|\Psi\rangle/\sqrt{p_{j}}=c_{j}|\Psi\rangle/\sqrt{\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\rangle}
is the state after outcome

E^{(M)}_{n_{f}}(\rho)\geq\sum_{j}p_{j}E^{(M)}_{n_{f}}(\rho_{j})\,,

-body
entanglement of formation

,
where the minimum is over all representations (

\rho

E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(\rho)

=\sum_{i}q_{i}E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi_{i}\rangle)\geq\sum_{i,j}q_{i}p_{ij}E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(|\Psi_{ij}\rangle)\geq\sum_{j}p_{j}E_{n_{f}}^{(M)}(\rho_{j})

where

and
p_{ij}=\langle\Psi_{i}|c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}|\Psi_{i}\rangle/N

Proof of Eq. (

Let

\displaystyle({\rho^{(M)}_{j}})_{\bm{\alpha\alpha}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle p_{j}^{-1}\,{\rm Tr}\,{\cal M}_{j}^{(1)}\rho{\cal M}^{(1){\dagger}}_{j}C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}

However, Eq. (
69
) does not hold, in general, for
\rho^{(M)}
with
M\geq 2
,
implying that
M
-body entanglement will not necessarily decrease after such measurement.
A simple analytic example is provided in Appendix B. Essentially, measurement of the occupancy of a sp state reduces the available sp space for “collective pairs” in a state like (
36
), implying a lower maximum eigenvalue
\lambda^{(M)}_{1}
in the post-measurement states and hence violation of the inequality (
69
) by
\rho^{(M)}
with
M\geq 2
. This result is expected as these measurements do not necessarily increase our knowledge of the
M
-body DM for
M\geq 2
.

III.4
M
-body density operators and generalized majorization relations

To each
M
-body DM we can associate an
M
-body density operator (DO)

\hat{\rho}^{(M)}=\sum_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\rho_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}^{(M)}C_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(M){\dagger}}|0\rangle\langle 0|C_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}^{(M)}\,,

(70)

which is the unique mixed state of
M
fermions satisfying

{\rm Tr}\,[\hat{\rho}^{(M)}C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(M)}]=\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}

(71)

\forall

\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}
, due to Eq. (
3
). The normal decomposition (
23
)–(
30
) provides its diagonal representation:

\hat{\rho}^{(M)}=\sum_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu}^{(M)}A_{\nu}^{(M){\dagger}}|0\rangle\langle 0|A_{\nu}^{(M)}\,.

(72)

Now consider again the measurement (
56
) applied on a general mixed state
\rho
of
N
fermions. If the result is unknown, the post-measurement state (with no post-selection)
\rho^{\prime}=\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho_{j}
, is the
N-1
fermion state

\rho^{\prime}=\sum_{j}{\cal M}^{(1)}_{j}\,\rho\,{\cal M}_{j}^{(1)^{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}c_{j}\,\rho\,c_{j}^{\dagger}\,.

(73)

Using Eq. (
65
) for
M=N-1
we obtain

{\rm Tr}\,[\rho^{\prime}\,C^{(N-1){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C^{(N-1)}_{\bm{\alpha}}]=\frac{1}{N}\rho^{(N-1)}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}

(74)

\forall

\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}
, implying
\rho^{\prime}=\hat{\rho}_{n}^{(N-1)}:=\hat{\rho}^{(N-1)}/N
, the normalized
(N-1)
-body DO
(
{\rm Tr}\,\hat{\rho}^{(N-1)}_{n}=1
). In summary,

\hat{\rho}^{(N-1)}=\sum_{j}c_{j}\rho c_{j}^{\dagger}\,,

(75)

The
(N-1)
-body DO is then just proportional to the post-measurement state (
73
), with the operation in (
75
) playing the role of a partial trace. Expressions similar or equivalent to (
75
) have been previously derived in refs.
Carlson and Keller 1961
;
Amosov and Filippov 2017
;
da Silva Souza et al. 2018
, with
Amosov and Filippov 2017
discussing its extension to states with no fixed fermion number.

These results, together with Eqs. (
57
)–(
62
), can be extended to
L
-body measurements, in which
L
fermions are annihilated. Such measurement can be obtained by applying previous measurement
L
times, i.e., by composing it with itself
L
times, and is described by the Kraus operators

{\cal M}^{(L)}_{\bm{\beta}}=\frac{C^{(L)}_{\bm{\beta}}}{\sqrt{\binom{N}{L}}}=\frac{c_{\beta_{1}}\ldots c_{\beta_{L}}}{\sqrt{\binom{N}{L}}}\,,

(76)

which, due to Eq. (
4
) satisfy

\sum_{\bm{\beta}}{\cal M}^{(L){\dagger}}_{\bm{\beta}}{\cal M}^{(L)}_{\bm{\beta}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{\binom{N}{L}}}\sum_{\bm{\beta}}C^{(L){\dagger}}_{\bm{\beta}}C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L)}=\mathbbm{1}_{N}

(77)

within the subspace of
N
-fermion states. Then,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\sum_{\bm{\beta}}C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L)}\rho\,C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L){\dagger}}C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\binom{N-M}{L}}\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}

(78)

\forall

\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}
for
M\leq N-L
. Hence, for
L=N-M
it implies

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(M)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\beta}}C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M)}\rho\,C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M){\dagger}}

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{(N-M)!}}\!\!\!\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N-M}}\!\!\!c_{j_{1}}\ldots c_{j_{N-M}}\rho\,c^{\dagger}_{j_{N-M}}\ldots c^{\dagger}_{j_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\binom{N}{N-M}}\sum_{\bm{\beta}}{\cal M}_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M)}\,\rho\,{\cal M}_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M){\dagger}}\,,

(80)

which generalizes Eq. (
75
). The sum in (
80
) is the post-measurement state (without post-selection)
of the
L=(N-M)
-fermion measurement (
76
).

For general
L\leq N
, this measurement has
\binom{D}{L}
distinct outcomes
\bm{\beta}
, with probabilities

p_{\bm{\beta}}={\rm Tr}\,\rho\,{\cal M}_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L){\dagger}}{\cal M}_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L)}=\rho^{(L)}_{\bm{\beta}\bm{\beta}}/{\textstyle\binom{N}{L}}\,,

(81)

satisfying
\sum_{\bm{\beta}}p_{\bm{\beta}}=1
, and post-selected states

\rho_{\bm{\beta}}=p_{\bm{\beta}}^{-1}\,{\cal M}_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L)}\rho{\cal M}^{(L){\dagger}}_{\bm{\beta}}=C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L)}\rho\,C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(L){\dagger}}/\rho^{(L)}_{\bm{\beta}\bm{\beta}}\,,

(82)

which generalize Eqs. (
58
)–(
59
).
From Eq. (
78
) we then obtain, for the ensuing conditional
M
-body DMs of elements
(\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}})_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}={\rm Tr}\,\rho_{\bm{\beta}}\,C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}
,

\sum_{\bm{\beta}}p_{\bm{\beta}}\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}}=\frac{\binom{N-M}{L}}{\binom{N}{L}}\rho^{(M)}\,,

(83)

for
M\leq N-L
. Eq. (
83
) is equivalent to

\sum_{\bm{\beta}}p_{\bm{\beta}}\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}}/{\textstyle\binom{N-L}{M}}=\rho^{(M)}/{\textstyle\binom{N}{M}}\,,

(84)

and hence to

\rho^{(M)}_{n}=\sum_{\beta}p_{\bm{\beta}}\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}n}\,,

(85)

for the normalized
M
-body DMs. Eq. (
85
) then implies the general majorization relation

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\prec\sum_{\beta}p_{\bm{\beta}}\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}n})\,,

(86)

between the initial and post-measurement conditional normalized
M
-body DMs, which generalizes Eq. (
57
) to the measurement described by the operators (
76
).
The associated entropies satisfy

S_{f}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\geq\sum_{\bm{\beta}}p_{\bm{\beta}}S_{f}(\rho^{(M)}_{\bm{\beta}n})\,.

(87)

For pure states this implies that the entanglement entropies (
51
) will not increase, on average, after these measurements.
The same occurs with the associated entanglement of formation for initial mixed states.

III.5
Mapping to bipartite systems

In the previous sections it was proposed to link the mixedness of either
\rho^{(M)}
or
\rho^{(N-M)}
, as reduced DM’s of a given
N
-fermion state, to the amount of correlations between
M
-body and
N-M
-body observables on such state. In this section we will show this relation is operationally justified by the existence of a class of quantum maps converting states of indistinguishable fermions into states of effectively distinguishable fermions, in such a way that entanglement in the target state is bounded by the entropy (
51
) of the normalized
M
-body DM.

Let
|\Psi\rangle
be an
N
-fermion state with support in a sp subspace
{\cal H}
of dimension
D\geq N
and let
{\cal H}_{A}
be a sp subspace of dimension
D_{A}\geq M

orthogonal
to
{\cal H}
, such that
\{c_{i_{A}},c_{j}\}=\{c^{\dagger}_{i_{A}},c^{\dagger}_{j}\}=\{c_{i_{A}},c^{{\dagger}}_{j}\}=0
for
i_{A}
(
j
) labelling sp states in
{\cal H}_{A}
(
{\cal H}
) (entailing
\langle i_{A}|j\rangle=\langle 0|c_{i_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{j}|0\rangle=0

\forall\,i_{A},j
). They are of course subspaces of a complete sp space
{\cal H}_{T}
such that
{\cal H}\oplus{\cal H}_{A}\subset{\cal H}_{T}
.
Consider now a CPTP map
{\cal T}_{M}
described by Kraus operators

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{N}{M}}}}\sum_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha}}T^{r}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\alpha}}\,C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\mu}}C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}\,,

(88)

where
C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\mu}}=(c^{\dagger}_{i_{1_{A}}},\ldots,c^{\dagger}_{i_{M_{A}}})
creates
M
fermions in
{\cal H}_{A}
while
C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}=(c_{j_{M}},\ldots,c_{j_{1}})
annihilates
M
fermions in
{\cal H}
, and
T^{r}
is a
\binom{D_{A}}{M}\times\binom{D}{M}
matrix. Assuming
{\cal H}_{A}
initially “empty” and the condition

\sum_{r}T^{r{\dagger}}T^{r}=\mathbbm{1}
, they will satisfy

\sum_{r}{\cal T}^{r{\dagger}}{\cal T}^{r}={\textstyle\frac{1}{\binom{N}{M}}}\sum_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},r}(T^{r{\dagger}}T^{r})_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\bm{\alpha}}C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}C^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}}=\mathbbm{1}_{N}

by virtue of Eq. (
4
), within
the Fock space
{\cal F}_{N}({\cal H})
of
N
fermion states with support in
{\cal H}
. Its action on an
N
-fermion state
|\Psi\rangle\in{\cal F}_{N}({\cal H})
is, using Eqs. (
8
) and (
12
),

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{r}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{N}{M}}}}\sum_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}T^{r}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\alpha}}\Gamma^{(M)}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\beta}}C_{\bm{\mu}}^{(M){\dagger}}C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M){\dagger}}|0\rangle

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\beta}}\Gamma^{r}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\beta}}C_{\bm{\mu}}^{(M){\dagger}}C_{\bm{\beta}}^{(N-M){\dagger}}|0\rangle\,,

where
\Gamma^{r}=T^{r}\Gamma^{(M)}/\sqrt{\binom{N}{M}}
. It is verified that the probability of outcome
r
,

p_{r}=\langle\Psi|{\cal T}^{r{\dagger}}{\cal T}^{r}|\Psi\rangle={\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{r{\dagger}}\Gamma^{r})\,,

(90)

satisfies
\sum_{r}p_{r}={\frac{1}{\binom{N}{M}}}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{(M){\dagger}}\Gamma^{(M)})=1
due to Eq. (
16
).

Operators (
88
) can therefore be regarded as Kraus operators describing a CPTP map on the
N
-fermion Fock space that takes, with probability
p_{r}
, the original state
|\Psi\rangle
into the
(M,N-M)
“bipartite” state
|\Phi_{AB}^{r}\rangle=(\sqrt{p_{r}})^{-1}{\cal T}^{r}|\Psi\rangle
containing
N-M
fermions in
{\cal H}

and
M
fermions in the orthogonal sp space
{\cal H}_{A}
.

After the map is implemented with outcome
r
, we can look at the reduced state of the fermionic modes in
{\cal H}_{A}
in the state
|\Phi_{AB}^{r}\rangle
(which will contain
M
fermions),

{\rho}_{A}^{r}={\rm Tr}_{B}|\Phi^{r}_{AB}\rangle\langle\Phi^{r}_{AB}|
,
such that
\langle\Phi_{AB}^{r}|O_{A}|\Phi_{AB}^{r}\rangle={\rm Tr}\,[\rho_{A}^{r}O_{A}]
for any number conserving operator
O_{A}
depending just on operators
c^{\dagger}_{i_{A}}
,
c_{i_{A}}
with support in
{\cal H}_{A}
. Using (
89
) we obtain
{\rho}_{A}^{r}=\sum_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\mu}^{\prime}}(\rho_{A}^{r})_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}^{\prime}}C^{(M){\dagger}}_{\bm{\mu}}|0\rangle\langle 0|C^{(M)}_{\bm{\mu}^{\prime}}
with

\rho_{A}^{r}=\Gamma^{r}\Gamma^{r{\dagger}}/p_{r}\,.

(91)

Similarly
\rho_{B}^{r}=\Gamma^{rT}\Gamma^{r*}/p_{r}
.

As a direct consequence of expression (
91
) we can derive the following important result: If
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{r}_{A})
and
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})
denote again the eigenvalue vectors (sorted in decreasing order) of
\rho_{A}^{r}
and
\rho^{(M)}_{n}=\rho^{(M)}/\binom{N}{M}
respectively, then the following majorization relation holds:

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\prec\sum_{r}p_{r}\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{A}^{r}),

(92)

where the vector of smaller dimension is assumed to be completed with zeros to match the dimensions.

Proof.

From Eqs. (
91
) and (
14
) we obtain

p_{r}\rho_{A}^{r}={\textstyle\frac{1}{\binom{N}{M}}}T^{r}\Gamma^{(M)}\Gamma^{(M){\dagger}}T^{r{\dagger}}=T^{r}\rho^{(M)}_{n}T^{r{\dagger}}\,.

We can also write

\rho^{(M)}_{n}=\sum_{r}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}T^{r{\dagger}}T^{r}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}\,,

which implies, using again (
68
),

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\prec\sum_{r}\bm{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}T^{r{\dagger}}T^{r}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}\right)=\sum_{r}p_{r}\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{A}^{r})

(93)

since the nonzero eigenvalues of

(\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}T^{r{\dagger}})(T^{r}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}})
are the same as those of

T^{r}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}\sqrt{\rho^{(M)}_{n}}T^{r{\dagger}}=p_{r}\rho_{A}^{r}
.
∎

In particular, Eq. (
92
) implies that the average bipartite entanglement entropy between
A
and
B
of the final states, defined by
\sum_{r}p_{r}E_{f}(|\Phi^{r}_{AB}\rangle)

with
E_{f}(|\Phi^{r}_{AB}\rangle=S_{f}(\rho_{A}^{r})=S_{f}(\rho_{B}^{r})
,
will never exceed the
M
-body entropy (
51
)
, which provides, therefore, an
upper bound
to the generated bipartite entanglement:

E_{f_{n}}^{(M)}(|\Psi\rangle)=S_{f}(\rho_{n}^{(M)})\geq\sum_{r}p_{r}S_{f}(\rho_{A}^{r})\,.

(94)

A second immediate corollary of (
92
) is that we can derive a condition for the conversion of a pure
N
-fermion state
|\Psi\rangle
into a bipartite state
|\Phi_{AB}\rangle
of
M
and
N-M
fermions in orthogonal sp spaces, i.e., with
M
fermions occupying sp states in
{\cal H}_{A}
, and
N-M
in
{\cal H}
, by means of a CPTP map described by the operators (
88
): for such conversion to be possible the relation

\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(M)}_{n})\prec\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{A}),

(95)

must hold, where
\rho_{A}
is the reduced state of
A
in
|\Phi_{AB}\rangle
.

Proof.

If the map takes
|\Psi\rangle
into
|\Phi_{AB}\rangle
and is described by the operators (
88
), then for all
r
we have

{\cal T}^{r}|\Psi\rangle=\sqrt{p_{r}}|\Phi_{AB}\rangle,

(96)

with
p_{r}
given by (
90
). It follows that
\rho^{r}_{A}=\rho_{A}

\forall

r
, and by virtue of (
92
), Eq. (
95
) follows.
∎

We have therefore identified a class of quantum maps that could be used to transform a pure state
|\Psi\rangle
of
N
indistinguishable fermions into another state
|\Phi_{AB}\rangle
which contains a definite number of fermions in two orthogonal sp subspaces, such that

M
fermions can be effectively “distinguished” from the remaining
N-M
due the orthogonality (for instance,
M
fermions confined in a spatial region well separated from that of the remaining
N-M
fermions, or in orbitals orthogonal to those occupied in
|\Psi\rangle
). The conversion is such that the entanglement between these two sets of particles in the target state is bounded from above, on average, by the entropy of the normalized
M
-body DM. This provides a clear operational meaning to the suggested link between the mixedness of the eigenvalues of this matrix and the amount of correlations between the indistinguishable particles in
|\Psi\rangle
.

As a trivial example, consider a two fermion state

|\Psi\rangle=(\alpha c^{\dagger}_{1}c^{\dagger}_{2}+\beta c^{\dagger}_{3}c^{\dagger}_{4})|0\rangle\,.

(97)

As mentioned in
II.2
, any two-fermion state with support in a sp subspace of dimension
4
(i.e. occupying just 4 sp levels) can be written in this way
Schliemann et al. 2001b
;
Schliemann et al. 2001a
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2015
.
Its SPDM
\rho^{(1)}
is diagonal in this basis, with
\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{i}\rangle=\delta_{ij}f_{i}
and
f_{i}=|\alpha|^{2}
for
i=1,2
,
f_{i}=|\beta^{2}|
for
i=3,4
and
f_{i}=0
otherwise, such that its nonzero eigenvalues are
\bm{\lambda}(\rho^{(1)})=(|\alpha|^{2},|\alpha|^{2},|\beta|^{2},|\beta|^{2})
, with
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
.
Consider now the simple map
{\cal T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i_{A}}c_{i}
, where
c_{i}
annihilates a fermion in
{\cal H}
(
i=1,\ldots,D
) while

c^{\dagger}_{i_{A}}
creates a fermion in
{\cal H}_{A}
, initially empty (and of dimension
D_{A}\geq D
). Then
{\cal T}^{\dagger}{\cal T}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
in
{\cal F}_{2}({\cal H})
and

{\cal T}|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\alpha(c^{\dagger}_{1_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{2}-c^{\dagger}_{2_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{1})+\beta(c^{\dagger}_{3_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{4}-c^{\dagger}_{4_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{3})]|0\rangle

(98)

is a normalized two-fermion state with one fermion in
{\cal H}_{A}
and one in
{\cal H}
. It leads to reduced states
\rho_{A(B)}
with spectrum
\bm{\lambda}(\rho_{A(B)})=(|\alpha|^{2},|\alpha|^{2},|\beta|^{2},|\beta|^{2})/2
identical to that of
\rho^{(1)}/2
, thus saturating the inequalities of the majorization relation (
92
). We note that even if
\beta=0
, in which case
|\Psi\rangle
is a SD,
{\cal T}|\Psi\rangle
is no longer a SD and has therefore non-zero one-body entanglement, in agreement with the result that bipartite entanglement with fixed fermion number or number parity at each side requires one-body entanglement
Gigena et al. 2020
.

In contrast, for
{\cal T}^{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}c^{\dagger}_{1_{A}}c_{r}
,

r=1,\ldots D
,
still
\sum_{r}{\cal T}^{r{\dagger}}{\cal T}^{r}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
in
{\cal F}_{2}({\cal H})
but

{\cal T}^{r}|\Psi\rangle
is either
0
or proportional to a state
c^{\dagger}_{1_{A}}c^{\dagger}_{j}|0\rangle
with
c^{\dagger}_{j}|0\rangle\in{\cal H}
, and no direct
A-B
entanglement is generated, thus trivially satisfying Eq. (
92
).

IV
Conclusions

We have examined a general bipartite-like representation and Schmidt decomposition of arbitrary
N
-fermion states, which expresses it in terms of general
M
- and (
N
-
M
)-fermion creation operators. It is directly connected to the reduced
M
and (
N
-
M
)-body DMs, which share the same non-zero eigenvalues, formally resembling the standard case of distinguishable components. It naturally leads to the concept of
M
-body entanglement, determined by the mixedness of the
M
or (
N
-
M
)-body DMs, which generalizes one-body entanglement and characterizes the correlations between
M
- and
(N
-
M)
-body operators. Such entanglement is of course independent of the choice of sp basis (it is not a mode-entanglement) and in fact also of the choice of
M
-fermion creation operators, as long as they satisfy Eqs. (
26
)–(
27
).

The full set of
M
-body DM’s
\rho^{(M)}
(
1\leq M\leq N/2
) provides a detailed characterization of the structure of correlated
N
-fermion states. We have explicitly seen that correlated states which look similar at the level of the one-body DM,
can lead to very distinct
M
-body DMs, as in the case of the states (
43
) and (
36
). In the latter, emergence of bosonic-like features is signaled by the appearance of an eigenvalue larger than
1
in the
M
-body DM for
M\geq 2
. On the other hand, all SDs (i.e. all free- or independent-fermion states) lead to idempotent
M
-body DMs (i.e. with eigenvalues
1
or
0
)
\forall

M
. The eigenvalues of
\rho^{(M)}
determine the average of
M
-body operators and its largest eigenvalue provides an upper bound to all averages
\langle A^{(M){\dagger}}A^{(M)}\rangle
(Eq. (
33
)) determined by “collective” operators
A^{(M){\dagger}}
creating a normalized state of
M
fermions.

Finally, we have investigated some operational implications of
M
-body entanglement. By demonstrating the majorization relation (
57
), we have shown that the entropy (
51
) of the normalized
M
-body DM
will not increase (and will in general decrease) under single fermion measurements determined by the Kraus operators (
56
). This result can be extended to general
L
-fermion measurements based on the operators (
76
) (Eqs. (
86
)–(
87
)),
which have the reduced
M
-body DMs as post-measurement states. Moreover,
by proving the majorization relation (
92
), we have shown that that such
M
-body entropy also provides an upper bound to the average bipartite entanglement entropy between
M
and
N-M
effectively distinguishable fermions generated by the mapping determined by the operators (
88
). Present results then provide the basis for a general theory of many-body entanglement beyond anti-symmetrization in fermion systems.

Appendix A
Eigenvalues of
\rho^{(M)}
in the states
|\Psi_{2k}\rangle

We first derive here the eigenvalues of the first three
M
-body DM’s in the states (
36
). Since fermions are created in pairs
c^{\dagger}_{2i-1}c^{\dagger}_{2i}
with equal probability, the elements of the one-body DM in these states are easily seen to be

\langle\Psi_{2k}|c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{j}|\Psi_{2k}\rangle=\delta_{ij}N/D\,,

(99)

implying
\rho^{(1)}=\frac{N}{D}\mathbbm{1}
. Thus, it is the maximally mixed SPDM compatible with the total fermion number
N
, being hence diagonal in
any
sp basis with a single
D
-fold degenerate eigenvalue
N/D
.

On the other hand, the elements of the two-body DM are blocked in two submatrices. The first one, comprising the contiguous pair creation operators
c^{\dagger}_{2i-1}c^{\dagger}_{2i}
that form the operator
A^{\dagger}
of Eq. (
34
), has elements

\langle\Psi_{2k}|c^{\dagger}_{2i-1}c^{\dagger}_{2i}c_{2j}c_{2j-1}|\Psi_{2k}\rangle=\alpha\delta_{ij}+\beta(1-\delta_{ij})\,,

(100)

where, using
k=N/2
and assuming
D
even,

\alpha={\textstyle\frac{\binom{D/2-1}{k-1}}{\binom{D/2}{k}}=\frac{2k}{D}\,,\;\;\beta=\frac{\binom{D/2-2}{k-1}}{\binom{D/2}{k}}=\frac{2k(D-2k)}{D(D-2)}\,.}

Hence, since this
\frac{D}{2}\times\frac{D}{2}
block is just

(\alpha-\beta)\mathbbm{1}+M
, with
M
a rank one matrix with all elements equal to
\beta
, it has just two distinct eigenvalues: a nondegenerate eigenvalue

\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm max}=\alpha+(\frac{D}{2}-1)\beta=k(1-2(k-1)/D)\,,

(101)

which is precisely that associated with the collective uniformly weighted pair creation operator
A^{\dagger}
:

\langle\Psi_{2k}|A^{\dagger}A|\Psi_{2k}\rangle=\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm max}\,,

(102)

and a
(D/2-1)
-fold degenerate smaller eigenvalue

\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm rest}=\alpha-\beta=\frac{4k(k-1)}{D(D-2)}\,.

(103)

The other block comprises the remaining
\binom{D}{2}-\frac{D}{2}
pair creation operators
c^{{\dagger}}_{i}c^{\dagger}_{j}
involving two distinct pairs and is directly diagonal,
with elements
\frac{\binom{D/2-2}{k-2}}{\binom{D/2}{k}}=\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm rest}
. Thus, the final result is one large nondegenerate eigenvalue
\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm max}\geq 1
, plus
\binom{D}{2}-1
smaller identical eigenvalues
\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm rest}<1
, satisfying

{\textstyle\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm max}+\left(\binom{D}{2}-1\right)\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm rest}=\binom{N}{2}\,.}

(104)

The same procedure can be applied to determine the eigenvalues of the three-body DM
\rho^{(3)}
.
For creation of three fermions with two of them in one of the contiguous pairs (
2i-1,2i

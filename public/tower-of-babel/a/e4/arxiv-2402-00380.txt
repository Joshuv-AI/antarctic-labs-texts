n n -Dimensional Volumetric Stretch Energy Minimization for Volume-/Mass-Preserving Parameterizations
Zhong-Heng Tan, Tiexiang Li, Wen-Wei Lin, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
In this paper, we develop an n n dimensional volumetric stretch energy ( n n -VSE) functional for the volume-/mass-preserving parameterization of the n n -manifolds topologically equivalent to n n -ball. The n n -VSE has a lower bound and equal to it if and only if the map is volume-/mass-preserving. This motivates us to minimize the n n -VSE to achieve the ideal volume-/mass-preserving parameterization. In the discrete case, we also guarantee the relation between the lower bound and the volume-/mass-preservation, and propose the spherical and ball volume-/mass-preserving parameterization algorithms. The numerical experiments indicate the accuracy and robustness of the proposed algorithms. The modified algorithms are applied to the manifold registration and deformation, showing the versatility of n n -VSE.

2
n
-dimensional Volumetric Stretch Energy

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{\mathbf{x}=h(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n})\in\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
be a smooth
n
-manifold topologically equivalent to an
n
-dimensional unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Here we suppose that
the map
h
satisfies that the Jacobian matrix
J_{h}
is column full rank (
\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})\neq 0
),
\mu
is the measure defined in
\mathcal{M}
and
\rho_{\mu}
be the corresponding density function.
Hence the mass of arbitrary subregion
B\subset\mathcal{M}
is
\mu(B)=\int_{B}d\mu=\int_{B}\rho_{\mu}d\sigma
.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be another
n
-manifold topologically equivalent to
\mathcal{M}
with
\nu
and
\rho_{\nu}
being the measure and density function defined in it. The density functions satisfy
0<L\leq\rho_{\mu},\rho_{\nu}<+\infty
.
An orientation preserving and bijective
C^{1}
map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is said to be mass-preserving if

\nu(A)/\nu(f(\mathcal{M}))=\mu(f^{-1}(A))/\mu(\mathcal{M})

for every subregion
A\subset f(\mathcal{M})
. From the mathematical analysis we have

\displaystyle\nu(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}d\nu=\int_{(f\circ h)^{-1}(A)}(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}ds,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu(f^{-1}(A))

\displaystyle=\int_{f^{-1}(A)}d\mu=\int_{(f\circ h)^{-1}(A)}(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}ds,

(2.2)

where
ds
is the volume element on
\Omega
. Therefore, the
C^{1}
map
f
should satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}}{\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}ds}=\frac{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}{\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}ds},

(2.3)

By the way, if
\rho_{\mu}=\rho_{\nu}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{M}
,
f
is said to be volume-preserving, which is a special case of the mass-preservation. In the rest of the paper, we only discuss the mass-preserving map.

To find the ideal mass-preserving map
f
, we first define the
n
-dimensional volumetric stretch energy (
n
-VSE) functional of the map
f
as

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left[\frac{\rho_{\nu}\circ f}{\rho_{\mu}}{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)\right]^{2}d\mu,

(2.4)

where
{\rm Det}(\cdot)
is the pseudo-determinant, the product of all nonzero eigenvalues of the matrix, and
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f:=J_{f\circ h}J_{h}^{\dagger}
is the tangential gradient of the map
f
on
\mathcal{M}
. By the property that
\lambda(AB^{\top})=\lambda(B^{\top}A)\cup\{0,\cdots,0\}
for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
m\leq n
with
\lambda
being the spectrum of a matrix, we have
{\rm Det}(AB^{\top})={\rm Det}(B^{\top}A)
. Then for
\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})\neq 0
and
\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})\neq 0
,

\displaystyle{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)^{2}=

\displaystyle{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f^{\top}\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)={\rm Det}(J_{f\circ h}(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})^{-1}J_{f\circ h}^{\top})

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Det}((J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})^{-1}(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h}))=\frac{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}.

(2.6)

Hence, the
n
-VSE in (
2.4
) can be written as

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\frac{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}}{\rho_{\mu}\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}\right)^{2}d\mu

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left((\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}\right)^{2}}{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}ds.

(2.8)

Remark 1
.

In (
2.7
), the formula
\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}
represents the volume ratio of
h(\tau)
to
\tau
with
\tau
being a infinitesimal region on
\Omega
, that is,
\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}=\lim_{|\tau|\to 0}\frac{|h(\tau)|}{|\tau|}
. Similarly, we also have
\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}=\lim_{|\tau|\to 0}\frac{|f\circ h(\tau)|}{|\tau|}
. Hence, from the derivation in (
2.6
),
{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)
means the volume ratio of the
f\circ h(\tau)
on
\mathcal{N}
to
h(\tau)
on
\mathcal{M}
. In the geometric perspective, letting
\sigma=h(\tau)
, the
n
-VSE is the sum of square of the mass ratios of each image infinitesimal region
f(\sigma)
to the origin infinitesimal region
\sigma
in
\mathcal{M}
. In other words,

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\lim_{\mu(\sigma)\to 0}\sum_{\sigma\subset\mathcal{M}}\left[\frac{\nu(f(\sigma))}{\mu(\sigma)}\right]^{2}\mu(\sigma)

(2.9)

.

Using the representation (
2.8
), we give a significant theorem for the lower bound of the
n
-VSE.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-manifold to be topologically equivalent to
\mathcal{N}
,
\mu
and
\nu
be the measures defined on
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
,
\rho_{\mu}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be the corresponding densities. For
n
-VSE
E_{V}(f)
defined in (
2.4
), let

\displaystyle S_{f}=\left\{E_{V}(f)~|~f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}\text{ is an orientation preserving and bijective $C^{1}$ map with }f(\partial\mathcal{M})=\partial\mathcal{N}\right\}.

(2.10)

Then we have

(i)

\min S_{f}\geq\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
;

(ii)

f^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}S_{f}=\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
if and only if
f^{*}
is mass-preserving.

Proof.

For the representation of the
n
-VSE in (
2.8
), we treat
(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}
as the variable
\xi
.
The
n
-VSE becomes

\displaystyle E_{V}(\xi)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\xi^{2}}{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}ds.

(2.11)

Consider the problem

\displaystyle\min_{\xi}S_{\xi}:=\left\{E_{V}(\xi)\left|\int_{\Omega}\xi ds=\nu(\mathcal{N}),\xi\geq 0\right.\right\}.

(2.12)

By Euler-Lagrange equation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2\xi}{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}+\lambda=0.

(2.13)

It follows that
2\xi=-\lambda(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}
. Integrating the both sides and using the equalities
\nu(\mathcal{N})=\nu(f(\mathcal{M}))=\int_{\Omega}\xi ds
and
\mu(\mathcal{M})=\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}ds
, we have
\lambda=-2\nu(\mathcal{N})/\mu(\mathcal{M})
. Then substituting this formula into (
2.13
), it follows that
\xi=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
. That is

\displaystyle\frac{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}}{\nu(\mathcal{N})}=\frac{\xi}{\nu(\mathcal{N})}=\frac{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})},

(2.14)

which means the mass-preservation. Additionally, it is easy to verify that the optimization problem (
2.12
) is convex. Therefore, the solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation is the minimizer of the optimization problem (
2.12
) and hence

\min_{\xi}S_{\xi}=\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
.
Since
S_{f}\subseteq S_{\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{f}S_{f}\geq\min_{\xi}S_{\xi}=\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})},

(2.15)

completing the proof of (i). Meanwhile, (
2.14
) implies that
\min_{f}S_{f}
reaches the lower bound
\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
if and only if the resulting map
f^{*}
is mass-preserving. Hence, (ii) is proved.
∎

Practically, we cannot expect an ideally mass-preserving and unique map as above mentioned. Hence, we take a step back and aim to find a mass-preserving as possible map for the given
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
. To measure the degree of mass-preservation, we define the mass-preserving error of a map

\displaystyle\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}

Definition 1
.

A map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
is an
\varepsilon
-mass-preserving map if
\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
.

Then, we further give the following theorem to show the measurement of the mass-preserving error
\varepsilon
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
be an orientation preserving and bijective
C^{1}
map with
f(\partial\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
and

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\nicefrac{{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f)}}{{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})}}}{\nicefrac{{\rho_{\mu}}}{{\mu(\mathcal{M})}}}{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)-1.

(2.18)

Then

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})^{2}}\|\delta\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}.

(2.19)

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}=\frac{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}}{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})},\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{\mu}=\frac{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\text{max}}:=\max\tilde{\mu}=\frac{\mu_{\text{max}}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\text{min}}:=\min\tilde{\mu}=\frac{\mu_{\text{min}}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}.

(2.22)

Then
\delta=\tilde{\xi}/\tilde{\mu}-1
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\xi}ds=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\mu}ds=1.

(2.23)

Using this equality and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon=

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)-\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}\left[\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\mu}(\delta+1)^{2}ds-1\right]

(2.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\mu}[(\delta+1)^{2}-1]ds=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\mu}\delta^{2}ds

(2.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\delta^{2}d\mu=\frac{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{2}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})^{2}}\|\delta\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}.

(2.26)

∎

The term

is the square of the relative error of
\frac{(\rho_{\nu}\circ f\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{f\circ h}^{\top}J_{f\circ h})}}{\nu(\mathbb{B}^{n})}
and
\frac{(\rho_{\mu}\circ h)\sqrt{\det(J_{h}^{\top}J_{h})}}{\mu(\mathcal{M})}
, which can represent a local error at a point in

. From (

among the manifold

. Hence, it is appropriate to choose

as the measurement. In this paper, we aim to find the

-mass-preserving map with as small error

as possible via minimizing

in (

3.2
Discrete Volumetric Stretch Energy

For the discrete
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the piecewise affine map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is easy to verify that the Jacobian matrix
J_{f^{-1}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\big|_{f(\sigma)}\right):=\int_{f(\sigma)}\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\right)ds=\frac{|\sigma|}{|f(\sigma)|}

(3.6)

for all
\sigma=[v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
. This is the ratio of volumes of the
n
-simplex and its image simplex. Hence, like the continuous case, the piecewise affine map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be induced by
\mathbf{f}
and is volume-preserving if the Jacobian
J_{f^{-1}}
satisfies
\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\big|_{f(\sigma)}\right)=\frac{|\mathcal{M}|}{|f(\mathcal{M})|}
, that is
\nicefrac{{|\sigma|}}{{|\mathcal{M}|}}=\nicefrac{{|f(\sigma)|}}{{|f(\mathcal{M})|}}
.
Additionally, let the density function
\rho
defined in
\mathcal{M}
be piecewise constant and the mass
\mu(\sigma)=\rho(\sigma)|\sigma|
. The map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is mass-preserving if and only if
\rho(\sigma)\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\big|_{f(\sigma)}\right)=\frac{\mu(\mathcal{M})}{|f(\mathcal{M})|}
, that is,
\nicefrac{{\mu(\sigma)}}{{\mu(\mathcal{M})}}=\nicefrac{{|f(\sigma)|}}{{|f(\mathcal{M})|}}
.
The Jacobian determinant is an important quantity to measure the mass-preservation. As far as
\rho(\sigma)\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\big|_{f(\sigma)}\right)=\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{|f(\sigma)|}
is constant for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
, the map
f
is mass-preserving. In
[
23
,
20
,
9
]
, it is termed the stretch factor as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu,f^{-1}}(\sigma)=\rho(\sigma)\det\left(J_{f^{-1}}\big|_{f(\sigma)}\right)=\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{|f(\sigma)|},

(3.7)

and expected to be a constant. Using the stretch factor,
[
23
,
20
,
9
]
have defined the
2
-dimensional stretch energy and
3
-dimensional stretch energy and given some theorems and properties associated to the stretch energy when
n=2
and
3
. Now, we generalize the stretch energy into
n
-dimensional and define the discrete
n
-dimensional volumetric stretch energy functional as

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}\big(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{V}(f)\mathbf{f}\big)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s=1}^{n}{\mathbf{f}^{s}}^{\top}L_{V}(f)\mathbf{f}^{s},

(3.8)

where
L_{V}(f)
is a volumetric stretch symmetric Laplacian matrix with

\big[L_{V}(f)\big]_{ij}=\begin{cases}-w_{ij}(f),&\text{if }[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\mathcal{M}),\\
\sum_{\ell\neq i}w_{i\ell}(f),&\text{if }i=j,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.9a)

in which the modified cotangent weights
w_{ij}(f)
is defined by

w_{ij}(f)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\frac{|f(\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}})|}{\sigma_{\mu,f^{-1}}(\sigma)}\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}(f)

(3.10a)

with
\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}
being the
(n-2)
-simplex containing the vertices
\mathbb{S}_{0}(\sigma)\setminus\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
and

\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}(f)
being the dihedral angle between subspaces
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
containing
\mathbb{S}_{0}(f(\sigma))\setminus\{\mathbf{f}_{i}\}
and
\mathbb{S}_{0}(f(\sigma))\setminus\{\mathbf{f}_{j}\}
respectively.

In the
Theorem

3
, we will illustrate that this definition in (
3.8
) is equivalent to our defined
n
-VSE in (
2.4
) in the discrete form

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{{\rm Det}(\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f)^{2}}{\rho}ds=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\frac{|f(\sigma)|^{2}}{\mu(\sigma)}.

(3.11)

To proof this theorem, we first generalize the Dirichlet energy to
n
-dimension and give some lemmas associated with it. For the smooth
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
and the orientation preserving and bijective
C^{1}
map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
n
-dimensional Dirichlet energy functional with respect to

is defined as

\displaystyle E_{D}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f\|_{F}^{2}ds,

(3.12)

In the discrete case, we consider the discrete
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
and the piecewise affine map
f
. The discrete Dirichlet energy is formulated as a quadratic form.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a discrete
n
-manifold embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f
defined on
\mathcal{M}
is a piecewise affine map. Then the
n
-dimensional Dirichlet energy defined in (
3.12
) can be formulated as

\displaystyle E_{D}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{D}\mathbf{f}),

(3.13)

where

\displaystyle\big[L_{D}\big]_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\tilde{w}_{ij},&\textrm{if }[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\mathcal{M}),\\
\sum_{\ell\neq i}\tilde{w}_{ij},&\textrm{if }i=j,\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.14)

with the cotangent weight

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{ij}=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M}):\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\subset\sigma}|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma},

(3.15)

in which
\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}
is the dihedral angle between subspaces containing
\mathbb{S}_{0}(\sigma)\setminus\{v_{i}\}
and
\mathbb{S}_{0}(\sigma)\setminus\{v_{j}\}
respectively.

Proof.

Let
q
be a point in the
n
-simplex
\sigma=[v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
.
f(q)
can be represented by the barycentre coordinates according to (
3.5
)

\displaystyle f(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}(q)\mathbf{f}_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt\alpha_{i}(q)=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\big|[v_{0},\cdots,v_{i-1},q,v_{i+1},\cdots,v_{n}]\big|.

(3.16)

For the discrete
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the piecewise affine map
f
, we have
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}f=\nabla f
.
In addition,
\nabla f
is a piecewise constant function and can be also represented by the barycentre coordinates as

\displaystyle\nabla f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}\nabla\alpha_{i}.

(3.17)

The volume of the simplex in (
3.16
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\big|[v_{0},\cdots,v_{i-1},q,v_{i+1},\cdots,v_{n}]\big|=\frac{1}{n}\big|\sigma_{\hat{i}}\big|h_{i}^{\sigma}(q),

(3.18)

where
h_{i}^{\sigma}(q)=(q-v_{\ell})^{\top}\vec{n}_{i}
,
\ell\neq i
, is the height corresponding to the face
\sigma_{\hat{i}}
, whose gradient with respect to
q
is
\vec{n}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}
, the inward unit normal vector of the face
\sigma_{\hat{i}}
. Therefore, by the representation in (
3.18
),
we have

\displaystyle\nabla\alpha_{i}=\frac{\nabla\big|[v_{0},\cdots,v_{i-1},q,v_{i+1},\cdots,v_{n}]\big|}{|\sigma|}=\frac{1}{n|\sigma|}|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|\vec{n}_{i}.

(3.19)

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathbf{f}_{i}\nabla\alpha_{i}\right\|^{2}_{F}=\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|}{n|\sigma|}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}\vec{n}_{i}\right\|^{2}_{F}

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}||\sigma_{\hat{j}}|}{n^{2}|\sigma|^{2}}\langle\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}\vec{n}_{i},\mathbf{f}_{j}^{\top}\vec{n}_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}||\sigma_{\hat{j}}|}{n^{2}|\sigma|^{2}}(\vec{n}_{i}\vec{n}_{j}^{\top})(\mathbf{f}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{j}^{\top})

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|^{2}}{n^{2}|\sigma|^{2}}\mathbf{f}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}-\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}||\sigma_{\hat{j}}|}{n^{2}|\sigma|^{2}}(\mathbf{f}_{i}\mathbf{f}_{j}^{\top})\cos\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}.

(3.22)

Similar to (
3.18
), we have
(k-1)|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|=|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|h_{j}^{\sigma_{\hat{i}}}
, where
h_{j}^{\sigma_{\hat{i}}}
is the height corresponding to the face
\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}
on the
(n-1)
-simplex
\sigma_{\hat{i}}
. It is easy to verify that
h_{j}^{\sigma}=h_{j}^{\sigma_{\hat{i}}}\sin\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}
, following that

\displaystyle\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}||\sigma_{\hat{j}}|}{n^{2}|\sigma|^{2}}=\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|}{n|\sigma|h_{j}^{\sigma}}=\frac{|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|}{n(n-1)|\sigma|\sin\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}}.

(3.23)

From
[
4
]
, we have

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\hat{i}}|=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|h_{i}^{\sigma}\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}.

(3.24)

Substituting (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) into (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}\mathbf{f}_{ij}\mathbf{f}_{j}^{\top}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\tilde{w}_{ij}^{\sigma}\mathbf{f}_{ij}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}.

(3.25)

As a result,

\displaystyle E_{D}(f)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f\|_{F}^{2}ds=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\|\nabla f\|_{F}^{2}|\sigma|

(3.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}_{0}(\sigma)}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\tilde{w}_{ij}^{\sigma}\mathbf{f}_{ij}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\tilde{w}_{ij}\mathbf{f}_{ij}\mathbf{f}_{i}^{\top}=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{D}\mathbf{f}).

(3.27)

∎

One can see that

has the identical sparse structure to

in (

). We further let

be the Laplacian matrix defined on

\tilde{w}_{ij}(f)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M}):\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\subset\sigma}|f(\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}})|\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}(f).

Then, a representation of

and its gradient associated with

Lemma 2
.

The volume of the discrete
n
-manifold
f(\mathcal{M})
and its gradient with respect to

can be represented as

\displaystyle|f(\mathcal{M})|=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{D}(f)\mathbf{f}),

(3.28)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{f}}|f(\mathcal{M})|=L_{D}(f)\mathbf{f}.

(3.29)

Proof.

By the definition of the
n

), we have
E_{D}(\emph{id})\big|_{\mathcal{M}}=|\mathcal{M}|
, where
\emph{id}
is the identity map. Hence, for the discrete

\displaystyle|f(\mathcal{M})|=E_{D}(\emph{id})\big|_{f(\mathcal{M})}:=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{D}(f)\mathbf{f}).

(3.30)

This proves the formula (
3.28
). On the other hand, from the formula (1) in

4
]
, that is,

\displaystyle\nabla_{v_{i}}|\sigma|=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}|\sigma_{\hat{i}\hat{j}}|\cot\theta_{ij}^{\sigma}v_{ij}=\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\tilde{w}_{ij}^{\sigma}v_{ij},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{f}_{i}}|f(\mathcal{M})|

Theorem 3
.

The volumetric stretch energy functional in (
3.8
) can be formulated as

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\frac{|f(\sigma)|^{2}}{\mu(\sigma)}.

(3.34)

Proof.

Split
\mathcal{M}
by its
n
-simplex
\{\sigma\}
and consider the volumetric stretch energy on
\sigma
, then the volumetric stretch energy can also be splitted as

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}E_{V}(f)\big|_{\sigma}.

(3.35)

From (
3.30
) we have
E_{D}(\emph{id})\big|_{f(\sigma)}=|f(\sigma)|
. Hence,

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)\big|_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}w_{ij}^{\sigma}(f)\|\mathbf{f}_{ij}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{n\sigma_{\mu,f^{-1}}(\sigma)}\sum_{[v_{i},v_{j}]\in\mathbb{S}_{1}(\sigma)}\tilde{w}_{ij}^{\sigma}(f)\|\mathbf{f}_{ij}\|^{2}

(3.36)

\displaystyle=\frac{|f(\sigma)|}{n\mu(\sigma)}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{D}(f)\mathbf{f})=\frac{|f(\sigma)|}{\mu(\sigma)}E_{D}(id)\big|_{f(\sigma)}=\frac{|f(\sigma)|^{2}}{\mu(\sigma)}.

(3.37)

Combining (
3.35
) with (
3.37
), (
3.34
) is obtained.
∎

Theorem
3
unifies the continuous and discrete

-VSE and reveals that the discrete

-VSE is formulated by the volumes of the original simplices
\sigma
and the image simplices
f(\sigma)
.
Next, we propose the discrete case of

1

is a constant.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a discrete
n
-manifold to be topologically equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\mu(\cdot)
be the measure defined in
\mathcal{M}
. Let

\displaystyle S_{f}=\left\{E_{V}(f)~|~f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{B}^{n}\text{ is orientation preserving and piecewise affine with }\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}|f(\sigma)|=C,f(\partial\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right\}.

(3.38)

Then we have

(i)

\min S_{f}\geq\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
;

(ii)

f^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}S_{f}=\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
if and only if
f^{*}
is volume-/mass-preserving, i.e.,
\frac{|f^{*}(\sigma)|}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}|f^{*}(\sigma)|}=\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
, for each
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

Let
m:=\#\big(\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})\big)
. Treating the volumes
|f(\sigma)|
as variables, (
3.34
) is temporarily represented as

\displaystyle E_{V}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\xi_{i}^{2}}{\mu(\sigma_{i})},\hskip 9.24994pt\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},

(3.39)

For the optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\xi}S_{\xi}\equiv\left\{E_{V}(\xi)~\left|~\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}=C,~\xi_{i}\geq 0,~i=1,2,\cdots,m\right.\right\},

(3.40)

its Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) equation is

\displaystyle\frac{2\xi_{i}}{\mu(\sigma_{i})}+\lambda=0,\hskip 9.24994pti=1,2,\cdots,m.

(3.41)

By the total volume constraints
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}=C
, we have
\lambda=-\frac{2C}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}
and
\xi_{i}=\frac{C\mu(\sigma_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}
. That is

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}}=\frac{\mu(\sigma_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})},\hskip 9.24994pti=1,2,\cdots,m.

(3.42)

Additionally, the problem (
3.40
) is convex, whose objective function and feasible region are all convex. Therefore, the solution of KKT equation (
3.41
) is the minimizer of the optimization problem (
3.40
) and hence
\min S_{\xi}=\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, (i)
\min S_{f}\geq\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
is obtained. Meanwhile, (
3.42
) demonstrates the volume-/mass-preserving property. As a consequence,
f^{*}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}S_{f}
attains the lower bound
\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
if and only if
\frac{|f^{*}(\sigma)|}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}|f^{*}(\sigma)|}=\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)},i=1,2,\cdots,m
, which leads to (ii).
∎

Like the
theorem

1
in the continuous case, (i) gives the lower bound of the discrete
n
-VSE under the constraints in (
3.38
) and (ii) indicates that the discrete
n
-VSE reaches the lower bound if and only if the resulting map is mass-preserving. Identically, the existence and uniqueness of the ideal discrete mass-preserving map from
\mathcal{M}
to a unit
n
-ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
are not theoretically guaranteed. Therefore, the minimum of

cannot always reach the lower bound

. Hence, we define the mass-preserving error of the map

as

\displaystyle\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)},

(3.43)

and the discrete

-mass-preserving map.

Definition 4
.

A map

is a discrete

-mass-preserving map if
\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
.

For the discrete
\varepsilon
-mass-preserving map, we can also give the measurement of

.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{B}
be an orientation preserving and piecewise affine map with
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}|f(\sigma)|=C
,
f(\partial\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and

\varepsilon=E_{V}(f)-\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}
and

\displaystyle\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\cdots),\hskip 9.24994pt\delta_{i}=\frac{\nicefrac{{|f(\sigma_{i})|}}{{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}|f(\sigma_{i})|}}}{\nicefrac{{\mu(\sigma_{i})}}{{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)}}}-1,

(3.44)

Then

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{min}}\frac{C^{2}}{\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)\right)^{2}}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\leq\mu_{\text{max}}\frac{C^{2}}{\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})}\mu(\sigma)\right)^{2}}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.45)

where
\mu_{\text{min}}
and
\mu_{\text{max}}
are the minimum and maximum of
\mu(\sigma)
among all
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
, respectively.

Proof.

Suppose
m:=\#(\mathbb{S}_{n}(\mathcal{M}))
. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}_{i}=\frac{|f(\sigma_{i})|}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f(\sigma_{i})|},\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\frac{\mu(\sigma_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})},

(3.46)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\text{max}}:=\max_{i}\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\frac{\mu_{\text{max}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})},

(3.47)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{\text{min}}:=\max_{i}\tilde{\mu}_{i}=\frac{\mu_{\text{min}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}.

(3.48)

Then
\delta_{i}={\tilde{\xi}_{i}}/{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}-1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\xi}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}=1.

(3.49)

We have

\displaystyle\varepsilon=

\displaystyle E_{V}(f)-\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}=\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}(\delta_{i}+1)^{2}-1\right]

(3.50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}[(\delta_{i}+1)^{2}-1]=\frac{C^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\sigma_{i})}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}.

(3.51)

Since
\tilde{\mu}_{i}>0
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}\leq\tilde{\mu}_{\text{max}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{i}^{2}=\tilde{\mu}_{\text{max}}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2},

(3.52a)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{\mu}_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}\geq\tilde{\mu}_{\text{min}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{i}^{2}=\tilde{\mu}_{\text{min}}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.52b)

Plugging (
3.51
), (
3.47
) and (
3.48
) into (
3.52a
) and (
3.52b
), the inequality (
3.45
) is obtained.
∎

As like the continuous case,

shows that we can use
\varepsilon
to measure the mass-preservation of the map. Meanwhile, minimizing
S_{f}
in (

) is a feasible method to find the

-mass-preserving map.

The ball

Specifically, the stretch transformation
is obtained by solving the eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{\mathtt{B}}^{\top}\tilde{V}_{\mathtt{B}}X=X\Lambda,

are the square of length of the principal axes. Then
\tilde{V}_{\mathtt{B}}X\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
are the vertices of the ball-like manifold. The solution of this problem can be calculated by the singular value decomposition of
\tilde{V}_{\mathtt{B}}
. We can verify that the left singular vector

After the computation of the boundary map

, we focus on the ball subproblem (

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{f}_{\mathtt{I}}}\hskip 9.24994ptE_{V}(f):=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{f}^{\top}L_{V}(f)\mathbf{f}).

Partition

\displaystyle L_{V}(f)=\begin{bmatrix}[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{B}\mathtt{B}}&[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{B}\mathtt{I}}\\
[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{I}\mathtt{B}}&[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{I}\mathtt{I}}\end{bmatrix}.

\displaystyle[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{I}\mathtt{I}}\mathbf{f}_{\mathtt{I}}=-[L_{V}(f)]_{\mathtt{I}\mathtt{B}}\mathbf{f}_{\mathtt{B}}.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose the
n
-VSE functional to achieve the mass-preserving parameterization for the
n
-manifold topologically equivalent to the
n
-ball. The
n
-VSE can be treated as the sum of the mass ratios among the manifold. We give a lower bound of the
n
-VSE and proof that the
n
-VSE reaches the lower bound if and only if the map is mass-preserving. This is the foundation of our proposed algorithms that aim to minimize the
n
-VSE. In the discrete case, the
n
-VSE is equivalent to the energies defined in
[
23
,
20
,
9
]
when
n=2
and
n=3
. The discrete
n
-VSE can be formulated as the quadratic form with respect to the Laplacian matrix and the gradient of the discrete
n
-VSE is of the simple formulation, that is, the product of the Laplacian matrix and the image vertices. This property makes the
n
-VSEM more practical. To compute the mass-preserving parameterization of the
n
-manifold, we solve the boundary and interior subproblems by developing the spherical and the interior algorithms, respectively. The numerical experiment shows the high accuracy of our developed algorithms, especially for the
3
-manifolds.

However, the efficiency and accuracy, especially for the higher dimensional manifolds, are not fully satisfactory. The high dimensional manifolds need more vertices and simplices to represent with high resolution, which makes the scale of the problem more huge. It is still a challenging problem.

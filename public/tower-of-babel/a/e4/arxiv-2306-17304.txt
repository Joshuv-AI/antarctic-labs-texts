Characters in p -adic Heisenberg and Lattice Vertex Operator Algebras
Daniel Barake, Cameron Franc

Abstract
. We study characters of states in p p -adic vertex operator algebras. In particular, we show that the image of the character map for both the p p -adic Heisenberg and p p -adic lattice vertex operator algebras contain infinitely-many non-classical p p -adic modular forms which are not contained in the image of the algebraic character map.



1.
Introduction

We study
p
-adic properties of certain vertex operator algebras. Motivated by both physical and number-theoretical methods, the authors of

2.
Background

2.1.
Vertex Operator Algebras

For elementary notions in VOA theory, the reader is invited to consult
[
LL04
]
. A vertex operator algebra consists of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space, or Fock space,

\displaystyle V=\coprod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{(n)}

where
\dim V_{(n)}<\infty
for all
n
and
V_{(n)}=0
for
n\ll 0
. We say the state
a\in V
is homogeneous of weight
k
if
a\in V_{(k)}
. The vertex operator linear map

\displaystyle Y:V

\displaystyle\to\text{End}(V)[[z,z^{-1}]]

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a(n)z^{-n-1}

packages the modes
a(n)\in\text{End}(V)
of the state
a
into a formal Laurent series, and these satisfy the truncation condition
a(n)b=0
for
n\gg 0
for
a,b\in V
. The vacuum state
\textbf{1}\in V_{(0)}
satisfies
Y(\textbf{1},z)=1
and
\lim_{z\to 0}Y(a,z)\textbf{1}=a
. These are the vacuum and creation properties, respectively. The conformal state
\omega\in V_{(2)}
has vertex operator given by

\displaystyle Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}\hskip 5.0pt\left(=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega(n)z^{-n-1}\right)

where the
L(i)\in\text{End}(V)
generate a copy of the Virasoro algebra with central charge
c_{V}\in\mathbb{C}
. The
L(0)
-eigenspace decomposition of
V
coincides with the grading of
V
, that is,
L(0)a=ka
where
a\in V_{(k)}
, and the
L(-1)
-derivative property holds, i.e.
L(-1)\textbf{1}=0
. Finally, the Jacobi identity is satisfied for any
a,b\in V
and triple of integers
(r,s,t)
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{r}{i}(-1)^{i}

\displaystyle a(s+r-i)b(t+i)-(-1)^{r}\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{r}{i}(-1)^{i}b(t+r-i)a(s+i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{s}{i}(a(r+i)b)(s+t-i).

We shall also require the notion of a
V
-module
M
in arguments involving lattice VOAs. Recall this is a
\mathbb{C}
-graded vector space

\displaystyle M=\coprod_{h\in\mathbb{C}}M_{(h)}

such that
\dim M_{(h)}<\infty
for all
h
and
M_{(h)}=0
for
h\ll 0
. Here also, the
L(0)
-eigenspace decomposition coincides with the grading above. We call
a\in M
homogeneous of weight
h
if
a\in M_{(h)}
. We have an analogous vertex operator linear map

\displaystyle Y_{M}:V

\displaystyle\to\text{End}(M)[[z,z^{-1}]]

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto Y_{M}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a(n)z^{-n-1}

where the truncation and vacuum conditions as well as the Jacobi identity (
eq.

2.1
) hold true for the above modes.

Given
a\in V_{(k)}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, one shows that
a(n):V_{(m)}\to V_{(m+k-n-1)}
(cf.
[
MT10
]
). Denote then the zero mode
o(a)
of
a
as
a(k-1)
such that
o(a):V_{(m)}\to V_{(m)}
, and extend the definition to all of
V
additively. By tracing the zero mode over each subspace
V_{(n)}
(recall
\dim V_{(n)}<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) and packaging these into a generating function, we obtain the character

\displaystyle Z(a,q)=q^{-c_{V}/24}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{Tr}_{V_{(n)}}o(a)q^{n}.

In the case of
V
-modules, the character is defined in a natural way where the zero mode
o(a)=a(k-1)\in\text{End}(M)
for
a\in V_{(k)}
preserves each homogeneous space
M_{(h)}
.

2.2.
p
-adic Vertex Operator Algebras

The definition of a VOA given above can be easily generalized over any field
k
of characteristic zero. In particular, it is perfectly reasonable to set
k=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. All of the VOAs of interest in our discussion (Heisenberg modules and lattice VOAs) have bases defined over the
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, such that the corresponding modes expressed in these bases are all
p
-adically integral, satisfying some mild hypotheses on boundedness of these operators. A
p
-adic VOA
\widehat{V}
is then formed by completing and inverting
p
. One description is to first consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{U}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
m\end{subarray}}U/p^{m}U

and then letting
\widehat{V}=\widehat{U}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Equivalently,
\widehat{V}
can be described in terms of a completion with respect to a sup-norm arising from the integral basis in
U
. See Section 7 of
[
FM22
]
for details on these constructions. For our purposes, it will be enough to think of elements of
\widehat{V}
as limits of convergent sequences of states in an underlying algebraic VOA, though the full axiomatic description of
\widehat{V}
is given in
[
FM22
]
.

For a concrete example, setting
V
to be the Heisenberg algebra (which we will describe momentarily below as polynomial ring in countably many indeterminates
h(i)
) over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, let
S_{r}
for
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
denote the completion of
V
with respect to the sup-norm

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{I}a_{I}h^{I}\right\rvert_{r}=\sup_{I}|a_{I}|r^{I}

where
I
is a finite multi-subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
with
|I|=-\sum_{i\in I}i
, and
h^{I}=\prod_{i\in I}h(i)
. Then Proposition 9.1 in
[
FM22
]
shows that elements of
S_{r}
are of the form
\sum_{I}a_{I}h^{I}
where

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{|I|\to\infty}|a_{I}|r^{|I|}=0.

In particular, with
r=1
, the ring
S_{1}
has the structure of a
p
-adic VOA, and there is a nested chain of subpaces
S_{1}\supseteq S_{2}\supseteq S_{3}\supseteq\cdots
.

2.3.
p
-adic modular forms

Following
[
Ser73
]
, for a prime
p\geq 2
we denote the
p
-adic weight space as

\displaystyle X=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
m\end{subarray}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}.

Note that when
p=2
, we have
X\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We say
k\in X
is even if
k\in 2X
, and that
k
is odd if it is not even. A
p
-adic modular form is then a formal series

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[z]]

such that
f
is the
p
-adic limit of modular forms
f_{i}
of weight
k_{i}
having rational coefficients. The ring
\mathfrak{M}_{p}
is then defined as the completion of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[E_{4},E_{6}]
by the sup-norm on the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}
, and
f\in\mathfrak{M}_{p}
has weight equal to the
p
-adic limit of the
k_{i}
, which is an element of
X
.

The ring
\mathfrak{M}_{p}
contains
p
-adic analogues of classical Eisenstein series, and these take the form

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\star}(q)=\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{p}(1-k)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1}^{\star}(n)q^{n}

where
\sigma_{k-1}^{\star}(n)
is defined as in
eq.

1.2
and
\zeta_{p}
denotes the Kubota-Leopoldt
p
-adic zeta function (cf.
[
KL64
]
). For even integers
k\geq 2
,
G_{k}^{\star}
is a modular form on the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. For example, taking the sequence
\{G_{2+p^{m}(p-1)}\}_{m\geq 0}
of classical Eisenstein series as
m\to\infty
for some
p\geq 2
yields
G_{2}^{\star}\in\mathfrak{M}_{p}
seen in
eq.

1.2
. The images in
1.1
and
1.2
will be obtained in a similar manner.

3.
p-adic characters in the Heisenberg VOA

3.1.
The Heisenberg VOA and the square-bracket formalism

We briefly outline the construction of the Heisenberg VOA
S_{\text{alg}}
. A rigorous treatment can be found in many texts such as
[
FLM88
,
LL04
,
MT10
,
FBZ04
]
. For generalities on Lie algebras alongside their representations, we follow Chapter 9 of
[
Car05
]
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite-dimensional vector space viewed as an abelian Lie algebra. Then associated the (untwisted) affine Lie algebra

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}=\left(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}\textbf{k}

where
k
is central. There are two graded subalgebras which we write with respect to the notation given in
[
LL04
]
(by weights) as:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{+}=\mathfrak{h}\otimes t^{-1}\mathbb{C}[t^{-1}]

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-}=\mathfrak{h}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t].

Define
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\leq 0}=\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-}\oplus\left(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\textbf{k}\right)
, and denote by
\mathcal{U}(\cdot)
the universal enveloping algebra. Let
\mathbb{C}\textbf{1}
be the
1
-dimensional
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\leq 0}
-module where
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-}
acts trivially on
\mathbb{C}
and
k
acts as the identity. Construct the induced module

\displaystyle S_{\text{alg}}=\text{Ind}_{\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\leq 0})}^{\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}})}\mathbb{C}\textbf{1}=\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\leq 0})}\mathbb{C}\textbf{1}

where we write the action of
h\otimes t^{n}
on
S_{\text{alg}}
as
h(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that
S_{\text{alg}}\cong S(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{+})
, the symmetric algebra. That is,
\{h(-1),h(-2),h(-3),\ldots\}
form a basis for
S_{\text{alg}}
. Also note the
h(n)
for
n\geq 1
act on
S_{\text{alg}}
as
n\partial_{h(-n)}
, and
h(0)
acts as zero. The Heisenberg vertex operator (or Heisenberg field) is defined as

\displaystyle h(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h(n)z^{-n-1}\in\text{End}(S_{\text{alg}})[[z,z^{-1}]].

This is no more than a generating function for the operators
h(n)\in\text{End}(S_{\text{alg}})
. Hence we may view
S_{\text{alg}}
as being generated by the Fourier coefficients of the state
h(-1)\textbf{1}
(see
[
FBZ04
]
).

Instrumental to establishing the connection between VOAs and modular forms is the notion of transforming a VOA by way of a map
z\mapsto\phi(z)
, introduced first in
[
Zhu96
]
. An overview of this technique can be found in detail in
[
MT10
]
and in Chapter 5 of
[
FBZ04
]
. In particular, setting
\phi(z)=e^{z}-1
formulates the vertex operators as living on a torus rather than a sphere, and for
S_{\text{alg}}
these are given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle Y(h(-1)\textbf{1},e^{z}-1)e^{z}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h[n]z^{-n-1}.

This resulting “square-bracket" VOA is equipped with a new grading which respects the grading on the space of quasi-modular forms. Multiplying
eq.

3.1
by
z^{n}
we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle h[n]

\displaystyle=\textup{Res}_{z}\hskip 3.0ptY(h(-1)\textbf{1},e^{z}-1)(e^{z})(z^{n}).

Note that
Y(h(-1)\textbf{1},e^{z}-1)
truncates when applied to any state in
S_{\text{alg}}
, and that we can use a change of variables theorem (cf. eq. (1.1.3) of
[
Zhu96
]
) with
w=e^{z}-1
to show

(3.3)

\displaystyle h[n]=\textup{Res}_{w}\hskip 3.0pth(w)\left(\log(w+1)\right)^{n}

Observe that
h[0]=h(0)
. For our purposes, we will only require the explicit expression where
n=-1
above. If we let
c_{0}=1
,
c_{i}=0
for
i<0
, and

\displaystyle c_{k}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i+1}c_{k-i}

so that

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\hskip 5.0ptc_{2}=-\frac{1}{12},\hskip 5.0ptc_{3}=\frac{1}{24},\hskip 5.0ptc_{4}=-\frac{19}{720},\hskip 5.0pt\cdots

then
c_{k}=\text{Coeff}_{w^{k-1}}(\log(w+1))^{-1}
and we see that

(3.4)

\displaystyle h[-1]=h(-1)+\frac{1}{2}h(0)-\frac{1}{12}h(1)+\frac{1}{24}h(2)-\frac{19}{720}h(3)+\cdots

3.2.
Some square-bracket states

We construct as in
[
FM22
]
a family of square-bracket states in
S_{\text{alg}}
which map under
f
(recall
eq.

1.1
) to an appropriate subspace of the ring of quasi-modular forms. In order to assess
p
-adic convergence in the subsequent
3.5
and
3.6
, we must write these square-bracket states in the round-bracket formalism, that is, in terms of the basis
\{h(-1),h(-2),h(-3)\ldots\}
of
S_{\text{alg}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For an odd integer
t\geq 1
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle h[-1]^{t}\textbf{1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k}\textbf{1}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. Recall that in
S_{\text{alg}}
,
eq.

3.4
shows that
h[-1]\textbf{1}=h(-1)\textbf{1}
which agrees with the statement. Suppose the claim holds up to some
t>1
. Then applying
h[-1]
to the right-hand side of
eq.

3.5
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k+1}\textbf{1}+\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k}\frac{2(2k)!(t-2k)}{k!(-24)^{k+1}}h(-1)^{t-2k-1}\textbf{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k+1}\textbf{1}+\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k+1}\frac{(2k+2)!}{(k+1)!(-24)^{k+1}}h(-1)^{t-2k-1}\textbf{1}.

Re-indexing the second sum, we get

\displaystyle=h(-1)^{t+1}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{t}{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k+1}\textbf{1}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{t}{2k-1}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k+1}\textbf{1},

and by using Pascal’s rule this equals
h[-1]^{t+1}\textbf{1}
which completes the induction.
∎

Remark
.

It is interesting to note that the expression given in

\displaystyle\text{He}_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\left(x-\alpha\partial_{x}\right)^{n}\cdot 1=e^{-\frac{\alpha(\partial_{x})^{2}}{2}}x^{n}

By comparing

For an odd integer

\displaystyle h(1)h[-1]^{t}\textbf{1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{t}{2k+1}\frac{(2k+1)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}h(-1)^{t-2k-1}\textbf{1}.

Proposition 3.3

Lemma 3.5

Fix

for some fixed integer

Proof.

Let

denote the
p
-adic absolute value. We have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\binom{t}{2k}\frac{n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}-\binom{t}{2k}\frac{n!S_{s}^{(n+1)}(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}\right\rvert_{p}=\left\lvert n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}-n!S_{s}^{(n+1)}\right\rvert_{p}\left\lvert\binom{t}{2k}\frac{(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}\right\rvert_{p}.

The rightmost term is dependent only on
t
and
k
which are fixed, and so denote by
x
the
p
-adic valuation of this term, which is also fixed. We show now that
n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}\equiv n!S_{s}^{(n+1)}\mod p^{a+1}
. From the statement of
3.3
, we have the formula

\displaystyle n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n+j}(j+1)^{r-1}.

If

then of course
(j+1)^{r-1}\equiv 0\mod p^{a+1}
. Suppose then, that
p\nmid(j+1)
. This means that
p
and
(j+1)
are coprime, and subsequently that

and
(j+1)
are coprime. Recall that

denotes the Euler totient function. So by Euler’s theorem,

\displaystyle(j+1)^{p^{a}(p-1)}\equiv 1\mod p^{a+1}.

Note also that

is an integer independent of

. Thus

\displaystyle n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
p\nmid(j+1)\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n+j}(j+1)^{l-1}\mod p^{a+1}

and since the right hand side above does not depend on

. Putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle\binom{t}{2k}\frac{n!S_{r}^{(n+1)}(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}\equiv\binom{t}{2k}\frac{n!S_{s}^{(n+1)}(2k)!}{k!(-24)^{k}}\mod p^{a+x+1}.

Finally since

\displaystyle 1-p^{p^{a}(p-1)+l}\equiv 1-p^{p^{b}(p-1)+l}\mod p^{a+x+1},

and so by combining the above two congruences together, we obtain the desired result.

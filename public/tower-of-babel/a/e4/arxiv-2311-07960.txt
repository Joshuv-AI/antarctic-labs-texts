Flat Connections from Irregular Conformal Blocks
Babak Haghighat, Yihua Liu, and Nicolai Reshetikhin

Abstract
In this work we study Liouville conformal blocks with degenerate primaries and one operator in an irregular representation of the Virasoro algebra. Using an algebraic approach, we derive modified BPZ equations satisfied by such blocks and subsequently construct corresponding integral representations based on integration over non-compact Lefschetz cycles. The integral representations are then used to derive novel types of flat connections on the irregular conformal block bundle.



1
Introduction and Summary

Irregular conformal blocks in the two-dimensional Liouville CFT were first studied
Gaiotto:2009ma
;
Gaiotto:2012sf
;
Bonelli:2011aa
in the context of the AGT correspondence
Alday:2009aq
. Their importance arises from the fact that the quantum geometry of 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
Argyres-Douglas theories
Argyres:1995jj
admits higher order or
irregular
singular points. To model such singularities from the viewpoint of the 2d CFT, one lets two conformal primaries collide in such a way that their OPE expansion with the energy momentum tensor gives rise to higher (than second) order poles. Using the operator state correspondence, such irregular operators can then be understood as eigenstates of the Virasoro generators
L_{n}
for positive
n
. In other words they correspond to coherent states in the Hilbert sapce.

There are two principal methods for the computation of conformal blocks with ordinary primary field insertions. One approach is to solve the so called
BPZ equation

Belavin:1984vu
which is a higher order ordinary differential equation of Fuchsian type arising from the insertion of a degenerate state. Such degenerate states lead to null states upon action of a certain combination of Virasoro operators of a particular degree which upon using conformal Ward identities leads to the corresponding differential equation. Another method is the
Coulomb gas
formalism of Dotsenko and Fateev
Dotsenko:1984nm
. Here one views the individual primaries as charged particles which together with one or several screening charges are subject to a two-dimensional logarithmic Coulomb potential. The conformal block then arises from the computation of the corresponding partition function. The result can be expressed as an integral over certain loci in configuration space which mathematically can be viewed as period integrals. This approach is particularly useful for the interpretation of conformal blocks as fractional quantum Hall (FQH) wavefunctions
Moore:1991ks
;
Santachiara:2010bt
;
VafaFQHE
;
Bergamin:2019dhg
. This gives a natural handle to compute the quantum statistics of Anyons in such systems and in particular to identify non-Abelian topological phases. These are gapped phases of matter where adiabatically transporting one Anyon around another gives rise to a unitary transformation of the degenerate groundstate wavefunctions. Such systems can therefore store topologically protected qubits and are important for topological quantum computation
DasSarma:2005zz
. From a more modern perspective, there will be one-form symmetries
Gaiotto:2014kfa
in the bulk acting on anyonic Wilson lines giving rise to non-invertible symmetries
Shao:2023gho
.

The presence of irregular operators, with which we are concerned in the current paper, changes the above structures as follows. The conformal Ward identities are modified leading to modified versions of the BPZ equation
Bonelli:2022ten
. We re-derive these modified BPZ equations by first constructing explicit representations of irregular vertex operators and subsequently compute their OPE with the energy momentum tensor. We then use the explicit form of the operators to compute the integrand of the Coulomb gas representation of irregular conformal blocks. The corresponding potential gets modified by polynomial terms in addition to the previously present logarithmic terms. From a more geometric perspective, conformal blocks of ordinary primaries correspond to Hypergeometric functions which can be viewed as sections of flat bundles over the configuration space. These sections have monodromies around regular singular points giving rise to representations of the braid group
cmp/1104201923
;
Varchenko
;
Witten:1988hf
. This structure results in a braided fusion category and ultimately in a modular tensor category
Moore-Seiberg
. Irregular vertex operators can be viewed as new types of monodromy defects and give rise to irregular singularities in configuration space. From a symmetry category point of view, these monodromy defects correspond to non-simple objects and are expected to give rise to new types of fusion categories
Gu:2023bjg
. The resulting conformal block bundles are more interesting from several points of view. First, there will be Stokes lines emanating from the irregular singularity which change the monodromy behavior and result in asymptotic expansions there
Gaiotto:2011nm
;
Gu:2023bjg
. Second, the corresponding flat connections have thus far never been studied and their integrability conditions have not been determined explicitly, yet. From yet another perspective, one expects a relation to the tt* equations of certain Landau-Ginzburg models with mixed logarithmic and polynomial potentials
Cecotti:2014wea
. We explicitly construct flat connections by taking derivatives of the previously derived period integral representations of irregular conformal blocks.

1.1
Coulomb gas formalism and Yang-Yang functional

Our method to compute the conformal block is the free field realization also known as the Coulomb gas formalism
Dotsenko:1984nm
. Considering a two-dimensional Liouville type CFT
Teschner_2001
;
https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.hep-th/9304011
, with classical action given by

S=\int dtd\sigma\left(\frac{1}{16\pi}((\partial_{t}\varphi)^{2}-(\partial_{\sigma}\varphi)^{2})-\mu e^{\varphi}\right),

(1)

the central charge and conformal dimensions of primary operators of the corresponding quantum theory are parameterized in terms of (a complex) coupling constant parameter
b
as follows,

Q=b+\frac{1}{b},\quad c=1+6Q^{2},\quad\Delta_{\alpha}=\alpha(Q-\alpha).

(2)

Here,
\alpha
is known as the momentum of the primary operator which is a vertex operator and can be expressed as
V_{\alpha}=e^{2\alpha\phi}
, with
\phi\underset{b\rightarrow 0}{\sim}\frac{1}{2b}\varphi
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. More useful for our purposes will be the following set of degenerate fields labeled by
r,s\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and constructed using the scalar field
\phi
(see for example
VafaFQHE
):

\Psi_{r,s}\equiv\exp\left([(1-r)b+\frac{1-s}{b}]\phi\right).

(3)

The corresponding conformal dimensions are given by

h_{r,s}=h_{0}-\frac{1}{4}\left(rb+s\frac{1}{b}\right)^{2},\quad h_{0}=\frac{1}{24}(c-1).

(4)

For
k_{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
the relation
V_{-k_{a}/2b}=\Psi_{1,k_{a}+1}
.
These operators satisfy the fusion rules

V_{-\frac{k_{1}}{2b}}\times V_{-\frac{k_{2}}{2b}}=\sum_{l=|k_{1}-k_{2}|}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}V_{-\frac{l}{2b}},\quad\Delta l=2.

(5)

Our goal is to compute conformal blocks
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma_{\{\alpha\}}}
arising in the computation of the correlation function of the following product of degenerate fields,

\langle\prod_{a}V_{-k_{a}/2b}(z_{a},\bar{z}_{a})\rangle\equiv\int\left[\mathcal{D}\phi\right]e^{-S[\phi]}\prod_{a}e^{-\frac{k_{a}}{b}\phi}=\sum_{\{\alpha\}}C_{\{k\},\{\alpha\}}\overline{C}_{\{k\},\{\alpha\}}\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma_{\{\alpha\}}}\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\Gamma_{\{\alpha\}}},

(6)

where
\{\alpha\}
denotes the collection of all fields arising as intermediate fusion states in the conformal block tree and
C_{\{k\},\{\alpha\}}
are suitable fusion coefficients. The conformal blocks
\mathcal{F}
can be computed using extra insertions of
V_{1/b}(w_{i})
operators by integrating the following free field correlator over suitable integration cycles
\Gamma

Dotsenko:1984nm
;
Teschner_2001
;
Gaiotto:2011nm
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\left(\prod_{i}V_{1/b}(w_{i})\prod_{a}V_{-k_{a}/2b}(z_{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i<j}(w_{i}-w_{j})^{\frac{-2}{b^{2}}}\prod_{i,a}(w_{i}-z_{a})^{\frac{k_{a}}{b^{2}}}\prod_{a<b}(z_{a}-z_{b})^{\frac{-k_{a}k_{b}}{2b^{2}}}

(7)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}}\mathcal{W}(w,z)\right),

where
\mathcal{W}
is given by

\mathcal{W}=\sum_{i<j}-2\log(w_{i}-w_{j})+\sum_{i,a}k_{a}\log(w_{i}-z_{a})-\sum_{a<b}\frac{1}{2}k_{a}k_{b}\log(z_{a}-z_{b}),

(8)

and is known as the Yang-Yang superpotential due to its relation to 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
Landau-Ginzburg models and integrable systems
Jeong:2018qpc
;
Yang:1968rm
. The
V_{1/b}(w_{i})
are known as screening charges and their number is restricted by the following charge conservation condition

\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{a}\alpha_{a}=Q,\quad\alpha_{i}=\frac{1}{b},

(9)

which corresponds to poles in the evaluation of the path integral (
6
) with residues given by the free field correlator (
7
) in the vicinity of a background charge
-Q
. One may employ the duality
\alpha_{i,a}\mapsto Q-\alpha_{i,a}
, in order to ensure that the constraint (
9
) holds. One then integrates over
w_{i}
variables along suitable cycles
\Gamma
to arrive at an expression for the conformal blocks of the interacting Liouville CFT,

\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}\equiv\int_{\Gamma}\mathcal{V}\left(\prod_{i}V_{1/b}(w_{i})\prod_{a}V_{-k_{a}/2b}(z_{a})\right)\prod_{i}dw_{i},

(10)

where we may have more than one integration variable, denoted by
w_{i}
, and the integration cycle
\Gamma
can be rather freely chosen as long as the integral converges. Let us give an example to clarify the procedure. To this end consider the 4-point function

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{1,2}(\infty)\Psi_{1,2}(1)\Psi_{1,2}(z)\Psi_{1,2}(0)\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\mathcal{V}\left(V_{\frac{1}{b}}(w)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(0)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(1)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(\infty)\right)dw

(11)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left[z(1-z)\right]^{-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma}dw\left[w(w-1)(w-z)\right]^{1/b^{2}},

where
\Gamma
can be one of two cycles
\Gamma_{\alpha}
,
\alpha=0,\frac{1}{b}
, defined as

\Gamma_{0}=\left[1,\infty\right],\quad\Gamma_{\frac{1}{b}}=\left[0,z\right].

(12)

Thus the two integration cycles correspond to two conformal blocks which in turn correspond to the two different fusion channels. More generally, the integration cycles are given by
Lefschetz thimbles

Gaiotto:2011nm
;
Gu:2023bjg
. Such integration thimbles are composed of steepest descent paths flowing out from critical points of
\mathcal{W}(w,z)
. So for a critical point
\sigma
of
\mathcal{W}(w,z)
, the associated Lefschetz thimble is obtained by joining all paths
\mathcal{W}(t)
,
t\in(-\infty,0]
which solve the first order equation

\frac{d\bar{w}}{dt}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial w},\quad\frac{dw}{dt}=-\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{W}}}{d\bar{w}},\quad\mathcal{W}(-\infty)=\sigma.

(13)

Such thimbles can either arise from flows between two critical points but in more general situations are non-compact and flow to infinity along both ends. These non-compact cycles become important once we compute conformal blocks with irregular operators which is the main subject of the present paper.

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{irr}}

2
Irregular representations of the Virasoro algebra

Irregular vectors were initially introduced by Gaiotto
Gaiotto:2009ma
, and are characterized as special states in the CFT Hilbert space that are eigenstates of Virasoro generators
L_{n}
with positive
n
with all but a finite set of eigenvalues vanishing. In terms of the Liouville CFT, the corresponding operators can be modeled as collision limits of two regular vertex operators as derived for example in
Gaiotto:2012sf
. In the first part of this section we will be reviewing the definition of irregular states and, by using the operator state correspondence, provide corresponding vertex operator representations. In the second part, we derive, using an algebraic approach, modified BPZ equations satisfied by conformal blocks with 3 regular primaries and one irregular operator at infinity.

2.1
Definition

Irregular states are classified by their rank which is the largest integer
r
for which the eigenvalue of
L_{2r}
is non-vanishing. Taking an algebraic point of view, in the simplest of rank
1
, the irregular state
\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}
satisfies the following properties
Gaiotto:2012sf
:

\displaystyle L_{0}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\alpha}+c\partial_{c})\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

\displaystyle L_{1}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=-c(\alpha-Q)\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

\displaystyle L_{2}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

(20)

where
\alpha
denotes the Liouville momentum and
c
is an arbitrary complex parameter not to be confused with the central charge. In Liouville theory, as reviewed before, we can use the Coulomb gas formalism to compute the correlation functions. Thus the
n
-point function introduced before can be obtained through the OPE of the vertex operators alone. In the following, we make a proposal (based on earlier work in
Gaiotto:2011nm
) for rank
1
irregular vertex operators. The corresponding expression, using the free field
\chi
, is given by

\displaystyle\exp(c\partial\chi(L)),\quad\text{with limit $L\rightarrow\infty$.}

(21)

To verify that the above vertex operator indeed gives rise to an irregular state, we compute its OPE with the energy momentum tensor which for Liouville theory is defined as follows,

\displaystyle T(z)=-:\partial\chi(z)\partial\chi(z):+Q\partial^{2}\chi(z),\text{with }Q=b+\frac{1}{b}.

(22)

We can hence deduce the action of the Virasoro algebra on the irregular state by employing the state-operator correspondence and the OPE of the stress-energy tensor with the irregular operator (
21
). Using
Wick’s theorem and the correlation function of the free field:

\displaystyle\braket{\chi(z)\chi(L)}=-\frac{1}{2}\log(z-L),

(23)

we thus obtain

\displaystyle T(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=(-:\partial\chi(z)\partial\chi(z):+Q\partial^{2}\chi(z))\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=-(\langle\partial\chi(z)c\partial\chi(L)\rangle^{2}\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle-2\langle\partial\chi(z)c\partial\chi(L)\rangle\partial\chi(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L)))+Q\partial^{2}\chi(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{(z-L)^{4}}+c\frac{1}{(z-L)^{2}}\partial\chi(z))\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle+Q\partial^{2}\chi(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{(z-L)^{4}}+c\frac{1}{(z-L)^{2}}\partial\chi(z))\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle+Qc\frac{1}{(z-L)^{3}}\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{(z-L)^{4}}+Qc\frac{1}{(z-L)^{3}}\right)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle+c\frac{1}{(z-L)^{2}}(\partial\chi(L)+(z-L)\partial^{2}\chi(L))\exp(c\partial\chi(L)).

(24)

To obtain the action of
L_{0}
on the corresponding irregular vector, we just use the integral expression

\displaystyle L_{n}=\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi i}z^{n+1}T(z),

(25)

giving

\displaystyle[L_{n},\exp(c\partial\chi(L))]=\oint_{\text{around }L}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}z^{n+1}T(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L)).

(26)

For
n=0
, we have

\displaystyle[L_{0},\exp(c\partial\chi(L))]

\displaystyle=\oint_{\text{around }L}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}zT(z)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=\oint_{\text{around }0}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}(z+L)(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{z^{4}}+Qc\frac{1}{z^{3}}

\displaystyle+c\frac{1}{z^{2}}(\partial\chi(L)+z\partial\chi(L)))\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=c\partial\chi(L)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))+cL\partial^{2}\chi(L)\exp(c\partial\chi(L))

\displaystyle=c\partial_{c}\exp(c\partial\chi(L))+cL\partial\exp(c\partial\chi(L)).

(27)

Similarly, for
n=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle[L_{1},\exp(c\partial\chi(L))]

\displaystyle=(Qc\exp(c\partial\chi(L)))+2cL\partial_{c}\exp(c\partial\chi(L))+cL^{2}\partial\exp(c\partial\chi(L)).

(28)

From the above equations, we can get the action of the conformal operators via the state-operator correspondence by letting
L\rightarrow 0
, that is

\displaystyle[L_{0},\exp(c\partial\chi(0))]\ket{0}

\displaystyle=c\partial_{c}\ket{I_{c}},

(29)

\displaystyle[L_{1},\exp(c\partial\chi(0))]\ket{0}

\displaystyle=Qc\ket{I_{c}},

(30)

\displaystyle[L_{2},\exp(c\partial\chi(0))]\ket{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\ket{I_{c}},

(31)

where

\ket{I_{c}}\equiv\exp(c\partial\chi(0))\ket{0}.

(32)

We can then see that this irregular state corresponds to the one from the definition given in (
20
) for
\alpha=0
. On the other hand, we can also construct vertex operators that satisfy the definition with nonzero
\alpha
, by setting

\displaystyle V(L)\equiv~:\exp(2\alpha\chi(L))\exp(c\partial\chi(L)):.

(33)

We can check that the OPE with the stress-energy tensor has the following form

\displaystyle T(z):\exp(2\alpha\chi(L))\exp(c\partial\chi(L)):

\displaystyle=(-:\partial\chi(z)\partial\chi(z):+Q\partial^{2}\chi(z)):\exp(2\alpha\chi(L))\exp(c\partial\chi(L)):

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{Q\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{(z-L)^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{z-L}\partial\chi(z)\right)V(L)-\frac{c\alpha}{(z-L)^{3}}V(L)

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{(z-L)^{4}}+c\frac{1}{(z-L)^{2}}\partial\chi(z)\right)V(L)+Qc\frac{1}{(z-L)^{3}}V(L).

(34)

Similar to before, we can then read off the action of
L_{i}
on the irregular state using the state-operator correspondence,

\displaystyle L_{0}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\alpha}+c\partial_{c}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

(35)

\displaystyle L_{1}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=-c(\alpha-Q)\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

(36)

\displaystyle L_{2}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}},

(37)

where
\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}=V(0)\ket{0}
, which is exactly the irregular vector of rank
1
introduced in
Gaiotto:2012sf
.

2.2
Derivation of modified BPZ equation

We can derive BPZ like equations utilizing the above OPEs. When considering the descendant states of the primary state corresponding to
V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}(0)\ket{0}:=\ket{V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}}
with conformal weight
\Delta=-\frac{1}{2b}(Q+\frac{1}{2b})
, one finds that there is a null state, which means a state of zero norm, of the following form

\displaystyle(L_{-2}+b^{2}L_{-1}^{2})\ket{V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}}.

(38)

Inserting the above null state into the correlation function should thus lead to vanishing of the correlation function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\bra{0}V(\infty)(L_{-2}+b^{2}L_{-1})V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}(z)V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}(z_{0})V_{\frac{-1}{2b}}(z_{1})\ket{0}=0,

(39)

which corresponds to the following equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-2}\mathcal{F}+b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\mathcal{F}=0.

(40)

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{-2}
is called the descent operator that directly creates the descendant state by employing the state-operator correspondence, and whose action on a primary operator
\phi
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-2}\phi(z)=\oint\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{(w-z)}T(w)\phi(z).

(41)

In the case when all fields in the bracket are primary fields, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{-2}
has the representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-2}=\left(\sum_{i}\frac{h_{i}}{(z_{i}-z)^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{i}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right).

(42)

This can be derived by considering the definition of the descendant fields in terms of contour integrals of the primary field OPE with the stress energy tensor
T(w)
where the contour in the
w
-plane goes around the singularities at
z_{i}
. Since in our case, there is an additional irregular vertex operator, the representation of the action of
\mathcal{L}_{-2}
should be modified. Computing directly, one finds the action on the irregular operator in the following way

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-2}V(\infty)=-\oint_{\text{around $\infty$}}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{w-z}T(w)V(\infty)

\displaystyle=-\oint_{\text{around $\infty$}}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{w}T(w)V(\infty)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v=\frac{1}{w}}}{{=}}-\oint_{\text{around $0$}}\frac{dv}{2\pi i}\frac{dw}{dv}v\left(\frac{dv}{dw}\right)^{2}T(v)V

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{dv}{2\pi i}v^{3}\left(\left(\frac{Q\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{v^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{v}\partial\chi(0)\right)V-\frac{c\alpha}{v^{3}}V+(-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{v^{4}}+c\frac{1}{v^{2}}\partial\chi(0))V+Qc\frac{1}{v^{3}}V\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}}{4}V.

(43)

Having obtained the action of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{-2}
on the irregular state, we can proceed to deduce its action on the entire correlator and hence arrive at the second order differential equation that the conformal block
\mathcal{F}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left((b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{1}-z)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{0}-z)^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}})-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(44)

We can derive further equations satisfied by our conformal blocks corresponding to global conformal Ward identities. Notice that global conformal invariance with respect to
L_{-1}
implies the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-1}\mathcal{F}(z,z_{0},z_{1},c)

\displaystyle=\bra{0}[L_{1},I_{\alpha,c}]V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{0})V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{1})\ket{0}

\displaystyle+\bra{0}I_{\alpha,c}[L_{-1},V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z)]V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{0})V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{1})\ket{0}

\displaystyle+\bra{0}I_{\alpha,c}V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z)[L_{-1},V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{0})]V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{1})\ket{0}

\displaystyle+\bra{0}I_{\alpha,c}V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z)V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{0})[L_{-1},V_{-\frac{1}{2b}}(z_{1})]\ket{0}=0.

(45)

Here the irregular state
\bra{I_{\alpha,c}}
is inserted at infinity, so the action of
L_{-1}
becomes its adjoint. From the action above, we have

\displaystyle L_{1}\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}=-c(\alpha-Q)\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}=(b+\frac{1}{b}-\alpha)c\ket{I_{\alpha,c}}.

(46)

Another global conformal Ward identity is obtained from the action of
L_{0}
. With these two equations, we can express the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}
in terms of differential operators with respect to
z
and
c
, i.e.

\displaystyle\text{$L_{-1}$ invariance}\Rightarrow\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+c(\alpha-Q)\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(47)

\displaystyle\text{$L_{0}$ invariance}\Rightarrow\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+z_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}+z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+3\Delta-(c\frac{\partial}{\partial c}+\Delta_{\alpha})\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(48)

Thus giving

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta}{(z_{1}-z_{0})}+z_{0}\frac{c(\alpha-Q)}{(z_{1}-z_{0})}+\frac{c}{(z_{1}-z_{0})}\frac{\partial}{\partial c}-\frac{z-z_{0}}{z_{1}-z_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\mathcal{F},

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta}{(z_{0}-z_{1})}+z_{1}\frac{c(\alpha-Q)}{(z_{0}-z_{1})}+\frac{c}{(z_{0}-z_{1})}\frac{\partial}{\partial c}-\frac{z-z_{1}}{z_{0}-z_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\mathcal{F}.

(50)

Inserting back in (
44
) finally gives the BPZ like differential equation satisfied by
\mathcal{F}
, namely

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{1}-z)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{0}-z)^{2}}-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\right)\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{-2z+z_{0}+z_{1}}{2\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}c\frac{\partial}{\partial c}+\frac{-z+z_{0}+z_{1}}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}c(\alpha-Q)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}(\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta)\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(51)

Setting
z_{1}=1,z_{0}=0
, we arrive at the final form of the BPZ equation:

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-(\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z-1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\Delta}{(z-1)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{z^{2}}+\frac{1}{z(z-1)}(c\frac{\partial}{\partial c}+\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta)-\frac{c(\alpha-Q)}{z}-\frac{c^{2}}{4}\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(52)

3.1
3-point function with irregular operator at infinity

Let us next turn to the case of a 3-point function with one irregular operator at infinity. Setting the

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(w,z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\log(z_{1}-z_{2})+\log(z_{1}-z_{3})+\log(z_{2}-z_{3}))+

(58)

\displaystyle\log(w-z_{1})+\log(w-z_{2})+\log(w-z_{3})-\Lambda

\displaystyle\left(\frac{z_{1}}{2}+\frac{z_{2}}{2}+\frac{z_{3}}{2}-w\right),

and the conformal blocks can be written as the integral over the corresponding Lefschetz thimbles. For example, in this case, one can use conformal transformations setting
z_{1}=z,z_{2}=1,z_{3}=0
. Then our superpotential becomes

\mathcal{W}(w,z)=\log(w-z)+\log(w-1)+\log(w)+\Lambda\left(w-\frac{z}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{0}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log(z(z-1)),

and the resulting conformal block has the following thimble representation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp(\frac{1}{b^{2}}\mathcal{W}(w,z))

\displaystyle=[z(z-1)]^{-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\Lambda(z+1)}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp\left(\frac{\Lambda w+\log(w-z)+\log(w-1)+\log(w)}{b^{2}}\right).

(59)

3.2
BPZ equation

Now let us show that the integral representation of our conformal blocks satisfies the BPZ equation (
52
). To this end, we first work with the generalized expression where the primaries are located at points
z
,
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
. Then the corresponding block is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\beta}[(z-z_{0})(z-z_{1})(z_{1}-z_{0})]^{-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\Lambda(z+z_{1}+z_{0})}{2b^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp\left(\frac{\Lambda w+\log(w-z)+\log(w-z_{1})+\log(w-z_{0})}{b^{2}}\right),

where we have introduced an overall scaling factor
\Lambda^{\beta}
whose significance will become clear later. We want to show that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
44

\mathcal{F}(z)=f(z,z_{0},z_{1},\Lambda)\times\phi(z,z_{0},z_{1},\Lambda),

(60)

where

\displaystyle\phi(z,z_{0},z_{1},\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp\left(\frac{\Lambda w+\log(w-z)+\log(w-z_{1})+\log(w-z_{0})}{b^{2}}\right)

(61)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp(I),

(62)

and

f(z,z_{0},z_{1},\Lambda)\equiv\Lambda^{\beta}[(z-z_{0})(z-z_{1})(z_{1}-z_{0})]^{-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\Lambda(z+z_{1}+z_{0})}{2b^{2}}}.

(63)

Henceforth we will abbreviate
\phi(z,1,0,\Lambda)=\phi(z)
. Then equation (
44

\displaystyle\left((b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{1}-z)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{0}-z)^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}})-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4b^{2}}\right)(f\phi)=0,

(64)

since we performed the rescaling (
55
),
so now the value of
c
under the transformation should be
\frac{\Lambda}{b}
. And we notice by direct substitution that

\displaystyle\left((b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{1}-z)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{0}-z)^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}})-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4b^{2}}\right)f=0.

(65)

Thus it remains to prove the following identity

\displaystyle f\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\phi+2b^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}=0.

(66)

As we will see in the next subsection and in more generality in Section

, the functions

) gives

\displaystyle b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial z^{2}}=\Lambda\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z_{0}})+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z_{1}}),

(67)

or after rearranging,

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\phi=\left(\Lambda+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}\right)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}.

(68)

We also notice that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}(\Lambda+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}})f,

(69)

and therefore

\displaystyle f\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\phi+2b^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=f\left(\Lambda+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}\right)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}+2b^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=f\left(\Lambda+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}\right)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}+2b^{2}(-\frac{1}{2b^{2}})(\Lambda+\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{1}})f\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=0.

(70)

64

(71)

\displaystyle\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+z_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}+z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+3\Delta-(\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}+\Delta_{\alpha})\right)\mathcal{F}=0,

(72)

,

). The result is

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{1}-z)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z_{0}-z)^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4b^{2}}\right)\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{-2z+z_{0}+z_{1}}{2\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}+\frac{-z+z_{0}+z_{1}}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}(\alpha-Q)\frac{\Lambda}{b}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)\left(z-z_{1}\right)}(\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta)\right)\mathcal{F}=0.

(73)

Finally, setting
z_{1}=1,z_{0}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left(b^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-(\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z-1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\Delta}{(z-1)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{z^{2}}+\frac{1}{z(z-1)}(\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}+\Delta_{\alpha}-3\Delta)-\frac{\Lambda(\alpha-Q)}{bz}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4b^{2}}\right)\mathcal{F}=0

(74)

as a special case of (
).

4
Derivation of KZ-type equation

This section is concerned with the derivation of KZ-type differential equations satisfied by irregular conformal blocks. We derive vector valued first order equations which define flat connections on the irregular conformal block bundle. We first derive such equations for 2-point functions with one additional irregular operator, 3-point functions with one irregular operator, and then subsequently generalize to the n-point case.

As we have seen, when including an irregular operator at infinity, the expression in (
8
) for
\mathcal{W}
gets modified in the following way,

\mathcal{W}(w,z)\rightarrow\mathcal{W}(w,z)+\Lambda(\sum_{i}w_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a}z_{a}),

(75)

where
\Lambda
is a constant proportional to the eigenvalue of
L_{1}
when acting on the irregular state. The added term is called the symmetry breaking term
Gaiotto:2011nm
. In the following, we will see how it gives rise to an irregular singularity at infinity in the monodromy representation of conformal blocks.

4.1
2-point function with one irregular operator

As a warm up, let us first have a look at the example of 2-point functions with one additional irregular operator. The superpotential is given as follows, corresponding to the insertion of two regular operators at
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
and an irregular operator at infinity,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(w,z_{1},z_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}(\log(z_{1}-z_{2}))+\log(w-z_{1})+\log(w-z_{2})-\Lambda(\frac{z_{1}}{2}+\frac{z_{2}}{2}-w).

(76)

In this case, all the conformal blocks corresponding to such a superpotential are given by integration over Lefschetz Thimbles
Gaiotto:2011nm
:

\displaystyle\phi(z_{1},z_{2})=\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\exp{\frac{1}{b^{2}}W}dw,

(77)

where the Lefschetz thimbles
\Gamma_{\alpha}
are determined by solving the gradient descent equations corresponding to BPS solitons from the Landau-Ginzburg point of view. Moreover, by conformal symmetry, we can take
z_{1}=z,z_{2}=0
utilizing a conformal transformation. So the integration can be rewritten as follows,

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\exp{\frac{1}{b^{2}}W}dw=z^{\frac{1}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(w-z)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}w^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda}{b^{2}}(-\frac{z}{2}+w)}dw.

(78)

Performing the suitable substitutions
w=\frac{z}{2}(t+1)\Leftrightarrow w-\frac{z}{2}=\frac{z}{2}t
, the integral becomes

\displaystyle\phi(z)=z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt.

(79)

We now have the following

Proposition 1
.

Let
\phi(z)
be defined as above. Furthermore, define

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(z)\equiv\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}t(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt.

(80)

Then the vector
\varphi\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\phi(z)\\
\phi_{1}(z)\end{pmatrix}
, satisfies the following first order differential equation,

\frac{d\varphi}{dz}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}&\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\\
\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}&\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}-\frac{2\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}}+1\right)}{z}\\
\end{array}\right)\varphi=\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\\
\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}&0\\
\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{z}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{5}{2b^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2b^{2}}-2\\
\end{array}\right)\right)\varphi~.

Proof.

We observe that,

\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi(z)+z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}\frac{\Lambda t}{2b^{2}}dt=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi(z)+\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\phi_{1}(z),

Moreover, differentiating further
\phi_{1}
, one arrives at

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\frac{\Lambda t^{2}}{2b^{2}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+\Lambda\frac{z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}}{2b^{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}(t^{2}-1)\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt+\Lambda\frac{z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}}{2b^{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+\Lambda\frac{z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}}{2b^{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}+1}\frac{2b^{2}}{\Lambda z}d(\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}})+\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\phi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\phi(z)-\frac{z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}}{z}\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}d[(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}+1}]\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\phi(z)-\frac{z^{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}}{z}(1+\frac{1}{b^{2}})\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}}(t^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{b^{2}}}2t\exp{\frac{\Lambda zt}{2b^{2}}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{5}{2b^{2}}}{z}\phi_{1}(z)+\frac{\Lambda}{2b^{2}}\phi(z)-\frac{2}{z}(1+\frac{1}{b^{2}})\phi_{1}(z).

Combining the expressions for
\phi^{\prime}(z)
and
\phi^{\prime}_{1}(z)
then gives the desired result.
∎

From the form of the equation satisfied by
\varphi
we immediately deduce the presence of a regular singularity at
z=0
and, for non-zero
\Lambda
, an irregular singularity at infinity upon performing the change of variables
z\rightarrow\frac{1}{\tilde{z}}
.

Getting the Stokes matrix using properties of Bessel functions.

Using the above linear equation, we can get a second order ODE that
\phi(z)
satisfies,

\displaystyle 4b^{4}z^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(z)+4b^{2}z\left(2b^{2}-3\right)\phi^{\prime}(z)-\left(10b^{2}+\Lambda^{2}z^{2}-5\right)\phi(z)=0,

(81)

which can be solved in terms of Bessel functions (see Appendix
) as follows,

\varphi(z)=z^{\frac{-b^{2}+3}{2b^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}J_{\frac{b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\\
J_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\varphi(z)=z^{\frac{-b^{2}+3}{2b^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}Y_{\frac{b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\\
Y_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix},

(82)

or after a basis transformation,

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=z^{\frac{-b^{2}+3}{2b^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}H_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}^{(1)}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\\
\frac{d}{dz}H_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}^{(1)}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(z)=z^{\frac{-b^{2}+3}{2b^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}H_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}^{(2)}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\\
\frac{d}{dz}H_{\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}^{(2)}\left(-\frac{i\Lambda z}{2b^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(83)

In the above we have used that
J_{\nu-1}(z)
is a linear combination of
J_{\nu}(z)
and
\frac{d}{dz}J_{\nu}(z)

, and
H^{(1)}_{\nu}=J_{\nu}+iY_{\nu},H^{(2)}_{\nu}=J_{\nu}-iY_{\nu}
are Hankel functions.

It is clear that there are two possible singularities of the functions, one is at zero, and the other at infinity. The first one being a regular singularity, and the latter being of irregular nature. It is possible to obtain the Stokes matrix at infinity using the relationship:

\displaystyle H_{\nu}^{(1)}(xe^{im\pi})=-\frac{\sin(\pi(m-1)n)}{\sin(\pi n)}H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)-\frac{e^{-i\pi n}\sin(\pi mn)}{\sin(\pi n)}H_{\nu}^{(2)}(x),

\displaystyle H_{\nu}^{(2)}(xe^{im\pi})=\frac{e^{i\pi n}\sin(\pi mn)}{\sin(\pi n)}H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)+\frac{\sin(\pi(m+1)n)}{\sin(\pi n)}H_{\nu}^{(2)}(x).

(84)

Together with the asymptotic behavior of the Hankel functions in some particular branches,

\displaystyle H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)=\hat{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)x^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{ix},H_{\nu}^{(2)}(x)=\hat{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)x^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-ix},

(85)

where
\hat{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)
has asymptotic series expansion in powers of
x^{-1}
, one deduces that there are two Stokes rays emanating from
x=0
along the positive and negative real axis. That is, the asymptotic behavior changes when crossing these rays. From the expression of (
83
) we know that the Stokes matrices of these two solutions don’t depend on the term
z^{\frac{-b^{2}+3}{2b^{2}}}
. Thus the Stokes matrices are those from the Hankel functions alone.

From these expressions we can extract the Stokes matrix around
z=\infty
when crossing the rays along the real axis (
4.1
). The result is the following expression encoding all Stokes matrices:

\displaystyle S=\begin{pmatrix}1&2e^{i\pi\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}}}\cos(\pi\frac{3b^{2}+2}{2b^{2}})\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(86)

4.2
3-point function with one irregular operator

When proving that our conformal block

satisfies the BPZ equation, we made crucial use of the identity (

In order to solve for the exact expression of

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp\left(\frac{\Lambda w+\log(w-z)+\log(w-1)+\log(w)}{b^{2}}\right)

Proposition 2

Let

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\phi^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp(I)\frac{1}{w-z}\\

One straightforwardly computes

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{b^{4}}\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp(I)\frac{1}{w}\frac{1}{w-z}

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b^{2}}(\frac{1}{b^{2}}-1)\int_{\Gamma}dw\exp(I)\frac{1}{(w-z)^{2}}

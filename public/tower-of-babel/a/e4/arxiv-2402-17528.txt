Constructions of t t -designs from weighing matrices and walk-regular graphs
Gary Greaves, Sho Suda

Abstract
We provide a method to construct t t -designs from weighing matrices and walk-regular graphs. One instance of our method can produce a 3 3 -design from any (symmetric or skew-symmetric) conference matrix, thereby providing a partial answer to a question of Gunderson and Semeraro JCTB 2017. We explore variations of our method on some matrices that satisfy certain combinatorial restrictions. In particular, we show that there exist various infinite families of partially balanced incomplete block designs with block size four on the binary Hamming schemes and the 3 3 -class association schemes attached to symmetric designs, and regular pairwise balanced designs with block sizes three and four.

Organisation

The organisation of this paper is as follows.

1.5
Preliminaries

In this subsection, we collect some preliminary results about determinants of principal submatrices that we will use in the later sections.
The following two lemmas provide a convenient way to express the characteristic polynomial of a principal submatrix, which we make use of in our main results.
The first lemma is a classical identity due to Jacobi (see
[
19
, Page 21]
or
[
20
, Page 52]
), which can be seen as a consequence of the Schur complement.

Lemma 1.3

(Jacobi)
.

Let
A
be a complex square matrix of order
v
and let
\alpha\subset[v]
.
Then

\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])=\det(xI-A)\det((xI-A)^{-1}[\alpha]).

Denote by
e_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})
the
j
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{s}]
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
A
be a
v\times v
complex matrix with
\det(xI-A)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}}
for distinct
\theta_{i}
.
Then, for each
\alpha\subset[v]
, we have

\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}-|\alpha|}\cdot\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s})x^{i-j}\right)A^{s-i}[\alpha]\right).

Proof.

Since

A^{s}-e_{1}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s})A^{s-1}+e_{2}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s})A^{s-2}+\cdots+(-1)^{s}e_{s}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s})I=O,

it is routinely shown that

(xI-A)^{-1}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s})x^{i-j})A^{s-i}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})}.

The lemma then follows from Lemma
1.3
.
∎

For convenience, we exhibit the equation of Lemma
1.4
for the case when

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
A
be a

complex matrix with
\det(xI-A)=(x-\lambda)^{m_{\lambda}}(x-\mu)^{m_{\mu}}
for
\lambda\neq\mu
.
Then, for each
\alpha\subset[v]
, we have

\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])=(x-\lambda)^{m_{\lambda}-|\alpha|}(x-\mu)^{m_{\mu}-|\alpha|}\det\left(A[\alpha]+(x-\lambda-\mu)I\right).

2.1
Hypergraphs from square matrices

Before we establish our main result, we require the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v
and
t
be positive integers and
\mathfrak{B}
be a family of subsets of
[v]
.
For
\beta\subset[v]
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma\subset\beta}(-1)^{|\gamma|}\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\gamma).

Suppose that, for each
i\in\{0,1,\dots,t\}
, there exists
\mu_{i}
such that
\mu_{i}=\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
for all
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}

Then, for each
i\in[t]
and
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\binom{i}{j}\mu_{j}.

Proof.

The proof of this fact is obtained by modifying the proof of
[
45
, Theorem 9.7]
as follows.
For any subset
\gamma\subset\beta
, it is clear that

\left|\bigcap_{z\in\gamma}\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}\;:\;z\not\in\alpha\}\right|=\left|\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}\;:\;\alpha\cap\gamma=\emptyset\}\right|=\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\gamma).

Then the principle of Inclusion-Exclusion asserts that

\displaystyle|\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}\;:\;\beta\subset\alpha\}|=\left|\mathfrak{B}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{z\in\beta}\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}\;:\;z\not\in\alpha\}\right)\right|=\sum_{\gamma\subset\beta}(-1)^{|\gamma|}\left|\bigcap_{z\in\gamma}\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}\;:\;z\not\in\alpha\}\right|,

which shows that

\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma\subset\beta}(-1)^{|\gamma|}\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\gamma).

The remaining assertions follow from this equation.
∎

The following is the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
t
,
v
, and
k
be positive integers.
Let

be a

(1)

D_{A}(k)=\{a,b\}
, where

, and

\lambda=\frac{(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{t}}{(a-b)\binom{v}{t}}c_{A}(\emptyset,k)-\frac{b}{a-b}\binom{v-t}{k-t}.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{B}_{A}(v,k,a)
.
First we claim that, for each
i\in\{0,1,\dots,t\}
, there exists
\mu_{i}
such that
\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\mu_{i}
for each
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
.
By Corollary

together with assumption (1), we obtain

\displaystyle c_{i}=c_{A}(\beta,k)=(-1)^{k}\left((a-b)\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)+b\binom{v-i}{k}\right),

from which the claim follows.
Hence, we write
\mu_{i}=\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
, where
i\in\{0,1,\dots,t\}
and
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
.

By Lemma

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\binom{i}{j}\mu_{j}.

Thus

.
Therefore the hypergraph

-design.

Finally we determine

Evaluate the value

\displaystyle X=\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in Y}\det(A[\alpha])

\displaystyle X=\sum_{\beta\in\binom{[v]}{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\binom{[v]}{k}\\
\beta\subset\alpha\end{subarray}}\det(A[\alpha])

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\binom{[v]}{t}}\left((a-b)\lambda+b\binom{v-t}{k-t}\right)=\binom{v}{t}\left((a-b)\lambda+b\binom{v-t}{k-t}\right),

\displaystyle X=\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{[v]}{k}}\sum_{\beta\in\binom{\alpha}{t}}\det(A[\alpha])=\binom{k}{t}\sum_{\alpha\in\binom{[v]}{k}}\det(A[\alpha])=(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{t}c_{A}(\emptyset,k).

\lambda=\frac{(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{t}}{(a-b)\binom{v}{t}}c_{A}(\emptyset,k)-\frac{b}{a-b}\binom{v-t}{k-t}.\qed

3

In this section, we obtain partially balanced incomplete block designs from a matrix

The parameters of the resulting PBIBDs can be determined from the spectral data of the matrix

3.1
Symmetric association schemes

Let
d
be a positive integer.
Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a finite set and
R_{i}
(
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
) be a nonempty subset of
\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}
.
The
adjacency matrix

A_{i}
of the graph with vertex set
\mathfrak{X}
and edge set
R_{i}
is a
(0,1)
-matrix indexed by
\mathfrak{X}
such that
(A_{i})_{xy}=1
if
(x,y)\in R_{i}
and
(A_{i})_{xy}=0
otherwise.
A (
symmetric
)
association scheme
with
d
classes is a pair
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X},\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
satisfying the following:

(1)

A_{0}=I_{|\mathfrak{X}|}
.

(2)

\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}=J_{|\mathfrak{X}|}
.

(3)

A_{i}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}=A_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

(4)

For any
i
and
j
, the product
A_{i}A_{j}
belongs to the linear span of the matrices
A_{0},\dots,A_{d}
.

The algebra generated by
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}
over
\mathbb{R}
is said to be the
Bose-Mesner algebra
of a symmetric association scheme.
An association scheme
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X},\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
can be defined indirectly in terms of the adjacency matrices
A_{0},\dots,A_{d}
(see Section
3.5
).

A
partially balanced incomplete block design
(PBIBD) on the association scheme
(\mathfrak{X},\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
is a
1
-design
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{B})
with the property that
for any
i
, there exists
\lambda_{i}
such that for any pair
(x,y)\in R_{i}
,
\{x,y\}
is contained in exactly
\lambda_{i}
hyperedges of
\mathfrak{B}

[
6
,
35
,
38
,
46
]
.
We denote it as
\text{PBIBD}(v,k;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
.
Note that if
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{d}
then the PBIBD is a
2
-design.

The following is an analog of Theorem
2.2
to the case of association schemes.
For a binary relation
R_{i}
, define
\tilde{R}_{i}=\{\{x,y\}\;:\;(x,y)\in R_{i}\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

,

\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma\subset\beta}(-1)^{|\gamma|}\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}&\text{ if }|\beta|=0,\\
\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}&\text{ if }|\beta|=1,\\
\mu_{0}-2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}(j)&\text{ if }|\beta|=2\text{
and }\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
does not depend on
\beta
for
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
when
i\in\{0,1\}
and depends only on
j
if
|\beta|=2
and
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}
.
Hence, the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,a)

is a PBIBD
(v,k;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
on
\mathcal{X}
.

Finally, we establish the expressions for the parameters
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
.
Since

\displaystyle\mu_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-b}\left((-1)^{k}c_{j}-b\binom{v-i}{k}\right)\quad\text{ for }i\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-b}\left((-1)^{k}c_{2}(j)-b\binom{v-2}{k}\right)\quad\text{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},

the parameters
\lambda_{j}
can be determined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}-2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a-b}\left(c_{0}-2c_{1}+c_{2}(j)\right)-\frac{b}{a-b}\left(\binom{v}{k}-2\binom{v-1}{k}+\binom{v-2}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a-b}\left(c_{0}-2c_{1}+c_{2}(j)\right)-\frac{b}{a-b}\binom{v-2}{k-2}.\qed

The following lemma shows that a matrix
A
taken from the Bose-Mesner algebra in an association scheme satisfies the assumption of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=([v],\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
be a symmetric association scheme and let
A
be an element of its Bose-Mesner algebra.
Then

(1)

\left|\left\{\det(xI-A[\overline{\beta}])\;:\;\beta\in\binom{[v]}{1}\right\}\right|=1
;

(2)

for each
i\in[d]
, we have
\left|\left\{\det(xI-A[\overline{\beta}])\;:\;\beta\in\tilde{R}_{i}\right\}\right|=1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\det(xI-A)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}}
.
Since
A^{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{j}(k)A_{k}
for some coefficients
a_{j}(k)
, by Lemma
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle\det(xI-A[\overline{\beta}])

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}-|\beta|}\cdot\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{s})x^{i-j}\right)A^{s-i}[\beta]\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}-|\beta|}\cdot\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{s})x^{i-j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{s-i}(k)A_{k}\right)[\beta]\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}-|\beta|}\cdot\det\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{s-i}(k)\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{s})x^{i-j}\right)A_{k}[\beta]\right).

Denote by
\delta_{k,\ell}
Kronecker’s delta.
For a subset
\beta
with
|\beta|=1
, we have
A_{k}[\beta]=\delta_{k,0}
.
For subsets
\beta
such that
|\beta|=2
,
we have
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in[d]
.
Furthermore

A_{k}[\beta]=\delta_{k,\ell}(J_{2}-I_{2})
, whence the conclusions follow.
∎

3.1
together with Lemma

to obtain the following corollary.

.

-matrix is the Bose-Mesner algebra of

3.2
Skew-Bush type Hadamard matrices and group divisible designs

Next, we give an example of PBIBD on a
2
-class association scheme from a skew-Bush type Hadamard matrix.

A Hadamard matrix of order
n^{2}
is said to be
skew-Bush type
if
H
is of block form
H=(H_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
, where each
H_{ij}
is an
n\times n
matrix, such that
H_{ii}=J
for any
i
and
H_{ij}J=JH_{ij}=O
,
H_{ij}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}=-H_{ji}
for any
distinct
i,j
.
Note that
H+H^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}=2I_{n}\otimes J_{n}
,
HJ=JH=nJ
and
H(I_{n}\otimes J_{n})=(I_{n}\otimes J_{n})H=nI_{n}\otimes J_{n}
.
Define the
\{0,1\}
-matrix
A
by
A=(J-H)/2
.
Note that
A
is the adjacency matrix of a tournament, see
[
33
]
.
Moreover,
A
satisfies the equations

AJ=JA=\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}J
,
A(I_{n}\otimes J_{n})=(I_{n}\otimes J_{n})A=\frac{n}{2}(J-I_{n}\otimes J_{n})
, and

\displaystyle A^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(J^{2}-JH-HJ+H^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}((n^{2}-2n)J-n^{2}I+2nI_{n}\otimes J_{n}).

(1)

It follows that the eigenvalues of
A
are

\frac{n(n-1)}{2},-\frac{n}{2},\frac{n\sqrt{-1}}{2},-\frac{n\sqrt{-1}}{2}
with multiplicities
1,n-1,\frac{n^{2}-n}{2},\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}
, respectively.
Let

\displaystyle R_{0}

\displaystyle=\{(x,x)\;:\;x\in[n^{2}]\};

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in[n^{2}]\times[n^{2}]\;:\;\lfloor(x-1)/n\rfloor=\lfloor(y-1)/n\rfloor\};\text{ and }

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\;:\;\lfloor(x-1)/n\rfloor\neq\lfloor(y-1)/n\rfloor\}.

The pair
\mathcal{X}_{1}:=([n^{2}],\{R_{0},R_{1},R_{2}\})
is a symmetric association scheme.
PBIBDs on this scheme are known as
group divisible designs

[
6
,
38
]
.
As before, define
\tilde{R}_{i}=\{\{x,y\}\;:\;(x,y)\in R_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.

In this case, the matrix
A
does not belong to the Bose-Mesner algebra of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
Hence, we cannot merely apply Corollary
3.3
.
To get around this, we check that assumption (2) of Theorem
3.1
holds in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
H
be a skew-Bush type Hadamard matrix of order
n^{2}
and
A=(J-H)/2
.
Then

(1)

\left|\left\{\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])\;:\;\alpha\in\binom{[n^{2}]}{1}\right\}\right|=1
.

(2)

for each
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have
\left|\left\{\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])\;:\;\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{i}\right\}\right|=1
.

Proof.

As shown above, the characteristic polynomial of
A
is
\det(xI-A)=\prod_{i=1}^{4}(x-\theta_{i})^{m_{i}}
where
\theta_{1}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2},\theta_{2}=-\frac{n}{2},\theta_{3}=\frac{n\sqrt{-1}}{2},\theta_{4}=-\frac{n\sqrt{-1}}{2}
and
m_{1}=1,m_{2}=n-1,m_{3}=\frac{n^{2}-n}{2},m_{4}=\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}
.
By Lemma
1.4
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])}{\det(xI-A)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\det\left(A^{3}[\alpha]+(x-e_{1})A^{2}[\alpha]+(x^{2}-e_{1}x+e_{2})A[\alpha]+(x^{3}-e_{1}x^{2}+e_{2}x-e_{3})I\right)}{\prod_{i=1}^{4}(x-\theta_{i})^{|\alpha|-m_{i}}},

where
e_{j}=e_{j}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{4})
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Here, using (
1
) and the fact that

\displaystyle A^{3}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{n^{2}(n^{2}-3n+4)}{2}J-n^{2}A-n^{2}I_{n}\otimes J_{n}\right),

we find that

\displaystyle\ A^{3}[\alpha]

\displaystyle+(x-e_{1})A^{2}[\alpha]+(x^{2}-e_{1}x+e_{2})A[\alpha]+(x^{3}-e_{1}x^{2}+e_{2}x-e_{3})I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n(2(n-2)x+n^{2})}{8}J+\frac{n(2x-n^{2}+n)}{4}\left(I_{n}\otimes J_{n}\right)[\alpha]+\left(x^{2}+\frac{2n-n^{2}}{2}x+\frac{n^{2}-n^{3}}{4}\right)A[\alpha]

\displaystyle+\left(x^{3}+(n-\frac{n^{2}}{2})x^{2}+\frac{n^{2}-n^{3}}{4}x\right)I.

Since

\displaystyle A[\alpha]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }|\alpha|=1,\\
O_{2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{1},\\
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\text{ or }\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{2},\end{cases}\qquad(I_{n}\otimes J_{n})[\alpha]=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }|\alpha|=1,\\
J_{2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{1},\\
I_{2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{2},\end{cases}

the conclusion follows.
∎

Now we are ready to apply Theorem
3.1
to
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
A
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
H
be a skew-Bush type Hadamard matrix of order
n^{2}
.
Suppose
A=(J-H)/2
,
k\in\{3,4\}
, and

.
Then
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(n^{2},k,a)
is a PBIBD
(n^{2},k;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
on
\mathcal{X}_{1}
, where

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\begin{cases}\left(n^{2}-2,\frac{(3n-4)(n+2)}{4}\right)&\text{ if }(k,a)=(3,0),\\
\left(0,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\right)&\text{ if }(k,a)=(3,1),\\
\left(\frac{n^{2}}{16},\frac{n^{2}(3n^{2}-10n+12)}{16}\right)&\text{ if }(k,a)=(4,-1),\\
\left(\frac{(n-2)(n+2)(7n^{2}-12)}{16},\frac{(n-2)(55n^{3}+20n^{2}-12n-24)}{16}\right)&\text{ if }(k,a)=(4,0).\end{cases}

Proof.

As shown in Table
9
, we have
|D_{A}(k)|=2
.
By Lemma
3.6
, assumption (2) in Theorem
3.1
is also satisfied.
Hence, we can apply Theorem
3.1
to deduce that the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(n^{2},k,a)

.
By expanding

in the proof of Lemma

and substituting their coefficients to

in Theorem

,
the parameters
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

∎

3.3
Extending to three distinct
k\times k
principal minors

The examples in the subsequent subsections do not satisfy the assumptions Theorem
3.1
.
To make our method more flexible, we extend Theorem
3.1
, by allowing the case of a complex matrix
A
satisfying
|D_{A}(k)|=3
for some
k
.
As above, for a binary relation
R_{i}
, we define
\tilde{R}_{i}=\{\{x,y\}\;:\;(x,y)\in R_{i}\}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=([v],\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
be a symmetric association scheme.
Let
k
be a positive integer, and
A
be a
v\times v
complex matrix such that

(1)

D_{A}(k)=\{a,b,c\}
, where
|D_{A}(k)|=3
,

(2)

C_{A}\left(\binom{[v]}{i},k\right)=\{c_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{0,1\}
and
C_{A}(\tilde{R}_{j},k)=\{c_{2}(j)\}
for each
j\in[d]
, and

(3)

the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,c)
is a PBIBD
(v,k;\eta_{2}(1),\dots,\eta_{2}(d))
on
\mathcal{X}
.

Then the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,a)

is a PBIBD
(v,k;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
on
\mathcal{X}
where, for each
j\in[d]
, we have

\lambda_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a-b}\left(c_{0}-2c_{1}+c_{2}(j)\right)-\frac{b}{a-b}\binom{v-2}{k-2}-\frac{c-b}{a-b}\eta_{2}(j).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{B}_{A}(v,k,a)
,
\eta_{0}=|\mathfrak{B}_{A}(v,k,c)|
, and
\eta_{1}=\left|\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{B}_{A}(v,k,c)\;:\;1\in\alpha\right\}\right|
.
First we claim that, for each
i\in\{0,1\}
, there exists
\mu_{i}
such that
\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\mu_{i}
for each
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
.
For
x\in[v]
, denote by
\nu_{1}
the number of hyperedges of
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,c)
that do not contain
x
.
For
\{x,y\}\in\tilde{R}_{i}
, denote by
\nu_{2}(i)
the number of hyperedges of
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,c)
that do not contain
\{x,y\}
.
Using a straightforward inclusion-exclusion argument, we can write
\nu_{1}=v-\eta_{1}
and
\nu_{2}(i)=v-2\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}(i)
for each
i\in[d]
.
Also, define
\nu_{0}=\eta_{0}
.
By Corollary
1.7

together with assumptions (1) and (3), for each
i\in\{0,1\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=c_{A}(\beta,k)=(-1)^{k}\left(a\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)+b\left(\binom{v-i}{k}-\nu_{i}-\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)\right)+c\nu_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\left((a-b)\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)+b\binom{v-i}{k}+(c-b)\nu_{i}\right)\text{ for }\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}.

from which our claim follows.
Next, for any
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{2}
, there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
such that
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}
.
Furthermore, we claim that, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, there exists
\mu_{2}(j)
such that
\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\mu_{2}(j)
for each
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}
.
Again, by Corollary
1.7

together with assumption (1) and (3), for each
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=c_{A}(\beta,k)=(-1)^{k}\left(a\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)+b\left(\binom{v-2}{k}-\nu_{2}(j)-\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)\right)+c\nu_{2}(j)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\left((a-b)\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)+b\binom{v-2}{k}+(c-b)\nu_{2}(j)\right)\text{ for }\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j},

from which the claim follows.
Hence, for each
i\in\{0,1\}
, we can write
\mu_{i}=\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
for some
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
and
\mu_{2}(j)=\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
for some
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}
.

By Lemma
2.1
,

\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma\subset\beta}(-1)^{|\gamma|}\mu_{\mathfrak{B}}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}&\text{ if }|\beta|=0,\\
\mu_{0}-\mu_{1}&\text{ if }|\beta|=1,\\
\mu_{0}-2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}(j)&\text{ if }|\beta|=2\text{ and }\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}.\end{cases}

Thus,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{B}}(\beta)
does not depend on
\beta
for
\beta\in\binom{[v]}{i}
when
i\in\{0,1\}
and depends only on
j
if
|\beta|=2
and
\beta\in\tilde{R}_{j}
.
Hence, the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(v,k,a)

is a PBIBD
(v,k;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
on
\mathcal{X}
.

Finally, we establish the expressions for the parameters
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
.
Since

\displaystyle\mu_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-b}\left((-1)^{k}c_{i}-b\binom{v-i}{k}-(c-b)\nu_{i}\right)\quad\text{for $i\in\{0,1\}$},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-b}\left((-1)^{k}c_{2}(j)-b\binom{v-2}{k}-(c-b)\nu_{2}(j)\right)\quad\text{for $j\in\{1,\dots,d\}$},

the parameters
\lambda_{j}
are determined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}-2\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a-b}\left(c_{0}-2c_{1}+c_{2}(j)\right)-\frac{b}{a-b}\left(\binom{v}{k}-2\binom{v-1}{k}+\binom{v-2}{k}\right)-\frac{c-b}{a-b}(\nu_{0}-2\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}(j))

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a-b}\left(c_{0}-2c_{1}+c_{2}(j)\right)-\frac{b}{a-b}\binom{v-2}{k-2}-\frac{c-b}{a-b}\eta_{2}(j).\qed

In the following two subsections, we apply Theorem
3.8
to two different infinite families of matrices to obtain new constructions of PBIBDs.

3.4
Signed hypercubes

Let
d
be a positive integer. Define the matrix
S_{d}
inductively as

S_{d+1}:=\begin{bmatrix}S_{d}&I\\
I&-S_{d}\end{bmatrix},

where
S_{0}
is the zero-matrix
O_{1}
of order
1
.
By the definition of
S_{d}
, it is clear that
S_{d}^{2}=dI
.
Since the trace of
S_{d}
is
0
, we find that
\det(xI-S_{d})=(x^{2}-d)^{2^{d-1}}
.
Furthermore, it is straightforward to verify that
D_{S_{d}}(4)=\{0,1,4\}
.

Define
E_{d}=\{\{x,y\}\in[2^{d}]\times[2^{d}]\;:\;(S_{d})_{xy}=\pm 1\}
.
The graph
Q_{d}:=([2^{d}],E_{d})
is called a hypercube.
As suggested by the title of this subsection, one can think of the matrix
S_{d}
as a
signed
adjacency matrix of a hypercube.
The matrix
S_{d}
was used in the recent celebrated solution to the so-called
sensitivity conjecture

[
28
]
.
Let
d(x,y)
denote the distance from the vertex
x
to the vertex
y
in the graph
Q_{d}
.
For each
i\in\{0,\dots,d\}
, define the relation
R_{i}
by

\displaystyle R_{i}:=\{(x,y)\in[2^{d}]\times[2^{d}]\;:\;d(x,y)=i\}.

Define
\mathcal{H}(d):=([2^{d}],\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d})
.
It is well-known that
\mathcal{H}(d)
is a symmetric association scheme called the
binary Hamming scheme
.
We now show that
\mathfrak{H}_{S_{d}}(2^{d},4,4)
is a PBIBD on
\mathcal{H}(d)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
d
be a positive integer with
d\geqslant 2
and set
A=S_{d}
.
The hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(2^{d},4,4)
is a PBIBD
(2^{d},4;d-1,1,0,\dots,0)
on
\mathcal{H}(d)
.

Proof.

Observe that
\det(S_{d}[\alpha])=4
for
\alpha\in\binom{[2^{d}]}{4}
if and only if
S_{d}[\alpha]=P^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}D\left((J_{2}-I_{2})\otimes\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)DP
for some permutation matrix
P
and diagonal matrix
D
whose diagonal entries are
\pm 1
.
By the definition of
S_{d}
and
Q_{d}
, it follows that each subset
\alpha\in\binom{[2^{d}]}{4}
with
\det(S_{d}[\alpha])=4
corresponds to an induced cycle of length
4
in
Q_{d}
.

The remainder of the proof follows from properties of the hypercube
Q_{d}
.
We refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 9.2]
for the details and merely sketch the key takeaways below.
One can prove by induction that each vertex belongs to precisely
\binom{d}{2}
induced cycles of
Q_{d}
of length
4
.
It follows that

3.5
Balanced generalised weighing matrices

This subsection closely resembles Section
3.4
.
The difference is that, instead of the binary Hamming scheme

First, we need to give a couple of definitions.
The
incidence matrix

\{0,1\}
-matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{B}

if and only if

2
-design

The incidence matrix

of a symmetric
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)

for
\lambda=\frac{k(k-1)}{v-1}
.
A

of

and

, denoted BGW

\{0,\pm 1\}
-matrix

of order

with

(v,k,\lambda)

[
29

30

Let

be a BGW
(v,k,\lambda)

By the definition of

, it is clear that the matrix

.
Since the trace of

.
Furthermore, it is straightforward to verify that

-class association scheme

Define

\displaystyle A_{0}=I_{2v},\quad A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}O&N\\

Next, we will show that

For each

5
Regular pairwise balanced designs

Let
v
and
\lambda
be positive integers, and let
K
be a set of positive integers.
A
pairwise balanced design
, denoted PBD
(v,K,\lambda)
, is a
v
-vertex hypergraph
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{B})
, where the cardinality of each hyperedge belongs to
K
and every
2
-subset of
\mathfrak{X}
is contained in exactly
\lambda
hyperedges of
\mathfrak{B}

[
45
, Sections 7, 8]
.
A PBD is called
regular
if it is a
1
-design.
Note that a regular PBD
(v,\{k\},\lambda)
is a
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design.
One can therefore think of a regular pairwise balanced design as generalisation of a
2
-design.

5.1
Pairwise balanced designs from Hadamard matrices

In this section, we provide an infinite family of regular pairwise balanced designs that can be obtained from a Hadamard matrix.

Let
H
be a Hadamard matrix of order
v
, and define
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&H\\
H^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
Clearly, the matrix
A
satisfies
A^{2}-2A-(v-1)I=O
.
It follows that the characteristic polynomial
\det(xI-A)=(x^{2}-2x-v+1)^{v}
.
Furthermore, it is straightforward to check that
D_{A}(3)=\{-1,1\}
,
D_{A}(4)=\{-3,-2,1\}
.

Let
A_{0}=I_{2v}
,
A_{1}=I_{2}\otimes(J_{v}-I_{v})
, and
A_{2}=(J_{2}-I_{2})\otimes J_{v}
.
For each
i\in\{0,1,2\}
define
R_{i}
by
(x,y)\in R_{i}
if and only if the
(x,y)
entry of
A_{i}
is
1
.
In the same spirit as in Section
3.4
and Section
3.5
, we will show that
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(2v,4,-3)
is a PBIBD on
\mathcal{X}_{3}
.
The proof of Lemma
5.1
below, is similar to that of Lemma
3.11
and we invite the reader to modify the proof accordingly.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
H
be a Hadamard matrix of order
v
and
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&H\\
H^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
The hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(2v,4,-3)
is a PBIBD
(2v,4;\frac{v(v-2)}{4},\frac{(v-1)(v-2)}{2})
on
\mathcal{X}_{3}
.

Define
\tilde{R}_{i}=\{\{x,y\}\;:\;(x,y)\in R_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.
We now construct a regular PBD from a Hadamard matrix.
The method is based on constructions of PBIBDs in Section
3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
H
be a Hadamard matrix of order
v
and
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&H\\
H^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\intercal$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\intercal$}}}&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
Then the hypergraph
([2v],\mathfrak{B}_{A}(2v,3,-1)\cup\mathfrak{B}_{A}(2v,4,-2))
is a regular PBD
(2v,\{3,4\},v^{2}-v)
.

Proof.

As mentioned above,
D_{A}(3)=\{-1,1\}
and
D_{A}(4)=\{-3,-2,1\}
.
Hence, assumption (1) in Theorem
3.1
is satisfied for
k=3
and assumption (1) in Theorem
3.8
is satisfied for
k=4
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
5.1
, assumption (3) in Theorem
3.8
is satisfied for
c=-3
.

It remains to verify assumption (2) in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.8
.
By Corollary
1.5
, we can write
\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])=(x^{2}-2x-v+1)^{v-|\alpha|}\det(A[\alpha]+xI)
.
Next, observe that if
|\alpha|=1
then
A[\alpha]=I_{1}
.
Moreover, if
|\alpha|=2
then
A[\alpha]=I_{2}
if
\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{1}
and
A[\alpha]=I_{2}\pm(J_{2}-I_{2})
otherwise.
Thus,

\displaystyle\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(x+1)(x^{2}-2x-v+1)^{v-1}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=1,\\
(x+1)^{2}(x^{2}-2x-v+1)^{v-2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{1},\\
(x+2)(x-1)(x^{2}-2x-v+1)^{v-2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2\text{ and }\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{2}.\end{cases}

Therefore,
\det(xI-A[\overline{\alpha}])
does not depend on the choice of
\alpha
if
|\alpha|=1
and depends only on
j
where
\alpha\in\tilde{R}_{j}
if
|\alpha|=2
.

By Theorem
3.1
,
the hypergraph
\mathfrak{H}_{A}(2v,3,-1)
is a PBIBD
(2v,3;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
on
\mathcal{X}_{3}
with

\lambda_{1}=v
and
\lambda_{2}=2(v-1)
.
By Theorem
3.8

\mathfrak{H}_{A}(2v,4,-2)
is a PBIBD
(2v,4;\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{2})
on
\mathcal{X}_{3}
with

\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=v(v-2)
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}=(v-1)(v-2)
.

Since

\lambda_{1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda^{\prime}_{2}=v^{2}-v
,
the pair
([2v],\mathfrak{B}_{A}(2v,3,-1)\cup\mathfrak{B}_{A}(2v,4,-2))
is a regular PBD
(2v,\{3,4\},v^{2}-v)
.
∎

A Short Proof of a Conjecture of Erdős Proved by Moreira, Richter and Robertson
Bernard Host

Abstract
We give a short proof of a sumset conjecture of Erdős, recently proved by Moreira, Richter and Robertson: every subset of the integers of positive density contains the sum of two infinite sets. The proof is written in the framework of classical ergodic theory.

Introduction

In this note we give a short proof of a result of Moreira, Richter and Robertson, conjectured by Erdős:

Theorem 1

(
[
MRR
]
)
.

If
A\subset{\mathbb{N}}
has positive density then there exist infinite subsets
B
and
C
of
{\mathbb{N}}
with
A\supset B+C
.

Here and below, by the
density
of a subset
A
of
{\mathbb{N}}
we mean its upper uniform density
{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(A)

defined by

{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(A):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{M\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|A\cap[M,M+N)|}{N}.

The proof extends to the case of countable abelian groups up to notational changes.
We believe that a generalization of the method to countable amenable groups is possible, by using the maximal isometric factor in place of the Kronecker factor.

The combinatorial part of our prof is closely related to the corresponding part of
[
MRR
]
: Theorem
3
is the dynamical counterpart of
[
MRR
, Theorem 2.2]
. But the whole proof of Theorem
1

presented here is very different of
[
MRR
]
, as it is written inside the framework of classical ergodic theory.

In
[
MRR
]
the authors ask whether any set of positive density contains the sum of three infinite sets. A possible strategy would be to show that it contains the sum of a set of positive density and an infinite set, and then to use Theorem
1
. However, this method does not work:

Proposition 2
.

There exists a set of positive density not containing any sum of a set of positive density and an infinite set.

Section
2
contains some preliminaries. Theorem
1
is proved in Section
3
and Proposition
2
in Section
4
.

2
Preliminaries

In this paper we use the convention that
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.

2.1
Følner sequences

We make a constant use of Følner sequences but, since we are dealing only with subsets of the integers, we never need general Følner sequences and we can assume that a Følner sequence is a sequence
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=(\Phi_{N})_{N\geq 1}
of intervals of
{\mathbb{N}}
whose lengths tend to
\infty
. If
A\subset{\mathbb{N}}
we write

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\Phi_{N}]}{|\Phi_{N}|};

\displaystyle d_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\Phi_{N}]}{|\Phi_{N}|}\text{ if this limit exists.}

Therefore,

{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(A)=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(A)

where the supremum is taken for all Følner sequences
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
such that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(A)
exists, and the supremum is attained.

2.2
Topological dynamical systems

A topological dynamical system
(X,T)
is a compact metric space endowed with a map
T\colon X\to X
, continuous and onto. We write
d_{X}
for the distance on
X
.

Let
x_{0}\in X
,
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
a Følner sequence and
\mu
a probability measure on
X
. We say that
x_{0}
is
generic for
\mu
along
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}
if

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\delta_{T^{n}x_{0}}\to\mu\text{ weakly* as }N\to\infty

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac mass at
x
. This is equivalent to

\text{for every }f\in{\mathcal{C}}(X),\quad\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{n}x_{0})\to\int f\,d\mu\text{ as }N\to\infty.

In this case,
\mu
is supported on the closed orbit of
x_{0}
under
T
and is invariant under
T
. For
U\subset X
we have

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\bigl(\{(n\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in U\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\mu(U)\text{ if $U$ is open;}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\bigl(\{(n\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in U\}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu(U)\text{ if $U$ is clopen (that is, open and closed).}

We remark that for every
x_{0}\in X
, every Følner sequence admits a subsequence along which
x_{0}
is generic for some measure
\mu
.

We say that
x_{0}
is generic for
\mu
without mention of a Følner sequence if it is generic for the usual Følner sequence
\bigl([0,N)\bigr)_{N\geq 1}
.

2.3
The key combinatorial result

The next theorem is the dynamical counterpart of
[
MRR
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system,
x_{0}\in X
,
E
a clopen subset of
X
and

A=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in E\}.

Let
X\times X
be endowed with the transformation
T\times T
.
Let
x_{1}\in X
and let
\nu
be a measure on
X\times X
, for which the point
(x_{0},x_{1})
is generic along some Følner sequence
{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}=(\Phi_{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
(m_{i})
of integers tending to
\infty
with

T^{m_{i}}x_{0}\to x_{1}\ \text{ and }\ \nu(T^{-m_{i}}E\times E)\geq\varepsilon\text{ for every }i.

(1)

Then there exist infinite subsets
B
,
C
of
{\mathbb{N}}
with
A\supset B+C
.

This theorem can be deduced from
[
MRR
, Proposition 2.5]
but we give a proof for completeness.
The construction gives that the set
B
ban be taken as included in any given infinite subset of
\{m_{i}\colon i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
; the set
C
can be taken as included in any infinite given subset of

\{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{\ell}x_{1}\in E\}
.

Proof.

We use the following result of Bergelson:

Lemma 4

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\lambda)
be a probability space,
\varepsilon>0
, and
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
a sequence in
{\mathcal{Y}}
with
\lambda(B_{n})\geq\varepsilon
for every
n
. Then this sequence admits a subsequence
(B_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
such that

\text{ for every }k\geq 1,\quad\lambda\Bigl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}B_{n_{j}}\Bigr)>0.

Applying this lemma to the probability
\nu
on
X\times X
and to the sets
T^{-m_{i}}E\times E
, we obtain
that the sequence
(m_{i})_{i\geq 1}
admits a subsequence, still written
(m_{i})
, such that

\text{for every }r\geq 1,\quad\nu\Bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}(T^{-m_{i}}E\times E)\Bigr)>0.

(2)

We write

L=\{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{\ell}x_{1}\in E\}.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
(T\times T)^{n}(x_{0},x_{1})\in T^{-m_{i}}E\times E
if and only if
n\in(A-m_{i})\cap L
. By definition of
\nu
and (
2
),

\text{for every }r\geq 1,\quad{\mathrm{d}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}\Bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}(A-m_{i})\cap L\Bigr)>0.

(3)

and in particular this set is infinite.

We build by induction strictly increasing sequences
(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(c_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with

b_{n}\in\{m_{i}\colon i\geq 1\}\text{ and }c_{n}\in L\text{ for every }n;\ b_{i}+c_{j}\in A\text{ for all }i,j\geq 1.

Let
n\geq 0
. If
n=0
we chose an arbitrary
c_{1}\in L
. Assume that
n\geq 1
and that
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
are built with
b_{i}+c_{j}\in A
for all
i,j\leq n
. By (
3
) applied with
r=b_{n}
we can chose
c_{n+1}>c_{n}
with

c_{n+1}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(A-b_{i})\cap L

and we have
b_{i}+c_{n+1}\in A
for
i\leq n
.

In both cases, for
j\leq n+1
we have
c_{j}\in L
that is
T^{c_{j}}x_{1}\in E
. Since
E
is open and since
T^{m_{i}}x_{0}\to x_{1}
, for every sufficiently large
i
we have

T^{m_{i}+c_{j}}x_{0}\in E
for every
j\leq n+1
, that is
m_{i}+c_{j}\in A
. We chose
i
with this property and moreover
m_{i}>b_{n}
if
n\geq 1
, and we set
b_{n+1}=m_{i}
.
We have
b_{n+1}+c_{j}\in A
for
1\leq j\leq n+1
and we are done.
∎

3.2
Construction of an ergodic measure

Let

be the ergodic decomposition of

Since

By the pointwise ergodic theorem, the measure

admits a generic point

Since the orbit of

\text{for every }N\text{ and }0\leq n<N,\ d_{X}(T^{m_{N}+n}x_{0},T^{n}x_{1})<1/N.

Substituting

(v)

3.3
Using the Kronecker factor

We recall that the Kronecker factor
(Z,m_{Z},S)
of an ergodic system
(X,\mu,T)
is a compact abelian group
Z
endowed with its Haar measure
m_{Z}
, with a translation

and with a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Z

\text{for all }\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mu)\text{ with }{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}(\psi\mid Z)=0\\
\text{for every }\varepsilon>0,\quad{\mathrm{d}}^{*}\Bigl(\Bigl\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\Bigl|\int T^{n}\psi\cdot\phi\,d\mu\Bigr|\geq\varepsilon\Bigr\}\Bigr)=0.

(4)

The transformation

\text{for every }z\in Z,\quad Sz=z+\alpha

(5)

where
\alpha
is a fixed element of

.

It should be noticed that, even when

Let

under

\pi\colon X\to Z

Let

Let

We apply this Proposition to the system

Choosing a point

We use now the fact that
\beta\in V
, the definition of this set and the bound
\int\phi^{2}\,dm_{Z}\geq\delta^{2}
and we obtain that the set

\Lambda=\big\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon n\alpha\in V\text{ and }\nu(T^{-n}E\times E)\leq\delta/4\bigr\}

satisfies

{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(\Lambda)=0.

3.6
Conclusion

We check now that the point
x_{1}
and the measure
\nu
satisfy all the hypothesis needed to apply
Theorem
3
. It remains only to build a good sequence
(m_{i})
.

For every integer
i>0
the set

X_{3}\cap\{x\in X\colon d_{X}(x,x_{1})<1/i\}

is an open neighborhood of
x_{1}
, and since
x_{1}
belongs to the topological support
X_{2}
of
\mu
, the measure of this set is
>0
and thus the set

F_{i}=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in X_{3}\text{ and }d_{X}(T^{n}x_{0},x_{1})<1/i\}

satisfies

\overline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(F_{i})>0
. Since
\overline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(\Lambda)=0
,
\overline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}(F_{i}\setminus\Lambda)>0
and in particular
F_{i}\setminus\Lambda
is infinite. We thus can chose a sequence of integers
(m_{i})
, tending to
\infty
, with

m_{i}\in F_{i}\setminus\Lambda
for every
i
. We have that

T^{m_{i}}x_{0}\to x_{1}\text{ as }i\to\infty

and, for every
i
,

\displaystyle T^{m_{i}}x_{0}\in X_{3}\text{ and thus }m_{i}\alpha\in V;

\displaystyle m_{i}\notin\Lambda\text{ and thus }\nu(T^{-m_{i}}E\times E)>\delta^{2}/4.

All the hypothesis of Theorem
3
are satisfied with
\varepsilon=\delta^{2}/4
and we are done.
∎

4
Proof of Proposition
2

Let
(X,T)
be an uniquely ergodic topological dynamical system with invariant measure
\mu
and assume that the measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
is mixing. Let
U\subset X
be an open subset with
0<\mu(U)\leq\mu(\overline{U})<1
,
x_{0}\in X
and

A=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in U\}.

Since
\mu(U)>0
and by unique ergodicity,
{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(A)>0
. We show that it does not contain a sum
B+C
with
{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(B)>0
and
C
infinite.

Assume by contradiction that
B
and
C
exist withe these properties and let

K=\overline{\{T^{n}x_{0}\colon n\in B\}}.

By unique ergodicity,

\mu(K)\geq{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon T^{n}x_{0}\in K\})\geq{\mathrm{d}}^{*}(B)>0.

On the other hand, for every
c\in C
we have
T^{c}\{T^{b}x_{0}\colon b\in B\}\subset U
and thus

\text{for every }c\in C,\quad T^{c}K\subset\overline{U}.

Since
(X,\mu,T)
is mixing and since
K
and
X\setminus\overline{U})
have positive measure, for every sufficiently large
n
we have

\mu(T^{n}K\cap(X\setminus\overline{U}))>0
and thus
T^{n}K\not\subset\overline{U}
. We have a contradiction. ∎

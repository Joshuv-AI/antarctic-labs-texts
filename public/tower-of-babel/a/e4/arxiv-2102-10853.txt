Geometry of the space of sections of twistor spaces with circle action
Florian Beck, Indranil Biswas, Sebastian Heller, Markus Röser

Abstract
. We study the holomorphic symplectic geometry of (the smooth locus of) the space of holomorphic sections of a twistor space with rotating circle action. The twistor space carries a line bundle with meromorphic connection constructed by Hitchin. We give an interpretation of Hitchin’s meromorphic connection in the context of the Atiyah–Ward transform of the corresponding hyperholomorphic line bundle. It is shown that the residue of the meromorphic connection serves as a moment map for the induced circle action, and furthermore its critical points are studied. Particular emphasis is given to the example of Deligne–Hitchin moduli spaces.

53C26, 53C28, 53C43, 14H60, 14H70

Introduction

Given a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus at least two, and a complex semisimple Lie group
G
, the moduli
space of
G
-Higgs bundles on
\Sigma
has a hyperKähler structure
[
28
]
. The corresponding twistor
space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)
is known as the Deligne–Hitchin moduli spaces
[
46
]
. These spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)

have been the topic of many papers in recent years; see for example
[
12
,
34
,
35
,
17
,
18
]
and
[
5
,
6
,
4
,
26
,
27
]
and references therein.

In
[
6
]
the last three
authors initiated a detailed investigation of the space
\mathcal{S}
of holomorphic
sections of the natural holomorphic projection
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. Our aim here is
to go deeper into the study of
\mathcal{S}
. There are two distinct sources
of motivation for doing this: One is the integrable systems approach to the theory of harmonic maps
from Riemann surfaces into symmetric spaces, the second one being the hyperKähler geometry.

We start by explaining the latter source of motivation. Let
K
be a compact real form of a complex semisimple Lie group

G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Hitchin’s self-duality equations on
\Sigma
are given by

(1.1)

F^{\nabla}+[\Phi\wedge\Phi^{*}]\,\,=\,\,0\,\,=\,\,\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}\Phi

[
28
]
.
Here
\nabla
is a
K
-connection and
\Phi\,\in\,\Omega^{1,0}(\Sigma,\,\mathfrak{g})
, which is called a Higgs
field, while
\Phi\,\longmapsto\,\Phi^{*}
is given by the negative of the Cartan involution
on
\mathfrak{g}
associated with the given real form

K\,\subset\,G
. Every solution of (
1.1
) gives rise to a family of flat
G
-connections
on
\Sigma
parametrized by
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\,=\,{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}

(1.2)

\nabla^{\lambda}\,=\,\nabla+\lambda^{-1}\Phi+\lambda\Phi^{*}

that satisfies a certain reality condition which is determined by the symmetric pair
(G,\,K)
.
The nonabelian Hodge correspondence
[
48
,
28
,
14
,
13
]
allows us to invert
this process, meaning any (reductive) flat
G
-connection on
\Sigma
actually
occurs in a suitable
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-family of flat
G
-connections
given by a solution of the self-duality equations in (
1.1
).

Recall from
[
46
]
that the Deligne–Hitchin moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)
is obtained by gluing the
two Hodge moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\Sigma,\,G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\overline{\Sigma},\,G)
of
\lambda
-connections via the Riemann
Hilbert correspondence, where
\overline{\Sigma}
is the conjugate of

\Sigma
(see §
4
for details). It enters the picture via the interpretation
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-family of flat
G
-connections associated with a solution of (
1.1
) as a family of

\lambda
-connections, which in fact extends to all of
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. From this point of view, we may think of the above

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-family as a holomorphic section of the fibration
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
satisfying an
appropriate reality condition. This observation fits naturally into the twistor theory of hyperKähler
manifolds, as we shall explain next.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
of solutions of (
1.1
) is a (typically singular) hyperKähler manifold which
comes equipped with a certain rotating circle action given by
(\nabla,\,\Phi)\,\longmapsto\,(\nabla,\,e^{i\theta}\Phi)
for all
\theta\,\in\,\mathbb{R}
. Here the word

rotating
means that the circle action is isometric, it preserves one of the three Kähler forms
while rotating
the other two (see §
3.1
and §
4.1
for details). The Deligne–Hitchin moduli
space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)
is the twistor space associated with
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
. The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-family of flat
connections on
\Sigma
associated with a solution to the self-duality equations
in (
1.1
) can then be interpreted as the twistor line corresponding to the point in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)

represented by
(\nabla,\,\Phi)
in (
1.1
). In this way, we can view
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
in a natural
way as a subset of the space
\mathcal{S}
of holomorphic sections of the twistor family
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.

In fact,
\mathcal{S}
may be interpreted as a natural complexification of the real analytic hyperKähler manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
. More generally, if
Z
is the twistor space of a hyperKähler manifold
M
, then the twistor construction of
hyperKähler metrics produces a hyperKähler metric on the space
M^{\prime}
of all real holomorphic sections (with
appropriate normal bundle) of the fibration
Z\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. From this point of view, it is a natural question
whether
M\,=\,M^{\prime}

[
46
]
. In the context of the self-duality equations, this question
translates into whether every
real holomorphic section of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
is actually obtained from a solution to
the self-duality
equations. In
[
6
]
an answer to this question was given in the case of
G\,=\,{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
by showing that in
general
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
is strictly contained in the space of real sections. Furthermore, in
[
6
]
the real
holomorphic sections belonging to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,G)
were characterized in terms of a certain
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-valued
invariant that can be associated with any real section.

HyperKähler manifolds with rotating circle action are an active field of research in hyperKähler geometry,
especially their connection with the well-known hyperKähler/quaternion Kähler correspondence is extensively
investigated

[
25
,
1
,
31
,
32
]
. Such manifolds are never compact, a main source of examples is the construction of Feix
[
20
]
(and independently Kaledin) of a hyperKähler metric on (a neighbourhood of the zero section in) the cotangent bundle of a Kähler manifold, where the circle action is just the usual action by scalar multiplication in the fibers. Such metrics also arise on many moduli spaces of solutions to gauge theoretic equations (e.g. magnetic monopoles, Higgs bundles). See the papers
[
31
,
32
]
for a discussion of examples of interest to physicists as well as of a purely geometric or Lie theoretic origin. Haydys
[
25
]
has observed the following: If
(M,\,\omega_{I},\,\omega_{J},\,\omega_{K})
is a hyperKähler manifold with rotating circle action such that
\omega_{I}
is integral, then there
exists a complex line bundle
L\,\longrightarrow\,M
with a unitary connection whose curvature is

\omega_{I}+dd^{c}_{I}\mu
, where
\mu
is
the moment map for the
S^{1}
-action with respect to the Kähler form
\omega_{I}
. This unitary connection on
L
is
hyperholomorphic, in the sense that its curvature is of type
(1,\,1)
with respect to every complex structure in the
family of Kähler structures on
M
parametrized by the sphere
S^{2}
. The moment map
facilitates a lift of the
S^{1}
-action on
M
to an
S^{1}
-action on
the total space of the principal
S^{1}
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
associated with
L
. Haydys
has shown that the corresponding
quotient
Q\,=\,P/S^{1}
for this action carries a quaternionic Kähler metric. The natural
S^{1}
-action on the
principal bundle
S^{1}
-bundle
P
descends to an
isometric circle action on
Q
, and
M
can be recovered
from it as a hyperKähler quotient of the Swann bundle of
Q
by
the lift of this isometric circle action.

This is a brief
outline of what is known as the hyperKähler/quaternion Kähler (HK/QK) correspondence.
Hitchin in
[
31
,
32
]
has described the HK/QK correspondence from a
purely twistorial point of view. A natural starting point for him is the observation that, by the Atiyah–Ward
correspondence, the hyperholomorphic line bundle on
M
produces a holomorphic line bundle
L_{Z}
on the twistor
space
Z
associated to
M
. Hitchin has shown how to construct
L_{Z}
directly on the twistor space
Z
and explained how the
circle action determines a distinguished meromorphic connection on
L_{Z}
. This meromorphic connection plays a key
role in the construction, from
Z
, of the twistor space
Z_{Q}
of the associated quaternionic Kähler manifold

Q
in the sense that it determines the contact distribution on
Z_{Q}
. Therefore, in order to make progress towards the
construction of the quaternionic Kähler manifold associated with
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,G)
, it is clearly important to obtain
information on this meromorphic connection.

As mentioned earlier, our other motivation comes from the integrable system approach to harmonic maps from

\Sigma
into symmetric spaces. As is well known, Hitchin’s self-duality equations (
1.1
) can be
interpreted as the gauge-theoretic equations for a (twisted) harmonic map
\Sigma\,\longrightarrow\,G/K

(a so-called
harmonic metric
).

One observes that the twisted harmonic maps into other (pseudo-Riemannian) symmetric spaces of the form
G/G_{{\mathbb{R}}}

and their duals have also analogous gauge-theoretic interpretations and in fact they also give rise to
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-families of flat

G
-connections satisfying different reality conditions; the reality condition actually
depends on the target of the harmonic map.
For example, if we have
G\,=\,{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
then
K\,=\,{\rm SU}(2)
, and the harmonic maps, from

\Sigma
, into the symmetric spaces

{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})/{\rm SU}(2),

{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})/{\rm SU}(1,1),

{\rm SU}(2),

{\rm SU}(1,1)
and also into the hyperbolic disc

{\rm SU}(1,1)/{\rm U}(1)
can all be encoded in
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-families of flat
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
connections on
\Sigma
satisfying appropriate
reality conditions; see for instance
[
44
,
49
,
29
,
15
]
.
Considering these
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-families as families of
\lambda
-connections on
\Sigma
with
\lambda\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
we can —
at least formally — interpret twisted harmonic maps from
\Sigma
into various symmetric spaces associated
with real forms of
G
as holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)
; see
[
6
]
. In this way we may view the
space of holomorphic sections of the fibration
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
as a
master space

for the moduli spaces of twisted harmonic maps from
\Sigma
into various (pseudo-Riemannian) symmetric spaces
associated with the group
G
.

This interaction with the theory of harmonic maps has provided useful guidance and intuition for the
predecessor

[
6
]
and
[
4
]
of this project. For example, the new real holomorphic sections for the

{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-case constructed in
[
6
]
are closely related to twisted harmonic maps
\Sigma\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})/{\rm SU}(1,1)
.

In
[
4
]
it is proved that on the space
\mathcal{S}
of holomorphic sections of the twistor family

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
there exists an interesting
and useful holomorphic functional, called the energy functional, whose
evaluation on a real section coming from a harmonic map is (a constant multiple of) the Dirichlet energy of the
associated harmonic map. This functional is also intimately related to the Willmore energy of certain immersions of

\Sigma
into the 3-sphere. It was then shown that this functional has a natural interpretation from the
hyperKähler point of view. In fact it can be interpreted as a holomorphic extension of the moment map,
associated with the rotating circle action, from the space
M
of twistor lines to the whole of
\mathcal{S}
.
Essentially, it is given by associating to any
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}
the residue of the meromorphic connections along

s
.

In this article, we continue our study of this setup in the context of the twistor space
Z
of a general
hyperKähler manifold
M
with a rotating circle action. The natural geometric structure on the space
\mathcal{S}
of holomorphic sections of
Z\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
, viewed as a complexification of the hyperKähler
manifold
M
, has been elucidated by Jardim and Verbitsky
[
37
,
38
]
. They show that
\mathcal{S}
comes
equipped with a certain family of closed two-forms called a trisymplectic structure. In particular, the
complexifications of the Kähler forms on
M
are contained in this family. We describe in this paper how the
picture gets enriched by the presence of a rotating circle action. It turns out that the energy functional

\mathcal{E}
is a moment map for the circle action on
\mathcal{S}
induced from the circle action on
M
with
respect to a natural holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega_{0}
which is a part of the canonical trisymplectic
structure on
\mathcal{S}
(see Theorem
3.3
). In particular, the critical points of
\mathcal{E}

turn out to be exactly the fixed points of the circle action.

We also explain how to use the Atiyah–Ward transform to obtain a natural holomorphic extension of the line
bundle
L\,\longrightarrow\,M
to
\mathcal{L}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{S}
, and how the meromorphic connection
on
L_{Z}
can be described naturally in terms of
\mathcal{L}
and the data of the circle action (see Theorem

3.14
and §
3.6
). This is implicit in Hitchin’s work
[
31
]
, but we believe our point of view
in terms of the geometry of the space
\mathcal{S}
of holomorphic sections might shed a new light onto his
constructions. Moreover, our results allow us to extend the knowledge about the global structure of
\mathcal{S}
(see
Theorem
3.15
)

We then apply this general framework to the space of holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
. Even
though
\mathcal{S}
is expected to be singular in this case, the general theory tells us that the critical points
of the energy functional are actually closely related to the fixed points of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{S}
. We analyze
these
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant sections in detail, building on
[
12
]
(see Proposition

5.5
). We obtain explicit formulas for the energy of a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant section
(see Proposition
5.12
) and we describe the second variation of the energy at such a
section (see Proposition
5.13
). Moreover, we establish an explicit formula for the degree of the hyperholomorphic
line bundle restricted to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant section (see Proposition
5.16
), from
which we are able to show that there exist sections along which the hyperholomorphic line bundle has non-zero
degree. Using this it can be deduced that
\mathcal{S}
is in general not connected (see Theorem
5.17
).

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
we discuss the twistor fibration
\varpi\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
associated with
a hyperKähler manifold
M
and describe the geometric structure induced on the space
\mathcal{S}
of
holomorphic sections of
\varpi
in a way that is suitable for our purpose. We then go on in §
3
to explain how
the geometry of
\mathcal{S}
is enriched by the presence of a rotating circle action on
M
and hence on
Z
. In
particular, we explain how the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
ties up naturally with the holomorphic symplectic
geometry on
\mathcal{S}
discussed in §
2
and with the meromorphic connection on the
hyperholomorphic line bundle. We have added a large amount of background material in §
2
and §
3
to keep the
paper somewhat self-contained. The particular example of the Deligne–Hitchin moduli space is described in §
4
. In
§
5
the abstract geometric framework developed in the first two sections is illustrated in the context of
the Deligne–Hitchin moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,G)
and the results mentioned above are proved.

2.
Geometry of the space of holomorphic sections of a twistor space

In this section we collect some aspects of the geometry of holomorphic sections of a hyperKähler twistor space
that will be used later. Useful references are
[
33
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
. See also
[
39
]
for
the similar, but different, quaternionic Kähler case.

2.1.
Twistor space

Let
(M,\,g_{0},\,I,\,J,\,K)
be a hyperKähler manifold of complex dimension
2d
,
where
I,\,J,\,K
are almost complex structures and
g_{0}
a Riemannian metric on
M
.
The associated Kähler forms are

\omega_{L}\,=\,g_{0}(L\cdot,\,\cdot)
,
\,L\,\in\,\{I,\,J,\,K\}
.
For convenience, the complex manifolds
(M,\,I)
and

(M,\,-I)
will sometimes be denoted by simply
M
and
\overline{M}
respectively; it will
be ensured that this abuse of notation does not create any confusion.

There is a family of Kähler structures on
M
with complex structures

(2.1)

\{I_{x}\,:=\,x_{1}I+x_{2}J+x_{3}K\,\mid\,\,x\,:=\,(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,x_{3})\,\in\,S^{2}\}

parametrized by the sphere

S^{2}\,:=\,\{(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,x_{3})\,\in\,{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,\mid\,x^{2}_{1}+x^{2}_{2}+x^{2}_{3}\,=\,1\}
.
The twistor space
Z\,=\,Z(M)
of
(M,\,g_{0},\,I,\,J,\,K)
is a complex manifold whose underlying smooth
manifold is
S^{2}\times M
(
[
33
, § 3 (F)]
).
The almost complex structure
I_{Z}
of
Z
at any point
(x,\,m)\,\in\,S^{2}\times M

is

I_{Z}|_{(x,m)}\,=\,(I_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}|_{T_{x}S^{2}})\oplus(I_{x}|_{T_{m}M})\,,

where

I_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
is the standard almost complex structure on
S^{2}\,=\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
and
I_{x}

is the almost complex structure in (
2.1
). Here
we identify
S^{2}
with
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
using the stereographic projection from
(-1,\,0,\,0)
to the plane in

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
spanned by the
x_{2}
axis and the
x_{3}
axis.
In particular,
(1,\,0,\,0)\,\in\,S^{2}
corresponds to
0\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
and
(-1,\,0,\,0)
corresponds to

\infty\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.
Throughout we shall use an affine coordinate
\lambda
on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
, so that
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\,\cong\,{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}

having two coordinate functions
\lambda
and
\lambda^{-1}
.

The hyperKähler structure on
M
is encoded in the following complex-geometric data on
Z
.
The natural projection
S^{2}\times M\,\longrightarrow\,S^{2}
corresponds to a holomorphic submersion

(2.2)

\varpi\,\,\colon\,\,Z\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}

with fibers

(2.3)

\varpi^{-1}(\lambda)\,=:\,Z_{\lambda}\,=\,(M,\,I_{\lambda})\,.

Here
I_{\lambda}
is the complex structure on
M
in (
2.1
) corresponding to
\lambda\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\,\cong\,S^{2}
.
For any complex vector bundle
V
on
Z
and any integer
m
, we use the notation
V(m)\,:=\,V\otimes\varpi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}(m)
.
Let

T_{\varpi}\,=\,T_{\varpi}Z\,:=\,(\ker d\varpi)\,\subset\,TZ

be the relative holomorphic tangent bundle (also called
the vertical tangent bundle) for the projection
\varpi
in (
2.2
).
Then
Z
carries the twisted relative holomorphic symplectic form

\omega\,\in\,H^{0}(Z,\,(\Lambda^{2}T_{\varpi}^{*})(2))
given by

(2.4)

\omega\,=\,\left(\omega_{J}+\mathsf{i}\omega_{K}+2\mathsf{i}\lambda\omega_{I}+\lambda^{2}(\omega_{J}-\mathsf{i}\omega_{K})\right)\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\,\in\,H^{0}(Z,\,(\Lambda^{2}T_{\varpi}^{*})(2))\,.

Moreover,
Z
carries an anti-holomorphic involution (or real structure)

(2.5)

\tau_{Z}\,\colon\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,Z

given by the map
S^{2}\times M\,\longrightarrow\,S^{2}\times M
,
\,(x,\,m)\,\longmapsto\,(-x,\,m)
,
using the diffeomorphism
Z\,\cong\,S^{2}\times M
. Note that
\tau_{Z}
covers the antipodal map

(2.6)

\tau_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}\,:\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\quad\lambda\,\longmapsto\,-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1},

which is an anti-holomorphic involution.
The relative twisted symplectic form
\omega
is real with respect to
\tau
in the sense that
\tau^{*}\overline{\omega}\,=\,\omega
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the space of all holomorphic sections of
the projection
\varpi
in (
2.2
); this space is discussed in detail in Section
2.2
.
We have the embedding

(2.7)

\iota\,\colon\,M\,\hookrightarrow\,\mathcal{S}

that sends any
m\,\in\,M
to the constant section
x\,\longmapsto\,(x,\,m)\,\in\,S^{2}\times M
,
which is called the twistor line
s_{m}
associated to
m
.
By
[
33
, § 3, (F)]
it is known that the normal bundle of a twistor line is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}(1)^{\oplus 2d}
.
The space
\mathcal{S}
has a real structure defined by

(2.8)

\tau\,\colon\,\mathcal{S}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{S},\ \ s\,\longmapsto\,\tau_{Z}\circ s\circ\tau_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}\,,

where
\tau_{Z}
and
\tau_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}
are the involutions in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
)
respectively; we note that while the section
\tau(s)
is holomorphic for fixed
s
, the map

\tau
itself is anti-holomorphic. The manifold
M
, considered as space of twistor lines, is a component of the
fixed point locus
\mathcal{S}^{\tau}\,\subset\,\mathcal{S}
, also known as the space of real sections.

We have described the complex-geometric data on
Z
induced by the hyperKähler structure on
M
. Conversely,
suppose we have a complex manifold
Z
with a holomorphic submersion
\varpi\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
, a twisted relative
symplectic form
\omega\in H^{0}(Z,\,(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}_{\varpi})(2))
and an antiholomorphic involution
\tau\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,Z

covering the antipodal map on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
satisfying
\tau^{*}\overline{\omega}\,=\,\omega
. Then the parameter space
M
of real
sections of
\varpi
with normal bundle isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}(1)^{\oplus 2d}
is a (pseudo-)hyperKähler
manifold of real dimension
4d
, if it is non-empty; see
[
33
, Theorem 3.3]
.

2.2.
Space of holomorphic sections as a complexified hyperKähler manifold

To examine the local structure of
\mathcal{S}
, for any
s\,\in\,{\mathcal{S}}
let

N_{s}\,\,=\,\,(s^{*}TZ)/ds(T{\mathbb{C}}P^{1})

be the normal bundle
of
s({\mathbb{C}}P^{1})\,\subset\,Z
. Since
s
is a section of
\varpi
, we have a canonical isomorphism

s^{*}T_{\varpi}Z\,\cong\,N_{s}\,,

where
T_{\varpi}Z\,\subset\,TZ
as before is the kernel of the differential
d\varpi
of the map
\varpi
. The
following proposition is well-known, however a proof of it is given because parts of the proof will be
used later.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Z
be the twistor space of a hyperKähler manifold
M
of complex dimension
2d
. Then the set
\mathcal{S}
of
holomorphic sections of

\varpi\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}

is a complex space in a natural way. The
tangent space of
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}
is
H^{0}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\,N_{s})
, and
\mathcal{S}

is smooth at a point
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}
if
H^{1}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\,N_{s})\,=\,0
. If

H^{1}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\,N_{s})\,=\,0
, then
\dim T_{s}\mathcal{S}\,=\,4d
.

Proof.

For any
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}
, the sufficiently small deformations of the complex submanifold

s({\mathbb{C}}P^{1})\,\subset\,Z
continue to be the image of a section of
\varpi
. Consequently,
\mathcal{S}

is an open subset of the corresponding Douady space of rational curves in
Z
; see also
[
42

Note that

at

Using

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{8\mathsf{i}}\kappa(\mathrm{ev}^{*}\omega(\pi_{2}^{*}V,\,\pi_{2}^{*}W),\,h)

\displaystyle=\,\mathsf{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{\lambda}}_{|\lambda=0}\omega_{s(\lambda)}\left(V_{s}(\lambda),\,W_{s}(\lambda)\right).

Recall the non-degenerate two-form
\Omega_{x}
defined in (

3.
The hyperholomorphic line bundle and the energy functional

3.1.
Rotating circle actions and the hyperholomorphic line bundle

Now assume that
M
is equipped with an action of
S^{1}
that preserves the Riemannian metric
g_{0}
and also preserves
the family of complex structures
\{I_{x}\}_{x\in S^{2}}
. We also assume that the resulting action of
S^{1}

on
S^{2}
is nontrivial. This implies that the
S^{1}
-action on
M
preserves the associative algebra structure of

({\mathbb{R}}\cdot{Id}_{TM})\oplus({\mathbb{R}}\cdot I)\oplus({\mathbb{R}}\cdot J)\oplus({\mathbb{R}}\cdot K)
.
Consequently, the action of
S^{1}
on
S^{2}
is a nontrivial rotation.
This means that without loss of generality we may
assume that the
S^{1}
-action on
M
preserves
I
(thus it also preserves
-I
) and rotates the plane spanned by

J,\,K
in the standard way. We therefore call this a
rotating circle action
. The Kähler forms

\omega_{I},\,\omega_{J},\,\omega_{K}
and the Killing vector field
X
on
M
associated with this circle
satisfy the following:

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega_{I}\,=\,0,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega_{J}\,=\,\omega_{K},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega_{K}\,=\,-\omega_{J}.

Note that the second equation in (
3.1
) implies that the Kähler form
\omega_{K}
is exact,
and the third equation in (
3.1
) implies that the Kähler form
\omega_{J}
is exact.
Therefore the manifold
M
must necessarily be non-compact.

The above
S^{1}
-action on
M
evidently induces a holomorphic
S^{1}
-action on
Z
.
In terms of the identification
Z\,=\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\times M
of the underlying smooth manifold, this
action of
S^{1}
is given by

(3.2)

\zeta.(\lambda,\,m)\,=\,(\zeta\lambda,\,\zeta.m),\ \ \ \zeta\,\in\,S^{1}\,.

Here
\lambda
is an affine coordinate on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
such that
I\,=\,I_{0}
. The
C^{\infty}
-vector field
on
Z
associated to the
S^{1}
-action in (
3.2
) will be denoted by
Y
. Note that

Y|_{Z_{0}\cup Z_{\infty}}
(see (
2.3
)) is actually a holomorphic vector field on the divisor

Z_{0}\cup Z_{\infty}\,=\,(M,\,I)\cup(M,\,-I),

because the
S^{1}
-action on
M
preserves both
I
and
-I
.

We normalize the affine coordinate
\lambda
on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
such that the antipodal map
S^{2}\,\longrightarrow\,S^{2}
,

\,x\,\longmapsto\,-x
, corresponds to the map
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
,
\,\lambda\,\longmapsto\,-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1}
. The coordinate
\lambda
is then uniquely determined up to multiplication by a constant
phase
e^{\mathsf{i}\theta_{0}}
,
\theta_{0}\,\in\,[0,\,2\pi)
.

Clearly, the
S^{1}
-action on
Z
is compatible with the real structure
\tau_{Z}
in (
2.5
) in the sense that

(3.3)

\tau_{Z}(\zeta.z)\,=\,(\overline{\zeta})^{-1}.\tau_{Z}(z)\,=\,\zeta.\tau_{Z}(z)

for all
z\,\in\,Z
and
\zeta\,\in\,S^{1}
. It is straightforward to check that for any
\zeta\,\in\,S^{1}
the
twisted relative
symplectic form
\omega
in (
2.4
) satisfies the equation

(3.4)

\zeta^{*}\omega\,=\,\omega,

in other words,
\omega
is
S^{1}
-invariant.

Let
\mu\,\colon\,M\,\longrightarrow\,\mathsf{i}{\mathbb{R}}\,=\,\mathfrak{u}(1)
be a moment map with respect to
\omega_{I}

for the
S^{1}
-action on
M
. Note that since our moment map is complex-valued, the moment map equation takes the form

(3.5)

d\mu(-)\,\,=\,\,\mathsf{i}\,\omega_{I}(X,\,-).

Haydys has shown in
[
25
]
that the
2
-form
\omega_{I}+\mathsf{i}dd_{I}^{c}\mu
is of type
(1,\,1)
with respect to
every complex structure
I_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.

For the remainder of §
3
we shall make the hypothesis that
\omega_{I}
(and hence
\omega_{I}+\mathsf{i}dd_{I}^{c}\mu
) is
integral
, i.e.

(3.6)

[\omega_{I}/2\pi]\,\in\,H^{2}(M,\,\mathbb{Z}).

Then there exists a
C^{\infty}
hermitian line bundle

(3.7)

(L_{M},\,h_{M})\,\longrightarrow\,M

with a compatible hermitian connection
\nabla_{M}
whose curvature is
\omega_{I}+\mathsf{i}dd_{1}^{c}\mu
.
Consequently, if
\nabla_{M}^{(0,1)_{\lambda}}
is the
(0,1)
-part of
\nabla_{M}
with respect to the complex structure

, we obtain a holomorphic line bundle
(L_{M},\,\nabla^{(0,1)_{\lambda}})\,\longrightarrow\,(M,\,I_{\lambda})
. Note that the Chern connection of the hermitian holomorphic line bundle
(L,\,e^{-\mu}h_{M})\,\longrightarrow\,(M,\,I)
has curvature
\omega_{I}
, and hence it is a prequantum line bundle on the Kähler
manifold
(M,\,\omega_{I})
.

Denote by

(3.8)

the
C^{\infty}
submersion
given by the natural projection

(3.9)

S^{2}\times M\,\longrightarrow\,M.

Note that for each

the fibre of

, where we use the notation of §
2.1
.
It follows that

is a holomorphic line bundle
over the twistor space

of

, which is trivial along each twistor line.

exhibiting a natural meromorphic connection

covers the standard

The following theorem is a holomorphic extension of (

Theorem 3.3
.

The energy function
\mathcal{E}\,\colon\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}
(see (

)) is a moment map for the natural

S^{1}
-action and holomorphic symplectic structure

on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, in other words,

(3.26)

In particular, the
S^{1}
-fixed points in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are precisely the critical points of
the function
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Take any

, and set

.
We first compute

for

By definition

, so

for some

(see Lemma

).
Further
V
is representable by a family

of sections with
s_{t}(0)\,=\,m
and

\partial_{t=0}s_{t}(\lambda)\,=\,V(\lambda)
.
Note that
\partial_{\lambda=0}V(\lambda)
is well-defined because
V(0)\,=\,0
.
Then a local computation shows that

\partial_{t=0}\dot{s}_{t}(0)\,=\,\partial_{\lambda=0}V(\lambda)\,=\,v\,\in\,T_{m}M.

Hence we conclude that

d_{s}\mathcal{E}(V)\,=\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\omega\left(\dot{s}_{t}(0)-\dot{s}_{m}(0)\right)+\mu(m)\right)\,=\,-\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\omega(v).

Next we consider
X_{s}\,\in\,H^{0}({\mathbb{C}}P^{1},\,N_{s})
, the fundamental vector field
X
evaluated at
s
.
Since
Y(m)\,=\,X_{s}(0)
(see Lemma

), it follows that

(3.27)

\Omega_{0}|_{s}(X_{s},V)\,=\,\tfrac{\mathsf{i}}{2}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}\omega_{s(\lambda)}(X_{s}(\lambda),V(\lambda))\,=\,\tfrac{\mathsf{i}}{2}\omega(Y(0),v)\,=\,\tfrac{\mathsf{i}}{2}\iota_{Y}\omega(v).

It remains to prove the claim for
V\,\,\in\,\,T_{\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}}{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\big|_{s}
.

To this end, observe that
d(\overline{\tau^{*}\mathcal{E}})\,=\,d\mathcal{E}
by (

) because
the function
s\,\longmapsto\,\mathrm{deg}(s^{*}L_{Z})
on
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is locally constant.
Further we know that
\overline{d\tau(T_{\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime})}\,=\,\tau^{*}T_{\mathrm{ev}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
so that every
V\,\in\,T_{\mathrm{ev}_{\infty}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\big|_{s}
is of the form

for

Then we compute the following using the previous result and

:

(3.28)

\displaystyle d\mathcal{E}_{s}(V)

\displaystyle\,=\,\overline{\tau^{*}d\mathcal{E}}_{s}(V)

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\,d\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau(s)}(W)

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\,\overline{\Omega}_{\tau(s)}(X_{\tau(s)},d\tau_{\tau(s)}(V))

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\,(\overline{\tau^{*}\Omega})_{s}(X_{s},V)

(3.32)

\displaystyle=\,\Omega_{s}(X_{s},V).

This completes the proof.
{\Box}

4.
Space of holomorphic sections of the Deligne–Hitchin moduli
space

In this section we illustrate the general theory described in §
2
and §
3
for the
Deligne–Hitchin twistor space of the moduli space of Higgs bundles on a compact Riemann
surface.

4.1.
Hitchin’s self-duality equations

Let
\Sigma
be a compact Riemann surface of genus at least two; denote its holomorphic
cotangent bundle by
K_{\Sigma}
. Consider
a smooth complex rank
n
vector bundle
E
of degree zero with structure group in
{\rm SU}(n)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}(E)
the space of
{\rm SU}(n)
-connections on
E
. Sending any
\nabla\,\in\,\mathcal{A}(E)
to its
(0,1)
-part
\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}
we may
identify
\mathcal{A}
with the space of holomorphic structures on
E
inducing the
trivial holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}
on the determinant bundle

\det E\,=\,\bigwedge^{n}E
of
E
, that is,
(\det E,\,\overline{\partial}^{\nabla})\,=\,(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma},\,\overline{\partial})
. Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is an affine space modelled
on
\Omega^{0,1}(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))
, where
\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E)
is the bundle of trace-free endomorphisms of
E
. The product

(4.1)

T^{*}\mathcal{A}\,=\,\mathcal{A}\times\Omega^{1,0}(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))

can be thought of as the cotangent bundle of
\mathcal{A}
. We will denote its elements by

(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)
, and we will call
\Phi
the
Higgs field
of the pair

(\overline{\partial},\,\Phi)
.
Formally,
T^{*}\mathcal{A}
carries a flat hyperKähler structure that can be described as
follows. The Riemannian metric is given by the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))
.
The almost complex structures
I,\,J,\,K\,=\,IJ
act on a tangent vector

(\alpha,\,\phi)\,\in\,T_{(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\Phi)}T^{*}\mathcal{A}\,=\,\Omega^{0,1}(\Sigma,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(\Sigma,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle I(\alpha,\,\phi)

\displaystyle\,=\,(\mathsf{i}\alpha,\,\mathsf{i}\phi)

(4.3)

\displaystyle J(\alpha,\,\phi)

\displaystyle\,=\,(-\phi^{*},\,\alpha^{*}).

The Kähler forms are given by

(4.4)

\begin{gathered}\omega_{I}((\alpha,\,\phi),\,(\beta,\,\psi))\,=\,\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}(\alpha^{*}\wedge\beta-\beta^{*}\wedge\alpha+\phi\wedge\psi^{*}-\psi\wedge\phi^{*})\\
(\omega_{J}+\mathsf{i}\omega_{K})((\alpha,\,\phi),\,(\beta,\,\psi))\,=\,2\mathsf{i}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}(\psi\wedge\alpha-\phi\wedge\beta).\end{gathered}

The group
\mathcal{G}\,:=\,\Gamma(\Sigma,\,{\rm SU}(E))
of unitary gauge transformations
of
E
acts (on the right) on
T^{*}\mathcal{A}
as

(4.5)

(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi).g\,=\,(g^{-1}\circ\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}\circ g,\,g^{-1}\Phi g).

This action preserves the flat hyperKähler structure, and, formally, the vanishing condition for the associated hyperKähler moment map yields
Hitchin’s self-duality equations
:

(4.6)

\displaystyle F^{\nabla}+[\Phi\wedge\Phi^{*}]

\displaystyle\,=\,0

(4.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}\Phi

\displaystyle\,=\,0.

The second equation implies that
\Phi\,\in\,H^{0}(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E)\otimes K_{\Sigma})
with respect to the holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}
. Note that these equations imply that the connection
\nabla+\Phi+\Phi^{*}
is flat.

Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,T^{*}\mathcal{A}
be the set of solutions of (
4.6
). The moduli space of solutions to the self-duality equations is the quotient

(4.8)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\,:=\,\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{G}.

Formally, it is the hyperKähler quotient of
T^{*}\mathcal{A}
by the action of
\mathcal{G}
.

A solution
(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)
to the self-duality equations (
4.6
)
is called
irreducible
if its stabilizer in
\mathcal{G}
is the center of

{\rm SU}(n)
. We write
\mathcal{H}^{irr}\,\subset\,\mathcal{H}
for the set of irreducible
solutions. It is known that

(4.9)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{H}^{irr}/\mathcal{G}

is the smooth locus of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}
in (
4.8
). It is equipped with a hyperKähler metric induced
by the flat hyperKähler structure on
T^{*}\mathcal{A}
described above. Moreover,
there is a rotating circle action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}
given by

(4.10)

\zeta.(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)\,\,=\,\,(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\,\zeta\Phi),

\zeta\,\in\,S^{1}
. It is Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{I}
, and the map

(4.11)

\mu_{I}\,\colon\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathsf{i}{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\mu_{I}(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)\,=\,-\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}(\Phi\wedge\Phi^{*})

restricts to a natural moment map on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}
.

The complex manifold
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr},\,I)
can be described as follows. An
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs
bundle
is a pair consisting of a holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\,\overline{\partial}_{E})
, such
that
\det(E,\,\overline{\partial}_{E})\,=\,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}
, together with a Higgs field

\Phi\,\in\,H^{0}(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E)\otimes K_{\Sigma})
. The group
\mathcal{G}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\,=\,\Gamma(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(E))
acts on the set of Higgs bundles by the same rule as
in (
4.5
). A Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\,\Phi)
is called
stable

if every
\Phi
-invariant holomorphic subbundle
E^{\prime}
has negative degree, and it
is called
polystable
if it is a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles
of degree zero. We denote by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{st}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{st}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\ \,\text{ and }\ \,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{ps}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{ps}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))

the moduli spaces of stable and polystable Higgs bundles
respectively. It is known that the map
(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)\,\longmapsto\,((E,\,\overline{\partial}^{\nabla}),\Phi)
induces a biholomorphism

(4.12)

(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr},\,I)\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{st}

that extends to a homeomorphism

(4.13)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{ps}.

The circle action described in (
4.10
) extends to a holomorphic
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}
:

(4.14)

\zeta\cdot(\overline{\partial},\,\Phi)\,=\,(\overline{\partial},\,\zeta\Phi).

While the complex structure
I
is induced from the complex structure on
\Sigma
, the
complex structure
J
has a more topological origin. Consider the space
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
connections on
E
. The group
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
acts on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}
as

(4.15)

\nabla.g\,=\,g^{-1}\circ\nabla\circ g.

A connection
\nabla\,\in\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}
is called
irreducible
if its stabilizer in

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the center of
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
, and
\nabla
is called
reductive
if it is
isomorphic to
a direct sum of irreducible connections, in other words, if any
\nabla
-invariant subbundle
E^{\prime}\,\subset\,E

admits a
\nabla
-invariant complement. Let

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))

be the moduli space of reductive flat
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-connections on
E
. Its smooth locus is given
by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}
, the moduli space of irreducible flat
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-connections. Then the
map
(\overline{\partial}^{\nabla},\,\Phi)\,\longmapsto\,\nabla+\Phi+\Phi^{*}
is a biholomorphism

(4.16)

(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr},\,J)\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}

that extends to a homeomorphism

(4.17)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}.

The Riemann–Hilbert correspondence gives a biholomorphism

(4.18)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}\,\cong\,{\rm Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))^{\mathrm{red}}/{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\,=:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\,=:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}.

The space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}
is known as the Betti moduli space.

4.2.
The Deligne–Hitchin moduli space

We now recall Deligne’s construction of the twistor space
Z(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}})
via
\lambda
-connections;
see
[
46
]
.

Take any
\lambda\,\in\,\mathbb{C}
. A holomorphic
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

\lambda
–connection on the

{\rm SU}(n)
-vector bundle
E\,\longrightarrow\,\Sigma
is a pair
(\overline{\partial},\,D)
, where
\overline{\partial}
is a holomorphic
structure on
E
and
D:\Gamma(\Sigma,E)\,\longrightarrow\,\Omega^{1,0}(\Sigma,E)
is a differential
operator such that

•

for any
f\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
we have
D(fs)\,=\,\lambda s\otimes\partial f+fDs
,

•

D
is holomorphic, that is,
\overline{\partial}\circ D+D\circ\overline{\partial}\,=\,0
, and

•

as a holomorphic vector bundle we have
(\bigwedge^{n}E,\,\overline{\partial})\,=\,({\mathcal{O}}_{\Sigma},\,\overline{\partial}_{\Sigma})
, and the holomorphic differential
operator on
\Lambda^{n}E
induced by
D
coincides with
\lambda\partial_{\Sigma}
.

Therefore, an
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

0
–connection is an
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
–Higgs bundle, and an

{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})

1
–connection is a usual holomorphic
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
–connection. More
generally, the operator
\overline{\partial}_{E}+\lambda^{-1}D(\lambda)
is a holomorphic
connection for every
\lambda\,\neq\,0
.

The group
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
of complex gauge transformations of
E
acts on the set of holomorphic
\lambda
–connections in the usual way:

(4.19)

(\overline{\partial},\,D)\cdot g\,=\,(g^{-1}\circ\overline{\partial}\circ g,\,g^{-1}\circ D\circ g).

We again call
(\overline{\partial},\,D)
stable if any
D
-invariant holomorphic subbundle

E^{\prime}\,\subset\,E
has negative degree, and
(\overline{\partial},\,D)
is
called polystable if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of stable
\lambda
-connections
of degree zero. We note that if
\lambda\,\not=\,0
, then the degree of any
\lambda
-connection
is automatically zero. Equivalently,
(\overline{\partial},\,D)
is stable, with
\lambda\,\not=\,0
, if it is
irreducible, that is, its stabilizer in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
is given by the center of
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
.
The Hodge–moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
is the moduli space of
polystable holomorphic
\lambda
-connections, where
\lambda
varies over
{\mathbb{C}}
:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,\,{\mathbb{C}}))\,\\
&=\,\{(\lambda,\overline{\partial},D)\,\mid\,\,\lambda\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}},\,\,\text{$(\overline{\partial},\,D)$ polystable holomorphic $\lambda$-connection}\}/\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{C}}.\end{split}

It has a natural holomorphic projection to
{\mathbb{C}}
given by

(4.20)

\varpi\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}},\qquad[\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D]\,\longmapsto\,\lambda.

The smooth locus
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}^{irr}
of the Hodge moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}

coincides with the locus of irreducible
\lambda
-connections, when
\lambda\,\not=\,0
.

The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action in (
4.14
) extends to a natural
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}
covering the standard
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}
by

(4.21)

\zeta\cdot(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D)\,=\,(\zeta\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,\zeta D).

The map
(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D)\,\longmapsto\,(\overline{\partial}+\lambda^{-1}D,\,\lambda)
induces a biholomorphism

(4.22)

\varpi^{-1}({\mathbb{C}}^{*})\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*},

where
\varpi
is the map in (
4.20
).

The Deligne–Hitchin moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
is obtained by gluing
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}

and
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\overline{\Sigma},\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
over
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
via the
Riemann–Hilbert correspondence:

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}\,:=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))

(4.24)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\,\dot{\cup}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}(\overline{\Sigma},{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})))/\sim

where

(4.25)

[(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D)]\,\sim\,[(\lambda,\,\lambda^{-1}D,\,\lambda^{-1}\overline{\partial})]

for any
[(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D)]\,\in\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}
with
\lambda\,\neq\,0
.

The projections from the respective Hodge moduli spaces to
{\mathbb{C}}
glue to give a holomorphic
projection

\varpi\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}P^{1}.

The smooth locus of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}
coincides with the locus

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}
of irreducible
\lambda
-connections, when
\lambda\,\not=\,\{0,\,\infty\}
, which in turn coincides with the twistor
space
Z(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr})
of the hyperKähler manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}
.

The space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
admits an anti-holomorphic involution
\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}}
covering the
antipodal involution
\lambda\,\longmapsto\,-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
which is
constructed as follows. For any
\rho\,\in\,{\rm Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
, define

(4.26)

\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}}(\rho,\,\lambda)\,=\,(\overline{\rho^{-1}}^{T},\,-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1}).

This produces the following antiholomorphic involution
\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}
:

\begin{split}\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}\,&\colon\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}},\\
&[(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D)]\,\longmapsto\,[(-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1},\,(\overline{\lambda})^{-1}\overline{D}^{*},\,-(\overline{\lambda})^{-1}\overline{\overline{\partial}}^{*})]\,=\,[(-\overline{\lambda}),\,\overline{\overline{\partial}}^{*},\,-\overline{D}^{*})].\end{split}

This involution
\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}
is compatible with the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action in the following sense.
For
\zeta\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,

(4.27)

\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}(\zeta.(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D))\,=\,\overline{\zeta}^{-1}.\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}(\lambda\,\overline{\partial},\,D).

The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Hod}}
extends to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}
. For any
\zeta\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
we have

(4.28)

\zeta\cdot[\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,D(\lambda)]\,=\,[\zeta\lambda,\,\overline{\partial},\,\zeta D(\lambda)].

Clearly, this action covers the natural
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.
Therefore the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed points of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}
are given by

(4.29)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\Sigma,\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}\amalg\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\overline{\Sigma},\,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}},

that is, by the locus of complex variations of Hodge structures on
\Sigma

and
\overline{\Sigma}
(see
[
48
]
).

The twisted relative symplectic form on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}
can be described as follows. Let

V_{i}\,=\,(\dot{\overline{\partial}_{i}},\,\dot{D}_{i})
,
i\,=\,1,\,2
, be a pair of tangent
vectors to the fiber
\varpi^{-1}(\lambda)
. Then

(4.30)

\omega_{\lambda}(V_{1},\,V_{2})\,=\,2\mathsf{i}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{D}_{2}\wedge\dot{\overline{\partial}}_{1}-\dot{D}_{1}\wedge\dot{\overline{\partial}}_{2}).

Note that at
\lambda\,=\,0
this exactly resembles
\omega_{J}+\mathsf{i}\omega_{K}
as defined in (
4.4
).

4.3.
The line bundle on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}

Let us describe the holomorphic line bundle
L_{Z}
for
Z\,=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}
along each fiber of

\varpi
. First consider the moduli space of holomorphic
{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-connections
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}
. Let

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime}
be the Zariski open subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}
such that the underlying holomorphic vector
bundle is stable. So we have a holomorphic map

(4.31)

f\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{N}\,,

where
\mathcal{N}
is the moduli space of stable

{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-bundles, that sends any pair
((E,\,\overline{\partial}_{E}),\,\nabla^{E})
to
(E,\,\overline{\partial}_{E})
.

The pullback map
f^{*}\,:\,\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{N})\,\longrightarrow\,\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism

[
10
]
. On the other hand,
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime})\,=\,{\mathbb{Z}}
, and holomorphic line bundles
on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime}
are uniquely determined by their first class
[
16
]
.
Also the restriction map
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr})\,\longrightarrow\,\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism,
because the codimension of the complement of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\prime}
inside
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}
is at least two
(see
[
9
, p. 6Lemma 3.1]
,
[
8
, p. 202–203]
).
Therefore, we have
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr})\,=\,\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{N})\,\cong\,\mathbb{Z}
, and
the holomorphic line bundles on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}
are uniquely determined by their first Chern class.

Let
L_{1}
denote the restriction of
L_{Z}
to
Z_{1}\,=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{irr}
. On
L_{1}
, the meromorphic connection

\nabla
on
L_{Z}
induces a holomorphic connection and its curvature on
Z_{1}
is the holomorphic symplectic
form
2\omega_{J}+2\mathsf{i}\omega_{I}
, which is cohomologous to
2\mathsf{i}\omega_{I}
, since
\omega_{J}
is exact. So by
Chern–Weil theory, we have
c_{1}(L_{1})\,=\,[\omega_{I}]
, where
[\omega_{I}
is the cohomology class of
\omega_{I}
.

It follows that
L_{1}
is the holomorphic line bundle on
Z_{1}
determined by
[\omega_{I}]
. To
calculate
[\omega_{I}]
, note that the above projection
f
has a
C^{\infty}
-section that sends
any stable vector bundle
(E,\,\overline{\partial}_{E})
to the unique unitary flat connection on
E

[
43
]
.
The restriction
of
\omega_{I}
to the image of that section coincides with the standard Kähler form on
\mathcal{N}
.
On the other hand, the first Chern class of the determinant line bundle
\xi
on
\mathcal{N}
is the
Kähler form
[
45
]
. In fact, the curvature for the Quillen metric on
\xi
is the Kähler form.

We conclude that
L_{1}
is holomorphically isomorphic to the determinant bundle on
Z_{1}
, which
coincides with the pullback of the determinant line bundle on
\mathcal{N}
because the determinant
line bundle is functorial. Similarly, the line bundles on the moduli space of stable Higgs
bundles, namely
Z_{0}\,=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}^{st}
, are uniquely determined by their first Chern class. Let
L_{0}

denote the restriction of
L_{Z}
to
Z_{0}
. Since
L_{1}
is isomorphic to the determinant line
bundle, and since the first Chern class of the family of line bundles
L_{t}\,\longrightarrow\,Z_{t}
,
t\,\in\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
, is independent of
t
, it follows that
L_{0}
is isomorphic to the determinant line
bundle. For a description of
L_{Z}
on all of
Z\,=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}
, see
[
31
, § 3.7]
.

4.4.
Irreducible and admissible sections

In this subsection we recall some concepts and definitions from
[
6
]
and
[
4
]
on
sections of the twistor projection
\varpi\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. We write

5.
Energy functional on sections of the Deligne–Hitchin moduli space

5.1.
The energy as a moment map

It was proven in
[
4
, Corollary 3.11]
that the energy of an irreducible section
s
with lift
\widehat{s}
as in (
4.32
) is given by

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}(s)\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2\pi\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}(\Phi\wedge\Psi).

In particular, this integral is independent of the lift
\widehat{s}
.
The reader should be aware of the different pre-factors in (
5.1
) and in (
4.11
).
In particular, if we think of
\mathcal{E}
as the energy of a harmonic map, it should be real-valued, while we want a moment map
for the
S^{1}
-action to be
\mathsf{i}{\mathbb{R}}
-valued. Working with the pre-factor
\tfrac{1}{2\pi\mathsf{i}}
also has the advantage that we get fewer factors of
2\pi\mathsf{i}
in the statements of the results below.

Remark 5.1
.

As pointed out in
[
6
, Remark 2.3]
, the energy is defined
for all local sections around
\lambda\,=\,0
which admit a lift as in (
4.32
).

Let us write again
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\,=\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}^{\prime}
for the space of
irreducible

sections whose normal bundle is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}(1)^{\oplus 2d}
. Take any
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. In terms of lifts of sections, a tangent vector
V\in T_{s}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is expressed as
follows. Let
\widehat{s}
be a lift of
s
as in (
4.32
), and denote the
curvature of the connection
\partial+\overline{\partial}
by
F^{\partial+\overline{\partial}}\,=\,\overline{\partial}\partial+\partial\overline{\partial}
. Expanding the integrability
condition

(5.2)

\overline{\partial}(\lambda)D(\lambda)+D(\lambda)\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\,=\,0

in powers of
\lambda
, the zeroth and first order coefficients yield

(5.3)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\Phi

\displaystyle\,=\,0

(5.4)

\displaystyle F^{\partial+\overline{\partial}}+[\Phi\wedge\Psi]

\displaystyle\,=\,0\,.

Consider a family of sections
(s_{t}\,\in\,{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}})_{t}
with
s\,=\,s_{0}
which
represents
V\,\in\,T_{s}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
The corresponding (lifted) infinitesimal variation

satisfies the linearisation
of (
5.2
), that is,

(5.5)

\overline{\partial}(\lambda)(\dot{D}(\lambda))+D(\lambda)(\dot{\overline{\partial}}(\lambda))\,=\,0.

Expanding
\dot{\widehat{s}}
into a power series

(5.6)

\dot{\widehat{s}}(\lambda)\,=\,\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_{k}\lambda^{k},\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}\lambda^{k},\,\lambda\right)\,,

for
\varphi_{k}\,\in\,\Omega^{1,0}(\mathfrak{sl}(E)),\,\psi_{k}\,\in\,\Omega^{0,1}(\mathfrak{sl}(E))
, the linearisation
of (
5.3
) becomes

(5.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\varphi_{0}+[\psi_{0}\wedge\Phi]

\displaystyle\,=\,0

(5.8)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\varphi_{1}+\partial\psi_{0}+[\varphi_{0}\wedge\Psi]+[\Phi\wedge\psi_{1}]

\displaystyle\,=\,0\,.

Variations along the gauge orbit of
\widehat{s}
are determined by infinitesimal gauge
transformations
{\mathbb{C}}\,\ni\,\lambda\,\longmapsto\,\xi(\lambda)\,\in\,\Gamma(\Sigma,\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(E))
and are of the form

(5.9)

(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\xi(\lambda),\,D(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)).

By expanding

, we get with (
5.9
) and (
4.32
)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)

\displaystyle\,=\,\overline{\partial}\xi_{0}+(\overline{\partial}\xi_{1}+[\Psi,\,\xi_{0}])\lambda+O(\lambda^{2})

(5.11)

\displaystyle D(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)

\displaystyle\,=\,[\Phi,\,\xi_{0}]+(\partial\xi_{0}+[\Phi,\,\xi_{1}])\lambda+O(\lambda^{2})\,.

Now let
s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
with lift
\widehat{s}
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and consider

V_{j}\,\in\,T_{s}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
j\,=\,1,\,2
, represented by

(5.12)

\dot{\widehat{s}}_{j}\,=\,(\lambda,\,\dot{\overline{\partial}}_{j}(\lambda),\,\dot{D}_{j}(\lambda))\,=\,(\lambda,\,\psi^{(j)}_{0}+\psi^{(j)}_{1}\lambda,\,\varphi_{0}^{(j)}+\varphi_{1}^{(j)}\lambda)+O(\lambda^{2}).

Then we define, recalling the definition of
\omega_{\lambda}
given in (
4.30
),

(5.13)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Omega}_{\widehat{s}}(V_{1},\,V_{2})

\displaystyle\,=\,-\tfrac{\mathsf{i}}{2}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}_{|\lambda=0}\omega_{\lambda}(V_{1}(\lambda),\,V_{2}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\mathsf{i}}{2}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}_{|\lambda=0}2\mathsf{i}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\dot{D}_{1}(\lambda)\wedge\dot{\overline{\partial}}_{2}(\lambda)+\dot{D}_{2}(\lambda)\wedge\dot{\overline{\partial}}_{1}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\,\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\varphi_{0}^{(1)}\wedge\psi_{1}^{(2)}+\varphi_{0}^{(2)}\wedge\psi_{1}^{(1)}-\varphi_{1}^{(1)}\wedge\psi_{0}^{(2)}+\varphi_{1}^{(2)}\wedge\psi_{0}^{(1)}\right).

We view
\widehat{\Omega}
in (

sections of
\varpi
at
\lambda\,=\,0

is
exactly (

Proposition 5.2
.

The two-form
\widehat{\Omega}
in (

defined in (

Proof.

Since the form
\widehat{\Omega}
is closed, it suffices to show that the one-form at
\widehat{s}
obtained by
contracting
\widehat{\Omega}_{\widehat{s}}
with any vertical tangent vector at

To this end, let
\widehat{s}
be a germ of a section near
\lambda\,=\,0
, and
let
\xi(\lambda)\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\xi_{k}\lambda^{k}
be an infinitesimal gauge transformation.
The corresponding tangent vector

is represented by

\dot{\widehat{s}}_{1}\,\,=\,\,(\lambda,\,\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\xi(\lambda),\,D(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)).

Then for an arbitrary tangent vector

\displaystyle\widehat{\Omega}_{\widehat{s}}(V_{1},\,V_{2})

\displaystyle\,=\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}_{|\lambda=0}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\dot{D}(\lambda)\wedge\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)+D(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)\wedge\dot{}\overline{\partial}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}_{|\lambda=0}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}\left((\overline{\partial}(\lambda)(\dot{D}(\lambda))+D(\lambda)(\dot{}\overline{\partial}(\lambda)))\xi(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,0,

{\Box}

Take

As in Section

]

\displaystyle 0

s\,\in\,\mathcal{S}
. Suppose that
\dot{s}
satisfies

(5.85)

[\psi_{0},\,\xi]\,=\,n_{0}\psi_{0},\quad[\psi_{1},\,\xi]\,=\,n_{1}\psi_{1},\quad[\varphi_{0},\,\xi]\,=\,m_{0}\varphi_{0},\quad[\varphi_{1},\,\xi]\,=\,m_{1}\varphi_{1}

for some
m_{i},\,n_{i}\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}
. Then

(5.86)

d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\dot{s})\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}((m_{1}+n_{0})\psi_{0}\wedge\varphi_{1}+(m_{0}+n_{1}+2)\psi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{0}).

Remark 5.15
.

Note that this resembles the discussion surrounding Eq. (8.10) in
[
30
]
. In fact, it does reproduce Hitchin’s result in the
case where
s
is the twistor line corresponding to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed point in
\mathcal{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}}

and the deformation
\dot{s}
is real, so that
\psi_{1}\,=\,\varphi_{0}^{*},\,\psi_{0}\,=\,-\varphi_{1}^{*}
.

5.5.
Sections and the degree of the hyperholomorphic line bundle

Our previous results together with the energy can be used to show that the space of irreducible sections is not connected.
We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
(\overline{\partial},\,\Phi)
be a stable
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed Higgs bundle, and let

s_{(\beta,\phi)}
be a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed section corresponding to
(\beta,\,\phi)\,\in\,\mathcal{B}^{+}_{(\overline{\partial},\Phi)}
.
If
s_{\beta,\phi}(\infty)
is given by a VHS on
\overline{\Sigma}
with underlying holomorphic bundle
E\,=\,\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l^{\prime}}E^{\prime}_{k}
, then the following holds:

(5.87)

\deg(s_{(\beta,\phi)}^{*}L_{Z})\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{l}(k-1)\deg(E_{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{l^{\prime}}(k-1)\deg(E_{k}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Proposition
5.12
allows us to compute
\mathcal{E}_{0}(s)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(s)
.
The assertion now follows from the formula

(5.88)

\deg(s_{(\beta,\phi)}^{*}L_{Z})\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{E}(s_{(\beta,\phi)})+\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(s_{(\beta,\phi)}).

This completes the proof.
{\Box}

∎

Theorem 5.17
.

There exist irreducible sections
s
of
\varpi\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
such that the pullback
s^{*}L_{Z}
of the holomorphic line bundle

L_{Z}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))
has non-zero degree. In particular, the space of irreducible sections is not connected.

Proof.

Let
K_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}
be a square-root of the canonical line bundle
K_{\Sigma}
.
Consider the uniformization (Fuchsian) flat connection

(5.89)

\nabla^{Fuchs}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{K_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}}&1^{*}\\
1&\nabla^{K_{\Sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\end{pmatrix}

on the rank two vector bundle
K_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\oplus K_{\Sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For a generic holomorphic quadratic differential
q\,\in\,H^{0}(\Sigma,\,K_{\Sigma}^{2})
, the anti-holomorphic structure

(5.90)

\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{K_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}}&q\\
1&\partial_{K_{\Sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\end{pmatrix}

is stable (that is, it defines a stable holomorphic vector bundle on
\overline{\Sigma}
).
Then,

(5.91)

\nabla\,\,:=\,\,\nabla^{Fuchs}+\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

gives a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant section
s_{\nabla}\,\in\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}
by the construction of Lemma
5.4
.
In view of Proposition
5.12
,
the energy at
\lambda\,=\,0
is given by

(5.92)

\deg{K_{\Sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\,=\,1-g\,\neq\,0.

By assumption,
\partial^{\nabla}
is stable, so the anti-Higgs field of
s
at
\lambda=\infty

vanishes, and the energy at
\lambda=\infty
is given by
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}=0.

Finally, we have

(5.93)

\deg(s^{*}L_{Z})\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{E}(s)+\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(s)\,\,\neq\,\,0

by the residue formula for the pull-back, under
s
, of the meromorphic connection to
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
(see
Section 3 of
[
4
]
).
{\Box}

∎

Remark 5.18
.

By the general theory explained in §
3.1
the
C^{\infty}
line bundle underlying
L_{Z}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
agrees on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
with the pull-back of a line bundle on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
under the
C^{\infty}
projection
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}^{irr}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\cong S^{2}\times\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{SD}}^{irr}(\Sigma,{\rm SL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))
. Since the twistor lines are just the fibres of this projection, it follows that
\deg(s^{*}L_{Z})=0
for any twistor line
s
.

Given a(n irreducible) section
s\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DH}}}
, it is in general very difficult to compute its normal bundle
N_{s}
.
However,
by using the methods of
[
26
]
, it can be shown that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed points considered in the proof of
Theorem
5.17
do not have
normal bundles of generic type, i.e., their normal bundle admits holomorphic sections with double zeros.

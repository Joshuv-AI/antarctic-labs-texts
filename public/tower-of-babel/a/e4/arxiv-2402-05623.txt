Mass dimension one fermions in FLRW space-time Preprint: APS/123-QED
Cheng-Yang Lee, Haomin Rao, Wenqi Yu, Siyi Zhou

Abstract
Elko is a massive spin-half field of mass dimension one. Elko differs from the Dirac and Majorana fermions because it furnishes the irreducible representation of the extended Poincaré group with a two-fold Wigner degeneracy where the particle and anti-particle states both have four degrees of freedom. Elko has a renormalizable quartic self interaction which makes it a candidate for self-interacting dark matter. We study Elko in the spatially flat FLRW space-time and find exact solutions in the de Sitter space. By choosing the appropriate solutions and phases, the fields satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations and have the correct time evolutions in the flat space limit.

V
Conclusion and outlook

The foundation of Elko in Minkowski space-time was established in
Ahluwalia et al. 2023
. Building upon this work, we initiate a systematic study of Elko in curved space-time.

In this work, we study Elko in the spatially flat FLRW space-time following
Hoff da Silva and Pereira 2014
. By working in the helicity basis and utilizing the dual-helicity structure of the spinors, we found exact solutions in the dS background and showed that the resulting mass dimension one fermionic fields satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations.

There are many open questions to be addressed. For more realistic situations such as inflation, it is necessary to study Elko in the quasi dS background. There are two cosmological models worthy of future research: (1) A model of scalar inflaton coupled to Elko. (2) Elko as an inflaton. The latter possibility was initially considered by Boehmer et al
Boehmer 2007a
;
Boehmer 2007b
;
Boehmer and Burnett 2008
;
Boehmer 2008
;
Boehmer and Burnett 2010
;
Boehmer et al. 2010
though at that time, the correct degrees of freedom have not yet been identified and these works mainly focused on the classical dynamics. Since Elko is a dark matter candidate, the second proposal is a model of dark matter driven inflation. In this case, it may be possible to connect inflation with the creation of dark matter.

In both scenarios, interactions coming from the scalar, vector and tensor perturbations of the space-time metric can be derived using the ADM formalism
Arnowitt et al. 2008
. The real challenge lies in computing observables such as multi-point correlation functions and particle creation rates. As noted in
Ahluwalia et al. 2024
, the Elko interactions in the Minkowski space-time are in general pseudo Hermitian. This feature is also present in curved space-time. To compute observables, a formalism to deal with pseudo Hermitian operators is required. In the Minkowski space-time, the pseudo Hermiticity structure of the interacting Hamiltonian allows one to define new inner-product that preserves time translation symmetry and generalized unitarity though further works are required to ascertain whether observables can be consistently computed. Formalism and techniques developed in flat space can in principle be generalized to curved space though subtleties may arise since time translation is no longer a symmetry.

Another open question is how Elko affects torsion in space-time. In Einstein-Cartan gravity, which is the gauge theory of the Poincaré group
Kibble 1961
;
Blagojević and Hehl 2013
, the spin density of matter can cause torsion
Hehl et al. 1976
;
Medina et al. 2019
. Since the Dirac and Elko field have different spin density tensors, it can be expected that the two fields will induce different torsion effects.

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left\{\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right\}\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda.

(71)

Our task is to evaluate the commutator on the third line of (
71
). Because
\nabla_{\nu}\lambda
is a spin-vector, its covariant derivative contains both the spin connection and the Christoffel symbol

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}\right)\lambda+\Gamma_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu\lambda}+\Gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\lambda+\Gamma_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}\lambda

(72)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{p}\partial_{\chi}\lambda+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\chi}\lambda

the commutator reads

\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda=\left(\partial_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\mu}\Gamma_{\nu}-\Gamma_{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu}\right)\lambda

(73)

substitute the definition of
\Gamma_{\mu}
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda=\left\{\frac{1}{4}\left(\partial_{\mu}{\omega_{\nu}}^{ab}-\partial_{\nu}{\omega_{\mu}}^{ab}\right)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b}+\right.

(74)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{16}{{\omega_{\mu}}^{a}}_{c}{\omega_{\nu}}^{cb}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b},\gamma_{c}\gamma_{d}\right]\right\}\lambda

Using the identity

[\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b},\gamma_{c}\gamma_{d}]=2(\eta_{bc}\gamma_{a}\gamma_{d}-\eta_{ac}\gamma_{b}\gamma_{d}+\eta_{bd}\gamma_{c}\gamma_{a}-\eta_{ad}\gamma_{c}\gamma_{b}),

(75)

we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda

(76)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e_{a\chi}e_{b\sigma}\left(\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}^{ab}+{{\omega_{[\mu}}^{a}}_{c}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{cb}\right)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}\lambda,

use equation (74) of
Yepez 2011

\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\lambda=\frac{1}{4}R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}\lambda.

(77)

Equation (
71
) becomes

\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda+\frac{1}{8}R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}\lambda.

(78)

The second term of (
78
) can be simplified by considering

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=-2R_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\nu}-R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=-2R_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\nu}+(R_{\mu\sigma\nu\chi}+R_{\mu\chi\sigma\nu})\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\sigma}

(79)

where
R_{\mu\nu}={R^{\sigma}}_{\mu\sigma\nu}
and we have used the Bianchi identity. Using the identity

\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\sigma}=\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}-2g^{\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}+2g^{\nu\sigma}\gamma^{\chi}

(80)

we obtain

R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}=-2R_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\nu}

(81)

and hence

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R_{\mu\nu\chi\sigma}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\chi}\gamma^{\sigma}.

(82)

Substituting (
82
) into (
78
), we obtain the Lichnerowicz formula

\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\lambda=\left(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-\frac{1}{4}R\right)\lambda.

(83)

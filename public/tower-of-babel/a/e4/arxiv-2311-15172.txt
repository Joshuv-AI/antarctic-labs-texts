Toward a density Corrádi–Hajnal theorem for degenerate hypergraphs
Jianfeng Hou, Caiyun Hu, Heng Li, Xizhi Liu, Caihong Yang, Yixiao Zhang

Abstract
Given an r r -graph F F with r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , let ex ⁡ ( n , ( t + 1 ) ​ F ) \mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)F) denote the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex r r -graph with at most t t pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of F F . Extending several old results and complementing prior work [ 32 ] on nondegenerate hypergraphs, we initiate a systematic study on ex ⁡ ( n , ( t + 1 ) ​ F ) \mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)F) for degenerate hypergraphs F F . For a broad class of degenerate hypergraphs F F , we present near-optimal upper bounds for ex ⁡ ( n , ( t + 1 ) ​ F ) \mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)F) when n n is sufficiently large and t t lies in intervals [ 0 , ε ⋅ ex ⁡ ( n , F ) n r − 1 ] \left[0,\frac{\varepsilon\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)}{n^{r-1}}\right] , [ ex ⁡ ( n , F ) ε ​ n r − 1 , ε ​ n ] \left[\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,F)}{\varepsilon n^{r-1}},\varepsilon n\right] , and [ ( 1 − ε ) ​ n v ⁡ ( F ) , n v ⁡ ( F ) ] \left[(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{v(F)},\frac{n}{v(F)}\right] , where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 is a constant depending only on F F . Our results reveal very different structures for extremal constructions across the three intervals, and we provide characterizations of extremal constructions within the first interval. Additionally, we characterize extremal constructions within the second interval for graphs. Our proof for the first interval also applies to a special class of nondegenerate hypergraphs, including those with undetermined Turán densities, partially improving a result in [ 32 ] .

The following simple inequalities will be useful.

Fact 2.1
.

Suppose that

and

\displaystyle\binom{n-t}{r}\leq\left(1-\frac{t}{n}\right)^{r}\binom{n}{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\binom{n}{r}\leq\left(\frac{n}{n-t-r}\right)^{r}\binom{n-t}{r}.

(
[

Suppose that

Fact 2.3
.

Suppose that

are integers satisfying

.
Then

\displaystyle\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-\ell-x}{2}\geq\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-\ell}{2}+x(n-\ell-x).

Fact 2.4
.

Suppose that

is an integer and

is a real number.
Then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{r}\leq 1+4rx.

Proof.

This is due to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{r}\leq(1+2x)^{r}\leq 1+2rx+\sum_{i=2}^{r}(2rx)^{i}\leq 1+2rx+2rx\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{i}\leq 1+4rx.

Fact 2.5
.

Suppose that
x\leq 1
and

are real numbers.
Then

\displaystyle(1-x)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq 1-\frac{x}{r}.

Proposition 2.8

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be an
m

\displaystyle t\leq\min\left\{\frac{\delta mn}{r-1}-r,\ \frac{n}{m}\right\}.

\displaystyle\left(1-\delta m\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-t-r}\right)^{r-1}\leq 1.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|

.

It follows from Proposition

Let

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{H}|-\left(\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-t}{r}\right)\geq\mathrm{ex}(n,F)\geq(1-c_{2}+\delta)\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F).

In this section, we prove Theorems

4.1
Graphs: proof of Theorem

Let us provide a concise overview of the proof strategy for Theorem

We will start with an extremal

Proof of Theorem

Let

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
s_{r}\geq\cdots\geq s_{1}\geq 2
be integers.
Let
s:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}
and
\mathbb{B}:=B_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r}
be an
r
-partite
r
-graph with part sizes
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
.
For every
\alpha>0
there exists
N_{0}
such that the following statement holds for all
n\geq N_{0}
and
t\leq\frac{\alpha n}{9s^{2}r!}
.
Every
n
-vertex
(t+1)\mathbb{B}
-free
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\leq\left(s_{1}t+\frac{17sr!}{\alpha}\right)\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r!}tn^{r-1}+\mathrm{ex}(n-st,\mathbb{B}).

If, in addition,
\Delta(\mathcal{H})\leq(1-\alpha)\binom{n-1}{r-1}
and
t\geq\frac{5}{\alpha s_{1}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}+\frac{18sr!}{\alpha}\right)
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\leq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s_{1}(t-1)}{r}.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha>0
and let
n
be sufficiently large.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a maximum
n
-vertex
(t+1)\mathbb{B}
-free
r
-graph.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbb{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{B}_{t}\}
be a collection of pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathcal{H}
.
Let

\displaystyle V:=V(\mathcal{H}),\ V_{1}:=\bigcup_{i\in[t]}V(\mathbb{B}_{i}),\ V_{2}:=V\setminus V_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad L:=\left\{v\in V_{1}\colon d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\geq\frac{\alpha s_{1}n^{r-1}}{2sr!}\right\}.

Claim 5.5
.

We have
|L|\leq\left(t+1+\frac{16sr!}{\alpha s_{1}}\right)s_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that this is not true.
Let

\displaystyle m:=\left\lceil\left(t+1+\frac{16sr!}{\alpha s_{1}}\right)s_{1}\right\rceil\leq 2\times\frac{\alpha n}{9s^{2}r!}\times s_{1}\leq\frac{\alpha s_{1}n}{4s^{2}r!}\leq\frac{\alpha(n-m)}{8s}.

We may assume that
|L|=m
, since otherwise we can replace
L
by a subset of size
m
.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be the collection of edges in
\mathcal{H}
that have exactly one vertex in
L
.
Notice that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is semibipartite and for every

, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(v)\geq d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)-|L|\binom{n-2}{r-2}\geq\frac{\alpha s_{1}n^{r-1}}{2sr!}-m\frac{r-1}{n-1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}\geq\frac{\alpha s_{1}n^{r-1}}{4sr!}.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.1
, the semibipartite
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains at least

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{m}{s_{1}}-4\left(\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{4sr!}\right)^{-1}\right\rfloor\geq\left\lfloor t+1+\frac{16sr!}{\alpha s_{1}}-\frac{16sr!}{\alpha s_{1}}\right\rfloor=t+1

copies of ordered
\mathbb{B}
, a contradiction.

It follows from Claim
5.5
and some simple calculations that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in L}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+\sum_{v\in V_{1}\setminus L}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+|\mathcal{H}[V_{2}]|

\displaystyle\leq\left(s_{1}t+s_{1}+\frac{16sr!}{\alpha}\right)\Delta(\mathcal{H})+st\times\frac{\alpha s_{1}n^{r-1}}{2sr!}+\mathrm{ex}(n-st,\mathbb{B})

\displaystyle\leq\left(s_{1}t+\frac{17sr!}{\alpha}\right)\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r!}tn^{r-1}+\mathrm{ex}(n-st,\mathbb{B}).

Assume additionally that
\Delta(\mathcal{H})\leq(1-\alpha)\binom{n-1}{r-1}
and
t\geq\frac{5}{\alpha s_{1}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}+\frac{18sr!}{\alpha}\right)
.
Then it follows from the inequality above that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(s_{1}t+\frac{17sr!}{\alpha}\right)(1-\alpha)\binom{n-1}{r-1}+\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r!}tn^{r-1}+\mathrm{ex}(n-st,\mathbb{B})

\displaystyle\leq s_{1}t\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\binom{n-1}{r-1}-s_{1}t\frac{\alpha}{2}\binom{n-1}{r-1}+\frac{17sr!}{\alpha}\binom{n-1}{r-1}+\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r!}tn^{r-1}+\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B}).

Since
n
is large and

, we have

\displaystyle-s_{1}t\frac{\alpha}{2}\binom{n-1}{r-1}+\frac{17sr!}{\alpha}\binom{n-1}{r-1}+\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r!}tn^{r-1}+\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2}t-\frac{18sr!}{\alpha}-\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{2r}t-o(1)-\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}\right)\binom{n-1}{r-1}-s_{1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\frac{\alpha s_{1}}{5}t-\frac{18sr!}{\alpha}-\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}\right)\binom{n-1}{r-1}-s_{1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}\leq-s_{1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}.

It follows
t\leq\frac{\alpha n}{9s^{2}r!}
, Facts
2.1
, and
2.4
that

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\binom{n-1}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-s_{1}t-r}\right)^{r-1}\binom{n-s_{1}t}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{4(r-1)(s_{1}t+r)}{n}\right)\binom{n-s_{1}t}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\binom{n-s_{1}t}{r-1}\leq\binom{n-s_{1}t}{r-1}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\leq s_{1}(t-1)\binom{n-s_{1}t}{r-1}+s_{1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}-s_{1}\binom{n-1}{r-1}\leq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s_{1}(t-1)}{r},

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
2.9

Theorem A.1

(Erdős
[
8
]
)
.

Suppose that
n\geq r\geq 3
and
s_{r}\geq\cdots\geq s_{1}\geq 1
are integers.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r})\leq\frac{(s_{2}+\ldots+s_{r}-r+1)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}}{r}n^{r-\frac{1}{s_{1}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+\frac{s_{1}-1}{r}\binom{n}{r-1}.

Proof.

We prove this theorem by induction on
r
.
The base case
r=2
is the Kővári–Sós–Turán Theorem.
So we may assume that
r\geq 3
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
K_{s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}}^{r}
-free
r
-graph on
n
vertices. Let
X
denote the number of copies of
K_{s_{1},1,\ldots,1}^{r}
in
\mathcal{H}
.
We use a standard double-counting argument to bound the number of edges in
\mathcal{H}
.
First, it follows from Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\binom{n}{r-1}}\binom{|N(T)|}{s_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n}{r-1}\binom{\sum_{T\in\binom{n}{r-1}}|N(T)|/\binom{n}{r-1}}{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r-1}\binom{r|\mathcal{H}|/\binom{n}{r-1}}{s_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{s_{1}!}\binom{n}{r-1}\left(\frac{r|\mathcal{H}|}{\binom{n}{r-1}}-(s_{1}-1)\right)^{s_{1}}.

(32)

From the other direction, notice that for every
S\in\binom{n}{s_{1}}
, the common link
L(S)
is
K_{s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r-1}
-free.
It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle|L_{\mathcal{H}}(S)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(s_{3}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+2)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+\frac{s_{2}-1}{r-1}\binom{n}{r-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}.

(33)

It follows that

\displaystyle X=\sum_{S\in\binom{n}{s_{1}}}|L(S)|\leq\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}\binom{n}{s_{1}}.

(34)

Combining (
A
) and (
34
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}\frac{n^{s_{1}}}{\binom{n}{r-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\binom{n}{r-1}+\frac{s_{1}-1}{r}\binom{n}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}}{r}n^{r-\frac{1}{s_{1}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+\frac{s_{1}-1}{r}\binom{n}{r-1},

completing the induction.

Appendix B
Proof of Proposition
2.11

Proposition B.1
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
,
s_{r}\geq\cdots\geq s_{1}\geq 1
, and
m,n\geq 1
are integers.
Then

\displaystyle Z(m,n,s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq\frac{\left(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}}{r-1}mn^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{1}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+(s_{1}-1)\binom{n}{r-1}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
m
by
n
semibipartite
r
-graph on
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
with
|V_{1}|=m
and
|V_{2}|=n
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
does not contain a copy of
K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}=K[W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}]
with
W_{1}
contained in
V_{1}
and
W_{2},\ldots,W_{r}
contained in
V_{2}
.
We use a standard double-counting argument to bound the size of
\mathcal{H}
.
Let

\displaystyle X:=\sum_{T\in\binom{V_{2}}{r-1}}\binom{|N(T)|}{s_{1}}=\sum_{S\in\binom{V_{1}}{s_{1}}}|L(S)|.

First, it follows from Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n}{r-1}\binom{\sum_{T\in\binom{V_{2}}{r-1}}|N(T)|/\binom{n}{r-1}}{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r-1}\binom{|\mathcal{H}|/\binom{n}{r-1}}{s_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{s_{1}!}\binom{n}{r-1}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{\binom{n}{r-1}}-(s_{1}-1)\right)^{s_{1}}.

(35)

From the other direction, notice that for every
S\in\binom{V_{1}}{s_{1}}
, the common link
L_{\mathcal{H}}(S)\subset\binom{V_{2}}{r-1}
is
K_{s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r-1}
-free.
So it follows from (
A
) that

\displaystyle|L_{\mathcal{H}}(S)|\leq\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\leq\binom{m}{s_{1}}\cdot\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{s_{1}!}\frac{s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1}{r-1}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}m^{s_{1}}.

(36)

Combining (
B
) and (
B
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1)m^{s_{1}}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}}{(r-1)\binom{n}{r-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\binom{n}{r-1}+(s_{1}-1)\binom{n}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1)m^{s_{1}}n^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{2}\cdots s_{r-1}}}}{r-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\frac{n^{r-1}}{(r-1)!}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{s_{1}}}+(s_{1}-1)\binom{n}{r-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}}{r-1}mn^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{1}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+(s_{1}-1)\binom{n}{r-1},

completing the proof of Proposition
2.11
.

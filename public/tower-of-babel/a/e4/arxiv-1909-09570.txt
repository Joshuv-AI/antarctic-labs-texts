Toric G G -Fano threefolds
Arman Sarikyan

Abstract
. We classify toric Fano threefolds having at worst terminal singularities such that the rank of the G G -invariant part of the class group equals one, where G G is a finite group acting on the variety by automorphisms.

2.2.
Divisors on the Toric Varieties.

Denote by
\Sigma(n)
the set of
n
-dimensional subcones of
\Sigma
. Then every one dimensional subcone (ray)

is associated with a
T
-invariant Weil divisor

. Moreover, every Weil divisor on

is linearly equivalent to some

-invariant divisor. Thus,

\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})=\bigoplus\limits_{r\in\Sigma(1)}\mathbb{Z}D_{r}.

2.4.
Toric Fano Varieties.

Definition 2.5
.

A convex lattice polytope
P\subset N_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a
Fano polytope
if
O\in\Int(P)
, where
O
is the origin of
N
, and the vertices of
P
are all primitive elements of
N
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
Kas06b
, Definition 3.7.2]
)
.

Let
P
be an
n
-dimensional Fano polytope.

(1)

If the vertices of any facet of
P
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
, then
P
is called a
regular
(or smooth) Fano polytope;

(2)

If every facet of
P
is an
(n-1)
-simplex, then
P
is called a
simplicial
(or
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial) Fano polytope;

(3)

If the dual polytope
P^{*}\subset M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is also a lattice polytope, then
P
is called a
reflexive
(or Gorenstein) Fano polytope;

(4)

If the only lattice points on or in
P
consist of the vertices and the origin, then
P
is called a
terminal
Fano polytope;

(5)

If the only interior lattice point of
P
is the origin, then
P
is called a
canonical
Fano polytope.

There is a correspondence between isomorphism classes of toric Fano varieties and isomorphism classes of Fano polytopes
[
Kas06b
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Up to the action of
\GL(2,\mathbb{Z})
there are exactly two convex lattice polygons
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that there are no lattice points on the boundary of
V
except the vertices and there are no interior lattice points. These are a triangle and a convex quadrangle.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the points
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
are consecutive vertices of
V
. Suppose
V
is not a triangle. Then the point
(1,1)
must be the vertex of
V
, otherwise, due to the convexity, it would be an interior point of
V
. Hence, again due to the convexity of
V
, the consecutive vertex must lie on the line
(0,n)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. But there no lattice points on the boundary of
V
, thereby
n=1
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

If
P
is a
3
-dimensional terminal Fano polytope, then
P
can have only triangle or quadrangle facets.

3.
Toric G-Fano Threefolds.

From now on, let
P
be a
3
-dimensional terminal Fano polytope and
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{3}
(hence
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{3}
). Let also
\Sigma
be a fan associated with
P
and
X_{\Sigma}
be the associated with
\Sigma
toric
G
-Fano threefold.

Proposition 3.1
.

In the notation above,
\rk\Cl(X_{\Sigma})^{G}=1
if and only if there exists a subgroup
H\subset N(T)
such that
\rk\Cl(X_{\Sigma})^{H}=1
, where
N(T)
is the normalizer of
T
in
\Aut(X_{\Sigma})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a set of maximal tori in
\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
, where
\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
is the connected identity component of
\Aut(X_{\Sigma})
. The group
\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
acts on
\mathfrak{M}
by conjugation and this action is transitive since
\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
is connected by
[
Bor91
, Corollary 11.3]
. Thereby, for each element
g\in G
there exists an element
h\in\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
such that
ghT(gh)^{-1}=T
. Now denote by
H
the group generated by all elements
gh
. Since the action of
\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
on
\Cl(X_{\Sigma})
is trivial and the images of
G
and
H
coincide in
\Aut(X_{\Sigma})/\Aut^{0}(X_{\Sigma})
, we are done. ∎

Consequently, we can assume that
G
is a subgroup of
N(T)
. Also, for
N(T)
we have the following split exact sequence:

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>T@>{}>{}>N(T)@>{\nu}>{}>W@>{}>{}>1,\end{CD}

where
W
is a finite subgroup of
\GL(3,\mathbb{Z})
, known as the Weyl group of
X_{\Sigma}
. Since the action of
G
on
\Cl(X_{\Sigma})
depends only on the image of
\nu
, we will assume that
G
is a subgroup of
W
.

Consider the exact sequence of vector spaces obtained from (
2.3.1
) by tensoring by
\mathbb{Q}
:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>M\otimes\mathbb{Q}@>{}>{}>\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{Q}@>{}>{}>\Cl(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{Q}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Taking the
G
-invariants gives us an isomorphism

\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}/M_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}\cong\Cl(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.
We prove it by considering the terminal Fano polytopes that correspond to the toric terminal Fano threefolds. The proof will be divided into four lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\dim\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}=4
,
\dim M_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}=3
. Then
X_{\Sigma}
is one of the following:

1)

\mathbb{P}^{3}
;

2)

the quotient
\mathbb{P}^{3}/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{5}
with weights
(1,2,3,4)
;

2)

the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(w_{0},\dots,w_{3})
with weights

(1,1,1,2),\,(1,1,2,3),\,(1,2,3,5),\,(1,3,4,5),\,(2,3,5,7),\,(3,4,5,7);

and
G
acts trivially.

Proof.

By assumption the group
G
has three invariant
1
-dimensional subspaces in
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
, hence it acts trivially on
\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Consequently, there are exactly four rays in

\rk\Cl(X_{\Sigma})=1
, so we are done by Theorem

∎

From now on, we will assume that

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\dim\Div_{T}(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}=2
,
\dim M_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G}=1
and
\rk\Cl(X_{\Sigma})>1
. Then

is one of the following:

1)

the quadric cone

and

;

2)

the terminal toric Fano threefold of degree

and

3)

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and

;

4)

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}
and

, where
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}
is generated by the involution that acts diagonally and faithfully on each factor;

5)

the complete intersection of two quadrics

Proof.

By assumption the group

has one invariant

and the vertices of

. Denote by

3.10 Case:

F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are the triangles.

In this case

is a triangle prism or a triangle antiprism. The case of triangle prism was considered above.

Let
P
be the triangle antiprism, which is the same as octahedron. Then
G
cyclicly permutes all vertices of each facet, so must be isomorphic to
\mathfrak{C}_{3}
and
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are equilateral lattice triangles, with respect to the combinatorial lattice distance. By
[

, Proposition 3]
, up to conjugation, there are only two subgroups of order

in
\GL(3,\mathbb{Z})
:

W_{1}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&1&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle,\ W_{2}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle.

Consider the following subcases.

3.10.1 Subcase:

G=W_{1}
.

Then the facets are contained in the planes of the form
x=n
for some integer
n
. So, the vertices of the facet have the following coordinates:

(n,b,c),\,(n,-c,b-c),\,(n,-b+c,-b),

where
b
and
c
are some integers. Thus, on the one hand, the area of the facet is equal to

\dfrac{1}{2}\left|3b^{2}-3bc+3c^{2}\right|

but on the other hand, it should be equal to
1/2
which is absurd.

3.10.2 Subcase:

G=W_{2}
.

Then the centers of the facets are on the line generated by the vector
(1,1,1)
, and also
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are contained in the planes of the form
x+y+z=n
for

. So, the vertices of the facet are

(a,b,n-a-b),\,(b,n-a-b,a),\,(n-a-b,a,b).

Hence, we have the following equation for the area of the facet

\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left|3a^{2}+3b^{2}+3ab-3an-3bn+n^{2}\right|=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.

Straightforward computations show that we have only the following solutions:

•

n=2,\ a=1,\,b=1
;

•

n=1,\,a=1,\,b=0
;

•

n=-1,\,a=-1,\,b=0
;

•

n=-2,\,a=-1,\,b=-1
.

Forming from the triangles defined by the solutions above the triangle antiprisms we get the following:

•

the polytope with the vertices

\pm(1,0,0),\,\pm(0,1,0),\,\pm(0,0,1),

which determines
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
(number

in
[

]
);

•

the polytope with the vertices

\pm(1,1,0),\,\pm(1,0,1),\,\pm(0,1,1),

which determines

(number

in
[
BK
]
).

3.11 Case:

F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are quadrangles.

In this case
P
is a quadrangle prism or a quadrangle antiprism. But there are no terminal Fano quadrangle antiprisms by
[
BK
]
. Let
P
be terminal Fano quadrangle prism. By
[

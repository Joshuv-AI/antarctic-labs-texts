Abstract

Given the definition of a circle (sphere) by the vertices of P n ​ ( T n ) P_{n}(T_{n}) and on the base of the cyclic averages are established the common metrical relations of P n ​ ( T n ) P_{n}(T_{n}) . 6
Sphere as Locus of Constant
\sum_{[R,L]}

For regular polygons with different vertices the number of the
\sum_{[R,L]}

\sum_{[R,L]}

\displaystyle\text{regular tetrahedron -- }\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(2)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(4)};

\displaystyle\text{octahedron and cube -- }\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(2)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(4)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(6)};

\displaystyle\text{icosahedron and dodecahedron -- }\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(2)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(4)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(6)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(8)},\;\;\sum\nolimits_{[R,L]}^{(10)}.

To prove these, we consider each Platonic solid separately. In all cases, we consider solids centered at the origin and use simple Cartesian coordinates.

6.1
Regular Tetrahedron

The coordinates of the vertices
T_{4}(R)
:

A_{1,2}(c,\pm c,\pm c),\;\;A_{3,4}(-c,\pm c,\mp c)\;\;\text{and}\;\;R=\sqrt{3}\,c.

Consider an arbitrary point in space
M(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4},L)
with the coordinates:
(x,y,z)
. The distance between
M
and the centroid
O
of the tetrahedron:

L^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d_{1,2}^{2}&=(x-c)^{2}+(y\mp c)^{2}+(z\mp c)^{2}=R^{2}+L^{2}+2c(-x\mp y\mp z),\\[5.69046pt]
d_{3,4}^{2}&=(x+c)^{2}+(y\mp c)^{2}+(z\pm c)^{2}=R^{2}+L^{2}+2c(x\mp y\pm z),\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{1}^{4}d_{i}^{4}&=\big(R^{2}+L^{2}+2c(-x\mp y\mp z)\big)^{2}+\big(R^{2}+L^{2}+2c(x\mp y\pm z)\big)^{2}\\[5.69046pt]
&=4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}+4c^{2}\big((x+y+z)^{2}+(-x+y+z)^{2}+(x-y+z)^{2}+(x+y-z)^{2}\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=4\Big((R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\,R^{2}L^{2}\Big).\end{aligned}

-

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{12}d_{i}^{6}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1}^{4}d_{i}^{6}+\sum_{5}^{8}d_{i}^{6}+\sum_{9}^{12}d_{i}^{6}

\displaystyle=4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+48c^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})(y^{2}+z^{2}\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;\,+4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+48c^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})(z^{2}+x^{2}\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle=12\big((R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+4R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})\big).

For the sum of the eighth power

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{4}d_{i}^{8}

\displaystyle=4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{4}+96c^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}(y^{2}+z^{2}\varphi^{2})+64c^{4}(y^{4}+z^{4}\varphi^{4}+6y^{2}z^{2}\varphi^{2}),

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{12}d_{i}^{8}

\displaystyle=12(R^{2}+L^{2})^{4}+96c^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})(1+\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+64c^{4}\big((x^{4}+y^{4}+z^{4})(1+\varphi^{4})+6(x^{2}y^{2}+x^{2}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{2})\varphi^{2}\big),

Because

\displaystyle\qquad 1+\varphi^{4}=3\varphi^{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\varphi^{2}=\frac{1}{5}\,(1+\varphi^{2})^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{12}d_{i}^{8}

\displaystyle=12\Big((R^{2}+L^{2})^{4}+8R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}+\frac{16}{5}\,R^{4}L^{4}\Big).

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{4}d_{i}^{10}

\displaystyle=4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{5}+80(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}c^{2}\big((y+z\varphi)^{2}+(y-z\varphi)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;\,+160(R^{2}+L^{2})c^{4}\big((y+z\varphi)^{4}+(y-z\varphi)^{4}\big)

\displaystyle=4(R^{2}+L^{2})^{5}+160(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}c^{2}(y^{2}+z^{2}\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;\,+320(R^{2}+L^{2})c^{4}(y^{4}+z^{4}\varphi^{4}+6y^{2}z^{2}\varphi^{2}).

\displaystyle\sum_{1}^{12}d_{i}^{10}

\displaystyle=12(R^{2}+L^{2})^{5}+160(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}c^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})(1+\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+320(R^{2}+L^{2})c^{4}\big((1+\varphi^{4})(x^{4}+y^{4}+z^{4})+3\varphi^{2}(2x^{2}y^{2}+2x^{2}z^{2}+2y^{2}z^{2})\big)

\displaystyle=12\Big((R^{2}+L^{2})^{5}+\frac{40}{3}\,R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+16R^{4}L^{4}(R^{2}+L^{2})\Big).

7
Cyclic Averages of Platonic Solids

Summarize the obtained results, in terms of the cyclic averages:

Theorem 7.1
.

The cyclic averages of the Platonic solids are the following:

\displaystyle S_{[4]}^{(2)}=S_{[6]}^{(2)}=S_{[8]}^{(2)}=S_{[12]}^{(2)}=S_{[20]}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=R^{2}+L^{2},

\displaystyle S_{[4]}^{(4)}=S_{[6]}^{(4)}=S_{[8]}^{(4)}=S_{[12]}^{(4)}=S_{[20]}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\,R^{2}L^{2},

\displaystyle S_{[6]}^{(6)}=S_{[8]}^{(6)}=S_{[12]}^{(6)}=S_{[20]}^{(6)}

\displaystyle=(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+4R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2}),

\displaystyle S_{[12]}^{(8)}=S_{[20]}^{(8)}

\displaystyle=(R^{2}+L^{2})^{4}+8R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{2}+\frac{16}{5}\,R^{4}L^{4},

\displaystyle S_{[12]}^{(10)}=S_{[20]}^{(10)}

\displaystyle=(R^{2}+L^{2})^{5}+\frac{40}{3}\,R^{2}L^{2}(R^{2}+L^{2})^{3}+16R^{4}L^{4}(R^{2}+L^{2}).

Eliminate
L
and
R
from the relations, we obtain direct relations among the cyclic averages of the Platonic solids.

Theorem 7.2
.

For each Platonic solid
(n=4,6,8,12,20)
:

S_{[n]}^{(4)}+\frac{16}{9}\,R^{4}=\Big(S_{[n]}^{(2)}+\frac{2}{3}\,R^{2}\Big)^{2}.

This result for regular simplicial and regular polytopic distances is obtained in
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
, respectively.

Theorem 7.3
.

For each Platonic solid, except the tetrahedron
(n=6,8,12,20)
:

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(6)}

\displaystyle=S_{[n]}^{(2)}\big((S_{[n]}^{(2)}+2R^{2})^{2}-8R^{4}\big),

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(6)}

\displaystyle=S_{[n]}^{(2)}\big(3S_{[n]}^{(4)}-2(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{2}\big).

Theorem 7.4
.

For the icosahedron and the dodecahedron
(n=12,20)
:

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(8)}-(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{4}

\displaystyle=8R^{2}(S_{[n]}^{(2)}-R^{2})\Big((S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{2}+\frac{2}{5}\,R^{2}(S_{[n]}^{(2)}-R^{2})\Big),

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(10)}-(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{5}

\displaystyle=8R^{2}S_{[n]}^{(2)}(S_{[n]}^{(2)}-R^{2})\Big(\frac{5}{3}\,(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{2}+2R^{2}(S_{[n]}^{(2)}-R^{2})\Big),

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(8)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\,\Big(9(S_{[n]}^{(4)})^{2}+12S_{[n]}^{(4)}(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{2}-16(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{4}\Big),

\displaystyle S_{[n]}^{(10)}

\displaystyle=S_{[n]}^{(2)}S_{[n]}^{(4)}\big(9S_{[n]}^{(4)}-8(S_{[n]}^{(2)})^{2}\big).

8
Conclusion

In the present paper, we introduce the
\sum_{[R,L]}
sums and define the cyclic averages of the regular polygons and the Platonic solids. We prove the main property of the cyclic averages – the equality of them for various regular polygons and Platonic solids. By means of the cyclic averages the distances of an arbitrary point to the vertices of the regular polygons (the plane case) and the Platonic solids (the space case) are investigated. All cases of constant sum of like powers of the distances, when the locus is a circle (a sphere), are found. General metrical relations for regular polygons (Platonic solids), which were known in special cases only, are established. Rational distances problem solved for the
n=24
case.

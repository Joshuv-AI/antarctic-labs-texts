Quantum oscillations with topological phases in a kagome metal CsTi 3 Bi 5
Yongkang Li, Hengxin Tan, Binghai Yan

Abstract
Quantum oscillations can reveal Fermi surfaces and their topology in solids and provide a powerful tool for understanding transport and electronic properties. It is well established that the oscillation frequency maps the Fermi surface area by Onsager’s relation. However, the topological phase accumulated along the quantum orbit remains difficult to estimate in calculations, because it includes multiple contributions from the Berry phase, orbital and spin moments, and also becomes gauge-sensitive for degenerate states. In this work, we develop a gauge-independent Wilson loop scheme to evaluate all topological phase contributions and apply it to CsTi 3 Bi 5 , an emerging kagome metal. We find that the spin-orbit coupling dramatically alters the topological phase compared to the spinless case. Especially, oscillation phases of representative quantum orbits demonstrate a strong 3D signature despite their cylinder-like Fermi surface geometry. Our work reveals the Fermi surface topology of CsTi 3 Bi 5 and paves the way for the theoretical investigation of quantum oscillations in realistic materials.

IV
Conclusion

We theoretically studied the quantum oscillations by revealing their frequencies and topological phases through a Wilson loop method in CsTi
3
Bi
5
.
We revealed three quantum orbits with
\theta=\pi
phase shift.
Despite most Fermi surfaces are quasi-2D,
the dimensional-related phase
\delta
, beyond the angle-dependent frequency, clearly indicates their 3D nature.
Our method can be applied to other quantum materials and provides a general way to study quantum oscillations assisted by first-principles calculations.

Appendix

The most general gauge transformation is a
U(D)
basis transformation among the degenerate bands

\displaystyle\left|u_{n\bm{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{m=1}^{D}U(\bm{k})_{mn}\left|u_{m\bm{k}}\right\rangle,\quad U^{-1}=U^{\dagger},

(18)

It has already been shown that the propagator
\mathcal{A}[\mathfrak{o}]
is gauge covariant under such transformation
Alexandradinata and Glazman 2018
provided that the same wave function is used at the initial point and the final point, i.e.
|u(\bm{k}_{N+1})\rangle=|u(\bm{k}_{1})\rangle
. Here we use the same way to show our numerical formula inherits this property so it’s appropriate for calculation.

First, covariant derivatives transform as states under the
U(D)
gauge transformation

\displaystyle\left|\overline{u}_{n,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left(U(\bm{k})^{\dagger}S_{\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}U(\bm{k}+\bm{q})\right)^{-1}_{n^{\prime}n}\left|u_{n^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n^{\prime},m,l,m^{\prime}}U(\bm{k}+\bm{q})^{-1}_{n^{\prime}m}(S_{\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{-1})_{ml}U(\bm{k})_{ln}

\displaystyle U(\bm{k}+\bm{q})_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\left|u_{m^{\prime},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,l}(S_{\bm{k},\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{-1})_{ml}U(\bm{k})_{ln}\left|u_{m,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}U(\bm{k})_{ln}\left|\overline{u}_{l,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\right\rangle

which makes the covariant derivative expression of Roth term (
13
) transform covariantly. This is also true for the matrix elements expression of Roth term (
6
) and spin matrix
\sigma_{z}
, meaning that

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})_{mn}\cdot d\bm{k}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U(\bm{k}_{i})^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})_{mn}\cdot d\bm{k}_{i}U(\bm{k}_{i})

(20)

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}(\bm{k}_{i})_{mn}\cdot d\bm{k}_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow U(\bm{k}_{i})^{-1}\sigma_{z}(\bm{k}_{i})_{mn}\cdot d\bm{k}_{i}U(\bm{k}_{i})

Therefore, the second term in (
8
) is also gauge covariant

\displaystyle\mathrm{exp}\left[i\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot d\bm{k_{i}}+iZ\frac{\sigma^{z}}{v^{\perp}}|d\bm{k}_{i}|\right]

(21)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{exp}\left\{iU(\bm{k}_{i})^{-1}\left[\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot d\bm{k_{i}}+Z\frac{\sigma^{z}}{v^{\perp}}|d\bm{k}_{i}|\right]U(\bm{k}_{i})\right\}

\displaystyle=U(\bm{k}_{i})^{-1}\mathrm{exp}\left[i\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot d\bm{k_{i}}+iZ\frac{\sigma^{z}}{v^{\perp}}|d\bm{k}_{i}|\right]U(\bm{k}_{i})

Besides, the overlap matrix
M^{i}_{mn}=\left\langle u_{m\bm{k_{i+1}}}|u_{n\bm{k_{i}}}\right\rangle
transforms like

\displaystyle M^{i}\rightarrow U(\bm{k}_{i+1})^{-1}M^{i}U(\bm{k}_{i})

(22)

Hence, the covariance of discretized propagator (
8
) follows from the transformation properties of the two separate terms as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{A}[\mathfrak{o}]&\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{N}U(\bm{k}_{i+1})^{-1}M^{i}U(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot U(\bm{k}_{i})^{-1}\\
&\qquad\mathrm{exp}\left[i\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot d\bm{k_{i}}+iZ\frac{\sigma^{z}}{v^{\perp}}|d\bm{k}_{i}|\right]U(\bm{k}_{i})\\
&=U(\bm{k}_{N+1})^{-1}\\
&\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{N}M^{i}\cdot\mathrm{exp}\left[i\bm{\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k}_{i})\cdot d\bm{k_{i}}+iZ\frac{\sigma^{z}}{v^{\perp}}|d\bm{k}_{i}|\right]\right\}U(\bm{k}_{1})\\
&=U(\bm{k}_{1})^{-1}\mathcal{A}[\mathfrak{o}]U(\bm{k}_{1})\end{aligned}

(23)

Since propagator
\mathcal{A}[\mathfrak{o}]
transforms covariantly, its spectrum
\{e^{i\lambda_{a}}\}_{a=1}^{D}
is gauge invariant. In other words, the phase
\lambda_{a}
obtained through these numerical formulas is uniquely determined (module
2\pi
) independent of gauge choice in the calculation.

GENERALIZED FUSION FRAMES IN HILBERT SPACES
Vahid Sadri, GHOLAMREZA RAHIMLOU, Reza Ahmadi, Ramazan Zarghami Farfar

Abstract
. After introducing g-frames and fusion frames by Sun and Casazza, combining these frames together is an interesting topic for research. In this paper, we introduce the generalized fusion frames or g-fusion frames for Hilbert spaces and give characterizations of these frames from the viewpoint of closed range and g-fusion frame sequences. Also, the canonical dual g-fusion frames are presented and we introduce Parseval g-fusion frames.

Primary 42C15; Secondary 46C99, 41A58

1.
Introduction

During the past few years, the theory of frames have been growing
rapidly and new topics about them are discovered almost every year.
For example, generalized frames (or g-frames), subspaces of frames
(or fusion frames), continuous frames (or c-frames),
k
-frames,
controlled frames and the combination of each two of them, lead
to c-fusion frames, g-c-frames, c-g-frames, c
k
-frames,
c
k
-fusion frames and etc. The purpose of this paper is to
introduce and review some of the generalized fusion frames (or g-fusion
frames) and their operators. Then, we will get some useful
propositions about these frames and finally, we will study g-fusion
frame sequences.

Throughout this paper,
H
and
K
are separable Hilbert spaces and
\mathcal{B}(H,K)
is the collection of all the bounded linear operators of
H
into
K
. If
K=H
, then
\mathcal{B}(H,H)
will be denoted by
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Also,
\pi_{V}
is the orthogonal projection from
H
onto a closed subspace
V\subset H
and
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
is a sequence of Hilbert spaces where
\mathbb{J}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
. It is easy to check that if
u\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is an invertible operator, then (
[
11
]
)

\pi_{uV}u\pi_{V}=u\pi_{V}.

Definition 1
.

(frame)
.
Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a sequence of members of
H
. We say that
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
is a frame for
H
if there exists
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each
f\in H

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}|\langle f,f_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

Definition 2
.

(g-frame)

A family
\{\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
is called a g-frame for
H
with respect to
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
, if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

(1)

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|\Lambda_{j}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},\ \ f\in H.

Definition 3
.

(fusion frame)
.
Let
\{W_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of closed subspaces of
H
and
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of weights (i.e.
v_{j}>0
for any
j\in\mathbb{J}
). We say that
(W_{j},v_{j})
is a fusion frame for
H
if there exists
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each
f\in H

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

If an operator
u
has closed range, then there exists a right-inverse operator
u^{\dagger}
(pseudo-inverse of
u
) in the following sences (see
[
6
]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{B}(K,H)
be a bounded operator with closed range
\mathcal{R}_{u}
. Then there exists a bounded operator
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{B}(H,K)
for which

uu^{\dagger}x=x,\ \ x\in\mathcal{R}_{u}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{B}(K,H)
. Then the following assertions holds:

(1)

\mathcal{R}_{u}
is closed in
H
if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{u^{\ast}}
is closed in
K
.

(2)

(u^{\ast})^{\dagger}=(u^{\dagger})^{\ast}
.

(3)

The orthogonal projection of
H
onto
\mathcal{R}_{u}
is given by
uu^{\dagger}
.

(4)

The orthogonal projection of
K
onto
\mathcal{R}_{u^{\dagger}}
is given by
u^{\dagger}u
.

(5)

\mathcal{N}_{{u}^{\dagger}}=\mathcal{R}^{\bot}_{u}
and
\mathcal{R}_{u^{\dagger}}=\mathcal{N}^{\bot}_{u}
.

2.
Generalized Fusion Frames and Their Operators

We define the space
\mathscr{H}_{2}:=(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\oplus H_{j})_{\ell_{2}}
by

(2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2}=\big\{\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\ :\ f_{j}\in H_{j},\ \sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}<\infty\big\}.

with the inner product defined by

\langle\{f_{j}\},\{g_{j}\}\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle.

It is clear that
\mathscr{H}_{2}
is a Hilbert space with pointwise operations.

Definition 4
.

Let
W=\{W_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of closed subspaces of
H
,
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of weights, i.e.
v_{j}>0
and
\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{j})
for each
j\in\mathbb{J}
. We say
\Lambda:=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
is a generalized fusion frame (or g-fusion frame ) for
H
if there exists
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each
f\in H

(3)

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

We call
\Lambda
a
Parseval g-fusion frame
if
A=B=1
. When the right hand of (
3
) holds,
\Lambda
is called a
g-fusion Bessel sequence
for
H
with bound
B
. If
H_{j}=H
for all
j\in\mathbb{J}
and
\Lambda_{j}=I_{H}
, then we get the fusion frame
(W_{j},v_{j})
for
H
. Throughout this paper,
\Lambda
will be a triple
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
with
j\in\mathbb{J}
unless otherwise stated.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
with bound
B
. Then for each sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
, the series
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}
converges unconditionally.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{I}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{J}
, then

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|=1}\big|\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j},g\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\|g\|=1}\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}g\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{B}\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty

and it follows that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}
is unconditionally convergent in
H
(see
[
7
]
page 58).
∎

Now, we can define the
synthesis operator
by Proposition
1
.

Definition 5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame for
H
. Then, the synthesis operator for
\Lambda
is the operator

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

defined by

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}.

We say the adjoint
T_{\Lambda}^{*}
of the synthesis operator the
analysis operator
and it is defined
by the following Proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame for
H
. Then, the analysis operator

T_{\Lambda}^{*}:H\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}_{2}

is given by

T_{\Lambda}^{*}(f)=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}.

Proof.

If
f\in H
and
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle T_{\Lambda}^{*}(f),\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,T_{\Lambda}\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}g_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\langle\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,g_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}},\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\rangle.

∎

Theorem 2.1
.

The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
with bound
B
.

(2)

The operator

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

is a well-defined and bounded operator with
\|T_{\lambda}\|\leq\sqrt{B}
.

(3)

The series

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

converges for all
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
.

Proof.

\textit{(1)}\Rightarrow\textit{(2)}
. It is clear by Proposition
1
.

\textit{(2)}\Rightarrow\textit{(1)}
. Suppose that
T_{\Lambda}
is a well-defined and bounded operator with
\|T_{\lambda}\|\leq\sqrt{B}
. Let
\mathbb{I}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{J}
and
f\in H
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\langle\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\Lambda}\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{I}},f\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\Lambda}\|\|v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|\|f\|

\displaystyle=\|T_{\Lambda}\|\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq\|T_{\Lambda}\|^{2}\|f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

\textit{(1)}\Rightarrow\textit{(3)}
. It is clear.

\textit{(3)}\Rightarrow\textit{(1)}
. Suppose that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}
converges for all
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
. We define

\displaystyle T:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}.

Then,
T
is well-defined. Let for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle T_{n}:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{n}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}.

Let
B_{n}:=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Since,
\|T_{n}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})\|\leq B_{n}\|f_{j}\{\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|
, then
\{T_{n}\}
is a sequence of bounded linear operators which converges pointwise to
T
. Hence, by the Banach-Steinhaus Theorem,
T
is a bounded operator with

\|T\|\leq\lim\inf\|T_{n}\|.

So, by Theorem
2.1
,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
with bound
B
if and only if for each finite subset
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{J}
and
f_{j}\in H_{j}

\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}f_{j}\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.1
and the proof of Proposition
1
.
∎

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame for
H
. The
g-fusion frame operator
is defined by

\displaystyle S_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:H\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle S_{\Lambda}f

\displaystyle=T_{\Lambda}T^{*}_{\Lambda}f.

Now, for each
f\in H
we have

S_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f

and

\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}.

Therefore,

AI\leq S_{\Lambda}\leq BI.

This means that
S_{\Lambda}
is a bounded, positive and invertible operator (with adjoint inverse). So, we have the reconstruction formula for any
f\in H
:

(4)

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

Example 1
.

We introduce a Parseval g-fusion frame for
H
by the g-fusion frame operator. Assume that
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
is a g-fusion frame for
H
. Since
S_{\Lambda}
(or
S_{\Lambda}^{-1}
) is positive in
\mathcal{B}(H)
and
\mathcal{B}(H)
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then there exist a unique positive square root
S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}
(or
S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}
) and they commute with
S_{\Lambda}
and
S_{\Lambda}^{-1}
. Therefore, each
f\in H
can be written

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}S_{\Lambda}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}f.

This implies that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}\pi_{S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}W_{j}}f\|^{2},

this means that
(S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda}W_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\Lambda},v_{j})
is a Parseval g-fusion frame.

Theorem 2.2
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
if and only if

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

is a well-defined, bounded and surjective.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
, the operator
S_{\Lambda}
is invertible. Thus,
T_{\Lambda}
is surjective. Conversely, let
T_{\Lambda}
be a well-defined, bounded and surjective. Then, by Theorem
2.1
,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
. So,
T^{*}_{\Lambda}f=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
for all
f\in H
. Since
T_{\Lambda}
is surjective, by Lemma
1
, there exists an operator
T^{\dagger}_{\Lambda}:H\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{2}
such that
(T^{\dagger}_{\Lambda})^{*}T^{*}_{\Lambda}=I_{H}
. Now, for each
f\in H
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(T^{\dagger}_{\Lambda})^{*}\|^{2}\|T^{*}_{\Lambda}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|T^{\dagger}_{\Lambda}\|^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
with lower g-fusion frame bound
\|T^{\dagger}\|^{-2}
and upper g-fusion frame
\|T_{\Lambda}\|^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
if and only if the operator

S_{\Lambda}:f\longrightarrow\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f

is a well-defined, bounded and surjective.

Proof.

The necessity of the statement is clear. Let
S_{\Lambda}
be a well-defined, bounded and surjective operator. Since
\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle\geq 0
for all
f\in H
, so
S_{\Lambda}
is positive. Then

\ker S_{\Lambda}=(\mathcal{R}_{S^{*}_{\Lambda}})^{\dagger}=(\mathcal{R}_{S_{\Lambda}})^{\dagger}=\{0\}

thus,
S_{\Lambda}
is injective. Therefore,
S_{\Lambda}
is invertible. Thus,
0\notin\sigma(S_{\Lambda})
. Let
C:=\inf_{\|f\|=1}\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle
. By Proposition 70.8 in
[
13
]
, we have
C\in\sigma(S_{\Lambda})
. So
C>0
. Now, we can write for each
f\in H

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}=\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle\geq C\|f\|^{2}

and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}=\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle\leq\|S_{\Lambda}\|\|f\|^{2}.

It follows that
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Lambda:=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
and
\Theta:=(W_{j},\Theta_{j},v_{j})
be two g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
with bounds
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, respectively. Suppose that
T_{\Lambda}
and
T_{\Theta}
be their analysis operators such that
T_{\Theta}T^{*}_{\Lambda}=I_{H}
. Then, both
\Lambda
and
\Theta
are g-fusion frames.

Proof.

For each
f\in H
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{4}

\displaystyle=\langle f,f\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle T^{*}_{\Lambda}f,T^{*}_{\Theta}f\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|T^{*}_{\Lambda}f\|^{2}\|T^{*}_{\Theta}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)B_{2}\|f\|^{2},

thus,
B_{2}^{-1}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}
. This means that
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
. Similarly,
\Theta
is a g-fusion frame with the lower bound
B_{1}^{-1}
.
∎

3.
Dual g-Fusion Frames

For definition of the dual g-fusion frames, we need the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
be a g-fusion frame for
H
. Then
(S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1},v_{j})
is a g-fusion frame for
H
.

Proof.

Let
A,B
be the g-fusion frame bounds of
\Lambda
and
f\in H
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\pi_{S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\|S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\|^{2}\|f\|^{2}.

Now, to get the lower bound, by using (
4
) we can write

\displaystyle\|f\|^{4}

\displaystyle=\big|\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{J}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f,\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f\|^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\pi_{S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big(B\|f\|^{2}\big),

therefore

\displaystyle B^{-1}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\pi_{S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}}f\|^{2}.

∎

Now, by Theorem
3.1
,
\tilde{\Lambda}=(S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1},v_{j})
is a g-fusion frame for
H
. Then,
\tilde{\Lambda}
is called the
(canonical) dual g-fusion frame
of
\Lambda
. Let
S_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=T_{\tilde{\Lambda}}T^{*}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
is the g-fusion frame operator of
\tilde{\Lambda}
. Then, for each
f\in H
we get

T^{*}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}f=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{S^{-1}_{\Lambda W_{j}}}f\}=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f\}=T^{*}_{\Lambda}(S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f),

so
T_{\Lambda}T^{*}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=I_{H}
. Also, we have for each
f\in H
,

\displaystyle\langle S_{\tilde{\Lambda}}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}\pi_{S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\Lambda}(S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f),S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f,f\rangle

thus,
S_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=S_{\Lambda}^{-1}
and by (
4
), we get for each
f\in H

(5)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}^{*}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,

where
\tilde{W_{j}}:=S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}\ ,\ \tilde{\Lambda_{j}}:=\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}.

The following Theorem shows that the canonical dual g-fusion frame
gives rise to expansion coefficients with the minimal norm.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame with canonical dual
\tilde{\Lambda}
.
For each
g_{j}\in H_{j}
, put
f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}
. Then

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\|^{2}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}-v_{j}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\|^{2}.

Proof.

We can write again

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle f,S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j},S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle g_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle g_{j},\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\rangle.

Therefore,
\mbox{Im}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle g_{j},\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\rangle\Big)=0
. So

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}-v_{j}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}-2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle g_{j},\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\rangle+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f\|^{2}

and the proof completes.
∎

4.
Gf-Complete and g-Fusion Frame Sequences

Definition 6
.

We say that
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is gf-complete , if

\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}=H.

Now, it is easy to check that
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is gf-complete if and only if

\{f:\ \Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=0,\ j\in\mathbb{J}\}=\{0\}.

Proposition 3
.

If
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
is a g-fusion frame for
H
, then
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is a gf-complete.

Proof.

Let
f\in(\mbox{span}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\})^{\perp}\subseteq H
. For each
j\in\mathbb{J}
and
g_{j}\in H_{j}
we have

\langle\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,g_{j}\rangle=\langle f,\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\rangle=0,

so,
\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{J}
. Since
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
, then
\|f\|=0
. Thus
f=0
and we get
(\mbox{span}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\})^{\perp}=\{0\}
.
∎

In the following, we want to check that if a member is removed from a g-fusion frame, will the new set remain a g-fusion frame or not?

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
be a g-fusion frame for
H
with bounds
A,B
and
\tilde{\Lambda}=(S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda},v_{j})
be a canonical dual g-fusion frame. Suppose that
j_{0}\in\mathbb{J}
.

(1)

If there is a
g_{0}\in H_{j_{0}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=g_{0}
and
v_{j_{0}}=1
, then
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})_{j\neq j_{0}}
is not gf-complete in
H
.

(2)

If there is a
f_{0}\in H_{j_{0}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}f_{0}=f_{0}
and
v_{j_{0}}=1
, then
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})_{j\neq j_{0}}
is not gf-complete in
H
.

(3)

If
I-\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j_{0}}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j_{0}}
is bounded invertible on
H_{j_{0}}
, then
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})_{j\neq j_{0}}
is a g-fusion frame for
H
.

Proof.

(1).
Since
\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}\in H
, then by (
5
),

\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}.

So,

\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=0.

Let
u_{j_{0},j}:=\delta_{j_{0},j}g_{0}
, thus

\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}u_{j_{0},j}.

Then, by Theorem
3.2
, we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|u_{j_{0},j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|v_{j}^{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}-u_{j_{0},j}\|^{2}.

Consequently,

\|g_{0}\|^{2}=\|g_{0}\|^{2}+2\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}\|^{2}

and we get
\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=0
.
Therefore,

\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=0.

But,
g_{0}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}^{*}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}=\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}\neq 0
, which implies that
S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g_{0}\neq 0
and this means that
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})_{j\neq j_{0}}
is not gf-complete in
H
.

(2).
Since
\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}f_{0}=f_{0}\neq 0
, we obtain
\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}f_{0}\neq 0
and

\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}f_{0}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{j_{0}}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j_{0}}}f_{0}.

Now, the conclusion follows from
(1)
.

(3)
. Using (
5
), we have for any
f\in H

\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

So,

(6)

(I-\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j_{0}}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j_{0}})\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}f=\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

On the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|=1}\big|\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\big\langle\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g\big\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)\sup_{\|g\|=1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}\pi_{\tilde{W}_{j}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{0}}g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{B}\|\Lambda_{j_{0}}\|^{2}(\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2})

where,
\tilde{B}
is the upper bound of
\tilde{\Lambda}
. Now, by (
6
), we have

\|\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}f\|^{2}\leq\|(I-\Lambda_{j_{0}}\pi_{W_{j_{0}}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j_{0}}}}\tilde{\Lambda}^{*}_{j_{0}})^{-1}\|^{2}\tilde{B}\|\Lambda_{j_{0}}\|^{2}(\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}).

Therefore, there is a number
C>0
such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq C\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}

and we conclude for each
f\in H

\frac{A}{C}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame for
H
with bounds
A,B
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(I)

The pair
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is gf-complete.

(II)

The operator

T_{\Lambda}:\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\mapsto\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

is a well-defined from
\mathscr{H}_{2}
into
H
and for each
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{T_{\Lambda}}
,

(7)

A\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}.

Proof.

First, suppose that
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame. By Proposition
3
, (I) is satisfied. By Theorem
2.1
,
T_{\Lambda}
is a well-defined from
\mathscr{H}_{2}
into
H
and
\|T_{\Lambda}\|^{2}\leq B
. Now, the right-hand inequality in (
7
) is proved.

By Theorem
2.2
,
T_{\Lambda}
is surjective. So,
\mathcal{R}_{T^{*}_{\Lambda}}
is closed. Thus

\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{T_{\Lambda}}=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{T^{*}_{\Lambda}}}=\mathcal{R}_{T^{*}_{\Lambda}}.

Now, if
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{T_{\Lambda}}
, then

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}=T^{*}_{\Lambda}g=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}g\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}

for some
g\in H
. Therefore

\displaystyle(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}g\|^{2})^{2}|\langle S_{\Lambda}(g),g\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{\Lambda}(g)\|^{2}\|g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{\Lambda}(g)\|^{2}\big(\frac{1}{A}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}g\|^{2}\big).

This implies that

A\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|S_{\Lambda}(g)\|^{2}=\|T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|^{2}

and (II) is proved.

Conversely, Let
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
be gf-complete and inequality
(
7
) is satisfied. Let
\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}=\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}+\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
,
where
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{\Lambda}}

and
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{\Lambda}}^{\perp}
. We get

\displaystyle\|T_{\Lambda}\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|T_{\Lambda}\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\|\{f_{j}\}+\{g_{j}\}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=B\|\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|^{2}.

Thus,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence.

Assume that
\{y_{n}\}
is a sequence of members of
\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
such that
y_{n}\rightarrow y
for some
y\in H
. So, there is a
\{x_{n}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{T_{\Lambda}}
such that
T_{\Lambda}\{x_{n}\}=y_{n}
. By (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle A\|\{x_{n}-x_{m}\}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\Lambda}\{x_{n}-x_{m}\}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|T_{\Lambda}\{x_{n}\}-T_{\Lambda}\{x_{m}\}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|y_{n}-y_{m}\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathscr{H}_{2}
. Therefore
\{x_{n}\}
converges to some
x\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
, which by continuity of
T_{\Lambda}
, we have
y=T_{\Lambda}(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda_{j}}}
. Hence
\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
is closed. Since
\mbox{span}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{\ast}(H_{j})\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
, by (I) we get
\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}=H
.

Let
T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}
denotes the pseudo-inverse of
T_{\Lambda}
. By Lemma
2
(4),
T_{\Lambda}T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}=H
. Thus for any
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
,

\displaystyle A\|T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}\|^{2}\leq\|T_{\Lambda}T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}\|^{2}=\|T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}\|^{2}.

By Lemma
2
(4),
\mathcal{N}_{{T}_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}}=\mathcal{R}^{\bot}_{T_{\Lambda}}
, therefore

\displaystyle\|T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{A}.

Also by Lemma
2
(2), we have

\|(T_{\Lambda}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{A}.

But
(T_{\Lambda}^{\ast})^{\dagger}T_{\Lambda}^{\ast}
is the
orthogonal projection onto

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{(T_{\Lambda}^{\ast})^{\dagger}}=\mathcal{R}_{(T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger})^{\ast}}=\mathcal{N}_{T_{\Lambda}^{\dagger}}^{\bot}=\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}=H.

So, for all
f\in H

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(T_{\Lambda}^{\ast})^{\dagger}T_{\Lambda}^{\ast}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A}\|T_{\Lambda}^{\ast}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v^{2}_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}.

This implies that
\Lambda
satisfies the lower g-fusion frame condition.
∎

Now, we can define a g-fusion frame sequence in the Hilbert space.

Definition 7
.

We say that
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame sequence if it is a g-fusion frame for
\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame sequence if and only if the operator

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

is a well-defined and has closed range.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, it is enough to prove that if
T_{\lambda}
has closed range, then
\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}=\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
.
Let
f\in\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}
, then

f=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}g_{n},\ \ \ g_{n}\in\mbox{span}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{T_{\Lambda}}.

Therefore,
\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{T_{\Lambda}}=\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
. On the other hand, if
f\in\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
, then

f\in\mbox{span}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}\subseteq\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}H_{j}\}

and the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame sequence if and only if

(8)

f\longmapsto\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}

defines a map from
H
onto a closed subspace of
\mathscr{H}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame sequence. Then, by Theorem
4.3
,
T_{\lambda}
is well-defined and
\mathcal{R}_{T_{\Lambda}}
is closed. So,
T^{*}_{\Lambda}
is well-defined and has closed range. Conversely, by hypothesis, for all
f\in H

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}<\infty.

Let

B:=\sup\big\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}:\ \ f\in H,\ \|f\|=1\big\}

and suppose that
g_{j}\in H_{j}
and
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{J}
be finite. We can write

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sup_{\|f\|=1}\big|\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j},f\rangle\big|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sup_{\|f\|=1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\big|\langle g_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\rangle\big|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\big)\big(\sup_{\|f\|=1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq B\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\big)

Thus, by Corollary
1
,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
. Therefore,
T_{\Lambda}
is well-defined and bounded. Furthermore, if the range of the map (
8
) is closed, the same is true for
T_{\Lambda}
. So, by Theorem
4.3
,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame sequence.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
be a g-fusion frame sequence Then, it is a g-fusion frame for
H
if and only if the map

(9)

f\longmapsto\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}

from
H
onto a closed subspace of
\mathscr{H}_{2}
be injective.

Proof.

Suppose that the map (
9
) is injective and
v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{J}
. Then, the value of the map at
f
is zero. So,
f=0
. This means that
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is gf-complete. Since,
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame sequence, so, it is a g-fusion frame for
H
.

The converse is evident.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a g-fusion frame for
H
and
u\in\mathcal{B}(H)
. Then
\Gamma:=(uW_{j},\Lambda_{j}u^{*},v_{j})
is a g-fusion frame sequence if and only if
u
has closed range.

Proof.

Assume that
\Gamma
is a g-fusion frame sequence. So, by Theorem
4.3
,
T_{\Lambda u^{*}}
is a well-defined operator from
\mathscr{H}_{2}
into
H
with closed range. If
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
, then

\displaystyle uT_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}u\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{uW_{j}}u\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{uW_{j}}(\Lambda_{j}u^{*})^{*}f_{j}

\displaystyle=T_{\Lambda u^{*}}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}},

therefore
uT_{\Lambda}=T_{\Lambda u^{*}}
. Thus
uT_{\Lambda}
has closed range too. Let
y\in\mathcal{R}_{u}
, then there is
x\in H
such that
u(x)=y
. By Theorem
2.2
,
T_{\Lambda}
is surjective, so there exist
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
such that

y=u(T_{\Lambda}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}).

Thus,
\mathcal{R}_{u}=\mathcal{R}_{uT_{\Lambda}}
and
u
has closed range.

For the opposite implication, let

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda u^{*}}:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda u^{*}}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{uW_{j}}(\Lambda_{j}u^{*})^{*}f_{j}.

Hence,
T_{\Lambda u^{*}}=uT_{\Lambda}
. Since,
T_{\Lambda}
is surjective, so
T_{\Lambda u^{*}}
has closed range and by Theorem
2.1
, is well-defined. Therefore, by Theorem
4.3
, the proof is completed.
∎

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, we could transfer some common properties in general frames to g-fusion frames with the definition of the g-fusion frames and their operators. Afterward, we reviewed a basic theorem about deleting a member in Theorem
4.1
with the definition of the dual g-fusion frames and the gf-completeness. In this theorem, the defined operator in part
3
could be replaced by some other operators which are the same as the parts
1
and
2
; this is an open problem at the moment. Eventually, the g-fusion frame sequences and their relationship with the closed range operators were defined and presented.

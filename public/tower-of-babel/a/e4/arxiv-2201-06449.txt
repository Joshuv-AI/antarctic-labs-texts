Boundary concentration of peak solutions for fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system
Shengbing Deng, Xingliang Tian

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to study the existence of peak solutions for the following fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system: { ε 2 ​ s ​ ( − Δ ) s ​ u + u + ϕ ​ u = u p , in ​ Ω , ( − Δ ) s ​ ϕ = u 2 , in ​ Ω , u = ϕ = 0 , in ​ ℝ N ∖ Ω , \displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+u+\phi u=u^{p},&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt] (-\Delta)^{s}\phi=u^{2},&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt] u=\phi=0,&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right. where s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , N > 2 ​ s N>2s , p ∈ ( 1 , N + 2 ​ s N − 2 ​ s ) p\in(1,\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}) , Ω \Omega is a bounded domain in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with Lipschitz boundary, and ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} is the fractional Laplacian operator, ε \varepsilon is a small positive parameter. By using the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method, we construct a single peak solution ( u ε , ϕ ε ) (u_{\varepsilon},\phi_{\varepsilon}) such that the peak of u ε u_{\varepsilon} is in the domain but near the boundary. In order to characterize the boundary concentration of solutions, which concentrates at an approximate distance ε 2 / 3 \varepsilon^{2/3} away from the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega as ε \varepsilon tends to 0, some new estimates and analytic technique are used. Keywords: Fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system; Peak solutions; Boundary concentration; Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35B40, 35J10, 35R11. Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary, we consider the following fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+u+\phi u=u^{p},&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=u^{2},&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=\phi=0,&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\frac{N+2s}{N-2s})
,
N>2s
,
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter, and
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian operator defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}w(x):=C_{N,s}\ {\rm P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy=2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

where
C_{N,s}=2^{2s-1}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(-s)}
. From the definition of
(-\Delta)^{s}
, we can know that the fractional Laplacian problems are nonlocal. The fractional spaces and the corresponding nonlocal equations have many important applications in various fields of science and engineering, we refer to
[
23
]
and references therein.

This paper deals with the
semiclassical limit
of system (
1
), i.e. it is concerned with the problem of finding nontrivial solutions and studying their asymptotic behavior when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
; hence such solutions are usually referred to as
semiclassical
ones. The analysis of the Schrödinger-Poisson equations in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
is not only a challenging mathematical task, but also of some relevance for understanding of a wide class of quantum phenomena. Indeed, according to the
correspondence principle
, letting
\varepsilon
tends to zero in the Schrödinger equation formally describes the transition from quantum mechanics to classical mechanics; it is therefore interesting to study which kind of semiclassical phenomena system (
1
) exhibits.

Now, let us go on stating our problem. It’s well known that when
N\leq 6s
(see Section
2
), for each
\varphi,\psi
, let
\Phi[\varphi\psi]
be the unique solution of the the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=\varphi\psi&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phi=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Then we can see that (
1
) is equivalent to the following fractional Schrödinger problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+u+\Phi[u^{2}]u=u^{p}&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Let us consider the following limiting problem:

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}U+U=U^{p}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ \ U\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \ U(0)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}U(y).

(1.16)

The uniqueness, decaying behavior and non-degeneration of the positive solutions to problem (
1.16
) was first obtained by Frank, Lenzmann
[
34
]
in
\mathbb{R}
. Later, Frank, Lenzmann and Silvestre
[
35
]
obtained the following important result.

Theorem A.

When
p\in(1,\frac{N+2s}{N-2s})
for
N>2s
or
p\in(1,\infty)
for
N\leq 2s
, problem
(
1.16
)
has a unique (up to translation) positive solution
U
which is radial and strictly decreasing in
|x|
. Moreover, there exist
0<\alpha\leq\beta
such that

\frac{\alpha}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\leq U(x)\leq\frac{\beta}{1+|x|^{N+2s}},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.17)

Furthermore,
U
is non-degenerate in the sense that the linear operator defined by

L:=(-\Delta)^{s}+U-pU^{p-1}

satisfies

\ker L\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})={\rm Span}\left\{\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{2}},\ldots,\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{N}}\right\}.

(1.18)

Then, we begin to construct the approximate solution as follows: denote
U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)=U(\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon})
and let
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
be the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=U^{p}_{\varepsilon,\xi}&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Our main result of this paper can be stated as following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
s\in(0,1)
,
2s<N\leq 6s
and
p\in(1,\frac{N+2s}{N-2s})
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, problem
(
1
)
has a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the form

u_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}}+\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}},

(1.22)

where
\xi_{\varepsilon}\in\Omega
satisfies
d(\xi_{\varepsilon},\partial\Omega)\rightarrow 0
,

\frac{d(\xi_{\varepsilon},\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}\rightarrow+\infty
,
and
\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\varepsilon^{2s}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}}|^{2}+\omega^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}})=\varepsilon^{N}o(\varepsilon^{\frac{N+4s}{3}}).

More precisely,
d(\xi_{\varepsilon},\partial\Omega)\in(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}+\theta},\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}-\theta})
for any small
\theta>0.

Remark 1.1
.

We remark that the existence of peak solutions for (
1
) with
s=1
and more general nonlinearities, has been obtained by D’Aprile and Wei
[
15
]
, and our proof is mainly inspired by their work, and also
[

,
29
]

In contrast to previous literatures for the Laplace case, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
with
0<s<1
on

is nonlocal, standard techniques for Laplacian may not be carried out directly. Actually, the fractional Laplacian has some features which are essentially different from the Laplacian. For example, the ground state for (
1.16
) with

decays algebraically at infinity, while that of Laplace problems decays exponentially at infinity. Hence, in deal with the fractional Laplacian, new techniques are needed to be developed. In particular, thanks to the work of the authors of
[
24
]
who have established the Green’s formula corresponding to fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, we will establish new basic estimates and give a precise estimation of the energy functional at the approximate solutions.

2
Functional setting and the reduction method

In this section, we first recall some useful information of the fractional order Sobolev spaces. We refer to
[
1
,
23
,
60
,
8
]
for more details.
Consider the Schwartz space
\mathcal{W}
of rapidly decaying
C^{\infty}
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The topology of this space is generated by the seminorms

P_{k}(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|^{k})\sum_{|\nu|\leq k}\left|D^{\nu}\varphi(x)\right|,\ \ k=0,1,2\ldots,

where
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}
. Let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
be the set of all tempered distributions, which is the topological dual of
\mathcal{W}
. As usual, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{W}
, we denote by

\mathcal{F}\varphi(\rho)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-i\rho\cdot x}\varphi(x)dx,

the Fourier transformation of
\varphi
and we recall that one can extend
\mathcal{F}
from
\mathcal{W}
to
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
.

We define the classical fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\begin{split}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=&\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\rho|^{2s})|\mathcal{F}u(\rho)|^{2}d\rho<\infty\right\}\\
=&\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\},\end{split}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy.

It is clear that
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Hilbert space. We define also the
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
space,

H^{s}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\right\},

(2.1)

endowed with the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}=\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy,

where the set
\mathcal{D}_{\Omega}
is given by
\mathcal{D}_{\Omega}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\mathcal{C}\Omega),\ \ \mathcal{C}\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.
Denote

\langle u,v\rangle_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v+uv\right)dx,

(2.2)

and
\|u\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}=\langle u,u\rangle_{\varepsilon}
. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the completion of the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
defined as previous. Noticing that for all
u,v\in\mathbb{H}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}v(-\Delta)^{s}u+uv\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v+uv\right)dx.

Thus

\|u\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)dx,

(2.3)

that justify in some sense our choice of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
. Since the embedding
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi})
is continuous for
2\leq q\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}
, then for each
u\in\mathbb{H}
, and denote
\tilde{u}(x)=u(\varepsilon x+\xi)
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx=&\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}|\tilde{u}|^{q}dx\leq C\varepsilon^{N}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\left(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{u}|^{2}+|\tilde{u}|^{2}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\\
=&C\varepsilon^{N}\left(\varepsilon^{-N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}=C\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{q}{2})N}\|u\|^{q}_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

(2.4)

that is,
|u|_{q}\leq C\varepsilon^{(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})N}\|u\|_{\varepsilon}
for all
2\leq q\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}
. Moreover,
|u|_{q}\leq C\|u\|_{\varepsilon}
for all
q\in(1,2]
.

For
\varphi,\psi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the linear functional
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\psi}
defined in
\mathbb{H}
by

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\psi}(v)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\psi vdx.

By the Hölder’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}|\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\psi}(v)|\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi\psi|^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{2N}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N-2s}{2N}}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{\frac{4N}{N+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{4N}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{\frac{4N}{N+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{4N}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N-2s}{2N}}\\
\leq&C\varepsilon^{-s}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\psi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|v\|_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

(2.5)

where we used (
2.4
) for
\frac{4N}{N+2s}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}
, i.e.
N\leq 6s
. Hence, by the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique
\Phi[\varphi\psi]\in\mathbb{H}
such that

\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi[\varphi\psi](-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}vdx=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\psi vdx,\quad v\in\mathbb{H},

(2.6)

that is
\Phi[\varphi\psi]
is a weak solution of

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=\varphi\psi\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{H}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have that for each
u\in\mathbb{H}
,

\begin{split}\|\Phi[u^{2}]\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}\leq&C\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi[u^{2}]|^{2}dx=C\int_{\Omega}\Phi[u^{2}]u^{2}dx\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}\|\Phi[u^{2}]\|_{\varepsilon},\\
\end{split}

that is

\begin{split}\|\Phi[u^{2}]\|_{\varepsilon}\leq&C\varepsilon^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\varepsilon},\quad\mbox{if}\quad N\leq 6s,\\
\end{split}

Thus

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[u^{2}]u^{2}dx\leq&C\varepsilon^{-N}\|u\|^{4}_{\varepsilon},\quad\mbox{if}\quad N\leq 6s.\\
\end{split}

(2.7)

Moreover, let us consider the problem

(-\Delta)^{s}W=\varphi\psi\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad W\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

then by the representation formula holds

W[\varphi\psi](x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi(y)\psi(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2s}}dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.8)

which is called
s
-Riesz potential, where
c_{N,s}
is a constant depending only on
N
and
s
. Thus, by the comparison theorem, we have

\Phi[\varphi\psi]\leq\Phi[|\varphi\psi|]\leq W[|\varphi\psi|]\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\varphi,\psi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(2.9)

Proposition 2.1
.

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
45
]
) Let
r,t>1
and
0<\mu<N
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N}=2
,
u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exists a sharp constant
C(r,t,\mu,N)
, independent of
u
and
v
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}\ast u(x)\right]v(x)dx\leq C(r,t,\mu,N)|u|_{r}|v|_{t}.

Thus, by the above inequality and (
2.4
), (
2.9
), we get that when
N\leq 6s
, for all
u\in\mathbb{H}
,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[u^{2}]u^{2}dx\leq&\int_{\Omega}W[u^{2}]u^{2}dx=c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}(y)u^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N-2s}}dydx\\
\leq&c_{N,s}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{\frac{4N}{N+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{N}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2s-N}\|u\|^{4}_{\varepsilon}.\\
\end{split}

(2.10)

Substituting
\Phi[u^{2}]
in (
1
), then we can see that (
1
) with
V\equiv 1
is equivalent to the following fractional Schrödinger problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+u+\Phi[u^{2}]u=u^{p}&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The energy functional associated with (
1
) is

\begin{split}J_{\varepsilon}(u)=&\frac{\varepsilon^{2s}C_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[u]u^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{u^{p+1}}{p+1}-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)dx\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[u^{2}]u^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1}dx.\end{split}

(2.12)

Define

E_{\varepsilon,\xi}:=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{H}:\left\langle\omega,\frac{\partial P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\right\rangle_{\varepsilon}=0,\ i=1,2,\ldots,N\right\},

(2.13)

where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let
D_{\varepsilon,R}:=\{\xi:\xi\in\Omega,\ R\varepsilon\leq d(\xi,\partial\Omega)\leq 1/R\}
, where
R>0
is a large constant.
In Section
6
, we will choose
\xi\in D_{\varepsilon,R}
for suitable domain
D_{\varepsilon,R}
such that
(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi})
is a solution of (
1
). Let

K(\xi,\omega)=J_{\varepsilon}\left(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\omega\right),\ \xi\in D_{\varepsilon,R},\ \omega\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}.

We expand
K(\xi,\omega)
near
\omega=0
as

K(\xi,\omega)=K(\xi,0)+L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\omega)+R_{\varepsilon}(\omega),

where

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)=\langle P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi},\omega\rangle_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega dx-\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}\omega dx,\end{split}

(2.14)

\begin{split}Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)=&\langle\omega,\eta\rangle_{\varepsilon}-p\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}\omega\eta dx+\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]\omega\eta dx\\
&+2\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\eta(x)dx,\ \ \forall\omega,\ \eta\in\mathbb{H},\end{split}

(2.15)

and

\begin{split}R_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=&\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega^{2}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega^{2}]\omega^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\omega)^{p+1}dx\\
&+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}dx+\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}\omega dx+\frac{p}{2}\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}\omega^{2}dx.\end{split}

(2.16)

We will prove in Lemma
5.1
that
L_{\varepsilon,\xi}:E_{\varepsilon,\xi}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear operator. In particular, by the Riesz representation theorem there is an
l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
such that
\langle l_{\varepsilon,\xi},\omega\rangle_{\varepsilon}=L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)
. In Lemma
5.2
, we will prove

|Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\omega)|\leq C\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}^{2},

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
. In particular, there is a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}:E_{\varepsilon,\xi}\rightarrow E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
such that
\langle\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega,\eta\rangle_{\varepsilon}=Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)
. Thus, finding a critical point for
K(\xi,\omega)
in
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
is equivalent to solving the following problem in
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
:

l_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega+R^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=0.

(2.17)

We will prove in Lemma
5.3
that the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}
is invertible in
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
. So we see that
l_{\varepsilon,x}+\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,x}\omega=0
has a unique solution in
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
for each fixed
\xi\in D_{\varepsilon,R}
. In Lemma
5.1
, we will prove that if
\omega
belongs to a suitable set, the term
R^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
is a small perturbation term in (
2.17
). Thus, we can use the contraction mapping theorem to prove that (
2.17
) has a unique solution for each fixed
\xi\in D_{\varepsilon,R}
.

3
Some preliminary estimates

In this section, we will give some preliminary estimates which are crucial for our result.

Firstly, let us analysis the properties of
U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
and
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
. Fix
\xi\in D_{\varepsilon,R}
,
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{N})
. From (
1.17
), we have

U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\leq C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,

(3.1)

for some
C>0
. Let
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi},

then from (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=U_{\varepsilon,\xi}&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it holds that

0\leq\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}.

(3.5)

Proof.

By using the estimation (
3.1
), we have that
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
. Hence, by the maximum principle, we can directly get (
3.5
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There is
C>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}](x)\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2s}}{1+\left|\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon}\right|^{N-2s}},

(3.6)

and

\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}](x)\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2s}}{1+\left|\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon}\right|^{N-2s}}.

(3.7)

Proof.

We only prove (
3.6
), and the proof of (
3.7
) is similar.
Noticing that
\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}](\varepsilon x+\xi)=\varepsilon^{2s}v_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
v_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}v_{\varepsilon}=U^{2}&\ x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi},\\[5.69054pt]
v_{\varepsilon}=0&\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}.\end{array}\right.

Let
W
be the solution of the following equation

(-\Delta)^{s}W=U^{2},\ \ W\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(3.9)

It is well known that

W(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U^{2}(y)}{|y-x|^{N-2s}}dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(3.10)

where
c_{N,s}
is a constant depending only on
N
and
s
. From
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have

\frac{C_{1}}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}\leq W(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1+|x|^{N-2s}},

(3.11)

for some
C_{2}\geq C_{1}>0.
Thus

\begin{split}W(0)=&c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U^{2}(y)}{|y|^{N-2s}}dy\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{C}{|y|^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
=&\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{C}{|y|^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{1}(0)}\frac{C}{|y|^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\leq&\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{C}{|y|^{N-2s}}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{1}(0)}\frac{C}{(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\leq C^{\prime},\end{split}

and also we can get
W(0)\geq C
. Then fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
|x|\geq 2
large and
t:=|x|/2
, and observe that if
y\in B_{t}(x)
then
|y|\geq|x|-|x-y|\geq|x|-t=\frac{|x|}{2}.
As a consequence,

\begin{split}\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{U^{2}(y)}{|y-x|^{N-2s}}dy\leq&\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{C}{|y-x|^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\leq&\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{C}{|y-x|^{N-2s}(1+(|x|/2)^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\leq&\frac{C}{1+|x|^{2(N+2s)}}\int_{B_{t}(0)}\frac{1}{|z|^{N-2s}}dz\\
\leq&\frac{C^{\prime}|t|^{2s}}{1+|x|^{2(N+2s)}}\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{1+|x|^{2N+2s}}.\end{split}

Moreover, if
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{t}(x)
then
|y-x|\geq t=\frac{|x|}{2},
thus

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{t}(x)}\frac{U^{2}(y)}{|y-x|^{N-2s}}dy\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{t}(x)}\frac{C}{(|x|/2)^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\leq&\frac{C^{\prime}}{|x|^{N-2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\leq&\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}.\end{split}

Furthermore, if
y\in B_{t}(0)
then
\frac{3|x|}{2}=3t\geq|y-x|\geq t=\frac{|x|}{2},
thus

\begin{split}\int_{B_{t}(0)}\frac{U^{2}(y)}{|y-x|^{N-2s}}dy\geq&\int_{B_{t}(0)}\frac{C}{(3|x|/2)^{N-2s}(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\geq&\frac{C^{\prime}}{|x|^{N-2s}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{N+2s})^{2}}dy\\
\geq&\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{1+|x|^{N-2s}}.\end{split}

Thus, (
3.11
) holds.
By the comparison theorem, we have
0<v_{\varepsilon}\leq W.
Thus we have the following estimation of
v_{\varepsilon}
:

v_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{N-2s}},\ \ \forall\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

i.e., there exists
C>0
such that (
3.6
) holds.
∎

Finally, at the end of this section, let us make a more accurate estimation of
\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\begin{split}\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}](x)=&\varepsilon^{2s}W\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon}\right)-\varepsilon^{N}BH(\xi,x)+O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right),\end{split}

where
W
is given in (
3.10
),
B=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}
,
H(\xi,x)
is the regular part of the Green function of the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, and
\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}
will be defined in (
3
).

Proof.

Let
S(x,\xi)
be the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}S=\delta_{\xi}&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
S(x,\xi)\rightarrow 0&\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\rightarrow\infty,\end{array}\right.

where
\delta_{\xi}
is the Dirac measure. By a direct calculation, we know that
S(x,\xi)
behaves like
\frac{1}{|x-\xi|^{N-2s}}
, i.e.

S(x,\xi)\simeq\frac{C}{|x-\xi|^{N-2s}}.

Then

\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}](x)=\int_{\Omega}\left(S(y,x)-H(y,x)\right)U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)dy.

Since
H(x,\xi)
is the regular part of the Green function of the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}_{x}H(x,\xi)=0&\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
H(x,\xi)=S(x,\xi)&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{array}\right.

we have

|D^{l}_{x}H(x,\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+l}},\ \ l=0,1,2.

(3.18)

By using the mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H(y,x)U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}H(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)U^{2}(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\left[H(\xi,x)+\sum^{N}_{k=1}\varepsilon D_{k}H(\xi,x)\cdot z_{k}+O\left(\sum^{N}_{i,j>0}\varepsilon^{2}D_{ij}H(\xi,x)\cdot z_{i}z_{j}\right)\right]U^{2}(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{N}H(\xi,x)\left(B-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}(z)dz\right)+\varepsilon^{N+1}\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{k}U^{2}(z)dz

\displaystyle+O\left(\varepsilon^{N+2}\sum^{N}_{i,j>0}D_{ij}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{i}z_{j}U^{2}(z)dz\right)

(3.19)

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}=\{x:\varepsilon x+\xi\in\Omega\}
.
Since
B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}
and by the behavior of
U
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}(z)dz\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}U^{2}(z)dz\\
\leq&C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{N+2s})^{2}}dz\\
=&O\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}.\end{split}

Since
U(z)=U(|z|)
and

|\sum^{N}_{k=1}z_{k}|\leq N|\sum^{N}_{k=1}z^{2}_{k}|^{1/2}=N|z|,\quad\forall z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\varepsilon^{N+1}\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{k}U^{2}(z)dz\right|=&\left|\varepsilon^{N+1}\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}\backslash B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}z_{k}U^{2}(z)dz\right|\\
\leq&\frac{C\varepsilon^{N+1}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+1}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}\backslash B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{|z|}{(1+|z|^{N+2s})^{2}}dz\\
=&O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{2N+2s}}\right).\end{split}

Moreover, since
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is bounded, we can choose
r>0
which is independent of
\varepsilon
such that
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}\subset B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}(0)
, then we have

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{N+2}\sum^{N}_{i,j>0}D_{ij}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{i}z_{j}U^{2}(z)dz\leq&\frac{C\varepsilon^{N+2}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{N+2s})^{2}}dz\\
\leq&\frac{C\varepsilon^{N+2}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2N+4s}}dz\\
=&O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N+2}|\log\varepsilon|}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}}\right),\end{split}

if
N+4s-2\geq 0
i.e.
N\geq 2,s\in(0,1)
or
N=1,s\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
. Otherwise, if
N+4s-2<0
i.e.
N=1,s\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, we have

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{N+2}\sum^{N}_{i,j>0}D_{ij}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{i}z_{j}U^{2}(z)dz=&O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}}\right).\end{split}

Define

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\varepsilon^{N+2}|\log\varepsilon|}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}},&{\rm if}\ \ N+4s-2\geq 0;\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{\varepsilon^{2N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{N-2s+2}},&{\rm if}\ \ N+4s-2<0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{N}H(\xi,x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}(z)dz=O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{2N+2s}}\right)=&O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right),\\
\varepsilon^{N+1}\sum^{N}_{k=1}D_{k}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{k}U^{2}(z)dz=&O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right),\\
\varepsilon^{N+2}\sum^{N}_{i,j}D_{ij}H(\xi,x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}z_{i}z_{j}U^{2}(z)dz=&O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right).\end{split}

(3.23)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}S(y,x)U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)dy=&\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}S(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)U^{2}(z)dz\\
=&\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}S(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)U^{2}(z)dz+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{2N+2s}}\right)\\
=&\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}S(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)U^{2}(z)dz+O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right).\end{split}

(3.24)

But
\varepsilon^{N-2s}S(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)
is a solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}w=\delta_{(x-\xi)/\varepsilon}&\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
w(x)\rightarrow 0&\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\rightarrow\infty.\end{array}\right.

As a result,

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{N-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}S(\varepsilon z+\xi,x)U^{2}(z)dz=W\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon}\right),\end{split}

(3.26)

where
W
is given in (
3.10
). Thus the result holds.∎

4
Calculations of the error term

Recall that we are looking for a solution of (
1
) in the form

u_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}}+\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}},

for suitable
\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi_{\varepsilon}}\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
. We need to estimate the error term
J_{\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})
. Now, let us give more accurate information of
D_{\varepsilon,R}
, that is

D_{\varepsilon,R}=\{\xi\in\Omega:\varepsilon R\leq\varepsilon^{1-\varpi}\leq d(\xi,\partial\Omega)\leq 1/R\},

(4.1)

as
\varepsilon
is small, where

\frac{N-2s}{3(N+2s)}<\varpi<\frac{1}{3},

(4.2)

and in the rest of this paper, we will give more information about
\varpi
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\varepsilon>0
is small, we have

\begin{split}J_{\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})=&\varepsilon^{N}A_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{N+2s}A_{2}-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2N}B^{2}H(\xi,\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\\
&+\varepsilon^{N}O\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{(1+p)(N+2s)-N}+\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\varepsilon^{2s+\zeta_{0}(N-2s)}\right).\end{split}

(4.3)

where
\zeta_{0}\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
closes to
\frac{1}{3}
,
\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}
is given in (
3
) and

\begin{split}A_{1}=&\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{p+1},\ \ A_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}W=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U^{2}(y)U^{2}(x)}{|y-x|^{N-2s}}dydx,\\
B=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2},\ \ \tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}=\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}\left(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right).\end{split}

(4.4)

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
J_{\varepsilon}
and using
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
as a text function in (
1
), we get

\begin{split}J_{\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{2}+(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}\right)dx\\
&-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]dx\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}dx\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]dx\\
=:&J^{1}_{\varepsilon}+J^{2}_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(4.5)

Firstly, we estimate
J^{1}_{\varepsilon}
. From
(\ref{upu})
we know that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

|U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|\leq C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\to 0,

thus for any
l>0
, it holds that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-l}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{l+1}\leq&\left|\frac{U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}}{U_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\right|^{l}_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&o(1)\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\end{split}

By using Taylor’s expansion for
(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}
at
U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
, we have

\begin{split}J^{1}_{\varepsilon}=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}\left(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\right)-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}))^{p+1}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
&-\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}}{p+1}+(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\right)+o(1)\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p+1}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{p+1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&\varepsilon^{N}A+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{p+1}.\end{split}

(4.6)

Noting that
U
is small in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}
if
\varepsilon
is small, then we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{p+1}\leq&C\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}\frac{t^{N-1}}{(1+t^{N+2s})^{p+1}}dt\\
=&O\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{(1+p)(N+2s)-N}\right)\\
\end{split}

(4.7)

So it remains to estimate
J^{2}_{\varepsilon}
. We have

\begin{split}J^{2}_{\varepsilon}=&\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}))^{2}\Phi[(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}))^{2}]dx\\
=&\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]dx\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\Big[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]+U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\Phi[(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}]\\
&+U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})]+\frac{1}{2}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]\\
&+\frac{1}{4}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}\Phi[(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}]\Big]dx\\
:=&J^{21}_{\varepsilon}+J^{22}_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(4.8)

From Lemma
3.3
, we obtain that

\begin{split}4J^{21}_{\varepsilon}=&\int_{\Omega}U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]dx\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}W\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\varepsilon}\right)dx-\varepsilon^{N}B\int_{\Omega}U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}H(x,\xi)dx+\int_{\Omega}U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)dx\\
=&\varepsilon^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}Wdz-\varepsilon^{2N}BH(\xi,\xi)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}dz+\varepsilon^{N}O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}dz\\
=&\varepsilon^{N+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}Wdz-\varepsilon^{2N}BH(\xi,\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U^{2}dz+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{3N+4s}}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)^{2N+2s}}\right)\\
&+\varepsilon^{N}O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U^{2}dz\\
=&\varepsilon^{N+2s}A_{2}-\varepsilon^{2N}B^{2}H(\xi,\xi)+\varepsilon^{N}O\left(\mu_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right).\end{split}

(4.9)

On the other hand, since
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
and by the behavior of
\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]
as shown in Lemma
3.2
, we have

\begin{split}|J^{22}_{\varepsilon}|\leq&C\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]dx\\
\leq&C|P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\Phi[U_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}]dx\\
\leq&C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\varepsilon^{2s}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N-2s})(1+|x|^{N+2s})}dx\\
\leq&C\varepsilon^{N+2s+\varpi(N+2s)}.\end{split}

From (
4.2
), we deduce that

N+2s+\varpi(N+2s)>\frac{4N+4s}{3},

then we can choose
\zeta_{0}\in(\frac{1}{3},1)
closes to
\frac{1}{3}
which is same as in Lemma
5.1
, such that

\begin{split}|J^{22}_{\varepsilon}|=&\varepsilon^{N}o\left(\varepsilon^{2s+\zeta_{0}(N-2s)}\right).\end{split}

(4.10)

Thus the result holds.
∎

Now, let us estimate
\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}
which is defined in (
4.4
). In order to obtain a proper result, we will introduce a new “nonlocal normal derivative” which is simply and helpful for this estimation. It is well known the Green’s formula for classical Laplacian
\Delta
that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)u+\int_{\partial\Omega}v\partial_{\nu}u.

In
[
24
]
, Dipierro, Ros-oton and Valdinoci have established a new “nonlocal normal derivative”
\mathcal{N}_{s}
which is defined as

\mathcal{N}_{s}u(x):=C_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\overline{\Omega},

(4.11)

and they get the Green’s formula corresponding to fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
,

\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\Omega}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy=\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)^{s}u+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}v\mathcal{N}_{s}u.

(4.12)

Following the proof of
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have:

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\varepsilon
is small, then there are
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that

\begin{split}c_{1}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}\leq\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq c_{2}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}.\end{split}

(4.13)

Proof.

Multiplying (
3
) by
U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
,
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}
respectively and integrating, we get

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=0,\end{split}

(4.14)

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=0.\end{split}

(4.15)

Then multiplying (
1
) by
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}=&\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\xi}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi})+\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi})\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\left(\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}+\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(-\Delta)^{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\\
=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}.\end{split}

(4.16)

Besides, using the Green’s formula (
4.12
) for (
4.15
), we obtain

\begin{split}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}=\|\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|^{2}_{\varepsilon}>0.\end{split}

(4.17)

Combining (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we obtain

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}>&-\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\mathcal{N}_{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\\
=&-C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)-U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&-C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U(x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{U(x)-U(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U(x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx.\end{split}

(4.18)

We can choose small
\varrho>0
which is independent of
\varepsilon
such that there exists
c>0
such that
U(y)-U(x)\geq cU(y)
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi},\ \forall y\in B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)
.
Define

\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}:\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon\leq d(x,\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi})\leq 3\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon\}.

Then we can find a suitable
\xi^{1}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(\xi^{1})\subset\mathcal{S}
and for all
x\in B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(\xi^{1})
,
(1+\varrho)d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq(1+3\varrho)d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon
.
Then
\forall x\in B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(\xi^{1}),\ \forall y\in B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)
, it holds that

d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon\leq|x-y|\leq(1+4\varrho)d(\xi,\partial\Omega)/\varepsilon.

Noticing the fact that

B_{R}(0)\subset B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}

for large
R>0
, and by (
1.17
), we obtain

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\geq&C_{1}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(\xi^{1})}U(x)\int_{B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
\geq&C_{2}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(\xi^{1})}U(x)\int_{B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{U(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
\geq&C_{3}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\left(\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\int_{B_{\frac{\varrho d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}U(y)dy\\
\geq&C_{4}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}\int_{B_{R}(0)}U(y)dy\\
\geq&C_{5}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}.\end{split}

(4.19)

On the other hand, from (
4.16
), we have

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}=&\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\left(\mathcal{N}_{s}\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}-\mathcal{N}_{s}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
&-C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U(x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{P_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx.\end{split}

(4.20)

If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}
, then
\frac{1}{|x|^{N+2s}}\leq\frac{2}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}
since
|x|>>1
. Then from

[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq C\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}dx\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}.\end{split}

(4.21)

In fact, from (
3
), we know that
\eta_{\varepsilon,\xi}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
.
Therefore,

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx\\
=&C_{N,s}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}U(x)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{U(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dydx.\end{split}

(4.22)

Moreover,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{U(y)}{|y-x|^{N+2s}}dy\leq&\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{U(y)}{||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|}dy\\
\leq&C_{6}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{N+2s})(||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|)}dy\\
\leq&\frac{C_{6}}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|y|^{N+2s}}+\frac{1}{||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|}\right)dy,\end{split}

then

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq&C_{6}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}dx\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{N+2s}}dy\\
&+C_{6}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|}dydx\\
\leq&C_{7}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}\\
&+C_{6}\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|}dydx.\end{split}

What’s more,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N+2s}-|y|^{N+2s}|}dydx\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|(|x|^{2s}+|y|^{2s})}dydx\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|}dydx\\
=&o\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}\right),\end{split}

(4.23)

thus we have

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon,\xi}\leq&C_{8}\varepsilon^{N}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}.\end{split}

(4.24)

Thus the result follows by (
4.19
) and (
4.24
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We remark that the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|}dydx

is integrable for each
R>0
, moreover, this integral value is decreasing for large
R>0
. Indeed,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|}dydx\\
=&C\int^{+\infty}_{R}\frac{t^{N-1-2s}}{(1+t^{N+2s})^{2}}\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}}{t^{N}-r^{N}}drdt\\
=&C\int^{+\infty}_{R}\frac{t^{N-1-2s}\log(\frac{t^{N}}{t^{N}-R^{N}})}{(1+t^{N+2s})^{2}}dt\\
=&C^{\prime}\int^{+\infty}_{1}\frac{(\log r)R^{N-2s}}{(r-1)^{2-\frac{2s}{N}}r^{\frac{2s}{N}}\left[1+(\frac{r}{r-1})^{\frac{N+2s}{N}}R^{N+2s}\right]^{2}}dr\\
=&C^{\prime}\int^{+\infty}_{1}\frac{(\log r)(\frac{r-1}{r})^{\frac{N+6s}{N}}}{(r-1)^{\frac{2s}{N}}\left[(r-1)^{1-\frac{2s}{N}}r\right]\left[(\frac{r-1}{r})^{\frac{N+2s}{N}}R^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}+R^{\frac{N+6s}{2}}\right]^{2}}dr\\
\end{split}

since
\log r\leq(r-1)^{\frac{2s}{N}}
for all
r\geq 1
and
N>2s
, we can know the above integral is integrable for each
R>0
and the integral value is decreasing for large
R>0
. Therefore, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|}dydx\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{N+2s})^{2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\int_{B_{\frac{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{1}{||x|^{N}-|y|^{N}|}dydx\\
\leq&C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+6s}\int^{+\infty}_{1}\frac{1}{(r-1)^{1-\frac{2s}{N}}r}dr\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+6s}=o\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+4s}\right).\end{split}

Thus (
4.23
) holds.

5
The reduction

In this section, we will calculate
L_{\varepsilon,\xi}
,
Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}
,
R_{\varepsilon}
and develop the reduction. Here, we take
\varpi
for
D_{\varepsilon,R}
in (
4.1
) as

\max\left\{\frac{N+4s}{6[p(N+2s)-N/2]},\frac{N+4s}{6(N+2s)}\right\}<\varpi<\frac{1}{3},

(5.1)

in which
N<8s
.
Firstly, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

As defined in
(
2.14
)
, we have
L_{\varepsilon,\xi}
is a bounded linear operator from
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
into
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
for all
\omega\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)=O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N+2s)+\zeta_{0}(N-2s)}{2}}\right)\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon},

(5.2)

for some
\zeta_{0}\in\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2s}{N-2s}\right)
closes to
\frac{1}{3}
.
In particular there is
l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
such that

L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)=\langle l_{\varepsilon,\xi},\omega\rangle_{\varepsilon},\ \ \forall\omega\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}.

(5.3)

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)
and using
\omega
as a test function in (
1
), we get

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega)=&\langle P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi},w\rangle_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega dx-\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}\omega dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega}((U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}-(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p})\omega dx+\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega dx\\
=&L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}+L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}.\end{split}

where

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}=\int_{\Omega}((U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}-(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p})\omega dx,\ \ L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega dx.\end{split}

Then, using Taylor’s expansion for
(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p}
at
P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}
and from (
3.5
), we get

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}&\leq C\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})\omega dx\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\left(\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\right)^{2(p-1)}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{N+2s}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\left(\int^{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}_{0}\frac{t^{N-1}}{\left(1+t^{N+2s}\right)^{2(p-1)}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\end{split}

where
r=\max_{x,y\in\Omega}|x-y|
. By a simple calculation, it holds that

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}_{0}\frac{t^{N-1}}{\left(1+t^{N+2s}\right)^{2(p-1)}}dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(\varepsilon^{2(p-1)(N+2s)-N}),&{\rm if}\ \ 1<p<1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)},\\[8.53581pt]
O(|\ln\varepsilon|),&{\rm if}\ \ p=1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)},\\[8.53581pt]
O(1),&{\rm if}\ \ 1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)}<p<\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
1<p<1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)}
, taking
q=1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)}-p
then

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}&\leq C|U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}|_{\infty}^{1-q}\int_{\Omega}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}(U_{\varepsilon,\xi}-P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{q}\omega dx\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{(1-q)(N+2s)}\left(\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\right)^{2(p-1)+2q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{(1-q)(N+2s)}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\left(\int^{\frac{r}{\varepsilon}}_{0}\frac{t^{N-1}}{1+t^{N}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C^{\prime\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{d(\xi,\partial\Omega)}\right)^{p(N+2s)-N/2}|\ln\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

then we obtain

\displaystyle L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\varpi[p(N+2s)-N/2]}|\ln\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon},&{\rm if}\ \ 1<p<1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)};\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\varpi(N+2s)}|\ln\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon},&{\rm if}\ \ p=1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)};\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\varpi(N+2s)}\right)\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon},&{\rm if}\ \ 1+\frac{N}{2(N+2s)}<p<\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}.\end{array}\right.

From (

\frac{N}{2}+\varpi[p(N+2s)-N/2]>\frac{2N+2s}{3}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \frac{N}{2}+\varpi(N+2s)>\frac{2N+2s}{3},

then we can choose

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{1}=O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N+2s)+\zeta_{0}(N-2s)}{2}}\right)\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(5.9)

As a result, from Lemma

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{2}\leq&C\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}]U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\right)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2s}\left(\varepsilon^{N}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{4N}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

for any

Then the result follows by (

Lemma 5.2
.

As the definition of
Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}
in
(
2.15
)
, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)\leq C\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}.

(5.11)

In particular there is
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}
such that

Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}\omega,\eta\rangle_{\varepsilon},\ \ \forall\omega,\ \eta\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}.

(5.12)

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)
that

\begin{split}Q_{\varepsilon,\xi}(\omega,\eta)=&\langle\omega,\eta\rangle_{\varepsilon}-p\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}\omega\eta dx+\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]\omega\eta dx\\
&+2\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\eta(x)dx.\end{split}

Obviously,
\langle\omega,\eta\rangle_{\varepsilon}\leq\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}
. On the one hand,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{p-1}\omega\eta dx\right|\leq C^{\prime}\left[\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}dx\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}dx\right]^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

and from Lemma
3.2
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\Phi[P^{2}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]\omega\eta dx\right|\leq&\int_{\Omega}\Phi[U^{2}_{\varepsilon,\xi}]|\omega||\eta|dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\omega||\eta|dx\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\left[\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}dx\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}dx\right]^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]
satisfies

\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]=\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{split}

then we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}](-\Delta)^{s}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]dx\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}(\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}(\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}])^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}(\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

then

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}(\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}dx.\end{split}

(5.13)

By Hölder’s inequality and (
5.13
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)\eta(x)dx\right|\leq&\left[\int_{\Omega}(\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}])^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int_{\Omega}(\eta P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C^{\prime}\left[\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi[\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi}]|^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}\\
\leq&C^{\prime\prime}\left[\int_{\Omega}(\omega P_{\varepsilon,\Omega}U_{\varepsilon,\xi})^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}\\
\leq&C^{\prime\prime\prime}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}\|\eta\|_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

(5.14)

Hence (
5.11
) holds, and (
5.12
) is a direct result.

Lemma 5.4

, it holds that

\begin{split}\|G(\omega)\|_{\varepsilon}\leq&C\|l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|_{\varepsilon}+C\|R_{\varepsilon}(\omega)\|_{\varepsilon}\\
\leq&C\|l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|_{\varepsilon}+C_{5}\left[\varepsilon^{2s-\frac{N}{2}}\|\omega\|^{3}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2s-N}\|\omega\|^{4}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{N\left(1-\frac{\min\{p+1,3\}}{2}\right)}\|\omega\|_{\varepsilon}^{\min\{p,2\}}\right]\\
\leq&C\|l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|_{\varepsilon}+C_{6}\left[\varepsilon^{N+2s+3\kappa}+\varepsilon^{N+2s+4\kappa}+\varepsilon^{N\left(1-\frac{\min\{p+1,3\}}{2}\right)}\varepsilon^{\min\{p,2\}(\frac{N}{2}+\kappa)}\right]\\
=&C\|l_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|_{\varepsilon}+C^{\prime}_{6}\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\kappa\min\{p,2\}}.\end{split}

Hence, using Lemma
5.1
and the fact
\min\{p,2\}>1
, we get

\begin{split}\|G(\omega)\|_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{7}\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\kappa}.\end{split}

(5.26)

Combining (
5.25
) and (
5.26
), we see that
G
is a contraction map from
E_{\varepsilon,\xi}\cap B(0,\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\kappa})
into itself. By the contraction mapping Theorem, we get the existence of unique
\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}\cap B(0,\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}+\kappa})
such that

\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}=G(\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}).

Furthermore from (
5.26
), we get

\|\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}\|_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}O(\varepsilon^{\kappa}).

We prove now that
\xi\rightarrow\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi}
is
C^{1}
. In order to do that, let us denote

\Psi(\varepsilon,\xi,\omega)=\omega-G(\omega),\ \ \omega\in E_{\varepsilon,\xi}.

Then,
\Psi(\varepsilon,\xi,\omega_{\varepsilon,\xi})=0
. On the other hand,

\partial_{\omega}\Psi(\varepsilon,\xi,0)(\eta)=\eta+\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{-1}(R^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\omega)\eta).

Using Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.4
, we conclude that
\partial_{\omega}\Psi(\varepsilon,\xi,0)
is, for
\varepsilon
small, invertible as a small perturbation of the identity. Thus the result follows by the implicit function Theorem.
∎

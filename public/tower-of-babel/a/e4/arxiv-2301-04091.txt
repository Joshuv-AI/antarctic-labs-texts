Reaction cleaving and complex-balanced distributions for chemical reaction networks with general kinetics
Linard Hoessly, Carsten Wiuf and Panqiu Xia

Abstract
. Reaction networks have become a major modelling framework in the biological sciences from epidemiology and population biology to genetics and cellular biology. In recent years, much progress has been made on stochastic reaction networks (SRNs), modelled as continuous time Markov chains (CTMCs) and their stationary distributions. We are interested in complex-balanced stationary distributions, where the probability flow out of a complex equals the flow into the complex. We characterise the existence and the form of complex-balanced distributions of SRNs with arbitrary transition functions through conditions on the cycles of the reaction graph (a digraph). Furthermore, we give a sufficient condition for the existence of a complex-balanced distribution and give precise conditions for when it is also necessary. The sufficient condition is also necessary for mass-action kinetics (and certain generalisations of that) or if the connected components of the digraph are cycles. Moreover, we state a deficiency theorem, a generalisation of the deficiency theorem for stochastic mass-action kinetics to arbitrary stochastic kinetics. The theorem gives the co-dimension of the parameter space for which a complex-balanced distribution exists. To achieve this, we construct an iterative procedure to decompose a strongly connected reaction graph into disjoint cycles, such that the corresponding SRN has equivalent dynamics and preserves complex-balancedness, provided the original SRN had so. This decomposition might have independent interest and might be applicable to edge-labelled digraphs in general.

Reaction networks offer a framework to model the dynamics of natural systems.
They are applied across the sciences, for example in epidemiology

The recent popularity of

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{y^{\prime}\colon\eta\to y^{\prime}}\lambda_{\eta\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{y\colon y\to\eta}\pi(x+\phi(y)-\phi(\eta))\lambda_{y\to\eta}(x+\phi(y)-\phi(\eta)),

The existence of a complex-balanced stationary distribution implies the reaction graph is a disjoint union of strongly connected components (in the example, there is one such component)
[
Cappelletti
]
.

A main contribution of this paper is to construct a cleaving operation on reaction graphs that decomposes a strongly connected reaction graph of an SRN into a reaction graph of a dynamically equivalent SRN consisting of disjoint cycles only (a cyclic SRN). The construction is iterative, by splitting nodes with multiple incoming edges into multiple nodes with single incoming edges, and underlies the proof of Theorem
4.9
in particular. In order to formulate the procedure, we extend the definition of a ‘classical’ SRN and allow multiple representations of the same complex and reaction (potentially with different labels) in the digraph.
Importantly, the cleaving operation preserves complex-balancedness: A cyclic SRN is complex-balanced if and only if the original SRN is. All statements in the paper are valid for classical SRNs as well as SRNs according to the new definition, since the classical definition is just a special case of the new definition.

The cleaving operation might be of independent interest. Furthermore, it is not restricted to reaction graphs of SRN, but works on any strongly connected digraph with real labels, such as reaction graphs of deterministic reaction networks, with few changes. These changes relate to the discrete nature of the dynamics of SRNs versus the continuous nature of the dynamics of deterministic SRNs.

2.
Complex-balanced stationary distributions

Let
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
be an SRN, where
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R})
is a digraph of complexes and reactions, and
\lambda
a labelling (kinetics) of the reactions (see Section
4
for the precise definition), and let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
be a (closed) irreducible component of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
. We assume the following compatibility condition by default, which states that a reaction might ‘fire’ only if the molecules of the source complex are available:

Condition 1
.

For
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
and
x\in\Gamma
,
\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)>0
if any only if
x\geq\phi(y)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A probability distribution
\pi
on
\Gamma
is a

(i)

stationary distribution
of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
, if for all
x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{y\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{y\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}}\pi\big(x-\phi(y^{\prime})+\phi(y)\big)\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}\big(x-\phi(y^{\prime})+\phi(y)\big),

where we set
\pi(x)=0
and
\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(z)=0
if
z\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
(same below).

(ii)

complex-balanced distribution
of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
, if for all complexes
\eta\in\mathcal{C}
, and all
x\in\Gamma
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{y^{\prime}\colon\eta\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda_{\eta\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{y\colon y\to\eta\in\mathcal{R}}\pi\big(x+\phi(y)-\phi(\eta)\big)\lambda_{y\to\eta}\big(x+\phi(y)-\phi(\eta)\big).

(iii)

detailed-balanced distribution
of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
, if for all complexes
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
, and for all
x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\pi(x)\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\pi\big(x+\phi(y^{\prime})-\phi(y)\big)\lambda_{y^{\prime}\to y}\big(x+\phi(y^{\prime})-\phi(y)\big),

where
\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}\equiv 0
if
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{R}
.

A detailed-balanced distribution is also complex-balanced, and a complex-balanced distribution is also stationary
[
JoshiCap
]
. Furthermore, complex-balancedness requires the digraph to be
weakly reversible
(all connected components are strongly connected), that is, every reaction
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
belongs to a cycle
\gamma\subseteq\mathcal{R}

[
Cappelletti
,
craciun2020efficient
]
. Detailed balancedness requires the digraph to be
reversible
, that is, if
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
then
y^{\prime}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y\in\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
be the connected components of the digraph of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
. Define

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\{x-\phi(y)\colon x\in\Gamma,y\in\mathcal{L}_{k}\}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},\quad k=1,\ldots,\ell.

A main theorem is the following sufficient condition for the existence of a complex-balanced distribution.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume the digraph of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
is weakly reversible with
\ell
connected components. Further, suppose for each
k=1,\dots,\ell
and
y\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
, that the kinetics factorises as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{y\to y^{\prime}}}{m_{k}(x-\phi(y))g(x)},\quad x\geq\phi(y),\quad x\in\Gamma,

where
g\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
m_{k}\colon\Gamma_{k}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
k=1,\dots,\ell
, are functions, and
\kappa_{y\to y^{\prime}}>0
is a constant, such that for all complexes
\eta\in\mathcal{C}
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{y^{\prime}\colon\eta\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}}\kappa_{\eta\to y^{\prime}}=\sum_{y\colon y\to\eta\in\mathcal{R}}\kappa_{y\to\eta}.

If
0<M\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\Gamma}g(x)<\infty
, then the distribution

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{1}{M}g(x),

is complex-balanced for
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
.

If the Markov chain is positive recurrent on
\Gamma
, then
\pi
(and
g
up to a normalising constant) is unique. Hence, also
m_{k}
is unique up to a constant. If the Markov chain is transient and explosive, there might be several stationary distributions, hence the choice of
g
and
m_{k}
might not be unique.

Theorem
2.2

extends the condition presented in
[
hong2023computational
, Theorem 4.1]
, where the

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume the digraph of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
is weakly reversible with
\ell
connected components. Further, suppose that
\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{y\to y^{\prime}}\lambda_{y}(x)
for
x\in\Gamma
and
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
, where
\alpha_{y\to y^{\prime}}>0
is a constant. Then, a probability distribution
\pi
on
\Gamma
is complex-balanced for
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
,
if and only if there exist non-negative functions
m_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,\ell
, such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\kappa_{y\to y^{\prime}}\big[\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)m_{k}(x-\phi(y))\big]^{-1},

for all
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
,
y\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
, and
x\in\Gamma

x\geq\phi(y)
, where
\kappa_{y\to y^{\prime}}>0

2.4

Assume the digraph of

A{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{3}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{3}}\mkern 5.0mu$}}}}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 5.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{3}}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{3}\mkern 9.0mu$}}}}$}}{}B.

The following system with mass-action kinetics

(2.10)

(A,1){}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{1}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{2}}\mkern 5.0mu$}}}}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 5.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{1}}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{2}\mkern 9.0mu$}}}}$}}{}(B,1),\quad(A,2){}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{2}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{1}}\mkern 5.0mu$}}}}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits^{\mkern 5.0mu\hphantom{{}\mathrm{2}}\mkern 9.0mu}_{\makebox{\raisebox{3.76735pt}[0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle\mkern 9.0mu{}\mathrm{1}\mkern 9.0mu$}}}}$}}{}(B,2),

and
\phi((A,i))=A
and
\phi((B,i))=B
for
i=1,2
, is a cleaved SRN of (
2.9
). The probability distribution
\pi(x)=\frac{M_{\Gamma}}{x!}
, where
M_{\Gamma}
is a normalising constant, is a complex-balanced distribution for (
2.9
) on any irreducible component
\Gamma

[
anderson2
, Theorem 4.1]
; hence also a stationary distribution. But
\pi
is not a complex-balanced distribution of (
2.10
); only a stationary distribution.

We end the section with a version of Theorem
2.2
for detailed-balanced SRNs. It is sufficient to consider only cycles of length two:

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume the digraph of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
is reversible. Then, a probability distribution
\pi
on
\Gamma
is detailed-balanced
if and only if there exist functions
m_{y\to y^{\prime}}\colon\Gamma_{y,y^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for all
y{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
, such that
m_{y\to y^{\prime}}=m_{y^{\prime}\to y}
and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=[m_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x-\phi(y))\pi(x)]^{-1},

where
\Gamma_{y,y^{\prime}}=(\{x-\phi(y)\colon x\in\Gamma\}\cup\{x-\phi(y^{\prime})\colon x\in\Gamma\})\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}.

3.
Examples

We present some examples to illustrate the results. Most reaction networks used in applications in biophysics, cellular biology and systems biology are not weakly reversible, let alone reversible. To remedy this, various techniques of network translation have been invented, that is, ways to transform a non-weakly reversible reaction network into a dynamically equivalent weakly reversible reaction network
[
hong2023computational
,
hong2021derivation
,
johnston1
,
johnston
]
. For example, one might add or delete species in equal numbers on both sides of a reaction, or split a reaction into two while preserving the total reaction rate. We will make use of these techniques too.

The main aim is to illustrate Theorem
2.2
. The one-node cleaving procedure used to prove Proposition
2.3
, is generally very laborious to apply (as there might be many cycles and nodes are cleaved one by one). We give one example of the procedure. In many cases, it seems more appropriate to adopt a manual approach to cleaving.

3.1.
Michaelis-Menten kinetics

Consider an enzyme-regulated mechanism for product formation with Michaelis-Menten kinetics
[

]

\displaystyle=\frac{x!}{\prod_{i=0}^{x_{S}}(\beta_{1}+i)\prod_{i=0}^{x_{P}}(\beta_{2}+i)},

\displaystyle m_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x!}{(\beta_{3}+x_{E})\prod_{i=0}^{x_{S}}(\beta_{1}+i)\prod_{i=0}^{x_{P}}(\beta_{2}+i)},

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{x_{S}}(\beta_{1}+i)\prod_{i=0}^{x_{P}}(\beta_{2}+i)}{x!},

4.
Stochastic reaction networks

In this section, we define SRNs and present a partial order on the space of SRNs. The main decomposition theorem (cleaving of SRNs) will make use of this partial order.

4.1.
Notation

Let
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be the set of real, non-negative and positive numbers, respectively. Let
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

be the set of integers and non-negative integers, respectively.
For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
x\geq y
, if
x_{i}\geq y_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
; and
x>y
if
x\geq y
and
x\neq y
. Furthermore, for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, the notation
x^{y}
is used for
\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{y_{i}}
, and for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we write
x!
for
x_{1}!\cdots x_{n}!
.

4.2.
Graph theory

Consider a
digraph

(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{V}
is a finite set of
nodes
and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
is a finite set of
edges
. A

(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
of
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is a digraph such that
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{E}
. Two sub-digraphs are
disjoint
if their sets of nodes are disjoint.

A
walk
is an ordered finite sequence of edges in
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, denoted by
\theta=(v_{1}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}v_{2},v_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}v_{3},\dots,v_{k-1}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}v_{k})
or
(v_{1}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}v_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}\cdots{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}v_{k})
for convenience. The walk is
closed
if
v_{1}=v_{k}
, and is
open
if it is not closed. An open walk
\theta
is
directed
from
v_{1}
to
v_{k}
, and
links

v_{1}
and
v_{k}
, vice versa
v_{k}
and
v_{1}
.
If all nodes are different, then
\theta
is a
path
, and if all nodes are different but
v_{1}=v_{k}
, then it is a
cycle
. Paths and cycles, but not walks, might be seen as sub-digraphs.

A digraph
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is
connected
, if for any pair of nodes
v,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
, there exist nodes
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1}
, and paths

in

, such that

and

and

for all

. A sub-digraph
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
is a
connected component
of

, if
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{E}
and no nodes

are linked to a node in
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. A connected component is

for any pair of nodes

The ensuing lemma then follows by definition.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a digraph satisfying the following

(i)

For any edge

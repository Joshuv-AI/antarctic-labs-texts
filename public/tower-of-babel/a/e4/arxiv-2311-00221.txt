Abstract
. We establish a uniform Sobolev inequality for Kähler metrics, which only require an entropy bound and no lower bound on the Ricci curvature. We further extend our Sobolev inequality to singular Kähler metrics on Kähler spaces with normal singularities. This allows us to build a general theory of global geometric analysis on singular Kähler spaces including the spectral theorem, heat kernel estimates, eigenvalue estimates and diameter estimates. Such estimates were only known previously in very special cases such as Bergman metrics. As a consequence, we derive various geometric estimates, such as the diameter estimate and the Sobolev inequality, for Kähler-Einstein currents on projective varieties with definite or vanishing first Chern class.



1.
Introduction

The Sobolev inequality is a fundamental tool in global geometric analysis. The optimal Sobolev constant reflects the geometric structure of the underlying Riemannian manifold, and geometric curvature bounds will in turn control the Sobolev constant. The classical works of Yau and his collaborators have built a wide range of geometric estimates for compact or complete Riemannian manifolds assuming a lower bound for the Ricci curvature (c.f.
[
30
,
5
,
6
]
). In
[
17
]
, we were able to establish uniform Green’s formula and diameter estimates for a large family of Kähler metrics on a Kähler manifold without such a Ricci curvature lower bound.
Rather, these estimates only depended on an entropy bound.
One of the first main results in the present paper
is to obtain a uniform Sobolev inequality for Kähler metrics, again assuming only an entropy bound and no Ricci curvature lower bound. As a consequence, under similar assumptions, we also obtain a wide range of geometric estimates such as heat kernel estimate and eigenvalue estimates.

In his celebrated work
[
40
]
on the Calabi conjecture, Yau initiated the theory of complex Monge-Ampère equations with singular data, in the hope of understanding canonical Kähler metrics with singularities and anticipating future applications to algebraic geometry. The analytic theory was subsequently developed by Kołodziej
[
22
]
using pluripotential theory, and later further extended to complex Monge-Ampère equations on singular Kähler varieties
[
12
,
9
]
. However, very little geometric information can be extracted by pluripotential theory for singular Kähler metrics, and particularly for Kähler-Einstein metrics on projective varieties with log terminal singularities. Very recently, an alternative proof of Kołodziej’s estimates has been found by Guo, Phong, and Tong in
[
19
]
, using PDE methods. These PDE methods have had many important consequences
[
16
,
15
,
17
]
.
Their key features are their flexibility and uniformity, which allow many geometric estimates to be passed on to Kähler spaces with normal singularities.

The next main goal of the present paper is to develop a geometric theory of complex Monge-Ampère equations with
singularities
. A theory with sharp geometric estimates had been a well-known challenge in presence of singularities. This is because geometric invariants such as the diameter and the Sobolev inequality are defined in terms of the underlying metric, whose very definition requires two derivatives of the potential. Bounds for such high derivatives of the potential are usually not available in presence of singularities. Another basic and subtle issue right at the start, is to identify suitable Kähler spaces with singularities which would contain Kähler spaces of interest, such as those carrying canonical metrics such as Kähler-Einstein metrics. In this context, we note that the standard notion on smooth Kähler metrics on normal varieties, defined as restrictions of smooth Kähler metrics on the ambiant space, is inadequate, since such notions would exclude Kähler-Einstein metrics.

Nevertheless, building in part on the methods of
[
19
,
18
,
17
]
and especially the uniform Sobolev estimates on Kähler manifolds obtained in the first part of the paper, we shall identify a natural class of singular Kähler spaces (c.f. Definition 3.1) and
establish for them a whole package of estimates such as Sobolev inequalities, the spectral theorem, heat kernel estimates, diameter estimates and volume non-collapsing estimates. These results provide both conceptual and technical platforms for a geometric regularity theory on singular Kähler spaces, which had long been an objective in both analytic and algebraic geometry. As an application, we derive a unique diameter bound for singular Kähler-Einstein spaces.
Applying our methods to the Kähler-Ricci flow, we can extend the Sobolev inequality to the metric completion of the singular Kähler current and get the diameter/noncollapsing bound from the Sobolev inequality without any curvature assumptions. We note that
diameter bounds for the metric completion
(\hat{X},d)
of normal Kähler spaces
have been an open problem for a long time except for special cases when
(X,\omega)
is a limit of a sequence of Kähler manifolds with suitable Ricci curvature bounds (cf.
[
28
,
32
]
).
On the analytic side, our Sobolev inequality allows classical PDE techniques such as the Moser’s iteration to work on singular Kähler spaces. Regarding the general problem of geometric analysis with singularities, we would like to mention the recent beautiful work of Cao et al.
[
4
]
on Hermitian-Einstein metrics on spaces with normal singularities in the sense of Grauert, which also relied in an essential manner on the estimates of
[
19
,
18
]
.

2.
Uniform bounds for smooth Kähler manifolds

We first state our results for smooth Kähler manifolds. Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold without boundary with
\omega_{X}
a fixed Kähler metric. Without loss of generality we normalize
\omega_{X}
such that
\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}=1
. Suppose that the complex dimension of
X
is
n
. Given a Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
, we denote its volume by

V_{\omega}=[\omega]^{n}=\int_{X}\omega^{n}

and define the relative volume function by

e^{F_{\omega}}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}.

Given
p\geq 1
we define the
p
-th Nash-Yau entropy of
\omega
by

{\mathcal{N}}_{p}(\omega)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\Big|\log\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\Big|^{p}\;{\omega^{n}}=\int_{X}|F_{\omega}|^{p}e^{F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}.

For a given
nonnegative

continuous
function
\gamma\in C^{0}(X)
, and given parameters
0<A\leq+\infty,K>0
, as in
[
17
]
, we consider the following subset of the space of Kähler metrics on
X
:

(2.1)

{\mathcal{W}}:={\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma):=\Big\{\omega:\;[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}<A,\,{\mathcal{N}}_{p}(\omega)\leq K,\,e^{F_{\omega}}\geq\gamma\Big\}.

It has been shown in
[
17
]
that if
p>n
and the vanishing locus of
\gamma
is relatively small (c.f. (
2.2
)), then the Kähler metrics
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
have uniformly bounded diameter and satisfy local noncollapsing. In this paper, we aim to study the heat kernels of the Laplacian
\Delta_{\omega}
of Kähler metrics
\omega
in this subset, and as applications, we give lower bounds of eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{\omega}
, by using the approach of Cheng-Li
[
5
]
. The main tool is the following Sobolev-type inequality. Throughout the paper, as in
[
17
]
we always assume
\gamma\in C^{0}(X)
satisfies

(2.2)

\dim_{{\mathcal{H}}}\{\gamma=0\}<2n-1,\quad\gamma\geq 0,

where
{\mathrm{dim}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}E
denotes the Hausdorff dimension of a closed subset
E\subset X
. In our earlier work
[
17
]
, uniform estimates are established for the Green’s function and the diameter associated to Kähler metrics in
\mathcal{W}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
. We denote

(2.3)

I_{\omega}=[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}

to be the intersection of the Kähler classes
[\omega]
and
[\omega_{X}]
. To state results for possibly unbounded Kähler classes, we will write the admissible set of Kähler metrics with
A=\infty
in (
2.1
) as
{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,\infty,K,\gamma)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
p>n
,
K>0
and
\gamma\in C^{0}(X)
satisfying (
2.2
), there exist
q=q(n,p)>1
and
C=C(n,p,K,\gamma,q)>0
such that for any Kähler metric
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,\infty,K,\gamma)
and any
u\in W^{1,2}(X)
, we have the following Sobolev-type inequality

(2.4)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u-\overline{u}|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C\frac{I_{\omega}}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n},

where
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}
is the average of
u
over
(X,\omega)
.

We remark that the Sobolev constant in Theorem
2.1
does not depend on the curvature of the Kähler metric
\omega
. The Sobolev inequality (
2.4
) is indeed
scale invariant
, although the exponent
q
may be smaller than
\frac{n}{n-1}
. We also stress that if we impose the Kołodziej-type condition on
e^{F_{\omega}}
, i.e. its
L^{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}(X,\omega_{X}^{n})
-norm is uniformly bounded for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, then the constant
q>1
can be chosen as close as possible to the exponent
\frac{n}{n-1}
as in the Euclidean case, with the constant
C_{q}
possibly blowing up as
q
approaches
\frac{n}{n-1}
. This fact will be clear from the proof of Theorem
2.1
by combining the almost sharp estimates on the Green’s functions established in
[
18
]
.

1

1

In
[
18
]
the metrics are assumed to be uniformly
big
, however, the same proof remains applicable to the degenerating metrics by slight modifications.
However, for the geometric applications in this paper, having
q>1
suffices.

We note that the Sobolev inequality (
2.4
) can also be used to prove the diameter estimate of
(X,\omega)
, which has been established in our previous work
[
17
]
using the Green’s formula. We are informed by Guedj, Guenancia and Zeriahi that they very recently obtained diameter estimates under hypotheses different from ours (c.f.
[
14
]
).

With the Sobolev inequality in Theorem
2.1
, we can now state the heat kernel upper bounds and eigenvalue lower bounds for Kähler metrics in
\mathcal{W}(n,p,\infty,K,\gamma)
. Recall that the heat kernel of the Laplacian
\Delta_{\omega}
satisfies the equation

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H(x,y,t)=\Delta_{\omega,y}H(x,y,t)\quad\mbox{and }\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}H(x,y,t)=\delta_{x}(y).

It is well-known that
H(x,y,t)
is a positive smooth function on
X\times X\times(0,\infty)
, and it is symmetric in
x
and
y
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
q=q(n,p)>1
be the constant in Theorem
2.1
. There exists
C=C(n,p,K,\gamma)>0
such that for any
x,y\in X

(2.6)

H(x,y,t)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\big(\frac{I_{\omega}}{t}\big)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\exp\big({-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{10t}}\big),&\quad\mbox{if }t\in(0,I_{\omega}]\\
\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\exp\big({-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{10t}}\big),&\quad\mbox{if }t\in(I_{\omega},\infty).\end{array}\right.

Here
d_{\omega}(x,y)
is the geodesic distance between
x
and
y
with repsect to the metric
\omega
.
When
x=y
, a stronger upper bound of the heat kernel on the diagonal of
X\times X
holds as below,

(2.7)

H(x,x,t)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}I_{\omega}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}t^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}.

Moreover, if
t\geq I_{\omega}
, we have for some positive constant
c>0

(2.8)

\sup_{x,y\in X}\big|H(x,y,t)-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\big|\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}e^{-cI_{\omega}^{-1}t}.

The classical heat kernel upper bound is achieved in
[
6
]
. Theorem
2.2
does not require any assumption on the Ricci curvature and it will also give uniform lower bounds for the eigenvalues associated to metrics in
\mathcal{W}(n,p,\infty,K,\gamma)
.
Let
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots
(counting multiplicity) be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of the Laplacian
-\Delta_{\omega}
. We have the following lower bound on these eigenvalues.

Corollary 2.1
.

There exists
c=c(n,p,K,\gamma)>0
such that for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\lambda_{k}\geq c\,I_{\omega}^{-1}k^{\frac{q-1}{q}}.

We observe that the uniform positive lower bound of
\lambda_{1}
was proved in
[
17
]
. The known estimates on higher order eigenvalues required lower bounds on the Ricci curvature
[
5
]
.

3.
Applications to singular Kähler spaces

In this section, we will apply the Sobolev inequality and the heat kernel estimates to normal Kähler varieties. We will focus on the family of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents whose log volume measure has only log type analytic singularities (c.f. Definition
7.2
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler space. Let
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
be a log resolution of singularities and let
\theta_{Y}
be a smooth Kähler metric on the nonsingular model
Y
. We define the set of admissible semi-Kähler currents

{\mathcal{AK}}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma),

to be the set of any semi-Kähler current
\omega
on
X
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

[\omega]
is a Kähler class on
X
and
\omega
has bounded local potentials.

(2)

[\pi^{*}\omega]\cdot[\theta_{Y}]^{n-1}\leq A
and
[\omega]^{n}\geq A^{-1}
.

(3)

The
p
-th Nash-Yau entropy is bounded for some
p>n
, i.e.

{\mathcal{N}}_{p}(\omega)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{Y}\left|\log\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}}{(\theta_{Y})^{n}}\right|^{p}(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}\leq K,

where
V_{\omega}=[\omega]^{n}
.

(4)

The log volume measure ratio

\log\left(\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}}{(\theta_{Y})^{n}}\right)

has log type analytic singularities (c.f. Definition
7.2
). There also exists a non-negative
\gamma\in C^{0}(Y)
such that
\{\gamma=0\}
is contained in a proper analytic subvariety of
Y
and

\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}}{(\theta_{Y})^{n}}\geq\gamma.

The nonsingular model
Y
is indeed Kähler (cf. Lemma 2.2
[
7
]
) in Definition
3.1
. The
\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
space is the extension of
\mathcal{W}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
with two key additional conditions: (1) uniform lower bound of the volume for
[\omega]
to prevent collapsing; (2) the volume measure ratio has log type analytic singularities to guarantee smoothness of
\omega
in an open Zariski subset of
X
.

Smooth Kähler metrics on a normal variety
X
are defined locally as the restriction of smooth Kähler metrics in the ambient space (cf. Definition
7.1
). In general,
canonical metrics on
X
such as Kähler-Einstein metrics are not smooth Kähler metrics in this sense. For example, if
X
is a projective variety with orbifold singularities, the smooth orbifold Kähler metrics are never smooth Kähler metrics on
X
. However, the Kähler-Einstein metrics on projective varieties belong to the
\mathcal{AK}
-space of Definition
3.1
and they are not smooth near the algebraic singularities. This is one of the reasons we consider the
\mathcal{AK}
-spaces.

Remark 3.1
.

The space
\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
in Definition (
3.1
) can be easily generalized for positive currents
\omega
with minimal singularities in a big and nef class.
Most of our results still hold for such positive currents
\omega
. We choose to state our results for
\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
with the Kähler assumptions for convenience and simplicity. In fact, the definition for
\mathcal{AK}
-space of currents can be further extended without the nef assumption.

For any
\omega\in\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
, we let
\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
be the union of the singular set of
X
and the singular set of
\pi_{*}\left(\log\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}}{(\theta_{Y})^{n}}\right)
. By definition,
\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
is an analytic subvariety of
X
. The current
\omega
is a smooth Kähler metric on
X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
(c.f. Proposition
7.1
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional a compact normal Kähler variety and

\omega\in\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma).

We define

(\hat{X},d)=\overline{(X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega},\omega|_{X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}})}

to be the metric completion of
\left(X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega},\omega|_{X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}}\right)
. We also denote the unique metric measure space associated with
(X,\omega)
by

(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n}).

We remark that
\omega^{n}
extends uniquely to a volume measure on
\hat{X}
because both
\omega
and
\omega^{n}
does not carry mass on
\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
.

Our goal is to extend Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
to the metric measure space
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
associated to
(X,\omega)
. We then apply the Sobolev inequality to establish the volume non-collapsing and diameter bounds for
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
in Section
9
. A spectral theorem and heat kernel estimates will also be proved in Section
10
and Section
11
. Finally, we will apply our estimates to singular Kähler-Einstein spaces in Section
12
and limiting spaces of non-collapsing finite time solutions of the Kähler-Ricci flow in Section
13
.

3.1.
Sobolev inequality, heat kernel and diameter estimates

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional normal Kähler variety. For any
p>n
and any
\omega\in\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
, we let
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
the metric measure space associated to
(X,\omega)
. We will consider the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
as in Definition
8.1
on the metric measure space
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
associated to
\omega
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact normal Kähler space. For any
\omega\in\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
, the metric measure space
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
associated to
(X,\omega)
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

There exists
C=C(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that

{\textnormal{diam}}(\hat{X},d)\leq C.

In particular,
(\hat{X},d)
is a compact metric space.

(2)

There exist
q>1
and
C_{S}=C_{S}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma,q)>0
such that

\Big(\int_{\hat{X}}|u|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C_{S}\left(\int_{\hat{X}}|\nabla u|^{2}~\omega^{n}+\int_{\hat{X}}u^{2}\omega^{n}\right).

for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
.

(3)

There exists
C=C(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that the following trace formula holds for the heat kernel of
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})

H(x,x,t)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}t^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}.

(4)

Let
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of the Laplacian
-\Delta_{\omega}
on
(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})
. Then there exists
c=c(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that

\lambda_{k}\geq ck^{\frac{q-1}{q}}.

To the best of our knowledge,
Theorem
3.1
is the first to provide general formulas for the Sobolev inequality, the heat kernel, and eigenvalue estimates on complex spaces with singularities. Such estimates were only known previously to hold in special cases such as the following:

(1)

X
is projective and
\omega
is a Bergman metric via projective embedding (cf.
[
24
,
3
]
).

(2)

(X,\omega)
is a sequential Gromov-Hausdorff limit of Kähler-Einstein manifolds (cf.
[
11
]
).

Geometric estimates are traditionally studied for smooth Kähler metrics on singular Kähler spaces. The most immediate concern is that, as we pointed out earlier, canonical Kähler metrics near algebraic singularities cannot be in general the restriction of smooth metrics via local or global embeddings. In addition, most algebraic singularities are not smoothable and so most singular Kähler metrics cannot be approximated by smooth Kähler metrics with Ricci curvature uniformly bounded from below.

Thus the traditional geometric estimates cannot be directly applied to the study of canonical metrics. Nor do the traditional methods of proof, which usually require that the metrics are smooth Kähler metrics on normal Kähler varieties.
For example, the heat kernel trace formula and Sobolev inequalities are obtained in
[
27
,
24
]
for Bergman metrics on projective varieties. Bergman metrics are restrictions of Fubini-Study metrics via global projective embeddings and so they are smooth Kähler metrics on normal Kähler varieties.

Canonical metrics, such as Kähler-Einstein metrics, require rather a different and much larger class of singular Kähler spaces, such as the classes
\mathcal{AK}
-spaces as in Definition
3.1
. These lie beyond the reach of traditional methods, but they can be treated by our Sobolev inequality and trace formula for the heat kernel. We also observe that our Sobolev inequality can be applied to the study of Hermitian-Einstein metrics on stable sheaves over singular normal Kähler spaces as developed by
[
2
,
4
]
.

As we had stressed in the Introduction,
diameter bounds for
(X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega})
or
(\hat{X},d)
have been an open problem for a long time. The uniform diameter bound in Theorem
3.1
is particularly striking, in the sense that it mostly relies on the entropy bound rather than the topological and complex structures of singularities.

3.2.
Singular Kähler-Einstein metrics

We will apply the diameter estimates and Sobolev estimates of Theorem
3.1
to Kähler-Einstein spaces.

Let
X
be a projective Calabi-Yau variety of
\dim X=n
, i.e.
X
is normal and
c_{1}(X)=0
. It is proved in

(1,1)

is a smooth Kähler metric on

, the regular part of

. We will apply the Sobolev inequality (

) to obtain the following geometric estimates.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a projective Calabi-Yau variety with log terminal singularities. Then for any Kähler class
\alpha

\omega_{CY}\in\alpha
satisfying the following.

(1)

Let
(\hat{X},d_{CY})
be the metric completion of
(X^{\circ},\omega_{CY})

(\hat{X},d_{CY},(\omega_{CY})^{n})
is a compact metric measure space.

(2)

For any
q\in(1,\frac{n}{n-1})
, there exists
C_{S}=C_{S}(X,n,\alpha,q)>0
such that

(3.1)

\Big(\int_{\hat{X}}|u|^{2q}(\omega_{CY})^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C_{S}\left(\int_{\hat{X}}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega_{CY}}~(\omega_{CY})^{n}+\int_{\hat{X}}u^{2}(\omega_{CY})^{n}\right).

for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\hat{X},d_{CY},(\omega_{CY})^{n})
.

(3)

For any
\mu>n
, there exists
\kappa=\kappa(X,n,\alpha,\mu)>0
such that for any
p\in\hat{X}
and
r\in(0,\textnormal{diam}(\hat{X},d_{CY})]
,

\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}(B(p,r))}{r^{2\mu}}\geq\kappa,

where
B(p,r)
denotes the ball in
(\hat{X},d)
with center
p
and radius
r
.

We note that Theorem
3.2
remains valid when
X
is only a
Kähler
Calabi-Yau variety. The proof remains unchanged.

We will also derive geometric estimates for Kähler-Einstein currents on canonical models. Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional projective normal variety with ample

and log terminal singularities. There exists a unique Kähler-Einstein current
\omega_{KE}\in[K_{X}]
constructed in
[
12
]
such that
\omega_{KE}
has bounded local potential and volume measure satisfying

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\omega_{KE})=-\omega_{KE}

smoothly on
X^{\circ}
, the regular part of
X
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional projective normal variety with ample
K_{X}
and log terminal singularities. Then the unique Kähler-Einstein current
\omega_{KE}\in[K_{X}]
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

Let
(\hat{X},d_{KE})
be the metric completion of
(X^{\circ},\omega_{KE})
. Then
(\hat{X},d_{KE},(\omega_{KE})^{n})
is a compact metric measure space.

(2)

For any
q\in(1,\frac{n}{n-1})
, there exists
C_{S}=C_{S}(X,n,q)>0
such that

\Big(\int_{\hat{X}}|u|^{2q}(\omega_{KE})^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C_{S}\left(\int_{\hat{X}}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega_{KE}}(\omega_{KE})^{n}+\int_{\hat{X}}u^{2}(\omega_{KE})^{n}\right).

for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\hat{X},d_{KE},(\omega_{KE})^{n})
.

(3)

For any
\mu>n
, there exists
\kappa=\kappa(X,n,\mu)>0
such that for any
p\in\hat{X}
and
r\in(0,\textnormal{diam}(\hat{X},d_{KE})]
,

\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}(B(p,r))}{r^{2\mu}}\geq\kappa.

The existence of Kähler-Einstein currents on a K-stable
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety is established in
[
23
,
25
]
as a singular version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture. The analogue of Theorem
3.3
also holds for such singular Kähler-Einstein metrics with positive scalar curvature.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety with log terminal singularities. If there exists a Kähler-Einstein current
\omega\in-[K_{X}]
with bounded local potentials, then
\omega\in C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
and
(\hat{X},d_{KE},(\omega_{KE})^{n})
associated with
(X,\omega_{KE})
is a compact metric measure space satisfying estimates in (2) and (3) of Theorem
3.3
.

We stress that the above diameter and non-collapsing estimates go considerably farther than those obtained in
[
17
]
. Indeed, the diameter and non-collapsing estimates
in
[
17
]
are for smooth Kähler manifolds, and even though they were uniform in many key parameters, they still do not apply to the present setting of singular spaces
(X,\omega)
by taking limits. This is because the convergence is only uniform on any compact subset of the regular part of
X
, which is too weak and not sufficient to obtain the convergence of geodesics or geodesic balls. Furthermore, the arguments in
[
17
]
cannot be adapted to the singular setting because of the lack of estimates of the Green’s functions on the singular space. Instead, we have to rely on the Sobolev inequality to prove these estimates.

The Sobolev inequality has the advantage that it can be passed down to the limit or singular space, thanks to the uniform Sobolev inequality we establish on the smooth resolution. However, this is not obvious for the Green’s function because the gradient of Green’s function is not in
L^{2}
. This latter is the technique we used in
[
17
]
.

It is also the Sobolev inequality which enables us to estimate the heat kernel of the Laplacian, and its eigenvalues, hence obtain a spectral theorem on singular spaces (more precisely on the metric completion of such spaces).

3.3.
Finite time singularities of the Kähler-Ricci flow

We consider the following unnormalized Kähler-Ricci flow on a Kähler manifold
X
with an initial Kähler metric
g_{0}

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-\textnormal{Ric}(g),}\\
\\
g|_{t=0}=g_{0}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}).\end{array}\right.

Let

(3.3)

T=\sup\{t>0~|~[g_{0}]+t[K_{X}]>0\}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}.

The Kähler-Ricci flow has a maximal solution
g(t)
for
t\in[0,T)
. If
T<\infty
, the flow (
3.2
) must develop singularities at
t=T
and the curvature tensors must blow up as
t\rightarrow T^{-}
. In this case,
\alpha_{T}=[g_{0}]+T[K_{X}]
is a nef class on
X
. Since
[g_{0}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
,
\alpha_{T}
is semi-ample by Kawamata’s base point free theorem and the numerical dimension of
\alpha_{T}
coincides with its Kodaira dimension. In particular,
\alpha_{T}
induces a surjective holomorphic map

(3.4)

\Phi:X\rightarrow Y.

We consider the case when
\alpha_{T}
is big, which is equivalent to either of the following conditions

(1)

(\alpha_{T})^{n}=\lim_{t\rightarrow T}{\rm Vol}_{g(t)}(X)>0.

(2)

\dim Y=n
.

We let
S
be the exceptional locus of
\Phi
, i.e., where
\Phi
is not biholomorphic.
S
is then an analytic subvariety of
X
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(X,g_{0})
be a Kähler manifold equipped with a Kähler metric
g_{0}
. If
g(t)
is the maximal solution of the Kähler-Ricci flow (
3.2
) for
t\in[0,T)
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and if the limiting class
\alpha_{T}
is big, then the following hold.

(1)

g(t)
converges smoothly a Kähler metric
g(T)
on
X\setminus S
. There exists
D=D(X,g_{0},n)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
,

diam(X,g(t))\leq D.

For any
\mu>n
, there exists
\kappa=\kappa(X,g_{0},n,\mu)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
and
r\in(0,D]
,

\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}_{g(t)}(B_{g(t)}(x,r))}{r^{2\mu}}\geq\kappa.

(2)

Let
\omega(T)
be the unique positive current extended by
g(T)
and
(\hat{X},d_{T})
be the metric completion of
(X\setminus S,g(T))
. Then the metric measure space
(X_{T},d_{T},\omega(T)^{n})
is a compact metric space. For any
\mu>n
, there exists
\kappa=\kappa(\mu)>0
such that for any
r\in(0,\textnormal{diam}(X_{T},d_{T}))
, we have

\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}_{\omega(T)^{n}}(B_{d_{T}}(x,r))}{r^{2\mu}}\geq\kappa

for any
x\in X_{T}
.

As in the previous section, it is worth pointing out that these results go beyond the corresponding ones in
[
17
]
, as they apply directly to the limiting Kähler current at the singular time
T
. In
[
17
]
, only uniform bounds for the diameter and noncollapsing for the smooth metrics along the KR flow were obtained, but it is not possible to take limits because the convergence on compact subsets of the smooth parts is too weak. Again, it is the Sobolev inequality extended to the metric completion of the singular Kähler current that can apply to
get the present diameter and noncollapsing estimates.

The first statement of Theorem
3.5
implies that after passing to a sequence
t_{j}\rightarrow T^{-}
,
(X,g(t_{j}))
always converges in Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a compact metric space
(\hat{X}_{T},\hat{d}_{T})
which satisfies the same volume non-collapsing condition. In fact, the identity map between
X\setminus S
and itself extends to a surjective Lipschitz map from
(X_{T},d_{T})
to
(\hat{X}_{T},\hat{d}_{T})
. We conjecture that

(\hat{X}_{T},\hat{d}_{T})
and
(X_{T},d_{T})
are isomorphic and they are both homeomorphic to the original variety
Y
. This conjecture holds for Kähler surfaces by the work of
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
31
]
. We would also like to remark that Theorem
3.5
still holds if
[g_{0}]
is a general Kähler class as a consequence of Remark
3.1
, since the limiting class
\alpha
is big and nef. Theorem
3.5
can also be generalized to the unique analytic solution
g(t)
to the Kähler-Ricci flow on projective varieties with log terminal singularities whenever
t>0
, because the semi-Kähler current
g(t)
lies in
\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,p,A,K,\gamma)
for suitable choices of a log resolution
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
,
\theta_{Y}
,
A
,
p>n
and
K>0
whenever
t
is uniformly bounded away from
0
.

4.
The Sobolev-type inequality

In this section we will prove the Sobolev inequality in Theorem
2.1
. We first let
A>0
be a finite number and consider the parameters
p>n,K>0
and
\gamma\in C^{0}(X)
satisfying (
2.2
). We will fix a Kähler metric
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
. We recall the
Green’s function

G_{\omega}(x,y)
of the Laplacian
\Delta_{\omega}
on
X
is defined by

\Delta_{\omega}G_{\omega}(x,\cdot)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}-\delta_{x}(\cdot),\quad\int_{X}G_{\omega}(x,\cdot)\omega^{n}=0,

where
\delta_{x}(\cdot)
is the Dirac function at the base point
x\in X
. It is well-known that
G_{\omega}
always exists and is smooth in
X\times X
off the diagonal. Moreover,
G_{\omega}
is symmetric in the sense that
G_{\omega}(x,y)=G_{\omega}(y,x)
for any
x\neq y
. We first recall the lower bound of
G_{\omega}
from
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

There exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that

\inf_{x,y\in X}G_{\omega}(x,y)\geq-C_{0}/V_{\omega}.

Given this lemma, we denote the positive function

{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}=G_{\omega}+\frac{C_{0}+1}{V_{\omega}}\geq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}},

which still satisfies the Green’s formula. We also need the following uniform integral estimates of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}
and
\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}
:

Lemma 4.2

(
[
17
,
18
]
)
.

(i). There exist constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,p)>0
such that

(4.1)

\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{V_{\omega}^{\varepsilon_{0}}}.

(ii). For any
\beta>0
we have

(4.2)

\sup_{x\in X}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}}{{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}.

With Lemma
4.2
, we now give the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
: By approximation, we may assume the function
u\in C^{1}(X)
. We apply the Green’s formula associated to the metric
\omega
to obtain

u(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}+\int_{X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot),\nabla u\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}

for any
x\in X
. Denote
{\overline{u}}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}
to be the average of
u
over
(X,\omega^{n})
. By the triangle inequality this gives

\displaystyle|u(x)-\overline{u}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)|_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{\omega}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)|_{\omega}}{{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{(1+\beta)/2}}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{(1+\beta)/2}|\nabla u|_{\omega}\omega^{n}

(by Hölder’s ineq.)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{\omega}}{{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}

(4.3)

by (
4.2
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}.

Here we fix the constant
\beta=\varepsilon_{0}/2
, where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is as in (
4.1
), and

(4.4)

q=\frac{1+\varepsilon_{0}}{1+\beta}=\frac{1+\varepsilon_{0}}{1+(\varepsilon_{0}/2)}>1.

Taking
(2q)
-th power on both sides of (
4.3
), we obtain

|u(x)-\overline{u}|^{2q}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta q}}{\beta^{q}}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}(y)\Big)^{q}.

We first integrate this inequality over
X
against the measure
\omega^{n}
, and then take the
q
-th root of the resulted integrals, which reads

(4.5)

\Big(\int_{X}|u(x)-\overline{u}|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}\Big\{\int_{x\in X}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}(y)\Big)^{q}\omega^{n}(x)\Big\}^{1/q}.

Applying the generalized Minkowski inequality to the right-hand side of (
4.5
), we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Big\{\int_{x\in X}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}(y)\Big)^{q}\omega^{n}(x)\Big\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla u(y)|^{2}_{\omega}\Big(\int_{x\in X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{(1+\beta)q}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{1/q}\omega^{n}(y).

By the symmetry of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}
, the integral factor in (
4.6
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{x\in X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{(1+\beta)q}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(y,x)^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sup_{y\in X}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(y,x)^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{1/q}

by (
4.1
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{C_{1}}{V_{\omega}^{\varepsilon_{0}}}\Big)^{1/q}.

Combining this with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\noindent\Big(\int_{X}|u(x)-\overline{u}|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}\frac{C_{1}^{1/q}}{V_{\omega}^{\varepsilon_{0}/q}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}=\frac{C_{2}}{V_{\omega}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n},

where
C_{2}=C_{1}^{1/q}/\beta>0
depends only on
n,p,A,K,\gamma
. From (
4.7
), it is clear that the Sobolev inequality holds, i.e.

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u(x)-\overline{u}|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}.

To see the scale-invariant inequality (
2.4
), suppose
\omega\in{\mathcal{K}}(X)
satisfies
F_{\omega}\geq\gamma
and
\|e^{F_{\omega}}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}(\omega_{X}^{n})}\leq K
. Set
\hat{\omega}=I_{\omega}^{-1}\omega
. It is elementary to see that
\hat{\omega}\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
with
A=1
. So the inequality (
4.8
) holds for the metric
\hat{\omega}
with a constant
C_{2}^{\prime}
which is independent of
A
as stated, i.e.,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\hat{\omega}}}\int_{X}|u(x)-\overline{u}|^{2q}\hat{\omega}^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C_{2}^{\prime}}{V_{\hat{\omega}}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\hat{\omega}}^{2}\hat{\omega}^{n}.

Then the inequality (
2.4
) follows by observing that
|\nabla u|_{\hat{\omega}}^{2}=I_{\omega}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}
and
\frac{\hat{\omega}^{n}}{V_{\hat{\omega}}}=\frac{\omega^{n}}{V_{\omega}}
.
The proof of Theorem
2.1
is completed. ∎

By combining (
4.8
) with the Minkowski inequality, we obtain the following form of the Sobolev inequality, which is better suited for the Moser’s iteration scheme:

(4.10)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(u^{2}+|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2})\omega^{n},

where
C_{3}=\max(2C_{2}^{2},1)
.

In case the Kähler metric
\omega
satisfies the conditions

e^{F_{\omega}}\geq\gamma,\quad\|e^{F_{\omega}}\|_{L^{1+\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\leq K,\quad[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}\leq A,

where
\gamma
satisfies the condition (
2.2
), and
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
is a positive constant, we have the following improved estimates on
(X,\omega)
which may be of independent interest.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the assumptions be as above. For any
1\leq p^{\prime}<n
and
q^{\prime}\in(p^{\prime},\frac{np^{\prime}}{n-p^{\prime}})
, there exists a constant
C>0
which depends on
n,K,A,\gamma,\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
such that

(4.11)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u-{\overline{u}}|^{2q^{\prime}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q^{\prime}}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2p^{\prime}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

for all
u\in C^{1}(X)
. In particular, we have the imbedding
W^{1,2p^{\prime}}(X,\omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2q^{\prime}}(X,\omega)
.

Proof.

We know from
[
18
,
17
]
that the Green’s function satisfies

\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{s}\omega^{n}(y)\leq\frac{C(s)}{V_{\omega}^{s-1}}

for all
s\in[1,\frac{n}{n-1})
,
for some constant
C(s)>0
depending additionally on
s
besides the given parameters. Given the constants
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
as in the statement of the theorem, we define

r=({1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}})^{-1}\in(1,\frac{n}{n-1}).

We choose the constant
\beta\in(0,1)
small enough in (
4.3
) such that
(1+\beta)r<\frac{n}{n-1}
.
We define a linear operator as follows: (below
r^{*}=\frac{r}{r-1}
)

{\mathbf{T}}:L^{r^{*}}\cap L^{1}\to L^{\infty}\cap L^{r},\quad{\mathbf{T}}f(x)=\int{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{1+\beta}f(y)\omega^{n}(y).

We can verify that
{\mathbf{T}}
is of strong types
(1,r)
and
(r^{*},+\infty)
. Indeed, we calculate as follows.

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{T}}f\|_{L^{r}(\omega^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X}|{\mathbf{T}}f(x)|^{r}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\int_{X}\Big(\int_{x\in X}{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{r(1+\beta)}\omega^{n}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}|f(y)|\omega^{n}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}V_{\omega}^{-(1+\beta)+1/r}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\omega^{n})},

where in the first inequality we have applied the generalized Minkowski inequality. For any
x\in X
, by Hölder’s inequality we have

|{\mathbf{T}}f(x)|\leq\Big(\int{\mathcal{G}}_{\omega}(x,y)^{r(1+\beta)}\omega^{n}(y)\Big)^{1/r}\|f\|_{L^{r^{*}}(\omega^{n})}\leq C_{1}V_{\omega}^{-(1+\beta)+1/r}\|f\|_{L^{r^{*}}(\omega^{n})},

which implies that
\|{\mathbf{T}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}V_{\omega}^{-(1+\beta)+1/r}\|f\|_{L^{r^{*}}(\omega^{n})}
. Then we apply the Riesz-Thorin Interpolation Theorem to conclude that for any
t\in(0,1)
, and the pair of numbers
p_{t},q_{t}
given by

\frac{1}{p_{t}}=\frac{1-t}{r^{*}}+{t},\quad\frac{1}{q_{t}}=\frac{t}{r},\,\mbox{respectively, }

{\mathbf{T}}
is of strong type
(p_{t},q_{t})
. More precisely, the following holds,

(4.12)

\|{\mathbf{T}}f\|_{L^{q_{t}}(\omega^{n})}\leq C_{1}V_{\omega}^{-(1+\beta)+1/r}\|f\|_{L^{p_{t}}(\omega^{n})}.

In particular, taking
t={r}/{q^{\prime}}\in(0,1)
, we see that
p_{t}=p^{\prime}
and
q_{t}=q^{\prime}
. To finish the proof we recall that from (
4.3
), we have the following

|u(x)-{\overline{u}}|^{2}\leq\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}{\mathbf{T}}(|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2})(x)

for all
x\in X
. We can now apply the inequality (
4.12
) with
f:=|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}
to conclude that

(4.13)

\||u-{\overline{u}}|^{2}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\omega^{n})}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\beta}V_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\||\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega^{n})}.

Clearly here the
\beta
can be chosen depending only on
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
. The desired inequality (
4.11
) follows easily from some rearrangement of (
4.13
).
∎

We next discuss a few examples when the Sobolev inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.10
) hold.

Example 4.1
Let
\pi:(X,\omega_{X})\to(Y,\omega_{Y})
be a surjective holomorphic map between compact Kähler manifolds
X
and
Y
. Assume that
{\mathrm{dim}}(X)=n\geq{\mathrm{dim}}(Y)=m
. Consider the continuous family of Kähler metrics
\omega=\omega_{t}:=t\omega_{X}+\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}
for
t\in(0,1]
. It is easy to see that
V_{\omega}\sim t^{n-m}
, and

e^{F_{\omega}}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\sim\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}t^{n-k}\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega_{Y})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\leq C

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n,m,\omega_{X},\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}
but independent of
t
. The intersection number
[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}
is clearly bounded. The expected lower bound
\gamma
of
e^{F_{\omega}}
can be taken as suitable multiple of
\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega_{Y})^{m}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-m}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}
, which is equal to the squared Jacobian of the holomorphic map
\pi:(X,\omega_{X})\to(Y,\omega_{Y})
. The Jacobian of
\pi
consists of nontrivial (local) holomorphic functions hence its vanishing locus
\{\gamma=0\}
is a proper analytic subvariety in
X
. Hence the assumption (
2.2
) on
\gamma
is also satisfied. Given these, we find that the Sobolev inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.10
) hold for this continuous family of Kähler metrics, with the constants depending on
n,\pi,\omega_{X},\omega_{Y}
, but not
t\in(0,1]
.

We remark that when
\pi:X\to Y
is the blown-up of a
smooth
center in
Y
, the uniform Sobolev inequality (
4.10
) is known by the work of Bando-Siu in
[
2
]
with
q=\frac{n}{n-1}
. However, for the case of general surjective holomorphic maps, as far as we know, the uniform Sobolev inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.10
) are new.

Example 4.2
Consider the finite-time Kähler-Ricci flow
\omega=\omega_{t}
:
\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}=-\textnormal{Ric}(\omega)
with
t\in[0,T)
and
T<\infty
. If the limit current
[\omega_{T}]
is big, in the sense that
[\omega_{T}]^{n}>0
, then by
[
17
]
, the metrics
\omega_{t}
lie in
{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
for suitable parameters depending only on
n,\omega_{t}|_{t=0}
and
[\omega_{T}]
. Hence the uniform Sobolev inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.10
) hold for these metrics
\omega_{t}
with
t\in[0,T)
.

Example 4.3
Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with nef canonical line bundle
K_{X}
and nonnegative Kodaira dimension. Suppose
\omega=\omega_{t}
satisfies the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow
\tfrac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}=-\textnormal{Ric}(\omega)-\omega
. Again by
[
17
]
, the metrics
\omega_{t}
lie in
{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
for suitable parameters depending only on
n,\omega_{t}|_{t=0}
and
K_{X}
. Hence the uniform Sobolev inequalities (
4.8
) and (
4.10
) hold for these metrics
\omega_{t}
with
t\in[0,\infty)
.

5.
Upper bound of the heat kernel

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
be a fixed Kähler metric, and
H(x,y,t)
be the heat kernel of
\Delta_{\omega}
as defined in the equation (
2.5
).

5.1.
Off diagonal estimates

Given the Sobolev inequality (
4.10
), we will make use of the ideas of Davies
[
8
]
(see also
[
41

to prove a Gaussian-type upper bound of the hear kernel
H(x,y,t)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(X)
be an arbitrary positive smooth function on
X
. For a given
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and an arbitrary Lipschitz function
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq 1

u(x,t)
defined by

(5.1)

u(x,t)=e^{-\alpha\phi(x)}\int_{X}H(x,y,t)e^{\alpha\phi(y)}u_{0}(y)\omega^{n}(y),

which is positive and satisfies the equation

(5.2)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=e^{-\alpha\phi}\Delta_{\omega}(ue^{\alpha\phi}),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)

for all
x\in X
.
Fix any time
T>0
and a constant
\beta\in(0,1)
(for our purpose, it suffices to take
\beta=1/2
). We define an increasing, piecewise smooth, and continuous function
r(t)
of

by

r=r(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2^{\beta}-1}{(T/2)^{\beta}}t^{\beta}+1,&\quad\mbox{if }t\in[0,T/2),\\
\frac{T^{\beta}}{(T-t)^{\beta}},&\quad\mbox{if }t\in[T/2,T),\end{array}\right.

which satisfies
r(0)=1
and
r(T^{-})=\infty
. Denote
\|u\|_{r}
to be the
L^{r}(X,\omega^{n})
-norm of
u
. By straightforward calculations, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\|u\|_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\log\|u\|_{r}+\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}u^{r-1}e^{-\alpha\phi}\Delta_{\omega}(ue^{\alpha\phi})\omega^{n}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}u^{r}(\log u)\,\omega^{n}.

By integration by parts, the last term in (
5.3

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}u^{r-1}e^{-\alpha\phi}\Delta_{\omega}(ue^{\alpha\phi})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}\langle\nabla(u^{r-1}e^{-\alpha\phi}),\nabla(ue^{\alpha\phi})\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}-(r-1)u^{r-2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\alpha(r-2)u^{r-1}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi\rangle_{\omega}+\alpha^{2}u^{r}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}-(r-1)(1-\delta)u^{r-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}(r-2)^{2}}{4(r-1)\delta}u^{r}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\alpha^{2}u^{r}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}-\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}|\nabla u^{r/2}|^{2}\omega^{n}+\frac{\alpha^{2}(r-2)^{2}}{4(r-1)\delta}+\alpha^{2},

where in the first inequality we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with
\delta\in(0,1)
a constant to be chosen later, and in the last line we use the condition that
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.

For notational convenience we set
v=\frac{u^{r/2}}{\|u^{r/2}\|_{2}}
, which satisfies
\|v\|_{2}=1
. Note that
\|u^{r/2}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u\|_{r}^{r}.
The term in (
5.4

\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\frac{1}{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}\int_{X}u^{r}\log u\,\omega^{n}=\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\int_{X}v^{2}\log v^{2}\,\omega^{n}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\log\|u\|_{r}.

Therefore, we have from (
5.3
) and (
5.5
) that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\|u\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}\int_{X}|\nabla v|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\int_{X}v^{2}\log v^{2}\,\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha^{2}(r-2)^{2}}{4(r-1)\delta}+\alpha^{2}.

We next apply the Sobolev inequality (
4.10
) to deal with the last term in (
5.6
).

\displaystyle\int_{X}v^{2}\log v^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{X}v^{2}\log v^{2(q-1)}\omega^{n}

(by Jensen’s inequality)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\log\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}v^{2q}\omega^{n}+\frac{\log V_{\omega}}{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(v^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})\omega^{n}\Big)+\frac{\log V_{\omega}}{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(\int_{X}(v^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})\omega^{n}\Big)-\log V_{\omega}+\frac{q}{q-1}\log C.

Combining this with (
5.6

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\|u\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}\int_{X}|\nabla v|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(1+\int_{X}|\nabla v|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log{C}-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log V_{\omega}+\frac{\alpha^{2}(r-2)^{2}}{4(r-1)\delta}+\alpha^{2}.

Claim:
The following inequality holds:

\displaystyle-\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}\int_{X}|\nabla v|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(1+\int_{X}|\nabla v|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(\frac{r^{\prime}q}{4(r-1)(1-\delta)(q-1)}\Big)+\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}.

This claim follows easily from the calculus inequality that the concave function

f(x):=-\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}x+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log(1+x)

achieves the maximum at
x:=\frac{r^{\prime}q}{4(r-1)(1-\delta)(q-1)}-1
.

This claim together with the inequality (
5.7
) implies that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\|u\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\frac{q}{q-1}\log\Big(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r-1}\Big)+\frac{4(r-1)(1-\delta)}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\Psi

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha^{2}(r-2)^{2}}{4(r-1)\delta}+\alpha^{2},

where the constant
\Psi
is given by

\Psi:=\frac{q}{q-1}\log\frac{q}{4(q-1)(1-\delta)}-\frac{q}{q-1}+\frac{q}{q-1}\log{C}-\log V_{\omega}.

We need the following elementary identities:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}dt=-\frac{1}{r(t)}\Big|_{t=0}^{T}=1.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r-1}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log\frac{\beta}{t}dt+\int_{T/2}^{T}\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{\beta T^{\beta}}{(T-t)(T^{\beta}-(T-t)^{\beta})}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{r_{s}^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log\frac{\beta}{Ts}ds+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{r_{s}^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{\beta}{T(1-s)(1-(1-s)^{\beta})}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r_{s}^{\prime}}{r^{2}}ds+\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{\beta(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta-1}}{((4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta}+1)^{2}}\log\frac{\beta}{s}ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{\beta}{(1-s)^{1-\beta}}\log\Big(\frac{\beta}{(1-s)(1-(1-s)^{\beta})}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log T+A_{\beta},

where we denote
A_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
to be the sum of the two integrals which are convergent by the choice of
\beta\in(0,1)
, and we note that
A_{\beta}\approx 2.455
if
\beta=1/2
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(r-2)^{2}}{r-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\frac{[(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})(t/T)^{\beta}-1]^{2}}{(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})(t/T)^{\beta}}dt+\int_{T/2}^{T}\frac{[T^{\beta}-2(T-t)^{\beta}]^{2}}{(T-t)^{\beta}[T^{\beta}-(T-t)^{\beta}]}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{[(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta}-1]^{2}}{(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta}}ds+\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{[1-2(1-s)^{\beta}]^{2}}{(1-s)^{\beta}[1-(1-s)^{\beta}]}ds\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle TB_{\beta},

where
B_{\beta}>0
is the sum of the two integrals. (If
\beta=1/2
, then
B_{\beta}\approx 2.008
).

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{r-1}{r^{2}}dt=T\Big(\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta}}{[(4^{\beta}-2^{\beta})s^{\beta}+1]^{2}}ds+\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-(1-s)^{\beta})(1-s)^{\beta}ds\Big)=TC_{\beta},

where
C_{\beta}\approx 0.192
if
\beta=1/2
.

Together with these identities, integrating (
5.8
) over
t\in(0,T)
yields that

\log\frac{\|u_{T}\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{0}\|_{1}}\leq\frac{q}{q-1}(A_{\beta}-\log T)+4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T+\Psi+\frac{B_{\beta}}{4\delta}\alpha^{2}T+\alpha^{2}T

where
u_{T}(x)=u(x,T)
. This implies that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|u_{T}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}\exp\Big(4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T+\frac{A_{\beta}q}{q-1}+\Psi+\frac{B_{\beta}}{4\delta}\alpha^{2}T+\alpha^{2}T\Big)\|u_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T+\frac{B_{\beta}}{4\delta}\alpha^{2}T+\alpha^{2}T}\|u_{0}\|_{1},

where the constant
C_{0}>0
depends on
\beta
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Since (
5.9
) holds for
any
positive function
u_{0}
, by duality and the representation formula (
5.1
), the inequality (
5.9
) implies the pointwise estimate for
H(x,y,t)
, for any two points
x,y\in X
, that

(5.10)

H(x,y,T)\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T+\frac{B_{\beta}}{4\delta}\alpha^{2}T+\alpha^{2}T}e^{\alpha(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}.

For two fixed points
x,y\in X
, if we take
\alpha=-\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{(2+B_{\beta}/2\delta)T}
, then from (
5.10
) we obtain

(5.11)

H(x,y,T)\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T}e^{-\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{(4+B_{\beta}/\delta)T}}.

Since (
5.11
) holds for
any
smooth function
\phi
with
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we can take a sequence of such functions which converge uniformly to
z\mapsto d_{\omega}(x,z)
, where
d_{\omega}(x,z)
is the function of distance of
z\in X
to the fixed point
x
. Therefore, from (
5.11
), we conclude that for any
T>0
, it holds that

(5.12)

H(x,y,T)\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T}e^{-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+B_{\beta}/\delta)T}}.

We next claim that the factor
e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T}
on the right-hand side of (
5.12
) can be replaced by a uniform constant independent of
T>0
. We have shown that (
5.12
) holds for
any
metric
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
. If we take
A=1
, the constant
C_{0}
in (
5.12
) can be chosen to be independent of the parameter
A
, which we will assume next.

Recall that we denote
I_{\omega}=[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}>0
to be the intersection number of the Kähler classes
[\omega]
and
[\omega_{X}]
. Given a metric
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,\infty,K,\gamma)
, we consider the rescaled metric
\tilde{\omega}=I_{\omega}^{-1}\omega
, which meets the equations
F_{\tilde{\omega}}=F_{\omega}
and
I_{\tilde{\omega}}=I_{\omega}^{-1}[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}=1
. Hence we have
\tilde{\omega}\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,1,K,\gamma)
. Applying (
5.12
) to the metric
\tilde{\omega}
we get

(5.13)

H_{\tilde{\omega}}(x,y,T)\leq C_{0}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\tilde{\omega}}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T}e^{-\frac{d_{\tilde{\omega}}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+B_{\beta}/\delta)T}},

and we stress that the constant
C_{0}
depends only on
n,p,K
,
\gamma
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. It is a standard fact that the heat kernels of
\omega
and
\tilde{\omega}
are related by

(5.14)

H_{\omega}(x,y,T)=I_{\omega}^{-n}H_{\tilde{\omega}}(x,y,T/I_{\omega}).

Combining (
5.13
) and (
5.14
) gives

(5.15)

\displaystyle H_{\omega}(x,y,T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}I_{\omega}^{-n}(T/I_{\omega})^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\tilde{\omega}}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T/I_{\omega}}e^{-\frac{I_{\omega}d_{\tilde{\omega}}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+B_{\beta}/\delta)T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{0}I_{\omega}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{4(1-\delta)C_{\beta}T/I_{\omega}}e^{-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+B_{\beta}/\delta)T}}.

The inequality (
5.15
) yields an upper bound of
H_{\omega}(x,y,T)
when
T\leq I_{\omega}
by choosing
\delta=1/2
, i.e.

(5.16)

H_{\omega}(x,y,T)\leq C_{0}I_{\omega}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}T^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{2C_{\beta}}e^{-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+2B_{\beta})T}}.

If
T\geq I_{\omega}
, we choose
1-\delta=\frac{I_{\omega}}{4T}\leq 1/4
, i.e.
1>\delta\geq 3/4
. Note that the constant
C_{0}\sim e^{\Psi}\sim(1-\delta)^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}
, hence, the inequality (
5.15
) implies that if
T\geq I_{\omega}

(5.17)

H_{\omega}(x,y,T)\leq C_{1}V_{\omega}^{-1}e^{C_{\beta}}e^{-\frac{d_{\omega}(x,y)^{2}}{(4+2B_{\beta})T}},

for some constant
C_{1}>0
which depends only on
n,p,K
and
\gamma
. The upper bound of
H_{\omega}(x,y,t)
in (
2.6
) follows from (
5.16
) and (
5.17
). ∎

5.2.
On diagonal estimates

In this subsection, we will derive a more precise upper bound of the heat kernel on the diagonal. We follow the classical arguments of Cheng-Li in
[
5
]
. We define

\tilde{H}(x,y,t)=H(x,y,t)-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}},

which satisfies
\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,t)\omega^{n}(y)=0
for any
t>0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\tilde{H}
satisfies the following semi-group property

(5.18)

\tilde{H}(x,z,t)=\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)\omega^{n}(y)

for all
s\in(0,t)
.

This lemma is well-known, but for completeness, we provide a sketched proof.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
. Denote
f(s)=\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)\omega^{n}(y)
. Taking derivative on
f(s)
, we get

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}[\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)-\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)]\omega^{n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}[\Delta_{\omega,y}\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)-\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\Delta_{\omega,y}\tilde{H}(y,z,t-s)]\omega^{n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

by integration by parts. Hence
f(s)
is independent of
s\in(0,t)
. It is not hard to see
f(s)\to\tilde{H}(x,z,t)
as
s\to 0^{+}
. Hence the lemma follows. ∎

In particular, setting
s=t/2
and
x=z
in Lemma
5.1
we get the equation

\tilde{H}(x,x,t)=\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)^{2}\omega^{n}(y).

Taking derivative with respect to
t
, this gives

(5.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=\frac{t}{2}}\tilde{H}(x,y,s)\omega^{n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)\Delta_{\omega}\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)\omega^{n}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}|\nabla_{y}\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}(y).

For a fixed
x\in X
and
t>0
, we consider the function
u(y):=\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)
. By the definition of
\tilde{H}(x,y,t/2)
, we have
\overline{u}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}=0
, hence the Sobolev inequality (
2.4
) implies that

(5.20)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u|^{2q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|u|\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{2q-2}{2q-1}}

by (
5.20
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2q-1}}\Big(\frac{2}{V_{\omega}}\Big)^{\frac{2q-2}{2q-1}}.

Thus it holds that

\displaystyle 0<\tilde{H}(x,x,t)=\int_{X}u^{2}\omega^{n}\leq{C}{V_{\omega}^{-\frac{q-1}{2q-1}}}\Big(\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2q-1}}.

Combining this with (
5.19
), we obtain the differential inequality:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)+CV_{\omega}^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)^{\frac{2q-1}{q}}\leq 0,

which implies that for a possible different constant
C>0
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)^{-\frac{q-1}{q}}\geq C(1-\frac{1}{q})V_{\omega}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}
. Note that
\tilde{H}(x,x,t)\to+\infty
as
t\to 0^{+}
. Integrating the inequality above over
t\in(0,T]
for any arbitrary
T>0
yields that

\tilde{H}(x,x,T)\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\frac{1}{T^{\frac{q}{q-1}}},

which combined with
\tilde{H}=H-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}
implies the upper bound of
H(x,x,t)
(we change
T
to
t
),

H(x,x,t)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}.

A simple scaling argument using (
5.14
) shows that the constant
C
can be chosen to be independent of
A>0
in the following form:

H(x,x,t)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+C\frac{I_{\omega}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}{V_{\omega}}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}.

To see (
2.8
), we observe that from the expansion formula (
5.21
) we have when
t\geq I_{\omega}
,

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}(x,y,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}|\phi_{k}(x)||\phi_{k}(y)|\leq e^{-\lambda_{1}t/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{k}t/2}|\phi_{k}(x)||\phi_{k}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda_{1}t/2}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)^{1/2}\tilde{H}(y,y,t)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}e^{-\lambda_{1}t/2}I_{\omega}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}t^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}e^{-cI^{-1}_{\omega}t},

where we have used the estimate
\lambda_{1}\geq 2cI_{\omega}^{-1}
for some constant
c>0
. In particular, the heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
converges to the constant function
1/V_{\omega}
exponentially as
t\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.1
.
We turn to the applications of the upper bound of heat kernel (
2.7
). Let
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots
(counting multiplicity) be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of the Laplacian
-\Delta_{\omega}
. We choose eigenfunctions
\phi_{k}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
such that
\{\phi_{k}\}
are orthonormal in
L^{2}(X,\omega^{n})
. It is a classical fact that
\{\phi_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(X,\omega^{n})
,
\phi_{0}=1/\sqrt{V_{\omega}}
, and the heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
can be expanded as

(5.21)

H(x,y,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y).

The upper bound (
2.7
) implies that

(5.22)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\phi_{j}(x)^{2}\leq\frac{CI_{\omega}^{q/(q-1)}}{V_{\omega}}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}.

Integrating (
5.22
) over
X
gives

(5.23)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\leq\frac{CI^{q/(q-1)}_{\omega}}{t^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}.

For any given
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, setting
t=1/\lambda_{k}
, the equation (
5.23
) implies

CI_{\omega}^{q/(q-1)}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{k}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{k}}\geq e^{-1}k.

Hence we conclude that there is a positive constant
c=1/(Ce)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}>0
such that

\lambda_{k}\geq cI_{\omega}^{-1}\,k^{\frac{q-1}{q}}.

This finishes the proof of Corollary
2.1
. ∎

We also have the following estimate on the time-derivative of the heat kernel, which will be useful in section
10
. In the following we assume
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}(n,p,A,K,\gamma)
as usual, and
I_{\omega}\leq A
by definition.

Lemma 5.2
.

The following inequalities hold: for any
t>0

(5.24)

\int_{X}\big(\dot{H}(x,y,t)\big)^{2}\omega^{n}(y)\leq t^{-2}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}t^{-2-\frac{q}{q-1}},

and

(5.25)

\sup_{x,y\in X}|\dot{H}(x,y,t)|\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}t^{-1-\frac{q}{q-1}},

where we denote
\dot{H}(x,y,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H(x,y,t)
.

Proof.

By the expansion formula (
5.21
) of the heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
, we have

\dot{H}(x,y,t)=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y).

Then we integrate
\dot{H}(x,y,t)^{2}
and obtain

\int_{X}\big(\dot{H}(x,y,t)\big)^{2}\omega^{n}(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-2\lambda_{j}t}\lambda_{j}^{2}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)\leq\frac{1}{t^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\tilde{H}(x,x,t)\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}t^{-2-\frac{q}{q-1}},

where we have used the elementary inequality
0<e^{-2\lambda_{j}t}\lambda_{j}^{2}=t^{-2}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\cdot(\lambda_{j}t)^{2}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}<t^{-2}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}
since the maximum of the function
x^{2}e^{-x}|_{x>0}
is
(2/e)^{2}
which is
<1
.

To see (
5.25
), we observe that the function
\dot{H}(x,y,t)
as a function of
(y,t)\in X\times(0,\infty)
satisfies the heat equation, hence by the representation formula for heat equations, we have for a fixed
t_{0}>0
and any
t>t_{0}

(5.26)

\displaystyle|\dot{H}(x,y,t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{X}H(z,y,t-t_{0})\dot{H}(x,z,t_{0})\omega^{n}(z)|=|\int_{X}\tilde{H}(z,y,t-t_{0})\dot{H}(x,z,t_{0})\omega^{n}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X}\tilde{H}(z,y,t-t_{0})^{2}\omega^{n}(z)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}\dot{H}(x,z,t_{0})^{2}\omega^{n}(z)\Big)^{1/2}

(5.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(y,y,2t-2t_{0})^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}\dot{H}(x,z,t_{0})^{2}\omega^{n}(z)\Big)^{1/2},

where (
5.26
) follows from Hölder’s inequality and in (
5.27
) we have applied the semi-group property (
5.18
) of
\tilde{H}
.
For a fixed
t>0
we take
t_{0}=t/2
. We then get (
5.25
) by applying the estimates (
5.24
) and (
2.7
). Alternatively, the inequality (
5.25
) also follows from the expansion of
\dot{H}(x,y,t)
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

We remark that

estimates of the eigenfunctions

setting

\phi_{k}(x)^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{k}}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)\leq\frac{CI^{q/(q-1)}_{\omega}}{V_{\omega}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}.

Choose

\max_{X}|\phi_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}(I_{\omega}\lambda_{k})^{\frac{q}{q-1}}.

6.
Local Sobolev inequality and Poincaré inequality

In this section, we show that the Sobolev inequality and Poincaré inequality continue to hold on domains in
X
. Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset X
be an open domain which for simplicity is assumed connected, and
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}
be a relatively compact subdomain of
\Omega^{\prime}
. We fix a compactly supported function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
\Omega
. The main result is

Lemma 6.1
.

There is a uniform constant
C=C(n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
, we have:

(1). The local Sobolev inequality:

(6.1)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega}|u-\bar{u}_{\Omega}|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\nabla(\eta u)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n},

and (2). The local Poincaré inequality:

(6.2)

\int_{\Omega}|u-\bar{u}_{\Omega}|^{2}\omega^{n}{}\leq{C}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\nabla(\eta u)|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n},

where
\bar{u}_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}u\omega^{n}
is the average of
u
over
\Omega
under the metric
\omega
and
V_{\omega}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\omega^{n}
is the
\omega
-volume of
\Omega
.

Proof.

By approximation, we may assume
u\in C^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
and
\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega})\omega^{n}<\infty
. For simplicity we denote
\tilde{u}=\eta u
which can be extended trivially to
X
as a
C^{1}
function. By the Green’s formula, we have for the function
\tilde{u}
,

(6.3)

\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\tilde{u}\omega^{n}+\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

Applying (
6.3
) to different points
x,z\in\Omega
and taking the difference result in the equation

(6.4)

\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(z)=\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}-\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(z,y),\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

Integrating (
6.4
) over
z\in\Omega
against
\omega^{n}
and dividing the obtained equation by
V_{\omega}(\Omega)
, we get

(6.5)

\tilde{u}(x)-\bar{\tilde{u}}_{\Omega}=\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{z\in\Omega}\omega^{n}\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(z,y),\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n},

which implies by triangle inequality that

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}(x)-\bar{\tilde{u}}_{\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}|(x,y)|\nabla\tilde{u}(y)|_{\omega}\omega^{n}+\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{z\in\Omega}\omega^{n}\int_{y\in X}|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}|(z,y)|\nabla\tilde{u}(y)|_{\omega}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)|^{2}}{{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)^{1+\beta}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle~+\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{z\in\Omega}\omega^{n}\Big(\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(z,y)|^{2}}{{\mathcal{G}}(z,y)^{1+\beta}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(z,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}

(6.6)

by (
4.2
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(z,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/2}\omega^{n}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta/2}}{\beta^{1/2}}\Big[\Big(\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}\int_{z\in\Omega}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(z,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big],

where the last inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality. To get the local Poincaré inequality (
6.2
), we take square on both sides of (
6.6
) and integrate the inequality over
x\in\Omega
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{u}-\bar{\tilde{u}}_{\Omega}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}\Big[\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(x,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+\int_{z\in\Omega}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(z,\cdot)^{1+\beta}|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}\Big],

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\beta}\int_{X}|\nabla\tilde{u}|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n},

where we apply the inequality (
4.1
) in the last line by choosing
\beta=\varepsilon_{0}
. The inequality (
6.2
) follows since
\tilde{u}=\eta u=u
in
\Omega
and
\tilde{u}
is supported in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

To prove the local Sobolev inequality (
6.1
), we choose
\beta=\varepsilon_{0}/2
and
q>1
as in (
4.4
). Taking
(2q)
-th power of both sides of (
6.6
) and arguing as that in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the inequality (
6.1
) follows.
∎

We denote
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
the
W^{1,2}
-functions with compact support in
\Omega
. For these functions, we have the following local Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities:

Lemma 6.2
.

There is a uniform constant
C=C(n,p,A,K,\gamma)>0
such that for any
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we have:

(1). The local Soblev inequality:

(6.7)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C\Big(1+\frac{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\Big)\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n},

and (2). The local Poincaré inequality:

(6.8)

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\omega^{n}{}\leq{C}\Big(1+\frac{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\Big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n},

where
\Omega^{c}=X\backslash\Omega
is the complement of
\Omega
in
X
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of the above lemma. So we just outline the main steps. We may assume
u\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u
can be viewed as a
C^{1}
function in
X
. By the Green’s formula, we have for any
x\in X

(6.9)

u(x)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}+\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla u(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

Applying (
6.9
) to
x\in\Omega^{c}=X\backslash\Omega
at which
u=0
and integrating the equation over
\Omega^{c}
, we obtain

(6.10)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}u\omega^{n}=-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla u(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

The equations (
6.10
) and (
6.9
) together yield that for any
x\in\Omega

(6.11)

u(x)=-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\int_{z\in\Omega^{c}}\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(z,y),\nabla u(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}+\int_{y\in X}\langle\nabla{\mathcal{G}}(x,y),\nabla u(y)\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}.

Taking square on both sides of (
6.11
) gives that for any
x\in\Omega

(6.12)

|u(x)|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}+\frac{V_{\omega}^{\beta}}{\beta}\int_{X}{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)^{1+\beta}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}.

Integrating (
6.12
) over
\Omega
yields that

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C\frac{V_{\omega}(\Omega)}{V_{\omega}(\Omega^{c})}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}+C\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n},

which is the inequality (
6.8
). The local Sobolev inequality (
6.7
) can be proved by taking
q
-th power of both sides of (
6.12
) and arguing similarly to that in Lemma
6.1
.
∎

We next consider a mean value inequality for sub-solutions of the Laplacian
\Delta_{\omega}
on geodesic balls in
X
. We fix a point
x\in X
and denote the geodesic ball
B_{\omega}(x,R)
by
B_{R}
for any
R>0
. Suppose
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{R})
satisfies

(6.13)

\Delta_{\omega}u\geq f,\quad\mbox{in }B_{R}.

By the classical Moser’s iteration argument (e.g.
[
20
]
), we can derive the following geometric mean value inequality.

Lemma 6.3
.

Given a constant
N>\frac{q}{q-1}
,
there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,A,K,\gamma,N)>0
such that for any
s>0

(6.14)

\sup_{B_{r}}u^{+}\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{N}\big)^{\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{2}{s(q-1)}}}\big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R}}(u^{+})^{s}\big)^{1/s},

for any
0<r<R
.

Proof.

We denote
k=(R-r)^{\beta}\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R},\omega^{n})}=(R-r)^{\beta}(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{N}\omega^{n})^{1/N}
if
|f|\not\equiv 0
, and
k>0
is an positive number if
f\equiv 0
, and here
\beta=\frac{2(q-1)N-2q}{(q-1)N}>0
. Replacing
u
by
u^{+}+k\geq k
, we may assume
u\geq k
. Fix any constants
0<r<R_{2}<R_{1}<R
. Choose a cut-off function
\eta
that depends on
d_{\omega}(x,\cdot)
, the distance function to the center
x\in B_{R}
, such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{R_{2}}
, supp
(\eta)\subset B_{R_{1}}
, and

\sup|\nabla\eta|_{\omega}\leq\frac{4}{R_{1}-R_{2}}.

Multiplying both sides of (
6.13
) by
\eta^{2}u^{m}
for some
1\leq m
, and applying integration by parts, we obtain (we omit the integral domain
B_{R}
and the measure
\omega^{n}
in the following calculations.)

\displaystyle\int m\eta^{2}u^{m-1}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int 2\eta u^{m}|\nabla\eta||\nabla u|+\int f\eta^{2}u^{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int m\eta^{2}u^{m-1}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2}{m}\int u^{m+1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\int f\eta^{2}u^{m},

hence

\frac{2m}{(m+1)^{2}}\int\eta^{2}|\nabla u^{\frac{m+1}{2}}|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{m}\int u^{m+1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\int f\eta^{2}u^{m},

which is equivalent to

\int|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{m+1}{2}})|^{2}\leq 8\int u^{m+1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+2m\int f\eta^{2}u^{m}.

By the Sobolev inequality (
2.4
), this implies

(6.15)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int\eta^{2q}u^{(m+1)q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\Big(\int(\eta^{2}u^{m+1}+u^{m+1}|\nabla\eta|^{2})+m\int f\eta^{2}u^{m}\Big).

We observe that the last term in (
6.15
) satisfies

(6.16)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{V_{\omega}}\int f\eta^{2}u^{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m}{V_{\omega}}\int\frac{|f|}{k}\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\leq\frac{m}{k}\|f\|_{L^{N}}\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{N^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{m}(R-r)^{-\beta}\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{N^{*}}}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality to the right-hand side of (
6.16
), we obtain

(6.17)

\displaystyle{m}(R-r)^{-\beta}\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{N^{*}}}\leq{m}(R-r)^{-\beta}\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{(q-1)N}}\cdot\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{(q-1)N-q}{N(q-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m(R-r)^{-\beta}\epsilon\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{q}}+Cm(R-r)^{-\beta}\epsilon^{-\frac{q}{(q-1)N-q}}\|\eta^{2}u^{m+1}\|_{L^{1}},

where in (
6.17
) we make use of Young’s inequality. We choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2Cm(R-r)^{-\beta}}>0
for
C>0
the constant on the right-hand side of (
6.15
). Then combining (
6.17
) and (
6.15
), we get

(6.18)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int\eta^{2q}u^{(m+1)q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\Big(\int(\frac{m^{\alpha}\eta^{2}u^{m+1}}{(R-r)^{2}}+u^{m+1}|\nabla\eta|^{2})\Big)

where we denote
\alpha=\frac{(q-1)N}{(q-1)N-q}>0
. By the choice of the cut-off function
\eta
and the inequality
\frac{1}{R-r}\leq\frac{1}{R_{1}-R_{2}}
, this yields

(6.19)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R_{2}}}u^{(m+1)q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q(m+1)}\leq C^{\frac{1}{m+1}}\frac{(m+1)^{\alpha/(m+1)}}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{2/(m+1)}}\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R_{1}}}u^{m+1}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m+1}}.

We will iteratively apply (
6.19
) to a sequence of numbers
m_{j}=2q^{j}
and radii

R_{j}=r+\frac{R-r}{2^{j}},\quad\mbox{for }j=0,1,2,\ldots

It is clear that
R_{0}=R
,
R_{\infty}=r
,
R_{j}-R_{j+1}={2^{-j-1}}(R-r)
. Iterating the ineuquality (
6.19
) with
R_{1}=R_{j}
and
R_{2}=R_{j+1}
for
j=0,\ldots,\ell
gives

(6.20)

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R_{\ell+1}}}u^{m_{\ell+1}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{m_{\ell+1}}}\leq\frac{(2^{\alpha}C)^{\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2q^{j}}}q^{\alpha\sum_{j}jq^{-j}}2^{\sum_{j}q^{-j}}}{(R-r)^{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}q^{-j}}}\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R}}u^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Letting
\ell\to\infty
, we get from (
6.20
) that

(6.21)

\sup_{B_{r}}u\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{R}}u^{2}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies by our choice of
u:=u^{+}+\|f\|_{L^{N}}
that

(6.22)

\sup_{B_{r}}u^{+}\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}\Big(\|u^{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+\frac{V_{\omega}(B_{R})^{1/2}}{V_{\omega}^{1/2}}(R-r)^{\beta}\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R})}\Big).

This proves (
6.14
) with
s=2
, and hence for all
s\geq 2
by Hölder’s inequality. Next let
s\in(0,2)
be a given number. Note that (
6.22
) tells that
for any
0<r<R^{\prime}\leq R

(6.23)

\displaystyle\|u^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\Big(\|u^{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R^{\prime}})}+\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R^{\prime}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\Big(\|u^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R^{\prime}})}^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\|u^{+}\|_{L^{s}(B_{R^{\prime}})}^{s/2}+\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R^{\prime}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R^{\prime}})}+\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{2}{s(q-1)}}}\|u^{+}\|_{L^{s}(B_{R^{\prime}})}+\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R^{\prime}})}.

If we denote
\phi(r):=\|u^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}
which is monotonically increasing and by (
6.23
) it satisfies the inequality

\phi(r)\leq\frac{1}{2}\phi(R^{\prime})+\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{2}{s(q-1)}}}A+\frac{C}{(R^{\prime}-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}B

with

and

. We can now apply the standard iteration lemma (see e.g. Lemma 4.3, Chap. 4, in
[
20
]
) to conclude that

\phi(r)\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{2}{s(q-1)}}}\|u^{+}\|_{L^{s}(B_{R})}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\|f\|_{L^{N}(B_{R})},

which is (

).
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
6.3
, we have the following

, which we do not expect to be sharp.

Corollary 6.1
.

The Green’s function

satisfies the upper bound: for any

G_{\omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}d_{\omega}(x,y)^{-\frac{2}{q-1}}.

Proof.

For any two different points

, we let
0<R=d_{\omega}(x,y)/2
, and consider the function
u(x)=G_{\omega}(x,y)
as a

function on

, which satisfies the differential equation

\Delta_{\omega}u=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}},\quad\mbox{in }B_{R}.

We apply Lemma
6.3
with

and

to conclude that

\sup_{B_{R/2}}u^{+}\leq C\Big(V_{\omega}^{-1}R^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+V_{\omega}^{-1}R^{-\frac{2}{q-1}}\Big)\leq CV_{\omega}^{-1}R^{-\frac{2}{q-1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Smooth approximation for
\mathcal{AK}
-currents

7.1.
Kähler varieties

We first recall the definition of a smooth Kähler metric on a Kähler space.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a complex space of dimension
n
. We say
\theta
is a smooth Kähler metric on
X
, if for any point
x\in X
there is an open subset
U
of
X
together with a local holomorphic embedding
\iota_{x}:U\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{x}}
such that
\theta|_{U}
is the pullback of some smooth Kähler metric on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{x}}
under
\iota_{x}
.

If
\theta
is a smooth Kähler metric in the sense of Definition
7.1
, then
\theta|_{X^{reg}}
is a
genuine
Kähler metric on the smooth manifold
X^{\circ}
, the regular part of
X
.
X
is said to be a Kähler space or variety if there exists a smooth Kähler metric on
X
. If
X
is a compact normal Kähler space, then there exists a resolution of singularities
\pi:Y\to X
such that the smooth model
Y
of
X
is also Kähler by
[
21
,
7
]
. It is straightforward to check that
[\pi^{*}\theta]
is big and nef on
Y
since one can construct a Kähler current on
Y
by
[
10
, Theorem 2.12]
. Then there exists an effective divisor
E
on
Y
such that
[\pi^{*}\theta]-\delta[E]
is a Kähler class for any sufficiently small
\delta>0
by Demailly’s regularization techniques (cf.
[
10
,
7
]
).

Starting from this section, we will assume
X
is an
n

Theorem 8.1
.

There exists

We first assume that

9.
Noncollapsing and diameter bounds for
\mathcal{AK}
-currents

Let
(X,\theta_{Y})
be an
n
-dimensional projective normal variety equipped with a smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{Y}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
be the union of the singular set of
X
and
\log\frac{(\pi^{*}\omega)^{n}}{(\theta_{Y})^{n}}
. We let
(\hat{X},d)
be the metric completion of
(X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega},\omega)
since
\omega
is a smooth Kähler metric on
X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
. The natural question is
whether

(\hat{X},d)
is a compact metric space.

We first verify that the distance function on
\hat{X}
with a fixed base point is Lipschitz.

Lemma 9.1
.

For any
fixed point

p\in\hat{X}
, the function

f(x)=d(p,x)

is a
1
-Lipschitz function on
(\hat{X},d)

Proof.

We can assume
p\in X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
since any point of
\hat{X}
can always be approximated by
a sequence of points in
X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
. For any
x,y\in X\setminus\mathcal{S}
, we have by the triangle inequality

-d(x,y)\leq f(x)-f(y)\leq d(x,y).

Then lemma immediately follows.
∎

The volume measure
\omega^{n}=e^{F}(\theta_{Y})^{n}
can be uniquely extended to a volume measure to
\hat{X}
by natural extension. Hence we will consider the metric measure space

(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n}).

We also define the Sobolev space

W^{1,2}(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})=W^{1,2}(X,\omega).

by extending

from

\hat{X}
trivially. By Lemma

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W^{1,2}}(\hat{X},d,\omega^{n})

.

and

,

(9.1)

\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}_{\omega}(B_{d}(p,r))}{V_{\omega}}\geq\min\{\frac{1}{2},\kappa{r^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}}\},

and

is the geodesic ball of radius

\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{d}(p,r)}|u|^{2q}\omega^{n}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{V_{\omega}}\int_{B_{d}(p,r)}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{n}

We let

\rho=0
on

. We let

Applying (

) to this function

(9.3)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}c_{0}^{\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}q^{-m}}=c_{0}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}_{\omega}(B_{d}(p,2^{-\ell-1}r))}{(2^{-\ell-1}r)^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}V_{\omega}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\ell+1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}_{\omega}(B_{d}(p,2^{-\ell-1}r))}{(2^{-\ell-1}r)^{2n}V_{\omega}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{1+\ell}}}r^{(2n-\frac{2q}{q-1})/q^{\ell+1}}2^{-(\ell+1)(2n-\frac{2q}{q-1})/q^{\ell+1}}=1,

gives the equation (
10.6
) for
\rho\in C(Y^{\circ})
.

Corollary 10.1
.

Suppose there exists another heat kernel
\hat{H}(x,y,t)
satisfying the conditions in Lemma
10.4
. Then we have

\hat{H}(x,y,t)=H_{\infty}(x,y,t)

for all
x,y\in Y^{\circ}
.
Hence the heat kernel
H_{\infty}
is unique in this sense.

Proof.

Take cut-off functions
\eta_{\epsilon}
as in Lemma
8.2
. Fix a time
T>0
. We claim that the function

t\in(0,T)\mapsto\phi(t):=\int_{Y^{\circ}}H_{\infty}(x,z,t)\hat{H}(z,y,T-t)\omega^{n}(z)

is independent of
t
. Denote
\phi_{\epsilon}(t):=\int_{Y^{\circ}}\eta_{\epsilon}(z)H_{\infty}(x,z,t)\hat{H}(y,z,T-t)\omega^{n}(z)
. By integration by parts and using the heat equation, we have for any small
\delta>0
and
t\in[\delta,T-\delta]
, there is a constant
C>0
depending additionally on
\delta>0
such that

|\phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)|=\Big|\int_{Y^{\circ}}(-\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla H_{\infty}\rangle\hat{H}+\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla\hat{H}\rangle H_{\infty})\omega^{n}\Big|\leq C\epsilon^{1/2},

by using the Hölder’s inequality together with the upper bounds of
\|\nabla H_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}
(and
\|\nabla\hat{H}\|_{L^{2}}
) and
H_{\infty}
(and
\hat{H}
) in (
10.3
) and (
10.2
), respectively. Integrating this over
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[\delta,T-\delta]
gives

|\phi_{\epsilon}(t_{1})-\phi_{\epsilon}(t_{2})|\leq C\epsilon^{1/2}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
yields
\phi(t_{1})=\phi(t_{2})
. Since
\delta>0
is an arbitrary constant, this easily implies that
\phi(t)
remains a constant for
t\in(0,T)
. By the Dirac property (
10.6
) of
H_{\infty}
and
\hat{H}
, we see that
\phi(t)\to\hat{H}(x,y,T)
as
t\to 0^{+}
and
\phi(t)\to H_{\infty}(x,y,T)
as
t\to T^{-}
. This finishes the proof of the uniqueness of the heat kernel.
∎

11.
A spectral theorem for normal Kähler spaces

Let
(X,\theta_{Y})
be a normal
Kähler
variety of
\dim X=n
, equipped with a smooth Kähler metric
\theta_{Y}
. Let
\omega\in\mathcal{AK}(X,\theta_{Y},n,A,p,K,\gamma)
. We consider the Laplace operator
\Delta=\Delta_{\omega}
with respect to
\omega
on
X^{\circ}:=X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega}
. The following lemma is a simple consequence of the integration by parts.

Lemma 11.1
.

The linear operator
-\Delta:C^{\infty}_{0}(X^{\circ})\to C^{\infty}_{0}(X^{\circ})
is self-adjoint, i.e. for any
u,v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(X^{\circ})
,
\int_{X}(-\Delta u)v\omega^{n}=\int_{X}(-\Delta v)u\omega^{n}
.

Note
-\Delta:L^{2}(X,\omega)\to L^{2}(X,\omega)
is a linear and densely defined operator. The associated bilinear quadratic form of
-\Delta
is given by
Q(u,u)=\int_{X}|\nabla u|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{n}
, for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(X^{\circ})
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}(Q)
be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(X^{\circ})
in
L^{2}(X,\omega)
under the norm
\|u\|_{L^{2}}+Q(u,u)^{1/2}
, which is equivalent to the
W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
-norm of
u
. Hence by Proposition
8.1
,
{\mathcal{D}}(Q)=W_{0}^{1,2}(X,\omega)=W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
. We then let
(-\Delta)^{F}:L^{2}(X,\omega)\to L^{2}(X,\omega)
be the
Friedrich extension
of
-\Delta
, which is a self-adjoint, densely defined and nonnegative operator. We note that the domain of
(-\Delta)^{F}
is a subspace of
{\mathcal{D}}(Q)=W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
(c.f.
[
26
]
). To ease notations, we will omit the superscript
F
and simply write
(-\Delta)^{F}
as
-\Delta
.

Theorem 11.1
.

There exists a countable set
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
such that the equation

-\Delta u=\lambda u,\quad\mbox{with}\quad u\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)

admits a unique solution iff
\lambda\not\in\Sigma
. Furthermore, we can write
\Sigma=\{\lambda_{k}\}
with

(11.1)

0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots.

Proof.

We consider the linear operator
Lu=-\Delta u+u
. The associated quadratic bilinear form
Q_{1}(u,u)
of
L
satisfies

Q_{1}(u,u)=Q(u,u)+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X,\omega)}=\|u\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(X,\omega)}.

Hence
Q_{1}
is bounded and coercive on the Hilbert space
W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
. By the Lax-Milgram Theorem, the inverse
L^{-1}
of
L
exists, and it is a continuous and injective map from
(W^{1,2})^{\dagger}
to
W^{1,2}
. For any
u\in W^{1,2}
, we define
I:W^{1,2}\to(W^{1,2})^{\dagger}
by
Iu(v)=\int_{X}uv\omega^{n}
, which can be decomposed as
I=I_{1}\circ I_{2}
, where
I_{2}:W^{1,2}\to L^{2}(X,\omega)
is the natural embedding, and
I_{1}:L^{2}(X,\omega)\to(W^{1,2})^{\dagger}
is defined similarly as
I
. By the compact embedding theorem in Corollary
8.1
,
I_{2}
is compact. It is also clear that
I_{1}
is continuous, hence
I
is compact. If we denote
T=L^{-1}\circ I,
then we have

\displaystyle\lambda\in\Sigma

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(-\Delta-\lambda I)\mbox{\, is not injective}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(L-(\lambda+1)I)\mbox{ is not injective}

(by left multiplication of
L^{-1}
)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(Id-(\lambda+1)T)\mbox{ is not injective}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda+1\neq 0\,\mbox{and $\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\in\sigma_{p}(T)$},

where
\sigma_{p}(T)
denotes the set of eigenvalues of the
compact operator

T
, which is countable and each number in
\sigma_{p}(T)
has finite multiplicity by the Fredholm Alternative. This shows that
\Sigma
is a discrete countable set, and its elements correspond to the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
, which can be listed as in (
11.1
) since
-\Delta
is nonnegative operator. The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}=0
is simple since any harmonic function
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
must be constant by Lemma
11.2
below. ∎

Lemma 11.2
.

Suppose
\lambda\geq 0
is an eigenvalue of
-\Delta
and
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
is an eigenfunction associated to
-\Delta
, i.e.

-\Delta f=\lambda f
. Then
f\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
. In particular, when
\lambda=0
,
f
must be constant.

Proof.

Given
f\in W^{1,2}
as above, by the standard elliptic regularity theory we know
f\in C^{\infty}(X\backslash\mathcal{S}_{X,\omega})
. We consider two cases:
\lambda>0
and
\lambda=0
.

Case 1:

\lambda>0
. We normalize
f
such that
\int_{X}f^{2}\omega^{n}=1
. Using the cut-off functions
\eta_{\epsilon}
we have as
\epsilon\to 0

\lambda|\int_{X}\eta_{\epsilon}f\omega^{n}|=|\int_{X}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla f\rangle\omega^{n}|\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0.

Hence we see that
\int_{X}f\omega^{n}=0
.
We consider the function

u(x,t)=\int_{X}H_{\infty}(x,y,t)f(y)\omega^{n}(y)

for all
t>0
.
By the boundedness of
\dot{H}_{\infty}(x,y,t)
as in (
10.4
), we have

(11.2)

\dot{u}(x,t)=\int_{X}\dot{H}_{\infty}(x,y,t)f(y)\omega^{n}(y)=\int_{X}\Delta_{y}H_{\infty}(x,y,t)f(y)\omega^{n}(y).

By the Dirac property (
10.6
) of
H_{\infty}(x,y,0)
, we see that
u(x,0)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}u(x,t)=f(x)
. For the cut-off functions
\eta_{\epsilon}
in Lemma
8.2
, we consider

(11.3)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\eta_{\epsilon}\Delta_{y}H_{\infty}f\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla H_{\infty}\rangle f-\int_{X}\eta_{\epsilon}\langle\nabla H_{\infty},\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla H_{\infty}\rangle f+\int_{X}H_{\infty}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla f\rangle+\int_{X}H_{\infty}\eta_{\epsilon}\Delta f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla H_{\infty}\rangle f+\int_{X}H_{\infty}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla f\rangle-\lambda\int_{X}H_{\infty}\eta_{\epsilon}f.

The last integral in (
11.3
) tends to
-\lambda\int_{X}H_{\infty}f
since
H_{\infty}
is
L^{\infty}
bounded by (
10.2
) and
f\in L^{2}(X,\omega)
. The second integral in (
11.3
) is bounded by
\|H_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
. The first integral in (
11.3
) satisfies the following:

(11.4)

\displaystyle\Big|-\int_{X}\langle\nabla\eta_{\epsilon},\nabla H_{\infty}\rangle f\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}(1-\eta_{\epsilon})(\Delta H_{\infty})f+\int_{X}(1-\eta_{\epsilon})\langle\nabla H_{\infty},\nabla f\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta H_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\nabla H_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})},

where
\Omega_{\epsilon}=X\backslash\{\eta_{\epsilon}=1\}
and by the construction
\eta_{\epsilon}
,
\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\omega^{n}\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Since
\|\nabla H_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(X)}
is finite by (
10.3
),
\|\Delta H_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is also bounded by (
10.4
), and
f
lies in
W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
by assumption, we see that the RHS of (
11.4
) tends to zero as
\epsilon\to 0
, by the absolute continuity of integrals. Combining all the above, letting
\epsilon\to 0
on both sides of (
11.3
), we obtain

\int_{X}\Delta H_{\infty}f\omega^{n}=-\lambda\int_{X}H_{\infty}f\omega^{n}
. This together with (
11.2
) implies that (because
\int_{X}f=0
we can replace
H_{\infty}
by
\tilde{H}_{\infty}=H_{\infty}-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}
)

\dot{u}(x,t)=-\lambda\int_{X}\tilde{H}_{\infty}f\omega^{n}=-\lambda u(x,t)

for all
t>0
.
Solving this ODE, we obtain
u(x,t)=e^{-\lambda t}f(x)
. Therefore, for any
x\in X^{\circ}
, we have by the upper bound (
10.2
) of
H_{\infty}

(11.5)

|f(x)|\leq e^{\lambda t}\tilde{H}_{\infty}(x,x,2t)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq e^{\lambda t}Ct^{-\frac{q}{2(q-1)}}.

We take
t=1/\lambda
and then we get

(11.6)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{q}{2(q-1)}}.

Case 2:

\lambda=0
. Replacing
f
by
f-\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}f
if necessary, we assume
\int_{X}f\omega^{n}=0
. The estimate (
11.5
) still holds in this case, and thus we obtain

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{q}{2(q-1)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for all
t>0.
Letting
t\to\infty
yields that
f\equiv 0
. Hence any harmonic function in
W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
must be constant.
∎

If we let
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be the eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
associated to the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}
as in (
11.1
). We also assume
\{\phi_{k}\}
are
orthonormal
, i.e.
(\phi_{k},\phi_{k^{\prime}})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. Lemma
11.2
implies
\phi_{k}\in L^{\infty}(X)
for each
k
. The following is a well-known fact about the set of eigenfunctions.

Lemma 11.3
.

The eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k}
form an orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space
L^{2}(X,\omega)
.

Proof.

We let
W
be the closure of the subspace spanned by
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
in
L^{2}(X,\omega)
. If
W
is a proper subspace of
L^{2}(X,\omega)
, then its orthogonal complement
W^{\perp}
is a nonempty closed subspace, and it is a simple fact that
\Delta|_{W^{\perp}}:W^{\perp}\to W^{\perp}
is also densely defined and self-adjoint. Indeed, if
u\in W^{\perp}
is in the domain of
\Delta|_{W^{\perp}}
, then by definition
u\in W^{1,2}(X,\omega)
, and for any
k

(11.7)

\int_{X}(\Delta u)\eta_{\epsilon}\phi_{k}=\int_{X}u(\eta_{\epsilon}\Delta\phi+\phi_{k}\Delta\eta_{\epsilon}+2\langle\nabla\phi_{k},\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\rangle).

We observe that

(11.8)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}u\langle\nabla\phi_{k},\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\rangle)\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{X}(1-\eta_{\epsilon})\big(u\Delta\phi_{k}+\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{k}\rangle\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\|\nabla\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\to 0,\mbox{ as }\epsilon\to 0,

where, as in the proof of Lemma
11.2
,
\Omega_{\epsilon}
is the complement of the set
\{\eta_{\epsilon}=1\}
and by the choice of
\eta_{\epsilon}
,
\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\omega^{n}\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
. We also have

\int_{X}u\phi_{k}\Delta\eta_{\epsilon}=-\int_{X}u\langle\nabla\phi_{k},\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\rangle-\int_{X}\phi_{k}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\rangle.

The first integral on the RHS tends to zero by (
11.8
) and so does the second integral by the fact that
\phi_{k}\in L^{\infty}
,
u\in W^{1,2}
and
\|\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
. Hence letting
\epsilon\to 0
on both sides of (
11.7
) yields that
\int_{X}(\Delta u)\phi_{k}=\int_{X}u(\Delta\phi_{k})=-\lambda_{k}\int_{X}u\phi_{k}=0
since
u\in W^{\perp}
. This shows that
\Delta u\in W^{\perp}
.

This will imply that
-\Delta|_{W^{\perp}}
has at least an eigenvalue
\lambda^{\prime}
, which is also an eigenvalue of
-\Delta
. This is a contradiction, and therefore,
W=L^{2}(X,\omega)
, in other words, the set of eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(X,\omega)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the eigenvalue estimates for the operator
-\Delta
.

Theorem 11.2
.

The following expansion formula holds for the heat kernel
H_{\infty}(x,y,t)
:

(11.9)

H_{\infty}(x,y,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}(y),\quad\mbox{ on }X^{\circ}\times X^{\circ}\times(0,\infty).

As a consequence, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
satisfy

(11.10)

\lambda_{k}\geq Ck^{\frac{q-1}{q}}

for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

For each fixed point
(x,t)\in X^{\circ}\times(0,\infty)
, we view the heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
as a function of
y
. It follows from the semi-group property (
10.5
) of
H_{\infty}
that
H_{\infty}(x,y,t)\in L^{2}(X,\omega)
. Hence by Lemma
11.3
, there exists a sequence of real numbers
\{c_{k}(x,t)\}_{k}
such that

H_{\infty}(x,y,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(x,t)\phi_{k}(y),\quad\mbox{ in the $L^{2}$ sense.}

Note that here
c_{k}(x,t)=\int_{X}H_{\infty}(x,y,t)\phi_{k}(y)\omega^{n}(y)
is the coefficient of
H_{\infty}(x,\cdot,t)
associated to
\phi_{k}
. By Parseval’s Identity, we have

(11.11)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|c_{k}(x,t)|^{2}=\|H_{\infty}(x,\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=H_{\infty}(x,x,2t).

On the other hand, in the proof of Lemma
11.2
, we have already shown that
c_{k}(x,t)=e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\phi_{k}(x)
since
\Delta\phi_{k}=-\lambda_{k}\phi_{k}
. This implies that we have the expansion formula (
11.9
) for
H_{\infty}

in the
L^{2}
sense
. From the equation (
11.11
), we see that by (
10.2
)

(11.12)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}\phi_{k}(x)^{2}=H_{\infty}(x,x,2t)\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}+Ct^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}.

Integrating (
11.12
) over
x\in X^{\circ}
we get

(11.13)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-2\lambda_{j}t}+1\leq 1+Ct^{-\frac{q}{q-1}},

since
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\phi_{0}=1/\sqrt{V_{\omega}}
. For
k\geq 1
, taking
t=1/\lambda_{k}
in (
11.13
), we get
\lambda_{k}\geq Ck^{\frac{q-1}{q}}
.

We now argue that the equation (
11.9
) holds smoothly. From the
L^{\infty}
estimate (
11.6
) of
\phi_{k}
, by elliptic regularity theory the eigenfunctions
\phi_{k}(x)
are uniformly bounded (with any derivatives) by
O(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{q}{2(q-1)}})
on any compact subset
{\mathcal{K}}\subset X^{\circ}
. This, together with the fast decaying of the factors
e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\leq\exp(-Ck^{\frac{q-1}{q}}t)
for any
t>0
, immediately implies that the series on the RHS of (
11.9
) converges smoothly on
{\mathcal{K}}
. Hence the two sides of (
11.9
) coincide as smooth functions.
∎

Lemma 12.1

There exist a decreasing sequence of

converges to
\varphi
pointwise on
X
. Furthermore,
\psi_{j}
converges smoothly to
\varphi
on any compact subset of
X^{\circ}
. In particular,

\sup_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y)}<\infty.

We will now consider the following complex Monge-Ampère equation

(\pi^{*}\omega_{0}+\epsilon_{j}\theta_{Y}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{j})^{n}=e^{-\psi_{j}+F_{j}+c_{j}}(\theta_{Y})^{n},~\sup_{Y}\varphi_{j}=0,

where
c_{j}
is the normalization constant. In particular,
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}c_{j}=0
. We let

\omega_{j}=\pi^{*}\omega_{0}+\epsilon_{j}\theta_{Y}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{j}

be the smooth Kähler metric on
Y
for
j\geq 1
.

Lemma 12.2
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
j\geq 1
,

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y)}\leq C.

Proof.

The lemma follows by combining the
L^{\infty}
-estimate for complex Monge-Ampère equation and (
12.2
) as well as the fact that
\psi_{j}
is uniformly bounded below.
∎

Let
D
be an effective divisor of
Y
such that its support conicides with the exceptional locus of
\pi
. Let
\sigma_{D}
be a defining section of
D
and
h_{D}
be a smooth hermitian metric of the line bundle associated to
D
. We can assume that for any sufficiently small
\delta
,
\pi^{*}\omega_{0}-\delta{\mathrm{Ric}}(h_{D})>0
is a Kähler metric. The following lemma follows by the same argument in the proof of Lemma
7.1
.

Lemma 12.3
.

There exists
K>0
,
C>0
such that

\Delta_{\theta_{Y}}\varphi_{j}\leq C|\sigma_{D}|^{-2K}_{h_{D}}.

Lemma 12.4
.

\omega_{j}
converges to
\omega
locally smoothly on
C^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
.

Proof.

The standard Schauder estimates and linear estimates can be obtain locally due to the second order estimates in Lemma. More precisely, for any
k>0
and
{\mathcal{K}}\subset\subset X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{X}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{C^{k}({\mathcal{K}})}\leq C.

Therefore
\varphi_{j}
converges to
\varphi_{\infty}\in PSH(Y,\pi^{*}\omega_{0})\cap C^{\infty}(\pi^{*}(X^{\circ}))
satisfying

(\pi^{*}\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{\infty})^{n}=e^{-\varphi}\pi^{*}\Omega.

Then
\varphi_{\infty}=\varphi
by the uniqueness of the bounded solution to the complex Monge-Ampère equation. The lemma is then proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.
Since
\omega_{j}
converges to
\omega
smoothly on
X^{\circ}
and
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|\varphi_{j}-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{K}})}=0
for any
{\mathcal{K}}\subset\subset X^{\circ}
, the estimates in Theorem
3.4
hold uniformly for
\omega_{j}
and the theorem is proved by letting

by applying the same arguments in Section
8
and Section

. ∎

13.
Finite time solutions of the Kähler-Ricci flow

Let
(X,g_{0})
be a projective manifold equipped with a Kähler metric
g_{0}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. We will consider the Kähler-Ricci flow (
3.2
) starting with
g_{0}
. Let
T>0
be the maximal time for the flow (
3.2
). Let
\omega_{0}
and
\omega(t)
be the Kähler forms associated to
g_{0}
and
g(t)
, the solutions of (
3.2
) for

T<\infty
, the limiting class
\alpha_{T}=\lim_{t\rightarrow T}([\omega_{0}]+T[K_{X}])
induces a unique surjective holomorphic map
\Phi:X\rightarrow Y
as in (
3.4
). If
\alpha_{T}
is big,
\dim Y=n
and we let
\mathcal{S}
be the exceptional locus of
\Phi
.
\mathcal{S}
is then an analytic subvariety of

.

The following lemma follows directly from the maximum principle (c.f.
[

,
33

).

Lemma 13.1
.

Suppose
T<\infty
, then there exists
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
,

(13.1)

\left(\omega(t)\right)^{n}\leq C\left(\omega_{0}\right)^{n}.

If we further suppose the limiting class
\alpha_{T}

such that

\omega^{n}(T)\leq C(\omega_{0})^{n}

is uniformly bounded above. Theorem
3.5

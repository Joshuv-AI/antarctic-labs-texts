1.
Introduction

It is well known that the two-color diagonal Ramsey number
R(4,4)
equals
18
. This was first proved by Greenwood and Gleason
[
12
]
in 1955. They exhibited a self-complementary graph of order 17 which does not contain a complete subgraph of order four, thus showing
17<R(4,4)
, and then combined this with elementary upper bounds. The graph they describe is one in the family of graphs which are now known as Paley graphs. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements and let
S
be its subset of non-zero squares. For
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
a prime power, the Paley graph of order
q
,
G(q)
, is the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
ab
is an edge if and only if
a-b\in S
. The Paley graphs are connected, self complementary and strongly regular with parameters
(q,\frac{q-1}{2},\frac{q-5}{4},\frac{q-1}{4})
. Please see
[
14
]
for more information on their main properties and for a nice exposition of their history since Paley’s original paper
[
25
]
in 1933.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{m}(G)
denote the number of complete subgraphs of order
m
contained in a graph
G
. While the work of Greenwood and Gleason tells us that
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G(17))=0
and
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G(q))>0
for
q>17
; Evans, Pulham and Sheehan
[
5
]
provide a simple closed formula for
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G(p))
for all primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Write
p=x^{2}+y^{2}
for integers
x
and
y
, with
y
even. Then

\mathcal{K}_{4}(G(p))=\frac{p(p-1)((p-9)^{2}-4y^{2})}{2^{9}\cdot 3}.

(1.1)

In 2009, Lim and Praeger
[
16
]
introduced
generalized Paley graphs
. Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
q
be a prime power such that
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,k)
if
q
is even, or,
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,2k)
if
q
is odd. Let
S_{k}
be the subgroup of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
of order
\frac{q-1}{k}
containing the
k
-th power residues, i.e., if
\omega
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
S_{k}=\langle\omega^{k}\rangle
. Then the
generalized Paley graph
of order
q
,
G_{k}(q)
, is the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
ab
is an edge if and only if
a-b\in S_{k}
. We note, due to the conditions imposed on
q
, that
-1\in S_{k}
so
G_{k}(q)
is a well-defined undirected graph.
G_{k}(q)
is connected if and only if
S_{k}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{q}
under addition.
When
k=2
we recover the original Paley graph.

The main purpose of this paper is to provide a general formula for
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{k}(q))
, thus extending the results of Evans, Pulham and Sheehan to generalized Paley graphs and to prime powers.
In the same way that a zero value for
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G(q))
means
q
is a strict lower bound for the two-color diagonal Ramsey number
R(4,4)
, we show that zero values for
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{k}(q))
yield lower bounds for the multicolor diagonal Ramsey numbers
R_{k}(4)=R(4,4,\cdots,4)
.
We also provide a general formula
\mathcal{K}_{3}(G_{k}(q))
and give lower bounds for the multicolor diagonal Ramsey numbers
R_{k}(3)=R(3,3,\cdots,3)
. In both cases, we state explicitly these lower bounds for small
k
and compare to other known bounds.

2.
Statement of Results

We present our results in two parts, the first relating to complete subgraphs of order four and then those relating to complete subgraphs of order three.

2.1.
Complete Subgraphs of Order Four

Many of our results in this section will be stated in terms of Greene’s finite field hypergeometric function
[
10
,
11
]
. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
denote the group of multiplicative characters of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
. We extend the domain of
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, by defining
\chi(0):=0
(including the trivial character
\varepsilon
) and denote
\overline{\chi}
as the inverse of
\chi
. We let
\varphi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
be the character of order two. For characters
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
we define the usual Jacobi sum
J(A,B):=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}A(a)B(1-a)
and define the symbol

\binom{A}{B}:=\frac{B(-1)}{q}J(A,\overline{B})
.
For characters
A_{0},A_{1},\dotsc,A_{n}
and
B_{1},\dotsc,B_{n}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and

\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define the finite field hypergeometric function

{{}_{n+1}F_{n}}{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}A_{0},&A_{1},&\dotsc,&A_{n}\\
&B_{1},&\dotsc,&B_{n}\end{array}\Big|\;\lambda\right)}_{q}:=\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{\chi}\binom{A_{0}\chi}{\chi}\binom{A_{1}\chi}{B_{1}\chi}\dotsm\binom{A_{n}\chi}{B_{n}\chi}\chi(\lambda),

where the sum is over all multiplicative characters
\chi
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.
Let
k\geq 2
be an integer, let
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,k)
be a prime power and let
\chi_{k}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
be a character of order
k
.
For
\vec{t}=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},t_{5})\in\left({\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\right)^{5}
we define

{{}_{3}F_{2}}\left(\vec{t}\;\big|\;\lambda\right)_{q,k}:={{}_{3}F_{2}}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{2}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}\\
&\chi_{k}^{t_{4}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{5}}\end{array}\Big|\;\lambda\biggr)_{q}.

In our first result, we show that
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{k}(q))
can be written quite simply in terms of
{{}_{3}F_{2}}
finite field hypergeometric functions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
q
be a prime power such that
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,k)
if
q
is even, or,
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,2k)
if
q
is odd. Then

\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{k}(q))=\frac{q^{3}(q-1)}{24\cdot k^{6}}\sum_{\vec{t}\in\left({\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\right)^{5}}{{}_{3}F_{2}}\left(\vec{t}\;\big|\;1\right)_{q,k}.

Many of the summands in Theorem
2.1
can be simplified using known reduction formulae for finite field hypergeometric functions. Splitting off these terms yields our second result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
q
be a prime power such that
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,k)
if
q
is even, or,
q\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,2k)
if
q
is odd. Then

\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{k}(q))=\frac{q(q-1)}{24\cdot k^{6}}\Biggl[10\,\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)^{2}+5\left(q-2k^{2}+1\right)\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)-15\,\mathbb{S}_{k}(q)+q^{2}\\
-5\left(2k^{2}-3k+2\right)q+15k^{3}-10k^{2}+1+q^{2}\sum_{\vec{t}\in X_{k}}{{}_{3}F_{2}}\left(\vec{t}\;\big|\;1\right)_{q,k}\Biggr],

where
X_{k}:=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},t_{5})\in\left({\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\right)^{5}\mid t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\neq 0,t_{4},t_{5}\,;\,t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}\neq t_{4}+t_{5}\}
,

\mathbb{R}_{k}(q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t=1\\
s+t\not\equiv 0\,(k)\end{subarray}}^{k-1}J\left(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t}\right)\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}_{k}(q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t,v=1\\
s+t,v+t,v-s\not\equiv 0\,(k)\end{subarray}}^{k-1}J\left(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t}\right)J\left(\overline{\chi_{k}}^{s},\chi_{k}^{v}\right).

This looks quite messy, which is the price we pay for a formula which holds for all
k
, but for a given
k
it tidies up somewhat.
Many of the summands that still remain in Theorem
2.2
are equal and we can establish an equivalence relation on the set
X_{k}
to reduce the number of hypergeometric terms to equivalence class representatives. We discuss this process in Section
6
.
In particular, for small
k
, the formula reduces to relatively few terms, as we see in the following results.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Jacobi Sums

We first recall some well-known properties of Jacobi sums. For further details see
[
3
]
, noting that we have adjusted results therein to take into account
\varepsilon(0)=0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For non-trivial
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
we have

(a)

J(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)=q-2
;

(b)

J(\varepsilon,\chi)=-1
; and

(c)

J(\chi,\overline{\chi})=-\chi(-1)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
,

J(\chi,\psi)=\chi(-1)J(\chi,\overline{\chi}\overline{\psi})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For non-trivial
\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
with
\chi\psi
non-trivial,

J(\chi,\psi)J(\overline{\chi},\overline{\psi})=q
.

Recall, if we let
k\geq 2
be an integer,
q\equiv 1\pmod{k}
be a prime power and
\chi_{k}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
be a character of order
k
,
then for
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, we have the orthogonal relation
[
3
, p11]

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{t}(b)=\begin{cases}1&\textup{ if $b$ is a $k$-th power},\\
0&\textup{ if $b$ is not a $k$-th power}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

We now develop some preliminary results which will be used in later sections. As a straightforward consequence of Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
we see that

\sum_{s,t=1}^{k-1}J(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t})J(\overline{\chi_{k}}^{s},\overline{\chi_{k}}^{t})=(k-1)\left[(k-2)q+1\right].

(3.2)

We define the quantities

\mathbb{J}_{0}(q,k):=\sum_{s,t=0}^{k-1}J(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t})\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\mathbb{JJ}_{0}(q,k):=\sum_{s,t,v=0}^{k-1}J(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t})J(\overline{\chi_{k}}^{s},\chi_{k}^{v})

which appear often in our reckonings. Using Propositions
3.1
&
3.2
and (
3.2
) it is a straightforward exercise to show that

\mathbb{J}_{0}(q,k)=\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)+q-3k+1

(3.3)

and

\mathbb{JJ}_{0}(q,k)=\mathbb{S}_{k}(q)-4\,\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)+q^{2}+k(k-5)q+k^{2}+6k-3,

(3.4)

where
\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)
and
\mathbb{S}_{k}(q)
are as defined in Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q=p^{r}
for a prime
p
, such that
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
if
q
is even, or,
q\equiv 1\pmod{6}
if
q
is odd. Let
\chi_{3}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}}
be a character of order
3
. When
p\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
, write
4q=c^{2}+3d^{2}
for integers
c
and
d
, such that
c\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
,
d\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
and
p\nmid c
. When
p\equiv 2\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
, let
c=-2(-p)^{\frac{r}{2}}
. Then

J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})+J(\overline{\chi_{3}},\overline{\chi_{3}})=c.

Proof.

When
p\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
,
[
26
, Prop 1]
tells us that

J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})+J(\overline{\chi_{3}},\overline{\chi_{3}})=\left(\frac{c}{2}+\frac{d}{2}\sqrt{-3}\right)+\left(\frac{c}{2}-\frac{d}{2}\sqrt{-3}\right)=c.

Now consider the case when
p\equiv 2\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
. Note
r
is even in this case. Let
\omega=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
. From
[
3
, Ch 2]
we have

(1)

J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
;

(2)

J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})J(\overline{\chi_{3}},\overline{\chi_{3}})=q
; and

(3)

J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})\equiv-1\pmod{(1-\omega)^{2}}
.

Noting (1) and the fact that
p
is inert in
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
, as
p\equiv 2\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,3)
, by (2) we must have
(J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3}))=(J(\overline{\chi_{3}},\overline{\chi_{3}}))=(p^{\frac{r}{2}})
as ideals. Now
(p^{\frac{r}{2}})=(-(-p)^{\frac{r}{2}})
and

\displaystyle-(-p)^{\frac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv-1\pmod{3}

\displaystyle\equiv-1\pmod{(1-\omega)^{2}}

as
3=(1-\omega)^{2}(1+\omega)
. Then by
[
26
, Lem 5]
,
J(\chi_{3},\chi_{3})=J(\overline{\chi_{3}},\overline{\chi_{3}})=-(-p)^{\frac{r}{2}}

4.
A pair of subgraphs of
G_{k}(q)
and Proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.9

In this section we define two subgraphs of the generalized Paley graph
G_{k}(q)
and relate the number of complete subgraphs of a given order for each graph, which we use to prove Theorems
2.1
and
2.9
.
For a graph
G
we denote its vertex set by
V(G)
and its edge set by
E(G)
, so the order of
G
is
\#V(G)
and the size of
G
is
\#E(G)
. For a given vertex
a
of
G
we denote the degree of
a
in
G
by
\deg_{G}(a)
. It is easy to see from its definition that
\#V(G_{k}(q))=q
,
\deg_{G_{k}(q)}(a)=\frac{q-1}{k}
for all vertices
a
, and, consequently,
\#E(G_{k}(q))=\frac{q(q-1)}{2k}
.

Let
H_{k}(q)
be the induced subgraph of
G_{k}(q)
whose vertex set is
S_{k}
, the set of
k
-th power residues of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore,
\#V(H_{k}(q))=|S_{k}|=\frac{q-1}{k}
.
Now

ab\in E(H_{k}(q))\Longleftrightarrow\chi_{k}(a)=\chi_{k}(b)=\chi_{k}(a-b)=1.

So, for
a\in V(H_{k}(q))
, using (
3.1
), we get that

\displaystyle\deg_{H_{k}(q)}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{a\}}\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{s}(b)\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{t}(a-b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum_{s,t=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{s+t}(a)J(\chi_{k}^{s},\chi_{k}^{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,\mathbb{J}_{0}(q,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)+q-3k+1)\qquad\textup{(by (\ref{for_J0R}))}.

This is independent of
a
so

\displaystyle\#E(H_{k}(q))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\#V(H_{k}(q))\cdot\deg_{H_{k}(q)}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{2k^{3}}\,\mathbb{J}_{0}(q,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{2k^{3}}\,(\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)+q-3k+1).

Let
H^{1}_{k}(q)
be the induced subgraph of
H_{k}(q)
whose vertex set is the set of neighbors of 1 in
H_{k}(q)
. Therefore

\#V(H^{1}_{k}(q))=\deg_{H_{k}(q)}(1)=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,\mathbb{J}_{0}(q,k)=\frac{1}{k^{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{k}(q)+q-3k+1),

and

ab\in E(H^{1}_{k}(q))\Longleftrightarrow\chi_{k}(a)=\chi_{k}(b)=\chi_{k}(1-a)=\chi_{k}(1-b)=\chi_{k}(a-b)=1.

Again using (
3.1
), and noting that
\chi_{k}(-1)=1
, we get that for
a\in V(H^{1}_{k}(q))
,

\displaystyle\deg_{H^{1}_{k}(q)}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{3}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1,a\}}\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}}(b)\sum_{t_{2}=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{t_{2}}(1-b)\sum_{t_{3}=0}^{k-1}\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}(a-b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{3}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}=0}^{k-1}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1,a\}}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}-t_{3}}(b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{3}-t_{2}}(1-b)\,\chi_{k}^{-t_{1}}(a-b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{3}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}=0}^{k-1}q\,{{}_{2}F_{1}}{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{2}}\\[3.61371pt]
&\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}\end{array}\Big|\;a\right)}_{q},\qquad\textup{(using (\ref{for_CharSum2F1})),}

where we have used a change of variables to get the second line. Finally, we get that

\displaystyle\#E(H^{1}_{k}(q))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a\in V(H^{1}_{k}(q))}\deg_{H^{1}_{k}(q)}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\chi_{k}(a)=\chi_{k}(1-a)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}=0}^{k-1}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1,a\}}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}}(b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{2}}(1-b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}(a-b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{5}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},t_{5}=0}^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}}(b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{2}}(1-b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}(a-b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{4}}(a)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{5}}(1-a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{5}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},t_{5}=0}^{k-1}\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}-t_{5}}(a)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{5}-t_{3}}(1-a)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{2}}(b)\,\chi_{k}^{t_{4}-t_{2}}(1-b)\,\chi_{k}^{-t_{1}}(a-b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{5}}\sum_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},t_{5}=0}^{k-1}q^{2}{{}_{3}F_{2}}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\chi_{k}^{t_{1}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{2}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{3}}\\
&\chi_{k}^{t_{4}},&\chi_{k}^{t_{5}}\end{array}\Big|\;1\biggr)_{q},\qquad\textup{(using (\ref{for_CharSum3F2})).}

Let

contains a complete subgraph

in color

Recall the generalized Paley graph of order

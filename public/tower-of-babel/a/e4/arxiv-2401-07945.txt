Canonical liftings of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces: Dwork hypersurfaces
Przemysław Grabowski

Abstract
. We explicitly compute canonical liftings modulo p 2 p^{2} in a sense of Achinger–Zdanowicz of Dwork hypersurfaces. The computation involves studying a compatibility between Hodge filtrations and a crystalline Frobenius. In particular, remarkably, we explicitly compute a partial data of the crystalline Frobenius modulo p 2 p^{2} .



Introduction

For every variety over a field of positive characteristic one can consider a space of all its liftings to characteristic zero. This space can be fully described in terms of deformation theory. Moreover, some special families of varieties such as abelian varieties admit distinguished elements in their spaces of liftings: canonical liftings. These can be used to form a functor from abelian varieties in positive characteristic to abelian varieties in characteristic zero. This functor, the canonical lifting, is a fascinating object to study with many applications. Nevertheless, abelian varieties are not the only family admitting canonical liftings, another one consists of Calabi–Yau varieties. In this paper, we compute some explicit equations related to canonical liftings of Calabi–Yau projective hypersurfaces.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let

F(I^{a})\subseteq I^{a}\quad\text{for every integer}\quad a\geq\frac{p}{p-1}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{m}\right\rceil-1\right).

Proof.

Let

F(I^{a})\subseteq(I^{p}+p^{m}A)^{a}=I^{pa}+p^{m}I^{p(a-1)}+\cdots+p^{m(j-1)}I^{p(a-j+1)},

The last part follows from the general statement by putting

3.
Dwork hypersurfaces

The goal of this section is to compute examples of canonical liftings modulo
p^{2}
. We perform it for a Dwork family of hypersurfaces, because they are highly symmetrical and this can be exploited to decrease the amount of computation required significantly.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>2
. Let
W_{2}(k)
be Witt vectors of length two over
k
. Let
N\geq 2
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a ring. A
Dwork hypersurface
(over
A
) for a parameter
\lambda\in A
is a hypersurface cut out by the equation

\lambda\left(x_{0}^{N+1}+\cdots+x_{N}^{N+1}\right)=(N+1)x_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{N}

in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{A}=\operatorname{Proj}(A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}])
, where
A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]
is the standard graded ring over
A
.

If
A=k
, then we denote this hypersurface by
X_{0}(\lambda)
. If
A=W_{2}(k)
, then we denote this hypersurface by
X(\lambda)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Clearly, given
N
fixed, Dwork hypersurfaces over
A
form an affine line family of projective hypersurfaces. This family admits an extension to a projective line family whose point at the infinity is a Fermat hypersurface cut out by the equation
x_{0}^{N+1}+\cdots+x_{N}^{N+1}
. Over
A=k
, whenever this hypersurface is ordinary and smooth, then it admits a canonical lifting modulo
p^{2}
. It is again a Fermat hypersurface cut out by the same equation, but over
W_{2}(k)
. This follows from exactly the same calculations as in this section, but simpler. As such, this computation can be treated as an exercise.

The computation is performed in the following way. We begin with an ordinary, smooth Dwork hypersurface
X_{0}=X_{0}(\lambda)
. We take its flat lifting
X=X(\eta)
over
W_{2}(k)
. (In particular,
\eta\equiv\lambda\ (\text{mod}\ p)
.) The Hodge spectral sequence of
X
degenerates by
[
sga73
, SGA7 Vol. II Expose XI Theoreme 1.5]
. Thus, by Section
2
, we know that
X
is the canonical lifting modulo
p^{2}
of
X_{0}
if and only if the crystalline Frobenius is compatible with the Hodge filtration of
X
. This is equivalent to the map
\gamma
being zero. And this, by Corollary
1.5
, Lemma
2.1
, and Proposition
2.2
, happens if and only if an image of an explicit arrow between explicit modules of homogeneous polynomials is zero. However, all of the maps involved are equivariant under linear symmetries of
X
. (These are introduced below.)
Consequently, after a small argument, this arrow is zero if and only if it is zero on invariant elements of its source. The module of invariant elements is cyclic, and we will provide its explicit generator. Therefore, it is enough to compute for each
\eta
the image of the generator is zero. This will turn out to be controlled by a single linear equation.

So, the computation is straightforward, however it still requires preparations.

3.1.
Symmetries

Fix
N\geq 2
. Consider the following two groups of isomorphisms of
S_{1}
, where
P=\mathbb{P}^{N}_{A}=\operatorname{Proj}(S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}])
:

•

G_{1}\quad:\quad
permutations of variables
x_{i}
,

•

G_{2}\quad:\quad
maps given by
x_{i}\mapsto\lambda_{i}x_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in A
,
\lambda_{i}^{N+1}=1
, and
\lambda_{0}\cdot\lambda_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\lambda_{N}=1
.

Each of these groups induces a group of isomorphisms of
P
. Clearly, all of them preserve all Dwork hypersurfaces. Moreover, these groups intersect trivially, and
G_{1}
naturally acts on
G_{2}
. Therefore, the group generated by both of them, we call it
G
, is their semiproduct. In particular, it is a finite group. This leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
be
k
or
W_{2}(k)
.
If
p>N+1
, then the order of
G
is not divisible by
p
.

Proof.

We assume that
A
is
k
or
W_{2}(k)
only to control a group of roots of unity. It is not an essential assumption.

Let
p>N+1
, then the group of
(N+1)
-roots of unity is a subgroup of a cyclic group of order
N+1
. Then, the group
G_{2}
is a subgroup of a group generated by maps given by
x_{i}\mapsto\lambda_{i}x_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in A
and
\lambda_{i}^{N+1}=1
. This group is of order dividing
{(N+1)}^{N+1}
. Therefore, the same is true for
G_{2}
. At the same time, the order of
G_{1}
is
(N+1)!
. Consequently, as
G
is a semiproduct of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we have that the order of
G
divides
(N+1)!{(N+1)}^{N+1}
and this number is not divisible by
p
.
∎

We will need a partial information about a ring of invariant elements under
G
action.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
A
admits a primitive
N+1
-root of unity
\zeta
.
Then every
G
-invariant homogeneous polynomial in
S_{N+1}
is of the form

a\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}+b\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i},

for some
a,b\in A
.

Proof.

Of course, any such polynomial is
G
-invariant.

Let
P
be an element of
S_{N+1}^{G}
.

G_{1}
is a subgroup of
G
, thus
P
is
G_{1}
-invariant. This means that
P
is a linear combination of symmetric polynomials. They are in bijection with monomials
m(e)\coloneqq x_{0}^{e_{1}}x_{1}^{e_{2}}\ldots x_{N}^{e_{N}}
, where
e_{1}\geq e_{2}\geq\ldots\geq e_{N}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=0}^{N}e_{i}=N+1
by the operation
m(e)\mapsto\sum^{\prime}_{g\in G_{1}}g(m(e))
, where the prime means that we sum each element of the orbit once. In particular, we have
m(N+1,0,\ldots,0)\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}
and
m(1,1,\ldots,1)\mapsto\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}
. Hereafter, we also use
m(e)
to denote the polynomial it correspond to. So, we have
P=\sum_{e}a(e)m(e)
, where
a(e)\in A
. We are going to show that
a(e)=0
unless
e
is
(N+1,0,\ldots,0)
, or
(1,1,\ldots,1)
.

First, observe that if a sum
\sum_{e}a(e)m(e)
is
G_{2}
-invariant, then we must have for each
e
that
g(m(e))=m(e)
for each
g\in G_{2}
, or
a(e)=0
. Indeed, it follows from a simple observation that both polynomials
g(m(e))
and
m(e)
are spanned on the same monomials.

Let
e\neq(N+1,0,\ldots,0),(1,1,\ldots,1)
. Let
0<k<N
be any index such that
e_{k}\neq 0
. Then
0<e_{k}<N+1
.
Consider
g\in G_{2}
that is determined by
x_{k}\mapsto\zeta x_{k}
and
x_{N}\mapsto\zeta^{-1}x_{N}
. Then, if we have an equality of polynomials
g(m(e))=m(e)
, then the coefficient next to the monomial
m(e)
is
\zeta^{e_{k}}
, but it should be one. So,
\zeta^{e_{k}}=1
, but
0<e_{k}<N+1
. This is a contradiction with the assumption that
\zeta
is primitive.
∎

3.3.
Hasse–Dwork Polynomials

This section introduces a family of polynomials that play an essential role in computing ordinarity of Dwork hypersurfaces, what is analogical to computing a Hasse invariant, see
[
Har77
, IV, Proposition 4.21]
, and, in this paper, computing canonical liftings modulo
p^{2}
. Therefore, we call them Hasse–Dwork polynomials.

Definition 3.7
.

We define a Hasse–Dwork polynomial for natural numbers
P,M
to be the following polynomial:

\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{M}^{P}(X)={(-M)}^{P}\left(1+M!\sum_{i=1}^{\left[\frac{P}{M}\right]}{{P}\choose{M\times i}}{\left(\frac{X}{-M}\right)}^{iM}\right),

where
M!{{P}\choose{M\times i}}={{P}\choose{i}}{{P-i}\choose{i}}{{P-2i}\choose{i}}\ldots{{P-(M-1)i}\choose{i}}
, i.e. the number
{{P}\choose{M\times i}}
is the number of all possible unordered
collections of
M
disjoint subsets of the set
\{1,\ldots,P\}
, each of the cardinality
i
.

The following lemma explains their role.

Lemma 3.8
.

An evaluation of a Hasse-Dwork polynomial
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{P}(\lambda)
is the coefficient next to monomial

\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{P}
in the polynomial

{\left(\lambda\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}\right)-\left(N+1\right)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)}^{P}
.

Proof.

One can compute the coefficient in the following way.
Let
i=0,\ldots,\left[\frac{P}{N+1}\right]
.
Choose
(N+1)\cdot i
factors out of
P
that we have.
Then, decompose it into
i
sets of size
N+1
each. Denote them by
A_{0},\ldots,A_{N}
. Take the summand
\lambda x_{i}^{N+1}
from the factors from
A_{i}
. From the factors outside the one chosen initially we take the summand
-\left(n+1\right)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}
. The product of all taken summands contributes to the coefficient, and the coefficient is the sum of all these contributions under all the choices. Namely:

\displaystyle\left({(-(N+1))}^{P}+\sum_{i=1}^{\left[\frac{P}{N+1}\right]}{{P}\choose{(N+1)\times i}}(N+1)!{\left(\lambda\right)}^{i(N+1)}{(-(N+1))}^{P-i(N+1)}\right)=

\displaystyle={(-(N+1))}^{P}\left(1+(N+1)!\sum_{i=1}^{\left[\frac{P}{N+1}\right]}{{P}\choose{(N+1)\times i}}{\left(\frac{\lambda}{-(N+1)}\right)}^{i(N+1)}\right)=\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{P}(\lambda).\quad\qed

Consequently, Hasse–Dwork polynomials have integral coefficients, so we can consider their reductions modulo
p
and
p^{2}
. We recall a Katz’s result
[
Kat72
, 2.3.7.18]
about a modulo
p
case first. (Observe that that paper’s notations and ours differ with respect where we put
\lambda
in the defining equation of a Dwork hypersurface. This is why his formula is not the same as the one below, but they are equivalent.)

Proposition 3.9

(Katz’s formula)
.

Let
p
not divide
N+1
. Then, we have a congruence:

\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(X)\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{\left[\frac{p-1}{N+1}\right]}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\{\frac{j}{N+1}\}_{i}}{{(i!)}^{N}}X^{i(N+1)}\ \left(mod\ p\right),

where
\{a\}_{i}\coloneqq a(a+1)(a+2)\ldots(a+i-1)
for
i\geq 1
, and
\{a\}_{0}\coloneqq 1
.

We have the following extension of this formula modulo
p^{2}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
p
not divide
N+1
.
Let
m=1,2
. We have congruences:

\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{mp-1}(X)\equiv{\left(-(N+1)\right)}^{mp-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\left[\frac{mp-1}{N+1}\right]}\frac{\left(1-mpH_{i(N+1)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}\{\frac{j}{N+1}\}_{i}}{{(i!)}^{N}}X^{i(N+1)}\ \left(mod\ p^{2}\right),

where
\{a\}_{i}\coloneqq a(a+1)(a+2)\ldots(a+i-1)
for
i\geq 1
,
\{a\}_{0}\coloneqq 1
and
H_{k}\coloneqq 1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\ldots+\frac{1}{k}
is the harmonic sequence. For
i(N+1)\geq p
, we have
pH_{i(n+1)}=1+p(H_{i(n+1)}-\frac{1}{p})
.

Proof.

We have to show that

(N+1)!{{mp-1}\choose{(N+1)\times i}}{\left(\frac{1}{-(N+1)}\right)}^{i(N+1)}\equiv\frac{\left(1-mpH_{i(N+1)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}\{\frac{j}{N+1}\}_{i}}{{(i!)}^{N}}\ \left(mod\ p^{2}\right).

By elementary transformations, this is equivalent to

{{mp-1}\choose{i(N+1)}}\equiv\left(1-mpH_{i(N+1)}\right){(-1)}^{i(N+1)}\ \left(mod\ p^{2}\right).

This is true due to simple elementary calculations, some in a form of an induction, and an observation that
\prod_{j=1}^{N}\{\frac{j}{N+1}\}_{i}
is a multiple of
p
for
i(N+1)\geq p
.
∎

This extension can be used to get a curious result.

Corollary 3.11

Proof.

By Proposition
3.10
, we can write
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{mp-1}(\eta)=A_{m}+pB_{m}

\eta=\lambda+p\lambda_{1}
,

A_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{\left[\frac{mp-1}{N+1}\right]}C_{i}^{m}\lambda^{i(N+1)}
,
and

B_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{\left[\frac{p-1}{N+1}\right]}C_{i}^{m}i(N+1)\lambda^{i(N+1)-1}\lambda_{1}
, where

C_{i}^{m}\coloneqq{\left(-(N+1)\right)}^{mp-1}\frac{\left(1-mpH_{i(N+1)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}\{\frac{j}{N+1}\}_{i}}{{(i!)}^{N}}
. Moreover, we observe that
A_{1}\equiv A_{2}
and
B_{1}\equiv B_{2}
modulo
p
, therefore
A_{2}
is not zero modulo
p
by the assumption
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\lambda)\neq 0
. This proves that the fraction is well defined.

The sole dependence on
\lambda
follows from the following calculation.

\frac{\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)}{\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta)}=\frac{A_{1}+pB_{1}}{A_{2}+pB_{2}}=\frac{(A_{1}+pB_{1})(A_{2}-pB_{2})}{A_{2}^{2}-{(pB_{2})}^{2}}=\frac{A_{1}A_{2}+pA_{2}B_{1}-pA_{1}B_{2}}{A_{2}^{2}}=\frac{A_{1}A_{2}}{A_{2}^{2}}.

∎

3.4.
Ordinarity

The following is a part of
[
Kat72
, 2.3.7.18]
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
X_{0}(\lambda)
be a smooth Dwork hypersurface. It is (
1
-)ordinary if and only if
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\lambda)\neq 0
.

Proof.

In this case, by a computation similar the one from
[
Har77
, IV Proposition 4.21]
, being ordinary is equivalent to the coefficient next to the monomial

\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{p-1}
in the polynomial

{\left(\lambda\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}\right)-\left(N+1\right)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)}^{p-1}
being non-zero. By Lemma
3.8
, this coefficient is
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\lambda)
.
∎

3.5.
Computation on Invariant Elements

Let
X=X(\eta)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}(k)}
be a Dwork hypersurface over
W_{2}(k)
whose reduction modulo
p
is smooth and ordinary. Let
f
be the homogeneous polynomial defining
X
. The group
G
acts on
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}(k)}
and it preserves
X
, therefore the map

H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
from Corollary
2.3
is
G
-equivariant.
In this subsection, we compute the
G
-invariant image of this map.

We have isomorphisms
H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})\simeq\operatorname{ker}(f:S^{\vee}_{-2(N+1)}\to S^{\vee}_{-(N+1)})
and
H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq S^{\vee}_{-(N+1)}
since the degree
d
of
f
is
N+1
.

Lemma 3.13
.

The induced action of
G
on
H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is trivial. In particular, we have
H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}
.

Proof.

The module
H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq S^{\vee}_{-(N+1)}

is spanned by
\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1}
. This element is
G
-invariant.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

We have the following equality of submodules of
H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})
:

\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}=H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})^{G},

where
d
is the map from Lemma
2.1
.
Moreover, this module is spanned by

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}+\eta\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{\vee}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-(N+1)}+\eta\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1}.

Proof.

Of course, we have
\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\subset H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})^{G}
, so it is enough to compute the bigger one, and check if it is killed by
d
.

Let
g^{\vee}\in S^{\vee}_{-2(N+1)}
be a dual polynomial to
g\in S^{\vee}_{N+1}
, i.e.
g^{\vee}(x_{i})\coloneqq g(x_{i}^{-1})\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1}
. It is clear that
g^{\vee}
is
G
-invariant if and only if
g
is, and, by Lemma
3.4
, this means that
g=a\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}+b\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i},

for some
a,b\in W_{2}(k)
.

We compute

\displaystyle g^{\vee}\times f=

\displaystyle\left(a\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}+b\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{\vee}\times\left(\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left((N+1)a\eta-b(N+1)\right)}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N+1){\left(a\eta-b\right)}^{\vee}.

By, Lemma
3.5
, we know that
N+1
is invertible. So,
g^{\vee}\times f=0
if and only if
a\eta-b=0
. Therefore,
H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})^{G}
is spanned by
g^{\vee}
for

g=\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}+\eta\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}
.

We do the check if

2\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\times g^{\vee}+f\times\frac{\partial g^{\vee}}{\partial x_{j}}=0
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle 2\left((N+1)(\eta x_{j}^{N}-\prod_{i\neq j}x_{i})\right)\times g^{\vee}+f\times\left(-(N+2)x_{j}^{N+2}-\sum_{k\neq j}x_{k}^{N+1}x_{j}-2\eta x_{j}\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{\vee}=

\displaystyle\left(2(N+1)\eta x_{j}^{\vee}-2(N+1)\eta x_{j}^{\vee}\right)+\left(-(N+2)\eta x_{j}^{\vee}-N\eta x_{j}^{\vee}+2(N+1)\eta x_{j}^{\vee}\right)=

\displaystyle 0.

Therefore,
d(g^{\vee})=0
and the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

The
G
-invariant map induced by the map from Corollary
2.3

\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}

is zero if and only if

(N+1)\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)+\varphi(\eta)\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta)=0,

where
\varphi
is the canonical Frobenius lifting on
W_{2}(k)
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.13
and
3.14
, we know that the map
\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}
is a map

S^{\vee}_{-2(N+1)}\supset H^{n}(X,I/I^{2})^{G}=\operatorname{span}(g^{\vee})\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=S^{\vee}_{-(N+1)}
.
Therefore, it is enough to compute when the image of
g^{\vee}
is zero.

By Proposition
2.3
, the image is the element

F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times\left(-f^{2p-1}+2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)\right),

where
F
is a Frobenius lifting on
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}(k)}
. Hereafter, we choose
F
from Example
2.4
.

We have:

\displaystyle g^{\vee}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-(N+1)}+\eta\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle f=

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i},

\displaystyle F^{*}(g^{\vee})=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-p(N+1)}+\varphi(\eta)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-p}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-p},

\displaystyle F^{*}(f)=

\displaystyle\varphi(\eta)\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{p(N+1)}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{p},

where
\varphi(N+1)=N+1
, because
\varphi
is the identity on integers.

First, we compute
F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times-f^{2p-1}
. We start by computing

x_{j}^{-p(n+1)}\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-p}\times\left(\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{2p-1}={{2p-1}\choose{p}}\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)

that follows from a simple combinatorics and Lemma
3.8
. Next, we compute

\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-2p}\times\left(\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{2p-1}=\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta)

using Lemma
3.8
. These, plus an elementary observation that
{{2p-1}\choose{p}}
is
1
modulo
p^{2}
, give

F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times-f^{2p-1}=-(N+1)\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)-\varphi(\eta)\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta).

Second, we compute
F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times 2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)
similarly. We have

2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)=2\left(\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{N+1}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}\right)^{p-1}\left(\varphi(\eta)\sum_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{p(N+1)}-(N+1)\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{p}\right).

Therefore, we compute

x_{j}^{-p(n+1)}\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-p}\times 2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)=2\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)\varphi(\eta)

and

\prod_{i=0}^{N}x_{i}^{-2p}\times 2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)=2\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)(-1)(N+1).

Consequently, we get

F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times 2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)=(N+1)2\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{p-1}(\eta)\varphi(\eta)-\varphi(\eta)2\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{p-1}(\eta)(N+1)=0

We combine the above to get

F^{*}(g^{\vee})\times\left(-f^{2p-1}+2f^{p-1}F^{*}(f)\right)=-(N+1)\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)-\varphi(\eta)\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta).

This finishes the proof.
∎

3.6.
Canonical Liftings Modulo
p^{2}

In this final subsection, we show that for Dwork hypersurfaces a computation of the map from Corollary
1.5
being zero can be reduced to a computation of this map on
G
-invariants being zero. In “most” of the cases this follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
A
be a ring. Let
G
be a finite group whose order is invertible in
A
. Let
f:M\to N
be a
G
-equivariant map of
A
-modules such that the action on
N
is trivial. Then the map
f
is zero if and only if
f^{G}:M^{G}\to N^{G}
is zero.

Proof.

The first implication is trivial.

The second follows from the following argument. Let
m\in M
, then
f(\sum_{g\in G}gm)=\sum_{g\in G}f(gm)=|G|f(m)
, therefore
f(\sum_{g\in G}gm)=0
if and only if
f(m)=0
. But the element
\sum_{g\in G}gm
is in
M^{G}
, thus this shows that if
f^{G}
is zero, then
f
is zero.
∎

Finally, our main result is the following theorem. (For the definition of Hasse–Dwork polynomials
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}^{P}_{M}
see Definition
3.7
.)

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>2
.
Let
W_{2}(k)
be Witt vectors of length
2
over
k
.
Let
N\geq 2
be an integer. Assume
p
does not divide
N+1
.
Let
\lambda\in k
. Assume
\lambda\neq 0
,
\lambda^{N+1}\neq 1
, and
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}^{p-1}_{N+1}(\lambda)\neq 0
. Then a Dwork hypersurface
X_{0}(\lambda)
defined by a homogeneous polynomial

\lambda\left(x_{0}^{N+1}+\cdots+x_{N}^{N+1}\right)-(N+1)x_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{N}

in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}=\operatorname{Proj}(k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}])
is a smooth, and
1
-ordinary projective variety such that
\omega_{X_{0}(\lambda)}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}(\lambda)}
.
Therefore, it admits a canonical lifting modulo
p^{2}
in the sense of

[
AZ21
]
.

This canonical lifting is isomorphic to a Dwork hypersurface
X(\eta)
over
W_{2}(k)
defined by a homogeneous polynomial

\eta\left(x_{0}^{N+1}+\cdots+x_{N}^{N+1}\right)-(N+1)x_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{N}

in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}(k)}
, where
\eta\in W_{2}(k)
, and
\lambda\equiv\eta\ (\operatorname{mod}\ p)
, such that
\eta
satisfies an equation

(N+1)\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)+\varphi(\eta)\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta)=0,

where
\varphi
is the canonical Frobenius lifting on
W_{2}(k)
.

Proof.

Hypotheses to admit a canonical lifting from the paper
[
AZ21
]
are satisfied by Lemma
3.5
, Proposition
3.12
, and a standard calculation of a canonical divisor, e.g.
[
Har77
, II Example 8.20.3]
.

We proof that the canonical lifting is given by the equation from the statement. Let
\eta\in W_{2}(k)
be such that
\lambda\equiv\eta\ (\operatorname{mod}\ p)
. Let
X=X(\eta)
, let
X_{0}=X_{0}(\lambda)
. By Corollary
1.5
,
X
is the canonical lifting of
X_{0}
if and only if the map

\operatorname{ker}(d)\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

is zero. However, what will be proved in a moment, this is equivalent to

\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}
being zero. This condition, by Lemma
3.15
, is precisely the equation in the theorem.

Now, we shall proof the equivalence.

First, we do “most” of the cases easily. Let
p>N+1
. Then, by Lemma
3.3
, we can apply Lemma
3.16
to the map
\operatorname{ker}(d)\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
. This finishes the proof.

However, for
p\leq N+1
, the prime
p
divides the order of
G
, so we cannot use such simple argument. Therefore, we provide the following more involved argument that works for all admissible pairs
p,N
.

First, observe that the equation
(N+1)\eta^{p}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{p-1}(\eta)+\varphi(\eta)\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}_{N+1}^{2p-1}(\eta)=0
has at most one solution, since it is “essentially” linear. Therefore, the map

\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}
can be zero for at most one Dwork hypersurface. So, if we know that a canonical lifting of a Dwork hypersurface is a Dwork hypersurface, then we know that the map
\operatorname{ker}(d)\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is zero for only one
\eta
. This would finish the proof in full generality. In the rest of this proof, we prove that a canonical lifting of a Dwork hypersurface is a Dwork hypersurface.

Indeed, recall a construction of a canonical lifting

[
AZ21
, Section 4.1.]
. Let
\sigma
be a
G
-invariant Frobenius splitting of
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
that descends to a Frobenius splitting on
X_{0}
. Such splitting exists by
[
BK05
, Exercise 1.3.E (5)]
. Let
I_{\sigma}
be an ideal sheaf from the construction for
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
, and let
J_{\sigma}
be such ideal for
X_{0}
.
Let
X
be a canonical lifting of
X_{0}
, by the construction
V(J_{\sigma})\simeq X
.
Then, we have the following diagram.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(I_{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{2}(X_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(J_{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(I_{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(J_{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{mod}p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{mod}p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

In the above diagram, the map
W_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k})\to\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}}
is given by a Teichmüller lifting of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}}(1)
. Moreover, the action of
G
lifts to a
G
-action on
W_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k})
by using Teichmüller liftings, and this action descends to the standard
G
-action on
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}}
: permutations act by permutations, and a multiplication by a scalar acts by multiplying by the Teichmüller lifting of the scalar. Now, since
X
is flat over
W_{2}(k)
, of codimension
1
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{W_{2}(k)}
, and
G
-invariant, we conclude that
X
is a
G
-invariant hypersurface. So, by Lemma
3.4
, it is a Dwork hypersurface.
This finishes the proof that a canonical lifting of a Dwork hypersurface is a Dwork hypersurface. The theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 3.18
.

In the above proof, we proved that a canonical lifting of a Dwork hypersurface is a Dwork hypersurface.
However, this fact is not completely obvious. A priori, it could happen that the canonical lifting is not a Dwork hypersurface. Then, there would exist exactly one Dwork hypersurface faking being a canonical lifting by having the map

\operatorname{ker}(d)\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
not equal zero, but having the map
\operatorname{ker}(d)^{G}\to H^{n}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})^{G}
equal zero. By our argument, this cannot happen.

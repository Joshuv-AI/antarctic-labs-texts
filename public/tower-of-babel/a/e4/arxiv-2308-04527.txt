Normalised solutions and limit profiles of the defocusing Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson equation
Riccardo Molle, Vitaly Moroz, Giuseppe Riey

Abstract
. We consider normalised solutions of the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson (GPP) equation with a defocusing local nonlinear term, − Δ ​ u + λ ​ u + | u | 2 ​ u = ( I α ∗ | u | 2 ) ​ u in ℝ 3 , ∫ ℝ 3 u 2 ​ 𝑑 x = ρ 2 , -\Delta u+\lambda u+|u|^{2}u=(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})u\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}dx=\rho^{2}, where ρ 2 > 0 \rho^{2}>0 is the prescribed mass of the solutions, λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} is an a-priori unknown Lagrange multiplier, and I α ​ ( x ) = A α ​ | x | 3 − α I_{\alpha}(x)=A_{\alpha}|x|^{3-\alpha} is the Riesz potential of order α ∈ ( 0 , 3 ) \alpha\in(0,3) . When α = 2 \alpha=2 this problem appears in the models of self–gravitating Bose–Einstein condensates, which were proposed in cosmology and astrophysics to describe Cold Dark Matter and Boson Stars. We establish the existence of branches of normalised solutions to the GPP equation, paying special attention to the shape of the associated mass–energy relation curves and to the limit profiles of solutions at the endpoints of these curves. The main novelty in this work is in the derivation of sharp asymptotic estimates on the mass–energy curves. These estimates allow us to show that after appropriate rescalings, the constructed normalized solutions converge either to a ground state of the Choquard equation or to a compactly supported radial ground state of the integral Thomas–Fermi equation. The behaviour of normalized solutions depends sensitively on whether α \alpha is greater than, equal to, or less than one.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Our starting point is the nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi=-\Delta\psi+a_{s}|\psi|^{2}\psi-\big(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2}\big)\psi,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.1)

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
*
is the convolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Equation (
1.1
), under the name of the Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson (GPP) equation, was proposed in cosmology as a model to describes the dynamics of the Cold Dark Matter (CDM) made of axions or bosons in the form of the Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC) at absolute zero temperatures
[
46
,
5
,
11
,
6
,
15
]
.
The nonlocal convolution term in (
1.1
) represents the Newtonian gravitational attraction between bosonic particles. The local cubic term accounts for the short–range quantum force self–interaction between bosons, measured by their scattering length
a_{s}
. The non–self–interacting case
a_{s}=0
corresponds to the Schrödinger–Newton (Choquard) model of self–gravitating bosons
[
42
]
, which is mathematically well–studied
[
39
]
. When
a_{s}<0
the quantum self–interaction between bosons is focusing/attractive, while for
a_{s}>0
the self–interaction is defocusing/repulsive. Introduction of the quantum self–interaction force
a_{s}\neq 0
was suggested as a way to solve the small scale problems of the CDM cosmological model, such as the “missing satellite” problem, and the “too big to fail” problem (see surveys
[
12
,
27
,
41
]
). Similar models appear in the literature under the names Ultralight Axion Dark Matter, and Fuzzy Dark Matter, see
[
27
,
6
]
for a history survey.

The standard standing–wave ansatz
\psi(t,x):=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)
in (
1.1
) leads to the stationary GPP equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u+a_{s}|u|^{2}u=\big(|x|^{-1}*|u|^{2}\big)u\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},

(1.2)

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the frequency of the standing–wave
\psi
.
The
mass

{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx,

and the
energy

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{a_{s}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\psi(t,x)|^{4}dx-\frac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(t,x)|^{2}|\psi(t,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy

are conserved quantities in (
1.1
).
Therefore, physically it is relevant to look for
normalised
stationary solutions
u_{\rho}
of (
1.2
) with a prescribed mass

\rho^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{\rho}^{2}dx,

and to analyse the
mass–energy
relation

\rho\;\mapsto\;{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi_{\rho}}

of the normalised solutions, where
\psi_{\rho}(t,x):=e^{i\lambda t}u_{\rho}(x)
.
In this work we are interested in the normalised stationary solutions of (
1.2
) in the defocusing case
a_{s}>0
.
The competition between the local and non–local terms of opposite signs but of the same cubic order of nonlinearity in (
1.2
) creates interesting and nontrivial mathematical effects.
In view of the scale invariance of (
1.2
), without loss of generality and up the unknown frequency
\lambda
, we may assume
a_{s}=1
.

1.2.
Framework

Mathematically, it is meaningful to include (
1.2
) into a family of equations

-\Delta u+\lambda u+|u|^{2}u=(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})u\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$}

(1.3)

where the nonlocal term is given by the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}(x):=A_{\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-3}
of a variable order
\alpha\in(0,3)
. The normalisation constant
A_{\alpha}:=\frac{\Gamma((3-\alpha)/2)}{\pi^{3/2}2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}

ensures that
I_{\alpha}(x)
could be interpreted as the Green function of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and that the semigroup property
I_{\alpha+\beta}=I_{\alpha}*I_{\beta}
holds for all
\alpha,\beta\in(0,3)
such that
\alpha+\beta<3
, see for example
[
26
]
. Recall that as
\alpha\to 0
, the Riesz potential

vaguely converges to the Dirac mass
\delta_{0}

[
26
,
43
]
. In particular, we note that as
\alpha=0
the nonlinear terms cancel out and equation (
1.3
) formally reduces to the linear Helmholtz equation
-\Delta u+\lambda u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
which has no

–solutions for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

While our motivation to introduce fractional order Riesz potentials is purely mathematical, the fractional Riesz potentials may become relevant in the Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) models, where modified gravitational interactions of order
\alpha\to 3
were proposed at large galactical scales
[
21
,
45
]
.
Normalised solutions of (
1.3
) with a prescribed mass
\rho^{2}>0
in the full admissible range
\alpha\in(0,3)
will be the main object of our study in this work.

Equation (
1.3

F(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|{\nabla}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{4}dx-\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{D}}(u),

where, and in what follows, we denote by

{\mathcal{D}}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})\,|u|^{2}\,dx=A_{\alpha}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}\,|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3-\alpha}}\,dx\,dy

the nonlocal interaction energy term.
By the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev (HLS) inequality (
3.2

is well-defined for all

.
As a consequence, it is standard to show that the energy

is of class

on

(cf.
[

Vice–versa, every nonzero weak solution

2.
Main results

2.1.
Scaling considerations and limit regimes.

Before we formulate our main results, we want to introduce several scaling considerations and to describe Choquard and Thomas–Fermi problems, which provide limit profiles for (

N
ρ

).

In what follows we are going to construct normalised solutions of (

N
ρ

) as local minimizers, or more generally, critical points of the energy
F
subject to constraint on
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
. Denote by

m_{\rho}:=\inf_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}}F=\inf\Big\{F(u)\ :\ u\in H^{1},\ |u|_{2}=\rho\Big\}

contains three different power–like terms that scale at different rates, the relation between

On the other hand when an energy includes two terms only, due to the scaling invariance, the mass–energy and mass–frequency relation is an explicit function of the mass. We consider this in the case of

and

Choquard limit for
\alpha\neq 1

Consider the Choquard functional

E_{\mathscr{C}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{D}}(u),

and the minimization problem

(2.2)

The case

For

If

Remark 2.6

3.
Preliminaries

Here for completeness we recall several well–known results that were frequently used in this paper.

Gagliardo–Nirenberg (GN) inequality

[
28
, Theorem 12.83]
. For every
s\in(2,6)
there exists
\bar{c}_{s}>0
such that ,

|u|_{s}\leq\bar{c}_{s}|{\nabla}u|_{2}^{3\frac{(s-2)}{2s}}|u|_{2}^{1-3\frac{(s-2)}{2s}},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}.

(3.1)

Remark 3.1
.

By the Sobolev embedding and by interpolation, it is readily seen that
\bar{c}_{s}\leq c_{*}^{3\frac{(s-2)}{2s}}
, where
c_{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\big(\frac{2}{\pi}\big)^{2/3}
.
See (2.22) in
[
36
]
for an implicit evaluation of
\bar{c}_{s}
.

Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev (HLS) inequality

[
32
, Theorem 4.3]
.
For every
\alpha\in(0,3)
, there exists a constant
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})|v|^{2}dx\leq c_{\alpha}|u|_{\frac{12}{3+\alpha}}^{2}|v|_{\frac{12}{3+\alpha}}^{2},\qquad\forall u,v\in L^{\frac{12}{3+\alpha}},

(3.2)

where
c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma((3-\alpha)/2)}{\pi^{2\alpha/3}2^{\alpha/3}\Gamma((3+\alpha)/2)}
, and
c_{\alpha}\to 1
as
\alpha\to 0
.

As a direct consequence of the HLS inequality, by using suitable triangulations, we obtain the following useful convergence properties of the Riesz potentials (cf. also
[
38
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence in
H^{1}
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{\frac{12}{3+\alpha}}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}(u_{n})\to{\mathcal{D}}(u)
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(I_{\alpha}*|u_{n}|^{2})u_{n}\varphi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})u\varphi\,dx,\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}.

(3.3)

Interpolation inequalities and Energy-Nehari-Pohožaev system.

The following
L^{2}
–estimates follow from the GN and HLS inequalities by interpolation,

\displaystyle|u|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{c}\,\rho^{1/4}|{\nabla}u|_{2}^{3/4},\qquad\forall u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho},

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}\,\rho^{\frac{4}{3}\alpha}|u|_{4}^{4-\frac{4}{3}\alpha},\qquad\forall u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho},

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\bar{c}}_{\alpha}\,\rho^{1+\alpha}|{\nabla}u|_{2}^{3-\alpha},\qquad\forall u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho},

(3.6)

where
\bar{c}:=\bar{c}_{4}
and
\bar{\bar{c}}_{\alpha}:=c_{\alpha}\,\bar{c}^{\left(4-\frac{4}{3}\alpha\right)}
.
As a consequence of (
3.4
) and (

), for every

the energy

It is standard to check that the constrained functional

is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
on

and
critical points of

are weak normalised solutions of (

N
ρ

),
i.e. if

is a critical point of

then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}u\varphi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{2})u\varphi\,dx=-\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\varphi\,dx,\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1},

for an unknown Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, if
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
is a weak normalised solution of (

N
ρ

) with a Lagrange multiplier

then

satisfies the
Nehari identity

|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2}+|u|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(u)=0.

(3.7)

It is standard to see that under minor regularity assumptions

also satisfies the
Pohožaev identity
.
The proof is an adaptation of
[
38
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.3

(Pohožaev identity)
.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
be a weak normalised solution of (

N
ρ

) with a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}
.
If

then

\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2}+\frac{3}{4}|u|_{4}^{4}-\frac{3+\alpha}{4}{\mathcal{D}}(u)=0.

(3.8)

For a normalised solution

that satisfies conditions of Proposition

A=|\nabla u|_{2}^{2},\quad B=|u|_{4}^{4},\quad C={\mathcal{D}}(u).

Then for any

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}A+\frac{1}{4}B-\frac{1}{4}C&=\mu_{\rho}\\
A+B-C&=-\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}A+\frac{3}{4}B-\frac{3+\alpha}{4}C&=-\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.10)

to deduce algebraic relations between various quantities involved. For instance, we obtain

\mu_{\rho}=\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{2(1-\alpha)A-\alpha B}{3-\alpha},\qquad\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2}=\frac{(1+\alpha)A+\alpha B}{3-\alpha},\qquad C=\frac{4A+3B}{3-\alpha}.

(3.11)

In particular, the 2nd relation in (

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a weak normalised solution of (

Proof.

A connection between normalised minimizers and fixed frequency ground states.

Given
\lambda\geq 0
, denote

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:=\{u\in H^{1}\setminus\{0\}:|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+\lambda|u|^{2}_{2}+|u|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(u)=0\}

the Nehari manifold of the
\lambda
–frequency problem
(

P
λ

)
.
The next two statements follow closely the arguments in
[
14
, Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
18
]
for similar results).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
,
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
and
\rho>0
. Then

F_{\lambda}(v)\geq m_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2}.

(3.15)

Equality holds in (
3.15
) if and only if
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
and it is both a minimizer for
F_{|{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}}
and a
\lambda
–frequency ground state of
(

P
λ

)
.

Proof.

Let
\rho>0
and
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
. By the definition of Nehari manifold, for every
t>0
it holds
F_{\lambda}(tv)\leq F_{\lambda}(v)
, with strict inequality unless
t=1
. Hence, given any
\rho>0
,

F_{\lambda}(v)\geq F_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{\rho}{|v|_{2}}v\Big)=F\Big(\frac{\rho}{|v|_{2}}v\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2}\geq m_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2},

(3.16)

since
\frac{\rho}{|v|_{2}}v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
and (
3.15
) is proved.

To conclude, note that if
v\in S_{\rho}
is a minimizer for
F_{|{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}}
then

F_{\lambda}(v)=F(v)+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2}=m_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2},

that is equality in (
3.15
) holds.

Conversely, if equality in (
3.15
) holds for some
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
then equality also holds in (
3.16
). This implies that
|v|_{2}=\rho
and hence
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
and
F(v)=m_{\rho}
.
Moreover,
v
must be a minimizer on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
and hence a
\lambda
–frequency ground state of
(

P
λ

)
,
otherwise there exists
w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
,

and such that
F_{\lambda}(w)<F_{\lambda}(v)=m_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\rho^{2}
, which contradicts (
3.15
).
∎

As a consequence of the last statement of Lemma
3.7
, it is readily seen that the following corollary holds.

Corollary 3.8
.

If
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
is a minimizer for

then

is a
\lambda_{v}
–frequency ground state of
(P_{\lambda_{v}})
. Moreover, any other
\lambda_{v}
–frequency ground state of

belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
and is a minimizer for

.

4.1.
Global minimizers for all

First we are going to establish the existence of global minimizers for

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,3)
and
\rho>0
.
Then
m_{\rho}<0
and there exists a positive normalised solution
u_{\rho}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}
of (

N
ρ

) such that
F(u_{\rho})=m_{\rho}

Moreover,

is radially symmetric and
u_{\rho}\in L^{1}\cap C^{2}\cap W^{2,s}

Proof.

In order to show that
m_{\rho}<0
, let us fix
\bar{u}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}

\alpha>1

Then, by Lemma

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,3)
and
\rho>0
.
The map
\rho\mapsto m_{\rho}
is continuous, strictly decreasing and strictly concave.

Proof.

The continuity follows from the concavity.

First, we are going to prove the monotonicity.
Let
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
and observe that
F(u_{\rho_{1}})<0
implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{4}_{\rho_{1}}dx-{\mathcal{D}}(u_{\rho_{1}})<0.

Then

\displaystyle m_{\rho_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}\,u_{\rho_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|{\nabla}u_{\rho_{1}}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{4}_{\rho_{1}}dx-{\mathcal{D}}(u_{\rho_{1}})\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{2}F(u_{\rho_{1}})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle m_{\rho_{1}},

that proves the claim.

To establish the concavity, let us fix
t\in(0,1)
and set
\bar{\rho}=t\rho_{1}+(1-t)\rho_{2}
.
By definition of
m_{\rho}
we see

\begin{split}t\,m_{\rho_{1}}+(1-t)\,m_{\rho_{2}}\leq&\,t\,F\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\bar{\rho}}u_{\bar{\rho}}\right)+(1-t)\,F\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\bar{\rho}}u_{\bar{\rho}}\right)\\
=&\left[t\,\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{2}+(1-t)\,\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{2}\right]\tilde{A}-\left[t\,\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{4}+(1-t)\,\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{4}\right]\tilde{D},\end{split}

where
\tilde{A}=\frac{1}{2}|{\nabla}u_{\bar{\rho}}|_{2}^{2}
and
\tilde{D}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|u_{\bar{\rho}}|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(u_{\bar{\rho}})\right)
.
Since
m_{\bar{\rho}}=\tilde{A}-\tilde{D}
, then the strict concavity is equivalent to

\left[t\,\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{2}+(1-t)\,\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{2}-1\right]\tilde{A}-\left[t\,\left(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{4}+(1-t)\,\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{4}-1\right]\tilde{D}<0.

(4.9)

From

there follows

, so (
4.9
) holds because

1<\frac{t\rho_{1}^{2}+(1-t)\rho_{2}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}^{2}}<\frac{t\rho_{1}^{4}+(1-t)\rho_{2}^{4}}{\bar{\rho}^{4}},

by the convexity of
\tau\mapsto\tau^{2}
and Lemma
4.3
below.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0
and
t\in(0,1)
, then for every

\frac{t\rho_{1}^{p_{1}}+(1-t)\rho_{2}^{p_{1}}}{(t\rho_{1}+(1-t)\rho_{2})^{p_{1}}}<\frac{t\rho_{1}^{p_{2}}+(1-t)\rho_{2}^{p_{2}}}{(t\rho_{1}+(1-t)\rho_{2})^{p_{2}}}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma>1
such that

.
Then by convexity we obtain

∎

4.2.
The Thomas–Fermi limit for
\rho\to\infty

Let
\rho\to\infty
. Consider the normalized family

as defined in (

), and recall that

m_{\rho}\sim-\rho^{4},\qquad\lambda_{\rho}\sim\rho^{2}.

(4.18)

Then

|z_{\rho}|_{2}^{2}=1
, and for every

,

z_{\rho}
is a global minimum on
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
of the rescaled energy

\tilde{F}_{\rho}(z)=\frac{\rho^{-2}}{2}|\nabla z|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|z|_{4}^{4}-\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{D}}(z).

Moreover,

\tilde{m}_{\rho}:=\min_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}}\tilde{F}_{\rho}=\tilde{F}_{\rho}(z_{\rho})=\frac{m_{\rho}}{\rho^{4}}\sim-1\quad\text{as $\rho\to\infty$},

and

satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation

where
\lambda_{\rho}\sim\rho^{2}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,3)
.
Then

\tilde{m}_{\rho}\to m^{T\!F}_{1},\qquad\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{-2}\to-4m^{T\!F}_{1},\quad\mbox{ as }\rho\to\infty,

and the rescaled minimizers

z_{\rho}
converge strongly in
L^{2}
and
L^{4}
to the radially symmetric minimizer

z_{\infty}
of the Thomas–Fermi minimization problem (
2.10
), in
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
.

Proof.

Taking
z_{\rho}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
as a trial function for
E_{T\!F}
, we see immediately that

m^{T\!F}_{1}\leq\tilde{m}_{\rho}-\frac{\rho^{-2}}{2}|\nabla z_{\rho}|_{2}^{2}<\tilde{m}_{\rho}\qquad\forall\rho>0.

(4.19)

Now, let
z_{\infty}\in C^{0,1/2}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
be the unique compactly supported ground state of the Thomas–Fermi problem (
2.10
) with
\rho=1
,
see Proposition
2.4
.
Note that

z_{\infty}\leq c(R_{*}-|x|)^{1/2}\quad\text{in $B_{R_{*}}$,}

where
R_{*}>0
is the support radius of

z_{\infty}
; and

z_{\infty}
is smooth inside the support.
Yet we can not conclude that

z_{\infty}\in H^{1}
because of the possible singularity of the gradient on the boundary of the support
(and we know that

z_{\infty}\not\in H^{1}
when
\alpha=2
, see Remark
2.9
).

If

z_{\infty}\in H^{1}
, we can use

z_{\infty}
as a trial function for
\tilde{F}_{\rho}
and, by using (
4.19
), we get the estimate

m_{1}^{{T\!F}}\leq\tilde{m}_{\rho}\leq\tilde{F}_{\rho}(z_{\infty})=E_{T\!F}(z_{\infty})+\frac{\rho^{-2}}{2}|\nabla z_{\infty}|_{2}^{2}=m^{T\!F}_{1}+\frac{\rho^{-2}}{2}|\nabla z_{\infty}|_{2}^{2}

that implies
\tilde{m}_{\rho}\to m_{1}^{{T\!F}}
as
\rho\to\infty
.

Now, assume

z_{\infty}\not\in H^{1}
.
For
r>0
we introduce the family of cut-off functions
\eta_{r}(x):=\bar{\eta}\big(r(|x|-R_{*})\big)
,
where
\bar{\eta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},[0,1])
verifies

\bar{\eta}(t)=1
for
t\leq-1
and
\bar{\eta}(t)=0
for
t\geq-\frac{1}{2}
.
Then, we set

z_{r}(x):=\tau_{r}\eta_{r}(x)z_{\infty}(x),

where
\tau_{r}:=|\eta_{r}z_{\infty}|_{2}^{-1}
, so that
|z_{r}|_{2}=1
.
As
r\to\infty
, it is elementary to see that
\tau_{r}\to 1
and

|z_{r}|_{4}^{4}=|z_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}+o(1),\quad{\mathcal{D}}(z_{r})={\mathcal{D}}(z_{\infty})+o(1).

(4.20)

Set

A_{r}:=|\nabla z_{r}|_{2}^{2}.

Since
z_{\infty}\not\in H^{1}
, by Fatou Lemma
A_{r}\to\infty
.
Set
\rho_{r}=A_{r}
. Then as
r\to\infty
,

\tilde{m}_{\rho_{r}}\leq\tilde{F}_{\rho_{r}}(z_{r})=E_{T\!F}(z_{r})+\frac{\rho_{r}^{-2}}{2}A_{r}=m^{T\!F}_{1}+o(1)+\frac{1}{2A_{r}}\to m^{T\!F}_{1}.

Therefore, by the continuity of
r\mapsto A_{r}
and by (
4.19
) we can conclude that, as
\rho\to\infty
,

m_{1}^{{T\!F}}\leq\tilde{m}_{\rho}\leq m^{T\!F}_{1}+o(1),

that implies again
\tilde{m}_{\rho}\to m_{1}^{T\!F}
as
\rho\to\infty
.

Now, let
\{\rho_{n}\}
any sequence such that
\rho_{n}\to\infty
, then

m^{T\!F}_{1}\leq E_{T\!F}(z_{\rho_{n}})\leq\tilde{F}_{\rho_{n}}(z_{\rho_{n}})=\tilde{m}_{\rho_{n}}\to m^{T\!F}_{1},

i.e.

\{z_{\rho_{n}}\}
is a minimizing sequence for
m^{T\!F}_{1}
.
Using an adaptation of the arguments in
[
34
, Theorem II.1]
we can conclude that

z_{\rho_{n}}
converges to the

z_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}
and
L^{4}
, up to a subsequence.
We outline a direct argument for completeness (cf.
[
35
, Proposition 6.1]
).

Recall that the sequence
\{z_{\rho_{n}}\}
verifies
|z_{\rho_{n}}|_{2}\equiv 1
and
z_{\rho_{n}}
is monotone radially decreasing.
Moreover,
|z_{\rho_{n}}|_{4}
is bounded, indeed by (

E_{T\!F}(z_{\rho_{n}})\geq\frac{1}{4}|z_{\rho_{n}}|_{4}^{4}-c\,|z_{\rho_{n}}|_{4}^{4-\frac{4}{3}\alpha}.

Then, by the Strauss’s radial lemma
[
4
, Lemma A.IV]

0\leq z_{\rho_{n}}\leq U(|x|):=C\min\{|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}},|x|^{-\frac{3}{4}}\}\quad\text{for all $|x|>0$}.

(4.21)

By Helly’s selection principle for monotone functions (cf.
[
32
, Proof of Theorem 3.7]
),
there exists a nonnegative radially symmetric nonincreasing function
\tilde{z}_{\infty}(|x|)\leq U(|x|)
such that, up to a subsequence,

z_{\rho_{n}}(x)\to\tilde{z}_{\infty}(x)\quad\text{a.e. in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ as $n\to\infty$}.

Moreover, we can assume
z_{\rho_{n}}\rightharpoonup\tilde{z}_{\infty}
weakly in
L^{2}
and in
L^{4}
.
Since

for all

, by the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence we conclude that

z_{\rho_{n}}\to\tilde{z}_{\infty}
in
L^{s}
, for every
s\in(2,4)
.
Since
s=\frac{12}{3+\alpha}\in(2,4)
, we get

by Lemma

.
Observe that
|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{2}\leq 1
and that, by the Brezis-Lieb lemma
[
32
, Theorem 1.9]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}+|z_{\rho_{n}}-\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}|z_{\rho_{n}}|_{4}^{4}.

Moreover

E_{T\!F}\left(\frac{\tilde{z}_{\infty}}{|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{2}}\right)\geq m^{T\!F}_{1}\ \Longrightarrow\ E_{T\!F}(\tilde{z}_{\infty})\geq|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{2}^{4}\,m^{T\!F}_{1}.

Then

\displaystyle 4m^{T\!F}_{1}=4\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{T\!F}(z_{\rho_{n}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}+|z_{\rho_{n}}-\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}\big)-\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{D}}(z_{\rho_{n}})

\displaystyle=4E_{T\!F}(\tilde{z}_{\infty})+\lim_{n\to\infty}|z_{\rho_{n}}-\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\geq 4|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{2}^{4}\,m^{T\!F}_{1}+\lim_{n\to\infty}|z_{\rho_{n}}-\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}^{4},

which implies
|\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{2}=1
and
|z_{\rho_{n}}-\tilde{z}_{\infty}|_{4}\to 0
, since
m^{T\!F}_{1}<0
.
We conclude
\tilde{z}_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
,
E_{T\!F}(\tilde{z}_{\infty})=m^{T\!F}_{1}
, and hence

\tilde{z}_{\infty}=z_{\infty}
by the uniqueness, and furthermore
z_{\rho_{n}}\to z_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}
and
L^{4}
.

To prove

, observe that by (

)

\lambda_{\rho}\rho^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\big(|u_{\rho}|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(u_{\rho})\big)-2m_{\rho},

hence

\frac{\lambda_{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\big(|z_{\rho}|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(z_{\rho})\big)-2\tilde{m}_{\rho}.

Then the claim follows, as
\rho\to\infty
, taking into account
|z_{\infty}|_{4}^{4}-{\mathcal{D}}(z_{\infty})=4m^{T\!F}_{1}
.
∎

.

.

5.37

5.39

Appendix A
L^{2}
–convergence of the fixed frequency ground states

Recall that in
[

of the fixed frequency problem (

Lemma A.1
.

In the above notations,
|w_{\lambda}-w_{*}|_{2}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
, up to a subsequence.

Proof.

The rescaled ground states

solve

and the rescaled energy takes the form

\bar{F}_{\lambda}(w):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|{\nabla}w|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{D}}(w)+\frac{\lambda^{\alpha/2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w|^{4}dx.

Denote

A_{\lambda}=|\nabla w_{\lambda}|_{2}^{2},\quad B_{\lambda}=|w_{\lambda}|_{4}^{4},\quad C_{\lambda}={\mathcal{D}}(w_{\lambda}),\quad\bar{\rho}_{\lambda}^{2}=|w_{\lambda}|_{2}^{2},\quad\bar{\mu}_{\lambda}=\bar{F}_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})

and

A_{*}=|\nabla w_{*}|_{2}^{2},\quad\quad C_{*}={\mathcal{D}}(w_{*}),\quad\bar{\rho}_{*}^{2}=|w_{\lambda}|_{2}^{2},\quad\bar{\mu}_{*}=\bar{E}_{\mathscr{C}}(w_{*}).

From the Energy–Nehari–Pohožaev relations
[
35
, Proposition 4.1]
we deduce for

the algebraic system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}A_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{\alpha/2}B_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{4}C_{\lambda}&=\bar{\mu}_{\lambda}\\
A_{\lambda}+\bar{\rho}_{\lambda}^{2}+\lambda^{\alpha/2}B_{\lambda}-C_{\lambda}&=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}A_{\lambda}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\rho}_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\lambda^{\alpha/2}B_{\lambda}-\frac{3+\alpha}{4}C_{\lambda}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and an analogous system for

In particular, we conclude that

\bar{\rho}_{\lambda}^{2}=\frac{1+\alpha}{3-\alpha}A_{\lambda}+\frac{\alpha}{3-\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha/2}B_{\lambda},\qquad\bar{\rho}_{*}^{2}=\frac{1+\alpha}{3-\alpha}A_{*}.

(A.2)

(A.3)

by

We conclude that

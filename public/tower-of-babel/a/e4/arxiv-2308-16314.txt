Abstract
. We consider the multiparameter random simplicial complex on a vertex set { 1 , … , n } \{1,\dots,n\} , which is parameterized by multiple connectivity probabilities. Our key results concern the topology of this complex of dimensions higher than the critical dimension. We show that the higher-dimensional Betti numbers satisfy strong laws of large numbers and central limit theorems. Moreover, lower tail large deviations for these Betti numbers are also discussed. Some of our results indicate an occurrence of phase transitions in terms of the scaling constants of the central limit theorem, and the exponentially decaying rate of convergence of lower tail large deviation probabilities.

1.
Introduction

The main theme of this paper is topological features of large
high-dimensional complex systems. A large complex system of interest
to us is a random simplicial complex, in which minor changes in a
low-dimensional and local structure may significantly affect
high-dimensional and global structure of the entire system. The
random simplicial complex considered in this paper is a
higher-dimensional analog of the Erdős-Rényi random graph on
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
, where edges are added with probability
p

independently of other potential edges. The literature contains
several higher-dimensional extensions of the Erdős-Rényi random graph. One is
the
random clique complex
, in which a set of vertices in
[n]

forms a face (or a simplex) if and only if these vertices form a
clique in the Erdős-Rényi random graph (
[
9
,
21
]
). The
Linial–Meshulam complex

[
12
]

is a
2
-dimensional random simplicial complex, which starts with the
full
1
-dimensional skeleton of the complete simplicial complex and
adds each
2
-dimensional face, independently with probability

p
. This complex was extended to its
d
-dimensional version by
Meshulam and Wallach (
[
14
]
). In this paper we
study the
multiparameter random simplicial complex
introduced
by Costa and Farber (
[
3
,
4
]
) as a
generalization of all the models described above. This is an abstract
simplicial complex on
[n]
, which is parameterized by a sequence of
connectivity probabilities
{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n-1}
. One can construct this complex recursively. First we
take
[n]
as the
0
-dimensional skeleton and add edges between pairs
of vertices independently with probability
p_{1}
, just like in the
Erdős-Rényi random graph. For each
i=2,\dots,n-1
, an
i
-dimensional face
(henceforth we simply say “
i
-face”) is added with probability

p_{i}
, independently of all other potential faces, conditioned on the
presence of all the
(i-1)
-faces that constitute a boundary of that

i
-face. The complex constructed this way is denoted as
X([n],{\bf p})
.

A number of studies of the multiparameter random simplicial complexes
have appeared recently, many of which focus on the asymptotic, as

n\to\infty
, behavior of topological invariants of the complex, such
as the
Betti numbers
and the
Euler characteristic
. The
model typically contains a particular dimension in which the Betti
number dominates the Betti numbers in other dimensions. This
dimension is called the
critical dimension
, and much of the
effort has been devoted to the analysis of Betti numbers at the
critical dimension
[
10
,
21
,
5
,
16
,
11
]
. There
are also results on the Betti numbers in dimensions lower than the
critical dimension. For example, by employing the cohomology
vanishing theorem
[
1
]
and the spectral
gap theorem
[
7
]
, Fowler proved that
the cohomology groups in dimensions lower than the critical dimension
vanish with high probability (
[
5
]
). However, very
little has been done about the asymptotics, as
n\to\infty
, of Betti
numbers in dimension higher than the critical dimension, except for
certain special cases in the context of random clique complexes; see

[
10
,
21
]
.

In this paper we derive various limit theorems for the Betti numbers of
dimension greater than the critical dimension. We start by
establishing the strong law of large numbers (SLLN) and the central
limit theorem (CLT) for these high-dimensional Betti numbers. The
obtained limit theorems characterize the “average” behavior and the
“likely deviations” from the average behavior of these Betti
numbers. We also derive a lower bound for the lower tail large deviation
probabilities for the Betti numbers of
dimension greater than the critical dimension.

This paper is structured as follows. Section

2
provides a precise setup of our
multiparameter random simplicial complex. In this section we also
present the SLLN, the CLT, and large deviations results on Betti
numbers in dimensions greater than the critical dimension. Some of
our results indicate presence of phase transitions in terms of the
scaling constants in the CLT and the exponentially decaying rate of
convergence for the lower tail large deviation probabilities. The
proofs are in Section
3
, with some auxiliary results
deferred to the Appendix.

We conclude this Introduction with several comments on our setup and
our results. We treat only the “static” version of the multiparameter
simplicial complex, where the complex is sampled once and for
all. The questions we are pursuing in this paper make sense also for
the dynamic multiparameter simplicial complexes considered in our previous work
[
16
]
and in

[
21
,
20
,
6
]
. In particular, the models in
[
21
,
16
]
involve on/off
processes to describe the state of various ingredients, so that the complex,
though distributionally stationary, may change with time. Since even
the “static” case is technically involved enough, we have decided
not to pursuit this direction in the present work. Further, in our
large deviation results, we present only “lower bound” for the lower
tail probabilities for the Betti numbers. We conjecture that the
logarithmic order of magnitude of our lower bounds in Theorem

2.5
is the tightest possible, but we do not yet have
corresponding upper bounds to justify that conjecture.
There are, clearly, a number of other
follow-up questions on large deviations. These questions are postponed to future work.

Throughout the paper, we will use the following notation. The
cardinality of a set
A
is denoted by
|A|
.
Given a sequence
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of real numbers, we denote

\bigvee_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}:=\sup_{i\geq 1}a_{i}
and

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}:=\inf_{i\geq 1}a_{i}
. Moreover,

\Rightarrow
denotes weak convergence in
{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathcal{N}(a,b^{2})
is a normal distribution with mean
a
and variance
b^{2}
,
and
{\mathbbm{1}}\{\cdot\}
represents an indicator function. Finally, we
will use the capital letter
C
and its versions (such as
C_{j},\,C^{*}
, etc) to denote finite positive constants whose exact values are
not important and can change from one appearance to the next.

2.
Setup and main results

We start with a precise setup for the multiparameter random simplicial
complex
X([n],{\bf p})
described in the previous section.
As in the previous studies
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
16
]
,
we choose the connectivity probabilities
{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n-1})
to be the following functions of
n
:

p_{i}=n^{-\alpha_{i}},\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n-1,

where each
\alpha_{i}\in[0,\infty]
is a parameter. Given a sequence of parameters
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
, let

(2.1)

\psi_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\binom{j}{i}\alpha_{i},\ \ j\geq 1.

Define
q:=\min\{i\geq 1:\alpha_{i}>0\}
, the smallest dimension
i
such that
p_{i}
goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
Notice that (
2.1
) is increasing in the sense
of
\psi_{i}<\psi_{j}
for all
q\leq i<j
. We assume, as is common in
the literature (
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
16
]
), that the system has
a
critical
dimension
k
, which is a dimension satisfying

0<\psi_{q}<\dots<\psi_{k}<1<\psi_{k+1}<\dots,\ \ \text{with }\ k\geq q.

Next, define

\tau_{j}:=j+1-\sum_{i=1}^{j}\psi_{i}=j+1-\sum_{i=1}^{j}\binom{j+1}{i+1}\alpha_{i},\ \ 0\leq j\leq n-1

(by convention we set
\sum_{i=1}^{0}\equiv 0
). Then, the monotonicity of
(\psi_{j})
ensures that

(2.2)

q=\tau_{q-1}<\tau_{q}<\cdots<\tau_{k}>\tau_{k+1}>\cdots,

which also implies that
(\tau_{j})
is maximized at the critical dimension
k
. Furthermore,
\tau_{j}
can be negative for large
j
.

The main theme of this paper is the asymptotic behavior of the Betti numbers of dimension higher than the critical dimension, i.e.,
\beta_{m,n}:=\beta_{m}\big(X([n],{\bf p})\big)
for
m\geq k+1
.
We obtain the SLLN and the CLT for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
. Moreover,
a lower bound for the lower tail large deviations for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
will be derived as well.
To state our main results, fix
m\geq k+1
. We prove the the SLLN and
the CLT under the assumption

(2.3)

{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}>0,\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \alpha_{m+1}\in(0,\infty).

Our first result is the SLLN for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the assumption (
2.3
), we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{\beta_{m,n}}{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}\to\frac{1}{(m+2)!},\ \ \text{a.s.}

Remark 2.2
.

For comparison, the SLLN for the Betti numbers at the critical
dimension
k
was established in

[
16
]
: as
n\to\infty
,

(2.4)

\frac{\beta_{k,n}}{n^{\tau_{k}}}\to\frac{1}{(k+1)!},\ \ \text{a.s.}

The proof of (
2.4
) heavily depends on the Morse inequality,

f_{k,n}-f_{k+1,n}-f_{k-1,n}\leq\beta_{k,n}\leq f_{k,n},

where
f_{j,n}
denotes the simplex count of dimension
j
in
X([n],{\bf p})
. It was shown in
[
16
]
that

f_{k,n}
dominates
f_{k\pm 1,n}
as
n\to\infty
, at least in the
sense of expectations, and the SLLN for
(\beta_{k,n})_{n\geq 1}
was
obtained as a direct result of the SLLN for
(f_{k,n})_{n\geq 1}
. A
much more delicate argument is required in
the current study, and it is based on an explicit representation of
the higher-dimensional Betti numbers.

Next, we state the CLT for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
with
m\geq k+1
. It
requires a condition in addition to (
2.3
).
For
n\geq m+2
and
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\subset[n]
with
|A_{i}|=m+2
, set

(2.5)

\ell_{ij}=|A_{i}\cap A_{j}|,\ \ 1\leq i<j\leq 3,\ \ \ell_{123}=|A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap A_{3}|

and denote

\mathcal{A}_{m,k}:=\big\{(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\subset[n]^{3}:|A_{i}|=m+2,\,i=1,2,3,\ \ell_{ij}\in\{k+2,\dots,m+1\},\,1\leq i<j\leq 3\big\}.

We assume that

(2.6)

\frac{3}{2}\big(\tau_{q}\wedge(\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1})\big)+\bigvee_{(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\in\mathcal{A}_{m,k}}\Big\{\tau_{\ell_{123}-1}-\tau_{\ell_{12}-1}-\tau_{\ell_{13}-1}-\tau_{\ell_{23}-1}\Big\}<0.

Note that it is possible to avoid including a selection of sets from

[n]
as a part of the assumption. It is not difficult to check that
numbers
\ell_{12},\ell_{13},\ell_{23},\ell_{123}
in
(
2.5
) are feasible in
(
2.6
) if and only if the following conditions hold:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\ell_{ij}\in\{k+2,\dots,m+1\},\,1\leq i<j\leq 3,

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{\ell_{12}+\ell_{13},\ell_{12}+\ell_{23},\ell_{13}+\ell_{23}\bigr\}-(m+2)\leq\ell_{123}\leq\min\bigl\{\ell_{12},\ell_{13},\ell_{23}\bigr\}.

Instead of referring to the restrictions (
2.7
), we
keep (
2.6
) as a “more operational” formulation in the
proof.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume conditions (
2.3
) and (
2.6
).

(i)
If
{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}>\tau_{q}
, then,

\frac{\beta_{m,n}-\mathbb{E}[\beta_{m,n}]}{n^{{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}-\tau_{q}/2}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}\big(0,(q+1)!((m-q+1)!)^{2}\big),\ \ \text{as }n\to\infty.

(ii)
If
{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}<\tau_{q}
, then,

\frac{\beta_{m,n}-\mathbb{E}[\beta_{m,n}]}{n^{\frac{1}{2}({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,(m+2)!),\ \ \text{as }n\to\infty.

(iii)
If
{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}=\tau_{q}
, then,

\frac{\beta_{m,n}-\mathbb{E}[\beta_{m,n}]}{n^{\frac{1}{2}({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}\left(0,\Big(\frac{1}{(q+1)!((m-q+1)!)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(m+2)!}\Big)^{-1}\right),\ \ \text{as }n\to\infty.

Remark 2.4
.

It is not difficult to verify that in some cases the conditions
(
2.3
) and (
2.6
) hold at the same
time. For example, if
m=k+1
, then (
2.6
) always holds!
Indeed, we know by (
2.7
) that, in this case,

\ell_{12}=\ell_{13}=\ell_{23}=k+2
and
\ell_{123}\in\{k+1,k+2\}
. Since
\tau_{k+1}<\tau_{k}
, one can upper
bound (
2.6
) by

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}(\tau_{k+2}+\alpha_{k+2})+\tau_{k}-3\tau_{k+1}=\frac{3}{2}(\tau_{k}+2-\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k+2}+\alpha_{k+2})+\tau_{k}-3(\tau_{k}+1-\psi_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}-\frac{3}{2}\left[\psi_{k+2}-\alpha_{k+2}-\psi_{k+1}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{k}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\left({k+2\choose i}-{k+1\choose i}\right)\alpha_{i}<0.

On the other hand, the assumption (
2.6
) is not
vacuous. A simple example of a situation where (
2.6
) does
not hold can be constructed by taking

\ell_{12}=\ell_{13}=\ell_{23}=m+1
,
\ell_{123}=m
for a
large even
m
, and choosing a
large
\alpha_{m/2}
.

It is shown in
[
16
]
that the Betti number in the critical dimension satisfies

\frac{\beta_{k,n}-\mathbb{E}[\beta_{k,n}]}{n^{\tau_{k}-\tau_{q}/2}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}\big(0,(q+1)!((k-q)!)^{2}\big),\ \ \text{as }n\to\infty,

so the power of
n
in the scaling depends directly on the value of

\tau_{k}
. In contrast, in Theorem
2.3
the power of
n
in the
scaling depends on
\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}
. Furthermore, Theorem

2.3
describes a phase transition not present in the critical
dimension.

Our final result takes a step towards understanding the lower tail
large deviations for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
with
m\geq k+1
.
In contrast to other limit theorems, such as the SLLN and CLT
(
[
11
,
16
,
21
,
10
]
), very little is
known about large deviations for Betti numbers in the multiparameter
simplicial complex or versions of that model.
The only exception is
[
19
]
, which
investigates the upper tail large deviations for the Betti number in the critical dimension.
In the result below, we are able to derive a lower bound for lower tail large
deviation probabilities for
(\beta_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume condition (
2.3
). Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

(i)
If
{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\geq\tau_{q}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\tau_{q}}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\beta_{m,n}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}{(m+2)!}\Big)\geq-C_{m,q}\varepsilon^{2},

where

(2.8)

C_{m,q}=\frac{5^{4}\cdot 2^{2(m+6)-q}\vee 2^{2(m+5)}}{(q+1)!}.

(ii)
If
{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}<\tau_{q}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\beta_{m,n}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}{(m+2)!}\Big)\geq-\frac{5000}{(m+2)!}\,\varepsilon^{2}.

3.
Proofs

We prove Theorem
2.5
first, as its proof requires
developing certain new techniques that are best seen first. The
proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.3
are postponed to later
sections.

3.1.
Preliminaries

Our proof relies on an explicit representation of Betti
numbers. Recall that an
\ell
-dimensional complex
X
is said
to be
pure
if every face of
X
is contained in an
\ell
-face.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a positive integer
\ell
, a simplicial complex
K
is called

strongly connected
of order
\ell
(we abbreviate it as “
\ell
-strongly connected”) if

(i)
every pair of
\ell
-faces
\sigma,\tau\in K
can be connected by a sequence of
\ell
-faces

\sigma=\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{j-1},\sigma_{j}=\tau,

such that dim
(\sigma_{i}\cap\sigma_{i+1})=\ell-1
for each
0\leq i\leq j-1
.

(ii)
the
\ell
-skeleton of
K
is pure.

Note that the dimension of
K
itself can be greater than

\ell
. Given a simplicial complex
X
, let
K
be an
\ell
-strongly
connected subcomplex of
X
. We say that
K
is
maximal
if
there is no
\ell
-strongly connected subcomplex
K^{\prime}\subset X

containing
K
as a strict subset.

Proposition 3.2

(Proposition A.6 in
[
16
]
)
.

For every
m\geq 1
,

(3.1)

\beta_{m,n}=\beta_{m}\big(X([n],{\bf p})\big)=\sum_{j=m+2}^{n}\sum_{r\geq 1}\sum_{\sigma\subset[n],\,|\sigma|=j}r\eta_{\sigma}^{(j,r,m)},

where
\eta_{\sigma}^{(j,r,m)}
is the indicator function of the event
that
X(\sigma,{\bf p})
, representing the restriction of
X([n],{\bf p})
to
\sigma
,
forms a maximal
m
-strongly connected subcomplex in

X([n],{\bf p})
such that
\beta_{m}\big(X(\sigma,{\bf p})\big)=r
.

We next provide an estimate of the probability that the restriction of

X([n],{\bf p})
to a finite number of points in
[n]
contains an
m
-strongly connected complex,
which has at least one trivial or non-trivial
m
-cycle. Since the
proof is almost identical to Lemma 7.1 of

[
16
]
, we omit it.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be an
m
-strongly connected simplicial complex on
j\geq m+2

points, which contains at least one trivial or non-trivial
m
-cycle.
Then
K
contains at least

\binom{m+2}{i+1}+(j-m-2)\binom{m}{i}

faces of dimension
i=q,\ldots,m
and, hence, for
\sigma\subset[n]
with
|\sigma|=j
,

\mathbb{P}\big(X(\sigma,{\bf p})\ \text{contains a subcomplex
isomorphic to}\ K\big)\leq j!\,\prod_{i=q}^{m}p_{i}^{\binom{m+2}{i+1}}\Big(\prod_{i=q}^{m}p_{i}^{\binom{m}{i}}\Big)^{j-m-2}.

Here, the isomorphism is that of simplicial complexes.

3.2.
Concepts and representations

The main purpose of this section is to introduce a number of concepts
together with their short-hand notations, that are used throughout
the proof of main theorems. First, recalling the indicator function in
(
3.1
), define, for
j\geq m+2
and
r\geq 1
,

(3.2)

T_{j,r}:=\sum_{\sigma\subset[n],\,|\sigma|=j}\eta_{\sigma}^{(j,r,m)}.

If
j=m+2
, then
\eta_{\sigma}^{(m+2,r,m)}
is identically zero for
all
r\geq 2
. In other words,
\eta_{\sigma}^{(m+2,1,m)}
is the only
indicator which can be non-zero. Because of that we will drop the
subscript “
1
” from
T_{m+2,1}
and simply
write
T_{m+2}:=T_{m+2,1}
. In particular, a non-zero value of

\eta_{\sigma}^{(m+2,1,m)}
requires that
X(\sigma,{\bf p})
forms a maximal
non-trivial
m
-cycle, such that
\beta_{m}\big(X(\sigma,{\bf p})\big)=1
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\beta_{m,n}

can be represented as

(3.3)

\beta_{m,n}=\sum_{j=m+2}^{n}\sum_{r\geq 1}rT_{j,r}=T_{m+2}+\sum_{j=m+3}^{n}\sum_{r\geq 1}rT_{j,r}.

Next, let us define for
j\geq m+2
,

(3.4)

S_{j}:=\sum_{\sigma\subset[n],\,|\sigma|=j}\xi_{\sigma}^{(j,m)},

where
\xi_{\sigma}^{(j,m)}
is the indicator function of the event on
which
X(\sigma,{\bf p})

contains an
m
-strongly connected subcomplex with at
least one trivial or non-trivial
m
-cycle.
If
j=m+2
, a non-zero value of the indicator

\xi_{\sigma}^{(m+2,m)}
requires that
X(\sigma,{\bf p})
forms a trivial or
non-trivial
m
-cycle. When
X(\sigma,{\bf p})
forms a non-trivial
m
-cycle, we
have that
\beta_{m}\big(X(\sigma,{\bf p})\big)=1
. Note also that a
“trivial
m
-cycle” means a simplicial complex of a single

(m+1)
-simplex.
Similarly, for
j\geq m+2
, let

(3.5)

R_{j}:=\sum_{\sigma\subset[n],\,|\sigma|=j}\widetilde{\xi}_{\sigma}^{(j,m)},

where
\widetilde{\xi}_{\sigma}^{(j,m)}
is the indicator function
of the event on
which
X(\sigma,{\bf p})

contains an
m
-strongly connected subcomplex. Clearly,
for every
j\geq m+2
we have

(3.6)

\sum_{r\geq 1}T_{j,r}\leq S_{j}\leq R_{j}.

For the proof of Theorem
2.5
, one needs to reformulate
the probability space on which (
3.3
),
(
3.4
), and (
3.5
) are defined, in the following way. Let
K_{n}
be the

(m+1)
-skeleton of a complete simplicial complex on
[n]
. In other
words,
K_{n}
is the set of all simplices in
[n]
with dimension
m+1

or less.
Given connectivity probabilities in
{\bf p}
, let
\Gamma_{\bf p}
be a
random subset of
K_{n}
such that for every
i=1,\dots,m+1
, a word of
length
i+1
is included in
\Gamma_{\bf p}
with probability

p_{i}
. More precisely,
\Gamma_{\bf p}=\Gamma_{\bf p}(\omega)
is a random subset
of
K_{n}
defined on the product space
\{0,1\}^{\sum_{\ell=2}^{m+2}\binom{n}{\ell}}
of Bernoulli random variables
with parameters

(\underbrace{p_{1},\dots,p_{1}}_{\binom{n}{2}},\underbrace{p_{2},\dots,p_{2}}_{\binom{n}{3}},\dots,\underbrace{p_{m+1},\dots,p_{m+1}}_{\binom{n}{m+2}})\in[0,1]^{\sum_{\ell=2}^{m+2}\binom{n}{\ell}}.

Throughout the paper we may and will assume that the
(m+1)
-skeleton of
X([n],{\bf p})

is defined on this probability space.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Under the assumption (
2.3
), for every
\eta\in\big(0,(1-\varepsilon)/3\big)
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{\tau_{q}\wedge({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\beta_{m,n}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\,\frac{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}{(m+2)!}\Big)\\

For the remaining terms in (

-strongly connected subcomplex on

-strongly connected subcomplex

|\sigma|=m+2

X\big(\sigma,p_{1}^{\downarrow},\dots,p_{m}^{\downarrow},p_{m+1}^{\uparrow}\big)
forms a
non-trivial
m
-cycle is given by
\prod_{i=q}^{m}(p_{i}^{\downarrow})^{\binom{m+2}{i+1}}(1-p_{m+1}^{\uparrow})
. Further,
\sigma
contains
\binom{m+2}{m}
faces of dimension
m-1
.
Consider a vertex
w\in[v_{N+1}]\setminus\sigma
and an
(m-1)
-face

in
\sigma
. Then,
w
and
f
form an
m
-face with probability

\prod_{i=q}^{m}(p_{i}^{\uparrow})^{\binom{m}{i}}=v_{N}^{-\psi_{m}}
.
By the inclusion-exclusion formula,
w
forms an
m
-face with at
least one of the
(m-1)
-faces in
\sigma
, with probability

\binom{m+2}{m}v_{N}^{-\psi_{m}}-u_{N}
, with
u_{N}
accounting for the
probability that multiple
m
-faces are formed between
w
and the

(m-1)
-faces in
\sigma
. Since there are at least
v_{N+1}-(m+2)

in
[v_{N+1}]\setminus\sigma
, the probability that no vertex
s\in[v_{N+1}]\setminus\sigma
forms an
m
-face with an
(m-1)
-face in

\sigma
can be written as

(3.34)

\Big(1-\binom{m+2}{m}v_{N}^{-\psi_{m}}+u_{N}\Big)^{v_{N+1}-(m+2)},

and (
3.33
) follows.

As
\psi_{m}>1
and
\alpha_{m+1}>0
, we see that (
3.34
) tends to
1
as
N\to\infty
, and
1-p_{m+1}^{\uparrow}\to 1
,
N\to\infty
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}[T_{m+2}^{\downarrow}]\sim\frac{v_{N}^{m+2}}{(m+2)!}\,v_{N+1}^{-\sum_{i=q}^{m}\binom{m+2}{i+1}\alpha_{i}}\sim\frac{v_{N+1}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}{(m+2)!},\ \ \ N\to\infty,

and so, there exists
N_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all

,

\mathbb{E}[T_{m+2}^{\downarrow}]\geq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}}{(m+2)!}\,v_{N+1}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}.

Once again, by Chebyshev’s inequality and (
4.17
) of Lemma
4.4
conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(T_{m+2}^{\downarrow}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}}{(m+2)!}\,v_{N+1}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(T_{m+2}^{\downarrow}-\mathbb{E}[T_{m+2}^{\downarrow}]\leq-\frac{\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}}{(m+2)!}\,v_{N+1}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C^{*}\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\big(N^{-\gamma\tau_{q}}\vee N^{-\gamma({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}\big)<\infty,

as required.
∎

In the next proposition we start addressing the rest of the terms in the representation
(

) of the Betti numbers.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume (

). Then for every

\frac{T_{j,r}}{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}\to 0\ \ \text{a.s. as }\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

Analogously to (

S_{j}^{\uparrow}:=\sum_{\sigma\subset[v_{N+1}],\,|\sigma|=j}\xi_{\sigma}^{(j,m,\uparrow)},\ \ \ j\geq m+3,

for every

, and
by the first Borel-Cantelli lemma, it is suffices to show that for every

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}v_{N+1}^{j}\prod_{i=q}^{m}(p_{i}^{\uparrow})^{\binom{m+2}{i+1}}\Big\{\prod_{i=q}^{m}(p_{i}^{\uparrow})^{\binom{m}{i}}\Big\}^{j-m-2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}v_{N}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\big(v_{N}^{1-\psi_{m}}\big)^{j-m-2}=o\big(v_{N}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\big),\ \ \ N\to\infty,

where the last equality is due to

(3.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(S_{j}^{\uparrow}\geq\varepsilon v_{N}^{{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}})&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(S_{j}^{\uparrow}-E[S_{j}^{\uparrow}]\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}v_{N}^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot\frac{\text{Var}(S_{j}^{\uparrow})}{v_{N}^{2({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}}\leq C_{j}\big(N^{-\gamma\tau_{q}}\vee N^{-\gamma({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})}\big),\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from (

) in

of the Appendix. The last term in

)
follows.
∎

\frac{1}{n^{\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}}}\Big(T_{m+2}+\sum_{j=m+3}^{D+m}\sum_{r\geq 1}rT_{j,r}\Big)\to\frac{1}{(m+2)!}\ \ \text{a.s. as }\ n\to\infty,

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume conditions (

) and (

Proof.

Our proof takes an approach similar to that of Proposition 3.6 in

[
16
]
.
Using (
3.17
) we write

W=:\frac{S_{m+2}-\mathbb{E}[S_{m+2}]}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(S_{m+2})}}=\sum_{\sigma\subset[n],\,|\sigma|=m+2}Z_{\sigma},

where

Z_{\sigma}=\frac{I_{\sigma}^{(m+2)}-\mathbb{E}[I_{\sigma}^{(m+2)}]}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(S_{m+2})}}.

Evidently we have
\mathbb{E}[W]=0
,
\text{Var}(W)=1
, and further,
(Z_{\sigma})
constitute a

dissociated set
of random variables in the sense of Barbour et

[
2
]
. Namely, as in (
3.16
), we identify each

(Z_{\sigma},\,\sigma\in L)

The weak convergence claimed in the proposition

the

W
and the

Y
,

(3.38)

d_{1}(W,Y):=\sup_{\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}}\Big|\,\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(W)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Y)\big]\,\Big|\to 0,\ \ \ n\to\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all

For

(3.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\alpha\subset[n],\,|\alpha|=m+2}\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta}Z_{\gamma}|\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta}|\big]\,\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{\gamma}|\big]\Big\}\\
&\leq\frac{C^{*}}{\big(\text{Var}(S_{m+2})\big)^{3/2}}\sum_{\alpha\subset[n],\,|\alpha|=m+2}\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\big[(I_{\alpha}^{(m+2)}+\mathbb{E}[I_{\alpha}^{(m+2)}])(I_{\beta}^{(m+2)}+\mathbb{E}[I_{\beta}^{(m+2)}])\\

).
Appealing to these bounds and using the fact that the number of ways

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\big(\text{Var}(S_{m+2})\big)^{3/2}}\Big\{n^{3({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})-2\tau_{q}}\vee n^{3({\tau_{m+1}+\alpha_{m+1}})-2\tau_{m}}\\

: Next we deal with the case

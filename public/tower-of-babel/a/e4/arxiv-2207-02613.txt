Structural phase-field crystal model for Lennard-Jones pair interaction potential
V Ankudinov

Abstract
A modification of structural phase-field crystal (XPFC) model for an arbitrary pair interaction potential is presented. Formation of 1D and 2D structures for the Lennard-Jones (LJ) potential was studied numerically. The equilibrium lattice parameters for the presented structures were found consistent to the correspondent LJ-distance parameters. The lattice parameter of 2D triangle’s structure matches the periodical in 1D, which shown to be consistent with the theory of freezing from the isotropic liquids. Numerically obtained XPFC phase diagram of two-dimensional structures qualitatively reproduces classical PFC diagram and coincides with the melting region of high-temperature part of LJ diagram.



Keywords
: Phase-field crystal, structural phase-field crystal, pair interaction potentials, pair correlations, Lennard-Jones potential, Lennard-Jones potential

Introduction

The phase-field crystal model (PFC) was formulated

;

;
elder21
;
Greenwood2011
;
Toth2011
to describe continuous transitions from the homogeneous to the various periodic states (similarly to the Landau–Brazovskii transitions

;

;

) and between them over diffusion times. The model is based on the description of a Helmholtz free energy, which is a functional of the atomic density field which is periodic in the solid phase and homogeneous in the liquid (disordered) state.
Recent advances in PFC-modeling of the different aspects of crystallization allow one to model many scenarios such as dynamics of freezing of colloids and polymers, epitaxial growth, ordering on nano-scales
bib:adv2012
;
Granasy2019
;
VanTeeffelen2009
and rapid crystallization

. Results of PFC simulations provide interface energies, pattern selection, multiphase solidification under non-equilibrium conditions, heteroepitaxy and multi-grain growth in presence of hydrodynamical flows

;
Elder2007
;
Guerdane2018
;
Fallah2013
;
Toth2010
;
Tang2011
;
Podmaniczky2017
.
As a simplification of classical density functional theory (cDFT) of freezing
rama79
;
Ryzhov1979
the PFC model utilizes several approximations

;
bib:berry2008
leading to the close to the hard-spheres-potential behavior of density peaks for a classical one-mode PFC. The validity of PFC was proofed by the molecular dynamics
Guerdane2018
;
asadi14
;
Asadi2015a
. But at the same time, rough PFC approximations lead to a bit of criticism concerning about PFC unable to carefully predict surface energies on long times and inaccuracy in quantitative description of elastic properties and emerging structures

;
Archer2019
.

The form of the classical PFC free energy is close to the Swift-Hohenberg equation
ge-swift
;
bib:pe
and in general allows one to describe phase transitions of the first and second order
Ankudinov20202
. Later to robust the structural transitions the artificial multipeaked correlation kernels
Greenwood2010
;
Greenwood2011
were introduced to the PFC models. Such kernels based on so-called structural PFC (XPFC) were generalized for a multi-component metallic systems
Greenwood2011a
;
Ofori-Opoku2013
;
Fallah2013
;
Smith2017
and stable 3D structures such as diamond
Chan2015
;
Baker2015
. Several cases of two-dimensional structures formation were also examined for three-point correlation functions
Seymour2016
;
Elder2018
. Those improvements of XPFC model mostly regard to the modification of the position and size of first and second peak of correlation function in reciprocal space (
k
-space).
It was shown that stabilization of graphene-like structure requires not only a repulsive term but a rotationally invariant correlation function of higher approximation, which also capable to stabilize kagome lattice
mkhonta
(for the case of three-mode PFC).
The multiscale PFC-like model were proposed by
Zapolsky2017
where the excessive energy term was expanded in reciprocal space in two components: short “condensation” term and spherically symmetrical long-range interaction term. Obtained results clearly states that the repulsive term is sufficient for the “structural” crystallization in presence of middle-range attraction written in the form of derivative of
n^{th}
mode. In
Zapolsky2017
the quasi-continuous approximation of the phase field proposed for the case of discrete values (1 or 0) of the atomic density in each lattice node (about 8 nodes per lattice period).
In cDFT the expansion of direct correlation functions with quadratic terms in reciprocal space was carried out by
Ghosh2017
. Such approach in fact is very close to the idea of eight-order
jaatinen09
and twelve-order
Ankudinov2020a
;
Ankudinov2020b
fitting in PFC. Although the derivation of PFC as a consequent approximation of dynamical density functional theory with a gradient expansion involving derivatives leads to the possible instability above a certain value of the average density
Archer2019
. The control of emerging in PFC structures is possible with the pair correlation function analysis proposed in
Kondo2021
.
The promisable approach consisted in introduction a pair-correlation interactions approximated by the rational function to XPFC is discussed in
Pisutha-Arnond2013
.

In present work we propose a modification of the structural phase-field crystal (XPFC) model with the Lennard-Jones pair interaction potential. A study of the structure formation and crystallization from the undercooled liquid (homogeneous) phase was carried out. In particular, the equilibrium lattice parameters, sequence of the emerged structures and their phase diagram were studied.

Classical density functional theory of freezing and phase-field crystal model

The classical PFC equation obtained from approximation of cDFT is suitable for the description of the crystallization of liquids to the simple crystalline structures

;
bib:pe
;
jaatinen09
;

;
Ryzhov1979

The derivation of this model is based on the approximation of cDFT free energy of single-particle’s probability density field
\rho({\bf{r}},t)
.

Let one consider the static isothermal approximation of cDFT free energy containing ideal and excess free energies neglecting any external potential

;
rama79
;
evans79
;
singh91

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\rho({\bf r})]=\mathcal{F}_{id}[\rho({\bf r})]+\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho({\bf r})].

(1)

The

;
bib:adv2012
;
bib:pe

\mathcal{F}_{id}([\rho({\bf{r}})])=k_{B}T\int d{\bf{r}}\,\rho({\bf{r}})(\ln(\lambda^{3}\rho({{\bf{r}}}))-1),

(2)

where

\mathcal{F}_{id}([\rho({\bf{r}})])=k_{B}T\rho_{0}\int\!\!d{\bf{r}}\,[(1+n({\bf{r}}))\,\,\mathrm{ln}\left(1+n({\bf{r}})\right)-n({\bf{r}})].

(3)

The second term

;

The exact expression of

;

;

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ex}

\displaystyle[\rho({\bf r})]=\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho_{0}]+\int d{\bf r}\tilde{\rho}({\bf r})\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho({\bf r})]}{\delta\rho({\bf r})}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\int d{\bf r}d{\bf r^{\prime}}\tilde{\rho}({\bf r})\tilde{\rho}({\bf r^{\prime}})\frac{\delta^{2}\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho({\bf r})]}{\delta\rho({\bf r})\delta\rho({\bf r^{\prime}})}+

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\int\int d{\bf r}d{\bf r^{\prime}}d{\bf r^{\prime\prime}}\tilde{\rho}({\bf r})\tilde{\rho}({\bf r^{\prime}})\tilde{\rho}({\bf r^{\prime\prime}})\frac{\delta^{3}\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho({\bf r})]}{\delta\rho({\bf r})\delta\rho({\bf r^{\prime}})\delta\rho({\bf r^{\prime\prime}})}+...

These functional derivatives of the excess energy are related to the

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{n}\mathcal{F}_{ex}[\rho({\bf r})]}{\delta\rho({\bf r}_{1})\delta\rho({\bf r}_{2})\cdot\cdot\cdot\delta\rho({\bf r}_{n})}=-k_{B}T\,C^{(n)}({\bf r}_{1},{\bf r}_{2}...{\bf r}_{n}).

(5)

Substituting the excess energy with direct correlation functions Eq. (
II

1

\displaystyle F(n)=\frac{\mathcal{F}}{k_{B}TV{\rho}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\int\!\!d{\bf{r}}\,[(1+n({\bf{r}}))\mathrm{ln}\left(1+n({\bf{r}})\right)-n({\bf{r}})]-

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!d{\bf{r}}\!\int\!\!d{\bf{r}}^{\prime}\,\,n({\bf{r}}){C}\|{\bf{r}}-{\bf{r}}^{\prime}\|n({\bf{r}}^{\prime}),

where
{C}\|{\bf{r}}-{\bf{r}}^{\prime}\|
is the pair correlation function.
Here the free energy is scaled by the energy of a reference state:
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}[\rho({\bf r})]-\mathcal{F}[\rho_{0}]
. The pair correlation function could be approximated in a reciprocal space (space of

II.1
Phase-field crystal

The ideal part
\mathcal{F}_{id}
of the scaled free energy Eq. (
6
) could be approximated in the form of Taylor expansion around the reference density
n({\bf r})=0

elder12
;
elder21
.

\mathcal{F}_{id}(n)=(1+n)\mathrm{ln}(1+n)-n\simeq\frac{a}{2}n^{2}-\frac{b}{3}n^{3}+\frac{v}{4}n^{4}.

(7)

Moreover such free energy introduces the first- and second-order phase transitions
Ankudinov20202
;
jaatinen09

k
-vectors) in a manner proposed by Ramakrishnan and Yusoff (RY)

;
Ryzhov1979

{C^{(2)}(k)}\approx-C_{0}+C_{2}k^{2}-C_{4}k^{4}+...

Thus, a truncated series of spatial derivatives in a direct space allow one to model the formation of periodic crystal:

\mathcal{F}_{ex}(n)=\frac{n}{2}(C_{0}-C_{2}\nabla^{2}+C_{4}\nabla^{4}-...)n

The resulted classical PFC free energy read as

\displaystyle{F}[n]=\!\displaystyle\int\!\left[\frac{n}{2}{\cal L}n-\frac{a}{3}n^{3}+\frac{v}{4}n^{4}\right]\!d{\bf{r}},

(10)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{1}\equiv\Delta B_{0}+B_{0}^{x}(q_{S0}^{2}+\nabla^{2})^{2}.

(11)

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!d{\bf{r}}\!\int\!\!d\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\,\,n({\bf{r}}){C}_{2}\|{\bf{r}}-\mathbf{r^{\prime}}\|n(\mathbf{r^{\prime}}).

III.3
Dynamical equation

The PFC and XPFC conserved dynamic equations for atomic density
n
are described with
elder21
;
bib:pe
:

\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}=\nabla^{2}\mu(n),\quad\quad\mu(n)=\frac{\delta F(n)}{\delta n},

(15)

where in classical one-mode PFC
\mu(n)
is a chemical potential defined by the functional derivative (Gateaux) of the free energy (
11
),

\mu=\Delta B_{0}n-an^{2}+vn^{3}+B_{0}^{x}(q_{S0}^{2}+\nabla^{2})^{2}n.

(16)

or the sum of two terms:

\mu=\frac{\delta F_{id}(n)}{\delta n}+\frac{\delta F_{ex}(n)}{\delta n}.

(17)

To find the dynamical equation for XPFC model we consider the convolution integral of excess part of the XPFC free energy Eq. (
12
). This functional term
F_{ex}(n)
enters the functional derivative (Gateaux) for the dynamical PFC equation.
Let one introduce the test function
\theta({\bf r})
and a small
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
so the Gateaux variation for this convolution will be:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}F_{ex}(n\!+\!\epsilon\theta)\right|_{\epsilon=0}\!\!\!\!\!=

(18)

\displaystyle\!\!\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\!\int\!\!\!d{\bf r}\!\!\int\!\!(n({\bf r})\!+\!\epsilon\theta({\bf r})){C}({\bf r}\!-\!{\bf r}^{\prime})(n({\bf r}^{\prime})\!\!+\!\epsilon\theta({\bf r}^{\prime}))d{{\bf r}^{\prime}}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\!\!\!\!\!=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\!\int\!\!d{\bf r}\int\!\!d{{\bf r}^{\prime}}{C}({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})\times

(19)

\displaystyle\times(n({\bf r})\epsilon\theta({\bf r}^{\prime})+n({\bf r}^{\prime})\epsilon\theta({\bf r})+\epsilon\theta({\bf r})\epsilon\theta({\bf r}^{\prime}))\Big|_{\epsilon=0}.

Here term
\epsilon\theta({\bf r})\epsilon\theta({\bf r}^{\prime})
is neglected by the order of magnitude as a small parameter. Resulted functional derivative is

\frac{\delta F_{ex}(n)}{\delta n}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int\!\!d{{\bf r}}n({\bf r}){C}({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})+\!\int\!\!d{{\bf r}^{\prime}}{C}({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})n({\bf r}^{\prime})\right),

(20)

which allows one to describe the dynamics in the structural-PFC model for the arbitrary pair correlation function.
Considering the isotropic correlation function this convolution becomes:

\frac{\delta F_{ex}(n)}{\delta n}=-\int\!\!d{{\bf{r}}^{\prime}}{C}||{\bf{r}}-{\bf{r}}^{\prime}||n({\bf{r}}^{\prime}),

(21)

where
{C}_{2}||{\bf{r}}-{\bf{r}}^{\prime}||
is the arbitrary pair correlation function in direct space. This function can be obtained in reciprocal space using the convolution theorem
Seymour2016
;
Elder2018
. The fitting of this function in reciprocal space is possible thith rational function fitting procedure
Pisutha-Arnond2013
. Also for calculation in direct space one can use the similar approximation with help of expansion of this convolution integral into two complex parts. In case of analytical form of
C_{2}
in direct space one can introduce the direct convolution integration to the numerical procedure presented in the next section.
Resulted XPFC chemical potential after substitution of the functional derivative of
F_{ex}
Eq. (
21
) to Eq. (
17
) will be:

\mu(n)=(1-\varepsilon)n({\bf r})+an({\bf r})^{2}+vn({\bf r})^{3}-\int\!\!d{{\bf r}^{\prime}}{C}_{2}({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})n({\bf r}^{\prime}).

(22)

VI
Conclusions

In present work we introduce the modification of the structural phase-field crystal (XPFC) model for Lennard-Jones pair interaction potential. The pair interactions is approximated using the second order term of the virial expansion and thus with the analytical form of the pair correlation function. We studied the presented model with simple LJ potential in one- and two-dimensional cases. The numerical implementation is also presented and discussed. The crystallization from the undercooled homogeneous liquid to the solid crystalline phase was carried out. In one-dimensional case the equilibrium lattice parameters were calculated and compared to the LJ-distance parameters and numerical solutions. The two-dimensional numerical simulations was performed and resulted liquid, triangle, striped phases and their mixtures.
With the obtained numerical data the lattice parameters for each structure was calculated.
The lattice parameter’s values of the two-dimensional triangle and one-dimensional periodical structure are exactly matched to each other and to the correspondent LJ-distance parameters.
The set of obtained numerically structures and the phase diagram by itself qualitatively coincide to the classical PFC model, the form of the melting region is consistent with the high-temperature region of two-dimensional LJ diagram.

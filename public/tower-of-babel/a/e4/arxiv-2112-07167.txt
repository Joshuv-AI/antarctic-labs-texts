Moderate deviation expansion for fully quantum tasks
Navneeth Ramakrishnan, Marco Tomamichel, Mario Berta

Abstract
The moderate deviation regime is concerned with the finite block length trade-off between communication cost and error for information processing tasks in the asymptotic regime, where the communication cost approaches a capacity-like quantity and the error vanishes at the same time. We find exact characterisations of these trade-offs for a variety of fully quantum communication tasks, including quantum source coding, quantum state splitting, entanglement-assisted quantum channel coding, and entanglement-assisted quantum channel simulation. The main technical tool we derive is a tight relation between the partially smoothed max-information and the hypothesis testing relative entropy. This allows us to obtain the expansion of the partially smoothed max-information for i.i.d. states in the moderate deviation regime.



I
Overview

Fundamental tasks in information theory are often characterized in the asymptotic setting: One considers i.i.d. copies of a resource such as a channel or a state, takes the limit of the number of copies to infinity, and then enforces that the error vanishes in the asymptotic limit to obtain an optimal coding rate
shannon1948mathematical
. While such results are fundamental theoretical bounds, practical settings involve only finitely many copies of the resource — the so-called finite block length setting. It is therefore of interest to also understand the quantitative trade-off between the error and the rate for a given block length.

In the moderate deviation setting one obtains a rate and an error in terms of the block length
n
, such that the rate approaches a capacity-like quantity and the error vanishes as the code length
n
increases. In contrast, second-order (small deviation) analyses
strassen1962asymptotische
;
li2014second
;
tomamichel2013hierarchy
;
hayashi2009information
;
polyanskiy2010channel
result in a rate that approaches the capacity but the error is only bounded by some constant. As for error exponent (large deviation) analyses
csiszar1971error
;
han1989strong
;
arimoto1973converse
;
dueck1979reliability
;
mosonyi2017strong
, these result in an error that vanishes exponentially with
n
but the difference between the rate and the capacity is a constant term. The moderate deviation regime is natural to consider since it achieves both these goals simultaneously and several classical information-theoretic tasks have been analysed in this setting. Classical channel coding has been studied in
altuug2014moderate
;
polyanskiy2010channel
, while asymmetric binary hypothesis testing has been studied in
sason2012moderate
. Moderate deviation analyses for several classical-quantum coding tasks and quantum hypothesis testing are found in
rouze2017finite
;
cheng2017moderate
;
chubb2017moderate
;

. Other settings such as majorization-based resource interconversion have also been explored
chubb2019moderate

, quantum soft covering

;

For the first case we have that for sufficiently large

\displaystyle b_{n}=\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}-\frac{\log k}{n}}=a_{n}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{\log k}{na_{n}^{2}}}\right)\leq a_{n}\left(1-\frac{\log k}{2na_{n}^{2}}\right)\leq a_{n}+\eta a_{n}\,,

(30)

where the first inequality uses
\sqrt{1-x}\leq 1-\frac{x}{2}
for
x\leq 1
and the second inequality uses the fact that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}na_{n}^{2}=\infty
. The second case follows the same argument except that we replace the constant
k
with
\text{poly}(n)
and note that for strictly moderate sequences,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log n^{r}}{na_{n}^{2}}=0
for any constant
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

In the following, the main idea is to obtain an expansion for the partially smoothed max-information of i.i.d. states. This is done by bounding the partially smoothed max-information with the hypothesis testing relative entropy and then using the expansion of the hypothesis testing relative entropy in the moderate deviation regime. Our main result in this section is the following characterization.

Theorem III.6
(State splitting moderate deviation)

The minimal quantum communication cost of
\varepsilon_{n}
-error state splitting of
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
for
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(AB)
with
\varepsilon_{n}=e^{-na^{2}_{n}}
for a moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}
has the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}q^{\star}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}I(R:B)_{\rho}+a_{n}\sqrt{V(R:B)_{\rho}}+o(a_{n})\,,

(31)

where
\rho_{ABR}
is any purification of
\rho_{AB}
.

The proof is developed in the following subsections.

III.3
Technical tools

As the first step for the proof of Theorem
III.6
, we clarify when the tighter triangle inequality for the purified distance
(
tomamichel2015quantum
, Proposition 3.16)
holds. The following lemmas may be of independent interest since this tighter triangle inequality yields tight bounds between the smoothed min-relative entropy and the smoothed max-relative entropy, as well as between the smoothed min-relative entropy and the hypothesis testing relative entropy.

Lemma III.7
(Tight triangle inequality purified distance)

For
\rho,\sigma,\tau\in\mathcal{S}(A)
we have

\displaystyle P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}+P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}\leq 1\quad\Rightarrow\quad P(\rho,\tau)\leq P(\rho,\sigma)F(\sigma,\tau)+P(\sigma,\tau)F(\rho,\sigma)\,.

(32)

Proof.
The proof of
(
tomamichel2015quantum
, Proposition 3.16)
shows that Eq. (
32
) holds when
\sin^{-1}(P(\rho,\sigma))+\sin^{-1}(P(\sigma,\tau))\leq\pi/2
. We show that this condition is equivalent to
P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}+P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}\leq 1
. By the monotonicity of the cosine function on
[0,\pi]
, we have

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}(P(\rho,\sigma))+\sin^{-1}(P(\sigma,\tau))\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\cos(\sin^{-1}(P(\rho,\sigma))+\sin^{-1}(P(\sigma,\tau)))\geq\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\cos(\sin^{-1}(P(\rho,\sigma)))\cos(\sin^{-1}(P(\sigma,\tau)))-\sin(\sin^{-1}(P(\rho,\sigma)))\sin(\sin^{-1}(P(\sigma,\tau)))\geq 0

(35)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}}\sqrt{1-P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}}-P(\rho,\sigma)P(\sigma,\tau)\geq 0

(36)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}+P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}\leq 1\,,

(37)

where the third line follows by using the addition formula for cosine and the fourth line uses that
\cos^{2}(\theta)=1-\sin^{2}(\theta)
.

The following lemma was already known for
\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}=I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{S}(AB)

(
tomamichel2012framework
, Remark 5.6)
but we get it for general quantum states.

Lemma III.8

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(A)
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(A)
, and
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in[0,1]
with
\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime 2}\leq 1
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq D^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log\left(1-\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{\prime 2}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(38)

Proof.
Let
\lambda=D^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{\max}(\tilde{\rho}\|\sigma)
for
\tilde{\rho}\approx_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\rho
, it then holds that
\tilde{\rho}\leq 2^{\lambda}\sigma
. For some
\bar{\rho}\approx_{\varepsilon}\rho
we have that

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{\min}(\bar{\rho}\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\log F(\bar{\rho},\sigma)^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq-\log F(\bar{\rho},2^{-\lambda}\tilde{\rho})^{2}

(40)

\displaystyle=\lambda-\log F(\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho})^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle=D^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log(1-P(\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho})^{2})

(42)

\displaystyle\leq D^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log\left(1-\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{\prime 2}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(43)

where the last step is via the tighter triangle inequality for the purified distance (Lemma
III.7
) and a basic monotonicity argument (Lemma
B.1
).

Note that the constraint on the choices of
(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})
is less stringent compared to
(
tomamichel2012framework
, Remark 5.6)
, which is due to the tighter triangle inequality. Next, we have the following upper bond on the min-relative entropy in terms of the hypothesis testing relative entropy.

Lemma III.9

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(A)
and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,k^{-1/2}\right]
for
k>1
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{k\varepsilon^{2}}_{h}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log\left(1-\left(\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(44)

Proof.
We choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}}
in the preceeding Lemma
III.8
to obtain

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log f(\varepsilon)\quad\text{for}\quad f(\varepsilon)=\log\left(1-\left(\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(45)

By a connection of the smooth max-relative entropy to the hypothesis testing relative entropy (Lemma
B.8
), we have for
\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle D^{\sqrt{\delta}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{h}^{1-\delta}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log(1-\delta)\,.

(46)

Combining the two inequalities, we get

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D^{\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}}}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log f(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq D_{h}^{1-(\sqrt{1-k\varepsilon^{2}})^{2}}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log f(\varepsilon)-\log k\varepsilon^{2}

(47)

\displaystyle\leq D_{h}^{k\varepsilon^{2}}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log f(\varepsilon)-\log k\varepsilon^{2}\,.

(48)

Finally, we recall the moderate deviation expansion for the hypothesis testing relative entropy of i.i.d. states.

Proposition III.10
(Hypothesis testing AEP

For any moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}

\varepsilon_{n}=e^{-a_{n}^{2}n}

\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(A)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D_{\mathrm{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)-\sqrt{2V(\rho\|\sigma)}a_{n}+\eta_{1}a_{n}.

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}D_{\mathrm{h}}^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)+\sqrt{2V(\rho\|\sigma)}a_{n}+\eta_{2}a_{n}\,.

(50)

Proof of Theorem

Proposition III.11
(Partially smoothed max-information AEP)

Let
\varepsilon_{n}=e^{-na^{2}_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq I(B:R)_{\rho}+a_{n}\sqrt{4V(B:R)_{\rho}}+\eta a_{n}\,.

Proof.
We start by showing the upper bound. For
\eta>0

n_{1}^{\star}

\displaystyle I^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{\max}(\dot{R}^{n};B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle\leq D^{\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{4}}_{\max}(\rho_{BR}^{\otimes n}\|\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}\otimes\rho_{R}^{\otimes n})+\log\frac{8+(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2})^{2}}{(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2})^{2}}

(53)

Quantum state merging can be understood as a time reversed version of a quantum state splitting

\rho_{ABR}
, where Alice holds the
A

B

IV
Quantum source coding

Here, we investigate the task of quantum source coding in the moderate deviation regime. There are two main settings for source coding, namely the ensemble and purification settings based on whether one uses the fidelity or entanglement fidelity to measure the success of source coding. The ensemble setting is further divided into the blind and visible settings, where the state to be compressed is either known or unknown. More precise definitions of these settings can be found in the literature
schumacher1995quantum
;
hayashi2009information
;
hayashi2002exponents
;
hayashi2002simple
. Here, we consider quantum source coding in the blind purification scheme, which is equivalent to quantum state splitting with a trivial
A
quantum register and no entanglement-assistance.

Definition IV.1
(Quantum source coding)

Let
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{S}(B)
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. A one-shot quantum source coding protocol consists of:

1.

Quantum registers Q and
A_{1}\cong B

2.

An encoding quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{A_{1}\rightarrow Q}

3.

A decoding quantum channel
\mathcal{D}_{Q\rightarrow B}
.

A
\{q,\varepsilon\}
-one-shot quantum source coding protocol of
\rho_{B}
is such that
q=\log|Q|
with

(\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{E})(\rho_{A_{1}R})\approx_{\varepsilon}\rho_{BR}
for any extension
\rho_{A_{1}R}
of
\rho_{A_{1}}
.

(67)

The minimal quantum communication cost of source coding of
\rho_{B}
is defined as

\displaystyle q^{\star}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{B})=\min\Big\{q\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\text{ a}\ \{q,\varepsilon\}\text{-one-shot quantum source coding protocol of }\rho_{B}\Big\}\,.

(68)

We start with the following lemma about the mutual information variance for pure states.

Lemma IV.2
(Pure state mutual information variance)

For
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(AB)
pure, the mutual information variance and the conditional entropy variance
V(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
take the form

\displaystyle V(A:B)_{\rho}=4V(A)_{\rho},\qquad V(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=V(A)_{\rho}\,,

(69)

respectively.

Proof.
From the definition of the relative entropy variance, we have

\displaystyle V(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}(\log\rho_{AB})^{2}\right)+\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{A}(\log\rho_{A})^{2}\right)+\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{B}(\log\rho_{B})^{2}\right)+2\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{A}\otimes\log\rho_{B}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{A}\right)-2\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{B}\right)-D(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})^{2}

(70)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{A}(\log\rho_{A})^{2}\right)+\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{B}(\log\rho_{B})^{2}\right)+2\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{A}\otimes\log\rho_{B}\right)-(S(A)_{\rho}+S(B)_{\rho})^{2}

(71)

\displaystyle=4V(A)_{\rho}\,,

(72)

where in the second equality we have removed various terms which are zero due to
\rho_{AB}
being pure, and in the third equality we have used that the marginals
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
share the same eigenvalues and that
\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{AB}\log\rho_{A}\otimes\log\rho_{B}\right)=\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{A}(\log\rho_{A})^{2}\right)
for pure
\rho_{AB}
. The last claim can be verified by expanding the left hand side in the Schmidt basis of
\rho_{AB}
. A similar argument shows the second equality in the lemma.

We now consider quantum source coding in the moderate deviation regime. Note that quantum source coding in the large deviation setting was explored in

;
hayashi2009information

16
of

(

. The minimal communication cost of quantum source coding in the low-error moderate deviation regime is characterized as follows.

Theorem IV.3
(Source coding moderate deviation)

The minimal quantum communication cost of one-shot
\varepsilon_{n}
-error source coding of
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
for
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{S}(B)
with
\varepsilon_{n}=e^{-na^{2}_{n}}
for a moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}q^{\star}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})=

\displaystyle S(B)_{\rho}+2a_{n}\sqrt{V(B)_{\rho}}+o(a_{n})\,.

(73)

Proof.
The converse statement follows as a corollary of our moderate deviation analysis of state splitting (Theorem
III.6
) and properties of the mutual information variance for pure states (Lemma
IV.2
). While a related achievability statement also follows from those results, this additionally assumes the presence of entanglement-assistance between the encoder and decoder which is not present in Definition
IV.1
. Thus, we use a different proof technique that does not require entanglement assistance. Namely, we employ the bounds shown in
datta2014second
;
abdelhadi2020second
, where the one-shot quantum source coding error is quantified in terms of the fidelity. We modify their result for our setting in purified distance. For
\varepsilon_{n}=e^{-a_{n}^{2}n}
for a moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}q^{\star}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\bar{H}_{s}^{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}_{n}}{2}}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{B})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\underline{D}_{s}^{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}_{n}}{2}}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{B}\|I_{B^{n}})

(74)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{n}D_{h}^{\varepsilon^{2}_{n}/4}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{B}\|I_{B^{n}})-\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\varepsilon^{2}_{n}}{4}

(75)

\displaystyle=-D(\rho_{B}\|I_{B})+a_{n}\sqrt{4V(\rho_{B}\|I_{B})}+o(a_{n})

(76)

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{BR}\|I_{B}\otimes\rho_{R})+a_{n}\sqrt{4V(\rho_{BR}\|I_{B}\otimes\rho_{R})}+o(a_{n})

(77)

\displaystyle=S(B)_{\rho}+2a_{n}\sqrt{V(B)_{\rho}}+o(a_{n})\,,

(78)

where the first inequality is due to the achievability bound in
(
datta2014second
, Theorem 5.5 (ii))
, the first equality is from the definition of the information spectrum entropy, the second inequality is from the bound between the hypothesis testing relative entropy and the information spectrum relative entropy (Lemma
B.7
), the second equality is due to the AEP for the hypothesis testing relative entropy (Proposition
III.10
) and because multiplicative factors on the error do not change the communication cost in the moderate deviation regime (Corollary
III.12
), and the third equality is through the duality of the relative entropy (Lemma
B.9
) and the final equality is due to properties of the mutual information variance for pure states (Lemma
IV.2
).

We note that the converse proof allows for entanglement-assistance. Hence, it follows that entanglement-assistance does not reduce the quantum communication cost of low-error moderate deviation quantum source coding.

In the large error regime, we set
\varepsilon_{n}=1-e^{-na_{n}^{2}}
for a moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}
. We find the following achievability result.

Proposition IV.4
(Source coding high-error moderate deviation)

The minimal quantum communication cost of one-shot
\varepsilon_{n}

for
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{S}(B)
with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}q^{\star}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leq S(B)_{\rho}-a_{n}\sqrt{V(B)_{\rho}}+o(a_{n})\,.

Proof.
We start with the achievability result. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}q^{\star}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\bar{H}_{s}^{1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{B})

V
Quantum channel simulation

Quantum state splitting is the underlying primitive to understand the task of quantum channel simulation. In this setting, Alice and Bob have access to pre-shared resource states and noiseless one-way communication which they will use to simulate i.i.d. copies of a noisy quantum channel. This problem is also known as the Quantum Reverse Shannon Theorem in the literature
bennett2009quantum
;
berta2011quantum
. We start with the measure we need to distinguish between two quantum channels. It is based on the channel fidelity
F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=\max_{\rho_{AR}}F((\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{R})(\rho_{AR}),(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{R})(\rho_{AR}))
, but we choose to work with the purified distance instead of the fidelity.

Definition V.1
(Channel purified distance)

For any pair of quantum channels
\mathcal{E}_{A\rightarrow B}
and
\mathcal{F}_{A\rightarrow B}
, the channel purified distance is defined as

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{\leq}(A\otimes R)}P\big((\mathcal{E}_{A\to B}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{R})(\rho_{AR}),(\mathcal{F}_{A\to B}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{R})(\rho_{AR})\big)

(88)

where the supremum is over quantum registers
R

We now comment on a few properties that allow us to simplify Definition
V.1

without loss of generality, as shown in Lemma
B.2
. The supremum over
\rho
can then be replaced by a maximum since the set of subnormalized quantum states is closed and bounded for fixed

B.3
, we have that normalized states will achieve the maximum. Next, since the purified distance is non-increasing under partial trace, we may choose
\rho_{AR}
to be pure. Finally, we remark that the channel purified distance is a metric since it inherits this property from the purified distance.

Here, we characterize the cost of one-shot entanglement-assisted channel coding in the high-error moderate deviation regime. We consider entanglement-assisted quantum communication in this section. However, the results of this section apply to entanglement-assisted classical communication as well. This is because superdense coding and teleportation establish and equivalence between these two tasks when Alice and Bob have access to shared entanglement.

Our analysis of this task relies on the close connection between high-error channel coding and low-error channel simulation. In the classical setting, this connection was explored in

VII
Conclusion

In summary, we have resolved the quantum communication cost of several quantum information processing tasks in the moderate deviation regime. The task of quantum state splitting emerged as the fundamental primitive and is closely connected with quantum source coding. Our quantum state splitting results in the low-error moderate deviation regime are then used to characterize quantum channel simulation in the low-error strictly moderate deviation regime. In turn, quantum channel simulation is used to obtain a tight bound for entanglement-assisted quantum channel coding rates in the high-error strict moderate deviation regime. This extends a result that was previously only available for a restricted class of channels (as reviewed in Appendix
C
). It would be interesting to explore if the findings in
gupta2015multiplicativity
based on sandwiched Rényi entropies could lead to similar moderate deviation results.

Several interesting problems remain open. Our proof technique for obtaining the AEP for the partially smoothed max-information (Proposition
III.11
) does not work for the large error moderate deviation regime, when the error goes as
1-e^{-na_{n}^{2}}
for a moderate sequence
\{a_{n}\}
. The missing technical ingredient appears to be a tighter bound on the partially smoothed max-information with the smoothed max-information (Lemma
B.11
). Hence, the problem of characterizing quantum state splitting in the high-error regime is open. In the case of quantum source coding, one can obtain a converse in the high-error regime using our quantum state splitting converse but this does not match the achievability statement in Proposition
IV.4
that we conjecture to be tight. As such, that problem also remains open. Finally, the polynomial multiplicative factor on the error in the de Finetti reduction (Proposition
B.14
) is another obstacle that prevents us from characterizing channel simulation in the high-error strictly moderate deviation regime. It would be interesting to find an achievability proof for channel simulation without using state splitting and de Finetti reductions. This would then also yield a tighter converse bound for channel coding in the low-error strictly moderate deviation regime.

Lastly, we remark that our quantum state splitting result does not resolve the second-order regime, where the error is a constant. This is due to a gap in the achievability and converse results in the AEP for the partially smoothed max-information (Proposition
III.11
). The technical ingredient required again appears to be a tighter relationship between the partially smoothed max-information and the smoothed max-information. In the moderate deviation analysis, this gap closes due to the fact that constant (
\text{poly}(n)
) multiplicative factors on the error do not affect the cost of the (strict) moderate deviation analysis as explained in Lemma
III.5
. However, that no longer holds for the second-order regime.

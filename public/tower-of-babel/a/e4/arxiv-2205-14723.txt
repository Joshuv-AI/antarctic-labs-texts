Global Solutions to the Tangential Peskin Problem in 2-D
Jiajun Tong

Abstract
. We introduce and study the tangential Peskin problem in 2-D, which is a scalar drift-diffusion equation with a nonlocal drift. It is derived with a new Eulerian perspective from a special setting of the 2-D Peskin problem where an infinitely long and straight 1-D elastic string deforms tangentially in the Stokes flow induced by itself in the plane. For initial datum in the energy class satisfying natural weak assumptions, we prove existence of its global solutions. This is considered as a super-critical problem in the existing analysis of the Peskin problem based on Lagrangian formulations. Regularity and long-time behavior of the constructed solution is established. Uniqueness of the solution is proved under additional assumptions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem formulation and main results

Consider the following scalar drift-diffusion equation on
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})=[-\pi,\pi)
,

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f=\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f,\quad f(x,0)=f_{0}(x).

Here
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform on
\mathbb{T}
,

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}f(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(y)}{2\tan\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dy,

and

(1.3)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dy=\mathcal{H}f^{\prime}(x).

In the paper, we want to study positive global solutions
f=f(x,t)
to (
1.1
) under rather weak assumptions on the initial data.

In Section
2
of the paper, we will show that the drift-diffusion equation (
1.1
) arises as a scalar model of the 2-D Peskin problem.
In general, the 2-D Peskin problem, also known as the 2-D Stokes immersed boundary problem in the literature, describes coupled motion of a 2-D Stokes flow and 1-D closed elastic string immersed in it
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Its mathematical formulation and related analytical works will be reviewed in Section
2.1
.
The equation (
1.1
) stems from a special setting of it, where an infinitely long and straight elastic string deforms and moves only
tangentially
in a Stokes flow in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
induced by itself.
If we assume the string lies along the
x
-axis, then
f=f(x,t)
in (
1.1
) represents how much the string segment at the spatial point
(x,0)
gets stretched in the horizontal direction (see (
2.9
)).
Thus, we shall call (
1.1
) the
tangential Peskin problem
in 2-D.
Analysis of such simplified models may improve our understanding of well-posedness and possible blow-up mechanism of the original 2-D Peskin problem. See Section
2.2
for more detailed discussions.

Suppose
f
is a positive smooth solution to (
1.1
).
It is straightforward to find that
F:=\frac{1}{f}
verifies a conservation law

(1.4)

\partial_{t}F=\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\big),\quad F(x,0)=F_{0}(x):=\big(f_{0}(x)\big)^{-1}.

This leads to the following definition of (positive) weak solutions to (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1

(Weak solution)
.

Let
f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
such that
f_{0}>0
almost everywhere, and
F_{0}:=\frac{1}{f_{0}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
For
T\in[0,+\infty]
, we say that
f=f(x,t)
is a (positive) weak solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
, if the followings hold.

(a)

f>0
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
, and
F\in L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))
, where
F=\frac{1}{f}
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
;

(b)

\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
;

(c)

For any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
,

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(x,0)F_{0}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{t}\varphi\cdot F\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt.

In this case, we also say
F
is a (positive) weak solution to (
1.4
).

If
T=+\infty
, the weak solution is said to be global.

Remark 1.1
.

Given (
1.1
), it is tempting to define the weak solutions by requiring

\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(x,0)f_{0}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{t}\varphi\cdot f\,dx\,dt\\
=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot f\,dx\,dt+2\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\varphi f)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}f\,dx\,dt\end{split}

to hold for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
.
Although this formulation imposes no regularity assumptions on
1/f
, it does not effectively relocate the derivative to the test function, so we choose not to work with it.

We introduce the
H^{s}
-semi-norms for
s>0
,

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k|^{2s}|\hat{f}_{k}|^{2},

where the Fourier transform of a function
f
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
is defined by

(1.6)

\mathcal{F}(f)_{k}=\hat{f}_{k}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)e^{-ikx}\,dx.

Our first result is on the existence of a global weak solution with nice properties.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
satisfies
f_{0}>0
almost everywhere, with
F_{0}:=\frac{1}{f_{0}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then (
1.1
) admits a global weak solution
f=f(x,t)
in the sense of Definition
1.1
.
It has the following properties.

(1)

(Energy relation)
f\in L^{\infty}([0,+\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{2}([0,+\infty);\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T}))
.
For any
t\geq 0
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},

while for any
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

\|f(\cdot,t_{2})\|_{L^{1}}+2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau=\|f(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{1}}.

(2)

(Instant positivity and smoothness) For any
t_{0}>0
, the solution
f
is
C^{\infty}
and positive in
\mathbb{T}\times[t_{0},+\infty)
, so
f
is a positive strong solution to (
1.1
) for all positive times (cf. Definition
3.1
).
To be more precise, define
F=\frac{1}{f}
pointwise on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty)
.
Then
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
are non-increasing in
t\in(0,+\infty)
.
They satisfy that

(1.7)

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\forall\,t\in(0,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}],

where
C
is a universal constant, and that

(1.8)

\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\exp\left[\coth\left(\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right]\quad\forall\,t>0.

Moreover, for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5})
,
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\in L^{1}([0,+\infty))
, satisfying that, for
t\in(0,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}]
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\,d\tau\leq C_{\alpha}t^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2}}\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}-2\pi f_{\infty}\big)^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2\alpha},

where
C_{\alpha}>0
is a universal constant depending on
\alpha
, and where
f_{\infty}
is defined in (
1.9
) below.

(3)

(Conservation law)

\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
for all
t\geq 0
, and the map
t\mapsto F(\cdot,t)/\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
is continuous from
[0,+\infty)
to
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Here
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{T})
denotes the space of probability densities on
\mathbb{T}
equipped with
1
-Wasserstein distance
W_{1}
(see e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 6]
).

(4)

(Long-time exponential convergence)
Define

(1.9)

f_{\infty}:=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{f_{0}(x)}\,dx\right)^{-1}.

Then for arbitrary
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\|\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{k}(f(\cdot,t)-f_{\infty})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
is finite for any
t>0
, and it decays to
0
exponentially as
t\to+\infty
.
It admits an upper bound that only depends on
t
,
j
,
k
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.
See Proposition
5.2
.

(5)

(Large-time analyticity and sharp decay) There exists
T_{*}>0
depending only on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, such that
f(x,t)
is analytic in the space-time domain
\mathbb{T}\times(T_{*},+\infty)
.
In addition, for any
t>0
,

\|f(\cdot,t+T_{*})\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\leq Cf_{\infty},

with

\nu(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[1+\exp(2f_{\infty}t)\right]-\frac{1}{2}\ln 2,

and
C>0
being a universal constant.
Here the
\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}_{\nu(t)}
semi-norms for Wiener-algebra-type spaces will be defined in Section
7
.

Remark 1.2
.

We remark that
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
is considered to be the energy class for the initial data
f_{0}
(see Remark
3.1
), while the condition
F_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
is natural given (
1.4
) (also see Remark
2.2
).
If in addition
F_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
for some
p>1
, the energy equality holds up to
t=0
for the constructed solution, i.e., for any
t\geq 0
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Note that we do not claim the above properties for all weak solutions, but only for the constructed one.
Regularity of general weak solutions to (
1.1
) is wildly open.

It will be clear in Section
2
that the equation (
1.1
) and Theorem
1.1
are formulated with a Eulerian perspective.
In Corollary
2.1
in Section
2.2
, we will recast these results in the corresponding Lagrangian coordinate, which is more commonly used in the analysis of the Peskin problem.
Roughly speaking, in the Lagrangian framework, for arbitrary initial data in the energy class satisfying some weak assumptions but no restriction on its size, we are able to well define a global solution to the 2-D tangential Peskin problem with nice properties.
Let us note that, in the existing studies on the Peskin problem with the Lagrangian perspectives, this is considered as a super-critical problem. See more discussions in Section
2
.

We also would like to highlight the following very interesting property of (
1.1
), which is helpful
when proving Theorem
1.1
.
It states that band-limited initial datum give rise to band-limited solutions, with the band width being uniformly bounded in time.
This allows us to easily construct global solutions from such initial datum.
This property is found by taking the Fourier transform of (
1.1
).
We will justify it in Section
6.1
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose
f_{0}>0
is band-limited, namely, there exists some
K\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\mathcal{F}(f_{0})_{k}=0
for all
|k|>K
.
Then (
1.1
) has a global strong solution
f=f(x,t)
that is also positive and band-limited, such that
\hat{f}_{k}(t)=0
for all
|k|>K
and
t\geq 0
.
Such a solution is unique, and it can be determined by solving a finite family of ODEs of its Fourier coefficients.
In fact, we let
\hat{f}_{k}(t)\equiv 0
for all
|k|>K
.
Denote
\bar{f}(t):=\mathcal{F}(f(\cdot,t))_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,t)\,dx
.
Then for
k\in[0,K]
,
\hat{f}_{k}(t)=\mathcal{F}(f(\cdot,t))_{k}
solves

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{k}(t)=-k\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{k}(t)-\sum_{j>0}2(k+2j)\hat{f}_{k+j}(t)\overline{\hat{f}_{j}(t)},\quad\hat{f}_{k}(0)=\mathcal{F}(f_{0})_{k},

where
\hat{f}_{0}(t)
should be understood as
\bar{f}(t)
.
Finally, let
\hat{f}_{k}(t)=\overline{\hat{f}_{-k}(t)}
for
k\in[-K,0)
.

Uniqueness of the weak solution to (
1.1
) is also an open question given the very weak assumptions on the initial data and the solution.
To achieve positive results on the uniqueness, we introduce a new notion of weak solutions.
We inherit the notations from Definition
1.1
.

Definition 1.2

(Dissipative weak solution)
.

Under the additional assumption
\ln f_{0}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
,
a weak solution
f=f(x,t)
to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
(in the sense of Definition
1.1
) is said to be a dissipative weak solution, if it additionally verifies

(a)

\ln f\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T}))
and
\partial_{x}\sqrt{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
, satisfying that (cf. Lemma
5.2
)

\|\ln f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\partial_{x}\sqrt{f}\big)^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\|\ln f_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

for any
t\in[0,T)
.

(b)

For any convex function
\Phi\in C^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty))
such that
\Phi(F_{0}(x))\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, it holds

(1.10)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(F(x,t))\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\Phi(F)-F\Phi^{\prime}(F)\big)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx\,dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(F_{0}(x))\,dx

for all
t\in[0,T)
(cf. Remark
6.1
).

Then we state uniqueness of the dissipative weak solutions under additional assumptions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\ln f_{0}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
.
Further assume
f_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
f_{0}\geq\lambda
almost everywhere with some
\lambda>0
.
Then (
1.1
) admits a unique dissipative weak solution.

In particular, the solution coincides with the one constructed in Theorem
1.1
, and thus it satisfies all the properties there.

1.2.
Related studies

The equation (
1.1
) is derived from a special setting of the 2-D Peskin problem, but we choose to review related works in that direction in Section
2.1
.
Beyond that, (
1.1
) is reminiscent of many classic PDEs arising in fluid dynamics and other subjects.
We list a few, but we note that the list of equations and literature below is by no means exhaustive.

(I)

In
[
6
]
, Córdoba, Córdoba, and Fontelos (CCF) studied

\partial_{t}\theta-\mathcal{H}\theta\cdot\partial_{x}\theta=-\nu(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta

on
\mathbb{R}
.
They showed finite-time blow-up for the inviscid case
\nu=0
, and yet well-posedness when
\nu>0
and
\alpha\geq 1
, with smallness of initial data assumed for the critical case
\alpha=1
.
See
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
for related results.

When
\nu>0
, the CCF model is known as a 1-D analogue of the dissipative quasi-geostrophic equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta,\quad u=(-\mathcal{R}_{2}\theta,\mathcal{R}_{1}\theta).

Here
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
denote the Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The case
\alpha=1
is again critical, for which global well-posedness results are established in e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
; also see
[
14
]
.

Compared with the CCF model with
\nu>0
and
\alpha=1
, our equation (
1.1
) features a fractional diffusion with variable coefficient.
The diffusion is enhanced when
f
is large, while it becomes degenerate when
f
is almost zero.
The latter turns out to be one of the main difficulties in the analysis of (
1.1
).
See Lemma
4.1
and Section
5.1
for how we handle such possible degeneracy.

(II)

The equation

(1.11)

\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\big(\rho\mathcal{H}\rho\big)=0,

or equivalently (at least for nice solutions
\rho=\rho(x,t)
),

(1.12)

\partial_{t}\rho=-\partial_{x}\rho\cdot\mathcal{H}\rho-\rho(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho,

has been studied extensively in several different contexts, such as fluid dynamics
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, and dislocation theory
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
Interestingly, (
1.11
) has similarities with both (
1.1
) and (
1.4
).
Indeed, (
1.12
) differs from (
1.1
) only in the sign of the drift term.
On the other hand, both (
1.4
) and (
1.11
) are conservation laws;
the transporting velocity in (
1.11
) is
\mathcal{H}\rho
, while it is
-\mathcal{H}(\frac{1}{F})
in (
1.4
).
It is not immediately clear how such differences lead to distinct features of these equations as well as different difficulties in their analysis, but apparently, it is less straightforward to propose and study the weak formulation of (
1.1
) than that of (
1.11
).

A generalization of (
1.11
) reads

\partial_{t}\rho+\theta\partial_{x}\big(\rho\mathcal{H}\rho\big)+(1-\theta)\mathcal{H}\rho\cdot\partial_{x}\rho=0

where
\theta\in[0,1]
is a constant
[
23
]
.
Formally, (
1.1
) corresponds to the case
\theta=-1
, up to a change of variable
\rho=-f
, which is not covered by existing works to the best of our knowledge.

Another generalization of (
1.11
) is

\partial_{t}\rho-\mathrm{div}\,\big(\rho\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}\rho\big)=0.

The case
s=1
arises in the hydrodynamics of vortices in the
Ginzburg-Landau theory
[
24
]
; its well-posedness was proved in
[
25
]
.
When
s\in(0,1)
, it was introduced as a nonlinear porous medium equation
with fractional potential pressure
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
. Its well-posedness, long-time asymptotics, regularity of the solutions, as well as other properties has been investigated.
If we set the equation in 1-D and denote
\alpha=2(1-s)
, then it can be written as

\partial_{t}\rho+\big(\partial_{x}^{-1}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho\big)\partial_{x}\rho=-\rho(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho.

This turns out to be a special case of the 1-D Euler alignment system for studying flocking dynamics
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
Clearly, (
1.11
) corresponds to the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
, or equivalently,
\alpha=1
in 1-D.

(III)

It was derived in
[
34
]
that the dynamics of real roots of a high-degree polynomial under continuous differentiation can be described by

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\left(\arctan\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}u}{u}\right)\right)=0,

or equivalently (at least for nice solutions
u=u(x,t)
),

(1.13)

\partial_{t}u=\frac{\mathcal{H}u\cdot\partial_{x}u-u(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u}{u^{2}+(\mathcal{H}u)^{2}}.

Also see
[

,

]
.
Recently, its well-posedness was studied in

Although (

) if one ignores the denominator of the right-hand side, its solutions can behave differently from those to (

) because of very different form of nonlinearity and degeneracy.
Even when
u
is sufficiently close to a constant
\bar{u}
,

]
formally derives that

should solve

\partial_{t}v=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}v+\partial_{x}(v\mathcal{H}v)

up to suitable scaling factors and higher-order errors.
This equation becomes (
1.11
) formally after a change of variable
\rho=1-v
.

(IV)

Letting

, we may rewrite (

\partial_{t}\omega+u\partial_{x}\omega=\omega\partial_{x}u,\quad u=\mathcal{H}\omega.

\partial_{t}\omega+u\partial_{x}\omega=\omega\partial_{x}u,\quad u_{x}=\mathcal{H}\omega.

Its analytic properties are quite different from (

[

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein.
Nevertheless, we refer the readers to Lemma

2.
The Tangential Peskin Problem in 2-D

2.1.
The 2-D Peskin problem

The 2-D Peskin problem, also known as the Stokes immersed boundary problem in 2-D, describes a 1-D closed elastic string immersed and moving in a 2-D Stokes flow induced by itself.
Let the string be parameterized by
X=X(s,t)
, where
s\in\mathbb{T}
is the Lagrangian coordinate (as opposed to the arclength parameter).
Then the 2-D Peskin problem is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\;-\Delta u+\nabla p=\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{X}(s,t)\delta(x-X(s,t))\,ds,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{div}\,u=0,\quad|u|,|p|\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\;\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}(s,t)=u(X(s,t),t),\quad X(s,0)=X_{0}(s).

Here
u=u(x,t)
and
p=p(x,t)
denote the flow field and pressure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, respectively.
The right-hand side of (
2.1
)
is the singular elastic force applied to the fluid that is only supported along the string.
F_{X}
is the elastic force density in the Lagrangian coordinate, determined by the string configuration
X
.
In general, it is given by
[
50
]

F_{X}(s,t)=\partial_{s}\left(\mathcal{T}(|X^{\prime}(s,t)|,s,t)\frac{X^{\prime}(s,t)}{|X^{\prime}(s,t)|}\right).

Here and in what follows, we write
\partial_{s}X(s,t)
as
X^{\prime}(s,t)
for brevity.

\mathcal{T}
denotes the tension along the string.
In the simple case of Hookean elasticity,
\mathcal{T}(|X^{\prime}(s,t)|,s,t)=k_{0}|X^{\prime}(s,t)|
, where
k_{0}>0
is the Hooke’s constant, and thus
F_{X}(s,t)=k_{0}X^{\prime\prime}(s,t)
.

The Peskin problem is closely related to the well-known numerical immersed boundary method
[
51
,
50
]
.
As a model problem of fluid-structure interaction, it has been extensively studied and applied in numerical analysis for decades.
Nonetheless, analytical studies of it started only recently.
In the independent works
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
,
the authors first studied well-posedness of the problem with
F_{X}=X^{\prime\prime}(s,t)
.
Using the 2-D Stokeslet

(2.4)

G_{ij}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(-\ln|x|\delta_{ij}+\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}\right),\quad i,j=1,2,

the problem was reformulated into a contour dynamic equation in the Lagrangian coordinate

(2.5)

\partial_{t}X(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(X(s,t)-X(s^{\prime},t))X^{\prime\prime}(s^{\prime},t)\,ds^{\prime}.

Based on this, well-posedness results of the string evolution were proved in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
in subcritical spaces
H^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
and
C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, respectively, under the so called
well-stretched condition
on the initial configuration —
a string configuration
Y(s)
is said to satisfy the well-stretched condition with constant
\lambda>0
, if for any
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
,

(2.6)

\big|Y(s_{1})-Y(s_{2})\big|\geq\lambda|s_{1}-s_{2}|.

It is worthwhile to mention that
[
2
]
also established a blow-up criterion, stating that if a solution fails at a finite time, then either
\|X(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1,\alpha}}
goes to infinity, or the best well-stretched constant shrinks to zero.

Since then, many efforts have been made to establish well-posedness under the scaling-critical regularity — in this problem, it refers to
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and other regularity classes with the same scaling.
García-Juárez, Mori, and Strain considered the case where the fluids in the interior and the exterior of the string can have different viscosities, and they showed global well-posedness for initial data of medium size in the critical Wiener space
\mathcal{F}^{1,1}(\mathbb{T})

[
52
]
.
In
[
53
]
, Gancedo, Granero-Belinchón, and Scrobogna introduced a toy scalar model that captures the string motion in the normal direction only, for which they proved global well-posedness for small initial data in the critical Lipschitz class
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Recently, Chen and Nguyen established well-posedness of the original 2-D Peskin problem in the critical space
\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{1}(\mathbb{T})

[
54
]
.
However, in the Hookean case, the total energy of the system is
\frac{1}{2}\|X(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}
.
Hence, the Cauchy problem with the initial data belonging to the energy class is considered to be super-critical.

In addition to these studies, the author introduced a regularized Peskin problem in
[
55
]
, and proved its well-posedness and its convergence to the original singular problem as the regularization diminishes.
Aiming at handling general elasticity laws beyond the Hookean elasticity,
[
3
]
and the very recent work
[
56
]
extended the analysis to a fully nonlinear Peskin problem.
Readers are also referred to
[
57
]
for a study on the case where the elastic string has both stretching and bending energy.

2.2.
Derivation of the tangential Peskin problem in 2-D

In this subsection, we will present a formal derivation of the tangential Peskin problem in 2-D, without paying too much attention to regularity and integrability issues.
It is not the main point of this paper to rigorously justify the derivation, although it clearly holds for nice solutions.

Consider an infinitely long and straight Hookean elastic string lying along the
x
-axis, which admits tangential deformation in the horizontal direction only.
By (
2.5
), this feature gets preserved for all time in the flow induced by itself.
With abuse of notations, we still use
X(s,t)
to denote the actual physical position (i.e., the
x
-coordinate) of the string point with the Lagrangian label
s\in\mathbb{R}
; note that
X(\cdot,t)
is now a scalar function.
Assume
X:s\mapsto X(s,t)
to be suitably smooth and strictly increasing, so that it is a bijection from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

Under the assumption of no transversal deformation, (
2.5
) reduces to (thanks to (
2.4
))

(2.7)

\partial_{t}X(s,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(-\ln|X(s,t)-X(s^{\prime},t)|+1\right)X^{\prime\prime}(s^{\prime},t)\,ds^{\prime}.

By integration by parts,

(2.8)

\partial_{t}X(s)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{X^{\prime}(s^{\prime})^{2}}{X(s)-X(s^{\prime})}\,ds^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{X^{\prime}(s^{\prime})}{X(s)-X(s^{\prime})}\,dX(s^{\prime}).

Here we omitted the
t
-dependence for conciseness.

We define
f=f(x,t)
such that

(2.9)

f(X(s,t),t)=X^{\prime}(s,t).

Physically,
f(x,t)
represents the extent of local stretching of the elastic string at the spatial position
x
.
Then (
2.8
) becomes

(2.10)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}X(s,t)=&\;-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(X(s^{\prime},t),t)}{X(s,t)-X(s^{\prime},t)}\,dX(s^{\prime},t)\\
=&\;-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y,t)}{X(s,t)-y}\,dy=-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{H}f(\cdot,t)\big|_{x=X(s,t)}.\end{split}

Here
\mathcal{H}
denotes the Hilbert transform on
\mathbb{R}
.
Differentiating (
2.10
) with respect to
s
yields

\partial_{t}\big(X^{\prime}(s,t)\big)=-\frac{1}{4}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(\cdot,t)\big|_{x=X(s,t)}\cdot X^{\prime}(s,t).

Using (
2.9
), this can be further recast as

\partial_{t}f(X(s,t),t)+\partial_{t}X(s,t)\cdot\partial_{x}f(X(s,t),t)=-f(X(s,t),t)\cdot\frac{1}{4}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(\cdot,t)\big|_{x=X(s,t)}.

Using (
2.10
) again on the left-hand side and letting
x=X(s,t)
, we obtain a scalar equation on
\mathbb{R}
for
f=f(x,t)

(2.11)

\partial_{t}f=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\right).

Now assuming
f(x,t)
to be spatially
2\pi
-periodic for all time, and discarding the coefficient
\frac{1}{4}
by a change of the time variable, we obtain (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}
.
Note that, in this periodic setting, the Hilbert transform and the fractional Laplacian in (
1.1
) should be re-interpreted as those on
\mathbb{T}
(see (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) for their definitions).

We introduce this special setting of the 2-D Peskin problem, mainly motivated by the interest of understanding global behavior of the 2-D Peskin problem with general initial data, which is a challenging problem.
Given the blow-up criterion in
[
2
]
mentioned above, it is natural to ask whether finite-time singularity would occur in some typical scenarios.
For example, if a local string segment is smooth at the initial time, and if it is known to “never gets close to other parts of the string”, one may ask whether or not it would spontaneously develop finite-time singularity, such as loss of regularity and aggregation of Lagrangian points, etc.
The tangential Peskin problem proposed here can be the first step in this direction.

We state a few remarks on (
2.11
) and the above derivation.

Remark 2.1
.

(
2.11
) provides the exact string dynamics in the special setting considered here, as no approximation is made in the derivation.
This is in contrast to the scalar model in
[
53
]
, where the tangential deformation of the string should have made a difference to the evolution but gets ignored purposefully.

Although the tangential problem (
2.11
) (or equivalently, (
2.8
)) is derived in a simplified geometric setting, different from that of the original 2-D Peskin problem (
2.5
) where the string is a closed curve,
we will reveal a surprising connection between the two problems in a forthcoming paper
[
58
]
.
We prove that, in the original 2-D Peskin problem, if the closed elastic string initially exhibits a perfectly circular shape, then it should remain to be a circle of the same radius for all time, while the center of the circle can move.
After normalizing the position of the circle center, we find that the tangential deformation along the circle is described
exactly
by the tangential model.
This allows us to fully characterize the global dynamics of such circular configurations under rather weak assumptions (also see Corollary
2.1
below).
Beyond that, analysis in this paper also provides useful guidance for studying the original 2-D Peskin problem there with more general initial data.

Remark 2.2
.

Given the physical interpretation of
f
,
F(x,t):=1/f(x,t)
represents the spatial density of Lagrangian material points along the elastic string.
Thus, there should be no surprise that
F
satisfies a conservation law (
1.4
), and it is natural to assume
F
to have
L^{1}
-regularity at least.

Remark 2.3
.

If
f=f(x,t)
is a sufficiently smooth solution to (
2.11
) on
\mathbb{R}
with initial data
f(x,0)=f_{0}(x)
, then for any
\lambda>0
,

\lambda f(x,\lambda t),\;f(\lambda x,\lambda t),\;\lambda f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},t\right)

are all solutions, corresponding to initial datum
\lambda f_{0}(x)
,
f_{0}(\lambda x)
, and
\lambda f_{0}(x/\lambda)
, respectively.

Remark 2.4
.

We imposed periodicity in (
2.11
) mainly for convenience of analysis.
Many results in this paper should still hold in the case of
\mathbb{R}
, given suitable decay conditions at the spatial infinity.
In that case, one has to pay extra attention to integrability issues from time to time.
We thus choose to work on
\mathbb{T}
to avoid such technicality.

If the spatial periodicity is to be imposed, it is enough to assume the
2\pi
-periodicity, as one can always rescale in space and time.
Also note that the
2\pi
-periodicity is imposed in the Eulerian coordinate.
In the Lagrangian coordinate,
X^{\prime}(\cdot,t)
is periodic as well given the monotonicity and bijectivity assumptions of
X(\cdot,t)
, but the period is
\frac{2\pi}{f_{\infty}}
, with
f_{\infty}
defined in (
1.9
).
Indeed,

X^{-1}(x+2\pi,t)-X^{-1}(x,t)=\int_{X^{-1}(x,t)}^{X^{-1}(x+2\pi,t)}\frac{1}{f(X(s,t),t)}\,dX(s,t)=\int_{x}^{x+2\pi}\frac{1}{f(y,t)}\,dy.

Observe that the last integral is a constant independent of
x
and
t
thanks to the periodicity of
f
and the conservation law (
1.4
) of
\frac{1}{f}
.

Remark 2.5
.

One may consider general elasticity laws.
Assume the tension takes the simple form
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(|X^{\prime}(s,t)|)
, with
\mathcal{T}(\cdot)
satisfying suitable assumptions.
Then we can follow the above argument to obtain (again with the coefficient
\frac{1}{4}
discarded)

(2.12)

\partial_{t}f=\mathcal{H}\big(\mathcal{T}(f)\big)\cdot\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{T}(f).

Here
f
is still defined by (
2.9
).
In this case,
F:=\frac{1}{f}
solves

(2.13)

\partial_{t}F=\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{T}(f)]\cdot F\big).

Suppose
\mathcal{T}:[0,+\infty]\to[0,+\infty]
is sufficiently smooth and strictly increasing.
Define
g(x,t):=\mathcal{T}(f(x,t))
.
Then (
2.12
) can be written as

(2.14)

\partial_{t}g=\mathcal{H}g\cdot\partial_{x}g-\mathcal{N}(g)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}g.

Here
\mathcal{N}
is a function that sends
\mathcal{T}(f)
to
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(f)f
, i.e.,

\mathcal{N}(g):=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\big(\mathcal{T}^{-1}(g)\big)\cdot\mathcal{T}^{-1}(g).

If
\mathcal{T}(f)=f^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
, then
\mathcal{N}(g)=\gamma g
.
Denoting
\omega:=-\gamma g
, we may write (
2.14
) as

\partial_{t}\omega+\gamma^{-1}u\partial_{x}\omega=\omega\partial_{x}u,\quad u=\mathcal{H}\omega.

This provides a formal analogue of Okamoto-Sakajo-Wunsch generalization
[
48
]
of the De Gregorio model (also see Section
1.2
).

Most of, if not all, the previous analytical studies of the 2-D Peskin problem are based on the Lagrangian formulations, such as (
2.5
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
),
whereas (
2.11
) (or equivalently (
1.1
)) is formulated in the Eulerian coordinate.
We will thus recast our main result Theorem
1.1
in the Lagrangian coordinate.
Even though it is concerned with a special setting of the 2-D Peskin problem, compared with the existing results, it significantly weakens the assumption on the initial data for which a global solution can be well-defined.

Recall that
X=X(s,t)
represents the evolving configuration of the infinitely long elastic string lying along the
x
-axis.
It is assumed to be periodic in space.
It is supposed to solve (
2.8
) in the Lagrangian coordinate with the initial condition
X(s,0)=X_{0}(s)
.
Regarding the existence of such an
X(s,t)
, we have the following result, which will be proved in Section
6.2
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Suppose
X_{0}:s\mapsto X_{0}(s)\in\mathbb{R}
is a function defined on
\mathbb{R}
, satisfying that

(i)

X_{0}(s)
is strictly increasing in
s
, and
X_{0}(s+2\pi)=X_{0}(s)+2\pi
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

When restricted on
[-\pi,\pi]
,
X_{0}\in H^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
;

(iii)

The inverse function of
X_{0}
on any compact interval of
\mathbb{R}
is absolutely continuous.

Then there exists a function
X=X(s,t)
defined on
\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty)
, satisfying that

(1)

X(s,t)
is a smooth strong solution of (
2.8
) in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,+\infty)
, i.e.,

\partial_{t}X(s,t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{X^{\prime}(s^{\prime},t)^{2}}{X(s,t)-X(s^{\prime},t)}\,ds^{\prime}

holds pointwise in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,+\infty)
.

(2)

For any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

X(s,t)-s\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty))
.
As a consequence,
X(\cdot,t)
converges uniformly to
X_{0}(\cdot)
as
t\to 0^{+}
.

(3)

For any
t\geq 0
,
X(\cdot,t)
verifies the above three assumptions on
X_{0}(\cdot)
, with
\|X(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}([-\pi,\pi])}
being non-increasing in
t\in[0,+\infty)
.
It additionally satisfies the well-stretched condition when
t>0
, i.e., there exists
\lambda=\lambda(t)>0
, such that

|X(s_{1},t)-X(s_{2},t)|\geq\lambda(t)|s_{1}-s_{2}|\quad\forall\,s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

In fact,
\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}
satisfies an estimate similar to (
1.8
).

(4)

There exists a constant
c_{\infty}
, such that as
t\to+\infty
,
X(s,t)
converges uniformly to
X_{\infty}(s):=s+c_{\infty}
with an exponential rate.
The exponential convergence also holds for higher-order norms.

For simplicity, we assumed in (
i
) that the periodicity of
X_{0}
is
2\pi
in both the Lagrangian and the Eulerian coordinate (cf. Remark
2.4
).
The assumption (
ii
) only requires
X_{0}
to belong to the energy class (see the discussion in Section
2.1
and also Remark
3.1
below), without imposing any condition on its size.
The assumption (
iii
) is a relaxation of the well-stretched assumption that is commonly used in the literature.
The function
X(s,t)
found in Corollary
2.1
can be naturally defined as a global solution to (
2.8
) with the initial data
X_{0}
.
For brevity, we choose not to elaborate the notion of the solution.

3.
Preliminaries

In Sections
3
-
5
, we will establish a series of a priori estimates for nice solutions to (
1.1
) (and also (
1.4
)), which prepares us for proving existence of weak solutions later.
Throughout these sections, we will always assume
f=f(x,t)
is a positive strong solution to (
1.1
).
The definition of the strong solution is as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
T\in[0,+\infty]
,
f(x,t)
is said to be a strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
, if

(a)

f\in C_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
;

(b)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f
can be defined pointwise as a function in
C_{loc}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
;

(c)

(
1.1
) holds pointwise in
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
.

Here the time derivative of
f
at
t=0
is understood as the right derivative.

When discussing strong solutions, we always implicitly assume that the initial data
f_{0}
is correspondingly smooth.

We start with a few basic properties of positive strong solutions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
f
is a positive strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
.
Then

(1)

(Energy estimate) For any
t\in[0,T)
,

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

(2)

(Decay of
L^{p}
-norms of
f
and
F
)
For any
p\in[1,+\infty)
,
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}
are non-increasing in
t
.
In particular,
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
is time-invariant, and
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\geq 2\pi f_{\infty}
, where
f_{\infty}
is defined in (
1.9
).

Proof.

(
3.1
) can be readily proved by integrating (
1.1
) and integration by parts.

For arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.2)

\partial_{t}f^{\alpha}=\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot f^{\alpha}\big)-(1+\alpha)f^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f.

Integrating (
3.2
) yields

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dx=-\frac{1+\alpha}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\alpha}(x,t)\cdot\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(x,t)-f(y,t)}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dy\,dx

Given the regularity assumption on
f(\cdot,t)
, we may exchange the
x
- and
y
-variables to obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dx=&\;-\frac{1+\alpha}{2\pi}\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x-y|\geq\delta}\big(f^{\alpha}(x,t)-f^{\alpha}(y,t)\big)\cdot\frac{f(x,t)-f(y,t)}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

It is clear that, for any
a,b\geq 0\in\mathbb{R}
,

\big(a^{\alpha}-b^{\alpha}\big)(a-b)\begin{cases}\geq 0,&\mbox{if }\alpha>0,\\
=0,&\mbox{if }\alpha=0,\\
\leq 0,&\mbox{if }\alpha<0.\end{cases}

Hence, for all
\alpha\in(-\infty,-1]\cup[0,+\infty)
,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dx\leq 0.

This complete the proof of the second claim.
The time-invariance of
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
follows directly from (
1.4
).
We can further prove the lower bound for
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Recall that (
1.1
) is derived from the Hookean elasticity case with
k_{0}=1
, where the elastic energy density is
\mathcal{E}(p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}
as
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(p)=\mathcal{T}(p)
.
We also find

\frac{1}{2}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X^{-1}(0,t)}^{X^{-1}(2\pi,t)}f(X(s,t),t)\,dX(s,t)=\int_{X^{-1}(0,t)}^{X^{-1}(2\pi,t)}\frac{1}{2}|X^{\prime}(s,t)|^{2}\,ds,

which is exactly the total elastic energy of the string in one period in the Lagrangian coordinate.
This is why we called (
3.1
) the energy estimate.
Hence, we shall also call
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
the energy class for (
1.1
).
In general, for (
2.12
), the energy estimate writes

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mathcal{E}(f)}{f}\,dx=-\|\mathcal{T}(f)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},\mbox{ where }\mathcal{E}(p):=\int_{0}^{p}\mathcal{T}(q)\,dq.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
f
to be a positive strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
.
Then
f
satisfies maximum and minimum principle,
i.e., for all
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<T
,

\inf_{x}f(x,t_{1})\leq\inf_{x}f(x,t_{2})\leq\sup_{x}f(x,t_{2})\leq\sup_{x}f(x,t_{1}).

If we additionally assume
\partial_{x}f\in C_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
and that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{x}f)
can be defined pointwise in
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
and is locally continuous,
then
\partial_{x}f
also satisfies the maximum and minimum principle, i.e.,

\inf_{x}\partial_{x}f(x,t_{1})\leq\inf_{x}\partial_{x}f(x,t_{2})\leq\sup_{x}\partial_{x}f(x,t_{2})\leq\sup_{x}\partial_{x}f(x,t_{1}).

Proof.

It follows immediate from (
1.1
) that
f
satisfies the maximum/minimum principle, since
f\geq 0
.
Differentiating (
1.1
) yields

\partial_{t}\big(\partial_{x}f\big)=\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\big(\partial_{x}f\big)-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\partial_{x}f\big).

Hence,
\partial_{x}f
also satisfies the maximum/minimum principle.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In the context of the Peskin problem, the well-stretched assumption (
2.6
) (with constant
\lambda>0
) corresponds to
f(\cdot,t)\geq\lambda
in (
1.1
) and (
2.11
).
As a result, strong solutions to (
2.7
) satisfy well-stretched condition for all time with the constant
\lambda
as long as it is initially this case.
Besides, since

\partial_{x}f(X(s,t),t)=\frac{X^{\prime\prime}(s,t)}{X^{\prime}(s,t)}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{s}\ln(|X^{\prime}(s)|^{2}),

if
f(x,0)\geq\lambda>0
and
\|X_{0}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}<+\infty
, we have
X^{\prime\prime}
to be uniformly bounded in time.

4.
Instant Positivity and Boundedness

In this section, we want to prove that for any
t>0
, the solution
f(\cdot,t)
admits positive and finite lower and upper bounds, which only depend on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
a priori.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
f=f(x,t)
is a positive strong solution on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
Denote

f_{*}(t):=\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f(x,t)
.
Then for all
t>0
,

(4.1)

f_{*}(t)\geq 8\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}\exp\left[-\coth\left(\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right].

or equivalently,

\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\exp\left[\coth\left(\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right].

In particular, when
t\leq\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
,

(4.2)

\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\exp\big(C\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}t^{-1}\big),

where
C
is a universal constant.

Proof.

Denote
A:=\sqrt{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1/2}>0
.
We first prove that

(4.3)

f_{*}(t)\geq\exp\left[-\frac{1}{A}\cdot\coth\left(\frac{A}{2\pi}t\right)\right].

Suppose the minimum
f_{*}(t)
is attained at
x_{*}(t)
.
Since
\coth(\frac{A}{2\pi}t)>1
, we may assume
f_{*}(t)<\exp(-\frac{1}{A})
for all time of interest, as otherwise (
4.3
) holds automatically. We first derive a lower bound for
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(x_{*}(t),t)
.
With
\delta\in(0,\pi)
to be determined,

\begin{split}&\;-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(x_{*},t)\\
\geq&\;\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}\backslash[-\delta,\delta]}\frac{f(x_{*}+y)-f(x_{*})}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{y}{2}\big)}\,dy\\
=&\;\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\backslash[-\delta,\delta]}\frac{f(x_{*}+y)}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{y}{2}\big)}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\backslash[-\delta,\delta]}\frac{1}{f(x_{*}+y)}\,dy\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\backslash[-\delta,\delta]}F(x_{*}+y)\,dy\right)^{-1}\\
&\;-\frac{f(x_{*})}{\pi\tan\big(\frac{\delta}{2}\big)}\\
\geq&\;\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\int_{\delta}^{\pi}\frac{1}{\sin\big(\frac{y}{2}\big)}\,dy\right)^{2}\|F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1}-\frac{f(x_{*})}{\pi\tan\big(\frac{\delta}{2}\big)}\\
\geq&\;\frac{4}{\pi}\left|\ln\tan\frac{\delta}{4}\right|^{2}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1}-\frac{f(x_{*})}{2\pi\tan\big(\frac{\delta}{4}\big)}.\end{split}

Here we used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\equiv\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
.
Since
f_{*}(t)<\exp(-\frac{1}{A})<1
, we may take
\delta\in(0,\pi)
such that

\tan(\frac{\delta}{4})=f(x_{*})
.
This gives

-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(x_{*},t)\geq\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1}\big|\ln f(x_{*},t)\big|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi},

Since
f\in C_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty))
, we may argue as in e.g.
[
59
, Theorem 3.1]
to find that

\frac{d}{dt}f_{*}(t)=-f(x_{*},t)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(x_{*},t)\geq f_{*}(t)\left[\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1}\big|\ln f_{*}(t)\big|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right].

Hence, with
A=\sqrt{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1/2}>0
,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{\ln f_{*}(t)}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[A^{2}-\big(\ln f_{*}(t)\big)^{-2}\right].

Denote
g_{*}(t)=(\ln f_{*}(t))^{-1}
.
Since
f_{*}(t)<\exp(-\frac{1}{A})
, we have
g_{*}(t)\in(-A,0]
for all time of interest (including
t=0
).
Then we write the above inequality as

\frac{d}{dt}\ln\left(\frac{A+g_{*}(t)}{A-g_{*}(t)}\right)\leq-\frac{A}{\pi}.

Since
g_{*}(0)\in(-A,0]
,

\ln\left(\frac{A+g_{*}(t)}{A-g_{*}(t)}\right)\leq-\frac{A}{\pi}t+\ln\left(\frac{A+g_{*}(0)}{A-g_{*}(0)}\right)\leq-\frac{A}{\pi}t.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{\ln f_{*}(t)}\leq A\cdot\frac{\exp\big(-\frac{A}{\pi}t\big)-1}{\exp\big(-\frac{A}{\pi}t\big)+1}.

Then (
4.3
) follows.

Recall that if
f=f(x,t)
is a strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}
, then for any
\lambda>0
,
f_{\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda f(x,\lambda t)
is a solution corresponding to initial condition
\lambda f_{0}(x)
.
Now applying (
4.3
) to
f_{\lambda}
, we deduce that,

f_{\lambda,*}(t):=\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\geq\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}A}\cdot\coth\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/2}A}{2\pi}t\right)\right].

Here we still defined
A=\sqrt{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{-1/2}
.
Since
\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f(x,\lambda t)
, for all
\lambda,\tau>0
,

\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}f(x,\tau)\geq\lambda^{-1}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}A}\cdot\coth\left(\frac{\lambda^{-1/2}A}{2\pi}\tau\right)\right].

Taking
\lambda=A^{-2}
yields (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We chose (
4.1
) instead of (
4.3
) as the main estimate, because (
4.1
) is scaling-invariant while (
4.3
) is not.
Note that
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t
is a dimensionless quantity under the scaling
f_{\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda f(x,\lambda t)
.

Next we turn to study the upper bound for
f(\cdot,t)
.
In the following lemma, we will prove a “from
L^{1}
to
L^{\infty}
” type estimate, which states that the
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
should enjoy a decay like
t^{-1/2}
when
t\ll 1
.
This may be reminiscent of similar boundedness results in, e.g.,
[
11
,
28
]
, which are proved by considering cut-offs of the solution and applying a De Giorgi-Nash-Moser-type iteration (without the part of proving Hölder regularity).
Here we provide a different argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
f=f(x,t)
is a strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
Then for
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C>0
is a universal constant.
Consequently (cf. Lemma
3.2
), for
t>\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Remark 4.2
.

The exponents of
t
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
in the above estimate are sharp in the view of dimension analysis (cf. the energy estimate in Lemma
3.1
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\geq 1
up to suitable scaling.
We proceed in several steps.

Step 1

(Basic setup)
.

Let
W:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a convex increasing function, which is to be specified later.
For the moment, we assume
W
is finite and locally
C^{1,1}
on
[0,+\infty)
,
W(0)=0
, and
W^{\prime}(0)=1
.
Define

I(t)=\begin{cases}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},&\mbox{if }t=0,\\
\frac{1}{t^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\big(t^{1/2}f(x,t)\big)\,dx,&\mbox{if }t>0.\end{cases}

Thanks to the assumption on
W
and that
f
is a strong solution,
I(t)
is locally finite near
t=0
, continuous at
0
, and continuously differentiable on
(0,+\infty)
whenever it is finite.

Let us explain the idea in the rest of the proof.
We introduce the above functional mainly to study the size of the set

(4.4)

S_{\Lambda}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\,f(x,t)>\Lambda/t^{1/2}\}

for large
\Lambda>0
.
We will show that, one can suitably choose
W
, which will depend on the Lebesgue measure of
S_{\Lambda}(t)
(denoted by
|S_{\Lambda}(t)|
), such that
I(t)
stays bounded for all time.
This in turn provides an improved bound for
|S_{\Lambda}(t)|
for sufficiently large
\Lambda
, which gives rise to an improved choice of
W
.
Then we bootstrap to find
|S_{\Lambda}(t)|=0
for all
\Lambda
’s that are sufficiently large.

Step 2

(The growth of
I(t)
)
.

Fix
t\in(0,T]
.
By direct calculation and integration by parts,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=&\;\frac{1}{2t^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}\big(t^{1/2}f\big)\cdot t^{1/2}f-W(t^{1/2}f)\,dx\\
&\;-\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[W^{\prime}\big(t^{1/2}f\big)\cdot t^{1/2}f-W\big(t^{1/2}f\big)\right]\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{1/2}f)\,dx\\
&\;-\frac{2}{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\big(t^{1/2}f\big)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{1/2}f)\,dx.\end{split}

Denote
g(x):=t^{1/2}f(x,t)
and
V^{2}(y):=W^{\prime}(y)y-W(y)
.
Then this becomes

(4.5)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=&\;\frac{1}{2t^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g(x)\big)\,dx-\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g(x)\big)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}g(x)\,dx\\
&\;-\frac{2}{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\big(g(x)\big)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}g(x)\,dx.\end{split}

With
\Lambda>0
to be determined,
we define

g_{\Lambda}(x):=\min\{g(x),\Lambda\},\quad\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x):=\max\{g(x),\Lambda\}.

In what follows, we shall first take
V
properly and then determine
W
.

Assume
V
to be increasing. Then for the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.5
), we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g(x)\big)\,dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[V\big(g_{\Lambda}(x)\big)+\big(V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\big)\right]^{2}\,dx\\
\leq&\;2\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g_{\Lambda}(x)\big)\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\big)^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

Assume
V^{2}(y)\leq y^{3}

for
y\in[0,\Lambda]
.
Then

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g_{\Lambda}(x)\big)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\min\big\{t^{1/2}f(x,t),\Lambda\big\}^{3}\,dx\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,t)^{3}\,dx.

For the second term in (
4.6
), we first assume

(4.8)

\Lambda\geq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

and then apply Hölder’s inequality and interpolation (see e.g.
[
60
]
) to derive that

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\big)^{2}\,dx\\
\leq&\;\left\|V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big|\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\,g(x,t)>\Lambda\}\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\;C\left\|V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Here we can use
H^{1/2}
-semi-norm because, for
\Lambda\geq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}
,
V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))-V(\Lambda)
is zero on a set of positive measure. Indeed, this is because, when
t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(x,t)\,dx=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\Lambda.

Hence, (
4.9
) gives

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)-V(\Lambda)\big)^{2}\,dx\leq C_{1}\big\|V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|,

where
C_{1}>0
is a universal constant.
Summarizing (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.10
), we obtain that

(4.11)

\frac{1}{2t^{3/2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}\big(g(x)\big)\,dx\leq\|f(x,t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+\frac{C_{1}}{t^{3/2}}\big\|V\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\big)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|.

Now we handle the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.5
).
Since
V^{2}
is increasing,

\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}(g(x))\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}g(x)\,dx\\
\geq&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\big(V^{2}(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))-V^{2}(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))\big)\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)-\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y)\big)}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dx\,dy\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)\neq\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y)\}}\frac{\big(V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))+V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))\big)\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)-\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y)\big)}{V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))-V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))}\cdot\frac{\big(V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))-V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))\big)^{2}}{4\sin^{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{2}\big)}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Suppose
V
is increasing and convex on
[0,+\infty)
, such that for all
z>\Lambda
and all
t\in[0,T]
,

\frac{V^{\prime}(z)}{V(z)}\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{1}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|\right)^{-1}.

Then by the mean value theorem,

\frac{\big(V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))+V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))\big)\big(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x)-\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y)\big)}{V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))-V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(y))}\geq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|.

Therefore,

(4.12)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V^{2}(g(x))\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}g(x)\,dx\geq\frac{C_{1}}{t^{3/2}}\big\|V(\tilde{g}_{\Lambda}(x))\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|.

The last term on the right-hand side of (
4.5
) is clearly non-positive, since
W
is non-decreasing.
Hence, combining
(
4.5
), (
4.11
), and (
4.12
) yields

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)\leq\|f(x,t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}.

Thanks to the energy estimate (
3.1
) and interpolation, for all
t>0
,

(4.13)

I(t)\leq I(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\,d\tau\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}=:C_{*}.

Note that
C_{*}
is a constant only depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.
Actually, when deriving (
4.13
), we first obtained

I(t)\leq I(\delta)+\int_{\delta}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\,d\tau,

and then sent
\delta\to 0^{+}
.

Step 3

(The choice of
W
)
.

Summarizing our assumptions on
V
, we need

•

V
is increasing and convex on
[0,+\infty)
.

•

For
y\in[0,\Lambda]
,
V^{2}(y)\leq y^{3}
.

•

For
y\in(\Lambda,+\infty)
,

(4.14)

\frac{V^{\prime}(y)}{V(y)}\leq\inf_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{1}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|\right)^{-1}=:\beta.

We remark that
\beta
admits an a priori lower bound that only depends on
\Lambda
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.
In fact, by the energy estimate (
3.1
) and interpolation,

\|f\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Since
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
is non-increasing in time (see Lemma
3.1
),
this implies

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{3}{4}}t^{-\frac{1}{4}},

which gives

\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|=\big|\big\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\,f(x,t)>\Lambda/t^{1/2}\big\}\big|\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{3}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{t}{\Lambda^{2}}.

Therefore,

(4.15)

\beta=\inf_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{1}\big|S_{\Lambda}(t)\big|\right)^{-1}\geq C_{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Lambda^{2},

where
C_{2}
is a universal constant.

We define
V
to be the unique continuous function on
[0,+\infty)
that satisfies

V(y)=y^{\frac{3}{2}}\mbox{ on }[0,\Lambda],\quad\mbox{and}\quad V^{\prime}(y)=\beta V(y)\mbox{ on }y\in(\Lambda,+\infty),

i.e.,

(4.16)

V(y)=\begin{cases}y^{3/2},&\mbox{if }y\in[0,\Lambda],\\
\Lambda^{3/2}e^{\beta(y-\Lambda)},&\mbox{if }y\in[\Lambda,+\infty).\end{cases}

We shall suitably choose
\Lambda
in order to guarantee that
V
is increasing and convex.
In fact, we only need
V^{\prime}(\Lambda^{-})\leq V^{\prime}(\Lambda^{+})
, i.e.,
\frac{3}{2}\Lambda^{1/2}\leq\beta\Lambda^{3/2}
.
By virtue of (
4.15
),
it is safe to take

(4.17)

\Lambda\geq C_{3}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{3}
is a universal constant.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
C_{3}\geq 2
so that (
4.17
) is no weaker than (
4.8
).

With this choice of
V
, we thus define
W
by

\frac{d}{dy}\left(\frac{W(y)}{y}\right)=\frac{V^{2}(y)}{y^{2}},\quad\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{W(y)}{y}=1.

For future use, we calculate it explicitly

(4.18)

W(y)=\begin{cases}y+\frac{1}{2}y^{3},&\mbox{if }y\in[0,\Lambda],\\
y\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{2}+\Lambda^{3}\int_{\Lambda}^{y}z^{-2}e^{2\beta(z-\Lambda)}\,dz\right),&\mbox{if }y\in[\Lambda,+\infty).\end{cases}

This resulting
W
is convex on
[0,+\infty)
.
Indeed, on
(0,\Lambda)\cup(\Lambda,+\infty)
,

W^{\prime\prime}(y)=\frac{1}{y}\big(W^{\prime}(y)y-W(y)\big)^{\prime}=\frac{2}{y}V(y)V^{\prime}(y)\geq 0,

and
W^{\prime}(\Lambda^{-})=W^{\prime}(\Lambda^{+})
because
V^{2}(y)
and
W(y)
are continuous at
y=\Lambda
.

By virtue of (
4.13
), for any
W
constructed in the above way, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\big(t^{1/2}f(x,t)\big)\,dx\leq C_{*}t^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies, for any
\lambda>0
,

\big|S_{\lambda}(t)\big|=\big|\big\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\,f(x,t)\geq\lambda/t^{1/2}\big\}\big|\leq C_{*}t^{\frac{1}{2}}W(\lambda)^{-1},

and thus,

(4.19)

\inf_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{1}\big|S_{\lambda}(t)\big|\right)^{-1}\geq(C_{1}C_{*})^{-1}W(\lambda).

Here
C_{1}
and
C_{*}
are defined in (
4.10
) and (
4.13
), respectively.
We will treat (
4.19
) as an improvement of (
4.15
).

Step 4

(Bootstrap)
.

Next we introduce a bootstrap argument.
Let
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
, and
C_{3}
be the universal constants defined in (
4.10
), (
4.15
), and (
4.17
), respectively.
With
C_{0}\geq C_{3}\geq 2
to be chosen, we let

\Lambda_{0}=C_{0}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\beta_{0}=C_{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Lambda_{0}^{2}=C_{2}C_{0}^{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
W_{0}
be defined by (
4.18
), with
\Lambda
and
\beta
there replaced by
\Lambda_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
.
For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, let

\Lambda_{j}=\left(C_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{j}k^{-2}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In the view of (
4.19
) and also (
4.14
), iteratively define

(4.20)

\beta_{j}=(C_{1}C_{*})^{-1}W_{j-1}(\Lambda_{j}),

while
W_{j}
is then constructed by (
4.18
), with
\Lambda
and
\beta
replaced by
\Lambda_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
.

Observe that
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j}
is increasing, and

(4.21)

C_{0}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\Lambda_{j}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{0}^{\prime}/C_{0}
is bounded by some universal constant.
With
C_{0}
being suitably large, we claim that, for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

(4.22)

\beta_{j-1}\geq j^{3}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

We prove this by induction.
First, choose
C_{0}\geq\max\{C_{2}^{-1/2},C_{3}\}
, so that (
4.22
) holds for
j=1
.
For
j\geq 2
, by the definitions of
\Lambda_{j}
and
W_{j}
,
we derive by (
4.18
) that for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

W_{j-1}(\Lambda_{j})\geq\Lambda_{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{j-1}^{2}+\frac{\Lambda_{j-1}^{3}}{\Lambda_{j}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda_{j}-\Lambda_{j-1}}e^{2\beta_{j-1}z}\,dz\right).

By (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), and using the definition of
C_{*}
in (
4.13
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\beta_{j}\geq&\;(C_{1}C_{*})^{-1}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_{j-1}^{3}}{\Lambda_{j}}\cdot\frac{e^{2\beta_{j-1}j^{-2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}}-1}{2\beta_{j-1}}\\
\geq&\;\frac{C(C_{0}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2})^{2}}{\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}\cdot j^{-2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}\cdot\frac{e^{2\beta_{j-1}j^{-2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}}-1}{2\beta_{j-1}j^{-2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}},\end{split}

where
C
’s are universal constants.
Using the induction hypothesis (
4.22
) and the assumption
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\geq 1
,

\beta_{j}\geq CC_{0}^{2}\cdot j^{-2}\cdot\frac{e^{2j}-1}{2j}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1/2}\geq CC_{0}^{2}(j+1)^{3}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1/2},

where
C
is a universal constant.
In the second inequality, we used the trivial fact that for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\frac{e^{2j}-1}{2j}\geq Cj^{2}(j+1)^{3}
for some universal
C>0
.
Now we choose
C_{0}
to be suitably large but universal, so that
CC_{0}^{2}\geq 1
in the above estimate.
This yields (
4.22
) with
j
replaced by
(j+1)
.
Therefore, by induction, (
4.22
) holds for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Using (
4.19
) and (
4.20
), we find for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot C_{1}\big|\big\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\,f(x,t)\geq\Lambda_{\infty}/t^{1/2}\big\}\big|\leq\beta_{j}^{-1}.

By (
4.22
) and the arbitrariness of
j
, we conclude that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

f(\cdot,t)\leq\Lambda_{\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{a.e.\;on }\mathbb{T}.

Since

\Lambda_{\infty}=\left(C_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{0}>0
is universal, the desired estimate follows.

∎

5.
Smoothing and Decay Estimates

5.1.
Higher regularity with time integrability at
t=0

With the estimates established so far, we are still not able to make sense of the integral

\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt

in the weak formulation (
1.5
).
Indeed, with initial data
f_{0},F_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we only have

f\in L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{2}_{T}H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\cap L^{2^{-}}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}),\mbox{ and }F\in L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}(\mathbb{T}),

whereas
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\not\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, and the Hilbert transform is not bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
either.
A natural idea to resolve this integrability issue is to prove that
f
has some spatial regularity higher than
H^{1/2}
, with the corresponding norm being time-integrable.
This seems not trivial because of the nonlinearity and degeneracy of the equation (
1.1
).
In particular, the very bad lower bound for
f
in Lemma
4.1
indicates extremely weak smoothing effect in some part of
\mathbb{T}
, so the higher-order norms of
f
could be terribly singular near
t=0
.
Fortunately, by studying the special properties of (
1.1
),
we can prove that certain Hölder norms of
f
are time-integrable near
t=0
(see Corollary
5.1
).
This is the theme of this subsection.

For convenience, we assume
f
to be a positive smooth solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
Define
h:=\ln f
.
Clearly,
h
is a smooth solution to

(5.1)

\partial_{t}h=\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}h-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f.

We start from the entropy estimate for
F
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\ln F\,dx+\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq 0.

Proof.

We derive with (
1.4
) that

(5.2)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\ln F\,dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\ln F)\partial_{t}F\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(1+\ln F)\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}F\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}F(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx.\end{split}

The right-hand side can be written as

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}F(x)\cdot\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{x-y}{2})}\,dy\,dx\\
=&\;-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{(F(x)-F(y))(f(y)-f(x))}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{x-y}{2})}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}(F(x)-F(y))(f(y)-f(x))=&\;\frac{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}}{F(x)F(y)}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{F(x)}{F(y)}}-\sqrt{\frac{F(y)}{F(x)}}\right)^{2}\\
\geq&\;\left(2\ln\sqrt{\frac{F(x)}{F(y)}}\right)^{2}=\big(\ln F(x)-\ln F(y)\big)^{2},\end{split}

we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Then we state an
H^{1/2}
-estimate for
h
.

Lemma 5.2
.

\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+4\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\big)^{2}\,dx=0.

Proof.

Recall that for a mean-zero smooth function
u
defined on
\mathbb{T}
, we have the Cotlar’s identity

(5.3)

(\mathcal{H}u)^{2}-u^{2}=2\mathcal{H}(u\mathcal{H}u).

We calculate with (
5.1
) and (
5.3
) that

(5.4)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=&\;2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}h\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\,dx\\
=&\;-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot\mathcal{H}\big(\partial_{x}h(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\big)\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\,dx\\
=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{h}\Big[\big(\partial_{x}h\big)^{2}-\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\big)^{2}\Big]\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\big(e^{h}\big)\cdot\partial_{x}h\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\Big[\big(\partial_{x}h\big)^{2}+\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\big)^{2}\Big]\,dx.\end{split}

Observing that
f(\partial_{x}h)^{2}=4(\partial_{x}\sqrt{f})^{2}
,
we obtain the desired result.
∎

We further show
\|\sqrt{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
satisfies another simple estimate, which implies it is non-increasing as well.

Lemma 5.3
.

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}=0.

Proof.

Denoting
\omega=f
and
u=-\mathcal{H}f
, we recast (
1.1
) as

\partial_{t}\omega+u\partial_{x}\omega=\omega\partial_{x}u.

Note that in order to keep
\omega
positive, here we take a different change of variable from that in (
1.14
).
Hence,

\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}+u\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\omega}\partial_{xx}u.

Then we derive that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\big\|\sqrt{w}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}-u\partial_{xx}\sqrt{\omega}\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\omega}\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}\partial_{xx}u\,dx\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}-u\partial_{x}\big(\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}\big)^{2}-\partial_{x}u\big(\partial_{x}\sqrt{\omega}\big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\omega\partial_{xx}u\,dx\\
=&\;\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\omega\partial_{xx}u\,dx.\end{split}

Plugging in
u=-\mathcal{H}\omega
yields the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

For the De Gregorio model (
1.15
), non-negative solutions
\omega=\omega(x,t)
that are sufficiently smooth are known to enjoy conservation of
\|\sqrt{\omega}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

[
47
]
.
Interestingly, we can recover that result by following the derivation above and plugging in
u_{x}=\mathcal{H}\omega
(cf. (
1.15
)) in the last line.

By Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
f(\cdot,t)
is positive and bounded for all positive
t
.
Hence, whenever
t>0
, that
f(\cdot,t)\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
implies
h(\cdot,t)\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
and vice versa.
This is because

(5.5)

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{(f(x)-f(y))^{2}}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{x-y}{2})}\,dx\,dy=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\frac{(e^{h(x)}-e^{h(y)})^{2}}{4\sin^{2}(\frac{x-y}{2})}\,dx\,dy,

and

(5.6)

\min_{\mathbb{T}}f^{2}\cdot(h(x)-h(y))^{2}\leq(e^{h(x)}-e^{h(y)})^{2}\leq\max_{\mathbb{T}}f^{2}\cdot(h(x)-h(y))^{2}.

Hence,

(5.7)

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
, for any
t>0
, there exists
t_{*}\in[\frac{t}{2},t]
, such that
\|f(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq Ct^{-1/2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}
.
Combining this with (
5.7
), Lemma
4.1
, and Lemma
5.2
, we find

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\exp\left[\coth\left(C\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right].

This bound is too singular near
t=0
for future analysis.
In what follows, we establish an improved estimate by applying Lemmas
5.1
-
5.3
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
t>0
,

(5.8)

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\coth\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for any
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

(5.9)

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{7}{4}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}},

where
C
’s are universal constants.

Remark 5.2
.

These estimates are good enough for future analysis, though we do not know whether they are sharp in terms of the singularity at
t=0
.
However, the large-time decay of (
5.8
) is not optimal.
We will prove exponential decay of
h
and
f
in Section
5.2
and Section
7
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
, for all
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\ln F\,dx\leq\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{1}{8}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\right)+\coth\left(\frac{4}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right].

On the other hand, since
x\mapsto x\ln x
is convex on
(0,+\infty)
, by Jensen’s inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\ln F\,dx\geq\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\right).

Lemma
5.2
shows that
\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}
is non-increasing.
Hence, by Lemma
5.1
, for any
t>0
,

\begin{split}\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq&\;Ct^{-1}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\ln F\left(x,\frac{t}{2}\right)\,dx-\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\right)\right]\\
\leq&\;Ct^{-1}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\coth\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right).\end{split}

This gives (
5.8
).
Similarly, since Lemma
5.3
shows
\|\sqrt{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
is non-increasing, by Lemma
5.2
, we find for any
t>0
,

(5.10)

\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,t)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-1}\|h(\cdot,t/2)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-2}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\coth\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right).

Since

(5.11)

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq C\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

we apply Lemma
4.2
and (
5.10
) to derive that, for
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{5}{4}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\coth\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}t\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In this case, thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

t^{-1}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\geq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\geq 4\pi^{2},

so
t\leq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.
Then we can further simply the above estimate to obtain (
5.9
).
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

For
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}

and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5})
, with
f_{\infty}
defined in (
1.9
),

(5.12)

\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\,d\tau\leq C_{\alpha}t^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2}}\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}-2\pi f_{\infty}\big)^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2\alpha},

where
C_{\alpha}>0
is a universal constant depending on
\alpha
.
To be simpler, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\,d\tau\leq C_{\alpha}t^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2\alpha}.

Proof.

With
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5})
and
\delta>0
to be determined, by Sobolev embedding, interpolation, and the Young’s inequality,

\begin{split}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\leq&\;C_{\alpha}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-2\alpha}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2\alpha}\\
\leq&\;2\delta\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{4\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}.\end{split}

Here
C_{\alpha}>0
is a universal constant depending only on
\alpha
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.1
and (
5.9
), for
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\,d\tau\leq&\;2\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\,d\tau+C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{4\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\,d\tau\\
\leq&\;\delta\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}-2\pi f_{\infty}\big)+C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\frac{7\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\,d\tau.\end{split}

The last integral is finite as long as
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5})
.
Calculating the integral and optimizing over
\delta>0
yields
(
5.12
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

By better incorporating the estimate for
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}
from Lemma
5.3
,
one may slightly improve the power of
t
in (
5.12
) to become
t^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2(1-2\alpha)}}
, up to corresponding changes in the other factors.
However, we omit the details as that is not essential for the future analysis.

5.2.
Instant smoothing and global bounds

Now we derive a priori estimates that show the solution
f
would instantly become smooth, and that it converges exponentially to the constant
f_{\infty}
(see (
1.9
)) as
t\to+\infty
.
We start with a higher-order generalization of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
for positive smooth solutions.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
f=f(x,t)
is a positive smooth solution on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
Let
f_{*}(t)
be defined in Lemma
4.1
.
For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and any
t>0
,

(5.13)

\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+f_{*}(t)\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\leq\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2m},

and

(5.14)

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}\mathds{1}_{\{k>1\}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},

where
C_{k}
’s are universal constants only depending on
k
.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.13
).
With
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we differentiate (
5.1
) in
x
for
k
-times, integrate it against
(-\Delta)^{1/2}\partial_{x}^{k}h
, and derive as in Lemma
5.2
that

(5.15)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}h\big)\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot\mathcal{H}\left[\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\right]dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\big(e^{h}\big)\cdot\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\,dx\\
&\;+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}h\big)-\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\right](-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\,dx\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\left[\big(\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big)^{2}-\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\big)^{2}\right]dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{h}\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\cdot\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\,dx\\
&\;+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}h\big)-\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\right](-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\,dx\\
&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(e^{h}\partial_{x}h\big)-e^{h}\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\right]\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\,dx\\
=:&\;-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\left[\big(\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big)^{2}+\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\big)^{2}\right]dx+\mathcal{R}_{k}(t),\end{split}

where

\mathcal{R}_{k}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}h\big)-\mathcal{H}f\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\right](-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h-\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(e^{h}\partial_{x}h\big)-e^{h}\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\right]\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\,dx.

In the third equality above, we used the Cotlar’s identity (
5.3
).

We bound
\mathcal{R}_{k}
as follows.

\begin{split}|\mathcal{R}_{k}|\leq&\;C_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big|\partial_{x}^{n}\mathcal{H}f\big|\big|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}h\big|\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}h\big|+\big|\partial_{x}^{n}f\big|\big|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}h\big|\big|\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big|\,dx\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}h\big\|_{L^{{\frac{2(k+1)}{k+1-n}}}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{n}f\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{n}}},\end{split}

where
C_{k}>0
is a universal constant depending on
k
.
Since

(5.16)

\partial_{x}^{n}f=\partial_{x}^{n}\big(e^{h}\big)=e^{h}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=n\atop a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}C_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{x}^{a_{i}}h,

with
C_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}}
depending on
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
,
we have that

(5.17)

\begin{split}|\mathcal{R}_{k}|\leq&\;C_{k}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\;\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}h\big\|_{L^{{\frac{2(k+1)}{k+1-n}}}}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=n\atop a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|\partial_{x}^{a_{i}}h\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{a_{i}}}}.\end{split}

By Sobolev embedding and interpolation, for
l\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
,

\big\|\partial_{x}^{l}h\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{l}}}\leq C_{k}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{l+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{l}{2(k+1)}}}\leq C_{k}\left(\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)^{\frac{l}{k+1}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-\frac{2l}{k+1}}.

Hence, (
5.17
) becomes

\begin{split}|\mathcal{R}_{k}|\leq&\;C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{m-1}\\
\leq&\;\frac{1}{2}f_{*}(t)\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}h\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}f_{*}(t)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2(m-1)}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.15
) and using the fact
\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq Cf_{*}(t)^{-1/2}\|\sqrt{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
, we obtain (
5.13
).
We also remark that, if
k=0
in (
5.15
), we obtain
\mathcal{R}_{0}(t)=0
and thus the estimate in Lemma
5.2
.

The proof of (
5.14
) is similar.
With
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

(5.18)

\begin{split}&\;\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\big(\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)^{2}\,dx\\
=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\big)\big(\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\partial_{x}^{k}f\cdot\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big)\,dx\\
=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\big)\big(\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\partial_{x}^{k}f\cdot\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\partial_{x}^{k}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)\,dx\\
&\;+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\partial_{x}^{k}f\left[\partial_{x}^{k}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big)-\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\partial_{x}^{k}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)\right]dx\\
=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\big)\big(\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)^{2}+F\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\big(\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)^{2}-2\partial_{x}^{k}f\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\,dx+\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k}\\
=&\;-2\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k},\end{split}

where

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k}(t):=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\partial_{x}^{k}f\left[\partial_{x}^{k-1}\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\partial_{x}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f\big)-\big(\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}\partial_{x}^{k}f-f(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big)\right]dx.

Deriving analogously as before, we can show that

\begin{split}\big|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k}\big|\leq&\;C_{k}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big|\partial_{x}^{k}f\big|\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\left(\big|\partial_{x}^{n}\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}^{k-1-n}\partial_{x}\partial_{x}f\big|+\big|\partial_{x}^{n}f\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k-1-n}\partial_{x}f\big|\right)dx\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\big\|\partial_{x}^{n}f\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{n}}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}f\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{k+1-n}}}.\end{split}

By Sobolev embedding and interpolation, for all
n\in\{1,\cdots,k-1\}
,

\begin{split}\big\|\partial_{x}^{n}f\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{n}}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k+1-n}f\big\|_{L^{\frac{2(k+1)}{k+1-n}}}\leq&\;C_{k}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{n+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2(k+1)}}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{(k+1-n)+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k+1-n}{2(k+1)}}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.\end{split}

Combining these estimates with (
5.18
) yields

(5.19)

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq C_{k}\mathds{1}_{\{k>1\}}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

and thus, by Young’s inequality,

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq\big\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}\mathds{1}_{\{k>1\}}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Then (
5.14
) follows.
Note that (
5.19
) with
k=1
exactly corresponds to the estimate in Lemma
5.3
.
∎

The quantities involved in the estimates in Lemma
5.4
can be linked as follows.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and any
t>0
,

\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot C_{k}\left[\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2n}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{n}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}^{n}\right]f_{*}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

and

\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\cdot C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{m},

where
C_{k}
’s are universal constants only depending on
k
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\begin{split}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=&\;\big\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}\big(F\partial_{x}f\big)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\big\|F\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=n\atop a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\left\|\left(F^{m+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\partial_{x}^{a_{j}}f\right)\partial_{x}^{k-n}f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\\
&\;+C_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=n\atop a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4(m+1)}}}^{m+1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\big\|\partial_{x}^{a_{j}}f\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4m}}}\right)\big\|\partial_{x}^{k-n}f\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

Here we used the inequality that, for any
s_{1}>\frac{1}{2}
and
s_{2}>0
with
s_{1}\geq s_{2}
,

\|fg\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{2}}}\leq C_{s_{1},s_{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{2}}}.

Since
a_{j}\in[1,k-1]
, we derive with interpolation and the condition
\sum_{j}a_{j}=n
that

\begin{split}&\;\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4(m+1)}}}^{m+1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\big\|\partial_{x}^{a_{j}}f\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4m}}}\right)\big\|\partial_{x}^{k-n}f\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\leq&\;\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{m+\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-\frac{1}{2m}-\frac{a_{j}}{k}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2m}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{a_{j}}{k}}\right)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{n}{k}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-\frac{n}{k}}\\
\leq&\;\left(f_{*}(t)^{-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{m+\frac{1}{2}}\left(f_{*}(t)^{-2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\leq&\;f_{*}(t)^{-(m+\frac{3}{2})}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2m}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

In the second inequality, we used the definition of
H^{1/2}
-semi-norm and the fact
F=e^{-h}
(cf. (
5.5
) and (
5.6
)) to obtain
\|F\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\leq f_{*}(t)^{-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}
.
In the last inequality, we used

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}
that is also derived from (
5.6
).
Hence,

\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C_{k}\left[f_{*}(t)^{-2}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^{n}f_{*}(t)^{-(m+\frac{3}{2})}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2m}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

This implies the first desired inequality.

On the other hand, thanks to (
5.16
), we derive using Sobolev embedding and interpolation that

\begin{split}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}=&\;\left\|e^{\frac{1}{2}h}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=k\atop a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}C_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{x}^{a_{i}}h\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=k\atop a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\big\|\partial_{x}^{a_{i}}h\big\|_{L^{\frac{2k}{a_{i}}}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=k\atop a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{a_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{a_{i}}{2k}}}\\
\leq&\;C_{k}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{m-1}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}.\end{split}

This proves the second desired estimate.
∎

Now we may bootstrap with
Lemma
5.2
, Lemma
5.4
, and Lemma
5.5
to prove finiteness of
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
at all positive times, as well as their exponential decay as
t\to\infty
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose
f=f(x,t)
is a positive smooth solution on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t>0
,

(5.20)

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\,\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}).

Here
\mathcal{C}_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
denotes a generic function of
k
,
t
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, which satisfies:

(i)

It is finite whenever
t>0
;

(ii)

It is non-increasing in
t
, and non-decreasing in
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
;

(iii)

It decays exponentially to
0
as
t\to+\infty
with an explicit rate that only depends on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, i.e., there exists some
\alpha(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})>0
and constant
C_{k}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})>0
, such that for all
t\gg 1
,

\mathcal{C}_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})\leq C_{k}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})\exp[-\alpha(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})t].

As a result, for any
t_{0}>0
,
f(x,t)
is a smooth solution to (
1.1
) on the space-time domain
\mathbb{T}\times[t_{0},+\infty)
.
As
t\to+\infty
,
f(x,t)
converges exponentially to
f_{\infty}
defined in (
1.9
).
To be more precise, for arbitrary
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
t>0
,

(5.21)

\big\|\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{k}(f(\cdot,t)-f_{\infty})\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\mathcal{C}_{j,k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}).

Here
\mathcal{C}_{j,k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
is a function having similar properties as

\mathcal{C}_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
above.

Remark 5.4
.

We do not pursue precise form of estimates for the above claims, as they are complicated but may not be optimal.
Nevertheless, in Section
7
below, we will prove sharp decay estimates for the solution when
t\gg 1
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we will use the notation
C_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
to denote a generic function of
k
,
t
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, which satisfies the properties (
i
) and (
ii
) above, and yet

(iii’)

C_{k}(t,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
stays bounded as
t\to+\infty
.

For brevity, we will write it as
C_{k}(t)
in what follows.
Like the commonly-used notation
C
for universal constants, its precise definition may vary from line to line.
If it turns out to not depend on
k
, we simply write it as
C(t)
.

By Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, and Propostion
5.1
, we find

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}},\,\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}},\,\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C(t).

As a result, Lemma
5.4

and Lemma
5.5

imply that, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and any
t>0
,

(5.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+f_{*}(t)\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\;\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}(t)\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},

(5.23)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\;\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot C_{k}(t)\mathds{1}_{\{k>1\}}\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},

and

(5.24)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq

\displaystyle\;\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot C_{k}(t)\big\|\sqrt{f}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

(5.25)

\displaystyle\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\;\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\cdot C_{k}(t).

Step 1

(Preliminary)
.

We start from bounding
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
and
\|\sqrt{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.

By (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.2
,

\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq-f_{*}(t)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\partial_{x}h\big)^{2}+\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}h\big)^{2}\,dx=-2f_{*}(t)\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Hence, for arbitrary
t\geq t_{*}>0
, we have

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq\big\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\exp\left(-2\int_{t_{*}}^{t}f_{*}(\tau)\,d\tau\right),

This together with Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
5.1
implies
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
verifies the properties in (
5.20
), although it is not included there.

Thanks to (
5.10
), for any
t>0
,

\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=4\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,t)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-1}\|h(\cdot,t/2)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Hence,
\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,t)}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
satisfies (
5.20
) as well.
Consequently, given a
C_{k}(t)
satisfying the assumptions at the beginning of the proof, we have

(5.26)

C_{k}(t)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,t)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}f_{*}(t)

for all
t\gg 1
, where the required largeness depends on the form of
C_{k}(\cdot)
, and thus on
k
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
essentially.

In the next two steps, we prove the claim (
5.20
) by induction.

Step 2

(Base step)
.

Let us verify (
5.20
) with
k=1
.
We have just proved
\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
satisfies (
5.20
).
On the other hand, by Lemma
5.3
, for arbitrary
t>0
, there exists
t_{*}\in[\frac{1}{2}t,\frac{3}{4}t]
, such that

\|f(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-1}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f(\cdot,t/2)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then (
5.24
) implies

\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f(\cdot,t/2)\big\|_{L^{2}}\cdot C(t_{*})\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,t_{*})}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq C(t).

Now applying (
5.22
) with
k=1
, we find

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\;\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}+\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\exp\left(\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}C(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)f_{*}(t^{\prime})\|h(\cdot,t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}\,dt^{\prime}\\
\leq&\;\exp\left(\int_{t_{*}}^{t}C(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}.\end{split}

Notice that

(5.28)

\int_{t_{*}}^{\infty}C(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq C(t_{*})\leq C(t),

so

\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C(t)
. Also, (
5.22
) and (
5.26
) imply that, for sufficiently large
t
,

\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{*}(t)\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\leq 0.

Hence,
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}
decays exponentially as
t\to+\infty
with an explicit rate (cf. (
4.1
)).
Therefore,
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}
satisfies (
5.20
).

We also derive from (
5.27
) that there exists
t^{\prime}_{*}\in[\frac{3}{4}t,\frac{7}{8}t]
, such that

(5.29)

\|h(\cdot,t_{*}^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-1}f_{*}(t_{*}^{\prime})^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{t_{*}}^{t}C(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\leq C(t).

Step 3

(Induction step)
.

Suppose that (
5.20
) holds for the case
k
, and that for any
t>0
, there exists
t_{*}\in[t-2^{-2k}t,t-2^{-2k-1}t]
, such that
\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{k+1}}\leq C_{k}(t)
(cf. (
5.29
)). In this step, we verify (
5.20
) for
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}
and
\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
by arguing as above.

Thanks to (
5.25
),

\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t_{*})\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|h(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{k+1}}\cdot C_{k+1}(t_{*})\leq C_{k+1}(t).

Then (
5.23
) yields

(5.30)

\begin{split}&\;\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{t_{*}}^{t}\exp\left(\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}C_{k+1}(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)\|f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\,dt^{\prime}\\
\leq&\;\exp\left(\int_{t_{*}}^{t}C_{k+1}(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t_{*})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

which implies
\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{k+1}(t)
(cf. (
5.28
)). In addition, since

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\geq\big\|f^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq f_{*}(t)\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(
5.23
) also implies that, for
t\gg 1
(cf. (
5.26
)),

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}f_{*}(t)\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0.

This gives the exponential decay of
\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
as
t\to+\infty
.
Therefore,
\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
satisfies (
5.20
).

Besides, (
5.30
) also implies that there exists
t_{*}^{\prime}\in[t-2^{-2k-1}t,t-2^{-2k-2}t]
, such that

\begin{split}\|f(\cdot,t_{*}^{\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\leq&\;C2^{2k}t^{-1}\exp\left(\int_{t_{*}}^{t}C_{k+1}(\tau)\big\|\sqrt{f(\cdot,\tau)}\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(\cdot,t_{*})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{k+1}(t).\end{split}

Then we can use (
5.22
) and (
5.24
) and argue as in the previous step to show that
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{k+\frac{3}{2}}}
satisfies (
5.20
).
Moreover, there exists
t^{\prime\prime}_{*}\in[t-2^{-2k-2}t,t-2^{-2k-3}t]
, such that

\|h(\cdot,t_{*}^{\prime\prime})\|_{\dot{H}^{k+2}}\leq C_{k+1}(t)
.
We omit the details.

By induction, we thus prove (
5.20
) for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Step 4

(Convergence to
f_{\infty}
)
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}^{2}=\big\|f^{\frac{1}{2}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|F^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{k}f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}
is bounded on
[t_{0},+\infty)
for arbitrary
t_{0}>0
, and it decays exponentially as
t\to+\infty
.
Hence,
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
satisfies the same properties.
Combining this with Lemma
3.1
, we find that
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
is non-increasing in time and its convergence at
t\to+\infty
is exponential.
Hence,
f(\cdot,t)
is smooth in space-time in
\mathbb{T}\times[t_{0},+\infty)
for any
t_{0}>0
, and it converges exponentially to some constant as
t\to+\infty
in
H^{k}(\mathbb{T})
-norms for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Recall that
\|F\|_{L^{1}}
is conserved (cf. Lemma
3.1
), so the constant must be
f_{\infty}
defined in (
1.9
).
This proves (
5.21
) with
j=0
.

The claim (
5.21
) with
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
immediately follows from the case
j=0
and equation (
1.1
).

∎

5.3.
Time-continuity at
t=0

We end this section by showing the time-continuity of the solution at
t=0
in a suitable sense.
Note that the time-continuity at all positive times is already covered in Proposition
5.2
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
f
is a positive strong solution of (
1.1
).
Then for any
0<t\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5})
,

W_{1}(F_{0},F(\cdot,t))\leq C_{\alpha}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big(t\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2}}\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}.

Here
W_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the 1-Wasserstein distance
[
5
, Chapter 6]
, and
C_{\alpha}>0
is a universal constant depending on
\alpha
.

Proof.

Given
t_{*}\in(0,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}]
and
\delta<t_{*}
, let
\eta_{\delta}(t)
be a smooth non-increasing cutoff function on
[0,+\infty)
, such that
\eta_{\delta}\equiv 1
on
[0,t_{*}-\delta]
and
\eta_{\delta}\equiv 0
on
[t_{*},+\infty)
.
Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
We take
\varphi(x,t)=\phi(x)\eta_{\delta}(t)
in (
1.5
) and derive that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x)F_{0}(x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x)F(x,t)\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\eta_{\delta}(t)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi^{\prime}(x)\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt.

Sending
\delta\to 0^{+}
and using the smoothness of
f
and
F
at positive times, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x)\big(F_{0}(x)-F(x,t_{*})\big)\,dx=\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi^{\prime}(x)\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt.

By Corollary
5.1
and the fact that
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
is conserved,

\begin{split}&\;\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x)\big(F_{0}(x)-F(x,t_{*})\big)\,dx\right|\\
\leq&\;C_{\alpha}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha}}\,dt\\
\leq&\;C_{\alpha}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big(t_{*}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{1-5\alpha}{2}}\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

By the equivalent definition of the
1
-Wasserstein distance (see
[
5
, Chapter 6]
)

W_{1}(F_{0},F(\cdot,t_{*}))=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x)\big(F_{0}(x)-F(x,t_{*})\big)\,dx,

we conclude.
∎

6.
Global Well-posedness

6.1.
Well-posedness for band-limited positive initial data

First we consider positive and band-limited initial data
f_{0}
; the latter assumption means that
f_{0}
is a finite linear combination of Fourier modes.
To prove the well-posedness in this case, it might be feasible to turn the equation (
1.1
) back to its Lagrangian formulation (cf. (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)), and argue as in
[
1
,
2
]
.
Indeed, we can recover the string configuration
X_{0}(s)
from
f_{0}(x)
through (
2.9
) (also see the proof of Corollary
2.1
in Section
6.2
), and
X_{0}
should be smooth and satisfy the well-stretched assumption (cf. Remark
3.2
).
Nonetheless, here we present a more straightforward approach by taking advantage of the special nonlinearity of (
1.1
).

Define the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as in (
1.6
).
Denote
\bar{f}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)\,dx=\mathcal{F}(f)_{0}
.
We write (
1.1
) as

\partial_{t}f+\bar{f}(t)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f=\mathcal{H}f\cdot\partial_{x}f-(f-\bar{f})\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f.

Taking the Fourier transform on both sides yields, for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{m}+|m|\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{m}=&\;\sum_{n\neq m}-i\mathrm{sgn}(m-n)\hat{f}_{m-n}\cdot in\hat{f}_{n}-\hat{f}_{m-n}\cdot|n|\hat{f}_{n}\\
=&\;-\sum_{n\neq 0,m}\big(1-\mathrm{sgn}(m-n)\mathrm{sgn}(n)\big)\hat{f}_{m-n}\cdot|n|\hat{f}_{n}.\end{split}

Given
n\neq 0,m
, we observe that
\mathrm{sgn}(m-n)\mathrm{sgn}(n)\neq 1
only when
(m-n)
and
n
have opposite signs, which implies
\max\{|m-n|,|n|\}>|m|
.
Hence, for
m\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{m}=&\;-m\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{m}-\sum_{\max\{|m-n|,|n|\}>m}2\hat{f}_{m-n}\cdot|n|\hat{f}_{n}\\
=&\;-m\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{m}-\sum_{n<0}2\big(|n|+|m-n|\big)\hat{f}_{m-n}\hat{f}_{n},\end{split}

i.e.,

(6.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{m}=-m\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{m}-\sum_{n>0}2(m+2n)\hat{f}_{m+n}\overline{\hat{f}_{n}}.

The equation for
\hat{f}_{m}
with
m<0
can be obtained by taking the complex conjugate.
Formally, this immediately implies that, if there exists some
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{F}(f_{0})_{k}=0
for all
|k|>K
,
then
\hat{f}_{k}(t)\equiv 0
for all
t\geq 0
and
|k|>K
, and moreover,
\hat{f}_{\pm K}^{\prime}(t)=-K\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{\pm K}(t)
.
Hence, we have the following well-posedness result.

Proposition 6.1

(Proposition
1.1
)
.

Suppose
f_{0}>0
is band-limited, i.e., there exists some
K\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\mathcal{F}(f_{0})_{k}=0
for all
|k|>K
.
Then (
1.1
) has a global strong solution
f=f(x,t)
that is also positive and band-limited, such that
\hat{f}_{k}(t)=0
for all
|k|>K
and
t\geq 0
.
Such a solution is unique, and it can be determined by a finite family of ODEs among (
6.1
) with the initial condition
\hat{f}_{k}(0)=\mathcal{F}(f_{0})_{k}
.

Proof.

As we pointed out, it is enough to solve the ODEs for
\hat{f}_{k}(t)
with
k\in[-K,K]
(see (
6.1
)) and simply put
\hat{f}_{k}(t)\equiv 0
if
|k|>K
.
The local well-posedness of the ODEs is trivial.
The local ODE solution can be extended to the time interval
[0,+\infty)
thanks to the boundedness of
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}
(see Lemma
3.1
) as well as the Paserval’s identity.
Positivity of
f
follows from Lemma
3.2
.
∎

6.2.
Weak solutions for initial data in the energy class

Now we turn to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Recall that we call
f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
an initial data in the energy class in the view of Remark
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(part).

In this part of the proof, we will establish existence of a global weak solution to (
1.1
), and then show it enjoys the properties (
1
)-(
4
) in Theorem
1.1
.
The remaining property (
5
) will be studied in Section
7
(see Corollary
7.1
).

To obtain weak solutions corresponding to general initial data, we shall first approximate them by smooth solutions, and then pass to the limit in the weak formulation (
1.5
).
In this process, we will need the Fejér kernel on
\mathbb{T}

[
61
, Chapter 1]

(6.2)

\mathscr{F}_{N}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi N}\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{N}{2}x)}{\sin^{2}\frac{x}{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{|k|<N}\left(1-\frac{|k|}{N}\right)e^{ikx}.

For any given
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathscr{F}_{N}
is non-negative and band-limited, having integral
1
on
\mathbb{T}
.

\{\mathscr{F}_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
is known to be an approximation of identity on
\mathbb{T}
.

We proceed in several steps.

Step 1

(Approximate solutions)
.

Given
f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with
F_{0}=\frac{1}{f_{0}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, define their cut-offs for
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
that

f_{0,M}:=\max\big\{\min\{f_{0},M\},M^{-1}\big\},\quad F_{0,M}=\big(f_{0,M}\big)^{-1}.

Then
f_{0,M},F_{0,M}\in[M^{-1},M]
on
\mathbb{T}
.
Clearly, as
M\to+\infty
,
f_{0,M}\to f_{0}
and
F_{0,M}\to F_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
Next, for
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we let

f_{0,M,N}:=\mathscr{F}_{N}*f_{0,M},\quad F_{0,M,N}:=\big(f_{0,M,N}\big)^{-1}.

Then
f_{0,M,N}
is positive and band-limited;
f_{0,M,N}\in[M^{-1},M]
on
\mathbb{T}
.
As
N\to+\infty
,
f_{0,M,N}\to f_{0,M}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. In particular, we have
\|f_{0,M,N}\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}
for all
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Moreover,
F_{0,M,N}\to F_{0,M}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as
N\to+\infty
, because

|F_{0,M,N}-F_{0,M}|\leq M^{2}|f_{0,M,N}-f_{0,M}|
.
We also have
\|F_{0,M,N}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}
for all
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
This is because, by Jensen’s inequality,

F_{0,M,N}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathscr{F}_{N}*f_{0,M}(x)}\leq\left[\mathscr{F}_{N}*\left(\frac{1}{f_{0,M}}\right)\right](x)=\mathscr{F}_{N}*F_{0,M}(x).

By Proposition
6.1
, there exists a positive and band-limited
f_{M,N}
that is a strong solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0,M,N}
.
Define
F_{M,N}=(f_{M,N})^{-1}
.
Hence, for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty))
and arbitrary
0<\delta\ll 1
,

(6.3)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(x,0)F_{0,M,N}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,\delta]}+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[\delta,+\infty)}\big(\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f_{M,N}-\partial_{t}\varphi\big)F_{M,N}\,dx\,dt.

Step 2

(Taking the limit in
N
)
.

Since
\|f_{0,M,N}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0,M,N}\|_{L^{1}}
are uniformly bounded in
N
, by Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
5.2
, there exist smooth functions
f_{M}
and
F_{M}
defined on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty)
, and subsequences
\{f_{M,N_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\{F_{M,N_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, such that,
as
j\to+\infty
,
f_{M,N_{j}}\to f_{M}
and
F_{M,N_{j}}\to F_{M}
in
C_{loc}^{k}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty))
(
\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}
), and moreover,
f_{M,N_{j}}\rightharpoonup f_{M}
in
L^{2}([0,+\infty);H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
.
It is then not difficult to verify the following facts.

(a)

F_{M}=\frac{1}{f_{M}}
on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty)
; by Lemma
3.2
,
f_{M},F_{M}\in[M^{-1},M]
on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty)
.

(b)

For all
t>0
,

\|F_{M}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|F_{M,N_{j}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|F_{0,M,N_{j}}\|_{L^{1}}=\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}.

(c)

For any
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

(6.4)

\frac{1}{2}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t_{2})\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|f_{M}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}.

Indeed, one first proves an identical energy relation for
f_{M,N_{j}}
(cf. (
3.1
)), and then send
j\to+\infty
.
This in particular implies the energy inequality, i.e., for all
t>0
,

\frac{1}{2}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{M}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}.

We will show later that the energy equality is actually achieved (see (
6.7
) below).

(d)

With
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
fixed, the estimates in Corollary
5.1
hold uniformly for all
f_{M,N_{j}}
and
f_{M}
, where
f_{0}
and
F_{0}
there should be replaced by
f_{0,M}
and
F_{0,M}
respectively.
Let us explain the estimates for
f_{M}
.
Indeed, (
5.9
) follows from the convergence
f_{M,N_{j}}\to f_{M}
at all positive times, and then the proof of (
5.12
) for
f_{M}
uses the property (
c
) above and a similar argument as in Corollary
5.1
.
As a consequence,
\mathcal{H}f_{M}\cdot F_{M}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty))
.

(e)

With
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
fixed, the estimate (
5.21
) in Proposition
5.2
holds uniformly for all
f_{M,N_{j}}
and
f_{M}
, again with
f_{0}
and
F_{0}
there replaced by
f_{0,M}
and
F_{0,M}
.
Observe that
F_{0,M,N}\to F_{0,M}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
implies
f_{\infty,M,N}\to f_{\infty,M}
(they are defined by (
1.9
) in terms of
f_{0,M,N}
and
f_{0,M}
respectively).
Hence,
f_{M}
converges to
f_{\infty,M}
exponentially as
t\to+\infty
.

Now in (
6.3
), we take
N
to be
N_{j}
and send
j\to+\infty
.
Note that
\delta
is arbitrary in (
6.3
).
By virtue of (
d
) above, as
\delta\to 0^{+}
,

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,\delta]}\big(\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f_{M,N_{j}}-\partial_{t}\varphi\big)F_{M,N_{j}}\,dx\,dt\mbox{ and }\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,\delta]}\big(\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f_{M}-\partial_{t}\varphi\big)F_{M}\,dx\,dt

both converge to

.
Hence,

(6.5)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(x,0)F_{0,M}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f_{M}\cdot F_{M}-\partial_{t}\varphi\cdot F_{M}\,dx\,dt,

Therefore,
f_{M}
is a global weak solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0,M}
.

We further confirm that
F_{M}
takes the “initial data”
F_{0,M}
in a more quantitative sense.
For
t>0
,

\begin{split}&\;W_{1}\left(\frac{F_{0,M}}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}},\frac{F_{M}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}}\right)\\
\leq&\;W_{1}\left(\frac{F_{0,M}}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}},\frac{F_{0,M,N_{j}}}{\|F_{0,M,N_{j}}\|_{L^{1}}}\right)+W_{1}\left(\frac{F_{M,N_{j}}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M,N_{j}}\|_{L^{1}}},\frac{F_{M}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}}\right)\\
&\;+W_{1}\left(\frac{F_{0,M,N_{j}}}{\|F_{0,M,N_{j}}\|_{L^{1}}},\frac{F_{M,N_{j}}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M,N_{j}}\|_{L^{1}}}\right).\end{split}

The first two terms on the right-hand side converge to zero as

because of strong

Thanks to Lemma
5.6
with e.g.

, the last term above satisfies that,
for arbitrary

and

(6.6)

W_{1}\left(\frac{F_{0,M}}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}},\frac{F_{M}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}}\right)\leq C\big(t\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}\big)^{\frac{9}{20}}

for all
t\in(0,\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1}]
.
This further implies
[
5
]
, as

,

\frac{F_{M}(\cdot,t)}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}}\mbox{ converges weakly in }\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})\mbox{ to }\frac{F_{0,M}}{\|F_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}},

where

denotes the space of probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
Since
|F_{M}|\leq M
, we further have that, for any

converges weakly in

to

as

.
Since

is convex on

,

\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}
(cf.
[

, Chapter 2]
).
This together with (

) implies that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}
and therefore, for any

,

(6.7)

\frac{1}{2}\|f_{M}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{M}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0,M}\|_{L^{1}}.

Step 3

(Taking the limit in
M
)
.

By virtue of the properties of
f_{0,M}
and
F_{0,M}
, as well as the uniform bounds for
f_{M}
and
F_{M}
proved above, there exist smooth functions
f
and
F
defined on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty)
, and subsequences
\{f_{M_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{F_{M_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that,
as

,
f_{M_{i}}\to f
and

in

(

), and moreover,

.
One can similarly verify:

(a’)

on

.

(b’)

For all

,
\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.

(c’)

For any

,

(6.8)

\frac{1}{2}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

(d’)

The estimates in Corollary

hold for

.
Hence,
\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty))
.

(e’)

The estimate (

) in Proposition

holds for

, i.e.,

exponentially as

6.3.
Uniqueness of the dissipative weak solutions

As is mentioned before, to prove uniqueness, we need to focus on the dissipative weak solutions that satisfy more assumptions.

Remark 6.1
.

The inequality (
1.10
) in the definition of the dissipative weak solutions is motivated by the following calculation.
Suppose
f
is a positive strong solution to (
1.1
).
Then
F:=\frac{1}{f}
verifies

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(F(x,t))\,dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi^{\prime}(F)\partial_{t}F\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\Phi^{\prime}(F))\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\Phi^{\prime\prime}(F)\partial_{x}F\cdot\mathcal{H}f\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\big(F\Phi^{\prime}(F)-\Phi(F)\big)\cdot\mathcal{H}f\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\Phi(F)-F\Phi^{\prime}(F)\big)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx.\end{split}

For the smooth positive solution
F
, taking
\Phi(y)=\frac{1}{y}
yields the energy estimate (
3.1
).
Taking
\Phi(y)=y\ln y
yields the entropy estimate (
5.2
).
In general, when
\Phi\in C^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty))
is convex,
\Phi(y)-y\Phi^{\prime}(y)
is non-increasing in
y
.
Hence, using
F=\frac{1}{f}
and a derivation similar to the previous equation, we find

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\Phi(F)-F\Phi^{\prime}(F)\big)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx\geq 0.

We first show that the dissipative weak solutions are bounded from above and from below if the initial data has such properties.

Lemma 6.1
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.2
, let
f=f(x,t)
be a dissipative weak solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
.
Then for all
t\in(0,T)
,

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})},\quad\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}.

As a result,
\partial_{x}f,\partial_{x}F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,T))
with a priori estimates depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\|\ln f_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
.

Proof.

We choose in (
1.10
)

\Phi(y)=\begin{cases}\big(y-\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1}\big)^{2},&\mbox{if }y<\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1},\\
0,&\mbox{if }y\in[\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1},\,\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}],\\
\big(y-\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{2},&\mbox{if }y>\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{cases}

Apparently,
\Phi\in C^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty))
is convex, so (
1.10
) gives that, for any
t\in(0,T)
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(F(x,t))\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\Phi(F)-F\Phi^{\prime}(F)\big)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f\,dx\,dt\leq 0.

The second term above is non-negative due to the convexity of
\Phi
(see Remark
6.1
).
Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi(F(x,t))\,dx\leq 0,

which further implies
F(\cdot,t)\in[\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}]
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.

The last claim follows from the first claim and the definition of the dissipative weak solution (also see (
5.11
)).
∎

\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f_{1}-f_{2})(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(F_{1}-F_{2})\,dx\\

6.13
) becomes

\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}(x,t_{*})\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}(x,t_{*})}{F_{2}(x,t_{*})}\right)dx+\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-2}\|(F_{1}-F_{2})(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}_{t_{*}}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\;-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}(F_{1}-F_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1}f_{2})\,dx\,dt-2\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{H}(f_{1}-f_{2})\cdot(F_{1}-F_{2})F_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{2}}\,dx\,dt\\
\leq&\;\frac{1}{2}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{4}_{t_{*}}L^{4}}^{2}\|f_{1}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{t_{*}}\dot{H}^{1}}+C\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{4}_{t_{*}}L^{4}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{4}_{t_{*}}L^{4}}\|F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t_{*}}L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{t_{*}}L^{2}}\\
\leq&\;C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{1}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{t_{*}}L^{2}}+\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{t_{*}}L^{2}}\right)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t_{*}}L^{2}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L_{t_{*}}^{2}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

Here
L_{t_{*}}^{p}
denotes the
L^{p}
-norm on the time interval
[0,t_{*}]
.
Applying Young’s inequality, we find that

(6.14)

\begin{split}&\;\left\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}\right)dx\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t_{*}}}+\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-2}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L_{t_{*}}^{2}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\;C\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L_{t_{*}}^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{1}}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt.\end{split}

Now we need a variation of Pinsker’s inequality for the relative entropy, under the condition that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with identical
L^{1}
-norms are both positive, bounded, and bounded away from zero.
The following argument is adapted from that in
[
63
]
(also see
[
64
]
).
Let
R:=\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}-1
.
Observe that
(1+x)\ln(1+x)-x\geq Cx^{2}
on any bounded interval in
(-1,+\infty)
, where
C
depends on the upper bound of the interval.
Since
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-1}\leq F_{i}\leq\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

(i=1,2)
, we derive that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}\right)dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{2}(1+R)\ln(1+R)-F_{2}R\,dx\geq C\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{2}R^{2}\,dx\\
=&\;C\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{2}^{-1}(F_{1}-F_{2})^{2}\,dx\geq C\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

where
C
essentially depends
on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Applying this to (
6.14
) yields

\begin{split}&\;\left\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}\right)dx\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t_{*}}}+\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{-2}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L_{t_{*}}^{2}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\;C\big(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|F_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\left\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}\right)dx\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t_{*}}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{1}}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt.\end{split}

Since
\|\partial_{x}\sqrt{f_{i}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}<+\infty
, we take
t_{*}
to be sufficiently small to conclude

\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\ln\left(\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}\right)dx=0\mbox{ on }[0,t_{*}],

and therefore,
F_{1}=F_{2}
on
[0,t_{*}]
.
Repeating this argument for finitely many times yields the uniqueness on the entire time interval
[0,T)
.

Therefore, we conclude the uniqueness of the dissipative weak solutions on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,+\infty)
.
∎

7.
Analyticity and Sharp Decay Estimates for Large Times

Let us introduce some notations.
Define the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as in (
1.6
).
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{m,1}}:=\sum_{k\neq 0}|k|^{m}|\hat{f}_{k}|.

With
\nu\geq 0
, we additionally define

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu}^{m,1}}:=\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{\nu|k|}|k|^{m}|\hat{f}_{k}|.

Clearly,
\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{0}^{m,1}}=\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{m,1}}
.

If
\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}
is suitably small compared to
f_{\infty}
, we can prove that the solution will become analytic in space-time instantly after the initial time.

Proposition 7.1
.

There exists a universal number
\epsilon>0
such that the following holds.
Suppose
f_{0}
in (
1.1
) is positive and smooth on
\mathbb{T}
, such that
\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}\leq\epsilon f_{\infty}
.
Let
f
be the unique strong solution to (
1.1
).
Then for all
t_{0}>0
,
f
is analytic in the space-time domain
\mathbb{T}\times(t_{0},+\infty)
.
In particular, for any
t\geq 0
,

(7.1)

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}.

Here

\nu(t):=\frac{1}{2}\ln\big[\theta+(1-\theta)\exp(2f_{\infty}t)\big],\quad\theta:=C_{*}f_{\infty}^{-1}\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}},

with
C_{*}
being a universal constant.
In fact, it would suffice to have required
\epsilon<C_{*}^{-1}
above.

Remark 7.1
.

Note that
\nu(t)\sim f_{\infty}t
for large
t
as long as
\epsilon<C_{*}^{-1}
.
In the view of the linearization of (
1.1
) around the equilibrium
f_{\infty}
, i.e.,

\partial_{t}f\approx-f_{\infty}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}f
,
the estimate (
7.1
) provides the sharp rate of analyticity and decay.

Proof.

First suppose
f_{0}
is band-limited (i.e., only finitely many Fourier coefficients of
f_{0}
are non-zero), and
f_{0}(x)\not\equiv f_{\infty}
.
It has been shown in Proposition
6.1
that there is a unique band-limited global solution
f(\cdot,t)
, which is automatically analytic.
In the view of Theorem
1.2
, it is also the unique strong solution starting from
f_{0}
.

To be more quantitative, we recall (
6.1
):
for all
k\geq 0
,

\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{k}=-k\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{k}-\sum_{j\geq 1}2(k+2j)\hat{f}_{k+j}\overline{\hat{f}_{j}}=:-k\bar{f}(t)\hat{f}_{k}-\hat{N}_{k}.

The case
k=0
corresponds to the energy estimate (
3.1
) in Lemma
3.1
,
while for all
k>0
,

\frac{d}{dt}\big|\hat{f}_{k}\big|=\frac{1}{2|\hat{f}_{k}|}\left(\hat{f}_{k}\frac{d}{dt}\overline{\hat{f}_{k}}+\overline{\hat{f}_{k}}\frac{d}{dt}\hat{f}_{k}\right)\leq-|k|\bar{f}(t)\big|\hat{f}_{k}\big|+\big|\hat{N}_{k}\big|.

This derivation is valid for
|\hat{f}_{k}|>0
, but it is not difficult to see the resulting inequality also holds when
|\hat{f}_{k}|=0
.
A similar inequality holds for negative
k
’s.
Summing over
k\neq 0
, with
\nu(t)\geq 0
to be determined, we derive that

(7.2)

\begin{split}&\;\frac{d}{dt}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}+\big(\bar{f}(t)-\nu^{\prime}(t)\big)\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{1,1}}\\
\leq&\;C\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{k\nu(t)}\sum_{j\geq 1}(k+2j)\big|\hat{f}_{k+j}(t)\big|\big|\hat{f}_{j}(t)\big|\\
\leq&\;C\sum_{j\geq 1}e^{-j\nu(t)}\big|\hat{f}_{j}(t)\big|\sum_{k\geq 1}(k+j)e^{(k+j)\nu(t)}\big|\hat{f}_{k+j}(t)\big|\\
\leq&\;\frac{1}{2}C_{*}e^{-2\nu(t)}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{1,1}},\end{split}

where
C_{*}>0
is a universal constant.
Note that, because
f
is a band-limited strong solution,
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}
is finite at all time and it is smooth in
t
as long as
\nu(t)
is finite and smooth.

Now let
\nu(t)
solve

(7.3)

f_{\infty}-\nu^{\prime}(t)=C_{*}e^{-2\nu(t)}\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}},\quad\nu(0)=0.

Denote
\theta:=C_{*}f_{\infty}^{-1}\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}
. Then
\mu(t):=e^{2\nu(t)}
solves

\mu^{\prime}(t)=2f_{\infty}\big(\mu(t)-\theta\big),\quad\mu(0)=1.

As long as
\theta<1
, or equivalently
\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}<C_{*}^{-1}f_{\infty}
, this equation has a unique positive solution
\mu(t)=\theta+(1-\theta)\exp(2f_{\infty}t)
on
[0,+\infty)
.
Then
\nu(t):=\frac{1}{2}\ln\mu(t)
satisfies (
7.3
).

With this
\nu(t)
, we claim that

(7.4)

\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

which further implies spatial analyticity of
f(\cdot,t)
for all
t>0
.
Indeed, if not, we let
t_{*}
denote the infimum of all times where (
7.4
) does not hold.
By the time-continuity of
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}
,
t_{*}
is positive, and the equality in (
7.4
) is achieved at time
t_{*}
.
This together with (
7.3
) and the fact
\bar{f}(t)\geq f_{\infty}
implies

\bar{f}(t)-\nu^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{*}e^{-2\nu(t)}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\quad\forall\,t\in[0,t_{*}].

Combining this with (
7.2
), we find

\|f(\cdot,t_{*})\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t_{*})}^{0,1}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}<2\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}},

which contradicts with the definition of
t_{*}
.

For smooth
f_{0}
that is not band-limited, we let
f
be the unique global solution corresponding to the initial data
f_{0}
, which is constructed in Section
6.2
.
Recall the Fejér kernel
\mathscr{F}_{n}
was defined in (
6.2
).
Let
f_{n}
be the unique band-limited global solution corresponding to the band-limited initial data
\mathscr{F}_{n}*f_{0}
.
Then with
\nu(t)
defined above, for arbitrary
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and all
t\geq 0
,

\sum_{j\neq 0}e^{\nu(t)\min\{|j|,m\}}|\mathcal{F}(f_{n})_{j}(t)|\leq\|f_{n}(\cdot,t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}<2\|\mathscr{F}_{n}*f_{0}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}}.

Similar to Section
6.2
, we can prove that, as
n\to+\infty
,
f_{n}\to f
in
C_{loc}^{k}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,+\infty))
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
In fact, we first obtain convergence along a subsequence, and then show the convergence should hold for the whole sequence due to the uniqueness of the solution
f
.
Hence, sending
n\to+\infty

and then letting
m\to+\infty
yields (
7.4
) again in this general case.

The space-time analyticity follows from the spatial analyticity of
f
and the equation (
1.1
).
∎

Corollary 7.1

(Property (
5
) of the weak solution constructed in Theorem
1.1
)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exists
T_{*}>0
depending only on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, such that the constructed solution
f
of (
1.1
) is analytic in the space-time domain
\mathbb{T}\times(T_{*},+\infty)
.
It satisfies that, for any
t>0
,

\|f(\cdot,t+T_{*})\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\nu(t)}^{0,1}}\leq Cf_{\infty},

with

\nu(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[1+\exp(2f_{\infty}t)\right]-\frac{1}{2}\ln 2,

and
C>0
being a universal constant.

Proof.

Using the notations in Proposition
7.1
, we take
\epsilon
there to satisfy
C_{*}\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
By the property (
4
) of the solution (also see Proposition
5.2
), there is
T_{*}>0
depending only on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, such that

\|f(\cdot,T_{*})\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{0,1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\|f(\cdot,T_{*})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\leq\epsilon f_{\infty}.

Here we used the embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{0,1}(\mathbb{T})
. Then we apply Proposition
7.1
to conclude.
In particular, since
\theta\leq\frac{1}{2}
in this case, the desired estimate for
\nu(t)
follows.
∎

Appendix A
An
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
-estimate for
F

It is worth mentioning another interesting estimate for
F
, although it is not used in the other parts of the paper.
For convenience, we assume
f
to be a positive strong solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)
.
Define

\bar{F}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F(x,t)\,dx.

By (
1.9
) and Lemma
3.1
,
\bar{F}=\frac{1}{f_{\infty}}
, which is a time-invariant constant.
Then we have the following estimate.

Lemma A.1
.

\frac{d}{dt}\big\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(F-\bar{F})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot(\mathcal{H}F)^{2}\,dx=2\pi\bar{F}-\bar{F}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,dx\leq 0.

Proof.

By the Cotlar’s identity (
5.3
) for mean-zero functions

(\mathcal{H}F)^{2}-\big(F-\bar{F}\big)^{2}=2\mathcal{H}\big((F-\bar{F})\mathcal{H}F\big).

Hence,

(\mathcal{H}F)^{2}-F^{2}-\bar{F}^{2}+2F\bar{F}=2\mathcal{H}(F\mathcal{H}F)-2\bar{F}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}F=2\mathcal{H}(F\mathcal{H}F)+2\bar{F}(F-\bar{F}),

which simplifies to

(A.1)

(\mathcal{H}F)^{2}-F^{2}+\bar{F}^{2}=2\mathcal{H}(F\mathcal{H}F).

Using this identity and the
F
-equation, we find that

\begin{split}&\;\frac{d}{dt}\big\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(F-\bar{F})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
=&\;2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}F\cdot(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(F-\bar{F})\,dx\\
=&\;-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\cdot\partial_{x}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(F-\bar{F})\,dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot\mathcal{H}\big(F\cdot\mathcal{H}F\big)\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot\big((\mathcal{H}F)^{2}-F^{2}+\bar{F}^{2}\big)\,dx\\
=&\;-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\cdot(\mathcal{H}F)^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\,dx-\bar{F}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,dx.\end{split}

Then the desired estimate follows by rearranging the equation and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

Under the additional assumption
F_{0}-\bar{F}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
, Lemma
A.1
implies
F-\bar{F}\in L^{\infty}_{T}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
f^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}F\in L^{2}_{T}L^{2}
.
Interestingly, this provides another way of making sense of the nonlinear term

\int_{\mathbb{T}\times[0,T)}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt

in the weak formulation (
1.5
) (cf. Section
5.1
).
Note that the additional assumption
F_{0}-\bar{F}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
has the same scaling as the assumption
F_{0}\in L^{1}
that we have been using.

Indeed, by Cotlar’s identity (
A.1
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\cdot(\mathcal{H}f)^{2}\,dx=&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\cdot\big((\mathcal{H}f)^{2}-f^{2}+\bar{f}^{2}\big)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,dx-\bar{f}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\,dx\\
\leq&\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}F\cdot 2\mathcal{H}(f\mathcal{H}f)\,dx=-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{H}F\cdot f\mathcal{H}f\,dx\\
\leq&\;2\big\|f^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}F\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|f^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{6}}\big\|\mathcal{H}f\big\|_{L^{3}}\\
\leq&\;C\big\|f^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}F\big\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{3}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

By the interpolation inequality
[
60
]
,

\|f\|_{L^{3}}^{3/2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{1/2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
A.1
, and the assumption
F_{0}-\bar{F}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain that
F^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}f\in L_{T}^{2}L^{2}
.

Then the facts
F^{\frac{1}{2}}\in L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}
and
F^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}f\in L_{T}^{2}L^{2}
allow us to well define the above nonlinear term in the weak formulation.
Alternatively, the facts
F-\bar{F}\in L_{T}^{\infty}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathcal{H}f\in L_{T}^{2}H^{\frac{1}{2}}
(see Lemma
3.1
) also suffice.
In either case, one can show that

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\varphi\cdot\mathcal{H}f\cdot F\,dx\,dt=O(t^{1/2}),

which may lead to a simpler proof of existence of the weak solutions.

Generalized Measure of Quantum Fisher Information
Akira Sone, M. Cerezo, Jacob L. Beckey, Patrick J. Coles

Abstract
In this work, we present a lower bound on the quantum Fisher information (QFI) which is efficiently computable on near-term quantum devices. This bound itself is of interest, as we show that it satisfies the canonical criteria of a QFI measure. Specifically, it is essentially a QFI measure for subnormalized states, and hence it generalizes the standard QFI in this sense. Our bound employs the generalized fidelity applied to a truncated state, which is constructed via the m m largest eigenvalues and their corresponding eigenvectors of the probe quantum state ρ θ \rho_{\theta} . Focusing on unitary families of exact states, we analyze the properties of our proposed lower bound, and demonstrate its utility for efficiently estimating the QFI.



I
Introduction

Quantum Fisher information (QFI)
Hayashi 2016
;
Liu et al. 2020
quantifies the ultimate precision with which one can estimate a parameter
\theta
from a
\theta
-dependent quantum state
\rho_{\theta}
via the quantum Cramér-Rao bound. This quantity is of fundamental importance for quantum metrology
Giovannetti et al. 2011
;
Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al. 2012
;
Degen et al. 2017
;
Pezzè et al. 2018
;
Meyer et al. 2021
. Moreover, the QFI has been studied in the context of quantum phase transitions
Wang et al. 2014
;
Ye et al. 2016
;
Macieszczak et al. 2016
, quantum information geometry
Amari and Nagaoka 2000
;
Fujiwara and Nagaoka 1995
, and quantum information
Pezzé and Smerzi 2009
;
Modi et al. 2011
;
Hyllus et al. 2012
;
Kim et al. 2018
;
Sone et al. 2018
;
Sone et al. 2019
;
Takeoka and Wilde
;
Katariya and Wilde 2021
.

The general definition of the QFI is

\displaystyle I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})={\rm Tr}[J_{\theta}^{2}\rho_{\theta}]\,,

(1)

where
J_{\theta}
is called symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) operator satisfying the following Lyapunov equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(J_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}+\rho_{\theta}J_{\theta})\,.

(2)

Also, the QFI is associated with the standard fidelity between the exact state
\rho_{\theta}
and the error state
\rho_{\theta+\delta}
as

\displaystyle I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})=8\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1-F(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta+\delta})}{\delta^{2}}\,,

(3)

where
F(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=|\!|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\sqrt{\rho_{2}}|\!|_{1}
is the standard fidelity, and with the trace norm given by
|\!|A|\!|_{1}={\rm Tr}[\sqrt{AA^{\dagger}}]
.

In spite of its theoretical significance, the QFI is in general a difficult quantity to compute. Calculating the SLD operator requires one to solve the Lyapunov equation, which in turn needs full knowledge of the exact state
\rho_{\theta}
, which is not always known in practice. In addition, when employing Eq. (
3
) to determine the QFI, one encounters the serious difficulty that there is no efficient algorithm to compute the fidelity between arbitrary states. The complexity of the classical algorithms for fidelity estimation can scale exponentially due to the exponentially large dimension of the density matrices with respect to the number of qubits
Cerezo et al. 2020a
. But even quantum algorithms face complexity theoretic arguments
Watrous
, and the fact that the nonlinear nature of fidelity implies that a finite number of copies of
\rho_{\theta}
cannot lead to an exact computation of the fidelity. Hence, instead of exactly computing the QFI, one can estimate the QFI by bounding it
Girolami 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2017
.

This is precisely the goal of this paper, where we introduce an efficiently computable lower bound for the QFI. Our bound is based on the truncated (and therefore subnormalized) state constructed by projecting the exact state
\rho_{\theta}
into the subspace of its
m
-largest eigenvalues. Particularly, we focus on the family of quantum states of the form
\rho_{\theta}=W(\theta)\rho W^{\dagger}(\theta)
, where
\rho
is called probe state, and we define
W(\theta)=e^{-i\theta G}
with a Hermitian and
\theta
-independent generator
G
. As in Ref.
Paris 2009
, we refer to the set of states of this form as a
unitary family
. This family of states is general enough to describe phase estimation tasks, such as magnetometry
Taylor et al. 2008
;
Bhattacharjee et al. 2020
;
Degen et al. 2017
.

Our results are derived by employing the concepts of generalized fidelity
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
and truncated states
Cerezo et al. 2020a
to construct an efficiently computable quantity which we call truncated quantum Fisher information (TQFI). Our main results are a series of lemmas that prove that TQFI lower bounds the standard QFI, and that TQFI satisfies various properties, including most of the canonical criteria for a measure of QFI. In addition, we also introduce a quantity that we call the generalized Bures distance, from which we provide a geometrical interpretation to the TQFI. We note that in our recent work
Beckey et al.
, we have proposed a trainable variational quantum algorithm to estimate QFI and further prepare the optimal state for phase estimation by using TQFI.

This paper is organized as follows. We first provide theoretical background in Sec.
II
. Then, Sec.
III
introduces the TQFI and its associated Hermitian SLD operator, and presents our main results. Finally, we offer some concluding remarks in Sec.
IV
.

II
Theoretical background

In this section we provide some theoretical background that will be useful to define the TQFI. Specifically, we discuss the generalized fidelity, a measure of distinguishability for subnormalized states. We then discuss how the generalized fidelity can be used to construct an upper bound for the standard fidelity. We remark that this bound will be the basis of the definition of the TQFI.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space. A quantum state
\rho
on
\mathcal{H}
is defined as a Hermitian, positive semi-definite operator of trace equal to 1. Hence, the set of normalized quantum states on
\mathcal{H}
can be defined as

\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})=\{\rho\,:\,\rho^{\dagger}=\rho\,,\,\rho\geqslant 0\,,\,{\rm Tr}[\rho]=1\}\,,

(4)

which forms a convex set with real dimension
(d^{2}-1)
. Relaxing the normalization condition, one arrives at the following definition.

Definition 1

(Subnormalized state)
.

A Hermitian, positive semi-definite operator
\tau
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a subnormalized quantum state if
{\rm Tr}[\tau]\leqslant 1
.

Definition
1
allows us to introduce
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
as the set of subnormalized states on
\mathcal{H}
, that is

\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})=\{\tau\,:\tau^{\dagger}=\tau\,,\,\tau\geqslant 0\,,\,{\rm Tr}[\tau]\leqslant 1\}\,.

(5)

As schematically shown in Fig.
1
, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
has dimensionality
d^{2}
, and can be obtained as the convex hull of the set of quantum states and the zero operator
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})=\text{Conv}(0,\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H}))

Cappellini et al. 2007
. subnormalized quantum states have been used in quantum information theory as a convenient generalization of normalized quantum states
Cappellini et al. 2007
;
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
. Moreover, exciting new work on near-term quantum algorithms utilizes truncated, and thus subnormalized, quantum states to avoid having to store an exponentially large density matrix, thus making the algorithms implementable on the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) computers
Cerezo et al.
;
Cerezo et al. 2020a
;
Beckey et al.
. This exciting new research direction is the primary motivation for this work.

In Refs.
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
the authors introduced a generalization of the standard quantum fidelity to subnormalized states, which is known as the
generalized fidelity
, and which is given as follows.

Definition 2

(Generalized fidelity)
.

Given two subnormalized states
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, the generalized fidelity between
\tau
and
\sigma
is

F_{*}(\tau,\sigma)=|\!|\sqrt{\tau}\sqrt{\sigma}|\!|_{1}+\sqrt{(1-{\rm Tr}[\tau])(1-{\rm Tr}[\sigma])}\,,

(6)

where
|\!|A|\!|_{1}={\rm Tr}[\sqrt{AA^{\dagger}}]
is the trace norm.

Note that the generalized fidelity reduces to the standard fidelity
F
if at least one of the two states is normalized. That is,

F_{*}(\tau,\sigma)=F(\tau,\sigma)=|\!|\sqrt{\tau}\sqrt{\sigma}|\!|_{1}\,,

(7)

if
\tau
or
\sigma
is in
\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})
.

Figure 1:
Set of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
for a subnormalized quantum state. Simplexes are shown for a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space with (a)
d=2
and (b)
d=3
. The eigenvalues of normalized states in
\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})
lie on a
(d^{2}-1)
-simplex: a line segment in (a), and a triangle in (b). The eigenvalues of a pure state lie on the edges of the simplex, while those of a mixed state are on the centroid of the simplex. The eigenvalues of subnormalized states in
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
can be obtained from the subnormalization condition
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\leqslant 1
. In the diagrams, the origin corresponds to the zero operator.

As shown in Refs.
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
;
Cerezo et al. 2020a
, the generalized fidelity has the following relevant properties:

•

Invariance under unitary transformations. Given two subnormalized states
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, and for any unitary
V
in the unitary group
U(d)
of degree
d
, we have

\displaystyle F_{*}(V\tau V^{\dagger},V\sigma V^{\dagger})=F_{*}(\tau,\sigma)\,.

(8)

•

Concavity. Given subnormalized states
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, and a real number
q\in[0,1]
, then

\begin{split}F_{*}(q\tau_{1}+&(1-q)\tau_{2},q\sigma_{1}+(1-q)\sigma_{2})\\
&\geqslant qF_{*}(\tau_{1},\sigma_{1})+(1-q)F_{*}(\tau_{2},\sigma_{2})\,.\end{split}

(9)

•

Monotonicity under completely positive trace non-increasing (CPTNI) maps. Given two subnormalized states
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, and a CPTNI map
\Phi
, then

\displaystyle F_{*}(\tau,\sigma)\leqslant F_{*}(\Phi(\tau),\Phi(\sigma))\,.

(10)

We note that CPTNI maps are the mathematical generalization of completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps, which become useful when one allows for subnormalized quantum states
Tomamichel 2015
;
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Cappellini et al. 2007
. Additionally, they have the physical interpretation of describing an experiment in which the measurement apparatus does not work with some probabilities
Cappellini et al. 2007
.

Let us now discuss how the generalized fidelity can be used to upper bound the standard fidelity. Consider a projection operator
\Pi
which maps states to a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. Note that
\Pi
defines a CPTNI map as
\Phi(\rho)=\Pi\rho\Pi
which maps states in
\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})
and in
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
to subnormalized states in
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
. Then, from the monotonicity under CPTNI maps of the generalized fidelity, the following bound on the standard fidelity
F(\rho,\widetilde{\rho})
holds for any pair of normalized states
\rho
and
\widetilde{\rho}

Cerezo et al. 2020a
:

F(\rho,\widetilde{\rho})\leqslant F_{*}(\Pi\,\rho\,\Pi,\Pi\,\widetilde{\rho}\,\Pi)\,.

(11)

In Ref.
Cerezo et al. 2020a
, the authors proposed an algorithm that can efficiently compute the upper bound in Eq. (
11
) for certain
\Pi
. Specifically, in that work,
\Pi
is the operator that projects onto the Hilbert space spanned by the eigenvectors of the
m
-largest eigenvalues of
\rho
. That is, given the spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}|
, we define

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rho}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}|\,.

(12)

This operator allows us to introduce the truncated states
\rho^{(m)}
and
\widetilde{\rho}^{(m)}
:

\begin{split}\rho^{(m)}&=\Pi_{\rho}^{m}\rho\Pi_{\rho}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}|\,,\\
\widetilde{\rho}^{(m)}&=\Pi_{\rho}^{m}\widetilde{\rho}\Pi_{\rho}^{m}\,,\end{split}

(13)

which leads to the following expression of the generalized fidelity for these states:

F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}\!,\widetilde{\rho}^{(m)})={\rm Tr}\left[\!\sqrt{T}\right]+\sqrt{(1\!-\!{\rm Tr}[\rho^{(m)}])(1\!-\!{\rm Tr}[\widetilde{\rho}^{(m)}])}\,.

(14)

Here,
T
is a positive semi-definite
m\times m
operator given by

T=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\langle\lambda_{i}\hskip 1.0pt|\widetilde{\rho}|\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{j}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{i}\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}\hskip 1.0pt|\,.

(15)

Finally, let us remark that the upper bound
F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}\!,\widetilde{\rho}^{(m)})\geqslant F(\rho,\widetilde{\rho})
gets monotonically tighter with
m
, with equality holding if
m=\text{rank}(\rho)

Cerezo et al. 2020a
.

III
Truncated Quantum Fisher Information

From the discussions above, in this section, we introduce a generalized measure of the QFI definable with the subnormalized state, which we call TQFI, and show that it is a lower bound on the standard QFI. We then present some of its properties in the form of lemmas, which we prove in the Appendices, and present its geometrical interpretation in the space of subnormalized states.

III.1
Definition of the TQFI

Consider the (normalized) exact state
\rho_{\theta}
, and the (normalized) error state
\rho_{\theta+\delta}
. These states encode the information of an unknown parameter
\theta
and of a shift
\delta
in a probe state
\rho
of rank
r
as

\begin{split}\rho_{\theta}&=W(\theta)\rho W^{\dagger}(\theta)\,,\\
\rho_{\theta+\delta}&=W(\theta+\delta)\rho W^{\dagger}(\theta+\delta)\,,\end{split}

(16)

with

W(\theta)=e^{-i\theta G}\,,

(17)

where
G
is a
\theta
-independent Hermitian operator.
Given the spectral decomposition of the exact state
\rho_{\theta}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|
with
|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle=W(\theta)|\lambda_{i}\rangle
, we define the operator that projects onto the Hilbert space spanned by the eigenvectors corresponding to the
m
-largest eigenvalues of
\rho_{\theta}
as
\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|
. Then, we define the truncated (subnormalized) states

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}&=\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}\rho_{\theta}\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|\,,\\
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta}&=\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}\rho_{\theta+\delta}\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}\,.\end{split}

(18)

Finally, we have the following definition for the TQFI.

Definition 3

(Truncated quantum Fisher information)
.

Given an exact state
\rho_{\theta}
and error state
\rho_{\theta+\delta}
in
\mathcal{S}_{=}(\mathcal{H})
, let
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}
and
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta}
denote their truncated versions according to (
18
) such that
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
. The truncated quantum Fisher information is

\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})=8\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1-F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})}{\delta^{2}}\,.

(19)

III.2
TQFI as a lower bound

From Eq. (
11
) we have that the following lemma holds.

Lemma 1
.

The TQFI of Definition
3
is a lower bound for the QFI

\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})\leqslant I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})\,,

(20)

where
I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})
is the QFI defined in (
3
).
In addition, the TQFI is monotonically increasing with
m
, i.e.,

\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})\leqslant\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m+1)}_{\theta})\,,

(21)

with the equality in (
20
) holding if
m=r
, where
r=\text{rank}(\rho)
.

Lemma
1
provides an operational meaning of the TQFI as a lower bound on the standard QFI. We remark that since the generalized fidelity is a tight bound for high purity states, the TQFI will also be a tight bound on the QFI in this case.

III.3
Computation of TQFI

Let us briefly discuss how the TQFI can be computed. We refer the reader to our work
Beckey et al.
for a much more detailed description of the estimation of TQFI. As previously mentioned, the generalized fidelity can be efficiently computed for
m\in O(\operatorname{poly}(\log(d)))
via a variational hybrid quantum-classical algorithm
Cerezo et al. 2020b
called the variational quantum fidelity estimation algorithm in Ref.
Cerezo et al. 2020a
, which uses state diagonalization as a subroutine
LaRose et al. 2019
;
Cerezo et al.
. Assuming this state diagonalization subroutine is efficient, it follows that one can efficiently approximate the TQFI and lower bound the QFI by using the algorithm in Ref.
Cerezo et al. 2020a
and computing
\left(1-F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})\right)/\delta^{2}
for small
\delta
.

III.4
Properties of the TQFI

To further understand the meaning of the TQFI, it is useful to express this quantity in the representation of the eigenbasis of
\rho
.

Lemma 2
.

The TQFI of Definition
3
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}|G_{ij}|^{2}-8\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|G_{ij}|^{2}

(22)

where
G_{ij}=\langle\lambda_{i}\hskip 1.0pt|G|\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{j}\rangle
, and where we recall that
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>r
.

Recalling that the standard QFI can be expanded in the eigenbasis of
\rho
as
Tóth and Apellaniz 2014

I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})=4\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|G_{ij}|^{2}-8\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|G_{ij}|^{2}

(23)

with again
\lambda_{i,j}=0
for
i,j>r
, we can see that the first two terms in (
22
) are simply obtained by truncating the summations of (
23
) so that
i,j=1,\cdots,m
; and while this may seem like the natural way to generalize the QFI to subnormalized states, the derivation of Eq. (
22
) and the proofs of the properties required for it to satisfy the canonical criteria of a QFI measure are non-trivial. Before listing these important properties, let us consider an alternative definition for the TQFI by introducing the truncated symmetric logarithmic derivative (TSLD).

Definition 4

(Truncated symmetric logarithmic derivative operator)
.

Given a subnormalized truncated exact state
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
defined according to (
18
), the TQFI of Definition
3
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})={\rm Tr}\left[L_{\theta}^{2}\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}\right]\,,

(24)

where

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}^{(m)}|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|\,

(25)

is the TSLD operator. For the unitary families, we particularly have

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=2i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\langle\lambda_{i}|G|\lambda_{j}\rangle|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|\,.

(26)

As we can see, the TSLD is simply obtained by truncating the summation of the SLD operator.

From the previous definitions and lemmas we can derive the following properties of the TQFI.

Lemma 3
.

From the definition of the TQFI, for the unitary families
\rho_{\theta}=W(\theta)\rho W^{\dagger}(\theta)
,
\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})
satisfies the following properties:

•

Invariance under unitary transformations. Given a truncated subnormalized state
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, for any
\theta
-independent unitary
V
in the unitary group
U(d)
of degree
d
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,V\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}V^{\dagger})=\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})\,.

(27)

•

Convexity. For two truncated subnormalized states
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\xi^{(m^{\prime})}_{\theta}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
with
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}=\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}\rho_{\theta}\Pi_{\rho_{\theta}}^{m}
and
\xi^{(m^{\prime})}_{\theta}=\Pi_{\xi_{\theta}}^{m^{\prime}}\xi_{\theta}\Pi_{\xi_{\theta}}^{m^{\prime}}
, with a real number
q\in[0,1]
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;&q\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}+(1-q)\xi^{(m^{\prime})}_{\theta})\\
&\leqslant q\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})+(1-q)\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\xi^{(m^{\prime})}_{\theta})\,.\end{split}

(28)

•

Monotonicity under CPTNI maps. Given a truncated subnormalized state
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, and a CPTNI map
\Phi
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\Phi(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}))\leqslant\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})\,.

(29)

•

Subadditivity for product of truncated states. Given a product of truncated states
\sigma=\bigotimes_{k}\rho^{(m_{k})}_{k,\theta}
, where
\rho^{(m_{k})}_{k,\theta}=\Pi_{\rho_{k,\theta}}^{m_{k}}\rho_{k,\theta}\Pi_{\rho_{k,\theta}}^{m_{k}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\sigma)\leqslant\sum_{k}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m_{k})}_{k,\theta})\,.

(30)

•

Additivity for direct sum of truncated states. Given a direct sum of truncated states
\sigma=\bigoplus_{k}\mu_{k}\rho^{(m)}_{k,\theta}
, where
\rho^{(m_{k})}_{k,\theta}=\Pi_{\rho_{k,\theta}}^{m_{k}}\rho_{k,\theta}\Pi_{\rho_{k,\theta}}^{m_{k}}
, and where
\mu_{k}
are
\theta
-independent coefficients such that
0<\sum_{k}\mu_{k}\leqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\sigma)=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m_{k})}_{k,\theta})\,.

(31)

Note that the TQFI satisfies the same properties as those that the standard QFI satisfies (see Ref.
Liu et al. 2020
for a review of the properties of the QFI), except for the additivity for product of states. Here, the TQFI satisfies instead a subadditivity property which naturally follows from the fact that the states are subnormalized.

Let us finally discuss the geometric interpretation of the TQFI. From Eq. (
19
) we first define the
generalized Bures distance
.

Definition 5

(Generalized Bures Distance)
.

Given two subnormalized states
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, the generalized Bures distance is

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\tau,\sigma)=2(1-F_{*}(\tau,\sigma))\,.

(32)

Here we remark that the generalized Bures distance is closely related to the purified distance for subnormalized states introduced in Refs.
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
. Hence, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 4
.

Given two subnormalized states
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
, the generalized Bures distance
B_{*}^{2}(\tau,\sigma)
is a distance metric on the space of subnormalized states.

Then, for the truncated exact state
\rho^{(m)}_{\theta}
defined in (
18
), we can obtain the following result.

Lemma 5
.

Let
B_{*}^{2}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})
be the generalized Bures distance. Then, for
|\delta|\ll 1
, we have

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3})\,.

(33)

Lemma
5
provides a geometrical interpretation for the TQFI as being related to the curvature of the generalized Bures distance in the space of subnormalized states.

IV
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have introduced the TQFI, which is demonstrated to be an efficiently computable lower bound on the quantum Fisher information. This quantity can be used for estimating QFI and to prepare the optimal state for metrology via the variational quantum algorithms on the near-term quantum computers. Specifically, the TQFI can be obtained from the generalized fidelity between the states obtained by projecting the exact state
\rho_{\theta}
and error state
\rho_{\theta+\delta}
onto the subspace spanned by the largest
m
eigenvalues of
\rho_{\theta}
. For unitary families, we have proven that the TQFI satisfies the criteria of the quantum Fisher information for subnormalized states. In addition, we have revealed the geometrical interpretation of the TQFI by introducing a generalized Bures distance, a distance measure on subnormalized states.

This lower bound can be employed to efficiently estimate the quantum Fisher information. This is especially useful in the context of quantum sensing, where one is interested in maximizing the quantum Fisher information. Hence, one can use our lower bound as a means to prepare states that maximize quantum Fisher information, to enhance sensing performance of the quantum sensors. Moreover, the quantum Fisher information is often used to witness metrologically useful entanglement
Hyllus et al. 2012
;
Qin et al. 2019
; therefore, an interesting future research direction will be exploring the use of TQFI for the entanglement witness in condensed matter systems.

Note.
Recently, Yamagata
Yamagata 2020
studied the properties of the quantum monotone metrics under the CPTNI maps. We note that TQFI belongs to this class.

Appendix

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

The fact that the TQFI is a lower bound on the QFI follows directly from the fact that the generalized fidelity is an upper bound for the fidelity. Recall the definition of the QFI and the TQFI, which are respectively defined as

\displaystyle I(\theta;\rho_{\theta})=8\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1-F(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta+\delta})}{\delta^{2}}\,,\quad\quad\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho^{(m)}_{\theta})=8\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1-F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})}{\delta^{2}}\,.

(34)

From the fact that

F(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta+\delta})\leqslant F_{*}(\rho^{(m)}_{\theta},\rho^{(m)}_{\theta+\delta})\,,

(35)

(37)

Appendix C
Truncated Symmetric Logarithmic Derivative

The standard QFI can be defined in terms of the so-called SLD operator. For the state
\rho_{\theta}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|
, the standard SLD operator is
Paris 2009

\displaystyle J_{\theta}=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|.

(63)

Analogously, we can also define the TQFI through a truncated SLD (TSLD) operator. Let the spectral decomposition of our truncated exact state be given as

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|,

(64)

which the parameter dependence left implicit to simplify notation. Then, the TSLD operator is

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|.

(65)

One can easily verify that
\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=i[\tau_{\theta},G]
, so that the explicit form of the TSLD operator becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{\theta}&=2i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|G|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|\\
&=2i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\langle\lambda_{i}|G|\lambda_{j}\rangle W(\theta)|\lambda_{i}\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}|W^{\dagger}(\theta),\end{split}

(66)

where we used the fact that
|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle=W(\theta)|\lambda_{j}\rangle
and
[W(\theta),G]=0
.
Taking the conjugate transpose and then exchanging
i
and
j
, we can easily verify that
L_{\theta}
is Hermitian, i.e.,
L_{\theta}=L_{\theta}^{\dagger}
. In addition to Hermiticity, the justification for regarding
L_{\theta}
as an SLD operator comes from the following propositions.

Proposition 1
.

For a
\theta
-parametrized subnormalized state
\tau_{\theta}=W(\theta)\tau W^{\dagger}(\theta)
, the TSLD operator satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}+\tau_{\theta}L_{\theta})\,,

(67)

with
{\rm Tr}[L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}]=0\,.

Proof.

First, using the fact that
\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=i[\tau_{\theta},G]
and
\tau_{\theta}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle=\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle
, we can write

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|[\tau_{\theta},G]|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|,

(68)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\langle\lambda_{i}|G|\lambda_{j}\rangle|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|.

(69)

Then, making use of the explicit expansions of
\tau_{\theta}
and
L_{\theta}
in the eigenbasis of
\tau_{\theta}
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}+\tau_{\theta}L_{\theta})

\displaystyle=i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\right)\langle\lambda_{i}(\theta)|G|\lambda_{j}(\theta)\rangle(|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|\tau_{\theta}+\tau_{\theta}|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|)

(70)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}\right)\langle\lambda_{i}|G|\lambda_{j}\rangle(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|

(71)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\langle\lambda_{i}|G|\lambda_{j}\rangle|\lambda_{i}(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda_{j}(\theta)|\,.

(72)

Comparing these two expressions, we see that indeed

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}+\tau_{\theta}L_{\theta}),

(73)

as is required of a well-defined SLD operator. Finally, because
{\rm Tr}[\tau_{\theta},G]=0
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}]=i{\rm Tr}[\tau_{\theta},G]={\rm Tr}\left[\frac{1}{2}(L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}+\tau_{\theta}L_{\theta})\right]={\rm Tr}[L_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}]=0\,.

(74)

∎

Proposition 2
.

The TQFI
\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})={\rm Tr}[L_{\theta}^{2}\tau_{\theta}]\,.

Appendix D
Proof of Lemma
3

In the following, we prove each property in Lemma
3
.

1.

Invariance under unitary transformations:
Given a unitary
V\in U(d)
which is
\theta
-independent, since the generalized fidelity
Tomamichel 2015
is unitary-invariant, i.e.,

\displaystyle F_{*}(V\tau_{\theta}V^{\dagger},V\tau_{\theta+\delta}V^{\dagger})=F_{*}(\tau_{\theta},\tau_{\theta+\delta})\,,

(88)

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;V\tau_{\theta}V^{\dagger})=\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})\,.

(89)

2.

Convexity:
Let
\tau_{\theta}
and
\xi_{\theta}
be subnormalized states. Since the generalized fidelity is jointly concave
Tomamichel 2015
, we have

\displaystyle F_{*}\Big(q\tau_{\theta}+(1-q)\xi_{\theta},q\tau_{\theta+\delta}+(1-q)\xi_{\theta+\delta}\Big)\geqslant qF_{*}(\tau_{\theta},\tau_{\theta+\delta})+(1-q)F_{*}(\xi_{\theta},\xi_{\theta+\delta})\,.

(90)

Hence, for all
\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;q\tau_{\theta}+(1-q)\xi_{\theta})\leqslant q\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})+(1-q)\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\xi_{\theta})\,.

(91)

3.

Monotonicity under CPTNI map:
Here, we employ the monotonicity of the generalized fidelity
Tomamichel 2015
:

\displaystyle F_{*}(\tau_{\theta},\tau_{\theta+\delta})\leqslant F_{*}(\Phi(\tau_{\theta}),\Phi(\tau_{\theta+\delta}))

(92)

for a CPTNI map
\Phi
.
For all
\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1-F_{*}(\tau_{\theta},\tau_{\theta+\delta})}{\delta^{2}}\geqslant\frac{1-F_{*}(\Phi(\tau_{\theta}),\Phi(\tau_{\theta+\delta}))}{\delta^{2}}\,,

(93)

so that we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})\geqslant\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\Phi(\tau_{\theta}))\,.

(94)

4.

Subadditivity for product of truncated states:
Consider a subnormalized state obtained from a tensor product of subnormalized state
\tau_{\theta}=\bigotimes_{k}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}
. We have

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=\sum_{k}\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{\overline{(k)}}=\sum_{k}\frac{L_{k,\theta}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}+\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}L_{k,\theta}}{2}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{\overline{(k)}}\,,

(95)

where we define

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}^{\overline{(k)}}=\bigotimes_{j\neq k}\tau_{\theta}^{(j)}\,.

(96)

Therefore, the TSLD operator becomes

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=\sum_{k}L_{k,\theta}\otimes\openone_{\overline{k}}\,.

(97)

Then, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})={\rm Tr}[L_{\theta}^{2}\tau_{\theta}]=\sum_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}{\rm Tr}[L_{k,\theta}^{2}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}]\,,

(98)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}=\prod_{j\neq k}{\rm Tr}\left[\tau^{(j)}_{\theta}\right]\leqslant 1\,.

(99)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})=\sum_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta}^{(k)})\leqslant\sum_{k}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta}^{(k)})\,.

(100)

5.

Additivity for direct sum of truncated states:
For
\tau_{\theta}=\bigoplus_{k}\mu_{k}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}
, where
\mu_{k}
is
\theta
-independent and
0<\sum_{k}\mu_{k}\leqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}=\bigoplus_{k}\mu_{k}\partial_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}=\bigoplus_{k}\mu_{k}\frac{L_{k,\theta}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}+\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}L_{k,\theta}}{2}\,.

(101)

Then, the TSLD operator becomes

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=\bigoplus_{k}L_{k,\theta}\,.

(102)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})={\rm Tr}[L_{\theta}^{2}\tau_{\theta}]=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}{\rm Tr}[L_{k,\theta}^{2}\tau_{\theta}^{(k)}]\,,

(103)

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta})=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\tau_{\theta}^{(k)})\,,

(104)

and
\sqrt{T}
is taken to be the unique, positive semi-definite square root of
T
.

Finally, let us remark that an alternative proof for the subadditivity for the product of truncated states can be obtained as follows. This is for the readers who are not familiar with the SLD operator. It will suffice to show it is true in the bipartite case (as larger product states follow by induction). So, we consider a subnormalized state of the form

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}=\tau_{\theta}^{(1)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H}_{2})\,,

(105)

where
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k})=d_{k}
with
k=1,2
. Here, we focus on the unitary families. First, recall that the TQFI is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\rho_{\theta}^{(m)})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}}|\langle\lambda_{i}\hskip 1.0pt|G|\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{j}\rangle|^{2}\,.

(106)

As this explicit form depends on the eigensystem of our state and the generator of the unitary dynamics, let us prove explicitly, when the subnormalized state of the subspace belongs to the unitary families, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\theta}^{(1)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{(2)}

(107)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\theta G^{(1)}}\tau^{(1)}e^{+i\theta G^{(1)}}\otimes e^{-i\theta G^{(2)}}\tau^{(2)}e^{+i\theta G^{(2)}}

(108)

\displaystyle=(e^{-i\theta G^{(1)}}\otimes e^{-i\theta G^{(2)}})(\tau^{(1)}\otimes\tau^{(2)})(e^{+i\theta G^{(1)}}\otimes e^{+i\theta G^{(2)}})\,.

(109)

A useful quantity needed here is the Kronecker sum defined as

\displaystyle A^{(1)}\oplus B^{(2)}=A^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes B^{(2)}\,,

(110)

and we recall here the following useful identity

\displaystyle e^{A}\otimes e^{B}=e^{A\oplus B}=e^{A^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes B^{(2)}}\,,

(111)

where
\oplus
is the Kronecker sum defined above. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}

\displaystyle=(e^{-i\theta G^{(1)}}\otimes e^{-i\theta G^{(2)}})(\tau^{(1)}\otimes\tau^{(2)})(e^{+i\theta G^{(1)}}\otimes e^{+i\theta G^{(2)}})

(112)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\theta(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})}(\tau^{(1)}\otimes\tau^{(2)})e^{+i\theta(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})}\,.

(113)

As for the eigensystem, we note that the Hilbert space is now of the form
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(2)}
so the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are now of the form

\displaystyle\tau_{\theta}^{(1)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{(2)}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{j}\rangle=\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{j}\rangle\,,

(114)

where we have that
i\in[1,m_{1}]
and
j\in[1,m_{2}]
, and where
0<\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\leqslant 1
and
0<\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\leqslant 1
. Together, TQFI becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\tau_{\theta}^{(1)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,k=1}^{m_{1}}\sum_{j,\ell=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell}}|\langle\lambda_{i}|\otimes\langle\lambda_{j}|(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})|\lambda_{k}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}\,.

(115)

Let us now expand the matrix element part of the expression

\displaystyle|\langle\lambda_{i}|\otimes\langle\lambda_{j}|(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})|\lambda_{k}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}

(116)

\displaystyle=(\langle\lambda_{i}|\otimes\langle\lambda_{j}|(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})|\lambda_{k}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle)

\displaystyle\times(\langle\lambda_{k}|\otimes\langle\lambda_{\ell}|(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})|\lambda_{i}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{j}\rangle)

(117)

\displaystyle=\big(\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle\delta_{j\ell}+\delta_{ik}\langle\lambda_{j}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle\big)\big(\langle\lambda_{k}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\delta_{j\ell}+\delta_{ik}\langle\lambda_{\ell}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{j}\rangle\big)

(118)

\displaystyle=|\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle|^{2}\delta_{j\ell}\delta_{j\ell}+(\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle)(\langle\lambda_{\ell}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{j}\rangle)\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}

\displaystyle+(\langle\lambda_{\ell}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{j}\rangle)(\langle\lambda_{k}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{i}\rangle)\delta_{j\ell}\delta_{ik}+|\langle\lambda_{j}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}\delta_{ik}\delta_{ik}

(119)

Replacing this expansion in the summation of TQFI sum, we see that the terms with
\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}
lead to
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell}=0
. Hence, the first term in the TQFI becomes

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i,k=1}^{m_{1}}\sum_{j,\ell=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell}}|\langle\lambda_{i}|\otimes\langle\lambda_{j}|(G^{(1)}\otimes\openone^{(2)}+\openone^{(1)}\otimes G^{(2)})|\lambda_{k}\rangle\otimes|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}

(120)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,k=1}^{m_{1}}\sum_{j,\ell=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\lambda_{\ell}}\left(|\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle|^{2}\delta_{j\ell}+|\langle\lambda_{j}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}\delta_{ik}\right)

(121)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,k=1}^{m_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})^{2}}{\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{k})}|\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{\ell})}|\langle\lambda_{j}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}

(122)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum_{i,k=1}^{m_{1}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})^{2}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{k}}|\langle\lambda_{i}|G^{(1)}|\lambda_{k}\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{j,\ell=1}^{m_{2}}\frac{(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell})^{2}}{\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{\ell}}|\langle\lambda_{j}|G^{(2)}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle|^{2}\,,

(123)

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\tau_{\theta}^{(1)}\otimes\tau_{\theta}^{(2)})\leqslant\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\tau_{\theta}^{(1)})+\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta,\tau_{\theta}^{(2)})\,

(124)

as desired.

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma
4

Let
\sigma,~\xi,
and
\eta
be subnormalized states in
\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
. Then we have that the following properties of the generalized Bures distance hold:

1.

Symmetry: Because of
F_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=F_{*}(\xi,\sigma)
, we have
B_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=B_{*}(\xi,\sigma)
.

2.

Identity of indiscernibles: Because
F_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=1
if and only if
\sigma=\xi
, we have
B_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=0
if and only if
\sigma=\xi
.

3.

Triangular inequality: Let
A_{*}(\sigma,\xi)
be the generalized angular distance
A_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=\arccos(F_{*}(\sigma,\xi)),

and
0\leqslant A_{*}(\sigma,\xi)\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Then, we can write

\displaystyle B_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=2\sin\left(\frac{A_{*}(\sigma,\xi)}{2}\right)\,.

(125)

From the triangle inequality for the generalized angular distance
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{*}(\tau,\xi)=2\sin\left(\frac{A_{*}(\sigma,\xi)}{2}\right)\leqslant 2\sin\left(\frac{A_{*}(\sigma,\eta)}{2}\right)+2\sin\left(\frac{A_{*}(\eta,\xi)}{2}\right)=B_{*}(\sigma,\eta)+B_{*}(\eta,\xi)\,.\end{split}

(126)

These prove that
B_{*}(\sigma,\xi)
is a distance metric on the space of subnormalized state. Let us finally remark that the generalized Bures distance can also be expressed as
B_{*}(\sigma,\xi)=2P^{2}(\sigma,\xi)
, where
P(\sigma,\xi)=\sqrt{1-F_{*}(\sigma,\xi)}
is the so-called purified distance
Tomamichel et al. 2010
;
Tomamichel 2015
.

Appendix F
Proof of Lemma
5

Let us consider
B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})
. Suppose that
B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})
has the form

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}\delta^{k}=\frac{b_{0}}{4}+\frac{b_{1}}{4}\delta+\frac{b_{2}}{4}\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3})\,,

(127)

where
b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
|b_{k}|<\infty
.
Defining
F_{*}(\delta)=F_{*}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})
, by definition, we have

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})=2(1-F_{*}(\delta))=2-2\left(F_{*}(0)+\delta\partial_{\delta}F_{*}(0)+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\partial_{\delta}^{2}F_{*}(0)+O(\delta^{3})\right)\,,

(128)

From
F_{*}(0)=1
and
\partial_{\delta}F_{*}(0)=0
because the generalized fidelity is a continuous function of
\delta
and becomes maximum at
\delta=0
, we have

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})=-\delta^{2}\partial_{\delta}^{2}F_{*}(0)+O(\delta^{3})\,.

(129)

Therefore, we arrive at the following equality

\displaystyle\frac{b_{0}}{4}+\frac{b_{1}}{4}\delta+\frac{b_{2}}{4}\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3})=-\delta^{2}\partial_{\delta}^{2}F_{*}(0)+O(\delta^{3})\,,

(130)

which has to be valid for any infinitesimal
\delta
. Therefore, we must have
b_{0}=b_{1}=0
, and

\displaystyle b_{2}=-4\partial_{\delta}^{2}F_{*}(0)\,.

(131)

Here, applying
F_{*}(0)=1
and
\partial_{\delta}F_{*}(0)=0
, by definition of the truncated QFI, we can also obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho_{\theta}^{(m)})=8\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1-F_{*}(\delta)}{\delta^{2}}=-4\partial_{\delta}^{2}F_{*}(0)\,,

(132)

which leads to

\displaystyle b_{2}=\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho_{\theta}^{(m)})\,.

(133)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle B_{*}^{2}(\rho_{\theta}^{(m)},\rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)})=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{I}_{*}(\theta;\rho_{\theta}^{(m)})\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3})\,.

(134)

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

The charm quark, not as heavy as the beauty quark,
induces a correction to the heavy quark limit
Neubert:1993mb
.
Consequently, the non-factorizable effect,
negligible in beauty hadron decays
Feldmann:2004mg
,
can be as sizeable as the factorizable effect in charm hadron decays,
posing a challenge for estimation.
Some studies have resorted to using
SU(3)_{f}
parameters and
global fits to extract the (non-)factorizable contribution in charmed baryon decays
Zhao:2018mov
;
Hsiao:2020iwc
;
Hsiao:2021nsc
;
Huang:2021aqu
;
Xing:2023dni
.
Currently, the
\Omega_{c}^{0}
decays are insufficient for a comprehensive global fit.
In Eq. (
30
), the approximate equivalence relations
enable us to combine or neglect certain parameters, thereby reducing their number.
This facilitates the determination of the topological parameters,
even in the absence of sufficient experimental data.

For
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}M
,

c^{\prime\prime}
and
c^{\prime}
, as well as
e^{\prime\prime}
and
e^{\prime}
,
are identical in topology but involve different anti-symmetric quark pairs in the baryon.
According to the KPW theorem and the equivalence relation in Eq. (
II
),
it follows that
c^{\prime\prime}=0
. Similarly,

e^{\prime\prime}\simeq 0
is supported by the KPW theorem and
the approximate equivalence relations in Eq. (
30
).
Since we demonstrate that

e_{\bf B}(e^{\prime}_{M})
related to the 15-plet parts of the weak Hamiltonian
contributes 35% of its topological counterpart
e^{\prime}_{\bf B}(e_{M})
,

e_{\bf B}(e^{\prime}_{M})
is neglected in the approximate equivalence relation
e_{\bf B}(e^{\prime}_{M})\simeq 0
.
This is similar to the common disregard for
SU(3)_{f}
symmetry breaking effects,
which are around 30%.
We therefore conclude that although there are two parameters
for each non-factorizable topological diagram, only one parameter remains significant.
Due to
t\simeq-c
,
t
and
c
are combined. Similarly,

e^{\prime}
,
e_{M}
and
e^{\prime}_{\bf B}
are combined using
e^{\prime}\simeq-e_{M}\simeq e^{\prime}_{\bf B}
.
Specifically,
e^{\prime}+e_{M}\simeq 0
forms a unique relation applied to
the CA and SCS decay channels, resulting in their disappearance,
while
e^{\prime}_{\bf B}
is also absent. Thus,
t
and
c^{\prime}
are derived
to represent the remaining contributions to the the CA and SCS amplitudes
for
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}M
.

For
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{*}M
, since
c^{\prime}_{d}
and
e^{\prime}_{d}
are
associated with the less significant
c_{+}H(15)
in the effective Hamiltonian,
we consider that
c^{\prime}_{d}
cannot compete with
c_{d}
,
and
e^{\prime}_{d}
cannot compete with
e_{d\bf B}
or
e_{dM}
.
This leads to
(c^{\prime}_{d},e^{\prime}_{d})\simeq 0
in Eq. (
31
).
Additionally, we combine
t_{d}
and
c_{d}
with
t_{d}\simeq-c_{d}

and combine
e_{d\bf B}
and
e_{dM}
with
e_{d\bf B}\simeq-e_{dM}
, without neglecting them.
Consequently, we use
t_{d}
and
e_{dM}

to represent the four significant parameters in
{\cal M}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{*}M)
.

With our analysis for
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{(*)}M
extended to
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{(*)}V
,
we use fewer parameters to express the amplitudes, as extracted in Eq. (
III
).
Thus, for the first time, we explain all existing data as listed in Eq. (
III
).
To illustrate our findings,
we present the following ratios using
{\cal M}_{\rm STDA}
in Table
2
:

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{1}\equiv\frac{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0})}{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+})}\simeq 1/3\,,

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{2}\equiv\frac{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+})}{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{0}\bar{K}^{0})}\simeq s_{c}^{2}/(1-c^{\prime}/t)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{3}\equiv\frac{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}K^{+})}{{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+})}\simeq s_{c}^{2}(1-e_{dM}/t_{d})^{2}\,,

(36)

where
s_{c}^{2}=0.05
.
With the extracted parameters of Eq. (
III
) for
(S1,S2)
,
we obtain
c^{\prime}/t=(1.8,0.2)
and
e_{dM}/t_{d}=(2.2,-0.1)
,
which lead to
({\cal R}_{1},{\cal R}_{2},{\cal R}_{3})=(0.33,0.08,0.07)
.
These results are in good agreement with

({\cal R}^{\rm exp}_{1},{\cal R}^{\rm exp}_{2},{\cal R}^{\rm exp}_{3})=(0.28\pm 0.11,0.10\pm 0.02,0.06\pm 0.01)
,
as estimated with the experimental data in Eq. (
I
).
This demonstrates the applicability of the simplified topological-diagram approach
based on the approximate equivalence relations.
In particular,
{\cal R}_{1}
is a simplified form of

{\cal R}^{\rm TDA}_{1}=(1/3)(\bar{c}_{d}/\bar{t}_{d}+\bar{c}^{\prime}_{d}/t_{d})^{2}

using
\bar{c}_{d}/\bar{t}_{d}\simeq-1
and
\bar{c}^{\prime}_{d}/t_{d}\simeq 0
,
which results in
{\cal R}_{1}\simeq{\cal R}^{\rm exp}_{1}
.

Since
{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{(*)}M,{\bf B}^{(*)}V)
are partially measured
with respect to
{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+})
,

\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+}
undoubtedly plays a key role.
Interestingly, it happens to be a pure factorizable decay channel,
for which the factorization approach should be able to give a reliable estimation.
However, Refs.
Xu:1992sw
;
Cheng:1996cs
and Ref.
Aliev:2022gxi

estimate
{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+})\sim 10^{-2}
,
around ten and five times larger than that in Ref.
Hsiao:2020gtc
, respectively,
showing divergent results. This is due to the
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{(*)}
transition form factors,
which cannot be conclusively determined from different calculations
Xu:1992sw
;
Cheng:1996cs
;
Perez-Marcial:1989sch
;
Cheng:1993gf
;
Cheng:1995fe
;
Pervin:2006ie
;
Zhao:2018zcb
;
Hsiao:2020gtc
;
Aliev:2022gxi
.

On the other hand, our approach provides an independent perspective.
In Eq. (
III
), it is evident that
t=0.25
GeV
3
and
\bar{t}=0.52
GeV
3

are comparable to
|T|=0.23
GeV
3
and
|\bar{T}|=0.38
GeV
3
extracted
in
{\cal B}_{3c}\to{\bf B}M

Hsiao:2021nsc
and

{\cal B}_{3c}\to{\bf B}V

B3ctoBV
, respectively.
This suggests that the factorizable contribution in
\Omega_{c}^{0}
should be
as significant as those in
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and
\Xi_{c}^{+(0)}
, rather than several times larger,
given that
\Omega_{c}^{0}
,
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
, and
\Xi_{c}^{+(0)}
all belong to
the charmed baryon section.

To test the factorizable effect, the absolute branching fractions of
the (nearly) pure factorizable decay channels can be useful
Hsiao:2019wyd
.
Here, the term “nearly” reflects the fact that the initial amplitudes of
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}(\Xi^{-}\rho^{+})

carry the non-factorizable parameter
e_{\bf B}(\bar{e}_{\bf B})
, neglected in STDA.
Utilizing the
t
-like parameters in Eq. (
III
),
we predict

\displaystyle{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+},\Xi^{-}\rho^{+})=(4.7\pm 0.4,16.4\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-4}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+},\Omega^{-}\rho^{+})=(28.1\pm 3.6,46.3\pm 7.4)\times 10^{-4}\,.

(37)

The non-factorizable effects play a key role in testing the heavy quark limit,
which can be constrained with
{\cal R}_{2,3}\simeq{\cal R}^{\rm exp}_{2,3}
in Eq. (
IV
).
As a result, the
W
-exchange parameters
c^{\prime}
and
e_{dM}
are found to be
two times larger than the external
W
-emission terms, or 20% and 10% of them,
respectively. This suggests that other theoretical studies, such as the pole model,
might overestimate the non-factorizable contributions. For comparison,
we predict the absolute branching fractions in scenarios 1 and 2 (
S_{1},S_{2}
) as

\displaystyle{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Lambda^{0}\bar{K}^{0})=(9.0\pm 0.4,0.2\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-4}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{0}\bar{K}^{0})=(47.0\pm 7.2,47.0\pm 7.2)\times 10^{-4}\,.

(38)

In contrast, the pole model predicts

{\cal B}_{\rm PM}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Lambda^{0}\bar{K}^{0},\Xi^{-}\pi^{+},\Xi^{0}\bar{K}^{0})=(80.5,93.4,378.0)

\times 10^{-4}

Hu:2020nkg
, which
leads to an inconsistency with
{\cal R}_{2}^{\rm BM}\simeq 2.5{\cal R}_{2}^{\rm exp}
.
Additionally, it is observed that
{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+})
in Eq. (
IV
)
is only 5% of
{\cal B}_{\rm PM}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+})
,
which can be attributed to the topology, like

,
neglected in STDA but significantly contributing in the pole model.
Notably,
{\cal M}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Lambda^{0}\bar{K}^{0})
,
predicted to be at most
10\%
of
{\cal B}_{\rm BM}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Lambda^{0}\bar{K}^{0})

In summary, we have developed the simplified topological-diagram approach
to investigate two-body non-leptonic
\Omega_{c}^{0}
decays.
Employing the
SU(3)_{f}
-induced topological approach,
we have depicted and parameterized the
W
-emission and

W
-exchange processes, enabling us to establish
stringent
SU(3)_{f}
relations for potential decay channels.
To address the issue of various non-factorizable terms,
we have utilized the irreducible
SU(3)_{f}
approach,
identifying the dominant terms as
c^{\prime}(\bar{c}^{\prime})
for

\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}M(V)
and
e_{dM}(\bar{e}_{dM})
for
\Omega_{c}^{0}\to{\bf B}^{*}M(V)
.
With the topological parameters determined using available data, we have interpreted
the following experimental ratios:

{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0})/{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\rho^{+})=0.28\pm 0.11
,

{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+})/{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{0}\bar{K}^{0})=0.10\pm 0.02
, and

{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}K^{+})/{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Omega^{-}\pi^{+})=0.06\pm 0.01

{\cal B}(\Omega_{c}^{0}\to\Xi^{*0}\bar{K}^{0})=(9.8\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-4}

Note on graph-based BCJ relation for Berends-Giele currents
Yi-Jian Du, Konglong Wu

Abstract
Graph-based Bern-Carasso-Johansson (BCJ) relation for Berends-Giele currents in bi-adjoint scalar (BS) theory, which is characterized by connected tree graphs, was proposed in an earlier work. In this note, we provide a systematic study of this relation. We first prove the relations based on two special types of graphs: simple chains and star graphs. The general graph-based BCJ relation established by an arbitrary tree graph is further proved, through Berends-Giele recursion. When combined with proper off-shell extended numerators, this relation induces the graph-based BCJ relation for Berends-Giele currents in Yang-Mills theory. The corresponding relations for amplitudes are obtained via on-shell limits.

2
Berends-Giele currents in BS theory and graph-based BCJ relation

In this section, we briefly review the definition and useful properties of Berends-Giele current in BS theory. The graph-based BCJ relation for BS currents is also introduced.

2.1
Berends-Giele currents in BS theory

In BS theory, the Berends-Giele current
\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\boldsymbol{\sigma})
, where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a permutation of
n-1
elements (which are not necessarily the elements
1
, …,
n-1
), can be recursively defined
Mafra:2016ltu
by

\displaystyle\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\boldsymbol{\sigma})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{1\dots n-1}}\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n-2}\Bigl[\phi\left(1,2,\dots,i|\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{i}\right)\phi\left(i+1,\dots,n-1|\sigma_{i+1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}\right)

(2.1)

\displaystyle~-\phi\left(1,2,\dots,i|\sigma_{n-i},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}\right)\phi\left(i+1,\dots,n-1|\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-i-1}\right)\Bigr].

In the above expression,
s_{12\dots n-1}\equiv(k_{1}+k_{2}+...+k_{n-1})^{2}
, where
k_{i}
is the momentum of the particle
i
which satisfies the on-shell condition
k_{i}^{2}=0
. This recursive definition starts with the one-point current
\phi(l|l^{\prime})
that is defined by

\displaystyle\phi(l|l^{\prime})=\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(l=l^{\prime})\\
0&(l\neq l^{\prime})\\
\end{array}.

A consequent result of the definition is
\phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{i}|b_{1},\dots,b_{i})=0
if
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i}\}\setminus\{b_{1},\dots,b_{i}\}\neq\emptyset
. The
n
-point on-shell partial amplitude
\mathcal{A}(1,2,\dots,n|\boldsymbol{\sigma})
is obtained by taking the following limit

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(1,2,\dots,n|\boldsymbol{\sigma})=s_{1\dots n-1}\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\boldsymbol{\sigma})\big|_{k_{n}^{2}=s_{1\dots n-1}\rightarrow 0}.

(2.5)

Berends-Giele currents (
2.1
) in BS theory satisfy Kleiss-Kuijf (KK) relation
Kleiss:1988ne

\displaystyle\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\boldsymbol{\beta},1,\boldsymbol{\alpha})=(-)^{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|1,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\beta}^{T}),

(2.6)

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
,
\boldsymbol{\beta}
are two ordered sets, and
\boldsymbol{\beta},1,\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a permutation of
n-1
elements. The notation
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|
stands for the number of elements in the ordered set
\boldsymbol{\beta}
, and
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{T}
denotes the inverse permutation of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
. For two ordered sets
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
,
\boldsymbol{A}\shuffle\boldsymbol{B}
denotes the set of all
shuffle permutations
, which are obtained by merging
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{B}
together with keeping the relative order of elements in each ordered set. For example,
\{2,4\}\shuffle\{3,5\}
involves the following permutations

\displaystyle\{2,4,3,5\},\quad\{2,3,4,5\},\quad\{3,2,4,5\},\quad\{2,3,4,5\},\quad\{3,2,5,4\},\quad\{3,5,2,4\}.

(2.7)

When the ordered set
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
in eq. (
2.6
) is empty, KK relation turns into the reflection relation

\displaystyle\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1})=(-)^{n}\phi\left(1,2,\dots,n-1|\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n-2},\dots,\sigma_{1}\right).

(2.8)

For given two ordered sets
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(\{i_{1},..,i_{l}\})
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,...,n-1\}\setminus\{i_{1},..,i_{l}\})
where
i_{1},..,i_{l}\in\{1,2,...,n-1\}
and
\mathcal{P}(\{i_{1},..,i_{l}\})
denotes the set of all permutations of
i_{1},..,i_{l}
, the Berends-Giele currents satisfy the following identity

\displaystyle\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\beta})=0,

(2.9)

which is essentially equivalent to the KK relation (
2.6
).

\displaystyle B^{(x)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,|\,\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b})

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{x}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b}}\Big[\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,i}|\boldsymbol{\beta})\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i+1,l}|\boldsymbol{\alpha})-\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,{l-i}}|\boldsymbol{\alpha})\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{l-i+1,l}|\boldsymbol{\beta})\Big],

5
Further discussions

Having proven the graph-based BCJ relation (
2.12
) for the Berends-Giele currents in BS, we now provide more discussions including the on-shell limits, the antisymmetry under the exchanging
\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}

5.1
On-shell limit

Supposing that there are
n-1
external on-shell particles, the on-shell limit of the LHS of (
2.12
) is taken as follows

\displaystyle\lim_{s_{1\dots n-1}\rightarrow 0}s_{1\dots n-1}\mathbb{B}^{(x)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,|\,\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{s_{1\dots n-1}\rightarrow 0}\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}}s_{1\dots n-1}\mathbb{B}^{(x)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,|\,\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b})

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}}\sum\limits_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b}}(-1)^{|xa|}s_{ac}\,\mathcal{A}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,n}\big|\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\beta},\sigma_{n}\bigr),

where the relation (
2.5
) between Berends-Giele currents and amplitudes has been applied and the sign for the node
x
(
x\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}
) is chosen as
+1
. The RHS of (
2.12
), when multiplied by
s_{1\dots n-1}
turns into zero in the limit
s_{1\dots n-1}\to 0
because it does not involve
\frac{1}{s_{1\dots n-1}}
. The on-shell limit hence provides the corresponding relation for on-shell BS amplitudes

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}}\sum\limits_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{b}}(-1)^{|xa|}s_{ac}\,\mathcal{A}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1,n}\big|\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\beta}|_{c\prec a},\sigma_{n}\bigr)=0,

(5.2)

whose Yang-Mills version was first proposed in
Hou:2018bwm
. Here, we emphasize that the above on-shell relation can be understood as a proper combination of the general BCJ relations
BjerrumBohr:2009rd
;
Stieberger:2009hq
;
Chen:2011jxa
. This fact has already been proven in
Hou:2018bwm
. Furthermore, the general BCJ relations can be generated by the fundamental ones
Feng:2010my
;
Ma:2011um
. In this sense, the relation (
5.2
) based on graphs is essentially a proper combination of the fundamental BCJ relations, and cannot further reduce the number of independent color-ordered amplitudes.

) can be straightforwardly extended to the partial amplitudes in other theories such as YM or nonlinear sigma model, through the framework of Cachazo-He-Yuan formula
Cachazo:2013gna
;
Cachazo:2013hca
;
Cachazo:2013iea
;

) to YM theory, by the help of the decomposition formula proposed in
Wu:2021bcy
.

The Berends-Giele currents with Feynman gauge in YM have been shown to satisfy a decomposition formula
Wu:2021bcy
which consists of a part vanishing under on-shell limit and an effective current
\widetilde{J}^{\,\rho}(1,2,...,n-1)
satisfying the following decomposition formula

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}^{\rho}(1,2,\dots,n-1)=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(2,n-1)}\,N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})\phi(1,2,\dots,n-1|1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}).

(5.5)

Here, the off-shell polynomial numerators
N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})
(which was constructed by a graphic rule in
Wu:2021bcy
), can generate the BCJ numerators with Lie symmetries. As pointed in
Wu:2021bcy
, the
N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})
satisfies the following factorization property

\displaystyle N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{A}^{\nu}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L})\Big[N_{C}^{\nu\rho}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})-\big(N_{B}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})\cdot 2k_{1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}}\big)\eta^{\nu\rho}\Big],

(5.6)

where
N_{C}^{\nu\rho}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})
and
N_{B}^{\mu}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})
are other two types of numerators, the permutations on both sides are related by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R}\}
. Having eq. (
5.5
) and eq. (
5.6
) in hands, we dress the numerators
N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})
to the Berends-Giele currents of BS in (
2.11
), then obtain

\displaystyle B_{\text{YM}}^{(x)}\big(\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}\big)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(2,n-1)}\,N_{A}^{\rho}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})\,B^{(x)}\big(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,\big|\,\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}\big)

(5.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-)^{|ax|}\sum\limits_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}}\left[\,\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\beta}|_{c\prec a}}s_{ac}\,\widetilde{J}^{\rho}(\boldsymbol{\gamma})\,\right].

where we have set the
b
in (
2.11
) as the particle
1
. When all
a\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}
are summed over, eq. (
2.12
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{\text{YM}}^{(x)}\big(\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}\big)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{T}_{A}}B_{\text{YM}}^{(x)}\big(\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}\bigr)

(5.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(2,n-1)}N^{\rho}_{A}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})\bigg[\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}}\Big(\phi(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2,i}|\boldsymbol{\beta})\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i+1,l}|\boldsymbol{\alpha})-\phi(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2,{l-i}}|\boldsymbol{\alpha})\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{l-i+1,l}|\boldsymbol{\beta})\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(2,n-1)}N^{\rho}_{A}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma})\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}|_{a}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}}\phi(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2,i}|\boldsymbol{\beta})\phi(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i+1,l}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}).

Since only the
\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}
involves the node
1
, the second term on the second line of the above expression must vanish. When inserting (
5.6
) into eq. (
5.8
), we finally get the graph-based BCJ relation for the effective current
\widetilde{J}^{\rho}(1,2,\dots,n-1)
in YM

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{\text{YM}}^{(x)}(\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}},\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl[\,\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}\in P(2,i)}N_{A}^{\nu}(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L})\phi\left(1,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}|\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{B}}|_{1}\right)\Biggr]

(5.9)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\times\Biggl[\,\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R}\in P(i+1,n-1)}\left[N_{C}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})-N_{B}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R})\cdot 2k_{1,i}\right]^{\nu\rho}\phi\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R}|\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{A}}\bigr|_{x}\right)\Biggr].

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{L}=\{\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{i}\}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{R}=\{\sigma_{i+1},...,\sigma_{n-1}\}
. In the on-shell limit, the RHS has to vanish when multiplied by
s_{1,...,n-1}
(
s_{1,...,n-1}=k_{n}^{2}\to 0

Hou:2018bwm
for on-shell YM amplitudes. An interesting result is that the first factor is the effective current (
5.5

F^{\mu\nu}\equiv k^{\mu}_{1,i}\widetilde{J}^{\nu}(1,...,i)-k^{\nu}_{1,i}\widetilde{J}^{\mu}(1,...,i)

Wu:2021bcy

6
Conclusions

In this work, the graph-based BCJ relation for the Berends-Giele currents in BS theory and YM theory, was systematically studied. In the relation, distinct permutations of external particles are characterized by graphs, while any connected tree graph is shown to be equivalent to a proper combination of simple chains. The RHS of the relation (
2.12
) in BS is given by the difference between products of two subcurrents. When both sides are dressed by the off-shell numerators, the relation (
2.12
) turns into the off-shell graph-based BCJ relation in YM theory. The latter reproduces the relation observed in earlier work
Hou:2018bwm
in the on-shell limit. When the graphs are simple chains and the relation is transformed into the antisymmetric form (
5.3
),
we get the one
Frost:2020bmk
;
Frost:2021asr
which was derived by free Lie algebra.

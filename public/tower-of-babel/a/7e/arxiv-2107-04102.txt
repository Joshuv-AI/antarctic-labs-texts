Splitting ring extensions
Gabriel Picavet and Martine Picavet-L’Hermitte

Abstract
. The paper deals with ring extensions R ⊆ S R\subseteq S and their lattices [ R , S ] [R,S] of subextensions and is mainly devoted to FCP extensions (extensions whose lattices are Artinian and Noetherian). The object of the paper is the introduction and the study of elements of the lattices that split in some sense ring extensions. The reason why is that this splitting was used in earlier paper without their common nature being recognized. There are some favorable cases allowing to build splitters, mainly when we are dealing with ℬ \mathcal{B} -extensions, for example integral extensions. Integral closures and Prüfer hulls of extensions play a dual role. The paper gives many combinatorics results with the explicit computation of the Prüfer hull of an FCP extension. We show that a split extension cannot be pinched, except trivially.

Introduction and Notation

We consider the category of commutative and unital rings, whose epimorphisms will be involved. If

the set of all
R

As a general rule the subscript

A

13
]

We start by explaining the ideas that lead us to write this paper. The two books
[
18
]
and
[
19
]

We are interested in

18
]
and comprehensively studied in

of an extension is called a factor of the extension if

\begin{matrix}{}&{}&U&{}&{}\\
{}&\nearrow&{}&\searrow&{}\\
R&{}&{}&{}&S\\
{}&\searrow&{}&\nearrow&{}\\
{}&{}&V&{}&{}\end{matrix}

Moreover, since

Now for an arbitrary extension, the nice properties of complements or factors that hold for Prüfer extensions are no longer true. As our paper focusses on FCP extensions and complements may appear in a hidden form, a fact we observed in our earlier papers on FCP extensions, it seemed to us that a systematic study was necessary.

The key idea to get a theory that provides substantial results is the following. We say that a ring extension

Another principal aim of the paper concerns almost-Prüfer extensions, that are extensions

Section 7 contains information on pinched extensions. If an extension is pinched at an element of its lattice, it cannot be split at this element, except it is trivial. The case of an FIP extension is particularly vivid. A special look is given at FCP extensions that are pinched at the integral closure (
id est

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be an FCP extension.

(1)

If

is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension, so is

.

(2)

\mathcal{B}
-extension and

\overline{R}\subseteq S

\mathcal{B}
-extension.

(3)

If

\mathcal{B}
-extension and

, then

\mathcal{B}
-extension.

We use the characterization of Proposition
3.1
.

(1) Assume that
\overline{R}\subseteq S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension and let

. To show that

is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension, it is enough to consider

. But

, Lemma 2.4]
. In particular,

. Let

be a maximal chain of
[\overline{R},S]
. There exists some

. Since
\overline{R}\subseteq S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension,

is contained in a unique maximal ideal of
\overline{R}
. Then,

is contained in a unique maximal ideal of

and
R\subseteq S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension.

(2) Assume that
R\subseteq S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension and that

is an
i
-extension. Let

and set

, so that

. The assumption gives that

. But

\mathcal{B}
-extension.

\mathrm{Supp}(T/R)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)

(1)

The following proposition allows us to generalize

According to

, an integrally closed extension

has FCP if and only if it is a normal pair such that
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
is finite if and only if it is an FCP Prüfer extension.

We recall the following definition and notation. A poset

is called a

if

in

implies that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are comparable (with respect to

).

A subset

is called an

if no two distinct elements of
Y
are comparable. Let
R\subset S
be an FCP Prüfer extension. Set

. For each

be the set of all antichains of
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
that have cardinality

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP Prüfer extension, (whence FIP). The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

R\subseteq S
is chained;

(2)

There exists a unique
T\in[R,S]
such that
R\subset T
is minimal;

(3)

\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
is linearly ordered;

(4)

|[R,S]|=1+|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|
.

If one of the above conditions is verified, then
R\subset S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Obvious.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) Set
M:=\mathcal{C}(R,T)\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
, where
T
is the unique element of
[R,S]
such that
R\subset T
is minimal. We claim that
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)

=\{M\}
. Assume there exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)\setminus\{M\}
. According to
[
27
, Lemma 1.8]
, there exists
T^{\prime}\in[R,S],\ T^{\prime}\neq T
such that
R\subset T^{\prime}
is minimal with
M^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}(R,T^{\prime})
, a contradiction. It follows that
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)=\{M\}
and
P\subseteq M
for any
P\in\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
.
By
[
14
, Proposition 4.2(b)]
,
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
is a tree, and then is linearly ordered since it has only one maximal element.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4) We keep notation of
[
14
, Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 4.2]
, with
m:=|\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)|
. Then, we have
|\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)|=1
, so that
m=1
and
|\Gamma_{1}|=|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|
because
\Gamma_{1}=\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
since any antichain of
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
has only one element. By
[
14
, Theorem 4.3]
, we get

.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(1) because
|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|=\ell[R,S]
by
[
13
, Proposition 6.12]
, so that
|[R,S]|=1+\ell[R,S]
, and there is a unique maximal chain in
[R,S]
.

The last statement follows from Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP Prüfer extension. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R\subset S
is locally minimal;

(2)

\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(R)
;

(3)

|[R,S]|=2^{|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|}

(4)

R\subset S
is a Boolean FIP
\mathcal{B}
-extension.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) Since

is an FCP Prüfer extension, so is

for any

. It follows from
[

that

.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3) by Corollary
3.2
because
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) Assume that

. We keep the notation of
[
14

. Set

, so that

. It follows that

\Rightarrow
(4)

(4)

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) since

(3) Assume that there is a minimal element

\Leftrightarrow
(4) Let

{\sum}_{j=1}^{r}(\sum_{k=1}^{s_{j}}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})])

=:A

M_{j,k}=M_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
for any
(k,k^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{s_{j}}\times\mathbb{N}_{s_{j^{\prime}}}
.

Then,
A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{i}/P_{j_{i}})]
, where
P_{j_{i}}=\Psi(M_{i})
and because each
M_{i}
appears one and only one time in
A
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{r}
, we have
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{s_{j}}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})]
, with equality if and only if
s_{j}=1
, since
P_{j}
is counted in
\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})
for each
M_{j,k}\in\Psi^{-1}(\{P_{j}\})
. It follows that
|\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})|=\sum_{k=1}^{s_{j}}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})]
if and only if
P_{j}
is contained in a unique maximal ideal
M_{j,k}
if and only if
\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})
is linearly ordered because
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
is a tree. Now,
|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|={\sum}_{j=1}^{r}|\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})|\leq{\sum}_{j=1}^{r}(\sum_{k=1}^{s_{j}}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})])=A
. But,
A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{i}/P_{j_{i}})]
.

To conclude, (5)
\Leftrightarrow|\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)|=A\Leftrightarrow|\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})|=\sum_{k=1}^{s_{j}}[1+\mathrm{ht}(M_{j,k}/P_{j})]
for each minimal element
P_{j}
of
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{V}_{R}(P_{j})
is linearly ordered for each minimal element
P_{j}
of
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)\Leftrightarrow
(3).
∎

4.
General properties of splitters

Given a ring extension
R\subseteq S
and
T\in[R,S]
, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(T/R)\cup\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/T)
.
The case when this equation defines a partition of
\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(S/R)
has a great interest, because we will show that in some contexts, we can reduce our results to this situation.

In the following, we denote by
X^{c}
the complement of a subset
X
of
\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/R)
in
\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/R)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be a ring extension. Assume that there is some
T\in[R,S]
such that
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)\cap\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=\emptyset
. Then,
T
is the unique
T^{\prime}\in[R,S]
such that
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T^{\prime})=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)
and

\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(T^{\prime}/R)
.

Proof.

Set
X:=\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)
and
Y:=X^{c}
, so that
Y=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)
. Let
T^{\prime}\in[R,S]
be such that
\mathrm{MSupp}(T^{\prime}/R)=X
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T^{\prime})=Y
. Since
X\cap Y=\emptyset
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)=X\cup Y
, we get
T_{M}=T^{\prime}_{M}=S_{M}
for any
M\in X
and
T_{M}=T^{\prime}_{M}=R_{M}
for any
M\in Y
. Moreover, since
R_{M}=T_{M}=T^{\prime}_{M}=S_{M}
for any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)\setminus(X\cup Y)
, it follows that
T^{\prime}=T
.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

If
R\subset S
is an extension and
T\in]R,S[
, we say that the extension
splits
at
T
, if
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)\cap\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=\emptyset
.

In fact, if
R\subset S
is an extension and
T\in]R,S[
, we have obviously
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)\cap\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=\emptyset
if and only if
\mathrm{Supp}(S/T)\cap\mathrm{Supp}(T/R)=\emptyset
. That is the reason why we work only with maximal ideals.

The following Proposition gives a first example of a split extension.
Section 6 is devoted to almost-Prüfer extensions.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
26
, Proposition 4.16]
and
[
24
, Proposition 3.6]
Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

R\subset S
is an almost-Prüfer extension;

(2)

R\subset S
splits at
\overline{R}
;

(3)

R\subset S
splits at
\widetilde{R}
;

(4)

R=\overline{R}\cap\widetilde{R}
and
S=\overline{R}\widetilde{R}
;

(5)

There exists
U\in[R,S]
such that
R=\overline{R}\cap U
and
S=\overline{R}U
;

If these conditions hold,
\widetilde{R}
is the only
U\in[R,S]
satisfying (5).

Definition 4.4
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be a ring extension and
X\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/R)
. We say that an element
T
of

is a

of the extension at
X
if

and
X^{c}=\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(S/T)
. Clearly, such a splitter splits the extension and each element that splits an extension is a splitter.

Remark 4.5
.

The FCP hypothesis on

is necessary in Proposition
4.3
. In
[
13
, Remark 2.9(c)]
, we give the following example. Let
(R,M)
be a one-dimensional valuation domain with quotient field

, and set

, where

is the class of

in

. We get that

and
\overline{R}=R+Sx
. But neither

nor

are splitters since

In fact, according to
[

, Theorem 4.6]
, a quasi-Prüfer extension

is an almost-Prüfer extension if and only if

is the complement of

, and according to

\widetilde{R}
.

According to Lemma

, a splitter at

is unique and we will denote it by
\sigma(X)
. It follows that if the extension splits at

, then

, where

We observe that if

and

The following Theorem shows that for an FCP
\mathcal{B}
-extension
R\subset S
and for any

, the splitter of the extension at
X
always exists. In fact, it is a new characterization of
\mathcal{B}
-extensions. A precise study of splitters in such extensions is the subject of Section 5.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. Then,
R\subset S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension if and only if for any
X\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
, the splitter of the extension at
X
exists.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
and set
Y:=X^{c}
. Assume that
R\subset S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension, so that the map
\varphi:[R,S]\to\prod_{M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)}[R_{M},S_{M}]
defined by
\varphi(T)=(T_{M})_{M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)}
is bijective. In particular, there exists a unique
T\in[R,S]
such that
T_{M}=S_{M}
for each
M\in X
and
T_{M}=R_{M}
for each
M\in Y
. This implies in particular that
\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=X
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)=Y
. Since
X\cap Y=\emptyset
, it follows that
T
is the splitter of
R\subset S
at
X
.

Conversely, assume that the splitter of
R\subset S
at
X
exists for any
X\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
and assume that
R\subset S
is not a
\mathcal{B}
-extension. Then there exists
P\in\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
which is contained in two maximal ideals
M_{1},M_{2}
of
R
. Obviously,
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Set
X:=\{M_{1}\}
and
Y:=X^{c}
, so that
M_{2}\in Y
, and let
T:=\sigma(X)
. It follows that
\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=X
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)=Y
. Let
\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
be a maximal chain of
[R,S]
containing
T
, so that
T=R_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{n-1}
. Since
P\in\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
, there is some
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n-1}
such that
P=\mathcal{C}(R_{i},R_{i+1})\cap R
. Then,
P\in\mathrm{Supp}_{R}(R_{i+1}/R_{i})
, which implies that
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(R_{i+1}/R_{i})
. If

, then
R_{i},R_{i+1}\in[R,T]
, and
M_{2}\in\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(T/R)
, a contradiction. A similar contradiction holds if

. To conclude,

is a
\mathcal{B}
-extension.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. Assume that there exists
X\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
such that the splitter of the extension at
X
does not exist. Then, there exists some

contained in two distinct ideals of
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.6
, there exists

such that the splitter of the extension at

does not exist if and only if

is not a
\mathcal{B}
-extension, which is equivalent to there exists some

is not local by Proposition

contained in two distinct ideals of
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
.
∎

When an FCP extension
R\subset S
splits at some
T\in[R,S]
, we get a bijection between
[R,S]
and
[R,T]\times[T,S]
which allows to get a general formula about cardinalities of set of intermediary rings in case of FIP extensions.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension that splits at
T
and set
X:=\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)
Then, the following statements hold:

(1)

There is an order-isomorphism
\psi:[R,S]\to[R,T]\times[T,S]
defined by
\psi(V):=(V\cap T,VT)
.

(2)

T
has a unique complement
T^{o}
, which is the splitter
\sigma(X^{c})
and satisfies
T+T^{o}=TT^{o}
.

(3)

\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T^{o})
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T)=

\mathrm{MSupp}(T^{o}/R)
;

(4)

If
R\subset S
is an FIP extension,
|[R,S]|=|[R,T]||[T,S]|
holds.

Proof.

(1) is
[
24
, Lemma 3.7]
for the bijective property. The condition about the order is obvious, considering the product order on
[R,T]\times[T,S]
.

(2) The existence of
T^{o}
comes from the fact that there exists a unique
U\in[R,S]
such that
\psi(U)=(R,S)
, so that
R=U\cap T\ (*)
and
S=UT\ (**)
shows that
U
is the complement
T^{o}
of
T
. Since
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(T^{o}/R)\cup\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T^{o})
, by
(*)

for any

and
(**)

(T^{o})_{M}=S_{M}
for any

. It follows that

for any

leads to

. At last,

and

shows that

splits at

. In particular,
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)\cup\mathrm{MSupp}(T^{o}/R)
since
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)=X\cup Y
.

(3) We have just proved that
X=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/T^{o})=\mathrm{MSupp}(T/R)
and

.

(4) comes from (1).
∎

Corollary 4.9

Let

(1)

(2)

(1)

(2)

.
∎

Theorem

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

Proof.

We have

[

and
(T^{o})_{M}

, a contradiction. If

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension.

(1)

If the extension is chained, it is crucial.

(2)

Conversely, if the extension is Prüfer and crucial, it is chained.

Proof.

(1) Assume that

(2) Assume that

of

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. There is an order-isomorphism
\psi:[R,S]\to\{(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\in[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]\mid T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
almost-Prüfer
\}
defined by
\psi(T):=(T\cap\overline{R},\overline{R}T)
for each
T\in[R,S]
. In particular, if
R\subset S
has FIP, then
|[R,S]|\leq|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
.

Proof.

Let
(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\in[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
. Then,
\overline{R}
is also the integral closure
\overline{T}^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}
in
T^{\prime\prime}
(and in
S
).

Let
T\in[R,S]
and set
T^{\prime}:=T\cap\overline{R}
and
T^{\prime\prime}:=\overline{R}T
. Then
T\in[T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}]
and
(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\in[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
. Assume first that
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime\prime}
; so that,
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime\prime}=\overline{R}
, which implies that
T=\overline{R}
and
\mathrm{Supp}_{T^{\prime}}(\overline{R}/T^{\prime})=\mathrm{Supp}_{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime\prime}/\overline{R})=\emptyset
. It follows that
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
is almost-Prüfer. Assume now that
T^{\prime}\neq T^{\prime\prime}
. Applying Proposition
4.3
to the extension
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
, we get that
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
is almost-Prüfer. Therefore, we can define
\psi:[R,S]\to\{(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\in[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]\mid T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
almost-Prüfer
\}
by
\psi(T):=(T\cap\overline{R},\overline{R}T)
for each
T\in[R,S]
.

Let
(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\in[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
and assume that
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
is almost-Prüfer. In view of Theorem
4.8
applied to the extension
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
, there exists
T\in[T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}]\subseteq[R,S]
such that
(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})=(T\cap\overline{T}^{\prime},\overline{T}^{\prime}T)=(T\cap\overline{R},\overline{R}T)
. Hence
\psi
is a surjection.

Now, let

be such that
\psi(T_{1})=\psi(T_{2})=(T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime})
which implies that
T_{1},T_{2}\in[T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}]
. Another use of Theorem
4.8
applied to the extension
T^{\prime}\subseteq T^{\prime\prime}
gives that
T_{1}=T_{2}
, so that
\psi
is injective, and then a bijection.

To end, we have
\psi([R,S])\subseteq[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
, which implies that
|[R,S]|\leq|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
when
R\subset S
has FIP.
∎

A converse of Theorem
4.8
is developed in the following Corollary:

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

R\subset S
is an almost-Prüfer extension.

(2)

There exists an order-isomorphism
\psi:[R,S]\to[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
defined by
\psi(T):=(T\cap\overline{R},\overline{R}T)
.

If these conditions hold, then
\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\widetilde{R})
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\overline{R})
.

Moreover, if
R\subset S
is an FIP extension, these conditions are equivalent to:

(3)
|[R,S]|=|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) by Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.8
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) Since
\psi
is a bijection, there exists
U\in[R,S]
such that
\psi(U)=(R,S)=(U\cap\overline{R},\overline{R}U)
. Then
R\subset S
is almost-Prüfer by Proposition
4.3
.

If these conditions hold, then
\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\widetilde{R})
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\overline{R})
by Theorem
4.8
.

Assume now that
R\subset S
has FIP.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) obviously.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) Assume that
|[R,S]|=|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
holds. The map
\psi
which holds in Theorem
6.1
is the same as the map defined in (2). Being injective, it defines a bijection over
[R,\overline{R}]\times[\overline{R},S]
, giving (2).
∎

We recall the fundamental role of the support in an FCP extension.

Proposition 6.3
.

[
14
, Proposition 4.1]

Let
R\subset S
be a ring extension.
If there exists a maximal chain
R=R_{0}\subset\cdots\subset R_{i}\subset\cdots\subset R_{n}=S
of extensions, where

is minimal with crucial ideal

is an FCP extension), then

is a finite set; in fact,
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\{M_{i}\cap R\mid i=0,\ldots,n-1\}
.

Moreover, for each
P\in\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
, we have
{\mathrm{V}}_{R}(P)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. In
[
26
, Theorem 4.6]
, we set
\vec{R}:=\overline{R}\widetilde{R}=\overline{\widetilde{R}}
, which is the greatest almost-Prüfer subextension of
R\subset S
(the
almost-Prüfer closure
of
R\subset S
).
In particular,
\overline{R}
(resp.
\widetilde{R}
) is also the integral closure (resp. Prüfer hull) of
R
in
\vec{R}
.
We get the following Theorem:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension. Then,
\widetilde{R}=\cap[R_{[M]}\mid M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)]=R_{[1+(R:\overline{R})]}
.

Proof.

We consider the almost-Prüfer extension
R\subseteq\vec{R}
. According to Proposition
4.3
,
R\subseteq\vec{R}
splits at
\overline{R}
, with
\widetilde{R}=(\overline{R})^{o}
, the complement of
\overline{R}
in
[R,\vec{R}]
.

Set
X:=\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)
and
Y:=X^{c}
, the complement of
X
in
\mathrm{MSupp}(\vec{R}/R)
, so that
Y=\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)
and
\widetilde{R}=\sigma(Y)=\sigma(X^{c})
by Theorem
4.8
. Then, Proposition
5.7
gives that
\widetilde{R}=\sigma(Y)=\cap[R_{[M,\vec{R}]}\,\,|\ M\in Y^{c}]=\cap[R_{[M,\vec{R}]}\mid M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)]
, because
X=(Y^{c})^{c}
. In particular,
\widetilde{R}\subseteq\cap[R_{[M]}\mid M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)]=:R^{\prime}
.

Assume that
\widetilde{R}\subset R^{\prime}
, so that there exists
U_{1}\in[\widetilde{R},R^{\prime}]
such that
\widetilde{R}\subset U_{1}
is a minimal, necessarily integral extension, by maximality of
\widetilde{R}
. Indeed, if
\widetilde{R}\subset U_{1}
is minimal Prüfer, then
R\subset U_{1}
is Prüfer, a contradiction. It follows that there exists
R_{1}\in[R,U_{1}]
such that
R\subset R_{1}
is a minimal integral extension. Let
x\in R_{1}
be such that
R_{1}=R[x]
and set
M:=\mathcal{C}(R,R_{1})\in\mathrm{MSupp}(R_{1}/R)\subseteq\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)
. Since
x\in R^{\prime}
, it follows that there exists
s\in R\setminus M
such that
xs\in R
, giving
x/1\in R_{M}
, so that
(R_{1})_{M}=R_{M}
, a contradiction. Then,
\widetilde{R}=R^{\prime}
.

Set
I:=(R:\overline{R})
. Since
R\subseteq\overline{R}
has FCP,
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
implies that
R/I
is an Artinian ring; so that,
{\mathrm{V}}(I):=\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}\}
is a finite set. By the previous equality, we get that
\widetilde{R}=\cap_{M_{i}\in{\mathrm{V}}(I)}R_{[M_{i}]}
since
{\mathrm{V}}(I)=\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)
(
[
14
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Let
y\in\widetilde{R}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, we have
y\in R_{[M_{i}]}
, and there exists
\lambda_{i}\in R\setminus M_{i}
such that
\lambda_{i}y\in R
. But, in
R/I
, we have
\mathrm{Spec}(R/I)=\{M_{1}/I,\ldots,M_{n}/I\}={\mathrm{D}}(\overline{\lambda}_{1})\cup\cdots\cup{\mathrm{D}}(\overline{\lambda}_{n})
, where
\overline{\lambda}_{i}
is the class of
\lambda_{i}
in
R/I
. Hence,
R/I=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(R/I)\overline{\lambda}_{i}
, and there exist
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}\in R
such that
x:=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{i}\in I
, giving that
y-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{i}=xy
. But,
y{\lambda}_{i}\in R
for each

implies that
(1-x)y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{i}\in R
, with
1-x\in 1+I
; so that
y\in R_{[1+I]}
.

Conversely, if
y\in R_{[1+I]}
, there exists some
x\in I
such that
(1+x)y\in R
, with
x\in M_{i}
for each
i
, whence

for each

. Then, for each

, we get that

and

. To conclude, we have
\widetilde{R}=R_{[1+I]}
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

An FCP extension
R\subseteq S
, with conductor
I:=(R:\overline{R})
is almost-Prüfer if and only if
R_{[1+I]}\subseteq S
is integral, in which case
\overline{R}\subseteq S=\overline{R}_{[1+I]}
is Prüfer.

Proof.

The first part follows from Theorem
6.4
. Set
\Sigma:=1+I
and assume that the extension is almost-Prüfer. Let
x\in S\setminus\overline{R}
. There exist a positive integer
n>1
and
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in R_{[\Sigma]}
such that
x^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}=0\ (*)
. For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, there exists
s_{i}\in\Sigma
such that
b_{i}:=a_{i}s_{i}\in R
. Set
s:=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}s_{i}=s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}
, where
s^{\prime}_{i}\in\Sigma
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
. Multiplying the two sides of
(*)
by
s^{n}
, we get
(sx)^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}s^{n-1-i}s^{\prime}_{i}b_{i}(sx)^{i}=0\ (**)
, so that
sx\in\overline{R}
. To sum up, we have proved that for any
x\in S
, there exists some
s\in\Sigma
such that
sx\in\overline{R}
, which means that
S=\overline{R}_{[\Sigma]}
. Since an almost-Prüfer extension is quasi-Prüfer,
\overline{R}\subseteq S=\overline{R}_{[\Sigma]}
is Prüfer.
∎

Theorem 6.13

Let

be an FCP extension. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R\subseteq S
is almost-Prüfer;

(2)

\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\overline{R})=\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)
;

(3)

\psi_{1}:[R,\widetilde{R}]\to[\overline{R},S]
defined by
\psi_{1}(T):=T\overline{R}
is an order-isomorphism;

(4)

The map
\psi^{\prime}_{1}:[R,\overline{R}]\to[\widetilde{R},S]
defined by
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(T):=T\widetilde{R}
is an order-isomorphism;

(5)

\theta(T,T^{\prime}):=TT^{\prime}
is an order-isomorphism.

If one of these conditions holds, then
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\widetilde{R})=\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)
.

If the extension has FIP, the preceding conditions are equivalent to each of the following conditions:

(6)

|[R,S]|=|[R,\widetilde{R}]||[R,\overline{R}]|
;

(7)

|[R,\widetilde{R}]|=|[\overline{R},S]|
;

(8)

|[R,\overline{R}]|=|[\widetilde{R},S]|

Proof.

First,
R\subset S
is an almost-Prüfer if and only if

by definition of
\vec{R}
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2), (3), (4) and (5): Use Proposition
6.10
(2) to get (2), Proposition
6.10
(3) to get (3) and (4), and Proposition
6.10
(4) to get (5). Moreover, Proposition
6.10
(2) give

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Since

[

. Then, use Proposition

.

(3), (4) or (5)
\Rightarrow
(1) because, in each case, we have

Assume now that

has FIP.

Then, obviously, (5)
\Rightarrow
(6), (3)
\Rightarrow
(7) and (4)
\Rightarrow

(6)
\Rightarrow

Corollary 7.7
.

[
2
, Theorem 26]
Let
(R,M)
be a local integral domain with quotient field

such that

has FCP. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R\subset K
is pinched at
\overline{R}
;

(2)

R\subseteq\overline{R}
is almost unbranched;

(3)

either
R\subset\overline{R}
is infra-integral,
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R\subset\overline{R}
is minimal decomposed and
R\subset\overline{R}
is pinched at
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R
, or
R\subset\overline{R}
is an i-extension.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2): By
[
2
, Theorem 26]
,
R\subset K
is pinched at
\overline{R}
if and only if
T
is local for any
T\in[R,\overline{R}[
, which is equivalent to
R\subseteq\overline{R}
is almost unbranched.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3): Since
(R:\overline{R})\subseteq M
, we have
\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})=\mathrm{V}_{\overline{R}}((R:\overline{R}))=\mathrm{Max}(\overline{R})
. Now, using Proposition
7.6
,
R\subset K
is pinched at
\overline{R}
if and only if
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{R}(K/R)|=1,\ \mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})\subseteq\mathrm{V}_{\overline{R}}((R:\overline{R}))
, conditions that always hold and one of conditions (1)–(4) of Proposition
7.6
holds: so that, (1) is equivalent to one of conditions (1)–(4) of Proposition
7.6
. Then, it is enough to get conditions equivalent to conditions (1)–(4) under the assumptions of the Corollary.

Condition (1):
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})|=2,\ R\subseteq\overline{R}
is infra-integral and
M\overline{R}=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
, where
\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})=\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
. These conditions are equivalent to
R\subseteq\overline{R}
is infra-integral,
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R\subset\overline{R}
is minimal decomposed and
R\subset\overline{R}
is pinched at
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R
, since there is no minimal ramified extension
U\subset\overline{R}
.

If one of Conditions (2)–(4) holds,
R\subset\overline{R}
is always an
i
-extension since
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})|

=1
implies that
\overline{R}
is a local ring.

Conversely, assume that
R\subset\overline{R}
is an
i
-extension. It follows that
\overline{R}
is a local ring, so that
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})|

=1
. Set
\mathrm{MSupp}_{\overline{R}}(K/\overline{R})=\{N\}
. Then
\overline{R}\subset K
is
N
-crucial.

If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\neq\overline{R}
, then

|\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R}(\overline{R}/{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R)|=1
, with
\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R}(\overline{R}/{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R)=\{N\}

7.6

\overline{R}/N^{\prime}\not\cong\overline{R}/N^{\prime\prime}
since there are not distinct
N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{V}_{\overline{R}}((R:\overline{R}))
, and, also any
N^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{V}_{\overline{R}}((R:\overline{R}))
such that

.

If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R=\overline{R}
, Condition (3) of Proposition
7.6
cannot happen because

is a local ring, contradicting

To sum up,
R\subset K
is pinched at

if and only if either

is infra-integral,
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R\subset\overline{R}
is minimal decomposed and

is pinched at
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R
or

is an
i
-extension.

. This leads us to the following example built from

by Dobbs-Jarboui.

. According to

, Example 3.3(a)]

is an AV-ring pair and

. Assume that

T\subseteq K
is an AV-ring pair such that
C:=(T:S)=X^{n}S
, and
S
is also the integral closure
\overline{T}
of
T
in
K
. Since
T/C\cong R/(R\cap C)=F
, then
T/C
is a field and
S
is a finitely generated
T
-module with basis
\{1,\ldots,X^{n-1}\}
. Therefore,
T\subseteq S
is an FCP integral extension. Then,
[
12
, Theorem 5.6]
shows that
T\subset K
is pinched at
S
. We recover in this example case (4) of Proposition

is subintegral.

Remark 7.9
.

We can note that an AV-ring pair is quasi-Prüfer. Let
R\subset S
be an AV-ring pair. We may assume that
\overline{R}\neq R,S
because if
\overline{R}=R,S
, the result is obvious. Then,
\overline{R}\subset S
is integrally closed and also an AV-ring pair. It follows from
[
12
, Proposition 2.2]
that
\overline{R}\subset S
is a normal pair, or equivalently, a Prüfer extension, so that
R\subset S
is quasi-Prüfer. Moreover,
R
is unbranched in
S
since
\overline{R}
is local, thanks to
[
12
, Theorem 2.6]
. If, in addition, we assume that
R\subset S
has FCP, with
\overline{R}\neq R,S
, we claim that
R\subset S
is not almost-Prüfer. Otherwise,
R\subset S
splits at
\widetilde{R}\neq R
. Then, applying again
[
12
, Theorem 2.6]
to the Prüfer extension (then a normal pair)
R\subset\widetilde{R}
, we get that
R
is a local ring, in contradiction with Theorem
6.13
since
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\widetilde{R})=\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)
and
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/\widetilde{R})\cap\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)=\emptyset=\mathrm{MSupp}(\overline{R}/R)\cap\mathrm{MSupp}(\widetilde{R}/R)=\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

In the context of FIP extensions, the concepts of split extensions and pinched extensions are mutually exclusive as it is explained in the next remark.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FIP extension and
T\in]R,S[
. Then
R\subset S
is pinched at
T
if and only if
|[R,S]|=|[R,T]|+|[T,S]|-1
.

Proof.

Since
[R,T]\cup[T,S]\subseteq[R,S]
with
[R,T]\cap[T,S]=\{T\}
, we always have
|[R,S]|\geq|[R,T]|+|[T,S]|-1
. Then,
|[R,S]|>|[R,T]|+|[T,S]|-1
if and only if there exists some
U\in[R,S]\setminus([R,T]\cup[T,S])
, so that
|[R,S]|=|[R,T]|+|[T,S]|-1
if and only if there does not exist any
U\in[R,S]\setminus([R,T]\cup[T,S])
if and only if
R\subset S
is pinched at
T
.
∎

Proposition 7.11
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FIP extension. Then, the following statements hold:

(1)

|[R,\overline{R}]|+|[\overline{R},S]|-1\leq|[R,S]|\leq|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
;

(2)

|[R,\overline{R}]|+|[\overline{R},S]|-1=|[R,S]|
if and only if
R\subset S
is pinched at
\overline{R}
;

(3)

[R,S]|=|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
if and only if
R\subset S
splits at
\overline{R}
.

Proof.

(1) The first inequality comes from
[R,\overline{R}]\cup[\overline{R},S]\subseteq[R,S]
, with
\overline{R}\in[R,\overline{R}]\cap[\overline{R},S]
. The second inequality comes from Theorem
6.1
.

(2) is Lemma
7.10
applied to
T:=\overline{R}
if
\overline{R}\neq R,S
. Otherwise, if
\overline{R}\in\{R,S\}
, the result is obvious (see Remark
7.12
(2)).

(3) comes from Corollary
6.2
and Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Remark 7.12
.

(1) The three statements of Proposition
7.11
show that when
R\subset S
is an FIP extension, the least value of
|[R,S]|
is gotten when
R\subset S
is pinched at
\overline{R}
, and its greatest value is gotten when
R\subset S
is split at
\overline{R}
. This means that the fact that
R\subset S
is either pinched at
\overline{R}
or split at
\overline{R}
leads to extreme situation.

(2) We have seen in Proposition
7.1
that a splitter of
[R,S]
is trivial when
R\subset S
is pinched at some
U\in]R,S[
. In particular, if
R\subset S
has FIP, the only case where
R\subset S
is both pinched and split at
\overline{R}
is when
|[R,\overline{R}]|+|[\overline{R},S]|-1=|[R,\overline{R}]||[\overline{R},S]|
. An easy calculation shows that this equation is equivalent to either
\overline{R}=R
or
\overline{R}=S
, which recovers Proposition
7.1
.

(3) We have seen in Remark
4.15
that there exists an FIP extension
R\subset S
and
T\in]R,S[
such that
|[R,S]|=|[R,T]||[T,S]|
although
R\subset S
is not split at
T
.

(4) As for the length, for an arbitrary FCP extension
R\subset S
, we always have
\ell[R,S]=\ell[R,\overline{R}]+\ell[\overline{R},S]
by
[
14
, Theorem 4.11]
.

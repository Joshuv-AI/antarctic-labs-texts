2.
Symmetric bilinear forms and Galois extensions

For each
\sigma\in G:=\gal(L/K)
and

we may define the symmetric bilinear form

(2.1)

\phi_{b,\sigma}(x,y)=\tr^{L}_{K}(b(x\sigma(y)+\sigma(x)y)),\qquad\forall x,y\in L.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi=\phi_{b,\sigma}
be a symmetric bilinear form defined above. Thus we have

\phi(x,y)=\tr((\sigma^{-1}(bx)+b\sigma(x)y))

for all

and

in
L
.

Corollary 2.1
.

An element

is in the radical of
\phi_{b,\sigma}
if and if

(2.2)

\sigma^{-1}(bx)=-b\sigma(x).

Recall that if
F
is an intermediate subfield and

then the
L/F
-norm

N_{L/F}(a)
of

is defined as
N_{L/F}(a)=\displaystyle\prod_{\theta\in\gal(L/F)}\theta(a)
. For the sake of convenience in what follows we shall denote the fixed field of
\sigma\in\gal(L/K)
by
L^{\langle\sigma^{i}\rangle}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\phi=\phi_{b,\sigma}
be a symmetric bilinear form defined above, with
b\in L^{\times}
, then
\phi
is degenerate if and only if
-\sigma(b)b^{-1}
is expressible in the form
\sigma^{2}(c)c^{-1}
for some
c\in L^{\times}
that is,
\phi
is degenerate if and only if

(2.3)

N_{L/L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}}(-\sigma(b)/b)=1.

Moreover, in this case
\rk(\phi)=n-\frac{n}{[L:L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}]}

Proof.

Let
R
denote the radical of
\phi
and let
x
be an element of
R
. By Corollary
2.1
and applying
\sigma
on (
2.2
), we obtain

bx=-\sigma(b)\sigma^{2}(x).

Consequently if
x\neq 0
then taking
x=c^{-1}
, we have

-\sigma(b)b^{-1}=\sigma^{2}(c)c^{-1}.

The next assertion is now clear in view of the Hilbert Theorem 90.

Now will compute the rank of
\phi
when
\phi
is degenerate. At first we claim that
\dim(R)=\frac{n}{[L:L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}]}
. Indeed let
x\in R
then it can be checked that
x\in c^{-1}L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}
, also conversely if
y\in c^{-1}L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}
then

. Thus
R=c^{-1}L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}
. Thus
\dim R=\frac{n}{[L:L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}]}
, that is,
\rk(\phi)=n-\frac{n}{[L:L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}]}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that the order of
\sigma
is odd then for each non-zero element

If possible, let there be a

Consequently there exists a

. Thus

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that the order of
\sigma
is even, say

and

Since

{\sigma(b)}{b^{-1}}=-1

-{\sigma(b)}{b^{-1}}=\sigma^{2}(c)c^{-1}.

Thus

is degenerate.

Next we will show the existancy of a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form. At first we assume that

is finite. Let

. It is straight forward to observe that the number of elements of the form

is

\frac{q^{2r}-1}{q^{2}-1}.

As

\frac{q^{2r}-1}{q^{2}-1}<\frac{q^{2r}-1}{q-1},

our claim follows in this case.

Now we suppose that

is infinite. If possible let
\phi_{b,\sigma}
be degenerate

N_{L/L^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}}(-\sigma(b)/b)=1,

for all

(-1)^{r}b\sigma^{2}(b)\ldots\sigma^{2r-2}(b)=\sigma(b)\sigma^{3}(b)\ldots\sigma^{2r-1}(b),

for all

. In particular, if we take any element

and replace

, we also obtain

(-1)^{r}(\alpha-b)\sigma^{2}(\alpha-b)\ldots\sigma^{2r-2}(\alpha-b)=\sigma(\alpha-b)\sigma^{3}(\alpha-b)\ldots\sigma^{2r-1}(\alpha-b).

We can choose an element

are all different and define the polynomials

\displaystyle p_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}(x-b)(x-\sigma^{2}(b))\ldots(x-\sigma^{2r-2}(b)),

\displaystyle p_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=(x-\sigma(b))(x-\sigma^{2}(b))\ldots(x-\sigma^{2r-1}(b)).

Let

have no roots in common so

in non-degenerate.

Remark 2.1

If

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\gal(L/K)
. We set

A^{\sigma}:=\{\phi_{b,\sigma}:b\in L\}.

It can be checked that

is a subspace of
\sym(L)
and

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\gal(L/K)
. Then

\dim(A^{\sigma})=\begin{cases}n,\quad&\text{if}~\ord(\sigma)\neq 2\\
n/2,\quad&\text{if}~\ord(\sigma)=2.\end{cases}

When
\sigma
has odd order then all the nonzero elements of

are non-degenerate.

When
\ord(\sigma)=2r
then the non-zero elements of
A^{\sigma}
either non-degenerate or else have rank

.

Proof.

Let
\psi:L\rightarrow A^{\sigma}
be a map define by

Clearly
\psi
is
K
-linear. Let

. By Corollary
2.1
,
\phi_{b,\sigma}=0
implies

-bx=\sigma(b)\sigma^{2}(x),

for all

. Taking

we obtain
\sigma(b)=-b
. Thus if

, we must have

\sigma^{2}(x)=x

for all

. Consequently
\ord(\sigma)=2
otherwise if
\sigma=\text{id}
then there is no non-zero

such that
\sigma(b)=-b
. Thus if
\ord(\sigma)\neq 2
then

is an isomorphism, consequently
\dim(A^{\sigma})=n
. On the otherhand if
\ord(\sigma)=2
then

. Consequently the dimension of the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue

is also

, thus

.
∎

4.
A short survey on
n
-subspace of

and
S_{n}(K)

For every

and every field
K
, let

be the vector space of the
(n\times n)
matrices over
K
, let
S(n,K)
be the vector space of the symmetric
(n\times n)
matrices over
K
. We recall that a
K
-subpace

of
M(n,K)
or
S(n,K)
is an
n
-subspace if all of its non-zero elements are invertible. Define

\tau_{n}(K)=\text{max}\{\dim S:S~\text{is an $n$ subspace of $M(n,K)$}\},

\mu_{n}(K)=\text{max}\{\dim S:S~\text{is an $n$ subspace of $S(n,K)$}\}.

It is easy to observed that
\mu_{n}(K)\leq\tau_{n}(K)
. For an arbitrary field
K
exact determination of
\tau_{n}(K)
or
\mu_{n}(K)
is a difficult problem. However
\tau_{n}(K)\leq n
, as any subspace
S\subset M_{n}(K)
of dimension greater than
n
must intersect nontrivially with the subspace of matrices with the first column equal to zero.
The invariant
\mu_{n}(K)
is intimately relate with the invariant
\tau_{n}(K)
. In general if
n
is even then we can easily checked that
\mu_{n}(K)\geq\tau_{n/2}(K)
. Since if
U
be an
n/2
-subspace of
M(n/2,K)
then the subspace of all
n\times n
symmetric matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\
A^{T}&0,\end{pmatrix}

where
A
runs over all
(n/2\times n/2)
matrices with entries in
U
and
A^{T}
denotes the transpose of
A
is an
n
-subspace of
S(n,K)
.

In the following, we will discuss the values of the invariants
\tau_{n}(K)
and
\mu_{n}(K)
across various fields.

4.1.
For algebraic closed field

Suppose
K
is an algebraic closed field. It can be easily checked that
\tau_{n}(K)=\mu_{n}(K)=1
as for any
A
,
B

\in A_{n}(K)
,
A-\lambda B
is singular where
\lambda
is an eigen-value of
AB^{-1}
.

4.2.
For real number field

Suppose
K
is real number field. Then it follows from
[
3
, Theorem 1]

\tau_{n}(K)=\rho(n)
, where
\rho(n)
denotes the Radon-Hurtwitz number and is defined by
\rho(n)=2^{c}+8d
, whenever

where

are integers with

. Now if
n
is odd then
\rho(n)=1
, consequently
\tau_{n}(K)=\mu_{n}(K)=1
. On the otherhand if
n
is even then
\tau_{n/2}(K)\leq\mu_{n}(K)\leq\tau_{n}(K)
. Thus

\mu_{n}(K)=8d,\quad\text{if}~~~~~~c=0

and

\mu_{n}(K)\in\begin{cases}[1+8d,2+8d]\quad&\text{if}~~~c=1\\
[2+8d,4+8d]\quad&\text{if}~~~c=2\\
[4+8d,8+8d]\quad&\text{if}~~~c=3.\end{cases}

4.3.
For algebraic number field

Suppose

is an algebraic number field. Then by
[
4
, Lemma 4]
for each

there exists a cyclic Galois extension

of

. Let

be a irreducible polynomial of degree

-subspace of

. Thus

. Although

but still we can still obtain an

-dimensional
n
-subspace inside

. Then by Theorem

2.2
and Remark

3.2
,

is an

-subspace inside

4.4.

Suppose

5.
Conclusion

The eigenspaces associated with elements of the Galois group yield constant rank subspaces in
\sym_{K}(L)
. If

, then in light of the Remark

, an

-subspace of dimension

. As mentioned earlier in this paper, the degeneracy criterion for the symmetric form

(defined in
[
1
]
), consequently, the quest for determining the maximum dimension of an

-subspace in

), unless

) or

[

). Furthermore, it is noted that if

forms an

-subspace of dimension

with exactly dimension

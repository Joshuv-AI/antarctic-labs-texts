Generating solutions of a linear equation and structure of elements of the Zelisko group
V.A. Bovdi, V.P. Shchedryk

Abstract
. Solutions of a linear equation b = a ​ x b=ax in a homomorphic image of a commutative Bézout domain of stable range 1.5 1.5 is developed. It is proved that the set of solutions of a solvable linear equation contains at least one solution that divides the rest, which is called a generating solution. Generating solutions are pairwise associates. Using this result, the structure of elements of the Zelisko group is investigated.

Introduction and Main results

Let
R
be a commutative elementary divisor ring with
1\not=0
(see
[
6
, p. 465]

be the ring of
{n\times n}
matrices over

and
{\rm GL}_{n}(R)
be groups of units of rings
R
and

, respectively. By the definition
[
6
, p. 465]
of elementary divisor rings, for each
A\in R^{n\times n}
, there exist
P,Q\in{\rm GL}_{n}(R)
(we call them left and right transforming matrices of the matrix

) such that

(1)

PAQ={\rm diag}(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k},0,\ldots,0),

where
\varphi_{k}\neq 0
and
\varphi_{i}
is a divisor of
\varphi_{i+1}
for

The diagonal matrix
\Phi:={\rm diag}(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k},0,\ldots,0)
in (

and
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}
are called

of the matrix

To the matrix

we associate a subgroup
{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}\leq{\rm GL}_{n}(R)
(see
[
10
, p. 62]
) which is called the

of the matrix

and it is definite as:

{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}=\{H\in{\rm GL}_{n}(R)\mid\;\exists S\in{\rm GL}_{n}(R)\quad\text{such that}\quad H\Phi=\Phi S\;\}.

This definition was first given by V. Zelisko
[
16
]
for the matrix over polynomial ring
F[x]
in which
F
is an algebraic closed field of characteristic
0
.
The definition of the Zelisko group
{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}
over the ring
R
is independent of the choice of the Smith form
\Phi
of

(see (
1
)).
Indeed, let
\Phi_{1}:=\Phi\Upsilon
in which
\Upsilon:={\rm diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k},1,\ldots,1)
and
\varepsilon_{i}\in U(R)
. If
H\in{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}
then

H\Phi_{1}=H(\Phi\Upsilon)=\Phi(S\Upsilon)=\Phi\Upsilon(\Upsilon^{-1}S\Upsilon)=\Phi_{1}S_{1},\quad(S_{1}:=\Upsilon^{-1}S\Upsilon)

and
{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}\subseteq{\bf{G}}_{\Phi_{1}}
.

Now, if
L\in{\bf{G}}_{\Phi_{1}}
, then
L\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{1}T
, where
T\in{\rm GL}_{n}(R)
, so

L\Phi=\Phi(\Upsilon T\Upsilon^{-1})
and
{\bf{G}}_{\Phi_{1}}\subseteq{\bf{G}}_{\Phi}
. Consequently,
{\bf{G}}_{\Phi_{1}}={\bf{G}}_{\Phi}
.

Note that, if

is an elementary divisor domain and

\frac{\varphi_{2}}{\varphi_{1}}h_{21}&h_{22}&\ldots&h_{2.n-1}&h_{2n}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\varphi_{1}}h_{n1}&\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\varphi_{2}}h_{n2}&\ldots&\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\varphi_{n-1}}h_{n.n-1}&h_{nn}\end{matrix}\right]\in R^{n\times n}.

If for the matrix

2.
Preliminaries and Proofs

Let
U(R)
be the group of units of a commutative Bézout domain
R
of stable range
1.5
. For each
m\in R\setminus\{0,U(R)\}
we define the homomorphism
\overline{\bullet}:R\to R_{m}=R/mR
. For each
a\in R
, we denote
\overline{a}:=\overline{\bullet}(a)\in R_{m}
.

We start our proof with the following.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\sigma\in R
such that
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\beta}\cdot\overline{\sigma}
.
There exist
a,b,c\in R
, such that

a=b\cdot c,\quad\overline{a}=\overline{\alpha},\quad\overline{b}=\overline{\beta},\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{c}=\overline{\sigma}.

Proof.

Set
\overline{a}:=\alpha+mR
,
\overline{b}:=\beta+mR
and
\overline{c}:=\sigma+mR
. Since
\beta\sigma\in\alpha+mR
, there exists
t\in R
such that
\beta\sigma=\alpha+mt
.
Put
a:=\alpha+mt
,
b:=\beta
and
c:=\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
a,b\in R
. Elements
\overline{a}
and
\overline{b}
are associates in
R_{m}
if and only if
(a,m)=(b,m)
.

Proof.

If
\overline{a}=\overline{b}\cdot\overline{c}
, then there are always exist
a,b,c\in R
such that
a=bc
by Lemma
1
.
We will use this fact freely. Set
\mu_{a}:=(a,m)
and
\mu_{b}:=(b,m)
.

\Leftarrow
. Clearly,
a=\mu_{a}a_{1}
and
m=\mu_{a}m_{1}
, where
(a_{1},m_{1})=1
and
a_{1},m_{1}\in R
. Thus, there exist
u,v\in R
such that
a_{1}u+m_{1}v=1
and for any
r\in R
we have

(3)

a_{1}(u+rm_{1})+m_{1}(v-ra_{1})=1.

As
(u,m_{1})=1
, this means that
(u,m_{1},\;m)=1
. Since
R
is a commutative Bézout domain of stable range
1.5
and
m\not=0
, there exists
r_{0}\in R
such that
(u+r_{0}m_{1},\;m)=1
. Hence
\overline{u+r_{0}m_{1}}\in U(R_{m})
. Putting
r=r_{0}
in (
3
) and multiplying by
\mu_{a}
we get that

(4)

a(u+r_{0}m_{1})+m(v-r_{0}a_{1})=\mu_{a},

so
\overline{a}
and
\overline{\mu}_{a}
are associates in
R_{m}
. Repeating the same reasoning, we get
\overline{b}
and
\overline{\mu}_{a}
are associates in
R_{m}
. By transitivity of the associability relation, the elements
\overline{a}
and
\overline{b}
are also associates.

\Rightarrow
. Let
\overline{a}=\overline{b}\cdot\overline{e}
, where
\overline{e}\in U(R_{m})
. There exist
a,b\in R
, such that
a=be
in which
(e,m)=1
by Lemma
1
. Consequently,
(a,m)=(be,m)=(b,m)
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Any element
\overline{a}
in
R_{m}
can be written as
\overline{a}=\overline{\mu}_{a}\;\overline{e}_{a}
, where
{\mu_{a}}:=(a,m)
is a preimage of
\overline{\mu}_{a}
,
a
is a preimage of
\overline{a}
, and
\overline{e}_{a}\in U(R_{m})
.

Proof.

We use notation of Lemma
2
.
Clearly,

\overline{a}\;(\overline{u+r_{0}m_{1}})=\overline{(a,m)}=\overline{\mu}_{a}

by (
4
) and
\overline{a}=\overline{\mu}_{a}\;\overline{e}_{a}
,
\overline{e}_{a}=(\overline{u+r_{0}m_{1}})^{-1}
.
Since g.c.d. of each element from the coset
a+mR
with the element
m
is equal to
(a,m)
, the proof is done.
∎

Note that the presentation of
\overline{a}\in R_{m}
in Lemma

Example 1

If

are multiples of each others, then
\overline{a}
and
\overline{b}
are associates in

.

Proof.

If
\overline{a}=\overline{b}\>\overline{c}

. Set
\mu_{a}:=(a,m)

\mu_{b}:=(b,m)

Similarly, from

\mu_{a}|\mu_{b}
. Since

Lemma 5

If

{\rm Ann}(\overline{b})=\overline{\alpha}_{b}R_{m}
, where
\alpha_{b}:=\frac{m}{\mu_{b}}\in R

Proof.

The ideal

consists of the images of

\textstyle\frac{b}{\mu_{b}}s=\frac{m}{\mu_{b}}p\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{m}{\mu_{b}}|\frac{b}{\mu_{b}}s.

We conclude that
\frac{m}{\mu_{b}}|s
and
s=\frac{m}{\mu_{b}}s^{\prime}
for some

because
\left(\frac{m}{\mu_{b}},\frac{b}{\mu_{b}}\right)=1
. Consequently,
\overline{c}=\overline{\alpha}_{b}\cdot\overline{s^{\prime}}\in\overline{\alpha}_{b}R_{m}
and

.

Since

\frac{m}{(b,m)}t

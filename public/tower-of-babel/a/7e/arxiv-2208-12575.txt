Perov type T-contractive Mappings on Cone b-Metric Spaces with Generalized c-Distance
Talat Nazir, Mujahid Abbas, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
Fixed point results of Perov type mapping which satisfy generalized T T -contractive conditions in the setup of cone b b -metric spaces associated with generalized c c -distance are proved and illustrated by nontrivial examples.



1
Introduction

Fixed point theory provides certain simple tools to solve various
problems arising in nonlinear analysis. The metric fixed point theory deals
with the structure of sets equipped with some notion of distance functions
and operators satisfying certain contraction conditions.

The concept of a
b
-metric space was described by Bakhtin
[
4
]
and Boriceanu
[
6
]
as an extension of a usual metric space. Some
useful fixed point results in the framework of a
b
-metric space were
established in
[
3
,
7
,
10
,
23
]
.

Huang and Zhang
[
16
]
extended the metric space structure
and defined a cone metric space by replacing the range of the distance
function with a normed space equipped with an order induced by the cone.
Then, they obtained some fixed point results in a cone metric space (also,
see
[
32
]
). Later on, the cone metric space was extended to a cone
b
-metric space in
[
13
,
17
]
.

The notion of
w
-distance on metric spaces was introduced by Kada
et al.
[
22
]
for solving non-convex minimization based problems.
While, the notion of
c
-distance on cone metric spaces was defined by Cho
et al.
[
8
]
that could be viewed as the cone model of a
w
-distance.
The concept of a
wt
-distance was initiated by Hussain et al.
[
18
]
on
a
b
-metric space and several fixed point results were proved using the
wt
-distance on a
b
-metric space (also, see
[
15
,
24
]
). Recently,
the notion of a generalized
c
-distance in the setup of cone
b
-metric
space was described by Bao et al.
[
5
]
and some fixed point theorems
were proved in this new framework.

On the other hand, Perov
[
25
]
employed contraction
matrices rather than the contraction constants to extend well known Banach
contraction principle. Fixed point results for Perov type contractions in a
cone metric space were presented in
[
11
,
12
]
. Abbas
et al.
[
1
]
obtained common fixed points of multivalued Perov type
contractive mappings the setup of a cone metric space equipped a directed
graph.

In this paper, we prove several fixed point results of Perov type
generalized
T
-contractive mappings defined on cone
b
-metric space
equipped with a generalized
c
-distance. The obtained results unify, extend and
generalize several results in the current literature.

2
Preliminaries

In the sequel

\mathbb{N},\mathbb{N}_{0},\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}

will denote the set of natural numbers, natural numbers with
0
, positive real numbers and real numbers, respectively.

Let
E
be a real Banach space. A cone
P
is the subset of
E

that satisfies

(i)

P
is closed, non-empty and
P\neq\{\theta\}

(where
\theta
is the zero element of
E
);

(ii)

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

\alpha,\beta\geq 0

with
a,b\in P
implies
\alpha a+\beta b\in P;

(iii)

-P\cap P=\{\theta\}
.

With a cone
P
, the partial ordering
\preceq
on
E
is
defined as:
u_{1}\preceq u_{2}
if
u_{2}-u_{1}\in P
and conversely.
Here
u_{1}\prec u_{2}
is used to indicate that
u_{1}\preceq u_{2}
but
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
and
u_{1}\ll u_{2}
means that
u_{2}-u_{1}

belongs to the interior of
P
denoted by
intP.
If
intP\neq\theta,

then
P
is known as solid cone.

A cone
P
is said to be semi monotone or normal if there exists a
positive constant
\kappa>0
such that
\theta\preceq u\preceq v
implies
that
\left\|u\right\|\leq\kappa\left\|v\right\|
for all

u,v\in P
. The smallest value of
\kappa
is known as the normal constant
of normal cone
P
. The definition of normal cone
P
is also equivalent to the following condition that

\inf\{\parallel u_{1}+u_{2}\parallel:u_{1},u_{2}\in P\text{ and}\parallel u_{1}\parallel=\parallel u_{2}\parallel=1\}>0.

If
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}
and

v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then
u\preceq v
means that
u_{i}\leq v_{i},
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
In this way,
P
becomes a normal cone with
\kappa=1
defined as

P=\{u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
13
,
17
]
)
.

Let
X
be a nonempty
set and
E
a real Banach space equipped with the partial ordering induced
by the cone
P\subset E
. For a real number
b\geq 1
and for all
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in X,
suppose that the mapping
d:X\times X\rightarrow E

satisfies the following conditions:

(b1)

\theta\preceq d(u_{1},u_{2})
and
d(u_{1},u_{2})=\theta
if
and only if
u_{1}=u_{2}
;

(b2)

d(u_{1},u_{2})=d(u_{2},u_{1})
;

(b3)

d(u_{1},u_{2})\preceq b[d(u_{1},u_{2})+d(u_{2},u_{3})].

Then
d
is called a cone
b
-metric on
X
and the pair
(X,d)

is known as a cone
b
-metric space.

Clearly, for
b=1
, the cone
b
-metric space becomes a cone
metric space. Moreover, for
E=\mathbb{R}
and
P=[0,\infty)
, the cone
b
-metric on
X
becomes
b
-metric on
X
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X=\left[0,1\right],

E=X\times X\
and
P=\{\left(x,y\right)\in E:x,y\geq 0\}=E.
Define
d:X\times X\rightarrow E
by
d(x,y)=\left(\left(\phi\left(x,y\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(x,y\right)\right)^{p}\right)
, where
\alpha\geq 1
and
p\geq 1\
and
\phi:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies
\phi\left(x,y\right)=0
if and only if
x=y;

\phi\left(x,y\right)=\phi\left(y,x\right)\
and
\phi\left(x,z\right)\leq\phi\left(x,y\right)+\phi\left(y,z\right).
Then
(X,d)
is a
complete cone
b
-metric space with
b\geq 2^{p-1}\geq 1
along with
P
a
solid cone.

Note that
d
trivially satisfies
(b1)
and
(b2)
.
The condition
(b3)
is satisfied since
\left(a+b\right)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(a^{p}+b^{p}\right)
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+},
and hence

\displaystyle d(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\phi\left(x,y\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(x,y\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\phi\left(x,z\right)+\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(x,z\right)+\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\left(\phi\left(x,z\right)\right)^{p}+\left(\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\left(\phi\left(x,z\right)\right)^{p}+(\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\left(\phi(x,z\right))^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(x,z\right)\right)^{p})+(\left(\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p},\left(\phi\left(z,y\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b\left(d\left(x,z\right)+d\left(z,y\right)\right).

Thus
d
is a cone
b
-metric on
X
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
b
-cone metric space,
a\in E
where
\theta\ll a
, and
\{u_{n}\}
a sequence in

Then

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

If every Cauchy sequence in

is
called a complete cone metric space.

A cone
b
-metric space

is said to be complete if every Cauchy
sequence

in

.

If a cone is normal, then the sequence

converges to a

as

Example 2.5
.

Let
X=[0,1],

E=\mathbb{R}^{2}\
and
P=\{\left(x,y\right)\in E:x,y\geq 0\}\subseteq E.

Define
d:X\times X\rightarrow E

(X,d)

Define
q\left(x,y\right)=\left(\psi\left(y\right),\eta\psi\left(y\right)\right),
where
\eta\geq 1
and
\psi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

where

Then

is a generalized
c
-distance on

Note that
q
is trivially satisfying

For
(q2)
, for any

\displaystyle q\left(x,z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(z\right),\eta\psi\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(az^{p},\eta az^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b\left(a\left(y^{p}+z^{p}\right),\eta a\left(y^{p}+z^{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\left(\left(ay^{p},\eta ay^{p}\right)+\left(az^{p},\eta az^{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\left(q\left(x,y\right)+q\left(y,z\right)\right).

For
(q3)

Since

that is

which gives

Finally to show
(q4)
, we need to show that if
e^{\ast}\in intP

\theta\ll e

Now

\displaystyle d(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right)+\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p},\alpha\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right)+\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right))^{p}+(\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right))^{p},\alpha\left(\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right)\right)^{p}+(\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\left((\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right))^{p},\alpha\left(\left(\phi\left(u_{1},u_{3}\right)\right)^{p})+((\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right))^{p},\alpha(\phi\left(u_{3},u_{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(\left(k_{1}\psi\left(u_{1}\right),\alpha k_{1}\psi\left(u_{1}\right)\right)+\left(k_{2}\psi\left(u_{2}\right),\alpha k_{2}\psi\left(u_{2}\right)\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(k_{1}\left(e_{1},\frac{\alpha}{\eta}e_{2}\right)+k_{2}\left(e_{1},\frac{\alpha}{\eta}e_{2}\right))\quad\quad\mbox{
{(}$k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0${)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)\left(e_{1},\frac{\alpha}{\eta}e_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(e_{1}^{\ast},e_{2}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\ast},

where

Clearly
\theta\ll e,
and for any
u_{1}
and

such that

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{2^{p-1}a\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)}}\sqrt[p]{e_{1}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{2^{p-1}a\alpha\left(k_{1}+k_{2}\right)}}\sqrt[p]{e_{2}^{\ast}},

The role of vector valued norm has much importance in the study of
semi-linear operator systems. For details, we refer to
[
26
]
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(E)
be the set of all bounded linear operators on

E

be the set of all linear operators on

Note that
\mathcal{B}(E)

Let us remark that if

(i)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{n}
is absolutely
convergent;

(ii)

the element

is invertible in
\mathcal{B}(E)

We start with the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete cone
b
-metric space,
P
a solid cone and
q
a generalized
c
-distance on
X.

Suppose that
T:X\rightarrow X
is a one to one, continuous and sequentially
convergent. If the mapping
f:X\rightarrow X
is a Perov
THR_{1}-
contraction, then
f
has a unique fixed point
u^{\ast}\in X.
And for any

x_{0}\in X,
the iterative sequence
\{f^{n}x_{0}\}
converges to the fixed
point of
f
. Moreover
q(Tu^{\ast},Tu^{\ast})=\theta
provided that
u^{\ast}=fu^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}
be any element in
X.
Set
x_{1}=fx_{0},

x_{2}=fx_{1}=f^{2}x_{0},..._{\cdot}
Define a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
by
x_{n+1}=f^{n}x_{0}=fx_{n}.
As the mapping
f
is a
Perov
THR_{1}-
contraction, we have

q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})=q(Tfx_{n-1},Tfx_{n}))\leq A(U_{T}(x_{n-1},x_{n})),

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle U_{T}(x_{n-1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\{q(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n}),q(Tx_{n-1},Tfx_{n-1}),q(Tx_{n},Tfx_{n})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n}),q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})\}.

If
U_{T}(x_{n-1},x_{n})=q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1}),
then

q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})\leq A(q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1}))
,
which by the Remark
2.10

implies that
q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})=\theta.

Thus, for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})\leq A(q(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n})).

Continuing this way, we get

q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})\leq A^{n}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1})).

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n,
it follows from
(q_{2})
that

\displaystyle q(Tx_{n},Tx_{m})

\displaystyle\leq b[q(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})+q(Tx_{n+1},Tx_{m})]

\displaystyle\leq bq(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})+b[bq(Tx_{n+1},Tx_{n+2})+q(Tx_{n+2},Tx_{m})]

\displaystyle\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle\leq bq(Tx_{n},Tx_{n+1})+b^{2}q(Tx_{n+1},Tx_{n+2})+\cdots+b^{m-n}q(Tx_{m-1},Tx_{m})

\displaystyle\leq(bA^{n}+b^{2}A^{n+1}\cdots+b^{m-n}A^{m-1})(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq bA^{n}\left(I-bA\right)^{-1}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1})).

(3.2)

Let
c\gg 0.
Choose
\delta>0
such that
c+N_{\delta}(\theta)\subseteq P\,,
where
N_{\delta}(\theta)=\{x\in E:\left\|x\right\|<\delta\}.
Also, choose
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
bA^{n}(I-bA)^{-1}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1}))\in N_{\delta}(\theta)

for all

. Thus for all
m>n>N_{1},
we have

q(Tx_{n},Tx_{m})\preceq bA^{n}(I-bA)^{-1}(d(Tx_{0},Tx_{1}))\ll c

which implies that
\{Tx_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
X
. Since
X
is
complete, there exists an element point
v\in X
such that

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Since
T
is subsequently convergent,
\{x_{n}\}
has a convergent
subsequence. So, there is
x^{\ast}\in X
and subsequence
\{x_{n_{i}}\}

such that
x_{n_{i}}\rightarrow x^{\ast}
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. As
T

is continuous, we obtain
\lim\limits_{n_{i}\rightarrow\infty}Tx_{n_{i}}=Tx^{\ast}
. The uniqueness of the limit implies that
Tx^{\ast}=v
. By (q
{}_{\text{3}}
), we obtain

q(Tx_{n},Tx^{\ast})\leq bA^{n}(I-bA)^{-1}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1})).

(3.3)

Also

\begin{array}[]{cc}q(Tx_{n},Tfx^{\ast})&=q(Tfx_{n-1},Tfx^{\ast})\\
&\leq A(U_{T}(x_{n-1},x^{\ast})),\end{array}

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle U_{T}(x_{n-1},x^{\ast})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\{q(Tx_{n-1},Tx^{\ast}),q(Tx_{n-1},Tfx_{n-1}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q(Tx_{n-1},Tx^{\ast}),q(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast})\}.

Now, if
U_{T}(x_{n-1},x^{\ast})=q(Tx_{n-1},Tx^{\ast}),
then we obtain

\displaystyle q(Tx_{n},Tfx^{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\left(q(Tx_{n-1},Tx^{\ast})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle bA^{n-1}(I-bA)^{-1}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1})).

Whenever

then we have

\displaystyle q(Tx_{n},Tfx^{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\left(q(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle bA^{n}(I-bA)^{-1}(q(Tx_{0},Tx_{1})).

Finally if

then we get

\displaystyle q(Tx_{n},Tfx^{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\left(q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle bA\left(q(Tx^{\ast},Tx_{n})\right)+bA\left(q(Tx_{n},Tfx^{\ast})\right),

\displaystyle bA\left(I-bA\right)^{-1}\left(q(Tx^{\ast},Tx_{n})\right)

(i)

\displaystyle\{q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle\{q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle\{q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast})\}.

in

\displaystyle\{q(Tx^{\ast},Ty^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tfx^{\ast}),q(Ty^{\ast},Tfy^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle\{q(Tx^{\ast},Ty^{\ast}),q(Tx^{\ast},Tx^{\ast}),q(Ty^{\ast},Ty^{\ast})\}

(i)
, it follows that

Since

is one to one, so

. Thus

has a
unique fixed point.

Let
G
be a nilpotent group and
p\in\pi(G)
. If
G
is not a
p
-group, then
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular.

Proof.

Assume that
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then as
G
is not a
p
-group, there exists a non-trivial
p^{\prime}
-subgroup
H
of
G
such that
G\cong H\times P
. Now let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)
be a non-trivial irreducible character of
H
. Then
\chi\times 1_{P}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. Applying
[
24
, Corollary 2.14]
, it is easy to see that
\sum_{g\in G^{0}}(\chi\times 1_{P})(g)=\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)=0
. Hence by Theorem
2.2
, we deduce that
G
has a non-principal
p
-block containing
\chi\times 1_{P}
. Therefore by Theorem
3.2
(a), the graph
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular.
∎

Suppose
G
is a finite group. The
Fitting subgroup

\mathrm{F}(G)
of
G
is the unique largest normal nilpotent subgroup of
G
. For a subgroup
H
of
G
, the
sentralizer
and the
normalizer
of
H
in
G
are defined as
\mathrm{C}_{G}(H):=\{g\in G|gh=hg,\,for\,all\,h\in H\}
and
\mathrm{N}_{G}(H):=\{g\in G|ghg^{-1}\in H,\,for\,all\,h\in H\}
, respectively. A subgroup
H
of the group
G
is
subnormal
, if there exists a finite chain of subgroups of the group
G
, each one normal in the next, beginning at
H
and ending at
G
. The cyclic group of order
n
is denoted by
C_{n}
. Let
p
be a prime. A
p
-element
of
G
is an element
g\in G
such that the order of
g
is a power of
p
. Finally, the
p
-core

O_{p}(G)
of the group
G
is the largest normal
p
-subgroup of
G
. We now apply Theorem
3.2
to obtain new classes of singular Cayley graphs.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume that
G
is a finite group and
p\in\pi(G)
. If one of the following conditions holds, then
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is a singular graph;

a)

There exists a non-trivial subnormal subgroup
H
of
G
such that either
p
does not divide the order of
H
, or
\Gamma_{p}(H)
is singular.

b)

For some
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
, there exists a subgroup
H
of
G
such that
P\mathrm{C}_{G}(P)\subseteq H\subseteq\mathrm{N}_{G}(P)
and
\Gamma_{p}(H)
is singular.

c)

For some
p
-element
x\in G
, the graph
\Gamma_{p}(\mathrm{C}_{G}(x))
is singular.

d)

p
is odd and
G
has a non-abelian minimal normal subgroup
N
.

e)

G
is a non-solvable group with a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup.

f)

The Fitting subgroup
\mathrm{F}(G)
of
G
is not a
p
-subgroup of
G
.

g)

p
is odd and
O_{p}(G)=1
.

Proof.

We carry the proof out by the following cases:

a)

There exists a non-trivial subnormal subgroup
H
of
G
such that either
p
does not divide the order of
H
, or
\Gamma_{p}(H)
is singular:
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
H
is a proper subgroup of
G
. Since
H
is a subnormal subgroup of
G
, for some positive integer
t
, there exist distinct subgroups
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{t}
of
G
such that
H\triangleleft H_{1}\triangleleft H_{2}\triangleleft\dots\triangleleft H_{t}=G
. We do the proof by induction on
t
. Let
t=1
. Then one of the following occurs:

i)

p
does not divide the order of
H
:
Using
[
32
, Theorem 2.12]
,
\mathrm{Irr}(H)=\mathrm{IBr}(H)
. Hence as
H
is non-trivial,
[
32
, Theorem 3.9]
implies that
|\mathrm{Bl}(H)|=|\mathrm{Irr}(H)|\geq 2
. Suppose
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)-\{1_{H}\}
and
B_{0}
is the principal
p
-block of
G
. Then by
[
32
, Theorem 9.2]
, we deduce that
B_{0}
does not cover the block
b:=\{\chi\}
of
H
. Thus there exists a non-principal block
B
of
G
such that
B
covers
b
. Therefore
|\mathrm{Bl}(G)|\geq 2
and Theorem
3.2
(a) completes the proof.

ii)

\Gamma_{p}(H)
is singular:
By Theorem
3.2
,
|\mathrm{Bl}(H)|\geq 2
. Hence
H
has a non-principal block
b
. Using
[
32
, Teorem 9.2]
, the block
b
is not covered by the principal block of
G
. Thus
|\mathrm{Bl}(G)|\geq 2
and again, Theorem
3.2
(a) completes the proof.

Now assume that
k\geq 2
is a positive integer,
t=k
and the assertion is true for
k-1
. If
p
divides the order of
H_{k-1}
, then by induction hypothesis and this fact that
H
is a non-trivial subnormal subgroup of
H_{k-1}
, we have
\Gamma_{p}(H_{k-1})
is singular. Therefore applying the initial case for
H_{k-1}
and
G
, we deduce that
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular.

b)

For some
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
, there exists a subgroup
H
of
G
such that
P\mathrm{C}_{G}(P)\subseteq H\subseteq\mathrm{N}_{G}(P)
and
\Gamma_{p}(H)
is singular:
By Theorem
3.2
(a), the group
H
has a non-principal
p
-block
b
. Also using
[
32
, Theorem 4.14]
and Brauer’s third main theorem
[
32
, Theorem 6.7]
, we deduce that
B:=b^{G}
is a non-principal
p
-block of
G
. Hence
|\mathrm{Bl}(G)|\geq 2
and by Theorem
3.2
, we are done.

c)

For some
p
-element
x\in G
, the graph
\Gamma_{p}(\mathrm{C}_{G}(x))
is singular:
By part (b), it is straight forward.

d)

p
is odd and
G
has a non-abelian minimal normal subgroup
N
:
If
p
does not divide the order of
N
, by part (a), we are done. Hence we can assume that
p
divides the order of
N
.
Since
N
is non-abelian, there exists a non-abelian simple subgroup
S
of
N
such that
N\cong S^{k}
, for some positive integer
k
.
Thus as
S
is a subnormal subgroup of
G
, using Lemma
3.3
and part (a), we deduce that
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular.

e)

G
is a non-solvable group with a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup:
Using
[
30
]
and Theorem
3.2
(a), we have nothing to prove.

f)

The Fitting subgroup
\mathrm{F}(G)
of
G
is not a
p
-subgroup of
G
:
By Lemma
3.4
and part (a), we are done.

g)

p
is odd and
O_{p}(G)=1
:
Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Since
O_{p}(G)
is the largest normal
p
-subgroup of
G
, the group
N
is not a
p
-subgroup of
G
. Thus either
N
is non-abelian or for some prime
q\neq p
, the group
N
is a non-trivial elementary abelian
q
-group. In the later case, part (a) implies that the graph
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular. Now suppose
N
is non-abelian. Then using part (d), we are done. ∎

Remark 3.6
.

Assume that
G
is the sporadic simple group
M_{22}
. Using
[
8
]
, we can see that the graph
\Gamma_{2}(G)
is not singular. This shows that the assumption ”
p
is odd” is necessary in Corollary
3.5
(d).

To determine the significance of these results, we wish to construct a so large class of integral singular graphs. For this goal, we associate an integral singular graph to each pair
(G,p)
, where
G
ranges over all finite groups, and
p
over all prime divisors of the order of
G
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose
G
is a finite group. Then for every prime
p\in\pi(G)
, the graph
\Gamma_{p}(G\times C_{6})
is singular.

Proof.

If
p=2
, then as the group
G\times C_{6}
has a nilpotent normal subgroup isomorphic to
C_{6}
, using Lemma
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
(a), we are done. Thus we can assume that
p
is odd. If
O_{p}(G)=1
, then by Corollary
3.5
(g),
\Gamma_{p}(G\times C_{6})
is singular. Now let
O_{p}(G)\neq 1
. It is clear that
O_{p}(G)\times C_{2}
is a nilpotent normal subgroup of
G
which is not a
p
-group. Hence applying Lemma
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
(a), we deduce that
\Gamma_{p}(G\times C_{6})
is singular.
∎

4
Proof of our main results

In this section, we wish to prove our main results. Assume that
N
is a normal subgroup of a group
G
. When we work with
G/N
, we can identify
\mathrm{Irr}(G/N)
with
\{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)|\,N\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)\}
, where for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, the
kernel of
\chi
is defined as
\mathrm{Ker}(\chi):=\{g\in G|\,\chi(g)=\chi(1)\}
. For every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, we set
\lambda_{\chi}(G,p):=1/\chi(1)|\sum_{x\in\Omega_{p}(G)}\chi(x)|
. We begin with the following observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group, for some
p\in\pi(G)
. Then

\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p).

Proof.

By Theorems
3.1
and
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)-\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p).

Applying
[
32
, Theorem 10.20]
, we deduce that
\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})
is the set of all irreducible characters of
G
lying over the trivial character
1_{O_{p^{\prime}}(G)}
and thus
\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})=\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))
. Hence

\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
a) Applying
[
32
, Theorem 10.20]
, we deduce that
\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})=\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))
. Also by
[
24
, Corollary 2.7]
,
\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))}\chi(1)^{2}=r_{p}(G)
. Thus using Theorem
3.2
(b), we are done.

b) Using Theorem
3.2
(c),
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is integral.

c) Since
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is a graph with precisely
c_{p}(G)
connected components, using Perron-Frobenius Theorem, we deduce that
d_{p}(G)
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{p}(G)
with multiplicity
c_{p}(G)
. Now suppose
M:=\{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))|\,\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)=d_{p}(G)\}
. Using Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in M}\chi(1)^{2}d_{p}(G)+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p).

Now let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M
. Applying Theorems
3.1
and
2.2
, we deduce that
\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)
is non-zero. Thus by part (b), we have
\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)
is a positive integer. Hence using
[
24
, Corollary 2.7]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))

\displaystyle\geq d_{p}(G)c_{p}(G)+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{p}(G)(d_{p}(G)-1)+r_{p}(G).

(1)

Since
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is a
d_{p}(G)
-regular graph of order
|G|
, it is clear that the graph
\Gamma_{p}(G)
has
|G|d_{p}(G)/2
edges. Hence applying part (a) and
[
4
, Corollary 4]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))\geq\sqrt{|G|d_{p}(G)+r_{p}(G)(r_{p}(G)-1)}.

(2)

Hence using inequalities (1) and (2), we conclude that
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G)
is at least the maximum of

\{c_{p}(G)(d_{p}(G)-1)+r_{p}(G),\sqrt{|G|d_{p}(G)+r_{p}(G)(r_{p}(G)-1)}\}.

d) Applying Theorem
3.2
(b) and
[
10
, Theorem 3.13]
, the diameter of
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is at most
|\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0})|
. Hence as every Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is a defect group of
B_{0}
, Theorem
2.3
completes the proof.∎

If Brauer’s
k(B)
-conjecture (see sect. 2) is true for principal blocks of finite groups, then we can state Theorem
1.1
(d) for every finite group. Thus we can propose the following conjecture;

Conjecture 4.2
.

Suppose
G
is a finite group. Then for every prime
p\in\pi(G)
, the diameter of
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is at most
|G|_{p}
.

Now we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

We claim that
\Omega_{p}(G)=PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, where
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. It is easy to see that
PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\Omega_{p}(G)
. Thus it suffices to show that
\Omega_{p}(G)\subseteq PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. On the contrary, suppose
x\in\Omega_{p}(G)-(PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G))
. Then there exist a non-trivial
p
-element
x_{p}
and a non-trivial
p^{\prime}
-element
x_{p^{\prime}}
such that
x=x_{p}x_{p^{\prime}}=x_{p^{\prime}}x_{p}
.
Since
G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is a

p
-group or a Frobenius group,
PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is a normal subgroup of
G
. If
x_{p^{\prime}}\in O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, then as every Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
is contained in
PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, we deduce that
x=x_{p}x_{p^{\prime}}\in PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)
which is a contradiction. Thus
x_{p^{\prime}}\notin O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
.
Hence
x_{p^{\prime}}O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is a non-trivial element of
C_{G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)}(x_{p}O_{p^{\prime}}(G))
which is a contradiction with the structure of
G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
(see
[
23
, Theorem 6.4]
). Therefore
\Omega_{p}(G)=PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Clearly,
H_{p}(G)=PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Since
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is a
d_{p}(G)
-regular graph with precisely
c_{p}(G)
connected components, applying Perron-Frobenius Theorem, we deduce that
d_{p}(G)=|H_{p}(G)|-|O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{p}(G)
with multiplicity
c_{p}(G)
. Now let
M:=\{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))|\,\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)=d_{p}(G)\}
.
Thus by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=|PO_{p^{\prime}}(G)-O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|c_{p}(G)+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=(|H_{p}(G)|-|O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|)c_{p}(G)+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)

\displaystyle=|G|-|G|_{p^{\prime}}+\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}\lambda_{\chi}(G,p).

Now let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M
and
y\in O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. If
P\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
, then
\Omega_{p}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
and we get
\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)=d_{p}(G)
which is a contradiction with this fact that
\chi\notin M
. Thus
P\nsubseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
. Hence using
[
24
, Corollary 2.14]
and this fact that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
, we deduce that
\sum_{z\in Py-\{1\}}\chi(z)/\chi(1)=-1
. Thus
\lambda_{\chi}(G,p)=|O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|
. Therefore applying
[
24
, Corollary 2.7]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{p}(G))

\displaystyle=|G|-|G|_{p^{\prime}}+|O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G))-M}\chi(1)^{2}

\displaystyle=|G|-|G|_{p^{\prime}}+|O_{p^{\prime}}(G)|(r_{p}(G)-c_{p}(G))

\displaystyle=2|G|-2|G|_{p^{\prime}}.

Hence
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is a non-hyperenergetic graph and this completes the proof.
∎

We end this section by the proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
:
On the contrary, suppose
p,q\in\pi(G)
are two distinct primes such that
\Gamma_{p}(G)
and
\Gamma_{q}(G)
are non-singular. Applying Theorem
1.1
(a), we deduce that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=O_{q^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
p
is odd. Since
O_{q^{\prime}}(G)=1
, we have
O_{p}(G)=1
. Hence using Theorem
3.2
(g),
\Gamma_{p}(G)
is singular which is a contradiction.∎

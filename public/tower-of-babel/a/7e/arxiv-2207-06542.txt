On Connections and their Curvatures
Gustavo Amilcar Saldaña Moncada & Gregor Weingart

Abstract
. This paper presents a brief study on connections on fiber, principal and vector smooth bundles as well as some relations with their curvatures. MSC 2010: 57R22. Keywords: Fiber bundles, non–linear connections, curvature



1.
Introduction

The study of bundles and their
ad hoc
connections is one of the must important subjects in differential geometry.
For example, the concept of vector bundle and linear connection appear on every introductory course in the form of the tangent bundle and the Levi–Civita connection, respectively. Principal bundles also play an important role; for example the frame bundle of a Riemannian manifold reflects different ways to give an orthonormal basis for tangent spaces, not to mentiontion that field theories in modern physics are developed by using principal bundles and principal connections
[
1
]
. All these objects arise from the more general concepts of fiber bundles and non–linear connections. This work’s aim is to present a brief study on connections on fiber, principal and vector smooth bundles as well as some relations with their curvatures.

This paper corresponds to an extended version of the second section of
[
3
]
, but focused in geometrical relations, not the functioral ones and it breaks down as follows: in the second section we present the basics of the theory of fiber bundles and non–linear connections, as well as the relations of the curvature. The third section is about principal connections on principal bundles; while the fourth section is about vector bundles and linear connections. Finally in the appendix A we present some calculations related to the curvature and its relation with covariant derivatives in local coordinates.

2.
Non–Linear Connection

A fiber bundle over a manifold
M
with model fiber manifold

\mathscr{F}
is a manifold
\mathscr{F}M
endowed with a smooth projection map
\pi:\,\mathscr{F}M\longrightarrow M
, which is locally trivializable in the sense that
there exists an open covering of
M
by open subsets
U\,\subset\,M
,
each of which allows for a diffeomorphism

\Phi:\;\;U\;\times\;\mathscr{F}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\pi^{-1}(\,U\,)

such that
\pi\,\circ\,\Phi:\,U\times\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow U
equals the
projection to
U
. The preimage of a point
p\,\in\,M
under
\pi
is called
the fiber of the bundle over
p
, it is a submanifold
\mathscr{F}_{p}M\,:=\,\pi^{-1}(\,p\,)\,\subset\,\mathscr{F}M
of the total space
\mathscr{F}M
diffeomorphic to the model
fiber
\mathscr{F}
. Morphisms between two fiber bundles are smooth maps
\varphi:\,\mathscr{F}M\longrightarrow\hat{\mathscr{F}}M
between the total spaces which commute with the respective
projections

(1)

and thus map the fibers of
\mathscr{F}M
to the fibers of
\hat{\mathscr{F}}M
over the same
point.

Intuitively, a non–linear or Ehresmann
connection on a fiber bundle
\mathscr{F}M
is a left hand side section
\nabla

of the short exact sequence of vector bundles

(2)

0\;\longrightarrow\;\hbox to0.0pt{\begin{picture}(0.0,0.0)\qbezier{}{89}{11}{66}{22}{45}{11}\put(45.0,11.0){\vector{-2}{-1}{0.0}}\put(60.0,20.0){$\nabla$}\end{picture}\hss}\mathrm{Vert}\;\mathscr{F}M\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subset}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;T\,\mathscr{F}M\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\pi^{*}TM\;\longrightarrow\;0

over
\mathscr{F}M
, the corresponding right hand side section is appropriately
called the horizontal lift associated to
\nabla
. In order to studying connections
we prefer the following version of this intuitive notion of
a non–linear connection:

Definition 2.1

(Non–linear Connections on Fiber Bundles)
.

A general or a non–linear connection on a fiber bundle
\mathscr{F}M
over a manifold
M

is a field
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}

\in

\Gamma(\,\mathscr{F}M,\,\mathrm{End}\,T\mathscr{F}M\,)
of projections

(\mathbb{P}^{\nabla})^{2}\,=\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on the tangent bundle
T\mathscr{F}M
such that its
image distribution equals the vertical foliation:

\mathrm{im}\;\Big(\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}_{f}:\;\;T_{f}\mathscr{F}M\;\longrightarrow\;T_{f}\mathscr{F}M\;\Big)\;\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\;\;\mathrm{Vert}_{f}\mathscr{F}M\ .

Every non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on a fiber bundle
\mathscr{F}M
allows us
to define the directional derivative
D^{\nabla}_{X}f\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,)
of a given local section
f\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathscr{F}M\,)

in the direction of a vector field
X
on
M
by:

(\;D^{\nabla}_{X}f\;)_{p}\;\;:=\;\;\Big(\;T_{p}M\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*,\,p}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;T_{f(p)}\mathscr{F}M\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}_{f(p)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\mathrm{Vert}_{f(p)}\mathscr{F}M\;\Big)\;X_{p}\ .

These directional derivatives assemble into a first order differential
operator

(3)

D^{\nabla}:\;\;\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)\;\times\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathscr{F}M\,)\;\longrightarrow\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,)\ ,

which is the non–linear analogue of the classical definition of covariant
derivatives on vector bundles. Somewhat annoyingly this covariant derivative

D^{\nabla}_{X}f
contains the redundant information
f\,=\,\pi_{\mathscr{F}M}\,\circ\,D^{\nabla}_{X}f
, where
\pi_{\mathscr{F}M}
denotes the vertical tangent bundle
projection
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}M
. The simplicity of linear
and principal connections stems from the fact that we can get rid of
this redundancy altogether, the reduced covariant derivative
\nabla_{X}f

captures only the partial derivatives of the section
f
.

The Nijenhuis or curvature tensor of a non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}

on a fiber bundle
\mathscr{F}M
over a manifold
M
is the horizontal
2
–form

R^{\nabla}
on the total space
\mathscr{F}M
of the fiber bundle with values in
the vertical tangent bundle defined for two arbitrary vector fields

X,\,Y
on
\mathscr{F}M
by:

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(\;X,\,Y\;)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,[\;(\,\mathrm{id}-\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,X,\;(\,\mathrm{id}-\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,Y\;]\;-\;(\,\mathrm{id}-\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,[\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}X,\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}Y\;]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,[\,X,\,Y\,]\,+\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,[\,X,\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}Y\,]\,+\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,[\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}X,\,Y\,]\,-\,[\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}X,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}Y\,]\ .

The strange sign is necessary to make this definition agree with the
classical definition of the curvature of a linear connection, compare
equation (
8
). By construction
R^{\nabla}\,\in\,\Omega^{2}_{\mathrm{hor}}(\,\mathscr{F}M,\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,)
is
C^{\infty}(\,\mathscr{F}M\,)
–bilinear and alternating.
The integrability constraint for the vertical foliation
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M
tells
us
(\,\mathrm{id}\,-\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,[\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}X,\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}Y\,]\,=\,0
,
and the simplified expression

(4)

R^{\nabla}(\;X,\;Y\;)\;\;=\;\;-\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,[\;(\,\mathrm{id}\,-\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,X,\;(\,\mathrm{id}\,-\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,)\,Y\;]\ ,

evidently results in a vertical vector field for all vector fields
X,\,Y

on
\mathscr{F}M
and vanishes for a vertical vector field
X
or
Y
no matter what
is the other. In particular the curvature
R^{\nabla}
measures exactly the
failure of the horizontal distribution
\ker\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,\subseteq\,T\mathscr{F}M

associated to
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
to be integrable.

An interpretation of the curvature tensor along classical lines as a
commutator of covariant derivatives requires a significant amount of
extra work and so we will only sketch the basic idea. The iterated tangent
bundle of a smooth manifold
\mathscr{F}
is a smooth manifold
T(\,T\mathscr{F}\,)
coming
along with a canonical involutive diffeomorphism
\Theta:\,T(\,T\mathscr{F}\,)\longrightarrow T(\,T\mathscr{F}\,)
characterized by

(5)

\Theta\;\left(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}\gamma(\,t,\,\varepsilon\,)\;\right)\;\;=\;\;\left(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}\gamma(\,\varepsilon,\,t\,)\;\right)

for all
\gamma:\,\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}
. By construction,
\Theta
fits
into the commutative diagram

(6)

where
\mathrm{swap}
interchanges the two summands and
\Pi
is the
double projection:

\Pi\left(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}\gamma(\,t,\varepsilon\,)\;\right)\;\;:=\;\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}\gamma(\,0,\,\varepsilon\,)\;\oplus\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma(\,t,\,0\,)\ .

The involution
\Theta
and double projection
\Pi
generalize
directly to iterated vertical tangent bundles, because we may identify
the fibers of
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M
considered as a fiber bundle over

M
in every point
p\,\in\,M
with the iterated tangent bundle

T(\,T[\,\mathscr{F}_{p}M\,]\,)\,=\,[\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,]_{p}

\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M

\Theta
and a double
projection
\Pi:\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\longrightarrow\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,\oplus\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M

\Theta
we extend a
non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on
\mathscr{F}M
to a unique non–linear

\mathbb{P}^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}
on
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M
by stipulating the identity

(7)

D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{X}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}f_{\varepsilon}\;\Big)\;\;=\;\;\Theta\,\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}D^{\nabla}_{X}f_{\varepsilon}\;\Big)

for every vector field
X\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)
and every
smooth one–parameter family
(\,f_{\varepsilon}\,)_{\varepsilon\,\in\,\mathbb{R}}

of local sections
f_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathscr{F}M
with infinitesimal variation

\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}f_{\varepsilon}\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,)
. In this construction of the induced connection
on
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M
, the involution
\Theta
is proper remedy for the nuisance

\begin{array}[]{ccccccl}\Pi\,\Big(&D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{X}(\,\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}f_{\varepsilon}\,)&\!\!\!\Big)\;\;=\;\;\Big(&\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}f_{\varepsilon}&\!\!\!\Big)\,\oplus\,\Big(&D^{\nabla}_{X}f_{0}&\!\!\!\Big)\\[7.0pt]

compare the commutative diagram (

reason the involution
\Theta
appears in the key identity linking the
curvature
R^{\nabla}
of a non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on a fiber
bundle
\mathscr{F}M
over
M
to the commutator

(8)

R^{\nabla}_{X,\,Y}f\;\;=\;\;\Big(\;D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{X}D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{Y}\!\!f\;-\;\Theta(\;D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{Y}D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{X}\!\!f\;)\;\Big)\;-\;D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{[\,X,\,Y\,]}f

of covariant derivatives of a local section

in the direction of vector fields

,
in which

is a simplified notation for the local section

over

defined in

\Big(\;R^{\nabla}_{X,\,Y}f\;\Big)_{p}\;\;:=\;\;R^{\nabla}_{f(p)}(\;f_{*,\,p}X_{p},\;f_{*,\,p}Y_{p}\;)\;\;\in\;\;\mathrm{Vert}_{f(p)}\mathscr{F}M\ .

which determines the inner difference in the curvature identity (

and
\mathbb{P}^{\hat{\nabla}}

of

The constraint
\hat{\pi}\,\circ\,\varphi\,=\,\pi
characterizing morphisms

\varphi_{*}(\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathscr{F}M\,)\,\subset\,\mathrm{Vert}\,\hat{\mathscr{F}}M,

hence a morphism

3.
Principal Connections

Having discussed general non–linear connections on fiber bundles in some
detail we now want to specialize to principal connections on principal bundles in this section. Recall first of all that a principal bundle
modelled on a Lie group
G
is a smooth fiber bundle
GM
with model fiber

G
endowed with a smooth right, fiber preserving action of
G
on its
total space
GM

\rho\,:\;\;GM\;\times\;G\;\longrightarrow\;GM,\qquad(\,g\,,\,\gamma\,)\;\longmapsto\;g\,\gamma\ ,

which is simply transitive on all fibers of
GM
over
M
. Every local
section
{\scriptstyle\Gamma}:\,U\longrightarrow GM
of the projection

\pi:\,GM\longrightarrow M
defined over an open subset
U
extends to
a
G
–equivariant local trivialization of
GM
over
U

(9)

\Phi:\;\;U\times G\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\Gamma}\times\mathrm{id}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\pi^{-1}(\,U\,)\times G\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\pi^{-1}(\,U\,)\ ,

satisfying
\Phi(u,g\gamma)\,=\,{\scriptstyle\Gamma}(u)g\gamma\,=\,\Phi(u,g)\,\gamma
by construction. The existence of
G
–equivariant
trivializations ensures that the unique set theoretic map

(10)

\backslash\;:\;\;GM\;\times_{M}\,GM\;\longrightarrow\;G,\qquad(\,g,\,\hat{g}\,)\;\longmapsto\;g^{-1}\,\hat{g}\ ,

satisfying
g\,(g^{-1}\hat{g})\,=\,\hat{g}
for all
g,\,\hat{g}\,\in\,GM
in
the same fiber is actually a smooth map, because it reads

\Phi(u,g)^{-1}\Phi(u,\hat{g})\,=\,g^{-1}\hat{g}

in every such trivialization
\Phi
.

The
automorphism group bundle of a principal bundle
GM
over a manifold
M

is the Lie group bundle
\mathrm{Aut}\;GM
over
M
defined by

(11)

\mathrm{Aut}\,GM\;\;:=\;\;\{\;\;(p,\psi)\;\;|\;\;\psi:\,G_{p}M\longrightarrow G_{p}M\textrm{\ is $G$--equivariant}\;\;\}

with the bundle projection
\pi_{\mathrm{Aut}\,GM}\,:\,\mathrm{Aut}\,GM\longrightarrow M,\,(\,p,\,\psi\,)\longmapsto p
. In mathematical physics, the Fréchet–Lie
group
\Gamma(\,M,\,\mathrm{Aut}\,GM\,)
of all global sections of the automorphism
bundle is called the gauge group of
GM
.

The fiber of the Lie group bundle
\mathrm{Aut}\;GM
over a point
p\,\in\,M
is a
Lie group
\mathrm{Aut}_{p}GM
isomorphic, although not canonically so, to the original
group
G
, in particular its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}_{p}GM\,\cong\,\mathfrak{g}
is
isomorphic to the Lie algebra of
G
. All these Lie algebras assemble
into a smooth Lie algebra bundle
\mathfrak{aut}\,GM
, whose global sections

\Gamma(\,M,\,\mathfrak{aut}\,GM\,)
form the Fréchet–Lie algebra of the gauge
group
\Gamma(\,M,\,\mathrm{Aut}\,GM\,)
of the principal bundle
GM
.

Definition 3.1

(Principal Connections)
.

A principal connection on a principal
G
–bundle
GM
over a manifold

M
is a non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on the fiber bundle
GM
,
which is invariant under the right action of
G
on
GM
in the sense
that the right translations
R_{\gamma}:\,GM\longrightarrow GM,\,g\longmapsto g\gamma,
are parallel automorphisms for all
\gamma\,\in\,G
.

In difference to general fiber bundles, the vertical tangent bundle
of a principal bundle
GM
is trivializable: The construction of the
Maurer–Cartan form on a Lie group
G
applies verbatim and results
in the vertical trivialization isomorphism

\mathrm{Vert}\,GM\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}GM\times\mathfrak{g},\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\longmapsto(g_{0},\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g^{-1}_{0}g_{t})

under the proviso that
the curve
t\longmapsto g_{t}
chosen to represent the vertical tangent
vector stays in the fiber so that the curve
t\longmapsto g_{0}^{-1}g_{t}

is defined by application (
10
). The composition of this vertical
trivialization with the projection to
\mathfrak{g}

(12)

induces isomorphisms
\mathrm{vtriv}_{g}:\,\mathrm{Vert}_{g}GM\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}

at every
g\,\in\,GM
and thus a bijection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,\longleftrightarrow\,\omega
between non–linear connections and
\mathfrak{g}
–valued
1
–forms

\omega\,\in\,\Omega^{1}(\,GM,\,\mathfrak{g}\,)
, which agree with
\mathrm{vtriv}
on
vertical tangent vectors:

(13)

\omega\;\;:=\;\;\mathrm{vtriv}\,\circ\,\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\;\;:=\;\;\mathrm{vtriv}^{-1}\,\circ\,\omega\ .

This description of non–linear connections on
GM
by
\mathfrak{g}
–valued

1
–forms
\omega
with
\left.\omega\right|_{\mathrm{Vert}\,GM}\,=\,\mathrm{vtriv}

is much more convenient and will be used for the rest of this article.

To characterize the principal connections specified in Definition

3.1
by their connection forms
\omega
we recall that the
right translation by
\gamma\,\in\,G
is a parallel morphism

R_{\gamma}:\,GM\longrightarrow GM,\;g\longmapsto g\gamma,

if and only
if its differential
R_{\gamma*}
maps the horizontal vectors

\ker\,\omega_{g}\,\subset\,T_{g}GM
at a point
g\,\in\,GM
to horizontal
vectors
\ker\omega_{g\gamma}\,\subset\,T_{g\gamma}GM
. On the
complementary vertical vectors the connection form behaves like

\mathrm{vtriv}\,=\,\left.\omega\right|_{\mathrm{Vert}\,GM}
:

(\mathrm{vtriv}\,\circ\,R_{\gamma*})\Big(\!\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\Big)\;\;=\;\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\!(\gamma g_{0})^{-1}(g_{t}\gamma)\;\;=\;\;\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}}\mathrm{vtriv}\Big(\!\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\Big)\ .

In consequence the condition
\omega\,\circ\,R_{\gamma*}\,=\,\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}}\,\circ\,\omega
for all
\gamma\,\in\,G
is
necessary and sufficient for a
\mathfrak{g}
–valued
1
–form
\omega
agreeing
on vertical vectors with
\mathrm{vtriv}\,=\,\left.\omega\right|_{\mathrm{Vert}\,GM}

to be the connection form of a principal connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
. The
chain rule in the guise of a Leibniz rule tells us for such a form

\displaystyle\omega_{g_{0}\gamma_{0}}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\,\gamma_{t}\;\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{g_{0}\gamma_{0}}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\,\gamma_{0}\;+\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{0}\,\gamma_{t}\;\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{g_{0}\gamma_{0}}\Big(\;(\,R_{\gamma_{0}}\,)_{*}\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\;\Big)\;+\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}(g_{0}\gamma_{0})^{-1}(g_{0}\gamma_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}_{0}}\;\omega_{g_{0}}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\;\Big)\;+\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}\gamma_{t}

for every curve
t\longmapsto g_{t}
in
GM
and
t\longmapsto\gamma_{t}
in
G
.
Put differently:

Lemma 3.2

(Principal Connection Axiom)
.

On every principal
G
–bundle
GM
the association
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\,\longleftrightarrow\,\omega
characterized by
\omega\,:=\,\mathrm{vtriv}\circ\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
induces a bijection between principal connections in the sense
of Definition
3.1
and
\mathfrak{g}
–valued
1
–forms
\omega
on
GM

satisfying the axiom

\omega_{g_{0}\gamma_{0}}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\,\gamma_{t}\;\Big)\;\;=\;\;\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}_{0}}\;\omega_{g_{0}}\Big(\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}g_{t}\;\Big)\;+\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma_{0}^{-1}\gamma_{t}

for all choices of smooth curves
t\longmapsto g_{t}
in
GM
and curves

t\longmapsto\gamma_{t}
in
G
.

Cartan’s Second Structure Equation
[
1
]
is a convenient description
of the image of the composition of the curvature tensor
R^{\nabla}
with the
vertical trivialization
\mathrm{vtriv}
in terms of the exterior derivative of the
connection form

(14)

\Omega\;\;:=\;\;\mathrm{vtriv}\,\circ\,R^{\nabla}\;\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\;\;d\omega\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,[\,\omega\,\wedge\,\omega\,]\ ,

where
\frac{1}{2}[\,\omega\wedge\omega\,](X,Y)\,:=\,[\,\omega(X),\,\omega(Y)\,]
;
the usefulness of the factor
\frac{1}{2}
in this definition is beyond question.
Cartan’s Second Structure Equation can be easily verified directly. On the
other hand it can be derived without too much additional effort from the
curvature identity (
8
) by considering the reduced covariant
derivative associated to a principal connection
\omega

\nabla:\;\;\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)\,\times\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,GM\,)\;\longrightarrow\;C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,\mathfrak{g}\,)

defined by
\nabla_{X}f\,:=\,\mathrm{vtriv}(\,D^{\nabla}_{X}f\,)
; in light of equation
(
13

(\;\nabla_{X}f\;)_{p}\;\;:=\;\;\mathrm{vtriv}(\;\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}_{f(p)}(\,f_{*,\,p}X_{p}\,)\;)\;\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\;\;(\,f^{*}\omega\,)_{p}(\,X\,)

for all
X\,\in\,\Gamma(M,TM)
. Iterating the vertical trivialization
isomorphism
\mathrm{Vert}\,GM\,\longrightarrow GM\times\mathfrak{g}
used to define

\mathrm{vtriv}
we obtain the isomorphism:

(15)

\Psi:\;\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,GM\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\mathrm{Vert}(\,GM\times\mathfrak{g}\,)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;(GM\times\mathfrak{g})\,\times\,(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g})\ .

Its inverse can be written down explicitly by unravelling the
definitions

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}(\,g,\,X,\,Y,\,Z\,)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\,\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}g\,e^{tX}\,e^{\varepsilon(\,Y\,+\,tZ\,)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\,\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0}g\,e^{tX\,+\,\varepsilon Y\,+\,t\varepsilon(\,Z\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\,[X,Y]\,)}\ ,

the second equality is the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff Formula
[
2
]

in the Lie group
G
ignoring as usual terms of order
O(\,t^{2},\,\varepsilon^{2}\,)
. In particular the involution
\Theta
defined in equation
(
5
) simply by the interchange
t\,\leftrightarrow\,\varepsilon

is not as innocent as it may appear, under the isomorphism
\Psi
it becomes:

(16)

\Theta(\;g,\;X;\;Y,\;Z\;)\;\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\;\;(\;g,\;Y;\;X,\;Z\,+\,[\,X,\,Y\,]\;)\ .

In accordance with
[
3
]
the vertical trivialization isomorphism
\mathrm{Vert}\,GM\longrightarrow GM\times\mathfrak{g}
is parallel with respect to the connections
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}

and
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\oplus\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{triv}}
on
\mathrm{Vert}\,GM
and
GM\times\mathfrak{g}
respectively,
using the fiber bundle isomorphism
\Psi
we thus obtain

\displaystyle\Psi(\;D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{X}\,D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{Y}\!\!f\;)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\;f,\;(f^{*}\omega)(X);\;(f^{*}\omega)(Y),\;X\,(f^{*}\omega)(Y)\;)

\displaystyle\Psi(\;D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{Y}\,D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{X}\!\!f\;)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\;f,\;(f^{*}\omega)(Y);\;(f^{*}\omega)(X),\;Y\,(f^{*}\omega)(X)\;)

for all local sections
f\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,GM\,)
and all
X,\,Y\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)
. Evidently the double projections under
\Pi

of these two local sections of
\mathrm{Vert}\,\mathrm{Vert}\,GM
do not agree unless we
apply the involution
\Theta
first picking up the rather unexpected
additional term of equation (

\displaystyle D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{X}\,D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{Y}\!\!f\;-\;\Theta(\;D^{\nabla^{\mathrm{Vert}}}_{Y}\,D^{\nabla^{\hphantom{V}}}_{X}\!\!f\;)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\;f,\;X(f^{*}\omega)(Y)\;-\;Y(f^{*}\omega)(X)\;-\;[\,(f^{*}\omega)(Y),\,(f^{*}\omega)(X)\,]\;\Big)

Subtracting
D^{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}f\,=\,(\,f,\,(f^{*}\omega)([X,Y])\,)
we obtain
eventually

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}_{X,\,Y}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\;f,\;d(f^{*}\omega)(X,Y)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}[\,(f^{*}\omega)\,\wedge\,(f^{*}\omega)\,](\,X,\,Y\,)\;)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\;f,\;(\,f^{*}\Omega\,)(\,X,\,Y\,)\;)

for all local sections
f\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,GM\,)
and all
X,\,Y\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,TM\,)
by using the naturality
d(f^{*}\omega)\,=\,f^{*}(d\omega)
of
the exterior derivative
d
and the definition of the
2
–form
\Omega
in
Cartan’s Second Structure Equation (

4.
Linear Connections

The strategy persued for linear connections on vector bundles
VM
follows
the model of principal connections closely. The tangent bundle of a vector
space is canonically trivializable
TV\,\cong\,V\times V
by taking
actual derivatives and this becomes via
[\,\mathrm{Vert}\,VM\,]_{p}\,=\,T(\,V_{p}M\,)

the vertical trivialization

\mathrm{Vert}\,VM\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}VM\oplus VM,\;\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}v_{t}\longmapsto v_{0}\oplus\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(v_{t}-v_{0}),

again under the proviso that the representative curve

t\longmapsto v_{t}
is chosen to stay in the same vector space so that the
quotient
\frac{1}{t}(v_{t}-v_{0})\,\in\,V_{\pi(v_{0})}M
is well–defined. Composing
with the projection to the right factor we obtain

(17)

,

which can be used to project out
\nabla_{X}v\,:=\,\mathrm{vtriv}(D^{\nabla}_{X}v)
the
redundant information from the covariant derivative
D^{\nabla}_{X}v
of a
section
v\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)
:

Definition 4.1

(Linear Connections on Vector Bundles)
.

A linear connection on a vector bundle
VM
on
M
is a non–linear
connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on
VM
such that the reduced covariant derivative
is
\mathbb{R}
–bilinear:

\nabla:\;\;\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)\;\times\;\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)\;\longrightarrow\;\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)\ .

In order to characterize linear connections by a condition easier to verify
we consider a vector bundle
VM
with projection
\pi:\,VM\longrightarrow M

and a local basis of sections
\mathbf{v}_{1},\,\ldots,\,\mathbf{v}_{n}\,\in\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{loc}}(\,M,\,VM\,)
defined over the open domain
U\,\subseteq\,M
of
a local coordinate chart
x:\,U\longrightarrow x(U)\,\subseteq\,\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In
this situation we can construct linear local coordinates for the total space

VM
of the vector bundle
(x,v):\,\pi^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow x(U)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

characterized by:

(\,x^{1},\,\ldots\,x^{m};\,v^{1},\,\ldots,\,v^{n}\,)\;\;\longleftrightarrow\;\;v^{1}\,\mathbf{v}_{1}\;+\;\ldots\;+\;v^{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{n}\;\;\in\;\;V_{(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m})}M\ .

In these linear local coordinates a general non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}

reads

(18)

\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}_{(x,v)}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\Big)\;\;=\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\qquad\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}_{(x,v)}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\Big)\;\;=\;\;\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x,\,v\,)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}

with local coefficient functions
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x,\,v\,)
generalizing
the classical Christoffel symbols, because the coordinate vector fields

\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{1}},\,\ldots,\,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{n}}
span the
vertical tangent bundle in every point of
\pi^{-1}(\,U\,)\,\subseteq\,VM
.

With respect to the local trivialization of the vector bundle
VM
provided
by the local basis
\mathbf{v}_{1},\,\ldots,\,\mathbf{v}_{n}
every section

v\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)
can be expanded as a sum
v\,=\,v^{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}\,+\,\ldots\,+\,v^{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}
with local coefficient functions

v^{1},\,\ldots,\,v^{n}
in
C^{\infty}(U)
, which of course reappear in the
local coordinate description of the section
v\,\in\,\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)

considered as a smooth map:

(\,x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{m}\,)\;\longmapsto\;(\,x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{m};\,v^{1}(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}),\,\ldots,\,v^{n}(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m})\,)\ .

Calculating the differential of
v
in these coordinates is straightforward:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\left(v_{*}\,\Big(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\,\Big)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}\Big(\;\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\;+\;\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(\,x\,)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\;\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\Big(\;\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(\,x\,)\;+\;\Gamma_{\mu}^{\alpha}(\,x,\,v(x)\,)\;\Big)\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\ .

Pushing this expression to a section of
VM
via the vertical trivialization

\mathrm{vtriv}
in diagram (
17
) converts the coordinate vector field

\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
back to the corresponding local section

\mathbf{v}^{\alpha}
, hence it reduces the covariant derivative to:

(19)

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}v\;\;:=\;\;\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\Big(\;\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(\,x\,)\;+\;\Gamma_{\mu}^{\alpha}(\,x,\,v(x)\,)\;\Big)\;\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}\ .

Directly from this expansion we conclude that a connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on

VM
will not be a linear connection in the sense of Definition
4.1

unless its Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mu}(\,x,\,v\,)
are linear in
the coefficient coordinates with an expansion

(20)

\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{m};\,v^{1},\,\ldots,\,v^{n}\,)\;\;=\;\;\sum_{\omega\,=\,1}^{n}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\omega}(\,x^{1},\,\ldots,\,x^{m}\,)\,v^{\omega}

and suitable local coefficient functions
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\omega}(\,x\,)
.
In the chosen linear coordinates the scalar multiplication
\Lambda_{\lambda}:\,VM\longrightarrow VM,\,v\longmapsto\lambda v,
with
\lambda\,\in\,\mathbb{R}
reads

(\,x^{1},\ldots,x^{m};\,v^{1},\ldots,v^{n}\,)\longmapsto(\,x^{1},\ldots,x^{m};\,\lambda v^{1},\ldots,\lambda v^{n}\,)
. It is thus a parallel endomorphism of

VM
with respect to the connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
, if and only if we have
for all
\mu
equality between the two tangent vector expressions

\displaystyle(\,\Lambda_{\lambda}\,)_{*,\,(x,v)}\Big(\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x,\,v\,)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\,\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\lambda\;\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x,\,v\,)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\;\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\sum_{\alpha\,=\,1}^{n}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\,x,\,\lambda v\,)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}

due to
(\,\Lambda_{\lambda}\,)_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}
and
(\,\Lambda_{\lambda}\,)_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\,=\,\lambda\,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
. Note that the tangent vectors on the left and right hand
side of this equation are horizontal in the points
(\,x,\,v\,)
and

(\,x,\,\lambda v\,)
of
VM
respectively.

In consequence the scalar multiplications
\Lambda_{\lambda}
are parallel
endomorphisms of
VM
for all scalars
\lambda\,\in\,\mathbb{R}
, if and only if
all the generalized Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu}(x,v)
are
homogeneous functions of degree one in the coefficient coordinates

v^{1},\,\ldots,\,v^{n}
. On the other hand every smooth homogeneous function
of degree one is actually a linear function, hence we have proved:

Lemma 4.2

(Characterization of Linear Connections)
.

A non–linear connection
\mathbb{P}^{\nabla}
on a vector bundle gives rise
to an
\mathbb{R}
–bilinear covariant derivative
\nabla:\,\Gamma(\,M,\,TM\,)\times\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\,M,\,VM\,)
, if and only
if the multiplication by every
\lambda\,\in\,\mathbb{R}
is a parallel endomorphism:

\Lambda_{\lambda}:\;\;VM\;\longrightarrow\;VM,\qquad v\;\longmapsto\;\lambda\,v\ .

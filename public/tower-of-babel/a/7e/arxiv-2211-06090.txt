Abstract
. M. Goresky and R. MacPherson intersection homology is also defined from the singular chain complex of a filtered space by H. King, with a key formula to make selections among singular simplexes. This formula needs a notion of dimension for subspaces S S of an Euclidean simplex, which is usually taken as the smallest dimension of the skeleta containing S S . Later, P. Gajer employed another dimension based on the dimension of polyhedra containing S S . This last one allows traces of pullbacks of singular strata in the interior of the domain of a singular simplex. In this work, we prove that the two corresponding intersection homologies are isomorphic for Siebenmann’s CS sets. In terms of King’s paper, this means that polyhedral dimension is a “reasonable” dimension. The proof uses a Mayer-Vietoris argument which needs an adapted subdivision. With the polyhedral dimension, that is a subtle issue. General position arguments are not sufficient and we introduce strong general position. With it, a stability is added to the generic character and we can do an inductive cutting of each singular simplex. This decomposition is realised with pseudo-barycentric subdivisions where the new vertices are not barycentres but close points of them.

Introduction

Intersection homology was introduced by Goresky and MacPherson to restore Poincaré duality
for some singular spaces called pseudomanifolds.
They first defined it for PL-pseudomanifolds in
[
9
]
and extended it to
topological pseudomanifolds in
[
10
]
by using a derived category of complexes of sheaves.
A presentation of intersection homology directly from the singular chain complex of a filtered space and its
concordance with the initial definition is made by H. King in
[
11
]
for CS sets (Definition
1.4
).
The starting point is a selection of some specific singular simplexes, called
\overline{p}
-allowable,
from a perversity introduced as a sequence of integers

\overline{p}(n)
(cf. Definition
1.6
)
or as a map
\overline{p}\colon{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, defined on the set of strata of
X
and
taking the value 0 on the regular strata (cf. Definition
1.5
).
Before stating it formally in Definition
2.2
,
let’s contemplate the key formula for a stratum
S
and a simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
,

(0.1)

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-{\rm codim\,}S+\overline{p}(S).

For regular strata, this inequality reduces to

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta
which is always true for any notion of dimension compatible with
the inclusions of sets. So we can restrict ourselves to the singular strata.
Notice that (
0.1
) contains three different kinds of dimension:

•

\dim\Delta
refers to the classical dimension of an Euclidean simplex;

•

{\rm codim\,}S
comes from the filtered dimension of
X
as in Definition
1.1
;

•

\dim\sigma^{-1}S
has to be specified.

Originally in
[
11
]
, King replaces (
0.1
) by “
\sigma^{-1}S
is included in the
(
\dim\Delta-{\rm codim\,}S+\overline{p}(S)
)-skeleton of
\Delta
”; i.e., the dimension of
\sigma^{-1}S
comes from the skeleta of
\Delta
.
This definition works perfectly and this is a common choice for many works in intersection homology,
see the book of G. Friedman (
[
6
]
) for an extensive repertoire of its properties.
It is easy to notice that, for a simplex
\Delta
of dimension
\ell\geq 2
and a perversity
\overline{p}\leq\overline{t}
,
the set
\sigma^{-1}S
is included in the
(\ell-2)
-skeleton of
\Delta
.
In
[
2
,
4
]
, another chain complex is built from singular simplexes
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
, with a filtration

\Delta=\Delta_{0}\ast\dots\ast\Delta_{n}
verifying
\sigma^{-1}X_{i}=\Delta_{0}\ast\dots\ast\Delta_{i}
.
They form a simplicial set denoted by
{\rm Sing}^{{\mathcal{F}}}X
that is not a Kan complex in general and allow a blown-up cohomology
(
[
1
]
) which gives a Poincaré duality (
[
3
]
) with cap products.
For any of these two choices, all the information on the singular
strata are contained in the boundary of
\Delta
.
This is not suitable if we are looking for a notion of intersection homotopy groups.
To achieve a presentation of intersection homology which fits with homotopy groups,
Gajer uses in
[
7
]
a notion of dimension
which allows traces of the pull-back of the singular strata in the interior of the singular simplexes.
For that, one says that a subspace of an Euclidean simplex,

A\subset\Delta
, is of dimension less than or equal to
\ell

if
A
is included in a polyhedron
Q
with
\dim Q\leq\ell
.

Call it the
polyhedral dimension.

Here, a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex is a singular simplex verifying (
0.1
) with the polyhedral
dimension for
\sigma^{-1}S
. A
\overline{p}
-allowable chain is a linear combination of

\overline{p}
-allowable simplexes and the
\overline{p}
-intersection chain complex is formed of
chains
\xi
such that
\xi
and its boundary
\partial\xi
are
\overline{p}
-allowable chains.
In this work, we prove that the homology of this complex coincides with the initial intersection homology,
for Siebenmann’s CS sets (Definition
1.4
).
To do this, we use a method initiated by King (see Theorem
6.3
) who gives
a series of properties which guarantee such identification.
On this problem, King wrote

if one changes the above definition to any reasonable notion of dimension,
one ends up with the same intersection homology groups.

Taking this sentence as a definition for “reasonable” and the construction of King as a reference, we can state the main result of this work (Theorem
6.2
) as follows.

Main Theorem ​​
.

The notion of polyhedral dimension is reasonable.

In the proof of the Main Theorem, a “sensitive issue” is the existence of subdivisions
for having a Mayer-Vietoris sequence.
In classical singular homology, they are the barycentric subdivisions which induce a chain map homotopic to the identity.
In intersection homology, we have to prove the existence of subdivisions keeping the
\overline{p}
-allowability property.
For our present situation, this is a subtle issue.
To illustrate it, consider a filtered space,
X
, of singular set
\Sigma
, and
a singular simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta^{2}\to X
such that
\sigma^{-1}\Sigma=b_{\sigma}
,
where
b_{\sigma}
is the barycentre of
\Delta^{2}
.
The simplex
\sigma
is
\overline{t}
-allowable for the top perversity
\overline{t}
.
But in the barycentric subdivision, the edges containing
b_{\sigma}
are no more of
\overline{t}
-intersection.
To avoid this phenomenon, we act in an inductive way, as in the barycentric subdivision, but
now the new vertices are not necessarily the barycentres but close points to them.
How to choose these points such that any element of this pseudo-barycentric subdivision
is a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex?
At each step, we have two simplexes
and we slightly move one of them to ensure that the new simplexes are in general position.
We can realize this operation if our process is
generic
.
But, at the same time, we need that the simplexes of the previous steps remain in general position. This is a
stability
condition.
The classical notion of general position does not meet these requirements (Example
3.2
)
and
we introduce the
notion of strong general position
in Definition
3.6
.
Such subdivisions are called
pseudo-barycentric subdivisions.

In Propositions
4.4
,
4.5
and
4.8
,
we show that pseudo-barycentric subdivisions preserving the
\overline{p}
-allowability exist, that they induce a chain
map homotopic to the identity and give a small simplexes
\overline{p}
-intersection theorem.

The preservation of
\overline{p}
-allowability is a significative step but we also have to take care of
\overline{p}
-intersection
chains. For that, we prove that
the allowability default of the boundary of an allowable simplex is characterized by the existence of one particular
face, that we call the
bad face.

The final stage for the existence of a Mayer-Vietoris sequence is a characterisation
of these bad faces in the case of a pseudo-barycentric subdivision, cf Proposition
5.5
.
The architecture of the rest of the proof comes from King’s original theorem (
[
11
, Theorem 10]
).
The properties are now in place and the verification of the hypotheses of this theorem follows a classic pattern.

This is time to warn the reader on a possible confusion:
the main part of
[
7
]
is devoted to the definition and investigation
of a simplicial set
I_{\overline{p}}X
associated to a filtered space and a perversity. It is known (
[
8
]
)
that the homology of
I_{\overline{p}}X
is not the
\overline{p}
-intersection homology of
X
.
The simplicial set
I_{\overline{p}}X
does not appear in this work.
We study it in
[
5
]
with a particular attention to its homotopy groups,
the possibility of their topological invariance
in terms of the space
X
and a Hurewicz theorem between them and
\overline{p}
-intersection homology.

Let

be a stratified map between two perverse spaces with
f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
.
The association
\sigma\mapsto f\circ\sigma
sends a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex on a
\overline{q}
-allowable simplex
and defines a chain map

f_{*}\colon C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)\to C_{*}^{\overline{q}}(Y;G)
.

Proof.

Let

\sigma\colon\Delta\to X

be a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.
Since
f_{*}(\partial\sigma)=\partial f_{*}(\sigma)
it suffices to prove
that
f_{*}(\sigma)
is a
\overline{q}
-allowable simplex.
By definition, the simplex
\sigma
verifies

(2.3)

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-D\overline{p}(S)-2,

for each singular stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, and we have to prove

(2.4)

\dim\sigma^{-1}f^{-1}T\leq\dim\Delta-D\overline{q}(T)-2,

for each singular stratum
T\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Y}
.
Since
f
is a stratified map, there exists a family of strata
\{S_{i}\mid i\in I\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{X}

with
f^{-1}(T)=\sqcup_{i\in I}S_{i}
, with
{\rm codim\,}T\leq{\rm codim\,}S_{i}
for each
i\in I
.
In particular, the strata
S_{i}
are singular strata.
Since
\sigma(\Delta)
is compact, the family
J=\{i\in I\mid S_{i}\cap\sigma(\Delta)\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.
We get

\displaystyle\dim\sigma^{-1}f^{-1}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\bigcup_{i\in J}\sigma^{-1}(S_{i})=\max\{\dim\sigma^{-1}(S_{i})\}

\displaystyle\leq_{(1)}

\displaystyle\dim\Delta-\min\{D\overline{p}(S_{i})\mid i\in J\}-2

\displaystyle\leq_{(2)}

\displaystyle\dim\Delta-D\overline{q}(T)-2,

where (1) comes from (
2.3
)
and (2) is a consequence of
f(S_{i})\subset T
and
f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon(X\times[0,1],\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
be
a homotopy between two stratified maps

f,\,g\colon(X,\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
with

\varphi^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
. Then

f
and
g

induce the same map in homology,

f_{*}=g_{*}\colon H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)\to H_{*}^{\overline{q}}(Y;G)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.5
, we get the homomorphism

\varphi_{*}\colon H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X\times[0,1])\to H_{*}^{\overline{q}}(Y)
.
The canonical injections,

\iota_{0},\,\iota_{1}\colon X\to X\times[0,1]
,
defined by
\iota_{k}(x)=(x,k)
for
k=0,\,1
, are stratified maps and induce homomorphisms

\iota_{0,*},\,\iota_{1,*}\colon H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X)\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X\times[0,1])
.
Since

f=\varphi\circ\iota_{0}
and
g=\varphi\circ\iota_{1}
, it suffices to prove

\iota_{0,*}=\iota_{1,*}
.

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta=[e_{0},\dots,e_{m}]\to X
be a simplex.
The vertices of the product

\Delta\times[0,1]
are
a_{j}=(e_{j},0)
and
b_{j}=(e_{j},1)
. We define an
(m+1)
-chain on
\Delta\times[0,1]
by

P=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}[a_{0},\dots,a_{j},b_{j},\dots,b_{m}].

This gives a chain homotopy,

h\colon C_{*}(X)\to C_{*+1}(X\times[0,1])
,
between
\iota_{0,*}
and
\iota_{1,*}
, defined by

\sigma\mapsto(\sigma\times{\rm id})_{*}(P)
.

Now, it remains to prove that the image
h(\sigma)
of a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex is a

\overline{q}
-allowable chain.
To any
j\in\{0,\dots,m\}
, we associate the simplex

\tau_{j}\colon\nabla=[v_{0},\dots,v_{m+1}]\to\Delta^{m}\times[0,1]
,
defined by

(v_{0},\dots,v_{m+1})\mapsto(a_{0},\dots,a_{j},b_{j},\dots,b_{m})
.
By construction we have

\Delta^{m}\times[0,1]=\cup_{j=0}^{m}\tau_{j}(\nabla)
.
For each stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, we have

\dim\tau_{j}^{-1}(\sigma\times{\rm id})^{-1}(S\times[0,1])\leq\dim\sigma^{-1}S+1\leq m-D\overline{p}(S)-1
.
We get

\displaystyle\dim h(\sigma)^{-1}S

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\dim\tau_{j}^{-1}(\sigma\times{\rm id})^{-1}(S\times[0,1])\mid j\in\{0,\dots,m\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m-D\overline{p}(S)-1=\dim\nabla-D\overline{p}(S\times[0,1])-2,

which implies the desired inclusion

h(C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X))\subset C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X\times[0,1])
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})

The inclusions
\iota_{z}\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}\times X
,

, with

,

The case of a cone

3.
Strong general position

If
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
is a singular simplex, the subset
\sigma^{-1}S
can be very wild.
We introduce a simplicial notion that can replace it.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a singular simplex on a filtered space
X
and
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
be a stratum.
A
simplicial envelope
of
\sigma^{-1}S
is a finite family
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
of Euclidean simplexes
such that

\sigma^{-1}S\subset\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}}T

and

\dim\sigma^{-1}S=\max\left\{\dim T\mid T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}\right\}
.
If
\sigma^{-1}S=\emptyset
we set
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}=\emptyset
.
A
simplicial system for
\sigma
is a family

{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}=\left\{\lx@nobreakspace{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}\mid S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\right\}

where each
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
is a simplicial envelope of
\sigma^{-1}S
.
A
simplicial system for
X
is a family
{\mathcal{T}}=\left\{{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in{\rm Sing}\,X\right\}

of simplicial systems for the simplexes of
X
.

Simplicial envelopes and systems always exist.
Given a simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
, a stratum
S
and a simplicial system
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
,
the allowability condition (
2.2
) is equivalent to the following inequalities,
for each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
,

(3.1)

\dim T\leq\dim\Delta-2-D\overline{p}(S).

3.1.
General position

Detecting if a simplex included in an allowable simplex is still allowable is a crucial point
and moving to general position will be a useful tool.
Let us recall some basic facts, sending to
[
13
]
for more information.

It is well known that two affine spaces
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with
E\cap F\neq\emptyset
, are in general position if

\dim(E\cap F)=\dim E+\dim F-m
.
General position is generic in the sense that it is always possible to slightly move two affine spaces in a general position.
This is also a stable situation in the sense that if two affine spaces are in general position and we slightly move one of them, then they remain in a general position.

The situation is different for polyhedra. Recall that two polyhedra
\{P,Q\}
included in a simplex
\Delta
, are in general position if

(3.2)

\dim(P\cap Q)\leq\dim P+\dim Q-\dim\Delta.

Given two polyhedra it is always possible to slightly move one of them in order to get the general position
(cf.
[
13
]
) and thus general position of polyhedra is generic.
But this notion is not stable as shows the following example.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\dim\Delta=2
and
a,b,c\in\Delta
be three distinct points aligned. Then, the 1-simplexes
P=[a,b]
and
Q=[b,c]
are in general position.
But the simplexes
\{P,Q_{\varepsilon}=[\varepsilon a+(1-\varepsilon)b,c]\}
are not in general position, for any
\varepsilon\in]0,1]
.
There is no stability.

With this lack of stability, general position is not the right situation for our purpose.
Thus, we introduce in Subsection
3.2
a more robust notion called
strong general position.

Before that, we show that the two situations of general position for affine spaces
and polyhedra coincide with an extra hypothesis.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{P,Q\}
be two simplexes living in a simplex

\Delta
and such that
\mathring{P}\cap\mathring{Q}\neq\emptyset
.
If
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
E
,
F
,
H
denote the affine spaces generated by
\Delta
,
P
,
Q
and
P\cap Q
respectively,
we have
H=E\cap F
. Moreover, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The affine spaces
E,\,F
are in general position in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

(2)

The simplexes
P,\,Q
are in general position in
\Delta
.

(3)

\dim(P\cap Q)=\dim P+\dim Q-\dim\Delta
.

Proof.

We fix a point
x_{0}\in\mathring{P}\cap\mathring{Q}
.
We clearly have
H\subset E\cap F
. If
E\cap F=\{x_{0}\}
then we get

H=E\cap F
. So, we can suppose that
E\cap F
contains some
z\neq x_{0}
.
Since
E
is the affine space generated by the simplex
P

and
x_{0}\in\mathring{P}
, there exists

x\in\mathring{P}\,\cap\,]x_{0},z[
.
Similarly, there exists

y\in\mathring{Q}\,\cap\,]x_{0},z[
and we may suppose
x=y
.
Then,
x\in\mathring{P}\cap\mathring{Q}\subset H
which implies
z\in H
. We have proven

E\cap F\subset H
and the first assertion.
The second assertion is a consequence of the equality between the dimensions of a polyhedron and of its associated affine space.
∎

Finally, we link the notions of general position and admissibility.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space
and
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.
We fix a simplicial system
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}
and a simplex
\nabla\subset\Delta
.
If, for any singular stratum
S
of
X
and any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
, the simplexes

\{T,\nabla\}
are in general position in
\Delta
, then the restriction
\sigma_{\nabla}\colon\nabla\to X
of
\sigma
is
a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.

Proof.

We have to prove

\dim\sigma^{-1}_{\nabla}S=\dim(\sigma^{-1}S\cap\nabla)\leq\dim\nabla-D\overline{p}(S)-2
.
By (
2.1
) and the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
, it suffices to prove that

\dim(T\cap\nabla)\leq\dim\nabla-D\overline{p}(S)-2
, for each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,S}
.
Applying (
3.2
) and (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\dim(T\cap\nabla)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim T+\dim\nabla-\dim\Delta\leq\dim\Delta-D\overline{p}(S)-2+\dim\nabla-\dim\Delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\nabla-D\overline{p}(S)-2.

∎

3.2.
Strong general position

We start with a simplex
\Delta
together with two simplexes,
T\subset\Delta
and
V\subset\partial\Delta
.
In order to construct a pseudo-barycentric subdivision in Section
4
,
we have to find out a point
u\in\mathring{\Delta}
such that the simplex
T
and the cone
{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V
are in general position.
In the main, the choice of the point
u
requires the notions of genericity and stability.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\Pi
be a property defined on each point of the interior
\mathring{\Delta}
of an Euclidean simplex
\Delta
. We set

\Delta_{\Pi}=\{u\in\mathring{\Delta}\mid\Pi(u)\text{ is true}\}.

The property
\Pi
is
generic
if
\Delta_{\Pi}
is a dense subset of
\mathring{\Delta}

and
stable
if
\Delta_{\Pi}
is an open subset of
\mathring{\Delta}
.

As a dense subset of
\mathring{\Delta}
cannot be empty, the genericity condition implies the existence of points
u

with
\Pi(u)
true.
The stability condition means that we can move the choice of such point
u
in in a neighborhood, and we will
use that possibillity in the inductive step.

The following notion of strong general position is both generic and stable,
as we show in Proposition
3.9
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\Delta
be an Euclidean simplex. We consider two Euclidean simplexes

T\subset\Delta
and
V\subset\partial\Delta
and a point
u\in\mathring{\Delta}
.
Denote by
{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V=u*V
the cone on
V
of apex
u
.
The simplexes
\{T,{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\}
are in a
strong general position
if

(3.3)

T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\subset\partial\Delta\ \ \ \hbox{ or }\ \ \ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset,\text{ and }\\
\dim(T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)=\dim T+\dim V+1-\dim\Delta.\end{array}\right.

This property is denoted
{\mathscr{P}}(u,T,V)
.
We start with a study of the two properties appearing in Definition
3.6
.
Notice that
T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\cap\partial\Delta=T\cap V
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\Delta
be an Euclidean simplex. We consider two Euclidean simplexes

T\subset\Delta
and
V\subset\partial\Delta
and a point
u\in\mathring{\Delta}
.

a)

If

T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\backslash\partial\Delta\neq\emptyset
,
then
u
belongs to the closure of
\Delta_{\Pi_{1}}
, where
\Pi_{1}
is defined by

\Pi_{1}(b)=``\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{b}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset.\text{''}

b)

The following property
\Pi_{2}
is stable, where

\Pi_{2}(b)=``\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{b}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\dim(T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{b}V)=\dim T+\dim V+1-\dim\Delta.\text{''}

Proof.

a) Let
z\in T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\backslash\partial\Delta
and
\varepsilon>0
.
We have to find
\omega\in B(u,\varepsilon)
such that
\mathring{T}\cap{({\mathtt{c}}_{\omega}V)^{\circ}}\neq\emptyset
. We make out three cases.

i) If
z\in\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}
, we take
w=u
.

ii) Suppose
z\in\partial T\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}
.
Since
z\in({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}
, then there exist
\lambda\in]0,1[
and
v\in V\backslash\partial V
such that
z=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v
.
Since
T\neq\partial T
, there exists
t\in\mathring{T}
.
Let
\vec{\alpha}=\overrightarrow{zt}
be the vector determined by

z=t+\vec{\alpha}
in the associated affine space.
We choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in]0,1[
small enough to assure that
w=u+\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\vec{\alpha}\in B(u,\varepsilon)
.
The point

\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}t+(1-\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime})z=z+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}\vec{\alpha}=\lambda\omega+(1-\lambda)v

belongs to
\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{\omega}V)^{\circ}
since
\lambda,\varepsilon^{\prime}\neq 0,1
and
v\in\mathring{V}
.
We have acquired
\Pi_{1}(\omega)
.

iii) Suppose
z\in T\cap\partial{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V
.
Since
z\in{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\backslash\partial\Delta
, then
z\not\in{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V\cap\partial\Delta=V
.
On the other hand, from
z\in\partial{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V
, we deduce
z\in{\mathtt{c}}_{u}\partial V\backslash\partial V
.
This gives
z=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v
with
v\in\partial V
and
\lambda\neq 0
.
Let
b\in\mathring{V}
. Let us denote
\vec{\alpha}=\overrightarrow{bz}
the vector determined by

z=b+\vec{\alpha}
.
We choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in]0,1[
small enough to assure that
w=u+\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\vec{\alpha}\in B(u,\varepsilon)
.
Set
b^{\prime}=\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}-\lambda}v+\frac{\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}-\lambda}b
.
We also have,

z=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}}w+\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)b^{\prime}.

The point
b^{\prime}
does not belong to
\partial V
because
\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Notice that
z\in({\mathtt{c}}_{\omega}V)^{\circ}
since
\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda\varepsilon^{\prime}}\neq 0,1
.
We deduce
T\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{w}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
and it suffices to apply i) or ii).

b) Let
u\in\mathring{\Delta}
with
\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
and

\dim(T\cap{\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)=\dim T+\dim V+1-\dim\Delta
.
We have to find
\varepsilon>0
such that for any point
\omega\in B(u,\varepsilon)
we have
\Pi_{2}(\omega)
.
Let
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{p}\}
be a basis of
T
and
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{q}\}
be a basis of
V
.
We choose
t_{0}\in\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{u}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
that we write

(3.4)

t_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}t_{j}=\lambda_{0}u+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{i}v_{i}.

Since
t_{0}
does not belong to the boundary, the coefficients are in
]0,1[
.
The generated affine spaces
being in general position (Lemma
3.3
), for any point
\omega\in B(u,\varepsilon)
and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(3.5)

\omega=u+\sum_{j=1}^{p}B_{j}\overrightarrow{t_{0}t_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}A_{i}\overrightarrow{t_{0}v_{i}}.

Denote
A=A_{1}+\dots+A_{q}
and
B=B_{1}+\dots+B_{p},
.
We consider the following two points:

(1)

y=\alpha_{0}\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_{i}v_{i}
, with

\alpha_{0}=\lambda_{0}/(1-A\lambda_{0})
,

\alpha_{i}=(\lambda_{i}-A_{i}\lambda_{0})/(1-A\lambda_{0}),\;i\in\{1,\dots,q\}

(2)

,
with

\beta_{j}=\mu_{j}(1-B\alpha_{0})+B_{j}\alpha_{0},\;j\in\{1,\dots,p\}

If we choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough then both combinations are convex combinations
verifying
s\in\mathring{T}
and
y\in({\mathtt{c}}_{\omega}V)^{\circ}
.
We are going to prove

which implies

\mathring{T}\cap({\mathtt{c}}_{\omega}V)^{\circ}\neq\emptyset.

The equality
y=s
is equivalent to the equality
\overrightarrow{t_{0}y}=\overrightarrow{t_{0}s}
.
A straightforward calculation gives the claim using the following properties:

•

\displaystyle\vec{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\overrightarrow{t_{0}t_{j}}=\lambda_{0}\overrightarrow{t_{0}u}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{i}\overrightarrow{t_{0}v_{i}}
(cf. (

•

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{t_{0}\omega}=\overrightarrow{t_{0}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}B_{j}\overrightarrow{t_{0}t_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}A_{i}\overrightarrow{t_{0}v_{i}}

(cf. (

4.
Pseudo-barycentric subdivisions

Given a singular simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
of a filtered space
X
, a pseudo-barycentric subdivision
{\mathcal{B}}
of
\Delta
is a subdivision
similar to the barycentric subdivision, except the fact that the new vertices are not barycentres but close points of them.
These points are chosen to control the relative position of the simplexes of the triangulation
{\mathcal{B}}
and the simplicial envelope
(see Definition
3.1
)
of the singular part
\sigma^{-1}\Sigma_{X}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
triangulation system
of a space
X
is a family
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\colon\Delta_{\sigma}\to X\}
of triangulations

{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
of
\Delta_{\sigma}
. The
diameter
of a triangulation is the maximum of the diameters of its simplexes.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a filtered space,
{\mathcal{T}}
be a simplicial system and

{\mathcal{P}}=\{u_{\sigma}\in\mathring{\Delta}_{\sigma}\mid\lx@nobreakspace\sigma\in{\rm Sing}\,X\}

be a family of points, called
pseudo-barycentres
.
A
{\mathcal{T}}
-pseudo-barycentric system
,
{\mathcal{B}}
, of
X
,
associated to
{\mathcal{P}}
, is a triangulation system verifying the properties
(PB1)-(PB5), for each

\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
:

(1)

u_{\sigma}\in\mathring{\Delta}_{\sigma}
.

(2)

For any
i
-th face

\partial_{i}\sigma\colon\partial_{i}\Delta_{\sigma}\to X
of
\sigma
,
we have

{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial_{i}\sigma}=({\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma})\cap\partial_{i}\Delta_{\sigma}
.

(3)

{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}=u_{\sigma}*{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}
if
\dim\Delta_{\sigma}>0
.

(4)

{\rm diam}\,{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}\leq 2\ell/(2\ell+1)
.

(5)

The simplexes
\{T,\ {\mathtt{c}}_{u_{\sigma}}B\}
are in strong general position in
\Delta_{\sigma}
for any
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}
and
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}
.

Observe that Property (PB2) allows the use of the triangulation
{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}
of
\partial\Delta
without
ambiguity. Property (PB1) gives
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}=\{u_{\sigma}\}
if
\dim\sigma=0
.
Before proving the existence of a pseudo-barycentric system we need the following lemma which gives a control in the case of
\overline{p}
-allowable simplexes.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space, with a locally finite stratification,
and
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.
Then, the following property, defined for each point
u\in\mathring{\Delta}
by

\Theta(u)=\hbox{``the simplex $\sigma_{u}\colon\Delta^{0}=[a_{0}]\to X$, $a_{0}\mapsto u$, is $\overline{p}$-allowable,''}

is generic in the sense of Definition
3.5
.

Proof.

The simplex
\sigma_{u}
is
\overline{p}
-allowable if

\dim\sigma_{u}^{-1}S\leq 0-D\overline{p}(S)-2

for each singular stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.
Since
\dim\sigma_{u}^{-1}S\leq 0
, this condition is fulfilled if
D\overline{p}(S)\leq-2
.
It remains to prove that the subset

(4.1)

\Delta_{\Theta}=\left\{u\in\mathring{\Delta}\mid\theta(u)\text{ is true}\right\}=\mathring{\Delta}\backslash\displaystyle\bigcup_{D\overline{p}(S)\geq-1}\sigma_{u}^{-1}S

is dense in
\Delta
.
The stratification being locally finite, the previous union is finite.
By dimensional reasons, it suffices to prove that

\dim\sigma_{u}^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-1
for each singular stratum
S
with
D\overline{p}(S)\geq-1
.
The allowability condition for the simplex
\sigma_{u}
gives

\dim\sigma_{u}^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-D\overline{p}(S)-2\leq\dim\Delta-1

and we get the claim.
∎

4.1.
Existence of pseudo-barycentric subdivisions

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a filtered space with a locally finite stratification and let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a simplicial system.
Then there exists a
{\mathcal{T}}
-pseudo-barycentric system
{\mathcal{B}}
of
X
,
of associated set of pseudo-barycentres
{\mathcal{P}}=\{u_{\sigma}\in\mathring{\Delta}_{\sigma}\mid\lx@nobreakspace\sigma\in{\rm Sing}\,X\}
.
Moreover, given a perversity
\overline{p}
on
X
, the
{\mathcal{T}}
-pseudo-barycentric system
{\mathcal{B}}
can be chosen such that,
for any
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex
\sigma
, the restriction of
\sigma
to any
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
with
u_{\sigma}\in B
is also
\overline{p}
-allowable.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
, we construct
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
by induction on
\ell
.
If
\ell=0
then
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
is given by (PB1).
For the inductive step, we suppose constructed
{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}
verifying properties (PB1)-(PB5)
and we have to find a point

u_{\sigma}\in\mathring{\Delta}^{\ell}

such that the triangulation
u_{\sigma}*{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}
verifies the properties (PB4) and (PB5).
Let us begin with (PB4).
We first show that it suffices to take
u_{\sigma}
in the open ball
B\left(b_{\sigma},\frac{\ell}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\right)
, where
b_{\sigma}
is the barycentre of
\Delta^{\ell}
.
By induction, we have

{\rm diam}\ {\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}\leq\frac{2\ell-2}{2\ell-1}\leq\frac{2\ell}{2\ell+1}.

{\rm dist}(u_{\sigma},a_{j})\leq\frac{2\ell}{2\ell+1}

for each vertex

of

{\rm dist}(u_{\sigma},a_{j})\leq{\rm dist}(u_{\sigma},b_{\sigma})+{\rm dist}(b_{\sigma},a_{j})<\frac{\ell}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}=\frac{2\ell}{2\ell+1}.

\mathring{\Delta}^{\ell}
.
(Recall that
{\mathscr{P}}(u,T,B)
is introduced in Definition
3.6
and
\Delta_{{\mathscr{P}}(u,T,B)}
in Definition
3.5

The subdivision

is finite. Since the stratification is locally finite then the family
{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}
is also finite.
Following Proposition
3.9
, this intersection is a non-empty open subset and it suffices to take
u_{\sigma}
in it.
This gives the first part of the proof.

As for the second part, we now suppose that
\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-allowable.
We choose
u_{\sigma}
in the subset

(4.2)

\Delta_{\Theta}\cap B\left(b_{\sigma},\frac{\ell}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\right)\cap\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\partial\sigma}\\
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{{\mathscr{P}}(-,T,B)}

where
\Delta_{\Theta}
is defined in (

).
This subset is not empty according to Lemma

and is included in
\mathring{\Delta}^{\ell}
.
Now, it suffices to prove that the restriction
\sigma_{B}
is a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex, that is,

(4.3)

\dim(B\cap\sigma^{-1}S)=\dim\sigma_{B}^{-1}S\leq\dim B-D\overline{p}(S)-2,

for each singular stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.
If

, the result comes from

.
Let us suppose

with

, the result comes from

.
∎

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a singular simplex,

We define

From Proposition

-allowable chain.

(2)

5.
Mayer-Vietoris sequence

In this section, we prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space and
\{U,V\}
be an open covering of
X
. Then there is a long exact sequence,
called Mayer-Vietoris sequence,

\ldots\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(U\cap V)\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(U)\oplus H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(V)\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X)\to H_{*-1}^{\overline{p}}(U\cap V)\to\dots

Let us consider an intersection chain
\xi
made up of a simplex.
Then there exists an integer
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that the simplexes of
{\rm sd}^{r}\xi
belongs to

C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(U)
or
C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(V)
(cf. Proposition
4.5
).
The same property happens if all the simplexes of
\xi
are intersection simplexes.
So we still have the case of allowable simplexes that are not intersection chains.
To study them, we follow a similar method used in
[
2
, Proposition A.14.(i)]
.
The key point is that the allowability defect of the boundary of an allowable simplex is concentrated in only one face,
cf Proposition
5.5
.

Let us notice that a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
is not a
\overline{p}
-intersection chain
if, and only if,
there exists a codimension one face
\nabla
of
\Delta
and a singular stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
with

0\leq\dim(\nabla\cap\sigma^{-1}S)=\dim\Delta-D\overline{p}(S)-2.

To study them, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space and
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.
A face
F\triangleleft\Delta
is a

if

and there exists a singular stratum

verifying

The set of critical faces of
\sigma
is denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}
.

The family of critical faces of a pseudo-barycentric subdivision has a nice structure that we present now.
To do it, we first introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
be a singular simplex and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
be a pseudo-barycentric subdivision of
\Delta
.
If
\{u_{0},\dots,u_{\ell}\}
are the vertices of
\Delta

is of the shape

{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}=\{u_{i_{0}\dots i_{a}}\mid 0\leq i_{0}<\dots<i_{a}\leq\ell\},

where
u_{i_{0}\dots i_{a}}
is the pseudo-barycentre contained in the interior of the face
[u_{i_{0}},\dots,u_{i_{a}}]
.

A face
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
such that
\dim B=\dim\sigma

is of the type

B=[u_{j_{0}},u_{j_{0}j_{1}},\dots,u_{j_{0}\dots j_{\ell}}]

\{j_{0},\dots,j_{\ell}\}=\{i_{0},\dots,i_{\ell}\}
.
The faces

are called

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space with a locally finite stratification,
{\mathcal{T}}
be a simplicial system and

{\mathcal{T}}

(5.11)

\xi\in C^{\overline{p}}_{*}(X)\Rightarrow{\rm sd}^{k}\xi\in C^{\overline{p}}_{*}(U)+C^{\overline{p}}_{*}(V)\text{ for some }k\geq 1.

Let
\xi
be a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain of
X
. The
canonical decomposition
of
{\rm sd}\,\xi
is
{\rm sd}\,\xi=\xi_{0}+\sum_{\mu\in I_{\xi}}\xi_{\mu}
where:

-

\xi_{0}
is the chain containing the simplexes of
\xi
without
\overline{p}
-bad faces;

-

I_{\xi}
is the family of the
\overline{p}
-bad faces of the simplexes of
{\rm sd}\,\xi
;

-

\xi_{\mu}
is the chain containing the simplexes of
{\rm sd}\,\xi
having
\mu
as
\overline{p}
-bad face.

The boundary
\partial\xi_{0}
is a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain.
A non-
\overline{p}
-allowable face
\tau
of a simplex
\sigma
of
\xi_{\mu}
contains necessarily
\mu
.
When
\partial\xi
, and therefore

, is a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain (cf. Proposition

) then
\tau
does not appear in

\partial{\rm sd}\,\xi
.
So, there exists another simplex

of
{\rm sd}\,\xi
having

Since

then

is the bad face of

We conclude that

is also a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain.
(These facts come from Proposition
5.5
.) For each

, we have proven

\xi\in C^{\overline{p}}_{*}(X)\Rightarrow\xi_{0},\xi_{\mu}\in C^{\overline{p}}_{*}(X).

The usual subdivision argument gives the existence of an integer

such that
the canonical decomposition of
{\rm sd}^{k}\xi
verifies the following properties.

-

Each simplex of
({\rm sd}^{k}\xi)_{0}

or in

.

-

For each

the chain

or in

.

This gives (

6.

6.1.

Let us specify what we mean by “original intersection homology.”

Definition 6.1

\overline{p}
-GM-intersection

\overline{p}
-GM-allowable.

\overline{p}
-intersection

-GM-intersection homology of

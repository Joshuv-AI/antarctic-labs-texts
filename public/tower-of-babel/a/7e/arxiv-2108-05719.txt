Compact equations for the envelope theory
Lorenzo Cimino, Claude Semay

Abstract
Abstract The envelope theory is a method to easily obtain approximate, but reliable, solutions for some quantum many-body problems. Quite general Hamiltonians can be considered for systems composed of an arbitrary number of different particles in D D dimensions. In the case of identical particles, a compact set of 3 equations can be written to find the eigensolutions. This set provides also a nice interpretation and a starting point to improve the method. It is shown here that a similar set of 7 equations can be determined for a system containing an arbitrary number of two different particles.



I
introduction

The envelope theory (ET)
hall80
;
hall83
;
hall04
is a technique to compute approximate eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
N
-body systems. This method, first developed for systems with identical particles, has been extended to treat non-standard kinematics in
D
dimensions in
sema13
;
sema19
, and it has been recently generalized for systems with different particles
sema20
. The big advantage of this method is that the computation cost is independent of the number of particles. Quite general Hamiltonians can be considered, and the approximate eigenvalues are lower or upper bounds in favorable cases. The method relies on the existence of an exact solution for the
N
-body harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian
hall79
;
cint01
. The accuracy of the method has been checked for various three-dimensional systems
sema15a
and one-dimensional systems containing up to 100 bosons
sema19
.

It is worth noting that the ET method has been rediscovered in 2008 under the name of the auxiliary field method, following an approach different from the one used by Hall
hall80
;
hall83
;
hall04
. It has been recognized later that both methods are actually completely equivalent. This story is described in
silv12
, where a lot of information is given about this approximation method.

The ET has been used to obtain physical results about hadronic systems as in
sema09
, and is especially useful when the number of particles can be arbitrary large as in the large-
N
formulation of QCD
buis11
;
buis12
. The method has allowed the study of a possible quasi Kepler’s third law for quantum many-body systems
sema21
. It can also be simply used to test accurate numerical calculations as in
char15
.

Let us consider the
N
-body Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{i}(p_{i})+\sum_{i<j=2}^{N}V_{ij}(r_{ij}),

(1)

where
T_{i}
is an arbitrary kinetic energy with some constraints
sema18a
and
V_{ij}
is a two-body central potential. We also define
p_{i}=\left\lvert\bm{p}_{i}\right\rvert
and
r_{ij}=\left\lvert\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right\rvert
, where
\bm{r}_{i}
and
\bm{p}_{i}
are respectively the position and the momentum of the
i
th particle. It is assumed in the following that we are always working in the centre of mass (CM) frame,
\bm{P}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{p}_{i}=\bm{0}
, and with natural units (
\hbar=c=1
).

As explained in
silv10
;
sema20
, in the framework of the ET, Hamiltonian (
1
) is replaced by an auxiliary Hamiltonian (it is the origin of the other name of the method)

\tilde{H}(\{\alpha\})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2\mu_{i}}+T_{i}(G_{i}(\mu_{i}))-\dfrac{G_{i}^{2}(\mu_{i})}{2\mu_{i}}\right]+\sum_{i<j=2}^{N}\left[\rho_{ij}\bm{r}_{ij}^{2}+V_{ij}(J_{ij}(\rho_{ij}))-\rho_{ij}J_{ij}^{2}(\rho_{ij})\right],

(2)

where
\{\alpha\}=\{\{\mu_{i}\},\{\rho_{ij}\}\}
is a set of auxiliary parameters to determine later, and where the auxiliary functions
G_{i}
and
J_{ij}
are such that

\begin{array}[]{cc}T^{\prime}_{i}(G_{i}(x))-\dfrac{G_{i}(x)}{x}=0,\\[14.22636pt]
V^{\prime}_{ij}(J_{ij}(x))-2xJ_{ij}(x)=0,\end{array}

(3)

where
U^{\prime}(x)=dU(x)/dx
. It is useful to write Hamiltonian (
2
) in the form

\tilde{H}(\{\alpha\})=H_{\text{ho}}(\{\alpha\})+B(\{\alpha\}),

(4)

where
H_{\text{ho}}
is the harmonic oscillator part and
B
is a function obtained by subtracting the harmonic oscillator contributions from (
2
).
An eigenvalue of (
2
) is given by

\tilde{E}(\{\alpha\})=E_{\text{ho}}(\{\alpha\})+B(\{\alpha\}),

(5)

where
E_{\text{ho}}
is an eigenvalue of
H_{\text{ho}}
. A procedure in
silv10
;
sema20
explains how to compute
E_{\text{ho}}
but an example will be given below. An eigenvalue
\tilde{E}
also depends on the set of parameters
\{\alpha\}=\{\{\mu_{i}\},\{\rho_{ij}\}\}
. The principle of the method is to search for the set of parameters
\{\alpha_{0}\}=\{\{\mu_{i0}\},\{\rho_{ij0}\}\}
such that

\frac{\partial\tilde{E}}{\partial\mu_{i}}\biggr\rvert_{\{\alpha_{0}\}}=\frac{\partial\tilde{E}}{\partial\rho_{ij}}\biggr\rvert_{\{\alpha_{0}\}}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ i,j.

(6)

Equations (
6
) can be easily implemented and solutions
\{\alpha_{0}\}
are easily found since we only need to find an extremum
sema20
. After solving (
6
), we obtain the desired approximate energy by substituting the set
\{\alpha_{0}\}
back to (
5
),
\tilde{E}(\{\alpha_{0}\})=\tilde{E}_{0}
.

In the case of identical particles, it has been showed
sema13
;
sema19
that we can equivalently find the eigenvalue
\tilde{E}_{0}
by using a set of three compact equations

\tilde{E}_{0}=N\,T(p_{0})+C^{2}_{N}\,V(\rho_{0}),

(7a)

N\,T^{\prime}(p_{0})\,p_{0}=C^{2}_{N}\,V^{\prime}(\rho_{0})\,\rho_{0},

(7b)

Q(N)=\sqrt{C^{2}_{N}}\,p_{0}\,\rho_{0},

(7c)

where
C^{2}_{N}=N(N-1)/2
is the number of pairs, and where
p_{0}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}_{i}^{2}\right\rangle
and
\rho_{0}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{r}_{ij}^{2}\right\rangle\hskip 5.69054pt\forall\ i,j
. The mean values are taken with an eigenstate of the auxiliary Hamiltonian corresponding to the global quantum number
Q(N)
for the set
\{\alpha_{0}\}
insuring the constraints (
6
). The eigenstate is also completely (anti)symmetric for the exchange between particles.

Q(N)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(2n_{i}+l_{i}+\frac{D}{2}\right)&\text{ if }D\geq 2\\[15.0pt]
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(n_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{ if }D=1\end{cases},

(8)

where the quantum numbers
\{n_{i},l_{i}\}
are associated with the internal Jacobi variables. Some values of
Q(N)
for the bosonic and fermionic ground states are given in
sema20
;
sema19
. In previous papers
sema13
;
sema19
, the variable
r_{0}^{2}=N^{2}\left\langle\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}\right)^{2}\right\rangle
, where
\bm{R}
is the CM position, was used instead of
\rho_{0}
because one-body and two-body potentials are treated together.

These equations are called compact because all the relevant variables appear in 3 equations giving the definition of the energy (
7a
), the equation of motion (
7b
) and the rule for the quantization (
7c
). Moreover, the uninteresting auxiliary parameters and functions are not present. Equations (
7
) can also be easily implemented and solved. There are good reasons to prefer the compact equations (
7
) over the “extremization” equations (
6
). First, the quantities
p_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
give direct access to more interesting expectation values than
\{\alpha_{0}\}
. Secondly, these equations have a nice semiclassical interpretation as explained in
sema13
. Thirdly, it is possible to improve the ET with the dominantly orbital state method starting from these equations
sema15b
, which is the main motivation to write these equations. As the improvement obtained can be significant in some cases, it is worth generalizing it beyond systems of identical particles. In the following section, we will present the compact equations for a system composed of two different sets of
N_{a}
and
N_{b}
identical particles.

II
\bm{N_{a}+N_{b}}
systems

Let us specify the auxiliary Hamiltonian (
2
) for this system. The harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian for a system of
N_{a}
particles of type
a
and
N_{b}
particles of type
b
is given by

H_{\text{ho}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2\mu_{a}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{b}}\frac{\bm{p}_{j}^{2}}{2\mu_{b}}+\sum_{i<i^{\prime}=2}^{N_{a}}\rho_{aa}\bm{r}_{ii^{\prime}}^{2}+\sum_{j<j^{\prime}=2}^{N_{b}}\rho_{bb}\bm{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{b}}\rho_{ab}\bm{r}_{ij}^{2}.

(9)

In the following, letters
i\text{ }(j)
are reserved for particles of type
a\text{ }(b)
. As explained in
sema20
;
hall79
, it is useful to write (
9
) in the form

H_{\text{ho}}=H_{a}+H_{b}+H_{\text{CM}}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{with}

(10a)

H_{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{a}}{\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2\mu_{a}}}-\frac{\bm{P}_{a}^{2}}{2M_{a}}+\sum_{i<i^{\prime}=2}^{N_{a}}\left(\rho_{aa}+\frac{N_{b}}{N_{a}}\rho_{ab}\right)\bm{r}_{ii^{\prime}}^{2},

(10b)

H_{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{b}}{\frac{\bm{p}_{j}^{2}}{2\mu_{b}}}-\frac{\bm{P}_{b}^{2}}{2M_{b}}+\sum_{j<j^{\prime}=2}^{N_{b}}\left(\rho_{bb}+\frac{N_{a}}{N_{b}}\rho_{ab}\right)\bm{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{2},

(10c)

H_{\text{CM}}=\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\mu}+N_{a}N_{b}\rho_{ab}\bm{r}^{2},

(10d)

where
\bm{P}_{\alpha}
and
M_{\alpha}=N_{\alpha}\,\mu_{\alpha}
are the total momentum and mass for the set
\alpha=\{a,b\}
,
\mu=\frac{M_{a}M_{b}}{M_{a}+M_{b}}
is a reduced mass, and
\bm{p}=\frac{M_{b}\bm{P}_{a}-M_{a}\bm{P}_{b}}{M_{a}+M_{b}}
and
\bm{r}=\bm{R}_{a}-\bm{R}_{b}
are the relative momentum and position between the CM of the two sets, respectively. The three parts of (
10a
) are entirely decoupled since (
10b
) and (
10c
) depends on the internal coordinates of their respective set, and (
10d
) on the relative coordinates between the two CM.

Then, an eigenvalue
E_{\text{ho}}
is easily obtained since (
10
) is composed of three decoupled parts
sema20

E_{\text{ho}}=Q(N_{a})\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu_{a}}(N_{a}\rho_{aa}+N_{b}\rho_{ab})}+Q(N_{b})\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu_{b}}(N_{b}\rho_{bb}+N_{a}\rho_{ab})}+Q(2)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu}N_{a}N_{b}\rho_{ab}}.

(11)

To be complete, the expression of the function
B(\{\alpha\})
is given by

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=N_{a}\left[T_{a}(G_{a}(\mu_{a}))-\frac{G^{2}_{a}(\mu_{a})}{2\mu_{a}}\right]+C^{2}_{N_{a}}\left[V_{aa}(J_{aa}(\rho_{aa}))-\rho_{aa}J^{2}_{aa}(\rho_{aa})\right]

(12)

\displaystyle+N_{b}\left[T_{b}(G_{b}(\mu_{b}))-\frac{G^{2}_{b}(\mu_{b})}{2\mu_{b}}\right]+C^{2}_{N_{b}}\left[V_{bb}(J_{bb}(\rho_{bb}))-\rho_{bb}J^{2}_{bb}(\rho_{bb})\right]

\displaystyle+N_{a}N_{b}\left[V_{ab}(J_{ab}(\rho_{ab}))-\rho_{ab}J^{2}_{ab}(\rho_{ab})\right].

When combining (
10
) and (
12
), we can see that our auxiliary Hamiltonian (
2
) is also composed of three distinct parts: one for the particles of type
a
, another for the particles of type
b
and a last one for the relative motion between the two sets.

The compact equations can then be established in a similar way as done for identical particles
silv12
. First, we apply the Hellmann-Feynman theorem
hell
on Hamiltonian (
2
) to evaluate extremization conditions (
6
). By using definitions (
3
) we get the following results

\begin{array}[]{lllll}&G_{a}^{2}(\mu_{a0})=p_{a}^{2}+\frac{P_{0}^{2}}{N_{a}^{2}}={p^{\prime}_{a}}^{2},\\[8.5359pt]
&G_{b}^{2}(\mu_{b0})=p_{b}^{2}+\frac{P_{0}^{2}}{N_{b}^{2}}={p^{\prime}_{b}}^{2},\\[8.5359pt]
&J^{2}_{aa}(\rho_{aa0})=r_{aa}^{2},\\[8.5359pt]
&J^{2}_{bb}(\rho_{bb0})=r_{bb}^{2},\\[8.5359pt]
&J^{2}_{ab}(\rho_{ab0})=\frac{N_{a}-1}{2N_{a}}r_{aa}^{2}+\frac{N_{b}-1}{2N_{b}}r_{bb}^{2}+R_{0}^{2}={r^{\prime}_{0}}^{2},\end{array}

(13)

where we have defined the six physical parameters

\begin{array}[]{lll}&p_{a}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}_{i}^{2}-\frac{\bm{P}_{a}^{2}}{N_{a}^{2}}\right\rangle\text{ and }p_{b}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}_{j}^{2}-\frac{\bm{P}_{b}^{2}}{N_{b}^{2}}\right\rangle,\\[8.5359pt]
&r_{aa}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{r}_{ii^{\prime}}^{2}\right\rangle\text{ and }r_{bb}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}\right\rangle,\\[8.5359pt]
&P_{0}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}^{2}\right\rangle\text{ and }R_{0}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{r}^{2}\right\rangle.\end{array}

(14)

The mean values are taken with an eigenstate of the auxiliary Hamiltonian corresponding to the quantum numbers
Q(N_{a})
,
Q(N_{b})
and
Q(2)
for the set
\{\alpha_{0}\}
insuring the constraints (
6
). The eigenstate is also completely (anti)symmetric for the exchange between the
N_{a}
or the
N_{b}
particles.

Then, by evaluating
\tilde{E}_{0}=\left\langle\tilde{H}(\{\alpha_{0}\})\right\rangle
and using results (
13
), we obtain the following equation for the energy

\tilde{E}_{0}=N_{a}T_{a}\left(p^{\prime}_{a}\right)+N_{b}T_{b}\left(p^{\prime}_{b}\right)+C^{2}_{N_{a}}V_{aa}\left(r_{aa}\right)+C^{2}_{N_{b}}V_{bb}\left(r_{bb}\right)+N_{a}N_{b}V_{ab}\left(r_{0}^{\prime}\right)

(15)

It is interesting to look at the meaning of the linear combinations
p^{\prime}_{a}
,
p^{\prime}_{b}
and
r^{\prime}_{0}
since they appear in (
15
). As shown in
sema20
, we can derive a similar equation for the energy by using the form (
9
), instead of (
10
), of the harmonic oscillator. By comparing, one can identify
{p^{\prime}_{a}}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}_{i}^{2}\right\rangle
,
{p^{\prime}_{b}}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{p}_{j}^{2}\right\rangle
and
{r^{\prime}_{0}}^{2}=\left\langle\bm{r}_{ij}^{2}\right\rangle
.

In order to find these parameters, we need 6 additional equations. We can find three of them by applying the virial theorem separately on each of the three parts of the auxiliary Hamiltonian
sema20
. One gets

N_{a}T^{\prime}_{a}(p^{\prime}_{a})\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{a}}=C^{2}_{N_{a}}V^{\prime}_{aa}(r_{aa})r_{aa}+\frac{N_{b}}{N_{a}}C^{2}_{N_{a}}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})\frac{r_{aa}^{2}}{r^{\prime}_{0}},

(16a)

N_{b}T^{\prime}_{b}(p^{\prime}_{b})\frac{p_{b}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{b}}=C^{2}_{N_{b}}V^{\prime}_{bb}(r_{bb})r_{bb}+\frac{N_{a}}{N_{b}}C^{2}_{N_{b}}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})\frac{r_{bb}^{2}}{r^{\prime}_{0}},

(16b)

\frac{1}{N_{a}}T^{\prime}_{a}(p^{\prime}_{a})\frac{P_{0}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{a}}+\frac{1}{N_{b}}T^{\prime}_{b}(p^{\prime}_{b})\frac{P_{0}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{b}}=N_{a}N_{b}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{r^{\prime}_{0}}.

(16c)

Finally, we obtain three last equations by using the exact eigenvalue (
11
) of the harmonic oscillator and comparing it to
\left\langle H_{\text{ho}}\{\alpha_{0}\}\right\rangle
. Thanks to (
10
), the comparison is done in a similar way as in
silv12
and one gets

Q(N_{a})=\sqrt{C^{2}_{N_{a}}}p_{a}r_{aa},

(17a)

Q(N_{b})=\sqrt{C^{2}_{N_{b}}}p_{b}r_{bb},

(17b)

Q(2)=P_{0}R_{0}.

(17c)

Equations (
16
) and (
17
) form a set of six equations which, combined with (
15
), form the ET compact equations for a system of
N_{a}+N_{b}
particles, and allow us to compute the approximate eigenvalue
\tilde{E}_{0}
. We have verified on several systems that these equations give the same results than those found with the extremization equations in
sema20
. We can note that the three equations (
16
) can be derived by minimizing (
15
) with respect to
r_{aa}
,
r_{bb}
and
R_{0}
, and using (
17
).

Equations (
15
)-(
17
) are more complicated than equations (
7
). But, when comparing the two sets, it is possible to find an interpretation for equations (
15
)-(
17
). Equation (
15
) is obviously the energy computed in terms of the mean momenta and relative distances. Equations (
16
) are the equations of motion determining these mean quantities, and equations (
17
) are the semiclassical quantifications of the various orbital and radial motions. These equations make clear what are the relevant quantities appearing in a quantum system containing two different sets of identical particles. It is worth recalling that solutions obtained by the ET are full quantum ones with eigenfunctions associated

;
sema20
and that observables can be computed

.

As a first check for these equations, we need to recover the three equations (
7
) when considering all particles identical. In this case
T_{a}=T_{b}
and
V_{aa}=V_{bb}=V_{ab}
, and we must impose the following symmetries

\begin{array}[]{cc}&\left\langle\bm{p}_{i}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{p}_{j}^{2}\right\rangle,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ i,j,\\[14.22636pt]
&\left\langle\bm{r}_{ii^{\prime}}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{r}_{jj^{\prime}}^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{r}_{ij}^{2}\right\rangle,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}.\end{array}

(18)

From the definitions of our 6 parameters, we conclude

\begin{array}[]{cc}&p^{\prime}_{a}=p^{\prime}_{b}=p_{0},\\[14.22636pt]
&r_{aa}=r_{bb}=r^{\prime}_{0}=\rho_{0},\end{array}

(19)

where
p_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
are defined as before in (

15
) reduces to (
7a
) with
N=N_{a}+N_{b}
. It is a matter of algebra to show that the sum of the three equations (
16

N_{a}T^{\prime}_{a}(p^{\prime}_{a})p^{\prime}_{a}+N_{b}T^{\prime}_{b}(p^{\prime}_{b})p^{\prime}_{b}=C^{2}_{N_{a}}V^{\prime}_{aa}(r_{aa})r_{aa}+C^{2}_{N_{b}}V^{\prime}_{bb}(r_{bb})r_{bb}+N_{a}N_{b}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})r^{\prime}_{0},

reduces to (

When all the particles are identical, it is not relevant to separate the energy on several subsets. We notice that
Q(N_{a})+Q(N_{b})+Q(2)=Q(N_{a}+N_{b})
. This is a hint that the sum of the three equations (

) must reduce to (

in terms of

Note that (

As a second test, we have substituted the harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian (

III
\bm{N_{a}=1}
or/and
\bm{N_{b}=1}

The 7 equations (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
) were computed for a system with
N_{a}+N_{b}
particles. It is interesting to look at what happens when only one particle is present in a set. For example, let’s look at the case
N_{b}=1
. Then, all the terms in

and

vanish. Equation (

) becomes trivial and (

) leads to

. As

. At the end, we are left with a system of 5 equations

(21a)

N_{a}T^{\prime}_{a}(p^{\prime}_{a})\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{a}}=C^{2}_{N_{a}}V^{\prime}_{aa}(r_{aa})r_{aa}+\frac{N_{a}-1}{2}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})\frac{r_{aa}^{2}}{r^{\prime}_{0}},

\frac{1}{N_{a}}T^{\prime}_{a}(p^{\prime}_{a})\frac{P_{0}^{2}}{p^{\prime}_{a}}+T^{\prime}_{b}(P_{0})P_{0}=N_{a}V^{\prime}_{ab}(r^{\prime}_{0})\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{r^{\prime}_{0}},

(21d)

Q(2)=P_{0}R_{0},

(21e)

where our four parameters are now defined as

IV
concluding remarks

We were able to build the 7 compact equations of the envelope theory for a system of
N_{a}+N_{b}
particles. These equations reduce to the 3 usual ones when considering identical particles. We also presented the special cases when
N_{b}=1
or/and
N_{a}=1
. Starting from these equations, it is possible to improve the envelope theory using a similar procedure than the one used in
sema15b
. This is performed for a system of
N_{a}+1
particles in
chev21
. With these 7 equations, it is possible to open new domains of applicability of the envelope theory, especially in hadronic physics where the method is proven to be useful as mentioned in the introduction. But the method can also be used to estimate the binding energies of other systems such as nuclei or clusters of cold atoms for which ab-initio calculations are already available, as for instance in
gatt13
. In particular, accurate calculations have been performed for large helium clusters
gatt11
;
kiev20
. For such systems, it is necessary to take into account a three-body forces that can be handled by the envelope theory
sema18b
.

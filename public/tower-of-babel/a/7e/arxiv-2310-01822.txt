Abstract
. A simplicial complex H H consists of a pair of sets ( V , E ) (V,E) where V V is a set of vertices and E ⊆ 𝒫 ⁡ ( V ) E\subseteq\mathscr{P}(V) is a collection of subsets of V V closed under taking subsets. Given a simplicial complex F F and n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathds{N} , the extremal number ex ​ ( n , F ) \text{\rm ex}(n,F) is the maximum number of edges that a simplicial complex on n n vertices can have without containing a copy of F F . We initiate the systematic study of extremal numbers in this context by asymptotically determining the extremal numbers of several natural simplicial complexes. In particular, we asymptotically determine the extremal number of a simplicial complex for which the extremal example has more than one incomplete layer.

1.
Introduction

Turán’s paper
[
23
]
in which he showed that complete, balanced,
(r-1)
-partite graphs maximise the number of edges among all
K_{r}
-free graphs is often seen as one of the starting points of extremal combinatorics.
Already in this paper, he asked for similar results for hypergraphs.
More than
80
years later, still little is known in this regard.

A hypergraph
H=(V,E)
consists of a vertex set
V
and an edge set
E\subseteq\mathscr{P}(V)=\{e\subseteq V\}
.
We say that
H
is
k
-uniform (or a
k
-graph) if
E\subseteq V^{(k)}=\{e\subseteq V:|e|=k\}
.
The extremal number for
n\in\mathds{N}
and a
k
-graph
F
is the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free
k
-graph on
n
vertices and is denoted by
\text{\rm ex}^{(k)}(n,F)
.
The Turán density of
F
is then
\pi(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\text{\rm ex}^{(k)}(n,F)}{\binom{n}{k}}^{-1}
(this limit was shown to exist by monotonicity in
[
16
]
).
The problem of determining the extremal numbers or the Turán densities of hypergraphs is often referred to as the Turán problem.
For graphs, this problem is reasonably well understood, as a result of Erdős, Stone, and Simonovits
[
10
,
9
]
, generalising Turán’s theorem, says that the Turán density of any graph
F
is
\frac{\chi(F)-2}{\chi(F)-1}
, where
\chi(F)
is the chromatic number of
F
.

In contrast, for hypergraphs, only a few Turán densities are known, one prominent example being that of the Fano plane
[
8
,
15
,
17
,
2
]
.
Even the Turán density of the complete
3
-graph on four vertices,
K_{4}^{(3)}
, is still not known, although it is widely conjectured to be
5/9
.
In fact, the problem is already open for the
3
-graph that consists of three edges on four vertices, denoted by
K_{4}^{(3)-}
.
For more on the hypergraph Turán problem, we refer the interested reader to the survey by Keevash
[
18
]
.

In this work, we consider the Turán problem for (abstract) simplicial complexes.
A hypergraph
H=(V,E)
is a simplicial complex if for all
e\in E
and
e^{\prime}\subseteq e
, we have
e^{\prime}\in E
.
In other words, a simplicial complex is a hypergraph whose edge set is closed under taking subsets (we also say that
E
is hereditary). The
k
-uniform layer of a simplicial complex
H=(V,E)
is
H_{k}=(V,E^{(k)})
, where
E^{(k)}=\{e\in E:|e|=k\}
.
In line with the geometric interpretation of simplicial complexes, we define the dimension of
H
to be

\dim(H)=\max\{k\geq 0:E^{(k)}\neq\varnothing\}-1\,.

For brevity, when describing the edge set of a simplicial complex, we will generally not mention the induced edges, i.e., the edges which are properly contained in another edge.

The extremal number for
n\in\mathds{N}
and a simplicial complex
F
is the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free simplicial complex on
n
vertices and is denoted by
\text{\rm ex}(n,F)
.
For a family of simplicial complexes
\mathcal{F}
, we define
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
analogously. We are by no means the first to study the extremal problem for simplicial complexes. For example, already in 1983,
Frankl
[
13
]
observed that
\text{\rm ex}(n,F)=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n}{i}
for
F=\big([s],\mathscr{P}([s])\big)
and derived the celebrated Sauer–Shelah–Vapnik–Chervonenkis lemma
[
20
,
21
,
24
]
as a corollary. However, we believe that we are the first to study the problem systematically, asymptotically determining the extremal numbers of several natural simplicial complexes.

As a first attempt, one might hope for some way to reduce the Turán problem for simplicial complexes to that for uniform hypergraphs.
Indeed, given a simplicial complex
F
of dimension
k-1
, let
H
be the simplicial complex with
|V(H)|=n
,
E^{(i)}=V(H)^{(i)}
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
and
E^{(k)}
the edge set of a
k
-graph on
V(H)
that is
F_{k}
-free. Then
H
is clearly
F
-free, yielding the following general lower bound.

Observation 1.1
.

If
F
is a simplicial complex of dimension
k-1
, then

\displaystyle\text{\rm ex}(n,F)\geq\text{\rm ex}^{(k)}(n,F_{k})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}\,.

We will show that this bound is asympotically optimal for several simplicial complexes. However, this is not always the case. To say more, we recall an old conjecture of Hajnal (originally formulated in terms of traces of sets rather than simplicial complexes).
Given positive integers
k
and
d
with
k>d
, let
\mathcal{F}(k,d)
be the family of all simplicial complexes with vertex set
[k]
and
1+\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{k}{i}
edges.
The problem of determining
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(k,d))
can be seen as a variant of the well-known Brown–Erdős–Sós problem
[
22
]
for simplicial complexes.
Hajnal’s conjecture (see
[
6
,
14
]
) is then that
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(k,d))=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}
, in keeping with the lower bound in Observation
1.1
.
Bollobás and Radcliffe
[
4
]
proved this conjecture for
k=4
and
d=2
.
However, in full generality, it was disproved by Frankl
[
12
]
and later, in a very strong sense, by Bukh and Goaoc
[
7
]
.
In particular, from their result it follows that for every
d\geq 2
and every exponent
\ell\in\mathds{N}
, there is a sufficiently large
k
such that
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(k,d))=\Omega(n^{\ell})
.
However, for
k\geq 5
and
d\geq 2
, the problem of determining the asymptotics of
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(k,d))
remains wide open.
Indeed, it seems to be significantly harder to determine the extremal number of a simplicial complex if it does not agree with the lower bound in Observation
1.1
.

Our main result, Theorem
1.2
below, breaks through this barrier, asymptotically determining the extremal number of a simplicial complex
B
for which the extremal number is not given by the bound in Observation
1.1
.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result of this type and shows that
the extremal behaviour of simplicial complexes can be genuinely different from that of the uniform hypergraph given by the largest layer.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
B=(\{v_{1},\dots,v_{5}\},\{v_{3}v_{4},v_{1}v_{2}v_{3},v_{1}v_{4}v_{5}\})
, there is a constant
C\in\mathds{N}
such that

\text{\rm ex}(n,B)\leq\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+C\,

for every
n\geq 5
.

As shown by the following example, this bound is best possible up to the constant.

Example 1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
with
n\equiv 6\mod{8}
, let
V=V_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{2}
for some sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
with
|V_{2}|
even and
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|=n
, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a perfect
2
-uniform matching on
V_{2}
.
Furthermore, let

\displaystyle E^{\prime}=\{abc:ab\in\mathcal{M}\text{ and }c\in V_{1}\}\cup V_{1}^{(2)}

and let
E
be the downward closure of
E^{\prime}
.
Then set
H=(V,E)
and note that
B\not\subseteq H
and

\displaystyle|E|=

\displaystyle\frac{n-|V_{1}|}{2}|V_{1}|+(n-|V_{1}|)|V_{1}|+\binom{|V_{1}|}{2}+\frac{n-|V_{1}|}{2}+n+1\,.

The right-hand side is maximised if
|V_{1}|=\frac{3}{4}n-\frac{1}{2}
, in which case it is equal to
\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+\frac{5}{4}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is the longest and most difficult part of this paper.
We believe that the approach used for this proof might have further applications for hypergraph Turán-type problems.
Roughly speaking, it proceeds by gaining more and more structure on an extremal
B
-free simplicial complex, ultimately yielding the same structure as Example
1.3
.
This is done by introducing and analysing a parameter
\varrho
, which is defined for every vertex and depends on its link, and symmetrising a subset of the vertices determined by
\varrho
.

We now state our results where, up to lower order terms, the extremal number is given by the lower bound in Observation
1.1
.
We begin with the comparatively simple case of two disjoint
3
-edges connected by a
2
-edge.

Theorem 1.4
.

For the simplicial complex
F=(\{v_{1},\dots,v_{6}\},\{v_{1}v_{2}v_{3},v_{4}v_{5}v_{6},v_{1}v_{4}\})
, there is
C\in\mathds{N}
such that
n^{2}-n+2\leq\text{\rm ex}(n,F)\leq n^{2}-n+C
.

Given a
3
-graph
F
and
A\subseteq V(F)
, let
F^{+}
be the simplicial complex obtained from
F
by adding a new vertex
a
and all
2
-edges from
a
to
A
(and then taking the downward closure).
Our next theorem follows from a more general result, Theorem
4.1
, that we prove in Section
4
.
It gives a general condition under which the extremal number of
F^{+}
is approximately equal to the bound in Observation
1.1
provided we also require that the host simplicial complex is
2
-dimensional.
Let us call a
3
-graph
proper
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is some
n_{0}\in\mathds{N}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, we have

(1+\varepsilon)\left[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)-\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)\right]\geq\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}\,.

For instance, if
\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)\approx Cn^{\alpha}
for some
C>0
and
\alpha\geq 1
, then
F
is proper.
Moreover, by duplicating a vertex that has at least the average degree in an extremal example for
F
on
n-1
vertices (combined with a standard double counting argument from
[
16
]
), one can easily see that if the shadow of
F
is complete, then
F
is proper.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every proper
3
-graph
F
,
A\subseteq V(F)
and
\eta,\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds.
If a
2
-dimensional simplicial complex
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n^{2}}+1\geq\Big(\frac{(|A|-1)}{|A|}+\eta\Big)\big[1+\frac{\Delta^{(3)}}{2}\big]\,,

where
\Delta^{(3)}=\max_{xy\in V(H)^{(2)}}d^{(3)}(xy)
, and
e(H)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]
, then
H
contains a copy of
F^{+}
.

The following corollary of Theorem
4.1
(using that the extremal number of the
3
-graph with two edges on four vertices has been determined in
[
11
]
) asymptotically determines the extremal number of another natural simplicial complex on five vertices.

Corollary 1.6
.

For the simplicial complex
F=(\{v_{1},\dots,v_{5}\},\{v_{1}v_{2}v_{3},v_{2}v_{3}v_{4},v_{2}v_{5},v_{3}v_{5}\})
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\text{\rm ex}(n,F)=(\frac{4}{3}\pm\varepsilon)\binom{n}{2}
for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

Finally, we determine the asymptotics for the extremal number of the simplicial complex on four vertices given by two
3
-edges that intersect in two vertices and a
2
-edge between the two vertices which do not lie in the intersection.

Theorem 1.7
.

For the simplicial complex
F=(\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\},\{v_{1}v_{2}v_{3},v_{2}v_{3}v_{4},v_{1}v_{4}\})
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
ex(n,F)=\big(\frac{2}{9}\pm\varepsilon\big)\binom{n}{3}
for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

Notation

For a simplicial complex
H=(V,E)
,
S\subseteq V
, and
i\in\mathds{N}
, we define the
i
-uniform degree of
S
by
d^{(i)}(S)=|\{e\in E:S\subseteq e,|e|=i\}|
.
Omitting parentheses around
1
- and
2
-sets, we have, in particular, that
d^{(i)}(v)=|\{e\in E:v\in e,|e|=i\}|
for
v\in V
.
We also write
d^{(\geq i)}(v)=\sum_{j\geq i}d^{(j)}(v)
.
The degree of
v
counts all edges containing
v
except the singleton, that is,
d(v)=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N},i\geq 2}d^{(i)}(v)
.
The neighbourhood of
v
is
N(v)=\{w\in V\smallsetminus v:\exists e\in E:v,w\in e\}
and, more generally, the neighbourhood of a set
S
is
N(S)=\{w\in V\smallsetminus S:\exists e\in E:S\subseteq e,w\in e\}
.
When considering simplicial complexes and the context is clear, we will usually only mention those edges whose downward closure is the edge set.
For a vertex
v\in V
, let
L_{v}
be the link of
v
in
H
, i.e., the simplicial complex with vertex set
V-v
and edge set
\{e-v:v\in e\in E\}
.
Finally, we write
E^{(i)}(H)=\{e\in E\colon|e|=i\}
,
e^{(i)}(H)=|E^{(i)}|
, and
e(H)=|E|
.
Similarly, we write
v(H)=|V|
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
H=(V,E)
be a simplicial complex with
|V|=n
,
B\not\subseteq H
, and assume for the sake of contradiction that
|E|\geq\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+C
, where
C
is some sufficiently large constant.

Roughly speaking, we proceed as follows.
By induction on the number of vertices, we may assume some minimum degree.
The proof is then centered around a parameter
\varrho(v)
, defined for every vertex
v
.
Depending on the role that
v
plays in the link of another vertex
w
, we know how
\varrho(v)
and
\varrho(w)
relate.
This allows us to determine the rough structure of
H
.
By continuing to analyse
\varrho
and by symmetrising a subset of the vertices determined by
\varrho
, we can simplify this structure further.
Finally, we obtain a
B
-free simplicial complex which equals the extremal example aside from maybe the sizes of the two partition classes and has at least as many edges as the initial
H
.
A simple optimisation then finishes the proof.

Let us formalise the proof by performing an induction on
n
.
Let
n_{0}\in\mathds{N}
be sufficiently large and choose
C\in\mathds{N}
large enough that every simplicial complex on
n<n_{0}
vertices with at least
\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+C
edges contains a copy of
B
.
We take those cases as the base of our induction.
The remainder of this section is devoted to the induction step.

For
n\geq n_{0}
, assume that every simplicial complex on
n-1
vertices with at least
\frac{9}{8}\binom{n-1}{2}+\frac{21}{16}(n-1)+C
edges contains
B
.
Let
H=(V,E)
be a simplicial complex with
|V|=n
,
B\not\subseteq H
, and assume for the sake of contradiction that
|E|\geq\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+C
.
If there is a vertex
v\in V
with
d(v)\leq\frac{9}{8}(n-1)+\frac{5}{16}
, we have that
|E(H-v)|\geq\frac{9}{8}\binom{n}{2}+\frac{21}{16}n+C-d(v)-1\geq\frac{9}{8}\binom{n-1}{2}+\frac{21}{16}(n-1)+C
and so, by induction,
H-v
contains a copy of
B
.
Therefore, we may assume that for every
v\in V
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle d(v)>\frac{9}{8}(n-1)+\frac{5}{16}\,.

Clearly,
H
cannot contain any edges of size at least five.
Thus, the links consist only of
3
-edges,
2
-edges, and
1
-edges (and the empty set).
Furthermore, we have the following.

Claim 2.1
.

For every
v\in V
, there is no
2
-uniform path with three edges in
L_{v}
.

Proof.

Assume that there is a
2
-uniform path
abcd
in the link of some
v\in V
.
Since
H
is a simplicial complex, we have
bc\in E
and, by the definition of the link, we have
abv,cdv\in E
.
Thus, there is a copy of
B
in
H[a,b,c,d,v]
, a contradiction.
∎

For a simplicial complex
F
and a subcomplex
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
, we say that
F^{\prime}
is
isolated
in
F
if
F
does not contain an edge containing both a vertex of
F^{\prime}
and a vertex in
V(F)\smallsetminus V(F^{\prime})
.
Claim
2.1
means that every link consists only of: isolated vertices, isolated (
2
-)edges, isolated induced (
2
-uniform) stars with at least two edges, isolated (
2
-uniform) triangles, and potentially isolated
3
-edges.
However, the next claim states that
H
does not have any
4
-edges, so the links do not in fact contain any
3
-edges.

Claim 2.2
.

There are no
4
-edges in
H
.

Proof.

Assume that
e=v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}
is a
4
-edge.
Note that no
3
-edge can intersect
e
in exactly two vertices, as otherwise
B\subseteq H
.
For
i\in[4]
, let
N_{i}
be the set of vertices in
V\smallsetminus e
which are contained in a
3
-edge with
v_{i}
.
Note that for all distinct
i,j\in[4]
, we have that
v_{i}
has no
2
-edge to any vertex in
N_{j}
(as otherwise, again,
B\subseteq H
), so in particular
N_{i}
and
N_{j}
are disjoint.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
|N_{1}|\leq\dots\leq|N_{4}|
.
Since all
2
-edges between
v_{1}
and the vertices in
N_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}N_{3}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}N_{4}
are missing,
d^{(2)}(v_{1})\leq(n-1)-(|N_{2}|+|N_{3}|+|N_{4}|)
. Together with (
2.1
), this implies that

\displaystyle d^{(3)}(v_{1})+d^{(4)}(v_{1})>|N_{2}|+|N_{3}|+|N_{4}|+\frac{n-1}{8}+\frac{5}{16}.

On the other hand, by Claim
2.1
,
L_{v_{1}}[N_{1}]
does not contain any
2
-uniform path on three edges.
It is easy to check that this means the number of
2
- and
3
-edges in
L_{v_{1}}
is at most
4|N_{1}|/3
.
Adding the
3
- and
4
-edges in
e
containing
v_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle d^{(3)}(v_{1})+d^{(4)}(v_{1})\leq 4+\frac{4}{3}|N_{1}|,

which is a contradiction for
n
sufficiently large.
∎

Definition and basic properties of
\varrho
.

Let us now introduce the central parameter of the proof.
Let
\varphi(v)
denote the number of isolated
2
-edges in
L_{v}
and define

\displaystyle\varrho(v)=e^{(2)}(L_{v})+\varphi(v)-2\,.

The idea behind this parameter is that it is (a lower bound on) the number of
2
-edges that must be missing at a vertex
w
if
w
appears in an isolated
2
-edge in
L_{v}
.
We make this precise with the following claim.

Claim 2.3
.

Let
v,w,x\in V
be such that
wx
is an isolated edge in
L_{v}
.
Then
d^{(2)}(w)\leq n-1-\varrho(v)
.
Moreover,

\varrho(w)\geq d^{(3)}(w)-2>\frac{n-1}{8}-\frac{27}{16}+\varrho(v)\,.

Proof.

Let
ab\in E^{(2)}(L_{v})\smallsetminus\{wx\}
and note that since
wx
is an isolated edge in
L_{v}
, we have that
a,b,w,x
are distinct.
If
aw\in E
, then
H[a,b,v,w,x]
contains a copy of
B
, a contradiction.
Thus, the only
2
-edge in
H
formed by
w
and a vertex that is contained in a
2
-edge of
L_{v}
is
wx
.
In other words,
w
is ‘missing’ a
2
-edge to every vertex spanned by a
2
-edge in
E^{(2)}(L_{v})\smallsetminus\{wx\}
.

Recall that, by Claims
2.1
and
2.2
,
L_{v}
consists only of isolated vertices, isolated
2
-edges, isolated induced (
2
-uniform) stars with at least two edges, and isolated (
2
-uniform) triangles.
Clearly, every isolated
2
-edge in
L_{v}
spans exactly two vertices.
Moreover, we have that isolated (
2
-uniform) triangles and stars span at least as many vertices as they have
2
-edges.
Therefore, the total number of vertices spanned by
E^{(2)}\smallsetminus\{wx\}
is at least
e^{(2)}(L_{v})+\varphi(v)-2=\varrho(v)
.
By the argument in the paragraph above, each of those vertices corresponds to a missing
2
-edge in
w
.
This yields the first inequality of the claim.

The moreover part simply follows from the first part, (
2.1
), and the definition of
\varrho
.
∎

The following claim is in a sense the reverse of the previous one.
For
v,w\in V
, we say that
w
is a
tip of a star in
L_{v}
, and write
w\leftarrow v
, if there are
x,y\in V
such that
wx,xy\in E(L_{v})
.

Claim 2.4
.

Let
v,w\in V
be such that
w\leftarrow v
.
Then
d^{(2)}(v)\leq n-\varrho(w)
.
Moreover,

\varrho(v)\geq d^{(3)}(v)-2>\frac{n-1}{8}-\frac{43}{16}+\varrho(w)\,.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in V
be such that
wx,xy\in E(L_{v})
and note that

Remark 2.10
.

In the remainder of this subsection, we will show how to symmetrise the pairs that form edges in

. This is not strictly necessary for the proof, so the reader may already jump to the next subsection. However, we believe that this technique of iterative partial symmetrisation, where we symmetrise certain vertex subsets and certain subsets of the pairs of vertices at appropriate junctures, has the potential to be more broadly useful in the study of hypergraph Turán problems, so we decided to include it regardless.

We symmetrise the pairs that form edges in

by essentially replacing any pair by a pair of larger degree as long as there still exist pairs which do not ‘look the same’.

\displaystyle E_{x}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\{e\smallsetminus x:e\in E(H),e\cap\{x,y\}=x\}\,,

\displaystyle E_{y}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\{e\smallsetminus y:e\in E(H),e\cap\{x,y\}=y\}\,,\text{ and }

\displaystyle E_{xy}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\{e\smallsetminus\{x,y\}:e\in E(H),e\cap\{x,y\}=\{x,y\}\}.

When the pair

is clear from context we will omit the

If there are

Without loss of generality, assume that

\displaystyle|E_{x}|+|E_{y}|+|E_{xy}|\geq|E_{x^{\prime}}|+|E_{y^{\prime}}|+|E_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}|.

Delete the vertices

Proof of Theorem

For the lower bound, take an

Proof of Theorem
1.5

We will actually prove the following more general theorem, of which Theorem
1.5
is a special case.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every proper
3
-graph
F
,
A\subseteq V(F)
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds.
If a simplicial complex
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3}n^{n-2}}{(n+3)^{n}}\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+n^{2}\big]\geq\Big[\frac{(|A|-1)}{|A|}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{v(F)-|A|}{|A|}\Big]\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]\,,

where
\Delta^{(i)}=\max_{xy\in V(H)^{(2)}}d^{(i)}(xy)
, and
e(H)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]
, then
H
contains a copy of
F^{+}
if either:

(1)

H
does not contain any edge of size
4
or

(2)

F
is contained in a
4
-edge.

To see that Theorem
4.1
indeed implies Theorem
1.5
, observe that
\big(\tfrac{n}{n+3}\big)^{n}
is a decreasing function and tends to
e^{-3}
as
n
tends to infinity.
Therefore, we have that (
1.1
) yields (
4.1
).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
F
be a proper
3
-graph,
A\subseteq V(F)
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
C
be a sufficiently large constant (
C^{-1}\ll\varepsilon,1/v(F)
).
We will show by induction on
n
that if
H
is a simplicial complex on
n\geq v(F^{+})
vertices satisfying (
4.1
) and
e(H)\geq(1+\varepsilon/2)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]+C
, then
H
contains a copy of
F^{+}
.
It then follows that there is some
n_{0}
such that every simplicial complex
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices satisfying (
4.1
) and
e(H)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]
contains a copy of
F^{+}
.

The base of the induction and the statement for small
n
both follow from
C
being large.
Now let
n
be large and assume that for every
n^{\prime}\leq n-1
, every simplicial complex
H^{\prime}
on
n^{\prime}
vertices satisfying (
4.1
) (with
n^{\prime}
in place of
n
) and
e(H^{\prime})\geq(1+\varepsilon/2)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n^{\prime},F)+\binom{n^{\prime}}{2}\big]+C
contains a copy of
F^{+}
.
Let
H
be a simplicial complex on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices satisfying (
4.1
) with
e(H)\geq(1+\varepsilon/2)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]+C
.
Assume for the sake of contradiction that
H
does not contain a copy of
F^{+}
.
We first derive a minimum degree condition.
Suppose that there is a vertex
v
in
H
with
d(v)\leq\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}\Big[\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}+n\Big]
.
Note that for some fixed
\eta>0
and large

, we have
\frac{(n+3)^{n}}{e^{3}n^{n}}(1+\varepsilon/2)>1+\eta
.
By the definition of proper (applied with
\eta
instead of
\varepsilon
) and since

is large, we therefore have

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\varepsilon/2)(n+3)^{n}}{e^{3}n^{n}}\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)-\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)\big]\geq\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}

or, after rearranging,

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon/2)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)-\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)\big]\geq\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}\,.

Keeping in mind that

is large and so
\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}<1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
, this in turn implies that

\displaystyle e(H\!-\!v)

\displaystyle\!\geq\!\Big(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+\binom{n}{2}\big]+C-\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}\Big[\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}+n\Big]-1

\displaystyle\!\geq\!\Big(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big)\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)+\binom{n-1}{2}\big]+C\,.

In order to apply induction, we must also check that (
4.1
) holds for
H-v
and
n-1
instead of
H
and
n
.
But, by a double counting argument from
[
16
]
, we have
\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)}{n-3}\geq\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}
, so,
since
n
is large, we have

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3}(n-1)^{n-3}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)+(n-1)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{3}(n-1)^{n-3}(n-3)}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n-1,F)}{n-3}+\frac{e^{3}(n-1)^{n-1}(n+3)^{n}}{(n+2)^{n-1}e^{3}n^{n}}\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{3}(n-1)^{n-3}(n-3)}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}+\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{e^{3}n^{n-2}}{(n+3)^{n}}\big[\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)+n^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\Big[\frac{(|A|-1)}{|A|}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{v(F)-|A|}{|A|}\Big]\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{H}^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\Big[\frac{(|A|-1)}{|A|}+\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{v(F)-|A|}{|A|}\Big]\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{H-v}^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]\,,

where the second inequality follows from
\frac{e^{3}(n-1)^{n-3}(n-3)}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{e^{3}n^{n-2}}{(n+3)^{n}}
for large

.
Hence, we can apply induction to

, a contradiction.
Thus, we may assume that

for every

If either of the conditions (

) or (

) holds, we know that

contains a copy of

.
Abusing notation slightly, we now use

and

to denote the images of

and

under some embedding.
For

, there must be a missing

-edge between every vertex in

Therefore, there is some vertex

\frac{n-v(F)}{|A|}
missing

Note also that since

Combining this with our lower bound on the degree (of any vertex) and our upper bound on

, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}\big[\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n}+n\big]

\displaystyle<d(a)\leq d^{(2)}(a)\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(|A|-1)n+v(F)-|A|}{|A|}\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]\,

\displaystyle\frac{e^{3}n^{n}}{(n+3)^{n}}\big[\frac{\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}(n,F)}{n^{2}}+1\big]<\Big[\frac{(|A|-1)}{|A|}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{v(F)-|A|}{|A|}\Big]\big[1+\sum_{i\geq 2}\frac{\Delta^{(i+1)}}{i}\big]\,.

But this contradicts (

), meaning that

.

6.
Concluding Remarks

In this work, we studied the Turán problem for simplicial complexes.
Since this problem seems to be of comparable difficulty to the Turán problem for uniform hypergraphs, any non-trivial result on the following problem would be of interest.

Problem 6.1
.

Given a simplicial complex
F
and a positive integer
n
, determine
\text{\rm ex}(n,F)
or its asymptotics.

Theorem
1.2
shows that this problem is distinct from the problem for uniform hypergraphs in that, for some
F
, the extremal construction for
F
has more than one incomplete layer.
On the other hand, we showed that for several simplicial complexes
F
, the extremal number is asymptotically given by the trivial bound
\text{\rm ex}^{(k)}(n,F_{k})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}
, where
k=\dim(F)+1
.
This suggests the following problem.

Problem 6.2
.

Given an integer
k\geq 3
, characterise the
(k-1)
-dimensional simplicial complexes
F
with
\text{\rm ex}(n,F)=\text{\rm ex}^{(k)}(n,F_{k})+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, Hajnal (see
[
6
,
15
]
) initiated the investigation of a variant of the Brown–Erdős–Sós problem
[
22
]
for simplicial complexes. We believe this special case of the Turán problem for simplicial complexes to be of particular interest.
Recall that
\mathcal{F}(k,d)
denotes the family of all simplicial complexes with vertex set
[k]
and
1+\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{k}{i}
edges.

Problem 6.3
.

For
k\geq 5
and
d\geq 2
, determine
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(k,d))
or its asymptotics.

We have already noted that Bollobás and Radcliffe
[
4
]
solved Problem
6.3
for
k=4
and
d=2
.
A simple inductive argument gives the following upper bound for the case
k=5
and
d=2
.

Proposition 6.4
.

For every
n\geq 5
,
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(5,2))\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{5/2}+3n^{2})
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction, noting that the base case
n=5
follows easily since
\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{5/2}+3n^{2})\geq 55>32=\sum_{i=0}^{5}\binom{5}{i}
.
Suppose now that
H
is a simplicial complex on
n
vertices with
e(H)>\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{5/2}+3n^{2})
.

If there is a vertex
v
such that
d(v)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{3/2}+3n-2)
, then

e(H-v)>\frac{1}{2}(n^{5/2}+3n^{2}-n^{3/2}-3n+2)-1\geq\frac{1}{2}\big((n-1)^{5/2}+3(n-1)^{2}\big),

so, by induction,
H-v
contains some
F\in\mathcal{F}(5,2)
.
Therefore, we may assume that

(6.1)

\displaystyle d(v)>\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{3/2}+3n-2)

for every vertex
v\in V(H)
.

Suppose that there is an edge
e\in E^{(4)}(H)
.
Then it is easy to see that for any vertex
v\in V(H)\smallsetminus e
the set
e\cup\{v\}
spans at least
17
edges. Hence, we may assume that
E^{(4)}=\varnothing
and so (
6.1
) yields that
d^{(3)}(v)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{3/2}+n)
for every vertex
v\in V(H)
.
Therefore, for any vertex
v\in V(H)
, the classical theorem of Kővari, Sós and Turán
[
19
]
implies that there is a (
2
-uniform) copy of
C_{4}
in the link of
v
.
It is not hard to check that the vertices of such a
C_{4}
together with
v
span at least
18
edges.
∎

Question 6.5
.

Is it true that
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(5,2))=\Omega(n^{5/2})
?

Let
LC_{4}^{(3)}
be the
3
-uniform hypergraph with
V(LC_{4}^{(3)})=\{u,v_{1},\dots,v_{4}\}
and
E(LC_{4}^{(3)})=\{uv_{i}v_{i+1}\colon i\in[4]\}
, where the indices are viewed as in
\mathds{Z}/4\mathds{Z}
.
In other words,
LC_{4}^{(3)}
is the
3
-uniform hypergraph consisting of a vertex
u
with a (
2
-uniform)
C_{4}
in its link.
By following the proof of Proposition
6.4
, we get that
\text{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{F}(5,2))\leq\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}\big(n,LC_{4}^{(3)}\big)+\binom{n}{2}+n+1
.
In particular, improving the bound
\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}\big(n,LC_{4}^{(3)}\big)=O(n^{5/2})
would answer Question
6.5
in the negative.
However, a result of Brown, Erdős and Sós
[
22
, Theorem 3]
shows that
\text{\rm ex}^{(3)}\big(n,LC_{4}^{(3)}\big)=\Omega(n^{5/2})
, perhaps giving some evidence towards a positive answer for Question
6.5
.

Let us conclude by briefly discussing the problem of determining the extremal number of the simplicial complex induced by the
k
-uniform clique on
t
vertices for
t\geq k+2
.
Denote the simplicial complex given by the downward closure of
K_{t}^{(k)}
by
K(t,k)
.

Problem 6.6
.

Given
t\geq k+2
, determine the asymptotics of
\text{\rm ex}(n,K(t,k))
.

A simple symmetrisation argument yields the following result for the case
k=2
.

Observation 6.7
.

For
t\geq 3
,
\varepsilon>0
and
n
sufficiently large,
\text{\rm ex}(n,K(t+1,2))=(1\pm\varepsilon)\frac{t!}{t^{t}}\binom{n}{{t}}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a simplicial complex on
n
vertices without a
K(t+1,2)
and, subject to this condition, with the maximum number of edges.
Assume that there are three vertices
x
,
y
and
z
such that
xy
and
yz
are not in
E(H)
but
xz
is.
If
d(y)\geq d(x),d(z)
, then we can replace
x
and
z
by copies of
y
.
This would yield a simplicial complex without a
K(t+1,2)
but with more edges than
H
, a contradiction.
Otherwise, for one of
x
and
z
, say, without loss of generality, for
x
, we have
d(x)>d(y)
.
In this case we can replace
y
by a copy of
x
, again yielding a simplicial complex without a
K(t+1,2)
but with more edges than
H
.
Thus, we may assume that non-adjacency is an equivalence relation, so that
H
is complete
r
-partite for some
r\leq t
, that is, the vertex set can be partitioned into
r
sets such that every edge contains at most one vertex from each of these sets.
Therefore,
H
has
\max_{r\in[t]}(1\pm\varepsilon)\frac{r!}{r^{r}}\binom{n}{r}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\frac{t!}{t^{t}}\binom{n}{t}
edges.
∎

This problem is related to another much-studied problem in extremal combinatorics.
Following Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
, given two
k
-graphs
F
and
G
, we write
\text{\rm ex}(n,F,G)
for the maximum number of copies of
F
in a
G
-free
k
-graph on
n
vertices.

Observation 6.8
.

For
t\geq k+2
,
\varepsilon>0
and
n
sufficiently large,

\text{\rm ex}(n,K(t,k))=(1\pm\varepsilon)\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{t-1}^{(k)},K_{t}^{(k)})\,.

Proof.

Consider a simplicial complex
H
on
n
vertices with at least
(1+\varepsilon)\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{t-1}^{(k)},K_{t}^{(k)})
edges.
Any
t
-edge in
H
would immediately yield a
K(t,k)
, so we can assume that
H
does not contain any edges of size larger than
t-1
.
Thus, since
n
is taken sufficiently large,
H
contains at least
(1+\varepsilon/2)\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{t-1}^{(k)},K_{t}^{(k)})
edges of size
t-1
.
Each of these induces a
K_{t-1}^{(k)}
.
But, by definition, this implies that
H
contains a
K_{t}^{(k)}
and, thereby, a
K(t,k)
.

Suppose now that
H^{\prime\prime}
is a
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices that maximises the number of copies of
K_{t-1}^{(k)}
without containing a
K_{t}^{(k)}
.
Let
H^{\prime}
be the
(t-1)
-uniform hypergraph on the same vertex set that is obtained by placing a
(t-1)
-edge for every
K_{t-1}^{(k)}
of
H^{\prime\prime}

and let
H
be the
downward closure of
H^{\prime}
.
Clearly, since
n
is large,
H
has more than
\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{t-1}^{(k)},K_{t}^{(k)})
edges.
It is also easy to check that
H
does not contain a copy of
K(t,k)
.
∎

Combining Observation
6.8
with
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
, which implies that
\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{t-1},K_{t})=(1+o(1))(\frac{n}{t})^{t}
, gives another proof of Observation
6.7
.
Moreover, using flag algebras, Bodnar
[
3
]
recently proved that
\text{\rm ex}(n,K_{4}^{(3)},K_{5}^{(3)})=(\frac{3}{8}+o(1))\binom{n}{4}
.
By Observation
6.8
, this implies that
\text{\rm ex}(n,K(5,3))=(\frac{3}{8}+o(1))\binom{n}{4}
.
To the best of our knowledge, Problem
6.6
remains open for all other
(t,k)
with
t\geq 6
or
k\geq 3
.

Abstract
. We show that the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators form natural spaces of initial data when applying ℓ p \ell^{p} -decoupling inequalities to local smoothing for the wave equation. This yields new local smoothing estimates which, in a quantified manner, improve the bounds in the local smoothing conjecture on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for p ≥ 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) / ( n − 1 ) p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1) , and complement them for 2 < p < 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) / ( n − 1 ) 2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1) . These estimates are invariant under application of Fourier integral operators, and they are essentially sharp.

1.
Introduction

This article concerns the local smoothing conjecture for the Euclidean wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The phenomenon of local smoothing revolves around determining, for each
1<p<\infty
, the minimal
s\in\mathbb{R}
for which there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(1.1)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The optimal fixed-time estimates for the half-wave propagator
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
from
[
16
,
15
]
imply that one may choose
s=2s(p)
, where we write

s(p):=\frac{n-1}{2}\Big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big|

throughout. This estimate is sharp for
1<p\leq 2
, but it can be improved for
p>2
and
n\geq 2
. In fact, the local smoothing conjecture, as originally formulated by Sogge in
[
24
]
, stipulates that (
1.1
) should hold with
s=\sigma(p)+\varepsilon
for each
\varepsilon>0
, where
\sigma(p)=0
for
2<p\leq 2n/(n-1)
, and
\sigma(p)=2s(p)-1/p
for
p>2n/(n-1)
.

Although the local smoothing conjecture is still open in full generality, there are many partial results available, and the conjecture was proved by Guth, Wang and Zhang
[
9
]
for
n=2
. For the purposes of the present article, however, it is relevant to highlight the article
[
3
]
by Bourgain and Demeter. Building on work by Wolff
[
27
]
, they showed that (
1.1
) holds with
s=\sigma(p)+\varepsilon
for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, by proving the
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality. More precisely, one has

(1.2)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{d(p)+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

d(p):=\begin{cases}2s(p)-\frac{1}{p}&\text{if }p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\\
s(p)&\text{if }2\leq p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.\end{cases}

Main results

The local smoothing conjecture is sharp, in the sense that (
1.1
) does not hold for
s<\sigma(p)
(see e.g.
[
2
, Remark 2.6]
). Nonetheless, in this article we obtain improved local smoothing estimates for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, by working with a different space of initial data in (
1.2
). Our main result, formulated in terms of the Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})={\langle}D\rangle^{-s}\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
over the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see Definition
2.1
), is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that

(1.3)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
4.4
, which considers the more general class of operators of the form
e^{it\phi(D)}
, where
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is positively homogeneous of degree
1
and such that
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)\equiv n-1
. Moreover, the exponent
d(p)-s(p)
in (
1.3
) is sharp, as is shown in Theorem
5.3
.

The Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for Fourier integral operators (FIOs) was introduced by Smith in
[
23
]
, and decades later his construction was extended to a scale
(\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))_{1\leq p\leq\infty}
of invariant spaces for FIOs by Hassell, Portal and the author in
[
10
]
. More precisely,
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under FIOs of order zero which have a compactly supported Schwartz kernel and are associated with a local canonical graph, and one has

(1.4)

W^{s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq W^{-s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for all
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, with the natural modifications involving the local Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p=1
, and
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p=\infty
. In particular,

W^{d(p)+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq W^{d(p)-2s(p)+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
2<p<\infty
, and (
1.3
) recovers (
1.2
). However, since the exponents in (
1.4
) are sharp, (
1.3
) is in fact a strict improvement of (
1.2
). And since
d(p)=\sigma(p)
for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, Theorem
1.1
improves upon the local smoothing conjecture for such
p
.

Note that
d(p)-s(p)=\max(0,s(p)-1/p)
,

d(p)-s(p)=0=\sigma(p)\quad\text{for}\quad 2<p\leq 2\frac{n}{n-1},

and

d(p)-s(p)=0<2s(p)-\frac{1}{p}=\sigma(p)<d(p)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\frac{n}{n-1}<p<2\frac{n+1}{n-1}.

Hence the sharpness of the embeddings in (
1.4
) implies that, for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, (
1.3
) neither follows from the local smoothing conjecture nor implies it.

It was shown in
[
12
]
that the
\varepsilon
loss in (
1.2
) can be removed for
n\geq 4
and
p>2+\frac{4}{n-3}>2\frac{n+1}{n-1}
. It is not clear whether one may also let
\varepsilon=0
in (
1.3
) for certain
p
. For
p>2+\frac{4}{n-3}
, by the sharpness of the exponents in (
1.4
), Theorem
1.1
neither follows from the local smoothing result in
[
12
]
nor implies it.

Applications

The invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under FIOs and related operators shows that (
1.3
) and (
1.2
) are invariant under application of such operators to the initial data. Corollary
4.5
collects a few instances of this paradigm.

In particular, the Cauchy problem associated to the half-wave equation
\partial_{t}u(t)=i\sqrt{-\Delta}u(t)
is well posed on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence in Theorem
1.1
one in fact has

[t\mapsto e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f]\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{p}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here it is relevant to note that, since
(e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group, one has
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\in\mathcal{H}^{r,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if this holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and no smoothing can occur. Instead, the half-wave group has a smoothing effect on initial data with respect to the Sobolev embeddings in (
1.4
).

Local smoothing estimates have applications to various problems in harmonic and geometric analysis, such as spherical maximal theorems (see
[
2
,
25
]
). It would be of interest to examine applications of the results in this article to these areas.

In
[
21
]
, Schippa obtained local smoothing estimates for the Schrödinger equation using invariant spaces for Schrödinger propagators, the modulation spaces. He then used those estimates to prove new local and global well-posedness results for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Very recently, in
[
20
]
, Schippa and the author applied this approach to function spaces related to the Hardy spaces for FIOs, to prove new local and global well-posedness results for nonlinear wave equations.

Idea behind the proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof exploits the full strength of the
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality, a corollary of the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality from
[
3
]
. This inequality bounds, for a suitable
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and for initial data
f
with frequency support in a dyadic annulus, the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
norm of
g(t)e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)
by an
\ell^{p}
sum involving a dyadic-parabolic decomposition of the frequency support of
f
. This dyadic-parabolic decomposition goes back to
[
8
]
and plays a key role in the proof of the optimal
L^{p}
regularity of Fourier integral operators
[
22
]
. On each dyadic annulus one makes a different discrete decomposition, and the right-hand side of the decoupling inequality involves a different
decoupling norm
on each annulus.

By contrast, the Hardy spaces for FIOs involve a continuous dyadic-parabolic decomposition which is the same on each frequency annulus, and one observation in this article is that the discrete and continuous decompositions are equivalent on dyadic annuli (see Proposition
4.1
). It then follows from the fact that the half-wave group is polynomially bounded on the Hardy spaces for FIOs that, when applied to the function
g(t)e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)
in the setting of Theorem
1.1
, the right-hand side of the
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality is equivalent to the
\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of
f
. In fact, Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of Theorem
4.4
, which is proved in the same manner but relies on a more general class of
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequalities, from
[
4
]
. As is shown in Corollary
4.2
, the Hardy spaces for FIOs are adapted to each of these decoupling inequalities.

Hence, at least when restricted to dyadic frequency annuli, the Hardy spaces for FIOs form the
largest
space of initial data for which one can obtain local smoothing estimates when applying
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequalities in the manner in which they are typically used. Moreover, since the exponent
d(p)-s(p)
in (
1.3
) is sharp, for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
one cannot improve upon the local smoothing conjecture by using the Hardy spaces for FIOs as spaces of initial data. Here it is relevant to note that the example which shows that the exponent
d(p)-s(p)
is sharp involves, for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, a square function which was used in
[
9
]
to prove the full local smoothing conjecture for
n=2
(see Lemma
5.2
). On the other hand, while the local smoothing conjecture is still open for
n\geq 3
, this article contains the essentially optimal local smoothing estimates with the Hardy spaces for FIOs as spaces of initial data.

2
contains background on the Hardy spaces for FIOs, and Section

Notation

The natural numbers are
{{\mathbb{N}}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}:={{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{0\}
. Throughout this article we fix
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with
n\geq 2
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
{\langle}\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
, and
\hat{\xi}=\xi/|\xi|
if
\xi\neq 0
. We use multi-index notation, where
\partial_{\xi}=(\partial_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\xi_{n}})
and
\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{\xi_{1}}\ldots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{\xi_{n}}

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
. Moreover,
\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi
is the mixed Hessian of a function
\Phi
of the variables
x
and
\eta
.
The Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
, and the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by
\varphi(D)
.

We write
f(s)\lesssim g(s)
to indicate that
f(s)\leq Cg(s)
for all
s
and a constant
C>0
independent of
s
, and similarly for
f(s)\gtrsim g(s)
and
g(s)\eqsim f(s)
.

2.
Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators

In this section we first introduce the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators, and then we collect a few of their basic properties.

2.1.
Definitions

Fix a non-negative radial
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\varphi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|>1
, and
\varphi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of zero. For
\omega\in S^{n-1}
,
\sigma>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, set
\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(\xi):=c_{\sigma}\varphi(\tfrac{\hat{\xi}-\omega}{\sqrt{\sigma}})
, where
c_{\sigma}:=(\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(\frac{e_{1}-\nu}{\sqrt{\sigma}})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu)^{-1/2}
for
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
the first basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(this choice is irrelevant). Here and throughout,
\mathrm{d}\nu
is the normalized surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. Also set
\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(0):=0
. Let
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative, radial, such that
\Psi(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\notin[1/2,2]
, and such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(\sigma\xi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}=1
for all
\xi\neq 0
. Now, for
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

\varphi_{\omega}(\xi):=\int_{0}^{4}\Psi(\sigma\xi)\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}.

Some properties of these functions are as follows (see
[
18
, Remark 3.3]
):

(1)

For all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
one has

(2.1)

\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)=0\text{ if }|\xi|<\tfrac{1}{8}\text{ or }|\hat{\xi}-\omega|>2|\xi|^{-1/2}.

(2)

For all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
there exists a
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
such that

|(\omega\cdot\partial_{\xi})^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}|\xi|^{\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}-\beta}

for all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
.

(3)

For all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
there exists a
C_{\alpha}\geq 0
such that

\Big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{-1}\Big|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{\frac{n-1}{4}-|\alpha|}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\geq 1/2
. Hence there exists a radial
m\in S^{(n-1)/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that if
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\xi|\geq 1/2\}
, then

f=\int_{S^{n-1}}m(D)\varphi_{\nu}(D)f\mathrm{d}\nu.

In (
3
),
S^{(n-1)/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the standard class of symbols
a
of order
(n-1)/4
. That is,
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
there exists a
C_{\alpha}\geq 0
such that

|\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}a(\eta)|\leq C_{\alpha}{\langle}\eta\rangle^{(n-1)/4-|\alpha|}

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Throughout, for simplicity of notation, we write
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
, and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the classical local Hardy space. We also fix a
q\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
q(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 2
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of those
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
q(D)f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for almost all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, and

\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{1/p}<\infty,

endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{1/p}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\langle}D\rangle^{s}f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|{\langle}D\rangle^{s}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In fact, this is not how the Hardy spaces for FIOs were originally defined in
[
23
]
and
[
10
]
, namely in terms of conical square functions and tent spaces over the cosphere bundle. That these definitions are equivalent was shown in
[
7
,
18
]
.

2.2.
Properties

First recall the Sobolev embeddings from (
1.4
). By
[
10
, Theorem 7.4]
, these in fact hold for all
p\in[1,\infty]
, upon replacing
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
{\langle}D\rangle^{-s}\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The Hardy spaces for FIOs form a complex interpolation scale. That is, let
p_{0},p_{1},p\in[1,\infty]
,
s_{0},s_{1},s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
and
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
. Then, as follows from
[
10
, Proposition 6.7]
, one has

[\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},p_{0}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]_{\theta}=\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Moreover, by
[
10
, Proposition 6.8]
, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
one has

(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here the duality pairing is the standard distributional pairing
{\langle}f,g\rangle
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By
[
10
, Proposition 6.6]
, the Schwartz functions lie dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so this uniquely determines the duality pairing.

The following lemma concerns a key feature of the Hardy spaces for FIOs: the relationship between the
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm and the
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of a function with frequency support in a dyadic-parabolic region.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that the following statements hold for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(2.2)

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\xi|\in[R/2,2R],|\hat{\xi}-\nu|\leq 2R^{-1/2}\}

for some
R\geq 1
and
\nu\in S^{n-1}
:

(1)

If
p\leq 2
, then

\frac{1}{C}R^{s-s(p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CR^{s-s(p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2)

If
p>2
, then

\frac{1}{C}R^{s+s(p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CR^{s+s(p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Since
f
has frequency support in a dyadic annulus, one has

\|{\langle}D\rangle^{s}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim R^{s}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim R^{s}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence the statement is a direct consequence of
[
7
, Proposition 6.4]
.
∎

3.
Fourier integral operators

For the general theory of Fourier integral operators, and the associated notions from symplectic geometry, we refer to
[
13
,
6
,
25
]
. To prove Theorem
1.1
we will only use Corollary
3.6
, concerning the operators
e^{it\phi(D)}
. However, these operators are part of a larger class of Fourier integral operators, introduced in Definition
3.1
, which are treated by the same methods. One of the key properties of the Hardy spaces for FIOs is their invariance under such operators.

3.1.
Definitions

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, recall that
S^{m}_{1,0}
consists of all
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
such that

(3.1)

\|a\|_{\alpha,\beta}:=\sup_{(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\langle}\eta\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}a(x,\eta)|<\infty

for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
. In
[
10
]
a larger symbol class was considered, the elements of which behave in an anisotropic manner which matches the dyadic-parabolic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This class, denoted by
S^{m}_{1/2,1/2,1}
, contains Hörmander’s
S^{m}_{1,1/2}
class but is strictly contained in
S^{m}_{1/2,1/2}
. The statements in Proposition
3.3
(
2
) and Lemma
3.5
below in fact extend to this larger symbol class.

In the following definition, a special case of
[
10
, Definition 2.11]
, and throughout,
o:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
denotes the zero section.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\in S^{m}_{1,0}
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o)
, and set

(3.2)

Tf(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(x,\eta)}a(x,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
T
is a Fourier integral operator of order
m
in
standard form
, associated with a
global canonical graph
, if:

(1)

\Phi
is real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree
1
in the
\eta
-variable;

(2)

\sup_{(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\Phi(x,\hat{\eta})|<\infty
for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq 2
;

(3)

\inf_{(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o}|\det\partial^{2}_{x\eta}\Phi(x,\eta)|>0
;

(4)

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\eta\mapsto\partial_{x}\Phi(x,\eta)
is a bijection on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

By Hadamard’s global inverse function theorem
[
14
, Theorem 6.2.8]
, condition (
4
) is superfluous for
n\geq 3
. Moreover, (
4
) holds if
\Phi(x,\eta)=x\cdot\eta+\phi(\eta)
for some
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
which is positively homogeneous of degree
1
.

If (
4
) holds, then it follows from the global inverse function theorem that the map
(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,\eta),\eta)\mapsto(x,\partial_{x}\Phi(x,\eta))
is a homogeneous canonical transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o
, and the canonical relation of
T
is the graph of this transformation.

Recall that a Fourier integral operator of order
m
, associated with a local canonical graph and having a compact Schwartz kernel, can, modulo an operator with a Schwartz kernel which is a Schwartz function, be expressed as a finite sum of operators which in appropriate coordinate systems are as in (
3.2
), where the symbol
a
has compact support in the
x
-variable (see e.g.
[
25
, Proposition 6.2.4]
). In this case, (
2
) is automatically satisfied, (
3
) holds on the support of
a
, and the map in (
4
) is a locally defined homogeneous canonical transformation. By contrast, in Definition
3.1
, the symbols are not required to have compact spatial support, but the conditions on the phase function are required to hold on all of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o
.

3.2.
Properties

The following proposition lists some classes of operators which are bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but in general not on
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
T
satisfy one of the following conditions:

(1)

T
is a Fourier integral operator of order zero, associated with a local canonical graph, with a compactly supported Schwartz kernel;

(2)

T
is a Fourier integral operator of order zero in standard form, associated with a global canonical graph;

(3)

T=\cos(t_{0}\sqrt{L})
, for
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
L
an elliptic divergence-form operator with bounded real-valued
C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coefficients. Moreover, one has
1<p<\infty
,
2s(p)<1
, and
-2+s(p)\leq s\leq 2-s(p)
.

Then
T:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded.

Proof.

(
1
): This follows in the same way as the case
s=0
from
[
10
]
; we will use the same terminology as there. By
[
10
, Proposition 2.14]
, there exist an
m\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, normal oscillatory integral operators
(T_{j,1})_{j=1}^{m}
and
(T_{j,2})_{j=1}^{m}
, and a smoothing operator
R
, such that
T=\sum_{j=1}^{m}T_{j,1}T_{j,2}+R
. As in the proof of
[
10
, Corollary 5.4]
, when conjugated with a suitable isometric wave packet transform
W:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the
T_{j,1}
and
T_{j,2}
can each be written as a sum of an operator which satisfies off-singularity bounds and a residual operator. The smoothing operator
R
yields a residual operator
WRW^{*}
as well, by
[
10
, Proposition 5.3]
. All these operators are bounded on tent spaces over the cosphere bundle, by
[
10
, Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 3.6]
, but for
s\neq 0
it is important to note that they are also bounded on the weighted tent spaces
T^{p}_{s}(S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n})
used in
[
11
]
. This is shown in the same way as
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
, where the canonical relations under consideration are canonical graphs on all of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o
, which plays no role in that proof. Moreover,
[
11
, Proposition 3.4]
says that
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|Wf\|_{T^{p}_{s}(S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. It follows from this that the
T_{j,1}
,
T_{j,2}
and
R
are all bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and therefore so is
T
.

(
2
): Let
q^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
equal
1
near zero, and set
T_{1}:=T(1-q^{\prime})(D)
and
T_{2}:=Tq^{\prime}(D)
. As in (
1
),
WT_{1}W^{*}
satisfies off-singularity bounds, by
[
10
, Corollary 5.2]
. Then the same arguments as above show that
T_{1}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

On the other hand, for the low-frequency term
T_{2}
, one can integrate by parts and apply Young’s inequality as in the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1.18]
to show that
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}T_{2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
. In particular,
T_{2}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{N,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Now choose
N>s+s(p)
, write
q^{\prime}=q^{\prime}q^{\prime\prime}
for some
q^{\prime\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so that
q^{\prime\prime}(D):\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and use (
1.4
), to obtain

T_{2}=T_{2}q^{\prime\prime}(D):\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{N,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(
3
): This is contained in
[
11
, Corollary 5.7]
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If the coefficients of the operator
L
in (
3
) have additional regularity, then the conditions on
p
and
s
can be weakened, cf.
[
11
]
. Moreover, similar statements hold for the other solution operator to the Cauchy problem associated with the wave equation
(\partial_{t}^{2}+L)u(t,x)=0
, and by
[
17
,
19
]
pseudodifferential operators with rough coefficients are also bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for suitable

and

.

In fact, we will need a more precise statement about the operator norm of families of Fourier integral operators as in (
2
).

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{t}
be a Fourier integral operator of order zero in standard form, associated with a global canonical graph, with symbol
a_{t}
and phase function
\Phi_{t}
. Suppose that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
, there exist
\varepsilon,\kappa,M>0
such that the following conditions hold for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

•

\|a_{t}\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq\kappa(1+|t|)^{M}
;

•

\sup_{(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\Phi_{t}(x,\hat{\eta})|\leq\kappa(1+|t|)^{M}
if
|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq 2
;

•

\inf_{(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus o}|\det\partial^{2}_{x\eta}\Phi(x,\eta)|\geq\varepsilon
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exist
C,N>0
such that

\|T_{t}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C(1+|t|)^{N}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This follows by keeping track of the constants in the proof of Proposition
3.3
(
2
). See also
[
10
, Remark 5.6]
and the remark after
[
10
, Theorem 6.10]
.
∎

We will apply this lemma to Fourier integral operators as in Remark

.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree

. Then there exist

such that

\|e^{it\phi(D)}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C(1+|t|)^{N}

for all

.

Remark 3.7
.

In the proof of our main result, we will only need bounds for

when restricted to dyadic frequency shells. In this case one can rely directly on kernel estimates, and one does not need bounds on all of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

).

4.
Main results

Throughout this section, for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, fix a maximal collection
\Theta_{k}\subseteq S^{n-1}
of unit vectors such that
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\geq 2^{-k/2}
for all
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Theta_{k}
. Note that
\Theta_{k}
has approximately
2^{k(n-1)/2}
elements.
Let
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be an associated partition of unity. That is, each
\chi_{\nu}
is positively homogeneous of degree
0
and satisfies
0\leq\chi_{\nu}\leq 1
and

\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\nu})\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\hat{\xi}-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2+1}\}.

Moreover,
\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)=1
for all
\xi\neq 0
, and for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
there exists a
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
independent of
k
such that, if
2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}
, then

|(\hat{\xi}\cdot\partial_{\xi})^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}2^{-k(|\alpha|/2+\beta)}

for all
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
. Such a collection is straightforward to construct, in a similar manner as the wave packets in Section
2
(see
[
26
, Section IX.4]
). Note also that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{\nu})\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\nu\in\Theta_{k}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

4.1.
A discrete description of the
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm

Using the decomposition of unity from above, we can give a discrete characterization of the
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of a function with frequency support in a dyadic annulus.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Then

\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
p<\infty
, while for
p=\infty
one has

\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})}\max_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

By a standard argument, using a Littlewood–Paley decomposition of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(and thereby also of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
), we may suppose that
s=-\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
.

Note that the statement is true for
p=2
. Indeed,
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\Big(\sum_{\nu}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\sum_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\Big)^{1/2}\eqsim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In the final step we used that
1\lesssim\sum_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)^{2}\leq(\sum_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\xi))^{2}=1
for all
\xi\neq 0
. More precisely, since there exists a
c_{n}\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, independent of
k
and
\Theta_{k}
, such that there are at most
c_{n}
elements
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
with
\chi_{\nu}(\xi)\neq 0
, one has
\sum_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)^{2}\geq\max_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\xi)^{2}\geq 1/c_{n}^{2}
.

Hence, by interpolation and duality, it suffices to prove the statement for
p=1
. We may also choose
k
large enough so that
q(D)f=0
, as follows e.g. from the Sobolev embeddings in (
1.4
). Note that Lemma
2.2
yields

(4.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(n-1)/4,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sum_{\nu}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(n-1)/4,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\sum_{\nu}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand,

\sum_{\nu}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(n-1)/4,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sum_{\nu}\int_{E_{\nu}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D){\langle}D\rangle^{(n-1)/4}\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\mathrm{d}\omega

for
E_{\nu}:=\{\omega\in S^{n-1}\mid|\omega-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2+3}\}
. Since the
\chi_{\nu}(D)
have kernels that are uniformly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and because the
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
are
2^{-k/2}
separated, one now has

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(n-1)/4,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu}\int_{E_{\nu}}\|{\langle}D\rangle^{(n-1)/4}\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(n-1)/4,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Together with (
4.1
), this concludes the proof.
∎

By combining this proposition with Corollary
3.6
, we can derive the following corollary, which will in turn play a key role in the proof of our main result.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\neq g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
p<\infty
, while for
p=\infty
one has

\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})}\max_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

We consider
p<\infty
. The argument for
p=\infty
is identical, up to a change in notation. By Proposition
4.1
, one has

\displaystyle 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\eqsim\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}.

Now Corollary
3.6
yields

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|(1+|t|)^{2}g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand,

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|e^{-it\phi(D)}e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

on any interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
|g(t)|\gtrsim 1
for all
t\in I
. It follows that

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\qed

4.2.
Main result

Our main theorem concerns more general constant-coefficient operators than the Euclidean half-wave group
(e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. The generality of our result is restricted by the validity of the following
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
and such that
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)\equiv n-1
. Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
|g(t)|\geq 1
for
t\in[0,1]
, and
\widehat{g}(\tau)=0
for
\tau\notin[-1,1]
. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
C>0
such that

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C2^{k(d(p)+\varepsilon)}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Proof.

First note that

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

and that the Fourier transform (in
n+1
variables) of
g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f(x)
is supported in
\{(\xi,\tau)\mid|\tau-\phi(\xi)|\leq 1\}
. Moreover, the condition
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)\equiv n-1
is equivalent to the condition that the cone
\{(\xi,\phi(\xi))\mid\xi\neq 0\}
has everywhere
n-1
non-vanishing principal curvatures. Since
e^{it\phi(D)}f=\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\chi_{\nu}(D)e^{it\phi(D)}f
, after rescaling to unit frequencies, the proposition follows from the
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality in
[
4
]
, for hypersurfaces with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature, in a similar manner as how the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality for the cone follows from the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality for the paraboloid in
[
3
]
. For more on this see
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
1
]
.
∎

We can now state and prove our main result, of which Theorem
1.1
is a corollary.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
and such that
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)\equiv n-1
. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
C>0
such that

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We may consider the case where
s=0
. We also claim that we may suppose that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\xi|\in[2^{k-1},2^{k+1}]\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and show that

(4.2)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Indeed, suppose that we have proved this, and let a general
f\in\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be given. Let
q^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\kappa>0
be such that
q(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|>\kappa
, and
q^{\prime}(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq\kappa
. For each
t\in(0,1)
, write
f=q^{\prime}(tD)q^{\prime}(D)q(D)f+(1-q)(D)f
. The kernels of
q^{\prime}(D)e^{i\phi(D)}
and
q^{\prime}(D){\langle}D\rangle^{-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon}
are integrable, so

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}q^{\prime}(tD)q^{\prime}(D)q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\|e^{it\phi(D)}q^{\prime}(tD)q^{\prime}(D)q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|q^{\prime}(D)q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\langle}D\rangle^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon}q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand, since
q(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 2
, we can use a standard Littlewood–Paley decomposition to write
(1-q)(D)f=\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D)f
. Here
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(2^{-k+1}\xi)=1
for all
\xi\neq 0
, and
\psi(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\notin[1/2,2]
. The convolution kernels of the
\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D)
form a uniformly bounded subset of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so

\|\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D){\langle}D\rangle^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|{\langle}D\rangle^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for an implicit constant independent of
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
f
(see
[
7
, Theorem 6.1]
). Moreover, for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has

\|\psi(2^{-k+1}D)h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{kr}\|\psi(2^{-k+1}D)h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

as follows from the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and from a standard fact about the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of functions with frequency support in a dyadic annulus.
Hence, using the assumption with
\varepsilon
replaced by
\varepsilon/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}(1-q)(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\|\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon/2,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|\psi(2^{-k+1}D)(1-q)(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This proves the claim, and it remains to establish (
4.2
) whenever
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\xi|\in[2^{k-1},2^{k+1}]\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Let the partition of unity
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}
be as above. Also let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
|g(t)|\geq 1
for
t\in[0,1]
, and
\widehat{g}(\tau)=0
for
\tau\notin[-1,1]
. Then Proposition
4.3
yields

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\|\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}g(t)\chi_{\nu}(D)e^{it\phi(D)}f\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(d(p)+\varepsilon)}\Big(\!\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)g(t)e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

for implicit constants independent of
f
and
k
. By Corollary
4.2
, the final quantity is equivalent to
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, as required.
∎

4.3.
Invariance of estimates

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the invariance of local smoothing estimates under FIOs and related operators. Indeed, under the assumptions of Theorem
4.4
, if
T
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then Theorem
1.1
yields a
C^{\prime}\geq 0
such that

(4.3)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}Tf\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now Proposition
3.3
yields the following result.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
and such that
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)\equiv n-1
. Let
T
be an operator as in Proposition
3.3
, where in (
3
) we suppose instead that
-2+2s(p)\leq s+d(p)+\varepsilon\leq 2
. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
C^{\prime}>0
such that (
4.3
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, by combining (
4.3
) with (
1.4
), one sees that the local smoothing estimates in (
1.2
) are invariant under application of the operators in Corollary
4.5
.

Remark 4.6
.

A variable-coefficient version of (
1.2
) holds on compact manifolds
[
1
]
. This implies in particular that (
1.2
) is invariant under application of FIOs associated with a local canonical graph which have a compactly supported Schwartz kernel, as in Proposition
3.3
(
1
). However, by itself this does not imply the stronger estimate (
4.3
) for such operators. In this article we will not explore variable-coefficient versions of Theorem
4.4
.

5.
Sharpness of the results

In this section we show that the exponent
d(p)-s(p)
in Theorems
1.1
and
4.4
is sharp. We also prove a proposition which deals with the case where
p\in[1,2]\cup\{\infty\}
.

We need two lemmas. The first one relates certain FIOs to translation operators, and will be applied to functions with frequency support in a dyadic-parabolic piece. A similar statement holds for more general FIOs, using bicharacteristic flows on phase space (see
[
11
]
), but we will not need such generality here.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
, and let
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is compact. Let
\nu\in S^{n-1}
and set

\kappa(\phi,h,\nu):=\sup\{|(\partial_{\xi}\phi(\hat{\xi})-\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\cdot\xi|\mid\xi\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\}.

Then

|e^{it\phi(D)}h(x)-h(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))|\leq\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\kappa(\phi,h,\nu)|t|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\kappa(\phi,h,\nu)|t|\leq 1
.

Proof.

We use the homogeneity of
\phi
to write

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{n}\big(e^{it\phi(D)}h(x)-h(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}(e^{it\phi(\xi)}-e^{it\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu)\cdot\xi})\widehat{h}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{it\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu)\cdot\xi}(e^{it(\partial_{\xi}\phi(\hat{\xi})-\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\cdot\xi}-1)\widehat{h}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

Now simply note that, in the last line, the term in brackets is bounded in absolute value by
|t|\kappa(\phi,h,\nu)e^{|t|\kappa(\phi,h,\nu)}
.
∎

Next, to prove sharpness for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, we will use the following lemma, which relates the Hardy spaces for FIOs to square functions similar to those in
[
9
]
. Throughout, we use notation as in Section
4
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
, and let
p\in[1,\infty)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
C\geq 0
be such that

(5.1)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then there exists a
C^{\prime}\geq 0
such that

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}|\chi_{\nu}(D)e^{it\phi(D)}f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
(\varepsilon_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}
be an independent sequence of Rademacher random variables, and for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
write

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\varepsilon_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(D)f(x).

Then Proposition
4.1
shows that
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Now one simply combines (
5.1
) with Khintchine’s inequality.
∎

We can now state the main result of this section, the proof of which relies on examples that are typically used to show sharpness of decoupling inequalities (see e.g.
[
9
,
27
]
). These examples were communicated to us by Po–Lam Yung.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
. Let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
C\geq 0
be such that

(5.2)

\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
s\geq d(p)-s(p)
.

Proof.

We want to prove
s\geq d(p)-s(p)=\max(s(p)-\frac{1}{p},0)
. We first show that
s\geq s(p)-\frac{1}{p}
. To this end we apply Lemma
5.1
in the case where the frequency support of the relevant function fills up a complete dyadic-parabolic piece.

More precisely, for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let
(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a collection with the following properties. For each
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
one has
\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{\nu}=f_{\nu}
, and
f_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x|\leq 2^{-k}
. Moreover,
\|\mathcal{F}(f_{\nu})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1
and
\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{2p}}
for implicit constants independent of
\nu
and
k
. To construct such a collection, one may start off with a function
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(x)\geq 1
whenever
|x|\leq 1
, and
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|>c
, for some small
c>0
. Then set

f_{\nu}(x):=e^{i2^{k}\nu\cdot x}\psi(2^{k}\nu\cdot x+2^{k/2}\Pi_{\nu}^{\perp}x)

where
\Pi^{\perp}_{\nu}
is the orthogonal projection onto the complement of the span of
\nu
.

Next, let
f:=\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}f_{\nu}
. Then Proposition
4.1
yields
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(5.3)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})}.

Moreover, Lemma
5.1
and the assumptions on
f_{\nu}
yield

(5.4)

|e^{it\phi(D)}f_{\nu}(x)-f_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))|\lesssim t2^{-k/2}

whenever
0\leq t\lesssim 2^{-k/2}
, for implicit constants independent of
\nu
and
k
. Also,
f_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\geq 1
if
t\leq 2^{-k}
and
|x|\leq c^{\prime}2^{-k}
for some small
c^{\prime}>0
, since
f_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
for
|x|\leq 2^{-k}
and because
\partial_{\xi}\phi
is uniformly bounded on
S^{n-1}
. By combining this with (
5.4
), we obtain

e^{it\phi(D)}f_{\nu}(x)\geq f_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))-|e^{it\phi(D)}f_{\nu}(x)-f_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))|\gtrsim 1

for
|x|\leq c^{\prime}2^{-k}
and
0\leq t\leq c^{\prime\prime}2^{-k}
for some small enough
c^{\prime\prime}>0
. Now (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) yield

\displaystyle 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{p}}2^{k\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}e^{it\phi(D)}f_{\nu}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})},

which implies that
s\geq s(p)-\frac{1}{p}
.

Next, we show that
s\geq 0
. Here we apply Lemma
5.1
to functions with frequency support of unit size in a dyadic-parabolic piece, and we also rely on Lemma
5.2
. Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be as before, and for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
set

g_{\nu}(x):=e^{i2^{k}\nu\cdot x}\psi(x).

Since
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|>c
, for some small
c>0
, we may again assume that
\chi_{\nu}(D)g_{\nu}=g_{\nu}
. Also,
\|g_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1\eqsim\|\mathcal{F}(g_{\nu})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Write
g:=\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}g_{\nu}
. Then
g\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

(5.5)

\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|g_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})},

by Proposition
4.1
. Moreover, since
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{F}g_{\nu})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\nu})
is of unit size, Lemma
5.1
yields

\big|e^{it\phi(D)}g_{\nu}(x)-g_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\big|\lesssim t

whenever
0\leq t\leq\kappa
, for some small
\kappa>0
independent of
k
and
\nu
. As before, one also has
g_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\geq 1
whenever
|x|\lesssim 1
and
0\leq t\lesssim 1
are small enough. Hence

e^{it\phi(D)}g_{\nu}(x)\geq g_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))-\big|e^{it\phi(D)}g_{\nu}(x)-g_{\nu}(x+t\partial_{\xi}\phi(\nu))\big|\gtrsim 1

for
|x|\leq\kappa^{\prime}
and
0\leq t\leq\kappa^{\prime\prime}
, for small enough
\kappa^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime\prime}>0
. Now combine this with (
5.2
), Lemma
5.2
and (
5.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle 2^{k\frac{n-1}{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}|e^{it\phi(D)}g_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}|\chi_{\nu}(D)e^{it\phi(D)}g(x)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})},

which implies that
s\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Recall that Theorem
4.4
relied on the
\ell^{p}
-decoupling inequality from
[
4
]
, for the hypersurface associated with
(e^{it\phi(D)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, in Proposition
4.3
. As a byproduct, Theorem
5.3
shows that each of these decoupling inequalities is sharp.

Remark 5.5
.

To prove the inequality
s\geq s(p)-\frac{1}{p}
in Theorem
5.3
, i.e. deal with the case where
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, we cannot simply rely on the fact that the local smoothing conjecture is sharp. Indeed, while such an approach suffices for
(e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, or more generally under the conditions of Theorem
4.4
, Theorem
5.3
does not make any assumptions on the rank of
\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi
, and the
L^{p}
-mapping properties of
e^{it\phi(D)}
improve when
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)
is small (see
[
22
]
). See also Remark
5.7
.

For completeness we also include the following proposition, for
p\notin(2,\infty)
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
. Let
p\in[1,2]\cup\{\infty\}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

One has
[t\mapsto e^{it\phi(D)}f]\in L^{p}([0,1];\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(2)

s\geq s(p)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2
and the Sobolev embeddings in (
1.4
), one has

(5.6)

[t\mapsto e^{it\phi(D)}]\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq L^{p}([0,1];\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which proves the implication (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
).

For
p=\infty
, the converse implication follows directly from the first part of the proof of Theorem
5.3
, up to a change of notation. For
p\leq 2
, let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1},|\hat{\xi}-\omega|\leq 2^{-k/2+1}\}

for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
\omega\in S^{n-1}
. Then Corollary
4.2
and Lemma
2.2
yield

2^{k(s(p)-s)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s(p)-s)}\|e^{i\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for any
t\in[0,1]
. Now integrate this in time and apply (
1
) to obtain

2^{k(s(p)-s)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|e^{it\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

so that
s\geq s(p)
.
∎

As in Remark
5.5
, Proposition
5.6
does not follow in full generality from the fact that no local smoothing can occur for
p\notin(2,\infty)
.

Remark 5.7
.

We briefly summarize what we have shown. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be positively homogeneous of degree
1
. By (
5.6
), one has

(5.7)

[t\mapsto e^{it\phi(D)}f]\in L^{p}([0,1];\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

whenever
s\geq s(p)
. Because
(e^{it\phi(D)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group, there can be no improvement of the index in the first inclusion in (
5.6
), not even at a single time. Moreover, the condition
s\geq s(p)
is also necessary for (
5.7
) in some situations, for example when
p\in[1,2]\cup\{\infty\}
or when
\phi\equiv 0
, by Proposition
5.6
and because the Sobolev embeddings in (
1.4
) are sharp. On the other hand, for
p\in(2,\infty)
, the condition
s\geq d(p)-s(p)
is necessary for any
\phi
, while
s>d(p)-s(p)
suffices when
\mathrm{rank}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi)
is maximal everywhere.

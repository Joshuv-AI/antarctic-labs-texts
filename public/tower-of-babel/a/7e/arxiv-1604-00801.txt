2
Preliminaries:

In this section we give some definitions and functional settings. At the end of this section, we state our main results. For this we define
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, the usual fractional Sobolev
space
W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in L^{p}(\Omega);\frac{(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}\in L^{p}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\}
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}}+\left(\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.1)

To study fractional Sobolev space in details we refer
[
30
]
.

Due to the non-localness of the operator, we define linear space as follows:

X=\left\{w|\;w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\text{is measurable},w|_{\Omega}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\;and\;\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{n+ps}{p}}}\in L^{p}(Q)\right\}

where
Q=\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\mathcal{C}\Omega)
and

\mathcal{C}\Omega:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. In case of
p=2
, the space
X
was firstly introduced by Servadei and Valdinoci
[
31
]
. The space X is a normed linear space endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|w\|_{X}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.2)

Then we define

X_{0}=\{w\in X:w=0\;\text{a.e. in}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}

with the norm

\displaystyle\|w\|=\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(2.3)

is a reflexive Banach space.
We notice that, the norms in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are not same because
\Omega\times\Omega
is strictly contained in
Q
. Now we define the space

C_{X_{0}}:=\{w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):w=0\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

Then
C_{X_{0}}
is a dense in the space
X_{0}
.

Define
S:=\inf_{w\in X_{0}}\left\{\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}|x-y|^{-(n+ps)}dxdy}{(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{p^{*}_{s}}dx)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{s}}}}\right\}
.

Definition 2.1

A weak solution of the problem
(P_{\lambda})
is a function
w\in X_{0}
,
w>0
in
\Omega
such that for every
v\in X_{0}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p-2}(w(x)-w(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{(n+ps)}}dxdy=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)(w^{-q}v)(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)(w^{r}v)(x)dx.

In order to present the existence of positive solution of
(P_{\lambda})
, we will consider the following problem

(P_{\lambda}^{+})\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|w(y)-w(x)|^{p-2}(w(y)-w(x))}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dy=a(x)w_{+}^{-q}+\lambda b(x)w_{+}^{r}\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\quad\quad w>0\;\text{in}\;\Omega,\quad w=0\;\mbox{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\\
\end{array}\quad\right.

where
w_{+}:=\max\{w,0\}
, denote the positive part of
w
.
Then the function
w\in X_{0}
,
w>0
in
\Omega
is a weak solution of the problem
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
if for every
v\in X_{0}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p-2}(w(x)-w(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{(n+ps)}}dxdy=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)(w_{+}^{-q}v)(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)(w_{+}^{r}v)(x)dx.

We note that if
w>0
is a solution of
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
then one can easily see that
w
is also a solution
(P_{\lambda})
. To find the solution of
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
, we will use variational approach. So we define the associated functional
J_{\lambda}:X_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{[}-\infty,\infty)
as

J_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{Q}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy-\frac{1}{1-q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx-\frac{\lambda}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx.

Now for
w\in X_{0}
, we define the fiber map
\phi_{w}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\phi_{w}(t)

\displaystyle=J_{\lambda}(tw)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}\|w\|^{p}-\frac{t^{1-q}}{1-q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx-\frac{\lambda t^{r+1}}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx.

Also

\displaystyle\phi_{w}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{p-1}\|w\|^{p}-t^{-q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx-\lambda t^{r}\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx,

\displaystyle\phi_{w}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(p-1)t^{p-2}\|w\|^{p}+qt^{-q-1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx-r\lambda t^{r-1}\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx.

It is easy to see that the energy functional
J_{\lambda}
is not
bounded below on the space
X_{0}
. But we will show that it is bounded below
on an appropriate subset of
X_{0}
and a minimizer on
subsets of this set gives rise to solutions of
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
. In
order to obtain the existence results, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:

\displaystyle=\{w\in X_{0}:\phi_{w}^{\prime}(t)=\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(w),w\rangle=0\}

\displaystyle=\left\{w\in X_{0}:\|w\|^{p}=\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx\right\}.

Note that
w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
if
w
is a solution of problem
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
. Also one can easily see that
tw\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
if and only if

\phi_{w}^{\prime}(t)=0
. In order to obtain our result, we decompose
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
with
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{0}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\left\{w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:\phi_{w}^{\prime\prime}(1)\gtrless 0\right\}=\left\{w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:(p-1+q)\|w\|^{p}\gtrless\lambda(r+q)\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:\phi_{w}^{\prime\prime}(1)=0\right\}=\left\{w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:(p-1+q)\|w\|^{p}=\lambda(r+q)\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx\right\}.

Our results are as follows:

Inspired by
[
9
]
, we show that how variational methods can be used to established some existence and multiplicity results for
(P_{\lambda}^{+})
:

Theorem 2.2

Suppose that
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, where

\Lambda:=\frac{(p-1+q)}{(r+q)}\left(\frac{r-p+1}{r+q}\right)^{\frac{r-p+1}{p-1+q}}\frac{1}{\|b\|}\left(\frac{S^{r+q}}{\|a\|^{r-p+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1+q}}

then the problem
(P_{\lambda})
has at least two solutions
w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}
,
W\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
with
\|W\|>\|w\|
.

Next, we obtain the blow up behavior of the solution
W_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
of problem
(P_{\lambda})
with
r=p-1+\epsilon
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Theorem 2.3

let
W_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
be the solution of problem
(P_{\lambda})
with
r=p-1+\epsilon
, where
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, then

\displaystyle\|W_{\epsilon}\|>C_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}},

where

C_{\epsilon}=\left(1+\frac{p-1+q}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1+q}}\|a\|^{\frac{1}{p-1+q}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{S}}\right)^{\frac{1-q}{p-1+q}}\rightarrow\infty\;\mbox{as}\;\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Namely,
W_{\epsilon}
blows up faster than exponentially with respect to
\epsilon
.

Remark:
If
w
is a positive solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|w(y)-w(x)|^{p-2}(w(y)-w(x))}{|y|^{n+ps}}dy=a(x)w^{-q}+\lambda b(x)w^{r}\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\quad\quad w>0\;\text{in}\;\Omega,\quad w=0\;\mbox{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\\
\end{array}\quad\right.

then one can easily see that
u=\lambda^{\frac{1}{r-1+p}}w
is a positive solution of the following problem

(Q_{\lambda})\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))}{|y|^{n+ps}}dy=\lambda^{\frac{p-1+q}{r-p+1}}a(x)u^{-q}+b(x)u^{r}\;\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\quad\quad u>0\;\text{in}\;\Omega,\quad u=0\;\mbox{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\\
\end{array}\quad\right.

That is, the problem
(Q_{\lambda})
has two positive solutions for
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
.

3
Fibering map analysis

In this section, we show that
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{\pm}
is nonempty and
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{0}=\{0\}
. Moreover,
J_{\lambda}
is bounded below and coercive.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
. Then for each
w\in X_{0}
with
\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx>0
, we have the following:

(i)

\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx\leq 0
, then there exists a unique
0<t_{1}<t_{max}
such that
t_{1}w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}
and
J_{\lambda}(t_{1}w)=\displaystyle\inf_{t>0}J_{\lambda}(tw)
,

(ii)

\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx>0
, then there exists a unique
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
with
0<t_{1}<t_{max}<t_{2}
such that
t_{1}w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}
,
t_{2}w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
and

J_{\lambda}(t_{1}w)=\displaystyle\inf_{0\leq t\leq t_{max}}J_{\lambda}(tw)
,
J_{\lambda}(t_{2}w)=\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{1}}J_{\lambda}(tw)
.

Proof.

For
t>0
, we define

\displaystyle\psi_{w}(t)=t^{p-1-r}\|w\|^{p}-t^{-r-q}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx.

One can easily see that
\psi_{w}(t)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Now

\displaystyle\psi_{w}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(p-1-r)t^{p-2-r}\|w\|^{p}+(r+q)t^{-r-q-1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx.

\displaystyle\psi_{w}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(p-1-r)(p-2-r)t^{p-r-3}\|w\|^{p}-(r+q)(r+q+1)t^{-r-q-2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx.

Then
\psi_{w}^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if
t=t_{max}:=\left[\frac{(r-p+1)\|w\|^{p}}{(r+q)\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx}\right]^{-\frac{1}{p-1+q}}
. Also

\displaystyle\psi_{w}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle(t_{max})=(p-1-r)(p-2-r)\left[\frac{(r-p+1)\|w\|^{p}}{(r+q)\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx}\right]^{\frac{r-p+3}{p-1+q}}\|w\|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-(r+q)(r+q+1)\left[\frac{(r-p+1)\|w\|^{p}}{(r+q)\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx}\right]^{\frac{r+q+2}{p-1+q}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\|w\|^{p}(r-p+1)(p-1+q)\left[\frac{(r-p+1)\|w\|^{p}}{(r+q)\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx}\right]^{\frac{r-p+3}{p-1+q}}<0.

Thus
\psi_{w}
achieves its maximum at
t=t_{max}
. Now using the Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}|a(x)|^{\frac{p^{*}_{s}}{p_{s}^{*}-1+q}}\right]^{\frac{p^{*}_{s}+q-1}{p^{*}_{s}}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\right]^{\frac{1-q}{p^{*}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|\left(\frac{\|w\|}{\sqrt[p]{S}}\right)^{1-q}.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}|b(x)|^{\frac{p^{*}_{s}}{p_{s}^{*}-1-r}}\right]^{\frac{p^{*}_{s}-r-1}{p^{*}_{s}}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\right]^{\frac{r+1}{p^{*}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b\|\left(\frac{\|w\|}{\sqrt[p]{S}}\right)^{r+1}.

(3.2)

Using (
3
) and (
3
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\psi_{w}(t_{max})

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1+q)}{(r+q)}\left(\frac{r-p+1}{r+q}\right)^{\frac{r-p+1}{p-1+q}}\frac{\|w\|^{\frac{p(r+q)}{(p-1+q)}}}{[\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{+}^{1-q}(x)dx]^{\frac{r-p+1}{p-1+q}}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{(p-1+q)}{(r+q)}\left(\frac{r-p+1}{r+q}\right)^{\frac{r-p+1}{p-1+q}}\left(\frac{(\sqrt[p]{S})^{(1-q)}}{\|a\|}\right)^{\frac{(r-p+1)}{(p-1+q)}}-\lambda\|b\|\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt[p]{S}}\right)^{r+1}\right]\|w\|^{r+1}

\displaystyle\equiv E_{\lambda}\|w\|^{r+1}.

(i)

\displaystyle\psi_{w}(t)=0

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{w}(t)>0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(p-1+q)\|tw\|^{p}-\lambda(r+q)\int_{\Omega}b(x)(tw)_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx>0

Similarly one can show that if
\psi_{w}(t)=0
and

.

Now
\phi_{w}^{\prime}(t)=t^{r}\psi_{w}(t)

. Moreover
t_{1}w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{+}
and
t_{2}w\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{-}
.

(ii)
If
\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx<0
and
\psi_{w}(t)\rightarrow-\lambda\int_{\Omega}b(x)w_{+}^{r+1}(x)dx>0

\psi_{w}(t)

Lemma 3.4

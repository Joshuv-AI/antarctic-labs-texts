Geometric Transformations on Null Curves in the Anti-de Sitter 3-Space
Emilio MUSSO a and Álvaro PÁMPANO b

Abstract
We provide a geometric transformation on null curves in the anti-de Sitter 3 3 -space (AdS) which induces the Bäcklund transformation for the KdV equation. In addition, we show that this geometric transformation satisfies a suitable permutability theorem. We also illustrate how to implement it when the original null curve has constant bending.

Introduction

In a previous paper
[
8
]
, we showed that, in the context of Lorentzian geometry on the anti-de Sitter
3
-space (
{\rm AdS}
, for short) there are flows (referred to as LIEN flows) on null curves that induce bending evolution by any partial differential equation (PDE) in the KdV hierarchy. In particular, the first order LIEN flow induces evolution by the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation in the form

\partial_{t}\kappa+\partial_{s}^{3}\kappa-6\kappa\partial_{s}\kappa=0.

Although similar flows exist on other Lorentzian space forms (see, for instance,
[
4
,
6
]
), the specific case of
{\rm AdS}
is special in the sense that the theory of null curves in
{\rm AdS}
is closely related to the theory of star-shaped curves in the centro-affine plane. Indeed, null curves in
{\rm AdS}
can be analyzed by combining together pairs of star-shaped curves in the centro-affine plane
[
8
]
.

The KdV equation is a classical PDE that originated as a model to understand the propagation of waves on shallow water surfaces
[
5
]
and is a prototype of completely integrable evolution equations. As a completely integrable PDE the KdV equation has a rich structure including, not only infinitely many conservation laws, but also a Bäcklund transformation
[
11
]
which generates new solutions from old. In this paper, we use Tabachnikov’s transformation on star-shaped curves
[
10
]
and a similar transformation of Terng–Wu
[
12
]
to define a geometric transformation (referred to as the
\mathcal{T}
-transform
) on null curves in
{\rm AdS}
that corresponds to the Bäcklund transformation for their bendings.

We begin, in Section
2
, by recalling the basic properties of null curves
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
without inflection points in the
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
model for
{\rm AdS}
. Such curves possess canonical parameterizations and a third order differential invariant
\kappa
, the
bending
(often known as curvature or torsion). In addition, they also have a
spinor frame field

(F_{+},F_{-})\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
defined along them. The components
F_{\pm}
of the spinor frame field along
\gamma
are, precisely, the canonical central affine frame fields of two star-shaped curves
\eta_{\pm}
in the centro-affine plane
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
with central affine curvatures
{\rm k}_{\pm}=\kappa\pm 1
, respectively
[
8
]
.

In Section
3
, we introduce the
\mathcal{T}
-transform on null curves in
{\rm AdS}
. We show, in Theorem
3.8
, how to build such a transformation using a solution of the Riccati equation

f^{\prime}+f^{2}=\kappa+\cosh(2\xi),

involving the bending
\kappa
of the null curve and a real parameter
\xi\neq 0
. We also prove that the
\mathcal{T}
-transform satisfies a suitable permutability theorem (Theorem
3.10
).

Next, in Section
4
, after briefly recalling some basic facts about the Bäcklund transformation for the KdV equation
[
11
,
13
]
, we use the
\mathcal{T}
-transform to find, in Theorem
4.4
, a geometric realization of it. In addition, such a transformation satisfies a permutability theorem as well. This geometric realization consists on applying the
\mathcal{T}
-transform to a solution of the LIEN flow
[
8
]
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we illustrate how to implement the
\mathcal{T}
-transform when the original null curve has constant bending. A detailed analysis of null curves in
{\rm AdS}
with constant bending has been carried out in
[
8
]
.

The results of this paper naturally suggest further directions for research. In particular, it is likely that these results (as well as those of
[
8
]
) can be reformulated in the context of holomorphic null curves in
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. Since the seminal work of R. Bryant
[
2
]
, it is known that the geometry of holomorphic null curves
[
3
,
7
]
in
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is related via the Bryant’s correspondence to the geometry of immersed surfaces with constant mean curvature one (CMC
1
, for short) in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. In this case, it is expected that the
\mathcal{T}
-transform corresponds to the Darboux transform for CMC
1
surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. It is also reasonable to ask if the stationary solutions of the holomorphic LIEN flow or its finite-gap solutions can be used to find new embedded CMC
1
surfaces with a finite number of ends, in the spirit of
[
1
]
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we will collect the basic information about the
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
model for the anti-de Sitter
3
-space (
{\rm AdS}
) and about the geometric features of null curves in
{\rm AdS}
. For a more detailed analysis, we refer the reader to
[
8
, Sections 2 and 3]
.

2.1
The anti-de Sitter 3-space

Consider the vector space of
2\times 2
real matrices
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
equipped with the quadratic form
{\rm q}
of signature
(-,-,+,+)
defined by

{\rm q}(X)=-\det(X)=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{1}-x_{1}^{1}x_{2}^{2},

for each
X=\bigl(x_{i}^{j}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. The corresponding inner product will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and we will consider the orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
determined by the volume form
\Omega={\rm d}x_{1}^{1}\wedge{\rm d}x_{2}^{2}\wedge{\rm d}x_{1}^{2}\wedge{\rm d}x_{2}^{1}
. On
\Lambda^{2}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\bigr)
we define an inner product
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle
by

\langle\langle U\wedge V,W\wedge Z\rangle\rangle=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle U,W\rangle&\langle U,Z\rangle\\
\langle V,W\rangle&\langle V,Z\rangle\end{pmatrix}.

A bivector
U\wedge V\in\Lambda^{2}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\bigr)
is of type
(-,-)
if the restriction of the above inner product to
{\rm span}\{U\wedge V\}
is negative definite, and of type
(-,0)
if this restriction is nonzero semi-negative definite and degenerate. We fix the bivector of type
(-,-)

U\wedge V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\wedge\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\Lambda^{2}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\bigr),

and we say that a bivector
W\wedge Z
of type
(-,0)
is
positive
if
\langle\langle U\wedge V,W\wedge Z\rangle\rangle>0
. The set of all positive bivectors of type
(-,0)
is denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{+}
.
This choice defines the notion of time orientation in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
.

Let
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
be the
special linear group
of degree
2
over
\mathbb{R}
(i.e.,
the group consisting of the
{2\times 2}
real matrices of determinant
1
with the ordinary matrix multiplication). The restriction of the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
of
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
to the special linear group
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
gives a Lorentzian metric of constant sectional curvature
-1
. The special linear group
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
endowed with the Lorentzian metric induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a model for the
anti-de Sitter
3
-space
(
{\rm AdS}
).

Remark 2.1
.

Throughout this paper, it will be implicitly assumed that the model for
{\rm AdS}
is the special linear group
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. For visualization purposes, we will identify
{\rm AdS}
with the open solid torus in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
swept out by the rotation of the (open) unit disc of the
Oxz
-plane centered at
(2,0,0)
around the
Oz
-axis.

Consider the normal vector field
Q\colon X\in{\rm AdS}\longmapsto-2X\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and the orientation in
{\rm AdS}
determined by the volume form
i_{Q}\Omega
. Given a null tangent vector
T\in T_{X}{\rm AdS}
, the bivector
{X\wedge T}
is of type
(-,0)
. We define a time-orientation on
{\rm AdS}
by declaring
T
to be future-directed if
{X\wedge T\in\mathcal{N}_{+}}
.

2.2
Geometry of null curves

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval. A smooth immersed curve
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
is
null
if its velocity vector
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
is a null (or, light-like) vector for each
s\in J
. In other words, if
\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle=0
for all
s\in J
. A null curve is
future-directed
if the bivector
\gamma\wedge\gamma^{\prime}
of type
(-,0)
is positive, that is, if
\gamma\wedge\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{+}
.

Let
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
be a future-directed null curve without inflection points (i.e., such that
\gamma^{\prime}\wedge\gamma^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
holds). Since
\gamma^{\prime}\wedge\gamma^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
, then
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
is a space-like vector. Using the terminology of
[
8
]
, we say that
\gamma
is parameterized by the
proper time
if
\langle\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s)\rangle=4
for every
s\in J
. The
bending
of a curve
\gamma
parameterized by the proper time is defined by

\kappa(s)=-\frac{1}{16}\bigl\langle\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\bigr\rangle.

Remark 2.2
.

From now on, we assume that all our curves
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
are null, future-directed, parameterized by their proper time
s\in J
, and have no inflection points. In this setting, the bending
\kappa(s)
completely determines the null curve
\gamma
, up to congruence.

Let
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
be a null curve with bending
\kappa
. The
spinor frame field
along
\gamma
is a lift
(F_{+},F_{-})\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle{\rm d}F_{+}=F_{+}\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa+1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}{\rm d}s,

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}F_{-}=F_{-}\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}{\rm d}s.

(2.2)

The spinor frame field is defined up to a sign. The equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are the spinorial counterpart of the classical Frenet-type equations and, hence, we will refer to them as the
spinorial Frenet-type equations
of
\gamma
. Observe that the null curve
\gamma
can be recovered from the spinor frame field
(F_{+},F_{-})
as

\gamma=F_{+}F_{-}^{-1}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The frame
(F_{+},F_{-})
can be explicitly obtained as a lifting to
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
of a moving frame adapted to the curve
\gamma
, with values in the automorphism group of
{\rm AdS}
. For the details of the construction, we refer to
[
8
]
.

In
[
8
]
, employing the spinor frame field
(F_{+},F_{-})
along
\gamma
, we related null curves in
{\rm AdS}
to a suitable pair of star-shaped curves in the centro-affine plane
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. For later use, we state this result here.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
\gamma\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
be a null curve with bending
\kappa
and spinor frame field
(F_{+},F_{-})
along it
(
which can be obtained by solving the spinorial Frenet-type equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)
)
. Then, the first column vectors of
F_{\pm}
form a pair of star-shaped curves
{(\eta_{+},\eta_{-})}
in
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with central affine curvatures
1
1

1

Our notion of central affine curvature coincides with that of Terng–Wu
[
12
]
and it has the opposite sign of that of Pinkall
[
9
]
.

{\rm k}_{+}=\kappa+1
,
{\rm k}_{-}=\kappa-1
, respectively.

Conversely, let
(\eta_{+},\eta_{-})
be a pair of star-shaped curves with central affine curvatures
{\rm k}_{+}
and
{\rm k}_{-}
related by
({\rm k}_{+}-{\rm k}_{-})/2=1
, and canonical central affine frame fields
F_{+}=\bigl(\eta_{+},\eta_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)
and
{F_{-}=\bigl(\eta_{-},\eta_{-}^{\prime}\bigr)}
, respectively. Then,
\gamma=F_{+}F_{-}^{-1}
,
is a null curve in
{\rm AdS}
with bending
\kappa=({\rm k}_{+}+{\rm k}_{-})/2
and spinor frame field
(F_{+},F_{-})
along it.

Remark 2.5
.

Following the terminology introduced in
[
8
]
, the pair of star-shaped curves
{(\eta_{+},\eta_{-})}
will be referred to as the pair of cousins associated with the null curve
\gamma
.

We briefly recall here the case of closed null curves with constant bending (for more details, see
[
8
, Example 3.5]
).

Example 2.6
.

Consider a closed null curve
\gamma\colon J=\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
with constant bending
\kappa
. Since

along

1.

If
m+n
is even, the curve
\gamma
represents a torus knot of type
((m-n)/2,(n+m)/2)

2.

If
m+n
is odd, the curve
\gamma
represents a torus knot of type
(m-n,n+m)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\gamma,\widetilde{\gamma}\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow{\rm AdS}
be two null curves with associated pairs of cousins
(\eta_{+},\eta_{-})
and
(\widetilde{\eta}_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{-})
, respectively. If
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
are
\mathcal{T}
-transforms of each other, then

\det\bigl(\eta_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+}\bigr)\det\bigl(\eta_{-},\widetilde{\eta}_{-}^{\prime}\bigr)-\det\bigl(\eta_{-},\widetilde{\eta}_{-}\bigr)\det\bigl(\eta_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)=\chi

(3.1)

holds for some constant
\chi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
be two null curves that are
\mathcal{T}
-transforms of each other. It then follows from Definition
3.3
that
\eta_{+}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-transform of
\widetilde{\eta}_{+}
and
\eta_{-}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-transform of
\widetilde{\eta}_{-}
. Moreover, Definition
3.1
then guarantees the existence of two nonzero constants
c_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
{\det(\eta_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+})=c_{+}}
and
\det(\eta_{-},\widetilde{\eta}_{-})=c_{-}
, respectively.

Then there exist smooth functions
f_{\pm}\colon J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that, respectively,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{+}=\frac{1}{c_{+}}\bigl(\eta_{+}^{\prime}-f_{+}\eta_{+}\bigr),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{-}=\frac{1}{c_{-}}\bigl(\eta_{-}^{\prime}-f_{-}\eta_{-}\bigr).

(3.3)

From now on, we only work with (
3.2
) and the sub-index
+
. The argument for (
3.3
) and the sub-index
-
is analogous.

Differentiating (
3.2
) and using the spinorial Frenet-type equations (
2.1
) (respectively, (
2.2
) when the sub-index is
-
) of
\gamma
, we obtain

\widetilde{\eta}_{+}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{c_{+}}\bigl(\bigl[\kappa+1-f_{+}^{\prime}\bigr]\eta_{+}-f_{+}\eta_{+}^{\prime}\bigr),

(3.4)

where
\kappa
is the bending of
\gamma
. Since
\eta_{+}
and
\widetilde{\eta}_{+}
are two star-shaped curves parameterized by the central affine arc length,
\det\bigl(\eta_{+},\eta_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)=1
and
\det\bigl(\widetilde{\eta}_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)=1
. Thus, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle 1=\det\bigl(\widetilde{\eta}_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)=\frac{1}{c_{+}^{2}}\det\bigl(\eta_{+}^{\prime}-f_{+}\eta_{+},\bigl[\kappa+1-f_{+}^{\prime}\bigr]\eta_{+}-f_{+}\eta_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)=\frac{1}{c_{+}^{2}}\bigl(f_{+}^{\prime}-\kappa-1+f_{+}^{2}\bigr).

Therefore, the function
f_{+}
is a solution of the Riccati equation

f_{+}^{\prime}+f_{+}^{2}=\kappa+1+c_{+}^{2}.

(3.5)

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain that the canonical central affine frame field
\widetilde{F}_{+}
along
\widetilde{\eta}_{+}
is given in terms of the canonical central affine frame field
F_{+}
along
\eta_{+}
by

\widetilde{F}_{+}=\frac{1}{c_{+}}F_{+}\begin{pmatrix}-f_{+}&f_{+}^{2}-c_{+}^{2}\\
1&-f_{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.6)

Since it will be used later, we also specify here the expression for the canonical central affine frame field
\widetilde{F}_{-}
along
\widetilde{\eta}_{-}
in terms of
F_{-}
, namely,

\widetilde{F}_{-}=\frac{1}{c_{-}}F_{-}\begin{pmatrix}-f_{-}&f_{-}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}\\
1&-f_{-}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.7)

From the above relation (
3.6
) between
\widetilde{F}_{+}
and
F_{+}
(respectively, (
3.7
)), the spinorial Frenet-type equations (
2.1
) (respectively, (
2.2
)) and the Riccati equation (

) satisfied by the function
f_{+}
(respectively, the analogue one for
f_{-}
), we conclude with

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{+}^{-1}{\rm d}\widetilde{F}_{+}

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{c_{+}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-f_{+}&c_{+}^{2}-f_{+}^{2}\\
-1&-f_{+}\end{pmatrix}\biggl[F_{+}^{-1}{\rm d}F_{+}\begin{pmatrix}-f_{+}&f_{+}^{2}-c_{+}^{2}\\

The analogue computations for the sub-index

We now distinguish between three cases:

Assume first that

and the analogue one for

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\pm\sqrt{2+c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}}\sinh(\phi)\phi^{\prime}+\bigl(2+c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}\bigr)\cosh^{2}(\phi)=\kappa+1+c_{+}^{2},\\
&\sqrt{2+c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}}\cosh(\phi)\phi^{\prime}+\bigl(2+c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}\bigr)\sinh^{2}(\phi)=\kappa-1+c_{-}^{2}.\end{split}

Subtracting both equations, we then see that

are also constant functions and, in addition,

Finally, we use (

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle{}=\det(\eta_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+})\det\bigl(\eta_{-},\widetilde{\eta}_{-}^{\prime}\bigr)-\det(\eta_{-},\widetilde{\eta}_{-})\det\bigl(\eta_{+},\widetilde{\eta}_{+}^{\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{c_{+}}\biggl(\frac{-f_{-}}{c_{-}}\biggr)-\frac{1}{c_{-}}\biggl(\frac{-f_{+}}{c_{+}}\biggr)=\frac{f_{+}-f_{-}}{c_{+}c_{-}},

which is a nonzero constant.

The case

Assume finally that

holds. From the Riccati equations ((

) and the analogue one) satisfied by

and

Thus, integrating this we obtain,

-transform on null curves to show a geometric realization of the Bäcklund transformation for the KdV equation. We first briefly recall some basic facts about this Bäcklund transformation.

4.1
Bäcklund transformation for the KdV

The Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation is the PDE given by

\partial_{t}\kappa+\partial_{s}^{3}\kappa-6\kappa\partial_{s}\kappa=0.

(4.1)

Let
J,I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be two open intervals (for convenience, we assume
0\in J,I
) and consider a solution
\kappa\colon(s,t)\in J\times I\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\longmapsto\kappa(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}
of the KdV equation (
4.1
). The
Bäcklund transform
of
\kappa
with spectral parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and transforming function
f\colon J\times I\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the function defined by

\widetilde{\kappa}=-\kappa+2f^{2}-2\lambda.

(4.2)

In
[
14
]
, Wahlquist and Estabrook showed that if the transforming function
f(s,t)
is a solution of the so-called Wahlquist–Estabrook equation

{\rm d}f=\bigl(\kappa-f^{2}+\lambda\bigr){\rm d}s+\bigl(4\lambda\bigl[f^{2}-\lambda\bigr]-2\bigl[f^{2}+\lambda\bigr]+2\kappa^{2}+2f\partial_{s}\kappa-\partial_{s}^{2}\kappa\bigr){\rm d}t,

(4.3)

then the Bäcklund transform (
4.2
) of
\kappa
with spectral parameter
\lambda\neq 0
and transforming function
f
is another solution of the KdV equation (
4.1
).

The Wahlquist–Estabrook equation (
4.3
) is an overdetermined system whose compatibility equation is the KdV equation (
4.1
). As a consequence, this system is locally solvable, in the sense that for every
(s_{o},t_{o})\in J\times I\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and every constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
there locally exists a unique solution of (
4.3
) with initial condition
f(s_{o},t_{o})=c
.

We will next describe a procedure to construct this solution. This method will employ the extended frames of solutions of the KdV equation
[
11
]
.

For a function
\kappa\colon J\times I\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
-valued
1
-form

\Gamma_{\lambda}=\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\,{\rm d}s+\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\,{\rm d}t,

(4.4)

where
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
are given by

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa+\lambda\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{s}\kappa&-\partial_{s}^{2}\kappa+2\kappa^{2}-2\lambda\kappa-4\lambda^{2}\\
2\kappa-4\lambda&\partial_{s}\kappa\end{pmatrix}.

(4.5)

The
1
-form
\Gamma_{\lambda}
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan compatibility equation if and only if the function
\kappa(s,t)
satisfies the KdV equation (
4.1
). Consequently, as shown in
[
15
]
, for a given solution
\kappa
of (

) and every

there exists a map

(4.6)

The maps
E_{\lambda}
depend in a real analytic fashion on
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The map
E_{\lambda}
is called an
extended frame
of
\kappa
with spectral parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

[
11
]
.
4
4

4

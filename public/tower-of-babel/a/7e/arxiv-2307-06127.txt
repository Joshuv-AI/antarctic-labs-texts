Stationary state of harmonic chains driven by boundary resetting
Ritwick Sarkar, Pritam Roy

Abstract
We study the nonequilibrium steady state (NESS) of an ordered harmonic chain of N N oscillators connected to two walls which undergo diffusive motion with stochastic resetting. The intermittent resetting of the walls effectively emulates two nonequilibrium reservoirs that exert temporally correlated forces on the boundary oscillators. These reservoirs are characterized by the diffusion constants and resetting rates of the walls. We find that, for any finite N N , the velocity distribution of the bulk oscillators remains non-Gaussian, as evidenced by a non-zero bulk kurtosis that decays ∼ N − 1 \sim N^{-1} . We calculate the spatio-temporal correlation of the velocity of the oscillators ⟨ v l ​ ( t ) ​ v l ′ ​ ( t ′ ) ⟩ \langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle both analytically as well as using numerical simulation. The signature of the boundary resetting is present at the bulk in terms of the two-time velocity correlation of a single oscillator and the equal-time spatial velocity correlation. For the resetting driven chain, the two-time velocity correlation decay as t − 1 2 t^{-\frac{1}{2}} at the large time, and there exists a non-zero equal-time spatial velocity correlation ⟨ v l ​ ( t ) ​ v l ′ ​ ( t ′ ) ⟩ \langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle when l ≠ l ′ l\neq l^{\prime} . A non-zero average energy current will flow through the system when the boundary walls reset to their initial positions at different rates. This average energy current can be computed exactly in the thermodynamic limit. Numerically we show that the distribution of the instantaneous energy current at the boundary is independent of the system size. However, the distribution of the instantaneous energy current in the bulk approaches a stationary distribution in the thermodynamic limit.

In this paper, we introduce a simple model of a nonequilibrium reservoir using stochastic resetting dynamics. The action of tnonequilibrium reservoir is realized in terms of the wall that performs Brownian motion and stochastically resets to its initial position at a specific rate. When the boundary oscillators of a one-dimensional harmonic chain are attached to two of these reservoirs, the chain attains NESS with an average nonzero energy current flowing through the system due to the action of these reservoirs. Model and Results

Figure 1:
Schematic figure of a linear chain of oscillators connected to two walls that perform free diffusion and reset to the initial position with different rates. The boundary oscillators are also attached to two Langevin baths of temperature

We consider a chain of
N
oscillators, each of mass
m
, coupled to its nearest neighbors by springs of stiffness constant
k
. Two boundary oscillators are in contact with Langevin heat baths at temperatures
T_{1}
and
T_{N}
. These heat baths exert white noises as well as damping forces, proportional to the velocities, on the boundary oscillators. The boundary oscillators are also attached to two walls with springs of the same stiffness constant. These walls perform free diffusion and undergo intermittent resetting to their initial positions with different rates
r_{1}
and
r_{N}
. We denote the displacement of the
l
-th oscillator from its equilibrium position as
x_{l}
and the displacements of the left and right resetting walls as
X_{1}
and
X_{N}
respectively. These moving walls exert stochastic forces on the boundary oscillators due to the intermittent resetting dynamics. The equations of motion of the oscillators are,

m\ddot{x_{l}}=\begin{cases}-k(2x_{1}-x_{2})+f_{1}-\gamma\dot{x}_{1}+\xi_{1},&\text{when $l=1$, }\\
-k(2x_{l}-x_{l-1}-x_{l+1}),&\text{$\forall l\in[2,N-1]$, }\\
-k(2x_{N}-x_{N-1})+f_{N}-\gamma\dot{x}_{N}+\xi_{N}.&\text{when $l=N$}.\end{cases}

(1)

f_{1}=kX_{1}
and
f_{N}=kX_{N}
are the stochastic forces exerted by the boundary walls.
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{N}
are white noise from the Langevin heat bath acting on the boundary oscillators and satisfy the fluctuation-dissipation relation
[
11
]
,

\langle\xi_{i}(t)\xi_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=2\gamma T_{i}\delta_{ij}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\quad\text{and}~i,j=1,N.

(2)

T_{1}
and
T_{N}
are the temperatures of the thermal reservoir at the two ends of the chain.
\gamma
is the damping coefficient of the respective reservoir. Note that, we assume the motion of the wall is independent of the boundary oscillators.

The boundary walls perform free diffusion with Poisson resetting
[
25
,
26
]
. In a small time interval
\Delta t
, the position of the
i
-th wall, relative to its equilibrium position, is updated by the following procedure
[
25
]
,

X_{i}(t+\Delta t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{with probability $r_{i}\Delta t$, }\\
X_{i}(t)+\sqrt{2D\Delta t}\,\eta_{i}(t)&\text{with probability $1-r_{i}\Delta t$, and}~i=1,N,\\
\end{cases}

(3)

where
D
is the diffusion constant which we assumed to be the same for both the boundary walls and
\eta_{i}(t)
has mean and second moment,
\langle\eta_{i}(t)\rangle=0
, and
\langle\eta_{i}(t)\eta_{j}(t)\rangle=\delta_{ij}
. The auto-correlation of the position
X_{i}(t)
is given by
[
54
]
,

\langle X_{i}(t)X_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\frac{2D}{r_{i}}e^{-r_{i}|t-t^{\prime}|}\Big(1-e^{-r_{i}~\text{min}(t,t^{\prime})}\Big)

(4)

A stationary state is reached when
t\to\infty,~t^{\prime}\to\infty
and
|t-t^{\prime}|
is finite. In the stationary state, the auto-correlation of the position
X_{i}(t)
becomes,

\langle X_{i}(t)X_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\frac{2D}{r_{i}}e^{-r_{i}|t-t^{\prime}|}.

(5)

The forces experienced by the boundary oscillators at the two boundaries are
f_{1}=kX_{1}
and
f_{N}=kX_{N}
which are stochastic hin nature. These stochastic forces are correlated as,

\langle f_{i}(t)f_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\,a_{i}^{2}\,e^{-r_{i}|t-t^{\prime}|}\quad\text{with}\quad a_{i}^{2}=\frac{2Dk^{2}}{r_{i}}\quad i=1,N.

(6)

Here
a_{i}
is the strength of the noise coming from the
i
-th resetting wall. The stochastic force
f_{i}(t)
acting on the boundary oscillator violates the fluctuation-dissipation relation. For a resetting driven linear chain, the equations of motion Eq. (
1
) can be solved in the frequency domain,

x_{l}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}[G_{l1}(\omega)\tilde{f}_{1}(\omega)+G_{lN}(\omega)\tilde{f}_{N}(\omega)].

(7)

Here
G(\omega)
denotes the Green’s function matrix [See
A
for a detailed derivation] and
\tilde{f}_{i}(\omega)
is the Fourier transform of
f_{i}(t)
with respect to
t
. The two-point correlation of the stochastic force
\tilde{f}_{i}(t)
in the Fourier domain and can be written using Eq. (
6
) as,

\langle\tilde{f}_{i}(\omega)\tilde{f}_{j}(\omega^{\prime})\rangle=2\pi\delta_{ij}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{i})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{i})=\frac{2a_{i}^{2}r_{i}}{r_{i}^{2}+\omega^{2}},

(8)

where
\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{i})
is the frequency spectrum of the resetting force.

It can be easily shown that for the harmonic chain, the average energy current has two independent components— a thermal component,
J_{\text{therm}}
and a resetting component
J_{\text{r}}
.
J_{\text{therm}}
is proportional to the temperature difference at the two ends of the chain,
T_{1}-T_{N}
, and
J_{\text{r}}
will depend on the resetting rates of the boundary walls. The main objective of this paper is to characterize the NESS driven by the boundary resetting and we choose
T_{1}=T_{N}=0
for the rest of the calculation. In this paper the numerical simulations are done using the scheme of
[
55
]
which are accurate to order
(\Delta t)^{2}
.

Before going into the detailed discussion, a summary of the result is presented.

•

Velocity fluctuation:
We measure the velocity distribution of the oscillators
P(v_{l})
in the NESS. We find that,
P(v_{l})
of the bulk oscillators approach Gaussian distribution in the thermodynamic limit,
N\to\infty
with width
\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}=m\langle\dot{x}_{l}^{2}\rangle
, which is a measure of the kinetic temperature. To characterize the finite-size dependence, we measure the kurtosis profile of velocity,
\kappa_{l}=\langle v_{l}^{4}\rangle/\langle v_{l}^{2}\rangle^{2}-3
. We find that for the bulk oscillators,
\kappa_{l}
decays as
N^{-1}
. The velocity distributions of the boundary oscillators [
P(v_{1})
or
P(v_{N})
] are found to be size-independent as well as non-Gaussian. The NESS of the thermally driven chain is Gaussian, independent of the size of the harmonic chain and this is a sharp difference between the resetting driven and thermally driven case.

•

Spatio-temporal correlation of velocity:
Another physical observable of interest is the spatio-temporal velocity correlation of the oscillators
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle
, in the steady state. We calculate two-time velocity correlation of single oscillator (
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle
) and equal-time spatial velocity correlation (
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
) for resetting driven chain explicitly. We show that in the thermodynamic limit, the two-time velocity correlation of a single oscillator in the bulk is,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\cos\Big[\omega_{c}\sin{\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)}(t-t^{\prime})\Big]\Bigg[\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}r_{N}}{r_{N}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}\Bigg].

(9)

where
\omega_{c}=2\sqrt{k/m}
. When
t-t^{\prime}\gg\omega_{c}^{-1}
,
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle\propto J_{0}\big(\omega_{c}(t-t^{\prime})\big)
, here
J_{0}(z)
is the Bessel function of first kind
[
56
]
. The equal-time spatial velocity correlation in thermodynamic limit is given by,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\Big[a_{1}^{2}\theta(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})+a_{N}^{2}\theta(r_{N},l,l^{\prime})\Big].

(10)

where,

\theta(r_{i},l,l^{\prime})=\frac{r_{i}}{mr_{i}^{2}+4k}\,{}_{3}\tilde{F}_{2}\,\Big[1/2,1,1;1-l^{\prime}+l,1+l^{\prime}-l;\frac{4k}{4k+mr_{i}^{2}}\Big].

(11)

Here
l^{\prime}-l
is an integer and
{}_{p}\tilde{F}_{q}
is a generalized regularized hypergeometric function
[
56
]
.

•

Fluctuation of energy current:
We also measure the distribution of instantaneous energy current flowing through the system in the steady state. For the resetting driven oscillator chain, the distribution of instantaneous energy current at the bulk,
\mathcal{J}_{l}
is found to approach

P(\mathcal{J}_{l})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{g_{l}}}e^{\frac{J_{\text{r}}}{g_{l}}\mathcal{J}_{l}}K_{0}\Big(\frac{u_{l}}{g_{l}}|\mathcal{J}_{l}|\Big).

(12)

in the thermodynamic limit. Here

is the zeroth-order modified Bessel function of the second kind and the definition of
J_{\text{r}}
,
g_{l}
and
u_{l}
is given in Eq. (
34
) and Eq. (
37
). The numerical simulation also confirms that the distribution of instantaneous energy current at the boundary
\mathcal{J}_{1}

Effective thermal limit:
At high resetting rate, the
i
-th boundary wall acts as effective Langevin bath with effective temperature
T_{i}^{\text{eff}}
. In this limit, the known result of the average energy current for a thermally driven oscillator chain is recovered
[
1
]
. The equal-time spatial velocity correlation also becomes uncorrelated for

in this effective thermal limit.

Velocity distribution and kurtosis profile of the velocity

The probability distributions of the velocity of the constituent oscillators play an important role in characterizing the NESS of the driven oscillator chain. For thermally or activity-driven oscillator chains, the velocity distributions of the oscillators at the bulk are found to be Gaussian with its width given by local kinetic temperature
\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}=m\langle v_{l}^{2}\rangle

[
1
,
24
]
. However, the velocity distribution of boundary oscillators depends on the specific dynamics of driving. For resetting driven harmonic chain also, we first measure the velocity distributions of bulk and boundary oscillators for different system sizes and different resetting rates at the two boundaries.

3.1
Distribution of velocity

The velocity of the
l
-th oscillator for resetting driven chain is [from Eq. (
7
)],

v_{l}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}(-i\omega)\big[G_{l1}(\omega)\tilde{f}_{1}(\omega)+G_{lN}(\omega)\tilde{f}_{N}(\omega)\big],

(13)

and we numerically measure the velocity distribution of the bulk oscillators
P(v_{l})
for different system sizes. In Fig.

\text{For}~N\to\infty,~~P(v_{l})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hat{T}_{bulk}}}\exp{\Bigg(\frac{-mv_{l}^{2}}{2\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}}\Bigg)},

(14)

4
Spatio-temporal correlation of velocity

Correlation functions play an important role in characterizing transport processes. For example, the integral of equilibrium velocity-velocity correlation
\langle v(t)v(t^{\prime})\rangle
is related to the diffusion coefficient of a Brownian particle. For thermally driven transport in the mass-disordered harmonic chain, the two-time correlation of the current determines the asymptotic size dependence of the current fluctuation in the steady state
[
57
]
. In this section, we discuss the two-point spatio-temporal correlation of velocity
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle
in the bulk.

Using Eq. (
13
), we can write the two-point spatio-temporal correlation of velocity as,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\big[G_{l1}(\omega)G_{l^{\prime}1}^{*}(\omega)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})+G_{lN}(\omega)G_{l^{\prime}N}^{*}(\omega)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{N})\big].

(18)

In the following, we calculate the two-time velocity correlation of a single oscillator
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle
and equal-time spatial velocity correlation
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
in the stationary state explicitly for the resetting driven chain.

4.1
Two-time velocity correlation of single oscillator

In the stationary state, the two-time velocity correlation of a single oscillator,
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle
is a function of
t-t^{\prime}
[see Eq. (
18
)]. To simplify the calculation, we consider
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle
which can be written as,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}e^{-i\omega t}\big[|G_{l1}(\omega)|^{2}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})+|G_{lN}(\omega)|^{2}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{N})\big].

(19)

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle=a_{1}^{2}{C}(r_{1},t)+a_{N}^{2}{C}(r_{N},t).

(21)

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle
in the thermodynamic limit as,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\cos\Big[\omega_{c}t\sin{\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)}\Big]\Bigg[\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}r_{N}}{r_{N}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}\Bigg].

5.1
Distribution of instantaneous energy current at the bulk

In this section, we will concentrate on the stationary state distribution of instantaneous current at the bulk
P(\mathcal{J}_{l})
. It is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{l}
mentioned in Eq. (

will depend on the joint distribution
\mathcal{P}(\{x_{l},v_{l}\})
. In Sec.

x_{l}
in the bulk. Therefore, we assume that the joint probability distribution of
\{x_{l},v_{l}\}
of the bulk oscillators,
\mathcal{P}(\{x_{l},v_{l}\})
is a multivariate Gaussian in the thermodynamic limit. Therefore, we can write the joint distribution of
\{v_{l-1},v_{l},x_{l-1},x_{l}\}

\mathcal{P}(v_{l-1},v_{l},x_{l-1},x_{l})=\frac{\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}{W}_{l}^{T}\mathcal{E}^{-1}_{l}{W}_{l}\right]}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{4}\text{det}(\mathcal{E}_{l})}},

(35)

where,
W_{l}^{T}=(v_{l-1}~v_{l}~x_{l-1}~x_{l})
and
\mathcal{E}_{l}=\langle W_{l}W_{l}^{T}\rangle

4\times 4

at the bulk is derived in Ref.
[
24

P(\mathcal{J}_{l})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{g_{l}}}\exp\Big({\frac{J_{\text{r}}}{g_{l}}\mathcal{J}_{l}\Big)}K_{0}\Big(\frac{u_{l}}{g_{l}}|\mathcal{J}_{l}|\Big).

(36)

Here

is the zeroth-order modified Bessel function of
the second kind, and

is the average energy current flowing through the resetting driven harmonic chain. Note that, the specific form of distribution function mentioned in Eq. (
36

[
58

g_{l}=\frac{k\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}}{2}[\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}+\langle v_{l-1}v_{l}\rangle]-J_{\text{r}}^{2},~~\text{and}~~u_{l}=\sqrt{g_{l}+J_{\text{r}}^{2}}.

(37)

Here

6
Effective thermal limit

In the limit of a high resetting rate, the colored noise coming from the resetting wall at the boundary becomes an effective white noise. Therefore an effective fluctuation-dissipation relation can be written in terms of an effective temperature,
T_{i}^{\text{eff}}
.

\langle f_{i}(t)f_{j}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{ij}a_{i}^{2}e^{-r_{i}|t-t^{\prime}|}\xrightarrow{r_{i}\to\infty}2\gamma\delta_{ij}T_{i}^{\text{eff}}\delta(t-t^{\prime})~\text{where,}~T_{i}^{\text{eff}}=\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{\gamma r_{i}}.

(45)

Therefore the resetting wall at the boundary acts as an effective Langevin bath with effective temperature
T_{i}^{\text{eff}}
. In this high resetting rate, the average energy flux in the NESS Eq. (
34
) becomes identical to the thermally driven harmonic chain
[
1
]
with higher order correction,

J_{\text{r}}=\frac{k(T_{1}^{\text{eff}}-T_{N}^{\text{eff}})}{2\gamma}\Bigg[1+\frac{mk}{2\gamma^{2}}-\frac{mk}{2\gamma^{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{4\gamma^{2}}{mk}}\Bigg]+O\Big(\frac{1}{r_{j}^{2}}\Big).

(46)

Similarly, we can evaluate the two-time velocity correlation of a single oscillator and equal-time spatial velocity correlation in the limit of a high resetting rate. For large
r_{i}
, Eq. (
22
) can be approximated as,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle\simeq\frac{T_{1}^{\text{eff}}+T_{N}^{\text{eff}}}{2m}J_{0}(\omega_{c}t).

(47)

Here
J_{0}(z)
is the Bessel function of the first kind. Equal-time spatial velocity correlation in the limit of high resetting rate can be evaluated using Eqs.(
25
) and (
26
) and the result is,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\simeq\frac{T_{1}^{\text{eff}}+T_{N}^{\text{eff}}}{2m}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}.

(48)

In Sec.
4
we discuss the two-time velocity correlation of single oscillator and equal-time spatial velocity correlation for resetting driven oscillator chain. For the resetting driven chain, the nonzero value of
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
for
l\neq l^{\prime}
in the NESS implies that there exists a finite correlation between the velocity of the oscillators in the bulk. However, in the effective thermal limit, this correlation vanishes. Similarly, in the effective thermal limit, the two-time velocity correlation of single oscillator
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle
is given in Eq. (
47
). The small time behavior (
t\ll\omega^{-1}_{c}
) of two-time velocity correlation
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle
is different for the resetting driven chain [Fig.
6
(a)] but the large time behavior is similar for both the cases.

7
Conclusion

In this work, we study the NESS of a harmonic chain driven by resetting dynamics at the boundary. The harmonic chain is attached to two walls at the boundaries which undergo one-dimensional Brownian motion and stochastically reset to their initial position at different rates. As a result, the boundary oscillators experience exponentially correlated forces that lead to a NESS. For the resetting driven harmonic chain, we conclude from the numerical simulation that the velocity distributions of the bulk oscillators reach Gaussian distribution when the system size is thermodynamically large. The velocity distributions of the boundary oscillators are non-Gaussian and size-independent. We have numerically computed the velocity kurtosis profile and found that velocity kurtosis of the bulk oscillators decay as
N^{-1}
, where
N
is the system size. We also show that the two-time velocity correlations of the bulk oscillators
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(0)\rangle
decay as
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
when
t\gg\omega_{c}^{-1}
. In the bulk, there exists a nonzero equal-time spatial velocity correlation
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
when
l\neq l^{\prime}
. The distribution of the instantaneous energy current at the bulk is found to be size-dependent and reaches a stationary distribution only when
N\to\infty
. However, The distribution of instantaneous current at the boundary is size-independent and depends only on the resetting rate of the boundary walls. It is important to note that, at a high resetting rate, an effective thermal picture emerges where we recover expression for the average energy current of a thermally driven harmonic chain. In this effective thermal limit, equal-time spatial velocity correlation
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
vanishes when
l\neq l^{\prime}
.

In this paper, the boundary walls perform free diffusion with Poisson resetting where the waiting time between the two resetting events is drawn from an exponential distribution. However, the dynamics of the boundary walls can be generalized where the waiting time between two consecutive resetting events follow a power-law distribution
[
34
,
59
,
60
]
or the resetting mechanism becomes non-instantaneous
[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
. It is expected that such changes in the dynamics of the boundary walls will affect the NESS of the ordered harmonic chain and it is intriguing to explore the transport properties of such NESS. Another interesting question is how our results change when instead of our simple model of nonequilibrium bath, the boundary oscillators of the harmonic chain are attached to explicit nonequilibrium baths that are modeled using the stochastic resetting e.g. the model of nonequilibrium bath described in Ref.
[
53
]
. It is also interesting to study how the energy transport in resetting driven oscillator chains gets affected if nonlinearity, mass disorder, and momentum non-conserving dynamics are introduced.

Appendix A
Matrix formulation

In this appendix, we will briefly discuss the correlation calculations. To begin with, let us start with the matrix form the Langevin equations Eq. (
1
),

M\ddot{X}=-\Phi X(t)-\Gamma\dot{X}(t)+F(t),

(49)

where
X(t)
is a state vector of dimension
N\times 1
, and the
l
-th component is
x_{l}(t)
, displacement of the
l
-th oscillator from the equilibrium position.
\Phi
and
\Gamma
are
N\times N
matrix and the definitions of
\Phi
and
\Gamma
are,

\Phi_{ij}=2k\delta_{ij}-k\delta_{i,j-1}-k\delta_{i,j+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{ij}=\gamma\delta_{i1}\delta_{j1}+\gamma\delta_{iN}\delta_{jN}.

(50)

F(t)
is the noise coming from the resetting wall. We can write these as,

F_{j}(t)=f_{1}(t)\delta_{j1}+f_{N}(t)\delta_{jN}.

(51)

The correlation of this noise in the frequency domain is,

\langle\tilde{F}(\omega)\tilde{F}^{T}(\omega^{\prime})\rangle_{ij}=2\pi\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\left[\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})\delta_{i1}\delta_{j1}+\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{N})\delta_{iN}\delta_{jN}\right],

(52)

where
\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{j})=\frac{2a_{j}^{2}r_{j}}{r_{j}^{2}+\omega^{2}}
. With the help of the Fourier transform, we can get rid of the time derivatives. The Fourier transform of the displacement matrix,

\tilde{X}(\omega)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dte^{i\omega t}X(t).

(53)

Therefore the Langevin equation Eq.(
49
) takes the form,

\tilde{X}(\omega)=G(\omega)\tilde{F}(\omega)\quad\text{where}\quad G(\omega)=[-M\omega^{2}+\Phi-i\omega\Gamma]^{-1}.

(54)

G(\omega)
is the inverse of the tridiagonal matrix. The explicit form of this matrix is,

G(\omega)=\begin{bmatrix}-m\omega^{2}+2k-i\omega\gamma&-k&\cdots\\
-k&-m\omega^{2}+2k&\cdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\cdots\\
0&\cdots&-m\omega^{2}+2k-i\omega\gamma\\
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}.

(55)

G
is the inverse of a symmetric matrix, therefore
G
it self is a symmetric matrix and
G^{*}(\omega)=G(-\omega)
.
We can write the components of
G(\omega)

[
65
]
,

G_{l1}=(-k)^{l-1}\frac{\theta_{N-l}}{\theta_{N}}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{lN}=(-k)^{N-l}\frac{\theta_{l-1}}{\theta_{N}}.

(56)

where
\theta_{l}
satisfy recursion relations,

\theta_{l}=(-m\omega^{2}+2k)\theta_{l-1}-k^{2}\theta_{l-2}\quad\forall l=2,3,\cdots N-1,

(57)

and,

\theta_{N}=(-m\omega^{2}+2k-i\omega\gamma)\theta_{N-1}-k^{2}\theta_{N-2},

(58)

with the boundary condition,

\theta_{0}=1,\qquad\theta_{1}=-m\omega^{2}+2k-i\omega\gamma.

(59)

Solving the recursion relations of Eq. (
57
) we get,

\theta_{l}=\frac{(-k)^{l-1}}{\sin{q}}[k\sin{(l+1)q}-i\omega\gamma\sin{lq}]\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{N}=\frac{(-k)^{N}}{\sin{q}}[a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}].

(60)

Here,

a(q)=-\frac{2i\gamma\omega}{k}+\cos{q}\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad b(q)=\sin{q}\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right),

(61)

and
\omega
and
q
are related through,

\cos{q}=\left(1-\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2k}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad\omega=\omega_{c}\sin{\frac{q}{2}}.

(62)

with
\omega_{c}=2\sqrt{k/m}
.

t>t^{\prime}
,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\big[|G_{l1}(\omega)|^{2}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})+|G_{lN}(\omega)|^{2}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{N})\big].

(65)

From the above equation, it is clear that the two-time velocity correlation of single oscillator
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle
for resetting driven chain, can be written as a sum of two separate contributions coming from two resetting walls at the two boundaries, i.e.,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})+\bar{C}(r_{N},t,t^{\prime}).

(66)

The contribution from the left resetting wall,

\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{2}e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}|G_{l1}(\omega)|^{2}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(67)

\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})
will have a non-zero contribution from the even terms with respect to
\omega
. Using Eq. (
56
) and keeping only the terms that are even in
\omega
, we get,

\displaystyle\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\omega^{2}\cos{[\omega(t-t^{\prime})]}\Bigg(\frac{k^{2}\sin^{2}{(Nq-lq+q)}}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}

(68)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{2}\gamma^{2}\sin^{2}{(Nq-lq)}}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}\Bigg)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})

(69)

We are interested in the time correlation of velocity in the bulk of the chain, therefore we take
l=N/2+\epsilon
, and take the limit
\epsilon\ll N
, which leads to,

\displaystyle\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\cos{[\omega(t-t^{\prime})]}\Bigg(\frac{(k^{2}+\omega^{2}\gamma^{2})}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}

(70)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}\cos{(Nq-2\epsilon q+2q)}+\omega^{2}\gamma^{2}\cos{(Nq-2\epsilon q)}}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}\Bigg)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(71)

For
\omega>\omega_{c}
,
q
becomes complex. In the thermodynamically large system size, the integrand vanishes exponentially as
e^{-2N\bar{q}}
in the region
\omega>\omega_{c}
, here
\bar{q}
is real. The range of the integration reduces to
0\leq\omega\leq\omega_{c}
, or
0\leq q\leq\pi
. In the large-
N
limit,
\cos{Nq}
is a highly oscillatory function and we can evaluate these integrations by averaging over fast oscillation in
x=Nq
, using the following identities (see 2.558 in
[
66
]
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{dx}{(c_{1}\sin{x}+d\cos{x})^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{c_{2}d},~\text{for}~c_{2}<0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{dx\cos{x}}{(c_{1}\sin{x}+d\cos{x})^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(72)

with
c_{2}=\text{Im}[a(q)]=-\frac{2\gamma\omega}{k}
and
d=\text{Re}[b(q)]=\sin{q}\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)
. Therefore Eq. (
71
) can be written as,

\displaystyle\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\cos{[\omega(t-t^{\prime})]}\frac{k^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}{-dc_{2}}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(73)

Finally, we arrive at,

\displaystyle\bar{C}(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{\pi}\Big|\frac{d\omega}{dq}\Big|\frac{\omega\cos{[\omega(t-t^{\prime})]}}{4\gamma\sin{q}}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{\cos\Big[\omega_{c}\sin{\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)}(t-t^{\prime})\Big]}{r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}=a_{1}^{2}C(r_{1},t,t^{\prime}),

(75)

where we have used the explicit form of
\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})
and
\omega(q)
from Eq. (
52
) and (
62
). Similarly, we can evaluate the contribution from the right resetting wall, and by combining these two results we arrive at the,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\cos\Big[\omega_{c}\sin{\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)}(t-t^{\prime})\Big]\Bigg[\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}r_{N}}{r_{N}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}\Bigg].

(76)

This integral can be evaluated numerically.

Large time behavior (
t-t^{\prime}\gg\omega_{c}^{-1}
):

To calculate the large time behavior of
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle
, we first substitute
z=\omega_{c}\sin\Big(\frac{q}{2}\Big)
in Eq. (
75
) and arrive at,

C(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})=\frac{r_{1}}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\omega_{c}}\frac{dz}{\pi}\frac{\cos\big(z(t-t^{\prime})\big)}{\sqrt{\omega_{c}^{2}-z^{2}}(r_{1}^{2}+z^{2})}.

(77)

For large
t-t^{\prime}
,
\cos\big(z(t-t^{\prime})\big)
is a bounded and fast oscillatory function. Therefore, the dominating contribution to the integral is coming from the region
z=\omega_{c}
and contributions from small
z
are negligible. We can now approximate
r_{1}^{2}+z^{2}
as
r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2}
. Therefore the last integral becomes,

C(r_{1},t,t^{\prime})\simeq\frac{r_{1}}{\gamma m}\int_{0}^{\omega_{c}}\frac{dz}{\pi}\frac{\cos\big(z(t-t^{\prime})\big)}{\sqrt{\omega_{c}^{2}-z^{2}}(r_{1}^{2}+\omega_{c}^{2})}=\frac{r_{1}}{2\gamma}\frac{J_{0}\big(\omega_{c}(t-t^{\prime})\big)}{(mr_{1}^{2}+4k)}.

(78)

Here
J_{0}(z)
is the Bessel function of the first kind. Therefore when
t-t^{\prime}
is large, using Eqs. (
75
) and (
78
) we get

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l}(t^{\prime})\rangle\simeq\Bigg[\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{2\gamma(mr_{1}^{2}+4k)}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}r_{N}}{2\gamma(mr_{N}^{2}+4k)}\Bigg]J_{0}\big(\omega_{c}(t-t^{\prime})\big).

(79)

Kinetic temperature profile at the bulk:
The bulk value of the kinetic temperature profile can be evaluated using Eq. (
76
) by taking
t=t^{\prime}
. Therefore, in the stationary state,

\langle v_{l}(t)^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\Bigg[\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}}{mr_{1}^{2}+4k\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}r_{N}}{mr_{N}^{2}+4k\sin^{2}{\frac{q}{2}}}\Bigg].

(80)

The last integral can be evaluated exactly, which leads to the following expression of the bulk value of the kinetic temperature profile,

\hat{T}_{\text{bulk}}=m\langle v_{l}^{2}(t)\rangle=\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{2\gamma\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+\frac{4k}{m}}}+\frac{a_{N}^{2}}{2\gamma\sqrt{r_{N}^{2}+\frac{4k}{m}}}.

(81)

B.2
Equal-time spatial velocity correlation:

For
l\neq l^{\prime}
and
t=t^{\prime}
, using Eq. (
64
),

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\big[G_{l1}(\omega)G_{l^{\prime}1}^{*}(\omega)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})+G_{lN}(\omega)G_{l^{\prime}N}^{*}(\omega)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{N})\big].

(82)

Similar to the previous calculation,
\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle
can be written as a sum of two separate contributions coming from resetting walls at the boundaries as the following,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle=\bar{\theta}(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})+\bar{\theta}(r_{N},l,l^{\prime}).

(83)

The contribution from the left resetting wall,

\bar{\theta}(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}G_{l1}(\omega)G_{l^{\prime}1}^{*}(\omega)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(84)

Using Eq. (
56
) and keeping only the terms that are even in
\omega
, we get,

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\Bigg(\frac{(k^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2})\cos{(l^{\prime}q-lq)}}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}

(85)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}\cos{(Nq-\epsilon q+2q)+\omega^{2}\gamma^{2}\cos{(Nq-\epsilon q)}}}{|a(q)\sin{Nq}+b(q)\cos{Nq}|^{2}}\Bigg)\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(86)

Now we are interested in
l=\frac{N}{2}
,
l^{\prime}=\frac{N}{2}+\alpha
where
\alpha
is an integer. For
\omega>\omega_{c}
, the integrand vanishes [see the discussion after Eq. (
71
)]. The final expression after averaging over fast oscillation using Eq. (
72
) is,

\bar{\theta}(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})=\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\Big|\frac{d\omega}{dq}\Big|\omega^{2}\frac{(k^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2})\cos{(l^{\prime}-l)q}}{-dc_{2}}\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1}).

(87)

Using
c_{2}=\text{Im}[a(q)]=-\frac{2\gamma\omega}{k}
and
d=\text{Re}[b(q)]=\sin{q}\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)
, and explicit form of
\tilde{g}(\omega,r_{1})
, we arrive at,

\bar{\theta}(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})=\frac{a_{1}^{2}r_{1}^{2}}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{\cos{(l^{\prime}q-lq)}}{mr_{1}^{2}+4k\sin^{2}\frac{q}{2}}=\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{2\gamma}\theta(r_{1},l,l^{\prime}),

(88)

where,

\theta(r_{i},l,l^{\prime})=\frac{r_{i}}{mr_{i}^{2}+4k}\,{}_{3}\tilde{F}_{2}\,\Bigg[1/2,1,1;1-l^{\prime}+l,1+l^{\prime}-l;\frac{4k}{4k+mr_{i}^{2}}\Bigg].

(89)

Here
l^{\prime}-l
is an integer and
{}_{p}\tilde{F}_{q}
is a generalized regularized hypergeometric function
[
56
]
. The definition of generalized regularized hypergeometric function
{}_{p}\tilde{F}_{q}
is,

{}_{p}\tilde{F}_{q}[x_{1},x_{2}\cdots x_{p};y_{1},y_{2},\cdots y_{q};z]=\frac{{}_{p}{F}_{q}[x_{1},x_{2}\cdots x_{p};y_{1},y_{2},\cdots y_{q};z]}{\Gamma(y_{1})\Gamma(y_{2})\cdots\Gamma(y_{q})},

(90)

where
\Gamma(z)
is the gamma function and
{}_{p}{F}_{q}
is the generalized hypergeometric function. The definition of generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{p}{F}_{q}
is,

{}_{p}{F}_{q}[x_{1},x_{2}\cdots x_{p};y_{1},y_{2},\cdots y_{q};z]=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x_{1})_{j}(x_{2})_{j}\cdots(x_{p})_{j}}{(y_{1})_{j}(y_{2})_{j}\cdots(y_{q})_{j}}\Big(\frac{z^{j}}{j!}\Big).

(91)

(x)_{j}
is the Pochhammer symbol defined as
(x)_{j}\equiv\Gamma(x+j)/\Gamma(x)

[
56
]
.
We can calculate the contribution coming from the right resetting wall
\bar{\theta}(r_{N},l,l^{\prime})
in a similar manner. Combining both contributions we arrive at the,

\langle v_{l}(t)v_{l^{\prime}}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\Big[a_{1}^{2}\theta(r_{1},l,l^{\prime})+a_{N}^{2}\theta(r_{N},l,l^{\prime})\Big].

(92)

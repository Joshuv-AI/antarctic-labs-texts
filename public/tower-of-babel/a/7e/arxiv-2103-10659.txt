Quasinormal modes and shadow of noncommutative black hole
J. A. V. Campos, M. A. Anacleto, F. A. Brito, E. Passos

Abstract
In this paper we investigate quasinormal modes (QNM) for a scalar field around a noncommutative Schwarzschild black hole. We verify the effect of noncommutativity on quasinormal frequencies by applying two procedures widely used in the literature. The first is the Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) approximation up to sixth order. In the second case we use the continuous fraction method developed by Leaver. Besides, we also show that due to noncommutativity, the shadow radius is reduced when we increase the noncommutative parameter. In addition, we find that the shadow radius is nonzero even at the zero mass limit for finite noncommutative parameter.

Initial studies for black hole perturbations were done by Regge and Wheeler

frequencies

The dominant quasinormal modes can be seen in gravitational wave signals and
in this case, the emitted waves are related to many physical processes such as astrophysical phenomena involving the evolution of binary systems and stellar oscillations or other highly dense objects in the early universe.
In this way these quasinormal modes have been observed experimentally by LIGO/VIRGO

II
Noncommutative black hole with Lorentzian smeared mass distribution

On this section we begin by considering a Lorentzian distribution
Nicolini:2005vd
;
Nozari:2008rc
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}(r)=\frac{M\sqrt{\theta}}{\pi^{3/2}(r^{2}+\pi\theta)^{2}},

(1)

where
\theta
is the noncommutative parameter of dimension
{length}^{2}
and
M
is the total mass diffused throughout the region
of linear size
\sqrt{\theta}
.
Thus, the smeared mass distribution function becomes
Anacleto:2019tdj

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\rho_{\theta}(r)4\pi r^{2}dr,

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2M}{\pi}\left[\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{\pi\theta}}\right)-\frac{r\sqrt{\pi\theta}}{\pi\theta+r^{2}}\right],

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M-\frac{4M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}r}+{\cal O}(\theta^{3/2}).

(4)

Hence, the line element of the Schwarzschild black hole in the noncommutative background is now given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(5)

with

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{8M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}r^{2}},

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(r-r_{+}\right)\left(r-r_{-}\right),

(7)

where

\displaystyle r_{+}=M+\sqrt{M^{2}-8M\sqrt{\theta/\pi}}\approx 2M-4\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}},

(8)

\displaystyle r_{-}=M-\sqrt{M^{2}-8M\sqrt{\theta/\pi}}\approx 4\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}},

(9)

which represent the radius of the event horizon and the Cauchy horizon, respectively.

The next step we consider the case of the massless scalar field described by the Klein-Gordon equation in the background (
7
)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\right)\Psi=0.

(10)

Thus, we apply the separation of variables method in the above equation by using the following Ansatz

\displaystyle\Psi(\textbf{r},t)=\dfrac{R_{\omega l}(r)}{r}Y_{lm}(\vartheta,\phi)e^{-i\omega t},

(11)

where
\omega
is the frequency and
Y_{lm}(\vartheta,\phi)
are the spherical harmonics.

Now, we can obtain a radial equation for
R_{\omega l}(r)
:

\left(r-r_{-}\right)\left(r-r_{+}\right)\dfrac{d^{2}R_{\omega l}(r)}{dr^{2}}+r^{2}\dfrac{df(r)}{dr}\dfrac{dR_{\omega l}(r)}{dr}+\left[\dfrac{\omega^{2}r^{4}}{\left(r-r_{-}\right)\left(r-r_{+}\right)}-r\dfrac{df(r)}{dr}-l(l+1)\right]R_{\omega l}(r)=0.

(12)

We can reduce the radial equation (
12
) into a Schrödinger like equation by introducing a new coordinate (called tortoise coordinate) given by
dr_{*}=f(r)^{-1}dr
and

r_{*}=r+\dfrac{r_{-}^{2}}{r_{-}-r_{+}}\log(r-r_{-})-\dfrac{r_{+}^{2}}{r_{-}-r_{+}}\log(r-r_{+}),

(13)

so that the radial equation becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}R_{\omega l}(r_{*})}{dr_{*}^{2}}+\left[\omega^{2}-V_{eff}\right]R_{\omega l}(r_{*})=0,

(14)

where

V_{eff}=\dfrac{f(r)}{r}\dfrac{df(r)}{dr}+\dfrac{f(r)l(l+1)}{r^{2}}.

(15)

In the following sections, we will obtain the quasinormal frequencies by two methods that are widely used in the literature. The first uses a sixth order WKB approximation, and the second method introduced by Leaver and improved by Nollert
Nollert:1993zz
, consists of using the continuous fraction method to find numerically quasinormal modes.

II.1
WKB approximation

Quasinormal modes correspond to solutions of the wave equation (
14
) that satisfy the conditions of the purely outgoing waves at infinity and purely incoming waves at the event horizon, i.e.,

R_{\omega l}(r_{*})\sim e^{\pm i\omega r_{*}},\qquad r_{*}\rightarrow\pm\infty.

(16)

In this section we will use the WKB approximation to find the quasinormal modes. The first works using the WKB approximation to evaluate the quasinormal modes were done by Schutz and Will
Schutz:1985zz
. Improvements in the method were made using corrections up to
third order
Iyer:1986np
;
Seidel:1989bp
and up to sixth order by Konoplya
Konoplya:2003ii
.
The quasinormal modes are obtained by using the sixth order corrections for the WKB approximation as follows

\dfrac{i\left(\omega_{n}^{2}-\bar{V}_{eff}\right)}{\sqrt{-2\bar{V}_{eff}^{\prime\prime}}}-\sum_{j=2}^{6}\Omega_{j}=n+\dfrac{1}{2},

(17)

where
\Omega_{j}
are the correction terms of the model. We have that
\bar{V}_{eff}
is the maximum effective potential at point
\bar{r}_{*}
and
(^{\prime\prime})
refers to the second derivative with respect to the tortoise coordinate. We can obtain the values of
\bar{r}_{*}
by making
\bar{V}_{eff}^{\prime}=0
. In figure
1
we show the curves of the effective potential for
l=1,2
and
\Theta=0.0,0.05,0.10,0.12
, where we define
\Theta=\sqrt{\theta}/(M\sqrt{\pi})
for
M=1
. In the tables (
1
-
3
) we present the tabulated quasinormal frequencies using sixth order WKB method.

Another possibility is to study scattering by using the WKB method done in
Iyer:1986np
. In order to develop this investigation, we use the boundary conditions for equation (
14
) in the form:

R_{\omega l}(r_{*})=\begin{cases}A_{int}e^{-i\omega r_{*}}+A_{out}e^{i\omega r_{*}},\qquad r_{*}\rightarrow\infty,\\
A_{tr}e^{-i\omega r_{*}},\hskip 76.82234ptr_{*}\rightarrow-\infty.\end{cases}

(18)

Notice that as we want to obtain the reflection and transmission coefficients, as done for tunneling in quantum mechanics, we need the condition
A_{int}\neq 0
. The quantity
\left(\omega^{2}-V_{eff}\right)
in
(
14
)
is assumed to be purely real, and with these imposed conditions we can find

\mathcal{K}=\dfrac{i\left(\omega^{2}-\bar{V}_{eff}\right)}{\sqrt{-2\bar{V}_{eff}^{\prime\prime}}}-\sum_{j=2}^{6}\Omega_{j}(\mathcal{K}),

(19)

where
\omega
is purely real and
\Omega_{j}(\mathcal{K})
are coefficients that depend on the effective potential and
\mathcal{K}
is a purely imaginary quantity. This way of studying scattering by using the WKB approximation can be also found in
Konoplya:2019hlu
;
Konoplya:2019xmn
. Thus, using the relationship between
\mathcal{K}
and the reflection and transmission coefficients obtained in
Iyer:1986np
we get the following:

\displaystyle|R|^{2}=\dfrac{|A_{out}|^{2}}{|A_{int}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-2i\pi\mathcal{K}}},\qquad 0<|R|^{2}<1,

(20)

\displaystyle|T|^{2}=\dfrac{|A_{tr}|^{2}}{|A_{int}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1+e^{2i\pi\mathcal{K}}}=1-|R|^{2}.

(21)

Now to find the coefficients, we just calculate the value
\mathcal{K}
that can be obtained by solving the equation (
19
). This method has a good approximation for
l>0
as we can see in Figure
2
, where we have a comparison between the numerical results and the WKB approximation for the transmission coefficient for
l=1,2,3
and
\Theta=0.05,0.12
.
Notice that the curves obtained by the WKB approximation are very close to those obtained by the numerical method used in the paper
Anacleto:2019tdj
, showing that the method presents excellent results. The sixth-order WKB approximation does not show good results for
l=0
, improving only when we take large
\omega
. This problem is also mentioned in
Konoplya:2019hlu

II.2
Leaver’s continued fraction

The numerical method is another way to obtain the quasinormal frequency, and this procedure has been described by Leaver
Leaver:1985ax
;
Leaver:1990zz
, and which is also found in other works
Konoplya:2011qq
;
Cardoso:2004fi
showing that it is a method with good precision.

We start analyzing the radial equation (
12
) which is subject to boundary conditions at infinity
r\rightarrow\infty
and near the event horizon
r\rightarrow r_{+}
, such that one obtains the asymptotic solutions

R_{\omega l}(r)\approx\begin{cases}e^{i\omega r}r^{i\omega(r_{-}+r_{+})},\hskip 183.52019ptr\rightarrow\infty,\\
e^{-i\omega r_{+}}\left(r_{+}-r_{-}\right)^{-i\omega r_{-}^{2}/(r_{-}-r_{+})}\left(r-r_{+}\right)^{i\omega r_{+}^{2}/(r_{-}-r_{+})},\qquad r\rightarrow r_{+}.\end{cases}

(22)

We can obtain a solution that has the desired behavior on the horizon
(
r=r_{+}
), and that can be written in the form

R=\frac{r}{r-r_{-}}\left(r-r_{-}\right)^{i\omega(r_{+}+r_{-})}\left(\frac{r-r_{+}}{r-r_{-}}\right)^{\frac{-i\omega r_{+}^{2}}{r_{+}-r_{-}}}e^{i\omega r}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\left(\frac{r-r_{+}}{r-r_{-}}\right)^{k}.

(23)

By replacing the solution (
23
) in the equation (
12
), we obtain the recurrence relation

\alpha_{0}a_{1}+\beta_{0}a_{0}=0,

(24)

and

\alpha_{k}a_{k+1}+\beta_{k}a_{k}+\gamma_{k}a_{k-1}=0,\qquad k\geq 1.

(25)

The recurrence relation coefficients
\alpha_{k}
,
\beta_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}
are simple functions of
k
and the parameters
\omega
,
l
and the radius
r_{-}
and
r_{+}
:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+k\right)\left[r_{-}\left(k+1\right)-r_{+}\left(1+k-2i\omega r_{+}\right)\right],

(26)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{+}\left[l(l+1)+1+2\left(k-2i\omega r_{+}\right)\left(1+k-2i\omega r_{+}\right)\right]-r_{-}\left[l(l+1)+1-2i\omega r_{+}+2k\left(1+k-2i\omega r_{+}\right)\right],

(27)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[k-2i\omega\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)\right]\left[k\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)+2i\omega r_{+}^{2}\right].

(28)

See that the solution (
23
) applied directly to the radial equation returns a three-term recurrence relation which makes it easier to use continued fractions. This is because our metric for a non-commutative black hole results in a generalized spheroidal wave equation whose solutions are connected by three-term recurrence relations — see Appendix
V
for further details. However, the Leaver’s method is not necessarily limited to recurrence relations of this type as shown in
Leaver:1990zz
, where it was considered an equation that describes odd-parity perturbations of a charged black hole, which equation has series solutions whose coefficients are connected by four-term recurrence relations.

The boundary condition at infinity will be satisfied for quasinormal frequency values
\omega=\omega_{n}
, so that the series in (
23
) is absolutely convergent. Hence, we have a recurrence relation with three terms to determine the coefficient
a_{k}
, and we can write in terms of a continuous fraction
Gautschi

\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}=\dfrac{-\gamma_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}-\dfrac{\alpha_{k+1}\gamma_{k+2}}{\beta_{k+2}-\dfrac{\alpha_{k+2}\gamma_{k+3}}{\beta_{k+2}-\cdots}}},

(29)

which can also be found as follows

\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}=\dfrac{-\gamma_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{k+1}\gamma_{k+2}}{\beta_{k+2}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{k+2}\gamma_{k+3}}{\beta_{k+3}-\cdots}.

(30)

We can obtain the characteristic equation for quasinormal frequencies by assigning
k=0
in (
30
) and comparing with reason
a_{1}/a_{0}=-\beta_{0}/\alpha_{0}
obtained from (
24
),

0=\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}-\dfrac{\gamma_{1}}{\beta_{1}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\gamma_{2}}{\beta_{2}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{2}\gamma_{3}}{\beta_{3}-\cdots}.

(31)

With the equation above, we can obtain the quasinormal frequencies
\omega_{n}
, by just calculating its roots numerically. However, the equation (
31
) is more used to find the fundamental frequency in the case of the more stable root, and another way of finding the modes is to invert this equation to a large number of
k
as follows

\beta_{k}-\dfrac{\alpha_{k-1}\gamma_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{k-2}\gamma_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-2}-}\cdots\dfrac{\alpha_{0}\gamma_{1}}{\beta_{0}}=\dfrac{\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}-}\dfrac{\alpha_{k+1}\gamma_{k+2}}{\beta_{k+2}-}\cdots.

(32)

To complement the analysis, we will check the behavior for very large
k
, as done in
Nollert:1993zz
.
Now, we have to reorganize the equation (
25
) dividing
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
by
a_{k}
to obtain

\alpha_{k}\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}+\beta_{k}+\gamma_{k}\dfrac{a_{k-1}}{a_{k}}=0.

(33)

We can see that,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}(a_{k+1}/a_{k})\simeq 1
, and we can get a more complete expression by expanding
a_{k+1}/a_{k}
in power series in terms of
\sqrt{k}
,

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}C_{i}k^{-i/2}=C_{0}+\dfrac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+\dfrac{C_{2}}{k}+\dfrac{C_{3}}{k^{3/2}}+\cdots.

(34)

By considering the series up to the third term and admitting
C_{0}=1
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 1+\dfrac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+\dfrac{C_{2}}{k}+\cdots,

(35)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{a_{k-1}}{a_{k}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}+\dfrac{(C_{1})^{2}-C_{2}}{k}+\dfrac{C_{1}\left(2C_{2}-1/2-(C_{1})^{2}\right)}{k^{3/2}}+\cdots.

(36)

Now, to obtain the values of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, we replace these two expressions above into equation (
33
) in the very large
k
regime by making the multiplications and restricting up to terms of the order
k^{-3/2}
to get

\dfrac{(C_{1})^{2}-2i\omega\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)}{k}-\dfrac{C_{1}}{2}\dfrac{\left[-3+2(C_{1})^{2}-4C_{2}-4i\omega\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)\right]}{k^{3/2}}\approx 0,

(37)

such that we find the following:

\displaystyle\left(C_{1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i\omega\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right),

(38)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\omega r_{+}-\dfrac{3}{4}.

(39)

Now we have,

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}\approx 1\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2i\omega\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)}}{\sqrt{k}}-\dfrac{2i\omega r_{+}+3/4}{k}.

(40)

Here, we see that at the limit
r_{-}\rightarrow 0
and
r_{+}\rightarrow 1
, we get the result for the Schwarzschild case initially found by Leaver.

III
Null geodesic and Shadow of a noncommutative black hole

We know that in the vicinity of a black hole all the photons are absorbed so that a distant observer looking at the black hole, in absence of any other source, will see a spot created by this absorption, and this spot is usually called the shadow of the black hole. These shadows have been studied long ago by Synge
Synge:1966okc
and Luminet
Luminet:1979nyg
who started studies for Schwarzschild black hole while Kerr black holes were studied by Bardeen
Bardeen
.

As these shadows correspond to the apparent shape of the photon capture orbits, the space-time metric itself is enough to determine them and thereby better understand the geometry of the near horizon. One method of determining the apparent shape of the black hole is through the shadow boundary that can be studied by the equations of null geodesics. Similar studies have been done in different contexts
Wei:2015dua
;
Atamurotov:2015xfa
;
Shaikh:2019fpu
;
Tsupko
;
Stuchlik:2019uvf
;
Jusufi:2019ltj

III.1
Null geodesic

We can find the geodesics from equation (
7
) by taking a Lagrangian in the form

\mathcal{L}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}.

(41)

Thus, we have

2\mathcal{L}=f(r)\dot{t}^{2}-\dfrac{\dot{r}^{2}}{f(r)}-r^{2}\left(\dot{\vartheta}^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta\dot{\phi}^{2}\right),

(42)

where the “
\cdot
” is the derivative with respect to an affine parameter.

We are interested in the path of a ray of light in the described metric, which is spherically symmetrical, so if we analyze in a plane, any ray of light that begins with a certain angle
\vartheta
must remain with the same angle. We will then consider an equatorial plane by setting the angle
\vartheta
to
\pi/2
.

Thus, two equations are enough to describe the movement of a beam of light.
We can put together a system with these equations that give rise to two geodesic motion constants
E
and
L
, which correspond to energy and angular momentum respectively:

E=f(r)\dot{t},\qquad\quad L=r^{2}\dot{\phi}.

(43)

Now, as our goal is to study the null geodesics we have to
g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=0
, and using the equations (
43
) we can write

\dot{r}^{2}+f(r)\dfrac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}=E^{2}.

(44)

Introducing a new variable
u=1/r
we can write the orbit equation as follows

\dfrac{du}{d\phi}=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{b^{2}}-u^{2}+2Mu^{3}-\dfrac{8M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}}u^{4}},

(45)

where
b=L/E
is the impact parameter defined as the perpendicular distance (measured at infinity)
between the geodesic and a parallel line that passes through the origin.
So differentiating (
45
) we have,

\dfrac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}=-u+3Mu^{2}-\dfrac{16M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}}u^{3}.

(46)

By solving the equations (
45
) and (
46
) numerically, we can obtain the behavior of the geodesic lines for different values of the impact parameter
b
.
In the figure

and also by varying the values of the noncommutative parameter.
In the figures, we have a black disk that represents the limit of the event horizon, the internal dotted circle is the radius for the photon sphere (critical radius), and the external dashed circle is the critical impact parameter (shadow).
Hence, we see that the noncommutative parameter decreases the effect of the black hole on the light beams.
For a similar effect see also
Crispino:2009ki

III.2
Critical orbit and shadows

It is known that the shadow of the black hole is directly related to the impact parameter for the photon orbit, as we will see below. So to determine the shadow limit, we will start by studying the effective potential that satisfies the equation of the null geodesic as follows

\dot{r}^{2}+{\cal V}_{eff}(r)=0,

(47)

where using (
44
), we have

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{eff}(r)=f(r)\dfrac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}-E^{2}.

(48)

In this case, we can obtain a critical radius or critical circular orbit for a photon
r_{c}
and critical impact parameter
b_{c}
, by using the following conditions:

{\cal V}_{eff}(r_{c})=0
and
\dfrac{d{\cal V}_{eff}(r_{c})}{dr}=0
.
So we find

\displaystyle r_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left(r_{+}+r_{-}\right)+\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{(r_{+}+r_{-})^{2}-\frac{32r_{+}r_{-}}{9}},

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3M}{2}+\frac{3M}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{64\sqrt{\theta}}{9M\sqrt{\pi}}},

(50)

\displaystyle b_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{r_{c}}{\sqrt{f(r_{c})}}=\frac{r^{2}_{c}}{\sqrt{(r_{c}-r_{+})(r_{c}-r_{-})}}.

(51)

Let us now compute the size of the black hole shadow that can be expressed via celestial coordinates as follows
Bardeen

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r_{o}\to\infty}\left[-r_{o}^{2}\sin\vartheta_{o}\dfrac{d\phi}{dr}\Bigr\rvert_{\vartheta=\vartheta_{o}}\right],

(52)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r_{o}\to\infty}\left[r_{o}^{2}\dfrac{d\vartheta}{dr}\Bigr\rvert_{\vartheta=\vartheta_{o}}\right],

(53)

where
\left(r_{o},\vartheta_{o}\right)
is the observer position at infinity.

As our study is restricted to the equatorial plane, the radius that delimits the size of the shadow is equivalent to the critical impact parameter, and so we have

R_{s}\equiv\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}=b_{c},

(54)

where

R_{s}=\left[\dfrac{\left(3M+\sqrt{9M^{2}-{64M\sqrt{\theta}}/{\sqrt{\pi}}+{128{\theta}}/{{\pi}}}\right)^{4}}{8\left(3M^{2}-{16M\sqrt{\theta}}/{\sqrt{\pi}}+{32\theta}/{\pi}+M\sqrt{9M^{2}-{64M\sqrt{\theta}}/{\sqrt{\pi}}+{128{\theta}}/{{\pi}}}\right)}\right]^{1/2}.

(55)

In a semiclassical description of the scattering
Ford1959

b=(l+1/2)/\omega

IV
Conclusions

In summary, in this work, we investigate the quasinormal frequencies for a noncommutative Schwarzschild black hole by two different methods in order to investigate and compare the results. Using the sixth-order WKB approximation and Leaver’s continuous fraction, we found that there is a small difference between the quasinormal frequencies obtained by each method mainly for small multipoles. The effects of the noncommutative parameter
\Theta
cause an increase in the real part of the quasinormal frequencies, while the magnitude of the imaginary part begins to grow and then decreases.
For the black hole shadow we use the results obtained by the WKB method to verify that in large
l
regimes the real part of quasinormal modes is inversely proportional to the shadow radius.
In addition, we have shown that the shadow radius is non-zero at the zero mass limit.
Therefore being proportional to a minimum mass.
However for
\theta=0
, we recover the shadow radius for the Schwarzschild black hole case. Finally, we also notice that the shadow radius is reduced when we increase the noncommutative parameter.

Normally located polyhedra
Ivan Arzhantsev

Abstract
. Lattice polyhedra Q 1 Q_{1} and Q 2 Q_{2} with the same tail cone are said to be normally located if every lattice point in the Minkowski sum Q 1 + Q 2 Q_{1}+Q_{2} is the sum of lattice points from Q 1 Q_{1} and Q 2 Q_{2} , respectively. We prove that if the normal fan of Q 1 Q_{1} refines the normal fan of Q 2 Q_{2} , then there is a positive integer k k such that for any positive integer s s the polyhedra s ​ k ​ Q 1 skQ_{1} and s ​ k ​ Q 2 skQ_{2} are normally located. This result is based on an interpretation of the problem in terms of graded algebras and earlier results on surjectivity of the multiplicaiton map on homogeneous components. Also we provide an example of two lattice triangles P P and Q Q on the plane such that for any positive integer k k the triangles k ​ P kP and k ​ Q kQ are not normally located.

1.
Introduction

Let us consider the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and the rational vector space
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
generated by
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. By a
lattice polytope
we mean a convex polytope
P
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
with vertices in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Let us assume additionally that the lattice points in
P
generate the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
; this can be achieved by replacing
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with a proper sublattice.

It is easy to construct examples of two lattice polytopes
P
and
Q
such that the Minkowski sum
P+Q
contains a lattice point that is not a sum of lattice points from
P
and
Q
, respective; see, e.g.,
[
8
]
. Moreover, starting from dimension
3
it may happen even when
P=Q
.

Definition 1
.

A lattice polytope
P
is called
normal
if for every positive integer
s
and every lattice point
z\in sP
there are lattice points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\in P
such that
z=z_{1}+\ldots+z_{s}
.

Normal polytopes play an important role in many areas of modern mathematics, see
[
5
]
for a recent survey on this subject. In particular, such polytopes define integrally closed graded monoid algebras and projectively normal embeddings of projective toric varieties.

Let us recall that a lattice polytope
P
is
smooth
if the primitive edge vectors at every vertex of
P
form a basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Smooth polytopes correspond to projective embeddings of smooth toric varieties. Oda’s question
[
5
, Section 1]
asks whether every smooth polytope is normal. This question is open in all dimensions
\geqslant 3
.

The proof of the following theorem may be found in
[
3
, Proposition 1.3.3]
or
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1
.

For every lattice polytope
P
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
the polytope
(d-1)P
is normal.

In particular, in dimension
2
any lattice polygon is normal.

The aim of this paper is to generalize the property of normality to a pair of polytopes.

Definition 2
.

Lattice polytopes
P
and
Q
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
are said to be
normally located
if for every lattice point
z\in P+Q
there are lattice points
z^{\prime}\in P
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in Q
such that
z=z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}
.

Oda’s Conjecture (see
[
8
]
or
[
6
, Section 1]
) states the following. Let
P
and
Q
be lattice polytopes. Assume that
P
is smooth and the normal fan of
Q
refines that of
P
. Then the polytopes
P
and
Q
are normally located. This is a generalization of Oda’s question on normality of a smooth polytope.

In
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, it is shown that if
P
and
Q
are lattice polygons such that the normal fan of
Q
refines that of
P
, then
P
and
Q
are normally located. In this paper we prove a version of Oda’s Conjecture (Theorem
3
) which generalizes
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
to higher dimensions. The result is obtained not only for polytopes, but also for polyhedra with the same tail cone.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give an explicit example of two lattice triangles
P
and
Q
on the plane such that for any positive integer
k
the triangles
kP
and
kQ
are not normally located. This example is intended to demonstrate that, unlike the normality property, the property of normal location cannot always be achieved just by rescaling two given polytopes.

In Section
3
we recall basic definitions and facts on polyhedra, their Minkowski sums and normal fans. Also we consider polyhedra that appear as fibers of a projection of the positive octant in a bigger lattice to a smaller lattice. Section
4
is devoted to interpretations of the objects defined above in terms of graded algebras and the multiplication map on homogeneous components. We recall the results of
[
1
]
that allow to relate the property of normal location (up to scalar) of a pair of polyhedra in fibers over two points with the location of these points with respect to the so-called GIT-fan. Finally, in Section
5
we explain how to realize a pair of polyhedra via the fiber construction and prove our main result (Theorem
3
).

2.
An example in dimension
2

Let us show that, in contrast to Theorem
1
, for two lattice triangles absence of the property of normal location is not just a question of scale.

Proposition 1
.

There are two lattice triangles
P
and
Q
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
such that for every positive integer
k
the triangles
kP
and
kQ
are not normally located.

Proof.

Take
P=\Conv(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
and
Q=\Conv(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
, where

a_{1}=(165,0),\ a_{2}=(175,0),\ a_{3}=(0,385)\quad\text{and}\quad b_{1}=(0,0),\ b_{2}=(35,0),\ b_{3}=(0,77).

Equivalently, the triangle
P
is given by inequalities

7x+3y\geqslant 1155,\quad 11x+5y\leqslant 1925,\quad y\geqslant 0

and
Q
is given by

11x+5y\leqslant 385,\quad x\geqslant 0,\quad y\geqslant 0.

The Minkowski sum
P+Q
is
\Conv(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})
, where

c_{1}=(165,0),\quad c_{2}=(210,0),\quad c_{3}=(0,385),\quad c_{4}=(0,462).

The inequalites that determine
P+Q
are

7x+3y\geqslant 1155,\quad 11x+5y\leqslant 2310,\quad x\geqslant 0,\quad y\geqslant 0.

It is easy to check that for every positive integer
k
the point
s=(1,385k-2)
is contained in
kP+kQ
.
Assume that
s=p+q
, where
p\in kP\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
q\in kQ\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. Then only two cases are possible.

Case 1
. Let
p=(1,a)
and
q=(0,385k-2-a)
for some non-negative integer
a
. Since
p
is contained in
P
, we have

7+3a\geqslant 1155k\quad\text{and}\quad 11+5a\leqslant 1925k.

These inequalities may be rewritten as

a\geqslant 385k-2-\frac{1}{3}\quad\text{and}\quad a\leqslant 385k-2-\frac{1}{5}.

Since
a
is integer, we come to a contradiction.

Case 2
. Let
p=(0,a)
and
q=(1,385k-2-a)
for some non-negative integer
a
. Since
p
is contained in
P
, we have

3a\geqslant 1155k\quad\text{and}\quad 5a\leqslant 1925k,

so

a\geqslant 385k\quad\text{and}\quad a\leqslant 385k.

We conclude that
a=385k
. Since
q
lies in
Q
, we have

385k-2-a=-2>0.

These two contradictions complete the proof.
∎

3.
Generalities on polyhedra

By a
polyhedron
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
we mean the intersection of finitely many closed affine half spaces. A
lattice polyhedron
is a polyhedron in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
whose vertices are in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Note that a polytope can be defined as a bounded polyhedron in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. For a polyhedron
Q
we define its
relative interior
as the set obtained by removing all proper faces from
Q
. Let us denote the relative interior of
Q
by
Q^{\circ}
. Further, a
cone
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
is the intersection of finitely many closed linear half spaces. A cone is
pointed
if it contains no line. If a cone has dimension at least
2
, it is pointed if and only if it is generated by its one-dimensional faces.

The set of all polyhedra in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
comes with a natural structure of a commutative semigroup: one defines the Minkowski sum of two polyhedra
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
to be the polyhedron

Q_{1}+Q_{2}:=\{w_{1}+w_{2};\ w_{1}\in Q_{1},w_{2}\in Q_{2}\}.

In the same way one may define the Minkowski sum of two arbitrary subsets in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.

Any polyhedron allows a Minkowski sum decomposition
Q=P+\sigma
, where
P
is a polytope and
\sigma
is a cone in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. In this decomposition, the
tail cone

\sigma
is unique; it is given by

\sigma=\{w\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{d};\ w^{\prime}+tw\in Q\ \text{for all}\ w^{\prime}\in Q,t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geqslant 0}\}.

A polyhedron with the tail cone
\sigma
is called a
\sigma
-polyhedron
. For example, polytopes are precisely
\sigma
-polyhedra with
\sigma=\{0\}
.

It is easy to check that for a fixed cone
\sigma
the set of all
\sigma
-polyhedra forms a commutative semigroup with respect to the Minkowski sum.

Let us recall that with any
\sigma
-polyhedron
Q
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
one may associate the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q)
: any point
v\in Q
defines the cone
\tau(v)
consisting of all linear functions
on
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
which reach their maximal value on
Q
at the point
v
. The collection of cones
\mathcal{N}(Q)=\{\tau(v)\,|\,v\in Q\}
is finite and it is a fan in a sense that a face of any cone in

\mathcal{N}(Q)
is contained in
\mathcal{N}(Q)
and the intersection of any two cones in
\mathcal{N}(Q)
is a face of each of them. Moreover, all cones in
\mathcal{N}(Q)
are pointed if and only if
Q
has full dimension in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.

The
support
of a fan
\mathcal{N}
is the union of all cones in
\mathcal{N}
. The support of the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q)
equals the dual cone

\sigma^{\lor}:=\{l\in({\mathbb{Q}}^{d})^{*}\ |\ l|_{\sigma}\geqslant 0\}.

We say that a fan
\mathcal{N}_{1}

refines
a fan
\mathcal{N}_{2}
, if every cone in
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is contained in some cone in
\mathcal{N}_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
be two fans with the same support. The
coarsest common refinement
of
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
is the fan
\mathcal{N}
with the same support, whose cones are
\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}
, where
\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
.

It is well-known that for normal fans
\mathcal{N}(Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{N}(Q_{2})
of two
\sigma
-polyhedra
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
the coarsest common refinement
\mathcal{N}
coincides with the normal fan

\mathcal{N}(Q_{1}+Q_{2})
.

Now let us consider a surjective homomorphism of lattices
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
and the induced linear map of vector spaces
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
. We denote by

{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
the cone of vectors in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
with non-negative coordinates and let
C:=\pi({\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0})\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
. Consider the cone

\sigma:=\pi^{-1}(0)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
. With any point
u\in C
one associates the polyhedron
P(u):=\pi^{-1}(u)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
u\in C
the polyhedron
P(u)
is a
\sigma
-polyhedron.

Proof.

For a vector
w\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
, the condition
w^{\prime}+tw\in P(u)
for all
w^{\prime}\in P(u)
and all
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geqslant 0}
means that
w\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
and

u=\pi(w^{\prime}+tw)=\pi(w^{\prime})+\pi(tw)=u+t\pi(w)
. It is equivalent to
w\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\pi(w)=0
, or
w\in\sigma
.
∎

Clearly, for every
u\in C
there is a positive integer
r
such that
P(ru)
is a lattice polyhedron. Also it is easy to check that
P(u_{1})+P(u_{2})
is contained in
P(u_{1}+u_{2})
for all

u_{1},u_{2}\in C
.

We are interested in the following three properties of a pair
(u_{1},u_{2})
with
u_{1},u_{2}\in C
:

(P1)
P(u_{1})+P(u_{2})=P(u_{1}+u_{2})
;

(P2)
(P(u_{1})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})+(P(u_{2})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=P(u_{1}+u_{2})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
;

(P3) there exists
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that for any
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
we have

(P(sku_{1})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})+(P(sku_{2})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=P(sk(u_{1}+u_{2}))\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

4.
Graded algebras

In this section we introduce an algebraic interpretation of the objects discussed above. The projection
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
gives rise to an effective
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading on the polynomial algebra
A:={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Namely, we put
\deg(x_{i})=\pi(e_{i})
, where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
is the standard basis of the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. For further purposes we assume the ground field
{\mathbb{K}}
to be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Remark 1
.

The Linearization Problem
[
7
]
claims that up to automorphism any effective
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading on
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is obtained this way.

Below we follow the presentation given in
[
1
]
in a somewhat more general situation. Let
A
be an associative, commutative, integral, finitely generated algebra with unit over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Suppose that
A
is graded by the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, i.e., we have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}A_{u}\ \ \ \ \text{with}\ \ \ \ A_{u_{1}}\cdot A_{u_{2}}\subseteq A_{u_{1}+u_{2}}.

By the
weight cone
of
A
we mean the cone
C(A)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
generated by all
u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
with
A_{u}\neq 0
. We investigate the following problem:
given
u_{1},u_{2}\in C(A)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, does there exist an integer
k>0
such that for any
s>0
the multiplication map

A_{sku_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}A_{sku_{2}}\ \to\ A_{sk(u_{1}+u_{2})},\qquad f\otimes g\ \mapsto\ fg

is surjective? We call a pair
u_{1},u_{2}\in C(A)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}

generating
if it has this property. If
A
is a polynomial algebra with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading given by the projection
\pi
, this is precisely property (P3) for a pair
(u_{1},u_{2})
of lattice points in
C
.

Let us recall from
[
2
]
the concept of the GIT-fan associated to a graded algebra. A
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading on
A
defines an action of the torus
T:=\Spec({\mathbb{K}}[{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}])
on
X:=\Spec(A)
such that for any
u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, the elements
f\in A_{u}
are precisely the semiinvariants of the character
\chi^{u}\colon T\to{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
, i.e., each
f\in A_{u}
satisfies

\displaystyle f(t\!\cdot\!x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\chi^{u}(t)f(x).

The
orbit cone
of a (closed) point
x\in X
is the cone
\omega(x)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
generated by all
u\in C(A)
admitting an
f\in A_{u}
with
f(x)\neq 0
.
The collection of orbit cones is finite, and thus one may associate to any element
u\in C(A)
its
GIT-cone
:

\displaystyle\lambda(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X,\\
u\in\omega(x)\end{subarray}}\omega(x).

These GIT-cones cover the weight cone
C(A)
and, by
[
2
, Theorem 3.11]
, the collection
\Lambda(A)
of all GIT-cones is a fan in the sense that if
\lambda\in\Lambda(A)
then
also every face of
\lambda
belongs to
\Lambda(A)
, and for
\tau,\lambda\in\Lambda(A)
the intersection
\tau\cap\lambda
is a face of both
\lambda
and
\tau
.
Note that we allow here a fan to have cones containing lines.

Theorem 2
.

[
1
, Theorem 1.1]

(1)

If
u_{1},u_{2}\in C(A)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
is a generating pair, then the weights
u_{1},u_{2}
lie in a common GIT-cone
\lambda\in\Lambda(A)
.

(2)

If
u_{1},u_{2}\in C(A)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
lie in a common GIT-cone
\lambda\in\Lambda(A)
and
u_{1}
belongs to the relative interior
\lambda^{\circ}
, then
(u_{1},u_{2})
is a generating pair.

If two weights
u_{1},u_{2}\in C(A)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
lie on the boundary of a common GIT-cone
\lambda\in\Lambda(A)
, then no general statement in terms of the GIT-fan is possible:
it may happen that
u_{1},u_{2}
is generating, and also it may happen that
u_{1},u_{2}
is not generating. For the first case there are obvious examples, and for the latter we present the following one.

Example 1
.

[
1
, Example 1.2]

Consider the polynomial ring
A:={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]
. Then one may define a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-grading on
A

by setting

\deg(x_{1})\ :=\ (4,1),\quad\deg(x_{2})\ :=\ (2,1),\quad\deg(x_{3})\ :=\ (1,2),\quad\deg(x_{4})\ :=\ (1,3).

The pair
u_{1}:=(2,1)
and
u_{2}:=(1,2)
is contained in a common GIT-cone but it is not generating:
one checks directly that the monomials
x_{1}x_{2}^{s-2}x_{3}^{s-1}x_{4}\in A_{s(u_{1}+u_{2})}
can never be obtained by multiplying elements from

A_{su_{1}}
and
A_{su_{2}}
.

Remark 2
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
, a criterion for a pair of weights
(u_{1},u_{2})
in one GIT-cone to be generating is given in terms of normality of the image of a morphism between certain quotient spaces.

The proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
is based on several propositions. A modification of one of them will be used below. To formulate this modification, we need some more notions from the theory of graded algebras.

A subalgebra
B
of a graded algebra
A=\oplus_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}A_{u}
is
homogeneous
if
B
is the direct sum of intersections of
B
with homogeneous components of
A
. Every homogeneous subalgebra in
A
inherits a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading.

With any weight
u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
one associates a homogeneous subalgebra
A(u):=\oplus_{r\geqslant 0}A_{ru}
. Note that the subalgebra
A(u)
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
-graded.

Further, with any weights
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
one associates a homogeneous subalgebra
A(u_{1},u_{2})
in
A(u_{1}+u_{2})
defined as

A(u_{1},u_{2}):=\bigoplus_{r\geqslant 0}A_{ru_{1}}\cdot A_{ru_{2}}.

We say that a homogeneous subalgebra
B
of a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
-graded algebra
A
is
big
, if the radical of the ideal
B_{+}:=\oplus_{r>0}B_{r}
coincides with
A_{+}:=\oplus_{r>0}A_{r}
.

Let
A
be the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading given by a projection
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. For every
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
we denote by
x^{v}
the monomial
x_{1}^{v_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{v_{n}}
.

Proposition 2
.

Condition (P1) on a pair of weights
(u_{1},u_{2})
is equivalent to each of the conditions:

(1)

the weights
u_{1},u_{2}
lie in a common GIT-cone;

(2)

the subalgebra
A(u_{1},u_{2})
is big in
A(u_{1}+u_{2})
.

Proof.

The equivalence of conditions (1) and (2) is proved in
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Let us prove that (P1) implies (2). Assume that
P(u_{1}+u_{2})=P(u_{1})+P(u_{2})
and take a monomial
x^{v}\in A(u_{1}+u_{2})
. We have to prove that
x^{v}
is contained in the radical of the ideal
A(u_{1},u_{2})_{+}
, i.e., there is a positive integer
q
such that
x^{qv}\in A(u_{1},u_{2})_{+}
. By assumption, we have
v=v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}
with
v^{\prime}\in P(u_{1})
and
v^{\prime\prime}\in P(u_{2})
. Then there is
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
qv=qv^{\prime}+qv^{\prime\prime}
with
qv^{\prime}\in P(u_{1})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
qv^{\prime\prime}\in P(u_{2})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. This proves the assertion.

Now we come to implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(P1). Note that
v\in P(u_{1}+u_{2})
if and only if there is
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
sv\in P(s(u_{1}+u_{2}))\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
or, equivalently, the monomial
x^{sv}
lies in
A(u_{1}+u_{2})_{+}
. By assumption, this implies that
x^{sv}
is contained in the radical of
A(u_{1},u_{2})_{+}
, or there exists
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that

x^{tsv}\in A(u_{1},u_{2})_{+}
. The later condition means that there are
v^{\prime}\in P(tsu_{1})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
v^{\prime\prime}\in P(tsu_{2})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
tsv=v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}
. This condition implies
v\in P(u_{1})+P(u_{2})
.
∎

5.
Positive results on normal location

We keep the notation introduced in the previous section. In particular, every surjective homomorphism of lattices
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
gives rise to a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
-grading on the algebra

{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and we speak about the weight cone, the orbit cones and the GIT-cones corresponding to this grading. In this situation, the weight cone coincides with

C=\pi({\mathbb{Q}}^{n}_{\geqslant 0})
and the orbits cones are precisely the cones generated by all subsets of the set
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}
, where
w_{i}:=\pi(e_{i})
.

Let
\sigma
be a pointed cone in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. Taking an appropriate basis in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
we may assume that
\sigma
is contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}
. Moreover, to any
\sigma
-polyhedra
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
we may apply a parallel translation and assume that
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are contained in the open octant
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}_{>0}
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be two
\sigma
-polyhedra in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
of full dimension. Then there are positive integers
n
and
m
with
d=n-m
and a surjective homomorphism
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
such that
Q_{1}=P(u_{1})
and
Q_{2}=P(u_{2})
for some points
u_{1},u_{2}\in C\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
lying in a common GIT-cone.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}(Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{N}(Q_{2})
be the normal fans of the polyhedra
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, respectively. Denote by
\mathcal{N}
the coarsest common refinement of the fans
\mathcal{N}(Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{N}(Q_{2})
.

Let
m
be the number of rays of the fan
\mathcal{N}
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}
be linear functions on
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
generating the rays of
\mathcal{N}
. Denote by
a_{i}
(resp. by
b_{i}
) the maximal value of
l_{i}
on
Q_{1}
(resp. on
Q_{2})
. Then the polyhedron
Q_{1}
(resp.
Q_{2}
) is given by inequalities
l_{i}\leqslant a_{i}
(resp.
l_{i}\leqslant b_{i}
) with
i=1,\ldots,m
. The fan
\mathcal{N}
is the normal fan of the polyhedron
Q_{1}+Q_{2}
, so the polyhedron
Q_{1}+Q_{2}
is given by inequalities
l_{i}\leqslant a_{i}+b_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

We may assume that the linear functions
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}
have integer coefficients. Let
n=d+m
and consider the projection
\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
given by

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\to(y_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,y_{m}+l_{m}).

Let
u_{1}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
. Then the polyhedron
P(u_{1})
is given by conditions

x_{1}\geqslant 0,\ldots,x_{d}\geqslant 0,\ y_{1}\geqslant 0,\ldots,y_{m}\geqslant 0,\ y_{1}+l_{1}=a_{1},\ldots,y_{m}+l_{m}=a_{m}.

This system is equivalent to

x_{1}\geqslant 0,\ldots,x_{d}\geqslant 0,\ l_{1}\leqslant a_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\leqslant a_{m}.

It proves that
P(u_{1})=Q_{1}
. The same arguments show that

P(u_{2})=Q_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad P(u_{1}+u_{2})=Q_{1}+Q_{2}.

Finally, by Proposition
2
the condition

P(u_{1})+P(u_{2})=P(u_{1}+u_{2})
implies that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
lie in a common GIT-cone.
∎

Let us denote by
\mathcal{N}_{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{2}
) the normal fan of the polyhedron
P(u_{1})
(resp.
P(u_{2})
) living in the space
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.

Proposition 4
.

The fan
\mathcal{N}_{1}
refines the fan
\mathcal{N}_{2}
if and only if
u_{1}
is an interior point of a GIT-cone containing
u_{2}
.

Proof.

For any point
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
, we define a subset in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
as

Z(c)=\{i\ |\ c_{i}=0\}.

The condition that
u_{1}
is an interior point of a GIT-cone containing
u_{2}
means that every orbit cone, which contains
u_{1}
, contains
u_{2}
as well. Note that the coordinates of a point
in
P(u_{1})
(resp.
P(u_{2})
) may be considered as coefficients of a linear combination of the vectors
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
that is equal to
u_{1}
(resp.
u_{2}
). Taking this into account, we may reformulate the above condition as: for every point
v_{1}\in P(u_{1})
there is a point
v_{2}\in P(u_{2})
such that
Z(v_{1})\subseteq Z(v_{2})
.

Since we assume that the polyhedra are contained in the open octant
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}_{>0}
, the coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
are positive on
P(u_{1})
and
P(u_{2})
. At the same time, for
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
the coordinate
y_{i}
is zero at a point
v_{1}
(resp.
v_{2}
) if and only if the linear function
l_{i}
reaches its maximum
a_{i}
on
P(u_{1})
at
v_{1}
(resp.
b_{i}
on
P(u_{2})
at
v_{2}
). The condition
Z(v_{1})\subseteq Z(v_{2})
means that every function
l_{i}
which is contained in the cone
\tau(v_{1})
of the normal fan
\mathcal{N}_{1}
(see Section
3
) is also contained in the cone
\tau(v_{2})
of the normal fan
\mathcal{N}_{2}
. Since the rays of the fans
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
are generated by some of the vectors
l_{i}
and every cone is uniquely determined by the set of rays it contains, the last condition means that every cone in
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is contained in a cone of
\mathcal{N}_{2}
or, equivalently, the fan
\mathcal{N}_{1}
refines the fan
\mathcal{N}_{2}
.
∎

The next theorem generalizes
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
from dimension
2
to higher dimensions.

Theorem 3
.

Let
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
be two
\sigma
-polyhedra in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
of full dimension. Assume that the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q_{1})
refines the normal fan
\mathcal{N}(Q_{2})
. Then there exists a positive integer
k
such that for every positive integer
s
the polyhedra
skQ_{1}
and
skQ_{2}
are normally located.

Proof.

Following Proposition
3
, we realize
Q_{1}
(resp.
Q_{2}
) as
P(u_{1})
(resp.
P(u_{2})
). By Theorem
2
, it suffices to show that the point
u_{1}
lies in the relative interior
of a common GIT-cone of the points
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. It follows from Proposition
4
.
∎

P=P(u_{1})
and
Q=P(u_{2})

\pi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

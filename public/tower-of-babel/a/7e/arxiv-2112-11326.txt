Relative entropy, Gaussian concentration and uniqueness of equilibrium states
J.-R. Chazottes, F. Redig

Abstract
For a general class of lattice-spin systems, we prove that an abstract Gaussian concentration bound implies positivity of lower entropy density. As a consequence we obtain uniqueness of translation-invariant Gibbs measures from the Gaussian concentration bound in this general setting. This extends earlier results from [ 4 ] with a different and very short proof.



1
Introduction

In
[
4
]
we studied the relation between the Gaussian concentration bound and the uniqueness of equilibrium
states (translation-invariant Gibbs measures) in the context of spin systems on the lattice
\mathds{Z}^{d}
, where the spin at each lattice site takes a finite
number of values. In this note we prove in a much more general setting that the Gaussian concentration bound implies the strict positivity of the lower relative entropy density. More
precisely, we obtain a lower bound for lower relative entropy density in terms of a natural distance between translation-invariant probability measures, reminiscent and in the spirit of the
result of Bobkov and Götze
[
2
]
who proved (in a different setting) a lower bound for the relative entropy in terms
of the square of the Wasserstein distance.
Because we work in the thermodynamic limit and are interested in translation-invariant probability measures, there is no translation-invariant distance on the configuration space for which we can apply Bobkov-Götze
theorem. We can avoid this problem by introducing a suitable distance on translation-invariant probability measures (rather than on configurations).

We start by proving the lower bound on the lower relative entropy density in the context of general lattice spin systems with state
space
\Omega=S^{\mathds{Z}^{d}}
where
the single spins take values in a metric space
S
of bounded diameter.
The bounded diameter property allows to associate to a quasi-local function
f
a natural sequence of oscillations
\delta_{i}f,i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
, where
\delta_{i}f
represents
the maximal influence on the function
f
of a change of the spin at site
i
.
In the final section of this paper, we provide a generalization of this by allowing more abstract single spin spaces, and more general
associated sequences of oscillations
\delta_{i}f,i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
.

The rest of our paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we introduce the basic setting of lattice spin systems and important function spaces. In Section 3 we introduce the Gaussian concentration bound, relative entropy (density) and formulate and prove our main result in the context of a single spin space with finite diameter. We discuss applications of our result to zero information distance sets, including e.g. the set of equilibrium states w.r.t.
absolutely summable translation-invariant potential.
In Section 4 we consider a generalization introducing an abstract sequence of oscillations, associated Gaussian concentration bound and state and proof the analogue of our main result in this generalized context.
In Section 5 we give various examples.

Local oscillations and function spaces

To a continuous function

\delta f:=(\delta_{i}f)_{i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}

\delta_{i}f=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\eta\,\in\,\Omega:\\
\sigma_{j}=\eta_{j},\forall j\not=i\end{subarray}}\big(f(\sigma)-f(\eta)\big).

(1)

For an integer

\|\delta f\|^{p}_{p}=\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}(\delta_{i}f)^{p},

We call a continuous function local if
\delta_{i}f\not=0

If

We denote by

For

\Delta^{p}(\Omega)=\{f\in{\mathscr{C}}(\Omega):\|\delta f\|_{p}<\infty\}.

In what follows we will assume that the set of bounded quasi-local functions is measure separating, i.e.,
for two probability measures
\mu\not=\mu^{\prime}
we have
that there exists a bounded quasi-local
f
such that

\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\neq\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}.

Because by definition bounded quasi-local functions can be uniformly approximated by bounded local functions, if the set of bounded quasi-local functions is measure separating
then also the set of bounded local functions is measure separating, i.e., for two probability measures
\mu\not=\mu^{\prime}
we have
that there exists a bounded local
f
(which is not constant) such that

\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\not=\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}.

We say that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
if for all bounded local functions we have
\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
(then by definition of quasilocality, the same holds for bounded quasi-local functions). This induces the so-called
weak quasi-local topology on probability measures. Notice that in our setting where by assumption the single spin space
S
is complete metric and separable, this topology coincides with the ordinary weak
topology, see
[
6
, p. 898]
.

3
Gaussian concentration bound and relative entropy

3.1
Abstract Gaussian concentration bound

We can now give the definition of the Gaussian concentration bound in our setting.

D
EFINITION
3.1
.

Let
\Omega=S^{\mathds{Z}^{d}}
where
S
is a standard Borel space with finite diameter.
Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\Omega
.
We say that
\mu
satisfies the Gaussian concentration bound with constant
C>0
, abbreviated
\mathrm{GCB}\!\left(C\right)
, if for all bounded local functions
f

we have

\int\e^{f-\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\e^{\tfrac{C}{2}\|\delta f\|^{2}_{2}}.

(4)

R
EMARK
3.1
.

(a)

Observe that the bound (
4
) does not change if
f
is replaced by
f+c
, where
c\in\mathds{R}
is arbitrary, since
\delta_{i}(f+c)=\delta_{i}(f)
for any
i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
. This “insensitivity” to constant offsets
in the left-hand side is ensured by the fact that we center
f
around its expected value. Also observe that (
4
) is trivially true for functions which are constant.

(b)

We have
\delta_{i}(\beta f)=|\beta|\,\delta_{i}f
, for all
i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
and
\beta\in\mathds{R}
, we thus have

\log\int\e^{\beta(f-\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq{\tfrac{C\beta^{2}}{2}\|\delta f\|^{2}_{2}},\;\forall\beta\in\mathds{R}.

This quadratic upper bound will be crucial in the sequel.

The following proposition shows that (
4
) automatically extends to a wider class of functions.

P
ROPOSITION
3.1

(Self-enhancement of GCB)
.

Suppose that (
4
) holds for all bounded local
f
. Then it holds for all
f\in\mathcal{QL}(\Omega)\cap\Delta^{2}(\Omega)
.

P
ROOF
.  By assumption, for
\xi\in\Omega
fixed,
f\in\mathcal{QL}(\Omega)\cap\Delta^{2}(\Omega)
can be uniformly approximated by the local functions

f_{\Lambda,\xi}(\eta)=f(\eta_{\Lambda}\xi_{\Lambda^{c}}).

By definition (
1
),
\delta_{i}f_{\Lambda,\xi}
is non-decreasing when
\Lambda
grows, and is bounded by

\delta_{i}f
.
By lemma
2.1
, it follows that
f_{\Lambda,\xi}
is bounded.

Therefore
\|\delta f_{\Lambda,\xi}\|_{2}
is bounded by
\|\delta f\|_{2}
which is finite because
f\in\Delta^{2}(\Omega)
.
Therefore, using the assumed uniform convergence of
f_{\Lambda,\xi}
to
f
, and the assumed bound (
4
) for bounded local functions
in
\Delta^{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain (using dominated convergence)

\int\e^{f-\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathds{Z}^{d}}\int\e^{f_{\Lambda,\xi}-\int f_{\Lambda,\xi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathds{Z}^{d}}\e^{\tfrac{C}{2}\|\delta f_{\Lambda,\xi}\|^{2}_{2}}=\e^{\tfrac{C}{2}\|\delta f\|^{2}_{2}}.

\;\qed

(10)

4
Applications: uniqueness of equilibrium states and beyond

In this subsection we provide some settings where we can conclude uniqueness of a set of “(generalized) translation-invariant Gibbs measures”
via Theorem
3.1
. We start with the set of translation-invariant Gibbs measures associated to an absolutely summable potential.
Then we consider generalizations and modifications of such sets.

4.1
Uniqueness of equilibrium states

In this subsection we briefly introduce the necessary basics of Gibbs measures. The reader familiar with
the theory of Gibbs measure can skip this subsection.
The reader is referred to
[
7
]
(especially chapter 16) or
[
6
, Chapter 2]
for more background on the Gibbs formalism.

Let
\lambda
be a probability measure on
S
, and for
\lambda_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda})=\otimes_{i\in\Lambda}\lambda(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i})
the corresponding
product measure on
S^{\Lambda}
.
The measure
\lambda
is called the “a priori” measure on
S
, with associated a priori measure
\otimes_{i\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}\lambda(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i})
on
\Omega
.

We call a uniformly absolutely summable translation-invariant potential a function

U:\mathcal{S}\times\Omega\to\mathds{R}

such that

(a)

Local potentials: for all
A\in\mathcal{S}
,
U(A,\cdot)
is
\mathfrak{B}\!_{A}
-measurable and continuous.

(b)

Absolutely summable:
\sum_{A\ni 0}\|U(A,\cdot)\|_{\infty}<\infty

(c)

Translation invariant:
U(A+i,\tau_{i}\sigma)=U(A,\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in\Omega
,
A\in\mathcal{S}
.

Let us call
{\mathcal{U}}
the
set of uniformly absolutely summable translation invariant potentials.
Then we build the local Gibbs measures with boundary condition
\xi\in\Omega
. For a finite subset
\Lambda\in\mathcal{S}
, the Gibbs measure in volume

\Lambda
with boundary condition
\xi
outside
\Lambda
is defined via

\gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda}|\xi)=\frac{\e^{-H^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\Lambda})}}{Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}}\lambda_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda})

where
H^{\xi}_{\Lambda}
is the Hamiltonian in volume
\Lambda
with boundary condition
\xi

H_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\sigma_{\Lambda})=\sum_{A\cap\Lambda\not=\emptyset}U(A,\sigma_{\Lambda}\xi_{\Lambda^{c}})

and where
{Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}}
is the normalization

{Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}}=\int{\e^{-H^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{\Lambda})}}\lambda_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda}).

The family
\gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda}|\cdot),\Lambda\in\mathcal{S}
is called the Gibbsian specification associated to
the potential
U
(with a priori measure
\lambda
).

By the uniform absolute summability of
U
, we automatically have that for all

f
local and continuous,
the function
\xi\mapsto\int f(\sigma_{\Lambda}\xi_{\Lambda^{c}})\gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda}|\xi)
is quasi-local and continuous.
We say that the specification

We then call a measure Gibbs

with potential
U
(and a priori measure

\gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda}|\xi)
if it

We denote by

The variational principle (

4.2
Sets of zero information distance

The example of the set of equilibrium states from the previous subsection leads naturally to the more general notion of “zero information distance sets” defined below.

D
EFINITION
4.1
.

We
call a subset
{\mathscr{K}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}(\Omega)
a zero information distance set if
for all
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{K}}
,
s_{*}(\mu|\mu^{\prime})=s_{*}(\mu^{\prime}|\mu)=0
.

From Theorem
3.1
we then obtain immediately the following proposition.

P
ROPOSITION
4.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{K}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}(\Omega)
be a zero information distance set. If there exists
\mu\in{\mathscr{K}}
which
satisfies
\mathrm{GCB}\!\left(C\right)
for some
C\in(0,\infty)
, then
{\mathscr{K}}
is a singleton.

We now give three further examples (beyond equilibrium states) of such zero information distance sets, illustrating
Proposition
4.2
.

a)

Asymptotically decoupled measures and
\Pi^{f}
-compatible measures.
A first generalization of the Gibbsian context is provided in the realm of “asymptotically decoupled measures” via the notion of
\Pi^{f}
-compatible measures, see

[
9
]
. This setting goes beyond quasi-local specifications and therefore includes many relevant examples of non-Gibbsian measures.

In this setting the set of
\Pi^{f}
-compatible measures (associated to a local function
f
) is a zero information set
((see
[
9
]
Theorem 4.1), and therefore, if this set contains an element
\mu
satisfying

\mathrm{GCB}\!\left(C\right)
, then it coincides with the singleton
\{\mu\}
.

b)

Renormalization group transformations of Gibbs measures.

Another important class of examples is the following. We say that a transformation
T:{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}(\Omega^{\prime})
preserves
zero information distance sets if a zero information distance set is mapped by
T
onto a zero information distance set.
Important examples of such transformations
T
are local and translation invariant renormalization group transformations studied in
[
6
]
, section 3.1, p 960, conditions T1-T2-T3. Examples of such transformations include block-spin averaging, decimation and stochastic transformations such as the Kadanoff transformation. Because the transformations are “local and translation invariant probability kernels”, one immediately infers the property
s_{*}(\mu T|\nu T)\leq s_{*}(\mu|\nu)
.

In this setting,
Proposition
4.2
implies that if
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
,
\mu\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau}(U)

\mu^{\prime}T=\mu T
for all
\mu^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau}(U)
. Indeed, in that case

s_{*}(\mu^{\prime}T|\mu T)\leq s_{*}(\mu^{\prime}|\mu)=0
.

Notice that
\mu T
can be non-Gibbs, therefore the implication

\nu=\mu T
for all
\nu
such that
s_{*}(\nu|\mu T)=0

c)

Stationary measures for Ising spin Glauber dynamics.

An additional example of a zero information distance sets is the set of stationary and translation-invariant measures for a (Ising spin, i.e.,

S
is finite) Glauber dynamics under the condition that this set contains at least one translation-invariant Gibbs measure as stationary measure, see
[
8
]
, Section 4.
As a consequence of Proposition
4.2
we then conclude that if there exists a translation-invariant Gibbs measure

Moreover, in this setting one can show that when starting the dynamics from a translation invariant initial measure

In the setting of Section

We consider now a general standard Borel

In order to proceed, we therefore associate to a function

D

.

We say that a map

1.

Translation invariance:
(\delta(\tau_{i}f))_{j}=\delta_{i+j}f

2.

Non-degeneracy:

3.

Monotonicity:
for

Then we require that for all

\delta_{i}f_{\Lambda}\leq\delta_{i}f
.

4.

Degree one homogeneity:
\delta_{i}(\beta f)=|\beta|\delta_{i}f

\beta\in\mathds{R}

\xi

The most natural examples different from (

More generally one can define

\delta_{i}f=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\eta\,\in\,\Omega:\\

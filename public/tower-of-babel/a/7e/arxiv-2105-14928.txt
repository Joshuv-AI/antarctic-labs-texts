Abstract
. This paper introduces the notion of pseudo-independence on the sublinear expectation space ( Ω , ℱ , 𝒫 ) (\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}) via the classical conditional expectation, and the relations between pseudo-independence and Peng’s independence are detailed discussed. Law of large numbers and central limit theorem for pseudo-independent random variables are obtained, and a purely probabilistic proof of Peng’s law of large numbers is also given. In the end, some relevant counterexamples are indicated that Peng’s law of large numbers and central limit theorem are invalid only with the first and second moment conditions respectively.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 60A05; Secondary 60F05

1.
Introduction

The notion of independence is very important in the classical probability theory, especially in the limit theorems, which was builded on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, where the classical probability
P
is additive. However, in some
areas this apparently natural property has been abandoned. Non-additive probabilities or nonlinear expectations as the useful tools for studying uncertainties have received more and more attention in many fields, such as mathematical economics, statistics, quantum mechanics and finance. One typical example of nonlinear expectations is the sublinear expectation, Peng
[
10
]
systematically studied the sublinear expectation theory and related stochastic analysis. In particular, the new notions of independence and identical distribution (i.i.d. for short) are introduced in Peng
[
10
]
and the related law of large numbers (LLN for short) and central limit theorem (CLT for short) under sublinear expectation are obtained. These notions and theorems are builded on the sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
, where
\mathcal{H}
is a linear space of random variables satisfying certain “closure property” for a class of continuous functions and
\mathbb{E}
is a sublinear functional on
\mathcal{H}
.

In most situations, the sublinear expectation can be represented as the supremum of a set
\mathcal{P}
of linear expectations, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[X]
, see Denis et al.
[
2
]
or Peng
[
10
]
. The sublinear expectation space of this paper is slightly different from the original one in Peng
[
10
]
. We consider
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
as the sublinear expectation space, where
\mathcal{P}
is a set of probability measures on the measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
. Following Peng’s idea for the notion of independence under sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
, we reconsider this notion on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
, in particular, the set
\mathcal{P}
can be constructed such that Peng’s independence also holds under the sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
introduced by
\mathcal{P}
. We first introduce the notion of pseudo-independence under sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
via conditional expectations. Then we can enlarge
\mathcal{P}
to
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
such that pseudo-independent sequence becomes the independent one under
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
. Thanks to the existing results of LLN and CLT, we can easily obtain the corresponding LLN and CLT for pseudo-independent sequence. In particular, a purely probabilistic proof of Peng’s LLN for i.i.d. sequence is given. But unlike the classical limit theorems, LLN and CLT for i.i.d. sequence may fail only with the finite first and second moment conditions respectively.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes some basic concepts and notations of the sublinear expectation theory. In Section 3, the properties of pseudo-independent sequence are discussed. LLN and CLT for pseudo-independent random variables are proved in Section 4 and Section 5. The relevant counterexamples of LLN and CLT are provided in Section 6.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a complete separable metric space
\Omega
, let
\mathcal{F}
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\Omega
and
\mathcal{M}
be the collection of all probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
. For a subset
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{M}
, the upper expectation of
\mathcal{P}
is defined as follows: for each random variable
X
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[X].

It is easy to verify that
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
is a sublinear expectation, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies following properties:

(i)

Monotonicity:
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y],
if
X\leq Y
,

(ii)

Constant preserving:
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[c]=c,\forall c\in\mathbb{R}
,

(iii)

Sub-additivity:
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X+Y]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]+\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y]
,

(iv)

Positive homogeneity:
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\lambda X]=\lambda\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X],\forall\lambda\geq 0
.

For each
A\in\mathcal{F}
, the upper and lower probabilities are defined corresponding to the set of probability measures
\mathcal{P}
respectively as follows:

V(A):=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P(A)

v(A):=\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P(A).

Obviously,
V(\cdot)
and
v(\cdot)
are conjugate to each other, that is

V(A)+v\left(A^{c}\right)=1.

The following notions of identical distribution and independence on the sublinear expectation space are initiated by Peng
[
9
]
(see also Peng
[
8
,
10
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two random variables on sublinear expectation spaces
(\Omega_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1},{\mathcal{P}_{1}})
and
(\Omega_{2},\mathcal{F}_{2},{\mathcal{P}_{2}})
respectively.
X
and
Y
are called identically distributed, if for each
\varphi\in C_{b,{Lip}}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{1}}[\varphi(X)]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{2}}[\varphi(Y)],

where
C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
is the set of all bounded and Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

It is worth pointing out that if
X
and
Y
are identical distributed by Definition
2.1
, we can not obtain that
V_{1}(X\in A)=V_{2}(Y\in A),\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
in general, where
V_{i}
is introduced by
\mathcal{P}_{i},i=1,2.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})=([0,1],\mathcal{B}[0,1])
,
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{\delta_{x},x\in[0,1)\}
,
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{\delta_{x},x\in[0,1]\}
, where
\delta_{x}
is Dirac measure at
x
. Consider the random variables
X
and
Y
defined by
X(\omega)=Y(\omega)=\omega
. Then for each continuous function
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{1}}[\varphi(X)]=\max_{x\in[0,1]}\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{2}}[\varphi(Y)],

but we have

V_{1}(X=1)=0\neq 1=V_{2}(X=1).

In particular, if we further assume that both
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are weakly compact, we can deduce that
V_{1}(X\in A)=V_{2}(Y\in A),\ \forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, see Proposition 14 in Hu and Peng
[
5
]
or Proposition 2.8 in Li
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
.
X_{n}
is said to be independent of
\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1}\right)
under
\mathcal{P}
, if for each
\varphi\in C_{b,\operatorname{Lip}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\left.\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},X_{n}\right)\right]\right|_{\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\right)=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1}\right)}\right].

The sequence of random variables
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is said to be independent, if
X_{n+1}
is independent of
\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the sublinear expectation space, there also exists
G
-normal distribution which plays an important role as an analogue of the normal distribution in the classical probability theory.

Definition 2.4
.

A random variable
\xi
on a sublinear expectation space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
is called

G
-normal distributed, denoted by

\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0;[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}])
, for a given pair

0\leq\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}<\infty
, if for each
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
, the following
function defined by

u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x+\sqrt{t}\xi)],(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

is the unique viscosity solution of the following parabolic partial
differential equation defined on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
:

\partial_{t}u-G(\partial^{2}_{xx}u)=0,u|_{t=0}=\varphi,

where

G(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}(\overline{\sigma}^{2}\alpha^{+}-\underline{\sigma}^{2}\alpha^{-}),\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, if
\overline{\sigma}=\underline{\sigma}
, then
\xi
defined above is the classical normal distribution.

3.
Properties of pseudo-independent random variables

Recently, Guo and Li
[
4
]
(see also Li
[
6
]
) introduced the concept of pseudo-independence, which characterizes the relations between classical conditional expectation and sublinear expectation.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
. Define

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right),n\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}.

If for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

(3.1)

E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(X_{n}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi\left(X_{n}\right)\right]\quad P\text{-a.s.},\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{b,\operatorname{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}),

then we call that
X_{n}
is pseudo-independent of
\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1}\right)
.

The pseudo-independent sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
can be defined in the same manner as the independent sequence.

Remark 3.2
.

It is worth pointing out that (
3.1
) is equivalent to

-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[-\varphi\left(X_{n}\right)\right]\leq E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(X_{n}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi\left(X_{n}\right)\right],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b,\operatorname{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}).

In particular, if
\mathcal{P}=\{P\}
is a singleton set, as shown in the classical probability theory,
X
being independent of the
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}
is equivalent to
E_{P}[\varphi(X)|\mathcal{F}]=E_{P}[\varphi(X)]
for each test function
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
. However, pseudo-independence means that the conditional expectation
E[\varphi(X)|\mathcal{F}]
may not exactly equal to the unconditional expectation
E_{P}[\varphi(X)]
, but lie in a non-random interval depending on
\varphi
.

Here is an important property of these concepts, which has been proved in Guo and Li
[
4
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

An independent sequence of random variables
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
on the sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
is pseudo-independent.

Pseudo-independence presents more extensive application scenarios than the independence in sublinear expectation space proposed earlier. For instance, independence under each probability
P
of
\mathcal{P}
apparently implies pseudo-independence, but usually can not guarantee independence in Definition
2.3
, see Example
3.6
in the end of this section. In general, we have the following properties.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a pseudo-independent sequence under
\mathcal{P}
, then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},X_{n+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},X_{n+1})]|_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})}].

Proof.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},X_{n+1})]=

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n+1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[E_{P}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},X_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]|_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},X_{n+1})]_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},X_{n+1})]|_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})}].

∎

Proposition
3.4
inspires us to enlarge
\mathcal{P}
to
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is independent under
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a pseudo-independent sequence under
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
. We consider
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
defined by

\bar{\mathcal{P}}=\{P\in\mathcal{M}:\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}),E_{P}[\varphi(X_{n})|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{n})],\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}

then we have
\bar{\mathcal{P}}\supset\mathcal{P}
and
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is independent under
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}
with
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(X_{n})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{n})],\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
.

Moreover,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n+1})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},X_{n+1})]|_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})}].

Proof.

For simplicity, we only consider the case of
n=2
. We consider

\bar{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P:\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}),E_{P}[\varphi(X_{1})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{1})]\ \text{and}\ E_{P}[\varphi(X_{2})|X_{1}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{2})]\}.

In this case, for each fixed
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exits
P^{*}\in\bar{\mathcal{P}}
(depending on
\varphi
and
\varepsilon
) such that

(i)
E_{P^{*}}[\psi(X_{1})]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\psi(X_{1})]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, where
\psi(x)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})]
.

(ii)
E_{P^{*}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})|X_{1}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, for all
x
belongs to the range of
X_{1}
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(X_{1},X_{2})]\geq

\displaystyle E_{P^{*}}[\varphi(X_{1},X_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P^{*}}[E_{P^{*}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})|X_{1}]_{x=X_{1}}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle E_{P^{*}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})]|_{x=X_{1}}]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})]|_{x=X_{1}}]-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(x,X_{2})]|_{x=X_{1}}]-\varepsilon.

Combining with Proposition
3.4
and noticing that
\varepsilon
can be arbitrarily small, we see that
X_{2}
is independent of
X_{1}
under
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
.
∎

We conclude this section by an example.

Example 3.6
.

We consider a probability set
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
and two binary random variables
X
and
Y
satisfying

\displaystyle P_{1}(X=0,Y=0)=\frac{1}{16},\ P_{1}(X=1,Y=1)=\frac{9}{16}

\displaystyle P_{1}(X=1,Y=0)=P_{1}(X=0,Y=1)=\frac{3}{16}

\displaystyle P_{2}(X=0,Y=0)=P_{2}(X=1,Y=1)=P_{2}(X=0,Y=1)=P_{2}(X=1,Y=0)=\frac{1}{4}

We can see that
X
and
Y
are independent of each other under
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
respectively, it is clear that
Y
is pseudo-independent of
X
under
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\varphi^{*}
be a bonded and Lipschitz function with

(3.2)

\varphi^{*}(0,0)=\varphi^{*}(1,1)=1,\quad\varphi^{*}(1,0)=\varphi^{*}(0,1)=0.

By the simple calculation, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi^{*}(X,Y)\right]=\frac{5}{8}<\frac{11}{16}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}\left[\varphi^{*}(x,Y)\right]|_{x=X}\right],

which indicates that
Y
is not independent of
X
.

Now we enlarge
\mathcal{P}
to
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
by

\bar{\mathcal{P}}:=\{P:\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}),E_{P}[\varphi(X)]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X)]\ \text{and}\ E_{P}[\varphi(Y)|X]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(Y)]\}.

In particular, for
\varphi^{*}
defined in (
3.2
), we can choose
P^{*}
by

\displaystyle P^{*}(X=1,Y=1)=\frac{9}{16}\ \ \

\displaystyle P^{*}(X=1,Y=0)=\frac{3}{16}

\displaystyle P^{*}(X=0,Y=1)=\frac{1}{8}\ \ \

\displaystyle P^{*}(X=0,Y=0)=\frac{1}{8}

It is easily to verify that
P^{*}\notin\mathcal{P}
but
P^{*}\in\bar{\mathcal{P}}
, Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}\left[\varphi^{*}(x,Y)]|_{x=X}\right]

\displaystyle=E_{P^{*}}\left[E_{P^{*}}\left[\varphi^{*}(x,Y)\right]|_{x=X}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{11}{16}=E_{P^{*}}[\varphi^{*}(X,Y)]=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi^{*}(X,Y)].

4.
Law of large numbers for pseudo-independent random variables

We firstly consider the law of large numbers for pseudo-independent sequence.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of random variables on the sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}),
and denote
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
,
S_{0}=0
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{n})
for
n\geq 1
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
. In this section, we require the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
consistent with the following hypothesis:

(H1)

There exist a random variable
X
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}nV(|X|\geq n)=0
and a constant
K
such that
V\left(|X_{n}|\geq x\right)\leq KV\left(|X|\geq x\right)
for each
x\geq 0
and
n\geq 1.

We have the following law of large numbers for pseudo-independent sequence.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a pseudo-independent sequence under
\mathcal{P}
satisfying (H1), we define

\overline{\mu}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}\mathbf{I}_{\{|X_{i}|<n\}}]}{n}\ \ \ \underline{\mu}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{i}\mathbf{I}_{\{|X_{i}|<n\}}]}{n}

then we have
\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
,

(4.1)

\min_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\leq\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu).

To complete the proof, we need some lemmas. The first lemma is Lemma 3.1 in Guo and Li
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfy (H1), then

(4.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{i}|^{2}\mathbf{I}_{\{|X_{i}|\leq n\}}]=0.

The next lemma generalizes the corresponding result for i.i.d. sequence in Chen et al.
[
1
]
to the pseudo-independent sequence. Here we give a very simple proof.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the same condition of Theorem
4.1
, we have, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}v(\underline{\mu}-\varepsilon\leq\frac{S_{n}}{n}\leq\overline{\mu}+\varepsilon)=1.

Proof.

Let
Y_{n,i}=X_{i}\mathbf{I}_{\{|X_{i}|<n\}}
and
T_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{n,i}
. For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, obviously,
E_{P}[Y_{n,i}^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}^{2}]
, and
-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-Y_{n,i}]\leq E_{P}[Y_{n,i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y_{n,i}]
.

Firstly, by Chebyshev’s inequality and Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle P(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{n,i}-E_{P}[Y_{n,i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])}{n}>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(E_{P}[Y_{n,i}^{2}]+E_{P}[(E_{P}[Y_{n,i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])^{2}])}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{P}[Y_{n,i}^{2}]}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\leq\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y_{n,i}^{2}]}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}.

Noting that,

P(S_{n}\neq T_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}P(|X_{i}|\geq n)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(|X_{i}|\geq n)\leq nKV(|X|\geq n),

thus

\displaystyle P(\frac{S_{n}}{n}>\overline{\mu}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq P(S_{n}\neq T_{n})+P(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{n,i}-E[Y_{n,i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])}{n}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +P(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}E[Y_{n,i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{n}>\overline{\mu}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})

\displaystyle\leq nKV(|X|\geq n)+\frac{8\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y_{n,i}^{2}]}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+P(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[Y_{n,i}]}{n}>\overline{\mu}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}).

Combining with Lemma
4.2
, we can imply that

V(\frac{S_{n}}{n}>\overline{\mu}+\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0,

Similarly, we can prove that
V(\frac{S_{n}}{n}<\underline{\mu}-\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0
, the proof is completed.
∎

Now we give the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

If we take
\varphi^{\prime}=-\varphi
in (
4.1
), we only need to prove that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\leq\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu).

For each
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\leq\max_{\underline{\mu}-\varepsilon\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}+\varepsilon}\varphi(\mu)+\max_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(\mu)(V(\frac{S_{n}}{n}>\overline{\mu}+\varepsilon)+V(\frac{S_{n}}{n}<\underline{\mu}-\varepsilon)).

Since
\varphi
is bounded, letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we can complete the proof.
∎

We reformulate Peng’s LLN (see Theorem 2.4.1 in Peng
[
10
]
) for the probability set
\mathcal{P}
and give a purely probabilistic proof. Such probabilistic proof was firstly considered in Li
[
6
]
(see Theorem 2.2) under the condition of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{1}|^{1+\delta}]<\infty
for some
\delta>0
. The current proof is totally different from the original one in Peng
[
10
]
based on the PDE estimation.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an i.i.d. sequence under
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
with
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[(|X_{1}|-\lambda)^{+}]=0
, then

(4.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{n})]=\max_{-\mathbb{E}[-X_{1}]\leq\mu\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]}\varphi(\mu),

where
\varphi
is any continuous function satisfying linear growth condition.

Proof.

We only need to prove that (
4.3
) holds for
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
. By the same argument in Peng
[
10
]
, it can be easily generalized to
\varphi
with linear growth condition.

It is clear that
nV(|X_{1}|\geq n)\rightarrow 0
and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\pm X_{1}\mathbf{I}_{\{|X_{1}|<n\}}]\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\pm X_{1}]
since
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[(|X_{1}|-\lambda)^{+}]=0
. Then by Theorem
4.1
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\leq\max_{-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{1}]\leq\mu\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}]}\varphi(\mu).

On the other hand, for fixed
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
with Lipschitz constant
L_{\varphi}
and bound
C_{\varphi}
, and for each
{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists
\mu\in(-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{1}],\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}])
such that
\varphi(\mu)\geq\max_{-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{1}]\leq\mu\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}]}\varphi(\mu)-{\varepsilon}
, we can find
E_{P}
such that
\{X_{i}\}
is an i.i.d. sequence under
E_{P}
with
E_{P}[X_{1}]=\mu
, where
P
can be chosen from
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
defined in Proposition
3.5
. In this case, by the classical weak law of large numbers,

P(|\frac{S_{n}}{n}-\mu|>\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0.

Then

|E_{P}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]-\varphi(\mu)|\leq L_{\varphi}\varepsilon+2C_{\varphi}P(|\frac{S_{n}}{n}-\mu_{n}|>\varepsilon),

We can obtain that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|E_{P}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]-\varphi(\mu)|=0,

which implies that, for each
{\varepsilon}>0
,

(4.4)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{n})]\geq\max_{-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{1}]\leq\mu\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}]}\varphi(\mu)-\varepsilon.

Let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the LLN holds.
∎

5.
Central limit theorem for pseudo-independent random variables

In this section, we consider the CLT for pseudo-independent sequence. We firstly reformulate the Peng’s CLT in Peng
[
10
]
on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
. The further result, for example, the convergence rate of CLT can be found in Fang et al.
[
3
]
and Song
[
11
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an i.i.d. sequence of random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
, we assume further

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0,

then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi)],

for all continuous function
\varphi
with linear growth condition, where
\xi
is a
G
-normally distributed random variable with
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{1}^{2}],\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}^{2}]])
.

Recently, Zhang
[
12
]
has weaken the condition
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0
to
nV(|X_{1}|^{2}\geq n)\rightarrow 0
, which inspires us to assume the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
consistent with the following hypothesis:

(H2)

There exist a random variable
X
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}nV(X^{2}\geq n)=0
and a constant
K
such that
V\left(|X_{n}|\geq x\right)\leq KV\left(|X|\geq x\right)
for each
x\geq 0
and
n\geq 1.

Let
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
, we have the following central limit theorem for pseudo-independent sequence.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a pseudo-independent sequence under
\mathcal{P}
satisfying (H2) and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{i}]=0,\forall i\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\overline{\sigma}^{2}:=\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}^{2}],\ \ \ \underline{\sigma}^{2}:=\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{i}^{2}].

We further assume that
\overline{\sigma}<+\infty
, then we have,
\forall\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
,

-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-\varphi(\xi)]\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi)],

where
\xi
is a
G
-normally distributed random variable with
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}])
.

To prove Theorem
5.2
, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\{X_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq i}^{\infty}
be the double array,
where for each row,
\{X_{ni}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is independent sequence under
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
.
We assume that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{ni}|]+\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{ni}])\rightarrow 0,

and

\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{ni}|^{3}]\rightarrow 0.

Define
\overline{\sigma}^{*}=\sup_{i,j}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{ij}^{2}]
and
\underline{\sigma}^{*}=\inf_{i,j}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{ij}^{2}]
, we further assume that
\overline{\sigma}<+\infty
.
Then we have

-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-\varphi(\xi^{*})]\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi^{*})],

where
\xi
is a
G
-normally distributed random variable with
\xi^{*}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[{\underline{\sigma}^{*}}^{2},{\overline{\sigma}^{*}}^{2}])
.

The proof is very similar to the corresponding one in Li
[
7
]
, so we omit the proof here.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an independent sequence under
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
satisfying (H2) and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{i}]=0,\forall i\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
\overline{\sigma}^{*}=\sup_{i}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{i}^{2}]
and
\underline{\sigma}^{*}=\inf_{i}-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X_{i}^{2}]
, we further assume that
\overline{\sigma}<+\infty
.
Then we have

-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-\varphi(\xi^{*})]\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi^{*})],

where
\xi
is a
G
-normally distributed random variable with
\xi^{*}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[{\underline{\sigma}^{*}}^{2},{\overline{\sigma}^{*}}^{2}])
.

Proof.

Let
X_{ni}=(-\sqrt{n})\vee X_{i}\wedge\sqrt{n}
, by the same argument in Zhang
[
12
]
,
\{X_{ni}\}
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
5.3
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ni}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi^{*})].

It is clear that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|\varphi(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ni}}{\sqrt{n}})-\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})|]\leq\sup_{x}|\varphi(x)|\sum_{i=1}^{n}V(|X_{i}|\geq\sqrt{n})\leq KnV(|X|\geq\sqrt{n})\rightarrow 0,

we can complete the proof.
∎

Now we give the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

By Proposition
3.5
, we can enlarge
\mathcal{P}
to
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is independent under
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}
with
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\pm X_{i}^{p}]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\pm X_{i}^{p}]
,
p=1,2
. We further assume that
\underline{\sigma}>0
. In fact, this condition can be removed by the perturbation method in
the proof of Theorem 2.4.7 in Peng
[
10
]
.

For each
0<\varepsilon<\underline{\sigma}
, we can choose
N
such that
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[X_{i}^{2}]\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}+\varepsilon
and
-\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[-X_{i}^{2}]\geq\underline{\sigma}^{2}-\varepsilon
, when
i\geq N
. Set
S_{n}^{N}=\sum_{i=N}^{n}X_{i}
, for
n\geq N
, by Lemma
5.4
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}^{N}}{\sqrt{n-N}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(\xi_{\varepsilon})],

where
\xi_{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2}-\varepsilon,\overline{\sigma}^{2}+\varepsilon])
.

It is easily seen that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})-\varphi(\frac{S_{n}^{N}}{n-N})]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[|\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})-\varphi(\frac{S_{n}^{N}}{\sqrt{n}})|]+\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[|\varphi(\frac{S_{n}^{N}}{\sqrt{n}})-\varphi(\frac{S_{n}^{N}}{\sqrt{n-N}})|]

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[|X_{i}|]}{\sqrt{n}}+C\frac{N\sum_{i=N}^{n}\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[|X_{i}|]}{\sqrt{n(n-N)(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n+N})}}\rightarrow 0,

Thus we obtain

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi_{\varepsilon})].

Note that
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]
, and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi_{\varepsilon})]\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\xi)]
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the proof is complete.
∎

6.
Example

In this section, we will present relevant counterexamples to illustrate that the LLN and CLT for i.i.d sequence may fail only with
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{1}|]<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X_{1}^{2}]<\infty
respectively.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{k},k\geq 1\}
, where
P_{k}(\{0\})=1-\frac{1}{k^{2}}
,
P_{k}(\{k\})=P_{k}(\{-k\})=\frac{1}{2k^{2}}
. Consider a function
X
on
\mathbb{Z}
defined by
X(n)=n,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It is obvious that
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X]=0
and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X^{2}]=-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-X^{2}]=1
. According to Proposition
3.5
or Peng
[
10
]
, we are able to construct an i.i.d. sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
under
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}
such that
X_{n}
has the same distribution with
X
.

We have the following properties for such example.

Proposition 6.2
.

In Example
6.1
, let
Y_{i}=X_{i}^{2}
and
\varphi(x)=1-x
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n}}{n})]=1.

Proof.

It is clear that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{x+Y_{i}}{n})]

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{(1-\frac{1}{k^{2}})\varphi(\frac{x}{n})+\frac{1}{k^{2}}(\varphi(\frac{x+k^{2}}{n})\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{\varphi(\frac{x}{n})+\frac{1}{k^{2}}(\varphi(\frac{x+k^{2}}{n})-\varphi(\frac{x}{n}))\}=\varphi(\frac{x}{n}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n}}{n})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{x+Y_{n}}{n})]|_{x=Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n-1}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n-1}}{n})]=\cdots=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{Y_{1}}{n})]=\varphi(0)=1.

∎

Proposition
6.2
indicates that the Peng’s LLN (see Theorem
4.4
) does not hold only with the finite moment condition. Indeed,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]=1\neq 0=\varphi(1)
.

Proposition 6.3
.

In Example
6.1
, let
\varphi(x)=1-|x|
, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]=1,

thus the central limit theorem does not hold.

Proof.

We first observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{x+X}{\sqrt{n}})]

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{(1-\frac{1}{k^{2}})\varphi(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}})+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}(\varphi(\frac{x-k}{\sqrt{n}})+\varphi(\frac{x+k}{\sqrt{n}}))\}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{\frac{2|x|-|x+k|-|x-k|}{2k^{2}}\}=\varphi(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{x+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]|_{x=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n-1}}]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}})]

\displaystyle=\cdots=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{X_{1}}{\sqrt{n}})]=\varphi(0)=1.

In this case,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})]=1>\mathcal{N}(\varphi)
, where
\mathcal{N}(\varphi)
is the expectation of
\varphi(\xi)
and
\xi
is standard normal distributed. Peng’s CLT (Theorem
5.1
) is invalid.
∎

In the end of the paper, we raise the following questions as the open problems:

Questions
Do the limit distributions of
\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\frac{X_{1}^{2}+\cdots+X_{n}^{2}}{n}
in Example
6.1
(or in general case) exist? If they exist, what are the corresponding limit distributions?

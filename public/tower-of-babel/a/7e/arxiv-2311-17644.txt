Revisiting stochastic inflation with perturbation theory Preprint: APS/123-QED
Gonzalo A. Palma a, Spyros Sypsas b,c

Abstract
A long-standing problem within the study of cosmic inflation consists in fully reconciling the stochastic approach with perturbation theory. A complete connection between both formalisms has remained elusive even in the simple case of a single scalar field with self-interactions determined by an arbitrary potential, in a fixed de Sitter background. Using perturbation theory, we compute the one-point probability density function for primordial fluctuations valid to first order in the potential. We examine under which conditions our solution respects the Fokker-Planck equation encountered within the stochastic approach. We identify discrepancies and elucidate their origins, allowing us to shed light on the status of the stochastic formalism.

Stochastic formalism.–
We begin by reviewing the stochastic approach to determine the statistics of primordial spectator fields. From (
1
), the equation of motion for
\varphi
reads

\ddot{\varphi}+3H\dot{\varphi}-a^{-2}\nabla^{2}\varphi+\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi)=0.

(2)

To proceed, we split the field as
\varphi=\varphi_{S}+\varphi_{L}
, where
\varphi_{L}=\int_{{{\bm{k}}}}e^{i{{\bm{k}}}\cdot{{\bm{x}}}}W(k)\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)
, with
\int_{{{\bm{k}}}}\equiv\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}
, while
\varphi_{S}
is the complement. Here,
\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)
is the Fourier transform of
\varphi({{\bm{x}}},t)
, and
W(k)
is a window function specifying the splitting, which we take as
ft1
:

W(k,t)=\theta(k_{*}(t)-k),

(3)

where
\theta(x)
is the Heaviside step function and
k_{*}(t)=\alpha Ha(t)
, for a small dimensionless parameter
\alpha>0
. This choice splits
\varphi
with a physical cutoff
p_{*}=\alpha H
, such that
\varphi_{L}
and
\varphi_{S}
represent long and short modes respectively.

The key feature underlying this framework is that one can treat short modes as linear but quantum, and long modes as nonlinear but classical. More to the point, for wavelengths smaller than
2\pi/k_{*}(t)
, one linearizes Eq. (
2
), treating
\varphi_{L}
as a background field:

\ddot{\tilde{\varphi}}_{{\bm{k}}}+3H\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}_{{\bm{k}}}+a^{-2}k^{2}\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}+\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{L})\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}=0.

(4)

The general solution for
\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}
respecting canonical commutation relations is
\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)=f(k,\tau)\hat{a}_{{\bm{k}}}+f^{*}(k,\tau)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{{\bm{k}}}}
, with creation and annihilation operators satisfying
[\hat{a}_{{\bm{k}}},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{{\bm{k}}}^{\prime}}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta({{\bm{k}}}-{{\bm{k}}}^{\prime})
, while
f(k,\tau)
is the mode function determined by Bunch-Davies initial conditions. Combining Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
), one arrives at a Langevin equation:

\dot{\varphi}_{L}+\frac{1}{3H}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi_{L})=H\hat{\xi}(t),

(5)

where
\hat{\xi}(t)
is a Gaussian stochastic noise given by:

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}\equiv H^{-1}\int_{{\bm{k}}}\left(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}W(k)\right)\,\tilde{\varphi}_{{\bm{k}}}(t).

(6)

By disregarding
\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{L})
in (
4
),
f(k,\tau)
acquires the massless de Sitter form
f(k,\tau)=\frac{H}{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}(1+ik\tau)e^{-ik\tau}
. Then, using
\dot{W}(k,t)=Hk_{*}(t)\delta(k_{*}(t)-k)
, one finds
\langle\hat{\xi}(t)\hat{\xi}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{H}{4\pi^{2}}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. With this normalization, the Langevin equation (
5
) leads to the following Fokker-Planck equation describing the PDF’s evolution:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=\frac{H^{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{3H}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}\left(\rho\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial\varphi}\right).

(7)

The first and second terms at the right-hand side correspond to diffusion and drift respectively. Diffusion describes how the variance of the distribution increases over time, whereas the drift term dictates how the distribution gets distorted by the potential gradients, enforcing that the most likely values of
\varphi
lie at the minima of
{\cal V}
.

Comments on the stochastic formalism.–
Equation (
7
) admits an equilibrium solution
\dot{\rho}=0
that may be reached asymptotically as
t\to\infty
. The solution is
\rho_{\infty}(\varphi)\propto\exp[-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3H^{4}}\mathcal{V}(\varphi)]
. However, because
\varphi_{L}
contains every mode with
k<k_{*}(t)
, then
\rho(\varphi,t)
not only describes the statistics of modes that crossed the horizon a short time before
t
, but also, that of modes that did so an infinite time earlier. Since there is an infinite number of such modes, the equilibrium solution should already dominate the statistics at any given time
ft2
, revealing a problem with the definition of
\varphi_{L}
. To amend this, we may restrict
\varphi_{L}
to a finite range of scales by requiring that, instead of (
3
), the window function is given by
Tsamis:2005hd

W(k)=\theta(k_{*}(t)-k)\times\theta(k-k_{*}(t_{\rm i})),

(8)

where
k_{*}(t_{\rm i})
is a comoving infrared cutoff that limits the amount of modes involved in
\varphi_{L}
such that the range is empty at an initial time
t=t_{\rm i}
. Now the full field is

\varphi=\varphi_{S}+\varphi_{L}+\varphi_{\rm IR},

(9)

where
\varphi_{\rm IR}
consists of modes with
k<k_{*}(t_{\rm i})
. The introduction of
k_{*}(t_{\rm i})
is only natural: the statistics cannot sensibly take into account the infinite range of wavelengths
\lambda>2\pi/\alpha H
, which in practice is non-accessible to observers. Thus, initially the range of scales described by
\rho
is empty, but as time elapses, modes feed the range by crossing the physical cutoff
p_{*}=\alpha H
, and
\rho
diffuses. This picture is easy to visualize in the case
\mathcal{V}=0
, where
\varphi_{L}
is Gaussian and its statistics is described by

\rho_{0}(\varphi,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}(t)}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}(t)}\right\},

(10)

with a variance given by the 2-point correlation function at the coincident limit:
\sigma^{2}(t)=\langle\varphi_{L}({{\bm{x}}},t)\varphi_{L}({{\bm{x}}}^{\prime},t)\rangle|_{{{\bm{x}}={{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}}}
. A direct computation yields

\sigma^{2}(t)=\frac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{k_{*}(t)}{k_{*}(t_{\rm i})}=\frac{H^{3}}{4\pi^{2}}(t-t_{\rm i}).

(11)

That is, at
t=t_{\rm i}
, the PDF corresponds to a Dirac-delta function
\delta(\varphi)
, which then starts to diffuse.

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{3}-(\tau^{\prime})^{3}}{3}\,\frac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int^{k_{*}(t)}_{k_{*}(t_{\rm i})}\!\frac{{\rm d}k}{k}\frac{\sin(kx)}{kx}k^{3}.\qquad

(25)

It follows that

, allowing us to expand

\big\langle\varphi^{n}(\tau)\big\rangle_{c}\!\!=-\frac{4\pi^{2}n\bar{\lambda}_{n}}{3H^{4}}\sigma^{2n}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}x}{x}K(x)\left(\frac{g_{R}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{n}

(26)

:

\int^{\infty}_{0}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}x}{x}K\!\left(\frac{g_{R}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{n}\!\!\simeq 1+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta N^{-2}\right),

where
\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta N^{-2}\right)\supset-\frac{\pi^{2}-3}{24\Delta N^{2}}(n-1)(n-2)

\bar{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi)
does not have too much structure within a field range of size

\int\frac{{\rm d}x}{x}\,K\left(\frac{g_{R}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{n}\simeq 1
for a sufficiently large

, in which case we end up with

\Big\langle\varphi^{n}(\tau)\Big\rangle_{c}=-\frac{4\pi^{2}n}{3H^{4}}\sigma^{2n}(t)\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n+2L}}{L!}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2}}{2}\right)^{L},

(28)

which differs from (
15
) significantly (e.g. here the IR-divergence goes as
\propto[\ln a(t)]^{n}
). We may now use (

{\cal O}_{\varphi}{\rm He}_{n}(\varphi/\sigma)=-n{\rm He}_{n}(\varphi/\sigma)
. This allows one to replace the overall
n

-{\cal O}_{\varphi}

\mathcal{V}_{\rm obs}(\varphi)\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n}\sigma^{n}}{n!}{\rm He}_{n}(\varphi/\sigma)

\bar{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi)

\mathcal{V}_{\rm obs}(\varphi,t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}}}\bar{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi).

(29)

With all of this, we are finally led to

\Delta(\varphi,t)=\frac{4\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}(t)}{3H^{4}}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{\rm obs}(\varphi)-\frac{\varphi}{\sigma^{2}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\rm obs}(\varphi)\right).

defined in (

Discrepancy between QFT and the stochastic formalism.–
Let us discern the origin of the discrepancy between (
7
) and (
31
). In deriving (
5
) from the full equation (
2
), we have only kept
\varphi_{L}
in the argument of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi)
, disregarding appearances of
\phi_{S}
and
\phi_{\rm IR}
, which is why the contractions of (
14
) only involve the restricted Gaussian field
\varphi_{G}
. However, if we restore the omitted appearance of
\phi_{S}
and
\phi_{\rm IR}
back in the Langevin equation (
5
), and time-integrate, we are led to

\varphi_{L}(t)\simeq\varphi_{G}(t)-\frac{1}{3H}\int^{t}_{t_{\rm i}}\!\!\!{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,W\left\{\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial\varphi}\left[\varphi(t^{\prime})\right]\right\},

(32)

where
\varphi_{G}=H\int^{t}_{t_{\rm i}}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\xi(t^{\prime})
, and

denotes a filter based on the window function (

) restricting the fields to the appropriate range of scales:
\varphi_{L}=W\left\{\varphi\right\}
. Notice that the field appearing in the argument of the potential is the full field
\varphi(t^{\prime})=\varphi_{S}(t^{\prime})+\varphi_{L}(t^{\prime})+\varphi_{\rm IR}(t^{\prime})
.

Before we continue, note that (
32
) may be alternatively understood as a consequence of the full QFT solution
\varphi(x,t)=U(t)\varphi_{I}(x,t)U^{{\dagger}}(t)
, where
\varphi_{I}(x,t)
is the interaction picture field satisfying
\ddot{\varphi}_{I}+3H\dot{\varphi}_{I}-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\nabla^{2}\varphi_{I}=0
, and
U(t)=\exp\big\{-i\int^{t}_{-\infty}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\int{\rm d}^{3}x^{\prime}\,\mathcal{V}(\varphi_{I}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))\big\}
is the unitary evolution operator. By expanding
\varphi(x,t)
in powers of
\mathcal{V}
, one arrives at
\varphi(t,x)\simeq\varphi_{I}(t,x)+i\int^{t}{\rm d}t^{\prime}\int{\rm d}^{3}x^{\prime}\,[\varphi_{I}(t,x),\varphi_{I}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial\varphi}\left(\varphi_{I}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right)
. Then, by restricting the range of scales to that of (
8
), one obtains back

\varphi_{L}(t)\simeq\varphi_{G}(t)-\frac{1}{3H}\int^{t}_{t_{\rm i}}\!\!\!{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,W\left\{\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial\varphi}\left[\varphi_{I}(t^{\prime})\right]\right\},

(33)

which is equivalent to (
32
) but now with
\varphi_{G}=W\left\{\varphi_{I}\right\}
.

Crucially, Eq. (
32
) differs from (
12
): in the former, the support of the operator
\varphi(t^{\prime})
appearing in the argument of
\mathcal{V}
is unrestricted. In the latter it is restricted by the window function. Just as we did with (
12
) we may now compute
n

32
) [or equivalently, from (
33
)] up to first order in
\mathcal{V}
. To do so, the field
\varphi(t^{\prime})=\varphi_{S}(t^{\prime})+\varphi_{L}(t^{\prime})+\varphi_{\rm IR}(t^{\prime})
inside the argument of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
must be treated as a free field. The result is

\displaystyle\Big\langle\varphi_{L}^{n}(t)\Big\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{3H}\int^{t}_{t_{\rm i}}\!\!\!{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,\Big(\langle\varphi_{G}(t)\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle\Big)^{n-1}

(34)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n+2L}}{L!}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\langle\varphi(t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle\Big)^{L}.

This is identical in form to (
34

\varphi(t^{\prime})
is not restricted to a particular range of scales (as opposed to
\varphi_{G}(t)
). Consequentially,
\langle\varphi_{G}(t)\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle
and
\langle\varphi(t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle

Discussion.–
Comparing (
14
) and (
34
) reveals the root of the discrepancy between the QFT and stochastic frameworks: To obtain (
12
) from the full equation of motion (
2
), we selectively retained
\varphi_{L}
within
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi)
, disregarding the contributions of
\phi_{S}
and
\phi_{\rm IR}
. Consequently, contractions in (
14
) only involve the restricted Gaussian field
\varphi_{G}
, leading to the omission of two important effects: The first one is that
\varphi_{G}(t)
correlates with
\varphi_{\rm IR}(t^{\prime})
inside the argument of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi)
. This is because
\varphi_{\rm IR}(t^{\prime})
is evaluated at a time
t^{\prime}<t
, and so it contains wavelengths that overlap with those of
\varphi_{G}(t)
. Including
\varphi_{\rm IR}(t^{\prime})
in the argument of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(\varphi)
leads to
\langle\varphi_{G}(t)\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle=\sigma^{2}(t)
instead of
\langle\varphi_{G}(t)\varphi_{L}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\sigma^{2}(t^{\prime})
. The second effect is that correlators
\langle\varphi(t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle
cover the entire range of physical momenta, and so one has
\langle\varphi(t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime})\rangle=\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2}
as already discussed. In other words, both long and short modes run in the loops.

Our analysis provides insight into previous reports claiming that (
7
) is fully compatible with perturbation theory (see for instance Refs.
Tsamis:2005hd
;
Baumgart:2019clc
;
Burgess:2014eoa
;
Finelli:2008zg
;
Riotto:2011sf
;
Garbrecht:2013coa
;
Garbrecht:2014dca
;
Vennin:2015hra
;
Burgess:2015ajz
;
Moss:2016uix
;
Collins:2017haz
;
Tokuda:2017fdh
;
Gorbenko:2019rza
;
Mirbabayi:2019qtx
;
Mirbabayi:2020vyt
): As we see from (
15
), the computation of correlation functions compatible with (
7
) requires restricting the range of internal momenta (entering the definition of fields participating in vertices) to the same range limiting external momenta. Proceeding in this way, for instance, leads to the computation of loop-integrals using cutoffs that coincide with the comoving cutoff defining the lowest external momenta entering
\varphi_{L}
, i.e., only long modes (those that belong to
\varphi_{L}
) run in the loops. This is precisely the procedure used in some approaches within perturbation theory leading to results compatible with the stochastic formalism. In our view, this procedure introduces a gross dependence of correlation functions on cutoffs, which is not well justified.

At face value, perturbation theory requires that every internal
physical
momentum appearing in diagrams be integrated in the full range
0<p<\infty
(which is the rule that leads to overall momentum conservation in the case of diagrams with various vertices
ft4
). To deal with this integration, in this work we have considered constant physical cutoffs
\Lambda_{\rm IR}
and
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
such that internal momentum is restricted to
\Lambda_{\rm IR}<p<\Lambda_{\rm UV}
. The infrared divergence implied by
\Lambda_{\rm IR}\to 0
found in the computation of loops is of a different nature than the infrared divergence dictated by powers of
\ln a(t)
appearing in correlators. As we have seen, both
\Lambda_{\rm IR}
and
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
appearing from loops can be absorbed by the bare potential
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)

ft5
, a result consistent with the fact that daisy-loops should lack any physical implication on observables, except for redefining bare couplings, as in the explicit case of (
20
).

To emphasize the previous point, consider the 2-point correlation function in momentum space in the specific example
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)=\frac{m^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4!}\varphi^{4}
. From (
20
) we see that its computation, including loops, is equivalent to the tree-level computation with the effective coupling
m^{2}_{\rm eff}\equiv m^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2}
, which is time-independent. This result is consistent with the recent finding of
Lee:2023jby
;
Creminelli:2024cge
where, employing dimensional regularization, it is found that the loop coming from
\lambda
cancels with a mass counterterm, rendering a 2-point function as if it was computed at tree-level with a time-independent effective mass
m^{2}_{\rm eff}
. In contrast, if one employs a co-moving cutoff, the resulting effective mass
m^{2}_{\rm eff}
becomes time dependent
Brunier:2004sb
, which is hard to understand in a theory which is time-translation invariant (recall that we are in the rigid de Sitter limit).

Concluding remarks.–
All in all, we have derived the Fokker-Planck equation (
31
) directly from the resummation of Feynman diagrams without the intermediate Langevin description (and the ambiguities surrounding it
Pinol:2018euk
). In other words, we have presented an exact solution of the Fokker-Planck equation (
31
) valid to linear order in the interactions. Taken at face value, the time dependent correction to the diffusion term of (
31
) implies that there is no equilibrium. However, a more cautious answer is that this asymptotic regime signals the need of considering higher order corrections
\propto\,\Delta N^{2}\mathcal{V}^{2}
. The distinct viewpoint that we offer here, is that our method allows for a complete, order-by-order, systematic comparison between field-theoretic tools and their stochastic counterparts, highlighting the need for infrared resummation (in both formalisms) especially towards the nonperturative regime where the equilibrium distribution lies.

Is it possible to account for a corrected Langevin equation leading to the modified Fokker-Planck equation (
31
)? Notably,
{\cal V}_{\rm obs}(\varphi_{L})
is nothing but the bare potential
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)
with both
\varphi_{S}
and
\varphi_{\rm IR}
integrated out at leading order
ft6
. This result hints towards a path for making the stochastic picture compatible with QFT by suggesting that a more accurate Langevin equation is given by
\dot{\varphi}_{L}+\frac{1}{3H}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\rm obs}(\varphi_{L})=H\hat{\xi}
, with a noise satisfying a normalization that is compatible with the Fokker-Planck equation (
31
). That is:

\Big\langle\hat{\xi}(t)\hat{\xi}(t^{\prime})\Big\rangle=\frac{H}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\Delta N}{3H^{2}}{\cal V}^{\prime\prime}_{\rm obs}(\varphi_{L})\right)\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(35)

Both, the potential
{\cal V}_{\rm obs}
and the new normalized noise would presumably account for the effects discussed in this work regarding the role of unrestricted fields participating in contractions. We leave such a derivation as an open challenge.

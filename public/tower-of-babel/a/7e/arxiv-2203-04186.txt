(with the induced subordering) so that
|T_{n}|\leq\kappa_{n}
(some of these might have countable height, this is fine). Each one is special by assumption. Let
f_{n}:T_{n}\to\omega
be a specializing function. Since the
T_{n}
’s are disjoint and cover
T
the disjoint union of the
f_{n}
’s is a map from
T
to countably many disjoint copies of
\omega
. But then this is a function from
T
to a countable set which is injective on chains and hence is a specializing function.
∎

As mentioned before, Koszimder has shown that
\mathfrak{st}
can be singular of uncountable cofinality.

3.
The Specializing Forcing

Fix a generalized Aronszajn tree
T=\langle T,\sqsubseteq_{T}\rangle
with root
\emptyset_{T}
. The point of this section is to study new reals, in fact new countable sequences of ordinals, added after forcing with
\mathbb{P}(T)
, the Baumgartner-Malitz-Reinhardt forcing from
[
3
]
to specialize
T
with finite approximations. Concretely a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}(T)
if it is a finite, partial function
p:T\to\omega
so that if
s\sqsubseteq_{T}t
and
s,t\in{\rm dom}(p)
then
p(s)\neq p(t)
. The order is reverse inclusion. This forcing is ccc (
[
3
, Theorem 3]
see also
[
14
, Lemma 16.19]
) and adds a specializing function for
T
. Throughout, let
G_{T}\subseteq\mathbb{P}(T)
be
V
-generic and let
g_{T}:=\bigcup G
be the generic specializing function. Also let
\dot{g}_{T}
be the canonical
\mathbb{P}(T)
-name for
g_{T}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A\subseteq T
be a non-empty, countable, downward closed subset of
T
. The
border
of
A
, denoted
\partial A
, is the set of all
s\notin A
so that every predecessor of
s
is in
A
i.e.
\partial A=\{s\in T\setminus A\;|\;P_{s}\subseteq A\}
. In other words it is the set of minimal elements of
T
not in
A
.

For the next few lemmas fix an
A
as above.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
t\notin A
, there is a unique element of
\{s\in T\;|\;s\sqsubseteq_{T}t\}
in
\partial A
.

Proof.

Since
t\notin A
if all of its predecessors are in
A
then
t\in\partial A
so we’re done. Otherwise, the set
s\in P_{t}
not in
A
is non-empty. Since this set is well-ordered there is a least such element, which must be in
\partial A
. If
s^{\prime}\in P_{t}
above
s
then
s^{\prime}\notin\partial A
since it has a predecessor not in
A
, namely
s
.
∎

Given this lemma we may define, for each
t\notin A
the
projection

{\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t)
to be this unique predecessor. Note that if
t\in\partial A
then
{\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t)=t
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
s\in A
there is a (usually not unique)
t\in\partial A
so that
s\sqsubseteq_{T}t
.

Proof.

Fix
s\in A
. Since
T
is normal and
A
is countable, there is an
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
so that
A\cap{\rm Lev}_{\alpha}(T)=\emptyset
and
s
is comparable with some
s^{\prime}\in{\rm Lev}_{\alpha}(T)
. In particular the collection of
t
above
s
not in
A
is non-empty. Since
T
is well-founded this set therefore has minimal elements and any such minimal element is in
\partial A
by definition.
∎

All of these simple observations and definitions are in the service of defining the following forcing notion, which is key.

Definition 3.4

(The forcing notion
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
)
.

For
A
as above define
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
to be the forcing notion consisting of pairs
(s,\pi)
(
\pi
for
promise
) so that the following hold.

(1)

s\in\mathbb{P}(T)
with
{\rm dom}(s)\subseteq A
.

(2)

\pi
is a finite partial function from
\partial A
to
[\omega]^{<\omega}

(3)

If
t\in{\rm dom}(p)
,
t^{\prime}\in{\rm dom}(\pi)
,
t\sqsubseteq_{T}t^{\prime}
and
n\in\pi(t^{\prime})
then
s(t)\neq n
.

The extension relation is defined by
(s,\pi)\leq(r,\tau)
if and only if
s\supseteq r
,
{\rm dom}(\pi)\supseteq{\rm dom}(\tau)
and for every
t\in{\rm dom}(\tau)
we have
\pi(t)\supseteq\tau(t)
.

The intuition for this definition is that
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
is a subforcing of
\mathbb{P}(T)
adding
g_{T}\upharpoonright A
. The point is that the second coordinate consists of the information relevant to
g_{T}\upharpoonright A
given by conditions
p\in G_{T}
with
{\rm dom}(p)\nsubseteq A
. To see this, observe that if
p
is a condition in
\mathbb{P}(T)
with domain not contained in from
A
, say
p(t)=n
for some
t\in{\rm dom}(p)\setminus A
and
n<\omega
, then the ramification for
g_{T}\upharpoonright A
is that for every
t^{\prime}\sqsubseteq_{T}t
in
A
, we have
p\Vdash\dot{g}_{T}(\check{t}^{\prime})\neq\check{n}
. This is what the promises give us. All of this is more formally expressed by the next lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a countable, downward closed subset of
T
.

(1)

\mathbb{Q}_{A}
is
\sigma
-centered.

(2)

If
H\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{A}
is
V
-generic then
h:=\bigcup_{(s,\pi)\in H}s
is a specializing function on
A
in the sense that
h
maps
A
to
\omega
and is injective on chains. Moreover if
b\subseteq A
is linearly ordered and there is a
t\in T
greater than every
t^{\prime}\in b
there are infinitely many
n\notin{\rm range}(h\upharpoonright b)
.

(3)

In
V[h]
define
\mathbb{R}(h)
the be the set of finite partial functions
p:T\setminus A\to\omega
so that
p\cup h
is a partial specializing function. Let
\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
-name for this partial order. Then
\mathbb{P}(T)
densely embeds into
\mathbb{Q}_{A}*\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
and
g_{T}\upharpoonright A=h
is the union of the first coordinates of the generic set added by
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
.

For simplicity in what follows if
(s,\pi)\in\mathbb{Q}_{A}
call
s
the
stem
of the condition.

Proof.

1. Fix a stem
s
and let
\pi
and
\tau
be so that
(s,\pi)
and
(s,\tau)
are both conditions in
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
. Let
\pi\cup\tau:\partial A\to[\omega]^{<\omega}
defined by
(\pi\cup\tau)(t)=\pi(t)\cup\tau(t)
for
t\in{\rm dom}(\pi)\cup{\rm dom}(\tau)
with
\pi(t)=\emptyset
for
t\in{\rm dom}(\tau)\setminus{\rm dom}(\pi)
and idem for
\tau(t)
. It’s easy to check that
(s,\pi\cup\tau)
is a condition in
\mathbb{Q}_{A}
extending both
(s,\pi)
and
(s,\tau)
so any two conditions with the same stem are compatible. Since there are only countably many stems this proves this first part.

2. This is a straightforward density argument.

3. First note that every element of
\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
evaluates to a ground model element (since it is a finite partial function between two ground model sets) so by strengthening if necessary we can decide a check name for every condition in
\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
hence
\mathbb{Q}_{A}*\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
has a dense subset of the form
(s,\pi,\check{p})
with
p\in\mathbb{P}(T)
and
{\rm dom}(p)\subseteq T\setminus A
. From now on we drop the check on top of
p
. Now there is a further dense subset so that the following hold:

(1)

For every
t\in{\rm dom}(p)
we have
{\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t)\in{\rm dom}(\pi)
and
p(t)\in\pi({\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t))

(2)

For every
r\in{\rm dom}(\pi)
and every
n\in\pi(r)
then there is a
t_{r}\in{\rm dom}(p)
with
{\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t_{r})=r
and
p(t_{r})=n

To see this, begin with any
(s,\pi,p)\in\mathbb{Q}_{A}*\dot{\mathbb{R}}(\dot{h})
and first, for every
t\in{\rm dom}(p)
add
({\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t),p(t))
to
\pi
. Then for every
(r,n)\in\pi
if there is not already some
t_{r}\in{\rm dom}(p)
with
p(t_{r})=n
then we are done otherwise no node in the domain of
p
projects to
r
and has value
n
under
p
so we can add a
t_{r}\notin{\rm dom}(p)
whose projection is
r
and assign this node value
n
. The fact that everything is finite, along with the fact that
\pi(r)\ni n
precludes
s(t)=n
for any
t
below
r
allows this to work.

Let
D
be this dense set of conditions. To summarize,
(s,\pi,p)\in D
if and only if
{\rm dom}(\pi)=\{{\rm proj}_{\partial A}(t)\;|\;t\in{\rm dom}(p)\}
and for each
t\in{\rm dom}(\pi)
we have that
\pi(t)=\{n\;|\;\exists s\in{\rm dom}(p)(t\sqsubseteq_{T}s\,{\rm and}\,p(s)=n)\}
. We claim that
\mathbb{P}(T)
is isomorphic to
D
. Indeed, by construction we can read off
\pi
from
p
so we can drop the middle coordinate at which point it becomes clear that
p\mapsto(p\upharpoonright A,p\upharpoonright T\setminus A)
is the desired isomorphism.
∎

The point of all of this is the following. Suppose
\dot{x}
is a
\mathbb{P}(T)
-name for a countable sequence of ordinals. For each
n<\omega
let
A_{n}
be a maximal antichain deciding
\dot{x}(\check{n})
. Note that
A_{n}
is countable for all
n<\omega
by the ccc. Let
A_{\dot{x}}
be the downward closure of
\bigcup\{{\rm dom}(p)\;|\;p\in\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_{n}\}
. Observe that all of
\dot{x}
is decided by
g_{T}\upharpoonright A_{\dot{x}}
: for each
n<\omega
in

there must be a
p\in A_{n}
so that
p\subseteq g_{T}\upharpoonright A_{\dot{x}}
since each
A_{n}
is a maximal antichain and the union of the domains of all its elements are contained in

. Thus
\dot{x}
was added by
\mathbb{Q}_{A_{\dot{x}}}
. In particular we have the following.

Theorem 3.6
.

Every countable set of ordinals added by
\mathbb{P}(T)
is in a
\sigma
-centered subextension.

A remark about this theorem is in order.

.

In general whether or not

. The following question appears to be open however and would be interesting to explore.

.

Is there a combinatorial condition on

\sigma
-centered? For instance, is it possible that
\mathbb{P}(T)
is
\sigma
-centered if and only if

6

. Recall that a forcing notion

\sqsubseteq^{\rm Random}

.

(1)

For any generalized Aronszajn tree

(2)

Let

Note that 2. follows from 1. by the iteration theorem

. For 1., fix a generalized Aronszajn tree

Remark 3
.

In our opinion the above proof and the aforementioned proof of Chodounský and Zapletal in

]
complement one another as each gives something the other does not. In
[

]

satisfies a condition they term Y-.c.c. and show that Y-.c.c. forcing notions do not add random reals, even when iterated. Every

.

If

If

Now we look at iterating forcing notions of the form

with an eye towards proving Main Theorem

Fix a regular cardinal
\kappa

(of length

in

What consequences does the inequality

5.
Conclusion and Open Questions

The (not even so) observant reader will notice that Main Theorem
1.1
actually shows that the cardinal characteristics of the Special Tree model are exactly the same as in the Cohen model (other than
\mathfrak{st}
of course, which is
\aleph_{1}
since there is always a Souslin tree in the Cohen model). This gives credence to the conjecture in Section 3 that every new real in a
\mathbb{P}(T)
-extension is already in a Cohen subextension. Of course these models are very different combinatorially. Indeed it’s well know that in the Cohen Model there are Souslin trees and in particular
\mathfrak{st}=\aleph_{1}
. More interestingly recall that the combinatorial principle
\diamondsuit({\rm non}(\mathcal{M}))
from
[
7
]
holds

The precise definition of
\diamondsuit({\rm non}(\mathcal{M}))
is somewhat technical and since we do not need it we omit it here. For the reader who does not know about parametrized diamond principles we simply note that
\diamondsuit({\rm non}(\mathcal{M}))
strengthens the statement
{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})=\aleph_{1}
in a similar way to how
\diamondsuit_{\omega_{1}}
strengthens
\mathsf{CH}
.
in the Cohen model and indeed in every iterated model of
{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})=\aleph_{1}
with “definable” iterands (see
[
7
, Theorem 6.6]
for a more precise statement of this result). However
\diamondsuit({\rm non}(\mathcal{M}))
implies there is a Souslin tree (
[
7
, Theorem 4.7]
), and hence must fail in the special tree model. In our view the special tree model is a relatively natural model witnessing
{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})=\aleph_{1}\land\neg\diamondsuit({\rm non}(\mathcal{M}))
. We ask how else the Cohen and special tree models can be separated.

Question 5
.

What other well studied principles distinguish the Cohen model and the special tree model?

On a more basic level, the results above do not address how
\mathfrak{st}
relates to the right hand side of the Cichoń diagram. However, this is partially addressed by Laver’s Theorem
2.7
above (in its slightly modified form). In that model
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{st}
while in the special tree model (or any length) we have
\mathfrak{st}\leq{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})
and hence
\mathfrak{st}\leq\mathfrak{d}
. The following is therefore open.

Question 6
.

Does
\mathsf{ZFC}
prove
\mathfrak{st}\leq\mathfrak{d}
?

We conjecture this is the case. What this would amount to in practice is that there is no way to specialize all generalized Aronszajn trees of size
\aleph_{1}
in an iterable way without adding unbounded reals. This question is interesting in its own right.

Question 7
.

Under which conditions on a generalized Aronszajn tree
T
is there a proper forcing notion
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
which specializes
T
without adding unbounded reals? Cohen reals? What if we drop “proper”?

If we replace “not adding unbounded (or Cohen) reals” with “not adding reals at all” then more is known about this question. Indeed Jensen, under
V=L
and, Shelah, with no additional hypothesis, have shown that for any (thin) Aronszajn tree
T
there is a proper forcing notion specializing
T
without adding reals and this is iterable, see
[
24
, Chapter V, Theorem 6.1]
. As a result they obtain models of
\mathsf{CH}\land\neg\diamondsuit
. In
[
25
]
the author investigated when “Aronszajn tree” could be replaced by “generalized Aronszjan tree of cardinality
\omega_{1}
” and gave a sufficient combinatorial condition on trees for modifying Shelah’s aforementioned forcing to the wide case. However we cannot hope to find for any generalized Aronszajn tree of size
\aleph_{1}
a stationary set preserving forcing notion specializing it without adding reals under
\mathsf{CH}
since the tree
T(S)
discussed in Proposition
2.3
cannot be made special without either adding reals or killing the stationarity of
S
.

Question 8
.

Are there (consistently) generalized Aronszajn trees
T
so that in any forcing extension in which
T
is special there is a Cohen real over the ground model?

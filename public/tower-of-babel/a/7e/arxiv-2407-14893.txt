Critical dimensions for polyharmonic operators: The Pucci-Serrin conjecture for solutions of bounded energy
Frédéric Robert

Abstract
. We prove a Pucci-Serrin conjecture on critical dimensions under a uniform bound on the energy. The method is based on the analysis of the Green’s function of polyharmonic operators with ”almost” Hardy potential.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 35J35, Secondary 35J60, 35B44, 35J08

1.
Introduction

Let
B
be the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n>2k\geq 2
. Consider
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
such that

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{k}u-\lambda u=|u|^{2^{\star}-2}u&\hbox{ in }B\\
u=\partial_{\nu}u=...=\partial_{\nu}^{k-1}u=0&\hbox{ on }\partial B\end{array}\right\}

where
2^{\star}:=\frac{2n}{n-2k}
. A very interesting conjecture of Pucci and Serrin (
[
pucci-serrin
]
, p58) is stated as follows:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
B
be the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n>2k\geq 2
. Assume that

2k<n<4k.

Then there exists
\lambda_{0}(n,k)>0
such that for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}(n,k)
, any radial solution to (
1
) is identically null.

Edmunds-Fortunato-Janelli
[
EFJ
]
and Grunau
[
grunau:1995
]
proved that there exists a positive radial solution to (
1
) for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
when
n>4k
, where
\lambda_{1}>0
is the first eigenvalue of
\Delta^{k}
on
B
with Dirichlet boundary condition. In particular, the expected range
(2k,4k)
is optimal. In this paper, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
B
be the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n>2k\geq 2
. Assume that

2k<n<4k.

Then, for any
M>0
, there exists
\lambda_{0}(n,k,M)>0
such that for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}(n,k,M)
, any radial solution to (
1
) satisfying that
\|u\|_{2^{\star}}\leq M
is identically null.

Concerning terminology, Pucci-Serrin defined that a dimension
n>2k
is
critical
if there exists
\lambda_{0}(n,k)>0
such that any radial solution of (
1
) is identically null when
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}(n,k)
. Theorem
1.1
proves the conjecture under any arbitrary fixed bound on the Lebesgue’s norm.

Here is a brief history of the problem. The conjecture turns to be true in the following situations:

•

k=1
(Brézis-Nirenberg
[
brezis-nirenberg
]
);

•

k=2
(Pucci-Serrin
[
pucci-serrin
]
);

•

k\geq 2
et
n=2k+1
(Pucci-Serrin
[
pucci-serrin
]
);

•

k\geq 2
et
2k<n<2k+6
(Bernis-Grunau
[
bernis-grunau
]
and Grunau
[
grunau:II
]
).

In these situations, the proofs are based on Pohozaev-type identities for radial functions. The larger
k
is, the trickier and longer the computations are and achieving
n<2k+6
is a true ”tour de force”. Moreover, beside the computational difficulties, the methods in these papers do not seem enough to tackle the full conjecture (see Grunau
[
grunau:II
]
for discussions on this issue).

The case of positive functions is interesting in itself. Grunau
[
grunau:positive
]
proved the validity of the conjecture when restricted to positive functions (
weakly critical dimensions
). In this situation, the key is to test a solution
u
to (
1
) against a carefully chosen positive polyharmonic function on
\overline{B}
. The case of arbitrary sign-changing solutions involved in the original conjecture, the one we address here, is much more involved.

As a final remark, we mention that Jannelli
[
jannelli
]
has formalized the notion of
critical dimensions
in a more general setting by connecting it to the
L^{2}-
integrability of the Green’s function.

In the present paper, we adopt a new approach that is based on the concentration analysis of families of solutions to (
1
): this permits to develop a method that is uniform and independent of the value of the power
k
. This approach is particularly relevant due to the critical exponent
2^{\star}
that may tolerate an unbounded family of solutions as
\lambda\to 0
: in this situation, this family should concentrate along explicit profiles referred to as bubbles. The general theory for second-order problems (
k=1
) has been performed in Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
DHR
]
for positive solutions and was based on the comparison principle, see also Hebey
[
hebey:zurich
]
for a modern point of view on such issues. We refer also to Druet-Laurain
[
dl
]
regarding a method for positive solutions and to Premoselli
[
premoselli
]
for a more recent and promising approach for sign-changing solutions. We also refer to Carletti
[
carletti
]
for a beautiful asymptotic analysis when
k>1
.

Due to the sign-change and to the lack of comparison principle when
k\geq 2
, we develop tools based on Green’s representation formula for a linear equations. More precisely, we rewrite (
1
) as
Pu=0\,+\,\{\hbox{bdy conditions}\}
where
P=\Delta^{k}-\lambda-|u|^{2^{\star}-2}
and we express
u
in terms of the Green’s function of
P
. The core and the bulk of our analysis is to get a sharp pointwise control of this Green’s function, which is the object of Theorem
5.2
. This control is based on the regularity Lemma
6.1
for solutions to linear equations with ”almost” Hardy-type potential.

The hypothesis on radial symmetry is essential. In addition to prescribing concentration at the center of the ball, radiality forces solutions of
\Delta^{k}u=|u|^{2^{\star}-2}u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to have a fixed sign, see Theorem
2.1
below. This does not happen in the non-radial case as shown for instance by Molica Bisci and Pucci
[
MBP
]
.

Most of the analysis is valid for any elliptic operator like
\Delta^{k}+...
: the restriction
n<4k
and the specificity of
\Delta^{k}-\lambda
are used only for the final argument involving the Pohozaev-Pucci-Serrin identity. We will make an intensive use of the elliptic regularity of the reference Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg
[
ADN
]
. For the convenience of the reader, the last section
7
is a collection of results contained in
[
ADN
]
.

Notations:

C(a,b,...)
will denote any constant depending only on
a,b,...
. The same notation might refer different constants from line to line, and even in the same line.

2.
Preliminary analysis

We prove Theorem
1.1
by contradiction. We fix
M>0
. If Theorem
1.1
is not true, then there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
radially symmetrical such that

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{k}u_{i}-\lambda_{i}u=|u_{i}|^{2^{\star}-2}u_{i}&\hbox{ in }B\\
u_{i}=\partial_{\nu}u_{i}=...=\partial_{\nu}^{k-1}u_{i}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial B\\
u_{i}\not\equiv 0&\\
\|u_{i}\|_{2^{\star}}\leq M&\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=0&\end{array}\right\}

In order to simplify the exposition, we assume that there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, there exists
u_{\lambda}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
radially symmetrical such that

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{k}u_{\lambda}-\lambda u_{\lambda}=|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}-2}u_{\lambda}&\hbox{ in }B\\
u_{\lambda}=\partial_{\nu}u_{\lambda}=...=\partial_{\nu}^{k-1}u_{\lambda}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial B\\
u_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0&\\
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2^{\star}}\leq M&\end{array}\right\}

We are performing an analysis of
u_{\lambda}
as
\lambda\to 0
. All the results and statements will be up to the extraction of subfamilies, although we will always refer to
u_{\lambda}
. A preliminary remark is that
u_{\lambda}\in C^{2k+1,\theta}(B)
,
0<\theta<1
, due to elliptic regularity.

2.1.
Sobolev spaces and inequalities

For any
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a smooth domain,
p\geq 1
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
H_{l}^{p}(\Omega)
(resp.
H_{l,0}^{p}(\Omega)
) as the completion of
\{u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\hbox{ s.t. }\|u\|_{H_{l}^{p}}<\infty\}
(resp.
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
) for the norm
u\mapsto\|u\|_{H_{l}^{p}}:=\sum_{i\leq l}\|\nabla^{i}u\|_{p}
. Given a finite set
S\subset\Omega
, we define
L_{loc}^{p}(\Omega\setminus S)=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\hbox{ s.t. }\eta u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\hbox{ for all }\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S)\}
,
H_{l,loc}^{p}(\Omega\setminus S)=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\hbox{ s.t. }\eta u\in H_{l}^{p}(\Omega)\hbox{ for all }\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S)\}
and
H_{l,0,loc}^{p}(\Omega\setminus S)=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}/\,\hbox{ s.t. }\eta u\in H_{l,0}^{p}(\Omega)\hbox{ for all }\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S)\}
. This notation is a bit abusive since
\Omega\setminus S
is open, but there will be no ambiguity in this paper. In the specific case
p=2
and
\Omega
is bounded, note that on
H_{k,0}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\|\cdot\|_{H^{2}_{k}}
is equivalent to
u\mapsto\|\Delta^{k/2}u\|_{2}
. Here and in the sequel,
\Delta^{\frac{i}{2}}=\nabla\Delta^{\frac{i-1}{2}}
when
i
is odd. Note that for
u\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\Omega
a smooth bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\Omega
is a half-space, then
\{u\in H_{k,0}^{2}(\Omega)\}\Leftrightarrow\{u=\partial_{\nu}u=...=\partial_{\nu}^{k-1}u=0\hbox{ on }\partial\Omega\}
.

We let
D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the norm
u\mapsto\|\Delta^{k/2}u\|_{2}
. It follows from Sobolev’s theorem that there exists
K(n,k)>0
such that

(4)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}}}\leq K(n,k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta^{\frac{k}{2}}u)^{2}\,dx\hbox{ for all }u\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

As one checks, this inequality is a also valid for all
u\in H_{k,0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
be a family radially symmetrical solution to (
3
). Then
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}=+\infty
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If the conclusion does not hold, then there exists
C>0
such that
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
\lambda>0
. It follows from elliptic theory (Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) that
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{C^{2k,1/2}}\leq C
for all
\lambda>0
. It then follows from Ascoli’s theorem that there exists
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
such that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}u_{\lambda}=u_{0}
in
C^{2k}(\overline{B})
. Passing to the limit in (
3
) yields

(5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{k}u_{0}=|u_{0}|^{2^{\star}-2}u_{0}&\hbox{ in }B\\
u_{0}=\partial_{\nu}u_{0}=...=\partial_{\nu}^{k-1}u_{0}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial B\end{array}\right\}

It then follows from Lazzo-Schmidt (point (a) of Corollary 3.10 of
[
ls
]
) that
u_{0}\equiv 0
.

Multiplying (
3
) by
u_{\lambda}
, integrating by parts and using Hölder’s inequality yield

\displaystyle\int_{B}(\Delta^{k/2}u_{\lambda})^{2}\,dx=\int_{B}u_{\lambda}\Delta^{k}u_{\lambda}\,dx=\lambda\int_{B}u_{\lambda}^{2}\,dx+\int_{B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx\leq C\lambda\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2^{\star}}^{2}+\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2^{\star}}^{2^{\star}}.

With the Sobolev inequality (
4
) and using that
u_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
and
u_{\lambda}\in H_{k,0}^{2}(B)
, we get that
K(n,k)^{-1}\leq C\lambda+\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2^{\star}}^{2^{\star}-2}
. Passing to the limit
\lambda\to 0
and using that
u_{0}\equiv 0
, we get a contradiction. This proves the Lemma.∎

Note that as a consequence of the preceding argument,
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is bounded in
H_{k,0}^{2}(B)
, that is there exists
C(M)>0
such that
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H_{k}^{2}}\leq C(M)
for all
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(y_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in B
and
(r_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be such that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}r_{\lambda}^{-1}|y_{\lambda}|=+\infty
. Then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\int_{B_{r_{\lambda}}(y_{\lambda})\cap B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx=0.

Proof.

Let us fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a group of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, say
G
, such that
\sharp G\geq N
and there exists
\epsilon_{N}>0
such that
d(\sigma(e_{1}),\tau(e_{1}))\geq\epsilon_{N}
for all
\sigma,\tau\in G
,
\sigma\neq\tau
. Here,
e_{1}
is the first vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, as one checks,
B_{r_{\lambda}}(\sigma(y_{\lambda}))\cap B_{r_{\lambda}}(\tau(y_{\lambda}))=\emptyset
for all
\sigma,\tau\in G
,
\sigma\neq\tau
and
\lambda>0
is small enough. With the invariance of
u_{\lambda}
under the action of the group
G
, we get that

\displaystyle M^{2^{\star}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx\geq\int_{\bigcup_{\sigma\in G}B_{r_{\lambda}}(\sigma(y_{\lambda}))\cap B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{B_{r_{\lambda}}(\sigma(y_{\lambda}))\cap B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx=\sharp G\int_{B_{r_{\lambda}}(y_{\lambda})\cap B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx

and therefore

\int_{B_{r_{\lambda}}(y_{\lambda})\cap B}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx\leq\frac{M^{2^{\star}}}{N}\hbox{ as }\lambda\to 0.

Since this is valid for all
N
, the conclusion follows.∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
be a family radially symmetrical solution to (
3
). Then there exists
C>0
such that
|x|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|u_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C
for all
x\in B
and
\lambda\to 0
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by contradiction. We set
w_{\lambda}(x):=|x|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|u_{\lambda}(x)|
for all
x\in B
and
\lambda>0
. Let us assume that

w_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda}):=\sup_{x\in B}w_{\lambda}(x)\to+\infty\hbox{ as }\lambda\to 0.

We define
r_{\lambda}:=|u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})|^{-\frac{2}{n-2k}}
. We have that

(6)

\frac{|y_{\lambda}|}{r_{\lambda}}=w_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})^{\frac{2}{n-2k}}\to\infty\hbox{ and }r_{\lambda}\to 0\hbox{ as }\lambda\to 0.

Case 1:
assume that

(7)

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{d(y_{\lambda},\partial B)}{r_{\lambda}}=+\infty.

We define

v_{\lambda}(x):=r_{\lambda}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda}+r_{\lambda}x)\hbox{ for }x\in\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}.

A change of variable in (
3
) yields

(8)

\Delta^{k}v_{\lambda}-\lambda r_{\lambda}^{2k}v_{\lambda}=|v_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}-2}v_{\lambda}\hbox{ in }\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}.

It follows from the definition of
y_{\lambda}
that

|y_{\lambda}+r_{\lambda}x|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda}+r_{\lambda}x)|\leq|y_{\lambda}|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})|\hbox{ for }x\in\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}},

and then

\left|\frac{y_{\lambda}}{|y_{\lambda}|}+\frac{r_{\lambda}}{|y_{\lambda}|}x\right|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|v_{\lambda}(x)|\leq 1\hbox{ for }x\in\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}.

We fix
R>0
. It follows from (
7
) and the above inequality that there exists
\lambda_{R}>0
such that

B_{R}(0)\subset\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}\hbox{ and }|v_{\lambda}(x)|\leq 2\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{R}(0)\hbox{ and }0<\lambda<\lambda_{R}.

With (
8
), it then follows from elliptic theory (Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) and Ascoli’s theorem that there exists
v\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}v_{\lambda}=v
in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Given
R>0
, with a change of variable, we get that

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx=\int_{B_{Rr_{\lambda}}(y_{\lambda})}|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}}\,dx.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
and (
6
) that passing to the limit yields
\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v|^{2^{\star}}\,dx=0
for all
R>0
, so that
v\equiv 0
since it is continuous. However, since
|v_{\lambda}(0)|=1
, we get that
|v(0)|=1
, which contradicts
v\equiv 0
. This ends Case 1.

Case 2:

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{d(y_{\lambda},\partial B)}{r_{\lambda}}=\rho\in[0,+\infty).

Up to a rotation, we then get that

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{B-y_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}=(-\infty,\rho)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

The proof is then similar to Case 1 by working on this half-space. We leave the details to the reader. This yields also to a contradiction.

In both cases, we have gotten a contradiction, which proves the Lemma.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
be a family radially symmetrical solution to (
3
). Then
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}u_{\lambda}=0
in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\overline{B}\setminus\{0\})
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.3
that for all
\delta>0
, there exists
C(\delta)>0
such that
|u_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C(\delta)
for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in B\setminus B_{\delta}(0)
. It follows from elliptic theory (Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) and Ascoli’s theorem that there exists
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B}\setminus\{0\})
such that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}u_{\lambda}=u_{0}
in
C^{2k}_{loc}(\overline{B}\setminus\{0\})
. Since
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H_{k}^{2}}\leq C(M)
for all
\lambda>0
, we also get that
u_{0}\in H_{k,0}^{2}(B)
and
u_{\lambda}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H_{k,0}^{2}(B)
. Passing to the limit
\lambda\to 0
in (
3
), we get that
u_{0}
is a weak solution to (
5
). Regularity theory (see Van der Vorst
[
vdv
]
and Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) yields
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
is a strong solution to (
5
), and then
u_{0}\equiv 0
by
[
ls
]
since it is radial. This proves the Lemma.∎

We will make use of the following classification:

Theorem 2.1

(Swanson
[
swanson
]
)
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such
2\leq 2k<n
. Let
u\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a distributional solution to
\Delta^{k}u=|u|^{2^{\star}-2}u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
u
is radially symmetric. Then there exists
\mu>0
and
\epsilon\in\{-1,0,+1\}
such that

u(x)=\epsilon\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}+a_{n,k}|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}},\hbox{ where }a_{n,k}:=\left(\Pi_{j=-k}^{k-1}(n+2j)\right)^{-\frac{1}{k}}.

Proof.

Although Swanson’s Theorem 4 in
[
swanson
]
is only stated for positive functions, the proof is working for any functions. More precisely, if
u(0)\neq 0
, we follow exactly Swanson’s proof. If
u(0)=0
, the arguments of Swanson (Lemma 7) yield
u\equiv 0
.∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(y_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in B
be such that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}|y_{\lambda}|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}|u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})|=c\in(0,+\infty)
. Then there exists
(r_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\in(0,+\infty)
such
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}r_{\lambda}=0
,
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}r_{\lambda}^{-1}|y_{\lambda}|=c^{\prime}\in(0,+\infty)
and

(9)

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}r_{\lambda}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\lambda}(r_{\lambda}\cdot)=\epsilon U\hbox{ in }C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

for some
\epsilon\in\{-1,+1\}
where

(10)

U(x)=\left(\frac{1}{1+a_{n,k}|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\hbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.4
that
y_{\lambda}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
. We set
s_{\lambda}:=|y_{\lambda}|
and we define
W_{\lambda}(x):=s_{\lambda}^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\lambda}(s_{\lambda}x)
for
x\in B_{1/s_{\lambda}}(0)
and
\lambda>0
. Lemma
2.3
yields

(11)

|W_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{1/s_{\lambda}}(0)\hbox{ and }\lambda>0.

A change of variable in (
3
) yields

(12)

\Delta^{k}W_{\lambda}-\lambda s_{\lambda}^{2k}W_{\lambda}=|W_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}-2}W_{\lambda}\hbox{ in }B_{1/s_{\lambda}}(0).

Due to elliptic theory (Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) and Ascoli’s theorem, (
11
) and (
12
) yield the existence of
W\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
such that

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}W_{\lambda}=W\hbox{ in }C^{2k}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

Since
W_{\lambda}\left(\frac{y_{\lambda}}{|y_{\lambda}|}\right)=|y_{\lambda}|^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}u_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})
, passing to the limit
\lambda\to 0
yields
|W(Y_{0})|=c>0
where
Y_{0}:=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{y_{\lambda}}{|y_{\lambda}|}
. Therefore
W\not\equiv 0
.

We prove that
W\in D_{k}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let us fix
l\in\{0,...,k\}
. It follows from Sobolev’s embedding that there exists
C(l,k,n)>0
such that

(13)

\left(\int_{B}|\nabla^{l}\varphi|^{2^{\star}(l)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}(l)}}\leq C(l,k,n)\int_{B}(\Delta^{k/2}\varphi)^{2}\,dx

for all
\varphi\in H_{k,0}^{2}(B)
, where
2^{\star}(l):=\frac{2n}{n-2(k-l)}
. Given
R>0
, with a change of variable, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)\setminus B_{R^{-1}}(0)}|\nabla^{l}W_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}(l)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}(l)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{Rr_{\lambda}}(0)\setminus B_{R^{-1}r_{\lambda}}(0)}|\nabla^{l}u_{\lambda}|^{2^{\star}(l)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}(l)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(l,k,n)\int_{B}(\Delta^{k/2}u_{\lambda})^{2}\,dx\leq C

since
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H_{k}^{2}}
is uniformly bounded. Letting
\lambda\to 0
and
R\to+\infty
yields

Therefore, setting

3.
Sharp analysis at the furthest scale

Proposition 3.1

4.
Conclusion via the Pohozaev-Pucci-Serrin identity

The following identities are essentially in Pucci-Serrin
[
pucci-serrin-var-id
]
and are generalizations of the historical Pohozaev identity
[
pohozaev
]
. We recall them for the sake of completeness. The first lemma is a straightforward iteration:

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{p}(x^{i}\partial_{i}v)=2p\Delta^{p}v+x^{i}\partial_{i}\Delta^{p}v&\hbox{ for all }p\in\mathbb{N}\hbox{ and }\\
\partial_{j}\Delta^{p}(x^{i}\partial_{i}v)=(2p+1)\partial_{j}\Delta^{p}v+x^{i}\partial_{i}(\partial_{j}\Delta^{p}v)&\hbox{ for all }p\in\mathbb{N}\hbox{ and }j=1,...,n.\end{array}\right\}.

These identities rewrite
\Delta^{\frac{l}{2}}(x^{i}\partial_{i}v)=l\Delta^{\frac{l}{2}}v+x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta^{\frac{l}{2}}v)
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain with
2\leq 2k<n
. Then for all
u\in C^{2k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta^{k}u-c|u|^{2^{\star}-2}u\right)T(u)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left((x,\nu)\left(\frac{|\Delta^{\frac{k}{2}}u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{c|u|^{2^{\star}}}{2^{\star}}\right)+S(u)\right)\,d\sigma

where
T(u):=\frac{n-2k}{2}u+x^{i}\partial_{i}u
and

(24)

\displaystyle S(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{E(k/2)-1}\left(-\partial_{\nu}\Delta^{k-i-1}u\Delta^{i}T(u)+\Delta^{k-i-1}u\partial_{\nu}\Delta^{i}T(u)\right)

\displaystyle-{\bf 1}_{\{k\hbox{ odd}\}}\partial_{\nu}(\Delta^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\Delta^{\frac{k-1}{2}}T(u)

Proof.

Integrating by parts, for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\geq 1
,

(25)

\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{l}U)V\,dX=\int_{\Omega}U(\Delta^{l}V)\,dX+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{(l)}(U,V)\,d\sigma

(26)

We first assume that

is even, with

4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta^{k}u-c|u|^{2^{\star}-2}u\right)T(u)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{p}u\Delta^{p}T(u)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{(p)}(\Delta^{p}u,T(u))\,d\sigma

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\Omega}c|u|^{2^{\star}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}cx^{i}\frac{\partial_{i}|u|^{2^{\star}}}{2^{\star}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{p}\left(\frac{n}{2}\Delta^{p}u+x^{i}\partial_{i}(\Delta^{p}u)\right)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{(p)}(\Delta^{p}u,T(u))\,d\sigma

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}\int_{\Omega}c|u|^{2^{\star}}\,dx-\frac{n}{2^{\star}}\int_{\Omega}c|u|^{2^{\star}}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)\frac{c|u|^{2^{\star}}}{2^{\star}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{x^{i}(\Delta^{p}u)^{2}}{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{(p)}(\Delta^{p}u,T(u))\,d\sigma-\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)\frac{c|u|^{2^{\star}}}{2^{\star}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left((x,\nu)\left(\frac{(\Delta^{p}u)^{2}}{2}-\frac{c|u|^{2^{\star}}}{2^{\star}}\right)+\mathcal{B}^{(p)}(\Delta^{p}u,T(u))\right)\,d\sigma

which proves Proposition
4.1
when
k
is even. When
k=2q+1
is odd, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{k}uT(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{q}(\Delta^{q+1}u)T(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{q+1}u\Delta^{q}T(u)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{B}^{(q)}(\Delta^{q+1}u,T(u))\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j}\partial_{j}(\Delta^{q}u)\partial_{j}(\Delta^{q}T(u))\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mathcal{B}^{(q)}(\Delta^{q+1}u,T(u))-\partial_{\nu}(\Delta^{q}u)\Delta^{q}T(u)\right)\,d\sigma

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{k}uT(u)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j}\partial_{j}(\Delta^{q}u)\left(\frac{n}{2}\partial_{j}\Delta^{q}u+x^{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Delta^{q}u\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mathcal{B}^{(q)}(\Delta^{q+1}u,T(u))-\partial_{\nu}(\Delta^{q}u)\Delta^{q}T(u)\right)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}\left(x^{i}\frac{(\partial_{j}\Delta^{q}u)^{2}}{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mathcal{B}^{(q)}(\Delta^{q+1}u,T(u))-\partial_{\nu}(\Delta^{q}u)\Delta^{q}T(u)\right)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}(x,\nu)\frac{|\nabla\Delta^{q}u|^{2}}{2}\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\mathcal{B}^{(q)}(\Delta^{q+1}u,T(u))-\partial_{\nu}(\Delta^{q}u)\Delta^{q}T(u)\right)\,d\sigma

Using the same computations as in the case when

4.1
.∎

6.
The regularity Lemma

For any domain

such that

and

if

, where

and

.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be such that

and
\delta,L>0
. Fix

and
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0

. Then for all
0<\gamma<n-2k
, there exists
\mu=\mu(\gamma,p,L,\delta_{1},\delta_{2})>0
and
C_{0}=C_{0}(\gamma,p,L,\delta_{1},\delta_{2})>0

such that

|V(x)|\leq\mu|x|^{-2k}\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{\delta_{2}}(0),

then for any

(for some

then we have that

(78)

|x|^{\gamma}|\varphi(x)|\leq C_{0}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(B_{\delta_{2}}(0))}\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{\delta_{1}}(0)-\{0\}.

and

\|\varphi\|_{H_{k}^{2}(B_{\delta_{1}}(0))}\leq C_{0}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(B_{\delta_{2}}(0))}.

Moreover, for any

, there exists

(79)

|x|^{\gamma+l}|\nabla^{l}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{l}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(B_{\delta_{2}}(0))}\hbox{ for all }x\in B_{\delta_{1}}(0)-\{0\}

For the reader’s convenience, we set
\delta:=\delta_{1}
and we assume that
\delta_{2}=3\delta_{1}=3\delta
. The general case follows the same proof by changing
2\delta
,
2.9\delta
, etc, into various radii

such that

Step 1: Proof of (

) when

around

Parabolic methods for ultraspherical interpolation inequalities ∗ Corresponding author: A. Zhang
Jean Dolbeault, and An Zhang

Abstract
The carré du champ method is a powerful technique for proving interpolation inequalities with explicit constants in presence of a non-trivial metric on a manifold. The method applies to some classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities on the sphere, with optimal constants. Very nonlinear regimes close to the critical Sobolev exponent can be covered using nonlinear parabolic flows of porous medium or fast diffusion type. Considering power law weights is a natural question in relation with symmetry breaking issues for Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities, but regularity estimates for a complete justification of the computation are missing. We provide the first example of a complete parabolic proof based on a nonlinear flow by regularizing the singularity induced by the weight. Our result is established in the simplified framework of a diffusion built on the ultraspherical operator, which amounts to reduce the problem to functions on the sphere with simple symmetry properties.

1
Introduction and main results

The
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev interpolation inequality
,

\tfrac{p-2}{d}\,\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}+\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)\,,

is usually attributed to W. Beckner
[
5
]
but can also be found in
[
7
, Corollary 6.1]
. With some restriction on
p
, it was even proved earlier in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
by the
carré du champ
method. Here
d\mu
is the uniform probability measure on the
d
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
corresponding to the measure induced by Lebesgue’s measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, up to a normalization constant
1/|\mathbb{S}^{d}|
, and
p
is an arbitrary exponent in the range
2<p\leq 2^{*}
if
d\geq 3
, where the critical exponent is defined as
2^{*}:=2\,d/(d-2)
. The inequality holds true for any finite
p>2
if
d=1
or
2
: in this case, we adopt the convention that
2^{*}=+\infty
and shall write that
p\in(2,2^{*})
. The norms are defined with respect to
d\mu
, that is,

\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|u|^{q}}\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}\,.

We can rewrite the interpolation inequality as

\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}-\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)\,,

(1)

which now makes sense for any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*}]
if
d\geq 3
, and for any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,+\infty)
if
d=1
or
d=2
. The case
p=2^{*}
is the
critical
case of the inequality and the usual Sobolev inequality on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with optimal constant, is recovered using the stereographic projection. The case
p=2
has of course to be excluded, but it is easy to pass to the limit in (
1
) as
p\to 2
and obtain the logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
i.e.
,

\|{\nabla u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{d}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|u|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{\|{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu)\setminus\{0\}\,.

(2)

For brevity, we shall refer to this case as the
p=2
case. Equality in (
1
) and (
2
) is achieved by constant functions. The constants
d/(p-2)
in (
1
) and
d/2
in (
2
) are optimal. If
\varphi
is an eigenfunction (a spherical harmonic function) of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
such that
-\,\Delta\varphi=d\,\varphi
, we use
u=1+\varepsilon\,\varphi
as a test function. A Taylor expansion at order two in
\varepsilon\to 0
shows that (
1
) and (
2
) cannot hold with larger constants. This is of course the easy part of the result. The really difficult part is to prove that (
1
) and (
2
) are true respectively with the constants
d/(p-2)
and
d/2
. Over the years various proofs have been proposed:

1)

The strategy of W. Beckner in
[
5
]
relies on the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities of E.H. Lieb in
[
32
]
, Legendre duality and monotonicity properties of the coefficients in a decomposition on spherical harmonics.

2)

The proof of M.-F. Bidaut-Véron and L. Véron in
[
7
]
is based on nonlinear elliptic methods initiated for the study of critical points in
[
31
]
.

3)

The
carré du champ
method introduced by D. Bakry and M. Émery in
[
2
]
uses semi-groups defined by linear parabolic flows and it is restricted for
d\geq 2
to
p\leq 2^{\#}
where
2^{\#}:=\frac{2\,d^{2}+1}{(d-1)^{2}}<2^{*}
. We refer to
[
4
]
for a general presentation of the method and results which go far beyond functional inequalities, and to
[
30
,
26
]
for recent developments.

4)

There are various other proofs based on stereographic projection and symmetrizations, transformations of Emden-Fowler type as in
[
34
]
or
[
19
]
, mass transport in
[
13
]
, inversion symmetry and direct use of spectral properties in
[
29
]
, at least for some specific values of
p
, which we will not discuss here.

It turns out that the approaches 2) and 3) are based on similar computations as was discussed by J. Demange in
[
14
]
and the restriction
p\leq 2^{\#}
can be lifted by extending the
carré du champ
method to nonlinear parabolic equations of porous medium and fast diffusion type. The overall strategy is based on the
entropy methods
: see
[
10
,
9
,
11
]
for early results on nonlinear parabolic equations by the
carré du champ
method,
[
15
]
for a recent review and
[
8
]
for further details. The method has been made systematic in a series of papers
[
17
,
20
,
18
,
23
,
16
]
with important consequences on
symmetry and symmetry breaking
issues studied in
[
22
,
25
,
24
]
, on which we will come back below.

The point of view of 3) is beautiful as it provides a clear structure and, at least from a formal point of view, a gradient flow interpretation. This allows to order computations which are otherwise extremely complicated in 2) and involve a rather non-intuitive use of test functions. However, there is a price to pay as the regularity needed to justify all necessary integrations by parts is not
a priori
granted for the solutions of the nonlinear parabolic equations when singular weights are present. A major advantage of 2) is indeed that the elliptic regularity theory can be invoked to justify the computations.

In this paper, we are interested in
weighted interpolation inequalities
of the form

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}\geq\frac{\mathscr{C}_{n,p}}{p-2}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|u|^{p}}\,d\mu_{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}\right)\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu_{n})

(3)

where
d\mu_{n}=Z_{n}^{-1}\,\rho^{n-d}\,d\mu
is a probability measure built on
d\mu
with a weight
\rho^{n-d}
where
\rho=\rho(x)
is such that
\rho(x)^{2}=1-(x\cdot\mathsf{e})^{2}
, for some given, fixed
\mathsf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and
n
is generically
not an integer
. A motivation to study of (
3
) is for instance to consider the case
0<n<d
so that the weight has a singularity at
x=\mathsf{e}
, or the case
n>d
so that the operator associated to the Dirichlet form degenerates at
x=\mathsf{e}
. Because of the rotation invariance, there is no loss of generality in assuming that
\mathsf{e}
is the north pole and we shall consider cylindrical coordinates
(z,\omega)\in[-1,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
x=z\,\mathsf{e}+\rho\,\omega
where
\rho=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}
. A deep reason for studying (
3
) comes from its relation with some
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities
(see
[
26
]
for more details), which goes as follows. Let us consider the stereographic projection from
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(with cylindrical coordinates) onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(with spherical coordinates) given by

[-1,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\ni(z,\omega)\mapsto(s,\omega)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

where

\textstyle s=\sqrt{\frac{1+z}{1-z}}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;z=\frac{s^{2}-1}{s^{2}+1}=1-\frac{2}{s^{2}+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}=\frac{2\,s}{s^{2}+1}\,.

To the function
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\mu_{n})
, we associate the function on
w
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

w(s,\omega)=\big(\tfrac{2}{1+s^{2}}\big)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\,u(z,\omega)\,.

Here we consider the case
p=2\,n/(n-2)
. Inequality (
3
) becomes

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,s^{n-1}\,ds\,d\omega\geq\frac{\mathscr{C}_{n,p}}{p-2}\,|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|w|^{p}\,s^{n-1}\,ds\,d\omega\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

which can be rewritten simply as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\nabla w|^{2}\,|x|^{n-d}}\,dx\geq\frac{\mathscr{C}_{n,p}}{p-2}\,|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|w|^{p}\,|x|^{n-d}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\,.

(4)

To relate (
4
) with the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities, let us do one more change of variables as in
[
22
]
and define

f(r,\omega)=w(s,\omega)\quad\mbox{with}\quad s=r^{\alpha}\,,\quad\alpha=\frac{d-2-2\,\mathsf{a}}{n-2}=\frac{1}{4}\,(d-2-2\,\mathsf{a})\,(p-2)

for some new parameter
\mathsf{a}\in(0,(d-2)/2)
. With

\textstyle\mathsf{b}:=\mathsf{a}+1-\frac{d}{n}\,,\quad\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}}=\frac{\mathscr{C}_{n,p}}{p-2}\,\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{d}|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{\alpha^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}f:=\left(\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\,\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial r},\tfrac{1}{r}\,\nabla_{\omega}f\right),

we can reformulate (
3
) as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}f|^{2}}{|x|^{2\,\mathsf{a}}}}\,dx\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|f|^{p}}{|x|^{\mathsf{b}\,p}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\,.

(5)

These equivalences are of course formal: the range of the exponents has limitations and in the case
n>d\geq 3
for instance, we should consider only
p=2\,p/(d-2+2\,\mathsf{b}-2\,\mathsf{a})
with the restriction
0\leq\mathsf{a}\leq\mathsf{b}\leq\mathsf{a}+1<d/2
. Moreover, the functional setting has to be properly defined by considering either functions
f
which are smooth with compact support in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, or a space obtained by completion with respect to the norm corresponding to the left-hand side in (
5
). A standard issue is to decide whether optimality is achieved among radial functions or not. This can be done by elliptic methods: see
[
12
,
27
,
22
]
. However, the intuition of the proof of
[
22
]
relies on the
carré du champ
method and, as it is discussed at length in
[
21
]
, a complete parabolic proof is so far missing:
the extension of the carré du champ method to weighted, nonlinear diffusion equations
is only formal.

In this paper, we focus on a parabolic approach of (
3
) based on a
regularization of the weight
and the
carré du champ method
. The discussion of the symmetry issues will be ignored as it involves various cases and requires elementary although very technical computations. For this reason, we shall restrict our study to symmetric functions on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
depending only on the
z
variable. In that setting, the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator becomes the
d
-ultraspherical operator
while the operator associated with (
3
) is now an
n
-ultraspherical operator
for some real valued parameter
n
.

Let us consider functions of the variable
z\in[-1,1]
. We define the probability measure

d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}(z):=Z_{n}^{-1}\,\rho^{n-2}\,dz\quad\mbox{where}\quad Z_{n}:=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}\,,\quad\rho=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\,,

for some real parameter
n>0
, the space
\mathrm{L}^{2}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}))
with scalar product
\left\langle{f_{1}},{f_{2}}\right\rangle:=\int_{-1}^{1}{f_{1}\,f_{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}
and the corresponding norm
\|f\|_{2}:=\left\langle{f},{f}\right\rangle^{1/2}
. For any
q>2
, let

\|f\|_{q}=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}{|f|^{q}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\right)^{1/q}\,.

The self-adjoint
ultraspherical
operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,f:=(1-z^{2})\,f^{\prime\prime}-n\,z\,f^{\prime}=\rho^{2}\,f^{\prime\prime}-n\,z\,f^{\prime}

has the fundamental property

\left\langle{f_{1}},{{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,f_{2}}\right\rangle=-\int_{-1}^{1}{f_{1}^{\prime}\,f_{2}^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

(6)

and if
f=f_{1}=f_{2}
, we have
-\left\langle{f},{{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,f}\right\rangle=\|\rho\,f^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}
. We denote by
\mathrm{H}^{1}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn})
the space of the functions
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn})
such that
\|\rho\,f^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\|f\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. We extend the notion of
critical exponent
using

2^{*}:=\frac{2\,n}{n-2}\quad\forall\,n>2

(7)

and adopt the convention that
2^{*}=+\infty
if
n\leq 2
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n>0
. Then for any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
if
n\leq 2
and any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*}]
if
n>2
, we have

\|\rho\,f^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{n}{p-2}\left(\|f\|_{p}^{2}-\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn})\,,

(8)

while for
p=2
, we have

\|\rho\,f^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{n}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}{f^{2}\log\left(\frac{f^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn})\,.

(9)

The constants in (
8
) and (
9
) are sharp.

The logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
9
) has been proved in
[
33
, Theorem 1]
for any real number
n\in(0,+\infty)
. It is obtained by taking the limit as
p\to 2
and we assume from now on that
p\neq 2
without further notice. Inequality (
8
) with
p=2^{*}
and a real parameter
n>2
can be found in
[
34
]
and in the whole range of
p>2
in
[
6
]
with a proof based on elliptic estimates which is very similar to the proof of
[
7
]
. Inspired by
[
14
]
, results for the ultraspherical operator with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
were proved in
[
17
,
18
,
23
]
using entropy methods, linear and nonlinear diffusion equations and appropriate versions of the carré du champ method. As far as we know, when
n
is not an integer, no rigorous proof based on flows has been provided if
n>1
and
2^{\#}<p<2^{*}
, where

2^{\#}:=\frac{2\,n^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}

(10)

is the
Bakry-Émery exponent
. The ultraspherical operator is considered in
[
2
]
and the limitation to the range
p\leq 2^{\#}
for using the heat flow is made clear in
[
3
]
. This limitation can be lifted by considering a nonlinear diffusion equation (see
[
23
]
for detailed explanations and earlier references therein) when
n
is an integer (we refer to
[
35
,
36
]
for the theory of fast diffusion and porous medium equations). With
n\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
, regularity properties and an approximation method are needed to prove Theorem
1
.

2
Ultraspherical operators and
carré du champ
methods

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
. We start by a few observations which motivate our method.

2.1
Preliminary remarks

The case
p=1
of (
8
) is the
Poincaré or spectral gap inequality
associated with
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,
and can be written as

-\left\langle{f},{{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,f}\right\rangle\geq n\,\|f\|_{2}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}([-1,1],d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn})\;\mbox{such that}\;\int_{-1}^{1}{f}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}=0\,.

The ultraspherical operator has purely discrete spectrum
\lambda_{k}=k\,(k+n-1)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with eigenfunctions
f_{0}=1
and
f_{1}(z)=z
associated with
k=0
and
k=1
. A basis of eigenfunctions is provided by the
Gegenbauer
or
ultraspherical polynomials
. The optimality of the constants in (
8
) and (
9
) is easy to check using
f=f_{0}+\varepsilon\,f_{1}
as a test function and then considering the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0_{+}
. See
[
18
,
16
,
28
]
for the next order term.

Equality in (
8
) and (
9
) is achieved only by constant functions if
p<2^{*}
and by
f_{a,b}(z)=a\,|1-b\,z|^{(n-2)/2}
for some real numbers
a
and
b
with
|b|<1
if
n>2
and
p=2^{*}
. The fact that the optimizers
f_{a,b}
are not limited to constant functions in the critical case reflects the invariance under conformal transformations. It is elementary to check that for an appropriate choice of
t\mapsto(a(t),b(t))
,
f_{a(t),b(t)}
solves the fast diffusion equation that will be considered in Section
2.3
. The family
(f_{a,b})
can be characterized by considering the remainder term in the
carré du champ
method. See
[
23
, Proposition 4.1]
when
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

As already mentioned, when
n
is an integer,
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,
can be interpreted as the restriction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to symmetric functions depending only on
z=x\cdot\mathsf{e}
,
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\mathsf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a given, fixed unit vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. An important consequence is that the
Comparison Principle
applies and the whole regularity theory of classical diffusion equations also applies. Arguing by density, one can indeed reduce the proof of the inequalities to functions which are bounded from above and from below by positive constants. Solutions of the evolution equations are then smooth and bounded away from
0
for any positive time and integrations by parts can be performed with no special precaution. However, when
n\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
, some additional care is needed. We shall distinguish two cases:

1.

If we use linear flows which preserve finite dimensional spaces generated by Gengenbauer polynomials, then we can argue by density. Again no special precautions are needed. All values of
p\geq 1
are covered if
n\leq 1
, while this induces the limitation
1\leq p\leq 2^{\#}
, with
2^{\#}
defined by (
10
) if
d>1
. Details are given in Section
2.2
.

2.

If either
1<n\leq 2
and
2^{\#}<p<+\infty
or
n>2
and
2^{\#}<p\leq 2^{*}
with
2^{*}
defined by (
7
), we have to use a nonlinear diffusion equation: see
[
23
, Proposition 4.2]
when
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Based on a formal computation in Section
2.3
and a regularization in Section
2.4
, Theorem
1
is proved in Section
2.5
.

In Case 1, it is possible to assume as in
[
4
, page 71]
that “the carré du champ operator
\Gamma
is defined on a suitable algebra of functions in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d},\,d\mu_{n})
-domain” of the diffusion operator. When the weighted heat flow has to be replaced by its nonlinear counterpart, which occurs in Case 2, this is not possible anymore.

2.2
A computation based on the heat flow

We consider the case
p\in(2,+\infty)
if
n\leq 1
and
p\leq 2^{\#}
otherwise. The two following identities are classical but rely on computations that will be generalized in Section
2.3
, so let us give some details.

Lemma 2
.

Let
n>0
be a real number. For any positive function
u\in C^{2}
such that
u^{\prime}(\pm 1)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u)^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}=\int_{-1}^{1}{|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}+n\int_{-1}^{1}{\rho^{2}\,|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{2}},{{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u}\right\rangle=\frac{n}{n+2}\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{4}}{u^{2}}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}-\,2\,\frac{n-1}{n+2}\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,u^{\prime\prime}}{u}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\,.

(12)

Proof.

Using the commutator
\left[\tfrac{d}{dz},{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,\right]u=-\,2\,z\,u^{\prime\prime}-\,n\,u^{\prime}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u)^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\,({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u)^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\,({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u^{\prime})\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\left(\left[\tfrac{d}{dz},{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,\right]u\right)\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime\prime}\,\big(u^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}\big)^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}+\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\left(2\,z\,u^{\prime\prime}+\,n\,u^{\prime}\right)\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}{|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}+n\int_{-1}^{1}{\rho^{2}\,|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\,,

which establishes (
11
). Identity (
12
) is obtained by writing that

\int_{-1}^{1}{({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,u)\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}=-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{2}\right)^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}\\
=\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{4}}{u^{2}}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}-\,2\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}+2\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{{u^{\prime}}^{3}}{u}\,\rho^{2}\,z}\,d\nu_{\kern-0.5ptn}

and using

Regularization

We consider the probability measure

On the space

\left\langle{f_{1}},{{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,f_{2}}\right\rangle=-\int_{-1}^{1}{f_{1}^{\prime}\,f_{2}^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,f:=(1-z^{2})\,f^{\prime\prime}-\,z\left(d+(n-d)\,\frac{1-z^{2}}{1+\varepsilon-z^{2}}\right)\,f^{\prime}=(1-z^{2})\,f^{\prime\prime}-\,\ell_{\varepsilon,n}\,f^{\prime}

Let us consider the regularized nonlinear flow

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=u^{2-2\beta}\left({\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,u+\kappa\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{2}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle-\,\varepsilon\,(n-d)\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{1+\varepsilon+\,z^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon-z^{2})^{2}}\,\rho^{2}\,|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}{({\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,u)^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}=-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\,({\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,u)^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\,({\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,u^{\prime})\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}-\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime}\left(\left[\tfrac{d}{dz},{\mathcal{L}}_{\varepsilon,n}\,\right]u\right)\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime\prime}\,\big(u^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}\big)^{\prime}\,\rho^{2}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

As for the second identity, we write that

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{4}}{u^{2}}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}-\,2\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}+2\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{{u^{\prime}}^{3}}{u}\,\rho^{2}\,z}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}\,.

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{d+2}\int_{-1}^{1}{u^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}-\,\frac{2}{d+2}\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{4}}{u^{2}}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}+\,\frac{2}{d+2}\int_{-1}^{1}{\frac{{u^{\prime}}^{3}}{u}\,\frac{\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{\zeta_{\varepsilon}}\,\rho^{4}}\,d\nu_{\varepsilon,n}

and (

Appendix A
On the parameters in the
carré du champ
method

Here we collect elementary computations which determine the range of the parameters for which the Bakry-Émery computation gives a sign to the remainder terms. This amounts to find the range of the parameters for which a quadratic form is nonnegative.

Set
n>0
and
\beta\neq 0
. Let us recall that
\kappa=\beta\,(p-2)+1
and to any
C^{2}
function
u
, we associate the function

\mathsf{q}[u]:=|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}-2\,b\,u^{\prime\prime}\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}+c\,\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{4}}{u^{2}}

with

a=1\,,\quad b=\frac{n-1}{n+2}\,(\kappa+\beta-1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c=\kappa\,(\beta-1)+\,\frac{n}{n+2}\,(\kappa+\beta-1)\,.

Then
\mathsf{q}=\mathsf{q}[u]
is pointwise nonnegative under the reduced discriminant condition

\delta:=b^{2}-a\,c\leq 0\,.

\bullet
If
\beta=1
,
\mathsf{q}
is negative if

0\geq\left(\frac{n-1}{n+2}\,\kappa\right)^{2}-\frac{n}{n+2}\,\kappa=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{n-1}{n+2}\right)^{2}\,(p-1)\left(p-2^{\#}\right)&\mbox{if}\quad n\neq 1\,,\\[8.0pt]
-\frac{n}{n+2}\,(p-1)&\mbox{if}\quad n=1\,,\end{array}\right.

that is, for any
p>1
if
n\leq 1
and, if
n>1
, any
p\in[1,2^{\#}]
.

\bullet
If
\beta\neq 1
,
\mathsf{q}
is negative if there exists a value of
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\delta
as defined in (
26
) is nonpositive. After replacing
\kappa
by its value, we can consider
\delta
as a quadratic polynomial of
\beta
, which can be written as

\delta(\beta)=\left(\frac{n-1}{n+2}\,\beta\,(p-1)\right)^{2}-\left[\frac{n}{n+2}\,\beta\,(p-1)+\big(1+\beta\,(p-2)\big)\,(\beta-1)\right]\,,

(25)

that is,
\delta(\beta)=A\,\beta^{2}-2\,B\,\beta+C
where

A=\left(\frac{(n-1)\,(p-1)}{n+2}\right)^{2}+2-p\,,\quad B=\frac{n+3-p}{n+2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C=1\,.

A very special case corresponding to
(n,p)=(3,6)
, has to be excluded, because in that case one has
A=B=0
. It can be handled either by approximation or by considering a power of
u
: see
[
18
,
23
]
for more details. In case
n>2
, since
\delta(\beta)
is quadratic in
\beta
and
\delta(0)=1
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a
\beta
such that
\delta(\beta)\leq 0
is that the reduced discriminant is nonnegative, which amounts to

B^{2}-A\,C=\frac{4\,n}{(n+2)^{2}}\,(p-1)\left(2\,n-p\,(n-2)\right)\geq 0\,.

If
n>2
, notice that this can be rewritten as

B^{2}-A\,C=\frac{4\,n\,(n-2)}{(n+2)^{2}}\,(p-1)\left(2^{*}-p\right)\geq 0\,.

Taking (
16
) into account, the condition
\delta\leq 0
determines the
admissible range of
m
and amounts to

m_{-}(n,p)\leq m\leq m_{+}(n,p)\quad\mbox{and}\quad p\leq 2^{*}\quad\mbox{if}\quad n>2\,,

(26)

where (see Fig.
1
and also
[
16
, Fig. 2]
)

m_{\pm}(n,p):=\frac{1}{(n+2)\,p}\left(n\,p+2\pm\sqrt{n\,(p-1)\,\big(2\,n-(n-2)\,p\big)}\right).

When
n>2
, note that the square root above is a well defined real number under the assumption
p>1
only if
p\leq 2^{*}
. With
n\leq 2
, notice that the admissible range is extended to arbitrarily large values of
p
and that

\lim_{p\to+\infty}m_{\pm}(n,p)=\frac{n+2\,\sqrt{n\,(2-n)}}{n+2}\,.

The range of the
admissible values of
\beta
is given by

\mathcal{R}(n,p)=\big\{\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,m_{-}(n,p)\leq m\leq m_{+}(n,p)\mbox{ where m is given by~\eqref{Id:mbeta} }\big\}\,.

Proposition 7

Let
n>0
and
p>2
. With the above notations, if

, then
\delta\leq 0
if and only if

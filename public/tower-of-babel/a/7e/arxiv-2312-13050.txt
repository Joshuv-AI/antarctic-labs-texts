New physics interpretations for nonstandard values of h → Z ​ γ h\to Z\gamma
Rafael Boto, Dipankar Das, Jorge C. Romão, Ipsita Saha, Joao P. Silva, [3mm] Centro de Física Teórica de Partículas-CFTP, Departamento de Física, Instituto Superior Técnico Universidade de Lisboa, Av Rovisco Pais, 1, P-1049-001 Lisboa, Portugal

Abstract
Current measurement of the h → Z ​ γ h\to Z\gamma signal strength invite us to speculate about possible new physics interactions that exclusively affect μ Z ​ γ \mu_{Z\gamma} without altering the other signal strengths. Additional consideration of tree-unitarity enables us to correlate the nonstandard values of μ Z ​ γ \mu_{Z\gamma} with an upper limit on the scale of new physics. We find that even when μ Z ​ γ \mu_{Z\gamma} deviates from the SM value by only 20 % 20\% , the scale of new physics should be well within the reach of the LHC.

To summarize, recent experimental data on
\mu_{Z\gamma}
instigates us to contemplate the possibility
of having all the Higgs signal strengths in excellent agreement with the corresponding SM expectations,
except
\mu_{Z\gamma}
which deviates substantially from its SM value. Our current article can be
considered as a theoretical preparation for such an eventuality in a bottom-up manner. We
provided a general template for the new physics interactions which will exclusively affect
h\to Z\gamma
.
We particularized our strategy with new charged scalars endowed with dominant off-diagonal couplings
with the Higgs and the
Z
bosons, as exemplified through Eq. (
2
).
However, as we have explicitly shown,
such interactions do not compromise unitarity at high-energies
indicating compatibility with spontaneously broken gauge theories.
The unitarity constraints have been used to place upper bounds on
the magnitudes of the new couplings. We have then translated them
into an upper
bound on the common charged scalar mass, which can be as low as 500 GeV for the current central value of

\mu_{Z\gamma}
.
This means that, if the current non-standard value of
\mu_{Z\gamma}
becomes statistically
significant as more data accumulates, discovery of new physics effects at the LHC should be just around the
corner. The charged scalars, owing to their couplings with the photon, can be pair-produced by the
Drell-Yan mechanism
[
26
,
27
]
. The subsequent decay modes of these charged scalars will, of course,
depend on the finer details of the BSM scenario from which they arise. Even if the charged scalars
are stable, they can be probed in the ongoing searches for long-lived charged particles
[
28
,
29
]
.
This anticipatory experimental scenario may be compared with the status of the LHC in the pre-Higgs-discovery era, as
a win-win machine
in the sense that the LHC should either observe the Higgs-boson or something equivalent, or violation of unitarity at high energies. In a similar spirit, LHC can again act as a win-win experiment for BSM searches if
\mu_{Z\gamma}
eventually settles towards a nonstandard value. That would definitely be an exciting future to look forward to.

by adjusting the parameters in the scalar potential.

Appendix B
Explicit calculations of the scattering amplitudes

In this appendix, we show the explicit calculations of the scattering amplitudes discussed in the
main text. First we consider the process

\displaystyle\centering\Huge Z_{L}(p_{1})+Z_{L}(p_{2})\to S_{1}^{+}(k_{1})+S_{1}^{-}(k_{2})\,.\@add@centering

(B.9)

Figure 4:
Feynman diagrams for
Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{1}^{-}
.

The vertex factor for the interaction
Z_{\mu}S_{1}^{+}(p)S_{2}^{-}(p^{\prime})
is written as
ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}}(p-p^{\prime})_{\mu}
where,
p
and
p^{\prime}

are the momenta of the incoming charged scalars.
Considering the momentum assignment of the initial and final states particles as in Eq. (
B.9
), we can write

\displaystyle p_{1}+p_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}\,.

(B.10)

Now, the Feynman amplitude for the
t
-channel diagram is given by,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}})^{2}\left[-(p_{1}-k_{1})+k_{1}\right]_{\mu}\frac{i}{t-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\left[-k_{2}-\left(p_{1}-k_{1}\right)\right]_{\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon^{\nu}(p_{2})\,,

(B.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{t-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\left(p_{1}-2k_{1}\right)_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}(p_{1})\left(p_{2}-2k_{2}\right)_{\nu}\epsilon^{\nu}(p_{2})\,,

where we used Eq. (
B.10
) in the last step. In a similar manner, we write down the
matrix element for the
u
-channel diagram as,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}})^{2}\left[-k_{2}-\left(k_{2}-p_{1}\right)\right]_{\mu}\frac{i}{u-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\left[k_{1}-\left(k_{2}-p_{1}\right)\right]_{\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon^{\nu}(p_{2})\,,

(B.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{u-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\left(p_{1}-2k_{2}\right)_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}(p_{1})\left(p_{2}-2k_{1}\right)_{\nu}\epsilon^{\nu}(p_{2})\,.

Next, we express the longitudinal polarization vector for the
Z
-boson as
\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}(p)\equiv{\epsilon^{\mu}(p)}/{M_{Z}}

with the understanding that
\epsilon^{\mu}(p)\epsilon_{\mu}(p)=-M_{Z}^{2}
and
p_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}(p)=0
.
The kinematics for the process in the CM frame has been schematically depicted in fig.
5
.
Following this, we may write

\displaystyle k_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{1})=\frac{E}{M_{Z}}(p-k\cos\theta)=k_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{2})\,;

(B.13a)

\displaystyle k_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{1})=\frac{E}{M_{Z}}(p+k\cos\theta)=k_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{2})\,.

(B.13b)

Figure 5:
Kinematics in the CM frame for the process in Eq. (
B.9
).

Using the relations given in Eq. (
B.13
), we now rewrite the matrix elements as,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\frac{E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\frac{(p-k\cos\theta)^{2}}{t-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\,,

(B.14a)

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\frac{E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\frac{(p+k\cos\theta)^{2}}{u-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\,.

(B.14b)

Now, using
t=(p_{1}-k_{1})^{2}
and
u=(p_{2}-k_{1})^{2}
in combination with Eq. (
B.10
), we can show that

\displaystyle\left(p-k\cos\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta m^{2}-t}{2p}\,,

(B.15a)

\displaystyle\left(p+k\cos\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta m^{2}-u}{2p}\,,

(B.15b)

where, we have defined
\Delta m^{2}=m_{C_{1}}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}
.
Thus, substituting Eq. (
B.15
) into Eqs. (
B.14
), we find,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}E^{2}}{p^{2}M_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Delta m^{2}-t\right)^{2}\frac{1}{t}\left(1-\frac{m_{C_{2}}^{2}}{t}\right)^{-1}\,,

(B.16a)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow{\cal M}_{t}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}E^{2}}{p^{2}M_{Z}^{2}}\left(t-2\Delta m^{2}+m_{C_{2}}^{2}\right)\,,

(B.16b)

\displaystyle{\rm and,~similarly}\quad{\cal M}_{u}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}E^{2}}{p^{2}M_{Z}^{2}}\left(u-2\Delta m^{2}+m_{C_{2}}^{2}\right)\,,

(B.16c)

where, we have neglected the terms
{\cal O}(M^{2}/E^{2})
in the high-energy limit.
Furthermore, using the identity

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}}{p^{2}}=E^{2}(E^{2}-M_{Z}^{2})^{-1}\simeq\left(1+\frac{M_{Z}^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,,

(B.17)

we reduce the above amplitudes into,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}t+g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left[\frac{t}{E^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right]+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,,

(B.18a)

\displaystyle{\rm and,}\quad{\cal M}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}u+g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left[\frac{u}{E^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right]+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,,

(B.18b)

where we defined
\Lambda^{2}=(2\Delta m^{2}-m_{C_{2}}^{2})
.
Next, the Feynamn amplitude for the quartic diagram is,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i(g_{zs_{1}s_{2}})^{2}\epsilon^{\mu}_{L}(p_{1})\epsilon_{\mu L}(p_{2})\,.

(B.19)

Replacing the longitudinal polarization vector for the
Z
-boson as
\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}(p)\equiv{\epsilon^{\mu}(p)}/{M_{Z}}

we get

\displaystyle\epsilon_{L}(p_{1})\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{2})=\frac{1}{M_{Z}^{2}}\epsilon(p_{1})\cdot\epsilon(p_{2})=\left(\frac{2E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-1\right)\,.

(B.20)

Thus the total amplitude will be given by,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{1}^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\cal M}_{t}+{\cal M}_{u}+{\cal M}_{Q})

(B.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}(t+u)+g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left[\frac{(t+u)}{E^{2}}-\frac{2\Lambda^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right]+2g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{2E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-1\right)+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,.

Substituting
t+u=2(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})-4E^{2}
we obtain,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{1}^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}E^{2}+\frac{g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\left[2(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})-2\Lambda^{2}-4M_{Z}^{2}\right]

(B.22a)

\displaystyle+2g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{2E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-1\right)+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,.

Now we can use the definition of
\Lambda
to write

\displaystyle\Rightarrow{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{1}^{-}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{4g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}E^{2}+\frac{2g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\left[M_{Z}^{2}+\left(m_{C_{1}}^{2}-m_{C_{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+2g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}\left(\frac{2E^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}-1\right)\,,

(B.22b)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{1}^{-}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\left(m_{C_{1}}^{2}-m_{C_{2}}^{2}\right)\,.

(B.22c)

It is quite interesting to note that the energy growths of
{\cal O}(E^{2})

arising from the
t
and
u
channel diagrams get exactly canceled by the
quartic diagram as expected in spontaneously broken gauge theories.

Next we consider the process

\displaystyle\centering\Huge Z_{L}(p_{1})+Z_{L}(p_{2})\to S_{1}^{+}(k_{1})+S_{2}^{-}(k_{2})\,.\@add@centering

(B.23)

Since we are assuming the presence of off-diagonal couplings only, with the Higgs and
the
Z
-boson, the above process can only proceed via the
s
-channel Higgs exchange.
The corresponding amplitude will be given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{2}^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{igM_{Z}}{c_{w}}\right)\frac{i}{s-m_{h}^{2}}\left(i\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}M_{W}\right)\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon_{L\mu}(p_{2})\,,

(B.24a)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{2}^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-gM_{Z}^{2}\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{s-m_{h}^{2}}\frac{\left(p^{2}+E^{2}\right)}{M_{Z}^{2}}\,,

(B.24b)

where we used
p_{1}^{\mu}=(E,p\hat{z})
and
p_{2}^{\mu}=(E,-p\hat{z})
in the CM frame.
Furthermore, using
p^{2}+E^{2}=s/2-M_{Z}^{2}
we get,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{Z_{L}Z_{L}\to S_{1}^{+}S_{2}^{-}}\approx-\frac{1}{2}g\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)\,.

(B.25)

Finally, we consider the process

\displaystyle\centering\Huge Z_{L}(p_{1})+S^{+}_{1}(p_{2})\to h(k_{1})+S_{1}^{+}(k_{2})\,.\@add@centering

(B.26)

The Feynman diagram along with the kinematics in the CM frame have been depicted in Fig.
6
.

Figure 6:
Feynman diagram for the process appearing in Eq. (
B.26
) and the corresponding kinematics
in the CM frame.

The amplitude for the process may be written as,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{Z_{L}S_{1}^{+}\to hS_{1}^{+}}=ig_{zs_{1}s_{2}}\left\{p_{2}+(p_{1}+p_{2})\right\}_{\mu}\frac{i}{s-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}(i\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}M_{W})\epsilon_{L}^{\mu}(p_{1})\,,

(B.27a)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{Z_{L}S_{1}^{+}\to hS_{1}^{+}}=-g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}\frac{M_{W}}{M_{Z}}\frac{1}{s-m_{C_{2}}^{2}}\left(2p_{2}\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})\right)\,.

(B.27b)

Now, following the kinematics in Fig.
6
we have the following relations,

\displaystyle p_{2}\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})=(E_{+}p+E_{z}p)=p(E_{+}+E_{z})\equiv p\sqrt{s}\,,

(B.28a)

\displaystyle{\rm and,}

\displaystyle p^{2}=E_{z}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}=E_{+}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2}\,,

(B.28b)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle M_{Z}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2}=E_{z}^{2}-E_{+}^{2}\,,

(B.28c)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle(E_{z}-E_{+})=\frac{M_{Z}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2}}{\sqrt{s}}\,.

(B.28d)

Alternatively, one can also write,

\displaystyle 2p^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{z}^{2}+E_{+}^{2}-(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})\,,

(B.29a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[(E_{+}+E_{z})^{2}+(E_{+}-E_{z})^{2}\right]-(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[s+\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2})^{2}}{s}-2(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})\right]\,,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow p^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[s+\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2})^{2}}{s}-2(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})\right]\,,

(B.29b)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}\left[1-2\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})}{s}+\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}-m_{C_{1}}^{2})^{2}}{s^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(B.29c)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}\left[1-\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})}{s}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{4}}{s^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(B.29d)

Thus, one may write

\displaystyle p_{2}\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{2}\left[1-\frac{(M_{Z}^{2}+m_{C_{1}}^{2})}{s}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{4}}{s^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(B.30)

Using this in Eq. (
B.27b
), the final expression for the amplitude, in the high-energy limit, can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{Z_{L}S_{1}^{+}\to hS_{1}^{+}}\approx-g_{zs_{1}s_{2}}\lambda_{hs_{1}s_{2}}\frac{M_{W}}{M_{Z}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{M^{2}}{s}\right)\,.

(B.31)

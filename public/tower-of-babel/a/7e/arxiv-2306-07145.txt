Tetrahedron instantons in Donaldson-Thomas theory
Nadir Fasola, Sergej Monavari

Abstract
. Inspired by the work of Pomoni-Yan-Zhang in String Theory, we introduce the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons as a Quot scheme on a singular threefold and describe it as a moduli space of quiver representations. We construct a virtual fundamental class and virtual structure sheaf à la Oh-Thomas, by which we define K K -theoretic invariants. We show that the partition function of such invariants reproduces the one studied by Pomoni-Yan-Zhang, and explicitly determine it, as a product of shifted partition functions of rank one Donaldson-Thomas invariants of the three-dimensional affine space. Our geometric construction answers a series of questions of Pomoni-Yan-Zhang on the geometry of the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons and the behaviour of its partition function, and provides a new application of the recent work of Oh-Thomas.

Introduction

1.1.
Tetrahedron instantons

Pomoni-Yan-Zhang
[
62
]
recently introduced the
tetrahedron instantons
, which can be realized in type IIB string theory as D1-branes probing a configuration of intersecting D7-branes in flat spacetime with constant
B

This is equivalently interpreted as describing instanton dynamics on
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}

Their construction of the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons is realised via gauge-theoretic methods, so that supersymmetric localisation techniques apply to define and study the associated instanton partition function non-perturbatively. In this article, we propose a rigorous mathematical perspective on the problem. We realise the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons as a Quot scheme and compute explicitly the generating series of the resulting virtual invariants, thus shedding light on the geometry of the constructions in
[
62
]
from the point of view of Donaldson-Thomas theory.

1.2.1.
Strategy of the proof

We explain here the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The torus fixed locus

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{\overline{r}}(q)=\sum_{\overline{\pi}}(-1)^{\sigma_{\overline{\pi}}}[-\mathsf{v}_{\overline{\pi}}]\cdot q^{|\overline{\pi}|}.

Let us pause for a moment to explain the notation in (

\overline{\pi}

|\overline{\pi}|
denotes their
size
. To each
\overline{\pi}

\mathsf{v}_{\overline{\pi}}
(the

of the virtual tangent space) and an (a priori non-explicit) sign
(-1)^{\sigma_{\overline{\pi}}}
. By Oh-Thomas virtual localisation, each tuple of plane partitions
\overline{\pi}
contributes to the localised invariants with rational functions in the equivariant parameters, obtained by applying the operator
[\cdot]
to the

\mathsf{v}_{\overline{\pi}}
, but only up to a
sign
. Despite this situation being very similar to the original treatment of the topological vertex in
[

]
, the presence of such a sign in our context is what makes the computation of the partition function
hard and cumbersome. We remark that the choice of square root

is not canonical; rather, one should think of the pair
(\mathsf{v}_{\overline{\pi}},(-1)^{\sigma_{\overline{\pi}}})
to be canonical in a suitable sense, cf.

In order to prove Theorem

The second result is of global nature, and asserts that the tetrahedron instantons partition function does not depend on the

1.3.
Relation to Donaldson-Thomas Theory

Our description of the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons and its partition function nicely fits into the realm of Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory. In fact, as already pointed out by Pomoni-Yan-Zhang
[
62
]
, tetrahedron instantons interpolate between (higher rank) DT theory of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
, whose relevant moduli spaces are
\Quot_{{\mathbb{C}}^{3}}({\oO^{r},n})
and
\Quot_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}(\oO^{r},n)
(cf.
[
33
,
18
,
42
]
). The interesting aspect of our construction is that it shares common features with both DT theories, in dimension three and four. In fact, despite
{\mathcal{M}}_{\overline{r},n}
is a moduli space of sheaves on a threefold and possesses a
zero-dimensional
virtual fundamental class — as in the classical DT theory of threefolds — it seems that its virtual structure cannot be defined directly with the machinery of (two-terms)
perfect obstruction theories
of Behrend-Fantechi and Li-Tian
[
6
,
43
]
, but crucially needs a symmetric (three-term) obstruction theory as in the work of Oh-Thomas
[
59
]
, which was specifically designed for DT theory of Calabi-Yau fourfolds. Nevertheless, for suitable choices of the vector
\overline{r}
, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\overline{r},n}
reproduces the Quot scheme
\Quot_{{\mathbb{C}}^{3}}({\oO^{r},n})
, whose virtual structure
does
come from a perfect obstruction theory, induced by a global critical locus structure
[
5
]
. This shows that the natural class one should consider is always the
twisted
virtual structure sheaf, which is natural from the point of view of DT theory of Calabi-Yau fourfolds, while in DT theory of threefolds, one should

the virtual structure sheaf by hand to make the invariants more relevant to study, following the circle of ideas initiated by Nekrasov-Okounkov
[

. On the other hand, Theorem

shows that the tetrahedron instantons partition function is a specialisation of the partition function of higher rank Donaldson-Thomas invariants of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
, whose explicit expression was proposed by Nekrasov-Piazzalunga
[
57
]
and which is conjectured to be the mother of all instanton partition functions
[

]
.

From the point of view of DT theory, one may ask if the zero-locus construction of Theorem

— and its induced virtual structure — could have an intrinsic deformation-theoretic interpretation, which does not require the construction of a smooth ambient space via a quiver with potential as in our work (cf.

1.4.
String Theory

The moduli space of tetrahedron instantons was introduced in the context of Supersymmetric String Theory in
[
62
]
. It describes the low energy theory of a system of D1-branes probing some general configuration of intersecting D7-branes. This in turn is interpreted as capturing instanton dynamics when codimension-two supersymmetric defects are present. On the geometric side, Supersymmetric String Theory in general, and D-branes systems specifically, have proved useful to provide a physical realisation of interesting moduli spaces and to compute several generating series of their (virtual) invariants. The point of contact between the mathematical and physical realms can be found in the study of the BPS sector of string theories. Indeed, this is a particularly amenable task on the physical side, as it consists of quantities which are known to be protected from quantum corrections and can therefore be studied non-perturbatively. Also, BPS-bound state counting can usually be interpreted in terms of counting/classification problems in geometry, and the technique of localisation often available in supersymmetric settings makes it feasible to compute relevant partition functions exactly. Here the connection to enumerative geometry is made even clearer, as supersymmetric localisation may be viewed as an infinite-dimensional analogue of equivariant localisation in different settings (eg., cohomological, cf.
[
2
]
, or K-theoretic localisation, cf.
[
68
]
).

One particular class of moduli spaces which can be obtained in physics from systems of D-branes are moduli spaces of (generalised) instantons. The ADHM construction of moduli spaces of anti-self dual connections as symplectic quotients shed light on their physical realisation either in the language of Sigma Models
[
70
]
, or as moduli spaces of BPS vacua of systems of D1-D5-branes in type IIB Supersymmetric String Theory
[
29
]
. Similarly, a resolution of singularities of the moduli space of ideal instantons on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, realised by moduli of framed torsion-free sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

[
52
]
, can be obtained from D-brane configurations in superstring theory, modelling instantons on noncommutative
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. Partition functions of such theories are then computed explicitly (and non-perturbatively) via equivariant localisation
[
51
,
58
]
. Several interesting generalisations of these constructions have been studied over the past years, e.g. the construction of instantons as systems of D-branes on orbifolds or in the presence of surface defects
[
30
,
40
,
12
,
14
]
, as well as on other four-dimensional manifolds
[
23
,
71
,
16
,
36
,
15
]
. Moreover, ADHM-type constructions can be exploited to construct instanton-like objects in higher dimensions
[
4
,
50
,
37

3.2.
Torus action

Let
\overline{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{4})
and

and define the algebraic tori

by scaling the fibers of

. Therefore, we have an induced

-action on

. Equivalently, the
\mathbf{T}
-action on

is the restriction of the
\mathbf{T}
-action on the moduli space of non-commutative Quot scheme

given on points by

are the coordinates of
\mathbf{T}_{1}
, for

and

.

Remark 3.1
.

The fixed locus
{\mathcal{M}}_{\overline{r},n}^{\mathbf{T}_{0}}
is proper. Indeed, a
\mathbf{T}_{0}
-invariant surjection

necessarily has the quotient

entirely supported at the origin

. Hence

sits as a closed subscheme inside the fiber over

of the Quot-to-Chow morphism

.
But
\rho^{-1}(n\cdot[0])
is proper, since
\rho
is a proper morphism.

The torus fixed locus of the moduli space of tetrahedron instantons is classified in terms of

.

Definition 3.2
.

A
plane partition

\pi
is a finite subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}_{\geq 0}
such that if
(i,j,k)\in\pi
with
(i,j,k)\neq 0

(i-1,j,k),(i,j-1,k),(i,j,k-1)\in\pi

. Alternatively, we can view the plane partition as boxes stacked in

, where the lattice points are determined by the

\overline{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r})
we denote its

by
|\overline{\pi}|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\pi_{i}|
.

It is well-known that plane partitions are in bijection with monomial ideals of
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,z]

\pi\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}_{\geq 0}\longleftrightarrow I_{\pi}=(x^{i}y^{j}z^{k})_{(i,j,k)\notin\pi}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,z],

where the lattice points of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}_{\geq 0}
correspond to monomials in three variables (see e.g.
[
33
, Sec. 3.4]
).

Proposition 3.3

Let
\overline{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{4})

-fixed locus

is reduced, zero-dimensional and in bijection with

By the same arguments as in
[
10

its

-fixed locus is scheme-theoretically isomorphic to

\mathcal{M}_{\overline{r},n}^{\mathbf{T}_{1}}\cong\bigsqcup_{n=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}n_{ij}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\prod_{j=1}^{r_{i}}\Quot_{\Delta}(\iota_{i,*}\oO_{{\mathbb{C}}^{3}_{i}},n_{ij}).

Moreover, we have an identification of each factor with a Hilbert scheme of points

such that the natural

∎

Define the

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}(q)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{n}}.

Corollary 3.4

Let

∎

]
. In the case of tetrahedron instantons, however, a perfect obstruction theory is not available, but virtual structures à la Oh-Thomas are induced by the zero-locus construction of Thm.
2.3
. Then the elliptic genus computed in
[
62
, Eqs. (211)-(214)]
is recovered exactly from our computation of the virtual Witten genus in §
3.7
. In this sense, one might view supersymmetric localisation as nothing but an infinite-dimensional analogue of equivariant localisation. However, despite the undoubted advantage that an in-depth knowledge of the structure of the moduli space of BPS vacua is not required for the calculation of the elliptic invariants in Def.
3.19
, carried out as in
[
62
, § 5]
, it nevertheless overshadows some interesting features which might be worth pointing out. Indeed, the fact that the virtual structure on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\overline{r},n}
is induced, via the seminal work of Oh-Thomas
[
59
]
, by the construction in Thm.
2.3
, means that the invariants computed in K-theory by localisation will depend on a choice of orientation via a sign, cf. §
3.3
. This sign, however, is not manifestly present in
[
62
, §§ 5-6]
. Moreover, the choice of a square root of the virtual tangent bundle as in §
3.3
is not unique, but the couple square root/sign is somewhat canonical (cf. Thm.
A.1
and
[
46
]
). Therefore, while the character
\chi_{\overline{\pi}}
in
[
62
, Eq. (147)]
is a good choice for a square root of the virtual tangent space at the points in the fixed locus
{\mathcal{M}}_{\overline{r},n}^{\mathbf{T}}
, it is not the only possible one (a different choice being given by our vertex term
\mathsf{v}_{\overline{\pi}}
in Eq.
3.8
), and in the computation of the invariants
[
62
, Eq. (153)]
, it should in principle be accompanied by the additional choice of an appropriate sign. Similarly, the observation in loc. cit. that the partition function thereby computed is invariant only up to a sign under permutations of the
\kappa_{i}
, is a shadow of the same phenomenon.

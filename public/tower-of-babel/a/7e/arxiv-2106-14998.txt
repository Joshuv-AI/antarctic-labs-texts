Finite Element Approximations of a Class of Nonlinear Stochastic Wave Equation with Multiplicative Noise
Yukun Li, Shuonan Wu, Yulong Xing

Abstract
Wave propagation problems have many applications in physics and engineering, and the stochastic effects are important in accurately modeling them due to the uncertainty of the media. This paper considers and analyzes a fully discrete finite element method for a class of nonlinear stochastic wave equations, where the diffusion term is globally Lipschitz continuous while the drift term is only assumed to satisfy weaker conditions as in [ 11 ] . The novelties of this paper are threefold. First, the error estimates cannot not be directly obtained if the numerical scheme in primal form is used. The numerical scheme in mixed form is introduced and several Hölder continuity results of the strong solution are proved, which are used to establish the error estimates in both L 2 L^{2} norm and energy norms. Second, two types of discretization of the nonlinear term are proposed to establish the L 2 L^{2} stability and energy stability results of the discrete solutions. These two types of discretization and proper test functions are designed to overcome the challenges arising from the stochastic scaling in time issues and the nonlinear interaction. These stability results play key roles in proving the probability of the set on which the error estimates hold approaches one. Third, higher order moment stability results of the discrete solutions are proved based on an energy argument and the underlying energy decaying property of the method. Numerical experiments are also presented to show the stability results of the discrete solutions and the convergence rates in various norms.



keywords

stochastic wave equation, multiplicative noise, finite element method, higher order moment, error estimate, stability analysis.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the nonlinear stochastic wave equation
with Neumann boundary condition and functional-type multiplicative
noise, taking the form of

(1)

\displaystyle du_{t}=\Delta udt+f(u)dt+g(u)dW(t),

\displaystyle\mbox{in }\mathcal{D}\times(0,T],

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0

\displaystyle\mbox{in }\partial\mathcal{D}\times(0,T],

(3)

\displaystyle u(0)=h_{1}(x)\quad u_{t}(0)=h_{2}(x).

\displaystyle\mbox{in }\mathcal{D}.

Here
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}~(d=1,2,3)
is a bounded
domain,
W:\Omega\times(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is a standard Weiner
process on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}:t\geq 0\},\mathbb{P})
. The nonlinear drift term

f(u)
is assumed to satisfy the conditions specified in

[
11
]
, which discusses the existence and
uniqueness for local and global solutions of (
1
) in Sobolev
space. More specifically, we assume

(4)

\displaystyle f(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}a_{j}(x)u^{j},

where
q
is an odd integer with
1\leq q\leq 3
for
d=3
and
q\geq 1
for

d=1,\,2
,
a_{j}(x)
are bounded and continuous for any

, and there
exist positive constants

and
\lambda>0
such that

(5)

\displaystyle F(u):=-\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds=-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{j+1}a_{j}(x)u^{j+1}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{q-1}\right)u^{2}.

One example that satisfies these conditions is
f(u)=-u-u^{3}
.
Furthermore, we assume that
g(u)
is continuously differentiable, globally Lipschitz continuous,
|g^{\prime\prime}(u)|
is bounded, and satisfies the growth condition, i.e., there exists a constant

C
such that

(6)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\in C^{1},

(7)

\displaystyle|g(a)-g(b)|

\displaystyle\leq C|a-b|,

(8)

\displaystyle|g(a)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+a^{2}),

(9)

\displaystyle|g^{\prime\prime}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq C.

Throughout this paper,

In this paper, we present the fully discrete methods for the
stochastic wave equation with multiplicative noise, by utilizing the
finite element approximation in space, and the implicit Euler/modified Crank-Nicolson
approximation in time. There are two main objectives in this paper.
First, we want to establish the second moment and higher order moment
stability results of the discrete solutions in
L^{2}
norm and various
energy norms. This is motivated by the conjecture that the numerical
solutions will inherit the stability properties of the strong
solutions. The goal is achieved by designing the proper numerical scheme, choosing the
test function and using an energy argument. Second, we want to provide the convergence rates of the
error estimates in both
L^{2}
and
energy norms for the proposed method when the nonlinear drift term is
not Lipschitz continuous and the diffusion term is multiplicative noise, and this would be the first work to achieve such goal, to our best knowledge. To achieve this objective, we rewrite the numerical scheme in the mixed form, utilize the above stability results, establish several Hölder continuity results, and construct a subset with nearly probability one, to handle
complex interaction between the multiplicative noise and super-linear
nonlinear term, such that the error estimates hold on this subset. This concept is proposed based on the bounds of the solutions and is motivated by the idea in
[
8

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section

Lemma 1
.

In the 1D and 2D settings, for each individual term

\|u^{q}-v^{q}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2(q-1)}+\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2(q-1)}\right)\|u-v\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

where

Proof.

In the 1D and 2D settings, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\|u^{q}-v^{q}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(u^{q-1}+u^{q-2}v+\cdots+v^{q-1})(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u^{q-1}(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v^{q-1}(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2(q-1)}+\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2(q-1)}\right)\|u-v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

by applying Young’s inequality and the embedding theorem. In the 3D
setting, the proof is similar to (

∎

\displaystyle\qquad+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}f(u(\zeta))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\zeta\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}g(u(\zeta))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\zeta\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\zeta)\|_{L^{4(q-1)}}^{4(q-1)}d\zeta\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}u(\zeta)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}d\zeta\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\zeta)\|_{L^{8}}^{8}d\zeta\big]+C.

Therefore, for any
s,t\in[0,T]
with
s<t
, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla^{2}(u(t)-u(s))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big](t-s)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t-s)^{2},

where
\xi\in(s,t)
and

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}\Delta u(\zeta)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\zeta\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\zeta)\|_{L^{4(q-1)}}^{4(q-1)}d\zeta\big]

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}u(\zeta)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}d\zeta\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\zeta)\|_{L^{8}}^{8}d\zeta\big]+C.

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3
Fully discrete finite element methods and stability estimates

In this section, we start by presenting the fully discrete finite
element methods for the stochastic wave equations
(
1
)–(
4
), and then establish several
stability estimates of the numerical solutions. In addition to the second moment stability in
L^{2}
norm and energy norms of the discrete numerical solutions,
the stability of higher order moments is also provided.

3.1
Notations and the finite element methods

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform triangulation of the domain
\mathcal{D}
.
We consider the
\mathcal{P}_{r}
-Lagrangian finite element space

(25)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\bigl\{v_{h}\in C(\bar{\mathcal{D}}):v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(K),\,\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigr\},

where
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
is the closure of the domain
\mathcal{D}
,
P_{r}(K)
denotes the space of all polynomials of degrees up to
r
on
K
, and

r\geq 1
is an integer. Consider a uniform partition of the time domain
[0,\,T]
with
\tau=T/N
, and denote
t_{n}=n\tau
for
n=0,1,\cdots N
.

The fully discretized numerical methods for (
1
) is to
seek an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
adapted
V_{h}
-valued process

\{u_{h}^{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
such that it holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely that:

(26)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{n+1}_{h}-2u^{n}_{h}+u^{n-1}_{h}}{\tau},w_{h}\right)+\tau(\nabla u^{n+1}_{h},\nabla w_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\tau(f^{n+1}_{h},w_{h})+(g(u^{n}_{h}),w_{h})\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\qquad\forall\,w_{h}\in V_{h},

where the notation
\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}
is defined by

(27)

\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}:=W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau),

and there are two choices for the discretization of the nonlinear drift term:

1.

Fully implicit discretization:

(28)

f^{n+1}_{h}:=f(u_{h}^{n+1}).

2.

Modified Crank-Nicolson discretization:

(29)

f_{h}^{n+1}:=\hat{f}(u_{h}^{n+1},u_{h}^{n})=\begin{cases}-\frac{F(u_{h}^{n+1})-F(u_{h}^{n})}{u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}}\quad&\mathrm{if}\ u_{h}^{n+1}\neq u_{h}^{n},\\
f(u_{h}^{n+1})\quad&\mathrm{if}\ u_{h}^{n+1}=u_{h}^{n},\end{cases}

where
F(\cdot)
is defined in (
5
).

The finite element method (
26
) involves a two-step implicit
temporal discretization, and would need two initial conditions
u_{h}^{0}

and
u_{h}^{-1}
to start. The initial condition
u_{h}^{0}=P_{h}u(x,0)
is
obtained via a standard
L^{2}
-projection operator defined as
P_{h}:L^{2}(\mathcal{D})\longrightarrow V_{h}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left(P_{h}u,w_{h}\right)=(u,w_{h})\qquad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h},

and
u_{h}^{-1}=u_{h}^{0}-\tau P_{h}u_{t}(0,x)
, namely, the backward Euler method is used for the initial step.
The discrete Laplace operator
\Delta_{h}:{V}_{h}\mapsto{V}_{h}
is defined
as follows: given
z_{h}\in{V}_{h}
,
\Delta_{h}z_{h}\in V_{h}
is chosen such
that

(30)

(\Delta_{h}z_{h},w_{h})=-(\nabla z_{h},\nabla w_{h})\qquad\forall\,w_{h}\in V_{h}.

3.2
Stability in
L^{2}
norm and energy norms

For the deterministic wave equation (i.e.,
g=0
in (
1
)),
it is well-known that this model preserves the Hamiltonian, defined as

\mathcal{H}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(F(u),1),

where
F(u)
satisfies the condition (
5
).
The discrete analogue of the Hamiltonian is defined as

(31)

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n}):=\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(F(u_{h}^{n}),1),

where
d_{t}
denotes the temporal difference operator defined by

(32)

\displaystyle d_{t}u_{h}^{n}=\frac{u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n-1}}{\tau}.

Before stating the stability estimate, we summarize the assumption on the nonlinear drift term below.

Assumption 3.1
.

The nonlinear drift term
f(u)
given in (
4
) satisfies (
5
).
Furthermore,
F(\cdot)
is convex if the fully implicit discretization (
28
) of the nonlinear term is utilized.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\{u_{h}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{N}
denote the numerical solutions of the
finite element methods (
26
). Under the Assumption

3.1
, the following inequality holds for any integer

\ell\in[1,N]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+(F(u_{h}^{\ell}),1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})\right]+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

Taking the test function
w_{h}=d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}
in (
26
), we have

(33)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{n+1}_{h}-2u^{n}_{h}+u^{n-1}_{h}}{\tau},d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)+\tau\left(\nabla u^{n+1}_{h},\nabla d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\tau\left(f^{n+1}_{h},d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)+\left(g(u^{n}_{h}),d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}.

The two terms on the left can be rewritten as:

(34)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u^{n+1}_{h}-2u^{n}_{h}+u^{n-1}_{h}}{\tau},d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)=\left(d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n},d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(35)

\displaystyle\tau\left(\nabla u^{n+1}_{h},\nabla d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)=\left(\nabla u^{n+1}_{h},\nabla u^{n+1}_{h}-\nabla u^{n}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

Taking the expectation on the last term yields

(36)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g(u^{n}_{h}),d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h}\right)\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\right)\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[1+\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right],

where the first equality comes from the fact that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g(u^{n}_{h}),d_{t}u^{n}_{h}\right)\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left(g(u^{n}_{h}),d_{t}u^{n}_{h}\right)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\right]=0,

and the second inequality is a result of the Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality, the growth condition of
g(u)
in (
8
) and the
property of
\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}
in (
27
).

The bound of the first term on the right-hand side is discussed case
by case:

1.

For the fully implicit discretization (
28
),
use Taylor’s formula to derive

\displaystyle F(u_{h}^{n})=F(u_{h}^{n+1})+f(u_{h}^{n+1})(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})^{2},

where
\xi
locates between
u_{h}^{n}
and
u_{h}^{n+1}
. Notice that

\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})^{2}\geq 0
under the Assumption
3.1
.

2.

For the modified Crank-Nicolson discretization (
29
),

\displaystyle F(u_{h}^{n})=F(u_{h}^{n+1})+\hat{f}(u_{h}^{n+1},u_{h}^{n})(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}).

Therefore, one can conclude that, for both fully implicit
(
28
) and modified Crank-Nicolson (
29
)
discretizations,

(37)

\displaystyle\tau(-f^{n+1}_{h},d_{t}u^{n+1}_{h})\geq(F(u_{h}^{n+1})-F(u_{h}^{n}),1).

Summing the equation (
33
) over
n
from
1
to

\ell-1
, taking the expectation on both sides and using the results
(
34
)-(
37
), we have

(38)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})\right]+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{1})\right]+C\tau\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+C.

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})\right]+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq C.

This gives the desired stability in
L^{2}
norm and energy norms.
∎

3.3
Stability of the higher order moments

The following stability of the higher order moments can be established based on the stability results in Theorem
8
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\{u_{h}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{N}
denote the numerical solutions of the
finite element methods (
26
). Under the Assumption

3.1
, for any integer
p\geq 2
, it holds for any integer

\ell\in[1,N]
that

(39)

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\|d_{t}u_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+(F(u_{h}^{\ell}),1)^{p}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

To ease the presentation, the proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1.
Following the results
(
33
)–(
37
) in the proof of Theorem

8
, we have

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+(F(u_{h}^{n+1})-F(u_{h}^{n}),1)\leq(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1})\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1},

which can be recast as follows thanks to the definition
(
31
),

(41)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})+\frac{1}{2}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}.

Utilizing the following identity

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})=\frac{3}{4}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right),

and multiplying (
41
) by the term
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})
, we obtain

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})^{2}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right).

The right-hand side of (
42
) can be rewritten as

(43)

\displaystyle\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\qquad+\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\qquad+\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\bigg)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right),

by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the growth condition of
g(u)
in (
8
).
The last two terms can be bounded as

(44)

\displaystyle\bigg(C(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}+\left(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\right)\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)^{2}+C(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{4}

\displaystyle+C\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}+C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n})\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}.

Combining the equation (
42
) with the results in (
43
)-(
44
) yields

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})^{2}-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\quad+\left(\frac{1}{4}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\leq C(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{4}+C\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\quad+C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1})^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(g(u_{h}^{n}),d_{t}u_{h}^{n})\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}.

Summing the equation (
45
) over
n
from
1
to
\ell-1
and taking expectation on both sides, we obtain

(46)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})]^{2}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{4}\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\quad-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{0})^{2}\right]+C\tau^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+C\tau\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\right]+C\tau\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\right].

Following the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})
in (
31
), one has

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}\max\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)
, which implies that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1\leq C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}+1,\quad

\displaystyle\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\leq C\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1\right)\leq C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}+1,\quad

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\leq C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}+\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+1\right)\leq C\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}+1.

Therefore, the following result can be obtained by applying Gronwall’s inequality:

(47)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})^{2}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|d_{t}u_{h}^{n+1}-d_{t}u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{h}^{n+1}-\nabla u_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C,

which gives us the second order moment stability (i.e., (
39
) when
p=2
).

Step 2.
Next, the higher order moment stability (
39
) can be established for
p=2^{m}
with any positive integer
m
.
Similar to the derivation in Step 1, we start from the equation (
45
) and multiply it by

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})^{2}
. After some simple algebra,
we can obtain the fourth moment stability of the numerical
solution
u_{h}^{\ell}
below

(48)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})^{4}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\bigl(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n+1})\bigr)^{2}-\bigl(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{n})\bigr)^{2}\right)^{2}\right]\leq C.

Applying this process repeatedly, the
2^{m}
-th moment stability of the numerical solution
u_{h}^{\ell}
can be obtained for any positive integer
m
.

Step 3.
For arbitrary positive integer
p
, suppose
2^{m-1}\leq p\leq 2^{m}
for some
m
, and one can apply the Young’s inequality to
obtain

(49)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(u_{h}^{\ell})^{2^{m}}\right]+C\leq C,

where the second inequality follows from the results in
Step 2
.

4
Error estimates

In this section, we present the error estimates of the proposed finite
element methods. The stability estimates studied in the previous
section are crucial in the analysis.

4.1
Error equations in mixed form

Denoting
v=u_{t}
, we can rewrite the SPDE (
1
) as

(50)

\left\{\begin{aligned} du&=vdt,\\
dv&=\Delta udt+f(u)dt+g(u)dW(t).\end{aligned}\right.

Define the numerical errors by
e_{u}^{n}:=u(t_{n})-u_{h}^{n}:=\eta_{u}^{n}+\xi_{u}^{n}
,
e_{v}^{n}:=v(t_{n})-v_{h}^{n}:=\eta_{v}^{n}+\xi_{v}^{n}
, where

\displaystyle\eta_{u}^{n}:=u(t_{n})-P_{h}u(t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{u}^{n}:=P_{h}u(t_{n})-u_{h}^{n},\quad n=0,1,2,...,N,

\displaystyle\eta_{v}^{n}:=v(t_{n})-P_{h}v(t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{v}^{n}:=P_{h}v(t_{n})-v_{h}^{n},\quad n=0,1,2,...,N,

represent the errors of the
L^{2}
-projection and the errors between the
numerical solution and projected strong solution, respectively.

It follows from (
50
) that for all
t_{n}
, there holds

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely

(51)

\displaystyle(u(t_{n+1})-u(t_{n}),w_{h})=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(v(s),w_{h})ds\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h},

(52)

\displaystyle(v(t_{n+1})-v(t_{n}),z_{h})+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(\nabla u(s),\nabla z_{h})ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(f(u(s)),z_{h})ds+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(g(u(s)),z_{h})dW(s)\quad\forall z_{h}\in V_{h}.

Also, the numerical scheme (
26
) can be rewritten in the equivalent mixed form as

(53)

\displaystyle(u^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h},w_{h})=\tau(v^{n+1}_{h},w_{h})\qquad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h},

(54)

\displaystyle\left(v^{n+1}_{h}-v^{n}_{h},z_{h}\right)+\tau(\nabla u^{n+1}_{h},\nabla z_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\tau(f^{n+1}_{h},z_{h})+(g(u^{n}_{h}),z_{h})\,\bar{\Delta}W_{n+1}\qquad\forall\,z_{h}\in V_{h}.

From the properties of the
L^{2}
-projection, we have
(\eta_{u}^{n},w_{h})=0
and
(\eta_{v}^{n},z_{h})=0
. Combining with the equations (
51
)-(
54
) leads to

(55)

\displaystyle(\xi_{u}^{n+1}-\xi_{u}^{n},w_{h})=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(v(s)-v_{h}^{n+1},w_{h})ds\qquad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h},

(56)

\displaystyle(\xi_{v}^{n+1}-\xi_{v}^{n},z_{h})+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(\nabla u(s)-\nabla u_{h}^{n+1},\nabla z_{h})ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(f(u(s))-f_{h}^{n+1},z_{h})ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}(g(u(s))-g(u_{h}^{n}),z_{h})dW(s)\qquad\quad\forall z_{h}\in V_{h}.

\displaystyle\,C\mathbb{E}\big[\|u_{t}(0)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta u(\zeta)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}d\zeta\big]+C\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\zeta)\|_{L^{2q}}^{2q}d\zeta\big]

, and applying Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

(74)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,\ell}}\|\xi_{u}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,\ell}}\|\nabla e_{u}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,\ell}}\|\xi_{v}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,n+1}}\|\xi_{v}^{n+1}-\xi_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,n+1}}\|\xi_{u}^{n+1}-\xi_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,n+1}}\|\nabla e_{u}^{n+1}-\nabla e_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,(C\kappa^{q-1}h^{2}+C\tau+C\kappa^{q-1}\tau^{2})h^{-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,(Ch^{2}|\ln h|+C\tau+C\tau^{2}|\ln h|)h^{-\beta}.

Using the Markov’s inequality, discrete Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequalities
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
23
]
, equation (
10
), and Theorem
8
, we have the following property

(75)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\tilde{\Omega}_{\kappa,\ell}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq\ell}\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\max\limits_{s\leq t_{\ell}}\|u(s)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right]}{(C\ln(h^{-\beta}))^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{1}{q-1}}(-\ln h)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\rightarrow 1\ \text{as}\ h\rightarrow 0.

By combining (
74
) with the properties of the
L^{2}
-projection, we finish the proof of this theorem.
∎

Character formula for Weil representations in terms of Frobenius traces
Tim Dokchitser, Vladimir Dokchitser

Abstract
. It is known that the étale cohomology of a potentially good abelian variety over a local field K K is determined by its Euler factors over the extensions of K K . We extend this to all abelian varieties, show that it is enough to take extensions where A A is semistable, and give a uniform version over p p -adic fields where the extensions are the same for all abelian varieties of a given dimension. The results are explicit, and apply to a wide class of Weil-Deligne representations.



Ramified
l
-adic local Galois representations tend to be unwelcome.
We give an explicit character formula which reconstructs such a representation from its Frobenius traces over extensions
where it is unramified.
We also record some consequences for étale cohomology of abelian varieties and other
l
-adic representations.
The paper is a number-theoretic counterpart of
[
13
]
, which concerns representations of finite groups.

Our setting is as follows. Let

,

and algebraic closure

We write

for the absolute inertia group and

Recall that the Weil group

is generated by

and

the usual profinite one on

and discrete on

We begin with an analogue of the character formula

Theorem 1
.

Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension of local fields and
\rho

a semisimple Weil representation over
K
that factors through
\Gal(F^{nr}/K)
.
Let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
be a set of irreducible
representations of
\Gal(F/K)
, with exactly one in each set of unramified twists
1
1

1

i.e.
\rho_{i}\neq\rho_{j}\otimes
(1-dim) unramified, and each irreducible of

\Gal(F/K)
is
\rho_{i}\otimes
(1-dim unramified) for some
i
.
Write
I<\Gal(F/K)
for the inertia group and
m_{i}=\langle\Res_{I}\rho_{i},\Res_{I}\rho_{i}\rangle

Then

(i)

There are unramified representations
\Psi_{i}
such that

\rho\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\Lambda}\rho_{i}\otimes\Psi_{i}.

(ii)

Fix

.
For every

,

\Tr\Psi_{i}(\Frob_{K}^{dm_{i}})=\frac{1}{|I|\,m_{i}}\sum_{L}\overline{\Tr\rho_{i}(\Frob_{L})}\,\Tr\rho(\Frob_{L}),

where
L
ranges over extensions of

in
\smash{F^{nr}}
of ramification degree
|I|

and residue degree

.
Note that
F^{nr}/L
is unramified, so
\rho(\Frob_{L}),\rho_{i}(\Frob_{L})
are
well-defined.

(iii)

The twist
\rho_{i}\otimes\Psi_{i}
is uniquely determined by
(ii)
.

(iv)

Concretely,
suppose

\dim\Psi_{i}\!\leq\!N
. There is a unique
0\!\leq\!n\!\leq\!N
and
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}

such that

\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{d}=\Tr\Psi_{i}(\Frob_{K}^{dm_{i}})
for

.
Then

\Psi_{i}(\Frob_{K})

has eigenvalues
\sqrt[m_{i}]{\lambda_{1}},...,\sqrt[m_{i}]{\lambda_{n}}

for some choice of the roots, and

\rho_{i}\otimes\Psi_{i}
is independent of this choice.

Let
\ell
be a prime different from the residue characteristic of
K
.
As an application, we deduce that the
\ell
-adic representation
\rho_{\ell}=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(A_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

of an abelian variety

is determined by the traces
\Tr\rho^{I_{L}}_{\ell}(\Frob_{L})
over the fields

where

is semistable.
(Here, as usual,

is the maximal subrepresentation unramified over

In fact, when

has characteristic 0, we can choose a universal finite list
of such fields to control all abelian varieties of a fixed dimension.

We now return to the case of arbitrary characteristic.

Here are some theoretical consequences of Theorem

for general representations.
The following corollary is essentially due to Saito

Corollary 3
.

Every semisimple Weil representation
\rho
over
K

is uniquely determined by the
traces

\Tr\rho(\Frob_{L}),

where
L
varies over finite separable extensions of
K

over which
\rho

Notation
.

Recall that a Frobenius semisimple Weil-Deligne representation

\rho=(\rho_{\rm Weil},N)
over
K
can be decomposed as

\rho=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\rho_{n}\otimes\Sp_{n},

where
\rho_{n}
are semisimple Weil representations (i.e. have

),
and
\Sp_{n}
is the

[

In particular,
\rho^{N=0}:=\ker N=\bigoplus\rho_{n}
.

We say that

is
weight-monodromy compatible
if
the eigenvalues of
\Frob_{K}
on
\rho_{i}
are Weil numbers of absolute value

q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

.
We use the same term for
\ell
-adic representations when their associated Weil-Deligne representation
is weight-monodromy compatible.

For every proper smooth variety
X/K
and
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
,

H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}(X_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})(\frac{i}{2})
is conjectured to be weight-monodromy

[
8

, and this is often known (see
[

Because a weight-monodromy compatible representation is determined by

\rho^{N=0}

we get

Corollary 4
.

Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne representations
\rho=(\rho_{\rm Weil},N)
over
K

uniquely determined by the
traces
\Tr\rho^{N=0}(\Frob_{L})
,
where
L
varies over finite separable extensions of
K
over which
\rho_{\rm Weil}
is unramified.

Corollary 5
.

Let
X/K
be a proper smooth variety and

.
If the

-representation

For semistable abelian varieties these traces are known to be independent of

Corollary 6
.

For an abelian variety

, the Weil–Deligne representation associated
to
H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(A_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

\ell

Remark 9
.

Our strategy of proving that Weil representations are determined by their
Frobenius traces over extensions where they are unramified is known,
and goes back at least to Chebotarev. He used the fact that after adjoining enough
roots of unity, a finite extension of number fields has abundance of cyclotomic extensions
inside it to reduce his density theorem to Hecke’s cyclotomic case
(see e.g.
[
19
]
). Similarly,
Corollary
3
relies on the abudance of unramified extensions inside
F^{nr}/K

for a finite Galois extension
F/K
.
What is new is Theorem
1
, which makes this explicit in practice.

As mentioned above, Corollary
3
is due to Saito
[
20
, Lemma 1(1)]
. His

is slightly stronger but the proof only uses

The proof of
[

does not explain why

is a function of the traces,
and the field cut out by

is not a finite extension of

However, both of these issues can be circumvented with an argument similar to the proof of

3
below, by comparing two representations instead of taking one.

Consider a genus 3 curve

whose Jacobian has bad reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
, but
X
acquires good reduction over
F
.
Our goal is to determine the Galois representation
\rho=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(X_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\mathbb{C}
.
By the Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion (or just the proper smooth base change theorem),

I_{K}
acts on
V
through
I_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{7}}=C_{7}
.
By Theorem
1
(i), and the fact that the characteristic polynomials of inertia elements have
rational coefficients by
[
22

\rho\cong(\rho_{1}\otimes\Psi_{1})\oplus(\rho_{2}\otimes\Psi_{2}),

for some unramified characters
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
.

Theorem
1
lets us determine the
\Psi_{i}
as follows.
Let
\tilde{F}
be the degree 7 unramified extension of
F
. For each element
g\in I_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{7}}=C_{7}
let
\tilde{g}\in\Gal(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{7})
be the unique element that projects to
g\in\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q}_{7})
and acts as
\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}}^{3}
on the residue field. Write
F_{g}={\tilde{F}}^{\langle\tilde{g}\rangle}
for the corresponding degree 21 extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
. Note that
\Jac X
has good reduction over
F_{g}
, since
\tilde{F}/F_{g}
is unramified.

By the theorem,

\Psi_{i}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}}^{3})=\frac{1}{|C_{7}|}\frac{1}{\langle\Res_{C_{7}}\rho_{i},\Res_{C_{7}}\rho_{i}\rangle}\sum_{g\in I_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{7}}}\overline{\Tr\rho_{i}(g)}\Tr\rho(\Frob_{F_{g}})=

=\frac{1}{21}\sum_{g\in I_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{7}}}\overline{\Tr\rho_{i}(g)}\cdot(7^{3}+1-\#\overline{\mathcal{X}_{F_{g}}}(\mathbb{F}_{7^{3}})),

where
\mathcal{X}_{F_{g}}
is a regular model of
X
over
F_{g}
,
by the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula (as in proof of Theorem
10
(iii)). Moreover,
\Psi_{i}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}})
can be taken to be any cube root of this value, as the discrepancy vanishes when taking the tensor product with
\rho_{i}
.

As a particular example, let
F/\mathbb{Q}_{7}
be the splitting field of

f(x)=x^{7}+21x^{6}+7,

taken from
[
16
]
.
Let
\alpha
be a root of
f
in
F
; it is a uniformiser of
F
.
Let
\zeta\in F
be a primitive 18th root of unity.
One checks that the roots
\alpha_{j}

f
have expansions

\alpha_{j}=\alpha+j\zeta\alpha^{2}+O(\alpha^{3}),\qquad j=0,...,6.

The generators of
G
are then determined by

\sigma\colon\alpha_{j}\mapsto\alpha_{j+1}\text{ (order 7)},\qquad\tau\colon\alpha_{j}\mapsto\alpha_{2j}\text{ (order 3)},

with indices taken modulo 7.

Now take
X:y^{2}=f(x)
. It acquires good reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{7}(\alpha)
, and using Magma
[
3
]

we find that

\#\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{F_{\id}}(\mathbb{F}_{7^{3}})=7^{3}+1=344,

\#\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{F_{\sigma}}(\mathbb{F}_{7^{3}})=\#\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{F_{\sigma^{2}}}(\mathbb{F}_{7^{3}})=\#\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{F_{\sigma^{4}}}(\mathbb{F}_{7^{3}})=7^{3}-7^{2}+1=295,

\Psi_{1}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}})=\sqrt{-7}

\Psi_{2}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}})=-\sqrt{-7}

Conversely, let
X^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{7}+420x^{6}-245x^{3}+1225x^{2}-833x+189
.
(The right-hand side is the minimal polynomial of

.)
This curves acquires good reduction over the

X

\Psi_{1}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}})=-\sqrt{-7}

\Psi_{2}(\Frob_{\mathbb{Q}_{7}})=\sqrt{-7}

What makes the example interesting is that the two curves have the same point counts over all extensions
of
\mathbb{Q}_{7}

Remark 11
.

The assumption
\vchar K=0
in Theorems
2
,
7
,
10
and Corollary
8
is necessary,
as we will now explain.

First, suppose
M/K
is any ramified separable extension of
prime degree
p>2
. It is obtained by adjoining the root of some Eisenstein polynomial
f(x)
. Consider
the hyperelliptic Jacobian of dimension
g=(p-1)/2

A=\Jac C,\qquad C:y^{2}=f(x).

In the terminology of
[
10
]
,
C
has one proper cluster of size
p

(because
I_{K}
acts transitively on roots of
f(x)
and
\deg f=p
is prime).
By
[
10
, Thm 1.20]
,

\rho_{l}=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(A_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong\gamma\otimes(\pi\ominus\boldsymbol{1}),

as an
I_{K}
-representation, where
\gamma
is some tame character and
\pi
is the permutation character
of
I_{K}
on the roots of
f
.

Now, suppose
\vchar K=p
.
By
[
18
]
, there are extensions
M/K
as above with arbitrary large valuation of the discriminant.
Therefore, by the conductor-discriminant formula and the definition of the conductor, for every
n\geq 1
there
is a separable polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
of degree
p
such that the higher ramification group
G_{n}\triangleleft I_{K}

acts non-trivially on the roots of
f(x)
.

Let
K_{g}/K
be any finite Galois extension. Then
G_{n}
acts trivially on
K_{g}
for some
n
. However, the above
construction produces an abelian variety of dimension
g
for which

\rho_{l}^{\smash{I_{K_{g}}}}\subset\rho_{l}^{G_{n}}=0
.
Therefore
A
has non-semistable reduction over
K_{g}
and
\Tr\rho^{I_{L}}_{\ell}(\Frob_{L})=0
for every subfield

K\subset L\subset K_{g}
. In other words,
Theorems
2
,
7
,
10
and Corollary
8
fail over function fields.

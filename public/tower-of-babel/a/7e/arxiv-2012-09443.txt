Trace anomaly contribution to hydrogen atom mass
Bao-dong Sun, Ze-hao Sun, Jian Zhou

Abstract
We compute trace anomaly contribution to hydrogen atom mass, which turns out to be related to the part of the Lamb shift. This finding might shed new light on our understandings of the mass structure of QCD bound states, such as, proton.



The origin of proton mass is one of the most fundamental questions remain to be answered in hadronic physics study. The current light quark masses generated through the Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism only makes up a small part of proton mass. At the classical level, the proton mass vanishes identically in the massless limit due to the exact cancellation between the quark’s kinetic energy and the negative potential energy according to the relativistic virial theorem
Brack 1983
, the field theory formulation of which states that the trace of the QCD energy momentum tensor(EMT) vanishes in the chiral limit. Therefore, the large piece of proton mass essentially originates from the quantum effect, i.e. the trace anomaly of the energy momentum tensor
(
Adler et al. 1977
;
Nielsen 1977
;
Collins et al. 1977
)
, which comes as the consequence of the violation of the approximate conformal symmetry of QCD
Collins et al. 1977
.

Due to the non-perturbative nature of low energy QCD, it appears to be impossible to solve the problem analytically. Instead, one can try to gain some insights into the proton mass structure by decomposing it into different pieces
(
Ji 1995a
;
Ji 1995b
)
based on the QCD EMT. These different contributions can be computed in lattice QCD or measured in high energy scatterings
(
Kharzeev 1996
;
Kharzeev et al. 1999
;
Hatta and Yang 2018
;
Hatta et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Zeng et al. 2020
;
Boussarie and Hatta 2020
)
. In particular, the tremendous progress on lattice calculations has been made in recent years
(
Hagler et al. 2003
;
Gockeler et al. 2004
;
Yang et al. 2015
;
Yang et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2020a
)
. On the other hand, the proton mass decomposition issue
(
Lorce 2018
;
Hatta et al. 2018
;
Tanaka 2019
;
Rodini et al. 2020
)
and the renormalization properties of the different terms of the energy momentum tensor
(
Hatta and Yang 2018
;
Hatta et al. 2019
;
Metz et al. 2020
;
Rodini et al. 2020
)
, have gained the renewed interests previously. We recognized that the QCD EMT also plays a key role in the various different context of strongly interacting matter studies
(
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Cosyn et al. 2019
;
Polyakov and Sun 2019
;
Freese and Cloët 2019
;
Sun and Dong 2020
;
Yang et al. 2020b
)
. In this short note, we make an attempt to promote our understanding of the trace anomaly contribution to bound state mass with a different strategy. Namely, we compute the contribution from the trace anomaly of the QED EMT to the mass of the simplest QED bound state, i.e. hydrogen atom, and relate it to other known physical quantities since this is a completely solvable problem in quantum mechanics.

We begin with introducing the trace of the QED EMT,

\displaystyle T_{\mu}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\gamma_{m}\right)m_{0}\bar{\Psi}\Psi+\frac{\beta\left(e\right)}{2e}\left[F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right]_{R},

(1)

where
\gamma_{m}=\frac{3\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}
and
\frac{\beta\left(e\right)}{2e}=\frac{\alpha_{em}}{6\pi}
at the next to leading order. The subscript
R
indicates that the gauge field is the renormalized one
(
Adler et al. 1977
;
Tarrach 1982
)
.
m_{0}
is electron bare mass. Notice that the first operator is renormalization invariant
Adler and Bardeen 1971
, so that one has

m_{0}\bar{\Psi}\Psi=\left[m\bar{\Psi}\Psi\right]_{R}+O(\alpha_{em})
.

We first compute the leading order contribution to the expectation value of the operator
m_{0}\bar{\Psi}\Psi
for the ground state of hydrogen atom. For simplicity, the proton inside hydrogen atom merely serves as an infinitely heavy charge source and thus becomes decoupled in our treatment. The hydrogen atom mass at the leading order is then given by(omitting proton mass),

\displaystyle M_{H,0}=\frac{\left\langle H\left|\int d^{3}x\ m_{0}\bar{\Psi}(x)\Psi(x)\right|H\right\rangle}{\left\langle H|H\right\rangle}=m\int d^{3}x\ \varphi_{0}^{\dagger}(x)\gamma^{0}\varphi_{0}(x)

(2)

where
m_{0}=m
at the leading order with
m
being the electron physical mass.

\varphi_{0}(x)
is the ground state wave function normalized to
\int\varphi_{0}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\varphi_{0}\left(x\right)d^{3}x=1
. Plugging the wave function of the ground state computed from the Dirac equation and taking the nonrelativistic limit, we derive,

\displaystyle M_{H,0}=m\sqrt{1-\alpha_{em}^{2}}

(3)

The difference between
M_{H,0}
and electron mass is given by,

\displaystyle M_{H,0}-m=m\sqrt{1-\alpha_{em}^{2}}-m\approx-13.6\text{eV}

(4)

which is precisely the ground state energy of hydrogen atom.
Therefore, the EM potential energy and electron kinematic energy is implicitly
included in the electron mass term.

We now proceed to compute the trace anomaly contribution and the NLO correction to the electron mass term. At higher order, two operators
m_{0}\bar{\Psi}(x)\Psi(x)
and
F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}\left(x\right)
mix with each other under renormalization. As pointed out in Refs.
(
Metz et al. 2020
;
Rodini et al. 2020
)
, the trace anomaly contribution to the mass of the free electron state is scheme dependent, and perhaps is not a physical observable. What matters is the difference between the anomaly contribution to the mass of free particle and that in a bound state. Here we specify the subtraction scheme following Ref.
(
Adler et al. 1977
;
Metz et al. 2020
;
Rodini et al. 2020
)
,

\displaystyle\left\langle e\left|[F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}\left(x\right)]_{R}\right|e\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma\left|[F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}\left(x\right)]_{R}\right|\gamma\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma\left|Z_{3}^{-1}F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}\left(x\right)\right|\gamma\right\rangle_{\text{Tree}}

(5)

which appears to be the most natural choice. By inserting the time evolution operator, we calculate the expectation value of the trace anomaly part with,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\penalty\ \ref{vac}(a)}=\frac{\left\langle H\left|\int d^{3}x\frac{\beta}{2e}\left[F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}\left(x\right)\right]_{R}{\cal T}e^{-i\int d^{4}yH_{I}(y)}\right|H\right\rangle}{\left\langle H|H\right\rangle}

(6)

The Feynman diagram contributing to this matrix element at the first non-trivial order is shown in Fig.
1
(a). By applying the standard Feynman rules in the Coulomb gauge, one obtains,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\penalty\ \ref{vac}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}\alpha^{2}_{em}\int d^{3}y\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{y}}}{\vec{q}^{2}+i\epsilon}\left[\bar{\varphi}_{0}(y)\gamma^{0}\varphi_{0}\left(y\right)\right]

(7)

At the NLO, the expectation value of the electron mass term receives the contribution from the vacuum polarization diagram Fig.
1
(b)
Cui et al. 2011
,

\displaystyle 2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{vac}(b)}=8\alpha^{2}_{em}\!\int\!\!d^{3}y\!\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{y}}}{\vec{q}^{2}+i\epsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\!\!da\frac{a(1-a)m^{2}}{m^{2}+a(1-a)\vec{q}^{2}}\left[\bar{\varphi}_{0}(y)\gamma^{0}\varphi_{0}\left(y\right)\right]

(8)

It is convenient to group these two contributions together,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\text{F}ig.\ref{vac}(a)}\!+\!2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{vac}(b)}\approx

\displaystyle 8\alpha^{2}_{em}\!\!\int\!d^{3}y\!\int\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{y}}\int_{0}^{1}\!da\frac{a^{2}(1-a)^{2}}{m^{2}}{\varphi}_{0}^{\dagger}(y)\varphi_{0}\left(y\right)=\frac{-4\alpha_{em}^{2}}{15m^{2}}{\varphi}_{0}^{\dagger}(0)\varphi_{0}\left(0\right)

(9)

To arrive at the above expression, we have made the Taylor expansion in terms of the power
\frac{\vec{q}^{2}}{m^{2}}
. This turns out to be just the part of the Lamb shift that is caused by the vacuum polarization effect.

We now turn to the calculation of the self-energy corrections. As a warm up exercise, we first compute the self-energy corrections to the mass of free electron state as shown in Figs.
2
. It is straightforward to obtain,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}m_{0}+m-\delta m=\gamma_{m}m_{0}+m-\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}m_{0}\int_{0}^{1}da(2-a)\ln\frac{a\Lambda^{2}}{(1-a)^{2}m_{0}^{2}}

(10)

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}m_{0}\int_{0}^{1}da\left\{2\ln\frac{a\Lambda^{2}}{(1-a)^{2}m_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2(2-a)}{(1-a)}\right\}

(11)

\displaystyle 2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(c)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}m_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\!da\left\{-a\ln\frac{a\Lambda^{2}}{(1-a)^{2}m_{0}^{2}}+\frac{2a(2-a)}{(1-a)}\right\}

(12)

where
\Lambda
is the UV regulator in the Pauli-Villars regularization. Summing up all three terms, one obtains,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle e\left|\left(1+\gamma_{m}\right)m_{0}\int d^{3}x\bar{\Psi}(x)\Psi(x)\right|e\right\rangle}{\left\langle e|e\right\rangle}=m

(13)

As expected, at the NLO, the physical mass of a free electron entirely comes from the electron mass term and the associated anomaly part. An all order proof of Eq.
13
in the subtraction scheme specified above can be achieved by invoking the Callan-Symanzik equation
(
Adler et al. 1977
)
.

Now we move on to compute the difference of the self energy correction between a free electron state and that in the bound state. The self energy correction of electron in hydrogen atom still arises from the the loop diagrams Fig.
2
(b) and Fig.
2
(c), but with the internal electron propagator being replaced by the one computed in the presence of the background Coulomb field. The calculation can be most conveniently formulated using the NRQED in the Coulomb gauge. The effective Lagrangian of NRQED is obtained from the full QED Lagrangian,

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\psi^{\dagger}\!\left(\!i\partial^{0}-eA^{0}-\frac{\vec{p}^{2}}{2m_{0}}+\frac{e}{2m_{0}}(\vec{p}^{\prime}\!+\!\vec{p})\cdot\!\vec{A}-\frac{e^{2}}{2m_{0}}\vec{A}^{2}-(1+O(\alpha_{em}))\frac{ie}{2m_{0}}\sigma\!\cdot[(\vec{p}\!-\!\vec{p}^{\prime})\!\times\!\vec{A}]\right)\!\psi+...

(14)

by expressing the four component spinor
\Psi
in terms of two component spinor
\psi
in the nonrelativistic limit,

\displaystyle\Psi\approx e^{-im_{0}t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(1-\frac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{p}-e\vec{A})}{2m_{0}})\psi\\
(1+\frac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{p}-e\vec{A})}{2m_{0}})\psi\end{array}\right)

where
\vec{p}
and
\vec{p}^{\prime}
are three momentum operators acting on the fields
\psi
and
\psi^{\dagger}
respectively.

Figure 3:
Part of the NLO correction to the electron mass term in NRQED in vacuum(diagram a), and that in the bound state(diagram b).

To simplify the calculation of Fig.
2
(c) in NRQED, we play a trick by making use of the fact that the vector current is conserved under renormalization,

\displaystyle 2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(c)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left\langle e\left|m_{0}\int d^{3}x\bar{\Psi}_{R}(x)\gamma_{0}\Psi_{R}(x)\right|e\right\rangle}{\left\langle e|\int d^{3}x{\Psi}_{R}^{\dagger}(x)\Psi_{R}(x)|e\right\rangle_{\text{Tree}}}=-\frac{\left\langle e\left|m_{0}\int d^{3}x{\Psi}_{R}^{\dagger}(x)\Psi_{R}(x)\right|e\right\rangle}{\left\langle e|e\right\rangle}

(18)

where
\Psi_{R}
stands for the renormalized field. Combining the above with Fig.
2
(b), one obtains,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(b)}+2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(c)}=\frac{\left\langle e\left|m_{0}\int d^{3}x\left[\bar{\Psi}_{R}(x)\Psi_{R}(x)-{\Psi}_{R}^{\dagger}(x)\Psi_{R}(x)\right]\right|e\right\rangle}{\left\langle e|e\right\rangle}

(19)

which can be converted into the following form with the help of Eq.
Trace anomaly contribution to hydrogen atom mass
,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(b)}+2\times{\text{F}ig.\ref{mass}(c)}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle e\left|\int d^{3}x\left\{\psi^{\dagger}\left[\frac{e}{2m_{0}}(\vec{p}^{\prime}\!+\!\vec{p})\cdot\!\vec{A}-\frac{\vec{p}^{2}}{2m_{0}}-\frac{e^{2}}{2m_{0}}\vec{A}^{2}-\frac{ie}{2m_{0}}\sigma\!\cdot[(\vec{p}\!-\!\vec{p}^{\prime})\!\times\!\vec{A}]\right]\psi\right\}\right|e\right\rangle}{\left\langle e|e\right\rangle}

(20)

where the operators
\psi^{\dagger}
and
\psi
should be understood as the renormalized ones. It is easy to verify that the spin-orbital coupling term
-\frac{ie}{2m_{0}}\sigma\!\cdot[(\vec{p}\!-\!\vec{p}^{\prime})\!\times\!\vec{A}]
does not contribute to the mass shift. We are now ready to compute the expectation values of these operators in NRQED.

One first notices that the operators
\psi^{\dagger}\frac{\vec{p}^{2}}{2m_{0}}\psi
,
\psi^{\dagger}\frac{e^{2}}{2m_{0}}\vec{A}\cdot\!\vec{A}\psi
in a free electron state and in a bound state yield the same contribution at the order of interest
(
Weinberg 2005
)
. The energy shift entirely comes from the operator
\psi^{\dagger}\frac{e}{2m_{0}}(\vec{p}^{\prime}\!+\!\vec{p})\cdot\!\vec{A}\psi
. By applying the standard Feynman rules of NRQED in the Coulomb gauge, the Fig.
3
(a) reads,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\penalty\ \ref{bound}(a)}=\frac{\alpha_{em}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\int d^{3}x\varphi^{\dagger}_{0}(x)\frac{\nabla^{2}}{m^{2}_{0}}\varphi_{0}(x)\right)\int d^{3}k\left[\frac{2}{3\vec{k}^{2}}-\frac{1-\vec{k}^{2}/(3(\vec{k}^{2}+\mu^{2}))}{\vec{k}^{2}+\mu^{2}}\right]

(21)

To arrive at the above expression, we have imposed a scale cut off
\mu
by replacing the photon propagator
\frac{1}{k^{2}+i\epsilon}\rightarrow\frac{1}{k^{2}+i\epsilon}-\frac{1}{k^{2}-\mu^{2}+i\epsilon}
following the Weinberg’s method
(
Weinberg 2005
)
. In a bound state, the internal electron propagator in the position space is given by
\sum_{M}\frac{\varphi_{M}(x)\varphi_{M}^{\dagger}(y)}{\Delta E_{M}+i\epsilon}
, where
\varphi_{M}
are a complete orthonormal set of state-vectors, and
\Delta E_{M}
is the difference between the energies of the state M and the ground state. Here we neglect the positron contribution to the propagator. Using the electron propagator in the bound state, one readily obtains,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\ref{bound}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{em}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{M}\left|\int d^{3}x\varphi^{\dagger}_{0}(x)\frac{\nabla}{m_{0}}\varphi_{M}(x)\right|^{2}

(22)

\displaystyle\times\int d^{3}k\left[\frac{2}{3|\vec{k}|(\Delta E_{M}+|\vec{k}|)}-\frac{1-\vec{k}^{2}/(3(\vec{k}^{2}+\mu^{2}))}{\sqrt{\vec{k}^{2}+\mu^{2}}(\Delta E_{M}+\sqrt{\vec{k}^{2}+\mu^{2}})}\right]

The energy shift for the ground state is then given by,

\displaystyle{\text{F}ig.\ref{bound}(b)}-{\text{F}ig.\ref{bound}(a)}\approx\frac{4\alpha^{2}_{em}}{3m^{2}}\varphi^{\dagger}_{0}(0)\varphi_{0}(0)\left[\ln\frac{\mu}{2\Delta E}+\frac{5}{6}\right]

(23)

where
\Delta E
is a mean excitation energy. This result was first derived by Bethe in 1947
Bethe 1947
. To justify the nonrelativistic treatment for electron, the scale
\mu
should be chosen to be smaller than
m
, and was actually just set to be
m
in the Bethe’s original work, leading to a rather good agreement with the observed energy shift. To remove the
\mu
dependence in a rigorous way, one has to include the high energy part
(
Weinberg 2005
)
, or in modern effective field language, performs a matching calculation for the vertex correction between the full QED and NRQED
(
Labelle and Mohammad Zebarjad 1999
;
Porto 2017
;
Huang et al. 2019
)
, which is however beyond the focus of this short note.

In summary, we have calculated the contribution from the trace anomaly of the QED energy momentum tensor to the hydrogen atom mass(Eq.9). It is shown to be related to the small part of the Lamb shift splitting that arises from the vacuum polarization effect. We further computed the radiative corrections to the electron mass term of the QED EMT in the bound state(Eq.21), which turns out to be consistent with the electron self-energy calculation in the conventional treatment of the Lamb shift problem, as it should be. The trace anomaly part is widely believed to be the dominant contribution to the mass of QCD bound states. It would be interesting to extend this analysis to heavy quarkonium system with an effective potential model and compare with the Lattice result
Sun et al. 2020
in the future.

Co-t-structures on derived categories of coherent sheaves and the cohomology of tilting modules Thanks: P.A. was supported by NSF Grant No. DMS-1802241. W.H. was supported by the ARC Discovery Grant No. DP170104318.
Pramod N. Achar, William Hardesty

Abstract
. We construct a co- t t -structure on the derived category of coherent sheaves on the nilpotent cone 𝒩 \mathcal{N} of a reductive group, as well as on the derived category of coherent sheaves on any parabolic Springer resolution. These structures are employed to show that the push-forwards of the “exotic parity objects” (considered in [ AHR1 ] ), along the (classical) Springer resolution, give indecomposable objects inside the coheart of the co- t t -structure on 𝒩 \mathcal{N} . We also demonstrate how the various parabolic co- t t -structures can be related by introducing an analogue to the usual translation functors. As an application, we give a proof of a scheme-theoretic formulation of the relative Humphreys conjecture on support varieties of tilting modules in type A A for p > h p>h .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>h
(where
h
is the Coxeter number for
\mathbf{G}
), and let
\mathbf{G}_{1}
be its first Frobenius kernel. This paper is concerned with the study of the
\mathbf{G}_{1}
-cohomology of
\mathbf{G}
-modules, denoted by
H^{\bullet}(\mathbf{G}_{1},M)
. Via the well-known identification of
H^{\bullet}(\mathbf{G}_{1},\Bbbk)
with the coordinate ring of the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}
of the Frobenius twist of
\mathbf{G}
(see
[
AJ
,
FP
]
), we see that
H^{\bullet}(\mathbf{G}_{1},M)
can be thought of as a coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{N}
.

The
relative Humphreys conjecture

[
Hu
]
describes the support of this coherent sheaf when
M
is an indecomposable tilting
\mathbf{G}
-module. This conjecture has been proved for
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
when
p>n
by the second author
[
H
]
, and for arbitrary reductive
\mathbf{G}
when
p\gg 0
by the authors together with S. Riche
[
AHR1
]
. The latter paper drew inspiration from Bezrukavnikov’s work on the quantum group version of the Humphreys conjecture
[
Be2
]
, especially in its invocation of derived equivalences with constructible sheaves on affine flag varieties.

However, one key feature of Bezrukavnikov’s work was absent from
[
AHR1
]
. In
[
Be2
]
, Bezrukavnikov gave an intrinsic characterization of the (complexes of) coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{N}
that can arise as the cohomology of tilting modules for a quantum group: they are precisely the simple objects in the heart of the
perverse-coherent
t
-structure
on
\mathcal{N}
. This characterization is false in the reductive group case considered in
[
AHR1
]
, and no alternative characterization was known at that time.

This paper was inspired by a desire to find such an intrinsic characterization. Instead of using
t
-structures, we equip the derived category of (dg) coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{N}
with a
co-
t
-structure
, and we show (see Lemma
6.4
) that the indecomposable objects in its coheart (called
silting objects
) are precisely the sheaves arising as
\mathbf{G}_{1}
-cohomology of indecomposable tilting
\mathbf{G}
-modules.

But the main results have to do with the interaction between our co-
t
-structure and (modified) translation functors between different blocks of
\mathbf{G}
-modules. Of course,
H^{\bullet}(\mathbf{G}_{1},M)=\Ext^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\Bbbk,M)
is zero unless
M
lies in the (extended) principal block of
\mathbf{G}
. But if
M
lies in some other (typically singular) block, one can instead study
\Ext^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\mathrm{St}_{I}^{\mathbf{G}},M)
, where
\mathrm{St}_{I}^{\mathbf{G}}
is a suitable “Steinberg-type module” (see Section
6
for the definition). These
\Ext
-groups still have the structure of a module over
\Ext^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)\cong\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
, and hence give coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{N}
.

The main technical result of the paper, Theorem
6.8
, implies that the module
\Ext^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\mathrm{St}_{I}^{\mathbf{G}},M)
again lies in the coheart of our co-
t
-structure. The support of
\Ext^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\mathrm{St}_{I}^{\mathbf{G}},M)
is easily seen to be contained in the closure of the Richardson orbit corresponding to the set
I
of simple reflections. Thus, Theorem
6.8
implies that there is a large supply of silting objects supported on Richardson orbit closures.

As an application, in Section
7
, we give a new, geometric proof of the relative Humphreys conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, valid for
p>n
. This proof hinges on the fact that every nilpotent orbit for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is Richardson.

In fact, we obtain a stronger statement than what was known before: we show that if
M
is an indecomposable tilting module in the principal block for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, then
H^{\bullet}(\mathbf{G}_{1},M)
is supported
scheme-theoretically
(not just set-theoretically) on the orbit closure predicted by Humphreys. We call this stronger statement the
scheme-theoretic (relative) Humphreys conjecture
. We expect that this scheme-theoretic version holds for any reductive group
\mathbf{G}
, and we hope that the tools developed in this paper may be useful for proving it.

2.
Background on co-
t
-structures

In this section, we consider various homological algebra constructions involving
co-
t
-structures
(the definition will be reviewed below). Most of the results in this section have close analogues for
t
-structures, found in
[
BBD
,
Be1
]
among other sources. From Section
2.2
on, we will emphasize the parallels between the
t
-structure and co-
t
-structure situations by including both settings in the statements of most lemmas and propositions below. However, we will usually give proofs only in the co-

2.1.
Definition and generalities

We begin with some notation for subcategories of triangulated categories. Given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}
in a triangulated category
\mathfrak{D}
, we define four full subcategories of
\mathfrak{D}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l}&\text{\it\ldots is defined to be the smallest full additive sub-}\\
\text{\it Notation}&\text{\it category of $\mathfrak{D}$ containing $\mathcal{X}$ and closed under \ldots}\\
\hline\cr\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}&\text{extensions}\\
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}&\text{extensions and direct summands}\\
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}&\text{$[\pm 1]$ and extensions}\\
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{tri},\oplus}&\text{$[\pm 1]$, extensions, and direct summands}\end{array}

The latter two are triangulated categories. We obviously have
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}\subset\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}
and
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}\subset\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle_{\mathrm{tri},\oplus}
; in some situations, these containments are equalities.

We now recall the definition and some basic facts on co-
t
-structures (see
[
Jo
,
KY
]
for an overview).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category. A
co-
t
-structure
on
\mathfrak{D}
is a pair of full additive subcategories
(\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0})
with the following properties:

(1)

Both
\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
are closed under direct summands.

(2)

We have
\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}[-1]\subset\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}[1]\subset\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
.

(3)

For
A\in\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
B\in\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}[1]
, we have
\Hom(A,B)=0
.

(4)

For any
X\in\mathfrak{D}
, there exists a distinguished triangle
A\to X\to B\to
with
A\in\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
B\in\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}[1]
.

(These axioms imply that
\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
are automatically closed under extensions.)
A co-
t
-structure
(\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0})
is said to be
bounded
if

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}[n]=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}[n]=\mathfrak{D}.

The additive subcategory
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
is called the
coheart
of the co-
t
-structure. Objects of
\mathfrak{C}
are called
silting objects
. A
silting generator
is a silting object
T
with the property that every object in
\mathfrak{C}
is a direct sum of direct summands of
T
.

Remark 2.2
.

Our definition of the term
silting
is not consistent with the co-
t
-structure literature, and the difference in usage is similar to the difference in the usage of the word
tilting
for algebraic groups or for finite-dimensional algebras. In most of the literature, a
silting object
is defined to be an object that generates a silting subcategory (see Definition
2.4
below) under direct sums and direct summands.

Definition 2.3
.

If
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
are two triangulated categories equipped with co-
t
-structures with cohearts

\mathfrak{C}\subset\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
, then we say that a triangulated functor

F:\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
is
co-
t
-exact
if
F(\mathfrak{C})\subset\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
(i.e.
F
preserves the cohearts).

Conversely, one can intrinsically characterize subcategories arising as cohearts of co-
t
-structures in the following way.

Definition 2.4
.

A full additive subcategory
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is a
silting subcategory
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\mathfrak{S}
is closed under direct summands.

(2)

For any two objects
S
,
S^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have
\Hom(S,S^{\prime}[k])=0
for all
k>0
.

(3)

\mathfrak{D}=\langle\mathfrak{S}\rangle_{\mathrm{tri},\oplus}
.

For a proof of the following proposition, see
[
MSSS
, Corollary 5.9]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category. A full subcategory
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is a silting subcategory if and only if it is the coheart of a bounded co-
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
. In this case, the co-
t
-structure is uniquely determined: it is given by

\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}=\langle\mathfrak{S}[-k]:k\geq 0\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus},\qquad\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}=\langle\mathfrak{S}[k]:k\geq 0\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}.

Remark 2.6
.

If
\mathfrak{D}=D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathfrak{A}
where
\mathfrak{A}
is a highest weight category, then
the subcategory
\mathsf{Tilt}(\mathfrak{A})\subset\mathfrak{A}
of tilting objects is a silting subcategory.
The corresponding co-
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
will be called the
natural co-
t
-structure
.
(An alternative description of the co-
t
-structure in terms of standard and co-standard objects will be given in Proposition
2.21
.)

The following proposition describes parallel ways of constructing
t
- and co-
t
-structures in a category generated by a single object.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear triangulated category. Suppose there is an object
A
that generates
\mathfrak{D}
as a triangulated category, and assume that
\End(A)\cong\Bbbk
. Let

\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)=\langle A[i]:i\geq 0\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)=\langle A[i]:i\leq 0\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}.

Then:

(1)

If
\Hom(A,A[i])=0
for
i<0
, then
(\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0),\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0))
is a
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
. In fact, it is the unique
t
-structure whose heart contains
A
. Moreover, the heart is a finite-length abelian category, and
A
is the unique simple object.

(2)

If
\Hom(A,A[i])=0
for
i>0
, then
(\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0),\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0))
is a co-
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
. In fact, it is the unique co-
t
-structure whose coheart contains
A
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{D}
is Karoubian, then the coheart is a Krull–Schmidt category, and
A
is the unique indecomposable object.

We will not prove this proposition; instead, we will prove a “graded” variant of it later on (see Proposition
2.13
). The proof given there is easily adapted to prove Proposition
2.7
. For the
t
-structure part of this statement, see
[
Be1
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear triangulated category equipped with a co-
t
-structure
(\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0})
. Suppose the following conditions hold:

(1)

The category
\mathfrak{D}
is Karoubian.

(2)

For
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
, the
\Bbbk
-vector space
\Hom(X,Y)
is finite-dimensional.

Then the coheart
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
is a Krull–Schmidt category.

Proof.

The coheart of our co-
t
-structure is closed under direct summands, so it is also Karoubian. Assumption (
2
) implies that
\Hom
-spaces in
\mathfrak{C}
are finite-dimensional. According to
[
CYZ
, Corollary A.2]
,
\mathfrak{C}
is Krull–Schmidt.
∎

\iota

\Pi

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{D}
, and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
be three triangulated categories, and suppose we have a recollement diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{D}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\mathrm{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}}

Assume that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
is equipped with a co-

-structure
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0})
, and that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
is equipped with a co-
t
-structure
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}_{\leq 0})
. Then the categories

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle=\{X\mid\iota^{\mathrm{L}}(X)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0},\Pi(X)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq 0}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle=\{X\mid\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(X)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0},\Pi(X)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}_{\leq 0}\}

\mathfrak{D}
.

For a proof, see
[
Bo

. (In
[

, co-

-structures are called

.) The new co-

-structure on
\mathfrak{D}

\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}

. Note that the functors
\iota

Lemma 2.10
.

In the setting of the recollement diagram of Proposition
2.9
, if
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
, then the map

(2.1)

\Pi:\Hom(X,Y)\to\Hom(\Pi(X),\Pi(Y))

is surjective. As a consequence, if the coheart
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{D}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{\leq 0}
is a Krull–Schmidt category, then
\Pi
sends any indecomposable object in
\mathfrak{C}
to either
0
or an indecomposable object.

Proof.

The recollement formalism gives us a distinguished triangle
\iota\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)\to Y\to\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi(Y)\to
, and hence a long exact sequence

(2.2)

\cdots\to\Hom(X,Y)\to\Hom(X,\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi(Y))\to\Hom(X,\iota\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)[1])\to\cdots.

We have
\Hom(X,\iota\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)[1])\cong\Hom(\iota^{\mathrm{L}}(X),\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)[1])
. From Proposition
2.9
, we have
\iota^{\mathrm{L}}(X)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}
and
\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0}
. From the definition of a co-
t
-structure, we have
\Hom(\iota^{\mathrm{L}}(X),\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(Y)[1])=0
, so the first map in (
2.2
) is surjective. Via the isomorphism
\Hom(X,\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi(Y))\cong\Hom(\Pi(X),\Pi(Y))
, we see that (
2.1
) is also surjective.

As a special case, for any object
X\in\mathfrak{C}
, the map
\End(X)\to\End(\Pi(X))
is surjective. In particular, if
\End(X)
is a local ring and
\Pi(X)
is nonzero, then
\End(\Pi(X))
is also a local ring (since any quotient of a local ring is local), and hence
\Pi(X)
is indecomposable. The last assertion then follows, since every indecomposable object in a Krull–Schmidt category has a local endomorphism ring.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

In the setting of the recollement diagram of Proposition
2.9
, let
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{C}
, and
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime}
denote the cohearts of the co-
t
-structures on
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Assume that all three cohearts are Krull–Schmidt categories.

(1)

If
T^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
is indecomposable, then
\iota(T^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{C}
is indecomposable.

(2)

If
T^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime}
is indecomposable, there is an indecomposable object
T\in\mathfrak{C}
, unique up to isomorphism, such that
\Pi(T)\cong T^{\prime\prime}
.

(3)

Every indecomposable object in
\mathfrak{C}
comes from parts (
1
) or (
2
) above.

Proof.

Recall that in a Krull–Schmidt category, an object is indecomposable if and only if its endomorphism ring is a local ring. Part (
1
) is immediate from the fact that
\iota
is fully faithful.

For part (
2
), consider the object
\iota^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}(T^{\prime\prime})\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
. By Definition
2.1
, there
exists a (noncanonical) distinguished triangle
A\to\iota^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}(T^{\prime\prime})\to B\to
with
A\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}[-1]
and
B\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0}
. Consider the composition
\iota(A)\to\iota\iota^{\mathrm{R}}\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}(T^{\prime\prime})\to\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}(T^{\prime\prime})
. Let
\tilde{T}
be the cone of this map, so that we have a distinguished triangle

\iota(A)\to\Pi^{\mathrm{L}}(T^{\prime\prime})\to\tilde{T}\to.

It is straightforward to see from the definitions that
\tilde{T}
lies in
\mathfrak{C}
. Moreover, since

\Pi(\tilde{T})\cong T^{\prime\prime}
is indecomposable,
\tilde{T}
must have a unique indecomposable summand that is not killed by
\Pi
. Let
T
denote this summand. This is an indecomposable object in
\mathfrak{C}
satisfying
\Pi(T)\cong T^{\prime\prime}
.

Before proving the uniqueness of
T
, let us consider part (
3
). Let
X\in\mathfrak{C}
be an indecomposable object. If
\Pi(X)=0
, then the recollement formalism implies that
X\cong\iota(T^{\prime})
, where
T^{\prime}=\iota^{\mathrm{L}}(X)\cong\iota^{\mathrm{R}}(X)
, so we are in case (
1
). If
\Pi(X)\neq 0
, then by Lemma
2.10
, it is indecomposable. Let
T^{\prime\prime}=\Pi(X)
.

The uniqueness in part (
2
) and the remainder of part (
3
) both come down to the following assertion:
If
X\in\mathfrak{C}
is indecomposable and
\Pi(X)\cong T^{\prime\prime}
, then
X\cong T
.
Let us prove this claim. Choose an isomorphism
\theta:\Pi(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}T^{\prime\prime}
. By Lemma
2.10
, the maps

\Hom(X,T)\to\Hom(\Pi(X),T^{\prime\prime})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Hom(T,X)\to\Hom(T^{\prime\prime},\Pi(X))

are both surjective. Therefore, there exist maps
\phi:X\to T
and
\psi:T\to X
such that
\Pi(\phi)=\theta
and
\Pi(\psi)=\theta^{-1}
. Note that
\phi\circ\psi
is an element of the local ring
\End(T)
whose image in
\End(T^{\prime\prime})
is the identity map. It follows that
\phi\circ\psi
is invertible. The same reasoning shows that
\psi\circ\phi
is invertible, and hence that
\phi
and
\psi
are themselves isomorphisms, as desired.
∎

2.2.
Categories with a Tate twist

For the remainder of this section, we will work in the “graded” setting: we always assume that our triangulated category
\mathfrak{D}
is equipped with an autoequivalence

\mathord{\lbag}1\mathord{\rbag}:\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}

such that for an object
X\in\mathfrak{D}
, we have

X\mathord{\lbag}1\mathord{\rbag}\cong X\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad X=0.

This functor is called the
shift-of-grading functor
(or sometimes the
Tate twist
). For any object
X\in\mathfrak{D}
and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we write
X\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
for the object obtained by applying the shift-of-grading functor
n
times.

Given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}
in a triangulated category
\mathfrak{D}
equipped with a shift-of-grading functor
\mathord{\lbag}1\mathord{\rbag}
, we define four subcategories of
\mathfrak{D}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l}&\text{\it\ldots is defined to be the smallest full subcategory}\\
\text{\it Notation}&\text{\it of $\mathfrak{D}$ containing $\mathcal{X}$ and closed under \ldots}\\
\hline\cr\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}&\text{extensions and $\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}$}\\
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}&\text{extensions, $\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}$, and direct summands}\\
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}&\text{$[\pm 1]$, extensions and $\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}$}\\
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri},\oplus}&\text{$[\pm 1]$, extensions and $\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}$, and direct summands}\end{array}

The latter two are triangulated categories. We obviously have
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}\subset\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}
and
\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}\subset\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri},\oplus}
; in some situations, these containments are equalities.

We will also use a graded variant of the “
*
” operation from
[
BBD
, §1.3.9]
. Given
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{D}
, we define

\mathcal{X}_{1}\mathbin{\underline{*}}\mathcal{X}_{2}

to be the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{D}
consisting of objects
X
such that there is a distinguished triangle

A_{1}\mathord{\lbag}n_{1}\mathord{\rbag}\to X\to A_{2}\mathord{\lbag}n_{2}\mathord{\rbag}\to\qquad\text{with $A_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}$, $A_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}$, and $n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

The proof of
[
BBD
, Proposition 1.3.10]
shows that
\mathbin{\underline{*}}
is associative. For each
n\geq 0
, we can set

\mathcal{X}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}=\underbrace{\mathcal{X}\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}\mathcal{X}}_{\text{$n$ factors}},

where the
0
-fold
\mathbin{\underline{*}}
-power of
\mathcal{X}
is understood to consist of just the zero object.
Observe that if we let
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}=\{X[i]\mid X\in\mathcal{X},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, then

\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{X}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\widehat{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}.

The following statement is the graded analogue of Proposition
2.7
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear triangulated category equipped with a shift-of-grading functor
\mathord{\lbag}1\mathord{\rbag}:\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}
. Suppose there is an object
A
such that
\mathfrak{D}=\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}
, and assume that

\Hom(A,A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag})\cong\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $n=0$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let

\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)=\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\geq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)=\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\leq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}.

Then:

(1)

If
\Hom(A,A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for
i<0
, then
(\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0),\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0))
is a
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
. In fact, it is the unique
t
-structure stable under

\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}
whose heart contains
A
. Moreover, the heart is a finite-length abelian category, and the simple objects are of the form
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
.

(2)

If
\Hom(A,A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for
i>0
, then
(\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0),\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0))
is a co-
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
. In fact, it is the unique co-
t
-structure
stable under
\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}
whose coheart contains
A
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{D}
is Karoubian, then the coheart is a Krull–Schmidt category, and the indecomposable objects are of the form
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
.

Proof.

(
1
) The ungraded analogue of this claim is proved in
[
Be1
, Lemma 3]
(see
[
AHR2
, Proposition A.1]
for a related argument), and that proof is easily adapted to the graded case. More precisely, it is shown in
loc. cit.
that the categories
\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\geq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}
and
\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\leq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}
constitute a
t
-structure. The subcategories defining a
t
-structure are automatically closed under direct summands (this follows from the existence of truncation functors), so in the setting of part (
1
) of the proposition, we have
\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\geq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}=\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\geq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}
and
\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\leq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext}}=\langle\!\langle A[i]:i\leq 0\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{ext},\oplus}
.

(
2
) Axioms (
1
) and (
2
) from Definition
2.1
are clear, and axiom (
3
) follows from the assumption that
\Hom(A,A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for
i>0
. Next, we claim that if
k>m
, then

(2.3)

A[k]\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[m]\subset A[m]\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k].

Indeed, the left-hand side consists of objects
X
that fit into a distinguished triangle
A[k]\mathord{\lbag}n_{1}\mathord{\rbag}\to X\to A[m]\mathord{\lbag}n_{2}\mathord{\rbag}\to A[k+1]\mathord{\lbag}n_{1}\mathord{\rbag}
. But since
k>m
, we have
k+1>m
as well, so our assumptions imply that the map
A[m]\mathord{\lbag}n_{2}\mathord{\rbag}\to A[k+1]\mathord{\lbag}n_{1}\mathord{\rbag}
is zero, i.e., the triangle splits. Thus, we have
X\cong A[k]\mathord{\lbag}n_{1}\mathord{\rbag}\oplus A[m]\mathord{\lbag}n_{2}\mathord{\rbag}\in A[m]\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k]
.

For any
X\in\mathfrak{D}
, there exist integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}
such that

(2.4)

X\in A[k_{1}]\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k_{j}].

Using (
2.3
) repeatedly, we may assume that
k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{j}
. Let
i
be the unique subscript such that
k_{i}\leq 0
but
k_{i+1}>0
(here we permit
i=0
or
i=j
if necessary). Then

(2.5)

X\in(A[k_{1}]\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k_{i}])\mathbin{\underline{*}}(A[k_{i+1}]\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k_{j}])\subset\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)\mathbin{\underline{*}}(\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)[1]).

We have proved axiom (
4
), and hence that
(\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0),\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0))
is a co-
t
-structure.

If there were another co-
t
-structure on
\mathfrak{D}
, say
(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0})
, whose coheart contained
A
and was stable under
\mathord{\lbag}{\pm 1}\mathord{\rbag}
, then we would clearly have
\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)\subset\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)\subset\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{\leq 0}
. This implies that the two co-
t
-structures coincide. We have proved the uniqueness claim in the proposition.

From now on, we assume that
\mathfrak{D}
is Karoubian. Let
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)\cap\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)
be the coheart of our co-
t
-structure. We claim that
\mathfrak{C}
is Krull–Schmidt. It is enough to check condition (
2
) from Lemma
2.8
. This condition follows easily from the observation that if
i\leq 0\leq j
, then
\Hom(A[i],A[j]\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag})
is finite-dimensional.

Note that
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
is indecomposable, because
\End(A)\cong\Bbbk
is a local ring.

Finally, let
X
be a nonzero object in
\mathfrak{C}
. It remains to show that
X
is a direct sum of objects of the form
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
. Find an expression as in (
2.4
), with
k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{j}
. We proceed by induction on
j
. If
j=1
, then
X\cong A[k_{1}]\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
X\in\mathfrak{C}
, we must have
k_{1}=0
, and we are done.

From now on, suppose
j>1
. Suppose first that
k_{j}>0
. Let
i
be such that
k_{i}\leq 0
but
k_{i+1}>0
, as in (
2.5
). If
i=0
, we would have
X\in\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)[1]
, a contradiction. We therefore have
1\leq i<j
. From (
2.5
), we get a distinguished triangle
X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to
with
X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)
and
X^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}(\leq 0)[1]
. The arrow
X\to X^{\prime\prime}
must be
0
, so
X^{\prime}\cong X\oplus X^{\prime\prime}[-1]
. This tells us that
X^{\prime\prime}[-1]\in\mathfrak{D}(\geq 0)
, so
X^{\prime\prime}[-1]
actually lies in the coheart. The triangle
X^{\prime\prime}[-1]\to X^{\prime}\to X\to
shows that
X^{\prime}
lies in the coheart as well. By induction, we know that
X^{\prime}\in A[k_{1}]\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}A[k_{i}]
is a direct sum of objects of the form
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
. Since
X
is a direct summand of
X^{\prime}
, and since the coheart is Krull–Schmidt, our claim for
X
holds.

Next, if
k_{1}<0
, we instead define
i
to be such that
k_{i}<0
but
k_{i+1}\geq 0
, and then consider the decomposition (
2.5
). The rest of the argument in this case is very similar to the preceding paragraph.

The remaining case is that in which
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{j}=0
. In this case, we get a distinguished triangle
X^{\prime}\to X\to A\mathord{\lbag}m\mathord{\rbag}\to
, where

X^{\prime}\in\underbrace{A\mathbin{\underline{*}}\cdots\mathbin{\underline{*}}A}_{\text{$j-1$ factors}}.

We have
\Hom(A\mathord{\lbag}m\mathord{\rbag},X^{\prime}[1])=0
, so the triangle splits, and
X\cong X^{\prime}\oplus A\mathord{\lbag}m\mathord{\rbag}
. By induction,
X^{\prime}
is a direct sum of objects of the form
A\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}
, so we are done.
∎

2.3.
Pre-exceptional sets

We will now develop a vast generalization of Proposition
2.13
. Let
(S,\leq)
be a partially ordered set. Assume that the partial order
\leq
admits a refinement to a total order
\leq^{\prime}
such that
(S,\leq^{\prime})
is isomorphic to a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. In particular,
S
is either finite or countable, and

(2.6)

the set
\{t\in S\mid t\leq s\}
is finite.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category equipped with a Tate twist
\mathord{\lbag}1\mathord{\rbag}
, and let
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a collection of objects in
\mathfrak{D}
indexed by
S
.
This collection is said to be a
graded pre-exceptional set
if it satisfies the following axioms:

(1)

If
s\not\geq t
, then
\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n,i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

We have

\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag})\cong\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $n=0$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3)

The collection
\{\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}\}_{s\in S,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
generates
\mathfrak{D}
as a triangulated category.

Suppose
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
is a graded pre-exceptional set. Then:

(
4^{+}
)

If
\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
i<0
, then
\{\nabla_{s}\}
is said to be a
graded quasi-exceptional set
.

(
4^{-}
)

If
\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
i>0
, then
\{\nabla_{s}\}
is said to be a
graded co-quasi-exceptional set
.

(
4^{\pm}
)

If
\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
i\neq 0
, then
\{\nabla_{s}\}
is said to be a
graded exceptional set
.

Exceptional and quasi-exceptional sets have been studied elsewhere in the literature, but we are not aware of any study of co-quasi-exceptional sets. It turns out, however, that many of the basic lemmas about exceptional and quasi-exceptional sets can be proved using only the axioms for pre-exceptional sets, so they remain valid for co-quasi-exceptional sets as well.

Remark 2.15
.

(1)

Of course, Definition
2.14
has an obvious ungraded analogue, as do the definitions and propositions below. We leave it to the reader to formulate the precise statements.

(2)

The definition of “graded quasi-exceptional” given above matches that in
[
Be1
]
, but not that in
[
A1
]
: the latter includes an extra condition that we will not impose.

If
\mathfrak{D}
is equipped with a graded pre-exceptional set
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
, then for any lower set
U\subset S
, we define

\mathfrak{D}_{U}=\langle\!\langle\nabla_{u}:u\in U\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}.

(Recall that a subset
U\subset S
is called a
lower set
if
t\in U
and
u\leq t
implies
u\in U
.) For the special case
U=\{u\in S\mid u\leq s\}
, we simply write
\mathfrak{D}_{\leq s}
. The categories
\mathfrak{D}_{<s}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\not\geq s}
are defined similarly.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a graded pre-exceptional set. It is said to be
dualizable
if for each
s\in S
, there is an object
\Delta_{s}\in\mathfrak{D}
and a morphism
\iota_{s}:\Delta_{s}\to\nabla_{s}
such that the following two conditions hold:

(1)

The cone of
\iota_{s}:\Delta_{s}\to\nabla_{s}
lies in
\mathfrak{D}_{<s}
.

(2)

If
s>t
, then
\Hom(\Delta_{s},\nabla_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n,i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.17
.

According to
[
Be2
, Proposition 3(b)]
, every graded exceptional set is automatically dualizable. (That statement assumes that
S
is finite, but the same reasoning applies in our situation thanks to (
2.6
).)

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a dualizable graded pre-exceptional set. For each
s\in S
, the pair
(\Delta_{s},\iota_{s}:\Delta_{s}\to\nabla_{s})
is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose there is another pair
(\Delta^{\prime}_{s},\iota^{\prime}_{s}:\Delta^{\prime}_{s}\to\nabla_{s})
satisfying the conditions in Definition
2.16
. Those conditions imply that if
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{<s}
, then
\Hom(\Delta_{s},X)=\Hom(\Delta^{\prime}_{s},X)=0
.

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
denote the cones of
\iota_{s}
and
\iota^{\prime}_{s}
, respectively. By the observation above, we have

\Hom(\Delta_{s},K^{\prime})=\Hom(\Delta_{s},K^{\prime}[-1])=0.

By
[
BBD
, Proposition 1.1.9]
, there is a unique map
f:\Delta_{s}\to\Delta^{\prime}_{s}
that makes the left-hand square in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{\prime}_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}_{s}}

commute. By swapping the roles of
\Delta_{s}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{s}
, one can see that
f
must be an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a dualizable graded pre-exceptional set, and let
\{\Delta_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be the dual set. Then:

(1)

If
s\neq t
, then
\Hom(\Delta_{s},\nabla_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n,i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
s\not\leq t
, then
\Hom(\Delta_{s},\Delta_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])=0
for all
n,i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

For all
n,i\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are natural isomorphisms

\Hom(\Delta_{s},\Delta_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\cong\Hom(\Delta_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\cong\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i]).

(4)

For any lower set
U\subset S
, we have
\mathfrak{D}_{U}=\langle\!\langle\Delta_{u}[i]:u\in U\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}
.

Proof.

Note that Definition
2.16
(
1
) implies that

(2.7)

\Delta_{u}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\leq u}\qquad\text{for all $u\in S$.}

Definition
2.14
(
1
) and Definition
2.16
(
2
) imply that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Hom(X,\nabla_{u})

\displaystyle=0

for all
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{\not\geq u}
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Hom(\Delta_{u},X)

\displaystyle=0

for all
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{<u}
,

respectively. Let us now prove the various parts of the present lemma.

(
1
) If
s>t
, this holds by Definition
2.16
(
2
). If
s\not\geq t
, it follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).

(
2
) By part (
1
), we can strengthen (
2.9
) to

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Hom(\Delta_{u},X)

\displaystyle=0

for all
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{\not\geq u}
.

The claim follows from this and (
2.7
).

(
3
) Let
K
be the cone of
\iota_{s}:\Delta_{s}\to\nabla_{s}
, and consider the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\Hom(\Delta_{s},K\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i-1])\to\Hom(\Delta_{s},\Delta_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\\
\to\Hom(\Delta_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\to\Hom(\Delta_{s},K\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\to\cdots.

The first and last terms vanish by (

), so the middle two are naturally isomorphic. The proof that
\Hom(\Delta_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])\cong\Hom(\nabla_{s},\nabla_{s}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}[i])
is similar.

(
4
) Let
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{U}=\langle\!\langle\Delta_{u}:u\in U\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}
. It follows from (

) that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{U}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{U}
. To prove equality, let us first consider the case where

is finite. We proceed by induction on the number of elements in

. Choose a maximal element

, and let

. Then
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{V}=\mathfrak{D}_{V}
by induction, so
\mathfrak{D}_{U}
is generated by
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{V}
together with
\{\nabla_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. By Definition
2.16
(

), it is also generated by
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{V}
together with
\{\Delta_{t}\mathord{\lbag}n\mathord{\rbag}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. We conclude that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{U}=\mathfrak{D}_{U}
.

If
U
is infinite, any object
X\in\mathfrak{D}_{U}
is still contained in a subcategory
\mathfrak{D}_{U^{\prime}}
for some finite subset

, so the preceding paragraph tells us that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}_{U}=\mathfrak{D}_{U}
in this case as well.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\{\nabla_{s}\}_{s\in S}
be a dualizable graded pre-exceptional set, and let

be a lower set. Let

be a maximal element of

, and let

. Let

be the inclusion functor, and let

be the Verdier quotient functor. Both of these functors admit left and right adjoints, and together, the six functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{D}_{U^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{D}_{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{D}_{U}/\mathfrak{D}_{U^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\mathrm{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\mathrm{R}}}

constitute a recollement diagram.

This result is proved in
[

Define
\mathfrak{D}_{t}=\langle\!\langle\Delta_{t}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{t}=\langle\!\langle\nabla_{t}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathrm{tri}}

, there are distinguished triangles

(2.11)

\displaystyle Y_{1}\to

\displaystyle X\to Y_{2}\to

\displaystyle\text{with $Y_{1}\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}$, $Y_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}_{U^{\prime}}$,}

\bigoplus_{k}\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\langle k\rangle)
is at most
1

For
\mathcal{N}
, take
\mathcal{F}=\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}[n]
and
\mathcal{G}=\overline{\nabla}_{\mu}[m]
. According to
[
A1
, Proposition 6.1]
, these objects come from a quasi-exceptional set, so if

says that our

\Hom(\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}[m],\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\cong\Hom(\pi_{*}\widetilde{\Delta}_{w_{0}\lambda},\pi_{*}\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}[n-m]\langle k\rangle)\\
\cong\Hom(\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\widetilde{\Delta}_{w_{0}\lambda},\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}[n-m]\langle k\rangle).

Since
\pi
is not smooth, the derived coherent pullback functor

takes values in

, and not in the bounded derived category. But of course
\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}[n-m]\langle k\rangle
is a bounded complex, so there is some integer

\Hom(\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}[m],\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\cong\Hom(\tau^{\geq N}\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\widetilde{\Delta}_{w_{0}\lambda},\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}[n-m]\langle k\rangle).

The right-hand side is now a

D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})
. The direct sum of these over all

is finite-dimensional by the previous paragraph.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\neq 0

(1)

n=k=0
, or

(2)

n>0

Before proving this lemma, we recall some facts about

from

]

Apply Lemma
2.20
to obtain a recollement diagram. We denote the functors involving the quotient

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{D}_{\leq\lambda}}

\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\nu})\cong\begin{cases}\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\langle-2\delta_{\nu}\rangle)&\text{if $\nu\in W\lambda$,}\\

We are now ready to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma

.

Let
\mathsf{M}(\lambda)
be the Weyl module for

with highest weight

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda)\in\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})

[

, Lemma 5.4]
, we have

(4.2)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda)\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\nu_{1}}*\cdots*\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\nu_{k}}*\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}

for some weights
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k}\in\mathbf{X}
that satisfy
\mathsf{dom}(\nu_{i})\leq\lambda
and
\nu_{i}\neq\lambda
for all
i
. In particular, this shows that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda)
lies in
\mathfrak{D}_{\leq\lambda}
, so it makes sense to apply
\Pi_{\lambda}
to it. Let

\mathcal{G}=\Pi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda))\langle-\delta^{*}_{\lambda}\rangle.

Combining (
4.2
) with (
4.1
), we find that
\mathcal{G}
belongs to a category of the form
\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{1}\rangle*\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{2}\rangle*\cdots*\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{k}\rangle*\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})
, where

are some positive integers. Thus, there is a distinguished triangle

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}\to\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})

with

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{1}\rangle*\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{2}\rangle*\cdots*\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})\langle-2r_{k}\rangle.

The proof of Lemma

shows that
\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}))\cong\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}
. We therefore have

\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda)\langle-\delta^{*}_{\lambda}\rangle,\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\\
\cong\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathsf{M}(\lambda)\langle-\delta^{*}_{\lambda}\rangle,\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}))[n]\langle k\rangle)\cong\Hom(\mathcal{G},\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})[n]\langle k\rangle)\\
\cong\Hom(\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{G}),\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}\Pi_{\lambda}(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda})[n]\langle k\rangle)\cong\Hom(\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{G}),\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle),

where the penultimate step uses the fact that

is fully faithful (
[

, (1.4.3.5)]

Let
\mathcal{F}=\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{G})
. Combining the calculation above with
[
A1
, Lemma 5.5(2)]
, we have

(4.3)

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\cong\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $n=k=0$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Next, let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
, so that we have a distinguished triangle

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}\to\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\to

where

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\langle-2r_{1}\rangle*\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\langle-2r_{2}\rangle*\cdots*\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\langle-2r_{k}\rangle.

This distinguished triangle gives rise to a long exact sequence

(4.5)

\cdots\to\Hom(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n-1]\langle k\rangle)\to\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\\
\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\to\cdots

Let us first take

to be the smallest integer such that
\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\neq 0
for some

. In view of (
4.4
), the first term in (
4.5
) vanishes. The second term is assumed to be nonzero, so the third term must be as well. But by (

), this implies that
n=k=0
. In particular, we have
\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)=0
if

.

Assume henceforth that

. We will prove by induction on

that if

. The case

has already been covered by the previous paragraph. Suppose now that

, and let

be the largest integer such that
\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\langle k\rangle)\neq 0
. (Such a

exists by Lemma

.) The third term of (

)), so in order for the second term to be nonzero, the first term must also by nonzero. By induction and (

), we must have

for some

. Since

, we conclude that

.
∎

We can now prove a complement to Lemma

.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}_{I})
or
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
, the space

\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\{k\})

is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

For
{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}_{I}
, the proof of Lemma
4.2
can be repeated verbatim. For
\mathcal{N}
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}=\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}[m]
and
\mathcal{G}=\overline{\nabla}_{\mu}[n]
. If
\lambda\neq\mu
, the reasoning in Lemma
4.2
applies again. If
\lambda=\mu
, then by Lemma
2.19
, we may replace
\mathcal{F}
by
\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[m]
. By Lemma
4.3
, the space

\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[m],\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n]\{k\})=\Hom(\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}[n-m+k]\langle-k\rangle)

vanishes unless

n-m+k=k=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad\begin{cases}n-m+k>0,\\
-k\leq-2n+2m-2k.\end{cases}

The latter condition is equivalent to
m-n<k\leq 2m-2n
. In particular, only finitely many integers

satisfy this condition, so our sum is finite-dimensional.
∎

Proposition 4.5

The set of all
\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}

, and its dual set consists of all

, and

(2)

graded co-quasi-exceptional with respect to

Proof.

As noted earlier, the fact that

By Lemma

, if the space

is nonzero, we either have

or

and

Theorem 4.7
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})_{\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{G}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})_{\leq 0}
, then
the natural homomorphism

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\rightarrow\Hom(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F},\pi_{*}\mathcal{G})

is surjective.

Proof.

We begin by defining two sets of objects
\widetilde{\Delta},\,\widetilde{\nabla}\subset D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\lambda}\{k\}[i]\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{X},\,i\leq 0,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{\nabla}_{\lambda}\{k\}[i]\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{X},\,i\geq 0,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We will now use the
\mathbin{\underline{*}}

with respect to
\{1\}
. The description of the co-

\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle=\text{direct summands of $\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}$,}

\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}})_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle=\text{direct summands of $\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\widetilde{\nabla}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}$.}

Thus, it suffices to verify the surjectivity of the morphism

(4.6)

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\rightarrow\Hom(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F},\pi_{*}\mathcal{G})

with
\mathcal{F}\in\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}n}
and
\mathcal{G}\in\widetilde{\nabla}^{\mathbin{\underline{*}}m}
, for some
n,m\geq 1
. We will proceed by double induction with respect to
n
and
m
.

First suppose that
n=m=1
and assume

\mathcal{F}=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\lambda}\{k_{1}\}[i_{1}]
and
\mathcal{G}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\{k_{2}\}[i_{2}]
with

,

,

and
i_{2}\geq 0
. Set
k=k_{2}-k_{1}
and

. We have

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})=\Hom(\widetilde{\Delta}_{\lambda},\widetilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\{k\}[i])=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $\lambda=\mu$, $i=k=0$},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that if
{\mathsf{dom}\lambda}\neq{\mathsf{dom}\mu}
, then both sides of (
4.6
) are zero, so the map is trivially surjective.
Now suppose that
{\mathsf{dom}\lambda}={\mathsf{dom}\mu}
, and let

. Using Lemma

and the fact that
\{\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}}
is co-quasi-exceptional, we have

\displaystyle\Hom(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F},\pi_{*}\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=\Hom(\overline{\Delta}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda}\langle\delta\rangle\{k\}[i])

\displaystyle=\Hom(\overline{\Delta}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda}\{k-\delta\}[i+\delta])

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $k=\delta$, $i=-\delta$},\\
0&\text{otherwise (since $i+\delta\geq 0$)}.\end{cases}

So it suffices to assume that
k=\delta=-i
because the natural map will again be trivially surjective otherwise.
But we have that both
i\geq 0
and
\delta=\delta^{*}_{\lambda}+\delta^{*}_{\mu}\geq 0
, and thus,

\delta=i=0
. In particular,

\delta^{*}_{\lambda}=\delta^{*}_{\mu}=0
which further implies that

. Hence,
(
4.6
) is given by

\Hom(\widetilde{\Delta}_{\lambda},\widetilde{\nabla}_{\lambda})\rightarrow\Hom(\overline{\Delta}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathsf{dom}\lambda}).

Any nonzero map

factors through

then implies that this map of

.

4.6
) is surjective for

. Let

and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime}\in\widetilde{\nabla}
. Applying

and

Again, there is a distinguished triangle

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
denote the silting subcategory of
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
.

(1)

For any
\mu\in\mathbf{X}
, the object
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
is either
0
or an indecomposable object of
\mathcal{S}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
. It is nonzero if and only if
\mu\in-\mathbf{X}^{+}
.

(2)

For
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
, set
\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}:=\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{w_{0}\lambda}
. Every indecomposable object of
\mathcal{S}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
is isomorphic to an object of the form

\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}\{r\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The vanishing of
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
for
\mu\notin-\mathbf{X}^{+}
was previously known: see, for instance, the comments in
[
AHR1
, §4.2]
.

Proof.

For any
\mu\in\mathbf{X}
, Corollary
4.6
tells us that
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
lies in
\mathcal{S}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
, and by Theorem
4.7
,
\pi_{*}
induces a surjective ring homomorphism

\End(\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu})\twoheadrightarrow\End(\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}).

Here,
\End(\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu})
is a local ring, since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
is an indecomopsable object in a Krull–Schmidt category. Its quotient ring
\End(\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu})
is therefore either
0
or a local ring. Since
\mathcal{S}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})
is also Krull–Schmidt, we conclude that
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
is either
0
or indecomposable.

Suppose now that
\mu\in-\mathbf{X}^{+}
, and let
\lambda=w_{0}\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
. According to Proposition
2.21
, the two compositions

\widetilde{\Delta}_{w_{0}\lambda}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{L}}_{w_{0}\lambda}\to\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\Delta}_{w_{0}\lambda}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{w_{0}\lambda}\to\widetilde{\nabla}_{w_{0}\lambda}

are equal (and nonzero). Now apply
\pi_{*}
to these maps. Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

(4.7)

\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}\to\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}\to\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{\Delta}_{\lambda}\to\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}\to\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}.

These compositions are again equal, and the first one is nonzero, so the second one is as well. In particular,
\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}\neq 0
.

Part (
2
) of the theorem now follows from the “abstract” classification of indecomposable silting objects given in Proposition
2.21
, together with the equality of the two compositions in (
4.7
).

To finish the proof of part (
1
), we must show that if
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}\neq 0
, then
\mu\in-\mathbf{X}^{+}
. By part (
2
), if
\pi_{*}\widetilde{\mathfrak{E}}_{\mu}
is nonzero, it is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}\{r\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
and some

\{[\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}]\}_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}.

Two other bases for
K\left(D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathcal{N})\right)
are

(7.5)

\{[\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}]\}_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{[\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}]\}_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}}.

Because the
\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}
’s are obtained from the the pre-exceptional set
\{\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}}
by a recollement construction, the transition matrix between these bases is “upper-triangular”: we have

[\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}]=[\overline{\nabla}_{\lambda}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{+}\\
\mu<\lambda\end{subarray}}a_{\lambda,\mu}(t)[\overline{\nabla}_{\mu}].

By the same reasoning, the transition matrix between (
7.3
) and the second basis in (
7.5
) is also upper-triangular. It follows that the transition matrix between (
7.3
) and the first basis in (
7.5
) is upper-triangular: we have

(7.6)

[\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}]=[\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{+}\\
\mu<\lambda\end{subarray}}b_{\lambda,\mu}(t)[\mathfrak{E}_{\mu}].

Now suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}_{\overline{C_{I}}}
. Then the object
\mathfrak{L}_{\lambda}
is supported on
\overline{C_{I}}
, so it lies in the span of (
7.4
). Since
[\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda}]
occurs with nonzero coefficient in (
7.6
), we conclude that
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
, and hence that
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=\mathbf{X}^{+}_{\overline{C}}
.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Conjecture
7.1
holds for
\mathbf{G}=GL_{N}(\Bbbk)
and

.

Proof.

Suppose
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
belongs to the lower closure of
w_{\lambda}\cdot_{p}\mathcal{C}_{p}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}_{C}
. The main result of
[
H
]
already tells us that the

has set-theoretic support equal to

Appendix A
Equivariant dg modules

Let
H
be an algebraic group over
\Bbbk
, and let
X=\Spec(\mathbf{A})
be an affine
H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-variety over
\Bbbk
. In other words,
\mathbf{A}
is a graded commutative finitely generated reduced
\Bbbk
-algebra equipped with a rational
H
-action. We assume throughout that the grading on the ring
\mathbf{A}
is concentrated in nonnegative degrees.

Let
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
be the abelian category of
H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant
\mathbf{A}
-modules, or, equivalently, of graded
H
-equivariant
\mathbf{A}
-modules. Each object
M\in\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
comes equipped with an “internal grading”
M=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{j}
, where each
M_{j}
is a rational
H
-module. For
M\in\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
, we set

M\langle 1\rangle=M\otimes\Bbbk_{-1},

so that the internal grading of
M\langle 1\rangle
is given by
(M\langle 1\rangle)_{j}=M_{j+1}
.

Given a (possibly infinite) collection
(M^{i})_{i\in I}
of rational
H
-modules, the product vector space
\prod_{i\in I}M^{i}
carries a (not necessarily rational) action of the abstract group
H(\Bbbk)
. A vector
v\in\prod_{i\in I}M^{i}
is said to be
rational
if it is contained in a finite-dimensional
H(\Bbbk)
-stable subspace on which
H
acts algebraically. The subspace consisting of rational vectors is denoted by

\sideset{}{{}^{\mathrm{rat}}}{\prod}_{i\in I}M^{i}\subset\prod_{i\in I}M^{i}.

This is a rational
H
-module. The same notion makes sense for
H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-modules. If the
M^{i}
are objects of
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
, then because the
H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathbf{A}
is rational, it is easy to see that
\prod^{\mathrm{rat}}_{i\in I}M^{i}
is an
\mathbf{A}
-submodule of
\prod_{i\in I}M^{i}
. Thus, in this context,
\prod^{\mathrm{rat}}_{i\in I}M^{i}
is again an object of
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}^{{\mathrm{fg}}}
be the subcategory consisting of those modules that are finitely generated over
\mathbf{A}
. Of course, we may identify

\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}=\mathsf{QCoh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}^{{\mathrm{fg}}}=\mathsf{Coh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X).

Next, let
C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
be the category of chain complexes
M=(\cdots\to M^{-1}\to M^{0}\to M^{1}\to\cdots)
that are “cohomologically doubly bounded below”: that is, the cohomology modules
H^{i}(M)\in\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
vanish for
i\ll 0
, and there is an integer
N
such that
H^{i}(M)_{j}=0
for all
j<N
and all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We do not impose any boundedness conditions on the underlying terms
M^{i}
. However, every chain complex in
C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
is quasi-isomorphic to one whose terms do satisfy such boundedness conditions. Denote the derived category of
C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
by

D^{++}\mathsf{QCoh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)

It is well known that the category
\mathsf{QCoh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)=\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
has enough injectives. (This follows from the fact that
\mathsf{QCoh}(X)
has enough injectives, and that the forgetful functor
\mathsf{QCoh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)\to\mathsf{QCoh}(X)
has an exact right adjoint, namely, the “averaging” functor.) Therefore, any complex in
C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded-below chain complex of injectives. (However, the internal grading of injective modules is usually
not
bounded below.)

Next, regard the graded ring
\mathbf{A}
as a dg ring with zero differential. Let
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}
denote the category of
H
-equivariant dg modules. Let
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+}\subset\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}
be the subcategory consisting of dg modules whose cohomology
H^{\bullet}(M)
is bounded below, and then let
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+,{\mathrm{fg}}}\subset\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+}
be the subcategory consisting of modules
M
for which
H^{\bullet}(M)
is finitely generated over
\mathbf{A}
. We denote by

D^{+,H}_{\mathsf{QCoh}}(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad D^{+,{\mathrm{fg}},H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)

the derived categories of
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+}
and
\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+,{\mathrm{fg}}}
, respectively.

We define

\xi:C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\to\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{dgmod}_{H}^{+}

to be the functor that sends a chain complex
M=(\cdots\to M^{-1}\to M^{0}\to M^{1}\to\cdots)
in
C^{++}\mathbf{A}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
to the dg module given by

\xi(M)^{n}=\sideset{}{{}^{\mathrm{rat}}}{\prod}_{i+j=n}M^{i}_{j}.

It is easy to see that

(A.1)

H^{n}(\xi(M))\cong\sideset{}{{}^{\mathrm{rat}}}{\prod}_{i+j=n}H^{i}(M)_{j}\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=n}H^{i}(M)_{j}.

(The “doubly bounded below” condition ensures that this direct sum is finite.) In particular,
\xi
sends acyclic complexes to acyclic complexes, so it induces functors

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle:D^{++}\mathsf{QCoh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)\to D^{+,H}_{\mathsf{QCoh}}(X),

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle:D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)\to D^{+,{\mathrm{fg}},H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X).

Lastly, we define

D^{H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)=\begin{array}[]{c}\text{the full triangulated subcategory of $D^{+,{\mathrm{fg}},H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)$ generated by}\\
\text{the essential image of $\xi:D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X)\to D^{+,{\mathrm{fg}},H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)$.}\end{array}

It seems likely that one has
D^{H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)=D^{+,{\mathrm{fg}},H}_{\mathsf{Coh}}(X)
in many cases of interest, but we will not try to prove this claim here.

Lemma A.1
.

(1)

Proof.

(

be a morphism of
H
-equivariant dg
\mathbf{A}
-modules. We must show that

is null-homotopic.

For each

is a dg module with zero differential. The underlying graded
\mathbf{A}
-module of

and its differential

Let

is a product of maps

f^{j}:M\to\xi(I^{j})[-j]\qquad\text{such that}\qquad d_{\xi(I)}^{j}f^{j}=f^{j+1}d_{M}.

Below, we will define a collection of maps

(A.2)

s^{j}:M\to\xi(I^{j})[-j-1]\qquad\text{such that}\qquad d_{\xi(I)}^{j-1}s^{j-1}+s^{j}d_{M}=f^{j}

for all

(
2

Moreover:

(1)

The functor
\Res_{K}^{H}
is left adjoint to
R\Ind_{K}^{H}
.

(2)

Let
d=\dim H-\dim K
. The functor
\Res_{K}^{H}({-})\otimes\bigwedge^{d}(\mathrm{Lie}(H)/\mathrm{Lie}(K))^{*}[d]
is right adjoint to
R\Ind_{K}^{H}
.

Proof.

The existence of functors
R\Ind_{K}^{H}
and
\Res_{K}^{H}
in the dg setting making these diagrams commute is straightforward (using
K
-injective resolutions for the former, and exactness for the latter). In the coherent sheaf setting, we noted above that
\Res_{K}^{H}
is left adjoint to
R\Ind_{K}^{H}
. In the dg setting, there is at least an obvious natural transformation
\epsilon:\Res_{K}^{H}R\Ind_{K}^{H}(M)\to M
for any
K
-injective object
M
. This natural transformation gives rise to a natural map

\Hom(N,R\Ind_{K}^{H}(M))\to\Hom(\Res_{K}^{H}(N),M).

This map is at least an isomorphism when
N
and
M
lie in the essential image of
\xi_{X}
(by the coherent sheaf version of the adjunction), so it is in fact an isomorphism for all
N
and
M
.

For the last assertion in the lemma, the dg version again follows from the coherent sheaf version. The coherent version is a well-known consequence of Serre–Grothendieck duality. Let us briefly explain how to obtain it. Let
p:H\times^{K}X\to X
be the map given by
p(h,x)=h\cdot x
. Then
p
is a smooth, projective bundle over
X
with fibers isomorphic to
H/K
. Serre–Grothendieck duality says that for
\mathcal{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{H}(H\times^{K}X)
and
\mathcal{G}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Coh}^{H}(X)
, there is a natural isomorphism

(A.3)

\Hom(\mathcal{F},p^{*}\mathcal{G}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}^{L}}\omega_{p}[d])\cong\Hom(p_{*}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}),

where
\omega_{p}
is the relative canonical bundle. (See
[
Ht
, Theorem III.11.1]
for the nonequivariant version of this statement, and see
[
AB
, Example 2.16]
for a discussion of how to deduce the equivariant version.) If we identify
\mathsf{Coh}^{H}(H\times^{K}X)
with
\mathsf{Coh}^{K}(X)
(cf. (
3.1
)), then
p_{*}
and
p^{*}
are identified with
R\Ind_{K}^{H}
and
\Res_{K}^{H}
, respectively, while
\omega_{p}
corresponds to the canonical bundle of
H/K
, i.e., the line bundle whose fiber over
1K\in K/H
is identified with
\bigwedge^{d}(\mathrm{Lie}(H)/\mathrm{Lie}(K))^{*}
. Thus, (
A.3
) yields the desired adjunction.
∎

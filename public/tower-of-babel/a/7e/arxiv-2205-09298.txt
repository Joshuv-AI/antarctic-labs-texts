Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov theorem for Calabi-Yau categories
Isamu Iwanari

Abstract




1.
Introduction

The theorem of Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov
[
5
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]

says that
deformations of a compact Calabi-Yau manifold are unobstructed.
Namely,
the local moduli space is smooth. This unobstructedness is described as
a certain abelian property of the differential graded Lie algebra consisting of
derived global sections of the tangent sheaf.
In this note we consider a generalization to Calabi-Yau categories.
For a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
over a field of characteristic zero
(or a pretriangulated differential graded category),
the deformation problem of
\mathcal{C}
is controlled by
the shifted Hochschild cochain complex
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}:=\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)[1]
, that
is a differential graded Lie algebra
[
26
, 5.3.16]

(see
loc. cit.
for the precise formulation in which it is formulated as a deformation problem on algebras over the little disks operad).
The purpose of this note is to prove a generalization of Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov theorem to Calabi-Yau categories (see Theorem
5.2

and Corollary
5.7
):

Theorem 1.1
.

let
k
be a field of characteristic zero.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
k
-linear proper Calabi-Yau stable
\infty
-category.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}
denote the shifted Hochschild cochain complex
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)[1]

that is a differential graded (dg)
Lie algebra. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}
is quasi-isomorphic to
an abelian dg Lie algebra, that is, a dg Lie algebra with the zero bracket.

We would like to take a brief look at a history
of work on Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov theorem related to the above theorem.
The above result was originally announced
in Katzarkov-Kontsevich-Pantev
[
20
, 4.4.1]
for
the case of a proper Calabi-Yau
differential graded (dg) algebra
A
. Unfortunately, the proof has not appeared
at the writing of this note.
In the unpublished preprint
[
19
]
the author gave the proof of the above result for a proper Calabi-Yau
dg algebra
A
.
Subsequently, Tu
[
32
]
proved a version of a

\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-graded finite-dimensional smooth cyclic dg (
A_{\infty}
) algebra under the assumption of the degeneration of
Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence in this situation.

Section
2

collects conventions and some of the notation that we will
use. We also review
some of background materials.
Sections
3
,
4
,
5
are devoted to the proof of
main results.
We may think that Section
3
is the first step,
Section
4
is the second step, and Section
5

consists of the completion of the proof.
We briefly mention some of the ingredients for the proof.
We will apply
a conceptual construction
[
17
]
of the algebraic
structure on the pair
of the Hochschild cohomology spectrum (Hochschild cochain complex in the dg setting)
and the Hochschild homology spectrum (Hochschild chain complex)

(\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k),\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k))

of a
k
-linear stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
(on a personal level, at the writing of
[
19
]
the author was dimly aware of ideas in the present arguments).
This algebraic structure is encoded by the Kontsevich-Soibelman operad.
Besides, we make use of the theory of factorization homology
[
1
]

and the degeneration of Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence
[
21
]
,
[
27
]

for a smooth and proper stable
\infty
-category.
Roughly speaking, our approach is modeled on the work of Iacono
and Manetti
[
16
]
, who give an algebraic proof of the classical Bogomolov-Tian-Todorov theorem.
Along with the above inputs,
observations on
categorical phenomena, which do not appear in the classical situation, such as Lemmas
3.4
and
3.7
,
Lemma
3.9
, and
Proposition
4.3
play an important role.
We also hope that the reader finds our method natural
at least compared to that in
[
19
]
. For example, the argument of the present note does not require a deep result of the formality
of the Kontsevich-Soibelman operad and complicated computations
of
L_{\infty}
-algebras needed in
[
19
]
.

2.1.

(\infty,1)
-categories.

We use

(\infty,1)

In several parts of this note one can work only with

feels that the theory of
(\infty,1)

We adopt the theory of

A quasi-category is a simplicial set which

Following

Our main references are
[

[

To an ordinary category, we can assign an

\infty

•

From model categories to
\infty
-categories.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall Lurie’s construction
by which one can obtain

\infty
-categories
from a category (more generally

\infty
-category) endowed with a prescribed collection of
morphisms (see
[
25
, 1.3.4, 4.1.7, 4.1.8]
for details).
It can be viewed as an alternative approach to Dwyer-Kan
hammock localization.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a category and let
W
be a collection
of morphisms in
\mathcal{D}
which is closed under composition
and contains all isomorphisms.
For
(\mathcal{D},W)
, there is an
\infty
-category
\operatorname{N}(\mathcal{D})[W^{-1}]

and a functor

\xi:\textup{N}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{N}(\mathcal{D})[W^{-1}]

such that for any
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
the composition
induces a fully faithful functor
\operatorname{Fun}(\operatorname{N}(\mathcal{D})[W^{-1}],\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{Fun}(\textup{N}(\mathcal{D}),\mathcal{C})

whose essential image consists of those functors
F:\textup{N}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{C}
such that
F
carry morphisms lying in
W

to equivalences in
\mathcal{C}
.
We shall refer to
\operatorname{N}(\mathcal{D})[W^{-1}]

as the
\infty
-category obtained from
\mathcal{D}

by inverting morphisms in
W
.

2.4.
Hochschild homology and Hochschild cohomology

We briefly recall Hochschild homology. We refer to
[
17
, Section 6]
for details.

The Hochschild homology (chain) complex functor is defined as a symmetric monoidal functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(-/k):\operatorname{\mathcal{S}t}_{k}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}_{k}^{\textup{SO}(2)}=\operatorname{Fun}(BS^{1},\operatorname{Mod}_{k})

which carries
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}t}_{k}
to the Hochschild homology complex (Hochchild chain complex)
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
.
Here the symmetric monoidal structure on
\operatorname{Fun}(BS^{1},\operatorname{Mod}_{k})

is induced by that of
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
(cf.
[
25
, 2.1.3.4]
).
We refer the reader to
[
17
, Section 6, 6.14]
for the construction
of
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(-/k)

(in
loc. cit.
we use the symbol
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
instead of
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
).
Let
B\in\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})
.
Let
\textup{LPerf}_{B}
be the smallest stable idempotent-complete subcategory of
\operatorname{LMod}_{B}

which contains
B
.
We will write
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(B/k)
for
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\textup{LPerf}_{B}/k)
.

Let
\Theta_{k}:\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\to\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}

the symmetric monoidal functor informally given by

(see e.g.
[
25
, 4.8.3]
for the construction or use the formalism of spectral categories).
Since
\Theta_{k}
is a symmetric monoidal functor,
it gives rise to
\theta_{k}:\operatorname{Alg}_{2}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\simeq\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\Theta_{k})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})
.
If
B
is an
\mathbf{E}_{2}
-algebra in
\operatorname{Mod}_{k}
, then
\operatorname{LMod}_{B}
has an associative monoidal
structure defined as
\theta_{k}(B)=\operatorname{LMod}_{B}^{\otimes}
.
Moreover,
\textup{LPerf}_{B}
is promoted to an object of
\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{\mathcal{S}t}_{k})
.
Thus, the symmetric monoidal functor
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(-/k)
gives
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(B/k)

which belongs to
\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Mod}^{\textup{SO}(2)}_{k})
.

We review the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}t}_{k}
.
Let
\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
be the Ind-category that belongs to

\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}))\in\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})

be the endomorphism algebra object of
\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})

which is endowed with a tautological action on
\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
(see
[
25
, 4.7.1]
for the formulation).
By
[
25
, 4.8.5.11, 4.8.5.16]
there exists an adjoint pair

\textstyle{\theta_{k}:\operatorname{Alg}_{2}(\operatorname{Mod}_{k})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k}):E_{k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

such that
E_{k}
sends
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
to the endomorphism algebra of the unit object
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}
where we think of an object of

\operatorname{Alg}_{1}(\operatorname{Pr}^{\textup{L}}_{k})
as an associative monoidal
k
-linear
\infty
-category.
The Hochschild cohomology complex (Hochschild cochain complex)
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)

is defined to be
E_{k}(\mathcal{E}nd_{k}(\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})))
.
We refer to
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
as the Hochschild cohomology complex of
\mathcal{C}
(over
k
).
Note that
the counit map

\operatorname{LMod}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)}^{\otimes}
on
\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})

Though we use the word “homology” and “cohomology”,

\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)

are not graded modules obtained by passing to (co)homology but spectra (or chain complexes) with algebraic structures.
Passing to homology of
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)

we have graded vector spaces which we denote by

and

We refer to
HH_{*}(\mathcal{C}/k)
and
HH^{*}(\mathcal{C}/k)

as Hochschild homology and Hochshchild cohomology, respectively.

D_{\mathcal{C}}:\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)[n]\to\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)

is equivalent to the morphism
d_{\mathcal{C}}:\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)[n]\to\operatorname{\mathcal{HH}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}/k)
.
In particular,
D_{\mathcal{C}}
is an equivalence if and only if
d_{\mathcal{C}}
is an equivalence.

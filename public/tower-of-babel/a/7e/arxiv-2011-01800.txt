1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the isometry groups of manifolds
\overline{M}_{p}
,
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, which are closed

(4n+1)
-dimensional contact manifolds with closed Reeb orbits.
Equivalently,
\overline{M}_{p}
is a circle bundle over a closed
4n
-dimensional integral
symplectic manifolds
M

[
1
, Thm. 3.9]
. We use Wodzicki-Chern-Simons (WCS) forms on the loop space
L\overline{M}_{p}
to determine that

\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}_{p}))
is infinite for
|p|\gg 0.
This extends results for circle bundles over Kähler surfaces in
[
14
]
(as corrected in
[
12
]
) to symplectic manifolds in arbitrarily high dimensions.

In general, the isometry group
{\rm Isom}(\overline{M})
of a closed manifold
\overline{M}
is well-known to be a compact Lie group. It follows that
{\rm Isom}(\overline{M})
is isomorphic to

(T^{k}\ltimes G)/F
, where
T^{k}
is the
k
-torus,
G
is a semisimple Lie group, and
F
is a finite group
[
2
, Thm. 6.9]
.
Since
\pi_{1}(G)
is finite,
\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M})):=\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}),{\rm Id})

is infinite iff
k\geq 1.

However, it seems difficult in general to determine
k
.

Suppose
\overline{M}
admits a nontrivial circle action
a:S^{1}\times\overline{M}\longrightarrow\overline{M}
via isometries. This gives a loop
a^{I}:S^{1}\longrightarrow{\rm Isom}(\overline{M})
of isometries and hence an element of

\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M})).
If the circle action is free,
\overline{M}
is the total space of a circle bundle over the orbit space
M
, with the action given by rotation of the circle fibers. It is natural to conjecture that
the class
[a^{I}]\in\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}))
has infinite order. This is not always true: for the canonical bundle
\overline{M}=S^{2n+1}
over
M={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
,
the fiber rotation is an isometry of the standard metric on
S^{2n+1}
.
In fact,
[a^{I}]
is the generator of
\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(S^{2n+1}))=\pi_{1}(SO(2n+2))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

In this example, the first Chern number of the canonical bundle is
1
.
The main result is that for sufficiently high Chern number, rotation in the circle fiber gives an element of infinite order in
\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M})).
More precisely:

Theorem
3.4
:

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed integral symplectic manifold of dimension
4n.
For
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
\overline{M}_{p}
be the circle bundle over
M
with first Chern class
p[\omega]
.Then for
|p|\gg 0
,

\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}_{p}))
is
infinite. Equivalently, let
\overline{M}
be a closed
(4n+1)
-dimensional contact manifold with closed Reeb orbits. Then
\overline{M}
covers infinitely many such contact manifolds
\overline{M}_{p}
with

\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}_{p}))
infinite.

In the concrete example of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, we proved in
[
14
]
that
\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}_{p}))
is infinite for

p\neq\pm 1.
In fact, the only example we know where
a^{I}
does not have infinite order in
\pi_{1}({\rm Isom}(\overline{M}_{p}))
is for
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}.

In §2 we give background material on pseudodifferential operators and WCS classes on
loop spaces. In §3 we prove the main result, both by direct calculation and computer verification. In §4, we apply our theory to the first example of a symplectic, non-Kähler manifold, due to Kodaira-Thurston. Through explicit calculations, we get the results in Theorem 3.4 for all
p
.

In §5,
we relate Pontryagin forms on
M
to WCS forms on

\overline{M_{p}}
in the Kähler case (Proposition
5.2
). Using this Proposition, we prove that a specific
Wodzicki-Pontryagin form on the free loop space
L\mathcal{L}_{p}
of
5
-dimensional lens spaces is nonvanishing (Theorem
5.3
). Surprisingly, these forms are in dimension
6
, where there are no Pontryagin forms in finite dimensions. These forms were predicted to vanish in
[
11
]
, and aside from a low dimensional example in
[
10
]
are the first examples of nonvanishing Wodzicki characteristic forms.

Appendices A, B, C and D give proofs of technical results. In Appendix E,
we discuss why symplectic manifolds of dimension
4n+2
are more difficult to treat. The online files
[
3
,
4
]
include a particularly long calculation for the Thurston example and computer codes verifying the main results.

2.1.
Finite dimensional background material

The complexified tangent bundle
of a Riemannian manifold
(M^{4n},g)
has Chern character

ch(M)\in H^{ev}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
with
2k
-component

ch_{[2k]}(M)=\frac{1}{k!(2\pi)^{k}}[\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega^{k})]\in H^{2k}(M,{\mathbb{R}}),

(2.1)

where
\Omega=\Omega_{M}
is the curvature form of
g
. There are associated Pontryagin-type forms

\tilde{p}_{k}(\Omega)=(-1)^{k}/[(2k)!(2\pi)^{2k}]\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega^{2k})
and classes

\tilde{p}_{k}(M)=[\tilde{p}_{k}(\Omega)]=(-1)^{k}ch_{[2k]}(M)\in H^{4k}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}).

The usual Pontryagin classes
p_{k}(M):=(-1)^{k}c_{2k}(M)
are built from the even Chern classes
c_{2k}(M)
.
By invariant theory for
SO(n)
, the rings generated by
\{\tilde{p}_{2k}\}
and

\{p_{2k}\}
are the same; this reduces to Newton’s identities relating the elementary symmetric functions in
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
to
\sum\lambda_{1},\ldots,\sum\lambda_{i}^{n}

[
15
, §16]
.

As part of Chern-Weil theory, for connections
\nabla^{0},\nabla^{1}
on
TM

with curvature forms
\Omega^{0},\Omega^{1}
, the Chern-Simons form

\widetilde{CS}_{4k-1}(\nabla^{0},\nabla^{1})\in\Lambda^{4k-1}(M)
,

\widetilde{CS}_{4k-1}(\nabla^{0},\nabla^{1})=2k\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{Tr}((\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})\wedge\overbrace{\Omega_{t}\wedge\ldots\wedge\Omega_{t}}^{2k-1})\ dt,

satisfies

d\widetilde{CS}_{4k-1}(\nabla^{0},\nabla^{1})=\tilde{p}_{k}(\Omega^{0})-\tilde{p}_{k}(\Omega^{1}).

(2.2)

Here
\omega_{t}=t\omega_{0}+(1-t)\omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{t}=d\omega_{t}+\omega_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}
.
Our sign convention is
\Omega(\partial_{k},\partial_{j})^{a}_{b}=g(R(\partial_{k},\partial_{j})\partial_{b},\partial_{a}),
where
R
is the curvature tensor of
g
.

Infinite dimensional background material

This material is taken from
[
13
,
14

. Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. For fixed

\langle X,Y\rangle_{0}=\int_{S^{1}}g(X_{t},Y_{t})_{\gamma(\theta)}dt,\ \ \langle X,Y\rangle_{1}=\int_{S^{1}}g((1+\Delta)X_{t},Y_{t})_{\gamma(\theta)}dt.

Here

In contrast to finite dimensions, there are two natural traces on

For example, if

11

Since these classes vanish, we focus on the associated Wodzicki-Chern-Simons (WCS) forms

By

\displaystyle CS^{W}_{2k-1}(g)(X_{1},...,X_{2k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2^{k-2}}\sum_{\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\int_{S^{1}}\operatorname{tr}[(R(X_{\sigma(1)},\cdot)\dot{\gamma})(\Omega^{M})^{k-1}(X_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(2k-1)})],

The analog of (

3.
WCS forms for circle bundles over symplectic manifolds

In §3.1, we prove the main result Theorem
3.4
. We first discuss the Riemannian geometry of circle bundles
\overline{M}_{p}
,
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, over symplectic manifolds
(M,\omega)
, where
c_{1}(\overline{M}_{p})=(2\pi)^{-1}[p\omega]
. We compute the curvature as a function of
p
. Using the curvature calculations, we prove that the WCS class on
L\overline{M}_{p}
is a polynomial in
p^{2}
with nonzero top coefficient. As we explain, this proves the Theorem. In §3.2, we discuss computer calculations that verify our calculations.

3.1.
Geometry of line bundles over integral symplectic manifolds

(M,\omega)

5.
The Kähler case

In this section, we prove that the lowest order term in the WCS form has
a geometric/topological interpretation on
Kähler manifolds (Prop.
5.2
); this appears to fail for general symplectic manifolds. We use this result to give non-vanishing results for a type of Wodzicki-Pontryagin form in dimension
4k+2
on loop spaces (Thm.
5.3
).
This is an infinite dimensional phenomenon, as the finite dimensional version of these forms vanish.
As noted in (
2.4
), the Wodzicki-Pontryagin classes vanish in

H^{4k+2}(LM)
; this non-vanishing of the representative forms gives the first known examples in arbitrarily high dimensions.

We start with a result about the real cohomology of loop spaces. We do not use this result later, but we think it is of general interest.

For a manifold
N
with an
S^{1}
action, deflne the ring homomorphism
L:\Lambda^{*}(N)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{*}(LN),\delta\mapsto\delta_{L}
by

\delta_{L}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,X_{k}(0)).

Then

a^{L,*}\circ L={\rm Id}.

(5.1)

To see this, take
v\in T_{p}N
and a curve
\gamma(s)
tangent to
v
at
p
. Then
a_{L,*}(v)=(d/ds)|_{s=0}a_{L}(\gamma(s))
, a vector field along the loop
a_{L}(p)(t).
Since
a_{L}(\gamma(s)(0)=\gamma(s)
, we get
a_{L,*}(v)(t=0)=v
. Thus

\displaystyle a^{L,*}\delta_{L}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})

\displaystyle=\delta_{L}(a_{L,*}X_{1},\ldots,a_{L,*}(X_{r}))=\delta[(a_{L,*}X_{1}](0),\ldots,[a_{L,*}(X_{r})](0))

\displaystyle=\delta(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}).

Lemma 5.1
.

L
induces an injection
L^{*}:H^{k}(N,{\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow H^{k}(LN,{\mathbb{R}})
for all
k
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle[(d_{LN}\circ L)\delta](X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i-1}X_{i}(\delta_{L}(X_{1},\ldots,\hat{X}_{i},\ldots X_{k+1}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\leq j}(-1)^{i+j}\delta_{L}([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{1},\ldots,\hat{X}_{i},\ldots,\hat{X}^{j},\ldots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i-1}X_{i}(\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots X_{k+1}(0)))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\leq j}(-1)^{i+j}\delta([X_{i},X_{j}](0),X_{1}(0)\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}^{j}(0),\ldots,X_{k+1}(0)),

\displaystyle[(L\circ d_{N})\delta](X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1})=d_{N}\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,X_{k+1}(0))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i-1}X_{i}(0)(\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots X_{k+1}(0)))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\leq j}(-1)^{i+j}\delta([X_{i},X_{j}](0),X_{1}(0)\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}^{j}(0),\ldots,X_{k+1}(0)).

Let
\gamma_{s}(t)
be a family of loops with tangent vector
X_{i}\in T_{\gamma_{0}}LN.
Extend the
X_{j}
to vector fields near
\gamma=\gamma_{0}.
Then

\displaystyle X_{i}(\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots X_{k+1}(0))

\displaystyle=(d/ds|_{s=0})\delta_{\gamma(s)(0)}(X_{1}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots X_{k+1}(0))

\displaystyle=X_{i}(0)(\delta(X_{1}(0),\ldots,\hat{X}_{i}(0),\ldots X_{k+1}(0)).

It follows that
d_{LN}\circ L=L\circ d_{N}
, so

L:\Lambda^{*}(N)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{*}(LN)
induces
L^{*}:H^{*}(N)\longrightarrow H^{*}(LN).
Then
a^{L,*}L^{*}=Id
implies

L^{*}:H^{*}(N)\longrightarrow H^{*}(LN)
is injective.
∎

In contrast to this general cohomological
result, our goal is to obtain
information on the Wodizicki-Pontryagin forms from the characteristic cohomology ring of
TM
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be an integral Kähler manifold of real dimension
4n
.
The Kähler form
\omega\in H_{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
determines the Riemannian metric
g(X,Y)=-\omega(JX,Y)
, where
J
is the complex structure. The key feature of the Kähler case for us is that
\nabla J=0
. Thus in Lemma
3.3
, the terms (ii) and (iv) vanish.

By (
2.3
) and Lemma
3.3
, the WCS forms

on

and their pullbacks

to

are polynomials in

:

\displaystyle\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1}(\overline{M}_{p},\overline{g}_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1,2i}\ p^{2i}\in\Lambda^{2k-1}(L\overline{M}_{p}),

(5.2)

\displaystyle a^{L,*}\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1}

\displaystyle=a^{L,*}\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1}(\overline{M}_{p},\overline{g}_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a^{L,*}\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1,2i}\ p^{2i}\in\Lambda^{2k-1}(\overline{M}_{p}),

where the forms

are curvature expressions independent of
p
. Indeed,

\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1,2i}
involves an integration of an
S^{1}
-invariant,

-independent curvature expression over the
S^{1}
-fiber in
\overline{M}_{p}
. Since

involves
\overline{\eta}=\overline{\eta}_{p}

\overline{\xi}=\overline{\xi}_{p}

\overline{\eta}_{p}(\overline{\xi}_{p})=1

each fiber has

independent of

. Thus
\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1,2i}
is independent of

We can explicitly compute the relevant part of
\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{2k-1,2}
used in the computations below.
Let
\Omega=\Omega_{M}
be the curvature of the Kähler metric.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\pi:\overline{M}_{p}\longrightarrow M
be the fibration. For
\overline{\xi}
the unit tangent vector to the fibers of

\pi
,

\displaystyle\imath_{\overline{\xi}}a^{L,*}\widetilde{CS}^{W}_{4k+1,2}

\displaystyle=(2k+1)2\cdot\pi^{*}\operatorname{tr}(\Omega_{M}^{2k})=(-1)^{k}(4k+2)(2\pi)^{2k+1}(2k)!\cdot\pi^{*}\tilde{p}_{k}(\Omega_{M}).

Thus the Pontryagin-type form

is related to

The proof is in
Appendix
D
.

There are no finite dimensional Pontryagin forms in dimensions

\Omega^{2k+1}
is skew-symmetric and hence

\text{Tr}(\Omega^{2k+1})=0
in finite dimensions. For the

Definition 5.1
.

The Wodzicki-Pontryagin form
\tilde{p}^{W}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\in\Lambda^{4k+2}(L\overline{M}_{p})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\overline{R}(\sigma(4k-2),\sigma(4k-1),\ell_{4k},\ell_{4k-1})\overline{R}(\sigma(4k),\sigma(4k+1),r,\ell_{4k}).

) of order

(\alpha)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2k+1}{2^{2k-1}}\cdot p^{2}

For dim

We only do the case where dim

Proof.

In the notation of (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle S_{7,8}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{0}=0}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\overline{R}_{0\ell_{1}0}{}^{n}\overline{R}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ell_{2}}{}^{\ell_{1}}\overline{R}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}\ell_{3}}{}^{\ell_{2}}\overline{R}_{\sigma_{5}\sigma_{6}n}{}^{\ell_{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{0}=0}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)(p^{2}\delta_{a_{1}}^{b})\cdot p^{2}[-J_{\sigma_{1}}^{a_{1}}J_{\sigma_{2}a_{2}}+J_{\sigma_{1}a_{2}}J_{\sigma_{2}}^{a_{1}}+2J_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}J_{a_{2}}^{a_{1}}]

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot p^{2}[-J_{\sigma_{3}}^{a_{2}}J_{\sigma_{4}a_{3}}+J_{\sigma_{3}a_{3}}J_{\sigma_{4}}^{a_{2}}+2J_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}J_{a_{3}}^{a_{2}}]\cdot p^{2}[-J_{\sigma_{5}}^{a_{3}}J_{\sigma_{6}b}+J_{\sigma_{5}b}J_{\sigma_{6}}^{a_{3}}+2J_{\sigma_{5}\sigma_{6}}J_{b}^{a_{3}}]

(E.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\cdot[-J_{\sigma_{3}}^{a_{2}}J_{\sigma_{4}a_{3}}+J_{\sigma_{3}a_{3}}J_{\sigma_{4}}^{a_{2}}+2J_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}J_{a_{3}}^{a_{2}}]\cdot[-J_{\sigma_{5}}^{a_{3}}J_{\sigma_{6}a_{1}}+J_{\sigma_{5}a_{1}}J_{\sigma_{6}}^{a_{3}}+2J_{\sigma_{5}\sigma_{6}}J_{a_{1}}^{a_{3}}].

Because
J_{ab}J^{b}_{c}=J^{d}_{b}g_{da}J^{b}_{c}=-\delta^{d}_{b}g_{da}=-g_{ba}
, the product of the first
two expressions in square brackets simplifies to

\displaystyle S_{7,8}

\displaystyle=p^{8}\sum_{\sigma_{0}=0}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)[-2J_{\sigma_{1}}^{a_{1}}J_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}g_{\sigma_{2}a_{3}}+2g_{\sigma_{1}a_{3}}J_{\sigma_{2}}^{a_{1}}J_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}-2J_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(-\delta_{\sigma_{3}}^{a_{1}})J_{\sigma_{4}a_{3}}

Large Deviations of Kac’s Conservative Particle System and Energy Non-Conserving Solutions to the Boltzmann Equation: A Counterexample to the Predicted Rate Function T1
[

Abstract
We consider the dynamic large deviation behaviour of Kac’s collisional process for a range of initial conditions including equilibrium. We prove an upper bound with a rate function of the type which has previously been found for kinetic large deviation problems, and a matching lower bound restricted to a class of sufficiently good paths. However, we are able to show by an explicit counterexample that the predicted rate function does not extend to a global lower bound: even though the particle system almost surely conserves energy, large deviation behaviour includes solutions to the Boltzmann equation which do not conserve energy, as found by Lu and Wennberg, and these occur strictly more rarely than predicted by the proposed rate function. At the level of the particle system, this occurs because a macroscopic proportion of energy can concentrate in 𝔬 ⁡ ( N ) \mathfrak{o}(N) particles with probability e − 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) e^{-\mathcal{O}(N)} .

We mention some other aspects of the Boltzmann/Kac dynamics which are related to the current work.

\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{t}\rangle
is constant in time, so that
\mu^{N}_{t}\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{M}}
if, and only if,
\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}
. Therefore, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mu^{N}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{M}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mu^{N}_{0}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)\leq e^{-MN}

and the first item follows. For the second item, we observe that the instantaneous rate of the Kac process, in either the hard spheres or Maxwell molecules case, is bounded by

\begin{split}N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma&\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(3+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star})\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq 3N(1+\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle)\end{split}

(41)

where we note that, for either kernel,
B(|v-v_{\star}|)\leq 1+|v|+|v_{\star}|\leq 3+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2}
, and in the final inequality, we recall again that the second moment
\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{t}\rangle
is independent of time. It therefore follows that we can construct a time-homogenous Poisson process
\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t}
, of constant, random rate
3N(1+\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle)
, such that
\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t}
has jumps on a superset of the times when
\mu^{N}_{t}
jumps. This leads to the bound, for any
s\leq t
,

(w^{N}_{t}-w^{N}_{s})([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\leq\frac{1}{N}(\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t}-\widetilde{w}^{N}_{s}).

For each
\delta
, we now pick a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}...<t_{m}
of size
\lceil T/\delta\rceil
by taking constant steps of size
\delta
. We now observe that
W(\mu^{N}_{t},\mu^{N}_{t-})\leq 4/N
at times when
\mu^{N}_{t}
jumps, and for any
|s-t|\leq\delta
, the interval
[s,t]
is contained in at most two adjacent intervals
[t_{i-1},t_{i+1}]
. Together, we conclude that

q^{N}(\delta)\leq 8\max_{i}\frac{1}{N}(\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i-1}}).

(42)

For
\lambda_{M}
as above and for any
z>0
, we bound

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}(\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i-1}})>\frac{\epsilon}{8}\hskip 2.84544pt\bigg|\hskip 2.84544pt\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)&\leq e^{-zN\epsilon/8}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{z(\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i-1}})}\hskip 2.84544pt\bigg|\hskip 2.84544pt\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right]\\
&\leq\exp\left(-N\left(\frac{z\epsilon}{8}-3(1+\lambda_{M})\delta(e^{z}-1)\right)\right)\end{split}

(43)

where, in the second line, we use the bound that the rate of
\widetilde{w}^{N}
is at most
3N(1+\lambda_{M})
if
\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}
. We now choose
z=8(M+1)/\epsilon
, and
\delta>0
small enough, depending on
z,\lambda_{M}
, so that
3(1+\lambda_{M})\delta(e^{z}-1)<1
. For this choice of
\delta
, the final expression in (
43
) is
e^{-NM}
, for each interval. Finally, we take a union bound:

\{q^{N}(\delta)>\epsilon\}\subset\{\mu^{N}_{0}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\}\cup\bigcup_{i\leq\lceil T/\delta\rceil}\left\{\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i}}-\widetilde{w}^{N}_{t_{i-1}}>\frac{N\epsilon}{8},\hskip 2.84544pt\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right\}.

By the choices of
\lambda_{M}
and
\delta
,

\mathbb{P}\left(q^{N}(\delta)>\epsilon\right)\leq(1+\lceil T/\delta\rceil)e^{-NM}

and the second item now follows. The final item follows in exactly the same way: following (
43
), for all
C>0
we bound

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(w^{N}(E)>C\big|\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{w}^{N}_{T}>CN\big|\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq e^{-CN}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\widetilde{w}^{N}_{T}}\big|\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right]\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\exp\bigg(-N\big(C-3(1+\lambda_{M})T(e-1)\big)\bigg)\end{split}

(44)

and choosing
C=M+3(1+\lambda_{M})T(e-1)
makes the final probability at most
e^{-MN}
. Using a union bound,

\mathbb{P}\left(w^{N}(E)>C\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(w^{N}(E)>C\big|\mu^{N}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mu^{N}_{0}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\lambda_{M}}\right)\leq 2e^{-MN}

(45)

from which (
40
) follows. ∎

Let us also record, for later use, the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{N}\ll\mathbb{P}
be changes of measure such that

\lim_{a\to\infty}\liminf_{N}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}\leq e^{Na}\right)=1.

(46)

Then the laws of
(\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})
under
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
are tight: for all
\epsilon>0
there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
such that

\sup_{N}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)<\epsilon.

(47)

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
1.1
i) and the hypothesis (
46
) by purely general considerations. Let us fix
\epsilon>0
; thanks to (
46
) we can choose
a
such that, for all but finitely many
N
,

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}>e^{Na}\right)<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

(48)

and, changing
a
if necessary, we can arrange that (
48
) holds for all
N
. We now choose
M=a-\log(\epsilon/2)
; by Proposition
1.1
i), there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}
such that

\limsup_{N}N^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K})<-M

(49)

which implies that, for all
N
sufficiently large,

\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)\leq e^{-MN}.

(50)

Since the space
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
is a separable metric space, each
(\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})
is tight so
\mathcal{K}
can be replaced with a larger compact set such that (
50
) again holds for all
N
. Together, (
48
,
50
) imply that

\begin{split}\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)&\leq\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}\leq e^{Na},(\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)+\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}>e^{Na}\right)\\[4.30554pt]
&<e^{Na}\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq e^{Na}e^{-N(a+\log(\epsilon/2))}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\epsilon\end{split}

(51)

and we are done. ∎

2.2
Variational Formulation of the Rate Function

In preparation for the upper bound, we will now present a variational formulation of the rate function. This will also allow us to prove the lower semicontinuity in Proposition
1.1
. We are aided in this equivalence by the inclusion of the flux in the large deviation principle: the choice of
K
, if it exists, is unique, which allows us to significantly simplify the proof of Léonard
[
33
]
.

We begin with the following construction. Let us write
C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for those functions
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
which are bounded and Lipschitz in the
v
-variable, with a bounded and
v
-Lipschitz time derivative
\partial_{t}f
, and such that
f_{0}=0
. For
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\in C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in C_{c}(E)
and
t\in[0,T]
, we define

\Xi(\mu_{\bullet},w,\varphi,f,g)_{t}=\Xi_{0}(\mu_{\bullet},\varphi)+\Xi_{1,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)+\Xi_{2,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)

(52)

where

\Xi_{0}(\mu_{\bullet},\varphi)=\langle\varphi,\mu_{0}\rangle-\log\langle e^{\varphi},\mu^{\star}_{0}\rangle;

(53)

\begin{split}\Xi_{1,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f):&=\langle f_{t},\mu_{t}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{s}\rangle ds\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{(0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Delta f(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\end{split}

(54)

and

\begin{split}\Xi_{2,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)&:=\int_{E}1(s\leq t)(g(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{E}(e^{g}-1)(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)).\end{split}

(55)

We write
\Xi(\mu_{\bullet},w,\varphi,f,g)
for the terminal value
\Xi(\mu_{\bullet},w,\varphi,f,g)=\Xi(\mu_{\bullet},w,\varphi,f,g)_{T}
. Let us note that these processes make sense at the level of the particle system
\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N}
. The function
f
here entering into
\Xi_{1,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)
will play the rôle of a Lagrange multiplier to enforce the constraint of the continuity equation. This is made precise by the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix
(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
. Then

\sup\left\{\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f):f\in C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }(\mu_{\bullet},w)\text{ solves (\ref {eq: CE})};\\
\infty&\text{else.}\end{cases}

(56)

Proof.

For the case where
(\mu_{\bullet},w)
solves (
CE
), we will show that, for all
f\in C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
t\in[0,T]
, we have the time-dependent equivalent of (
CE
):

\langle f,\mu_{t}\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{s}\rangle ds+\int_{E}1(s\leq t)\Delta f(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).

(57)

This will immediately imply that
\Xi_{1,t}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)=0
for all
f
and all
t
, which implies the claim. The proof of this formulation is slightly complicated by the lack of regularity, since we only assume
a priori
that
\mu_{\bullet}
is càdlàg rather than continuous; we will instead use the facts about càdlàg paths from Proposition
A.1
. Since (
57
) is linear in
f
, we can assume that
f_{t},\partial_{t}f_{t}
belong to the class
\mathcal{F}
which are
1
-bounded and
1
-Lipschitz. Fix
t\in[0,T]
,
\epsilon>0
,
\delta>0
.

Let us write
L\subset[0,T]
for those
s\in[0,T]
with
W(\mu_{s-},\mu_{s})\geq\epsilon
; thanks to Proposition
A.1
a),
L
is finite, and we write
m=|L|<\infty
for its cardinality. Possibly making
\delta>0
smaller, Proposition
A.1
b) guarantees that
\delta
can be chosen so that any interval
[u,v)
of length
\delta
either contains a point of
L
, or for all
s\in[u,v)
,
W(\mu_{s},\mu_{u})<\epsilon
. Now, for such
\delta
, we decompose
(0,t]
into intervals
(t_{i},t_{i+1}]
of length at most
\delta
, and add

\langle f_{t_{i+1}},\mu_{t_{i+1}}-\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle=\int_{E}1(s\in(t_{i},t_{i+1}])\Delta f(t_{i+1},v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma);

(58)

\langle f_{t_{i+1}}-f_{t_{i}},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle ds

(59)

to obtain

\begin{split}\langle f_{t_{i+1}},\mu_{t_{i+1}}\rangle-\langle f_{t_{i}},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle&=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle ds\\
&+\int_{E}1(s\in(t_{i},t_{i+1}])\Delta f(t_{i+1},v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).\end{split}

(60)

We approximate the two terms by

\begin{split}\left|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle ds-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{s}\rangle ds\right|&\leq\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}W(\mu_{s},\mu_{t_{i}})ds\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\epsilon(t_{i+1}-t_{i})+2\delta\cdot 1(L\cap[t_{i},t_{i+1})\neq\emptyset)\end{split}

(61)

since, by the choice of
\delta
, either
W(\mu_{s},\mu_{t_{i}})\leq\epsilon
for all
s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, or there is a point of
L
in
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
, in which case we use the trivial bound
W(\mu_{s},\mu_{t_{i}})\leq 2
and recall that the interval is of length at most
\delta
. For the second term

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{E}1(s\in(t_{i},t_{i+1}])\left(\Delta f(t_{i+1},v,v_{\star},\sigma)-\Delta f(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\right)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\right|\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left|\int_{E}1(s\in(t_{i},t_{i+1}])4\|f_{t_{i+1}}-f_{s}\|_{\infty}w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\right|\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 113.81102pt\leq 4\delta w\left((t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right)\end{split}

(62)

where in the final line we recall that we have scaled so that
\|\partial_{t}f_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Adding, we conclude that

\begin{split}&\bigg|\langle f_{t_{i+1}},\mu_{t_{i+1}}\rangle-\langle f_{t_{i}},\mu_{t_{i}}\rangle-\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{s}\rangle\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt-\int_{E}1(s\in(t_{i},t_{i+1}])\Delta f(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\bigg|\\
&\leq\epsilon(t_{i+1}-t_{i})+2\delta\cdot 1\left(L\cap[t_{i},t_{i+1})\neq\emptyset\right)+4\delta w\left((t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right).\end{split}

(63)

Summing over all such intervals
(t_{i},t_{i+1}]
covering
(0,t]
, and recalling that
f_{0}\equiv 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\langle f_{t},\mu_{t}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}f_{s},\mu_{s}\rangle ds-\int_{E}1(s\leq t)\Delta f(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\right|\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\epsilon t+2m\delta+4\delta w(E)\end{split}

(64)

and the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily small by taking
\epsilon,\delta\to 0
, recalling that
w
is a finite measure by hypothesis, so the claim (
57
) is proven, and we conclude that
\sup_{f}\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)=0
as claimed. Otherwise, if (
CE
) fails, there exists some
t_{0}\in(0,T]
and some
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\langle g,\mu_{t_{0}}\rangle-\langle g,\mu_{0}\rangle-\int_{E}1(s\leq t_{0})\Delta g(v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)>1.

(65)

Let us assume that
t_{0}\in(0,T)
; the proof
t_{0}=T
is similar and strictly easier. We now fix a smooth, increasing function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\chi=0
on
(-\infty,0]
and
\chi=1
on
[1,\infty)
, and for
0<\delta<\min(t_{0},T-t_{0})
, we construct
f^{\delta}\in C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
by defining

f^{\delta}_{t}(v):=\begin{cases}\chi(t/\delta)g(v)&\text{if }t\in[0,\delta];\\
g(v)&\text{if }\delta<t\leq t_{0};\\
\chi(1-(t-t_{0})/\delta)g(v)&\text{if }t_{0}<t<t_{0}+\delta\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(66)

Thanks to right-continuity of
\mu_{t}
, we observe that
\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}f^{\delta}_{t},\mu_{t}\rangle dt\to\langle g,\mu_{0}\rangle-\langle g,\mu_{t_{0}}\rangle
, and using dominated convergence,

\int_{E}\Delta f^{\delta}(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\to\int_{E}1(s\leq t_{0})\Delta g(v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).

(67)

Therefore,
\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f^{\delta})
converges to

\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f^{\delta})\to\langle g,\mu_{t_{0}}\rangle-\langle g,\mu_{0}\rangle-\int_{E}1(s\leq t_{0})\Delta g(v,v_{\star},\sigma)w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(68)

and in particular, we can choose
\delta>0
small enough that
\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f^{\delta})>1
. By linearity, for all
\lambda>0
,
\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,\lambda f^{\delta})>\lambda
, and so the supremum is infinite, as claimed. ∎

We now use this equality to show that the functions
\Xi
above give a variational formulation of the rate function
\mathcal{I}
given in the introduction.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
, we have

\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)=\sup\left\{\Xi(\mu_{\bullet},w,\varphi,f,g):\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\in C^{1,1}_{0,b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),g\in C_{c}(E)\right\}.

(69)

Proof.

Let us write
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
for the right-hand side. Since
\Xi_{0}
depends only on
\varphi
,
\Xi_{1}
only on
f
and
\Xi_{2}
only on
g
, the supremum decomposes as

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\mu_{\bullet},w)=\sup_{\varphi}\Xi_{0}(\mu_{\bullet},\varphi)+\sup_{f}\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)+\sup_{g}\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)

(70)

where the suprema run over the same sets as above. Optimising over
\varphi
produces the well-known variational formulation
\sup_{\varphi}\Xi_{0}(\mu_{0},\varphi)=H(\mu_{0}|\mu_{0}^{\star})
of the relative entropy. This identity can be found in
[
31
, Appendix 1]
, or derived using essentially the same argument as for
\Xi_{2,T}
below. Thanks to Lemma
2.3
, the supremum over
f
is infinite unless the continuity equation (
CE
) holds, in which case this term vanishes.

We now deal with the third term. If
w\not\ll\overline{m}_{\mu}
, there is a compact set
E^{\prime}\subset E
with
w(E^{\prime})>0
but
\overline{m}_{\mu}(E^{\prime})=0
, and by since
E
is a metric space and
w
is a Borel measure, we can find open
U_{n}\downarrow E^{\prime}
and closed
U_{n}\supset A_{n}\supset E^{\prime}
with
\overline{m}_{\mu}(U_{n})\downarrow 0
. We now choose
g_{n}\in C_{c}(E)
so that
0\leq g_{n}\leq 1
,
g_{n}=1
on
A_{n}
, and
=0
except on
U_{n}
, and bound for
\lambda>0
,

\int_{E}\lambda g_{n}w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\geq\lambda w(E^{\prime});

(71)

\int_{E}(e^{\lambda g_{n}}-1)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\leq(e^{\lambda}-1)\overline{m}_{\mu}(U_{n})

(72)

so that

\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,\lambda g_{n})\geq\lambda w(E^{\prime})-(e^{\lambda}-1)\overline{m}_{\mu}(U_{n}).

(73)

By taking
\lambda=\lambda_{n}\to\infty
slowly enough, the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily large as
n\to\infty
, so in this case
\sup_{g}\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)=\infty
. On the other hand, if
w\ll\overline{m}_{\mu}
, let us write
K
for the tilting function
\frac{dw}{d\overline{m}_{\mu}}
, so that

\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)=\int_{E}(Kg-e^{g}+1)(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).

(74)

Observing that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R},y\geq 0
, it holds that
xy\leq(e^{x}-1)+(y\ln y-y+1)=(e^{x}-1)+\tau(y)
, the first term
\int Kg
can be bounded by

\begin{split}\int_{E}Kg(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)&\leq\int_{E}\tau(K)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&+\int_{E}(e^{g}-1)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\end{split}

(75)

which leads to the bound, uniformly in
g\in C_{c}(E)
,

\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)\leq\int_{E}\tau(K(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma))\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(76)

whether or not the right-hand side is finite. On the other hand, let us fix
M
. By Lusin’s theorem, we can construct continuous, bounded
g_{n}\in C_{c}(E)
with
g_{n}\to\ln K\land M
for
\overline{m}_{\mu}
-almost all
(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)
, so that

Kg_{n}-e^{g_{n}}+1\leq MK+1

(77)

and

Kg_{n}-e^{g_{n}}+1\to K(\ln K\land M)-(K\land e^{M})+1

(78)

\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)
-almost everywhere. Since
K\in L^{1}(\overline{m}_{\mu})
, we can use dominated convergence to obtain

\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g_{n})\to\int_{E}(K(\ln K\land M)-(K\land e^{M})+1)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(79)

and the supremum is at least the right-hand side. The integrand is increasing in
M
, and converges to
\tau(K)
pointwise, so the whole integral converges to
\int_{E}\tau(K)d\overline{m}_{\mu}
. We conclude that

\sup\left\{\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g):g\in C_{c}(E)\right\}\geq\int_{E}\tau(K(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma))\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(80)

and (
76
) shows that this is an equality. Putting everything together, we have shown that

\begin{split}&\sup_{f,g}\left\{\Xi_{1,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,f)+\Xi_{2,T}(\mu_{\bullet},w,g)\right\}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt=\begin{cases}\int_{E}\tau(K)\overline{m}_{\mu}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)&\text{if }(\mu_{\bullet},w)\text{is a measure flux pair;}\\
\infty&\text{else}\end{cases}\end{split}

Thanks to this variational form, we readily obtain the lower semi-continuity claimed in Proposition

Lemma 2.5
.

For fixed

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv)\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv_{\star})\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star}).\end{split}

Lemma 2.6

For fixed

Using the variational formulation above, we now prove the upper bound in Theorem

Lemma 2.7

In order to prove Theorem

of the Kac process and for the Boltmzann equation, and a result on the energy of the Boltzmann equation.
[

Proposition 3.1

(Moments of the Kac process and Boltzmann Equation)

Let

(111)

ii).

For the same

as in item i), if

is a solution to the Boltzmann equation (

\langle|v|^{2},\mu_{t}\rangle=a

\langle|v|^{p},\mu_{t}\rangle\leq Cs^{2-p}

(112)

for some

.

iii).

For any solution
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to the Boltzmann equation (
BE
), the energy

is nondecreasing in time.

Moment estimates similar to the first two items go back to Desvillettes
[

and Wennberg
[

[

and Lu
[

The other property we will need are the changes of probability measure necessary to perturb the initial data and dynamics. The changes of measure we will use are as follows.

Proposition 3.2

(Kac process under change of measure)
.

Let
\mu^{N}_{t}
be a Kac process with collision kernel
B
, and velocities initially sampled independently from
\mu_{0}^{\star}
, which is a Markov process on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}=&\exp\bigg(N\langle\varphi,\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle+N\langle\log K(\mu^{N}_{0},\cdot),w^{N}\rangle\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-N\int_{E}(K-1)(\mu^{N}_{0},t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{w}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\bigg)c_{N}^{-1}1_{A}\end{split}

4

We now give a proof of the lower bound with the additional integrability hypothesis. The restricted lower bound is based on the following approximation lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(Approximation by Regular Paths)
.

Let
(\mu_{\bullet},w)
be a measure-flux pair such that

\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)<\infty;\qquad\langle 1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2},w\rangle<\infty.

(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})
of measure-flux pairs whose tilting functions
K^{(n)}

K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star},\sigma)

\mu^{\star}_{0}

\sup_{t\geq 0}W(\mu^{(n)}_{t},\mu_{t})+d(w^{(n)},w)\to 0;\qquad\limsup_{n}\mathcal{I}(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w)\leq\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w).

(115)

(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})
is the unique measure-flux pair starting from
\mu^{(n)}_{0}
and with tilting function

Throughout, we write the indexes

in the superscripts in brackets, to distinguish them from similar notation for the Kac process
\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N}
.The proof of this lemma is rather technical, and so is deferred until Subsection
4.1

1.2
ii) follows straightforwardly from standard ‘tilting’ arguments, using the change-of-measure given in Proposition
3.2
via the following law of large numbers.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\mu_{\bullet},w)
be a measure-flux pair with
\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)<\infty

is continuous and such that
KB(v-v_{\star})

(\mu_{\bullet},w)

\mathcal{U}\ni(\mu_{\bullet},w)

Proof.

We start by applying Proposition

\begin{split}M^{N,g}_{t}&=\langle g,w^{N}_{t}\rangle-\int_{(0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}g(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)K(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\dots B(v-v_{\star})ds\mu^{N}_{s}(dv)\mu^{N}_{s}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\end{split}

We will now show that the (random) exponential cost induced by the change of measure (

&\hskip-56.9055pt-\int_{E}(K-1)B(v-v_{\star})(\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star})-\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star}))d\sigma\end{split}

\begin{split}(g_{\lambda}\star w)(A)=&\int_{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{A}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\dots\times\overline{m}_{\mu}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip-56.9055pt=\int_{E}1_{A}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})\right)\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\dots\times dudu_{\star}dtd\sigma.\end{split}

(161)

Now, let us define
K^{\lambda}
by

K^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma):=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{B(u-u_{\star})(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})}

(162)

and observe that this is a well-defined function, since
K
was assumed to be bounded and
B
is bounded away from
0
, and where in the denominator
(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)>0
denotes the density of the measure
g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Returning to (
161
), this definition yields

\begin{split}(g_{\lambda}\star w)(A)&=\int_{E}1_{A}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)K^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)B(u-u_{\star})(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)du(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})du_{\star}dtd\sigma\\
&=\int_{E}1_{A}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)K^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,du,du_{\star},d\sigma).\end{split}

(163)

We conclude that
K^{\lambda}
is a tilting function for
(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{\bullet},g_{\lambda}\star w)
, and so this is a measure-flux pair as claimed, and the bound (
147
) is immediate. For future convenience, we will now define

\overline{r}^{\lambda}_{t}(u,u_{\star}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})

(164)

and introduce the proxy to
K^{\lambda}
given by

\begin{split}\overline{K}^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma):&=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{\overline{r}^{\lambda}_{t}(u,u_{\star})}\\[8.61108pt]
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\nu^{\lambda}_{(t,u,u_{\star})}(dv,dv_{\star})\end{split}

(165)

which we have written in terms of the integral against the probability measure

\nu^{\lambda}_{(t,u,u_{\star})}(dv,dv_{\star})=\frac{B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(v-u)\mu_{t}(dv)g_{\lambda}(v_{\star}-u_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{\overline{r}^{\lambda}_{t}(u,u_{\star})}.

(166)

For any
u,u_{\star},\sigma
, the quotient is given by the function

\begin{split}\frac{K^{\lambda}}{\overline{K}^{\lambda}}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)&=\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star}):=\frac{\overline{r}^{\lambda}_{t}(u,u_{\star})}{B(u-u_{\star})(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})}\\[12.91663pt]
&=\frac{1}{B(u-u_{\star})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}B(v-v_{\star})\frac{g_{\lambda}(v-u)\mu_{t}(dv)}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}\frac{g_{\lambda}(v_{\star}-u_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})}.\end{split}

(167)

which depends only on
\mu_{t}
, and not on
w
.

Step 3: Decomposition of the Rate Function

We now break the rate function up into several parts which can be more easily manipulated; for the rest of the proof, let us consider only
0<\lambda\leq 1
. We start from

\mathcal{J}(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{\bullet},g_{\lambda}\star w)=\int_{E}(\tau-1)(K^{\lambda}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)+\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(E)

(168)

where we recall that
(\tau-1)(x)=x\log x-x
is a convex function on
[0,\infty)
. Next, we observe that

(\tau-1)(K^{\lambda})=K^{\lambda}\log\psi_{\lambda}+\psi_{\lambda}(\tau-1)(\overline{K}^{\lambda}).

(169)

For the first term, we start with the observation that

\begin{split}&K^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,du,du_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dtd\sigma dudu_{\star}\end{split}

(170)

from which it follows that we can rewrite the integral of the first term as an error term

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mu_{\bullet},w)&:=\int_{E}K^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,du,du_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&=\int_{E\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\dots\times dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dudu_{\star}.\end{split}

(171)

To integrate the second term, we note that

\begin{split}\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,du,du_{\star},d\sigma)&=\overline{r}_{t}(u,u_{\star})dtdudu_{\star}d\sigma.\end{split}

(172)

Since
\nu^{\lambda}_{(t,u,u_{\star})}
are probability measures, we can apply Jensen to the convex function
\tau-1
to find

\begin{split}(\tau-1)(\overline{K}^{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma))&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\tau-1)(K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))\nu_{(t,u,u_{\star})}(dv,dv_{\star})\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt=\frac{1}{\overline{r}^{\lambda}_{t}(u,u_{\star})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\tau-1)(K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\dots\times\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star}).\end{split}

(173)

Gathering (
172
,
173
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{E}\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})(\tau-1)(\overline{K}^{\lambda})(t,u,u_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}(dt,du,du_{\star},d\sigma)\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\int_{E\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\tau-1)(K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{E}(\tau-1)(K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))B(v-v_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{E}(\tau-1)(K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma))\overline{m}_{\mu}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).\end{split}

(174)

Returning to (
168
) and using the analagous equation for
\mu_{\bullet}
, we finally obtain the decomposition

\mathcal{J}(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{\bullet},g_{\lambda}\star w)\leq\mathcal{J}(\mu_{\bullet},w)+(\overline{m}_{g_{\lambda}\star\mu}-\overline{m}_{\mu})(E)+\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mu_{\bullet},w).

(175)

It is very straightforward to show that the second term converges to
0
as
\lambda\to 0
, with a rate depending only on
\sup_{t}\langle|v|^{2},\mu_{t}\rangle
.

Step 4: Analysis of
\psi_{\lambda}

We now turn to the error term
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
identified in (
171
), which depends on the continuity of
\log B
. We remark first that this term cannot be avoided purely on general considerations; consider, for example, the kernel
B(v)=1_{|v|\geq 1}
in which case
\mathcal{J}(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{\bullet},g_{\lambda}\star w)
can become infinite due to contributions in the region
\{|v-v_{\star}|<1\}
.

We start with an upper bound for
\psi_{\lambda}
. We define first the measures

\xi^{\lambda}_{(t,u)}(dv):=\frac{g_{\lambda}(v-u)\mu_{t}(dv)}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}.

(176)

Setting
R:=\sqrt{2\sup_{s}\langle|v|^{2},\mu_{s}\rangle}
, a simple Chebychev inequality shows that
\mu_{t}(|v|\leq R)\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
t
, which leads to the lower bound

(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-(|u|+R)^{2})/2\lambda\right)/(2\pi\lambda)^{d/2}.

(177)

We now estimate

\begin{split}\left\langle|v|^{2}1[|v-u|>R+|u|],\xi^{\lambda}_{(t,u)}\right\rangle&=\frac{\int_{|v-u|>R+|u|}e^{-|v-u|^{2}/2\lambda}|v|^{2}\mu_{t}(dv)/(2\pi\lambda)^{d/2}}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}\\[8.61108pt]
&\leq\frac{e^{-(|u|+R)^{2}/2\lambda}/(2\pi\lambda)^{d/2}}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}\int_{|v-u|>R+|u|}|v|^{2}\mu_{t}(dv)\\[12.91663pt]
&\leq 2\sup_{s}\langle|v|^{2},\mu_{s}\rangle=R^{2}.\end{split}

(178)

Together with a trivial bound for the remaining region, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left\langle|v|^{2},\xi^{\lambda}_{(t,u)}\right\rangle\leq 3R^{2}+4|u|^{2}\leq C(1+|u|^{2})\end{split}

(179)

since
R\geq 1
depends only on the quantities in (
145
). Using the lower bound
B(u-u_{\star})\geq 1
and the upper bound
B(v-v_{\star})\leq 1+|v|+|v_{\star}|\leq C(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})
, we now return to (
167
) to obtain

\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})\leq C(1+|u|^{2}+|u_{\star}|^{2}).

(180)

This bound will be useful in general, for
(u,u_{\star})
where
\psi_{\lambda}
cannot be shown to be close to
1
. We complement this with a bound which will show that, for most points
(u,u_{\star})
in the support of
(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})^{\otimes 2}
,
\psi_{\lambda}
is not too much bigger than 1. We will exploit, repeatedly, the observation that, for the kernels given in (
3
),

B(v-v_{\star})\leq(1+|v-u|+|v_{\star}-u_{\star}|)B(u-u_{\star}).

(181)

Fix
t\geq 0
, and suppose that
v^{0},v^{0}_{\star}
are such that there exists a sets
U\ni v^{0},U_{\star}\ni v^{0}_{\star}
of diameter
c\sqrt{\lambda}
and
\mu_{t}(U),\mu_{t}(U_{\star})\geq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}
, and
(u,u_{\star})
is such that
|(u,u_{\star})-(v^{0},v^{0}_{\star})|<x\sqrt{\lambda}
, for some constant
c
and parameters
\epsilon>0,x<\infty
to be chosen later. In this case, we bound the denominator below by observing that

(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)\geq\frac{1}{(2\pi\lambda)^{d/2}}e^{-(x\sqrt{\lambda}+c\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}/2\lambda}\mu(U)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-x^{2}-c^{2}\right)

(182)

and similarly for
u_{\star}
. We now return to (
167
) and split the integral defining
\psi_{\lambda}
into the regions
E_{1}=\{(v,v_{\star}):|(v,v_{\star})-(u,u_{\star})|<\lambda^{1/3}+3x\sqrt{\lambda}\}
and its complement
E_{2}
. On
E_{1}
,

B(v-v_{\star})\leq(1+2\lambda^{1/3}+6x\sqrt{\lambda})B(u-u_{\star})

(183)

and so

\begin{split}&\int_{E_{1}}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{B(v-v_{\star})}{B(u-u_{\star})}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{g_{\lambda}(v-u)\mu_{t}(dv)}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{g_{\lambda}(v_{\star}-u_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})}\leq 1+2\lambda^{1/3}+6x\sqrt{\lambda}.\end{split}

(184)

On the other hand, using (
183
) and the trivial bound
B(v-v_{\star})/B(u-u_{\star})\leq C(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)
, we bound the term from the second region by

\begin{split}&\int_{E_{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{B(v-v_{\star})}{B(u-u_{\star})}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{g_{\lambda}(v-u)\mu_{t}(dv)}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u)}\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{g_{\lambda}(v_{\star}-u_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})}{(g_{\lambda}\star\mu_{t})(u_{\star})}\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 85.35826pt\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\lambda^{-d}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\lambda}(9x^{2}\lambda+\lambda^{2/3})\right)\epsilon^{-2}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\dots\times\exp(2x^{2}+2c^{2})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})\\[8.61108pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\exp\left(2c^{2}-x^{2}\right)\epsilon^{-2}.\end{split}

(185)

where, in the final line, we recall that
\lambda^{-d}\exp(-\lambda^{-1/6}/{2})
is uniformly bounded on
(0,\infty)
, and that the remaining integral is controlled in terms of the second moments of
\mu_{t}
, so can be absorbed into
C
. Gathering (
184
,
185
), we conclude that for
(u,u_{\star}),x,\epsilon
as above,

\begin{split}\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})&\leq 1+2\lambda^{1/3}+6x\sqrt{\lambda}+C\exp(2c^{2}-x^{2})\epsilon^{-2}\\
&\leq(1+2\lambda^{1/3}+6x\sqrt{\lambda})\left(1+C\exp(2c^{2}-x^{2})\epsilon^{-2}\right).\end{split}

(186)

Step 5: Analysis of
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}

Equipped with this preliminary analysis of
\psi_{\lambda}
in the previous step, we bound the final term
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
appearing in (
175
). Together with the observation under (
175
), this suffices to prove (
146
) and finish the proof of the lemma.

We break up the integration space
E\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
as follows. For
M\geq 2,R\in[3,\infty)\cap\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{N}
,
x\in(0,\infty),\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
to be chosen later, we form a partition
\mathfrak{P}
of
(-R,R]^{d}
into
(2R/\sqrt{\lambda})^{d}
translates of
(0,\sqrt{\lambda}]^{d}
, and for
v\in(-R,R]^{d}
, write
\mathcal{B}(v)
for the unique
\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{P}
containing
v
. We now consider the partition of
E\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\hskip-14.22636ptA_{1}:=\left\{v,v_{\star}\in(-R,R]^{d},K\leq M,\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(w))\geq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2},\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v_{\star}))\geq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2},|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|<x\sqrt{\lambda}\right\};

\hskip-14.22636ptA_{2}:=\left\{v,v_{\star}\in(-R,R]^{d},K\leq M,\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(w))\geq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2},\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v_{\star}))\geq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2},|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|\geq x\sqrt{\lambda}\right\};

A_{3}:=\left\{v,v_{\star}\in(-R,R]^{d},K\leq M,\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\text{ or }\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v_{\star}))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\right\};

A_{4}:=\left\{v,v_{\star}\in(-R,R]^{d},K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)>M\right\};

A_{5}:=\left\{(v,v_{\star})\not\in(-R,R]^{2d}\right\}.

(187)

We analyse the contributions from these regions one-by-one. Roughly,
A_{1}
is the ‘good’ region, containing most of the contributions from the integrating measure, where
\log\psi_{\lambda}
is small by (
186
), and the remaining terms are small, depending on the parameters
M,R,x,\epsilon
; at the end, we will optimise, so that
M,R,x\to\infty
and
\epsilon\to 0
as functions of
\lambda\to 0
.

Step 5a: Contribution from
A_{1}

For the region
A_{1}
, we observe that the hypotheses leading to (
186
) hold, with
U=\mathcal{B}(v),U_{\star}=\mathcal{B}(v_{\star})
and
c=\sqrt{d}
is an absolute constant. Further,
K<M
and
B(v-v_{\star})\leq 1+2R\leq CR
, so we integrate (
186
) to find

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{1}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq CMR\left(\log(1+2\lambda^{1/3}+6x\sqrt{\lambda})+\log(1+\epsilon^{-2}e^{-x^{2}})\right).\end{split}

(188)

Step 5b: Contribution from
A_{2}

For
A_{2}
, we use the general upper bound (
180
), which is valid without restriction on
u,u_{\star}
. For fixed
v,v_{\star}
we use Hölder’s inequality to see that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})1[|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|\geq x\sqrt{\lambda}]g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log(1+|u|^{2}+|u_{\star}|^{2})1[|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|\geq x\sqrt{\lambda}]\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\dots\times g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|u|^{2}+|u_{\star}|^{2})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\dots\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}1[|(u^{\prime},u^{\prime}_{\star})|>x\sqrt{\lambda}]g_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})g_{\lambda}(u^{\prime}_{\star})du^{\prime}du^{\prime}_{\star}\right)^{1/2}\\[8.61108pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\exp\left(-x^{2}/16d\right)\end{split}

(189)

where for the first factor in the final line, we integrated
\int|u|^{2}g_{\lambda}(u-v)du=|u|^{2}+d\lambda^{2}\leq|v|^{2}+d
, and for the second factor we used standard tail estimates for the normal distribution, absorbing constants into the prefactor
C
. Bounding
B(v-v_{\star}),K
as above, and integrating over
t,v,v_{\star},\sigma
, we find

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{2}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\leq CMR\exp\left(-x^{2}/16d\right).\end{split}

(190)

Step 5c: Contribution from
A_{3}

Similarly to the previous step, we start from a bound on the integrals over
u,u_{\star}
, with
t
and
v,v_{\star}
fixed. We split the integral over
u,u_{\star}
into
\{|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|\leq(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\}
and
\{|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|>(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\}
. In the first region, thanks to (
181
),

\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})\leq C\log(1+|u|^{2}+|u_{\star}|^{2})\leq C\log(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})

(191)

while the contribution from the second region is controlled by using Hölder’s inequality in the same way as (
189
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{|(u,u_{\star})-(v,v_{\star})|>(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)}\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\leq C(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\exp(-(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)^{2}/16d).\end{split}

(192)

This term can be absorbed into the contribution from (
191
), and we conclude that, for all
t
and
v,v_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}\leq C\log(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2}).

(193)

In particular, when
v,v_{\star}\in(-R,R]^{d}
, the right-hand side can be replaced by
C\log R
, and
B(v-v_{\star})\leq CR
. We now integrate over
A_{3}
to find

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{3}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dudu_{\star}\\
&\leq CM(R\log R)\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{t}^{\otimes 2}\left((v,v_{\star})\in(-R,R]^{2d}:\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\text{ or }\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v_{\star}))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\right)dt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq CM(R\log R)\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{t}\left(v\in(-R,R]^{d}:\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\right)dt\end{split}

(194)

where the last line follows using a union bound, absorbing the factor of
2
into
C
. The integrand is now

\begin{split}\mu_{t}\left(v\in(-R,R]^{d}:\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B}(v))<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}\right)&=\sum_{\mathcal{B}\in\mathfrak{P}}\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B})1(\mu_{t}(\mathcal{B})<\epsilon\lambda^{d/2})\\[8.61108pt]
&\leq\epsilon\lambda^{d/2}(\#\mathfrak{P})=\epsilon(2R)^{d}\end{split}

(195)

recalling that
\#\mathfrak{P}=(2R/\sqrt{\lambda})^{d}
. Substituting this bound back into (
194
) we conclude that

\begin{split}&\int_{A_{3}}K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})\log\psi_{\lambda}(t,u,u_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dudu_{\star}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq CM(R^{d+1}\log R)\epsilon.\end{split}

(196)

Step 5d: Contribution from
A_{4}

In
A_{4}
, we use the same bound (
193
) on
\int\log\psi_{\lambda}g_{\lambda}(u-v)g_{\lambda}(u_{\star}-v_{\star})dudu_{\star}
, and observe that, on
A_{4}
,
K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\leq\frac{M}{\tau(M)}\tau(K)
. Integrating, it follows that

(199)

Proof of Lemma

(207)

and the form (
149

is immediate by construction. Further, the conntinuity equation (

Step 2: Convergence of the Truncated Measure-Flux

Firstly, we show that

uniformly in a weighted total variation norm. At time

,

\begin{split}\|(1+|v|^{2})(\mu^{(n)}_{0}-\mu_{0})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq c_{n}^{-1}\langle 1+|v|^{2},\nu^{(n)}\rangle+\frac{|1-c_{n}|}{c_{n}}\langle 1+|v|^{2},\mu_{0}\rangle.\end{split}

In the first term,

&+4|1-c_{n}^{-1}|\int_{E}(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})w(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\rightarrow 0\end{split}

and we conclude that

\begin{split}(\tau-1)(K^{(n)})\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)&=(\log K^{(n)}-1)w^{(n)}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\

and that, on

\begin{split}(\tau-1)(K^{(n)})\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)&\leq c_{n}^{-1}(\log K-1+\log c_{n})1_{E_{n}}w(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\[4.30554pt]

Integrating, and recalling the definition of

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})-\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(E)&=\int_{E}(\tau-1)(K^{(n)})\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\[4.30554pt]

&\hskip 28.45274pt+(c_{n}^{-1}\log c_{n})w(E).\end{split}

and we are done. ∎ Combining the previous two results, we prove Lemma

\lambda_{n}\in(0,1],\lambda_{n}\to 0
which decays slowly enough that
a_{\lambda_{n}}\langle 1+|v|^{2},\nu^{(n)}\rangle\to 0
. We now define
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}:=\mu^{(n,\lambda_{n})}_{\bullet},w^{(n)}:=w^{(n,\lambda_{n})}
. Every term except the first on the right-hand side of (
239
) converges to
0
, and in particular
\limsup_{n}\mathcal{I}(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})\leq\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)
.

Step 4: Conclusion

We now check that the diagonal sequence extracted has all the desired properties. First, thanks to (
147
), the convolution with
g_{\lambda_{n}}
preserves the boundedness, so

\sup_{(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)}K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\leq\sup_{(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)}K^{(n,0)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})<\infty.

(240)

To see convergence of the overall sequence, note that
W(\mu,g_{\lambda}\star\mu)\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}
for all measures
\mu
, and since
W
is dominated by the total variation distance,

\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T}W(\mu^{(n)}_{t},\mu_{t})&\leq\sup_{t\leq T}W(g_{\lambda_{n}}\star\mu^{(n,0)}_{t},\mu^{(n,0)}_{t})+\sup_{t\leq T}\left\|\mu^{(n,0)}_{t}-\mu_{t}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\[8.61108pt]
&\leq C\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}+\sup_{t\leq T}\left\|(1+|v|^{2})(\mu^{(n,0)}_{t}-\mu_{t})\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0.\end{split}

(241)

Similarly,
d(g_{\lambda_{n}}\star w^{(n,0)},w^{(n,0)})\leq C\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}
, so that
d(w^{(n)},w)\to 0
. ∎
Finally, we prove Lemma
4.8
, which allows us to impose an asymptotic lower bound on
K
, so we control how fast
|\log K|
grows as
v,v_{\star}\to\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
4.8
.

Let us consider the space
\mathcal{MS}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of signed measures with finite second moment
\langle 1+|v|^{2},|\xi|\rangle<\infty
, equipped with the complete distance given by the weighted total variation norm
\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}:=\|(1+|v|^{2})\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
. We start from a measure-flux pair
(\mu_{\bullet},w)
as in the statement, so that the tilting function
K
is continuous in
v,v_{\star}
, and
B(v-v_{\star})K
is bounded; since
B
is bounded away from
0
, this implies the same for
K
.

Step 1: Construction of
K

We begin with a family of mollifiers. Let us fix a smooth function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
, supported on
[-1,1]
and such that
\int\eta ds=1
, and for
t\in[0,T],\lambda>0
, define

\eta_{\lambda}(s,t)=\frac{\eta((s-t)/\lambda)}{\int_{0}^{T}\eta((u-t)/\lambda)du}

(242)

so that
\eta_{\lambda}
is continuous in both arguments,
\eta_{\lambda}(\cdot,t)
is supported on
[0,T]\cap[t-\lambda,t+\lambda]
, and
\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{\lambda}(s,t)ds=1
. For the spherical directions, let
h_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the heat kernel on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, so that
h_{\lambda}(\cdot,\sigma)
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
which integrates to
1
, and
h_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma)d\sigma^{\prime}\to\delta_{\sigma}(d\sigma^{\prime})
weakly as
\lambda\to 0
. With these fixed, we define
K^{(n)}
by

K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma):=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}K(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma^{\prime})\eta_{1/n}(s,t)h_{1/n}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma)dsd\sigma^{\prime}+\frac{1}{nB(v-v_{\star})}.

(243)

From the construction, the continuity of
K
in
v,v_{\star}
implies that each
K^{(n)}
is continuous on
E
.
K^{(n)}
also inherit the upper bound: there exists
M
such that

\sup_{n}\sup_{t,v,v_{\star},\sigma}B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\leq M;\qquad\sup_{t,v,v_{\star},\sigma}B(v-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\leq M

(244)

and by construction
\inf_{E}B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}\geq\frac{1}{n}>0.
Finally, for all
(v,v_{\star})
fixed,
dtd\sigma
almost everywhere,
K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\to K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)
.

Step 2: Construction of
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
by Picard-Lindelöf

We now construct processes
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
, which at this stage may be signed measures, via the machinery of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem. We consider the space
\mathcal{MS}_{2}
of signed measures for which the quadratic total variation norm
\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}:=\langle 1+|v|^{2},|\xi|\rangle
is finite. For any
t\in[0,T]
,
\xi\in\mathcal{MS}_{2}
, define the signed measures

\Phi(t,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Delta(v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})K(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\xi(dv)\xi(dv_{\star})d\sigma;

(245)

\Phi_{n}(t,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Delta(v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\xi(dv)\xi(dv_{\star})d\sigma.

(246)

Using the uniform boundedness of
B(v-v_{\star})K,B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}
, it is easy to see that

\|(\Phi_{n}(t,\xi)-\Phi_{n}(t,\xi^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\|(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}(\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}+\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2})

(247)

for some constant
C
, uniformly in
n
, and similarly for
\Phi
. It then follows from the Picard-Lindelöf theorem that, for any
\xi^{(n)}_{0},\xi_{0}
, there exist unique local solutions to the integral equations

\xi^{(n)}_{t}=\xi^{(n)}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{n}(s,\xi^{(n)}_{s})ds;\qquad\xi_{t}=\xi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s,\xi_{s})ds.

(248)

Further, observing that
\|\Phi_{n}(t,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
, it follows that
\|\xi^{(n)}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
grows at most exponentially in time; using the similar estimate that
\|\Phi_{n}(t,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\leq C\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\|\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
by taking
\xi^{\prime}=0
above, the same holds for
\|\xi^{(n)}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}
, so the solutions are globally defined.

Let us now consider these equations to construct our approximations. For the initial data, we recall that the finiteness of the entropy
H(\mu_{0}|\mu^{\star}_{0})\leq\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)<\infty
implies that
\mu_{0}
has a density with respect to
\mu^{\star}_{0}
, and we set

\mu^{(n)}_{0}(dv)=c_{n}\left(\frac{d\mu_{0}}{d\mu^{\star}_{0}}\land n\right)\mu^{\star}_{0}(dv)

(249)

for a normalising constant
c_{n}\to 1
which makes
\mu^{(n)}_{0}
a probability measure. We now take
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}
to be the unique solution
\xi^{(n)}_{t}
produced to
\partial_{t}\xi^{(n)}_{t}=\Phi_{n}(t,\xi^{(n)}_{t})
for this choice of initial data. It follows by definition of
K
that the process
\mu_{t}
given satisfies
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\Phi(t,\mu_{t})
, which must then by the unique solution.

Step 3: Positivity of
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}

To see that this gives positive measures, we use an integrating factor introduced by Norris
[
43
]
in the context of a similar construction for the Smolouchowski equation. We define

\theta^{(n)}_{t}(v):=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(K^{(n)}(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)+K^{(n)}(s,v_{\star},v,\sigma))B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{(n)}_{s}(dv_{\star})d\sigma ds\right)

(250)

and

\Phi^{+}_{n}(t,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\theta^{(n)}_{t}(v^{\prime})\delta_{v^{\prime}}+\theta^{(n)}_{t}(v^{\prime}_{\star})\delta_{v^{\prime}_{\star}})B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\xi(dv)\xi(dv_{\star})d\sigma.

(251)

Thanks to the boundedness,
\theta^{(n)}_{t}
is bounded and bounded away from
0
, uniformly on compact time intervals, and
\Phi^{+}_{n}(\xi)\geq 0
whenever
\xi\geq 0
. These integrating factors are such that
\partial_{t}(\theta^{(n)}_{t}\mu^{(n)}_{t})=\Phi^{+}_{n}(t,\theta^{(n)}_{t}\mu^{(n)}_{t})
, while applying the same arguments as above in the smaller space
(\mathcal{P}_{2},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2})
shows that the unique solution to
\partial_{t}\nu_{t}=\Phi_{n}^{+}(t,\nu_{t})
remains positive if
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
is a positive measure. It follows that
\theta^{(n)}_{t}\mu^{(n)}_{t}\geq 0
are positive measures, and hence so are
\mu^{(n)}_{t}
; recalling again the boundedness, energy conservation implies that
\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{(n)}_{t}\rangle=\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{(n)}_{0}\rangle<\infty
is constant for each
n
, and we conclude that
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{D}
. We define the corresponding flux
w^{(n)}
by

w^{(n)}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma):=K^{(n)}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(252)

so that
\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)}
is a measure-flux pair. Moreover, if
(\mu^{\prime}_{\bullet},w^{\prime})
is any measure-flux pair with
\mu^{\prime}_{0}=\mu^{(n)}_{0}
and with tilting function
K^{(n)}
, then
\partial_{t}\mu^{\prime}_{t}=\Phi_{n}(t,\mu^{\prime}_{t})
, which implies that
\mu^{\prime}_{t}=\mu^{(n)}_{t}
by the uniqueness in step 2, and
w^{\prime}=K\overline{m}_{\mu^{\prime}}=K\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}=w
, so each approximating pair
(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})
is uniquely characterised by the initial value and tilting function, as claimed.

Step 4: Convergence of the Approximations

Let us now show that the measure-flux pairs constructed above converge as
n\to\infty
. We start from

\begin{split}\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu^{(n)}_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}&\leq\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu^{(n)}_{t})-\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}+\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\\
&\hskip-85.35826pt\leq C\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}-\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}(\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}+\|\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2})+\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\end{split}

(253)

using (
247
). In the first term, we observe that
\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}=\langle 1+|v|^{2},\mu^{(n)}_{0}\rangle
is bounded uniformly in
n,t
, thanks to energy conservation and the construction of
\mu^{(n)}_{0}
, and we absorb this constant factor into
C
. We can now use Grönwall’s Lemma to obtain

\sup_{t\leq T}\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}-\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\leq e^{CT}\left(\|\mu^{(n)}_{0}-\mu_{0}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}dt\right).

(254)

The first term is readily seen to converge to
0
using the construction (
249
) of
\mu^{(n)}_{0}
, recalling that
\mu_{0}
has finite second moment. For the second term, we return to the definition of
\Phi,\Phi_{n}
to see that

\begin{split}\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}&\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})(K^{(n)}-K)(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\dots\times B(v-v_{\star})\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\end{split}

(255)

and integrating over
t\in[0,T]
produces

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\|\Phi_{n}(t,\mu_{t})-\Phi(t,\mu_{t})\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}dt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq 2\int_{E}(1+|v|^{2}+|v_{\star}|^{2})(K^{(n)}-K)B(v-v_{\star})dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma.\end{split}

(256)

We now apply dominated convergence to see that the right-hand side converges to
0
, since
B(v-v_{\star})(K^{(n)}-K)
is bounded by (
244
), and converges to
0
for
dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma
almost all
(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)
, while
\mu_{t}
has constant, finite second moment. Returning to (
254
), we conclude that
\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}-\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}+2}\to 0
, which is stronger than the required convergence. For the flux, we estimate

\begin{split}\|w^{(n)}-w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}&\leq\int_{E}|K^{(n)}-K|B(v-v_{\star})dt\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv)\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{E}KB(v-v_{\star})dt|\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})-\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv)\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv_{\star})|d\sigma.\end{split}

(257)

The first term converges to
0
as above, and recalling (
244
), the second term is bounded by

\begin{split}\int_{E}KB(v-v_{\star})dt|\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})-\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv)\mu^{(n)}_{t}(dv_{\star})|d\sigma\leq 2C\int_{0}^{T}\|\mu^{(n)}_{t}-\mu_{t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}dt\to 0\end{split}

(258)

and we have proven that
\|w^{(n)}-w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0
as desired.

Step 5: Convergence of the Cost Function

We finally check the convergence of the rate function
\mathcal{I}
along our subsequence. First,
\mu^{(n)}_{0}=\mu_{0}
by construction, so it suffices to prove the same thing for the dynamic cost
\mathcal{J}
; by the usual lower semicontinuity, it suffices to prove
\limsup_{n}\mathcal{J}(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})\leq\mathcal{J}(\mu_{\bullet},w)
. We start by writing

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(\mu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})&=\int_{E}\tau(K^{(n)})\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\mathcal{J}(\mu_{\bullet},w)+\int_{E}\tau(K^{(n)})(\overline{m}_{\mu^{(n)}}-\overline{m}_{\mu})(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{E}(\tau(K^{(n)})-\tau(K))\overline{m}_{\mu}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma).\end{split}

(259)

In the second term,
B(v-v_{\star})K^{(n)}\leq M
everywhere, and since
B\geq 1
, this implies the same bound for
K^{(n)}
and hence the bound
\tau(K^{(n)})\leq 1+\tau(M)
, uniformly in
n
. The second term is now at most
(1+\tau(M))\|w^{(n)}-w\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0
. Similarly,
\tau(K^{(n)})\to\tau(K)
converges
dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma
almost everywhere, and hence
\overline{m}_{\mu}
almost everywhere, with the same uniform bound as above. Since
\overline{m}_{\mu}(E)<\infty
, we can apply dominated convergence to see that the second term
\to 0
, and we are done. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

We now turn to the proof of the main result Theorem

. Let us fix, throughout,
\Theta
and

as in the theorem. We first present the proofs in detail in the case of the regularised hard sphere potential

\mathbb{Q}^{N}

5.1
Construction of a change of measure
\mathbb{Q}^{N}

Throughout, let us fix
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
on which are defined regularised hard sphere Kac processes
\mu^{N}_{\bullet}
and their empirical fluxes

. We now use the Girsanov formula recalled in Proposition

, and construct the tilting
\varphi
of the initial data and dynamic modification of

of the dynamics separately.

Step 1. Construction of Initial Data

Let us consider the random variables
X_{M},M\geq 0
, which describe the initial localisation of the energy in the initial data:

X_{M}=\left\langle|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M],\mu^{N}_{0}\right\rangle.

(260)

Since the particles are sampled independently from
\mu_{0}^{\star}
, we can write
X_{M}
as the mean of
N
independent variables, which each have the distribution
Y_{M}=|V|^{2}1[|V|\geq M];V\sim\mu_{0}^{\star}
. We write
\psi_{M}
for the cumulant generating function for
Y_{M}
, and
\psi^{\star}_{M}
for the associated Legendre transform:

\psi_{M}(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda Y_{M}}\right];\qquad\psi^{\star}_{M}(a)=\sup\left\{a\lambda-\psi_{M}(\lambda)\right\}.

By Hypothesis
1
ii), it follows that
\psi_{M}(\lambda)=\infty
for all
\lambda\geq z_{2}
and all
M
, which implies that
\psi^{\star}_{M}(a)\leq az_{2}
, uniformly in
M
.

For
M>0
and
\lambda\in[0,z_{2})
to be chosen later, we will take
\varphi_{N,M}
to be the function

\varphi_{M,\lambda}(v)=\lambda|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]-\psi_{M}(\lambda)

(261)

so that, under any change of measure of the form (
113
) for this choice of
\varphi
, each initial velocity
V^{i}_{0}
is distributed independently with law

\mu^{\star}_{0,\lambda,M}(dv)=\exp\left(\lambda|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]-\psi_{M}(\lambda)\right)\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv).

(262)

Step 2: Choice of
\lambda

We now choose
\lambda
as a function of
M
. For fixed

, it is standard to check that

E_{M}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2}\exp\left(\lambda|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]-\psi_{M}(\lambda)\right)\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv)=\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{\star}_{0,\lambda,M}\rangle

is continuous on
[0,z_{2})
,
E_{M}(0)=1
, and

diverges to infinity as

With this choice of
\lambda
, we write
\varphi_{M}=\varphi_{M,\lambda_{M}}
and
\mu^{\star}_{0,M}
for
\mu^{\star}_{0,\lambda_{M},M}
.

Step 3: Choice of
K

We next choose the dynamic tilting function
K=K^{M,r,N}
, depending on the same parameter

and an additional parameter

, to be chosen later. Given

, let

(263)

and set

. By Hypothesis
1

\mu^{\star}_{0,M}
has a density, and in particular the function

\left\langle|v|^{2}1[|v|\leq M_{i}],\mu^{\star}_{0,M}\right\rangle=\Theta(t_{i}+)

(264)

and observe that

K^{M,r,N}(\mu^{N}_{0},t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if either }v,v_{\star}\in\text{Supp}(1(|v|\geq M_{i-1})\mu_{0}^{N})=S_{t};\\
N1(N_{t}\geq 1)/N_{t}&\text{else}\end{cases}

where

(266)

Throughout, we will suppress the dependence of

With this choice of

Step 4: Choice of

By the law of large numbers, as

\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{M,r}\left(W(\mu^{N}_{0},\mu^{\star}_{0,M})>\delta\right)\to 0

(267)

for any

\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{M,r}\left(\text{For all }i=0,...,r,\hskip 2.84544pt\left|\langle|v|^{2}1[|v|\leq M_{i},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle-\Theta(t^{(r)}_{i}+)\right|<\delta\right)\to 1;

(268)

\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{M,r}\left(\left|\frac{N_{0}(M,r)}{N}-\langle 1[|v|\leq M_{0}],\mu^{\star}_{0,M}\rangle\right|<\delta\right)\to 1.

(269)

Now, we compare the equations

\displaystyle e^{\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\lambda_{M}|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]}\mu^{\star}_{0}(dv);

(270)

\displaystyle\Theta(T)e^{\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\lambda_{M}|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]}|v|^{2}\mu^{\star}_{0}(dv)

(271)

to obtain

\begin{split}e^{\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})}&=\int_{|v|<M}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv)+\int_{|v|\geq M}e^{\lambda_{M}|v|^{2}}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv)\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq 1+\frac{1}{M^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2}e^{\lambda_{M}|v|^{2}1[|v|\geq M]}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv)\end{split}

(272)

which implies that

e^{\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})}\leq 1+\frac{\Theta(T)}{M^{2}}e^{\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})}

(273)

and hence
\psi_{M}(\lambda_{M})\rightarrow 0
as

. This implies the convergence of
\mu^{\star}_{0,M}
to
\mu^{\star}_{0}
: for any

For the rate function, we return to the definition (

We now prove (

,
28

be any open set, and
S^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{N}
a subsequence such that

\lim_{N\to\infty,N\in S^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U}\right)=\liminf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U}\right).

(323)

For the changes of measure

(324)

Taking the logarithm and the limit

and then the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty,N\in S^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U}\right)\geq-\Theta(T)z_{2}

and by the choice (

For the second item (

\begin{split}\liminf_{N}\left[\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{V}\right)\right]&\leq\liminf_{N}\left[\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle>\frac{1+\Theta(T)}{2}\right)\right]\\[4.30554pt]

(328)

5.4

We now give the proof in the case of Maxwell molecules. In this case, since the kernel

is bounded, the moment creation property no longer holds; we also change measure so that, under
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
, the Kac process has a kernel
\widetilde{B}=\widetilde{B}_{\delta}
with linear growth. The previous argument then applies, albeit with an additional (small) exponential cost. Since the argument is almost identical, we will discuss only the essential modifications relative to the regularised hard spheres case. As before, let us fix
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
on which are defined Maxwell molecule Kac processes
\mu^{N}_{\bullet}
and their empirical fluxes

Proof of Theorem
1.3
b).

Fix
\Theta,P,\delta>0
as in the statement. We construct the modification of the initial data via
\varphi_{M}
exactly as for the case of hard spheres above. With the same notation on
M_{i},t^{(r)}_{i}
and the special set of ‘frozen’ particles
S_{t}
, we now choose
K
to be given by

K^{N,M,r}(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if either }v,v_{\star}\in S_{t};\\
N(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)1(N_{t}\geq 1)/N_{t}&\text{else}\end{cases}

(329)

where, again, we supress the argument
\mu^{N}_{0}
. In this way, the non-frozen particles interact as a Kac process with kernel
(1+\delta|v|)
on
N_{t}
particles on each time interval
[t^{(r)}_{i-1},t^{(r)}_{i})
. We choose
M_{N},r_{N}\to\infty
in exactly the same way as before, and write
\mathbb{Q}^{N}
for the resulting changes of measure via Proposition
3.2
:

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}&=\exp\bigg(N\langle\varphi_{M_{N}},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle+\langle\log K^{N},w^{N}\rangle\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(K^{N}-1)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\bigg).\end{split}

(330)

We will write
K=(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)
for the limiting tilting function. The strategy is now similar to the previous case. The law of large numbers follows in the same way for the new definition of
\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}
without essential modification, allowing
\alpha
to depend on
\delta
and arguing in the same was as leading to (
287
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{E}|K^{N}-K|(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\leq CT\left(\left(\frac{N}{N_{0}(M_{N},r_{N})}-1\right)+\frac{1}{M_{N}}\right)\langle 1+|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle^{2}.\end{split}

(331)

This is, in fact, the same estimate as before, up to the inclusion of
\delta
; the linear factor
(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)
now included in
K,K^{N}
replaces the equivalent one previously in the measure
\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}=B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma dt
so that the previous calculations are unchanged. With these modifications, the proof of the law of large numbers works exactly as before.

We again estimate the change of measure
\frac{1}{N}\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}
. In this case, we will only find an estimate which asymptotically holds with sufficiently large probability, rather than with probabilities converging to
1
as we did before; this will not affect the final result. We recall that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}&=\langle\varphi_{M_{N}},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle+\langle\log K^{N},w^{N}\rangle\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{E}(K^{N}-1)(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\end{split}

(332)

where again,
K^{N}
is allowed to depend on
\mu^{N}_{0}
. Let us fix
\epsilon>0
. The term from the change of initial data is exactly as in the hard spheres case:

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\langle\varphi_{M_{N}},\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle>z_{2}\Theta(T)+\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right)\to 0.

(333)

In the second term, we now use the upper bound

\begin{split}\log K^{N}&\leq\log N/N_{0}(M_{N},r_{N})+\log(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)\\
&\leq\log N/N_{0}(M_{N},r_{N})+\delta(|v|+|v_{\star}|).\end{split}

(334)

As in the hard spheres case, the first term contributes at most
(\log N/N_{0})w^{N}(E)\leq\epsilon/2
with high

\begin{split}&\delta\langle|v|+|v_{\star}|,w^{N}_{t}\rangle\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\delta\int_{(0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{N}{N_{0}}(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)(|v|+|v_{\star}|)ds\mu^{N}_{s}(dv)\mu^{N}_{s}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\end{split}

is a

such that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}\left[\delta\langle|v|+|v_{\star}|,w^{N}\rangle 1\left(N_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}N\right)\right]\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\delta\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}\left[\int_{E}2(|v|+|v_{\star}|+2\delta|v|^{2}+2\delta|v_{\star}|^{2})ds\mu^{N}_{s}(dv)\mu^{N}_{s}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\right]\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\delta C\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{N}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{s}\rangle ds\right]=\delta CT\Theta(T).\end{split}

(336)

Therefore, up to a new choice of
C
, for all
N
,

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\delta\langle|v|+|v_{\star}|,w^{N}\rangle>\delta CT\Theta(T),\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544ptN_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}N\right)\leq\frac{1}{9}

(337)

and recalling (
276

N
sufficiently large,
\mathbb{Q}^{N}(N_{0}\leq N/2)\leq\frac{1}{9}
, so, for all
N
sufficiently large,

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\delta\langle|v|+|v_{\star}|,w^{N}\rangle>\delta CT\Theta(T)\right)\leq\frac{2}{9}

(338)

and including the term

, we conclude that

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\langle\log K^{N},w^{N}\rangle>\delta CT\Theta(T)+\epsilon/3\right)<\frac{1}{3}.

(339)

For the final term of (

|K^{N}-1|\leq|K^{N}-K|+|K-1|\leq|K^{N}-K|+\delta(|v|+|v_{\star}|)

(340)

and arguing from (
331

N
sufficiently large,

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\int_{E}|K^{N}-K|\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)>\epsilon/3\right)\to 0

(341)

while in the second term, we have the pathwise inequality

\begin{split}\int_{E}\delta(|v|+|v_{\star}|)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\leq 2\delta\int_{0}^{T}\langle|v|^{2},\mu^{N}_{t}\rangle dt=2\delta T\langle|v|^{2},\mu_{0}\rangle.\end{split}

(342)

The right-hand side converges with
\mathbb{Q}^{N}

by (

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\int_{E}|K^{N}-1|\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)>2\delta T\Theta(T)+\epsilon/3\right)\to 0.

(343)

Gathering (

,
339
,
338
,
343

and all

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{N}\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}>\Theta(T)(z_{2}+C\delta)+\epsilon\right)<\frac{1}{4}.

(344)

Exactly the same argument also implies that

\lim_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{N}\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}>e^{Na}\right)=0

so that Corollary

applies.

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}(\mu_{\bullet},w)&=\int_{E}\tau(1+\delta|v-v_{\star}|)dt\mu_{t}(dv)\mu_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma\\

and recalling that

\mathcal{U}\supset\mathcal{A}_{\Theta,\delta}

S^{\prime}
be an infinite subsequence along which

\liminf

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}

and, for

\mathbb{Q}^{N}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U},\frac{1}{N}\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{d\mathbb{P}}\leq\Theta(T)(z_{2}+C\delta)+\epsilon\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}.

1.3
in either case. For a contradiction, let
(\mu^{N}_{\bullet})_{N\in S}
be a subsequence which satisfies a large deviation principle with a rate function
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\mu_{\bullet},w)=\infty
if
\mu_{\bullet}
does not conserve energy. Since no paths in
\mathcal{A}
conserve energy, we know that
\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w)=\infty
for all
(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{A}
by construction. Due to exponential tightness in Proposition
1.1
, the rate function must be good; that is, the sublevel sets
\{(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E):\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(\mu_{\bullet},w)\leq a\}
are compact in
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
for any
a\in[0,\infty)
. Now, for any
a
,
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\leq a\}
is disjoint from
\mathcal{A}
, and since
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
is a normal topological space, there exists an open set
\mathcal{U}_{a}\supset\mathcal{A}
whose closure
\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}
is disjoint from
\{\widetilde{I}\leq a\}
. By hypothesis,

\begin{split}\limsup_{N\in S}\left[\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right)\right]&\leq-\inf\left\{\widetilde{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w):\mu_{\bullet}\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right\}\\
&\leq-a.\end{split}

(360)

This is inconsistent with (
27
,
30
) for
a
large enough, and we have the desired contradiction. ∎

Finally, we prove the result on entropy as a quasipotential.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

This follows from Theorem
1.2
using a contraction principle argument. Since we do not have a true large deviation principle, and must further compensate for the failure of the rate function
\mathcal{I}
to be good, the arguments do not follow from any statement of the contraction principle we have found in the literature, and we present the arguments in detail.

Let us fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
and
\epsilon>0
and consider

\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E):W(\mu_{T},\mu)<\epsilon\right\}

(361)

so that the closure is

\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}:=\left\{(\mu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E):W(\mu_{T},\mu)\leq\epsilon\right\}

(362)

Let us take
\mu_{0}^{\star}=\gamma
and, for each
N
, sample initial velocities independently from
\gamma
. In this case, the
N
-particle system is in equilibrium, so that the distribution of
\mu^{N}_{T}
is that of a
N
-particle independent sample from
\gamma
, and in particular, Sanov’s theorem applies, so that
\mu^{N}_{T}
satisfies a large deviation principle with rate function
H(\cdot|\gamma)
. We first prove the first item (
34
): by Sanov’s Theorem

\begin{split}\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right)&=\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left(W(\mu^{N}_{T},\mu)<\epsilon\right)\\
&\geq-\inf\{H(\nu|\gamma):W(\nu,\mu)<\epsilon\}\\
&\geq-H(\mu|\gamma)\end{split}

(363)

while, immediately

\begin{split}\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right)&\leq\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right).\end{split}

(364)

If
H(\mu|\gamma)=\infty
there is, of course, nothing to prove; otherwise, we choose
M>H(\mu|\gamma)
and using Proposition
1.1
i), pick a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
such that

\limsup_{N}N^{-1}\mathbb{P}((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K})\leq-M.

(365)

From (
364
) and applying Theorem
1.2
i),

\begin{split}&\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\max\left(\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K}\right),\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\max\left(-\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K})\right\},-M\right).\end{split}

(366)

Comparing against (
363
), we must have that

H(\mu|\gamma)\geq\min\left(\inf\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K}\},M\right)

(367)

and since
M>H(\mu|\gamma)
by construction,

H(\mu|\gamma)\geq\inf\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K}\}

(368)

We claim that the right-hand side converges as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
:

\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{K})\right\}\to\inf\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu,(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{K}\}.

(369)

It is immediate that the left-hand side is increasing as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
and that the right-hand side is an upper bound; it is therefore sufficient to prove convergence on a subsequence. For each
n
, pick
(\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{1/n}\cap\mathcal{K}
with error at most
1/n
from the infimum. Since
\mathcal{K}
is compact, we can pass to a subsequence with
(\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})\to(\nu_{\bullet},w)
; the limit has
\nu_{T}=\mu
and
(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{K}
. By lower-semicontinuity from Proposition
1.1
ii), we have

\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w)\leq\liminf_{n}\mathcal{I}(\nu^{(n)}_{\bullet},w^{(n)})\leq\liminf_{n}\left(\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{1/n}\cap\mathcal{K})\right\}+\frac{1}{n}\right)

(370)

so that

\inf\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu,(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{K}\}\leq\liminf_{n}\left(\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{1/n}\cap\mathcal{K})\right\}\right)

(371)

which proves the claim (
369
). Returning to (
368
), we take
\epsilon\to 0
to find

\begin{split}H(\mu|\gamma)&\geq\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu,(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{K}\right\}\\
&\geq\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu\right\}\end{split}

(372)

and observe that the right-hand side is exactly the claimed bound in (
34
). For the second item (
35
), we apply the lower bound of Sanov:

\begin{split}\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right)&=\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left(W(\mu^{N}_{T},\mu)\leq\epsilon\right)\\
&\leq-\inf\{H(\nu|\gamma):W(\nu,\mu)\leq\epsilon\}.\end{split}

(373)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\right)&\geq\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left((\mu^{N}_{\bullet},w^{N})\in\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\right)\\
&\geq-\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}\right\}\\
&\geq-\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu,(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{R}\right\}.\end{split}

(374)

We conclude that

\inf\{H(\nu|\gamma):W(\nu,\mu)\leq\epsilon\}\leq\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(\nu_{\bullet},w):\nu_{T}=\mu,(\nu_{\bullet},w)\in\mathcal{R}\right\}.

(375)

As
\epsilon\to 0
, the left-hand side converges to
H(\mu|\gamma)
by the lower semi-continuity of entropy (cf. Lemma
2.6
), and the right-hand side is exactly the right-hand side of (
35
), so we are done. ∎

Appendix A
Some Properties of Skorohod Paths

We will now recall some facts about right-continuous, left-limited (càdlàg) paths, and the resulting Skorohod topology. For a fixed metric space
(X,d)
and
T
, we write
D([0,T],(X,d))
for the set of all such functions
x_{\bullet}:[0,T]\to X
, which we equip with the metric

\rho(x_{\bullet},y_{\bullet})=\inf\left\{\max\left(\sup_{t\leq T}d(x(t),y(\iota(t))),\sup_{t\leq T}|t-\iota(t)|\right):\iota\in\Lambda\right\}

(376)

where the infimum runs over the set
\Lambda
of increasing, continuous bijections
\iota:[0,T]\to[0,T]
. We say that
x
has a jump of size at least
\epsilon>0
at
t
if
d(x(t),x(t-))\geq\epsilon
.

Our first result is a replacement for uniform continuity in the context of such paths.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
x_{\bullet}\in D([0,T],(X,d))
and fix
\epsilon>0
. Then

a).

There exists at most finitely many
t\in[0,T]
such that
d(x(t),x(t-))>\epsilon
.

b).

There exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
either
there exists
s\in[t,t+\delta)\cap[0,T]
with a jump discontinuity of size at least
d(x(s-),x(s))\geq\epsilon

or
, for all
s\in[t,t+\delta)\cap[0,T]
, we have
d(x(t),x(s))<\epsilon
.

Proof.

For the first item, suppose that we can find a countable sequence of distinct
t_{n}\in(0,T]
such that
d(x(t_{n}-),x(t_{n}))>\epsilon
, and up to passing to an infinite subsequence, we can also arrange that
t_{n}
converges monotonically, either increasingly or decreasingly, to a limit
t\in[0,T]
. We consider the two cases separately:

1.

If
t_{n}\uparrow t
, we can pick
s_{n}\in[t_{n}-n^{-1},t_{n}]
such that
d(x(s_{n}),x(t_{n}))>\epsilon
, which contradicts the fact that both
x(s_{n}),x(t_{n})\to x(t-)
by the left-limitedness.

2.

If
t_{n}\downarrow t
, we can pick
s_{n}\in(t,t_{n})
, still so that
d(x(s_{n}),x(t_{n}))>\epsilon
, and obtain the same contradiction by the convergence
x(t_{n}),x(s_{n})\to x(t)
by right-continuity.

In either case, we have a contradiction, so the claim is proven.

We now prove item b). Suppose, for a contradiction, that the conclusion is false, so that we can construct
t_{n},s_{n}
, with
t_{n}<s_{n}<t_{n}+n^{-1}
, such that there is no jump of size
\geq\epsilon
in
[t_{n},s_{n})
, but such that
d(x(t_{n}),x(s_{n}))\geq\epsilon
. As before, by passing to a infinite subsequence, we can arrange that either
t_{n}\downarrow t
or
t_{n}\uparrow t
. We again deal with the cases separately.

1.

If
t_{n}\uparrow t
, we split further into cases, depending on whether
s_{n}>t
infinitely often or not.

(a)

If
s_{n}>t
infinitely often, we can pass to a further subsequence so that
t_{n}\uparrow t,s_{n}\downarrow t
, so that
d(x(t_{n}),x(s_{n}))\to d(x(t-),x(t))
. Since
d(x(t_{n}),x(s_{n}))\geq\epsilon
for all
n
by construction, we conclude that there is a jump discontinuity of size
\geq\epsilon
at
t
, which contradicts the hypothesis that
[t_{n},s_{n})
contains no such jumps.

(b)

Otherwise,
s_{n}\leq t
eventually, so by passing to a subsequence,
t_{n},s_{n}\uparrow t
and
x(t_{n}),x(s_{n})\to x(t-)
, which is a contradiction in the usual way.

2.

If
t_{n}\downarrow t
, then
s_{n}\downarrow t
and
x(t_{n}),x(s_{n})\to x(t)
, contradicting that
d(x(t_{n}),x(s_{n}))\geq\epsilon
.

Since all possible cases lead to a contradiction, the claim is proven. ∎

We next classify some continuity properties for the Skorokhod convergence. These results are standard and included for completeness.

Proposition A.2
.

a).

The maps
x_{\bullet}\mapsto x(0)
,
x_{\bullet}\mapsto x(T)
are continuous with respect to the metric
\rho
.

b).

If
x^{n}_{\bullet}\in D([0,T],(X,d))
converge to
x_{\bullet}
with respect to
\rho
, then for all but countably many
t\in[0,T]
,
d(x^{n}(t),x(t))\to 0
.

c).

If, in b), the limit path
x_{\bullet}
is continuous, then we additionally have the uniform convergence
\sup_{t}d(x^{n}(t),x(t))\to 0
.

Proof.

For the first item, observe that
\iota(0)=0,\iota(T)=T
for all
\iota\in\Lambda
, which implies that
d(x(0),y(0))\leq\rho(x_{\bullet},y_{\bullet})
for all
x_{\bullet},y_{\bullet}
, and similarly at
T
. For the second item, from the previous proposition,
x_{\bullet}
is continuous at all but countably many
t\in[0,T]
. For points of continuity
t
of
x_{\bullet}
, fix
\epsilon>0
: there exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
s
with
|s-t|<\delta
,
|x(s)-x(t)|<\epsilon/2
. For all
n
sufficiently large, we have
\rho(x^{n}_{\bullet},x_{\bullet})<\min(\delta,\epsilon/2)
and so we can pick
\iota\in\Lambda
such that
\sup|t-\iota(t)|<\delta
and
\sup_{t}|x^{n}(\iota(t))-x(t)|<\epsilon/2
. We now conclude: we have
|t-\iota^{-1}(t)|<\delta
, and so

|x^{n}(t)-x(t)|\leq|x^{n}(t)-x(\iota^{-1}(t))|+|x(\iota^{-1}(t))-x(t)|<\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon

(377)

and we are done. The final item also follows, noting that as
x_{\bullet}
is continuous, it is uniformly continuous, which implies that
\delta
, and hence
n
, can be chosen independently of
t\in[0,T]
. ∎

Appendix B
A Singular Girsanov Theorem for Jump Processes

We now justify the changes of measure in Proposition
3.2
. We start from a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, on which is defined a Kac process
\mu^{N}_{\bullet}
its empirical flux
w^{N}_{t}
. We have a deterministic tilting
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of the initial data, such that
\int e^{\varphi(v)}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv)=1
, and
K:\mathcal{P}_{2}^{N}\times E\to[0,\infty)
be measurable, with a bound
K/(1+|v|+|v_{\star}|)\leq C
, for some absolute constant
C
. The modification of the dynamics with therefore be random, depending on the initial value
\mu^{N}_{0}
: our new measures are given by

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}=&\exp\bigg(N\langle\varphi,\mu^{N}_{0}\rangle+N\langle\log K(\mu^{N}_{0},\cdot),w^{N}_{T}\rangle\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-N\int_{E}(K-1)(\mu^{N}_{0},t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(dt,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\bigg)\end{split}

(378)

where, if
w^{N}
has any point with
K=0
, then the integral
\log K(\mu^{N}_{0},\cdot),w^{N}\rangle=-\infty
and the density is understood to be
0
.

We start from a disintegration of
\mathbb{P}
: let
L_{0}
be the law of
\mu^{N}_{0}
on
\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}
, and for any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}
, let
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
be the law of the Kac process started from
\nu
, so that, for any
A\in\mathfrak{F}
,

\mathbb{P}(A)=\int_{\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(A)L_{0}(d\nu).

(379)

We now write, again for any
A\in\mathfrak{F}
,

\mathbb{Q}(A)=\int_{\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[Z^{\delta,\nu}_{T}1_{A}\right]\left(e^{N\langle\varphi,\nu\rangle}L_{0}\right)(d\nu)=\int_{\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}(A)\widetilde{L}_{0}(d\nu)

(380)

where we define the modified law
\widetilde{L}_{0}
by

\widetilde{L}_{0}(d\nu)=e^{N\langle\varphi,\nu\rangle}L_{0}(d\nu)

(381)

and modify the conditional law
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
by

Z^{\nu}_{t}=\exp\left(N\langle\log K(\nu,\cdot),w^{N}_{t}\rangle-N\int_{E}1_{s\leq t}(K^{\delta}-1)(\nu,s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}}(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\right)

(382)

where we again set
Z^{\nu}_{t}=0
if there are any point with
K=0
, and
\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}=Z^{\nu}_{T}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
.

For the initial law, we can formally describe
L_{0}
as the pushforward of
(\mu_{0}^{\star})^{\otimes N}
by the map
\pi^{N}:(v_{1},..,v_{N})\to N^{-1}\sum\delta_{v_{i}}
, and that
\exp(N\langle\varphi,\nu\rangle)=\exp(\sum\varphi(v_{i}))
. It therefore follows that
\widetilde{L}_{0}
is the pushforward of the measure
\exp(\sum\varphi(v_{i}))\prod_{i}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv_{i})=\prod_{i}e^{\varphi(v_{i})}\mu_{0}^{\star}(dv_{i})
by
\pi^{N}
. Since
\varphi
was chosen so that
\int e^{\varphi}d\mu_{0}^{\star}=1
, each factor is a probability measure, and so
\widetilde{L}_{0}
is the probability measure on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{N}
for the empirical measure of sampling
N
particles independently from the probability measure
e^{\varphi}\mu_{0}^{\star}
, as claimed.

We now consider the modification of each
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
. We observe that the conservation of energy guarantees that there exists
M=M_{\nu}
such that,
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
-almost surely,
\mu^{N}_{t}
is supported on
[-M,M]^{d}
for all
t
, and
w^{N}
is supported on
(t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)\in E
with
|v|,|v_{\star}|\leq M
. In particular, thanks to the hypothesised bound, one finds the upper bound

\sup_{t\leq T}Z^{\nu}_{t}\leq\exp\left(CNM_{\nu}(1+w^{N}(E))\right)

(383)

and the right-hand side has all moments finite, since
Nw^{N}_{t}(E)
can be dominated by a Poisson process of rate
3(1+\langle|v|^{2},\nu\rangle)
, as in Section
2.1
. We now observe that, at collisions,
Z^{\nu}_{t}
changes by

Z^{\nu}_{t}-Z^{\delta,\nu}_{t-}=Z^{\nu}_{t-}\left(e^{\log K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)}-1\right)=Z^{\nu}_{t-}\left(K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)-1\right)

(384)

which is valid whether or not
K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)
, while in between collisions,
Z^{\nu}_{t}
is differentiable, with

\frac{d}{dt}Z^{\nu}_{t}=-N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(K-1)(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star}).

(385)

Together, we obtain

Z^{\nu}_{t}=Z^{\nu}_{0}+\int_{E}1_{s\leq t}NZ^{\nu}_{s-}\left(K(\nu,s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)-1\right)(w^{N}-\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}})(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)

(386)

which is a
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
-local martingale, and hence a true martingale using the upper bound (
383
), with constant mean
Z^{\nu}_{0}=1
. It follows that each
\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}
is a probability measure, and hence so is
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let us now describe the dynamics under each
\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}
. Let us fix a bounded, measurable function
F:\mathcal{P}^{2}_{N}\times\mathcal{P}^{2}_{N}\times\mathcal{M}(E)\to\mathbb{R}
, and let
A_{t}
be given by

\begin{split}A_{t}=&N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(F(\nu,\mu^{N,v,v_{\star},\sigma},w^{N,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma})-F(\nu,\mu^{N},w^{N})))\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\dots\times K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}(dv)\mu^{N}(dv_{\star})d\sigma.\end{split}

(387)

We now consider
Y^{\nu}_{t}:=Z^{\nu}_{t}(F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{t},w^{N}_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}ds)
. The changes at jumps are given by

Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\nu}_{t-}=Z^{\nu}_{t-}(K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)F(\nu,\mu^{N,v,v_{\star},\sigma}_{t-},w^{N,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma}_{t-})-F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{t-},w^{N}_{t-}))

(388)

while the drift between jumps is

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}Y^{\nu}_{t}=Z^{\nu}_{t-}\left(F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{t-},w^{N}_{t-})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}_{t}(dv)\mu^{N}_{t}(dv_{\star})d\sigma-A_{t}\right)\end{split}

(389)

and together we conclude that

\begin{split}Y^{\nu}_{t}-Y^{\nu}_{0}&=\int_{E}1_{s\leq t}(F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{s-}+\Delta(v,v_{\star},\sigma),w^{N}_{s-}+N^{-1}\delta_{(s,v,v_{\star},\sigma)})-F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{s-},w^{N}_{s-}))\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt....K(\nu,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)(w^{N}-\overline{m}_{\mu^{N}})(ds,dv,dv_{\star},d\sigma)\end{split}

(390)

which is again a
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
-martingale, using almost sure bound on the supports of
\overline{m}_{\mu},w^{N}
under
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
as commented above. It follows that
F(\nu,\mu^{N}_{t},w^{N}_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}ds
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}
-martingale, and we conclude that
(\mu^{N}_{0},\mu^{N}_{t},w^{N}_{t})
is a
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})
-Markov process with time-dependent generator

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{t}F(\nu^{\prime},\mu^{N},w^{N})=&N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(F(\nu^{\prime},\mu^{N,v,v_{\star},\sigma},w^{N,t,v,v_{\star},\sigma})-F(\nu^{\prime},\mu^{N},w^{N}))\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip 28.45274pt\dots\times K(\nu^{\prime},t,v,v_{\star},\sigma)B(v-v_{\star})\mu^{N}(dv)\mu^{N}(dv_{\star})d\sigma.\end{split}

(391)

Using the same boundedness arguments as before, this generator characterises a unique semigroup
P^{K}_{s,t}
of transition kernels on
\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}\times\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}\times\mathcal{M}(E)
, so that for any
0=t_{0}<t_{1}....<t_{n}
and Borel sets
A_{i}\subset\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}\times\mathcal{P}^{N}_{2}\times\mathcal{M}(E)

Abstract
. We describe the structure of the coefficient ring W ∗ ​ ( p ​ t ) = Ω W ∗ W^{*}(pt)=\varOmega_{W}^{*} of the c 1 c_{1} -spherical bordism theory for an arbitrary S ​ U SU -bilinear multiplication. We prove that for any S ​ U SU -bilinear multiplication the formal group of the theory W ∗ W^{*} is Landweber exact. Also we show that after inverting the set 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of Fermat primes there exists a complex orientation of the localized theory W ∗ ​ [ 𝒫 − 1 ] W^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}] such that the coefficients of the corresponding formal group law generate the whole coefficient ring Ω W ∗ ​ [ 𝒫 − 1 ] \varOmega_{W}^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}] .



Introduction

Special unitary bordism, or
SU
-bordism, is the bordism theory of stably complex manifolds with a special unitary structure, where an
SU
-structure on a manifold
M
is defined by a reduction of the structure group of the stable tangent bundle to the group
SU(N)
(or, equivalently, by a trivialization of the one-dimensional complex bundle
\det TM
). Details of the construction of the
SU
-bordism theory and a description of its coefficient ring
\varOmega^{SU}_{*}
can be found in
[
8
,
11
,
3
]
.

All known calculations of the coefficient ring
\varOmega^{SU}_{*}
involve the
c_{1}
-spherical bordism groups
\varOmega^{W}_{*}
, which sit between
\varOmega^{SU}_{*}
and complex bordism groups
\varOmega^{U}_{*}
(of manifolds with a stably complex structure).

Originally, the groups
\varOmega_{*}^{W}
appeared in the work of Conner and Floyd
[
5
]
as the kernel of a certain operation
\Delta
in complex bordism (see also
[
11
,
3
]
), and exactly in this form the groups
\varOmega_{*}^{W}
arise in the calculation of the special unitary bordism groups
\varOmega_{*}^{SU}
via the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence
[
8
,
3
]
.

The groups
\varOmega^{W}_{*}
are the coefficient groups of the
c_{1}
-spherical bordism theory
W_{*}
introduced by Stong
[
11
]
. Namely, a
c_{1}
-spherical stably complex structure on a manifold
M
is given by a reduction of the classifying map
M\to\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
of the bundle
\det TM
to a map to the “sphere”
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
. (Note that a stably complex structure is an
SU
-structure if and only if the bundle
\det TM
is trivial, that is, its classifying map is reduced to a map to the point “
\mathbb{C}P^{0}
”.) Details of the definition and properties of the theory
W_{*}
can be found in
[
11
,
3
,
4
]
.

Complex orientations of the theory
W^{*}
and the corresponding formal group laws were introduced in the work of V. M. Buchstaber
[
2
]
, and subsequently studied in detail in the work
[
4
]
. The theory
W^{*}
is a module over the
SU
-bordism theory, and all
SU
-bilinear multiplications on
W^{*}
were described in
[
4
]
. In this paper we describe the structure of the coefficient ring
W^{*}(pt)=\varOmega_{W}^{*}
for an arbitrary
SU
-bilinear multiplication (Theorem
2.1
), prove the Landweber exactness of the formal group law in the theory of
W^{*}
for an arbitrary
SU
-bilinear multiplication (Theorem
2.2
), and also show that after inverting of Fermat primes there exists a complex orientation for which the coefficients of the formal group law generate the whole localized ring
\varOmega_{W}^{*}
. The latter result was stated in
[
2
]
. We give a proof of this fact (see Theorem
2.4
).

1.
Preliminaries

Denote by
m_{k}
the greatest common divisor of the binomial coefficients
{k+1\choose i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Then the following well known equality holds

m_{k}=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $k+1=p^{s}$ for a prime $p$,}\\
1&\text{in other cases.}\end{cases}

Recall
[
7
]
that the coefficient ring of complex cobordism has the form
\varOmega_{U}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
,
|a_{k}|=-2k
, and the polynomial generators
a_{i}
are characterized by the property that their higher
s
-numbers are given by
s_{k}(a_{k})=\pm m_{k}
. We denote by
J
the ideal of elements of nonzero degree in the ring
\varOmega_{U}^{*}
. Thus,
J^{2}
is the ideal of decomposables.

The standard complex orientation of the complex cobordism theory
\mathit{MU}^{*}
corresponds to the formal group law
F(u,v)=u+v+\sum\alpha_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}
in complex cobordism. This formal group law is the universal formal group law in the sense of Lazard
[
9
]
, that is, for any formal group law
F_{R}
over any ring
R
there exists a unique ring homomorphism
\psi\colon\varOmega^{*}_{U}\to R
mapping the formal group law
F
to
F_{R}
. In this case we say that the homomorphism
\psi
classifies the formal group law
F_{R}
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
F_{R}(u,v)
be a graded formal group law over a non-positively graded ring
R
. Then there exist elements
r_{k}\in R^{-2k}
such that

F_{R}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}r_{k}\frac{((u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1})}{m_{k}}\mod J_{R}^{2},

where
J_{R}=R^{<0}
and
J_{R}^{2}
is the ideal of decomposables.

The
n
-th power series corresponding to a formal group law
F_{R}(u,v)
satisfies the equality

[n]_{R}(u)=nu+\sum r_{k}\frac{(nu)^{k+1}-nu^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod{J_{R}^{2}}.

Proof.

There is the following formula for the universal formal group law modulo decomposables:

F(u,v)=u+v+\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\frac{(u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod{J^{2}},

where
a_{k}
is a polynomial generator of the ring
\varOmega^{*}_{U}
with the
s
-number
s_{k}(a_{k})=-m_{k}
(see
[
1
]
). Then for the
n
th power we get

[n](u)=nu+\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\frac{(nu)^{k+1}-nu^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod{J^{2}}.

The required formulas are obtained by applying the homomorphism
\psi\colon\varOmega^{*}_{U}\to R^{*}
classifying
F_{R}
to the above and observing that
\psi(J^{2})\subset J_{R}^{2}
. Note that
r_{k}=\psi(a_{k})
.
∎

There is a natural inclusion
W^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathit{MU}^{*}
of the
c_{1}
-spherical cobordism theory
W^{*}
into complex cobordism. Furthermore,
W^{*}
splits as a direct summand by means of natural projections, for example, the classical Stong projection
\pi_{0}\colon\mathit{MU}^{*}\to W^{*}
. On the coefficients of the theories, the Stong projection sends the bordism class of a stably complex manifold
M
to the bordism class of the submanifold
N
in
M\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}
dual to the one-dimensional complex bundle
\det TM\otimes\mathcal{O}(1)
. Using the Stong projection, one can define a multiplication on
W^{*}
by the formula
a*b=\pi_{0}(ab)
(note that the subgroup
W^{*}
is not closed under the standard product
ab
in
\mathit{MU}^{*}
). We have

(1)

a*b=ab+2\alpha_{12}\partial a\partial b,

where
\alpha_{12}
is the corresponding coefficient of the formal group law in complex cobordism, and
\partial
is the operation in complex cobordism acting on the coefficients by sending the bordism class of a manifold
M
to the bordism class of the submanifold
N
dual to
\det TM
. For details, see
[
11
]
,
[
4
]
.

With respect to the multiplication
a*b=\pi_{0}(ab)
defined by the Stong projection
\pi_{0}
, the ring of coefficients of the theory
W^{*}
has the form

(2)

\varOmega_{W}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{k\geq 3}],\,|x_{k}|=-2k,

where the polynomial generators are characterized by the property that
s_{k}(x_{k})=\pm m_{k}m_{k-1}
, and can be chosen such that
\partial(x_{2i})=x_{2i-1}
for
i>1
(and hence
\partial(x_{2i-1})=0
)
[
11
, Chapter X]
,
[
3
, Theorem 6.10]
. Note that
x_{1}=a_{1}=\pm[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]
.

We have the forgetful homomorphism from
SU
-bordism to
c_{1}
-spherical bordism
\varOmega^{*}_{SU}\to\varOmega^{*}_{W}
. After inverting
2
this homomorphism becomes injective and, moreover,

(3)

\varOmega^{*}_{W}[1/2]=\varOmega^{*}_{SU}[1/2]\oplus x_{1}\varOmega^{*}_{SU}[1/2].

Furthermore,

(4)

\varOmega^{*}_{SU}[1/2]=\mathbb{Z}[1/2][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots].

Here one can take
y_{2}=x_{1}*x_{1}
and
y_{i}=x_{i}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}\partial(x_{i})
for
i>2
(see
[
11
]
,
[
3
]
).
As a result, we get

\varOmega^{*}_{W}[1/2]=\mathbb{Z}[1/2][x_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*x_{1}=y_{2}).

Recall that for a given (graded) formal group law
F_{R}
over a graded ring
R_{*}
and a fixed prime number
p
, the coefficient for
u^{p^{n}}
in the power series
[p]_{F_{R}}(u)
is traditionally denoted by
v_{n}
(this notation does not reflect the prime
p
). Then we have the following

Theorem 1.2

(Landweber Exact Functor Theorem
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose that for any prime number
p
the sequence
(p,v_{1},v_{2},\ldots)
is regular in the ring
R_{*}
, that is, for any
n
multiplication by the element
v_{n}
is injective in the quotient ring
R_{*}/(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})
(including the multiplication by
p
in
R_{*}
). Then the functor
\mathit{MU}_{*}(-)\otimes_{\varOmega^{U}_{*}}R_{*}
is exact, so it defines a homology theory. Here the structure of an
\varOmega^{U}_{*}
-module on
R_{*}
is given by the ring homomorphism
\varOmega^{U}_{*}\to R_{*}
classifying the given formal group law
F_{R}
. Similarly, the formula
\mathit{MU}^{*}(-)\otimes_{\varOmega_{U}^{*}}R^{*}
defines a ring cohomology theory on finite cell complexes.

A formal group law
F_{R}
satisfying the conditions of the theorem above is called
Landweber exact
.

Corollary 1.3
.

If the formal group law corresponding to a complex-oriented theory
h^{*}
is Landweber exact, then there is a natural isomorphism of multiplicative cohomology theories
h^{*}(-)\simeq\mathit{MU}^{*}(-)\otimes_{\varOmega_{U}^{*}}h^{*}(pt)
on finite cell complexes, and an isomorphism of the corresponding homology theories.

The following fact is well known.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two formal group laws over a ring
R
, and let
g\colon F_{1}\to F_{2}
be an isomorphism between them given by a power series
g(u)=\lambda u+\cdots\in R[[u]],
where
\lambda\in R^{\times}
,
F_{2}(u,v)=g(F_{1}(g^{-1}(u),g^{-1}(v)))
and
[p]_{2}(u)=g([p]_{1}(g^{-1}(u)))
. Denote by
v_{n}
and
v_{n}^{\prime}
the coefficients for
u^{p^{n}}
in the series
[p]_{1}(u)
and
[p]_{2}(u)
, respectively.

Then
v^{\prime}_{n}=sv_{n}\mod{(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})},\,s\in R^{\times}
. In particular, the ideal
(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
depends only on the isomorphism class of the formal group law.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. If
n=0
, the ideal in question is
(p)
and the statement is trivial. Let us prove that the equality
(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})=(p,v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n-1})
implies that
v^{\prime}_{n}=sv_{n}\mod{(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})},\,s\in R^{\times}
.

It is known that if for a formal group law
F_{R}
the equalities
0=p=\cdots=v_{n-1}
hold in
R
, then
[p]_{R}(u)=\varphi(u^{p^{n}}),\,
where
\varphi(u)=v_{n}u+\cdots
is a power series in
u
(see, for example,
[
10
, Lemma A2.2.6]
).

Therefore, reducing the formal group laws modulo
(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})
we obtain
[p]_{1}(u)=v_{n}u^{p^{n}}+\cdots
and
[p]_{2}(u)=v^{\prime}_{n}u^{p^{n}}+\cdots
. On the other hand,
[p]_{2}(u)=g([p]_{1}(g^{-1}(u)))=\lambda\lambda^{-p^{n}}v_{n}u^{p^{n}}+\cdots
. Thus,
v^{\prime}_{n}=\lambda^{1-p^{n}}v_{n}\mod{(p,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})}
.
∎

Corollary 1.5
.

The Landweber exactness of the formal group law corresponding to a complex-oriented theory does not depend on a complex orientation.

2.
Main results

The theory
W^{*}
is a module over the
SU
-bordism theory
MSU^{*}
. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.21]
, an arbitrary
SU
-bilinear multiplication on
W^{*}
has the form

(5)

a\tilde{*}b=ab+(2\alpha_{12}+w)\partial a\partial b,

where
w
is an arbitrary element of
\varOmega^{-4}_{W}
.
For the standard multiplication
a*b=\pi_{0}(ab)
given by the Stong projection
\pi_{0}
, we have
w=0
. If the multiplication
a\tilde{*}b=\pi(ab)
is defined by an arbitrary
SU
-linear projection
\pi\colon\mathit{MU}^{*}\to W^{*}
, then the element
w
is divided by
2
.

By (
2
) we have
\varOmega^{-4}_{W}=\mathbb{Z}\langle x_{1}*x_{1}\rangle
, hence the element
w
in (
5
) has the form
qx_{1}*x_{1}
,
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then an
SU
-bilinear multiplication takes the form

(6)

a*_{q}b=ab+(2\alpha_{12}+qx_{1}*x_{1})\partial a\partial b=a*b+qx_{1}*x_{1}\partial a\partial b.

In this notation, the standard multiplication is
a*_{0}b
.

Theorem 2.1
.

With respect to the multiplication
*_{q}
, the ring
\varOmega^{*}_{W}
has the form

(\varOmega^{*}_{W},*_{q})=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2}).

In particular, for any
SU
-bilinear multiplication of
a*_{q}b
, except for the standard one
a*b=ab+2\alpha_{12}\partial a\partial b
(defined by the Stong projection
\pi_{0}
), the ring
\varOmega^{*}_{W}
is not polynomial.

Proof.

Consider the elements
x_{i}\in\varOmega^{*}_{W},\,i\neq 2
from (
2
) with
x_{1}=[\mathbb{C}P^{1}]
and set
x_{2}=x_{1}*x_{1}
. Then (
6
) implies
x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=x_{1}*x_{1}+4qx_{1}*x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2}
, since
\partial x_{1}=2
. Therefore, we have a ring homomorphism

(7)

\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2})\to(\varOmega^{*}_{W},*_{q}).

We claim that this is an isomorphism. To see this, first note that there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2})=\mathbb{Z}[x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]\oplus x_{1}\mathbb{Z}[x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]

Since the rings in (
7
) are graded, and the graded components are free abelian group of finite rank, it suffices to prove that
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}/(2)
and
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
are isomorphisms.

1)
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}/(2)
is an isomorphism

By reducing modulo
2
we obtain

\mathbb{Z}/(2)[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2})=\mathbb{Z}/(2)[x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},\ldots]

Consider the filtrations

\mathbb{Z}/(2)[x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},\ldots]\supset(x_{1})\supset(x_{1}^{2})\supset\cdots

and

(\varOmega^{*}_{W}/(2),*_{q})\supset x_{1}*_{q}\varOmega^{*}_{W}/(2)\supset x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}*_{q}\varOmega^{*}_{W}/(2)\supset\cdots

The homomorphism
\varphi
preserves these filtrations, and therefore it is sufficient to prove that it induces an isomorphism on the associated quotients.

The quotient
(x_{1}^{n})/(x_{1}^{n+1})
has a basis consisting of monomials
x_{1}^{n}x_{3}^{i_{3}}x_{4}^{i_{4}}\cdots
.

Since
\partial(x_{1})=2
, it follows from (
6
) that
x_{1}*_{q}a=x_{1}*a
in the ring
(\varOmega^{*}_{W}/(2),*_{q})
. Therefore, the ideal in
\varOmega^{*}_{W}
generated by the
n
th power
x_{1}^{*_{q}n}
with respect to the multiplication
*_{q}
coincides with the ideal generated by the
n
th power
x_{1}^{*n}
with respect to the standard multiplication. By virtue of formula (
2
) the quotient
x_{1}^{*_{q}(n+1)}*_{q}\varOmega^{*}_{W}/x_{1}^{*_{q}n}*_{q}\varOmega^{*}_{W}=x_{1}^{*(n+1)}*\varOmega^{*}_{W}/x_{1}^{*n}*\varOmega^{*}_{W}
has a basis consisting of monomials
x_{1}^{*n}*x_{3}^{*i_{3}}*x_{4}^{*i_{4}}\cdots
. In this quotient, the identity
x_{1}^{*_{q}n}*_{q}x_{3}^{*_{q}i_{3}}*_{q}x_{4}^{*_{q}i_{4}}\cdots=x_{1}^{*n}*x_{3}^{*i_{3}}*x_{4}^{*i_{4}}\cdots
holds, by formula (
6
). Since the homomorphism
\varphi
takes
x_{1}^{n}x_{3}^{i_{3}}x_{4}^{i_{4}}\cdots
to
x_{1}^{*_{q}n}*_{q}x_{3}^{*_{q}i_{3}}*_{q}x_{4}^{*_{q}i_{4}}\cdots
, it is an isomorphism on the associated quotients of filtrations.

2)
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
is an isomorphism

Formulas (
3
) and (
4
) imply that

\varOmega^{*}_{W}[1/2]=\mathbb{Z}[1/2][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]\oplus x_{1}\mathbb{Z}[1/2][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots],

where
\mathbb{Z}[1/2][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]=\varOmega^{*}_{SU}[1/2]
,
y_{2}=x_{1}*x_{1}
,
y_{2i-1}=x_{2i-1}
and
y_{2i}=x_{2i}-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}y_{2i-1}
for
i>1
. Furthermore, since
\partial
vanishes on
\varOmega^{*}_{SU}[1/2]
, the multiplication
*_{q}
is given by

(a_{1}+x_{1}b_{1})*_{q}(a_{2}+x_{1}b_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}+x_{1}(a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}a_{2})+(4q+1)y_{2}b_{1}b_{2}

for
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[1/2][y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]
.
Hence, there is an isomorphism

(\varOmega^{*}_{W}[1/2],*_{q})=\mathbb{Z}[1/2][x_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)y_{2}).

Therefore, the homomorphism
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
has the form

\mathbb{Z}[1/2][x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}[1/2][x_{1},y_{2},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)y_{2}),

where
x_{1}\mapsto x_{1}
,
x_{2}\mapsto y_{2}
,
x_{2i-1}\mapsto y_{2i-1}
and
x_{2i}\mapsto y_{2i}+\frac{1}{2}x_{1}y_{2i-1}
with
i>1
. It follows that
\varphi\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
is an isomorphism.
∎

The projection
\pi_{0}
defines a complex orientation
w=\pi_{0}(u)
on

, where

is the standard orientation of

. The complex orientation and multiplication

Theorem 2.2
.

The formal group law

over the ring

is Landweber exact.

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
SU
-bilinear multiplication on the theory

, there is a natural isomorphism
W_{*}(-)=\mathit{MU}_{*}(-)\otimes_{\varOmega^{U}_{*}}\varOmega^{W}_{*}
and a ring isomorphism

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

By Theorem
2.1
we have

(\varOmega^{*}_{W},*_{q})=Z[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2}).

Denote by
J_{W}
the ideal of elements of nonzero degree in
\varOmega^{*}_{W}
and by
J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}\subset\varOmega_{W}^{*}
the ideal of decomposable elements in the ring
(\varOmega_{W}^{*},*_{q})
. By
*
we denote the standard multiplication
*_{0}
defined by the Stong projection.

If we consider the groups
\varOmega_{W}^{*}
as subgroups in
\varOmega_{U}^{*}
, then by
[
4
, Lemma 3.8, Lemma 3.11]
we have the following equation

F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}w_{k}\frac{((u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1})}{m_{k}}\mod{J^{2}},

where

(8)

w_{1}=-x_{1},\quad w_{2}=-2qx_{1}*x_{1}\mod{J^{2}},\\
w_{k}=a_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1))\mod{J^{2}}\quad\text{ for }k>2,

and
a_{k}
is a polynomial generator of the ring
\varOmega^{*}_{U}
with
s_{k}(a_{k})=-m_{k}
.

It is clear that
w_{1}=\omega_{11}
. Moreover, by formula (
2
) we get that
\varOmega_{W}^{-4}=\mathbb{Z}\langle x_{1}*x_{1}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathbb{C}P^{1}*\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rangle
. Now (
1
) implies
\mathbb{C}P^{1}*\mathbb{C}P^{1}=9(\mathbb{C}P^{1})^{2}-8\mathbb{C}P^{2}
in
\varOmega_{U}^{*}
. Hence, there is an indecomposable part in the generator of
\varOmega_{W}^{-4}
. It follows that
J^{2}\cap\varOmega^{-4}_{W}=0
and therefore
w_{2}=-2qx_{1}*x_{1}
. Hence,
\omega_{12}=-2qx_{1}*x_{1}=-2qx_{2}
.

Expanding the
n

F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\omega_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}
up to the third term we get

[n]_{W}(u)=nu+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\omega_{11}u^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{3}\omega_{12}+\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}\omega_{11}^{2}\Bigr)u^{3}+\cdots

Substituting
\omega_{11}=-x_{1}
and
\omega_{12}=-2qx_{2}
here we obtain

(9)

[n]_{W}(u)=nu+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x_{1}u^{2}\\
+\Bigl(-2q\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{3}x_{2}+\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}\Bigr)u^{3}+\cdots\\
=nu+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x_{1}u^{2}+\Bigl((4q+1)\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}-2q\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{3}\Bigr)x_{2}u^{3}+\cdots

Next we calculate the higher terms in the expansion of

[n]_{W}(u)
modulo decomposables in
(\varOmega^{W}_{*},*_{q})
. By Lemma
1.1
,

F_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}r_{k}\frac{((u+v)^{k+1}-u^{k+1}-v^{k+1})}{m_{k}}\mod J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}

for some
r_{k}\in\varOmega_{W}^{-2k}
.
Since in dimensions
>4
elements from
J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}
are decomposable in
\varOmega^{*}_{U}
, by formula (
8
) we have
s_{k}(r_{k})=s_{k}(w_{k})=s_{k}(a_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)))=-m_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1))
for
k>2
.
On the other hand, by Theorem
2.1
we have
r_{k}=\lambda_{k}x_{k}\mod J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}
for
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
-m_{k}(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1))=s_{k}(r_{k})=\lambda_{k}s_{k}(x_{k})=\lambda_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}
. As a result, we obtain that
r_{k}=-\frac{1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)}{m_{k-1}}x_{k}\mod J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}
(one can check that the coefficient is always an integer).

For the
n
th power, Lemma
1.1
gives

[n]_{W}(u)=nu+\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}r_{k}\frac{(nu)^{k+1}-nu^{k+1}}{m_{k}}\mod{J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}}.

Combining this with the formula (
9
), we obtain that the coefficient for
u^{k+1}
in the series
[n]_{W}(u)
is

(10)

\begin{cases}\hskip 86.11084ptn&\text{for $k=0$,}\\
\hskip 73.19421pt-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x_{1}&\text{for $k=1$},\\
\hskip 4.30554pt\bigl((4q+1)\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}-2q\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{3}\bigr)x_{2}&\text{for $k=2$,}\\
-\frac{(n^{k+1}-n)}{m_{k}}\frac{(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1))}{m_{k-1}}x_{k}\mod{J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}}&\text{for $k>2$.}\end{cases}

Note that substituting
k=1
in the expression for
k>2
in (
10
) gives
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x_{1}
, which coincides with the formula for
k=1
up to a sign.

Now we can check the regularity of the sequence
(p,v_{1},\ldots)
from the statement of Landweber’s theorem.

Fix a prime
p
. Then for the elements
v_{n}
, except for the case
v_{1}
for
p=3
, we obtain from (
10
) that

v_{n}=\pm\frac{(p^{p^{n}}-p)}{p}\frac{(1\pm p^{n})}{m_{p^{n}-2}}x_{p^{n}-1}\mod{J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}}=\varepsilon_{n}x_{p^{n}-1}\mod{J_{W}*_{q}J_{W}},

where
\varepsilon_{n}\not\equiv 0\mod p
.
This implies the regularity of the sequence
(p,v_{1},\ldots)
for
p\neq 3
.

For the case of
p=3
, we note that
v_{1}
is a coefficient for
u^{3}
in
[3]_{W}(u)
, that is,
(4q+1-16q)x_{2}=(1-12q)x_{2}\equiv x_{2}\mod 3
.
Thus, the sequence
(3,v_{1},\ldots)
is also regular.
∎

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes of the form
p=2^{k}+1
(Fermat primes) greater than
3
. Consider the theory
W^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
obtained from
(W^{*},*_{q})
by inverting all
p\in\mathcal{P}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For the theory
W^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
, there exists a complex orientation such that the coefficients of the corresponding formal group law generate the whole ring
(\varOmega_{W}^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}],*_{q})
(as a
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
-algebra).

Theorem
2.4
is stated in
[
2
]
for the standard multiplication given by the Stong projection. Below we present its proof based on the results obtained in
[
4
]
.

Proof.

We consider the theory
(W^{*},*_{q})
with an arbitrary complex orientation. By Theorem
2.1
we have
(\varOmega^{*}_{W},*_{q})=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(x_{1}*_{q}x_{1}=(4q+1)x_{2})
. By
[
4
, Lemma 3.8]
, for the formal group law
\widetilde{F}_{W}(u,v)=u+v+\sum\widetilde{\omega}_{ij}*_{q}u^{*_{q}i}*v^{*_{q}j}
defined by a complex orientation of
W^{*}
, we have
\widetilde{\omega}_{11}=(2l+1)x_{1}
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{12}=3w_{2}-2qx_{2}
for
w_{2}\in\varOmega^{-4}_{W}
, where
l
and
w_{2}
are defined by the complex orientation and can take arbitrary values. Therefore, choosing an orientation such that
l=0,\,w_{2}=0
, we get
x_{1}=\widetilde{\omega}_{11},\,x_{2}=\widetilde{\omega}_{11}*_{q}\widetilde{\omega}_{11}+2\widetilde{\omega}_{12}
.

It remains to prove that for some orientation the generators
x_{k}
,
k>2
, can be expressed via the coefficients of
\widetilde{F}_{W}
. To do this, it is necessary and sufficient to show that there exists an integral linear combination of
\widetilde{\omega}_{ij},\,i+j=k+1>3
, with the
s
-number equal to
s_{k}(x_{k})=m_{k}m_{k-1}
up to elements invertible in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
. By
[
4
, Lemma 3.11]
we have

(11)

\gcd\bigl\{s_{i+j-1}(\widetilde{\omega}_{ij})\mid i+j=k+1\bigr\}=m_{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}\bigr),\quad k>2,

where
c_{k}
can be arbitrary integers depending on the orientation.

The same argument as in
[
4
, Lemma 3.11]
shows that for formal group laws over the localized rings
W^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
the equality (
11
) holds with
c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
.

Thus, to prove the theorem
2.4
it is necessary and sufficient to prove that for every
k>2
there exists
c_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
such that

m_{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}\bigr)=\varepsilon_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1},

where
\varepsilon_{k}
is an invertible element of the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
, or equivalently

(12)

1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}=\varepsilon_{k}m_{k-1}.

Let us show that for
k\neq 8
the number
c_{k}
can be chosen so that equality (
12
) holds with
\varepsilon_{k}=1
. In this case

c_{k}=\frac{m_{k-1}-1-(-1)^{k}(k+1)}{m_{k}m_{k-1}}

and we need to show that
c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
.

If
m_{k-1}=1
then
c_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k+1}{m_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
since
m_{k}
always divides
k+1
.

If
m_{k-1}=p
is an odd prime, then
k=p^{s}
. Hence
m_{k}=1
or
2
. Then
c_{k}=\frac{p-1+p^{s}+1}{pm_{k}}=\frac{p^{s-1}+1}{m_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
p^{s-1}+1
is even.

If
m_{k-1}=2
, then
k=2^{\ell}
and
c_{k}=\frac{2-1-2^{\ell}-1}{2m_{k}}=-\frac{1}{m_{k}}2^{\ell-1}
. In this case, either
m_{k}=1
, in which case
c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
, or
m_{k}=p>2
. In the latter case, we have that
k+1=2^{\ell}+1=p^{s}
. Then either
k=8=2^{3}=3^{2}-1
or
\ell=2^{n}
and
s=1
(see, for example,
[
4
, Lemma 3.15]
). In the latter case, we have
p=2^{2^{n}}+1
is a Fermat prime, and
p=k+1>3
, and hence
p\in\mathcal{P}
. Then
c_{k}=-\frac{1}{p}2^{2^{n}-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
.

It remains to consider the case
k=8
. Then (
12
) becomes

1+9+6c_{k}=2\varepsilon_{k}.

Setting
c_{k}=0
, we see that the equality holds for
\varepsilon_{k}=5\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Remark
.

In
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
it is proved that the ring (
\varOmega_{W}^{*}[1/2],*_{q})
is generated by the coefficients of the formal group law
F_{W}
for some orientation of the theory
W^{*}
. On the other hand, the statement of Theorem
2.4
is only about orientations of the localized theory, which corresponds to non-integer values of
c_{k}
. The author doesn’t know if it is possible to generate the localized ring
\varOmega_{W}^{*}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
by the coefficients of the formal group law corresponding to the “integral” orientation of the theory
W^{*}
, that is, if there exist integers
c_{k}
such that
1+(-1)^{k}(k+1)+c_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}=\varepsilon_{k}m_{k-1}
for invertible elements
\varepsilon_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}^{-1}]
.

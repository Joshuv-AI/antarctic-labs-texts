Permutation Inequalities for Walks in Graphs Work done in part while the first author was with University of Konstanz; material is based upon the first author’s bachelor’s thesis [ 19 ] .
Nadja Willenborg, Sven Kosub

Abstract
Using spectral graph theory, we show how to obtain inequalities for the number of walks in graphs from nonnegative polynomials and present a new family of such inequalities.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of nonnegative integers,
[n]
the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
and
a,b,c,k,\ell,p\in\mathbb{N}
.
By
w_{k}(G)
we denote the number of walks of length
k
(counted in terms of edges) in a graph
G
.
As we will always consider the class of all simple and undirected graphs, we simply write
w_{k}
throughout.

Counting walks in graphs has been studied for a long time.
In particular, pure binomial inequalities involving walk numbers, i.e., inequalities of the form

w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}}\leq w_{\beta_{1}}\cdots w_{\beta_{n}}
, for
\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
are useful for many applications in graph theory (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
for an extensive discussion).
The prominent inequality
w_{1}^{k}\leq w_{0}^{k-1}w_{k}
was established by Erdős and Simonovits in
[
6
]

and is based on results in
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
.
Lagarias
et al.
showed in
[
11
]
that
w_{2a+b}w_{b}\leq w_{0}w_{2(a+b)}
.
Defining a suitable scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{|V|}
and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, Dress and Gutman
showed that
w_{a+b}^{2}\leq w_{2a}w_{2b}

[
4
]
.
All these inequalities were generalized by Täubig
et al.
in
[
18
]
.
Using spectral graph theory, they obtained the following two types of inequalities:

w_{2a+c}w_{2(a+b)+c}\leq w_{2a}w_{2(a+b+c)}

(1)

and

w_{2\ell+p}^{k}\leq w_{2\ell+pk}w_{2\ell}^{k-1}.

(2)

Most recently, in
[
2
]
, Blekherman and Raymond determined the convex cone of index vectors
\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
of valid pure binomial inequalities involving walk numbers, among others. Moreover, their results show that all these inequalities can be derived by a finite combination of inequalities defining the tropicalization. They also verified this for the two types of inequalities given in
[
18
]
.

In this note, we present a natural family of inequalities involving walk numbers

w_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{\sigma(n)}}\leq w_{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{2\alpha_{n}},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
, which can be obtained in a very simple way via positive semidefinite forms.

2
Preliminaries

For basic concepts about polynomials, the reader may consult, e.g.,
[
14
]
.

A multivariate polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is called
form (or homogeneous polynomial)

iff all monomials in
f
have same degree.
A form
f
is
positive semidefinite (psd)
iff it takes only nonnegative values.
We will often use
0\leq f
as a shorthand for nonnegative polynomials.
Note that a psd form must be of even degree, since if
f
is a form of odd degree
d
then by homogeneity

f(-\bm{x})=(-1)^{d}f(\bm{x})
, which is nonnegative only if
f
is the zero form.
The set of all forms of degree
2d
in
n
variables is a cone, denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{n,2d}
.
A form
f
is called a
sum of squares (sos)
iff there exist other forms
h_{j}
such that

f=h_{1}^{2}+\dots+h_{k}^{2}
.
The cone of degree-
2d
sos forms in
n
variables is denoted by
\Sigma_{n,2d}
.
Obviously, it holds that
\Sigma_{n,2d}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n,2d}
.

Hilbert proved that every degree-
2d
psd form in
n
variables is sos if and only if
n=2
or
d=1
or
(n,2d)=(3,4)
(cf.
[
9
]
;
see also
[
7
]
for an extension to even symmetric forms).
In
[
10
]
, Hurwitz gave an explicit representation of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{2d}x_{i}^{2d}-2d\prod_{i=1}^{2d}x_{i}
as a sum of squares.
Later, in
[
15
]
, Reznick generalized this to the following result (in a slightly different form).

Lemma 1
.

[
15
, Theorem 2.6]

Let
f(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2d}-2dx_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
be a form such that

\mathbf{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\mathbf{\alpha}|=2d
. Then,
f
is sos.

Given any point set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
convex hull
of
X
is the set

\text{conv}(X):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}:m\in\mathbb{N},\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},x_{i}\in X,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\right\}

which is the smallest convex set
C
such that
X\subseteq C
.
A convex hull of a finite set is called a
polytope
.
If
P
is a polytope then a point
x\in P
is a
vertex
(respectively
extreme point
) of
P
iff there are no
y,z\in P
such that

y\neq z
and
x=(y+z)/2
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be such that
f(\bm{x})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}},c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the finite set
\textnormal{supp}(f)=_{\rm def}\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
is called the
support
of
f
and
its convex hull
N(f)=_{\rm def}\textnormal{conv}(\textnormal{supp}(f))\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called the
Newton polytope
of
f
.
Furthermore, we say that
c_{\alpha}
is a
vertex coefficient
of
f
if there exists a vertex
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of
N(f)
such that
c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
is a term of
f
.
It is known that every vertex of the Newton polytope of a polynomial lies in the support of the polynomial.
Therefore, its vertex coefficients are always
\neq 0
.
(For more details on the Newton polytope and its geometric interpretation see, e.g.,
[
16
]
, Section 2.4.)

The following necessary condition for the nonnegativity of a polynomial over the reals will give us a necessary condition for the validity of walk inequalities.

Lemma 2
.

[
5
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
N(f)
be its Newton polytope. Then all vertex coefficients of
f
are positive and the vertices of
N(f)
are even.

We use the following folklore notion when considering walk inequalities in graphs.

Definition 3
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial of degree
d
.
The
symmetrization
of
f
is defined as

f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\bm{x})=_{\rm def}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}f(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)}).

Obviously, the nonnegativity of a polynomial remains invariant under its symmetrization. Note that generally, the reverse is not true.
For example, consider
f(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}
, a form that is not positive semidefinite.
However, its symmetrization
f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-2x_{1}x_{2}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}
is positive semidefinite (see also Lemma
1
).

3
Inequalities for walks via nonnegative polynomials

For basic notions of algebraic graph theory, the reader may consult, e.g.,
[
3
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with
n
nodes,
m
edges, and adjacency matrix
A=A(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
Since
A
is symmetric, all eigenvalues of
A
are real and
A
is diagonalizable by an orthogonal matrix
U
;
so, the adjacency matrix can be written as
A(G)=UDU^{T}
, where
D=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is
the diagonal matrix of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
and the columns of
U
are formed by
an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.

We study (the number of) walks, i.e., sequences of nodes, where each pair of consecutive nodes is connected by an edge. Nodes and edges can be used repeatedly in the same walk. The length
k
of a walk is counted in terms of edges. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in V
, we denote by
w_{k}(x,y)
the number of walks of length
k
starting at node
x
and ending at node
y
. By
w_{k}(x)=\sum_{y\in V}w_{k}(x,y)
we denote the number of all walks of length
k
that start at node
x
. Consequently,
w_{k}=\sum_{x\in V}w_{k}(x)
denotes the total number of walks of length
k
. For each node
v
we have
w_{0}(v)=1
and
w_{1}(v)=d_{v}
, where
d_{v}
is the degree of
v
. This implies that
w_{0}=n
and
w_{1}=2m
.

The total number of walks of length
k
is equal to the sum of all entries of the matrix power
A(G)^{k}=UD^{k}U^{T}
(cf.
[
8
,
4
]
). Hence,

w_{k}=\mathbf{1}_{n}^{T}A(G)^{k}\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{n}^{T}UD^{k}U^{T}\mathbf{1}_{n},

where
\mathbf{1}_{n}
is the all-one vector. If we let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
be the coordinates of
\mathbf{1}_{n}
with respect to the orthonormal basis of
n
eigenvectors this number is given by

w_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{k}\mu_{j}^{2}.

(3)

From Eq. (
3
) we get the following relationship between symmetrization and the number of walks in graphs.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph,
|V|=n
.
Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of adjacency matrix
A
(listed according to their multiplicities).
Let
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
be the coordinates of
\mathbf{1}_{n}
with respect to an orthonormal basis of
n
eigenvectors.
Consider a multivariate polynomial

f(\bm{x})=_{\rm def}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}\cdot x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}},

where
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{\alpha}=0
for almost all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.Then, it holds that

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}\cdot w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\ w_{\alpha_{k}}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq n}^{n}\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\cdot f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\lambda_{i_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}})\cdot\mu_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdots\mu_{i_{k}}^{2}

for some
\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})>0
.

Proof.

Applying Eq. (
3
) and rearranging terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}\cdot w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\ w_{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}\lambda_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\mu_{i_{1}}^{2}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{n}\lambda_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}\mu_{i_{k}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n}f(\lambda_{i_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}})\cdot\mu_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdots\mu_{i_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq n}\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}f\bigl(\lambda_{i_{\sigma(1)}},\dots,\lambda_{i_{\sigma(k)}}\bigr)\right)\mu_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdots\mu_{i_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq n}\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\cdot f_{\rm sym}(\lambda_{i_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}})\cdot\mu_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdots\mu_{i_{k}}^{2}

Here,
\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})^{-1}
is the number of permutations
\sigma\in S_{k}
that leave the tuple
(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})
unchanged.
The exact value of
\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})^{-1}
is not important for our purposes; but notice that
1\leq\gamma(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})^{-1}\leq k!
.
This shows the proposition.
∎

Immediately, this gives a sufficient condition for verifying walk inequalities on graphs.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f(\textbf{x})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
be a polynomial such that

c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{\alpha}=0
for almost all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.
If
f_{\textnormal{sym}}
is nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
then the following inequality holds in all graphs

0\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}c_{\alpha}\cdot w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\ w_{\alpha_{k}}.

(4)

Example 6
.

If
f
is a univariate polynomial then
f=f_{\rm sym}
.
Consider the even polynomial
f(x)=x^{2k}-a_{1}x^{2k-1}-\cdots-a_{2k-1}x
, where all numbers
a_{1},\dots,a_{2k-1}
are nonnegative and at least one of them is nonzero. Then,
f
has root
0
and a single positive root
p
(note that
f
has exactly one change of signs of the non-vanishing coefficients).
Since
f(x)\leq f(-x)
for all
x\geq 0
, there is a unique
0<\gamma_{0}<p
such that
f(\gamma_{0})<0
and
f(x)\geq f(\gamma_{0})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,
f(x)-f(\gamma_{0})
is nonnegative.
Corollary
5
implies that

w_{2k}-f(\gamma_{0})w_{0}\geq a_{1}w_{2k-1}+\cdots+a_{2k-1}w_{1}

(5)

for all graphs.
Here, using the Grace-Heawood theorem,
|f(\gamma_{0})|
can be roughly bounded by

|f(\gamma_{0})|\leq(p-\gamma_{0})|h(z)|\leq\frac{p}{2}(1+\cot(\pi/2k))|h(z)|

where
h(x)=f(x)/(x-p)
is a polynomial of degree
2k-1
,

z\in(\gamma_{0},p)
satisfies
f^{\prime}(z)=-f(\gamma_{0})/(p-\gamma_{0})
, and
p\leq 1+\max\{a_{1},\dots,a_{2k-1}\}
.
Inequalities similar to Ineq. (
5
) can be easily obtained for polynomials where
a_{j}<0
for some
j
.

Proposition 7
.

Assume that
w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{k}}\leq w_{\beta_{1}}\cdots w_{\beta_{k}}
is valid in all graphs.
If this inequality can be verified by Corollary
5
, i.e., the symmetrization of
f(\textbf{x})=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\beta_{k}}-x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
is nonnegative,
then
\beta_{i}\equiv 0\mod 2
for all
i\in[k].

Proof.

Since
0\leq f_{\textnormal{sym}}
we get by Lemma
2
that the coefficients of the vertices are positive.
Thus,
N(f_{\textnormal{sym}})=\textnormal{conv}(\{(\beta_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\beta_{\sigma(k)}):\sigma\in S_{k}\}).

Furthermore, by the same result, all vertices of
N(f_{\textnormal{sym}})
are even and therefore,
\beta_{i}\equiv 0\mod 2
for all
i\in[k]
.
∎

Corollary 8
.

Assume that
w_{\alpha_{1}}w_{\alpha_{2}}\leq w_{\beta_{1}}w_{\beta_{2}}
with
|\alpha|=|\beta|
holds in all graphs.
If this inequality can be verified by Corollary
5
, i.e., the symmetrization of
f(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}-x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}
is psd.
Then, there are
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\alpha_{1}=2a+c,\hskip 5.0pt\alpha_{2}=2(a+b)+c,\hskip 5.0pt\beta_{1}=2a,\hskip 5.0pt\beta_{2}=2(a+b+c).

Proof.

From Proposition
7
we see that
\beta_{i}\equiv 0\mod 2
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
We also have
0\leq f_{\textnormal{sym}}
and

f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}+x_{1}^{\beta_{2}}x_{2}^{\beta_{1}}-x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}-x_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{1}}.

This gives
\beta_{1}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{2}
where
\alpha_{1}\equiv\alpha_{2}\mod 2
.
Hence, we have
\beta_{1}=2a,\alpha_{1}=2a+c,\alpha_{2}=2(a+b)+c
and
\beta_{2}=2(a+b+c)
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Corollary
8
fully characterizes pure binomial inequalities with only two factors which can be derived via a psd form. In fact, Ineq. (
1
) is the only inequality of this type that can be derived via a psd form.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k},|\alpha|=2d
. Then,
w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{k}}\leq w_{0}^{k-1}w_{2d}
holds in all graphs.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
1
together with Corollary
5
.
∎

Similarly, we present an alternative proof for Ineq. (
1
).

Theorem 10
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
w_{2a+c}w_{2(a+b)+c}\leq w_{2a}w_{2(a+b+c)}
holds in all graphs.

Proof.

Consider the form
f(\bm{x})=_{\rm def}x_{1}^{2a}x_{2}^{2(a+b+c)}-x_{1}^{2a+c}x_{2}^{2(a+b)+c}
with symmetrization

f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{2a}x_{2}^{2a}(x_{1}^{2b+c}-x_{2}^{2b+c})(x_{1}^{c}-x_{2}^{c})
.
It is easy to see that
f_{\textnormal{sym}}\in\mathcal{P}_{2,4a+2(b+c)}
and hence, the statement follows from
Corollary
5
.
∎

Remark 2
.

More generally, given the inequality
w_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}}\leq w_{\beta_{1}}\cdots w_{\beta_{n}}
and

f(\textbf{x})=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}-x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
such that

f_{\textnormal{sym}}
is nonnegative, we can consider the nonnegative polynomial

\tilde{f}(\textbf{x})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{2a}\cdot f_{\textnormal{sym}}(\textbf{x})
and derive the inequality
w_{\alpha_{1}+2a}\cdots w_{\alpha_{n}+2a}\leq w_{\beta_{1}+2a}\cdots w_{\beta_{n}+2a}
.

However, considering Ineq. (
2
) and a form
f(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{2\ell+pk}(x_{2}\cdots x_{k})^{2\ell}-(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})^{2\ell+pk}
,
we get
\textnormal{deg}(f_{\textnormal{sym}})=k(2\ell+p)
. Since a psd form must be of even degree, we cannot validate Ineq. (
2
) in all cases using a psd form.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
\sigma\in S_{k}
. Then, the inequality

w_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{\sigma(k)}}\leq w_{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{2\alpha_{k}}

holds in all graphs.

Proof.

Consider the form
f(\bm{x})=\bigl(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}-x_{\sigma(1)}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\sigma(k)}^{\alpha_{k}}\bigr)^{2}
.
From
0\leq f_{\textnormal{sym}}
we derive

2w_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{\sigma(k)}}\leq w_{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{2\alpha_{k}}+w_{2\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots w_{2\alpha_{\sigma(k)}}

and hence,

w_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots w_{\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{\sigma(k)}}\leq w_{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots w_{2\alpha_{k}}.

This proves the theorem.
∎

Note that the case
k=2
and
\sigma=(1\,2)
is the inequality given by Dress and Gutman.
Using induction on
k
and the representation of
\sigma\in S_{k}
as a product of disjoint cycles,
we conclude that the only elements of
S_{k}
that can give rise to new inequalities for walks are
cyclic permutations.

4
Conclusion

Our results show that not every known pure binomial inequality for the number of walks can be derived via a nonnegative polynomial and is therefore, not as powerful as the tropicalization given by Blekherman and Raymond in
[
2
]
.
However, regarding the inequalities given in Theorem
11
we assume the polynomial approach to be still a simple and strong approach for proving walk inequalities. Moreover, using the polynomial approach, we also obtained some non-binomial inequalities (see Example
6
), which could not be obtained by tropicalization.
Generally, tropicalization is suitable for characterizing universal binomial inequalities, i.e., binomial inequalities that are valid for all graphs. In contrast, the pure binomial inequality
w_{0}w_{r+s}\geq w_{r}w_{s}
is not universal (see
[
11
,
17
]
for counterexamples), but it is valid for regular graphs and, potentially, for subdivision graphs
[
17
]
. It is advisable to see whether tropicalization and lightweight methods can interact in order to find a precise characterization of all these graph classes.

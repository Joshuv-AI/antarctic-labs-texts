Prime Fano threefolds of genus 12 with a G m \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}} -action and their automorphisms
Alexander Kuznetsov, Yuri Prokhorov

Abstract
We give an explicit construction of prime Fano threefolds of genus 12 with a G m \mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}} -action, describe their isomorphism classes and automorphism groups.



Keywords.
Fano threefolds; automorphism group; Sarkisov link;
Mukai–Umemura threefold;
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action

1.
Introduction

We work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers.
Recall that a
prime Fano threefold
is a smooth projective variety of dimension 3 with
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}K_{X}
and
-K_{X}
ample.
The genus
{\mathrm{g}}(X)
is defined by the equality
(-K_{X})^{3}=2{\mathrm{g}}(X)-2
.
We refer to
[
IP99
]
for the general theory of Fano varieties and their classification in dimension 3.

In this paper we discuss prime Fano threefolds of genus 12, which means
(-K_{X})^{3}=22
.
Specifically, we give an explicit description of those of them that admit a faithful action of a one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Thus, this paper complements to
[
Pro90
]
,
[
KPS16
,
§§5.3–5.4
]
,

[
DKK17
]
, and
[
D08
,
D17
,
CS18
]
.
To state the main result, we introduce some notation.

Consider the projective four-space
\mathbb{P}^{4}
with a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action with weights
(0,1,3,5,6)
and let
y_{0},y_{1},y_{3},y_{5},y_{6}
be the corresponding coordinates.
Consider the map
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{4}

(t_{0}:t_{1})\longmapsto(y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{3}:y_{5}:y_{6})=(t_{0}^{6}:t_{0}^{5}t_{1}:t_{0}^{3}t_{1}^{3}:t_{0}t_{1}^{5}:t_{1}^{6}),

(-1.1)

and let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be its image.
This is a smooth rational sextic curve.

Consider the pencil of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
generated by

Q_{0}=\{y_{0}y_{6}-y_{3}^{2}=0\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{\infty}=\{y_{3}^{2}-y_{1}y_{5}=0\}.

(-1.2)

For
u=(u_{0}:u_{1})\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
denote by
Q_{u}=u_{0}Q_{0}+u_{1}Q_{\infty}
the corresponding quadric in the pencil.
All quadrics
Q_{u}
pass through
\Gamma
and
Q_{u}
is smooth if and only if
u\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
.

There is a natural bijection between
isomorphism classes of prime Fano threefolds of genus
12
with a faithful
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action, and
the set
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
of smooth quadrics
Q_{u}
passing through the curve
\Gamma
.

Set
u=u_{0}/u_{1}
.
If
X^{\mathrm{m}}(u)
denotes the threefold corresponding to the quadric
Q_{u}
, then

\operatorname{Aut}(X^{\mathrm{m}}(u))\cong\begin{cases}\PGL_{2},&\text{if $u=-1/4$},\\
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $u\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,-1/4,\infty\}$},\end{cases}

where the action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
in the semidirect product is given by the inversion.

In particular,
X^{\mathrm{m}}(-1/4)\cong X^{\mathrm{MU}}
is the Mukai–Umemura threefold.

The proof of the theorem takes the rest of the paper.
We compute the automorphisms in Corollary
3
,
establish the bijection of isomorphism classes in Corollary
4
,
and identify the Mukai–Umemura threefold in Proposition
4
.

2.
A birational transformation

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on a
Sarkisov link
described in Theorem
2
below.
To state it we need to remind the notion of quadratical normality for projective curves.

Recall that a curve in a projective space is called
quadratically normal

if restrictions of quadrics form a complete linear system on the curve.
We will need the following evident observation.

A rational sextic curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is quadratically normal if and
only if the linear system of quadrics passing through
\Gamma
is one-dimensional.
Indeed, this follows immediately from the exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{4},I_{\Gamma}(2))\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{4},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(2))\to H^{0}(\Gamma,\mathscr{O}_{\Gamma}(12))

since
\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{4},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(2))=15
and
\dim H^{0}(\Gamma,\mathscr{O}_{\Gamma}(12))=13
.
In particular, a quadratically normal rational sextic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is not contained in a hyperplane.

The next result is the base of our construction.

Let
X
be a prime Fano threefold of genus
12
and let
C
be a smooth conic on
X
.
Then there exists the following commutative diagram
of birational maps

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\chi}
\textstyle{Q^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{Q}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\xi}
\textstyle{Q}

(-1.6)

where

•

Q
is a smooth quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
,

•

\sigma_{Q}
is the blow up of a smooth rational quadratically normal sextic curve
\Gamma\subset Q
,

•

\sigma_{X}
is the blow up of
C
,

and
\chi
is a flop.

Furthermore, let
H_{X}
and
H_{Q}
be the ample generators of the Picard groups
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
and
\operatorname{Pic}(Q)
, respectively,
and denote
H^{\prime}_{X}:=\sigma_{X}^{*}H_{X}
and
H^{\prime}_{Q}:=\sigma_{Q}^{*}H_{Q}
.
Let
E_{C}:=\sigma_{X}^{-1}(C)
and
E_{\Gamma}:=\sigma_{Q}^{-1}(\Gamma)
be the exceptional divisors of
\sigma_{X}
and
\sigma_{Q}

Then

(i)

The map

is given by the linear system
|H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C}|
and contracts the unique divisor from
|2H^{\prime}_{X}-5E_{C}|
.

(ii)

The map
\sigma_{X}\circ\chi^{-1}\colon Q^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X\subset\mathbb{P}^{13}
is given by the linear system
|5H^{\prime}_{Q}-2E_{\Gamma}|
and contracts the unique divisor from
|2H^{\prime}_{Q}-E_{\Gamma}|
.

Let
X^{\prime}
be the blowup of
X
along
C
.
The linear system
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
is base point free and defines a morphism that does not contract divisors by Lemma
2

Therefore,
[
Tak89
]
or
[
IP99

applies
and gives the diagram, and most of the details of the theorem.
The only thing left is to show that the curve
\Gamma
is quadratically normal.

The argument of Remark
2
shows that
the curve
\Gamma
is quadratically normal if and only if the linear system
|2H^{\prime}_{Q}-E_{\Gamma}|
is zero-dimensional.
On the other hand, the map

identifies this linear system with
|2(H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C})-(2H^{\prime}_{X}-5E_{C})|=|E_{C}|

\square

Let
X^{\prime}
be the blowup of
X
along a smooth conic
C
.
The linear system
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
is base point free and defines a morphism that does not contract divisors.

Recall that
H_{X}=-K_{X}
is very ample,

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))=14,

(-1.8)

see
[
IP99
, Corollary 2.1.14(ii)]
, and so
|-K_{X}|
embeds
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{13}
.
Note that
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{13}
does not contain planes (because
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}H_{X}
)
and is an intersection of quadrics (see
[
Isk80
, Proposition IV.1.3 and Theorem II.3.4]
).

Note that
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|=|H^{\prime}_{X}-E_{C}|
is the strict transform of the linear system of hyperplane sections of
X
passing through the conic
C
.
Their scheme-theoretic intersection is the intersection of the linear span of
C
with
X
.
Since
X
is an intersection of quadrics and does not contain planes, this is equal to
C
.
Therefore, the linear system
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
on
X^{\prime}
is base point free
(moreover, it follows that the morphism defined by this linear system is birational, although we do not need this).
In particular,
-K_{X^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma\geq 0
for any curve
\gamma
on
X^{\prime}
.

Assume
\gamma
is contracted by
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
, so that
-K_{X^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma=0
.
Then
H^{\prime}_{X}\cdot\gamma\neq 0
, since
\gamma
is an effective curve, so since
H^{\prime}_{X}
is nef we have
H^{\prime}_{X}\cdot\gamma>0
.
Therefore
E_{C}\cdot\gamma>0
and so

(H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C})\cdot\gamma<(H^{\prime}_{X}-E_{C})\cdot\gamma=0,

which means that
\gamma
is contained in the base locus of the linear system
|H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C}|
.
The natural exact sequences

0\to H^{0}(X,I_{C}(H_{X}))\to H^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(H_{X}))\to H^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(2))

and

0\to H^{0}(X,I^{2}_{C}(H_{X}))\to H^{0}(X,I_{C}(H_{X}))\to H^{0}(C,I_{C}/I^{2}_{C}(2))

and equalities

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(H_{X}))=14,\qquad\dim H^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(2))=3,\qquad\dim H^{0}(C,I_{C}/I^{2}_{C}(2))=6

(the first equality is (
-1.8
), the second is evident,
and the third follows from (
-1.34

imply that we have an inequality

\dim|H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C}|\geq 13-3-6=4.

Since moreover
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}H_{X}
, every hyperplane section of
X
is irreducible,
and since the linear system
|H^{\prime}_{X}-2E_{C}|
is of positive dimension,
the only possible divisorial component of its base locus is
E_{C}
.
Thus, it remains to check that
E_{C}
is not contracted by
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
.
This, however, easily follows from the equality
(-K_{X^{\prime}})^{2}\cdot E_{C}=(H^{\prime}_{X}-E_{C})^{2}\cdot E_{C}=4
.

\square

Let

Then

If

If

\psi

Automorphisms

To apply the results of Section
2

for a description of prime Fano threefolds

-action and of their automorphism groups,

-invariant.
For this we will need a description of

-invariant lines from

.
Recall the Mukai–Umemura threefold

Its Hilbert scheme of lines

Now we can pass to conics.
Recall that a conic on a projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a subscheme
C\subset X
with Hilbert polynomial
p_{C}(t)=1+2t
.
There are three types of conics — smooth conics, i.e., curves isomorphic to the second Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
reducible conics, i.e., unions of two lines meeting at a point,
and non-reduced conics, i.e., non-reduced schemes
C
such that
C_{\mathrm{red}}=L
is a line and
I_{L}/I_{C}\cong\mathscr{O}_{L}(-1)
,
see
[
KPS16
, Lemma 2.1.1(ii)]
.

Let
X
be a prime Fano threefold of genus
12
with a faithful
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action which is not isomorphic to the Mukai–Umemura threefold
X^{\mathrm{MU}}
.
Then
X
contains a smooth
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant conic.

In Lemma
4
(ii) we will show that an
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant conic on such
X
is unique.

By
[
Pro90
]
or
[
KPS16
, §5.4]
the identity component of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
is exactly the torus
\mathbb{G}_{{\mathrm{m}}}
.
Consider the Hilbert scheme
S(X)
of conics on
X
.
We have

S(X)\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}

(-1.16)

by
[
KS04
]
or
[
KPS16
, Proposition B.4.1]
.
The
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-invariant lines
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on

are special, hence by
[
KPS16
, Remarks 2.1.2 and 2.1.7]

there are non-reduced conics
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
(C_{i})_{\mathrm{red}}=L_{i}
, and these are the only non-reduced conics on
X
.
Clearly, these conics are
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-invariant.

Let
S(X)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\subset S(X)
be the fixed point locus of
\mathbb{G}_{{\mathrm{m}}}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)
.
Since the natural
\mathbb{G}_{{\mathrm{m}}}
-action on

is faithful by
[

there are two possibilities:

\text{either $S(X)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}$\qquad or $S(X)^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}=\{c_{0}\}\cup\ell$},

(-1.17)

where
\ell
is a line on

and the points

and

correspond to the non-reduced conics
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
In the first case the conic corresponding to the third point

\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant.
In the second case, if the points
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
lie on

is
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant.
Finally, if only one of the points

and

lies on
\ell
, then this point is
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant,
hence the finite group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

\operatorname{Aut}(X)/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is a cyclic group, hence it has yet another fixed point on

\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant conic.
Thus, in all these cases we have found an
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-invariant conic

and
C_{2}
.
The only thing left is to show that

Indeed, as we mentioned above

and
C_{2}

On the other hand, if a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-invariant conic

\displaystyle P_{6}:=(0:0:0:0:1),

\displaystyle P_{1}:=(0:1:0:0:0),\quad

\displaystyle P_{5}:=(0:0:0:1:0),

and
F
contains exactly four lines

\ell_{0,1}=\langle P_{0},P_{1}\rangle,\qquad\ell_{1,6}=\langle P_{1},P_{6}\rangle,\qquad\ell_{6,5}=\langle P_{6},P_{5}\rangle,\qquad\ell_{5,0}=\langle P_{5},P_{0}\rangle.

The curve
\Gamma
passes through
P_{0}
and
P_{6}
and does not pass through
P_{1}
and
P_{5}
.
The lines
\ell_{0,1}
and
\ell_{6,5}
are
3
-tangent to the curve
\Gamma
.
The surface
F
can be realized as the quotient of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
by the involution
(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto(-w_{1},-w_{2})

with the quotient map
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to F\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
given by

(w_{1},w_{2})\longmapsto(y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{3}:y_{5}:y_{6})=(1:w_{1}^{2}:w_{1}w_{2}:w_{2}^{2}:w_{1}^{2}w_{2}^{2}).

In particular,
F
is a toric del Pezzo surface with
\operatorname{Pic}(F)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
\operatorname{Cl}(F)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
.
One can also realize
F
as the anti-canonical image of the blowup
F^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

at four points
(0,0)
,
(0,\infty)
,
(\infty,\infty)
,
(\infty,0)
.

The two
3
-tangent lines
\ell_{0,1}
and
\ell_{6,5}
are contained in the flopping locus for
\chi^{-1}
.
Since by Proposition
5
the flopping locus consists of two irreducible components,
the strict transforms in
Q^{\prime}
of these two lines form the whole flopping locus of
\chi^{-1}
.

Finally, we give a description of one of the boundary points in the family
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}

of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-invariant threefolds
X
.
Note that the quadric
Q_{1}=\{y_{0}y_{6}-y_{1}y_{5}=0\}
is a cone over a smooth quadric surface and contains
\Gamma
.
Its vertex

P_{3}=(0:0:1:0:0)

is an ordinary double point on
Q_{1}
that lies away from the surface
F
described in Remark
5
.
In particular, the point
P_{3}
lies away from
\Gamma
and away from the flopping lines of
\chi^{-1}
.
Therefore, the construction of Theorem
2
can be applied to
(Q_{1},\Gamma)

and produces a prime Fano threefold
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
of genus
12
with one ordinary double point and a faithful
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action.

On the other hand, Fano threefolds of genus 12 with a single ordinary double point were classified in
[
Pro16
]
.
In the next proposition we identify
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
with a threefold of type (IV);
a detailed description of such threefolds is given in
[
Pro16
, §7]
.

The threefold
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
is a threefold of type
(IV)
from
[
Pro16
, Theorem 1.2]
.

A general plane
\Pi_{\lambda}
in the pencil

\{y_{5}=\lambda y_{0},\,y_{6}=\lambda y_{1}\}

is contained in
Q_{1}
,
meets
\Gamma
at
5
distinct points, passes through
P_{3}
, and does not meet the flopping locus of
\chi^{-1}

(see Remark
5
).
Let
\Pi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\subset Q^{\prime}_{1}

be the strict transform of
\Pi_{\lambda}
(it is isomorphic to the blowup of
\Pi_{\lambda}
at 5 points).
The composition
\sigma_{X}\circ\chi^{-1}
is regular near
\Pi_{\lambda}^{\prime}
and
it is easy to see that
\deg(\sigma_{X}(\chi^{-1}(\Pi_{\lambda}^{\prime})))=5

(the map
\sigma_{X}\circ\chi^{-1}
contracts the strict transform of the conic
\Pi_{\lambda}\cap Q_{0}
).
Therefore,
\sigma_{X}(\chi^{-1}(\Pi_{\lambda}^{\prime}))
generates a pencil of quintic del Pezzo surfaces
on
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
with a base point
P_{3}
which is singular on
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
.
Blowing up the Weil divisor
\sigma_{X}(\chi^{-1}(\Pi_{\lambda}^{\prime}))
(this blowup is a small resolution of
X^{\mathrm{m}}(1)
)
we obtain a base point free pencil of quintic del Pezzo surfaces.
By
[
Pro16
, Theorem 1.2]
this property characterizes the case (IV).

\square

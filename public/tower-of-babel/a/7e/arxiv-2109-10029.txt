Random iteration on hyperbolic Riemann surfaces
Marco Abate, Argyrios Christodoulou

Abstract
Let { f ν } ⊂ Hol ( X , X ) \{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X) be a sequence of holomorphic self-maps of a hyperbolic Riemann surface X X . In this paper we shall study the asymptotic behavior of the sequences obtained by iteratively left-composing or right-composing the maps { f ν } \{f_{\nu}\} ; the sequences of self-maps of X X so obtained are called left (respectively, right) iterated function systems. We shall obtain the analogue for left iterated function systems of the theorems proved by Beardon, Carne, Minda and Ng for right iterated function systems with value in a Bloch domain; and we shall extend to the setting of general hyperbolic Riemann surfaces results obtained by Short and the second author in the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} for iterated function systems generated by maps close enough to a given self-map.



1
Introduction

A classical result in one variable holomorphic dynamics is the
Wolff-Denjoy theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Wolff
[
16
,
15
,
17
]
, Denjoy
[
5
]
; 1926)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be a holomorphic self-map of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
, not an elliptic automorphism. Then there exists a point
\tau\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that the sequence
\{f^{k}\}
of iterates of
f
converges, uniformly on compact subsets, to the constant
\tau
.

In this statement, an
elliptic automorphism
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
with a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
. Up to a conjugation an elliptic automorphism is a rotation, and so its dynamics is well understood; Theorem
1.1
describes completely the dynamics of all other holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. The point
\tau
in the statement is the
Wolff point
of the function
f
.

This result has been generalised by Heins in 1941 to hyperbolic Riemann surfaces as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Heins
[
11
]
; 1941)
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface, and
let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
. Then either:

(i)

f
has an attracting fixed point in
X
, or

(ii)

f
is a periodic automorphism, or

(iii)

f
is a pseudoperiodic automorphism, or

(iv)

the sequence
\{f^{k}\}
is compactly divergent.

Furthermore, the case
(iii)
can occur only if
X
is either simply
connected (and
f
has a fixed point) or doubly connected (and
f
has no
fixed points).

An
attracting fixed point

z_{0}
of
f
is a fixed point (that is,
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
) where the derivative of
f
(which is well-defined because
z_{0}
is a fixed point) has modulus less than 1; in particular, it follows that the sequence of iterates
\{f^{k}\}
converges, uniformly on compact subsets, to the constant function
z_{0}
. A
periodic automorphism
is an automorphism
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
such that
f^{q}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{X}
for some
q\geq 1
— and then the dynamics of
f
is trivial. A
pseudoperiodic automorphism
is an automorphism
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
such that there exists a subsequence of iterates converging to the identity
\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{X}
. Elliptic automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
are always periodic or pseudoperiodic. A doubly connected hyperbolic Riemann surface is necessarily biholomorphic to a pointed disk or to an annulus; furthermore,
pseudoperiodic automorphisms are conjugated to rotations, either of the disk or of the pointed disk or of an annulus, and so their dynamics is well known.

Finally, a sequence
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,Y)
of holomorphic maps between two Riemann surfaces is
compactly divergent
if for every compact
K\subseteq X
and every compact
L\subseteq Y
there is a
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{\nu}(K)\cap L=\mathord{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle/\hfil$\crcr$\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\textstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\textstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}}
for all
\nu\geq\nu_{0}
.
Roughly speaking, a compactly diverging sequence is diverging to infinity (or, more precisely, is converging to the infinity point of the Alexandroff compactification of
Y
). Restricting to the case of self-maps for simplicity, when
X=Y=D
is a domain in a larger compact Riemann surface
\widehat{X}
then a compactly divergent sequence of iterates is converging toward the boundary, in the sense that all accumulation points of the sequence are constant maps contained in the boundary, and the set of accumulation points is closed and connected. Furthermore, when the boundary of
D
is sufficiently nice then Heins has obtained a complete analogue of the Wolff-Denjoy theorem:

Theorem 1.3

(Heins
[
9
]
; 1988)
.

Let
D\subset\widehat{X}
be a hyperbolic domain in a compact Riemann surface
\widehat{X}
. Assume that
\partial D
consists of a finite number of isolated points or disjoint Jordan curves. Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(D,D)
be such that the sequence of iterates
\{f^{k}\}
is compactly divergent in
D
.
Then the sequence
\{f^{k}\}
converges, uniformly on compact subsets, to a point
\tau\in\partial D
.

See also
[
1
]
for proofs and other related results.

A sequence of iterates is obtained by composing the same map over and over. Stimulated from problems coming from continued fractions theory, computer simulations of dynamical systems and other sources, in the ’80s mathematicians have started to study properties of sequences obtained by composing different self-maps. This area of exploration is often called “random dynamics” because the self-maps to consider can be chosen at random according to a suitable probability distribution, for instance with mass concentrated around a fixed self-map, thus obtaining random perturbations of the sequence of iterates. We shall not pursue a probabilistic approach in this paper, preferring a more topological approach, but we kept the term “random dynamics” in the title as done, for instance, in
[
2
]
.

Let us define the sequences of self-maps appearing in random dynamics.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\{f_{\nu}\}
be a sequence of self-maps of a Riemann surface
X
, all different from the identity.
The
left
(or
direct
or
forward
)

iterated function system
(or
composition system
) generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
is the sequence of self-maps
\{L_{\nu}\}
given by

L_{\nu}=f_{\nu}\circ f_{\nu-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{0}\;.

The
right
(or
reverse
or
backward
)
iterated function system
generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
is instead the sequence of self-maps
\{R_{\nu}\}
given by

R_{\nu}=f_{0}\circ f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\nu}\;.

When all the maps
f_{\nu}
belong to a given family
\cal F
of self-maps of
X
we shall say that the corresponding left or right iterated function system is
in

\cal F
.

Clearly, general left or right iterated function systems can have very erratic behaviours. Moreover, left and right iterated function systems generated by the same sequence can behave very differently; consider for instance the case when all
f_{\nu}
’s are constant. So to get meaningful theorems one has somehow to restrict the class
\mathcal{F}
of functions used to generate the iterated function systems.

In this paper we shall consider iterated function systems generated by two classes of self-maps:
maps belonging to
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
, where
\Omega\subset X
is a Bloch subdomain of
X
; and maps sufficiently close to a given self-map
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
.

Roughly speaking (see Section
2
for a precise definition) a Bloch subdomain
\Omega
of a hyperbolic Riemann surface is a domain
\Omega\subset X
such that all holomorphic maps from
X
into
\Omega
are strict contraction with respect to the Poincaré distance of
X
. Right iterated function systems in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
have been thoroughly studied by Beardon, Carne, Minda and Ng
[
2
]
in 2004. Left iterated function systems, on the other hand, are not so well studied, with the exception of a few results due to Gill in the unit disk (see
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
) and to Keen and Lakic on plane domains (see
[
12
, Section 11.2]
).

In this paper, along the lines followed in
[
2
]
for right iterated function systems, we shall complete the study of left iterated function systems in Bloch domains proving the following result:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a Bloch domain in a hyperbolic Riemann surface
X
, and let
\{f_{\nu}\}
be a sequence of holomorphic self-maps of
X

such that
f_{\nu}(X)\subseteq\Omega
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, that is
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
. Then:

(i)

all limit points of the left iterated function systems
\{L_{\nu}\}
generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
are constant;

(ii)

let
z_{\nu}\in\Omega
be the unique fixed point of
f_{\nu}
. Then
\{L_{\nu}\}
converges to a constant
z_{\infty}\in X
if and only if
z_{\nu}\to z_{\infty}
.

Random iteration for holomorphic self-maps ot the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
sufficiently close to a given map have been thoroughly studied by Short and the second author in 2019 (see
[
4
]
). In this paper we shall extend their results to the setting of generic hyperbolic Riemann surfaces in the spirit of Heins theorems.
According to Theorem
1.3
we need to distinguish three cases: when
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
has an attracting fixed point; when the sequence
\{F^{\nu}\}
of iterates of
F
is compactly divergent; and when
F
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphism of
X
.

When
F
has an attracting fixed point we shall prove a fairly complete result:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
be with an attracting fixed point
z_{0}\in X
. Then:

(i)

there exists a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
F
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
such that every right iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathcal{U}
converges to a constant in
X
;

(ii)

if
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
is a sequence converging to
F
then the left iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
converges to
z_{0}
.

When the sequence of iterates of
F
is compactly divergent, simple examples (see, e.g., Example
4.6
) show that in general we cannot deduce much on the behavior of right iterated function systems generated by functions close to
F
. On the other hand, if
f_{\nu}
converges to
F
fast enough we shall prove that the behavior of
\{L_{\nu}\}
is dictated by the dynamical behavior of
F
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface, and let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
be such that the sequence of iterates
\{F^{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent. Then we can find a sequence
of neighbourhoods
\mathcal{U}_{\nu}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
of
F
such that if
f_{\nu}\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
then the left iterated function system
\{L_{\nu}\}
generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent. Furthermore, if
X\subset\widehat{X}
is a hyperbolic domain in a compact Riemann surface and
\{F^{\nu}\}
converges to a point
\tau\in\partial X
then

\{L_{\nu}\}
converges to
\tau
.

Surprisingly enough, if the convergence of
f_{\nu}
to
F
is too slow then the left iterated function system might be oscillating even when the sequence of iterates of
F
converges to a point in the boundary; see Example
4.5
.

Finally, if
F
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphism of
X
, in Section
4
we shall see that the only interesting case is
X=\mathbb{D}
, that has already been
studied in
[
4
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we collect known definitions and results that we shall use in the sequel.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface. We shall denote by
\omega_{X}
the Poincaré distance on
X
, which is a complete distance whose main property is the classical Schwarz-Pick lemma:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two hyperbolic Riemann surfaces,
and
f\colon X\to Y
a holomorphic function. Then

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall z_{1},\,z_{2}\in X\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt$}\hfil\omega_{Y}\bigl(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2})\bigr)\leq\omega_{X}(z_{1},z_{2})\;.

Furthermore, equality at some
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
implies that
f
is a covering map; conversely, if
f
is a covering map then for every
w_{1}
,
w_{2}\in Y
and
z_{1}\in X
with
f(z_{1})=w_{1}
we can find
z_{2}\in X
so that
f(z_{2})=w_{2}
and
\omega_{Y}(w_{1},w_{2})=\omega_{X}(z_{1},z_{2})
.

For a proof see, e.g.,
[
3
]
.

For each
z\in X
and
R>0
we shall denote by
B_{X}(z,R)
the ball with respect to
\omega_{X}
centred in
z
and with radius
R
. The completeness of
\omega_{X}

implies that the closed balls
\overline{B_{X}(z,R)}
are compact in
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\Omega\subset X
is a domain in a hyperbolic Riemann surface
X
and
z\in\Omega
put

R(z;\Omega,X)=\sup\{r>0\mid B_{X}(z,r)\subseteq\Omega\}

and

R(\Omega,X)=\sup_{z\in\Omega}R(z;\Omega,X)=\sup\{r>0\mid\exists z\in\Omega:B_{X}(z,r)\subseteq\Omega\}\;.

We say that
\Omega
is a
Bloch domain
of
X
if
R(\Omega,X)<+\infty
.

It is easy to see that relatively compact subdomains of a hyperbolic Riemann surface are Bloch domains; but it is not too difficult to find examples of Bloch domains which are not relatively compact (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
).

The main property of Bloch domains is the following:

Proposition 2.3

(Beardon, Carne, Minda, Ng
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a Bloch domain in a hyperbolic Riemann surface
X
. Then there exists
0<\ell<1
such that

\omega_{X}\bigl(f(z),f(w)\bigr)\leq\ell\,\omega_{X}(z,w)

for all
z
,
w\in X
and all
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
.

A
hyperbolic domain

D
in a compact Riemann surface
\widehat{X}
is a domain
D\subset\widehat{X}
which is a hyperbolic Riemann surface on its own.
A useful property of the Poincaré distance of hyperbolic domains is the following:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
D\subset\widehat{X}
be a hyperbolic domain in a compact Riemann surface.
Take
\tau_{0}\in\partial D
, and a sequence
\{z_{\nu}\}\subset D

converging to
\tau_{0}
. Let
\{w_{\nu}\}\subset D
be another sequence, and
assume there is
M>0
so that

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt$}\hfil\omega_{D}(z_{\nu},w_{\nu})<M\;.

Then
w_{\nu}\to\tau_{0}
as
\nu\to+\infty
.

For a proof see, e.g.,
[
14
]
.

We shall need a few properties of the spaces of holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, that we shall always consider endowed with the compact-open topology (which is equivalent to the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets).
The first one is the classical Vitali theorem (for a proof see, e.g.,
[
1
]
*Theorem 1.1.45):

Theorem 2.5

(Vitali)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, and
let
\{f_{\nu}\}
be a sequence of functions in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,Y)
. Assume there is a
set
A\subset X
with at least one accumulation point such that
\{f_{\nu}(z)\}

converges for every
z\in A
. Then
\{f_{\nu}\}
converges uniformly on compact
subsets of
X
to a function
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,Y)
.

The next two results are again due to Heins
[
10
]
:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface
with non-abelian fundamental group. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{X}
is isolated in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
.
In particular,
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
is discrete, and each
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
is isolated in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface
not biholomorphic to
\mathbb{D}
or
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
is open and closed
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
.

For a modern proof see, e.g.,
[
1
]
*Theorem 1.2.19, Corollary 1.2.24.

The next result is another consequence of Heins’ works; we include a proof for completeness.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
a self-covering of
X
. Assume that
f
has a fixed point
z_{0}\in X
. Then
f
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphisms of
X
, and
z_{0}
is not attracting.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{X}\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
the universal covering map.
Given
\tilde{z}_{0}\in\pi_{X}^{-1}(z_{0})
we can choose a lifting
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
of
f
such that
\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}_{0})=\tilde{z}_{0}
. Since (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
*Lemma 12.1)

\tilde{f}
is a self-covering of
\mathbb{D}
, it must be an automorphism; therefore
|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{z}_{0})|=1
, by the classical Schwarz-Pick lemma. This clearly implies that
|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|=1
,
and the assertion follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Finally, we recall a few standard facts about doubly connected hyperbolic Riemann surfaces; for proofs see, e.g.,
[
1
]
.

A Riemann surface is
doubly connected
if and only if its fundamental group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. A doubly connected hyperbolic Riemann surface is necessarily biholomorphic either to the pointed disk
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
or to an annulus

A(r,1)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid r<|z|<1\}

for some
0<r<1
. A hyperbolic Riemann surface has abelian fundamental group if and only if it is simply connected (and hence is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{D}
) or doubly connected.

Finally, we can completely describe the automorphisms of doubly connected hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. Indeed, every automorphism of
\mathbb{D}^{*}
is of the form
\gamma(z)=e^{i\theta}z
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
; and every automorphism of
A(r,1)
is either of the form

\gamma(z)=e^{i\theta}z
or of the form
\gamma(z)=e^{i\theta}rz^{-1}
for some

\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

3
Random iteration on Bloch domains

In this section we shall study left iterated function systems in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
, where
\Omega\subset X
is a Bloch domain in a hyperbolic Riemann surface
X
.
As in
[
2
]
the main result is of topological nature:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset C^{0}(X,X)
be a sequence of continuous self-maps of a metric space
(X,d)
, and assume that there exists
0<\ell<1
such that

(1)

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall x,y\in X\ \forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt$}\hfil d\bigl(f_{\nu}(x),f_{\nu}(y)\bigr)\leq\ell d(x,y)\;.

Let
\{L_{\nu}\}
be the left iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
. Then

(i)

every (pointwise) limit point of
\{L_{\nu}\}
is constant;

(ii)

if a subsequence
\{L_{\nu_{k}}\}
converges pointwise to a constant
x_{0}\in X
then it converges to
x_{0}
uniformly on compact subsets;

(iii)

assume that every
f_{\nu}
has a (necessarily unique) fixed point
x_{\nu}\in X
. Then
\{L_{\nu}\}
converges uniformly on compact subsets to a constant function if and only if the sequence
\{x_{\nu}\}
converges in
X
.

Proof.

(i) Assume that a subsequence
\{L_{\nu_{k}}\}
converges pointwise to a map
g\in C^{0}(X,X)
. Then for every
x
,
y\in X
we have

d\bigl(g(x),g(y)\bigr)=\!\lim_{k\to+\infty}d\bigl(f_{\nu_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{0}(x),f_{\nu_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{0}(y)\bigr)\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\ell^{\nu_{k}+1}d(x,y)=0\;,

and thus
g
is constant.

(ii) Assume that
L_{\nu_{k}}(x)\to x_{0}
for each
x\in X
. Let
K\subseteq X
be compact, and let
d_{0}=\max_{x\in K}\{d(x_{0},x)\}<+\infty
. Then

\displaystyle d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x),x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x),L_{\nu_{k}}(x_{0})\bigr)+d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x_{0}),x_{0}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{\nu_{k}+1}d(x,x_{0})+d\bigl(L_{\nu}(x_{0}),x_{0})\leq\ell^{\nu_{k}+1}d_{0}+d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x_{0}),x_{0})\;,

for all
x\in K
, and thus
L_{\nu_{k}}\to x_{0}
uniformly on
K
.

(iii) Assume first that
x_{\nu}\to x_{\infty}\in X
. Then for all
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle d\bigl(L_{\nu}(x),\,x_{\infty}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq d\bigl(f_{\nu}\circ\cdots\circ f_{0}(x),f_{\nu}(x_{\nu})\bigr)+d(x_{\nu},x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\ell d\bigl(f_{\nu-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{0}(x),x_{\nu}\bigr)+d(x_{\nu},x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\ell^{\nu+1}d(x,x_{0})+\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\ell^{\nu-j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})+d(x_{\nu},x_{\infty})\;.

The first and third addends in the last line clearly goes to zero as
\nu\to+\infty
. To prove that the second addend goes to zero too, let
M=\max_{j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})<+\infty
,
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
d(x_{j},x_{j+1})<\varepsilon(1-\ell)/2
as soon as
j\geq\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell^{j}<\varepsilon(1-\ell)/(2M)
as soon as

j\geq\nu_{1}
. Then if
\nu\geq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\ell^{\nu-j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-\nu_{1}}\ell^{\nu-j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})+\sum_{j=\nu-\nu_{1}+1}^{\nu-1}\ell^{\nu-j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1})

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{j=0}^{\nu-\nu_{1}}\ell^{\nu-j}+\frac{\varepsilon(1-\ell)}{2}\sum_{j=\nu-\nu_{1}+1}^{\nu-1}\ell^{\nu-j}

\displaystyle=M\sum_{j=\nu_{1}}^{\nu}\ell^{j}+\frac{\varepsilon(1-\ell)}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\nu_{1}-1}\ell^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{1-\ell}\ell^{\nu_{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon(1-\ell)}{2}\frac{1-\ell^{\nu_{1}}}{1-\ell}\leq\varepsilon\;.

In this way we have proved that
L_{\nu}\to x_{\infty}
pointwise — and hence uniformly on compact subsets by (ii) — as claimed.

Conversely, assume that
L_{\nu}\to x_{\infty}
and, by contradiction, that there exist
r>0
and a subsequence
\{x_{\nu_{k}}\}
such that
d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})>r

for all
k
. Fix
x\in X
. Since
L_{\nu}(x)\to\infty
, we know that

d\bigl(L_{\nu}(x),x_{\infty}\bigr)<\frac{1-\ell}{1+\ell}r

for
\nu
large enough. Then for
k
large enough we have

\displaystyle d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})-\frac{1-\ell}{1+\ell}r

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})-d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x),x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}}(x),x_{\nu_{k}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ell d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}-1}(x),x_{\nu_{k}}\bigr)\leq\ell d\bigl(L_{\nu_{k}-1}(x),x_{\infty}\bigr)+\ell d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\ell}{1+\ell}\ell r+\ell d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})\;,

and then
d(x_{\nu_{k}},x_{\infty})\leq r
, contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
(X,d)
is complete the Banach fixed point theorem implies that every
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
satisfying (
1
) has a fixed point in
X
, and thus the hypothesis in (iii) is automatically satisfied.

As recalled in Section
2
, if
\Omega\subset X
is a Bloch domain then there exists
0<\ell<1
such that

\omega_{X}\bigl(f(z),f(w)\bigr)\leq\ell\omega_{X}(z,w)

for every
z
,
w\in X
and every
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
. Therefore Theorem
1.5
is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, as shown in the following result, where for the sake of completeness we report also what has been proved in
[
2
]
for right iterated function systems:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a Bloch domain in a hyperbolic Riemann surface
X
, and let
\{f_{\nu}\}
be a sequence of holomorphic self-maps of
X

such that
f_{\nu}(X)\subseteq\Omega
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, that is
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,\Omega)
. Then:

(i)

all limit points of the left and right iterated function systems,
\{L_{\nu}\}
and
\{R_{\nu}\}
, generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
are constant;

(ii)

let
z_{\nu}\in\Omega
be the unique fixed point of
f_{\nu}
. Then
\{L_{\nu}\}
converges to a constant
z_{\infty}\in X
if and only if
z_{\nu}\to z_{\infty}
;

(iii)

if there is
z_{0}\in X
such that the set
\{f_{\nu}(z_{0})\}
is relatively compact in
X
then
\{R_{\nu}\}
converges to a constant function.

Proof.

The statements for
\{R_{\nu}\}
are in
[
2
]
. For
\{L_{\nu}\}
,
since the Poincaré distance of
X
is complete, by Proposition
2.3
and Remark
3.2
we can apply Theorem
3.1
, and we are done.
∎

4
Random iteration of small perturbations

In this section we shall discuss the behaviour of iterated function systems generated by functions close enough to a given self-map
F
; in particular we would like to understand whether the dynamics of the iterated function systems mimics the dynamics of the sequence of iterates of
F
.

Recalling Theorem
1.2
, we see that we have three cases to consider: when
F
has an attracting fixed point, when
F
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphism and when the sequence
\{F^{k}\}
is compactly divergent.

In the first case we have a fairly complete result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
be with an attracting fixed point
z_{0}\in X
. Then:

(i)

there exists a neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
F
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
such that every right iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathcal{U}
converges to a constant in
X
;

(ii)

if
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
is a sequence converging to
F
then the left iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
converges to
z_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
r>0
and let
D=B_{X}(z_{0},r)
. Since
\overline{D}
is compact and
F
is not a self-covering of
X
(see Corollary
2.8
), by the general Schwarz-Pick lemma Theorem
2.1
there is
0<k<1
such that

\omega_{X}\bigl(F(z),F(w)\bigr)\leq k\omega_{X}(z,w)
for all
z
,
w\in\overline{D}
. In particular, since
F(z_{0})=z_{0}
, we have
F(\overline{D})\subseteq\overline{B_{X}(z_{0},kr)}\subset D
.
Choose a real number
t
such that
kr<t<r
and put

\mathcal{U}=\{h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)\mid h(\overline{D})\subset B_{X}(z_{0},t)\}\;;

clearly
\cal U
is a neighbourhood of
F
. Notice that
D
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface, and
B_{X}(z_{0},t)
is a Bloch domain, because it is relatively compact in
D
.
If
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\cal U
then, by Corollary
3.3
.(iii), the corresponding right iterated function system converges in
D
—and hence, by Vitali theorem (Theorem
2.5
), in
X
—to a constant contained in
\overline{B_{X}(z_{0},t)}
, and thus (i) is proved.

For (ii), since
f_{\nu}\to F
we have
f_{\nu}\in\cal U
for all
\nu
large enough; truncating
L_{\nu}
by finitely many terms on the right and relabelling we can assume
without loss of generality that
f_{\nu}(\overline{D})\subset D
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Choose now
z\in\overline{D}
. We have
F^{\nu}(z)
,
L_{\nu}(z)\in\overline{D}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
; hence

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),F^{\nu}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),F\bigr(L_{\nu-1}(z)\bigr)\bigr)+\omega_{X}\bigl(F\bigr(L_{\nu-1}(z)\bigr),F^{\nu}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{w\in\overline{D}}\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{\nu}(w),F(w)\bigr)+k\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z),F^{\nu-1}(z)\bigr)\;.

Repeating this argument we get by induction

\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),F^{\nu}(z)\bigr)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}k^{j}\sup_{w\in\overline{D}}\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{\nu-j}(w),F(w)\bigr)\;.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
0<k<1
and, by assumption,
f_{\nu}\to F
uniformly on compact subsets, we can find
\nu_{0}
large enough so that
k^{j}<\frac{(1-k)\varepsilon}{4r}
and
\sup\limits_{w\in\overline{D}}\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{j}(w),F(w)\bigr)<\frac{(1-k)\varepsilon}{2}
as soon as
j\geq\nu_{0}/2
. Therefore if
\nu\geq\nu_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),F^{\nu}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\nu/2}k^{j}\sup_{w\in\overline{D}}\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{\nu-j}(w),F(w)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=\nu/2}^{\nu}k^{j}\sup_{w\in\overline{D}}\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{\nu-j}(w),F(w)\bigr)

\displaystyle<\left[\frac{(1-k)\varepsilon}{2}+2rk^{\nu/2}\right]\sum_{j=0}^{\nu/2}k^{j}<2\frac{(1-k)\varepsilon}{2}\frac{1}{1-k}=\varepsilon\;,

and so
\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),F^{\nu}(z)\bigr)\to 0
uniformly on
\overline{D}
as
\nu\to+\infty
. Since Theorem
1.2
implies
F^{\nu}\to z_{0}
, it follows that
L_{\nu}\to z_{0}
on
\overline{D}
and hence, again by Vitali theorem, on
X
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In Theorem
4.1
.(i) by changing
f_{0}
one can change the constant limit of the right iterated function system
\{R_{\nu}\}
.

The next example, taken from
[
4
]
, shows that in Theorem
4.1
.(ii) we cannot replace the hypothesis
f_{\nu}\to F
by the hypothesis
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathcal{U}

for any neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
F
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be given by
F(z)=\frac{1}{2}z
, and let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be a neighbourhood of
F
. Given
\delta>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, put
f_{\delta,\theta}(z)=\frac{1}{2}z+\delta e^{i\theta}
. Clearly
f_{\delta,\theta}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
as soon as
\delta<1/2
.

We claim that we can choose
\delta
small enough so that
f_{\delta,\theta}\in\mathcal{U}
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, fix a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{D}
and let
V\subset\mathbb{D}
be an open neighbourhood of
F(K)
, so that
F\in\mathcal{U}(K,V)=\{h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\mid h(K)\subset V\}
.
Since
F(K)\cap\partial V=\mathord{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle/\hfil$\crcr$\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\textstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\textstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}}
, there is a
\delta_{0}>0
such that
d\bigl(w,F(K)\bigr)<\delta_{0}
implies
w\in V
, where
d
denotes the Euclidean distance. As a consequence,
if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
we have
f_{\delta,\theta}(K)\subset V
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
contains a finite intersection of sets of the form
\mathcal{U}(K,V)
the claim follows.

Given
\{\theta_{\nu}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
it is easy to check by induction that the left iterated function system
\{L_{\nu}\}
generated by
\{f_{\delta,\theta_{\nu}}\}
is given by

L_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}z+\delta\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}\frac{1}{2^{j}}e^{i\theta_{\nu-j}}\;.

For instance, taking
e^{i\theta_{j}}=(-1)^{j}
we get

L_{\nu}(0)=\delta(-1)^{\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{j}

that does not converge when
\nu\to+\infty
, and thus
\{L_{\nu}\}
cannot be convergent.

The next case is when
\{F^{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent. If
f_{\nu}
converges to
F
fast enough then the dynamics of the left iterated function system generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
is dictated by the dynamics
of
\{F^{\nu}\}
:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface, and let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
be such that the sequence of iterates
\{F^{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent. Then we can find a sequence
of neighbourhoods
\mathcal{U}_{\nu}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
of
F
such that if
f_{\nu}\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
then the left iterated function system
\{L_{\nu}\}
generated by
\{f_{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent. Furthermore, if
X\subset\widehat{X}
is a hyperbolic domain in a compact Riemann surface
\widehat{X}
and
\{F^{\nu}\}
converges to a point
\tau\in\partial X
then

\{L_{\nu}\}
converges to
\tau
.

Proof.

Fix a reference point
z_{0}\in X
. For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
set

D_{\nu}=\overline{B_{X}\bigl(z_{0},1+\omega_{X}(F^{\nu}(z_{0}),z_{0})\bigr)}\;.

Since
\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)\to+\infty
as
\nu\to+\infty
we have
X=\bigcup\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}D_{\nu}
.
Given
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in D_{\nu}
such that
F(D_{\nu})\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{r}B_{X}\bigl(F(z_{j}),(3\cdot 2^{\nu+1})^{-1}\bigr)
.
Put
B_{j}=B_{X}\bigl(F(z_{j}),(3\cdot 2^{\nu+1})^{-1}\bigr)
,
K_{j}=D_{\nu}\cap F^{-1}(\overline{B_{j}})
and
\widetilde{B_{j}}=B_{X}\bigl(F(z_{j}),2^{-\nu-2}\bigr)
; in particular,
D_{\nu}=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{r}K_{j}
and if
z\in K_{j}

then
\omega_{X}\bigl(F(z),F(z_{j})\bigr)\leq(3\cdot 2^{\nu+1})^{-1}
. Finally, put

\mathcal{U}_{\nu}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}\bigl\{h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)\bigm|h(K_{j})\subset\widetilde{B_{j}}\bigr\}\;;

this is a neighbourhood of
F
because
F(K_{j})\subseteq\overline{B_{j}}\subset\widetilde{B_{j}}
for all
j=1,\ldots,r
by construction.

Take
h\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
. If
z\in D_{\nu}
we must have
z\in K_{j}
for some
j=1,\ldots,r
; then

(2)

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(h(z),F(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}\bigl(h(z),F(z_{j})\bigr)+\omega_{X}\bigl(F(z_{j}),F(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2^{\nu+2}}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{\nu+1}}<\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}\;.

Now take
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
with
f_{\nu}\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
; notice that (
2
) implies that
f_{\nu}\to F
. We will prove,
by induction, that

\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),F^{\nu+1}(z_{0})\bigr)<1-\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. For
\nu=0
it follows immediately from (
2
). Assume it holds for
\nu-1
; then

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),

\displaystyle F^{\nu+1}(z_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr)\bigr)+\omega_{X}\bigl(F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr),F^{\nu+1}(z_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr)\bigr)+\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0}),F^{\nu}(z_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle<\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr)\bigr)+1-\frac{1}{2^{\nu}}\;.

Now, since

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0}),F^{\nu}(z_{0})\bigr)+\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)

\displaystyle<1+\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)\;,

we have
L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\in D_{\nu}
. Therefore since
f_{\nu}\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
we can use (
2
) to get

\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr)\bigr)=\omega_{X}\bigl(f_{\nu}\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr),F\bigl(L_{\nu-1}(z_{0})\bigr)\bigr)<\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}

and thus

\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),\,F^{\nu+1}(z_{0})\bigr)<\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}+1-\frac{1}{2^{\nu}}=1-\frac{1}{2^{\nu+1}}

as claimed.

As a consequence for any
z\in X
we have

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),

\displaystyle z_{0}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu+1}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)-\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu+1}(z_{0}),L_{\nu}(z_{0})\bigr)-\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),L_{\nu}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{X}\bigl(F^{\nu+1}(z_{0}),z_{0}\bigr)-1-\omega_{X}(x_{0},z)\to+\infty

as
\nu\to+\infty
, and thus
\{L_{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent.

Finally, assume that
X\subset\widehat{X}
is a hyperbolic domain and that
F^{\nu}\to\tau\in\partial X
. Then since

\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z_{0}),\,F^{\nu+1}(z_{0})\bigr)<1
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
we can apply Proposition
2.4
to get
L_{\nu}(z_{0})\to\tau
.
Moreover, for any
z\in D
we also have
\omega_{X}\bigl(L_{\nu}(z),L_{\nu}(z_{0})\bigr)\leq\omega_{X}(z,z_{0})
; so another application of Proposition
2.4

yields
L_{\nu}(z)\to\tau
for all
z\in X
, and we are done.
∎

Surprisingly enough, if the convergence of
f_{\nu}
to
F
is too slow then the left iterated function system might not be convergent, as the next example shows.

Example 4.5
.

Let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+},\mathbb{H}^{+})
be given by
F(w)=w-1
. Moreover, for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
put

\varphi_{\nu}(w)=\frac{\nu w-1}{w+\nu}=\frac{w\cos\theta_{\nu}-\sin\theta_{\nu}}{w\sin\theta_{\nu}+\cos\theta_{\nu}},

where
\theta_{0}=\pi/2
and
\theta_{\nu}=\arctan\frac{1}{\nu}
for
\nu\geq 1
. It is easy to check that each
\varphi_{\nu}
is an elliptic automorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{+}
fixing
i
; furthermore
\varphi_{\nu}\to\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{H}^{+}}
as
\nu\to+\infty
.

Now let
g_{\nu}=\varphi_{\nu}\circ F\circ\varphi^{-1}_{\nu}
. By construction, each
g_{\nu}
is a parabolic automorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{+}
; furthermore we have

g_{\nu}(w)=\frac{(\nu^{2}+\nu+1)w-\nu^{2}}{w+\nu^{2}-\nu+1}\;,

and a quick computation shows that
g_{\nu}(\nu)=\nu
. In particular, for each
w\in\mathbb{H}^{+}
as soon as
k
is sufficiently large we have
g_{\nu}^{k}(w)
arbitrarily close to
\nu
. Moreover,
g_{\nu}\to F
as
\nu\to+\infty
.

We shall now build by induction a sequence
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+})
generating a left iterated function system
\{L_{\nu}\}
and an increasing sequence
\{\nu_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
with the following properties:

(a)

\nu_{0}=0
and
\nu_{1}=1
;

(b)

f_{0}=g_{0}
and
f_{1}=F
;

(c)

\nu_{2j}\geq\nu_{2j-1}+j
for all
j\geq 1
;

(d)

f_{\nu_{2j-1}+1}=\cdots=f_{\nu_{2j}-j}=g_{j}
and
f_{\nu_{2j}-j+1}=\cdots=f_{\nu_{2j+1}}=F
for all
j\geq 1
;

(e)

|L_{\nu_{2j}}(i)|<1/2^{j}
and
|L_{\nu_{2j+1}}(i)|>j
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since
|g_{0}(i)|<1
and
|F\circ g_{0}(i)|>0
condition (e) is satisfied for
j=0
.
Now choose
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|g_{1}^{n_{1}}\bigl(L_{\nu_{1}}(i)\bigr)-1|<1/2
; in particular,
\bigl|(F\circ g_{1}^{n_{1}})\bigl(L_{\nu_{1}}(i)\bigr)\bigr|<1/2
. Choose now
m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}

so that
\bigl|(F^{m_{1}+1}\circ g_{1}^{n_{1}})\bigl(L_{\nu_{1}}(i)\bigr)\bigr|>1
; putting
\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}+n_{1}+1
,
\nu_{3}=\nu_{2}+m_{1}
,
f_{\nu_{1}+1}=\cdots=f_{\nu_{2}-1}=g_{1}
and
f_{\nu_{2}}=\cdots=f_{\nu_{3}}=F
we get
|L_{\nu_{2}}(i)|<1/2
and
|L_{\nu_{3}}(i)|>2
, that is conditions (c)–(e) are satisfied for
j=1
.

Now given
j\geq 1
assume by induction that we have found
\nu_{0}<\cdots<\nu_{2j-1}
and
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\nu_{2j-1}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+})
satisfying (a)–(e). Choose
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|g_{j}^{n_{j}}\bigl(L_{\nu_{2j-1}}(i)\bigr)-j|<\frac{1}{2^{j}}\;;

in particular,
\bigl|(F^{j}\circ g_{1}^{n_{j}})\bigl(L_{\nu_{2j-1}}(i)\bigr)\bigr|<1/2^{j}
. Choose now
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}

so that
\bigl|(F^{m_{j}+j}\circ g_{j}^{n_{j}})\bigl(L_{\nu_{2j-1}}(i)\bigr)\bigr|>j
; putting
\nu_{2j}=\nu_{2j-1}+n_{j}+j
and
\nu_{2j+1}=\nu_{2j}+m_{j}
and choosing

f_{\nu_{2j-1}+1},\ldots,f_{\nu_{2j+1}}
as in (d) we get
|L_{\nu_{2j}}(i)|<1/2^{j}
and
|L_{\nu_{2j+1}}(i)|>j
, as required.

In this way we have constructed a sequence
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+})
converging (very slowly) to
F
generating a left iterated function system with
L_{\nu_{2j}}(i)\to 0

and
L_{\nu_{2j+1}}(i)\to\infty
as
j\to+\infty
; in particular
\{L_{\nu}\}
does not converge.

The left iterated function system we constructed in this example, though not converging, it is still compactly divergent. It would be interesting to find an example (or to prove that it does not exists) of a self-map
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
such that
\{F^{\nu}\}
is compactly divergent and of a sequence
\{f_{\nu}\}
converging to
F
so that
\{L_{\nu}\}
is not compactly divergent.

Finally, there is no hope to get a version of Theorem
4.4
for right iterated function systems:

Example 4.6
.

Let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+},\mathbb{H}^{+})
be given by
F(w)=w+1
, and define
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}^{+},\mathbb{H}^{+})
by setting
f_{0}(w)=i+e^{2\pi iz}
and
f_{\nu}=F
for
\nu\geq 1
. Then

f_{\nu}\to F
in the fastest possible way but
R_{\nu}=f_{0}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, and thus
\{R_{\nu}\}
is not even compactly divergent.

We are left with the case when
F
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphism of
X
. If the fundamental group of
X
is not abelian, Theorem
2.6
implies that
F
is isolated in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
; therefore any map close enough to
F
coincides with
F
and the study of random iteration of functions sufficiently close to
F
reduces to the study of the dynamics of
F
, which is trivial.

If the fundamental group of
X
is abelian, we know that
X
is biholomorphic either to
\mathbb{D}
or to
\mathbb{D}^{*}
or to an annulus
A(r,1)
with
0<r<1
.
If
X
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{*}
then every holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{*}
extends to a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, and thus random iteration on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
reduces to random iteration on
\mathbb{D}
.

If
X
is biholomorphic to an annulus
A(r,1)
then Proposition
2.7
says that
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
is open in
\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(X,X)
; in particular, maps sufficiently close to
F
are automorphisms of
X
. The group
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits\bigl(A(r,1)\bigr)
has two connected components,
A_{1}=\{\varphi_{\theta,1}\mid\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}

and
A_{-1}=\{\varphi_{\theta,-1}\mid\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where
\varphi_{\theta,1}(z)=e^{i\theta}z
and
\varphi_{\theta,-1}(z)=e^{i\theta}rz^{-1}
.
If
F\in A_{1}
then every holomorphic self-map of
A(r.1)
sufficiently close to
F
belongs to
A_{1}
; since
A_{1}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
, in this case random iteration of holomorphic self-maps close to
F
is reduced to random iteration on
\mathbb{D}
. If instead
F\in A_{-1}
then every holomorphic self-map of
A(r.1)
sufficiently close to
F
belongs to
A_{-1}
. Since it is easy to check that

\varphi_{\theta,-1}\circ\varphi_{\eta,-1}=\varphi_{\theta-\eta,1}\;,\ \varphi_{\theta,1}\circ\varphi_{\eta,-1}=\varphi_{\theta+\eta,-1}\ \hbox{and}\ \varphi_{\theta,-1}\circ\varphi_{\eta,1}=\varphi_{\theta-\eta,-1}\;,

we see that every iterated function system generated by self-maps close enough to
F
splits in the union of an iterated function system contained in
A_{1}
, obtained considering an even number of maps, and of the composition of
\varphi_{0,-1}
with an iterated function system again contained in
A_{1}
, obtained considering an odd number of maps. Thus in this case too we are led to the study of random iteration in
\mathbb{D}
.

Summing up, when
F
is a periodic or pseudoperiodic automorphism of
X
for our aims we can safely assume that
X=\mathbb{D}
. This situation has been studied in
[
4
]
; for the sake of completeness we conclude this paper by reporting the main result in this case, referring to
[
4
]

for proofs, examples and comments.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
F\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
be a periodic or pseudoperiodic (and hence elliptic) automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
; the case
F=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{D}}
is allowed. Let
\{f_{\nu}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be a sequence of non-constant holomorphic self-maps
of
\mathbb{D}
for which

(3)

\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\omega\bigl(f_{\nu}(a),F(a)\bigr)<+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\omega\bigl(f_{\nu}(b),F(b)\bigr)<+\infty

for two distinct points
a
,
b\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f_{\nu}\to F
as
\nu\to+\infty
, and the sequences
\{F^{-\nu}\circ L_{\nu}\}
and
\{R_{\nu}\circ F^{-\nu}\}
converge to non-constant
holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
.

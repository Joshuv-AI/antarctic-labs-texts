Blowup algebras of determinantal modules Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 13C40, 13A30, 13P10, Secondary 14N07, 14M12 Thanks: Keyword: Multi-Rees algebra, determinantal, Cohen–Macaulay, normality, singularity
Kuei-Nuan Lin and Yi-Huang Shen

Abstract
. We study the blowup algebras of the modules that are direct sums of ideals generated by either maximal minors of a ladder matrix or unit interval determinantal ideals. Specifically, we determine Gröbner bases for the presentation ideals of multi-Rees algebras and their special fiber rings. Our analysis reveals that the multi-blowup algebras are Koszul Cohen–Macaulay normal domains, possess rational singularities in characteristic zero, and are F-rational in positive characteristic.



1.
Introduction

Rees algebras, which have been extensively discussed in
[
37
]
, occupy a central position in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. Their applications extend to fields such as elimination theory, geometric modeling, chemical reaction networks, and algebraic statistics
[
11
,
16
,
17
,
26
]
. Equally significant are the determinantal varieties in these fields, as highlighted in
[
7
]
.

The classical concept of the Rees algebra of an ideal can be extended to the Rees algebra of a module. When
M
is a finitely generated module with a rank over a Noetherian ring
R
, the
Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}(M)
is defined as the symmetric algebra of
M
modulo its
R
-torsion submodule (see
[
19
]
). In the case where
M
is either the module of differentials or the module of top differential forms of the homogeneous coordinate ring of a projective
{\mathbb{K}}
-variety
Y
, the
special fiber ring

{\mathbb{K}}\otimes\mathcal{R}(M)
is the homogeneous coordinate ring of the tangential variety or the Gauss image of
Y
, respectively (see
[
34
]
).

In this note, we look at the special case where
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}I
is the direct sum of a homogeneous ideal
I
in the polynomial ring
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. In this context, the Rees algebra
is also known as the
multi-Rees algebra
of the ideal
I
. Similar to the classical case, it can be defined as the multi-graded
R
-subalgebra

\mathcal{R}(M)\coloneqq R[It_{1},\ldots,It_{r}]\subseteq R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}],

with auxiliary variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
introduced. This algebra is the homogeneous coordinate ring of the blowup along the subschemes defined by these ideals. The associated
special fiber ring
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}(M)
, corresponds to the image resulting from the blowup map.
These two blowup algebras can be represented as the quotients of polynomial rings:

\mathcal{R}(M)\cong S[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{J}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{F}(M)\cong\mathbb{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]/\mathcal{K},

where
\mu
is the total number of minimal generators of
M
.
One significant challenge in studying the multi-Rees algebras is determining the implicit equations of the presentation ideals
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
. Cox, Lin, and Sosa showed that a comprehensive understanding of
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
can illuminate the equilibrium solutions of chemical reaction networks
[
17
]
.
The problem of identifying the generating equations of
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
poses a substantial challenge in the fields of elimination theory and geometric modeling. For further details, please see, for example,
[
16
]
.

It is readily seen that
\mathcal{F}(M)
is isomorphic to the Segre product
\mathcal{F}(I)\#_{{\mathbb{K}}}{\mathbb{K}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
. Therefore, to establish the Koszul, normal, and similar properties for
\mathcal{F}(M)
, it suffices to do so for
\mathcal{F}(I)
; see for instance
[
22
,
3
]
. One may also argue that
\mathcal{R}(M)
is isomorphic to the Segre product
\mathcal{R}(I)\#_{R}R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
. Notice that this Segre product is taken over
R
, where the graded rings have
R
in the
0
-th degree. Since this is not about the Segre product of two graded rings defined over a field, its treatment does not fall into the framework of the classical papers
[
22
,
3
]
.

The study of determinantal ideals is another complex undertaking. Although the multi-Rees algebras for direct sums of maximal minors of a generic matrix have been scrutinized by Burns and Conca
[
8
]
, the problem of implicit equations for the multi-Rees algebras of direct sums of general determinantal ideals remains unresolved.

In this study, we will investigate the multi-blowup algebras of two classes of determinantal ideals. Specifically, we will extend the findings of Celikbas et al.
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
and Almousa et al.
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
to the realm of multi-blowup algebras.
The conclusions we present are grounded in the understanding of the blowup algebras
associated with maximal minors of generic matrices.

Consider a generic matrix
\mathbf{X}
, where the entries are distinct variables. Let
I
denote the ideal of all its maximal minors. It is well-known that the special fiber ring
\mathcal{F}(I)
of
I
forms the coordinate ring of a Grassmannian variety. Moreover, both
\mathcal{R}(I)
and
\mathcal{F}(I)
are Cohen–Macaulay algebras.
The normality of
\mathcal{F}(I)
then follows from its Cohen–Macaulay property, given the smoothness of the Grassmannian variety.
Moreover, the powers and symbolic powers of
I
coincide, implying that
\mathcal{R}(I)
is normal. Furthermore, the deformation theorem shows that both
R(I)
and
F(I)
have quadratic Gröbner bases.
Consequently, both blowup algebras are Koszul, see details in
[
7
, Chapter 6]
. Then by
[
4
]
, all powers of the ideal
I
have a linear resolution.

Let
L=I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
be the ideal generated by the maximal minors of a ladder matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
(see Definition
4.1
).
When the initial ideal of
L
can be associated with a Ferrers diagram, the blowup algebras have been studied by
[
10
, Proposition 4.3]
. Here,
we will investigate both
\mathcal{R}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}L)
and
\mathcal{F}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}L)
. Additionally, let
U
be a unit interval determinantal ideal (refer to Definition
6.1
). Our work will take care of
\mathcal{F}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}U)
and
\mathcal{R}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}U)
.

It is important to point out that although the ideal
L
is associated with the ladder matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
, it is not the ladder determinantal ideals of minors in the literature.
Recall that this notion was introduced and studied by Abhyankar
[
1
]
, and subsequently by other authors. For example, Conca and De Negri showed that the Rees algebra of a ladder determinantal ideal coincides with the symmetric algebra
[
12
, Theorem 5.2]
.
In contrast, even when
r=1
, the Rees algebra of
L
may not be equal to its symmetric algebra.
In a recent contribution, Ramos and Simis identified the presentation ideal of the special fiber of cofactors for a given square ladder
[
32
, Corollary 3.11]
.
However, their work pertains to a completely different class of ideals and does not encompass considerations of multi-Rees algebras.

The following is an outline of the road map.
We first establish basic terminology and notation in
Section

2
. In
Proposition

2.4
, we provide a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra isomorphism between the multigraded Segre product of
\mathcal{R}(J)
and
\mathcal{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}J)
for a monomial ideal
J
. We then use the toric fiber product to extract a Gröbner basis of the Segre product in
Theorem

2.6
.
After that,
we start with a natural distributive lattice structure from the index set of the maximal minors, whose Hibi ring is isomorphic to the toric ring related to the initial ideal of the maximal minors of the generic matrix. We prove that a non-empty sublattice induces an isomorphism of its Hibi ring to the associated toric subring in
Proposition

3.2
. The structure of the ladder matrix or the unit interval characteristic ensures that we have the sublattice property (
Lemma

4.4
and
Lemma

6.2
). Therefore, we obtain quadratic Gröbner bases for the fiber cones of the direct sum of the initial ideals of
L
and
U
(see Theorems
4.8
and
6.3
).
In contrast to the constructive methodologies proposed by Almousa et al.
[
2
]
and Celikbas et al.
[
10
]
,
this approach is inspired by the work of De Negri
[
18
]
for the fiber ring of a lexsegment ideal.
Our proof strategy is considerably more concise and efficient than those in
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
with regard to
the special fiber of the ideal cases, while also recovering their results.
As a matter of fact, in
[
2
]
, the authors use the so-called sorting order and clique-sorted structure to represent the comparable pairs in the Hibi ideal, which demands cumbersome notations and more involved index tracking.
Subsequently, we leverage the
\ell
-exchange property to derive the Gröbner basis of the Rees algebra of the initial ideals of maximal minors of ladder matrices. We then use the Segre product to obtain the Gröbner basis of the multi-Rees algebra of the direct sum of these initial ideals (see
Proposition

2.4
,
Theorem

2.6
, and
Theorem

4.15
).

In the final sections of our work, we employ the
Sagbi
(Khovanskii) technique to derive the defining equations of the multi-Rees algebras and their fibers of the original determinantal modules.
Consequently, we can demonstrate that
presentation ideals of these multi-blowup algebras have
quadratic Gröbner bases (see Theorems
4.8
and
6.4
).
Moreover, these algebras are Koszul normal Cohen–Macaulay domains, and we provide a classification of their singularities (see Corollaries
5.7
and
6.4
).

The main focus of this work is to provide an explicit description of the presentation ideals of the multi-Rees algebras and their corresponding fibers for the original determinantal modules. A byproduct of our approach is that many important properties of these algebras emerge naturally using the distributive lattice structure, allowing Hibi ring theory to be applied.
In contrast, the existing literature about finding properties of Rees algebra often derives these properties using powers of ideals or modules; see for example,
[
8
]
and
[
30
]
.

The explicit descriptions of presentation ideals have practical significance in real-world applications. Our approach, based on Gröbner bases and
Sagbi
bases, enables the
application of Gröbner deformation and
Sagbi
deformation to compute numerical invariants, such as regularity and multiplicity, of these algebras via the initial ideals of their defining ideals; see, for example,
[
15
]
and
[
29
]
.

Our main results are summarized below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
is a ladder matrix obtained from a generic matrix
\mathbf{X}
. Let
L=I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
be the
determinantal ideal generated by the maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
. Meanwhile, assume that
U
is a unit interval determinantal ideal arising from some of the maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}
. Let
M_{L}=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}L
and
M_{U}=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}U
be the modules associated to those ideals.

(1)

The presentation ideals of
\mathcal{F}(M_{L})
and
\mathcal{F}(M_{U})
are generated by the inherited Plücker relations, respectively. These relations form a quadratic Gröbner basis of the ideal they generate.

(2)

The multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(M_{L})
is of fiber type, meaning that its presentation ideal is generated by the relations of the symmetric algebra of
M_{L}
and by the Plücker relations on the maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
. Moreover, these equations form a Gröbner basis of the ideal they generate.

(3)

The blowup algebras
\mathcal{R}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{F}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{R}(M_{U})
, and
\mathcal{F}(M_{U})
are Cohen–Macaulay normal domains. Furthermore, they have rational singularities in characteristic zero and are
F
-rational in positive characteristic.

(4)

The blowup algebras
\mathcal{R}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{F}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{R}(M_{U})
, and
\mathcal{F}(M_{U})
are Koszul.
In particular, the powers of
L
have a linear resolution.

(5)

The natural algebra generators of
\mathcal{R}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{F}(M_{L})
,
\mathcal{R}(M_{U})
, and
\mathcal{F}(M_{U})
are
Sagbi
bases of the algebras they generate.

Preliminaries on the multi-Rees algebra and its special fiber

We start by going over the basic facts and notions that we will employ in this work.
In particular, we will take the following convention for the notations.

Notation 2.1
.

(1)

To avoid confusion, the set of positive integers is denoted by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and the set of non-negative integers is denoted by
{\mathbb{N}}
.

(2)

If
p
and
q
are two positive integers such that

, we denote the set

by

2.1.
Blowup algebras

In this subsection, we explain in more detail the presentation ideals of the blowup algebras, tailored to our determinantal setting.

Let
m,n
, and
r
be three positive integers. Suppose that
\mathbf{X}=(x_{i,j})
is a generic
n\times m
matrix with the entries
x_{1,1},\dots,x_{n,m}
being indeterminates over a fixed field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{X}]
be the polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{K}}
in these variables.
Suppose that
I
is an equigenerated ideals in
R
, and
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{\mu}\}
is a minimal homogeneous generating set of
I
. In the following, we will consider the blowup algebras associated with
M=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}I
.

To describe the presentations of the
Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}(M)\coloneqq R[It_{1},\ldots,It_{r}]=\bigoplus_{a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}}I^{a_{1}}\cdots I^{a_{r}}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{a_{r}}\subseteq R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}],

and the
special fiber ring

\mathcal{F}(M)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}(M)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}\cong{\mathbb{K}}[f_{i}t_{j}\mid i\in[\mu],j\in[r]]

of this module,
we introduce two polynomial rings over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
:

R[\mathbf{T}]\coloneqq R[T_{i,j}\mid i\in[\mu],j\in[r]]\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{T}]\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[T_{i,j}\mid i\in[\mu],j\in[r]].

From them, we have
two surjective ring homomorphisms:

\displaystyle\phi:R[\mathbf{T}]\to\mathcal{R}(M)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi:{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{T}]\to\mathcal{F}(M).

Here, for each
l\in[n]
,
k\in[m]
,
i\in[\mu_{j}]
and
j\in[r]
, we require that
\phi(x_{l,k})=x_{l,k}
, and
\phi(T_{i,j})=\psi(T_{i,j})=f_{i,j}t_{j}
.

Definition 2.2
.

(1)

The kernel ideals
\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\phi)
and
\mathcal{K}\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(\psi)
are called the
presentation ideals
of
\mathcal{R}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(M)
, respectively. Alternatively, they are sometimes referred to as
the
Rees ideal
and the
special fiber ideal
, respectively.
Meanwhile, the
defining equations
of an algebra form a system of generators for its presentation ideal.

(2)

If
\mathcal{K}S
together with the relations of the symmetric algebra

\operatorname{Sym}(M)

generate
\mathcal{J}
, then
\mathcal{R}(M)
is
of fiber type
.

From now on, in this paper, all unspecified tensor products
\otimes
are taken over the common ground field
\mathbb{K}
.

Let
A
and
B
be two graded rings over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
To recover the presentation ideal of the Segre product of
A
and
B

and

[
27

21
]

In multigraded case, Kahle and Rauh show in

that
the Segre product is presented by the toric fiber product of two ideal, under some assumptions. The case that we are interested in this paper can take advantage of this observation. As a result, we establish notations for Rees algebra of initial ideals and recall some of the work related to the toric fiber product and Segre product here.

In the context under consideration, the set
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{\mu}\}
is also assumed to be a Gröbner basis of degree

I

\phi^{\ast}:R[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I))\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{\ast}:\mathbb{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{\mu}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I))

Then
\mathcal{J}^{\ast}=\operatorname{ker}(\phi^{\ast})

\operatorname{deg}(T_{i})=\operatorname{deg}(t_{j})=(0,1).

Let

Proposition 2.4

Adopting the notations established above, we have

\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{T}]/(\mathcal{J}^{\ast}\times_{\mathcal{A}}0)\cong\mathcal{R}(\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I))\#_{\mathbb{N}\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{K}[y,t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]\cong\mathcal{R}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I)),

Theorem 2.6

Adopt the setting in

\operatorname{Lift}(G)\cup\operatorname{Quad}_{\mathbf{C}}
is a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{J}^{\ast}\times_{\mathcal{A}}0
in
\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{Z},T_{i,j}\mid i\in[\mu],j\in[r]]
with respect to some weight order

.
In particular, we have

\mathcal{R}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I))\cong\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{Z},T_{i,j}\mid i\in[\mu],j\in[r]]/(\operatorname{Lift}(G)\cup\operatorname{Quad}_{\mathbf{C}}).

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{J}^{*}
has a squarefree initial, then so does
\mathcal{J}^{*}\times_{\mathcal{A}}0
.

Proof.

The Gröbner basis statement is by
[
33
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[

, Corollary 14]
.
The isomorphism in the “in particular” part is established by
Proposition

2.4
. The final squarefree statement follows from
[
36

.
∎

In this subsection, we will only recall the definition of a

Definition 2.7

2.3.
Distributive lattice and Hibi ring

Given that our treatment draws upon the Hibi ring theory concerning distributive lattices, it is necessary to elucidate the pertinent terminology.
Let
\mathscr{L}
be a finite lattice. By definition, it is a poset with respect to a partial order
\leq
. Furthermore, for any two elements
a,b\in\mathscr{L}
, there is a unique greatest lower bound
a\wedge b
, called the
meet
of
a
and
b
, and there is a unique least upper bound
a\vee b
, called the
join
of
a
and
b
.
A subposet
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{L}
is called a
sublattice
if
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
itself is a lattice, and for any
a,b\in\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
, the meet and join of
a
and
b
in
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
coincide with those in
\mathscr{L}
, respectively.

Suppose that the join and meet operations of
\mathscr{L}
satisfy

(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge c)=a\wedge(b\vee c)\quad\text{and}\quad(a\vee b)\wedge(a\vee c)=a\vee(b\wedge c)

for all
a,b,c\in\mathscr{L}
, then
\mathscr{L}
is called a
distributive lattice
.

Given a distributive lattice
\mathscr{L}
, we can consider the polynomial (known as the
Hibi relation
)

f_{a,b}\coloneqq T_{a}T_{b}-T_{a\wedge b}T_{a\vee b}

in the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{c}\mid c\in\mathscr{L}]
over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
The ideal
I_{\mathscr{L}}\coloneqq\braket{f_{a,b}\mid a,b\in\mathscr{L}}
is called the
join-meet ideal
or
Hibi ideal
associated with
\mathscr{L}
.

Let
<
be a monomial order on
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{c}\mid c\in\mathscr{L}]
. If for all incomparable
a,b\in\mathscr{L}
, one has
\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{a,b})=T_{a}T_{b}
, the monomial order
<
is called
compatible
in the language of
[
23
]
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathscr{L}}
be the collection of all
f_{a,b}
such that
a
and
b
are incomparable in
\mathscr{L}
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\mathscr{L}}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathscr{L}}
by
[
23
, Theorem 6.17]
with respect to any compatible monomial order.

Furthermore,
I_{\mathscr{L}}
is a prime ideal. The quotient ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathscr{L}]\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[T_{c}\mid c\in\mathscr{L}]/I_{\mathscr{L}}
is called the
Hibi ring
associated with
\mathscr{L}
. It is known that
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathscr{L}]
is a toric ring, by
[
23
, Theorem 6.19]
.

3.
Initial algebra of the multi-fiber ring of generic matrices

The presentation ideal of the fiber ring for the ideal generated by the maximal minors of a generic matrix is well-known to be generated by the Plücker relations. This fiber ring serves as the coordinate ring of the Grassmannian variety. Additionally, the maximal minors constitute a
Sagbi
basis of the fiber ring, as detailed in
[
20
, Theorem 6.46]
. Specifically, a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal for the fiber ring of the initial ideal of the maximal minors can be lifted to a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal for the fiber ring of the maximal minors, generated by Plücker relations. Motivated by this, our goal is to determine a Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal for the fiber ring of the direct sum of the initial ideal of the maximal minors.

As in
Section

2
,
\mathbf{X}=(x_{i,j})
is an
n\times m
generic matrix over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
, where
n\leq m
. Meanwhile,
R={\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{X}]
is the associated polynomial ring, equipped with the term order
\tau
, as delineated in
Setting

2.8
.

Let
I_{n}(\mathbf{X})
be the ideal in
R
, generated by the maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}
. It is well-known that the set of maximal minors is a Gröbner basis of
I_{n}(\mathbf{X})
with respect to any diagonal monomial order, by
[
20
, Theorem 6.33]
.
To take advantage of this information,
let

\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\{{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}\mid a_{1}<\cdots<a_{n}\leq m\},

and we write

[{\bm{a}}]\coloneqq\det[x_{i,a_{j}}\mid i,j\in[n]]

for the maximal minor of
\mathbf{X}
with columns indexed by the tuple
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
.
Since
\tau
is a diagonal monomial order, we have

\operatorname{in}_{\tau}(I_{n}(\mathbf{X}))=\braket{\mathbf{x}_{\bm{a}}\coloneqq x_{1,a_{1}}\cdots x_{n,a_{n}}\mid{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}}.

There is a natural poset structure on
\mathcal{D}
, given by

(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\leq(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a_{i}\leq b_{i}\quad\text{for all $i$.}

Under this partial ordering,
\mathcal{D}
becomes a lattice.
In fact, the
join
is given by

(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\vee(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})=(\max\{a_{1},b_{1}\},\dots,\max\{a_{n},b_{n}\}),

while the
meet
is given by

(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\wedge(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})=(\min\{a_{1},b_{1}\},\dots,\min\{a_{n},b_{n}\}).

It is obvious that
\mathcal{D}
becomes a distributive lattice with respect to the above meet and join operations.

By abuse of notation, we will also consider
[r]\coloneqq\{1,2,\dots,r\}
to be a linearly ordered set with respect to the natural comparison. It is a distributive lattice, where
i\vee j=\max\{i,j\}
and
i\wedge j=\min\{i,j\}
for all
i
and
j
in
[r]
.
Furthermore, the Cartesian product lattice of
\mathcal{D}
and
[r]
,
\mathcal{D}\times[r]
, is still distributive.

As mentioned in the previous section, a Hibi ring is a toric ring. However, it is sometimes too naive to expect that endowing a finite set of integral vectors
A
with a distributive lattice structure will automatically result in an isomorphism between the associated Hibi ring and the semigroup ring
{\mathbb{K}}[A]
. Therefore, we must extend
[
20
, Theorem 6.45]
to the upcoming
Proposition

3.2
, since we intend to apply Hibi ring theory to our toric rings.

Proposition 3.1
.

The toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
is isomorphic to the Hibi ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
.
In particular, the join-meet ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
is the toric ideal of the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
in
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
. The set
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
of nontrivial Hibi relations
is a Gröbner basis of this toric ideal with respect to compatible monomial orders.

Proof.

The ring map

\psi^{\ast}:{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]\to{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]],\quad T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}

is clearly surjective. Furthermore, the join-meet ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
is a subideal of
\operatorname{ker}(\psi^{\ast})
. Since both the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D},i\in[r]]
and the Hibi ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
are integral domains, it suffices to show that the Krull dimensions of these two rings are the same.

On one hand, since
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D},i\in[r]]
is a finitely generated integral domain over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{c}}t_{i}\mid(\mathbf{c},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr.deg}_{{\mathbb{K}}}({\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{c}}t_{1}\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{D}])+(r-1)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{c}}\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{D}])+(r-1)

\displaystyle=n(m-n)+1+(r-1),

where the last equality follows from
[
20
, Theorem 6.45]
.

On the other hand, it follows from
[
20
, Lemma 6.44]
that the join irreducible elements of
\mathcal{D}
are
{\bm{c}}_{ik}\coloneqq(1,2,\dots,i,i+1+k,i+2+k,\dots,n+k)
with
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
and
k=1,\dots,m-n
. Hence one can easily check that the join irreducible elements of
\mathcal{D}\times[r]
are
{\bm{c}}_{ik}^{\prime}\coloneqq(1,2,\dots,i,i+1+k,i+2+k,\dots,n+k,1)
and
{\bm{a}}_{n\ell}\coloneqq(1,\ldots,n,\ell)
with
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
,
k=1,\dots,m-n
, and
\ell\in[2,r]
. It follows from
[
23
, Theorem 6.38]
that
\operatorname{dim}({\mathbb{K}}[\mathcal{D}\times[r]])=n(m-n)+(r-1)+1
. Therefore, the two integral domains have the same Krull dimension, as desired.
∎

From now on, we fix a compatible monomial order
<
on
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be a nonempty sublattice of
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
Then the set
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}
of nontrivial Hibi relations
in the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
is a Gröbner basis of the toric ideal of the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
with respect to the monomial order induced from
<
.
In particular, the join-meet ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}

in
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
is the toric ideal of the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
, and the toric subring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{{\bm{a}}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
is isomorphic to the Hibi ring

{\mathbb{K}}[\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
be the set of nontrivial Hibi relations associated to the product lattice
\mathcal{D}\times[r]
. We have seen in
Proposition

3.1
that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
is a Gröbner basis of the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}\subset{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
of the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{\bm{a}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]]
, with respect to the compatible monomial order
<
.

Every
f\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}\times[r]}
takes the form

f_{({\bm{a}},i),({\bm{b}},j)}\coloneqq T_{{\bm{a}},i}T_{{\bm{b}},j}-T_{({\bm{a}},i)\vee({\bm{b}},j)}T_{({\bm{a}},i)\wedge({\bm{b}},j)}

for non-comparable
({\bm{a}},i),({\bm{b}},j)\in\mathcal{D}\times[r]
. If
\operatorname{in}(f_{({\bm{a}},i),({\bm{b}},j)})=T_{{\bm{a}},i}T_{{\bm{b}},j}\in{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{c}},k}\mid({\bm{c}},k)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
, it is clear that
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Therefore,
{\bm{a}}\wedge{\bm{b}},{\bm{a}}\vee{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
by our assumption. This implies that
f\in{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{c}},k}\mid({\bm{c}},k)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
. Therefore, it follows from
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}
is a Gröbner basis of the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}\subset{\mathbb{K}}[T_{{\bm{a}},i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
of the toric ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{x}_{\bm{a}}t_{i}\mid({\bm{a}},i)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]]
, with respect to the monomial order induced from
<
.
It remains to notice the trivial fact that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}
generates the join-meet ideal
I_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times[r]}
.
∎

4.
Multi-Rees algebras of the initial of ideal of maximal minors of ladder matrices

The ensuing next two sections are devoted to the examination of multi-Rees algebra associated with the maximal minors of a ladder matrix, thereby extending the findings presented
in
[
10
]
.

In this preliminary section, the focus is on the algebra defined by the initials.
For the sake of reference,

\mathbf{X}
is an
n\times m
generic matrix with the assumption
n\leq m
.

Definition 4.1
.

A subset
\mathbf{L}
of
\mathbf{X}
is called a
ladder
if it has the following property: whenever
x_{i,j},x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{L}
and
i\leq i^{\prime}
,
j\geq j^{\prime}
, then
x_{u,v}\in\mathbf{L}
for any pair
(u,v)
with
i\leq u\leq i^{\prime}
and
j^{\prime}\leq v\leq j
. In other words, a ladder has the property that whenever the upper right and the lower left corners of a submatrix of
\mathbf{X}
belong to the ladder, then all the indeterminates in the submatrix belong to the ladder.
As a result, there are integers

\displaystyle 1=\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\ell_{n}\leq m

and

\displaystyle 1\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{n}=m,

such that the element
x_{i,j}
of
\mathbf{X}
belongs to
\mathbf{L}
if and only if
\ell_{i}\leq j\leq k_{i}
.

Given a ladder
\mathbf{L}
, there are two types of minor ideals associated with it.

(1)

Let
I_{t}(\mathbf{L})
be the ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{L}]
generated by all the
t
-minors of
\mathbf{X}
involving only indeterminates of
\mathbf{L}
. This ideal is known as a
ladder determinantal ideal
in the literature. The
ladder determinantal ring

{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{L}]/I_{t}(\mathbf{L})
has been well studied; see, for instance,

[
1
,
14
,
31
]
.
It is known that this ring is a Cohen–Macaulay normal domain.

(2)

Meanwhile, let
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
be the matrix obtained from
\mathbf{X}
by setting to zero all the elements not in
\mathbf{L}
.
We will call this matrix the
ladder matrix
associated with
\mathbf{L}
. Let
I_{t}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
be the ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{L}]
generated by all the
t
-minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
. In general, the ideal
I_{t}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
is not prime.

In this paper, we are only interested in the ideal
L=I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
, which is generated by the maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
. We will demonstrate that its multi-Rees algebra exhibits favorable properties. For
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
, we write
[{\bm{a}}]
for the maximal minor of
\mathbf{X}
spanned by the columns corresponding to the indices
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
. Similarly, we will let
[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}
denote the maximal minor of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}\mid[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}\neq 0\}
, and call it the
associated column index set
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{D}
. Then
[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}\neq 0
if and only if the
x_{i,a_{i}}\in\mathbf{L}
for all
i\in[n]
.

Proof.

The monomial
x_{1a_{1}}x_{2a_{2}}\cdots x_{na_{n}}
is a term in the full expansion of the determinant
[{\bm{a}}]
. If
x_{i,a_{i}}\in\mathbf{L}
for all
i\in[n]
, this monomial is also a nonzero term of
[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}
. In particular,
[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}\neq 0
.

On the other hand, if the
(i_{0},a_{i_{0}})
entry of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
is zero for some
i_{0}\in[n]
, then either
a_{i_{0}}<\ell_{i_{0}}
or
a_{i_{0}}>k_{i_{0}}
. In the first case, the
(i,j)
entry of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
is
0
for all
i\geq i_{0}
and
j\leq a_{i_{0}}
. Using the Laplace cofactor expansion from linear algebra, it is easy to see that
[{\bm{a}}]_{\mathbf{L}}=0
. The second case is symmetric and similar.
∎

From this point forward, we will assume that the associated column index set
\mathcal{L}
is nonempty. First of all, we will show that
\mathcal{L}
inherits the lattice structure of
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the componentwise comparison,
\mathcal{L}
becomes a sublattice of
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

In the definition of the ladder
\mathbf{L}
, we have the left boundary
{\bm{\ell}}=(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n})
and the right boundary
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
. Notice that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
is a distributive lattice with respect to the componentwise comparison, and
\mathcal{D}
is a sublattice of it.
Let
{\bm{\ell}}^{\dagger}\coloneqq\bigwedge\{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}\mid{\bm{\ell}}\leq{\bm{a}}\}

{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\coloneqq\bigvee\{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}\mid{\bm{a}}\leq{\bm{k}}\}

It is readily seen that
{\bm{\ell}}^{\dagger},{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{L}
is precisely the closed interval
[{\bm{\ell}}^{\dagger},{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]\coloneqq\{{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{D}\mid{\bm{\ell}}^{\dagger}\leq{\bm{a}}\leq{\bm{k}}^{{\dagger}}\}
of the lattice
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Suppose that
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathcal{D}
satisfy

{\bm{a}}\in[{\bm{\ell}}^{\dagger},{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]

{\bm{b}}\in{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}

is an

in the sense of
[

is a subvariety of the toric variety associated with

[

Similar to the generic matrix
\mathbf{X}
, the canonical generators in the set

form a Gröbner basis.

Lemma 4.6
.

The nonzero maximal minors of a ladder matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
form a minimal generating set for
L=I_{n}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}})
and
serve as a universal Gröbner basis for the determinantal ideal

Proof.

Notice that the ladder matrix
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
is a sparse matrix. Therefore, it follows from
[
5

that

has a linear free resolution arising from a specialization of the Eagon–Northcott complex. In particular, nonzero maximal minors of
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{L}}
form a minimal generating set of

The remaining universal Gröbner basis property follows from
[
5
, Proposition 5.4]
.
∎

Clearly,

Remark 4.9

Let

The fiber ring

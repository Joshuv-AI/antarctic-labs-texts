Abstract
In this paper, we obtain explicit expressions for Pandharipande–Pixton–Zvonkine relations in the second rational cohomology of ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} and comparing the result with Arbarello–Cornalba’s theorem we prove Pixton’s conjecture in this case.

Introduction

The study of cohomology of the moduli space of curves was initiated by Mumford
[
3
]
in the 1980s. The most progress has been made in understanding the subring
R^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
of tautological classes. A systematic study by Faber and Zagier of the algebra of
\kappa
classes on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of nonsingular genus g curves led to a conjecture in 2000 of a concise set FZ of relations among
\kappa
classes in

R^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})\subset A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}).

There are several proofs of the Faber–Zagier conjecture, one of them is given by Pandharipande and Pixton in
[
5
]
, exploiting moduli space of stable quotients on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Recent progress in 2012 has appeared in Pixton’s paper
[
8
]
about hypothetically the full set of relations in the tautological ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Pixton’s relations recover FZ when restricted to
{\mathcal{M}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}_{g}}}
.

Using homogeneity of Witten’s 3-spin class,
authors of
[
6
]
proved Pixton’s relations hold in
H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)
, proving Faber–Zagier conjecture in cohomology.
The set of Pixton’s relations (in Chow or cohomology) is conjectured to be full.

In this paper, we explicitly obtain the Pandharipande–Pixton–Zvonkine (PPZ or also called
r
-spin) relations
[
6
,
7
]
in the group
RH^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)

(although we omit the easiest case
g=0
for brevity). We show that the PPZ relations we obtained coincide with relations from Arbarello–Cornalba’s result
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
. In
[
1
]
, the authors proved that their set of relations in
H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)
is full. Also
using the fact that PPZ (for
r
= 3) relations coincide with Pixton’s relations and hold in
H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)
, proved in
[
6
]
, we prove Pixton’s conjecture in cohomology for this codimension.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Tautological classes on
\boldsymbol{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}

Throughout the article, we are working with cohomology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the Deligne–Mumford compactification by stable curves of the moduli space of nonsingular complex genus
g
curves with
n
markings, see
[
13
]
for the survey. Let

\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

be the universal curve. Considering
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
as orbifolds is sufficient for our purposes.

The fiber of a cotangent line bundles
\mathbb{L}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
over a point
x\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a cotangent line to a curve
(C_{x},x)\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}
at
i
-th marked points.
Denote the first Chern class of the line bundles
\mathbb{L}_{i}
by
\psi_{i}

(
\psi
-classes
)

\displaystyle\psi_{i}=c_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big).

\kappa
-classes
are defined via the universal map
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}=\pi_{*}\big(\psi^{i+1}_{n+1}\big)\in H^{2i}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big).

Natural maps between moduli spaces of curves include:

(i)

Maps forgetting the last m points and further stabilizing the curve

p_{m}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

(ii)

Gluing maps of separating kind

r\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

which identify the point with label
n_{1}+1
on the first curve with point label
n_{2}+1
on the second curve.

(iii)

Gluing maps of nonseparating kind

q\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,n+2}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

which identifies points with labels
n+1
and
n+2
.

Let

RH^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)\subset H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)

be the subring of tautological
classes in the rational cohomology ring. It is defined as the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebra closed under push-forwards by forgetting and gluing maps.

First examples of elements in
RH^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
include the following classes.

1\in H^{0}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
is tautological since
RH^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
is a ring with unit by definition. Classes represented by boundary strata and their intersections also lie in tautological ring since they are images under gluing maps.
It is clear that
\kappa
- and
\psi
-classes lie in
RH^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
from their definition and from relation
\psi_{i}=-\pi_{*}\big(D^{2}_{i}\big)
, where
D_{i}\in H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1},\mathbb{Q}\big)
is a class of a divisor whose generic point is represented by a nodal curve with two irreducible components of genera
g
and
0
and

i
-th and
(n+1)
-th points lie on a rational component.

The natural question are the rings
RH^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)\subset H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
isomorphic is open but there was a significant progress from the pioneering studies of moduli of curves initiated by Mumford.

2.2
Cohomological field theories

We recall here the basic definitions of a cohomological field theory (CohFT) by Kontsevich and Manin, see
[
4
]
for a survey.

It consists of the following data (
V
,
\eta
,
{\mathbf{1}}
,
(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
), where

(i)

V
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space,

(ii)

\eta
is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear 2-form (metric),

(iii)

{\mathbf{1}}\in V
is a distinguished element(unit vector).

Choosing a basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
V
, denote by
\eta_{jk}=\eta(e_{j},e_{k})
the value of the metric on the basis vectors and by
\eta^{jk}
the inverse matrix.
Given these data
(V,\eta,{\mathbf{1}})
the
(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
is a collection of linear operators

c_{g,n}\in H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes n}
.
It means that
c_{g,n}
associate a (non-homogeneous)cohomology class
c_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})\!\in H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)
to a vector

x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}\in V^{\otimes n}
.

The CohFT axioms imposed on
c_{g,n}
are the following.

Definition 2.1
.

A tuple (
V
,
\eta
,
{\mathbf{1}}
,
(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
) is called a
cohomological field theory
(
with unit
)
if it satisfies the following axioms:

(i)

For all
x_{i}\in V
,

\displaystyle c_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})=c_{g,n}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{\sigma(n)})

for each
\sigma
in the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
acting on the set of marked points and simultaneously on
copies of
V
in the tensor product
V^{\otimes n}
.

(ii)

The pullback of
c_{g,n}
under the gluing map
q
of nonseparating kind equals the contraction of
c_{g-1,n+2}
with

\eta^{jk}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{k}
inserted at the two identified points
1
1

1

Here and in the rest of the paper, we use the Einstein summation convention.

\displaystyle q^{*}c_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})=\sum_{j,k}\eta^{jk}c_{g-1,n+2}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{k})

in
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,n+2},\mathbb{Q})
for all
x_{i}\in V
.
The pullback of
c_{g,n}
under a gluing map
r
of separating kind equals the contraction
c_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\otimes c_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}
again with
\eta^{jk}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{k}
inserted at the two identified points

\displaystyle r^{*}(c_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}))

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{j,k}\eta^{jk}c_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n_{1}}\otimes e_{j})\otimes c_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}(x_{n_{1}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}\otimes e_{k})

in
H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},{n_{1}+1}},\mathbb{Q}\big)\otimes H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},{n_{2}+1}},\mathbb{Q}\big)
for all
x_{i}\in V
.

(iii)

The pullback of
c_{g,n}
under the map
p
forgetting the last point is required to satisfy

\displaystyle c_{g,n+1}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n}\otimes{\mathbf{1}})=p^{*}c_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})\

for all
x_{i}\in V
.
In addition, the equality

c_{0,3}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes{\mathbf{1}})=\eta(x_{i},x_{j})

is required for all
{x_{i}\in V}
.

Remark 2.2
.

A tuple (
V
,
\eta
,
(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0})
is called a
cohomological field theory
(
without unit
)
if it satisfies properties (i) and (ii) above.

The degree zero part
\omega
of a CohFT
c=(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
is called the
topological part
of
c

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n}=[c_{g,n}]^{0}\in H^{0}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes n}.

Via property (ii) one sees that
\omega_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})

is determined by considering stable curves with a maximal number of nodes.
Such a curve is obtained by identifying several rational curves with three marked points. The value of
\omega_{g,n}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})
is
thus uniquely specified by the values of
\omega_{0,3}
and by the quadratic form
\eta
. In other words, given
V
and
\eta
, a topological part is uniquely determined by the associated quantum product, introduced in the next subsection.

Our main example of CohFT will be the CohFT associated with Witten’s
r
-spin class and its shifted version.

Example 2.3

(Witten’s
r
-spin class)
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. For every
r
, there is a CohFT obtained from
Witten’s
r
-spin class, introduced by Witten in
[
12
]
. See
[
9
,
10
]
for an algebraic construction of Witten’s top Chern class where
it was proved that the axioms of CohFT (i)–(iii) hold.

Witten’s
r
-spin theory gives a family of classes

\displaystyle W^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big)

for
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\{0,\dots,r-2\}
.

Now given an
(r-1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V_{r}
with basis

e_{0},\dots,e_{r-2}
, metric

\displaystyle\eta_{ab}=\eta(e_{a},e_{b})=\delta_{a+b,r-2},

and unit vector
{\mathbf{1}}=e_{0}
one defines a CohFT
\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n}
by

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n}\colon\ V_{r}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow H^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big),\qquad\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n}(e_{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{a_{n}})=W^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

Witten’s class

W^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
has (complex) degree given
by the formula

\displaystyle\text{deg}_{\mathbb{C}}W^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\frac{(r-2)(g-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}{r}.

If
\mathsf{D}^{r}_{g,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is not an integer, the corresponding
Witten’s class vanishes.

To formulate the Givental–Teleman classification result, we need the following notion of semisimplicity of a CohFT.

Let
(V,\eta,{\mathbf{1}})
be a data associated with a CohFT
c_{g,n}
.
For
x_{1},x_{2}\in V
, the
quantum product

{x_{1}\bullet x_{2}\in V}
is uniquely determined by the condition:
for every
x_{3}\in V

\displaystyle\eta(x_{1}\bullet x_{2},x_{3})=c_{0,3}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes x_{3})\in\mathbb{Q}.

The quantum product
\bullet
is commutative by CohFT axiom (i). Let us check that associativity of
\bullet
follows from CohFT axiom (ii).
Further, we often write just
c_{0,3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
instead of
{c_{0,3}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes x_{3})}
for short.
Fix a basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
V
. Then the product in this algebra gives rise to structure constants
e_{a}\bullet e_{b}=c^{i}_{ab}e_{i}
, where the Einstein summation convention is assumed. Hence

\displaystyle\eta((e_{a}\bullet e_{b})\bullet e_{c},e_{d})=c^{i}_{ab}\eta(e_{i}\bullet e_{c},e_{d})=c^{i}_{ab}c^{j}_{ic}\eta_{jd}.

Observe that
c^{i}_{ab}=\eta^{ic}c_{0,3}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{c})
, hence we get

\displaystyle\eta^{ip}c_{0,3}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{p})\eta^{jq}c_{0,3}(e_{i},e_{c},e_{q})\eta_{jd},

which is the same as
q^{*}c_{0,4}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{c},e_{d})
by axiom (ii). On the other hand

\displaystyle\eta(e_{a}\bullet(e_{b}\bullet e_{c}),e_{d})

\displaystyle=c^{i}_{bc}\eta(e_{a}\bullet e_{i},e_{d})=c^{i}_{bc}c^{j}_{ai}\eta_{jd}=\eta^{ip}c_{0,3}(e_{b},e_{c},e_{p})\eta^{jq}c_{0,3}(e_{a},e_{i},e_{q})\eta_{jd}

\displaystyle=c_{0,3}(e_{b},e_{c},e_{p})\eta^{ip}c_{0,3}(e_{a},e_{i},e_{d}),

which is again
q^{*}c_{0,4}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{c},e_{d})
by axioms (i) and (ii). Then from the nondegeneracy of
\eta
the associativity follows. Moreover, a simple check shows that
(V,\bullet,{\mathbf{1}})
is a Frobenius algebra, see
[
2
]
.
A CohFT
c_{g,n}
is called
semisimple
if the algebra
(V,\bullet,{\mathbf{1}})
is semisimple, i.e., the complexification
{V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}}
has a basis
\{e_{i}\}
of idempotents

e_{i}\bullet e_{j}=\delta_{ij}e_{i}
.
Since
\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n}
itself is not semisimple, we will need its shifted version.

Example 2.4

(shifted Witten’s
r
-spin class)
.

Given a vector
\tau\in V_{r}
, the shifted Witten’s class is defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{g,n}^{r,\tau}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}p_{m*}\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n+m}\big(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\tau^{\otimes m}\big),

where
p_{m}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the forgetful map.
The
shifted Witten’s class

\mathsf{W}^{r,\tau}
determines a CohFT with unit. Namely,
applying a map forgetting the last point, we get

\displaystyle p^{*}_{1}\mathsf{W}_{g,n}^{r,\tau}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}p_{m*}p^{*}_{1}\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n+m}\big(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\tau^{\otimes m}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}p_{m*}\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n+m+1}\big(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\tau^{\otimes m}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{W}^{r}_{g,n+1}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\otimes{\mathbf{1}}).

Moreover, let us choose a particular point
\widetilde{\tau}=(0,r\phi,0,\dots,0)=r\phi e_{1}\in V_{r}
and check the semisimplicity of this CohFT.

Denote by
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle^{\tau}:=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}\mathsf{W}_{0,n}^{r,\tau}(e_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_{n}})
the
n-point function
.
Firstly, we use the known values for Witten’s class

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{0,3}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{p})=\delta_{p,r-2-a-b},\qquad\mathsf{W}_{0,4}(e_{1},e_{1},e_{r-2},e_{r-2})=\frac{1}{r}[pt]\in H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4},\mathbb{Q}\big).

Using
[
7
, Proposition 4.1]
,
we compute the 3-point function

\langle a,b,p\rangle^{\widetilde{\tau}}\!=\!\delta_{p,r-2-a-b}+\delta_{p,2r-3-a-b}\phi
.
Then we compute

\displaystyle e_{a}\bullet_{\widetilde{\tau}}e_{b}=c^{i}_{ab}e_{i}=\eta^{ip}\mathsf{W}_{0,3}^{r,\widetilde{\tau}}(e_{a},e_{b},e_{p})e_{i}=\begin{cases}\eta^{i,r-2-a-b}e_{i}=e_{a+b}&\text{if }a+b\leq r-2,\\
\phi\eta^{i,2r-3-a-b}e_{i}=\phi e_{a+b-r+1}&\text{if }a+b\geq r-1.\end{cases}

Then changing a basis to
\widetilde{e}_{a}:=\phi^{-a/(r-1)}e_{a}
the new product is

\displaystyle\widetilde{e}_{a}\bullet_{\widetilde{\tau}}\widetilde{e}_{b}=\phi^{-(a+b)/r-1}e_{a}\bullet e_{b}=\begin{cases}\widetilde{e}_{a+b}&\text{if }a+b\leq r-2,\\
\widetilde{e}_{a+b-r-1}&\text{if }a+b\geq r-1\end{cases}=\widetilde{e}_{a+b\mod r-1}.

Finally, after another change of basis
\bar{e}_{i}:=\sum^{r-2}_{a=0}\xi^{ai}\widetilde{e}_{a}
,
\xi^{r-1}=1
, we derive a basis of idempotents

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{i}\bullet_{\widetilde{\tau}}\bar{e}_{j}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{r-2}\xi^{ai+bj}\widetilde{e}_{a+b\mod r-1}=\sum_{a,c=0}^{r-2}\xi^{a(i-j)+cj}\widetilde{e}_{j}=\sum^{r-2}_{a=0}\xi^{a(i-j)}\bar{e}_{j}=(r-1)\delta_{ij}\bar{e}_{j},

since if
i-j=x\neq 0
then
1+\xi^{x}+\dots+\xi^{(r-2)x}=0
. So,
\{\bar{e}_{i}/(r-1)\}
is a basis of idempotents.

Now we recall how to assign to the given CohFT
(V,\eta,\mathbf{1},(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0})
a Frobenius manifold structure on
V
. Choose a basis
V=\mathbb{C}\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{N}\rangle
and a distinguished vector
\mathbf{1}=e_{1}
.
A full CohFT potential
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}[[t^{*,*},\hbar]]
is the following formal power series in
t^{d_{k},i_{k}}
,
d_{k}\geq 0
,
i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(\big\{t^{d_{k},i_{k}}\big\},\hbar\big):=\sum_{g\geq 0}\hbar^{g-1}\mathcal{F}_{g},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{g}\big(\big\{t^{d_{k},i_{k}}\big\}\big):=\sum_{n\geq x(g)}\frac{1}{(n-x(g))!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\\
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\end{subarray}}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}c_{g,n}(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}t^{d_{1},i_{1}}\cdots t^{d_{n},i_{n}}.

The stability condition is captured by

\displaystyle x(g):=\begin{cases}3&\text{if}\ g=0,\\
1&\text{if}\ g=1,\\
0&\text{if}\ g\geq 2.\end{cases}

Note that the intersection numbers above are nonzero only in degrees
3g-3+n
.

A
Frobenius potential

F\in\mathbb{C}\big[\big[t^{1},\dots,t^{N}\big]\big]
is defined as

\displaystyle F\big(t^{1},\dots,t^{N}\big):={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}\big(\big\{t^{d_{k},i_{k}}\big\}\big)|_{\{t^{d_{k},*}=0,d_{k}>0\}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 3}\frac{1}{(n-3)!}\sum^{N}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}=1}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}c_{0,n}(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{n}})t^{i_{1}}\cdots t^{i_{n}},

where we put
t^{\mu}:=t^{0,\mu}
.

It gives the structure of
formal Frobenius manifold
with flat metric

\eta_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial t^{1}\partial t^{\mu}\partial t^{\nu}}

and a Frobenius algebra structure with multiplication
e_{\alpha}\bullet e_{\beta}:=c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\eta^{\gamma\theta}e_{\theta}
, where

\displaystyle c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(t^{*})=\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial t^{\alpha}\partial t^{\beta}\partial t^{\gamma}},\qquad\big(\eta^{\alpha\beta}\big):=(\eta_{\alpha\beta})^{-1}.

CohFT axioms force
F
to satisfy the WDVV equation

c_{\alpha\beta\theta}\eta^{\theta\delta}c_{\delta\gamma\rho}=c_{\alpha\gamma\theta}\eta^{\theta\delta}c_{\delta\beta\rho}
.

2.3
Reconstruction

Let
c=(c_{g,n})_{2g-2+n>0}
be a CohFT, not necessarily with unit,
associated with data
(V,\eta)
.
Let
R
be a matrix series

\displaystyle R(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}R_{k}z^{k}\in\mathrm{Id}+z\cdot\operatorname{End}(V)[[z]],

which satisfies the symplectic condition

\eta(R(z)x,R(-z)y)=\eta(x,y)

for all
x
,
y
in
V
. The group of such series acts on a space of CohFTs and we now recall the action, following closely
[
4
, Section 1.2.1]
.

One can define a
new CohFT
Rc

on the vector space
(V,\eta)
is expressed as a sum over stable graphs
\Gamma
, see
[
6
, Section 0.2]
for an exposition,
with summands given by
products of vertex, edge, and leg contributions. Let
\mathsf{G}_{g,n}
denote the set of all stable graphs
(up to isomorphism) of genus
g
with
n
legs. To each stable graph
\Gamma
, one can associate the moduli space

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}=\prod_{v\in\mathrm{V}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{g}(v),\mathrm{n}(v)}

with
2g(v)-2+n(v)>0
.
There is a
canonical morphism

6
Higher genera and conclusion

In genus 3 with no markings, we get zero contributions since the parity conditions for vertices are not compatible. We can not satisfy the conditions. For example, curves with a node separating genus 1 and genus 2 components give

\displaystyle-\delta_{1}\sum_{p,q=0}^{1}\omega_{1,1}(p)\omega_{1,1}(p)\omega_{2,1}(q)\big(R_{1}^{-1}\big)^{p}_{1-q}.

So,
p
and
q
must be
p=0
,
q=1
, hence
\big(R_{1}^{-1}\big)^{0}_{0}
is zero. We can not satisfy the conditions
{\sum a_{i}=1\mod 2}
and
1>\frac{\sum a_{i}+2}{3}
at all.

In genus 4 with no markings, we get the expression of the Witten’s class instead of a tautological relation since

D^{3}_{4,0}=1
.
And in higher genera, the degree of Witten’s class is bigger than 1 for all
r
. These considerations agree with early computations and do not contradict the fact that the only relations in
RH^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)
with
g\geq 3
have the trivial form
\delta_{h}=\delta_{g-h}
,
h=0,\dots,g-1
.

From above, it is clear that the tautological PPZ relations for every particular
r
are in fact the full set relations in
H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)

\displaystyle RH^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big)=H^{2}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}\big),

which confirms Pixton’s hypothesis in this codimension.

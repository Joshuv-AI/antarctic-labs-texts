Thick subcategories on weighted projective curves and nilpotent representations of quivers
Alexey ELAGIN

Abstract
. We continue the study of thick triangulated subcategories, started by Valery Lunts and the author in “Thick subcategories on curves”, and consider thick subcategories in the derived category of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective curve and the corresponding abelian thick subcategories. Our main result is that any thick subcategory on a weighted projective curve either is equivalent to the derived category of nilpotent representations of some quiver (we call such categories quiver-like) or is the orthogonal subcategory to an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves (we call such subcategories big). We examine the structure of thick subcategories: in particular, for weighted projective lines, we prove that any admissible subcategory is generated by an exceptional collection and any exceptional collection is a part of a full one. We show that the derived categories of weighted projective curves satisfy the Jordan–Hölder property and do not contain phantoms. Finally, we extend and simplify results from loc. cit., providing sufficient criteria for a triangulated or abelian category to be quiver-like.



1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the classification problem of thick triangulated subcategories in a given triangulated category, which attracted a lot of attention in the last decades.
We continue the work started in our paper
[
17
]
with Valery Lunts, where thick subcategories in the derived category
D^{b}(\coh X)
of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective curve
X
were studied. Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh X)
is called
thick
if
\mathcal{T}
is triangulated and closed under taking direct summands in
D^{b}(\coh X)
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
be the category of right representations of
Q
over a field
\Bbbk
. Denote by
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
the triangulated subcategory in
D^{b}(\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
, generated by the simple modules concentrated in the vertices of
Q
. We call triangulated categories, equivalent to a category of the form
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
,
quiver-like
.

The main result in
[
17
]
is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field. Then any finitely generated thick subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh X)
, different from
D^{b}(\coh X)
, is
quiver-like
.

In the present paper we extend and generalise results of
[
17
]
in several directions.
First, we obtain a more transparent and general sufficient condition for a triangulated category to be quiver-like. More precisely, we prove

Theorem A

(See Theorem
4.12
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an algebraic triangulated category, linear over a field
\Bbbk
. Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is classically generated by a set
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of objects, and for all
i,j\in I

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Hom^{p}(t_{i},t_{j})=0,

\displaystyle\text{if $p\neq 0,1$,}

\displaystyle\Hom(t_{i},t_{j})=0,

\displaystyle\text{if $i\neq j$,}

\displaystyle\Hom(t_{i},t_{i})=\Bbbk.

Then
\mathcal{T}
is quiver-like and
t_{i}
-s correspond to simple modules.

Compared with
[
17
]
, we get rid of finiteness assumptions on the generating set, of assuming
\mathcal{T}
has a dg enhancement, and of assuming that the dg endomorphism algebra is formal.
Instead, we establish equivalence of
\mathcal{T}
with the perfect derived category of certain
A_{\infty}
-category with the set of objects
\{t_{i}\}
and the graded space of morphisms from
t_{i}
to
t_{j}
being
\oplus_{p}\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{i},t_{j})
. Due to the grading constraints the
A_{\infty}
-structure must be trivial, yielding the equivalence of
\mathcal{T}

and the quiver-like category whose simple modules have the same
\Ext
-spaces as
t_{i}
-s have.

Second, for the derived category of a hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, there is a bijection between the thick triangulated subcategories in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and the
thick
(that is, abelian exact extension-closed) subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
. This is shown in
[
10
]
; see our Proposition
3.7
for a precise statement. Therefore, statements about thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
can easily be transformed into statements about thick subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
and vice versa. We extend our description in terms of quivers from thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
to the corresponding thick subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
\mathrm{Mod{-}}\ kQ
be the category of right representations of
Q
over a field
\Bbbk
. Denote by
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\subset\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
the full subcategory of finite-dimensional nilpotent representations. We will call abelian categories, equivalent to a category of the form
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
,
quiver-like
.

As a corollary of Theorem
A
, we get

Theorem B

(See Corollary
4.14
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category linear over a field
\Bbbk
, and
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of objects in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying conditions (
1.1
). Let
\mathcal{T}=\langle t_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
be the thick subcategory generated by
t_{i}
-s, let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{A}
be the corresponding thick subcategory in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like, and the
t_{i}
-s correspond to simple modules.

As another application of Theorem
A
we deduce a characterization of abelian quiver-like categories:

Theorem C

(See Theorem
4.16
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category linear over a field
\Bbbk
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is quiver-like if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is essentially small, all objects have finite length, one has
\End(S)=\Bbbk
for any simple object
S\in\mathcal{A}
and
\Ext^{p}(S_{1},S_{2})=0
for any
p\geqslant 2
and simple objects
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Results similar to Theorems
A
,
B
,
C
are not new and can be obtained by alternative methods. In particular, Theorem
C
can be deduced, following ideas of Gabriel
[
18
]
and without passing to derived categories. One can use a characterisation of finite length abelian categories via pseudocompact modules over pseudocompact algebras from
[
18
]
and then relate the latter to finite-dimensional comodules over coalgebras by
[
49
, Prop. 5.9]
. Given that the abelian category was hereditary, the coalgebra has to be the path coalgebra of a quiver by
[
15
]
, and then the category of finite-dimensional comodules is equivalent to the quiver-like category for the dual quiver by
[
49
, Prop. 8.1(d)]
. For Theorem
A
, one can construct a hereditary bounded t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
, whose heart
\mathcal{H}
is extension-generated by objects
t_{i}
-s and is a finite length hereditary abelian category with
t_{i}
-s being simple objects
(see
[
1
]
or
[
30
, Prop. 5.4]
). Then one can show that there is an equivalence
D^{b}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{T}
using
[
13
, Th. 2.3 and Th. 3.3]
, see also
[
25
, Th. 1.1]
for the case where
\mathcal{T}
is not assumed to be algebraic. Hence Theorem
A
would follow from Theorem
C
applied to
\mathcal{H}
. We thank the referee for pointing out the approach to Theorems
A
–
C
outlined above. We find it useful to provide in the present paper a new and probably shorter way of proving these results. Our method is different in that it treats triangulated categories first and deduces the result about abelian categories as a corollary. It also avoids the machinery of topological rings and modules of
[
18
]
and uses
A_{\infty}
-categories as the main technical instrument.

Third (and most importantly), we apply the methods developed in
[
17
]
and in the first part of this work to the description of thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
, where
\mathbb{X}
is a weighted projective curve. Geometrically, a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
is given by a smooth projective curve
X
and a weight function
w\colon X\to\mathbb{N}
such that
w(x)=1
for all but a finite number of points
x\in X
. The points where
w>1
are called
orbifold
points. For the definition of the category
\coh\mathbb{X}
of coherent sheaves see Section
6.2
or
[
36
]
; see also
[
19
]
for the special case of weighted projective
lines
.

In the second part of this paper we give an analogue of Theorem
1.2
for a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}
. We demonstrate that
most
of the thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
are quiver-like. Exceptions are in a sense “close to” the whole category
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
, they can be explicitly constructed and form a finite set (recall that the only exception in the case of a smooth projective curve is
D^{b}(\coh X)
itself). The reason for such non-quiver-like subcategories to appear is the presence of exceptional torsion sheaves, which are concentrated in the orbifold points of
\mathbb{X}
. For any point
x\in\mathbb{X}
of weight
r=w(x)
the category
\coh_{x}\mathbb{X}
of torsion sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
supported at
x
is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional nilpotent representations of a cyclic quiver of length
r
. Such a category is called a

tube
of rank
r
and denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{r}
. If
r\geqslant 2
then
\coh_{x}\mathbb{X}
has exceptional objects: for example, simple sheaves supported at orbifold points are exceptional.

Definition 1.4

(See Definition
7.1
)
.

We call a subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
generated by an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves
small
and the orthogonal subcategory to a small subcategory
big
. We use the same terminology for the corresponding subcategories in
\coh\mathbb{X}
.

The main result of the second part of the paper is the following

Theorem D

(See Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective curve over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be a thick subcategory, and

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
. Then
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are big or
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like.

Note that in contrast with Theorem
1.2
we do not assume that
\mathcal{T}
is finitely generated, and give a description for the abelian subcategory
\mathcal{S}
as well. Note also that two alternatives in Theorem
D
are not exclusive: big triangulated subcategories can be quiver-like.

Let us explain briefly the idea of the proof of Theorem
D
. We consider two cases: whether a thick subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
contains simultaneously a vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf (see Definition
2.1
) or not. In the first case, one can show that
\mathcal{T}
is big. In the second case,
\mathcal{T}
has a semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
contains only torsion sheaves and
\mathcal{T}_{2}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
only torsion-free sheaves. We show that the corresponding abelian categories
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{i}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
are finite length categories: the length of a sheaf
F\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
as an object in
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is bounded by
\dim(H^{0}(F))
for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and by
\rank(F)
for
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. The families of simple objects in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, taken together, form a generating set for
\mathcal{T}
satisfying assumptions of Theorem
A
. By Theorem
A
we deduce that
\mathcal{T}
is quiver-like.

To clarify the notion of a big subcategory, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
be a weighted projective curve. We call a thick subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}

curve-like
if there exists a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}=(X,w^{\prime})
and a fully faithful functor
\Phi\colon\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\to\coh\mathbb{X}
preserving rank and support of coherent sheaves with
\im\Phi=\mathcal{S}
(note that the underlying curves of
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
are the same).

It is known that, given a simple exceptional torsion sheaf
S
on a weighed projective curve
\mathbb{X}
,
its orthogonal subcategory
\{F\in\coh\mathbb{X}\mid\Ext^{i}(S,F)=0\quad\text{for all $i$}\}
is curve-like. As next theorem shows, big subcategories are close to curve-like.

Theorem E

(See Proposition
7.7
)
.

For a weighted projective curve over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
, the following conditions on a thick subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{S}=\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle^{\perp}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}

for some exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
of torsion sheaves (that is,
\mathcal{S}
is big);

(2)

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
is curve-like and
\mathcal{S}_{2}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
is small;

(3)

\mathcal{S}
contains a curve-like subcategory of
\coh\mathbb{X}
;

(4)

\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf;

(5)

\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and
\mathcal{S}
is invariant under functors
c_{x}
of “twisting by line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{X}}(x)
” for any point
x\in X
(see Definition
6.10
).

Note that exceptional collections in a tube
\mathcal{U}_{r}
have been explicitly classified in
[
16
]
and
[
32
]
. Therefore small (and hence big) subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
can also be explicitly classified.

While preparing this manuscript, we discovered a recent paper
[
14
]
by Yiyu Cheng studying thick subcategories on weighted projective
lines
(i.e.,
rational
curves), whose results and methods overlap with ours, in particular, with Theorem
E
. Its proof, given in
[
14
]
, is similar to ours. We decided to include our proof of Theorem
E
for the sake of completeness and because we work in greater generality, allowing curves of higher genus. The main difference between our setting and the case of rational curves is in dealing with condition (5) since for a rational curve all twist functors
c_{x},x\in X
are isomorphic and in general they are not.

We use Theorem
D
to obtain information about the structure of thick subcategories on a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}
. Let us mention some of these results. In Proposition
8.3
we show that any admissible subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
that is not big is generated by an exceptional collection. In particular (Corollary
8.4
), if
\mathbb{X}
is a weighted projective
line
then any admissible subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is generated by an exceptional collection, and any exceptional collection is a part of a full exceptional collection. On the contrary (Corollary
8.5
), if
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
where
X\not\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then any admissible subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is either big or small. One can use this to get an alternative proof of a well-known theorem by Okawa about semi-orthogonal indecomposability of the derived category of a smooth projective curve, see Remark
8.6
.

Recall that a triangulated category is said to satisfy the
Jordan–Hölder property
if all maximal semi-orthogonal decompositions have the same collection of components up to equivalence. Also recall that a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}\neq 0
is called a
phantom
if
K_{0}(\mathcal{T})=0
.
Violation of the Jordan–Hölder property and existence of phantoms can be viewed as pathologies, however, they happen. Failure of the Jordan–Hölder property for the derived categories of some algebraic varieties was shown in
[
4
]
,
[
34
]
, and the first phantoms were constructed in
[
22
]
and
[
5
]
. Some positive results in this direction are also available: in
[
46
]
the Jordan–Hölder property was proved for the derived category of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and absence of phantoms was demonstrated for del Pezzo surfaces. These are essentially the only geometric examples of dimension
>1
known so far where semi-orthogonal decompositions exist and there provably are no phantoms.
In this direction, we contribute the following:

Theorem F

(Corollaries
8.9
and
8.10
)
.

The derived categories of weighted projective curves have the Jordan–Hölder property and do not contain phantom subcategories.

We call a thick subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})

torsion
(resp.
torsion-free
) if all sheaves in
\mathcal{T}
are torsion (resp. torsion-free). A torsion subcategory
\mathcal{T}
decomposes into the orthogonal direct sum

\mathcal{T}=\oplus_{x\in X}\mathcal{T}_{x},

where
\mathcal{T}_{x}
is a subcategory supported at point
x
. For any point
x
thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
supported at
x
are in bijection with thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{r})
, where
\mathcal{U}_{r}
is a tube of rank
r=w(x)
. The latter have been classified in
[
16
]
and
[
32
]
. On the other hand, we demonstrate (Proposition
8.12
) that any thick subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
that is not big has unique semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is torsion and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is torsion-free. This indicates that description of torsion-free subcategories is the major part of the classification of thick subcategories on weighted projective curves.

1.1.
Open question, further directions

We did not investigate here which quivers are realizable on curves, i.e., which quiver-like categories
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
appear as thick subcategories in categories of coherent sheaves on weighted projective curves. This question is apparently more difficult than for ordinary smooth curves and will probably be studied elsewhere. Recall that all module categories over wild algebras are “equally complex” in the sense that for any two such algebras
A,B
there is a functor
\mathrm{mod{-}}A\to\mathrm{mod{-}}B
which is injective on isomorphism classes of indecomposables. In general, however, this functor cannot be fully faithful with thick image, and
\mathrm{mod{-}}A
is not equivalent to a thick subcategory of
\mathrm{mod{-}}B
. Likewise, one should not expect that any wild category
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
can be embedded into any wild category
\coh\mathbb{X}
.

It seems that quiver-like categories are interesting on their own as natural generalisations of hereditary module categories, and deserve some attention.

For example, we do not know what the
Auslander – Reiten quivers of
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
and
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
look like in general. For module categories of finite acyclic quivers it is classically known that
D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)\cong D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q^{\prime})
if and only if
Q
is obtained from
Q^{\prime}
by a finite number of reflections. Also, in
[
42
]
the group of autoequivalences of
D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
is described: it is the semi-direct product of the automorphism group of the Auslander – Reiten quiver of
D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
and the product
\prod_{i,j\in Q_{0}}GL_{d_{ij}}(\Bbbk)
, where
d_{ij}
is the number of arrows from
i
to
j
in
Q
. We are not aware of analogues of these results for triangulated quiver-like categories if quivers can have cycles or be infinite.

1.2.
Outline

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we recall necessary background on abelian, triangulated, dg and
A_{\infty}
-categories. In Section
3
we gather less standard information about abelian hereditary categories, their derived categories, and thick subcategories in these. In Section
4
we introduce quiver-like categories and generalise results from
[
17
]
providing sufficient conditions for categories to be quiver-like. Theorems
A
,
B
, and
C
are proved here. We also describe proper, finitely generated, strongly finitely generated quiver-like categories, and those having a Serre functor, see Proposition
4.6
. In Section
5
we recall standard results about tubes and representations of linear
A_{n}
-quivers, in particular, we describe their thick subcategories as direct sums of some categories of the same form. In Section
6
we give definitions and basic facts about weighted projective curves and their categories of coherent sheaves. In Section
7
we introduce big, small, and curve-like subcategories in coherent sheaves on weighted projective curves, and give different characterisations of big subcategories from Theorem
E
. Results of this section are similar to those from
[
14
]
but are obtained in greater generality. In Section
8
we prove our main result, Theorem
D
. Further we discuss the structure and the variety of thick subcategories on weighted projective lines.
Finally, in Section
9
we provide some examples of thick quiver-like subcategories on weighted projective lines and compute corresponding quivers.

2.
Background, conventions, notation

We work over a fixed field
\Bbbk
. Starting from Section
6
we assume that
\Bbbk

is algebraically closed. For a
\Bbbk
-vector space
V
, we denote its dual by
V^{*}
. An additive category
A
is
\Bbbk
-linear
if all its
\Hom
groups are
\Bbbk
-vector spaces and composition maps are
\Bbbk
-bilinear. A
\Bbbk
-linear additive category is
\Hom
-finite
if all
\Hom
spaces are finite-dimensional over
\Bbbk
.

2.1.
Background on abelian categories

An object in an abelian category is
simple
if it has no non-trivial subobjects. An object
F
is said to have
finite length
if it has a finite filtration with simple quotients. The number of such quotient does not depend on filtration and is called the
length
of
F
.
An abelian category is a
finite length category
if any its object has finite length.

An abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is
connected
if it has no non-trivial decompositions into a direct product
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

An object
A
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is
uniserial
if it has unique filtration
0=A_{0}\subset A_{1}\subset\ldots\subset A_{m}=A
with simple quotients. All subobjects of a uniserial object are terms of this filtration. An abelian category is
uniserial
if all its indecomposable objects are uniserial. Any object in a uniserial category is uniquely determined by its length
m
and its top simple quotient
A_{m}/A_{m-1}
.

The
radical

\mathrm{rad}(A)
of an object
A
in a finite length abelian category is the intersection of all maximal subobjects. Equivalently, radical of
A
is the kernel of the universal semi-simple quotient of
A
. The latter is called the
top
of
A
and denoted
\mathrm{top}(A)
.

For an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{A})
a set of representatives of isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{A}
.

2.2.
Background on triangulated and derived categories

We refer to
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
43
]
for the definitions and basic concepts on triangulated and derived categories. We denote shift functor by
[\>]
and call distinguished triangles
exact
.

For objects
X,Y
in a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
we denote
\Hom^{i}(X,Y):=\Hom(X,Y[i])
and

\Hom^{\bullet}(X,Y):=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom^{i}(X,Y),

this is a graded abelian group. A triangulated
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{D}
is
proper
if for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
the
\Bbbk
-vector space
\Hom^{\bullet}(X,Y)
is finite-dimensional.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a subcategory (or a collection of objects, or a single object) in a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
. We denote by
[\mathcal{G}]
the smallest strict full triangulated subcategory in
\mathcal{D}
that contains
\mathcal{G}
and call it
the triangulated subcategory generated by

\mathcal{G}
. We denote by
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
the smallest strict idempotent closed full triangulated subcategory in
\mathcal{D}
that contains
\mathcal{G}
and call it
the thick subcategory generated by

\mathcal{G}
.

Subcategories
[\mathcal{G}],\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle\subset\mathcal{D}
can be described constructively as follows. Denote by
[\mathcal{G}]_{0}
the full subcategory in
\mathcal{D}
whose objects are finite direct sums of shifts of objects in
\mathcal{G}
. For
n\geqslant 1
, denote by
[\mathcal{G}]_{n}
the full subcategory in
\mathcal{D}
whose objects
X
fit into an exact triangle

X_{0}\to X\to X_{n-1}\to X_{0}[1]
, where
X_{i}\in[\mathcal{G}]_{i}
. Let
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle_{n}
be the idempotent closure of
[\mathcal{G}]_{n}
. Then
[\mathcal{G}]=\cup_{n}[\mathcal{G}]_{n}
and
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle=\cup_{n}\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle_{n}
.
We say that
\mathcal{G}

generates
\mathcal{D}
as a triangulated category
if
[\mathcal{G}]=\mathcal{D}
.

An object
G
in
\mathcal{D}
is called a
generator
of
\mathcal{D}
if
\langle G\rangle=\mathcal{D}
, and is called a
strong generator
of
\mathcal{D}
if
\langle G\rangle_{n}=\mathcal{D}
for some
n
.

A
Serre functor
on a triangulated
\Hom
-finite
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{D}
is an autoequivalence
S\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}

such that there exist natural isomorphisms

\Hom(X,Y)\cong\Hom(Y,S(X))^{*}

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
. Such functor, if exists, is exact and unique up to an isomorphism.

For a subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{D}
(or a family of objects, or for a single object) define its
left
and
right orthogonals
as full subcategories in
\mathcal{D}
, given respectively by

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid\Hom^{\bullet}(X,T)=0\quad\text{for any $T\in\mathcal{T}$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle:=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid\Hom^{\bullet}(T,X)=0\quad\text{for any $T\in\mathcal{T}$}\}.

One says that a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
has a
semi-orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}\rangle

if
\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}
are full triangulated subcategories and

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{i}\subset\mathcal{T}_{j}^{\perp}
for all
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, and

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is the smallest strict full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
that contains all
\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

In this case the following holds:

•

For any
X\in\mathcal{D}
there is unique diagram

0=X_{n+1}\to X_{n}\to\ldots\to X_{2}\to X_{1}=X,

such that
Y_{i}:=Cone(X_{i+1}\to X_{i})\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
for all
i=1\ldots n
.

•

There are well-defined exact functors
\pi_{i}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{i},\pi_{i}(X)=Y_{i}
, called
projection functors
.

•

\pi_{1}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}
is left adjoint to the inclusion
\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}
, and

\pi_{n}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{n}
is right adjoint to the inclusion
\mathcal{T}_{n}\to\mathcal{D}
.

•

One has

\mathcal{T}_{i}=^{\perp}\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle\mathcal{T}_{i+1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}\rangle^{\perp}.

•

Associativity: for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant n
there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{p-1},\langle\mathcal{T}_{p},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{q}\rangle,\mathcal{T}_{q+1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}\rangle.

A subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called
left
(resp.
right
)
admissible
if the inclusion functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{D}
has a left (resp. right) adjoint functor. A triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is left admissible if and only if it appears as a left component in some semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\rangle
,
and if and only if there is semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{T},^{\perp}\mathcal{T}\rangle
. Similar statement holds for right admissible subcategories.

A subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called
admissible
if it is both left and right admissible.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated
\Bbbk
-linear category. An object
E\in\mathcal{D}
is
exceptional
if

\Hom(E,E)=\Bbbk,\quad\Hom^{i}(E,E)=0\quad\text{for $i\neq 0$}.

An object
E\in\mathcal{D}
is
sphere-like
(more precisely,
1
-sphere-like, but we will not encounter any other sphere-like objects) if

\Hom(E,E)=\Bbbk,\quad\Hom^{1}(E,E)\cong\Bbbk,\quad\Hom^{i}(E,E)=0\quad\text{for $i\neq 0,1$}.

A collection of objects
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
is
exceptional
if

•

any
E_{i}
is exceptional, and

•

\Hom^{\bullet}(E_{q},E_{p})=0\quad\text{for all $1\leqslant p<q\leqslant n$}
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is proper then the triangulated subcategory in
\mathcal{D}
generated by an exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
is admissible, we denote this subcategory

\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle.

In particular, one has semi-orthogonal decompositions

\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle=\langle\langle E_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle E_{n}\rangle\rangle

and

\mathcal{D}=\langle\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle^{\perp},\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle,^{\perp}\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle\rangle.

A triangulated category is called
algebraic
if it is the stable category of a Frobenius exact category (see
[
31
]
or
[
35
]
). Algebraic triangulated categories include homotopy and derived categories of abelian categories, and also their triangulated subcategories and Verdier localizations. All triangulated categories that we come across in this paper are algebraic.

For an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
we denote by
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
its bounded derived category. We will tacitly identify
\mathcal{A}
with the image of the fully faithful functor
\mathcal{A}\to D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
. For a subcategory

\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
we denote

\mathcal{T}_{\Ab}:=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{A},

this is a subcategory in
\mathcal{A}
. In particular, for a subcategory or an object
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
we have

\mathcal{S}^{\perp}_{\Ab}:=\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{A}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}\mid\Ext^{i}(S,A)=0\quad\text{for all $i$ and $S\in\mathcal{S}$}\}

and similarly for
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}_{\Ab}
.

We will speak about exceptional and sphere-like objects and collections in an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, meaning that they are such in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
.

Recall that the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is the abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes
[A]
of objects
A\in\mathcal{A}
and relations
-[A]+[B]-[C]=0
for all exact triples

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
. Similarly, the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{D})
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is generated by the isomorphism classes
[X]
of objects
X\in\mathcal{D}
and relations
[X]+[X[1]]=0
and
-[X]+[Y]-[Z]=0
for all exact triangles

X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
in
\mathcal{D}
. There is a natural isomorphism
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\to K_{0}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))
, sending
[A]
to
[A]
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Assume that
\mathcal{D}
is
\Bbbk
-linear and proper, then there is bilinear Euler form
\chi
on
K_{0}(\mathcal{D})
, defined by

\chi([X],[Y])=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\dim\Hom^{i}(X,Y)

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
.

2.3.
Background on dg and
A_{\infty}
-categories

We refer to
[
6
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
35
]
for main definitions and constructions related to differential graded (=dg) categories and

A_{\infty}
-categories. We will be very brief here as we use these machinery only in the proof of Theorem
4.12
.

By definition, a differential graded (dg) category is a
\Bbbk
-linear category, whose
\Hom
spaces carry a structure of differential complexes of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, composition maps are homogeneous and satisfy graded Leibniz rule:

d(a\cdot b)=d(a)\cdot b+(-1)^{p}a\cdot d(b)

for any
X,Y,Z\in\Ob(\mathcal{A})
,
a\in\Hom^{p}(Y,Z),b\in\Hom^{q}(X,Y)
.
Given a dg category, one defines its
derived category

D(\mathcal{A})
as the localisation of the homotopy category of right dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules by the class of quasi-isomorphisms, see
[
28
]
for details. Then
D(\mathcal{A})
is a triangulated category, it contains a family of
representable modules

h^{X}
,
X\in\Ob(\mathcal{A})
.
Perfect derived category

\Perf(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as the smallest triangulated subcategory in
D(\mathcal{A})
containing all modules
h^{X}
for
X\in\Ob(\mathcal{A})
and closed under taking direct summands.

By definition, an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
over
\Bbbk
is given by

•

a family of objects
\Ob(\mathcal{A})
,

•

a family of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces
\Hom(X,Y)
for all
X,Y\in Ob(\mathcal{A})
,

•

for any
n\geqslant 1
a
\Bbbk
-linear homogeneous of degree
2-n
homomorphism

m_{n}\colon\Hom(X_{n-1},X_{n})\otimes\ldots\otimes\Hom(X_{1},X_{2})\otimes\Hom(X_{0},X_{1})\to\Hom(X_{0},X_{n}),

which are subject to relations

(2.1)

\sum_{n=r+s+t,r,t\geqslant 0,s\geqslant 1}(-1)^{r+st}m_{r+1+t}\circ(1^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes 1^{\otimes t})=0

for any
n\geqslant 1
.

Parallel to the case of dg categories, for an
A_{\infty}
-category one defines its
derived category

D^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
and
perfect derived category

\Perf^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})\subset D^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
(see
[
29
]
), these categories are triangulated. The latter is generated as an idempotent-closed triangulated subcategory in
D^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
by the family of
representable modules

h^{X},X\in\Ob(\mathcal{A})
.

An
A_{\infty}
-category is called
strictly unital
if there are elements
1_{X}\in\Hom(X,X)
of degree
0
for all
X\in\Ob(\mathcal{A})
such that

m_{n}(a_{n}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{1})=0

as soon as at least one of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is
1_{X}
for some
X
, and

m_{2}(a\otimes 1_{X})=a=m_{2}(1_{Y}\otimes a)

for any
a\in\Hom(X,Y)
.

An
A_{\infty}
-category is called
minimal
if
m_{1}=0
.

Assume
\mathcal{A}
is a strictly unital
A_{\infty}
-category
and all
m_{n},n\geqslant 3
vanish.
Then relations (
2.1
) take the form

m_{1}^{2}=0,\quad m_{1}m_{2}=m_{2}(1\otimes m_{1}+m_{1}\otimes 1),\quad m_{2}(m_{2}\otimes 1)=m_{2}(1\otimes m_{2}),

so that
m_{2}
is associative. Hence
m_{2}
makes
\mathcal{A}
a category (a general
A_{\infty}
-category is not a category!),
m_{1}
makes any
\Hom(X,Y)
a differential complex, and composition maps

\Hom(Y,Z)\otimes\Hom(X,Y)\to\Hom(X,Z)
are compatible with the differentials.
Therefore,
\mathcal{A}
is in fact a dg category.

3.
Abelian hereditary categories, their derived categories, and their subcategories

In this paper we deal with hereditary abelian categories and their derived categories, and study thick subcategories in them. Here we give necessary definitions and explain that, given a hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, thick subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
and in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
are in a very natural bijection.

Definition 3.1
.

An abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is called
hereditary
if
\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{i}(X,Y)=0
for all
i\geqslant 2
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
.

There are several reasonable properties for a subcategory in an abelian category.

Definition 3.2

(See
[
31
, Sect. 4.4]
,
[
16
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
be a full subcategory of an abelian category. Then

•

\mathcal{S}
is called
wide
if
\mathcal{S}
is closed under taking kernels, cokernels, and extensions,

•

\mathcal{S}
is called
thick
if
\mathcal{S}
is closed under taking direct summands and
2-of-the-3 condition
is satisfied: if in an exact sequence
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
two of
X,Y,Z
are in
\mathcal{S}
, then the third one is also in
\mathcal{S}
,

•

\mathcal{S}
is called
Serre
if
\mathcal{S}
is closed under taking subobjects, quotient objects, and extensions.

Note that a wide subcategory of an abelian category is abelian, and the inclusion functor is exact.
Also note that the terminology slightly varies across the literature.

Proposition 3.3
.

Any wide subcategory of an abelian category is thick. Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary then any thick subcategory in
\mathcal{A}
is wide.

Proof.

The first is obvious, for the second see
[
16
, Th. 3.3.1]
or
[
31
, Rem. 4.4.16]
.
∎

In this paper we will deal with wide (= thick) subcategories of hereditary abelian categories and will call them thick.
We will make use of the following important

Proposition 3.4

(See
[
47
, Lemma A.1, Prop. A.2]
)
.

(1)

An abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary if and only if the functor
\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,-)
is right exact for any
X\in\mathcal{A}
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be hereditary and
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
be a thick subcategory. Then
\mathcal{S}
is also a hereditary abelian category, and the inclusion functor is exact.

We will also deal with subcategories of derived categories. Recall

Definition 3.5
.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{T}
in a triangulated category is
thick
if
\mathcal{T}
is closed under taking shifts, cones, and direct summands. We will also assume that
\mathcal{T}
is closed under isomorphisms.

Derived category
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
of a hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{A}
has a simple structure: recall well-known

Proposition 3.6

(See, for example,
[
31
, Prop. 4.4.15]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be abelian hereditary category. Then any object
X\in D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is isomorphic to the direct sum of its shifted cohomology:

X\cong\oplus_{i}H^{i}(X)[-i].

In particular, any indecomposable object in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is a shift of some indecomposable object in
\mathcal{A}
.

This allows to relate thick subcategories in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian hereditary category.

(1)

Then there is a bijection between thick subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
and in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, given by assignments

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\supset\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{X\mid H^{i}(X)\in\mathcal{S}\quad\text{for all $i$}\}=\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\supset\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\mapsfrom\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A}).

(2)

Moreover, let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
be corresponding thick subcategories. Then
the derived functor
D^{b}(\mathcal{S})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
of the inclusion is fully faithful with the image
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

This is
[
10
, Th. 5.1]
or
[
31
, Prop. 4.4.17]
for (1) and Prop.
3.4
combined with
[
31
, Prop. 4.4.17]
for (2).
∎

4.
Quiver-like categories

Here we introduce one of the main characters of this paper, quiver-like categories.
Most results of this section can be found in
[
17
]
. However, now we present a different point of view and work in greater generality. In particular, we do not assume quivers to be finite, in contrast to loc. cit., do not rely on enhancements of triangulated categories, and remove formality assumptions.

A
quiver

Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
is given by a set
Q_{0}
of vertices, a set
Q_{1}
of arrows, and two maps
s
(source) and
t
(target) from
Q_{1}
to
Q_{0}
. Let
\Bbbk Q
denote the path algebra of
Q
over a field
\Bbbk
. Let
\rad(\Bbbk Q)\subset\Bbbk Q
be the two-sided ideal, spanned by all paths of positive length. A
(right) representation
of
Q
over
\Bbbk
is a collection of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces
V_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
, and homomorphisms
V_{t(a)}\to V_{s(a)}
for
a\in Q_{1}
.
Any representation
(V_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
of
Q
can be viewed as a (right) module
\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}V_{i}
over
\Bbbk Q
.
A representation
(V_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
is
finite-dimensional
if the vector space
\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}V_{i}
is finite-dimensional. A representation
(V_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
is
radical-nilpotent
if
(\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}V_{i})\cdot\rad(\Bbbk Q)^{N}=0
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
denote the category of right representations of
Q
over
\Bbbk
(we will also call them modules), it is a hereditary abelian category.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\subset\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
be the full subcategory of finite-dimensional radical-nilpotent representations, this is a thick and wide (and even Serre) subcategory. Let
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\subset D^{b}(\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
denote the full subcategory of complexes with cohomology in
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
, this is a thick subcategory.

Denote by
s_{i}
the simple module concentrated in vertex
i\in Q_{0}
. It is easy to see that
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
consists precisely of modules having a finite filtration with quotients
s_{i}
, and

D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
is generated by the modules
s_{i}
as triangulated category.

Definition 4.2
.

An abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is
quiver-like
if there is an additive equivalence
\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
for some quiver
Q
.
A triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is called
quiver-like
if there is an exact equivalence
\mathcal{T}\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
for some quiver
Q
.

Remark 4.3
.

Quiver
Q
can be reconstructed from the associated abelian category
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
as the
\Ext
-quiver of the collection of simple objects, see Definition
4.10
below.

On the contrary, there are non-trivial equivalences between triangulated quiver-like categories. For example,

D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\cong D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q^{\prime})
if
Q
is finite acyclic and
Q^{\prime}
is obtained from
Q
by a sequence of reflections (known since
[
23
]
, see also
[
45
]
). We do not know when, for general quivers
Q,Q^{\prime}
, the categories
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
and
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q^{\prime})
are equivalent.

Proposition 4.4
.

The category
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
is hereditary, and the natural functor

D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

It follows from Propositions
3.4
and
3.7
, applied to the hereditary category
\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
.
∎

We have trivial but pleasant

Proposition 4.5

(
[
17
, Corollary 3.4]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a quiver-like triangulated category with an equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\to\mathcal{T}
. Put
t_{i}:=\Phi(s_{i})
. Then we have the following.

(1)

For any indecomposable object
X\in\mathcal{T}
there exists
d\in\mathbb{Z}
and a diagram

0=X_{0}\to X_{1}\to\ldots\to X_{m}=X[d]

such that
Cone(X_{j-1}\to X_{j})\cong t_{i_{j}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
and some
i_{j}\in Q_{0}
.

(2)

K_{0}(\mathcal{T})\cong\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot[t_{i}]
.

(3)

\mathcal{T}
is generated by
t_{i},i\in Q_{0}
, as a triangulated category.

Next we provide some general properties of quiver-like categories.
Recall that a quiver is
acyclic
if it has no oriented cycles.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver and
\mathcal{T}=D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
be the corresponding quiver-like category. Assume also that
Q
is connected. Then

(1)

\mathcal{T}
has a generator
\Longleftrightarrow

Q_{0}
is finite. If this is the case, then
G:=\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}s_{i}
is also a generator.

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is proper
\Longleftrightarrow
any two vertices are connected by finitely many arrows.

(3)

\mathcal{T}
has a strong generator
\Longleftrightarrow

Q_{0}
is finite and
Q
is acyclic.

(4)

\mathcal{T}
has a Serre functor
\Longleftrightarrow
one of the following holds:

(a)

for any vertex
i
in
Q
there are only finitely many paths containing
i
,

(b)

Q
is a cycle
Z_{n}
of length
n\geqslant 1
,

(c)

Q
is
A_{\infty,\infty}
:

\ldots\to\bullet\to\bullet\to\bullet\to\ldots

Proof.

(1) and (2) are easy and left to the reader.

For (3), assume
Q
is acyclic with finitely many vertices. Then
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
is the category of all finite-dimensional modules over path algebra
\Bbbk Q
, let
R=\rad(\Bbbk Q)
. Let
n
be the maximal length of a path in
Q
, then
R^{n+1}=0
. Put
G:=\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}s_{i}
, we claim that
[G]_{n}=D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
. Indeed, for a bounded complex
M
of
\Bbbk Q
-modules, consider its filtration by subcomplexes

0=M\cdot R^{n+1}\subset M\cdot R^{n}\subset\ldots\subset M\cdot R\subset M.

Any quotient
F_{j}:=M\cdot R^{j}/M\cdot R^{j+1}
is a bounded complex, where each term is a finite direct sum of some modules
s_{i},i\in I
. The category
\add(G)

of such direct sums is semi-simple, therefore
F_{j}
is quasi-isomorphic to the direct sum of its cohomology modules, which are in
\add(G)
. Hence
F_{j}\in[G]_{0}
.
It follows that
M\in[G]_{n}
, and
G
is a strong generator of
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
.

Contrary, assume

has a strong generator, then any generator is strong (see

). From (1) we deduce that

is finite and

is a strong generator:
\langle G\rangle_{n}=D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
for some

. Note that

. Therefore,
M\cdot R^{n+1}=0
for all
M\in\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
. It follows then that the length of paths in

is bounded by

, in particular,

cannot have cycles.

(4) is based on the classification of Noetherian hereditary abelian categories with a Serre functor, given in
[
47
]
. If (a) holds, then all indecomposable
projective and injective
Q
-modules are in
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
, and
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
has enough projectives and injectives. Then the derived Nakayama functor

has a Serre functor by
[
47
, Th. B]
.

The other way, recall that
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
is a connected Noetherian hereditary abelian category of finite length. Assume that

has a Serre functor (in particular, that
\mathcal{A}
is

[

, Th. B]
:

(1)

\mathcal{A}
has no non-zero projective objects. Then
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Z_{n}
or
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk A_{\infty,\infty}
(this is case (a) in
[
47
, Th. B]
or
[
47
, Th. III.1.1]
).

(2)

\mathcal{A}
has a non-zero projective object. Then by
[

, Th. II.4.9]

is equivalent to category
\wdtrep(\Gamma)
, defined in
[

, Sect. II]
, where

is a quiver obtained in the following way. Start with a quiver
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that any vertex is contained in only finitely many paths. Then, to each vertex

, attach finitely many (possibly zero) infinite rays of the form

:

(4.1)

\ldots\to 3\to 2\to 1\to 0,

by gluing their terminal vertex

In case (1) it remains to note that equivalence

implies

, see Remark
4.3
.
In case (2) we prove that

actually has no infinite rays attached, and

. We will need only two properties of category

(see
[

, Th. II.1.3]
):

(*)

\wdtrep(\Gamma)
contains

, the category of finitely presented right representations of

(**)

\wdtrep(\Gamma)=\rep(\Gamma)
if

has a Serre functor.

Assume that
\Gamma
contains an infinite ray (
4.1
). Then projective

-modules

give an infinite descending chain in

is a finite length category, we get a contradiction. Therefore, in

. By the above arguments,

has a Serre functor. Using (**), we have

\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q=\mathcal{A}\cong\wdtrep(\Gamma)=\rep(\Gamma)=\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk\Gamma

By Remark

∎

Remark 4.7
.

It is interesting to note that for an acyclic quiver
Q
with finite
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
one always has
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\cong D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
and the Rouquier dimension (
[
48
]
) of
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
is
\leqslant 1
. However, it is not clear what the dimension of
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
is for acyclic quivers
Q
with finite
Q_{0}
but possibly infinite
Q_{1}
.

To recognize a quiver-like category, one needs to find objects, corresponding to simple modules.
We leave to the reader the following easy, but very important computation:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Mod{-}}\Bbbk Q
and let
s_{i}\in\mathcal{A}

be simple modules. Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{i},s_{i})=\Bbbk,\quad\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{i},s_{j})=0\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\dim\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{i},s_{j})=\text{number of arrows in $Q$ from $j$ to $i$},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Ext^{p}_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{i},s_{j})=0\quad\text{for $p\geqslant 2$.}

It is now convenient to make

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated
\Bbbk
-linear category. A collection of
objects
t_{i},i\in I
, in
\mathcal{T}
is called
vertex-like
if

\Hom(t_{i},t_{i})=\Bbbk,\quad\Hom(t_{i},t_{j})=0\quad\text{for $i\neq j$,}\quad\Hom^{p}(t_{i},t_{j})=0\quad\text{ for all $i,j$ and
$p\neq 0,1.$}

A model example of a vertex-like collection is given (see Lemma
4.8
) by simple modules
s_{i}
in
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, where
i
runs through
Q_{0}
.

We are going to prove next that any vertex-like collection generates a quiver-like triangulated category. Lemma
4.8
suggests how to define a quiver.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a vertex-like set in a triangulated category. Its
\Ext
-quiver
is defined as follows: take
Q_{0}=I
and take
\dim\Hom^{1}(t_{j},t_{i})
arrows from
i
to
j
(this can be infinite, then
\dim
denotes the cardinality of a basis).

The key argument is contained in the following

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an algebraic idempotent-complete
\Bbbk
-linear category. Assume
\mathcal{T}
is generated by a vertex-like set
t_{i},i\in I
, of objects. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the dg category with
\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{E})=I
, and with

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}^{p}(i,j)=\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{i},t_{j})
for all
i,j\in I,p\in\mathbb{Z}
, viewed as a complex with zero differential.
Then one has an exact equivalence

\mathcal{T}\to\Perf(\mathcal{E})
, sending
t_{i}
to the representable
\mathcal{E}
-module
h^{i}
.

Proof.

By
[
35
, Th. 7.6.0.6]
, there exists a strictly unital minimal
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{E}
with

\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{E})=I
and an equivalence
\mathcal{T}\to\Perf^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
of triangulated categories, sending
t_{i}
to the representable
\mathcal{E}
-module
h^{i}
.
Moreover, by construction one has

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}^{p}(i,j)=\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{i},t_{j})
for all
i,j\in I,p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We claim that all operations
m_{n},n\geqslant 3
vanish. This is because of the grading: by the definition of a vertex-like collection, there are only scalar endomorphisms and
\Hom
-s of degree
1
in
\mathcal{E}
. More precisely, let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
be homogeneous morphisms in
\mathcal{E}
,
n\geqslant 3
. If some
a_{k}
has degree
0
, then
a_{k}\in\Bbbk\cdot 1_{i}
for some object
i
in
\mathcal{E}
. Hence

m_{n}(a_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{n})=0
by strict unitality of
\mathcal{E}
. Otherwise all
a_{i}
-s have degree
1
.
Then

\deg m_{n}(a_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{n})=\deg m_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\deg a_{k}=2-n+n=2,

but there are no non-zero morphisms in degree
2
in
\mathcal{E}
.

Hence
\mathcal{E}

is actually a dg category, and its differential
m_{1}
is zero by the minimality assumption. It remains to note that the perfect derived category
\Perf^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
of
A_{\infty}
-modules over
\mathcal{E}
is equivalent to the perfect derived category
\Perf(\mathcal{E})
of dg modules over
\mathcal{E}
, see
[
35
, Cor. 4.1.3.11]
.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an algebraic
\Bbbk
-linear category. Assume
\mathcal{T}
is generated by a vertex-like set
t_{i},i\in I
, of objects. Let
Q
be the
\Ext
-quiver of this set. Then
\mathcal{T}
is quiver-like: there exists an exact equivalence
\mathcal{T}\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, sending
t_{i}
to
s_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8

(4.5)

\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{i},t_{j})\cong\Hom^{p}_{D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)}(s_{i},s_{j})\quad\text{for all $i,j\in I$ and $p\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

Assume first that
\mathcal{T}
is idempotent-complete. Then we can apply Proposition
4.11
to categories
\mathcal{T}
and
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
with vertex-like collections
\{t_{i}\}
and
\{s_{i}\}
. By (
4.5
), both
\mathcal{T}
and
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
are equivalent to the same category
\Perf(\mathcal{E})
, and for any
i\in I
the objects
t_{i}\in\mathcal{T}
and
s_{i}\in D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
are sent to the same object in
\Perf(\mathcal{E})
. Hence the statement follows.

If
\mathcal{T}
is not known to be idempotent-complete, the same arguments prove that there is a fully faithful exact functor
\Phi\colon\mathcal{T}\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, sending
t_{i}
to
s_{i},i\in I
. But objects
s_{i}
generate
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
as a triangulated category (without adding direct summands). Therefore the essential image of
\Phi
is all
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, and
\Phi
is an equivalence.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Theorem
4.12
was first proved in
[
17
, Proposition 3.9]
under extra conditions:
I
was assumed finite and the dg endomorphism algebra of
\oplus t_{i}
was assumed formal.

We can say even more if a vertex-like set in an abelian category is given.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian category.
Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
be a thick subcategory and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{A}
. Assume there is a vertex-like set
t_{i},i\in I
, in
\mathcal{S}
that generates
\mathcal{T}
as a thick triangulated subcategory. Let
Q
be the associated
\Ext
-quiver. Then

(1)

There are equivalences

D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S},

sending
s_{i}
to
t_{i}
.

(2)

Objects
t_{i}
represent isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{S}
.

(3)

\mathcal{S}
is the smallest subcategory in
\mathcal{A}
that contains
t_{i}
-s and is closed under extensions.

(4)

The natural functor
D^{b}(\mathcal{S})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is fully faithful with the essential image
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{T}
is an algebraic triangulated category, so we can apply Theorem
4.12
. We get that there is an exact equivalence
\mathcal{T}\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
. Recall equivalence
D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)\to D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
from Proposition
4.4
. Let

\Phi\colon D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)\to\mathcal{T}

be the resulting equivalence. Note that
\Phi
sends simple modules
s_{i}
to objects
t_{i}
. Any module in
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
is an iterated extension of
s_{i}
-s, hence
\Phi
sends the subcategory
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\subset D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)
to
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}
. Let us check that the restriction

\phi=\Phi|_{\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q}\colon\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\to\mathcal{S}

is essentially surjective.
Recall that any object in
D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)
is the direct sum of it cohomology. Let
F\in\mathcal{S}
and
E=\Phi^{-1}(F)
. Then

F\cong\Phi(E)\cong\Phi(\oplus_{i}H^{i}(E)[-i])\cong\oplus_{i}\phi(H^{i}(E))[-i].

It follows that
\phi(H^{i}(E))=0
and
H^{i}(E)=0
for
i\neq 0
. Hence
E\cong H^{0}(E)\in\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q
.
Consequently,
\Phi
restricts to an equivalence
\phi\colon\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\to\mathcal{S}
.

(2) and (3) follow easily from equivalence
\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q\to\mathcal{S}
.

For (4) it suffices to check that the derived functor

\Phi^{\prime}:=D(\phi)\colon D^{b}(\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Q)\to D^{b}(\mathcal{A})

is fully faithful with the image
\mathcal{T}
. While we are unsure if
\Phi^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\Phi
, we note that

\Phi^{\prime}
and
\Phi
coincide on objects
s_{i}[d]
, where
i\in I,d\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
\Phi^{\prime}
is fully faithful on such objects along with
\Phi
, and by standard dévissage technique
\Phi^{\prime}
is fully faithful on its domain. Finally, we see that the image of
\Phi^{\prime}
is generated by objects
t_{i}[d]
and therefore is
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

We find it convenient for applications to formulate another consequence of Theorem
4.12
.
Note that Corollary
4.15
appears in
[
17
, Proposition 3.10]
with extra assumptions including finiteness.

Corollary 4.15
.

Assume that the field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian hereditary
category. Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
be a
\Hom
-finite thick essentially small subcategory, and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{A}
. Assume there exists a linear function

r\colon K_{0}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathbb{Z},

such that for any non-zero
F\in\mathcal{S}
one has
r([F])>0
. Then the category
\mathcal{S}
is of finite length, its simple objects form a vertex-like collection generating
\mathcal{T}
, and categories
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are quiver-like.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4

\mathcal{S}
is an abelian hereditary category.
One can prove that any object
F
in
\mathcal{S}
has finite length by induction in
r([F])
. Indeed, if
r([F])=0
then
F=0
and there is nothing to prove, and if
0\to F^{\prime}\to F\to F^{\prime\prime}\to 0
is a non-trivial exact sequence, then
r([F])=r([F^{\prime}])+r([F^{\prime\prime}])
,
r([F^{\prime}]),r([F^{\prime\prime}])<r([F])
, and one can proceed by induction.

Let
t_{i},i\in I
, denote the set of (isomorphism classes of) simple objects in
\mathcal{S}
. Then
t_{i}
-s generate
\mathcal{S}
(because any object in
\mathcal{S}
is an iterated extension of simples) and
\mathcal{T}
(because any object in
\mathcal{T}
is a direct sum of its cohomology objects, which are in
\mathcal{S}
). Further,
t_{i}
-s form a vertex-like family. Indeed,
\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{i},t_{j})=\Hom^{p}_{\mathcal{A}}(t_{i},t_{j})=0
for
p\neq 0,1
since
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary,
\Hom(t_{i},t_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
because they are simple objects. Finally,
\End(t_{i})
is a finite-dimensional division
\Bbbk
-algebra (by Schur’s lemma and our assumptions). Since
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed,
\End(t_{i})
is isomorphic to
\Bbbk
. Now by Corollary
4.14

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are quiver-like.
∎

Now we provide an analogue of Theorem
4.12
, giving a characterisation of abelian quiver-like categories.

Theorem 4.16
.

An abelian
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{A}
is quiver-like if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is essentially small, has finite length,
\End(S)=\Bbbk
for any simple object
S\in\mathcal{A}
, and

\Ext^{j}(S_{1},S_{2})=0
for
j\geqslant 2
and for any simple objects
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

“Only if” part is trivial, let’s prove “if” part. Consider
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, it is an algebraic triangulated category. Choose a representative set
t_{i},i\in I,
for isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a vertex-like set by our assumptions. Note that this set generates
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
as a triangulated category: any object in
\mathcal{A}
has a finite filtration with quotients isomorphic to some
t_{i}
, hence belongs to the triangulated subcategory in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
generated by
t_{i}
-s.
So we can apply Corollary
4.14
with
\mathcal{T}=D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and deduce that
\mathcal{A}
is quiver-like.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

Note that we do not require
\mathcal{A}
to be hereditary in Corollary
4.14
and Theorem
4.16
.

5.1.
Linear quivers

For
n\geqslant 1
, let
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be the category of right representation of the quiver of type

:

\textstyle{\bullet_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ldots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet_{n}}

For

we put

. Categories
\mathcal{A}_{n}
are connected, hereditary, and uniserial. They have finitely many indecomposable objects, all off which are exceptional. Despite of their omnipresence, categories
\mathcal{A}_{n}
do not seem to have a standard name. Thick subcategories in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and

are well-understood, we summarize relevant facts in

Proposition 5.1

(See
[

]
,
[

]
)
.

Let

.

(1)

Let
E\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
be an indecomposable object of length
m,1\leqslant m\leqslant n
. Then
E^{\perp}_{\Ab}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}_{n-m}\times\mathcal{A}_{m-1}
, and

D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{n})=\langle D^{b}(E^{\perp}_{\Ab}),\langle E\rangle\rangle
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
be a thick subcategory, and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{A}_{n}
. Then

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is generated by an exceptional collection in
\mathcal{S}
, in particular,
\mathcal{T}
is admissible.

(3)

One has

\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{A}_{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{A}_{n_{k}},

where
k\geqslant 0
and
n_{i}\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

We provide a short proof to illustrate methods from Section
4
.

For
1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n
, denote by
M_{ij}
the indecomposable right representation of
A_{n}
, concentrated in vertices
i,i+1,\ldots,j-1,j
. We can assume
E=M_{pq}
with

.
One can check that
E^{\perp}

is generated by the vertex-like family of modules

(5.1)

M_{11},M_{22},\ldots,M_{p-2,p-2},M_{p-1,q},M_{q+1,q+1},\ldots,M_{nn}\>;M_{pp},M_{p+1,p+1},M_{q-1,q-1}

Note that the

-quiver of (

) is

. By Corollary

, we get an equivalence

E^{\perp}_{\Ab}\cong\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk(A_{n-m}\sqcup A_{m-1})\cong\mathcal{A}_{n-m}\times\mathcal{A}_{m-1}.

By Proposition

(2) is clear since any thick subcategory in

contains an indecomposbale object, which is necessarily exceptional. (3) follows from (1) by induction, because

is the orthogonal to an exceptional collection.

5.2.
Tubes

Let
n\geqslant 1
, let

be the oriented cycle with

\textstyle{\bullet_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ldots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

\mathcal{U}_{n}:=\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk Z_{n}
. It is a connected abelian uniserial hereditary category. Moreover,
\mathcal{U}_{n}
has Serre duality in the form

(5.2)

\Ext^{1}(X,Y)^{*}\cong\Hom(Y,\tau X).

where
X,Y\in\mathcal{U}_{n}

\tau\colon\mathcal{U}_{n}\to\mathcal{U}_{n}
is the autoequivalence induced by the rotation of
Z_{n}
against the arrows. Note that
\tau^{n}=\id
. The derived category
D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{n})
has a Serre functor, given by
\tau[1]
.
Let
S
be some simple object in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, then all simple objects are
S,\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{n-1}S
. Further, for a simple object
S
and
i\geqslant 1
, denote by
S^{[i]}
the unique indecomposable object
M
with
M/\mathrm{rad}(M)\cong S
and of length
i
. Such
S^{[i]}
fits into a non-trivial extension

0\to\tau S^{[i-1]}\to S^{[i]}\to S\to 0.

Then all indecomposable objects of length
i
in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
are
S^{[i]},\tau S^{[i]},\ldots,\tau^{n-1}S^{[i]}
. In particular, there are infinitely many indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
.
We will call
\mathcal{U}_{n}
a
tube of rank

.

Object

is exceptional if and only if

, and is sphere-like if and only if

.
For

the object
S^{[i]}
is not exceptional nor sphere-like, it has nilpotent endomorphisms.

One can show that any connected abelian category of finite length with Serre duality (

) and with finitely many simples is equivalent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
for some

, see
[
47
]
.

Thick subcategories in tubes have also been studied, see
[
32
]
,
[
14
]
, or
[

]
.
Here we collect the results from these sources that we will use later.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
.

(1)

Let
E\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
be an indecomposable object of length
m,1\leqslant m\leqslant n
. Then
E^{\perp}_{\mathrm{A}b}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{U}_{n-m}\times\mathcal{A}_{m-1}
,
and we have the semi-orthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{n})=\langle D^{b}(E^{\perp}_{\Ab}),\langle E\rangle\rangle
(where
\mathcal{U}_{n-m}
does not appear if
m=n
).

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{n})
be a thick subcategory, and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
. Then

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is admissible and exactly one of the following holds:

(a)

If
\mathcal{T}
contains no sphere-like objects then
\mathcal{T}
is generated by an exceptional collection in
\mathcal{S}
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{T}
contains a sphere-like object then
\mathcal{T}
is generated by a semi-orthogonal collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
in
\mathcal{S}
, where
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m-1}
are exceptional and
E_{m}
is sphere-like.

(3)

According to (2), one has:

(a)

(5.3)

\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{A}_{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{A}_{n_{k}},

where
k\geqslant 0
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
. All indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{T}
are exceptional.

(b)

(5.4)

\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{U}_{s}\times\mathcal{A}_{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{A}_{n_{k}},

where
s\geqslant 1
,
k\geqslant 0
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
.

\mathcal{T}
is not generated by an exceptional collection.

Moreover,
\mathcal{T}
is of type (a) if and only if
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
(and
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{T}
) is of type (b).

Proof.

As in Proposition
5.1
we sketch a proof, cf.
[
14
, Sect. 4]
.

We can assume
E=S^{[m]}
for a simple object
S
.
One can check that
E^{\perp}

is generated by the vertex-like family of modules

(5.5)

\tau^{-1}S,\tau^{-2}S,\ldots,\tau^{m+1}S,S^{[m+1]}\>;\tau S,\tau^{2}S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S

Note that the
\Ext
-quiver of (
5.5
) is
Z_{n-m}\sqcup A_{m-1}
. By Corollary
4.14
, we get an equivalence

E^{\perp}_{\Ab}\cong\mathrm{mod}_{0}{-}\Bbbk(Z_{n-m}\sqcup A_{m-1})\cong\mathcal{U}_{n-m}\times\mathcal{A}_{m-1}.

If
n=m
then the first group in (
5.5
) contains
n-m
modules, so is empty, and
Z_{n-m}
above does not occur. Note that
E
together with (
5.5
) generate
D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{n})
, and we deduce (1). In particular,
\langle E\rangle
is right, and similarly left admissible.

Any thick subcategory
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
contains a simple in
\mathcal{S}
object

, which must be exceptional or sphere-like. Passing to

and arguing by induction using (1), we see that any thick subcategory

is generated by a semi-orthogonal sequence of exceptional and sphere-like objects, hence is admissible. Moreover, assume
\mathcal{T}
contains a sphere-like object

by (1). Hence

has no sphere-like objects, and one can find a generating semi-orthogonal sequence for

with only one sphere-like object as required. This explains (2).

For (3), use that
\mathcal{T}
is admissible and thus
\mathcal{T}=(^{\perp}\mathcal{T})^{\perp}
. Consider two cases:
\mathcal{T}
is the right orthogonal to a subcategory as in (2a) or as in (2b).
Use induction, (1), and Proposition

to see that in the first case

is equivalent to (

) and hence of type (b), and in the second case
\mathcal{S}
is equivalent to (

) and hence of type (a).
∎

6.
Weighted projective curves

Here we collect necessary facts about weighted projective curves and associated categories of coherent sheaves.
In this section and further we assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed.

6.1.
Weighted projective curves

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
\Bbbk
and
w\colon X\to\mathbb{N}
be a function on the set of closed points of
X
such that
w=1
except for finitely many points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Function
w
is called the
weight function
, points
x\in X
with
w(x)=1
are called
regular
or
homogeneous
, points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are called
orbifold
(or
non-homogeneous, stacky, weighted, singular
, etc), and numbers
r_{i}:=w(x_{i})\geqslant 2
are called
multiplicities
or
weights
. The pair
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
will be called a
weighted projective curve
.

6.2.
Category of coherent sheaves

To a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}
one can associate an abelian category
\coh\mathbb{X}
, called
the category of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
. It can be defined in several ways:

•

One can construct a Deligne – Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
from
(X,w)
by applying
root construction
with centres
x_{i}
and multiplicities
r_{i}
, see e.g.
[
11
]
. Then define
\coh\mathbb{X}
as the category
\coh\mathcal{X}
of coherent sheaves on the stack
\mathcal{X}
.

•

One can obtain
\coh\mathbb{X}
from
\coh X
be
inserting weights

r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
at points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in purely categorical terms. Let us describe briefly one step of this procedure. Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
be a weighted curve,
x\in X
be a point, and
p\geqslant 1
. Denote
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}=(X,w^{\prime})
with
w^{\prime}(x)=pw(x)
and
w^{\prime}(y)=w(y)
for
y\neq x
. The idea of obtaining
\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
from
\coh\mathbb{X}
is to formally take
p
-th root of the twist functor
\sigma_{x}\colon\coh\mathbb{X}\to\coh\mathbb{X}
and of the functor morphism
\eta_{x}\colon\id\to\sigma_{x}
, see Definition
6.5
and Proposition
6.6
.
We refer to
[
37
, Section 4]
for details.

•

If
n\geqslant 3
, there exists a smooth projective curve
Y
, a finite group
G
and a faithful
G
-action on
Y
such that
Y/G\cong X
and for any
y\in Y
the stabilizer of
y
is a cyclic group of order
w(\pi(y))
(where
\pi\colon Y\to X
is the quotient morphism). Then the stack
\mathcal{X}
is isomorphic to the quotient stack
Y/\!\!/G
and one can define
\coh\mathbb{X}
as the category of
G
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
Y
. See
[
39
]
for details.

•

Following an approach by Artin and Zhang
[
3
]
, one can consider one-dimensional non-commutative projective schemes. Let
R=\oplus_{i\geqslant 0}R_{i}
be a finitely generated commutative associative unital graded
\Bbbk
-algebra with
R_{0}=\Bbbk
. Assume
R
is an isolated singularity, has Krull dimension
2
and is graded Gorenstein. Then the quotient category

\mathrm{qgr}(R):=\frac{\mathrm{grmod{-}}R}{\mathrm{grmod_{0}{-}}R}

of finitely generated graded
R
-modules by the subcategory of finite-dimensional modules is equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves on weighted projective curve
(\Proj(R),w)
with certain
w
. Moreover, any weighted projective curve can be obtained in this way.

Unfortunately, none of the above definitions is straightforward. Alternatively, following Lenzing, one can define coherent sheaves on weighted projective curves axiomatically, as abelian categories satisfying some properties, see
[
36
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
47
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

A
category of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective curve
is a small
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the following axioms:

(H1)

\mathcal{H}
is abelian, connected, and any object in
\mathcal{H}
is Noetherian,

(H2)

\mathcal{H}
is
\Ext
-finite: all
\Ext
spaces are finite-dimensional,

(H3)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies Serre duality in the following sense: there exists an autoequivalence
\tau\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and natural isomorphisms

(6.1)

\Ext^{1}(F_{1},F_{2})\cong\Hom(F_{2},\tau F_{1})^{*}

of vector spaces for all
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
,

(H4)

\mathcal{H}
contains an object of infinite length.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
the full subcategory of objects having finite length. Clearly
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is preserved by
\tau
. We additionally require

(H5)

Any object in
\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
has finite length, and

(H6)

There are infinitely many
\tau
-orbits of simple objects in
\mathcal{H}
.

Remark 6.2
.

(1)

It follows from condition (H3) that the derived category
D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
has a Serre functor, which is given by
\tau[1]
(the composition of
\tau
and the cohomological shift by
1
). Moreover, (H3) is equivalent to the following:
\mathcal{H}
is hereditary,
D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
has a Serre functor, and there are no non-zero projective objects in
\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

It can be shown that (H1)-(H4) actually imply (H5).

(3)

Condition (H6) is needed to exclude some degenerate examples.

In fact, any category, satisfying (H1)-(H6), is equivalent to
\coh\mathbb{X}
for some weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
, see
[
47
]
.
Below we explain how to associate a curve with a weight function to a category from Definition
6.1
.

6.3.
First properties of coherent sheaves on weighted projective curves: points, torsion and torsion-free sheaves

From now on, we assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a category satisfying conditions (H1)-(H6). We refer to
[
40
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
47
]
for the properties of
\mathcal{H}
collected below.
Objects in
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{H}_{0}
) will be called
sheaves
(resp.
torsion sheaves
). A sheaf is called
torsion-free
, or a
vector bundle
, if it has no torsion subsheaves. The full subcategory of torsion-free sheaves will be denoted
\mathcal{H}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Any sheaf is a direct sum of a torsion sheaf and a torsion-free sheaf.

Category
\mathcal{H}_{0}
decomposes into a direct sum

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\oplus_{x\in X}\mathcal{H}_{x}

of connected thick (and moreover, Serre) subcategories, where index set
X
is the set of closed points of a smooth projective curve, also denoted by
X
.

Autoequivalence
\tau\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is called
translation
, it restricts to autoequivalences on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{+}
.
Any subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{x}
is
\tau
-stable, and is equivalent to the tube
\mathcal{U}_{w(x)}
for some
w(x)\geqslant 1
.
Moreover, for all points
x\in X
except for a finite number one has
w(x)=1
. Thus
w
is indeed a weight function, and we recover a weighted projective curve
(X,w)
as defined in Section
6.1
. From Section
5.2
we know that
\mathcal{H}_{x}
has
w(x)
simple sheaves, and they belong to the same
\tau
-orbit. In other words,
\tau
-orbits of simple objects of
\mathcal{H}
correspond to points of
X
.

Section
5.2
implies

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
be an indecomposable torsion sheaf of length
i
.

(1)

E
is exceptional if and only if
i<w(x)
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{x}
contains exceptional sheaves if and only if
x
is an orbifold point.

(2)

E
is sphere-like if and only if
i=w(x)
. Moreover, any category
\mathcal{H}_{x}
contains
w(x)\geqslant 1
sphere-like sheaves.

For a non-zero sheaf
F\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
we say that
F
is
supported
at
x
. For a general torsion sheaf
F
its support
\Supp(F)\subset X
is defined as the union of supports of the indecomposable summands of
F
. For a sheaf
F
that is not torsion we say that
\Supp(F)=X
.

The quotient category
\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces over the field

\Bbbk(X)
of rational functions on the curve
X
. The
rank
of a vector bundle
V\in\mathcal{H}
is defined as the dimension of its image in
\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Vector bundles of rank one are called
line bundles
. Any vector bundle has a filtration by line bundles. For convenient reference we state

Lemma 6.4
.

(1)

For any
V\in\mathcal{H}_{+},F\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
one has

\Hom(F,V)=\Ext^{1}(V,F)=0.

(2)

For any non-zero
V\in\mathcal{H}_{+}
and any
x\in X
there is some simple
S\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
such that

\Hom(V,S)\neq 0
. Moreover,

\sum_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\dim\Hom(V,S)=\rank(V).

(3)

For any non-zero
V\in\mathcal{H}_{+}
, any
x\in X
, and any sphere-like
M\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
one has

\Hom(V,M)\neq 0
. Moreover,

\dim\Hom(V,M)=\rank(V).

6.4.
Twist functors

Here we recall definitions and properties of two families of autoequivalences
\sigma_{x}
and
c_{x}
of
\coh\mathbb{X}
, where
x\in X
. Functors
\sigma_{x}
are often called mutations. We consider their iterations
c_{x}=\sigma_{x}^{w(x)}
and call them twists, they should be viewed as the tensor multiplication by the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{X}}(x)
. We will need twists in Proposition
7.7
.

Definition 6.5

(See
[
38
, Th. 10.8]
, also
[
37
]
,
[
41
]
)
.

Let
x\in X
be a point. One can define an autoequivalence

\sigma_{x}\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{H})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
given for an object
F
by the exact triangle

(6.2)

\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\Hom^{\bullet}(S,F)\otimes S\to F\to\sigma_{x}(F)\to\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\Hom^{\bullet}(S,F)\otimes S[1],

where
S
runs over the set
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
of isomorphism classes of simple sheaves supported at
x
.
The inverse functor is given by the exact triangle

(6.3)

\sigma_{x}^{-1}(F)\to F\to\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\Hom^{\bullet}(F,S)^{*}\otimes S\to\sigma_{x}^{-1}(F)[1],

where
\Hom^{\bullet}(F,S)^{*}
is the dual graded
\Bbbk
-vector space:
(\Hom^{\bullet}(F,S)^{*})^{i}=\Hom^{-i}(F,S)^{*}
.

Conceptual explanation for the fact that these formulas define mutually inverse functors is that
\sigma_{x}
is a spherical twist in the sense of
[
2
]
. Indeed, let

\mathcal{A}=\add\left(\bigoplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}S\right)\subset\mathcal{H}

be the additive envelope of simple objects in
\mathcal{H}_{x}
, this is a semi-simple abelian category. Then the functor
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is spherical, and the associated spherical twist is
\sigma_{x}
, see
[
33
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Proposition 6.6

(See
[
41
]
,
[
37
]
)
.

Functors
\sigma_{x}
have the following properties

(1)

\sigma_{x}
restricts to an autoequivalence
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
,

(2)

\sigma_{x}
preserves subcategories
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{y},\mathcal{H}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}
for all
y\in X
,

(3)

\sigma_{x}=\tau^{-1}
on
\mathcal{H}_{x}
,
\sigma_{x}=\id
on
\mathcal{H}_{y}
for
y\neq x
,

(4)

for
V
a vector bundle, (
6.2
) is the
\mathcal{H}_{x}
-universal extension

0\to V\to\sigma_{x}(V)\to\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\Ext^{1}(S,V)\otimes S\to 0,

(5)

for
V
a vector bundle, (
6.3
) is the
\mathcal{H}_{x}
-universal semi-simple quotient

0\to\sigma_{x}^{-1}(V)\to V\to\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\Hom(V,S)^{*}\otimes S\to 0,

(6)

(
6.2
) defines a natural transformation of functors
\eta_{x}\colon\id\to\sigma_{x}
.

We study the universal semi-simple quotients in more details.
For any
x\in X
and any
F\in\mathcal{H}
we define
\Top_{x}(F)
as the maximal semi-simple quotient of
F
, supported at
x
. Explicitly,
\Top_{x}(F)
is given by the natural morphism

(6.4)

F\to\oplus_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}S\otimes\Hom(F,S)^{*}=\Top_{x}(F).

Recall that for a vector bundle
V
one has exact triple

(6.5)

0\to\sigma^{-1}_{x}(V)\xrightarrow{\eta_{x}}V\to\Top_{x}(V)\to 0,

and

\len(\Top_{x}(V))=\sum_{S\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{x})}\dim\Hom(V,S)=\rank(V)

by Lemma
6.4
.
We arrive at useful

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
V
be a vector bundle,
x\in X
and
Q\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
be a quotient sheaf of
V
. Then
Q
has at most
\rank(V)
indecomposable summands.

Proof.

Let
Q=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}
with non-zero
Q_{i}
. Then any
\Top_{x}(Q_{i})
is non-zero and semi-simple and there is a surjection
V\to\Top_{x}(Q)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Top_{x}(Q_{i})
. By the above discussion,
\Top_{x}(Q)
is the quotient of
\Top_{x}(V)
and
n\leqslant\len(\Top_{x}(Q))\leqslant\len(\Top_{x}(V))=\rank(V)
.
∎

Definition 6.8
.

For a vector bundle
V
and
m\geqslant 1
, consider the composition of injections

\eta_{x}^{m}\colon\sigma^{-m}(V)\xrightarrow{\eta_{x}}\sigma^{-m+1}(V)\xrightarrow{\eta_{x}}\ldots\sigma^{-1}(V)\xrightarrow{\eta_{x}}V.

Denote by
\Top^{[m]}_{x}(V)
its cokernel.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
V
be a vector bundle,
x\in X
and
Q=\Top_{x}^{[m]}(V)
. Then
Q\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}

where
r=\rank(V)
, any
Q_{i}
is an indecomposable torsion sheaf supported at
x
and of length
m
. Moreover, assume

\Top_{x}(V)\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}S_{i}

with
S_{i}
simple, then (recall notation from Section
5.2
)

\Top^{[m]}_{x}(V)\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}S_{i}^{[m]}.

Proof.

Let
Q\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}
with indecomposable
Q_{i}
. By Lemma
6.7
,
r\leqslant\rank(V)
. By construction,
Q=\Top^{[m]}_{x}(V)
has a filtration
F_{j}
of length
m
with semi-simple factors
\Top_{x}(\sigma^{-j}_{x}(V))
,
j=0,\ldots,m-1
.
Intersecting
F_{j}
with
Q_{i}
one gets a filtration of
Q_{i}
of length
m
with semi-simple factors. Since
Q_{i}
is uniserial, these factors are simple (if non-zero), and
\len(Q_{i})\leqslant m
. Also we see that the length of
Q
is
m\cdot\rank(V)
. That is,

m\cdot\rank(V)=\len(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\len(Q_{i})\leqslant mr\leqslant m\cdot\rank(V).

It follows that we have
\len(Q_{i})=m
for all
i
and
r=\rank(V)
. We have

\oplus_{i}S_{i}\cong\Top_{x}(V)\cong\Top_{x}(Q)\cong\oplus_{i}\Top_{x}(Q_{i}),

and we may assume, up to renumbering, that
\Top_{x}(Q_{i})\cong S_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{x}
is a uniserial category, it follows that
Q_{i}\cong S_{i}^{[m]}
.
∎

Definition 6.10
.

For
x\in X
, consider the autoequivalence
c_{x}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
given by

c_{x}=\sigma_{x}^{w(x)}.

We will call
c_{x}
a
twist
.

Note that one has an exact sequence

(6.6)

0\to c_{x}^{-1}(V)\to V\to\Top_{x}^{[w(x)]}(V)\to 0

for any vector bundle
V
.

Lemma 6.11
.

The twist
c_{x}
preserves subcategories of torsion sheaves and torsion-free sheaves, and preserves rank and support of sheaves. Moreover,
c_{x}
acts by identity on torsion sheaves.

Proof.

Follows from the definition and Proposition
6.6
. For the last statement, let
F\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
, then
c_{x}(F)=\sigma_{x}^{w(x)}(F)\cong\tau^{-w(x)}F\cong F
since
\tau
has order
w(x)
on
\mathcal{H}_{x}
. For
y\neq x
and
F\in\mathcal{H}_{y}
, one has
\sigma_{x}(F)\cong F
and
c_{x}(F)=\sigma_{x}^{w(x)}(F)\cong F
.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

Geometrically, let
\mathcal{H}
be the category of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
, then
c_{x}
is the tensor twist with the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(x)
. In particular, for
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, twists
c_{x}
do not depend on the point
x
up to an isomorphism.

6.5.
Orthogonal subcategories and reduction of weights

Here we describe the orthogonal subcategory to an exceptional torsion sheaf, this is a crucial instrument for our further considerations. This description is well-known to experts, at least in the case of a weighted projective line (=rational curve), or for
m=1
, since
[
20
]
. We find it convenient to sketch a proof for completeness, which is analogous to
[
14
, Prop. 6.5]
, where
\mathbb{X}
is supposed to be a weighted projective line.

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\coh(\mathbb{X})
be a category of coherent sheaves on a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
. Let
E
be an exceptional torsion sheaf supported at point
x\in X
, and
m
be its length.
Let
E^{\perp}_{\Ab}=E^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}
be the orthogonal full subcategory. Then

E^{\perp}_{\Ab}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{S}_{1}\cong\coh(X,w^{\prime}),\mathcal{S}_{2}\cong\mathcal{A}_{m-1},

and
w^{\prime}(x)=w(x)-m
,
w^{\prime}(y)=w(x)
for
y\neq x
.
Moreover, the induced functor
\coh(X,w^{\prime})\to\coh(X,w)
preserves the rank and the support of sheaves, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is supported at
x
.

Proof.

We consider the case
m=1
first. For
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
this is
[
20
, Th. 9.5]
. We give a proof in general case, basing on axioms from Definition
6.1
.

Assume
E
is a simple exceptional sheaf, supported at point
x
of weight
w(x)=r\geqslant 2
. Denote
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=E^{\perp}_{\Ab}\subset\mathcal{H}
. By Proposition
3.7
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}
is thick, and by Proposition
3.4
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is abelian hereditary. Also, by Proposition
3.7

D^{b}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\cong E^{\perp}
is a full subcategory of
D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
, hence
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
\Ext
-finite. Clearly,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is Noetherian. Now we prove that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
has Serre duality in the form (
6.1
). Consider semi-orthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(\mathcal{H})=\langle D^{b}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}),\langle E\rangle\rangle,

let
\pi\colon D^{b}(\mathcal{H})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be the left adjoint functor to the inclusion. Explicitly, for

F\in D^{b}(\mathcal{H})
it is defined by the exact triangle

(6.7)

E\otimes\Hom^{\bullet}(E,F)\to F\to\pi(F)\to E\otimes\Hom^{\bullet}(E,F)[1].

It is known
[
7
, Prop. 3.7]
that
D^{b}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
has a Serre functor
S_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
, and one has

S_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}^{-1}(F)\cong\pi\circ S_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(F)

for any
F\in D^{b}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. It suffices to verify that
S_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
sends
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}[1]
, then one can put
\tau^{\prime}:=S_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}[-1]
. Recall that
S_{\mathcal{H}}=\tau[1]
, so we are to check that
\pi
sends
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
(i.e., to complexes concentrated in degree
0
).
Let
F\in\mathcal{H}
, the long exact sequence of cohomology associated with (
6.7
) shows that

H^{i}(\pi(F))=0
except for possibly
i=0,-1
, and that

H^{-1}(\pi(F))=\ker(E\otimes\Hom(E,F)\to F).

The canonical map
E\otimes\Hom(E,F)\to F
is injective since
E
is simple, so
H^{-1}(\pi(F))=0
and
\pi(F)
is isomorphic to an object of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
satisfies (H3). Also note that

(6.8)

\tau^{\prime-1}\cong\pi\circ\tau^{-1}.

For any point
y\neq x
torsion sheaves in
\mathcal{H}
supported at
y
belong to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, so (H6) holds. Pick some line bundle
L
in
\mathcal{H}
, then (
6.7
) gives the universal extension

0\to L\to\pi(L)\to E\otimes\Ext^{1}(E,L)\to 0,

hence
\pi(L)
is a line bundle in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Let
0\neq V\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be a vector bundle. Pick a point
y\neq x
and a sphere-like sheaf
S_{y}
supported at
y
. Using Lemma
6.4
, one constructs an infinite sequence in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

V=V_{0}\supset V_{1}\supset V_{2}\supset\ldots

of decreasing sub-bundles with
\coker(V_{i+1}\to V_{i})\cong S_{y}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Thus
V
has infinite length in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, and

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{0}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}_{0}
: object in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
has finite length in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
if and only if it has finite length in
\mathcal{H}
. So, (H4) holds. It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}/\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{0}\cong\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Hence, (H5) follows and notions of rank in
\mathcal{H}
and in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
agree.

Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
satisfies Definition
6.1
.

Using (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), we see that
\tau^{\prime}
preserves subcategory
\mathcal{H}_{y}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
for any point
y\in X
. For
y\neq x
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{y}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{H}_{y}
, so there is one
\tau^{\prime}
-orbit of simples in
\mathcal{H}_{y}
. For

, note that
\mathcal{H}_{x}\cong\mathcal{U}_{r}
and by Proposition

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}_{x}\cong\mathcal{U}_{r-1}
. Therefore
\tau^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{H}_{x}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
coincides with
\tau_{\mathcal{U}_{r-1}}
, and there is one
\tau^{\prime}
-orbit of
r-1
simple objects in
\mathcal{H}_{x}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. It follows that points of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are the same as points of
\mathcal{H}
, the weight function of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is as stated, and that notions of support for
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and for
\mathcal{H}
agree.

Now we treat the general case. We have
E=S^{[m]}
for some simple sheaf
S
. One can check as in
[
14
, Prop. 6.5]
that

(6.9)

E^{\perp}_{\Ab}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{S}_{1}=\langle S,\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S\rangle^{\perp}_{\Ab},\mathcal{S}_{2}=\langle\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S\rangle_{\Ab}.

Indeed,
\Hom^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{1})=0
by definition and
\Hom^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2})\cong\Hom^{\bullet}(\tau^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{1})^{*}=0
again by definition. Clearly

(note that the simple factors of

are

). Further,

together with
\mathcal{S}_{2}
generate

. Since

is an exceptional collection,
one has

D^{b}(\mathcal{H})=\langle\langle S,\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S\rangle^{\perp},\langle S,\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle,\langle\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle,\langle E\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle\times\langle\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle,\langle E\rangle\rangle.

Then (
6.9

E^{\perp}_{\Ab}=(\langle\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle\times\langle\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle)_{\Ab}=\langle\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle_{\Ab}\times\langle\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle_{\Ab}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}.

Family
\tau S,\ldots,\tau^{m-1}S
in

is quiver-like with the

\mathcal{S}_{2}\cong\mathcal{A}_{m-1}
. Remaining statements regarding

are obtained by applying inductively

is an exceptional collection of simple sheaves.
∎

6.6.
Weighted projective lines

Here we recall briefly the construction of the category
\coh\mathbb{X}
for a weighted projective line
\mathbb{X}=(\mathbb{P}^{1},w)
, proposed by Geigle and Lenzing
[
19
]
. We will need this description to provide some examples in Section
9
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be the weighted points of
\mathbb{X}
and let
r_{i}=w(x_{i})
. Let
V=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(1))
, choose non-zero
u_{i}\in V
such that
u_{i}(x_{i})=0
. Denote

A_{\mathbb{X}}:=S^{\bullet}(V)\otimes\Bbbk[U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}]/(U_{1}^{r_{1}}-u_{1},\ldots,U_{n}^{r_{n}}-u_{n}),

where
S^{\bullet}(V)
denotes the symmetric algebra.
Let
\mathbb{L}
be the abelian group generated by elements
\bar{c},\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n}
and relations
\bar{c}=r_{i}\cdot\bar{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
A_{\mathbb{X}}
is an
\mathbb{L}
-graded commutative algebra with the grading given by
\deg(U_{i})=\bar{x}_{i}
,
\deg(v)=\bar{c}
for
v\in V
.
Define
\coh\mathbb{X}
as the Serre quotient

\coh\mathbb{X}=\frac{\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}A_{\mathbb{X}}}{\mathrm{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}A_{\mathbb{X}}},

where
\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}A_{\mathbb{X}}
is the category of finitely generated
\mathbb{L}
-graded
A_{\mathbb{X}}
-modules, and
\mathrm{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}A_{\mathbb{X}}\subset\mathrm{mod}^{\mathbb{L}}{-}A_{\mathbb{X}}
is the subcategory of finite-dimensional modules.

For any
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{L}
, denote the object in
\coh\mathbb{X}
given by the shifted free module
A_{\mathbb{X}}(\bar{x})
by
\mathcal{O}(\bar{x})
. This is a line bundle, and all line bundles in
\coh\mathbb{X}
have this form.

One computes
\Hom
and
\Ext
spaces between line bundles as follows. Any element in
\mathbb{L}
can be written uniquely as

a\bar{c}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\bar{x}_{i},\quad\text{where}\quad 0\leqslant b_{i}\leqslant r_{i}-1.

This presentation is called
normal form
. One has

\dim\Hom(\mathcal{O}(\bar{x}),\mathcal{O}(\bar{y}))=\begin{cases}a+1,&a\geqslant 0,\\
0,&a<0,\end{cases}

where
a
is the coefficient at
\bar{c}
in the normal form of
\bar{y}-\bar{x}
.

Denote
\bar{\omega}=-2\bar{c}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}-1)\bar{x}_{i}
, then Serre duality for
\coh\mathbb{X}
is given by the shift of grading by
\bar{\omega}
:

\Ext^{1}(F_{1},F_{2})\cong\Hom(F_{2},F_{1}(\bar{\omega}))^{*}.

This allows one to find
\Ext^{1}
between line bundles easily.

Finally, remark that the functors
\sigma_{x}
and
c_{x}\colon\coh\mathbb{X}\to\coh\mathbb{X}
from Section
6.4
are given by the shift of grading on graded modules:

\sigma_{x_{i}}(F)=F(\bar{x}_{i}),\quad\sigma_{x}(F)=F(\bar{c})

for
i=1,\ldots,n
and any non-singular point
x\in X
, while

c_{x}(F)=F(\bar{c})

for all
x\in X
.

7.
Thick subcategories on weighted projective curves, big and small

Here we introduce two classes of thick subcategories on weighted projective curves that play special role. Similar results have been obtained in
[
14
]
for weighted projective
lines
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
be a weighted projective curve.
We denote by
\coh_{x}\mathbb{X}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
the full subcategory of torsion sheaves supported at a point
x\in X
, and by
\coh_{0}\mathbb{X}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
the full subcategory of all torsion sheaves.
We denote by
D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
the full subcategory of complexes with cohomology supported at
x
. By Proposition
3.7
,
D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})\cong D^{b}(\coh_{x}\mathbb{X})\cong D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{w(x)})
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
and
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be the corresponding thick subcategories (recall Proposition
3.7
). We will say that

(1)

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are
small
if

\mathcal{T}
is generated by an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves;

(2)

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are
big
if

\mathcal{T}
is the orthogonal to an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves;

(3)

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are
curve-like
if
\mathcal{S}
is the essential image of a fully faithful exact functor
\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\to\coh\mathbb{X}

preserving rank and support of sheaves for some weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}=(X,w^{\prime})
.

Remark 7.2
.

If
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is curve-like and
\Phi\colon\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\to\coh\mathbb{X}
is the functor from Definition
7.1
, then the functor
D(\Phi)\colon D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})\to D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is fully faithful with the image
\mathcal{T}
(by Proposition
3.7
).

Remark 7.3
.

By Lemma
6.3
, small subcategories are supported at orbifold points. By Proposition
5.2
, any small subcategory is equivalent to a direct product of the form
\mathcal{A}_{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{A}_{n_{k}}
. By Proposition
5.2
any subcategory of a small category is also small, and any subcategory containing a big subcategory is big.
Also note that small and big subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
are admissible.

Remark 7.4
.

By definition, there is a bijection between small and big subcategories of
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
, given by
\mathcal{T}\mapsto\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
.

Remark 7.5
.

Note that big subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
are in bijection with collections
(\mathcal{T}_{x})_{x}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{x}\subset D^{b}(\coh_{x}\mathbb{X})
is a subcategory generated by an exceptional collection, and
x
runs over orbifold points of
\mathbb{X}
(a collection of subcategories
(\mathcal{T}_{x})_{x}
corresponds to its right orthogonal, which is big). For any orbifold point
x\in X
the number of subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh_{x}\mathbb{X})\cong D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{w(x)})
generated by an exceptional collection is finite, and by Proposition
5.2
equals
\frac{1}{2}
of the total number of thick subcategories in
\mathcal{U}_{w(x)}
.
The number of thick subcategories in
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is
\binom{2n}{n}
by
[
16
, Prop. 2.4.2]
. Hence, the number of big subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is

\prod_{x\colon w(x)\geqslant 2}\frac{1}{2}\binom{2w(x))}{w(x)}.

Remark 7.6
.

Note that for a thick subcategory in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
condition “to be curve-like” is stronger than “to be equivalent to some category
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
for a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
” as Example
9.2
below shows. On the contrary, any thick abelian subcategory in
\coh\mathbb{X}
that is equivalent to some category
\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
for a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
, is curve-like, as we will see in Corollary
8.7
.

Now we give several equivalent definitions of big subcategories, highlighting their importance.
Some of the equivalences below appear in
[
14
, Prop. 6.2, Th. 6.3]
in the case of a weighted projective line.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective curve and
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
be a thick subcategory. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{S}=\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle_{\Ab}^{\perp}

for some exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
of torsion sheaves (that is,
\mathcal{S}
is big);

(1’)

\mathcal{S}=^{\perp}\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle_{\Ab}

for some exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
of torsion sheaves;

(2)

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is curve-like and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is small;

(3)

\mathcal{S}
contains a curve-like subcategory in
\coh\mathbb{X}
;

(4)

\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf;

(5)

\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and
\mathcal{S}=c_{x}(\mathcal{S})
for any point
x\in X
(cf. Definition
6.10
);

(6)

\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and
\mathcal{S}=c_{x}(\mathcal{S})
for some point
x\in X
.

Proof.

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(1’) by Serre duality.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) by iterated application of Proposition
6.13
.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(3) is trivial.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(4) is easy: let
\Phi\colon\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}\to\coh\mathbb{X}
be a fully faithful functor preserving rank and support of sheaves with
\im(\Phi)=\mathcal{S}
. Take any sphere-like torsion sheaf and vector bundle on
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
and apply
\Phi
to get a sphere-like torsion sheaf and a vector bundle on
\mathbb{X}
in
\mathcal{S}
.

(4)
\Longrightarrow
(1) is contained in a separate Lemma
7.8
below due to its length, this is the less trivial implication.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(5): if
\mathcal{S}
consists only of torsion sheaves, then
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})=\langle\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle,\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle\rangle
also does, and we get a contradiction. For the second, note that
c_{x}(E_{i})\cong E_{i}
for any
i
by Lemma
6.11
. Consequently,
c_{x}
preserves the orthogonal to
\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle
, which is
\mathcal{S}
.

(5)
\Longrightarrow
(6) is trivial.

(6)
\Longrightarrow
(4): let
V\neq 0
be a vector bundle in
\mathcal{S}
. Consider exact sequence (
6.6
)

0\to c_{x}^{-1}(V)\to V\to Q\to 0.

By Lemma
6.9
,
Q\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{\rank(V)}Q_{i}
, where
Q_{i}
are indecomposable sheaves of length
w(x)
supported at
x
. Hence
Q_{i}
are sphere-like by Lemma
6.3
. Note that
c_{x}^{-1}(V)\in\mathcal{S}
and thus
Q_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
as needed.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective curve and
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
be a thick subcategory. Assume
\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf. Then
\mathcal{S}
is big.

Proof.

We argue by induction in the total weight of orbifold points. The base case is
w=1
, that is,
\mathbb{X}=X
is a smooth projective curve. We refer to
[
17
, Lemma 4.1]
to see that
\mathcal{S}=\coh X
as soon as
\mathcal{S}
contains a torsion sheaf and a vector bundle. Hence,
\mathcal{S}
is big as the orthogonal to the empty exceptional collection. Now we consider the general case. We divided the argument into several steps.

Step 1. We may assume that
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
(or, equivalently,
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
) contains no simple exceptional sheaves.
Indeed, assume
E
is a simple exceptional sheaf and
\mathcal{S}\subset E^{\perp}_{\Ab}
. By Proposition
6.13
,
E^{\perp}_{\Ab}\cong\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
for a weighted projective curve
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
with smaller total weight of orbifold points. We observe that
\mathcal{S}
as a subcategory of
\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
also contains a vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf. Hence by induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{S}
is the right orthogonal in
\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
to an exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
of torsion sheaves on
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
. We deduce that
\mathcal{S}
is the right orthogonal in
\coh\mathbb{X}
to the exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},E
of torsion sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
.

Step 2. We may assume that
\mathcal{S}
contains no exceptional torsion sheaves. To check this, we find it more convenient to work with derived categories, let
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
.
Assume
E\in\mathcal{T}
is an exceptional torsion sheaf, supported at
x\in X
.
By Propositions
6.13
and
3.7
,
{}^{\perp}E\cong D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{l-1})\times D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
, where
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
is a weighted projective curve with smaller total weight of orbifold points,
l
is the length of
E
, and
D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{l-1})
is generated by an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves supported at
x
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{T}\cap^{\perp}E=\mathcal{T}_{1}\times\mathcal{T}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{l-1})
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
are thick subcategories.
We have semi-orthogonal decompositions

D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})=\langle\langle E\rangle,^{\perp}E\rangle=\langle\langle E\rangle,D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{l-1}),D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}=\langle\langle E\rangle,\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle.

We claim that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
as a subcategory of
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
also contains a vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf. For the former, assume
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is a torsion subcategory, then all

\mathcal{T}=\langle\langle E\rangle,\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle
is a torsion subcategory, and contains no vector bundles.
For the latter, assume
\mathcal{T}_{2}
has no sphere-like torsion sheaves.
Then
\mathcal{T}_{2}\cap D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is generated by an exceptional collection (see Proposition
5.2
), as well as
\mathcal{T}_{1}
(see Proposition
5.1
). It follows that

\mathcal{T}\cap D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})=\langle\langle E\rangle,\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle\cap D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})=\langle\langle E\rangle,\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\cap D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})\rangle

is also generated by an exceptional collection. Recall that
D^{b}_{x}(\coh\mathbb{X})\cong D^{b}(\mathcal{U}_{w(x)})
, Proposition
5.2
implies now that

\mathcal{T}\cap\coh_{x}\mathbb{X}
contains no sphere-like sheaves. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}
contains a sphere-like sheaf
M
supported at some other point
y\in\mathbb{X}
, and clearly
M\in^{\perp}E
and
M\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
. So, the claim holds.

Applying induction hypothesis to
\mathcal{T}_{2}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
, we see that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is big:

D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})=\langle\mathcal{T}_{2},\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle\rangle,

where
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
is an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves on
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
. By Proposition
5.1
,

D^{b}(\mathcal{A}_{l-1})=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\rangle\rangle,

where
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
is an exceptional collection of torsion sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
.

8.
Main results

Throughout this section
\mathbb{X}=(X,w)
is a weighted projective curve.
Here we prove our main result (Theorem
8.2
) saying that any thick subcategory in
\coh\mathbb{X}
or
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is either big or quiver-like, and obtain some consequences of this.

8.1.
Main theorem

We start from the following observation.

Proposition 8.1
.

Category
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is quiver-like if and only if
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathbb{X}
has domestic type. On the contrary, the category
\coh\mathbb{X}
is not a finite length category, therefore never is quiver-like.

Proof.

Note that
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
has a strong generator. Indeed, it has a semi-orthogonal decomposition

\langle D^{b}(\coh X),\mathcal{T}\rangle
into the derived category of a smooth projective curve (which has a strong generator by
[
8
, Th. 3.1.4]
) and a small subcategory
\mathcal{T}
(which is generated by an exceptional collection hence also has a strong generator).
Assume
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})\cong D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, then
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
has a strong generator. By Proposition
4.6
,
Q
is finite and acyclic. Hence
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\cong D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
, where
\Bbbk Q
is a hereditary finite-dimensional algebra. It is known then that
\mathbb{X}
is a weighted projective line of domestic type (see
[
38
, Sect. 10]
).
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective curve,
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be a thick subcategory, and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
. Then at least one of the following holds:

•

\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are big, or

•

\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{T}
is big
\Longleftrightarrow

\mathcal{S}
is big by definition.

Case 1:
\mathcal{S}
contains a non-zero vector bundle and a sphere-like torsion sheaf. Then
\mathcal{S}
is big by Lemma
7.8
.

Case 2:
\mathcal{S}
does not contain non-zero vector bundles, that is,
\mathcal{S}
consists only of torsion sheaves. Then
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like by Corollary
4.15
applied to
\mathcal{A}=\coh\mathbb{X}
and the function
r([F])=\len(F)
, where
F
is a torsion sheaf.

Case 3:
\mathcal{S}
does not contain sphere-like torsion sheaves. We prove then that
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
be the maximal exceptional collection of torsion sheaves in
\mathcal{T}
, then
\mathcal{T}=\langle\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\rangle
for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}:=^{\perp}\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{T}
. By our assumptions,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
does not contain exceptional torsion sheaves and sphere-like torsion sheaves. It follows then (see Proposition
5.2
) that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
does not contain torsion sheaves at all. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is quiver-like by
Corollary
4.15
applied to
\mathcal{A}=\coh\mathbb{X}
and the function
r([F]):=\rank(F)
(this argument, originating from
[
17
]
, is the heart of the paper). Let
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a vertex-like generating family of vector bundles in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Also,
\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle
is quiver-like by Case 2, let
E^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,E^{\prime}_{n}
be a vertex-like generating family of torsion sheaves in
\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle
. Then
\{E^{\prime}_{i}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\cup\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a vertex-like generating family for
\mathcal{T}=\langle\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\rangle
. Indeed,
\Hom(E^{\prime}_{i},V_{j})=0
since
E^{\prime}_{i}
is torsion and
V_{j}
is torsion-free, and
\Hom(V_{j},E^{\prime}_{i})=0
by semi-orthogonality. Hence,
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
are quiver-like by Corollary
4.14
.
∎

Now we formulate some consequences of Theorem
8.2
and examine the variety of thick subcategories.

8.2.
Admissible subcategories

Proposition 8.3
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be a quiver-like admissible subcategory. Then
\mathcal{T}
is generated by an exceptional collection.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be an admissible category. Then
\mathcal{T}
is big or
\mathcal{T}
is generated by an exceptional collection.

Proof.

For (1), recall that
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
has a strong generator, hence
\mathcal{T}
also has a strong generator. Assume
\mathcal{T}\cong D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)
, then by Proposition
4.6

Q
is finite and acyclic. Therefore
D^{b}_{0}(\Bbbk Q)\cong D^{b}(\mathrm{mod{-}}\Bbbk Q)
, and the latter category is generated by an exceptional collection (for example, of indecomposable projective
\Bbbk Q
-modules).

For (2), suppose
\mathcal{T}
is not big, then
\mathcal{T}
is quiver-like by Theorem
8.2
, and part (1) applies.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

Assume that
\mathbb{X}
is a weighted projective line: that is,
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then any admissible subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is generated by an exceptional collection. Moreover, any exceptional collection
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}
in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
can be completed to a full one.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.2
,
\mathcal{T}
is big or quiver-like. If
\mathcal{T}
is big then
\mathcal{T}\cong\mathcal{T}_{1}\times\mathcal{T}_{2}
by Proposition
7.7
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cong D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{Y})
for some weighted projective line
\mathbb{Y}=(\mathbb{P}^{1},w)
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is small. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{1}
(see
[
19
]
) and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are generated by exceptional collections, hence
\mathcal{T}
also does. Alternatively, if
\mathcal{T}
is quiver-like then Proposition
8.3
(1) applies.

The second part follows by considering the orthogonal
\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\rangle^{\perp}
and applying the first part.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

Assume that
\mathbb{X}
is not a weighted projective line: that is,
g(X)\geqslant 1
. Then any admissible subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is either small or big. In particular,
there are no exceptional vector bundles on
\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

One has a semi-orthogonal decomposition
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})=\langle\mathcal{T}^{\perp},\mathcal{T}\rangle
. If
\mathcal{T}
is not small neither big then neither of
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
is big, and both are generated by an exceptional collection by Proposition
8.3
(2). Consequently, all
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is generated by an exceptional collection, what is impossible for
g(X)\geqslant 1
(for example, because
K_{0}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is not finitely generated).

For the second statement note that a subcategory generated by an exceptional vector bundle is admissible and by the above is small or big and hence contains non-trivial torsion sheaves, which is not possible.
∎

Remark 8.6
.

Corollary
8.5
provides an alternative way to prove a well-known fact: the derived category of a connected smooth projective curve
X
of genus
>0
has no non-trivial semi-orthogonal decompositions. Indeed,
D^{b}(\coh X)
has no non-trivial small/big subcategories. Note that the classical argument by Okawa
[
44
]
is based on the fact that the canonical line bundle on
X
has enough non-vanishing sections, and we make no use of that.

Corollary 8.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
be a thick subcategory and
\mathcal{S}\cong\coh\mathbb{Y}
for some weighted projective curve
\mathbb{Y}=(Y,w^{\prime})
. Then
\mathcal{S}
is curve-like.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.2
,
\mathcal{S}
is either big or quiver-like. Since
\coh\mathbb{Y}
is not quiver-like (Proposition
8.1
),
\mathcal{S}
is big. By Proposition
7.7
,
\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is curve-like and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is small. Since
\coh\mathbb{Y}
is indecomposable into a direct product, we have
\mathcal{S}_{2}=0
and
\mathcal{S}
is curve-like.
∎

Remark 8.8
.

Note that the analogue of Corollary
8.7
for derived categories does not hold. See Example
9.2
for a thick subcategory
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
, which is equivalent to
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{Y})
for a weighted projective line
\mathbb{Y}
, but is not curve-like.

8.3.
The Jordan–Hölder property and absence of phantoms

We observe next that the derived categories of weighted projective curves do not demonstrate such pathologies as phantom subcategories or violation of the Jordan–Hölder property.

Recall the a semi-orthogonal decomposition is called
maximal
if its components are semi-orthogonal indecomposable. A triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is said to satisfy the Jordan–Hölder property if for any two maximal semi-orthogonal decompositions
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{n}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}\rangle
one has
n=m
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}\cong\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)}
for some permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
and all
i
.

Corollary 8.9
.

Category
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
satisfies the Jordan–Hölder property. More precisely, any maximal semi-orthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})

•

is generated by an exceptional collection if
X\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or

•

contains one copy of
D^{b}(\coh X)
, while other components are generated by exceptional objects if
g(X)\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
8.3
(2), an indecomposable admissible subcategory
\mathcal{T}
in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is big or is generated by an exceptional object. In the first case
\mathcal{T}
is equivalent to
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime})
where
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}=(X,w^{\prime})
(see Proposition
7.7
), and since
\mathcal{T}
is indecomposable we have
w^{\prime}=1
,
g(X)\geqslant 1
, and
\mathcal{T}\cong D^{b}(\coh X)
.
∎

Recall that a (smooth, proper) triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is called a
phantom
if
K_{0}(\mathcal{T})=0
while
\mathcal{T}\neq 0
.

Corollary 8.10
.

The category
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
does not contain a full triangulated subcategory which is a phantom.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
8.2
. Indeed, assume
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
is non-zero. If
\mathcal{T}\cong D^{b}_{0}(Q)
is quiver-like then
K_{0}(\mathcal{T})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|Q_{0}|}\neq 0
(see Proposition
4.5
), and if
\mathcal{T}
is big then
K_{0}(\mathcal{T})\supset K_{0}(X)\neq 0
(see Proposition
7.7
).
∎

8.4.
Torsion and torsion-free subcategories

Finally, we discuss torsion-free subcategories in
\coh\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 8.11
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective curve and
\mathcal{S}\subset\coh\mathbb{X}
be a thick subcategory. We will say that
\mathcal{S}
is

•

torsion
if all sheaves in
\mathcal{S}
are torsion;

•

torsion-free
if all torsion sheaves in
\mathcal{S}
are zero;

•

mixed
if
\mathcal{S}
contains both non-zero torsion and torsion-free sheaves.

We will use the same terminology for the corresponding subcategories in
D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
.

It follows from Theorem
8.2
that any torsion and any torsion-free subcategory is quiver-like. It follows from definitions that any small subcategory is torsion and any big subcategory is mixed. In particular, any small or big subcategory contains non-trivial torsion sheaves.

As the following proposition shows, a mixed subcategory is made of a torsion and a torsion-free subcategory.

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset D^{b}(\coh\mathbb{X})
be a thick subcategory. Assume
\mathcal{T}
is not big, then
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is torsion and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is torsion-free. Semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{T}
with such properties is unique. Moreover, if
\mathcal{T}_{2}\neq 0
then
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is small.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.2
,
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
are quiver-like. Let
F_{i},i\in I
be the vertex-like family in
\coh\mathbb{X}
, generating
\mathcal{T}
.
Denote by
I_{1}
(resp.
I_{2}
) the set of
i\in I
such that
F_{i}
is torsion (resp. torsion-free).
Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\langle F_{i}\rangle_{i\in I_{1}}
,
\mathcal{T}_{2}=\langle F_{i}\rangle_{i\in I_{2}}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
together generate
\mathcal{T}
. Also, for any
i_{1}\in I_{1},i_{2}\in I_{2}
one has
\Hom(F_{i_{2}},F_{i_{1}})=0
by the definition of vertex-like and
\Ext^{1}(F_{i_{2}},F_{i_{1}})=0

since
F_{i_{2}}
is torsion-free and
F_{i_{1}}
is torsion. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subset\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\perp}
and we get a semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\rangle
.
Note that
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cap\coh\mathbb{X}
coincides with the family of all torsion sheaves in
\mathcal{T}
, and the uniqueness follows.

For the second part, assume
\mathcal{T}_{2}\neq 0
. If
\mathcal{T}_{1}
contains a sphere-like torsion sheaf then
\mathcal{T}
is big by Lemma
7.8
, which contradicts to assumptions. If
\mathcal{T}_{1}
has no sphere-like torsion sheaves then
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is generated by an exceptional collection of sheaves (see Proposition
5.2
), hence is small.
∎

Example 9.3
.

Assume that

.

A Classical Limit of Noumi’s 𝒒 \boldsymbol{q} -Integral Operator
Alexei BORODIN † 1 {}^{{\dagger}^{1}}, Ivan CORWIN † 2 † 3 † 4 {}^{{\dagger}^{2}{\dagger}^{3}{\dagger}^{4}} and Daniel REMENIK † 5 {}^{{\dagger}^{5}}

Abstract
We demonstrate how a known Whittaker function integral identity arises from the t = 0 t=0 and q → 1 q\to 1 limit of the Macdonald polynomial eigenrelation satisfied by Noumi’s q q -integral operator.

The
class-one
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
-Whittaker functions
are of great interest in representation theory, integrable systems and number theory (see Gerasimov, Lebedev and Oblezin
[
8
]
and references therein).
Givental
[
5
,
9
]
gave the following integral representation, which we will take as our definition:

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N(N-1)/2}}\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{d}x_{k,i}\,e^{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)},

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
,
X=(x_{k,i}\colon 1\leq i\leq k\leq N)
,
x_{N,i}=x_{i}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(X)={\rm i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{k,i}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_{k-1,i}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(e^{x_{k,i}-x_{k+1,i}}+e^{x_{k+1,i+1}-x_{k,i}}\right).

Whittaker functions satisfy the following two integral identities.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
u>0
and
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
with
\Re(\lambda_{i}+\nu_{j})>0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}x\,e^{-ue^{x_{1}}}\psi_{-{\rm i}\lambda}(x)\psi_{-{\rm i}\nu}(x)=u^{-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_{j}+\nu_{j})}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Gamma(\lambda_{i}+\nu_{j}),

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}x\,e^{-ue^{-x_{N}}}\psi_{{\rm i}\lambda}(x)\psi_{{\rm i}\nu}(x)=u^{-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\lambda_{j}+\nu_{j})}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Gamma(\lambda_{i}+\nu_{j}).

The first of these identities is due to Stade
[
13
]
, while the second follows readily by appealing to the fact that
\psi_{\lambda}(x)=\psi_{-\lambda}(x^{\prime})
where
x^{\prime}=-x_{N-i+1}
. They also follow from
[
12
, Corollaries 3.6 and 3.7]
once one observes that the multiplicative Whittaker functions
\Psi^{N}_{\lambda}(x)
in
[
12
]
are related to those defined above via
\Psi^{N}_{\lambda}(x)=\psi_{{\rm i}\lambda}(\log x)
, with
\log x=(\log x_{1},\ldots,\log x_{N})
.

The Plancherel theory for Whittaker functions (see, for example,
[
2
, Section 4.1.1]
) implies that for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\psi_{-\lambda}(x)\psi_{\lambda}(y)m_{N}(\lambda)=\delta(x-y),

where the Sklyanin measure
m_{N}(\lambda)
is defined as

\displaystyle m_{N}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}N!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{\Gamma({\rm i}\xi_{i}-{\rm i}\xi_{j})},

and
\delta(x-y)
is the Dirac delta function for
x=y
. Using the above identity one readily observes the equivalence of the results of Proposition
1
and the following two eigenrelations. The integral operator on the right-hand side of (
1
) is referred to as a
dual Baxter operator
by Gerasimov, Lebedev and Oblezin
[
6
]
, wherein the below result is demonstrated in a rather different manner than that followed in this present work.

Proposition 2
.

For
u>0
and
\mathrm{Im}(w_{i})<0
,
1\leq i\leq N
,

\displaystyle e^{-ue^{-x_{1}}}\psi_{-w}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,m_{N}(\xi)u^{{\rm i}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(w_{i}+\xi_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Gamma(-{\rm i}\xi_{i}-{\rm i}w_{j})\psi_{\xi}(x),

(1)

and

\displaystyle e^{-ue^{-x_{N}}}\psi_{w}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,m_{N}(\xi)u^{{\rm i}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}(w_{i}+\xi_{i})}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\Gamma(-{\rm i}\xi_{i}-{\rm i}w_{j})\psi_{-\xi}(x).

(2)

The aim of this note is to explain how the eigenrelation in (
2
) arises as the
q\to 1
limit of a certain eigenrelation involving Noumi’s
q
-integral operator and the Macdonald symmetric polynomials.

Definition 3
.

The
q
-shift operator
T_{q,z_{i}}
acts by mapping function
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i},\ldots,z_{N})\mapsto f(z_{1},\ldots,qz_{i},\ldots,z_{N})
. For
u\in\mathbb{C}
of suitably small modulus,
Noumi’s
q
-integral operator

\mathfrak{N}^{u}
acts on the space of analytic functions in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}^{\zeta}=\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{N}}\zeta^{|\nu|}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{q^{\nu_{i}}z_{i}-q^{\nu_{j}}z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tz_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\nu_{i}}}{(qz_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\nu_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(T_{q,z_{i}})^{\nu_{i}}.

Here
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{N}
.

This operator as well as the below eigenrelation it satisfies is due to M. Noumi. It first appeared in
[
4
, Proposition 3.24]
, and further details regarding its derivation are forthcoming in
[
11
]
. It was subsequently discussed in
[
3
, Section 4]
and a proof of the eigenrelation was given in an appendix therein by E. Rains. The Macdonald
q
-difference operators are likewise diagonal in the basis of Macdonald symmetric polynomials with eigenvalues of the form
e_{r}\big(q^{\lambda_{1}}t^{N-1},q^{\lambda_{2}}t^{N-2},\ldots,q^{\lambda_{N}}\big)
, with
e_{r}
the
r^{\rm th}
elementary symmetric polynomial. While these difference operators must commute with the Noumi
q
-integral operator there is presently no easy way to express them through each other.

Recall the Macdonald symmetric polynomial
P_{\lambda}(z)
indexed by partitions
\lambda
and symmetric in the
z
-variables with coefficients which are rational functions of two auxiliary parameters
q,t\in[0,1)
(see, for example,
[
2
,
10
]
). We have the following eigenrelation.

Proposition 4
.

Noumi’s
q
-integral operator is diagonal in the basis of Macdonald symmetric polynomials so that for any partition
\lambda
,

\displaystyle\big(\mathfrak{N}^{\zeta}P_{\lambda}\big)(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{\big(q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{N+1-i}\zeta;q\big)_{\infty}}{\big(q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{N-i}\zeta;q\big)_{\infty}}P_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

(3)

This eigenrelation can be understood as a formal power series identity in
\zeta
or as a convergent series identity, provided
|\zeta|
is suitably small.

The remainder of this note will be devoted to showing how when
t=0
and
q\to 1
under particular scaling this eigenrelation yields that of (
2
). We will proceed formally. Various uniform estimates would be necessary for this particular route to yield a rigorous derivation of (
2
). Since this identity has already been proved through other means, we do not provide these necessary details.

Our derivation has two steps. The first takes the termwise limit of the Noumi operator, yielding the eigenrelation (
5
). It is this step which we do not fully justify. The second step uses residue calculus to rewrite the resulting summation as the claimed contour integral formula.

From here on set
t=0
and take the following scalings

\displaystyle q=e^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\lambda_{k}=(N-2k+1)\varepsilon^{-1}\log\big(\varepsilon^{-1}\big)+\varepsilon^{-1}x_{k},\qquad z_{k}=e^{{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{k}},\qquad\zeta=-u\varepsilon^{N}.

Furthermore, define the following scaled version of
t=0
Macdonald symmetric polynomial

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon}_{w}(x)=\varepsilon^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}A(\varepsilon)}P_{\lambda}(z),\qquad A(\varepsilon)=-\tfrac{1}{6}\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}-\varepsilon^{-1}\log(\varepsilon/2\pi).

Note, at
t=0
, the Macdonald symmetric polynomial has been identified with the class-one
q
-Whittaker functions, as defined in the work of
Gerasimov, Lebedev and Oblezin
[
7
]
(the term “
q
-Whittaker function” is used to denote different objects by different authors).

Since we will not be proving a theorem, let us summarize here the result we will formally demonstrate. Under the above scaling, assume that a sequence of symmetric function
f^{\varepsilon}(z)
have a limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
to some
\tilde{f}(w)
. Then, formally we will show that

\displaystyle\big(\mathfrak{N}^{\zeta}f^{\varepsilon}\big)(z)\to\big(\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{-u}\tilde{f}\big)(w),

where the limiting operator
\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{u}
is defined in (
4
) and is identified with the dual Baxter operator through Lemma
5
. This is the key result of this paper.

Step 1.
Let us consider how (
3
) scales as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The right-hand side at
t=0
equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{\lambda_{N}}\zeta;q)_{\infty}}P_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}).

Observe that from the convergence of the
e_{q}
-exponential to the usual exponential (see
[
2
, Section 3.1.1]
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\zeta q^{\lambda_{N}};q)_{\infty}}\longrightarrow e^{-ue^{-x_{N}}}.

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, it was explained how
t=0
Macdonald polynomials (i.e.,
q
-Whittaker functions) converge to Whittaker functions. Certain tail estimates necessary for that argument were further provided in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1.7]
. The resulting convergence holds that

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon}_{w}(x)\longrightarrow\psi_{w}(x).

Note that in both
[
8
]
and
[
2
]
there was a mistake – the prefactor for
A(\varepsilon)
in the definition of
\psi^{\varepsilon}_{w}(x)
should be
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
(as above) whereas in
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
2
, Theorem 4.1.7]
it was written as
\frac{(N-1)(N+2)}{2}
. Let us briefly explain where this error came from (in reference to the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 4.1.7]
). Comparing equation (3.8) with (4.25) we find that in (4.25) the term
\Delta^{\varepsilon}(\underline{x}_{\ell})
should actually be
\Delta^{\varepsilon}(\underline{x}_{\ell+1})
(here
\ell+1=N
). This error resulted in neglecting
\ell+1
extra factors of
e^{A(\varepsilon)}
which jives with the difference between the incorrect and correct powers
\frac{(N-1)(N+2)}{2}-\frac{N(N-1)}{2}=N
.

Turning to the left-hand side, let us consider how each term in the summation scales:

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\nu_{i}}z_{i}-q^{\nu_{j}}z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}=\frac{e^{-\varepsilon\nu_{i}+{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{i}}-e^{-\varepsilon\nu_{j}+{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{j}}}{e^{{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{i}}-e^{{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{j}}}\longrightarrow\frac{{\rm i}(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})+(w_{j}-w_{i})}{w_{j}-w_{i}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(qz_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\nu_{i}}}=\frac{1}{1-e^{-\varepsilon+{\rm i}\varepsilon(w_{i}-w_{j})}}\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\varepsilon+{\rm i}\varepsilon(w_{i}-w_{j})}}\dotsm\frac{1}{1-e^{-\nu_{i}\varepsilon+{\rm i}\varepsilon(w_{i}-w_{j})}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{1}{(qz_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\nu_{i}}}}{}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon(1-{\rm i}(w_{i}-w_{j}))}\dotsm\frac{1}{\varepsilon(\nu_{i}-{\rm i}(w_{i}-w_{j}))}+\rm{l.o.t.}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{1}{(qz_{i}/z_{j};q)_{\nu_{i}}}}{}=\varepsilon^{-\nu_{i}}\frac{\Gamma(1+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}+\rm{l.o.t.},

where
\rm{l.o.t.}
denotes lower order terms in
\varepsilon
. Also note that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}T^{\nu_{i}}_{q,z_{i}}P_{\lambda}(z)=P_{\lambda}\big(e^{{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{1}-\varepsilon\nu_{1}},\dots,e^{{\rm i}\varepsilon w_{N}-\varepsilon\nu_{N}}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}S^{\nu_{i}}_{{\rm i},w_{i}}\psi^{\varepsilon}_{w}(x)\varepsilon^{-\frac{N(N-1)}{2}-\frac{(N+2)(N-1)}{2}A(\varepsilon)},

where the effect of
S_{a,w_{i}}
is to shift
w_{i}
by
a
. Putting all of this together we
deduce that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{u}=\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{N}}(u)^{|\nu|}\prod_{i<j}\frac{w_{j}-w_{i}+{\rm i}(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})}{w_{j}-w_{i}}\prod_{i,j}\frac{\Gamma(1+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(S_{{\rm i},w_{i}})^{\nu_{i}}

(4)

satisfies the eigenrelation

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{-u}\psi_{w}(x)=e^{-ue^{-x_{N}}}\psi_{w}(x).

(5)

This completes the first step of our derivation. As we already mentioned, various estimates are needed in order to turn this into a rigorous proof.

Step 2.
The purpose of the second step is to show how
\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{-u}
can be rewritten in terms of contour integrals through residue calculus. We will obtain this as a consequence of the following slightly more general result:

Lemma 5
.

Let
a
be a positive real number and consider a symmetric function
f
defined on the set
\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\colon\mathrm{Im}(w_{i})\leq-a,\,i=1,\dots,N\}
which is bounded and analytic in each variable in this set.
Then

\displaystyle\big(\tilde{\mathfrak{N}}^{-u}f\big)(w)=\int_{(a+{\rm i}\mathbb{R})^{N}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,s_{N}(\xi)\,u^{\sum_{i}({\rm i}w_{i}-\xi_{i})}\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\Gamma(\xi_{j}-{\rm i}w_{i})f(-{\rm i}\xi)

(6)

for
u>0
, where
s_{N}
is the following variant of the Sklyanin measure:

\displaystyle s_{N}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{N}N!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\xi_{i}-\xi_{j})}.

To see that this implies the desired result, let
f(w)=\psi_{w}(x)
and observe that
f
satisfies the necessary boundedness thanks to
[
2
, Lemma 4.1.19]
.
Making the change of variables
\xi\mapsto-{\rm i}\xi
and combining (
6
) with (
5
) we easily deduce (
2
).

Proof of Lemma
5
.

We will prove the result by expanding the integral on the right-hand side of (
6
) into residues and matching the result with the summation on the right-hand side of (
4
).

For
i,j=1,\dots,N
, the integrand on the right-hand side of (
6
) has singularities coming from the factor
\Gamma(\xi_{j}-{\rm i}w_{i})
at each point of the form
\xi_{j}={\rm i}w_{i}-\nu
for
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(where
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
stands for the set of non-negative integers). By shifting each contour to the left to
-\infty
we will pick up each of these singularities, and the result is that the integral will equal the sum of the associated residues (The fact that the contours of integration can be deformed in this way follows from our assumption on
f
and the known asymptotics of the Gamma function, which give
c_{1}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|\mathrm{Im}(z)|}|\mathrm{Im}(z)|^{\eta}\leq|\Gamma(z)|\leq c_{2}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|\mathrm{Im}(z)|}|\mathrm{Im}(z)|^{\eta}
for
\mathrm{Re}(z)
in some finite interval and some
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, see, e.g.,
[
1
, (6.1.45)]
;
observe also that, thanks to the factor
u^{\sum_{i}({\rm i}w_{i}-\xi_{i})}
, there is no pole at
-\infty
).
Each of these residues is evaluated at a point of the form
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})=({\rm i}w_{m_{1}}-\nu_{1},\dots,{\rm i}w_{m_{N}}-\nu_{N})
for some
(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})\in\{1,\dots,N\}^{N}
and
\nu_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,N
, so the right-hand side of (
6
) equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{N})\in\{1,\dots,N\}^{N}}\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{N}}u^{\sum_{i}({\rm i}w_{i}-{\rm i}w_{m_{i}}+\nu_{i})}\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}}}{\prod_{i}\nu_{i}!}f(w_{m_{1}}+{\rm i}\nu_{1},\dots,w_{m_{N}}+{\rm i}\nu_{N})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\prod_{i\neq j}\Gamma({\rm i}w_{m_{j}}-\nu_{j}-{\rm i}w_{i})\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{\Gamma({\rm i}w_{m_{i}}-\nu_{i}-{\rm i}w_{m_{j}}+\nu_{j})}.

If
m_{i}=m_{j}
for some
i\neq j
then the associated term in the above sum equals 0. In fact, such a term has a factor of the form
\Gamma(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})^{-1}\Gamma(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})^{-1}
, which equals 0 because
\nu_{j}-\nu_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence the above sum is restricted to the case where
m_{i}=\sigma(i)
for some
\sigma\in S_{N}
and equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{N}}u^{\sum_{i}({\rm i}w_{i}-{\rm i}w_{\sigma(i)}+\nu_{i})}\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}}}{\prod_{i}\nu_{i}!}f(w_{\sigma(1)}+{\rm i}\nu_{1},\dots,w_{\sigma(N)}+{\rm i}\nu_{N})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\prod\limits_{j\neq i}\Gamma({\rm i}w_{\sigma(j)}-\nu_{j}-{\rm i}w_{i}))}{\prod\limits_{i<j}\Gamma({\rm i}w_{\sigma(i)}-\nu_{i}-{\rm i}w_{\sigma(j)}+\nu_{j}))\Gamma({\rm i}w_{\sigma(j)}-\nu_{j}-{\rm i}w_{\sigma(i)}+\nu_{i}))}.

By the symmetry of
f
, the sum in
\nu
does not depend on

\sigma\in S_{N}
, and therefore if we choose
\sigma
to be the identity we deduce that
the sum equals

\sum_{\nu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{N}}(-u)^{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i}\nu_{i}!}f(w+{\rm i}\nu)\frac{\prod\limits_{i\neq j}\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i})-\nu_{j}))}{\prod\limits_{i<j}\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{i}-w_{j})-\nu_{i}+\nu_{j})\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i})-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i})}.

To complete the argument matching the right-hand side of (
6
) to the right-hand side of (
4
) it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{i}\nu_{i}!}\frac{\prod\limits_{i\neq j}\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i})-\nu_{j}))}{\prod\limits_{i<j}\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{i}-w_{j})-\nu_{i}+\nu_{j})\Gamma({\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i})-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\prod_{i<j}\frac{w_{j}-w_{i}+{\rm i}(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})}{w_{j}-w_{i}}\prod\limits_{i,j}\frac{\Gamma(1+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+{\rm i}(w_{j}-w_{i}))}.

Observe first of all that in the last product on the right-hand side of the above equation, the terms
i=j
equal

\Gamma(1+\nu_{i})^{-1}=1/(\nu_{i}!)
. Therefore if we write
r_{i}={\rm i}w_{i}
, we need to show that

\displaystyle\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{\Gamma(r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}))}{\Gamma(r_{i}-r_{j}-\nu_{i}+\nu_{j})}=\prod_{i<j}\frac{r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i}}{r_{j}-r_{i}}\frac{\Gamma(1+r_{i}-r_{j})\Gamma(1+r_{j}-r_{i})}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{j}+r_{i}-r_{j})\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+r_{j}-r_{i})}.

(7)

Recalling Euler’s reflection formula

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}

(8)

and the fact that
z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(1+z)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1+r_{i}-r_{j})\Gamma(1+r_{j}-r_{i})}{r_{j}-r_{i}}=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi(r_{j}-r_{i}))}

and

\displaystyle\frac{r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i}}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{j}+r_{i}-r_{j})\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+r_{j}-r_{i})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{\sin(\pi(r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i}))}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(1-r_{j}+r_{i}+\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{j}+r_{i}-r_{j})}\frac{\Gamma(1+r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}+\nu_{i})}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{i}+r_{j}-r_{i})}.

Using these identities and the fact that
\sin(\pi(a+k))=(-1)^{k}\sin(\pi a)
, the right-hand side of (
7
) becomes

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}}\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{\Gamma(1-r_{j}+r_{i}+\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{j}+r_{i}-r_{j})},

and hence (
7
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}}\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{\Gamma(1-r_{i}+r_{j}+\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})}{\Gamma(1+\nu_{j}+r_{i}-r_{j})}\frac{\Gamma(r_{i}-r_{j}-\nu_{i}+\nu_{j})}{\Gamma(r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j})}=1.

But using (
8
) again the left-hand side equals

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}}\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi(r_{i}-r_{j}-\nu_{i}+\nu_{j}))}\frac{\sin(\pi(r_{j}-r_{i}-\nu_{j}))}{\pi}=(-1)^{\kappa}

with

\displaystyle\kappa=\sum_{i<j}(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})+\sum_{i\neq j}(\nu_{i}-2\nu_{j})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\kappa}{}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}(n-2m+1)\nu_{m}-(n-1)\sum_{m=1}^{n}\nu_{m}=2\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1-m)\nu_{m},

which finishes our derivation since
\kappa
is even.
∎

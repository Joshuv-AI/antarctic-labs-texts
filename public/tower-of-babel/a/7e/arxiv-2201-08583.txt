Tensor-based Basis Function Learning for Three-dimensional Sound Speed Fields Preprint: Author, JASA
Lei Cheng, Xingyu Ji, Hangfang Zhao, Jianlong Li, Wen Xu

Abstract
Basis function learning is the stepping stone towards effective three-dimensional (3D) sound speed field (SSF) inversion for various acoustic signal processing tasks, including ocean acoustic tomography, underwater target localization/tracking, and underwater communications. Classical basis functions include the empirical orthogonal functions (EOFs), Fourier basis functions, and their combinations. The unsupervised machine learning method, e.g., the K-SVD algorithm, has recently tapped into the basis function design, showing better representation performance than the EOFs. However, existing methods do not consider basis function learning approaches that treat 3D SSF data as a third-order tensor , and thus cannot fully utilize the 3D interactions/correlations therein. To circumvent such a drawback, basis function learning is linked to tensor decomposition in this paper, which is the primary drive for recent multi-dimensional data mining. In particular, a tensor-based basis function learning framework is proposed, which can include the classical basis functions (using EOFs and/or Fourier basis functions) as its special cases. This provides a unified tensor perspective for understanding and representing 3D SSFs. Numerical results using the South China Sea 3D SSF data have demonstrated the excellent performance of the tensor-based basis functions.



I
Introduction

Basis function learning for sound speed fields (SSFs) has played a vital role in a wide range of acoustic signal processing tasks, such as ocean acoustic tomography
munk2009ocean
;
zhu2020
, underwater target localization/tracking
li2011time
;
michalopoulou2021matched
, and underwater communications
qu2014two
.
Effective basis functions
can significantly reduce the number of unknown parameters to be estimated, thereby making the originally under-determined SSF inversion problem much more manageable. The underlying rationale is that sound speeds are correlated across spatial and temporal domains
munk2009ocean
;
zhu2020
;
bianco2016compressive
;
huang2014method
, making SSFs viable to be accurately represented by a set of basis functions.
These basis functions are expected to
have high expressive power such that only a few of them are capable of accurate SSF representation.

Recently, this goal
was noticed to coincide
with the aim of unsupervised representation learning
bianco2018machine
, and thus has triggered the surging development of machine learning
theodoridis2020machine
for ocean
acoustics

bianco2019machine
;
niu2021mode
;
ozanich2020feedforward
. Specifically, the classical empirical orthogonal functions (EOFs)
leblanc1980underwater
can be interpreted as the basis vectors derived from principal component analysis (PCA)
wold1987principal
, which suggests that the nonlinear variants of PCA, e.g., kernel PCA
scholkopf1997kernel
, can potentially give nonlinear SSF representations. Furthermore, popular dictionary learning (DL) methods
tovsic2011dictionary
, e.g., K-SVD
aharon2006k
, which have shown remarkable performance in the image and video de-noising, were introduced to learn a
reduced-order
representation of SSFs
bianco2017dictionary
, showing improved generalization performance in SSF reconstruction. The success behind EOFs and K-SVD-based approach lies in that the basis functions are directly learnt from the training SSF data
elad2010sparse
,
while
not generated
ad hoc
from a standard set of functions such as Fourier basis functions
bracewell1986fourier
or wavelets
antonini1992image
,
which
exemplifies the effectiveness of data-driven approach in representation learning.

Figure 1:
Illustration of three-dimensional (3D) sound speed field (SSF) data and its matrix unfolding operation.

For 3D SSF data, as illustrated in Fig.
1
,
sound speeds
are correlated across 3D coordinates,
since the processes driving the ocean sound speed profiles are inherently continuous in space and time.
On the other hand, both EOFs and K-SVD algorithm are designed for two-dimensional (2D) data, e.g., an SSF matrix, and thus did not take the multi-dimensional correlations among sound speeds into account. Therefore, to use EOFs or K-SVD method, we should firstly unfold the 3D SSF data into an SSF matrix (see Fig.
1
). However, the matrix unfolding operation (also called matricization)
breaks
the original 3D structure of data and thus induce information loss
sidiropoulos2017tensor
;
panagakis2021tensor
. This drawback has been theoretically proved in the multi-dimensional harmonic retrieval task
roemer2014analytical
, and reported in various signal processing applications, including directional-of-arrival (DOA) estimation
cheng2015subspace
, blind source separation
cheng2020learning
and image completion
zhao2015bayesian
. This difficulty leads to an immediate question:
how to avoid the information loss caused by matricization in a principled manner?

This question invites the framework of
tensor decomposition
and the associated
multi-linear algebra

kolda2009tensor
, which are much richer than their matrix-based counterparts. Over the past two decades, tensor decomposition has become the primary drive for understanding/representing multi-dimensional data, and has achieved great success in various machine learning and signal processing applications
sidiropoulos2017tensor
;
panagakis2021tensor
.
In this paper, we not only show the state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance of tensor-based basis function learning for 3D SSFs via extensive numerical results, but also theoretically prove that the classical basis functions (using EOFs and/or Fourier basis functions)
leblanc1980underwater
;
cornuelle1989ocean
;
morawitz1996three
are interestingly the special case of the proposed tensor-based learning framework. The latter insight justifies the effectiveness of the tensor-based approach from another angle, and further paves the way for future investigation of better basis functions through the lens of a unified tensor perspective.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we briefly review classical basis functions as well as the recent ones for SSF representation. In Section
III
,
representation learning using tensor decomposition is introduced. Then, we propose a tensor-based basis function learning framework for 3D SSF, and further reveal the connections between the classical basis functions and the tensor-based counterparts in Section
IV
. Tensor-based basis function learning algorithms for one 3D SSF and multiple 3D SSFs are introduced in Section
IV
. Extensive numerical results are reported in Section
V
. Finally, conclusions and future research directions are discussed in Section
VI
.

II
Basis Functions for Sound Speed Fields

II.1
2D SSF: PCA and EOFs

Consider a 2D SSF matrix
\mathbf{Y}=[\mathbf{y}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{y}_{J}]\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}

containing

J
1D temporal/spatial sound speed profiles (SSPs), where
I

usually denotes
the number of discrete points in depth.

In order to extract the basis functions that capture the most variances of data, PCA
wold1987principal
is performed on the SSF matrix
\mathbf{Y}
. Particularly,
\mathbf{Y}
is first centered by subtracting a mean matrix
\mathbf{M}=[\mathbf{m},\cdots,\mathbf{m}]\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
with
\mathbf{m}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times 1}
, giving a zero-mean SSF matrix
\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{x}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{J}]=\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
, in which each element is known as SSF perturbation
munk2009ocean
;
zhu2020
. Then, the eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) of the correlation matrix
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}
finds the EOFs
leblanc1980underwater
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}},

(1)

where
\mathbf{E}=[\mathbf{e}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{e}_{I}]

contains
the EOFs
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\}_{i}
(eigenvectors) and
\mathbf{\Lambda}=\text{diag}([\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{I}])

contains the eigenvalue

\lambda_{i}
associated with the
i
-th EOF
\mathbf{e}_{i}
, for
i=1,\cdots,I
. Without loss of generality, it is assumed that
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{I}
.

Typically, we only retain
K(K\leq I)
leading-order EOFs to represent the SSF matrix for dimensionality reduction. Given the EOF matrix
\mathbf{E}_{K}=[\mathbf{e}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{e}_{K}]\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times K}
, the zero-mean SSF matrix can be approximately represented by
leblanc1980underwater

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\approx\mathbf{E}_{K}\mathbf{W},

(2)

where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times J}
is the representation coefficient matrix. Since the EOF matrix
\mathbf{E}_{K}
is orthonormal (i.e.,
\mathbf{E}_{K}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{E}_{K}=\mathbf{I}_{K}
), the least-squares (LS) estimates of the coefficient matrix can be efficiently computed by
leblanc1980underwater

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{E}_{K}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{X}.

(3)

For an unseen zero-mean SSF sample
\mathbf{X}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J^{\prime}}
, given EOF matrix
\mathbf{E}_{K}
,
the
coefficient matrix
\mathbf{W}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times J^{\prime}}
used for SSF representation is computed by
leblanc1980underwater

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{*}=\mathbf{E}_{K}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{X}^{*}.

(4)

II.2
2D SSF: K-SVD and Over-complete Dictionary

To seek a more
effective reduced-order
representation of SSF, dictionary learning methods
tovsic2011dictionary
;
aharon2006k
, which were originally designed for image/video de-noising, has recently tapped into ocean
signal processing

bianco2017dictionary
. The key idea is to jointly optimize an over-complete dictionary matrix
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times Z}(Z\geq I)
and the associated sparse coefficient matrix
\mathbf{V}
such that the reconstruction error is minimized
aharon2006k
;
bianco2017dictionary
:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times Z}}\left\{\min_{\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{Z\times J}}||\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{V}||_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~||\mathbf{V}_{:,j}||_{0}\leq T,~~j=1,\cdots J,

(5)

where
T
is a pre-defined upper bound value for the number of non-zero elements in each column
\mathbf{V}_{:,j},\forall j
.

To solve
the
problem
in Eq. (
5
)
in a computationally efficient manner, K-SVD
aharon2006k
was proposed to alternatively update the dictionary matrix
\mathbf{Q}
(called dictionary update step) and the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{V}
(called sparse coding step). More concretely, in the
t
-th iteration, given dictionary matrix
\mathbf{Q}^{t-1}
and denote the
j
-th column of matrix
\mathbf{V}
as
\mathbf{v}_{j}
, sparse coding step consists of
J
subproblems
aharon2006k
:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{v}_{j}}||\mathbf{x}_{j}-\mathbf{Q}^{t-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}||_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~~||\mathbf{v}_{j}||_{0}\leq T,~~j=1,\cdots,J,

(6)

each of which can be efficiently solved by “off-the-shelf” sparsity-aware optimization algorithms
theodoridis2020machine
, including orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP)
tropp2007signal
, approximate message passing (AMP)
donoho2010message
, and so forth. Then, given the learnt coefficient matrix
\mathbf{V}=[\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{J}]
, K-SVD algorithm utilizes the K-means method for vector quantization (VQ) codebook design to give an updated dictionary matrix
\mathbf{Q}^{t+1}
.
The iterative K-SVD algorithm was shown to converge
to a local minima of the problem in Eq. (
5
)
aharon2006k
.

After the convergence of the K-SVD algorithm, the learnt basis functions for SSF representation are the columns of dictionary matrix
\mathbf{Q}
, which demands a large memory to maintain
I\times Z
dictionary entries. For an unseen zero-mean SSF sample
\mathbf{X}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J^{\prime}}
, the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{V}^{*}=[\mathbf{v}_{1}^{*},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{J^{\prime}}^{*}]\in\mathbb{R}^{Z\times J^{\prime}}
has no closed-form solution. Instead, an iterative algorithm, e.g., OMP
tropp2007signal
, needs to be resorted to solve a sparse coding problem (see problem (
6
)) for estimating each column
\mathbf{v}_{j^{\prime}}^{*},\forall j^{\prime}
, which costs more computational resources than the EOF-based counterpart. Nevertheless, numerical results have demonstrated that the basis functions learnt from K-SVD algorithm can improve the reconstruction performance of SSF
bianco2017dictionary
, compared to the results using EOFs.

II.3
3D SSF: 2D Fourier Basis Functions and 1D EOFs

For a 3D SSF, as illustrated in Fig.
1
, one could first unfold it into a 2D SSF matrix and then apply the EOF-based
leblanc1980underwater
or K-SVD-based
aharon2006k
;
bianco2017dictionary
algorithm. The unfolding step, however, has broken the inherent 3D structure of SSF data, thereby leading to performance degradation.
A
classical method for 3D SSF representation relies on 2D Fourier basis functions and 1D EOFs
cornuelle1989ocean
;
morawitz1996three
. The key idea is to use EOFs to capture the variations of SSF across different depths
and
use 2D Fourier basis functions to describe the horizontal slices of SSF. Specifically,

each de-mean SSF data in
\{c(x,y,z)\}_{x=1,y=1,z=1}^{M,N,I}
is
assumed to have the following expression
cornuelle1989ocean
;
morawitz1996three
:

\displaystyle c(x,y,z)=\sum_{f_{1}=1}^{N_{F_{1}}}\sum_{f_{2}=1}^{N_{F_{2}}}\sum_{k=1}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}K_{F}}}w_{f_{1},f_{2},k}\underbrace{[\mathbf{E}_{K_{F}}]_{z,k}}_{\text{1D EOF}}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{x(f_{1}-1)}{L_{x}}\right]\right)\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{y(f_{2}-1)}{L_{y}}\right]\right)}_{\text{2D Fourier basis function}},

(7)

where
[\mathbf{E}_{K_{F}}]_{z,k}
denotes the
(z,k)
-th element of EOF matrix
\mathbf{E}_{K_{F}}
, which has
K_{F}
leading-order EOFs.

w_{f_{1},f_{2},k}
is the corresponding coefficient.
M
and
N
denote the two horizontal dimensions (i.e., length and width) of 3D SSF data.

N_{F_{1}}
and
N_{F_{2}}
denote the number of Fourier basis functions for the two horizontal axes.
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
describe the periodicity of the associated Fourier basis function.

The EOF matrix
\mathbf{E}_{K_{F}}
is obtained by firstly unfolding the 3D SSF along the vertical axis (as illustrated in Fig.
1
) and then
performing
the EVD on the resulting SSF matrix
\mathbf{X}^{\text{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times MN}
(as introduced in Section
II.1
). Note that the matrix
\mathbf{X}^{\text{u}}
describes the depth-range characteristics of 3D SSF.
The 2D Fourier basis functions can be generated according to
Eq. (
7
)
. For the brevity of notation, we define the 2D Fourier matrix
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{MN\times N_{F_{1}}N_{F_{2}}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{F}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{F}_{1},

(8)

where
\mathbf{F}_{1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{2}
are defined in
Eq. (9) and (10)
, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{2\pi j}{L_{x}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{N_{F_{1}}-1}{L_{x}}\right]\right)\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{4\pi j}{L_{x}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{2(N_{F_{1}}-1)}{L_{x}}\right]\right)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{2(M-1)\pi j}{L_{x}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{(M-1)(N_{F_{1}}-1)}{L_{x}}\right]\right)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{F_{1}}},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{2\pi j}{L_{y}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{N_{F_{2}}-1}{L_{y}}\right]\right)\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{4\pi j}{L_{y}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{2(N_{F_{2}}-1)}{L_{y}}\right]\right)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\exp\left(\frac{2(N-1)\pi j}{L_{y}}\right)&\cdots&\exp\left(2\pi j\left[\frac{(N-1)(N_{F_{2}}-1)}{L_{y}}\right]\right)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{F_{2}}}.

(10)

Each column in
\mathbf{F}
represents a 2D Fourier basis function.

Given the 1D EOFs and 2D Fourier basis functions, following
Eq. (
7
)
, any unseen 3D SSF data
\{c^{*}(x,y,z)\}_{x=1,y=1,z=1}^{M,N,I}
can be represented by 3D coefficients
\{w^{*}_{f_{1},f_{2},k}\}_{f_{1}=1,f_{2}=1,k=1}^{N_{F_{1}},N_{F_{2}},K_{F}}
. To efficiently compute these coefficients, both
the 3D SSF data
\{c^{*}(x,y,z)\}_{x=1,y=1,z=1}^{M,N,I}
and coefficients
\{w^{*}_{f_{1},f_{2},k}\}_{f_{1}=1,f_{2}=1,k=1}^{N_{F_{1}},N_{F_{2}},K_{F}}
can be unfolded into 2D matrices
\mathbf{X}^{*,\text{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times MN}
and
\mathbf{W}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{K_{F}\times N_{F_{1}}N_{F_{2}}}

,
respectively (see Fig.
1
).
The coefficients can be computed by

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{*}=\mathbf{E}_{K_{F}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{X}^{*,\text{u}}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}})^{\dagger}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}.}

(11)

Note that under this scheme, the number of coefficients required for SSF representation is
N_{F_{1}}N_{F_{2}}K_{F}
.

Remark 1:
If the considered spatial area is large and the associated sound speeds have significant variations, larger values of
N_{F_{1}}
and
N_{F_{2}}
should be chosen. Otherwise, smaller values of
N_{F_{1}}
and
N_{F_{2}}
could be selected. If history data is available, trial-and-error method is viable to select these two values
theodoridis2020machine
. In recent machine learning, Bayesian approach was leveraged to achieve automatic model order selection
theodoridis2020machine
;
cheng2020learning
;
zhao2015bayesian
;
xule21
. Namely, the hyper-parameters (e.g.,
N_{F_{1}}
and
N_{F_{2}}
) might be learnt directly from training data, which is an interesting future research direction.

III
Representation Learning Via Tensors

Before moving to the exploration of more effective 3D SSF representation, we first provide some touches on the preliminaries of tensors
kolda2009tensor
, including terminologies, tensor operations, and tensor decomposition formats. Then, we interpret tensor decomposition in the context of representation learning
panagakis2021tensor
;
sidiropoulos2017tensor
, showing its paramount role in modern data science and ocean signal processing.

III.1
Scalar, Vector, Matrix, and Tensor

Figure 2:
Illustration of scalar, matrix and tensor.

In multilinear algebra, the term
order
measures the number of indices used to assess each data element (in scalar form)
kolda2009tensor
. Specifically, vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}}
is the
1
-st order tensor since its element
\mathbf{a}_{j_{1}}
can be assessed via only one index. Matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times J_{2}}
is the
2
-nd order tensor, because two indices are enough to traverse all of its elements
\mathbf{A}_{j_{1},j_{2}}
. As a generalization, tensors are of order three or higher. A
P
-th order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
utilizes
P
indices to address its elements
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{P}}
. For illustration, we depict the scalar, vector, matrix and tensor in Fig.
2
.

For a
P
-th order tensor
\mathcal{A}
, each index corresponds to a
mode
kolda2009tensor
, which is used to generalize the concepts of rows and columns of matrices to tensors. For example, for a third order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times J_{2}\times J_{3}}
, given indices
j_{2}
and
j_{3}
, the vectors
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}_{:,j_{2},j_{3}}
are termed as
mode-1 fibers
.

Remark 2:
The 3D SSF data
\{c(x,y,z)\}_{x=1,y=1,z=1}^{M,N,I}
can be naturally represented by a third-order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}
, with each element
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{x,y,z}
being
c(x,y,z)
.

III.2
Tensor Unfolding

Tensor unfolding aims to re-organize the fibers in one mode into a matrix. For a
P
-th order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
, since it has
P
modes, there are
P
types of unfolding, each termed as
mode-
p
unfolding
.
It is formally defined as follows
kolda2009tensor
and illustrated
in Fig.
3
.

Figure 3:
Illustration of tensor unfolding.

Definition 1 (Mode-
p
Unfolding)
Given a tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
, its mode-
p
unfolding gives a matrix
\mathbf{A}_{(p)}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{p}\times\prod_{k=1,k\neq p}^{P}J_{k}}
. Each tensor element
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{P}}
is mapped to the matrix element
\left[\mathbf{A}_{(p)}\right]_{j_{p},q}
, where
q=1+\sum_{k=1,k\neq p}^{P}(j_{k}-1)I_{k}
with
I_{k}=\prod_{m=1,m\neq p}^{k-1}J_{m}
.

Tensor unfolding is one of the most important operations in tensor-based machine learning and signal processing
panagakis2021tensor
;
sidiropoulos2017tensor
, since it gives a “matrix” view to describe a tensor data, such that fruitful results in linear algebra can be leveraged.
Typically
, tensor-based algorithms were mostly developed upon the matrices provided by unfolding operations.

Then, the
p
-mode product between a tensor and a matrix
is introduced
as follows
kolda2009tensor
.

Definition 2 (
p
-mode Product)
The
p
-mode product between a tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
and a matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{C}^{R\times J_{p}}
results in a tensor
(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\times_{p}\mathbf{M})

\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{p-1}\times R\times J_{p+1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
, with each element being

\displaystyle(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\times_{p}\mathbf{M})_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{p-1},r,j_{p+1},\cdots,j_{P}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{p}=1}^{J_{P}}m_{r,j_{p}}\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{P}}.

(12)

Remark 3:
The unfolding rule introduced in Fig.
1
is essentially the mode-
3
unfolding of a 3D tensor. That is, the unfolding matrix
\mathbf{X}^{\text{u}}
in Section
II.3
corresponds to
\mathbf{X}_{(3)}
introduced in Definition 1.

III.3
Tensor Decomposition for Representation Learning

To extract low-dimensional yet informative parameters (in terms of smaller tensors, matrices and vectors) from multi-dimensional data, tensor decomposition, which generalizes matrix decomposition to tackle higher-order tensors, has come up as the major tool in recent machine learning and signal processing studies
panagakis2021tensor
;
sidiropoulos2017tensor
.
The extracted parameters are expected to preserve the structures endowed by physical sciences and have clear interpretations.
To achieve this goal, various tensor decomposition formats
kolda2009tensor
have been proposed, in which canonical polyadic decomposition (CPD) and Tucker decomposition are the most well-known and widely adopted.

In this paper, we focus on tensor Tucker decomposition,
which
includes CPD as a special case.
The definition of Tucker decomposition
is given as follows
kolda2009tensor
.

Definition 3 (Tucker Decomposition)

For a
P
-th order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{P}}
, tensor Tucker decomposition is defined as

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{G}$}\times_{1}\mathbf{U}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{U}^{(2)}\times_{3}\cdots\times_{P}\mathbf{U}^{(P)},

(13)

where each factor matrix
\mathbf{U}^{(p)}\in\mathbb{C}^{J_{p}\times R_{p}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\forall p=1,2,\cdots,P}
, and is usually orthonormal. The core tensor is
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{G}$}\in\mathbb{C}^{R_{1}\times R_{2}\times\cdots\times R_{P}}
. The tuple
(R_{1},\cdots,R_{P})
is known as the multi-linear rank.

Note that the definition above utilizes the
p
-mode product (see Definition 2). The illustration of tensor Tucker decomposition is provided in Fig.
4
. Usually, we have
R_{p}\ll J_{p},\forall p
. Note that when the core tensor
\mathcal{G}
is super-diagonal and
R_{1}=\cdots=R_{P}
, Tucker decomposition reduces to CPD
kolda2009tensor
.

More insights can be drawn after interpreting Tucker decomposition (
13
) in the context of representation learning. In particular, the factor matrices
\{\mathbf{U}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{P}
can be treated as the dictionary matrices, thereby providing a common set of basis functions for data representation. On the other hand, the core tensor
\mathcal{G}
, as seen in (
13
), acts as the weighting coefficients that encode the information of
tensor data. In other words, relying on Tucker decomposition, the essence of tensor-based representation learning is to acquire the factor matrices
\{\mathbf{U}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{P}
(i.e., basis functions) from training data
\mathcal{A}
, based on which any unseen/test data
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{A}$}^{*}
can be represented by the core tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{G}$}^{*}
. We make this interpretation concretely using the 3D SSF data in the next section.

IV
Tensor-based Basis Function Learning

In this section, 3D SSF representation
is re-visited
under the lens of tensor decomposition. In contrast to Section
II.3
that designs the basis functions in an empirical manner, we view the 3D SSF data as a third-order tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}
(see Remark 2), and propose to learn the basis functions via a data-driven approach. Then, theoretical insights are given that interpret the classical basis functions (using EOFs and/or Fourier basis functions)
leblanc1980underwater
;
cornuelle1989ocean
;
morawitz1996three
as the special cases of the proposed tensor-based learning framework.
Finally, if multiple 3D SSFs
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}\}_{t=1}^{T}
(e.g., from different seasons) are available as training data, we extend the proposed tensor-based basis function learning to jointly process these 3D SSFs.

IV.1
Tensor-based Basis Function Learning Framework

In this subsection, a tensor-based basis function learning framework relying on Tucker decomposition is introduced, under which the higher-order orthogonal iteration (HOOI) algorithm is presented to learn the basis functions from one 3D SSF
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}
. As introduced in Section
III.3
, basis functions are provided by the three factor matrices in Eq. (
13
). In the context of representing 3D SSF
\mathcal{X}
, they are denoted by
\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}}
,
\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}}
and
\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}}
, respectively. The core tensor, which contains the coefficients for SSF representation, is denoted by
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}}
. Consequently, we propose the tensor-based basis function learning framework as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$},\mathbf{B}^{(1)},\mathbf{B}^{(2)},\mathbf{B}^{(3)}}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\times_{1}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\times_{3}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}},

\displaystyle~~~~~f(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$})\geq\mathbf{0},~\bar{f}(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$})=\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}},~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}},~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}},

\displaystyle~~~~~g_{p}(\mathbf{B}^{(p)})\geq\mathbf{0},~\bar{g}_{p}(\mathbf{B}^{(p)})=\mathbf{0},~p=1,2,3,

(14)

where
f(\cdot)
and
g_{p}(\cdot)
denote the inequality constraints of the argument; and
\bar{f}(\cdot)
and
\bar{g}_{p}(\cdot)
represent the equality constraints of the argument. We can devise these constraints by incorporating the prior knowledge of SSF, or for the saving of computational resources.
For example, practitioners can devise these constraint functions to embed the structures (e.g., non-negativeness, orthogonality, smoothness) into the basis function learning
sidiropoulos2017tensor
;
panagakis2021tensor
;
cheng2020learning
.

Algorithm 1: (The HOOI Algorithm)

Input:

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}
, multi-linear rank
(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
.

Initialize:

\mathbf{B}^{(1),0},\mathbf{B}^{(2),0},\mathbf{B}^{(3),0}
.

For

t=1,2,3,\cdots

\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(1)}(\mathbf{B}^{(3),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(2),t-1})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(1),t}\leftarrow

L_{1}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}
,

\mathbf{C}_{(2)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(2)}(\mathbf{B}^{(3),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(1),t})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(2),t}\leftarrow

L_{2}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(2)}^{t}
,

\mathbf{C}_{(3)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(3)}(\mathbf{B}^{(2),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(1),t})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}\leftarrow

L_{3}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(3)}^{t}
,

Until Convergence

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}^{t}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\times_{1}[\mathbf{B}^{(1),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{2}[\mathbf{B}^{(2),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{3}[\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}
.

Return

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}^{t},\mathbf{B}^{(1),t},\mathbf{B}^{(2),t},\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}.

To reduce the number of matrix inversion, which is computationally demanding, orthonormal constraints are usually imposed on the factor matrices.
More specifically,
\bar{g}_{p}(\mathbf{B}^{(p)})=\mathbf{0}
is set to be:
\left[\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\right]^{T}\mathbf{B}^{(p)}-\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{0}
, i.e.,
\left[\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\right]^{T}\mathbf{B}^{(p)}=\mathbf{I}
, where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix with matching dimensions. Besides this, other constraints are not devised. Then the problem in Eq. (
14
) reduces to the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$},\mathbf{B}^{(1)},\mathbf{B}^{(2)},\mathbf{B}^{(3)}}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\times_{1}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\times_{3}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{3}}.

(15)

If factor matrices
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{3}
are given, the core tensor
\mathcal{S}
can be solved to be
kolda2009tensor

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\times_{1}[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{2}[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{3}[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}.

(16)

After substituting
Eq. (
16
)
into
Eq. (
15
)
, expanding the Frobenius norm and utilizing the orthonormal property of factor matrices, the problem in
Eq. (
15
)
is equivalent to the following problem:

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{3}}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\times_{1}[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{2}[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{3}[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{3}}.

(17)

Problem (
17
) can be solved via alternating least-squares (ALS) method. In the
t
-th iteration, after fixing factor matrices
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p),t-1}\}_{p=2}^{3}
, problem (
17
) reduces to

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}}}\left\|\left[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\right]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{1}},

(18)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(1)}(\mathbf{B}^{(3),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(2),t-1}).

(19)

Note that
\mathbf{X}_{(1)}
is the mode-
1
unfolding matrix of tensor data
\mathcal{X}
(see Definition 1).
The solution to the problem in Eq. (
18
)
can be acquired via the singular value decomposition (SVD) of matrix
\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}
, giving the following update step:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{(1),t}=\left[\mathbf{u}^{t}_{1},\mathbf{u}^{t}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{u}^{t}_{L_{1}}\right],

(20)

where
\{\mathbf{u}^{t}_{l}\}_{l=1}^{L_{1}}
are
L_{1}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}
. Similar update steps can be derived for other two factor matrices
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p),t}\}_{p=2}^{3}
. Using these results, the algorithm that solves
the problem in Eq. (
15
)
is summarized in Algorithm 1, which is known as
higher-order orthogonal iteration
(HOOI) algorithm
kroonenberg1980principal
.
The HOOI algorithm was proved to be convergence guaranteed
kroonenberg1980principal
.

Using the HOOI algorithm, basis functions (i.e.,
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{3}
) can be learnt from training SSF data
\mathcal{X}
. For representing any unseen/test 3D SSF data
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}^{*}
, the core tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}^{*}
, which has
L_{1}L_{2}L_{3}
parameters, can be learnt via
Eq. (
16
)
.

The classical basis functions for 3D SSF, expressed by the EOF matrix

and the two Fourier matrices
\mathbf{F}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{F_{1}}},\mathbf{F}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{F_{2}}}
, are the optimal solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$},\mathbf{B}^{(1)},\mathbf{B}^{(2)},\mathbf{B}^{(3)}}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}-\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}\times_{1}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\times_{3}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{F}_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{F_{1}}},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}=\mathbf{F}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{F_{2}}},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times K_{F}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}=\mathbf{I}_{K_{F}},

(22)

which is a special case of the proposed tensor-based basis function learning framework (

).

.

Proposition 1 and Proposition 2 point out that the classical basis functions
leblanc1980underwater
;
cornuelle1989ocean
;
morawitz1996three
and the one learnt using the HOOI algorithm are all special cases of the proposed tensor-based basis function learning framework (
14
). Through this unified perspective, the differences of these three types of basis functions are evident, as shown in Table
1
. Particularly, in problem (
21
),
the two factor matrices
are restricted to be the identity matrices, which are too rigid to allow effective SSF representation. On the other hand, in problem (
22
), the two factor matrices are designed to the Fourier basis matrices, thus with a higher representation capability than the identity matrices. The identity matrices and Fourier basis matrices are manually designed. On the contrary, in problem (
15
), three factor matrices are all learnt from the data. Therefore, problem (
15
) and the associated HOOI algorithm endue the basis functions (expressed by the learnt factor matrices) a higher flexibility, making them a promising candidate for more effective 3D SSF representation, as corroborated in Section
V
.

IV.3
Learning Basis Functions from Multiple 3D SSFs

In Section
IV.1
and
IV.2
, tensor-based basis function learning using one 3D SSF is introduced. Due to the relatively high correlations of 3D SSFs in several tens of days (e.g., one month)
munk2009ocean
;
zhu2020
, the learnt basis functions are capable of reduced-order yet accurate 3D SSF representation in such a period. Therefore, using only one 3D SSF
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}
, the proposed approach is useful for underwater applications that require short-term SSF forecasting/inversion, e.g., geoacoustic inversion
jiang2008short
, shallow water sound speed profile inversion
zhang2015inversion
, internal wave reconstruction and acoustic propagation calculation
casagrande2011novel
.

On the other hand, if multiple 3D SSFs
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}\}_{t=1}^{T}
(e.g., from different seasons) are available, the joint learning of basis functions has potential to realize long-term effective representation
lu2004spatial
;
long2021variations
, since more SSF variations (e.g., from different seasons) are taken into account. To achieve this goal, we extend the tensor-based basis function learning problem in Eq. (
15
) from dealing with one 3D SSF to processing multiple 3D SSFs:

\displaystyle\min_{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}_{t},\mathbf{B}^{(1)},\mathbf{B}^{(2)},\mathbf{B}^{(3)}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}_{t}\times_{1}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\times_{3}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{3}}.

(23)

Note that factor matrices
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{3}
contain common basis functions for multiple 3D SSFs
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
, and the core tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}_{t}
is associated with the 3D SSF
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}
, for
t=1,\cdots,T
.

Using tensor algebra
kolda2009tensor
, it can be shown that the problem in Eq. (
23
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}},\mathbf{B}^{(1)},\mathbf{B}^{(2)},\mathbf{B}^{(3)}}\left\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}}-\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}}\times_{1}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\times_{2}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\times_{3}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\times_{4}\mathbf{I}_{T}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}\times T},

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times L_{1}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(1)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(1)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times L_{2}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(2)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(2)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~\mathbf{B}^{(3)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times L_{3}},~[\mathbf{B}^{(3)}]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}^{(3)}=\mathbf{I}_{L_{3}},

(24)

where
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I\times T}
and
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}\times T}
are obtained by stacking
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I}\}_{t=1}^{T}
and
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_{1}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}}\}_{t=1}^{T}
along their fourth modes, respectively.

According to Eq. (
13
) in Definition 1, the problem in Eq. (
24
) is a variant of Tucker decomposition problem with one factor matrix being an identity matrix
\mathbf{I}_{T}
. Therefore, a modified HOOI algorithm (labeled as M-HOOI)
kolda2009tensor
, which is summarized in Algorithm 2, can be applied to solve the problem in Eq. (
24
). The derivations of M-HOOI algorithm are similar to those presented in Section
IV.1
.

Using M-HOOI algorithm, basis functions (i.e.,
\{\mathbf{B}^{(p)}\}_{p=1}^{3}
) can be jointly learnt from multiple 3D SSFs
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
. For representing an unseen 3D SSF
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}^{*}
, the coefficients in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}^{*}
can be acquired via Eq. (
16
).

Algorithm 2: (The M-HOOI Algorithm)

Input:

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times I\times T}
, multi-linear rank
(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
.

Initialize:

\mathbf{B}^{(1),0},\mathbf{B}^{(2),0},\mathbf{B}^{(3),0}
.

For

t=1,2,3,\cdots

\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(1)}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(3),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(2),t-1})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(1),t}\leftarrow

L_{1}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(1)}^{t}
,

\mathbf{C}_{(2)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(2)}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(3),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(1),t})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(2),t}\leftarrow

L_{2}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(2)}^{t}
,

\mathbf{C}_{(3)}^{t}=\mathbf{X}_{(3)}(\mathbf{I}_{T}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(2),t-1}\otimes\mathbf{B}^{(1),t})
,

\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}\leftarrow

L_{3}
leading left singular vectors of
\mathbf{C}_{(3)}^{t}
,

Until Convergence

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}}^{t}=\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{X}$}}\times_{1}[\mathbf{B}^{(1),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{2}[\mathbf{B}^{(2),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{3}[\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}]^{\mathrm{T}}\times_{4}\mathbf{I}_{T}
.

Return

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{S}$}}^{t},\mathbf{B}^{(1),t},\mathbf{B}^{(2),t},\mathbf{B}^{(3),t}.

Remark 4:
For the basis functions learnt via data-driven approaches, including EOF, K-SVD, and the tensor-based methods, their performance will degrade when the test data become less correlated with training data. Consequently, the update of basis functions using new training data is required to maintain the good performance of basis functions. But fortunately, the processes driving ocean SSFs are inherently continuous in space and time, the update (or the re-training) of basis functions need not to be very frequent in most cases. There is a trade-off between the sustainability of learnt basis functions and the cost of training data over a span of time. If multiple 3D SSFs across a long span of time (e.g., different seasons) are used as the training data, the learnt basis functions are more likely to realize long-term effective SSF representation, as will be demonstrated in the next section.

Remark 5:
The tensor-based basis function learning leverages low-rank tensor decomposition models to exploit multi-dimensional correlations inside 3D SSFs. It has advantages over classical matrix-based methods when the considered 3D SSFs can be more accurately represented by low-rank tensor models. Under this perspective, the requirements of 3D SSFs are introduced in Appendix
D
.

VI
Conclusions and Future Directions

In this paper, by treating the 3D SSF data as a third-order tensor, a tensor-based basis function learning framework was introduced. Under this framework, the classical basis functions using EOFs and Fourier basis functions can be treated as the special cases. Relying on the Tucker tensor decomposition format, the HOOI algorithm and M-HOOI algorithm were introduced to learn the effective basis functions from one 3D SSF and multiple 3D SSFs in a data-driven fashion. Numerical results using SCS 3D SSF data have showcased the excellent performance of tensor-based basis functions in terms of both reconstruction accuracy and running time.

The HOOI and M-HOOI algorithms exemplify the use of tensor tools (e.g., the proposed tensor-based basis function learning framework) in multi-dimensional ocean signal processing. In future research, it is possible to obtain better basis functions by imposing informative equality/inequality constraints that incorporate more information of 3D SSF, e.g., the fact that sound speeds vary much more significantly in the shallow ocean than those in the deep ocean.
The integration
of
physical science
and
data science
will bring us closer towards the
Universal Representation (UR)
of ocean signals.

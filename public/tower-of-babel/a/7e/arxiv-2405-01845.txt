The Hurwitz tree obstruction for the refined local lifting problem
Huy Dang

Abstract
. We prove that the differential Hurwitz tree obstruction for the cyclic p p -power refined local lifting problem always vanishes. Given a cyclic extension of degree p n p^{n} in characteristic p p and a lift of its degree- p n − 1 p^{n-1} subextension, we construct a compatible differential Hurwitz tree extending the tree of that lift. Realizing the resulting tree by a cover remains a separate problem.

1.
Introduction

The lifting problem asks whether a curve defined over a field
k
with a faithful group action can be lifted to characteristic zero
[
Obu12
]
. Grothendieck showed that curves with a trivial action can be lifted to the ring of Witt vectors
W(k)

[
Gro63
, III]
. To understand the liftability of curves with a nontrivial action, one can reduce the problem to understanding Galois extensions of the ring of power series via a local-to-global principle
[
BM00
]
.

It was conjectured by Oort
[
Oor87
]
that all cyclic extensions lift to characteristic
0
. The first result was established by Oort, Sekiguchi, and Suwa, who demonstrated that the conjecture holds for all
\mathbb{Z}/pm
-covers, where
(m,p)=1

[
SOS89
]
. Subsequently, Green and Matignon proved the case when
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}m

[
GM98
]
. Obus–Wewers and Pop resolved the conjecture for cyclic groups in 2014
[
OW14
,
Pop14
]
.

Saïdi’s
refined lifting conjecture

[
Saï12
, Conj-O-Rev]
predicts the liftability of cyclic coverings of curves in towers. An affirmative answer to the following local question implies this conjecture.

Question 1.1
.

Let
k[[z]]/k[[x]]
be a

-Galois extension where

is cyclic and

is a quotient of

. Suppose we are given a discrete valuation ring

Remark 1.2
.

The rigid generic fiber of the formal scheme
\spf R^{\prime}[[X]]
is an open unit disc
D
in the rigid analytic projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1,{\rm an}}_{\Frac(R^{\prime})}
. This allows us to study the degeneration of covers of
D
using non-archimedean analysis.

A
Hurwitz tree
records the degeneration of a cover of a rigid disc: its underlying tree comes from a semistable model, and its vertices carry depth and differential Swan conductors. Henrio introduced this technique for the order-
p
lifting problem
[
Hen00
]
; subsequent developments include
[
BW06
,
BW09
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

In equal characteristic, Hurwitz trees yield the refined lifting result
[
Dan26
, Theorem 1.2]
and describe the geometry of the
p
-fiber of moduli spaces of cyclic torsors
[
DH24
]
.

In this paper, we define the
differential Hurwitz tree obstruction
for the refined local lifting problem and prove that it always vanishes for cyclic
p
-power covers.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. In the setting of Question
2.3
, let
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
be the differential Hurwitz tree of the prescribed lift
\Phi_{n-1}
. There exists a compatible étale
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
differential Hurwitz tree
\mathcal{T}_{n}
extending
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
whose root reduction type represents the prescribed local extension
k[[z_{n}]]/k[[x]]
. In particular, the differential Hurwitz tree obstruction for the cyclic
p
-power refined local lifting problem always vanishes.

Realizing this abstract differential tree by a cover remains the next step in Question
1.1
.

2.
Degeneration of cyclic characters

2.1.
Cyclic covers of the projective line

Recall that every
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
-cover
\phi_{n}
of the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}=\Proj k[x,w]
can be represented by the class of a length-
n
Witt vector over
k(x)

\underline{g}=(g^{1},\ldots,g^{n})\in W_{n}(k(x))/\wp(W_{n}(k(x)))\cong\cohom^{1}(k(x),\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}).

Here
W_{n}(k(x))
is the ring of length-
n
Witt vectors, and
\wp=F-\mathrm{id}
is the Artin–Schreier–Witt operator, with
F
the Witt Frobenius. For characters, the power
\chi^{p^{a}}
denotes the multiple
p^{a}\chi
in the additive cohomology group. If
\chi
has order
p^{n}
and
0\leq a<n
, we regard the resulting quotient character as taking values in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n-a}
.
We identify
\phi_{n}
with a character in
\cohom^{1}(k(x),\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
. We say
\underline{g}
is
reduced
if, in the partial fraction decomposition of each entry, every nonzero pole order is prime to
p
, every nonconstant monomial in the polynomial part has degree prime to
p
, and the constant term is zero. Since
k
is algebraically closed, every class in
W_{n}(k(x))/\wp(W_{n}(k(x)))
has a unique representative in this reduced form. Moreover, for
1\leq i<n
, the character
\phi_{i}:=(\phi_{n})^{p^{n-i}}
corresponds to the unique
\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
-subcover
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
of
\phi_{n}
represented by
(g^{1},\ldots,g^{i})
.

Remark 2.1
.

Write
\mathbb{B}(\phi_{n})
for the branch locus of
\phi_{n}
; we use the same notation for the branch locus of the cover associated with a character. Let
Q_{j}
lie above
P_{j}\in\mathbb{B}(\phi_{n})
, and suppose its inertia group is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}
, with
m\leq n
. Since
k
is algebraically closed, the induced extension of completed local rings
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{n},Q_{j}}/\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},P_{j}}
is
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}
-Galois. We say the
i
-th ramification break
of
\phi_{n}
at
P_{j}
, denoted by
m_{j,i}
, is
-1
for
i\leq n-m
. When
i>n-m
, the
i
-th ramification break of
\phi_{n}
at
P_{j}
is the
(i-n+m)
-th one of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{n},Q_{j}}/\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},P_{j}}
. We call
(m_{j,n-m+1},\ldots,m_{j,n})
the sequence of
upper ramification breaks
of
\phi_{n}
at
P_{j}
. The following result computes these invariants from the reduced representation.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Gar02
, Theorem 1]
)
.

With the notation above, suppose that
\underline{g}
is reduced. Then we have

(1)

m_{j,i}=\max\{p^{i-l}\deg_{(x-P_{j})^{-1}}(g^{l})\mid l=1,\ldots,i\},

for
i>n-m
. Here
\deg_{(x-P_{j})^{-1}}(g^{l})
denotes the pole order of
g^{l}
at
P_{j}
, taken to be
0
if
g^{l}
is regular there. At
P_{j}=\infty
, use the local parameter

instead of

.

Semi-stable models and a partition of a disc

2.4.1.
A coordinate system for a marked disc

A node (an ordinary double point)

on the branch beyond

\{P\in D\mid r_{-}(e)<\nu(X(P)-x_{j,K})<r_{+}(e)\}.

We represent its radial interval by

2.5.1.
Variation of the depth on a disc

Fix a closed disc

Remark 2.5

Suppose

Proposition 2.6

(2)

For an admissible character on a disc

bound the slope in each finite residue direction from above by the number of branch points in that residue class minus one. If the character has good reduction, this bound is attained.

Proposition 2.7

((cf.
[
OW14
, Corollary 5.15]
))
.

Let

be an admissible character of order

, and let

and

of the canonical reduction of

\ord_{\overline{w}}(\omega_{\chi}(r,z))\geq-|\mathbb{B}(\chi)\cap U(\mathcal{D}[r,z],\overline{w})|.

Furthermore, equality holds if

Corollary 2.8
.

Suppose

has good reduction, and let

be a proper subdisc such that the restricted character

\delta(\chi|_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})>0

\omega(\chi|_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})=\frac{c\,dx}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}(x-a_{i})^{m_{i}}},

where

\delta_{\chi}(r)=\delta_{\chi}(r_{2})-(|\mathbb{B}(\chi)\cap\mathcal{D}[r_{2}]|-1)(r_{2}-r).

Proof.

The claim that

2.5.2.
Conditions on the refined Swan conductors of cyclic covers

Fix the Gauss valuation of a closed disc. Let
\chi_{n}\in\cohom^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
have order

, with

, and suppose that the residue extension at this valuation is purely inseparable of degree

. For

The tuple

is called the

associated to
\chi_{n}
. For

(\delta_{j},\omega_{j})
is called
\chi_{n}
’s

.

The next theorem describes the possible depth and differential conductors of
\chi_{n}
when those of

are prescribed.

We write
\mathcal{C}
for the
Cartier operator
on rational differentials over

. In a local parameter

\mathcal{C}\!\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{j}u^{j}\,du\right)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{pq+p-1}^{1/p}u^{q}\,du.

The

th roots of the coefficients are unique because

is perfect. A rational differential is called

if it has the form
dg
for
g\in k(x)
, and

if it has the form

for

.

Theorem 2.9

((cf. Theorem 4.3
[
Wew14
]
))
.

Let
\chi\in\cohom^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
be a cyclic character of order

, whose associated residue extension is purely inseparable of degree

. Let

denote the ramification data associated with

. Then the following holds.

(1)

. Moreover, we have

(a)

\delta_{1}=\frac{p}{p-1}\iff\mathcal{C}(\omega_{1})=\omega_{1},

(b)

\delta_{1}<\frac{p}{p-1}\iff\mathcal{C}(\omega_{1})=0
.

(2)

Suppose

(3)

Suppose

then

Moreover, we have

(a)

(b)

3.
Hurwitz tree

Let
R
be a complete discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with fraction field
K
and algebraically closed residue field
k
. We associate Hurwitz trees to cyclic covers of
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
that are étale outside an open disc
D\subset C^{\an}
, using semistable
R
-models of
C
. These trees give an obstruction to refined local lifting, analogous to the lifting obstruction in
[
BW09
]
.

3.1.
Orientation on a rooted tree

A
rooted tree

T=(V,E)
has a distinguished vertex
v_{0}
, called the
root
, incident to a unique edge
e_{0}
, called the
trunk
. Orient every edge away from the root, and write
s(e)
and
t(e)
for its source and target. If
v=s(e)
and
v^{\prime}=t(e)
, we call
v^{\prime}
a
successor
of
v
and write
v\to v^{\prime}
. We write
v\leq v^{\prime}
if there is a directed path from
v
to
v^{\prime}
, and
v\prec v^{\prime}
if the path has positive length. The target
v_{1}=t(e_{0})
is called the
first vertex
.

The root
v_{0}
is the unique minimal vertex. A maximal component vertex is called
terminal
. We keep component vertices separate from marked leaves:
B
is a finite nonempty set indexing marked points, represented by legs attached to component vertices. These legs are not metric edges in
E
. If
v(b)
is the component vertex carrying
b\in B
, put

B_{v}:=\{b\in B\mid v\leq v(b)\}.

Thus
B_{v}
consists of the marked leaves on or beyond
v
.

3.2.
Hurwitz tree and the deformation problem

Definition 3.1
.

A

•

\overline{C}
over

•

We allow subdivisions by unmarked rational components with two special points (nodes or

). We require

to be stably marked by
((x_{b}),x_{0})
after these subdivision components are contracted. Their vertices are
inessential

The

of a decorated tree

is obtained from its dual graph by adjoining a root
v_{0}
and a trunk

joining it to the vertex of the component containing

\overline{C}_{v}
for the component corresponding to

Since

from

by removing the nodes and marked points, and also

when

The following definition applies in both mixed and equal characteristic.

A

For each

and

of

.

•

of

These data satisfy the following conditions:

(
H
1)

Every nonroot vertex has positive depth.

(
H
2)

For each

, the differential form

does not have zeros or poles on

.

(
H
3)

For every edge

, we have the equality

(
H

For every edge

, we have

d_{\mathcal{T}}(e)=\ord_{z_{e}}\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(t(e))+1\underset{s(e)\neq v_{0}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{c3Hurwitz}}}}{{=}}}-\ord_{z_{e}}\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(s(e))-1.

.

(

For every edge

(

For

be the component containing the point

(

Remark 3.3

For each

A Hurwitz tree arising from a mixed-characteristic cover satisfies the following additional conditions.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\chi\in\cohom^{1}(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
be an admissible character of order
p^{n}
over the mixed-characteristic field

, whose degeneration data define a differential Hurwitz tree
\mathcal{T}
as in §

. Then:

(1)

If marked leaves are attached directly to
v
, and
i(v)
is their maximal index, then
\delta_{\mathcal{T}}(v)=p/(p-1)+i(v)-1
.

(2)

If
v
is terminal, then
\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(v)
is logarithmic.

(3)

Every marked leaf has conductor

.

Proof.

The depth formula is
[
BW09
, Lemma 3.10(iii)]
. All leaves attached directly to a fixed vertex have the same index: otherwise, after passing down the quotient tower until the smallest index is
1
, the larger index forces the lower depth to be above
1/(p-1)
. Theorem
2.9
would then identify the upper differential with the negative of the lower one, although the upper differential has a pole in the index-
1
direction and the lower differential is regular there.

At a terminal vertex whose attached leaves have index
1
, the local monodromy is of order
p
and the depth is
p/(p-1)
, so the differential is logarithmic. At a terminal vertex whose attached leaves have higher index, quotienting lowers every leaf index by one, and Theorem
2.9
gives
\omega=-\omega_{\rm lower}
. Induction proves
(2)
.

For
(3)
, first take a leaf of index
1
. Here the lower depth and differential refer to the immediately preceding level of the quotient tower. If its vertex is terminal, or the lower depth is strictly below

, the upper differential is logarithmic and the pole is simple.

In the remaining case the lower differential
\omega^{\prime}
is Cartier-nilpotent and the critical relation is
\mathcal{C}(\omega)=\omega+\omega^{\prime}
. Choose
s

\mathcal{C}^{s}(\omega^{\prime})=0
and put
\eta=-\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{C}^{j}(\omega^{\prime})
. Then
\mathcal{C}(\omega-\eta)=\omega-\eta
. By the characterization of Cartier-fixed differentials as logarithmic,
\omega-\eta=dg/g
for some

. The lower character is unramified in the direction corresponding to this index-
1

\omega^{\prime}
is regular there. Cartier preserves regularity, so
\eta
is also regular there; the pole comes from

and is simple.

For a leaf of higher index, quotienting and the identity
\omega=-\omega_{\rm lower}
reduce to the preceding case.
∎

\Phi_{n-1}

\mathcal{T}_{n}
is étale with the prescribed reduction type
(g^{1},\ldots,g^{n})
, then
\Phi_{n}
solves the refined local lifting problem.

Proof.

The root depth is zero and the prescribed root type identifies the residual character with
(g^{1},\ldots,g^{n})
. Let
u_{n}
be its final upper jump. By Proposition
3.8
(3)
, all marked leaves have conductor
1
. Summing
(H7)
gives

|\mathbb{B}(\Phi_{n})|=\sum_{b\in B}h_{\mathcal{T}_{n}}(b)=u_{n}+1.

The good-reduction criterion in Proposition
2.6
(1)
therefore shows that
\Phi_{n}
has good reduction. Its reduction is the prescribed one-point cover, and its quotient is the given
\Phi_{n-1}
.
∎

3.4.
The compatibility of the differential conductors

Fix residue coordinates on the nonroot components with the incoming point at infinity, and retain the prescribed root coordinate. For trees arising from covers, use the normalization fixed in §
3.3
. All coefficient comparisons use these choices.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a differential Hurwitz tree. At a nonroot component

, put
S_{v}:=\overline{C}_{v}\setminus(\overline{U}_{v}\cup\{\infty\})

-coefficient, is

.

At a depth-zero root, use the coefficient rule of Theorem
3.13
. These conventions apply to abstract trees as well as to trees arising from covers.

Definition 3.12
.

(3)

(4)

\mathcal{T}_{n}

5.
Proof of the vanishing theorem

We first construct differentials satisfying the Cartier equation at a critical vertex. We then use these differentials to extend the lower tree, one branch at a time. Throughout this section,
k
is algebraically closed of characteristic
p
.

5.1.
Two Cartier constructions

Write
\omega=c\,dx/D
, where
c\in k^{\times}
and
D
is monic of degree
m+1
, with
m\geq 1
. At a critical vertex we need a differential
\Omega
with no finite zeros and with
\mathcal{C}(\Omega)=\Omega+\omega
. The
incoming slope
of
\Omega
is
\ord_{\infty}(\Omega)+1
. A
minimal solution
has incoming slope
pm
; a
subminimal solution
has incoming slope
M<pm
.

Proposition 5.1

(Minimal solution)
.

There is a monic squarefree polynomial
Q
, relatively prime to
D
, of degree
(p-1)m
such that

\Omega=\frac{c^{p}\,dx}{DQ},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\Omega)=\Omega+\omega,\qquad\ord_{\infty}(\Omega)=pm-1.

Proof.

For a nonzero polynomial
H
, put

\Theta(H)=-H^{p}\partial^{p-1}(1/H),\qquad\partial=d/dx.

The denominator of
\partial^{p-1}(1/H)
divides
H^{p}
, so
\Theta(H)
is a polynomial. Since
\partial^{p}=0
, its derivative is zero. It is therefore the
p
th power of a unique polynomial
\Phi(H)
.

We need two properties of
\Phi
. First, write
H=\sum_{j=0}^{t}h_{j}x^{j}
. Each coefficient of
\Theta(H)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
p-1
in
h_{0},\ldots,h_{t}
, with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Put
b_{j}=h_{j}^{1/p}
. Taking
p
th roots in
\Theta(H)=\Phi(H)^{p}
shows that each coefficient of
\Phi(H)
is a polynomial of total degree at most
p-1
in
b_{0},\ldots,b_{t}
.

Second, if
H
is monic of degree
pm+1
, expansion at infinity and
(p-1)!=-1
give

\Theta(H)=x^{p(p-1)m}+\text{lower-degree terms}.

Thus
\Phi(H)
is monic of degree
(p-1)m
.

Set
N=(p-1)m
and write
Q=x^{N}+\sum_{j<N}u_{j}^{p}x^{j}
. To obtain the required Cartier relation, we seek
Q
satisfying

(7)

\Phi(DQ)=Q+c^{p-1}

This is a system
u_{j}^{p}-R_{j}(u)=\beta_{j}
for
0\leq j<N
, where
R_{j}(u)
is the coefficient of
x^{j}
in
\Phi(DQ)
,
\beta_{0}=-c^{p-1}
, and
\beta_{j}=0
for
j>0
. Since
D
is fixed, the
p
th roots of the coefficients of
DQ
are affine-linear in the
u_{j}
, so the coefficient bound above gives
\deg R_{j}\leq p-1
.

To solve it, consider the polynomial map with coordinates
u_{j}^{p}-R_{j}(u)
. Its homogenization defines a morphism
\mathbb{P}^{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
: at infinity the coordinates are
u_{j}^{p}
, so they do not vanish simultaneously. The pullback of
\mathcal{O}(1)
is
\mathcal{O}(p)
, which makes the morphism finite and surjective. Points at infinity map to infinity, so an affine point has an affine preimage. Thus a monic
Q
satisfying (
7
) exists.

With
H=DQ
, this equation gives

c^{p}\Theta(H)=(c^{p}+cQ)^{p}.

The identity
\mathcal{C}(f\,dx)=g\,dx\iff\partial^{p-1}f=-g^{p}
now yields
\mathcal{C}(\Omega)=\Omega+\omega
. Finally,

\Theta(H)\equiv(H^{\prime})^{p-1}\pmod{H}.

Indeed, in the numerator of
-H^{p}\partial^{p-1}(1/H)
, the only term not divisible by
H
is
(H^{\prime})^{p-1}
. At a root
z
of
Q
we obtain

c^{p}\bigl(D(z)Q^{\prime}(z)\bigr)^{p-1}=c^{p^{2}}\neq 0.

Thus
Q
is squarefree and has no common root with
D
. The formula for
\Omega
shows that it has no finite zeros and has order
pm-1
at infinity.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Congruent subminimal solution)
.

Suppose
\omega=c\,dx/D
is Cartier-nilpotent. There is a squarefree polynomial
Q
, relatively prime to
D
, such that

\Omega=\frac{c^{p}\,dx}{DQ},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\Omega)=\Omega+\omega,

and, with
M=m+\deg Q
,

m\leq M\leq pm-1,\qquad M+1\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

In particular,
\Omega
has no finite zeros and
\ord_{\infty}(\Omega)=M-1
. Here
Q
need not be monic.

Proof.

We first solve the Cartier equation, then add a logarithmic differential to obtain a solution with constant numerator. Choose
s
with
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\omega)=0
and set

\eta=-\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{C}^{j}(\omega)=\frac{E\,dx}{D},\qquad f=E/D,\qquad T=\partial+f.

Here
T
acts on
k(x)
by
T(F)=F^{\prime}+fF
.
Cartier introduces no new poles and does not increase their orders. Thus
\eta=E\,dx/D
with
E\in k[x]
. Since
\omega
is regular at infinity and Cartier preserves regularity, so is
\eta
; hence
\deg E\leq m-1
. The sum defining
\eta
gives
\mathcal{C}(\eta)=\eta+\omega
. Jacobson’s identity for a derivation plus multiplication is

(\partial+f)^{p}=\partial^{p}+\partial^{p-1}(f)+f^{p},

where the last two terms act by multiplication. Together with the Cartier formula, this gives

T^{p}(F)=-\frac{c^{p}}{D^{p}}F\qquad(F\in k(x)).

Let
r
be the least nonnegative residue of
m+1
modulo
p
and choose a divisor
R\mid D
of degree
r
. This choice makes
\deg(D^{p-1}R)
divisible by
p
. Put

h=D^{p-1}R,\qquad g=-T^{p-1}h.

Then

(8)

Dg^{\prime}+Eg=c^{p}R.

For
P\in k[x]
and
j\geq 1
, the formula
T(D^{j}P)=D^{j-1}(jD^{\prime}P+DP^{\prime}+EP)
shows that
g
is polynomial.

We next show that
g=RQ
for a polynomial
Q
having no common root with
D
. A simple root of
D
would give
\omega
a nonzero residue, which could not vanish under any Cartier iterate. Thus every root of
D
is multiple. At a root
a
of
D
, put
l=\ord_{a}D\geq 2
. The pole order of
\partial^{p-1}f
is at most
l+p-1<pl
. Multiplying
\partial^{p-1}f+f^{p}=-c^{p}/D^{p}
by
D^{p}
and evaluating at
a
therefore gives
E(a)^{p}=-c^{p}
, hence
E(a)=-c
.

Put
t=\ord_{a}R\geq 0
. Since
E(a)=-c\neq 0
, we have
\ord_{a}(E/D)=-l
. For
\ord_{a}F=q
, the terms
(E/D)F
and
F^{\prime}
have orders
q-l
and at least
q-1
, respectively. Since
l\geq 2
,
\ord_{a}(TF)=q-l
. Starting from
\ord_{a}h=(p-1)l+t
and applying this identity repeatedly gives

\ord_{a}(T^{j}h)=(p-1-j)l+t\qquad(0\leq j\leq p-1).

Thus
\ord_{a}g=\ord_{a}R
at every root of
D
, so
g=RQ
with
Q\in k[x]
and
(Q,D)=1
. Dividing (
8
) by
Dg
gives

\eta+dg/g=\frac{c^{p}\,dx}{DQ}=\Omega.

Since Cartier fixes the logarithmic differential
dg/g
, we have
\mathcal{C}(\Omega)=\Omega+\omega
. If
Q(z)=0
, equation (
8
) gives
D(z)Q^{\prime}(z)=c^{p}
, proving that
Q
is squarefree.

It remains to bound the degree of
Q
and prove the congruence. Put
A=\deg h=(p-1)(m+1)+r
. Since
p\mid A
and
\deg E-\deg D\leq-2
, the first application of
T
lowers degree by at least two, and each subsequent application lowers it by at least one. Hence

\deg g\leq A-p,\qquad 0\leq\deg Q\leq(p-1)m-1.

If
p\nmid\deg g
, the term
Dg^{\prime}
in (
8
) has degree
m+\deg g
, strictly greater than both
\deg(Eg)
and
\deg R
. This is impossible. Therefore
p\mid\deg g=\deg Q+r
, and
M+1=m+\deg Q+1\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
∎

5.2.
The recursive extension problem

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we extend the prescribed lower tree one vertex at a time. The upper differential chosen at a vertex prescribes the root principal parts for its outgoing branches. We therefore need an extension statement for these branch problems as well as for the original depth-zero root.

At a nonroot vertex, the differential is the sum of its finite principal parts, with no polynomial part, by Corollary
2.8
or the Hurwitz-tree condition at infinity. For an edge
e
, let
\mathcal{T}(e)
consist of
e
and all components and marked leaves at or beyond
t(e)
, rooted at
s(e)
. Retain the depths, differentials, edge lengths, and marked-leaf data, except for the root modifications below. At a positive-depth root, keep only the principal part in the
e
-direction and translate the point corresponding to this direction to
x=0
. At depth zero, retain the root Witt vector.

Give the branch root the group
H=G_{\mathcal{T}}(t(e))
. If the current ambient group is
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{\ell}
and
|H|=p^{m}
, let
q_{i}:G\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}
be the quotient map at level
i
. The image
q_{i}(H)
is trivial for
i\leq\ell-m
; the retained level
i=\ell-m+j
has image of order
p^{j}
. Relabel this retained quotient as local level
j
, for
1\leq j\leq m
. Restrict the data at each retained level to the branch.

An
inherited branch problem
asks for an extension of the lower data obtained by these restrictions and reindexings, with upper root data prescribed as in Proposition
5.4
. We allow repeated restriction and subdivision at rational interior places, provided the root depth at the highest retained lower level is less than
1/(p-1)
. Taking principal parts commutes with Cartier and retains the leading polar coefficient in the chosen coordinate.

Lemma 5.3

(Subdivision)
.

Let
e
be an edge of the prescribed lower tree or of the lower tree of an inherited branch problem, with thickness
\epsilon_{e}
, slope
d
, and coefficient
c
. At rational distance
0<\rho<\epsilon_{e}
from
s(e)
, insert an unmarked vertex with depth
\delta_{\mathcal{T}}(s(e))+d\rho
, group
G_{\mathcal{T}}(t(e))
, and differential
c\,dx/x^{d+1}
. The two segments have lengths
\rho
and
\epsilon_{e}-\rho
and slope
d
. With the endpoint data unchanged, this gives a subdivision of the original tree.

Proof.

The new differential has orders
d-1
at infinity and
-d-1
at zero. Its infinity and leading polar coefficients are both
c
, and the two depth increments sum to the original increment. At each retained quotient level, the branch points inside the disc represented by the interior place lie in one residue direction. Corollary
2.8
and Theorem
3.13
therefore give the stated monomial differential with that level’s edge coefficient. These are the degeneration data of the lower lift, so the quotient relations in Theorem
2.9
are preserved, also on inherited branches.
∎

The depth-zero case of the following proposition gives Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n-1}
be the compatible tree of the prescribed lower lift or the lower tree of an inherited branch problem, with its retained quotient tower and root depth
0\leq\alpha<1/(p-1)
at the highest lower level. Here
n-1\geq 1
is the local tower length after reindexing. If

, prescribe a reduced length-
n
root Witt vector extending the lower root type. If

, put

and prescribe a finite principal part
\Omega_{0}=\sum_{j\geq 2}a_{j}\,dx/x^{j}
satisfying

\mathcal{C}(\Omega_{0})=\omega_{n-1}(v_{0}),\qquad-\ord_{0}\Omega_{0}-1=N,

where
N=pd
, or
N>pd
and
p\nmid N
. Then there is a compatible extending tree with the prescribed root data and root depth
p\alpha
.

At depth zero, let
d
and
N
be the final upper jumps of the lower and extending root types. We call the root data
minimal
if
N=pd
, and
nonminimal
if
N>pd
. At positive depth, if
\omega_{n-1}(v_{0})=\sum_{i}b_{i}\,dx/x^{i}
, then
\sum_{i}b_{i}^{p}\,dx/x^{p(i-1)+1}
has the required Cartier image and minimal slope. For nonminimal slope
N
, add a nonzero multiple of
dx/x^{N+1}
, which is exact. The proposition allows any principal part satisfying its Cartier and pole-order conditions, including those arising in the induction.

Construction of the extending tree

For every old vertex or marked leaf with group of order

Base case:

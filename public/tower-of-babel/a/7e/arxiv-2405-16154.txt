Abstract
. Several constructions have been given for families of simple braces, but few examples are known of simple skew braces which are not braces. In this paper, we exhibit the first example of an infinite family of simple skew braces which are not braces and which do not arise from nonabelian simple groups. More precisely, we show that, for any primes p p , q q such that q q divides ( p p − 1 ) / ( p − 1 ) {(p^{p}-1)}/{(p-1)} , there are exactly two simple skew braces (up to isomorphism) of order p p ​ q p^{p}q .

1.
Introduction

Braces were introduced by Rump
[
Rum07
]
in order to study nondegenerate involutive set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. They were later generalised by Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
GV17
]
, who defined skew braces and showed that these give rise to set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation which are nondegenerate but not necessarily involutive. (Skew) braces have been investigated intensively, and connections have been found to many other topics in algebra including Hopf-Galois theory
[
SV18
]
, semigroups of
I
-type
[
GIVdB98
]
, triangular semisimple Hopf algebras
[
EG98
]
and racks
[
AG03
]
.

A (left) skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
consists of a set
B
with two binary operations such that
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,\circ)
are both groups, and a certain compatibility condition holds (see Definition
2.1
below). We will usually write
B
in place of
(B,\cdot,\circ)
. The order of
B
is the cardinality
|B|
of the underlying set. We shall refer to
(B,\cdot)
as the additive group of
B
, and to
(B,\circ)
as the multiplicative group.
The skew brace
B
is a brace if
(B,\cdot)
is abelian.

There is a natural construction of quotient (skew) braces, and one can therefore consider simple (skew) braces. In the case of
braces
, Bachiller
[
Bac18
]
showed how to obtain an infinite family of simple braces by a matched product construction. This approach has been generalised in several ways
[
BCJO18
,
BCJO19
,
CJO20
]
so that many examples of simple braces are now known. Indeed, every finite abelian group occurs as a subgroup of the additive group of a simple brace
[
CJO21
]
. For a prime
p
, the only simple brace of
p
-power order is the unique brace of order
p

[
Rum07
, Corollary to Proposition 8]
. Smoktunowicz
[
Smo18
, Theorem 5]
has given conditions on the prime factorisation of a natural number
n
which are necessary for the existence of a simple brace of order
n
. Thus there are many values of
n
for which it is known that no simple brace of order
n
exists. However, apart from these negative results, there do not seem to be any composite
n
for which a classification of all simple braces of order
n
is known.

In this paper, we will be concerned with simple
skew braces
. Of course, any simple brace is also a simple skew brace. Moreover, there are examples of simple skew braces which arise from nonabelian simple groups (see §
2.4
below). As with simple braces, there are no simple skew braces of order
p^{r}
for
p
prime and
r>1

[
CSV19
, Corollary 4.9]
. This leaves open the possibility of finite simple skew braces which are not braces, but whose additive and multiplicative groups are both soluble. Indeed, such simple skew braces do exist. Konovalov and Vendramin constructed a database of skew braces of order
n
for
n\leq 63
,
n\neq 32
,
48
,
54
, and this contains exactly two skew braces with soluble but nonabelian additive and multiplicative groups. These both have order
12

[
KSV21
, Proposition 4.4]
.

The results of this paper can be summarised as follows. For each natural number
m
, we write
C_{m}
for the cyclic group of order
m
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
and
q
be primes such that
q
divides
(p^{p}-1)/(p-1)
, and let
n=p^{p}q
. Then there are, up to isomorphism, exactly two simple skew braces
B
of order
n
. These are mutually opposite. They have
additive group

(B,\cdot)\cong V\rtimes C_{q}

where
V\cong C_{p}^{p}
is an elementary abelian group of order
p^{p}
with
C_{q}
acting nontrivially on
V
,
and they have multiplicative group

(B,\circ)\cong C_{q}\rtimes P

where
P
is a certain group of order
p^{p}
acting on
C_{q}
via an automorphism of order
p
. The group
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
of skew brace automorphisms of
B
is cyclic of order
p
.

The first few cases are
n=12
with
(p,q)=(2,3)
;
n=351
with
(p,q)=(3,13)
;
n=34\,374
with
(p,q)=(5,11)
; and
n=221\,875
with
(p,q)=(5,71)
.

Theorem
1.1
on the one hand shows the existence of an infinite family of pairs of simple skew braces, the first member of which is the pair of order
12
found by Konovalov and Vendramin, and on the other hand (and in contrast to what is known for simple braces) gives a complete classification of simple skew braces whose order
n
is of the special form considered.

The paper is organised as follows.

In §2, we recall some preliminary definitions and results. This includes the description of a skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
in terms of a regular subgroup of the holomorph
\mathrm{Hol}(N,\cdot)=N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N,\cdot)
of its additive group. It is this description which underlies the link between skew braces and Hopf-Galois theory, and it will be essential to our approach in this paper. We also explain how the opposite of a skew brace can be identified in this description (Lemma
2.6
), and we briefly review known results about simple skew braces arising from nonabelian simple groups (§
2.4
).

In §3, we construct a simple skew brace of order
n=p^{p}q
as in Theorem
1.1
by specifying the group
N
of order
n
which will be its additive group and then exhibiting a specific regular subgroup
G
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. We show in Theorem
3.13
that the skew brace
B
corresponding to this group
G
is simple. The techniques we use for calculating in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
have been developed in the context of Hopf-Galois theory, see for example
[
Byo04b
,
BC12
]
. We also give an explicit description of a regular subgroup
G^{*}
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
corresponding to the opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
.

In §4, we show (Theorem
4.4
) that the simple skew braces
B
and
B^{\mathrm{op}}
are not isomorphic, so that we have indeed found two distinct simple skew braces of order
n
.

The method of proof of Theorem
4.4
is modified in §5 to describe
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
in Theorem
5.1
.

Finally, in §6, we show in Theorem
6.1
that every simple skew brace of order
n
must be isomorphic to
B
or
B^{\mathrm{op}}
. Theorems
3.13
,
4.4
,
5.1
and
6.1
together give all the assertions of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic definitions

Definition 2.1
.

A skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
consists of a set
B
and two binary operations such that
(B,\cdot)
,
(B,\circ)
are groups and
the brace relation

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c)\mbox{ for }a,b,c\in B

holds, where
a^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
a
in
(B,\cdot)
. We will usually just write
B
instead of
(B,\cdot,\circ)
.

A brace is a skew brace
B
in which
(B,\cdot)
is abelian.

A homomorphism of skew braces
\theta:(B,\cdot,\circ)\to(B^{\prime},\cdot^{\prime},\circ^{\prime})
is a function
B\to B^{\prime}
with
\theta(a\cdot b)=\theta(a)\cdot^{\prime}\theta(b)
and
\theta(a\circ b)=\theta(a)\circ^{\prime}\theta(b)
for all
a
,
b\in B
. We write
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
for the group of skew brace automorphisms of
B
.

If
B
is a skew brace, there is a homomorphism of groups
\lambda:(B,\circ)\to\mathrm{Aut}(B,\cdot)
given by

a\mapsto\lambda_{a}\mbox{ where }\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b).

Definition 2.2
.

A left ideal in a skew brace
B
is a subgroup
I
of
(B,\cdot)
such that

\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
for all
a\in B
. It is then also a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
.
An ideal in
B
is a left ideal which is a normal subgroup in both
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,\circ)
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
I
is a characteristic subgroup in
(B,\cdot)
, it is automatically a left ideal which is normal in
(B,\cdot)
. If it is also normal in
(B,\circ)
then it is an ideal.

If
I
is an ideal in a skew brace
B
, we can form the quotient skew brace
B/I
.

Definition 2.4
.

A skew brace
B
is simple if
|B|>1
and
B
has no ideals except
\{1\}
and
B
.

2.2.
Connection with the holomorph

We define the holomorph of a group
N
as the semidirect product

\mathrm{Hol}(N)=N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N),

and we view this as a group of permutations of (the underlying set of)
N
. Thus if
(\eta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Hol}(N)
with
\eta\in N
,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
, then we have

(\eta,\alpha)\mu=\eta\alpha(\mu)\mbox{ for }\mu\in N,

so that, in particular,
(\eta,\alpha)1_{N}=\eta
, where
1_{N}
is the identity element of
N
.
Multiplication in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is given by

(\eta,\alpha)(\mu,\beta)=(\eta\alpha(\mu),\alpha\beta).

A subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is regular if, given any
\eta
,
\mu\in N
, there is a unique
g\in G
with
g\eta=\mu
.

For a skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
, the maps
\lambda_{a}
allow us to embed
(B,\circ)
into
\mathrm{Hol}(B,\cdot)
by

b\mapsto(b,\lambda_{b}).

The image of this embedding is a regular subgroup
G\cong(B,\circ)
of
\mathrm{Hol}(B,\cdot)
. Conversely, let
(N,\cdot)
be a group and let
G
be a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Then, for each
\eta\in N
, there is a unique element
g_{\eta}=(\eta,\alpha_{\eta})\in G
whose first component is
\eta
, and we have a skew brace
(N,\cdot,\circ)
where
\circ
is defined by

(2.1)

g_{\eta\circ\eta^{\prime}}=g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}.

Two regular subgroups
G
,
G^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
give isomorphic skew braces if and only if they are conjugate in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
via an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(N)

[
GV17
, Proposition 4.3]
.

2.3.
Opposite skew braces

Definition 2.5
.

[
KT20
]

If
(B,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace, then its opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
is
(B,\cdot^{\mathrm{op}},\circ)
where
a\cdot^{\mathrm{op}}b=b\cdot a
.

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B^{\mathrm{op}})=\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
N
be a finite group, and let
G=\{g_{\eta}:\eta\in N\}
be a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, where
g_{\eta}=(\eta,\alpha_{\eta})
. Define

g^{*}_{\eta}=(\eta^{-1},\mathrm{conj}(\eta)\alpha_{\eta})\in\mathrm{Hol}(N)

where
\mathrm{conj}(\eta)
is the inner automorphism of
N
given by conjugation by
\eta
. Let
G^{*}=\{g_{\eta}^{*}:\eta\in N\}
. Then
G^{*}
is a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, and the skew brace determined by
G^{*}
is the opposite skew brace to that determined by
G
. Moreover
G
and
G^{*}
are isomorphic via
g_{\eta}\mapsto g^{*}_{\eta}
.

Proof.

Since
(\eta\cdot\eta^{\prime})^{-1}=\eta^{-1}\cdot^{\mathrm{op}}\eta^{\prime-1}
, it suffices by (
2.1
) to show that if
g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=g_{\mu}
then
g^{*}_{\eta}g^{*}_{\eta^{\prime}}=g^{*}_{\mu}
. Now

g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=(\eta\alpha_{\eta}(\eta^{\prime}),\alpha_{\eta}\alpha_{\eta^{\prime}}),

so

\mu=\eta\alpha_{\eta}(\eta^{\prime}),\qquad\alpha_{\mu}=\alpha_{\eta}\alpha_{\eta^{\prime}}.

We have

g^{*}_{\eta}g^{*}_{\eta^{\prime}}=(\eta^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{conj}(\eta)\alpha_{\eta}\bigr)(\eta^{\prime-1}),\mathrm{conj}(\eta)\alpha_{\eta}\mathrm{conj}(\eta^{\prime})\alpha_{\eta^{\prime}}\bigr).

The first component is

\eta^{-1}\eta\alpha_{\eta}(\eta^{\prime})^{-1}\eta^{-1}=\mu^{-1},

while the second is the automorphism taking
\nu\in N
to

\displaystyle\eta\alpha_{\eta}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}\alpha_{\eta^{\prime}}(\nu)\eta^{\prime-1}\bigr)\eta^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\alpha_{\eta}(\eta^{\prime})\alpha_{\eta}(\alpha_{\eta^{\prime}}(\nu))\alpha_{\eta}(\eta^{\prime})^{-1}\eta^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\alpha_{\mu}(\nu)\mu^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{conj}(\mu)\alpha_{\mu}(\nu).

Hence

g^{*}_{\eta}g^{*}_{\eta^{\prime}}=(\mu^{-1},\mathrm{conj}(\mu)\alpha_{\mu})=g^{*}_{\mu}.

∎

2.4.
Simple skew braces coming from nonabelian simple groups

Since an ideal in a brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
is a normal subgroup in each of the groups
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,\circ)
, it is clear that if either of these groups is a simple group then
B
must be a simple skew brace.

In the context of Hopf-Galois theory, it has been shown
[
Byo04a
]
that if the holomorph
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
of some finite group
N
contains a regular subgroup
G
which is a nonabelian simple group then
N\cong G
and
G
is the image of either the left or right regular representation of
N
. This means that if
B
is a finite skew brace for which
(B,\circ)
is a nonabelian simple group then
B
is either the trivial skew brace
(B,\circ,\circ)
or its opposite skew brace
(B,\circ^{\mathrm{op}},\circ)
.

On the other hand, there are finite skew braces where
(B,\cdot)
is a nonabelian simple group but
(B,\circ)\not\cong(B,\cdot)
. We will say that
(B,\cdot)
has an exact factorisation
A\cdot C
if
A
and
C
are subgroups of
(B,\cdot)
such that every element of
B
can be written uniquely in the form
a\cdot c
with
a\in A
and
c\in C
. By
[
SV18
, Theorem 2.3]
, any such exact factorisation gives rise to a skew brace with additive group
B
and multiplicative group
A\times C
. For example, writing
A_{n}
for the alternating group of degree
n
and
C_{m}
for the cyclic group of order
m
, the exact factorisation
A_{5}=A_{4}\cdot C_{5}
gives a skew brace whose additive group is the nonabelian simple group
A_{5}
and whose multiplicative group is the soluble group
A_{4}\times C_{5}
. Tsang
[
Tsa23
]
has determined all cases where the additive group is a nonabelian simple group and the multiplicative group is soluble.

3.
Construction of our simple skew braces

Notation 3.1
.

For the rest of the paper, we fix primes
p
and
q
such that
q
divides
(p^{p}-1)/(p-1)
, and we fix

n=p^{p}q
, as in Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\gcd(p-1,p^{p}-1)=p-1
, the condition on
p
and
q
holds if and only if
q
divides
p^{p}-1
but does not divide
p-1
. Our hypotheses mean that the multiplicative order of
p
mod
q
is
p
, so in particular
p
divides
q-1
. It is possible that
p^{2}
divides
q-1
, the smallest instance of this being
p=59
,
q=141\,579\,233
. (The author is indebted to Andrew Darlington for finding this example.)

In this section, we will construct a simple skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
of order
n=p^{p}q
as a regular subgroup in the holomorph of certain group
N
. Thus
(B,\cdot)\cong N
. We will then make explicit the operation
\circ
, and give a similar description of the opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
. We fix the following notation, which will be in force for the rest of the paper.

Notation 3.2
.

Let
V=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p}
be the space of
p
-component column vectors over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
p
elements. Then we may view
V
as an elementary abelian group of order
p^{p}
, and also as a left module for the matrix ring
\mathrm{Mat}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Further, let
K=\mathbb{F}_{p^{p}}
be the field of
p^{p}
elements. Given a matrix
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
whose minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is irreducible and of degree
p
, we may identify the subring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
of
\mathrm{Mat}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
with
K
, and so view
V
as a
1
-dimensional vector space over
K
. We will variously view
V
as a space of column vectors, an abelian group, or a
K
-vector space, as convenient.

3.1.
Characterisation of the additive group

The additive group
N
of the simple skew brace we will construct will be a group of order
n=p^{p}q
containing a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
. In any such group
N
, any Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
is a complement to
P
, so that
N
is a semidirect product
N\cong P\rtimes Q
for some action of
Q\cong C_{q}
on
P
. The next results shows that, if
Q
is not also normal on
N
, then there is exactly one possibility for
N
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Up to isomorphism, there is a unique group
N
of order
n
which has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
but does not have a normal Sylow
q
-subgroup. It has
the following (noncanonical) description. Let
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a matrix of order
q
. Then
N
is the group of block matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{p+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\mbox{ for }0\leq k\leq q-1\mbox{ and }v\in V,

and
P
is the subgroup of such matrices with
k=0
.

Proof.

We first note that the existence of a matrix
M
of order
q
is guaranteed. The multiplicative group
K^{\times}
of
K
is cyclic of order
p^{p}-1
, so, by our hypotheses in Notation
3.1
,
K
contains an element
\alpha
of order
q
and
\mathbb{F}(\alpha)=K
. The minimal polynomial
f(X)
of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is then irreducible of degree
p
, and any matrix
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
with characteristic polynomial
f(X)
, for example the companion matrix of
f(X)
, has order
q
.

Now let
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be any matrix of order
q
. As
q
is prime,
M
has an eigenvalue
\alpha
of order
q
in some extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and our hypotheses ensure that
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha)=K
. The minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is irreducible of degree
p
and therefore coincides with the minimal polynomial of
M
. Starting with this matrix
M
, we obtain a group
N
of
order
p^{p}q
consisting of block matrices, as in the statement of the Lemma. The elements of
N
with
k=0
form a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
. We identify this subgroup with
V
. The elements with
v=0
form a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
which is not normal in
N
.

Finally, let
H
be any group of order
n
which has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
. Let
Q
be a Sylow
q
-subgroup, and assume that
Q
is not normal in
H
. We will show that
H\cong N
. (In particular, this will show that, up to isomorphism, the group
N
does not depend on the choice of
M
.) Our hypotheses on
H
ensure that
H=P\rtimes Q
where
Q
acts nontrivially on
P
, so conjugation by a generator
\sigma
of
Q
induces an automorphism of
P
of order
q
. Let
F
be the Frattini subgroup of
P
. Then
P/F
is an elementary abelian group of order
p^{r}
for some

. By
[
Rob96
, 5.3.3]
,
|\mathrm{Aut}(P)|
divides
p^{(p-r)r}|\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
. Since
q\nmid|\mathrm{GL}_{s}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
when

, it follows that

and

is trivial. Hence we can (and do) identify

with
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{p}
, and
\sigma
acts on

as some matrix
M_{1}\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of order

. Identifying
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M_{1}]
with

, we then have that

acts on

as multiplication by some element
\beta\in K^{\times}
of order

. Replacing

by a suitable power, we may assume that

coincides with the eigenvalue

of the matrix

used to construct

. Then

and

have the same minimal polynomial, which is irreducible of degree

. It follows that
M_{1}
and

are conjugate in
\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Making a change of basis, we may therefore assume that

. Hence

.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

In the group

-subgroup

. Also,

, and some element of order

.

We next record a number of facts about the the matrix

Lemma 3.5

(i)

The only

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

The centraliser in

, which is cyclic of order

, generated by a matrix
T
with
T^{(p^{p}-1)/q}=M
.

(v)

If
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
AM=MA
, then the only
A
-invariant subspaces of
V
are
0
and
V
unless
A
is a scalar matrix
\lambda I
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.

(vi)

There is a matrix
J\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that
JMJ^{-1}=M^{p}
and
J^{p}=I
. Any such
J
has minimal polynomial
X^{p}-1=(X-1)^{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(vii)

The normaliser in
\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of the subgroup
\langle M\rangle
is generated by
T
and
J
and has order
(p^{p}-1)p
. Any element
A
of this normaliser can be written
A=T^{i}J^{j}
with
0\leq i<p^{p}-1
and
0\leq j<p
. Then
AMA^{-1}=M^{p^{j}}
. Also,
A
has order
p
if and only if
i
is divisible by
p-1
and
i\neq 0
,
j\neq 0
, and
A
has order
q
if and only if
A=M^{r}
with
1\leq r\leq q-1
.

Proof.

(i) We again identify
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
with
K
, so that conjugation by
M
corresponds to multiplication by an element
\alpha\in K
of order
q
. If
W
is an
M
-invariant
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace of
V
then
\alpha W=W
. As
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha)=K
, we must have
W=0
or
V
.

(ii) By (i), the subspace
\{v\in V:Mv=v\}
is trivial. Hence
M-I
is invertible.

(iii) Since the minimal polynomial of
M
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
has degree
p
, it is clear that the matrices
I
,
M+I
,
M^{2}+M+I
, …,
M^{p-1}+\cdots+M+I
form a basis of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
. Multiplying by the invertible matrix
M-I
, we find that
M-I
,
M^{2}-I
, …,
M^{p}-I
is also a basis. Thus the
(M^{i}-I)w
span
V
.

(iv) By the Double Centraliser Theorem
[
Pie82
, §12.7]
, the centraliser of the subalgebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
in
\mathrm{Mat}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
itself. Hence the centraliser of
\langle M\rangle
in
\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]^{\times}\cong K^{\times}
, which is cyclic of order
p^{p}-1
. As
M
has order
q
, there is some generator
T
with
T^{(p^{p}-1)/q}=M
.

(v) We have
A=T^{i}
for some
i
, and
A
acts on
V
as multiplication by some
\lambda\in K^{\times}
. If
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
then
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\lambda)=K
and the only
A
-invariant subspaces are
0
and
V
.

(vi) By the Noether-Skolem Theorem
[
Pie82
, §12.6]
, every automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
is induced by conjugation by some element of
\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. In particular, there is some matrix
J
inducing the Frobenius automorphism
\phi:x\mapsto x^{p}
. Thus
JMJ^{-1}=M^{p}
. Then
J^{p}
centralises
\langle M\rangle
, so
J^{p}
has order dividing
p^{p}-1
by (iv).
Replacing
J
by
J^{1-p^{p}}
, we may therefore assume that
J^{p}=I
. Clearly
J
has characteristic polynomial
X^{p}-1=(X-1)^{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so it only remains to verify that
(J-I)^{p-1}\neq 0
. Now
(J-I)^{p-1}=J^{p-1}+\cdots+J+I
acts on
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]
as the trace operator
\phi^{p-1}+\cdots+\phi+1
from
K
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Since the trace is nondegenerate, we have
(J-I)^{p-1}\neq 0
as required.

(vii) If
AMA^{-1}=M^{s}
then conjugation by
A
induces an automorphism of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[M]\cong K
. Since the Galois group of
K/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is cyclic of order
p
, generated by
\phi
, we must have
AMA^{-1}=M^{p^{j}}
with
0\leq j\leq p-1
. Then
AJ^{-j}
centralises
\langle M\rangle
, so, by (iv),
A=T^{i}J^{j}
for some
i
, and
AMA^{-1}=A^{p^{j}}
. Hence the normaliser is generated by
T
and
J
and has order
(p^{p}-1)p
. We have

A^{k}=T^{i(1+p^{j}+p^{2j}+\cdots+p^{(k-1)j})}J^{jk}.

For
A
to have order
p
, we need
j\neq 0
and
i(1+p^{j}+p^{2j}+\cdots+p^{(k-1)j})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{p}-1}
. But for
j\neq 0
, the residue classes
p^{j}
,
p^{2j}
,…,
p^{(p-1)j}
mod
p^{p}-1
are congruent in some order to
1
,
p
,
p^{2}
, …,
p^{p-1}
, so
A
has order dividing
p
if and only if
i(1+p+\cdots+p^{p-1})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{p}-1}
, which holds precisely when
p-1
divides
i
. For
A
to have order
q
we need
j=0
, so that
A^{k}=T^{ik}
, with
i\neq 0
and
i=(p^{p}-1)r/q
for some
r
. Then
A=M^{r}
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Any matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
with minimal polynomial
X^{p}-1
is conjugate to the Jordan matrix

(3.1)

J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&1&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&1&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\ldots&1&1\\
0&0&0&0&\ldots&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

so, by a change of basis, we choose the matrix
M
of order
q
so that the matrix
J
in Lemma
3.5
(vi) is as in (
3.1
).

We next describe the automorphisms of the group constructed in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.7
.

\mathrm{Aut}(N)
can be identified with the group of block matrices

\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right):A\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mbox{ normalises }\langle M\rangle,\,v\in V\right\}

acting on
N
by conjugation in
\mathrm{GL}_{p+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. As
P
is characteristic in
N
, there is some
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, uniquely determined by
\theta
, such that

\theta\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&Aw\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

for all
w\in V
. Also

\theta\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

for some
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,q-1\}
and some
u\in V
. Applying
\theta
to the relation

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&Mw\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),

we have

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&Aw\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&AMw\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

This holds for all
w
if and only if
AMA^{-1}=M^{k}
. Hence
A
normalises
\langle M\rangle
. The matrix
I-AMA^{-1}=A(M-I)A^{-1}
is invertible by Lemma
3.5
(ii). Thus there is a unique
v\in V
with
(I-AMA^{-1})v=u
. Then
\theta
corresponds to conjugation by the block matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

Conversely, for any
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
normalising
\langle M\rangle
and any
v\in V
, we easily verify that conjugation by the above matrix gives an automorphism of
N
.
∎

We record that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A^{-1}&-A^{-1}v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

and, for
k\geq 0
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)^{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A^{k}&A^{[k]}v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

where we write

(3.2)

A^{[0]}=0,\qquad A^{[k]}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}A^{i}\mbox{ for }k\geq 1.

We will use these facts without further comment.

3.2.
Construction of a simple skew brace

From now on,
N
denotes the group of order
p^{p}q
constructed in Lemma
3.3
, where
M
and
J
are chosen as in Remark
3.6
. In particular,

is the Jordan matrix (
3.1
).
Let
e_{1}
, …,
e_{p}
be the standard basis of

.

By Lemma
3.7
, we can write elements of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
in the form
[R,\mathrm{conj}(S)]
where

R=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\qquad S=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

with
0\leq k\leq q-1
,
AMA^{-1}=M^{s}
for some

, and

,

.
We include the notation

in elements of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
to emphasise that the action of

on

is by conjugation rather than simply by matrix multiplication. Thus multiplication in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is given by

[R_{1},\mathrm{conj}(S_{1})][R_{2},\mathrm{conj}(S_{2})]=[R_{1}S_{1}R_{2}S_{1}^{-1},\mathrm{conj}(S_{1}S_{2})].

We consider the following elements of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
:

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],

Y_{v}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&-v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]\mbox{ for each }v\in V,

\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&-e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

In particular,

, the identity element of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
.

We will record in Lemma
3.9
various relations satisfied by these elements. We first make some preliminary observations.

Clearly for
i\geq 0
we have

(3.3)

X^{i}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{i}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

To obtain a formula for
Z^{k}
, we first note that routine induction arguments give

(3.4)

J^{k}e_{p}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}e_{p-i},

and

(3.5)

J^{[k]}e_{p}=\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}{k\choose h+1}e_{p-h}=ke_{p}+{k\choose 2}e_{p-1}+\cdots+e_{p+1-k}.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

Z^{k}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{k}&-J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

Proof.

We again use induction: the stated formula holds for
k=0
, and if it holds for some
k\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle Z^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&-e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]\;\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{k}&-J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[A,\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{k+1}&-J^{[k+1]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&-e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{-1}&J^{-1}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{-1}&J^{-1}e_{p}+J^{[k]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&J^{[k+1]}e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

The elements
X
,
Y_{v}
,

satisfy the following relations for

,
w\in V
:

(i)

,

(ii)

.

(iii)

,

(iv)

Y_{v}X=XY_{v}
,

(v)

Y_{v}Y_{w}=Y_{v+w}
;

(vi)

ZY_{v}=Y_{Jv}Z
.

Proof.

(i) is immediate from (
3.3
).

(ii) follows from the case

of Proposition
3.8
since

and, using (
3.5
),
J^{[p]}e_{p}=e_{1}
.

(iii) We calculate

\displaystyle ZX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&-e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]\;\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&e_{p}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&-e_{p}\\

\displaystyle\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}JMJ^{-1}&JMJ^{-1}e_{p}\\

and, using (

),

\displaystyle X^{p}Z

\displaystyle=

Corollary 3.10
.

For each

we have
ZY_{v}Z^{-1}Y_{v}^{-1}=Y_{Jv-v}
. In particular,

ZY_{e_{i}}Z^{-1}Y_{e_{i}}^{-1}=Y_{e_{i-1}}\mbox{ for }2\leq i\leq p.

We now consider the subgroup

(3.16)

.

Notation 3.11
.

Let

denote the

-subspace of

From (

Lemma 3.12

The group

in (

. Moreover, every element

We first consider the expressions (

) and (

repeatedly, with find that

Using Lemma

. This shows the equivalence of (

), and also that every element of the form (

. On the other hand, using the relations in Lemma

, we may rewrite the product of any two such elements in the form

,

and

. Since

. By Lemma

∎

We now come to the central result of this paper.

Theorem 3.13
.

Under the correspondence described in §
2.2
, the subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
defined in (
3.16
) gives a simple skew brace
(B,\cdot,\circ)
of order
n=p^{p}q
with
(B,\cdot)\cong N
and
(B,\circ)\cong G
.

Proof.

Since
G
is a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, it corresponds to a skew brace
B
whose additive and multiplicative groups are as stated. To show that
B
is simple, recall from Lemma
3.4
that the only nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
N
is its (unique) Sylow
p
-subgroup. Since an ideal in
B
must be normal in both
(B,\cdot)
and
(B,\circ)
, it follows that
B
must be simple unless
G
also has a normal (and hence unique) Sylow
p
-subgroup. However, it is immediate from Lemma
3.9
and Lemma
3.12

that

P=\{Y_{v}Z^{k}:v\in V_{0},\;0\leq k\leq p-1\}

is a subgroup of
G
of order
p^{p}
, and it is not normal since
X^{-1}ZX=X^{p-1}Z
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

The Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
has exponent
p^{2}
, and when
p>2
it has derived length
2
since its commutator subgroup is the elementary abelian group
\langle Y_{e_{p-2}},Y_{e_{p-3}},\ldots,Y_{e_{1}}\rangle
of index

. On the other hand, it follows from Corollary
3.10
that the nilpotency class of
P
is

, the maximum possible for a group of order
p^{p}
. Thus the structure of
P
is in some sense extremal. Of course
P
is abelian if and only if
p=2
.

3.3.
The opposite skew brace to
B

The skew brace
B
constructed in Theorem
3.13
has an opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
and, as explained in Lemma
2.6
,

corresponds to a regular subgroup

in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Since

, the proof of Theorem

also shows that

, analogous to those already given for the regular subgroup

corresponding to

. These will not be required in the rest of the paper.

It is convenient to take the inverses of the generators of

given above: we have

G=\langle X^{-1},Y_{v}^{-1},Z^{-1}:v\in V_{0}\rangle

X^{-1}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-1}&0\\

\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],

\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]\mbox{ for each }u\in V_{0},

\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

Replacing each of the original generators
g
of

by the element

, where the operation

is as in Lemma

, we obtain the following set of generators for the group

.

X^{*}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-1}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],

\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J^{-1}&0\\

Analogously to Lemma

, we find that the elements

4.
B
and
B^{\mathrm{op}}
are not isomorphic

We have obtained two mutually opposite simple skew braces
B
,
B^{\mathrm{op}}
of order
n=p^{p}q
corresponding to the regular subgroups
G
,
G^{*}
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
given in (
3.16
) and §
3.3
respectively. Our goal in this section is to verify that
B
and
B^{\mathrm{op}}
are not isomorphic, so that we have indeed found two distinct simple skew braces of order
n
. Rather than working directly with the generators of
G^{*}
given in §
3.3
, we adopt a more indirect approach.

If
\Phi:B\to B^{\mathrm{op}}
is an isomorphism of skew braces, then

\Phi
is simultaneously an automorphism of
(B,\circ)
and an anti-automorphism of
(B,\cdot)
.
The anti-automorphisms of
(B,\cdot)
are precisely the functions
b\mapsto\alpha(b)^{-1}
for
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(B,\cdot)
. We therefore look for an automorphism
\alpha
of
(B,\cdot)
such that the function

\Phi:B\to B
,
\Phi(b)=\alpha(b)^{-1}
, is an automorphism of
(B,\circ)
. By (
2.1
), this condition on
\Phi
is equivalent to

(4.1)

g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=g_{\mu}\Rightarrow g_{\Phi(\eta)}g_{\Phi(\eta^{\prime})}=g_{\Phi(\mu)}\mbox{ for }\eta,\eta^{\prime},\mu\in N,

where, as before,
g_{\eta}
denotes the unique element of
G
with first component
\eta
.
The bijection
g_{\eta}\mapsto\eta
from
G
to
N
is described explicitly by (
3.20
) in the proof of Lemma
3.12
, and its inverse is described implicitly in that proof. To make the latter description explicit, we introduce the following notation.

Definition 4.1
.

Define
\kappa:V\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
by

\kappa(k_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+k_{p}e_{p})=k_{p}\mbox{ for }k_{1},\ldots,k_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

For
v\in V
, let
\widetilde{\kappa}(v)
to be the unique integer with
0\leq\widetilde{\kappa}(v)\leq p-1
whose residue class in
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is
\kappa(v)
.

We then have
v-\widetilde{\kappa}(v)e_{p}=v-\kappa(v)e_{p}\in V_{0}
, so also
v-J^{[\widetilde{\kappa}(v)]}e_{p}\in V_{0}
by (
3.17
). We further define

\pi:V\to V_{0}
by

\pi(v)=v-J^{[\widetilde{\kappa}(v)]}e_{p}.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that
\kappa
is a linear map, but
\pi
is linear only when
p=2
. The distinction between
\kappa
and
\widetilde{\kappa}
matters since
J^{[p]}\neq J^{[0]}
, and also since
Z
has order
p^{2}
.

Now by (
3.20
), if
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
u\in V_{0}
and
0\leq k<p
, we have

(4.2)

g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{u}Z^{k}\Rightarrow\eta=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{i}&M^{i}(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),

and conversely, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v\in V
, we have

(4.3)

\eta=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{i}&M^{i}v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\Rightarrow g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{\pi(v)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(v)}.

By Lemma
3.7
, the automorphism
\alpha
of
(B,\cdot)=N
is given by conjugation by a matrix of the form

(4.4)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

where
w\in V
and
A
satisfies
AMA^{-1}=M^{s}
. By Lemma
3.5
(vii), we have
s=p^{j}
with
0\leq j\leq p-1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta\in N
, and write
g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{u}Z^{k}
with
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
u\in V_{0}
,
0\leq k<p
. Then

g_{\alpha(\eta)}=X^{is}Y_{\pi(v)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(v)}

where
v=(M^{-is}-I)w+A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})
, and

g_{\Phi(\eta)}=g_{\alpha(\eta)^{-1}}=X^{-is}Y_{\pi(y)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(y)}

with
y=(M^{is}-I)w-AM^{i}(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})
.

Proof.

As
\eta
is given by (
4.2
), we calculate that

\displaystyle\alpha(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{is}&(I-M^{is})w+AM^{i}(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{is}&M^{is}\big((M^{-is}-I)w+A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

Here we have used that
AM^{i}=M^{is}A
. The expression for
g_{\alpha(\eta)}
then follows using (
4.3
). Taking the inverse, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-is}&-(M^{-is}-I)w-A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-is}&M^{-is}\big((M^{is}-I)w-M^{is}A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),

giving the expression for
g_{\Phi(\eta)}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The simple skew brace
B
given in Theorem
3.13
is not isomorphic to its opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that the skew braces
B
and
B^{\mathrm{op}}
are isomorphic. Then, for some choice of
A
and
w
in (
4.4
), the corresponding function
\Phi
is an automorphism of
(N,\circ)
. Thus (
4.1
) holds.

We will make two applications of (
4.1
).
First, take
\eta
,
\eta^{\prime}
,
\mu
so that
g_{\eta}=X^{i}
,
g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X
,
g_{\mu}=X^{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For brevity, we set
\pi(i)=\pi\big((M^{is}-I)w\big)
and
\widetilde{\kappa}(i)=\widetilde{\kappa}\big((M^{is}-I)w\big)
.
Then by Lemma
4.3
we have
g_{\Phi(\eta)}=X^{-is}Y_{\pi(i)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i)}
, with similar expressions for
g_{\Phi(\eta^{\prime})}
and
g_{\Phi(\mu)}
. Hence by (
4.1
), we have

X^{-is}Y_{\pi(i)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i)}X^{-s}Y_{\pi(1)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(1)}=X^{-(i+1)s}Y_{\pi(i+1)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i+1)}.

Using the relations
ZX=X^{p}Z
,
ZY_{u}=Y_{Ju}Z
and
Y_{u}X=XY_{u}
in Lemma
3.9
,
and writing
k=\widetilde{\kappa}(i)
, this becomes

X^{-is-sp^{k}}Y_{\pi(i)+J^{k}\pi(1)}Z^{k+\widetilde{\kappa}(1)}=X^{-(i+1)s}Y_{\pi(i+1)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i+1)}.

Comparing exponents on
X
, we find that

-is-sp^{k}\equiv-(i+1)s\pmod{q},

so
k=\widetilde{\kappa}(i)=0
.
This means that
(M^{is}-I)w\in V_{0}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
3.5
(iii) (applied to
M^{s}
in place of
M
), we therefore have
w=0
. Thus the formula for
g_{\Phi(\eta)}
in Lemma
4.3
simplifies to

g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{u}Z^{k}\Rightarrow g_{\Phi(\eta)}=X^{-is}Y_{\pi(-AM^{i}(u+J^{[k]}e_{p}))}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-AM^{i}(u+J^{[k]}e_{p}))}.

For our second application of (
4.1
), let
g_{\eta}=Y_{u}
,
g_{\eta}^{\prime}=X
,
g_{\mu}=Y_{u}X=XY_{u}
for arbitrary
u\in V_{0}
. We have

g_{\Phi(\eta)}=Y_{\pi(-Au)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-Au)},

g_{\Phi(\eta^{\prime})}=X^{-s},

g_{\Phi(\mu)}=X^{-s}Y_{\pi(-AMu)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-AMu)}.

Hence we have the equation

Y_{\pi(-Au)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-Au)}X^{-s}=X^{-s}Y_{\pi(-AMu)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-AMu)}

in
G
. Writing
h=\widetilde{\kappa}(-Au)
, we therefore have

X^{-sp^{h}}Y_{\pi(-Au)}Z^{h}=X^{-s}Y_{\pi(-AMu)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(-AMu)}.

Comparing exponents on
X
, we see that
h=0
, and comparing exponents on
Z
, we also find that
\widetilde{\kappa}(-AMu)=0
. Hence
-Au\in V_{0}
and
-AMu\in V_{0}
for all
u\in V_{0}
. It follows that
M^{s}V_{0}=(-AM)(-A)V_{0}=V_{0}
. But by Lemma
3.5
(i), there are no
M^{s}
-invariant subspaces of
V
except
0
and
V
, so we have obtained the required contradiction.
∎

5.
Automorphisms

It is of interest to determine the group
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
of automorphisms of the simple skew braces we have constructed. Since we can do this by a similar argument to Theorem
4.4
, we include the result here. As observed in §
2.3
, we automatically have
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B^{\mathrm{op}})\cong\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
B
be the simple skew brace in Theorem
3.13
. Then
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{sb}}(B)
has order
p
and is generated by the automorphism of
(B,\cdot)=N
given by conjugation by the block matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

Proof.

Any automorphism
\alpha
of
(N,\cdot)
is induced by conjugation by a matrix as in (
4.4
) for some
w\in V
and some
A
with
AMA^{-1}=M^{s}
. Then
\alpha
will be an automorphism of the skew brace
B
if and only if also

(5.1)

g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=g_{\mu}\Rightarrow g_{\alpha(\eta)}g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}=g_{\alpha(\mu)}.

We apply this for several choices of
\eta
,
\eta^{\prime}
,
\mu
.

Firstly, let
g_{\eta}=X^{i}
,
g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X
,
g_{\mu}=X^{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Writing
\pi(i)=\pi\big((M^{-is}-I)w\big)
and
\widetilde{\kappa}(i)=\widetilde{\kappa}\big((M^{-is}-I)w\big)
, we see from Lemma
4.3
that

g_{\alpha(\eta)}=X^{is}Y_{\pi(i)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i)},

and similarly for
g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}
and
g_{\alpha(\mu)}
. This gives the equation

X^{is}Y_{\pi(i)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i)}X^{s}Y_{\pi(1)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(1)}=X^{(i+1)s}Y_{\pi(i+1)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}(i+1)}.

Rewriting the left-hand side and comparing exponents on
X
, we find that
\widetilde{\kappa}(i)=0
. Thus
(M^{-is}-I)w=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
3.5
(iii) we then have
w=0
.

Next, take
g_{\eta}=Y_{u}Z^{k}
,
g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X
,
g_{\mu}=Y_{u}Z^{k}X=X^{p^{k}}Y_{u}Z^{k}
with
u\in V_{0}
and
0\leq k<p
. So we have

g_{\alpha(\eta)}=Y_{\pi\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)},

g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}=X^{s},

g_{\alpha(\mu)}=X^{sp^{k}}Y_{\pi\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}.

Hence

Y_{\pi\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}Z^{\widetilde{\kappa}\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}X^{s}=X^{sp^{k}}Y_{\pi\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}Z_{\widetilde{\kappa}\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}.

Rewriting and equating exponents on
X
, we find that
\widetilde{\kappa}\big(A(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)=k
for all
u\in V_{0}
and
0\leq k<p
. In particular,
AV_{0}=V_{0}
, and
(A-I)e_{p}\in V_{0}
. By Lemma
3.5
(vii), we have
s=p^{j}
for some
j
. Let
B=AJ^{-j}
. Then
BM=MB
. Since
JV_{0}=V_{0}
, we also have
BV_{0}=V_{0}
. Hence by Lemma
3.5
(v),
B=\lambda I
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Since
(A-I)e_{p}\in V_{0}
and
(J-I)e_{p}=e_{p-1}\in V_{0}
, we also have
(B-I)e_{p}\in V_{0}
, so
\lambda=1
. We conclude that
A=J^{j}
. Thus any skew brace automorphism of
B
arises from conjugation by some power of the block matrix in the statement of the Theorem.

It remains to verify that conjugation by this block matrix does indeed give a skew brace automorphism. Clearly it will then have order
p
. Thus we take
\alpha
to be given by
w=0
and
A=J
. Then
s=p
, and now

g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{u}Z^{k}\Rightarrow g_{\alpha(\eta)}=X^{ip}Y_{\pi\big(J(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)}Z^{k}

where

\pi\big(J(u+J^{[k]}e_{p})\big)=Ju+JJ^{[k]}e_{p}-J^{[k]}e_{p}=Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}.

We will check that (
5.1
) holds for arbitrary elements
g_{\eta}=X^{i}Y_{u}Z^{k}
and
g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X^{i^{\prime}}Y_{u^{\prime}}Z^{k^{\prime}}
of
G
, where
u
,
u^{\prime}\in V_{0}
and
0\leq k,k^{\prime}<p
. Let
\mu\in N
be the element satisfying

(5.2)

g_{\mu}=g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X^{i+i^{\prime}p^{k}}Y_{u+J^{k}u^{\prime}}Z^{k+k^{\prime}}.

Then we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha(\eta)}g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X^{ip}Y_{Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}}Z^{k}X^{i^{\prime}p}Y_{Ju^{\prime}+(J^{k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}}Z^{k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X^{ip+i^{\prime}p^{k+1}}Y_{Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}+J^{k}\big(Ju^{\prime}+(J^{k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}\big)}Z^{k+k^{\prime}}.

The expressions (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) are not necessarily in our standard form (
3.18
) since possibly
k+k^{\prime}\geq p
. We therefore distinguish two cases.

Firstly, if
k+k^{\prime}<p
, then from (
5.2
) we have

g_{\alpha(\mu)}=X^{ip+i^{\prime}p^{k+1}}Y_{J(u+J^{k}u^{\prime})+(J^{k+k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}}Z^{k+k^{\prime}}.

The exponents on
X
and
Z
agree with those in (
5.3
), so (
5.1
) holds in this case if the subscripts on
Y
coincide, that is, if

J(u+J^{k}u^{\prime})+(J^{k+k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}=Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}+J^{k}\big(Ju^{\prime}+(J^{k^{\prime}}-I\big)e_{p}).

Expanding both sides, we see that this identity does indeed hold.

Secondly, if
k+k^{\prime}\geq p
then we use the the relation
Z^{p}=Y_{e_{1}}
to rewrite (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) as

g_{\mu}=g_{\eta}g_{\eta^{\prime}}=X^{i+i^{\prime}p^{k}}Y_{u+J^{k}u^{\prime}+e_{1}}Z^{k+k^{\prime}-p},

g_{\alpha(\eta)}g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}=X^{ip+i^{\prime}p^{k+1}}Y_{Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}+J^{k}\big(Ju^{\prime}+(J^{k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}\big)+e_{1}}Z^{k+k^{\prime}-p}.

Then

g_{\alpha(\mu)}=X^{ip+i^{\prime}p^{k+1}}Y_{J(u+J^{k}u^{\prime}+e_{1})+(J^{k+k^{\prime}-p}-I)e_{p}}Z^{k+k^{\prime}-p},

which agrees with
g_{\alpha(\eta)}g_{\alpha(\eta^{\prime})}
provided that

\displaystyle J(u+J^{k}u^{\prime}+e_{1})+(J^{k+k^{\prime}-p}-I)e_{p}=

\displaystyle Ju+(J^{k}-I)e_{p}+J^{k}\big(Ju^{\prime}+(J^{k^{\prime}}-I)e_{p}\big)+e_{1}.

Expanding both sides, and recalling that
Je_{1}=e_{1}
and

, we again find that the required identity holds.
∎

6.
Classification of simple skew braces order
n

Our goal in this final section is the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Any simple skew brace of order n as in Notation
3.1
is isomorphic to either the skew brace
B
of Theorem
3.13
or its opposite skew brace
B^{\mathrm{op}}
.

6.1.
Determining the additive and multiplicative groups

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
H
be a group of order
p^{p}q
, and let
P
and
Q
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup and a Sylow
q
-subgroup respectively. Then at least one of
P
and
Q
is normal in
H
, and
H
has one of the following structures:

(i)

H=P\times Q
;

(ii)

H\cong N
with
N
as in Lemma
3.3
, so that in particular
H
is a semidirect product
P\rtimes Q
and
Q
is not normal in
H
;

(iii)

H
is a semidirect product
Q\rtimes P
and
P
is not normal in
H
.

Proof.

If
P\lhd H
then either
H
is a direct product as in (i), or
Q
is not normal in
H
. In the latter case, Lemma
3.3
shows that (ii) holds. It therefore suffices to show that if
P
is not normal in
H
then
Q\lhd H
, since in that case
P
is a complement to
Q
and (iii) holds.

Suppose that
P
is not normal in
H
, and let

\{1\}=H_{0}\lhd H_{1}\lhd\ldots\lhd H_{p+1}=H

be a composition series for
H
. Then exactly one quotient
H_{s+1}/H_{s}
has order
q
, and
s<p
since otherwise
H_{p}
would be a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
. Writing
P_{i}=H_{i}\cap P
for each
i
, we obtain the series

\{1\}=P_{0}\lhd P_{1}\lhd\ldots P_{s}=P_{s+1}\lhd\cdots\lhd P_{p+1}=P,

which is a composition series of
P
with one term repeated. Then
H_{s}=P_{s}
and
H_{s+1}=P_{s}Q
for some choice of Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
of
H
. Thus
P_{s}\lhd P_{s}Q
, and
Q
acts by conjugation on
P_{s}
. As
P_{s}
has order
p^{s}
with
s<p
, and the order of
p
mod
q
is
p
, a Frattini argument (as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
) shows that
P_{s}
has no automorphism of order
q
. Thus
Q
acts trivially on
P_{s}
and
Q\lhd P_{s}Q=H_{s+1}
. Now if
s+1\leq t\leq p
and
Q\lhd H_{t}
then
Q
is the unique Sylow
q
-subgroup of
H_{t}
and is therefore characteristic in
H_{t}
. As
H_{t}\lhd H_{t+1}
, it follows that
Q\lhd H_{t+1}
. By induction, we therefore have
Q\lhd H_{p+1}=H
as required.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
H
be a group of order
p^{p}q
and let
G
be a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(H)
giving a skew brace
B
. If
B
is simple then, in the classification of Lemma
6.2
,
H
is of type (ii) and
G
is of type (iii).

Proof.

If
H
contains a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
then
P
is characteristic in
H
and, by Remark
2.3
, gives a left ideal in
B
. This is then a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If this subgroup is normal then
B
contains an ideal of order
p^{p}
. Similarly, if both
H
and
G
contain a normal Sylow
q
-subgroup, then
B
contains an ideal of order
q
. Thus, for
B
to be simple, either
H
is of type (ii) and
G
of type (iii), or vice versa. Suppose for a contradiction that the second of these possibilities holds:
H=Q\rtimes P
and
G=V\rtimes C_{q}\cong N
.
Since
q<p^{p}
, the kernel of the action of
P
on
Q
is a subgroup
P_{0}
of
P
of order
p^{i}
where
1\leq i\leq p-1
. Then
Q\times P_{0}
is a subgroup of
H
of order
p^{i}q
, contradicting Lemma
3.4
.
∎

6.2.
Regular subgroups

By Lemma
6.3
, any simple skew brace of order
n
comes from a regular subgroup in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, where
N
is the group constructed in Lemma
3.3
. Moreover, we only need to consider regular subgroups with the structure
Q\rtimes P
as in Lemma
6.2
(iii). Our aim is to show that, up to conjugation by
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
, the only possibilities are the group
G
in (
3.16
) and the corresponding group
G^{*}
obtained from
G
via Lemma
2.6
.

Any such subgroup contains a normal subgroup of order
q
. Let us begin by looking for suitable subgroups of order
q
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. We will say that a subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
acts freely on
N
if the stabiliser of each element of
N
is trivial. Any subgroup of a regular group acts freely.

Lemma 6.4
.

Up to conjugation by
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
, any subgroup of order
q
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
which acts freely on
N
has a generator of one of the following forms:

(i)

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right];

(ii)

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-1}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right];

(iii)

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

with
1\leq k\leq q-2
and
v
arbitrary.

Moreover the generator in (ii) is obtained from that in (i) via the operation
*
in Lemma
2.6
.

Proof.

A typical element
X
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
has the form

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],

where
A
normalises
\langle M\rangle
, say
AMA^{-1}=M^{s}
.

We determine when
X
has order
q
. The second component of
X^{q}
is

\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A^{q}&A^{[q]}w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right).

Thus we need
A^{q}=I
and
A^{[q]}w=0
. There are two possibilities:

(a)

A=I
, so
A^{[q]}=qI
with
w=0
;

(b)

A
has order
q
, so
A=M^{r}
with
1\leq r\leq q-1
by Lemma
3.5
(vii). Then
A-I
is invertible and
A^{[q]}=(A-I)^{-1}(A^{q}-I)=0
with
w
arbitrary.

In case (a) we have

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

For
X
to have order
q
, we also need
k\neq 0
. Replacing
X
by a suitable power, we may assume
k=1
.
Let
u=(M-I)^{-1}v
. Inside
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, we conjugate
X
by the automorphism

(6.1)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

of
N
, i.e. by the element

\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. This replaces
v
by
0
, so we obtain the element in (i).

In case (b),
X
has the form

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{r}&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

for some
v
,
w
. Replacing
X
by a suitable power, we may assume that
r=1
.

If
k=0
then

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

which fixes

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&(I-M^{-1})v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),

so the subgroup generated by
X
does not act freely.

If
k\neq 0
, we may conjugate by the automorphism of the form (
6.1
) with
u=(M-I)^{-1}w
. Thus, replacing
v
by some other element of
V
, we can assume that

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

Inductively, we then have

X^{j}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{kj}&(M^{k+1})^{[j]}v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{j}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]\mbox{for }j\geq 1.

If
k=q-1
, we have
(M^{k+1})^{[q]}v=qv
so
v=0
. Then

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{-1}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

is as in (ii). If
1\leq k\leq q-2
then
(M^{k+1})^{[q]}=0
and we obtain no restriction on
v
, giving (iii).

Finally, applying the operation
*
from Lemma
2.6
to the generator
X
in (i), we obtain

\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)^{-1},\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],

which coincides with the generator in (ii).
∎

We will look for regular subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
containing a normal subgroup of order
q
generated by an element
X
of order
q
of type (i) or (iii) in Lemma
6.4
. We do not need to consider type (ii) since the resulting groups will be obtained by applying the operation
*
of Lemma
2.6
to those coming from type (i).

Lemma 6.5
.

For
X
as in Lemma
6.4
(i), the corresponding skew brace is the simple skew brace
B
of Theorem
3.13
.

Proof.

We take

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

and look for elements of
p
-power order normalising
\langle X\rangle
.
If

U=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

is such an element, with
UXU^{-1}=X^{s}
, then expanding
UX=X^{s}U
we find

AMA^{-1}=M^{s},\qquad v+M^{k}w-M^{k}AMA^{-1}w=M^{s}v.

We conclude firstly by Lemma
3.5
(vii) that
s=p^{j}
for some
j
, so that conjugation by
U
gives an automorphism of
\langle X\rangle
of order
1
or
p
, and secondly that

(I-M^{s})v=-(I-M^{s})M^{k}w.

Since
I-M^{s}
is invertible by Lemma
3.5
(ii), we have

v=-M^{k}w.

Recall that are looking for a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
with the structure
Q\rtimes P
, where
Q=\langle X\rangle
and the group
P
of order
p^{p}
acts nontrivially on
Q
. Thus the kernel
P_{0}
of the action of
P
on
Q
has order
p^{p-1}
and consists of elements
U\in\mathrm{Hol}(N)
as just described which commute with
X
. The group
P
itself will be generated by
P_{0}
and one further element
Z
with
ZXZ^{-1}=X^{p}
.

If
U\in P_{0}
then
A
commutes with
M
and has
p
-power order. By Lemma
3.5
(iv), we then have
A=I
. For
U
to have order a power of
p
, we then require
k=0
. Hence
U
is of the form

Y_{v}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&-v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

for some
v\in V
. For elements of this form, we have
Y_{v}Y_{w}=Y_{v+w}
. Let

V^{\prime}=\{u\in V:Y_{u}\in P_{0}\}.

Then
V^{\prime}
is a
(p-1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace of
V
, and the function
u\mapsto Y_{u}
is an isomorphism between
V^{\prime}
and
P_{0}
as abelian groups. In particular,
P_{0}
is an elementary abelian group of order
p^{p-1}
.

For the remaining generator
Z
, we require that
ZX=X^{p}Z
,
Z^{p}\in P_{0}
, and
Z
normalises
P_{0}
. Multiplying
Z
by a power of
X
, we may assume that

Z=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&-v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

for some
v\in V
, where
AMA^{-1}=M^{p}
. Then
v\not\in V^{\prime}
since our group must be transitive on
N
. Also,
A
must have order
p
, so that
A=T^{(p-1)h}J
for some
h
by Lemma
3.5
(vii). Since
JTJ^{-1}=T^{p}
, we have
T^{h}AT^{-h}=J
, and conjugation by the automorphism

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}T^{h}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

of
N
replaces
A
by
J
,
v
by
T^{h}v
, and
V^{\prime}
by
T^{h}V^{\prime}
. We may therefore assume that
A=J
. We then
have
ZY_{u}=Y_{Ju}Z
, so
V^{\prime}
must be the unique subspace of
V
of dimension
p-1
which is invariant under
J
, namely
V_{0}
(c.f. Notation
3.11
). Replacing
Z
by
Y_{u}Z
for a suitable
u\in V_{0}
, we can suppose that
v=\lambda e_{p}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. After a final conjugation by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\lambda^{-1}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),

we have
v=e_{p}
. Then
X
,
Y_{u}
and
Z
are the same elements as in (
3.16
) and we obtain the simple skew brace
B
of Theorem
3.13
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

For
X
as in Lemma
6.4
(iii), there are no regular subgroups
G
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
containing
X
.

Proof.

Let

X=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{k}&v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

with
1\leq k\leq q-2
and
v
arbitrary. Then

X^{s}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{ks}&(M^{k+1})^{[s]}v\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{s}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

Thus if

U=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{t}&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&w\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right]

satisfies
UX=X^{s}U
then we have from the second components that

AM=M^{s}A,\qquad w=M^{s}w.

By Lemma
3.5
(vii),
s
is a power of
p
, and by Lemma
3.5
(ii),
w=0
. Moreover, the automorphism obtained by conjugation by
U
has order
1
or
p
, so there must be a subgroup of order
p^{p-1}
consisting of such elements
U
for which
s=1
.

As before, if
s=1
and
A
has
p
-power order then
A=I
and we obtain

U=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}M^{t}&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right].

This element cannot have order a power of
p
unless
t=0
. Thus there is a subspace
V^{\prime}
of
V
of dimension
p-1
such that
G
contains the elements

Y_{u}=\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&u\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right),\mathrm{conj}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)\right],\qquad u\in V^{\prime}.

From the first component of the relation
Y_{u}X=XY_{u}
, we then obtain

u+v=v+M^{k+1}u,

As
k\neq q-2
, the matrix
M^{k+1}-I
is invertible, so
u=0
. Thus
V^{\prime}=0
. This is a contradiction since
V^{\prime}
has dimension
p-1
. Hence there is no simple skew brace with
X
of type (iii) in Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Theorem
6.1
now follows immediately from Lemmas
6.4
,
6.5
and
6.6
.

On the trace theorem to Volterra-type equations with local or non-local derivatives
Jae-Hwan Choi, Jin Bong Lee, Jinsol Seo, Kwan Woo

Abstract
. This paper considers traces at the initial time for solutions of evolution equations with local or non-local derivatives in vector-valued L p L_{p} spaces with A p A_{p} weights. To achieve this, we begin by introducing a generalized real interpolation method. Within the framework of generalized interpolation theory, we make use of stochastic process theory and two-weight Hardy’s inequality to derive our trace and extension theorems. Our results encompass findings applicable to time-fractional equations with broad temporal weight functions.

1.
Introduction and Main results

In this article, we are interested in the trace and extension theorem for the following evolution equations in vector-valued
L_{p}
spaces with
A_{p}
weights:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t)=f(t),\quad u(0)=u_{0},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\kappa}u(t)=f(t),\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

(1.2)

Here,
\partial_{t}^{\kappa}
stands for a (generalized) time-fractional derivative with a kernel
\kappa
given by

\partial_{t}^{\kappa}u(t):=\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)(u(s)-u(0))~\mathrm{d}s\right).

Note that
\partial_{t}^{\kappa}
becomes the Caputo fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
for
\kappa(t)=t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function.

Our trace theorems also hold for the following Volterra-type equations:

\int_{0}^{t}(u(s)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\varkappa(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(1.3)

Under our assumptions on kernel
\kappa
, the evolution equation (
1.2
) can be rewritten as the Volterra-type equation (
1.3
),
where
\varkappa
is a kernel that is uniquely determined by
\kappa
.
We discuss the equivalence at the end of Section
2.2
.

In the studies of trace theorems, interpolation theory is mainly used in the literature.
That is, one expects to obtain the following inequality:

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},t^{\gamma_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},t^{\gamma_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}.

(1.4)

Note that
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
is an interpolation space whose norm is given for a functional
K
by

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta p}K\left(t,u_{0};X_{0},X_{1}\right)^{p}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/p},\quad\theta\in(0,1),\,\,p\in[1,\infty).

(1.5)

Precise information for
K
and other interpolation methods are given in Section
3
.
The idea of trace inequalities in the literature is that one can express the functional
K
as certain quantities of
\|u\|_{X_{0}}
and
\|f\|_{X_{1}}
,
and the remained term
t^{-\theta p}
contributes to a time weight in the right-hand side of (
1.4
).
For detailed argument, we recommend
[
1
,
14
,
25
]
and references therein.

From the perspective of the classical approaches, to obtain (
1.4
) in terms of general temporal weights,
it is natural to consider a generalization of
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
-interpolation in the sense that we put
\phi(t^{-1})
rather than just
t^{-\theta}
. One of the novelties of this paper is that we suggest a function class,
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
, introduced in
[
12
]
for such
\phi
as well as construct general interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\phi,p}
, whose norm is given by

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\phi,p}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi\left(t^{-1}\right)^{p}K\left(t,u_{0};X_{0},X_{1}\right)^{p}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/p},\quad\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1).

(1.6)

The definition of
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
and its properties are given in the first part of Section
2
, and we explain the interpolation in Section
3
.
For short introduction of
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
, we note that
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
if and only if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\lambda^{a+\varepsilon}\lesssim\frac{\phi(\lambda t)}{\phi(t)}\lesssim\lambda^{b-\varepsilon},\quad\forall t>0,\,\,\lambda\geq 1.

The advantage of the general interpolation is that we can handle not only trace inequalities for time local equations (
1.1
) but also time non-local equations (
1.2
), including time-fractional derivatives for broader weight classes than those in the literature.
To the best of our knowledge, our result is new even for the local derivative case, in the sense that trace and extension theorems hold with Muckenhoupt’s
A_{p}
-weight classes.

Before introducing the main results of this paper, we give a brief overview of the literature related to initial value problems in (weighted) Sobolev spaces with or without non-local derivatives.
The initial value problem of parabolic equations in
L_{p}
-Sobolev spaces with local time derivatives has been studied for a long time, and trace (and extension) theorem with or without power-type temporal weights can be found in, for example, Weidemaier
[
26
]
, Prüss
[
22
]
, Lindemulder and Veraar
[
17
]
and references therein.
In the case of the parabolic equations with non-local derivatives, Zacher
[
27
,
28
]
obtained an unweighted
L_{p}
-theory for the Volterra-type equations, including the time-fractional heat equations.
In particular, the kernel
\varkappa
of (
1.3
) considered in
[
27
,
28
]
belongs to a certain class, and its Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}[\varkappa]
satisfies some growth conditions near zero and infinity, in which case the solution space for (
1.3
) is given as the vector-valued Bessel potential spaces
H_{p}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1/p
.
See
[
21
]
for a detailed description of kernel
\kappa
of

and solution spaces used in
[
27
,
28
]
.
In Meyries and Schnaubelt
[
18
]
, Meyries and Veraar
[
19
]
, Agresti, Lindemulder, and Veraar
[
1
]
, the initial trace results in
[
27
,
28
]
were extended to the case of vector-valued Triebel-Lizorkin spaces containing power-type temporal weight:

\text{$F_{p,q}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X_{0})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X_{1})$, $w_{\gamma}(t)=|t|^{\gamma}$},

where
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
and
\alpha>(1+\gamma)/p
.
While the aforementioned results are basically based on the semigroup approach, Dong and Kim
[
7
]
, Dong and Liu
[
8
]
, and Kim and Woo
[
14
]
use different approaches to solve initial value problems for time-fractional parabolic equations and derive initial trace estimates for solutions.
Particularly, the authors in
[
14
]
construct new solution spaces suitable for discussing the initial value problem of time-fractional evolution equations and investigate the initial behavior of the solution using an appropriate representation via standard mollification.
The trace estimates in
[

are partially consistent with Theorem

in this paper. (See Remark

For a certain type of evolution equation with local derivatives, Choi, Kim, and Lee

We emphasize that our main results allow one to consider

1.1.
Main results

The main results of this paper are trace and extension theorems that identify the optimal function space for the initial conditions of equations in Sobolev spaces with
A_{p}
weights.
Notably, the initial data spaces are closely related to the temporal weights and the properties of local or non-local time derivatives.

We introduce trace theorems with local and non-local derivatives, respectively.
Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple and

X_{0}+X_{1}
be a Banach space with the norm

\|a\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}:=\inf\{\|a_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+\|a_{1}\|_{X_{1}}:a=a_{0}+a_{1},\,a_{0}\in X_{0},\,a_{1}\in X_{1}\}.

Theorem 1.1

(Trace theorem with local derivative; half line)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
. Suppose that
u\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})
,
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})
,
u_{0}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
, and

u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s

(1.7)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}
and

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}}\lesssim_{p,[w]_{A_{p}}}\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})},

(1.8)

where
W(t):=\int_{0}^{t}w(s)~\mathrm{d}s
.

For (
1.2
), assume that
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a right-continuous decreasing function with
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
.
Note that
\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
contains the kernel
t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
generating the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Let us denote

\kappa^{\ast}(t):=\kappa^{-1}\big(1/t\big).

(1.9)

Here,
\kappa^{-1}
is a generalized inverse of
\kappa
defined in (
2.19
).
Then the following theorem holds:

Theorem 1.2

(Trace theorem with non-local derivative; half line)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
. Suppose that
W\circ\kappa^{*}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,p)
,
u\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})
,
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})
,
u_{0}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
, and

\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\left(u(s)-u_{0}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.10)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}
and

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}}\lesssim_{p,[w]_{A_{p}},\kappa}\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}.

(1.11)

Remark 1.3
.

If
\kappa
is a strictly decreasing continuous function, then the assumption
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
and
W\circ\kappa^{\ast}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,p)
can be replaced by the following: there exist constants
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
and
0<b_{1}<b_{2}<p
such that for any
t>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
,

\lambda^{a_{1}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\kappa(t)}{\kappa(\lambda t)}\right)^{a_{2}}\lesssim\frac{W(\lambda t)}{W(t)}\lesssim\left(\frac{\kappa(t)}{\kappa(\lambda t)}\right)^{b_{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{b_{1}}.

For this, see Lemma
2.3
-
(vi)
.

For the Volterra-type equations (
1.3
), it is shown in Section
2.2
that
u
and
f
of (
1.3
) satisfy (
1.10
).
Thus (
1.11
) also holds for
u
and
f
of (
1.3
).
The same is true for Theorem
1.6
.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we assume that
u
(and
f
) is defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, but if
X_{0}
is continuously embedded into
X_{1}
,
i.e.
,
X_{0}\subset X_{1}
, we also obtain trace estimates of
u
defined on a finite time interval
(0,T)
.
See Section
5
for this.

Another novelty is that we construct
u
and
f
satisfying (
1.10
) by means of probability theory, which is motivated by spectral theory.
Regarding the case of (
1.1
), it is natural to consider its fundamental solution as one-parameter semigroups.
Then together with generalized interpolation theory given in Section
3
, we have the following result:

Theorem 1.5

(Extension theorem with local derivative)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}
, and
a\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}
.
Then there exist
u\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})
and
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})

such that
u(0)=a
and

u(t)=a+\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Furthermore,

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}\lesssim_{p,[w]_{A_{p}}}\|a\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}}.

Even if we have a non-local kernel
\kappa
as in (
1.2
), we can accomplish a similar result.
It is because the kernel
\kappa
corresponds to a Bernstein function
\phi
, and
\phi
yields a subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
whose Laplace exponent is
\phi
.
Then one can establish a solution to (
1.2
) by making use of the subordinator
S
.
This procedure is given in the second part of Section
2
, and particularly in Proposition
2.12
.
Finally, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.6

(Extension theorem with non-local derivative)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
. Suppose that
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
,
W\circ\kappa^{*}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,p)
, and
a\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}
.
Then there exist
u\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})
and
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})

such that

\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\left(t-s\right)\left(u(s)-a\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(1.12)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
u(0)=a
. Furthermore,

\|u\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}\lesssim_{p,[w]_{A_{p}},\kappa,W\circ\kappa^{*}}\|a\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}}

(1.13)

Note that (
1.13
) in Theorem
1.6
depends on
W\circ\kappa^{*}
while (
1.11
) in Theorem
1.2
does not.
The difference arises because equivalence of
\|a\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}^{K}}
and
\|a\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{*})^{1/p},p}^{J}}
depends on
W\circ\kappa^{*}
, and we use
K
and
J
methods to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
, respectively.
In other words, the trace and extension theorems with non-local derivatives essentially depend on
p
,
[w]_{A_{p}}
and
\kappa
.

Remark 1.7
.

In this paper, while the results on the local derivative (Theorem
1.1
, Corollary
5.1
and Theorem
1.5
) hold for any

weights, the results on the non-local derivatives (Theorems

.
In particular, if we consider the Caputo fractional derivative

in (

,

and

hold for

with
-1<\gamma<p-1
and
\gamma+1<p\alpha
, which is a special case of main results in
[
1
]
and
[
14
]
.

Indeed, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
q\in(1,\infty)
, and
w(t)=|t|^{\gamma}
where
\gamma\in(-1,q-1)
and
1+\gamma<q\alpha
. Note that
w\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
.
If we take
\kappa(t)=\big(\Gamma(1-\alpha)\big)^{-1}t^{-\alpha}
in the definition of
I^{\kappa}
(see (
2.12
)), for sufficiently smooth function
u=u(t,x)
defined on
[0,\infty)\times\Omega
,

I^{\kappa}u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\big(u(s,x)-u(0,x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s=I^{1-\alpha}\big(u(\cdot,x)-u(0,x)\big)(t),

where

stands for the classical fractional integral,

, the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral.
Also let
X_{0}=H_{p}^{2}
and
X_{1}=L_{p}
,

, where
H_{p}^{2}
is a usual Sobolev space for the second order PDEs.
By the choice of
\kappa
, it is clear that

and

\kappa^{\ast}(t)=\big(\Gamma(1-\alpha)\big)^{1/\alpha}t^{1/\alpha}.

Moreover, since

, we also have
(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})(\lambda)\simeq\lambda^{(1+\gamma)/\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,q)
by the choice of
\alpha
,
q
, and
\gamma
. Then by Theorem
1.2
,

u(0,\cdot)\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})^{1/q},q}=(H_{p}^{2},L_{p})_{(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})^{1/q},q}

where

.
On the other hand, by Proposition

(

(H_{p}^{2},L_{p})_{(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})^{1/q},q}=B_{p,q}^{(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})^{1/q}(2,0)}
.
That is, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{(W\circ\kappa^{\ast})^{1/q}(0,2)}}\,

\displaystyle=\|S_{0}f\|_{p}+\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{2qj}\big(W\circ\kappa^{\ast}\big)(2^{-2j})\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Thus,
u(0,\cdot)\in B_{p,q}^{2-\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{q\alpha}}
and this coincides with
[
14
, Theorem 3.11]
for

in there. The extension part can be dealt with similar way.

We also remark that if we take

and

becomes a weighted Besov space with variable smoothness, which is characterized by means of difference operators.
For more detail, see
[

]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Functions of type
\mathcal{I}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
and their properties

We introduce a class
\mathcal{I}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
.
In particular, functions in
\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
are essential in a generalized interpolation introduced in Section
3
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define

s_{\phi}(\lambda)=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\phi(\lambda t)}{\phi(t)}\,,

so that
s_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow(0,\infty]
.
Observe that
s_{\phi}
is
submultiplicative
,
i.e
.,
s_{\phi}(\lambda\tau)\leq s_{\phi}(\lambda)s_{\phi}(\tau)
for
\lambda,\tau>0
.
Together with the definition of
s_{\phi}(\lambda)
we introduce the following class of functions. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(a,b):=\{\phi:\text{$s_{\phi}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{a})$ as $\lambda\to 0$, and $s_{\phi}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{b})$ as $\lambda\to\infty$}\}.

(2.1)

We also define
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
, where
o
denotes that we replace
O
-notation with
o
-notation in (
2.1
). Clearly,
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)\subsetneq\mathcal{I}(a,b)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Due to
1=s_{\phi}(1)\leq s_{\phi}(\lambda)\,s_{\phi}(\lambda^{-1})
, we obtain that
\mathcal{I}(a,b)=\emptyset
for
a>b
, and
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)=\emptyset
for
a\geq b
.
In addition, for
a<b
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
if and only if there exists a bounded function
K_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

s_{\phi}(\lambda)\leq\left(\lambda^{a}+\lambda^{b}\right)K_{\phi}(\lambda)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}K_{\phi}(\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}K_{\phi}(\lambda)=0.

See, for example,
[
12
, Proposition 1.1]
.

The following lemma provides useful information about the classes
\mathcal{I}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}(a,b)
.

(i)

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
t^{\alpha}\phi(t)\in\mathcal{I}(a+\alpha,b+\alpha)
.

(ii)

For
\alpha\geq 0
,
\phi(t^{\alpha}),\phi(t)^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}(\alpha a,\alpha b)
.

(iii)

For
\alpha\leq 0
,
\phi(t^{\alpha}),\phi(t)^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}(\alpha b,\alpha a)
.

(iv)

Let
a,b>0
and
\phi
be strictly increasing, continuous, and

\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\infty}\phi(\lambda)=\infty.

Then its inverse
\phi^{-1}
is of class
\mathcal{I}(\frac{1}{b},\frac{1}{a})
.

(v)

For
a<c
,
b>d
,
\mathcal{I}(c,d)\subset\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
and

\displaystyle\bigcup_{c>a,\,d<b}\mathcal{I}(c,d)=\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b).

(2.2)

(vi)

\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
if and only if there exists
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\lambda^{a+\varepsilon}\lesssim\frac{\phi(\lambda t)}{\phi(t)}\lesssim\lambda^{b-\varepsilon}\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\lambda\geq 1.

(2.3)

(vii)

If

(2.4)

(viii)

If
p\geq 0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-p,0)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1\wedge\frac{t}{x}\right)^{p}\phi(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\lesssim\phi(x),

(ix)

For

, the assertions

also hold for
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
instead of
\mathcal{I}(a,b)

Proof.

(i)
–

and
(ix)
follows from direct computations.

For
(iv)
, first, we observe that there exists a constant

such that

\phi(\lambda t)\leq C\lambda^{a}\phi(t)

for

and
\lambda\in(0,1]
.
On the other hand, for

, by the assumption for
\phi

such that

. Then, again by the assumption for
\phi

\lambda\phi^{-1}(x/C)\leq\phi^{-1}(\lambda^{a}x)
, and hence

\frac{\phi^{-1}(x/(C\lambda^{a}))}{\phi^{-1}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}

for all

and
\lambda\in(0,1]

This certainly implies that

as

.
Similarly, we also have

as

.

(v)
Clearly,
\mathcal{I}(c,d)\subset\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
for

Therefore, it suffices to show that “

). Let

.
Then for

such that

, which yields

\displaystyle s_{\phi}\left(\lambda\right)\leq\left(s_{\phi}\left(2^{-N}\right)\right)^{k-1}s_{\phi}\left(2^{N\left(k-1\right)}\lambda\right)\leq 2^{-k}\lambda^{a}\leq 2\lambda^{a+\frac{1}{N}}

(2.5)

by the submultiplicativity. Similarly, for

such that

, which yields

\displaystyle s_{\phi}\left(\lambda\right)\leq\left(s_{\phi}\left(2^{N}\right)\right)^{l-1}s_{\phi}\left(2^{-N\left(l-1\right)}\lambda\right)\leq 2^{-l}\lambda^{b}\leq 2\lambda^{b-\frac{1}{N}}.

By (

) and (

),

. This proves (

).

(vi)
This is a direct result of

and its proof with Remark

.

(vii)
By

and

, there exists

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1\wedge\frac{x}{t}\right)^{p}\frac{\phi(t)}{\phi(x)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\cdots\,\,+\int_{x}^{\infty}\cdots

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{x}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)^{\varepsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}+\int_{x}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)^{p}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)^{p-\varepsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\lesssim 1.

. Using

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

-

Proof.

(i)

-

2.2.
Volterra-type equations associated with Bernstein functions

In this subsection, we give a connection between a kernel
\kappa
of non-local derivative in the evolution equation (
1.2
) and a Bernstein function in probability theory.
Moreover, we use properties of Bernstein functions to construct
u
and
f
satisfying (
1.10
).

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a right-continuous decreasing function with
\kappa(0+)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\kappa(t)=\infty
and
\kappa(\infty)=0
.
Then there exists a unique non-negative measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\kappa(s)-\kappa(t)=\mu\left((s,t]\right),

(2.10)

for
0<s<t<\infty
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1\wedge t)\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s)\mathrm{d}s\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mu(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\kappa(0+)=\infty.

(2.11)

We will consider an evolution equation

I^{\kappa}u(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\left(u(s)-u(0)\right)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\,,

(2.12)

or for simplicity, we just denote (
2.12
) by
\partial_{t}^{\kappa}u=f
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
, then by Lemma
2.3
-(
vi
), there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

s^{-\varepsilon}\kappa(1)\lesssim\kappa(s)\lesssim s^{-1+\varepsilon}\kappa(1)

for
s\leq 1
and

\kappa(r)\lesssim r^{-\varepsilon}\kappa(1)

for
r\geq 1
(see (
2.3
)). Therefore, we have

\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s)\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\kappa(t)=\infty,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\kappa(t)=0.

Definition 2.6
.

An infinitely differentiable function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a Bernstein function if

(-1)^{n}D^{n}\phi(\lambda)\leq 0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\,\lambda\in(0,\infty).

It is well known (
e.g.

[
24
, Theorem 3.2]
) that Bernstein function
\phi
has a unique representation

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda)=a+b\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\right)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t),

(2.13)

where
a,b\geq 0
and

\mu
is a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1\wedge t\right)\mu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty.

(2.14)

In (
2.13
),
a
,
b
, and
\mu
are called the
killing term
,
drift
, and
Lévy measure
of
\phi
, respectively.
Furthermore, the triplet
(a,b,\mu)
, which determines a Bernstein function
\phi
uniquely, is called the
Lévy triplet
of
\phi
.
In this article, we only consider Bernstein functions without killing term,
i.e.

\phi(0+)=0
.
Indeed, it is well-known the connection between Bernstein functions without killing term and subordinators. Before its description, we first give definitions of subordinators and convolution semigroup of probability measures.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a real-valued Lévy process defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. We say
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
subordinator
if
\mathbb{P}(S_{t}\geq 0,\,\forall t\geq 0)=1
. A
convolution semigroup of probability measures
on
[0,\infty)
is a family of probability measures
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the following properties;

(i)

For all
t\geq 0
,
\mu_{t}([0,\infty))=1
.

(ii)

For all
t,s\geq 0
,
\mu_{t}\ast\mu_{s}=\mu_{t+s}
, where

\mu_{t}\ast\mu_{s}(B):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{B}(t^{\prime}+s^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}t^{\prime})\mu_{s}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}).

(iii)

For every compactly supported continuous functions
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t^{\prime})\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}t^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t^{\prime})\varepsilon_{0}(\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}),

where
\varepsilon_{0}
is the centered Dirac measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

For the definition and properties of Lévy processes, see
e.g.

[
23
]
. Now we give a short description for the connection between Bernstein functions without killing term and subordinators.

[
24
, Theorem 5.2]
provides one-to-one correspondence between Bernstein functions and convolution semigroups of sub-probability measures in the sense that

\int_{[0,\infty)}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}r)=\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(\lambda)},\quad\forall(t,\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Here, convolution semigroups of sub-probability measures implies Definition
2.7
with
\mu_{t}([0,\infty))=1
replaced by
\mu_{t}([0,\infty))\leq 1
.
However, the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 5.2]
includes that there is one-to-one correspondence between Bernstein functions without killing term and convolution semigroups of probability measures.
Similarly, from
[
24
, Proposition 5.5]
, we also obtain the fact that every law of subordinators is a convolution semigroup of probability measures on
[0,\infty)
.
Combining these results, for a given subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, there exists a Bernstein function
\phi
such that
\phi(0+)=0
and

\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}(\omega)}\,\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=:\right)\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}}]=\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(\lambda)},\quad\forall(t,\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(2.15)

For the converse, we observe that any convolution semigroups of probability measures on
[0,\infty)
is infinitely divisible (see e.g.
[
23
, Definition 7.1]
). Then by
[
23
, Theorem 7.10]
, there exists a real-valued Lévy process
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
(\mu_{1})^{t}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(S_{t}\in\cdot)
, where
(\mu_{1})^{t}
is a probability measure satisfying

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{iz\cdot r}(\mu_{1})^{t}(\mathrm{d}r)=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{iz\cdot r}\mu_{1}(\mathrm{d}r)\right)^{t},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}.

If
t
is a positive rational number, then by Definition
2.7

(ii)
,
(\mu_{1})^{t}=\mu_{t}
. Using the density argument and Definition
2.7

(iii)
,
(\mu_{1})^{t}=\mu_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
. Hence
\mu_{t}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(S_{t}\in\cdot)
. Since the support of
\mu_{t}
is contained in
[0,\infty)
, the state space of
S
is
[0,\infty)
. This certainly implies that
S
is a subordinator. Therefore for a given Bernstein function
\phi
without killing term, there exists a subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying (
2.15
). Summarizing these facts,

Subordinators

\displaystyle\rightleftharpoons\text{Convolution semigroups of probability measures on }[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\rightleftharpoons\text{Bernstein functions without killing term}.

Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between Bernstein functions without killing term and subordinators, we say a subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
subordinator with Laplace exponent

\phi
if (
2.15
) holds.

Let

The following lemma shows that the second assertion of (
2.11
) is equivalent to a subordinator
S
being strictly increasing.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinator with Laplace exponent

\phi(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})\mu(\mathrm{d}t),

i.e., (
2.15
) holds for
(t,\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
\mu
is a nonnegative measure defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with (
2.14
). Then
\mu(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\infty
if and only if
S
is strictly increasing almost surely.

Proof.

We only give a sketch of the proof.
By the definition of subordinator,
S
is strictly increasing almost surely if and only if

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\cup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\\
p>q\end{subarray}}\{S_{p}-S_{q}=0\}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\\
p>q\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{p}-S_{q}=0\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\\
p>q\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{p-q}=0\right)=0.

(2.20)

Also (
2.20
) is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}(S_{t}=0)=0
for any
t>0
, which means that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(\lambda)}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}}]=\mathbb{P}(S_{t}=0)+\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{S_{t}>0\}}]

for any
t>0
. Since
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{S_{t}>0\}}]=0
by the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
\mathbb{P}(S_{t}=0)=0
is equivalent to
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(\lambda)}=0
.
This proves that
S
is strictly increasing almost surely if and only if
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\phi(\lambda)=\infty
, that is,
\mu(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\infty
.
∎

N\geq 1

\displaystyle\lambda\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\phi(\lambda)\leq N\lambda\phi^{\prime}(\lambda).

(2.23)

In particular,
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
.

(iv)

(
[
4
, Proposition 3.3.(i)]
) Suppose that
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
.
Then there are constant
c_{1},\,c_{2}>0
such that for all
r,t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(S_{r}\geq t\big(1+\mathrm{e}^{r\phi(1/t)}\big)\Big)\leq c_{1}r\phi(1/t)

(2.24)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-c_{2}r\phi(1/t)}.

(2.25)

In particular, for each
L
, there exist constants
c_{1,L},c_{2,L}>0
such that for all
r\phi(1/t)\leq L
,

\displaystyle c_{1,L}r\phi(1/t)\leq\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\leq c_{2,L}r\phi(1/t).

(2.26)

The constants
c_{1},c_{2}
,
c_{1,L}
and
c_{2,L}
in (
2.24
)–(
2.26
) depends only on
K_{\phi}
in Remark
2.2
.

The following lemma helps us to prove Theorem
1.6
by constructing appropriate
u
and
f
satisfying (
1.2
).

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a right-continuous decreasing function with
\kappa(0+)=\infty
,
\kappa(\infty)=0
, and
\kappa\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(-1,0)
. We denote
\mu
a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by
\kappa
such that
\mu
satisfies
\kappa(s)-\kappa(t)=\mu\left(\left[s,t\right)\right)
for
0<s<t<\infty
,
and

\Theta(t,\lambda):=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a subordinator with Laplace exponent

\phi(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t})\mu(\mathrm{d}t).

(i)

For
t,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the function
\Theta
satisfies

\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\big(\Theta(s,\lambda)-\Theta(0,\lambda)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s=:I^{\kappa}\Theta(t,\lambda)=-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\Theta(s,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}s,

that is,

\partial_{t}^{\kappa}\Theta(t,\lambda)=-\lambda\Theta(t,\lambda),

where
\kappa(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{(x,\infty)}\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
.

(ii)

For
t,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\Theta(t,\lambda)\simeq 1\wedge\frac{\phi(t^{-1})}{\lambda}\,,

where the equivalence depends only on
K_{\phi}
in Remark
2.2
.

Proof.

First, note that for fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)
is (Borel) measurable with respect to
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
by the definition of subordinator, so
\Theta(t,\lambda)
is well-defined.

(i)
It is clear that
\kappa
and
\mu
satisfy
\kappa(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{(x,\infty)}\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
and
\Theta(0,\lambda)=1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle I^{\kappa}\big(\Theta(\cdot,\lambda)\big)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\big(\Theta(s,\lambda)-1\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}r-K(t),

where
K(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\mathrm{d}s
.
From (
2.22
), it is easily seen that

I^{\kappa}\big(\Theta(\cdot,\lambda)\big)(t)=-\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\mathbb{E}[K(t-S_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq S_{r}\}}]\mathrm{d}r.

On the other hand, (
2.21
) and Fubini’s theorem implies that

\int_{0}^{t}\Theta(s,\lambda)\mathrm{d}s=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\mathbb{E}[K(t-S_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq S_{r}\}}]\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore, we prove
\partial_{t}^{\kappa}\Theta(t,\lambda)=-\lambda\Theta(t,\lambda)
for
t,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

(ii)
Due to Proposition
2.8
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
. Then by (
2.25
), there exists a constant
c_{2}=c_{2}(K_{\phi})=c_{2}(\kappa)>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-c_{2}r\phi(t^{-1})}
and then

\Theta(t,\lambda)\geq\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-c_{2}r\phi(t^{-1})}\right)\mathrm{d}r=1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+c_{2}\phi(t^{-1})}.

Obviously, one can take
c_{2}<1
in the above estimate.
Then since
1\wedge a\simeq a/(1+a)
for
a>0
and

\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+c_{2}\phi(t^{-1})}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\lambda}{c_{2}\phi(t^{-1})}+1\lesssim_{c_{2}}\frac{\lambda}{\phi(t^{-1})}+1,

we obtain
\Theta(t,\lambda)\gtrsim_{c_{2}}1\wedge\frac{\phi(t^{-1})}{\lambda}
.

Now, we prove the opposite inequality.
Divide
\Theta
into two parts;

\Theta(t,\lambda)=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{2/\phi(t^{-1})}\cdots+\int_{2/\phi(t^{-1})}^{\infty}\cdots=:I_{1}(t,\lambda)+I_{2}(t,\lambda).

By taking
L=2
in (
2.26
), there exists a constant
c_{2,L}=c_{2,L}(K_{\phi})=c_{2,L}(\kappa)>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\leq c_{2,L}r\phi(t^{-1})
for any
r
and
t
satisfying
r\phi(t^{-1})\leq 2(=L)
. Then,

I_{1}(t,\lambda)\leq c_{2,L}\phi(t^{-1})\lambda\int_{0}^{2/\phi(t^{-1})}r\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\,\mathrm{d}r.

By direct calculation, one can check that

\lambda\int_{0}^{2/\phi(t^{-1})}r\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\wedge\frac{2}{\phi(t^{-1})},

and hence
I_{1}(t,\lambda)\lesssim_{c_{2,L}}1\wedge\frac{\phi(t^{-1})}{\lambda}
.
For
I_{2}
, since
\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)\leq 1
for all
r,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

I_{2}(t,\lambda)\leq\lambda\int_{2/\phi(t^{-1})}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\mathrm{d}r=\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda/\phi(t^{-1})}\lesssim 1\wedge\frac{\phi(t^{-1})}{\lambda},

where we use the fact that
\mathrm{e}^{-t}\lesssim 1\wedge t^{-1}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We obtain
\Theta(t,\lambda)\lesssim_{c_{2,L}}1\wedge\frac{\phi(t^{-1})}{\lambda}
.
The proposition is proved.
∎

We end this section by showing the equivalence of two equations:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)(u(s)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Longleftrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}(u(s)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\varkappa(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s,

(2.27)

where
\varkappa(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\leq t)\,\mathrm{d}r
and
S_{r}
is a subordinator introduced in Proposition
2.11
.
By Proposition
2.11
,

\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\varkappa(\mathrm{d}s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\kappa(t-S_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{t\geq S_{r}}]\mathrm{d}r=\lim_{r\to{\infty}}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq t)=1,

(2.28)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\varkappa(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\leq s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[K(t-S_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq S_{r}\}}]\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{t}\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{r}\geq l)\mathrm{d}l=t,

where
K(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\mathrm{d}s
.
Taking the integration to the left equation in (
2.27
) with respect to
\varkappa(\mathrm{d}s)
, and making use of Fubini’s theorem and (
2.28
), we have the right equation in (
2.27
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(u(l)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}l

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t-l}\kappa(t-l-s)\varkappa(\mathrm{d}s)\right)(u(l)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}l

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-s}\kappa(t-s-l)(u(l)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}l\,\varkappa(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-s}f(l)\mathrm{d}l\,\varkappa(\mathrm{d}s)=\int_{0}^{t}\varkappa(t-l)f(l)\mathrm{d}l.

Similarly, taking the non-local derivative
\partial_{t}^{\kappa}
to the right equation in (
2.27
), and making use of Fubini’s theorem and (
2.29
), we have the left equation in (
2.27
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-l)(u(l)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}l

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}K(t-l)(u(l)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}l\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{\kappa}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(u(s)-u_{0})\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{\kappa}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\varkappa(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(t-s)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\varkappa(s-l)f(l)\mathrm{d}l\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-l)f(l)\mathrm{d}l\right)=\int_{0}^{t}f(l)\mathrm{d}l.

3.
Generalized real interpolation

In this section, we present a generalized real interpolation in the sense that we put
\phi(t^{-1})
rather than
t^{-\theta}
in (
1.5
).
The function
\phi
is chosen to be of class
\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
introduced in the first part of Section
2
.
Among various interpolation methods,
K
and
J
methods are our main focus.

Definition 3.1
.

Given two Banach spaces
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
, we say that an ordered pair
(A_{0},A_{1})
is an interpolation couple if both
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are continuously embedded in the same Hausdorff topological vector space
Z
.

It can be easily checked that the two subspaces of
Z

\begin{gathered}A_{0}\cap A_{1}:=\{a\in Z:a\in A_{0},\,a\in A_{1}\},\\
A_{1}+A_{1}:=\{a\in Z:a=a_{0}+a_{1},\,a_{0}\in A_{0},\,a_{1}\in A_{1}\}\end{gathered}

are Banach spaces with the norm

\begin{gathered}\|a\|_{A_{0}\cap A_{1}}:=\max(\|a\|_{A_{0}},\|a\|_{A_{1}}),\\
\|a\|_{A_{0}+A_{1}}:=\inf\{\|a_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+\|a_{1}\|_{A_{1}}:a=a_{0}+a_{1},\,a_{0}\in A_{0},\,a_{1}\in A_{1}\}.\end{gathered}

Through this section, we assume that
(A_{0},A_{1})
is an interpolation couple,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

For
t>0
we define

\displaystyle K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1}):=\inf\{\|a_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+t\|a_{1}\|_{A_{1}}:a=a_{0}+a_{1},a_{0}\in A_{0},a_{1}\in A_{1}\},

\displaystyle J(t,a;A_{0},A_{1}):=\max_{a\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}}(\|a\|_{A_{0}},t\|a\|_{A_{1}}).

Note that
K
and
J
are merely the functional used in
K
-method and
J
-method of the classical interpolation theory, respectively.
For measurable functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, the functional
\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(F)
is defined by

\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(F):=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\phi(t^{-1})F(t)\right)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/p}\quad&\textrm{if}\quad p\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{t>0}\phi(t^{-1})F(t)\quad&\textrm{if}\quad p=\infty.\end{cases}

The space
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}
is defined by

\begin{gathered}(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}:=\{a\in A_{0}+A_{1}:\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}:=\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(K(\cdot,a;A_{0},A_{1}))<\infty\}.\end{gathered}

The space
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
is defined by

\begin{gathered}(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}:=\{a\in A_{0}+A_{1}:\|a\|_{(A_{1},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}}:=\inf_{v}\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(J(\cdot,v(\cdot);A_{0},A_{1}))<\infty\},\end{gathered}

where the infimum is over all measurable functions
v:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to A_{0}\cap A_{1}
satisfying

a=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

We provide concrete examples of
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}
and
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
in Appendix
A
.

By
\ell_{p}
, we denote the set of all real sequences
a=(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
\|a\|_{\ell_{p}}<\infty
, where

\|a\|_{\ell_{p}}:=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\quad&\textrm{for}\quad p\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j}|\quad&\textrm{for}\quad p=\infty.\end{cases}

The following proposition suggests sequential expression for interpolation norms:

Proposition 3.2
.

(i)

For
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}
,

\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}\simeq_{\phi}\|\alpha^{\phi}_{K}(a)\|_{\ell_{p}},

where
\alpha^{\phi}_{K}(a):=\big(\alpha^{\phi}_{K,j}(a)\big)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}:=\big(\phi(2^{-j})K(2^{j},a;A_{0},A_{1})\big)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

(ii)

For
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
,

\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}}\simeq_{\phi}\inf_{u}\|\alpha^{\phi}_{J}(u)\|_{\ell_{p}},

where
\alpha^{\phi}_{J}(u):=(\alpha^{\phi}_{J,j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}:=\big(\phi(2^{-j})J(2^{j},u_{j};A_{0},A_{1})\big)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and the infimum is over all sequences
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset A_{0}\cap A_{1}
satisfying

a=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{j}\,\,\textrm{(convergence in }A_{0}+A_{1}).

Proof.

(i)
It can be easily checked that for
t\in[2^{j},2^{j+1}]
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

K(2^{j},a;A_{0},A_{1})\leq K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})\leq 2K(2^{j},a;A_{0},A_{1}).

Since
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
, by Lemma
2.3
-
(vi)
, we have

1\leq(2^{-j}t)^{\varepsilon}\lesssim\frac{\phi(2^{-j})}{\phi(t^{-1})}\lesssim(2^{-j}t)^{1-\varepsilon}\leq 2^{1-\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{for all}\,\,t\in[2^{j},2^{j+1}]\,,

i.e.
,

\phi(t^{-1})\simeq\phi(2^{-j})\quad\textrm{for all}\,\,t\in[2^{j},2^{j+1}].

(3.1)

This certainly implies that

\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}\simeq_{\phi}\|\alpha^{\phi}_{K}(a)\|_{\ell_{p}}.

(ii)
We only prove for
p\in[1,\infty)
since the proof of the case
p=\infty
is similar. Since
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
, there exists a measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to A_{0}\cap A_{1}
such that
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
. Put

u_{j}:=\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}u(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\in A_{0}\cap A_{1},

then
a=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{j}
. By the definition of
J
-functional and Minkowski’s inequality,

\alpha_{J,j}^{\phi}\leq\phi(2^{-j})\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\max(\|u\|_{A_{0}},t\|u\|_{A_{1}})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\lesssim\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\phi(t^{-1})J(t,u(t);A_{0},A_{1})\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t},

where we use (
3.1
) for
t\in[2^{j},2^{j+1}]
.
This implies that
\|\alpha^{\phi}_{J}(u)\|_{\ell_{p}}\lesssim_{\phi}\Phi^{\phi}_{p}\big(J(\cdot,u;A_{0},A_{1})\big)
, and then,

\inf_{u}\|\alpha^{\phi}_{J}(u)\|_{\ell_{p}}\lesssim_{\phi}\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}}.

For the converse, assume that there exists
u=(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq A_{0}\cap A_{1}
such that

\alpha_{J}^{\phi}(u)\in\ell_{p},\quad a=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{j}.

Set

v(t):=\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{j}\mathbbm{1}_{(2^{j},2^{j+1}]}(t)\in A_{0}\cap A_{1},

then
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
. Clearly,
v
is (Bochner) measurable. Therefore, by (
3.1
) for
t\in[2^{j},2^{j+1}]
again, we have

\displaystyle\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}}^{p}\leq\Phi^{\phi}_{p}\big(J(\cdot,v;A_{0},A_{1})\big)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\big(\phi(t^{-1})J(t,v(t);A_{0},A_{1})\big)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi(2^{-j})J(2^{j},u_{j};A_{0},A_{1}))^{p}=\|\alpha_{J}^{\phi}(u)\|_{\ell_{p}}^{p},

and taking the infimum in
u
, we have the result. The proposition is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
, there exists an infinitely differentiable function
v:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to A_{0}\cap A_{1}
such that
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative infinitely differentiable function with compact support in
(0,\infty)
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(s^{-1})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}=1.

(3.2)

Since
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
, there exists measurable
u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to A_{0}\cap A_{1}
such that
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
. Put

v(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)u\left(s\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

Clearly,
v
is an infinitely differentiable function from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
A_{0}\cap A_{1}
. By Fubini’s theorem (
e.g.

[
13
, Proposition 1.2.7.]
) and (
3.2
),

a=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
,

K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})\lesssim_{\phi,p}\frac{\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}}{\phi(t^{-1})}

Proof.

The case
p=\infty
is trivial, so we only prove the case
p\in[1,\infty)
.
By the definition of
K
-functional, for
t,s>0
,

K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})\leq\max(1,t/s)K(s,a:A_{0},A_{1}).

This also implies that

\min(1,s/t)K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})\leq K(s,a;A_{0},A_{1}).

Taking
\Phi^{\phi}_{p}
both sides with respect to
s
,

\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(\min(1,\cdot/t))K(t,a,;A_{0},A_{1})\leq\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}.

Therefore it suffices to prove that
\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(\min(1,\cdot/t))\gtrsim\phi(t^{-1})
, and this can be verified by Lemma
2.3
-
(vi)
since

\frac{\Phi^{\phi}_{p}(\min(1,\cdot/t))}{\phi(t^{-1})}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\phi(s^{-1})}{\phi(t^{-1})}\left(1\wedge\frac{s}{t}\right)\right|^{p}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right)^{1/p}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

We present two lemmas to show equivalence between
K
and
J
methods (Theorem
3.5
) and further properties of our generalized interpolation (Proposition
3.6
).

Theorem 3.5

(Equivalence theorem)
.

(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}
and
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
have same elements.
In addition, for any
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}\big(=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}\big)
,

\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}\simeq\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}}.

Proof.

The proof is comparable to the proof of classical real interpolation. However, for the sake of completeness, we present proof.
Let
a\in(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{J}
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, there exists an infinitely differentiable
v:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to A_{0}\cap A_{1}
such that
a=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
. Then by
[
2
, Lemma 3.2.1.]
,

\displaystyle K(t,a;A_{0},A_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}K(t,v(s);A_{0},A_{1})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(1,t/s)J(s,v(s);A_{0},A_{1})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(1,s^{-1})J(ts,v(ts);A_{0},A_{1})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

Since
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
, by Lemma
2.3
-
(vi)
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\frac{\phi(t^{-1}s)}{\phi(t^{-1})}\lesssim s^{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{s\leq 1}+s^{1-\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{s>1}.

(3.3)

Then by (
3.3
), for
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\|a\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,p}^{K}}

Definition 4.4
.

For

, the (uncentered) Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

is defined by

\mathcal{M}h(t):=\sup_{[a,b]\ni t}\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}|h(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

It is well-known that for

is bounded if and only if

.
Moreover,

\|\mathcal{M}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}t)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}t)}\lesssim_{p}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

For more detail, see
[
10
, Chapter 7]
.

Lemma 4.5

(

]

Let

and

Then

(4.4)

Moreover, if (

Lemma 4.6
.

For any measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
and
s>0
,

g(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-t}h(st)\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\mathcal{M}(h\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}})(s).

Here
\mathcal{M}
is the (uncentered) Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator defined in Definition
4.4
, and the inequality is independent of
h
and
s
.

Proof.

Observe that
g(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\ldots+\int_{1}^{\infty}\ldots
and

\int_{0}^{1}te^{-t}h(st)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{t}{s}e^{-t/s}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\frac{1}{2s}\int_{0}^{2s}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{M}(h\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}})(s).

On the other hand,

\int_{1}^{\infty}te^{-t}h(st)\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-t/2}h(st)\,\mathrm{d}t=2\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-x/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)h(st)\,\mathrm{d}t

\simeq\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{1}^{x}h(st)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)e^{-x/2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{xs}h(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\frac{e^{-x/2}}{s}\,\mathrm{d}x

5.

In this section, we assume that

As a corollary of Theorem

Corollary 5.1

(Trace theorem with local derivative; finite interval)
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}
.
Assume that
X_{0}
is continuously embedded into
X_{1}
, and
u\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})
,
f\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})
and
u_{0}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
satisfy that

u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

Then
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}
and

\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{W^{1/p},p}}\lesssim_{p,[w]_{A_{p}},N_{X_{0},X_{1}},T}\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}\,,

where
W(t):=\int_{0}^{t}w(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
.

Proof.

Take a nonnegative decreasing
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\zeta(s)=1
on
s\leq\frac{T}{2}
and
\zeta(s)=0
for all
s\geq T
.
Put
\widetilde{u}:=\zeta u
and
\widetilde{f}:=\zeta f+\zeta^{\prime}u
.
Since
X_{0}
is continuously embedded into
X_{1}
,

\begin{gathered}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}\leq\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})},\\
\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{1})}+N(N_{X_{0},X_{1}},T)\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;X_{0})}.\end{gathered}

Moreover

\widetilde{u}(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{f}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By applying Theorem
1.1
for
(u,f)
replaced by
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{f})
, the corollary is proved.
∎

\displaystyle A+B.

Estimate of

: Note that

\displaystyle A\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(W\circ\psi)(\tau^{-1})\left|\frac{\mathcal{L}[\|u\|_{X_{0}}+\|f\|_{X_{1}}](1/\psi(\tau^{-1}))}{\psi(\tau^{-1})}\right|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}

Estimate of

\displaystyle J^{p}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}(W\circ\psi)(\tau^{-1})\left|\frac{1}{\psi(\tau^{-1})^{2}}\left(\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{\psi(\tau^{-1})}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\kappa(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\right|^{p}~\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\leq N,

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(W\circ\psi)(\tau^{-1})\left|\frac{1}{\psi(\tau^{-1})^{2}}\left(\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{\psi(\tau^{-1})}}t\phi(t^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\right|^{p}~\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\tau}\right)^{1/p}

Examples of generalized real interpolation spaces

In this appendix, we present concrete examples of spaces
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\phi,p}
when
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are Sobolev or Besov spaces.
First, we recall the definitions of Sobolev and Besov spaces.

Definition A.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
w\in A_{p}
.
The sets
H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)
and
B_{p,q}^{\gamma}(w)
are Banach spaces equipped with norms given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\gamma}(w)}:=\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\left\|\left\{(2^{j\gamma}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\right\|_{\ell_{q}(\mathbb{N})},

where

\displaystyle(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\mathcal{F}[f]],\quad\Delta_{j}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\Psi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}[f]],\quad S_{0}:=1-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta_{j}.

Here
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms and
\Psi
is a nonnegaive infinitely smooth function satisfying
supp(\Psi)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we also denote the homogeneous spaces
\mathring{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(w)
and
\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\gamma}(w)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathring{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f\|_{L_{p}(w)},\quad\|f\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\gamma}(w)}:=\left\|\left\{(2^{j\gamma}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\ell_{q}(\mathbb{Z})}.

We also introduce a result of the generalized interpolation on
L_{p}
spaces which yields Orlicz-type spaces
L_{\phi,p}
.
To describe the Orlicz-type spaces, we need a definition of decreasing rearrangements of
f
.

Definition A.2
.

Let
X
be a measure space with a
\sigma
-finite positive measure
\mu
.

(i)

For a measurable function
f
on
X
,
d_{f}(\lambda):=\mu\big(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\lambda\}\big)
is called the distribution function of
f
.

(ii)

For a measurable function
f
on
X
, the function

f^{*}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty),\quad f^{\ast}(t):=\inf\{s>0:d_{f}(s)\leq t\},\quad t\in[0,\infty).

is called the decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

(iii)

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
,

denotes the set of all measurable functions satisfying

\|f\|_{L_{\phi,p}}:=\|\phi f^{*}\|_{L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)}<\infty.

Lemma B.1

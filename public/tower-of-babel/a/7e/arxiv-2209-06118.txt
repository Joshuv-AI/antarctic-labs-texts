Reduced relative quantum entropy
Frank Hansen

Abstract
We introduce the notion of reduced relative quantum entropy and prove that it is convex. This result is then used to give a simplified proof of a theorem of Lieb and Seiringer MSC2020 47A63, 15A45 Key words and phrases: Reduced relative quantum entropy; concavity theorems; Golden-Thompson’s trace inequality.



1
Reduced relative entropy

Let
H
be a contraction. We introduce the reduced relative quantum entropy
S_{H}(A,B)
for positive definite matrices
A
and
B
by setting

S_{H}(A\mid B)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(A\log A-H^{*}AH\log B-A+B\bigr).

Note that for
H=I
(the identity matrix) we recover the relative quantum entropy

S(A\mid B)=S_{I}(A\mid B)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(A\log A-A\log B-A+B\bigr).

Theorem 1.1
.

The reduced relative quantum entropy is convex.

Proof.

Let
H
be a contraction, and let
A
and
B
be positive definite matrices. By Lieb’s concavity theorem
[
6
, Corollary 1.1]
, cf. also
[
2
, Corollary 2.2]
, the function

(A,B)\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}\qquad 0\leq p\leq 1

is concave. The function

(A,B)\to\frac{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HH^{*}A}{p}

is therefore also concave for
0<p\leq 1.
By letting
p
tend to zero we obtain that the limit function

(A,B)\to\frac{d}{dp}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}\Bigr|_{p=0}

is concave. Since by calculation

\frac{d}{dp}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(HB^{p}\log(B)H^{*}A^{1-p}-HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}\log A\big)

we obtain

\frac{d}{dp}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ HB^{p}H^{*}A^{1-p}\Bigr|_{p=0}={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H\log(B)H^{*}A-HH^{*}A\log A\bigr).

The map

(1)

(A,B)\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H^{*}AH\log B-HH^{*}A\log A\bigr)

is thus concave, where we used the cyclicity of the trace. We then write the reduced quantum relative entropy on the form

\begin{array}[]{l}S_{H}(A\mid B)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(A\log A-H^{*}AH\log B-A+B\bigr)\\[8.61108pt]
=-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H^{*}AH\log B-HH^{*}A\log A\bigr)+{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl((I-HH^{*})A\log A-A+B\bigr).\end{array}

The first term is convex by concavity of the map in (
1
). The second term is
convex since
HH^{*}\leq I
and
A\to A\log A
is convex.

QED

The relative quantum entropy
S(A\mid B)
is non-negative with equality for
A=B.
Therefore,

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ B=\max_{X>0}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (X\log B-X\log X+X)

for any positive operator
B,
cf.
[
8
, Lemma 6]
. If we to a contraction
H
put

B=\exp\bigl(L+H^{*}\log(A)H\bigr),

where
L
is self-adjoint, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(L+H^{*}\log(A)H\bigr)\\[8.61108pt]
=\displaystyle\max_{X>0}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(X(L+H^{*}\log(A)H)-X\log X+X\bigr)\\[8.61108pt]
=\displaystyle\max_{X>0}\bigl\{-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (X\log X-HXH^{*}\log A-X+A)+{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (XL+A)\bigr\}\\[8.61108pt]
=\displaystyle\max_{X>0}\bigl\{-S_{H^{*}}(X\mid A)+{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (XL+A)\bigr\}.\end{array}

Since the function

(X,A)\to-S_{H^{*}}(X\mid A)+{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (XL+A)

is jointly concave, it follows by a well-known theorem
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
that the partial maximisation over
X
is concave. We have thus proved.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
H
be a contraction and
L
self-adjoint. The function

\varphi(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(L+H^{*}\log(A)H\bigr)

is concave in positive definite matrices.

Finally, we recover a theorem of Lieb and Seiringer
[
7
, Theorem 3]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
L
be a self-adjoint
n\times n
matrix and consider
m\times n
matrices
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
with

H_{1}^{*}H_{1}+\cdots+H_{k}^{*}H_{k}\leq I_{n}

where
I_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
unit matrix. The trace function

(2)

\varphi(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(L+H^{*}_{1}\log(A_{1})H_{1}+\cdots+H^{*}_{k}\log(A_{k})H_{k}\bigr)

is concave in
k
-tuples of positive definite
m\times m
matrices.

Proof.

We consider the
k\times k
block matrices

\hat{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&A_{k}\end{pmatrix},\,\hat{L}=\begin{pmatrix}L&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix},\,\hat{H}=\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
H_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
H_{k}&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

with zero matrices of suitable orders inserted and note that
H
is a contraction. Furthermore,

\hat{L}+\hat{H}^{*}(\log\hat{A})\hat{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle L+\sum_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}^{*}(\log A_{i})H_{i}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(\hat{L}+\hat{H}^{*}\log(\hat{A})\hat{H}\bigr)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\Bigl(L+\sum_{i=1}^{k}H^{*}_{i}\log(A_{i})H_{i}\Bigr)+(k-1)n

and the statement follows from the preceding Theorem.

QED

1.1
Interpolation between GT and Jensen

Note that the trace function in (
2
) becomes positively homogeneous if

H_{1}^{*}H_{1}+\cdots+H_{k}^{*}H_{k}=I_{n}\,.

Under this extra condition we may now recover the inequality

(3)

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\Bigl(L+\sum_{i=1}^{k}H^{*}_{i}B_{i}H_{i}\Bigr)\leq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(L)\sum_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}^{*}\exp(B_{i})H_{i}

for self-adjoint
n\times n
matrices
L
and self-adjoint
m\times m
matrices
B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
by using Corollary
1.3
and following the steps in
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
. This is for
m=n
the same bound as obtained when all the matrices commute. We are thus allowed to estimate partition functions or the Helmhotz function in quantum statistical mechanics and obtain bounds on the same form as they appear in classical physics.

Note that (
3
) reduces to the Golden-Thompson inequality for
k=1,

m=n,
and
H_{1}=I_{n}
and to convexity under the trace of the exponential function for
L=0.
The inequality may thus be considered as an interpolation inequality between Golden-Thompson’s inequality and Jensen’s trace inequality
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
, cf. also
[
4
]
. However, we cannot derive (
3
) from these special cases. If we first apply Golden-Thompson’s inequality then we obtain

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\Bigl(L+\sum_{i=1}^{k}H^{*}_{i}B_{i}H_{i}\Bigr)\leq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp\bigl(L)\exp\sum_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}^{*}B_{i}H_{i}

but this inequality is insufficient to obtain (
3
), since
L
is arbitrary and the exponential function is not operator convex.

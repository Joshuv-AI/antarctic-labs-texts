Contact seaweeds II: type C
Vincent E. Coll, Jr, Nicholas Russoniello

Abstract
This paper is a continuation of earlier work on the construction of contact forms on seaweed algebras. In the prequel to this paper, we show that every index-one seaweed subalgebra of A n − 1 = 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n ) A_{n-1}=\mathfrak{sl}(n) is contact by identifying contact forms that arise from Dougherty’s framework. We extend this result to include index-one seaweed subalgebras of C n = 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 2 ​ n ) C_{n}=\mathfrak{sp}(2n) . Our methods are graph-theoretic and combinatorial.

If

Remark 1
.

It was recently shown in
(
[
7
]
, 2023)
that an index-one seaweed is contact if and only if it is quasi-reductive. Combining this with a result of Panyushev

[
14

[

[

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we define seaweed algebras of type C and detail the construction of an associated planar graph: the seaweed’s
meander
. Using a combinatorial formula based on the number and type of connected components in the meander, one readily obtains the index of the corresponding seaweed. A collection of deterministic, graph-theoretic moves yield a “winding-down” procedure for a meander, revealing the meander’s
homotopy type

3

component meander
of the seaweed – which is used to identify the
core

peak
of the seaweed. These distinguished sets are certain sets of matrix locations, and they provide the grist for the Dougherty construction. Remarkably, when restricted to index-one, type-C seaweeds, the Dougherty construction yields regular forms that are also contact. Section
4
consists of the proof of the main result, which is then used – along with a convenient index formula for certain type-C seaweeds – in Section
5
to quickly construct infinite families of contact, type-C seaweeds.

Seaweeds and meanders

A seaweed subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
of

\mathfrak{gl}(n)
is defined by two compositions of
n
. The passage to seaweeds of “classical type” is realized by requiring that elements of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfy additional algebraic conditions. For example, the type-A case (
A_{n-1}=\mathfrak{sl}(n)
) is defined by a vanishing trace condition. A type-C seaweed in its “standard (matrix) form” is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
and is
parametrized by two
partial compositions
of
n
as follows. Let

\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m})\text{ and }\underline{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{t})\text{ with }\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\leq n\text{ and }\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}\leq n,

and let
\mathscr{L}
and

\mathscr{U}
denote, respectively, the subspaces of
M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
consisting of all lower and upper triangular matrices, respectively. If
X_{\underline{a}}
is the vector space of block-diagonal matrices whose blocks have sizes

\displaystyle a_{1}\times a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\times a_{m},2\left(n-\sum a_{i}\right)\times 2\left(n-\sum a_{i}\right),a_{m}\times a_{m},\dots,a_{1}\times a_{1}

(1)

and similarly for

M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
given by

(X_{\underline{a}}\cap\mathscr{L})~\cup~(X_{\underline{b}}\cap\mathscr{U}).

We denote a type-C seaweed with such a standard form by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{a_{1}|\dots|a_{m}|2n-2\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}|a_{m}|\dots|a_{1}}{b_{1}|\dots|b_{t}|2n-2\sum_{j=1}^{t}b_{j}|b_{t}|\dots|b_{1}}

(2)

or, more succinctly, by
\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{a_{1}|\dots|a_{m}}{b_{1}|\dots|b_{t}}

2

A meander
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
can be contracted via a set of winding moves, each of which is predicated on the defining partial compositions of the seaweed at the time of move application. The “winding-down” process, detailed in Lemma
8
below, continues until one of the partial compositions is reduced to the empty composition. This process corresponds precisely to the reduction algorithm given in (Panyushev
[
15
]
, 2001; cf.
[
14
]
, 2005) for type C, but we state the graph-theoretic version of the reduction as a set of winding moves as in
[
6
]
. The
signature
of the meander is the sequence of moves required to transform
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
to a meander defined by at least one empty composition.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{a_{1}|\dots|a_{m}}{b_{1}|\dots|b_{t}}
be a type-C seaweed with associated meander
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
– in this setting, we say the meander
M=M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
is of fractional form
\frac{a_{1}|\dots|a_{m}}{b_{1}|\dots|b_{t}}.
Create a meander
M^{\prime}
by one of the following moves.

1.

Pure Contraction
(
P
)
: If
a_{1}>2b_{1}
, then
M\mapsto M^{\prime}
of fractional form
\frac{(a_{1}-2b_{1})|b_{1}|a_{2}|\cdots|a_{m}}{b_{2}|b_{3}|\cdots|b_{t}}
.

2.

Block Elimination
(
B
)
: If
a_{1}=2b_{1}
, then
M\mapsto M^{\prime}
of fractional form
\frac{b_{1}|a_{2}|\cdots|a_{m}}{b_{2}|b_{3}|\cdots|b_{t}}
.

3.

Rotation Contraction
(
R
)
: If
b_{1}<a_{1}<2b_{1}
, then
M\mapsto M^{\prime}
of fractional form
\frac{b_{1}|a_{2}|\cdots|a_{m}}{(2b_{1}-a_{1})|b_{2}|\cdots|b_{t}}
.

4.

Component Deletion
(
C
(
c
))
: If
a_{1}=b_{1}=c
, then
M\mapsto M^{\prime}
of fractional form
\frac{a_{2}|\cdots|a_{m}}{b_{2}|\cdots|b_{t}}
.

5.

Flip
(
F
)
: If
a_{1}<b_{1}
, then
M\mapsto M^{\prime}
of fractional form
\frac{b_{1}|\cdots|b_{t}}{a_{1}|\cdots|a_{m}}
.

The five moves are called winding-down moves. For all moves, except the Component Deletion move,
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

(
the seaweed with meander
M_{k}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=M^{\prime}
,
k\leq n
)
have the same index.

Now, define the (
type-C
)
homotopy type

of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{a_{1}|\dots|a_{m}}{b_{1}|\dots|b_{t}}
with meander
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
to be

\mathcal{H}_{C}(c_{1},\dots,c_{q_{1}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c_{q_{1}+1},\dots,c_{q_{2}}},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}c_{q_{2}+1}}),

where
C(c_{i}),

1\leq i\leq c_{q_{1}},
are the component deletion moves in the signature of
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}),
the fully wound-down meander is
M_{k}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}),
where
k\leq n
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{p}_{2k}^{C}\frac{c_{q_{1}+1}|\dots|c_{q_{2}}}{\emptyset},
and

Notice that the fully wound-down meander
M_{k}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
consists only of tail and aftertail vertices, and moreover, neither the tail nor the aftertail are affected by the winding-down moves. Thus, the tail and aftertail of
M_{k}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})

M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}).

3.2

In order to define the core and peak of

If

\mathcal{H}_{C}(c_{1},\dots,c_{q_{1}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c_{q_{1}+1},\dots,c_{q_{2}}},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}c_{q_{2}+1}}),

\mathfrak{g}
is

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{q_{2}+1}\mathfrak{g}|_{c_{i}},

(4)

where
\mathfrak{g}|_{c_{i}}
is the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}

3.3
Regular Forms

The core and peak of an index-one seaweed
\mathfrak{g}
assist in constructing a family
\Phi_{C}
of regular one-forms on
\mathfrak{g}
that are dependent upon the homotopy type of
\mathfrak{g}.
Utilizing the symmetry conditions across the antidiagonal in a type-C seaweed, we can, without loss of generality, express an element of
\Phi_{C}
as
\sum e_{i,j}^{*}
, where the sum is over a restricted set of admissible locations
(i,j)
of
\mathfrak{g}
for which
i+j\leq 2n+1.
The restricted set of admissible locations is visualized by placing a black dot in a location (
i,j
) if and only if
e_{i,j}^{*}
is a summand of the regular one-form (see Example
16
). Since the restricted set of locations is determined by the homotopy type of
\mathfrak{g}
, we will explicitly construct a regular one-form
\varphi\in\Phi_{C}
for each homotopy type listed in Corollary
12
. First, we briefly describe the algorithm for defining
\varphi\in\Phi_{C}
on a seaweed with arbitrary homotopy type and index.

Begin by identifying the tail and aftertail components in the seaweed. For tail components, form a triangle of black dots of height
\lfloor\frac{c_{i}}{2}\rfloor,
where
c_{i}
is the size of the tail component, in the upper left-hand corner of each core block and in all entries on the diagonal of each peak block, so long as these locations
(i,j)
satisfy
i+j\leq 2n+1.
For the aftertail component, form a triangle of black dots of height
\frac{c_{i}}{2},
where
c_{i}
is the size of the aftertail component, in the upper left-hand corner of the (unique) core block. Note there are no peak blocks in an aftertail component. The only remaining components are those which are neither tail nor aftertail components. For components of this form, black dots are placed on and above the antidiagonal of each core block and on the diagonal of each peak block. Example
16
illustrates this general construction.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
be a type-C seaweed with homotopy type consisting of at least one component of size
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3}
. Then let
\mathscr{C}=(v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}},v_{i_{1}})
be a cycle in the full meander
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
corresponding to a component of size
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}
, and let
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j+1}e_{i_{j},i_{j}}.
If
v_{i_{r}},v_{i_{\ell}}\in\mathscr{C}
and
r,\ell\in I,
where
v_{I}
is a vertex in the component meander
CM(\mathfrak{g}),
then
e_{i_{r},i_{r}}
and
e_{i_{\ell},i_{\ell}}
have coefficients with opposite sign in
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
has a component of size
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}
, there exist vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{i+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}-1}
in
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}-1}\}
is an edge in
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}).
Fix such vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{i+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}-1}.
Considering
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}),
since
v_{i_{r}}
and
v_{i_{\ell}}
are identified in the construction of
CM(\mathfrak{g})
by assumption, it follows that the length of the shortest path from
v_{i_{r}}
to
v_{i}
is equal to the length of the shortest path from
v_{i_{\ell}}
to
v_{i+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}-1}.
Therefore, the length of the path from
v_{i_{r}}
to
v_{i_{\ell}}
is odd, so
e_{i_{r},i_{r}}
and
e_{i_{\ell},i_{\ell}}
have coefficients with opposite sign in
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}.

∎

Theorem 19
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
be a type-C seaweed with homotopy type
\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1,c,1,\dots,1}),
where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},
and let
\mathscr{C}=(v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}},v_{i_{1}})
be the unique cycle in the full meander
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}).
If
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
is defined as in Section
3.3
, then

\ker(B_{\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}})=\text{span}\{h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\},

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},
and

h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j+1}e_{i_{j},i_{j}}.

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
is regular, we need only show that
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\in\ker(B_{\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}}),
for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3}.
Note that by the definition of the full meander
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}),
the length of any path from
v_{i_{j}}
to
v_{2n-i_{j}+1}
is odd, so the coefficients of
e_{i_{j},i_{j}}
and
e_{2n-i_{j}+1,2n-i_{j}+1}
in the expansion of
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
have opposite sign, so
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\in\mathfrak{g}
for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3}.
Now, to see that
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\in\ker(B_{\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}}),
consider the following:

(a)

\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}([h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},e_{i,i}-e_{2n-i+1,2n-i+1}])=0,
for all
1\leq i\leq n,

(b)

\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}([h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},e_{i,j}\pm e_{2n-j+1,2n-i+1}])=0,
for all
i,j
such that
i+j<2n+1
and
e_{i,j}^{*}
is not a summand of
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},

(c)

\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}([h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},e_{i,j}])=0,
for all
i,j
such that
i+j=2n+1
and
e_{i,j}^{*}
is not a summand of
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},

(d)

\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}([h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},e_{i,j}\pm e_{2n-j+1,2n-i+1}])=\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\left(e_{i,j}\pm e_{2n-j+1,2n-i+1}-(e_{i,j}\pm e_{2n-j+1,2n-i+1})\right)=0,
for all
i,j
such that
i+j<2n+1
and
e_{i,j}^{*}
is a summand of
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},
and

(e)

\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}([h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})},e_{i,j}])=\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}(e_{i,j}-e_{i,j})=0,
for all
i,j
such that
i+j=2n+1
and
e_{i,j}^{*}
is a summand of
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}.

Equations
(a)-(c)
follow immediately from
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
being a semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g},
and Equation
(d)
follows from Lemma
18
in the following way.

Without loss of generality, let
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}.
In
\mathfrak{g}|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}},
each peak block consists of exactly four entries. Fix such a peak block and call the entries – from left to right, top to bottom –
(s,t),(s,t+1),(s+1,t),
and
(s+1,t+1).
The entries on the antidiagonal of the peak block,
(s,t+1)
and
(s+1,t),
correspond to the edges
(v_{s},v_{t+1})
and
(v_{s+1},v_{t})
of the oriented full meander
\overrightarrow{M}_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}).
Therefore,
e_{s,s}
and
e_{t+1,t+1}
have coefficients with opposite sign in the expansion of
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2})},
and similarly for the summands
e_{s+1,s+1}
and
e_{t,t}.
Further, the vertices
v_{t}
and
v_{t+1}
of
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
are identified in the construction of the component meander
CM(\mathfrak{g}),
so Lemma
18
implies that
e_{t,t}
and
e_{t+1,t+1}
have coefficients with opposite sign in the expansion of
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2})}.
Therefore, the coefficients of
e_{s,s}
and
e_{t,t}
in the expansion of
h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2})}
have the same sign, and similarly for
e_{s+1,s+1}
and
e_{t+1,t+1}.
We then conclude that Equation
(d)
holds since
e_{s,t}^{*}
and
e_{s+1,t+1}^{*}
are the summands of
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2})}
arising from the chosen peak block. Equation
(e)
follows via similar reasoning.
∎

4
Main results

In this section, we establish the main result of this article. In particular, we prove that all type-C seaweeds of index one are contact by showing that each of the one-forms discussed in Sections
3.3.1
,
3.3.2
, and
3.3.3
are contact. We accomplish this by utilizing the following lemma, which is a classical characterization of contact Lie algebras in terms of an associated regular one-form
\varphi
and the kernel of the associated
B_{\varphi}.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be an arbitrary Lie algebra. If
{\rm ind\hskip 2.84544pt}\mathfrak{f}=1,
then a regular one-form
\varphi\in\mathfrak{f}^{*}
with
\ker(B_{\varphi})=\text{span}\{x\}
is contact if and only if
\varphi(x)\neq 0.

We are now in a position to prove the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 30
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a type-A or type-C seaweed, then
\mathfrak{g}
is contact if and only if
{\rm ind\hskip 2.84544pt}\mathfrak{g}=1.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a type-A seaweed, then by Theorem 24 of
[
6
]
,
\mathfrak{g}
is contact if and only if
{\rm ind\hskip 2.84544pt}\mathfrak{g}=1.
So let
\mathfrak{g}
be an index-one seaweed of type C. Recall from Corollary
12
that this means
\mathfrak{g}
has one of the following homotopy types:

1a.

\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1,2,1,\dots,1}),

1b.

\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1,3,1,\dots,1})
,

2.

\mathcal{H}_{C}(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1})
, or

3.

\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})
.

We proceed by treating each homotopy type as its own case, using the constructions of Sections
3.3.1
,
3.3.2
, and
3.3.3
, and then applying Lemma
29
.

Case 1:
The seaweed
\mathfrak{g}
has homotopy type
\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1,c,1,\dots,1}),
where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3}.
We claim that
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
is a contact form on
\mathfrak{g},
for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},
respectively. Recall from Theorem
19
that if
\mathscr{C}=(v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots,v_{i_{k}},v_{i_{1}})
is the unique cycle in the full meander
M_{2n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g}),
then

\ker(B_{\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}})=\text{span}\{h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}\}=\text{span}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j+1}e_{i_{j},i_{j}}\right\}.

By Lemma
18
and the fact that
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}
only contains summands
e_{i,j}^{*}
such that
i+j\leq 2n+1,
we have that
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})}(h_{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c})})=\frac{k}{4}\neq 0,
for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}c}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}
or
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3}.
An application of Lemma
29
establishes the claim.

Case 2:
The seaweed
\mathfrak{g}
has homotopy type
\mathcal{H}_{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2}).
We claim that
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})}
is a contact form on
\mathfrak{g}.
Recall from Theorem
23
that

\ker(B_{\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})}})=\text{span}\{h_{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})}\}=\text{span}\{e_{n,n}-e_{n+1,n+1}\},

and notice that
\varphi_{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})}(h_{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2})})=1\neq 0.
An application of Lemma
29
establishes the claim.

Case 3:
The seaweed
\mathfrak{g}
has homotopy type
\mathcal{H}_{C}(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1,\dots,1}).
We claim that
\varphi_{(1)}
is a contact form on
\mathfrak{g}.
Recall from Theorem
27
that if
P
is the unique path in the meander
M_{n}^{C}(\mathfrak{g})
with zero vertices in the tail, then

\ker(B_{\varphi_{(1)}})=\text{span}\{h_{(1)}\}=\text{span}\left\{\sum_{v_{i}\in V(P)}e_{i,i}-e_{2n-i+1,2n-i+1}\right\},

and notice that
\varphi_{(1)}(h_{(1)})=|V(P)|\neq 0.
An application of Lemma
29
establishes the claim.
∎

5
Examples

For type-C seaweeds, Theorem
30
simplifies the question about the existence of a contact form on a seaweed
\mathfrak{g}
to one about
\mathfrak{g}
’s index. But the index may be computed from
\mathfrak{g}
’s meander (see Theorem
5
). This is an elegant reduction, but some burdensome complexity remains, for one must first construct the meander and then count the number and type of connected components. Fortunately, as with type-A, for type-C seaweeds defined by (partial) compositions consisting of a small number of parts, we can quickly determine the index of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 31

(Coll et al.
[
4
]
, 2017)
.

Let
\alpha+\beta=n
. If
\gamma=n-1
or
\gamma=n-2
, then

{\rm ind\hskip 2.84544pt}\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{\alpha|\beta}{\gamma}=\gcd(\alpha+\beta,\beta+\gamma)-1.

Making use of Theorem
31
, we can construct a spate of contact Lie algebras – we need only ensure that the seaweed
\mathfrak{p}_{2n}^{C}\frac{\alpha|\beta}{\gamma}
satisfies
\gamma=n-1
or
\gamma=n-2
and
\gcd(\alpha+\beta,\beta+\gamma)=2.
This is easy to do: For
\gamma=n-2
, consider
\mathfrak{p}_{18}^{C}\frac{5|3}{7},
and for
\gamma=n-1
, consider
\mathfrak{p}_{14}^{C}\frac{4|2}{6},
or the much larger
\mathfrak{p}_{100}^{C}\frac{15|13}{49}
. The reader will have no difficulty in developing others.

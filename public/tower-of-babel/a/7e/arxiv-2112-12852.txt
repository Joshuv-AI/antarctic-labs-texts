Asymptotics of quantum invariants of surface diffeomorphisms I: Conjecture and algebraic computations Thanks: This work was partially supported by the grants DMS-1711297, DMS-2005656 (PI: Francis Bonahon), DMS-1841221, DMS-1906323 (PI: Helen Wong) and DMS-1812008 (PI: Tian Yang) from the US National Science Foundation.
Francis Bonahon, Helen Wong, Tian Yang

Abstract
. The Kashaev-Murakami-Murakami Volume Conjecture connects the hyperbolic volume of a knot complement to the asymptotics of certain evaluations of the colored Jones polynomials of the knot. We introduce a closely related conjecture for diffeomorphisms of surfaces, backed up by numerical evidence. The conjecture involves isomorphisms between certain representations of the Kauffman bracket skein algebra of the surface, and the bulk of the article is devoted to the development of explicit methods to compute these isomorphisms. These combinatorial and algebraic techniques are exploited in two subsequent articles, which prove the conjecture for a large family of diffeomorphisms of the one-puncture torus and are much more analytic.



Introduction

This work is motivated by the Kashaev Volume Conjecture
[
Kas97
]
, as rephrased by Murakami-Murakami
[
MM01
]
, which connects the asymptotics of the
n

J_{K}^{(n)}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
of a knot

to the volume

of the complete hyperbolic metric of its complement (if it exists). More precisely, the conjecture is that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left\lvert J_{K}^{(n)}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}\big)\right\rvert=\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(S^{3}-K).

This attractive conjecture, combining two very different areas of low-dimensional topology and geometry, has generated much work in the past twenty years, much of it on the combinatorial side.

The current series of three articles, consisting of this one and of its companions
[
BWY22a
,
BWY22b
]
, aims at developing analytic and geometric tools to attack this Kashaev-Murakami-Murakami Conjecture. With this goal in mind, it introduces another conjecture, which has the deceptive appearance of being only 2–dimensional, but similarly relates the asymptotics of purely combinatorial invariants to 3–dimensional hyperbolic volumes. The reader familiar with Kashaev’s original approach
[

]

[

,

,
BB07

[

This new conjecture involves relatively recent work

FKBL19
,
GJS19
]

1.
The Volume Conjecture for surface diffeomorphisms

1.1.
The
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
–character variety and the Kauffman bracket skein algebra of a surface

Many notions in quantum algebra and quantum topology are noncommutative deformations of a (commutative) algebra of functions over a geometric object, and depend on a parameter
q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}
. For instance, the
Kauffman bracket skein algebra

\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
of an oriented surface
S
is a deformation of the algebra of regular functions over the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
–character variety

\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)=\{r\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\}/\negthinspace\negthinspace/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

consisting of group homomorphisms from the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)
to the algebraic group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
considered (in the sense of geometric invariant theory) up to conjugation by elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
Tur91
,
PS00
,
BFKB02
]
.

A general phenomenon is that, when the quantum parameter
q
is a root of unity, a point in the geometric object usually determines an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of the associated quantum object, up to finitely many well-understood choices. As a consequence, an “interesting” geometric situation determines a finite but high-dimensional representation of an algebraic object, which carries a lot of information and from which invariants can be extracted. This principle was explicitly stated for the quantum Teichmüller space of a surface in
[
BL07
]
, but it also occurs in many other contexts such as quantum cluster algebras
[
BZ05
,
FG09b
]
, quantum cluster ensembles
[
FG09a
]
or quantum character varieties
[
GJS19
]
.

We will here restrict attention to the case of the skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
of an oriented surface
S
of finite topological type, and rely on the results of
[
BW11a
,
FKBL19
,
GJS19
]
. The precise definition of the Kauffman bracket skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
will not be important for our purposes. We will just say that it involves the consideration of framed links in the 3–dimensional thickening
S\times[0,1]
of the surface
S
, considered modulo certain relations, the most important of which is the
Kauffman bracket skein relation
that

K_{1}=q^{\frac{1}{2}}K_{0}+q^{-\frac{1}{2}}K_{\infty}

whenever the three links
K_{1}
,
K_{0}
and
K_{\infty}\subset S\times[0,1]
differ only in a little ball where they are as represented on Figure
1
.
In particular, although this is not reflected in the notation, the skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
depends on the choice of a square root
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
for the quantum parameter
q
.

Theorem 2

(
[

,

,

]
)
.

Let the quantum parameter

–root of unity with

1.

2.

Conversely, every data of a character

See

Suppose that

1.2.
Kauffman bracket intertwiners as invariants of surface diffeomorphisms

The combination of Theorems
2
and
3
shows that a point
[r]
of the character variety
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
determines, up to
n^{p}
possible choices of puncture weights, a representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
of dimension
n^{3g+p-3}
, at least as long as
[r]
does not belong to the “bad” algebraic subset consisting of reducible characters. We will apply this setup to very specific characters
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
.

1.2.1.
Characters that are invariant under the action of a diffeomorphism

Consider an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
. It induces a homomorphism
\varphi_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(S)
, well-defined up to conjugation by an element of
\pi_{1}(S)
. It therefore acts on the character variety by
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, defined by the property that
\varphi^{*}\big([r]\big)=[r\circ\varphi_{*}]
.

We are interested in the fixed points of the action of
\varphi
on the smooth part of the character variety
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
. These are the characters represented by irreducible homomorphisms
r\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
r\circ\varphi_{*}
is conjugate to
r
by an element of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We can therefore express this in terms of the
mapping torus

M_{\varphi,r}
, obtained from
S\times[0,1]
by gluing
S\times\{1\}
to
S\times\{0\}
through
\varphi
, and whose fundamental group admits the presentation

\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})=\langle\pi_{1}(S),t;\,t\alpha t^{-1}=\varphi_{*}(\alpha),\forall\alpha\in\pi_{1}(S)\rangle.

Then the irreducible character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is fixed by the action of
\varphi
precisely when
r
extends to a homomorphism
r\colon\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

When
\varphi
is a pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphism, there is a preferred finite family of such
\varphi
–invariant characters. Indeed, the mapping torus
M_{\varphi,r}
then admits a unique complete hyperbolic metric
[
Thu88
,
Ota96
,
Ota01
]
. Identifying the group of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
to
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the monodromy of this hyperbolic metric provides a homomorphism
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, well-defined up to conjugation by an element of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, such that
M_{\varphi,r}
is isometric to
\mathbb{H}^{3}/r_{\mathrm{hyp}}\big(\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})\big)
by an orientation-preserving isometry.

As an orientable 3–dimensional manifold,
M_{\varphi,r}
is parallelizable and we can use this property to lift
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
to a homomorphism
r_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
; the number of such lifts is equal to the cardinal of
H^{1}(M_{\varphi,r};\mathbb{Z}/2)
. Restricting these lifts
r_{\mathrm{hyp}}
to
\pi_{1}(S)\subset\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi,r})
, the corresponding characters
[r_{\mathrm{hyp}}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
are by construction fixed by the action of
\varphi
. The number of the
\varphi
–invariant hyperbolic characters
[r_{\mathrm{hyp}}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
thus associated to the monodromy
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}
is equal to the cardinal of the kernel of the subtraction
H^{1}(\varphi;\mathbb{Z}/2)-\mathrm{Id}_{H^{1}(S;\mathbb{Z}/2)}
, where
H^{1}(\varphi;\mathbb{Z}/2)\colon H^{1}(S;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^{1}(S;\mathbb{Z}/2)
denotes the homomorphism induced by
\varphi
.

However, there are many more fixed points, in particular when
S
has at least one puncture. For instance, near the hyperbolic character
[\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}]
, the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
–character variety
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is smooth with complex dimension equal to the number
c
of cusps of
M_{\varphi,r}
, by Weil rigidity
[
Wei60
,
Wei62
,
GR70
]
applied to
M_{\varphi,r}
; see also
[
Thu81
, §5]
,
[
BP92
, §E.6]
or the discussion in §
3.2
. Note that the number
c
of topological ends of
M_{\varphi,r}
is also the number of orbits of the action of
\varphi
on the set of punctures of the surface
S
. Lifting these characters to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
then provides a complex
c
–dimensional submanifold of
\varphi
–invariant characters
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
near each
\varphi
–invariant lift
[r_{\mathrm{hyp}}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
of the hyperbolic character
[\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
.

1.2.2.
Invariants of surface diffeomorphisms

The diffeomorphism
\varphi
also acts on the skein algebra by
\varphi_{*}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
in such a way that
\varphi_{*}\big([K]\big)=\big[(\varphi\times\mathrm{Id}_{[0,1]})(K)\big]
for every element
[K]\in\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
represented by a framed link
K\subset S\times[0,1]
.

If we are given an irreducible
\varphi
–invariant character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, and if we choose puncture weights
p_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
that are
\varphi
–invariant
in the sense that
p_{\varphi(v)}=p_{v}
for every puncture
v
, the uniqueness property of Theorem
3
shows that the representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
associated to this data by Theorem
2
is isomorphic to the representation
\rho\circ\varphi_{*}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
. This means that there exists a linear isomorphism
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon V\to V
such that

(\rho\circ\varphi_{*})(X)=\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\circ\rho(X)\circ(\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q})^{-1}\in\mathrm{End}(V)

for every
X\in\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
.

Note that this property is unchanged if we replace the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
by a scalar multiple. Lacking a better idea, we normalize it so that
\left|\det\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\right|=1
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon V\to V
be the above intertwiner, normalized so that
\left|\det\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\right|=1
. Then, up to conjugation and multiplication by a scalar with modulus
1
,
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
depends only on the diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
, the
\varphi
–invariant character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, the primitive
n
–root of unity
q
and the
\varphi
–invariant puncture weights
p_{v}
.

In particular, the modulus
|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}|
of its trace is uniquely determined by the above data.

To be specific the uniqueness statement means that, if different intermediate choices lead to another intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q\,\prime}\colon V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}
, there exists a linear isomorphism
\Lambda\colon V\to V^{\prime}
such that
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q\,\prime}=s\,\Lambda\circ\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\circ\Lambda^{-1}
for some scalar
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
|s|=1
.

Proof.

We made two implicit choices: a representative
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
in an isomorphism class of representations; and the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
.

If
\rho
is given, the irreducibility property of this representation implies that the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is unique up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar
s\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, by Schur’s lemma. Our hypothesis that
\left|\det\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\right|=1
then constrains this scalar
s
to have modulus
1
.

Replacing
\rho
by an isomorphic representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V^{\prime})
will only replace
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
by its conjugate
\Lambda\circ\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\circ\Lambda^{-1}
, where
\Lambda\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is the isomorphism between
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
.

This proves the property of uniqueness up to conjugation and multiplication by scalar with modulus 1. The uniqueness property for
|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}|
immediately follows.
∎

Remark 5
.

We could have required that
\det\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is exactly equal to 1, in which case the above argument shows that
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is unique up to conjugation and multiplication by a
d
–root of unity, where
d=n^{3g+p-3}
is the dimension of
V
. However, we do not know of any good use for this more precise statement at this point.

1.3.
The Volume Conjecture for surface diffeomorphisms

The modulus
\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\right|
of Proposition
4
depends on the odd integer
n
, the primitive
n
–root of unity
q
, the
\varphi
–invariant character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, and the
\varphi
–invariant puncture weights
p_{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, constrained for every puncture
v
by the Chebyshev condition that
T_{n}(p_{v})=-\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})
as in Theorem
2
. After fixing the diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
and the
\varphi
–invariant character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, we want to consider the asymptotic behavior of this quantity as
q
tends to 1, namely as
n
tends to
\infty
. However, we need to choose the other quantities in a consistent way as functions of
n
.

For the quantum parameter
q
, we take it to be equal to
q_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
.

For the puncture weights
p_{v}
, we need solutions of the equation
T_{n}(x)=-\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})
. An elementary property of the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}
(see for instance
[
BW17
, Lem. 17]
) is that, if we write
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})=-\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{v}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta_{v}}
for some
\theta_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
, the solutions of the equation
T_{n}(x)=-\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})
are all numbers of the form
x=y+y^{-1}
where
y
is an
n
–root of
\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{v}}
. In particular, once the numbers
\theta_{v}
are chosen, we can take
p_{v}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}
. Since
[r]
is
\varphi
–invariant, we can choose the
\theta_{v}
to be
\varphi
–invariant in the sense that
\theta_{\varphi(v)}=\theta_{v}
for every puncture
v
; then the puncture weights
p_{v}
will be
\varphi
–invariant as well.

Conjecture 6

(The Volume Conjecture for surface diffeomorphisms)
.

Let
\varphi\colon S\to S
be a pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphism of the surface
S
, and let
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
be a
\varphi
–invariant character which, in the fixed point set of the action of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, is in the same component as a hyperbolic character
[r_{\mathrm{hyp}}]
. Choose
\varphi
–invariant puncture weights
\theta_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})=-\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{v}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\theta_{v}}
for every puncture
v
, where
\alpha(v)\in\pi_{1}(S)
is represented by a loop going once around
v
. With this data, for every odd integer
n
, let
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
be the intertwiner associated by Proposition
4
to the quantum parameter
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
, the
\varphi
–invariant character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
and the
\varphi
–invariant puncture weights
p_{v}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}
. Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})

where
\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})
is the volume of the complete hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi,r}
of
\varphi
.

Conjecture
6
admits a straightforward generalization to the root of unity
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2k\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
for some fixed integer
k\geqslant 1
, in which case heuristic and experimental evidence suggest that the limit should be
\frac{1}{4k\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})
. The case
k=1
is sufficiently complicated that we will restrict attention to this framework.

Once the character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is fixed, the puncture weights
\theta_{v}
are determined only modulo
2\pi\mathrm{i}
(and also up to a change of sign that does not impact the puncture weights
p_{v}
). Different choices yield different intertwiners
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
. The conjecture predicts the same limit for all choices but, as we will see in §
2
and
[
BWY22a
,
BWY22b
]
, these choices do impact the mode of convergence.

2.
Experimental evidence

In preliminary work to test Conjecture
6
, we developed computer code (running on
Mathematica
™) that computes
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
for diffeomorphisms
\varphi
of the simplest surface where it applies, the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
.

For the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
, the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and it is well-known that every orientation preserving element is conjugate to a composition

\varphi=\pm\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{k_{0}}

where each diffeomorphism
\varphi_{k}
corresponds to one of the matrices

0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and

1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. The
\pm
sign turns out to be irrelevant for our purposes, and the diffeomorphism
\varphi
is pseudo-Anosov precisely when both
L
and

occur in the list of the elementary diffeomorphisms
\varphi_{k}
.

The simplest pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphism corresponds to
\varphi=LR=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\

, in which case the mapping torus
M_{\varphi,r}
is diffeomorphic to the complement of the figure-eight knot. In this example, it turns out that the algebraic expression of the trace
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is relatively simple, as is the geometry of the hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi,r}
. This enables us, in
[
BWY22a
]
, to prove Conjecture
6
for this case “by hand”.

The next example by order of complexity is

, for which there does not seem to exist any elementary proof. The two diagrams of Figure

3.
Computing the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
using ideal triangulations

The proof of the uniqueness property of Theorem
3
is rather abstract, and consequently so is the construction of the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
of Proposition
4
. When the surface
S
has at least one puncture, the explicit constructions of
[
BW17
]
provide a more practical determination of
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
, by connecting it to the intertwiners of
[
BL07
, §9]
. This section describes this approach, which we will use in our explicit computations. The first two subsections §
3.1
and §
3.2
cover classical geometric material, but are needed to carefully set up the correspondence between the two points of view. They will also play an important role in the geometric part of the arguments of
[
BWY22b
]
.

In the rest of the article, the surface
S
will always be assumed to admit an ideal triangulation as defined below. This is equivalent to requiring that
S
has at least one puncture, and even at least 3 punctures if its genus is 0.

3.1.
Ideal triangulations, complex edge weights and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
–characters

If we represent the punctured surface
S=\widehat{S}-\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{p}\}
as the complement of
p
points in a compact surface
\widehat{S}
, an
ideal triangulation
of
S
is a triangulation of
\widehat{S}
whose vertex set is equal to the set
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{p}\}
of the punctures. If
S
has genus
g
and
p
punctures, such a triangulation necessarily has
6g+3p-6
edges and
4g+2p-4
triangular faces. In particular, the existence of an ideal triangulation requires, and is in fact equivalent to, the condition that
p\geqslant 3
if
g=0
(and
p>0
in all cases).

We will insist that the data of an ideal triangulation
\tau
includes, in addition to the triangulation itself, an indexing of its edges as
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, …,
\gamma_{e}
for
e=6g+3p-6
(but no indexing of its triangle faces). We consider such ideal triangulations up to isotopy of
S
.

Given an ideal triangulation
\tau
, assigning a nonzero complex weight
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
to each edge
\gamma_{i}
of
\tau
determines a character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
. We give a quick outline of the construction, and refer to, for instance,
[
BL07
, §8]
for details.

More precisely, the edge weights
a_{i}
enable us to construct a pleated surface
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
from the universal cover
\widetilde{S}
of
S
to the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, pleated along the triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
of
\widetilde{S}
induced by
\tau
and
\bar{r}
-equivariant for a unique group homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. By definition, this map is totally geodesic on the faces of
\widetilde{\tau}
, and its local geometry along an edge
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
of
\widetilde{\tau}
lifting an edge
\gamma_{i}
of
\tau
is determined by the following property: If we consider the four points of the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
that are the images under
\widetilde{f}
of the vertices of the square formed by the two faces of
\widetilde{\tau}
adjacent to
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
, then the crossratio of these four points is equal to
-a_{i}
. (Since the crossratio of four points depends on an ordering of these points, we refer to
[
BL07
, §8]
for details.) The
\bar{r}
–equivariance property means that, for the standard isometric action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
\widetilde{f}(\alpha\widetilde{x})=\bar{r}(\alpha)\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x})
for every
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(S)
and
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{S}
. In this construction, the edge weights
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
are the
shear-bend parameters
of the pleated surface
\widetilde{f}
.

This pleated surface
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
is uniquely determined by the ideal triangulation
\tau
and the edge weights
a_{i}
, up to post-composition by an orientation-preserving isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and pre-composition by the lift to
\widetilde{S}
of an isotopy of
S
. In particular, the homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is uniquely determined up to conjugation by an element of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. As a consequence, the corresponding character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is uniquely determined by the edge weights
a_{i}
.

We combine the weights
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
into an
edge weight system

a=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{e})\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
.

The following statement shows that “most” characters
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
are associated in this way to an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
.

Lemma 7
.

Given an ideal triangulation
\tau
, there exists a Zariski-open dense subset
U\subset\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
such that every character
[\bar{r}]\in U
is associated as above to an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
for
\tau
.

This is a well-known property, but we will need to refer to the details of its proof for the less familiar Lemma
10
below.

Proof.

The key idea is that, for a group homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, an
\bar{r}
–equivariant pleated surface
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
pleated along
\tau
can be reconstructed from fixed point data of the action of
\bar{r}\big(\pi_{1}(S)\big)\subset\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on the complex projective line
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

A puncture of the surface
S
determines infinitely many
peripheral subgroups
of
\pi_{1}(S)
, all conjugate to each other. These are each generated by an element of
\pi_{1}(S)
represented by a small loop going once around the puncture.

In particular, for every edge
\widetilde{\gamma}
of the ideal triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
of
\widetilde{S}
induced by
\tau
, each end of
\widetilde{\gamma}
determines a peripheral subgroup
\pi\subset\pi_{1}(S)
. We can then consider the image
\widetilde{f}\left(\widetilde{\gamma}\right)
under the pleated surface
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
. The endpoints of this geodesic
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
are each fixed by the image
\bar{r}(\pi)\subset\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the peripheral subgroup
\pi\subset\pi_{1}(S)
associated to the corresponding end of
\widetilde{\gamma}
. Because
\widetilde{f}
sends every face of
\widetilde{\tau}
to an ideal triangle in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
(such that any two sides of the triangle share an endpoint), this endpoint
\xi(\pi)\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
depends only on the peripheral subgroup
\pi
, not on the particular edge
\widetilde{\gamma}
leading to it.

The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)
acts by conjugation on the set
\Pi
of its peripheral subgroups. A
\tau
–enhancement
for a homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a map
\xi\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
such that:

1.

The map
\xi
is
\bar{r}
–equivariant, in the sense that

\xi\left(\alpha\pi\alpha^{-1}\right)=\bar{r}(\alpha)\xi(\pi)

for every peripheral subgroup
\pi\in\Pi
and for every
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(S)
.

2.

If
\pi
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
are the peripheral subgroups respectively associated to the two ends of an edge
\widetilde{\gamma}
of the ideal triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
of
\widetilde{S}
induced by
\tau
, then
\xi(\pi)\neq\xi(\pi^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

We just saw that an
\bar{r}
–equivariant pleated surface
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
pleated along
\tau
uniquely determines a
\tau
–enhancement. Conversely, given a
\tau
–enhancement
\xi
, one can use the second condition in the definition of enhancements to reconstruct an
\bar{r}
–equivariant pleated surface
\widetilde{f}
realizing it, by considering the geodesics and ideal triangles of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
that will form the images under
\widetilde{f}
of the edges and faces of the ideal triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
. The shear-bend parameters of this pleated surface then form an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
, associated to the character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
by construction.

We consequently have converted the problem of finding an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
associated to
[\bar{r}]
to finding a
\tau
–enhancement for
\bar{r}
.

With this in mind, we consider a homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and look for a
\tau
–enhancement for
\bar{r}
. This will require us to impose some restrictions on the character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
.

Since every element of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
has at least one fixed point in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, the image
\bar{r}(\pi)\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of each peripheral subgroup
\pi\in\Pi
admits a fixed point
\xi(\pi)\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, and we can easily turn this to an
\bar{r}
–equivariant map
\xi\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
by considering the finitely many orbits of the action of
\pi_{1}(S)
on
\Pi
.

If we want
\xi
to be a
\tau
–enhancement for
\bar{r}
, we need to make sure that
\xi(\pi)\neq\xi(\pi^{\prime})
whenever
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
are associated to the endpoints of an edge of the ideal triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
. One easy way to guarantee this is to arrange that the cyclic subgroups
\bar{r}(\pi)
and
\bar{r}(\pi^{\prime})\subset\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
have no common fixed points, and to take advantage of the following elementary property: Two elements
A
,
B\in\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
have a common fixed point in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\left(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}\right)=+2
. (Note that, although the trace of an element of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is only defined up to sign, the trace of a commutator is uniquely determined.)

For this purpose, pick a generator
\alpha_{\pi}\in\pi
for each peripheral subgroup
\pi\in\Pi
, and do this in a
\pi_{1}(S)
–equivariant way in the sense that
\alpha_{\beta\pi\beta^{-1}}=\beta\alpha_{\pi}\beta^{-1}
for every
\beta\in\pi_{1}(S)
and
\pi\in\Pi
. Then define
U\subset\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
as the set of characters
[\bar{r}]
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\bar{r}\left(\alpha_{\pi}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}\alpha_{\pi}^{-1}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}\right)\neq+2
whenever
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
are associated to the endpoints of an edge of the ideal triangulation
\widetilde{\tau}
.

By
\bar{r}
–equivariance and because
\tau
only has finitely many edges, there are only finitely many such conditions
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\bar{r}\left(\alpha_{\pi}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}\alpha_{\pi}^{-1}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}\right)\neq+2
that are relevant. It follows that
U
is Zariski-open.

For any two distinct peripheral subgroups
\pi
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
, the set of group homomorphisms
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\bar{r}\left(\alpha_{\pi}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}\alpha_{\pi}^{-1}\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}\right)\neq+2
is easily seen to be dense in the representation variety
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{2g+p-1}
consisting of all homomorphisms
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, by consideration of a suitable presentation for the free group
\pi_{1}(S)
. It follows that its image in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is dense. Since
U
is the intersection of finitely many such images, it is dense in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
.

We consequently found a dense Zariski-open subset
U\subset\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
such that every group homomorphism
\bar{r}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with
[\bar{r}]\in U
admits a
\tau
–enhancement, and is therefore associated to an edge weight system for the ideal triangulation
\tau
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\tau=\tau^{(0)}
,
\tau^{(1)}
, …,
\tau^{(k_{0}-1)}
,
\tau^{(k_{0})}=\varphi(\tau)
be an ideal triangulation sweep from
\tau
to
\varphi(\tau)
. If the weight system

,
a^{(1)}
, …,
a^{(k_{0})}\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}

a^{(k_{0})}=a^{(0)}

[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is fixed by the action of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)

Conversely, there is a Zariski-open dense subset

\varphi

,
a^{(1)}
, …,

Proof.

Lift
\varphi
to a diffeomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\widetilde{S}

\varphi_{*}
–equivariant for a suitable homomorphism
\varphi_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(S)

\widetilde{\varphi}\left(\alpha\widetilde{x}\right)=\varphi_{*}(\alpha)\widetilde{\varphi}\left(\widetilde{x}\right)
for every
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{S}
and
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(S)
.

If
\widetilde{f}^{(0)}
,
\widetilde{f}^{(k_{0})}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
are
\bar{r}
–equivariant pleated surfaces respectively associated to the ideal triangulations
\tau^{(0)}
,
\tau^{(k_{0})}
and to the edge weight systems
a^{(0)}
,
a^{(k_{0})}
, then
\widetilde{f}^{(k_{0})}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}
is pleated along
\tau^{(0)}
according to the edge weight system

. If
a^{(0)}=a^{(k_{0})}
, it follows that
\widetilde{f}^{(k_{0})}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}
coincides with
\widetilde{f}^{(0)}
up to post-composition with an isometry

of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and pre-composition with the lift to
\widetilde{S}
of an isotopy of

. Note that
\widetilde{f}^{(k_{0})}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}
is
\bar{r}\circ\varphi_{*}
–equivariant. Since

is the only homomorphism

is
\bar{r}
–equivariant, it follows that
\bar{r}(\alpha)=A\left(\bar{r}\circ\varphi_{*}(\alpha)\right)A^{-1}
for every

In particular,
[\bar{r}]=[\bar{r}\circ\varphi_{*}]
in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
. In other words, the character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is fixed by the action of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
.

For the second statement, we will use the set-up of Lemma
7
and its proof. For each

,
1
, …,
k_{0}

\bar{r}

\bar{r}
. (Note that the
\bar{r}

\bar{r}(\pi)

Suppose that the character
[\bar{r}]\in U

\varphi

[\bar{r}]=[\bar{r}\circ\varphi_{*}]
in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)

is necessarily irreducible by definition of the

in the proof of Lemma

\bar{r}\circ\varphi_{*}(\alpha)=A\,\bar{r}(\alpha)A^{-1}
for every

.

The group homomorphism
\varphi_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(S)

In view of the above observation, we would like to find an

–equivariant map

for every

. As usual, we will proceed by considering the finitely many orbits of the group generated by the actions of

and

–enhancement of

for every

,

–equivariant pleated surface

. Let

defined by the pleated surface

.

By definition, the

,

Lemma 11
.

For an ideal triangulation sweep
\tau=\tau^{(0)}
,
\tau^{(1)}
, …,
\tau^{(k_{0}-1)}
,
\tau^{(k_{0})}=\varphi(\tau)
, there is a unique periodic edge weight system
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(0)}
,
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(1)}
, …,
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k_{0}-1)}
,
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k_{0})}=a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(0)}
whose associated character
[\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is represented by the restriction
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the monodromy of the complete hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi,r}
.

In the space
\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
of edge weight systems for the ideal triangulation
\tau^{(0)}
, those corresponding to periodic edge weight systems for the ideal triangulation sweep form a subspace of complex dimension
c
near this “hyperbolic” edge weight system
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(0)}
, where
c\geqslant 1
is the number of orbits of the action of
\varphi
on the set of punctures of
S
.

Proof.

We rely on the following two properties of the homomorphism
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The first one is that, for every peripheral subgroup
\pi\in\Pi
, the image
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(\pi)
is a parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and in particular fixes a unique point of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. The second property is that
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}
is injective.

Because of the first property, there is a unique
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}
–equivariant map
\xi\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Two parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
that fix the same point of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
necessarily commute with each other and, any two distinct parabolic subgroups
\pi
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
generate a free group on two generators in
\pi_{1}(S)
. Since
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}
is injective it follows that, for distinct
\pi
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
, the parabolic subgroups
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(\pi)
and
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(\pi^{\prime})
fix different points of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. As a consequence, for any ideal triangulation
\tau
, the map
\xi\colon\Pi\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is a
\tau
–enhancement of
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}
and this enhancement is unique.

We can then use this enhancement
\bar{\xi}
to construct pleated surfaces
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k)}\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
, respectively pleated along the ideal triangulations
\tau^{(k)}
. The shear-bend parameters of these pleated surfaces provide an edge weight system
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(0)}
,
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(1)}
, …,
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k_{0})}
for the ideal triangulation sweep. Since
[\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\circ\pi_{*}]=[\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}]
, it follows from the uniqueness of the
\tau^{(0}
–enhancement
\xi
that the pleated surfaces
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k)}
and
\widetilde{f}_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k)}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}
has the same shear-bend parameters (see the end of the proof or Lemma
10
), which proves that
a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(k_{0})}=a_{\mathrm{hyp}}^{(0)}
and completes the proof of the first statement.

For the second statement, we use the well-known property that the character variety
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(M_{\varphi,r})
is smooth near its hyperbolic character, and that its complex dimension there is equal to the number
c

3.3.
The Chekhov-Fock algebra of an ideal triangulation, and its representations

The
Chekhov-Fock algebra

\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)=\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},X_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{e}^{\pm 1}]_{\tau}^{q}
of the ideal triangulation
\tau
is the algebra of Laurent polynomials in variables
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, …,
X_{e}
associated to the edges of
\tau
, which do not commute but instead are subject to the skew-commutativity relations

X_{i}X_{j}=q^{2\sigma_{ij}}X_{j}X_{i},

where each
\sigma_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer determined by adjacency properties between the
i
–th and
j
–th edges in the triangulation
\tau
. See
[
FC99
,
Liu09
,
BL07
]
for precise definitions of these coefficients
\sigma_{ij}
.

Algebraically,
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
is what is known as a quantum torus. In particular, its representation theory is fairly simple, at least once we know how to diagonalize over the integers the antisymmetric matrix whose entries are the
\sigma_{ij}
. This is done in
[
BL07
]
.

The classification of irreducible representations of
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
involves special elements
H_{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
associated to each puncture
v
of
S
. If
\gamma_{i_{1}}
,
\gamma_{i_{2}}
, …,
\gamma_{i_{j}}
are the edges of
\tau
that are adjacent to
v
(with an edge occurring twice in this list if both of its ends lead to
v
,), then

H_{v}=q^{-\sum_{1\leqslant k<k^{\prime}\leqslant j}\sigma_{kk^{\prime}}}X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}\dots X_{i_{j}}

where the
\sigma_{kk^{\prime}}
are the coefficients occurring in the skew-commutativity relations defining
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
. The power of
q
is specially designed so that this element
H_{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
does not depend on the ordering of the edges
\gamma_{i_{1}}
,
\gamma_{i_{2}}
, …,
\gamma_{i_{j}}
.

Proposition 13

(
[
BL07
, Theorem 21]
)
.

If
q
is a primitive
n
–root of unity with
n
odd, and if

is an irreducible representation of the Chekhov-Fock algebra

, then
\dim V=n^{3g+p-3}
if the surface

has genus

and
p
punctures, and there exist numbers
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, …,
a_{e}
,
h_{v_{1}}
,
h_{v_{2}}
, …,

such that

\displaystyle\rho(X_{i}^{n})

\displaystyle=a_{i}\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}

\displaystyle\rho(H_{v_{j}})

\displaystyle=h_{v_{j}}\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}

for every edge
\gamma_{i}
and puncture

of the ideal triangulation
\tau

In addition,

is determined up to isomorphism by this set of invariants
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, …,
a_{e}
,
h_{v_{1}}
,
h_{v_{2}}
, …,

In particular, an irreducible representation

3.5.
Comparing the Kauffman and Chekhov-Fock intertwiners

The proof will use a third algebra, the balanced Chekhov-Fock (square root) algebra
\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
of an ideal triangulation, which contains both the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
and the Kauffman bracket skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
.

The introduction of this balanced Chekhov-Fock algebra in
[
Hia10
]
is grounded in the following two geometric facts. The first one is that, if the character
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is associated to an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
for
\tau
as in §
3.1
, the trace
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\bar{r}(\alpha)
of an element
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(S)
can be expressed as an explicit Laurent polynomial in the
square roots
of the coordinates
a_{i}
of
a
. The second fact is that, algebraically, these square roots are not well-behaved under changes of the ideal triangulation
\tau
; for instance, in the case considered in Equation (
1
), solving for
\sqrt{a_{i}^{\prime}}
as a function of the
\sqrt{a_{j}}
yields an expression that may involve terms
\sqrt{1+\sqrt{a_{i_{0}}}^{2}}
and consequently may not be rational. The first fact leads us to consider formal square roots
Z_{i}
for the generators
X_{i}
of the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
. The technical difficulties arising from the second fact are addressed by introducing parity constraints.

More precisely, we first choose a
4
–root
q^{\frac{1}{4}}
of
q
and, for every ideal triangulation
\tau
of
S
, we consider the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)=\mathbb{C}\left[Z_{1}^{\pm 1},Z_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,Z_{e}^{\pm 1}\right]^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}
defined by generators
Z_{i}
associated to the edges of
\tau
and by the skew-commutativity relations

Z_{i}Z_{j}=\big(q^{\frac{1}{4}}\big)^{2\sigma_{ij}}Z_{j}Z_{i}

where
\sigma_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the same integer coefficients as in the definition of
\mathcal{T}^{q}_{\tau}(S)
, and is determined by the combinatorics of the ideal triangulation
\tau
.

In particular, there is a natural embedding
\mathcal{T}^{q}_{\tau}(S)\to\mathcal{T}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
sending each generator
X_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{q}_{\tau}(S)
to the square
Z_{i}^{2}
of the generator
Z_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
associated to the same edge of
\tau
.

The
balanced Chekhov-Fock algebra
of the ideal triangulation
\tau

for every three ideal triangulations
\tau

\tau^{\prime}
,
\tau^{\prime\prime}

Proof of Theorem
16
.

Let us first summarize the data of Theorem
16
. We have a
\varphi
–invariant irreducible character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
that admits no nontrivial sign-reversal symmetry, and whose projection
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is associated to an edge weight system for an ideal triangulation sweep from
\tau
to
\varphi(\tau)
.
The data of
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
and of
\varphi
–invariant compatible puncture weights

define an irreducible representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
as in Theorem
2
, and Proposition
4
then provides an isomorphism
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon V\to V
between the representations
\rho\circ\varphi_{*}
and
\rho
. The edge weight system and a data of
\varphi
–invariant compatible puncture weights

define an irreducible representation
\bar{\rho}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\bar{V})

15
now provides an isomorphism
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}\colon\bar{V}\to\bar{V}
between the representations
\bar{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q}
and
\bar{\rho}
.

We want to show that, under the hypothesis that
p_{v}^{2}=h_{v}+h_{v}^{-1}+2
for every puncture

, the two intertwiners
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
and
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
are conjugate, up to scalar multiplication by a root of unity.

It is time to reveal what underlies this condition that
p_{v}^{2}=h_{v}+h_{v}^{-1}+2
. In Theorem
2
, the puncture weight
p_{v}
is constrained by the property that
T_{n}(p_{n})=-\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}r(\alpha_{v})
for the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{n}
and for an element
\alpha_{v}\in\pi_{1}(S)
represented by a small loop going once around the puncture. In Proposition
13
, the constraint on

is that

must be equal to a quantity which, by Lemma
8
and Remark
9
, turns out to be equal to
\lambda_{v}^{2}
for an eigenvalue
\lambda_{v}
of
r(\alpha_{v})\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
singled out by the presentation of
[\bar{r}]
in terms of an edge weight system for the ideal triangulation
\tau
. Therefore, for the eigenvalue
\lambda_{v}
determined by the geometric setup,
p_{v}
and
h_{v}
are constrained by the equations
T_{n}(p_{v})=-\lambda_{v}-\lambda_{v}^{-1}
and

. By an elementary property of the Chebyshev polynomial already mentioned in §
1.3
(see for instance
[
BW17

), the solutions of the first equation are the numbers of the form
p_{v}=-k_{v}-k_{v}^{-1}
as

ranges over all
n

. A simple algebraic manipulation (slightly more elaborate when
\lambda_{v}=\pm 1
or

) then shows that the equation
p_{v}^{2}=h_{v}+h_{v}^{-1}+2
is equivalent to the existence of an
n
–root
k_{v}
of
\lambda_{v}
such that
p_{v}=-k_{v}-k_{v}^{-1}
and
h_{v}=k_{v}^{2}
.

Therefore, for every puncture
v
, we have an
n
–root
k_{v}
of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{v}
, such that
p_{v}=-k_{v}-k_{v}^{-1}
and
h_{v}=k_{v}^{2}
. We also have a character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
whose projection
[\bar{r}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
is associated to an edge weight system
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
for the ideal triangulation
\tau
. Proposition
17
associates to this data a representation
\breve{\rho}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
.

We first consider the restrictions of
\breve{\rho}
to the Kauffman bracket skeing algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
and to the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
.

Claim 18
.

The restriction
\breve{\rho}_{|\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
is isomorphic to the representation
\bar{\rho}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\bar{V})
.

Proof.

The representation
\breve{\rho}_{|\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)}
is irreducible, by comparison of the dimensions in Propositions
13
and
17
.

Because the generator
X_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
corresponds to
Z_{i}^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\tau}(S)
,
\breve{\rho}
sends
X_{i}
to
a_{i}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}
, where
a_{i}
if the
i
–coordinate of
a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
.

Also, the puncture element
H_{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
is equal to
K_{v}^{2}
for the puncture element
K_{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
, and is therefore sent to
k_{v}^{2}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}=h_{v}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}
by
\breve{\rho}
. It follows that the irreducible representation of
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
provided by the restriction of
\rho
is classified by the same invariants as

. By Proposition

, these two representations are therefore isomorphic.
∎

We can also consider, for the embedding
\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
, the restriction
\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
of
\breve{\rho}
to
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
.

Claim 19
.

The restriction
\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
is isomorphic to the representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)

Proof.

The classical shadow of
\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
is equal to
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, by the third conclusion of Proposition
17
.

For a puncture

, its invariant is defined by considering an element
[P_{v}]\in\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
represented by a simple closed loop going around the puncture. Lemma 18 of
[
BW17
]
shows that

in

. Therefore,

\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\big([P_{v}]=\breve{\rho}(K_{v}+K_{v}^{-1})=-(k_{v}+k_{v}^{-1})\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}=p_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}.

As a consequence, the representations
\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
and
\rho
of
\mathcal{K}^{q}
have the same dimension

, the same classical shadow
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
and the same puncture invariants
p_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
. Theorem
3
then shows that they are isomorphic.
∎

We have not yet used the diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
, the ideal triangulation sweep
\tau=\tau^{(0)}
,
\tau^{(1)}
, …,
\tau^{(k_{0}-1)}
,
\tau^{(k_{0})}=\varphi(\tau)
, or the periodic edge weight system
a=a^{(0)}
,
a^{(1)}
, …,
a^{(k_{0}-1)}
,
a^{(k_{0})}=a\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{e}
.

Let
\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)\to\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
be the Chekhov-Fock-Hiatt coordinate change isomorphism.
By Lemma 28 of
[
BW19
]
applied to each diagonal exchange, the existence of the edge weight system guarantees that the representation
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
makes sense. As in the definition of the intertwiner
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
, the diffeomorphism
\varphi
also induces an isomorphism
\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(S)\to\mathcal{T}_{\varphi(\tau)}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(S)
which restricts to an isomorphism
\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)\to\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)
between the corresponding balanced Chekhov-Fock algebras. We can then consider the representation
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
.

Claim 20
.

The representations
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}
and
\breve{\rho}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Because the square
Z_{i}^{2}\in\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
is also the
i
–th generator of the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
, the argument that we used in §
3.4
for
\bar{\rho}
, using Proposition
14
and the periodicity of the edge weight system, show that

\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(Z_{i}^{2})=a_{i}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}.

Also, for every puncture
v
, the homomorphism
\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(S)\to\mathcal{T}_{\varphi(\tau)}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(S)
induced by
\varphi
clearly sends the puncture element
K_{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
to
K_{\varphi(v)}\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)
, while Lemma 29 of
[
BW19
]
shows that the Chekhov-Fock-Hiatt coordinate change isomorphism
\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)\to\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
sends
K_{v^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\varphi(\tau)}(S)
to
K_{v^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
. It follows that

\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(K_{v})=\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}(K_{\varphi(v)})=\breve{\rho}(K_{\varphi(v)})=k_{\varphi(v)}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}}=k_{v}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\breve{V}},

using the
\varphi
–invariance of the puncture weights
k_{v}
.

Finally, a fundamental property of the quantum trace homomorphism
\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
is that it is well behaved with the Chekhov-Fock-Hiatt coordinate change isomorphisms, in the sense that

for any two ideal triangulations

[

in
[

]

\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\varphi_{*}
where
\varphi_{*}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
is the homomorphism induced by
\varphi
. Then,

\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}=\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\varphi_{*}=\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\circ\varphi_{*}

Looking at the definition of the classical shadow in
[
BW16
]
, it immediately follows that, since
\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
has classical shadow
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, then
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
has classical shadow
[r\circ\varphi_{*}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
where, this time,

denotes an arbitrary isomorphism of

induced by

is

–invariant by hypothesis,

and we conclude that
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
and

.

Therefore, the representations
\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}
and
\breve{\rho}
have the same invariants in the sense of Proposition

has no nontrivial sign-reversal symmetry, we can apply that statement and conclude that the two representations are isomorphic.
∎

We are now ready to conclude the proof of Theorem
16
.

By Claim
18
, the restriction
\breve{\rho}_{|\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
is isomorphic to the representation
\bar{\rho}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\bar{V})
.
Modifying
\bar{\rho}
by the corresponding isomorphisms (which only changes the intertwiner
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
by a conjugation), we can therefore assume that
\bar{V}=\breve{V}
and that
\bar{\rho}
is the restriction of
\breve{\rho}
to the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)\subset\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
.

Similarly, we can use Claim
19
to arrange that the original representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
of the skein algebra is equal to

\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(\breve{V})
.

By Claim
20
, there exists an intertwiner
\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon\breve{V}\to\breve{V}
such that

\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(Z)=\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}\cdot\breve{\rho}(\varepsilon Z)\cdot\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}

for every
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\tau}(S)
.

Since we arranged that the representations
\bar{\rho}
and
\breve{\rho}
coincide on
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
,

\bar{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q}(X)=\breve{\rho}\circ\Phi_{\tau\varphi(\tau)}^{q}\circ\Psi_{\varphi(\tau)\tau}^{q^{\frac{1}{4}}}(X)=\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}\cdot\breve{\rho}(\varepsilon X)\cdot\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}=\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}\cdot\bar{\rho}(X)\cdot\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}

for every
X\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S)
. This is the same intertwining property satisfied by
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
and, since
\bar{\rho}
is irreducible, it follows that
\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is a scalar multiple of
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
. Since the determinants of both isomorphisms have modulus 1, this scalar must be also have modulus 1.

The same argument applied to the other restriction
\rho=\breve{\rho}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\tau}^{q}
shows that
\breve{\Lambda}_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is obtained by multiplying
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
by a scalar with modulus 1.

This shows that the intertwiners
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
and
\bar{\Lambda}_{\varphi,\bar{r}}^{q}
coincide up to multiplication by a scalar with modulus 1, which concludes the proof of Theorem
16
.
∎

4.1.
Chekhov-Fock algebras for the one-puncture torus

An ideal triangulation
\tau

As a consequence of its definition, the Chekhov-Fock algebra

is then isomorphic to the algebra
\mathbb{C}\left[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1},Z^{\pm 1}\right]^{q}
of Laurent polynomials in variables
X
,
Y
and
Z
, respectively associated to the edges

,
f
,
g
, satisfying the

–commutativity relations

\displaystyle XY

\displaystyle=q^{4}YX

\displaystyle YZ

\displaystyle=q^{4}ZY

\displaystyle ZX

\displaystyle=q^{4}ZX.

The clockwise order of

,

,

around the faces of

This will enable us to express our computations in terms of the single abstract algebra
\mathcal{T}^{q}=\mathbb{C}\left[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1},Z^{\pm 1}\right]^{q}
defined by the above relations. In particular, we will now insist that an ideal triangulation
\tau
of

comes with a labelling of its edges as

,
f
,
g
, appearing clockwise in this order around the faces of
\tau
. This specifies a canonical isomorphism

from the abstract algebra
\mathcal{T}^{q}=\mathbb{C}\left[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1},Z^{\pm 1}\right]^{q}
to the Chekhov-Fock algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{q}(S_{1,1})
, sending the generators
X
,
Y
and
Z
to the generators respectively associated to the edges
e
,
f
,
g
.

4.2.
Standard representations of the algebra
\mathcal{T}^{q}

We now restrict attention to the case where
q
is a primitive
n
–root of unity with
n
odd.

The
standard representation
of the algebra
\mathcal{T}^{q}=\mathbb{C}\left[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1},Z^{\pm 1}\right]^{q}
associated to the nonzero numbers
x
,
y
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
is the representation
\rho_{xyz}\colon\mathcal{T}^{q}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
where, if
\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{xyz}(X)(w_{i})

\displaystyle=xq^{4i}w_{i}

\displaystyle\rho_{xyz}(Y)(w_{i})

\displaystyle=yq^{-2i}w_{i+1}

\displaystyle\rho_{xyz}(Z)(w_{i})

\displaystyle=zq^{-2i}w_{i-1}

for every
i=1
,
2
, …,
n
and counting indices modulo
n
.

Note that
\rho_{xyz}(X^{n})=x^{n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
,
\rho_{xyz}(Y^{n})=y^{n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
,
\rho_{xyz}(Z^{n})=z^{n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
and
\rho_{xyz}\big([XYZ]\big)=xyz\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
for the Weyl quantum ordering
[XYZ]=q^{-2}XYZ
of the monomial
XYZ
.

The following property is easily proved by elementary linear algebra (see also
[
BL07
, §4]
). Note that it crucially uses the fact that
n
is odd.

Proposition 21
.

1.

Every standard representation
\rho_{xyz}
is irreducible.

2.

Every irreducible representation of
\mathcal{T}^{q}
is isomorphic to a standard representation
\rho_{xyz}
.

3.

Two standard representation
\rho_{xyz}
and
\rho_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}}
are isomorphic if and only if
x^{n}=x^{\prime n}
,
y^{n}=y^{\prime n}
,
z^{n}=z^{\prime n}
and
xyz=x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}
. ∎

As a consequence, an irreducible representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{T}^{q}\to\mathrm{End}(V)
is classified, up to isomorphism, by the numbers
a
,
b
,
c
,
h\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\rho(X^{n})=a\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}
,
\rho(Y^{n})=b\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}
,
\rho(Z^{n})=c\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}
and
\rho\big([XYZ]\big)=h\,\mathrm{Id}_{V}
. Note that these four invariants
a
,
b
,
c
,
h
are tied by the relation
h^{n}=abc
, but that this is the only constraint between them. To compare with Proposition
13
, note that the puncture invariant
h_{v}
arising there is equal to
h^{2}
, and that
h
is the unique square root of
h_{v}
such that
h^{n}=abc
. .

4.3.
The discrete quantum dilogarithm

We introduce a fundamental building block for our computations of intertwiners.

The
discrete quantum dilogarithm
, or
Fadeev-Kashaev quantum dilogarithm

[
FK94
]
, is the function of the integer
i
defined by

{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,i)=\prod_{k=1}^{i}\frac{1+uq^{-2k}}{v}=v^{-i}\prod_{k=1}^{i}(1+uq^{-2k}),

where the parameters
u
,
v\in\mathbb{C}
are such that
v^{n}=1+u^{n}\neq 0
, and where
q
is a primitive
n
–root of unity with
n
odd.

An elementary computation provides the following periodicity property.

Lemma 22
.

{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,i+n)={\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,i).

for every index
i
. ∎

In particular, Lemma
22
enables us to define
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,i)
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

4.4.
The left and right diffeomorphisms, isomorphisms and intertwiners

It is well known that, up to isotopy, a diffeomorphism of
S_{1,1}
is completely determined by its action on the homology group
H_{1}(S_{1,1})
. If we choose an isomorphism
H_{1}(S_{1,1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, this provides us with an identification between the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})
and the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Once such an identification is chosen, the following elements

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{matrix}\right)

of
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
will play a fundamental role in our computation. The
left
and
right
elements
L
and
R
are well-known generators of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, with the terminology coming from their action on the Farey tessellation.

The following isomorphisms of the fraction algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{q}
will play an important role in our computations. We will see in §
4.6
that they are closely related to the above generators
L
and
R
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cong\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})
.

The
left isomorphism

\mathcal{L}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{q}\to\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{q}
and
right isomorphism

\mathcal{R}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{q}\to\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{q}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(X)

\displaystyle=Y^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)

\displaystyle=Z^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(Y)

\displaystyle=(1+qY)(1+q^{3}Y)X

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(Y)

\displaystyle=(1+qZ)(1+q^{3}Z)Y

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(Z)

\displaystyle=(1+qY^{-1})^{-1}(1+q^{3}Y^{-1})^{-1}Z

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(Z)

\displaystyle=(1+qZ^{-1})^{-1}(1+q^{3}Z^{-1})^{-1}X

For a standard representation
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\colon\mathcal{T}^{q}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, we want to determine the representations
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{L}
and
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{T}^{q}
.

Proposition 23
.

Suppose that
y_{1}^{n}\neq-1
.
Then the representation
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{L}\colon\mathcal{T}^{q}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
makes sense, and is isomorphic to any standard representation
\rho_{x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}}
with

\displaystyle x_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=y_{1}^{-n}

\displaystyle y_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+y_{1}^{n})^{2}x_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle z_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+y_{1}^{-n})^{-2}z_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}.

Similarly, if
z_{1}^{n}\neq-1
,
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{R}
makes sense and is isomorphic to any
\rho_{x_{3}y_{3}z_{3}}
with

\displaystyle x_{3}^{n}

\displaystyle=z_{1}^{-n}

\displaystyle y_{3}^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+z_{1}^{n})^{2}y_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle z_{3}^{n}

\displaystyle=(1+z_{1}^{-n})^{-2}x_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle x_{3}y_{3}z_{3}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}.

Proof.

This computation of the invariants of
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{L}
and
\rho_{x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}}\circ\mathcal{R}
can be found in
[
BL07
, Lemma 27]
. We already encountered this property in Proposition
14
.
∎

We now explicitly determine the isomorphisms whose existence is abstractly predicted by Proposition
23
.

We first define, for
u
,
v\in\mathbb{C}
with
v^{n}=1+u^{n}\neq 0
, the quantity

\displaystyle D^{q}(u)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,2i)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)

\displaystyle=v^{-\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+uq^{-2k})^{n-k+1}=(1+u^{n})^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+uq^{-2k})^{n-k+1}

which depends only on
u
,
q
and
n
.

The
left
and
right intertwiners
are the linear isomorphisms

and

,

with

for the standard basis

of

.

The normalization by the factor

was introduced to achieve the following property.

Lemma 24
.

The determinants
\det L_{uv}
and
\det R_{uv}
of the above linear isomorphisms have modulus

.

Proof.

By definition of

and remembering that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}=\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(2n+1)
,

\det R_{uv}=\frac{D^{q}(u)}{\left|D^{q}(u)\right|}\,q^{\frac{2}{3}n(n+1)(2n+1)}\det A

where

is the matrix whose

A_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}q^{-4ij}
.

The matrix

is a variation of the well-known discrete Fourier transform matrix. In particular, one easily computes that
A^{4}
is the identity matrix. It follows that
\det A
is a 4–root of unity, and in particular has modulus 1. This proves that
\left|\det R_{uv}\right|=1
.

A similar argument shows that
\left|\det L_{uv}\right|=1
.
∎

We now have the following explicit version of Proposition

.

\displaystyle=v_{2}^{-2}y_{1}^{2}z_{1}.

4.5.
The twist intertwiner

If

The formulas are a little simpler if we use the

,

.

Define

of

.

.

If

,

and

,

, then

for every

. In addition,

.

Proof.

This is again an elementary computation.
∎

In its full generality, the method above involves the computation of a lot of data. In practice, it is often possible to reduce this data by using various combinatorial arguments.

For instance, in the case of the one-puncture torus, there exist two (labelled) ideal triangulations up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, but we were able to restrict attention to one type. Also, by combining each diagonal exchange with an edge relabelling, we only had to consider two moves, the “left” move and the “right” move.

At Least One-in-Six Galaxies Is Always Dead
L.E. Abramson, D.D. Kelson

Abstract
Via numerical experiments, we show that the ∼ \sim 10%–20% passive fraction seen at z ≳ 3 z\gtrsim 3 is consistent with galaxy star formation histories being maximally correlated stochastic processes. If so, this fraction should reflect a time-independent baseline that holds at any epoch or mass regime where mean star formation rates are rising. Data at log ⁡ M ∗ ≤ 10 \log M_{\ast}\leq 10 and z ≲ 0.5 z\lesssim 0.5 bear this out, as should future James Webb Space Telescope observations.



Galaxy star formation histories (SFHs) can be modeled as stochastic
processes
(
Kelson 2014
;
Kelson et al. 2016
;
Kelson et al. 2020
)
. In this framework, star formation rates (SFRs)
are non-negative sums of random numbers. The SFR at the epoch of observation,
T
, can
be independent of values at earlier times,
t
, or correlated with them, on average, via a
weighting factor
(T-t)^{H-1/2}
. Here,
H
defines the strength of the correlation

(
Mandelbrot & Van Ness 1968
)
. If
H=1/2
, the SFH returns to a purely random Brownian walk.
If
1/2<H\leq 1
, past SFR changes bias future changes in the same direction.

Ensembles of stochastic SFHs describe coeval galaxy populations. While the star formation rate
of any individual member cannot be predicted, the ensemble’s mean and dispersion evolve
deterministically in time as functions of
H

(
Kelson 2014
)
:

\displaystyle\langle{\rm SFR}(T)\rangle

\displaystyle\propto\frac{T^{H}}{H},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sig}[{\rm SFR}(T)]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{H}\,\langle{\rm SFR}(T)\rangle.

(2)

These equations have been shown to hold at redshifts preceding the peak of cosmic star formation

(
Kelson et al. 2016
)
and in mass regimes where sSFRs (
\equiv{\rm SFR}/M_{\ast}
) are
mass-independent
(
Kelson 2014
)
.

While an ensemble’s mean SFR always rises in time, its dispersion grows more slowly for all
H<1
.
So, at fixed epoch, more objects will reach
{\rm SFR}(T)=0
if SFHs are maximally correlated (
H=1
)
rather than random (
H=1/2
). Intuitively, this trend reflects the fact that random walks spend less
time at
{\rm SFR}=0
than ones in which past declines bias future changes toward zero. Physically, it
means that the passive galaxy fraction constrains SFH stochasticity.

Additional processes separate what we have calculated—the fraction of galaxies expected
to have zero star formation given normal fluctuations in hospitable conditions—from the “quenched”
fraction measured at higher masses or later times
(
Peng et al. 2010

. The framework discussed
here does not yet capture those forces, so our results should be interpreted as a floor or null
expectation for the passive fraction in complete ensembles.

As such, we predict that future unbiased JWST surveys will find 16% to 20% of galaxies to
be classifiable as passive at all
z\gtrsim 3
, and also at lower redshifts in mass regimes where
sSFRs are mass-independent. We look forward to forthcoming tests!

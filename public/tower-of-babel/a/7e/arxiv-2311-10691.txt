Generalized products and Lorentzian length spaces
Elefterios Soultanis

Abstract
. We construct a Lorentzian length space with an orthogonal splitting on a product I × X I\times X of an interval and a metric space, and use this framework to consider the relationship between metric and causal geometry, as well as synthetic time-like Ricci curvature bounds. The generalized Lorentzian product naturally has a Lorentzian length structure but can fail the push-up condition in general. We recover the push-up property under a log-Lipschitz condition on the time variable and establish sufficient conditions for global hyperbolicity. Moreover we formulate time-like Ricci curvature bounds without push-up and regularity assumptions, and obtain a partial rigidity of the splitting under a strong energy condition.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and outline of results

A (smooth) globally hyperbolic metric
g
on a spacetime
M
admits an orthogonal splitting

(1.1)

\displaystyle g=-h^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+g_{t},\quad M=(a,b)\times S

where
g_{t}
is a “time dependent” Riemannian metric on the spacelike hypersurface
\{t\}\times S\subset M
and
h\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive (smooth) function
[
6
]
. Galloway’s splitting theorem
[
15
]
further improves (
1.1
) to the isometric identification
(M,g)\simeq(\mathbb{R}\times S,-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+g_{S})
(with
g_{S}
constant in
t
) assuming the
strong energy condition

{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for time-like vectors
X
, and that
M
contains a time-like geodesic line. Galloway’s result can be interpreted as
rigidity
imposed on (
1.1
) by the strong energy condition and the existence of complete time-like lines.

For continuous globally hyperbolic metrics only a
topological
splitting
M\approx(a,b)\times S
is available, as a result of the stability of global hyperbolicity
[
34
]
, see also
[
17
]
. The continuous case is an important step towards a theory of non-smooth Lorentzian geometry, motivated e.g. by the study of singularity theorems and by Penrose’s cosmic censorship conjecture. In this direction, the so called
Lorentzian pre-length spaces
, introduced by Kunzinger–Sämann
[
29
]
, have become a popular framework. They consist of a causal set
Y=(Y,\leq,\ll)
satisfying the
push-up property
, and a time separation function
\tau:Y\times Y\to[0,\infty]
satisfying a
reverse triangle inequality
(cf. Definition
2.2
). Lorentzian pre-length spaces provide an axiomatization of the causal geometry of smooth spacetimes in the spirit of metric geometry.

In this setting, synthetic curvature conditions have been proposed in analogy with the metric theories of sectional (Alexandrov) and Ricci (RCD) curvature bounds. For
sectional curvature bounds

[
29
,
1
,
4
]
, an analogue of Galloway’s splitting theorem has been obtained in
[
4
]
. For
Ricci curvature bounds
, introduced in
[
32
,
12
]
using optimal transport, general singularity theorems were proven in
[
13
]
but splitting theorems are not yet available. This is due in part to the lack of a notion of infinitesimal Hilbertianity. In the metric theory this notion arises from the “analytic” description of synthetic Ricci bounds via the Bochner inequality
[
2
]
and is known to be a necessary condition for the validity of splitting theorems
[
18
,
19
]
. Analytic non-smooth Lorentzian geometry is based on
lower gradients
(to appear in the forthcoming work
[
3
]
), but its connection to optimal transport is yet to be explored.

In general, despite the keen interest in Lorentzian length spaces and metric techniques in recent years
[
9
,
30
,
33
,
23
,
5
]
, a number of questions remain open, e.g. (i) examples of Lorentzian length spaces, (ii) the relationship between metric and causal geometry, and (iii) splitting theorems under Ricci bounds. Indeed, not many examples of Lorentzian length spaces are known beyond those arising from smooth (or
C^{1,1}
) spacetimes, while the connection of metric products and causal geometry has only been considered in the work of Alexander et. al.
[
1
]
on Lorentzian warped products – a special case of (
1.1
) – for sectional curvature bounds.

In this article we construct a generalization of (
1.1
) on product spaces where the hypersurface
S
is a metric space. Indeed, given a metric space
X
, an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, a continuous function
h:I\times X\to(0,\infty)
, and a family
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{s}\}_{s\in I}
of compatible metrics on

, we construct the
generalized Lorentzian product
, denoted
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
. For it, we establish (1) the push-up principle (Theorem
1.3
) under a log-Lipschitz condition, (2) sufficient conditions for global hyperbolicity (Theorem
1.5
), and (3) partial rigidity of splittings under strong energy conditions (Corollary
1.7
).

The construction of the generalized Lorentzian product gives new examples of Lorentzian length spaces, cf. Corollary
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
. It moreover generalizes the Lorentzian warped product considered in
[
1
]
and is compatible with (
1.1
) in the setting of manifolds: if
X
is a smooth manifold and
\mathcal{F}
is given by continuous Riemannian metrics, then
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
coincides with the Lorentzian length structure induced by (

), cf. Corollary

.

In contrast to the warped products of
[

]
(which correspond to

for a continuous function

and a fixed metric

on

), pathological causal phenomena may occur in the generality of our setting (see e.g.
[
14
,
16
]
), violating the push-up property required in the definition of Lorentzian length spaces. In Lorentzian manifolds, causal theory is known to break down for metrics below Lipschitz regularity
[
14
,
21
]
. We identify a log-Lipschitz condition (

) on
\mathcal{F}
– inspired by the work of Kopfer–Sturm
[
27
,
28
,
26
]
– ensuring that the generalized Lorentzian product

is a Lorentzian length space (cf. Corollary

). Moreover we show the regularity of

for conformal metrics in Theorem

, generalizing the corresponding fact for Lipschitz continuous Lorentzian metrics
[

,

]
in the special case of conformal metrics. Applying synthetic time-like curvature bounds to the generalized Lorentzian product, we obtain the following rigidity statement (Corollary

equipped with a measure

are independent of

.

1.2.
Generalized Lorentzian product

Throughout this paper
X
is a locally compact topological space and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an (open) interval. Suppose
h:I\times X\to(0,\infty)
is continuous, and
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{s}\}_{s\in I}
is a family of metrics on
X
whose induced length metrics generate the topology of
X
.
1
1

1

This is the case for example when each metric in
\mathcal{F}
is a length metric or a quasiconvex metric.
We make the following local continuity assumption: for each compact
J\subset I
and
K\subset X
, there exists
r=r_{J,K}>0
and a modulus of continuity
\omega=\omega_{J,K}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(1.2)

\Big|\log\frac{d_{s}(x,y)}{d_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)}\Big|\leq\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|),\quad\textrm{ for }s,s^{\prime}\in J,\ x,y\in K\textrm{ with }\inf_{s\in J}d_{s}(x,y)<r.

In particular all the metrics in
\mathcal{F}
are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent, and the absolute continuity of a curve
\beta:[a,b]\to X
is independent of the chosen metric in
\mathcal{F}
. The
generalized metric speed
of an absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\to X
is a map
v_{\beta}:I\times[a,b]\to[0,\infty]
with the following properties (cf. Lemma
3.2
):

•

for each
t\in[a,b]
,
s\mapsto v_{\beta}(s,t)
is continuous in
I
, and

•

for each
s\in I
we have that
v_{\beta}(s,t)=|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{s}}
a.e.
t
.
2
2

2

Here
|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{s}}
denotes the metric speed of
\beta
with respect to the metric
d_{s}
.

The
generalized length element
of an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to I\times X
is

(1.3)

\displaystyle|\tau_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=h(\gamma_{t})^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2},

and we say that
\gamma
is (a)
causal
, if
|\tau_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}(t)\geq 0
a.e.
t\in[a,b]
, and (b)
timelike
, if
|\tau_{\gamma}^{\prime}|^{2}(t)>0
a.e.
t\in[a,b]
, cf. Definition
4.1
. A standard construction detailed in Section
4.1
gives rise to a causal set
(I\times X,\leq,\ll)
and time separation function
\tau
on
I\times X
. In the terminology of
[
1
, Appendix A]
, the collection
(I\times X,\leq,\ll,\tau)
is a
Lorentzian length structure
, which we denote by
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
and call the
generalized Lorentzian product
. In Corollary
4.15
we show that when
X
is a smooth manifold and
\mathcal{F}
given by continuous Riemannian metrics, the length elements given by (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) coincide. As a direct consequence we obtain the compatibility of
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
and the Lorentzian length structure induced by the Lorentzian product metric.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth manifold,
h:I\times X\to(0,\infty)
continuous, and suppose
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in I}
is a family of Riemannian metrics on
X
so that the local coordinate representations
(g_{s})_{ij}(p)
are continuous in
(s,p)
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{g_{s}}\}_{s\in I}
, where
d_{g_{s}}
is the length metric associated to
g_{s}
for each
s\in I
. Then
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is the Lorentzian length structure induced by
g\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-h^{2}\mathrm{d}s^{2}+g_{s}
.

1.3.
Lorentzian length spaces and global hyperbolicity

Let
h
and
\mathcal{F}
be as in Section
1.2
. Under these hypotheses, the generalized Lorentzian product
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
might have causal bubbles (see
[
16
]
) and thus fail to be a Lorentzian length space, as a result of failing the push-up property. Recall that a causal set
(Y,\leq,\ll)
satisfies the push-up property if
x\ll z
whenever
x\leq y
and
y\ll z
or
x\ll y
and
y\leq z
.

To prevent this failure, we impose a log-Lipschitz condition in the spirit of
[
27
,
28
,
26
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies a local log-Lipschitz condition if, for every compact
J\subset I
,
K\subset X
, there exists
C=C_{J,K},r=r_{J,K}>0
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Big|\log\frac{d_{s}(x,y)}{d_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)}\Big|\leq C|s-s^{\prime}|,\quad\textrm{ for }s,s^{\prime}\in J,\ x,y\in K\textrm{ with }\inf_{s\in J}d_{s}(x,y)<r.

In other words, we require that the moduli of continuity
\omega
in (
1.2
) are linear, corresponding to a Lipschitz-type control on the time-variable. We remark here that (
1.4
) in Definition
1.2
is inspired by the work of Kopfer–Sturm on Super Ricci flows on metric spaces
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
, where a similar condition appears. Whereas they focus on the evolution and gradient flows of “variable metric spaces,” we instead consider the Lorentzian geometry of the product.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
1.4
) and, for every compact
J\subset I
,
K\subset X
there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(1.5)

|h(s,x)-h(s^{\prime},x)|\leq C^{\prime}|s-s^{\prime}|,\quad s,s^{\prime}\in J,\ x\in K.

Then
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
satisfies the push-up property.

The push-up property further implies the lower semicontinuity of the associated time separation function
\tau
on
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
. These facts together ensure the generalized Lorentzian product is a Lorentzian length space.

Corollary 1.4
.

If
\mathcal{F}
and
h
satisfy (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), respectively, then
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is a Lorentzian length space.

The Lorentzian length space
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is moreover strongly local, see Proposition
5.4
. However, local conditions such as (
1.4
) do not guarantee global hyperbolicity. While it is easy to see that
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is globally hyperbolic if
h
is bounded and the metrics in
\mathcal{F}
are globally bi-Lipschitz to a proper metric (with uniformly bounded bi-Lipschitz constants), examples in
[
35
]
show that properness of the slices does not imply global hyperbolicity, nor does global hyperbolicity imply that the slices are proper. For smooth spacetimes, global hyperbolicity is nevertheless equivalent the existence of a proper metric on the underlying space: in
[
10
]
this is shown by considering the null-distance associated to a suitable time function. However in our context the natural choice of time function
(t,x)\mapsto t
does not necessarily yield a proper null-distance, cf.
[
35
, § 6.1]
.

In Theorem
1.5
below we give a sufficient condition for the global hyperbolicity in terms of
\mathcal{F}
and
h
. The criterion (
1.6
) essentially characterizes the properness of a natural length metric on
I\times X
associated with
\mathcal{F}
and
h
(see Section
3.2
) and is phrased in terms of curves
tending to infinity
. A locally absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[0,\infty)\to X
is said to tend to infinity if, for every compact
K\subset X
, there exists
T>0
such that
K\cap\beta([T,\infty))=\varnothing
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
and
h
satisfy (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), and moreover

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)}{h(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})}\mathrm{d}t=\infty

whenever
\beta:[0,\infty)\to X
tends to infinity and
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to I
is locally absolutely continuous. Then
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is globally hyperbolic.

Observe that (
1.6
) does not imply the properness of the metrics in
\mathcal{F}
but instead of the conformally weighted metrics
(d_{s})_{h(s,\cdot)^{-1}}
; however it is a stronger condition than the propeness of each
(d_{s})_{h(s,\cdot)^{-1}}
,
s\in I
.

1.4.
Regularity

The regularity of
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
seems more difficult to establish. Roughly speaking, a Lorentzian length space is called regular, if
maximizing
curves are either null or time-like (have a definite causal character). These are curves maximizing the Lorentzian length of curves joining given points, and they replace
geodesics
in the current non-smooth framework where no geodesic equation is available. Unlike geodesics, maximizing curves in Lorentzian length spaces need not have a definite causal character; they can have null-segments while having positive Lorentzian length. Regularity is known to hold for spacetimes with a Lorentzian metric of Lipschitz regularity, due to a weak formulation of the geodesic equation
[
20
,
31
]
. However, even in the setting of Lorentzian metrics, (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are weaker than Lipschitz regularity, and we do not know whether they imply regularity of the spacetime in the absence of a suitable Lipschitz condition on the space variable. However we establish regularity of
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
in the current framework in the special case
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{\rho(s,\cdot)}\}_{s\in I}
, where
\rho:I\times X\to(0,\infty)
is a (locally Lipschitz) is a given
conformal weight
. Here, given a length metric
d
on
X
and a continuous
g:X\to(0,\infty)
, the conformally weighted metric
d_{g}
is a length metric on
X
such that

|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{g}}=g(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\quad a.e.\ t

for curves
\beta
in
X
. The precise definition is given in Section
2.1
, compare (
2.2
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
d
be a locally compact length metric on
X
. Suppose that
h,\rho:I\times X\to(0,\infty)
are locally Lipschitz, and set
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{\rho(s,\cdot)}\}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
1.4
), and
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is a regular Lorentzian length space.

The metric techniques used in

Here,

is Borel whenever

is Borel, (b)
\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{m_{s}(B_{d_{s}}(x,2r))}{m_{s}(B_{d_{s}}(x,r))}<\infty

m_{s}
-a.e.
x\in X
for each
s\in I
(the infinitesimal doubling property), and (c) for each compact
J\subset I
and
K\subset X
, there exists
C=C(J,K)>0
with

(1.7)

\displaystyle m_{s^{\prime}}|_{K}\leq Cm_{s}|_{K},\quad s,s^{\prime}\in J.

In Section

(

)-condition on Lorentzian length structures; the ensuing notion (Definition

2.
Background

2.1.
Metric spaces

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A curve
\beta:[a,b]\to X
is called absolutely continuous if there exists
g\in L^{1}(a,b)
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle d(\beta_{s},\beta_{t})\leq\int_{t}^{s}g(r)\mathrm{d}r,\quad a\leq t\leq s\leq b.

In this case, the limit
\displaystyle|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{d(\beta_{t+h},\beta_{t})}{|h|}
exists a.e.
t\in[a,b]
, and
t\mapsto|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|
is the pointwise a.e. smallest function for which (
2.1
) holds. We define the length of
\beta
as
\displaystyle\ell(\beta)=\int_{a}^{b}|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t.
Moreover, there exists a non-decreasing function
s_{\beta}:[0,\ell(\beta)]\to[a,b]
and a parametrization
\bar{\beta}:[0,\ell(\beta)]\to X
, called the
arc-length parametrization
of
\beta
, such that
\beta\circ s_{\beta}=\bar{\beta}
and
|\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\prime}|=1
for every
t\in[0,\ell(\beta)]
. See
[
8
,
22
]
. We say that
d
is a length metric if

\displaystyle d(x,y)=\inf\{\ell(\beta)|\ \beta:x\curvearrowright y\},\quad x,y\in X.

To define metrics with a given conformal factor, we first define the line integral of a Borel function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
along an absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\to X
by

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}g\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{a}^{b}g(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t.

Note that the line-integral is independent of the parametrization of
\beta
, cf.
[
22
, Theorem 3.12]
. Suppose now
d
is a locally compact length metric on
X
, and let
\rho:X\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous function. We define the weighted length

\displaystyle L_{\rho}(\beta)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\beta}\rho\,\mathrm{d}s

for any absolutely continuous curve
\beta
in
X
. Then
L_{\rho}
is lower semicontinuous, and the arising length structure
(L_{\rho},AC(X))
on
X
is compatible with the metric topology of
(X,d)
; the length metric

\displaystyle d_{\rho}(x,y)=\inf\Big\{\int_{a}^{b}\rho(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\Big|\ \beta:x\curvearrowright y\},\quad x,y\in X,

induces the same topology as
d
. Moreover by
[
8
, Theorem 2.4.3]
we have that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{\rho}}=\rho(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\quad a.e.\ t\in[a,b]

for every absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\to(X,d)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(X,d)
is a locally compact length space, and
\rho:X\to(0,\infty)
continuous. If
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t=\infty
whenever
\beta:[0,\infty)\to X
is locally absolutely continuous and leaves every compact subset of
X
, then
(X,d_{\rho})
is proper and geodesic.

Proof.

By the Hopf–Rinow theorem it suffices to show that
(X,d_{\rho})
is complete. Suppose
(x_{j})\subset X
is a Cauchy sequence for
d_{\rho}
. The claim follows if we show that
(x_{j})
contains a subsequence converging to a point
x\in X
with respect to
d_{\rho}
. Pass to a subsequence (labeled with the same indices) such that
d_{\rho}(x_{j},x_{j+1})<2^{-j-1}
, and let
\beta_{j}:[j,j+1]\to X
be an absolutely continuous curve connecting
x_{j}
to
x_{j+1}
constant speed parametrized (with respect to
d
) such that
L_{\rho}(\beta_{j})<d_{\rho}(x_{j},x_{j+1})+2^{-j-1}<2^{-j}
. Consider the concatenation
\beta:[0,\infty)\to X
defined such that
\beta|_{[0,N]}=\beta_{N-1}\cdots\beta_{1}\beta_{0}
. We have that
\beta
is locally absolutely continuous and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d}\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}L_{\rho}(\beta_{j})<2,

hence– by our assumption – it cannot leave every compact set. Consequently there exists a compact set
K\subset X
such that
(x_{j})\subset K
. We conclude that a subsequence of
(x_{j})
converges to
x\in X
in
d
, and thus in
d_{\rho}
.

∎

2.2.
Lorentzian length spaces

The definitions below are taken from
[
29
]
, which the interested reader should consult for more details. A causal set is a space
Y
equipped with a transitive and reflexive relation
\leq
, together with a transitive relation
\ll
contained in
\leq
. We say that a causal set
(Y,\leq,\ll)
satisfies the push-up property, if
x\ll z
whenever
x\leq y
and
y\ll z
or
x\ll y
and
y\leq z
.

Definition 2.2
.

A Lorentzian pre-length space
Y=(Y,\leq,\ll,\tau)
consists of a causal set
(Y,\leq,\ll)
together with a metric
d
on
Y
, and a lower semicontinuous time-separation function
\tau:Y\times Y\to[0,\infty]
such that
\tau(x,y)>0
if and only if
x\ll y
, satisfying the reverse triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\tau(x,y)+\tau(y,z)\leq\tau(x,z)\quad\textrm{if}\quad x\leq y\leq z.

A Lorentzian pre-length space satisfies the push-up property, and the past

I^{+}(x)=\{y:x\ll y\}
and future
I^{-}(x)=\{y:y\ll x\}
of a point
x\in Y
are open. The corresponding sets with
\ll
replaced by
\leq
are denoted
J^{\pm}(x)
. A Lorentzian pre-length space
Y
is called

•

locally causally closed
, if every point in
Y
has a neighbourhood
U
such that
\leq
is closed in
\bar{U}\times\bar{U}
;

•

strongly causal
, if the metric topology of
Y
is generated by the chronological diamonds
I(x,y)=I^{+}(x)\cap I^{-}(y)
,
x,y\in Y
;

•

totally non-imprisoning
, if for any compact
K\subset Y
there exists
C
such that
L_{\tau}(\gamma)\leq C
for any causal curve
\gamma
in
K
;

•

Globally hyperbolic
, if it is totally non-imprisoning and causal diamonds
J(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=J^{+}(x)\cap J^{-}(y)
(
x,y\in Y
) are compact.

A curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to Y
is future-directed causal (resp. time-like) if
\gamma_{t}\leq\gamma_{s}
(resp.
\gamma_{t}\ll\gamma_{s}
) whenever
a\leq t<s\leq b
. The
Lorentzian length
or
\tau
-length of a causal curve
\gamma
is

(2.3)

\displaystyle L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau(\gamma_{t_{i-1}},\gamma_{t_{i}}):\ a=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N}=b\Big\},

and
\gamma
is called
maximal
, if
L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\tau(\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b})
. We call a Lorentzian pre-length space
Y

(i)

causally path connected
, if there exists a causal curve
\gamma:x\curvearrowright y
whenever
x\leq y
, and a time-like curve
\gamma:x\curvearrowright y
whenever
x\ll y
;

(ii)

geodesic
, if there exists a maximal curve
\gamma:x\curvearrowright y
whenever
x\leq y
;

(iii)

localizable
, if every point in
Y
has a neighbourhood
U
such that

L_{\tau}^{U}(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup\{L_{\tau}(\gamma):\ \gamma:p\curvearrowright q\textrm{ causal, and }\gamma\subset U\},\quad p,q\in U

is bounded from above and
L_{\tau}^{U}\leq\tau|_{U\times U}
, and moreover
Y_{U}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(U,\leq_{U\times U},\ll_{U\times U},L_{\tau}^{U})
is a geodesic Lorentzian pre-length space;

(iv)

locally regularizable
, if the neighbourhood
U
above can be chosen so that maximizing curves in
Y_{U}
with positive
\tau
-length are time-like.

A neighbourhood
U
as in (iii) and/or (iv) is called a localizing neighbourhood. Locally regularizability ensures that maximizing curves have a definite causal character, i.e. they are either time-like or null (zero
\tau
-length), cf.
[
29
, Theorem 3.18]
.

Definition 2.3
.

(Definition 2.8 and 3.22 in
[
29
]
)
A localizable, locally causally closed, and causally path connected Lorentzian pre-length space
(Y,\leq,\ll,\tau)
is called a

(1)

Lorentzian length space
, if
\tau(x,y)=\sup\{L_{\tau}(\gamma):\ \gamma:x\curvearrowright y\textrm{ causal}\}
whenever
x\leq y
;

(2)

regular Lorentzian length space
, if it is a locally regularizable Lorentzian length space;

(3)

Lorentzian geodesic space
, if it is a geodesic Lorentzian length space.

2.3.
Lorentzian length structures

We sketch the idea of Lorentzian lengths structures here and refer to
[
1
, Appendix A]
for the precise definitions. Suppose
\mathcal{I}^{+}\subset\mathcal{C}^{+}\subset AC(Y,d)
are families of curves in a metric space
(Y,d)
closed under concatenation, restriction, and reparametrization. If
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\gamma}}
denotes the curve
\gamma
with reversed orientation and
\mathcal{I}^{-}=\{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\gamma}}:\ \gamma\in\mathcal{I}^{+}\}
,
\mathcal{C}^{-}=\{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\gamma}}:\ \gamma\in\mathcal{C}^{+}\}
, then
(\mathcal{I}^{\pm},\mathcal{C}^{\pm})
is admissible in the sense of
[
1
, Definition A.1]
. For the following definition set
\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{I}^{-}\cup\mathcal{I}^{+}
,
\mathcal{C}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{C}^{-}\cup\mathcal{C}^{+}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A Lorentzian length structure on
Y
consists of such a tuple
(\mathcal{I}^{\pm},\mathcal{C}^{\pm})
together with a Lorentzian length functional
L:\mathcal{C}\to[0,\infty]
that is additive, invariant under reparametrization, strictly positive on
\mathcal{I}
, and compatible with the topology: if
\gamma:[a,b]\to Y
is in
\mathcal{C}
, then
t\mapsto L(\gamma|_{[a,t]})
is continuous.

We call curves in
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}
) past/future time-like (resp. causal). A Lorentzian length structure
(Y,\mathcal{I}^{\pm},\mathcal{C}^{\pm},L)
induces a causal set
(Y,\leq,\ll)
, where
x\leq y
if there exists
\gamma:x\curvearrowright y
in
\mathcal{C}^{+}
, and
x\ll y
if there exists
\gamma:x\curvearrowright y
in
\mathcal{I}^{+}
, cf.
[
1
, Lemma A.4]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\tau_{L}(x,y)=\sup\{L(\gamma):\ \gamma:x\curvearrowright y\textrm{ is in }\mathcal{C}^{+}\}

(with the convention
\sup\varnothing=0
) defines a time-separation function which is positive on
\ll
and satisfies the reverse triangle inequality, cf.
[
1
, Lemma A.6 and A.7]
. The induced tuple
(Y,\leq,\ll,\tau_{L})
is causally path connected, and satisfies the length condition in Definition
2.3
(1). However
\tau
is not lower semicontinuous and the push-up property may not hold in general.

Remark 2.5
.

If
(Y,\leq,\ll,\tau)
is a Lorentzian length space and
\mathcal{I}^{+}
,
\mathcal{C}^{+}
are the families of future directed time-like and causal curves, respectively, and if
L
is given by (
2.3
), then
(\mathcal{I}^{\pm},\mathcal{C}^{\pm},L)
is a Lorentzian length structure whose induced causal set coincides with
(Y,\leq,\ll)
, and
\tau=\tau_{L}
.

2.4.
Synthetic time-like Ricci curvature bounds

We define the (weak) TCD
p
(
K,N
)-condition for Lorentzian length structures

for

,
K\in\mathbb{R}
and

following the ideas in
[

,
7
]
.

Given two measures

, a coupling of

is a measure
\bm{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(Y\times Y)
such that
(p_{1\ast}\bm{\pi},p_{2\ast}\bm{\pi})=(\mu,\nu)
. A coupling is causal if it is concentrated on
\leq
, and their collection is denoted
\Pi_{\leq}(\mu,\nu)
. Causal couplings are closely related with

, that is measures
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,1],Y))
concentrated on
\mathcal{C}^{+}
: if
\bm{\eta}
is a causal plan, then
(e_{0},e_{1})_{\ast}\bm{\eta}
is a causal coupling of
(e_{0\ast}\bm{\eta},e_{1\ast}\bm{\eta})
. Here
e_{t}:C([0,1],Y)\to Y
is the evaluation map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma_{t}
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Definition 2.6
.

A functional
S:\mathcal{P}(Y)\to[-\infty,\infty]
is
(K,N)
-convex along a causal plan
\bm{\eta}
, if the function
t\mapsto u(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\exp(-S(e_{t\ast}\bm{\eta})/N)
satisfies

\displaystyle u(t)\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{1-t}(T_{\bm{\eta}})u(0)+\sigma_{K/N}^{t}(T_{\bm{\eta}})u(1),\quad\textrm{where}\quad T_{\bm{\eta}}=\bigg(\int L(\gamma)^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}(\gamma)\bigg)^{1/2}.

Equivalently,
u
is semiconvex and satisfies
\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{K}{N}T_{\bm{\eta}}\,u(t)
a.e.
t\in[0,1]
.

In the definition above we use the auxiliary functions

\displaystyle\sigma_{\kappa}^{t}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{s_{\kappa}(t\theta)}{s_{\kappa}(\theta)}&\kappa\theta^{2}<0\textrm{ or }\kappa\theta^{2}\in(0,\pi^{2})\\
t&\kappa\theta^{2}=0\\
\infty&\kappa\theta^{2}\geq\pi^{2}\end{array}\right.,\quad s_{\kappa}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}\theta)}{\sqrt{\kappa}}&\kappa>0\\
\theta&\kappa=0\\
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-\kappa}\theta)}{\sqrt{-\kappa}}&\kappa<0.\end{array}\right.

Next we define
p

with the convention

. Moreover, let

denote the family of curves

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})=(s-t)L(\gamma),\quad a\leq t\leq s\leq b.

Note that, since

, its elements are automatically continuous.

Definition 2.7
.

A

-optimal time-like plan is a causal plan concentrated on

\displaystyle\ell_{p}(e_{0\ast}\bm{\eta},e_{1\ast}\bm{\eta})^{p}=\int L(\gamma)^{p}\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}(\gamma).

Let

.

Let

3.
A generalized product metric

3.1.
Generalized metric speed

Let
X
be a topological space,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an interval, and
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{s}\}_{s\in I}
a family of metrics on
X
satisfying (
1.2
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\beta:[a,b]\to X
be an absolutely continuous curve. We define
v_{\beta}:I\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle v_{\beta}(s,t)=\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}|\beta^{\prime}_{\tau}|_{d_{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
J\subset I
and
K\subset X
are compact. Let
\beta:[a,b]\to K
be an absolutely continuous curve. There exists
C=C(J,K)
such that

(1)

for every
s\in I
,
v_{\beta}(s,t)=|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{s}}
a.e.
t\in[a,b]
;

(2)

for every compact subinterval
J\subset I
we have that

\frac{v_{\beta}(s,t)}{v_{\beta}(s^{\prime},t)}\leq 1+C\omega(|s^{\prime}-s|),\quad t\in[a,b],\ s,s^{\prime}\in J,

where
\omega=\omega_{J,K}
is the modulus of continuity in (
1.2
).

In the proof we use the observation
e^{A}\leq 1+C(D)A
for
A\in[0,D]
, which implies that (
1.2
) can be equivalently expressed as

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d_{s}(x,y)}{d_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)}\leq 1+C\omega_{J,K}(|s-s^{\prime}|)\quad\textrm{ for }s,s^{\prime}\in J,\ x,y\in K\textrm{ with }\inf_{s\in J}d_{s}(x,y)<r,

for some constants
C=C_{J,K},r=r_{J,K}>0
for each compact
J\subset I
and
K\subset X
. If
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
1.4
), the right hand side of (
3.1
) can be replaced by
1+C|s-s^{\prime}|
. In light of this, Lemma
3.2
has the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
1.4
). For every compact
K\subset X
and
J\subset I
, there exists
C=C_{K,J}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{v_{\beta}(s^{\prime},t)}{v_{\beta}(s,t)}\leq 1+C|s^{\prime}-s|,\quad t\in[a,b],\ s,s^{\prime}\in J,

for any absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\to K
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem since, for fixed
s\in I
, the map
t\mapsto|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{s}}
is
L^{1}
-integrable by the absolute continuity of
\beta
with respect to
d_{s}
.

By (
3.1
) we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{d_{s}}(\beta|_{[t,t+h]})}{h}\leq\frac{\ell_{d_{s^{\prime}}}(\beta|_{[t,t+h]})}{h}(1+C\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|))

and, upon taking limsup as
h\to 0
,

v_{\beta}(s,t)\leq v_{\beta}(s^{\prime},t)(1+C\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|))

for
s,s^{\prime}\in J
. This proves (2).
∎

The following change of variables formula for
v_{\beta}
will be very useful.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\beta:[a,b]\to X
be an AC-curve, and
\varphi:[c,d]\to[a,b]
an absolutely continuous bijection. Then

v_{\beta\circ\varphi}(s,t)=|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|v_{\beta}(s,\varphi(t))

for all
s\in I
and
t\in[a,b]
for which
\varphi^{\prime}(t)
exists.

In particular if
\alpha:[a,b]\to I
is an absolutely continuous curve we have that

v_{\beta\circ\varphi}(\alpha(t),t)=|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|v_{\beta}(\alpha(t),\varphi(t))\quad a.e.\ t.

Proof.

We may assume that
\varphi
is increasing. Let
s\in I
and observe that

\displaystyle v_{\beta\circ\varphi}(s,t)=\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{\ell_{s}(\beta|_{[\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+\delta(h)]})}{h},

where
\delta(h):=\varphi(t+h)-\varphi(t)
. If
\varphi^{\prime}(t)
exists we thus have that

\displaystyle v_{\beta\circ\varphi}(s,t)=\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{\delta(h)}{h}\frac{\ell_{s}(\beta|_{[\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+\delta(h)]})}{\delta(h)}=\varphi^{\prime}(t)\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\ell_{s}(\beta|_{[\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+\delta]})}{\delta}=\varphi^{\prime}(t)v_{\beta}(s,\varphi(t)).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose a sequence
\gamma_{j}=(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}):[a,b]\to I\times X
of AC-curves converges uniformly to an AC-curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{d}v_{\beta}(\alpha(t),t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{c}^{d}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha_{j}(t),t)\mathrm{d}t

whenever
a\leq c\leq d\leq b
.

Proof.

By considering the restriction to
[c,d]
we may assume that
c=a
and
d=b
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By uniform convergence
(\alpha_{j})
is equicontinuous and thus there exists
\delta>0
such that
\sup_{j}|\alpha_{j}(t)-\alpha_{j}(s)|\leq\varepsilon
whenever
|t-s|<\delta
. Let
a=a_{0}<\cdots<a_{M}=b
be a partition of
[a,b]
such that
a_{i}-a_{i-1}<\delta
,
i=1,\ldots,M
. For each
i
we have by Lemma
3.2
(2) that

\displaystyle\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}v_{\beta}(\alpha(t),t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq(1+\omega(\varepsilon))\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}v_{\beta}(\alpha(a_{i}),t)\mathrm{d}t\leq(1+\omega(\varepsilon))\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha(a_{i}),t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq(1+\omega(\varepsilon))^{2}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha_{j}(t),t)\mathrm{d}t

Summing over
i
and taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
we have the estimate

\int_{a}^{b}v_{\beta}(\alpha(t),t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha_{j}(t),t)\mathrm{d}t,

as claimed.
∎

3.2.
Generalized product metric

We define the generalized product metric
d_{\mathcal{F},h}
on
I\times X
as the length metric arising from the length functional

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{F},h}(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{h(\gamma_{t})\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}+v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t,\quad\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to I\times X,

see Section
2.1
. We note that
d_{\mathcal{F},h}
generates the product topology on
I\times X
, and
(I\times X,d_{\mathcal{F},h})
is therefore a locally compact length space. Note that, if
J\subset I
is a subinterval (we will mostly consider compact subintervals) and
\mathcal{F}_{J}=\{d_{s}\}_{s\in J}
, then
(J\times X,d_{\mathcal{F}_{J},h})
is homeomorphic with
(J\times X,d_{\mathcal{F},h})
but the metrics need not coincide.

Another convenient choice of background metric on
I\times X
in what follows will be the conformally weighted metric
(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}
, see Section
2.1
. We have that
(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}
generates the product topology, and a curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to I\times X
is in
AC(X,d_{\mathcal{F},h})
if and only if it is in
AC(X,(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}})
, and in this case

\displaystyle|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|_{(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}}=\frac{|\gamma_{t}^{\prime}|_{d_{\mathcal{F},h}}}{h(\gamma_{t})}=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\frac{v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},s)^{2}}{h(\gamma_{t})^{2}}}\quad a.e.\ t\in[a,b].

If not explicitly stated otherwise, we tacitly equip
I\times X
with the metric
(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}
.

4.

4.1.
Causal structure

Let
X
and
\mathcal{F}
be as in Section

– we assume in particular that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (

) – and let

be continuous. Throughout this subsection we denote

.

Definitions

An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to Y
is said to be

if
\alpha^{\prime}>0
a.e. and
past directed
if
\alpha^{\prime}<0
a.e.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to Y
be a future or past directed curve. We say that
\gamma
is

(a)

causal
if
v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}\leq h(\gamma_{t})^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}
a.e.

;

(b)

timelike
if
v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}<h(\gamma_{t})^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}
a.e.
t\in[a,b]
;

We also say that
\gamma
is a

if
v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}=h(\gamma_{t})\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}
a.e.
t\in[a,b]
. Note that by Lemma
3.4
the notions above are invariant under absolutely continuous orientation preserving reparametrizations. Given a causal curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to Y
recall its
Lorentzian length
given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{h(\gamma(t))^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(\alpha(t),t)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

Lorentzian length is independent of parametrization (cf. Lemma
3.4
) and additive under concatenation of (causal) curves.

Remark 4.2
.

If
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is a (future or past directed) causal curve, then

\displaystyle\ell_{(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}}(\gamma)\leq\sqrt{2}|\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{a}|.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\ell_{(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}}(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\frac{v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}}{h(\gamma_{t})^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t\leq\sqrt{2}\int_{a}^{b}|\alpha^{\prime}(t)|\mathrm{d}t=\sqrt{2}|\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{a}|.

We now define the causal structure and time separation function on
Y
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
p,q\in Y
. We say that
p
and
q
are

(a)

causally
related, denoted
p\leq q
, if there exists a future directed causal curve
\gamma:p\curvearrowright q
;

(b)

related, denoted

, if there exists a future directed timelike curve
\gamma:p\curvearrowright q
;

A standard argument yields that
\leq
is a preorder and
\ll
is a transitive relation contained in
\leq
, so that
(Y,\ll,\leq)
is a
causal set
, cf.
[
29
, Definition 2.1]
. The causal and timelike
future
and
past
of a point
p\in Y
are denoted

\displaystyle I^{+}(p)=\{q\in Y:\ p\ll q\},\quad J^{+}(p)=\{q\in Y:\ p\leq q\}

\displaystyle I^{-}(p)=\{q\in Y:q\ll p\},\quad J^{-}(p)=\{q\in Y:q\leq p\},

and the chronological and causal diamonds
I^{\pm}(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=I^{+}(p)\cap I^{-}(q)
,
J^{\pm}(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q)
. The time separation function is defined in a standard way.

Definition 4.4
.

The
time separation function

\tau:Y\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tau(p,q)=\sup_{\gamma:p\curvearrowright q}\tau(\gamma),

where the supremum is taken over all causal curves joining
p
and
q
(with the convention
\sup\varnothing=0
).

Remark 4.5
.

The time separation function satisfies the following two standard properties:

(1)

If
p\ll q
then
\tau(p,q)>0
;

(2)

If
p\leq z\leq q
then
\tau(p,z)+\tau(z,q)\leq\tau(p,q)
.

However we do not know at this stage whether
\tau
is lower semicontinuous. We will establish this in the next section assuming (
1.4
).

4.2.
Basic properties of causal curves

We first establish the stability of causal curves and the upper semicontinuity of Lorentzian length.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
\gamma_{j}=(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}):[a,b]\to Y
are causal curves converging uniformly to an AC-curve
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):[a,b]\to Y
. If
\alpha^{\prime}>0
(or
<0
) then
\gamma
is a causal curve.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
\alpha^{\prime}>0
a.e. We will prove the following equivalent form of the causality condition:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}v_{\beta}(\alpha(\tau),\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}h(\gamma(\tau))\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad a\leq a^{\prime}<b^{\prime}\leq b.

By Lemma
3.5
we have that

\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}v_{\beta}(\alpha(\tau),\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha_{j}(\tau),\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. The causality of
\gamma_{j}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}v_{\beta_{j}}(\alpha_{j}(\tau),\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}h(\gamma_{j}(\tau))\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}(h(\gamma(\tau))+\varepsilon)\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

for large enough
j
. Since
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(t)\mathrm{d}t\to\alpha^{\prime}(t)\mathrm{d}t
weakly as measures we obtain that

\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}(h(\gamma(\tau))+\varepsilon)\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\to\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}(h(\gamma(\tau))+\varepsilon)\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain (
4.2
).
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Lorentzian length is upper semicontinuous, i.e. if
\gamma_{j}
is a sequence of causal curves converging uniformly to a causal curve
\gamma
then

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\tau(\gamma_{j})\leq\tau(\gamma).

Proof.

By passing to a subsequence we may assume that
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\tau(\gamma_{j})
is a limit. By the invarience of reparametrization we may assume that
\gamma_{j}(t)=(c_{j}t,\beta_{j}(t))
,
t\in[0,1]
, and that
c_{j}\to c
,
\beta_{j}\to\beta
(uniformly) and

\tau(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{c^{2}h(ct,\beta(t))^{2}-v_{\beta}(ct,t)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

Since
v_{\beta_{j}}(c_{j}t,t)\leq c_{j}h(c_{j}t,\beta_{j}(t))
a.e. we may pass to a (further) subsequence such that
g_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=v_{\beta_{j}}(c_{j}\cdot,\cdot)
and
f_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sqrt{c_{j}^{2}h(c_{j}\cdot,\beta_{j}(\cdot))^{2}-g_{j}^{2}}
converge weakly in
L^{2}([0,1])
to
g
and
f
, respectively. The lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to weak convergence implies that, for every
0\leq a<b\leq 1
we have

\int_{a}^{b}g^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}g_{j}^{2}\mathrm{d}t,\quad\int_{a}^{b}f^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}f_{j}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

and thus

\int_{a}^{b}(g^{2}+f^{2})\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}(g_{j}^{2}+f_{j}^{2})\mathrm{d}t=c^{2}\int_{a}^{b}h(ct,\beta(t))^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

By Lemma
3.5
we have that

\int_{a}^{b}v_{\beta}(ct,t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}v_{\beta_{j}}(c_{j}t,t)\mathrm{d}t=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}g_{j}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{a}^{b}g\mathrm{d}t.

Since
a,b
are arbitrary it follows that

v_{\beta}(ct,t)^{2}+h(t)^{2}\leq g(t)^{2}+f(t)^{2}\leq c^{2}h(ct,\beta(t))^{2}\quad a.e.\ t\in[0,1].

From this we obtain that

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{c^{2}h(ct,\beta(t))^{2}-v_{\beta}(ct,t)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}h\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}h_{j}\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{j\to\infty}\tau(\gamma_{j}).

∎

We single out the following corollary of Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
.

Corollary 4.8
.

(a)

Assume
p,q\in I\times X
and
J(p,q)
is contained in a compact set. Then
J(p,q)
is compact and, for every
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in J(p,q)
, there exists a maximizing causal curve
\gamma:p^{\prime}\curvearrowright q^{\prime}
.

(b)

Let
p_{0}\in I\times X
and
h>0
. If
\overline{B}_{(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}}(p_{0},4h)
is compact, then
J(p,q)
is compact for every
p,q\in\overline{B}_{(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}}(p_{0},h)
.

Proof.

It follows from Remark
4.2
that

(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}(p,q^{\prime})+(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}(q^{\prime},q)\leq\sqrt{2}(b-a)

for any

, whenever
p=(a,x)
and
q=(b,y)
are causally related. Assume
J(p,q)
is contained in a compact set. To show that
J(p,q)
is compact it suffices to show it is closed. If
(q_{j}^{\prime})\subset J(p,q)
converges to
q^{\prime}
, let
\gamma_{j}^{p}:p\curvearrowright q_{j}^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{j}^{q}:q_{j}^{\prime}\curvearrowright q
be causal curves parametrized with affine first component. We have that the curves
\gamma_{j}^{p}
and
\gamma_{j}^{q}
lie inside a compact set and have uniformly bounded length. Thus the sequences converge up to a subsequence to causal curves
\gamma^{p}:p\curvearrowright q^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{q}:q^{\prime}\curvearrowright q
, respectively. This shows that
q^{\prime}\in J(p,q)
, and thus
J(p,q)
is closed. Moreover, if
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in J(p,q)
and
\gamma_{j}:p^{\prime}\curvearrowright q^{\prime}
is a sequence of causal curves with
\tau(\gamma_{j})\to\tau(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
for

(\gamma_{j})
converges to a causal curve

satisfying

. Thus
\gamma
is a maximizing curve. We have proved (a).

To prove (b) note that, if

, and thus

(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}(p_{0},q^{\prime})\leq(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}(p_{0},p)+(d_{\mathcal{F},h})_{h^{-1}}(p,q^{\prime})\leq(1+2\sqrt{2})h<4h.

This shows that

for all

is compact.
∎

Variational length of causal curves

Recall the Lorentzian length (
2.3
) associated to the time separation function
\tau
.

Our next proposition states that it agrees with (
4.1
).

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to Y
be a causal curve. Then
L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\tau(\gamma).

Proof.

The inequality
\tau(\gamma)\leq L_{\tau}(\gamma)
is a straightforward consequence of the definitions. We will prove the opposite inequality.

By reparametrization we may assume that
\gamma=(\mathrm{id},\beta):[a,b]\to Y
. For each
t\in[a,b]
and
h>0
such that
t+h\in[a,b]
, let
\gamma_{t,h}=(\mathrm{id},\beta_{t,h})
be a maximizing geodesic
\gamma_{t}\curvearrowright\gamma_{t+h}
. Using Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{t+h})^{2}\leq h\int_{t}^{t+h}[h(s,\beta_{t,h}(s))-v_{\beta_{t,h}}(s,s)^{2}]\mathrm{d}s,\quad\textrm{ or equivalently}

\displaystyle\frac{\tau(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{t+h})^{2}}{h^{2}}+\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v_{\beta_{t,h}}(s,s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}h(s,\beta_{t,h}(s))^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

For fixed
t_{0}\in[a,b]
, the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v_{\beta_{t,h}}(s,s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v_{\beta_{t,h}}(s,s)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(1+\omega(t-t_{0}))^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v_{\beta_{t,h}}(t_{0},s)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+\omega(t-t_{0}))^{2}}\left(\frac{d_{t_{0}}(\beta_{t},\beta_{t+h})}{h}\right)^{2}

implies (using a similar argument in case
h<0
) that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0}\frac{\tau(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{t+h})^{2}}{h^{2}}+\frac{v_{\beta}(t_{0},t)^{2}}{(1+\omega(t-t_{0}))^{2}}\leq h(t,\beta(t))^{2}\quad a.e.\ t.

Choosing a countable dense set of
t_{0}\in[a,b]
this easily implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0}\left(\frac{\tau(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{t+h})}{h}\right)^{2}\leq h(t,\beta(t))^{2}-v_{\beta}(t,t)^{2}\quad a.e.\ t

Since
L_{\tau}(\gamma|_{[a,t+h]})-L_{\tau}(\gamma|_{[a,t]})\leq\tau(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{t+h})
, it follows that the function
t\mapsto L_{\tau}(\gamma_{[a,t]})
is absolutely continuous and its derivative squared is bounded a.e. from above by
h(t,\beta(t))^{2}-v_{\beta}(t,t)^{2}
. This implies that
L_{\tau}(\gamma)\leq\tau(\gamma)
and completes the proof.
∎

The following result will be useful in proving that future and past lightcones are open under the assumption (

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose
\gamma_{0}:q_{0}\curvearrowright p_{0}
is a future directed Lipschitz time-like curve with

c>0
and open neighbourhoods

of
q_{0}
and
p_{0}

and

with

In the proof we need a technical lemma.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
J_{0}\subset I
and
K\subset X
be compact. Suppose
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):J\to J_{0}\times K\subset hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is a future directed curve such that

\displaystyle\tau_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)^{2}=h(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}\geq c_{0}^{2}>0

for a.e.
t\in J
, and

\displaystyle\max\{\sup_{J_{0}\times K}h,|\alpha(J)|^{-1},\|\alpha^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)},\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{0}\times J)}\}\leq L.

Then there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(L,J_{0},K)
with the following property. Whenever

is a compact subinterval with
|J^{\prime}\triangle\alpha(J)|<\delta_{0}

is an increasing affine bijection, the perturbed curve
\gamma_{A}:=(A\circ\alpha,\beta)

\tau_{\gamma_{A}}^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{0}/2
a.e.

.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(J)=[a,b]
and
J^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
. Then

A(s)=a+\frac{b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}}{b-a}(s-a),\quad s\in[a,b]

and

. Observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\alpha(J)}|A(s)-s|\leq 2\delta\quad\textrm{and}\quad|(A^{\prime})^{2}-1|\leq\frac{3\delta}{|\alpha(J)|}

as long as
\delta\leq|\alpha(J)|
. By (
1.2
) and Lemma
3.2
have that

\displaystyle h(A(\alpha_{t}),\beta_{t})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq h(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})^{2}-\omega_{h^{2}}(2\delta)

\displaystyle v_{\beta}(A(\alpha_{t}),t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq[v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)+\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{0}\times J)}\omega(2\delta)]^{2}\leq v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}+3\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{0}\times J)}^{2}\omega(2\delta),

where

is the modulus of continuity of

. Denoting

we have the estimate

\displaystyle\tau_{\gamma_{A}}^{\prime}(t)^{2}=

\displaystyle h(A(\alpha_{t}),\beta_{t})^{2}(A^{\prime})^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(A(\alpha_{t}),t)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(A^{\prime})^{2}[h(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})^{2}-\omega_{h^{2}}(2\delta)]\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}-3\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{0}\times J)}^{2}\omega(2\delta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})^{2}\alpha^{\prime}(t)^{2}-\frac{3\delta}{|\alpha(J)|}\|\alpha^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}^{2}(\sup_{J_{0}\times K}h)^{2}-4\|\alpha^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}^{2}\tilde{\omega}(2\delta)

\displaystyle-v_{\beta}(\alpha_{t},t)^{2}-3\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{0}\times J)}^{2}\tilde{\omega}(2\delta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{0}^{2}-3L^{5}\delta-7L^{2}\tilde{\omega}(2\delta)

The proof is completed by choosing

small enough such that

.
∎

4.3.
Lorentzian manifolds

Assume that
X=M
is a smooth manifold,
h:I\times M\to(0,\infty)
is continuous, and
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in I}
is a collection of continuous Riemannian metrics on
M
such that the local coordinate representations
(s,p)\mapsto(g_{s})_{ij}(p)
are continuous in
(s,p)
.

Remark 4.13
.

It is standard that the continuity assumption above is equivalent to the following:
(s,p)\mapsto g_{s}(\bm{X}_{p},\bm{Y}_{p})
is continuous for any smooth vector fields
\bm{X},\bm{Y}\in C^{\infty}(TM)
.

It follows that

(4.5)

\displaystyle g\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-h^{2}\mathrm{d}s^{2}+g_{s}

is a continuous Lorentzian metric on
I\times M
. Thus
(I\times M,g)
is a continuous space-time and we denote by
(I\times M,\leq_{g},\ll_{g},\tau_{g})
the induced Lorentzian length structure.

Now consider the family of length metrics
\mathcal{F}=\{d_{g_{s}}\}_{s\in I}
on
M
associated to
\{g_{s}\}_{s\in I}
.

Lemma 4.14
.

Under the hypotheses above,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
1.2
). Moreover, for any absolutely continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\to X
there exists a null set
N\subset[a,b]
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle v_{\beta}(s,t)^{2}=g_{s}(\beta_{t}^{\prime},\beta_{t}^{\prime}),\quad(s,t)\in I\times([a,b]\setminus N).

Proof.

Let
J\subset I
and
K\subset M
be compact, and let
\tilde{g}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}+g_{s}
be the Riemannian metric on
I\times M
. Set
\tilde{d}_{s}=d_{\tilde{g}}|_{\{s\}\times X}
. Choose
r>0
so that
\overline{B}_{d_{\tilde{g}}}(J\times K,r)
is compact. By the continuity of the metric, there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\sqrt{g_{s}(v,v)}\leq\sqrt{g_{s^{\prime}}(v,v)}+\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|)\sqrt{g_{s^{\prime}}(v,v)}
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in J_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=pr_{I}(\overline{B}_{d_{\tilde{g}}}(J\times K,r))
and
v\in T_{p}M
with
p\in K_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=pr_{X}(\overline{B}_{d_{\tilde{g}}}(J\times K,r))
. In particular if
x,y\in K
and
\inf_{s\in J}d_{\tilde{g}}((s,x),(s,y))<r
, then
d_{s}(x,y)\leq(1+\omega(|J_{r}|)))\tilde{d}_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)
. Thus the metrics
d_{s},\tilde{d}_{s^{\prime}}
are locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent for
s,s^{\prime}\in J
with uniform bi-Lipschitz constant
C=C(J,K)
. For
x,y\in K
with
\inf_{s\in J}d_{s}(x,y)<r/C
, we have that any
d_{s}
-geodesic
\beta:x\curvearrowright y
lies in
K
for any
s\in J
, and thus

\displaystyle d_{s^{\prime}}(x,y)\leq

\displaystyle\ell_{d_{s^{\prime}}}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{g_{s^{\prime}}(\beta_{t}^{\prime},\beta_{t}^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1+\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|))\sqrt{g_{s}(\beta_{t}^{\prime},\beta_{t}^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\omega(|s-s^{\prime}|))d_{s}(x,y).

This proves (
1.2
).

To prove the second claim no that, since
\beta
is absolutely continuous,
\beta_{t}^{\prime}
exists for a.e.
t
. Let

5.1.
Modifying causal curves via Caratheodory theory of ODE’s

Throughout this subsection we assume (

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):q\curvearrowright p

\tau(\gamma)>0

\displaystyle h(y_{t},\beta_{t})^{2}y^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(y_{t},t)^{2}=c\in(0,\tau(q,p)]

.

Remark 5.2
.

(a)

In particular
(y,\beta)
is a time-like curve with the same start and end-points as

, and

(b)

the Lipschitz constant of

is at most
\displaystyle\frac{\|v_{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\alpha(J)\times J)}+\tau(\gamma)}{\underset{\alpha(J)\times\beta(J)}{\inf}f}

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

\gamma_{1}=(b,y)

By (

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,t)-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s^{\prime},t)|\leq L(t)|s-s^{\prime}|,\quad s,s^{\prime}\in\mathrm{im}(\alpha),

is Caratheodory-Lipschitz (uniformly in

implies that the ODE

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y^{\prime}=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(y,t)\\

. For each

\displaystyle h(y_{\varepsilon}(t),\beta_{t})^{2}y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)^{2}-v_{\beta}(y_{\varepsilon}(t),t)^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}

and
\tau(\gamma_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon
. We show that

(5.1)

\displaystyle y_{0}(1)<b\quad\textrm{and}\quad y_{\tau(q,p)}(1)\geq b.

Since

\displaystyle y_{0}^{\prime}-\frac{v_{\beta}(y_{0}(t),t)}{h(y_{0}(t),\beta_{t})}=y_{0}^{\prime}-\Phi_{0}(y_{0},t)=0\leq\alpha^{\prime}_{t}-\Phi_{0}(\alpha,t),

the comparison principle for ODE’s (Theorem

, contradicting the fact that

. Thus we have that

. To finish the proof of (

We conclude the proof of the proposition using (

5.2.
hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
is a Lorentzian length space

Proof of Theorem

.

Suppose

. By the reverse triangle inequality (cf. Remark

. Proposition

yields the existence of a time-like curve

. This completes the proof.
∎

Next we focus on Corollary

. To establish it we need to show the lower semicontinuity of

. This, in turn will be an easy consequence of the openness of the relation

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
q_{0},p_{0}\in hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
,
q_{0}\ll p_{0}
. Then there exist neighbourhoods

and

of

and

whenever

and

.

In particular,

is open for any

Proof.

The claim follows from Proposition
4.10
once we establish the existence of a Lipschtiz curve
\gamma_{0}:q_{0}\curvearrowright p_{0}
with
\tau_{\gamma_{0}}^{\prime}\geq c
for some

. To this end let
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta):q_{0}\curvearrowright p_{0}
be a causal curve with
\tau(\gamma)>0
parametrized so that

and

are Lipschitz. Then

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

We have that
(hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X,\leq,\ll)
is a causal set which satisfies the push-up property (Theorem
1.3
). Moreover
\tau
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality (Remark

).

We use the argument in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.25]
to show that
\tau
is lower semicontinuous. Let
p,q\in hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
,
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and assume without loss of generality that
\tau(p,q)>0
. Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to hI\times_{\mathcal{F}}X
be a causal curve

with

\tau(\gamma)>\tau(q,p)-\varepsilon/3
. We may find

Recall the metric

5.4.

Throughout this subsection we assume that

Lemma 5.7
.

The family

defined above satisfies (

).

Proof.

Let

and

be compact. Let

and

, then

lies in

.

Now suppose

and
\displaystyle\inf_{s\in J}d_{s}(x,y)<Ar_{0}

. By the observation above any sequence
(\beta_{j})
of constant speed curves

and thus subconverges to a

\displaystyle d_{s}(x,y)\leq

\displaystyle\int\rho(s,\beta_{t})|\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t\leq d_{s_{0}}(x,y)+\int|\rho(s,\beta_{t})-\rho(s_{0},\beta_{t})||\beta_{t}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}t

Appendix A
Caratheodory theory of ODE’s

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open. We say that
\Phi:D\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
Carathéodory condition
if
\Phi(\cdot,t)
is continuous for fixed
t
and
f(s,\cdot)
is measurable for fixed
s
. We say that
\Phi
satisfies a (local)
Carathéodory-Lipschitz condition
if in addition, for every compact
I\times J\subset D
with
I,J
closed intervals, there exists
L\in L^{1}(J)
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle|\Phi(s,t)-\Phi(s^{\prime},t)|\leq L(t)|s-s^{\prime}|,\quad s,s^{\prime}\in I.

We have the following existence and uniqueness theorem.

Theorem A.1

(Theorem XVIII and XX, Ch. III §10 in
[
37
]
)
.

If
\Phi
is locally Carathéodory-Lipschitz and
(y_{0},a)\in D
, then the ODE

(A.2)

y^{\prime}=\Phi(y,t)

with initial value
y(a)=y_{0}
has a unique solution which can be extended to the left and right (of
a
) up to the boundary of
D
. In particular the unique solution varies continuously with
y_{0}
.

The following is a differential comparison theorem.

Theorem A.2

(Theorem XXI, Ch. III §10 in
[
37
]
)
.

Suppose
\Phi
is locally Carathéodory-Lipschitz continuous, and that
\varphi,\psi\in AC(J)
,
J=[\xi,\xi+a]
satisfy

(a)

\varphi(\xi)\leq\psi(\xi)
,

(b)

P\varphi\leq P\psi
a.e. in
J
, where
P\phi=\phi^{\prime}-\Phi(\phi,x)
.

Then either
\varphi<\psi
in
J
or there exists
c\in[\xi,\xi+a]
s.t.
\varphi=\psi
on
[\xi,c]
and
\varphi<\psi
in
(c,\xi+a]
. A corresponding statement holds for the interval
J^{\prime}=[\xi-a,\xi]
with the inequality reversed in (b).

Josh Kline, Feng Li, Nageswari Shanmugalingam

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the setting of a locally compact, non-complete metric measure space ( Z , d , ν ) (Z,d,\nu) equipped with a doubling measure ν \nu , under the condition that the boundary ∂ Z := Z ¯ ∖ Z \partial Z:=\overline{Z}\setminus Z (obtained by considering the completion of Z Z ) supports a Radon measure π \pi which is in a σ \sigma -codimensional relationship to ν \nu for some σ > 0 \sigma>0 . We explore existence, uniqueness, comparison property, and stability properties of solutions to inhomogeneous Dirichlet problems associated with certain nonlinear nonlocal operators on Z Z . We also establish interior regularity of solutions when the inhomogeneity data is in an L q L^{q} -class for sufficiently large q > 1 q>1 , and verify a Kellogg-type property when the inhomogeneity data vanishes and the Dirichlet data is continuous.

1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we are interested in a nonlocal Dirichlet problem, namely the one given in
Definition
2.18
in the nonsmooth setting of compact metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure.
The Dirichlet condition is imposed in the sense of traces (
2.17
).
The operator in question is the trace of a
p
-Laplace-type operator on a “higher dimensional” uniform domain that
sees the compact metric space as its boundary. This is part of an overarching program of
exploring properties of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator on the compact space, first initiated in the
setting of complete doubling metric measure spaces in
[
33
,
27
,
28
]
.
In the classical Euclidean setting, with
Z
a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p=2
,
the problem stated in Definition
2.18
corresponds to the problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{\theta}u=G&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=f&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Here,
\Delta_{\Omega}
is the uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator on
\Omega
given by

\Delta_{\Omega}u(x)=-\text{div}(A(x)\nabla u(x))
on the Euclidean domain
\Omega
, see for example
[
25
]
.
We keep in mind that there are other approaches to setting up fractional/nonlocal problems in the Euclidean
setting as well, as for example found in the works of Warma
[
72
]
, which were based
directly on the Besov energy, in contrast to the approach of
[
25
]
, which is based more on
the trace of a Laplace-Beltrami operator on a higher dimensional space. Unless the domain in question is the
entire Euclidean space, it is not known that the operator studied by Caffarelli and Stinga
[
25
]
is the
same one studied in
[
72
]
. When the Euclidean domain is the entire Euclidean space and
p=2
,
the approach of constructing the fractional operator via such an extension agrees with the one studied in
[
72
]

and with the fractional power of the classical Laplacian on the Euclidean space. For more studies involving various aspects of analogs of
this problem in the Euclidean setting, see
[
24
,
25
,
70
,
54
,
17
,
68
,
72
,
44
,
9
]
, for a sampling, and as well as
[
34
]
in the Carnot group setting. In the more general metric setting,
if the geometric properties of the metric space allows for a doubling measure that supports a
2
-Poincaré inequality,
then the three approaches of constructing fractional operators yields the same operator as well, see for instance
[
33
]
;
however, when the space is equipped with a doubling measure but does not support a
2
-Poincaré inequality,
there may not be a natural choice of a Laplacian as a local operator; in this case, the approaches of Warma
[
72
]

and
[
25
]
may differ; the present paper focuses on the approach of
[
25
]
due to its links to the
Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators. Moreover, we consider non-linear analogs of fractional powers
p
-Laplace operator
even in the setting where there is no natural (local)
p
-Laplace operator. Examples such as the vonKoch snowflake curve,
the Sierpinski carpet, and more general fractal objects do not have a natural
p
-Laplace operator, but when
equipped with their natural Hausdorff measures, are doubling spaces in which the results of the present paper apply
but no other approaches are currently available, see Section
2.7
.

Aspects of the above problem have also been studied in the setting of Dirichlet forms in
[
7
,
30
]
(for the case
p=2
).
This interpretation is known in literature on probability as the
problem corresponding to the killed process.
In considering the killed process, the problem can be re-stated as an energy minimization problem, but the associated
the energy takes into account the oscillation of the functions outside of the domain as well, subject to the constraint that
the functions agree with
f
outside the domain.
More particularly, given a function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the energy
\mathcal{E}_{G}(u)
is a globally defined
non-local
energy
whose value is changed even if
u
is only changed outside of the domain
\Omega
. One then attempts
to find a function
u
with minimal energy among all functions
v
with
v=f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.
Thus, if we perturb the value of
f
far away from
\Omega
, the
solution to the above minimization problem will change even though the boundary values of
f
may not change.
This problem is well-understood in the Euclidean setting, and the corresponding function-space where the
energy has as a component
the globally-defined Besov energy associated with the Besov
space
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
; these are functions
u\in L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for which

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{|y-x|^{n+\theta p}}\,dy\,dx<\infty.

We refer the interested reader to
[
30
,
54
,
17
,
68
,
9
,
44
]
and
the references therein for more on this topic.

In contrast, in this paper we are interested in trace processes that are related to the so-called reflected jump processes, or censored processes, see
[
16
]
. In such situations, competitor functions need only address the boundary data in the sense of traces, rather than agreeing with prescribed data in the entirety of the exterior of the domain. Thus, if we perturb the value of
f
far away from
\Omega
, the
solution to the above problem will not change because the boundary values of
f
does not change.
This is a more nuanced problem, for one also then needs to make sense of traces of Besov functions, given that
Besov functions can be arbitrarily perturbed on sets of measure zero.
Due to this difficulty, this problem does not have as extensive a literature presently, in comparison to the problem related
to the killed process. In the Euclidean setting, this problem has been studied in
[
72
]
, and
[
72
]

has an excellent exposition on the background related to the problem in the Euclidean setting, with
substantial literature in the bibliography.

Our precise setting is the following: we consider a a locally compact, non-complete bounded metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
, with
\nu
a doubling measure, whose boundary is
\partial Z=\overline{Z}\setminus Z
, where
\overline{Z}
is the completion of
Z
. We extend
\nu
to
\partial Z
by the zero extension, so that
\nu(\partial Z)=0
. For
\sigma>0
, we then equip
\partial Z
with the codimension
\sigma
Hausdorff measure
\pi:=\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\nu}
, see (
2.3
), and assume that
0<\pi(\partial Z)<\infty
. In order to rigorously frame our problem given by Definition
2.18
in this setting, we first establish the following trace theorem pertaining to Besov spaces on
Z
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\theta<1
and
1<p<\infty
, and let
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
.

(i)

If
p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
and if there is a sequence
u_{k}\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, compactly supported in
Z
, such that
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, then for
\pi
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
,

(1.2)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u|^{p}d\nu=0.

(ii)

If (
1.2
) holds for
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial\Omega
, then there is a sequence
u_{k}\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, compactly supported in
Z
, such that
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
.

Furthermore, if
p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
and
\pi
is
\sigma
-codimensional Ahlfors regular with respect to
\nu
(see Definition
2.4
), then there exist bounded linear trace and extension operators

T_{Z}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi),\qquad E_{Z}:B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)

such that
T_{Z}\circ E_{Z}
is the identity map on
B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
. For each
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, the trace operator also satisfies

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u-T_{Z}u(\zeta)|d\nu=0

for
\pi
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
.

Theorem
1.1
is proven via Proposition
3.1
, Proposition
3.5
, and Lemma
4.1
, using trace and extension results from
[
13
]
between Besov spaces on
Z
and Newton-Sobolev spaces on hyperbolic fillings of
Z
.

Having established the relevant trace theory, we then study well-posedness for the inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem given by Definition
2.18
. The nonlocal (and nonlinear) operator associated with the problem is given via an energy form
\mathcal{E}_{T}
as in Definition
2.15
. This operator was introduced in
[
27
]
, where the approach of
[
24
,
25
]
towards constructing nonlocal operators via Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps was adapted to our current metric setting. The following theorem is a compilation of existence, uniqueness, comparison property,
and stability results established in this paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a locally compact, non-complete metric space equipped with a doubling measure
\nu
, and
\sigma>0
such that

\partial Z:=\overline{Z}\setminus Z
is equipped with a Radon measure
\pi
that is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\nu
.
Let
\max\{1,\sigma\}<p<\infty
and
\sigma/p<\theta<1
. Then the following claims hold true:

(1)

For each
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and each
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
there exists exactly one

u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}G\,v\,d\nu\text{ whenever }v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\text{ with }T_{Z}v=0\ \ \ \text{ $\pi$-a.e.\penalty\ in }\partial Z,

\displaystyle T_{Z}u

\displaystyle=f\ \ \ \text{ $\pi$-a.e. in }\partial Z.

Such a solution
u
is called a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem.

(2)

If
f_{1},f_{2}\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
with
f_{1}\leq f_{2}

\pi
-a.e. in
\partial Z
and

G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, then with
u_{i}
the solution to the
(f_{i},G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem for
i=1,2
, we have that

u_{1}\leq u_{2}

\nu
-a.e. in
Z
.

(3)

If
(G_{k})_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
with limit
G
and
(f_{k})_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence in

B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
with limit
f
, and if
u_{k}
is the solution to the
(f_{k},G_{k})
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem,
then
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
with
u
the solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous problem.

Theorem
1.3
is proved in stages; the existence and uniqueness Claim (1) is established as
Theorem
5.2
,
comparison property Claim (2) as
Proposition
5.8
, and the stability Claim (3) as Theorem
6.12
.
The proof of existence of the solution is accomplished using the direct method of calculus of variation, but the
difficulty is in the details, as the problem is neither local nor is it homogeneous. To overcome this difficulty we
combined tools of potential theory related to Sobolev spaces with the direct method. The stability of the problem
rests on two data here-the inhomogeneity data
G
and the boundary data
f
. Given the nonlinearity and inhomogeneity, together
with the non-locality, of the problem, the standard way of proving stability for the
p
-Laplace equation does not work here,
and so we had to employ a two-step process in combination with the results from
[
13
,
27
]
in order to
establish the stability result. We point out here that if
\partial Z
is empty, that is, there is no boundary data to consider,
the global results of
[
27
]
already yields the stability of solutions with respect to the inhomogeneity data
G
, but the
introduction of the non-empty boundary means that we cannot appeal to
[
27
]
.

In considering matters of continuity of solutions to the problem, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Under the setting of Theorem
1.3
, let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and

G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
. Then with
Q_{\nu}
the lower mass bound exponent associated with the measure
\nu

in the sense of (
2.2
),

(1)

if
G
also belongs to
L^{q}(Z,\nu)
for some
q>\max\{1,Q_{\nu}/(p\theta)\}
, then the solution
u
to
the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem is locally Hölder continuous in
Z
.

(2)

if
\partial Z
is uniformly perfect and

f
is continuous on
\partial Z
, then with
u
the solution to the
(f,0)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem,
we have that for every
\xi\in\partial Z
,

\lim_{Z\ni z\to\xi}u(z)=f(\xi).

Note that if
Q
is the lower mass bound exponent associated with the auxiliary measure
\mu_{\beta}
on the uniformized
hyperbolic filling
X_{\varepsilon}
of
\overline{Z}
(as described in subsection
2.4
), then
Q_{\nu}=Q-\beta/\varepsilon
.

The interior regularity Claim (1) of Theorem
1.4
is established in this paper via Theorem
7.4
, and
the Kellogg property Claim (2) of Theorem
1.4
is proved via Theorem
7.13
.
The interior regularity result follows immediately from
[
28
]
, and Theorem
7.4

merely points this out. The Kellogg property would also follow immediately from
[
12
]
if we know that

\partial Z
is uniformly
p
-fat (with respect to the potential theory related to the
p
-Laplacian on the filling
X_{\varepsilon}
).
The uniform
p
-fatness is established in the proof of Theorem
7.13
, and then the
results of
[
12
]
are invoked to complete the proof of the Kellogg property. We note here that we do not presently have
knowledge of Kellogg property if the inhomogeneity data

does not vanish in a neighborhood of
\partial Z
,
and the classical proof for inhomogeneous but local

-Laplace equation does not apply here.

In summary, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
are established in Sections 5–7. Before doing so, we provide
a description of the various notions associated with the nonsmooth metric setting in Section 2, and Sections 3 and 4 describe
traces of Sobolev and Besov functions at boundaries of domains.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we gather together the notions and tools used throughout the paper. The triple
(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
will stand in for a generic metric
measure space. For
w\in W
and
r>0
we denote the ball
\{z\in W\,:\,d_{W}(z,w)<r\}
by
B_{W}(w,r)
. If the metric space
W
is understood in context,
we can also drop the subscript and denote the ball by
B(w,r)
.

Throughout this paper, we let
C
denote a constant which depends only on the structural constants of the metric space (such as the doubling constant), unless otherwise specified. Its precise value is not of interest to us and may change with each use, even within the same line. Furthermore, given quantities
A
and
B
, we will often use the notation
A\simeq B
to mean that there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that
C^{-1}A\leq B\leq CA
. Likewise, we use
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
if the left and right inequalities hold, respectively.

2.1.
Metric and measure-theoretic notions

Definition 2.1
.

Given a metric space
(W,d_{W})
and a set
A\subset W
, we say that
A
is
uniformly perfect
if there is a constant
K\geq 2
such that
whenever
w\in A
and
0<r<\diam(A)
, the annulus
B(w,r)\setminus B(w,r/K)
has a nonempty intersection with
A
. If
A=W
, we say that
the metric space
W
is uniformly perfect.

Given a set
A\subset W
, we
say that the measure
\mu_{W}
is
doubling on
A
, if there exists
C_{d}\geq 1
such that for all
w\in A
and for all
r>0
, we have

0<\mu_{W}(B(w,2r))\leq C_{d}\mu_{W}(B_{W}(w,r))<\infty.

When
A=W
, we merely say that
\mu_{W}
is doubling.
If
\mu_{W}
is doubling, then by iterating the above inequality, there exists
Q\geq 1
and
C\geq 1
, depending on
C_{d}
, such that

(2.2)

\frac{\mu_{W}(B(w,r))}{\mu_{W}(B(z,R))}\geq C^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}

for all
z\in W
,
0<r\leq R
, and
w\in B(z,R)
.

When
W
is not complete, by
\partial W
we mean the set
\overline{W}\setminus W
, with
\overline{W}
the metric completion of
W
.
We can extend

\mu_{W}
to
\partial W
as a null measure.
We equip
\partial W
with the
codimension
\sigma
Hausdorff measure
with respect to
\mu_{W}
, denoted by

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
, with
\sigma>0
, as follows. For sets
A\subset\partial W
, we set

(2.3)

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(A)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\mu_{W},\varepsilon}(A),

where

\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\mu_{W},\varepsilon}(A):=\inf_{\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{W}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\sigma}}

with the infimum over all covers
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
of
A
with balls
B_{i}\subset\overline{W}
such that

\rad(B_{i})<\varepsilon
for each
i\in I
. We extend
\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
to
W
as a null measure. Thus we consider
\mu_{W}

and
\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
to
be Radon measures on
\overline{W}
, the first charging only subsets of
W
and the second charging
only subsets of
\partial W
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
s>0
.
We say that a measure
\mu_{0}
on
\overline{W}
such that
\mu_{0}(W)=0
,
is
s
-codimensional Ahlfors regular with respect to the measure
\mu_{W}
if there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(2.5)

C^{-1}\,\frac{\mu_{W}(B(\xi,r))}{r^{s}}\leq\mu_{0}(B(\xi,r))\leq C\,\frac{\mu_{W}(B(\xi,r))}{r^{s}}

whenever
\xi\in\partial W
and
0<r<2\,\diam(W)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
\mu_{W}
is doubling on
W
(and hence on
\overline{W}
).
A measure
\mu_{0}
on the space
\overline{W}
, with
\mu_{0}(W)=0
, is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\mu_{W}
if and only if

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\mu_{W}
and
\mu_{0}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
.

Proof.

If both
\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
and
\mu_{0}
are
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\mu_{W}
, then clearly
\mu_{0}\approx\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
;
so it
suffices to show that if there is a measure
\mu_{0}
on
\overline{W}
that is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\mu_{W}
,
then so is
\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}
. To this end, we assume that
\mu_{0}
is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\mu_{W}
.
As
\mu_{W}
is doubling, it follows that
\mu_{0}
is doubling on
\partial W
.

Let
\xi\in\partial W
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
be a cover of
B(\xi,r)
in
\overline{W}
with
\rad(B_{i})<\varepsilon
for
each
i\in I
. Let
I_{1}
be the collection of all
i\in I
for which
B_{i}\cap\partial W
is non-empty, and for
each
i\in I_{1}
we set
\widehat{B}_{i}
to be a ball centered at some point in
\partial W
and with radius at most
twice the radius of
B_{i}
, such that
B_{i}\cap\partial W\subset\widehat{B}_{i}\subset 3B_{i}
; then

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{W}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\sigma}}\geq C_{d}^{-2}\,\sum_{i\in I_{1}}\frac{\mu_{W}(\widehat{B}_{i})}{\rad(\widehat{B}_{i})^{\sigma}}\geq(C_{d}^{2}C)^{-1}\,\sum_{i\in I}\mu_{0}(\widehat{B}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq(C_{d}^{2}C)^{-1}\,\mu_{0}(B(\xi,r)\cap\partial W)

\displaystyle=(C_{d}^{2}C)^{-1}\mu_{0}(B(\xi,r)).

Taking the infimum over all such covers and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we see that

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(B(\xi,r))\geq C^{-1}\,\mu_{0}(B(\xi,r)).

On the other hand, for
\eta>0
there exists
\varepsilon_{\eta}>0
so that for each
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\eta}

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(B(\xi,r))=\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(B(\xi,r)\cap\partial W)\leq\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\mu_{W},\varepsilon}(B(\xi,r)\cap\partial W)+\eta.

We ensure that
\varepsilon<r
as well, and find a cover of
B(\xi,r)
by balls
B_{i}
,
i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}
,
each centered at points in
B(\xi,r)\cap\partial W
with
radius
\varepsilon
, so that the balls
\tfrac{1}{5}B_{i}
are pairwise disjoint. This is always possible because of the doubling
property of
\overline{W}
, see for example
[
48
]
.
Note that
B_{i}\subset B(\xi,2r)
.
It follows that

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(B(\xi,r))-\eta\leq\,\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{W}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\sigma}}\leq C_{d}^{3}\,\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{W}(\tfrac{1}{5}B_{i})}{5^{\sigma}\rad(\tfrac{1}{5}B_{i})^{\sigma}}\leq C\,\frac{C_{d}^{3}}{5^{\sigma}}\,\sum_{i\in I}\mu_{0}(\tfrac{1}{5}B_{i})\leq C\,\frac{C_{d}^{3}}{5^{\sigma}}\,\mu_{0}(B(\xi,2r)).

Letting
\eta\to 0^{+}
and then applying the doubling property of
\mu_{0}
yields

\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{W}}^{-\sigma}(B(\xi,r))\leq C\,\frac{C_{d}^{3}}{5^{\sigma}}\mu_{0}(B(\xi,2r))\leq C\,\frac{C_{d}^{4}}{5^{\sigma}}\mu_{0}(B(\xi,r)).\qed

2.2.
Potential theory

Let
(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
be a metric measure space, and let
1\leq p<\infty.
Given a family
\Gamma
of non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves in

W
, we define the
p
-modulus
of
\Gamma
by

\Mod_{p}(\Gamma)=\inf_{\rho}\int_{W}\rho^{p}d\mu_{W},

where the infimum is taken over all Borel functions
\rho:W\to[0,\infty]
such that
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Given a function
u:W\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that a Borel function
g:W\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
u
if the following holds for all non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to W
:

|u(\gamma(b))-u(\gamma(a))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

whenever
u(\gamma(a))
and
u(\gamma(b))
are both finite, and
\int_{\gamma}g\,ds=\infty
otherwise. We say that
g
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
if the
p
-modulus of the family of curves where the above inequality fails is zero. The notion of
upper gradients first appeared in
[
47
]
, and the notion of weak upper gradients first appeared in
[
45
]
; interested readers can find more
on these topics from
[
48
]
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})
to be the class of all functions in
L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})
which have an upper gradient belonging to
L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})
. Defining

\|u\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})}+\inf_{g}\|g\|_{L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})},

where in the infimum is taken over all upper gradients
g
of
u
, we then define an equivalence relation in
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})

by
u\sim v
if and only if
\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})}=0
. The
Newton-Sobolev space

N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})

is then defined to be
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})/\sim
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})}:=\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})}
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset W
, one can similarly define
N^{1,p}(\Omega,\mu_{W})
.

When
u\in N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})
, there is a distinguished
p
-weak upper gradient, denoted
g_{u}
, of
u
, such that

g_{u}\in L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})
and
g_{u}\leq g
for every other
p
-weak upper gradient
g\in L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})
of
u
. This
g_{u}
is a local object in the
sense that if
u\in N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})
and
\Omega
is a nonempty open subset of
W
, then the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u|_{\Omega}

is
g_{u}|_{\Omega}
.
For more on Newton-Sobolev spaces and
upper gradients, we refer the interested reader to
[
10
,
48
]
.

For
0<\theta<1
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and a function
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(W,\mu_{W})
, we define

\|u\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(W,\mu_{W})}^{p}:=\int_{W}\int_{W}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{\theta p}\mu_{W}(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu_{W}(y)d\mu_{W}(x).

We then define the
Besov space

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(W,\mu_{W})
as the set of all functions in
L^{p}(W,\mu_{W})
for which the above seminorm is finite.
The theory of Besov spaces has a large volume of literature, see for instance
[
35
,
31
,
51
]

and the references therein for the Euclidean setting, and
[
42
,
21
,
71
,
52
,
41
]

for the setting of metric measure spaces equipped with a doubling measure.
Since the extant literature on this topic is vast, we cannot hope to list them all here, and the references
we give here are merely a sampling.

We can now define the following capacities with respect to the Newton-Sobolev and Besov spaces. For
E\subset W
, by
\BCap_{p}^{W}(E)
we mean the number

\BCap_{p}^{W}(E):=\inf_{u}\left(\int_{W}|u|^{p}\,d\mu_{W}+\inf_{g}\int_{W}g^{p}\,d\mu_{W}\right),

where the first infimum is over all functions
u\in N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})

that satisfy
u\geq 1
on
E
, and the second infimum is over all upper gradients
g
of
u
. By
\BCap^{W}_{\theta,p}(E)
we mean the number

\BCap_{\theta,p}^{W}(E):=\inf_{u}\left(\int_{W}|u|^{p}\,d\mu_{W}+\|u\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(W,\mu_{W})}^{p}\right),

where the infimum is over all functions
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(W,\mu_{W})
that satisfy
u\geq 1
on a
neighborhood of
E
in
W
. We will also use the following variational (Newton-Sobolev) capacity: given an open set
\Omega\subset W
and a set
E\subset\Omega
, by
\operatorname{cap}^{W}_{p}(E,\Omega)
we mean the number

\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{W}(E,\Omega):=\inf_{u}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu_{W},

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})
such that
u\geq 1
on
E
and
u=0
in
W\setminus\Omega
. In this paper, we will often consider Newton-Sobolev and Besov spaces as defined with respect to different metric
measure spaces. For this reason, we have kept the dependence on
W
in the above notation.

We say that a property holds
\BCap_{p}^{W}
-q.e. if the set of all points for which the property fails is a
\BCap_{p}^{W}
-capacitary null set.
Similarly, we say that a property holds
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{W}
-q.e. if the set of all points for which the property fails is a
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{W}
-capacitary null set.

Let
1\leq q,p<\infty
. Following
[
45
]
, we say that

(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that the following holds for balls
B\subset W
and all function-upper gradient pairs
(u,g)
:

\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{q}d\mu_{W}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\rad(B)\left(\fint_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu_{W}\right)^{1/p}.

Here and throughout this paper, we use the notation

u_{B}=\fint_{B}u\,d\mu_{W}=\frac{1}{\mu_{W}(B)}\int_{B}u\,d\mu_{W}.

When
(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
is a geodesic space which supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality, then we can take
\lambda=1
,
as shown in
[
45
, Corollary 9.8]
.
For more on Poincaré inequalities we refer the interested reader to
[
48
]
.

It was also shown in
[
45
, Theorem 5.1]
that for doubling metric measure spaces,
the validity of
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality implies the validity of
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality. Putting this together
with
[
10
, Theorem 5.53]
, we obtain
the following Maz’ya-type inequality, see for example
[
10
, Theorem 5.53]
:

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that
(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
is a doubling geodesic space and that it
supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality for some
1\leq p<\infty
. For
u\in N^{1,p}_{\text{loc}}(W,\mu_{W})
, let
S:=\{x\in W:u(x)=0\}
.
Then for all balls
B\subset W
,

\fint_{B}|u|^{p}d\mu_{W}\leq\frac{C(\rad(B)^{p}+1)}{\BCap^{W}_{p}(B\cap S)}\int_{2B}g_{u}^{p}d\mu_{W}.

Here
g_{u}
is the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
.

2.3.
Uniform domains

The notion of uniform domains first arose in the study of quasiconformal mapping theory, as see for example
[
37
,
62
,
36
,
8
]
,
but since then has also played crucial roles in Euclidean potential theory
see for example
[
50
,
1
,
2
,
57
]
and
potential theory in metric measure spaces
[
60
,
15
,
13
]
.
It is also worth noting that in a geodesic metric space, every domain can be approximated
by uniform domains, as proved in
[
69
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

A noncomplete, locally compact metric space
(W,d_{W})
is said to be a uniform domain if there is a constant
A>1
such that, with

\partial W=\overline{W}\setminus W
, for each pair of points
x,y\in W
we can find a curve
\gamma
in
W
with end points
x,y

such that the length
\ell(\gamma)\leq A\,d_{W}(x,y)
and in addition, for each point
z
in the trajectory of
\gamma
, we have

d(z,\partial W):=\dist(z,\partial W)\geq A^{-1}\,\min\{\ell(\gamma_{x,z}),\ell(\gamma_{z,w})\},

where
\gamma_{x,z}
and
\gamma_{z,w}
denote each subcurve of
\gamma
with end points
x,z
and
z,w
respectively.
Curves
\gamma
satisfying the above condition are called uniform curves.

The potential-theoretic utility of uniform domains comes from the ability to connect pairs of points in the domain by chains of Whitney-type
balls.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
39
, Section 3.1]

Let
\Omega\subset W
be a uniform domain. Then for each
w,z\in\partial\Omega
, there exists a uniform curve
\gamma
joining
w
and
z
and a chain of balls
\{B_{k}:=B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
covering
\gamma
such that

(1)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}x_{k}=w
and
\lim_{k\to-\infty}x_{k}=z
.

(2)

If
k\geq 0
and
x\in B_{k}
, then
r_{k}\simeq d(x,\partial\Omega)\simeq d(x,w)\lesssim 2^{-|k|/(4C)}d(w,z)
.

(3)

If
k<0
and
x\in B_{k}
, then
r_{k}\simeq d(x,\partial\Omega)\simeq d(x,z)\lesssim 2^{-|k|/(4C)}d(w,z)
.

(4)

The collection
\{4B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
has bounded overlap.

2.4.
Compact doubling metric measure spaces as boundaries of uniform domains of globally controlled geometry

From
[
13
]
we know that every compact doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
(up to a biLipschitz change in the metric) is the boundary
of a bounded uniform domain. We denote this uniform domain
X_{\varepsilon}
, as it is obtained as the uniformization of a hyperbolic filling graph of

Z
with parameter
\varepsilon>0
. In
[
13
]
, a one parameter family of measures
\mu_{\beta}
,
\beta>0
, was also constructed on
X_{\varepsilon}
,
based on the measure
\nu
on
Z
, such that the measure
\nu
is
\beta/\varepsilon
–codimensional with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
.

It was also shown in
[
13
]
that the measure
\mu_{\beta}
is doubling on the metric space
(X_{\varepsilon},\,d_{\varepsilon})
and that
the metric measure space
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, which, thanks to the Hölder inequality,
is the strongest of all the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequalities.

The notion of hyperbolic filling was proposed by Gromov
[
43
]
, and developed further in
[
26
,
21
,
18
,
23
,
19
,
20
]
.
In this subsection we describe a construction of the so-called hyperbolic filling of a compact doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
as
given in
[
13
]
.

Given a compact doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
,
by rescaling the metric if necessary (that is, replacing
d
with
(2\,\diam(Z))^{-1}d
), in constructing the hyperbolic filling we may assume
that
\diam(Z)<1
.
We fix a point
z_{0}\in Z
and set
A_{0}=\{z_{0}\}
. We also fix
\alpha>2

and
\tau>2
, and for each positive integer
n
we choose a maximal
\alpha^{-n}
-separated subset
A_{n}
of
Z
such that
A_{n-1}\subset A_{n}
.
We then set

V=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}\times\{n\},

and the set
V
forms the vertex set for the graph
G
we construct next. For
(\xi,n),(\zeta,m)\in V
, we say that
(\xi,n)
is a
neighbor of
(\zeta,m)
,
if
|n-m|\leq 1
and in addition, either
n=m
and
B_{Z}(\xi,\alpha^{-n})
intersects
B_{Z}(\zeta,\alpha^{-n})
, or else
n=m\pm 1
and

B_{Z}(\xi,\tau\,\alpha^{-n})
intersects
B_{Z}(\zeta,\tau\,\alpha^{-m})
.
We turn
G
into a metric graph
X
by gluing unit-length intervals
[v,w]
between
each pair of neighboring vertices
v,w
. Let
d_{X}
denote the resulting graph metric on
X
.

The construction of
X
, as given here, is from
[
13
]
, and is a simple modification of that given
in
[
21
,
23
,
19
,
20
]
, and it is known that
X
is a Gromov hyperbolic space with
visual boundary that is quasisymmetric to the metric space
Z
. Motivated
by
[
8
]
, we use the tool of uniformization of the metric on
X
as follows.
Let
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
. With
v_{0}=(z_{0},0)
playing the role of a root vertex, when
x,y\in X
we set

d_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\varepsilon\,d_{X}(\gamma(t),v_{0})}\,ds(t),

where the infimum is over all paths
\gamma
in
X
with end points
x,y
. We also lift up the measure
\nu
on
Z
to
X_{\varepsilon}
as follows.
For each edge
[v,w]
with
v=(\xi_{v},n_{v})
and
(\xi_{w},n_{w})=w\in V
, we set

a_{(v,w)}=\frac{\nu(B_{Z}(\xi_{v},\alpha^{-n_{v}}))+\nu(B_{Z}(\xi_{w},\alpha^{-n_{w}}))}{2},

and then for
A\subset X_{\varepsilon}
we set

\mu_{\beta}(A):=\int_{A}\left(\sum_{(v,w)}a_{(v,w)}\chi_{[v,w]}(x)\,e^{-\beta d_{X}(x,v_{0})}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x).

The results from
[
13
]
related to the construction
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
are summarized below.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact, doubling metric measure space, let
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\alpha,\,\tau>1
be the parameters from the above construction. Let
\varepsilon=\log\alpha.
Then for each
\beta>0
, the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
constructed above satisfies the following:

(1)

(Z,d)
is biLipschitz equivalent to
(Z,d_{\varepsilon})
, with the biLipschitz constant depending on
\varepsilon
and
\diam(Z)
.

(2)

Both
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
are doubling and support a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality;
moreover, we have
N^{1,p}(X_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})=N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
.

(3)

For all
z\in Z
and
0<r\leq 2\diam(Z)
,

\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(z,r)\cap Z)\simeq\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(z,r))}{r^{\beta/\varepsilon}},

that is,
\nu
is
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensional with respect to the measure
\mu_{\beta}
.

(4)

When
\beta/\varepsilon<p
, there exist bounded linear trace and extension operators

T_{X}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\quad\text{and}\quad E_{X}:B^{1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})

such that
T_{X}\circ E_{X}=\Id
. The boundedness of
T_{X}
and
E_{X}
are with respect to the full norms of the respective spaces
(which includes the
L^{p}
-norms), and in addition, we also get boundedness in energy as well.
Furthermore, if
u\in B^{1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, then
\nu
-a.e.
z\in\overline{Z}
is
a Lebesgue point of
E_{X}u
.

In all of the above, the constants depend only on
\alpha
,
\tau
,
\beta
,
p
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
.

Indeed, an explicit construction is given as follows. For
w\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
\xi\in Z
, we have

(2.11)

T_{X}w(\xi)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\xi,r)}w\,d\mu_{\beta},

while, for
u\in B^{1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
and vertices
v\in X
, we have

(2.12)

E_{X}u(v)=\fint_{D(v)}u\,d\nu,

and then extend
E_{X}u
linearly to edges in
X
. Here
D(v)=B_{Z}(\xi_{v},\alpha^{-n_{v}})
when
v=(\xi_{v},n_{v})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.10
, it was also shown in
[
13
, Proposition 13.2]
that
when
\beta/\varepsilon<p
and
\theta=1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)
, for
E\subset\overline{Z}

we have that

(2.13)

\BCap^{\overline{Z}}_{\theta,p}(E)\simeq\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}(E).

Now we discuss some notational caveats. The primary focus of this paper is a metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
with

(Z,d)
locally compact and bounded but not complete, and
\nu
a doubling measure on
Z
. Thus the hyperbolic filling
uniform domain is associated not with
Z
but its metric completion
\overline{Z}
. Thus
\partial\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}=\overline{Z}
.
Since
Z
is locally compact, it follows that
Z
is relatively open in
\overline{Z}
. Moreover, as
X_{\varepsilon}
is a uniform domain
in the sense of Definition
2.8
, so is
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
.

For
x\in\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
and
r>0
, by
B(x,r)
we mean the ball centered at
x
, with radius
r
in
the metric
d_{\varepsilon}
. At various stages we might want to focus on balls in
X_{\varepsilon}
,
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
,
Z
, or
\partial Z
, but
these are intersections of balls in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
with the relevant subsets, and the need to distinguish these balls arise only
when integrating over such balls; as the integrals are with respect to measures supported on these various subsets, the intersection
of the ball in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
with these subsets will be replaced here by the measures in the relevant integrals.

2.5.
Cheeger differential structure and the definition of differential
\nabla f

From
[
29
]
we know that whenever
(W,d_{W},\mu_{W})
is a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré
inequality, there is a positive integer
N
and a countable family of measurable sets
W_{\alpha}\subset W
with
\mu_{W}(W_{\alpha})>0
,
and for each
\alpha
a Lipschitz map
\Phi_{\alpha}:W\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and a measurable inner product

W_{\alpha}\ni w\mapsto\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}

satisfying the following condition. Whenever
f:W\to\mathbb{R}
is
a Lipschitz function, there is a measurable function
D_{\alpha}f:W_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for
\mu_{W}
-a.e.
w\in W_{\alpha}
we have

\limsup_{y\to w}\frac{f(y)-f(w)-\langle D_{\alpha}f(w),\Phi_{\alpha}(y)-\Phi_{\alpha}(w)\rangle_{w}}{d(y,w)}=0.

Moreover
D_{\alpha}
is a linear operator on the class of all Lipschitz functions on
W
, and

\langle D_{\alpha}f(w),D_{\alpha}f(w)\rangle_{w}\approx g_{f}(w)^{2}

for
\mu_{W}
-a.e.
w\in W_{\alpha}
; such a differential structure extends also to functions in
N^{1,p}(W,\mu_{W})
.

In the setting where
W=X_{\varepsilon}
,
d_{W}=d_{\varepsilon}
, and
\mu_{W}=\mu_{\beta}
, there is a natural differential structure
on
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, for
X_{\varepsilon}
is a graph with edges that are intervals
of varying length
(from the
uniformization procedure conducted to obtain
d_{\varepsilon}
). We know that
each edge in
X_{\varepsilon}
forms a singleton family of paths in
X_{\varepsilon}
with positive
p
-modulus; hence
when
f\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, we know that
f
is absolutely continuous on each such edge and hence is
differentiable there. In this case, we can set
Df(x)=f^{\prime}(x)
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every
x
in that
edge by first choosing an orientation for that edge. The choice of orientation does not matter as long as
we consistently use the same orientation for all
f\in N^{1,p}(X_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
; as
\mu_{\beta}(\partial X_{\varepsilon})=0
,
we know that such
\nabla f
is well-defined
\mu_{\beta}
-a.e. in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
. So we set

\nabla f(x):=f^{\prime}(x)
whenever
x
belongs to an edge and
f^{\prime}(x)
exists;
\mu_{\beta}
-almost every

x\in X_{\varepsilon}
is such a point. Thus
\nabla f
is well-defined with
\nabla f:X_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
, with

|\nabla f|^{2}=g_{f}^{2}=\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle
. This differential structure is a special choice of a Cheeger
differential structure, where each
W_{\alpha}
is an edge of
X_{\varepsilon}
, and
\Phi_{\alpha}
measures
the location of
x\in W_{\alpha}
from one end of the edge (which also then determines the orientation of that edge)
with respect to the metric
d_{\varepsilon}
. The fact that this structure satisfies the limit that defines the Cheeger differential
structure condition is merely Taylor’s theorem for the interval.

In the setting of infinitesimally Hilbertian spaces (spaces where minimal weak upper gradients can be endowed with an inner product
structure that agrees with the pointwise values of the upper gradient) as in
[
40
]
(see also
[
4
]
for a nice
exposition on the topic of test plans and application of Wasserstein spaces), we can replace the Cheeger structure
Df
with the
upper gradient structure. This is because under the assumption of infinitesimal Hilbertianity there is an Euler-Lagrange equation associated
with the upper gradient-based energy minimization problem. In this paper we focus on the natural differential structure
\nabla f
,
keeping in mind such a flexibility beyond the hyperbolic filling setting.

Definition 2.14
.

Recalling the construction of the uniformization of the hyperbolic filling
X_{\varepsilon}
from
Subsection
2.4
, we say that a function
\overline{u}\in N^{1,p}(X_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})=N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is the

p
-harmonic extension of a function
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
if
T_{X}(\overline{u})=u
and whenever

v\in N^{1,p}(X_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
with
T_{X}v=0
, we have

\int_{X_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\nabla v\rangle\,d\mu_{\beta}=0.

Given that functions in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
have an extension to
X_{\varepsilon}
that lies in
N^{1,p}(X_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
,
such
p
-harmonic extensions always exist, and given the support of
p
-Poincaré inequality on
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, we
also have the uniqueness of such an extension, see
[
10
, Theorem 7.2]
or
[
29
, Theorem 7.8, Theorem 7.14]
.

2.6.
Standing assumptions and statement of the Dirichlet problem for fractional operators on
Z

In this final subsection of Section 2, we state the standing assumptions we will operate under for the rest of the paper, and
state the Dirichlet problem that is the principal focus of the paper.

Standing assumptions:
In this paper,
(Z,d,\nu)
is a locally compact, non-complete bounded metric
measure space with
\nu
a doubling measure on
Z
, and we equip
\partial Z:=\overline{Z}\setminus Z
with the codimension

\sigma
Hausdorff measure with respect to
\nu
, namely
\pi:=\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\nu}
, and we extend
\nu
to
\partial Z

so that
\nu(\partial Z)=0
, and extend
\pi
to
Z
by setting
\pi(Z)=0
.
We will also assume that
0<\pi(\partial Z)<\infty
.

We fix
\varepsilon>\log 2
. Then, for each
\beta>0
consider

(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
to be the uniform domain with
\mu_{\beta}
doubling on
X_{\varepsilon}
and supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality,
and (by perturbing the metric on
Z
in a biLipschitz fashion if necessary) so that
\overline{Z}=\partial X_{\varepsilon}=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus X_{\varepsilon}
.

Since
Z
is locally compact, we know that
Z
is an open subset of
the compact metric space
\overline{Z}
. Observe that
\overline{Z}
is compact because the measure
\nu
on
Z

is doubling, and so the zero-extension of
\nu
to
\partial Z
is also doubling; bounded complete doubling metric measure spaces
are necessarily compact, see for example
[
48
, Lemma 4.1.14]
.

We now outline the construction of the nonlocal operator which forms the basis of our problem, as introduced in
[
27
]
.

Definition 2.15
.

We fix
1<p<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
, and choose
\beta=\varepsilon\,(1-\theta)\,p
.
This choice leads to the Besov space
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
as the trace space of the Newton-Sobolev space

N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. We define the operator
\mathcal{E}_{T}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\times B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to\mathbb{R}
, induced from this relationship, by

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v):=\int_{X_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\,\nabla\overline{v}\rangle\,d\mu_{\beta},

where
\overline{u}
and

\overline{v}
are the
p
-harmonic extensions of
u
and
v
respectively to
X_{\varepsilon}
given by Definition
2.14
.

The operator
\mathcal{E}_{T}
has the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v+w)=\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)+\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,w)
,

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{T}(0,v)=\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,0)=0
,

(3)

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\alpha u,\beta v)=|\alpha|^{p-2}\alpha\beta\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)
whenever
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4)

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,u)\geq 0
, and
\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,u)=0
if and only if
u
is constant,

(5)

We also have the comparison

\|u\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)}^{p}:=\int_{Z}\int_{Z}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{\theta p}\,\mu(B(y,d(x,y)))}\,d\nu(y)\,d\nu(x)\approx\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,u).

Remark 2.16
.

In the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{T}
, we do not have to consider the
p
-harmonic extension of the second function,
v
; indeed,
any choice
\widehat{v}
of extension of
v
to
X_{\varepsilon}
would do as long as
T_{X}\widehat{v}=v
. This is because, as
\overline{u}
itself
is
p
-harmonic in the domain
X_{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\int_{X_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\,\nabla(\widehat{v}-\overline{v})\rangle\,d\mu_{\beta}=0.

In what follows,
T_{X}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
and

T_{Z}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
are the relevant trace operators,
with
T_{X}
as described in Theorem
2.10
above, and
T_{Z}
as described in
Lemma
4.1
below. In particular, for
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, and for
\zeta\in\partial Z
,
the number
T_{Z}u(\zeta)
, if it exists, satisfies

(2.17)

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u-T_{Z}u(\zeta)|\,d\nu=0.

We will show below that if the measure
\pi=\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\nu}
on
\partial Z

is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\nu
, then the trace operator
T_{Z}

also exists, see the discussion in Section
4
. However, to discuss Besov functions on
Z
with null
trace on
\partial Z
, we do not need
\pi
to be
\sigma
-codimensional, see Section
3
.

The goal of this paper is to demonstrate the existence of solutions to the Dirichlet boundary value problem
on
(Z,d,\nu)
with boundary data defined on
\partial Z
, and prove stability properties of this problem.
We now pose the problem studied here.

Definition 2.18
.

Given
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
with
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)

and a function
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
,
we say that
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem
if

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}G\,v\,d\nu\text{ whenever }v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\text{ with }T_{Z}v=0\ \ \text{$\pi$-a.e. in }\partial Z,

\displaystyle T_{Z}u=

\displaystyle f\ \ \text{$\pi$-a.e. in }\partial Z.

If
G\equiv 0
, then we call the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem also as the
homogeneous Dirichlet problem with
boundary data
f
.

Remark 2.19
.

A function
u
solves the above Dirichlet problem if and only if it is a minimizer of the energy

(2.20)

I_{G}(v):=\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p}\,d\mu-p\int_{Z}vG\,d\nu

over all functions
v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
with trace
T_{Z}v=f
on
\partial Z
,
because the weak equation
\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)=\int_{Z}v\,G\,d\nu
is the Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to the
minimization of the energy
I_{G}
. Recall that
\overline{v}
denotes the
p
-harmonic extension of
v
to
X_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.21
.

Existence of the operator
T_{Z}
is known only under certain circumstances, namely that the measure
\pi
on

\partial Z
is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to the measure
\nu
on
Z
, see (
2.5
) (with
s=\sigma
).
However,even in the classical Euclidean setting,
if
\partial Z
does not support such a codimensional measure with respect to
\nu
, then neither the existence of
such a trace operator, nor its boundedness, is known. In this case, we can consider a weaker boundary value problem, given
as follows. With the “boundary data”
f
now belonging to
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, we want to find

u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}G\,v\,d\nu\text{ whenever }v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\text{ with }T_{Z}v=0\,\text{$\pi$-a.e. in }\partial Z,

\displaystyle u-f\in

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{p,p,0}(Z,\nu),

where
B^{\theta}_{p,p,0}(Z,\nu)
is the closure of the collection of functions from
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
which have compact support in
Z
,
see Proposition
3.1
below.

2.7.
Examples

Our results can be applied for any domain in a doubling metric measure space for which the restriction of the ambient measure is also doubling, and whose boundary is equipped with a measure which is codimensional with respect to the ambient measure. While our results can therefore be applied in the setting of Riemannian manifolds, Carnot groups, and other metric measure spaces which support a Poincaré inequality, such as the Laakso space and Bourdon-Pajot space, our results do not require the assumption of a Poincaré inequality. As such, they are also applicable in numerous fractal-type spaces where Poincaré inequalities do not hold. We now provide a few examples of such settings.

Example 2.22

(Rickman’s rug)
.

Let
L
be the standard vonKoch snowflake curve, which is of dimension
d_{L}:=\log(4)/\log(3)
. The metric space

Z=L\times(0,1)
is equipped with the product measure
\mu
obtained from the Hausdorff measure on
L
with the Lebesgue measure
on
(0,1)
. Note that
\partial Z=L\times\{0\}\cup L\times\{1\}
is equipped with the Hausdorff measure
\pi
of
L
.
In this case
\mu
is Ahlfors
d_{L}+1
-regular, and
\pi
is Ahlfors
d_{L}
-regular, which is
\sigma
-codimensional to
\mu

where
\sigma=1
. Our theorems apply here with
1<p<\infty
and
1/p<\theta<1
.

On the other hand, if
L_{1}
is the vonKoch snowflake curve without its two end points, and
Z=L_{1}\times[0,1]
, then

\partial Z
consists of two disjoint copies of the interval
[0,1]
, and hence in this case

\sigma=d_{L}
. In this case, our theorems apply with
d_{L}<p<\infty
and
d_{L}/p<\theta<1
.

Example 2.23

(Fractal examples)
.

Fractals that arise from a self-similar construction form a rich subclass of examples for us. As described by
Hino and Kumagai in
[
49
]
, if we start with a finite collection of Euclidean similarities with the
same scaling constant that leads
to a fractal set as the compact invariant set, then a small subcollection of these similarities yields a compact
subset that is the boundary of the domain that is the original invariant set sans the subset. Both invariant sets
are Ahlfors regular, but with different dimensions. If there are
n
number of similarities in the original collection
and
m
number of similarities in the subcollection, and if the similarities have scaling
\lambda
with
0<\lambda<1
,
then the co-dimensionality
\sigma=\log(n/m)/\log(1/\lambda)
.

As a concrete example we may consider the Sierpinski carpet, which has
[0,1]\times\{0\}
as one side-edge. With
Z
the
carpet with this edge removed, we have that the boundary
\partial Z
is this edge. As the dimension of the carpet
is
\log(8)/\log(3)
and the edge has dimension
1
, in this case we have that
\sigma=\log(8/3)/\log(3)
, and
so we are permitted to consider
1<p<\infty
and
\sigma/p<\theta<1
.

Another concrete example is the pentagasket (called the pentakun in
[
49
]
), with a Koch-type fractal curve
forming part of the boundary. As explained in
[
49
, Subection 5.2, page 604]
, the dimension of the
pentagasket is
\log(5)/\log(\alpha)
with
\alpha=\tfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}
, and the dimension of the Koch-type curve
is
\log(4)/\log(\alpha)
, leading us to the co-dimension
\sigma=\log(5/4)/\log(\alpha)<1
, and so again
we can consider
1<p<\infty
and
\sigma/p<\theta<1
.

The paper
[
49
]
has a lovely illustration of these two concrete examples. That paper also considers
a trace result of a Besov class from the larger fractal to the smaller fractal boundary, but the Besov class on the
larger fractal is different than the one considered in our paper and is related more to the Korevaar-Schoen spaces
rather than Besov spaces, as discussed for example in
[
5
,
3
,
66
]
.

3.
Besov functions with zero trace

To define what a solution to the problem of interest is, we need a solid foundation of the theory of functions in

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)=B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
that have zero trace at
\partial Z
,
see Definition
2.18
. The focus of this section is to
lay such a foundation. We do so with minimal assumptions on the boundary
\partial Z
of
Z
as such a foundation may be useful in
other contexts as well.

Recall the standing assumptions from subsection
2.6
.
In the next section, we will assume that
\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\nu}
is codimension
\sigma
Ahlfors regular with respect to
\nu
;
but we do not need this strong assumption in this section. We merely continue to assume that
0<\pi(\partial Z)<\infty
and that
\nu(\partial Z)=0
.
Since
\nu(\partial Z)=0
, we also have that
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)=B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
. Note that as
(Z,d,\nu)
is locally compact,
Z
is open in
\overline{Z}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
0<\theta<1
, and let
p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{k}\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be compactly supported in
Z
, and suppose that there exists
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
. Then for

\pi
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
, we have

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u|^{p}d\nu=0.

In particular, the number
T_{Z}u(\zeta)=0
for
\pi
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha>2
,
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
, and
\beta=\varepsilon\,(1-\theta)\,p
in the construction of the uniform domain
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
for which

\overline{Z}=\partial X_{\varepsilon}
.
By Theorem
2.10
, we have that
E_{X}u_{k},\,E_{X}u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
.
Moreover,
E_{X}u_{k}\to E_{X}u
in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, which follows from the boundedness and
linearity of the extension operator
E_{X}
.
Since the
u_{k}
are compactly supported in
Z
, it follows
from the construction of the extension operator
E_{X}
, see (
2.12
),
that the functions
E_{X}u_{k}
are compactly supported in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
, and so
E_{X}u_{k}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z,\mu_{\beta})
.
Note also that
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
is a uniform domain as well.
As
N^{1,p}_{0}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z,\mu_{\beta})
is a closed subspace of
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, see
[
10
, Theorem 2.36]
for example, it follows that
E_{X}u\in N^{1,p}_{0}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z,\mu_{\beta})
. From
[
55
, Theorem 1.1]
, it then follows
that for
\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
–q.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
, we have

(3.2)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,r)}|E_{X}u|d\mu_{\beta}=0.

Under our hypotheses on
p
and the construction of the measure
\pi
supported on
\partial Z
, we will see towards the end of this
proof that
\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
-null subsets of

\partial Z
are necessarily
\pi
-null.
By the construction of the trace operator
T_{X}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
given in
Theorem
2.10
, see (
2.11
), the condition
(
3.2
) implies that
T_{X}\circ E_{X}u(\zeta)=0
.

Fix
r>0
, and let
\zeta\in\partial Z
such that (
3.2
) holds. By Theorem
2.10
, we have that
\nu
-a.e.
w\in B_{Z}(\zeta,r)
is a Lebesgue point of
E_{X}u
with
T_{X}\circ E_{X}u(w)=u(w)
. As
X_{\varepsilon}
is a uniform domain in its completion, we can join such
w
to
\zeta
by a uniform curve, and obtain the corresponding chain of balls
\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by Lemma
2.9
. We then have that

\displaystyle|u(w)|=|T_{X}\circ E_{X}u(w)-T_{X}\circ E_{X}u(\zeta)|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(E_{X}u)_{B_{k}}-(E_{X}u)_{B_{k+1}}|\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{k}\left(\fint_{4B_{k}}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{1/p},

where
r_{k}:=\rad(B_{k})
, and we have applied the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality to obtain the last inequality.

Since
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, we can choose
\delta>0
such that
\beta/\varepsilon+\delta<p
. By the Hölder inequality, we then have that

\displaystyle|u(w)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{k}^{1-\frac{\beta/\varepsilon+\delta}{p}}\left(r_{k}^{\beta/\varepsilon+\delta}\fint_{4B_{k}}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{k}^{\frac{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}{p-1}}\right)^{1-1/p}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{k}^{\beta/\varepsilon+\delta}\fint_{4B_{k}}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{1/p}.

By Lemma
2.9
, we have that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{k}^{\frac{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}{p-1}}\simeq d(w,\zeta)^{\frac{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}{p-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k|\frac{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}{p-1}}\simeq d(w,\zeta)^{\frac{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}{p-1}},

and so it follows that

\displaystyle|u(w)|^{p}\lesssim d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r_{k}^{\beta/\varepsilon+\delta}}{\mu(B_{k})}\int_{4B_{k}}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}.

For
k\geq 0
, we have from the Lemma
2.9
that
r_{k}\simeq d(x,w)
for each
x\in 4B_{k}
. Furthermore, by Theorem
2.10
, we have that

\mu_{\beta}(B_{k})\simeq\mu_{\beta}(B(w,d(x,w)))\simeq d(x,w)^{\beta/\varepsilon}\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))

for each
x\in 4B_{k}
. Likewise, for
k<0
, the same comparisons hold with
w
replaced by
\zeta
. Therefore, letting
C_{w,\zeta}^{1}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}4B_{k}
and
C_{w,\zeta}^{2}:=\bigcup_{k<0}4B_{k}
, we have that

\displaystyle|u(w)|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}\left(\int_{C_{w,\zeta}^{1}}\frac{g_{E_{X}u}(x)^{p}\,d(x,w)^{\delta}}{\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))}d\mu_{\beta}(x)+\int_{C_{w,\zeta}^{2}}\frac{g_{E_{X}u}(x)^{p}\,d(x,\zeta)^{\delta}}{\nu(B(\zeta,d(x,\zeta)))}d\mu_{\beta}(x)\right).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(\zeta,r)}|u|^{p}d\nu

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(\zeta,r)}\int_{C_{w,\zeta}^{1}}\frac{g_{E_{X}u}(x)^{p}\,d(x,w)^{\delta}d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}}{\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))}d\mu_{\beta}(x)d\nu(w)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{B(\zeta,r)}\int_{C_{w,\zeta}^{2}}\frac{g_{E_{X}u}(x)^{p}\,d(x,\zeta)^{\delta}d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}}{\nu(B(\zeta,d(x,\zeta)))}d\mu_{\beta}(x)d\nu(w)=:I+II.

We first estimate
I
. Note that there exists a constant
C\geq 1
independent of
w
such that
C^{1}_{\zeta,w}\subset B(\zeta,Cr)
. By Tonelli’s theorem, we then have that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}(x)^{p}\int_{B(\zeta,r)}\frac{d(x,w)^{\delta}d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}}{\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))}\chi_{C_{w,\zeta}^{1}}(x)d\nu(w)d\mu_{\beta}(x).

Note that if
x\in C_{w,\zeta}^{1}
, then
d(x,w)\simeq d(x,\overline{Z})
, and so there exists
C\geq 1
so that
w\in B(x,Cd(x,\overline{Z}))
. Using this, and the doubling property of
\nu
, it follows that for
x\in B(\zeta,Cr)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B(\zeta,r)}\frac{d(x,w)^{\delta}d(w,\zeta)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}}{\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))}

\displaystyle\chi_{C_{w,\zeta}^{1}}(x)d\nu(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{B(x,Cd(x,\overline{Z}))}\frac{1}{\nu(B(w,d(x,w)))}d\nu(w)\lesssim r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}.

Hence, we have that

(3.4)

I\lesssim r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}.

We estimate
II
in a similar manner: using Tonelli’s theorem and the fact that
x\in C_{w,\zeta}^{2}
implies that
d(x,\zeta)\simeq d(x,\overline{Z})
, we obtain

II\lesssim r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}\int_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}.

Combining this estimate with (
3.4
) and (
3
), and using Theorem
2.10
, we obtain

\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u|^{p}d\nu\lesssim\frac{r^{p}}{r^{\beta/\varepsilon}\nu(B(\zeta,r))}\int_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\lesssim r^{p}\fint_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}

for
\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}
-q.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
and every
r>0
. Since
p>\sigma/\theta
by assumption, and
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
, it follows that
p>\sigma+\beta/\varepsilon
. Therefore, by
[
38
, Proposition 3.11]
, the above inequality holds for
\mathcal{H}^{-(\sigma+\beta/\varepsilon)}_{\mu_{\beta}}
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
. Furthermore, by
[
38
, Lemma 3.10]
, we have that

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{p}\fint_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{\sigma+\beta/\varepsilon}\fint_{B(\zeta,Cr)}g_{E_{X}u}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}=0

for
\mathcal{H}^{-(\sigma+\beta/\varepsilon)}_{\mu_{\beta}}
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
. Since
\pi\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-(\sigma+\beta/\varepsilon)}_{\mu_{\beta}}|_{\overline{Z}}
by Theorem
2.10
, it follows that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|u|^{p}d\nu=0.

for
\pi
-a.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be such that
T_{Z}u=0

\BCap^{\overline{Z}}_{\theta,p}
-q.e. on
\partial Z
.
Then there exist
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
with
\supt(u_{k})\Subset Z
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\zeta\in\partial Z
is such that
T_{Z}u(\zeta)=0
. Consider
E_{X}u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
as given by Theorem
2.10
, and fix
0<r<2\diam(Z)
. Let
N_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest positive integer such that
\alpha^{-N_{r}}<r
, and for

, let

I_{i,r}:=\{j\in\mathbb{N}:z_{i,j}\in A_{i}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,r)\},

where
A_{i}
is the maximal
\alpha^{-i}
-separated set chosen in the construction of the hyperbolic filling, see Section

.
Recall that we adopt the convention that
B(x,r)
,
x\in\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
and
r>0
, is the ball with respect to the metric
d_{\varepsilon}
.
However, keeping in mind that the original metric

on
Z
differs (biLipschitzly) from

and that the hyperbolic filling
used the metric
d
, we will use the notation
B_{Z}(\xi,r)
for

and

to denote balls in

with respect to the metric

,
as this is used to construct the extension operator
E_{X}
.
By the construction of the extension operator
E_{X}
, we then have that

\displaystyle\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|E_{X}u|d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\sum_{i=N_{r}-1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in I_{i,r}}\int_{B(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})}|E_{X}u|d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\sum_{i=N_{r}-1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in I_{i,r}}\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i}))|u_{B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\sum_{i=N_{r}-1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in I_{i,r}}\alpha^{-i\beta/\varepsilon}\nu(B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i}))\fint_{B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})}|u|d\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\sum_{i=N_{r}-1}^{\infty}\alpha^{-i\beta/\varepsilon}\sum_{j\in I_{i,r}}\int_{B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})}|u|d\nu

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\int_{B_{Z}(\zeta,2r)}|u|d\nu\sum_{i=N_{r}-1}^{\infty}\alpha^{-i\beta/\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r^{\beta/\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,r))}\int_{B_{Z}(\zeta,2r)}|u|d\nu\simeq\fint_{B_{Z}(\zeta,2r)}|u|d\nu.

Here we have used the bounded overlap of the collection
\{B_{Z}(z_{i,j},\alpha^{-i})\}_{j\in I_{i,r}}
as well as the codimensional relationship between
\mu_{\beta}
and
\nu
as given by Theorem
2.10
. Since
T_{Z}u(\zeta)=0
, it follows that

(3.6)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\zeta,r)}|E_{X}u|d\mu_{\beta}=0.

Since
T_{Z}u(\zeta)=0
for
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}
-q.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
, it follows from
[
13
, Proposition 13.2]
that (
3.6
) holds for

\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}
-q.e.
\zeta\in\partial Z
. Therefore, by
[
55
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have that

E_{X}u\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z,\mu_{\beta})
, and so by
[
10
, Theorem 5.46]
, there exists

\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
with
\supt(f_{k})\Subset\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z

such that
f_{k}\to E_{X}u
in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. Setting
u_{k}:=T_{X}f_{k}\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
,
where
T_{X}
is the trace operator given by Theorem
2.10
, we have from the construction
of
T_{X}
that
\supt(u_{k})\Subset Z
. Furthermore, by boundedness and linearity of
T_{X}
, it follows that
u_{k}\to u
in

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
.
∎

4.
Traces of Besov spaces on
Z
, at
\partial Z
.

In this section we complete the set of tools we need in order to study the problem given in Definition
2.18
, by
constructing the trace operator
T_{Z}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
. To do so,
in addition to the assumptions on
Z
and
\partial Z
adopted in the previous section, we will also assume that
the measure
\pi=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{-\sigma}
on
\partial Z
is
\sigma
-codimensional Ahlfors regular with respect to the measure
\nu
,
as in Definition
2.4
, and that
p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There is a bounded linear trace operator
T_{Z}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
; furthermore,
we also have a bounded linear extension operator
E_{Z}:B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)

such that
T_{Z}\circ E_{Z}
is the identity map on
B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha>2
,
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
, and
\beta=\varepsilon p(1-\theta)
in the construction of the uniform domain
(X_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
for which
\overline{Z}=\partial X_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
is a uniform domain and
\pi
is
(\sigma+\tfrac{\beta}{\varepsilon})
-codimensional Ahlfors regular
with respect to the measure
\mu_{\beta}
, it follows from
[
60
,
39
]
that there is an extension operator

E_{X,\partial}:B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)\to N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and a trace operator

T_{X,\partial}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, both bounded and linear,
with
T_{X,\partial}\circ E_{X,\partial}
the identity map on
B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
.
As by construction
\nu
is
\beta/\varepsilon
-codimensional Ahlfors regular with respect to
\mu_{\beta}
, and
X_{\varepsilon}
is a
uniform domain, we also have similar trace and extension operators

T_{X}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
and

E_{X}:B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)\to N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, see Theorem
2.10
.
Setting
E_{Z}=T_{X}\circ E_{X,\partial}
yields the desired conclusion once we have constructed the trace operator
T_{Z}
.
We will consider as a candidate for
T_{Z}
the operator
T=T_{X,\partial}\circ E_{X}
.
In order for
T
to be a suitable trace operator, we need to verify that

(a)

T\circ E_{Z}
is the identity map on
B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
,

(b)

for each
f\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, for
\pi
-a.e.
\xi\in\partial Z
we have that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\xi,r)}|f-Tf(\xi)|\,d\nu=0.

From
[
13
]
we know that the class of Lipschitz functions on
Z
and
\partial Z
are dense in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
and

B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
respectively. Lipschitz functions on
Z
and
\partial Z
are extended as Lipschitz
functions in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
by the respective extension operators
E_{X}
and
E_{X,\partial}
, and the trace operators
act on Lipschitz functions on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
as restrictions, that is, if
f
is a Lipschitz function on
X_{\varepsilon}
, then
it has a Lipschitz extension (also denoted
f
) to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, and then
it can be seen from the construction of the trace operators that
T_{X}f=f|_{Z}
and
T_{X,\partial}f=f|_{\partial X}
.
Thus for Lipschitz functions
f\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
we have that
Tf=f|_{\partial Z}
, and so

T\circ E_{Z}(f)=f
for Lipschitz
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
; by the bounded linearity property of
T\circ E_{Z}
it now
follows that
T\circ E_{Z}
is the identity map on
B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
as required in (a).

Fix
f\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, and let
\xi\in\partial Z
be a Lebesgue point of
E_{X}f
with respect to the measure
\mu_{\beta}
.
As noted at the end of the proof of Proposition
3.1
,
\pi
-almost every point is such a point. This means that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(\xi,r)}|E_{X}f-T_{X}\circ E_{X}f(\xi)|\,d\mu_{\beta}=0.

Moreover, we also require
\xi
to satisfy the condition that

(4.2)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{p}\,\fint_{B(\xi,Cr)}g_{E_{X}f}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}=0;

and we also know from
[
38
, Lemma 3.10]
that
\pi
-almost every
\xi\in\partial Z
is such a point.
For the rest of this proof we will also denote
E_{X}f
by
f
(note that
T_{X}(E_{X}f)=f
). Fix
r>0
; then for Lebesgue points
\zeta\in B(\xi,r)\cap Z
,
as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
we see that with a fixed choice of
\delta
with
0<\delta<p-\beta/\varepsilon
,

|f(\zeta)-f(\xi)|^{p}\lesssim d(\zeta,\xi)^{p-\beta/\varepsilon-\delta}\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r_{k}^{\beta/\varepsilon+\delta}}{\mu(B_{k})}\,\int_{4B_{k}}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta},

with
r_{k}\approx d(x_{k},\xi)
when
k\geq 0
and
x_{k}
is the center of the ball
B_{k}
, and
r_{k}\approx d(x_{k},\zeta)
when
k<0
.
Proceeding further as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

\fint_{B(\xi,r)}|f(\zeta)-f(\xi)|^{p}\,d\nu(\zeta)\lesssim r^{p}\,\fint_{B(\xi,Cr)}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}.

Now the condition (b) follows from (
4.2
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

With the above constructions in place, we have
T_{X,\partial}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)

as a bounded linear trace operator. On the other hand, we also have

T_{Z}\circ T_{X}:N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\to B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
as a bounded linear operator. For Lipschitz
functions
w\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, we know that

T_{Z}\circ T_{X}(w)=T_{Z}(w|_{Z})
, with
w|_{Z}
also Lipschitz continuous on
Z
; hence it has a unique Lipschitz extension to
\partial Z
,
given by
w|_{\partial Z}
. Thus
T_{Z}\circ T_{X}(w)=w|_{\partial Z}=T_{X,\partial}(w)
. Thus the two bounded linear operators agree on
the subclass of all Lipschitz functions in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. As
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is doubling and
supports a
1
-Poincaré inequality, it follows that Lipschitz functions form a dense subclass of
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})

(see for instance
[
48
, Theorem 8.2.1]
);
hence on
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
we have that
T_{Z}\circ T_{X}=T_{X,\partial}
.

5.
Existence of solutions to the inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem

The goal of this section is to establish the existence of solutions to the problem given in Definition
2.18
, and to show
that such solutions are unique. We also prove a comparison principle for solutions, provided that the inhomogeneity data
G
remains the same.

We continue to adopt the standing assumptions from the previous section here; in particular,
\pi=\mathcal{H}^{-\sigma}_{\nu}
is
\sigma
-codimensional with respect to
\nu
, and

p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
.
We let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
and
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and set the class

\mathcal{A}(f):=\{u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu):T_{Z}u=f\ \ \ \pi\text{-a.e.\ on }\partial Z\}.

For each
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, we consider the functional
I_{G}
as defined in Remark
2.19
, and then consider the minimization problem

(5.1)

\min\left\{I_{G}(u):u\in\mathcal{A}(f)\right\},

which corresponds to the inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem given in Definition
2.18
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
and
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
. Then there exists
u\in\mathcal{A}(f)
such that

I_{G}(u)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}(f)}I_{G}(v).

Moreover, if
w\in\mathcal{A}(f)
is another such minimizer, then
w=u
on
Z
.

Remark 5.3
.

Recall from our standing assumptions for this section that
p>\sigma/\theta
. Under this requirement on
p
,
we know from
[
38
, Proposition 3.11]
and (
2.13
)
that any measurable set
A\subset\partial Z
with
\pi(A)>0
must have

\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(A)>0
. If
p
does not satisfy this condition, then

\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(\partial Z)=0
, and in this case, the above uniqueness will have
to be replaced with the statement that
w=u+c
for some constant
c
, and in addition, we would
have to require that
\int_{Z}G\,d\nu=0
.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Since
E_{Z}f\in\mathcal{A}(f)
, we have that

I:=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}(f)}I_{G}(v)<\infty,

and so we choose
u_{k}\in\mathcal{A}(f)
such that

I_{G}(E_{Z}f)\geq I_{G}(u_{k})\to I,

as
k\to\infty
. By a slight abuse of notation, we will also denote by
f
the extension
E_{X}\circ E_{Z}f\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
in what follows. By the Hölder inequality and Theorem
2.10
, we have that

\displaystyle p\int_{Z}u_{k}G\,d\nu\leq p\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(Z,\nu)}\

\displaystyle\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\lesssim\left(\|\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla{\overline{u}}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\right)\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\overline{u}_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\right)\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}.

Since
u_{k}\in\mathcal{A}(f)
and since
\BCap^{\overline{Z}}_{\theta,p}\simeq\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
on
\overline{Z}
by (
2.13
), it follows that
\overline{u}_{k}-f=0

\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
-q.e. on
\partial Z
. Since
\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(\partial Z)>0
, we then have from the Maz’ya-type inequality Theorem
2.7
that

(5.4)

\|\overline{u}_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\lesssim\|\nabla(u_{k}-f)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\leq\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}.

Note that the comparison constant above depends on
p
,
\theta
, the doubling constant of
\nu
, as well as
\diam(Z)
,
\nu(Z)
, and
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(\partial Z)
, but is independent of
k
.

Substituting this into the previous expression, we obtain

\displaystyle p\int_{Z}u_{k}G\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\right)\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}

\displaystyle=:C_{G}\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C_{f,G}.

Hence, we have that

\displaystyle I_{G}(E_{Z}f)

\displaystyle\geq I_{G}(u_{k})=\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}-p\int_{Z}u_{k}G\,d\nu

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}-\left(C_{G}\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C_{f,G}\right).

Therefore, it follows that

\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}(\|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p-1}-C_{G})\leq I_{G}(E_{Z}f)+C_{f,G},

where the right hand side is finite and independent of
k
. Thus, we see that the sequence

\{|\nabla\overline{u}_{k}|\}_{k}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, which then implies that the sequence
\{\overline{u}_{k}\}_{k}
is also bounded in

L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
by (
5.4
). As such,
\{\overline{u}_{k}\}_{k}
is bounded in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
.

Since
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is reflexive (see for instance
[
48
, Theorem 13.5.7]
),
there exists
w\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and a subsequence
\{\overline{u}_{k}\}_{k}
, not relabeled, such that
\overline{u}_{k}\to w
weakly in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. Therefore, by Mazur’s lemma, there exists a convex combination sequence

w_{k}:=\sum_{i=k}^{N(k)}\lambda_{k,i}\overline{u}_{i}

such that
w_{k}\to w
in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, it follows from
[
10
, Proposition 2.3, Corollary 6.3]
that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}.

Furthermore, boundedness and linearity of the trace operator
T_{X}
and the Hölder inequality implies that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Z}T_{X}w_{k}G\,d\nu-\int_{Z}T_{X}wG\,d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{X}(w_{k}-w)\|_{L^{p}(Z,\nu)}\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{k}-w\|_{N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. By convexity of the functional
v\mapsto\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla v|^{p}d\mu
and linearity of
T_{X}
, we also have that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}T_{X}w_{k}\,G\,d\nu\leq\sum_{i=k}^{N(k)}\lambda_{k,i}\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu-p\int_{Z}u_{i}G\,d\nu\right)=\sum_{i=k}^{N(k)}\lambda_{k,i}I_{G}(u_{i})\to I

as
k\to\infty
.

We claim that
T_{Z}\circ T_{X}w=f
. By the triangle inequality and linearity of the trace operators, we have that

\displaystyle\|T_{Z}\circ T_{X}w-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}\leq\|T_{Z}\circ T_{X}(w-w_{k})\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}+\|T_{Z}\circ T_{X}w_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}.

Boundedness of the trace operators gives us that

\|T_{Z}\circ T_{X}(w-w_{k})\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}\lesssim\|T_{X}(w-w_{k})\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)}\lesssim\|w-w_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Recall that
T_{Z}u_{i}=f
as
u_{i}\in\mathcal{A}(f)
, and so linearity of the trace operators gives us

\|T_{Z}\circ T_{X}w_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}=\left\|\sum_{i=k}^{N(k)}\lambda_{k,i}T_{Z}u_{i}-f\right\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}=0.

This proves the claim, and so we have that
T_{X}w\in\mathcal{A}(f)
.

Since
\overline{T_{X}w}
is the
p
-harmonic extension of
T_{X}w
to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, it follows that

\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{T_{X}w}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}.

As
T_{X}w\in\mathcal{A}(f)
, we then have from (
5.5
), (
5
), and (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle I\leq I_{G}(T_{X}w)=\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{T_{X}w}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}T_{X}w

\displaystyle G\,d\nu\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}T_{X}wG\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}T_{X}w_{k}\,G\,d\nu\right)\leq I.

Therefore, setting
u:=T_{X}w
, we have that
I_{G}(u)=I
, completing the proof of the existence.

To complete the proof of the theorem, we now suppose that
w\in\mathcal{A}(f)
is another minimizer of
I_{G}
. Then we have
that
I_{G}(u)=I_{G}(w)
, and so

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,u-w)=\int_{Z}G(u-w)\,d\nu=\mathcal{E}_{T}(w,u-w).

It follows that

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,u-w)-\mathcal{E}_{T}(w,u-w)=0.

By the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{T}
, we have that

\int_{X_{\varepsilon}}\langle|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{u}-|\nabla\overline{w}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{w},\ \nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{w})\rangle\,d\mu_{\beta}=0.

It follows that for
\mu_{\beta}
-a.e. point in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
we have
\nabla\overline{u}-\nabla\overline{w}=0
, and so

u-w
is constant on
X_{\varepsilon}
by the Poincaré inequality on
(X,d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. As
T_{Z}u=T_{Z}w=f
, it follows that

u=w

\nu
-a.e. in
Z
.
∎

Proposition 5.8

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, and let
f_{1},f_{2}\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
be such that
f_{1}\leq f_{2}

\pi
-a.e. on
\partial Z
. For
i=1,2
, let
u_{i}\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be a minimizer for (
5.1
) with boundary data
f_{i}
. Then
u_{1}\leq u_{2}

\nu
-a.e. in
Z
.

Proof.

Let
u:=\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and
v:=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. Since
T_{Z}u_{1}=f_{1}
and
T_{Z}u_{2}=f_{2}
, it then follows that
T_{Z}u=f_{1}
and
T_{Z}v=f_{2}
, that is,
u\in\mathcal{A}(f_{1})
and
v\in\mathcal{A}(f_{2})
. Let

A:=\{x\in\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}:\overline{u}_{1}(x)>\overline{u}_{2}(x)\},

where
\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu)
are the
p
-harmonic extensions of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
, respectively. Since
v\in\mathcal{A}(f_{2})
and
u_{2}
is a minimizer of (
5.1
) for boundary data
f_{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}_{2}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}u_{2}G\,d\nu=I_{G}(u_{2})\leq I_{G}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}vG\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\max\{\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}G\,d\nu.

Here, the last inequality follows from the fact that
\overline{v}
is the
p
-harmonic extension of
v=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and
T_{X}\max\{\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\}=\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. We then have that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}_{2}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}u_{2}G\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}|\nabla\overline{u}_{1}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}+\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus A}|\nabla\overline{u}_{2}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{A\cap Z}u_{1}G\,d\nu-p\int_{Z\setminus A}u_{2}G\,d\nu,

and so it follows that

(5.9)

\int_{A}|\nabla\overline{u}_{2}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{A\cap Z}u_{2}G\,d\nu\leq\int_{A}\nabla\overline{u}_{1}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{A\cap Z}u_{1}G\,d\nu.

Using the fact that
\overline{u}
is the
p
-harmonic extension of
u=\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and that
T_{X}\min\{\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\}=\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, along with (
5.9
), we have that

\displaystyle I_{G}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}uG\,\nu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\min\{\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}G\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{A}|\nabla\overline{u}_{2}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}+\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus A}|\nabla\overline{u}_{1}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{A\cap Z}u_{2}G\,d\nu-p\int_{Z\setminus A}u_{1}G\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}_{1}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}-p\int_{Z}u_{1}G\,d\nu=I_{G}(u_{1}).

As
u_{1}
is a minimizer of (
5.1
) for boundary data
f_{1}
and
u\in\mathcal{A}(f_{1})
, it follows that
u
is also a minimizer. By Theorem
5.2
,
u_{1}
is unique, and so
\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=u_{1}

\nu
-a.e. in
Z
, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

A note of caution is in order here. In the case that the function
G=0
, the comparison principle also implies a maximum principle: if the boundary data
f
satisfies
f\leq M
for some real number
M
, then the solution also is bounded above by
M
. This is because when
G=0
, the constant function
M
is a solution with boundary data
M
, and the comparison theorem applied to these two solutions yields the maximum principle. When
G\neq 0
however, the maximum principle need not hold, and so the comparison theorem does not yield the validity of maximum principle when the inhomogeneity data
G
is not
the zero function. Indeed, with the boundary data
f=0
, the zero-function is
never
a solution to the minimization problem when
G
is not the zero function.

6.
Stability

In this section, we continue to adopt the standing assumptions of the previous sections and show that
solutions to the inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem given by Definition
2.18
are stable
with respect to perturbation of both the Dirichlet data
f
and the inhomogeneous data
G
.
We are interested in knowing whether if the data
(f,G)
is approximated by a sequence of data
(f_{k},G_{k})
,
then the solution
u_{k}
to the

(f_{k},G_{k})
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem converges to the solution
u
of the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet
problem. We will prove this in the final subsection below,
see Theorem
6.12
.
We first handle two simpler cases, namely, if the inhomogeneity data
G_{k}=G
for some fixed
G
, or if the Dirichlet boundary data

f_{k}=f
for some fixed
f
; these simpler
cases are handled in the first two subsections respectively, and provide tools that are used in proving the final Theorem
6.12
at
the end.

We begin by proving the following gradient estimate for solutions to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem. Recall that given
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, we denote by
\overline{u}
the
p
-harmonic extension of
u
to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
. If
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem, then

\displaystyle\|\nabla\overline{u}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}^{p}+\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\|f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}+\left(1+\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\right)=:K(f,G).

Here, the constant
C
depends only on

p
,
\theta
, the doubling constant of
\nu
,
\diam(Z)
,
\nu(Z)
, and
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(\partial Z)
.

Proof.

Since we will not consider the Dirichlet data
f
to be extended to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
as a
p
-harmonic extension,
for ease of notation we will denote
E_{X,\partial}f
and
T_{X}\circ E_{X,\partial}f=E_{Z}f
also by
f
.
Since
u
is a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem, we have by
the Hölder inequality and boundedness of the trace operator
T_{X}
that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}+p\int_{Z}(u-f)G\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}+p\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\|u-f\|_{L^{p}(Z,\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}+C\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\left(\|\overline{u}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla(\overline{u}-f)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\right).

Since
\overline{u}-f=0

\pi
-a.e. on
\partial Z
, we have from Theorem
2.7
that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\leq C\|\nabla(\overline{u}-f)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\leq C\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}.

Substituting this into the previous expression, applying the triangle inequality, and regrouping the terms yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\left(\|\nabla\overline{u}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}-C\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\right)\leq\|\nabla f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}.

From this, we see that either
\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p-1}\leq C\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}+1
(in which case (
6.1
) holds), or

\displaystyle\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|^{p}_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}+\|G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\|f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}\right),

where we have used boundedness in energy
of the extension operator
E_{X,\partial}
in the last inequality. Thus the conclusion of the lemma holds, with constant
C\geq 1
depending only on
the boundedness constants of the operators
T_{X}
and
E_{X,\partial}
, and the constant in the Maz’ya inequality Theorem
2.7
,
but these constants depend only on
p
,
\theta
, the doubling constant of
\nu
,
\diam(Z)
,
\nu(Z)
, and
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(\partial Z)
.
∎

6.1.
Stability with respect to the Dirichlet boundary data

In this subsection we consider the stability of the Dirichlet problem when perturbing the Dirichlet boundary data.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, and let
f,g\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
. Let
u,v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be solutions to the
(f,G)
- and
(g,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problems respectively, and let
K(f,G)
and
K(g,G)
be the respective
constants on the right hand side of (
6.1
). Then, when
p\geq 2
, we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)},

and when
1<p<2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla

\displaystyle(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)^{p/2}\left(K(f,G)^{p}+K(g,G)^{p}\right)^{(2-p)/2}\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}^{p/2}.

Here the constant
C\geq 1
depends only on
p
,
\theta
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
.

Proof.

As before, we denote by
f
and
g
the corresponding extensions of
f
and
g
to

Z
and
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
given by
E_{Z}
and
E_{X,\partial}
. We then have that

(u-f)-(v-g)=u-v-f+g\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, with
T_{Z}(u-v-f+g)=0

\pi
-a.e. in
\partial Z
.
From Remark
2.16
, we know that

\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,(u-f)-(v-g))
can be computed using the
p
-harmonic extension
\overline{u}
of
u
to
X_{\varepsilon}
and with
the extension
(\overline{u}-f)-(\overline{v}-g)
as the extension of
(u-f)-(v-g)
.
Since
u
is a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\,\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v}-f+g)\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=\int_{Z}(u-v-f+g)Gd\nu,

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\,\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=\int_{Z}(u-v-f+g)Gd\nu+\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\,\nabla(f-g)\rangle d\mu_{\beta}.

Likewise, since
v
is a solution to the
(g,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{v},\,\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=\int_{Z}(u-v-f+g)Gd\nu+\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{v},\,\nabla(f-g)\rangle d\mu_{\beta}.

By subtracting this from the previous equality and applying the Hölder inequality, Lemma
6.1
, and boundedness of the extension operator
E_{X,\partial}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\langle|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{u}-|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{v},\

\displaystyle\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\langle|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{u}-|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{v},\ \nabla(f-g)\rangle d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-1}+|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-1}\right)|\nabla(f-g)|d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\nabla\overline{u}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\nabla\overline{v}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p-1}\right)\|\nabla(f-g)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)},

where
K(f,G)
and
K(g,G)
denote the constants on the right hand side of the inequality in Lemma
6.1
for
u
and
v
respectively.

By
[
59
, page 95]
, it follows that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{u}-|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{v},\

\displaystyle\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle\geq\begin{cases}C\,|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p},&p\geq 2,\\
C\,\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|+|\nabla\overline{v}|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{2},&1<p<2,\end{cases}

with constant
C\geq 1
depending only on
p
. Thus, when
p\geq 2
, it follows from (
6.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)},

which gives us (
6.4
).

When
1<p<2
, it follows from the Hölder inequality, (
6.7
), (
6.1
), and Lemma
6.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{2}\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|+|\nabla\overline{v}|\right)^{p-2}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{p/2}\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|+|\nabla\overline{v}|\right)^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{(2-p)/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)^{p/2}\left(\|\nabla\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}+\|\nabla\overline{v}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}^{p}\right)^{(2-p)/2}\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K(f,G)^{p-1}+K(g,G)^{p-1}\right)^{p/2}\left(K(f,G)^{p}+K(g,G)^{p}\right)^{(2-p)/2}\|f-g\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}^{p/2},

which gives (
6.3
). We note that in either case, the constant
C\geq 1
depends only on
p
,
\theta
, and the boundedness constants from the relevant trace and extension operators, which depend only on
p
,
\theta
and the doubling constant of
\nu
.
∎

6.2.
Stability with respect to the inhomogeneity data

Having established that the problem is stable under perturbation of the Dirichlet boundary data in the prior subsection, we now
turn our attention to establishing stability under perturbation of the inhomogeneity data.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, let
G,H\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, and let
u,v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be solutions to the
(f,G)
- and
(f,H)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problems respectively. Then, if
p\geq 2
, we have that

(6.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq C\,\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}^{p^{\prime}},

and when
1<p<2
, we have

(6.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq C\,\left(K(f,G)^{p}+K(f,H)^{p}\right)^{(2-p)}\,\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}^{p}.

Here the constant
C\geq 1
depends only on
p
,
\theta
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
, as well as
\diam(Z)
,
\nu(Z)
, and
\BCap_{\theta,p}^{\overline{Z}}(\partial Z)
.

Proof.

Since
T_{Z}u=f=T_{Z}v

\pi
-a.e. on
\partial Z
, we have that
T_{Z}(u-v)=0

\pi
-a.e. on
\partial Z
. Therefore, since
u
and
v
are solutions to the
(f,G)
- and
(f,H)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problems, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\ \nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}(u-v)Gd\nu

as well as

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{v},\ \nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}(u-v)Hd\nu,

thanks to Remark
2.16
.

Subtracting the second equality from the first, and using Hölder’s inequality, boundedness of
T_{X}
, and Theorem
2.7
,
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\langle|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{u}-|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\overline{v},\

\displaystyle\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=\int_{Z}(u-v)(G-H)d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(Z,\nu)}\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|\overline{u}-\overline{v}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\right)\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\,\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}.

When
p\geq 2
, it follows from (
6.7
) and (
6.2
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq C\,\|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\,\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)},

which gives us (
6.9
).

When
1<p<2
, it follows from the Hölder inequality, (
6.7
), (
6.2
), and Lemma
6.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-

\displaystyle\overline{v})|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\leq\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})|^{2}\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|+|\nabla\overline{v}|\right)^{p-2}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{p/2}\left(\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\nabla\overline{u}|+|\nabla\overline{v}|\right)^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{(2-p)/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left(\|\nabla(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}\,\|G-H\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}\right)^{p/2}\,\left(K(f,G)^{p}+K(f,H)^{p}\right)^{(2-p)/2},

which gives us (
6.10
).
∎

6.3.
Complete stability

We now have the tools needed to prove the full stability question.

Theorem 6.12
.

Suppose that
(f_{k})_{k}\subset B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and
(G_{k})_{k}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, and

f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
,
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
such that
f_{k}\to f
and
G_{k}\to G
in their
respective function-spaces. Let
u_{k}
be a solution to the
(f_{k},G_{k})
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem on
Z
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
u
a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem on
Z
. Then
u_{k}\to u
in
B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
.

Proof.

Since
(f_{k})_{k}
and
(G_{k})_{k}
are Cauchy in their respective function-spaces, it follows that their norms are bounded.
Hence, from the definition of the functional
K
from (
6.1
), we have that the sequences

\{K(f_{k},G_{k}\}_{k}
and
\{K(f_{k},G)\}_{k}
are bounded; set

M:=\sup_{k}\ K(f_{k},G_{k})+K(f_{k},G)+K(f,G).

We will prove this theorem in the case
p\geq 2
, with the corresponding modifications for the case
1<p<2
from the
two propositions yielding the corresponding results for
1<p<2
; we leave this modification for the reader.

Let
w_{k}
be a solution to the
(f_{k},G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem. Such a solution exists by Theorem
5.2
. Then by Proposition
6.8

we have that

(6.13)

\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{w}_{k})|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\leq C\,\|G_{k}-G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}^{p^{\prime}},

and from Proposition
6.3
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{w}_{k}-\overline{u})|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left(K(f_{k},G)^{p-1}+K(f,G)^{p-1}\right)\,\|f_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}

(6.14)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\,M^{p-1}\,\|f_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}.

Combining (
6.13
) and (
6.3
) we see that

\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u})|^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\leq 2^{p}\,C\,(1+M^{p-1})\,\left(\|G_{k}-G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}^{p^{\prime}}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}\right),

from which we conclude that
\nabla\overline{u}_{k}\to\nabla\overline{u}
in
L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. Applying Theorem
2.7
to

(\overline{u}_{k}-f_{k})-(\overline{u}-f)
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\overline{u}_{k}-f_{k})-(\overline{u}-f)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\nabla[(\overline{u}_{k}-f_{k})-(\overline{u}-f)]\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\nabla(\overline{u}_{k}-\overline{u})\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+C\,\|\nabla(f_{k}-f)\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,(1+M^{p-1})\,\left(\|G_{k}-G\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)}^{p^{\prime}}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)}+\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p}(\partial Z,\pi)}\right),

where we have also used the boundedness of the extension operator
E_{X,\partial}
. It follows that
\overline{u}_{k}\to\overline{u}

in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
. Now by the continuity of the linear trace operator
T_{X,\partial}
, we see that
u_{k}\to u
in

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, completing the proof.
∎

7.
Regularity

In this section, we continue to adopt the standing assumptions of the previous sections and discuss regularity results for
solutions to the inhomogeneous and homogeneous Dirichlet problem. For the inhomogeneous problem, these results follow
mutatis mutandis from earlier results, namely
[
56
,
61
,
27
]
, with slight modifications. For this reason, we
do not include the full proofs, only descriptions of the necessary adaptations to our setting. For the homogeneous
problem, we relate solutions to
p
-harmonic functions in the hyperbolic filling to obtain a Kellogg-type property in Theorem
7.13
. The
Kellogg property
states that
at capacity-a.e. boundary point, if the Dirichlet boundary data is continuous, then the corresponding solution to the
Dirichlet problem has a limit to that point, from within the domain, and this limit agrees with the boundary data at that point.
For more on the Kellogg property see for instance
[
58
,
22
,
46
,
53
]
,
and for the metric setting see
[
12
,
14
,
11
]
. For the nonlocal setting with
G=0
in the Euclidean
setting see
[
9
,
54
]
.
A criterion, called the
Wiener criterion
, identifies points on the boundary
where such continuous limits exist when the problem is associated with elliptic PDEs, see for instance
[
65
]
for the
Euclidean setting
and
[
10
, Section 11.7]
for the metric setting. Since
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
falls within the framework of
[
10
]
,
we have access to the Wiener criterion for the domain
\Omega:=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
.

7.1.
Interior regularity

In
[
27
]
, the authors considered a problem closely related to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem
involving the operator
\mathcal{E}_{T}
. However, this problem did not involve Dirichlet boundary data. Precisely,
given
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
with
\int_{Z}G\,d\nu=0
, the problem considered in
[
27
]
was to find

u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)

satisfying

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}(u,v)=

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{Z}}G\,v\,d\nu\text{ whenever }v\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu).

There, it was shown that solutions
u
to this problem are locally bounded. That is, if
z\in\overline{Z}
and
0<R\leq\diam(\overline{Z})
, then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(z,R),\nu)}\leq d,

where
d>0
is a constant depending on
\theta
,
p
, the doubling constant of
\nu
, as well as
R
,
B(z,R)
, and the quantities

(7.2)

u(0,R):=\left(\fint_{B(z,R)}u_{+}^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\right)^{1/p},\quad\psi(0,R):=\fint_{B(z,R)}|G|u_{+}\,d\nu,

see
[
27
, Section 8]
, in particular
[
27
, Lemmas 8.6, 8.11]
. The arguments there modify the proofs
of
[
61
, Lemma 5.10,  Theorem 5.2]
, where such local
L^{\infty}
-bounds were obtained for
inhomogeneous data
G\in L^{\infty}(Z,\nu)
by the De Giorgi method.

Since the problem we consider in this paper involves Dirichlet boundary data
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
in addition to inhomogeneous data
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, we are not able to attain local
L^{\infty}
-bounds on all of
\overline{Z}
; we are only able to attain such bounds on balls in
Z
which stay away from
\partial Z
. When considering only such balls, however, the exact
arguments from
[
61
]
and
[
27
]
provide the following lemma.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, let
G\in L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
, and let
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
be a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem. If
z_{0}\in Z
and
R_{0}>0
is such that
B(z_{0},2R_{0})\cap\partial Z=\varnothing
, then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(z_{0},R_{0}),\nu)}\leq d,

where
d>0
is a constant depending only on
\theta
,
p
, the doubling constant of
\nu
as well as
R_{0}
,
B(z_{0},R_{0})
, and the quantities
u(0,R_{0})
and
\psi(0,R_{0})
as given by (
7.2
).

We note that while
[
61
]
and
[
27
]
assume that
\int_{\overline{Z}}G\,d\nu=0
, this
assumption is used primarily to obtain existence and uniqueness of solutions and plays no role in the regularity results therein.

To study Hölder regularity of solutions, we note that when
p>Q
, where
Q
is as in (
2.2
) with respect to the
doubling measure
\mu_{\beta}
, we know that functions in
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, and hence functions in

B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
, are
(1-Q/p)
-Hölder continuous, see for example
[
45
, Theorem 5.1(3)]

or
[
48
, Theorem 9.2.14]
. As such, in the following discussion, we consider only the case
1<p\leq Q
.

In
[
27
, Theorem 1.6]
, the authors showed that solutions to the problem given by (
7.1
) are
locally Hölder continuous in all of
\overline{Z}
. As in the case of the above local boundedness estimates,
we are able to use the argument there verbatim to prove Theorem
7.4
below, with the same Hölder exponent, provided we consider only balls in
Z
which are far from
\partial Z
. Again, this is due to the presence of Dirichlet data on
\partial Z
in our problem.

If
\Omega
is an open subset of a complete doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality,
then any
v\in N^{1,p}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
which is
p
-harmonic in
\Omega
is locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
\Omega
,
where
0<\alpha\leq 1
is a constant depending only on the doubling constant and constants associated with the
Poincaré inequality, see
[
56
, Theorem 5.2]
. The constant
0<\alpha\leq 1

appearing in Theorem
7.4
below is the local Hölder regularity exponent for
p
-harmonic functions
on domains in the metric measure space

(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, obtained from
[
56
]
.
As such,
\alpha
depends only on
\theta
,
p
, the doubling constant of
\nu
, and the parameters chosen
in the construction of
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}<p\leq Q
, where
Q
is the lower mass bound for
\mu_{\beta}
, as in (
2.2
).
Let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
and let
G\in L^{q}(Z,\nu)\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(Z,\nu)
with

\frac{Q-p(1-\theta)}{p\theta}<q.

If
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is a solution to the
(f,G)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem, then for all
z_{0}\in Z
,
R_{0}>0
such that
B(z_{0},2R_{0})\cap\partial Z=\varnothing
, and for all
z,w\in B(z_{0},R_{0})\cap Z
, we have that

|u(z)-u(w)|\leq Cd(z,w)^{1-\gamma},

where

\gamma:=\max\left\{1-\alpha,\frac{p(1-\theta)(q-1)+Q}{pq}\right\}.

That is,
u
is locally
(1-\gamma)
-Hölder continuous in
Z
. Here, the constant
C
depends on
\theta
,
p
, the doubling constant of
\nu
, as well as on
R_{0}
,
B(z_{0},R_{0})
, and the constant
d
given by Lemma
7.3
.

The requirement that
G\in L^{q}(Z)
is automatically satisfied if
p^{\prime}>(Q-\beta/\varepsilon)/(p-\beta/\varepsilon)
, with the choice then of
q=p^{\prime}
.
The estimates for
q
that guarantees the above local Hölder regularity of the solution
u
agrees with that of
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]

when
p=2
,
where
n
, the dimension of the Euclidean space, corresponds to the lower mass bound dimension
Q-\beta/\varepsilon
of the measure
\nu
,
and the fractional exponent
s
of
[
25
]
corresponds to
\theta=1-\beta/\varepsilon p
.

7.2.
Kellogg-type property for the homogeneous problem

When considering the problem given by Definition
2.18
with
G\equiv 0
, that is, the
homogeneous Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, we
are able to obtain Kellogg-type results in Theorem
7.13
below.
Note that here,
p
is not required to have any relationship with
Q
; we continue to adopt the standing
assumptions from the previous section, in particular assuming that
p>\max\{1,\sigma/\theta\}
.
We first relate solutions to this homogeneous Dirichlet problem to solutions to the Dirichlet
problem for
p
-harmonic functions on
\Omega:=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
.
Note that
\partial\Omega=\partial Z
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
f\in B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, and denote
\Omega:=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
. If
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is a solution to the homogeneous Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, then the
p
-harmonic extension
\overline{u}
of
u
to
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem for
p
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
. That is,

(7.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\overline{u},\nabla v\rangle d\mu_{\beta}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all }v\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})\text{ with }T_{X,\partial}v=0\;\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}\text{-q.e.\ on }\partial\Omega,

(7.7)

\displaystyle T_{X,\partial}\overline{u}=f

\displaystyle\quad\pi\text{-a.e.\ on }\partial\Omega.

Moreover, by modifying
f
on a
\pi
-null set if necessary, we also have that for
\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}
-q.e.
\xi\in\partial\Omega
,

T_{X,\partial}\overline{u}(\xi)=f(\xi).

Proof.

By our construction of the solution to the
(f,0)
-inhomogeneous Dirichlet problem and by the uniqueness of the solution, we know
that (
7.6
) is satisfied whenever
v\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
with
T_{X,\partial}v=0
; here we have
also used Remarks
2.16
and
4.3
.
We also have the validity of (
7.7
) by the construction of
\overline{u}
,
thanks to Remark
4.3
.

As
\overline{u}\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and since
\mu_{\beta}
is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, it follows that
\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
-q.e.
\xi\in\partial Z
is a Lebesgue point of
\overline{u}
, see
[
48
, Theorem 9.2.8]
for example. Hence, we can modify
f
on a set of
\pi
-measure zero so that
T_{X,\partial}\overline{u}=f

\BCap^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}_{p}
-q.e. on
\partial Z
.∎

The rest of this section is devoted to the matter of boundary continuity when the Dirichlet data is continuous. For this, we assume that

\partial Z
is uniformly perfect (as in Definition
2.1
).
We aim to show that every point on
\partial Z
satisfies the Wiener criterion with respect to the domain

\Omega=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
, see for example
[
10
, Chapter 11.4]
. To do so, we first obtain the following capacity estimates:

Lemma 7.8
.

Assume that
\partial Z
is
K
-uniformly perfect for some
K\geq 2
. Let
z_{0}\in\partial Z
and let
0<r<\diam(\partial Z)/(4K^{3})
. Then,

(7.9)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r),\,B(z_{0},2r))\lesssim\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{\theta p}},

and

(7.10)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r)\cap\partial Z,\,B(z_{0},2r))\gtrsim\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{\theta p}},

with the comparison constants depending only on
K
,
p
,
\theta
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
.

Proof.

Consider the function
u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
given by

u(x):=(1-(\dist(x,z_{0})/r-1)_{+})\chi_{B(z_{0},2r)}(x),

which satisfies
u=1
on
B(z_{0},r)
and
u=0
in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus B(z_{0},2r)
. Since
u
is
1/r
-Lipschitz in

\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
with upper gradient
g_{u}\leq\tfrac{1}{r}\,\chi_{B(z_{0},2r)\setminus B(z_{0},r)}
,
by the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
and Theorem
2.10
we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r),B(z_{0},2r))\leq\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{r^{p}}\chi_{B(z_{0},2r)\setminus B(z_{0},r)}d\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{p}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}}=\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{\theta p}},

which gives us (
7.9
). Note that in the last equality, we have used the relationship
\beta/\varepsilon=p(1-\theta)
.

Let
E:=B(z_{0},r)\cap\partial Z
and let
F:=B(z_{0},2K^{3}r)\cap\partial Z\setminus B(z_{0},2r)
. Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a cover of
E
by
balls that also intersect
E
, such that
\rad(B_{i})\leq 2K^{3}r
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a ball
\widetilde{B}_{i}

centered at
E
such that
B_{i}\subset\widetilde{B}_{i}
and
\rad(\widetilde{B}_{i})=2\rad(B_{i})
. By the 5-covering lemma, there
exists a disjoint subcollection
\{\widetilde{B}_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
such that
E\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}5\widetilde{B}_{i}
.
Thus, by the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
and the
(\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma)
-codimensional relationship between

\mu_{\beta}
and
\pi
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\gtrsim\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(5\widetilde{B}_{i})}{\rad(5B_{i})^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\simeq\sum_{i\in I}\pi(5\widetilde{B}_{i})\geq\pi(E)\simeq\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},2K^{3}r))}{(2K^{3}r)^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}.

Since the collection
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is arbitrary, we have that

(7.11)

\mathcal{H}^{-(\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma)}_{\mu_{\beta},2K^{3}r}(E)\gtrsim\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},2K^{3}r))}{(2K^{3}r)^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}.

Similarly, let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a cover of
F
by balls such that
\rad(B_{i})\leq 2K^{3}r
. Choosing
\widetilde{B}_{i}\supset B_{i}
as above, centered at
F
with comparable radius to
B_{i}
, we use the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
and codimensionality with respect to
\pi
to obtain

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\gtrsim\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(5\widetilde{B}_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\simeq\sum_{i\in I}\pi(5\widetilde{B}_{i})\geq\pi(F).

Since
\partial Z
is
K
-uniformly perfect, and since
\partial Z\setminus B(z_{0},2K^{3}r)\neq\varnothing
, there exists
\zeta\in\partial Z\cap B(z_{0},2K^{2}r)\setminus B(z_{0},2Kr)
, and so it follows that
B(\zeta,2(K-1)r)\cap\partial Z\subset F
. Using the codimensional relationship of
\mu_{\beta}
and
\pi
along with the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
then gives us that

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\gtrsim\pi(B(\zeta,2(K-1)r)\cap\partial Z)\simeq\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(\zeta,2(K-1)r))}{(2(K-1)r)^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}\simeq\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},2K^{3}r))}{(2K^{3}r)^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}.

Since
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is arbitrary, we then have that

(7.12)

\mathcal{H}^{-(\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma)}_{\mu_{\beta},2K^{3}r}(F)\gtrsim\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},2K^{3}r))}{(2K^{3}r)^{\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma}}.

Let
u\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
be such that
u=1
on
B(z_{0},r)\cap\partial Z=E
and
u=0
in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus B(z_{0},2r)\supset F
. Note that
\beta/\varepsilon+\sigma<p
by our assumption that
p>\sigma/\theta
, and since
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, it supports a
(1,q)
-Poincaré inequality for all
1<q<\infty
. Therefore, by (
7.11
) and (
7.12
),
we apply
[
32
, Lemma 3.1]
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\geq\int_{B(z_{0},2K^{3}r)}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu_{\beta}\gtrsim\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{p}}\simeq\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{p-\beta/\varepsilon}}=\frac{\nu(B(z_{0},r))}{r^{\theta p}},

where we have again used the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
as well as the codimensional relationship between
\mu_{\beta}
and
\nu
.
This inequality gives us (
7.10
), as
u
is an arbitrary admissible function for computing
\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r)\cap\partial Z,\,B(z_{0},2r))
.
∎

Using the previous lemmas, we now obtain the following boundary regularity result for the homogeneous Dirichlet problem.

Theorem 7.13
.

Suppose that
\partial Z
is uniformly perfect, and let
f\in C(\partial Z)\cap B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
. If
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is a solution to the homogeneous Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, then for all
z_{0}\in\partial Z
, we have that

\lim_{Z\ni z\to z_{0}}u(z)=f(z_{0}).

Proof.

By Lemma
7.8
, it follows that each
z_{0}\in\partial Z
satisfies the Wiener criterion with respect to the domain
\Omega=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r)\cap\partial Z,\,B(z_{0},2r))}{\operatorname{cap}_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}(B(z_{0},r),B(z_{0},2r))}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}\gtrsim\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{1}{r}dr=\infty,

where
\delta=\diam(\partial Z)/(4K^{3})
with
K\geq 2
the uniform perfectness constant of
\partial Z
. Therefore,
by
[
10
, Theorem 11.24]
or
[
12
]
for example, it follows that for each
z_{0}\in\partial Z
, the following holds: for
every
f\in C(\partial Z)\cap B^{\theta-\sigma/p}_{p,p}(\partial Z,\pi)
, if
v
is
p
-harmonic in

\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
with
T_{X,\partial}v=f

\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}
-a.e. on
\partial Z
, then

\lim_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z\ni x\to z_{0}}v(x)=f(z_{0}).

For such
f
, we know from Lemma
7.5
that if
u\in B^{\theta}_{p,p}(\overline{Z},\nu)
is
a solution to the homogeneous Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, then
\overline{u}
is
p
-harmonic
in
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
and
T_{X,\partial}\overline{u}=f

\BCap_{p}^{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}
-a.e. in
\partial Z
.
As
\overline{u}
is continuous on
\Omega=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial Z
by
[
56
]
, it follows
from the continuity of
f
that
\overline{u}
is continuous on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
.
Hence, since
u=T_{X}\overline{u}
on
Z
, it follows that

\lim_{Z\ni z\to z_{0}}u(z)=f(z_{0}).\qed

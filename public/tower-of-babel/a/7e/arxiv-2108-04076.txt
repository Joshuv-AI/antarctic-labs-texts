Lie n {n} -algebras and cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter operators on n {n} -Lie algebras
Ming Chen, Jiefeng Liu, Yao Ma

Abstract
. Based on the differential graded Lie algebra controlling deformations of an n n -Lie algebra with a representation (called an n n - 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieRep} pair), we construct a Lie n n -algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements characterize relative Rota-Baxter operators on n n - 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieRep} pairs. The notion of an n n -pre-Lie algebra is introduced, which is the underlying algebraic structure of the relative Rota-Baxter operator. We give the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators and study infinitesimal deformations and extensions of order m m deformations to order m + 1 m+1 deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators through the cohomology groups of relative Rota-Baxter operators. Moreover, we build the relation between the cohomology groups of relative Rota-Baxter operators on n n - 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieRep} pairs and those on ( n + 1 ) (n+1) - 𝖫𝗂𝖾𝖱𝖾𝗉 \mathsf{LieRep} pairs by certain linear functions.

The notion of an

cker relation in the physics literature. See the review article

The notion of a Lie

-algebra was introduced by Hanlon and Wachs in
[

. Lie

-algebra, in which only the
n
-ary bracket is nonzereo. See

-algebras that characterize relative Rota-Baxter operators on
n
-Lie algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators.

The classical Yang-Baxter equation plays a significant role in many fields in mathematics and mathematical physics such as quantum groups (
[
10
,
17
]
) and integrable systems (
[
46
]
). In order to gain better understanding of the relationship between the classical Yang-Baxter equation and the related integrable systems, the more general notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator (also called

-operator) on a

pair was introduced by Kupershmidt (

). To study solutions of

-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation, the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a

[

. See the book
[
29
]
for more details and applications about Rota-Baxter operators.

In
[
48
]
, the authors showed that a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra is a Maurer-Cartan element of a graded Lie algebra. Recently, it was shown in
[
49
]
that a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra is a Maurer-Cartan element of a Lie
3

n
-Lie algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements of Lie
n
-algebras.

Pre-Lie algebras are a class of nonassociative algebras coming from the study of convex
homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups, and the
cohomologies of associative algebras. See the survey
[
8
]
and the references therein for more details. The beauty of a pre-Lie algebra is that the commutator gives rise to a Lie algebra and the
left multiplication gives rise to a representation of the commutator Lie algebra. Conversely, a relative Rota-Baxter operator action on a Lie algebra gives rise to a pre-Lie algebra (
[
28
,
35
]
) and thus the pre-Lie algebra can be seen as the underlying algebraic structure of a relative Rota-Baxter operator. In this paper, we introduce the notion of an
n
-pre-Lie algebra, which gives an
n
-Lie algebra naturally and its left multiplication operator gives rise to a representation of this
n
-Lie algebra. An
n
-pre-Lie algebra can also be obtained through the action of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on an
n
-Lie algebra.

1.2.
Deformations and cohomology theories

The theory of deformation plays a prominent role in mathematics and physics. In physics, the ideal of deformation appears in the perturbative quantum field theory and quantizing classical mechanics. The idea of treating deformation as a tool to study the algebraic structures was introduced by Gerstenhaber in his work of associative algebras (
[
24
,
25
]
) and then was extended to Lie algebras by Nijenhuis and Richardson (
[
41
,
42
]
). Deformations of
3
-Lie algebras and
n
-Lie algebras are studied in
[
1
,
19
,
36
,
51
]
. See the review paper
[
13
,
37
]
for more details. Recently, people pay more attention on the deformations of morphisms (
[
1
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
38
,
53
]
), relative Rota-Baxter operators (
[
12
,
48
,
49
]
) and diagram of algebras (
[
7
,
28
,
39
]
).

Usually the cohomology theory is an important tool in the study of deformations of a mathematical structure. Typically, infinitesimal deformations are classified by a suitable second cohomology group and the obstruction of an order
n
deformation extending to an order
n+1
deformation can be controlled by the third cohomology group. Cohomology and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators (
\mathcal{O}
-operators) on associative algebras, Lie algebras and
3
-Lie algebras were studied in
[
12
,
48
,
49
]
.

In the present paper, we study the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on
n
-
\mathsf{LieRep}
pairs. By using the underlying
n
-Lie algebra of a relative Rota-Baxter operator, we introduce the cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on an
n
-
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair. Then we study infinitesimal deformations and order
n
deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on an
n
-
\mathsf{LieRep}
pair. We show that infinitesimal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators are classified by the first cohomology group and that a higher order deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator is extendable if and only if its obstruction class in the second cohomology group of the relative Rota-Baxter operator is trivial.

2.1.
Representations and cohomology of
n

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
20
]
)

An
n
-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a skew-symmetric linear map
[-,\cdots,-]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\wedge^{n}{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
such that for
x_{i},y_{j}\in{\mathfrak{g}},1\leq i\leq n-1,1\leq j\leq n
, the following identity holds:

(1)

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

For
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, define
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}}\in\mathfrak{\mathfrak{gl}}(\mathfrak{g})
by
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}}y:=[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1},y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}}
is a derivation, i.e.

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1}}y_{i},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
34
]
)

A
representation
of an
n
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,\cdots,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a vector space
V
is a linear map:
\rho:\wedge^{n-1}{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}l(V)},
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{g},
the following equalities hold:

(2)

\displaystyle[\rho(\mathfrak{X}),\rho(\mathfrak{Y})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\mathfrak{X}\circ\mathfrak{Y}),

(3)

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{i}},\cdots,y_{n})\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},y_{i}),

where
\mathfrak{X}=x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1},\mathfrak{Y}=y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}
and
\mathfrak{X}\circ\mathfrak{Y}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1})
.

Let
(V;\rho)
be a representation of an
n
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,\cdots,-]_{\mathfrak{g}}).
Denote by

C^{m}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m-1}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g})\wedge\mathfrak{g},V),\quad(m\geq 1),

which is the space of
n
-cochains. Define
\partial_{\rho}:C^{m}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};V)\rightarrow C^{m+1}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};V)
by

\displaystyle(\partial_{\rho}f)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{m},x_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq m}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},\mathfrak{X}_{j}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{k},\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{m},x_{m+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{m},[\mathfrak{X}_{j},x_{m+1}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j+1}\rho(\mathfrak{X}_{j})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{m},x_{m+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+m-i+1}\rho(x^{1}_{m},\cdots,\widehat{x^{i}_{m}},\cdots,x^{n-1}_{m},x_{m+1})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{m-1},x^{i}_{m})

for any
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i}^{n-1}\in\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}},i=1,2,\cdots,m,x_{m+1}\in\mathfrak{g}.

It was proved in
[
9
]
that
\partial_{\rho}\circ\partial_{\rho}=0.
Thus,
(\oplus^{+\infty}_{m=1}C^{m}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};V),\partial_{\rho})
is a cochain complex.

Definition 2.3
.

The cohomology of the
n
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus^{+\infty}_{m=1}C^{m}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};V),\partial_{\rho})

The corresponding

2.2.
Bidegrees and graded Lie algebras on
C^{*}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be two vector spaces. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{l,k}
the subspace of

\otimes^{m}(\wedge^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}))\wedge(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})

which contains the number of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
) is
l
(resp.
k
).

The vector space

\otimes^{m}(\wedge^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}))\wedge(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
can be extended into the direct sum of
\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},l+k=m(n-1)+1
. Furthermore, we have the following isomorphism

(4)

C^{m}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})\cong\sum\limits_{l+k=m(n-1)+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{1})\oplus\sum\limits_{l+k=m(n-1)+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2}).

An element
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{1})
(resp.
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
) naturally gives an element
\hat{f}\in C^{m}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
, which is called its lift. For example, the lifts of linear maps
\mu:\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1},\rho:\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are defined by

(5)

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}((x_{1},u_{1}),\cdots,(x_{n},u_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),0),

(6)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}((x_{1},u_{1}),\cdots,(x_{n},u_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})(u_{i})),

respectively. Let
H:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
be a linear map. Its lift is given by
\hat{H}(x,u)=(H(u),0)
.

Definition 2.4
.

A linear map
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}(\wedge^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}))\wedge(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
has a
bidegree

k|l
, which
is denoted by
\|f\|=k|l
, if f satisfies the following four conditions:

(1)

k+l=m(n-1)

(2)

If
X
is an element in
\mathfrak{g}^{k+1,l}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}

(3)

If
X
is an element in
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l+1}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}

(4)

All the other case,
f(X)=0
.

We denote the set of linear maps of bidegree
k|l
by
C^{k\mid l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
. A linear map
f
is said to be homogeneous if
f
has a bidegree.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. We consider the graded vector space

C^{*}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{m\geq 0}C^{m}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g})\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

Then the graded vector space
C^{*}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the graded commutator bracket

(7)

[P,Q]_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}=P\circ Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\circ P,\qquad\forall~P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

is a graded Lie algebra (
[
44
]
), where
P\circ Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\displaystyle(P\circ Q)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{p}_{k=1}(-1)^{(k-1)q}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S(k-1,q)}(-1)^{\sigma}\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}P(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},x^{1}_{k+q}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{i-1}_{k+q}\wedge

\displaystyle Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x^{i}_{k+q})\wedge x^{i+1}_{k+q}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{n-1}_{k+q},\mathfrak{X}_{k+q+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S(p,q)}(-1)^{pq}(-1)^{\sigma}P(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q)},x)),

for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x^{1}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{n-1}_{i}\in\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},i=1,2,\cdots,p+q
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,

defines an
n
-Lie structure on

if and only if

, i.e.
\mu
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{n-\mathrm{Lie}})
. Moreover, the coboundary operator
\partial
of the
n
-Lie algebra with the coefficients in the adjoint representation can be given by

\partial_{\mathrm{ad}}f=(-1)^{p}[\mu,f]_{n-\mathrm{Lie}},\quad\forall f\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

The following lemma shows that the graded Lie algebra structure on

(\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}C^{k(n-1)\mid 0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[-,-]_{n-\mathrm{Lie}})

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\mu+\rho,\mu+\rho]_{n-\mathrm{Lie}}\big(x_{1}+u_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}+u_{n-1},y_{1}+v_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}+v_{n-1},y_{n}+v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\mu+\rho)(y_{1}+v_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1}+v_{i-1},(\mu+\rho)(x_{1}+u_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}+u_{n-1},y_{i}+v_{i}),y_{i+1}+v_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}+v_{n})

Definition 3.20

Lemma 4.1

Proof.

Let
T

n
-
\mathsf{LieRep}

(\mathfrak{g},[-,\cdots,-]_{\mathfrak{g}};\rho)

Define

It is obvious that
\bar{T}\circ\bar{T}=0

is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect product

-Lie algebra

(

It was shown in
[

that if

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,\cdots,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})

is an

-Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle\{-,\cdots,-\}^{j}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}}[\cdots,Nx_{i_{1}},\cdots,Nx_{i_{j}},\cdots]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]^{j-1}_{N},

\displaystyle\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}^{1}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}[x_{1},\cdots,Nx_{i},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad 2\leq j\leq n-1.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle[x_{1}+u_{1},\cdots,x_{n}+u_{n}]_{\bar{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-1}}[\cdots,\bar{T}(x_{i_{1}}+u_{i_{1}}),\cdots,\bar{T}(x_{i_{n-1}}+u_{i_{n-1}}),\cdots]_{\rho}

\displaystyle-\bar{T}\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-2}}[\cdots,\bar{T}(x_{i_{1}}+u_{i_{1}}),\cdots,\bar{T}(x_{i_{n-2}}+u_{i_{n-2}}),\cdots]_{\rho}

\displaystyle-\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\sum\limits_{j<i}(-1)^{n-j}T\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,Tu_{n})(u_{j})

\displaystyle-\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\sum\limits_{j>i}(-1)^{n-j}(-1)^{n-i-1}T\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,Tu_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})

\displaystyle-\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\sum\limits_{j<i}(-1)^{n-j}(-1)^{n-i}T\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{n-i}\big([Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n},x_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\sum\limits_{j>i}(-1)^{n-j-1}T\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,Tu_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})

\displaystyle-\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}\sum\limits_{j<i}(-1)^{n-j}T\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})\big)+\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n})(u_{i})

\displaystyle[u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}]_{T}+\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{n-i}\rho_{T}(u_{1},\cdots,\widehat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{n})(x_{i}),

which implies that
(\mathfrak{g};\rho_{T})
is a representation of the
n
-Lie algebra
(V,[-,\cdots,-]_{T})

\displaystyle(\partial_{T}f)(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{m},u_{m+1})

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq m}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{U}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{k-1},\mathfrak{U}_{j}\circ\mathfrak{U}_{k},\mathfrak{U}_{k+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{m},u_{m+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j+1}\rho_{T}(\mathfrak{U}_{j})f(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{U}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{m},u_{m+1})

(32)

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

\displaystyle(\partial_{T}\delta_{T}(\mathfrak{X}))(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}[Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n},T\rho(\mathfrak{X})(u_{i})]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}[Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n},[\mathfrak{X},Tu_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-T\rho(\mathfrak{X})(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{i}},\cdots,Tu_{n})(u_{i}))+[\mathfrak{X},[Tu_{1},\cdots,Tu_{i},\cdots,Tu_{n}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-T(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\rho(Tu_{1},\cdots,\widehat{Tu_{j}},\cdots,Tu_{i-1},T\rho(\mathfrak{X})(u_{i})-[\mathfrak{X},Tu_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},Tu_{i+1},\cdots,Tu_{n})(u_{j}))

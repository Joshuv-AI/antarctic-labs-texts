Positive definite maps on amenable groups, II
Mikaël Pichot, Erik Séguin

Abstract
. We introduce an Ulam-type stability condition for positive definite maps defined on a countable group and prove that this condition characterizes amenability.



1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of ref.
[
6
]
on Ulam stability
[
8
]
for positive definite maps on groups.

A countable discrete group
G
is said to be
Ulam stable
(following
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
) if for every
{\delta>0}
, there exists some
{\varepsilon>0}
such that for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|<\delta

for all
{x\in G}
, where
U(\mathcal{H})
denotes the group of unitary operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on the algebra
B(\mathcal{H})
of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
, and a map
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
is said to be an
\varepsilon
-representation
of
G
if

\|\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
x,y\in G
.

It is a well–known theorem of Kazhdan
[
5
, Theorem 1]
that every countable discrete amenable group
[
4
]
is Ulam stable. The converse is an open problem (see in particular
[
3
, Question 1.5]
).

Ulam stability can also be formulated in terms of positive definite maps.
Namely, the following two assertions are equivalent (see
[
6
, Prop. 1.2]
) for every group
G
and Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
:

(0)

there exist real numbers
\kappa>0
and
0<\delta<1
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon

for all
x\in G
;

(0’)

there exist real numbers
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
,
0<\delta<1
, and
p>1
such that for every
0<\varepsilon<1
such that
\kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\leq\delta^{p-1}
and every
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unital positive definite
{\kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{p}}
-representation
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\kappa_{2}\varepsilon

for all
{x\in G}
.

In this equivalence, the first assertion is a variation on Ulam stability, in which
\varepsilon
is required to depend linearly on
\delta
, and the second assertion can be understood as an Ulam stability condition for sufficiently multiplicative positive definite maps on the group
G
.

In the present paper we propose a new stability condition for positive definite maps on
G
and prove that it characterizes amenability. This extends the results of
[
6
]
and provides further justification for the shift in emphasis from unitary representations to positive definite maps.

Let us first quote results from
[
6
]
to motivate the new condition.
A map
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
uniformly bounded
if

{\|{\varphi}\|:=\sup_{x\in G}\,\|{\varphi(x)}\|<\infty}.

The following characterization of amenability is part of Theorem 1.3 of
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

there exists a state
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
such that for every uniformly bounded map
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}
.

The discussion of this result in
[
6
]
isolates two important characteristics of Condition (2):

(A)

the fact that the state
\tau
in (2) provides an invariant mean on a group
G
; in fact, Condition (2) can be viewed as a characterization of the invariance of a mean in terms of positive definite maps;

(B)

the fact that (2) applies to all uniformly bounded maps, as opposed to restricting to
\varepsilon
-representations.

We look for an Ulam-type stability condition for positive definite maps which takes these directions into account. By Problem (A) and Problem (B), we mean the formulation of a condition characterizing amenability analogous to (2) which does not refer to the state
\tau
, and, respectively, restricts to
\varepsilon
-representations.

Considering the equality

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})},\ \ \forall{x\in G},\ \ \forall{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}

in relation with Problem (B), the following result was established in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Suppose that there exists a state
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
and
0<\delta<1
such that for every
\delta
-representation
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}
.

Then
G
is Ulam stable.

Here is a tentative formulation of an Ulam-type stability condition for positive definite maps:

(3)

for every
{\varepsilon>0}
, every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
x\in G
.

Note that Condition (3) does not refer to a state on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
and the statement applies to
\varepsilon
-representations
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Question 1.3
.

Does there exist a non-amenable countable discrete group
G
for which Condition (3) holds?

Thus, a negative answer to this question would resolve both Problem (A) and Problem (B).

Condition (3) should be compared with the conclusion of Kazhdan’s theorem in the case of unitary representations, by which we mean the following assertion (which corresponds to Condition (0) and Condition (4) in
[
6
, Prop. 1.2]
at
\kappa=2
):

there exists a real number
0<\delta<1
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
, every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|\leq 2\varepsilon

for all
x\in G

We shall make
some remarks in particular on the “numerical” discrepancies between the above assertion for unitary maps and Condition (3) for positive definitive maps.

The first remark concerns the constant
\kappa=2
(in the notation of
[
6
, Prop. 1.2(4)]
) in the conclusion of Kazhdan’s theorem. This constant is not intrinsic to the group
G
and it can be removed by using positive definite maps (namely, the fact that
G
is amenable implies (3) with constant 1).

The second remark concerns the constant
\delta
(with respect to
\kappa=2
). Kazhdan proves that
\delta=\frac{1}{200}
is small enough. The corresponding “Ulam stability gap”, namely, the interval
[\delta,1]
, can be reduced, for example by using the techniques developed in
[
2
,
3
]
. Thus, one may choose
\delta=\frac{1}{4}
for instance (see
[
7
]
). (Since
\kappa=2
, stability is tautological for
\varepsilon>1
.) This constant is also unnecessary in the case of positive definite maps (namely, the fact that
G
is amenable implies (3) without imposing a restriction on the values of
\varepsilon
).

One may introduce several variations on Condition (3).
For example, the following formulation was put forward in
[
6
, Question 8.3]
:

(4)

for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every unitary map
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\sup_{y\in G}\|\varphi(x)\varphi(y)-\varphi(xy)\|}

for all
x\in G
.

Question 8.3 in
[
6
]
, which is open, asks if this condition characterizes amenability.
An equivalence between the amenability of
G
and Condition (4) would resolve Problem (A), and would partially resolve Problem (B) in the sense that (4) ensures that
\psi
is close to
\varphi
in the case that

\varphi
is an
\varepsilon
-representation.

In the present paper, we consider a variation of Condition (4) in which the “proximity” relation between
\psi
and
\varphi
is expressed in the strong operator topology, rather than the operator norm. Furthermore, we prove that the new formulation is equivalent to amenability.

This resolves Problem (A), because Condition (5) does not make reference to a state on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
, and this resolves Problem (B) partially, in the sense that Condition (5) ensures that
\psi
is close to
\varphi
—in the operator norm—in the case that

\varphi
is an
\varepsilon
-representation.

Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(5)

for every (separable) Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every uniformly bounded map
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that for every finite set
F\subset G
, every integer
n\geq 1
, and every finite family of functions
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\zeta_{1},\ldots\zeta_{n}\colon F\to\mathcal{H}
, there exists a
y\in G
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x\in F}\|\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}\,\xi_{i}(x)-\psi(x)\,\xi_{i}(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x\in F}\|\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}\,\xi_{i}(x)-\zeta_{i}(x)\|^{2}.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
It relies on the results of
[
6
]
, Tarski’s characterization of amenability (in terms of paradoxical decompositions
[
9
]
), and uses a new characterization of closed convex hulls for a set of bounded operators with respect to the strong operator topology (see §
2
).

We also provide an alternative argument relying on compactness rather than Tarski’s theorem (see Remark
3.5
).

For the sake of completeness, let us prove here the remark we made before the statement of the main theorem:

.

Condition (5) implies that
\psi
is close to
\varphi

\varphi
is a unitary

-representation.

Proof.

Choose

F=\{x\}

n=1

\zeta(x)=\varphi(x)\xi(x)

for all
x\in G
and all

for every

2.
SOT closed convex hulls

We shall begin with a new characterization of closed convex hulls in
B(\mathcal{H})
with respect to the strong operator topology:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
be a bounded operator,
X
be a nonempty set, and
{\varphi:X\to B(\mathcal{H})}
be a map. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\forall\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\forall\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\exists x\in X
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-T\xi_{i}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\|^{2};

(2)

{T\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{\varphi(x):x\in X\}}}
, where the closure is with respect to the strong operator topology.

We require the following lemma which characterizes closed convex hulls in Hilbert spaces in terms of closed half-spaces associated with certain
orthogonal
(namely, median) hyperplanes. It could be viewed as a sort of “separation theorem”, although it is of course restricted in scope compared to the Hahn–Banach theorem due to its Hilbertian nature.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a vector,
X
be a nonempty set, and
{f:X\to\mathcal{H}}
be a map. The following are equivalent

(1)

\forall\eta\in\mathcal{H}
,
\exists x\in X
such that

\|f(x)-\xi\|\leq\|f(x)-\eta\|;

(2)

{\xi\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{f(x):x\in X\}}}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology.

Proof.

Let
{C=\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{f(x):x\in X\}}}
and suppose that

\forall\eta\in\mathcal{H},\ \exists x\in X:\|f(x)-\xi\|\leq\|f(x)-\eta\|

and
{\xi\notin C}
. Let
\eta
denote the orthogonal projection of
\xi
onto
C
. Clearly,
\eta\neq\xi
. The median hyperplane for
[\xi,\eta]
divides
\mathcal{H}
into two disjoint open half-spaces; we denote by
H
the open half-space which contains
\eta
. Since
\eta
is the orthogonal projection of
\xi
onto
C
, the closed convex set
C
is included in
H
, and therefore

\|\zeta-\eta\|<\|\zeta-\xi\|

for every
\zeta\in C
.
For
{x\in X}
as above we obtain

\|f(x)-\xi\|\leq\|f(x)-\eta\|<\|f(x)-\xi\|

which is a contradiction.

Conversely, suppose that

\exists\eta\in\mathcal{H},\forall x\in X:\|f(x)-\xi\|>\|f(x)-\eta\|

and
{\xi\in C}
. Note that
\xi\neq\eta
. Let
H
denote the closed half-space of the median hyperplane for
[\xi,\eta]
which contains
\eta
. Clearly,
f(x)\in H
for every
x\in X
. Since
H
is closed and convex, this implies that
C\subset H
.

Thus,
\xi\in H
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Consider
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
such that
\forall\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\forall\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\exists x\in X
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-T\xi_{i}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\|^{2}.

Fix
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
. Consider
\xi=(T\xi_{1},\ldots,T\xi_{n})\in\mathcal{H}^{\oplus n}
and let
\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n})\in\mathcal{H}^{\oplus n}
be an arbitrary vector. By assumption, there exists
x\in X
such that

\displaystyle\|f(x)-\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-T\xi_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f(x)-\eta\|^{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
,
{\xi\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{f(x):x\in X\}}}
, and therefore there exists a net
(\mu_{\alpha})
of finitely supported probability measures on
X
such that

\int_{X}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}\to\xi

in the norm topology. Let

T_{\alpha}:=\int_{X}\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{conv}\{\varphi(x):x\in X\}.

Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T_{\alpha}\xi_{i}-T\xi_{i}\|^{2}=\big\|\int_{X}f_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}-\xi\big\|^{2}\to 0.

This shows that
T\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{\varphi(x):x\in X\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the strong operator topology.

Conversely, suppose
T\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{\varphi(x):x\in X\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the strong operator topology, and let

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
. Define
f\colon X\to\mathcal{H}^{\oplus n}
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\oplus n}
as above. Then

{\xi\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{f(x):x\in X\}}},

where the closure is with respect to the norm topology, and therefore, by Lemma
2.2
, there exists an
x\in X
such that

\|f(x)-\xi\|\leq\|f(x)-\eta\|.

Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-T\xi_{i}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\varphi(x)\,\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}\|^{2}

establishing (1).
∎

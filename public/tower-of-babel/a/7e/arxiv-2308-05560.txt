Van der Corput’s difference theorem for amenable groups and the left regular representation
Sohail Farhangi

Abstract
We establish a connection between two variants of van der Corput’s Difference Theorem (vdCDT) for countably infinite amenable groups G G and the ergodic hierarchy of mixing properties of a unitary representation U U of G G . In particular, we show that one variant of vdCDT corresponds to subrepresentations of the left regular representation, and another variant of vdCDT corresponds to the absence of finite dimensional subrepresentations. We then obtain applications for measure preserving actions of countably infinite abelian groups.



Math subject classifications: 22D40, 37A30, 43A35

Keywords: Følner sequence, disjointness, ultraproduct, ergodic theorem, Lebesgue spectrum, weak mixing

1.1
Introduction

Bergelson in
[
4
]
introduced the following forms of van der Corput’s Difference Theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
a bounded sequence of vectors.

(i)

If for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n+h},x_{n}\rangle=0\text{, then }\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(1)

(ii)

If

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n+h},x_{n}\rangle\right|=0\text{, then }\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(2)

(iii)

If

\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n+h},x_{n}\rangle\right|=0\text{, then }\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(3)

In
[
18
]
the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
(i) was proven by the author. For the sake of presentation, we defer the precise definitions of spectrally Lebesgue and spectrally singular sequences to Section 3. For now, it suffices to know that constant sequences and almost periodic sequences are spectrally singular.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
a bounded sequence of vectors. If

\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n+h},x_{n}\rangle\right|^{2}<\infty,

(4)

then
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a spectrally Lebesgue sequence. In particular, if
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is bounded and spectrally singular, then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}x_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(5)

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(X,\mu)
, and
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is bounded and spectrally singular, then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}y_{n}x_{n}\right|\right|=0.

(6)

In
[
18
]
, Theorem
1.2
was used to prove various ergodic theorems for noncommuting

-actions. One of the main goals of this article is to generalize Theorem

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be a countably infinite abelian group, let
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a Følner sequence in

\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, and let
u:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a bounded map. If

\sum_{h\in G}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{g\in F_{N}}\langle u(g+h),u(g)\rangle\right|^{2}<\infty,

(7)

then

is spectrally Lebesgue. In particular, if

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{F_{N}}c(g)u(g)\right|\right|=0.

(8)

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(X,\mu)

y:G\rightarrow L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is bounded and spectrally singular, then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left|\left|\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{F_{N}}y(g)u(g)\right|\right|=0.

(9)

Remark 1.4
.

We work with non-compact groups because those are the groups that require averaging along Følner sequences. In particular, if
G
was a compact group, we would have
\lambda(G)=1
where
\lambda
is the Haar measure, so we could just average over the entire group rather than a Følner sequence. Since our
G
will be non-compact, we will always have that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|F_{n}|=\infty
for any Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Furthermore, the Peter-Weyl Theorem tells us that every strongly continuous irreducible representation of a compact group is a finite dimensional subrepresentation of the left regular representation. The applications of our generalizations of van der Corput’s Difference Theorem are based on disjointness of representations with all finite dimensional representations of
G
, or disjointness of representations from the left regular representation of
G
. If
G
is compact, then there are no representations disjoint from the finite dimensional representations, and there are no representations disjoint from the left regular representation, so our applications would be vacuous in this setting.

We restrict our attention to countable discrete groups to avoid difficulties that arise when checking that certain functions on the group are continuous in Section 3.

1.3
Background

In
[
25
]
Furstenberg gave a new ergodic theoretic proof of the celebrated Szemerédi Theorem by showing that it is a corollary of the following result, which is now known as the Furstenberg multiple recurrence theorem.

Theorem 1.5

Let

-system and

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu\left(B\cap T^{-n}B\cap T^{-2n}B\cap\cdots\cap T^{-(k-1)n}B\right)>0.

(11)

(12)

where

G

, and

. We note that the existence and the value of the limit of multiple ergodic averages is not strictly needed to deduce combinatorial corollaries, as Furstenberg only needed a single

The purpose of this section is to gather some facts about representations of groups so that we can state Theorem
2.1

\lambda

All Hilbert spaces in this paper will be separable, and it is worth noting that the assumption that the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
is standard implies that

\pi

G

\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}

\lim_{g\rightarrow e_{G}}||\pi(g)f-f||=0

g,h\in G

\pi
is a unitary representation of

on
\mathcal{H}
and
\rho

G

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

intertwinning operator
if
T\pi(g)=\rho(g)T
for all

\pi
and
\rho
is denoted by
C(\pi,\rho)
. The representations
\pi
and
\rho
are
unitarily equivalent
if

disjoint
if
C(\pi,\rho)=\{0\}
. The representations
\pi
and
\rho

if for any nontrivial subrepresentations
\pi^{\prime}
of
\pi
and
\rho^{\prime}
of
\rho

C(\pi^{\prime},\rho)\neq\{0\}
and
C(\pi,\rho^{\prime})\neq\{0\}
. The representation
\pi
is

if there is no nontrivial closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
that is invariant under the action of
\pi

if the center of

consists of scalar multiples of the identity. We let
\widehat{G}
denote the set of unitary equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of

\sigma

A Hilbert space of sequences

In this section
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathscr{H}
will always denote Hilbert spaces and

a countably infinite amenable group. Let

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the norm and inner product on

and let
||\cdot||_{\mathscr{H}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

Given a right-Følner sequence

SA(\mathcal{H},(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}):=\left\{f:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\ |\ \limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{g\in F_{N}}||f(g)||^{2}\ <\infty\right\}.

(29)

Furthermore, we denote the collection of uniformly bounded sequences by

\displaystyle UB(\mathcal{H}):=\bigg\{f:G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\ |\ \sup_{g\in G}||f(g)||<\infty\bigg\}.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in SA(\mathcal{H},(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\left|\sum_{g\in F_{N}}\langle f_{1}(g),f_{2}(g)\rangle\right|\leq\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{g\in F_{N}}||f_{1}(g)||\cdot||f_{2}(g)||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{g\in F_{N}}||f_{1}(g)||^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{N}|}\sum_{g\in F_{N}}||f_{2}(g)||^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

It follows that given any countable set
I
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in I}\subseteq SA(\mathcal{H},(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})

and

For a countable set

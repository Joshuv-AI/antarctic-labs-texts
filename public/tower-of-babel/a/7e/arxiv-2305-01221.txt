Affine Toda system of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} and 𝐂 t \mathbf{C}^{t} type: compactness and affine Weyl group Thanks: The third author is partially supported by National Key R&D Program of China 2022YFA1006800, NSFC No. 12171456 and NSFC No. 12271369.
Leilei Cui, Zhaohu Nie, Wen Yang

Abstract
. The local mass is a fundamental quantized information that characterizes the blow-up solution to the Toda system and has a profound relationship with its underlying algebraic structure. In [ 24 ] , it was observed that the associated Weyl group can be employed to represent this information for the 𝐀 n \mathbf{A}_{n} , 𝐁 n \mathbf{B}_{n} , 𝐂 n \mathbf{C}_{n} and 𝐆 2 \mathbf{G}_{2} type Toda systems. The relationship between the local mass of blow-up solution and the corresponding affine Weyl group is further explored for some affine 𝐁 \mathbf{B} type Toda systems in [ 11 ] , where the possible local masses are explicitly expressed in terms of 8 8 types. The current work presents a comprehensive study of the general affine 𝐀 \mathbf{A} and 𝐂 t \mathbf{C}^{t} type Toda systems with arbitrary rank. At each stage of the blow-up process (via scaling), we can employ certain elements (known as ”set chains”) in the corresponding affine Weyl group to measure the variation of local mass. Consequently, we obtain the a priori estimate of the affine 𝐀 \mathbf{A} and 𝐂 t \mathbf{C}^{t} type Toda systems with arbitrary number of singularities.



Keywords:

Affine Toda system, Affine Weyl group, Pohozaev identity, Blow-up analysis.

35B44, 35J61, 35R01.

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{f(\ell)}\mu_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mu_{j}\Big)+2m_{i},\ \text{for some}\ m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\ i=1,\cdots,n,

As previously mentioned, the group structure of the corresponding Lie algebra is known to play a crucial role in determining the local mass of blow-up solutions. Our findings in the current work and the result in

Additionally, the process of obtaining a clear transformation in each blow-up combination differs between the classical and affine Toda systems. In the classical case, the permutation map is used, while exhaustively exploring all possibilities is sufficient for the affine case with low rank. Nevertheless, for the affine case with arbitrary rank, neither of these methods seems to work. Instead, we must represent each transformation of the local mass by the composition of generators. Remark 2

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(-2\int_{\Omega}e^{u^{k}_{i}}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}e^{u^{k}_{i}}(x\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}S-\int_{\Omega}e^{u^{k}_{n+1}}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}x\right)

For the second term
\mathcal{P}_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u^{k}_{i}|^{2}(x\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\partial\Omega}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\nu\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}S\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{\theta}(0)}|\nabla u^{k}_{i}|^{2}(x\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\partial B_{\theta}(0)}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\nu\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}S\right)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}|\nabla u^{k}_{i}|^{2}(x\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}S\right),

. Using integration by parts, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}a^{ij}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u^{k}_{j}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}x=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}a^{ij}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u^{k}_{j}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\int_{\partial B_{\theta}(0)}\left(\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\nu\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)+\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\big(\nu\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)-(x\cdot\nu)\big(\nabla u^{k}_{i}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\right)\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\left(\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)+\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)-(x\cdot\mathbf{n})\big(\nabla u^{k}_{i}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\right)\mathrm{d}S.

Applying the same argument of
[
26
, Lemma 4.1]
, we obtain the following estimate

(2.10)

\nabla u^{k}_{i}(x)=-2\alpha_{i}x/|x|^{2}+o(1)\ \text{near the origin}.

Thus it follows from (

) that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\left(\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)+\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)-(x\cdot\mathbf{n})\big(\nabla u^{k}_{i}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{j}\big)\right)\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=-8\pi\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}+o(1)

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}|\nabla u^{k}_{i}|^{2}(x\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\big(x\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u^{k}_{i}\big)\mathrm{d}S\right)=-4\pi\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\alpha_{i}^{2}+o(1).

Since the elements of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
have fast decay on

, by the same arguments of the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
, we can choose

such that

has fast decay on
\partial B(0,R^{k}r_{k})
and

\sigma^{k}_{i}(R^{k}r_{k})=\sigma^{k}_{i}(r_{k})+o(1),\ \text{for}\ i\in I.

Letting
\theta=\sqrt{R^{k}}r_{k}
, then we have

-4\pi\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma^{k}_{i}(\theta)=-4\pi\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma^{k}_{i}(r_{k})+o(1)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum\limits_{i\in I}\theta\int_{\partial B_{\theta}(0)}e^{u^{k}_{i}}\mathrm{d}S=o(1).

Hence it holds that

(2.13)

\mathcal{P}_{3}=-4\pi\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma^{k}_{i}(r_{k})+o(1).

Similar to the estimate (
2.10
) (by
[

, Lemma 4.1]
and a scaling argument), for

, we have

\nabla u^{k}_{i}(x)=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{i}\Big)+\frac{o(1)}{|x|},~x\in\partial B_{\sqrt{R^{k}}r_{k}}(0).

Then (
2.11
) and (

(2.14)

\mathcal{P}_{1}=2\pi\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{i}\Big)\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{jt}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{j}\Big)-8\pi\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}+o(1)

and

(2.15)

\mathcal{P}_{2}=\pi\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{i}\Big)^{2}-4\pi\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\alpha_{i}^{2}+o(1).

Substituting (

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{i}\Big)\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{jt}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{j}\Big)-8\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma^{k}_{t}(r_{k})-2\alpha_{i}\Big)^{2}-4\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a^{ii}\alpha_{i}^{2}-4\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma^{k}_{i}(r_{k})=o(1).

As a consequence, by taking

It remains to simplify (

(2.17)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma_{t}\sum\limits_{s\in I}k_{js}\sigma_{s}-2\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{t\in I}k_{it}\sigma_{t}\alpha_{j}-2\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{s\in I}k_{js}\sigma_{s}\alpha_{i}-4\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}a_{js}k_{i,{n+1}}\sigma_{s}\sigma_{n+1}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}k_{i,{n+1}}k_{j,{n+1}}\sigma_{n+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{Q}_{11}+\mathcal{Q}_{12}+\mathcal{Q}_{13}+\mathcal{Q}_{14}.

By (
2.5
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{11}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j,t,s=1}^{n}a_{js}\sigma_{t}\sigma_{s}\delta_{jt}=\sum\limits_{j,s=1}^{n}a_{js}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{s}=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}^{2}-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{12}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{i,t=1}^{n}a_{it}\sigma_{t}\big(-a^{i1}-a^{in}\big)=-\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{i,t=1}^{n}a_{it}\sigma_{t}=-\sigma_{n+1}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{n}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{13}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{j,s=1}^{n}a_{js}\sigma_{s}\big(-a^{1j}-a^{nj}\big)=-\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{j,s=1}^{n}a_{js}\sigma_{s}=-\sigma_{n+1}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{n}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{14}

\displaystyle=\big(a^{11}+a^{1n}+a^{n1}+a^{nn}\big)\sigma_{n+1}^{2}=2\sigma_{n+1}^{2}.

Hence

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}^{2}-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}-2\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n+1}-2\sigma_{n+1}\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})^{2}+(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})^{2}+\cdots+(\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{n+1})^{2}+(\sigma_{n+1}-\sigma_{1})^{2}.

Furthermore, using (
2.5
) again we find that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{n}a_{it}\sigma_{t}\alpha_{j}+2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}k_{i,{n+1}}\sigma_{n+1}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{j,t=1}^{n}\sigma_{t}\alpha_{j}\delta_{jt}+2\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\big(-a^{1j}-a^{nj}\big)=2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{j}-2\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=2\sum\limits_{j\in I}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{j}

and

(2.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{3}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}a_{js}\sigma_{s}\alpha_{i}+2\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}k_{j,{n+1}}\sigma_{n+1}\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{i,s=1}^{n}\sigma_{s}\alpha_{i}\delta_{is}+2\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big(-a^{i1}-a^{in}\big)=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\sigma_{i}-2\sigma_{n+1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=2\sum\limits_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\sigma_{i}.

Therefore, substituting (
2.18
), (
2.19
) and (
2.20

2.17

(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})^{2}+(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})^{2}+\cdots+(\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{n+1})^{2}+(\sigma_{n+1}-\sigma_{1})^{2}=4\sum\limits_{i\in I}\mu_{i}\sigma_{i},

which is equivalent to (
2.7
). One can directly check that (
2.1
) is equivalent to (
2.7

2.5

Proposition 3.1

Definition 3.2

(
[

)

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n+1})\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})

We prove this proposition by induction. By the definitions of
\mathfrak{R}_{i}
’s (

1
-st level. Suppose that the conclusion holds for all

in the
M
-th level (

\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}
,
i\in I
. Assuming that

, where

\delta_{{i_{0}}j}-\sum\limits_{s\in I}k_{i_{0},s}n_{s,j}+n_{i_{0},j}\geq 0,\ \forall\ j\in I.

Here we set
n_{ij}
and

by
n_{i,j}
and
k_{i,j}

We substitute the expression of
\bm{\sigma}
into (
2.1

for each

(3.2)

\sum\limits_{i\in I}\left(n_{i,{j}}-n_{i+1,{j}}\right)^{2}=2n_{{j},{j}}.

For (

as a solution of the quadratic equation

(3.3)

2x^{2}-2\big(1+n_{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{i_{0}}}\big)x+n_{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}}^{2}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{i_{0}}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\in I\setminus\{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}\}}\big(n_{i,{i_{0}}}-n_{i+1,{i_{0}}}\big)^{2}=0.

Then it is easy to see the other solution of (

1+n_{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}}-n_{{i_{0}},{i_{0}}}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{i_{0}}}\geq 0.

Similarly, for (
3.2
), we can also regard
n_{{i_{0}},j}
,

2x^{2}-2\big(n_{{i_{0}}-1,{j}}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{j}}\big)x+n_{{i_{0}}-1,{j}}^{2}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{j}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\in I\setminus\{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}\}}\big(n_{i,{j}}-n_{i+1,{j}}\big)^{2}-2n_{jj}=0.

Proof of case (a)

\displaystyle n_{{i_{0}}-1,{j}}^{2}+n_{{i_{0}}+1,{j}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\in I\setminus\{{i_{0}}-1,{i_{0}}\}}\big(n_{i,{j}}-n_{i+1,{j}}\big)^{2}-2n_{jj}

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
be defined as

\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})=\left\{\bm{\sigma}\ \big|\ \bm{\sigma}\ \text{satisfies the Pohozaev identity \eqref{Affine-Toda-Section-2-Eq-6-Pohozaev}},\ \sigma_{i}=2\sum\limits_{j\in I}n_{ij}\mu_{j},\ n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\ i\in I\right\}.

\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu}).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.3
, we find that
\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
. Thus, it remains to show
\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
. For any
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n+1})\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, by Proposition
3.1
, each
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}
,
i\in I,
satisfies the Pohozaev identity (
2.7
). We claim that

(3.8)

\text{if}\ \bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu}),\ \text{then}\ \mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu}),\ \text{for each}\ i\in I.

In fact, for any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, we suppose that
{\sigma}_{i}=2\sum_{j\in I}n_{ij}\mu_{j}
, where
n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
i,j\in I
. By the same arguments of Proposition
3.3
, we obtain that

\delta_{{i}j}-\sum\limits_{s\in I}k_{is}n_{sj}+n_{ij}\geq 0,\ \forall\ i,j\in I,

Thus (
3.8
) is proved.

Then by the same arguments of
[
24
, Proposition 3.4]
, we define a partial order
\preceq
in
\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, i.e.,
\bm{\sigma}_{1}\preceq\bm{\sigma}_{2}

for each

. For any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})

\bm{\sigma}_{1},\bm{\sigma}_{2}\in\Gamma_{N}^{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})

\text{either}\ \Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{1}}^{\mathbf{A}}=\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{2}}^{\mathbf{A}}\ \text{or}\ \Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{1}}^{\mathbf{A}}\cap\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{2}}^{\mathbf{A}}=\emptyset.

One can easily deduce that

3.2.
The group representation

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n+1})\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, denote by
\mu_{s}^{*}=\mu_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in I}k_{st}\sigma_{t}
for

consists of consecutive indices for some
j\in I
and
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
\bm{\sigma}^{*}=\left(\sigma^{*}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{*}_{n+1}\right)
be defined as

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{s}^{*}&=\sigma_{s},\ &&\text{for}\ s\in I\setminus J,\\
\\
\sigma_{s}^{*}&=\sigma_{s}+2\sum\limits_{t\in J}k^{st}\big(\mu_{t}^{*}+\mu_{t^{*}}^{*}\big),\ &&\text{for}\ s\in J,\end{aligned}\right.

where
t^{*}=2j+|J|-t-1
and
(k^{st})_{|J|\times|J|}
stands for the inverse of
(k_{st})_{|J|\times|J|}
,
s,t\in J
. Then
\bm{\sigma}^{*}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
.

In order to prove Lemma
3.5
, we shall identify an element
\mathfrak{R}_{J}\in\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{A}}
such that
\bm{\sigma}^{*}=\mathfrak{R}_{J}\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
. To achieve this, we introduce the concept of a
set chain
.

Definition 3.6
.

Suppose that
J\subsetneqq I
consists of consecutive indices, denoted by
J=\{j,j+1,\cdots,j+l\}
for some
j\in I
and
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We define the
J

Remark 7
.

We can apply Lemma

to address the blow-up case

, as seen in Proposition

-(4). However, when dealing with the blow-up case

in Proposition

-(4), we need additional techniques as outlined below.

Definition 3.7
.

(a)

A permutation map
f
on the index set (tuple)
I
is said to be an
r^{+}
-permutation
if there exists an element
r\in I
with
r\geq 2
such that

f:(1,2,\cdots,r-1,r,r+1,\cdots,n+1)\rightarrow(r,r+1,\cdots,n+1,1,2,\cdots,r-1).

In other words, we can view
f
as a bijective map on
I
satisfying that

(3.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(1)=r\\
&f(2)=r+1\\
&\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
&f(n-r+2)=n+1\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(n-r+3)=1\\
&f(n-r+4)=2\\
&\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
&f(n+1)=r-1\end{aligned}\right.\quad.

(b)

A permutation map
f
on the index set (tuple)
I
is said to be an
r^{-}
-permutation
if there exists an element
r\in I
with
r\leq n
such that

f:(1,2,\cdots,r,r+1,r+2,\cdots,n+1)\rightarrow(r+1,r+2\cdots,n+1,1,2,\cdots,r).

We can explicitly express
f
as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(1)=r+1\\
&f(2)=r+2\\
&\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
&f(n-r+1)=n+1\end{aligned}\right.\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left\{\begin{aligned} &f(n-r+2)=1\\
&f(n-r+3)=2\\
&\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
&f(n+1)=r\end{aligned}\right.\quad.

In other words, an
r^{-}
-permutation is an
(r+1)^{+}
-permutation for
r\in I\setminus\{n+1\}
.

Remark 8
.

(a)

The
1^{+}
-permutation and
(n+1)^{-}
-permutation are exactly the identity.

(b)

Any
r^{+}
-permutation is the

-permutation for
r\in I\setminus\{1\}
.

For the

-permutation, we provide some properties for later application.

\bm{\sigma}_{f}=\left(\sigma_{f,1},\cdots,\sigma_{f,n+1}\right)\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, where
\sigma_{f,i}=\sigma_{f(i)}
for

. Denote by

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{J_{p}}=f^{-1}(J_{p})=\{\overline{i}_{p},\overline{i}_{p}+1,\cdots,\overline{i}_{p}+l_{p}\},\ \text{where}\ \overline{i}_{p}=i_{p}+(n-r_{2}+2),\ p=1,\cdots,\vartheta,\\

&\overline{N}=f^{-1}(N)=\{s+(n-r_{2}+2)\mid s\in N\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then it is easy to see that
I=\overline{J_{0}}\cup\overline{J_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{J_{\vartheta}}\cup\overline{N}
and each
\overline{J_{p}}
,

consists of the maximal consecutive indices. We define
\left(\bm{\sigma}_{f}\right)^{*}=\left((\sigma_{f,1})^{*},(\sigma_{f,2})^{*},\cdots,(\sigma_{f,n+1})^{*}\right)
with

(\sigma_{f,i})^{*}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{f,i},\ &&\text{for}\ i\in\overline{N},\\
&\sigma_{f,i}+2\sum\limits_{j\in\overline{J_{p}}}k_{p}^{f(i)f(j)}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j}+\overline{\mu}_{\overline{j}_{p}^{*}}\big),\ &&\text{for}\ i\in\overline{J_{p}},\ p=0,1,\cdots,\vartheta,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\overline{j}_{p}^{*}=2\overline{i}_{p}+|\overline{J_{p}}|-j-1
, and
(k_{p}^{f(i)f(j)})_{|\overline{J_{p}}|\times|\overline{J_{p}}|}
is the inverse of

,

.

Step 2.
For any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu})
, we denote by

for

\sigma_{j},\ &\text{if}\ j\neq i.\end{cases}

where

is the corresponding
\overline{J_{p}}
-chain with respect to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{i}
,

and

Hence we conclude that

Noticing that for each

\sigma_{f,s}=\sigma_{f(s)}=(\mathfrak{R}_{f(t)}\bm{\sigma})_{f(s)},\ &\text{if}\ s\neq t,\end{cases}

Step 3.
Let
\bm{\sigma}^{*}=(\sigma^{*}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{*}_{n+1})
be defined as in (

) and denote by

. First we claim that

. In fact, for any

, i.e.,

, then we set

and obtain that

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}_{f(t)}=\sigma^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}+2\sum\limits_{j\in J_{p}}k_{p}^{ij}\big(\mu_{j}^{*}+\mu_{j_{p}^{*}}^{*}\big),\ \ \ j_{p}^{*}=2i_{p}+|J_{p}|-j-1,

\displaystyle=\sigma_{f(t)}+2\sum\limits_{s\in\overline{J_{p}}}k_{p}^{f(t)f(s)}\big(\mu_{f(s)}^{*}+\mu_{f(\overline{s}_{p}^{*})}^{*}\big),\ \ \ \overline{s}_{p}^{*}=2\overline{i}_{p}+|\overline{J_{p}}|-s-1,

\displaystyle=\sigma_{f,t}+2\sum\limits_{s\in\overline{J_{p}}}k_{p}^{f(t)f(s)}\big(\overline{\mu}_{s}+\overline{\mu}_{\overline{s}_{p}^{*}}\big),\ \ \ \overline{s}_{p}^{*}=2\overline{i}_{p}+|\overline{J_{p}}|-s-1.

, i.e.,

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s_{i}}+2\sum\limits_{j\in\{1,2,\cdots,|J_{0}|\}}k_{0}^{s_{i}s_{j}}\left(\mu_{s_{j}}^{*}+\mu_{s_{j}^{*}}^{*}\right),\ \ \ s_{j}^{*}=s_{1+|J_{0}|-j},

\displaystyle=\sigma_{f,t}+2\sum\limits_{s\in\overline{J_{0}}}k_{0}^{f(t)f(s)}\left(\overline{\mu}_{s}+\overline{\mu}_{\overline{s}_{0}^{*}}\right),\ \ \ \overline{s}_{0}^{*}=1+|\overline{J_{0}}|-s.

By the above discussion, we get that
\left(\bm{\sigma}^{*}\right)_{f}=\left(\bm{\sigma}_{f}\right)^{*}
. As a consequence,

\left(\bm{\sigma}^{*}\right)_{f}=\left(\bm{\sigma}_{f}\right)^{*}=\left(\mathfrak{R}_{J_{0}}\mathfrak{R}_{J_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{R}_{J_{\vartheta}}\bm{\sigma}\right)_{f}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(\bm{\mu}).

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will present the proof for Theorem
1.1
, which involves utilizing a selection process to identify the ”bad” points and their corresponding bubbling disks. We then compute the local mass contributions of each bubbling disk containing the ”bad” points and analyze the transformations that occur at various stages. This approach has been successfully applied to the classical Toda systems, including types
\mathbf{A}_{n}
,
\mathbf{B}_{n}
,
\mathbf{C}_{n}
,
\mathbf{G}_{2}
and
\mathbf{B}_{2}^{(1)}
, as detailed in
[
11
,
20
,
22
,
24
]
. Here, we will outline the proof of the entire process. First, we identify all the bubbling disks as described in section
2
. Then, we begin with one bubbling disk and compute all possible contributions of local mass from each disk before it meets the nearest bubbling disk. Next, we collect the adjacent bubbling disks into a ”group”, a concept introduced in
[
22
]
, and calculate the types of local mass in the group. The algebraic structure plays an essential role in determining the principles of the transformations that occur in the group.

Before sketching out the proof, we make some preparations. Throughout this section, for any sequence
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})=\{(x^{k},s_{k})\}
, we always denote by

\sigma^{k}_{i}(B(x^{k},s_{k}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(x^{k},s_{k})}e^{u^{k}_{i}(x)}\mathrm{d}x,\ \text{for}\ i\in I.

We define the local mass
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}(B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}))=(\hat{\sigma}_{1}(B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})),\cdots,\hat{\sigma}_{n+1}(B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})))
as

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}))=\begin{cases}\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma^{k}_{i}(B(x^{k},s_{k})),\ &\text{if}\ u^{k}_{i}\ \text{has fast decay on}\ \partial B(x^{k},s_{k}),\\
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma^{k}_{i}(B(x^{k},rs_{k})),\ &\text{if}\ u^{k}_{i}\ \text{has slow decay on}\ \partial B(x^{k},s_{k}).\end{cases}

For simplicity we set
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(\mathbf{s})=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\bm{0},\mathbf{s}))
if
x^{k}=0
. We will calculate all possible values of
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(\mathbf{s})
as
\mathbf{s}
increases, such that the disk
B(\bm{0},\mathbf{s})
includes all the ”bad” points. The argument is divided into the following five steps:

Step 1.
In this step, we shall compute the energy of the blow-up solutions in each bubbling disk
B(x_{t}^{k},l_{t}^{k})
,
t=1,\cdots,m
, as introduced in Proposition
2.2
. For any fixed
x^{k}_{t}\in\Sigma_{k}\setminus\{0\}
,
l^{k}_{t}\ll\tau^{k}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x^{k}_{t},\Sigma_{k}\setminus\{x^{k}_{t}\})
. Applying Proposition
2.2
-(1), the Harnack-type inequality holds

u^{k}_{i}(x)+2\log|x-x^{k}_{t}|\leq C,\ |x-x^{k}_{t}|\leq\tau^{k}_{t},\ \forall\ i\in I.

In
B(x^{k}_{t},l_{t}^{k})
, we perform the scaling on
\mathbf{u}^{k}
as

v^{k}_{i}(y):=u^{k}_{i}(x^{k}_{t}+\varepsilon_{t}^{k}y)+2\log\varepsilon_{t}^{k},\ \text{for}\ i\in I.

Then by Proposition
2.2
-(4),
\mathbf{u}^{k}
has fast decay on
\partial B(x^{k}_{t},l^{k}_{t})
and one of the following alternatives holds:

(I).
the index set
I
is decomposed into the form
I=J\cup N
and
J=J_{1}\cup\cdots\cup J_{\vartheta}
for some
\vartheta\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying (I-1)–(I-3).

(II).
the index set
I
is decomposed into the form
I=J\cup N
and
J=J_{0}\cup J_{1}\cup\cdots\cup J_{\vartheta}
for some
\vartheta\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
satisfying (II-1)–(II-4).

In any case, by the classification result
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
, we obtain that there exists a sequence
R_{k}
with
R_{k}\rightarrow+\infty
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
such that
l_{t}^{k}=R_{k}\varepsilon^{k}_{t}\ll\tau_{t}^{k}
and

\int_{B(0,{R_{k}})}e^{v_{i}(y)}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v_{i}(y)}\mathrm{d}y+o(1),\ \text{for}\ i\in J.

One can easily conclude that
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))=0
for
i\in N
and

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v_{i}(y)}\mathrm{d}y,\ \text{for}\ i\in J,

where
(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t})
stands for the sequence
\{(x^{k}_{t},l^{k}_{t})\}
. By Theorem
3.9
-(a) (or Lemma
3.5
), we deduce that

\hat{\bm{\sigma}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))=\left(\hat{\sigma}_{1}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t})),\cdots,\hat{\sigma}_{n+1}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))\right)\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(1,\cdots,1).

Step 2.
Next, we analyze the variation in energy as we transition from
B(x^{k}_{t},l^{k}_{t})
to
B(x^{k}_{t},\tau^{k}_{t})
. If

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t})),\ \forall\ i\in I,

where
(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t})
stands for the sequence
\{(x^{k}_{t},\tau^{k}_{t})\}
, then we conclude that

\hat{\bm{\sigma}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))=\left(\hat{\sigma}_{1}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t})),\cdots,\hat{\sigma}_{n+1}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))\right)\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}}(1,\cdots,1),

as required. Otherwise, there exists some
i_{0}\in I
such that

\hat{\sigma}_{i_{0}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))>\hat{\sigma}_{i_{0}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t})).

By the same arguments of
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
, we can choose a sequence
\{s_{k}\}
,
l_{t}^{k}\ll s_{k}\ll\tau^{k}_{t}
such that

(i)

some components of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
have slow decay on
\partial B(x^{k}_{t},s_{k})
,

(ii)

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{s}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))
for
i\in I
, where
(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{s})
stands for the sequence
\{(x^{k}_{t},s_{k})\}
.

We scale
\mathbf{u}^{k}
by
v^{k}_{i}(y)=u^{k}_{i}(x_{t}^{k}+s_{k}y)+2\log{s_{k}}
for
i\in I
and denote by

J=\big\{i\in I\mid u^{k}_{i}\ \text{has slow decay on}\ \partial B(x_{t}^{k},s_{k})\big\}\subsetneqq I.

Then, each
v^{k}_{i}
,
i\in I\setminus J
has fast decay on
\partial B(x_{t}^{k},s_{k})
, i.e.,
v^{k}_{i}(y)\rightarrow-\infty
in

and

for

satisfies the system

\Delta v_{i}(y)+\sum\limits_{j\in J}k_{ij}e^{v_{j}(y)}=4\pi\alpha_{i}^{*}\delta_{0}\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v_{i}(y)}\mathrm{d}y<+\infty,\ \ \forall\ i\in J.

Here,
\alpha_{i}^{*}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in I}k_{ij}\hat{\sigma}_{j}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))>-1
for
i\in J
. For the index set

, we shall encounter the same two alternatives
(I)
and
(II)
as in
Step 1
. In any case, we deduce that there exists a sequence
N_{k}^{*}
with
N_{k}^{*}\rightarrow+\infty
as

such that

,
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{N^{*}s}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))
for

and

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{N^{*}s}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{l}_{t}))+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v_{i}(y)}\mathrm{d}y,\ \text{for}\ i\in J,

where

stands for the sequence
\{x_{t}^{k},N_{k}^{*}s_{k}\}
. Thus we get from Theorem
3.9
that

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{N^{*}s}))\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\ \text{for}\ i\in I.

Let
s_{k,1}=N_{k}^{*}s_{k}
. If
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{1}))
for

, where
\mathbf{s}_{1}
stands for the sequence

(some finite number) steps. Hence
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))=\hat{\bm{\sigma}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}}))
with
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{t},\bm{\tau}_{t}))\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for each

, where
\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}}
stands for the sequence

.

Next, we gather the adjacent disks into a ”group” and calculate the local mass of this group. As we have did in
[
11
]
,

can be decomposed into a disjoint union of

forms a group comprising of relatively close elements in

Then
\tau_{j,l}^{k}\ll\tau_{S^{k}_{j}}^{k}
for

. By
Step 2
, the local mass
\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,l},\bm{\tau}_{j,l}))=2m_{j,l,i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for
i\in I
, where
(\mathbf{x}_{j,l},\bm{\tau}_{j,l})
stands for the sequence of pair
\{(x^{k}_{j,l},\tau^{k}_{j,l})\}
for

.

Let
\sigma_{i}=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\bm{\tau}_{S^{k}_{j}}))
, one can show that
\sigma_{i}\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for
i\in I
. In fact, by the same arguments of Proposition
[
11

, we would come across one of the following cases:

Case (1).

\mathbf{u}^{k}
has fast decay on

Then direct computation shows that
\sigma^{k}_{i}(B(x^{k}_{j,1},l_{j}^{k}(S^{k}_{j})))=2\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}}m_{j,l,i}+o(1)
for

. If

where
(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\mathbf{l}_{j}(S^{k}_{j}))
and
(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\bm{\tau}_{S^{k}_{j}})
stand for the sequences
\{(x^{k}_{j,1},l_{j}^{k}(S^{k}_{j}))\}
and

\hat{\sigma}_{i_{0}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\bm{\tau}_{S^{k}_{j}}))>\hat{\sigma}_{i_{0}}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\mathbf{l}_{j}(S^{k}_{j}))).

Then we can apply the same arguments of
Step 2
to prove that

\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\bm{\tau}_{S^{k}_{j}}))=\hat{\sigma}_{i}(B(\mathbf{x}_{j,1},\mathbf{s}_{\ell}))\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\ \forall\ i\in I,

for some sequence
\mathbf{s}_{\ell}=\{s^{k}_{\ell}\}
satisfying that
l_{j}^{k}(S^{k}_{j})\ll s^{k}_{\ell}\ll\tau^{k}_{S^{k}_{j}}
.

Case (2).
Some components of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
have slow decay on
\partial B(x^{k}_{j,1},\tau^{k}_{j,1})
. We denote by

J=\big\{i\in I\mid u^{k}_{i}\ \text{has slow decay on}\ \partial B(x^{k}_{j,1},\tau^{k}_{j,1})\big\}.

Since
l_{j}^{k}(S^{k}_{j})\thicksim\tau^{k}_{j,l}
for

,

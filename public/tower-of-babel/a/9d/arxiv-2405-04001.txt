Abstract
. For three dimensional complete, non-compact Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature and uniformly positive scalar curvature, we obtain the sharp linear volume growth ratio and the corresponding rigidity. Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C21, 53C23, 53C24



1.
Introduction

From the celebrated Bishop-Gromov Volume Comparison Theorem, it is well-known that for any complete non-compact Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with Ricci curvature
Rc\geq 0
, the
n
-dim
asymptotic volume growth ratio
of
M^{n}
, denoted as

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M^{n},n}\vcentcolon=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}},

is well-defined and is independent of the choice of
p
; where
B_{p}(r)
is the open geodesic ball centered at
p\in M^{n}
with radius
r>0
,
V(B_{p}(r))
is the volume of
B_{p}(r)
, and
\omega_{n}
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore
\mathrm{V}_{M^{n},n}\in[0,1]
, and
\mathrm{V}_{M^{n},n}=1
if and only if the manifold
M^{n}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the rigidity part of the Bishop-Gromov’s volume comparison Theorem.

Furthermore, uniformly positive scalar curvature
R
possibly controls the volume growth better. Gromov
[
7
, (5’)]
made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(Volume conjecture)
.

There is a universal constant
C(n)>0
, such that if
(M^{n},g)
has
Rc\geq 0
and the scalar curvature
R\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{p\in M^{n}\atop r>0}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{r^{n-2}}\leq C(n).

Remark 1.2
.

Note Gromov
[
7
, 2.B.]
also sketched a proof of the following result: If
(M^{n},g)
has the sectional curvature
K\geq 0
and the scalar curvature
R\geq 1
, then there exists some
C(n)>0
such that
\displaystyle V(B_{p}(r))\leq C(n)\cdot r^{n-2}
.

There is an asymptotic version of Gromov’s volume conjecture as follows:

Conjecture 1.3

(Volume growth conjecture)
.

There is a universal constant
C(n)>0
, such that if
(M^{n},g)
has
Rc\geq 0
and
R\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{r^{n-2}}\leq C(n),\quad\quad\quad\forall p\in M^{n}.

Following Gromov’s strategy and combining Cheeger-Colding’s theory of Ricci limit spaces from
[
3
]
, Anderson-Cheeger’s compactness theorem in
[
1
]
, and Gromov’s spherical Lipschitz bound theorem in
[
9
]
, Zhu
[
19
]
succeeded in proving: If
(M^{n},g)
has
Rc\geq 0,R\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\inf_{q\in M^{n}}V(B_{q}(1))\geq\varepsilon>0
, then
\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{r^{n-2}}<\infty
for any
p\in M^{n}
.

Using the property of some suitable positive harmonic function on
(M^{3},g)
, Conjecture
1.3
for
n=3
is firstly proved by Munteanu-Wang
[
12
]
as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

There is a universal constant
C>0
, such that if
(M^{3},g)
has
Rc\geq 0
and
R\geq 2
, then
\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{r}\leq C
for any
p\in M^{3}
.

Later, Chodosh-Li-Stryker
[
5
]
gave another proof of the volume growth conjecture for
n=3
by using Cheeger-Colding’s almost splitting theorem
[
2
]
and
\mu
-bubble’s diameter estimate.

Motivated to get a generalization of the Cohn-Vossen’s inequality in higher dimension, Yau has posed the following question
[
18
, Problem 9]
: For any complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
, and for any
p\in M^{n}
, is it true that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r^{n-2}}<\infty?

Note that a positive answer to Yau’s question implies Conjecture
1.3
.

Shi and Yau
[
15
]
verified Yau’s conjecture on Kähler manifolds with additional assumption. If there is
p\in(M^{n},g)
such that the corresponding exponential map
\exp_{p}:T_{p}M^{n}\rightarrow M^{n}
is a diffeomorphism, we call
(M^{n},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole. For
3
-dim Riemannian manifold
M^{3}
with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
, the third named author
[
16
]
shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r}=8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},3}).

This formula shows that general
3
-dim complete, noncompact manifolds with
Rc\geq 0
possibly has some sharp asymptotic relationship between scalar curvature and volume growth. In this paper, we address this sharp asymptotic relationship reflected in Conjecture
1.3
.

Similar to the concept of asymptotic volume ratio, for complete non-compact Riemannian manifolds
(M^{n},g)
with
n\geq 3
, we define the
(n-2)
-dim asymptotic volume growth ratio of
M^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M^{n},n-2}\vcentcolon=\varlimsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{\omega_{n-2}r^{n-2}},\quad\quad\quad\forall p\in M^{n}.

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{V}_{M^{n},n-2}
is well-defined and is independent of the choice of
p
.

In this paper, we prove the sharp version of Conjecture
1.3
for
n=3
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any complete non-compact Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
and
R\geq 2
,

(1)

. if
(M^{3},g)
has only one end, then
\displaystyle 0<\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}\leq 2\pi
,

(2)

. if
(M^{3},g)
has at least two ends, then
(M^{3},g)
is isometric to a cylinder
S\times\mathbb{R}
with
S
being a closed surface of sectional curvature at least one, and
\displaystyle 0<\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}\leq 4\pi
.

(3)

. moreover
\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}=4\pi
if and only if
(M^{3},g)
is isometric to the cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is the unit round sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Meanwhile, for any
0<b<1
, we construct distinct noncompact manifolds
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i})
with
Rc\geq 0
and
R\geq 2
, where
i=1,2
, such that
\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M_{i}^{3},1}=4\pi b
. See Remark
6.2
and Remark
6.3
for details.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
follows the strategy of
[
5
]
closely.

From Cheeger-Colding’s almost splitting theorem, the
L
-neighborhood (where
L>0
) of some part of a ray (which is
[(k-5)\cdot L,(k+5)\cdot L]
away from
p
, where
k>>1
), is
\delta
-Gromov-Hausdorff close to, the product of an interval with a length space (a cylindrical region); where
\delta>0
goes to
0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, from theory of
\mu
-bubble surfaces, we can get the area upper bound of the
\mu
-bubble surfaces in the above almost cylindrical region in term of
L
.

Remark 1.7
.

There are two key observations in this paper:

(A)

. The area of
\mu
-bubble surfaces is bounded by the area of unit round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, as
L\rightarrow\infty
. This bound is sharp in asymptotic sense, where asymptotic means
L\rightarrow\infty
.

(B)

. The area of the intersection of geodesic spheres with the neighborhood of the ray, has an upper bound in term of the area bound of corresponding
\mu
-bubble surfaces.

When computing
\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}
, we take
r=k\cdot L
for fixed
L
, and express the upper bound of
\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}
as a limit in terms of
\delta,L
with respect to
k\rightarrow\infty
(see (
6.4
)). Finally we let
L\rightarrow\infty,\delta\rightarrow 0
to obtain the sharp linear volume growth ratio.

2.
The
\mu
-bubble surfaces and diameter estimate

The soap bubble was firstly proposed by Schoen to Gromov
[
8
, Section
5\frac{5}{6}
]
. We recall its definition as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Assume
N
is a smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial N=\partial_{-}N\cup\partial_{+}N
, where
\partial_{-}N\cap\partial_{+}N=\emptyset
and
\partial_{\pm}N\neq\emptyset
. Fix a smooth function
u>0
on
N
and a smooth function
h
on
\mathring{N}
with
h\rightarrow\pm\infty
on
\partial_{\pm}N
, where
\mathring{N}
is the interior of
N
. Choose a Caccioppoli set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq N
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{C}-\partial_{+}N\ \text{is smooth},\quad\quad\partial\mathcal{C}-\partial_{+}N\subseteq\mathring{N}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\partial_{+}N\subseteq\partial\mathcal{C}.

Define
\mathscr{C}(N,\mathcal{C})
as the set of all Caccioppoli sets
\Omega\subseteq N
with
\Omega\triangle\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathring{N}
.

We define
\mathcal{A}:\mathscr{C}(N,\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(W)=\int_{\partial^{*}W-\partial N}ud\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{N}(\chi_{W}-\chi_{\mathcal{C}})\cdot h\cdot ud\mathcal{H}^{n},\quad\quad\quad\forall W\in\mathscr{C}(N,\mathcal{C}),

where
\partial^{*}W
is the reduced boundary of
W
.

If
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=\inf_{W\in\mathscr{C}(N,\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{A}(W)
for some
\Omega\in\mathscr{C}(N,\mathcal{C})
, we call
\Omega
as a
\mu
-bubble with respect to
(h,u,\mathcal{C})
in
(N,\partial_{-}N,\partial_{+}N)
(See Figure
5
).

For any
\mu
-bubble
\Omega
with respect to
(h,u,\mathcal{C})
in
(N,\partial_{-}N,\partial_{+}N)
, we call any connected component of
\partial\Omega-\partial N
as a
\mu
-bubble hypersurface with respect to
(h,u,\mathcal{C})
in
(N,\partial_{-}N,\partial_{+}N)
. When
\partial\Omega-\partial N
is two dimensional, we also call its connected components as
\mu
-bubble surfaces.

Proposition 2.2

We use the second variation formula for
\mu
-bubble hypersurface
\Sigma
repeatedly (see
[

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\Sigma
is a
\mu
-bubble hypersurface with respect to
(h,u,\mathcal{C})
in
(N,\partial_{-}N,\partial_{+}N)
, where
N
is
n
-dim Riemannian manifold with
n\geq 3
; then for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}(R_{N}-R_{\Sigma}+|\mathring{A}_{\Sigma}|^{2})\cdot\psi^{2}u+(\Delta_{N}u-\Delta_{\Sigma}u)\psi^{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{N}u,\vec{n}\rangle^{2}\psi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\{h^{2}+2\langle\nabla_{N}h,\vec{n}\rangle\}\psi^{2}u\geq 0;

where
\mathring{A}_{\Sigma}
is the traceless part of second fundamental form of
\Sigma\subseteq N
,
\vec{n}
is the unit normal vector of
\Sigma\subseteq N
, and
R_{N},R_{\Sigma}
are the intrinsic scalar curvature of
N
and
\Sigma
respectively.

∎

For later height estimates of cylindrical region, we need the diameter bound of
\mu
-bubble surfaces. Similar diameter bound is given in
[
6
, Lemma
16
]
. Our method follows closely
[
13
]
(also see
[
14
]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

For compact
(\Sigma^{2},g)
, if there is a smooth function
u>0
such that

\displaystyle-\Delta u\geq(K-K_{\Sigma})u,

for some constant
K>0
, then
\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3K}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The sharp upper bound of
\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)
in the above lemma is unknown. Comparing the well-known Bonnet-Myers Theorem, we can view Lemma
2.4
as a type of conformal Bonnet-Myers Theorem for surfaces.

Proof:

Step (1)
. Assume
\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)=d_{g}(p,q)
, where
p,q\in\Sigma
. Define
I[\gamma]
by

\displaystyle I[\gamma]=\int_{\gamma}uds,

where
ds
is arclength along
\gamma
and
\gamma
is any curves from
p
to
q
. Let
\gamma_{0}
be one minimizer of
I[\cdot]
.

As in the proof of
[
13
, Proposition
1
]
(also see
[
11
, Proposition
2.2
]
), let
V(s)=f(s)\cdot v(s)
be the variation vector field along
\gamma(s)
, where
v(s)
is the unit normal vector field along
\gamma
. The first variation formula yields

(2.3)

\displaystyle u(\gamma_{0}(s))\nabla_{\partial s}\partial s-(\nabla u)^{\perp}=0,\quad\text{along}\ \ \gamma_{0}.

The non-negativity of the second variation of
I
at
\gamma_{0}
gives

(2.4)

\displaystyle 0\leq\delta^{2}I[\gamma_{0}]=\int_{\gamma_{0}}[D^{2}u(v,v)-2u\cdot H^{2}]\cdot f^{2}+u\cdot(|f^{\prime}|^{2}-K_{\Sigma}\cdot f^{2})ds,

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\gamma\subseteq\Sigma
.

Note
\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u=D^{2}u(v,v)+D^{2}u(\partial s,\partial s)
, combining (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u

\displaystyle=D^{2}u(v,v)+D^{2}u(\partial s,\partial s)=D^{2}u(v,v)+u^{\prime\prime}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\partial s}\partial s}u

(2.5)

\displaystyle=D^{2}u(v,v)+u^{\prime\prime}-H^{2}\cdot u,

where
u^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\frac{d}{ds}u(\gamma_{0}(s))
.

Plugging (
2.5
) into (
2.4
), using the equation of
u
in the assumption, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}[\Delta_{\Sigma}u-u^{\prime\prime}(s)-u\cdot H^{2}]\cdot f^{2}+u\cdot(|f^{\prime}|^{2}-K_{\Sigma}\cdot f^{2})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}-u^{\prime\prime}(s)\cdot f^{2}+u\cdot(|f^{\prime}|^{2}-Kf^{2})ds.

Assume
l=\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{0})
, then integration by parts yields

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l}u(f^{\prime})^{2}+2fu^{\prime}f^{\prime}-Kuf^{2}ds\geq 0,\quad\quad\forall f\in C_{0}^{\infty}[0,l].

Step (2)
. Put
f(s)=u^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\gamma_{0}(s))\sin(\frac{\pi}{l}s)
into (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{l}-\frac{3}{4}[(\ln u)^{\prime}]^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)+\frac{\pi}{l}(\ln u)^{\prime}\sin(\frac{\pi}{l}s)\cdot\cos(\frac{\pi}{l}s)ds

(2.7)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{l}\left(K\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)-(\frac{\pi}{l})^{2}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)\right)ds.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}[(\ln u)^{\prime}]^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)+\frac{\pi}{l}(\ln u)^{\prime}\sin(\frac{\pi}{l}s)\cdot\cos(\frac{\pi}{l}s)\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot(\frac{\pi}{l})^{2}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s).

Plugging (
2.8
) into (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\cdot(\frac{\pi}{l})^{2}\int_{0}^{l}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)ds\geq K\int_{0}^{l}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)ds.

This implies
l\leq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3K}}
.

∎

3.
The separating
\mu
-bubble surfaces

Definition 3.1
.

If a smooth compact hypersurface
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq E_{k}-\overline{E_{k+1}}
satisfies the following property: “for any continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{E_{k}}-E_{k+1}
with
\gamma(0)\in\partial E_{k},\gamma(1)\in\partial E_{k+1}
, we have
\gamma\cap\Sigma_{k}\neq\emptyset
”, then we say that
\Sigma_{k}
separates
\partial E_{k}
from
\partial E_{k+1}
.

In the applications of
\mu
-bubble surface, we require that it satisfies the above separating property. The following proposition is important to obtain such separating property.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold with only one end and is simply connected, then there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, such that
\overline{E_{k}}-E_{k+1}
and
\partial E_{k}
are both connected for
k\geq k_{0}
.

Proof:
See
[
4
, Proposition
3.2
]
.

∎

Now we present the main result of this section, which provides us the separating
\mu

E_{k}-\overline{E_{k+1}}
when
k

L\rightarrow\infty
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
(M^{3},g)
is a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
R\geq 2
and only one end, also it is simply connected and
p\in M^{3}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
and
L>\sqrt{2}\pi(1+\varepsilon)
, there is
k_{0}=k_{0}(\varepsilon,L)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
; such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, one can find a smooth compact surface
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq E_{k}-\overline{E_{k+1}}
, which satisfies

(1)

.
\Sigma_{k}
separates
\partial E_{k}
from
\partial E_{k+1}
;

(2)

.
\displaystyle V(\Sigma_{k})\leq\frac{4\pi}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}
.

(3)

.
\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma_{k})\leq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3(1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}})}}
.

Remark 3.4
.

The separating property of suitable
\mu
-bubble surface
\Sigma_{k}
(the property (1) above) was firstly observed in the argument of
[
4

5.4
]

Proof:

Step (1)
. It is obvious that
E_{k+1}\subseteq E_{k}
. Recall
N_{k}=E_{k}-\overline{E_{k+1}}
, and
\partial_{+}N_{k}=\partial E_{k},\partial_{-}N_{k}=\partial E_{k+1}
. Then
\partial N_{k}=\partial_{-}N_{k}\sqcup\partial_{+}N_{k}
. From Lemma
A.2
, we can choose a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M^{3})
with

\displaystyle f\big|_{\partial E_{k}}\leq kL,\quad f\big|_{\partial E_{k+1}}>(k+\frac{1}{2})L,\quad\text{and}\ (k+\frac{1}{2})L\ \text{is a regular value of}\ f.

Define
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\{x\in M^{3}:f(x)<(k+\frac{1}{2})L\}\cap N_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{k}
is a Caccioppoli set satisfying (
2.1
).

From Lemma
A.3
there is a function
\rho_{k}(x):\overline{N_{k}}\to[-L/2,L/2]
, which is continuous on

Lemma 5.1

\displaystyle\det(X+Y)=

\theta^{1},\theta^{2},\cdots,\theta^{n-1}
, then

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}(\theta)=\sqrt{\det\Bigg[\Bigg(\Big<\frac{\partial\varphi(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}},\frac{\partial\varphi(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big>_{q}\Bigg)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Bigg]}.

where
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{q}
is the inner product with respect to the metric on
T_{q}M
and
q=\varphi(\theta)
.

Note

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)\left(\frac{\partial\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right)=\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial r(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}}\theta+r(\theta)\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial r(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}}\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)(\theta)+r(\theta)\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\bigg).

For simplicity, let

\displaystyle r_{i}=\frac{\partial r(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}},\quad\quad A=\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)(\theta),\quad\quad B_{i}=r(\theta)\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\bigg).

We have
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}}=r_{i}A+B_{i}
.

From the definition, we know
\left<\theta,\theta\right>_{p}=1
. So

\displaystyle\left<\theta,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right>_{p}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n-1.

From Gauss lemma,

\displaystyle\left<A,B_{i}\right>_{q}=

\displaystyle r(\theta)\left<\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)(\theta),\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}(r(\theta)\theta)\left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right)\right>_{q}=r(\theta)\left<\theta,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right>_{p}=0.

From the above we get

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}(\theta)^{2}=\det\Big[\Big(r_{i}r_{j}|A|_{q}^{2}+\left<B_{i},B_{j}\right>_{q}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Big].

For fixed
t>0
, define
\displaystyle\psi_{t}(\theta)=\exp_{p}(t\theta):\mathbb{S}_{p}(M)\rightarrow M^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\psi_{t}(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right|_{t=r(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\left.\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}(t\theta)\left(t\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right)\right|_{t=r(\theta)}=r(\theta)\mathrm{d}\exp_{p}\big(r(\theta)\theta\big)\bigg(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\bigg)=B_{i}.

And the Jacobian of
J_{\psi_{t}}
is as follows:

\displaystyle J_{\psi_{t}}(\theta)|_{t=r(\theta)}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left.\det\Bigg[\Bigg(\Big<\frac{\partial\psi_{t}(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{i}},\frac{\partial\psi_{t}(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big>_{q}\Bigg)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Bigg]\right|_{t=r(\theta)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det\Big[\Big(\left<B_{i},B_{j}\right>_{q}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Big]}.

Since
\theta^{1},\theta^{2},\cdots,\theta^{n-1}
are normal coordinates,

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}},\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{j}}\right>=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n-1.

Combining
\left<\theta,\theta\right>_{p}=1
and
\left<\theta,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{i}}\right>_{p}=0
, we know that
\theta,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{1}},\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{2}},\cdots,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\theta^{n-1}}
form an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}M
. So
J(t,\theta)=J_{\psi_{t}}(\theta)
. From Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}(\theta)^{2}-J(r(\theta),\theta)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\Big[\Big(r_{i}r_{j}|A|_{q}^{2}+\left<B_{i},B_{j}\right>_{q}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Big]-\det\Big[\Big(\left<B_{i},B_{j}\right>_{q}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Big]\geq 0.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
(M^{n},g)
has
Rc\geq 0
and only one end, suppose
\Sigma\subset N_{k-1}
is a smooth, compact hypersurface separating
\partial E_{k-1}
from

\displaystyle V\Big((\partial B_{p}(s)\cap N_{k})-\mathrm{Cut}(p)\Big)\leq(\frac{s}{(k-1)L})^{n-1}\cdot V(\Sigma),\quad\quad\forall s>kL.

Proof:

Let
\tilde{\Sigma}\vcentcolon=\Sigma-\mathrm{Cut}(p)

as
\displaystyle\Pi(q)=\frac{\exp_{p}^{-1}(q)}{|\exp_{p}^{-1}(q)|_{g(p)}}
, where

is the unit tangent space of

at

. From the definition of cut points, the map
\Pi
is a well-defined smooth map.

From the separating property of
\Sigma
, we know that

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{s}\exp_{p}^{-1}\big((\partial B_{p}(s)\cap N_{k})-\mathrm{Cut}(p)\big)\subseteq\Pi(\tilde{\Sigma}).

Applying Sard’s theorem on the map
\Pi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{S}_{p}(M)
, we obtain that the set of critical values of
\Pi
(denoted as
\mathcal{C}(\Pi)
) satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C}(\Pi))=0

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(n-1)

Define

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}^{\prime}=\Theta_{k}-\mathcal{C}(\Pi).

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset(\tilde{\Sigma}\cap\Pi^{-1}(\Theta_{k}))
be the regular points of
\Pi
with image in
\Theta_{k}
. Then
\Sigma^{\prime}
is an open subset of
{\bf\mathbb{S}}_{p}M
from the implicit function theorem, and

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}^{\prime}\subset\Pi(\Sigma^{\prime}).

For any
q_{0}\in\Sigma^{\prime}

of
\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\varphi(\theta)\vcentcolon=\exp_{p}(r(\theta)\cdot\theta)=(\Pi\big|_{U})^{-1}(\theta),

For any

, note

. From Lemma
5.2

Theorem 6.1
.

For any complete non-compact Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
and
R\geq 2
,

(1)

. if
(M^{3},g)
has only one end, then
\displaystyle 0<\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}\leq 2\pi
,

(2)

. if
(M^{3},g)
has at least two ends, then
(M^{3},g)
is isometric to a cylinder
S\times\mathbb{R}
with
S
being a closed surface of sectional curvature at least one, and
\displaystyle 0<\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}\leq 4\pi
.

(3)

. moreover
\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}=4\pi
if and only if
(M^{3},g)
is isometric to a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof:

Step (1)
. Note any complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature has at least linear volume growth (see
[
17
]
). We know that
\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}>0
generally.

If
(M^{3},g)
has more than one ends, then there is at least one geodesic line in
M^{3}
.
From Cheeger-Gromoll’s splitting Theorem, we know that
M^{3}
is isometric to
N^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. Using
R\big|_{M}\geq 2
, we get that
R\big|_{N}\geq 2
. From Bonnet-Myers’ Theorem and the Bishop-Gromov’s volume comparison Theorem, we get that
N^{2}
is compact and
V(N^{2})\leq V(\mathbb{S}^{2})=4\pi
. The direct computation yields

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M^{3},1}=\varlimsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B_{p}(r))}{2r}\leq\varlimsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(N^{2})\times(2r)}{2r}\leq 4\pi.

The rigidity part of (
6.1
) follows from the rigidity part of the Bishop-Gromov’s volume comparison Theorem for
N^{2}
.

Step (2)
. In the rest argument, we assume that
(M^{3},g)
has only one end. We firstly consider the case
\pi_{1}(M^{3})=0
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1),\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{100})
, choose
L>8\pi
. Choose
k_{0}=k_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta,L)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, such that for
k\geq k_{0}
, the conclusions in Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
4.6
both hold.

In the rest argument, unless otherwise mentioned, all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Now from Proposition
3.3
, there is a compact smooth surface
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq N_{k}
separating
\partial E_{k}
from
\partial E_{k+1}
, and

(6.2)

\displaystyle V(\Sigma_{k})\leq\frac{4\pi}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}.

From Lemma
5.3
and (
6.2
), for any
r\in(kL,(k+1+10\delta)\cdot L+2c)
, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle V\Big((\partial B_{p}(r)\cap N_{k})-\mathrm{Cut}(p)\Big)\leq\left(\frac{r}{(k-1)L}\right)^{2}\cdot V(\Sigma_{k-1})\leq\left(\frac{r}{(k-1)L}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}},

where
\displaystyle c=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3(1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}})}}
.

Note
V(\mathrm{Cut}(p))=0
, where

is the measure with respect to metric
g
. Now using (
6.3
) and Lemma
4.6
, we get

\displaystyle V(N_{k})

\displaystyle=V(N_{k}-\mathrm{Cut}(p))\leq\int_{kL}^{(k+1+10\delta)\cdot L+2c}V\Big((\partial B_{p}(r)\cap N_{k})-\mathrm{Cut}(p)\Big)dr

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{k^{2}+(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})k+3^{-1}(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}}\Big)\cdot\frac{4\pi\cdot[(1+10\delta)L+2c]}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}.

Therefore for

\displaystyle V(B_{p}(r))

\displaystyle\leq V(M-E_{k_{0}})+\sum_{j=k_{0}}^{k}V(N_{j})

\displaystyle\leq C+\frac{4\pi\cdot[(1+10\delta)L+2c]}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}\cdot\sum_{j=k_{0}}^{k}\Big(\frac{j^{2}+(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})j+3^{-1}(1+10\delta+4^{-1})^{2}}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C+\frac{4\pi\cdot[(1+10\delta)L+2c]}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}\cdot\sum_{j=k_{0}}^{k}\Big(\frac{j^{2}+(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})j}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big),

Direct computation yields

(6.4)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(B(r))}{r}\leq\varlimsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C+\frac{4\pi\cdot[(1+10\delta)L+2c]}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}\cdot\sum_{j=k_{0}}^{k}\Big(\frac{j^{2}+(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})j}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big)}{kL}\leq\frac{4\pi\cdot(1+10\delta+\frac{2c}{L})}{1-\frac{2\pi^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{L^{2}}}.

Lemma A.1
.

For any closed subset

T_{x}M
. Let
\varphi_{t}(v)=\mathrm{d}\exp_{x}(\tilde{\gamma}(t))(v)
be the linear isomorphism between
T_{x}M
and
T_{\gamma(t)}M
. Then

\displaystyle\rho_{k,\alpha}(\gamma(t))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\int_{T_{\gamma(t)}M}\eta\Big(\frac{v}{\alpha}\Big)\tilde{\rho}_{k}(\exp_{\gamma(t)}(v))\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\int_{T_{x}M}\eta\Big(\frac{\varphi_{t}(v)}{\alpha}\Big)\tilde{\rho}_{k}(\exp_{\gamma(t)}(\varphi_{t}(v)))J_{\varphi_{t}}(v)\mathrm{d}v.

Since
\exp_{x}(\tilde{\gamma}(t)+sv)
is smooth about
(s,t)
in a small neighborhood of
(0,0)
,
\varphi_{t}(v)=\mathrm{d}\exp_{x}(\tilde{\gamma}(t))(v)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}|_{s=0}\exp_{x}(\tilde{\gamma}(t)+sv)
is also smooth about
t
in a small neighborhood of
0
. So
\eta\Big(\frac{\varphi_{t}(v)}{\alpha}\Big)
,
\exp_{\gamma(t)}(\varphi_{t}(v))
and
J_{\varphi_{t}}(v)
are smooth functions of
t
near
0
. Then
\rho_{k,\alpha}(\gamma(t))
is smooth about
t
near
0
. Hence
\rho_{k,\alpha}
is smooth at
x
.

We further assume
\exp_{x}(B_{0}^{3}(\alpha))\subset\overline{B_{p}((k+1+\delta)L)}-B_{p}((k+1-\delta)L)
for all
x\in\partial B_{p}((k+1)L)
, and
\exp_{x}(B_{0}^{3}(\alpha))\subset\overline{B_{p}((k+\delta)L)}-B_{p}((k-\delta)L)
for all
x\in\partial B_{p}(kL)
by taking
\alpha
small. When
x\in\partial B_{p}((k+1)L)
,

\displaystyle\rho_{k,\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\int_{T_{x}M}\eta\Big(\frac{v}{\alpha}\Big)\frac{L}{2}\mathrm{d}v=\frac{L}{2}.

When
x\in\partial B_{p}(kL)
,

\displaystyle\rho_{k,\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\int_{T_{x}M}\eta\Big(\frac{v}{\alpha}\Big)\Big(-\frac{L}{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}v=-\frac{L}{2}.

Moreover
-L/2\leq\rho_{k,\alpha}(x)\leq L/2
on
N_{k}
.

Since
\rho_{k,\alpha}(x)
converges to
\tilde{\rho}_{k}
uniformly on
\overline{B_{p}((k+1+\delta)L)}-B_{p}((k-\delta)L)
, there is
\alpha_{0}
such that the Lipschitz constants of
\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{k}
satisfy
\mathrm{Lip}(\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}})<\mathrm{Lip}(\tilde{\rho}_{k})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<1+\varepsilon
.

Let
U=\{x\in N_{k}:\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}}(x)<0\}
,
V=\{x\in N_{k}:\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}}(x)>0\}
be two open sets. From Lemma
A.1
there exists smooth bump functions
f
,
g
on
M
such that

\displaystyle f|_{U}>0,\quad f|_{U^{c}}=0,\quad g|_{V}>0,\quad g|_{V^{c}}=0.

And we may assume
f<L/4
,
g<L/4
,
|\nabla_{M}f|<\varepsilon/2
,
|\nabla_{M}g|<\varepsilon/2
after a scaling.

Let

\displaystyle\rho_{k}=\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}}|_{\overline{N_{k}}}+f|_{\overline{N_{k}}}-g|_{\overline{N_{k}}}

then
\rho_{k}
is smooth,
\rho_{k}|_{\partial E_{k}}=-\frac{L}{2}
,
\rho_{k}|_{\partial E_{k+1}}=\frac{L}{2}
,
\rho_{k}(N_{k})\subset(-L/2,L/2)
and

\displaystyle|\nabla_{M}\rho_{k}|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\rho_{k,\alpha_{0}})+|\nabla_{M}f|+|\nabla_{M}g|<(1+\varepsilon)^{2}.

∎

First, we recall the famous Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequality that is the starting point of all our research in the domain. Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
a symmetric diffusion semigroup on
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},\mu)
in Stein’s sense
[
55
, section III.1]
. Namely,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies the following conditions

\bullet

T_{t}
is a contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\bullet

T_{t}T_{s}=T_{t+s}
,

\bullet

\lim_{t\to 0}T_{t}(f)=f
in
L_{2}(\Omega)
for every
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
,

\bullet

T_{t}
is positive (i.e. positivity preserving),

\bullet

T_{t}
is selfadjoint on
L_{2}(\Omega)
,

\bullet

T_{t}(1)=1
.

The last condition is the markovianity; the next to last is the symmetry. Thus such a semigroup is also called a
symmetric markovian semigroup
. A semigroup satisfying all the above conditions except markovianity is usually called a
symmetric submarkovian semigroup
(the submarkovianity means
T_{t}(1)\leq 1
).

It is a classical fact that the orthogonal projection
\mathsf{F}
from
L_{2}(\Omega)
onto the fixed point subspace of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
extends to a contractive projection on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for
every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
\mathsf{F}
is also positive and
\mathsf{F}\big(L_{p}(\Omega)\big)
is the fixed point subspace of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
.

Stein’s celebrated extension of the classical Littlewood-Paley inequality asserts that for every symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
and every
1<p<\infty

(1.1)

\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\approx_{p}\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(f)\Big|^{2}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

The classical inequality corresponds to the case where
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the Poisson semigroup on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
or the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Stein’s inequality above is the core of
[
55
]
in which Stein developed a beautiful general theory. Later, Cowling
[
12
]
presented an elegant alternative approach to Stein’s theory for symmetric submarkovian semigroups; Cowling’s goal is to show that the negative generator of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
has a bounded holomorphic functional calculus, then to deduce the maximal inequality on
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
which is another fundamental result of Stein.

In the present article we are concerned with the vector-valued case. Given a Banach space
X
let
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
denote the
L_{p}
-space of strongly measurable functions from
\Omega
to
X
. It is a well known elementary fact that if
T
is a positive bounded operator on
L_{p}(\Omega)
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then
T\otimes{\rm Id}_{X}
is bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with the same norm. For notational convenience, throughout this article, we will denote
T\otimes{\rm Id}_{X}
by
T
too. Thus
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is also a semigroup of contractions on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
for any Banach space
X
with
\mathsf{F}\big(L_{p}(\Omega;X)\big)
as its fixed point subspace.

The vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory consists in investigating (
1.1
) for
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
(with the absolute value on the right hand side replaced by the norm of
X
). It is
not hard to show that the equivalence (
1.1
) continues to hold in the
X
-valued setting for the Poisson semigroup on
{\mathbb{T}}
iff
X
is isomorphic to a Hilbert space (cf.
[
20
,
64
]
). However, if one requires only the validity of one of the two one-sided inequalities, the corresponding family of Banach spaces is much larger: the upper estimate corresponds to 2-uniformly smooth spaces while the lower one to 2-uniformly convex spaces (up to a renorming).

These geometrical properties of Banach spaces can be characterized by martingale inequalities. Recall that a Banach space
X
is of
martingale cotype

q
(with
2\leq q<\infty
) if there exists a positive constant
c
such that every finite
X
-valued
L_{q}
-martingale
(f_{n})
satisfies the following inequality

\sum_{n}\mathbb{E}\big\|f_{n}-f_{n-1}\big\|_{X}^{q}\leq c^{q}\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\big\|f_{n}\big\|_{X}^{q}\,,

where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the underlying expectation.
X
is of
martingale type

q
(with
1<q\leq 2
) if the reverse inequality holds (with
c^{-1}
in place of
c
). The corresponding best constant will be denoted by
\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)
for the martingale cotype
q
and by
\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)
for the martingale type
q
. Pisier’s famous renorming theorem asserts that
X
is of martingale type (resp. cotype)
q
iff
X
admits an equivalent norm that is
q
-uniformly smooth (resp. convex). We refer the reader to
[
51
,
53
,
54
]
for more information.

Note that in the study of one-sided inequalities in the vector-valued case, the index
2
on the right hand side of (
1.1
) plays no special role and can be replaced by
1<q<\infty
,
q\leq 2
for the upper estimate and
q\geq 2
for the lower. Now we can summarize the main results of
[
64
,
41
,
66
]
as follows.

Theorem A
.
Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<q<\infty
.

(i)

X
is of martingale cotype
q
iff for every symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
and for every
1<p<\infty

(
equivalently, for some
1<p<\infty)
there exists a constant
c
such that

\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

(ii)

X
is of martingale type
q
iff for every symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
and for every
1<p<\infty

(
equivalently, for some
1<p<\infty)
there exists a constant
c
such that

\big\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq c\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

Note that
\mathsf{F}(f)
does not contribute to the norm on the left hand side of the inequality in (i) above since
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(\mathsf{F}(f))=0
for any
t>0
; so if this inequality holds, it automatically holds with
f
replaced by
f-\mathsf{F}(f)
on the right hand side. In the sequel, when cotype inequalities will be considered, we will often use simply
f
instead
f-\mathsf{F}(f)
as in (i). However, for the type inequalities as in (ii), we must use
f-\mathsf{F}(f)
on the left hand side.

Both “if” parts in the above theorem are proved in
[
64
]
; for that purpose we need only the case where
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the usual Poisson semigroup on
{\mathbb{T}}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as in
[
41
]
). This is the easy direction thanks to the classical link between Poisson integral and Brownian motion. The other direction is harder. It is first proved in
[
64
]
for the Poisson semigroup on the unit circle, then in
[
41
]
for the Poisson semigroup subordinated to any symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Left as an open problem in
[
41
]
, the statement for
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself as above was finally settled in
[
66
]
. Note that like in
[
55
]
, the key tool in
[
41
,
66
]
is Rota’s martingale dilation of a symmetric diffusion semigroup that allows us to adapt the scalar Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory developed in
[
55
]
.

The use of Rota’s dilation prevented us from weakening the assumption on a symmetric diffusion semigroup. Cowling’s approach in
[
12
]
does not use Rota’s dilation but it requires the semigroup in consideration to be symmetric and submarkovian. It has been an open problem of establishing the results of
[
41
]
or
[
66
]
in Cowling’s setting. In fact, since a long time it has been a desire to extend all previous results to more general semigroups. This was done in some special cases (Hermite, Laguerre and Bessel semigroups) by Betancor and coauthors (cf.
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
).

The objective of the present article is to resolve the above problems. We will develop a vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory for semigroups of regular operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for a
single

1<p<\infty
, thereby going considerably beyond Stein-Cowling’s setting.

Recall that an operator
T
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) is
regular
if there exists a constant
c
such that

\big\|\sup_{k}|T(f_{k})|\big\|_{p}\leq c\,\big\|\sup_{k}|f_{k}|\big\|_{p}

for all finite sequences
\{f_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{p}(\Omega)
. The least constant
c
is called the
regular norm
of
T
. Obviously, any positive operator is regular with regular norm equal to its operator norm. It is well known that, conversely, if
T
is regular, then there exists a positive operator
S
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
such that
|T(f)|\leq S(|f|)

for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
with
\|S\|
equal to the regular norm of
T
; such a positive
S
is unique and called the absolute value of
T
and denoted by
|T|
(see
[
43
, Chapter 1]
). For presentation simplicity, in this article we will only consider
contractively regular
operators, i.e., those with regular norms less than or equal to
1
, and will simply call these operators as regular operators with a light abuse of terminology.

It is well known (and easy to check) that if
T
is a contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then
T
is regular on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. Like positive operators, a regular operator
T
extends to a contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
for any Banach space
X
. This extension will be denoted by
T
too.

Now let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup of regular operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
with
1<p<\infty
. Extended to
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
remains to be a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.
Let again
\mathsf{F}
be the projection from
L_{p}(\Omega)
onto the fixed point subspace of

\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Then
\mathsf{F}
is also regular, so extends to a contractive projection on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Note that
\mathsf{F}(L_{p}(\Omega;X))
coincides with the fixed point subspace of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.

Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the Poisson semigroup subordinated to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
:

(1.2)

P_{t}(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-s}}{\sqrt{s}}\,T_{\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}(f)ds.

Recall that if
A
denotes the negative infinitesimal generator of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
(i.e.,
T_{t}=e^{-tA}
), then
P_{t}=e^{-t\sqrt{A}}
.
Instead of the square root, one can, of course, consider other subordinated semigroups
e^{-tA^{\alpha}}
for
0<\alpha<1
; but we will not deal with the latter here.

To proceed further, we need to introduce some notions. Define

\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for
f
in the definition domain of
A
in
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.
X
is said to be of
Luzin cotype
q
relative to

\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
if there exists a constant
c
such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\big\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}

for all
f
as above.
The smallest
c
is denoted by
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
. Similarly, we define the
Luzin type
q
of
X
by reversing the above inequality and changing
c
to
c^{-1}
, the corresponding type
q
constant is denoted by
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
. See section
4
below for more information.

Remark 1.1
.

The subordination formula (
1.2
) immediately implies the pointwise inequality
\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\leq C\,\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)
for any
f
, where
C
is an absolute positive constant. It then follows that

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\geq C\,\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\;\text{ and }\;\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\leq C\,\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X).

In
[
64
]
, the Luzin type and cotype relative to the Poisson semigroup on the unit circle are shown to be equivalent to the martingale type and cotype, respectively. Theorem A above extends this to symmetric diffusion semigroups.

In the sequel, we will use the following convention:
A\lesssim B
(resp.
A\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B
) means that
A\leq CB
(resp.
A\leq C_{\varepsilon}B
) for some absolute positive constant
C
(resp. a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
depending only on
\varepsilon
).
A\approx B
or
A\approx_{\varepsilon}B
means that these inequalities as well as their inverses hold. The index
p
will be assumed to satisfy
1<p<\infty
and
p^{\prime}
will denote its conjugate index.

Below is our first principal result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<p,q<\infty
. Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup of regular operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
and
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
its subordinated Poisson semigroup.

(i)

If
X
is of martingale cotype
q
, then
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
and

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X).

(ii)

If
X
is of martingale type
q
, then
X
is of Luzin type
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
and

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p,\,p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X).

The two above inequalities can be reformulated in another (clearer) way, for instance, the first one reads as

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle p^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)&\textrm{ if}&p\geq q,\\
\displaystyle p^{\prime}\,\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)&\textrm{ if}&p<q.\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.3
.

All the growth orders, except the one on
\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
as
p\to 1
, are optimal since they are already so in the scalar case
X={\mathbb{C}}
. More precisely,

(i)

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})\gtrsim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)
for all
1<p<\infty
when
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the classical Poisson semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see Proposition
8.5
below);

(ii)

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})\gtrsim p
as
p\to\infty
when
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the Poisson semigroup subordinated to a symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, as shown by Zhendong Xu and Hao Zhang
[
68
]
; in fact, they proved the stronger inequality
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})\gtrsim p
as
p\to\infty
for a symmetric diffusion semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

Part (i) of the above theorem cannot hold for the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself without additional assumption (see Remark
4.4
below). It turns out that the missing condition is the analyticity of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Recall that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
if
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
extends to a bounded analytic function from an open sector
\Sigma_{\beta_{0}}=\big\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|{\rm arg}(z)|<\beta_{0}\big\}
to
B\big(L_{p}(\Omega;X)\big)
for some
0<\beta_{0}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, where
B(Y)
denotes the space of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
Y
. In this case,

(1.4)

\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}=\sup\big\{\big\|T_{z}\big\|_{B(L_{p}(\Omega;X))}:z\in\Sigma_{\beta_{0}}\big\}<\infty.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
and
p,q
be as above.

(i)

If
X
is of martingale type
q
, then
X
is of Luzin type
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
and

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p,\,(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X).

(ii)

Assume additionally that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies (
1.4
). Let
\beta_{q}=\beta_{0}\min(\frac{p}{q}\,,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})
.
If
X
is of martingale cotype
q
, then
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
and

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\beta_{q}^{-3}\,\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}^{\min(\frac{p}{q},\,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})}\,\max\big(p^{\frac{2}{q}},\,(p^{\prime})^{1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{c,q}(X).

The two previous theorems considerably improve Theorem A. Firstly, the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
now acts on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for a single
p
. Secondly, the markovianity or submarkovianity is not assumed (in fact,
T_{t}(1)
is even not defined if the measure on
\Omega
is infinite). Thirdly, the symmetry is not needed either since the semigroup does not act on
L_{2}(\Omega)
if
p\neq 2
.

Another improvement concerns the precise estimates on the best constants, the present estimates are much better than all previously known ones, even in the scalar case (see section
8
below for historical comments). Moreover, except one case, they give the optimal orders of growth as
p\to 1
and as
p\to\infty
, as already pointed out in Remark
1.3
. This is perhaps a major novelty of our method.

The aforementioned optimality allows us to answer a question raised by Naor and Young about the optimal orders of
\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
and
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
when
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the heat semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see the appendix of
[
47
]
). In fact, we will show a much stronger result. Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be an integrable function satisfying

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle|\varphi(x)|\leq\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}},&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\\
\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq\frac{|x-y|^{\delta}}{(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon+\delta}}+\frac{|x-y|^{\delta}}{(1+|y|)^{d+\varepsilon+\delta}},&x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\\
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)dx=0\end{array}\right.

for some positive constants
\varepsilon
and
\delta
.

We will also need
\varphi
to be
nondegenerate
in the sense that there exists another function
\psi
satisfying (
1.5
) such that

(1.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(t\xi)\,\widehat{\psi}(t\xi)\,\frac{dt}{t}=1,\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

This nondegeneracy allows us to use the Calderón reproducing formula. There exist plenty of functions satisfying these conditions, for instance, the kernel of
t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}T_{t}
, where
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is either the heat or Poisson semigroup on

, as well as any Schwartz function
\varphi
verifying that for any

.

Let
\varphi_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{d}}\varphi(\frac{x}{t})
for

and

. We define

(1.7)

G_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{t}*f(x)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

for any (reasonable) function

. Let
\mathsf{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
be the best constant

such that

\big\|G_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq c\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)},\quad f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X).

Similarly, we introduce
\mathsf{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
for the reverse inequality (with

instead of

).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be a Banach space and

. Assume that
\varphi
satisfies (
1.5

(i)

If

(ii)

Assume additionally that
\varphi
is nondegenerate.
If
X
is of martingale type
q
, then

\mathsf{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\max\big(p,\,(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X).

Let
\{\mathbb{H}_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the classical heat semigroup on

whose convolution kernel is given by

\mathbb{H}_{t}(x)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}\,.

Its subordinated Poisson semigroup is the usual Poisson semigroup

with the convolution kernel

\mathbb{P}_{t}(x)=\frac{c_{d}\,t}{(|x|^{2}+t^{2})^{(d+1)/2}}\,.

The above theorem implies the following corollary that resolves Naor and Young’s problem.

Corollary 1.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\;\text{ and }\;\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim_{d}\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X).

Moreover,

\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\gtrsim\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})\gtrsim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\;\text{ and }\;\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,q}(X)\gtrsim\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,q}(X)\gtrsim\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X).

Remark 1.7
.

Like the martingale type and cotype, the Luzin type and cotype behave well with duality as shown by Theorem

Remark 1.11
.

The analyticity assumption in Theorem

is superreflexive (

A summary of the main techniques and the contents seems to be in order. Our approach is different from all the previous ones. It is based on holomorphic functional calculus, which constitutes perhaps one of the major ideas of this article. In this regard, it shares some common points with Cowling’s approach that deals with the bounded
H^{\infty}
functional calculus of the generator of a symmetric submarkovian semigroup and the related maximal inequality. We need, however, to adapt McIntosh’s
H^{\infty}
functional calculus for our purpose. This is done in the preparatory section
2
in which we introduce a key notion of the article: the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness of a family of operators on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
; it gives rise to the definitions of
\ell_{q}
-sectorial operators and
\ell_{q}
-analytic semigroups. We transfer to this setting some well known results about sectorial operators and analytic semigroups. After this preparation, we prove Theorem
1.10
in section
3
. This projection theorem is a crucial ingredient for the duality studied in section
4
. Theorem
4.5
establishes our duality result between the Luzin cotype of
X
and the Luzin type of the dual space
X^{*}
; this result is as nice as the corresponding one in the martingale case, except the links between the involved constants (compare the constants in Theorem
4.5
and those in (
6.2
) below). This section also contains some general properties of the Luzin type and cotype, in particular, a characterization by lacunary discrete differences (Theorem
4.7
).

As Rota’s dilation is no longer available in the present situation, we use instead Fendler’s dilation for semigroups of regular operators. Fendler’s theorem transfers Theorem
1.2
(i) to the special case where
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the translation group of
{\mathbb{R}}
. This allows us to exploit techniques from harmonic analysis. Our strategy is built, in a crucial way, on Calderón-Zygmund singular integral theory and modern real-variable Littlewood-Paley theory. We present all this in the preparatory section
5
that will be needed for the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.5
. These proofs constitute the most heavy and technical part of the article. The proofs of Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
are done in section
6
. We then use transference to show Theorem
1.2
in section
7
. To that end, we first need to represent the
g
-function associated to the Poisson semigroup subordinated to the translation group of
{\mathbb{R}}
as a singular integral operator. Theorem
1.4
will follow from Theorem
1.2
by functional calculus; Corollary
1.9
is an easy consequence of Theorem
1.2
(i).

An additional major significant aspect of the new approach is the fact that it improves the growth orders on
p
of the relevant best constants even in the scalar Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequalities, see section
8
; moreover, except the case of the Luzin type constant as
p\to 1
, it yields the optimal orders, which is not the case by the previous methods of Stein and Cowling (see the historical comments in section
8
). This shows, to a certain extent, that our method is optimal. Section
8
also contains the optimality of the best constants in Corollary
1.6
. We end the article by an appendix that gives some examples of semigroups.

The techniques developed in this article allow one to simplify and extend many recent results in the scalar Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory, in particular, those on positive operators on
L_{2}
with kernels satisfying Gaussian upper estimates. On the other hand, they are also applicable to the noncommutative setting. We will carry out all this elsewhere.

2.
The
\ell_{q}
-boundedness

This section is the preparatory part of the article. We will introduce the notion of
\ell_{q}
-boundedness that is the direct extension to the vector-valued setting of the
R_{q}
-boundedness introduced by Weis
[
59
]
. In fact, though not explicitly stated, this notion appeared before in harmonic analysis with regard to vector-valued inequalities of classical operators. Most results below are the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness analogues of well known results or those of Kunstmann and Ullmann
[
33
]
in the scalar case. I learnt the existence of
[
59
,
33
]
after the submission of this article for publication, and I thank Emiel Lorist for pointing out these references to me.

We start with a brief introduction to holomorphic functional calculus in order to fix notation (see
[
13
]
for more information). Recall that a densely defined closed operator
B
on a Banach space
Y
is called a
sectorial operator of type

\alpha
with
0\leq\alpha<\pi
if
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}
is contained in the resolvent set of
B
for any
\gamma>\alpha
and

\sup\big\{\big\|z(z-B)^{-1}\big\|_{B(Y)}\,:z\notin\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}\big\}<\infty,

where
\Sigma_{\gamma}
is the open sector
\big\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|{\rm arg}(z)|<\gamma\big\}
in the complex plane. Let
\beta>\gamma
and
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\beta})
denote the space of bounded analytic functions in
\Sigma_{\beta}
and
H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
its subspace consisting of all
\varphi
satisfying

|\varphi(z)|\leq\frac{c|z|^{\delta}}{1+|z|^{2\delta}}\;\text{ for some }\;c>0\text{ and }\delta>0.

Let
\Gamma
be the boundary of
\Sigma_{\beta}
, oriented in the positive sense. Then for any
\varphi\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
the integral

\varphi(B)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)(z-B)^{-1}dz

defines a bounded operator on
Y
, where the integral absolutely converges in
B(Y)
.

The following resolution of the identity will be useful later. Let
\psi\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(t)\frac{dt}{t}=1.

Then the following integral

(2.1)

y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tB)(y)\frac{dt}{t}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{C\to\infty}\int_{\varepsilon}^{C}\psi(tB)(y)\frac{dt}{t}

exists for every
y\in\overline{{\rm im}B}
.
This is
[
28
, Proposition 10.2.5]
. Let us include its easy verification for completeness. For
\varphi\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
, we have

\varphi(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tz)\varphi(z)\frac{dt}{t}\,,\quad z\in\Sigma_{\beta}.

Thus for any
y\in Y

\varphi(B)(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tB)\varphi(B)(y)\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Choose

\varphi(z)=\frac{n^{2}z}{(n+z)(1+nz)}\,.

Then
\varphi(B)(y)\to y
in
Y
as
n\to\infty
for any
y\in\overline{{\rm im}B}
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.8]
), whence (
2.1
) by virtue of the convergence lemma (
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A family
{\mathcal{F}}\subset B(L_{p}(\Omega;X))
is said to be
\ell_{q}
-
bounded
if there exists a constant
c
such that

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k}\|A_{k}(f_{k})\|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k}\|f_{k}\|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

for all finite sequences
\{A_{k}\}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
and
\{f_{k}\}\subset L_{p}(\Omega;X)
, with the usual modification for
q=\infty
in the above inequality.

Remark 2.2
.

It is clear that the sums in the above definition can be replaced by integrals without changing the constant
c
. On the other hand, it is easy to show that the absolutely convex hull of an
\ell_{q}
-bounded family is again
\ell_{q}
-bounded with the same constant.

Accordingly, we introduce the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness versions of sectoriality of operators and analyticity of semigroups. Recall that a semigroup
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
on a Banach space
Y
is said to be analytic if it extends to an analytic function from
\Sigma_{\beta}
to
B(Y)
for some
0<\beta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
and bounded in any smaller sector. In this case, we call
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
an
analytic semigroup of type

\beta
.

Definition 2.3
.

(i)

A densely defined closed operator
B
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
is called an
\ell_{q}
-
sectorial operator of type

\alpha
with
0\leq\alpha<\pi
if
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}
is contained in the resolvent set of
B
for any
\gamma>\alpha
and the family

\big\{z(z-B)^{-1}:z\notin\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}\big\}

is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.

(ii)

A semigroup
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
is called an
\ell_{q}
-
analytic semigroup of type

\beta
with
0<\beta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
if
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
extends to an analytic function from
\Sigma_{\beta}
to
B(L_{p}(\Omega;X))
and for any
\nu<\beta
the family

\big\{S_{z}:z\in\Sigma_{\nu}\big\}

is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.

The following is the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness analogue of a classical characterization of analytic semigroups.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a strongly continuous bounded semigroup on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
B
its negative generator. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-analytic of type
\beta
for some
0<\beta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
;

(ii)

B
is
\ell_{q}
-sectorial of type
\alpha
for some
\alpha<\frac{\pi}{2}
;

(iii)

\{S_{t},tBS_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the classical argument (cf. e.g. the proof of
[
50
, Theorem 5.2]
). As we want to track the links between the different constants involved, we give below an outline.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Let
\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta
. For
\alpha<\gamma<\frac{\pi}{2}
choose
0<\nu<\beta
such that
\gamma+\nu>\frac{\pi}{2}
, for instance, we can take
\nu=\beta-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{2}
so that
\gamma+\nu=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{2}
. Then for any
z=re^{{\rm i}\theta}\notin\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}
and
z\neq 0
, we have

z(z-B)^{-1}=-z\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{tz}S_{t}dt=-ze^{{\rm i}{\rm sgn}(\theta)\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{tre^{{\rm i}(\theta+{\rm sgn}(\theta)\nu)}}S_{te^{{\rm i}{\rm sgn}(\theta)\nu}}dt.

If
C_{\nu}
denotes the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness constant of the family
\big\{S_{\zeta}:\zeta\in\Sigma_{\nu}\big\}
, then by Remark
2.2
we deduce that
\big\{z(z-B)^{-1}:z\notin\Sigma_{\gamma},\,z\neq 0\big\}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant
C_{\gamma}
given by

C_{\gamma}\leq C_{\nu}\sup_{z\notin\Sigma_{\gamma}}|z|\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|e^{tre^{{\rm i}(\theta+{\rm sgn}(\theta)\nu)}}\big|dt\leq\frac{C_{\nu}}{|\cos(\theta+{\rm sgn}(\theta)\nu)|}\leq\frac{C_{\nu}}{|\cos(\gamma+\nu)|}\,.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
\alpha<\gamma<\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\Gamma
be the boundary of
\Sigma_{\gamma}
oriented in the positive sense. Then

tBS_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}t\lambda e^{-t\lambda}(\lambda-B)^{-1}d\lambda,\quad t>0.

Thus
\{tBS_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant

C^{\prime}_{d}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{\pi}\sup_{t>0}\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-tr\cos\gamma}dr=\frac{C_{\gamma}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r\cos\gamma}dr=\frac{C_{\gamma}}{\pi\cos\gamma}.

To show the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness of
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
we need to slightly modify the contour
\Gamma
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the union of the part of
\Gamma
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
and the arc in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{\gamma}
of the circle with the origin as center and radius
1
. Then we have

S_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}}e^{-t\lambda}(\lambda-B)^{-1}d\lambda,\quad t>0.

The change of variables
\zeta=t\lambda
yields

S_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{t\Gamma^{\prime}}e^{-\zeta}\,\frac{1}{t}\,(\frac{\zeta}{t}-B)^{-1}d\zeta,

where
t\Gamma^{\prime}
is the union of the part of
\Gamma
with
|\lambda|\geq t
and the arc in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{\gamma}
of the circle with the origin as center and radius
t
. However, the Cauchy formula insures that we can go back to
\Gamma^{\prime}
:

S_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}}e^{-\zeta}\,\frac{1}{t}\,(\frac{\zeta}{t}-B)^{-1}d\zeta.

This implies that
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant

C^{\prime\prime}_{d}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-r\cos\gamma}\frac{dr}{r}+\frac{C_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma}^{2\pi-\gamma}e^{-\cos\theta}d\theta\leq\frac{CC_{\gamma}}{\cos\gamma}\,.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
C_{d}
denote the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness constant of
\{S_{t},tBS_{t}\}_{t>0}
. The function
t\mapsto S_{t}
is infinitely derivable in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and for any positive integer
n

(S_{t})^{(n)}=\big(S^{\prime}_{\frac{t}{n}}\big)^{n}\,.

This shows that
\{t^{n}(S_{t})^{(n)}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant
(C_{d})^{n}
. Let
\beta=\arctan\frac{1}{eC_{d}}
. Then
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
becomes an
\ell_{q}
-analytic semigroup of type
\beta
thanks to the following formula

S_{z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(S_{t})^{(n)}}{n!}\,(z-t)^{n}\,,\quad z\in\Sigma_{\beta}

and for any
\nu<\beta
the family
\big\{S_{z}:z\in\overline{\Sigma_{\nu}}\big\}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant

C_{\nu}\leq\frac{1}{1-eC_{d}\tan\nu}\,.

The proof is thus complete.
∎

The following is again the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness version of an elementary result on sectorial operators. The case used later concerns only
\sqrt{B}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
B
be an
\ell_{q}
-sectorial operator of type
\alpha
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with
\alpha<\pi
. Let
\theta>0
such that
\theta\alpha<\pi
. Then
B^{\theta}
is an
\ell_{q}
-sectorial operator of type
\theta\alpha
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}>\theta\alpha
. Given
z\notin\overline{\Sigma_{\gamma}}
, writing

z=[z^{\theta^{-1}}(z-\lambda^{\theta})+(z^{\theta^{-1}}\lambda^{\theta}-z\lambda)](z^{\theta^{-1}}-\lambda)^{-1}\,,

we have

z(z-\lambda^{\theta}\,)^{-1}=z^{\theta^{-1}}(z^{\theta^{-1}}-\lambda)^{-1}+\varphi(\lambda),

where
\varphi(\lambda)=(z^{\theta^{-1}}\lambda^{\theta}-z\lambda)(z-\lambda^{\theta})^{-1}(z^{\theta^{-1}}-\lambda)^{-1}
. Thus

z(z-B^{\theta}\,)^{-1}=z^{\theta^{-1}}(z^{\theta^{-1}}-B)^{-1}+\varphi(B).

Note that
\varphi
is analytic in
\Sigma_{\gamma\theta^{-1}}
. Let
\Gamma
be the boundary of
\Sigma_{\gamma^{\prime}\theta^{-1}}
. Then

\varphi(B)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(\lambda)(\lambda-B)^{-1}d\lambda=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(\lambda)[\lambda(\lambda-B)^{-1}]\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.

The change of variables
\zeta=z^{-\theta^{-1}}\lambda
yields

\int_{\Gamma}|\varphi(\lambda)|\,\frac{|d\lambda|}{|\lambda|}=\int_{z^{-\theta^{-1}}\Gamma}\frac{|\zeta^{\theta}-\zeta|}{|1-\zeta^{\theta}|\,|1-\zeta|}\,\frac{|d\zeta|}{|\zeta|}\,.

Decomposing the last integral into three parts corresponding to
|\zeta|
close to
0
,
1
and
\infty
, respectively, we get

\int_{\Gamma}|\varphi(\lambda)|\,\frac{|d\lambda|}{|\lambda|}\lesssim\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})^{2}}\,.

Hence by Remark
2.2
, we deduce the desired assertion.
∎

Now we return back to our distinguished semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
of regular operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. We have extended
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
to
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Recall our convention that the regular operators considered in this article are all assumed contractively regular. Also recall the fact that
T
is regular on
L_{p}(\Omega)
iff

\big\|\sum_{k}|T(f_{k})|\big\|_{p}\leq\big\|\sum_{k}|f_{k}|\big\|_{p}

for all finite sequences
\{f_{k}\}
in
L_{p}(\Omega)
(see
[
40
]
). Consequently,
T
is regular on
L_{p}(\Omega)
iff its adjoint
T^{*}
is regular on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. In particular,
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup of regular operators on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

M_{t}=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}ds,\quad t>0.

The family
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})
.

Proof.

The celebrated theorem of Akcoglu
[
2
]
asserts that
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies the following maximal ergodic inequality

\big\|\sup_{t>0}|M_{t}(f)|\,\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq p^{\prime}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega);

see also
[
31
, Theorem 5.2.5]
.
The regularity of
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
insures that this inequality remains valid for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Thus for any finite sequences
\{t_{k}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
\{f_{k}\}\subset L_{p}(\Omega;X)
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{k}\|M_{t_{k}}(f_{k})\|_{X}\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\sup_{k}|M_{t_{k}}|(\|f_{k}\|_{X})\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\sup_{k}|M_{t_{k}}|(\sup_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{X})\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq p^{\prime}\big\|\sup_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{X}\,\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

This means that
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{\infty}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
p^{\prime}
. On the other hand,
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
is bounded, so
\ell_{p}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
1
. Thus by complex interpolation,
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
(p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}}
for
q>p
.

The case of
q<p
is treated by duality. Applying the previous discussion to the adjoint semigroup
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, we deduce that
\{M^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{\infty}
-bounded on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
with constant
p
, so
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{1}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
p
. The assertion for
q<p
then follows by complex interpolation once more.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

Armed with the preparation in section
2
, we will follow the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 4.14]
. In the sequel, we will use the abbreviation that
\partial=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
. Recall that
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is equipped with the measure
\frac{dt}{t}
. Also recall our convention that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup of regular operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
and
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
its subordinated Poisson semigroup.

We first show part (i) concerning the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Fix
\frac{\pi}{4}<\alpha<\beta<\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Let
\Gamma
be the boundary of
\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Define
F(z)=-ze^{-z}
. Then
F\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
. For any
t>0
we have

(3.1)

t\partial P_{t}=F(t\sqrt{A}\,)=\frac{1}{2\pi\textrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}F(tz)R(z)dz,

where
R(z)=(z-\sqrt{A}\,)^{-1}
. Recall that the map
\mathcal{P}
is defined by (
1.8
) (with
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
instead of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
there). It can be rewritten as

(3.2)

\mathcal{P}(h)_{s}=\frac{2}{\pi\textrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(sz)F(tz)zR(z)(h_{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{dz}{z}\,,\quad s>0.

Let
\Gamma
be equipped with the measure
\frac{|dz|}{|z|}
. We define three maps as follows:

\bullet

\Phi_{1}:L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))\to L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))
by

\Phi_{1}(h)_{z}=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(tz)h_{t}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,\quad z\in\Gamma,\;h\in L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X)),

\bullet

\Phi_{2}:L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))\to L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))
by

\Phi_{2}(g)_{s}=\int_{\Gamma}F(sz)g_{z}\,\frac{dz}{z}\,,\quad s>0,\;g\in L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))

\bullet

\Phi:L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))\to L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))
by

\Phi(g)_{z}=\frac{2}{\pi\textrm{i}}\,zR(z)(g_{z}),\quad z\in\Gamma,\;g\in L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X)).

Then
\mathcal{P}=\Phi_{2}\Phi\Phi_{1}
. Thus it remains to show that the three newly defined maps are all bounded. Consider first the case
q<\infty
. By the Hölder inequality, we have

\big\|\Phi_{1}(h)_{z}\big\|_{X}^{q}\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(tz)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{q-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(tz)|\,\big\|h_{t}\big\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Note that for
z=re^{\pm{\rm i}\alpha}\in\Gamma

\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(tz)|\,\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(te^{\pm{\rm i}\alpha})|\,\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{1}{\cos\alpha}\,.

On the other hand, for any
t>0

\int_{\Gamma}|F(tz)|\frac{|dz|}{|z|}=\frac{2}{\cos\alpha}\,.

We then deduce that

\big\|\Phi_{1}(h)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}(\Gamma;X))}\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\cos\alpha}\,\big\|h\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}\,.

Thus

\big\|\Phi_{1}\big\|\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\cos\alpha}\,.

The same upper estimate holds for
\big\|\Phi_{2}\big\|
too.
Finally, the boundedness of
\Phi
is just a reformulation of the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness of
\big\{\frac{2}{\pi\textrm{i}}\,zR(z):z\in\Gamma\setminus\{0\}\big\}
. Thus by Proposition
2.8
,

\big\|\mathcal{P}\big\|\lesssim\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}).

This finishes the proof of the first assertion for
q<\infty
(choosing
\alpha
close to
\frac{\pi}{4}
). The boundedness of
\mathcal{P}
for
q=\infty
is obtained from that for
q=1
by duality.

Let us show that
\mathcal{P}
is a projection. Let
h\in L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))
be given by
h_{t}=t\partial P_{t}(f)
for some
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Then by (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(h)_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi\textrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(sz)F(tz)F(tz)R(z)(f)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi\textrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}F(sz)R(z)(f)dz\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(t))^{2}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\textrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}F(sz)R(z)(f)dz

\displaystyle=F(s\sqrt{A}\,)(f)=s\partial P_{s}(f).

Thus
\mathcal{P}(h)=h
, so
\mathcal{P}
is a projection. This shows the first assertion (i).

Assertion (ii) on the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself is proved exactly in the same way. Indeed, letting
\alpha_{q}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta_{q}
, by Proposition
2.9
,
A
is
\ell_{q}
-sectorial of type
\alpha_{q}
. Let
\beta=\frac{\beta_{q}}{2}
and
\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta
. Then
\alpha_{q}<\alpha<\frac{\pi}{2}
and

\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}\approx\frac{1}{\beta_{q}^{2}},\quad\frac{1}{(\beta_{q}-\beta)^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{\beta_{q}^{2}}.

Thus using the estimate on the
\ell_{q}
-sectoriality constant of

and repeating the above argument, we show that
\mathcal{T}
is bounded with the announced norm estimate.

4.
Luzin type and cotype

In this section we study Banach spaces that are of Luzin coype or type. Before proceeding we briefly discuss the projection
\mathsf{F}
onto the fixed point subspace of

\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
(equivalently, of
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
). By the mean ergodic theorem,
\mathsf{F}
is given by

\mathsf{F}(f)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(f)ds,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Thus
\mathsf{F}
is also regular, so extends to a contractive projection on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Then the above formula remains valid for
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
\mathsf{F}(L_{p}(\Omega;X))
coincides with the fixed point subspace of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. It follows that
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
admits the following direct sum decomposition:

(4.1)

L_{p}(\Omega;X)=\mathsf{F}(L_{p}(\Omega;X))\oplus\ker\mathsf{F}.

On the other hand,
\ker\mathsf{F}
is the closure of
\big\{({\rm Id}-T_{t})(L_{p}(\Omega;X)):t>0\big\}
. Moreover,

(4.2)

\mathsf{F}(L_{p}(\Omega;X))=\ker A=\ker\sqrt{A}\;\text{ and }\;\ker\mathsf{F}=\overline{{\rm im}A}=\overline{{\rm im}\sqrt{A}}.

By the paragraph before Lemma
2.6
, we know that the adjoint semigroup
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is regular on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
too. Thus the above discussion also applies to the semigroup
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
that is extended to
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
again. Consequently, (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) transfer to this dual setting. We should draw the reader’s attention to the fact that
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
is in general not the dual of
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
but an isometric subspace. With this in mind, we have

{\rm Id}_{X^{*}}\otimes T_{t}^{*}=\big({\rm Id}_{X}\otimes T_{t})^{*}\Big|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})}\,.

A similar formula holds for the negative generator
A
of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and the negative generator
A^{*}
of
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
, that is, the restriction to
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
of the adjoint of the former coincides with the latter. Moreover,
\mathsf{F}^{*}\big|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})}
is the fixed point projection associated to
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
. All this allows us to use duality arguments without any problem as when
L_{p}(\Omega;X)^{*}=L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
(which is the case for reflexive
X
).

According to
[
64
]
, we introduce the following definition already mentioned before Theorem
1.2
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

Define

(4.3)

\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t\partial T_{t}(f)\|^{q}_{X}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

(ii)

X
is said to be of
Luzin cotype
q
relative to

\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
if there exists a constant
c
such that

(4.4)

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

The smallest
c
is denoted by
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
.

(iii)

X
is said to be of
Luzin type
q
relative to

\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
if there exists a constant
c
such that

(4.5)

\big\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq c\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

The smallest
c
is denoted by
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
.

In (

),
f
is implicitly assumed to belong to the definition domain of
A
in order to guarantee the derivability of
T_{t}(f)
in
t
.
Note that if
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
,
\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)
is defined for any

. When it is defined,
\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)
is a positive measurable function on
\Omega
but may not belong to
L_{p}(\Omega)
in which case
\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}
is interpreted as
\infty
(then (
4.5
) is trivially verified for such
f
). On the other hand, the above definition implicitly depends on
p
, but this dependence is not essential thanks to the fact that in most cases, if (
4.4
) or (
4.5
) holds for one
p
, so it does for any allowed
p
. Thus to lighten the terminology, we have decided to not explicitly mention
p
in the above notions; anyway, this dependence on
p
is reflected in the constants
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
and
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Without additional assumption on
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, the definition may be insignificant. For instance, if
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the translation group of
{\mathbb{R}}
, it is easy to check that

\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|t\partial T_{t}(f)|^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|^{p}_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|tf^{\prime}(s+t)|^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}ds=\infty

for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
and for any
f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
with
f^{\prime}
not identically zero. Thus
{\mathbb{C}}
is not of Luzin cotype
q
for any
q
relative to the translation group of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

This remark shows that to have a meaningful theory of Luzin type and cotype some minimal condition should be imposed to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
. It turns out that this minimal condition is the analyticity of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. As shown in section
2
, the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
always satisfies this condition.

It is sometimes convenient to have a discrete version of
\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)
. Recall that if
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega)
, we have the following maximal inequality

(4.6)

\big\|\sup_{t>0}|T_{t}(f)|\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\mathsf{T}_{\max}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega)

for some constant
\mathsf{T}_{\max}
(see
[
38
]
). A similar inequality holds for the adjoint semigroup
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, the relevant constant being denoted by
\mathsf{T}^{*}_{\max}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
a>1
. Then for any
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)

c_{T,q,a}^{-1}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|a^{k}\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{T,q,a}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,

where

\displaystyle c_{T,q,a}=q^{-\frac{1}{q}}(a^{q}-1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}),

\displaystyle C_{T,q,a}=q^{\frac{1}{q}}(1-a^{-q})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\,.

Similar inequalities hold for
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
in place of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
without any additional assumption on
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, the corresponding constants being given by

\displaystyle c_{P,q,a}=C^{-1}q^{-\frac{1}{q}}(a^{q}-1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}),

\displaystyle C_{P,q,a}=Cq^{\frac{1}{q}}(1-a^{-q})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\,.

Proof.

Using (
4.6
), its adjoint version and repeating the proof of Lemma
2.6
, we show that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with constant
\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})
.
Write

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)^{q}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{a^{k}}^{a^{k+1}}\big\|t\partial T_{t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\frac{dt}{t}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{1}^{a}\big\|a^{k}t\partial T_{a^{k}t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Using
\partial T_{t+s}=T_{s}\partial T_{t}
, we have
\partial T_{a^{k}t}(f)=T_{a^{k}(t-1)}\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)
. Then the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
yields

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{1}^{a}\big\|a^{k}t\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=q^{-\frac{1}{q}}(a^{q}-1)^{\frac{1}{q}}\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|a^{k}\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

For the converse inequality, we write

\big\|a^{k}\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}=q(1-a^{-q})^{-1}\int_{a^{-1}}^{1}\big\|a^{k}tT_{a^{k}(1-t)}\partial T_{a^{k}t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\,.

As above, we then deduce

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|a^{k}\partial T_{a^{k}}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq q^{\frac{1}{q}}(1-a^{-q})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\max(\mathsf{T}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,{\mathsf{T}^{*}}_{\max}^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

The second part on
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
is just a particular case with
\mathsf{P}_{\max}=Cp^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{P}^{*}_{\max}=Cp
by virtue of Remark
2.7
.
∎

Recall the classical fact that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
iff
\{t\partial T_{t}:t>0\}
is uniformly bounded on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Thus the following remark immediately follows from the above result; it shows in particular that the analyticity of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
is necessary for
X
to be of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
for some
q
.

Remark 4.4
.

Assume that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega)
.
If
X
is of Luzin cotype
(
resp. type
)

q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, then
X
is of Luzin cotype
(
resp. type
)

r
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
for any
r>q

(
resp.
r<q)
. Moreover, if
X
is of Luzin cotype
\infty
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, then
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
must be analytic on

.

The following is one of the main results of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X^{*}
is of Luzin type
q^{\prime}
relative to
\{P^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

Moreover, the relevant constants satisfy

\mathsf{L}^{P^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\lesssim\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\mathsf{L}^{P^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\,.

(ii)

Assume additionally that
1<q<\infty
and
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies (
1.4
) for some
0<\beta_{0}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X^{*}
is of Luzin type
q^{\prime}
relative to
\{T^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Moreover, the relevant constants satisfy

\mathsf{L}^{T^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\lesssim\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\beta_{q}^{-4}\,\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}^{\min(\frac{p}{q},\,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})}\,\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\,\mathsf{L}^{T^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})

with
\beta_{q}=\beta_{0}\min(\frac{p}{q},\,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

(i) Assume that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
. Let
g\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
with
\mathsf{F^{*}}(g)=0
. Let
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. We want to estimate

\langle f,\,g\rangle
, where the duality bracket is that between
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
. By (
4.1
) and its dual version, we can assume
\mathsf{F}(f)=0
, which, together with (
4.2
), implies that
f\in\ker\mathsf{F}=\overline{{\rm im}\sqrt{A}}
. With
F(z)=-ze^{-z}
and by (
2.1
) we have

f=4\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t\sqrt{A}\,)F(t\sqrt{A}\,)(f)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Thus by the Hölder inequality and the Luzin cotype
q
of
X

\displaystyle\big|\langle f,\,g\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=4\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle F(t\sqrt{A}\,)(f),\,F(t\sqrt{A^{*}})(g)\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P^{*}}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 4\,\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\,\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P^{*}}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\,.

Taking the supremum over
f
with norm 1, we show that
X^{*}
is of Luzin type
q^{\prime}
with

\mathsf{L}^{P^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\leq 4\,\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X).

To show the converse implication, let
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
h\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X^{*}))
(recalling that
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is equipped with
\frac{dt}{t}
). We have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle t\partial P_{t}(f),\,h_{t}\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle f,\,t\partial P^{*}_{t}(h_{t})\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}=\langle f,\,g\rangle,

where

g=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\partial P^{*}_{t}(h_{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}.

Applying Theorem
1.10
(i) to
\{P^{*}_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;X^{*})
, we have

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P^{*}}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\lesssim\max(p^{\prime 1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\big\|h\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X^{*}))}\,.

Combining the above inequalities, we get

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle t\partial P_{t}(f),\,h_{t}\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}\right|\lesssim\max(p^{\prime 1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\mathsf{L}^{P^{*}}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\,\big\|h\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X^{*}))}\,,

which implies the Luzin cotype
q
of
X
with

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max(p^{\prime 1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\mathsf{L}^{P^{*}}_{t,q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\,.

(ii) The proof of this part is similar by using Theorem
1.10
(ii).
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Any Banach space
X
is of Luzin type
1
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
, so relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
too. If
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
, then
X
is of Luzin cotype
\infty
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, so
X
is always of Luzin cotype
\infty
relative to the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

Proof.

Indeed, let
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
such that
\mathsf{F}(f)=0
. Then by (
4.2
) and (
2.1
) we have

f=-\int_{0}^{\infty}t\partial P_{t}(f)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,

whence

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{1}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

Thus
X
is of Luzin type
1
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
, hence also relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
by virtue of Remark
1.1
. Passing to duality by means of Theorem
4.5
, we see that
X
is of Luzin cotype
\infty
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
under the analyticity assumption of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
.
∎

The following formulation of the Littlewood-Paley function
\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)
by discrete lacunary differences is of interest in its own right.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
a>1
.

(i)

X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff there exists a constant
c
such that

(4.7)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t})(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}

for all
1\leq t\leq a
and
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Moreover, the best
c
and
\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)
are linked by

(\log a)^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}c\leq\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim(\log a)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\frac{a+1}{a-1}\,\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})c.

(ii)

X
is of Luzin type
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff there exists a constant
c
such that

(4.8)

\big\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq c\Big\|\Big(\int_{1}^{a}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t})(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

for all
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Moreover, the best
c
and
\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
are linked by

\frac{a-1}{a+1}\big(\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\big)^{-1}c\ \lesssim\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim(a-1)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\log a)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}c.

(iii)

Similar statements hold for
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
under the additional assumption that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
1<q<\infty
.

Proof.

(i) We have

\big\|(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t})(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}=\Big\|\int_{a^{k}t}^{a^{k+1}t}\partial P_{s}(f)ds\Big\|_{X}^{q}\leq(\log a)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\int_{a^{k}t}^{a^{k+1}t}\big\|s\partial P_{s}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\frac{ds}{s}\,,

which implies

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t})(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\leq(\log a)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)^{q}\,.

Thus if
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
, then (
4.7
) holds with

c\leq(\log a)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\mathsf{L}^{P}_{c,q,p}(X)\,.

To show the converse implication, let
b=\frac{1}{2}(1+a)
. We use an idea from
[
27
]
(see also
[
66
]
) to write

\partial P_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(\partial P_{b^{k}t}-\partial P_{b^{k+1}t}\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\partial P_{b^{k}2^{-1}t}\big(P_{b^{k}2^{-1}t}-P_{ab^{k}2^{-1}t}\big).

Hence

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\big\{t\partial P_{b^{k}2^{-1}t}\big(P_{b^{k}2^{-1}t}-P_{ab^{k}2^{-1}t}\big)(f)\big\}_{t>0}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b^{-k}\Big\|\big\{t\partial P_{t}\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\}_{t>0}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(a+1)}{a-1}\Big\|\big\{t\partial P_{t}\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\}_{t>0}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}\,.

By Propositions
2.4
and
2.8
, the family
\{t\partial P_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
\ell_{q}
-bounded with constant
C\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})
. Therefore,

\Big\|\big\{t\partial P_{t}\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\}_{t>0}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}\lesssim\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\Big\|\big\{\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\}_{t>0}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}\,.

To estimate the norm on the right hand side, we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{a^{k}}^{a^{k+1}}\big\|\big(P_{t}-P_{at}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{a}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Note that the function

t\mapsto\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

is continuous from
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
to
L_{p}(\Omega)
, so there exists
t_{0}\in[1,\,a]
such that

\int_{1}^{a}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq(\log a)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(P_{a^{k}t_{0}}-P_{a^{k+1}t_{0}}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,.

We then deduce the Luzin cotype
q
of
X
from (
4.7
) with

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim(\log a)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\frac{a+1}{a-1}\,\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})c.

(ii) The above argument yields the following discretization of
\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)
:

\displaystyle\frac{a-1}{a+1}

\displaystyle\big(\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\big)^{-1}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\;\;\lesssim\Big\|

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{a}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(P_{a^{k}t}-P_{a^{k+1}t}\big)(f)\big\|^{q}_{X}\,dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq(a-1)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\log a)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

This immediately implies assertion (ii).

(iii) is proved similarly by virtue of Propositions
2.4
and
2.9
.
∎

We have seen that the proofs of Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
1.10
are based on functional calculus for the special function
F(z)=-ze^{-z}
. It is known that functional calculus allows us to use more general functions.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
B
be an
\ell_{q}
-sectorial operator of type
\alpha
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
with
\alpha<\pi
. Let
\beta>\alpha
and
\varphi\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
be a nonzero function. Define

\mathcal{G}^{B}_{q,\varphi}(f)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|\varphi(tB)(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

The following result is a variant of
[
42
, Theorem 5]
(see also
[
66
, Lemma 20]
).

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two nonzero functions in
H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\beta})
. Then

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{B}_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{B,q,\varphi,\psi}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{B}_{q,\psi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

Proof.

Let

a=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(t)^{2}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Then

1=\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tz)^{2}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,\quad z\in\Sigma_{\beta}.

Combined with (
2.1
), this implies

f=\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tB)^{2}(f)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,\quad f\in\overline{{\rm im}B},

whence

\varphi(sB)(f)=\frac{1}{a}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(sB)\psi(tB)\big(\psi(tB)(f)\big)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,\quad s>0.

Let
\alpha<\gamma<\beta
and
\Gamma
be the boundary of
\Sigma_{\gamma}
. We then deduce

\varphi(sB)(f)=\frac{1}{2a\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(sz)\psi(tz)z(z-B)^{-1}\big(\psi(tB)(f)\big)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{dz}{z}\,.

These equalities are the analogues of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) with
h_{t}=\psi(tB)(f)
. It remains to repeat the proof of Theorem
1.10
to conclude

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{B}_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2|a|\pi}\,C_{B,\gamma}C_{\varphi,\gamma}C_{\psi,\gamma}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{B}_{q,\psi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,

where
C_{B,\gamma}
is the
\ell_{q}
-boundedness constant of
\big\{z(z-B)^{-1}:z\in\Gamma\setminus\{0\}\big\}
,

C_{\varphi,\gamma}=\max_{\varepsilon=\pm 1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi(te^{{\rm i}\varepsilon\gamma})|\,\frac{dt}{t}

and
C_{\psi,\gamma}
is similarly defined.
∎

In particular, combining the previous proposition with the results in section
2
, we obtain the following

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff for every nonzero
\varphi\in H_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\beta})
with
\beta>\pi/4

(
equivalently, for some nonzero
\varphi\in H_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\beta}))
there exists a constant

such that

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\sqrt{A}}_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

(ii)

X
is of Luzin type
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff for every nonzero
\varphi\in H_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\beta})
with
\beta>\pi/4

(
equivalently, for some nonzero
\varphi\in H_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\beta}))
there exists a constant

such that

\big\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq c\,\big\|\mathcal{G}^{\sqrt{A}}_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X).

(iii)

Similar statements hold for

when

is analytic on

and

.

We conclude this section by some remarks on general

-sectorial operators on

for which we have defined the

. In fact, what we have done so far for semigroups can be developed for these operators too.

.

(ii)

.

Under the above assumption,

Proof.

Noting that Theorem

∎

5.
Dyadic martingales and singular integrals

The proofs of Theorem
1.2
(i) and Theorem
1.5
heavily rely on tools from harmonic analysis, notably from modern real-variable Littlewood-Paley theory. This section is a preparation for using these tools. We will mainly follow Wilson’s beautiful treatment in
[
62
]
(see also
[
60
,
61
]
) for the part on Littlewood-Paley theory.

5.1.
Dyadic martingales

All cubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
considered in the sequel are bounded and with sides parallel to the axes.
|Q|
and
\ell(Q)
denote respectively the volume and side length of a cube
Q
;
tQ
stands for the cube with the same center as
Q
and
\ell(tQ)=t\ell(Q)
for
t>0
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be the family of all dyadic cubes of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}\subset{\mathcal{D}}
the subfamily of all cubes with side length
2^{-k}
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
the associated conditional expectation. For
f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
,

{\mathbb{E}}_{k}(f)=\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\,\int_{Q}f\Big){\mathds{1}}_{Q}.

Let
d_{k}(f)={\mathbb{E}}_{k}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{k-1}(f)
and

S_{q}(f)=\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|d_{k}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

S_{q}(f)
is the
q
-variant of the usual martingale square function of
f
. It is useful to note that
d_{k}(f)
is of vanishing mean on every
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k-1}
and constant on every
R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
.

Thus if
X
is of martingale type
q
, then

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)\big\|S_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\,,\quad f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X).

We will need
dyadic-like families of cubes
that Wilson calls good families.
{\mathcal{F}}
is such a family if

a)

for
Q\in{\mathcal{F}}
, all its
2^{d}
immediate dyadic subcubes belong to
{\mathcal{F}}
too;

b)

every
Q\in{\mathcal{F}}
is one of the
2^{d}
immediate dyadic subcubes of another one in
{\mathcal{F}}
;

c)

for all
Q,R\in{\mathcal{F}}
,
Q\subset R
, or
R\subset Q
, or
Q\cap R=\emptyset
.

For
{\mathcal{F}}
a dyadic-like family of cubes, we define the associated
S_{q,{\mathcal{F}}}
:

S_{q,{\mathcal{F}}}(f)=\Big(\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}}\,\sum_{R\in{\mathcal{F}},\,R\subset Q,\,\ell(R)=\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}}\big\|\frac{1}{|R|}\,\int_{R}f-\frac{1}{|Q|}\,\int_{Q}f\big\|^{q}_{X}\,{\mathds{1}}_{R}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It is easy to see that given a finite number of cubes in
{\mathcal{F}}
, we can bring the subfamily consisting of those cubes in
{\mathcal{F}}
that are contained in one of the given cubes to a subfamily of
{\mathcal{D}}
after appropriate translation and rescaling. Thus we have the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a dyadic-like family of cubes. If
X
is of martingale type
q
, then

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)\big\|S_{q,{\mathcal{F}}}(f)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

for all
f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
supported by cubes from
{\mathcal{F}}
.

An important case needed later concerns the family
\{3Q\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}
. The following is due to Wilson
[
60
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The family
\{3Q\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}
is a disjoint union of
3^{d}
dyadic-like families.

It suffices to consider the case
d=1
. Then every
3Q
can be written in the form
[\frac{3j+s}{2^{k}},\,\frac{3(j+1)+s}{2^{k}})
with
j,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
s\in\{0,1,2\}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{s}
be the collection of all such intervals for given
k
and
s
. Then the desired union is

\cup_{s=0}^{2}\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{2^{|k|}s\;{\rm mod}\;3}
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a dyadic-like family of cubes and
\delta>0
. Consider a family
\{a_{Q}\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}}
of
X
-valued functions satisfying the following conditions:

(5.1)

{\rm supp}(a_{Q})\subset Q,\quad\int a_{Q}=0\;\text{ and }\;\|a_{Q}(x)-a_{Q}(y)\|_{X}\leq|Q|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\,\big(\frac{|x-y|}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}.

These are
smooth atoms
. Let
\{\lambda_{Q}\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}}
be a finite family of complex numbers and
f=\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}}\lambda_{Q}\,a_{Q}
.

The following is the adaptation of a lemma due to Wilson to the present setting. We include its proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the above assumption, we have

S_{q,{\mathcal{F}}}(f)\lesssim_{d,\delta}\Big(\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\,{\mathds{1}}_{Q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{D}}
, so
S_{q,{\mathcal{F}}}(f)=S_{q}(f)
. Since
a_{Q}
is of vanishing mean,
d_{k}(a_{Q})=0
whenever
k\leq k(Q)
, where
2^{-k(Q)}=\ell(Q)
. Let
R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k-1}
with
k>k(Q)
and
R\subset Q
. Then on
R
,

\displaystyle d_{k}(a_{Q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k},\,I\subset R}\big(\frac{1}{|I|}\,\int_{I}a_{Q}-\frac{1}{|R|}\,\int_{R}a_{Q}\big){\mathds{1}}_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k},\,I\subset R}\frac{1}{|I|\,|R|}\,\int_{I\times R}\big(a_{Q}(x)-a_{Q}(y)\big)dx\,dy\,{\mathds{1}}_{I}.

Thus by the last condition of (
5.1
),

\|d_{k}(a_{Q})\|_{X}\lesssim_{d}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\,\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta};

hence on
R
,

\displaystyle\|d_{k}(f)\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\Big(\sum_{Q:Q\supset R}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\,\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\sum_{Q:Q\supset R}\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\delta}\Big(\sum_{Q:Q\supset R}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\,\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle S_{q}(f)^{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\sum_{R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k-1}}\|d_{k}(f)\|^{q}_{X}\,{\mathds{1}}_{R}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\delta}\sum_{R:x\in R}\,\sum_{Q:Q\supset R}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\,\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}\,{\mathds{1}}_{R}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\delta}\sum_{Q}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\;\sum_{R:x\in R\subset Q}\,\big(\frac{\ell(R)}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}\,{\mathds{1}}_{R}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\delta}\sum_{Q}\frac{|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}}{|Q|}\,{\mathds{1}}_{Q}(x)\,.

This gives the desired assertion.
∎

5.2.
Singular integrals

Given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be the class of all integrable functions
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfying (
1.5
). Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. We consider the vector-valued kernel
K
defined by
K(x)=\{\varphi_{t}(x)\}_{t>0}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, that is,
K
is a function from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
. With a slight abuse of notation, we use
K
to denote the associated singular integral too:

K(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(x-y)f(y)dy.

Then

G_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)=\big\|K(f)(x)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X)}\,,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Lemma 5.4
.

The kernel
K
satisfies the regularity properties:

\big\|K(x)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\;\text{ and }\;\big\|K(x+y)-K(x)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{|y|^{\delta}}{|x|^{d+\delta}},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\;|x|>2|y|.

Proof.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Then by (
1.5
),

\displaystyle\big\|K(x)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi_{t}(x)|^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[\frac{1}{t^{d}}\,\frac{1}{\big(1+\frac{|x|}{t}\big)^{d+\varepsilon}}\big]^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{dq}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\varepsilon q}}{(1+t)^{(d+\varepsilon)q}}\,\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|x|^{dq}}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\big\|K(x+y)-K(x)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|^{\delta q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[\frac{1}{t^{d+\delta}}\,\frac{1}{\big(1+\frac{|x|}{t}\big)^{d+\varepsilon+\delta}}\big]^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{|y|^{\delta q}}{|x|^{(d+\delta)q}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\varepsilon q}}{(1+t)^{(d+\varepsilon+\delta)q}}\,\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{|y|^{\delta q}}{|x|^{(d+\delta)q}}.

This lemma is proved. ∎

5.3.
A quasi-orthogonal decomposition

Beside
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
introduced in the previous subsection, we will need its subclass of functions supported in the unit ball. More precisely, let
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\delta}
be the class of integrable functions
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

(5.2)

{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset B(0,1),\quad|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq|x-y|^{\delta},\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)dx=0.

Here
B(x,t)
denotes the ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with center
x
and radius
t
.

Any function in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
can be decomposed into a series of functions in
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\delta}
thanks to the following lemma due to Uchiyama
[
57
]
(see also
[
61
]
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. Then there exist a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and a sequence of functions
\psi^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\delta}
such that

\varphi=C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-\varepsilon k}(\psi^{(k)})_{2^{k}}\,.

Proof.

The proof is elementary. Let
\eta
be a smooth function supported in
\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\frac{1}{2}<|x|<2\}
such that

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\eta(2^{-k}x)=1,\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

Define

\rho_{0}(x)=\sum_{j\leq-1}\eta(2^{-j}|x|),\quad\rho_{k}(x)=\eta(2^{-k+1}|x|)\;\text{ for}\;k\geq 1

and

\zeta_{k}=\frac{\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\rho_{j}\varphi}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\rho_{k}}\,\rho_{k}\,.

Then the desired decomposition is given by

\varphi=(\varphi\rho_{0}-\zeta_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\varphi\rho_{k}-\zeta_{k}+\zeta_{k-1})=C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-\varepsilon k}(\psi^{(k)})_{2^{k}}.

∎

By our convention that
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is equipped with the measure
\frac{dt}{t}
, the upper half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}
is equipped with the product measure
\frac{dx\,dt}{t}
. Consistent with our convention before, we write a function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}\to X
as
h(x,t)=h_{t}(x)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
h\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X)
with compact support. Consider the following function

g(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\varphi_{t}(y-x)h_{t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}.

We will decompose
g
into a series of smooth atoms

g=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}a_{i},

where the
a_{i}
’s satisfy (
5.1
) relative to
\{3Q\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}
and the
\lambda_{i}
’s are reals such that

\Big(\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\big\|h\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X)}.

This is the so-called the
quasi-orthogonal decomposition
of
g
. First, using Lemma
5.5
, we reduce our problem to the case where
\varphi
is supported in the unit ball:

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-\varepsilon k}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}(\psi^{(k)})_{2^{k}t}(y-x)h_{t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}

\displaystyle=C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-\varepsilon k}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}(\psi^{(k)})_{t}(y-x)h_{2^{-k}t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}.

Note that
h_{2^{-k}\,\cdot}
has the same norm as
h
in
L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X)
. Thus it suffices to do the decomposition for each
\psi^{(k)}
in place of
\varphi
.

In the following, we will assume that
\varphi
itself belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\delta}
. The argument below is classical. For
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}
, let
T_{Q}=\{(y,t):y\in Q,\;\frac{\ell(Q)}{2}<t\leq\ell(Q)\}
. Then
\{T_{Q}\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}
is a partition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}
. So

g(x)=\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}\int_{T_{Q}}\varphi_{t}(y-x)h_{t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}\;{\mathop{=}^{\rm def}}\,\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}\lambda_{Q}a_{Q}(x)

with

\lambda_{Q}=\Big(\int_{T_{Q}}\|h_{t}(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Clearly,

\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}}|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\|h_{t}(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}=\big\|h\big\|^{q}_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X)}.

Since
\varphi
is supported in the unit ball and of vanishing mean, we see that
a_{Q}
is supported in
3Q
and of vanishing mean too.
On the other hand, since
\varphi
is in the Hölder class
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\delta}
, by the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\|a_{Q}(x)-a_{Q}(x^{\prime})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{T_{Q}}|\varphi_{t}(y-x)-\varphi_{t}(y-x^{\prime})|^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{T_{Q}}\big[\frac{1}{t^{d}}\,\big(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{t}\big)^{\delta}\big]^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\delta}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\,\big(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\ell(Q)}\big)^{\delta}.

Thus
a_{Q}
is a smooth atom. This yields the desired quasi-orthogonal decomposition.

Combining the above discussion with Lemmas
5.1
–
5.3
, we get the following

Lemma 5.6
.

Keep the above notation and assume that
X
is of martingale type
q
. Then

\big\|g\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)\,\big\|h\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X)}.

6.
Proofs of Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6

With the preparation in section
5
, we are in a position to show Theorem
1.5
.
For clarity, we divide the proof into several steps.
X
will be assumed of martingale cotype
q
in the first four steps, and of martingale type
q
in the last step.

Step 1: A weighted norm inequality
. Let
\varphi
be a function satisfying (
1.5
), i.e.,
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. Beside the
g
-function defined by (
1.7
), we will need the Luzin integral function

S_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)=\Big(\int_{|y-x|<t}\big\|\varphi_{t}*f(y)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{d+1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for nice
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to X
. The key of this proof is the following weighted norm inequality:

For any locally integrable nonnegative function
w
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and any
f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)

(6.1)

\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(S_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}w(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\|f(x)\big\|_{X}^{q}M(w)(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
M(w)
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
w
:

M(w)(x)=\sup\Big\{\frac{1}{|B|}\,\int_{B}w:x\in B,B\text{ ball}\Big\}.

First consider the unweighted case, i.e.,
w\equiv 1
.
By the Fubini theorem, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(S_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}dx=c_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\|\varphi_{t}*f(x)\|^{q}_{X}\,\frac{dx\,dt}{t}\,.

Let
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}\to X^{*}
be a compactly supported smooth function such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\|h_{t}(x)\|^{q^{\prime}}_{X^{*}}\,\frac{dx\,dt}{t}\leq 1.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\langle\varphi_{t}*f(y),\,h_{t}(y)\rangle\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle f(x),\,g(x)\rangle dx\,,

where

g(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\varphi_{t}(y-x)h_{t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}.

Recall that it is well known (and easy to check) that
X
is of martingale type
q
iff
X^{*}
is of martingale cotype
q^{\prime}
with the following relation between the relevant constants:

(6.2)

\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)\leq\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q^{\prime}}(X^{*})\leq 2\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X).

Thus applying Lemma
5.6
to
h
and
g
with
(X^{*},q^{\prime})
in place of
(X,q)
there, we get

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q^{\prime}}(X^{*})\,\big\|h\big\|_{L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X).

Then taking the supremum over all
h
in the unit ball of
L_{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})
, we deduce

\big\|S_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\big\|f\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}.

Namely, the unweighted version of (
6.1
) holds.

We will deduce the weighted version by a trick from
[
11
]
. By Lemma
5.5
, we can assume that
\varphi
is supported in the unit ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Given a weight
w
, we write (recalling that
B(y,t)
denotes the ball of center
y
and radius
t
)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(S_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}w(x)dx=c_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\|\varphi_{t}*f(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\Big(\frac{1}{|B(y,t)|}\int_{B(y,t)}w(x)dx\Big)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}.

Let

F_{k}=\big\{(y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}:2^{k}<\frac{1}{|B(y,t)|}\int_{B(y,t)}w(x)dx\leq 2^{k+1}\big\},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Clearly,
(y,t)\in F_{k}
implies
B(y,t)\subset E_{k}=\{x:M(w)(x)>2^{k}\}
. Together with the fact that
\varphi_{t}(y-\cdot)
is supported in
B(y,t)
, this implies
\varphi_{t}*f(y)=\varphi_{t}*(f{\mathds{1}}_{E_{k}})(y)
whenever
(y,t)\in F_{k}
. Thus using the unweighted version already proved (applied to
f{\mathds{1}}_{E_{k}}
), we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(S_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k}\int_{F_{k}}\|\varphi_{t}*f(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k}\int_{F_{k}}\|\varphi_{t}*(f{\mathds{1}}_{E_{k}})(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)^{q}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k}\int_{E_{k}}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)^{q}\ \int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{q}M(w)(x)\,dx.

Thus (
6.1
) is proved.

Step 2: Another weighted norm inequality
. We need to show that (
6.1
) remains valid for
G_{q,\varphi}
instead of
S_{q,\varphi}
:

(6.3)

\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(G_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}w(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\|f(x)\big\|_{X}^{q}M(w)(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

To this end, we have to control the
g
-function by the Luzin area function. If
\varphi
is the Poisson kernel, this is a classical fact thanks to harmonicity. In the present setting, we need a little bit more efforts.

If additionally all partial derivatives of
\varphi
with order up to
d
belong to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, then we can show

G_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\lesssim_{d}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq d}S_{q,D^{\alpha}\varphi}(f)(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
D^{\alpha}=\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{d}}}{\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}
for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
and
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}
. The proof of this inequality is elementary (see
[
63
, Lemma 4.3]
).
Thus (
6.3
) holds for such
\varphi
.

For a general
\varphi
, we need to adapt the arguments of
[
61
]
to the present setting by introducing the vector-valued
q
-variants of Wilson’s intrinsic square functions:

\displaystyle S_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)(x)^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y-x|<t}\,\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\big\|\varphi_{t}*f(y)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t^{d+1}},

\displaystyle G_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)(x)^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\big\|\varphi_{t}*f(x)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}.

One can show, quite easily, that
S_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)(x)\approx_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}G_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)(x)
for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see
[
61
]
for more details).

On the other hand, for a compactly supported smooth function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}\to X^{*}
choose a family

\{\varphi^{(y,t)}\}_{(y,t)\in K}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
(
K
being the support of
h
) such that

\big\|\varphi^{(y,t)}_{t}*f(y)\big\|_{X}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\big\|\varphi_{t}*f(y)\big\|_{X}\,,\quad(y,t)\in K.

Then by adapting the arguments in subsection
5.3
to the present situation and by repeating step 1, one can estimate the integral

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\langle\varphi^{(y,t)}_{t}*f(y),\,h_{t}(y)\rangle\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle f(x),\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\varphi^{(y,t)}_{t}(y-x)h_{t}(y)\,\frac{dy\,dt}{t}\rangle dx\,

to conclude that

\big\|S_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{c,q}(X)\big\|f\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}.

This implies (
6.1
) with
S_{q,\psi}
replaced by
S_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}
by the passage from the unweighted case to the weighted one. Then the pointwise equivalence
S_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)\approx_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}G_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}(f)
shows that (
6.3
) holds for
G_{q,\varepsilon,\delta}
instead of
G_{q,\varphi}
, whence (
6.3
) for every
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
.

Step 3: Proof of Theorem
1.5
(i) for
p\geq q
. We can now easily prove part (i) of Theorem
1.5
for
p\geq q
. Indeed, the case
p=q
is just the unweighted version of (
6.3
). For
p>q
, let
w
be a nonnegative function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
L_{r}
-norm equal to 1, where
r
is the conjugate index of
\frac{p}{q}
. Then for
f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(G_{q,\varphi}(f)(x)\big)^{q}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)^{q}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\|f(x)\big\|_{X}^{q}M(w)(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)^{q}\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}^{q}\,\big\|M(w)\big\|_{L_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}r^{\prime}\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)^{q}\,\big\|f\big\|^{q}_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)}.

Taking the supremum over all
w
, we get

\big\|G_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)},

whence
\mathsf{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\mathbf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)
.

Step 4: Proof of Theorem
1.5
(i) for
p<q
. We deal with the case
p<q
by singular integrals. Let
K
be the singular integral associated to
\varphi
as in subsection
5.2
. We reduce to showing that
K
is bounded from
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
to
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))
. The previous step insures this boundedness for
p=q
. On the other hand, Lemma
5.4
shows that
K
is a regular Calderón-Zygmund kernel. Thus
K
satisfies the assumption of
[
16
, Theorem V.3.4]
. Note that
[
16
, Theorem V.3.4]
is formulated for kernels satisfying the regularities in Lemma
5.4
with
\delta=1
; however, it is well known that
[
16
, Theorem V.3.4]
remains valid for any kernel as in Lemma
5.4
with the same proof. Therefore,
K
is of weak type
(1,1)
, so by the vector-valued Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.3.1]
and its proof),
K
is bounded from
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
to
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))
with norm controlled by
C_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\,p^{\prime}\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)
for
1<p<q
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.5
(i).

Step 5: Proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii)
. In this last step we show part (ii) by duality. Let
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
such that (
1.6
) holds. Let
f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
and
g\in L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})
. Then (
1.6
) implies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle f(x),\,g(x)\rangle\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}}\langle\varphi_{t}*f(x),\,\psi_{t}*g(x)\rangle\,\frac{dxdt}{t}.

In the scalar case, this Calderón reproducing formula is proved by taking Fourier transforms of both sides. Then by linearity, the formula extends to the vector-valued case too when both
f
and
g
take values in finite dimensional subspaces, which can be assumed by approximation. Therefore,

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle f(x),\,g(x)\rangle\,dx\Big|\leq\big\|G_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\,\big\|G_{q^{\prime},\psi}(g)\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Since we are in part (ii) of Theorem
1.5
,
X
is of martingale type
q
, so
X^{*}
is of martingale cotype
q^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{M}_{c,q^{\prime}}(X^{*})\leq 2\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)
by (
6.2
). Thus by part (i) already proved, we have

\displaystyle\big\|G_{q^{\prime},\psi}(g)\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{L}^{\psi}_{\mathsf{c},q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(X^{*})\,\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\max\big((p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},\,p\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q^{\prime}}(X^{*})\,\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})}.

Hence

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle f(x),\,g(x)\rangle\,dx\Big|\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\max\big(p,\,(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)\,\big\|G_{q,\varphi}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\,\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})}.

Taking the supremum over all
g
with
\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X^{*})}\leq 1
, we deduce

\mathsf{L}^{\varphi}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim_{d,\varepsilon,\delta}\max\big(p,\,(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{t},q}(X)

as desired. So the proof of Theorem
1.5
is complete.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

The first part of this corollary immediately follows from Theorem
1.5
. The first two inequalities of the second part are consequences of Remark
1.1
and Proposition
8.5
below.
Finally, the last inequality
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,q}(X)\gtrsim\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)
is obtained by combining
[
41
]
and
[
64
]
.

∎

7.
Proofs of Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.9

In this section we will first prove Theorem
1.2
. Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.9
will then follow quite easily. Our strategy for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to reduce part (i), via transference, to the special case of the translation group to which we can apply Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Again, we divide this proof into several steps.
X
will be assumed to be of martingale cotype
q
in the first two steps, and of martingale type
q
in the last step.

Step 1: The case of the translation group.
For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
let
\tau_{t}
be the translation by
t
on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
, i.e.,
\tau_{t}(f)(s)=f(s+t)
. Then
\{\tau_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is a strongly continuous group of positive isometries on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
. As usual,
\{\tau_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
extends to a group of isometries on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
too. Let
\{P_{t}^{\tau}\}_{t>0}
be the associated Poisson subordinated semigroup. Our aim in this step is to show

(7.1)

\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X).

We need to express
t\partial P^{\tau}_{t}
as a convolution operator:

\sqrt{t}\,\partial P^{\tau}_{\sqrt{t}}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{t}(x-y)f(y)dy=\phi_{t}*f(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\;t>0.

Then

(7.2)

\mathcal{G}^{P^{\tau}}_{q}(f)(x)=2^{-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\phi_{t}*f(x)\|^{q}_{X}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=2^{-\frac{1}{q}}G_{q,\phi}(f)(x)\,.

Elementary computations show that
\phi
is the function with Fourier transform

(7.3)

\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=-\sqrt{-2\pi{\rm i}\,\xi}\,e^{-\sqrt{-2\pi{\rm i}\,\xi}\,}=-\sqrt{2\pi|\xi|}\,e^{-\frac{{\rm i}\,{\rm sgn}(\xi)\pi}{4}}\exp\big(-\sqrt{2\pi|\xi|}\,e^{-\frac{{\rm i}\,{\rm sgn}(\xi)\pi}{4}}\big)\,.

We are going to show that
\phi
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{H}}_{\frac{1}{2},1}
introduced in subsection
5.2
. More precisely,
\phi
satisfies the following estimates:

(7.4)

|\phi(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\;\text{ and }\;|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{5}{2}}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\xi^{k}\widehat{\phi}(\xi)
is integrable on
{\mathbb{R}}
for any nonnegative integer
k
,
\phi
is of class
C^{\infty}
with bounded derivatives of any order. Thus it suffices to prove the estimates for
|x|\geq 1
.

Let
\eta
be a
C^{\infty}
even function on
{\mathbb{R}}
, supported in
\{\xi:\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\}
, such that

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\eta(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}.

Let

m_{j}(\xi)=\widehat{\phi}(\xi)\eta(2^{-j}\xi)

and
\phi^{(j)}
be defined by
\widehat{\phi^{(j)}}=m_{j}
.
Then

\phi=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi^{(j)}.

Using (
7.3
), one easily shows

\int_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\,\widehat{\phi}(\xi)\Big|d\xi\lesssim 2^{j(1-k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\sqrt{2^{j}}\,)^{\ell+1}e^{-\sqrt{\pi 2^{j-1}}}\,,\quad 0\leq k\leq 3,

This implies

(7.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\,m_{j}(\xi)\Big|d\xi\lesssim 2^{j(1-k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\sqrt{2^{j}}\,)^{\ell+1}e^{-\sqrt{\pi 2^{j-1}}}\,,\quad 0\leq k\leq 3.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|x|>1
. We consider
j
according to two cases, I:
2^{j}|x|\leq 1
and II:
2^{j}|x|>1
.

In Case I, we must have
j\leq-1
. Using (
7.5
) for
k=1
, we then have

|x\phi^{(j)}(x)|\lesssim\sqrt{2^{j}}\,.

Thus

\sum_{j\in{\rm I}}|\phi^{(j)}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,.

On the other hand, if
j\in{\rm II}
, we use (
7.5
) for
k=3
to get

|x^{3}\phi^{(j)}(x)|\lesssim 2^{-\frac{3}{2}j}\,.

We deduce again

\sum_{j\in{\rm II}}|\phi^{(j)}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,.

Hence, the first estimate of (
7.4
) is proved.

The second is shown in a similar way. Indeed, since
\widehat{\phi^{\prime}}(\xi)=2\pi{\rm i}\,\xi\widehat{\phi}(\xi)
, we have

\int_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\,\widehat{\phi^{\prime}}(\xi)\Big|d\xi\lesssim 2^{j(2-k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(\sqrt{2^{j}}\,)^{\ell+1}e^{-\sqrt{\pi 2^{j-1}}}\,,\quad 0\leq k\leq 3,

It then remains to repeat the above argument with
\phi
replaced by
\phi^{\prime}
.

Thus by (
7.2
), Theorem
1.5
implies (
7.1
). Let us note that for the kernel
\phi
here, we can avoid Wilson’s intrinsic square functions considered in step 2 of the proof of Theorem
1.5
since
\phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\frac{1}{2},1}
too. Indeed, repeating the proof of (
7.4
), we show

|\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{7}{2}}}.

Consequently, as pointed out in step 2 of the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we have

G_{q,\phi}(f)(x)\lesssim S_{q,\phi}(f)(x)+S_{q,\phi^{\prime}}(f)(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The proof of this inequality is very easy. It suffices to consider
x=0
. Let
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|y|<t
. We write

\|\phi_{t}*f(y)\|^{q}_{X}-\|\phi_{t}*f(0)\|^{q}_{X}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\,\|\phi_{t}*f(sy)\|^{q}_{X}ds.

Then

\displaystyle\Big|\|\phi_{t}*f(y)\|^{q}_{X}-\|\phi_{t}*f(0)\|^{q}_{X}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq q\int_{0}^{1}\|\phi_{t}*f(sy)\|^{q-1}_{X}\,\|\phi^{\prime}_{t}*f(sy)\|_{X}\,\frac{|y|}{t}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq q\int_{0}^{|y|}\|\phi_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|^{q-1}_{X}\,\|\phi^{\prime}_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|_{X}\frac{ds}{t}

\displaystyle\leq q\int_{0}^{|y|}\big(\|\phi_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|^{q}_{X}+\|\phi^{\prime}_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|^{q}_{X}\big)\frac{ds}{t}

Thus

\displaystyle\|\phi_{t}*f(0)\|^{q}_{X}\leq\|\phi_{t}*f(y)\|^{q}_{X}+q\int_{0}^{|y|}\big(\|\phi_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|^{q}_{X}+\|\phi^{\prime}_{t}*f(s\frac{y}{|y|})\|^{q}_{X}\big)\frac{ds}{t}.

Integrating both sides against
\frac{dydt}{t^{2}}
over the cone
\{(y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}:|y|<t\}
, we deduce the desired inequality.

Step 2: Transference.
We now use the transference principle to bring the general case to the special one of the translation group. To that end, we first need to dilate our semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
to a group of isometries. Fendler’s dilation theorem is at our disposal for this purpose. It insures that there exist another larger measure space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mu})
, a strongly continuous group
\{\widetilde{T}_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
of regular isometries on
L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega})
, a positive isometric embedding
D
from
L_{p}(\Omega)
into
L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega})
and a regular projection
P
from
L_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega})
onto
L_{p}(\Omega)
such that

T_{t}=P\widetilde{T}_{t}D,\quad\forall\;t>0.

This theorem is proved in
[
17
]
for positive
T_{t}
and then extended to regular
T_{t}
in
[
18
]
.

To prove part (i) of Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to show

(7.6)

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\leq\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X).

By the above dilation, we can assume that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself is a group of regular isometries on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. So its extension to
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
is a group of isometries too. Recall that
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
denote the ergodic averages of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
in Lemma
2.6
. We use
\{M^{\tau}_{t}\}_{t>0}
to denote the corresponding averages of the translation group
\{\tau_{t}\}_{t>0}
. By (
1.2
), we have

P_{t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\big(-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}\big)\,T_{s}ds.

Thus

\displaystyle t\partial P_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\frac{t}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{t^{3}}{2s^{\frac{5}{2}}}\big)\exp\big(-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}\big)\,T_{s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\frac{t}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{t^{3}}{2s^{\frac{5}{2}}}\big)\exp\big(-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}\big)\,(sM_{s})^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(\frac{t}{\sqrt{s}})M_{s}\,\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M_{t^{2}s}\,\frac{ds}{s},

where

\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{16\sqrt{\pi}}\,(12x-12x^{3}+x^{5})e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}.

Let
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
be an element of norm
1
. Let
a>0
(large). Then

\int_{a}^{\infty}|\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})|\,\frac{ds}{s}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{a}}\,.

Thus for any
t>0

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{a}^{\infty}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M_{t^{2}s}(f)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}\leq\int_{a}^{\infty}|\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})|\big\|M_{t^{2}s}(f)\big\|_{X}\,\frac{ds}{s}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{a}}\,M^{*}(f),

where
M^{*}(f)=\sup_{v>0}\big\|M_{v}(f)\big\|_{X}
. By Lemma
2.6
,

\big\|M^{*}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq p^{\prime}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}=p^{\prime}.

Let
b
be another large number. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\big\|t\partial P_{t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\Big\|\int_{0}^{a}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M_{t^{2}s}(f)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{C_{p,q,b}}{\sqrt{a}},

where
C_{p,q,b}=Cp^{\prime}q^{-\frac{1}{q}}(2\log b)^{\frac{1}{q}}
. Denote the first term on the right hand side by I. Using the fact that
\{T_{t}\}
is a group of isometries on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
, we introduce an additional variable
u
in the integrand of I:

{\rm I}\leq\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\Big\|\int_{0}^{a}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M_{t^{2}s}T_{u}(f)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,,\quad u>0.

Let
c>0
. Now define
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to L_{p}(\Omega;X)
by
g(u)={\mathds{1}}_{(0,\,ab^{2}+c]}(u)T_{u}(f)
. We easily verify that

M_{t^{2}s}T_{u}(f)=M^{\tau}_{t^{2}s}(g)(u)\,,\quad 0<s\leq a,\;0<t\leq b,\;0<u\leq c.

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm I}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c}\int_{0}^{c}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\Big\|\int_{0}^{a}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M^{\tau}_{t^{2}s}(g)(u)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}d\omega\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c}\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\Big\|\int_{0}^{a}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M^{\tau}_{t^{2}s}(g)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|^{p}_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)}\,.

Let
(M^{\tau})^{*}(g)=\sup_{v>0}\big\|M^{\tau}_{v}(g)\big\|_{X}
, so

\big\|(M^{\tau})^{*}(g)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)}\leq p^{\prime}\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega;X)}\leq p^{\prime}(ab^{2}+c)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Reversing the preceding procedure with
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
replaced by
\{P^{\tau}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle\leq c^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}})M^{\tau}_{t^{2}s}(g)\,\frac{ds}{s}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)}+\frac{C_{p,q,b}}{\sqrt{a}}\,\Big(\frac{ab^{2}+c}{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{-\frac{1}{p}}\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P^{\tau}}_{q}(g)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega;X)}+\frac{C_{p,q,b}}{\sqrt{a}}\,\Big(\frac{ab^{2}+c}{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

However,

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P^{\tau}}_{q}(g)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)}\leq\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\big\|g\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega;X)}\leq\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)(ab^{2}+c)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

Combining all inequalities obtained so far, we finally deduce

\left\|\Big(\int_{b^{-1}}^{b}\big\|t\partial P_{t}(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\Big(\frac{ab^{2}+c}{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{C_{p,q,b}}{\sqrt{a}}\left(1+\Big(\frac{ab^{2}+c}{c}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\,.

Letting successively
c\to\infty
,
a\to\infty
and
b\to\infty
, we get

\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\mathsf{L}^{P^{\tau}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\,,

whence (
7.6
).

Step 3: Duality.
Assertion (ii) follows from (i), Theorem
4.5
and (
6.2
).
∎

Remark 7.1
.

The step 1 of the previous proof of Theorem

can be largely shortened for the case

instead of the optimal

Remark 7.2
.

The boundedness of
\mathcal{G}^{P}_{q}
on

can be proved by using Theorem
4.7
and the following inequality from
[
22
]
: For a Banach space
X

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(M^{\tau}_{2^{k}}-M^{\tau}_{2^{k+1}}\big)(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\big\|f\big\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}};X)}\,,\quad f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}};X).

By the discussion in the previous remark, the validity of the above inequality characterizes the martingale cotype
q
of

. More generally, let
1<p<\infty
and

. Then

such that

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\big(M^{\tau}_{2^{k}}-M^{\tau}_{2^{k+1}}\big)(f)\big\|_{X}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq c\,\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)}\,,\quad f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X).

See
[

]
for related results.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Remark
1.1
,
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)\lesssim\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},q,p}(X)
. Thus assertion (i) follows from Theorem
1.2
(ii).

Assertion (ii) is an easy consequence of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
4.10
. But we will use Proposition
4.9
in order to explicitly track the relevant constants. Let
\varphi(z)=-ze^{-z}
and
\psi(z)=-\sqrt{z}\,e^{-\sqrt{z}}
. Then

\mathcal{G}_{q,\varphi}^{A}(f)=\mathcal{G}_{q}^{T}(f)\quad\text{and }\quad\mathcal{G}_{q,\psi}^{A}(f)=\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{G}_{q}^{P}(f).

By Proposition
2.9
and the notation there,
A
is
\ell_{q}
-sectorial of type
\alpha_{q}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta_{q}
. Let
0<\beta<\beta_{q}
and
\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta
. Then by Proposition
4.9
and the estimates obtained in its proof, we get

\big\|\mathcal{G}_{q}^{T}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim(\cos\alpha\cos\frac{\alpha}{2})^{-1}(\beta_{q}-\beta)^{-2}\,\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}^{\min(\frac{p}{q},\,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})}\max((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}})\big\|\mathcal{G}_{q}^{P}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

Choosing
\beta=\frac{\beta_{q}}{2}
yields

\big\|\mathcal{G}_{q}^{T}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim D\,\big\|\mathcal{G}_{q}^{P}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},

where

D=\beta_{q}^{-3}\,\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}^{\min(\frac{p}{q}\,,\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}})}\,\max\big((p^{\prime})^{1-\frac{p}{q}},\,p^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\big).

We then deduce Theorem
1.4
(ii) from Theorem
1.2
(i).
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.9
.

By
[
44
,
45
]
,

\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(L_{p}(\Omega;X))\lesssim\max\big((p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}},\,\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\big).

Fix
f_{0}\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with norm 1. Given
f\in L_{p}(\Omega;X)
let
\widetilde{f}=f\otimes f_{0}
. We view
\widetilde{f}
as a function from
\Omega
to
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
by
\omega\mapsto f(\omega)f_{0}
. Applying Theorem
1.2
(i) to this function with
X
replaced by
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
and
p=q
(noting then that

\max\big(q^{\frac{1}{q}},\,q^{\prime}\big)\approx 1
since
q\geq 2
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{t}(f)\otimes f_{0}\big\|^{q}_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\big((p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}},\,\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\big)\big\|f\otimes f_{0}\big\|_{L_{q}(\Omega;L_{p}(\Omega;X))}

\displaystyle=\max\big((p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}},\,\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)\big)\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}.

The left hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{t}(f)\otimes f_{0}\big\|^{q}_{L_{q}(\Omega;L_{p}(\Omega;X))}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{t}(f)\big\|^{q}_{L_{p}(\Omega;X)}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Combining the above estimates we get the desired inequality of the corollary.

To show the optimality of the constant, we consider the special case where
X={\mathbb{C}}
and the classical Poisson semigroup
\{\mathbb{P}_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
f=\mathbb{P}_{1}
. Then

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\approx 1\;\text{and }\;\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbb{P}_{t}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\approx\frac{t}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}.

Thus

\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbb{P}_{t}(f)\big\|^{q}_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\,\frac{dt}{t}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\approx p^{\prime\frac{1}{q}}.

We then deduce the announced optimality.
∎

8.
The scalar case revisited and optimality

The approach previously presented gives new insights even in the scalar case with regard to the involved best constants. Let

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}=\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{c},2,p}({\mathbb{C}})\;\text{ and }\;\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},p}=\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{t},2,p}({\mathbb{C}}).

Thus
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}
and
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},p}
are the best constants in the following inequalities

(\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},p})^{-1}\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\big\|\mathcal{G}^{T}_{2}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},\quad f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Let us restate Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
for
X={\mathbb{C}}
and
q=2
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a semigroup of regular contractions on
L_{p}(\Omega)
with
1<p<\infty
and
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
its subordinated Poisson semigroup.

(i)

We have

\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{c},p}\lesssim\max(\sqrt{p},\,p^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},p}\lesssim\max(p,\,\sqrt{p^{\prime}}).

(ii)

Assume that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies (
1.4
) for
X={\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\beta_{p}=\beta_{0}\min(p\,,p^{\prime})
.
Then

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}\lesssim\beta_{p}^{-3}\,\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}^{\frac{1}{2}\,\min(p,\,p^{\prime})}\max\big(p,\,(p^{\prime})^{\,\frac{3}{2}}\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},p}\lesssim\max(p,\,\sqrt{p^{\prime}}).

For symmetric diffusion semigroups we have the following more precise orders than those in part (ii) above. We are very grateful to the anonymous referee for pointing out the references
[
32
,
37
]
that allow us to improve our previous estimate on
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}
based on Stein’s classical analyticity angle of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a semigroup of contractions on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for
every

1\leq p\leq\infty
. Assume that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is strongly continuous on
L_{2}(\Omega)
and each
T_{t}
is a selfadjoint operator on
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Then

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},p}\lesssim\max\big(p^{\frac{5}{2}},\,(p^{\prime})^{\,3}\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{t},p}\lesssim\max(p,\,\sqrt{p^{\prime}}).

Proof.

First note that
T_{t}
is a regular contraction on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and any
t>0
. The selfadjointness of
T_{t}
implies that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is an analytic semigroup of type
\frac{\pi}{2}
with constant
1
on
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Then by
[
37
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
32
, Corollary 6.2]
,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic of type
\beta_{p}^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\arcsin|1-\frac{2}{p}|
with constant
1
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
. Note that the angle
\beta_{p}^{\prime}
is optimal and better than Stein’s classical one that is
\frac{\pi}{2}\big(1-|1-\frac{2}{p}|\big)
(see
[
55
, section III.2]
). It remains to apply Theorem
8.1
(ii) with
\beta_{0}=\beta_{p}^{\prime}\approx\min\big(\sqrt{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}},\,\sqrt{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\,\big)
and
\mathsf{T}_{\beta_{0}}=1
. The corresponding
\beta_{p}
is now equivalent to
\min\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}},\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}}}\big)
. We then deduce the desired assertion from Theorem
8.1
.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

The orders on

for they are already optimal for the classical Poisson semigroup on

However, at the time of this writing, we are unable to determine the optimal orders of

as

Problem 8.4
.

It would be interesting to determine the optimal orders of
\mathsf{L}^{P}_{\mathsf{t},p}
as

when
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric submarkovian (or markovian) semigroup. In particular, does there exist a constant

(possibly depending on

) such that

\|f-\mathsf{F}(f)\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\big\|\mathcal{G}^{P}_{2}(f)\big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\,,\;\forall\,f\in L_{1}(\Omega)\,?

The dual version of the above inequality is related to the BMO space considered in
[
19
]
. It is true when
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
is the Poisson or heat semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

We conclude this section by the proof of the optimality of the growth orders of the best constants in Corollary
1.6
in the scalar case, i.e.,
X={\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
67
]
for more related results). We will denote
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}({\mathbb{C}})
simply by
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}
and
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}
, respectively. It suffices to consider
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Then

\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\gtrsim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\;\text{ and }\;\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\gtrsim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big).

Proof.

By Remark
1.1
, it suffices to show the assertion on the Poisson semigroup.

Let us first consider the case
p\leq q
. Fix
s>0
and let
f=\mathbb{P}_{s}
. Then

t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbb{P}_{t}(f)(x)=\frac{t}{\pi}\,\frac{x^{2}-(t+s)^{2}}{(x^{2}+(t+s)^{2})^{2}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

For
x\geq 6s
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbb{P}}_{q}(f)(x)\geq\Big(\int_{\frac{x}{3}-s}^{\frac{x}{2}-s}\big|t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbb{P}_{t}(f)(x)\big|^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{x}.

Thus

\big\|{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbb{P}}_{q}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\gtrsim\Big(\int_{6s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{p}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\gtrsim\frac{s^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}{p-1}.

On the other hand,

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\approx s^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Hence,
\mathsf{L}^{\mathbb{P}}_{c,q,p}\gtrsim p^{\prime}
.

Unfortunately, the above simple argument does not apply to the case
p>q
. Our proof for the latter is much harder. By periodization, it is equivalent to considering the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
(equipped with normalized Haar measure). The
g
-function relative to the Poisson semigroup on
{\mathbb{T}}
is defined by

G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(f)=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big|(1-r)\frac{d}{dr}\mathsf{P}_{r}(f)\big|^{q}\,\frac{dr}{1-r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

where
\mathsf{P}_{r}
denotes the corresponding Poisson kernel:

\mathsf{P}_{r}(\theta)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^{2}}\,.

It is shown in
[
64
]
that the inequality

\|G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(f)\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\|f\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}

is equivalent to the corresponding dyadic martingale inequality on
\Omega=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. It is well known that the relevant constant in the latter martingale inequality for
q=2
is of order
\sqrt{p}
as
p\to\infty
. To reduce the determination of optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}
to the martingale case, we need to refine an argument in the proof of
[
64
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Keeping the notation there, let
M=(M_{k})_{0\leq k\leq K}
be a finite dyadic martingale and

M_{k}-M_{k-1}=d_{k}(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{k-1})\,\varepsilon_{k},

where
(\varepsilon_{k})
are the coordinate functions of
\Omega
.
The transformation
\varepsilon_{k}={\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k})
establishes a measure preserving embedding of
\Omega
into
{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Accordingly, define

\displaystyle a_{k}(e^{{\rm i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k-1}})

\displaystyle=d_{k}({\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{1}),\cdots,{\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k-1})),

\displaystyle b_{k}(e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k}})

\displaystyle={\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k}).

Given
(n_{k})
a rapidly increasing sequence of positive integers, put

\displaystyle a_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})

\displaystyle=a_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta};e^{{\rm i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k-1}})=a_{k}(e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{1}+n_{1}\theta)},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{k-1}+n_{k-1}\theta)}),

\displaystyle b_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})

\displaystyle=b_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta};e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k}})=b_{k}(e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta)}),

\displaystyle f_{(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})

\displaystyle=f_{(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta};e^{{\rm i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}\theta_{K}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})b_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta}).

The functions
f_{(n)}
,
a_{k,(n)}
and
b_{k,(n)}
are viewed as functions on
{\mathbb{T}}
for each
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{K})
arbitrarily fixed. Furthermore, by approximation, we can assume that all
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are polynomials. Then, if the sequence
(n_{k})
rapidly increases, Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5 of
[
64
]
imply

\frac{1}{2}\,G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(f_{(n)})\leq\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k,(n)}|^{q}\,G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(b_{k,(n)})^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq 2G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(f_{(n)}).

Therefore,

(8.1)

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k,(n)}|^{q}\,G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(b_{k,(n)})^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq 2\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\,.

The discussion so far comes from
[
64
]
. Now we require a finer analysis of the
g
-function
G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(b_{k,(n)})
. To this end we write the Fourier series of the function
b={\rm sgn}(\cos\theta)
:

b(e^{{\rm i}\theta})=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2j+1}\,\big[e^{{\rm i}(2j+1)\theta}+e^{-{\rm i}(2j+1)\theta}\big].

Then

\frac{d}{dr}\mathsf{P}_{r}(b_{k,(n)})(e^{{\rm i}\theta})=\frac{4}{\pi}\,n_{k}r^{n_{k}-1}{\rm Re}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}r^{2n_{k}j}e^{{\rm i}(2j+1)(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta)}\Big).

Elementary computations show

\left|\frac{d}{dr}\mathsf{P}_{r}(b_{k,(n)})(e^{{\rm i}\theta})\right|^{q}\geq c^{q}n^{q}_{k}r^{q(n_{k}-1)}\cos^{q}(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta).

Here and below,
c,\,C
denote absolute positive constants.
Thus

\displaystyle G_{q}^{\mathsf{P}}(b_{k,(n)})^{q}

\displaystyle\geq c^{q}\,\cos^{q}(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta)\,n^{q}_{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{q-1}r^{q(n_{k}-1)}dr

\displaystyle\approx c^{q}\,\big[1+{\rm O}(\frac{1}{n_{k}})\big]\cos^{q}(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta).

Now lifting both sides of (
8.1
) to power
p
, then integrating the resulting inequality over
{\mathbb{T}}^{K}
with respect to
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{K})
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{1}+n_{1}\theta)},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{k-1}+n_{k-1}\theta)})|^{q}\big[1+{\rm O}(\frac{1}{n_{k}})\big]\cos^{q}(\theta_{k}+n_{k}\theta)\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\big(C\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\big)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}\big|f_{(n)}(e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{1}+n_{1}\theta)},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}(\theta_{K}+n_{K}\theta)})\big|^{p}d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}d\theta\,.

For each fixed
\theta
, the change of variables
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{K})\mapsto(\theta_{1}-n_{1}\theta,\cdots,\theta_{K}-n_{K}\theta)
being a measure preserving transformation of
{\mathbb{T}}^{K}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|a_{k,(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k-1}})|^{q}\,\big[1+{\rm O}(\frac{1}{n_{k}})\big]\cos^{q}\theta_{k}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\big(C\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\big)^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}\big|f_{(n)}(e^{{\rm i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{{\rm i}\theta_{K}})\big|^{p}\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}\,.

Letting
n_{1}\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|d_{k}({\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{1}),\cdots,{\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k-1}))|^{q}\,\cos^{q}\theta_{k}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\big(C\,\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\big)^{p}\big\|M_{K}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,.

Now we consider an elementary example where
M
is simple random walk stopped at
\pm 2
, namely

d_{k}={\mathds{1}}_{\{\tau\geq k\}}\;\text{ with }\;\tau=\inf\big\{k:\big|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}\big|=2\big\}.

Note that the probability of the event
\{\tau=j\}
is zero for odd
j
and
2^{-\frac{j}{2}}
for even
j
. On the other hand, recalling
\varepsilon_{k}={\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k})
and letting

A_{j}=\big\{\tau=j,\;|\cos\theta_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\;1\leq k\leq j\big\},

we easily check that the probability of
A_{j}
is
8^{-\frac{j}{2}}
for even
j
. Thus for
K\geq j

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}|d_{k}(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{k-1})|^{q}\,\cos^{q}\theta_{k}\geq{\mathds{1}}_{A_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{j}{\mathds{1}}_{\{\tau\geq k\}}\cos^{q}\theta_{k}\geq 2^{-\frac{q}{2}}j\,{\mathds{1}}_{A_{j}};

consequently, for
K=2J
with
J\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{K}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}|d_{k}({\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{1}),\cdots,{\rm sgn}(\cos\theta_{k-1}))|^{q}\,\cos^{q}\theta_{k}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{K}\geq c^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{J}j^{\frac{p}{q}}8^{-j}\geq c^{p}p^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Noting that
|M_{K}|\leq 2
and combining all the previous inequalities together, we finally obtain

\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}\gtrsim p^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Examples

There exist plenty of examples of semigroups to which the results of this article apply. Many second order differential operators in analysis generate such semigroups. In the following we will only discuss the cotype case since the type case can be dealt with by duality. Note that it is obvious that if
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
, it is so relative to the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

A main task in the study of the vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein theory would be the following

Problem A.1
.

Determine the family of semigroups
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
such that a Banach space
X
is of Luzin type (resp. cotype)
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
or its subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X
is of martingale type (resp. cotype)
q
.

Example A.2
.

Laplacian operators
. The classical heat semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is given by
\mathbb{H}_{t}=e^{t\Delta}
, where
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator. It is well known (and easy to check) that
\{\mathbb{H}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic of angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
for any
1\leq p<\infty
and any Banach space
X
; the relevant constant as in (
1.4
) with
\beta_{0}=\frac{\pi}{2}
depends only on
d
. By
[
27
,
66
]
, if
X
is of martingale cotype
q
, then
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{\mathbb{H}_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Conversely, suppose that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{\mathbb{H}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, then
X
is also of Luzin cotype
q
relative to the classical Poisson semigroup
\{\mathbb{P}_{t}\}_{t>0}
, thereby
X
is of martingale cotype
q
by virtue of
[
41
]
. Thus the Luzin cotype relative to the classical heat semigroup is equivalent to the martingale cotype.

Example A.3
.

Schrödinger operators
. Let
\Omega
be a region in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
equipped with Lebesgue measure. Let
a(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
be a positive matrix whose entries are locally integrable real functions on
\Omega
such that

\alpha(x)\leq a(x)\leq\beta(x)

for two positive continuous functions
\alpha
and
\beta
on
\Omega
. We consider the following elliptic operator

L(f)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(a_{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\Big).

Given
V
a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\Omega
, define
A(f)=L(f)+Vf
. It is well known that
-A
generates a symmetric submarkovian (markovian for
V=0
) semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
\Omega
(cf.
[
15
, Theorem 1.8.1]
). In particular,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for any
1<p<\infty
. Thus if
X
is of martingale cotype
q
, then it is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to the Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
subordinated to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
for any
1<p<\infty
.

Assume in addition that
L
is uniformly elliptic, namely, the above two functions
\alpha
and
\beta
are constant. Then the integral kernel
K^{0}_{t}(x,y)
of
e^{-tL}
satisfies the following Gaussian upper bound (cf.
[
15
, Theorem 3.2.8]
)

K^{0}_{t}(x,y)\leq C_{\delta,\alpha}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(1+\delta)\beta t}\right),\quad t>0,\;x,y\in\Omega,\;0<\delta<1.

By the Trotter formula

e^{-tA}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(e^{-\frac{tL}{n}}\,e^{-\frac{tV}{n}}\big)^{n}(f),

we deduce that the integral kernel
K_{t}(x,y)
of
e^{-tA}
is majorized by
K^{0}_{t}(x,y)
:

K_{t}(x,y)\leq K^{0}_{t}(x,y).

Thus
K_{t}(x,y)
satisfies the same Gaussian upper bound as
K^{0}_{t}(x,y)
. Let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
{\rm Re}z>0
. By
[
15
, Theorem 3.4.8]
, the complex time heat kernel of
e^{-zA}
satisfies

|K_{z}(x,y)|\lesssim_{\delta,\alpha}({\rm Re}z)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}\,{\rm Re}(z^{-1})}{(1+\delta)\beta}\right).

Then we easily show that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
extends to an analytic semigroup of type
\frac{\pi}{2}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
for any
X
and any
1<p<\infty
(
p
can be equal to
1
too). It then follows that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
whenever
X
is of martingale cotype
q
.

As in the case of Laplacian operators, it is likely that the Luzin cotype relative to the semigroups generated by Schrödinger operators characterizes the martingale cotype. This is indeed the case if
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
,
L=-\Delta
and the potential
V
satisfies a reverse Hölder inequality (see
[
1
]
). Let us formulate the general case explicitly as a conjecture.

Conjecture A.4
.

Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the Poisson semigroup subordinated to the heat semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
generated by a Schrödinger operator as above. If a Banach space
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
, then
X
is of martingale cotype
q
. The same is conjectured for
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself when the underlying differential operator
L
is uniformly elliptic.

Example A.5
.

Laplace-Beltrami operators
. The preceding examples can be extended to the setting of Riemannian manifolds. Let
M
be a complete
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with metric
g
. Let
a(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
be a positive matrix smoothly depending on
x\in M
. The associated second order elliptic operator
L
is represented as

L(f)=-g^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(g^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)

in local coordinates. Then
T_{t}=e^{-tL}
extends to a symmetric diffusion semigroup on
M
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
(cf.
[
14
]
). Thus our previous results apply to the associated subordinated Poisson semigroup. The most important case is the one where
-L=\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Then the celebrated theorem of Li and Yau
[
39
]
asserts that the integral kernel of
e^{t\Delta}
has a Gaussian upper bound under the additional assumption that the Ricci curvature be nonnegative (see also
[
15
, Theorem 5.5.6]
). Thus as in the Euclidean case, the heat semigroup
\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t>0}
extends to an analytic semigroup on
L_{p}(M;X)
for any Banach space
X
and
1<p<\infty
. It would be interesting to determine whether the Luzin cotype of
X
relative to
\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t>0}
characterizes the martingale coptye of
X
.

Example A.6
.

Hermite operators
. The Hermite operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a particular Schrödinger operator:
A=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
. The associated semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric submarkovian semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The integral kernel of
T_{t}
is given by

K_{t}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi\sinh(2t)}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{4}\big[|x-y|^{2}\coth t+|x+y|^{2}\tanh t\big]\Big).

Using the Trotter formula, we see that
K_{t}(x,y)
is less than or equal to the heat kernel:

K_{t}(x,y)\leq\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi t}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\,.

This Gaussian upper bound can be deduced from the above explicit formula of
K_{t}
. It then follows that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is an analytic semigroup of type
\frac{\pi}{2}
on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
for any Banach space
X
and
1<p<\infty
. Betancor
et al
showed in
[
7
]
that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X
is of martingale cotype
q
(see also
[
9
]
for related results).

Example A.7
.

Laguerre operators
. For simplicity, we only consider the Laguerre semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the multi-dimensional case can be treated by tensor product. In this example,
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is equipped with Lebesgue measure, contrarily to our usual convention. Let
\alpha>-1
and

{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x^{2}+\frac{1}{x^{2}}(\alpha^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\Big),\quad x>0.

We have

{\mathcal{A}}(\varphi^{\alpha}_{k})=\lambda^{\alpha}_{k}\varphi^{\alpha}_{k},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
\lambda^{\alpha}_{k}=2k+|\alpha|+1
and

\varphi^{\alpha}_{k}(x)=\left(\frac{2\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamma(k+1+\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x^{2})

with
L_{k}^{\alpha}
the k-th polynomial of type
\alpha
(see
[
35
, p. 76]
).
\{\varphi^{\alpha}_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is an orthonormal basis in
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
.

For every
f\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, setting

c_{k}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\varphi^{\alpha}_{k}(x)dx,

we consider the operator
A
formally defined by

A(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(f)\lambda^{\alpha}_{k}\varphi^{\alpha}_{k}.

Note that
A(f)={\mathcal{A}}(f)
if
f
is compactly supported and smooth.
Then
-A
generates a symmetric semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
of positive contractions on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
:

T_{t}(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(f)e^{-\lambda^{\alpha}_{k}\,t}\varphi^{\alpha}_{k}.

with kernel given by

K_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sinh t}\,\sqrt{xy}\,I_{\alpha}\big(\frac{xy}{\sinh t}\big)\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\coth t\big),

where
I_{\alpha}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind and order
\alpha
:

I_{\alpha}(z)=2^{-\alpha}z^{\alpha}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k}}{2^{2k}\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}\,.

It is proved in
[
48
]
that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is contractive on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
iff
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
or
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
, and that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a bounded semigroup on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
if
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
. However, for
-1<\alpha<-\frac{1}{2}
,
T_{t}
is unbounded on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for
p\leq p_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{2\alpha+3}
and
p\geq p_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.

On the other hand,
[
7
]
shows that for
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
, a Banach space
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X
is of martingale cotype
q
; as a byproduct,
[
7
]
also shows that for the same range of
\alpha
,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X)
for any
X
.

In the remaining case of
-1<\alpha<-\frac{1}{2}
, it is quite easy to show that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a bounded semigroup on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for
p_{\alpha}<p<p_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. Let us outline the argument for the convenience of the reader. By dilation invariance via the change of variables
u={x}/{\sqrt{\sinh t}}
and
v={y}/{\sqrt{\sinh t}}
, the kernel
K_{t}
is brought to

\varphi(x,y)=\sqrt{xy}\,I_{\alpha}(xy)\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\cosh t\big).

Let
\Phi
be the associated integral operator:

\Phi(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(x,y)f(y)dy.

To estimate the norm of
\Phi
in
B(L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))
for
p_{\alpha}<p<p_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, we appeal to the following estimates of the Bessel function (cf.
[
35
, Chapter 5]
)

I_{\alpha}(z)\approx\frac{z^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\;\text{ as }\;z\to 0\;\text{ and }\;I_{\alpha}(z)\approx\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{\pi z}}\;\text{ as }\;z\to\infty.

Accordingly,
I_{\alpha}
is decomposed as

I_{\alpha}(xy)=I_{\alpha}(xy){\mathds{1}}_{xy\leq 1}+I_{\alpha}(xy){\mathds{1}}_{xy>1}\lesssim(xy)^{\alpha}{\mathds{1}}_{xy\leq 1}+(xy)^{-1/2}e^{xy}{\mathds{1}}_{xy>1}

with the relevant constant depending only on
\alpha
. Thus

\varphi(x,y)\lesssim\varphi_{1}(x,y)+\varphi_{2}(x,y),

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x,y)=(xy)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\cosh t\big){\mathds{1}}_{xy\leq 1}\,,

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(x,y)=e^{xy}\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\cosh t\big){\mathds{1}}_{xy>1}\,.

Let
\Phi_{i}
be the integral operator corresponding to
\varphi_{i}
. Then by the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\Phi_{1}\big\|_{B(L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}e^{-px^{2}\frac{\cosh t}{2}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{p^{\prime}(\alpha+1/2)}e^{-{p^{\prime}}y^{2}\frac{\cosh t}{2}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha,p}(\cosh t)^{-(\alpha+1)}\leq C_{\alpha,p}\,,

where we have used the assumption that
p_{\alpha}<p<p_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. Noting that
\varphi_{2}(x,y)\leq e^{-(x-y)^{2}/2}
, we see that
\big\|\Phi_{2}\big\|_{B(L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X))}\leq 1
.

In particular,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric semigroup of positive contractions on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
, so analytic. Applying the previous sections to the associated subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
, we recover the result of
[
9
]
that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}};X)
whenever
X
is of martingale cotype
q
. Moreover,
[
9
]
shows that the converse is also true. Note that
[
9
]
also extends this result to all
p\in(p_{\alpha},\,p_{\alpha}^{\prime})
.

We do not know, however, to determine the analyticity of
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
for the range
-1<\alpha<-\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark A.8
.

We would like to point out an interesting phenomenon revealed by this example. It is easy to get a semigroup of contractions on
L_{2}
thanks to spectral theory. If the contractions are further positive (or regular), the results of the previous sections apply. In many concrete examples, one can then extrapolate
L_{2}
to
L_{p}
using tools from harmonic analysis. This is indeed the case for all previous examples.

Example A.9
.

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
. Now
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is equipped with the canonical Gaussian measure
\gamma_{d}
. Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
whose negative generator is given by
A=-\Delta+x\cdot\nabla
. This is again a symmetric diffusion semigroup. By
[
52
]
,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\gamma_{d};X)
iff
X
is K-convex (a property weaker than the finite martingale cotype). On the other hand, by
[
41
]
,
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to the Poisson semigroup subordinated to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
iff
X
is of martingale cotype
q
. We then deduce that the Luzin cotype
q
of
X
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
itself on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\gamma_{d};X)
characterizes the martingale cotype
q
of
X
. It is worth noting that in contrast to
[
41
]
, all estimates obtained by the present method or by
[
66
]
are dimension free.

Example A.10
.

Walsh semigroup
. Let
\Omega=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the dyadic group equipped with normalized Haar measure. The coordinate functions
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
on
\Omega
form an independent sequence of symmetric random variables (Rademacher functions). We introduce the Walsh system
(w_{A})
: for any finite subset

A\subset{\mathbb{N}}
let

w_{A}=\prod_{k\in A}\varepsilon_{k}\,.

If
A=\emptyset
,
w_{\emptyset}=1
. All such
w_{A}
’s form an orthonormal basis of
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Any
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
admits the following Fourier expansion:

f=\sum_{A}\alpha_{A}w_{A}.

Define

T_{t}(f)=\sum_{A}e^{-t|A|}\alpha_{A}w_{A}.

Then
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric diffusion semigroup on
\Omega
, it can be viewed as a baby model of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup. Again, by
[
52
]
,
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
is analytic on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
iff
X
is K-convex.

Remark A.11
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the semigroup in the above example. Then the Luzin cotype relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
characterizes the martingale cotype.

Indeed, assume that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
. Then by an argument via the central limit theorem as in
[
3
]
, we can show that
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup too, so by the previous example,
X
is of martingale cotype
q
.

It would be interesting to show the analogue of Corollary
1.6
for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Walsh semigroup.

Problem A.12
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Walsh semigroup as above and
X
be of martingale cotype
q
. Does one have

\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)?

It would, of course, suffice to consider the Walsh case. On the other hand, it is likely that in the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck setting one could get a dimension dependent estimate
\mathsf{L}^{T}_{\mathsf{c},q,p}(X)\lesssim_{d}\max\big(p^{\frac{1}{q}},\,p^{\prime}\big)\mathsf{M}_{\mathsf{c},q}(X)
by standard techniques on the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup. However, here a dimension free estimate is more important than the corresponding one for the heat semigroup in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in view of analysis in Wiener space.

Example A.13
.

Translation semigroup
. We have already used the translation semigroup
\{\tau_{t}\}_{t>0}
in in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. It is not analytic on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
for any
p
. By (
7.6
), if
X
is of Luzin cotype
q
relative to the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P^{\tau}_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}};X)
, so is
X
relative to the Poisson semigroup subordinated to any semigroup of regular contractions
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega;X)
. Consequently, the Luzin cotype relative to
\{P^{\tau}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is equivalent to the martingale cotype.

Example A.14
.

L_{2}
-theory
. Let
A
be a positive densely defined operator on
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Then
-A
generates an analytic semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
of contractions on
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Being positivity preserving can be characterized by means of the Dirichlet form associated to
A
(cf.
[
15
, Theorem 1.3.2]
). Many classical examples are built in this way. Note, however, that it can happen that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
extends to a semigroup of bounded operators on
L_{p}(\Omega)
for
p

Smooth projective Calabi-Yau complete intersections and algorithms for their Frobenius manifolds and higher residue pairings
Younggi Lee, Jeehoon Park, Jaehyun Yim

Abstract
. The goal of this article is to provide an explicit algorithmic construction of formal F F -manifold structures, formal Frobenius manifold structures, and higher residue pairings on the primitive middle-dimensional cohomology ℍ \mathbb{H} of a smooth projective Calabi-Yau complete intersection variety X X defined by homogeneous polynomials G 1 ​ ( x ¯ ) , … , G k ​ ( x ¯ ) G_{1}(\underline{x}),\dots,G_{k}(\underline{x}) . Our main method is to analyze a certain dGBV (differential Gerstenhaber-Batalin-Vilkovisky) algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} obtained from the twisted de Rham complex which computes ℍ \mathbb{H} . More explicitly, we introduce a notion of a weak primitive form associated to a solution of the Maurer-Cartan equation of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and the Gauss-Manin connection, which is a weakened version of Saito’s primitive form ( [ 19 ] ). In addition, we provide an explicit algorithm for a weak primitive form based on the Gröbner basis in order to achieve our goal. Our approach through the weak primitive form can be viewed as a unifying link (based on Witten’s gauged linear sigma model, [ 24 ] ) between the Barannikov-Kontsevich’s approach to Frobenius manifolds via dGBV algebras (non-linear topological sigma model, [ 1 ] ) and Saito’s approach to Frobenius manifolds via primitive forms and higher residue pairings (Landau-Ginzburg model, [ 22 ] ).

3.3
) with
\alpha=\beta
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
\Gamma,A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
, and
\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
satisfy the equation (
3.2
). Then
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
satisfies
(associativity)
,
(commutativity)
, and
(potential)
.
In particular,
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
equip
\mathbb{H}=H_{\mathrm{prim}}^{n-k}(X,\mathbb{C})
with a formal
F
-manifold structure by Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

By applying
\hbar\downtriangle_{\gamma}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}
to the equation (
3.2
), we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\gamma}(\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\beta}(\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\alpha}1))=\hbar\downtriangle_{\gamma}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\Gamma_{\rho}+(Q_{S+\Gamma}+\hbar\Delta)(\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\gamma}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho\in I}\Big[\hbar\partial_{\gamma}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\Gamma_{\rho}+A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\gamma}\Gamma_{\rho}\Big]+(Q_{S+\Gamma}+\hbar\Delta)(\hbar\downtriangle^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}_{\gamma}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in I}\bigg[\hbar\partial_{\gamma}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\delta}+\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}A_{\gamma\rho}{}^{\delta}\bigg]\Gamma_{\delta}+(Q_{S+\Gamma}+\hbar\Delta)\Big(\hbar\downtriangle_{\gamma}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\Lambda_{\gamma\rho}\Big).

Note that

\hbar\downtriangle_{\gamma}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}(\hbar\downtriangle_{\beta}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}(\hbar\downtriangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}1))=\hbar^{2}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\hbar(\Gamma_{\alpha}\Gamma_{\beta\gamma}+\Gamma_{\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\gamma}+\Gamma_{\gamma}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta})+\Gamma_{\alpha}\Gamma_{\beta}\Gamma_{\gamma}.

Since the left hand side of equation (
3.4
) is invariant under the permutation of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
at each
\hbar
-power term and the coefficient of
\Gamma_{\delta}
in the right hand side is unique by Lemma
2.19
,

\sum_{\rho}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}A_{\gamma\rho}{}^{\delta}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}

are also invariant under the permutation of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Therefore, the associativity and the potential are satisfied. It is important that
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
has no
\hbar
-power terms.

The commutativity follows from the equation (
3.2
) directly by the same argument.
∎

Therefore, the key question to construct a formal
F
-manifold structure on
\mathbb{H}=H_{\mathrm{prim}}^{n-k}(X,\mathbb{C})
is the following:

Can we find a Maurer-Cartan solution
\Gamma
whose connection matrix
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
(and
\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
) has no
\hbar
-power terms?

3.3.
An explicit algorithm

We will provide an explicit algorithm which calculates
\Gamma,A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
satisfying the equation (
3.2
). We use the following notations:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}u_{\alpha}\cdot t^{\alpha}+\sum_{m\geq 2}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in I^{m}}\frac{1}{m!}u_{\underline{\alpha}}t^{\underline{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}[\![\underline{t}]\!],

\displaystyle A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=a_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in I^{m}}\frac{1}{m!}a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}t^{\underline{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{C}[\![\underline{t}]\!],

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in I^{m}}\frac{1}{m!}\lambda_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}t^{\underline{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1}[\![\underline{t}]\!],

where
u_{\underline{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}
,
a_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho},a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\lambda_{\alpha\beta},\lambda_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1}
, and
t^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}:=t^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t^{\alpha_{m}}
. We are assuming that
u_{\underline{\alpha}},a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho},\lambda_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}
are invariant under the permutation of indices of
\underline{\alpha}
under our notation. This assumption is important for our algorithm (see Remark
3.7
). In order to make
\{\Gamma_{\rho}:\rho\in I\}
be a
\mathbb{C}[\![\underline{t}]\!][\![\hbar]\!]
-basis of
\mathcal{H}_{S+\Gamma}^{(0)}
, we define the
t^{\alpha}
-term of
\Gamma
as
u_{\alpha}
where
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}/Q_{S}(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1})
; see Lemma
2.19
. We have to determine
u_{\underline{\alpha}},a_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho},a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}
which satisfy the equation (
3.2
).

For the algorithm, Definition
3.5
and Lemma
2.8
(the weak
Q_{S}\Delta
-lemma) are required.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
|\underline{\alpha}|=m
which means that
\underline{\alpha}\in I^{m}
. We define the notation
u_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}
as follows:

u^{(i)}_{\underline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\alpha}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\underline{\alpha}_{m-i}=\underline{\alpha}\\
\underline{\alpha}_{j}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m-i)!}u_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots u_{\underline{\alpha}_{m-i}},\quad(0\leq i\leq m-1),

where the notation
\underline{\alpha}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\underline{\alpha}_{m-i}=\underline{\alpha}
means
\underline{\alpha}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\underline{\alpha}_{m-i}=\underline{\alpha}
and
\underline{\alpha}_{k}\cap\underline{\alpha}_{\ell}=\emptyset
for
\ell\neq k
.

Note that
u_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}
is invariant under the permutation of indices of
\underline{\alpha}
. For example,

\begin{aligned} u_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(0)}&=u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}u_{\gamma},\\
u_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(1)}&=u_{\alpha}u_{\beta\gamma}+u_{\beta}u_{\alpha\gamma}+u_{\gamma}u_{\alpha\beta}\\
u_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(2)}&=u_{\alpha\beta\gamma},\end{aligned}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\begin{aligned} u_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{(0)}&=u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}u_{\gamma}u_{\delta},\\
u_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{(1)}&=u_{\alpha}u_{\beta\gamma\delta}+u_{\beta}u_{\alpha\gamma\delta}+u_{\gamma}u_{\alpha\beta\delta}+u_{\delta}u_{\alpha\beta\gamma},\\
u_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{(2)}&=u_{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma\delta}+u_{\alpha\gamma}u_{\beta\delta}+u_{\alpha\delta}u_{\beta\gamma},\\
u_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}^{(3)}&=u_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.\end{aligned}

Now we give the algorithm for
\Gamma
and
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
as follows:

Step-0:

Choose a
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
of
J_{S}:=\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}/Q_{S}(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1})
. Note that
J_{S}
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, and there is a partition of
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
in terms of weights, i.e., we decompose
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}=\bigcup_{r=0}^{n-k}\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I_{r}\}
where
I=I_{0}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{n-k}
. We use the notations
u_{\mathrm{min}}=u_{\alpha_{1}}
and
u_{\mathrm{max}}=u_{\alpha_{2}}
for
\alpha_{1}\in I_{0}
and
\alpha_{2}\in I_{n-k}
where
|I_{0}|=|I_{n-k}|=1
(since
X_{\underline{G}}
is a Calabi-Yau manifold).

Step-1:

Determine
a_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}
and
\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}
using a basis
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
of
J_{S}
as follows:

u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}({\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}}).

Note that
a_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}{}^{\rho}\in\mathbb{C}
is unique and
\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}
is unique up to
\ker Q_{S}
. Therefore,
\Delta(\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)})
is unique up to
\mathrm{Im}(Q_{S})
by the weak
Q_{S}\Delta
-lemma
2.8
. Then, define
u_{\alpha\beta}:=\Delta(\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)})
and
a_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}:=a_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)}{}^{\rho}
.

Step-
\boldsymbol{\ell}

(\ell\geq 2)
:

Suppose that
|\underline{\alpha}|=\ell+1
. Determine
a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}{}^{\rho}
and
\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}
(
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
) in sequence as follows:

(3.6)

\displaystyle u_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(0)}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(0)}),

\displaystyle u_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(1)}-\Delta(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(0)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(1)}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(1)}),

\displaystyle\vdots

\displaystyle u_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)}-\Delta(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-2)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)}).

Since
\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}
is unique up to
\ker Q_{S}
for
0\leq i\leq\ell-2
,
\Delta(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)})
is unique up to
\mathrm{Im}(Q_{S})
by the weak
Q_{S}\Delta
-lemma
2.8
. Therefore,
a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)}{}^{\rho}
is independent of the choices of
\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(i)}
. Then, define
a_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}:=a_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)}{}^{\rho}
and
u_{\underline{\alpha}}:=\Delta(\lambda_{\underline{\alpha}}^{(\ell-1)})
.

By this inductive algorithm, we can completely determine
\Gamma,A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
, and
\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
which turn out to satisfy (see subsection
3.4
) the equations (
3.3
).

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose that
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}[\![\underline{t}]\!]
satisfy the equation (
3.2
) with the connection matrices
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
and
A^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
respectively. If
\Gamma_{\alpha}|_{\underline{t}=0}=\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha}|_{\underline{t}=0}=u_{\alpha}
for some
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
of
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}/Q_{S}(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1})
, then
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}=A^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
. In other words, the result
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
of the above algorithm depends only on the choice of
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
of
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{0}/Q_{S}(\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1})
.

Remark 3.7

(Non-triviality of solving the differential equations (
3.3
))
.

The symmetries among the indices of
\underline{\alpha}
in
u_{\underline{\alpha}},a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho},\lambda_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}
play an important role. If one tries to solve the equations in a naive way by comparing the coefficients of the
\underline{t}
-powers, then one would get into trouble that the symmetries among the indices is not guaranteed. But our algorithm guarantees that the relevant quantities such as

u_{\underline{\alpha}},a_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho},\lambda_{\alpha\beta\underline{\alpha}}
are invariant under the permutation of
\underline{\alpha}
as the equations (
3.6
) indicate. Suppose that there is no condition such as
u_{\alpha\beta}=u_{\beta\alpha}
, i.e., we do not put symmetry restrictions on the index notation. Then, the equation

\partial_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\Gamma=\Delta(\Lambda_{\alpha\beta})

in the equations (
3.3
) gives the following equation

u_{\alpha\beta}+u_{\beta\alpha}=2\Delta(\lambda_{\alpha\beta}),

but then it cannot determine the values of
u_{\alpha\beta},u_{\beta\alpha}
individually from the data
2\Delta(\lambda_{\alpha\beta})
of the previous step. This implies that the naive way of solving (
3.3
) inductively with non-symmetric notations does not work well, either.

Remark 3.8

(Difficulty of constructing an algorithm for a flat metric)
.

For a formal Frobenius manifold structure (see Definition
5.1
), we need to define a flat metric
g
on
T_{\mathbb{H}}
(i.e.,
\partial_{\gamma}g(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})=0
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in I
), which is compatible with the formal
F
-manifold structure. A natural candidate of a metric would be
g(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})=A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\max}
using the weight filtration (the Hodge filtration), since such a choice corresponds to a cup product on
\mathbb{H}
and satisfies the compatibility condition (Invariance) in Definition
5.1
. To make
g
be flat, we have to find
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
satisfying the equation (
3.2
) with
a_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\mathrm{max}}=0
for all
|\underline{\alpha}|\geq 3
. However, our algorithm for solving the equation (
3.2
) is not refined enough to satisfy the conditions
a_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\mathrm{max}}=0
for all
|\underline{\alpha}|\geq 3
. For example, if we run the algorithm, we get non-zero
u_{\alpha\beta}
and the step 3 in our algorithm gives the following equations:

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}u_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(0)}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}(\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(0)}),

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}u_{\beta\gamma}+u_{\beta}u_{\alpha\gamma}+u_{\gamma}u_{\alpha\beta}-\Delta(\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(0)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}+Q_{S}(\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{(1)}).

It is hard to choose
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
satisfying
a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}{}^{\mathrm{max}}=0
. Note that Corollary
3.6
implies that
a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}{}^{\mathrm{max}}
only depends on the choice of
\{u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
. On the other hand, in section
5
, we will use a Maurer-Cartan solution
L=\sum_{\underline{\alpha}}t^{\alpha}u_{\alpha}
, i.e.,
\Gamma
with the condition
u_{\underline{\alpha}}
=0 for
|\underline{\alpha}|\geq 2
, and solve a differential equation (
5.2
) instead of (
3.3
) in order to construct a formal Frobenius manifold.
Note that both (
5.2
) and (
3.3
) are special cases of the differential equation (
1.1
) for weak primitive forms.

3.4.
A proof why the algorithm works

In this subsection, we give a proof of the algorithm in subsection
3.3
.

Definition 3.9
.

We define quantities
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}
, which are our key players in the proof of the algorithm, as follows:

\mathbf{U}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}:=\hbar\downtriangle_{\alpha_{m}}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}\cdots\hbar\downtriangle_{\alpha_{1}}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}1,

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}^{\rho}:=&{A_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}^{\rho},\\
{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}}^{\rho}:=&\sum_{\delta\in I}{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}^{\delta}{\mathbf{B}_{\delta\alpha_{3}}}^{\rho}+\hbar\cdot\partial_{\alpha_{3}}({\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}^{\rho}),\\
&\vdots\\
{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}}^{\rho}:=&\sum_{\delta\in I}{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}}^{\delta}{\mathbf{B}_{\delta\alpha_{m}}}^{\rho}+\hbar\cdot\partial_{\alpha_{m}}({\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}}^{\rho}),\end{aligned}\right.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbf{C}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}:=&\Lambda_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}},\\
\mathbf{C}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}:=&\sum_{\delta\in I}{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}^{\delta}\mathbf{C}_{\delta\alpha_{3}}+\hbar\downtriangle_{\alpha_{3}}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}\mathbf{C}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}},\\
&\vdots\\
\mathbf{C}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m}}:=&\sum_{\delta\in I}{\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}}^{\delta}\mathbf{C}_{\delta\alpha_{m}}+\hbar\downtriangle_{\alpha_{m}}^{\frac{S+\Gamma}{\hbar}}\mathbf{C}_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-1}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 3.10
.

The equations (
3.2
) hold for all
\alpha,\beta\in I
if and only if the following inductive equations hold for all
m\geq 2
and all

(3.7)

\mathbf{U}_{\underline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\rho\in I}{\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\alpha}}}^{\rho}\Gamma_{\rho}+(Q_{S+\Gamma}+\hbar\Delta)(\mathbf{C}_{\underline{\alpha}}).

Proof.

It is trivial that the above inductive equations imply the equation (
3.2
) because the

3.7
) is same as (
3.2
).

Suppose that the equation (
3.2
) holds for all
\alpha,\beta\in I
. Therefore, the inductive equations hold for all
\underline{\alpha}\in I^{2}
. Assume that the inductive equations hold for all
\underline{\alpha}\in I^{\ell}
, i.e.,

(3.8)

Lemma 3.12
.

The constant term of
\underline{t}^{\underline{\alpha}}
-expansion of
{}^{m-2}B_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}
is
a_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}
which appeared in (

{}^{m-2}B_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}|_{\underline{t}=0}=a_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\rho}.

Proof.

It is enough to show that

(3.11)

5.1.
Definition of formal Frobenius manifolds

In this subsection we briefly review the definition of Frobenius manifolds in

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold of finite dimension, and
t_{M}:=\{t^{\alpha}\}
be the formal coordinates on the open subset
U_{i}\subset M
. Choose sufficiently small open subset
U
of
U_{i}
, then we could assume that
\mathcal{O}(U)=\mathbb{C}[\![t_{M}]\!]
. On the local coordinates
\{t_{M},U\}
, the product
\delta\circ\delta^{\prime}
is written as the linear combinations of
\{\partial_{\alpha}:=\partial/\partial_{t^{\alpha}}\}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}[\![t_{M}]\!]
. Let
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t_{M}]\!]
be a formal power series representing the 3-tensor field such that

\partial_{\alpha}\circ\partial_{\beta}:=\sum_{\gamma}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}.

Let
g
be a non-degenerate symmetric pairing on
\mathbb{C}
-vector space spanned by
\{\partial_{\alpha}\}
. Then one can extend
g
to the symmetric
\mathbb{C}[\![\underline{t}]\!]
-pairing. Let
g_{\alpha\beta}:=g(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})
. Then
(M,\circ,g)
is called a formal Frobenius manifold if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(Associativity)

\sum_{\rho}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}A_{\rho\gamma}{}^{\delta}=\sum_{\rho}A_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}A_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\delta}.

(2)

(Commutativity)

A_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\gamma}=A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}.

(3)

(Invariance) Put
A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\sum_{\rho}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}g_{\rho\gamma}
,

A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=A_{\beta\gamma\alpha}.

(4)

(Flat identity) The distinguished one
\partial_{0}
is the identity with respect to
\circ
:

A_{0\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\alpha}^{\beta}\quad(\text{where $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\alpha}^{\beta}$ is the Kronecker delta}).

(5)

(Potential)

\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\delta}=\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha\gamma}{}^{\delta}.

Suppose that both the commutativity condition
2
and the invariance condition
3
hold. Then the potential condition
5
implies the existence of a single power series function
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}[\![t_{M}]\!]
satisfying

g(\partial_{\alpha}\circ\partial_{\beta},\partial_{\gamma})=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\partial_{\gamma}\Phi.

Proposition 5.2
.

The 3-tensor field
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}
and

satisfy the axioms of the formal Frobenius manifold.

Proof.

(i) (Commutativity/Associativity) Since
u_{\alpha}\cdot u_{\beta}=u_{\beta}\cdot u_{\alpha}
,
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}={A}_{\beta\alpha}{}^{\gamma}
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.

For associativity, consider the following computations:

\displaystyle(u_{\alpha}\cdot u_{\beta})\cdot u_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\cdot u_{\rho}+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\alpha\beta})\right)\cdot u_{\gamma}=\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\cdot u_{\rho}\cdot u_{\gamma}+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\left(\sum_{\delta\in I}{A}_{\rho\gamma}{}^{\delta}\cdot u_{\delta}+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\rho\gamma})\right)+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\in I}\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\gamma}{}^{\delta}\right)u_{\delta}+Q_{S}\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}{\Lambda}_{\rho\gamma}+{\Lambda}_{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma}\right),

and

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}\cdot(u_{\beta}\cdot u_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=u_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}\cdot u_{\rho}+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\beta\gamma})\right)=\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}\cdot u_{\rho}\cdot u_{\alpha}+Q_{S}(u_{\alpha}{\Lambda}_{\beta\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}\left(\sum_{\delta\in I}{A}_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\delta}\cdot u_{\delta}+Q_{S}({\Lambda}_{\rho\alpha})\right)+Q_{S}(u_{\alpha}{\Lambda}_{\beta\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\in I}\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\delta}\right)u_{\delta}+Q_{S}\left(\sum_{\rho\in I}{\Lambda}_{\rho\alpha}+u_{\alpha}{\Lambda}_{\beta\gamma}\right).

Since
(u_{\alpha}u_{\beta})u_{\gamma}=u_{\alpha}(u_{\beta}u_{\gamma})
and
\{u_{\rho}\}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\![\underline{t}]\!]
-basis of
J_{S}^{\underline{t}}
, we get

\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\gamma}{}^{\delta}=\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\delta}

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
.

(ii) (Invariance) By associativity

{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\sum_{\rho\in I}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\gamma}{}^{\mathrm{max}}=\sum_{\rho\in I}{A}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\rho}{A}_{\rho\alpha}{}^{\mathrm{max}}={A}_{\beta\gamma\alpha},

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.

(iii) (Identity) Since we choose
u_{\mathrm{min}}=u_{0}=1
,
{A}_{0\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
for all
\alpha,\beta
.

(iv) (Potential) Note that
A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}=a_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Hence,
\partial_{\delta}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}=0
for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
.
∎

and the right hand side of the equation (

\sum_{\rho}{}^{0}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}u_{\rho}{}^{0}\zeta+\hbar({}^{0}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}(u_{\rho}{}^{1}\zeta+{}^{0}\zeta_{\rho}))+\hbar^{2}({}^{0}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}\cdot{}^{1}\zeta_{\rho})+(Q_{S+L}+\hbar\Delta)(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\alpha\beta}).

Then, we get the equation

u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}{}^{1}\zeta+u_{\beta}{}^{0}\zeta_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}{}^{0}\zeta_{\beta}=\sum_{\rho}{}^{0}A_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\rho}(u_{\rho}{}^{1}\zeta+{}^{0}\zeta_{\rho})+Q_{S+L}({}^{1}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta})+\Delta({}^{0}\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}),

which is difficult to solve by the similar algorithm in subsection

But again if we allow the

-coefficient

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}{}^{0}\zeta=

\displaystyle u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}{}^{1}\zeta+u_{\beta}{}^{0}\zeta_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}{}^{0}\zeta_{\beta}=

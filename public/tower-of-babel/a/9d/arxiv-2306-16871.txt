Abstract
Discount is the difference between the face value of a bond and its present value. I propose an arbitrage-free dynamic framework for discount models, which provides an alternative to the Heath–Jarrow–Morton framework for forward rates. I derive general consistency conditions for factor models, and discuss affine term structure models in particular. There are several open problems, and I outline possible directions for further research. Keywords: discount, factor models, stochastic partial differential equation, term structure models, zero-coupon bonds JEL classification: C32, G12, G13 MSC 2020 classification: 91B70, 91G20, 91G30



1
Discount

Let
P(t,T)
denote the time-
t
price of a zero-coupon bond with maturity
T
, or short, a
T
-bond. Define the corresponding
discount

H(t,T):=1-P(t,T).

The discount
H(t,T)
is the difference between the face value of the bond and its present value. It is the interest earned on investing in a
T
-bond at
t
and hold it to maturity
T
. As such, it quantifies the time value of money. It also equals the time-
t
price of a long position in a floating rate note paying overnight short rates
r_{t}=-\partial_{T}P(t,T)|_{T=t}
minus a short position in a
T
-bond. We call this long/short portfolio the
T
-discount
. The net cash flow generated by the
T
-discount is
r_{t}\,dt
at any
t\leq T
and
0
after
T
. The payments of the principals of the floating rate note and the zero-coupon bond at
T
offset each other. A
T
-discount is therefore identical to the floating leg of an overnight indexed swap with maturity
T
.

The gains process, say
G(t,T)
, from holding a
T
-discount over
[0,t]
, where the cash flows are continuously invested in the money market account that earns interest at the short rate, is given by the sum

G(t,T)=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{\int_{s}^{t}r_{u}\,du}r_{s}\,ds}_{\text{accumulated cash flow}}+\underbrace{H(t,T)}_{\text{spot value}}={\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}-P(t,T).

(1)

2
Discount framework

In analogy to the Heath–Jarrow–Morton (HJM) approach
[
Heath et al., 1992
]
for modeling the forward rates
f(t,T)=-\partial_{T}\log P(t,T)
, we now formulate an arbitrage-free dynamic framework for discount models. We assume a filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},{\mathbb{Q}})
, satisfying the usual conditions, carrying a
n
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W
. For simplicity of exposition, we skip technical details and assume throughout that all stochastic processes are adapted, and regular enough such that the differential and integral operations are well defined. For more background and technical details, also on arbitrage pricing, we refer to the textbooks
[
Bjork, 2020
, Chapter 19]
and
[
Filipović, 2009
]
.

We recall that
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a risk-neutral
(local) martingale measure
for the bond market if all discounted
T
-bond price processes
{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}P(t,T)
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-(local) martingales. In view of (
1
), this holds if and only if all discounted
T
-discount gains processes
{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}G(t,T)
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-(local) martingales. It is well known that the bond market is arbitrage-free if and (essentially) only if
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a local martingale measure, see, e.g.,
[
Bjork, 2020
, Chapter 11]
or
[
Filipović, 2009
, Section 4.3.4]
.

We represent the
T
-discount price at any
t\leq T
in terms of its maturity derivative,

H(t,T)=\int_{t}^{T}h(t,s)\,ds,

where the
discount derivative

h(t,T)
is assumed to be an Itô process with dynamics of the form

dh(t,T)=\alpha(t,T)\,dt+\sigma(t,T)\,dW_{t},

(2)

for some drift and volatility processes
\alpha(t,T)
and
\sigma(t,T)
, respectively. We then specify the
T
-bond price by

P(t,T)=1-\int_{t}^{T}h(t,s)\,ds.

(3)

The
T
-bond specification (
3
) is the linearized counterpart to the relation
P(t,T)={\rm e}^{-\int_{t}^{T}f(t,s)\,ds}
. Differentiating in
T
, the relationship between the discount derivative and forward rate is obtained as

h(t,T)=P(t,T)f(t,T).

In particular,

h(t,t)=f(t,t)=r_{t}

(4)

equals the short rate.

The challenge of our linear approach is that
T
-bond prices should be positive,
P(t,T)>0
, which is equivalent to requiring that

\int_{t}^{T}h(t,s)\,ds<1,\quad\text{for all $t\leq T$.}

(5)

The latter follows in particular if we can show that
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a martingale measure for the bond market specified by (
2
) and (
3
). We now derive necessary and sufficient conditions for this to hold. We thus let
h(t,T)
be given by (
2
) and define the
T
-bond prices by (
3
) and the short rates by (
4
). Here is our first result, which shows that the drift
\alpha(t,T)
is fully determined by the requirement that
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a local martingale measure.

Proposition 2.1
.

{\mathbb{Q}}
is a local martingale measure if and only if the following
discount drift condition
holds

\alpha(t,T)=h(t,T)h(t,t).

(6)

Proof.

The implied
T
-bond price dynamics is

dP(t,T)=-d\bigg(\int_{t}^{T}h(t,s)\,ds\bigg)=r_{t}\,dt-\int_{t}^{T}\alpha(t,s)\,ds\,dt-\int_{t}^{T}\sigma(t,s)\,ds\,dW_{t}.

(7)

On the other hand, the discounted
T
-price process
{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}P(t,T)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-local martingale if and only if the drift of
P(t,T)
equals
P(t,T)r_{t}\,dt
. Matching this with the drift in (
7
) gives
\int_{t}^{T}\alpha(t,s)\,ds=H(t,T)r_{t}
. Differentiating in
T
, we obtain (
6
), which proves the proposition.
∎

Given the drift condition (
6
) and using the existence of a local martingale measure as synonym for the absence of arbitrage, we can paraphrase that the generic dynamics (
2
) for an arbitrage-free discount derivative model is of the form

\displaystyle dh(t,T)

\displaystyle=h(t,T)h(t,t)\,dt+\sigma(t,T)\,dW_{t},\quad 0\leq t\leq T,

(8)

\displaystyle h(0,T)

\displaystyle=h_{0}(T),

for an initial discount derivative curve
h_{0}
. Note that, in contrast to the HJM drift condition on the forward rates, see, e.g.,
[
Filipović, 2009
, Theorem 6.1]
, the derivative drift condition (
6
) does not depend on the volatility
\sigma(t,T)
.

We next show that, for a given volatility
\sigma(t,T)
, the system of stochastic differential equations (
8
) uniquely determines
h(t,T)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a given volatility process
\sigma(t,T)
,
0\leq t\leq T<\infty
and initial discount derivative curve
h_{0}
, there exists at most one solution
h(t,T)
,
0\leq t\leq T<\infty
to (
8
).

Proof.

Let
h
and
\tilde{h}
be solutions to (
8
), then their discounted difference

q(t,T):={\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{h}(s,s)\,ds}\big(\tilde{h}(t,T)-h(t,T)\big)

satisfies

dq(t,T)=h(t,T)q(t,t)\,dt,\quad q(0,T)=0.

(9)

Integrating gives, for
T=t
,

q(t,t)=\int_{0}^{t}h(s,t)q(s,s)\,ds,\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$.}

We claim that
q(t,t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
, and by (
9
) thus
\tilde{h}=h
. Indeed, by contradiction assume that there exist
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}
such that
q(s,s)=0
, for all
s\leq t_{0}
, and
|q(t_{1},t_{1})|>0
. We let
\beta(s)
be a positive process such that
|h(s,t)|\leq\beta(s)
for all
t_{0}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{1}
. Then
|q(t,t)|\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\beta(s)|q(s,s)|\,ds
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, and Grönwall’s inequality implies that
q(t,t)=0
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
. This contradicts the assumption, whence the claim is proved.
∎

The problem remains that we still have no guarantee that bond prices in (
3
) are positive. Our main result is the following theorem, which provides sufficient conditions such that the discount framework (
8
) defines a feasible arbitrage-free price system for
T
-bonds. It thus represents an alternative to the HJM framework of forward rates.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
h(t,T)
,
0\leq t\leq T<\infty
, be any solution to (
8
) such that
r_{t}=h(t,t)
is well defined and

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}r_{u}\,du}\sigma(s,T)\,dW_{s}
,
0\leq t\leq T
, is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-martingale, for all
T
.

(10)

Then
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a martingale measure and the implied
T
-bond prices in (
3
) satisfy

P(t,T)={\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[{\rm e}^{-\int_{t}^{T}r_{s}\,ds}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\bigg],

(11)

and are thus positive, that is, (
5
) holds, in particular.

Proof.

Let
\{h(t,T):0\leq t\leq T<\infty\}
be a solution to (
8
). Denote
r_{t}=h(t,t)
and set

M(t,T):={\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}h(t,T).

Then
M(t,T)
has zero drift and dynamics

dM(t,T)={\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}\sigma(t,T)\,dW_{t},

and thus, by assumption (
10
), is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-martingale. We obtain

h(t,T)={\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}M(t,T)={\rm e}^{\int_{0}^{t}r_{s}\,ds}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}[M(T,T)\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t}]={\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[{\rm e}^{-\int_{t}^{T}r_{s}\,ds}r_{T}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\Big].

(12)

Integrating over
T
gives

H(t,T)=\int_{t}^{T}h(t,u)\,du={\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\int_{t}^{u}r_{s}\,ds}r_{u}\,du\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\bigg]=1-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[{\rm e}^{-\int_{t}^{T}r_{s}\,ds}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\Big],

(13)

as desired.
∎

The following remarks provide further details and discussion on the above discount framework.

Remark 2.4
.

The identities (
12
) and (
13
) are of independent interest, and show the economic meaning of
h(t,T)
and
H(t,T)
as present values of future cash flows
r_{T}
and
r_{u}
for
u\in[t,T]
, respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

The expression (
7
) shows that the induced volatility
v(t,T)
of the
T
-bond returns is given by
P(t,T)v(t,T)=-\int_{t}^{T}\sigma(t,s)\,ds
.

Remark 2.6
.

The equivalent physical measure
{\mathbb{P}}\sim{\mathbb{Q}}
is related to
{\mathbb{Q}}
by the market price of risk
\theta
such that the Radon–Nikodym derivative satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}}{d{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{T}]=\exp(\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{t}^{\top}dW_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\theta_{t}\|^{2}\,dt)
, for any time horizon
T>0
. This implies the
{\mathbb{P}}
-Brownian motion
dW^{\mathbb{P}}_{t}=dW_{t}-\theta_{t}\,dt
. Hence the dynamics under
{\mathbb{P}}
of
h(t,T)
is
dh(t,T)=(h(t,T)h(t,t)+\sigma(t,T)\theta_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t,T)\,dW^{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The proofs of Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
rely on technical properties, e.g., that
T\mapsto h(t,T)
is locally bounded in
T\geq t
a.s., or that the order of integration and expectation can be changed. Such properties can be asserted by imposing sufficient technical assumptions.

Remark 2.8
.

Existence of a solution for the system of SDEs (
8
) is an open problem. Existence could be an issue in view of the quadratic drift (
6
), which may cause explosion in finite time. Example
2.9
and Section
3.3
below give feasible specifications. A natural approach for a systematic study is to state (
8
) as a stochastic partial differential equation for the discount derivative curve
\psi_{t}(x):=h(t,t+x)
in an appropriate function space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Such an SPDE is of the form

d\psi_{t}(x)=\big(\partial_{x}\psi_{t}(x)+\psi_{t}(x)\psi_{t}(0)\big)dt+B(\psi_{t})\,dW_{t},

(14)

for some appropriate volatility operator
B:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
, so that
\sigma(t,t+\cdot)=B(\psi_{t})
. An example of an appropriate function space is the weighted Sobolev space
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
consisting of weakly differentiable functions
\psi:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\|\psi\|_{w}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi^{\prime}(x)^{2}w(x)\,dx<\infty
and
\psi(\infty)=0
, for some increasing and continuously differentiable weight function
w
, such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{-1/3}(x)\,dx<\infty
. E.g.,
w(x)={\rm e}^{\alpha x}
for some
\alpha>0
. This is similar to the space
H_{w}
introduced in
[
Filipović, 2001
, Chapter 5]
. It can be shown, as in
[
Filipović, 2001
, Equation (5.7)]
, that the
L^{1}
-norm of any
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
is bounded by
\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|\,dx\leq C_{w}\|\psi\|_{w}
, for some finite constant
C_{w}
. It can further be shown, as in
[
Filipović, 2001
, Theorem 5.1.1]
, that the differential operator
\partial_{x}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
{\mathcal{H}}_{w}
. Hence one can study existence and uniqueness of (local) mild and weak solutions to (
14
) in the spirit of
[
Da Prato and Zabczyk, 1992
]
. In fact, uniqueness follows as soon as the volatility operator
B(\psi_{t})
is Lipschitz continuous in
\psi_{t}
, see
[
Filipović, 2001
, Corollary 2.4.1]
. This is a direct improvement of Lemma
2.2
, since there we assumed that the volatility process
\sigma(t,T)
is given as exogenous and does not depend on
\psi_{t}
. Similarly, global existence would follow if one could show that any local weak solution of (
14
) with
\|\psi_{0}\|_{w}<C_{w}^{-1}
remains bounded,
\|\psi_{t}\|_{w}<C_{w}^{-1}
for all
t\geq 0
. Combined with the above
L^{1}
-bound this would imply that
h(t,s)=\psi_{t}(s-t)
satisfies (
5
), so that the induced bond prices (
3
) are positive.

For the special, deterministic, case where
B=0
, the unique local solution to (
14
) is given by

\psi_{t}(x)=\frac{\psi_{0}(t+x)}{1-\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{0}(s)\,ds}.

This solution does not explode in finite time if and only if the initial curve satisfies (
5
), that is,
\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{0}(s)\,ds<1
for all finite
t
. In this case, it follows easily that also
\psi_{t}
satisfies (
5
), as
\int_{0}^{T-t}\psi_{t}(x)\,dx=(\int_{0}^{T}\psi_{0}(s)\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{0}(s)\,ds)(1-\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{0}(s)\,ds)^{-1}<1
for all finite
T\geq t
.

The following example illustrates that the discount framework (
8
) admits feasible solutions.

Example 2.9
.

A simple toy model specification is
h(t,T)=\phi(T-t)r_{t}
, for some deterministic function
\phi
with
\phi(0)=1
, and where the short rate
r_{t}
follows an Itô process of the form
dr_{t}=\mu_{t}\,dt+\nu_{t}\,dW_{t}
. The induced dynamics of
h(t,T)
is

dh(t,T)=(\phi(T-t)\mu_{t}-\phi^{\prime}(T-t)r_{t})\,dt+\phi(T-t)\nu_{t}\,dW_{t}.

The drift condition (
6
) now reads as consistency condition

\phi(T-t)\mu_{t}-\phi^{\prime}(T-t)r_{t}=\phi(T-t)r_{t}^{2},

(15)

which does not depend on the volatility process
\nu_{t}
, whereas the volatility in (
8
) is simply induced as
\sigma(t,T)=\phi(T-t)\nu_{t}
. This property holds more general in affine discount term structure models, see Remark
3.1
below.

For
T=t
, as
\phi(0)=1
, we obtain the short rate drift
\mu_{t}=(r_{t}+\phi^{\prime}(0))r_{t}
and volatility
\nu_{t}=\sigma(t,t)
. Assume that
\phi^{\prime}(0)=-\theta
is negative, for some parameter
\theta>0
, and assume that
\sigma(t,t)\to 0
fast enough as
r_{t}\to 0
and
r_{t}\to\theta
, respectively, then the risk-neutral dynamics

dr_{t}=-(\theta-r_{t})r_{t}\,dt+\sigma(t,t)\,dW_{t}

is well-behaved with values in
[0,\theta]
, for any initial value
r_{0}\in[0,\theta]
. This short rate dynamics is reminiscent of the linear-rational framework
[
Filipović et al., 2017
, Section IV.E]
.

Plugging
\mu_{t}=-(\theta-r_{t})r_{t}
back in (
15
), we obtain
-\phi(T-t)\theta r_{t}-\phi^{\prime}(T-t)r_{t}=0
, which is equivalent to
\phi(s)={\rm e}^{-\theta s}
. This results in a term structure of
T
-discounts of

H(t,T)=r_{t}\int_{0}^{T-t}\phi(s)\,ds=\big(1-{\rm e}^{-(T-t)\theta}\big)\frac{r_{t}}{\theta}

and thus a term structure of
T
-bonds of

P(t,T)=1-\big(1-{\rm e}^{-(T-t)\theta}\big)\frac{r_{t}}{\theta}.

As a technical note, from above we have
r_{t}
takes values in
[0,\theta]
, which implies that the discount curve
T\mapsto P(t,T)
is decreasing and
P(t,T)>0
for all finite maturities
T>t
.

3
Discount factor models

We now elaborate on discount factor models, extending Example
2.9
. We first derive the consistency conditions for a general factor model. We then study in more detail the affine discount factor models.

3.1
General factor models

We study consistent discount factor models of the form

h(t,T)=\phi(T-t,Z_{t})

for some function
\phi:[0,\infty)\times{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, where
{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is some state space with non-empty interior, and the factor
Z_{t}
is some
{\mathcal{Z}}
-valued diffusion process, with dynamics

dZ_{t}=\mu(Z_{t})\,dt+\nu(Z_{t})\,dW_{t},

for a drift function
\mu:{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and volatility function
\nu:{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times n}
. We denote the corresponding diffusion function by
c(\cdot)=\nu(\cdot)^{\top}\nu(\cdot)
.
The induced dynamics of
h(t,T)
is

\displaystyle dh(t,T)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\partial_{1}\phi(T-t,Z_{t})+\mu(Z_{t})^{\top}\nabla_{z}\phi(T-t,Z_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\trace[c(Z_{t})\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi(T-t,Z_{t})]\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{z}\phi(T-t,Z_{t})^{\top}\nu(Z_{t})\,dW_{t}.

Matching the drift term with the arbitrage-free dynamics (
8
) point-wise gives the following consistency equation

-\partial_{x}\phi(x,z)+\mu(z)^{\top}\nabla_{z}\phi(x,z)+\frac{1}{2}\trace[c(z)\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi(x,z)]=\phi(x,z)\phi(0,z),

(16)

whereas the induced volatility is

\sigma(t,T)=\nabla_{z}\phi(T-t,Z_{t})^{\top}\nu(Z_{t}).

(17)

3.2
Affine discount term structure models

We now assume an affine term structure

\phi(x,z)=\phi_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}(x)z_{i},

(18)

for some functions
\phi_{j}:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
j=0,1,\dots,d
. Plugging in the affine term structure (
18
) in the consistency equation (
16
) gives

-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)z_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}(x)\mu_{i}(z)=\phi_{0}(x)\gamma_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(\phi_{0}(x)\gamma_{j}+\phi_{j}(x)\gamma_{0}\big)z_{j}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}\phi_{j}(x)\gamma_{k}z_{j}z_{k},

(19)

where we denote

\gamma_{j}:=\phi_{j}(0),\quad j=0,1,\dots,d.

Remark 3.1
.

We note that the consistency condition (
19
) does not depend on the diffusion matrix
c(z)
of the factor process
Z_{t}
. This is in contrast to the non-linear case as seen in (
16
) where
c(z)
shows up. In other words, an affine discount term structure leaves the underlying volatility unspanned. This is in contrast to affine models of the forward rates, see
[
Filipović et al., 2019
]
, and reminiscent of the linear-rational framework, see
[
Filipović et al., 2017
, Section I]
. In fact, it follows directly from (
18
) that
T
-bond prices become affine expressions in
Z_{t}
. We derive the explicit expressions in (
32
) below.

We henceforth assume that the functions

\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{d}
are linearly independent.

(20)

Equation (
19
) then implies that every drift function
\mu_{i}(z)
is a quadratic polynomial in
z
,

\mu_{i}(z)=b_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{ij}z_{j}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}B_{i,jk}z_{j}z_{k}

(21)

for some coefficients
b_{i}
,
\beta_{ij}
,
B_{i,jk}
. Plugging (
21
) in (
19
) and matching coefficients of same order in
z
gives

\displaystyle-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}(x)\gamma_{0},

(22)

\displaystyle-\phi_{j}^{\prime}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{ij}\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}(x)\gamma_{j}+\phi_{j}(x)\gamma_{0},\quad j=1,\dots,d,

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}(B_{i,jk}+B_{i,kj})\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{j}(x)\gamma_{k}+\phi_{k}(x)\gamma_{j},\quad j,k=1,\dots,d.

(24)

We arrive at the following result, where we write
\bar{Z}_{t}=(1,Z_{1,t},\dots,Z_{d,t})^{\top}
for the extended factor process including the constant 1,
e_{0}=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\top}
,
\bar{\phi}(x):=(\phi_{0}(x),\dots,\phi_{d}(x))^{\top}
, and
\bar{\gamma}:=(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{d})^{\top}=\bar{\phi}(0)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume (
20
). The factor process
Z_{t}
in an arbitrage-free affine discount term structure model of the form (
18
) has a quadratic drift of the form

\mu_{i}(z)=b_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\beta_{ij}z_{j}+z_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}z_{j},\quad i=1,\dots,d,

(25)

for some coefficients
b_{i}
,
\beta_{ij}
, and
\gamma_{j}
. The functions
\phi_{i}
in turn are given by

\bar{\phi}(x)={\rm e}^{Ax}\bar{\gamma},

(26)

where
A
is defined as the
(d+1)\times(d+1)
matrix on the right hand side of (
29
) below. The
T
-discounts and short rates are linear in
\bar{Z}_{t}
,

\displaystyle H(t,T)

\displaystyle=1-e_{0}^{\top}{\rm e}^{A^{\top}(T-t)}\bar{Z}_{t},

(27)

\displaystyle r_{t}

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{t}.

(28)

Proof.

In view of (
20
), condition (
24
) is equivalent to

B_{i,jk}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{k},&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
\gamma_{j},&\text{if $i=k$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Hence the drift (
21
) takes the form (
25
).

Equations (
22
)–(
23
) are equivalent to the linear system of ordinary differential equations

\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)\\
\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{d}^{\prime}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}-\gamma_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}&\dots&b_{d-1}&b_{d}\\
-\gamma_{1}&\beta_{11}-\gamma_{0}&\beta_{21}&\dots&\beta_{d-1,1}&\beta_{d1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
-\gamma_{i}&\beta_{1i}&&\beta_{ii}-\gamma_{0}&&\beta_{di}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\gamma_{d}&\beta_{1d}&\beta_{2d}&\dots&\beta_{d-1,d}&\beta_{dd}-\gamma_{0}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:A}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}(x)\\
\phi_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{i}(x)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{d}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

The system of ODEs (
29
) can be written in compact form as

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=A\bar{\phi}(x),

(30)

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}(0)

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}.

The solution of (
30
) is (
26
).

Note that

\bar{\gamma}=-Ae_{0}.

(31)

Hence
\bar{\phi}(x)=-{\rm e}^{Ax}Ae_{0}=-\partial_{x}{\rm e}^{Ax}e_{0}
, and the primitive functions

\Phi_{j}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\phi_{j}(u)\,du,\quad j=0,\dots,d,

are given by

\bar{\Phi}(x)=(\Phi_{0}(x),\dots,\Phi_{d}(x))^{\top}=\big(I_{d+1}-{\rm e}^{Ax}\big)e_{0}.

The expressions (
27
) and (
28
) for the
T
discount
H(t,T)=\bar{\Phi}(T-t)^{\top}\bar{Z}_{t}
and short rates

now follow by elementary calculus.
∎

As announced in Remark
3.1
, we conclude from (

) that the induced
T
-bond prices are linear in
\bar{Z}_{t}
,

P(t,T)=e_{0}^{\top}{\rm e}^{A^{\top}(T-t)}\bar{Z}_{t}.

(32)

Straightforward differentiation using (

) shows that forward rates are linear-rational in
\bar{Z}_{t}
,

f(t,T)=\frac{\bar{\gamma}^{\top}{\rm e}^{A^{\top}(T-t)}\bar{Z}_{t}}{e_{0}^{\top}{\rm e}^{A^{\top}(T-t)}\bar{Z}_{t}}.

3.3
Well behaved factor processes

As noted in Remark
2.8
, existence of global factor processes may be an issue in view of their quadratic drift (
25
), which may cause explosion in finite time. We discuss here some well behaved specifications.

More specifically, we show that there exists factor processes
Z_{t}
, with quadratic drift of the form (
25
), taking values in the half-open solid simplex

{\mathcal{Z}}:=\bigg\{z\in[0,1]:\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}<1\bigg\}.

(33)

This can always be achieved, as we show now. Thereto, we first specify a diffusion
U_{t}
with values in
[0,\infty)^{d}
and then apply the diffeomorphism
G:[0,\infty)^{d}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
defined by

G_{i}(u):=u_{i}\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}\bigg)^{-1},\quad i=1,\dots,d,

with inverse
G^{-1}_{i}(z)=z_{i}(1-\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j})^{-1}
.

The dynamics of
U_{t}
could be of the form

dU_{t,i}=\bigg(\kappa_{i}U_{t,i}+\theta_{i}\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{t,j}\bigg)\bigg)dt+q_{i}\sqrt{U_{t,i}\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{t,j}\bigg)}dW_{t,i},

(34)

for some parameters
\kappa_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\theta_{i},q_{i}\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a
[0,\infty)^{d}
-valued weak solution
U_{t}
to (
34
). The drift of the transformed process
Z_{t}:=G(U_{t})
is quadratic in
Z_{t}
of the form (
25
), and assumption (
10
) holds, so that Theorem
2.3
applies.

Proof.

Existence of a
[0,\infty)^{d}
-valued weak solution
U_{t}
to (
34
) follows from
[
Filipović and Larsson, 2016
, Theorem 5.3 and Proposition 6.4]
.

Now define
V_{t}:=1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{t,j}
, so that
Z_{t,i}=U_{t,i}(V_{t})^{-1}
, and denote
\theta_{V}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\theta_{j}
. Using Itô calculus, we obtain

\displaystyle dU_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\big(\kappa_{i}U_{t,i}+\theta_{i}V_{t}\big)\,dt+q_{i}\sqrt{U_{t,i}}\sqrt{V_{t}}\,dW_{t,i},

and thus

\displaystyle dV_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}dU_{t,j}=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}U_{t,j}+\theta_{V}V_{t}\bigg)dt+\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}\sqrt{U_{t,j}}\sqrt{V_{t}}\,dW_{t,j},

and

\displaystyle d(V_{t})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-(V_{t})^{-2}dV_{t}+(V_{t})^{-3}d\langle V,V\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=-(V_{t})^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}Z_{t,j}+\theta_{V}\bigg)dt-(V_{t})^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}\sqrt{Z_{t,j}}\,dW_{t,j}+(V_{t})^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}^{2}Z_{t,j}\,dt.

Integration by parts thus gives

\displaystyle dZ_{t,i}

\displaystyle=U_{t,i}\,d(V_{t})^{-1}+(V_{t})^{-1}\,dU_{t,i}+d\langle U_{i},V^{-1}\rangle_{t}

(35)

\displaystyle=-Z_{t,i}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}Z_{t,j}+\theta_{V}\bigg)dt-Z_{t,i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}\sqrt{Z_{t,j}}\,dW_{t,j}+Z_{t,i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}^{2}Z_{t,j}\,dt

\displaystyle+\big(\kappa_{i}Z_{t,i}+\theta_{i}\big)\,dt+q_{i}\sqrt{Z_{t,i}}\,dW_{t,i}+q_{i}^{2}Z_{t,i}\,dt

\displaystyle=\bigg(\theta_{i}+\big(-\theta_{V}+\kappa_{i}+q_{i}^{2}\big)Z_{t,i}+Z_{t,i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(-\kappa_{j}+q_{j}^{2}\big)Z_{t,j}\bigg)dt

\displaystyle+q_{i}\big(1-Z_{t,i}\big)\sqrt{Z_{t,i}}\,dW_{t,i}-Z_{t,i}\sum_{j\neq i}q_{j}\sqrt{Z_{t,j}}\,dW_{t,j}.

This shows that the drift function of
Z_{t}
is of the form (
25
).

The induced short rate process
r_{t}
is linear in
Z_{t}
given by (
28
) and thus uniformly bounded. The volatility function of
Z_{t}
is also uniformly bounded, and hence the induced discount derivative volatility given in (
17
). Hence assumption (
10
) holds, which completes the proof.
∎

The existence result in Lemma
3.3
is only partially an answer to the question about the global existence of SPDE (
14
) in Remark
2.8
. In fact, the affine discount term structure model (
18
) generates only discount derivative curves of the form
\psi_{t}(x)=\phi_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}(x)Z_{t,i}
, which lie in a
d
-dimensional affine subspace, say
{\mathcal{A}}
, of
{\mathcal{H}}
. In view of (
20
), there exist points
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{d}
such that the factor
Z_{t}
is an affine function of the
d
discount derivative values
(\psi_{t}(x_{1}),\dots,\psi_{t}(x_{d}))
. Combining this with (
17
) and (
35
), we easily see that the volatility operator
B(\psi_{t})
in (
14
) is a (non-Lipschitz continuous) function of the values
(\psi_{t}(x_{1}),\dots,\psi_{t}(x_{d}))
when restricted to
\psi_{t}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. However, it is not clear how to extend
B
beyond
{\mathcal{A}}
so a solution to (
14
) still exists outside
{\mathcal{A}}
. And uniqueness remains a problem anyway because of the lack of Lipschitz continuity at
Z_{t,i}=0
of the volatility of

given in (

), as the following remark shows.

Remark 3.4
.

While pathwise uniqueness for (
34
) is proved in
[
Filipović and Larsson, 2016
, Theorem 4.3]
in dimension

, it remains an open problem in higher dimensions

i

i
th coordinate of the process.

4
Conclusion

An arbitrage-free dynamic discount model defines an arbitrage-free price system for bonds. Modeling discount derivatives thus provides a valuable alternative to modeling forward rates. Of particular interest are affine discount term structure models, for which I provide feasible specifications.

The paper identifies various open problems outlined in Remarks
2.7
,
2.8
, and
3.4
, which point to directions for further research. Other research directions include the implementation of discount models for pricing and hedging interest rate derivatives.

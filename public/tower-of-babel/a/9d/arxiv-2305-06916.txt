Unique continuation estimates on manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below
Christian Rose, Martin Tautenhahn

Abstract
We prove quantitative unique continuation estimates for relatively dense sets and spectral subspaces associated to small energies of Schrödinger operators on Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below. The upper bound for the energy range and the constant appearing in the estimate are given in terms of the lower bound of the Ricci curvature and the parameters of the relatively dense set.



1
Introduction and main result

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold,
S\subset M
, and
H
a lower semi-bounded self-adjoint Schrödinger operator acting in
L^{2}(M)
. Quantitative unique continuation estimates on
M
refer to inequalities of the form

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S)}

(1.1)

for functions
\varphi
in the spectral subspaces of
H
up to energy
E
. The constant
C
depends on the geometric properties of the manifold
M
, the set
S
, the energy
E
, and the operator
H
, but should be independent of the function
\varphi
. There exist numerous notions of inequalities of the type (
1.1
) in the mathematical literature, e.g., uncertainty relation, quantitative unique continuation, energy estimate, spectral inequalities, or spectral estimates. The various applications of such a unique continuation estimate also benefit from an explicit (and optimal) dependence of
C
on these model parameters.
Of particular interest is the study of conditions on
M
,
S
, and
H
such that (
1.1
) is true for all
I\subset(-\infty,E_{0}]
with some
E_{0}>\min\sigma(H)
.

Classically, problem (
1.1
) is investigated in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
H=\Delta\geq 0
, and
S
is a so-called thick set, then (
1.1
) follows from the Logvinenko-Sereda theorem
[
LS74
]
, see also
[
Pan61
,
Kac73
,
Kov00
,
Kov01
]
.
Up to now, there is a huge amount of literature on quantitative unique continuation estimates on classes of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
under various assumptions on
H
, the geometric properties of
S
, and the length of the energy interval
I
, see, e.g.,
[
LR95
,
LZ98
,
JL99
,
RMV13
,
Kle13
,
KT16
,
NTTV18
,
NTTV20
,
DRST23
,
Egi21
,
ES21
]
and the references therein. Recently, (
1.1
) was proven for uniform elliptic operators without any regularity conditions on the symbol in convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and relatively dense control sets for subspaces related to small energies in
[
SS21
]
.
The interest in quantitative unique continuation estimates stems particularly from the diverse applications in different fields of mathematics. It proved to be a powerful tool, e.g., in control theory of the heat equation and the theory of random Schrödinger operators, see
[
BK05
,
CHK07
,
GK01
,
GK13
,
BK13
,
MRM22
,
ST20
]
.

Our focus is on unique continuation estimates on Riemannian manifolds rather than subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is motivated by their strong geometric implications.
Prominent examples of utilizing unique continuation estimates in Riemannian geometry are
[
DF88
,
DF90b
,
DF90a
]
, where the authors employed variants of (
1.1
) to derive vanishing order estimates for eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator for a given compact manifold
M
.
Since then, unique continuation properties have been investigated for various manifolds
M
and
S\subset M
, see, e.g.,
[
EV20
,
LM
,
BM
,
BM23
,
Mil05
,
DV
]
. However, all known results treating (
1.1
) on manifolds depend explicitly on the symbol of the Laplace–Beltrami operator. More precisely, they can only be formulated in terms of ellipticity and Lipschitz constants of the metric tensor
g
or the defect with respect to a given metric. While being quantitative, the drawback of those results is that
g
must be known a priori in a certain way, i.e., the full description of
M
is needed to obtain quantitative unique continuation estimates.
It is well-known that there is no possibility in general of recovering the metric tensor completely from local intrinsic geometric information. Rather than prescribing a metric tensor, it is natural to impose local geometric restrictions to conclude global geometric and analytic properties of manifolds such as curvature bounds. We aim at deriving (
1.1
) with explicit
E
and
C
for manifolds satisfying curvature restrictions in order to treat
classes
of Riemannian manifolds satisfying the same curvature conditions. This allows in particular to consider perturbations of metrics and derive uniform results as well apart from perturbations of Euclidean space.

Our contribution is a quantitative unique continuation estimate for Schrödinger operators on Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below at energies close to the bottom of the spectrum. Moreover, we consider operators of the form
H=\Delta+V
assuming only minimal assumptions on the positive part of
V
, and some form boundedness condition on the negative part of
V
.

Let
M=(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
and Ricci tensor
\Ric
. For a comprehensive introduction to Riemannian geometry, see, e.g., the excellent
[
GHL87
]
. We denote the distance function by
\mathrm{d}:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
, and the volume element by
\dvol
. For
x\in M
and
r>0
we denote by
B_{x}(r)=\{y\in M\colon\mathrm{d}(y,x)<r\}
the open ball with radius
r
and center
x
. Given
0<\rho<R
, a set
S\subset M
is called
(R,\rho)
-relatively dense
provided

\forall x\in M\ \exists y\in S\colon B_{x}(R)\cap S\supset B_{y}(\rho).

Given
x\in M
and
R>0
, we call
R
a
proper radius for
x
, if
\mathrm{diam}(M)>R
. Moreover, we call a
(R,\rho)
-relatively dense set
S\subset M

proper
, if
3R
is a proper radius for all
x\in M
.
Let us now turn to the operator theoretic definitions. We define the quadratic
form
\mathcal{E}\colon D(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{R}
by

D(\mathcal{E})=W^{1,2}(M),\quad\mathcal{E}(f)=\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}\dvol,

and denote by
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
M
, that is, the unique self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(M)
associated to the form
\mathcal{E}
. Note that
\Delta\geq 0
by construction.
Now we define successively two form perturbations of
\mathcal{E}
.
First, let
\mathcal{V}_{+}\colon D(\mathcal{V}_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
be a densely defined non-negative quadratic form. We assume that the form sum
\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{V_{+}}
with domain
D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+})
is densely defined and closed, and thus defines a unique non-negative self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(M)
, see
[
Kat95
, Theorem VI.2.6]
. This is for example the case if
\mathcal{V_{+}}
is relatively bounded with respect to
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}
-bound smaller than one, see
[
Kat95
, VI.1.33]
.
Second, let
\mathcal{V}_{-}\colon D(\mathcal{V}_{-})\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative quadratic form relatively bounded with respect to
\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{V}_{+}
, i.e.
D(\mathcal{V}_{-})\supset D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+})
and there are constants
a\in(0,1)
and
b\geq 0
such that

\mathcal{V}_{-}(f)\leq a(\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{V}_{+})(f)+b\lVert f\rVert^{2},\quad f\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+}).

(1.2)

As a consequence, the form

\mathcal{H}\colon D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+})\to\mathbb{R},\quad f\mapsto\mathcal{E}(f)+\mathcal{V}_{+}(f)-\mathcal{V}_{-}(f),

is a densely defined, closed form bounded
from below
[
Kat95
, Theorem VI.1.33]
. We denote the unique lower semi-bounded self-adjoint operator by
H
and its domain by
D(H)
, cf.
[
Kat95
, Theorem VI.2.6]
. Let us stress that our operator theoretic setting includes measure perturbations, e.g. as in
[
SV96
]
), or singular potentials.

Our main theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 3
, and
0<\rho<R
. There are
\kappa=\kappa(K,R,\rho,n)>0
and
E_{0}=E_{0}(K,R,\rho,n,a,b)\in\mathbb{R}
such that for any complete Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n
with
\Ric\geq K
, any proper
(R,\rho)
-relatively dense
S\subset M
, and any
I\subset(-\infty,E_{0}]
, we have

\chi_{I}(H)\mathbf{1}_{S}\chi_{I}(H)\geq\kappa\ \chi_{I}(H),

where the inequality holds in quadratic form sense.

Note that the statement of the theorem is void if
E_{0}<\min\sigma(H)
. We will restate Theorem
1.1
with explicit constants as Theorem
5.1
in Section
5
. In particular, we observe that
E_{0}>0
if the negative part
\mathcal{V}_{-}
is small, i.e. if
a
and
b
are small.
Moreover, the constants naturally depend on quantities of comparison spaces
M_{K}=M_{K}^{n}
of constant sectional curvature
K
. More precisely, denoting by
\mathrm{sn}_{K}
resp.
\Vol_{K}
the density and volume of
M_{K}
, we have

\kappa\sim\frac{\rho^{2}\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)^{n-2}}{\Vol_{K}(R)}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{K}R}+\rho^{2}K+\left|\ln\left(\frac{\Vol_{K}(R)}{\rho^{2}\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)^{n-2}}\right)\right|\right]^{-2}

and

E_{0}\sim(1-a)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)^{n-2}}{\Vol_{K}(R)}-b.

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
is the first quantitative unique continuation estimate for manifolds where the constants
\kappa
and
E_{0}
only depend on the lower bound of the Ricci curvature instead of assumptions on a particular given metric. In particular, we obtain uniform bounds for whole classes of manifolds. By applying the technique in
[
SS21
]
, it is feasible to apply our result to uniformly elliptic divergence type operators with only measurable and bounded coefficients on manifolds, without any regularity assumption on the symbol.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

If
K=0
, we recover the results from
[
SS21
]
in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is, quantitative unique continuation estimates at low energies for Schrödinger operators defined in quadratic form sense. However, even in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
our result applies to a more general class of potentials.

(ii)

In
[
DRST23
]
, the authors obtained (
1.1
) for all energies for Schrödinger operators with a certain class of singular potentials. In contrast to the last mentioned paper, our operators are defined via quadratic forms, and thus applies even for certain measure perturbations.

(iii)

If a manifold
M
satisfies
\Ric\geq-K
,
K\geq 0
, then

0\leq\inf\sigma(\Delta^{M})\leq\lambda^{K,n},

where
\lambda^{K,n}
denotes the bottom of the spectrum of the Laplace–Beltrami operator in the model space
M_{K}^{n}
.
This implies that the bottom of the spectrum is zero if
\Ric\geq 0
. Moreover, if a non-compact manifold satisfies
\Ric\geq-K
,
K\geq 0
, then there is some essential spectrum in the interval
[0,K^{2}]
,
[
Don81
]
. If
\Ric\geq K>0
the manifold is compact by the classical Bonnet-Myers theorem such that the spectrum is purely discrete with smallest eigenvalue zero. The classical Lichnerowicz estimate yields the lower bound
nK/(n-1)
for the first positive eigenvalue. In contrast, by the work of Zhong-Yang, the latter is bounded below by
\pi^{2}/D^{2}
if
\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq D
and
\Ric\geq 0
.

In order to prove Theorem
5.1
we adapt the strategy developed in
[
LSS20
,
SS21
]
to complete Riemannian manifolds with a lower Ricci curvature bound. While general functional analytic principles used in these articles carry over to our setting, our geometric assumptions reveal some new abstract insights and technical difficulties.
The main observation used in the articles
[
LSS20
,
SS21
]
goes back to
[
BLS11
]
. More precisely, the latter article shows that a quantitative unique continuation estimate can be drawn from the fact that the bottom of the spectrum of
\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S}
can be raised above a given energy interval
I
in the large coupling limit
\beta\to\infty
.

If
B\supset S
is slightly larger than
S
and
\Delta^{M,S}
denotes the Dirichlet-Laplacian on
L^{2}(M\setminus S)
, the spectral bottoms of
the large coupling limits of
\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{B}
and
\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus S}
can be expected to be comparable for large
\beta
.
This comparability will follow from a norm bound on the associated heat semigroups via a generalization of the hit-and-run lemma from
[
MSS95
,
SS21
]
to arbitrary Riemannian manifolds: the probability that a particle starting in
M
which hits
S
does not stay too long in
B
. In contrast to the Euclidean case, we cannot just use the reflection principle as this is not available in general. Instead, we provide a general bound on the semigroup differences above in terms of the first exit time of the diffusion on
M
and the coupling constant
\beta
.

By combining the above steps, we derive an abstract quantitative unique continuation estimate depending on the first exit time of the Brownian motion, the large coupling constant, and the infimum of the spectrum of
\Delta^{M,S}
.
It remains to estimate the spectral bottom of
\Delta^{M,S}
and the first exit time depending on the geometry of
S
,
B
, and
M
. Upper and lower bounds on the bottom of the spectrum are reduced to bound the bottom of the Laplacian in balls inside the complement of
S
respectively star-shaped domains in terms of the lower Ricci curvature bound.
In order to bound the first exit time we follow the strategy of
[
HSC01
]
. Keys in these proofs are the well-established Bishop-Gromov volume comparison estimate and appropriate heat kernel upper bounds depending on the lower Ricci curvature bound.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
Section
2
is devoted to the proof of the abstract quantitative unique continuation estimate in terms of the first exit time. In Section
3
we obtain a lower bound of the Dirichlet-Laplacian of relatively dense complements in terms of the volume density. Section
4
is reserved to obtain all the quantitative bounds in terms of the geometric assumptions. The main theorem is then discussed and proven in Section
5
.

2
Norm estimates for the semigroup at large coupling and an abstract quantitative unique continuation estimate

Let
S\subset M
be open,
\rho\geq 0
, and
S_{\rho}=\{y\in M\colon\mathrm{d}(y,S)<\rho\}
the open tubular neighbourhood of
S
with radius
\rho
. Furthermore, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in M},\allowbreak(X_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0})
be the Brownian motion associated to
\Delta
. For
\alpha_{0}>0
we denote the occupation time in
S_{\rho}
in the interval
[0,\alpha_{0}]
by
T^{\tau}_{S,\rho}:\Omega\to[0,\alpha_{0}]
, that is,

T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}(\omega)=\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}\circ X_{s}(\omega)\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, we denote the first hitting time of
S_{\rho}
by
\sigma_{S,\rho}:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
, as well as the first exit time of the ball with radius
r>0
by
\tau_{r}:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
, that is,

\sigma_{S,\rho}(\omega)=\inf\{s\geq 0\colon X_{s}(\omega)\in S_{\rho}\},\quad\tau_{r}{(\omega)}=\inf\{s\geq 0\colon X_{s}{(\omega)}\not\in B_{r}(X_{0}(\omega))\}.

If
\rho=0
we set
\sigma_{S}=\sigma_{S,0}
.
The following lemma is a variant of a result obtained for the Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
MSS95
]
.
It has been recaptured in
[
SS21
]
.
Since we do not impose any geometric assumption on
M
in this section, our upper bound is given in terms of the first exit time. If the Ricci curvature is bounded below, then we obtain an explicit upper bound, cf. Section
4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
S\subset M
open,
\alpha_{0},\rho>0
,
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, and
x\in M
we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0},T_{S,\rho}^{\alpha_{0}}\leq\alpha)\leq\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha).

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Fix
\alpha_{0}
,
\rho
and
\alpha
as in the assumptions of the theorem. Let
m>\alpha_{0}
be fixed and set
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}=\min\{\sigma_{S,\rho/2},m\}
. Note that the stopping time
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}
is
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely finite for any
x\in M
. This will be used when applying the strong Markov property below.
Assume that
\omega\in\Omega
is such that the corresponding sample path is continuous and

\forall s\in[0,\alpha]\colon\quad\mathrm{d}(X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)}(\omega),X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)+s}(\omega))<\rho/2.

(2.1)

Then we have

\omega\not\in\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0},T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}\leq\alpha\}.

(2.2)

Indeed, if
\sigma_{S}(\omega)>\alpha_{0}
we conclude (
2.2
).
If
\sigma_{S}(\omega)\leq\alpha_{0}
and
X_{0}(\omega)\in S_{\rho/2}
, we have
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)=\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)=0
, and by assumption (
2.1
) we find
X_{s}(\omega)\in B_{\rho/2}(X_{0}(\omega))\subset S_{\rho}
for all
s\in[0,\alpha]
. Since
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
we find
T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}(\omega)>\alpha
which implies (
2.2
).
Assume now
\sigma_{S}(\omega)\leq\alpha_{0}
and
X_{0}(\omega)\not\in S_{\rho/2}
. Since
m>\alpha_{0}
and
\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)<\sigma_{S}(\omega)
we have
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)=\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)
. Thus, assumption (
2.1
) implies
X_{t}(\omega)\in B_{\rho/2}(X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)}(\omega))\allowbreak\subset S_{\rho}
for all
t\in[\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega),\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)+\alpha]
.
Since
B_{\rho/2}(X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)}(\omega))\cap S=\emptyset
, this implies
\sigma_{S,\rho/2}(\omega)+\alpha<\sigma_{S}(\omega)\leq\alpha_{0}
. Thus we have
T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}>\alpha
which implies (
2.2
).

Fix now
x\in M
.
Since the sample paths are almost surely continuous, the contraposition of the implication (
2.1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2.2
) implies

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0},T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}\leq\alpha\})\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(E),

(2.3)

where

E=\{\omega\in\Omega\colon d(X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)}(\omega),X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}(\omega)+s}(\omega))\geq\rho/2\ \text{for some}\ s\in[0,\alpha]\}.

By (
2.3
) and the definition of the conditional expectation we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0},T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}\leq\alpha)\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{E})=\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{E}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}})),

(2.4)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}}
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra of
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}
-past, that is,

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}\colon A\cap\{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}\leq t\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\ \text{for any}\ t\geq 0\}.

Let
E_{0}=\{\omega\in\Omega\colon d(X_{0}(\omega),X_{s}(\omega))\geq\rho/2\ \text{for some}\ s\in[0,\alpha]\}
. Then, by (
2.4
), the fact that the stopping time
\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}
is
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely finite, and the strong Markov property, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0},T^{\alpha_{0}}_{S,\rho}\leq\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbb{E}_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{E}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{X_{\sigma_{S,\rho/2,m}}}(\mathbf{1}_{E_{0}})\bigr)\leq\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha).\qed

For a non-empty and open set
G\subset M
, and
S\subset G
open, we define the quadratic form
\mathcal{E}^{G,S}
on
L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{S})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{G,S})

\displaystyle=\overline{\{u\in L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{S})\colon\hat{u}\in C^{1}(G),\ u\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{S})\}}^{W^{1,2}(G\setminus\overline{S})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{G,S}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{G\setminus\overline{S}}|\nabla f|^{2}\dvol.

Here,
\hat{u}\in L^{2}(G)
is the extension of
u
by zero, i.e.
\hat{u}=u
on
G\setminus\overline{S}
and
\hat{u}=0
on
\overline{S}
, and
u\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{S})
means that there is
\overline{u}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{S})
such that
\overline{u}=u
on
G\setminus\overline{S}
. The form
\mathcal{E}^{G,S}
is densely defined, symmetric and closed, and we denote the unique self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{S})
associated with the form
\mathcal{E}^{G,S}
by
\Delta^{G,S}
and its domain by
\mathcal{D}(\Delta^{G,S})
. Note that this way we describe Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial S
and Neumann boundary conditions at the boundary of
G
. If
G=M
, the operator
\Delta^{G,S}
is merely the Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{S})
.

In order to formulate the following proposition, we denote by
\smash{\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}}}\colon L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{S})\to L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{S})
and
\smash{\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}}\colon L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)
the multiplication operator by the characteristic function of
S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}
and
S_{\rho}
, respectively. Moreover, if we are concerned with bounded operators on
L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{S})
, we interpret them as operators on
L^{2}(M)
by setting them to zero on
L^{2}(S)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
S\subset M
be open,
\alpha_{0},\rho>0
,
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, and
\beta\geq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}\Bigr\|^{2}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta\alpha}+\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha/2).

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
SS21
, Proposition 3.2]
.
Recall the Feynman-Kac formula for the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
associated to
\Delta
and the potential
\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}
: for all
t,\beta\geq 0
,
f\in L^{2}(M)
, and all
x\in M
we have

\mathrm{e}^{-t(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(f\circ X_{t/2}\exp\left(-2\beta\int_{0}^{t/2}\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}\circ X_{s}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right),

as well as

\mathrm{e}^{-t(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(f\circ X_{t/2}\exp\left(-2\beta\int_{0}^{t/2}\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}\circ X_{s}\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}>t/2\}}\right).

Specifying
t=\alpha_{0}
implies

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}f(x)-

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}f(x)\bigr|=\bigl|\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl(f\circ X_{\alpha_{0}/2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta T^{\alpha_{0}/2}_{S,\rho}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2\}}\bigr)\bigr|.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\Bigl|\Bigl(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}\Bigr)f(x)\Bigr|^{2}\\
\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}\Bigl(|f|^{2}\circ X_{\alpha_{0}/2}\Bigr)\mathbb{E}_{x}\Bigl(\mathrm{e}^{-4\beta T^{\alpha_{0}/2}_{S,\rho}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2\}}\Bigr).

Since
\mathbb{E}_{x}(|f|^{2}\circ X_{\alpha_{0}/2})=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}\Delta/4}|f|^{2}(x)

and
\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\Delta}\|\leq 1
for
t\geq 0
we conclude

\Bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}\Bigr\|\leq\Bigl(\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{-4\beta T^{\alpha_{0}/2}_{S,\rho}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2\}}\bigr)\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Since

\mathrm{e}^{-4\beta T^{\alpha_{0}/2}_{S,\rho}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2\}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta\alpha}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2\}\cap\{T^{\alpha_{0}/2}_{S,\rho}\leq\alpha/2\}},

we conclude from Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{M,S}+\beta\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}\Bigr\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta\alpha}+\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{S}\leq\alpha_{0}/2,T_{S,\rho}^{\alpha_{0}/2}\leq\alpha/2)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta\alpha}+\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha/2).\qed

The following lemma is a generalization of the spectral theoretic quantitative unique continuation estimate developed in
[
BLS11
]
(in the case
V=0
), and refined in
[
Kle13
,
TV20
]
. Let us stress that our result can be applied to operators defined via form sums where the negative part is relatively bounded with respect to
\Delta
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a complex Hilbert space,
h_{1}
,
h_{2}^{+}
,
h_{2}^{-}
,
h_{3}
lower bounded quadratic forms on
X
,
h_{2}^{+},h_{2}^{-}\geq 0
,
h_{2}:=h_{2}^{+}-h_{2}^{-}
,
a^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,
b^{\prime}\geq 0
,

h_{2}^{-}(x)\leq a^{\prime}(h_{1}+h_{2}^{+})(x)+b^{\prime}\|x\|^{2},\quad x\in D(h_{1})\cap D(h_{2}),

E_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta>0
,

Y:=\left\{x\in D(h_{1})\cap D(h_{2})\cap D(h_{3})\colon h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(x)\leq E_{0}\|x\|^{2}\right\},

and

\gamma(\beta):=\inf\left\{\frac{(h_{1}+\beta h_{3})(x)}{\|x\|^{2}}\colon x\in D(h_{1})\cap D(h_{3})\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

Then we have

h_{3}(x)\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\left((1-a^{\prime})\gamma\left(\frac{\beta}{1-a^{\prime}}\right)-E_{0}-b^{\prime}\right)\|x\|^{2},\quad x\in Y.

Remark 2.4
.

The forms
h_{2}^{+}
and
h_{2}^{-}
do not necessarily have to be the positive and negative part of
h_{2}
. It suffices that they are lower bounded and that
h_{2}^{-}
is relatively bounded with respect to
h_{1}+h_{2}^{+}
.

Proof.

We follow and modify the proof of
[
TV20
, Lemma 3.5]
. By the definition of
\gamma(\beta)
, we have
(h_{1}+\beta h_{3})(x)\geq\gamma(\beta)\|x\|^{2}
for all
x\in D(h_{1})\cap D(h_{3})
. Hence, for all
x\in Y

\displaystyle\beta h_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\beta h_{3}(x)-E_{0}\|x\|^{2}+h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\beta h_{3}(x)-E_{0}\|x\|^{2}+h_{1}(x)+h_{2}^{+}(x)-h_{2}^{-}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\beta h_{3}(x)-E_{0}\|x\|^{2}+h_{1}(x)+h_{2}^{+}(x)-a^{\prime}h_{1}(x)-h_{2}^{+}(x)-b^{\prime}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-a^{\prime})\left(h_{1}(x)+\frac{\beta}{1-a^{\prime}}h_{3}(x)\right)-(E_{0}+b^{\prime})\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\bigl((1-a^{\prime})\gamma(\beta/(1-a^{\prime}))-E_{0}-b^{\prime}\bigr)\|x\|^{2}.

Dividing by
\beta
yields the claim.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\rho,\alpha_{0},\beta>0
,
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
,
E_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
I\subset(-\infty,E_{0}]
,

be open,
\mu_{0}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta^{M,S})
, and
\lambda_{M,S_{\rho}}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta^{M,S_{\rho}})
. Then we have

\chi_{I}(H)\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}\chi_{I}(H)\geq\kappa\chi_{I}(H),

where

\kappa=\frac{1}{\beta}\left[(1-a)\left(\mu_{0}-\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{0}\lambda_{M,S_{\rho}}/2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\beta\alpha}{2(1-a)}}+\sqrt{\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha/2)}\right)\right)-E_{0}-b\right],

and
a\in[0,1)
and
b\geq 0
are as in (
1.2
).

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.3
to the quadratic forms
h_{1}=\mathcal{E}
,
h_{2}^{+}=\mathcal{V}_{+}
,
h_{2}^{-}=\mathcal{V}_{-}
, and
h_{3}\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
,
u\mapsto\langle\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}u,u\rangle
, and obtain

\langle\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}u,u\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\left((1-a)\lambda_{\beta_{a}}-E_{0}-b\right)\langle u,u\rangle,\quad u\in Y,

where

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{-})\cap D(h_{3})\colon(\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{V}_{+}-\mathcal{V}_{-})(u)\leq E_{0}\langle u,u\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap D(\mathcal{V}_{+})\colon\mathcal{H}(u)\leq E_{0}\langle u,u\rangle\right\}.

and

\lambda_{t}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta+t\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}}),\quad t\geq 0,\quad\beta_{a}:=\frac{\beta}{1-a}.

If
u\in\operatorname{Ran}(\chi_{I}(H))
for some

, then
u\in D(H)
and
\mathcal{H}(u)\leq E_{0}\langle u,u\rangle
. Then we have

.
Thus, the statement follows if

((1-a)\lambda_{\beta_{a}}-E_{0}-b)/\beta\geq\kappa.

(2.5)

In order to show this inequality, we set

\displaystyle\mu_{t}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta^{M,S}+t\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}}),\quad t\geq 0.

By the choice of our boundary conditions we have
\lambda_{t}\leq\mu_{t}\leq\lambda_{M,S_{\rho}}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Fix
t\geq 0
to be chosen later. Since we have

\left\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\Delta/2+t\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}})}\right\rVert=\mathrm{e}^{-(\alpha_{0}/2)\lambda_{2t}},\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}(\Delta^{M,S}/2+t\mathbf{1}_{S_{\rho}\setminus\overline{S}})}\right\rVert=\mathrm{e}^{-(\alpha_{0}/2)\mu_{2t}},

the reverse triangle inequality,
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
for

, and Proposition
2.2
imply for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})

\mathrm{e}^{-(\alpha_{0}/2)\lambda_{2t}}-\mathrm{e}^{-(\alpha_{0}/2)\mu_{2t}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-t\alpha}+\sqrt{\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha/2)}.

(2.6)

By the mean value theorem there exists
\xi\in[\alpha_{0}\lambda_{2t}/2,\alpha_{0}\mu_{2t}/2]
such that

\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}\mu_{2t}-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}\lambda_{2t}=\mathrm{e}^{\xi}(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}\lambda_{2t}/2}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}\mu_{2t}/2})\leq\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{0}\lambda_{M,S_{\rho}}/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}\lambda_{2t}/2}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{0}\mu_{2t}/2}).

(2.7)

From (

), (
2.7
),
\mu_{2t}\geq\mu_{0}
, and

we conclude for all

(1-a)\lambda_{2t}\geq(1-a)\left(\mu_{0}-\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{0}\lambda_{M,S_{\rho}}/2}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-t\alpha}+\sqrt{\sup_{z\in M}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\tau_{\rho/2}\leq\alpha/2)}\right)\right).

We choose

and conclude (

).
∎

yields a non-trivial result only if

. In the following sections we exploit certain geometric properties of

yielding the positivity of

.

3
Spectral estimates for Laplacians on domains with relatively dense complement

A set
G\subset M
is called
star-shaped with respect to
x\in G
if for all
y\in G
distance minimizing geodesics from
x
to
y
are contained in
G
.
For
x\in M
let
\omega=\omega_{x}\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}\to[0,\infty)
be the density of the volume form with respect to the product measure on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}
, i.e.,

\dvol=\omega(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta=\omega_{x}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta,

cf.
[
GHL87
]
.
Note that
\omega
is almost everywhere positive and finite and smooth up to the cut locus. We introduce the function

\Lambda\colon M\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\quad(x,\rho,R)\mapsto\left(\esssup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{R}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\omega(r,\theta)}{\omega(s,\theta)}\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\right)^{-1}.

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
x\in M
,
0<\rho<R<\infty
,
G\subset M
open and star-shaped with respect to
x
,
B_{x}(\rho)\subset G\subset B_{x}(R)
.
Then we have

\lambda_{G,B_{x}(\rho)}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta^{G,B_{x}(\rho)})\geq\Lambda(x,\rho,R).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
B=B_{x}(\rho)
. By the variational principle we have

\lambda_{G,B}=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{G\setminus\overline{B}}|\nabla f|^{2}\dvol}{\int_{G\setminus\overline{B}}\lvert f\rvert^{2}\dvol}\colon f\in\mathcal{F}\right\},

where

\mathcal{F}=\{f\colon G\setminus\overline{B}\to\mathbb{C}\colon f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(G\setminus\overline{B}),\ \hat{f}\in C^{1}(G),\ f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{B})\}.

Here,
\hat{f}\in L^{2}(G)
is the extension of
f
by zero, and
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{B})
means that there is
\overline{f}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{G}\setminus\overline{B})
such that
\overline{f}=f
on
G\setminus\overline{B}
.
Thus, it suffices to prove an (
f
-independent) lower bound on

\lVert\nabla f\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{B})}/\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{B})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
. We fix
f\in\mathcal{F}
.
For
\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}
we denote by
\gamma_{x}^{\theta}\colon[0,\infty)\to M
the geodesic with
\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(0)=x
,
(\gamma_{x}^{\theta})^{\prime}(0)=\theta
, and
L_{\theta}\geq 0
be the distance to the cut-locus of
x
in direction
\theta
.
Moreover, we let
R_{\theta}=\sup\{r\in(0,R]\colon\forall s\in[0,r]\colon\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s)\in G\}
. Since
G
is star-shaped with respect to
x
we have
\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(r)\in G
for all
r\in[0,R_{\theta})
.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain for all
\theta\in S_{x}^{n-1}
and all
r\in[\rho,R_{\theta}\wedge L_{\theta})

\displaystyle|(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})(r)\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert\int_{\rho}^{r}\partial_{s}(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})(s)\ \mathrm{d}s\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\rho}^{r}\partial_{s}(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})(s)\omega(s,\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega(s,\theta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ \mathrm{d}s\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\rho}^{r}|\partial_{s}(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})|^{2}(s)\omega(s,\theta)\ \mathrm{d}s\int_{\rho}^{r}\omega(s,\theta)^{-1}\ \mathrm{d}s.

By the chain rule, Cauchy-Schwarz, and since
\gamma_{x}^{\theta}
is parametrized by arc length, we have for all
s\in[0,R_{\theta})

|\partial_{s}(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})(s)|^{2}=|\langle\nabla f(\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s)),\dot{\gamma}_{x}^{\theta}(s)\rangle|^{2}\leq|\nabla f(\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s))|^{2}\lvert\dot{\gamma}_{x}^{\theta}(s)\rvert^{2}=|\nabla f(\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s))|^{2}.

Integration along distance spheres gives

\displaystyle\|f

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{B})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}|f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(r)|^{2}\omega(r,\theta)\ \mathrm{d}r\ \mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}\omega(r,\theta)\int_{\rho}^{r}|\partial_{s}(f\circ\gamma_{x}^{\theta})(s)|^{2}\omega(s,\theta)\ \mathrm{d}s\int_{\rho}^{r}\omega(s,\theta)^{-1}\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\ \mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}\omega(r,\theta)\int_{\rho}^{\rho(\theta)\wedge R_{\theta}}|\nabla f(\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s))|^{2}\omega(s,\theta)\ \mathrm{d}s\int_{\rho}^{r}\omega(s,\theta)^{-1}\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\ \mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\omega(r,\theta)}{\omega(s,\theta)}\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}|\nabla f(\gamma_{x}^{\theta}(s))|^{2}\omega(s,\theta)\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\omega(r,\theta)}{\omega(s,\theta)}\ \mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\ \|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(G\setminus\overline{B})}^{2}.

Since
L_{\theta}\wedge R_{\theta}\leq R
this yields the claim.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
0<\rho<R<\infty
and let
S\subset M
be
(R,\rho)
-relatively dense. Then, there is
\Sigma\subset S
with the following properties:

(a)

B_{\rho}(\Sigma):=\bigcup_{p\in\Sigma}B_{\rho}(p)
is
(3R,\rho)
-relatively dense and
B_{\rho}(\Sigma)\subset S
.

(b)

\bigcup_{p\in\Sigma}B_{3R}(p)\supset M
.

(c)

If
p\in\Sigma
and
\Sigma\setminus\{p\}\neq\emptyset
, then

R\leq\mathrm{d}(p,\Sigma\setminus\{p\})\leq 6R.

In particular,
\Sigma
can be chosen uniformly discrete and
B_{\rho}(\Sigma\setminus\{p\})
is
(6R,\rho)
-relatively dense in
M
.

For a convex subset
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Proposition
3.2
is proven in
[
SS21
]
.
The proof literally applies to our setting.

In the following we give a lower bound of the bottom of the Laplacian in the complement of
S
in terms of the function
\Lambda
. The idea is taken from
[
SS21
]
with the slight difference that Voronoï cells are in general not convex. This property however can be replaced by star-shapedness.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<\rho<R<\infty
and
S\subset M
be a proper
(R,\rho)
-relatively dense subset. Then we have

\lambda^{M,S}:=\inf\sigma(\Delta^{M,S})\geq\inf_{x\in M}\Lambda(x,\rho/2,3R).

Proof.

Let
\Sigma\subset S
be as in Proposition
3.2
.
We denote the Voronoï cell associated with
p\in\Sigma
by

G_{p}:=\{x\in M\colon\forall q\in\Sigma\setminus\{p\}\colon\mathrm{d}(x,p)<\mathrm{d}(x,q)\}.

Then, by construction

(i)

\overline{\bigcup_{p\in\Sigma}G_{p}}=M
,

(ii)

{G_{p}}\cap G_{q}=\emptyset
for
p,q\in\Sigma
with
p\neq q
,

(iii)

for all
p\in\Sigma
we have
B_{\rho/2}(p)\subset G_{p}\subset B_{3R}(p)
, and

(iv)

for all
p\in\Sigma
the Voronoï cell
G_{p}
is star-shaped with respect to
p
.

Note that
G_{p}
is in general not convex. In order to see the star-shapedness, let
x\in G_{p}
and
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
be a distance minimizing geodesic from
p
to
x
. Then for all
t\in[0,1]
we have

\mathrm{d}(\gamma(t),p)=\mathrm{d}(x,p)-\mathrm{d}(\gamma(t),x)

and consequently for all
q\in\Sigma
with
q\not=p

\mathrm{d}(\gamma(t),p)=\mathrm{d}(x,p)-\mathrm{d}(\gamma(t),x)<\mathrm{d}(x,q)-\mathrm{d}(\gamma(t),x)\leq\mathrm{d}(q,\gamma(t)).

Hence
\gamma(t)\in G_{p}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Let

\mathcal{F}=\{f\colon M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)}\to\mathbb{C}\colon f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)}),\ \hat{f}\in C^{1}(M),\ f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)})\},

where as before
\hat{f}\in L^{2}(M)
is the extension of
f
by zero. From Proposition
3.2
and the above properties we conclude for all
f\in\mathcal{F}

\|f\|_{L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)})}^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Sigma}\|f\mathbf{1}_{G_{p}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)}}\|_{L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)})}^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Sigma}\|f|_{G_{p}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)}}\|_{L^{2}(G_{p}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)})}^{2}.

Applying Proposition
3.1
to any
f|_{G_{p}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)}}
,
p\in\Sigma
, and summing up yields

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in M}\Lambda(x,\rho/2,3R)^{-1}\sum_{p\in\Sigma}\|\nabla f|_{G_{p}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(p)})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in M}\Lambda(x,\rho/2,3R)^{-1}\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M\setminus\overline{B_{\rho/2}(\Sigma)})}.\qed

4
Consequences of Ricci curvature lower bounds and the main result

We assume from now on that the Ricci curvature
\Ric
is bounded below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Recall that
M_{K}=M_{K}^{n}
denotes the model space of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
and constant curvature
K
, and
\Vol_{K}
its volume form. It is well-known that the volume density of
M_{K}
is given by
\omega_{K}=\mathrm{sn}_{K}^{n-1}
, cf.
[
GHL87
, p. 138]
,
where

\displaystyle\mathrm{sn}_{K}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sin(\sqrt{K}r)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-K}}\sinh(\sqrt{-K}r)&\text{if}\ K<0,\\
r&\text{if}\ K=0.\end{cases}

This explicit representation yields a lower bound for the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian in the complement of the relatively dense set. In order to obtain this, we will use the following estimate for the lifted spectrum of star-shaped domains. Although the proof is rather elementary, we give a complete proof for convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\rho
,
R
,
x
, and
G
as in Theorem
3.1
. There exists a constant
C_{n}>0
such that

\Lambda(x,\rho,R)\geq C_{n}\frac{(\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)\wedge\mathrm{sn}_{K}(R))^{n-2}}{\Vol_{K}(R)}.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that
\mathrm{sn}_{K}
is non-decreasing for
K\leq 0
, while for
K>0
it is non-decreasing on
[0,\pi/(2\sqrt{K})]
and non-increasing on
[\pi/(2\sqrt{K}),\pi/\sqrt{K}]
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

The Bishop-Gromov comparison theorem
[
CGT82
]
implies that for almost all
\theta\in S_{x}^{n-1}
and almost all
s,t\in(0,\infty)
with
s\leq r
we have

\frac{\omega(r,\theta)}{\omega(s,\theta)}\leq\frac{\omega_{K}(r)}{\omega_{K}(s)}.

Hence,

\Lambda(x,\rho,R)^{-1}=\esssup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}_{x}^{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{R}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\omega(r,\theta)}{\omega(s,\theta)}\mathrm{d}s\ \mathrm{d}r\\
\leq\int_{\rho}^{R}\omega_{K}(r)\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\omega_{K}(s)}\ \mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{\Vol_{K}(R)}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\omega_{K}(s)}.

We are left with estimating the integral on the right-hand side.
As
\omega_{K}(r)=\mathrm{sn}_{K}^{n-1}(r)
, we distinguish the cases of
K
being zero, negative, or positive.

\mathbf{1^{\mathrm{st}}}
case
\mathbf{K=0}
:

In this case, we have

\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\omega_{K}(s)}=\int_{\rho}^{R}s^{-n+1}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{R^{n-2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n-2}\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}=\frac{1}{n-2}\frac{1}{\mathrm{sn}_{0}(\rho)^{n-2}}.

\mathbf{2^{\mathrm{nd}}}
case
\mathbf{K<0}
:

Substituting
t=\mathrm{sn}_{K}(s)
, we obtain using the first case

\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\omega_{K}(s)}=\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(s)^{n-1}}=\int_{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)}^{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(R)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}{t^{n-1}}}\leq\int_{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)}^{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(R)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{{t^{n-1}}}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{n-2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)^{n-2}}.

\mathbf{3^{\mathrm{rd}}}
case
\mathbf{K>0}
:

In this case, the function
s\mapsto\mathrm{sn}_{K}(s)
is increasing on
[0,\pi/(2/\sqrt{K})]
, and decreasing on
[\pi/(2/\sqrt{K}),\pi/\sqrt{K}]
. We distinguish the following subcases.

(i)

Assume
0<\rho<R\leq\pi/(2\sqrt{K})
. By using the elementary inequality
2x/\pi\leq\mathrm{sn}_{K}(x)\leq x
and the first case, we obtain

\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\omega_{K}(s)}=\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(s)^{n-1}}\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{\rho}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{n-1}}\leq\frac{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{n-1}}{n-2}\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\leq\frac{\frac{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{n-1}}{n-2}}{\mathrm{sn}_{K}(\rho)^{n-2}}.

(ii)

Assume

Lemma 4.8

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\rho,m}\leq\alpha,X_{2\alpha+\tau_{\rho,m}}\in B_{\rho/2}(x))\\

One loop reduced QED for massive fermions within an innovative formalism
Victor Miguel Banda Guzmán, Adnan Bashir, Luis Albino, Dania Rodríguez-Tzintzun

Abstract
We carry out a detailed study of the three-point fermion-photon interaction vertex at one loop order for massive fermions in reduced quantum electrodynamics. This calculation is carried out in arbitrary covariant gauges and space-time dimensions within a recently proposed innovative approach based upon an efficient combination of the first and second order formalisms of quantum electrodynamics. This procedure provides a natural decomposition of the vertex into its components which are longitudinal and transverse to the photon momentum. It also separates the spin and scalar degrees of freedom of a fermion interacting electromagnetically, allowing us to readily establish the gauge-independence of the Pauli form factor and compute it in an expeditious manner. All incoming and outgoing momenta are taken off-shell at the outset. However, we present results for cases of particular kinematic interest whenever required. For the sake of completeness, we also provide expressions for the massive fermion self energy and photon vacuum polarization, verifying known expressions for massless reduced quantum electrodynamics and computing the renormalization constants 𝒵 1 {\cal Z}_{1} , 𝒵 2 {\cal Z}_{2} and 𝒵 3 {\cal Z}_{3} . As we provide general expressions for the computed Green functions, we readily reproduce and confirm the results for standard quantum electrodynamics. Comparing the two cases, we infer that the Pauli form factor for reduced quantum electrodynamics is 8 / 3 8/3 times that for the standard QED in four dimensions, implying a higher Landé g g -factor. We expect our perturbative calculation of the fermion-photon vertex to serve as a guide for any non-perturbative construction of this Green function, invariably required in the Schwinger-Dyson equation studies of the subject. We also comment on the Landau-Khalatnikov-Fradkin transformations of the massive fermion propagator and provide a comparison with its one loop calculation.

Introduction

Electromagnetism is a fundamental force of nature, more studied and far better understood than any other interaction at the elementary level, i.e., strong or weak interactions. It is characterized by the following action of quantum electrodynamics (QED) :

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptS=\int d^{d_{e}}x\Big[-\dfrac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\dfrac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu})^{2}+\overline{\psi}(i\not{\partial}-m)\psi

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\,e\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi A_{\mu}\Big]\,,

(1)

where
\psi
is the fermion field,
A^{\mu}
is the gauge field,
F^{\mu\nu}
is the electromagnetic tensor field,
e
is the electric charge of the fermion,
\xi
is the covariant gauge fixing parameter and
d_{e}
is the number of space-time dimensions (see appendix
A
for our conventions). For
d_{e}=4

1

d_{e}=3
it is super-renormalizable. Though QED has been extensively studied for more than 50 years, it continues to be an active field of research. For example, precision tests of the standard model, see for example
Aoyama et al. 2020
;
Colangelo et al. 2022

Aznauryan et al. 2013
;
Aguilar et al. 2019
;
Hernández-Pinto et al. 2023
, ensure QED interactions are increasingly relevant and important.

Moreover, new variations of standard QED find applications in condensed matter systems. In the last decades, for example, a considerable research effort has been dedicated to study an extended version of the action in Eq. (
1

Gorbar et al. 2001

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptS_{\text{NL}}=\int d^{d_{e}}x\Big[-\dfrac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}D^{s-2}F^{\mu\nu}-\dfrac{1}{2\xi}\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}D^{s-2}\partial_{\nu}A^{\nu}

\displaystyle\hskip 54.06023pt+\,\overline{\psi}(i\not{\partial}-m)\psi-e\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi A_{\mu}\Big]\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\int d^{d_{e}}x\;D^{s}\phi(x)e^{ikx}=|k|^{s}\hat{\phi}(k)\,,

II
The one loop Vertex
V^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)

While we refer the reader to Ref.
Guzmán and Bashir 2023

1.

We arrive at our result efficiently and the decomposition of the fermion-photon vertex into its longitudinal and transverse components is achieved naturally without a Ball-Chiu
Ball and Chiu 1980
decomposition.

2.

Moreover, the combined analysis allows us to track those terms which identically vanish when external momenta are taken on-shell.
The operator
(\not{p}-m)

(\not{p}+m)
is kept on the left. Therefore, when on-shell conditions
(\not{p}-m)u_{s}(p)=0

Employing on-shell symmetries of the Feynman integrals, we observe that the terms which depend explicitly on the covariant gauge parameter
\xi

These cancellations lead to a compact expression for this quantity in an arbitrary space-time dimension in terms of scalar integrals with: (i) higher powers of scalar propagators and (ii) shifted dimensions. It hints towards a possible analogous simplification in the evaluation of the anomalous magnetic moment of charged fermions at higher orders of perturbation theory.

\displaystyle-2(4-d_{e})k\cdot p^{\prime}\,J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}-4k\cdot p\,J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}+4k\cdot p\,J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}

\displaystyle+2k\cdot p(1-\tilde{\xi})\Big[]J_{1,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}-2p\cdot p^{\prime}J_{2,2,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}-4m^{2}\,J_{3,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}-J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}\Big],

\displaystyle f_{2T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2m(6-d_{e})\,J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}-2m\,J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}

(23)

\displaystyle+m(1-\tilde{\xi})\Big[J_{1,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}-2(m^{2}+p\cdot p^{\prime})J_{2,2,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}-4(m^{2}+p\cdot p^{\prime})J_{3,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}-2J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}\Big]\,.

The spinor product
\overline{u}_{s}(p^{\prime})V^{\mu}u_{s}(p)
can thus be written in the standard form

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{s^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})V^{\mu}u_{s}(p)=e\overline{u}_{s^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\left[F_{1}(k^{2})\,\gamma^{\mu}-\dfrac{1}{2m}F_{2}(k^{2})\sigma^{\mu\nu}k_{\nu}\right]u_{s}(p)\,,

(24)

where, explicitly,

\displaystyle F_{1}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{2}\Gamma(\overline{\epsilon}_{e})}{(4\pi)^{\epsilon_{e}+\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\Big\{(d_{e}-2)J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}+(2-d_{e})J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}-2(2m^{2}-k^{2})J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}

(25)

\displaystyle+\left[4(4-d_{e})m^{2}-(6-d_{e})k^{2}\right]J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}+4m^{2}(d_{e}-2)\left(J_{2,2,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}+2J_{3,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}\right)

\displaystyle-(1-\tilde{\xi})\Big[J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}-(2m^{2}-k^{2})J_{1,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}+(4m^{4}+(2m^{2}-k^{2})^{2})J_{2,2,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}

\displaystyle+8m^{2}(2m^{2}-k^{2})J_{3,1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}+(4m^{2}-k^{2})J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}\Big]\,\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle F_{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{4e^{2}m^{2}\Gamma(\overline{\epsilon}_{e})}{(4\pi)^{\epsilon_{e}+\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\left[2J_{2,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}+(2-d_{e})(2J_{3,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4}+J_{2,2,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+4})\right]\,.

(26)

These are the general expressions for the Dirac and Pauli form factors at one loop order for RQED as a funcion of the photon momentum squared
k^{2}
while the incoming and outgoing fermions are on-shell.
We now discuss each of these form factors in greater detail in the following subsections.

III.1
The Dirac form factor

Focusing on the the Dirac form factor
F_{1}(k^{2})
given in Eq. (
25
), we can mould it into a compact form if we make systematic use of the equations
in (
71
). It can be rewritten as a linear combination of the scalar integrals
J_{0,2,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
,
J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
and
J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
as follows:

\displaystyle F_{1}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{2}\Gamma(1-\epsilon_{e})}{(4\pi)^{\epsilon_{e}+\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\Bigg\{\left(\dfrac{1}{2(d_{e}-2+\epsilon_{e})(d_{e}-3+\epsilon_{e})}\right)\Bigg[2(d_{e}-2)(3-d_{e}-\epsilon_{e})^{2}J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}

(27)

\displaystyle+\Big(4m^{2}[4-2d_{e}+(2-4d_{e}+d_{e}^{2})\epsilon_{e}+2(d_{e}-1)\epsilon_{e}^{2}]+k^{2}[d_{e}^{3}+3d_{e}^{2}(\epsilon_{e}-3)

\displaystyle-8(\epsilon_{e}-2)^{2}+2d_{e}(15-10\epsilon_{e}+\epsilon_{e}^{2})]\Big)J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}\Bigg]-(1-\tilde{\xi})\Bigg(J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}+\dfrac{2m^{2}J_{2,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}}{d_{e}-3+\epsilon_{e}}\Bigg)\Bigg\}\,.

Since

\displaystyle J_{a,0,b}^{d_{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma(d_{e}-a-2b)}{\Gamma(d_{e}-a-b)\Gamma(a)}\Gamma\left(a+b-\dfrac{d_{e}}{2}\right)\,(m^{2})^{\frac{d_{e}}{2}-a-b}\,,

(28)

we readily obtain

\displaystyle J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}+\dfrac{2m^{2}J_{2,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}}{d_{e}-3+\epsilon_{e}}=0\,.

(29)

Thus the Dirac form factor in
Eq. (
27
) reduces to a gauge-independent expression:

\displaystyle F_{1}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{2}\Gamma(1-\epsilon_{e})}{(4\pi)^{\epsilon_{e}+\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\left(\dfrac{1}{2(d_{e}-2+\epsilon_{e})(d_{e}-3+\epsilon_{e})}\right)\Bigg[2(d_{e}-2)(3-d_{e}-\epsilon_{e})^{2}J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}

(30)

\displaystyle+\Big(4m^{2}[4-2d_{e}+(2-4d_{e}+d_{e}^{2})\epsilon_{e}+2(d_{e}-1)\epsilon_{e}^{2}]+k^{2}[d_{e}^{3}+3d_{e}^{2}(\epsilon_{e}-3)

\displaystyle-8(\epsilon_{e}-2)^{2}+2d_{e}(15-10\epsilon_{e}+\epsilon_{e}^{2})]\Big)J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}\Bigg].

If we set
\epsilon_{e}=0
and use the identities in Eqs. (
72
), we arrive at the Dirac form factor for standard QED:

\displaystyle F_{1}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{e^{2}}{m^{2}(4\pi)^{\frac{d_{e}}{2}}(d_{e}-4)(d_{e}-3)(k^{2}-4m^{2})}\Bigg[-2(d_{e}-2)[k^{2}+(d_{e}-5)(d_{e}-2)m^{2}]J_{0,1,0}^{d_{e}}

(31)

\displaystyle-m^{2}(d_{e}-3)[(16+d_{e}^{2}-7d_{e})k^{2}-8m^{2}]J_{1,1,0}^{d_{e}}\Bigg],

which agrees with the result computed in the seminal work of Davydychev et. al. in Ref.
Davydychev et al. 2001
.

III.2
The Pauli form factor

For the simplification and eventual evaluation of the Pauli form factor, it is easy to appreciate that the combination of scalar integrals in Eq. (
26
) can be rewritten in a much simpler manner by a direct application of Feynman parameterization without the need to use elaborate expressions given in Appendix
B
to express it in terms of master integrals. Therefore, with some straightforward algebra, it can be shown that,

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptF_{2}(k^{2})=\dfrac{4e^{2}m^{2}\Gamma\left(3-\epsilon_{e}-\frac{d_{e}}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\epsilon_{e}+\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt\times\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy\,\dfrac{2x+(2-d_{e})(x^{2}+xy)}{(1-x-y)^{\epsilon_{e}}A^{3-\epsilon_{e}-\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\,,

(32)

where
A
has been defined as

\displaystyle A\equiv(x+y)^{2}m^{2}-xyk^{2}+(1-x-y)m^{2}_{\gamma}\,.

(33)

Here we have introduced a photon mass regulator
m_{\gamma}
to regularize infrared divergences. For
\epsilon_{e}=0
, which implies
d_{\gamma}=d_{e}
, the Pauli form factor in Eq. (
32
) reads as,

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptF_{2}^{S}(k^{2})=\dfrac{4e^{2}m^{2}\Gamma\left(3-\frac{d_{e}}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt\times\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1-x}dy\,\dfrac{2x+(2-d_{e})(x^{2}+xy)}{A^{3-\frac{d_{e}}{2}}}\,.

(34)

It yields the standard (as the superscript “S” suggests) result for
d_{e}=4
and
d_{e}=3
corresponding to conventional QED4 and QED3, respectively, (see for example Ref.
Guzmán and Bashir 2023
). If we use the notation
F_{2}^{S-4}(k^{2})
for the Pauli form factor in 4 space-time dimensions for standard QED, we easily reproduce the result obtained first by Schwinger:

\displaystyle F_{2}^{S-4}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha}{2\pi}\,.

(35)

Now notice that
d_{\gamma}=4
and
d_{e}=3
implies
\epsilon_{e}={1}/{2}
. In this case of RQED (as the notation below with the superscript
R
signifies), the Pauli form factor of Eq. (
32
) simplifies to the following integral form over the Feynman parameters:

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98857ptF_{2}^{R}(k^{2})=\dfrac{e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\int_{0}^{1-x}dy\,\dfrac{2x-(x^{2}+xy)}{\sqrt{1-x-y}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\dfrac{1}{(x+y)^{2}-xyc}\right)\,,

(36)

where
c={k^{2}}/{m^{2}}
. Moreover, we have discarded the redundant photon mass regulator in this case as the result is convergent anyway.

After a convenient change of variables
y\xrightarrow{}x\left({1}/{y}-1\right)
in the second integral over the
y
-variable, and interchanging the order of integration, we can readily integrate over the variable
x
. The above equation then reduces to:

\displaystyle F_{2}^{R}(k^{2})=\dfrac{8\alpha}{3\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,\dfrac{y}{1-c\,y(1-y)}\,,

(37)

where, as usual, the fine structure constant or the QED coupling is defined as
\alpha=e^{2}/(4\pi)
. Now making use of the simple identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dy\,\dfrac{y}{1+cy(1-y)}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,\dfrac{1}{1+cy(1-y)}\,,

(38)

Eq. (
37
) for the Pauli form factor for the case of RQED in
d_{\gamma}=4
and
d_{e}=3
dimensions for the photons and charged fermions, respectively, can be cast in the following equivalent form

\displaystyle F_{2}^{R}(k^{2})=\dfrac{4\alpha}{3\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,\dfrac{1}{1-c\,y(1-y)}\,.

(39)

Notice that
F_{2}^{S-4}(k^{2})
for the Pauli form factor in
4
space-time dimensions for standard QED, Eq. (
34
), is trivially related to
F_{2}^{R}(k^{2})
, Eq. (
39
), through the following multiplication constant:

\displaystyle F_{2}^{R}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{8}{3}\,F_{2}^{S-4}(k^{2})\,,

(40)

since,
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
,

\displaystyle F_{2}^{S-4}(k^{2})=\dfrac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{dy}{1+cy(1-y)}\,.

(41)

Thus the Pauli form factor for the reduced theory is the same as in the standard QED in four dimensions scaled up by a factor of
\frac{8}{3}
.

We can now take the photon on-shell and consider the case
k^{2}=0
which corresponds to the conventional definition of the anomalous magnetic moment of the fermion. Therefore,
Eq. (
39
) implies that,

\displaystyle F_{2}^{R}(0)=\dfrac{4\alpha}{3\pi}\,.

(42)

It agrees with the result reported in Ref.
Menezes et al. 2017
modulus the sign. As we reproduce known results in the literature for other cases and do not expect a change of sign in the value of the anomalous magnetic moment of the fermion as compared to the one for standard QED, we are confident of our result.

V
Renormalization constants

When
d_{\gamma}=d_{e}=4
, and
d_{\gamma}=4
with
d_{e}=3
, the scalar integrals
J_{1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
,
J_{2,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}
and
J_{1,1+\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}
in equation (
45
) for the fermion self energy are ultraviolet (UV) divergent. For the case of the vertex function
V^{\mu}
the only UV divergent scalar integrals in Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) are
J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
and
J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}
. Note that the divergences of the fields, charge and mass can be absorbed into the renormalization constants
{\cal{Z}}_{1}
,
{\cal{Z}}_{2}
,
{\cal{Z}}_{3}
and
{\cal Z}_{m}
which relate the bare fermionic field
\psi
, the bare coupling constant
e
, the bare photon field
A^{\mu}
and the fermion mass
m
, respectively, to the renormalized fermion field
\psi_{r}
, charge
e_{r}
, photon field
A_{r}^{\mu}
and mass
m_{r}
as:

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle={\cal{Z}}_{2}\psi_{r}\,,

\displaystyle\quad e

\displaystyle={\cal{Z}}_{1}e_{r}\,,

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle={\cal{Z}}_{3}A^{\mu}_{r}\,,

\displaystyle\quad m

\displaystyle={\cal Z}_{m}m_{r}\,.

(58)

Let us proceed to evaluate these constants. Notice that after the Feynman parameterization, the
two-point scalar integrals
J_{a,b}^{D}(p)
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle J_{a,b}^{D}(p)=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(a+b-\frac{D}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\dfrac{x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}}{B^{a+b-\frac{D}{2}}}\,,

(59)

where

\displaystyle B=x(x-1)p^{2}+x\,m^{2}+y\,m_{\gamma}^{2}\,.

(60)

Thus the ultraviolet (UV) divergent parts of the fermion self energy in Eq. (
45
) read as

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\Sigma_{\text{UV}}^{S-4}=-\dfrac{e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[-m\left(\dfrac{3+\xi}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)+\dfrac{\xi}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\not{p}\right],

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\Sigma_{\text{UV}}^{R}=-\dfrac{e^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[-m\left(\dfrac{4+2\tilde{\xi}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)+\left(\dfrac{6\tilde{\xi}-4}{3\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)\not{p}\right].

(61)

Here
\Sigma_{\text{UV}}^{S-4}
represents the UV divergent part for standard QED where we have fixed
\epsilon_{e}=0
, and
d_{e}=4-2\epsilon_{\gamma}
while
\Sigma_{\text{UV}}^{R}
represents the UV divergent part for RQED were we have set
\epsilon_{e}=\frac{1}{2}
, and
d_{e}=3-2\epsilon_{\gamma}
.

Therefore, one can readily compute the renormalizations constants
{\cal{Z}}_{2}
from Eqs. (
61
) at the one loop order in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme:

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{2}^{S-4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}\left(\dfrac{\xi}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{2}^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{\alpha}{6\pi}\left(\dfrac{3\tilde{\xi}-2}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)\,,

(62)

which agree, respectively, with the well known result for standard QED in
d_{e}=4
dimension, see for example
Srednicki 2007
, and for the massless result in RQED in
d_{e}=3
and
d_{\gamma}=4
dimensions reported in Ref.
Teber 2012
.

Similarly, drawing on Eqs. (
61
) again, we obtain the following fermion mass renormalizations constants in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme:

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{m}^{S-4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}\left(\dfrac{3+\xi}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{m}^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}\left(\dfrac{4+2\tilde{\xi}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right).

(63)

Now, since
J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}
and
J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}
are the only UV divergent scalar integrals that show up in the vertex, the term
(d_{e}-3+\tilde{\xi})J_{1,0,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}}+(2-d_{e})J_{1,1,\overline{\epsilon}_{e}}^{d_{e}+2}
in equation (
16
) is the unique contribution to the UV-divergent part for the vertex
V^{\mu}
. Thus, following a similar analysis as presented for the fermion self energy, the UV-divergent parts for
V_{\mu}
, within the dimensional regularization scheme, read as

\displaystyle V_{\mu}^{S-4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{3}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\dfrac{\xi}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle V_{\mu}^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{3}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(\dfrac{6\tilde{\xi}-{4}}{3\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)\,\gamma_{\mu}\,.

(64)

The first equation corresponds to the standard QED in 4 dimensions, and the second equation to RQED in
d_{\gamma}=4
and
d_{e}=3
.
We can identify
{\cal{Z}}_{1}
through Eq. (
64
), concluding that at the one loop order for both the standard QED as well as RQED:

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{1}={\cal Z}_{2}\,,

(65)

in agreement with the non-perturbative Ward-Takashi identity.

Finally, according to Eq. (

VI
Summary and conclusions

In this article, we obtain general expressions for the vertex function
V^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)
in general and Dirac and Pauli form factors
F_{1}(k^{2})
and
F_{2}(k^{2})
, respectively, in particular
in arbitrary covariant gauges and dimensions at one loop order in perturbation theory for QED.
We allow the bulk gauge field to reside in
d_{\gamma}
space-time dimensions interacting with a fermionic field whose dynamics might be restricted to different
space-time dimensions
d_{e}
, with
d_{e}\leq d_{\gamma}
. The main feature of this work is the novel computational tool available to us by a combination of first and second order formulation of QED as was employed in our previous work
Guzmán and Bashir 2023
.
As stated earlier, this coupled formalism has numerous advantageous as compared to the usual solely first order formulation of QED calculations:

1.

It renders the calculation efficient.

2.

It implements Ward-Takahashi identity and decomposes the fermion-photon vertex naturally into its longitudinal and transverse components with respect to the photon momentum without resorting to the customary Ball-Chiu construction.

3.

It allows us to systematically demonstrate that the Pauli form factor for on-shell fermions is independent of the gauge parameter for this general setup of potentially different
d_{\gamma}
and
d_{e}
dimensions by invoking identities which stem from the combined formalism.

4.

The extraction of the anomalous magnetic moment of the charged fermions is greatly expedited and we hope this combined formalism will be of practical usage in the computation of this and other physical observables at higher loops.

For the sake of completeness, we also compute fermion self energy
\Sigma
and photon vacuum polarization tensor

Abstract
The metric dimension, dim ( G ) \dim(G) , of a graph G G is a graph parameter motivated by robot navigation that has been studied extensively. Let G G be a graph with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) , and let d ⁡ ( x , y ) d(x,y) denote the length of a shortest x − y x-y path in G G . For a positive integer k k and for distinct x , y ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) x,y\in V(G) , let d k ​ ( x , y ) = min ⁡ { d ⁡ ( x , y ) , k + 1 } d_{k}(x,y)=\min\{d(x,y),k+1\} and let R k ​ { x , y } = { z ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) : d k ​ ( x , z ) ≠ d k ​ ( y , z ) } R_{k}\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d_{k}(x,z)\neq d_{k}(y,z)\} . A subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) is a distance- k k resolving set of G G if | S ∩ R k ​ { x , y } | ≥ 1 |S\cap R_{k}\{x,y\}|\geq 1 for any pair of distinct x , y ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) x,y\in V(G) , and the distance- k k dimension , dim k ( G ) \dim_{k}(G) , of G G is the minimum cardinality over all distance- k k resolving sets of G G . In this paper, we study the distance- k k dimension of graphs. We obtain some general bounds for distance- k k dimension. For all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , we characterize connected graphs G G of order n n with dim k ( G ) ≥ n − 2 \dim_{k}(G)\geq n-2 . We determine dim k ( G ) \dim_{k}(G) when G G is a cycle or a path. Introduction

Let
G
be a finite, simple, undirected, and connected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The
distance
between two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, denoted by

, is the length of a shortest path between
x
and
y
in
G
. Metric dimension, introduced by Slater
[
23
]
and by Harary and Melter
[
15
]
, is a graph parameter that has been studied extensively. For distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, let
R\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d(x,z)\neq d(y,z)\}
. A subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
resolving set
of
G
if
|S\cap R\{x,y\}|\geq 1
for any pair of distinct vertices
x
and
y
in
G
. The
metric dimension
of
G
, denoted by
\dim(G)
, is the minimum cardinality over all resolving sets of
G
. It is

-hard in general to compute
\dim(G)

[
19
,
12
]
.

Khuller et al.
[
19
]
considered robot navigation as one of the applications of metric dimension, where a robot that moves from node to node knows its distances to a set of landmarks, which are placed on the elements of the resolving set. Assuming that a sensor that can detect long distance to landmarks can be costly, the authors of
[
17
]
consider the situation where a robot can only detect landmarks that are adjacent to it. They define the adjacency dimension,
\textnormal{adim}(G)
, of
G
to be the minimum number of such landmarks that are needed for the robot to determine its position. More generally, if the landmark detection range of a robot is
k>0
, then the minimum number of such landmarks needed to determine the robot’s position on the graph is called the distance-
k
dimension (see
[
14
]
).

For a positive integer
k
and for
x,y\in V(G)
, let
d_{k}(x,y)=\min\{d(x,y),k+1\}
. For a positive integer
k
and for distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, let
R_{k}\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d_{k}(x,z)\neq d_{k}(y,z)\}
. A subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is a

k
resolving set
of
G
if
|S\cap R_{k}\{x,y\}|\geq 1
for any pair of distinct vertices
x
and
y
in
G
, and the
distance-
k
dimension
of
G
, denoted by
\dim_{k}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality over all distance-
k
resolving sets of
G
. The distance-
k
dimension of graphs was studied in
[
1
]
, where it was also investigated more generally for metric spaces. The complexity of the problem was studied in
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
, where it was shown that computing

is an NP-hard problem for any positive integer

were characterized in
[
9
]
, which also investigated the problem in a more general setting.

For an ordered set

of distinct vertices, the metric code and the distance-

metric code, respectively, of

with respect to

and the

of

. Jannesari and Omoomi
[
17

introduced adjacency dimension as a tool to study the metric dimension of lexicographic product graphs.

In this paper, we study the distance-

dimension of graphs. The paper is organized as follows. In Section

dimension of graphs. We prove that the maximum possible order of a graph

In Section

In Section

S\cap\{u,w\}\neq\emptyset

follows from the lower bounds that we proved in the last paragraph. However if

in this case.

Now we show that

.

Case 1:

, where

and
0\leq j\leq 1
. Note that

. Let

. So,
\dim_{k}(C_{n})\leq|S_{k}|=2x=\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
.

Case 2:

, where

and
2\leq j\leq k+2
. Note that
\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor=2x+1
. Let
S_{k}=\{u_{0},u_{2k+2}\}\cup(\cup_{i=1}^{x-1}\{u_{(3k+2)i+1},u_{(3k+2)i+2k+2}\})\cup\{u_{(3k+2)x+1}\}

is a distance-

with

\dim_{k}(C_{n})\leq|S_{k}|=2x+1=\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
.

Case 3:

, where

and
\lceil\frac{3k+5}{2}\rceil\leq j\leq 3k+1
. Note that
\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor=2x+2

\dim_{k}(C_{n})\leq|S_{k}|=2x+2=\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
.
∎

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
k
be any positive integer.

(a)

If
n\leq k+2
, then
\dim_{k}(P_{n})=1
.

(b)

If
k+3\leq n\leq 3k+3
, then
\dim_{k}(P_{n})=2
.

(c)

If
n\geq 3k+4
, then

\dim_{k}(P_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor&\mbox{ if }n\equiv 0,1,\ldots,k+2\pmod{(3k+2)},\\
\lfloor\frac{2n+4k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor&\mbox{ if }n\equiv k+3,\ldots,\lceil\frac{3k+5}{2}\rceil-1\pmod{(3k+2)},\\
\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor&\mbox{ if }n\equiv\lceil\frac{3k+5}{2}\rceil,\ldots,3k+1\pmod{(3k+2)}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
k
be a positive integer.

(a) If
n\leq k+2
, then
\dim_{k}(P_{n})=1
by Corollary
3.4
(a).

(b) Suppose
k+3\leq n\leq 3k+3
. If
P_{n}
is given by
u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1}
, then
\{u_{k},u_{\alpha}\}
, where
\alpha=\min\{2k+1,n-1\}
, forms a distance-
k
resolving set of
P_{n}
; thus
\dim_{k}(P_{n})\leq 2
. By Corollary
3.4
(a),
\dim_{k}(P_{n})=2
.

(c) Let

. First, we show that
\dim_{k}(P_{n})\leq\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
if
n\equiv 0,1,\ldots,k+2\pmod{(3k+2)}
or
n\equiv\lceil\frac{3k+5}{2}\rceil,\ldots,3k+1\pmod{(3k+2)}
, and
\dim_{k}(P_{n})\leq\lfloor\frac{2n+4k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
if
n\equiv k+3,\ldots,\lceil\frac{3k+5}{2}\rceil-1\pmod{(3k+2)}
. By Lemmas
5.15
and
5.11
, for
n\geq 3k+4
every distance-
k
resolving set of
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
, respectively, has cardinality at least three. Moreover, there exists a minimum distance-
k
resolving set
S
of

with a gap containing
2k+1
vertices

in

, where

and the subscript is taken modulo
n
. If

, then
S
forms a distance-
k
resolving set of
P_{n}
; thus

and the desired upper bounds follow from Theorem
5.12
.

Second, we show that
\dim_{k}(P_{n})\geq\lfloor\frac{2n+3k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
; moreover, we show that
\dim_{k}(P_{n})\geq\lfloor\frac{2n+4k-1}{3k+2}\rfloor
if

. Let
S_{k}
be a minimum distance-
k
resolving set of
P_{n}
such that the union gap of
S_{k}
contains
2k+1
vertices. If
|S_{k}|=2\ell
for some positive integer

, then at most
\ell-1
gaps contain more than

vertices by Lemma
5.15
(b) and those

(a); thus, the number of vertices belonging to the gaps of

or the union gap of

, and hence

, which implies

. If

(b) and those
\ell-1

or the union gap of

Theorem 6.10
.

For any integer
k\geq 2
, the value of
\dim_{k}(G)-\dim_{k}(G-e)
can be arbitrarily large.

Proof.

Let
G
be the graph in Figure
4
. For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
,
N_{G}(x_{i})=N_{G}(y_{i})=\{z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i}\}=N_{G-e}(x_{i})=N_{G-e}(y_{i})
. Let
k\geq 2
and
a\geq 2
be any integers. Let
S
be any minimum distance-
k
resolving set for
G-e
, and let
S^{\prime}
be any distance-
k
resolving set for
G
. By Observation
2.1
(b),
S\cap\{x_{i},y_{i}\}\neq\emptyset
and
S^{\prime}\cap\{x_{i},y_{i}\}\neq\emptyset
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots a\}
; without loss of generality let
S_{0}=\cup_{i=1}^{a}\{x_{i}\}\subseteq S\cap S^{\prime}
.

First, we show that
\dim_{k}(G-e)=a+1
. Since
r_{k,S_{0}}(z_{i})=r_{k,S_{0}}(z^{\prime}_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
in
G-e
,
|S|\geq a+1
, and hence
\dim_{k}(G-e)\geq a+1
. Since
S_{0}\cup\{v\}
forms a distance-
k
resolving set of
G-e
,
\dim_{k}(G-e)\leq a+1
. So,
\dim_{k}(G-e)=a+1
.

Second, we show that
\dim_{k}(G)=2a
. Note that, for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
,
r_{k,S_{0}}(z_{i})=r_{k,S_{0}}(z^{\prime}_{i})
in
G
and
R_{k}\{z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i}\}=\{z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i},t_{i}\}

k

\dim_{k}(G)\leq 2a
. Thus,
\dim_{k}(G)=2a
.

Therefore,
\dim_{k}(G)-\dim_{k}(G-e)=2a-(a+1)=a-1\rightarrow\infty
as

. ∎

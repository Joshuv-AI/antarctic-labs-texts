A refined study of Mazur’s Eisenstein theory
Jun Wang

Abstract
. We apply the methods of Fukaya, Kato and Sharifi to refine Mazur’s study of the Eisenstein ideal. Given prime numbers N N and p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 such that p | φ ⁡ ( N ) p\mid\varphi(N) , we study the quotient of the cohomology group of modular curve X 0 ​ ( N ) X_{0}(N) by the square of the Eisenstein ideal. We study two invariants b , c b,c attached to this quotient and compute c c . We propose a conjecture about the invariant b b which relates the structure of the ray class group of conductor N N to the modular symbols of X 0 ​ ( N ) X_{0}(N) . Assuming this conjecture, We compute the invariant b b .



1.
Introduction

In Mazur’s seminal work
[
4
]
, he gave a description of the Eisenstein quotient of the étale cohomology group of modular curve of prime level. In this paper, we use the methods of Fukaya, Kato and Sharifi to study the quotient of this cohomology group by the square of the Eisenstein ideal.

1.1.
Motivations

Let
N
be a prime and let
p\geq 5
such that
p\mid(N-1)
. let
\mathfrak{h}
be the
p
-adic Hecke algebra acting on the cohomology of étale cohomology group of modular curve
X_{0}(N)
, let
I
be the Eisenstein ideal.
In
[
4
]
, Mazur proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Mazur)
.

There is a direct sum decomposition of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})
-modules
H/IH=H^{+}/IH^{+}\oplus H^{-}/IH^{-}
,
where
H=H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X_{0}(N)_{/\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})
,
H^{\pm}
denote the eigenspaces for complex conjugation.

One natural question is: what can we say about the
\mathfrak{h}[\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})]
-module
H/I^{2}H
?
Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
, where
q
is the highest power of
p
dividing
N-1
. Viewing
H/I^{2}H
as representation of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)
, we have following four
group homomorphisms
:

(1)

a:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)\rightarrow\text{Aut}_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{-}/I^{2}H^{-})
,

(2)

b:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{+}/I^{2}H^{+},H^{-}/I^{2}H^{-})
,

(3)

c:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{-}/I^{2}H^{-},H^{+}/I^{2}H^{+})
,

(4)

d:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)\rightarrow\text{Aut}_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{+}/I^{2}H^{+})
.

One can prove that the homomorphisms
a
and
d
cut out a field extension
F_{0}
, the homomorphism
b
cuts out a field
F_{-1}
, and the homomorphism
c
cuts out a field
F_{1}
, where
F_{i}
means that the group
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{i}/K)
via
\chi_{p}^{i}
.

Let
U=\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times})^{q}
. Using global class field theory and Galois cohomology, we have the following canonical isomorphisms of abelian groups:

(1)

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{0}/K)\cong U
,

(2)

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{1}/K)\cong\mu_{q}
,

(3)

\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{-1}/K)\cong U^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}
,

where
\mu_{q}^{-1}=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mu_{q},\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
. The
F_{i}
are class fields, and all of them are totally tamely ramified at
N
over
K
.

Remark 1.2
.

We identify
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{-1}/K)
with
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{N}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{G}
, where
G=Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})/\mathbb{Q})
. The isomorphism
(3)
is from this identification, for the details, one can see Proposition
6.7
.

In
[
4
]
, Mazur uses modular symbols to construct a canonical winding isomorphism:

e:I/I^{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{-}/IH^{-}\otimes\mu_{q}\xrightarrow{\sim}U.

We know that the images of
a,d
are both in
1+I/I^{2}
. Identifying
1+I/I^{2}
with
I/I^{2}
, we may view
a
and
d
as homomorphisms:

(1)

a:U\rightarrow I/I^{2}
,

(2)

d:U\rightarrow I/I^{2}
.

Using the winding homomorphism
e
, we get maps
I/I^{2}\rightarrow I/I^{2}
that are multiplication by an integer in
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
. We use
\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{d}
to denote these two numbers. The invariants
\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{d}
have been already computed by B. Mazur, F. Calegari, R. Sharifi and W. Stein in the unpublished manuscript
[
6
]
, and their results are
\widetilde{a}=-1,\widetilde{d}=1
.

For the homomorphisms
b
and
c
, we need a more careful analysis. We carefully choose an isomorphism
H^{+}/IH^{+}\cong\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
: see Remark
3.6
. For
H^{-}/IH^{-}
, we can identify it with
I/I^{2}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}
using
e
.

It is easy to see that the image of
b\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{+}/IH^{+}
,
IH^{-}/I^{2}H^{-})
, the image of
c\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{-}/IH^{-},IH^{+}/I^{2}H^{+})
.

Since

(1)

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{+}/IH^{+}
,
IH^{-}/I^{2}H^{-})\cong IH^{-}/I^{2}H^{-}\cong I^{2}/I^{3}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}
,

(2)

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(H^{-}/IH^{-},IH^{+}/I^{2}H^{+})\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}(I/I^{2}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1},I/I^{2})\cong\mu_{q}
,

we can rewrite
b
and
c
as follows:

(1)

b:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{-1}/K)\cong H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{N}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{G}\cong U^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}\cong I^{2}/I^{3}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}\rightarrow I^{2}/I^{3}\otimes\mu_{q}^{-1}
,

(2)

c:\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{1}/K)\cong\mu_{q}\rightarrow\mu_{q}

Hence the homomorphisms

and

give us two integers in
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
which were constructed in
[
6
]

\widetilde{b}
and
\widetilde{c}
to denote them. The integers
\widetilde{b}
and
\widetilde{c}
are units in

, which can be seen using
[
10
]
, or
[
19
]
. What we want to understand in the paper are the exact values of

and
\widetilde{c}
.

Remarks 1.3
.

(1)

The map
b
is a map from class group
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(F_{-1}/K)
to
IH^{-}/I^{2}H^{-}
. Note that

can be described explicitly by modular symbols, so the map

(2)

1.2.
Results and the relations to the work of Fukaya, Kato and Sharifi

Our first result is:

Theorem 1.4
.

The invariant
\tilde{c}
is 1.

Remark 1.5
.

Our proof follows from the method of
[
21
, Section 9.6]
.

The homomorphism
b
has a conjectural inverse which is closely related to the work of Fukaya, Kato and Sharifi. We give a brief introduction of their work here.

In
[
20
]
, for positive integers
M
and prime
p\geq 5
such that
p\nmid M
, Sharifi formulated a series of remarkable conjectures which relate the arithmetic of cyclotomic fields to the homology of modular curves. Roughly speaking, he used cup products of cyclotomic units to define two maps:

(1)

\varpi_{M_{r}}^{0}:H_{1}(X_{1}(M_{r}),\tilde{C}_{\infty},{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{Np},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))^{+},\,\,\,\,\varpi_{M_{r}}:H_{1}(X_{1}(M_{r}),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))^{+},

where
M_{r}:=Mp^{r}(r\geq 0)
,
\tilde{C}_{\infty}
is the set of cusps of
X_{1}(M_{r})
that do not lie above the
0
-cusp of
X_{0}(M_{r})
. The map
\varpi_{M_{r}}^{0}
takes the adjusted Manin symbol
[u,v]^{*}
(Definition
6.9
) to the cup product
(1-\zeta_{M_{r}}^{u},1-\zeta_{M_{r}}^{v})
.

The first conjecture Sharifi made is:

Conjecture 1.6

(Sharifi)
.

(Eisenstein quotient conjecture)
Let
\mathfrak{I}_{\infty}
be the ideal of the modular Hecke algebra generated by
T_{l}-1-\langle l\rangle l\,\,\text{for}\,\,l\nmid M_{r}
, and
T_{q}-1
for
q\mid M_{r}
, and let
I_{\infty}
be the image of
\mathfrak{I}_{\infty}
in the cuspidal Hecke algebra. Then
\varpi_{M_{r}}^{0},\varpi_{M_{r}}
induce maps:

\varpi_{M_{r}}^{0}:H_{1}(X_{1}(M_{r}),\tilde{C}_{\infty},{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}/\mathfrak{I}_{\infty}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{Mp},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))^{+},

\varpi_{M_{r}}:H_{1}(X_{1}(M_{r}),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}/I_{\infty}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))^{+}.

Remark 1.7
.

In
[
21
]
, Fukaya and Kato proved these conjectures for the modular curve
X_{1}(M_{r})
when
r\neq 0
. For modular curves with levels not divisible by
p
, the conjecture is still open.

Now let’s fix an even character

\theta:(\mathbb{Z}/Np\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\overline{{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}}^{\times},

and let
\omega
be the teichmüller character.
Under the following assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

(1)

p\nmid\varphi(M)
and
r>0
.

(2)

\theta
is primitive.

(3)

If the restriction of
\theta\omega^{-1}
to
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/Mp\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is trivial, then the restriction
\theta\omega^{-1}|_{(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}
satisfies
\theta\omega^{-1}(p)\neq 1
.

(4)

In the case
M=1
,
\theta\neq\omega^{2}
.

Sharifi constructed a map

\Upsilon_{\theta}:H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{\theta}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1}(M_{r}),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})_{\theta}/I_{\infty}

and he made another conjecture:

Conjecture 1.8
.

The map
\varpi_{\theta}
and
\Upsilon_{\theta}
are inverse to each other.

Remark 1.9
.

This conjecture has been proved by Fukaya and Kato under certain assumptions. For the details, one can see
[
21
]
.

In our setting, we propose a new conjecture that is analogous to Conjecture
1.8
.

Conjecture 1.10
.

The following two maps are inverse to each other.

b:H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{N}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{G}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})_{\Delta}/I_{\infty},

\varpi_{N,G}:H_{1}(X_{1}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})_{\Delta}/I_{\infty}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{N}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{G}.

Here
\Delta
is the automorphism group generated by diamond operators.

Remarks 1.11
.

Our setting is different from the setting in
[
20
]
in the following ways:

(1)

The level
N
is not divisible by
p
. Moreover,
N\equiv 1\bmod p
.

(2)

Our tame character is the trivial character since we are essentially considering the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
.

(3)

In the work of Fukaya, Kato and Sharifi, their
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p},\zeta_{M_{r}}],{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}(2))_{\theta}
is related to unramified extension of cyclotomic field. In our case,

is related to the tame ramified extension

.

Using Mazur’s results and duality, we can relates the homology of
X_{1}(N)
with the cohomology of
X_{0}(N)
. Our result for
\widetilde{b}
is:

Theorem 1.12
.

Assume Conjecture
1.6
for
X_{1}(N)
and Conjecture
1.10
, the invariant
\widetilde{b}
is
1
.

Lemma 6.35
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
,
the image of
(l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*}
in
\frac{IH_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}{I^{2}H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}\cong I/I^{2}\otimes\frac{H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}{IH_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}
is
\eta_{l}\otimes\frac{u}{v}
, where we view
\frac{u}{v}
as an element in
U\cong\frac{H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}{IH_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
\pi((l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*})=\eta_{l}\pi([u,v]^{*})
. Now, let us compute
\pi([u,v]^{*})=\pi(W_{\zeta_{N}}([u,v]))=W_{N}\pi([u,v])
. Using Hensel’s lemma, we have that the image of
W_{N}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{0}(N)_{\mathfrak{P}}

H_{1}(X_{0}(N),{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p})^{+}_{\mathfrak{P}}
, we have
\pi([u,v]^{*})=-\pi(\langle v\rangle[\frac{u}{v},1])=-[\frac{u}{v},1]
. Let
x\in\mathbb{Z}
be a lifting of
\frac{u}{v}
. By computation, we have
[\frac{u}{v},1]=\{0,\frac{1}{x}\}
. Using Theorem
2.6
, we have that the element
-\{0,\frac{1}{x}\}
corresponds to
\frac{u}{v}\in U
.
∎

Lemma 6.36
.

Via

Lemma 6.37
.

Via
\varpi_{N}
, the element
(l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*}
maps to
l^{l-1}\otimes\frac{u}{v}

Proof.

Note that by definition, we have

\varpi_{N}((l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*})=\varpi_{N}^{0}((l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*}).

If we assume the Eisenstein quotient conjecture for

and note that

(20)

l+\langle l\rangle-T(l)=(l-1)(1-\langle l\rangle)\mod I_{\infty}.

We have

\varpi_{N}^{0}((l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*})=\varpi_{N}^{0}((l-1)(1-\langle l\rangle)[u,v]^{*})=(l-1)(1-\sigma_{l}^{-1})\varpi_{N}^{0}([u,v]^{*}).

By the isomorphism in Corollary

\partial\varpi_{N}^{0}([u,v]^{*})=\frac{u}{v}\in U

\varpi_{N}((l+\langle l\rangle-T(l))[u,v]^{*})=l^{l-1}\otimes\frac{u}{v}

∎

Theorem 6.38
.

Suppose that Conjecture
6.27
and Conjecture
6.32
hold. Then the invariant
\tilde{b}
equals 1.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.35
and Lemma
6.37
, we know that

\varpi_{N,G}(\eta_{l}\otimes\frac{u}{v})=l^{l-1}\otimes\frac{u}{v}.

Assuming Conjecture
6.32

\chi_{b}(l^{l-1}\otimes\frac{u}{v}\otimes\zeta_{q})=\eta_{l}\otimes\frac{u}{v}\otimes\zeta_{q}.

Since the winding isomorphism identifies

with
\eta_{l}
(Theorem

), we know that
\tilde{b}=1
.
∎

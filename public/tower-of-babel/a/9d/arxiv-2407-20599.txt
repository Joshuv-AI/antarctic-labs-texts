Cooperative Nernst Effect of Multilayer Systems: Parallel Circuit Model Study
Hiroyasu Matsuura, Alexander Riss, Fabian Garmroudi, Michael Parzer, Ernst Bauer

Abstract
Transverse thermoelectric power generation has emerged as a topic of immense interest in recent years owing to the orthogonal geometry which enables better scalability and fabrication of devices. Here, we investigate the thickness dependence of longitudinal and transverse responses in film-substrate systems i.e., the Seebeck coefficient, Hall coefficient, Nernst coefficient and anomalous Nernst coefficient in a unified and general manner based on the circuit model, which describes the system as the parallel setup. By solving the parallel circuit model, we show that the transverse responses exhibit a significant peak, indicating the importance of a cooperative effect between the film and the substrate, arising from circulating currents that occur in these multilayer systems in the presence of a temperature gradient. Finally, on the basis of realistic material parameters, we predict that the Nernst effect in bismuth thin films on doped silicon substrates is boosted to unprecedented values if the thickness ratio is tuned accordingly, motivating experimental validation.

II
Linear response for the electrical and thermoelectric effects

In this section, we study the linear response for electrical and thermoelectric effects with/without magnetic field
[
38
]
.
When applied fields in the system A comprise an electric field (
{\bm{E}}_{\rm A}
), a thermal gradient (
({\nabla}T)_{\rm A}
), and a magnetic field along the
z
-axis (
B_{z,{\rm A}}
), the linear response in the
x
,
y
directions of system A is

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}j^{x}_{\rm e,A}\\
j^{y}_{\rm e,A}\\
j^{x}_{\rm Q,A}\\
j^{y}_{\rm Q,A}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\bar{L}_{\rm A}\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,{\rm A}}\\
E_{y,{\rm A}}\\
-(\nabla T)_{x,{\rm A}}/T\\
-(\nabla T)_{y,{\rm A}}/T\\
\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
{\bm{j}}_{\rm e}=(j^{x}_{\rm e,A},j^{y}_{\rm e,A})
and
{\bm{j}}_{\rm Q}=(j^{x}_{\rm Q,A},j^{y}_{\rm Q,A})
are electrical and thermal current densities in system A, respectively.

\bar{L}_{\rm A}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{\rm A}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}&\bar{L}_{12,{\rm A}}\\
\bar{L}_{21,{\rm A}}&\bar{L}_{22,{\rm A}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where
\bar{L}_{ij,{\rm A}}
is a
2\times 2
matrix of system A represented by

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{ij,{\rm A}}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{xx}&L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{xy}\\
L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{yx}&L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{yy}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

In this paper
L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{kl}
is called the linear response coefficient of system A, and
L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{xy}=-L_{ij,{\rm A}}^{yx}
.
In particular,
L_{11}^{xx(yy)}
and
L_{11}^{xy}
are the same as
\sigma^{xx(yy)}
and
\sigma^{xy}
, which are the electrical conductivity and the Hall conductivity, respectively.
As shown next, the Hall coefficient, Seebeck coefficient, Nernst coefficient and anomalous Nernst coefficient, which are transverse thermoelectric effects with/without a magnetic field, can be expressed in terms of the linear response coefficients.

II.1
Hall coefficient

The Hall effect measures the electric field that appears in the
y
direction when an electrical current is applied in the
x
direction in the presence of a perpendicular magnetic field
B_{z,{\rm A}}
and in the absence of a thermal gradient.
The Hall coefficient of system A (
R_{\rm H,A}
) is defined as

\displaystyle R_{\rm H,A}=\frac{E_{y,{\rm A}}}{j_{e,{\rm A}}^{x}B_{z,{\rm A}}}.

(4)

From Eqs. (
1
)-(
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}j^{x}_{\rm e,A}\\
j^{y}_{\rm e,A}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,{\rm A}}\\
E_{y,{\rm A}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

By solving Eq. (
5
) in the condition of
j^{y}_{\rm e,A}=0
, the Hall coefficient is given as

\displaystyle R_{\rm H,A}=\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xx}L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}B_{z,{\rm A}}}.

(6)

II.2
Seebeck and Nernst coefficients

The Seebeck and Nernst coefficients are obtained by measuring the electric field that appears in the
x
and
y
direction, respectively, when a thermal gradient is applied in the
x
direction, and, for the Nernst coefficient, a magnetic field is present.
The Seebeck coefficient
S_{xx,\text{A}}
and Nernst coefficient
\nu_{yx,\text{A}}
are defined as

\displaystyle S_{xx,{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{x,{\rm A}}}{(\nabla T)_{x,{\rm A}}},

(7)

\displaystyle\nu_{yx,{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{y,{\rm A}}}{(\nabla T)_{x,{\rm A}}B_{z,{\rm A}}}.

(8)

If
{\bm{j}}_{e}
is zero, the linear response equation reads

\displaystyle 0=\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,{\rm A}}\\
E_{y,{\rm A}}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\bar{L}_{12,{\rm A}}\begin{pmatrix}-(\nabla T)_{x,{\rm A}}/T\\
-(\nabla T)_{y,{\rm A}}/T\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

Since the thermal gradient in the
y
direction can be neglected,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,{\rm A}}\\
E_{y,{\rm A}}\\
\end{pmatrix}=-\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}^{-1}\bar{L}_{12,{\rm A}}\begin{pmatrix}-(\nabla T)_{x,{\rm A}}/T\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(10)

where
\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}^{-1}
is the inverse matrix of
\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}
.

Ignoring the term containing
L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yx}
, a second-order effect of the magnetic field, yields the Seebeck and Nernst coefficients as

\displaystyle S_{xx,{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{12,{\rm A}}^{xx}}{TL_{11,{\rm A}}^{xx}},

(11)

\displaystyle\nu_{yx,{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}L_{12,{\rm A}}^{xx}-L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xx}L_{12,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{TL_{11,{\rm A}}^{xx}L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}B_{z,{\rm A}}}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(S_{xx,{\rm A}}\theta_{{\rm H},{\rm A}}-S_{yy,A}\alpha_{{\rm N},{\rm A}})/B_{z,{\rm A}},

where
\nu_{yx,{\rm A}}=-\nu_{xy,{\rm A}}
.
\theta_{{\rm H},{\rm A}}
and
\alpha_{{\rm N},{\rm A}}
are the Hall and Peltier angles
[
25
]
defined as (See Appendix A)

\displaystyle\tan{\theta_{{\rm H},{\rm A}}}\simeq\theta_{{\rm H},{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}},

(13)

\displaystyle\tan{\alpha_{{\rm N},{\rm A}}}\simeq\alpha_{{\rm N},{\rm A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{12,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{12,{\rm A}}^{yy}}.

(14)

In the Nernst effect,
L_{11,A}^{xy}
is proportional to the magnetic field, i.e.,
L_{11,A}^{xy}\propto B_{z,{\rm A}}
.
Thus, using Eq. (
6
), the Hall angle is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{{\rm H},{\rm A}}=R_{\rm H,A}L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xx}B_{z,{\rm A}}.

(15)

II.3
Thermoelectric transports with anomalous components

In the above discussion, we studied the Hall, Seebeck and Nernst coefficients.
On the other hand,
L^{xy}_{ij}
can be finite in ferromagnetic materials even without a magnetic field, yielding to
anomalous
effects.

The anomalous Nernst coefficient is defined as

\displaystyle N_{yx,A}=\frac{E_{y,A}}{(\nabla T)_{x,A}}.

(16)

Using Eqs. (
7
), (
10
) and (
16
), the Seebeck coefficient with anomalous components and the anomalous Nernst coefficient are

\displaystyle S_{xx,A}^{AN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{11,A}^{yy}L_{12,A}^{xx}+L_{11,A}^{xy}L_{12,A}^{xy}}{T(L_{11,A}^{xx}L_{11,A}^{yy}+(L_{11,A}^{xy})^{2})}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S_{xx,A}+\theta_{\rm H,A}\alpha_{\rm N,A}r_{12}S_{yy,A}}{1+\theta_{\rm H,A}^{2}r_{11}},

\displaystyle N_{yx,A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{11,A}^{xy}L_{12,A}^{xx}-L_{11,A}^{xx}L_{12,A}^{xy}}{T(L_{11,A}^{xx}L_{11,A}^{yy}+(L_{11,A}^{xy})^{2})}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S_{xx,A}\theta_{\rm H,A}-S_{yy,A}\alpha_{\rm N,A}}{1+\theta_{\rm H,A}^{2}r_{11}},

where
r_{ij}=L_{ij}^{yy}/L_{ij}^{xx}
and
N_{yx,A}=-N_{xy,A}
.

VI
Conclusion

In conclusion, we studied the thickness dependence of longitudinal and transverse responses in film-substrate systems in a unified and general manner based on the parallel circuit model in matrix form.
We found that in transverse responses such as the Hall coefficient, the Nernst effect and the anomalous Nernst effect, there is a potential to obtain huge responses by adjusting the thickness of the film or the substrate, thereby fine-tuning the conductance ratio
\epsilon_{\sigma}
.
Our work emphasizes the importance of the cooperative effect in film and substrate systems.
Finally, in order to verify the role of the cooperative effect in realistic scenarios, bismuth film-doped silicon substrate systems were considered.
Using experimental parameters for the transport properties of bismuth and silicon, the Nernst effect of the system is drastically enhanced, originating from the interplay of film and substrate via the
cooperative effect
.

Appendix A
On the definitions of Hall and Nernst angles

In the absence of a temperature gradient, zero heat current, and also zero electrical current in the
y
direction, the electric field arising in the
y
direction, when an electric field is applied in the
x
direction, is obtained from

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}j^{x}_{\rm e,A}\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}=\bar{L}_{11,{\rm A}}\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,{\rm A}}\\
E_{y,{\rm A}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(48)

The ratio of
E_{y}
and
E_{x}
, namely Hall angle, is

\displaystyle\tan{\theta_{H,{\rm A}}}\simeq\theta_{H,{\rm A}}=\frac{E_{y}}{E_{x}}=-\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yx}}{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}}=\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}},

(49)

where we assumed that the Hall angle is much smaller than
\pi/2
.
The Hall mobility is defined as

\displaystyle\mu_{H,{\rm A}}=\frac{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{11,{\rm A}}^{yy}B_{z,{\rm A}}}=\frac{\sigma_{{\rm A}}^{xy}}{\sigma_{{\rm A}}^{yy}B_{z,{\rm A}}}.

(50)

The Peltier angle is also obtained by the ratio of
(\nabla T)_{x}
and
(\nabla T)_{y}
as

\displaystyle\tan{\alpha_{N,{\rm A}}}\simeq\alpha_{N,{\rm A}}=\frac{(\nabla T)_{y}}{(\nabla T)_{x}}=\frac{L_{12,{\rm A}}^{xy}}{L_{12,{\rm A}}^{yy}}.

(51)

where we also assumed that the Peltier angle is much smaller than
\pi/2
.

Appendix B
Hall resistivity in film-substrate system

The electrical resistivity in system A is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{x,A}\\
E_{y,A}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho^{xx}_{A}&\rho^{xy}_{A}\\
\rho^{yx}_{A}&\rho^{yy}_{A}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j_{x}^{e,A}\\
j_{y}^{e,A}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(52)

By comparison with eq. (
5

\displaystyle\rho^{xy}_{A}=\frac{-L^{xy}_{11,A}}{L^{xx}_{11,A}L^{yy}_{11,A}+(L^{xy}_{11,A})^{2}}.

(53)

The Hall resistivity in the film-substrate system becomes

\displaystyle\rho^{xy}_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-L^{xy}_{11,\rm tot}}{L^{xx}_{11,\rm tot}L^{yy}_{11,\rm tot}+(L^{xy}_{11,\rm tot})^{2}}

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{d_{f}}{d_{f}+d_{s}}L_{11,f}^{xx}}\frac{\theta_{H,\rm tot}}{(1+\epsilon_{\sigma})(1+\theta_{H,\rm tot}^{2})}.

\displaystyle\rho^{xy}_{\rm tot}\simeq\frac{1}{\frac{d_{f}}{d_{f}+d_{s}}L_{11,f}^{xx}}\frac{\theta_{\rm H,f}+\epsilon_{\sigma}\theta_{\rm H,s}}{(1+\epsilon_{\sigma})^{2}}.

(55)

n-layer system

As discussed in Eq. (

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{\rm tot}^{{\rm n-layer}}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{d_{i}}{d_{\rm tot}}\bar{L}_{i}.

(56)

where
d_{i}
indicates the thickness of the i-th layer, and
d_{\rm tot}
is the total thickness defined as
d_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
.
Here we define the ratio corresponding to Eq. (
28
) as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}=\frac{L_{11,i}^{xx}d_{i}}{L_{11,1}^{xx}d_{1}}=\frac{R_{1}}{R_{i}}.

(57)

where
R_{i}
and
L_{11,i}^{xx}
are the electrical resistivity in the i-th layer, and the electrical conductivity in the
i
-th layer, respectively.

56

6
), (
11

13
) and (
14

\displaystyle R_{H,\rm tot}^{{\rm n-layer}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{d_{\rm tot}}{d_{i}}\epsilon_{i}^{2}R_{H,i}}{(\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i})^{2}},

(58)

\displaystyle S_{\rm tot}^{{\rm n-layer}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}S_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}},

(59)

\displaystyle\theta_{H,{\rm tot}}^{{\rm n-layer}}

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\Theta_{H,i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}},

Equivalence principle and HBAR entropy of an atom falling into a quantum corrected black hole
Soham Sen a† , Rituparna Mandal a∗ and Sunandan Gangopadhyay a‡

Abstract
In this work we have investigated the phenomenon of acceleration radiation exhibited by an atom falling into a quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole. We observe that the excitation-probability of the atom with simultaneous emission of a photon satisfies the equivalence principle when we compare it to the excitation probability of a mirror accelerating with respect to an atom. We also demonstrate the validity of the equivalence principle for a generic black hole geometry. Then we calculate the horizon brightened acceleration radiation (HBAR) entropy for this quantum corrected black hole geometry. We observed that the HBAR entropy has the form identical to that of Bekenstein-Hawking black hole entropy along with universal quantum gravity corrections.

The quantum corrected black hole metric in Rindler form

To begin our analysis, we start from the Minkowski metric in 1+1-dimensional form

ds^{2}=c^{2}dt^{2}-dz^{2}~.

(4)

For a particle moving with a constant proper acceleration ‘
a
’ in this flat spacetime, the time and position coordinates in terms of the proper time
\tau
can be written as

\displaystyle t(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{a}\sinh\left(\frac{a\tau}{c}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle z(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}}{a}\cosh\left(\frac{a\tau}{c}\right)~.

(6)

We shall now use the following coordinate transformations in eq.(
4
)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{c}\sinh(\frac{\tilde{a}\tilde{t}}{c})

(7)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\rho\cosh(\frac{\tilde{a}\tilde{t}}{c})~.

(8)

Using eq.(s)(
7
,
8
) in eq.(
4

ds^{2}=\left(\frac{\tilde{a}\rho}{c^{2}}\right)^{2}c^{2}d\tilde{t}^{2}-d\rho^{2}~.

(9)

Eq.(
9
) is said to be the Rindler form of the metric describing constant acceleration of a particle. If we now compare eq.(
7
) with eq.(
5
), we see that the proper time of the particle takes the form

\tau=\frac{\tilde{a}\tilde{t}}{a}~.

(10)

Comparing eq.(
8
) with eq.(
6
), we get the form of the uniform acceleration of the particle in Minkowski spacetime as

a=\frac{c^{2}}{\rho}~.

(11)

With this background, we shall now reduce the metric of the quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole in the Rindler form to obtain the uniform acceleration of a freely falling particle close to the horizon. The horizons for the quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole can be determined by setting
f(r)=0
in eq.(
2
). The forms of the outer and inner horizons read

r_{\pm}=GM\pm\sqrt{G^{2}M^{2}-\tilde{\omega}G}~.

(12)

In eq.(
12
),
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
denotes the outer and inner horizons of the black hole. The outer horizon and
f(r)
upto order
\tilde{\omega}
(considering
\tilde{\omega}
to be small) in natural units are given by

\displaystyle r_{+}

\displaystyle\approx 2GM-\frac{\tilde{\omega}}{2M}~

(13)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle\approx 1-\frac{2GM}{r}+\frac{2\tilde{\omega}G^{2}M}{r^{3}}~.

(14)

We shall now carry out a near horizon expansion with respect to the outer horizon to obtain the Rindler form. The near horizon expansion can be obtained by making a Taylor series expansion of
f(r)
about the outer horizon
r_{+}
. Keeping terms upto first order in the near horizon expansion parameter

, we get

f(r)\cong f(r_{+})+(r-r_{+})\frac{df(r)}{dr}\biggr|_{r=r_{+}}=(r-r_{+})f^{\prime}(r_{+})

(15)

where we have used

.
Using this expansion, we obtain the line element in
1+1
-dimensions to be

\begin{split}ds^{2}&=f(r)c^{2}dt^{2}-f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}\\
&\cong(r-r_{+})f^{\prime}(r_{+})c^{2}dt^{2}-\frac{1}{(r-r_{+})f^{\prime}(r_{+})}dr^{2}~.\end{split}

(16)

We now define a transformation of coordinates to obtain the Rindler form as follows

\rho=2\sqrt{\frac{r-r_{+}}{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}}~.

(17)

Using the above transformation in eq.(
16
), we obtain the Rindler form of the metric in
1+1
-dimensions as follows

ds^{2}\cong\frac{\rho^{2}f^{\prime 2}(r_{+})}{4}c^{2}dt^{2}-d\rho^{2}~.

(18)

Here
f^{\prime}(r_{+})
upto order
\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\omega})
reads

f^{\prime}(r_{+})=\frac{c^{2}}{2GM}-\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}c^{3}}{8G^{2}M^{3}}~.

(19)

Comparing eq.(
9
) with eq.(
18
), we get the uniform acceleration corresponding to curves of constant
\rho
upto linear order in
\tilde{\omega}
as

\begin{split}a&=\frac{c^{2}}{\rho}=\frac{c^{2}}{2\sqrt{\frac{r-r_{+}}{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}}}\\
&\cong\frac{c^{2}\sqrt{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}}{2\sqrt{r}}\left(1+\frac{r_{+}}{2r}\right)\\
&\cong\frac{c^{3}}{\sqrt{2GMr}}\left[1+\frac{GM}{rc^{2}}-\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega c}}{8GM^{2}}\left(1+\frac{3GM}{c^{2}r}\right)\right]~.\end{split}

(20)

We shall use this result in the subsequent section to check the validity of the Einstein’s equivalence principle.

Atom falling into the quantum corrected black hole

In this section, we shall calculate the probability of transition from the ground state of a two-level atom falling into a quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole to the excited state along with the emission of a photon. We will denote the ground state and the excited state by
g
and
e
. Before proceeding further, we note that in the quantum corrected black hole geometry the atom trajectory is given by the following equations

\displaystyle\tau(r)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1-f(r)}}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2GM}}\left(\frac{2}{3}r^{\frac{3}{2}}-\tilde{\omega}Gr^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)+C^{\prime}~

(21)

\displaystyle t(r)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{dr}{f(r)\sqrt{1-f(r)}}

\displaystyle t(r)

\displaystyle\approx-4GM\left(\frac{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2GM}}+\frac{r^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3(2GM)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{2GM}}-1}{\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{2GM}}+1}\right)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\omega}G\left(\frac{r^{\frac{1}{2}}/2GM}{(r-2GM)}+\frac{1}{2(2GM)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\ln\frac{\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{2GM}}-1}{\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{2GM}}+1}\right)+C

(23)

where

C

In the

\frac{1}{T(t)}\frac{d^{2}T(t)}{dt^{2}}-\frac{f(r)}{r^{2}R(r)}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{2}f(r)\frac{dR(r)}{dr}\right)=0

The general solution of eq.(

(27)

In eq.(

&=\mathcal{G}^{2}\left|\int d\tau e^{i\nu t(\tau)-i\nu r_{*}(\tau)}e^{i\Omega\tau}\right|^{2}\\

where the forms of
\tau(r)
and

are given in eq.(s)(
21
,
23

y=\frac{2\Omega}{3}(r^{\frac{3}{2}}-1)~

where

Using eq.(

P_{g,0\rightarrow e,1}=\frac{\mathcal{G}^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}\biggr|\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\mathcal{A}_{0}e^{-i\nu\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)}e^{-i\Omega\mathcal{A}_{2}(y)}\biggr|^{2}

(31)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\omega}}{4M}\left(1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=2\left(1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right)+\left(1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle+2\ln\biggr[\left(1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}-1\biggr]+\frac{\tilde{\omega}}{2M}\biggr\{-\ln\left[1+\frac{3y}{2\Omega}\right]^{\frac{2}{3}}

Simplify eq.(

Atom falling into a generic black hole spacetime

The previous sections dealt with the case of a quantum corrected black hole. We will now consider a generic black hole geometry with lapse function
f(r)
having an event horizon at
r_{+}

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{f,r_{+}}=&\mathcal{G}^{2}\left|\int_{\infty}^{r_{+}}dr\frac{d\tau}{dr}e^{i\nu t(r)-i\nu r_{*}(r)}e^{i\Omega\tau(r)}\right|^{2}\\

52
), we get the following form of the probability

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{f,r_{+}}=&\frac{4\pi\mathcal{G}^{2}\left(\frac{\nu}{2a}f^{\prime}(r_{+})\right)}{f^{\prime}(r_{+})\Omega^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{4\pi\left(\frac{\nu}{2a}f^{\prime}(r_{+})\right)}{f^{\prime}(r_{+})}}-1}\\
=&\frac{2\pi\mathcal{G}^{2}\nu}{a\Omega^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{2\pi\nu}{a}}-1}~.\end{split}

(53)

With proper dimensional reconstruction, eq.(
53
) can be rewritten as follows

\mathcal{P}_{f,r_{+}}=\frac{2\pi\mathcal{G}^{2}\nu c}{a\Omega^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{2\pi\nu c}{a}}-1}~.

(54)

Hence, following the same arguments after eq.(
45
), we can conclude that the equivalence principle holds for a generic black hole spacetime.

Modified HBAR entropy

The term
horizon brightened acceleration radiation entropy
(HBAR entropy) was first introduced in
[
1
]
. In this section we will calculate the HBAR entropy for the quantum corrected black hole background. We are considering a case when two-level atoms with transition frequency
\Omega
are falling into the event horizon of the black hole at a rate
\kappa
. In this work we have used the quantum statistical approach to determine the entropy and hence we have used the density matrix formalism. If the microscopic change in the field density matrix is
\delta\rho_{a}
for one atom then the overall macroscopic change in the field density matrix for
\Delta\mathcal{N}
atoms is given by
[
1
]

\Delta\rho=\sum\limits_{a}\delta\rho_{a}=\Delta\mathcal{N}\delta\rho

(55)

where

\frac{\Delta\mathcal{N}}{\Delta t}=\kappa~.

(56)

Putting
\Delta\mathcal{N}
in eq.(
56
), we obtain

\frac{\Delta\rho}{\Delta t}=\kappa\delta\rho~.

(57)

We now use the Lindblad master equation for the density matrix given by

\begin{split}\frac{d\rho}{dt}=&-\frac{\Gamma_{abs}}{2}\left(\rho b^{\dagger}b+b^{\dagger}b\rho-2b\rho b^{\dagger}\right)\\
&-\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{2}\left(\rho bb^{\dagger}+bb^{\dagger}\rho-2b^{\dagger}\rho b\right)\end{split}

(58)

where
\Gamma_{exc}
is the excitation rate and
\Gamma_{abs}
is the absorption rate given by
\Gamma_{exc/abs}=\kappa P_{exc/abs}
with
P_{exc/abs}

being given by eq.(s)(
37
,
38
).
Taking the expectation of eq.(
58
) with respect to some arbitrary state
|n\rangle
, we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{\rho}_{n,n}=&-\Gamma_{abs}\left(n\rho_{n,n}-(n+1)\rho_{n+1,n+1}\right)\\
&-\Gamma_{exc}\left((n+1)\rho_{n,n}-n\rho_{n-1,n-1}\right)~.\end{split}

(59)

The steady state solution is now used to obtain the HBAR entropy. So we set
\dot{\rho}_{n,n}=0
in eq.(
59
) and for
n=0
we obtain the relation between
\rho_{1,1}
and
\rho_{0,0}
as follows

\rho_{1,1}=\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\rho_{0,0}~.

(60)

Repeating this procedure, we finally obtain

\rho_{n,n}=\left(\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\right)^{n}\rho_{0,0}~.

(61)

To obtain
\rho_{0,0}
in the above relation, we use the condition
\trace(\rho)=1
. This gives

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n}\rho_{n,n}

\displaystyle=1\implies\rho_{0,0}\sum_{n}\left(\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\right)^{n}=1

\displaystyle\implies\rho_{0,0}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}~.

(62)

Using
\rho_{0,0}
from the above equation in eq.(
61
), we obtain the steady state solution of the density matrix to be

\rho_{n,n}^{\mathcal{S}}=\left(\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\right)^{n}\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\right)

(63)

where
\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}
is given by (in the approximation
\Omega\gg\nu
)

\frac{\Gamma_{exc}}{\Gamma_{abs}}\approx\left(1+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}}{\mathcal{R}Mc}\frac{5\nu}{\Omega}\right)e^{-4\pi\nu\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}}{c}+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}}{2Mc^{2}}\right)}~.

(64)

Here,
\mathcal{R}=\frac{2GM}{c^{2}}
. The von-Neumann entropy for the system is given by

S_{\rho}=-k_{B}\sum\limits_{n,\nu}\rho_{n,n}\ln(\rho_{n,n})

(65)

and the rate of change of entropy due to the generation of real photons is obtained as

\dot{S_{\rho}}=-k_{B}\sum\limits_{n,\nu}\dot{\rho}_{n,n}\ln(\rho_{n,n})~.

(66)

The rate of change of the entropy, using the steady state density matrix solution, is given as

\dot{S_{\rho}}\approx-k_{B}\sum\limits_{n,\nu}\dot{\rho}_{n,n}\ln(\rho^{\mathcal{S}}_{n,n})~.

(67)

Using the form of the density matrix
\rho_{n,n}^{\mathcal{S}}
in eq.(
63
), we obtain

\begin{split}\dot{S}_{\rho}=&4\pi k_{B}\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}}{c}+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}}{2Mc^{2}}\right)\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(\sum\limits_{n}n\dot{\rho}_{n,n}\right)\nu\\
&-k_{B}\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(\sum\limits_{n}n\dot{\rho}_{n,n}\right)\ln\left[1+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}}{\mathcal{R}Mc}\frac{5\nu}{\Omega}\right]\\
\approx&4\pi k_{B}\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}}{c}+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\omega}}{2Mc^{2}}\right)\sum\limits_{\nu}\dot{\bar{n}}_{\nu}\nu-\frac{5\hbar\tilde{\omega}k_{B}}{\mathcal{R}\Omega Mc}\sum\limits_{\nu}\dot{\bar{n}}_{\nu}\nu~.\end{split}

(68)

In eq.(
68
),
\dot{\bar{n}}_{\nu}
is the flux due to photons from the atoms freely falling in the black hole and the total rate of energy loss due to emitted photons is
\hbar\sum\limits_{\nu}\dot{\bar{n}}_{\nu}\nu=\dot{m}_{p}c^{2}
. The black hole area in our case is given by

\begin{split}A_{Qbh}&=4\pi r_{+}^{2}\\
&\approx\frac{16\pi G^{2}M^{2}}{c^{4}}-\frac{8\pi\hbar\tilde{\omega}G}{c^{3}}\end{split}~.

(69)

Taking time derivative of the both sides in eq.(
69
), we get

\dot{A}_{Qbh}=\frac{32\pi G^{2}M\dot{M}}{c^{4}}

(70)

where
\dot{M}=\dot{m}_{p}+\dot{m}_{atom}
. We now define the rate of change in the black hole area due to emitting photons as follows

\dot{A}_{p}=\frac{32\pi G^{2}M\dot{m}_{p}}{c^{4}}~.

(71)

From these results we can infer that when no atoms are falling in the black hole, then
A_{p}
should be equivalent to the area of the black hole and
A_{atom}
should be zero. A better way to understand the above result is as follows. Before the atom crosses the black hole event horizon, that is, before the atom contributes to the black hole mass, the freely falling atom emits HBAR radiation. Therefore, one can separate in time the change of the black hole entropy associated with HBAR radiation from an atom and that associated with that atom’s mass.

The final form of
\dot{S}_{\rho}
in terms of
A_{p}
can therefore be written as

\begin{split}\dot{S}_{\rho}\approx&\frac{k_{B}c^{3}}{4\hbar G}\dot{A}_{p}+\tilde{\omega}\pi k_{B}\frac{d}{dt}(\ln A_{p})+\frac{10\tilde{\omega}\sqrt{\pi}k_{B}c}{\Omega}\frac{d}{dt}\left(A_{p}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
=&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{k_{B}c^{3}}{4\hbar G}A_{p}+\tilde{\omega}\pi k_{B}\ln A_{p}+\frac{10\tilde{\omega}\sqrt{\pi}k_{B}c}{\Omega}A_{p}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)~.\end{split}

(72)

We find that the horizon brightened acceleration radiation entropy for the case of a simple Schwarzschild black hole
[
1
]
gets modified with the time derivatives of a log term and an inverse square root term due to quantum gravity corrections introduced in the Schwarzschild black hole. This indicates that logarithmic corrections also appear in HBAR entropy just as it appears in usual Bekenstein-Hawking black hole entropy
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
41
]
.

Conclusion

In this work we have considered the case of a two-level atom freely falling near the outer horizon of a renormalization group improved Schwarzschild black hole. The entire system is analysed by considering that the outer horizon of the black hole is surrounded with a mirror to prevent infalling atoms from interacting with the Hawking radiation. Further, the mirror ensures that the initial state of the field appears vacuum-like in the reference frame of the external observer. Our investigation in this context involved the computation of the atom-field excitation probability and the calculation of the rate of change of the horizon brightened acceleration radiation entropy or the HBAR entropy. The excitation probability obtained in this work involves a Planck like factor indicating emission of real photons in the virtual process. Interestingly, we find that even with quantum gravity corrections, the Einstein equivalence principle holds indicating that the principle may be a fundamental reality even in a quantum gravity setting. We then extended this calculation for a generic black hole metric and observed that the equivalence principle holds in a general setting as well. For the next part of the work we have calculated the HBAR entropy using the quantum optics approach
[
1
]
and we have observed that the HBAR entropy has a Bekenstein-Hawking like entropy term along with a logarithmic and inverse square root quantum gravity term. The nature of the corrections appearing in the HBAR entropy are remarkably the same as those obtained in case of usual Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of a black hole.

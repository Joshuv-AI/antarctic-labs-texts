Dirac structures and Nijenhuis operators
Henrique Bursztyn, Thiago Drummond, Clarice Netto

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of compatibility between (almost) Dirac structures and ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -tensor fields extending that of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures. We study several properties of the “Dirac-Nijenhuis” structures thus obtained, including their connection with holomorphic Dirac structures, the geometry of their leaves and quotients, as well as the presence of hierarchies. We also consider their integration to Lie groupoids, which includes the integration of holomorphic Dirac structures as a special case.



1.
Introduction

There are several situations of interest in geometry that involve the compatibility of a given geometric structure with a suitable
(1,1)
-tensor field. An illustrative example is the study of holomorphic objects on a complex manifold.
In this paper we introduce a notion of compatibility between (almost) Dirac structures and general
(1,1)
-tensor fields that
extends, and is inspired by, the theory of
Poisson-Nijenhuis structures

[
31
,
37
]
. In this context the compatibility of a Poisson structure with a Nijenhuis operator has remarkable consequences, such as the presence of bi-hamiltonian structures
[
24
,
35
]
, Poisson hierarchies and integrals of motion that underpin many integrable systems, see e.g.
[
3
,
19
,
20
,
36
,
37
]
. In Poisson-Lie theory, Poisson-Nijenhuis structures (see
[
29
]
) play a central role in certain quantization schemes
[
1
,
2
]
.
Poisson-Nijenhuis structures also emerge naturally in the characterization of holomorphic Poisson structures in terms of their “real parts”
[
32
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.3]
, and
this viewpoint is key to establish the holomorphic version of the integration of Poisson structures by symplectic groupoids
[
33
,
41
]
(see also
[
17
]
). The notion of
Dirac-Nijenhuis structure
introduced in this paper provides a similar viewpoint to holomorphic Dirac structures and leads to more general integration results.

Dirac structures
[
16
]
are common generalizations of Poisson structures and presymplectic forms, originally motivated by constrained mechanics (as the intrinsic geometry of submanifolds of Poisson manifolds), with a wide array of recent applications, see e.g.
[
4
,
38
]
and references therein.
A Dirac structure on a manifold
M
is a subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M:=TM\oplus T^{*}M
that is lagrangian (for the canonical symmetric pairing on
\mathbb{T}M
) and involutive with respect to the Courant-Dorfman bracket; any Poisson structure
\pi
on
M
may be regarded as a Dirac structure
L_{\pi}
given by the graph of
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\to TM
,
\alpha\mapsto i_{\alpha}\pi
. Dirac structures have also been considered in the holomorphic category, e.g. in connection with generalized Kähler geometry
[
25
]

and Poisson-Lie theory
[
9
]
.

To better explain our results, recall that, in Poisson-Nijenhuis theory, one finds a notion of compatibility between a bivector field
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
and a
(1,1)
-tensor field
r:TM\to TM
that is expressed by two conditions:

(1)

\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{*}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}
,

(2)

For
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,

R_{\pi}^{r}(X,\alpha):=\pi^{\sharp}(\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\alpha)-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\alpha)-(\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}r)(X)=0.

The expression in (2) is known as the
concomitant
of
\pi
and
r
. The starting point of this paper is a new interpretation of these conditions that
clarifies how to extend them to Dirac structures.

By considering the map
(r,r^{*}):\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{T}M
, the purely algebraic condition (1) can be rephrased in terms of
L_{\pi}
as
(r,r^{*})(L_{\pi})\subset L_{\pi}
.
Finding a parallel reformulation of the vanishing of the concomitant is less evident. For that, a central role is played by
two “connection-like” operators,
D^{r}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
and
D^{r,*}:\Omega^{1}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M,T^{*}M)
, given by

\displaystyle D^{r}_{X}(Y)=i_{X}(D^{r}(Y)):=(\mathcal{L}_{Y}r)(X),

\displaystyle D^{r,*}_{X}(\alpha)=i_{X}(D^{r,*}(\alpha)):=\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\alpha)-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\alpha

for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Setting
\mathbb{D}^{r}:=(D^{r},D^{r,*})
, condition (2) is equivalent to
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L_{\pi}))\subseteq\Gamma(L_{\pi})
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

[
21
, Sec. 2]
. This leads to the following definition: a lagrangian subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
is said to be
compatible
with a
(1,1)
-tensor field
r
if

(1.1)

(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L,\qquad\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L),

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
When
L
is a Dirac structure and
r
is a Nijenhuis operator, the pair
(L,r)
is called a
Dirac-Nijenhuis
structure. Besides recovering Poisson-Nijenhuis structures when
L
is the graph of a Poisson structure, Dirac-Nijenhuis structures agree with the so-called
\Omega N
-structures
1
1

1

In this paper, we will refer to
\Omega N
-structures as
presymplectic-Nijenhuis
structures.
of
[
37
]

when
L
is the graph of a closed 2-form (see
\lx@sectionsign

3
).
We stress that the notion of compatibility in (
1.1
) does not rely on the Courant-integrability of
L
(or even the fact that it is lagrangian) nor the Nijenhuis-integrability of
r
.
This degree of generality makes it possible to accommodate, with no extra effort, weaker forms of integrability of
L
and
r
, such as the “quasi-Nijenhuis” structures of
[

]
(see
[

[

,
our Dirac-Nijenhuis structures fit into a more general theory of “Courant-Nijenhuis algebroids”.

We pass to the description of the main results and structure of the paper. We start by explaining in
\lx@sectionsign

2
the general context in which the operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
naturally arise, namely that of
generalized derivations of degree 1

[
21

, or

1-derivations
for simplicity. Just as usual derivations of a vector bundle are in one-to-one correspondence with its linear vector fields (see e.g.
[

, Thm. 3.4.5]
), 1-derivations on a vector bundle
E\to M
are derivation-like objects that correspond to
linear

(1,1)
-tensor fields on the total space

. For a
(1,1)
-tensor field

on
M

(D^{r},r,r)
and
(D^{r,*},r^{*},r)
are examples of (dual) 1-derivations on

(1,1)

[

of

r^{tg}:T(TM)\to T(TM),\qquad r^{cotg}:T(T^{*}M)\to T(T^{*}M).

Similarly,
(\mathbb{D}^{r},(r,r^{*}),r)

\mathbb{T}M

(r^{tg},r^{cotg}):T(\mathbb{T}M)\to T(\mathbb{T}M)

1.1
) in the definition of a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure
(L,r)
are seen to be equivalent to the single condition

(r^{tg},r^{cotg})(TL)\subset TL.

In the particular case of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures
(\pi,r)

T\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{cotg}=r^{tg}\circ T\pi^{\sharp},

which provides a re-formulation of the compatibility conditions
(1) and (2) as one natural equation (see Proposition
3.3
). It is also recalled in Example
2.3
that holomorphic structures on (real) vector bundles (regarded as partial flat connections as in
[
39
]
) can be conveniently seen as particular 1-derivations. When
r
is a complex structure on
M
, the
1

D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
codify the holomorphic structures
on
TM
and
T^{*}M
arising from their usual identifications with
T^{1,0}M
and
(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
, respectively (see Example
2.6
). By means of these identifications, we prove in Proposition
3.16
that Dirac-Nijenhuis structures
(L,r)
, with
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
, are equivalent to holomorphic Dirac structures
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M=T^{1,0}M\oplus(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
.

After the definition and basic examples of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures are presented in §
3

4

4.4

The last two sections of the paper concern integration to Lie groupoids. A central result in Poisson geometry is the infinitesimal-global correspondence relating Poisson manifolds to objects known as
symplectic groupoids

[

As observed in

2.
Preliminaries: linear (1,1)-tensors and 1-derivations

In this section we explain the correspondence between linear
(1,1)
-tensor fields on vector bundles and objects that we call
1
-derivations
, a higher-degree analogue of the equivalence between linear vector fields and derivations on vector bundles. We then explain how the operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
naturally arise in this context.

2.1.
The correspondence

Let
q:E\to M
be a (real) vector bundle, and consider its tangent bundle
TE\to E
. Recall that
TE
carries a second vector-bundle structure
Tq:TE\to TM
(its “tangent prolongation”) making it a
double vector bundle

[
34
]
. We denote by
V_{E}=\mathrm{Ker}(Tq)\subset TE
the vertical bundle of
E
.

A
(1,1)
-tensor field
K\in\Gamma(T^{*}E\otimes TE)=\Omega^{1}(E,TE)
on the total space of
E

is called
linear
if, regarded as a map
K:TE\to TE
, it is a vector-bundle morphism

\textstyle{TE\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{K}
\textstyle{TE\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{TM\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\textstyle{TM,}

for some
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Note that linear
(1,1)
-tensors are higher-degree analogs of linear vector fields on
E
,
which are elements in
\Omega^{0}(E,TE)=\Gamma(TE)
that, viewed as maps
E\to TE
, are vector-bundle morphisms from
E\to M
to
TE\to TM
. (Equivalently, linear vector fields are characterized as those with flows given by vector-bundle automorphisms.)

Linear vector fields on
E
are known to be in one-to-one correspondence with
derivations
of
E
, i.e., pairs
(\Delta,X)
, where
\Delta:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
is
\mathbb{R}
-linear and
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a vector field, satisfying the Leibniz rule

\Delta(fu)=f\Delta(u)+(\mathcal{L}_{X}f)u,

for
u\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. One direct way to define the derivation
(\Delta,X)
corresponding to a linear vector field
Z
is via the relations

(2.1)

\Delta(u)^{\uparrow}=[u^{\uparrow},Z],\qquad q^{*}\langle\alpha,X\rangle=\langle q^{*}\alpha,Z\rangle,

for
u\in\Gamma(E),\,\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
; here

u^{\uparrow}\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
is the
vertical lift
of
u\in\Gamma(E)
,

u^{\uparrow}(e)=\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big|_{\tau=0}(e+\tau u(x)),\qquad\mbox{ for }e\in E|_{x}.

As proven in
[
7
, Sec. 4.4]
, this correspondence between linear vector fields and derivations is part of a general characterization of linear tensor fields on vector bundles, which establishes, in particular, a bijection between linear tensors of type
(1,p)
(i.e., vector-valued forms) on
E
and objects called
generalized derivations
, see
[
21
, Sec. 2]
. More specifically, linear
(1,1)
-tensor fields on
E
bijectively correspond to
generalized derivations of degree 1
(or
1-derivations
for simplicity), which are triples
(D,l,r)
, where
D:\Gamma(E)\to\Omega^{1}(M,E)
is
\mathbb{R}
-linear,
l\in\mathrm{End}(E)
,
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, satisfying the Leibniz-type rule

(2.2)

D(fu)=fD(u)+df\wedge l(u)-r^{*}(df)\wedge u,

for
u\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. In particular, each
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
gives rise to an operator
D_{X}:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
,
D_{X}(u):=i_{X}(D(u))
, satisfying

(2.3)

D_{X}(fu)=fD_{X}(u)+(\mathcal{L}_{X}f)l(u)-(\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}f)u.

We collect the main facts that we will need regarding linear
(1,1)
-tensors in the next result. Consider the map
\mathcal{V}:\Omega^{1}(M,E)\to\Omega^{1}(E,TE)
,
\alpha\otimes u\mapsto q^{*}\alpha\otimes u^{\uparrow}
, for
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
u\in\Gamma(E)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(i)

Any linear
(1,1)
-tensor
K
on
E\to M
defines a 1-derivation
(D,l,r)
via

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}_{u^{\uparrow}}K=\mathcal{V}(D(u)),\;\;\;K(u^{\uparrow})=l(u)^{\uparrow},\;\;\;\langle K,q^{*}\beta\rangle=q^{*}\langle\beta,r\rangle

for
u\in\Gamma(E)
and
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, and this correspondence establishes a bijection between linear
(1,1)
-tensor fields and 1-derivations.

(ii)

For
i=1,2
, let
E_{i}\to M
be a vector bundle equipped with a linear
(1,1)
-tensor field
K_{i}
, with corresponding 1-derivation
(D^{i},l_{i},r_{i})
. Let
\Phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a vector-bundle map over the identity (and we keep the same notation for the induced map between spaces of sections).
Then
T\Phi\circ K_{1}=K_{2}\circ T\Phi
if and only if

(2.5)

\Phi\circ D^{1}_{X}=D^{2}_{X}\circ\Phi,\;\;\;\Phi\circ l_{1}=l_{2}\circ\Phi,\;\;\;r_{1}=r_{2}.

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Part (i) follows from the general results in
[
7
]
. We present a proof of part (ii) in Appendix
A
.
A consequence of the theorem (keeping the notation of part (i)) is that a subbundle
(F\to M)\subseteq(E\to M)
satisfies
K(TF)\subseteq TF
if and only if
l(F)\subseteq F
and
D(\Gamma(F))\subseteq\Gamma(F)
.

Example 2.2

(Connections)
.

The one-to-one correspondence between linear
(1,1)
-tensors and
1

E\to M

(i) restricts to a bijection between linear
(1,1)
-tensors

\Theta^{2}=\Theta

(D,l,r)
such that

and

such that

, whereas

defines a 1-derivation of the type

if and only if it is a connection operator on

[

Example 2.3

(Holomorphic structures)
.

A holomorphic vector bundle can be defined as a complex vector bundle over a complex manifold
M
equipped with a flat
T^{0,1}M
-connection
[
39
]
. Equivalently, one may regard a holomorphic vector bundle as a real vector bundle
E\to M
together with a holomorphic structure consisting of
(1) a complex structure
r:TM\to TM
on
M
, (2)
a fiberwise complex structure on
E
, i.e., an endomorphism

l:E\to E
with
l^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{E}
, and (3) a flat
T^{0,1}M
-connection
\nabla
on the complex vector bundle
(E,l)
. The partial connection
\nabla:\Gamma(T^{0,1}M)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
is the same as a Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma((T^{0,1}M)^{*}\otimes E)
, so we refer to it as a Dolbeault connection. A (local) section of
E
is holomorphic if and only if it is flat. The holomorphic structure on
E
may be alternatively represented by the triple
(D^{\mathrm{hol}},l,r)
, where
D^{\mathrm{hol}}:\Gamma(E)\to\Omega^{1}(M,E)
is given by

(2.6)

D^{\mathrm{hol}}_{X}(u):=l(\nabla_{X+\mathbf{i}\,r(X)}u).

One may verify that
(D^{\mathrm{hol}},l,r)
is in fact a 1-derivation on
E
, that we will call the Dolbeault 1-derivation associated with a holomorphic vector bundle. Such 1-derivations admit an intrinsic characterization as the 1-derivations
(D,l,r)
on a vector bundle
E\to M
satisfying the following set of equations (see
[
7
]
):

(2.7)

r^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{TM},\;\;\;l^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{E},\;\;\;l(D_{X}(u))+D_{r(X)}(u)=0,

as well as

(2.8)

\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)=0,\;\;\,l(D_{X}(u)=D_{X}(l(u)),\;\;\,l(D_{[X,Y]}(u))=[D_{X},D_{Y}](u)+D_{[X,Y]_{r}}(u),

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
u\in\Gamma(E)
, where
\mathcal{N}_{r}
is the Nijenhuis torsion of
r
(see (
2.16
) below) and

[\cdot,\cdot]_{r}
is given by
[X,Y]_{r}=[r(X),Y]+[X,r(Y)]-r([X,Y]
. Note that these equations just spell out conditions (1), (2) and (3) above, for
\nabla
defined by
\nabla_{X+\mathbf{i}\,r(X)}u=-l(D_{X}(u))
.
The linear (1,1)-tensor on
E
associated to a Dolbeault 1-derivation
(D^{\mathrm{hol}},l,r)
via Theorem

is the complex structure
J
on the total space of
E\to M
, see
[
7
, §6]
and

(here (

Analogously to ordinary derivations, any 1-derivation

(D^{*},l^{*},r)

\langle D^{*}_{X}(\xi),u\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{X}\langle\xi,l(u)\rangle-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\langle\xi,u\rangle-\langle\xi,D_{X}(u)\rangle,

, and

One can also describe this duality in terms of the corresponding linear
(1,1)
-tensor fields. For such

Consider the non-degenerate pairing
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle:TE\times_{TM}T(E^{*})\to TM\times\mathbb{R}

One can verify that, for every

(2.10)

\langle\!\langle K(U),K^{\top}(V)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle Tl(U),V\rangle\!\rangle.

2.2.
The operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}

For a given
(1,1)
-tensor field

on

,
consider the following nonlinear operators:
D^{r}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle D^{r}_{X}(Y)=i_{X}(D^{r}(Y))

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{L}_{Y}r)(X)

\displaystyle=[Y,r(X)]-r([Y,X]),

for

, and

(2.12)

\displaystyle D^{r,*}_{X}(\alpha)=i_{X}(D^{r,*}(\alpha))

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\alpha)-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\alpha

\displaystyle=i_{X}d(r^{*}\alpha)-i_{r(X)}d\alpha,

for
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and

Any
(1,1)
-tensor field

M

(1,1)

TM
and another on
T^{*}M
,

r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}\in\Omega^{1}(TM,T(TM)),\qquad r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}\in\Omega^{1}(T^{*}M,T(T^{*}M)),

known as the
tangent
and
cotangent lifts
of
r
, see e.g.
[
43
]
.
The tangent lift
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}:T(TM)\to T(TM)
is obtained from the tangent map
Tr
via
J_{M}\circ Tr\circ J_{M}
, where
J_{M}:T(TM)\to T(TM)
is the canonical involution of the double tangent bundle
[
34
, §9.6]
. For the definition of the cotangent lift
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}
, consider the canonical symplectic structure
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
, and let
\phi_{r}:=r^{*}:T^{*}M\to T^{*}M
. Then
r^{cotg}
is determined by

i_{r^{cotg}(U)}\omega_{\mathrm{can}}=i_{U}(\phi_{r}^{*}\,\omega_{\mathrm{can}}),

see also
[
11
]
.

The next result is proven in
[
21
, §3]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, the
(1,1)
-tensor fields
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}
and
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}
are linear and
satisfy
r^{cotg}=(r^{tg})^{\top}
. Their corresponding 1-derivations are
(D^{r},r,r)
and
(D^{r,*},r^{*},r)
.

In particular, the operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) satisfy the Leibniz-type identity in (
2.2
) and are dual to one another as 1-derivations:

(2.13)

\langle D^{r,*}_{X}(\alpha),Y\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{X}\langle\alpha,r(Y)\rangle-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\langle\alpha,Y\rangle-\langle\alpha,D^{r}_{X}(Y)\rangle.

Remark 2.5

(Naturality properties)
.

Consider
r_{i}\in\Omega(M_{i},TM_{i})
,
i=1,2
, and
a smooth map
\psi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
. Suppose that
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are
\psi
-related, i.e.,
r_{2}\circ d\psi=d\psi\circ r_{1}
. Let
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{i})
,
i=1,2
, be vector fields which are also
\psi
-related; we use the notation
X_{1}\sim_{\psi}X_{2}
. The following can be directly verified:

(2.14)

\displaystyle Y_{1}\sim_{\psi}Y_{2}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;D^{r_{1}}_{X_{1}}(Y_{1})\sim_{\psi}D^{r_{2}}_{X_{2}}(Y_{2}),

(2.15)

\displaystyle D^{r_{1},*}_{X_{1}}(\psi^{*}\beta)=\psi^{*}D^{r_{2},*}_{X_{2}}(\beta),\quad\forall\,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M_{2}).

\diamond

We will be particularly interested in
Nijenhuis operators

r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, i.e., endomorphisms
r:TM\to TM
with vanishing Nijenhuis torsion
\mathcal{N}_{r}\in\Omega^{2}(M,TM)
:

(2.16)

\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y):=[r(X),r(Y)]-r([r(X),Y]+[X,r(Y)]-r[X,Y])=0.

We note that the Nijenhuis torsion of
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
admits the following expressions in terms of
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
: for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=r(D_{X}^{r}(Y))-D^{r}_{X}(r(Y))

(2.18)

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle r^{*}(D_{X}^{r,*}(\alpha))-D_{X}^{r,*}(r^{*}(\alpha)),Y\rangle.

The next example explains the meaning of the operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
when
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
is a complex structure, i.e.,
r
is a Nijenhuis operator satisfying
r^{2}=-\mathrm{id}
.

Example 2.6

(Tangent and cotangent holomorphic structures)
.

On a complex manifold
(M,r)
,
T^{1,0}M
and
(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
are holomorphic vector bundles. Explicitly, the Dolbeault connection on
T^{1,0}M
is given by

\nabla_{X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}}}(Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}})=\mathrm{pr}_{10}([X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}},Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}]),

where
\mathrm{pr}_{10}
is the projection of
TM\otimes\mathbb{C}=T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
onto
T^{1,0}M
,
while
(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
carries the dual Dolbeault connection, defined by the condition

(2.19)

\langle\nabla^{*}_{X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}}}(\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}),Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}}}\langle\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}},Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}\rangle-\langle\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}},\nabla_{X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}}}(Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}})\rangle,

where
X^{\scriptscriptstyle{0,1}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{0,1}(M)
,
Y^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{1,0}(M)
, and
\alpha^{\scriptscriptstyle{1,0}}\in\Omega^{1,0}(M)
.
Consider the usual identifications (of real vector bundles)

(2.20)

\Phi_{T}:TM\to T^{1,0}M,\;X\mapsto\frac{1}{2}(X-\mathbf{i}\,r(X)),\qquad\Phi_{T^{*}}:T^{*}M\to(T^{1,0}M)^{*},\;\alpha\mapsto\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}(\alpha).

One can then verify (see
[
21
, Prop. 5.3]
) that the Dolbeault 1-derivations codifiying the holomorphic structures on
TM
and
T^{*}M
resulting from these identifications are
(D^{r},r,r)
and
(D^{r,*},r^{*},r)
; in particular, by Proposition
2.4
, the complex structures on
TM
and
T^{*}M
are
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}
and
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}
, respectively.

3.
Compatibility of Dirac structures and
(1,1)
-tensor fields

We introduce in this section a notion of compatibility for (almost) Dirac structures and
(1,1)
-tensor fields,
extending the well-known case of Poisson-Nijenhuis manifolds that we recall first.

3.1.
The Poisson-Nijenhuis compatibility revisited

On a manifold
M
, let
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
be a bivector field and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
.
Consider the bundle map
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\to TM
,
\alpha\mapsto i_{\alpha}\pi
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the pair
(\pi,r)
is compatible if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{*}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}
;

(2)

For
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, we have

(3.1)

R_{\pi}^{r}(X,\alpha):=\pi^{\sharp}(\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\alpha)-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\alpha)-(\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)}r)(X)=0.

Note that by (1) we have another bivector field
\pi_{r}\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
defined by the condition

\pi_{r}^{\sharp}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}.

We also notice that
R_{\pi}^{r}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
is
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear if and only if condition (1) holds; in this case
R_{\pi}^{r}
defines a tensor field called the
Magri-Morosi concomitant

[
37
]
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the literature, condition (2) above is often expressed through the vanishing of an alternative concomitant,
C^{r}_{\pi}:\Omega^{1}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M)
, given by

(3.2)

C_{\pi}^{r}(\alpha,\beta):=[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi_{r}}-([r^{*}(\alpha),\beta]_{\pi}+[\alpha,r^{*}(\beta)]_{\pi}-r^{*}([\alpha,\beta]_{\pi})),

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\Lambda}
is the bracket on
\Omega^{1}(M)
defined by a bivector field
\Lambda
via

[\alpha,\beta]_{\Lambda}:=\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda^{\sharp}(\alpha)}\beta-i_{\Lambda^{\sharp}(\beta)}d\alpha
.
The concomitants
R^{r}_{\pi}
and
C^{r}_{\pi}
are related by

\langle\beta,R_{\pi}^{r}(X,\alpha)\rangle=\langle C_{\pi}^{r}(\alpha,\beta),X\rangle,

so
R_{\pi}^{r}=0
if and only if
C_{\pi}^{r}=0
.
\diamond

A
Poisson-Nijenhuis structure
(or simply a
PN structure
) on a manifold
M
, as introduced in
[
37
]
(see also
[
31
]
), is a compatible pair
(\pi,r)
, where
\pi
is a Poisson bivector field and
r
is a Nijenhuis operator.
In this setting, a geometric interpretation of the compatibility conditions in terms of Lie bialgebroids can be found in
[
30
]
.

Our extension of the compatibility condition of PN structures to Dirac structures relies on a suitable reformulation of condition (2) above, see
[
21
, §2.3]
. Let
\pi
be a bivector field on
M
and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Consider the operators
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
defined in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
).
Then the Magri-Morosi concomitant can be written as

(3.3)

R_{\pi}^{r}(X,\alpha)=\pi^{\sharp}(D^{r,*}_{X}(\alpha))-D^{r}_{X}(\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)).

As a consequence of Prop.
2.4
and Theorem
2.1
, part (ii),
we have the following alternative description of the compatibility of the pair
(\pi,r)
in terms of tangent and cotangent lifts:

Proposition 3.3
.

The pair
(\pi,r)
is compatible if and only if
T\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}=r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}\circ T\pi^{\sharp}
.

3.2.
The presymplectic-Nijenhuis compatibility revisited

Given
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, we now introduce a notion of compatibility that is parallel to the one in Section
3.1
.

Consider
\omega^{\flat}:TM\to T^{*}M
,
X\mapsto i_{X}\omega
, and define (in analogy with (
3.3
))

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=D^{r,*}_{X}(\omega^{\flat}(Y))-\omega^{\flat}(D^{r}_{X}(Y))

\displaystyle=i_{X}d(r^{*}\circ\omega^{\flat}(Y))-i_{r(X)}d\omega^{\flat}(Y)-\omega^{\flat}([Y,r(X)]-r([Y,X])),

for
X
,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Suppose that
\omega^{\flat}\circ r=r^{*}\circ\omega^{\flat}
, so that we have
\omega_{r}\in\Omega^{2}(M)
given by

i_{X}\omega_{r}:=i_{r(X)}\omega.

Let
(d\omega)_{r}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\wedge^{2}T^{*}M)
be given by

i_{X}(d\omega)_{r}=i_{r(X)}d\omega
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\omega^{\flat}\circ r=r^{*}\circ\omega^{\flat}
, then
\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}=d(\omega_{r})-(d\omega)_{r}
.

Proof.

The expression for
\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}
in (
3.4
) can be re-written as follows:

\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)=i_{X}di_{Y}\omega_{r}-i_{r(X)}di_{Y}\omega-i_{[Y,r(X)]}\omega+i_{[Y,X]}\omega_{r}.

From the identities

\displaystyle i_{X}di_{Y}\omega_{r}=i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega_{r}-i_{X}i_{Y}d(\omega_{r}),

\displaystyle i_{r(X)}di_{Y}\omega=i_{r(X)}\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega-i_{r(X)}i_{Y}d\omega

\displaystyle i_{[Y,r(X)]}\omega=\mathcal{L}_{Y}i_{r(X)}\omega-i_{r(X)}\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega,

\displaystyle i_{[Y,X]}\omega_{r}=\mathcal{L}_{Y}i_{X}\omega_{r}-i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega_{r},

we see that

\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)=-i_{X}i_{Y}d(\omega_{r})+i_{r(X)}i_{Y}d\omega=i_{Y}i_{X}d(\omega_{r})-i_{Y}i_{X}(d\omega)_{r}.

∎

Definition 3.5
.

For
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, we say that the pair
(\omega,r)
is compatible if

(1)

\omega^{\flat}\circ r=r^{*}\circ\omega^{\flat}
,

(2)

\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}=0
(equivalently,
d(\omega_{r})=(d\omega)_{r}
).

In analogy with Proposition
3.3
, this notion of compatibility has the following interpretation:

Proposition 3.6
.

The pair
(\omega,r)
is compatible if and only if
r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}}\circ T\omega^{\flat}=T\omega^{\flat}\circ r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}}
.

Alongside Poisson-Nijenhuis structures,
Magri and Morosi considered in
[
37
]

presymplectic-Nijenhuis structures
(called
\Omega N
-structures
therein), which were defined as compatible pairs
(\omega,r)
, where
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is closed and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
is Nijenhuis. In this case, condition (2) above reduces to
d(\omega_{r})=0
.

Remark 3.7
.

In the original formulation of
[
37
]
, condition
(2)
above was written as the vanishing of a concomitant
S^{r}_{\omega}\in\Omega^{2}(M,T^{*}M)
given by

S_{\omega}^{r}(X,Y):=i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{r(Y)}\omega-i_{Y}\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\omega-i_{r([X,Y])}\omega+d(\omega(r(Y),X)).

Using condition (1), a direct application of Cartan calculus
shows that

S^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)=i_{Y}i_{X}d(\omega_{r})-i_{Y}i_{r(X)}d\omega-i_{r(Y)}i_{X}d\omega.

When
\omega
is closed, we see that

S^{r}_{\omega}=d(\omega_{r})=\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}
. In general, the concomitant
S^{r}_{\omega}
does not agree with our
\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}
in (
3.7
); they are related by
\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)=S^{r}_{\omega}(X,Y)+d\omega(X,r(Y))
.

\diamond

3.3.
Dirac-Nijenhuis structures

Let
M
be a smooth manifold, and consider
the direct sum
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M

with natural projections
\mathrm{pr}_{T}:\mathbb{T}M\to TM
and
\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}:\mathbb{T}M\to T^{*}M
. We equip
\mathbb{T}M
with
the nondegenerate fiberwise symmetric pairing

\langle(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\rangle=\beta(X)+\alpha(Y),

and the Courant-Dorfman bracket
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
on
\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Omega^{1}(M)
,

\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]=([X,Y],\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-i_{Y}d\alpha).

A subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
is called
lagrangian
if
L=L^{\perp}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. A
Dirac structure

[
16
]
on
M
is a lagrangian subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
which is involutive with respect to
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
. Important examples
of lagrangian subbundles include bivector fields
\pi
and 2-forms
\omega
, viewed as subbundles of
\mathbb{T}M
by means of the graphs of the
corresponding maps
\pi^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\to TM
and
\omega^{\flat}:TM\to T^{*}M
; the additional involutivity condition to make these subbundles into Dirac structures amount to
\pi
being Poisson and
\omega
being closed.

Given
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
,
consider the operator

(3.5)

\mathbb{D}^{r}:\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)\to\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{T}M),\qquad\mathbb{D}^{r}=(D^{r},D^{r,*}).

For each vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
we then have an
\mathbb{R}
-linear operator
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}:\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)
,

(3.6)

\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(Y,\alpha)=(D^{r}_{X}(Y),D^{r,*}_{X}(\alpha)),

for
(Y,\alpha)\in\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Omega^{1}(M)
.
From the Leibniz-type equations for
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
, we see that
\mathbb{D}^{r}
satisfies

(3.7)

\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(f\,\sigma)=f\,\mathbb{D}(\sigma)+(\mathcal{L}_{X}f)(r,r^{*})(\sigma)-(\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}f)\,\sigma,

for
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)
, and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, so the triple
(\mathbb{D}^{r},(r,r^{*}),r)
is a 1-derivation on
\mathbb{T}M
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
be a lagrangian subbundle and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. We say that the pair
(L,r)
is compatible if

(1)

(r,r^{*})(L)\subseteq L

(2)

\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L),\>\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

(Note that this notion of compatibility makes sense for any subbundle of
\mathbb{T}M
, not necessarily lagrangian.)

The previous definition has an interpretation that naturally extends Propositions
3.3
and
3.6
: a pair
(L,r)
is compatible if and only if

TL\subset T(TM\oplus T^{*}M)=T(TM)\times_{TM}T(T^{*}M)

satisfies
(r^{\scriptscriptstyle{tg}},r^{\scriptscriptstyle{cotg}})(TL)\subset TL
.

Remark 3.9
.

Condition (2) in Definition
3.8
can be also written in terms of the vanishing of a concomitant: for a lagrangian subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
satisfying
(r,r^{*})(L)\subseteq L
, the expression

(3.8)

C^{r}_{L}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}):=\langle\mathbb{D}^{r}_{(\cdot)}(\sigma_{1}),\sigma_{2}\rangle\in\Omega^{1}(M),\qquad\sigma_{1},\,\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(L),

defines an element in
\Gamma(\wedge^{2}L^{*}\otimes T^{*}M)
. (The skew-symmetry of
C^{r}_{L}
is a consequence of the duality (
2.13
), and the
C^{\infty}(M)
-linearity follows from (
3.7
).) The fact that
L
is lagrangian implies that (2) holds if and only if
C^{r}_{L}=0
.

\diamond

Definition 3.10
.

A Dirac-Nijenhuis structure on
M
is a compatible pair
(L,r)
where
L
is a Dirac structure and
r
is a Nijenhuis operator.

More general Dirac-Nijenhuis structures in abstract “Courant-Nijenhuis algebroids” will be the subject of
[
8
]
.

Example 3.11
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
and consider the lagrangian subbundle
L_{\pi}:=\mathrm{graph}(\pi^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{T}M
. Given
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, then
(r,r^{*})(L_{\pi})\subseteq L_{\pi}
if and only if
\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{*}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}
. It follows from (
3.3
) that
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L_{\pi}))\subset\Gamma(L_{\pi})
if and only if
R_{\pi}^{r}=0
. Hence the pair
(L_{\pi},r)
is compatible if and only if so is the pair
(\pi,r)
. (Under the natural identification
L_{\pi}\cong T^{*}M
, the concomitants
C_{L_{\pi}}^{r}
and
C_{\pi}^{r}
, see Remarks
3.2
and
3.9
, are in fact identified.) In particular,
(\pi,r)
is a Poisson-Nijenhuis structure if and only if
(L_{\pi},r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis.

Other examples of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures can be found on submanifolds of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures, see Corollary
4.2
.

Example 3.12
.

For
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
, let
L_{\omega}=\mathrm{graph}(\omega^{\flat})\subset\mathbb{T}M
be the corresponding lagrangian subbundle. Let
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Then condition
(r,r^{*})(L_{\omega})\subseteq L_{\omega}
is equivalent to
\omega^{\flat}\circ r=r^{*}\circ\omega^{\flat}
, while condition
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
is equivalent to

\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}=0
, see (
3.4
).
So the pair
(\omega,r)
is compatible if and only if so is the pair
(L_{\omega},\omega)
.
When
\omega
is closed,
\widetilde{S}^{r}_{\omega}=d(\omega_{r})

by Lemma
3.4
, so

\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
if and only if
d(\omega_{r})=0
.
In particular,
(\omega,r)
is a presymplectic-Nijenhuis structure if and only if
(L_{\omega},r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure.

Recall that any involutive distribution
F\subseteq TM
defines a Dirac structure
F\oplus\mathrm{Ann}(F)\subseteq\mathbb{T}M
.
The next example illustrates Dirac-Nijenhuis structures of this type (cf.
[
27
, Sec. 6]
).

Example 3.13
.

Let
r
be a Njenhuis operator of constant rank, and consider the distribution
F=\mathrm{Im}(r)\subseteq TM
.
The Nijenhuis property implies that
F
is involutive, so
L_{r}:=F\oplus\mathrm{Ann}(F)
is a Dirac structure.
Since
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{Im}(r))=\ker(r^{*})
, it is simple to check that
(r,r^{*})(L_{r})\subseteq L_{r}
. Condition (2) in Definition
3.8
follows from the identities
2.17
and (
2.18
), so
(L_{r},r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis.

Remark 3.14

(Quasi-Nijenhuis)
.

There are variants of the definition of Dirac-Nijenhuis structure obtained by weakening the integrability conditions on
L
or
r
. For example, a Dirac quasi-Nijenhuis structure is a triple
(L,r,\phi)
, where
L
is a Dirac structure compatible with
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
,
\phi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
is a closed 3-form,
and

(3.9)

\langle\alpha,\mathcal{N}_{r}(Y,Z)\rangle=-\phi(X,Y,Z),\qquad\forall\,(X,\alpha)\in L.

Notice that even when
\phi=0
this condition is weaker than the vanishing of the Nijenhuis torsion
\mathcal{N}_{r}
: it just says that the image of
\mathcal{N}_{r}
lies in
L\cap TM=\mathrm{Ann}(\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}(L))
. When
L
is the graph of a Poisson structure
\pi
, then (
3.9
) becomes

\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)=\pi^{\sharp}(\phi(X,Y,\cdot)),

hence recovering the Poisson quasi-Nijenhuis structures of
[
41
, Def. 3.3]

\diamond

3.4.
Holomorphic Dirac structures

Suppose that
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
is a complex structure on
M
. It is known (see e.g.
[
32
, Sec. 3]
) that holomorphic Poisson structures on the complex manifold
(M,r)
are characterized by the fact that their real parts are Poisson-Nijenhuis structures with respect to
r
. We will now extend this result to Dirac structures.

On a complex manifold
(M,r)
, we consider the holomorphic vector bundle
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M=T^{1,0}M\oplus(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
equipped with its natural
holomorphic Courant algebroid
structure, consisting of the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
(obtained from the duality of
T^{1,0}M
and
(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
), the projection
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M\to T^{1,0}M
and the
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear bracket on the sheaf of holomorphic sections
\Gamma_{\mathrm{hol}}(\cdot,\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M)
given by

\left[\!\left[(\mathsf{X},\xi),(\mathsf{Y},\zeta)\right]\!\right]=([\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}],\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{X}}\zeta-\textit{i}_{\mathsf{Y}}\partial\xi),

where
(\mathsf{X},\xi),(\mathsf{Y},\zeta)
are local holomorphic sections of
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
.
A
holomorphic Dirac structure on
M
(see e.g.
[
25
]
) is a holomorphic subbundle
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
which is lagrangian with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and whose sheaf of holomorphic sections is involutive with respect to
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
.

To explain the sense in which holomorphic Dirac structures are equivalent to Dirac-Nijenhuis structures, note that, from Example
2.6
, the
holomorphic structure on
\mathbb{T}M
coming from its identification with
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
via

(3.10)

\Phi:\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M,\qquad\Phi(X,\alpha)=\Big(\frac{1}{2}(X-\mathbf{i}\,r(X)),\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}(\alpha)\Big)

is given by the Dolbeault 1-derivation
(\mathbb{D}^{r},(r,r^{*}),r)
.
Another key property of the map
\Phi
is that it preserves Courant brackets of holomorphic sections.

Lemma 3.15
.

For (local) holomorphic sections
(X,\alpha),\,(Y,\beta)
of
\mathbb{T}M
, we have

\Phi(\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right])=\left[\!\left[\Phi(X,\alpha),\Phi(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right].

Proof.

Recall that
\left[\!\left[\Phi(X,\alpha),\Phi(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]
has
T^{1,0}M
-component given by

(3.11)

\frac{1}{4}[X-\mathbf{i}\,r(X),Y-\mathbf{i}\,r(Y)]

and
(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
-component given by

(3.12)

\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X-\mathbf{i}\,r(X)}(\beta-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}\beta)-i_{Y-\mathbf{i}\,r(Y)}\,\partial(\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}\alpha)).

On the other hand,

\Phi(\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right])=\left(\frac{1}{2}([X,Y]-\mathbf{i}\,r[X,Y]),\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-i_{Y}d\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-i_{Y}d\alpha)\right)

Recall (see (
2.6
)) that
(X,\alpha)
is holomorphic if and only if
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Z}(X,\alpha)=0
, for all
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and this condition implies the following identities for
(X,\alpha)
(similarly for
(Y,\beta)
):

\displaystyle[X,r(Z)]=r([X,Z]),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\gamma)=r^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{r(Z)}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{Z}r^{*}(\alpha),\qquad\;\;\;\;i_{r(Z)}d\alpha=i_{Z}d(r^{*}(\alpha)),

for all
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.
It follows that (
3.11
) equals

\frac{1}{4}([X,Y]-[r(X),r(Y)]-\textbf{i}([r(X),Y]+[X,r(Y)]))\\
=\frac{1}{2}([X,Y]-\textbf{i}r([X,Y])).

Similarly, (
3.12
) equals (recall that
d=\partial
on holomorphic forms)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}r^{*}(\beta)-{\mathbf{i}\,}(\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\beta+\mathcal{L}_{X}r^{*}(\beta))-2(i_{Y}d\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,i_{r(Y)}d\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-i_{Y}d\alpha-\mathbf{i}\,r^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-i_{Y}d\alpha).

∎

We now verify that holomorphic Dirac structures correspond to Dirac-Nijenhuis structures under the isomorphism (
3.10
).

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
be a complex structure on
M
and
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
be a lagrangian subbundle. The pair
(L,r)
is compatible if and only if
\Phi(L)
is a holomorphic lagrangian subbundle of
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
, and
(L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure if and only if
\Phi(L)
is a holomorphic Dirac structure.

Proof.

A real subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
is a complex subbundle if and only if
(r,r^{*})(L)\subseteq L
, and such a subbundle is holomorphic if and only if
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{X}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Also, for a complex subbundle
L\subseteq\mathbb{T}M

one can directly check that
\Phi(L)^{\perp}=\Phi(L^{\perp})
, so
L
is lagrangian in
\mathbb{T}M
if and only if
\Phi(L)
is lagrangian in
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
. This proves the first assertion of the proposition.

For the second assertion, we note that the involutivity of
L
is equivalent to the vanishing of
T\in\Gamma(\wedge^{3}L^{*})
given by

T(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}):=\langle\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right],\sigma_{3}\rangle.

For holomorphic sections
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\in\Gamma(L)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\left[\!\left[\Phi(\sigma_{1}),\Phi(\sigma_{2})\right]\!\right],\Phi(\sigma_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi(\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right]),\Phi(\sigma_{3})\rangle=T(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})-\mathbf{i}\,T(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},(r,r^{*})(\sigma_{3})).

The condition
\Phi(L)=\Phi(L)^{\perp}
implies that the sheaf of holomorphic sections of
\Phi(L)
is involutive if and only if
T
vanishes on holomorphic sections of
L
, which is in turn equivalent to the vanishing of
T

(since any smooth section
\sigma
of
L
can be written locally as
\sigma=\sum_{i}f_{i}\sigma_{i}
, where
f_{i}\in{C}^{\infty}(M)
and
\sigma_{i}
is holomorphic).
∎

For a complex lagrangian subbundle
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
, we define its
real part
as the real lagrangian subbundle
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
such that
\Phi(L)=\mathcal{L}
.

Example 3.17
.

Let
\Pi=\pi+\mathbf{i}\,\pi_{1}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM\otimes\mathbb{C})
be a complex bivector field. Then
\Pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{1,0}M)
if and only if
\pi_{1}^{\sharp}=-\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{*}
, i.e.,
\pi_{1}=-\pi_{r}
. In this case, the lagrangian subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
defined as the graph of
\Pi^{\sharp}:(T^{1,0}M)^{*}\to T^{1,0}M
has real part given by
L=\mathrm{graph}(4\pi^{\sharp})
. By Proposition
3.16
, we have a bijective correspondence between Poisson-Nijenhuis structures
(\pi,r)
and holomorphic Poisson structure
\Pi=\pi-\mathbf{i}\,\pi_{r}
, see
[
32
, Prop. 2.6]
.

Example 3.18
.

Let
\Omega=\omega+\mathbf{i}\,\omega_{1}\in\Gamma(\wedge(TM\otimes\mathbb{C})
be a complex 2-form. Then
\Omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}(T^{1,0}M)^{*})
if and only if
\omega_{1}=-\omega_{r}
, and the real part of the lagrangian subbundle
\mathcal{L}:=\mathrm{graph}(\Omega^{\flat})\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
, where
\Omega^{\flat}:T^{1,0}M\to(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
, is given by
L=\mathrm{graph}(\omega^{\flat})
. Proposition
3.16
thens gives a bijective correspondence between presymplectic-Nijenhuis structures
(\omega,r)
and holomorphic closed 2-forms
\Omega=\omega-\mathbf{i}\,\omega_{r}
.

We will generalize Example
3.18
to higher-degree forms in Proposition
5.4
below.

4.
Properties of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures

In this section we discuss presymplectic foliations and Poisson quoients of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures,
as well as hierarchies that extend those of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures.

4.1.
Backward maps and presymplectic leaves

Let
N
be a smooth manifold, and let
\psi:N\to M
be a smooth map. If
L_{N}
,
L_{M}
are Dirac structures on
N
and
M
, respectively, recall that
\psi
is a
backward Dirac map
(see e.g.
[
4
]
) if

(L_{N})_{x}=\{(X,(T_{x}\psi)^{*}(\beta))\in\mathbb{T}_{x}N\,\,|\,\,(T_{x}\psi(X),\beta)\in(L_{M})_{\psi(x)}\}.

Recall that
r_{N}\in\Omega^{1}(N,TN)
and
r_{M}\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
are
\psi
-related
if
r_{M}\circ d\psi=d\psi\circ r_{N}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\psi:(N,L_{N})\to(M,L_{M})
is a backward Dirac map
and that
r_{N}
and
r_{M}
are
\psi
-related.
If
L_{M}
is compatible with
r_{M}
, then
L_{N}
is compatible with
r_{N}
.

Proof.

The fact that
r_{N}
and
r_{M}
are
\psi
-related directly implies that
(r_{N},r_{N}^{*})(L_{N})\subset L_{N}
.
To show that
L_{N}
verifies condition (2) in Definition
3.8
, we can equivalently verify the vanishing of the concomitant
C^{r_{N}}_{L_{N}}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}L_{N}^{*}\otimes T^{*}N)
of Remark
3.9
.

We say that (local) sections
a=(X,\alpha)
of
\mathbb{T}N
and
b=(Y,\beta)
of
\mathbb{T}M
are
\psi
-related if
X
and
Y
are
\psi
-related, and
\alpha=\psi^{*}\beta
. Note that if
a\in\Gamma(\mathbb{T}N)
is
\psi
-related to
b\in\Gamma(L_{M})
, then it follows from the definition of backward Dirac map that
a\in\Gamma(L_{N})
.

Suppose that
a\in\Gamma(L_{N})
is
\psi
-related to
b\in\Gamma(L_{M})
, and let
Z_{1}\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
and
Z_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
be
\psi
-related.
It directly follows from the naturality properties (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) that
\mathbb{D}_{Z_{1}}^{r_{N}}(a)
is
\psi
-related to
\mathbb{D}_{Z_{2}}^{r_{M}}(b)\in\Gamma(L_{M})
(since we are assuming that
L_{M}
is
r_{M}
-compatible), and hence

\mathbb{D}_{Z_{1}}^{r_{N}}(a)\in\Gamma(L_{N})
. Now suppose that
x\in N
satisfies the following property: any
a_{x}\in L_{N}|_{x}
and
(Z_{1})_{x}\in TN|_{x}
can be extended to local sections
a
of
L_{N}
and
Z_{1}
of
TN
which are
\psi
-related to some
local sections
b
of
L_{M}
and
Z_{2}
on
TM
, respectively; in this case,
C^{r_{N}}_{L_{N}}|_{x}=0
. We finally observe that the points
x\in N
with this property form an open dense subset of
N
(see the proof of Prop. 5.6 in
[
4
]
), hence
C^{r_{N}}_{L_{N}}=0
.
∎

On a Dirac manifold
(M,L)
, let
\psi:C\hookrightarrow M
be a submanifold. It is known (see e.g.
[
4
, Sec. 5]
) that if the lagrangian distribution

(4.1)

\mathcal{B}_{\psi}(L)=\{(X,\psi^{*}\alpha)\in\mathbb{T}C\>|\>T\psi(X),\alpha)\in L\}\subset TC\oplus T^{*}C

defined by pointwise pullback of
L
to
C
fits into a smooth vector bundle (this is ensured by suitable clean-intersection conditions
[
16
]
, see also
[
4
]
), then it defines a Dirac structure on
C
for which
\psi
is a backward Dirac map.

For
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, we call a submanifold
C\hookrightarrow M

r
-invariant
if
r(TC)\subseteq TC
; we define
r_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,TC)
by
r|_{TC}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
L
be a Dirac structure on
M
compatible with
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Let
\psi:C\hookrightarrow M
be an
r
-invariant submanifold for which
\mathcal{B}_{\psi}(L)
in (
4.1
) is smooth, hence a Dirac structure on
C
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\psi}(L)
is compatible with
r_{C}
.

Note that
r_{C}
is Nijenhuis if
r
is Nijenhuis. So the previous corollary illustrates how Dirac-Nijenhuis structures naturally arise on submanifolds of Dirac-Nijenhuis manifolds.

A special case is that of presymplectic leaves of a Dirac manifold
(M,L)
. Recall that
L
defines a (singular) foliation on
M
whose leaves are tangent to the distribution given by the projection of
L
on
TM
; moreover, each leaf
\mathcal{O}
carries a closed
2
-form
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{O})
, which can be seen as the pullback of
L
to
\mathcal{O}
. Note also that the condition
(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L
implies that
T\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{pr}_{T}(L)
is preserved by
r
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
L
be a Dirac structure on
M
compatible with
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Then each leaf
\mathcal{O}
is
r
-invariant, and its presymplectic form
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}
is compatible with the restriction
r_{\mathcal{O}}
. In particular, if
(M,L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis manifold, then its leaves are presymplectic-Nijenhuis manifolds.

4.2.
Forward maps and Poisson quotients

A map
\psi:(M,L_{M})\to(N,L_{N})
between Dirac manifolds is a
forward Dirac map
if

(L_{N})_{\psi(x)}=\{(T_{x}\psi(X),\beta)\in\mathbb{T}_{\psi(x)}N\,\,|\,\,(X,(T_{x}\psi)^{*}\beta)\in(L_{M})_{x}\}.

As in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, one can use the naturality properties of Remark
2.5
to
show the following result:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\psi:(M,L_{M})\to(N,L_{N})
be a surjective submersion that is a forward Dirac map. Suppose that
r_{M}\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
is
\psi
-related to
r_{N}\in\Omega^{1}(N,TN)
. Then
L_{N}
is compatible with
r_{N}
provided
L_{M}
is compatible with
r_{M}
.

Note that if
r_{M}
is Nijenhuis, then so is
r_{N}
, hence the previous result illustrates how Dirac-Nijenhuis structures may arise on quotients. The following is a special case.

Recall
[
16
]
(see also
[
4
]
) that the
null distribution
of a Dirac structure
L
on
M
is

K:=L\cap TM\subseteq TM.

At each point
x\in M
,
K
agrees with the kernel of the presymplectic form on the leaf through
x
. Whenever
K
has constant rank, it is integrable and defines the so-called
null foliation

\mathcal{K}
. If the null foliation is simple, then
Q:=M/\mathcal{K}
inherits a Poisson structure
\pi_{Q}
uniquely determined by the fact that the quotient map
M\to Q
is a forward Dirac map.

Given
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
and a surjective submersion
\psi:M\to N
, we say that
r

descends
to
N
if
there exists
r_{N}\in\Omega^{1}(Q,TN)
that is
\psi
-related to
r
, in which case
r_{N}
is uniquely determined by this property.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
L
be a Dirac structure on
M
whose null foliation
\mathcal{K}
is simple, let
Q
be the leaf space with induced Poisson structure
\pi_{Q}
, and suppose that
L
is compatible with
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
. Then
r
descends to
r_{Q}\in\Omega^{1}(Q,TQ)
which is compatible with
\pi_{Q}
.
In particular, if
(M,L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis manifold, its quotient
Q=M/\mathcal{K}
is a Poisson-Nijenhuis manifold.

Proof.

Once we prove that
r
descends to
Q
, the result follows directly from Proposition
4.4
.

The condition
(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L
implies that
r(K)\subseteq K
and
r^{*}(\mathrm{Ann}(K))\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}(K)
(since
\mathrm{Ann}(K)=\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}(L)
). To verify that
r
descends to
Q
, we must check that
r
preserves projectable vector fields, i.e.,
r(\mathfrak{X}(M)_{pr})\subseteq\mathfrak{X}(M)_{pr}
,
where

\mathfrak{X}(M)_{pr}=\{Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,\,|\,\,[X,Y]\in\Gamma(K),\,\,\forall X\in\Gamma(K)\}.

So let
\alpha\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Ann}(K))
,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)_{pr}
and
X\in\Gamma(K)
, and note that

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,[X,r(Y)]\rangle

\displaystyle=i_{X}\mathcal{L}_{r(Y)}\alpha-\cancel{\mathcal{L}_{r(Y)}i_{X}\alpha}^{=0}=i_{X}(-D^{r,*}_{Y}(\alpha)+\mathcal{L}_{Y}(r^{*}\alpha))

\displaystyle=-\cancel{\langle D^{r,*}_{Y}(\alpha),X\rangle}^{=0}+\cancel{i_{[X,Y]}r^{*}(\alpha)}^{=0}+\cancel{\mathcal{L}_{Y}i_{X}r^{*}(\alpha)}^{=0}=0,

where for the last equality, besides the fact that
r^{*}
preserves
\mathrm{Ann}(K)
, we used that
D^{r,*}_{Y}
preserves
\Gamma(\mathrm{Ann}(K))
(which follows from
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
and
\mathrm{Ann}(K)=\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}(L)
).
∎

4.3.
Traces of powers of
r
.

An important property of a PN manifold
(\pi,r)
is that

(4.2)

\phi_{j}:=\frac{1}{j}\mathrm{trace}(r^{j}),\,\,\,j\geq 1,

are functions in involution:
\{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\}=0
,
\forall\,i,\,j\geq 1
, see
[
37
]
. We now present a generalization of this result for Dirac-Nijenhuis manifolds.

Recall that, for a Dirac structure
L
on
M
, the space of
admissible functions
on
M
, denoted by
C^{\infty}_{adm}(M)
, is the space of functions
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
for which there exists
X_{f}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
satisfying
(X_{f},df)\in\Gamma(L)
. Although such
X_{f}
may not be uniquely defined, there is a Poisson bracket on
C^{\infty}_{adm}(M)
defined by the usual formula

\{f,g\}=dg(X_{f}),

see
[
16
]
. In case the the null distribution is regular, a function is admissible if and only if it is constant along the leaves of the null foliation
\mathcal{K}
. If
\mathcal{K}
is simple, the natural identification
C^{\infty}(M/\mathcal{K})\cong C^{\infty}_{adm}(M)
is an isomorphism of Poisson algebras.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(L,r)
be a Dirac-Nijenhuis manifold. If
\phi_{1}=\mathrm{trace}(r)
is admissible, then
\phi_{i}
is admissible for every
i\geq 2
and

\{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\}=0,\,\,\forall\,i,j\geq 1.

Proof.

Recall the useful formula
r^{*}(d\phi_{i})=d\phi_{i+1}
(see
[
37
, Eq. 4.2]
). So, if
\phi_{1}
is admissible, there exists
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that
(X,d\phi_{1})\in\Gamma(L)
. In this case, since
L
and
r
are compatible,

\Gamma(L)\ni(r,r^{*})^{i-1}(X,\phi_{1})=(r^{i-1}(X),d\phi_{i})\Longrightarrow\text{ $\phi_{i}$ is admissible. }

Also, a simple calculation in local coordinates shows that

\langle d\,\mathrm{trace}(r),X\rangle=\mathrm{trace}(D^{r}_{(\cdot)}(X)).

Now, using that
\mathrm{trace}(AB)=\mathrm{trace}(BA)
and

D^{r^{j}}_{X}(Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{j}r^{j-k}(D^{r}_{r^{k}(X)}(Y)),

one shows that

\displaystyle\{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\}

\displaystyle=d\phi_{j}(r^{i-1}(X))=\mathrm{trace}(r^{j}(D_{\cdot}^{r}(r^{i-1}(X)))),

\displaystyle\{\phi_{j},\phi_{i}\}

\displaystyle=d\phi_{i}(r^{j-1}(X))=\mathrm{trace}(r^{i}(D_{\cdot}^{r}(r^{j-1}(X)))).

As
D^{r}(r(X))=r(D^{r}(X))
(see (
2.17
)), one obtains that
\{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\}=\{\phi_{j},\phi_{i}\}
, which can only happen if
\{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\}=0
.
∎

The following simple example shows that the admissibility condition on
\mathrm{trace}(r)
is not automatic.
Moreover, even when it holds and the Poisson-Nijenhuis quotient
Q=M/\mathcal{K}
is well defined (see Cor.
4.5
), we will see that the relationship between
\mathrm{trace}(r)
and the pull-back of
\mathrm{trace}(r_{Q})
is not straightforward.

Example 4.7
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2})
, consider the distribution
F
generated by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
. Consider
L=F\oplus\mathrm{Ann}(F)
and

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=a(x_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\otimes dx_{1}+b(x_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\otimes dx_{2}

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}

\displaystyle=a(x_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\otimes dx_{1}+c(x_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\otimes dx_{2}

for
a,b,c:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
smooth functions. One can check that
(L,r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis, but
\mathrm{trace}(r)
is admissible if and only if
b
is a constant function.

On the other, the pair
(L,\widetilde{r})
is compatible,
\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r})
is admissible, but
\widetilde{r}
is Nijenhuis if and only if

(4.3)

(a-c)\frac{dc}{dx_{1}}=0.

Then the relationship between
\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r})=a+c
and
\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r}_{Q})=a
is that

\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r})=2\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r}_{Q})\;\;\mbox{ or }\;\;\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r})=\mathrm{trace}(\widetilde{r}_{Q})+\lambda,

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, depending on whether
a=c
or
dc/dx_{1}=0
, respectively. But it is possible to have a mixture of these situations if there exist points
\bar{x}_{1}
where
d^{n}a/dx_{1}^{n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
. In this case,
c
defined as
a
on
(-\infty,\bar{x}_{1})
and as
\lambda=a(\bar{x}_{1})
on
[\bar{x}_{1},+\infty)
is smooth and satisfies (
4.3
).

4.4.
Hierarchies of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures

A fundamental aspect in the theory of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures
(\pi,r)
is the existence of a hierarchy of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures
(\pi_{n},r)
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, recursively defined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}:=\pi,\qquad

\displaystyle\pi_{n}^{\sharp}=r\circ\pi_{n-1}^{\sharp}=r^{n}\circ\pi^{\sharp},

see e.g.
[
37
,
31
]
.
This hierarchy is a key ingredient in the construction of integrals for bihamiltonian systems admitting a Poisson-Nijenhuis description. Similarly, a presymplectic-Nijenhuis structure
(\omega,r)
gives rise to a hierarchy of presymplectic-Nijenhuis structures
(\omega_{n},r)
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, where

(4.5)

\omega_{n}^{\flat}=\omega^{\flat}\circ r^{n}.

In particular, symplectic-Nijenhuis structures give rise to two hierarchies, one of Poisson structures, and the other of presymplectic forms. We now see how to extend these hierarchies to Dirac-Nijenhuis structures.

Let
(L,r)
be a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure on
M
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(4.6)

L_{(n,0)}:=(r^{n},\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})(L),\qquad L_{(0,n)}:=(\mathrm{id}_{TM},(r^{*})^{n})(L).

Proposition 4.8
.

(1) If
\ker(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L}=0
, then
(L_{(n,0)},r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
; (2) if
\ker(\mathrm{id}_{TM},r^{*})|_{L}=0
, then
(L_{(0,n)},r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We will prove (1); the proof of (2) is similar.

Suppose that
\ker(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L}=0
. In this case,
L_{(1,0)}
is a smooth subbundle of
\mathbb{T}M
with the same rank as
L
. We will prove that
L_{(1,0)}
is isotropic (hence lagrangian) and involutive with respect the Courant bracket. The result for a general
L_{(n,0)}
will follow by induction.

To prove that
L_{(1,0)}
is isotropic, note that, for

(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\in\Gamma(L)
,

\langle(r(X),\alpha),(r(Y),\beta)\rangle=\langle(X,\alpha),(r,r^{*})(Y,\beta)\rangle=0

since
(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L
. To prove that involutivity of
L_{(1,0)}
, we will show that

\langle\left[\!\left[(r(X),\alpha),(r(Y),\beta)\right]\!\right],(r(Z),\gamma)\rangle=0

for any
(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta),(Z,\gamma)\in\Gamma(L)
. From the definitions of
D^{r}
and
D^{r,*}
, see (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), one can check that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\langle\left[\!\left[(r(X),\alpha),(r(Y),\beta)\right]\!\right],(r(Z),\gamma)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,\left[\!\left[(r(X),r^{*}(\alpha)),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right],(r(Z),r^{*}(\gamma))\,\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-70.0pt+\langle\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(X,\alpha),(r(Z),r^{*}(\gamma))\rangle+\langle\gamma,\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)\rangle.

Since
(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L
,
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(\Gamma(L))\subset\Gamma(L)
and
\mathcal{N}_{r}=0
, we conclude that
L_{(1,0)}
is a Dirac structure on
M
. The fact that
L_{(1,0)}
and
r
are compatible is a direct consequence of (
2.17
). For the inductive step, notice that, if
L_{(n-1,0)}
is a Dirac structure, then

\ker(r^{n},\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L}=\ker(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L_{(n-1,0)}}\,\,\,\text{ and }L_{(n,0)}=(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})(L_{(n-1,0)}).

∎

In this section we extend the compatibility in Def.

We refine the infinitesimal description of multiplicative forms in

]
to include their compatibility with
(1,1)
-tensor fields (Thm.

), leading to a holomorphic version of
[

(Thm.

5.1.
Review of multiplicative structures

We begin with a brief review of multiplicative differential forms and
(1,1)
-tensor fields on Lie groupoids and their infinitesimal descriptions, following
[
5
,
7
]
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid. We denote its source and targent maps by

s,t:\mathcal{G}\to M
, and its multiplication map by
m:\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
is the fiber product
\mathcal{G}_{\,s}\!\!\times_{t}\mathcal{G}
. We will often identify
M
with its image under the unit map. The Lie algebroid of
\mathcal{G}
is
A_{\mathcal{G}}:=\ker(Ts)|_{M}
, with anchor
Tt|_{A_{\mathcal{G}}}
and bracket defined by the identification of
\Gamma(A_{\mathcal{G}})
with right-invariant vector fields on
\mathcal{G}
.

A differential
p
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(\mathcal{G})
is called
multiplicative
if

m^{*}\omega=\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega,

where
\mathrm{pr}_{i}:\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is the natural projection, for
i=1,2
. These objects have infinitesimal counterparts given as follows. An
IM p-form
on a Lie algebroid
A\to M
is defined by a pair
(\mu,\nu)
, where

\mu:A\rightarrow\wedge^{p-1}T^{*}M,\qquad\nu:A\rightarrow\wedge^{p}T^{*}M

are vector-bundle maps (over the identity on
M
)
satisfying the so-called
IM equations
:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\mu([a,b])=\mathcal{L}_{\rho(a)}\mu(b)-i_{\rho(b)}(d\mu(a)+\nu(a))\\
\nu([a,b])=\mathcal{L}_{\rho(a)}\nu(b)-i_{\rho(b)}d\nu(a)\\
i_{\rho(a)}\mu(b)=-i_{\rho(b)}\mu(a)\end{cases}

for all
a,b\in\Gamma(A)
. Any multiplicative
p
-form
\omega
on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
gives rise to an IM
p
-form
(\mu,\nu)
on the corresponding Lie algebroid
A=A_{\mathcal{G}}
via

(5.2)

i_{\overrightarrow{a}}\,\omega=t^{*}\mu(a),\,\;\;\;\;\;i_{\overrightarrow{a}}\,d\omega=t^{*}\nu(a).

Here
\overrightarrow{a}
denotes the right-invariant vector field on
\mathcal{G}
corresponding to
a\in\Gamma(A)
.
When
\mathcal{G}
is source 1-connected, (
5.2
) defines a one-to-one correspondence between multiplicative
p
-forms on
\mathcal{G}
and IM
p
-forms on
A
, see
[
5
]
.
Under this bijection, closed multiplicative forms correspond to IM-forms with
\nu=0
, that we will also refer to as
closed
.

A
(1,1)
-tensor field
K\in\Gamma(T^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes T\mathcal{G})
is called
multiplicative
if there is a vector bundle map
r:TM\rightarrow TM
such that
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TM\par}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

is a groupoid morphism. Their infinitesimal counterparts are described as follows
[
7
, Sec. 6]
. On a Lie algebroid
A
, an
IM
(1,1)
-tensor
is a triple

(D,l,r)
, where
l:A\rightarrow A
and
r:TM\to TM
are vector-bundle maps (over the identity on
M
) and
D:\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes A)
is a
\mathbb{R}
-linear map satisfying
the Leibniz-type rule

D(fa)=fD(a)+df\wedge l(a)-a\wedge r^{*}(df),\quad\mbox{ for }\,f\in{C}^{\infty}(M),a\in\Gamma(A),

and such that the following
IM-equations
hold:

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}r\circ\rho&=\rho\circ l,\\
\rho(D_{X}(a))&=D_{X}^{r}(\rho(a)),\\
l([a,b])&=[a,l(b)]-D_{\rho(b)}(a)\\
D_{X}([a,b])&=[a,D_{X}(b)]-[b,D_{X}(a)]+D_{[\rho(b),X]}(a)-D_{[\rho(a),X]}(b).\end{array}\right.

In the language of
\lx@sectionsign

2.1
,
(D,l,r)
is a 1-derivation on the vector bundle
A
satisfying the additional compatibilities (

) with respect to its Lie-algebroid structure.

The IM
(1,1)
-tensor
(D,l,r)
corresponding to a multiplicative

-tensor field

is defined by the conditions

(5.4)

K(\overrightarrow{a})=\overrightarrow{l(a)},\,\,\quad Tt(K(U))=r(Tt(U)),\,\,\quad(\mathcal{L}_{\overrightarrow{a}}K)(U)=\overrightarrow{D_{Tt(U)}(a)},

for

and
t
-projectable
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
. With the identification
T\mathcal{G}|_{M}=TM\oplus A
, we directly see that
r=K|_{TM}
and

As before, when
\mathcal{G}
is a source 1-connected Lie groupoid, we have a bijective correspondence between

(1,1)
-tensor fields and IM
(1,1)
-tensors on

By regarding vector bundles as Lie groupoids (with respect to fiberwise addition), one can check that multiplicative

For a multiplicative

5.2.
Global and infinitesimal compatibility

We will deal here with a natural extension of the compatibility in Definition
3.5
to differential forms of arbitrary degrees.
Given a differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, we denote by
\omega_{r}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\wedge^{p-1}T^{*}M)
the tensor field given by

\omega_{r}(X_{1};X_{2},\dots,X_{p})=\omega(r(X_{1}),X_{2},\dots,X_{p}).

Definition 5.1
.

We say that the pair
(\omega,r)
is compatible if

(a)

\omega_{r}
is skew-symmetric, i.e.
\omega_{r}\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

(b)

d(\omega_{r})=(d\omega)_{r}
.

A motivating example for this notion of compatibility is given in Proposition
5.4
below.

Denote by
\Omega_{r}^{p}(M)
the space of differential
p
-forms
\omega
such that
\omega_{r}\in\Omega^{p}(M)
. Then the operator
D^{r,*}
(
2.12
) can be extended to

D^{r,*}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Omega_{r}^{p}(M)\to\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes\wedge^{p-1}T^{*}M)

via

(5.7)

D^{r,*}_{Y}(\omega)=i_{Y}d(\omega_{r})-i_{r(Y)}d\omega,\qquad\forall\,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

So a pair
(\omega,r)
is compatible if and only if
\omega\in\Omega_{r}^{p}(M)
and
D^{r,*}(\omega)=0
.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that an IM 2-form
(\mu,\nu)
and an IM
(1,1)
-tensor
(D,l,r)
on a Lie algebroid
A
are compatible if

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mu(l(a))=\mu(a)_{r},\,\,\qquad\quad\;\;\;\nu(l(a))=\nu(a)_{r}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mu(D_{X}(a))=D_{X}^{r,*}(\mu(a)),\,\,\,\;\nu(D_{X}(a))=D_{X}^{r,*}(\nu(a)),

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
a\in\Gamma(A)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid endowed with multiplicative
K\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G},T\mathcal{G})
and
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(\mathcal{G})
, with corresponding infinitesimal data
(D,l,r)
and
(\mu,\nu)
. If the pair
(\omega,K)
is compatible then
(D,l,r)
and
(\mu,\nu)
are compatible,
and the converse holds if
\mathcal{G}
is source-connected.
Moreover, the IM
p
-form corresponding to
\omega_{K}
is
(\mu\circ l,\nu\circ l)
.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\Gamma(\otimes^{p}T^{*}\mathcal{G})
be tensor fields on
\mathcal{G}
defined by

\tau_{1}(U_{1},\dots,U_{p})=\omega(K(U_{1}),U_{2},\dots,U_{p}),\,\,\,\tau_{2}(U_{1},\dots,U_{p})=\omega(U_{1},K(U_{2}),U_{3},\dots,U_{p}),

for
U_{1},\dots,U_{p}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
. Note that
\omega\in\Omega_{K}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
if and only if
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
. Since
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are multiplicative tensor fields, assuming that
\mathcal{G}
is source connected they will be equal if and only if (see
[
7
, Rem. 3.16]
)

(A)

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(\cdot,\overbrace{\overrightarrow{a}}^{i\text{th-entry}},\cdot)

\displaystyle=\tau_{2}(\cdot,\overbrace{\overrightarrow{a}}^{i\text{th-entry}},\cdot),\,\,\quad\forall\,i=1,\dots,p,

(B)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\overrightarrow{a}}\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\overrightarrow{a}}\tau_{2}.

We first claim that (A) is equivalent to the first equation in (
5.8
). Indeed, it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.4
) that

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(\overrightarrow{a},U_{1},...,U_{p-1})

5.3.
Holomorphic multiplicative forms

In this section we specialize the results from the previous section to the holomorphic setting.

We start with a general observation relating the compatibility in Definition

with holomorphic forms.
Let

Proposition 5.4

A complex form

(\omega,r)
is compatible and
\omega_{1}=-\omega_{r}

Proof.

The condition

for all

, which in turn holds if and only if

.
We now verify that condition

. For

6.
Integration of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures

In this section we refine the inifinitesimal-global correspondence between Dirac structures and presymplectic groupoids to include compatible with
(1,1)
-tensor fields. In this way we obtain the global counterparts of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures and, as a particular case, the integration of holomorphic Dirac structures (see
[
33
,
41
]
for the special cases of Poisson (quasi-) Nijenhuis and holomorphic Poisson structures).

6.1.
Dirac structures and presymplectic groupoids

We now briefly recall the integration of Dirac structures to presymplectic groupoids
[
6
]
, as an application of the correspondence between multiplicative and IM-forms in
[
5
]
.

The starting point is the observation that Dirac structures can be identified with special types of IM 2-forms. Any Dirac structure
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
is naturally a Lie algebroid with respect to the restriction of the Courant bracket
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]
to
\Gamma(L)
and anchor
\rho=\mathrm{pr}_{T}|_{L}:L\to TM
. One can then directly verify that the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}|_{L}:L\to T^{*}M
defines a closed IM 2-form on
L
. Conversely, the closed IM 2-forms that arise in this way have a simple characterization.
Let
\mu:A\to T^{*}M
be a closed IM 2-form on a Lie algebroid
A
such that

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\mathrm{rank}(A)=\dim(M),\\
\ker(\rho)\cap\ker(\mu)=\{0\}.\end{cases}

A consequence of the IM-equations (
5.1
) (with
\nu=0
) is that the image of the map

(\rho,\mu):A\to\mathbb{T}M
,

(6.2)

L:=\mathrm{Im}(\rho,\mu),

is a Dirac structure. The Lie algebroid
A
is identified with
L
under this map, in such a way that
\mu=\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}|_{L}
.

A
presymplectic groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
equipped with a closed multiplicative 2-form
\omega
such that

(6.3)

\begin{cases}\dim(\mathcal{G})=2\dim(M)\\
\ker(Ts)_{x}\cap\ker(Tt)_{x}\cap\ker(\omega)_{x}=\{0\},\,\,\,\forall\,x\in M.\end{cases}

It is shown in
[
5
, Sec. 4]
that conditions (
6.3
) on
\mathcal{G}
and
\omega
are equivalent to conditions
(
6.1
) for the corresponding Lie algebroid
A
and closed IM 2-form
\mu:A\to T^{*}M
.
It follows that if
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a presymplectic groupoid, then
M
inherits a Dirac structure
L
, as in (
6.2
), together with an induced isomorphism of Lie algebroids between
L
and
A
. Moreover, the first equation in (
5.2
) implies that
L
is uniquely determined by the fact that
t:\mathcal{G}\to M
is a forward Dirac map. When this happens, we say that the presymplectic groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\omega)

integrates
the Dirac structure
L
.

The opposite direction, going from Dirac structures to presymplectic groupoids, follows from the integration of IM-forms (see
\lx@sectionsign

5.1
): given a Dirac structure on
L
that is integrable as a Lie algebroid and its source 1-connected integration
\mathcal{G}
,
the integration of the IM 2-form
\mu=\mathrm{pr}_{T^{*}}|_{L}
defines a
unique (up to isomorphism) closed multiplicative 2-form
\omega
such that
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a presymplectic groupoid integrating
L
.

6.2.
Integration of Dirac structures with compatible
(1,1)
-tensor fields

We will now relate compatible
(1,1)
-tensor fields on presymplectic groupoids and on their associated Dirac manifolds.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
L
be a Dirac structure on
M
,

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{T}\circ(r,r^{*})=

We are now ready to show the infinitesimal-global correspondence of presymplectic-Nijenhuis groupoids and Dirac-Nijenhuis structures.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
be a presymplectic groupoid integrating a Dirac structure
L
on
M
.

(a)

Let

be multiplicative, and let

If the pair
(\omega,K)
is compatible (resp. presymplectic-Nijenhuis) then the pair
(L,r)
is compatible (resp. Dirac-Nijenhuis).

(b)

If
\mathcal{G}
is source 1-connected, the correspondence
K\mapsto r=K|_{TM}
is a bijection between multiplicative
K\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G},T\mathcal{G})
compatible with
\omega
and
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
compatible with
L
(moreover,
K
is Nijenhuis if and only if so is
r

Proof.

Suppose that the pair
(\omega,K)
is compatible.
Let
(D,l,r)
be the IM
(1,1)
-tensor corresponding to
K
and
\mu
the closed IM 2-form of
\omega
, so that
L=(\rho,\mu)(A)
.
It follows from the first two equalities in (
5.3

r\circ\rho=\rho\circ l,\qquad\rho(D_{X}(a))=D_{X}^{r}(\rho(a)),

and the compatibility conditions (
5.8
) and (

5.3
, recalling that
\nu=0

that the pair
(L,r)
is compatible. Moreover, if
K
is Nijenhuis then so is

(see (

)). This proves part (a). Note also that, under the Lie-algebroid isomorphism
(\rho,\mu):A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}L
, the IM
(1,1)
-tensor

is identified with
(\mathbb{D}^{r}|_{\Gamma(L)},(r,r^{*}),r)

\mathcal{G}
is source connected, any

that is multiplicative, compatible with
\omega
, and satisfies

, must agree with
K
.

Suppose now that
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is a source 1-connected integration of
L
.
Let

be compatible with
L
. By Lemma
6.1
,

(\mathbb{D}^{r}|_{\Gamma(L)},(r,r^{*}),r)
is an IM
(1,1)
-tensor on
L
, which is easily seen to be compatible with the closed IM 2-form

It follows that the IM
(1,1)
-tensor

through the identification

\mu

obtained by integration of

is compatible with
\omega

by Thm.

satisfies

and, as argued above, any multiplicative
(1,1)
-tensor field on

∎

When (a) holds, we say that

.

Remark 6.4

(The quasi-Nijenhuis version)
.

The correspondence in Theorem
6.3
has the following version for quasi-Nijenhuis structures (see Remark

(L,r,\phi)\text{ is Dirac quasi-Nijenhuis }\Longleftrightarrow\omega(\cdot,\mathcal{N}_{K}(\cdot,\cdot))=s^{*}\phi-t^{*}\phi.

Indeed, by considering

\tau(U,V,W)=\omega(U,\mathcal{N}_{K}(V,W))

and its infinitesimal description, one can show that
\tau
is skew-symmetric (hence a 3-form) with corresponding IM 3-form
\tilde{\mu}:A\to\wedge^{2}T^{*}M
,
\tilde{\nu}:A\to\wedge^{3}T^{*}M
on

given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}(\mu(a))

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}(a)|_{(X,Y,Z)}

\displaystyle=d\mu(a)(X,\mathcal{N}_{r}(Y,Z))-\langle\mu(D^{2}_{(Y,Z)}(a)),X\rangle-d(\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}\mu(a))(X,Y,Z).

Since the pair
(\omega,K)
is compatible, it follows from Theorem

and (

\mu(D^{2}_{(Y,Z)}(a))=(D^{r,*})^{2}_{(Y,Z)}(\mu(a))=i_{Y}i_{Z}d(\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}\mu(a))-i_{\mathcal{N}_{r}(Y,Z)}d\mu(a),

.

In conclusion, we obtain a natural bijective correspondence between source 1-connected presymplectic-Nijenhuis groupoids and Dirac-Nijenhuis manifolds (which are integrable as Lie algebroids),

(\mathcal{G},\omega,K)\;\;\leftrightharpoons\;\;(M,L,r),

where

is the forward image of
\omega
by

and

is
t

(and the same holds for the quasi-Nijenhuis condition).
This restricts to a correspondence between symplectic (quasi-)Nijenhuis groupoids and Poisson (quasi-)Nijenhuis structures, recovering
[

.

We have the following simple relation between integration of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures and the “

Corollary 6.5
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{G},\omega,K)

∎

6.3.
Integration of holomorphic Dirac structures

We now introduce the global counterparts of holomorphic Dirac structures.

Definition 6.7
.

A holomorphic presymplectic groupoid is a holomorphic Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
endowed with a multiplicative holomorphic
2
-form
\Omega
such that
\partial\Omega=0
,
\dim(\mathcal{G})=2\dim(M)
, and

(6.9)

\ker(\Omega)_{x}\cap\ker(Ts)_{x}\cap\ker(Tt)_{x}=\{0\},\qquad\forall\,x\in M.

The intersection condition (
6.9
) takes place in
T^{1,0}\mathcal{G}|_{M}
. We denote by
\widehat{\mu}:\mathcal{A}\to(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
the holomorphic IM 2-form corresponding to
\Omega
,
\widehat{\mu}(u)=(i_{u}\Omega)|_{T^{1,0}M}
(note that
\widehat{\nu}=0
since
\partial\Omega=0
).

We will prove the infinitesimal-global correspondence relating holomorphic Dirac structures and holomorphic presymplectic groupoids as a consequence of Theorem
5.5
, though it can also be derived from Theorem
6.3
by working with real parts (see comments after Thm.
6.9
below).

Just as in the real smooth case, holomorphic presymplectic groupoids give rise to holomorphic Dirac structures on their spaces of units.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Omega)\rightrightarrows M
be a holomorphic presymplectic groupoid. (1) There exists a unique holomorphic Dirac structure
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
for which
t:\mathcal{G}\to M
is a forward Dirac map. (2) The map
(\widehat{\rho},\widehat{\mu}):\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
induces an isomorphism of Lie algebroids
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the real case.
Holomorphic Dirac structures
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
can be identified with closed holomorphic IM 2-forms
\widehat{\mu}:\mathcal{A}\to(T^{1,0}M)^{*}
satisfying

(6.10)

\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{A})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(M),\qquad\ker(\widehat{\rho})\cap\ker(\widehat{\mu})=\{0\},

(cf.
\lx@sectionsign

6.1
) as the image of
(\widehat{\rho},\widehat{\mu}):\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{T}^{1,0}M
,

(6.11)

\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{Im}(\widehat{\rho},\widehat{\mu}).

The multiplicativity of
\Omega
implies that

(6.12)

i_{\overrightarrow{u}}\Omega=t^{*}\widehat{\mu}(u),

for any section of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
. By using this last property, one can check that
(
6.9
) boils down to the intersection condition in (
6.10

\mathcal{L}
as in
(
6.11

Moreover,

Using the identity

it also follows from (

∎

c\in\Gamma(E)
, and using basic properties of the cotangent prolongation bundle
T^{*}E\to E^{*}
(see
[
34
, § 9.4]
, one has that

\displaystyle\langle\xi,D_{X}(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Tu(r(X))+_{\scriptscriptstyle{T}}h(r(X),c),\mathcal{R}(d\ell_{u}(\xi))+_{\scriptscriptstyle{E^{*}}}0_{\xi}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\langle Tu(r(X)),\mathcal{R}(d\ell_{u}(\xi))\rangle_{E}+\langle h(r(X),c),0_{\xi}\rangle_{E},

where
+_{\scriptscriptstyle{E^{*}}}
is the fiberwise sum and
0_{\xi}
is the zero of the cotangent prolongation
T^{*}E\to E^{*}
. As
\langle h(r(X),c),0_{\xi}\rangle_{E}=\langle\xi,c\rangle
, it only remains to prove that
\langle Tu(r(X)),\mathcal{R}(d\ell_{u}(\xi))\rangle_{E}=0
. This will follow from (see
[
34
, Prop. 9.5.3]
)

\mathcal{R}(d\ell_{u}(\xi(x)))=d\ell_{\xi}(u(x))-q^{*}(d\langle\xi,u\rangle)(u(x)),\,\,\,x\in M.

Indeed, this identity implies that

\displaystyle\langle Tu(r(X)),\mathcal{R}(d\ell_{u}(\xi))\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X}\langle\xi,u\rangle-\mathcal{L}_{X}\langle\xi,u\rangle=0.

∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
, notice that
\Phi
being a vector bundle map implies that

T\Phi(h(X,e_{1}))=h(X,\Phi(e_{1})),

for
e_{1}\in E_{1}
. It follows from (
A.2
) that

T\Phi\circ K_{1}(h(X,e_{1}))=K_{2}(T\Phi(h(X,e_{1})))\Longleftrightarrow r_{1}=r_{2}=r,\,\,\,\ \Phi\circ l_{1}=l_{2}\circ\Phi.

Similarly, from (
A.1
),

\displaystyle T\Phi(K_{1}(Tu(X)))

\displaystyle=T(\Phi(u))(r(X))+_{\scriptscriptstyle{T}}h(r(X),\Phi(D^{1}_{X}(u))),

\displaystyle K_{2}(T\Phi(Tu(X)))

\displaystyle=T(\Phi(u))(r(X))+_{\scriptscriptstyle{T}}h(r(X),D^{2}_{X}(\Phi(u))).

Therefore

T\Phi(K_{1}(Tu(X)))=K_{2}(T\Phi(Tu(X)))\Longleftrightarrow\Phi\circ D^{1}_{X}=D^{2}_{X}\circ\Phi.

Appendix B
Other notions of Dirac-Nijenhuis structures

In this section, we relate our notion of Dirac-Nijenhuis structure with other existing definitions in the literature
[
12
,
14
,
26
]
. Our main tool to compare the different definitions is the bracket on
\mathbb{T}M
defined by

(B.1)

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\left[\!\left[(r(X),r^{*}(\alpha)),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]+\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(X,\alpha)

\displaystyle=([X,Y]_{r},\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\beta-i_{r(Y)}d\alpha),

for
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
(and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{r}
defined in (
5.6
)). The properties of
(\mathbb{T}M,\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}})
will be discussed in
[
8
]
. Here, we only need two facts about the bracket (
B.1
): (1) if
L
is a Dirac structure that is compatible with
r
, then
L
is involutive with respect to
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}
; 2) the following identity holds:

(B.2)

\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}+\left[\!\left[(Y,\beta),(X,\alpha)\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}=d\langle(r(X),r^{*}(\alpha)),(Y,\beta)\rangle.

Dirac-Nijenhuis structures of contraction type

A vector-bundle morphism
N:\mathbb{T}M\rightarrow\mathbb{T}M
(over the identity on
M
) induces a bracket on sections of
\mathbb{T}M
called
the contracted bracket
:

(B.3)

\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right]_{N}:=\left[\!\left[N\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right]+\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},N\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right]-N(\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right])

for all
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{T}M)
. One can also define a Nijenhuis-type torsion by

(B.4)

T_{N}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=N(\left[\!\left[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right]_{N})-\left[\!\left[N\sigma_{1},N\sigma_{2}\right]\!\right].

To distinguish from our definition of Dirac-Nijenhuis structure, we shall say that a Dirac structure
L\subset\mathbb{T}M
and
N
as above form a
Dirac-Nijenhuis structure of contraction type
if
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{N}
restricts to skew-symmetric bracket on
\Gamma(L)
and
T_{N}|_{\Gamma(L)}=0
. Dirac-Nijenhuis structures of contraction type were defined and studied in
[
12
,
14
]
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
L
be a Dirac structure on
M
,
r\in\Omega^{1}(M,TM)
, and consider
N=(r,0)
. Then
(L,N)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure of contraction type if and only if

(i)

(r,r^{*})(L)\subseteq L
,

(ii)

\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
, for every
Y\in\mathrm{pr}_{T}(L)
,

(iii)

\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)=0
, for every
X,Y\in\mathrm{pr}_{T}(L)
.

In particular, if
(L,r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis, then
(L,N)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis of contraction type.

Proof.

It is a direct verification that, for
N=(r,0)
,

\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{N}=\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}.

Hence, it follows from (
B.2
) that
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{N}
is skew-symmetric on
L
if and only if

d\langle(r,r^{*})(\sigma_{1}),\sigma_{2}\rangle=0,\,\,\;\forall\,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(L).

We claim that this is equivalent to
(r,r^{*})(L)\subset L
. Indeed, write
\mathbb{T}M=L\oplus L^{\prime}
, where
L^{\prime}
is a lagrangian complement of
L
, and take a local basis
\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m},\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{m}\}
, where

\sigma_{i}\in\Gamma(L),\,\;\sigma^{j}\in\Gamma(L^{\prime}),\;\langle\sigma_{i},\sigma^{j}\rangle=\delta_{i}^{j},\,\,\;\;\;i,j=1,\dots,m.

By writing
(r,r^{*})(\sigma_{k})=a_{k}^{i}\sigma_{i}+b_{kj}\sigma^{j}
, one sees that
b_{kj}
must be zero since

d\langle\sigma_{k},f\sigma_{j}\rangle=0,\,\,\forall\,f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M).

Now, once
(r,r^{*})(L)\subseteq L
, it follows from (
B.1
) that

\langle\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}},(Z,\gamma)\rangle=\langle\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(X,\alpha),(Z,\gamma)\rangle,\,\,\;\;\forall\,(X,\alpha),\,(Y,\beta),\,(Z,\gamma)\in\Gamma(L).

Since
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{N}=\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}
and
L
is lagrangian, it follows that
L
is involutive if and only if
\mathbb{D}^{r}_{Y}(\Gamma(L))\subseteq\Gamma(L)
, for every
Y\in\mathrm{pr}_{T}(L)
. At last, condition (iii) is a consequence of

T_{N}((X,\alpha),(Y,\beta))=(\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y),0).

∎

In the particular case of Poisson structures, Dirac structures of contraction type lead to objects which are strictly more general than Poisson-Nijenhuis structures, in contrast with what is described in Example
3.11
.
Indeed, when
L=\mathrm{graph}(\pi)
for a Poisson structure
\pi
, Proposition
B.1
says that
(L,r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis of contraction type if and only if
\pi^{\sharp}\circ r^{*}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}
,

\pi^{\sharp}(C_{\pi}^{r}(\alpha,\beta))=0,

and
\mathcal{N}_{r}(X,Y)=0
for
X,Y\in\pi^{\sharp}(TM)
, where
C_{\pi}^{r}
is the concomitant (
3.2
).

Dirac-Nijenhuis structures of double type

A Nijenhuis operator
r:TM\to TM
makes the pair
(TM,T^{*}M)
into a Lie bialgebroid, where
T^{*}M
has the trivial Lie algebroid structure and
TM
has
[\cdot,\cdot]_{r}
as Lie bracket and anchor
r
. The corresponding double is the Courant algebroid structure
\mathbb{T}M_{r}=TM\oplus T^{*}M
with the natural pairing, anchor given by
r\circ\mathrm{pr}_{T}
, and bracket given by

(B.5)

\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{dbl}:=([X,Y]_{r},\mathcal{L}_{X}^{r}\beta-i_{Y}d^{r}\alpha),

where
\mathcal{L}^{r}
and
d^{r}
denote the Lie derivative and differential associated with the Lie algebroid
(TM,[\cdot,\cdot]_{r},r)
, respectively.

We say that a pair
(L,r)
is a
Dirac-Nijenhuis structure of double type
if
r
is a Nijenhuis operator, and
L
and
L_{(1,0)}=(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})(L)
are Dirac structures in
\mathbb{T}M
and
\mathbb{T}M_{r}
, simultaneously. This notion of Dirac-Nijenhuis structure was studied in
[
26
]
in greater generality.

Proposition B.2
.

If
(L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure and
(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L}
is injective, then
(L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure of double type.

Proof.

One can check that

i_{Y}d^{r}\alpha=i_{r(Y)}d\alpha+\langle D^{r,*}_{(\cdot)}(\alpha),Y\rangle,\;\;\;\,\,\,\mathcal{L}_{X}^{r}\beta=\mathcal{L}_{r(X)}\alpha-\langle\alpha,D^{r}_{(\cdot)}(X)\rangle.

It follows that

\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{dbl}=\left[\!\left[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}-\langle\mathbb{D}^{r}_{(\cdot)}(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\rangle,

where
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}^{r}}
is defined by (
B.1
).

Therefore, if
(L,r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure, since
\langle\mathbb{D}_{(\cdot)}^{r}(\sigma_{1}),\sigma_{2}\rangle=0
, for all
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(L)
, one has that

\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{dbl}|_{\Gamma(L)}=\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{\mathbb{D}}|_{\Gamma(L)}.

This implies that
L
is involutive under
\left[\!\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\!\right]_{dbl}
.
Also, if
(r,\mathrm{id}_{T^{*}M})|_{L}
is injective, it follows from Prop.
4.8
that
(L_{(1,0)},r)
is a Dirac-Nijenhuis structure on
\mathbb{T}M
and we can apply the same argument above to obtain that
L_{(1,0)}
is a Dirac structure on
\mathbb{T}M_{r}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We remark that, just as for Dirac structures of contraction type, Dirac structures of double type are also strictly more general than Poisson-Nijenhuis structures when
L=\mathrm{graph}(\pi)
, for a Poisson structure
\pi
. Indeed, in this case it is proved in
[
26
]
that
(L,r)
is Dirac-Nijenhuis of double type if and only if
r
is Nijenhuis,
\pi_{r}^{\sharp}=r\circ\pi^{\sharp}
, and

[\pi,\pi_{r}]=0.

In general, the condition
[\pi,\pi_{r}]=0
is weaker than the vanishing of the concomitant
C_{\pi}^{r}
(they are equivalent if
\pi
is symplectic, see
[
42
, Thm. 2.1]
).

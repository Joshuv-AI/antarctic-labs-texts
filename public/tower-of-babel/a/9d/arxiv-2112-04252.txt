Abstract




Abstract.
In this article, the authors establish a general (two-weight) boundedness criterion for
a pair of functions,
(F,f)
, on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the scale of weighted Lebesgue spaces, weighted Lorentz spaces,
(Lorentz–)Morrey spaces, and variable Lebesgue spaces.
As applications, the authors give a unified approach to prove the (two-weight) boundedness of
Calderón–Zygmund operators, Littlewood–Paley
g
-functions, Lusin area
functions, Littlewood–Paley
g^{\ast}_{\lambda}
-functions, and fractional integral operators,
in the aforementioned function spaces. Moreover, via applying the above (two-weight) boundedness criterion,
the authors further obtain the (two-weight) boundedness of
Riesz transforms, Littlewood–Paley
g
-functions, and fractional integral operators associated with
second-order divergence elliptic operators with complex bounded measurable coefficients on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

in the aforementioned function spaces.

As applications, we further obtain a boundedness criterion for a pair of functions,

Applying those (two-weight) boundedness criterion, we prove the (two-weight) boundedness of some classical

To describe some backgrounds and the main results of this article,
we first recall the definitions of weights, the Muckenhoupt weight class,
and the reverse Hölder class (see, for instance,
[
11
,
21
,
22
,

Let
E
be a measurable subset in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\omega
be a weight function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Denote by
L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{loc}}}(E)
the

set of all locally integrable functions on
E
. Furthermore, for any
f\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{loc}}(E)
,
we denote the integral
\int_{E}|f(x)|\omega(x)\,dx
simply by

\int_{E}|f|\omega\,dx
and, when
|E|\in(0,\infty)
, we often use the following notation

\fint_{E}f\,dx:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)\,dx.

Definition 1.1
.

(i)

A measurable function
\omega
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a
weight
if
\omega
is non-negative and locally integrable on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

(ii)

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
. A weight
\omega
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is said to belong to the
Muckenhoupt weight class

A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, denoted by
\omega\in A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, if, when
q\in(1,\infty)
,

[\omega]_{A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)\left(\fint_{B}\omega^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\,dx\right)^{q-1}<\infty

and, when
q=1
,

[\omega]_{A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)\left\{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{y\in B}[\omega(y)]^{-1}\right\}<\infty,

where the suprema are taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Moreover, define

A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\bigcup_{p\in[1,\infty)}A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

(iii)

Assume that
s\in(1,\infty]
. A weight
\omega
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

is said to belong to the
reverse Hölder class

RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
denoted by
\omega\in RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, if, when
s\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle[\omega]_{RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega^{s}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)^{-1}<\infty

and, when
s=\infty
,

[\omega]_{RH_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{y\in B}\omega(y)\right]\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)^{-1}<\infty,

where the suprema are taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

For the Muckenhoupt weight class and the reverse Hölder class, it is well known that

A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\bigcup_{p\in[1,\infty)}A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=\bigcup_{s\in(1,\infty]}RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

(see, for instance,
[
22
, Chapter 7]
).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
{\gamma}\in[0,1)
. Then the
fractional Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is defined by setting, for any
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{loc}}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}(f)(x):=\sup_{B\ni x}\left(|B|^{{\gamma}}\fint_{B}|f|\,dy\right),

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
containing
x
.

In particular, when
{\gamma}=0
, the fractional Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
is just the well-known
Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
; in this case,
we denote
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
simply by
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Assume that
p\in(0,\infty)
and
\omega
is a weight on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
Recall that the
weighted Lebesgue space

L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is defined by setting

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\ \text{is measurable on}\ {{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ \|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left[\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|f|^{p}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\}.

In particular, when
\omega\equiv 1
, the weighted space
L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

is denoted simply by
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, which is just the classical Lebesgue space.

In what follows, for any given
q\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
q^{\prime}

its
conjugate exponent
, namely,
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Let
\omega
be a weight on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

and
E
a measurable subset of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
Denote the integral
\int_{E}\omega(x)\,dx
simply by
\omega(E)
.

Muckenhoupt
[
29
]
proved that, for any given
p\in(1,\infty)
,
the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded on the weighted Lebesgue space

L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
if and only if
\omega\in A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. However, in the two-weight case, for any
given
p\in(1,\infty)
, the analogous two-weight
A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
condition,

(1.1)

\left[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\right]_{A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)\left(\fint_{B}v^{1-p^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty

with the supremum taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
is necessary but not sufficient for
{\mathcal{M}}
to be bounded from
L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

(see, for instance,
[
21
,
29
]
). Meanwhile, Muckenhoupt
[
29
]
showed that, for any given
p\in(1,\infty)
,
the weak type inequality

\sup_{\lambda\in(0,\infty)}\left\{\lambda\left[\int_{\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ {\mathcal{M}}(f)(x)>\lambda\}}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

with
C
being a positive constant independent of
f
, holds true for any
f\in L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

if and only if
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
.
Moreover, Sawyer
[
44
]
proved that, for any given
p\in(1,\infty)
,

{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
if and only if
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
,
where

\left[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\right]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\frac{1}{v^{1-p^{\prime}}(B)}\int_{B}\left[{\mathcal{M}}\left(v^{1-p^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{B}\right)\right]^{p}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

with the supremum taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Here and thereafter,
\mathbf{1}_{B}

denotes the
characteristic function
of
B
. It is known that, if
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
, then

[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{p}<\infty

(see, for instance,
[
44
]
). Conversely, if
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
and
v
satisfies

v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, then
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
(see, for instance,
[
33
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
35
, Corollary 1.4]
).
A defect of the condition
[\omega,v^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{S_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty
is that it involves the maximal operator
{\mathcal{M}}
itself
which hence motivates a search for sufficient conditions that are simpler and similar to
the two-weight
A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
condition (
1.1
). For this question, Neugebauer
[
32
]

showed that, for any given
p\in(1,\infty)
, if the weights
\omega
and

satisfy
[\omega^{r},v^{(1-p^{\prime})r}]_{A_{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty

for some

(which is called a
“power bump” condition

then
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. The result of Neugebauer
[
32

was improved by Pérez
[
33
]
via proving that the “power bump” condition can be replaced
by a weak “Orlicz bump” condition. To describe the result of Pérez
[
33
]
, we first
recall some necessary definitions and notation.

Definition 1.3
.

(i)

A function
\Phi:\ [0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function
if
\Phi
is continuous, convex,
strictly increasing,
\Phi(0)=0
, and
\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}\to\infty
as

\Phi
is

if there exists a positive constant

such that, for any

\Phi(2t)\leq C\Phi(t)
.

(ii)

Let
\Phi
be a Young function and
B
a ball in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. For any
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{loc}}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
the
normalized Luxembourg norm

\|f\|_{\Phi,\,B}
of

on
B
is defined by setting

\|f\|_{\Phi,\,B}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\ \fint_{B}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
B
be a ball in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, and
\Phi(t):=t^{p}
for any

\Phi

\|f\|_{\Phi,\,B}=\left(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=:\|f\|_{p,\,B}.

Pérez
[

showed that, for any given

(1.2)

\int_{c}^{\infty}\left[\frac{t^{p^{\prime}}}{\Phi(t)}\right]^{p-1}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty

(1.3)

\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)\left\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\right\|_{\Phi,\,B}^{p}<\infty

to

A typical example of

Definition 1.6
.

Assume that
q\in(0,\infty)
,
t\in(0,\infty]
, and
\omega

{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

The

L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is defined by setting

L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\ \text{is measurable on}\ {{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ \|f\|_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty\right\},

where, when

\|f\|_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\left\{q\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\lambda^{q}\omega\left(\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ |f(x)|>\lambda\right\}\right)\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right\}^{\frac{1}{t}},

and

\|f\|_{L^{q,\,\infty}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{\lambda\in(0,\infty)}\left\{\lambda\left[\omega\left(\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ |f(x)|>\lambda\}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}.

It is easy to see that, when

L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=L^{q}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
If
\omega\equiv 1
, then we write
L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=L^{q,\,t}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Remark 1.7
.

Let

,

,

, and

{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

\||f|^{s}\|_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\|f\|^{s}_{L^{qs,\,ts}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

(see, for instance,
[

In what follows, for any

and

, we always let

B(x,r):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ |y-x|<r\}

Definition 1.8
.

Let
q\in(0,\infty)
,
t\in(0,\infty]
, and
\theta\in[0,n]
.
The

L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\left\{f\ \text{is measurable on}\ {{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ \|f\|_{L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty\right\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,r\in(0,\infty)}\left\{r^{\frac{\theta-n}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q,\,t}(B(x,r))}\right\}:=\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,r\in(0,\infty)}\left\{r^{\frac{\theta-n}{q}}\|f\mathbf{1}_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{q,\,t}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\right\}

2.2

In this subsection, we establish the (two-weight) boundedness of Riesz transforms, Littlewood–Paley
g
-functions,
and fractional integral operators, associated with second-order divergence elliptic operators with complex
bounded measurable coefficients on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, in the scale of weighted Lebesgue spaces, weighted Lorentz spaces,
(Lorentz–)Morrey spaces, and variable Lebesgue spaces. We begin with recalling the
second-order divergence elliptic operators with complex
bounded measurable coefficients on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

For any given
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
A(x):=\{a_{ij}(x)\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
denote
an
n\times n
matrix with complex-valued, bounded, measurable entries.
Then
A
is said to satisfy the
uniform ellipticity condition

if there exists a positive constant
\mu_{0}\in(0,1]
such that,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\xi,\,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\mu_{0}|\xi|^{2}\leq\Re(A(x)\xi\cdot\overline{\xi})\quad\ \text{and}\quad\ |A(x)\xi\cdot\overline{\zeta}|\leq\mu_{0}^{-1}|\xi||\zeta|,

where
\Re(A(x)\xi\cdot\overline{\xi})
denotes the
real part
of
A(x)\xi\cdot\xi

and
\overline{\xi}
denotes the
conjugate vector
of
\xi
.
Denote by
L
the
maximal-accretive operator
on
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with largest domain

D(L)\subset W^{1,2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
such that, for any
f\in D(L)
and
g\in W^{1,2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,

\langle Lf,g\rangle=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla f(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla g(x)}\,dx,

where
W^{1,2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
denotes the classical
Sobolev space
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\nabla f
the
distributional
gradient
of
f
. In this sense, for any
f\in D(L)
, we write

(2.3)

Lf:=-{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{div}}}(A\nabla f).

It is well known that
-L
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
and
L

has a bounded holomorphic functional calculus in
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see, for instance,
[
3
]
).
Moreover, the Riesz transform
\nabla L^{-1/2}
,
associated with the operator
L
, is defined by setting, for any
f\in L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,

\nabla L^{-1/2}(f):=\frac{1}{\pi^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla e^{-tL}(f)\frac{dt}{t^{1/2}}.

Let
(p_{-}(L),\,p_{+}(L))
be the
interior of the maximal interval of exponents

p\in[1,\,\infty]
for which the semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
is bounded on
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
Furthermore, denote by
(q_{-}(L),\,q_{+}(L))
the
interior of
the maximal interval of exponents

p\in[1,\,\infty]
for which the family of operators,
\{\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
, is bounded on
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Remark 2.13
.

Let the operator
L

To prove Theorem

by using Theorem
1.5
, we need the following
two-weight boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
{\mathcal{M}}
in the scale
of weighed Lebesgue spaces and weighted Lorentz spaces.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\Phi
be a doubling Young function
satisfying

\int_{c}^{\infty}\left[\frac{t^{p^{\prime}}}{\Phi(t)}\right]^{p-1}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty

for some constant

. Assume that the weights
\omega
and
v
satisfy
that

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

(i)

Then
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

(ii)

Let
r\in(0,\infty]
. Assume further that
\omega\in A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, there exists a
p_{0}\in(1,p)

such that
\int_{c}^{\infty}[\frac{t^{p_{0}^{\prime}}}{\Phi(t)}]^{p_{0}-1}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty
for some constant

,
and

\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right)\left\|v^{-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\right\|^{p_{0}}_{\Phi,\,B}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p,\,r}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

to
L^{p,\,r}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Proof.

The conclusion of (i) is well known (see, for instance,
[
11
, Theorem 5.14]
and
[
33
, Theorem 1.2]
).
Here we only give the proof of (ii).

By
\omega\in A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and Lemma
3.1
(i), we conclude that there exists an
s\in(1,\infty)
such that

\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega^{s}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left\|v^{-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\right\|^{p_{0}}_{\Phi,\,B}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. From this and
[
11
, Theorem 6.13]
, we deduce that
there exist a weight
u\in A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for any
q\in(p_{0},\infty)
and positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

such that
c_{1}\omega\leq u\leq c_{2}v
. By the one-weight boundedness of
{\mathcal{M}}
(see, for instance,
[
11
,
22
]
)
and the assumption that

for any

, we know that

{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded on both
L^{p-{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{u}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
L^{p+{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{u}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, where
{\varepsilon}_{0}\in(0,p-p_{0})

is a constant, which, combined with
c_{1}\omega\leq u\leq c_{2}v
, further implies that

{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p-{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p-{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, and
from
L^{p+{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p+{\varepsilon}_{0}}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
From this and the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem of sublinear operators
in the scale of Lorentz spaces (see, for instance,
[
22
, Theorem 1.4.19]
),
it follows that
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from
L^{p,\,r}_{v}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p,\,r}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
This finishes the proof of (ii) and hence of Lemma
3.5
.

\omega_{x}(y):=\min\{|x-y|^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}},r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}\}

From Lemma

Thus, applying the conclusion of (ii) in the special case that

and
\Phi(t):=t^{(\frac{q}{p_{2}})^{\prime}s_{0}}

,
we find that, for any

,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{q,\,t}(B(x,r))}=r^{\frac{n-\theta+\rho_{0}}{q}}\|F\|_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega_{x}}(B(x,r))}\lesssim r^{\frac{n-\theta+\rho_{0}}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega_{x}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

For any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, let

.
Then, by the fact that
\omega_{x}\leq r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, we know that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\omega_{x}(E_{\lambda})=\int_{E_{\lambda}}\omega_{x}\,dy\leq\int_{0}^{r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\left|E_{\lambda}\cap B\left(x,\alpha^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\right)\right|\,d\alpha.

To finish the proof of (iii), we consider the following three cases
on the index

.

Case 1.

. In this case, from the definition of
the space

3.18
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q,\,\infty}_{\omega_{x}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda\in(0,\infty)}\left\{\lambda\left[\omega_{x}(E_{\lambda})\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}\leq\sup_{\lambda\in(0,\infty)}\left\{\lambda\left[\int_{0}^{r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\left|E_{\lambda}\cap B\left(x,\alpha^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\right)\right|\,d\alpha\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}

Case 2.

) and the Minkowski inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{t}_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega_{x}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\lambda^{q}\int_{E_{\lambda}}\omega_{x}\,dy\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq q\left\{\int_{0}^{r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\lambda^{q}\left|E_{\lambda}\cap B\left(x,\alpha^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\right)\right|\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right]^{\frac{q}{t}}\,d\alpha\right\}^{\frac{t}{q}}

which implies that

.
From this and the arbitrariness of

and

\|F\|_{L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q,\,t;\,\theta}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

Case 3

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\left|E_{\lambda}\cap B\left(x,\alpha^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\right)\right|\,d\alpha\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}

\displaystyle\|f\|^{t}_{L^{q,\,t}_{\omega_{x}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\lambda^{q}\int_{E_{\lambda}}\omega_{x}\,dy\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{t}\int_{0}^{2r^{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\left[\alpha\left|E_{\lambda}\cap B\left(x,\alpha^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho_{0}}}\right)\right|\right]^{\frac{t}{q}}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

From this and (

), it follows that

of the case

To show Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a Calderón–Zygmund operator as in Definition

is bounded from

to

[

,
50

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a bounded operator from

to

,
\nu\in(0,1)

of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}

is well known (see, for instance,
[
20

and
[
22
, p. 100]

We now prove Theorem
2.2
via using Theorem
1.11
, and Lemmas

and
4.2

In what follows, let

on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with compact support
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let

and

. For any ball

of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with

and

. Then

By the assumption

(i), we find that there exists an

From Lemmas

and

, we deduce that, for any

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}|F_{B}|^{\nu}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\lesssim\fint_{8B}|f|\,dx\lesssim{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1}).

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}}(f-f\mathbf{1}_{8B})\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(8B)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\|T(f\mathbf{1}_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(8B)})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{y\in B}\left\{\left|T(f\mathbf{1}_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(8B)})(y)\right|\right\}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\fint_{B}|T(f\mathbf{1}_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(8B)})|^{\nu}\,dy\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\sim\left[\fint_{B}|R_{B}|^{\nu}\,dy\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1}).

4.2
) and (

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|R_{B}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|R_{B}|^{\nu}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|F|^{\nu}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+\left(\fint_{B}|F_{B}|^{\nu}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|F|^{\nu}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1})\lesssim\left[{\mathcal{M}}(|F|^{\nu})(x_{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}(f)(x_{1}).

,

. For any ball

Lemma 4.4

\displaystyle\leq\nu\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{\nu-1}\min\left\{|E|,\,\left[\frac{C}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\right]^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}\right\}d\lambda

(4.21)

\displaystyle\left\|R_{B}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{y\in B}\left\{\left|I_{\alpha}(f\mathbf{1}_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(8B)})(y)\right|\right\}+{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha/n}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\fint_{B}|I_{\alpha}(f)|^{\nu}\,dz\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+\left[\fint_{B}|I_{\alpha}(f\mathbf{1}_{8B})|^{\nu}\,dz\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha/n}(f)(x_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B}|F|^{\nu}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha/n}(f)(x_{1})\lesssim{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha/n}(f)(x_{1})+\left[{\mathcal{M}}(|F|^{\nu})(x_{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}.

Then, by (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), we conclude that (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) hold true
for
p_{1}:=\nu,\,p_{2}:=1
,
p_{3}:=\infty
, and
\epsilon:=0
. Thus, by Theorem
1.12
, we then complete
the proof of Theorem
2.11
.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
L
be as in (
2.3
) and
p_{0},\,q_{0}\in(q_{-}(L),q_{+}(L))
with
p_{0}\leq q_{0}
. Assume that
B:=B(x_{B},r_{B})

is a ball of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with
x_{B}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
r_{B}\in(0,\infty)
. Then, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\rm c}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{B}\left|\nabla L^{-1/2}\left(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}L}\right)^{m}(f)\right|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{1}(j)\left(\fint_{2^{j+1}B}|f|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}},

and, for any
k\in\{1,\,\ldots,\,m\}
and
f\in L^{p_{0}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
satisfying
\nabla f\in L^{p_{0}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{B}\left|\nabla e^{-kr_{B}^{2}L}(f)\right|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{2}(j)\left(\fint_{2^{j+1}B}|\nabla f|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}},

where, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
g_{1}(j):=C_{(m)}2^{j\theta}4^{-mj}
and
g_{2}(j):=C_{(m)}2^{j}(\sum_{\ell=j}^{\infty}2^{\ell\theta}e^{-\alpha 4^{\ell}})

for some
\theta,\,\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Here
C_{(m)}
is a positive constant depending only on
m
.

Now, we prove Theorem
2.14
via using Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
1.11
.

Proof of Theorem
2.14
.

From the assumption
v^{1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)})^{\prime}}\in A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, it follows that there exists an
r_{0}\in(1,\infty)

such that
v^{1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)})^{\prime}}\in RH_{r_{0}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Let
p_{0}\in(q_{-}(L),q_{+}(L))
be such that

(1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)})^{\prime})r_{0}=1-(\frac{q}{p_{0}})^{\prime}
. This, combined with the assumption (
2.4
),
further implies that, for any ball
B
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{p_{0}}{q}}\left[\fint_{B}v^{1-(\frac{q}{p_{0}})^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{1/(\frac{q}{p_{0}})^{\prime}}\right\}^{\frac{q_{-}{(L)}}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left[\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{q_{-}{(L)}}{q}}\left[\fint_{B}v^{[1-(\frac{q}{q_{(L)}})^{\prime}]r_{0}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{r_{0}}\frac{1}{(\frac{q}{q_{-}{(L)}})^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left[\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right]^{\frac{q_{-}{(L)}}{q}}\left[\fint_{B}v^{1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}{(L)}})^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{(\frac{q}{q_{-}{(L)}})^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left[\omega,v^{1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)})^{\prime}}\right]_{A_{\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}^{\frac{q_{-}{(L)}}{q}}.

By this, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left[\omega,v^{1-(\frac{q}{p_{0}})^{\prime}}\right]_{A_{\frac{q}{p_{0}}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\sup_{B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\fint_{B}\omega\,dx\right]\left[\fint_{B}v^{1-(\frac{q}{p_{0}})^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}-1}\lesssim\left[\omega,v^{1-(\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)})^{\prime}}\right]_{A_{\frac{q}{q_{-}(L)}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}<\infty.

Moreover, from the assumption
\omega\in RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
s>(\frac{q_{+}(L)}{q})^{\prime}
, we deduce that there exists a
q_{0}\in(q,q_{+}(L))

such that
\omega\in RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
s>(\frac{q_{0}}{q})^{\prime}
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be large enough such that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{1}(j)\lesssim 1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{2}(j)\lesssim 1
,
where, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
g_{1}(j)
and
g_{2}(j)
are as in Lemma
5.1
. Assume that
B:=B(x_{B},r_{B})
,
with
x_{B}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
r_{B}\in(0,\infty)
, is a ball of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\rm c}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
Let
F:=\nabla L^{-1/2}(f)
,
F_{B}:=\nabla L^{-1/2}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}}L)^{m}(f)
,
and
R_{B}:=\nabla L^{-1/2}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}}L)^{m}](f)
. Then
|F|\leq|F_{B}|+|R_{B}|
on
B
. By Lemma
5.1
,
we find that, for any
x_{1},\,x_{2}\in B
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}|F_{B}|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{1}(j)\left(\fint_{2^{j+1}B}|f|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\lesssim\left[{\mathcal{M}}(|f|^{p_{0}})(x_{1})\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}|R_{B}|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left[\fint_{B}\left|\nabla e^{-kr_{B}^{2}L}L^{-1/2}(f)\right|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{2}(j)\left[\fint_{2^{j+1}B}|\nabla L^{-1/2}(f)|^{p_{0}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{2}(j)\left[{\mathcal{M}}(|\nabla L^{-1/2}(f)|^{p_{0}})(x_{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\lesssim\left[{\mathcal{M}}(|F|^{p_{0}})(x_{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

From (
5.1

), it follows that (
1.4
) and (

1.11

2.14

Moreover, by the assumption
\omega\in RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
s>(\frac{q_{+}(L)}{q})^{\prime}
, we know that there exists a
q_{0}\in(q,q_{+}(L))

\omega\in RH_{s}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

m\in{\mathbb{N}}

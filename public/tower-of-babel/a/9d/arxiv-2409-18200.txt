Abstract
. In this paper we consider a multidimensional random walk killed on leaving a right circular cone with a distribution of increments belonging to the normal domain of attraction of an α \alpha -stable and rotationally-invariant law with α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) ∖ { 1 } \alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\} . Based on [ 7 ] describing the tail behaviour of the exit time of α \alpha -stable process from a cone and using some properties of Martin kernel of the isotropic α \alpha -stable process, in this paper we construct a positive harmonic function of the discrete time random walk under consideration. Then we find the asymptotic tail of the distribution of the exit time of this random walk from the cone. We also prove the corresponding conditional functional limit theorem.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

It is known that fluctuation theory serves as a powerful machinery to analyse and understand the asymptotic behaviour of one-dimensional random walks. Accompanied by the distinguished Doob
h
-transform method this approach attracted much of attention over few last decades and allowed one to study random walls conditioned to stay positive. Most relevant for the current work are the papers concerned with invariance principles, such as
[
27
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
9
]
. As the positive half-line is a prototype of a
cone
in one dimension, it was a natural further research task to focus on random walks conditioned to stay in a cone, or other unbounded domains in higher dimensions. Since fluctuation identities are not applicable in the multidimensional setting, there was need for a novel approach, which was recently developed in
[
13
]
for random walks having finite second (or higher) moments. In the present work we aim to understand the asymptotic behaviour of random walks that have infinite second moments and are constrained to stay in a circular cone.

More precisely,
we consider a random walk
\{S(n):\,n\geq 1\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle S(n)=X(1)+\ldots+X(n),

where
X(1),X(2),\ldots
are independent copies of a generic random vector
X

with
the absolutely continuous law with the density
p_{X}(y)
.
We assume that
X
belongs to the normal domain of attraction of
an
\alpha
-stable and rotationally-invariant law
p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
.
That is, the function
p_{Z}(y)
is density of
Z(1)
for the isotropic
\alpha
-stable process
\{Z(t):t\geq 0\}
.

The assumption that
X
belongs to the normal domain of attraction means that a properly rescaled walk converges in distribution towards the process
Z
, that is,

(1.2)

\frac{S(nt)}{n^{1/\alpha}}\Rightarrow Z(t).

In particular, since there is no centering in relation (
1.2
), we have to assume that
\mathbb{E}X(1)=\mathbb{E}Z(1)=0
in the case when
\alpha>1
.
We additionally assume that for some
\nu>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right|\leq C\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{d+\alpha+\nu}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For an angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
we denote by
K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\measuredangle{(x,e_{d})}<\theta\}
the
right circular cone
of aperture
\theta
, where
e_{d}=(0,0\ldots,0,1)
.
Let

(1.4)

\displaystyle\tau_{x}=\inf\{n\geq 1:x+S(n)\notin K\}

be the first exit time from a cone
K
of the random walk
x+S(n)
, which starts at
x\in K
.

We investigate the asymptotic behaviour of

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)

and the random walk conditioned to stay in cone
K
.
There is a considerable amount of research on the asymptotic behaviour of random walks with

finite second moments
in cones and other Lipszhitz domains
which we briefly revise in Section
1.3
below.
To the best of our knowledge, the present article is the first work where the
asymptotic behaviour of multidimensional random walks
with infinite variance in unbounded domains is analysed.
The methodology that we have developed to treat the random walks in the domain of attraction
of
\alpha
-stable processes is quite general and we
hope that it will be applicable in a number of other situations.
One of the difficulties of the
\alpha
-stable case is that
one cannot assume existence of additional moments as it is usually done in the finite variance case.
Instead, we enforce the local (closeness) assumption displayed at (
1.3
), cf. also Subsection
1.2
.

A key object in our investigations is the so-called Martin kernel with pole at infinity of the cone
K
related to the isotropic
\alpha
-stable process
Z(t)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Its existence, uniqueness and main properties were investigated in the seminal paper
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
where among other things it was proved that there exists a unique function
M\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
such that
M\equiv 0
on
K^{c}
and, for any open and bounded set
B\subset K
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle M(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[M(Z(\tau_{B}^{Z}))],

where
\tau_{B}^{Z}
is the first exit time from
B
of the
\alpha
-stable process
Z(t)
.
Identity (
1.5
) means that
M
is
\alpha
-harmonic in
K
.
The function
M
is locally bounded and homogeneous of order
\beta=\beta(K,\alpha)\geq 0
, that is,

(1.6)

\displaystyle M(x)=|x|^{\beta}M(x/|x|),\quad x\neq 0.

The number
\beta
is called
the index of the cone

K
. We have
\beta=0
if
K^{c}
is a polar set for
Z(t)
and
0<\beta<\alpha
otherwise.

We shall first formulate the two main results of the article and only later we will discuss their connections with other existing related results that appeared in the literature.
Our first result concerns the asymptotic behaviour of the tails of the exit time
\tau_{x}
.
We will write
h(n)\sim g(n)
, as
n\to\infty
, if
\lim_{n\to\infty}h(n)/g(n)=1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the generic increment
X
of the random walk
S(n)

has an absolutely continuous law with the density belonging to the domain of attraction of
an
\alpha
-stable and rotationally-invariant law for
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
.
Assume also that (
1.3
) holds true
and that
\mathbb{E}X=0
if
\alpha>1
.
Then
the function

(1.7)

V(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M(x+S(n));\tau_{x}>n]

is well-defined and is harmonic
for the walk
S(n)
killed at exiting from the circular cone
K
, that is,

V(x)=\mathbb{E}[V(x+S(1));\tau_{x}>1],\quad x\in K.

Furthermore, for every
x\in K
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)\sim\varkappa\frac{V(x)}{n^{\beta/\alpha}},\quad\mathrm{as}\ n\to\infty,

for some constant
\varkappa>0
.

In fact, the constant
\varkappa
from (
1.8
) is the same as the constant given in equation (3.3) of
[
7
]
and it is determined in terms of the Kelvin transform of the Martin kernel
M
.
To be more precise, we recall the definition of
the heat kernel of the process
Z(t)
killed on leaving cone
K
which is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle p_{K}(t,x,y)=p_{Z}(t,x,y)-\mathbb{E}\big[p_{Z}(t-\tau_{K},Z(\tau_{K}),y);\,\tau_{K}^{Z}<t\big],\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0,

where
\tau_{K}^{Z}
is the first exit time of the process
Z(t)
from
K
.
The related killed Green function is defined as

(1.10)

\displaystyle G_{K}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{K}(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t.

The constant
\varkappa
is then computed as follows

\displaystyle\varkappa=\left(\lim_{K\ni x\to 0}\frac{G_{K}(x,e_{d})}{M(x)}\right)\int_{K}\int_{K}|y|^{\alpha-d}M(y/|y|^{2})p_{K}(1,y,z)\nu_{K}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
e_{d}=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;
\nu_{K}(z)=\int_{K^{c}}\nu(z-y)\,\mathrm{d}y,
and
\nu(y)
stays for the density of the Lévy jump measure of the process
Z(t)
.

The second main result concerns the conditional
functional limit theorem for the random walk conditioned to stay in cone
K
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, the following stochastic process

\left(\frac{x+S(nt)}{n^{1/\alpha}}\,\Big|\,\tau_{x}>n\right)_{t\in[0,1]}

converges weakly in the Skorohod space
D[0,1]
to a process
me_{K}(t)
which we shall call the meander of the
\alpha
-stable process
Z(t)
in the cone K.

The existence of the limiting process is ’almost known’ in the literature. More precisely, one knows from
[
30
, Theorem 3.3]
that the Doob
h
-transform of
Z
killed at leaving
K
has a weak limit as the starting point
x
converges to zero. Combining this with the entrance law obtained in
[
7
]
, one can easily obtain the existence of the meander. We perform the needed calculations in Subsection
5.1
preceding the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Comments on the assumptions and the method

Assumption (
1.3
) on the closeness between the densities should be understood as a control of the rate of convergence in (
1.2
). In the case of random walks with finite variance one can quantify the rate of convergence towards the Brownian motion by assuming finiteness of higher moments. Since this is not possible in the case
\alpha<2
, we impose (
1.3
). Assuming some closeness between distributions of
X
and
Z(1)
is rather standard in the area of asymptotically stable random
walks, see e.g. the book by Christoph and Wolf
[
10
]
.
Assumptions about the local behaviour
of densities are usually imposed
while analysing the behaviour of
the Green function in the infinite variance case, see for example

[
15
, Theorem 4]
.
In fact, if these assumptions are not in place, the behaviour of the Green function
can be quite irregular as demonstrated
in
[
39
]
.
Since our method is based on analysis of harmonic functions and
the Green function of the process
Z
, we had to assume that densities are close to each other.
It seems reasonable to expect that the geometric assumption that
the cone is right circular can be relaxed.
We have imposed this assumption since the estimates for the killed Green function of the
\alpha
-stable processes and for the Martin kernel
M

that are currently available in the literature
are proved only for such cones.
It is thus justified to forebode that these estimates should hold in more general cones. We decided, however, not to consider this question in the present paper to keep it less technical
and to concentrate on the discrete-time framework.

Our strategy stems from the asymptotics (
1.8
),
which factorises in the product of
V(x)
and
n^{-\beta/\alpha}
.
We thus find first positive harmonic function

V(x)
and then use it to study the tails of exit times and conditional
limit theorems.
Similar strategy
was used in
[
16
]
, where firstly harmonic function
was found differently using recursive estimates and repulsion from boundaries
and secondly repulsion from boundaries with KMT (Komlós-Major-Tusnády)
coupling
were used to transfers asymptotic results for Brownian motion to random walks.

For random walks with bounded increments in
[
37
]
firstly a
related approach
was used to find suitable sub/superharmonic functions and then
secondly machinery of Dirichlet forms was used
together with these functions
to obtain estimates for the tail probabilities
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)
.

We give now a high level description of the methodology used in the paper.
In the first step we prove existence of harmonic function.
For that we start off with bounding the error function

f(y)=\mathbb{E}[M(y+X)]-M(y)

with

\frac{\Lambda(x)}{(1+\delta(x))^{\varepsilon/2}}
, where
\delta(x):={\rm dist}(x,\partial K)
and

\displaystyle\Lambda(x)=\frac{M(x)}{(1+\delta(x))^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}.

We then introduce an error compensator

(1.11)

\displaystyle U_{\Lambda}(x)=\int_{K}G_{K}(x,y)\Lambda(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where the killed Green function
G_{K}(x,y)
is given in (
1.10
).
This allows us to construct the following superharmonic function

W(x)=M(x)+U_{\Lambda}(x).

As a result, we
prove that for sufficiently large
R
and some
x_{0}\in K
,

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{(c)}

\displaystyle=W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n)){\rm 1}\{\tau_{x}>n\}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+c\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(k)){\rm 1}\{\tau_{x}>k\},

is a supermartingale.
Since the error function
f
is bounded from above by
\Lambda
,
the existence of the harmonic function
V(x)
is a consequence of the martinagle property of

L_{n}=M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n\wedge\tau_{x}))-\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k)),\quad n\geq 0,

the optional stopping theorem and the dominated convergence theorem,
together with bounds that follow from the supermartingale
Y_{n}^{(c)}
.

In the second step we then proceed to study of tails and conditional limit theorems.
Here, we first obtain sharp upper bound for
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)

and then probabilities of large deviations by using recursive
bounds. These bounds together with functional central limit theorems
deliver the final results.

In our proof the main
difficulty is in proving the supermartingale property of
Y_{n}^{(c)}
.
To do so we have to show completely new estimates in the context
of the
\alpha
-stable processes and random walks with increments
distribution belonging to the
\alpha
-stable law with
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
.
In particular, in Lemma
2.5
we find the estimates of the error function
f

and in Lemma
2.6
we identify the
upper bound of the killed Green function
G_{K}(x,y)
.

The main novelty and difficulty are in showing the estimates related
to
U_{\Lambda}(x)
given in (
1.11
). In particular, a completely new approach is required in finding
the proper upper bound for the gradient of
U_{\Lambda}(x)
(see Lemma
3.2
),
for
|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)|

if
0<\alpha<1
(see Lemma
3.3
)
and for
|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\cdot y|

if
1<\alpha<2
(see Lemma
3.4
).

We also proved some other facts that are of own interest, like the existence of the meander
m_{K}(t)
(see Theorem
1.2
).

1.3.
Literature overview

The asymptotic behaviour of random walks with finite variance
in cones has been
extensively studied in the literature.
The most general result was obtained by
Denisov and Wachtel
[
16
]

who proved counterparts of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

under additional moment condition
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{2}\log(1+|X|)]<\infty
which evidently does not fit the scope of the present article.
See also
[
13
,
14
]
for similar result but proved using different method under stronger moment conditions.
In these studies an important role
was played by the KMT coupling of Brownian motion
and random walks with finite variance.
This tool is not available in the infinite-variance case, which required a new approach.
Markov chains in cones have been studied recently in
[
17
]
,
where also Functional Central Limit Theorem was used instead of the KMT coupling.
Raschel and Tarrago in
[
35
]
also studied the asymptotic behaviour of zero-drift random walks
confined to multidimensional convex cones but they focused on the case when the endpoint is close to the boundary and
they derived a local limit theorem in the fluctuation regime.
Invariance principle was obtained in
[
22
]
, which corresponds to our Theorem
1.2
, but in this case a Brownian meander is appearing in the limit.
See also
[
21
]
for the asymptotic behaviour of the Green function
G(x,y)
of the killed random walk on exiting from the cone where
y
tends to infinity in different ways.

The research question posed in this paper has gained lots of attention in various related contexts.
The asymptotic tail of the distribution of the exit time from a cone has been studied first for Brownian motion, see
[
11
,
4
]
.
For one dimensional Lévy processes and half-line it has been studied in
[
31
,
29
]
. For the result concerning one-dimensional
self-similar processes and half-line see
[
26
]
.
The multivariate case of
\alpha
-stable processes has been in analysed in
[
7
,
30
]
.
One can derive similar result under additional assumption that multidimensional stochastic process has a skew-product structure; see
[
33
]
for details.

Strongly related with the results concerning the continuous-time stochastic processes are discrete-time counterparts.
For more general cones though and essentially for random
walks with increments having a finite support, Varopoulos in
[
37
]
gave lower and
upper bounds for the probability
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)
.
Behaviour of the Green function of asymptotically stable random walk
on a half-space has been recently considered in
[
15
]
.

Weak convergence of a normalized two-dimensional random walk conditioned to stay in a cone
to the analogous conditional distribution of a two-dimensional Wiener process was established in
[
36
]
.
In
[
28
]
a complete representation of the Martin boundary of killed random walks on the quadrant
\mathbb{N}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
was derived.

Random walks with drift have been studied in
[
20
,
19
,
23
]
.
Different approach is taken in
[
24
]
, where for a random walk having integrable increments with drift in the interior of the closed cone, the asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)
is derived. It is in the form
of the sum of survival probability and a certain sequence
\rho^{n}B_{n}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
and
B_{n}\to 0
satisfying
B_{n}^{1/n}\to 1
as
n\to+\infty
.

The Weyl chamber of the form
K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{d}\}
is a specific cone that
attracted a lot of attention.
In this case there are many papers related to the probability
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)
; see for example
[
12
,
25
,
5
,
34
]

and references therein.

Finally, the probability
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)
can determine so-called persistency which corresponds to identifying
the rate of decay of this probability; see
[
12
,
13
,
38
,
1
,
2
]
for details and other related references.

1.4.
Organisation of the paper

The paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
we collect and prove the key estimates of the Martin kernel
M(x)
, of the so-called error function
f(x)=\mathbb{E}M(x+X)-M(x)
, and of
the killed Green function denoted by
G(x,y)
. In Section
3
we construct the harmonic function
V(x)
defined in (
1.7
).
Using this result, in Section
4
, we find the asymptotic tail of the distribution of
\tau_{x}
, cf. Theorem
1.1
.
In the closing Section
5
we display the proof of the conditional
functional limit theorem stated in Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Estimates of the Martin kernel

We recall that by (
1.5
) the function
M
is harmonic in the circular cone
K
for the isotropic
\alpha
-stable process
Z(t)
killed on the exiting from
K
.
Throughout this paper we assume that
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}

and write
C
or
c
with some possible indexes to denote some constants.
By
[
32
, Lemma 3.3]
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle M(x)\leq C|x|^{\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2},\quad x\in K,

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle M(x)\geq c|x|^{\beta-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2},\quad x\in K,

where

\delta(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial K).

Hence, there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle M(x)\leq C|x|^{\beta},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

From (
2.3
) we can immediately conlcude the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

It holds

(2.4)

\displaystyle|M(x+y)-M(x)|\leq C\left(|x|\vee|y|\right)^{\beta},\quad x,x+y\in K.

We will also need a slightly different estimate.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C
such that

M(x+y)\leq C\left(M(x)+|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}|y|^{\alpha/2}\right),\quad x,x+y\in K,\ |y|\leq|x|.

Proof.

Using (
2.1
) and recalling that

\beta-\alpha/2<\alpha/2<1
, we have

\displaystyle M(x+y)

\displaystyle\leq C|x+y|^{\beta-\alpha/2}\delta(x+y)^{\alpha/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}+|y|^{\beta-\alpha/2}\right)\left(\delta(x)^{\alpha/2}+|y|^{\alpha/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2C|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}\left(\delta(x)^{\alpha/2}+|y|^{\alpha/2}\right).

Applying now (
2.2
) we get the desired estimate.
∎

The following estimate for the gradient of
M
follows directly from
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla M(x)|\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)},\quad x\in K.

We need another new estimate related to
M(x)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
0<\eta<1
and any
x\in K
with
\delta(x)>1
and
|y|\leq\delta(x)^{1-\eta}
it holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle|M(x+y)-M(x)-\nabla M(x)\cdot y|\leq C\,|y|^{2}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{2}}.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

We first find an estimate for the second derivative of
M
.
We make use of the following representation (see e.g.
[
6
]
) for the function
M
.
For any
z_{0}\in K
and any
r>0
such that
B_{r}(z_{0})\subset K
, it holds

(2.7)

\displaystyle M(z)=\int_{B_{r}(z_{0})^{c}}P_{r}(z-z_{0},w-z_{0})M(w)\,\mathrm{d}w,\quad z\in B_{r}(z_{0}),

where by
P_{r}(\theta,w)
we denote the Poisson kernel of the centred ball
B_{r}(0)
given by

\displaystyle P_{r}(\theta,w)=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}{\pi^{d/2+1}}\left(\frac{r^{2}-|\theta|^{2}}{|w|^{2}-r^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\!\!|w-\theta|^{d},\quad\mathrm{for}\ |\theta|<r\ \mathrm{and}\ |w|>r.

Routine calculations yield

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta_{i}}P_{r}(\theta,w)=P_{r}(\theta,w)F_{i}(\theta,w),\quad\mathrm{where}\ F_{i}(\theta,w)=\frac{-\alpha\theta_{i}}{r^{2}-|\theta|^{2}}+d\frac{w_{i}-\theta_{i}}{|w-\theta|^{2}}.

From this we easily derive formulas for the second derivatives:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&D^{2}_{\theta_{i}\theta_{i}}P_{r}(\theta,w)\\
&=P_{r}(\theta,w)\left\{F^{2}_{i}(\theta,w)-\frac{\alpha(r^{2}-|\theta|^{2})+2\alpha\theta_{i}^{2}}{(r^{2}-|\theta|^{2})^{2}}+d\,\frac{2(\theta_{i}-w_{i})^{2}-|w-\theta|^{2}}{|w-\theta|^{4}}\right\}\end{split}

and

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&D^{2}_{\theta_{i}\theta_{j}}P_{r}(\theta,w)\\
&=P_{r}(\theta,w)\left\{F_{i}(\theta,w)F_{j}(\theta,w)-\frac{2\alpha\theta_{i}\theta_{j}}{(r^{2}-|\theta|^{2})^{2}}-\frac{2d(w_{i}-\theta_{i})(\theta_{i}-w_{i})}{|w-\theta|^{4}}\right\}.\end{split}

Combining identity (
2.7
) with equation (
2.8
) implies that for any
z\in K
such that
B_{r}(z)\subset K
we have

\displaystyle D^{2}_{z_{i}z_{i}}M(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(z)^{c}}M(w)P_{r}(0,w-z)\left\{F_{i}^{2}(0,w-z)-\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}}+\frac{2d(w_{i}-z_{i})^{2}-|w-z|^{2}}{|w-z|^{4}}\right\}\mathrm{d}w.

Further, we easily verify that
|F_{i}(0,w-z)|\leq d/|w-x|
and whence, for any
z\in K
and any
r>0
such that
B_{r}(z)\subset K
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle|D^{2}_{z_{i}z_{i}}M(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{M(z)}{r^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\to\delta(z)}}{{\longrightarrow}}C\frac{M(z)}{\delta^{2}(z)},\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

With the aid of (
2.9
) we can similarly show that for any
z\in K
and any
r>0
such that
B_{r}(z)\subset K
,

(2.11)

) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\frac{M(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\frac{\delta(y)^{\alpha/2}|y|^{\beta-\alpha/2}}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\leq C_{3}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}r^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}r\leq C_{4}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\varepsilon/2}}

and the proof is completed.
∎

In the next lemma we find the upper bound for the gradient of
U_{\Lambda}(x)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\alpha>1
.
We have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{1+\varepsilon/2}},\quad x\in K,\quad\delta(x)\geq 1.

Proof.

The proof is simiilar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
. For any fixed
A\geq 1/2
we use the decomposition

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)&=\Bigg(\,\,\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in K,\,|x|\leq|y|,\\
|x-y|\geq A|x|\end{subarray}}+\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in K,\,|y|\leq|x|,\\
|x-y|\geq A|x|\end{subarray}}+\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in K\\
\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\leq|x-y|\leq A|x|\end{subarray}}+\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in K\\
|x-y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\Bigg)\nabla G(x,y)\Lambda(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)+I_{3}(x)+I_{4}(x).\end{split}

To estimate integrals
I_{i}(x)
for
i=1,2,3
we observe that in view of (
2.23
),

\displaystyle\nabla G(x,y)\leq C\frac{G(x,y)}{\delta(x)},\quad|x-y|\geq\delta(x)/2.

Using this estimate in conjunction with Lemma
2.6
and by repeating the arguments from the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we can easily show that

\displaystyle I_{i}(x)\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{1+\varepsilon/2}},\quad i=1,2,3.

To estimate
I_{4}(x)
it is enough to notice that, by (
2.23
),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\nabla G(x,y)\leq C\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha+1}},\quad|x-y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}.

We can again repeat the procedure for
I_{4}(x)
from the proof of Lemma
3.1
and combine it with (
3.12
) to obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}(x)\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{1+\varepsilon/2}},

and this allows us to conclude the required estimate in (
3.10
).
∎

In the next lemmas we analyse how

|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)|
behaves asymptotically (for large
\delta(x)
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
0<\alpha<1
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that for sufficiently large
\delta(x)

we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)|\leq C\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha},\quad|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}.

Proof.

According to (
3.1
) we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right|\leq\int_{K}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

To obtain the estimate in (
3.13
) we split the integral in (
3.14
) into five parts and we consider the corresponding cases below.

1. Estimate in
D_{1}=\{z\in K:\,|z-x|\leq\frac{3}{4}|y|\}
.
We start by noting that as
|y|\leq\delta(x)/2
and
|z-x|\leq 3\delta(x)/8
, we have

|z|\leq\left(1+\frac{3}{8}\right)|x|,\quad\delta(z)\geq\delta(x)-|z-x|\geq\frac{5}{8}\delta(x),\quad z\in D_{1}.

It follows that
\Lambda(z)\leq C\Lambda(x)
, for all
z\in D_{1}
. We bound the killed Green function with the full Green function of the process
Z(t)
and we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{D_{1}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{D_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{|x+y-z|^{d-\alpha}}+\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha}}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

It evidently holds
|x+y-z|\geq|y|/4
, for
z\in D_{1}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{D_{1}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|

\displaystyle\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\int_{0}^{3|y|/4}\left(|y|^{-d+\alpha}+r^{-d+\alpha}\right)r^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r\leq C_{2}\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha}.

2. Estimate in
D_{2}=\{z\in K:\,|x+y-z|\leq\frac{3}{4}|y|\}
. For
z\in D_{2}
we have

|z|\leq(1+7/8)|x|
and
\delta(z)\geq\delta(x)-|x-z|\geq\delta(x)/8
, and consequently,
\Lambda(z)\leq C\Lambda(x)
, for all
z\in D_{2}
.
Further,
|x-z|\geq|y|/4
, for
z\in D_{2}
, thus we can argue similarly as in the case of
D_{1}
to show that

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha}.

3. Estimate in
D_{3}=\{z\in K:\,|x-z|\leq\frac{{3}\delta(x)}{{4}}\}\cap(D_{1}\cup D_{2})^{c}
.
We first notice that the Taylor formula ensures that for
z\in D_{3}
and
|y|\leq\delta(x)/2
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\leq|y||\nabla_{x}G(x+\varrho_{0}y,z)|,\quad\varrho_{0}\in(0,1).

Further, for
z\in D_{3}
we have
|z|\leq{7}|x|/{4}
and
\delta(x)/{4}\leq\delta(z)\leq 7\delta(x)/4
, and whence
\Lambda(z)\leq C\Lambda(x)
. By (
2.23
) and by bounding the killed Green function with the full Green function we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{D_{3}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|

\displaystyle\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)|y|\int_{D_{3}}\frac{1}{|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|^{d-\alpha}}\frac{1}{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)\wedge|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|}\mathrm{d}z.

Next, we observe that for
z\in D_{3}

\displaystyle|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|\geq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}|x-z|,&\varrho_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2};\\
\frac{1}{3}|x+y-z|,&\varrho_{0}>\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Further, for
z\in D_{3}
,
|x+y-z|\leq\delta(x)
and
\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)\geq\delta(x)/2
, for
|y|\leq\delta(x)/2
. These estimates yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|^{d-\alpha}}\frac{1}{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)\wedge|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\left(|x-z|\wedge|x+y-z|\right)^{d-\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{|y|^{d-\alpha+1}},&|x-z|\leq 3|y|;\\
\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha+1}},&|x-z|>3|y|\end{cases}

and this allows us to conclude that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{D_{3}}&\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
&\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|\left\{|y|^{\alpha-1}+\int_{|x-z|>3|y|}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha+1}}\mathrm{d}z\right\}\leq C_{2}\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

4. Estimate in
D_{4}=\{z\in K:\,\frac{3}{4}\delta(x)<|x-z|\leq 3|x|\}\cap(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3})^{c}
. We again apply the Taylor formula from (
3.15
) for
z\in D_{4}
and we combine it with (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) to bound the gradient of
G
as follows

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x+\varrho_{0}y,z)|\leq C\,\frac{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)^{\alpha/2}\delta(z)^{\alpha/2}}{|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|^{d}}\frac{1}{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)\wedge|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|}.

Since for
z\in D_{4}
it holds
|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|\geq\delta(x)/4
and
\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)\leq 3\delta(x)/2
, we infer

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x+\varrho_{0}y,z)|\leq C_{1}\,\delta(x)^{\alpha/2-1}\frac{\delta(z)^{\alpha/2}}{|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|^{d}}\leq C_{2}\,\delta(x)^{\alpha/2-1}\frac{\delta(z)^{\alpha/2}}{|x-z|^{d}},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
|x+\varrho_{0}y-z|\geq|x-z|/3
, for
z\in D_{4}
. This and (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) imply

\displaystyle\int_{D_{4}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2-1}|y|\int_{D_{4}}\frac{\delta(z)^{\alpha/2}}{|x-z|^{d}}\Lambda(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2-1}|y|\int_{D_{4}}\frac{|z|^{\beta-\alpha/2}}{|x-z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2-1}|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}|y|\int_{D_{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)}|y|\int_{D_{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}.

Next, we note that for any
r>0
the Lebesgue measure of each of the sets

\displaystyle A_{k}(r)=\{z:\,r\leq|x-z|\leq r+1,\,k\leq\delta(z)\leq k+1\},\quad k=0,1,\ldots,r

does not exceed
c_{0}r^{d-2}
, for a constant
c_{0}>0
which does not depend on
r
, nor on
k
.
This allows us to estimate the last integral as follows

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{D_{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}&\leq C\int_{\frac{3}{4}\delta(x)}^{\infty}\frac{r^{d-2}}{r^{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}\frac{1}{(1+k)^{\varepsilon/2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&\leq C_{1}\int_{\frac{3}{4}\delta(x)}^{\infty}r^{-1-\varepsilon/2}\mathrm{d}r=C_{2}\delta(x)^{-\varepsilon/2}\end{split}

and whence

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{4}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{1+\varepsilon/2}}|y|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}|y|^{\alpha}\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha}.

5. Estimate in
D_{5}=\{z\in K:\,|x-z|>3|x|\}\cap(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3}\cup D_{4})^{c}
. In this region we use the bound from (
2.25
) in the form

\displaystyle G(x+\varrho_{0}y,z)\leq C\frac{M(x+\varrho_{0}y)M(z)}{|z|^{d-\alpha+2\beta}}\leq C_{1}\frac{M(x)M(z)}{|z|^{d-\alpha+2\beta}},\quad z\in D_{5},

which together with (
2.23
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\nabla G(x+\varrho_{0}y)\right|\leq C\frac{M(x)M(z)}{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)|z|^{d-\alpha+2\beta}}\leq C_{1}\frac{M(x)M(z)}{\delta(x+\varrho_{0}y)|z|^{d-\alpha+2\beta}},\quad z\in D_{5}.

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\int_{D_{5}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)}|y|\int_{D_{5}}\frac{M^{2}(z)}{|z|^{d-\alpha+2\beta}(1+\delta(z))^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)}|y|\int_{D_{5}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}.

To estimate the last integral one can proceed similarly as in (
3.17
) by using sets
A_{k}(r)
and this would imply

\displaystyle\int_{D_{5}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|z|^{d}(1+\delta(z))^{\varepsilon/2}}\leq C\int_{3|x|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}(1+k)^{-\varepsilon/2}\mathrm{d}r\leq C_{1}|x|^{-\varepsilon/2}.

We finally conclude that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{5}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{1+\varepsilon/2}}|y|\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha}

and the proof is finished.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
1<\alpha<2
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that for sufficiently large
\delta(x)

it holds

(3.20)

\displaystyle|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\cdot y|\leq C\Lambda(x)|y|^{\alpha},\quad|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}.

Proof.

The proof goes along the same lines as the proof of Lemma
3.3
and we briefly sketch the main steps. We start we the upper bound

|U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\cdot y|\\
\leq\int_{K}|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)-y\cdot\nabla_{x}G(x,z)|\Lambda(z)\mathrm{d}z

and then we split the last integral over the same regions
D_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,5
. In regions
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
we first apply the triangle inequality and then we do the same reasoning as before, separately for
|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)|
, and for
|y||\nabla_{x}G(x+y,z)|
, where for the part involving the gradient we use the estimate from (
2.23
).

In the three remaining regions we repeat the same arguments as in Lemma
3.3
, but this time we use the Taylor expansion in the form

\displaystyle\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)-y\cdot\nabla_{x}G(x,z)\right|\leq C|y|^{2}\max_{i,j=1,\ldots n}|D^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}G(x+\varrho_{0}y,z)|,

which holds for some
\varrho_{0}\in(0,1)
, and we combine it with the estimate from (
2.24
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

There exist two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-U_{\Lambda}(x)\leq-c_{1}\Lambda(x)+c_{2}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon}},\quad x\in K\text{ with }\delta(x)\geq 1.

Proof.

Recall
\tau_{x}^{Z}
defined in (
2.14
).
We start by splitting the integral

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]=\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z);\tau_{x}^{Z}\leq 1]+\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z);\tau_{x}^{Z}>1].

By Lemma
3.1
we know that
U_{\Lambda}(x)\leq cM(x)
. This followed by the estimate of the
J
- integral from Lemma
2.5
yields

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z);\tau_{x}^{Z}\leq 1]\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}.

Recall that
G(x,y)=G_{K}(x,y)
. For brevity we use the similar notation for the killed heat kernel
p_{K}(t,x,y)=p(t,x,y)
which was defined in (
1.9
).
For the second integral we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z);\tau_{x}^{Z}>1]

\displaystyle=\int_{K}p(1,x,u)U_{\Lambda}(u)\mathrm{d}u=\int_{K}p(1,x,u)\int_{K}G(u,y)\Lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\Lambda(y)\int_{K}p(1,x,u)\,G(u,y)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\Lambda(y)\int_{K}p(1,x,u)\int_{0}^{\infty}p(t,u,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\Lambda(y)\int_{1}^{\infty}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\Lambda(y)\left(G(x,y)-\int_{0}^{1}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=U_{\Lambda}(x)-\int_{K}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\Lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Hence, by (
3.22
) and (
3.23
),

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-U_{\Lambda}(x)\leq-\int_{K}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\Lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}

and we are left to show that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\Lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\geq C_{1}\Lambda(x).

To do this end, we notice that, by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\inf_{|y-x|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\Lambda(y)\geq C\frac{|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}\delta(x)^{\alpha/2}}{(1+\delta(x))^{\alpha+\varepsilon/2}}\geq C_{1}\Lambda(x)

and whence, for all
x\in K
with
\delta(x)\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p(t,x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\Lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq C\Lambda(x)\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\leq t}|Z_{s}-x|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq C\Lambda(x)\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\leq t}|Z_{s}-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t\geq C_{1}\Lambda(x).

Finally, combining (
3.24
) with (
3.25
) yield the desired estimate in (
3.21
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
0<\alpha<1
,

(3.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+X)-U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]=o(\Lambda(x)),\quad\text{as $\delta(x)\to\infty$.}

Proof.

For any fixed
R>0
we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle[U_{\Lambda}(x+X)-U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{|y|\leq R}+\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}+\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\right)\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)+I_{3}(x).

We first estimate
I_{2}(x)
. By Lemma
3.3
and our assumption (
1.3
) we immediately obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}|y|^{-d-\alpha-\nu}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Lambda(x)\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{1+\nu}}\leq C_{3}\frac{\Lambda(x)}{R^{\nu}}

and the last expression is equal to
o(\Lambda(x))
if we choose
R=R(x)\to\infty
.

To estimate
I_{3}(x)
we use (
1.3
) and (
2.21
) which yield

\displaystyle|I_{3}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq U_{\Lambda}(x)\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y+\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{U_{\Lambda}(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\nu}}+C_{1}\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

We next handle the last integral from the estimate above. By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-U_{\Lambda}(x)+U_{\Lambda}(x)\mathbb{P}(|Z_{1}|>\delta(x/2))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq-c_{1}\Lambda(x)+c_{2}\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\varepsilon}}+CU_{\Lambda}(x)\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y

and for the last integral we apply Lemma
3.3
to get

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\log\delta(x).

It follows that

\displaystyle I_{3}(x)\leq C\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}+C_{1}\frac{\Lambda(x)\log\delta(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}\leq C_{2}\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu/2}}.

We finally estimate the integral
I_{1}(x)
. We split this integral into three parts

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq R}\left(\int_{K_{1}}+\int_{K_{2}}+\int_{K_{3}}\right)\left(G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right)\Lambda(z)\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y,

where

\displaystyle K_{1}=\mathrm{conv}\left(B(x,\varepsilon(x)\delta(x))\cup B(x+y,\varepsilon(x)\delta(x))\right)

and

\displaystyle K_{2}=\left\{z\in K:\,|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\right\}\cap K_{1}^{c},\quad K_{3}=\left\{z\in K:\,|x-z|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\right\}\cap K_{1}^{c},

for some function
\varepsilon(x)
such that
\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)\to\infty
. The integral over the region
K_{2}
can be handled similarly as the integral over the set
D_{3}
in the proof of Lemma
3.3
(cf. (
3.16
)) and this results in the following bound

\displaystyle\int_{K_{2}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)|y|\int_{|x-z|>\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}|y|\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\left(\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)\right)^{1-\alpha}}.

To estimate the integral over
K_{3}
we apply estimates (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) over sets
D_{4}
and
D_{5}
from Lemma
3.3
which yield

(3.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{K_{3}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z&\leq\int_{D_{4}\cup D_{5}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
&\leq C\frac{M(x)}{|x|^{1+\varepsilon/2}}|y|\leq C\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{1-\alpha}}|y|.\end{split}

We next set

\Delta(u,z)=\frac{A_{d,\alpha}}{|u-z|^{d-\alpha}}-G(u,z).

To estimate the integral over
K_{1}
we first notice that
\delta(z)\sim\delta(x)
uniformly in
z\in K_{1}
. Furthermore, applying (
2.6
),
one infers that
M(z)\sim M(x)
uniformly in
z\in K_{1}
. As a result,
\Lambda(z)\sim\Lambda(x)

uniformly in
z\in K_{1}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{K_{1}}\left(G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)\right)\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\Lambda(x)\left[\int_{K_{1}}\left(\frac{A_{d,\alpha}}{|x+y-z|^{d-\alpha}}-\frac{A_{d,\alpha}}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha}}\right)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{K_{1}}\left(\Delta(x+y,z)-\Delta(x,z)\right)\mathrm{d}z\right]

(3.28)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+o\left(\Lambda(x)\int_{K_{1}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha}}\mathrm{d}z\right).

Observe that the first integral in this estimate disappear in view of symmetry of the set
K_{1}
and translation invariance of the full Green function.
To estimate the second integral we fix
\varepsilon_{0}(x)\geq\varepsilon(x)
tending to zero.
With
T=(\varepsilon_{0}(x)\delta(x))^{\alpha}
and the full heat kernel
p_{Z}(t,u,z)
we have

\Delta(u,z)=\int_{0}^{T}(p_{Z}(t,u,z)-p^{K}_{Z}(t,u,z))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{T}^{\infty}(p_{Z}(t,u,z)-p^{K}_{Z}(t,u,z))\,\mathrm{d}t.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left|\Delta(u,z)-\int_{0}^{T}(p_{Z}(t,u,z)-p^{K}_{Z}(t,u,z))\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}^{\infty}p(t,u,z)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{-d/\alpha}p_{Z}\left(1,0,\frac{z-u}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq CT^{1-\frac{d}{\alpha}}=C(\varepsilon_{0}(x)\delta(x))^{\alpha-d}

and consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{K_{1}}\left(\Delta(x+y,z)-\Delta(x,z)\right)\mathrm{d}z\leq C(\varepsilon_{0}(x)\delta(x))^{\alpha-d}(\varepsilon(x)\delta(x))^{d}.

The last expression may be done arbitrarily small by a proper choice of
\varepsilon_{0}(x)
.
Since
K_{1}
is a subset of the ball centred at
x
and with radius
(R+\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)
,

\int_{K_{1}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha}}\mathrm{d}z\leq C(R+\varepsilon(x)\delta(x))^{\alpha}.

This implies that if
R(x)\to\infty
and

\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)\to\infty
sufficiently slow, then the last term on the right hand side of (
3.1
) is
o(\Lambda(x))
.

To finish the proof it remains to notice that integrating over
\{y:|y|\leq R(x)\}
does not destroy convergence to zero provided that
R(x)\to\infty
sufficiently slow.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Under above assumptions, for
1<\alpha<2
, it holds

(3.29)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+X)-U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]=o(\Lambda(x)),\quad\delta(x)\to\infty.

Proof.

For any fixed
R>0
we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle[U_{\Lambda}(x+X)-U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-y\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{|y|\leq R}\!\!+\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\!\!+\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\right)\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-y\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)\left(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)+I_{3}(x),

where we used the fact that
\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[Z]=0
.
We first estimate
I_{2}(x)
. By Lemma
3.4
and our assumption (
1.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\int_{R<|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}|y|^{-d-\alpha-\nu}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Lambda(x)\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r^{1+\nu}}\leq C_{3}\frac{\Lambda(x)}{R^{\nu}}

and the last expression is equal to
o(\Lambda(x))
if we take
R=R(x)\to\infty
as

\delta(x)\to\infty
appropriately large.

To estimate
I_{3}(x)
we use (
1.3
) and (
2.21
) which yield

\displaystyle|I_{3}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq U_{\Lambda}(x)\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y+|\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)|\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y||p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)|p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{U_{\Lambda}(x)}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\nu}}+C_{1}\frac{|\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)|}{\delta(x)^{\alpha+\nu-1}}+C_{2}\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

We next handle the last integral from the estimate above.
We have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{|y|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}&U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y=\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}U_{\Lambda}(x+y)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\mathbb{E}[U_{\Lambda}(x+Z)]-U_{\Lambda}(x)+U_{\Lambda}(x)\mathbb{P}(|Z_{1}|>\delta(x/2))\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\quad-\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-y\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}y\,p_{Z}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Since
\mathbb{E}[Z]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}y\,p_{Z}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|=\left|\int_{|y|\geq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}y\,p_{Z}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq C\frac{1}{\delta(x)^{\alpha-1}}.

To estimate the penultimate integral in (
3.30
)
we apply Lemma
3.4
which yield

\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}\left(U_{\Lambda}(x+y)-U_{\Lambda}(x)-y\nabla U_{\Lambda}(x)\right)p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}|y|^{\alpha}p_{Z}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\log x.

By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.5
,
it follows from (
3.30
) that

\displaystyle|I_{3}(x)|\leq C\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu/2}}+C_{1}\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}+C_{2}\frac{\Lambda(x)\log x}{\delta(x)^{\nu}}\leq C_{3}\frac{\Lambda(x)}{\delta(x)^{\nu/2}}.

We finally estimate the integral
I_{1}(x)
. We consider the three following integrals

\displaystyle\left(\int_{K_{1}}+\!\!\int_{K_{2}}+\!\!\int_{K_{3}}\right)\left(G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)-y\cdot\nabla_{x}G(x,z)\right)\Lambda(z),

and

\displaystyle K_{2}=\left\{z\in K:\,|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\right\}\cap K_{1}^{c},\quad K_{3}=\left\{z\in K:\,|x-z|>\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\right\},

for some function
\varepsilon(x)\to 0
such that
\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)\to\infty
. For the integral over
K_{2}

3.6
. By (

), we have

\int_{K_{2}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)-y\nabla_{x}G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
\leq C\Lambda(x)|y|^{2}\int_{|x-z|\geq\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha+2}}\\
\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|^{2}\left(\varepsilon(x)\delta(x)\right)^{\alpha-2}.

To use this inequality to estimate the integral
I_{1}(x)
, we need to integrate the obtained upper bound over all

and thus we can choose

, for a constant

, so that this part of the integral

.
For the integral over

), but this time we base upon Lemma

\int_{K_{3}}\left|G(x+y,z)-G(x,z)-y\cdot\nabla_{x}G(x,z)\right|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\\
\leq C\frac{M(x)}{\delta(x)^{2+\nu/2}}|y|^{2}\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)|y|^{2}\delta(x)^{\alpha-2}

and we conclude that in this case the same choice of the function
R(x)
will force the right rate of decay.

In the last integral over
K_{1}
the part involving the difference

can be handled in the same way as in the proof of Lemma

. It remains to estimate the part involving the gradient, that is

\displaystyle\int_{K_{1}}|\nabla G(x,z)|\Lambda(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda(x)\int_{K_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{d-\alpha+1}}\leq C_{1}\Lambda(x)\delta(x)^{\alpha-1}.

If we integrate the last upper bound over

and use the fact that
\mathbb{E}[Z]=\mathbb{E}[X]=0
combined with (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|y|\leq R}y(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y))\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{|y|\geq R}y(p_{X}(y)-p_{Z}(y))\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq CR^{1-\alpha-\nu}

and we again conclude that the choice

will suffice to make all the parts of the integral

to be

, as desired.
∎

Let

3.2.
Supermartingale and harmonic function

We denote

(3.33)

W(x)=M(x)+U_{\Lambda}(x),\quad x\in K.

We fix
x_{0}\in K
such that
\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},x_{0}+K)>0
. As the cone is circular, we could evidently choose
x_{0}=e_{d}
.
For any
R\geq 0
and
c\geq 0
we set

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{(c)}

\displaystyle=W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n)){\rm 1}\{\tau_{x}>n\}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+c\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(k)){\rm 1}\{\tau_{x}>k\},

and for
c=0
we use the following simplified notation

(3.35)

Y_{n}=Y_{n}^{(0)}.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{n}
the natural filtration generated by the random walk
S(n)
.
The key result for our further analysis is the following fact.

Lemma 3.9
.

For every
0\leq c<c_{0}
with
c_{0}
specified in Lemma
3.8
there exists
R>0
such that the sequence

Y_{n}^{(c)}

is a
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-supermartingale.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{n}^{(c)}-Y_{n-1}^{(c)}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\mathbb{E}[W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n\}-W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+c\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}.

Since the functions
M
and
U_{\Lambda}
are nonnegative (and hence
W(x)\geq 0
), then for the error function
f
defined in (
2.12
) and
for the function
f_{\Lambda}
given in (
3.31
)
we can get the following upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{n}^{(c)}-Y_{n-1}^{(c)}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}\mathbb{E}[W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n))-W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+c\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\big(f(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))+f_{U}(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\big)\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+c\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}.

According to Lemma
3.8

\mathbb{E}[Y_{n}^{(c)}-Y_{n-1}^{(c)}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]\leq-\left(c_{0}-c\right)\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(n-1))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>n-1\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

To construct a positive harmonic function
we need
the following auxiliary estimate.

Lemma 3.10
.

There exists
C>0
, such that for sufficiently large
R
,

(3.36)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\right]\leq C\,W(x+Rx_{0}).

Furthermore, there exists
\varepsilon(R)\downarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow+\infty
such that

(3.37)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}|f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))|\right]\leq\varepsilon(R)\,M(x+Rx_{0}).

Proof.

Let
R
be sufficiently large and
c
sufficiently small such that
(Y_{n}^{(c)})_{n\geq 0}
defined in (
3.2
)
is a supermartingale.
Then,

\mathbb{E}[Y_{n}^{(c)}]\leq Y_{0}^{(c)}=W(x+Rx_{0}).

Due to non-negativity of
M
and
U_{\Lambda}
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Lambda(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>k\}\right]\leq CW(x+Rx_{0}).

Letting here
n\to\infty
completes the proof of (
3.36
).
Estimate (
3.37
) follows from (
3.36
), Lemma
3.1

and estimate (
3.32
).
∎

In the next result we establish the existence of the ’shifted’ harmonic
function
V_{R}(x)
for killed random walk on exiting from the cone
K
.

Proposition 3.11
.

For
R>0
sufficiently large, the following function

(3.38)

V_{R}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}>n]

is finite and harmonic for the random walk
S(n)
killed on exiting from
K
, that is,

(3.39)

V_{R}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[V_{R}(x+S(n));\tau_{x}>n\right],\quad x\in K,\ n\geq 1.

Proof.

We consider the following process

L_{n}=M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n\wedge\tau_{x}))-\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k)),\quad n\geq 0.

Recalling that
f(y)=\mathbb{E}[M(y+X)]-M(y)
, we can conclude that
L_{n}
is a martingale.
By the optional stopping theorem applied to this martingale,

\displaystyle M(x+Rx_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[L_{0}]=\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n))-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}>n\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\mathbb{E}\left[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(\tau_{x}))-\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}\leq n\right].

Consequently,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}&>n]\\
&=M(x+Rx_{0})-\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(\tau_{x}));\tau_{x}\leq n]\\
&\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}\leq n\right]\\
&\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}>n\right].\end{split}

By the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(\tau_{x}));\tau_{x}\leq n]=\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(\tau_{x}))].

Further, by (
3.37
) for
R>0
large enough the random variable
\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))
is integrable and whence we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}\leq n\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\right]

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k));\tau_{x}>n\right]\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}|f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))|;\tau_{x}>n\right]=0.

We arrive at

(3.41)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}>n]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=M(x+Rx_{0})-\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(\tau_{x}))]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\right].\end{split}

By (
3.37
), the limit above is finite and thus we are left to prove that the function

\displaystyle V_{R}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}>n]

is harmonic for the killed random walk.
To do this end, we use the following identity

(3.42)

\mathbb{E}[M(x+Rx_{0}+S(n+1));\tau_{x}>n+1]\\
=\int_{K}\mathbb{P}(x+S(1)\in\mathrm{d}y)\,\mathbb{E}[M(y+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}>n].

Applying (
3.40
) and the fact that the function
M
is non-negative and bounded by the function
W
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[M(y+Rx_{0}+S(n));\tau_{x}>n]

\displaystyle\leq W(y+Rx_{0})+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{x}-1}f(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\,W(y+Rx_{0})\leq C_{1}|y+Rx_{0}|^{\beta},

where we applied (
3.36
) and the fact that
W(x)\leq C|x|^{\beta}
. Since
\beta<\alpha
, the
\beta
-moment of
X
is finite and whence we are allowed to use the dominated convergence theorem in (
3.42
) which yields

\displaystyle V_{R}(x)=\int_{K}\mathbb{P}(x+S(1)\in\mathrm{d}y)\,V_{R}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\mathbb{E}[V_{R}(x+X);\tau_{x}>1],

and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Asymptotic behaviour of
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)

4.1.
Preliminary estimates

We first prove a uniform upper bound for the tail of
\tau_{x}
.
We recall that function
W
is defined in (
3.33
).

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist finite constants
C>0
and
R>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)\leq C\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}},\quad n\geq 1.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
one has

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2))\geq\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})+\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2))<\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha}).

Set
D_{1}=\{z\in K:\delta(z)<\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha}\}
.
By the Markov property,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2))<\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})=\int_{D_{1}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(n/2))\in dz,\tau_{x}>n/2)\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n/2).

Since
K
is convex, we can upper bound the tail of
\tau_{z}
by the tail of exit time from the half space. Therefore, for every
z\in D_{1}
we have the following bound: there exist
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)
and an absolute constant
C_{1}
such that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n/2)\leq C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2},\quad z\in D_{1},\ n\geq n_{0}.

This implies that

(4.2)

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2))<\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})\leq C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n/2).

Set now
D_{2}=K\setminus D_{1}
.
It follows from homogeneity (
1.6
) of
M(x)
and (
2.2
) that there exists constant
C
such that

W(z+Rx_{0})\geq CM(z)\geq c(\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})^{\beta},\quad z\in D_{2}.

Consequently, by the Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2))\geq\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})\leq\frac{1}{c\varepsilon^{\beta}n^{\beta/\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\left[W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n/2));\tau_{x}>n/2\right].

Recalling that
Y_{k}=W(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>k\}
defined in (
3.35
) is a supermartingale (see Lemma
3.9
), we obtain

(4.3)

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n,\delta(x+S(n/2)\geq\varepsilon n^{1/\alpha})\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{-\beta}\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}.

Plugging (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) into (
4.1
), we have, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{-\beta}\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}+C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n/2).

If
\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\geq n_{0}
then we can repeat this procedure

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{-\beta}\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(2^{\beta/\alpha}C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}\right)^{j}+(C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2})^{m}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n/2^{m}).

Choosing

, we infer that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)\leq\frac{C_{2}\varepsilon^{-\beta}}{1-2^{\beta/\alpha}C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}}\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}+(C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2})^{m}.

To bound the second summand on the right hand side we choose

m

such that

\frac{n}{2^{m-1}}\geq n_{0}
and
\frac{n}{2^{m}}<n_{0}

Combining the latter inequality with

(C_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha})^{m}\leq(2^{m})^{-\beta/\alpha}\leq(n_{0}/n)^{-\beta/\alpha}.

Recalling that

∎

We next prove a bound for the tail of the expectation of the supermartingale

\displaystyle Y_{k}=W(x+Rx_{0}+S(k))\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{x}>k\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
x\in K
we have, uniformly in
n
,

\mathbb{E}\left[W(x+Rx_{0}+S(n));\max_{k\leq n}|x+S(k)|>An^{1/\alpha}\right]\leq C(x,R)A^{\beta-\alpha}.

Proof.

For brevity, we use the following notation

\displaystyle\widehat{S}(j)=\max_{k\leq j}|x+S(k)|.

Fix some
\gamma\in(0,1)
and split the expectation into two parts:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\max_{k\leq n}|x+S(k)|>An^{1/\alpha}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)>A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right].

Using the supermartingale property of
Y_{n}
, one can easily see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)>A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n/2)>A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]

(4.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n/2};\widehat{S}(n/2)>2^{1/\alpha}A\gamma(n/2)^{1/\alpha}\right].

It follows easily from the definition of
Y_{n}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[|x+Rx_{0}+S(n)|^{\beta};\tau_{x}>n,\,\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right].

On the event
\{\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\}
we have

\widetilde{S}(n)=\max_{k\in[n/2,n]}|S(k)-S(n/2)|>(1-\gamma)An^{1/\alpha}.

Therefore,

|x+Rx_{0}+S(n)|\leq C\widetilde{S}(n)

and, consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{S}(n)^{\beta};\tau_{x}>n,\widetilde{S}(n)>(1-\gamma)An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{S}(n)^{\beta};\tau_{x}>n/2,\widetilde{S}(n)>(1-\gamma)An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=C\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n/2)\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{S}(n)^{\beta};\,\widetilde{S}(n)>(1-\gamma)An^{1/\alpha}\right].

Noting that, due to the functional limit theorem for the process
S(nt)/n^{n^{1/\alpha}}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{S}(n)^{\beta};\widetilde{S}(n)>(1-\gamma)An^{1/\alpha}\right]\leq Cn^{\beta/\alpha}((1-\gamma)A)^{\beta-\alpha}

and applying Lemma
4.1
, we conclude that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha},\,\widehat{S}(n/2)\leq A\gamma n^{1/\alpha}\right]\leq C_{1}A^{\beta-\alpha}W(x+Rx_{0}),

where
C_{1}
depends on
\gamma
. Plugging (
4.1
) and
(
4.6
) into (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n/2};\widehat{S}(n/2)>2^{1/\alpha}A\gamma(n/2)^{1/\alpha}\right]+C\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{A^{\alpha-\beta}}.

Iterating this procedure
m
times, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n/2^{m}};\widehat{S}(n/2^{m})>2^{m/\alpha}A\gamma^{m}(n/2^{m})^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{A^{\alpha-\beta}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(2^{1/\alpha}\gamma)^{-(\alpha-\beta)j}.

Take
\gamma<1
so that
2^{1/\alpha}\gamma>1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n};\widehat{S}(n)>An^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n/2^{m}};\widehat{S}(n/2^{m})>2^{m/\alpha}A\gamma^{m}(n/2^{m})^{1/\alpha}\right]+C\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{A^{\alpha-\beta}}.

Using the fact that
Y_{k}\leq C|x+Rx_{0}+S(k)|^{\beta}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n/2^{m}};\widehat{S}(n/2^{m})>2^{m/\alpha}A\gamma^{m}(n/2^{m})^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C(x,R)\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{S}(n/2^{m})^{\beta};\,\widehat{S}(n/2^{m})>2^{m/\alpha}A\gamma^{m}(n/2^{m})^{1/\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C(x,R)(n/2^{m})^{\beta/\alpha}\left(2^{m/\alpha}A\gamma^{m}\right)^{\beta-\alpha}.

If we can choose
m=m(n)
so that
n/2^{m}\to\infty
and
(n/2^{m})^{\beta/\alpha}\left(2^{m/\alpha}\gamma^{m}\right)^{\beta-\alpha}\leq 1
, then we get the desired bound.
Set
\theta=\frac{\beta}{\alpha\log(2\gamma^{\alpha-\beta})}
.
It is easy then to check that any integer from the interval

(\theta\log n+2,\theta\log n+4)
possesses the properties mentioned above.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

for sufficiently large

\displaystyle\int_{D_{1}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n-m)\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}A^{\beta-\alpha/2}\frac{W(x+Rx_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}.

\displaystyle\int_{D_{3}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n-m)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C(x,R)A^{\beta-\alpha}n^{-\beta/\alpha}.

If we choose

so that

sufficiently slow then, combining the functional limit theorem for

with Theorem 3.1 in
[

]
, we conclude that, for every

,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n-m)\sim\mathbb{P}_{zn^{-1/\alpha}}(\tau^{Z}_{K}>1)\sim\varkappa M(zn^{-1/\alpha})\sim\varkappa\frac{M(z+R^{\prime}x_{0})}{n^{\beta/\alpha}},\quad\mathrm{as}\ n\to\infty,

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}>n-m)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{\varkappa+o(1)}{n^{\beta/\alpha}}\int_{D_{2}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz)M(z+R^{\prime}x_{0}).

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)M(z+R^{\prime}x_{0})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\mathbb{E}[M(x+R^{\prime}x_{0}+S(m));\tau_{x}>m]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{D_{1}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)M(z+R^{\prime}x_{0})

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{3}}\mathbb{P}(x+S(m)\in dz,\tau_{x}>m)M(z+R^{\prime}x_{0})\leq C(x,R)A^{\beta-\alpha}.

Furthermore, using (

) and applying then Lemma

(4.12)

Letting first

and then

\displaystyle n^{\beta/\alpha}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>n)=(\varkappa+o(1))\mathbb{E}[M(x+R^{\prime}x_{0}+S(m));\tau_{x}>m]+o(1).

5.
Conditional functional limit theorem

5.1.
Existence of the meander.

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
denote the distribution of the Doob
h
-transform of the process
Z
killed at leaving
K
. It was shown in
[
30
, Theorem 3.3]
that
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
converges, as
x\to 0
, to a probability measure
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
in the Skorohod space. We now transfer this into convergence of conditional distributions.

Let
g
be a bounded continuous functional on
D[0,1
]. Without loss of generality we may assume that
g
takes nonnegative values only.

It is immediate from the definition of the Doob transform that

\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(Z);\tau^{Z}_{K}>1]=M(x)\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}\right].

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(Z)\mid\tau^{Z}_{K}>1]=\frac{M(x)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau^{Z}_{K}>1)}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}\right].

Applying Theorem 3.1 from
[
7
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(Z)\mid\tau^{Z}_{K}>1]\sim\varkappa^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}\right],\quad\text{as }x\to 0.

Thus, it remains to show that
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}\right]
converges.
Since the functional
\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}
is not bounded we cannot apply the convergence of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
directly. Fix some
\varepsilon>0
. Then, combining the weak convergence of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
with the absolute continuity of
Z(1)
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
, we conclude that

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))};|Z(1)|>\varepsilon\right]\to\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))};|Z(1)|>\varepsilon\right],\quad\mathrm{as}\ x\to 0.

Furthermore, by Theorem 3.3 from
[
7
]
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))};|Z(1)|\leq\varepsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M(x)}\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(Z);|Z(1)|\leq\varepsilon,\tau^{Z}_{K}>1]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{g}}{M(x)}\mathbb{P}_{x}(|Z(1)|\leq\varepsilon,\tau^{Z}_{K}>1)

\displaystyle\to C_{g}\varkappa\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}n_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{as }x\to 0,

where the limit

n_{1}(y)=\lim_{K\ni x\to 0}\frac{p_{K}(1,x,y)}{\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x}>1)}

exists and it is a finite and continuous in
y

for the heat kernel
p_{K}(t,x,y)
of the process
Z
killed on
leaving cone
K
and defined formally in (
1.9
).

Moreover, by the definition of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))};|Z(1)|\leq\varepsilon\right]\leq C_{g}\varkappa\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}n_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

Using (3.8) from
[
7
]
it is easy to see that
\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}n_{1}(y)dy
converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This gives the desired convergence:

\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(Z)\mid\tau^{Z}_{K}>1]\to\varkappa^{-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\frac{g(Z)}{M(Z(1))}\right],\quad\text{as }x\to 0,

for every continuous bounded
g
.
Having above limiting law well-defined,
we can define the corresponding stochastic process
me_{K}(t)
via Kolmogorov Existence Theorem.

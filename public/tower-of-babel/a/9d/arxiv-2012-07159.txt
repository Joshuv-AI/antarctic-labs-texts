Cotorsion Pairs in Hopfological Algebra
Mariko Ohara, Dai Tamaki

Abstract
In an intriguing paper [ math/0509083 ] Khovanov proposed a generalization of homological algebra, called Hopfological algebra. Since then, several attempts have been made to import tools and techiniques from homological algebra to Hopfological algebra. For example, Qi [ 1205.1814 ] introduced the notion of cofibrant objects in the category 𝑪 A , H H \bm{C}_{A,H}^{H} of H H -equivariant modules over an H H -module algebra A A , which is a counterpart to the category of modules over a dg algebra, although he did not define a model structure on 𝑪 A , H H \bm{C}_{A,H}^{H} . In this paper, we show that there exists an Abelian model structure on 𝑪 A , H H \bm{C}_{A,H}^{H} in which cofibrant objects agree with Qi’s cofibrant objects under a slight modification. This is done by constructing cotorsion pairs in 𝑪 A , H H \bm{C}_{A,H}^{H} which form a Hovey triple in the sense of Gillespie [ 1512.06001 ] . This can be regarded as a Hopfological analogues of the work of Enochs, Jenda, and Xu [ Enochs-Jenda-Xu1996 ] and Avramov, Foxby, and Halperin [ Avramov-Foxby-Halperin ] . By restricting to compact cofibrant objects, we obtain a Waldhausen category 𝒫 ​ erf A , H H \mathcal{P}\mathrm{erf}_{A,H}^{H} of perfect objects. By taking invariants of this Waldhausen category, such as algebraic K K -theory, Hochschild homology, cyclic homology, and so on, we obtain Hopfological analogues of these invariants.

Introduction

Khovanov
[

An important observation of Khovanov is that the existence of an

This aim of this paper is to construct a model structure on

Since

]

]

Recall that a Hovey triple in

of subcategories such that both
(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv}\cap\mathbf{Fib})

subcategory. Recall also that cotorsion pairs are defined in terms of

. See §

In the case of the category of chain complexes, and, more generally,
in the category of left modules over a dg algebra, the corresponding
orthogonality has been studied by Enochs, Jenda, and Xu

[

]
and Avramov, Foxby, and Halperin

]

As is stated in Hovey’s paper, their results lead to a Hovey triple

We introduce the notions of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

H
-module category.
Denote the full subcategories of

Then the triple

By analogy, we call compact cofibrant objects in our model category

The full subcategory

Besides algebraic

Recall that there is another approach to the algebraic

[

]

Given a dg category

-modules,
whose cofibrations are morphisms

which admits a retraction in

\underline{A}
-modules, where
\underline{A}
is the
underlying (graded) linear category of

We may also define a Hopfological analogue of this construction by using
a cotorsion pair.
For a left
H
-module category

, we define a structure of an exact
category on
\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H}
by declaring
\underline{A}
-split

\underline{A}
is the linear

Let us denote this exact category by

We show that the pair
(\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H,\mathrm{split}},\mathbf{Cntr}_{A,H}^{H})

Although this cotorsion pair is not a part of a Hovey triple, a recent

The Waldhausen subcategory of compact objects is another choice for

(A,H)
.
We note that this is closer to Qi’s approach to the

\mathcal{D}^{c}(A,H)
.

Finally we remark that Kaygun and Khalkhali
[
math/0606341
]

introduced another kind of “projective” modules for an
H
-module

, called

-equivariantly projective

-modules in their

Their purpose is to define the Hopf-cyclic homology of

by using the

H
-equivariantly projective

-modules.
We may use this exact category to define an algebraic

-theory.
From this point of view, however, the action of

Thus the

-theory obtained from

should be regarded as a generalization of Thomason’s equivariant

Proof.

in

Definition 2.38

Lemma 2.39
.

For
\varphi\in E(M)
, define an action of

on

\varphi

(h\cdot\varphi)(g)=\varphi(S(h)g)

Then it defines a left
H
-module structure on

.

A
and we obtain an exact functor

E:{\underline{A}}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H}.

Proof.

Let us verify the associativity. For

,

\displaystyle(h\cdot(h^{\prime}\cdot\varphi))(g)

\displaystyle=(h\cdot\varphi)(S(h^{\prime})g)

\displaystyle=\varphi(S(h^{\prime})S(h)g)

\displaystyle=\varphi(S(hh^{\prime})g)

\displaystyle=((hh^{\prime})\cdot\varphi)(g).

We also have

, since

In order to verify that

is an

-equivariant

\displaystyle\left((h_{(1)}a)(h_{(2)}\varphi)\right)(g)

\displaystyle=(h_{(1)}a)\varphi(S(h_{(2)})g)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon(h_{(1)})a)\varphi(S(h_{(2)})g)

\displaystyle=a\varphi(S(\varepsilon(h_{(1)})h_{(2)})g)

\displaystyle=a\varphi(S(h)g)

\displaystyle=(h\cdot(a\varphi))(g),

which means that the

Proposition 2.40
.

The functor
E
is right adjoint to the forgetful functor

U:\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H}\to{\underline{A}}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}

Proof.

For an
H
-equivariant

-module

\displaystyle\Phi(\varphi)(h^{\prime}m)(h)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(S^{-1}(h)h^{\prime}m\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\Phi(\varphi)(m)\right)(S(h^{\prime})h)

2.4
Model structures on Abelian categories

This is a summary of Hovey’s theory of Abelian model categories and
cotorsion pairs used in this paper.
Our main reference is Gillespie’s survey
[
1512.06001
]
.

Let us first recall the definition of cotorsion pairs introduced by Salce in

[
Salce1979
]
.

Definition 2.43
.

Let
\bm{A}
be an Abelian category.
For objects
X,Y\in\bm{A}
, define

X\perp Y\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{\bm{A}}^{1}(X,Y)=0.

More generally for a class
\bm{C}
of objects, define

\displaystyle\bm{C}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{Y\in\bm{A}_{0}\,\middle|\,C\perp Y\text{
for all }C\in\bm{C}\right\}

\displaystyle{}^{\perp}\bm{C}

\displaystyle=\left\{X\in\bm{A}_{0}\,\middle|\,X\perp C\text{
for all }C\in\bm{C}\right\}.

These classes are also regarded as full subcategories.

Definition 2.44
.

Let
\bm{A}
be as above.
A
cotorsion pair
on
\bm{A}
is a pair

(\bm{P},\bm{I})
of classes
of objects of
\bm{A}
satisfying

(1)

\bm{P}={}^{\perp}\bm{I}
.

(2)

\bm{I}=\bm{P}^{\perp}
.

Definition 2.45
.

A cotorsion pair
(\bm{P},\bm{I})
is said to
have enough
projectives
if,
for any
X
in
\bm{A}
, there exists
a short exact sequence of the form

0\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}A\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}B\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}X\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}0

with
B\in\bm{P}
and
A\in\bm{I}
.
It is said to
have enough injectives
if, for any
X
in

\bm{A}
, there exists a short exact sequence of the form

0\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}X\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}B^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}0

with
A^{\prime}\in\bm{I}
and
B^{\prime}\in\bm{P}
.

It is called
complete
if it has both enough projectives and
injectives.

Example 2.46
.

Let
\mathbf{Prj}(\bm{A})
and
\mathbf{Inj}(\bm{A})
be the classes of projectives
and injectives in an Abelian category
\bm{A}
.
Then
(\bm{A},\mathbf{Inj}(\bm{A}))
and
(\mathbf{Prj}(\bm{A}),\bm{A})
are cotorsion
pairs.
The former is complete precisely when
\bm{A}
has enough injectives
and the latter is complete precisely when
\bm{A}
has enough
projectives.

These are called
categorical cotorsion pairs
by Hovey.
Let us call the former the
projective cotorsion pair
and the
latter the
injective cotorsion pair
.

In the case of Grothendieck Abelian categories, the completeness of
cotorsion pairs is closely related to the notion of generation of
cotorsion pairs. The following terminology is used in
[
1005.3248
]
.

Definition 2.47
.

Let
\bm{G}
be a set of objects in an Abelian category
\bm{A}
.
Then the pair
({}^{\perp}(\bm{G}^{\perp}),\bm{G}^{\perp})
is called
the
cotorsion pair generated by
\bm{G}
.

Remark 2.48
.

The pair
({}^{\perp}(\bm{G}^{\perp}),\bm{G}^{\perp})
is always a
cotorsion pair by definition.
Some authors say that the cotorsion pair is
cogenerated by

\bm{G}
in the above situaltion. For example, this terminology is
used by Hanno Becker in
[
1205.4473
]
.

The following fact can be
found as Proposition 1.2.1 in
[
1205.4473
]
and is attribued to

[
1005.3248
]
.

Proposition 2.49
.

Let
\bm{A}
be a Grothendieck Abelian category.
If
(\bm{D},\bm{E})
is a cotorsion pair generated by a set
\bm{X}
,
then the following hold:

(1)

The pair
(\bm{D},\bm{E})
has enough injectives.

(2)

The pair
(\bm{D},\bm{E})
has enough projectives if and only if

\bm{D}
is generating.

The following terminology is used in
[
1205.4473
]
.

Definition 2.50
.

A cotorsion pair
(\bm{D},\bm{E})
is called
small
if
\bm{D}
is
generated by a set and
\bm{D}
is generating.

Corollary 2.51
.

If
\bm{A}
is a Grothendieck Abelian category with enough projectives,
then any cotorsion pair generated by a set is small. Thus it is

Definition 2.52
.

Let
\bm{A}
be a bicomplete Abelian category.
A model structure on
\bm{A}
is called
Abelian
if

(1)

a morphism is a cofibration if and only if it is a
monomorphism with cofibrant cokernel,

(2)

a morphism is a trivial cofibration if and only if it is a
monomorphism with trivially cofibrant cokernel,

(3)

a morphism is a fibration if and only if it is an
epimorphism with fibrant kernel, and

(4)

a morphism is a trivial fibration if and only if it is a
epimorphism with trivially fibrant kernel.

The following terminology is introduced by Gillespie

[
1512.06001
]
.

Definition 2.53
.

Let
\bm{A}
be an Abelian category. A triple of subcategories

(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})
is called a

Hovey triple
if

(1)

\mathbf{Triv}
is a thick subcategory.

(2)

(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Fib}\cap\mathbf{Triv})
is a
complete cotorsion pair.

(3)

(\mathbf{Cof}\cap\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})
is a
complete cotorsion pair.

Theorem 2.54

(Hovey)
.

Let
\bm{A}
be a bicomplete Abelian category. Suppose
\bm{A}
is
equipped with an Abelian model structure.
Denote the full subcategories of trivial, cofibrant, and fibrant
objects by
\mathbf{Triv}
,
\mathbf{Cof}
, and
\mathbf{Fib}
, respectively.
Then
(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})
is a Hovey triple

Conversely, given a Hovey triple
(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})

there exists a unique Abelian model structure on
\bm{A}
such that

\mathbf{Triv}
,
\mathbf{Cof}
, and
\mathbf{Fib}
are subcategories of trivial, cofibrant,
and fibrant objects, respectively.

By definition, in the Abelian model structure defined by a Hovey triple

(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})
, a morphism
f:X\to Y
is

•

a cofibration if and only if
f
is a monomorphism and

\operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathbf{Cof}
,

•

a trivial cofibration if and only if
f
is a monomorphism and

\operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathbf{Cof}\cap\mathbf{Triv}
,

•

a fibration if and only if
f
is an epimorphism and

\operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathbf{Fib}
, and

•

a trivial fibration if and only if
f
is an epimorphism and

\operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathbf{Fib}\cap\mathbf{Triv}
.

Furthermore the following characterization of weak equivalences is
obtained by Hovey.

Lemma 2.55
.

In the Abelian model structure defined by a Hovey triple

(\mathbf{Cof},\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Fib})
, a morphism
f:X\to Y
is a weak equivalence if and
only if there exist an epimorphism
p
with
\operatorname{Ker}p\in\mathbf{Triv}
and a
monomorphism
i
with
\operatorname{Coker}i\in\mathbf{Triv}
such that

f=p\circ i
.

By the projective cotorsion pair (Example

2.46
) and Corollary

2.51
, we obtain
the following.

Lemma 2.56
.

Let
\bm{A}
be a Grothendieck Abelian category having enough projectives.
Given a thick subcategory
\mathbf{Triv}
,

({}^{\perp}\mathbf{Triv},\mathbf{Triv},\bm{A})
is a Hovey triple
if and only if

(1)

{}^{\perp}\mathbf{Triv}\cap\mathbf{Triv}=\mathbf{Prj}(\bm{A})
,
and

(2)

\left({}^{\perp}\mathbf{Triv}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{Triv}
,

where
\mathbf{Prj}(\bm{A})
is the full subcategory of projective objects in

\bm{A}
.

3.1
Equivariant projective modules

In
[

]

various notions of projectives in the category of dg modules over a dg

In this section, we study Hopfological counterparts.
Throughout this section, we fix a left
H
-module category

Given an
H
-equivariant

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{A,H}(P,-)

\displaystyle:\bm{C}_{A,H}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}{H}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H}(P,-)

\displaystyle:\bm{C}_{A,H}^{H}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}{k}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}.

Various notions of projectivities are defined by the degrees of
preservations of surjectivities by these functors.

Definition 3.1

Let

be an
H
-equivariant left
A
-module.

(1)

is called
\Sigma
-linearly projective

is surjective for all

.

(2)

P
is called
\Sigma
-homotopically projective
, if,
for any
\Sigma
-quism

3.2

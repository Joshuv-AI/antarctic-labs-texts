Pairwise Reachability Oracles and Preservers under Failures
Diptarka Chakraborty, Kushagra Chatterjee, Keerti Choudhary

Abstract
In this paper, we consider reachability oracles and reachability preservers for directed graphs/networks prone to edge/node failures. Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a directed graph on n n -nodes, and 𝒫 ⊆ V × V {\cal P}\subseteq V\times V be a set of vertex pairs in G G . We present the first non-trivial constructions of single and dual fault-tolerant pairwise reachability oracle with constant query time. Furthermore, we provide extremal bounds for sparse fault-tolerant reachability preservers, resilient to two or more failures. Prior to this work, such oracles and reachability preservers were widely studied for the special scenario of single-source and all-pairs settings. However, for the scenario of arbitrary pairs, no prior (non-trivial) results were known for dual (or more) failures, except those implied from the single-source setting. One of the main questions is whether it is possible to beat the O ⁡ ( n ​ | 𝒫 | ) O(n|{\cal P}|) size bound (derived from the single-source setting) for reachability oracle and preserver for dual failures (or O ⁡ ( 2 k ​ n ​ | 𝒫 | ) O(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|) bound for k k failures). We answer this question affirmatively. Below we summarize our contributions. • For an n n -vertex directed graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and 𝒫 ⊆ V × V {\cal P}\subseteq V\times V , we present a construction of O ⁡ ( n ​ | 𝒫 | ) O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}) sized dual fault-tolerant pairwise reachability oracle with constant query time. We further provide a matching (up to the word size) lower bound of Ω ⁡ ( n ​ | 𝒫 | ) \Omega(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}) on the size (in bits) of the oracle for the dual fault setting, thereby proving that our oracle is (near-)optimal. • Next, we provide a construction of O ⁡ ( n + min ⁡ { | 𝒫 | ​ n , n ​ | 𝒫 | } ) O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},~n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}\}) sized oracle with O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) query time, resilient to single node/edge failure. In particular, for | 𝒫 | |{\cal P}| bounded by O ⁡ ( n ) O(\sqrt{n}) this yields an oracle of just O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) size. We complement the upper bound with a lower bound of Ω ⁡ ( n 2 / 3 ​ | 𝒫 | 1 / 2 ) \Omega(n^{2/3}|{\cal P}|^{1/2}) (in bits), refuting the possibility of a linear-sized oracle for 𝒫 {\cal P} of size ω ⁡ ( n 2 / 3 ) \omega(n^{2/3}) . • We also present a construction of O ⁡ ( n 4 / 3 ​ | 𝒫 | 1 / 3 ) O(n^{4/3}|{\cal P}|^{1/3}) sized pairwise reachability preservers resilient to dual edge/vertex failures. Previously, such preservers were known to exist only under single failure and had O ⁡ ( n + min ⁡ { | 𝒫 | ​ n , n ​ | 𝒫 | } ) O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},~n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}\}) size [Chakraborty and Choudhary, ICALP’20]. 1.1
Sensitivity Oracle

In the Sensitivity oracle, the goal is to design a data structure for a network prone to edge/vertex failures to efficiently answer queries pertaining to the graph structure (e.g., connectivity, reachability, distance, etc.).
We first formally define the notion of Fault-Tolerant Reachability Oracle (FTRO).

Definition 1.1

(
FTRO
)

Let

To date, no non-trivial bounds were known for FT-pairwise reachability oracle.
The only known results are for single-source setting (i.e.,

For single-source setting, i.e., when

1.2
Reachability Preservers

In the context of graph sparsification,

s
to
t
in
H

G

[
18
,
8
,
1
]

Let us formally define fault-tolerant reachability subgraph (
FTRS

Definition 1.2

(
FTRS
)
.

Let
{\cal P}\in V\times V
be any set of pairs of vertices. A subgraph
H
of
G

k
-Fault-Tolerant

of
G
for
{\cal P}

\textsc{$k$-FTRS}(G,{\cal P})
, if for any pair
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
and for any subset

F\subseteq E
of at most
k
edges,
t
is reachable from
s
in
G\setminus F
if and only if
t
is reachable from

s
in
H\setminus F
.

For the particular case of single-source, i.e.,
{\cal P}=\{s\}\times V
,
Baswana, Choudhary, and Roditty
[
4

provided a polynomial-time algorithm that, given any
n
-node directed graph, constructs an

-sized
k
-FTRS

For any directed graph

Technical Overview

Construction of a 2-
FTRS
for a single pair.

Our starting point is a simple construction of a linear (in the number of vertices) sized 2-
FTRS
for a single pair. Recall, we already know of such a subgraph by
[
4
]
. However, this new alternate construction will shed more light on the specific structure of a 2-
FTRS
, which will play a pivotal role in our constructions of pairwise 2-
FTRS
and 2-
FTRO
. Given a directed graph
G
and a vertex-pair

, we construct a subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
as follows: First, consider two
“maximally disjoint”

s-t
paths
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
(that meet only at the cut-edges and cut-vertices). We refer to these two paths as
outer strands
. Next, we add several
coupling paths
between these two outer strands, which are edge-disjoint with the outer strands. For each vertex
v
on the outer strands, we check for the
“earliest”
vertex on the strand
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
(and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
), from which there is a path
Q_{(s,t),v}^{1}
(and
Q_{(s,t),v}^{2}
) to
v
that is edge-disjoint with both the outer strands. We refer to these path
Q_{(s,t),v}^{i}
as coupling paths. Roughly speaking, two outer strands together with the coupling paths constitute the subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
(see Figure
1
). The actual construction is slightly different. Let us first briefly discuss why the above subgraph is a 2-
FTRS
for the pair
(s,t)
. Then we will comment on the issue with the above simple construction and how we overcome that.

, and the subpaths of

,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
below
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
. Since by assumption

does not form an

,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}

consists of all the coupling paths, we get a surviving path in

is a 2-

must be of the following form: It first follows one of the outer strand from

As we mentioned earlier, our actual construction is slightly different. The main issue with the above simple construction is that the constituted subgraph could be of size
\omega(n)
after adding all the coupling paths. To mitigate this issue, instead of adding all the coupling paths, we only add the

. The guarantee of the existence of nice paths also remains unaffected. Of course, the correctness argument will become slightly more intricate.

Next, we use the above construction of a 2-
FTRS
of a single pair to study the pairwise dual fault-tolerant graph structures (reachability oracle and preserver). Our input is a directed graph

with

.

Pairwise 2-
FTRO
: Upper bound.

One of the main contributions of this work is a construction of a dual fault-tolerant pairwise reachability oracle (2-
FTRO
) of size
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
. For simplicity, below, we briefly describe a construction that provides a slightly weaker bound, in particular,
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}\log n)

We start with the 2-
FTRS

H_{(s,t)}
, for each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
. First, for all
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, we consider the top and bottom
\Theta(n/\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})

,

Observe, for any two failure edges

,

(s,t)\in{\cal P}

Now, it only remains to consider the case when the surviving nice path in

\Theta(n/\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
length subpath of a outer strand. Informally, we only need to consider the scenario when both the outer strands are of length

During the query for a pair

Optimality of pairwise dual fault-tolerant oracle.

In this paper, we show that for pairwise 2-
FTRO
, our
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
bound is essentially tight up to the word size (Theorem
1.2
). Actually, we show that for any source-wise 2-
FTRO
for a designated source set
S
, the trivial
O(n|S|)
upper bound followed from
[
17
]
, is tight. To do that, we first provide a
\Omega(n|S|)
lower bound for source-wise 2-
FTRS
(leading to Theorem
1.6
) by constructing a hard instance. Then we extend that lower bound to source-wise 2-
FTRO
using communication complexity.

We construct the hard instance for 2-
FTRS
as follows. Take any integer
N,r
. We consider two
r
-sized sets of vertex-disjoint (directed) paths each of length
N
. Then include a (directed) complete bipartite graph between the left set of
r
vertices and the right set of
r
vertices, for each level
k\in[N]
(See Figure
3
). The set
S
contains the start vertices of the paths in the left set and the terminal vertices of the paths in the right set. So,
|S|=2r
. The number of vertices and the edges in this graph are
n=2Nr
and
\Theta(Nr^{2})
respectively. It is not difficult to observe that each edge in this graph must be present in any 2-
FTRS
for this graph with the pair set
{\cal P}=S\times S
(See Figure
3
). Consequently, we get a
\Omega(n|S|)=\Omega(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
lower bound.

Dual fault-tolerant reachability preserver.

We then extend the above construction to 2-
FTRO
. Note,
FTRO
is a Boolean data structure that only decides reachability between a pair of vertices - does not report a path (if exists). If the data structure also reports a path (if exists), then using a standard information-theoretic argument, it is possible to show that the data structure must be of a size equal to that of a
FTRS
. Such an argument does not work in general for
FTRO
. This is a general hurdle that we need to overcome to extend the lower of
FTRS
to
FTRO
. Fortunately, the hard instance we constructed for
FTRS
allows us to provide a reduction from (a close variant of) the

problem. Then we use the randomized one-way communication complexity lower bound for the Index problem
[
29
]
to get a lower bound of
\Omega(n|S|)
on the size (in bits) of any 2-
FTRO
. It is worth mentioning that our lower bound holds irrespective of the query time of 2-
FTRO
.

Pairwise 1-
FTRO
: Upper and lower bound.

The above lower bound only holds for dual failures. So it remains open whether for single failure we can attain better than
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
size bound with
o(n)
(ideally, constant) query time. In this section, we provide a construction of 1-
FTRO
of size
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
and query time
O(1)
. We show the construction by first designing a reachability oracle of size
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
resilient to single vertex failure. Then we extend that vertex fault-tolerant data structure to an edge fault-tolerant data structure of the same size.

One of the key ingredients of our construction is a clever data structure that could answer all-pairs reachability query under single vertex failure as long as all three vertices involved in the query belong to a cut-set. Formally, for a pair
(s,t)
, let
C
be any subset of
s-t
cut-vertices in the graph
G
. We build an
O(|C|)
size data structure that, for any three vertices
x,y,z\in C
, decides the reachability between
y,z
upon failure of vertex
x
, in constant time. This data structure is inspired by the loop-nesting-forest
[
38
]
. We consider the ordering
\sigma
among the cut-vertices in
C
. Next, we build a predecessor forest and a successor forest with respect to this ordering
\sigma
. In the predecessor forest, the parent of any node
w
is the immediate predecessor
u
such that
u,w
are strongly connected even after the removal of all the predecessors of
u
from
G
. We symmetrically build the successor forest. Using constantly many Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) and Level Ancestor (LA) queries on these forests, we can now decide the reachability between
y,z
upon failure of the vertex
x
(for any
x,y,z\in C
). By deploying any standard linear space LCA/LA data structure, we attain
O(|C|)
space-bound and
O(1)
query time. We refer the readers to Section
5.1
for the details.

Next, we use the above data structure to construct a pairwise fault-tolerant reachability oracle for single vertex failure. Observe, to decide the reachability between a pair upon failure, we need to check whether the failure vertex is a cut-vertex for that pair or not. At the high level, we need to keep the information about the cut-vertex-set for each pair. However, we cannot store them explicitly using small space. We first consider the cut-vertex-set for all the pairs in
{\cal P}
. Then we identify a
core pair-set
as follows: Take any pair in
{\cal P}
with at least
\sqrt{n}
cut-vertices, and add it into the core pair-set. Then iteratively add pairs from
{\cal P}
with at least
\sqrt{n}
new/uncovered (not part of the cut-vertex-set of any previously added pair) cut-vertices. For each pair added in the core pair-set, it
owns
the corresponding cut-vertices that were uncovered till then. Once we get the core pair-set, we are left with pairs each having at most
\sqrt{n}
uncovered cut-vertices. Further note, the size of the core pair-set is at most
O(\sqrt{n})
. Consider the union of all the cut-vertices of the core pair-set, and build the previously mentioned data structure on it. For the remaining pairs, we store the uncovered cut-vertices associated with them using any static dictionary data structure. Note, each pair might have cut-vertices that are owned by some core pair. To keep track of them, for each pair and each core pair, we store the first and the last cut-vertex shared by them. These all constitute the final data structure. It is not hard to see that the size is
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
. We show that it suffices to either perform a query on the previously mentioned data structure or check whether the failure vertex is a cut-vertex in the dictionary structure during the query. (See Section
5.2
.)

Now, we extend the vertex fault-tolerant oracle to edge fault-tolerant oracle. We consider the set
{\cal C}
of all the cut-edges for all the pairs in
{\cal P}
. Then we find the subset
{\cal C}_{0}
that only contains the edges whose endpoints are strongly connected in
G
, but not after the failure of that edge. Observe, all the edges in
{\cal C}
must be part of any strong-connectivity certificate, and all the edges in
{\cal C}_{0}
must be part of any reachability preserver without any failure. Thus
|{\cal C}|=O(n)
and
|{\cal C}_{0}|=O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},(n|{\cal P}|)^{2/3}\})
by
[
1
]
. Next, we construct a new graph with
n+|{\cal C}_{0}|
vertices such that checking the reachability upon an edge failure in the original graph reduces to checking the reachability (perhaps between a different pair) upon vertex failure in the new graph. As a consequence, we get an
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
sized 1-
FTRO
with constant query time.

We complement our upper bound result with a lower bound of
\Omega(n^{2/{d+1}}|{\cal P}|^{(d-1)/d})
size (in bits) for any
d\geq 2
. We prove our lower bound by establishing a connection between the optimal size of a pairwise
k
-FTRO
and that of a pairwise
(k-1)
-
FTRS
. In particular, we show that the optimal size of
FTRO
for any
n
-node graph and
p
-sized pair-set is at least that of
(k-1)
-
FTRS
for any
n/2
-node graph and
p
-sized pair-set (Theorem
8.5
). Such a connection between
FTRO
and
FTRS
is entirely new. To prove this relation, we use information-theoretic encoding-decoding argument. (See Section
8.3
.) Then the lower bound of 1-
FTRO
follows from the lower bound of reachability preserver without any failure by
[
1
]
.

Pairwise 2-
FTRS
: Upper bound.

We have already shown a lower bound of
\Omega(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
on the size of a pairwise 2-

described earlier. Before proceeding further, let us emphasize that the main underlying idea behind our construction could be generalized to

To get a sparse 2-

for each of the pairs of

[

u
lying on the top
\Theta(n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3})
length subpath of a outer strand.

If we could include the top and bottom
\Theta(n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3})

\Theta(n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3})

), and let us denote that by

“essential”

“small”
(at most
O(n^{1/3}|P|^{1/3})
). Next, for each of the high-frequency vertices, compute linear-sized single-source and single-destination 2-

, and take a union of them to form a subgraph

for

. It is worth mentioning that the correctness proof works only because of a guarantee of the existence of the nice path. Note, a nice path follows at most one coupling path as a subpath. Thus either that nice path follows the top or bottom
\Theta(n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3})
portion of the outer strands, or the coupling sub-path is part of

and
H_{4}

Observe, each of the

O(n^{4/3}|P|^{1/3}\log n)
.

Pairwise
k
-FTRS
: Beating the
O(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|)
bound.

Currently, for any
k\geq 1
, we only know there always exists a pairwise
k
-FTRS
of size
O(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|)
for any pair set
{\cal P}

[
4
]

. We cannot directly extend that bound for
k
-FTRS
. The main obstacle is that for pairwise 2-
FTRS
, we have used a particular structure of a 2-
FTRS
for a single pair (i.e., the subgraph

). It is pretty difficult get such kind of structure for
k
-FTRS
for any

. However, the main underlying idea behind our pairwise 2-
FTRS
construction is to handle the
“long”
and
“short”
surviving paths separately. Informally, in the case of 2-
FTRS
, the hitting set helps us to look into only the short paths in

. We do a similar thing for
k
-FTRS
using randomization.

Take a parameter
\ell
, whose value we will fix later. We sample a uniformly random subset

of vertices of size
\tilde{O}(kn/\ell)
. Then we build a single-source and single-destination
k
-FTRS
from those vertices. Let us denote the union of all these
k
-FTRS

. The size of

is
O(k2^{k}n^{2}/\ell)
by
[

]

of size at most

, the length of any shortest
s-t
surviving path in

is at least
\ell

s-t

(which is of length at least
\ell

intersects that path with high probability (Lemma

. This part is similar to what we get in 2-

using greedy hitting set.

So now on, we only need to handle the case when a shortest surviving path is of length at least

. To do this, roughly speaking, we enumerate over all possible failure-set of size at most
k
and add a shortest surviving path in the subgraph. Trivially, we get a bound of only
O(n^{k})
on the number of possible failure edge-sets of size at most
k
. However, observe, we only need to handle the case when a surviving shortest path is of length at most
\ell
. So before the failure also the length of a shortest path was at most
\ell
. The initial shortest path (before any failure) will get destroyed only if one or more failure edges lie on that shortest path. This observation reduces the number of possible failure-set to
O(\ell^{k})
. So, for each pair, we add
O(\ell^{k})
paths, each of length at most
\ell
. For all the pairs, the total number of added edges is at most
O(\ell^{k+1}|{\cal P}|)
. We can further improve this bound to
O(\ell^{k}|{\cal P}|)
by enumerating the failure-set of size up to
k-1
and then adding two maximally edge-disjoint shortest paths. (See Section
7
for the details.) At the end, we get a subgraph of size
O(k2^{k}n^{2}/\ell+\ell^{k}|{\cal P}|)
. By optimizing the parameter
\ell
, we get a size bound of
\widetilde{O}(k~2^{k}~n^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}~|{\cal P}|^{\frac{1}{k+1}})
. Note, this size bound beats the
O(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|)
bound whenever
|{\cal P}|=\omega(kn^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\log n)
.

1.5
Conclusion

In this paper, we study compact oracle and sparse preservers for the problem of pairwise reachability under failures. For dual failures, we provide a construction of
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
sized reachability oracle
that has constant query time, along with a matching (up to the word size) lower bound. It (almost) settles down the question on the optimal sized dual fault-tolerant pairwise reachability oracle. For single failure, we achieve a bound of
O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}\})
on the size of oracle with constant query time. We complement our upper bound with a
\Omega(n^{2/3}|{\cal P}|^{1/2})
size (in bits) lower bound, refuting the possibility of getting a linear-sized oracle for a single failure. We would like to pose the problem of closing the current gap between the upper and lower bound for a single fault-tolerant oracle as an open problem.

In the case of reachability preserver, we show an upper bound of
O(n^{4/3}|{\cal P}|^{1/3})
edges for any
n
-node graph and a vertex-pair set
{\cal P}
, for dual failures setting. This improves the naive bound of
O(n|{\cal P}|)
preserver (obtained from the result known for single-source setting). In addition, we obtain a lower bound of
\Omega(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
on the size of our reachability preserver under dual failures. Prior to our work it was known that for single failure, we can have preserver with
O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},~n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}\})
edges. Thus our lower bound provides a striking difference between the single and dual fault-tolerant setting as it gives a separation of
n^{1/4}
factor for
|{\cal P}|=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
. One immediate open question after our work is whether a sparser pairwise reachability preserver exists for dual failures.

Next, we go beyond the dual failures and consider the question of getting sparse pairwise reachability preserver resilient up to
k
failures for any
k\geq 1
. Can there always exist a pairwise preserver of size
o(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|)
for any
k
failures? We answer this question affirmatively by providing a randomized polynomial-time construction of a
o(2^{k}n|{\cal P}|)
size preserver. We leave the question of finding an optimal
k
fault-tolerant pairwise preserver as an important open problem.

Static Dictionary Data Structure.

One of the classic data structure problems is the

. In this problem, we are given a set
S
of items, where each item is a (key,value) pair. Keys are from an arbitrary universe
U
. The task is to build a data structure that can efficiently answer the following query.

Search(
k
): For a given
k
, if the key
k
is present in
S
, return the value associated with the key
k
in
S
; otherwise return NULL.

The celebrated result by Fredman, Komlós and Szemerédi
[
26
]
provide a data structure of size
O(|S|)
with worst-case query (search) time
O(1)
. Since then various randomized and deterministic schemes have been developed for this problem. Currently, the best deterministic data structure attains
O(|S|)
space bound and
O(1)
query time. One of the key ingredients of our constructions is this data structure of the (static) dictionary problem. In particular, we use the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
37
]
)
.

Given any set
S
, there is a data structure of size
O(|S|)
, for the dictionary problem, that can answer each search query in worst-case
O(1)
time. Moreover, the data structure can be constructed in deterministic polynomial (in
|S|
) time.

Fractional Hitting Set.

Let
U:=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
be the universe. A set
S\subseteq U
is said to be a
hitting set
of a family of subsets
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{m}\subseteq U
if and only if for all
i\in[m]
,
S\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset
(i.e.,
S
intersects all the subsets
S_{i}
). Given a family of subsets, the problem of finding the minimum sized hitting set is referred to the
hitting set problem
, and is known to be NP-complete. However, there is a simple greedy algorithm that given any family of subsets
S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}
each of size at least
k
, finds a hitting set of size at most
O(\frac{n}{k}\log n)
.

In this paper, we consider a variant of the hitting set problem, which we call
fractional hitting set
. A set
S\subseteq U
is said to be a hitting set with slack of a family of subsets
S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}
if and only if
|\{i\in[m]\mid S\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset\}|=\Omega(m)
(i.e.,
S
has a non-empty intersection with at least a constant fraction of the input subsets). It is not difficult to see that the above mentioned greedy hitting set algorithm finds a fractional hitting set of size at most
O(n/k)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

There is a deterministic algorithm that given a family of subsets
S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}\subseteq[n]
each of size at least
k
, finds a fractional hitting set of size at most
O(n/k)
in time
O(n+mk+\frac{n}{k}\log n)
.

Proof.

W.l.o.g. assume, each
S_{i}
is of size exactly
k
; otherwise we can just consider any
k
elements from each
S_{i}
. Let us now consider a slightly modified version of the standard greedy algorithm for the hitting set problem. Initialize
{\cal F}:=\{S_{1},\cdots,S_{m}\}
, and
S=\emptyset
. For each
v\in[n]
, compute the number of subsets in
{\cal F}
that contains
v
, i.e.,
c(v):=|\{S_{i}\in{\cal F}\mid v\in S_{i}\}|
. Let
v_{max}
be a maximizer of
c(v)
. Include
v_{max}
in
S
, i.e,
S\leftarrow S\cup\{v_{max}\}
. Remove all the subsets that contains
v_{max}
from
{\cal F}
, i.e.,
{\cal F}\leftarrow{\cal F}\setminus\{S_{i}\mid v_{max}\in S_{i}\}
. Continue this process
R=4n/k
times, and output
S
. (Note, instead to continuing the process until
{\cal F}=\emptyset
as in the standard greedy algorithm for the hitting set problem, we terminate after a fixed number of steps.)

Clearly,
|S|=R=O(n/k)
. We would like to claim that
S
is a fractional hitting set. For that purpose, it suffices to show that at the end of the algorithm
|{\cal F}|\leq m/10
. Let
n_{j}
denote the size of
{\cal F}
after the
j
-th element is added in
S
.
n_{0}=m
, and we need to show that
n_{R}\leq m/10
. Consider the step when the
j
-th element has been added in
S
. Observe,
\sum_{v\in[n]\setminus H}c(v)=n_{j-1}k
, and thus by a simple averaging, an element
v_{max}^{j}
that maximizes
c(v)
must satisfy
c(v_{max}^{j})\geq\frac{n_{j-1}k}{n-j+1}
. Thus,

n_{j}\leq\Big(1-\frac{k}{n-j+1}\Big)n_{j-1}\leq m\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}\Big(1-\frac{k}{n-r}\Big)<m(1-k/n)^{j}\leq me^{-kj/n}.

Then for
R=4n/k
,
n_{R}<m/10
.

The running time of the modified algorithm is bounded by the standard greedy algorithm for the hitting set problem. For the sake of completeness, let us briefly comment on the running time analysis. At the beginning we can compute the values of
c(v)
’s, and then store them using any standard heap data structure (e.g.
[
11
]
). Then at each step, we extract the max value, and then we update the values of the others. This gives the total time to be
O(n+mk+\frac{n}{k}\log n)
.
∎

Sparsifiers with Slack.

In case of pairwise FTRO (FTRS) we want to construct an oracle (subgraph) that provides correct reachability information for all the given node-pairs even after edge failures. This is the most standard definition of pairwise FTRO (FTRS). Next, we define a variant which we refer to as
pairwise FTRO (FTRS) with slack
. Interested readers may find more works on sparsifiers with slack in
[
13
,
28
,
21
,
9
]
. Given a graph
G
and a pair set
{\cal P}
, a data structure
{\cal O}
(subgraph
H
) is a
k
-FTRO
with slack (
k
-FTRS
with slack) if there is
{\cal P}^{\prime}\subseteq{\cal P}
,
|{\cal P}^{\prime}|=\Omega(|{\cal P}|)
such that
{\cal O}
(
H
) is a
k
-FTRO
(
k
-FTRS
) for the pair set
{\cal P}^{\prime}
.

The following lemma from
[
9
]
show that to construct a
k
-FTRS
for a pair set it suffices to construct a
k
-FTRS
with slack.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let us consider constants
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
and a parameter
p^{*}
. Consider a graph
G(V,E)
with
n
nodes. If

•

There is a
k
-FTRS
of size
O(n^{\alpha})
for any node-pair set
{\cal P}
of size at most
p^{*}
, and

•

There is a
k
-FTRS
with slack of size
O(n^{\beta}|{\cal P}|^{\gamma})
for any node-pair set
{\cal P}
of size at least
p^{*}
,

then there is a
\textsc{$k$-FTRS}(G,{\cal P})
of size at most
O(n^{\alpha}+n^{\beta}|{\cal P}|^{\gamma})
. Moreover, if the
k
-FTRS
for at most
p^{*}
pairs and the
k
-FTRS
with slack for at least
p^{*}
pairs can be computed in polynomial time, then the
\textsc{$k$-FTRS}(G,{\cal P})
can also be computed in polynomial time.

A similar claim also holds for oracles. The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
2.5
in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let us consider constants
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\kappa,\nu>0
and a parameter
p^{*}
. Consider a graph
G(V,E)
with
n
nodes. If

•

There is a
k
-FTRO
of size
O(n^{\alpha})
and query time
O(n^{\delta})
, for any node-pair set
{\cal P}
of size at most
p^{*}
, and

•

There is a

-FTRO
with slack of size
O(n^{\beta}|{\cal P}|^{\gamma})
and query time

of size at least

then there is a
\textsc{$k$-FTRO}(G,{\cal P})
of size at most

. Moreover, if the

-FTRO
for at most

is because the final

-FTRO

to specify which data structure to query.

3
An (Alternate) Construction of 2-
FTRS
for a Single Pair

Given any directed graph
G
with
n
vertices, for any pair of vertices
(s,t)
, we already get an
O(n)
size 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
from
[
4
]
(see Theorem
2.1
). However, now we study the structure of a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
more closely and provide a slightly different construction which will be useful for our constructions of 2-
FTRO
and 2-
FTRS
for multiple pairs. Let us first define a notation to denote the ordering of vertices on a specific path. For any path
P
and two vertices
u,v
on
P
, we will write
u<_{P}v
if and only if
u
appears before
v
on
P
.

Suppose we are given a directed graph
G
and a pair
(s,t)
. Let us consider two
s-t
paths
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, that intersect only at
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices. We call these two paths
outer strands
. Next, we define coupling paths between these two outer strands. For each vertex on the outer strands, let us first define two
coupling points
of it (one on each outer strand). Consider a vertex
v\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2})
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then consider the vertex
u_{(s,t),v}^{i}\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}
(if exists) for which there is a
u_{(s,t),v}^{i}-v
path that is edge-disjoint with both the outer strands, and there is no vertex
u^{\prime}\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}
such that
u^{\prime}<_{P_{(s,t)}^{i}}u_{(s,t),v}^{i}
and there is a
u^{\prime}-v
path that is edge-disjoint with both the outer strands. We refer to
u_{(s,t),v}^{1},u_{(s,t),v}^{2}
as
coupling points
of
v
. For each
v
on the outer strands and each coupling point
u_{(s,t),v}^{i}
of it, consider any arbitrary
u_{(s,t),v}^{i}-v
path (denoted as
Q_{(s,t),v}^{i}
) of
G
, that is edge-disjoint with both the outer strands. We refer to the path
Q_{(s,t),v}^{i}
as a
coupling path
to
v
. Note, for each vertex
v
on the outer strands, there are at most two coupling points of it (one on each outer strand) and from each coupling point there is a coupling path to
v
. We take two outer strands and some of the coupling paths to build a subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
. More specifically, for a vertex
v\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j})
, if for all the vertices below
v
on that strand, their coupling points on a strand
P_{(s,t)}^{i}
is below the coupling point of
v
on that strand, then only add the coupling path from the strand
P_{(s,t)}^{i}
to
v
. Later, we prove that keeping these
“essential”
coupling paths is sufficient.

Below we provide a formal description of the procedure to construct
H_{(s,t)}
. Given
G
and a pair
(s,t)
, we build a subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
as follows:

1.

V(H_{(s,t)})=V(G)
.

2.

Add all the edges on the paths
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
(two outer strands) to
H_{(s,t)}
.

3.

For all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
and
v\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j})

(a)

If for all
v^{\prime}\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j}[v-t])
,
u_{(s,t),v}^{i}<_{P_{(s,t)}^{i}}u_{(s,t),v^{\prime}}^{i}
, then take the coupling path
Q_{(s,t),v}^{i}
, and add all its edges to
H_{(s,t)}
.

Readers may refer to Figure
1
for an example subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
. Note, the above construction procedure of the subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
runs in polynomial time.

Before arguing that
H_{(s,t)}
is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
, let us introduce a notion of
nice path
. We call an
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}

nice
if it first follows a outer strand till some vertex
u
, then take a coupling path to some vertex
u^{\prime}
, and finally follows the outer strand on which
u^{\prime}
lies till
t
. More formally, an
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}
is nice if and only if it is of the form
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]\circ Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}\circ P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
, for some
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, where subpath
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
starts from
u
and is edge-disjoint with both the outer strands
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
. (
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
could be empty.)

The following claim shows that
H_{(s,t)}
is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
, and also will be useful in the subsequent sections while arguing about the correctness of our pairwise 2-
FTRO
and 2-
FTRS
construction.

Claim 3.1
.

For any two edges
f_{1},f_{2}
, if there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, then there must be a nice
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
.

Proof.

First of all, one of
f_{1},f_{2}
must be on
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and another on
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
. Otherwise, since
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
intersect only at
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices, at least one of
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
remains intact in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, and thus there is nothing to prove. So from now on without loss of generality, assume,
f_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1})
lies on
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
f_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})
lies on
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
.

Let
P
be an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Observe,
P
must intersect either
P_{(s,t)}^{1}[y_{1}-t]
or
P_{(s,t)}^{2}[y_{2}-t]
. Let
z^{\prime}
be the first vertex on
P
, that is also in
V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}[y_{1}-t]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2}[y_{2}-t])
(such a vertex exists because
P
,
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
all end at

). Let
z^{\prime}
lie on
P_{(s,t)}^{j}
, for some
j\in\{1,2\}
. Now consider the last vertex before
z^{\prime}
on
P
, that is also in
V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2})
(such a vertex exists because
P
,
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
all start from
s
), and let
z
be that vertex which is on
P_{(s,t)}^{i}
, for some
i\in\{1,2\}
. (
i
could be equal to
j
.) Observe,
z
must lie on the subpath
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-x_{i}]
. Furthermore,
P[z-z^{\prime}]
is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
. Hence, by the construction of

, there must be a path
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
for some

which starts from a vertex
u<_{P_{(s,t)}^{i}}z<_{P_{(s,t)}^{i}}x_{i}
(

could be equal to
z
). Hence,

remains intact in

. Note,
u^{\prime}\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j}[y_{j}-t]
(because
u^{\prime}\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j}[z^{\prime}-t])
). Thus the subpath
P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
also remains intact in

. Lastly, since

is edge-disjoint with

and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, it also remains intact in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Hence, we get an

path
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]\circ Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}\circ P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
in

.
∎

Next, let us use the above claim to conclude that
H_{(s,t)}
is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
. Furthermore, we also argue that the size of
H_{(s,t)}
is
O(n)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

H_{(s,t)}
is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
and contains
O(n)
edges.

Proof.

Since
H_{(s,t)}
is a subgraph of
G
, from Claim
3.1
, we conclude that for any two edges
f_{1},f_{2}
, there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
if and only if there is an
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. So,
H_{(s,t)}
is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
.

Let us now compute the size of
H_{(s,t)}
. First, we would like to claim that for each vertex
v\in V(H_{(s,t)})\setminus V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2})
, in-degree of
v
in
H_{(s,t)}
is at most 4. If not, then there exists a vertex
v\in V(H_{(s,t)})\setminus V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2})
with at least five incoming edges
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}
. Suppose during the construction of
H_{(s,t)}
, for each
r\in[5]
,
e_{r}
has been included while adding a path
Q_{(s,t),v_{r}}^{i_{r}}
, where
v_{r}\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{j_{r}})
. Since for each
r\in[5]
,
i_{r},j_{r}\in\{1,2\}
, by the pigeonhole principle, there must exist
r\neq r^{\prime}\in[5]
such that
i_{r}=i_{r^{\prime}}(=i)
and
j_{r}=j_{r^{\prime}}(=j)
. Without loss of generality, assume,
v_{r}<_{P_{s,t}^{j}}v_{r^{\prime}}
. Since
Q_{(s,t),v_{r}}^{i}
and
Q_{(s,t),v_{r^{\prime}}}^{i}
intersect at
v
, either
u_{(s,t),v_{r^{\prime}}}^{i}=u_{(s,t),v_{r}}^{i}
or
u_{(s,t),v_{r^{\prime}}}^{i}<_{P_{(s,t)}^{i}}u_{(s,t),v_{r}}^{i}
. Hence, by the Step 3(a) of the construction procedure of
H_{(s,t)}
,
Q_{(s,t),v_{r}}^{i_{r}}
would not be added, which leads to a contradiction.

Observe, by the construction of
H_{(s,t)}
, in-degree of each
v\in V(P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2})
is also at most 4. Hence,
H_{(s,t)}
has total
O(n)
edges.
∎

4
Dual Fault-tolerant Pairwise Reachability Oracle

In this section, we present a construction of a dual fault-tolerant reachability oracle (2-
FTRO
) for a given
n
vertex (directed) graph
G=(V,E)
and a vertex-pair set
{\cal P}\subseteq V\times V
. The size of the data structure/oracle is
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
. Upon given any two failure edges
f_{1},f_{2}
and a pair
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, the oracle correctly decides whether there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
in
O(1)
time. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Here we provide a construction of 2-
FTRO
with slack, which together with Lemma
2.6
shows Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

A directed graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
vertices can be processed in randomized polynomial time for a given set
{\cal P}\subseteq V\times V
of vertex-pairs, to build a 2-
FTRO
with slack of size
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
, having query time
O(1)
.

Let us start with the description of the oracle with slack. Below we will use the notations
H_{(s,t)},P_{(s,t)}^{i},Q_{(s,t),v}^{j}
defined in Section
3
.

Procedure to Construct a 2-
FTRO
with slack for the input
(G,{\cal P})
:

1.

First, for all the pairs
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, construct
H_{(s,t)}=2\text{-}\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
using the construction provided in Section
3
. (Recall,
H_{(s,t)}
contains two
s-t
paths
P^{1}_{(s,t)}
and
P^{2}_{(s,t)}
, that intersect only at
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices.)

2.

Consider the following family of subsets
{\cal F}:=\{V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[L]),V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[-L])\mid i\in\{1,2\},(s,t)\in{\cal P}\}
, where
L=\Theta(n/\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
. Construct a fractional hitting set
S
of
{\cal F}
(by Theorem
2.4
). Let
{\cal Q}:=\{(s,t)\in{\cal P}\mid\forall_{i\in\{1,2\}}V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[L])\cap S\neq\emptyset\text{ and }V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[-L])\cap S\neq\emptyset\}
.

3.

For each
v\in S
, construct a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,\{v\}\times V)

O_{v}
and a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,V\times\{v\})

I_{v}
(using Theorem
2.2
), and store them.

4.

For each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, maintain a list
T_{(s,t)}
of size 4, that stores an (arbitrarily chosen) vertex from the set
P^{i}_{(s,t)}[L]\cap S
and an (arbitrarily chosen) vertex from the set
P^{i}_{(s,t)}[-L]\cap S
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
.

5.

For each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, let
M_{(s,t)}=V\big(\bigcup_{i\in\{1,2\}}(P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L])\big)
. Build a dictionary data structure
\mathcal{M}_{(s,t)}
for the set
M_{(s,t)}
(using Theorem
2.3
).

6.

For each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, construct the following auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
(see Figure
2
):

(a)

Vertex set of
A_{(s,t)}
is
V\big(\bigcup_{i\in\{1,2\}}(P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L])\big)
.

(b)

For each edge in
E\big(\bigcup_{i\in\{1,2\}}(P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L])\big)
, add an edge to
A_{(s,t)}
. We refer these edges as
path edges
.

(c)

For all pair of vertices
u,v\in V(A_{(s,t)})
, if there is a
u-v
path in
H_{(s,t)}
, that is edge-disjoint with
P^{1}_{(s,t)}
and
P^{2}_{(s,t)}
, then add an edge
(u,v)
to
A_{(s,t)}
. We refer these edges as
auxiliary edges
.

7.

For each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, construct a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(A_{(s,t)},s)

D_{(s,t)}
.

8.

The final data structure consists of
I_{v},O_{v}
for each
v\in S
, and for each pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, the list
T_{(s,t)}
, the dictionary data structure
\mathcal{M}_{(s,t)}
, and
D_{(s,t)}
.

We will show that the data structure constructed using the above procedure is a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,{\cal Q})
. Given any two edges
f_{1},f_{2}
and a pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, the query algorithm works as follows:

Query Algorithm (
f_{1},f_{2},(s,t)
):

1.

For each vertex
v\in T_{(s,t)}
, check if there is an
s-v
and
v-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
using
I_{v}
and
O_{v}
respectively. If for at least one vertex
v\in T_{(s,t)}
, both
s-v
and
v-t
path exist, then return
“there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
”
.

2.

Otherwise, check whether any of the endpoints of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is not present in
\mathcal{M}_{(s,t)}
. If any of them is not, discard that edge and continue to Step 3. Let
F\subseteq\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
be the set of remaining edges after the above pruning.

3.

Use the oracle
D_{(s,t)}
to decide whether there is an
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. If yes, then return
“there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
”
; else return
“there is no
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
”
.

Note, the sole purpose of step 2 of the above query algorithm is to make sure the edges
f_{1},f_{2}
are defined over the set of vertices present in the auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
. If any of them is not, then there is no point in deleting that edge from
A_{(s,t)}
(at step 3).

Size of the data structure.

By Theorem
2.4
,
|S|=O(\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
. For a vertex
v\in S
, to store
I_{v},O_{v}
we need
O(n)
space by Theorem
2.2
. So, for all the
I_{v}
’s and
O_{v}
’s, we need
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
space. To store the lists
T_{(s,t)}
, for all
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, we need
O(|{\cal Q}|)
space. Since for a pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, the set
M_{(s,t)}
is of size at most
O(L)
, the dictionary data structure
\mathcal{M}_{(s,t)}
requires
O(L)
space using Theorem
2.3
. Next, observe, for each
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
, the auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
contains at most
O(L)
vertices and thus at most
O(L^{2})
edges. However, we do not store
A_{(s,t)}
as part of the data structure. Instead, we just store
D_{(s,t)}=2\text{-}\textsc{FTRO}(A_{(s,t)},s)
, which requires
O(L)
space again by Theorem
2.2
. So, the total space required by the whole data structure is
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|}+L\cdot|{\cal Q}|)=O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
, for
L=\Theta(n/\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
(note,
|{\cal Q}|\leq|{\cal P}|\leq n^{2}
).

Query time.

By Theorem
2.2
, each of
I_{v},O_{v}
and
D_{(s,t)}
has query time
O(1)
. Checking the presence of the end points of
f_{1},f_{2}
in the data structure
\mathcal{M}_{(s,t)}
also requires
O(1)
time by Theorem
2.3
. Thus the total query time is also
O(1)
.

Correctness of pairwise 2-
FTRO
with slack.

Proving the constructed data structure is a 2-
FTRO
with slack consists of two steps. First, we show that the constructed data structure is a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,{\cal Q})
. Second, we show that
|{\cal Q}|=\Omega(|{\cal P}|)
. The second part is quite straightforward. By the definition of fractional hitting set,
S
intersects all but
1/10
fraction of the subsets of
{\cal F}
. Hence,
|{\cal Q}|\geq\frac{3}{5}|{\cal P}|
. So it only remains to show the first part, for which we need to show that the query algorithm is correct for any pair in
{\cal Q}
.

Now, to prove the correctness of the query algorithm, it suffices to show the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any pair
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
and two edges
f_{1},f_{2}
, there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
if and only if

(a)

Either
\exists v\in T_{(s,t)}
such that there is an
s-v
and
v-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
,

(b)

Or, there is an
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
.

Note,
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
may not lie on the auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
. In case an edge
f
does not belong to
A_{(s,t)}
,
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f\}=A_{(s,t)}
. Before proving Lemma
4.2
, let us now argue on the correctness of the query algorithm by assuming this lemma. Observe, step 1 of the query algorithm checks whether
\exists v\in T_{(s,t)}
such that there is an
s-v
and
v-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Step 2 prunes the edges from
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
if some of them are not defined over the vertices of
A_{(s,t)}
, and let
F\subseteq\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
be the remaining set of edges after the pruning. Then in step 3, we decide whether there is an
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus F
. It is thus immediate that the correctness of the query algorithm directly follows from Lemma
4.2
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Consider the subgraph
H_{(s,t)}
, which is a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
(that we have constructed in the step 1 of the oracle construction procedure).

(
\Rightarrow
)
Suppose there is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. By Claim
3.1
, there must be a nice
s-t
path
R
in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. By definition of nice path,
R
would be of the form
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]\circ Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}\circ P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
, for some
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, where the subpath
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
starts from the vertex
u
and is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
. (
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
could be empty; in that case,
R=P_{(s,t)}^{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
.) By the definition of
{\cal Q}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cap S\neq\emptyset
and
P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L]\cap S\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
v\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cap S
and
v^{\prime}\in P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L]\cap S
are stored in
T_{(s,t)}
. Now, if
u
does not lie on
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]
, i.e., the length of the subpath
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]
is at least
L
, then
R
must pass through
v\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cap S
. Similarly, if
u^{\prime}
does not lie on
P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L]
, then
R
must pass through
v^{\prime}\in P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L]\cap S
. In both the scenarios, for some
v\in T_{(s,t)}
, there is an
s-v
and
v-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
(and thus in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
).

So let us now focus on the case when
u
lies on
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]
and
u^{\prime}
lies on
P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L]
. Then by the construction of the auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
,
u,u^{\prime}\in V(A_{(s,t)})
. Moreover, since
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1},P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, the edge
(u,u^{\prime})
is present in
A_{(s,t)}
. Hence, the path
R_{A}=P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]\circ(u,u^{\prime})\circ P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
is an
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}
. Observe,
f_{1},f_{2}\not\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-u]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{j}[u^{\prime}-t]
. So,
R_{A}
survives in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
if and only if
f_{1}
(or
f_{2}
) is not the edge
(u,u^{\prime})
. Note, it is possible to have
f_{1}
(or
f_{2}
) same as the edge
(u,u^{\prime})
and still the
u-u^{\prime}
subpath
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
exists in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. (This is because in
G
, and also in
H_{(s,t)}
, there can be an edge
(u,u^{\prime})
and also another path
Q_{(s,t),u^{\prime}}^{i}
that is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1},P_{(s,t)}^{2}
.) Recall,
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
,
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
intersect only at the
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices. So, if
f_{1}
(or
f_{2}
) is the edge
(u,u^{\prime})
, since
(u,u^{\prime})\not\in P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, either
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
or
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
survives in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Let
P_{(s,t)}^{i}
be a surviving path. If that path is of length more than
L
, then the Case (a) happens. Otherwise,
P_{(s,t)}^{i}
is included in the auxiliary graph
A_{(s,t)}
(by the construction) and thus also part of
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
.

(
\Leftarrow
)
If
\exists v\in T_{(s,t)}
such that there is an
s-v
path
R_{1}
and
v-t
path
R_{2}
in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, then clearly the path
R_{1}\circ R_{2}
is an
s-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
.

Now, suppose
\nexists v\in T_{(s,t)}
such that there is an
s-v
and
v-t
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
, but there is an
s-t
path
R^{\prime}
in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Then we have to show that there must also be an
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Let us denote the path
R^{\prime}
as
R^{\prime}_{1}\circ(u_{1},v_{1})\circ R^{\prime}_{2}\circ(u_{2},v_{2})\circ\cdots\circ R^{\prime}_{\ell}\circ(u_{\ell},v_{\ell})\circ R^{\prime}_{\ell+1}
, where each
R^{\prime}_{j}
consists of only the path edges (that means, is a subpath of either
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
or
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
), and
(u_{j},v_{j})
is some auxiliary edge of
A_{(s,t)}
. (Any of the
R_{j}
’s could be empty.) Now, if both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
lie on
P_{(s,t)}^{1}\cup P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, then from
R^{\prime}
we deduce a (directed) walk from
s
to
t
in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
as follows:
R^{\prime}_{1}\circ R_{(u_{1},v_{1})}\circ R^{\prime}_{2}\circ R_{(u_{2},v_{2})}\circ\cdots\circ R^{\prime}_{\ell}\circ R_{(u_{\ell},v_{\ell})}\circ R^{\prime}_{\ell+1}
, where
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
denote a
u_{j}-v_{j}
path in
H_{(s,t)}
that is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
. Such a path
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
exists; otherwise there would not be an auxiliary edge
(u_{j},v_{j})
in
A_{(s,t)}
(by the construction). From the above directed walk, we can easily get an
s-t
path
R
using a standard process of removing the walk-segment between two repeated occurrences of the same vertex.

Clearly,
R
is a valid
s-t
path in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
when all the subpaths
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
remain intact in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Suppose,
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
, for some
j\in[\ell]
, contains
f_{1}
(or
f_{2}
) and thus gets destroyed in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. In that case, we claim that either
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
or
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
remains intact. The reason is as follows: First of all, if
(a,b)
is an
s-t
cut-edge, then there is no path from a vertex
u\in P_{(s,t)}^{i}[s-a]
to a vertex
u^{\prime}\in P_{(s,t)}^{j}[b-t]
for any
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, that is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, and thus there is no auxiliary edge
(u,u^{\prime})
in
A_{(s,t)}
. This implies, if there is an
s-t
cut edge
e\not\in\bigcup_{i\in\{1,2\}}(P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L])
, there cannot be any
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}
. Further, all the
s-t
cut-edges must be present in any
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Hence,
R^{\prime}
is an
s-t
path in
A_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
means none of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is an
s-t
cut-edge. Recall,
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
only share the
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices. Now, if
f_{1}
(or
f_{2}
) lies on
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
, for some
j\in[\ell]
, since
R_{(u_{j},v_{j})}
is edge-disjoint with
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, either
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
or
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
survives in
H_{(s,t)}\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
(and so in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
as well). This completes the proof.
∎

Let us now combine Theorem
4.1
with Lemma
2.6
to finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For single pair, by
[
17
]
, we have a 2-
FTRO
of size
O(n)
and query time
O(1)
. By Theorem
4.1
, we have a 2-
FTRO
with slack of size
O(n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|})
and query time
O(1)
. Theorem now directly follows from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

5
Single Fault-tolerant Pairwise Reachability Oracle

In this section we will focus on computing a compact pairwise reachability oracle for single failure. We first we discuss about the single vertex failure case and then extend that result to a single edge failure. For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a vertex-set
S\subseteq V
, we use the notation
G-S
to denote the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all the vertices in
S
(and all the edges incident on them). We prove the following theorem for single failure.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a digraph with
n
vertices and
{\cal P}\subseteq V\times V
be a set of demand pairs.
We can preprocess
G
in polynomial time to construct an
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
-sized oracle
that given any failing edge
f
(or vertex
x
), and any pair
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, reports
whether or not there is an
s
to
t
path in
G\setminus\{f\}
(or
G-x
) in
O(1)
time.

This theorem together with Theorem
1.1
implies Theorem
1.3
. The above theorem is especially interesting as we get a
FTRO
of linear (in number of vertices) size
whenever number of pairs for which we have to preserve reachability is at most
O(\sqrt{n})
.

5.1
All-Pairs reachability restricted to an (s,t)-cut-set

One of the key ingredients of our construction of reachability oracle is the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a digraph and
p=(s,t)
be a vertex pair in
G
. Further, let
C
be any subset of
(s,t)
cut-vertices.
Then, we can compute in polynomial time an
O(|C|)
size oracle that for any three vertices
x,y,z\in C

reports whether or not there is a
y
to
z
path in graph
G-x
in
O(1)
time.

Let
\sigma=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{|C|})
be the cut-vertices in
C
in the order they appear on each
s
to
t
path in
G
,
and
\pi
be an arbitrary
s
to
t
path in
G
.
For simplicity, we assume
s,t\in C
. We use the notation
<_{\sigma}
(and
\leq_{\sigma}
) to denote the relative ordering of any two elements in the ordered set
\sigma
. For each
v\in C
, let
L(v)
be the set of predecessors of
v
in
\sigma
, and

R(v)
be the set of successors of
v
in
\sigma
.
Note that a node itself is not included in its predecessor and successor set.
We borrow the ideas of loop-nesting-forests
[
38
]
to compute two auxiliary rooted forests

T_{pred},T_{succ}
with vertex-set
C
as follows.

In forest
T_{pred}
, the parent of
w\in C
is the immediate predecessor (if it exists) of
w
in
\sigma
, say
u
, that satisfies
u,w

are strongly connected in graph
G-L(u)
.
In forest
T_{succ}
, the parent of
w\in C
is the immediate successor (if it exists) of
w
in
\sigma
, say
u
, that satisfies
u,w

are strongly connected in graph
G-R(u)
.
We compute the Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA)
and Level Ancestor (LA) data-structure over the two trees that takes
O(|C|)

space and answers any LCA/LA query in constant time.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
x,y\in C
satisfying
x<_{\sigma}y
, we can find the immediate successor of
x

strongly-connected to
y
in
G-x
in
O(1)
time.

Proof.

Let
r
be root of tree in
T_{pred}
containing
y
. If
x<_{\sigma}r
, then we output
r
since no
predecessors of
r
are strongly connected with
y
. So let us suppose
r\leqslant_{\sigma}x
.
In such a case
x,y,r
are contained in same tree in
T_{pred}
(with root
r
),
since
x,y,r
are strongly-connected in
G
.

Let
w
to be the immediate successor of
x
satisfying
y,w
are strongly-connected in
G-x
.
Then
x<_{\sigma}w\leq_{\sigma}y
. We will prove
w
is also the immediate successor of
x
satisfying

y,w
are strongly-connected in
G-L(w)
. Each
y-w
path is disjoint with set
L(w)
.
Indeed, if a
y
to
w
path contains a vertex in
L(w)\cap R(x)
, then it violates the definition of
w
;
and if a
y
to
w
path contains a vertex in
L(x)
then it violates the fact that any path from
L(x)
to
R(x)

must pass through

. Now
\pi[w,y]
is a
w
to
y
path disjoint with
L(w)
.
Hence,
y,w
are strongly-connected in
G-L(w)
.
Clearly, for any
x<_{\sigma}w^{\prime}\leq_{\sigma}y
satisfying

y,w^{\prime}
are strongly-connected in
G-L(w^{\prime})
, we have
y,w^{\prime}
are strongly-connected in
G-x
.
This proves,
w
is indeed the immediate successor of
x
satisfying

y,w
are strongly-connected in
G-L(w)
.

Let
a
be the LCA of
x
and
y
in
T_{pred}
. So,
a\leq_{\sigma}x<_{\sigma}y
.
Let
b
be the child of
a
on
a-y
path in
T_{pred}
. The vertices
a,b
are computable in

O(1)
time using LCA/LA data-structures. It is easy to verify that

are strongly-connected in
G-L(b)
.

We next prove

. Observe descendants of
b
in

are precisely
the set of vertices in
R(b)
that are strongly connected to
b
in
G-L(b)
.
Thus if
x
is identical to or a successor of

, then
b,x,y
must be strongly connected
in
G-L(b)
which violates the fact that

is not contained in the sub-tree of

.

Finally, we will argue that
w=b
. Let us assume on contrary that

.
Let
c
be the immediate predecessor of
b
, satisfying
x<_{\sigma}c
and

y,c
are strongly-connected in
G-L(c)
. Observe,
c
is also the immediate predecessor
of
b
, satisfying
x<_{\sigma}c
and
b,c
are strongly-connected in
G-L(c)
.
Recall,
a
is the parent of
b
, so it follows
c\leq_{\sigma}a
.
This results in a contradiction as
a\leq_{\sigma}x<_{\sigma}c
.
Therefore,
w=b
.

The lemma follows from the fact that
b
is computable in

O(1)
time
∎

Similar to Lemma
5.3
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
x,y\in C
satisfying
y<_{\sigma}x
, we can find the immediate predecessor of
x

strongly-connected to
y
in
G-x
in
O(1)
time.

For any
a\in C
, we store in
H(a)
the first vertex
b
in
\sigma
satisfying
there is an
a
to
b
path in
G-(R(b)\cap L(a))
.
Notice
H(a)\leq_{\sigma}a
.

Now given a triplet

, we answer
y
to
z
reachability query in
G-x
as follows.

Case 1:
x
appears between
y
and
z
in
\sigma
:

If
y<_{\sigma}x<_{\sigma}z
, then we answer unreachable as there is no path from
L(x)
to
R(x)
in
G-x
.
If
z<_{\sigma}x<_{\sigma}y
, then we proceed as follows.
Use Lemma
5.3
to compute the first successor of
x
, say
y_{0}
,
such that

are strongly-connected in
G-x
.
Similarly, compute the first predecessor of
x
, say

,
such that

, using Lemma

.
Now it suffices to check whether or not there is a

to

.
If there exists a path
\pi_{0}
from

to

path in

,
then, by definition of

and

, it follows that

is disjoint with

.
Thus, there is path from

to

(or equivalently, there is a path from

to

)
in

if and only if
H(y_{0})\leq_{\sigma}z_{0}
.

Case 2:
x
appears before
y
and
z
in
\sigma
:

If

, then
\pi[y,z]
is a path from

to

in

.
Let us now consider the case

.
Use Lemma

to compute the first successor of
x
, say
y_{0}
,
such that

are strongly-connected in

.
Observe that there is no path from

to vertices in

in

.
Thus, there is path from

to

(or equivalently, a path from

to

)
in

.

Case 3:

appears after

and

in

:

If

, then

.
Let us now consider the case

Use Lemma

,

Thus, there is path from

The conditions stated in the above three cases are verifiable in constant time.
Our oracle takes

over trees

.

.

5.2
Reachability Oracle to Handle Vertex Failures

We will now present a pairwise reachability oracle for handling vertex failures, and
later extend this to handle edge failures in the Subsection
5.3
.
For each pair
p=(s,t)\in{\cal P}
define
cut-vertex-set
for
p
, denoted by
{\cal CV}(p)
, as the set of all vertices

x
in
G
satisfying there is no
s
to
t
path in
G-x
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be an integer parameter.
Compute a subset
{\cal Q}
of
{\cal P}
as follows.
Iterate over pairs in
{\cal P}
, and include a pair
p
in
{\cal Q}
if and only if
{\cal CV}(p)\setminus\big(\cup_{q\in{\cal Q}}{\cal CV}(q)\big)

is larger than
\alpha
. The following simple observation is due to the fact that each pair in
{\cal Q}
is associated with
\alpha

distinct cut vertices.

Observation 5.5
.

|{\cal Q}|\leq(n/\alpha)
.

Define
V_{{\cal Q}}:=\cup_{q\in{\cal Q}}{\cal CV}(q)
to be set of cut vertices for pairs in
{\cal Q}
.
Then for every
p
not in
{\cal Q}
, size of
{\cal CV}(p)\setminus V_{{\cal Q}}
is bounded by
\alpha
.

It turns out that the most non-trivial scenario is when failing vertex lies in
V_{{\cal Q}}
.
Let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{\ell}
be pairs in
{\cal Q}
(here
\ell=|{\cal Q}|
), and
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
be a partition of
V_{\cal Q}

satisfying
V_{i}\subseteq{\cal CV}(q_{i})
.
For each
p\in{\cal P}
and
i\in[1,\ell]
, let
\textsc{first}(p,i)
and
\textsc{last}(p,i)
be
respectively the first and last cut vertices for pair
p
that lies in set
V_{i}
.
Since all the cut vertices for any pair, say
(s,t)
, always appear in same
order on each
s-t
path, the notion of first and last cut vertices is well defined.

In order to efficiently handle failures in set
V_{i}
, for
i\in[1,\ell]
, we present in next lemma a linear size data-structure for
all-pairs reachability as long as the query pair and the failing vertex are restricted to
V_{i}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

For each
i\in[1,\ell]
, we can compute in polynomial time an
O(|V_{i}|)
size data-structure that
given any triplet
x,y,z\in V_{i}
reports whether or not there is a
y-z
path in
G-x
in
O(1)
time.

The proof of the above lemma is immediate from Theorem
5.2
. Now using the
above we show the desired bound on the reachability oracle for handling vertex failures.

Let
F
be a function that maps each
x\in V_{\cal Q}
to the index
i=F(x)
satisfying
x\in V_{i}
.
Our oracle comprises of function
F
; for each
p\in{\cal P}
and
i\in[1,\ell]
, the vertices
\textsc{first}(p,i)
and
\textsc{last}(p,i)
;
a dictionary
{{\cal D}}_{{\cal Q}}
to maintain
V_{{\cal Q}}
; and dictionaries
{{\cal D}}_{p}
to maintain
{\cal CV}(p)\setminus V_{\cal Q}
, for
p\in{\cal P}
.

The query algorithm given any failing vertex
x
and pair
p=(s,t)\in{\cal P}
proceeds as follows.
If
x\notin V_{\cal Q}
then it reports ‘Reachable’ if and only if
x\notin({\cal CV}(p)\setminus V_{\cal Q})
.
Using the dictionaries
{{\cal D}}_{{\cal Q}}
and
{{\cal D}}_{p}
, this is verifiable in constant time.
If
x\in V_{\cal Q}
, then the next step is to extract in constant time index
i=F(x)
and vertices
a=\textsc{first}(p,i)
,
b=\textsc{last}(p,i)
.
By definition,
a
and
b
are cut vertices for
p
as well as
q_{i}\in{\cal Q}
.
Since
a,b
are cut vertices for
p
, and
a
appears before
b
on any
s-t
path,

t
is reachable from
s
in
G-x
if and only if
b
is reachable from
a
in
G-x
. The later condition is verifiable in
constant time using Lemma
5.6
.

The space complexity of the oracle is
O(n+\ell\cdot|{\cal P}|+|{\cal P}|\cdot\alpha)
, where
\ell=|{\cal Q}|\leq(n/\alpha)
.
Substituting the value of
\alpha
as
\sqrt{n}
provides a bound of
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
on the size of oracle.

This proves the bound of
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n})
on the size of pair-wise
reachability oracle for handling a single vertex failure in constant time.

5.3
Reachability Oracle to Handle Edge Failures

We will now extend the above result to handle edge failures. Let
{\cal C}\subseteq E(G)
be the set of all those edges
e
that are
cut-edge
with respect to some pair
p\in{\cal P}
.
Let
{\cal C}_{0}
be the subset containing those edges
e=(x,y)
in
{\cal C}
that satisfy that
x
and
y
are strongly connected in
G
,
but not in
G\setminus\{e\}
.

We compute two dictionaries
{\cal D}_{\cal C}
and
{\cal D}_{{\cal C}_{0}}
so that given an edge
e\in E(G)

it is verifiable in constant time whether or not
e
lies in
{\cal C}
and/or
{\cal C}_{0}
.

Our main idea for handling edge failures is due to the following simple observation.

Lemma 5.7
.

For any
e=(a,b)\in{\cal C}\setminus{\cal C}_{0}
and any pair
p\in{\cal P}
,

e
is a cut-edge for
p
if and only if both
a
and
b
are cut-vertices for
p
.

Proof.

Let us suppose
e
is a cut-edge for
p=(s,t)
. Then each
s
to
t
path must pass through

a
as well as
b
, which implies
a
and
b
are cut-vertices for
p
.

Now let suppose us assume
a,b
are cut-vertices for
p
.
Since
e=(a,b)
is cut-edge for some pair
q\in{\cal P}
, there is no path from
a
to
b
in
G\setminus\{e\}
.
Thus,
a
and
b
cannot be strongly-connected in
G
, because otherwise
e
will lie in set
{\cal C}_{0}
.
This implies
a
is not reachable from
b
in
G
, and thus
a
must precede
b
on each
s
to
t
path.
Further, since there is no path from
a
to
b
in
G\setminus\{e\}
, edge
e
must be a cut-edge for
p
.
∎

All that remains is to handle failures in the set
{\cal C}_{0}
.
We construct a new graph
{\cal G}
with
n+|{\cal C}_{0}|
vertices by splitting each edge
e=(a,b)\in{\cal C}_{0}

by inserting a vertex
v_{e}
in between. Therefore, the edge
(a,b)
in
G
translates to
(a,v_{e},b)
path in
{\cal G}
.
To answer reachability query for a pair
p=(s,t)
on failure of an edge
w\in{\cal C}_{0}
,
it suffices to check whether or not there is an
s
to
t
path in
{\cal G}-v_{e}
.

We provide below a bound on cardinalities of sets
{\cal C}
and
{\cal C}_{0}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

|{\cal C}_{0}|\leq 2n
, and
|{\cal C}|\leq O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|},(n|{\cal P}|)^{2/3}\})
.

Proof.

Let
H_{0}
be strong-connectivity certificate (or subgraph) of
G
, and
H_{{\cal P}}
be a pair-wise reachability certificate (or subgraph) for pair-set
{\cal P}
(without any failure).
Then edges in
{\cal C}_{0}
must be contained in
H_{0}
, and edges in
{\cal C}
must be contained in
H_{\cal P}
.
It is straightforward to compute a strong-connectivity certificate with
2n
edges, and Abboud and Bodwin
[
1
]
showed that

|E(H_{\cal P})|=O(n+\min\{|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n},n\sqrt{|{\cal P}|},(n|{\cal P}|)^{2/3}\})
. Thus the claim follows.
∎

By employing the vertex fault-tolerant reachability oracle result from previous subsection, and using Lemma
5.7

and Lemma
5.8
, we obtain an
O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n}+(n|{\cal P}|)^{2/3}))

=O(n+|{\cal P}|\sqrt{n}))
sized data-structure
that for any pair
(s,t)
in
{\cal P}
and any edge failure
e
, answers reachability query for
(s,t,e)
in constant time. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

6
Dual Fault-tolerant Pairwise Reachability Preserver

In this section, we present a construction of a dual fault-tolerant reachability preserver (2-
FTRS
) for a given (directed) graph
G=(V,E)
and a vertex-pair set
{\cal P}\subseteq V\times V
. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.5
. Here we provide a construction of 2-
FTRS
with slack, which together with Lemma
2.5
shows Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For any directed graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
vertices and a set
{\cal P}\subseteq V\times V
of vertex-pairs, there exists a 2-
FTRS
with slack having at most
O(n^{4/3}|{\cal P}|^{1/3})
edges. Furthermore, we can find such a subgraph in polynomial time.

We will use the notations
H_{(s,t)},P_{(s,t)}^{i},Q_{(s,t),v}^{j}
defined in Section
3
. Apart from them, we need one more notation to denote the frequency of a vertex in a set of paths. For any set
B
of paths in
G
, for any vertex
v\in V
, we use
\textsc{freq}_{B}(v)
to denote the number of paths in
B
that contains
v
.

Procedure to Construct a 2-
FTRS
with slack for the input
(G,{\cal P})
:

1.

For each
(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, construct
H_{(s,t)}=2\text{-}\textsc{FTRS}(G,(s,t))
using the construction provided in Section
3
. (Recall,
H_{(s,t)}
contains two
s-t
paths
P_{(s,t)}^{1}
and
P_{(s,t)}^{2}
, that intersect only at
s-t
cut-edges and
s-t
cut-vertices.) Define
H_{inter}=\bigcup_{(s,t)\in{\cal P}}H_{(s,t)}
.

2.

Consider the following family of subsets
{\cal F}:=\{V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[L]),V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[-L])\mid i\in\{1,2\},(s,t)\in{\cal P}\}
, where
L=n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3}
. Construct a fractional hitting set
S
of
{\cal F}
(by Theorem
2.4
). Let
{\cal Q}:=\{(s,t)\in{\cal P}\mid\forall_{i\in\{1,2\}}V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[L])\cap S\neq\emptyset\text{ and }V(P^{i}_{(s,t)}[-L])\cap S\neq\emptyset\}
.

3.

For each
v\in S
, construct a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,\{v\}\times V)
and 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,V\times\{v\})
, and take union of all these subgraphs to get a subgraph
H_{1}
.

4.

For each
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
and
i\in\{1,2\}
, consider the subpaths
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]
and
P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L]
. Let
H_{2}
be the graph
\bigcup_{(s,t)\in{\cal P},i\in\{1,2\}}(P_{(s,t)}^{i}[L]\cup P_{(s,t)}^{i}[-L])
.

5.

Let
B
be the set of all the paths
Q_{(s,t),v}^{i}
(defined in Section
3
) for
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
,
v\in V(\cup_{j\in\{1,2\}}P_{(s,t)}^{j}[-L])
and
i\in\{1,2\}
.

6.

Initialize an empty set
W
.

7.

While
\exists v\in V
such that
\textsc{freq}_{B}(v)\geq\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|}

(a)

Add
v
to
W
and remove all the paths that contains
v
from
B
.

8.

For each
w\in W
, construct a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,\{w\}\times V)
and 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,V\times\{w\})
, and take union of all these subgraphs to get a subgraph
H_{3}
.

9.

Let
H_{4}
be the subgraph obtained by taking the union of all the (remaining) paths in
B
.

10.

Return the subgraph
H=H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}\cup H_{4}
.

Size of the subgraph
H
.

By Theorem
2.4
,
|S|=O(n/L)=O((n|{\cal P}|)^{1/3})
. We use Theorem
2.1
to build a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,\{v\}\times V)
and 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,V\times\{v\})
. So the size of
H_{1}
is
O((n|{\cal P}|)^{1/3}\cdot n)
. By the construction (Step 4),
H_{2}
is of size at most
O(L\cdot|{\cal P}|)
.

Observe, while defining
B
(Step 5) before the start of the while loop, we add at most
O(L)
paths for each
(s,t)\in{\cal Q}
. So,
|B|=O(L|{\cal Q}|)
. At each iteration of the while loop (Step 7(a)), we remove at least
\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|}
paths from
B
and add a new vertex in
W
. At the end of the while loop, for each vertex
v\in V\setminus W
,
\textsc{freq}_{B}(v)<\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|}
. So, at the end of the while loop
|W|\leq O\Big(\frac{L|{\cal Q}|}{\sqrt{L|Q|}}\Big)=O(\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|})
. Again, we use Theorem
2.1
to build a 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,\{w\}\times V)
and 2-
\textsc{FTRS}(G,V\times\{w\})
for each
w\in W
. Thus the size of
H_{3}
is at most
O(n\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|})
.

Since
H_{4}
is the union of all the leftover paths in
B
after the while loop, in-degree of each vertex in
H_{4}
is at most
\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|}
. So the size of
H_{4}
is
O(n\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|})
. Hence, we conclude that the size of the final subgraph
H
is
O((n|{\cal P}|)^{1/3}\cdot n+L\cdot|{\cal Q}|+n\sqrt{L|{\cal Q}|})=O(n^{4/3}|P|^{1/3})
, for
L=n^{2/3}|P|^{-1/3}
(note,

to the problem of constructing 2-
FTRO
. We modify the graph
G
constructed in Section
8
as follows: For each
i,j\in[r]
and each
k\in[N]
, we keep the edge
(p_{k,i},q_{k,j})\in A_{k}\times B_{k}
in
G
if and only if the
(i,j)^{th}
bit of matrix
Z_{k}
is one. Now, we claim that for any triplet
(k,i,j)
, where
k\in[N]
and
i,j\in[r]
, the
(i,j)^{th}
bit of the matrix
Z_{k}
can be computed by deciding whether there is a
a_{i}-b_{j}
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
for
f_{1}=(p_{k,i},p_{k+1,i}),~~f_{2}=(q_{k-1,j},q_{k,j})
. This is because, on failure of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, there is a path from
a_{i}
to
b_{j}
in
G
if and only if
(p_{k,i},q_{k,j})\in E(G)
.

So, given any instance of
3-D Index
, Alice will construct a graph
G
as above, and consider a source-set
S=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
(as in Section
8
). Then she will construct a 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,S\times S)
and send that data structure to Bob. Upon receiving the data structure, Bob (given
(k,i,j)
) can now check whether there is a
a_{i}-b_{j}
path in
G\setminus\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
for
f_{1}=(p_{k,i},p_{k+1,i}),~~f_{2}=(q_{k-1,j},q_{k,j})
and accordingly output the
(i,j)^{th}
bit of the matrix
Z_{k}
.

Now, since randomized one-way communication complexity of
3-D Index
is
\Omega(Nr^{2})
, any 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,S\times S)
must be of size at least
\Omega(Nr^{2})=\Omega(nr)
bits (recall,
n=\Theta(Nr)
). So, we conclude with the following theorem.

Theorem 8.4
.

For any positive integers
n,r~(r\leq n)
, there exists an
n
-vertex directed graph with a source set
S

of
r
vertices, such that any 2-
\textsc{FTRO}(G,S\times S)
must be of size
\Omega\big(n|S|\big)
(in bits).

The above theorem is true even if we allow the oracle to err with probability at most
1/3
. As an immediate corollary of the above theorem, we get Theorem
1.2
.

8.3
Any lower bound of
FTRS
implies that for
FTRO

Above we use the hard instance for 2-
FTRS
to argue about the same size lower bound for 2-
FTRO
. However, in general, so far we do not know how to turn a lower bound for pairwise
FTRS
to that for pairwise
FTRO
. In this section, we establish such a connection. Let
{\cal S}(n,p,k)
and
{\cal O}(n,p,k)
be the respective bounds on the extremal size of
FTRS
and
FTRO

for
n
vertex graphs with
|P|=p
demand pairs under
k
edge failures.

Theorem 8.5
.

For any
n,k\geq 1
, the functions
\cal O
and
\cal S
satisfy:
{\cal O}(2n,p,k)=\Omega(k^{-1}{\cal S}(n,p,k-1))
.

Proof.

For a given
n,p
, let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
vertex graph, and
{\cal P}
be a pair-set of size
p
, for which
the optimal
\textsc{FTRS}(G,{\cal P},k-1)
, say
{\cal G}
, contains
{\cal S}(n,p,k-1)
edges.
For each edge
e\in E({\cal G})
, there must exist a pair, say
P_{e}=(s,t)\in{\cal P}
, and an edge-set, say
C_{e}
,
of size at most
k
satisfying that
C_{e}
is a minimal
(s,t)
-cut in
{\cal G}
.
Indeed, if
e
is in none of the minimal cuts of size
k
, then for each
F\subseteq E({\cal G})
of size upto
k-1
,

e
would not be an essential edge to preserve reachability between pairs
{\cal P}
in graph
{\cal G}
, and so
we could eliminate
e
from
{\cal G}
.
Compute a set
{\cal L}
by
iterating over the edges of
{\cal G}
, and including an edge
e
in
{\cal L}

if and only if
e
is not contained in cuts
C_{e^{\prime}}
, for each
e^{\prime}
in partially computed set
{\cal L}
.
Clearly
|{\cal L}|=\Omega(k^{-1}E({\cal G}))
.
The set
{\cal L}
satisfies the property that
for each
e\in{\cal L}
, the set
(C_{e}\setminus\{e\})
has an empty intersection with
{\cal L}
.

We will present an information-theoretic argument by encoding
|{\cal L}|
-length binary-vectors
in the fault-tolerant pair-wise reachability oracle of
2n
vertex graphs. No data structure can store this in
o(|{\cal L}|)
bits.

For each
J\in\{0,1\}^{|{\cal L}|}
, construct a graph
{\cal H}_{J}
on
2n
vertices as follows.
Initialize
{\cal H}_{J}
to
{\cal G}
.
For each
v\in V
, add to
{\cal H}_{J}
another copy of
v
, say
v_{0}
.
Finally, for each
e=(x,y)\in{\cal L}
satisfying that
e_{J}=0
, add edges
(x,y_{0})
and
(y_{0},y)
to
{\cal H}_{J}
.
Now let
{\cal A}
be an optimal
k
-fault-tolerant reachability oracle for graph family
\{G_{J}~|~J\in\{0,1\}^{{\cal L}}\}

with respect to pair-set
{\cal P}
.
Consider an edge
e\in{\cal L}
, and the corresponding pair
P_{e}
and cut-set
C_{e}
.
Our proof works by observing that set
C_{e}\in{\cal L}
is a cut-set (of size at most
k
) for pair
P_{e}
in
{\cal H}_{J}

if an only if
e_{J}=1
.
Thus by querying reachability after
k
failures with respect to pairs in
{\cal P}
, we can infer the set
J
through oracle
{\cal A}
.
As the set
J
can have
2^{|L|}
configurations, by standard information-theoretic arguments, this
provides a lower bound of
\Omega(|{\cal L}|)=\Omega(k^{-1}{\cal S}(n,p,k-1))
on the size of oracle
{\cal A}
.
∎

As a direct corollary of the above theorem together with the lower bound of the reachability preserver without failure of
[
1
]
, we get Theorem
1.4
. By setting
d=2
in Theorem
1.4
, for
p=O(n)
, we get a lower bound of
\Omega(n^{2/3}p^{1/2})
on the size of
1
-FTRO.

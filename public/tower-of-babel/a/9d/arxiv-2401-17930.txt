Abstract
. We study minimally Terracini finite sets of points in the projective plane and we prove that the sequence of the cardinalities of minimally Terracini sets can have any number of gaps for degree great enough.

1.
Introduction

Terracini loci in projective varieties have been recently introduced in
[
3
,
1
]
and
subsequentely studied e.g. in

[
4
,
11
,
5
,
10
,
12
]
.

A collection of double points is called a
Terracini locus
when they are in special position, i.e. when they impose an unexpected number of conditions to a linear system of a given degree.
In the theory of higher secant varieties of projective varieties (see
[
6
]
for a general reference), a set of points is Terracini if they are
dependent
in the sense that the tangent spaces at these points span a linear space of dimension less than the expected one.

The study of Terracini loci in the cases of Veronese and Segre varieties
is relevant also for applications to the theory of tensors (rank, decomposition and identifiability of tensors), see e.g.
[
7
]
.

In this note, we focus on the case of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and, following
[
1
, Definition 2.8]
, we impose the additional condition that the sets of points span the whole space.
Indeed a set of points spanning a smaller linear subspace is Terracini in such subspace, by
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]
.

More precisely we give the following definition.
For any
x\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x):=\{\text{all the sets of $x$ points of $\mathbb{P}^{n}$}\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We call
Terracini locus
and we denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
the set of all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
such that

•

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.

•

\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

We say that
S
is

Terracini with respect to

\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

Given a Terracini set of cardinality
x
it is easy to construct Terracini sets of any cardinality
y\geq x
.
Hence, in an attempt to classify the possible cardinalities of Terracini loci, we need a more restrictive definition.
For this reason, in
[
2
]

we have introduced the notion of
minimal Terracini loci
.

Definition 1.2
.

We call
minimal Terracini locus
and we denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
the set of all
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
, such that

•

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))=0
for all
A\subsetneq S
.

We say that
S
is
minimally Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
.

In this note we consider minimally Terracini sets in the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix an integer
c\geq 2
. Then for all
d\geq 14c+2
there are integers
x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq c
, and
y_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq c-1
, such
that

•

x_{1}<y_{1}<x_{2}<y_{2}<\cdots<x_{c-1}<y_{c-1}<x_{c},

•

\mathbb{T}(2,d;x_{i})^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for all
i=1,\dots,c
, and

•

\mathbb{T}(2,d;y_{i})^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all
i=1,\dots,c-1
.

We expect the same phenomenon to occur also in projective spaces of higher dimension, more precisely we formulate the following conjecture for
n\geq 3
:

Conjecture 1.4
.

Fix an integer
c\geq 2
. Then there exists an integer
d_{0}(c,n)
such that for all
d\geq d_{0}(c,n)
there are integers
x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq c
, and
y_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq c-1
, such
that

•

x_{1}<y_{1}<x_{2}<y_{2}<\cdots<x_{c-1}<y_{c-1}<x_{c},

•

\mathbb{T}(n,d;x_{i})^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for all
i=1,\dots,c
, and

•

\mathbb{T}(n,d;y_{i})^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all
i=1,\dots,c-1
.

The techniques we use in this paper are based mainly on the two notions of
critical schemes
for a Terracini set, and
numerical character
of a zero dimensional scheme in the plane. The numerical character is a combinatorial description of the Hilbert function, which is specific for the plane and has useful consequences. In particular we prove in Theorem
2.11
that the numerical character of any critical scheme for a Terracini set is connected, and this fact is crucial in our main proof.

In the following section we recall some preliminary results, we define the critical schemes (Definition
2.5
) and we describe their properties. We also explain what is the numerical character of zero-dimensional schemes. The last Section
3
is devoted to the proof of our main Theorem
1.3
.

We thank the referee for pointing out a mistake in the first version of this paper and for useful comments.

2.
Preliminaries

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
.

Given a scheme
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we denote by
|\cal{I}_{X}(d)|:=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\cal{I}_{X}\otimes\cal{O}(d)))
the linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
d
containing
X
.

We recall first some preliminary facts.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a curve of degree
t\leq d
and
Z\subset C
be a zero-dimensional scheme. Since
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d-t))=0
, the restriction map
H^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d))\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{0}(\cal{O}_{C}(d))
is surjective.
Thus
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z,C}(d))
.
Moreover we have

h^{0}(\cal{O}_{C}(d))=\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-t+2}{2}=\frac{1}{2}t(2d+3-t)

and

h^{0}(\cal{O}_{C}(d+1))=\frac{1}{2}t(2d+5-t).

Remark 2.2
.

Let
W\subset Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be zero-dimensional schemes. Since
h^{i}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))=0
for all
i\geq 2
and
h^{i}(\cal{I}_{W,Z}(d))=0
for all
i\geq 1
, we have:

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{W}(d))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{W}(d))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d)),

and

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d+1))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d+1))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d)).

Notation 2.3
.

For any zero-dimensional scheme
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
Z\neq\emptyset
, let

\tau(Z)
be the maximal integer
t
such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))>0
and

s(Z)
be the minimal integer
t
such that
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))>0
.
In other words
s(Z)
is the minimal degree of a hypersurface containing
Z
.

Note that since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))>0
for any
t\leq-1
, then
\tau(Z)\geq-1
and
\tau(Z)=-1
if and only if
\deg(Z)=1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any finite and non-empty set
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
we have

s(S)<s(2S)\leq 2s(S).

Proof.

If
D
is a hypersurface in the linear system
|\cal{I}_{S}(s(S)|
, then
2S\subset 2D
and hence
s(2S)\leq 2s(S)
.

On the other hand, by Remark
2.2

it follows
s(S)\leq s(2S)
.
Now we prove that
s(S)<s(2S)
.
Assume by contradiction that
s(S)=s(2S)
.
Then there is a hypersurface
D\in|\cal{I}_{S}(s(S))|
such that
S\subseteq\mathrm{Sing}(D)
. Let
D
be defined by
f=0
for some form
f
of degree
d
. Fix coordinates
(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})
such that
\partial_{x_{n}}(f)(0:\cdots:1)\neq 0
.
Then the degree
s(S)-1
hypersurface defined by
\partial_{x_{n}}(f)=0
contains
S
, which contradicts the minimality of
s(S)
.
∎

In Example
2.13
we will see that it is possible that
s(2S)=2s(S)
even for
S\in\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)
.

We recall their definition from

]
:

.

Given

Proposition 2.6
.

Given

and any critical scheme

of

,
then we have

•

\tau(2S)=d

By

.

The following technical result will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

be a complete intersection of a plane curve

of degree

and a plane curve

Bézout gives

. Since

and

, the Koszul complex associated to the equations of the plane curves

and

gives

for all

and

.

To prove that

for all

of degree

.
Fix

. The curves

and

links

.

Notation 2.8
.

Given an integer
d
, we define the functions

f(t):=t(d+3-t)/2

and

g(t):=t(d+(5-t)/2).

Note that the functions
f
and
g
are strictly increasing in the intervals
(0,(d+3)/2)
and
(0,d+(5/2))
respectively.

We recall now the crucial notion of
numerical character
, which is a way to encode the Hilbert function of a zero-dimensional scheme
Z
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, see
[
8
,
9
,
13
]
.

The Hilbert function of any subvariety
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
with homogeneous ideal
I(Y)
is defined as

H_{Y}:\mathbb{N}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{N},\quad H_{Y}(t)=\dim(\mathbb{C}[V]_{t}/I(Y)_{t}).

and its first difference is

\Delta_{Y}(t):=H_{Y}(t)-H_{Y}(t-1).

Assume now that
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a zero-dimensional scheme of degree
z
and
set
s:=s(Z)
and
\tau:=\tau(Z)
as in Notation
2.3
.

For all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the sequence
\{h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))\}_{t\geq 0}
. Since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))=z-H_{Z}(t)
, we have

\Delta_{Z}(t)=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t-1))-h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))\geq 0.

The knowledge of the sequence of integers
\Delta(t)
is equivalent to the knowledge of the sequence of integers
n_{0},n_{1},\cdots,n_{s-1}
, called the
numerical character
of
Z
, such that

•

n_{0}\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s-1}\geq s
,

•

for all
t\geq 0

(1)

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}((n_{i}-t-1)_{+}-(i-t-1)_{+}),

where we denote by
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In particular, since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z})=z-1
, we have
z=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(n_{i}-i)
, i.e.

(2)

\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}n_{i}=z+\binom{s}{2}.

Since
n_{0}\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s-1}\geq s
, from (
2
) we also obtain:

(3)

z\geq s^{2}-\binom{s}{2}=\binom{s+1}{2}.

Obviously
\Delta_{Z}(t)=t+1
if
t<s
. For all
t\geq s
from (
1
) one sees that
{\Delta_{Z}}(t)
is the number of integers
i
such that
n_{i}\geq t+1
.

Note also that
n_{0}=\tau+2
, by
[
8
]
.
Then from (
2
) we also easily deduce the following inequality:

(4)

z\leq s(\tau+2)-\binom{s}{2}=s\left(\tau-\frac{5-s}{2}\right).

The numerical character
n_{0}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s-1}
is said to be
connected
if
n_{i}\leq n_{i+1}+1
.
By (
1
), the numerical character of
Z
is connected if and only if the sequence
\{\Delta_{Z}(t)\}
is strictly decreasing in the range
\sigma_{0}\leq t\leq\tau
, where
\sigma_{0}
is the minimal
\sigma
such that
\Delta_{Z}(\sigma)>\Delta_{Z}(\sigma+1)
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
Z
be a zero-dimensional scheme in the plane and let
n_{0}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s-1}
be its numerical character.
Then, by
[
9
, Proposition at p. 112]
(see also
[
8
, Prop. 4]
), if
n_{t-1}>n_{t}+1
, then
there is a degree

such that the scheme

has numerical character

.
Moreover
the scheme

has numerical character
m_{0},\dots,m_{s-t-1}
with
m_{i}=n_{t+i}-t
.

There is a characterization of the connectedness of the numerical character of a zero-dimensional scheme

in term of the minimal free resolution of

(
[
8
, Theorem 7]
).

The following theorem is an important property of the numerical character of a critical scheme.

The proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we prove our main result, Theorem
1.3
.

The following proposition gives the non-emptyness of the Terracini loci for suitable number of points.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix an integer
t\geq 2
and an integer
d

such that

•

d+3-t
is even and

•

d\geq 3t-1
.

Set
x:=t(d+3-t)/2
.
Then there exists
S\in\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)^{\prime}
which admits a critical scheme of degree
2x
.

Proof.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a set of
x
non-collinear points which is the complete intersection
of a smooth curve
C
of degree
t
and a curve of degree
(d+3-t)/2
. Set
Z:=C\cap 2S
.
Since
Z
is the complete intersection of
C
and a curve of degree
d+3-t
,
by Lemma
2.7
we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))=1
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}}(d))=0
for all
Z^{\prime}\subsetneq Z
.
Since
C
is smooth, for any
A\subseteq S
the residual exact sequence of
C
is:

(7)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{A}(d-t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2A}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{{2A\cap C,C}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

Thus to prove that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))=1

and that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))=0
for all
A\subsetneq S
(and hence to prove that
S
is minimal), it is sufficient to prove that

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{S}(d-t))=0
. This is true by Lemma
2.7
, because
S
is the complete intersection of a curve of degree
t
and a curve of degree
(d+3-t)/2
and
d-t\geq t+(d+3-t)/2-2
by assumption.
∎

We are finally ready to prove our main
result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:

Given
c\geq 2
, we fix an integer
d
such that

(8)

d\geq 14c+2.

Step 1.

First we define the numbers
x_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,c
, and we prove that

\mathbb{T}(2,d;x_{i})^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

Consider the function
f(t)=t(d+3-t)/2
, defined in Notation
2.8
and recall that it is strictly increasing in the interval
(0,(d+3)/2)
.

For any
i=1,\ldots,{c}
, we define:

x_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(2i)=i(d+3-2i)&\textrm{ if $d$ is odd},\\
f(2i+1)=(2i+1)(d+2-2i)/2&\textrm{ if $d$ is even}\end{array}\right.

Note that the numbers
x_{i}
are the integer values of
f(t)
in the interval
2\leq t\leq 2c+1
.
Since
2c+1\leq(d+3)/2
by assumption (
8
), then we have

x_{1}<\ldots<x_{c}.

Setting
t=2i
if
d
is odd and
t=2i+1
if
d
is even, it is easy to check that the assumptions of
Proposition
3.1
are satisfied. Indeed the second condition is verified since
d\geq 6c+2
by (
8
).

Then it follows by Proposition
3.1
that

\mathbb{T}(2,d;x_{i})^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

Step 2.

Now we define the numbers
y_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,c-1
, and we prove to that

\mathbb{T}(2,d;y_{i})^{\prime}=\emptyset
by contradiction.

For any
i=1,\ldots,c-1
, let us define

y_{i}:=x_{i+1}-1.

We clearly have
x_{1}<y_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<y_{c-1}<x_{c}.

Assume by contradiction the existence of
S\in\mathbb{T}(2,d;y_{i})^{\prime}
.

We have
\deg(2S)=3y_{i}
and
\tau(2S)=d
, by Lemma
2.6
.

Let
Z
be a critical scheme for
S
.
Now let
s=s(Z)
and take a curve
C\in|\cal{I}_{Z}(s)|
.
Let

W
be a zero-dimensional scheme such that
Z\subseteq W\subset C
,

W_{\red}=S
and each connected component of
W
has degree
2
.
Then by Lemma
2.12
we know that
\tau(W)=d
,
s(W)=s(Z)=s
.
Clearly
w:=\deg(W)=2y_{i}
.

Case I:

Let us assume first that
d
is odd.
Hence

x_{i}=f(2i)=i(d+3-2i)
and

y_{i}=x_{i+1}-1=(i+1)(d+1-2i)-1
.

Set
a=2i+2
.
Since we want to apply Lemma
2.10
to
W
, we check the following four hypotheses:

(a)

s\leq(d+3)/2
.

Indeed if by contradiction we assume
s>(d+3)/2
, then by (
3
) we would have

w\geq(d+5)(d+3)/8
.
On the other hand, since
w=2y_{i}\leq 2y_{c-1}
, we have

(d+5)(d+3)/8\leq 2c(d+3-2c)-2

which is false as soon as

d>8c-4+\sqrt{32c^{2}-16c-15},

which is true by (
8
).

(b)
The numerical character of
W
is connected by Theorem
2.11
and Lemma
2.12
.

(c)

w\geq a^{2}
.

Indeed
y_{i}-a^{2}=(i+1)(d+1-2i)-1-(2i+2)^{2}=(i+1)(d-6i-3)-1\geq 0

because, by (
8
),

d>6c-3>6i+3
.
Then we have

w=2y_{i}\geq y_{i}\geq a^{2}
.

(d)

\tau(W)>a-3+w/a
.

Indeed,

d>a-3+w/a
is equivalent to

2f(a)>w
. Since
f(a)=x_{i+1}
and
w=2y_{i}=2(x_{i+1}-1)
, then
we conclude that
\tau(W)>a-3+w/a
.

Then, we can apply Lemma
2.10
and we have the following two possibilities:

(i)
either
a=s
and
w=s(d+3-s)
. In this case we get to a contradiction, because we know that
w<a(d+3-a)
by condition (d);

(ii)
or we have
s<a
and there is an integer
0<m<a
and a degree
m
curve
D
such that

\tau(W\cap D)=d
.

Recall that
Z
is a critical scheme for the minimal Terracini set
S
and
S\subseteq Z\subseteq W\subset 2S
.

We now prove that
S\subset D
. Indeed if
S\cap D=S^{\prime}\neq S
, then we would have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{W\cap D}(d))>0
, which contradicts the minimality of
S
.

Let
Z^{\prime}
be a subscheme of
W\cap D
such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}}(d))>0
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=0
for any
X\subset Z^{\prime}
. Then
Z^{\prime}
is critical for
S
.

Let
W^{\prime}
be a zero-dimensional scheme
such that
that
Z^{\prime}\subset W\cap D\subseteq W^{\prime}\subset D
,
and each connected component of
W
has degree
2
.

Clearly, we have

\deg(W^{\prime})=2y_{i}
and
s^{\prime}:=s(W^{\prime})\leq m
.
Moreover by Lemma
2.12
we have
\tau(W^{\prime})=d
.
Then by (
4
) we have

2y_{i}\leq s^{\prime}(d+(5-s^{\prime})/2)=g(s^{\prime}),

where the function
g
is defined in Notation
2.8
and is strictly decreasing in the interval
(0,d+(5/2))
.
Since

0<s^{\prime}\leq m<2i+2=a\leq d+5/2,

where the last condition holds by condition (
8
) since
i\leq c
, it follows
that

2y_{i}\leq g(2i+1).

On the other hand it is easy to check that
2y_{i}>g(2i+1)

and this give a contradiction.

Case II:

Now let us assume that
d
is even.
Hence

x_{i}=f(2i+1)=(2i+1)(d+2-2i)/2
and

y_{i}=x_{i+1}-1=(2i+3)(d-2i)/2-1.

In this case we set
a=2i+3
and we check again the hypotheses of Lemma
2.10
for the scheme
W
as follows:

(a)
s\leq(d+3)/2
.

Indeed if by contradiction we assume
s>(d+3)/2
, then, arguing as in the previous case, we would obtain

(d+5)(d+3)/8\leq(2c+1)(d-2c+2)-2

which is false as soon as

d>8c+\sqrt{32c^{2}+16c+17},

which is true by (
8
).

(b) The numerical character of
W
is connected by Theorem
2.11
and Lemma
2.12
.

(c)
w\geq a^{2}
.

Indeed
y_{i}-a^{2}=(2i+3)(d-2i)/2-1-(2i+3)^{2}=(2i+3)(d-6i-6)/2-1\geq 0

because, by assumption,
d>6c+6>6i+6
.
Then we have

w\geq y_{i}\geq a^{2}
.

(d)
\tau(W)>a-3+w/a
.

Indeed,

d>a-3+w/a
is equivalent to

2f(a)>z
. Since
f(a)=x_{i+1}
and
w=2y_{i}=2(x_{i+1}-1)

we conclude.

Then we can apply Lemma
2.10
and following the same argument of Case I, we get again to a contradiction. This conclude the proof of the theorem.
∎

Reconstructing algebra objects from NIM-reps in pointed, near-group and quantum group-like fusion categories
Samuel Hannah, Ana Ros Camacho

Abstract
. In this article we study the possible Morita equivalence classes of algebras in three families of fusion categories (pointed, near-group and A ​ ( 1 , l ) 1 2 A\left(1,l\right)_{\frac{1}{2}} ) by studying the Non-negative Integer Matrix representations (NIM-reps) of their underlying fusion ring, and compare these results with existing classification results of algebra objects. 2.1.
Group Actions

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
a set.
A
G-action on
S
is a binary operation
\ast\colon G\times S\to S
such that, for all
s\in S
,

e\ast s=s\text{, and }(g\cdot h)\ast s=g\ast(h\ast s),

where

is the group identity element. A set with such an action is called a
G-set
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a
G
-set, and
s\in S
.

-

The
orbit of G through s
is the subset of
S
defined by

\mathrm{Orb}(s)=\{g\ast s|g\in G\}.

-

The
G
-action on
S
is called
transitive
if
\mathrm{Orb}(s)=S
.

-

The
stabiliser of s
is the subgroup of
G
defined by

\mathrm{Stab}(s)=\{g\in G|g\ast s=s\}.

For two elements
s,t\in S
, the orbits
\mathrm{Orb}(s),\mathrm{Orb}(t)
are either equal or disjoint. Hence the set
S
can be partitioned into a collection of transitive
G
-sets.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S
be a
G
-set, and take
s\in S
. There is an isomorphism of
G
-sets between the orbit
\text{Orb}(s)
and the set of left cosets
G/\text{Stab}(s)
, where
G
acts on the set of left cosets by left-translation.

Proposition 2.4
.

Two left coset
G
-sets
G/H
,
G/K
are isomorphic as
G
-sets if and only if
H,K
are conjugate as subgroups of
G
.

As a result of these propositions, we can study all
G
-sets by studying the
G
-sets of left cosets for all conjugacy classes of subgroups in
G
.

2.2.
Fusion Categories

A
monoidal category

\cal{C}
consists of a tuple
\left(\cal{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1},\alpha,\lambda,\rho\right)
where
\cal{C}
is a category,

satisfying coherence axioms (pentagon and triangle).

A monoidal category is called
rigid
if it comes equipped with left and right dual objects — that means, for every

\widetilde{\mathsf{ev}}_{X}\colon X\otimes{}^{*}X\to\mathbbm{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{coev}}_{X}\colon\mathbbm{1}\to{}^{*}X\otimes X
satisfying in both cases the usual conditions.

A
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
\cal{C}
is

if, for any two objects

\mathsf{Hom}{C}\left(V,W\right)
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space and every object has a finite filtration by simple objects. Further, we say

is
finite
if there are finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects. A
tensor category
is a locally finite, rigid, monoidal category such the the tensor product is
\mathbbm{k}
-linear in each slot and the monoidal unit is a simple object of the category.

Example 2.7
.

For
\cal{C}
a fusion category with

the representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple objects, the tensor product on

induces a natural multiplication on
\mathrm{Gr}\left(\cal{C}\right)
defined by the formula:

X_{i}X_{j}:=\left[X_{i}\otimes X_{j}\right]=\sum\limits_{k\in I}\left[X_{i}\otimes X_{j}\colon X_{k}\right]X_{k}

where

. This multiplication is associative, and thus

-ring with unit

.

Let
(R,B)
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-ring, and let

such that

appears in the decomposition of 1. Then, let

\tau\left(b_{i}\right)=\begin{cases}1&i\in I_{0},\\

Definition 2.8
.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-ring
(R,B)
is called a

if there exists an involution
({})^{*}\colon I\to I
,

of the label set

where

\tau\left(b_{i}b_{j}\right)=\begin{cases}1&i=j^{*},\\

We arrive to one of the main definitions of this paper:

Definition 2.9

A

is a unital, based ring of finite rank.

In order to later introduce the NIM-reps, it is convenient to observe the following property:

Proposition 2.10
.

(Rigidity property)
A fusion ring
(R,B)
can be equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
(-,-)
satisfying
(b_{i}b_{j},b_{k})=(b_{j},b_{i^{*}}\cdot b_{k})
.

Proof.

Let
(-,-)
be the symmetric bilinear form defined by the condition
(b_{i},b_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
. Then the property of
\tau(b_{i}b_{j})=\delta_{i,j^{*}}
can be reformulated as

(1,b_{i}b_{j})=(b_{i},b_{j^{*}})=(b_{i^{*}},b_{j})

It is clear that
(1,(b_{i}b_{j})b_{k})\tau((b_{i}b_{j})b_{k})=c_{ij}^{k^{*}}
, and by the cyclic invariance we get that

(1,(b_{i}b_{j})b_{k})=(1,b_{i}(b_{j}b_{k}))

Thus, we find that

((b_{i}b_{j})^{*},b_{k})=(b_{j^{*}}b_{i^{*}},b_{k})=(b_{i^{*}},b_{j}b_{k})

where the first equality uses that the induced map is an anti-involution. Relabelling of the indices gives the stated result.
∎

is governed by the matrix equation

(3.2.4)

CBC=\alpha\cdot C+|G|\cdot B^{-1}.

We are thus seeking to find choices of subgroups
\{H_{i}\}
such that there is an non-negative integer-valued symmetric matrix
C
satisfying
Equation

3.2.4
.

As the matrix
B
is invertible, we obtain a quadratic matrix equation in the variable
CB
;

(CB)^{2}=\alpha\cdot(CB)+|G|\cdot I,

where
I
is the identity matrix. As all of the coefficient matrices commute with each other, we can solve via the quadratic equation, giving us

(3.2.5)

CB=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\cdot I\pm\sqrt{(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)\cdot I}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\cdot I\pm\sqrt{(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)}\cdot Y

where
Y
is a square root of the identity matrix
I
. As all the elements in
CB
are non-negative, the non-diagonal elements of
Y
must all have the same sign. As both
Y
and
-Y
are square roots of the identity matrix, only one can provide a valid solution for
CB
, so we can always take the sign in
Equation

3.2.5
to be positive.

Remark 3.13
.

As the elements of
CB
are integers, the non-diagonal elements of
Y
must be divisible by
(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)^{-1/2}

Hence, the problem of classifying NIM-reps over
K(G,\alpha)
comes down to finding suitable square roots of identity matrices. We do this in full detail for
p=1,2
in the following propositions.

Proposition 3.14
.

NIM-reps over
K(G,\alpha)
consisting of one group orbit are parametrised by pairs
(H,c_{1,1})
, where
H\subseteq G
a subgroup and
c_{1,1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, such that
\alpha=c_{1,1}|G:H|-\frac{|H|}{c_{1,1}}
,
c_{1,1}
divides
|H|
and
(c_{1,1})^{2}|G:H|\geq|H|
.

Proof.

As
p=1
,
Y=1
is the only possible choice that leads to a valid solution. Let
H
be the subgroup of
G
that governs this orbit. The action of the non-invertible element
X
is given by a single non-negative integer
c_{1,1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. By
Remark

2.21
, this integer is in fact strictly positive.

If we switch to the element-wise notation,
Equation

3.2.5
can be written as

\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}=c_{1,1}|G:H|-\frac{1}{2}\alpha

By squaring both sides,

\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|=(c_{1,1})^{2}|G:H|^{2}-\alpha c_{1,1}|G:H|+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}

Rearranging this for
\alpha
gives the required equation, with the other conditions following from the condition that
\alpha
must be a non-negative integer.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

All irreducible NIM-reps over
K(G,\alpha)
consisting of two group orbits are parametrised by tuples
(H_{1},H_{2},c_{1,1},c_{2,2})
, where
H_{1},H_{2}\subseteq G
are subgroups and
c_{1,1},c_{2,2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, such that
\alpha=c_{1,1}|G:H_{1}|+c_{2,2}|G:H_{2}|
,
|G|
divides
|H_{1}||H_{2}|
, and
(\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|G|}+c_{1,1}c_{2,2})
is a square number.

Proof.

It is well known that all square root
Y
s of the 2-by-2 identity matrix are either diagonal matrices whose non-zero elements are from the set
\{-1,1\}
, or have the form

(3.2.6)

Y=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1,1}&y_{1,2}\\
y_{2,1}&-y_{1,1}\end{pmatrix}\text{ where }y_{1,1}^{2}+y_{1,2}y_{2,1}=1,\quad y_{1,2},y_{2,1}\neq 0.

It is clear by
Remark

3.11
that irreducible NIM-reps can only come from square root matrices of the form in
Equation

3.2.6
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be subgroups of
G
that govern the group orbits in the NIM-rep, and
C=\{c_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
the matrix governing the NIM-action of
X
.
By inputting this data into
Equation

3.2.5
, we get the following system of equations:

\displaystyle c_{1,1}|G:H_{1}|=\frac{1}{2}\alpha+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}y_{1,1}

\displaystyle c_{2,2}|G:H_{2}|=\frac{1}{2}\alpha-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}y_{1,1}

\displaystyle c_{1,2}|G:H_{2}|=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}y_{1,2}

\displaystyle c_{1,2}|G:H_{1}|=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}y_{2,1}

By adding the first two equations, we get that
\alpha=c_{1,1}|G:H_{1}|+c_{2,2}|G:H_{2}|
.
By multiplying the first two equations together and the last two equations together, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{1,1}c_{2,2}|G:H_{1}||G:H_{2}|=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}-\Bigg(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|\Bigg)(y_{1,1})^{2},

\displaystyle(c_{1,2})^{2}|G:H_{1}||G:H_{2}|=\Bigg(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|\Bigg)y_{1,2}y_{2,1}.

By using the defining relation in
Equation

3.2.6
, and rearranging to solve for
(c_{1,2})^{2}
, we find that

\displaystyle(c_{1,2})^{2}=\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|G|}+c_{1,1}c_{2,2}.

Hence we can always obtain
c_{1,2}
from the other input data.
The remaining conditions come from the fact that
c_{1,2}
must be a positive integer.

Hence, a two-orbit NIM-rep over
K(G,\alpha)
has input data of
(H_{1},H_{2},c_{1,1},c_{2,2})
satisfying the above conditions, and explicit
X
-action given by

\displaystyle X\vartriangleright m^{1}_{i}=c_{1,1}\sum_{k=1}^{|G:H_{1}|}m^{1}_{k}+\sqrt{\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|G|}+c_{1,1}c_{2,2}}\sum_{k=1}^{|G:H_{2}|}m^{2}_{k},

\displaystyle X\vartriangleright m^{2}_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|G|}+c_{1,1}c_{2,2}}\sum_{k=1}^{|G:H_{1}|}m^{1}_{k}+c_{2,2}\sum_{k=1}^{|G:H_{2}|}m^{2}_{k}.

∎

While this only completes the classification for NIM-reps consisting of two group orbits, this is sufficient to completely classify irreducible NIM-reps over the Tambara-Yamagami fusion rings.

Corollary 3.16
.

All irreducible NIM-reps over the Tambara-Yamagami fusion ring
K(G,0)
consist of at most two group orbits.

Proof.

By setting
\alpha=0
in
Equation

3.2.4
, we easily see that the matrix
CBC
must be diagonal. Element-wise, this means that

(CBC)_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}c_{i,k}c_{k,j}|G:H_{i}|=0,

for all
i\neq j
. However, by
Remark

3.11
, there always exist a choice of
i\neq j
such that both
c_{i,k},c_{i,j}\neq 0
when
p\geq 3
. Thus, as
C
is symmetric, the result follows.
∎

Example 3.17
.

(NIM-reps over the Ising fusion ring)

Recall
Example

2.14
. The Ising fusion ring can be viewed as the Tambara-Yamagami fusion ring
K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},0)
. So by
Corollary

3.16
, we only need to check for 1 and 2-orbit NIM-reps.

When
p=1
, we must have
c_{1,1}|\mathbb{Z}_{2}:H|=|H|/c_{1,1}
by
Proposition

3.14
. But as the only choices for
H
are the trivial group and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, both of which lead to non-integer values for
c_{1,1}
, we see that there are no 1-orbit NIM-reps over
K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},0)
.

For
p=2
, by
Proposition

3.15
we must have
0=c_{1,1}|G:H_{1}|+c_{2,2}|G:H_{2}|
, which only occurs when
c_{1,1}=c_{2,2}=0
. Hence, we see that
(c_{1,2}^{2})=\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|G|}
, which must be an square number.
The only possible choice of subgroups is
H_{1}\cong\{1\},H_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(or vice versa, which gives an equivalent NIM-rep). In this case,
c_{1,2}=1
.

Thus, there is only one irreducible NIM-rep over the Ising fusion ring, given by the tuple
(\{1\},\mathbb{Z}_{2},0,0)
.

For
p\geq 3
, there is not an explicit classification of square roots of the identity matrix. For instance, it is still unknown whether there exists a Hadamard matrix of order
4k
for every positive integer
k
. So, in what follows we will detail some relevant examples.

Example 3.18
.

A Hadamard matrix of order
n
satisfies
H_{n}H_{n}=nI_{n}
, and rows are mutually orthogonal and the matrix contains only
+1
and
-1
, so clearly
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}H_{n}
is a square root of the identity. However,
H_{n}
contains exactly
n(n-1)/2
elements that are
-1
, which contradicts the fact all non-diagonal elements of
Y
must be non-negative, of which there are
n(n-1)
. So the only value valid is
n=1
, which is the trivial case.

Example 3.19
.

(NIM-rep over
K(\mathbb{Z}_{q},q-1),q
prime)

Consider the
p
-by-
p
matrix that has
-\frac{1}{2}\alpha
as its diagonal elements, and
1
for all off-diagonal elements;

(3.2.7)

Z_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha&1&\ldots&1\\
1&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&1\\
1&\ldots&1&-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\end{pmatrix}

If there is a NIM-rep that corresponds to this matrix, it must have
c_{i,i}=0
for all i by looking
Equation

3.2.5

We want to find values of
\alpha
and
G
such that this matrix is a square root of
(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)\cdot I_{p}
. It is easily verified that that

(Z_{p}^{2})_{i,i}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+p-1,\quad(Z_{p}^{2})_{i,j}=p-2-\alpha,\;i\neq j.

Thus, the matrix
Z_{p}
may give a NIM-rep via

3.2.5
only if
\alpha=p-2
and
|G|=p-1
. Thus
Z_{p}
may give a NIM-rep over
K(G,p-2)
, where
|G|=p-1
. The existence of such a NIM-rep depends on the particular choice of group, but we can provide the case such that
p=q+1
, where
q
is prime.

The only group of order
q
is the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, so
Z_{q+1}
defines a NIM-rep only over
K(\mathbb{Z}_{q},q-1)
. By putting
Equation

3.2.7
into
Equation

3.2.5
, it is clear that
c_{i,j}|G:H_{j}|=1
for all

. As the only subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
are the trivial subgroup and the group itself, the only choice that leads to an integer value of

is if
H_{j}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q}
for all

. This gives
c_{i,j}=1
.

Thus, for
q
prime, we have a NIM-rep
(A,M)
over
K(\mathbb{Z}_{q},q-1)
where the

consists of
q+1
elements
\{m^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq q+1}
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-action on

is trivial, and the action of the non-invertible element
X
is given by

X\vartriangleright m^{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{q+1}m^{j}.

Note that to look at the possible case that a NIM-rep over
K(G,\alpha)
can be partitioned into three or more orbits, one would need to find symmetric integer matrix solutions to the matrix equation. We can use the following result to reduce the options in some circumstances.

Proposition 3.20
.

A NIM-rep over
K(G,\alpha)
consists of an odd number of group orbits if and only
\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|}
is a rational number.

Proof.

As the matrix
CB
contains only integers, from
Equation

3.2.5
we see that the matrix
\sqrt{(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)}\cdot Y
contains only rational numbers. Its determinant is then rational. But
Y
is a square root of the identity matrix, that has determinant
\pm 1
. Hence, the determinant of
\sqrt{(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)}\cdot Y
is equal to
(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)^{p/2}
. The result is then immediate, as the rational numbers are closed under multiplication, so this determinant is rational if and only if
(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+|G|)^{1/2}
is rational.
∎

We can also express
\alpha
in terms of elements of
C
and
B
:

Corollary 3.21
.

Suppose
(A,M)
is an irreducible NIM-rep over
K(g,\alpha)
that can be partitioned into
p

G
-orbits.

If
p=1
, then
\alpha=c_{1,1}|G:H_{1}|-|H_{1}|/c_{1,1}
.

If
p\geq 2
. Then
\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{c_{i,j}c_{j,q}}{c_{i,q}}|G:H_{j}|
for any orbit labels
i\neq q
where
c_{i,q}>0
.

Proof.

For the
p=1
case, we simply rearrange
Equation

3.2.2
for
\alpha
.
The
p=2
case is just as immediate, instead rearranging
Equation

3.2.3
. Using the fact
c_{i,1}
is non-zero by
Remark

3.11
, we always have at least one choice of matrix elements to write

in this form.
∎

3.3.
Admissible NIM-Reps and Algebra Objects for Pointed and Near-Group Fusion Categories

Finally, we extract algebra objects from the NIM-reps we have computed. While we cannot read out explicit algebra morphisms, we still are able to recover a collection of familiar results.

Proposition 3.24
.

(Admissible NIM-reps for group rings)
All irreducible NIM-reps over a group fusion rings
R(G)
are admissible.

Proof.

Let
M(H)
be a NIM-rep over
R(G)
. Recall that the basis elements are parametrised by the left cosets
G/H
. For any two NIM-basis elements
m_{g_{i}}
,
m_{g_{j}}
, we can have that
(g_{j}g_{i}^{-1})\vartriangleright m_{g_{i}}=m_{g_{j}}
. Hence any NIM-basis element can take the role of
m_{0}
in
Proposition

2.35
, thus
M(H)
is admissible.
∎

Remark 3.25
.

The algebra object associated to a NIM-rep
M(H)
over
R(G)
is then given by
\bigoplus_{h\in H}b_{h}
. It is clear from
Examples

3.5
and
3.4
that this object can be seen by counting the self-loops present in the NIM-graph. This agrees with the classification of algebras in pointed categories given in
[
23
,
28
]
.

Proposition 3.26
.

An admissible irreducible NIM-rep over a near-group fusion ring consists of either one group orbit, parameterised by
(H,c_{1,1})
, or two group orbits, parametrised by
(H_{1},H_{2},0,c_{2,2})
.

Proof.

For a NIM-rep consisting of one orbit, we can argue in the same way as
Proposition

2.35
that any NIM-basis element can be set as
m_{0}
in
Proposition

2.35
.

For a NIM-rep consisting of more than one group orbit, the only way to have an
m_{0}
that connects to the orbits it does not lie in is via the non-invertible element
X
. So for
n\in M
, where
n
is not in the same orbit as
m_{0}
, we must have
X\vartriangleright m_{0}=n
. But then by the fusion rules

X\vartriangleright n=X^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}=\sum_{i\in G\cdot m_{0}}m_{i}+\alpha X\vartriangleright m_{0}=\sum_{i\in G\cdot m_{0}}m_{i}+\alpha n

From this we see that the group orbits of
m_{0}
and
n
are only connected to each other, so by the irreducibility of the NIM-rep it only contains two group orbits.

If we label the orbit containing
m_{0}
by 1, and the one containing
n
by 2, we note that the condition
X\vartriangleright m_{0}=n
implies
c_{1,1}=0
. This gives the result.
∎

Remark 3.27
.

We thus have two forms of algebra objects arising in categories associated to near-group fusion rings.

•

For a NIM-rep paramatrised by
(H,c_{1,1})
, the algebra is given as an object by
\oplus_{h\in H}b_{h}\oplus c_{1,1}X
.

•

For a NIM-rep parametrised by
(H_{1},H_{2},0,c_{2,2})
, the algebra object is given by
\oplus_{h\in H_{1}}b_{h}
, i.e it is of the form of a group algebra object.

Algebra objects representing module categories over near-group categories have been studied previously at
[
22
,
15
]
: in the case of non-group theoretical Tambara-Yamagami categories with abelian
G
we have twisted group algebras, see
[
15
, Proposition 5.7]
.
[
22
, Section 8 and 9]
proceed in a slightly more general way, for
G
-graded fusion categories with
G
not necessarily abelian. Only for the case of Tambara-Yamagami (see
[
22
, Section 9]
) they recover the same two families of algebras we observe.

3.4.
(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
Fusion Rings and its Admissible NIM-Reps and Algebra Objects

Following
[
24
]
(other useful references are
[
13
,
14
]
), we can construct a modular tensor category
(A_{1},l)
from a quantum group of type
A_{1}
at level
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. The Grothendieck ring
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l))
has basis
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in[0,l]}
, and the fusion coefficients of
V_{i}V_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{l}c_{i,j}^{k}V_{k}
are given by

(3.4.1)

c_{i,j}^{k}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }|i-j|\leq k\leq\mathrm{min}(i+j,2k-i-j)\text{ and }k\equiv i+j\;\mathrm{mod}\;2,\\
0,&\mathrm{else}\end{cases}

We note here that, as seen by the fusion rules,
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l))
is commutative.

Definition 3.28
.

For an object
V_{i}\in\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l))
, we define the
length
of
V_{i}
to be
\mathrm{length(V_{i}):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{l}}c_{i,j}^{k}.

When
l
is a positive odd integer, we can define a modular subcategory
(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
by taking the full subcategory with simple objects

\mathrm{Irr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})=\Bigg\{V_{2i}\;|\;0\leq i\leq\frac{-1}{2}\Bigg\}.

Remark 3.29
.

In the fusion ring
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\mathrm{pt}})
, it is simple to check using the fusion rules that
V_{i}\neq V_{j}
if and only if
\mathrm{length}(V_{i})\neq\mathrm{length}(V_{j})
.

We shall now focus on finding the admissible NIM-reps of
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
. As
l=1
results in the trivial ring, we shall assume
l\geq 3
. Recall from
Proposition

2.35
that an admissible NIM-rep
(A,M)
over a fusion ring has a distinguished basis element
m_{0}
. We have seen in
Section

3.3
that in the case of group and near-group fusion rings, we can have objects in the ring basis
b_{i},b_{j}
such that
b_{i}\vartriangleright m_{0}=b_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}
, due to the invertibility of the group parts of these fusion rings. This is not the case when working with
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
(A,M)
be an admissible NIM-rep over
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
, and
V_{i}\neq V_{j}\in\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
such that
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{0},V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
. Then
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{0}\neq V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}
.

Proof.

If we assume that
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}
, then as the fusion ring is commutative, we have that
V_{i}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{j}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}
. But by
Remark

3.29
,
\mathrm{length}(V_{i})\neq\mathrm{length(V_{j})}
, and so the only way that
V_{i}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{j}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}
is if some
V_{k}
in the decomposition of
V_{i}^{2}
or
V_{j}^{2}
(whichever has larger length), satisfies
V_{k}\vartriangleright m_{0}=0
. This has been ruled out by
Remark

2.21
.
∎

Remark 3.31
.

If we have objects
V_{i},V_{j}\in\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\mathrm{pt}})
such that
\mathrm{length}(V_{i})>\mathrm{length}(V_{j})
, then it is easily verified using the fusion rules in
Equation

3.4.1
that

(V_{i}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})>(V_{j}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})
. Thus, if
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{p}\in M
, then we immediately have that
V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{p}\in M
. In the case of an admissible NIM-rep
(A,M)
over
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\mathrm{pt}})
, if the basis
M
has cardinality d, we immediately get that the objects
V_{j}
that satisfy
V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
are exactly those of
\mathrm{length}(V_{j})\leq d
.

Proposition 3.32
.

In any NIM-rep
(A,M)
over the fusion ring
(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
(V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{q})\leq 1
for all
m_{p},m_{q}\in M
.

Proof.

If we assume that
(V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{q})=a_{l-1,p}^{q}\geq 2
, we can use the fusion rules in
Equation

3.4.1
to obtain

m_{p}+V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{p}=V_{l-1}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{p}=a_{l-1,p}^{q}V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{q}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in L\\
k\neq q\end{subarray}}a_{l-1,p}^{k}V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{k}

Applying the form
(-,m_{p})
, and using the rigidity condition of the NIM-rep, we find that

(3.4.2)

(V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})\geq a_{l-1,p}^{q}(V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{q},m_{p})-1\geq 3

The fusion rules in
Equation

3.4.1
give that, when
l\geq 3
,

V_{2j}V_{2}=V_{2j-2}+V_{2j}+V_{2j+2},
when
1\leq j\leq\frac{l-3}{2},
and
V_{2}V_{l-1}=V_{l-3}+V_{l-1}.

We let
h_{i,p}:=\sum_{k\in L}a^{k}_{i,p}
, which counts the number of NIM-basis elements in the decomposition of
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{p}
. Applying the fusion rules to
V_{2}jV_{2}\vartriangleright m_{p}=\sum_{k\in L}a_{2,p}^{k}V_{2}j\vartriangleright m_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{2j-2}\vartriangleright m_{p}+V_{2j+2}\vartriangleright m_{p}

\displaystyle=(a_{2,p}^{p}-1)V_{2j}\vartriangleright m_{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in L\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}a_{2,p}^{k}V_{2j}\vartriangleright m_{k},\quad 1\leq j\leq\frac{l-3}{2}

\displaystyle V_{l-3}\vartriangleright m_{p}

\displaystyle=(a_{2,p}^{p}-1)V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in L\\
k\neq p\end{subarray}}a_{2,p}^{k}V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{k}

By noting that
h_{i,p}>1
for all choices of
i,p
, and
a_{2,p}^{p}\geq 3
by
Equation

3.4.2
, when we count the NIM-basis elements on each side we obtain the following inequalities;

\displaystyle h_{2j-2,p}+h_{2j+2,p}\geq 2h_{2j,p}+h_{2,p}-3,\quad 1\leq j\leq\frac{l-3}{2},

\displaystyle h_{l-3,p}\geq h_{l-1,p}+h_{2,p}-3

By taking the inequality for each
1\leq j\leq\frac{l-1}{2}
and summing them together, we obtain

\displaystyle 1+h_{2,p}+2h_{4,p}+...+2h_{l-3,p}+h_{l-1,p}

\displaystyle\geq 2h_{2,p}+2h_{4,p}+...+2h_{l-1,p}+\frac{l-1}{2}(h_{2,p}-3)

\displaystyle\implies\frac{3l-1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{l+1}{2}h_{2,p}+h_{l-1,p}\geq\frac{l+1}{2}h_{2,p}+2

\displaystyle\implies\frac{3l-5}{l+1}

\displaystyle\geq h_{2,p}

where the last inequality in the second line follows due to our initial assumption.
However, the last inequality cannot be satisfied as the fraction on the left-hand side is strictly less than 3 for all values of l, which contradicts
(V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})=a_{2,p}^{p}\geq 3
. Hence we have a contradiction, and so,
(V_{l-1}\ast m_{p},m_{q})\leq 1
, for all
m_{p},m_{q}\in M
.
∎

Lemma 3.33
.

In any NIM-rep
(A,M)
over
(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
(V^{2}_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})\leq 3
for all
m_{p},m_{q}\in M
.

Proof.

If we assume that
(V^{2}_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})>4
, then by
Proposition

3.32
, we have that
h_{l-1,p}=(V^{2}_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})>4
. By the fusion rules in
Equation

3.4.1
, we have that
(V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{p},m_{p})\geq 3
. We are in a very similar setup to the proof of
Proposition

3.32
, which if we follow through results in the inequality, we get

\frac{3l-9}{l+1}\geq h_{2,p}.

This fraction is also strictly less than 3, so we obtain the desired contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.34
.

Let
(A,M)
be an admissible NIM-rep over
(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then there exists no
m_{k}\in M
such that
(V^{2}_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{k},m_{k})=3
.

Proof.

Assume there exists some
m_{k}\in M
such that
(V^{2}_{k-1}\vartriangleright m_{k},m_{k})=3
. By
Lemma

3.33
, we can write

V_{k-1}\vartriangleright m_{k}=m_{x}+m_{y}+m_{z},

where
m_{x},m_{y},m_{z}\in M
are distinct NIM-basis elements. The fusion rule of
V_{l-1}^{2}
gives us that
(V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{k},m_{k})=2
.

As the NIM-rep is admissible and
m_{x},m_{y},m_{y}
are distinct NIM-basis elements, the cardinality of the NIM-basis
M
is at least
3
, so by
Remark

3.31
we know that
V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{0},V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
. We also know that there exists a
V_{j}\in(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
such that
V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}=m_{k}
. Using the fusion rules, we find that

V_{l-1}\vartriangleright m_{k}=V_{l-1}V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{l-1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{l+1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}.

Acting with
V_{l-1}
again on both sides and then using the form
(-,m_{k})
, we have

\displaystyle m_{k}+V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{k}

\displaystyle=V^{2}_{k-1}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}+2V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{j+4}\vartriangleright m_{0}

(3.4.3)

\displaystyle\implies V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{k}

\displaystyle=2V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{j+4}\vartriangleright m_{0}.

A second way to calculate
V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{k}
is as follows:

(3.4.4)

V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{k}=V_{2}V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{j-2}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0}.

By applying the form
(-,m_{k})
to both
Equation

3.4.3
and
Equation

3.4.4
, we have that

(3.4.5)

\displaystyle 2=2(V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})+(V_{j+4}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})

(3.4.6)

\displaystyle 1=(V_{j-2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})+(V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})

By the fusion rules
Equation

3.4.1
and
Remark

3.31
, it is clear that we can only satisfy
Equation

3.4.5
when
j\leq\frac{l\pm 1}{2}
, where the sign is determined by
l\equiv\mp 1\text{ mod }4
. But then we immediately have that
(V_{j-2}\vartriangleright m_{k},m_{1})=0
by
Remark

3.31
, so the above equations give us that

(3.4.7)

(V_{j+2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})=1,\;\text{ and }(V_{j+4}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})=0.

If we calculate the fusion rules, we see that the only term that appears in
(V_{j+2}V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{0})
and not in
(V_{j+4})
is
(V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{k})
. But as
V_{2}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
, we have by
Lemma

3.30
that this term is non-zero. Hence we can never satisfy
Equation

3.4.7
, giving a contradiction. Thus we must have
(V_{l-1}^{2}\vartriangleright m_{0},m_{0})\leq 2
for all
m_{k}\in M
.
∎

Proposition 3.35
.

Up to isomorphism, there is only one admissible NIM-rep over
A(1,l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Suppose we have an admissible NIM-rep
(A,M)
over
A(1,l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
where
M
has cardinality
d
. Then there exists a
V_{j}\in A(1,l)_{\frac{1}{2}}
where
\mathrm{length(V_{j})=d}
and
V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
. Acting with
V_{l-1}
we get that

V_{l-1}V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}=V_{l-1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}+V_{l+1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}.

Using the fusion rules
Equation

3.4.1
and
Definition

3.28
, if
j<\frac{k\pm 1}{2}
, (where the sign depends on
k\equiv\mp 1\text{ mod }4
), then length
(V_{l+1-j})=d+1
and so
V_{l-1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\notin M
, by
Remark

3.31
. Similarly, if
j>\frac{k\pm 1}{2}
, then
V_{l+1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\notin M
. In both cases, this leads to

(V^{2}_{l-1}V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0},V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0})\geq(V_{l-1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0},V_{l-1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0})+(V_{l+1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0},V_{l+1-j}\vartriangleright m_{0})=3.

This contradicts
V_{j}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
by
Proposition

3.34
, and so we must have that
j=\frac{k\pm 1}{2}
. All other objects
V_{i}
have length less than
V_{j}
, and so every
m_{k}\in M
is of the form
V_{i}\vartriangleright m_{0}\in M
. This gives that full NIM-rep structure is simply generated by the fusion rules.
∎

Now that we have all admissible NIM-reps over
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
, we turn our attention back to
(A_{1},l)
. We can form a second full subcategory
(A_{1},l)_{\mathrm{pt}}
by taking the simple objects
\mathrm{Irr((A_{1},l)_{pt})}=\{V_{0},V_{l}\}
.

Lemma 3.36

(
[
24
]
Section 4)
.

There is an equivalence of modular tensor categories
(A_{1},l)\simeq(A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}}\boxtimes(A_{1},l)_{\mathrm{pt}}

On the level of Grothendieck rings, this equivalence tells us that the basis set of
\mathrm{Gr((A,l))}
is equivalent to the direct product of basis sets of
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})=R(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, with the ring structure induced component-wise from the ring structures of
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
and
R(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
respectively.

If we have a NIM-rep
(A,M)
over
\mathrm{Gr((A,l))}
, we immediately gain a NIM-rep over both
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l)_{\frac{1}{2}})
by restricting along the natural embedding. NIM-reps over the Grothendieck ring
\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l))
have been classified in
[
12

and are in one-to-one correspondence with simply laced Dynkin diagrams with Coxeter number h=l+2. In the case that

\mathrm{Gr}((A_{1},l))
viewed as a NIM-rep over itself. The restriction of this NIM-rep to

.

Appendix A
Modular invariants and the exponents conjecture for low rank modular tensor categories, joint with Devi Young

Here we would like to test a conjecture that relates NIM-reps of modular tensor categories and their so-called modular invariants. Let us first introduce several notions.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\cal{C}
be a modular tensor category of rank
k
with modular data
S
and
T
. A
modular invariant
is a squared matrix
\mathscr{Z}
, with rows and columns labeled by
\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)
, satisfying:

MI1:

\mathscr{Z}S=S\mathscr{Z}
and
\mathscr{Z}T=T\mathscr{Z}
;

MI2:

\mathscr{Z}_{\mathrm{ab}}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\forall a,b\in\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)
; and

MI3:

\mathscr{Z}_{00}=1
.

The easiest example of a modular invariant is the identity matrix. Indeed, the most interesting ones would be different ones than this one.

Definition A.2
.

Given a modular invariant
M
associated to a modular tensor category
\cal{C}
of rank k with
\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)=\{\mathds{1},X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k-1}\}
, the
exponent
of
M
is the multi-set
\mathscr{E}_{M}
where
a\in\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)
appears with multiplicity
M_{\mathrm{aa}}
. We will denote the exponent as:
\mathscr{E}_{M}=\left(\mathds{1}^{M_{00}},X_{1}^{M_{11}},\ldots,X_{k-1}^{M_{k-1k-1}}\right)
.

Given a certain NIM-rep
N
in a modular tensor category, observed in the sense of
[
17
,
18
]
described in
Remark

2.25
, the matrices
N_{a}
can be simultaneously diagonalised by a unitary matrix. Each eigenvalue of
N_{a}
equals
S_{a,b}/S_{0,b}
, for some
b\in\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)
. Each eigenvalue corresponds to at most one element in
\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)
. This allows us to introduce a second, seemingly different notion of an exponent attached to the modular data:

Definition A.3
.

Let
N
be a NIM-rep associated to a modular tensor category of rank k with
\mathrm{Ob}\left(\cal{C}\right)=\{\mathds{1},X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k-1}\}
. The multiset associated to the assignment
a\mapsto N_{a}
is defined to be
\mathscr{E}_{a}\left(N\right)=\left(\mathds{1}^{n}_{0},X_{1}^{n_{1}},...,X_{k-1}^{n_{k-1}}\right)
, where
n_{b}\in\{0,1\}
is the multiplicity of
S_{a,b}/S_{0,b}
corresponding to an eigenvalue in
N_{a}
. This multiset is independent of
a
, so we define the
exponent
of
N
to be
\mathscr{E}\left(N\right):=\mathscr{E}_{a}\left(N\right)
.

These two notions seem to be related in the following way:

Conjecture A.4
.

Consider a rational conformal field theory described by a modular tensor category
\cal{C}
. Then, for every modular invariant
M
there is a NIM-rep
N
satisfying that:

\mathscr{E}\left(N\right)=\mathscr{E}_{M}.

There is certain evidence that this conjecture is not true in general, see e.g.
[
16
]
. Still, it is an interesting question to see up to which point it holds, and why it is the case. In what follows we test this conjecture for all unitary modular tensor categories of rank less or equal 4 as classified in
[
29
]
. For this, we use the NIM-reps we have computed in the present article since they all fall into the families we have classified. Tables
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
, and
5
contain the modular invariants and NIM-reps associated to each of these categories, as well as their respective associated exponents.

Here, note that the fusion rings of the Ising and
A\left(1,2\right)
MTCs, and the fusion rings of the toric and
D\left(1,4\right)
MTCs, are the same. As the NIM-reps do not carry any of the modular data beyond this, the NIM-reps for each of these pair of categories are the same. For the Ising and
A\left(1,2\right)
pair, the remaining modular data is so similar that we cannot even distinguish them using their modular invariants. This is not the case for the toric and
D\left(1,4\right)
pair.

What we see is that for rank 2,3 MTCs each modular invariant has a corresponding NIM-rep, and the correspondence is true. However, at rank 4 it already starts breaking down: the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
MTC has a NIM-rep that does not correspond to a modular invariant, and the toric and
D(4,1)
MTCs each having two modular invariants associated to a single NIM-rep.

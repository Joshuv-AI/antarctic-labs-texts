Motion of vortices for the extrinsic Ginzburg-Landau flow for vector fields on surfaces
Giacomo Canevari, Antonio Segatti

Abstract
. We consider the gradient flow of a Ginzburg-Landau functional of the type F ε extr ​ ( u ) := 1 2 ​ ∫ M | D ​ u | g 2 + | 𝒮 ​ u | g 2 + 1 2 ​ ε 2 ​ ( | u | g 2 − 1 ) 2 ​ vol g F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|Du\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\mathscr{S}u\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}_{g}-1\right)^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g} which is defined for tangent vector fields (here D D stands for the covariant derivative) on a closed surface M ⊆ ℝ 3 M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3} and includes extrinsic effects via the shape operator 𝒮 \mathscr{S} induced by the Euclidean embedding of M M . The functional depends on the small parameter ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . When ε \varepsilon is small it is clear from the structure of the Ginzburg-Landau functional that | u | g \left|u\right|_{g} “prefers” to be close to 1 1 . However, due to the incompatibility for vector fields on M M between the Sobolev regularity and the unit norm constraint, when ε \varepsilon is close to 0 0 , it is expected that a finite number of singular points (called vortices) having non-zero index emerges (when the Euler characteristic is non-zero). This intuitive picture has been made precise in the recent work by R. Ignat & R. Jerrard [ 7 ] . In this paper we are interested the dynamics of vortices generated by F ε extr F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}} . To this end we study the behavior when ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 of the solutions of the (properly rescaled) gradient flow of F ε extr F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}} . In the limit ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 we obtain the effective dynamics of the vortices. The dynamics, as expected, is influenced by both the intrinsic and extrinsic properties of the surface M ⊆ ℝ 3 M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3} . 1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper we continue the analysis of the dynamics of Ginzburg-Landau
vortices on a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold started in
[
3
]

by considering the effects of the extrinsic geometry of the manifold.
More precisely, let
M
be a closed, oriented two-dimensional Riemannian manifold isometrically embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with genus
\mathfrak{g}=\frac{2-\chi(M)}{2}\geq 0
,
\chi(M)
being the Euler Characteristic of
M
. We denote with
g
the metric induced by the embedding and with
D
the Levi Civita connection of the metric
g
on
M
.
Let us denote with
N
the unit normal to
M
chosen accordingly with the orientation. The role of the extrinsic geometry, which is related to the fact that the manifold
M
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, is played by the so-called shape operator of
M
.
The shape operator is the self-adjoint operator

(1)

\mathscr{S}:TM\to TM,\qquad\mathscr{S}v:=-\nabla_{v}N,

where
\nabla
is the connection with respect to the standard metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Being self-adjoint, we can define the operator

\mathscr{S}^{2}:TM\to TM,\qquad\left(\mathscr{S}^{2}u,\,v\right)_{g}:=(\mathscr{S}u,\,\mathscr{S}v)_{g}\quad\textrm{for any }u,v\in TM.

For any
\varepsilon>0
we consider the following evolution for the vector field
u_{\varepsilon}\colon M\times[0,\,+\infty)\to TM

(2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}-\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}+\mathscr{S}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-1\right)u_{\varepsilon}=0,\,\,\,\,\hbox{ a.e. in }M\times(0,T),\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\,\,\,\,\hbox{ a.e. in }M.\end{cases}

The operator
\Delta_{g}:=-D^{*}D
is the so-called rough Laplacian (
D^{*}
is the adjoint of the covariant derivative, see
[
3
, Section 2]
for the definition).
The evolution (
2
) is the gradient flow of the following functional (called the extrinsic Ginzburg-Landau functional, see
[
16
]
)

(3)

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|Du\right|_{g}^{2}+\left|\mathscr{S}u\right|^{2}_{g}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}_{g}-1\right)^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Given a smooth vector field
u
, we smoothly extend it to a vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and we call this extension
\tilde{u}
. For
p\in M
, we define the surface gradient of
u
as

(4)

\nabla_{s}u(p):=\nabla\tilde{u}(p)\,P(p),

where
P(p):=(\textrm{Id}-N(p)\otimes N(p))
is the orthogonal projection on
T_{p}M
. This differential operator is well defined and does not depend on the chosen extension
\tilde{u}
. Moreover, in general,
\nabla_{s}u\neq Du=P\nabla\tilde{u}
. Finally, thanks to the Gauss Formula

\nabla_{v}u=D_{v}u+(\mathscr{S}u,v)_{g}N\qquad\textrm{for any }v\in T_{p}M,\ p\in M,

we readily get

\nabla_{s}u[v]=\nabla_{v}u=D_{v}u+(\mathscr{S}u,v)_{g}N\qquad\textrm{for any }v\in T_{p}M,\ p\in M.

Thus, since the above decomposition is orthogonal, we deduce

(5)

\left|\nabla_{s}u\right|^{2}=\left|Du\right|^{2}+\left|\mathscr{S}u\right|^{2}

at any point of
M
.
Therefore, the extrinsic Ginzburg-Landau energy (
3
) rewrites as

(6)

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|\nabla_{s}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}_{g}-1\right)^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

This type of energy enters in the modeling of the so-called Nematic Shells. A Nematic Shell is a thin film of nematic liquid crystal coating a rigid and curved substrate
M
, typically modeled as a two-dimensional closed and oriented surface (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
).
In this framework, the preferred direction of the molecular alignment is usually described by a unit-norm vector field tangent to
M
and the energy
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}
is a
relaxation (relaxing in particular the unit-norm constraint) of the elastic energy (see
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
)

(7)

F_{0}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\nabla_{s}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g}\qquad&\textrm{if }\left|u\right|=1\ \textrm{a.e. in }M\\
+\infty\qquad&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

A feature of the functional
F_{0}^{\mathrm{extr}}
is to incorporate,
via the surface gradient
\nabla_{s}
,
the extrinsic geometry of
M
in the local distorsion element.
From the analysis standpoint,
due to the incompatibility between the unit-norm constraint and the Sobolev regularity, this energy is well-defined and not identically equal to
+\infty

only if
\chi(M)=0
(see
[
16
]
and
[
4
]
).
When
\chi(M)=0
one can prove (see e.g.
[
5
]
) that
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{0}^{\mathrm{extr}}

in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence.
When
\chi(M)\neq 0
the analysis is more involved as one has to deal with the emergence of vortices. In this case, Ignat & Jerrard obtained (see
[
7
]
) a
\Gamma
-convergence result for
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}
. After a proper scaling, what emerges in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
is a renormalized energy that takes into account the interactions between the vortices and between the vortices and the geometry of the surface, both intrinsic and extrinsic.
This energy is given by the sum of the renormalized energy obtained in the intrinsic case and of a new, purely extrinsic, term.

As in the analysis of the intrinsic case, a central role
is played by the so-called vorticity.
We recall that that the vorticity
\omega(u)
of a smooth vector field
u

is defined as the
2
-form

(8)

\omega(u):=\mathrm{d}j(u)+\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}_{g},

where
\kappa
is the Gauss curvature of
M
and
j(u)
is the
1
-form

(9)

j(u):=(Du,iu)_{g}.

The symbol

denotes an almost complex structure on
M
, that is,
an operator
TM\to TM
which restricts to a linear map
on each tangent plane
T_{p}M
and satisfies
i^{2}+1=0

(where
1
denotes the identity on
TM
).
In the Euclidean setting, when
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
the vorticity
\omega(u)
essentially reduces to the
Jacobian determinant of
u
, i.e.
\omega(u)=2(\det\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x
.

Let
W^{1,p}_{\tang}(M)
be the set of maps
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\mathbb{R}^{3})

such that
u\cdot N=0
a.e. (see e.g.
[
16
]
,
[
7
]

and
[
3
]
for the details). Since

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u)\geq F_{\varepsilon}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|Du\right|_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}_{g}-1\right)^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g}\qquad\textrm{for any }u\in W^{1,2}_{\tang}(M),

sequences
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfying a energy bound such as
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq n\pi\left|\log\varepsilon\right|+C

(for some constants
n
and
C
that do not depend on
\varepsilon
)
have compact vorticities. More precisely (see
[
7
]
),
there exist distinct points
a_{1}
, …,
a_{n}
in
M
,
integers
d_{1}
, …,
d_{n}
,
a form
\xi\in\mathrm{Harm}^{1}(M)
and a (non-relabelled) subsequence
such that, denoting with
\mathbb{P}_{H}
the
L^{2}
-projection
onto the space
\mathrm{Harm}^{1}(M)
of harmonic
1
-forms,

\displaystyle\omega(u_{\varepsilon})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}\delta_{a_{j}}\qquad\hbox{ in }W^{-1,p}(M)\quad\textrm{for any }p\in(1,\,2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{H}j(u_{\varepsilon})\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}\xi

The integers
d_{j}
represent the charges of
topological singularities which arise at the points
a_{j}
.
They satisfy

\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}=\chi(M),

in accordance with the Poincaré-Hopf theorem.
The harmonic
1
-form
\xi\in\mathrm{Harm}^{1}(M)
,
which emerges when the genus
\mathfrak{g}\neq 0
, depends non-trivially
on
\mathbf{a}:=\left(a_{1},\,\ldots,\,a_{n}\right)

and
\mathbf{d}:=(d_{1},\,\ldots,\,d_{n})
, as it must satisfy
the constraint
\xi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d})

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d})
is a suitable subset of
\mathrm{Harm}^{1}(M)

(see
[
7
, Section 2.1]
and
[
3
, Section 3]

for the definition of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d})
.)

In case the
u_{\varepsilon}
’s are minimizers of
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}
,
the results of
[
7
]
provide a more detailed description
of the asymptotic behaviour as
\varepsilon\to 0
. First, the number of
the singular points
a_{1}
, …,
a_{n}
that arise in the limit is
exactly equal to
n=\left|\chi(M)\right|
, and each singularity
has degree
d_{j}=\sign(\chi(M))
. Second, not only the
vorticities
\omega(u_{\varepsilon})
but also the minimisers
u_{\varepsilon}

themselves converge, strongly in
W^{1,p}(M)
for any
p<2
,
up to extraction of a subsequence. The limit field
u_{0}

belongs to
W^{1,p}(M)
for any
p<2
,
is smooth in
M\setminus\{a_{1},\,\ldots,\,a_{n}\}
,
and satisfies
\left|u_{0}\right|=1
a.e. Moreover,
u_{0}
can be written
in the form

(10)

u_{0}=e^{i\theta}u^{*}:=(\cos\theta)u^{*}+(\sin\theta)iu^{*}

The vector field
u^{*}
in the right-hand side of Equation (
10
)
is the so-called
canonical harmonic field

associated with
(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi)
.
That means,
u^{*}
is a
W^{1,1}
-vector field on
M

that satisfies
\left|u^{*}\right|=1
a.e. and
j(u^{*})=\mathrm{d}^{*}\Psi+\xi
,
where the
2
-form
\Psi=\Psi[\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d}]
is uniquely determined
(up to an additive constant) as a solution of

-\Delta\Psi=2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}\delta_{a_{j}}-\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Such a vector field
u^{*}
exists if and only if
\xi

satisfies the constraint
\xi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d})
;
if it exists,
u^{*}
is essentially unique
(i.e., it is unique up to a constant rotation;
see
[
7

).
The limit field
u_{0}
differs from the canonical harmonic
vector field
u^{*}
by a rotation
e^{i\theta}
.
The rotation angle
\theta\in H^{1}(M)
in (
10
)
must be chosen so as to minimize the functional

(11)

\mathscr{G}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi,\,\theta):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|\mathrm{d}\theta\right|^{2}_{g}+\left|\mathscr{S}(e^{i\theta}u^{*})\right|^{2}_{g}\mathrm{vol}_{g}

and
u^{*}=u^{*}[\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi]
is as above.
The functional
\mathscr{G}
accounts for the “extrinsic”
contributions to the energy, i.e., the effects of the shape operator.
Finally, the results of
[
7
]
provide an energy expansion
(by
\Gamma
-convergence); in particular,
minimizers
u_{\varepsilon}
of
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}
satisfy

(12)

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{extr}}(u_{\varepsilon})=\pi n\left|\log\varepsilon\right|+\widetilde{W}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi)+n\gamma+\mathrm{o}(1)\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

The constant
\gamma>0
, analogously to the intrinsic case,
is the so-called core energy and accounts for the energy
in a suitably small neighbourhood of each singular point
a_{j}
.
The energy
\widetilde{W}
is the so-called
renormalized energy
,
that accounts for the interactions among the singularities.
The renormalised energy accounts for both intrinsic and
extrinsic contributions. It is given by

(13)

\widetilde{W}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi):=W^{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi)+\inf_{\theta\in H^{1}(M)}\mathscr{G}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi,\,\theta)

The intrinsic component
W^{\mathrm{intr}}
is defined as

(14)

W^{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{n}B_{\rho}(a_{j})}\left|Du^{*}\right|^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}^{2}\left|\log\rho\right|\right)

where
u^{*}=u^{*}[\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi]
is the (essentially unique)
canonical harmonic field for
(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi)
.
The extrinsic contributions are accounted for by the functional
\mathscr{G}
,
as defined in (
11
). While it is known that
W^{\mathrm{intr}}

is well-defined and smooth (see e.g.
[
7

),
it is unclear whether
\widetilde{W}
is smooth,
for the functional
\mathscr{G}(\mathbf{a},\,\mathbf{d},\,\xi,\,\cdot)

When dealing with the analysis of the solutions of (
2

\varepsilon\to 0

[

\begin{split}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}{2}\int_{M}\left|-\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}(t)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(|u_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}-1)u_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{2}_{g}\,\mathrm{vol}_{g}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\nabla_{a_{j}}\widetilde{W}(\mathbf{a}(t),\mathbf{d},\xi(t))\right|_{g}^{2}\end{split}

Unfortunately, for the moment, such an estimate is
not
available.
The reason is twofold. The first reason, as we have seen,
is that the minimization of
\mathscr{G}
introduces a source of
non-smoothness in the energy
\widetilde{W}
and makes

\widetilde{W}
.
The second reason is the following.
Given a sequence of solutions

of (

),
that the sequence

is compact

More precisely, for a.e.

there exists a subsequence

\varepsilon_{h}(t)\to 0

converges (strongly in

)
to a vector field of the form

As in (

Defining the gradient of

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{D}_{a_{k}}\mathscr{G}(u^{*}[\mathbf{a}^{0}],\,\theta)[v]}{2\pi d_{k}}&=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi\eta}\int_{\partial B_{\eta}(a_{k}^{0})}(i\nu,\,w)_{g}\left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\nu}+\frac{\theta-\theta(a_{k}^{0})}{\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{1}\end{split}

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{D}_{a_{k}}\mathscr{G}(u^{*}[\mathbf{a}^{0}],\,\theta)[v]}{2\pi d_{k}}&=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{1}{\pi\eta}\int_{\partial B_{\eta}(a_{k}^{0})}(i\nu,\,w)_{g}\,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{1}\end{split}

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{D}_{a_{k}}\mathscr{G}(u^{*}[\mathbf{a}^{0}],\,\theta)[v]}{2\pi d_{k}}&=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(-v^{1}\sin\phi+v^{2}\cos\phi\right)\,\left(\partial_{1}\theta(\eta e^{i\phi})\cos\phi+\partial_{2}\theta(\eta e^{i\phi})\sin\phi\right)\mathrm{d}\phi\\

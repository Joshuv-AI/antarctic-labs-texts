Applications of the icosahedral equation for the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction


Abstract
Let R ⁡ ( q ) R(q) denote the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction for | q | < 1 |q|<1 . By applying the RootApproximant command in the Wolfram language to expressions involving the theta function f ⁡ ( − q ) := ( q , q ) ∞ f(-q):=(q;q)_{\infty} given in modular relations due to Yi, this provides a systematic way of obtaining experimentally discovered evaluations for R ⁡ ( e − π ​ r ) R\big(e^{-\pi\sqrt{r}}\big) , for r ∈ ℚ > 0 r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0} . We succeed in applying this approach to obtain explicit closed forms, in terms of radicals over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , for the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction that have not previously been discovered or proved. We prove our closed forms using the icosahedral equation for R R together with closed forms for and modular relations associated with Ramanujan’s G G - and g g -functions. An especially remarkable closed form that we introduce and prove is for R ⁡ ( e − π ​ 48 / 5 ) R\big(e^{-\pi\sqrt{48/5}}\big) , in view of the computational difficulties surrounding the application of an order-25 modular relation in the evaluation of G 48 / 5 G_{48/5} .

Preliminaries and background

The
elliptic lambda function
satisfies

\lambda^{\ast}(r)=\frac{\theta_{2}^{2}\left(0,e^{-\pi\sqrt{r}}\right)}{\theta_{3}^{2}\left(0,e^{-\pi\sqrt{r}}\right)},

(8)

where the Jacobi theta functions
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
are such that

\theta_{2}(z,q)=2q^{1/4}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)}\cos((2n+1)z)

(9)

and

\theta_{3}(z,q)=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}\cos(2nz).

(10)

The special function in (
8

Ramanujan’s
G
- and
g
-functions, which may, respectively, be defined so that

G_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}e^{-\pi\sqrt{n}/24}}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(1+e^{-(2j+1)\pi\sqrt{n}}\right)

and

g_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}e^{-\pi\sqrt{n}/24}}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-(2j+1)\pi\sqrt{n}}\right).

Explicitly, we have that

\lambda^{\ast}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+G_{n}^{-12}}-\sqrt{1-G_{n}^{-12}}\right)

(11)

and that

\lambda^{\ast}(n)=g_{n}^{6}\left(\sqrt{g_{n}^{12}+g_{n}^{-12}}-g_{n}^{6}\right).

The Jacobi theta functions in (
9
) and (
10
)
play a key role in the below formulation of the

icosahedral equation
, which is of key importance in our work.
The icosahedral equation for the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction
[
8
]

may be formulated in such a way so that

1728J(\tau)=-\frac{\left(r^{20}-228r^{15}+494r^{10}+228r^{5}+1\right)^{3}}{r^{5}\left(r^{10}+11r^{5}-1\right)^{5}},

(12)

where

denotes
Klein’s absolute invariant
, which may be defined as follows,
and where
r=r(\tau)=R\big(e^{2\pi i\tau}\big)

By analogy with (

\lambda(\tau)=\frac{\theta_{2}^{4}(e^{i\pi\tau})}{\theta_{3}^{4}(e^{i\pi\tau})}.

The

J(\tau)=\frac{4}{27}\frac{\left(1-\lambda(\tau)+\lambda^{2}(\tau)\right)^{3}}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)(1-\lambda(\tau))^{2}}.

Preliminaries

Let
\bar{M}
be an
n
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold
[
18
]

\xi

(2.1)

g(\xi,\xi)=-1.

Since

there exists a non-zero 1-form
\eta

(2.2)

g(X,\xi)=\eta(X),

the equation of the following form holds

31

(2.3)

(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\eta)(Y)=\alpha\{g(X,Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y)\},\ \ \ (\alpha\neq 0)

(2.4)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi=\alpha\{X+\eta(X)\xi\},\ \ \ \alpha\neq 0

X
,
Y

\bar{\nabla}
denotes the

(2.5)

{\bar{\nabla}}_{X}\alpha=(X\alpha)=d\alpha(X)=\rho\eta(X),

\rho
being a certain scalar function given by
\rho=-(\xi\alpha)
.
If we put

(2.6)

\phi X=\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi,

2.4

2.6

(2.7)

\phi X=X+\eta(X)\xi,

(2.8)

g(\phi X,Y)=g(X,\phi Y)

from which it follows that
\phi
is a symmetric (1,1) tensor and
called the structure tensor of the manifold. Thus the Lorentzian
manifold
\bar{M}
together with the unit timelike concircular vector
field
\xi
, its associated 1-form
\eta
and an (1,1) tensor field

is said to be a Lorentzian concircular structure manifold

-manifold),

[
15

-manifold

(2.9)

\eta(\xi)=-1,\ \ \phi\xi=0,\ \ \ \eta(\phi X)=0,\ \ \ g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y),

(2.10)

\phi^{2}X=X+\eta(X)\xi,

(2.11)

(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y=\alpha\{g(X,Y)\xi+2\eta(X)\eta(Y)\xi+\eta(Y)X\},

(2.12)

(X\rho)=d\rho(X)=\beta\eta(X)

is a scalar function.

with

(2.13)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}V=-A_{V}X+\nabla^{\perp}_{X}V

,

For any

(2.15)

\displaystyle\text{(a)}\ \phi X=PX+QX,\ \ \text{(b)}\ \phi V=bV+cV

A submanifold

\phi(T_{p}M)\subseteq T_{p}M

A submanifold

\mathcal{D}:p\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{p}\subseteq T_{p}M

\mathcal{D}

The normal space of a CR-submanifold

is decomposed as
T^{\bot}M=Q\mathcal{D^{\bot}}\oplus\nu
,

\nu

M
with respect to
\phi
.

A submanifold
M

(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}

X\in T_{p}M

\phi X

(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}

\mathcal{D^{\bot}}
and

(i) TM admits the orthogonal direct decomposition

,

(ii) The distribution
\mathcal{D^{\bot}}
is anti-invariant,

(iii) The distribution
\mathcal{D^{\theta}}
is slant with slant angle
\theta\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
.

From the definition it is clear that if
\theta=0
, then
M
is a CR-submanifold. We say that a pseudo-slant submanifold is proper if

\theta\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
. The normal space of a pseudo-slant submanifold

is decomposed as
T^{\bot}M=Q\mathcal{D^{\theta}}\oplus\phi\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus\nu
.

On a slant submanifold
M
of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
, we have

4
]

(2.16)

\displaystyle P^{2}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta[X+\eta(X)\xi],

(2.17)

\displaystyle Q^{2}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta[X+\eta(X)\xi],

where
\theta
is the slant angle of
M
in
\bar{M}
.

From (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we get

(2.18)

\displaystyle g(PX,PY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta[g(X,Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y)],

(2.19)

\displaystyle g(QX,QY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta[g(X,Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y)],

for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TM)
.

Also for a slant submanifold from (

) and (

(2.20)

bQX=\sin^{2}\theta(X+\eta(X)\xi)\ \ \text{and}\ \ cQX=-QPX

For a Riemannian manifold
\bar{M}
of dimension
n
and a smooth function

on

\bar{M}

\nabla f
, the gradient of

which is defined by

Definition 2.1
.

[
6
]

Let
(N_{1},g_{1})
and

be two Riemannian manifolds with Riemannian metric

and

respectively and

N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}=(N_{1}\times N_{2},g)
, where

(2.22)

g=g_{1}+f^{2}g_{2}.

A warped product manifold

Proposition 2.1
.

[
18

Let

\displaystyle\nabla_{U}X=\nabla_{X}U=(X\ln f)U,

for any
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TN_{1})

.

Theorem 2.1

.

[
7

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and

and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are both involutive such that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a totally geodesic foliation and
\mathcal{D}_{2}

and

\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
,

3.
characterization for contact CR-warped product submanifolds

In
[
11
]
it is shown that contact CR-warped product submanifolds of
\bar{M}
of the form

N_{\bot}\times_{f}N_{T}
, where
N_{T}
and
N_{\bot}
are invariant and anti-invariant submanifolds of
\bar{M}
respectively,
exists if
\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
and does not exists if
\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{T})
.
In this section we find a characterization for a submanifolds
M
of
\bar{M}
to be contact CR-warped product of the form

N_{\bot}\times_{f}N_{T}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
. First we prove the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M=N_{\bot}\times_{f}N_{T}
be a warped product submanifold of
\bar{M}
such that

\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)=-\alpha\eta(Z)g(X,Y)-(Z\ln f)g(\phi X,Y)

for
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TN_{T})
and
Z\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
.

Proof.

For
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TN_{T})
and
Z,\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
we have
from (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,Z)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi Y,\nabla_{X}Z)-\alpha g(X,Y)\eta(Z).

By virtue of Proposition 2.1 from the above relation we get (
3.1
).
∎

Now interchanging
X
by
\phi X
and
Y
by
\phi Y
, we get the following respective relations

(3.2)

\displaystyle g(h(\phi X,Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\eta(Z)g(\phi X,Y)-Z(\ln f)g(X,Y),

(3.3)

\displaystyle g(h(X,\phi Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\eta(Z)g(\phi X,Y)-Z(\ln f)g(X,Y),

(3.4)

\displaystyle g(h(\phi X,\phi Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\eta(Z)g(X,Y)-Z(\ln f)g(\phi X,Y).

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
M=N_{\bot}\times_{f}N_{T}
be a warped product submanifold of
\bar{M}
such that

\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
. Then

\displaystyle g(h(\phi X,Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(h(X,\phi Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ \ \ \ g(h(\phi X,\phi Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)

for
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TN_{T})
and
Z\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
.

Now we have the following characterization theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be a contact CR-submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi
is
tangent to the anti-invariant distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
. Then
M
is locally a warped product submanifold if and only if

(3.5)

A_{\phi Z}X=-\alpha\eta(Z)X-(Z\mu)\phi X

for any
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and also for some smooth function
\mu
on
M
such that

(Y\mu)=0
for any
Y\in D
.

Proof.

If
M
be a contact CR-warped product submanifold, then for any
X\in\Gamma(TM_{T})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(TM_{\bot})
,
we have

g(A_{\phi Z}X,W)-g(h(X,W),\phi Z)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{W}X,\phi Z)=g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{W}X,Z).

Using (
2.11
) in the above equation we get

(3.6)

g(A_{\phi Z}X,W)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{W}\phi X,Z).

Then from (
2.13
) and Proposition 2.1 we have from (
3.6
) that
g(A_{\phi Z}X,W)=0
and therefore
A_{\phi Z}X
has no
component in
\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
. Hence by virtue of Lemma 3.1, the relation (
3.5
) follows.

Conversely, let
M
be a contact CR-submanifold of
\bar{M}
with the invariant and anti-invariant distributions

D
and
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
such that the relation (
3.5
) holds. Then for any
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, and
using (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{Z}W,\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)W,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(A_{\phi W}X,Z).

Using (
3.5
) in above relation we get

g(\nabla_{Z}W,\phi X)=0.

Similarly, we get
g(\nabla_{W}Z,\phi X)=0.

Thus we obtain

(3.7)

g(\nabla_{Z}W+\nabla_{W}Z,\phi X)=0,

Which implies that
\nabla_{Z}W+\nabla_{W}Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
is integrable and its leaves are totally geodesic in
M
.
Again for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,\phi Z)+\eta(Z)g(Y,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi).

Using (
2.6
) in (
3.8
), we get

(3.9)

g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=g(h(X,\phi Y),\phi Z)+\alpha\eta(Z)g(Y,\phi X).\\

Interchanging
X
and
Y
in (
3.9
), we get

(3.10)

g(\nabla_{Y}X,Z)=g(h(\phi X,Y),\phi Z)+\alpha\eta(Z)g(X,\phi Y).

From (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we have

(3.11)

g([X,Y],Z)=g(h(X,\phi Y),\phi Z)-g(h(\phi X,Y),\phi Z).

Using (
3.5
) in (
3.11
), we get
g([X,Y],Z)=0
and therefore
\mathcal{D}
is integrable on
M
.

Let us consider a leaf
N_{T}
of
\mathcal{D}
in
M
and let
h^{T}
be the second fundamental form
of
N_{T}
in
M
, then we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle g(h^{T}(X,Y),Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Y}X,\phi Z)-\eta(Z)g(\bar{\nabla}_{Y}X,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi X,\bar{\nabla}_{Y}\phi Z)+\eta(Z)g(X,\bar{\nabla}_{Y}\xi).

Using (
2.6
) and (
2.14
), (
3.12
) yields

(3.13)

g(h^{T}(X,Y),Z)=g(\phi X,A_{\phi Z}Y)+\alpha\eta(Z)g(X,\phi Y).

From (
3.5
) and (
3.13
), we obtain

(3.14)

g(h^{T}(X,Y),Z)=-(Z\mu)g(X,Y).

Using (
2.21
) in (
3.14
), we get

(3.15)

\displaystyle h^{T}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\nabla\mu)g(X,Y),

where
\nabla\mu
is the gradient of the function
\mu
and therefore
N_{T}
is totally umbilical in
M
with
mean curvature
-\nabla\mu
. Moreover, the condition
(Y\mu)=0
, for any
Y\in\mathcal{D}
implies that the leaves
of
\mathcal{D}
are extrinsic spheres in
M
, i.e., the integral manifold
N_{T}
of
\mathcal{D}
is umbilical and its mean curvature
vector field is non zero and parallel along
N_{T}
. Hence by Hiepko’s theorem
M
is locally a warped product
N_{\bot}\times_{f}N_{T}
,
where
N_{T}
and
N_{\bot}
denote the integral manifolds of the distributions
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
respectively and
f
is the warping
function. Thus the theorem is proved completely.
∎

4.
characterization for warped product pseudo slant submanifolds

Recently Hui et al.
[
10
]
studied warped product pseudo-slant submanifolds of
(LCS)_{n}
-manifolds. In this section we
obtain a characterization for a submanifold
M
of
\bar{M}
to be a warped product pseudo-slant submanifold of the form

N_{\theta}\times_{f}N_{\bot}
, where
N_{\theta}
is a slant submanifold tangent to
\xi
and

N_{\bot}
is an anti-invariant submanifolds of
\bar{M}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a proper pseudo-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
with anti-invariant and proper slant dfistributions

\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
, respectively such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
. Then

(4.1)

g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=\sec^{2}\theta[g(h(X,PY),\phi Z)+g(h(X,Z),QPY)],

for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(D^{\bot})
.

Proof.

For any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,\phi Z)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}XPY,\phi Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(h(X,PY),\phi Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}X\phi QY,Z)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)QY,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(h(X,PY),\phi Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}bQY,Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}cQY,Z).

Using (
2.20
) in the above relation, we get

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(h(X,PY),\phi Z)+\sin^{2}\theta g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QPY,Z).

From which (
4.1
) follows.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
M
be a proper pseudo-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
with anti-invariant and proper slant distributions

\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
, respectively such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
. Then the distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}

defines a totally geodesic foliation if and only if

g(h(X,PY),\phi Z)+g(h(X,Z),QPY)=0

for every
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M=N_{\theta}\times_{f}N_{\bot}
be a warped product submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
, where
N_{\bot}
and
N_{\theta}
are ant-invariant
and proper slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(TN_{\theta})
. Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)+g(h(X,Z),QY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle g(h(Z,W),QX)+g(h(X,Z),QW)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi X\ln f)g(Z,W),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ \ \ g(h(Z,W),QPX)+g(h(PX,Z),QW)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta[(X\ln f)+\alpha\eta(X)]g(Z,W).

Proof.

For any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TN_{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}PY,Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,Z).

Using Proposition 2.1 in the above relation, we get

(4.5)

g(h(X,Y),\phi Z)=(X\ln f)g(Z,PY)-g(h(X,Z),QY).

Thus (
4.2
) follows from (
4.5
).
Also, for any
X\in\Gamma(TN_{\theta})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(TN_{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g(h(Z,W),QX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\phi X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,PX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)W,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,PX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(h(X,Z),\phi W)+g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}PX).

Using Proposition 2.1 in the above relation we get (
4.3
).
Interchanging
X
by
PX
in (
4.3
) we get (
5.4
)
∎

Now, we prove the following characterization theorem for warped product pseudo-slant submanifolds.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a proper pseudo-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
with anti-invariant distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}

and proper pseudo-slant distribution
\mathcal{D^{\theta}}
, respectively such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
. Then
M
is locally
a mixed-geodesic warped product submanifold of the form
N_{\theta}\times_{f}N_{\bot}
if and only if

(4.6)

A_{\phi Z}X=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ A_{QPX}Z=\cos^{2}\theta[(X\mu)+\alpha\eta(X)]Z,

for any
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D^{\theta}}),\ Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D^{\bot}})
and for some function
\mu
on
M
satisfying
(Z\mu)=0
, for
any
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
.

Proof.

Let
M=N_{\theta}\times_{f}N_{\bot}
be a mixed geodesic warped product submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
such that (
4.6
) holds.
Then for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, from (
4.2
) and (
5.4
), we get (
4.6
).

Conversely, Let
M
is a proper pseudo-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
such that (
4.6
) holds.

Then for any for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and for any
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, from (
4.1
) and (
4.6
), we get

g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=0
and hence the leaves of
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
are totally geodesic in
M
.

Also, for any
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and

Z,\ W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g([Z,W],X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{W}Z,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\phi X)-g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{W}Z,\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,\phi X)-g(\bar{\nabla}W\phi Z,\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi X)+g(\phi Z,\bar{\nabla}_{W}\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}PX)-g(\phi W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QX)+g(\phi Z,\bar{\nabla}_{W}PX)+g(\phi Z,\bar{\nabla}_{W}QX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi W,h(Z,PX))-g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}bQX)-g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}cQX)

\displaystyle+g(\phi Z,h(W,PX))+g(Z,\bar{\nabla}_{W}bQX)+g(Z,\bar{\nabla}_{W}cQX).

Using (
2.20
) in the above relation, we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle g([Z,W],X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(A_{\phi W}PX,Z)+g(A_{\phi Z}PX,W)+\sin^{2}\theta g([Z,W],X)

\displaystyle+g(A_{QPX}Z,W)-g(A_{QPX}W,Z).

Using (
4.6
) in (
4.7
), we get

(4.8)

\cos^{2}\theta g([Z,W],X)=0.

Since
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}
is proper pseudo-slant so,
\theta\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
. Therefore,
g([Z,W],X)=0
and hence the anti-invariant distribution

\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
is integrable.

Now, let
h^{\bot}
be the second fundamental form of a leaf
N_{\bot}
of
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
in
M
. Then for any
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle g(h^{\bot}(Z,W),X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,\phi X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,PX)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,QX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(A_{\phi W}Z,PX)-g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi QX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(A_{\phi W}PX,Z)-g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}bQX)-g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}cQX)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(A_{\phi W}PX,Z)+\sin^{2}\theta g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,X)-g(A_{QPX}W,Z).

Therefore

(4.9)

\cos^{2}\theta g(h^{\bot}(Z,W),X)=-g(A_{\phi W}PX,Z)-g(A_{QPX}W,Z).

Using (
4.6
) in (
4.9
), we get

(4.10)

\cos^{2}\theta g(h^{\bot}(Z,W),X)=-\cos^{2}\theta[(X\mu)+\alpha\eta(X)]g(Z,W).

Thus, we get

h^{\bot}(Z,W)=-[\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu+\alpha\xi]g(Z,W),

where
\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu
is gradient of the function
\mu
.

Therefore
N_{\bot}
is totally umbilical in
M
with
the mean curvature
H^{\bot}=-(\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu+\alpha\xi)
.

Now, let
\mathcal{D}^{N}
be the normal connection of
N_{\bot}
in
M
. Then
for any
Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we have

g(\mathcal{D}_{Z}^{N}\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu+\alpha\xi,Y)=g(\nabla_{Z}\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu,Y)+\alpha g(\nabla_{Z}\xi,Y).

Also, from (
2.6
) and (
2.13
) we get
\nabla_{Z}\xi=0
.

Therefore,
g(\mathcal{D}_{Z}^{N}\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu+\alpha\xi,Y)=g(\nabla_{Z}\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{\bot}\mu,Y)=0
, since
(Z\mu)=0
for
every
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and hence the mean curvature of
N_{\bot}
is parallel.

Thus the leaves of the distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}

are totally umbilical in
M
with non-vanishing parallel mean curvature vector
H^{\bot}
, i.e.
N_{\bot}
is an extrinsic sphere in
M
. Therefore by
Theorem 2.1,
M
is a warped product submanifold.
∎

5.
warped product bi-slant submanifolds of
(LCS)_{n}
-manifolds

Definition 5.1
.

A submanifold
M
of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be a bi-slant submanifold if there exists a pair of orthogonal distributions

\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
of
M
such that

(\textit{i})TM=\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{2}

(\textit{ii})\phi\mathcal{D}_{1}\bot\mathcal{D}_{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \phi\mathcal{D}_{2}\bot\mathcal{D}_{1}

(\textit{iii})\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}
are slant submanifolds with slant angles
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, respectively.

If we assume
\theta_{1}=0
and
\theta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
M
is a CR-submanifold and if
\theta_{1}=0
and
\theta_{2}\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
, then

M
is a semi-slant submanifold. Also, if
\theta_{1}=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\theta_{2}\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
M
is a pseudo-slant submanifold.
A bi-slant submanifold
M
of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be proper if the slant distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and

\mathcal{D}_{2}
are of slant angles
\theta_{1},\ \theta_{2}\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
.

For a proper bi-slant submanifold
M
of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold, the normal bundle of
M
is decomposed as

T^{\bot}M=Q\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus Q\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\nu,

where
\nu
is the invariant normal subbundle of
M
.

Now we will construct a bi-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the semi-Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
with the cartesian coordinates
(x_{1},y_{1}\cdots,x_{3},y_{3},\,t)
and paracontact structure

\phi\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}},\,\,\,\,\phi\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\,\,\phi\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)=0,\,\,\,\,1\leq i,j\leq 3.

It is clear that
{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
is a Lorentzian metric manifold manifold with usual semi-Euclidean metric tensor.
For any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
let
M
be a submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{7}
defined by

\chi(u,v,w,s,\,t)=(w+u\cos\theta_{1},\,u\sin\theta_{1},\,s+v\cos\theta_{2},\,v\sin\theta_{2},\,0,\,0,\,t).

Then the tangent space of
M
is spanned by the following vectors

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}},

\displaystyle Z_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},

\displaystyle Z_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},

\displaystyle Z_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\phi Z_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sin\theta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}},

\displaystyle\phi Z_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\sin\theta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},

\displaystyle\phi Z_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi Z_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi Z_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

We take
{\mathcal{D}_{1}}={\rm{Span}}\{Z_{1},\,Z_{4}\}
and
{\mathcal{D}_{2}}={\rm{Span}}\{Z_{2},\ Z_{3}\}
, then
g(Z_{1},\phi Z_{4})=\cos\theta_{1}

and
g(Z_{2},\phi Z_{3})=\cos\theta_{2}
. Thus the distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are slant with slant angles
\theta_{1}
and

\theta_{2}
respectively and hence
M
is a bi-slant submanifold.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M
be a proper bi-slant submanifold of a
(LCS)_{n}
-manifold
\bar{M}
with the slant distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}

such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1})
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{2}g(\nabla_{X_{1}}X_{2},Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X_{1}}PX_{2},PY_{2})+g(h(X_{1},PX_{2}),QY_{2})

\displaystyle+g(h(X_{1},Y_{2}),QPX_{2}),

for any
X_{1}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1})
and
X_{2},\ Y_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{2})
, where
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are the slant angles
of slant distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
respectively.

Proof.

For any
X_{1}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1})
and
X_{2},\ Y_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X_{1}}X_{2},Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}X_{2},\phi Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}\phi X_{2},\phi Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}PX_{2},PY_{2})+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}PX_{2},QY_{2})+g(\bar{\nabla}-{X_{1}}QX_{2},\phi Y_{2}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X_{1}}PX_{2},PY_{2})+g(h(X_{1},PX_{2}),QY_{2})

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}bQX_{2},Y_{2})+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}cQX_{2},Y_{2}).

Using (
2.20
) in the above relation we get (
5.1
).
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

There does not exists a proper warped product bi-slant submanifold
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
of
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(TM_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
M=M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
be a proper warped product bi-slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
. Then for
X_{1}\in\Gamma(TM_{1})
and

X_{2},\ Y_{2}\in\Gamma(TM_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(h(X_{1},PX_{2}),QY_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}PX_{2},\phi Y_{2})+g(PX_{2}\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}PY_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{2}g(\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}X_{2},Y_{2})-g(h(X_{1},Y_{2}),QPX_{2})+g(PX_{2},\bar{\nabla}_{X_{1}}PY_{2}).

Using Proposition 2.1 in the above relation, we get

(5.2)

g(h(X_{1},PX_{2}),QY_{2})+g(h(X_{1},Y_{2}),QPX_{2})=2\cos^{2}\theta_{2}(X_{1}\ln f)g(X_{2},Y_{2}).

Again using Proposition 2.1 in (
5.1
), we get

(5.3)

g(h(X_{1},PX_{2}),QY_{2})+g(h(X_{1},Y_{2}),QPX_{2})=0.

From (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we get

(5.4)

\cos^{2}\theta_{2}(X_{1}\ln f)=0.

Since
M
is a proper warped product bi-slant submanifold so,
\theta_{2}\neq\frac{\pi}{2}
. Therefore
(X_{1}\ln f)=0
for every

X_{1}\in\Gamma(TM_{1})
and hence
M
does not exists.
∎

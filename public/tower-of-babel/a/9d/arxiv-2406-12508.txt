An Alternative to Homotopy Transfer for A ∞ A_{\infty} -Algebras
C. A. Cremonini, V. E. Marotta

Abstract
. In this work, we propose a novel approach to the homotopy transfer procedure starting from a set of homotopy data such that the first differential complex is a differential graded module over the second one. We show that the module structure may be used to induce an A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra on the second differential complex, constructed in a similar fashion to the homotopy transfer A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra. We prove that, under certain conditions, the A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras obtained with this procedure are quasi-isomorphic to the homotopy transfer one. On the other hand, when the side conditions do not hold, we find that there are cases where the existence of an A ∞ A_{\infty} -quasi-isomorphism with the homotopy transfer A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra is obstructed. In other words, we obtain a new A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra on the second complex, inequivalent to the homotopy transfer one. Lastly, we prove that these A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras are not infinitesimal Hochschild deformations of the homotopy transfer A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra.



1.
Introduction

\infty
-algebras were introduced by Stasheff
[
Sta63
]
, under the name
strongly homotopy algebras
, to describe “group-like” topological space. Soon after their first appearance, they found a vast realm of applications, both in mathematics and physics. For instance, they represent a cornerstone in the study of formality of Kähler manifolds
[
Del+75
,
Mer99
]
and deformation quantisation of Poisson manifolds
[
Kon03
]
, whereas in physics they appear to be the underlying structure describing quantum field theory
[
Jur+19
,
Jur+20
,
Ćir+20
]
and string field theory, see
[
Wit86
,
Zwi98
,
EKS14
,
CG20
,
Dou+20
]
and references therein.

1.1.
Motivation

In this paper, we aim at constructing an
A_{\infty}
-algebra by considering two quasi-isomorphic differential complexes such that (a subring of) the first one admits a bimodule structure on the second one. In this way, we may induce an
A_{\infty}
-algebra on the second complex by a technique that is reminiscent of the one used for the Homotopy Transfer Theorem. The main difference resides in the type of algebraic data that is used to induce an
A_{\infty}
-algebra on the second complex. In order to motivate why this specific setting became relevant to us, let us discuss the setting of Open Superstring Field Theory as in
[
EKS14
]
. In that context, the authors consider three complexes, the BRST complexes of string fields with picture numbers
0,\,-1
and
-2
, respectively. For the sake of simplicity, consider the picture numbers as labels for the three complexes. These three complexes are quasi-isomorphic, where the quasi-isomorphisms are called
Picture Changing Operators
, and are bimodules over the picture-number 0 complex, which is also the only one naturally equipped with an algebraic structure. The authors used these structures to define on the picture
-1
complex a 2-product (interpreted as a vertex of interaction between three string fields) which turned out to be non-associative, thus spoiling the gauge-invariance of the action. The gauge-invariance is recovered by adding higher products (i.e., higher vertices of interactions) that encoded the structure of an
A_{\infty}
-algebra. Notably, the construction of this
A_{\infty}
-algebra does not rely on the transfer of algebraic data between complexes. We analyse here a similar setting and study under which conditions one can leverage on module structures to induce algebraic structures from one complex to another. In particular, this construction will heavily rely on a novel type of interplay between algebraic and homotopy data.

Supermanifolds and the quasi-isomorphic complexes of differential and integral forms
[
Man97
,
Noj23
,
Cre23
]
give another relevant case at the heart of our motivations. Integral forms are defined as a complex of modules over differential forms but are not equipped with a ring structure. Thus the quest for the induction of algebraic structures on this complex. By using the quasi-isomorphisms and the dga-algebra structure on differential forms one can rely on the usual Homotopy Transfer Theorem to transfer an
A_{\infty}
-algebra on integral forms. Nonetheless, the very rich structure we just described suggests alternative constructions of
A_{\infty}
-algebras coming from the module structure. We partly explore this topic here and leave the full discussion to the upcoming work
[
CM
]
, where we shall use this technology to introduce intersection theory on supermanifolds.

We shall briefly discuss here the basic setting of the
A_{\infty}
-category, for more details see
[
Kel01
,
LV14
]
.

1.2.
A_{\infty}
-Algebras

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space over
\Bbbk
,

(1)

A\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i},

where the
A_{i}
are
\Bbbk
-vector spaces for every

Definition 1

(
A_{\infty}
-Algebra
)
.

An
{A}_{\infty}
-algebra is a pair
(A,\{m_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}})
, where
A
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\Bbbk
-vector space and
\{m_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is a collection of homogeneous
\Bbbk
-linear maps of degree
2-n
,

(2)

m_{n}\colon A^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow A,

such that they satisfy the following condition for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
:

(3)

\sum_{n=r+s+t}(-1)^{r+st}m_{r+t+1}(\mathds{1}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\mathds{1}^{\otimes t})=0.

The maps
m_{n}
are called
n
-products. We will denote it by
(A,m_{n}).

This definition encodes the fact that all of the
n
-products satisfy higher associativity relations
up to homotopy
. For more details, see
[
Sta63
,
Kel01
,
Val14
,
LV14
]
and references therein.

Note that the maps
\{m_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
in the definition of the
A_{\infty}
-algebra belong to the
\mathsf{Hom}
-space of
\Bbbk
-multilinear maps
\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
for any
n\geq 0
. The space
\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
is a chain complex
\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathsf{Hom}_{k}(A^{\otimes n},A),
where
\mathsf{Hom}_{k}(A^{\otimes n},A)
is the space of multilinear maps
f\colon A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow A
of degree
k
, with the differential

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{A}\colon\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{A}\varphi\coloneqq[\mathrm{d},\varphi]=\mathrm{d}\circ\varphi-(-1)^{|\varphi|}\varphi\circ\mathrm{d}_{A^{\otimes n}}.

for any
\varphi\in\mathsf{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A),
where the differential
\mathrm{d}_{A}
is extended to a map
\mathrm{d}_{A^{\otimes n}}\colon A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow A^{\otimes n}
by

\mathrm{d}_{A^{\otimes n}}\coloneqq\sum_{n-1=s+t}\mathds{1}^{\otimes s}\otimes\mathrm{d}\otimes\mathds{1}^{\otimes t}
.

Lastly, let us recall the notion of morphism between
A_{\infty}
-algebras. An
A_{\infty}
-morphism between
the
A_{\infty}
-algebras
(A,m_{n})
and
(A^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{n})
is a family of maps
f_{n}\colon A^{\otimes n}\to A^{\prime},
for all
n\geq 1
of degree
1-n
satisfying the relations

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s+t=n}(-1)^{r+st}f_{r+t+1}\left(\mathds{1}_{A}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\mathds{1}_{A}^{\otimes t}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{u}m^{\prime}_{l}(f_{i_{1}}\otimes f_{i_{2}}\otimes...\otimes f_{i_{l}})\,,

where
i_{1}+i_{2}+...+i_{n}=n
and
u=(l-1)(i_{1}-1)+(l-2)(i_{2}-1)+\ldots+(i_{l-1}-1)
.

1.3.
Homotopy Transfer of Algebraic Data

The homotopy transfer of algebraic data represents the main source of inspiration for this work. We briefly discuss it here and refer to
[
Kad80
,
Val14
,
LV14
]
for more details on this construction.

Consider the homotopy data

(7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip-5.69046pt\left(A^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}_{A}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(B^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}_{B}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}

with
h_{A}
degree
-1
map satisfying

(8)

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{A}-Z\circ Y=\mathrm{d}_{A}\circ h_{A}+h_{A}\circ\mathrm{d}_{A}\,,

together with the algebraic data given by an associative product
\wedge
on
A^{\bullet}
making
A^{\bullet}
a differential graded associative algebra. By using the quasi-isomorphism
Z,Y
, the product on
A^{\bullet}
can be transported to
B^{\bullet}
by defining

(9)

\displaystyle m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\coloneqq Y\circ\wedge\circ Z^{\otimes 2}\colon B^{\otimes 2}\to B\,.

It can be shown that, in general,
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
is not associative, in other words, this transport procedure does not preserve the associativity of the product. Nonetheless, it can easily be seen that the associativity of
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
is violated in a controlled way, i.e.

(10)

\displaystyle m_{2}^{\text{ht}}(m_{2}^{\text{ht}}(b\otimes b^{\prime})\otimes b^{\prime\prime})-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}(b\otimes m_{2}^{\text{ht}}(b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime}))=\partial_{B}m_{3}^{\text{ht}}(b\otimes b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime})\,,

for all
b,b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\in B,
where

(11)

\displaystyle m_{3}^{\text{ht}}\coloneqq-Y\circ\wedge\circ\bigl((h_{A}\circ\wedge\circ Z^{\otimes 2})\otimes Z\bigr)+Y\circ\wedge\circ\bigl(Z\otimes(h_{A}\circ\wedge\circ Z^{\otimes 2})\bigr)

and
\partial_{B}
is defined as in Equation (
5
).
This construction can be iterated and it is possible to show that
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
and
m_{3}^{\text{ht}}
are associative up to an exact term with respect to
\partial_{B}.
This leads to stating the following remarkable result, proved in
[
Kad80
]
for
B=\mathsf{H}_{\bullet}(A)
.

Theorem 1

(
Homotopy Transfer Theorem
)
.

Consider the homotopy data introduced above and let
B^{\bullet}
be endowed with the (higher) products
m_{n}^{\text{ht}},
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},
obtained as described above. Then
(B^{\bullet},m_{n}^{\text{ht}})
forms an
A_{\infty}
-algebra.

This construction is one of our main guides in order to build a procedure inducing an
A_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the differential complex
(B^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}_{B})
starting from a different type of algebraic data on
A^{\bullet}
.

1.4.
Outline and Results

The outline of the paper is as follows: in Section
2
we discuss the basic assumptions that will be the main framework of this paper.
In Section
3
we show the existence of new
A_{\infty}
-algebras coming from an analogue of the homotopy transfer procedure performed under new assumptions. In Section
4
we discuss how this novel procedure relates to homotopy transfer.

Section

2
begins with the definition of the main object of this work, the homotopy data given by two quasi-isomorphic cochain complexes
(A,\mathrm{d}_{A})
and
(B,\mathrm{d}_{B})
with quasi-isomorphisms
Y,Z
such that
A
is a differential graded associative algebra,
B
is a differential graded
\mathrm{Im}(Z)
-bimodule and the quasi-isomorphisms
Y,Z
are
\mathrm{Im}(Z)
-bimodule homomorphisms, where
\mathrm{Im}(Z)
is assumed to be a subring of
A
. We call this
Homotopy Data with Module Structure
, see Definition
2
. As an example, we show that the de Rham complex on a (split) supermanifold and its quasi-isomorphic complex of integral forms can be endowed with homotopy operators such that they represent an example of Homotopy Data with Module Structure. We thus proceed to show that the above homotopy data satisfying at least one of the so-called side conditions only admit an associative algebra on
B
coming from the homotopy transfer of the algebra structure on
A,
i.e. we have the following

Proposition

2
. Let
(A,\mathrm{d}_{A})
and
(B,\mathrm{d}_{B})
be homotopy data with module structure as in Definition
2
, endowed with the homotopy transfer
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(B,m_{n}^{\text{ht}})
. Moreover, suppose that

satisfies the left-side condition. Then the homotopy transfer

-algebra
(B,m_{n}^{\text{ht}})
reduces to an associative algebra, i.e.,
m_{n}^{\text{ht}}=0
for

This represents one of our motivations for the investigation of an alternative homotopy transfer procedure, so that we may obtain an
A_{\infty}
-algebra on
B
admitting higher terms even when the aforementioned side condition holds.

Inspired by
[
Mar01
]
, we explore under which circumstances we can preserve the right side condition under a change of homotopy data removing the obstruction to the existence of a strong homotopy equivalence. Moreover, we show that if the quasi-isomorphisms satisfy
Z\circ Y\circ Z=Y,
there always exists a redefinition of the homotopy operator
h_{B}
implementing the right side condition with the same quasi-isomorphisms. We stress that if the right side condition
Z\circ h_{B}=0
holds, then we have that the quasi-isomorphisms satisfy
Z\circ Y\circ Z=Z.
Furthermore, if the right side condition holds, then the obstruction to the existence of a strong homotopy equivalence can always be removed.

We also discuss a weaker notion of the side conditions given by

and
h_{B}\circ Y\circ Z=0,
called

. We show that if the quasi-isomorphisms satisfy the condition

then the homotopy operator can always be redefined in such a way that the weak side conditions hold. The weak side conditions will appear later on in the construction of an

m_{1}^{\text{ht}}=\mathrm{d}_{B}
and the bracket is the extension to
\mathsf{Hom}(\mathsf{T}(B),B)
of the commutator on
\mathsf{Hom}(B,B).

The nilpotency of
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
follows from the
A_{\infty}
-relations (
3
). Given an element
\mu\in\mathsf{Hom}\left(\mathsf{T}\left(B\right),B\right)
, we say that it is an
infinitesimal deformation
of the
A_{\infty}
-algebra if
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mu=0
. If
\mu=\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\nu
, for some
\nu\in\mathsf{Hom}\left(\mathsf{T}\left(B\right),B\right)
, we say that the deformation is
trivial
. It is then clear that non-trivial (infinitesimal) deformations are given by the cohomology groups determined by
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
; this cohomology is known as
Hochschild cohomology
and it is denoted as
\mathsf{H}\mathsf{H}\left(B\right)
.

In the previous sections, we have shown how to induce on the complex
B
algebraic structures with a procedure leveraging on the
\mathrm{Im}(Z)
-bimodule structure; in this section, we address whether the Homotopy Module-Induced
A_{\infty}
-structures are related to the
A_{\infty}
-algebra obtained from the homotopy transfer procedure. In particular, we will discuss if the structures obtained in Section
3
are a Hochschild deformation (possibly trivial) of the usual one. It will turn out that this is not the case.

Proposition 18
.

Assume that Theorem (
11
) holds and let
(B,m_{n}^{\text{ht}})
and
(B,m_{n})
be the
A_{\infty}
-algebras induced by the homotopy transfer and the Homotopy Module-Induction, respectively. Then
(B,m_{n})
is not an infinitesimal deformation of
(B,m_{n}^{\text{ht}}).

Proof.

Consider the map

(192)

\displaystyle\mu\coloneqq\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\left(m_{i}-m_{i}^{\text{ht}}\right)\in\mathsf{Hom}\left(\mathsf{T}\left(B\right),B\right)\ ;

we need to verify whether
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mu=0
holds. We will proceed by checking the equality in each degree. The first relation to check reads

(193)

\displaystyle\partial_{B}\left(m_{2}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\right)=0\ ,

which is true since both
m_{2}
and
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
are Leibniz-compatible with
\mathrm{d}_{B}
. The second relation reads

(194)

\displaystyle\partial_{B}(m_{3}-m_{3}^{\text{ht}})-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ(m_{2}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})-(m_{2}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}=0\,,

which can be written as

(195)

\displaystyle\partial_{B}m_{3}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ m_{2}-m_{2}\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}=0\,,

where we used
\partial_{B}m_{3}^{\text{ht}}=0
and
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}=0,
see Proposition
6
.
By using the properties of the map
Z
, we have

(196)

\displaystyle m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ m_{2}\left(b\otimes b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime}\right)=m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\left(m_{2}\left(b\otimes b^{\prime}\right)\otimes b^{\prime\prime}\right)-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\left(b\otimes m_{2}\left(b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)=0\ .

On the other hand, we can explicitly verify that

(197)

\displaystyle m_{2}\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\left(b\otimes b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle k_{1}\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\right)\rhd b^{\prime\prime}-k_{2}b\lhd\left(Z(b^{\prime})\wedge Z(b^{\prime\prime})\right)

(198)

\displaystyle+(k_{2}-k_{1})Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\wedge Z(b^{\prime\prime})\right)\ .

Considering coefficients
k_{1},\,k_{2}
such that
k_{1}\neq-1
and
k_{2}\neq-1
, Equation (
197
) is different from
\partial_{B}m_{3}
, see Equation (
88
), thus showing that
\mu
does not correspond to a deformation.

In the case
k_{1}=k_{2}=-1,
we immediately see from Equations (
197
) and (
88
) that Equation (
194
) is verified.
Thus, the next relation to check reads

(199)

\displaystyle\partial_{B}(m_{4}-m_{4}^{\text{ht}})

\displaystyle-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ(m_{3}-m_{3}^{\text{ht}})+(m_{3}-m_{3}^{\text{ht}})\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}

(200)

\displaystyle+m_{3}^{\text{ht}}\circ(m_{2}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})-(m_{2}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})\circ m_{3}^{\text{ht}}=0\ .

Note that we can use the
A_{\infty}
-relation for the homotopy transfer with
n=4
together with Proposition
6
in order to cancel the terms having
m_{n}^{\text{ht}}
only. Moreover,
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ m_{3}=0
because of the properties
3
of
Z
, together with
Z\circ h_{B}=0,
and
m_{3}\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}=0
, because of
h_{B}\circ Y=0
. Thus Equation (
199
) can be written as

(201)

\displaystyle\partial_{B}m_{4}+m_{3}^{\text{ht}}\circ m_{2}-m_{2}\circ m_{3}^{\text{ht}}=0\,,

In order for Equation (
201
) to hold, one should verify that the terms containing
h_{B}
(i.e.,
\partial_{B}m_{4}
) and those containing
h_{A}
(the last two) vanish separately. This would imply that
\partial_{B}m_{4}=0
, which is not satisfied. We conclude that

(202)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mu\neq 0\ ,

so that there is no deformation connecting the homotopy transfer
A_{\infty}
-algebra and the Homotopy Module-Induced one.
∎

Example 2

(
Associative algebras
)
.

The failure of the infinitesimal deformation prescription happens even at the level of associative algebras. In particular, let
\left(B,m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\right)
and
\left(B,\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}\right)
be associative algebras, where the product of the latter algebra is defined by fixing the coefficients of Equation (
78
) to
k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0.
We assume the same conditions for the maps
Z
and
Y
as in Definition (
2
). In order to check whether the product
\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}
can be obtained via a deformation of
m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
, we need to check if

(203)

\displaystyle\mu\coloneqq\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}

is closed under the action of the Hochschild differential given by
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}=\left[m_{2}^{\text{ht}},\,\cdot\,\right],
as discussed in the proof of Proposition
18
. Note that if the closure condition of
\mu
with respect to
\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}
held, then there would exist a one-parameter family of maps
m(t)\in\mathsf{Hom}\left(B^{\otimes 2},B\right)[t]
at most linear in
t
such that
m(0)=m_{2}^{\text{ht}}
and
m(1)=\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}
. Explicitly, we have

(204)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mu

\displaystyle=-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ(\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})-(\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}-m_{2}^{\text{ht}})\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}

(205)

\displaystyle=-m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\circ\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}-\left.m_{2}\right|_{k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0}\circ m_{2}^{\text{ht}}\ ,

which gives

(206)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\mathsf{H}}\mu\left(b\otimes b^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle-Y\left(Z\left(Z(b)\rhd b^{\prime}\right)\wedge Z(b^{\prime\prime})\right)+Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z\left(Z\left(b^{\prime}\right)\rhd b^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)

(207)

\displaystyle-Z\circ Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\right)\rhd b^{\prime\prime}+Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\wedge Z(b^{\prime\prime})\right)

(208)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Z\circ Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\right)\rhd b^{\prime\prime}+Y\left(Z(b)\wedge Z(b^{\prime})\wedge Z(b^{\prime\prime})\right)\neq 0\ .

This shows that
\mu
is not a Hochschild cocycle, thus there is no infinitesimal deformation connecting the two algebraic structures.

Markovian lifting and asymptotic log-Harnack inequality for stochastic Volterra integral equations
Yushi Hamaguchi

Abstract
We introduce a new framework of Markovian lifts of stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs for short) with completely monotone kernels. We define the state space of the Markovian lift as a separable Hilbert space which incorporates the singularity or regularity of the kernel into the definition. We show that the solution of an SVIE is represented by the solution of a lifted stochastic evolution equation (SEE for short) defined on the Hilbert space and prove the existence, uniqueness and Markov property of the solution of the lifted SEE. Furthermore, we establish an asymptotic log-Harnack inequality and some consequent properties for the Markov semigroup associated with the Markovian lift via the asymptotic coupling method.



Keywords:
Stochastic Volterra integral equation; Markovian lift; asymptotic log-Harnack inequality.

Introduction

We consider the following stochastic Volterra integral equation (SVIE for short):

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t>0,

(1.1)

where
W
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion,
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are deterministic maps,
x
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued deterministic function called the forcing term, and
K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a deterministic function called the kernel. On the one hand, if
K
and
x
are constants or of the exponential forms
K(t)=e^{-\beta t}
and
x(t)=e^{-\beta t}y
for some
\beta>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the SVIE (
1.1
) reduces to a standard stochastic differential equation (SDE for short). On the other hand, the case of the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
corresponds to a kind of time-fractional SDE (cf.
[
33
]
), where
\Gamma(\alpha)
denotes the Gamma function. SVIEs were first studied by Berger and Mizel
[
11
,
12
]
, and then applied in many research areas including population dynamics, tumor growth, and volatility models in mathematical finance. SVIEs provide suitable models for dynamics with hereditary properties, memory effects and the power-law property of the volatility (cf.
[
8
,
22
,
34
,
19
]
).

It is well-known that the solutions of SVIEs are neither Markovian nor semimartingales in general, and thus we cannot apply Itô’s stochastic calculus to SVIEs directly. However, if we “lift” the state space of SVIEs to an infinite dimensional space, we can recover a kind of Markov property. To see this phenomenon, let us consider the following Volterra process:

B_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t>0,

with a
1
-dimensional Brownian motion
W
and the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
of order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. The Volterra process
B
is a Riemann–Liouville type fractional Brownian motion of the Hurst parameter
H=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Carmona and Coutin
[
13
]
proposed a Markovian representation approach for
B
of the following form:

B_{t}=\int_{[0,\infty)}Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ t>0,

(1.2)

where
\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\theta^{-\alpha}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(0,\infty)}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta
, and each
Y(\theta)
is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with the mean reverting parameter
\theta
, which solves the SDE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=0.\end{cases}

The above representation means that the fractional Brownian motion
B
can be represented as a superposition of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes with various mean reverting parameters. Although the fractional Brownian motion
B
is neither Markovian nor a semimartingale, its lift
(Y(\theta))_{\theta\in(0,\infty)}
can be seen as a Markov process in a function space. This approach was adopted and applied to the studies of tractable financial models with fractional features
[
1
,
3
,
24
]
, optimal control problems for SVIEs
[
4
]
, and efficient numerical approximations of SVIEs
[
5
,
9
]
, among others.

In this paper, motivated by
[
13
]
, we formulate a new framework of Markovian lifting of SVIEs and investigate some asymptotic behaviours of the associated Markov semigroup. Here, let us give a formal observation. We assume that the kernel
K
is completely monotone, that is,
K
is infinitely differentiable and satisfies
(-1)^{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}t^{k}}K(t)\geq 0
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
and any nonnegative integers
k
. By Bernstein’s theorem, there exists a unique Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
such that

K(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ t>0,

where
{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\subset[0,\infty)
denotes the support of
\mu
. Furthermore, assume that the forcing term
x
is of the form

x(t)=(\mathcal{K}y)(t):=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ t>0,

for some Borel measurable function
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Under suitable integrability conditions, by the (stochastic) Fubini theorem, the SVIE (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{K}y)(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big\{e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big\}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta).

Thus,
X
can be represented by

X_{t}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ t>0,

(1.3)

with
Y:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

Y_{t}(\theta)=e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t\geq 0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},

or equivalently, in the differential form

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ t>0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.\end{cases}

The above formal observation means that the solution of the SVIE (
1.1
) can be represented as a superposition of the collection of the parametrized processes
Y(\theta)
,
\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
. Then, we can guess that the function-valued lifted process
Y=(Y(\theta))_{\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}}
solves a kind of infinite dimensional stochastic evolution equation (SEE for short) and enjoys the Markov property as an infinite dimensional process. From this formal observation, the following natural questions arise:

Q 1.

What is the reasonable state space in which the lifted process
Y
becomes a Markov process?

Q 2.

If we can get a reasonable state space of the Markov process, what can we say more about the associated Markov semigroup?

In this paper, we address the above questions. The following are our main contributions in this paper.

(1)

We propose and formulate a state space of lifted processes as a separable Hilbert space, and investigate its fundamental properties.

2.1

(2)

We rigorously prove the equivalence (in some sense) between the SVIE (
1.1

2.2

See

(3)

Under suitable assumptions, we show that the lifted SEE has a unique solution which satisfies the Markov and Feller properties.
See

.

(4)

Under suitable assumptions, we derive an asymptotic log-Harnack inequality for the associated Markov semigroup.
See

Let us make some comments about the above questions and contributions.

Concerning the question Q 1, a first possible candidate of the state space of lifts would be
L^{1}(\mu)
. Indeed, Carmona and Coutin
[
13
]
, Harms and Stefanovits
[
24
]
and Abi Jaber, Miller and Pham
[
4
]
adopted the space
L^{1}(\mu)
as the state space of lifts. This choice is natural in view of the integral representations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). However, it has a drawback that each non-zero constant vector does not belong to
L^{1}(\mu)
in general. Indeed,
\mu
becomes an infinite measure when the kernel
K
is singular (in the sense that
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)=\infty
). As a consequence, the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued drift coefficient
b
or the
\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
-valued diffusion coefficient
\sigma
cannot be realized as
L^{1}(\mu)
-valued maps. Moreover,
L^{1}(\mu)
is not a Hilbert space, which makes the infinite dimensional analysis of the lifted equation difficult. A second candidate would be
L^{2}(\mu)
which was treated in
[
24
]
. In this case, although it is a Hilbert space, the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
cannot be realized as
L^{2}(\mu)
-valued maps by the same reasons as above. Furthermore, when the kernel
K
is singular, the measure becomes an infinite measure, and thus
L^{2}(\mu)\setminus L^{1}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
in general. Hence, the integral representations (
1.2
) or (
1.3
) might not make sense, and this would be one reason why
[
4
,
13
]
avoided to choose
L^{2}(\mu)
as the state space; see
[
4
, Remark 2.4]
. Cuchiero and Teichmann
[
14
,
15
]
considered Markovian lifts of SVIEs in view of measure-valued stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs for short), and formulated a framework of associated Markov processes called generalized Feller processes. In our notations, the measure-valued process
\lambda_{t}(\mathrm{d}\theta):=Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
corresponds to the Markovian lift considered therein. In their framework, the state space of lifts is taken as the space of finite signed (or vector-valued) regular Borel measures on the extended real half-line
[0,\infty]
endowed with its weak-
*
topology. They applied an abstract framework of measure-valued SPDEs to affine Volterra processes and obtained the affine transformation formula in the infinite dimensional point of view. However, since the state space is not a Polish space
1
1

1

The continuous dual space of an infinite dimensional Banach space is not metrizable with respect to the weak-
*
topology.
, one cannot apply general theory of Markov processes on Polish spaces to their framework directly.

In contrast to the aforementioned works, we propose a
Hilbert-valued Markovian lifting approach
for SVIEs. Our framework covers SVIEs with (completely monotone) singular kernels such as the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
and the Gamma kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta t}t^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\beta>0
. Our idea is to incorporate the singularity/regularity of the kernel into the definition of the state space explicitly as a weight of an
L^{2}
-space. We demonstrate that the state space has good structures, and the lifted SEE fits into the well-established framework of SPDEs in Hilbert spaces. In this new framework, we show that the solution of an SVIE is represented by the solution of a lifted SEE. Then, we show the existence, uniqueness and the Markov and Feller properties of the solutions of lifted SEEs. Our general framework can be a useful tool to study SVIEs by means of Itô’s stochastic calculus in Hilbert spaces, and it has potential applications to mathematical finance, optimal control theory, ergodic theory and numerical analysis for SVIEs.

In our new framework, we address the question Q 2 mentioned above. Specifically, we are concerned with the long-time asymptotic behaviour and the ergodicity of the Hilbert-valued Markovian lifts of SVIEs. There are only a few previous works in this direction. Friesen and Jin
[
18
]
studied Volterra square-root processes and showed the existence of the long-time limiting distributions under suitable integrability conditions of the kernel. They applied their results to the Volterra CIR process with a Gamma kernel of the form
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta t}t^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\beta>0
and characterized its stationary distribution.
Benth, Detering and Krühner
[
10
]
investigated long-time limiting distributions of SVIEs by means of abstract results of SPDEs. Although this work covers rather general classes of SVIEs, it requires that the kernel
K
is sufficiently regular which excludes singular kernels such as the fractional kernel or the Gamma kernel.

In the last half of this paper, by means of the Hilbert-valued Markovian lifting approach which we introduce in the first half, we study the long-time asymptotic behaviour and the ergodicity for the lifted SEE of the SVIE (
1.1
). We begin with detailed analysis of a Gaussian Volterra process as the simplest case of our general framework. This corresponds to the (one-dimensional) SVIE (
1.1
) with
b=0
and
\sigma=1
, and the associated lifted SEE becomes an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on the Hilbert space. In this case, we characterize the invariant probability measure for the associated lifted SEE and construct a stationary process for the original SVIE. The existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability measure are completely characterized by the integrability condition of the kernel
K
; see
3.1
. Concerning the general case with the coefficients having non-linear dependence on the current state, we focus on the so-called (asymptotic) log-Harnack inequality and show some important consequent properties including the uniqueness of the invariant probability measure for the lifted SEE.

The dimension-free Harnack inequality was initiated by Wang
[
36
]
for elliptic diffusion semigroups on Riemannian manifolds, and he also introduced the log-Harnack inequality in
[
37
]
. For a Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on the class
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)
of real-valued bounded Borel measurable functions on a Polish space
E
, the log-Harnack inequality is of type

P_{t}\log f(\bar{x})\leq\log P_{t}f(x)+\Phi(x,\bar{x})

for any
t>0
,
x,\bar{x}\in E
and any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)
such that
f\geq 1
, where
\Phi
is a nonnegative measurable function on
E\times E
such that
\lim_{\bar{x}\to x}\Phi(x,\bar{x})=\lim_{\bar{x}\to x}\Phi(\bar{x},x)=0
for any
x\in E
. This inequality implies gradient estimates (hence, the strong Feller property), heat kernel estimates, the uniqueness of invariant probability measures, and the irreducibility of the Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
; see the book
[
38
]
for more detailed theory of Harnack inequalities and applications to SDEs, SPDEs and stochastic functional (partial) differential equations. Unfortunately, it turns out that the strong Feller property is invalid for the lifted SEE associated with a Gaussian Volterra process, which is the simplest example of the SVIE (
1.1
), and hence the log-Harnack inequality does not hold in our framework; see
3.3
. A main difficulty of our framework is that the lifted SEE is
highly degenerate
; the state space is (typically) infinite dimensional, while the underlying Brownian motion is finite dimensional. Similar difficulties arise in SPDEs with degenerate noise such as degenerate stochastic 2D Navier–Stokes equations. In order to prove the ergodicity for such degenerate stochastic systems which do not satisfy the strong Feller property, Hairer and Mattingly
[
23
]
introduced the notion of the
asymptotic strong Feller property
which is weaker than the strong Feller property. Then, as an alternative to the log-Harnack inequality, Xu
[
40
]
and Bao, Wang and Yuan
[
7
]
investigated the
asymptotic log-Harnack inequality
(which was introduced in
[
40
]
by the name “modified log-Harnack inequality”, whereas the “asymptotic log-Harnack inequality” was named by
[
7
]
taking into account the long-time asymptotic behaviour of the inequality). For a Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)
, the asymptotic log-Harnack inequality is of type

P_{t}\log f(\bar{x})\leq\log P_{t}f(x)+\Phi(x,\bar{x})+\Psi_{t}(x,\bar{x})\|\nabla\log f\|_{\infty}

for any (sufficiently large)
t>0
,
x,\bar{x}\in E
and any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)
such that
f\geq 1
and
\|\nabla\log f\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Here,
\|\nabla\log f\|_{\infty}
represents the Lipschitz constant of
\log f
with respect to a compatible metric on
E
, and
\Phi,\Psi_{t}:E\times E\to[0,\infty)
are measurable maps such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Psi_{t}(x,\bar{x})=0
for any
x,\bar{x}\in E
. Under some additional conditions for
\Phi
and
\Psi
, the asymptotic log-Harnack inequality implies asymptotic gradient estimates (hence, the asymptotic strong Feller property), asymptotic heat kernel estimates, the uniqueness of invariant probability measures, and the asymptotic irreducibility of the Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
; see
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
. By means of the asymptotic coupling method, the asymptotic log-Harnack inequalities were established for the 2D stochastic Navier–Stokes equation driven by highly degenerate but essentially elliptic noise
[
40
]
, SDEs with infinite memory
[
7
,
39
]
, SPDEs with degenerate multiplicative noise
[
25
]
, monotone SPDEs with multiplicative noise
[
31
]
, the stochastic Cahn–Hilliard equation with the logarithmic free energy with highly degenerate but essentially elliptic noise
[
21
]
, the 3D stochastic Leray-
\alpha
model with degenerate noise
[
26
]
, and 2D hydrodynamical-type systems with degenerate noise
[
27
]
.

In this paper, following the asymptotic coupling method adopted in the aforementioned papers, we derive an asymptotic log-Harnack inequality for the Hilbert-valued Markovian lift of the SVIE (
1.1
). We note that, although the lifted SEE can be seen as a kind of monotone SPDEs, it is beyond the frameworks of previous works mentioned above; see
4.6
. Thus, the construction of an asymptotic coupling is a non-trivial task. By means of the special structure of the lifted SEE, we succeed to obtain a positive result.

We have to point out that, however, our results on the asymptotic log-Harnack inequality for the lifted SEE (
4.2
and
4.5
) are informative only for the case
\beta:=\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}>0
. Loosely speaking, the number
\beta
corresponds to the exponential decay rate of the kernel
K(t)
as
t\to\infty
. On the one hand, our results are valid for the case of the Gamma kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta t}t^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\beta>0
, which is a kind of singular (as
t\downarrow 0
) kernel with the exponential damping property (as
t\to\infty
). On the other hand, the condition
\beta:=\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}>0
excludes the case of the fractional kernel (which is the Gamma kernel with
\beta=0
). We need further considerations for the case
\beta=0
, which we leave to the future research. Also, in this paper, we do not study the existence of the invariant probability measure for the lifted SEE associated with a general SVIE, and we leave this important topic to the future research as well.

2
Hilbert-valued Markovian lifting of SVIEs

In this section, we introduce a suitable framework of Markovian lifts of SVIEs. We first analyse some basic properties of completely monotone kernels. Let
K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a completely monotone kernel. By Bernstein’s theorem, there exists a unique Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
such that
K(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for any
t>0
. We say that the kernel
K
is
regular
if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)<\infty
and that
K
is
singular
if it is not regular.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a completely monotone kernel, and let
\mu
be the corresponding Radon measure on
[0,\infty)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

K
is regular if and only if
\mu([1,\infty))<\infty
.

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then
\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
if and only if
\int_{[1,\infty)}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
. Furthermore, these conditions imply
\int^{1}_{0}K(t)^{1/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
.

Proof.

The assertion (i) is clear. We show (ii). Suppose
\int_{[1,\infty)}\theta^{-a}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}\Big(\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{[0,\infty)}\Big(\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\theta t}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\mu([0,1))+\Gamma(\alpha)\int_{[1,\infty)}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty,

and, by Minkowski’s integral inequality,

\displaystyle\Big(\int^{1}_{0}K(t)^{1/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int^{1}_{0}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big(\int^{1}_{0}e^{-\theta t/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\mu([0,1))+\int_{[1,\infty)}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty.

Conversely, suppose
\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\infty)}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{[1,\infty)}\Big(\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\theta t}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}\underbrace{\Big(\int_{[1,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)}_{\leq K(t)\wedge(e^{-t/2}K(t/2))}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{K(1/2)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{\infty}_{1}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t/2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

The above lemma shows that the regularity/singularity of the kernel
K
is characterized by the lightness/heaviness of the tail of the measure
\mu
. On the other hand, the value of
\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\geq 0
characterizes the asymptotic behaviour of
K(t)
as
t\to\infty
. Indeed, for each
\beta\geq 0
,

e^{\beta t}K(t)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-(\theta-\beta)t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\to\begin{cases}\infty\ &\text{if $\beta>\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}$,}\\
\mu(\{\beta\})\ &\text{if $\beta=\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}$,}\\
0\ &\text{if $\beta<\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}$,}\end{cases}

as
t\to\infty
. In particular, if
\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}>0
, then
K(t)
decays exponentially fast as
t\to\infty
.

With the above observations in mind, throughout this paper, we impose the following assumption on the kernel
K
appearing in the SVIE (
1.1
).

Assumption 1
.

K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is completely monotone. Furthermore, the corresponding Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
satisfies

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty

for some non-increasing Borel measurable function
r:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})\leq r(\theta)\leq 1
for any
\theta\in[0,\infty)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The function
r
determines the regularity/singularity of the kernel
K
. In particular, the above assumption implies
\int^{1}_{0}t^{-1/2}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
and
\int^{1}_{0}K(t)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
.

Here, we give some important examples of kernels
K
which satisfy
1
.

Example 2.3
.

(i)

Let
K_{\exp}
be a finite combination of exponential functions:

K_{\exp}(t):=\sum^{k}_{i=1}c_{i}e^{-\theta_{i}t},\ t>0,

with
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{i}>0
and
\theta_{i}\in[0,\infty)
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Then
K_{\exp}
is completely monotone, and the corresponding Radon measure is

\mu_{\exp}(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}c_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}}(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}_{\exp}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\},

where
\delta_{\theta_{i}}
denotes the Dirac measure at point
\theta_{i}
. Clearly,
K_{\exp}
is regular, and we can take
r\equiv 1
in
1
.

(ii)

Let
K_{\mathrm{frac}}
be the fractional kernel of order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
:

K_{\mathrm{frac}}(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1},\ t>0.

It is well-known that
K_{\mathrm{frac}}
is completely monotone, and the corresponding Radon measure is

\mu_{\mathrm{frac}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}_{\mathrm{frac}}=[0,\infty).

The fractional kernel
K_{\mathrm{frac}}
is singular, and we can take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-1/p})
in
1
for any
p\in[2,\frac{1}{1-\alpha})
.

(iii)

Let
K_{\mathrm{gamma}}
be the Gamma kernel of the form

K_{\mathrm{gamma}}(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta t}t^{\alpha-1},\ t>0,

for some
\beta>0
and
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
K_{\mathrm{gamma}}
is completely monotone, and the corresponding Radon measure is

\mu_{\mathrm{gamma}}(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}(\theta-\beta)^{-\alpha}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(\beta,\infty)}\mathrm{d}\theta,\ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}_{\mathrm{gamma}}=[\beta,\infty).

The Gamma kernel
K_{\mathrm{gamma}}
is singular, and we can take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-1/p})
in
1
for any
p\in[2,\frac{1}{1-\alpha})
.

(iv)

More generally, for any
\beta>0
and any kernel
K
satisfying
1
, the exponentially damped kernel

K_{\mathrm{damp}}(t):=e^{-\beta t}K(t),\ t>0,

satisfies
1
with the same function
r
. The corresponding Radon measure is

\mu_{\mathrm{damp}}(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[\beta,\infty)}(\theta)\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta-\beta),\ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}_{\mathrm{damp}}=\{\theta\in[\beta,\infty)\,|\,\theta-\beta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\}.

(v)

For any
\delta>0
and any completely monotone kernel
K
with the corresponding Radon measure
\mu
, the sifted kernel

K_{\mathrm{shift}}(t):=K(t+\delta),\ t>0,

satisfies
1
with the function
r\equiv 1
. In particular, the kernel
K_{\mathrm{shift}}
is regular. The corresponding Radon measure is

\mu_{\mathrm{shift}}(\mathrm{d}\theta)=e^{-\delta\theta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}_{\mathrm{shift}}={\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.

Let
1
hold. Consider the SVIE (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which we write here again:

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t>0.

(2.1)

Consider the case of
x(t)=(\mathcal{K}y)(t)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for some function
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As a lift of the above SVIE, we introduce the following SEE defined on some function space:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ t>0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\mu[\eta]\text{``$=$''}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\eta(\theta)\,\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for a suitable function
\eta:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
; see (
2.3
) for the precise definition of the map
\mu[\cdot]
. We introduce a suitable state space of the lift
Y
in which the lifted SEE (
2.2
) is well-posed, and rigorously prove the equivalence of the SVIE (
2.1
) and the lifted SEE (
2.2
). Furthermore, we show that the solution
Y
of the lifted SEE (
2.2
) is Markovian in the proposed state space.

2.1
State space of Markovian lifts

First, we introduce the state space of the lifted process
Y
. Under
1
, for each Borel measurable function
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}

and

\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
the space of equivalent classes of Borel measurable functions
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<\infty
. Similarly, we denote by
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
the space of equivalent classes of Borel measurable functions
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}<\infty
. Then both
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
and
(\mathcal{V}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}})
are separable reflexive Banach spaces. Furthermore, we equip
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the natural inner product

\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}:=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\langle y_{1}(\theta),y_{2}(\theta)\rangle\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu},

hence
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
is a separable Hilbert space. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
continuously and densely. Denote the continuous dual spaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
by
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
, respectively. By means of the Riesz isomorphism, we identify
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\mu}
. Then we see that
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
continuously and densely. The duality pairing
{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}
is then compatible with the inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
in the sense that
{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{y^{\prime}},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}=\langle y^{\prime},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
whenever
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
. From this compatibility, it can be easily shown that
(\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}})
is identified with the space of equivalent classes of Borel measurable functions
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}<\infty
, where

\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)^{-1}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}.

By the above constructions, we obtain a Gelfand triplet of Hilbert spaces:

\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}.

We adopt the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
(or the Gelfand triplet
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
) as the state space of Markovian lifts of SVIEs.

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

Although
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
become Hilbert spaces with suitable inner products, these Hilbert space-structures are unnecessary in this paper.

(ii)

As we mentioned in
Introduction
, regarding the state space of Markovian lifts of SVIEs, Carmona and Coutin
[
13
]
and Abi Jaber, Miller and Pham
[
4
]
adopted the space
L^{1}(\mu)
, while Cuchiero and Teichmann
[
14
,
15
]
adopted the space of signed (or vector-valued) Borel measures. In their approaches, the (stochastic) calculus of lifted equations are difficult due to the lack of Hilbert space-structure. In contrast, it will be turned out that our new framework fits into the well-established framework of SPDEs and Itô’s calculus in Hilbert spaces. We remark that, unlike
[
4
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, the definitions of the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
take into account the singularity/regularity of the kernel via the weight function
r
.

Here, we show some important properties of the function spaces defined above.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1
hold.

(i)

Each constant vector
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and satisfies
\|b\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\big)^{1/2}|b|
. Each constant matrix
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
belongs to the space
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
of Hilbert–Schmidt operators from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and satisfies
\|\sigma\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}_{\mu})}=\big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\big)^{1/2}|\sigma|
.

(ii)

The map
e^{-\cdot t}:y\mapsto(\theta\mapsto e^{-\theta t}y(\theta))
is a linear contraction operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
for any
t\geq 0
. Furthermore,
\{e^{-\cdot t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the infinitesimal generator
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
given by

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(A)=\left\{y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta^{2}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty\right\},\\
(Ay)(\theta)=-\theta y(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ y\in\mathcal{D}(A).\end{cases}

The map
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
can be uniquely extended to a linear contraction operator (again denoted by
A
) from
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
to
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
. Furthermore, we have

{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{Ay},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}=-\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\leq 0

for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
.

(iii)

Any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
is
\mu
-integrable and satisfies

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}|y(\theta)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}.

Furthermore, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
M=M(\mu,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}|y(\theta)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+M\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

The assertion (i) is trivial from the definition.

For the proof of (ii), noting that
{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\subset[0,\infty)
, we have

\|e^{-\cdot t}y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\leq\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}=\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
t\geq 0
. Thus,
e^{-\cdot t}
is contractive on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
for any
t\geq 0
. The semigroup property of
\{e^{-\cdot t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is trivial. For any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|e^{-\cdot t}y-y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)(e^{-\theta t}-1)^{2}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}=0.

Therefore, the semigroup
\{e^{-\cdot t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous. Now let
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
be the infinitesimal generator of
\{e^{-\cdot t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, and define
\bar{A}:\mathcal{D}(\bar{A})\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
by

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(\bar{A}):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta^{2}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty\right\},\\
(\bar{A}y)(\theta):=-\theta y(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ y\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{A}).\end{cases}

Let
y\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{A})
. By the dominated convergence theorem, we see that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{t}\{e^{-\cdot t}y-y\}-\bar{A}y\Big\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\Big|\frac{1}{t}\{e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)-y(\theta)\}+\theta y(\theta)\Big|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big\{1-\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta s}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\}^{2}\theta^{2}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\to 0

as
t\downarrow 0
. Thus,
y\in\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\bar{A}y=Ay
. Conversely, for any
y\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta^{2}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\Big|\frac{1}{t}\{e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)-y(\theta)\}\Big|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\Big|\frac{1}{t}\{e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)-y(\theta)\}\Big|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|(Ay)(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty.

Hence,
y\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{A})
. Therefore,
\mathcal{D}(A)=\mathcal{D}(\bar{A})
and
Ay=\bar{A}y
for any
y\in\mathcal{D}(A)
. Furthermore, for any
y\in\mathcal{D}(A)
,

\displaystyle\|Ay\|_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)^{-1}r(\theta)|-\theta y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\leq\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}=\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}.

Since
\mathcal{D}(A)
is dense in
(\mathcal{V}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}})
, the operator
A
(more precisely, the composition of
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and the embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
) can be uniquely extended to a contraction operator
A:\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
. Furthermore, for any
y\in\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{Ay},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\langle Ay,y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)+\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=-\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}.

By the density of
\mathcal{D}(A)
in
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and the continuity of
A:\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
, we get the last assertion of (ii).

We show (iii). For any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}|y(\theta)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}.

By the assumption
r(\theta)\geq 1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})
and the following observation:

(\theta+1)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\leq(\theta+1)^{-1}(1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2}))^{-1}\leq 1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})\leq r(\theta),

we have

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}|y(\theta)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}<\infty.

Similarly, for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and any
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}|y(\theta)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+m)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+m)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+m)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\big(\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+(m-1)\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\big).

Since
(\theta+m)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\leq(\theta+1)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\leq r(\theta)
and
\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we see that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+m)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=0
. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
m=m(\mu,\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+m)^{-1}r(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\varepsilon.

Then, by letting
M:=\varepsilon(m-1)
, we get the assertion (iii).
∎

Remark 2.6
.

(i)

We do not need the conditions that
r
is non-increasing or
r\leq 1
for
2.5
(and
2.7
below). Such conditions will be crucial in
Section

4
.

(ii)

By
2.5
(ii), we have the continuous embeddings
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, where
(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}})
is a separable Banach space defined by
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\cap L^{1}(\mu)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}
. Also, noting that
r(\theta)\leq 1\leq(\theta+1)r(\theta)
, we have the continuous embeddings
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mu)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. The spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
L^{1}(\mu)
have no inclusion-relation in general. On the one hand, if the kernel
K
is singular, or equivalently if
\mu([1,\infty))=\infty
, then we have
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\setminus L^{1}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, any non-zero constant vectors belong to
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\setminus L^{1}(\mu)
. On the other hand, if there exists a Borel set
B\subset{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
such that
\mu(B)>0
and
\mu
is atomless on
B
, then it can be easily shown that
L^{1}(\mu)\setminus\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}\neq\emptyset
, and in particular
L^{1}(\mu)\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\neq\emptyset
.

(iii)

It is easy to see that the closed operator
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is self-adjoint and nonpositive. The sets of spectra, point spectra and continuous spectra of the nonnegative operator
-A
are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{spec}\,(-A)={\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},

\displaystyle\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{p}}(-A)=\{\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\,|\,\mu(\{\theta\})>0\}\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{spec}_{\mathrm{c}}(-A)=\{\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\,|\,\mu(\{\theta\})=0\},

respectively.

(iv)

We remark on some measurability issues. Since
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is a separable Hilbert space, by the Pettis measurability theorem, a function
f:S\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
defined on a measurable space
(S,\mathcal{S})
is (strongly) measurable (with respect to
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
) if and only if it is weakly measurable (i.e.,
s\mapsto\langle y,f(s)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
is measurable for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
). Furthermore, since
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
are Polish spaces with continuous embeddings, the Lusin–Suslin theorem yields that
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
,
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu})
, and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V}_{\mu})=\{A\cap\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\,|\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})\}
,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})=\{A\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\,|\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu})\}
. In particular, the restriction map
y\mapsto\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}(y)y
from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
is
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{V}_{\mu})
-measurable. Also, the map
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to[0,\infty]
(which is the norm on
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and takes
\infty
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
) is
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
-measurable. Similarly, we have
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu})=\{A\cap L^{1}(\mu)\,|\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})\}
, and the restriction map
y\mapsto\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}}(y)y
from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}
is
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu})
-measurable.

In our studies of the SVIE (
2.1
) and the lifted SEE (
2.2
), the integral
\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
of a function
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to
\mu
plays a key role. Recall that
(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}})
is a separable Banach space defined by
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\cap L^{1}(\mu)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}
. Clearly, the integral operator
\mu_{0}[\cdot]:\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mu_{0}[y]:=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ y\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu},

is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and in particular it is
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable. We extend this map to
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
by the following way: For each
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, we define
\mu[y]\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\mu[y]:=\mu_{0}[\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}}(y)y]=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\ &\text{if $y\in L^{1}(\mu)$},\\
0\ &\text{if $y\notin L^{1}(\mu)$}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

By the
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu})
-measurability of the restriction map
y\mapsto\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}}(y)y
(see
2.6
(iv)), we see that the map
\mu[\cdot]:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable. In particular, for any
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
-valued predictable process
Y
, the “integrated process”
\mu[Y]=(\mu[Y_{t}])_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued predictable process. On the one hand, since
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mu}
continuously (see
2.5
(iii)), we have
\mu[\cdot]=\mu_{0}[\cdot]
on
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, and it is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On the other hand, the measurable map
\mu[\cdot]:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
itself is neither continuous nor linear on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
in general.

Next, for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, we define

(\mathcal{K}y)(t):=\mu[e^{-\cdot t}y],\ t\geq 0.

(2.4)

Then
\mathcal{K}y:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Borel measurable. Note that, for any
t>0
and any

, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-\cdot t}y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta t+1}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2t}\vee 1\Big)\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2t}\vee 1\Big)\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<\infty.

Hence,
e^{-\cdot t}
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, and
(\mathcal{K}y)(t)=\mu_{0}[e^{-\cdot t}y]=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for any
t>0
and any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Furthermore, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\|e^{-\cdot t}y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big(\frac{1}{2\theta}\wedge T\Big)(\theta+1)r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}+T\Big)\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}+T\Big)\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<\infty.

Thus, by
2.5
(iii), we see that the map
\mathcal{K}:y\mapsto(\mathcal{K}y)(\cdot)
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
T>0
.

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Let

(i)

-continuous version of

. Since

a.s. By Tonelli’s theorem, we have

\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto\hat{\sigma}(t,y)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
are continuous for any
t\geq 0
a.s.

•

(Monotonicity). It holds that

2{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{\hat{b}(t,y)-\hat{b}(t,\bar{y})},{y-\bar{y}}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\|\hat{\sigma}(t,y)-\hat{\sigma}(t,\bar{y})\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}_{\mu})}\leq\gamma\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
t\geq 0
and
y,\bar{y}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
a.s.

•

(Coercivity). It holds that

2{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{\hat{b}(t,y)},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\|\hat{\sigma}(t,y)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}_{\mu})}\leq-\kappa\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\gamma\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\psi_{t}

for any
(t,y)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
a.s.

•

(Growth). It holds that

\|\hat{b}(t,y)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\leq\psi_{t}+\gamma\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}

for any
(t,y)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
a.s.

Therefore, by the well-known result of monotone SPDEs (cf.
[
28
]
), the equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=\hat{b}(t,Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\hat{\sigma}(t,Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}=\eta,\end{cases}

with
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
has a unique variational solution, which is the mild solution of the generalized SEE (
2.9
) in our framework.

However, the well-posedness of the generalized SEE (
2.9
) under
2
(iii)’ (the local Lipschitz condition) is not so trivial from the context of monotone SPDEs. Although the well-posedness of SPDEs with locally (in the space variable) monotone coefficients was proved by Liu and Röckner
[
30
]
, we cannot apply their results to our setting; our assumption is local in both space (the
y
variable) and time.

Thus, we need further observations for the well-posedness of the generalized SEE (
2.9
), especially in the local Lipschitz case. Here, we state our results, which will be proved in
Appendix
.

2.4
Markov property of the mild solution of the lifted SEE (
2.2
)

We turn to the SVIE (
2.1
) and the lifted SEE (
2.2
) with deterministic coefficients
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. The following theorem shows that the mild solution
Y
of the lifted SEE (
2.2
) is indeed a “Markovian” lift of the SVIE (
2.1
). The proof is standard (see for example
[
17
, Chapter 9]
), but we give a complete proof here for readers’ convenience.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
1
hold. Suppose that the maps
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are globally Lipschitz continuous. Then for any initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, there exists a unique mild solution
Y^{y}
of the lifted SEE (
2.2
). The family
\{Y^{y}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0,y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
of the mild solutions is a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined by

P_{t}f(y):=\mathbb{E}[f(Y^{y}_{t})],\ t\geq 0,\ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\ f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}),

where
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
denotes the set of bounded Borel measurable functions
f:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,
\{Y^{y}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0,y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
has the Feller property, namely,
P_{t}f\in C_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
for any
f\in C_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
and any
t\geq 0
, where
C_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
denotes the set of bounded continuous funcions
f:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By
2.17
, there exists a unique mild solution
Y^{y}
of the lifted SEE (
2.2
) for any initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. More generally, for any
t_{0}\geq 0
,
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
and
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, consider the following SEEs:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu[\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu[\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{t},\ t>0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\\
\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{0}(\theta)=\eta(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{t},\ t>0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\\
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

where
W^{t_{0}}_{t}:=W_{t_{0}+t}-W_{t_{0}}
,
t\geq 0
, which is a Brownian motion independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}
. By
2.17
, there exist unique mild solutions
\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}
and
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}
to the above SEEs. Clearly,
Y^{y}=\mathcal{Y}^{0,y}=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{0,y}
for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Note that, for any
t_{0},t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle Y^{y}_{t_{0}+t}(\theta)

\displaystyle=e^{-\theta(t_{0}+t)}y(\theta)+\int^{t_{0}+t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t_{0}+t-s)}b(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t_{0}+t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t_{0}+t-s)}\sigma(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\theta t}\Big\{e^{-\theta t_{0}}y(\theta)+\int^{t_{0}}_{0}e^{-\theta(t_{0}-s)}b(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t_{0}}_{0}e^{-\theta(t_{0}-s)}\sigma(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{t_{0}+t}_{t_{0}}e^{-\theta(t_{0}+t-s)}b(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t_{0}+t}_{t_{0}}e^{-\theta(t_{0}+t-s)}\sigma(\mu[Y^{y}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\theta t}Y^{y}_{t_{0}}(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(\mu[Y^{y}_{t_{0}+s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(\mu[Y^{y}_{t_{0}+s}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{s}

for
\mu
-a.e.
\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
a.s. Thus, by the uniqueness, we have
Y^{y}_{t_{0}+t}=\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},Y^{y}_{t_{0}}}_{t}
for any
t\geq 0
a.s. Furthermore, by the uniqueness in law of the SEE (
2.2
), which follows from the pathwise uniqueness and the Yamada–Watanabe theorem (see
[
29
]
for the general form of the Yamada–Watanabe theorem), we see that
Y^{y}
and
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}
have the same law in the space
C([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
for any
t_{0}\geq 0
and any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. This implies that

\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t})]=\mathbb{E}[f(Y^{y}_{t})]=P_{t}f(y)

for any
t,t_{0}\geq 0
,
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
.

Now we show that:

(i)

there exists a version of
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}
for each
t_{0}\geq 0
and
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
such that the map
(\omega,t,y)\mapsto\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}(\omega)\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is
\mathbb{F}^{t_{0}}
-predictable, and hence independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}
, where
\mathbb{F}^{t_{0}}=(\mathcal{F}^{t_{0}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the filtration generated by the Brownian motion
W^{t_{0}}
;

(ii)

for any
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
, we have
\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\omega)=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)
for any
t\geq 0
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

The above two assertions imply that

\mathbb{E}\left[f\big(Y^{y}_{t_{0}+t}\big)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f\Big(\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},Y^{y}_{t_{0}}}_{t}\Big)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f\Big(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},Y^{y}_{t_{0}}}_{t}\Big)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}\right]=P_{t}f\big(Y^{y}_{t_{0}}\big)\ \text{a.s.}

for any
t,t_{0}\geq 0
,
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
, which means that
\{Y^{y}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0,y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
is a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

We prove the assertion (i). Let
t_{0}\geq 0
be fixed. For each
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, Define
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,0}_{t}:=0
,
t\geq 0
, and

\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}_{t}(\theta):=e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k-1}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k-1}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{s}

for
t\geq 0
,
\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Noting
[
32
, Theorem 63 and its corollary]
for the joint measurability of parametrized stochastic integrals, by the induction, we see that each
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}
has a version (again denoted by
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}
) such that the map
(\omega,t,y)\mapsto\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}_{t}(\omega)\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is
\mathbb{F}^{t_{0}}
-predictable. Define

\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,\infty}_{t}:=\begin{cases}\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}_{t}\ &\text{if the limit exists},\\
0\ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the limit is taken in
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Then
(\omega,t,y)\mapsto\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,\infty}_{t}(\omega)
is
\mathbb{F}^{t_{0}}
-predictable. From the proof of
2.17
(see also
A.1
), we see that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,k}\|_{T,\lambda,\kappa}=0
for any
T>0
, where the constants
\lambda,\kappa>0
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{T,\lambda,\kappa}
were defined in the proof of
2.17
. This implies that
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,\infty}_{t}
a.s. for any
t\geq 0
and any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Therefore,
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y,\infty}
is a desired version of
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}
.

Next, we prove the assertion (ii). We first assume that
\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
is of the form
\eta(\theta,\omega)=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}y_{i}(\theta)\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}(\omega)
, where
y_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, are deterministic and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}
-measurable partition of
\Omega
. Define
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\theta,\omega):=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta(\omega)}_{t}(\theta,\omega)
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{i}}_{t}(\theta)\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\Big\{e^{-\theta t}y_{i}(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{i}}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{i}}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{s}\Big\}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\theta t}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}y_{i}(\theta)\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}b(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{i}}_{s}])\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\sigma(\mu[\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{i}}_{s}])\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{s}

\displaystyle=e^{-\theta t}\eta(\theta)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}b(\mu[\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\theta(t-s)}\sigma(\mu[\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta}_{s}])\,\mathrm{d}W^{t_{0}}_{s},

for
\mu
-a.e.
\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
a.s. for any
t\geq 0
. Thus, by the uniqueness, we have
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}=\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}
for any
t\geq 0
a.s., which shows the assertion (ii) in the special case.

In the general case, we can take a sequence
\eta_{k}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\eta_{k}
are of the above special forms and
\|\eta_{k}-\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\leq\frac{1}{k}
a.s. for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta_{k}}_{t}(\omega)=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta_{k}(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)
for any
t\geq 0
a.s. By
2.17
(ii), there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
T>0
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{1}}_{t}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]\leq Ce^{CT}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, and

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta_{1}}_{t}-\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]\leq Ce^{CT}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\big]

for any
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
. On the one hand, noting that the map
(\omega,t,y)\mapsto\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y}_{t}(\omega)
is
\mathbb{F}^{t_{0}}
-predictable and hence independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta_{k}(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{1}}_{t}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},y_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]\Big|_{y_{1}=\eta(\omega),y_{2}=\eta_{k}(\omega)}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta-\eta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\big]\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}Ce^{CT}

for any
T>0
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}-\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta_{k}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]\leq Ce^{CT}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta-\eta_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\big]\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}Ce^{CT}

for any
T>0
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we have
\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)-\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\omega)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\big]=0
for any
T>0
, and hence
\mathcal{Y}^{t_{0},\eta}_{t}(\omega)=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{t_{0},\eta(\omega)}_{t}(\omega)
for any
t\geq 0
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. This proves the assertion (ii).

It remains to show the Feller property. Again by
2.17
(ii), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{y_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]\leq Ce^{CT}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
T>0
and any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. This implies that the map
y\mapsto Y^{y}_{t}
is continuous in probability for any
t\geq 0
. Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem, for any
f\in C_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
,
t\geq 0
, and any sequence
y_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}y_{k}=y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}P_{t}f(y_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[f(Y^{y_{k}}_{t})\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[f(Y^{y}_{t})\big]=P_{t}f(y).

Therefore,
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has the Feller property. This completes the proof.

n=d=1
. Let

) with

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t>0,

for some

. The corresponding lifted SEE (

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ t>0,\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\\

(3.2)

The unique mild solution of the above SEE is given by

Y_{t}=e^{-\cdot t}y(\cdot)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t\geq 0.

Similar infinite dimensional processes were considered in

,

]
, where

and

were adopted as the state spaces. However, in their settings, since the diffusion coefficient
1\notin L^{1}(\mu)\cup L^{2}(\mu)
in general, one cannot use the usual framework, for example
[
16
,
17
]
, of the infinite dimensional stochastic calculus.

In our framework, the process
Y
is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. The associated transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
, and the transition probability
P_{t}(y,A):=P_{t}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{A}(y)
,
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
,
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
t\geq 0
, is given by
P_{t}(y,\cdot)=\mathcal{N}(e^{-\cdot t}y,Q_{t})
. Here,
\mathcal{N}(e^{-\cdot t}y,Q_{t})
denotes a Gaussian measure on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the mean
e^{-\cdot t}y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and the covariance operator
Q_{t}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
given by

Q_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot s}\iota\iota^{*}(e^{-\cdot s})^{*}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\iota:\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
denotes the inclusion operator. It is easy to see that
e^{-\cdot t}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is self-adjoint and that
\iota^{*}y=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, and hence

Q_{t}y=\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot s}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)e^{-\theta s}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int^{t}_{0}(e^{-\cdot s}1)\otimes(e^{-\cdot s}1)(y)\,\mathrm{d}s

for each
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Here, for
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
y_{1}\otimes y_{2}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
denotes the tensor product.

In the rest of this section, we characterize the invariant probability measure and discuss the strong Feller property for the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
). We can apply the general theory of
[
16
,
17
]
to our framework.

3.1
Characterization of the invariant probability measure

We characterize the existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability measure of the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Recall that a probability measure
\pi
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is called an
invariant probability measure
of
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
if

\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}P_{t}(y,A)\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)=\pi(A)

for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
and any
t\geq 0
. By means of the general theory on the ergodicity of infinite dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes (cf.
[
17
, Section 11.3]
), we get the following results.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1
hold.

(i)

If both
\mu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(0,1]}\theta^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
hold (or equivalently if
\int^{\infty}_{1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
holds), then there exists a unique invariant probability measure of the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
). The invariant probability measure is a centred Gaussian measure
\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
with the covariance operator
Q:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
given by

(Qy)(\theta)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{r(\theta^{\prime})}{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}y(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}.

Furthermore, it holds that

\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{t}(y,\cdot)=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)\ \text{weakly on $\mathcal{H}_{\mu}$}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

(ii)

If either
\mu(\{0\})>0
or
\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(0,1]}\theta^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\infty
holds (or equivalently if
\int^{\infty}_{1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty
holds), then the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
) does not have invariant probability measures.

Proof.

By
[
17
, Theorem 11.17]
, there exists an invariant probability measure of
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
if and only if
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\mathrm{Tr}\,[Q_{t}]<\infty
, where
\mathrm{Tr}\,[Q_{t}]
denotes the trace of the operator
Q_{t}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Observe that

\mathrm{Tr}\,[Q_{t}]=\int^{t}_{0}\|e^{-\cdot s}1\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta s}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s=t\mu(\{0\})+\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{-2\theta t}}{2\theta}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta).

By the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{-2\theta t}}{2\theta}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{-2\theta t}}{2\theta}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{r(\theta)}{2\theta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta).

Therefore,
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\mathrm{Tr}\,[Q_{t}]<\infty
if and only if both
\mu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(0,1]}\theta^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
hold. It is easy to see that the latter is equivalent to
\int^{\infty}_{1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
.

Suppose that
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\mathrm{Tr}\,[Q_{t}]<\infty
. Noting that
\mu(\{0\})=0
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-\cdot t}y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}=0

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Therefore, by
[
17
, Theorems 11.17 and 11.20]
, the invariant probability measure of
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is unique, it is a centred Gaussian measure
\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
with the covariance operator
Q:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
given by

\displaystyle(Qy)(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\big(e^{-\cdot t}\iota\iota^{*}(e^{-\cdot t})^{*}y\big)(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\theta t}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta^{\prime})e^{-\theta^{\prime}t}y(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{r(\theta^{\prime})}{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}y(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}),\ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu},

and it holds that
\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{t}(y,\cdot)=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
weakly on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

On the one hand, if
K=K_{\mathrm{frac}}
is the fractional kernel, then
\int^{\infty}_{1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty
, and thus the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
) does not have invariant probability measures. On the other hand, if
K=K_{\mathrm{gamma}}
is the Gamma kernel, then the associated transition semigroup has a unique invariant probability measure; see
2.3
. The above result indicates that the existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability measure is more about the asymptotic behaviour of the kernel
K(t)
as
t\to\infty
than it is about the regularity/singularity at
t=0
.

Suppose that both
\mu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(0,1]}\theta^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
hold, and let
\pi=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
, which is the invariant probability measure of the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
). Since the mild solution
Y=Y^{y}
of the lifted SEE (
3.2
) with a given initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
satisfies
Y^{y}_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
a.s. for a.e.
t>0
, we have
P_{t}(y,\mathcal{V}_{\mu})=1
for a.e.
t>0
for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, and hence
\pi(\mathcal{V}_{\mu})=1
. Furthermore, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|Qy\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)\Big|\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{r(\theta^{\prime})}{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}y(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\Big|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta+1)r(\theta)\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{\theta^{\prime}+1}{(\theta+\theta^{\prime})^{2}}r(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\,\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(\theta^{\prime}+1)^{-1}r(\theta^{\prime})|y(\theta^{\prime})|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{(\theta+1)(\theta^{\prime}+1)}{(\theta+\theta^{\prime})^{2}}r(\theta)r(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})\,\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{(\theta+1)(\theta^{\prime}+1)}{(\theta+\theta^{\prime})^{2}}r(\theta)r(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}\Big)r(\theta)r(\theta^{\prime})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\theta}\Big)r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{2}<\infty,

we see that
Q(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, and
Q:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
can be extended to a bounded linear operator
Q:\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
. Thus, for any
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}e^{\sqrt{-1}{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{y^{\prime}},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}e^{\sqrt{-1}\langle y^{\prime},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}e^{\sqrt{-1}\langle y^{\prime},y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle y^{\prime},Qy^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{y^{\prime}},{Qy^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is dense in
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
, by using the dominated convergence theorem and the continuity of
Q:\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, we see that

\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}e^{\sqrt{-1}{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{y^{\prime}},{y}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}{}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}}\langle{y^{\prime}},{Qy^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}}

holds for any
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
. Therefore,
\pi=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
can be seen as a centred Gaussian measure on
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
with the covariance operator
Q:\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
.

By means of the invariant probability measure
\pi
of the lifted SEE (
3.2
), we can construct a “stationary solution”
X
of the SVIE (
3.1
). Let
\eta:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
be an initial condition (independent of
W
) with the law
\pi=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
, and let
\xi(t)=(\mathcal{K}\eta)(t)=\mu[e^{-\cdot t}\eta]
,
t\geq 0
. Recall that, for any
t>0
,
e^{-\cdot t}
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, and hence
\xi(t)=\mu_{0}[e^{-\cdot t}\eta]
. It is easy to see that the adjoint operator
\mu^{*}_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
of the integral operator
\mu_{0}:\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
\mu^{*}_{0}[1](\theta)=r(\theta)^{-1}
,
\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
. Thus, the law of
\xi(t)
(on
\mathbb{R}
) is a centred Gaussian measure with the variance

\mu_{0}e^{-\cdot t}Q(\mu_{0}e^{-\cdot t})^{*}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{e^{-(\theta+\theta^{\prime})t}}{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})=\int^{\infty}_{0}K(t+s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let
\bar{Y}
be the mild solution of the lifted SEE (
3.2
) with the initial condition
\eta
, and consider the Volterra process

\bar{X}_{t}=\xi(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ t>0.

Then we have
\bar{Y}_{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
and
\bar{X}_{t}=\mu[\bar{Y}_{t}]=\mu_{0}[\bar{Y}_{t}]
a.s. for a.e.
t>0
. Thus, for a.e.
t>0
, the law of
\bar{X}_{t}
(on
\mathbb{R}
) is a centred Gaussian measure with the variance

\mu_{0}Q\mu^{*}_{0}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\frac{1}{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})=\int^{\infty}_{0}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

which does not depend on
t
.

3.2
Failure of the strong Feller property

Recall that a Markov transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
strongly Feller
at time
t>0
if
P_{t}f
is continuous for any
\mathbb{R}
-valued bounded measurable function
f
. It is well-known that if the noise is nondegenerate (i.e. the diffusion is elliptic in some sense), the strong Feller property can be obtained as a consequence of the Bismut–Elworthy–Li formula (cf.
[
17
, Section 9.4]
) or the log-Harnack inequality (cf.
[
38
]
). However, since the noise of the lifted SEE (
3.2
) is degenerate (i.e.
\iota\iota^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is not elliptic), there is no reason that the strong Feller property holds for the associated transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Indeed, the following theorem shows that, unfortunately, the strong Feller property does not hold in a typical infinite dimensional situation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1
hold. Assume that there exists a Borel set
B\subset{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}
such that
\mu(B)>0
and
\mu
is atomless on
B
. Then the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the lifted SEE (
3.2
) is not strongly Feller. More precisely, for any
t>0
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
such that
y\mapsto P_{t}f(y)
is not continuous on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
t>0
be fixed. By
[
16
, Theorem 7.2.1]
,
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Feller at time
t
if and only if

e^{-\cdot t}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})\subset Q^{1/2}_{t}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}).

(3.3)

Furthermore, noting that the operators
e^{-\cdot t}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and
Q^{1/2}_{t}:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
are self adjoint, by
[
17
, Proposition B.1]
, (
3.3
) holds if and only if there exists a constant
k>0
such that
\|e^{-\cdot t}y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\leq k\|Q^{1/2}_{t}y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Therefore, to prove our assertion, it suffices to show that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
such that
\|Q^{1/2}_{t}y_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<\varepsilon\|e^{-\cdot t}y_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
.

Let
M\in(0,\infty)
be such that
\mu(B\cap[0,M])>0
. Fix an arbitrary number
\varepsilon>0
. By the assumption, there exists a Borel set
B_{\varepsilon}\subset B\cap[0,M]
such that

0<\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\frac{2M\varepsilon^{2}}{e^{2Mt}-1}.

Take
y_{\varepsilon}:=\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{B_{\varepsilon}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Note that

\|e^{-\cdot t}y_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\Big(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2}>0.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|Q^{1/2}_{t}y_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{t}y_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\Big|\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)e^{-\theta s}y_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\Big|\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)e^{-\theta s}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta s}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}e^{2M(t-s)}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2Mt}-1}{2M}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\|e^{-\cdot t}y_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon^{2}\|e^{-\cdot t}y_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}},

and hence
\|Q^{1/2}_{t}y_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<\varepsilon\|e^{-\cdot t}y_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
K
is either the fractional kernel
K_{\mathrm{frac}}
or the Gamma kernel
K_{\mathrm{gamma}}
, then the corresponding Radon measure
\mu
is diffusive, and thus the associated transition semigroup of the lifted SEE (
3.2
) is not strongly Feller; see
2.3
.

The above negative result indicates that it is difficult to capture the “smoothing effect” even in the simple example. The difficulty comes from the fact that the lifted SEE (
3.2
) is
highly degenerate
; the state space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is infinite dimensional, while the noise (Brownian motion) is one dimensional. Nevertheless, we can show a weaker version of the strong Feller property for general lifted SEEs (under suitable assumptions). This can be recovered by means of the

asymptotic log-Harnack inequality
, which is our main topic in the next section.

4
Asymptotic log-Harnack inequality

In this section, we investigate asymptotic properties of the transition semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the (general) lifted SEE (
2.2
) defined in
2.19
. In particular, we derive an
asymptotic log-Harnack inequality
for
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and show some important consequences.

We impose the following assumptions on the maps
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.

Assumption 3
.

There exists a constant
L>0
such that

|b(x)-b(\bar{x})|+|\sigma(x)-\sigma(\bar{x})|\leq L|x-\bar{x}|

for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore,
\|\sigma\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\sigma(x)|<\infty
, and for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the matrix
\sigma(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
has a right inverse
\sigma^{-1}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
such that the map
x\mapsto\sigma^{-1}(x)
is measurable and
\|\sigma^{-1}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\sigma^{-1}(x)|<\infty
.

Remark 4.1
.

We note that the above assumption requires that the dimension
d
of Brownian motion
W
is greater than or equal to the dimension
n
of solutions of SVIEs, and the matrix
\sigma(x)
is of rank
n
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the following, for a function
f:\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}
, we use the notations:

|\nabla f|(y):=\limsup_{\bar{y}\to y}\frac{|f(y)-f(\bar{y})|}{\|y-\bar{y}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}},\ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu},

and
\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}|\nabla f|(y)
.

4.1
Main result and applications

The following is the main result of this section, which is proved in the next subsection.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1
and
3
hold. Then the following
asymptotic log-Harnack inequality
holds:

\begin{split}P_{t}\log f(\bar{y})&\leq\log P_{t}f(y)+\frac{1}{2}\|\sigma^{-1}\|^{2}_{\infty}\Big(1+2L^{2}\Big(1+\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)r(m)^{-2}\Big)\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+r(m)^{-1/2}e^{-\beta t/2}\|y-\bar{y}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\|\nabla\log f\|_{\infty}\end{split}

(4.1)

for any
t\geq 0
,
y,\bar{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
such that
f\geq 1
and
\|\nabla\log f\|_{\infty}<\infty
, where
\beta:=\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\geq 0
, and
m\geq 1
is an arbitrary constant satisfying

2L^{2}\Big(1+\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap[m,\infty)}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq 1.

(4.2)

Remark 4.3
.

(i)

Under
1
, there exists a constant
m\geq 1
such that (
4.2
) holds.

(ii)

4.2
holds true even for
\beta:=\inf{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}=0
. However, in order to control the asymptotic decay of the remainder term of (
4.1
) as
t\to\infty
, we need
\beta>0
. This excludes the case of the fractional kernel, while the Gamma kernel satisfies this condition; see
2.3
. We need further considerations for the case
\beta=0
, and we leave this problem to the future research.

By means of the general results established in
[
7
,
40
]
, we see that the asymptotic log-Harnack inequality (
4.1
) (with
\beta>0
) implies that the Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
asymptotically strong Feller
,
asymptotically irreducible
, and
possesses at most one invariant probability measure
. Before stating the results, let us recall the definition of the asymptotic strong Feller property introduced by Hairer and Mattingly
[
23
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
E
be a Polish space endowed with the Borel
\sigma
-field, and
\mathcal{P}(E)
the set of probability measures on
E
.

•

A continuous function
d:E\times E\to[0,\infty)
is called a
pseudo-metric
on
E
if
d(x,x)=0
and
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
hold for
x,y,z\in E
. An increasing sequence of pseudo-metrics
\{d_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(i.e.,
d_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)\leq d_{k+1}(\cdot,\cdot)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
) is said to be a
totally separating system
if
\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{k}(x,y)=1
for any
x\neq y
.

•

For a pseudo-metric
d
, the
transportation cost
(which is also called the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance when

\hat{\tau}_{k}
by

\hat{\tau}_{k}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\|Y^{k}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}>k\}\wedge\tau_{k}.

Then by
2.17
(ii), for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k_{1}>k_{2}
and any
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k_{1}}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}-Y^{k_{2}}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}_{0}\|Y^{k_{1}}_{t}-Y^{k_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{k_{1}}e^{C_{k_{1}}T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}_{0}\Big\{\big|b_{k_{1}}(t,\mu[Y^{k_{2}}_{t}],Y^{k_{2}}_{t})-b_{k_{2}}(t,\mu[Y^{k_{2}}_{t}],Y^{k_{2}}_{t})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\big|\sigma_{k_{1}}(t,\mu[Y^{k_{2}}_{t}],Y^{k_{2}}_{t})-\sigma_{k_{2}}(t,\mu[Y^{k_{2}}_{t}],Y^{k_{2}}_{t})\big|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big],

for some constant
C_{k_{1}}>0
which depends only on
\mu
and
L_{k_{1}}
. By the definitions of
b_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
and
\hat{\tau}_{k}
, noting that
k_{1}>k_{2}
, we see that the right-hand side is equal to zero. Therefore, we see that

Y^{k_{1}}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}=Y^{k_{2}}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}}\ \text{for any $t\geq 0$ a.s.}

This further implies that
\hat{\tau}_{k_{1}}\geq\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}
a.s. Indeed, for any
T\geq 0
,
\hat{\tau}_{k_{2}}\geq T
implies that
\tau_{k_{1}}\geq\tau_{k_{2}}\geq T
by the assumption of
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and

k_{1}>k_{2}\geq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k_{2}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k_{1}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}},

which implies that
\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\|Y^{k_{1}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}>k_{1}\}\geq T
, and hence
\hat{\tau}_{k_{1}}\geq T
. Thus, the sequence
\{\hat{\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of stopping times is non-decreasing. Furthermore, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{\tau}_{k}\leq T\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\hat{\tau}_{k}\leq T\big)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\tau_{k}\leq T\ \text{or}\ \sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}>k\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{k}\leq T\big)+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}>k\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big],

and the last term is zero due to the uniform bound (
A.5
). This implies that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{\tau}_{k}=\infty
a.s. Now we define

Y_{t}:=\begin{cases}\lim_{k\to\infty}Y^{k}_{t}\ &\text{if the limit exists},\\
0\ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the limit is taken in
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Then we see that
Y
is an
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
-valued adapted process and satisfies

Y_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k}}=Y^{k}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k}}\ \text{for any $t\geq 0$ a.s.}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since each
Y^{k}
is
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
-continuous and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{\tau}_{k}=\infty
a.s., we see that
Y
is
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
-continuous a.s. Furthermore, by Fatou’s lemma and (
A.5
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T}_{0}\|Y_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k}}_{0}\|Y^{k}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\eta\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T}_{0}\varphi^{2}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]<\infty

for any
T>0
. In particular, by
2.5
(iii) and
2
(ii) (the linear growth condition), we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T}_{0}\Big\{|b(t,\mu[Y_{t}],Y_{t})|+|\sigma(t,\mu[Y_{t}],Y_{t})|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]<\infty

for any
T>0
. Then, by the definitions of
b_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
and
\hat{\tau}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{t}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{t\leq\hat{\tau}_{k}\}}

\displaystyle=Y^{k}_{t}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{t\leq\hat{\tau}_{k}\}}

\displaystyle=\Big\{e^{-\cdot t}\eta(\cdot)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}b_{k}(s,\mu[Y^{k}_{s}],Y^{k}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}\sigma_{k}(s,\mu[Y^{k}_{s}],Y^{k}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big\}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{t\leq\hat{\tau}_{k}\}}

\displaystyle=\Big\{e^{-\cdot t}\eta(\cdot)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}b(s,\mu[Y_{s}],Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}\sigma(s,\mu[Y_{s}],Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big\}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{t\leq\hat{\tau}_{k}\}}

a.s. for any
t\geq 0
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By taking the limit
k\to\infty
, we see that

Y_{t}=e^{-\cdot t}\eta(\cdot)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}b(s,\mu[Y_{s}],Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\cdot(t-s)}\sigma(s,\mu[Y_{s}],Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

a.s. for any
t\geq 0
. Therefore,
Y
is a mild solution of the generalized SEE (
2.9
).

Next, we prove the uniqueness. Let
\bar{Y}
be another mild solution of the generalized SEE (
2.9
), and define

\bar{\tau}_{k}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\|\bar{Y}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}>k\}\wedge\hat{\tau}_{k}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\{\bar{\tau}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of stopping times such that
\bar{\tau}_{k}\leq\bar{\tau}_{k+1}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{\tau}_{k}=\infty
a.s. By the construction of
Y
and
2.17
(ii), for any
T>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}-\bar{Y}_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}_{0}\|Y_{t}-\bar{Y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}-\bar{Y}_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}_{0}\|Y^{k}_{t}-\bar{Y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}e^{C_{k}T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}_{0}\Big\{|b_{k}(t,\mu[\bar{Y}_{t}],\bar{Y}_{t})-b(t,\mu[\bar{Y}_{t}],\bar{Y}_{t})|^{2}+|\sigma_{k}(t,\mu[\bar{Y}_{t}],\bar{Y}_{t})-\sigma(t,\mu[\bar{Y}_{t}],\bar{Y}_{t})|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

for some constant
C_{k}>0
depending only on
\mu
and
L_{k}
. By the definitions of
b_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
and
\bar{\tau}_{k}
, we see that the last term is zero. Thus,
\bar{Y}_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}=Y_{t\wedge\bar{\tau}_{k}}
for any
t\geq 0
a.s. for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{\tau}_{k}=\infty
a.s., we see that
\bar{Y}_{t}=Y_{t}
for any
t\geq 0
a.s. This proves the uniqueness.
∎

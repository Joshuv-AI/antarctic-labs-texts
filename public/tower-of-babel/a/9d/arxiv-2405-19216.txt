Central Limit Theorem for Tensor Products of Free Variables via Bi-Free Independence Thanks: This research was completed with the support of NSERC (Canada) grant RGPIN-2024-06269.
Paul Skoufranis

Abstract
. In this paper, a connection between bi-free probability and the asymptotics of random quantum channels and tensor products of random matrices is established. Using bi-free matrix models, it is demonstrated that the spectral distribution of certain self-adjoint quantum channels and tensor products of random matrices tend to a distribution that can be obtained by an averaged sum of products of bi-freely independent pairs. Subsequently, using bi-free techniques, a Central Limit Theorem for such operator is established.

1.
Introduction

One essential concept in Quantum Information Theory is that of quantum channels which are mathematical/physical models for how quantum information can be transmitted. Given a quantum channel
\Phi:M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, the (non-unique) Kraus decomposition of
\Phi
implies there exists
\{K_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}\subseteq M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\Phi(X)=\sum^{d}_{j=1}K_{j}XK_{j}^{*}

for all
X\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Viewing
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, it is natural to examine
\Phi
through its Kraus operator

K_{\Phi}=\sum^{d}_{j=1}K_{j}\otimes\overline{K_{j}}

where
\overline{K_{j}}
denotes the entry-wise conjugation of
K_{j}
. Furthermore,
\{K_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}
can be assumed to be self-adjoint when
\Phi
is self-adjoint in the sense that
{\mathrm{tr}}(X\Phi(Y))={\mathrm{tr}}(\Phi(X)Y)
for all
X,Y\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.

In
[
6
]
the asymptotic behaviour of random self-adjoint quantum channels was examined. This was accomplished by fixing a
d
, taking the above
\{K_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}
to be random self-adjoint
n\times n
matrices, and examining the behaviour of the Kraus operator as
n
tends to infinity. In particular, the following was demonstrated.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
]
*Theorem 2.2)
.

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{d}
be centred, self-adjoint random
n\times n
matrices such that

•

W_{j}
converges weakly in probability and expectation to
\mu_{j}
as
n
tends to infinity for all
j
,

•

\{W_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}
are in probability and expectation asymptotically free as
n
tends to infinity, and

•

\mathbb{E}\left(W_{j}\otimes\overline{W_{j}}\right)
converges weakly to 0 as
n
tends to infinity for all
j
.

Let
\Phi_{d}
be the quantum channel with Kraus operator

\sum^{d}_{j=1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}W_{j}\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\overline{W_{j}},

let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
be freely independent random variables with respect to
\varphi
with distributions
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d}
respectively, and let

\Delta_{d,n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\left(W_{j}\otimes\overline{W_{j}}-\mathbb{E}\left(W_{j}\otimes\overline{W_{j}}\right)\right).

Then in probability and expectation the distribution of
\Delta_{d,n}
(and thus the spectral distribution of
\Phi_{d}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{d})
) tends to the distribution of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{d}_{j=1}a_{j}\otimes a_{j}
with respect to
\varphi\otimes\varphi
as
n
tends to infinity.

Moreover, if
d=d(n)
diverges as
n
tends to infinity and
\{W_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}
are normalized, independent, and identically distributed, then in probability and expectation the distribution of
\Delta_{d,n}
(and thus the spectral distribution of
\Phi_{d}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{d})
) tends to the centred semicircular distribution of variance 1 as
n
tends to infinity.

To obtain the semicircular distribution,
[
6
]
demonstrated that the distribution of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{d}_{j=1}a_{j}\otimes a_{j}
with respect to
\varphi\otimes\varphi
tends to the semicircular distribution as
d
tends to infinity. Furthermore, the same authors in
[
7
]
developed a Central Limit Theorem for tensor products of freely independent operators in the case where the

need not be centred (see Theorem
5.1
). The above results led
[
7
]
to pose the question of whether there was a general notion of independence corresponding to such objects where properties and limits could be derived.

In
[
14
]
Voiculescu introduced the notion of bi-free independence. Since then the theory has been quite extensively developed (e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,

,
11
,

). Unlikely other notions of independence, bi-free independence is a notion of independence for pairs of algebras. In particular, if
({\mathcal{A}}_{1},{\mathcal{B}}_{1})
and
({\mathcal{A}}_{2},{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
are bi-freely independent, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are freely independent,
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
are freely independent,
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

{\mathcal{A}}_{2}

are classically independent. Although the converse need not hold in general (see

The goal of this paper is to demonstrate that bi-free independence is indeed an appropriate notion of independence where properties of sums of tensor products of freely independent operators can be studied, and properties and limits can be derived. In particular, we will demonstrate that the results mentioned above can be verified using bi-free techniques in a more direct fashion. Consequently, this paper establishes a direct connection between bi-free probability and the asymptotics of random quantum channels and tensor products of random matrices.

This paper also directly advances the techniques of bi-free probability. In the current literature involving bi-free independence, the left and right operators remain isolated from one another. For example, the bi-free cumulants (see Section
2
) distinguish which entries are left and which entries are right (mixing is not allowed), all bi-free transformations are two variable transformations with one variable for left operators and one variable for right operators, and the existing notion of bi-free linearization (
[
1
]
) can only handle the joint distribution of a polynomial in left variables and a polynomial in right variable (that is, it cannot handle a single polynomial in left and right variables). The only mixing of left and right operators that has occurred was with bi-free conjugate variables in
[
4
]
as the bi-free conjugate variables were in the
L_{2}
-space generated by the left and right operators, but this did not yield any spectral information about polynomials in left and right operators. This work shows that sums of products of left and right operators can be studied using bi-free techniques thereby yielding hope the theory can be used to examine arbitrary polynomials in left and right operators.

Excluding this introduction, this paper has four sections structured as follows. Section

Sections

2.
Background on Bi-Free Independence

To study bi-free independence, some structures used in free independence are required. For a natural number
n
, let
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. A partition
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}
of
[n]
is a collection of disjoint sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
, called blocks of
\pi
, such that

[n]=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}(n)
denote the set of all partitions on
[n]
. Given
\pi,\sigma\in{\mathcal{P}}(n)
, it is said that
\sigma
is a
refinement
of
\pi
, denoted
\sigma\leq\pi
, if every block of
\sigma
is contained in a single block of
\pi
.

A partition
\pi
on
[n]
is said to be
non-crossing
if whenever there are blocks
V,W\in\pi
with
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
such that
v_{1}<w_{1}<v_{2}<w_{2}
, then
V=W
. The set of all non-crossing partitions on
[n]
will be denoted
{\mathrm{NC}}(n)
. A non-crossing partition
\pi
is said to be a
pair non-crossing partition
if every block of
\pi
contains precisely two elements. The set of all pair non-crossing partitions on
[n]
is denoted
{\mathrm{NC}}_{2}(n)
. Note
n
must be even for
{\mathrm{NC}}_{2}(n)
to be non-empty.

For the purposes of this paper, a
non-commutative probability space
is a pair
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital C
∗
-algebra and
\varphi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a unital positive linear map. We do not require
\varphi
to be tracial as it can be observed the state in the following definition of bi-free independence need not be tracial.

Definition 2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space. Pairs of unital C
∗
-subalgebras
\{({\mathcal{A}}_{\ell,k},{\mathcal{A}}_{r,k})\}_{k\in K}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
are said to be
bi-freely independent with respect to
\varphi
if there exist Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, unit vectors
\xi_{k}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, and unital
*
-homomorphisms
\alpha_{k}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell,k}\to\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
and
\beta_{k}:{\mathcal{A}}_{r,k}\to\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
such that if
{\mathcal{H}}=\ast_{k\in K}({\mathcal{H}}_{k},\xi_{k})
is the reduced free product space,
\lambda_{k},\rho_{k}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{k})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
are the left and right regular representations respectively, and
\varphi_{0}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
is the vacuum vector state, the joint distribution of
\{({\mathcal{A}}_{\ell,k},{\mathcal{A}}_{r,k})\}_{k\in K}
with respect to
\varphi
is equal to the joint distribution of
\{(\lambda_{k}(\alpha_{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{\ell,k})),\rho_{k}(\beta_{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{r,k})))\}_{k\in K}
with respect to
\varphi_{0}
.

Although the above definition is very general, this paper will only require a certain class of bi-free independent pairs. It is elementary from the definitions of free and bi-free independence that unital
*
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
are free with respect to
\varphi
if and only if
\{({\mathcal{A}}_{k},{\mathbb{C}})\}^{n}_{k=1}
are bi-free with respect to
\varphi
if and only if
\{({\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{A}}_{k})\}^{n}_{k=1}
are bi-free with respect to
\varphi
. Thus the notion of free independence embeds into bi-free independence. Moreover, it is not difficult to see classical independence (also known as tensor independence) embeds into the bi-free setting.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space. To simplify the need for representations, assume
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
for some Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a unit vector so that
\varphi
is the vector state corresponding to
\xi
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}={\mathcal{H}}
,
\xi_{k}=\xi
for
k=1,2
, and
{\mathcal{K}}=({\mathcal{H}}_{1},\xi_{1})\ast({\mathcal{H}}_{2},\xi_{2})
with vacuum vector
\xi_{0}
. By the definition of the reduced free product and the left and right regular representations, it is easy to verify that
\lambda_{1}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})
and
\rho_{2}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})
commute with each other,
\lambda_{1}({\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1}))\rho_{2}({\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2}))\xi_{0}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
, and

\langle\lambda_{1}(T)\rho_{2}(S)\xi_{0},\xi_{0}\rangle=\varphi(T)\varphi(S)

for all
T,S\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Consequently,
\lambda_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}:{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})
is a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
such that the vector state defined by the vacuum vector
\xi_{0}
is
\varphi\otimes\varphi
. Consequently
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})
and
({\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})
are bi-free pairs with respect to
\varphi\otimes\varphi
.

By combining the above and the associativity of bi-free independence, we obtain the following which is the only collection bi-free operators required in this paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
be free with respect to
\varphi
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
be free with respect to
\varphi
. Then

\{(a_{k}\otimes 1,1\otimes 1)\}^{n}_{k=1}\cup\{(1\otimes 1,1\otimes b_{k})\}^{n}_{k=1}

are bi-free in
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}},\varphi\otimes\varphi)
.

To summarize the combinatorics of bi-free independence, a map

To obtain the bi-free cumulants, we require a Möbius inversion of the moments. The
bi-non-crossing Möbius function
is the function

\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}:\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\bigcup_{\chi:[n]\to\{\ell,r\}}{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)\times{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)\to{\mathbb{Z}}

defined such that
\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}(\pi,\sigma)=0
unless
\pi
is a refinement of
\sigma
, and otherwise defined recursively via the formulae

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)\\
\pi\leq\tau\leq\sigma\end{subarray}}\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}(\tau,\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)\\
\pi\leq\tau\leq\sigma\end{subarray}}\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}(\pi,\tau)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }\pi=\sigma\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Due to the similarity of the lattice structures, the bi-non-crossing Möbius function is related to the non-crossing Möbius function
\mu_{{\mathrm{NC}}}
via the formula

\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}(\pi,\sigma)=\mu_{{\mathrm{NC}}}(s^{-1}_{\chi}\cdot\pi,s^{-1}_{\chi}\cdot\sigma).

To define the bi-free cumulants, fix a non-commutative probability space
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
and a map
\chi:[n]\to\{\ell,r\}

\pi\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)

\varphi_{\pi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})=\prod_{V\in\pi}\varphi\left(\prod_{q\in V}Z_{q}\right)

where the product (like all products in this paper) is performed in increasing order.

Definition 2.5
.

The
bi-free cumulant of

with respect to

\displaystyle\kappa_{\chi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)}\varphi_{\pi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})\mu_{{\mathrm{BNC}}}(\pi,1_{n}),

(1)

where
1_{n}=\{[n]\}
denotes the full partition.

If for
\pi\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)
we define

\kappa_{\pi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})=\prod_{V\in\pi}\kappa_{\chi|_{V}}\left((Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})|_{V}\right)

where
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})|_{V}
denotes restricting the

\displaystyle\varphi(Z_{1}\cdots Z_{n})=\varphi_{1_{n}}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in{\mathrm{BNC}}(\chi)}\kappa_{\pi}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}).

(2)

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\infty}}(\mu)
denote the collection of all random variables that have moments of all order with respect to a probability measure
\mu
. Consider the algebra

{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\infty}}(\mu)\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{{\mathrm{op}}}

where
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{{\mathrm{op}}}
denotes

with the opposite multiplication. Further define a linear “expectation”
E:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

E(f\otimes T\otimes S)=\mathbb{E}(f){\mathrm{tr}}(TS)

where
{\mathrm{tr}}
denotes the normalized trace on

. Note
E
is not positive.

Let
L,R:\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\infty}}(\mu)\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathcal{A}}
be the linear maps such that

L(f\otimes T)=f\otimes T\otimes I_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R(f\otimes T)=f\otimes I_{n}\otimes T.

For self-adjoint
\{X_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\infty}}(\mu)\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
(i.e. our usual notion of random matrices), it is elementary to verify that the joint distribution of
\{L(X_{j})\}^{d}_{j=1}
with respect to

is the joint distribution of
\{X_{j}\}^{d}_{j=1}
with respect to
{\mathrm{tr}}\circ\mathbb{E}
. Moreover, the joint distribution of
\{R(X_{j})\}^{d}_{j=1}
with respect to

is the joint distribution of
\{\overline{X_{j}}\}^{d}_{j=1}
with respect to
{\mathrm{tr}}\circ\mathbb{E}
as

\displaystyle E(R(X_{j_{1}})\cdots R(X_{j_{k}}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left({\mathrm{tr}}(X_{j_{k}}\cdots X_{j_{1}})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left({\mathrm{tr}}\left((X_{j_{k}}\cdots X_{j_{1}})^{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left({\mathrm{tr}}\left(X_{j_{1}}^{t}\cdots X_{j_{k}}^{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left({\mathrm{tr}}\left(\overline{X_{j_{1}}}\cdots\overline{X_{j_{k}}}\right)\right).

Note for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\infty}}(\mu)\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
that
L(X)R(Y)=R(Y)L(X)
.

Theorem 3.1

Let

\displaystyle=\varphi(a_{\theta(k_{0})})\varphi(b_{\theta(k_{0})})(\varphi\otimes\varphi)\left(Z_{\theta(1)}\cdots Z_{\theta(k_{0}-1)}Z_{\theta(k_{0}+1)}\cdots Z_{\theta(m)}\right).

Hence

has a block with exactly one element.

If

and thus

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-|\pi|+1)}{n^{\frac{m}{2}}}=0.

Combining the above, we obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\varphi\otimes\varphi)(S^{m}_{n})=\frac{1}{\delta^{m}}\sum_{\pi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(m)}(\varphi\otimes\varphi)(\pi)

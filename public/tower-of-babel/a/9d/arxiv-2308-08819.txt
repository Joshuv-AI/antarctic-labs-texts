Furstenberg sets estimate in the plane
Kevin Ren, Hong Wang

Abstract
. We fully resolve the Furstenberg set conjecture in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , that a ( s , t ) (s,t) -Furstenberg set has Hausdorff dimension ≥ min ⁡ ( s + t , 3 ​ s + t 2 , s + 1 ) \geq\min(s+t,\frac{3s+t}{2},s+1) . As a result, we obtain an analogue of Elekes’ bound for the discretized sum-product problem and resolve an orthogonal projection question of Oberlin.



1.
Introduction

For
s\in(0,1]
and
t\in(0,2]
, a
(s,t)
-Furstenberg set is a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the following property: there exists a family
\mathcal{L}
of lines with
\dim_{H}\mathcal{L}\geq t
such that
\dim_{H}(E\cap\ell)\geq s
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
. The Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{H}\mathcal{L}
is defined as the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{L}
in the space
\mathcal{A}(2,1)
of all affine lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
26
, Section 3.16]
). The Furstenberg set question asks: what is the minimum possible dimension of
\dim_{H}(E)
?

Motivated by his work on
\times 2,\times 3
-invariant sets
[
15
]
, Furstenberg considered a set
E
with the following property: there exists a family of lines
\mathcal{L}
, one in every direction, such that
\dim_{H}(E\cap\ell)\geq s
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
. He conjectured (in unpublished work, but recalled by Wolff in
[
43
, Remark 1.5]
) that such a set
E
(which is a special
(s,1)
-Furstenberg set) has Hausdorff dimension
\geq\frac{3s+1}{2}
. Furstenberg’s conjecture was later generalized to
(s,t)
-Furstenberg sets: any
(s,t)
-Furstenberg set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
has Hausdorff dimension

(1)

\dim_{H}E\geq\min\{s+t,\frac{3s+t}{2},s+1\}.

As observed by Wolff
[
43
]
, the conjectured bound
\frac{3s+t}{2}
is optimal for the continuum version of a sharp example for Szemerédi-Trotter theorem
[
40
]
.
The bound
s+t
for
0\leq t\leq s\leq 1
was proved by Lutz-Stull
[
23
]
and Héra-Shmerkin-Yavicoli
[
19
]
, and the bound
s+1
for
s+t\geq 2
was proved recently by Fu and the first author
[
14
]
. Wolff
[
43
]
gave a sharp construction for
\frac{3s+t}{2}
in the case
t=1
based on a grid example (which easily generalizes to other values of
t
), the sharp construction for
s+t
can be taken as the product of a
t
-dim Cantor set and an
s
-dim Cantor set, and Fu and the first author
[
14
]
gave a sharp construction for
s+1
(the product of an interval and a
s
-dim Cantor set).

The main case
s<t<2-s
remains open. Elementary bounds due to Wolff
[
43
]
in the case
t=1
, Molter-Rela
[
28
]
, Héra
[
18
]
, and Lutz-Stull
[
23
]
give

\dim_{H}E\geq\max(t/2+s,s+\min(s,t)).

Meanwhile, an
\varepsilon
-improvement
\dim_{H}(E)\geq 2s+\varepsilon(s,t)
was proven in the special case
(s,t)=(1/2,1)
by Katz-Tao
[
21
]
and Bourgain
[
1
]
, then for general
(s,2s)
by Héra-Shmerkin-Yavicoli
[
19
]
. Explicit improvement for
(s,2s)
-Furstenberg sets was obtained by Benedetto-Zahl
[
7
]
.

A breakthrough by Orponen-Shmerkin
[
30
]
proves an
\varepsilon
-improvement over
2s
for general
(s,t)
with
s<t
(see Section
1.3
for how this result leads to recent progress on several problems). Later, work of Shmerkin and the second author
[
38
]
provides an
\varepsilon
-improvement over the bound
s+\frac{t}{2}
using ideas in
[
30
]
. Very recently, Orponen and Shmerkin
[
31
]
made another breakthrough and proved
\dim_{H}E\geq\max(t/2+s,s+\min(s,t))+C(t,s)
for some good explicit constant
C(t,s)
depending only on
s
and
t
which is not far from the conjecture. In this paper, we prove the Furstenberg conjecture on the Hausdorff dimension of
(s,t)
-Furstenberg sets.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
s\in(0,1]
and
t\in(0,2]
. Any
(s,t)
-Furstenberg set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
has Hausdorff dimension

\dim_{H}F\geq\min\{s+t,\frac{3s+t}{2},s+1\}.

The
(s,t)
-Furstenberg problem is known to be connected to the discretized sum-product problem and projection problem, as shown in Section
1.1
, Section
1.2
, and
[
31
, Section 6]
.

Recently, Wang and Wu
[
42
]
proved a “two-ends” generalization of our Theorem
1.1
and used it with Bourgain-Demeter decoupling theory to make progress on Stein’s Fourier restriction conjecture. Their proof uses Theorem
1.1
as a black box and combines it with multi-scale analysis and induction on scales; we refer the reader to that paper and
[
6
]
for more details.

1.1.
Exceptional set estimate for orthogonal projections

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an arbitrary Borel set and
0\leq u\leq\min\{\dim_{H}K,1\}
. The exceptional set problem of orthogonal projections asks for the optimal upper bound on

\dim_{H}\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim_{H}\pi_{\theta}(K)<u\}.

In 1968,
Kaufman
[
22
]
proved that

\dim_{H}\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim_{H}\pi_{\theta}(K)<u\}\leq u,

sharpening a result of Marstrand
[
24
]
.
In 2000, Peres-Schlag
[
33
]
showed that when
\dim_{H}K>1
, one can improve the estimate to

\dim_{H}\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim_{H}\pi_{\theta}(K)<u\}\leq\max\{u+1-\dim_{H}K,0\}.

Kaufman’s bound is sharp when
u=\dim_{H}K
and the Peres-Schlag bound is sharp when
u=1
. In fact, the case
u=1
was obtained previously by Falconer
[
11
]
. For general cases, Oberlin
[
29
]
conjectured that the right-hand side should be
\max\{2u-\dim_{H}K,0\}.

Further improvements on the exceptional set estimate originate from Bourgain’s projection theorem
[
2
]
, which says

(2)

\dim_{H}\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim_{H}\pi_{\theta}(K)\leq\dim_{H}K/2\}=0.

Bourgain’s projection theorem concerns the deep nature of real numbers and has been used in Bourgain-Furman-Lindenstrauss-Mozes
[
3
]
on random walk on torus and in Katz-Zahl
[
20
]
on the Hausdorff dimension of Kakeya sets in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Recent improvements
[
30
,
31
]
on the exceptional set estimate are associated to the improvements on the Furstenberg sets problem.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
(in fact, its discretized version Theorem
4.1
), we prove Oberlin’s conjecture
[
29
, (1.8)]
on the exceptional sets of the orthogonal projections, whose AD-regular version was established in
[
31
, Theorem 1.13]
. This deduction is analogous to the procedure used to establish
[
31
, Theorem 1.13]
(see
[
31
, Section 6.1]
), so we omit the details.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an arbitrary Borel set. Then for all
0\leq u\leq\min\{\dim_{H}(K),1\}
, we have

\dim_{H}\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim_{H}\pi_{\theta}(K)<u\}\leq\max\{2u-\dim_{H}K,0\}.

1.2.
Discretized sum-product

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
A,B\subset\mathbb{F}
, the sum set
A+B
is defined as

A+B:=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}

and the product set is defined as

A\cdot B:=\{a\cdot b:a\in A,b\in B\}.

The sum-product problem asks whether the size of
A+A
or
A\cdot A
can be substantially larger than the size of
A
. When
A
is a finite set, we measure the size of
A
by its cardinality and it was conjectured by Erdös and Szemerédi that when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\max\{|A+A|,|A\cdot A|\}\geq C_{\epsilon}|A|^{2-\epsilon}.

See
[
39
,
27
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
for some recent results on the Erdös-Szemerédi sum-product conjecture.

When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, a “fractal” analogue of the discrete sum-product problem concerns the
(\delta,s,C)
-sets, which are discretizations of sets of Hausdorff dimension
\geq s
in
\mathbb{R}
. For a bounded set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
|A|_{\delta}
denote the dyadic
\delta
-covering number of
A
. This concept will be precisely defined in Definition
2.2
, but for now one can regard
|A|_{\delta}
as being the same (up to a constant factor) as the least number of
\delta
-balls needed to cover
A
.

Definition 1.3

(
(\delta,s,C)
-set)
.

For
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
,
s\in[0,d]
and
C>0
, a non-empty bounded set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if

|A\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Cr^{s}|A|_{\delta},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r\in[\delta,1].

If
A
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set, then
|A|_{\delta}\geq C^{-1}\delta^{-s}
and if
A^{\prime}\subset A
satisfies
|A^{\prime}|_{\delta}\geq C^{\prime-1}|A|_{\delta}
, then
A^{\prime}
is a
(\delta,s,CC^{\prime})
-set.

Definition
1.3
is to be contrasted with the notion of a
(\delta,s,C)
-Katz-Tao set, which imposes the bound

|A\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{s},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r\in[\delta,1].

Definition 1.4

(AD-regular set)
.

A
(\delta,s,C)
-set
A
is called Alfors-David regular at scale
\delta
if
C>0
is a constant independent of
\delta
and

C^{-1}r^{s}|A|_{\delta}\leq|A\cap B(x,r)|_{\delta}\leq Cr^{s}|A|_{\delta},\quad\forall x\in A,r\in[\delta,1].

We say that
A
is
(s,\epsilon)
-almost AD-regular if
C=\delta^{-\epsilon}
. Note that in the above condition, we have
x\in A
, not
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
!

Katz and Tao
[
21
]
made the following discretized ring conjecture and showed that it is equivalent to an
\epsilon
-improvement of the
(1/2,1)
-Furstenberg set problem and Falconer distance set problem for sets of Hausdorff dimension
1
on the plane. When
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, if
A\subset[1,2]
is a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\varepsilon})
-set with
s=1/2
, then

\max\{|A+A|_{\delta},|A\cdot A|_{\delta}\}\geq C_{s,\varepsilon}\delta^{-s-\varepsilon}.

This conjecture was proved in 2003 by Bourgain
[
1
]
. (In related work, Edgar and Miller
[
9
]
proved the qualitative statement that there do not exist
s
-dimensional Borel subrings of
\mathbb{R}
for any
s\in(0,1)
). Later Bourgain generalized the conjecture to the aforementioned projection estimate (
2
). More recently, Guth-Katz-Zahl
[
16
]
used an elementary method inspired by Gaarev’s approach to the sum-product problem in finite fields to prove a quantitative result, replacing
\epsilon
by an explicit constant depending on
s
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
, we prove a new estimate for the discretized sum-product problem, which complements the analogous results of Elekes
[
10
]
for
\mathbb{R}
and Mohammadi-Stevens
[
27
]
for finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This deduction is analogous to the procedure used to establish
[
31
, Theorem 1.26]
(see
[
31
, Section 5.2]
), so we omit the details.

Theorem 1.5
.

Given
s\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
and
\eta>0
, there is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(s,\eta)>0
such that the following holds for small enough
\delta>0
. Let
A\subset[1,2]
be a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\varepsilon})
-set. Then

\max\{|A+A|_{\delta},|A\cdot A|_{\delta}\}\geq\delta^{-(5/4)s+\eta}.

Theorem
1.5
improves the very recent result of Máthé-O’Regan
[
25
, Theorem B]
and Orponen-Shmerkin
[
31
, Theorem 1.26]
where
5/4
was replaced by
7/6
and extends
[
31
, Theorem 1.26]
for almost AD-regular sets to general
(\delta,s,C)
-sets.

Remark 1.6
.

If
s\geq\frac{2}{3}
, then
[
14
, Corollary 1.7]
gives the lower bound
\delta^{-\frac{s+1}{2}+\eta}
. This bound is sharp, see
[
25
, Remark 1.13]
.

While the sum-product theorem can be viewed as a special case of the Furstenberg sets problem,
the asymmetric sum product, i.e.
A+B\cdot C
for different sets
A,B,C\subset[0,1]
, plays an important role in Orponen and Shmerkin’s recent work
[
31
]
on the Furstenberg set conjecture.

1.4.
Structure of the paper

In Section 2, we introduce preliminaries and notations about dyadic tubes, dimensions of sets, and the high-low method. In Section 3, we state and prove some pigeonholing and multiscale lemmas that will be crucial later in the proof. In Section 4, we re-prove the special case
s+t=2
of the Furstenberg set problem, as well as introduce the key Lemma
4.8
that will allow us to solve the Furstenberg set problem for a class of semi-well spaced sets. In Section 5, we prove Lemma
4.8
using a high-low method. Finally, in Section 6, we prove a decomposition lemma for Lipschitz functions that reduces the full Furstenberg set conjecture to the AD-regular and semi-well spaced cases.

2.
preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and lemmas from
[
31
]
and the high-low method.

The letter
C
will usually denote a constant, while the letter
K
will usually denote a parameter depending on
\delta
(e.g.
K=C\delta^{-\varepsilon}
).

We say
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for some constant
C
, and define
A\gtrsim B
,
A\sim B
similarly. We write
A\lesssim_{n}B
to emphasize the implicit constant may depend on
n
.

For a finite set
A
,
|A|
denotes cardinality of
A
. For a measurable and infinite set
A
(usually a ball or a rectangle),
|A|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
A
.

For any compact set
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let
A^{(r)}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:d(x,A)\leq r\}
denote the
r
-neighborhood of
A
.

Definition 2.1

(Dyadic cubes)
.

If
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta=2^{-n}
, denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{[a\delta,(a+1)\delta)\times[b\delta,(b+1)\delta):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}
the family of dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of side-length
\delta
. If
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(\mathcal{P}):=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):Q\cap\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset\}.

We abbreviate
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}:=\mathcal{D}_{\delta}([0,1)^{d})
for
n\geq 0
. If
\delta<\Delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
and
p\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
, let
p^{\Delta}
denote the dyadic cube
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta}
containing
p
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a bounded set
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta=2^{-n}
,
n\geq 0
, define the dyadic
\delta
-covering number

|P|_{\delta}:=|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(P)|.

As remarked before,
|P|_{\delta}
is comparable (up to a universal constant factor) to the least number of
\delta
-balls needed to cover
P
. (This latter definition is the more common definition of covering number.) If
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
is a set of dyadic cubes, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if the union of
\delta
-cubes in
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set in the sense of Definition
1.3
and we use
|\mathcal{P}|
to denote the number of dyadic cubes in
\mathcal{P}
and
|\mathcal{P}|_{\Delta}:=|\mathcal{D}_{\Delta}(P)|
(hence
|\mathcal{P}|_{\delta}=|\mathcal{P}|
).

Definition 2.3

(Dyadic
\delta
-tubes)
.

Let
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
. A
dyadic
\delta
-tube
is a set of the form
T=\cup_{x\in p}\mathbf{D}(x)
, where
p\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}([-1,1]\times\mathbb{R}),
and
\mathbf{D}
is the
point-line duality map

\mathbf{D}(a,b):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y=ax+b\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

sending the point
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a corresponding line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Abusing the notation, we abbreviate
\mathbf{D}(p):=\cup_{x\in p}\mathbf{D}(x).
The collection of all dyadic
\delta
-tubes is denoted

\mathcal{T}^{\delta}:=\{\mathbf{D}(p):p\in\mathcal{D}_{\delta}([-1,1]\times\mathbb{R})\}.

A finite collection of dyadic
\delta
-tubes
\{\mathbf{D}(p)\}_{p\in\mathcal{P}}
is called a
(\delta,s,C)
-set if
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,s,C)
-set.

If
\delta<\Delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
and
T\in\mathcal{T}^{\delta}
, let
T^{\Delta}
denote the dyadic tube
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Delta}
containing
T
.

For a set of dyadic
\delta
-tubes
\mathcal{T}
and
\delta<\Delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
, define the dyadic
\Delta
-covering number
|\mathcal{T}|_{\Delta}:=|\mathcal{D}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{T})|
where
\mathcal{D}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{T}):=\{\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Delta}:\exists T\in\mathcal{T},\mathbf{T}=T^{\Delta}\}
. When
\delta=\Delta
, sometimes we omit the subscript
\delta
from
|T|_{\delta}
.

Definition 2.4

(Slope set)
.

The slope of a line
\ell=\mathbf{D}(a,b)
is defined to be the number
a\in\mathbb{R}
, write
\dir(\ell):=a.

If
T=\mathbf{D}(p)
is a dyadic
\delta
-tube, we define the slope
\dir(T)=\cup_{x\in p}\dir(\mathbf{D}(x))
. Thus
\dir{T}
is an interval of length
\sim\delta
. If
\mathcal{T}
is a collection of dyadic
\delta
-tubes, we write
\dir(\mathcal{T}):=\{\dir(T):T\in\mathcal{T}\}
. If
\mathcal{T}(p)
is a collection of dyadic
\delta
-tubes through a fixed point
p
, we identify
\mathcal{T}(p)
with
\dir(\mathcal{T}(p))
.

If
0<\delta<\Delta\leq 1
,
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\delta}
and
\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Delta}
, write
\mathcal{T}\cap\mathbf{T}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}:T\subset\mathbf{T}\}
.

2.1.
Thickening and high-low method

Definition 2.5

(Incidences)
.

Let
\delta\leq\Delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
. For a set of distinct weighted dyadic
\delta
-cubes
\mathcal{P}
with weight function
w:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
and a set of (not necessarily distinct) dyadic
\Delta
-tubes
\mathcal{T}
, define the incidence count

I_{w}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}):=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T},p^{\Delta}\subset 6T}w(p).

For each dyadic
\Delta
-tube,
6T:=T^{(6\Delta)}
, the
6\Delta
-neighborhood of
T
.
We usually assume
w\equiv 1
or
w
is explicitly defined beforehand, in which case we drop it from the notation.

The following proposition is a slightly
weaker version of
[
4
, Proposition 5.2.1]
.
We include a proof inspired by
[
5
]
here for completeness.

Proposition 2.6
.

Fix
\delta\in 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
, let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of distinct weighted dyadic
\delta
-cubes contained in
[0,1)^{2}
with weight function
w:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{T}
be a set of distinct dyadic
\delta
-tubes. If
\delta^{-\varepsilon/100}\leq S\leq\delta^{-1}
, then

I_{w}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T})\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\left(S\delta^{-1}|\mathcal{T}|\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}w(p)^{2}\right)^{1/2}+S^{-1+\varepsilon}I_{w}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}^{S\delta}),

where
\mathcal{T}^{S\delta}:=\{T^{S\delta}:T\in\mathcal{T}\}
is counted with multiplicity.

Proof.

For each
T\in\mathcal{T}
, let
\phi_{T}
be the affine transformation that maps
T
to
[-1/2,1/2]^{2}
. Let
\psi
be a smooth bump function
=1
on
[-3,3]^{2}
and supported on
[-4,4]^{2}
. Define
f_{T}=\psi\circ\phi_{T}
, then
f_{T}=1
on
6T
is supported on
8T
. Then

I_{w}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T})\lesssim\delta^{-2}\int fg.

\delta
-cubes and distinct
\delta

\int fg\lesssim|\int f^{h}g|\leq(\int|f^{h}|^{2})^{1/2}(\int|g|^{2})^{1/2}.

Since

. Through each

S
many
\theta

\displaystyle\int|f^{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int|\widehat{f^{h}}|^{2}\lesssim S\int\sum_{\theta}|\widehat{f_{\theta}^{h}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim S\sum_{\theta}\int\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\theta}}|f_{T}|^{2}\lesssim S\delta|\mathcal{T}|.

-tubes

|4(T^{2S\delta})\cap Q\cap\mathcal{P}^{2S\delta}|\geq kb
. Let
b^{\prime}:=kb
. Since
k\lesssim S
, we get
b^{\prime}\lesssim Sb
. Recall
a\geq 2\Delta^{-2\varepsilon}
, we have
a^{\prime}\gtrsim S^{-\varepsilon}(\log S)^{-1}a\geq 2
. Also
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\gtrsim S^{1-\varepsilon}(\log S)^{-1}ab>\max\{(S\delta)^{1-2\varepsilon}|\mathcal{P}|,2\}
, we can apply the inductive hypothesis to
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
to get

|\mathcal{T}_{a,b}|\lesssim S^{2}\log S\cdot|\tilde{\mathcal{T}}|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}S^{2}\log S\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{P}|^{2}}{(a^{\prime})^{3}(b^{\prime})^{2}}(S\delta)^{-5\varepsilon}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(\log S)^{4}S^{-2\varepsilon}\frac{|\mathcal{P}|^{2}b^{\prime}}{Sa^{3}b^{3}}\delta^{-5\varepsilon}\leq S^{-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{P}|^{2}}{a^{3}b^{2}}\delta^{-5\varepsilon}.

The
S^{-\varepsilon}=\Delta^{-\varepsilon^{2}/100}
cleans up the losses from
\lesssim_{\varepsilon}
if

. This closes the inductive step and finishes the proof of Lemma

∎

6.
General case using Orponen-Shmerkin’s regular Furstenberg result

In the previous sections, we dealt with the Furstenberg set problem for a semi-well spaced set

. Now we turn to the other extreme, when

is almost AD-regular. This case has been solved by Orponen-Shmerkin

Theorem 6.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0,s\in(0,1],t\in[s,2-s]
, there exists

and

with

(t,\eta)
-almost AD regular as in Definition

Remark 6.2
.

There are two differences between our proposition and

-regular set in
[

Proof.

We resolve the differences mentioned in the remark. First, we show that if

is
(t,\eta)
-almost AD-regular set, then it is also a
(\delta,t,\delta^{-3\eta})
-regular set in the sense of
[

, Definition 4.2]
. Indeed, the upper bound in Definition

shows that
\mathcal{P}
is a
(\delta,t,4\delta^{-\eta})
-set. (More precisely, if
\mathcal{P}\cap B(x,r)\neq\emptyset

, then let

; then
\mathcal{P}\cap B(x,r)\subset\mathcal{P}\cap B(y,2r)

\delta\leq r\leq R\leq 1
and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{R}(\mathcal{P})
, we have

4\delta^{-\eta}\cdot R^{t}\cdot|\mathcal{P}|_{\delta}\geq|\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbf{p}|_{\delta}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbf{p})}|Q\cap\mathcal{P}|_{\delta}\gtrsim|\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbf{p}|_{r}\cdot\delta^{\eta}r^{t}|\mathcal{P}|_{\delta}.

The last inequality uses the lower bound in Definition

and the fact that for any

|\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbf{p}|_{r}\lesssim\delta^{-2\eta}\cdot\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{t}

The next difference is that the
\eta
in
[

, Theorem 5.7]
depends a priori on
\varepsilon,s,t

denote this parameter, and reserve

for the desired parameter which is independent of

.

To remove the dependence on

, we use a compactness argument. For fixed

, the set
S=[s,2-s]\subset\mathbb{R}
is compact (note that the end points

are almost AD-regular cases of Lemma

and Proposition

). Now let
\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\tilde{\eta}(\varepsilon/2,s,t),\varepsilon\}>0
and define

for

. Then

, so

admits a finite subcover

where

. We claim that this choice of

works for any

. Indeed, given

such that
|t-t^{\prime}|\leq\eta_{t^{\prime}}

\eta+|t-t^{\prime}|\leq 2\eta_{t^{\prime}}\leq\tilde{\eta}(\varepsilon/2,s,t)
, we see that a
(\delta,t,\delta^{-\eta})
-set

is also a
(\delta,t^{\prime},\delta^{-\tilde{\eta}(\varepsilon/2,s,t^{\prime})})
-set. Hence, by
[

and

∎

Thus, we know that Theorem

Definition 6.3

(Branching function)
.

Let

, and let

be a
\{\Delta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
-uniform set, with

We define the

, and

f(j):=\frac{\log|\mathcal{P}|_{2^{-jT}}}{T}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\log N_{i},\quad i\in\{1,\dots n\}.

We now try to convert the notions of
(\delta,s,C)
-set, AD-regular set, and semi-well spaced set into the language of branching functions. Let
s_{f}(a,b)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}
denote the slope of a line segment between
(a,b)
and
(f(a),f(b))
. We say that a function

(\sigma,\varepsilon)
-superlinear on

is
(\sigma,\varepsilon)
-superlinear, if

f(x)\geq f(a)+\sigma(x-a)-\varepsilon(b-a),\qquad x\in[a,b].

If
\sigma=s_{f}(a,b)

(f,a,b)
is
\varepsilon
-superlinear.
Since
f(a)+s_{f}(a,b)(x-a)=f(b)-s_{f}(a,b)(b-x)

(f,a,b)
being
\varepsilon
-superlinear is

f(x)\geq f(b)-s_{f}(a,b)(b-x)-\varepsilon(b-a),\qquad x\in[a,b].

We will use both criteria in later proofs. Finally,
(f,a,b)
is
\varepsilon
-linear if

|f(x)-f(a)-s_{f}(a,b)(x-a)|\leq\varepsilon(b-a),\qquad x\in[a,b].

If
\mathcal{P}
is a

-superlinear. Likewise, if

-linear. The reader is encouraged to ponder what happens if

Proof.

for any
r\in[\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}},1]
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(\mathcal{P}_{j})
.

Proof.

For any
0\leq k\leq d_{j}-c_{j}
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta^{k}}(\mathcal{P}_{j})
, we get that

(32)

|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap Q|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}=|\mathcal{P}\cap\phi_{\mathbf{p}}^{-1}(Q)|_{\Delta^{d_{j}}}=\frac{|\mathcal{P}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}}}}{|\mathcal{P}|_{\Delta^{c_{j}+k}}}=\Delta^{f(c_{j}+k)-f(d_{j})}.

In particular, when
k=0
, we get
|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}=\Delta^{f(c_{j})-f(d_{j})}
. Plugging back into (
32
) gives

(33)

|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap Q|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}=\Delta^{f(c_{j}+k)-f(d_{j})}=|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\cdot\Delta^{f(c_{j}+k)-f(c_{j})}.

We start by analyzing condition
(1)(a)
, which says that letting
t_{j}=s_{f}(c_{j},d_{j})
,

(34)

|f(x)-f(c_{j})-t_{j}(x-c_{j})|\leq\varepsilon(d_{j}-c_{j}),\qquad\forall x\in[c_{j},d_{j}].

We check that
\mathcal{P}_{j}
is a
(\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}},t_{j},\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon-3})
-AD regular set. Fix
r\in(\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}},1)
and
y\in\mathcal{P}_{j}
; we wish to show

\Delta^{\varepsilon(d_{j}-c_{j})}r^{t_{j}}|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq\Delta^{-3}|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap B(y,r)|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}r^{t_{j}}\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon-3}

For the upper bound, note that (
33
) and (
34
) give us
|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap Q|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\cdot\Delta^{kt_{j}}\cdot\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon}
for all
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta^{k}}([0,1]^{2})
(indeed, if
Q\notin\mathcal{D}_{\Delta^{k}}(\mathcal{P}_{j})
, then the LHS is zero). Since any ball of radius
r\in(\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}},1)
can be covered by
O(\Delta^{2})
many squares in
\mathcal{D}_{\Delta^{k}}([0,1]^{2})
, where
k\in[0,d_{j}-c_{j}]
is the largest integer with
r<\Delta^{k}
(so
\Delta^{k}<r\Delta^{-1}
), we get
|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap B(y,r)|\lesssim|\mathcal{P}_{j}|\Delta^{-kt_{j}}\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon}\leq|\mathcal{P}_{j}|r^{t_{j}}\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon-3}
when
\Delta
is sufficiently small to absorb the implicit constant in
\lesssim
.

The lower bound is similar: choose
k
to be the smallest integer with
r>2\Delta^{k}
(so
2\Delta^{k-1}>r
). Then by applying (
33
) to
Q=\mathcal{D}_{\Delta^{k}}(y)
and (
34
), we verify the desired condition

\Delta^{\varepsilon(d_{j}-c_{j})+3}r^{t_{j}}|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq\Delta^{\varepsilon(d_{j}-c_{j})}\Delta^{kt_{j}}|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap Q|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}\leq|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap B(y,r)|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}.

This completes the analysis of condition
(1)(a)
.

In a similar fashion, the condition in
(1)(b)
becomes
f(d_{j})-f(c_{j})=t_{j}(d_{j}-c_{j})
and

f(x)\geq\min(f(c_{j})+(2-s)(x-c_{j}),f(d_{j})-s(d_{j}-x))-\varepsilon(d_{j}-c_{j}),\qquad x\in[c_{j},d_{j}].

As before, from (
32
) with
k=0
, we have
|\mathcal{P}_{j}|_{\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}}}=\Delta^{f(c_{j})-f(d_{j})}=\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})t_{j}}
. Finally, for a given
r\in[\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}},1]
, use (
32
), (
33
), and pick the largest
k\in[0,d_{j}-c_{j}]
with
r<\Delta^{k}
to obtain
|\mathcal{P}_{j}\cap Q|\leq\Delta^{-(d_{j}-c_{j})\varepsilon-3}\cdot\max(r^{2-s}|\mathcal{P}_{j}|,(r/\Delta^{d_{j}-c_{j}})^{s})
for any
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(\mathcal{P}_{j})
.

∎

We finally complete the proof of the Furstenberg set conjecture for general sets.

Proposition 6.7
.

Fix
N\geq 2
and a sequence
\{\Delta_{j}\}_{j=0}^{n}\subset 2^{-\mathbb{N}}
with

0<\delta=\Delta_{N}<\Delta_{N-1}<\cdots<\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{0}=1.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{0})\subset\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\times\mathcal{T}^{\delta}
be a
(\delta,s,C,M)
-nice configuration. Then there exists a set
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{P}_{0}
such that:

(1)

|\mathcal{D}_{\Delta_{j}}(\mathcal{P})|\approx_{\delta}|\mathcal{D}_{\Delta_{j}}(\mathcal{P}_{0})|
and
|\mathcal{P}\cap\textbf{p}|\approx_{\delta}|\mathcal{P}_{0}\cap\textbf{p}|
,
1\leq j\leq N
,
\textbf{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta_{j}}(\mathcal{P})
.

(2)

For every
0\leq j\leq N-1
and
\textbf{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta_{j}}
, there exist numbers
C_{\textbf{p}}\approx_{\delta}C
and
M_{\textbf{p}}\geq 1
, and a family of tubes
\mathcal{T}_{\textbf{p}}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\Delta_{j+1}/\Delta_{j}}
with the property that
(\phi_{\textbf{p}}(\mathcal{P}\cap\textbf{p}),\mathcal{T}_{\textbf{p}})
is a
(\Delta_{j+1}/\Delta_{j},s,C_{\textbf{p}},M_{\textbf{p}})
-nice configuration.

Furthermore, the families
\mathcal{T}_{\textbf{p}}
can be chosen such that if
\textbf{p}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Delta_{j}}(\mathcal{P})
for
0\leq j\leq N-1
, then

\frac{|\mathcal{T}_{0}|}{M}\gtrapprox_{\delta}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{|\mathcal{T}_{\textbf{p}_{j}}|}{M_{\textbf{p}_{j}}}.

Here,
\gtrapprox_{\delta}
means
\gtrsim_{N}\log(1/\delta)^{C}
, and likewise for
\lessapprox_{\delta},\approx_{\delta}
.

.

6.4

\{[c_{j},d_{j}]\}_{j=1}^{n}

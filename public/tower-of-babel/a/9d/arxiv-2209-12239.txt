Abstract
. Recently, much attention has been given to various inequalities among partition functions. For example, Nicolas, and later DeSavlvo–Pak, proved that p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) is eventually log-concave, and Ji–Zang showed that the cranks are eventually unimodal. This has led to a flurry of recent activity generalizing such results in different directions. At the same time, Stanton recently made deep conjectures on the positivity of certain polynomials associated to ranks and cranks of partitions, with the ultimate goal of pointing the way to “deeper” structure refining ranks and cranks. These have been shown to be robust in recent works, which have identified further infinite families of such conjectures in the case of colored partitions. In this paper, we employ the Circle Method to prove unimodality for ranks. As a corollary, we prove Stanton’s original conjecture. This points to future study of the positive, integral coefficients Stanton conjectured to exist, hinting at new combinatorial structure yet to be uncovered.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

The study of the sizes and of various inequalities among partitions has a long history, going back to Hardy and Ramanujan’s original development of the Circle Method to prove their famous asymptotic for
p(n)
, the number of integer partitions of
n
. Recently, there has been a reinvigoration of this subject in several directions. Nicolas proved, and later DeSalvo-Pak independently reproved
[

is log-concave starting at

p(n)^{2}\geq p(n-1)p(n+1),\quad\text{ for }n\geq 26.

An infinite family of generalizations of log-concavity was conjectured by Chen–Jia–Wang
[
7
]
and proven by Griffin, Ono, Zagier, and the third author
[
12
]
. This was in direct analogy with a program of Jensen–Polá on the Riemann Hypothesis, and uncovered a general phenomenon which gave new evidence for it. Another striking multiplicative-additive inequality recently was proven by Bessenrodt–Ono
[
5

p(a)p(b)\geq p(a+b)\qquad\text{ for }a,b\geq 2\ a+b>8.

In this paper we prove further inequalities in the world of partitions, namely for rank counting functions. Although analytic in nature, the motivation behind these inequalities, based on ideas of Stanton, hints at combinatorial structure “refining” partition ranks. In conjunction with recent works extending Stanton’s conjectures, this suggests that the deeper combinatorial structure Stanton posited is waiting to be discovered. We highlight this in Question
1

To set up this picture, we recall the classical story of ranks and cranks.

Here, we are motivated by a recent search, spearheaded by Stanton, for combinatorial structure refining ranks and cranks.
While the rank and crank split partitions into classes of equal size, there is no known direct map between these classes. That is, while ranks and cranks provide a combinatorial explanation for Ramanujan’s congruences, the proofs of the rank and crank equidistribution properties are also done via

We require the following generating function

2.2.
Euler–Maclaurin summation

To produce further asymptotics in the Circle Method, we require the Euler–Maclaurin summation. In general, it gives an exact formula for infinite sums in terms of an associated integral.
To this end, let

be a differentiable function, such that
f
and its derivative

have rapid decay, and let

(2.2)

\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}f(j)=\int_{N}^{\infty}f(u)du+\frac{f(N)}{2}+\int_{N}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(u)B_{1}(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)du,

where
B_{1}(u)=u-\frac{1}{2}
is the first Bernoulli polynomial.

3.1.
The setup

For

(3.1)

\beta_{n}:=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6\left(n-\frac{1}{24}\right)}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{C}_{n}:=\left({n-\frac{1}{24}}\right)\beta_{n}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{24}}.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}

(3.2)

N(m,n)-N(m+1,n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)}{\left(e^{-Z};e^{-Z}\right)_{\infty}}e^{nZ}dy.

We split the integral into two parts: the
major arc integral

(3.3)

\mathfrak{M}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|{y}\right|\leq\beta_{n}}\frac{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)}{(e^{-Z};e^{-Z})_{\infty}}e^{nZ}dy,

and the

(3.4)

\mathfrak{m}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\beta_{n}\leq\left|{y}\right|\leq\pi}\frac{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)}{(e^{-Z};e^{-Z})_{\infty}}e^{nZ}dy.

We bound the minor arc contributions and approximate the major arc contributions, which give our main terms.

We begin by expanding

m\geq 0

and
r\geq 1
. Hence

\left|{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)}\right|\leq\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)r\right)\beta_{n}}+2e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)r\right)\beta_{n}}+e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{3}{2}\right)r\right)\beta_{n}}\right)\leq 4\sum_{r\geq 0}e^{-\frac{\beta_{n}r^{2}}{2}}.

It is not hard to see that we may bound

(3.5)

\left|{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{n}}}+4.

Using this, we now turn to bounding the minor arc integral
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}
, we have

\left|{\mathfrak{m}}\right|\leq 13e^{\frac{3\mathcal{C}_{n}}{2}}.

Proof.

By (
2.4
), for
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}
and
\beta_{n}\leq\left|{y}\right|\leq\pi
we have

\left|{\frac{1}{\left(e^{-Z};e^{-Z}\right)_{\infty}}}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{\beta_{n}}e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\beta_{n}}}.

Hence

|\mathfrak{m}|\leq 4\left(\sqrt{2\pi}+2\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\right)e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\beta_{n}}+n\beta_{n}}.

Now a direct calculation gives the claim.
∎

3.3.
Major arc bounds

Next we consider the major arc integral
\mathfrak{M}
. Again we assume
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}
. On the major arc, we have
|y|\leq\beta_{n}
, so we can use
Lemma

2.3
and obtain

\mathfrak{M}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|{y}\right|\leq\beta_{n}}\frac{H_{m}\left({e^{-Z}}\right)e^{nZ}}{(e^{-Z};e^{-Z})_{\infty}}dy=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|{y}\right|\leq\beta_{n}}\left({\sqrt{\frac{Z}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{Z}{24}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6Z}}+C}\right)H_{m}\left({e^{-Z}}\right)e^{nZ}dy,

for some
|C|\leq 1
. Let

(3.6)

\mathcal{M}^{[1]}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left|{y}\right|\leq\beta_{n}}\sqrt{\frac{Z}{2\pi}}e^{\left(n-\frac{1}{24}\right)Z+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6Z}}H_{m}\left({e^{-Z}}\right)dy,\quad\mathcal{E}^{[1]}:=\mathfrak{M}-\mathcal{M}^{[1]}.

Then it follows from (
3.5
) that for
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}
, we have

(3.7)

\left|{\mathcal{E}^{[1]}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|y|\leq\beta_{n}}\left|{H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)e^{nZ}}\right|dy\leq\frac{2\beta_{n}}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{n}}}+2\right)e^{n\beta_{n}}\leq e^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}.

To give an estimate to
\mathcal{M}^{[1]}
, we need a bound for
H_{m}(e^{-Z})
. Note that
|Z|\leq\sqrt{2}\beta_{n}
. For any
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we approximate
H_{m}(e^{-Z})
by a sum of
L-1
terms. Specifically, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\ell,m}(Z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2Z^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{r+1}r^{2\ell}e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)r\right)Z},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L,m}(Z)

\displaystyle:=H_{m}\left(e^{-Z}\right)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\mathcal{M}_{\ell,m}(Z).

Moreover, we set

(3.9)

C_{1}(L,x_{0}):=\frac{2^{L+1}e^{\frac{x_{0}}{2}}}{(2L-1)!}\left({\frac{\Gamma\left({L+\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2}+e^{-L}L^{L}\sqrt{x_{0}}}\right).

In terms of these quantities, we obtain the following estimate for the error term
\mathcal{E}_{L,m}(Z)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then we have, for
\beta_{n}\leq x_{0}
,

\left|{\mathcal{E}_{L,m}(Z)}\right|\leq C_{1}(L,x_{0})\beta_{n}^{L-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\beta_{n}}.

Proof.

Using Taylor’s Theorem, we obtain for
L\in\mathbb{N}

\left(e^{\frac{w}{2}}-e^{-\frac{w}{2}}\right)^{2}=w^{2\ell}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{2}{(2\ell)!}+\frac{1}{(2L-1)!}\int_{0}^{w}(w-u)^{2L-1}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{u}}\right)^{2L}\left(e^{-\frac{u}{2}}-e^{\frac{u}{2}}\right)^{2}du.

Letting
w=rZ
yields the bound

\left|{\left(e^{\frac{rZ}{2}}-e^{-\frac{rZ}{2}}\right)^{2}-2\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\frac{(rZ)^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{(2L-1)!}(r|Z|)^{2L}e^{r\beta_{n}}.

Note that

(3.10)

|\mathcal{E}_{L,m}(Z)|\leq\frac{2^{L+1}\beta_{n}^{2L}e^{-\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\beta_{n}}}{(2L-1)!}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)(r-1)\right)\beta_{n}+r\beta_{n}}.

Next we estimate the sum in (
3.10
). Since
m\geq 0
, we have
\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+(m+\frac{1}{2})(r-1)-r\geq\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(r-1)-r\geq r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}
for
r\geq 1
. We deduce that

(3.11)

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)(r-1)\right)\beta_{n}+r\beta_{n}}\leq e^{\frac{\beta_{n}}{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-r^{2}\beta_{n}}.

The Euler–Maclaurin summation formula (
2.2
) gives (using that
L>0
)

(3.12)

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-r^{2}\beta_{n}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2L}e^{-\beta_{n}u^{2}}du+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{u}}\left(u^{2L}e^{-\beta_{n}u^{2}}\right)B_{1}(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)du.

For the first integral in (
3.12
), we evaluate (see
[
11
, 3.326.2]
)

(3.13)

\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2L}e^{-\beta_{n}u^{2}}du=\frac{\Gamma\left({L+\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2}\beta_{n}^{-L-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\left|{B_{1}(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, the absolute value of the second integral in (
3.12
) is bounded by

(3.14)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|{\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\left(u^{2L}e^{-\beta_{n}u^{2}}\right)}\right|du=\left(\frac{L}{e\beta_{n}}\right)^{L},

using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. Plugging (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) into (
3.12
), we obtain

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-r^{2}\beta_{n}}\leq\frac{\Gamma\left(L+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2}\beta_{n}^{-L-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\frac{L}{e\beta_{n}}\right)^{L}.

Putting this into (
3.11
) then yields

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{2L}e^{-\left(\frac{3r^{2}}{2}+\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)(r-1)\right)\beta_{n}+r\beta_{n}}\leq e^{\frac{\beta_{n}}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(L+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2}\beta_{n}^{-L-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\frac{L}{e\beta_{n}}\right)^{L}\right).

Finally, inserting this into (
3.10
) yields

\left|{\mathcal{E}_{L,m}(Z)}\right|\leq\frac{2^{L+1}\beta_{n}^{2L}e^{-\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\beta_{n}+\frac{\beta_{n}}{2}}}{(2L-1)!}\left({\frac{\Gamma\left({L+\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{2}\beta_{n}^{-L-\frac{1}{2}}+\left({\frac{L}{e\beta_{n}}}\right)^{L}}\right).

This gives the claim.
∎

Thus, we may estimate

by

up to a known error bound.

Proposition 3.3

(

Before beginning the proof, we also require basic facts about difference operator. To this end for

\displaystyle\Delta^{0}(h(r)):=h(r),\quad\Delta(h(r)):=h(r+1)-h(r),\quad\Delta^{j+1}(h(r)):=\Delta^{j-1}(h(r+1))-\Delta^{j-1}(h(r)).

For any arithmetic function

Lemma 3.5

(
[

Let

, and

with

, and
\left|{y}\right|\leq x

(3.19)

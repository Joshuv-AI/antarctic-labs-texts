A characterization of the planes meeting a hyperbolic quadric of PG ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PG}(3,q) in a conic
Bikramaditya Sahu

Abstract
In this article, a combinatorial characterization of the family of planes of PG ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PG}(3,q) which meet a hyperbolic quadric in an irreducible conic, using their intersection properties with the points and lines of PG ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PG}(3,q) , is given. Keywords: Projective space, Hyperbolic quadric, Irreducible conic, Combinatorial characterization AMS 2010 subject classification: 05B25, 51E20

A characterization of the family of planes meeting a non-degenerate quadric in

[
3

[

Let

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We refer to
[
7
]
for the basics on finite generalized quadrangles. Let
s
and
t
be positive integers. A
generalized quadrangle
of order
(s,t)
is a point-line geometry
\mathcal{X}=(P,L)
with point set
P
and line set
L
satisfying the following three axioms:

(Q1)

Every line contains
s+1
points and every point is contained in
t+1
lines.

(Q2)

Two distinct lines have at most one point in common (equivalently, two distinct points are contained in at most one line).

(Q3)

For every point-line pair
(x,l)\in P\times L
with
x\notin l
, there exists a unique line
m\in L
containing
x
and intersecting
l
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(P,L)
be a generalized quadrangle of order
(s,t)
. Then,
|P|=(s+1)(st+1)
and
|L|=(t+1)(st+1)

[
7
, 1.2.1]
. If
P
is a subset of the point set of some projective space
PG(n,q)
,
L
is a set of lines of
PG(n,q)
and
P
is the union of all lines in
L
, then
\mathcal{X}=(P,L)
is called a
projective generalized quadrangle
. The points and the lines contained in a hyperbolic quadric in
\mbox{\rm PG}(3,q)
form a projective generalized quadrangle of order
(q,1)
. Conversely, any projective generalized quadrangle of order
(q,1)
with ambient space
\mbox{\rm PG}(3,q)
is a hyperbolic quadric in
\mbox{\rm PG}(3,q)
, this follows from
[
7
, 4.4.8]
.

The following two lemmas complete the proof of Theorem

.

The points of

.

We have

.

Each black line contains

We verify the axiom (Q3). Let

. By Lemma

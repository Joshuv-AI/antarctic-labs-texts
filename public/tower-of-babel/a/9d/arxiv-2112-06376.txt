Demazure product of the affine Weyl groups
Xuhua He, Sian Nie

Abstract
. The Demazure product gives a natural monoid structure on any Coxeter group. Such structure occurs naturally in many different areas in Lie Theory. This paper studies the Demazure product of an extended affine Weyl group W ~ \tilde{W} . The main discovery is a close connection between the Demazure product of an extended affine Weyl group and the quantum Bruhat graph of the finite Weyl group. As applications, we obtain explicit formulas on the generic Newton points and the Demazure products of elements in the lowest two-sided cell/shrunken Weyl chambers of W ~ \tilde{W} , and obtain an explicit formula on the Lusztig-Vogan map from the coweight lattice to the set of dominant coweights.

Introduction

An extended affine Weyl group
\tilde{W}
parameterizes the affine Schubert varieties in an affine flag variety. It plays an important role in the study of affine Hecke algebras and
p
-adic groups. The group
\tilde{W}
has two descriptions:

(1)

it is a semidirect product of the coweight lattice with a finite Weyl group;

(2)

it is a quasi-Coxeter group, i.e.,
\tilde{W}=W_{a}\rtimes\Omega
, where
W_{a}
is a Coxeter group and
\Omega
is a group of length-preserving automorphisms on
W_{a}
.

The multiplication on
\tilde{W}
can be described easily via Description (1). Description (1) is also very useful in the study of the center of
\tilde{W}
and its affine Hecke algebras.

Description (2) allows us to define the length function and Bruhat order on
\tilde{W}
. It is essential in the definition of affine Hecke algebras and is important in the study of the convolution product on the affine flag varieties.

Moreover, the quasi-Coxeter structure allows us to define the Demazure product
\ast
on
\tilde{W}
. The Demazure product gives a monoid structure on
\tilde{W}
, which is useful in the study of affine
0
-Hecke algebras and in Lusztig’s theory of total positivity.

It is a natural question to understand the Demazure product
\ast
via description (1) of
\tilde{W}
. As we will see later in the introduction, the answer to this question has several interesting applications in Lie theory.

Note that any element in
\tilde{W}
is of the form
t^{\lambda}x
for
\lambda\in X_{*}
and
x\in W_{0}
. If
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
are both dominant, then
t^{\lambda}\ast t^{\lambda^{\prime}}=t^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}
. However, for arbitrary coweights
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
,
t^{\lambda}\ast t^{\lambda^{\prime}}
does not equal
t^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}
. Some correction term is needed.

The main result of this paper is that the translation part of the correction term is given by the quantum Bruhat graph introduced by Brenti, Fomin, and Postnikov in 1999. See Theorem
1.1
for a precise statement.

First, let us explain why the quantum Bruhat graph and the Demazure product are related. Lam and Shimozono discovered in
[
LS10
]
that the Bruhat orders of the elements in
\tilde{W}
with very regular translation part could be described via the quantum Bruhat graph. Note that
w\ast w^{\prime}
, by definition, is the maximal element in the set
\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
. It indicates a possible connection between the Demazure product and the quantum Bruhat graph. New evidences come from the recent study of generic Newton points.

The generic Newton point of an element
w\in\tilde{W}
, roughly speaking, is the maximal Frobenius-twisted conjugacy class in the loop group that intersects the Bruhat cell corresponding to
w
. Milicévić in
[
Mi21
]
established a formula of the generic Newton point of elements in
\tilde{W}
with “superregular” translation part via the quantum Bruhat graph. Finally, the first author in a very recent work
[
He21+
]
gave a formula of the generic Newton point for any element in
w
in terms of the Demazure powers of
w
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we do not use the explicit formula of in
[
He21+
]
, but the idea of using Demazure product to calculate the generic Newton point. We then compare it with the formula in
[
Mi21
]
. It is worth pointing out that although the proof of Theorem
1.1
here is only half a page, it is not an easy result. Our proof uses as a black box the main result of
[
Mi21
]
, the proof of which involves a long and technical analysis on the paths in the quantum Bruhat graph encoding saturated chains in the strong Bruhat order on the affine Weyl group.

Note that the Demazure product of
w
with a translation element “in the same direction” as
w
is easy to calculate. Thus one may artificially add a suitable superregular translation to
w
(so that one may use
[
Mi21
]
), and then subtract this translation element in the end. This is how we bypass the “superregularity” assumption of
[
Mi21
]
in Theorem
1.1
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in §
2
.

Now we discuss some applications of our main result.

In
[
Mi21
]
, the formula for the generic Newton point of
w
is given under two assumptions: the group is split, and the translation part of the element
w
is superregular. Using the Demazure product, both assumptions can be much weakened. In proposition
3.1
, we obtain the formula for any quasi-split adjoint groups and for any
w
in the lowest two-sided cell (i.e. the shrunken Weyl chambers) of
\tilde{W}
. Note that the quasi-split adjoint assumption is not essential. In §
3.2
, we explain how the generic Newton points for arbitrary reductive groups can be deduced from quasi-split adjoint groups.

Note that our main result only determines the translation part of the Demazure product. However, for elements in the lowest two-sided cell (i.e. the shrunken Weyl chambers) of
\tilde{W}
, we have an explicit formula for the whole Demazure product. This is proposition
3.3
.

Finally, the Demazure product on
\tilde{W}
induces a map from the pairs of coweights to the dominant coweights (see §
3.4
). We give an explicit formula for this map in proposition
3.4
. As a consequence, we obtain in corollary
3.5
an explicit formula for the map
[
LV21+
]
from coweights to dominant coweights. This answers a question of Lusztig and Vogan.

1.
Preliminary

1.1.
Extended affine Weyl groups

Let
\mathfrak{R}=(X^{*},R,X_{*},R^{\vee},\Pi)
be a based reduced root datum, where
X^{*}
is the weight lattice,
X_{*}
is the coweight lattice,
R\subseteq X^{*}
is the set of roots,
R^{\vee}\subseteq X_{*}
is the set of coroots and
\Pi\subseteq R
is the set of simple roots. Let
V=X_{*}\otimes\mathbb{R}
. For any
\alpha\in R
, we have a reflection
s_{\alpha}
on
V
sending
v
to
v-\langle\alpha,v\rangle\alpha^{\vee}
, where
\langle~,~\rangle:X^{*}\times X_{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is the natural perfect pairing between
X^{*}
and
X_{*}
. The reflections
s_{\alpha}
generate the
finite Weyl group

W_{0}=W_{0}(\mathfrak{R})
of
\mathfrak{R}
. Let
\mathbb{S}_{0}=\{s_{\alpha};\alpha\in\Pi\}
be the set of simple reflections. Then
(W_{0},\mathbb{S}_{0})
is a Coxeter system. Set

\displaystyle W_{a}=W_{a}(\mathfrak{R}):=\mathbb{Z}R^{\vee}\rtimes W_{0}=\{t^{\lambda}w;\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}R^{\vee},w\in W_{0}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{W}=\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R}):=X_{*}\rtimes W_{0}=\{t^{\lambda}w;\lambda\in X_{*},w\in W_{0}\}.

We call
W_{a}
the
affine Weyl group
and
\tilde{W}
the
extended affine Weyl group
.

Let
R^{+}\subseteq R
be the set of positive roots determined by
\Pi
. The
base alcove
is the set
\mathfrak{a}=\{v\in V;0<\langle\alpha,v\rangle<1\text{ for every }\alpha\in R^{+}\}.
Let
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
be the set of reflections along the walls of the base alcove
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\mathbb{S}_{0}\subseteq\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
and
(W_{a},\tilde{}\mathbb{S})
is a Coxeter group.

Let
\Omega
be the isotropy group in
\tilde{W}
of the base alcove
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\tilde{W}=W_{a}\rtimes\Omega
. We extend the length function
\ell
and the Bruhat order
\leqslant
on the Coxeter group
W_{a}
to
\tilde{W}
in a natural way. By
[
IM65
]
, we have

\ell(t^{\lambda}w)=\sum_{\alpha>0,w^{-1}(\alpha)>0}|\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle|+\sum_{\alpha>0,w^{-1}(\alpha)<0}|\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle-1|.

For any
J\subseteq\tilde{S}
, we denote by
W_{J}\subseteq W_{a}
the parabolic subgroup generated by
J
. Let
{}^{J}\tilde{W}=\{w\in\tilde{W};w=\min(W_{J}w)\}
, where the minimum is taken with respect to the Bruhat order
\leqslant
. Set
\tilde{W}^{J}=({}^{J}\tilde{W})^{-1}
. If moreover,
W_{J}
is finite, then we denote by
w_{J}
the longest element in
W_{J}
. We simply write
w_{0}
for
w_{\mathbb{S}_{0}}
. We set

\displaystyle{}^{J}\tilde{W}_{\max}=\{w\in\tilde{W};w=\max(W_{J}w)\}=\{w_{J}w;w\in{}^{J}\tilde{W}\},

\displaystyle\tilde{W}^{J}_{\max}=({}^{J}\tilde{W}_{\max})^{-1}=\{w\in\tilde{W};w=\max(wW_{J})\}=\{ww_{J};w\in\tilde{W}^{J}\}.

If
J\subset\mathbb{S}_{0}
, then we write
{}^{J}W_{0}
for
{}^{J}\tilde{W}\cap W_{0}
and
W_{0}^{J}
for
\tilde{W}^{J}\cap W_{0}
.

Let
X_{*}^{+}=\{\lambda\in X_{*};\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\geqslant 0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Pi\}
be the set of dominant coweights. For any
\lambda\in X_{*}^{+}
, we set
I(\lambda)=\{s\in\mathbb{S}_{0};s(\lambda)=\lambda\}
. Each element of
\tilde{W}
can be written uniquely as
xt^{\mu}y
with
\mu\in X_{*}^{+}
and
x,y\in W_{0}
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. Here the condition
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
is equivalent to that
y\in{}^{I(\mu)}W_{0}
. In this case,
\ell(xt^{\mu}y)=\ell(x)+\ell(t^{\mu})-\ell(y)
.

1.2.
Demazure product

We follow
[
He07
, §1]
. For any
x,y\in\tilde{W}
, the subset
\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
(resp.
\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}
,
\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
) of
\tilde{W}
contains a unique maximal element (with respect to the Bruhat order
\leqslant
). Moreover, we have

\max\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}.

We denote this element by
x\ast y
and we call it the
Demazure product
of
x
and
y
. Moreover,
(\tilde{W},\ast)
is a monoid and the Demazure product can be determined by the following two rules

•

x\ast y=xy
if
x,y\in\tilde{W}
such that
\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)
;

•

s\ast w=w
if
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
,
w\in\tilde{W}
such that
sw<w
.

The Demazure product on
\tilde{W}
is encoded in the corresponding 0-Hecke algebra
H_{0}
. By definition,
H_{0}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra with a linear basis
\{t_{w};w\in W\}
, subject to the following relations

(1)

t_{w}t_{w^{\prime}}=t_{ww^{\prime}}
for
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
such that
\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
;

(2)

t_{s}^{2}=-t_{s}
for
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
.

Then for
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
we have
t_{w}t_{w^{\prime}}=(-1)^{\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})-\ell(w\ast w^{\prime})}t_{w\ast w^{\prime}}
.

The Demazure product also occurs naturally in the convolution product of Bruhat cells, which we will discuss in §
2.2
. It has also found applications in Lusztig’s theory of total positivity
[
Lu94
]
and
[
Lu19
]
, and more recently, in the study of generic Newton points
[
He21+
]
.

1.3.
Quantum Bruhat graphs

The quantum Bruhat graph was introduced by Brenti, Fomin, and Postnikov in
[
BFP99
]
. It is an extension of the graph formed by covering relations in the Bruhat order of a finite Weyl group. It is naturally related to the quantum cohomology rings of flag varieties, see
[
FGP97
]
. It has also found applications in the description of the Bruhat order of affine Weyl groups
[
LS10
]
and in the study of generic Newton points
[
Mi21
]
.

By definition, the quantum Bruhat graph
\Gamma
associated to the root datum
\mathfrak{R}
is a directed graph with

(1)

vertices indexed by elements of
W_{0}
;

(2)

upward arrows
w\rightharpoonup ws_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in R^{+}
if
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1
;

(3)

downward arrow
w\rightharpoondown ws_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in R^{+}
if
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
.

Here
\rho
is half sum of the positive roots in
R
. The weight of an upward arrow is defined to be zero, and the weight of a downward arrow
w\rightharpoondown w^{\prime}
is defined to be
\alpha^{\vee}
. The weight of a directed path is the sum of the weights of edges in the path. Following
[
Po05
, Lemma 1]
, for
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{0}
,
{\rm wt}(w,w^{\prime})
is defined to be the weight of any/some shortest directed path in
\Gamma
from
w
to
w^{\prime}
. Note that
{\rm wt}(w,w^{\prime})
is a nonnegative integral combination of simple coroots in
\mathfrak{R}
.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish an interesting connection between the Demazure product on
\tilde{W}
and the quantum Bruhat graph of
\mathfrak{R}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in X_{*}^{+}
and
x,y\in W_{0}
. If
t^{\mu_{1}}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
,
xt^{\mu_{2}}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
, then

(1)

\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x)\in X_{*}^{+}
;

(2)

(t^{\mu_{1}}y)\ast(xt^{\mu_{2}})\in W_{0}t^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x)}
.

2.
Proof of the Main theorem

Theorem
1.1
is a combinatorial statement, and it is possible to be proved in a purely combinatorial way. However, we will argue in a more geometric way, by relating the Demazure product to the generic Newton points and then using the work of Milicévić
[
Mi21
]
on the connection between the generic Newton points and the quantum Bruhat graphs.

2.1.
Iwahori-Weyl groups

First, we recall the Iwahori-Weyl groups and their relations with the extended affine Weyl groups in §
1.1
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean field and let
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. The Frobenius automorphism of
\breve{F}
over
F
is denoted by
\sigma
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
F
. Let
S
be a maximal
\breve{F}
-split torus defined over
F
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the apartment of
G_{\breve{F}}
corresponding to
S_{\breve{F}}
. Fix a
\sigma
-stable alcove

in

. Set
\breve{G}=\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
and let
\breve{I}\subseteq\breve{G}
be the Iwahori subgroup corresponding to

. Then
\breve{I}
is
\sigma
-stable. Let
T
be the centralizer of
S
in
\mathbf{G}
and
N
be the normalizer of
T
in
\mathbf{G}
. The Iwahori-Weyl group is defined by

\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})\cap\breve{I}.

The affine Weyl group is defined by

W_{a}(\mathbf{G})=N(\breve{F})\cap\breve{G}_{1}/T(\breve{F})\cap\breve{I},

where
\breve{G}_{1}\subseteq\breve{G}
is the subgroup generated by all Iwahori subgroups of
\breve{G}
.

Let
W_{0}(\mathbf{G})=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})
be the relative Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
. Then

\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})=X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}\rtimes W_{0}(\mathbf{G}),

where
\Gamma=\Gal(\overline{\breve{F}}/\breve{F})
is the absolute Galois group, and
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}
is the group of
\Gamma
-coinvariants of the coweight lattice
X_{*}(T)
of

.

By
[
PR08

, there is a reduced root system

associated to
\mathbf{G}
such that
W_{0}(\mathbf{G})=W_{0}(R)
and
W_{a}(\mathbf{G})=W_{a}(R)
. Moreover, any extended affine Weyl group can be realized as the Iwahori-Weyl group of some connected reductive group split over

. On the other hand,

for any connected reductive group

, in general, may not be an extended affine Weyl group in §

. The reason is that the group
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}
is not torsion-free in general. However, the torsion part

always lies in the center of

and
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})/X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma,\text{tor}}\cong\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
for some reduced root datum

whose underlying root system is

. See
[
HN18
, A1]
.

Under the identification
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})/X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma,\text{tor}}\cong\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
, the length functions and Bruhat orders on
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
and
\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
are compatible. We still denote the length function on
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
by
\ell
and the Bruhat order on
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
by
\leqslant
.

The Frobenius automorphism
\sigma
induces actions on
\breve{G}
and on
\tilde{W}
, which are still denoted by
\sigma
. Then
\sigma
preserves the length function
\ell
and the Bruhat order

.

2.2.
Convolution product

The Demazure product on
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
can be realized via the convolution product of Schubert varieties in the affine flag variety
\breve{G}/\breve{I}
. In this paper, we will present a variation of the convolution product.

For any
w\in\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
, we choose a representative
\dot{w}
in
N(\breve{F})
. We have the decomposition
\breve{G}=\bigsqcup_{w\in\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})}\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
. Note that
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
is an admissible subscheme of
\breve{G}
in the sense of Grothendieck (see
[
He16
, A2]
). The closure
\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}}
of
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
equals
\bigsqcup_{w^{\prime}\leqslant w}\breve{I}\dot{w}^{\prime}\breve{I}
. Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
and
w^{\prime\prime}=w\ast w^{\prime}
. It is well-known that

\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}}\,\cdot\,\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}^{\prime}\breve{I}}=\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}^{\prime\prime}\breve{I}}.

2.3.

For any

\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}}
. Hence
[b_{w}]
is the unique maximal
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\breve{G}
that is contained in
\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}}
.

Set
w_{3}=w_{1}\ast w_{2}
and
w_{4}=w_{2}\ast\sigma(w_{1})
. We have that

\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{3}\breve{I}}=\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{1}\breve{I}}\,\cdot\,\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{2}\breve{I}})=\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{2}\breve{I}}\,\cdot\,\sigma(\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{1}\breve{I}}))=\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}\dot{w}_{4}\breve{I}}.

Thus
[b_{w_{3}}]=[b_{w_{4}}]
. The statement is proved.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
\mathbf{G}
be the connected, split reductive group over
F
with root datum
\mathfrak{R}
. In this case, the action of
\sigma
on

is the identity map. Let
w_{1}=t^{\mu_{1}}y
and
w_{2}=xt^{\mu_{2}}
. By our assumption,

On the other hand, let

2.5.
Some comments

We have the following interesting property on the quantum Bruhat graph. Namely, for any
x,y\in W_{0}(\mathfrak{R})
and any simple root
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{R}
, we have

\langle{\rm wt}(y,x),\alpha\rangle\leqslant\delta_{y\alpha}+\delta_{x\alpha}.

Here for a root
\beta\in R
,
\delta_{\beta}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\beta\in R^{+}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.
For the root datum of adjoint type, the claim follows directly from Theorem
1.1
(1). The statement for arbitrary root datum can be deduced easily from the adjoint root datum.

Theorem
1.1
is stated for extended affine Weyl groups
\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
, not for the Iwahori-Weyl groups
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
. As we mentioned in §
2.1
, the Iwahori-Weyl groups
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
, in general, may not be of the form
\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
for some reduced root datum
\mathfrak{R}
. However, using the identification
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})/X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma,\text{tor}}\cong\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
and the fact that
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma,\text{tor}}
lies in the center of
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
, the statement in Theorem
1.1
remains valid for arbitrary Iwahori-Weyl group
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
.

Applications

3.1.
Lowest two-sided cells

Let
C_{\text{lowest}}
be the lowest Kazhdan-Lusztig two-sided cell of
\tilde{W}
. The explicit description is obtained by the work of Lusztig
[
Lu85
]
, Shi
[
Sh87
]
and Bédard
[
Be88
]
. Let
x,y\in W_{0}
and
\mu\in X_{*}^{+}
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. Then
xt^{\mu}y\in C_{\text{lowest}}
if and only if
\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle+\delta_{x\alpha}-\delta_{y^{-1}\alpha}\neq 0
for any
\alpha\in\Pi
. In other words,
C_{\text{lowest}}
consists of elements in the shrunken Weyl chambers. The latter terminology is often used in the literature on the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties. The following result generalizes Theorem
2.1
to the elements in the lowest two-sided cell, which was conjectured by Milicévić in
[
Mi21
, §6.3]
for split groups.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-split and adjoint over
F
. Let
x,y\in W_{0}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mu\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}^{+}
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
. If
xt^{\mu}y\in C_{\text{lowest}}
, then
[b_{xt^{\mu}y}]=[\mu-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))]
.

Remark 3.2
.

One important application of the explicit formula of the generic Newton points is to study the cordial elements introduced by Milicévić and Viehmann
[
MV20
]
. One may use proposition
3.1
to extend Theorem 1.2 (b), (c) and Theorem 4.2 in
[
MV20
]
from the superregular elements to the elements in the lowest two-sided cells and from split groups to quasi-split groups.

Proof.

Set
w=xt^{\mu}y
. We simply write
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
by
\tilde{W}
and write
W_{0}(\mathbf{G})
by
W_{0}
. By
[
LLHLM20+
, Proposition 2.1.5 & Corollary 2.1.7]
,
w=w_{1}w_{2}
for some
w_{1}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
and
w_{2}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
with
\ell(w)=\ell(w_{1})+\ell(w_{2})
. Since
w_{1}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
, we have
w_{1}=x^{\prime}t^{\mu_{1}}
for some
x^{\prime}\in W_{0}
and
\mu_{1}\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}^{+}
. Since
w_{2}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
, we have
w_{2}=t^{\mu_{2}}y^{\prime}
for some
y^{\prime}\in W_{0}
and
\mu_{2}\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}^{+}
. Thus
w=x^{\prime}t^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}y^{\prime}
. It is easy to see that
t^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. So we have
x^{\prime}=x,y^{\prime}=y
and
\mu=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
.

By Theorem
1.1
and the comments in §
2.5
,
\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))
is dominant and
w_{2}\ast\sigma(w_{1})=zt^{\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))}
for some
z\in W_{0}
. By Lemma
2.3
and
2.3
(3),

[b_{w}]=[b_{w_{1}\ast w_{2}}]=[b_{w_{2}\ast\sigma(w_{1})}]=[b_{zt^{\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))}}]=[\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))].

Moreover,
\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))+(\mu_{2}-\sigma(\mu_{2}))=\mu-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))
. Thus
[\mu_{1}+\sigma(\mu_{2})-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))]=[\mu-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},\sigma(x))]
. The statement is proved.
∎

3.2.
Generic Newton points: reduction to quasi-split groups

The study of generic Newton points for arbitrary reductive groups can be reduced to the quasi-split adjoint groups.

Let
\mathbf{G}_{\text{ad}}
be the adjoint group of
\mathbf{G}
. Let
\sigma_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
be the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
. Let
w\in\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G})
and
w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
be its image in
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
. The maximal element
[b_{w}]^{\mathbf{G}}
of
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
is determined by its image
[b_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}]^{\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}\in B(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}
and its image under the Kottwitz map
\kappa:B(\mathbf{G})\pi(\mathbf{G})_{\sigma}
.

We then study
[b_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}]^{\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}
. Note that there exists a length-zero element
\tau
in
\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
such that
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)\circ\sigma
preserves the set of simple reflections
\mathbb{S}_{0}\subset\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. Set
\sigma_{0}={\mathrm{Ad}}(\dot{\tau})\circ\sigma
. Let
\mathbf{H}
be the associated inner form of
\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
. Since
\sigma_{0}(\mathbb{S}_{0})=\mathbb{S}_{0}
,
\mathbf{H}
is a quasi-split inner form of
\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
. We have
\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}(\breve{F})=\mathbf{H}(\breve{F})
. It is easy to see that the map
bb\dot{\tau}
induces a natural bijection
B(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})\cong B(\mathbf{H})
and this bijection preserves the partial order
\leqslant
defined via the closure relations in
\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}(\breve{F})=\mathbf{H}(\breve{F})
. Under the map
bb\dot{\tau}
,
\breve{I}\dot{w}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\breve{I}
is mapped to
\breve{I}\dot{w}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\dot{\tau}\breve{I}
. Hence the natural bijection
B(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})\cong B(\mathbf{H})
restricts to a natural bijection
B(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}\cong B(\mathbf{H})_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\tau}
for any
w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\in\tilde{W}(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})=\tilde{W}(\mathbf{H})
. In particular, the maximal element
[b_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}]^{\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}
of
B(\mathbf{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}
corresponds to the maximal element
[b_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\tau}]^{\mathbf{H}}
of
B(\mathbf{H})_{w_{{\mathrm{ad}}}\tau}
.

3.3.
Demazure product in the lowest two-sided cell

If
w\in\tilde{W}_{\text{lowest}}
and
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
such that
w\leqslant ws
, then it follows by definition that
ws\in\tilde{W}_{\text{lowest}}
. Similarly, if
w\leqslant sw
, then
sw\in\tilde{W}_{\text{lowest}}
. Thus
w\ast w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}_{\text{lowest}}
if one of
w,w^{\prime}
belongs to
\tilde{W}_{\text{lowest}}
. This observation is pointed out to us by Felix Schremmer.

Now we give an explicit formula for the Demazure product of elements in
C_{\text{lowest}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in W_{0}
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in X_{*}^{+}
with
t^{\mu}y,t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. If
xt^{\mu}y,x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime}\in C_{\text{lowest}}
, then

(xt^{\mu}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime})=xt^{\mu+\mu^{\prime}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{W}=\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R})
. We first consider the case where
\mathfrak{R}
is a root datum of adjoint type.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we have
xt^{\mu}y=w_{1}\ast w_{2}
and
x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime}=w^{\prime}_{1}\ast w^{\prime}_{2}
, where
w_{1}=xt^{\mu_{1}}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
,
w_{2}=t^{\mu_{2}}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
,
w^{\prime}_{1}=x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}_{1}}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
,
w^{\prime}_{2}=t^{\mu^{\prime}_{2}}y^{\prime}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
for some dominant coweights
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu^{\prime}_{1},\mu^{\prime}_{2}
with
\mu=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
and
\mu^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime}_{1}+\mu^{\prime}_{2}
.

By Theorem
1.1
,
w_{2}\ast w^{\prime}_{1}=zt^{\mu^{\prime}_{1}+\mu^{\prime}_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}
for some
z\in W_{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{1}+\mu^{\prime}_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})
is dominant. Hence

\displaystyle(xt^{\mu}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(w_{1}\ast w_{2})\ast(w^{\prime}_{1}\ast w^{\prime}_{2})=w_{1}\ast(w_{2}\ast w^{\prime}_{1})\ast w^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=w_{1}\ast(zt^{\mu^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})})\ast w^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=(w_{1}\ast z)\ast(t^{\mu^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}\ast w^{\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle=w_{1}\ast(t^{\mu^{\prime}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=xt^{\mu+\mu^{\prime}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}.

Notice that
w_{1}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
and
t^{\mu^{\prime}+\mu_{2}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
.

Now we consider the general case. Let
\mathfrak{R}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
be the root system of adjoint type associated to
\mathfrak{R}
and
\tilde{W}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}=\tilde{W}(\mathfrak{R}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
. Then we have a natural projection map
\pi_{{\mathrm{ad}}}:\tilde{W}\tilde{W}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
. For any
\lambda\in X_{*}
, we denote by
\lambda_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
its image in
(X_{*})_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
. Then we have

\pi_{{\mathrm{ad}}}((xt^{\mu}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime}))=(xt^{\mu_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}}y^{\prime})=xt^{\mu_{{\mathrm{ad}}}+\mu^{\prime}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}.

On the other hand,
(xt^{\mu}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime})\in W_{a}t^{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}W_{a}
. So
(xt^{\mu}y)\ast(x^{\prime}t^{\mu^{\prime}}y^{\prime})=xt^{\mu+\mu^{\prime}-{\rm wt}(y^{-1},x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}
.
∎

3.4.
Demazure product on the coweight lattice

For any coweight
\lambda\in X_{*}
, we denote by
\bar{\lambda}
the unique dominant coweight in the
W_{0}
-orbit of
\lambda
. We define the map
pr:\tilde{W}X_{*}
, which sends any element
w\in\tilde{W}
to the unique dominant coweight
\lambda
with
w\in W_{0}t^{\lambda}W_{0}
.

Now we define the Demazure product on the coweight lattice by

\ast:X_{*}\times X_{*}X_{*}^{+},\quad(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})pr(t^{\lambda_{1}}\ast t^{\lambda_{2}}).

We have the following explicit formula.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in X_{*}
. For
i=1,2
, let
w_{i}
be the unique element in
W_{0}^{I(\bar{\lambda}_{i})}
with
\lambda_{i}=w_{i}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})
. Then

\lambda_{1}\ast\lambda_{2}=\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}-{\rm wt}(w_{1},w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}).

Proof.

We have
t^{\lambda_{1}}=w_{1}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}}w_{1}^{-1}
. Since
w_{1}\in W_{0}^{I(\bar{\lambda}_{1})}
,
t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}}w_{1}^{-1}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. We have
t^{\lambda_{2}}=w_{2}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}}w_{2}^{-1}\in w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}}W_{0}
. Since
w_{2}\in W_{0}^{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}
,
w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}}\in\tilde{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}_{\max}
. By Theorem
1.1
,
\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}-{\rm wt}(w_{1},w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})})\in X_{*}^{+}
and

\displaystyle t^{\lambda_{1}}\ast t^{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\in(w_{1}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}}w_{1}^{-1})\ast(w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}}W_{0})

\displaystyle=\bigl((w_{1}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}}w_{1}^{-1})\ast(w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}})\bigr)W_{0}

\displaystyle=w_{1}\ast\bigl((t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}}w_{1}^{-1})\ast(w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{2}})\bigr)W_{0}

\displaystyle\subseteq w_{1}\ast(W_{0}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}-{\rm wt}(w_{1},w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})})})W_{0}

\displaystyle=W_{0}t^{\bar{\lambda}_{1}+\bar{\lambda}_{2}-{\rm wt}(w_{1},w_{2}w_{I(\bar{\lambda}_{2})})})W_{0}.

The statement is proved.
∎

3.5.
Lusztig-Vogan map

Define
\iota:\tilde{W}\tilde{W}
by
\iota(t^{\lambda}y)=w_{0}t^{-\lambda}yw_{0}
for
\lambda\in X_{*}
and
y\in W_{0}
. It is easy to see that
\iota
is a length-preserving involutive group automorphism on
\tilde{W}
. Let
\mathbf{I}_{\iota}=\{w\in\tilde{W};\iota(w)=w^{-1}\}
be the set of twisted involutions of
\tilde{W}
. For
x\in\mathbf{I}_{\iota}
we define

\pi_{x}:\tilde{W}\mathbf{I}_{\iota},\quad ww\ast x\ast\iota(w)^{-1}.

In
[
LV12
]
and
[
Lu12
]
, Lusztig and Vogan constructed a module
M
of the Hecke algebra
H
of
\tilde{W}
over
\mathbb{Z}[q]
, which has a linear basis indexed by
\mathbf{I}_{\iota}
. If we take
q=0
, then
H
becomes the
0
-Hecke algebra
H_{0}
in §
1.2
and
M
becomes a module
M_{0}=\oplus_{x\in\mathbf{I}_{\iota}}\mathbb{Z}a_{x}
of the 0-Hecke algebra
H_{0}
. It is proved in
[
LV21+
]
that the action of
H_{0}
on
M_{0}
has the following simple expression

t_{w}a_{x}=(-1)^{\ell(x)+\ell(w)+\ell(\pi_{x}(w))}a_{\pi_{x}(w)}.

A particular interesting case is
x=1
and
w\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}_{\max}
. Note that the map
\lambda w_{0}\ast t^{\lambda}
gives a bijection from
X_{*}
to
{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}_{\max}
. Thus the map
\pi_{1}
induces

{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\pi:X_{*}X_{*}^{+},\qquad\lambda pr\bigl((w_{0}\ast t^{\lambda})\ast\iota(w_{0}\ast t^{\lambda})^{-1}\bigr).

As a consequence of proposition
3.4
, we obtain the following formula for
{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\pi
. This answers a question of Lusztig and Vogan in
[
LV21+
, 0.4]
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\lambda\in X_{*}
and
w
be the unique element in
W_{0}^{I(\bar{\lambda})}
with
\lambda=w(\bar{\lambda})
. Then
{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\pi(\lambda)=2\bar{\lambda}-{\rm wt}(w,w_{0}w)
.

Proof.

Note that
(w_{0}\ast t^{\lambda})\ast\iota(w_{0}\ast t^{\lambda})^{-1}=w_{0}\ast(t^{\lambda}\ast\iota(t^{\lambda})^{-1})\ast w_{0}\in W_{0}(t^{\lambda}\ast\iota(t^{\lambda})^{-1})W_{0}
. Moreover,
\iota(t^{\lambda})^{-1}=t^{w_{0}(\lambda)}
. Thus
{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\pi(\lambda)=\lambda\ast(w_{0}(\lambda))
. Note that
\overline{w_{0}(\lambda)}=\bar{\lambda}
and
w_{0}(\lambda)=w_{0}w(\bar{\lambda})
, where
w_{0}w\in(W_{0}^{I(\bar{\lambda})})_{\max}
. By Proposition
3.4
,
\lambda\ast(w_{0}(\lambda))=2\bar{\lambda}-{\rm wt}(w,w_{0}w)
.
∎

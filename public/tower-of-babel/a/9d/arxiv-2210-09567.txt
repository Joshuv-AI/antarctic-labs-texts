Almost optimal polynomial approximation on convex sets in ℂ \mathbb{C}
Liudmyla Kryvonos

Abstract
For a function f f , continuous on a compact convex set K K and analytic in its interior we construct a sequence of almost optimal polynomials that converge with a geometric rate at points of analyticity of f f .

Introduction

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact set with connected complement
\Omega:=\mathbb{\overline{C}}\backslash K
, where
\overline{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
is the extended complex plane. The set of continuous functions on
K
that are analytic in the interior
\textnormal{Int}K
of
K
will be denoted by
A(K)
.
Let
\mathbb{P}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,...\}
be the class of complex polynomials of degree at most
n
.

The rate of the best uniform approximation of a function
f\in A(K)
by polynomials of degree at most
n
we denote by

E_{n}(f)=E_{n}(f,K):=\underset{P_{n}\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}{\inf}\|f-P_{n}\|_{K}.

(1)

Here
\|\cdotp\|_{K}
means the supremum norm over
K
.

By almost optimal, or "near-best" approximants
p_{n}
to a function
f\in A(K)
we call polynomials, such that

\|f-p_{n}\|_{K}\leq CE_{n}(f),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}

with a fixed
C>1
.

One of the starting points for the study of almost optimal polynomials traces back to the work by Shirokov
[
13
]
. It was pointed out that for a
D
-approximation, first introduced by Dzjadyk (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
), the rate of decay of the error strictly inside a continuum can be
cn^{-M}
, with an arbitrary fixed
M
.

Later on, the existence of almost optimal polynomials, possible rate of convergence inside
K
and its dependence on the geometric properties of
K
were studied in works by Saff and Totik
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
, Maimeskul
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, Shirokov and Totik
[
15
]
, Kryvonos
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
.

In particular, it was proved by Saff and Totik
[
11
]
that if
K
is bounded by an analytic curve, then there are almost optimal approximants
p_{n}
such that, for every compact set
E\subset\textnormal{Int}K
,

|f(z)-p_{n}(z)|\leq Ce^{-\beta n}E_{n}(f),\;\;\;z\in E,

where
\beta>0
,
C\geq 1
are independent of
f
. In
[
6
]
it was shown that the same behavior holds for almost optimal harmonic polynomials.

Our primary motivation for this paper is to extend the family of compact sets for which the geometric convergence of "near-best" approximants is achieved whenever it is permitted.
Using the rapidly decreasing polynomials introduced by Shirokov
[
14
]
, we prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact convex set with nonempty interior
\textnormal{Int}K
. Then for any
f\in A(K)
there exists a sequence of "near-best" polynomials
\{p_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}
, such that

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\overline{\lim}}\|f-p_{n}\|_{E}\;e^{cn}=0

holds for any compact set
E\subset\textnormal{Int}K
, with
c=c(E)
.

It turns out, if a compact set has an external angle smaller than
\pi
, then geometric convergence of almost optimal polynomials might be impossible.
For a disc with a sector of angle
\alpha\pi
removed,
\alpha<1
, this was shown in
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
. Nevertheless, the value of an external angle alone in general does not characterize the behavior of almost optimal polynomials, as we demonstrate in the next example.

For
0<\alpha<1
and some
R>0
, consider a compact set
K
whose boundary consists of the arcs
\{re^{\pm i\alpha\pi/2},0\leq r\leq R\}\cup\{re^{i(\pi\pm\alpha\pi/2)},0\leq r\leq R\}\cup\{Re^{i\phi},-\alpha\pi/2\leq\phi\leq\alpha\pi/2\}\cup\{Re^{i(\pi+\phi)},-\alpha\pi/2\leq\phi\leq\alpha\pi/2\}
. For such compact set we obtain the next result.

Theorem 2
.

Consider a function
f\in A(K)
, that is not analytic at
z=0
. Then, if
\alpha\leq\beta:=1-\alpha
, there exists a sequence of "near-best" polynomials that have geometric rate of convergence in the interior points of
K
. If
\alpha>\beta
, then the geometric rate of convergence in the interior of
K
is impossible.

Rapidly decreasing polynomials (
3
) can also be applied for enhancing rational approximation on convex sets. Gopal and Trefethen in
[
5
]
showed that for a function
f
with corner singularities approximation by rational functions can achieve root-exponential convergence. We show that approximants can be modified so that at points of analyticity of
f
they achieve geometric convergence.

Theorem 3
.

Let
K
be a convex polygon with corners
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
and let f be an analytic function in
K
that is analytic on the interior of each side segment and can be analytically continued to a disc near each
\omega_{i}
with a slit along the exterior bisector there. Assume
f
satisfies
f(z)-f(\omega_{i})=O(|z-\omega_{i}|^{\delta})
,
z\rightarrow\omega_{i}
for each
i
for some
\delta>0
.
There exist rational functions
\{r_{n}\}
of degree
n
,
1\leq n<\infty
satisfying

\|f-r_{n}\|_{K}=O(e^{-C\sqrt{n}}),

for some
C>0
, and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\overline{\lim}}\|f-r_{n}\|_{E}e^{cn}=0,

for an arbitrary compact subset
E\in\textnormal{Int}K
with
c=c(E)>0
independent of
n
.

\frac{1}{\pi}\log\frac{2}{|J|}\leqslant m(\psi(\Gamma))=m(\Gamma)=\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}\pi}\ln\bigg(\frac{c_{1}}{d}\bigg)+c_{2}

which implies

|J|\curlyeqsucc d^{\alpha/(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}\curlyeqsucc w^{\alpha/(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}.

Consequently, together with estimate
m_{\gamma}(z)\leqslant 1
we obtain

\log|Q(z)|\leqslant c_{1}nw^{1/\beta}-c_{2}n\;w^{\alpha/(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}+\log(C),

which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

To finish the proof of the theorem, assume that there exists a sequence of almost optimal polynomials
P_{n}
satisfying

\|f-P_{n}\|_{\overline{V}}\leq CE_{n}(f)e^{-cn}

for some open
V\subset\textnormal{Int}K
.
Then, by Lemma
2
,

|P_{n}(z)-P_{n-1}(z)|\leqslant C\exp(-c^{\prime}n|z|^{\alpha/(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}+c^{\prime\prime}n|z|^{1/\beta}),\;\;\;\;\;|z|\leqslant\varepsilon.

Since
(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}>\alpha\beta
, there exists some
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
such that

|P_{n}(z)-P_{n-1}(z)|\leqslant C\exp(-c_{3}n),\;\;\;\;\;|z|\leqslant\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Thus,
P_{n}
converge to some analytic function in
|z|\leqslant\varepsilon^{\prime}
, that is a contradiction, since
f
is not analytic at
z=0
.

Approximation with rational functions

Theorem
3
follows directly from the next result.

Theorem 5
.

Consider
A_{\Theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1,-\Theta<\arg z<\Theta\}
and let
\Theta\in(0,\pi/2)
be fixed. Let
f
be a bounded analytic function in the slit disc
A_{\pi}
that satisfies
f(z)=O(|z|^{\delta})
as
z\rightarrow 0
for some
\delta>0
. Then, for
\rho\in(0,1)
, there exist rational functions
\{r_{n}\}
,
1\leq n<\infty
, such that

\|f-r_{n}\|_{\overline{M}}=O(e^{-C\sqrt{n}}),

as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
C>0
, where
M=\rho A_{\Theta}
,
and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\overline{\lim}}\|f-r_{n}\|_{E}\;e^{cn}=0,

for any compact
E\subset M
, with
c=c(E)
.

Proof.

Let

\phi(z):=\prod^{n-1}_{j=0}(z-\alpha_{j})/\prod^{n-1}_{j=0}(z-\beta_{j}),

where we define poles
\beta_{j}
by

\beta_{j}:=-e^{\ \sigma j/\sqrt{n}},\;\;\;0\leq j\leq n-1,

with some fixed
\sigma>0
, and interpolation points
\alpha_{j}
by

\alpha_{0}=0,\;\;\;\alpha_{j}=-\beta_{j},\;\;\;1\leq j\leq n-1.

Let
\Gamma
be a boundary of the slit disc
A_{\pi}
. We will approximate the Cauchy integral of the function
f
over the contour
\Gamma
.
It is enough to approximate the integral over
[-1,0]
only, since the integral over the circular part, by Runge’s theorem
[
10
]
, can be approximated with geometric rate on
\overline{M}
.
Set

r_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{[-1,0]}q_{[\frac{n}{2}]}(\zeta,z)f(\zeta)d\zeta,

where

q_{n}(\zeta,z):=\frac{1}{\phi(\zeta)}\frac{\phi(\zeta)-\phi(z)}{\zeta-z}+\frac{\phi(z)}{\phi(\zeta)}\frac{1-R_{n}(\zeta,z)}{\zeta-z}

is a rational function of order at most
2n
.

For
\varepsilon=\exp(-\sigma(n-1)/\sqrt{n})
, split
[-1,0]
into two pieces,

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\{\zeta\in\Gamma:|\zeta|<\varepsilon\},\;\;\;\Gamma_{1}=[-1,0]\setminus\Gamma_{\varepsilon}

Then we get

\displaystyle f(z)-r_{2n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi(z)}{\phi(\zeta)}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}R_{n}(\zeta,z)d\zeta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\frac{\phi(z)}{\phi(\zeta)}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}R_{n}(\zeta,z)d\zeta=I_{\varepsilon}+I_{1}.

To bound
I_{\varepsilon}
, first notice that for
\zeta\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, we have
|z|\leq|\zeta-z|
.
Also the estimates
|z-\alpha_{0}|/|z-\beta_{0}|\leq|z|
and
|z-\alpha_{j}|/|z-\beta_{j}|
,
j\geq 1
imply

|\phi(z)|\leq|z|,\;\;\;z\in A_{\Theta}.

(8)

Thus,

\frac{|\phi(z)|}{|\zeta-z|}\leq 1.

To bound
f(t)/\phi(t)
, over
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, first observe that

|\phi(\zeta)|\geq|\zeta|,\;\;\;\zeta\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon},

since
|\zeta-\alpha_{0}|/|\zeta-\beta_{0}|\geq|\zeta|
, and
|\zeta-\alpha_{j}|/|\zeta-\beta_{j}|\geq 1
for
j\geq 1
.
Therefore, taking into account the behavior of
f(z)
as
z\rightarrow 0
, it’s enough to bound the integral

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{t^{\delta}}{t}dt=\frac{\varepsilon^{\delta}}{\delta}

Since
\varepsilon=\exp(-\sigma(n-1)/\sqrt{n})
, the integral is of order
\exp(-\sigma\delta\sqrt{n})
.

By (
8
), the integral over
I_{1}
is root-exponentially small for
z\in A_{\Theta}
with
|z|<2\varepsilon
.
For
z\in A_{\Theta}
with
|z|\geq 2\varepsilon
, it is enough to show that order
\sqrt{n}
of factors
|z-\alpha_{j}|/|z-\beta_{j}|
is bounded by some constant
A<1
that is independent of
z
.
For each
z
, choose the factors satisfying
|z|/2<\alpha_{j}<|z|
. It’s easy to see that the number of these factors grows in proportion to
\sqrt{n}
,
n\rightarrow\infty
and that each factor is bounded by some constant
A<1
, depending on
\Theta
but independent of
z
.
Moreover, for the points
z\in M
the approximants converge with geometric rate, due to the properties (
2
) of the polynomials
R_{n}
.

∎

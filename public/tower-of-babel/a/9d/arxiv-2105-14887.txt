Parameterized Complexity of Propositional Inclusion and Independence Logic
Yasir Mahmood, Jonni Virtema

Abstract
We give a comprehensive account on the parameterized complexity of model checking and satisfiability of propositional inclusion and independence logic. We discover that for most parameterizations the problems are either in FPT or paraNP-complete.

Introduction

The research program on team semantics was conceived in the early 2000s
to create a unified framework to study logical foundations of different notions of dependence between variables.
Soon after the introduction of first-order dependence logic
[
28
]
, the framework was extended to cover propositional and modal logic
[
29
]
.
In this context, a significant step was taken in
[
5
]
, where the focus shifted to study dependencies between formulas instead of variables.
The framework of team semantics has been proven to be remarkably malleable. During the past decade the framework has been re-adapted for the needs of an array of disciplines.
In addition to the modal variant, team semantics has been generalized to temporal
[
19
]
and probabilistic
[
4
]
frameworks, and fascinating connections to fields such as database theory
[
11
]
, statistics
[
1
]
, real valued computation
[
10
]
, verification
[
20
]
, and quantum information theory
[
16
]
have been identified.

Boolean satisfiability problem (SAT) and quantified Boolean formula problem (QBF) have had a widespread influence in diverse research communities.
In particular, QBF solving techniques are important in application domains such as planning, program synthesis and
verification, adversary games, and non-monotonic reasoning, to name a few
[
27
]
. Further generalizations of QBF are the dependency quantified Boolean formula problem (DQBF) and alternating DQBF which allow richer forms of variable dependence
[
9
,
24
,
25
]
. Propositional logics with team semantics offer a fresh perspective to study enrichments of SAT and QBF.
Indeed, the so-called
propositional dependence logic
(
\mathcal{PDL}
) is known to coincide with DQBF,
whereas quantified propositional logics with team semantics have a close connection to alternating DQBF
[
9
,
30
]
.

Propositional dependency logics extend propositional logic with atomic dependency statements describing various forms of variable dependence.
In this setting, formulas are evaluated over propositional teams (i.e, sets of propositional assignments with common variable domain).
An
inclusion atom

\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
is true in a team
T
, if
\forall s\in T\,\exists t\in T
such that
s(\mathbf{x})=t(\mathbf{y})
.
An
independence atom

\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}
expresses that in a team
T
, for any fixed value for the variables in
\mathbf{z}
the values for
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
are informationally independent.
The extension of propositional logic with
inclusion and independence atoms yield
inclusion (
\mathcal{PINC}
) and independence (
\mathcal{PIND}
) logics, respectively.

The complexity landscape of the classical (non-parameterized) decision problems — satisfiability, validity, and model checking — is well mapped in the propositional and modal team semantics setting (see

[
14
, page 627]
for an overview).
Parameterized complexity theory,
pioneered by Downey and Fellows
[
2
]
,
is a widely studied subarea of complexity theory.
The motivation being that it provides a deeper analysis than the classical complexity theory by providing further insights into the source of intractability.
The idea here is to identify meaningful parameters of inputs such that fixing those makes the problem tractable. One example of a fruitful parameter is the treewidth of a graph.
A parameterized problem (PP) is called fixed parameter tractable, or in
FPT
for short, if for a given input
x
with parameter
k
, the membership of
x
in PP can be decided in time
f(k)\cdot p(|x|)
for some computable function
f
and polynomial
p
.
That is, for each fixed value of
k
the problem is tractable in the classical sense of tractability (in
P
), and the degree of the polynomial is independent of the parameter.
The class
para
NP
consists of problems decidable in time
f(k)\cdot p(|x|)
on a non-deterministic machine.

In the propositional team semantics setting, the study of parameterized complexity was initiated by Meier and Reinbold
[
23
]
in the context of parameterized enumeration problems, and by Mahmood and Meier
[
22
]
in the context of classical decision problems, for
\mathcal{PDL}
.
In the first-order team semantics setting, Kontinen et al.
[
18
]
studied parameterized model checking of dependence and independence logic, and in
[
17
]
introduced the
weighted-definability
problem for dependence, inclusion and independence logic thereby establishing a connection with the parameterized complexity classes in the well-known
W
-hierarchy
.

We focus on the parameterized complexity of model checking (
\mathrm{MC}
) and satisfiability (
\mathrm{SAT}
) of propositional inclusion and independence logic.
We consider both
lax
and
strict
semantics.
The former is the prevailing semantics in the team semantics literature. The past rejection of strict semantics was based on the fact that it does not satisfy locality
[
8
]
(the locality principle dictates that satisfaction of a formula should be invariant on the truth values of variables that do not occur in the formula).
Recent works have revealed that locality of strict semantics can be recovered by moving to multiteam semantics (here teams are multisets)
[
3
]
. Since, in propositional team semantics, the shift from teams to multiteams has no complexity theoretic implications, we stick with the simpler set based semantics for our logics.

Preliminaries

We assume familiarity with standard notions in complexity theory such as classes
\textbf{P},\textbf{NP}
and
EXP

[
26
]
.
We give a short exposition of relevant concepts from parameterized complexity theory.
For a broader introduction consider the textbook of Downey and Fellows
[
2
]
, or that of Flum and Grohe
[
7
]
.

A
parameterized problem
(PP)
\Pi\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
consists of tuples
(x,k)
, where
x
is called an instance and
k
is the (value of the) parameter.

FPT
and
para
NP
.

Let
\Pi
be a PP over
\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\Pi
is
fixed parameter tractable
(
FPT
for short) if it can be decided by a deterministic algorithm
\mathcal{A}
in time
f(k)\cdot p(|x|)
for any input
(x,k)
, where
f
is a computable function and
p
is a polynomial.
If the algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is non-deterministic instead, then
\Pi
belongs to the class
para
NP
.

The notion of hardness in parameterized setting is employed by fpt-reductions.

fpt-reductions.

Let
\Pi\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
and
\Theta\subseteq\Gamma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
be two PPs.
Then
\Pi
is
fpt-reducible
to
\Theta
, if there exists an fpt-computable function
g\colon\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}\to\Gamma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
such that (1) for all
(x,k)\in\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
we have that
(x,k)\in\Pi\Leftrightarrow g(x,k)\in\Theta
and (2) there exists a computable function
h\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for all
(x,k)\in\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
and
g(x,k)=(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})
we have that
k^{\prime}\leq h(k)
.

We will use the following result to prove
para
NP
-hardness. Let
\Pi
be a PP over
\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
.
Then the
\ell
-slice of
\Pi
, for
\ell\geq 0
, is the set
\Pi_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\,x\mid(x,\ell)\in\Pi\,\}
.

Proposition 2

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.14]
)
.

Let
\Pi
be a PP in
para
NP
.
If there exists an
\ell\geq 0
such that
\Pi_{\ell}
is
NP
-complete, then
\Pi
is
para
NP
-complete
.

Moreover, we will use the following folklore result to get several upper bounds.

Proposition 3
.

Let
Q
be a problem such that
(Q,k)
is
FPT
and let
\ell
be a parameter with
k\leq f(\ell)
for some computable function
f
. Then
(Q,\ell)
is
FPT
.

Propositional Team Based Logics.

Let
\mathrm{Var}
be a countably infinite set of variables.
The syntax of propositional logic (
\mathcal{P\!L}
) is defined via the following grammar:

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathop{:}}{\mathop{:}}}}=x\mid\lnot x\mid\phi\lor\phi\mid\phi\land\phi,

where
x\in\mathrm{Var}
. Observe that we allow only atomic negations. As usual
\top\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x\lor\neg x
and
\bot\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x\land\neg x
.
Propositional dependence logic
\mathcal{PDL}
is obtained by extending
\mathcal{P\!L}
by atomic formulas of the form
{=}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
, where
\mathbf{x,y}\subset\mathrm{Var}
are finite tuples of variables.
Similarly, adding inclusion atoms
\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
where (
|\mathbf{x}|=|\mathbf{y}|
) and independence atoms
\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}
gives rise to propositional inclusion (
\mathcal{PINC}
) and independence (
\mathcal{PIND}
) logic, respectively.
When we wish to talk about any of the three considered logics, we simply write
\mathcal{L}
.
That is, unless otherwise stated,
\mathcal{L}\in\{\mathcal{PDL},\mathcal{PINC},\mathcal{PIND}\}
.
For an assignment
s
and a tuple
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
s(\mathbf{x})
denotes
(s(x_{1}),\ldots,s(x_{n}))
.

Team Semantics.

Let
\phi,\psi
be
\mathcal{L}
-formulas and
\mathbf{x,y,z}\subset\mathrm{Var}
be finite tuples of variables.
A
team

T
is a set of assignments
t\colon\mathrm{Var}\to\{\,0,1\,\}
.
The satisfaction relation
\models
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle T\models x

iff

\displaystyle\forall t\in T:t(x)=1,

\displaystyle T\models\lnot x

iff

\displaystyle\forall t\in T:t(x)=0,

\displaystyle T\models\phi\land\psi

iff

\displaystyle T\models\phi\text{ and }T\models\psi,

\displaystyle T\models\phi\lor\psi

iff

\displaystyle\exists T_{1},T_{2}(T_{1}\cup T_{2}=T):T_{1}\models\phi\text{ and }T_{2}\models\psi,

\displaystyle T\models\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}\quad

iff

\displaystyle\forall t\in T\,\exists t^{\prime}\in T:t(\mathbf{x})=t^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}),

\displaystyle T\models\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}\quad

iff

\displaystyle\forall t,t^{\prime}\in T:t(\mathbf{z})=t^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})\text{, }\exists t^{\prime\prime}:t^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{xzy})=t(\mathbf{xz})t^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}).

Intuitively, an inclusion atom
\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
is true if the value taken by
\mathbf{x}
under an assignment
t
is also taken by
\mathbf{y}
under some assignment
t^{\prime}
.
Moreover, the independence atom
\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}
has the meaning that whenever the value for
\mathbf{z}
is fixed under two assignments
t
and
t^{\prime}
, then there is an assignment
t^{\prime\prime}
which maps
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
according to
t
and
t^{\prime}
, respectively.
We can interpret the dependence atom
{=}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
as the independence atom
\mathbf{y}\bot_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y}
. The operator
\lor
is also called a split-junction in the context of team semantics.
Note that in the literature there exist two semantics for the split-junction:
lax
and
strict
semantics (e.g., Hella et al.
[
14
]
).
Strict semantics requires the “splitting of the team” to be a partition whereas lax semantics allows an “overlapping” of the team.
Regarding
\mathcal{PDL}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
, the complexity for
\mathrm{SAT}
and
\mathrm{MC}
is the same irrespective of the considered semantics.
However, the picture is different for
\mathrm{MC}
in
\mathcal{PINC}
as depicted in
[
14
, page 627]
.
For any logic
\mathcal{L}
, we denote
\mathrm{MC}
under strict (respectively, lax) semantics by
\mathrm{MC}_{s}(\mathrm{MC}_{l})
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{MC}_{l}
is in
P
for
\mathcal{PINC}
and consequently, we have only
\mathrm{MC}_{s}
in Table
2
.

3
Graph Representation of the Input

In order to consider specific structural parameters, we need to agree on a representation of an input instance.
We follow the conventions given in
[
22
]
.
Well-formed
\mathcal{L}
-formulas, for every
\mathcal{L}\in\{\mathcal{PDL},\mathcal{PINC},\mathcal{PIND}\}
, can be seen as binary trees (the syntax tree) with leaves as atomic subformulas (variables and dependency atoms).
Similarly to
\mathcal{PDL}

[
22
]
, we take the syntax structure (defined below) rather than syntax tree as a graph structure in order to consider treewidth as a parameter.
We use the same graph representation for each logic
\mathcal{L}
.
That is, when an atom
{=}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
is replaced by either
\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
or
\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{\emptyset}}\mathbf{y}
, the graph representation, and hence, the treewidth of this graph remains the same.
Also, in the case of
\mathrm{MC}
, we include assignments in the graph representation.
In the latter case, we consider the Gaifman graph of the structure that models both, the team and the input formula.

Syntax Structure.

Let

\phi
be an
\mathcal{L}
-formula with propositions
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
T=\{\,s_{1},\ldots s_{m}\,\}
a team.
The
syntax structure

\mathcal{A}_{T,\phi}
has the vocabulary,

\tau_{{T,\phi}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\,\mathsf{VAR}^{1},\textsf{SF}^{1},\succcurlyeq^{2},\mathsf{DEP}^{2},\mathsf{isTrue}^{2},\mathsf{isFalse}^{2},r,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\,\},

where the superscript denote the arity of each relation.
The universe of
\mathcal{A}_{T,\phi}
is
A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\textrm{SF}(\phi)\cup\mathrm{Var}(\phi)\cup\{\,c_{1}^{\mathcal{A}},\ldots,c_{m}^{\mathcal{A}}\,\}
, where
\textrm{SF}(\phi)
and
\mathrm{Var}(\phi)
denote the set of subformulas and variables appearing in
\phi
, respectively.

•

SF
and
\mathsf{VAR}
are unary relations: ‘is a subformula of
\phi
’ and ‘is a variable in
\phi
’.

•

\succcurlyeq
is a binary relation such that
\psi\succcurlyeq^{\mathcal{A}}\alpha
iff
\alpha
is an immediate subformula of
\psi
. That is, either
\psi=\neg\alpha
or there is a
\beta\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
such that
\psi=\alpha\oplus\beta
where
\oplus\in\{\land,\lor\}
. Moreover,
r
is a constant symbol representing
\phi
.

•

\mathsf{DEP}
is a binary relation connecting
\mathcal{L}
-atoms and its parameters. For example, if
\alpha=\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
and
x,y\in\mathbf{x}\cup\mathbf{y}
, then
\mathsf{DEP}(\alpha,x)
and
\mathsf{DEP}(x,y)
are true.

•

The set
\{\,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\,\}
encodes the team
T
. Each
c_{i}\in\tau_{T,\phi}
corresponds to an assignment
s_{i}\in T
for
i\leq m
, interpreted as
c_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}\in{A}
.

•

\mathsf{isTrue}
and
\mathsf{isFalse}
relate
\mathsf{VAR}
with
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
.
\mathsf{isTrue}(c,x)
(resp.,
\mathsf{isFalse}(c,x)
) is true iff
x
is mapped to
1
(resp.,
0
) by the assignment in
T
interpreted by
c
.

The
syntax structure

\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
over a vocabulary
\tau_{\phi}
is defined analogously.
Here
\tau_{\phi}
neither contains the team related relations nor the constants
c_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Gaifman graph.
Let
T
be a team,
\phi
an
\mathcal{L}
-formula,
\mathcal{A}_{T,\phi}
and
A
as above. The
Gaifman graph

G_{T,\phi}=(A,E)
of the
\tau_{T,\phi}
-structure
\mathcal{A}_{T,\phi}
is defined as

E\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\big\{\,\{u,v\}\;\big|\;u,v\in A,\text{ such that there is an }R\in\tau_{T,\phi}\text{ with }(u,v)\in R\,\big\}.

Analogously, we let
G_{\phi}
to be the
Gaifman graph
for the
\tau_{\phi}
-structure
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
.

Note that for
G_{\phi}
we have
E=\mathsf{DEP}\;\cup\succcurlyeq
and for
G_{T,\phi}
we have that
E=\mathsf{DEP}\,\cup\succcurlyeq\cup\,\mathsf{isTrue}\cup\mathsf{isFalse}
.

Treewidth.

A
tree decomposition
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a tree
T=(B,E_{T})
, where the vertex set
B\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V)
is a collection of
bags
and
E_{T}
is the edge relation such that (1)
\bigcup_{b\in B}b=V
, (2) for every
\{\,u,v\,\}\in E
there is a bag
b\in B
with
u,v\in b
, and (3) for all
v\in V
the restriction of
T
to
v
(the subset with all bags containing
v
) is connected.
The
width
of a tree decomposition
T=(B,E_{T})
is the size of the largest bag minus one:
\max_{b\in B}|b|-1
.
The
treewidth
of a graph
G
is the minimum over widths of all tree decompositions of
G
.
The treewidth of a tree is one.
Intuitively, it measures the tree-likeness of a given graph.

Parameterizations.

We consider eight different parameters for
\mathrm{MC}
and six for
\mathrm{SAT}
.
For
\mathrm{MC}
, we include
formula-tw
,
formula-team-tw
,
team-size
,
formula-size
,
\#\textsf{variables}
,
formula-depth
,
\#\textsf{splits}
and
arity
.
However, for
\mathrm{SAT}
,
formula-team-tw
and
team-size
are not meaningful.
All these parameters arise naturally in problems we study.
Let
T
be a team and
\phi
an
\mathcal{L}
-formula.

\#\textsf{splits}
denotes the number of times a split-junction
(\lor)
appears in
\phi
and
\#\textsf{variables}
denotes the number of distinct propositional variables.

formula-depth
is the depth of the syntax tree of
\phi
, that is, the length of the longest path from root to any leaf in the syntax tree.

team-size
is the cardinality of the team
T
, and
formula-size
is
|\phi|
.
For a dependence atom
{=}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
and inclusion atom
\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
, the arity is defined as
|\mathbf{x}|
(recall that
|\mathbf{x}|=|\mathbf{y}|
for an inclusion atom), whereas, for an independence atom
\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}
, it is
the number of distinct variables appearing in
\mathbf{x}\bot_{\mathbf{z}}\mathbf{y}
. Finally,
arity
denotes the maximum arity of any
\mathcal{L}
-atom in
\phi
.
Regarding treewidth, recall that for
\mathrm{MC}
, we also include the assignment-variable relation in the graph representation.
This yields two graphs:
G_{\phi}
for
\phi
, and
G_{T,\phi}
for
\langle T,\phi\rangle
.
Consequently, there are two treewidth notions.
formula-tw
is the treewidth of
G_{\phi}
and
formula-team-tw
is the treewidth of
G_{T,\phi}
.
The name emphasises whether the team is also part of the graph.
As we pointed out
formula-team-tw
and
team-size
are both only relevant for
\mathrm{MC}
because an instance of
\mathrm{SAT}
does not contain a team.

Given an instance
\langle{T,\phi}\rangle
and a parameterisation
\kappa
, then
\kappa({T,\phi})
denotes the parameter value of
\langle{T,\phi}\rangle
.
The following relationship between several of the aforementioned parameters was proven for
\mathcal{PDL}
.
It is easy to observe that the lemma also applies to
\mathcal{PINC}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
.

Lemma 5

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\{\mathcal{PDL},\mathcal{PINC},\mathcal{PIND}\}
,
\phi
an
\mathcal{L}
-formula and
T
be a team. Then,
\textsf{team-size}({T,\phi})\leq 2^{\#\textsf{variables}({T,\phi})}
,
\textsf{team-size}({T,\phi})\leq 2^{\textsf{formula-size}({T,\phi})}
, and
\textsf{formula-size}({T,\phi})\leq 2^{2\cdot\textsf{formula-depth}({T,\phi})}
.

Moreover, recall that we use the same graph representation for
\mathcal{PDL},\mathcal{PINC}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
.
As a consequence, the following result also applies.

Corollary 6

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\{\mathcal{PDL},\mathcal{PINC},\mathcal{PIND}\}
,
\phi
an
\mathcal{L}
-formula and

be a team.
Then
\textsf{formula-team-tw}({T,\phi})
bounds
\textsf{team-size}({T,\phi})
.

4
Complexity of inclusion and independence logic

We start with general complexity results that hold for any team based logic whose atoms are
P
-checkable.
An atom
\alpha
is
P
-checkable if given a team
T
,
T\models\alpha
can be checked in polynomial time.
It is immediate that each atom considered in this paper is
P
-checkable.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a team based logic such that
\mathcal{L}
-atoms are
P
-checkable, then
\mathrm{MC}
for
\mathcal{L}
when parameterized by
team-size
is
FPT
.

Proof.

We claim that the bottom up (brute force) algorithm for the model checking of
\mathcal{PDL}

[
22
, Thm. 17]
works for any team based logic
\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathcal{L}
-atoms are
P
-checkable.
The algorithm begins by checking the satisfaction of atoms against each subteam.
This can be achieved in
FPT
-time since teamsize and consequently the number of subteams is bounded.
Moreover, by taking the union of subteams for split-junction and keeping the same team for conjunction the algorithm can find subteams for each subformula in
FPT
-time.
Lastly, it checks that the team
T
is indeed a satisfying team for the formula
\phi
.
For any team based logic
\mathcal{L}
, the
FPT
runtime is guaranteed if
\mathcal{L}
-atoms are
P
-checkable.
Finally, the proof works for both strict and lax semantics.
∎

The following corollary to Theorem
7
is derived using Lemma
5
and Proposition
3
.

Corollary 8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a team based logic such that
\mathcal{L}
-atoms are
P
-checkable, then
\mathrm{MC}
for
\mathcal{L}
when parameterized by
k
is
FPT
, if
k\in\{\textsf{formula-team-tw},\linebreak\textsf{formula-depth},\#\textsf{variables},\textsf{formula-size}\}
.

The following theorem states results for satisfiability.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a team based logic s.t.
\mathcal{L}
-atoms are
P
-checkable, then
\mathrm{SAT}
for
\mathcal{L}
when parameterized by
k
is
FPT
, if
k\in\{\textsf{formula-depth},\#\textsf{variables}\}
.

Proof.

Notice first that the case for
formula-size
is trivial because any problem parameterized by input size is
FPT
.
Moving on, bounding
formula-depth
also bounds
formula-size
, this yields
FPT
-membership for
formula-depth
in conjunction with Prop.
3
.
Finally, for
\#\textsf{variables}
, one can enumerate all of the
2^{2^{\#\textsf{variables}}}
-many teams in
FPT
-time and determine whether any of these satisfies the input formula.
The last step requires that the model checking parameterized by
team-size
is
FPT
, which is true due to Theorem
7
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Our main technical contributions are the following two theorems which establish that the satisfiability problem of
\mathcal{PINC}
parameterized by
arity
is
para
NP
-complete, and that
\mathrm{SAT}
of
\mathcal{PINC}
without disjunctions is tractable.
We start with the former.
The hardness follows from the
NP
-completeness of
\mathcal{P\!L}
.
For membership, we give a non-deterministic algorithm
\mathbb{A}
solving
\mathrm{SAT}
.

Theorem 10
.

There is a non-deterministic algorithm
\mathbb{A}
that, given a
\mathcal{PINC}
-formula
\phi
with arity
k
, runs in
O(2^{k}\cdot p(|\phi|))
-time and outputs a non-empty team
T
such that
T\models\phi
if and only if
\phi
is satisfiable.

Proof.

We present the proof for lax semantics first, towards the end we describe some modifications that solve the case for strict semantics.
Given an input
\mathcal{PINC}
-formula
\phi
, the algorithm
\mathbb{A}
operates on the syntax tree of
\phi
and constructs a sequence of teams
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)
for each
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
as follows.
We let
f_{\footnotesize{{0}}}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\emptyset
for each
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
.
Then,
\mathbb{A}
begins by non-deterministically selecting a singleton team
f_{\footnotesize{{1}}}(\phi)
for
\phi
.
For
i\geq 1
,
\mathbb{A}
implements the following steps recursively.

For odd
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{i}(\psi)
is defined in a
top-down
fashion as follows.

1.

f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\phi)
for
i\geq 3
.

2.

If
\psi=\psi_{0}\land\psi_{1}
, let
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi_{0})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)
and
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi_{1})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)
.

3.

If
\psi=\psi_{0}\lor\psi_{1}
, then non-deterministically select two teams
P_{0},P_{1}
such that
P_{0}\cup P_{1}=f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)\setminus f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\psi)
and set
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\psi_{j})\cup P_{j}
for
j=0,1
.

For even
i
,
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)
is defined in a
bottom-up
fashion as follows.

4.

If
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
is an atomic literal, then immediately reject if
f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\psi)\not\models\psi
and set
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\psi)
otherwise.
If
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
is an inclusion atom, then construct
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)\supseteq f_{\footnotesize{{i-1}}}(\psi)
such that
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)\models\psi
. For
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
, this is done by (I) adding partial assignments
t(\mathbf{y})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=s(\mathbf{x})
whenever an assignment
s
is a cause for the
failure
of
\psi
, and (II) non-deterministically selecting extensions of these assignments to the other variables.

5.

If
\psi=\psi_{0}\land\psi_{1}
, or
\psi=\psi_{0}\lor\psi_{1}
let
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi_{0})\cup f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi_{1})
.

\mathbb{A}
terminates by accepting when a fixed point is reached. That is, we obtain
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)=f_{\footnotesize{{i+1}}}(\psi)
for each
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
when
i\geq j
. Moreover,
\mathbb{A}
rejects if Step
4
triggers a rejection.
Notice that the only step when new assignments are added is at the atomic level.
Whereas the split in Step
3
concerns those assignments which arise from other subformulas through union in Step
5
.
We first prove the following claim regarding the overall runtime for
\mathbb{A}
.

Claim I.
\mathbb{A}
runs in at most
O(2^{k}\cdot p(|\phi|))
steps for some polynomial
p
, where
k
is the arity of
\phi
. That is, a fixed point or rejection is reached in this time.

Proof of Claim.

In each iteration
i
, either
\mathbb{A}
rejects, or keeps adding new assignments.
Furthermore, new assignments are added only in the cases for inclusion atoms.
As a result, if
\mathbb{A}
has not yet reached a fixed point the reason is that some inclusion atom has generated new assignments.
Since we take union of subteams in the bottom-up step, the following top-down iteration in Steps
2
and
3
may also add assignments in a subteam.
That is, the subteams from each
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
are propagated to other subformulas during each iteration.
Now, each inclusion atom of arity
l\leq k
can generate at most
2^{l}
new assignments due to Step
4
in the algorithm.
Let
n
denote the number of inclusion atoms in
\phi
and
k
be their maximum arity.
Then
\mathbb{A}
iterates at most
2^{k}\cdot cn
times, where
c
is some constant due to the propagation of teams to other subformulas.
This implies that, if no rejection has occured, there is some
j\leq 2^{k}\cdot cn
such that
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)=f_{\footnotesize{{j}}}(\psi)
for each subformula
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
and
i\geq j
.
We denote this fixed point by
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)
for each
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
.

Now, we analyze the time it takes to compute each iteration.
For odd
i\geq 1
, Steps
1
and
2
set the same team for each subformula and therefore take linear time.
Notice that the size of team in each iteration is bounded by
2^{k}\cdot n
.
This holds because new assignments are added only in the case of inclusion atoms and
\mathbb{A}
starts with a singleton team.
Consequently, Step
3
non-deterministically splits the teams of size
2^{k}\cdot n
in each iteration
i
for odd
i\geq 1
.
Moreover, Step
4
for even
i
requires (1) polynomial time in
|f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)|
, if
\psi
is an atomic literal, and (2) non-deterministic polynomial time in
2^{l}\cdot|f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)|
if
\psi
is an inclusion atom of arity
l\leq k
.
Finally, the union in Step
5
again requires linear time.
This implies that each iteration takes at most a runtime of
2^{k}\cdot p(|\phi|)
for some polynomial
p
.
This completes the proof of Claim 1.

We now prove that
\mathbb{A}
accepts the input formula
\phi
if and only if
\phi
is satisfiable. Suppose that
\mathbb{A}
accepts and let
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\phi)
denote the fixed point.
We first prove by induction that
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)\models\psi
for each subformula
\psi
of
\phi
.
Notice that there is some
i
such that
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)=f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\psi)
.
The case for atomic subformulas is clear due to the Step 4 of
\mathbb{A}
.
For conjunction, observe that the team remains the same for each conjunct. That is, when
\psi=\psi_{0}\land\psi_{1}
and the claim holds for
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi_{i})
and
\psi_{i}
, then
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)\models\psi_{0}\land\psi_{1}
is true.
For disjunction, if
\psi=\psi_{0}\lor\psi_{1}
and
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi_{i})
are such that
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi_{i})\models\psi_{i}
for
i=0,1
,
then we have that
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)\models\psi
where
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi)=f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi_{0})\cup f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\psi_{1})
.
In particular
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\phi)\models\phi
and the correctness of our algorithm follows.

For the other direction,
suppose
\phi
is satisfiable and
T
is a witnessing team.
Then there exists a labelling function for
T
and
\phi
, given as follows.

1.

The label for
\phi
is
T
.

2.

For every subformula
\psi=\psi_{0}\oplus\psi_{1}
with subteam label
P\subseteq T
, the subteam label for
\psi_{i}
is
P_{i}
(
i=0,1
) such that we have
P_{0}=P_{1}=P
, if
\oplus=\land
, and
P_{0}\cup P_{1}=P
if
\oplus=\lor
,

3.

P_{\psi}\models\psi
for every
\psi\in\textrm{SF}(\phi)
with label
P_{\psi}
.

Then we prove that there exists an accepting path when the non-deterministic algorithm
\mathbb{A}
operates on
\phi
.
We claim that when initiated on a subteam
\{s\}\subseteq T
,
\mathbb{A}
constructs a fixed point
f_{\footnotesize{{\infty}}}(\phi)
and halts by accepting
\phi
.
Recall that
\mathbb{A}
propagates teams back and forth until a fixed point is reached.
Moreover, the new assignments are added only at the atomic level.
Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbf{x}\subseteq\mathbf{y}
be an inclusion atom such that
f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\alpha)\not=\emptyset
for odd
i
, then
\mathbb{A}
constructs a subteam
f_{\footnotesize{{i+1}}}(\alpha)\supseteq f_{\footnotesize{{i}}}(\alpha)

(on a non-deterministic branch) containing assignments
t
from
P_{\alpha}
such that
f_{\footnotesize{{i+1}}}(\alpha)\models\alpha
.
Since, there are at most
2^{|\mathbf{y}|}
-many different assignments for
\mathbf{y}
, we know that Step
4
applies to
\alpha
at most
2^{|\mathbf{y}|}
times.
That is, once all the different assignments for
\mathbf{y}
have been checked in some iteration
i
: Step
4
does not add any further assignments to
f_{\footnotesize{{i^{\prime}}}}(\alpha)
for
i^{\prime}\geq i+1
.
Finally, since there is a non-empty team
T
such that
T\models\phi
, this implies that
\mathbb{A}
does not reject
\phi
in any iteration (because there is a choice for
\mathbb{A}
to consider subteams guaranteed by the labelling function).
Consequently,
\mathbb{A}
accepts by constructing a fixed point in at most
O(2^{k}\cdot p(|\phi|))
-steps (follows from Claim I).
This completes the proof and establishes the correctness.

A minor variation in the algorithm
\mathbb{A}
solves
\mathrm{SAT}
for the strict semantics.
When moving downwards,
\mathbb{A}
needs to ensure that an assignment goes to only one side of the split.
Moreover, since the subteams are selected non-deterministically for atomic subformulas, (in the bottom-up iteration) only subteams which can split according to the strict semantics are considered.
∎

Theorem 14
.

There is a deterministic algorithm
\mathbb{B}
that given a
\mathcal{PINC}
-formula
\phi
with no splits runs in
P
-time and accepts if and only if
\phi
is satisfiable.

Proof.

We give a recursive labelling procedure (
\mathbb{B}
) that runs in polynomial time and accepts if and only if
\phi
is satisfiable.
The labelling consists of assigning a value
c\in\{0,1\}
to each variable
x
.

1.

Begin by labelling all
\mathcal{P\!L}
-literals in
\phi
by the value that satisfies them, namely
x=1
for
x
and
x=0
for
\neg x
.

2.

For each inclusion atom
\mathbf{p}\subseteq\mathbf{q}
and a labelled variable
q_{i}\in\mathbf{q}
, label the variable
p_{i}\in\mathbf{p}
with same value
c
as for
q_{i}
. Where
p_{i}
appears in
\mathbf{p}
at the same position, as
q_{i}
in
\mathbf{q}
.

3.

Propagate the label for
p_{i}
from the previous step. That is, consider
p_{i}
as a labelled variable and repeat Step
2
for as long as possible.

4.

If
some variable
x
is labelled with two opposite values, then reject. Otherwise, accept.

The fact that
\mathbb{B}
works in polynomial time is clear because each variable is labelled at most once.
If a variable is labelled to two different values, then it gives a contradiction and the procedure stops.

For the correctness, notice first that if
\mathbb{B}
accepts then we have a partition of
\mathrm{Var}(\phi)
into a set
X
of labelled variables and a set
Y=\mathrm{Var}(\phi)\backslash X
.
When
\mathbb{B}
stops, due to step 3,
\phi
does not contain an inclusion atom
\mathbf{p}\subseteq\mathbf{q}
such that
q_{i}\in\mathbf{q}
and
p_{i}\in\mathbf{p}
for some
q_{i}\in X,p_{i}\in Y
,
where
p_{i}
appears in
\mathbf{p}
at the same position as
q_{i}
in
\mathbf{q}
.
Let
T=\{s\in 2^{\mathrm{Var}(\phi)}\mid\text{$x$ is labelled with $s(x)$, for each $x\in X$}\}
.
Since
\mathbb{B}
accepts, each variable
x\in X
has exactly one label and therefore assignments in
T
are well-defined.
Moreover
T
includes all possible assignments over
Y
.
One can easily observe that
T\models\phi
.

T
satisfies each literal because each
s\in T
satisfies it.
Let
\mathbf{p}\subseteq\mathbf{q}
be an inclusion atom and
s\in T
be an assignment.
We know that for each
x\in\mathbf{q}
that is fixed by
s

5
Concluding Remarks

We presented a parameterized complexity analysis for
\mathcal{PINC}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
.
The problems we considered were satisfiability and model checking.
Interestingly, the parameterized complexity results for
\mathcal{PIND}
coincide with that of
\mathcal{PDL}

[
22
]
in each case.
Moreover, the complexity of model checking under a given parameter remains the same for all three logics.
We proved that for a team based logic
\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathcal{L}
-atoms can be evaluated in
P
-time,
\mathrm{MC}
for
\mathcal{L}
when parameterized by
team-size
is always
FPT
.

It is interesting to notice that for
\mathcal{PDL}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
,
\mathrm{SAT}
is easier than
\mathrm{MC}
when parameterized by
formula-tw
.
This is best explained by the fact that
\mathcal{PDL}
is downwards closed and a formula is satisfiable iff some singleton team satisfies it.
Moreover,
\mathcal{PIND}
also satisfies this ‘satisfiable under singleton team’ property.
The parameters
team-size
and
formula-team-tw
are not meaningful for
\mathrm{SAT}
due to the reason that we do not impose a size restriction for the satisfying team in
\mathrm{SAT}
.
Furthermore,
arity
is quite interesting because
\mathrm{SAT}
for all three logics is
para
NP
-complete.
This implies that while the fixed
arity
does not lower the complexity of
\mathrm{SAT}
in
\mathcal{PDL}
and
\mathcal{PIND}
, it does lower it from
EXP
-completeness to
NP
-completeness for
\mathcal{PINC}
.
As a byproduct, we obtain that the complexity of satisfiability for the fixed arity fragment of
\mathcal{PINC}
is
NP
-complete.
Thereby, we answer an open question posed by Hella and Stumpf
[
15
, P.13]
.
The
para
NP
-membership of
\mathrm{SAT}
when parameterized by
arity
implies that one can encode the problem into classical satisfiability and employ a SAT-solver to solve satisfiability for the fixed arity fragment of
\mathcal{PINC}
.
We leave as a future work the suitable SAT-encoding for
\mathcal{PINC}
that runs in
FPT
-time and enables one to use SAT-solvers.
Further future work involves finding the precise complexity of
\mathrm{SAT}
for
\mathcal{PINC}
when parameterized by
\#\textsf{splits}
and
formula-tw
.

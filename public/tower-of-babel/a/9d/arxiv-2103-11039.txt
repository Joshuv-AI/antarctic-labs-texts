A priori bounds for quasi-linear SPDEs in the full sub-critical regime
Felix Otto, Jonas Sauer, Scott Smith, Hendrik Weber

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with quasi-linear parabolic equations driven by an additive forcing ξ ∈ C α − 2 \xi\in C^{\alpha-2} , in the full sub-critical regime α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) . We are inspired by Hairer’s regularity structures, however we work with a more parsimonious model indexed by multi-indices rather than trees. This allows us to capture additional symmetries which play a crucial role in our analysis. Assuming bounds on this model, which is modified in agreement with the concept of algebraic renormalization, we prove local a priori estimates on solutions to the quasi-linear equations modified by the corresponding counter terms.

da(v)^{\beta^{\prime}}:=\prod_{k\geq 1}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}a}{du^{k}}(v)\bigg)^{\beta(k)},

and introduce a scaled norm of such a multi-index as follows

(17)

|\beta|_{s}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}k\beta(k).

We will show that the renormalization
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
appearing in the
Main Theorem
is given by

(18)

\displaystyle h(v):=\sum_{|\beta|_{s}=0}^{\mathsf{n}-1}da(v)^{\beta^{\prime}}q_{\beta^{\prime}}(a(v)),

where we recall that
q\in\mathsf{T}
depends on a variable
a_{0}
by the definition of
\mathsf{T}
.

Estimate (
15
) is only the lowest of a whole hierarchy of estimates resembling the controlled rough path condition in
[
17
, Definition 1]
. In fact, we will
show that the functions
\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}
describe the solution close to
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
to any order
\kappa<2
, in the sense that for all
r\in(0,1)
and all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in B_{1-r}(0)
there holds

(19)

\displaystyle r^{\kappa}|u(\mathbf{y})-u(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{|\beta|<\kappa}\nu^{{\beta_{x}}}(\mathbf{x})da(u(\mathbf{x}))^{\beta^{\prime}}\Pi_{\mathbf{x}\beta}(\mathbf{y};a(u(\mathbf{x})))|\lesssim(\|u\|+[\Pi])(1\vee[\Pi]^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha}})d^{\kappa}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}),

where the Gubinelli derivative
\nu
is given by

(20)

\displaystyle\nu(\mathbf{x}):=\nabla u(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{|\beta|<1}da(u(\mathbf{x}))^{\beta^{\prime}}\nabla\Pi_{\mathbf{x}\beta}(\mathbf{x};a(u(\mathbf{x}))).

Here
\Pi_{\mathbf{x}\beta}(\mathbf{y};a_{0})
denotes the coefficient of
\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})\in\mathsf{T}
in front of
\mathsf{z}^{\beta}
evaluated at
a_{0}
.

Since the first version of this work appeared on arXiv, there have been some further developments in this direction. In particular, the paper
[
6
]
studies renormalization of a class of quasi-linear SPDEs containing (
1
), focusing on the initial value problem for small times. An advantage of
[
6
]
is that the authors are able to provide an existence and uniqueness theory for the SPDE. However, the result in
[
6
]
is conditional on the existence and continuity properties of a suitable model. The model involves heat kernels which are not translation invariant and have limited regularity near the initial time, which rules out a direct application of the results of
[
11
]
, so presently the hypotheses in
[
6
]
have not been verified.

The approach in
[
6
]
has a number of similarities with this paper and our previous work
[
30
]
.
The main departure from our methodology is that the ‘freezing in’ of the quasi-linear term
a(u)\Delta u
is performed globally, with respect to a reference function approximating the initial condition (similar to
[
5
]
, which implements this with paracontrolled calculus).
This allows the authors to apply Hairer’s analytic results
[
19
]
to close a fixed point argument in a space of modelled distributions and then recover the counter-terms by modifying the arguments of
[
4
]
.
The choice of reference function has some collateral damage in terms of the form of counter-terms in the renormalized equation, which the authors can mitigate on a case by case basis.
The arguments in
[
6
]
are an instance of the traditional bottom-up approach to singular SPDE via a tree-based model.

In contrast, our work introduces a new regularity structure and a top-down methodology.
In particular, we show that by indexing the local expansion more efficiently and defining the right structure group, the correct counter-terms in the PDE appear automatically.
This viewpoint is not limited to quasi-linear equations, and leads to a particularly transparent simplification in settings like KPZ or
\Phi^{4}
where one can index the model (and corresponding local expansion) simply by powers of the coupling constant and polynomials.
Finally, we mention that it does not seem clear how to apply the approach of
[
6
]
on a reference domain of a fixed size, which is the setup of our main result.
In fact, in order to achieve this in the present work, even for solutions small in the supremum norm, we require more refined estimates on the solution (closer to what is needed for global bounds), which is a key reason why we don’t attempt to apply directly the results of
[
19
]
as in the approach of
[
6
]
.

1.1.
A Formal Series Expansion for the Solution

In this section, we motivate our algebraic objects and local expansion by a formal analysis of the PDE (
1
) without renormalization. The discussion below is completely formal, as it involves manipulating formal power series which are not expected to converge. Let us freeze in the coefficients at a base point
\mathbf{x}
and re-write (
1
) as

(21)

(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)u=(a(u)-a_{0})\Delta u+\xi,

where
a_{0}:=a(u(\mathbf{x}))
. Our goal is to find a formal series solution to (
21
) of the form

(22)

u-u(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}.

We will demand that
\tau\in\mathsf{T}\mapsto f(\mathbf{x}).\tau
is linear and has the morphism property: for
\sigma,\tau\in\mathsf{T}

f(\mathbf{x}).(\sigma\tau)=(f(\mathbf{x}).\sigma)(f(\mathbf{x}).\tau),

as well as
f(\mathbf{x}).\mathsf{z}_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}a^{(k)}(u(\mathbf{x}))
. Similar to
\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}
, one should think of
\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}
as a
\mathsf{T}
-valued function, the hat being used to distinguish the two since at this stage there will be no renormalization. Our goal is to show that formally,
u
satisfies (
21
) provided that the coefficients of
\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}
satisfy a certain hierarchy of PDE’s, see (
26
) below. In the next section, we will explain that by adjusting this hierarchy slightly via a suitable renormalization, we are led to a definition of
\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}
which has been shown in
[
27
]
to satisfy the bounds imposed in the present work. We now turn to the calculation.
First note that considering the left-hand side of (
21
), by linearity we clearly have that for
u
satisfying (
22
) it holds

(23)

(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)u=f(\mathbf{x}).(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}.

Furthermore, turning to the right-hand side of (
21
) and applying
\Delta
to (
22
) together with Taylor’s formula,

\displaystyle(a(u)-a_{0})\Delta u

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k!}a^{(k)}(u(\mathbf{x}))(u-u(\mathbf{x}))^{k}f(\mathbf{x}).\Delta\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\big(f(\mathbf{x}).\mathsf{z}_{k}\big)\big(f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}\big)^{k}f(\mathbf{x}).\Delta\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}=f(\mathbf{x}).\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathsf{z}_{k}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\Delta\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}},

where we used the morphism property in the last step. Note also that for
\mathsf{1}\in\mathsf{T}
being the constant power series with value
1
, it holds
f(\mathbf{x}).\mathsf{1}=1
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Hence, matching the terms we see that (
1
) holds provided that

(25)

(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathsf{z}_{k}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\Delta\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}+\xi\mathsf{1}.

At the level of components, recalling the componentwise definition of multiplication of power series, this reads as

(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta}=\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta}^{-},

where

(26)

\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta}^{-}:=\begin{cases}\xi&\text{if}\ \beta=0\\
{\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{k+1}+e_{k}=\beta}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta_{1}}\cdots\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta_{k}}\Delta\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}\beta_{k+1}}}\ &\text{if}\ \beta\neq 0.\end{cases}

Note that the sum in
k
is effectively finite due to the appearance of
e_{k}
in the second sum.
Here
e_{k}
is a multi-index with
1
in component

and zero in all other components.
Note that with this definition, the identity (
24
) turns into

(27)

(a(u)-a_{0})\Delta u+\xi=f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}.

The similarity between (
22
) and (
27
) is effectively the reason why it suffices to use a single index set for both the positive and the negative model simultaneously.

Remark 1
.

A simple way to achieve the properties of
\tau\mapsto f(\mathbf{x}).\tau
demanded above is as follows. Define the linear form
\tau\in\mathsf{T}\mapsto f(\mathbf{x}).\tau
by evaluating the formal power series
\tau
, by setting the abstract variables
\mathsf{z}
to be (an a priori unknown function)
\mathsf{z}_{x}=\nu(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathsf{z}_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}a}{du^{k}}(u(\mathbf{x}))
. That is, writing
\tau=\tau(\mathsf{z}_{x},(\mathsf{z}_{k})_{k\geq 1};a_{0})
we introduce

(28)

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}).\tau:=\tau(\nu(\mathbf{x}),(\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}a}{du^{k}}(u(\mathbf{x})))_{k\geq 1};a(u(\mathbf{x}))).

Note that this is in line with the motivation given for our model space
\mathsf{T}
, cf. (
3
) and (
4
), where now we use a general base point
\mathbf{x}
rather than fixing the origin arbitrarily.
We remark again that this definition is only formal, as the evaluation of a formal power series is unlikely to converge. Ignoring this issue, we see that such a form
f(\mathbf{x})
clearly has the morphism property
(f(\mathbf{x}).\sigma)(f(\mathbf{x}).\tau)=f(\mathbf{x}).(\sigma\tau)
.
We also emphasize that this reasoning shows that evaluation is the driving principle behind the definition of modelled distributions: it takes elements from the abstract model space to concrete objects.

We now turn our attention to the structure group
\tau\mapsto\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}\tau
and claim that its definition arises naturally from
demanding that the modelled distribution
f(\mathbf{x})
has the following (formal) covariance property:

(29)

f(\mathbf{y}).\tau=f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}\tau

for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and all
\tau\in\mathsf{T}
. Before deriving the action of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}
, we start by giving a motivation for (

) based on demanding the consistency of the local expansion (
22
) across two different base-points, together with the re-expansion property (
10
). In fact, for two points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, using the relation (
22
) first at
\mathbf{y}
and then at

yields

(30)

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle=u-u(\mathbf{y})=u-u(\mathbf{x})-(u(\mathbf{y})-u(\mathbf{x}))=f(\mathbf{x}).(\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}-\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}))=f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{y}},

where we used (
10
) in the last step.
The reader acquainted with regularity structures will notice that Hairer’s definition of a modelled distribution

is a quantification of the defect in (
29
) for
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
close together. Since

describes a formally exact solution

We will now use (

28
) to deduce the action of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}
on
\mathsf{z}_{j}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y}).\mathsf{z}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}a^{(j)}(u(\mathbf{y}))=\sum_{k\geq j}\frac{1}{j!}(u(\mathbf{y})-u(\mathbf{x}))^{k-j}\frac{1}{(k-j)!}a^{(k)}(u(\mathbf{x})).

Inserting (
22
) and using the morphism property of

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y}).\mathsf{z}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq j}\frac{1}{j!}(f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}))^{k-j}\frac{1}{(k-j)!}a^{(k)}(u(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq j}{\binom{k}{j}}(f(\mathbf{x}).\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}))^{k-j}f(\mathbf{x}).\mathsf{z}_{k}=f(\mathbf{x}).\sum_{k\geq j}{\binom{k}{j}}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})^{k-j}\mathsf{z}_{k}.

Since

is linear, we see that ensuring (

(31)

\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}\mathsf{z}_{j}:=\sum_{k\geq j}{\binom{k}{j}}\hat{\Pi}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})^{k-j}\mathsf{z}_{k}.

1.2.
The Renormalized Model

Accepting that some form of counter-term in (
1
) is necessary, we aim to choose it in a minimally intrusive way.
This is in line with the axiomatic approach common in the physics community,
see also
[
27
]
. In particular, we ask that it is scaling wise lower order and respects the symmetries of the SPDE and its solution manifold, which then restricts the possible functional dependence.
Hence in view of
[
8
]
, the appropriate ansatz for a counter term in equation
(
1
) is

(32)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-a(u)\Delta u+h(u)+H(u)\cdot\nabla u=\xi.

The ansatz (
32
) is thus parameterized by the
nonlinearities
h
and
H
that one postulates to be deterministic, i.e., dependent only on the
law but not the realizations of
\xi
.
In making the ansatz (
32
), we have in mind a
\xi
whose law is invariant
under shifts of space-time and implicitly
use symmetry as follows. As the original
nonlinear operator
u\mapsto\partial_{t}u-a(u)\Delta u
does not depend explicitly on space-time, we may assume that the same is true for
h
and
H
.
There is a further symmetry-related reduction: we demand that if the law of
\xi
is invariant under the spatial reflection
x_{i}\mapsto-x_{i}
for

, then the same is true for the solution
u
. Since
u\mapsto\partial_{t}u-a(u)\Delta u

commutes with spatial reflection, this requires
H\equiv 0
. Hence
(
32
) collapses to (
14
).

In fact, there is a final, but crucial symmetry observation related to the (functional)
dependence of the function
h=h[a](u)
of
u\in\mathbb{R}
on the nonlinearity
a
. For this, recall that the solution manifold has the important shift invariance (
2
).
By our principle of minimal intrusiveness regarding the symmetries of the SPDE we therefore assume the following covariance under shifts

(33)

\displaystyle h[a(\cdot+v)](u)=h[a](u+v).

This implies that
h
is determined by a functional
q=h[\cdot](0)
on the space of nonlinearities via

(34)

\displaystyle h[a](v)=q[a(\cdot+v)].

At least heuristically, there is a one-to-one correspondence between functionals

and
\mathsf{S}:=\{\tau\in\mathsf{T}:\ \tau\text{ independent of }\mathsf{z}_{x}\}
.
In particular, (
34
) can be recast for fixed

as

(35)

\displaystyle h(v)=g(v).q,\quad v\in\mathbb{R},

where
\mathsf{S}\ni\tau\mapsto g(v).\tau
acts via

.
On the one hand, (
35
) is a non-truncated version of (

), as can be seen via (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}a(\cdot+v)}{du^{k}}(0)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}a}{du^{k}}(v),\qquad\mbox{for }k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

On the other hand, Taylor’s formula gives for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(u)&=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}(u-u(\mathbf{x}))^{k}(\partial_{u}^{k})|_{u=u(\mathbf{x})}h=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}(u-u(\mathbf{x}))^{k}(\partial_{u}^{k})|_{u=u(\mathbf{x})}g.q\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}(u-u(\mathbf{x}))^{k}g(u(\mathbf{x})).(D^{(0)})^{k}q,\end{split}

where the derivation
D^{(0)}
is the infinitesimal generator of shifts on the algebra
\mathsf{T}
determined via
\partial_{u}g.\tau=g.D^{(0)}\tau
for all
\tau\in\mathsf{T}
.
We remark that

is given by (
46
) as shown in Lemma

Furthermore, by (

for
\tau\in\mathsf{S}
.
Using (

, we therefore expect

\displaystyle h(u)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}(f(\mathbf{x}).\Pi_{\mathbf{x}})^{k}f(\mathbf{x}).(D^{(0)})^{k}q=f(\mathbf{x}).\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}^{k}(D^{(0)})^{k}q.

Following the same steps as in Section
1.1
, we see that if we demand

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-a_{0}\Delta)\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathsf{z}_{k}\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\Delta\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\Pi_{\mathbf{x}}^{k}(D^{(0)})^{k}q+\xi\mathsf{1}=:\Pi^{-}_{\mathbf{x}}.

then

given by (

In particular, keeping in mind that

2.
Model Space and Structure Group

In this section, we introduce the algebraic framework which underlies our local expansion for the solution and is used to quantify our assumptions on the forcing
\xi
.
For a multi-index
\mathfrak{a}=(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{d})
we use the standard notation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}!

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{a}_{1}!\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{d}!,\quad|\mathfrak{a}|:=\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\ldots+\mathfrak{a}_{d},\quad x^{\mathfrak{a}}:=x_{1}^{\mathfrak{a}_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\mathfrak{a}_{d}},

with the convention that
0^{0}=1
.

2.1.
The Model Space

Recall the definition of the model space
\mathsf{T}
as the linear space of formal power series
\sum_{\beta}\tau_{\beta}\mathsf{z}^{\beta}
in the abstract variables
\mathsf{z}=(\mathsf{z}_{x},\mathsf{z}_{1},\mathsf{z}_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. It will be important that

forms an algebra with unit element
\mathsf{1}
defined via
\mathsf{1}(\mathsf{z}):=1
, and given

the product
\sigma\tau
is identified with its coefficients via

(36)

(\sigma\tau)_{\beta}:=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}\sigma_{\beta_{1}}\tau_{\beta_{2}}.

A special role is played by the monomials
\mathsf{z}_{x},\{\mathsf{z}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, and in addition we define
\mathsf{z}_{0}:=a_{0}\mathsf{1}
. It will be convenient to separate the polynomial sector
\bar{\mathsf{T}}
of
\mathsf{T}
from the rest, that is we write

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}=\bar{\mathsf{T}}\oplus\mathsf{T}_{-}

with

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{T}}

\displaystyle:=\{\tau\in\mathsf{T}:\tau_{({\beta_{x}},\beta^{\prime})}=0\quad\mbox{unless}\;{\beta_{x}}\neq 0,\beta^{\prime}=0\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{-}

\displaystyle:=\{\tau\in\mathsf{T}:\tau_{({\beta_{x}},0)}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\;{\beta_{x}}\neq 0\}.

We denote the projection of
\mathsf{T}
onto
\bar{\mathsf{T}}
by
\mathsf{P}
. Notice that in particular
\mathsf{1}\in\mathsf{T}_{-}
, which should be compared with the
\beta=0
constraint of (

), and we warn the reader that we are departing from the notational convention in
[

]
.

2.2.
Homogeneities

We now define a grading of
\mathsf{T}
by assigning a homogeneity to each elementary monomial
\mathsf{z}^{\beta}
, or equivalently to each multi-index
\beta
. Specifically, recalling (
17
) we define an integer-valued function
\beta\mapsto\langle\beta\rangle
by

(37)

\langle\beta\rangle:=|\beta|_{s}+1_{\beta_{x}=0},

cf. (
17
) and use this to define the homogeneity

(38)

|\beta|:=\langle\beta\rangle\alpha+|{\beta_{x}}|.

Remark 2
.

We want to emphasize that we are not making any departures from the traditional homogeneity counting in regularity structures. For example, for
\beta_{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\beta_{x}|=1
we have
|(\beta_{x},0)|=1
to be compatible with (
5
) and
|0|=\alpha
which should be compared with the
\beta=0
constraint of (
8
), keeping in mind
\xi\in C^{\alpha-2}
.

Remark 3
.

The definition can also be motivated by a scaling argument. Indeed, observe that the
C^{\alpha-2}
-norm of
\xi
is invariant under the scaling
\xi(\mathbf{x})\mapsto\tilde{\xi}(\mathbf{x}):=\lambda^{\alpha-2}\xi(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
,
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}:=(\lambda^{-2}t,\lambda^{-1}x)
. Such scaling leaves (
1
) invariant if we define
\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x}):=\lambda^{\alpha}u(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})
and
\tilde{a}(v)=a(\lambda^{-\alpha}v)
. In view of (
3
) and (

), this leads to the rescaled coordinates
\tilde{\mathsf{z}}:=(\lambda^{\alpha-1}\mathsf{z}_{x},\lambda^{-\alpha}\mathsf{z}_{1},\lambda^{-2\alpha}\mathsf{z}_{2},\ldots)
. Thus, for the partial derivatives
\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}
of
\tilde{u}
with respect to
\tilde{\mathsf{z}}
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})=\partial_{\tilde{\mathsf{z}}}^{\beta}|_{\tilde{\mathsf{z}}=0}\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{\alpha+\alpha\sum_{k\geq 1}k\beta(k)+(1-\alpha)|\beta_{x}|}\partial_{\mathsf{z}}^{\beta}|_{\mathsf{z}=0}u(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})=\lambda^{|\beta|}\Pi_{\beta}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}),

which is exactly corresponding to (
37
) in the relevant case
|\beta_{x}|=0,1
.

This assignment of homogeneities naturally generates a finite set of homogeneities

\mathsf{A}:=\big\{|\beta|<2\big\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}\alpha+\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The reader should keep in mind that the least element of
\mathsf{A}
is
\alpha

\beta=0
, cf. Remark

. A distinguished role will also be played by

,
the least homogeneity in
\mathsf{A}
larger than 1. Moreover we note that if
\beta
and

are such that

, then the choice of

implies that
|\beta|_{s}=|\gamma|_{s}
,
\langle\beta\rangle=\langle\gamma\rangle
, and
\beta_{x}=\gamma_{x}
. The notion of homogeneity leads to a grading on
\mathsf{T}
as follows: for each

we define

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\{\tau\in\mathsf{T}:\,\,\tau_{\beta}=0\quad\text{if}\quad|\beta|\neq\delta\},\quad\quad\mathsf{T}_{\geq\delta}:=\bigcup_{|\beta|\geq\delta}\mathsf{T}_{|\beta|},

and analogously

. It will be important to keep in mind how the multiplication of power series in the sense of (

. This is easily seen to be false by considering

Lemma 1
.

Let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
be multi-indices.

(1)

The following identity holds:

(39)

|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}|=\begin{cases}|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha\quad&\text{if}\quad|\beta_{1,x}|\cdot|\beta_{2,x}|=0.\\
|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|\quad&\text{else}\end{cases}

(2)

The following implication holds:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{rl}\sigma&\in\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|}\\
\tau&\in\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{2}|}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\sigma\tau\in\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha}\subset\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|\vee|\beta_{2}|}.

Moreover, if
|\beta_{1,x}|\cdot|\beta_{2,x}|=0
, then

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{rl}\sigma&\in\mathsf{T}_{|\beta_{1}|}\\
\tau&\in\mathsf{T}_{|\beta_{2}|}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\sigma\tau\in\mathsf{T}_{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha}.

Proof.

To see the identity (
39
), notice that

\displaystyle|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}|

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(|\beta_{1}|_{s}+|\beta_{2}|_{s}+1_{\beta_{1,x}+\beta_{2,x}=0}\big)+|\beta_{1,x}|+|\beta_{2,x}|

\displaystyle=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|+\alpha\big(1_{\beta_{1,x}+\beta_{2,x}=0}-1_{\beta_{1,x}=0}-1_{\beta_{2,x}=0}\big).

Hence, if at least one of
\beta_{1,x},\beta_{2,x}
is zero, then
|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}|=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha
and otherwise all indicator functions above vanish and
|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}|=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|
. This establishes (
39
).
We now turn to the implication (
2
) and suppose
\sigma\in\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|}
,
\tau\in\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{2}|}
. We now argue that
(\sigma\tau)_{\gamma}=0
if
|\gamma|<|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha
. Indeed, keeping in mind (
36
), if
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma
, then by (
39
) it holds
|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|\leq|\gamma|+\alpha<|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|
. Hence,
|\gamma_{i}|<|\beta_{i}|
for at least one of

, which implies that
\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}\tau_{\gamma_{2}}=0
, yielding the claim. The inclusion
\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha}\subset\mathsf{T}_{\geq|\beta_{1}|\vee|\beta_{2}|}
follows immediately since all homogeneities in
\mathsf{A}
are at least
\alpha
.
To show (
2
), let
\sigma\in\mathsf{T}_{|\beta_{1}|}
,
\tau\in\mathsf{T}_{|\beta_{2}|}
and consider
(\sigma\tau)_{\gamma}
, where
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
. Then
(\sigma\tau)_{\gamma}=0
unless
|\gamma_{1}|=|\beta_{1}|
and
|\gamma_{2}|=|\beta_{2}|
, and hence
|\gamma_{1,x}|\cdot|\gamma_{2,x}|=|\beta_{1,x}|\cdot|\beta_{2,x}|=0

Thus, by (
39

|\gamma|=|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|-\alpha=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|-\alpha
.
∎

We now define a subgroup

2.4.

\displaystyle g.(\sigma\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta}\nu^{{\beta_{x}}}da(u)^{\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}\sigma_{\beta_{1}}(a(u))\tau_{\beta_{2}}(a(u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\nu^{\beta_{1,x}}\nu^{\beta_{2,x}}da(u)^{\beta_{1}^{\prime}}da(u)^{\beta_{2}^{\prime}}\sigma_{\beta_{1}}(a(u))\tau_{\beta_{2}}(a(u))=\big(g.\sigma\big)\big(g.\tau\big).

is the interaction between differentiation in
(u,\nu)

used in the definition of

, cf. (

Lemma 2
.

For all

and
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
it holds

(56)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}^{k}\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}(g.\tau)=g.D^{(k,\mathfrak{a})}\tau.

Note that it suffices to show (

) for the special cases

and

which read

(57)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}(g.\tau)=g.D^{(0)}\tau,\quad\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}(g.\tau)=g.(D^{(1)})^{\mathfrak{a}}\tau\quad\mbox{for}\;|\mathfrak{a}|=1.

The general case then follows by iteration.
To establish (
57

55
) to extend to a general
\tau
.
Indeed, note that for monomials
\mathsf{z}_{x}
and
\mathsf{z}_{j}
,

, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{u}(g.\mathsf{z}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{(j+1)}(u)}{j!}=(j+1)g.\mathsf{z}_{j+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{as30}}}{{=}}g.D^{(0)}\mathsf{z}_{j},

\displaystyle\partial_{u}(g.\mathsf{z}_{x})

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}\nu=0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{as30}}}{{=}}g.D^{(0)}\mathsf{z}_{x},

as well as

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}(g.\mathsf{z}_{j})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{a^{(j)}(u)}{j!}=0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{as37}}}{{=}}g.(D^{(1)})^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{z}_{j},

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}(g.\mathsf{z}_{x}^{\mathfrak{a}})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}=1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{as37}}}{{=}}g.(D^{(1)})^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{z}_{x}^{\mathfrak{a}}.

is a derivation, cf. (

Similarly,
\partial_{\nu}^{\mathfrak{a}}(g.\tau\tau^{\prime})=g.(D^{(1)})^{\mathfrak{a}}(\tau\tau^{\prime})

\tilde{M}\lesssim[u]_{\eta}^{\frac{\eta+\alpha}{\eta}}+([\nu]_{\eta}^{\prime})^{\frac{\eta+\alpha}{\eta}}+[\Pi]\delta^{-\eta}.

We now consider the remaining contributions to (
146

\displaystyle(\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}f_{\eta}\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}+\|\nu\|_{1}^{\prime})[\Pi]

\displaystyle\lesssim([u]_{\eta-\alpha}+[\nu]_{\eta-\alpha}^{\prime}+\delta^{-(\eta-\alpha)}+[u]_{\eta}^{\frac{1}{\eta}})[\Pi]

\displaystyle\lesssim\big([u]_{\eta}+[\nu]_{\eta-\alpha}^{\prime}\big)^{\frac{\eta-\alpha}{\eta}}[\Pi]+\epsilon^{\eta}[u]_{\eta}+\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-1}{\eta}}[\Pi]^{\frac{\eta}{\eta-1}}+\delta^{-\eta}[\Pi]

\displaystyle[u]_{\eta}+[\nu]_{\eta}^{\prime}

\displaystyle[u]_{\kappa}

\displaystyle\lesssim[u]_{\eta}^{\frac{\kappa}{\eta}}\big(\|u\|+[\Pi]\big)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\eta}}+\big(\|u\|+[\Pi]\big)(1\vee[\Pi]^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha}})

If

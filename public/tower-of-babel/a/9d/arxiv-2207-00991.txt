On weak(measure valued)-strong uniqueness for Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with Dirichlet boundary condition
Nilasis Chaudhuri

Abstract
In this paper our goal is to define a measure valued solution of compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier system for a heat conducting fluid with Dirichlet boundary condition for temperature in a bounded domain. The definition is based on the weak formulation of entropy inequality and ballistic energy inequality. Moreover, we obtain the weak(measure valued)-strong uniqueness property of this solution with the help of relative energy.

Introduction

We consider the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system in the time-space cylinder
Q=(0,T)\times\Omega
, where
T>0
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d=2
or
3
. The time evolution of the
density

\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
,
velocity

{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
and the
absolute temperature

\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
of a compressible, viscous and heat conducting fluid is given by the following system of field equations which describes conservation of mass, momentum and internal energy:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\text{div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+\text{div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\text{div}_{x}{\mathbb{S}}+\varrho{\bf g},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+\text{div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+\text{div}_{x}{\bf q}={\mathbb{S}}\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}-p(\varrho,\vartheta)\text{div}_{x}{\bf u},

(1.3)

where
{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u})
,
p=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the pressure and
e=e(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the internal energy and
{\bf g}
is an external force. The pressure and the internal energy is interrelated by means of
Gibbs’ equation

\displaystyle\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right),

(1.4)

where
s=s(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the
entropy
and
D=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial\vartheta}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

We assume the fluid is Newtonian, hence the
viscous stress tensor
({\mathbb{S}})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}\mathbf{u})=\mu(\varrho,\vartheta)\bigg(\frac{\nabla_{x}\mathbf{u}+\nabla_{x}^{T}\mathbf{u}}{2}-\frac{1}{d}(\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{I}\bigg)+\lambda(\varrho,\vartheta)(\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{I},

(1.5)

where
\mu
is the
shear viscosity
coefficient and
\lambda
is the
bulk viscosity
coefficient with
\mu>0
and
\lambda\geq 0
. The
heat flux

{\bf q}
is given by the
Fourier law
,

\displaystyle{\bf q}(\varrho,\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=-\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta,

(1.6)

where
\kappa
is the
heat conductivity
coefficient.
Since we consider the bounded domain
\Omega
, our goal is to discuss the solvability of the initial-boundary value problem for the system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) endowed with the constitutive relations (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) where we consider

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\vartheta|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}>0.

(1.8)

2
Measure valued solution

2.1
Weak formulation: Revisit

At first we quickly recall the weak formulation described in Feireisl and Novotný
[
16
, Chapter 2]
. Although it deals with a no-flux boundary condition for temperature, i.e.

\displaystyle{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(2.1)

The weak formulation of the problem depends on the weak formulation of continuity equation, momentum equation and replaces the
internal energy equation
(
1.3
) by the
entropy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta))+\text{div}_{x}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+\text{div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\varrho,\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\right)\\
\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\varrho,\vartheta,{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}):{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\varrho,\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right),\end{split}

along with the weak form of
total energy balance
:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\;\text{d}t\,}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e\right]\;\!\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf g}\;\!\text{d}x.

(2.2)

Instead of boundary condition (
2.1
), if we consider inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature (
1.8
), we need to proceed as prescribed
[
6
, Section 2.4]
. In this case, assuming all quantities in consideration are smooth, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e\right]+\text{div}_{x}\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e+p(\varrho,\vartheta)\right]{\bf u}\right)+\text{div}_{x}{\bf q}=\text{div}_{x}\left({\mathbb{S}}{\bf u}\right)+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf g},

(2.3)

and consequently

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\;\text{d}t\,}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e\right]\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}\ \text{d}\sigma_{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf g}\;\!\text{d}x

(2.4)

where
\sigma_{x}
is the surface measure on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Therefore, the total energy balance is unavailable. But, considering a smooth function
\tilde{\vartheta}
such that

\tilde{\vartheta}>0\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\text{ with }\tilde{\vartheta}=\vartheta_{B}\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

and multiplying the entropy inequality (
2.1
) by
\tilde{\vartheta}
along with performing the integrating by parts formula, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\text{d}}{\;\text{d}t\,}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\partial\Omega}{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}\,\text{d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\;\!\text{d}x.

(2.5)

Adding (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we obtain the
ballistic energy inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\;\text{d}t\,}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right]\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\;\!\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf g}\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\;\!\text{d}x.

We recall that for some smooth function
\widetilde{\Theta}
, the ballistic energy is denoted by
H_{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\varrho,\vartheta)
and it reads as

H_{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho[e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}s(\varrho,\vartheta)].

Therefore we consider the weak formulation with ballistic energy inequality instead of energy balance. In this paper our goal is to provide a suitable definition of a measure valued solution based on the above discussion for boundary condition (
1.8
). To define it, we take motivation from Březina, Feireisl and Novotný
[
4
]
that covers the the boundary condition (
2.1
). This definition will give in terms of Young measure and suitable defect measures. For a simpler consideration, from now on we consider
{\bf g}=0
.

2.1.1
Phase space and Young measure

A natural candidate for the phase space is given by the state variables
[\varrho,{\bf u},\vartheta]
. Since we are looking for a more general class of solutions and
\nabla_{x}{\bf u}
and
\nabla_{x}\vartheta
are present in the system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), thus gradient of velocity and temperature have been included along with the natural choices. Hence a proper phase space is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{[\varrho,{\bf u},\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{u}},{\bf D}_{\vartheta}]\big|\varrho\in[0,\infty),\;\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\vartheta\in[0,\infty),\;\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\text{sym}},\;{\bf D}_{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

(2.6)

We consider a Young measure
\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{V}\equiv\{\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\}_{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{weak-(*)}}\big((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})\big),

(2.7)

2.1.2
Initial data

Let
\mathcal{V}_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{weak-(*)}}\big(\Omega;\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\big)
such that

\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0},\vartheta_{0})|\varrho_{0}\in[0,\infty),\,{\bf u}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\vartheta_{0}\in[0,\infty)\}
with finite energy, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{0,x};\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}(0,x)\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x<\infty

(2.8)

for each
0<\widetilde{\Theta}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
with
\widetilde{\Theta}=\vartheta_{B}
on
\partial\Omega
.

2.1.3
Compatibility relations for the Young measure

We consider a very general phase space for the Young measure. It satisfies the following compatibility conditions.

•

Velocity compatibility:
The identity

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};{\bf u}\right\rangle\cdot\text{div}_{x}{\mathbb{T}}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};{\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

(2.9)

holds for any
{\mathbb{T}}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q_{T}};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\text{sym}})
.

•

Temperature compatibility:
The identity

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\vartheta-\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle\text{div}_{x}\psi\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};{\bf D}_{\vartheta}-\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle\psi\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

(2.10)

holds for any
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
and
\widetilde{\Theta}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
with
\widetilde{\Theta}=\vartheta_{B}
on
\partial\Omega
.

2.1.4
Field equations with defect

•

Equation of continuity:
For a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
and
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega})
, the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\tau,x};\varrho\rangle\psi(\tau,\cdot)\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{0,x};\varrho\rangle\psi(0,\cdot)\;\!\text{d}x\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho\rangle\partial_{t}\psi+\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho{\bf u}\rangle\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi\big]\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.\end{split}

(2.11)

•

Momentum equation:
There exists a
measure

r^{M}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{weak-(*)}}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}))
such that for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
and every
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\tau,x};\varrho{\bf u}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau,\cdot)\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{0,x};\varrho{\bf u}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}(0,\cdot)\;\!\text{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho{\bf u}\rangle\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho{\bf u}\otimes\mathbf{u})\rangle:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};p(\varrho,\vartheta)\rangle\text{div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\big]\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mathbb{S}(\varrho,\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{v}})\rangle:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,+\int_{0}^{\tau}\langle r^{M};\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\{\mathcal{M}(\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),C(\bar{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\}}\;\text{d}t\,.\end{split}

(2.12)

•

Entropy inequality:
For a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
and
0\leq\phi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T]\times{\Omega})
, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\tau,x};\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\rangle\phi(\tau,x)\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{0,x};\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\rangle\phi(0,x)\;\!\text{d}x\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\rangle\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}-\frac{\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\vartheta}{\bf D}_{\vartheta}\right\rangle\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left({\mathbb{S}}(\varrho,\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}+\frac{\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\vartheta}|{\bf D}_{\vartheta}|^{2}\right)\right\rangle\phi(t,x)\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\end{split}

(2.13)

•

Ballistic energy inequality:
For any
\widetilde{\Theta}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}),\,\widetilde{\Theta}>0,\;\widetilde{\Theta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}
, there exists a
dissipation defect

\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T),\;\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\geq 0,

and the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left({\mathbb{S}}(\varrho,\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}+\frac{\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\vartheta}|{\bf D}_{\vartheta}|^{2}\right)\right\rangle\widetilde{\Theta}(t,x)\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,+\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\tau)\\

&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\rangle\partial_{t}\widetilde{\Theta}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\rangle\cdot\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\Theta}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\frac{\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\mathbf{D}_{\vartheta}\right\rangle\cdot\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\Theta}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.\end{split}

(2.14)

3
Relative energy inequality

Following Feireisl and Novotný in
[
16
, Chapter 9]
, we consider the relative energy with the help of Ballistic energy and it is given by

\displaystyle E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\,|\,\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta},\tilde{{\bf u}})=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|^{2}+\left(H_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\varrho,\vartheta)-\frac{\partial H_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta})}{\partial\varrho}(\varrho-\tilde{\varrho})-H_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta})\right),

where
(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta},\tilde{{\bf u}})
are smooth functions such that they satisfy

\displaystyle\tilde{\varrho}>0,\tilde{{\bf u}}\text{ with }\tilde{{\bf u}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\text{ and }\tilde{\vartheta}>0\text{ with }\tilde{\vartheta}|_{\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}.

(3.1)

As observed in
[
18
, Section 1.2]
, if the relative energy is interpreted in terms of the conservative entropy variables
(\varrho,S=\varrho s,{\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u})
, represents a Bregman distance/divergence associated with the energy functional

E(\varrho,S,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+\varrho e(\varrho,S).

To obtain that the reltive enrgy functional is non-negative, Indeed we need the
hypothesis of thermodynamic stability
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\varrho}>0,\;\frac{\partial e(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}>0,

(3.2)

which in turn yields the convexity of the internal energy
\varrho e(\varrho,S)
with respect to the variables
(\varrho,S)
. In addition,

\frac{\partial(\varrho e(\varrho,S))}{\partial\varrho}=e-\vartheta s+\frac{p}{\varrho},\ \frac{\partial(\varrho e(\varrho,S))}{\partial S}=\vartheta.

(3.3)

Thus the relative energy expressed in the conservative entropy variable may be interpreted as

At first, we introduce a cut-off function

Proof.

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\colon\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{1}(\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta})\left({\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-\frac{\vartheta}{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{0}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left|{\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-\frac{\vartheta}{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{1}\tilde{\vartheta}\left|{\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\colon\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{1}(\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta})\left({\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu_{1}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\colon\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\frac{1}{\tilde{\vartheta}}\mu_{1}(\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta})^{2}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.

Using Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\colon\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{1}(\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta})\left({\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\mu_{1}\tilde{\vartheta}\left|{\mathbb{D}}_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\bf u}})-{\mathbb{D}}_{0}(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\right|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+C(\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\bf u}})\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left[\left|\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta}\right|^{2}\right]_{\text{ess}}+\left[1+\varrho e_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta)\right]_{\text{res}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.

Similarly, we derive the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\tilde{\vartheta})\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}\cdot\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};(\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta})\left(\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}-\frac{{\bf D}_{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left|\frac{{\bf D}_{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}-\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}\right|^{2}\right\rangle\!\!\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,+C(\epsilon,\tilde{\vartheta})\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left|\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta}\right|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}\cdot\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};(\kappa(\varrho,\vartheta)-\kappa(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta}))\left(\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}-\frac{{\bf D}_{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}\cdot\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};(\vartheta^{\beta}-\tilde{\vartheta}^{\beta})\left(\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}-\frac{{\bf D}_{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\right)\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};(1+\vartheta^{\beta})\left|\frac{{\bf D}_{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}-\frac{\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}}{\tilde{\vartheta}}\right|^{2}\right\rangle\!\!\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+C(\epsilon,\tilde{\vartheta})\bigg(\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};[|\vartheta-\tilde{\vartheta}|^{2}]_{\text{ess}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};[1+\vartheta^{\beta}]_{\text{res}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\bigg).

Since
\beta\leq 2
, the second term s controlled by radiation pressure
p_{R}
.
Therefore, our choice of
p
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left[\vartheta^{2}+|p(\varrho,\vartheta)|+|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|+\varrho|s(\varrho,\vartheta)|\;|{\bf u}|\right]_{\text{res}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}_{mv}(t)\;\text{d}t\,+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left[\varrho|s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)|^{2}\;+\varrho s_{R}|{\bf u}|\right]_{\text{res}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.

Furthermore, for any
\epsilon>0
, we get the following inequality

\displaystyle\varrho s_{R}|{\bf u}|\leq C(\tilde{{\bf u}})(\vartheta|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|+\varrho s_{R})\leq C(\tilde{{\bf u}})\left(\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\vartheta^{2}+\epsilon|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|^{2}+\varrho s_{R}\right).

(4.17)

On the other hand, the structural assumption on
p_{M}
and
S_{M}
gives us

\displaystyle\varrho|s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)|^{2}\leq C(1+\varrho+\varrho e_{M}).

(4.18)

Finally, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left[\vartheta+|p(\varrho,\vartheta)|+|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|+\varrho|s(\varrho,\vartheta)|\;|{\bf u}|\right]_{\text{res}}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}_{mv}(t)\;\text{d}t\,+2\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};|{\bf u}-\tilde{{\bf u}}|^{2}\right\rangle\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,.

Now proper choice of
\epsilon
, generalized Korn-Poincaré inequality and Grönwall’s argument gives us the desired result.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

We prove the weak strong uniqueness result for
\kappa(\vartheta)=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}\vartheta^{\beta}
,
0\leq\beta\leq 2
. This method does not allow us to prove the weak (measure valued)-strong uniqueness when
\beta>2
.

5
Comments on measure valued solution

We try to obtain a priori bounds for the system in which the pressure law follows (
4.8
) with (
4.9
) and (
4.15
) and the transport coefficients
\mu,\;\lambda
and
\kappa
follow

\displaystyle\begin{split}0<\underline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta\right)&\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta\right),\ |\mu^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq c\ \mbox{for all}\ \vartheta\geq 0,\\
0&\leq\eta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}\left(1+\vartheta\right),\\
0<\underline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right)&\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right),\ \beta\geq 2.\end{split}

(5.1)

We also invoke the third law of thermodynamics (
4.13
).

5.1
A priori estimate

Let us first recall the ballistic energy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\text{d}}{\;\text{d}t\,}&\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right]\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\;\!\text{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf g}\;\!\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\vartheta}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right)+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\;\!\text{d}x,\end{split}

(5.2)

where
\tilde{\vartheta}
is a smooth function such that

\tilde{\vartheta}>0\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega\text{ with }\tilde{\vartheta}=\vartheta_{B}\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

In order to control the last integral in (
5.2
), we proceed analogously as in
[
6
, Section 4.1]
. Hence, we consider the extension
\tilde{\vartheta}
to be the unique solution of the Laplace equation

\Delta\tilde{\vartheta}(\tau,\cdot)=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ \tilde{\vartheta}(\tau,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=\vartheta_{B}\ \mbox{for any}\ \tau\in[0,T].

The maximum principle for the Laplace equation gives

\min_{[0,T]\times\partial\Omega}\vartheta_{B}\leq\tilde{\vartheta}(t,x)\leq\max_{[0,T]\times\partial\Omega}\vartheta_{B}\ \mbox{for any}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega.

Let us denote this particular extension by
\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}
.
At first we note that

-\int_{\Omega}\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\nabla_{x}\vartheta\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}K(\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}K(\vartheta_{B})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\cdot{\bf n},

where
K^{\prime}(\vartheta)=\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}
.
Next, as
\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}
is continuously differentiable in time, we obtain

-\int_{\Omega}\varrho s\partial_{t}\vartheta_{B}\;\!\text{d}x\leq\left[(1+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\;\!\text{d}x\right].

Therefore, for the term

-\int_{\Omega}\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta B}\;\!\text{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x-{2a}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x,

using the inequality in
[
17
, Section 4, formula (4.6)]
, we have

\varrho\left|\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\right|\leq\left(\varrho+\varrho|\log(\varrho)|+\varrho[\log(\vartheta)]^{+}\right)\ \mbox{for any}\ \varrho\geq 0,\ \vartheta\geq 0.

Consequently, it yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathcal{S}^{2}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\;\!\text{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varrho\vartheta\;\!\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{2}\;\!\text{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\;\!\text{d}x\right).

For the other term, we notice that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\vartheta{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\widehat{\vartheta_{B}}\;\!\text{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{d})}+c(\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{2}\;\!\text{d}x.

From the definition of the radiation pressure, we conclude

\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{2}\;\!\text{d}x\leq C(\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}(\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s)\;\!\text{d}x+c(\vartheta_{B})+\epsilon\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{d})}.

Therefore, chossing
\epsilon
properly, we are able to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)(\tau,\cdot)\;\!\text{d}x

\displaystyle+\inf_{[0,T]\times\partial\Omega}\{\vartheta_{B}\}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{d})}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\right)\;\!\text{d}x\;\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+\varrho_{0}e(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0})-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho_{0}s(\varrho_{0},\vartheta_{0})\right)\;\!\text{d}x

\displaystyle+c(\vartheta_{B})\left[1+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s\right)\;\!\text{d}x\;\;\text{d}t\,\right].

Now we can use Grönwall’s argument to deduce a priori estimate for
\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s
.

Moreover for this particular pressure law (
4.8
) with (
4.9
) and (
4.15
) along with the third law of thermodynamics (
4.13
), following Feireisl and Březina
[
5
]
, we are able to conclude that

\displaystyle\text{ess}\sup_{(0,T)}\|\varrho s\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\text{ess}\sup_{(0,T)}\|\varrho s{\bf u}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C

for some
q>1
. Although they consider only for the molecular pressure, the radiation pressure case is quite straight forward. Therefore we obtain the following a priori estimate :

\displaystyle\text{ess}\sup_{(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho+\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+|\varrho|s(\varrho,\vartheta)|^{q}|\right]\;\!\text{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\underline{\mu}\left(1+\frac{1}{\vartheta}\right)\left|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{T}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{d}\text{div}_{x}{\bf u}\right|^{2}+\frac{\lambda(\vartheta)}{2\vartheta}|\text{div}_{x}{\bf u}|^{2}\right]\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\underline{\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{\vartheta^{2}}+\vartheta^{\beta-2}\right)|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\leq C(\vartheta_{B}),

for some
q>1
.

5.2
Comments on compatibility conditions

Now suppose we assume a young measure
\mathcal{V}
is generated by a family of sequences
(\varrho_{\epsilon},{\bf u}_{\epsilon},{\mathbb{D}}_{x}{\bf u}_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\epsilon})_{\{\epsilon>0\}}
that satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{ess}\sup_{(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{\epsilon}+\varrho_{\epsilon}|{\bf u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\varrho_{\epsilon}e(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})+|\varrho_{\epsilon}|s(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})|^{q}|\right]\;\!\text{d}x\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\mu(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})}{2\vartheta_{\epsilon}}\left|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{\epsilon}+\nabla_{x}^{T}{\bf u}_{\epsilon}-\frac{2}{d}\text{div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{\mu(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})}{2\vartheta_{\epsilon}}|\text{div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\right]\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})|\log\vartheta_{\epsilon}|^{2}\;\!\text{d}x\;\text{d}t\,\leq C,\end{split}

(5.3)

uniformly with respect to
\epsilon
and
\mu,\lambda\text{ and }\kappa
follows (
5.1
) and
q>1
. Clearly the bound (
5.3
) is motivated from the discussion we have in Section
5.1
. Then following
[
4
, Section 2.4]
, we are able to deduce the velocity and temperature compatibility.
Also, Generalized Korn-Poincaré inequality can be derived in the similar way.

Defect measures and its compatibility

The bound (
5.3
), gives an uniform estimate of
\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\epsilon}|\mathbf{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})-\widetilde{\Theta}\varrho s(\varrho_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon})\right)_{\epsilon>0}
with respect to
\epsilon
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
.
We denote the weak* limit of in
L^{\infty}_{\text{weak-(*)}}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(\Omega))
by

\overline{\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)}.

.Next, we define the corresponding
defect measure
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}=\overline{\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)}-\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\widetilde{\Theta}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)\right\rangle.

Now from the estimate (
5.3
) for entropy, we are able to conclude

\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\right\rangle=\overline{\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)}\text{for a.e.}(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega

where
\overline{\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)}
is a weak limit of
\varrho_{\epsilon}s_{\epsilon}
.
This implies
\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}
is independent of
\widetilde{\Theta}
and eventually we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\overline{\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)}-\left\langle\mathcal{V}_{t,x};\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)\right)\right\rangle.

From the comparison lemma for defect measures in Feireisl et. al
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can claim the compatibility of defects (
2.15
), since
r^{M}
is only related to the term
\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}+p(\varrho,\vartheta){\mathbb{I}}
. Therefore, this particular pressure law (
4.8
) allows us to have weak convergence for the terms
\varrho s
and
\varrho s{\bf u}
in some
L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))
, for some
q>1
. Hence, we avoid defect measure for entropy inequality and eventually for ballistic energy inequality.

Concluding remarks

Here we briefly discuss the possible extensions and limitations of the problem:

•

We have given no proof of the existence of a measure valued solution. One way to prove existence is to use
consistent approximations
. By consistent approximation, we mean an approximated system of the original system with error terms that vanish in the limiting case. The approximate solutions with some uniform bounds of state variables generate a Young measure, which is eventually a measure valued solution of the system. For a detailed discussion, see
[
15
, Chapter 5, Section 5.3.1]
.

•

Another way to construct measure valued solution is to consider it as a weak limit of weak solutions. From
[
6
]
, we know that there exists a weak solution for

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{\beta}\right),\ \beta>6,

\displaystyle p_{R}=a\vartheta^{4},

Therefore, at least for system (
1.1
)-(
1.8
) with (
4.14
) and pressure following (
4.9
), (
4.15
), we expect to obtain a measure valued solution as a suitable limit of this weak solutions. If we consider a modified radiation pressure and the heat conductivity coefficient as

\displaystyle p_{\epsilon,R}=a\vartheta^{2}+\epsilon\vartheta^{4}

and

\displaystyle\kappa_{\epsilon}(\vartheta)=1+\vartheta^{\beta}+\epsilon\vartheta^{\gamma},\text{ with }\beta\leq 2\text{ and }\gamma>6.

Then one can expect to generate a measure value solution by sequence of weak solutions
\{\varrho_{\epsilon},{\bf u}_{\epsilon},\vartheta_{\epsilon}\}_{\{\epsilon>0\}}
.

•

Navier-slip boundary condition for velocity: Instead of boundary condition (
1.7
), we consider the Navier-Slip boundary condition

\displaystyle{\bf u}\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\cdot\mathbf{n}]_{\text{tan}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

We can provide a similar definition by modifying the Korn-Poincare inequality suitably, see
[
7
, Section 1.3.6]
. A similar weak(measure valued)-strong uniqueness result is expected to be true.

•

Limitation with radiation pressure following Stefan-Boltzman law: Unfortunately, we are not able to prove our results for the radiation pressure following Stefan-Boltzman law
p_{R}(\vartheta)=a\vartheta^{4}
with
a>0
. The main difficulty is to deal the term
\left[\varrho s_{R}|{\bf u}|\right]_{\text{res}}
. Following Feireisl
[
19
]
, we notice that, the estimation of the term needs certain Sobolev embedding which is missing in our definition. Our choice of
p_{R}
in (
4.15
) is motivated from the models of Neutron star, see Lattimer et al.
[
22
]
.

Subgraph densities and scaling limits of random graphs with a prescribed modular decomposition
Théo Lenoir

Abstract
. We consider large uniform labeled random graphs in different classes with prescribed decorations in their modular decomposition. Our main result is the estimation of the number of copies of every graph as an induced subgraph. As a consequence, we obtain the convergence of a uniform random graph in such classes to a Brownian limit object in the space of graphons. Our proofs rely on combinatorial arguments, computing generating series using the symbolic method and deriving asymptotics using singularity analysis.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

This article lays at the interface of graph theory and combinatorial probability.

The first motivation comes from modular decomposition, which is a standard tool in graph theory. This notion will be reviewed in a comprehensive way in
Section

2
. Roughly speaking, it is a decomposition of a graph into disjoint subsets of vertices called
modules
. The modules can be thought of as generalizations of connected components. Since a module can contain a proper module, the modular decomposition is a recursive decomposition of a graph. It is analogous to prime factor decomposition of integers: graphs without trivial modules cannot be decomposed, and thus will be called prime for the modular decomposition.

From an algorithmic perspective, the modular decomposition can be computed in linear time
[
9
]
, making it an essential tool for quickly solving various problems such as determining belonging to several graph classes (
e.g.
cographs,
P_{4}
-sparse graphs, permutations graphs, etc). We refer the readers to
[
8
]
and
[
13
]
for more details.

The study of modular decomposition from a probabilistic point of view started very recently. The case with no prime graphs,
i.e.
cographs, has been studied simultaneously in
[
5
]
and
[
20
]
. In a somewhat different context, the author
[
16
]
used modular decomposition to study the case of graphs with few
P_{4}
’s (for
k\geq 2
,
P_{k}
denotes the graph consisting of a line with
k
vertices). The aim of this article is to generalize these results: we will study the structure of large typical random graphs defined by a set of prime graphs allowed in its modular decomposition.

The second motivation is the study of classes of graphs with forbidden induced subgraphs. Indeed, our results can be interpreted in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs, as the interdiction of some prime induced subgraphs is heavily connected to the modular decomposition. Examples of classes covered by our results include those with a finite number of prime induced subgraphs allowed, or classes where all prime induced subgraphs are lines.

The asymptotic study of random graphs has been a well-established subject of research for several decades. Depending on the graph models and graph parameters of interest, several notions of limit can be considered.

•

A first point of view consists in studying the local limit of the graph around a typical vertex. This gives information on local neighborhoods (
e.g.
degrees). See
[
15
]
for more details.

•

Alternatively, we can adopt a global viewpoint, in at least two very different frameworks.

–

We may consider the scaling limit of graphs viewed as metric spaces, by assigning distances to edges. This gives estimates of typical distances or diameters. In this context, a universal object arises: the Continuum Random Tree (CRT). For examples, refer to
[
2
,
1
,
19
]
.

–

Instead, we may consider occurrences of induced subgraphs, the proper framework for which is graphons. Introduced in
[
7
]
, graphon convergence can be seen as convergence of renormalized adjacency matrices for the so-called cut metric (a good reference on graphon theory is
[
17
]
). This sometimes also gives estimation on typical degrees
[
5
]
, on extremal statistics (cliques and independent sets as in
[
4
]
), or on the growth rate of classes of graphs
[
14
]
.

The last two frameworks answer very different questions and are adapted respectively to sparse graphs (graphs with a linear number of edges) and dense graphs (graphs with a quadratic number of edges).

1.2.
Main results

For a set of prime graphs
\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
be the set of graphs
G
such that every prime subgraph in the modular decomposition of
G
is in
\mathcal{P}
: the aim of this article is to study the structure of a uniform graph of size
n
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
when
n
tends towards
+\infty
. We only consider the case where
\mathcal{P}
is stable by automorphism. Let
P(z)=\sum\limits_{H\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{z^{|H|}}{|H|!}
be the exponential generating function associated to a given set
\mathcal{P}
and let
R_{0}\in[0,+\infty]
be the radius of convergence of
P
. Set
\Lambda(w):=P(\exp(w)-1)+\exp(w)-1-w
,
\Lambda
is a power series with nonnegative coefficients. An important condition which will be crucial on
\Lambda
is the following:

Condition (C)
.

R_{0}>0
and
\Lambda^{\prime}\left(\log(1+R_{0})\right)>1

(By convention, we set
\Lambda^{\prime}\left(\log(1+R_{0})\right)=+\infty
if the series
\Lambda^{\prime}
diverges at
\log(1+R_{0})
.)
This condition implies that the number of graphs of size
n
in
\mathcal{P}
does not grow too fast, see
Section

1.3
for more details.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
be a graph of size
n
taken uniformly at random in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
. For any graph
H
, denote by
\mathrm{Occ}_{H}(\mathbf{G}^{(n)})
the number of labeled induced subgraphs of
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
isomorphic to
H
. Our first contribution is the estimation of the expected number of copies of
H
in
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
,
i.e.

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Occ}_{H}(\mathbf{G}^{(n)})]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of prime graphs stable by automorphism such that Condition (C) holds. Let
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
be a graph of size
n
taken uniformly at random in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Then for every graph
H
there exists
K_{H}\geq 0
such that:

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Occ}_{H}(\mathbf{G}^{(n)})]\sim K_{H}n^{|H|-\beta(H)}

where the constant
K_{H}
is given in
6.2
and
\beta(H)
is defined in
Eq.

1
.

Let us briefly comment on the theorem.

•

The quantity
\beta(H)
is nonnegative, does not depend on
\mathcal{P}
, and is equal to
0
if and only if
H
is a cograph. Thus at fixed size, the graphs
H
that appear the most as induced subgraphs of
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
are the cographs. Informally, the quantity
\beta(H)
can be thought of how far
H
is from cographs.

•

We have that
K_{H}>0
if and only if each prime subgraph in the modular decomposition of
H
is an induced subgraph of an element of
\mathcal{P}
. This criterion will follow from the expression of
K_{H}
given in
6.2
.

•

Applying
1.1
with the graph
H
consisting of a single edge, we get that
\mathbb{E}[\#\text{edges}\ \text{of}\ \mathbf{G}^{(n)}]=\Theta(n^{2})
: thus
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
is in the dense regime.

1.1
has an important consequence in terms of scaling limit: since we are in the dense regime, we use the framework of graphons. Graphon convergence is equivalent to the joint convergence of subgraphs density. More formally, Diaconis and Janson provided in
[
10
]
a criterion for the convergence of random graphs in the sense of graphons: the convergence of a family
(\mathbf{H}^{(n)})_{n\geq 1}
of random graphs is characterized by the convergence in distribution of
\frac{\mathrm{Occ}_{H}(\mathbf{H}^{(n)})}{n^{|H|}}
for every finite graph
H
. Therefore
1.1
ensures that
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
has a limit in the sense of graphons. All the needed materials for the convergence of graphons will be presented in
Section

3
.

For the case of cographs (
i.e.

\mathcal{P}=\varnothing
), studied simultaneously in
[
5
]
and
[
20
]
, the authors exhibit a Brownian limit object for a uniform cograph, called the Brownian cographon, which can be explicitly constructed from the Brownian excursion.

Our second contribution is that, for all sets
\mathcal{P}
satisfying Condition (C), the scaling limit is a one parameter deformation of the Brownian cographon. This answers partially a question in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
be a graph of size
n
taken uniformly at random in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Under Condition (C),
\mathbf{G}^{(n)}
converges in distribution towards
\mathbf{W}^{p}
in the sense of graphons, where
\mathbf{W}^{p}
is the Brownian cographon of parameter
p
, and
p
is a parameter in
[0,1]
depending on
\mathcal{P}
whose expression is given in
Eq.

23
.

This theorem shows
\mathbf{W}^{p}
for various
p\neq 1/2
as a scaling limit of natural combinatorial objects.

1.3.
Discussion on Condition (C)

First observe that Condition (C) is verified in many situations, for example when:

•

\mathcal{P}
is finite,
i.e.

P
is a polynomial;

•

P
has a radius of convergence greater than
1
;

•

P^{\prime}
diverges in
R_{0}
;

•

the number of graphs in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
grows as
Bn!R^{n}n^{a}
with
B,R>0
and
a\geq-2
;

•

P
is rational.

As we will see in
4.12
, Condition (C) implies that the graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
all share the universal exponent
-3/2
in the sense that the number of graphs of size
n
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
is asymptotically equivalent to

D\frac{n!}{R^{n}n^{3/2}},

with
D>0
and
R>0
explicit constants depending on
\mathcal{P}
, as proved in
4.12
. All the details on the exponential generating functions and asymptotics will be given in
Section

4
.

Let us illustrate the connection between
1.2
and our initial motivation for graphs with forbidden induced subgraphs.

•

The case where all but finitely many prime induced subgraphs are forbidden corresponds to a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
where
\mathcal{P}
is finite thus Condition (C) is clearly verified.

•

The case where all prime subgraphs except lines are forbidden corresponds to the family of graphs
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of lines of size at least
4
. Thus
P=\frac{z^{4}}{2(1-z)}
and Condition (C) is verified. The parameter
p
of
1.2
is approximately
0.288
.

This work can be seen as the counterpart for graphs to the study of limits of substitution-closed permutations in
[
6
]
. However multiple additional difficulties (graph automorphisms, encoding of graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
by
non
-plane trees through the modular decomposition) arise, making the enumeration of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
more involved.

Condition (C) is necessary in
1.2
in the sense that if it is not verified, other behaviors can appear: under other general assumptions on the generating series, we can get a very different scaling limit where each induced subgraph may appear in the limiting object. In particular,
\mathbb{G}_{n}
does not converge to the Brownian cographon.

1.4.
Proof strategy

Proofs are essentially combinatorial: we use the tree encoding of the modular decomposition to obtain exact enumerations for a large family of graph classes. We exploit those enumerative results with tools from analytic combinatorics to get asymptotic estimates like the ones of the number of graphs of size
n
in each class.

The most challenging part of the proofs is the one of
5.8
, giving the combinatorial decomposition of graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
with a given induced subgraph. This theorem is the key theorem to get the needed asymptotic estimates.

2.1.
Labeled graphs

In the following all the graphs considered are simple and finite. Each time a graph
G
is defined, we denote by
V
its set of vertices and
E
its set of edges. Whenever there is an ambiguity, we denote by
V_{G}
(resp.

) the set of vertices (resp. edges) of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
G=(V,E)
is a
weakly-labeled graph
if every element of
V
has a distinct label in
\mathbb{N}
and that
G=(V,E)
is a
labeled graph
if every element of
V
has a distinct label in
\{1,\dots,|V|\}
.

The
size of a graph

G
, denoted by
|G|
, is its number of vertices.

The
minimum of a graph

G
, denoted
\mathrm{min}(G)
, is the minimal label of its vertices.

In the following, every graph is labeled, otherwise we mention explicitly that the graph is weakly-labeled.

Remark
.

We do not identify a vertex with its label. A vertex of label
i
is denoted
v_{i}
. The label of a vertex
v
is denoted
\ell(v)
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any weakly-labeled object (graph or tree) of size
n
, we call

the operation that reduces its labels to the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
while preserving the relative order of the labels.

For example if
G
labels
2,4,12,63
then the reduced version of

is a copy of
G
in which
2,4,12,63
are respectively replaced by
1,2,3,4
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\pi
be a permutation of
\{1,\dots,|G|\}
. The
\pi
-relabeling
of

is the graph

such that:

•

V_{G^{\prime}}=V_{G}

•

for every vertex
v
in
V_{G^{\prime}}
, we replace the label of the leaf
v
by
\pi(\ell(v))
.

We write

if

and there exists a permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\dots,|G|\}
such that

is isomorphic to the
\pi
-relabeling of

.

Note that

Definition 2.4

(Induced subgraph)
.

Let

be a graph,

a positive integer and

a partial injection from the set of labels of

. The

•

For two vertices

of

,

is an edge of

if and only if it is an edge of

.

Definition 2.5
.

For every pair of graphs

, let

be the number of partial injection

from the vertex labels of

to

such that

is isomorphic to

.

Definition 2.6
.

Let

be a nonnegative integer We say that

is a

blossom
if, for every

, exactly one vertex of

Remark
.

For

.

Let

to be the labeled graph obtained after the following transformations:

2.2.
Encoding graphs with trees

A key construction to make the modular decomposition effective is the graph substitution (also called substitution-composition).

Definition 2.8

(Graph substitution)
.

Let
G
be a graph of size
n
and
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
be weakly-labeled graphs such the vertices of
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
have mutually distinct labels. The graph
G[H_{1},\dots,H_{n}]=(V,E)
is the graph whose set of vertices is
V=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_{H_{i}}
and such that:

•

for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and every pair
(v,v^{\prime})\in V_{H_{i}}^{2}
,
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
if and only if
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E_{H_{i}}
;

•

For every
(i,j)\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
, and every pair
(v,v^{\prime})\in V_{H_{i}}\times V_{H_{j}}
,
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
if and only if
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E_{G}
.

Notation
.

In the following we use the shortcut
\oplus
for the complete graph of size
n
. Thus
\oplus[H_{1},\dots,H_{n}]
is the graph obtained from copies of
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
in which for every
i\neq j
every vertex of
H_{i}
is connected to every vertex of
H_{j}
. This graph is called the
join
of
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}

We use the shortcut
\ominus
for the graph with no edge of size
n
. Thus
\ominus[H_{1},\dots,H_{n}]
is the graph given by the disjoint union of
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
This graph is called the
union
of
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
.

This construction allows us to transform non-plane labeled trees with internal nodes decorated with graphs,
\oplus
and
\ominus
into graphs.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{0}
be the set of rooted non-plane trees whose leaves have distinct labels in
\mathbb{N}
and whose internal nodes carry decorations satisfying the following constraints:

•

internal nodes are decorated with
\oplus
,
\ominus
or a graph;

•

If a node is decorated with some graph

then
|G|\geq 2
and this node has
|G|
children. If a node is
decorated with
\oplus
or
\ominus
then it has at least 2 children.

A tree

is called a

if the labels of its leaves are in
\{1,\dots,|t|\}
.

We call

the internal nodes decorated with
\oplus
or
\ominus
and

the other ones.

Notation
.

For a non-plane rooted tree
t
, and an internal node
\mathfrak{n}
of

, let
t_{\mathfrak{n}}
be the multiset of trees attached to
\mathfrak{n}
and let

be the non-plane tree rooted at
\mathfrak{n}
containing only the descendants of
\mathfrak{n}
in

.

Convention
.

We only consider non-plane trees. However it is sometimes convenient to order the subtrees of a given node. The convention is that for some
\mathfrak{n}
in a tree

the trees of
t_{\mathfrak{n}}
are ordered according to their minimal leaf labels.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
t
be an element of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, the weakly-labeled graph
\mathrm{Graph}(t)
is inductively defined as follows:

•

if
t
is reduced to a single leaf labeled

,
\mathrm{Graph}(t)
is the graph reduced to a single vertex labeled

;

•

otherwise, the root

of

is decorated with a graph

, and

\mathrm{Graph}(t)=H[\mathrm{Graph}(t_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{Graph}(t_{|H|})]

where
t_{i}
is the
i
-th tree of
t_{r}
.

If
G
is a graph and

is a tree in

, we say that

is a substituion tree of

if

.

.

2.3.
Modular decomposition

In this short section we gather the main definitions and properties of modular decomposition. The historical reference is
[
12
]
, the interested reader may also look at
[
8
]
or
[
18
]
.

The next definitions and theorems allow to get a unique recursive decomposition of any graph in the sense of
2.10
, the modular decomposition, and to encode it by a tree.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
G
be a graph (labeled or not). A
module

M
of
G
is a subset of
V
such that for every
(x,y)\in M^{2}
, and every
z\in V\backslash M
,
\{x,z\}\in E
if and only if
\{y,z\}\in E
.

Remark
.

Note that
\emptyset,V
and
\{v\}
for
v\in V
are always modules of
G
. Those sets are called the trivial modules of
G
.

Definition 2.12
.

A graph
G
is
prime
if it has at least
3
vertices and its only modules are the trivial ones.

Definition 2.13
.

A graph is called
\ominus
-indecomposable
(resp.
\oplus
-indecomposable
) if it cannot be written as
\ominus[G_{1},\dots,G_{k}]
(resp.
\oplus[G_{1},\dots,G_{k}]
) for some
k\geq 2
and weakly-labeled graphs
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
.

Note that a graph is
\ominus
-indecomposable if and only if it is connected, and
\oplus
-indecomposable if and only if its complementary is connected.

Theorem 2.14

(Modular decomposition,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least
2
vertices, there exists a unique partition
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\}
for some
k\geq 2
(where the
M_{i}
’s are ordered by their smallest element), where each
M_{i}
is a module of
G
and such that either

•

G=\oplus[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
and the
(M_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
are
\oplus
-indecomposable;

•

G=\ominus[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
and the
(M_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
are
\ominus
-indecomposable;

•

there exists a unique prime graph
P
such that
G=P[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
.

This decomposition can be used to encode graphs by specific trees to get a one-to-one correspondence.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
t
be a substitution tree. We say that
t
is a
modular decomposition tree
if its internal nodes are either
\oplus
,
\ominus
or prime graphs, and if there is no child of a node decorated with
\oplus
(resp.
\ominus
) which is decorated with
\oplus
(resp.
\ominus
).

To a graph
G
we associate a modular decomposition tree by recursively applying the decomposition of

2.14
to the modules
(M_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
, until they are of size
1
. First of all, at each step, we order the different modules increasingly according to their minimal vertex labels. Doing so, a labeled graph
G
can be
encoded by a modular decomposition tree. The internal nodes are decorated with the different graphs that are encountered along the recursive decomposition process (
\oplus
if
G=\oplus[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
,
\ominus
if
G=\ominus[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
,
P
if
G=P[M_{1},\dots,M_{k}]
).

At the end, every module of size
1
is converted into a leaf labeled by the label of the vertex.

This construction provides a one-to-one correspondence
between labeled graphs and modular decomposition trees that maps the size of a graph to the size of the corresponding tree.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
G
be a graph, and
t
its modular decomposition tree, then
t
is the only modular decomposition tree such that
\mathrm{Graph}(t)=G
.

Remark
.

It is crucial to consider modular decomposition trees as non-plane: otherwise, since prime graphs appearing in the decorations can have several labelings, there would be several modular decomposition trees associated with the same graph.

2.4.
Expanded trees

An important tool that will be used in

Definition 2.17
.

Let
G

We introduce the inflation operation to build expanded tree from any given tree, and to count later the number of expanded tree corresponding to a graph. Let
t

\mathfrak{n}
an internal node of
t

\tau

•

•

-th leaf of

-th tree of

.

The resulting tree is called the inflation of

at
\mathfrak{n}
with

Definition 3.1

A graphon is an equivalence class of symmetric functions

Intuitively graphons can be seen as continuous analogous of graph adjacency matrices, where graphs are considered up to relabeling (hence the quotient by

Definition 3.2

Let

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in[0,1]^{2}\quad w_{G}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=1_{\lceil nx\rceil\text{connected to}\lceil ny\rceil}

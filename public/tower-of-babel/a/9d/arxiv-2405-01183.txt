An efficient quantifier elimination procedure for Presburger arithmetic
Christoph Haase, Shankara Narayanan Krishna, Khushraj Madnani, Om Swostik Mishra, Georg Zetzsche

Abstract
All known quantifier elimination procedures for Presburger arithmetic require doubly exponential time for eliminating a single block of existentially quantified variables. It has even been claimed in the literature that this upper bound is tight. We observe that this claim is incorrect and develop, as the main result of this paper, a quantifier elimination procedure eliminating a block of existentially quantified variables in singly exponential time. As corollaries, we can establish the precise complexity of numerous problems. Examples include deciding (i) monadic decomposability for existential formulas, (ii) whether an existential formula defines a well-quasi ordering or, more generally, (iii) certain formulas of Presburger arithmetic with Ramsey quantifiers. Moreover, despite the exponential blowup, our procedure shows that under mild assumptions, even 𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{NP}} upper bounds for decision problems about quantifier-free formulas can be transferred to existential formulas. The technical basis of our results is a kind of small model property for parametric integer programming that generalizes the seminal results by von zur Gathen and Sieveking on small integer points in convex polytopes.

1
Introduction

Presburger arithmetic is the first-order theory of the integers with
addition and order. This theory was shown decidable by Mojżesz
Presburger in 1929
[
25
]
by establishing a quantifier elimination procedure
in the extended structure additionally consisting of infinitely many
predicates
m\mid{\cdot}
for all integers
m>0
, asserting
divisibility by a constant. Recall that a logical theory
T
admits
quantifier elimination whenever for any formula

\Phi(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\equiv\exists x\,\varphi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})

with
\varphi
being quantifier free there is a computable
quantifier-free formula
\Psi(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
such that

\Phi\leftrightarrow\Psi
is a tautology in
T
. Presburger’s
quantifier elimination procedure has non-elementary running time. In
the early 1970s, Cooper
[
6
]
developed an improved version of
Presburger’s procedure, which was later shown to run in triply
exponential time
[
23
]
. Ever since, various other quantifier
elimination procedures have been established and analyzed, especially
for fragments of Presburger arithmetic with a fixed number of
quantifier alternations, see e.g.
[
26
,
32
]
. Weispfenning
[
33
]

analyzed lower bounds for quantifier-elimination procedures and showed
that, assuming unary encoding of numbers,
any
quantifier
elimination procedure requires triply exponential time. In the same
paper, Weispfenning also claims that any algorithm eliminating a single block of
existential quantifiers inherently requires
doubly
exponential
time
[
33
, p. 50]
.

The main contribution of this paper is to develop a quantifier
elimination procedure for Presburger arithmetic that eliminates a
block of existentially quantified variables in
singly

exponential time. This, of course, contradicts Weispfenning’s claim,
which actually turns out to be incorrect as we point out in detail
in
Appendix

C
. The key technical insight underlying
our procedure is a kind of small model property for parametric integer
programming. Given an integer matrix
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times n}
and

\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
, recall that integer programming is to decide whether
there is some
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
. It is
well-known by the work of von zur Gathen and Sieveking
[
31
]
,
and Borosh and Treybig
[
4
]
, that if such an
\bm{x}
exists then
there is one whose bit length is polynomially bounded in the bit
lengths of
A
and
\bm{b}
. In this paper, we refer to the situation in
which
\bm{b}
is not fixed and provided as a parameter as
parametric
integer programming
. Our main technical result states that, in this
setting, if
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has a solution for a given

\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
then there are
D\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times\ell}
and

\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, both of bit length polynomial in the bit length of

A
, such that
\bm{x}=D\bm{b}+\bm{d}
is integral and also a
solution. Observe that there is only an exponential number (in the bit
length of
A
) of possible choices for
D
and
\bm{d}
. Eliminating a
block of variables
\bm{x}
from a system of linear inequalities thus becomes
easy: we have that
A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
is equivalent to the
disjunction of systems of the form
A(D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d})\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}

for all
D
and
\bm{d}
of bit length polynomial in
A
. Using standard
arguments, this approach can then be turned into a quantifier
elimination procedure that eliminates a block of existentially
quantified variables in exponential time.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all vectors
\bm{z}
are treated as column vectors unless
mentioned otherwise. For a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, let
\|\bm{x}\|_{\infty}
be the
maximal absolute value of all components of
\bm{x}
. Moreover, let

\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}
be the maximal absolute value of all
numerators
and

denominators
of components in
\bm{x}
. The latter is important for
representations: Note that a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
with
\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq m
can
be represented using
O(n\log m)
bits. We use analogous notations
\|A\|_{\infty}

and
\|A\|_{\mathsf{frac}}
for matrices
A
. We will sometimes refer to the

Hadamard inequality

[
19
]
, which implies that for a square matrix

A\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
, we have
|\det(A)|\leq(n\cdot\|A\|_{\infty})^{n}
. In particular,
the determinant of
A
is at most exponential in the maximal absolute value of
entries of
A
.

Presburger arithmetic

Presburger arithmetic
(PA) is the first-order theory of the structure
\langle\mathbb{Z};+,<,0,1\rangle
. In order to enable quantifier elimination, we have to permit modulo constraints. Thus technically, we are working with the structure
\langle\mathbb{Z};+,<,(\equiv_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}},0,1\rangle
, where
a\equiv_{m}b
stands for
a\equiv b\bmod{m}
.
In our syntax, we allow atomic formulas of the forms
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\leq b
(called
linear inequalities
) or
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\equiv b\bmod{m}
(called
modulo
or
divisibility constraints
), where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are variables and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b,m\in\mathbb{Z}
are constants encoded in binary. A formula is
quantifier-free
if it contains no quantifiers or, equivalently, is a Boolean combination of atomic formulas. Notice that conjunctions of linear inequalities can be written as systems of linear
inequalities
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
.

The
size
of a PA Formula
\varphi
, denoted
|\varphi|
, is the number of letters used to write it down, where we assume all constants to be encoded in binary. (Sometimes, we say that a formula obeys a size bound even if constants are encoded in unary; but this will be stated explicitly).

Fixed quantifier alternation fragments

The
\Sigma_{k}
fragment of PA consists of formulas of the form
\exists\bm{u_{1}}\forall\bm{u_{2}}\dots Q_{k}\bm{u_{k}}\colon\varphi(\bm{u_{1}},\bm{u_{2}},\dots,\bm{u_{k}},\bm{z})
where
\bm{u_{i}}
is a vector of quantified variables,
\bm{z}
is a vector of free variables,
\varphi(\bm{u_{1}},\bm{u_{2}},\dots,\bm{u_{k}},\bm{z})
is a quantifier free PA formula, and

Q_{k}
denotes
\forall
or
\exists
depending on whether
k
is even or odd respectively.
Similarly, the
\Pi_{k}
fragment of PA consists of formulas of the form
\forall\bm{u_{1}}\exists\bm{u_{2}}\dots Q_{k}\bm{u_{k}}\colon\varphi(\bm{u_{1}},\bm{u_{2}},\dots,\bm{u_{k}},\bm{z})
where
Q_{k}
denotes
\forall
or
\exists
depending on whether
k
is odd or even respectively.

Bounded existential Presburger arithmetic

In addition to our quantifier elimination result, we shall prove a somewhat stronger version, which states that one can compute a compact representation of a quantifier-free formula in polynomial time.
As compact representations, we introduce a syntactic variant of existential Presburger arithmetic, which we call
bounded existential Presburger arithmetic
, short
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
.
Essentially,
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
requires all quantifiers to be restricted to bounded intervals, but also permits polynomials over the quantified variables. Using standard methods, one can translate every formula in
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
in polynomial time into an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula. However, the converse is not obvious, and our main results states that this is possible. Syntactically, an
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formula over free variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
is of the form

\exists^{\leq k_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\exists^{\leq k_{n}}x_{n}\colon\varphi,

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are variables, each
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is a number given in binary, and
\varphi
is a quantifier-free formula where every atom is of one of the forms:

\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}y_{i}\leq q\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}y_{i}\equiv r\bmod{q},

(1)

where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},q,r\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
are polynomials over the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Thus, where
\exists\mathrm{PA}
allows constant integral coefficients,
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
allows polynomials from
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. The quantifiers
\exists^{\leq k_{i}}x_{i}
are interpreted as “there exists
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|x_{i}|\leq k_{i}
”.

Remark 1
.

Now indeed, a
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formula can be converted in polynomial time into an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula: The bounded quantification is clearly expressible in
\exists\mathrm{PA}
.
The terms
p_{i}y_{i}
and
q
in (
1
) (recall
p_{i}
and
q
are polynomials are from
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
) are also expressible, because
multiplication with exponentially bounded variables can be expressed using
polynomial-size
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formulas. This is because given a polynomial

p\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and a variable
y
, we can construct a
polynomial-size existential formula
\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,z)
expressing

z=p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\cdot y\wedge|x_{1}|\leq k_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge|x_{n}|\leq k_{n}
.
This, in turn, follows from the fact that given
\ell
in unary, we can
construct an existential formula
\mu_{\ell}(x,y,z)
, of size linear in
\ell
, expressing
z=x\cdot y\wedge|x|\leq 2^{\ell}
(see
[
17
, Sec. 3.1]
or
[
18
, p. 7]
). Thus, we can construct
\pi
in
\exists\mathrm{PA}
by introducing a
variable for each subterm of
p
(which can clearly all be bounded exponentially).

Making
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formulas quantifier-free

Moreover, a
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formula can easily be converted (in exponential time) into
an exponential-size quantifier-free formula: Just take an exponential
disjunction over all assignments of the existentially bounded variables

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and replace the variables by their values in all the atoms.
Thus,
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formulas can be regarded as compact representations of quantifier-free formulas.

3
Main results

Here, we state and discuss implications of the main result of this paper:

Theorem 2
.

Given a formula of
\exists\mathrm{PA}
, we can construct in
polynomial time an equivalent formula in
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
.

From
Theorem

2
, we can deduce the following, since by the remark
Remark

1
in
Section

2
, one can easily convert a
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formula
into an exponential-sized quantifier-free formula.

Corollary 3
.

Given a formula
\varphi
in existential Presburger arithmetic, we can compute
in exponential time an equivalent quantifier-free formula
\psi
of size
exponential in
\varphi
. Moreover, all constants in
\psi
are encoded in
unary.

In
Section

6
, we will see that an exponential blowup
cannot be avoided when eliminating a block of existential quantifiers,
even if we allow constants to be encoded in binary in the quantifier-free formula.

There are several applications of

Theorems

2
and
3
. The most obvious type of
applications are those, where, for every problem

semantic

that is in
{\mathsf{NP}}
(resp.
{\mathsf{coNP}}
) for quantifier-free
formulas, the same problem belongs to
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
(resp.
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
) for

some examples.

A direct consequence of
Corollary

3
(and the
{\mathsf{NP}}
membership of the quantifier free fragment of PA) is the following.

Corollary 4
.

The
\Sigma_{2}
-fragment of Presburger arithmetic belongs to
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
.

The
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
upper bound is known and was shown by Haase
[

. In fact, the

-fragment is known to be

-complete: An

Grädel
[

]
, already for the

\exists\forall^{*}
-fragment.

Ramsey quantifiers

In fact, combining
Corollary

3
with the results from
[
2
]
, we can strengthen
Corollary

4
. The
Ramsey quantifier

\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}
states the existence of infinite (directed) cliques. More precisely, if
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
is a Presburger formula where
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
are vectors of
n
variables each, then
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
is satisfied for
\bm{z}
if and only if there exists an infinite sequence
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\ldots\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of pairwise distinct vectors with
\varphi(\bm{a}_{i},\bm{a}_{j},\bm{z})
for every
i<j
. As mentioned in
[
2
]
, Ramsey quantifiers can be applied to deciding liveness properties, deciding monadic decomposability (see below), and deciding whether a formula defines a well-quasi-ordering (see below).

In
[
2
, Thm. 5.1]
, it is shown that if
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
is an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula, then one can compute in polynomial time an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula
\varphi^{\prime}(\bm{z})
equivalent to
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
.

Corollary 5
.

Given a
\Sigma_{2}
-formula
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
, we can construct an exponential-size
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula equivalent to
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
. In particular, deciding the truth of
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\colon\psi
for

\Sigma_{2}
-formulas
\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
-complete.

Indeed,
Corollary

3
lets us convert
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
into an exponential-size existential formula
\varphi^{\prime}
, so that we can apply the above result of
[
2
]
to the formula
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\colon\varphi^{\prime}(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})
, which results in an equivalent exponential
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula. The
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
lower bound in the second statement follows from
{\mathsf{NEXP}}
-hardness of the
\Sigma_{2}
-fragment and the fact that for a given
\Sigma_{2}
-formula
\chi
without free variables, the statement
\exists^{\mathsf{ram}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\colon\chi\wedge\bm{x}<\bm{y}
is equivalent to
\chi
.

Detecting WQOs

A
well-quasi-ordering
(WQO) is a reflexive and transitive ordering
(X,\leq)
such that for every sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\in X
, there are
i<j
with
x_{i}\leq x_{j}
.
Well-quasi-orderings are of paramount importance in the widely applied theory of well-structured transition systems
[
7
,
1
,
10
]
.
The problem of deciding whether a given Presburger formula
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
defines a WQO was recently raised by Finkel and Gupta
[
8
]
, with the hope of establishing automatically that certain systems are well-structured. As observed in
[
9
, Prop. 12]
, this problem reduces to evaluating Ramsey quantifiers, which is decidable by
[
28
]
.
Based on an
{\mathsf{NP}}
algorithm for Ramsey quantifiers, it is shown in
[
2
, Sec. 8.3]
that given a quantifier-free formula
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, where
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
are vectors of
n
variables each, it is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete whether the relation
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
defined by
\varphi
is a WQO. Our results allow us to settle the complexity for existential formulas:

Corollary 6
.

Given an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula
\varphi
, it is

{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-complete to decide whether
\varphi
defines a
WQO.

The upper bound follows directly from
Corollary

3
and the fact that it is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete to decide whether a given quantifier-free formula defines a well-quasi-ordering
[
2
, Sec. 8.3]
. This yields a
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
procedure overall. It should be noted that
Corollary

6
can also be deduced from
Corollary

5
(using the same idea as in
[
2
, Sec. 8.3]
). However, we find it instructive to demonstrate how quantifier elimination permits a direct transfer of the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
algorithm as a black box. We show the
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
lower bound in
Section

5
.

Monadic decomposability

A Presburger formula is
monadic
if each of its atoms contains at most one variable. Moreover, we say that a Presburger formula
\varphi
is
monadically decomposable
if
\varphi
is equivalent to a monadic Presburger formula. Motivated by the role monadic formulas play in constraint databases
[
15
,
21
]
, Veanes, Bjørner, and Nachmanson, and Bereg recently raised the question of how to decide whether a given formula is monadically decomposable
[
30
]
. For Presburger arithmetic, decidability follows from
[
13
, p. 1048]
and for quantifier-free formulas, monadic decomposability was shown
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete in
[
20
, Thm. 1]
(in
[
2
, Cor. 8.1]
, the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound is shown via Ramsey quantifiers).
Corollary

3
allows us to settle the case of
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formulas.

Corollary 7
.

Monadic decomposability of
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formulas is
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-complete.

This is because given an
\exists\mathrm{PA}
formula, we can compute an exponential-sized quantifier-free formula and apply the existing
{\mathsf{coNP}}
procedure, yielding a
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
upper bound overall. Again, the
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
upper bound could also be deduced from
Corollary

5
(but this proof shows again how to transfer algorithms using quantifier elimination). The
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
lower bound follows the same idea as the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
lower bound in
[
2
]
, see
Section

5
.

{\mathsf{NP}}
upper bounds

In addition to new
{\mathsf{NEXP}}

{\mathsf{coNEXP}}

{\mathsf{NP}}

\mathfrak{p}

\mathfrak{p}(S_{1})
or
\mathfrak{p}(S_{2})

[(i)]

1.

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}(S)
if and only if

.

2.

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}(S)
if and only if
S

3.

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}(S)
if and only if
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and
S
contains a power of

.

4.

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}(S)
if and only if

and

6.

The predicate
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathfrak{p}(S)

For each such predicate, we consider the problem
\mathfrak{p}(\exists\mathrm{PA})

. For (
4
), this follows from the fact that Ramsey quantifiers can be evaluated in
{\mathsf{NP}}

[
2
, Thm 5.1]
. Our results imply that for proving
{\mathsf{NP}}
upper bounds, we may always assume a quantifier-free input formula. This is perhaps surprising, because one might expect that for non-linear predicates, it is difficult to bound the quantified variables.

Corollary 8
.

For every admissible predicate
\mathfrak{p}
, the problem
\mathfrak{p}(\exists\mathrm{PA})
is in
{\mathsf{NP}}
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}(\mathrm{QF})
is in
{\mathsf{NP}}
.

Here, the “only if” direction is trivial, and the “if” direction follows
from
Theorem

2
. This is because
Theorem

2

allows us to assume that
\varphi
is given as a
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
formula
\exists^{\leq k_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\exists^{\leq k_{n}}x_{n}\colon\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
.
Moreover, admissibility of
\mathfrak{p}
implies that
\mathfrak{p}
is satisfied for
\varphi

if and only if there exists an assignment
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
for the bounded
variables such that the quantifier-free formula

\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
satisfies
\mathfrak{p}
. Thus, we can guess the
assignment (which occupies polynomially many bits) and run the
{\mathsf{NP}}
algorithm
for quantifier-free formulas.

4
Quantifier elimination

In this section, we prove
Theorem

2
.
The following is our main geometric ingredient.

Proposition 9
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times n}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
, and let
\Delta
be an upper bound on all absolute values of the subdeterminants of
A
. If the system
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has an integral solution, then it has an integral solution of the form
D\bm{b}+\bm{d}
, where
D\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times\ell}
and
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
with
\|D\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq\Delta
and
\|\bm{d}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq n\Delta^{2}
.

Before we prove
Proposition

9
, let us see how it implies
Theorem

2
and
Corollary

3
.

Proof of
Corollary

3

While
Corollary

3
follows from
Theorem

2
, it
follows very directly from
Proposition

9
and the proof
is a good warm-up for the proof of
Theorem

2
. Therefore, we
first derive
Corollary

3
from

Proposition

9
. Suppose we are given a Presburger
formula
\exists\bm{x}\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and

\bm{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
are variables and
\varphi
is quantifier-free.

It is well-known that divisibility constraints can be eliminated in favor of
existentially quantified variables, since
a\equiv b\bmod{m}
if and only if

\exists x\colon a-b=mx
. Thus, we may assume that
\varphi
contains no
divisibility constraints. Then, by moving all negations inwards and using the
standard equivalence
\neg(r\leq t)\iff t+1\leq r
, we may assume that
\varphi

is a positive Boolean combination of atoms
\bm{a}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}^{\top}\bm{y}+c
, where

\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and
c\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By bringing
\varphi
into DNF, we can write it as a disjunction of
exponentially many systems of inequalities of the form
A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
,
where
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times m}
, and
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.
Thus, it suffices to construct a quantifier-free formula for
\exists\bm{x}\colon A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
. Let
\Delta
be an upper bound for all absolute values of
subdeterminants of
A
. Since the transformation into DNF does not change
the appearing
constants, we have that
\Delta\leq((m+n)\|A\|_{\infty})^{m+n}
is at most exponential in the
size of the input formula.

According to
Proposition

9
, a vector
\bm{x}
with

\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
exists if and only if there exists a matrix
D\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times\ell}
and
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
with
\|D\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq\Delta
and
\|\bm{d}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq n\Delta^{2}
such that (i) substituting
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}
for
\bm{x}
satisfies

A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
and also (ii) the vector
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}
is integral.
Therefore, the formula
\exists\bm{x}\colon A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
is equivalent to

\bigvee_{(D,\bm{d})\in P}A(D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d})\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}~\wedge~D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

where
P
is the set of all pairs
(D,\bm{d})
with
D\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times\ell}
,
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
,
\|D\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq\Delta
, and
\|\bm{d}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq n\Delta^{2}
. Clearly,
P
contains at most exponentially many elements. Moreover, note that the condition
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a set of
n
modulo constraints.

Proof of
Theorem

2

The proof of
Theorem

2
is similar to the above
construction—we just need to circumvent the exponential conversion into DNF.
We proceed as follows.

As above, we are given a Presburger
formula
\exists\bm{x}\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and

\bm{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
are variables and
\varphi
is quantifier-free. Moreover, we may assume that
\varphi
contains no divisibility constraints and is a positive Boolean combination of atoms

\bm{a}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}^{\top}\bm{y}+c
, where
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and

c\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}_{i}^{\top}\bm{y}+c_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
be the set of all atoms occurring in
\varphi
and let
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times n}
be the matrix with rows
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}
and
B\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times m}
be the matrix of rows
\bm{b}_{i}^{\top}
, and let
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
be the (column) vector with entries
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}
.
Thus, our formula
\varphi
consists of
\ell
atoms, each of which is a row in the system of linear inequalities
A\bm{x}\leq B\bm{y}+\bm{c}
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}
be the formula obtained from
\varphi
by replacing the atom
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}_{i}^{\top}\bm{y}+c_{i}
by
z_{i}=1
, where
z_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, is a fresh variable for each of the
\ell
atoms.
Now let
\Delta
be an upper bound on all absolute values of the
subdeterminants of
A
. Then
\Delta\leq((m+n)\|A\|_{\infty})^{m+n}
is at most exponential in the size of the input formula.
Consider the formula

\exists z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\in\{0,1\}\colon\quad\exists D\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times\ell},\|D\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq\Delta\colon\\
\exists\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n},~\|\bm{d}\|_{\mathsf{frac}}\leq n\Delta^{2}\colon\quad\varphi^{\prime}~\wedge~D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}~\wedge~\bigwedge_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(z_{i}=1\to\psi_{i}\right),

(2)

where
\psi_{i}
is the formula
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}(D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d})\leq\bm{b}_{i}^{\top}\bm{y}+c_{i}
. Note that (
2
) is expressible in
\exists^{\leq}\mathrm{PA}
: We introduce (i) one variable for each
z_{i}
, (ii) two variables for each entry of
D
(one for the numerator, and one for the denominator), and (iii) two variables for each entry of
\bm{d}
.

Each of the
n
divisibility constraints of
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and each of the atoms
\psi_{i}
can be written in the forms (
1
). To see this, let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
be the bounded variables used for the numerators or denominators in
D
and
\bm{d}
. Observe that the vector
B\bm{y}
is a linear combination of
\bm{y}
with integer coefficients. The matrix
D
and the vector
\bm{d}
consist of quotients of bounded variables, hence rational functions in
\mathbb{Z}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}]
. Thus, the vector
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}
has in each entry an expression
s+\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}y_{i}
, where
r_{1},\ldots,r_{m},s\in\mathbb{Z}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}]
. Hence, by multiplying with the product of all denominators, we can write each inequality
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}(D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d})\leq\bm{b}_{i}^{\top}\bm{y}+c_{i}
in the form of (
1
). Moreover, for the requirement
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we can write each row of
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}
as a quotient
\tfrac{1}{q}(r+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}y_{i})
, where
q,r,p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}]
, so that membership in
\mathbb{Z}
is equivalent to
\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}y_{i}\equiv-r\pmod{q}
.

Let us argue why (
2
) is equivalent to

\exists\bm{x}\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
. Clearly, if (
2
) is satisfied, then

\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell})
yields a set of atoms that, if satisfied, makes

\varphi
true. Moreover, the vector
D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d}
is an integer vector
that satisfies all the atoms specified by
\bm{z}
.

Conversely, suppose

\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
holds for some
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. First, we set exactly those
z_{i}
to
1
for which the
i
-th atom in
\varphi
is satisfied by
\bm{x},\bm{y}
. Recall that each row of
A
(and each row of
B
, and of
\bm{c}
) corresponds to an atom in
\varphi
. Let
A^{\prime}
be the matrix obtained from
A
by selecting those rows that correspond to atoms that are satisfied by our
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
. Define
B^{\prime}
similarly from
B
, and
\bm{c}^{\prime}
from
\bm{c}
. Then we have
A^{\prime}\bm{x}\leq B^{\prime}\bm{y}+\bm{c}^{\prime}
. Now
Proposition

9
yields a matrix
D^{\prime}
and a vector
\bm{d}
(each with
n

A^{\prime}(D^{\prime}(B^{\prime}\bm{y}+\bm{c}^{\prime})+\bm{d})\leq B^{\prime}\bm{y}+\bm{c}^{\prime}
. Now the set of rows of
B^{\prime}\bm{y}+\bm{c}^{\prime}
is a subset of the rows of
B\bm{y}+\bm{c}

, we can construct a matrix

. Hence, we have
A^{\prime}(D(B\bm{y}+\bm{c})+\bm{d})\leq B^{\prime}\bm{y}+\bm{c}^{\prime}
. The latter means exactly that

is satisfied for every

.
Thus, this choice of

satisfies (

2
.

Constructing solutions as affine transformations

4.1.1
Convex geometry

Before we start with the proof of
Proposition

9
,
we recall some standard definitions from convex geometry from Schrijver’s
book
[
29
]
. Below, we let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{r\in\mathbb{R}\>|\>r\geq 0\}
.
A
polyhedron
is a set
P=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}\}
, where
A
is
an
\ell\times n
integer matrix and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.
Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, then

C
is a
convex cone
if
\lambda\bm{x}+\mu\bm{y}\in C
for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in C
and
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Given a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
,

\cone(X)=\{\lambda_{1}\bm{x}_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{t}\bm{x}_{t}\mid t\geq 0,~\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{t}\in X,~\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}\,.

The
convex hull
of a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is the smallest convex set containing that set, i.e.,

\convhull(X)=\{\lambda_{1}\bm{x}_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{t}\bm{x}_{t}\mid t\geq 1,~\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\dots\bm{x}_{t}\in X,\\
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+},~\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{t}=1\}\,.

Next, we recall some terminology concerning the structure of polyhedra.
The
characteristic cone
of a polyhedron
P=\{\bm{x}\mid A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the set
\charcone(P)\coloneqq\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid A\bm{y}\leq 0\}
.
The
lineality space
of polyhedron
P
is the set
\linspace(P)\coloneqq\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid A\bm{y}=0\}
.

Definition 10

(Faces)
.

Given a polyhedron
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F\subseteq P
is a
face
of
P
if and only if
F
is non-empty and

F=\{\bm{x}\in P\>|\>A^{\prime}\bm{x}=\bm{b}^{\prime}\}

for some subsystem
A^{\prime}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}^{\prime}
of
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
. We call

F\subseteq P
a
proper face
of
P

if
F\neq\emptyset
and
F\neq P
.

It follows that
P
has only finitely many faces. A
minimal face
of
P
is a face not containing any other face.
We have the following characterization of minimal faces
[
29
, Thm 8.4]
,

Proposition 11
.

A set
F
is a minimal face of a polyhedron
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if
\emptyset\neq F\subseteq P
and

F=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>A^{\prime}\bm{x}=\bm{b}^{\prime}\}

for some subsystem
A^{\prime}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}^{\prime}
of
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
, such that the matrix
A^{\prime}
has the same rank as
A
.

The following is shown in
[
29
, Sec. 8.8]
:

Proposition 12
.

Let
C
be the cone
\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>|\>A\bm{x}\leq\bm{0}\}
. There is a finite collection
G_{1},G_{2}.\dots,G_{s}
of subsets, which are of the form
G_{i}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq 0,~A^{\prime}\bm{x}=\bm{0}\}
, where
\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}\\
\bm{a}_{i}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}
is a subset of the rows of
A
, such that the following holds. If we choose for each
i=1,\ldots,s
a vector
\bm{y}_{i}
from
G_{i}\setminus\linspace(C)
and choose
\bm{z}_{0},\ldots,\bm{z}_{t}
in
\linspace(C)
such that
\linspace(C)=\cone(\bm{z}_{0},\ldots,\bm{z}_{t})
, then

C=\cone(\bm{y}_{1},\ldots,\bm{y}_{s},\bm{z}_{0},\ldots,\bm{z}_{t}).

Here, the sets
G_{i}
are also called minimal proper faces (but

Proposition

12
is not a characterization of those).

4.1.2
Proof of
Proposition

9

We now prove
Proposition

9
. For the remainder of the
section, let
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell\times n}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
. Moreover, let
\Delta

be an upper bound on all absolute values of the sub-determinants of

.
Our first step is a simple application of standard facts about polyhedra.

Lemma 13
.

If the system
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has a solution in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, then it has one of the form
\frac{1}{a}E\bm{b}
, where
E\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times\ell}
,

,
|a|\leq\Delta
, and
\|E\|_{\infty}\leq\Delta

Proof.

It is well-known that if
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has a rational solution, then there is a solution inside a minimal face of the polyhedron
P=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}\}
defined by the system of linear inequalities
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}

[
29
, Thm. 8.5]
. Recall that a
minimal face
is a non-empty subset
F\subseteq P
of the form

F=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid A^{\prime}\bm{x}=\bm{b}^{\prime}\},

(3)

where
A^{\prime}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}^{\prime}
is a subset of the inequalities in
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
such that the matrix
A^{\prime}
has the same rank as

(see

11
or

, Thm. 8.4]
). Suppose

is a non-empty minimal face and satisfies (

such that

We also employ the following well-known fact, which again uses standard arguments.

Lemma 14

We also rely on the well-known theorem of Carathéodory

Theorem 15

If

is some subset and

9

Lemma 16
.

Suppose the system
A\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has an integral solution, and let
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}

\bm{z}^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Proof.

An illustration of the proof is given in

.
Let
\bm{z}

\bm{b}
into two sets of rows, depending on in which coordinates

We begin with the lower bound for

\displaystyle(x=0\wedge y>0)\vee(x=0\wedge y=0)

An exponential lower bound for quantifier elimination

Our main results show that one can eliminate a block of existential quantifiers
with only an exponential blow-up.
Using an example from

In the presence of binary encoded constants, we cannot use Weispfenning’s lower

Instead, we measure the periodicity of infinite sets. Recall that every
Presburger formula with one free variable defines an

of

\varphi

the

\varphi

[
17
, Thm. 2]
, Haase
constructs
3

.
a sequence

. The following will imply that

\Phi_{n}
require exponential-sized quantifier-free equivalents:

Lemma 18

Let

Now indeed, if

We elaborate on a flaw in Weispfenning’s
paper
[

]
which is a
consequence of misinterpreting results from
the literature, from which he incorrectly concludes that the elimination of a
block of existential quantifiers from a formula of Presburger arithmetic
results in an inherent doubly exponential blow-up.

The main result of Section 3 of
[
33
]

is Theorem 3.1, which states that performing quantifier elimination on

formulas of Presburger arithmetic results in an
inherent triply exponential blow-up, assuming unary encoding of
numbers. To this end, Weispfenning invokes a result by Fischer and

[

who showed that there exists a function

such that for almost all

concluding that the smallest quantifier-free formula defining the set

\{z\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\Phi_{n}(1,z,z)\}

Weispfenning then continues sketching how to adapt this approach in the
presence of a bounded number of quantifier alternations. To this end,
he appeals to a result by

[

]
, which states that for some constant

, one can define multiplication up to

2^{2^{(n/a)^{ra}}}

(4)

using a formula of length

and

concludes that eliminating a block of existential quantifiers yields an

of such a family in the third paragraph

[

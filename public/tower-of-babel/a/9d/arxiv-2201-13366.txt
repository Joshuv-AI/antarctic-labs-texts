(2-)Drinfel’d Double and (2-)BF Theory hank.chen@uwaterloo.ca fgirelli@uwaterloo.ca
Hank Chen, Florian Girelli

Abstract
The gauge symmetry and shift/translational symmetry of a 3D BF action, which are associated to a pair of dual Lie algebras, can be combined to form the Drinfel’d double. This combined symmetry is the gauge symmetry of the Chern-Simons action which is equivalent to the BF action, up to some boundary term. We show that something similar happens in 4D when considering a 2-BF action (aka BFCG action), whose symmetries are specified in terms of a pair of dual strict Lie 2-algebras (ie. crossed-modules). Combining these symmetries gives rise to a 2-Drinfel’d double which becomes the gauge symmetry structure of a 4D BF theory, up to a boundary term. Concretely, we show how using 2-gauge transformations based on dual crossed-modules, the notion of 2-Drinfel’d double defined in [ 1 ] appears. We also discuss how, similarly to the Lie algebra case, the symmetric contribution of the r r -matrix of the 2-Drinfel’d double can be interpreted as a quadratic 2-Casimir, which allows to recover the notion of duality.

2
Recovering the Drinfel’d double as a gauge symmetry from dual gauge theories

In this section, we begin with a demonstration of our strategy to extract the structure of the Drinfel’d double from gauge theory.

We consider a pair of dual gauge theories in the sense that their underlying Lie algebras,
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are dual to each other. Because of the duality, we can induce gauge transformations of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and vice-versa, through the co-adjoint representations. We can then perform gauge transformations of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on a sector, say
\mathfrak{g}
in different order. Requiring that we get the same result, whatever the order put strong constraints on the shape of the transformations. The constraints are equivalent to the compatibility relations of a matched pair of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}

[
43
]
. To have such compatibilitiy relations means that we need to modify accordingly the notion of covariant derivative in each sector
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. This modification is what we called the antisymmetrization procedure. As a result, the covariant derivative (and therefore the gauge transformation) in a given different sector will then depend on
both
connections.

These new gauge transformations can be combined together, these are the Drinfel’d double gauge transformations.

We will then discuss how we can construct a theory invariant under such theory either in terms of connections based on the Drinfel’d double
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as a gauge symmetry (Chern-Simons), or based on the components
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
(BF theory).

2.1
Dual gauge theories

Gauge theory

Let
P\rightarrow M
denote a principal
G
-bundle over a
d
-dimensional manifold
M
.
It is standard lore that the
G
-connection
A\in\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
and its associated curvature

F=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]\in\Omega^{2}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}

undergoes the following gauge transformations

A\rightarrow A^{\lambda}=A+d_{A}\lambda,\qquad F\rightarrow F^{\lambda}=F+[F,\lambda]

(2.1)

parameterized by a 0-form
\lambda\in\Omega^{0}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
is the Lie algebra of

.

Dual gauge theory

We can construct another principal bundle,
P^{\prime}\rightarrow M
which is a
G^{*}
-bundle over the
d
-dimensional manifold

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\psi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{2\wedge}

[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}(X)=(g\wedge g^{\prime})(\psi(X)),\qquad\forall\penalty\ g,g^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},X\in\mathfrak{g}.

The Jacobi identity for the bracket is equivalent to the
cocycle condition

[
17
]
which is expressed in term of the coadjoint action

(see below)

d\psi(X,X^{\prime})=\psi([X,X^{\prime}])-D(\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{X})\psi(X^{\prime})+D(\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{X^{\prime}})\psi(X)=0,

(2.2)

where

is the

. As such,
\psi\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{2\wedge})
is a
Lie algebra 1-cocycle
, and

a
Lie bialgebra

[
17
]

.

is a Lie bialgebra, so is its dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

Let us now turn to the gauge structure generated by the dual space

Coadjoint representations.

Because the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are dual to each other, we can dualize their respective adjoint actions, which are respectively

\operatorname{ad}_{X}(X^{\prime})=[X,X^{\prime}],\qquad\mathfrak{ad}_{g}(g^{\prime})=[g,g^{\prime}]_{*},

where

\langle\langle X+g,X^{\prime}+g^{\prime}\rangle\rangle=g(X^{\prime})+g^{\prime}(X)

\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}

A\rightarrow A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A,\qquad B\rightarrow B+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B.

(2.5)

To summarize, because we have a pair of dual Lie algebras, we can introduce a gauge transformation of one sector say
\mathfrak{g}

and vice-versa. Indeed, the transformations Eqs. (

in order for the direct sum

to form a

2.2
The Drinfel’d double as gauge symmetry

Let us now consider how the connections
A,B
transform under both
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}
. Since the sectors
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are acting on each other, the gauge parameters will themselves be subject of gauge transformations.
For example, the pure-gauge
B=d\tilde{\lambda}
transforms non-trivially under
\lambda
; similarly for the pure-gauge
A=d\lambda
under
\tilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle d\tilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle d\tilde{\lambda}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}d\tilde{\lambda}=d\tilde{\lambda}+d(\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda)-\mathfrak{ad}_{d\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda

\displaystyle d\lambda

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle d\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}d\lambda=d\lambda+d(\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda})-\operatorname{ad}_{d\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda},

where we have used the Leibniz rule.

Now if we assume, in the first equation, that
\tilde{\lambda}
is slowly-varying (so that
\lambda d\tilde{\lambda}
is small), then this induces a transformation of the gauge parameter
\lambda

\lambda\rightarrow\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda+o(\lambda d\tilde{\lambda}).

Similarly, if we assume
\lambda
to be slowly-varying, then we have

\tilde{\lambda}\rightarrow\tilde{\lambda}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}+o(\tilde{\lambda}d\lambda)

in the dual sector.

In the following, we denote by
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}
the combined gauge parameter, and a direction of an arrow over it indicates the order at which the gauge transformation parameterized by which parameter is performed. For instance,
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
indicates that we perform a gauge transformation by
\lambda

\tilde{\lambda}
, and vice versa for
\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

Combined gauge transformation.

With this, we may then use Eqs. (

2.5

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A\xrightarrow{\lambda}A+d_{A}\lambda

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+d_{A}\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}d\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad+[\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A,\lambda]+[A,\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda]+[\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A,\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda],

while

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\lambda}A+d_{A}\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}d\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}[A,\lambda]+\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}d_{A}\lambda.

If we neglect quadratic terms in the small gauge parameter
\lambda

[\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A,\lambda]+[A,\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda}}\lambda]-\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}[A,\lambda]-\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A

On the dual side, we may argue analogously by exchanging the original and the dual sectors:

\lambda\leftrightsquigarrow\tilde{\lambda},\qquad\operatorname{ad}^{*}\leftrightsquigarrow\mathfrak{ad}^{*},\qquad A\leftrightsquigarrow B.

More explicitly, we compute from (

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle B\xrightarrow{\lambda}B+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}B+d_{B}\tilde{\lambda}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}d\tilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}[B,\lambda]_{*}+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda}^{*}B+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda}^{*}d_{B}\tilde{\lambda},

while

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle B\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}B+d_{B}\tilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\lambda}B+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B+d_{b}\tilde{\lambda}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}d\tilde{\lambda}

The antisymmetrization procedure.

One crucial point to note is that, to impose the compatibility conditions Eqs. (
2.6
), (
2.7
), we must introduces the terms
\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}

These additional terms, however, cannot just be introduced from thin air. We must induce it from a modification of the gauge transformations we have assumed in Eqs. (

\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda,\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}

A\rightarrow A+d_{A}\lambda-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda,\qquad B\rightarrow B+d_{B}\tilde{\lambda}-\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}.

We call this the

It is simple to see why these extra terms are necessary. By construction, the combined gauge transformation

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:A\rightarrow\dots-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda,

\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}

upon performing the antisymmetrization procedure.

) implies that the

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

{\bf A}=A+B\in\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*})

valued in the direct sum

, upon which the combined gauge transformation

acts. This is a key observation that shall finally allow us to extract the compatibility conditions in a Manin triple.

\displaystyle D_{\bf A}\lambda

\displaystyle=d_{A}\lambda-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda

2.7
) in terms of the Lie algebra values:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}[X,X^{\prime}]=[\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}X,X^{\prime}]+[X,\mathfrak{ad}^{*}_{g}X^{\prime}]-\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{X^{\prime}}^{*}g}^{*}X+\mathfrak{ad}_{\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}g}^{*}X^{\prime},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}=[\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}g,g^{\prime}]_{*}+[g,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{X}g^{\prime}]_{*}-\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{g^{\prime}}^{*}X}^{*}g+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}X}^{*}g^{\prime}

(2.11)

for each
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g},g,g^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then we in fact recover the necessary and sufficient conditions for the coadjoint representations
\operatorname{ad}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}
to come together and form the
standard Manin triple

[

]

\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ _{\operatorname{ad}^{*}}\bowtie_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

equipped with the standard Lie bracket

\boldsymbol{[}X+g,X^{\prime}+g^{\prime}\boldsymbol{]}=[X,X^{\prime}]+[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}+\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}g^{\prime}+\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}X-\mathfrak{ad}_{g^{\prime}}^{*}X^{\prime}-\operatorname{ad}_{X^{\prime}}^{*}g.

(2.12)

It is known that this double Lie algebra structure
\mathfrak{d}
integrates to a
Poisson-Lie group

D

[

, and hence serves as a model for the
Drinfel’d double

\mathfrak{d}=D(\mathfrak{g})

Manin Rigidity

We have demonstrated that the gauge theory built on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of the Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
manifests canonically the structure of a Manin triple if and only if the combined gauge transformation under
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}
is unambiguous. In particular, the naturally induced coadjoint representations
\operatorname{ad}^{*},\mathfrak{ad}^{*}
of the Lie bialgebra and its dual on each other must necessarily satisfy Eq. (
2.11
).

Now if we consider generic algebra action
\mathfrak{g}\rhd\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and back-action
\mathfrak{g}\lhd\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, equipped with the same non-degenerate invariant bilinear form Eq. (
2.4
), we can prove the following gauge-enforced rigidity result:

Corollary 2.1
.

The induced combined gauge transformations
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}
is unambiguous if and only if the double

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}

is isomorphic to the standard Manin triple
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ _{\operatorname{ad}^{*}}\bowtie_{\mathfrak{ad}^{*}}\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

The same argument as in Section
2.2
, leading up to
Theorem
2.1
, goes through for the generic action/back-action
\rhd,\lhd
, such that the compatibility condition Eq. (
2.11
) must hold, with
\operatorname{ad}^{*}
replaced by
\rhd
and
\mathfrak{ad}^{*}
replaced by
\lhd
. The induced Lie bracket must then take the same form as Eq. (
2.12
), which forces
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\cong\mathfrak{d}
.
∎

The rigidity of the Manin triple is well-known, but here we have demonstrated by gauge theoretic considerations.

We shall generalize our method in the following to a
2-gauge structure
, based on a Lie algebra crossed-module
\mathfrak{g}
. We shall seek to extract compatibility conditions for the (strict) coadjoint representations necessary for the notion of a
2-Manin triple
.

2.3
3D topological action invariant under the Drinfel’d double

The fact that the Manin triple
\mathfrak{d}
forms a Lie algebra implies that we can do gauge theory on it based on the standard bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{d}}=\boldsymbol{[}\cdot,\cdot\boldsymbol{]}
in Eq. (
2.12
). In particular, given the combined connection
{\bf A}
we can write a covariant curvature quantity

\displaystyle{\bf F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d{\bf A}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{[}{\bf A}\wedge{\bf A}\boldsymbol{]}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]+\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A))+(dB+\frac{1}{2}[B\wedge B]_{*}+\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B))

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\bar{F}+\bar{F}^{*},

called the
combined curvature
, which has also appeared in Ref.
[
44
]
. The covariance of
{\bf F}
is manifest; for an explicit proof, see Appendix
A
.

Chern-Simons theory and interacting BF theory.

Given the combined curvature
{\bf F}
, one may then assemble a gauge-invariant action and study its dynamics. Using the natural invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form Eq. (
2.4
) on
\mathfrak{d}
, we begin with the
Chern-Simons action

S_{\text{CS}}[{\bf A}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\langle\langle{\bf F}\wedge{\bf F}\rangle\rangle=\int_{M}\langle\bar{F}^{*}\wedge\bar{F}\rangle,

(2.14)

where
M
is a 4-dimensional manifold. It can be clearly seen that, due to the covariance of the combined curvature
{\bf F}
, the action Eq. (
2.14
) is invariant under the Drinfel’d double
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}
gauge transformations.

If we write down the components of
\bar{F},\bar{F}^{*}
explicitly, then one may compute

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}^{*}\wedge\bar{F}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d\left[\langle B\wedge(dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A])\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge A\rangle\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\langle[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge[A\wedge A]\rangle+\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle,

where the equality stands up to a boundary term.
By writing the final term as

\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}(\langle B\wedge[A\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)]\rangle+\langle[B\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)]_{*}\wedge A\rangle),

we can make use of Eq. (
2.11
) to have

[A\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)]=-\frac{1}{4}\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge[A\wedge A]),\qquad[B\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)]_{*}=-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge[B\wedge B]_{*}),

which yields

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}(\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{B}(\wedge B)\wedge[A\wedge A]\rangle+\langle[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\langle[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge[A\wedge A]\rangle.

This allows us to write the Chern-Simons action as

S_{\text{CS}}[{\bf A}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z}\langle B\wedge(dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A])\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge A\rangle,

where
Z=\partial M
is the 3-dimensional boundary of
M
. If we recall the ordinary curvature 2-form

F=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A],

then we can rewrite

S_{\text{CS}}[{\bf A}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z}\langle B\wedge F\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle B\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z}\langle B\wedge F\rangle+\langle\frac{1}{2}[B\wedge B]_{*}\wedge A\rangle,

which is in fact a rewritting (up to a boundary term) of the standard BF theory with a volume term
[
13
]
.

If we vary
B
, then we achieve the flatness condition

\delta_{A}S_{\text{CS}}=0\implies\bar{F}=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)=0

for the
\mathfrak{g}
-sector of the combined curvature Eq. (
2.13
). On the other hand, if we vary
A
, then we obtain the flatness condition

\delta_{B}S_{\text{CS}}=0\implies\bar{F}^{*}=dB+\frac{1}{2}[B\wedge B]_{*}-\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)=0

for the
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
-sector of the combined curvature.

Pairings on the Drinfel’d double; non-standard Chern-Simons theory.

The form of the Chern-Simons action Eq. (
2.14
) depends on the choice of the bilinear form
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle
on
\mathfrak{d}
. From the natural choice Eq. (
2.4
) given by the evaluation pairing
(\cdot,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the above result yields an interacting BF theory. However, more general choices are possible.

Given a bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\psi)
, we can form the structure of a Manin triple on the direct sum
\mathfrak{d}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by endowing it with a symmetric non-degenerate invariant bilinear form
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle:\mathfrak{d}^{2\otimes}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, with respect to which
\mathfrak{d}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{d}
is self-dual as bialgebras. It is clear that the natural evaluation pairing Eq. (
2.4
) is merely one component of
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle
. The other component is given by the alternative pairing
[
45
]

\langle\langle X+g,X^{\prime}+g^{\prime}\rangle\rangle^{\prime}=\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle+\langle g,g^{\prime}\rangle

that is "diagonal" in the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. It is invariant in the sense that

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{ad}_{X}X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle X^{\prime},\operatorname{ad}_{X}X^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{g}g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle g^{\prime},\mathfrak{ad}_{g}g^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle X^{\prime},\mathfrak{ad}_{g}^{*}X^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle g^{\prime},\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}g^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0

for each
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
g,g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Such a pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
exists if, for instance,
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, whence
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Killing form.

The Drinfel’d double
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{*}
has the same structure Eq. (
2.12

2.14

S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime}[{\bf A}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\langle\langle{\bf F}\wedge{\bf F}\rangle\rangle^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\langle\bar{F}^{*}\wedge\bar{F}^{*}\rangle+\langle\bar{F}\wedge\bar{F}\rangle.

Standard results states that the Chern-Simons polynomial
\langle\langle{\bf F}\wedge{\bf F}\rangle\rangle^{\prime}
is exact, hence
S^{\prime}_{\text{CS}}[{\bf A}]
reduces to the boundary action

S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime}[{\bf A}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z}\langle\langle{\bf A}\wedge d{\bf A}\rangle\rangle^{\prime}+\frac{1}{3}\langle\langle{\bf A}\wedge\boldsymbol{[}{\bf A}\wedge{\bf A}\boldsymbol{]}\rangle\rangle^{\prime}.

From Eq. (
2.13
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{[}{\bf A}\wedge{\bf A}\boldsymbol{]}=(\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]+\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A))+(\frac{1}{2}[B\wedge B]_{*}+\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)),

hence we see that

\displaystyle S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime}[{\bf A}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z}\langle A\wedge(dA+\frac{1}{3}[A\wedge A])\rangle+\langle B\wedge(dB+\frac{1}{3}[B\wedge B]_{*})\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{Z}\langle A\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle+\langle B\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(S_{\text{CS}}[A]+S_{\text{CS}}[B])+\int_{Z}\langle A\wedge\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}(\wedge A)\rangle+\langle B\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge B)\rangle,

where we have defined the usual Chern-Simons action
S_{\text{CS}}[A],S_{\text{CS}}[B]
in each of the individual
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*}
-sectors. The rest are interaction terms.

One may consider the Chern-Simons theory
S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime\prime}
given by a linear combination of the alternative pairing with the canonical one

\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle^{\prime\prime}=\alpha\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle+\beta\langle\langle\cdot\,\cdot\rangle\rangle^{\prime},

such that the non-degeneracy requires the real parameters

to satisfy
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\neq 0

S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime\prime}[{\bf A}]=\alpha S_{\text{CS}}[{\bf A}]+\beta S_{\text{CS}}^{\prime}[{\bf A}].

Lie algebra crossed-module and its dual

In this section, we construct a pair of dual Lie algebra crossed-modules, just like we defined a pair of dual Lie algebras. We also discuss the motion of adjoint and co-adjoint representations in this set up.

3.1
Lie algebra crossed-module.

We first review the notion of a Lie algebra crossed-module. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
be a pair of Lie algebras, a
Lie algebra crossed-module
is given in terms of a map

t:\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{0},

called the "
t
-map" or the
differential
, together with an action
\rhd
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
such that the
Pfeiffer identities

t(X\rhd Y)=[X,tY],\qquad tY\rhd Y^{\prime}=[Y,Y^{\prime}],\qquad\forall\penalty\ X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},\penalty\ Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}

(3.1)

are satisfied, in addition to the
2-Jacobi identities

\displaystyle[X,[X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}]]+[X^{\prime},[X^{\prime\prime},X]]+[X^{\prime\prime},[X,X^{\prime}]]=0,

\displaystyle X\rhd(X^{\prime}\rhd Y)-X^{\prime}\rhd(X\rhd Y)-[X,X^{\prime}]\rhd Y=0

(3.2)

for all
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
. Notice here that, by the second Pfeiffer identity Eq. (
3.1
), the Lie algebra bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
is completely determined by the structures
t,\rhd
. A Lie algebra crossed-module is also called a
strict Lie 2-algebra
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call a Lie algebra crossed-module
trivial
if
t=\operatorname{id}
, and
skeletal
if
t=0

[
46
,
32
,
28
,
1
]
.

We shall denote a Lie algebra crossed-module by
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\xrightarrow{t}\mathfrak{g}_{0},\rhd,[\cdot,\cdot])
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. This is indeed the minimal information one needs since, the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
is determined from the second Pfeiffer identity in terms of the action and
t
-map. The subscripts on the Lie algebras are called the
degrees of the grading
in
\mathfrak{g}
. It is a very useful tool for keeping track of the crossed-module structures.

Notice that there is a natural adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself. Since we have two pieces,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
, we can split this adjoint representation into two components, one coming from
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and one from
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
. Indeed, we have the bracket of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
on itself but also the action
\rhd
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
. The adjoint representation of the
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
sector is also specified by the crossed-module action
\rhd
.
This is denoted by

\operatorname{ad}=(\operatorname{ad}_{0},\operatorname{ad}_{-1}):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}\mathfrak{g},

(3.3)

which comes in graded components
\operatorname{ad}_{0},\operatorname{ad}_{-1}
defined by

\operatorname{ad}_{0}(X)=(\operatorname{ad}_{X}\equiv[X,\cdot]\,,\,\chi_{X}\equiv X\rhd\cdot)\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}),\qquad\operatorname{ad}_{-1}(Y)=\cdot\rhd Y\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{-1})

for each
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
.

Example 3.1
.

In general, there are two ways of associating a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
to a Lie algebra crossed-module.

1.

The canonical embedding: trivial crossed-module
: for any 1-algebra
\mathfrak{l}
, we define the crossed-module

\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{l}}=(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}}\mathfrak{g}_{0},\rhd,[\cdot,\cdot]),\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{l}

whose
t
-map is the identity. The Pfeiffer identities Eq. (
3.1
) force the action
\rhd=[\cdot,\cdot]
to be the adjoint action. We call the association
\mathfrak{l}\mapsto\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{l}}
the
canonical embedding
, and in fact embeds the category of Lie algebras into the category of Lie algebra crossed-modules
[
1
,
47
]
.

2.

The suspension embedding: skeletal crossed-module
: given the semidirect product
V\rtimes\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{u}
is a Lie algebra acting on the (abelian) Lie algebra
V
, we can associate a skeletal crossed-module structure to
V\rtimes\mathfrak{u}
,

\mathfrak{g}^{0}=(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\xrightarrow{0}\mathfrak{g}_{0},\rhd,[\cdot,\cdot]),\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=V,\penalty\ \mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{u}

to it. The Pfeiffer identities Eq. (
3.1
) enforce
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=V
to be Abelian (ie. a vector space), but otherwise imposes no constraints on the action
\rhd
. Provided

is Abelian, the crossed-module structure of
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
coincides with the Lie bracket of

[X+Y,X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}]=[X,X]+X\rhd Y^{\prime}-X^{\prime}\rhd Y,\qquad X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g},\penalty\ Y,Y^{\prime}\in V.

(3.4)

\mathfrak{g}^{0}
and

In the following, we shall also need the notion of a

A crossed-module is defined in terms of the Lie algebra

1.

The canonical bicrossed-module:
It is clear that any Lie 2-bialgebra structure on

2.

The suspension bialgebra crossed-module:
Given the semidirect product

\displaystyle((f+g)\wedge(f^{\prime}+g^{\prime}))(\psi(X+Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f+g,f^{\prime}+g^{\prime}]_{*}(X+Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f,f^{\prime}]_{*}(Y)+[g,g^{\prime}]_{*}(X)+f\rhd^{*}g^{\prime}(X)-f^{\prime}\rhd^{*}g(X)

[f,f^{\prime}](Y)=(f\wedge f^{\prime})(\delta_{-1}Y),\qquad(f\rhd^{*}g)(X)=(f\wedge g)(\delta_{0}X).

If the 1-cocycle

4
Recovering the 2-Drinfel’d double as a 2-gauge symmetry from dual 2-gauge theories

Similarly to the 1-gauge case, we are going to define a pair of 2-gauge theories, based on dual crossed-modules. Because they are dual, we will be able to define an action of the dual 2-gauge on the 2-gauge data and vice-versa. Then demanding that the order does not matter will put constraints which are the higher gauge equivalent of the matched pair of Lie algebras. Since now we have to deal with both 1- and 2-gauge transformations, there are more compatibility rules to consider.

4.1
Dual 2-gauge theories

Given the principal 2-bundle
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow M
with structure 2-group
\mathcal{G}
, it is equipped a
2-connection

(A,\Sigma)
consisting of a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-valued 1-form
A
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
-valued 2-form
\Sigma
.

The 2-gauge (infinitesimal) transformations are given by
[

,

,

(recall
[\cdot,\cdot]=\operatorname{ad}
)

\lambda:\begin{cases}A\rightarrow A^{\lambda}=A+d_{A}\lambda\\
\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma^{\lambda}=\Sigma+\lambda\rhd\Sigma\end{cases},\qquad L:\begin{cases}A\rightarrow A^{L}=A+tL\\
\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma^{L}=\Sigma+d_{A}L+\frac{1}{2}[L\wedge L]\end{cases},

where
d_{A}\lambda=d\lambda+[A,\lambda]
and
\lambda\in\Omega^{0}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
L\in\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
are gauge parameters. Given the following quantities

\mathcal{F}=F-t\Sigma,\qquad\mathcal{G}=d_{A}\Sigma

(4.1)

where
F=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A]
is the curvature of
A

[

,

\lambda:\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{F}+[\lambda,\mathcal{F}]\\
\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{G}+\lambda\rhd\mathcal{G}\end{cases},\qquad L:\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{L}=\mathcal{F}\\
\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{L}=\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{F}\wedge^{\rhd}L\end{cases}.

(4.2)

The quantity
\mathcal{F}
is known as

is the

4.2
The 2-Drinfel’d double as 2-gauge symmetry

We follow the same strategy as in Section
2.2
, and use Eqs. (
4.2
), (
4.4
), (
4.5

We begin by defining the combined 2-gauge parameter by

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}\in\Omega^{0}(M)\otimes(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}^{*}),\qquad{\bf L}=L+\tilde{L}\in\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*}).

We grouped them so that they have the same degree, as forms but also in terms of the grading.

We are going to consider the different combinations we can make in different order and impose that the order does not matter. This will impose a set of constraints which will be equivalent to the definition of the 2-Drinfel’d double.

4.2.1
Combined 1-gauge transformations

As in Section
2.2
, the 1-connection
A

modulo variations in the 1-gauge parameters.

1-gauge transformations of the 1-form connections.

We can follow an analogous treatment as previously, but with
\mathfrak{ad}^{*},\operatorname{ad}^{*}

This leads us to consider the

{\bf A}=A+C\in\Omega^{1}(M)\otimes(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}_{-1}^{*})

is unambiguous (independent of the order at which

iff

\displaystyle\eta^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda}}[A,\lambda]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\eta^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda}}A,\lambda]+[A,\eta^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda}}\lambda]-\eta_{\chi_{A}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda+\eta_{\chi_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A,

\displaystyle\chi^{*}_{\lambda}[C,\tilde{\lambda}]_{*}

\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda=-\tilde{\Delta}_{B}(\lambda),\qquad\operatorname{ad}_{\Sigma}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}=-\Delta_{\Sigma}(\tilde{\lambda}).

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\lambda}\rhd^{*}B)

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}\rhd^{*}(\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B)+(\chi_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{\lambda})\rhd^{*}B-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\lambda}B+\Delta_{\tilde{\Delta}_{B}(\lambda)}(\tilde{\lambda}).

The
B

combined 2-connection

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\Sigma+B\in\Omega^{2}(M)\otimes(\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*})

(-1)
components of the direct sum

Moreover, a modified covariant action is also introduced by the combined 2-connection

\displaystyle D_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\rhd\Sigma-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda=\operatorname{ad}_{-1}(\Sigma)(\lambda)-\mathfrak{ad}_{B}^{*}\lambda,

\displaystyle D_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\tilde{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}\rhd^{*}B-\operatorname{ad}_{\Sigma}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}=\mathfrak{ad}_{-1}(B)(\tilde{\lambda})-\operatorname{ad}_{\Sigma}^{*}\tilde{\lambda}.

This yields the following result:

Lemma 4.2
.

The 1-gauge transformation (including antisymmetrized terms in Eq. (
4.11
))

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}+(D_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\lambda+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\Sigma)+(D_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\tilde{\lambda}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}B)

o(\lambda^{2})+o(\tilde{\lambda}^{2})+o(\tilde{\lambda}d\lambda)+o(\lambda d\tilde{\lambda})

iff

second compatibility conditions

4.2.2
Combined 2-gauge transformations

Let us now study the 2-gauge transformations under the 2-gauge parameter
{\bf L}=L+\tilde{L}
. As
A,C
do not transform respectively under
\tilde{L},L
, we see that there is no ambiguity in
{\bf A}
under
{\bf L}

{\bf A}\rightarrow{\bf A}^{\bf L}={\bf A}+tL+\tilde{t}\tilde{L}={\bf A}+T{\bf L},

where we introduced the total

On the other hand, the 2-connections
\Sigma,B
do transform under
\tilde{L}
and
L
respectively, but only as shifts by

and
\tilde{L}
on each other, and terms of cubic order

do not effect our results, and we shall neglect them in the following.

Now we consider the 2-gauge shift transformations on the 2-connections
\Sigma,B
, and demand the difference in the different order to be zero. Similar to before, this leads to a modification of the 2-gauge transformation, because we must antisymmetrize the terms in these differences with respect to
\tilde{L},A
and
L,C
, respectively. This can be accomplished by inserting the terms
-\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}\tilde{L}
into how

\tilde{L}
in Eq. (
4.5
). Similarly, we insert the term
-\mathfrak{ad}_{C}^{*}L
into how
\Sigma
transforms under
L
in Eq. (
4.2
). In other words, we introduce the 2-gauge covariant derivatives

D^{(1)}_{\bf A}L=d_{A}L-\mathfrak{ad}_{C}^{*}(\wedge L),\qquad D^{(1)}_{\bf A}\tilde{L}=d_{C}\tilde{L}-\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge\tilde{L})

(4.16)

of the matched pair
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*}
in degree-(-1).

We can check explicitly now that the order of the transformations does not matter provided we have some consistency conditions that we shall now derive. We compute directly with Eqs. (
4.2
), (
4.4
) and the modified 2-gauge covariant derivatives Eq. (
4.16
):

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\bf L}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\Sigma\xrightarrow{L}\Sigma+d_{A}L-\mathfrak{ad}_{C}^{*}(\wedge L)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}\Sigma+d_{A}L+\tilde{\Delta}_{\tilde{L}}(\wedge A)-\mathfrak{ad}_{C}^{*}(\wedge L)

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{t}\tilde{L}}^{*}(\wedge L).

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\bf L}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\Sigma\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}\Sigma+\tilde{\Delta}_{\tilde{L}}(\wedge A)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{L}\Sigma+d_{A}L+\tilde{\Delta}_{\tilde{L}}(\wedge A)-\mathfrak{ad}_{C}^{*}(\wedge L)

Similarly, we may compute with Eqs.(

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\bf L}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle B\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}B+d_{C}\tilde{L}-\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{*}(\wedge\tilde{L})

\displaystyle\qquad-\operatorname{ad}_{tL}^{*}(\wedge\tilde{L}).

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\bf L}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle B\xrightarrow{L}B+\Delta_{L}(\wedge C)

from which we see that the condition

We therefore have the following result:

Lemma 4.3

The combined gauge transformation (including antisymmetrized terms)

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\xrightarrow{\bf L}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\bf L}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}+(D_{\bf A}^{(1)}L+\tilde{\Delta}_{\tilde{L}}(\wedge A))+(D_{\bf A}^{(1)}\tilde{L}+\Delta_{L}(\wedge C))

iff

\tilde{t}=t^{*}
and
\Delta=\operatorname{ad}_{-1}^{*},\tilde{\Delta}=\mathfrak{ad}_{-1}^{*}

4.2.3
Mixed 1- and 2-gauge transformations

Let us now study the combined gauge transformations under the mixed parameter
(\tilde{\lambda},L)

First, as
C

By Leibniz rule, we see that we have

dL\rightarrow dL+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}dL=dL+d(\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}L)-\mathfrak{ad}_{d\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\wedge L,

hence this induces an action of
\tilde{\lambda}
on
L

L\rightarrow L+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}L+o(d\tilde{\lambda}L)

modulo terms proportional to
d\tilde{\lambda}
.

On the dual side, we similarly have no action of
\tilde{L}
on
\lambda
, as
A
is invariant under
\tilde{L}
. On the other hand, we have

d\tilde{L}\rightarrow d\tilde{L}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}d\tilde{L}=d\tilde{L}+d(\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{L})-\operatorname{ad}_{d\lambda}^{*}\wedge\tilde{L},

which induces the action of
\lambda
on
\tilde{L}
:

\tilde{L}\rightarrow\tilde{L}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}L+o(d\lambda\tilde{L})

modulo terms of order
o(d\lambda\tilde{L})
.

On 1-connections.

With this acquired, we now consider how the 1-connections transform under these mixed gauge transformations. First as
\tilde{L}
acts trivially on
A
, and similarly
L
acts trivially on
C
, we see that there is in fact no ambiguity

On the other hand, we see that we have

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{(\tilde{\lambda},L)}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}A+\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A\xrightarrow{L}A+tL+\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}tL,

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{(\tilde{\lambda},L)}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A\xrightarrow{L}A+tL\xrightarrow{\tilde{\lambda}}A+\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+tL+t\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}L,

and similarly

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{(\lambda,\tilde{L})}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle C\xrightarrow{\lambda}C+\chi_{\lambda}^{*}C\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}C+\tilde{t}\tilde{L}+\chi_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{t}\tilde{L},

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{(\lambda,\tilde{L})}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle C\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}C+\tilde{t}\tilde{L}\xrightarrow{\lambda}C+\chi_{\lambda}^{*}C+\tilde{t}\tilde{L}+\tilde{t}\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}L.

and the condition for them to vanish — with

understood — is in fact nothing but the Pfeiffer identities

\eta_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}\tilde{t}^{*}L=\tilde{t}^{*}\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}L,\qquad\chi_{\lambda}^{*}t^{*}\tilde{L}=t^{*}\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{L}

in Eq. (
3.12
). In other words, we have the following result:

Lemma 4.4
.

The mixed gauge transformations

A\xrightarrow{(\tilde{\lambda},L)}A+tL+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}A+\mathfrak{ad}_{\tilde{\lambda}}^{*}tL,\qquad C\xrightarrow{(\lambda,\tilde{L})}C+\tilde{t}\tilde{L}+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}C+\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda}^{*}\tilde{t}\tilde{L}

iff

\tilde{t}=t^{*}
and
\operatorname{ad}_{0}^{*}=(\operatorname{ad}^{*},\chi^{*}),\mathfrak{ad}_{0}^{*}=(\mathfrak{ad}^{*},\eta^{*})

3.12

We therefore see that

4.4
make Eqs. (

On 2-connections.

We now turn to the mixed gauge transformations of the 2-connections

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{(\lambda,\tilde{L})}

\displaystyle\Sigma\xrightarrow{\lambda}\Sigma+\lambda\rhd\Sigma\xrightarrow{\tilde{L}}\Sigma+\lambda\rhd\Sigma+\tilde{\Delta}_{\tilde{L}}(\wedge A)

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we extracted the structures of the Drinfel’d double
\mathfrak{d}
of a Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and subsequently derived the compatibility relations Eq. (
2.11
) from gauge-theoretic considerations. Moreover, we have generalized (ie. "categorified") this method to the case of the Lie 2-bialgebra/bialgebra crossed-module, and explicitly derived the compatibility relations Eqs. (
4.20
), (
4.21
), (
4.22
) of the 2-Drinfel’d double through 2-gauge theory.

It is important to note that our results, in particular
Theorems
2.1
,
4.1
, agree completely with existing mathematical literature
[
16
,
17
,
32
,
1
]
. Though these derived compatibility conditions are not strictly speaking new results, the new insight is that we can in fact derive them from gauge-theoretical considerations. This means that our approach opens the door for understanding the structure of higher Drinfel’d doubles in a similar manner. For instance, we could start with a 3-gauge theory
[
49
]
, and apply our techniques to develop the notion of 3-Drinfel’d double associated to 3-bialgebras (which are defined in
[
57
]
).

We had also briefly studied topological theories based on the (2-)Drinfel’d doubles. The
BF
theory Eq. (
5.6
) is of particular interest. Indeed, it is an essential tool to construct quantum gravity amplitude in the spin foam approach
[
34
,
37
]
. It has been argued that it could be relevant to also include some edge decorations to have access to the frame field, which can be done using 2-gauge theories
[
58
,
50
,
56
]
. To unlock new types of symmetries could therefore open new ways to construct spin foam models.

In general, we believe that such a procedure would lead to a systematic understanding of the structures of a "quantum 2-group", which is very much sought after in many areas of physics
[
59
,
60
,
61
]
.

We would like to highlight some directions we find interesting to develop in a near future.

Recovering the Crane-Yetter-Broda amplitude.

As we emphasized, the Drinfel’d double fits in a 3D Chern-Simons theory Eq. (
2.14
), while the 2-Drinfel’d double fits in a 4D BF-theory Eq. (
5.6
). The trivial canonical embedding
\mathfrak{d}\mapsto\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{d}}
can therefore be seen as the most natural way of "promoting" a 3D topological gauge theory to a 4D one, while keeping all of the algebra of the field contents identical.
With this in mind, it would interesting to see whether one can recover (more rigorously) the Crane-Yetter-Broda amplitude (ie. a 15-
j
symbol) defined in terms of a quantum group
[
62
,
36
]
as the amplitude associated to the
BF+BB
action. This would in particular bring more strength to the quantum gravity models which implement the cosmological constant using a quantum group deformation. It would also clarify how and why the presence of a non-zero cosmological constant is also incorporated in the quantum gravity regime through a quantum group structure, in 4D.

2-graded integrable systems.

In this paper, we studied the 2-graded classical
r
-matrices on
\mathfrak{d}
, as well as the quadratic 2-Casimirs. Particular examples in the cases
t=0,1
were worked out explicitly, and we have found that, in these cases, the notion of duality for the 1-algebra case
[
17
]
all carry over to the Lie 2-algebra context, including the classification of the classical
r
-matrices.

The notion of quantum/Poisson group was initially developed in the context of integrable systems. The use of the
r
-matrix is quite instrumental in understanding and classifying the notion of integral system. Now that we have Lie 2-bialgebras and the associated notion of
r
-matrix, we can explore how such formalism can be used to develop new notions of integrable systems. This is currently under investigation.

Fourier transform between dual 2-groups.

A key feature of the Drinfel’d double is that we have a duality between Lie algebras, which can be extended to Hopf algebras
[
7
]
. In more physical terms, this duality pertains a notion of Fourier transform, relating possibly non-commutative structures
[
43
,
63
,
64
]
. This notion of Fourier transform is different than (but related to) the standard Fourier transformation of group elements in terms of representations. The representation theory is still missing for most 2-groups (except the skeletal case in
[
65
]
), so that if we intend to perform a Fourier transform for the notion of 2-groups, we need to resort to the duality between functions over dual 2-groups. Such duality is embedded in the definition of the 2-Drinfel’d double, hence we can expect many of the key-notions the plane-wave need to satisfy should be recoverable from the (quantum) 2-Drinfel’d double.

Weak Lie 2-algebras and their 2-double.

All along in this paper we have worked with strict Lie 2-algebras, which are equivalent to crossed-modules. However, there is in fact an additional piece of datum that characterizes a crossed-module
\mathfrak{g}
. If we denote by
V=\operatorname{ker}t
and
\operatorname{coker}t=\mathfrak{u}
the kernel and cokernel of the crossed-module map

\kappa\in H^{3}(\mathfrak{u},V)
called the
Postnikov class

[
59
]
, which classifies
\mathfrak{g}
up to elementary equivalence
[
66
]
. Working explicitly with the crossed-module structure, it would be interesting to see how our above (2-)gauge-theoretic formalism extends to take the Postnikov class into account.

The motivation for doing so arises from the correspondence between crossed-modules with non-trivial Postnikov class and
weak 2-algebras
. In essence, the structure of a weak Lie 2-algebra introduces a skew-trilinear
homotopy map

\displaystyle[X,[X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}]]+[X^{\prime},[X^{\prime\prime},X]]+[X^{\prime\prime},[X,X^{\prime}]]=t\eta(X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle X\rhd(X^{\prime}\rhd Y)-X^{\prime}\rhd(X\rhd Y)-[X,X^{\prime}]\rhd Y=\eta(X,X^{\prime},tY)

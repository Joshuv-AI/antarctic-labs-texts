Spin relaxation dynamics with a continuous spin environment: the dissipaton equation of motion approach
Wenxiang Ying, Yu Su, Zi-Hao Chen, Yao Wang, Pengfei Huo

Abstract
We present the quantum dynamics of a spin coupling to a bath of independent spins via the dissipaton equation of motion (DEOM) approach. The bath, characterized by a continuous spectral density function, is composed of spins that are independent level systems described by the 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{su}(2) Lie algebra. This represents an extreme class of anharmonic environment. Based on the conclusion drawn by Suarez and Silbey [J. Chem. Phys. 95, 9115 (1991)] and Makri [J. Chem. Phys. 111, 6164 (1999)] that the spin bath can be mapped to a Gaussian environment under its linear response limit, we derive the fluctuation-dissipation theorem (FDT) of the spin bath from a microscopic perspective, and generalize the discussion to the case of arbitrary bath spin quantum number S S . Next, the time-domain Prony fitting decomposition scheme is applied to the bare-bath time correlation function (TCF) given by FDT to generate the exponential decay basis (or pseudo modes) for DEOM construction. The accuracy and efficiency of this strategy has been justified by a variety of numerical results. We envision this work provides new insights to extend the hierarchical equations of motion (HEOM) and DEOM approach to certain types of anharmonic enviroments with arbitrary TCF or spectral density.

Linear response of the spin bath, and the generalized theory of effective spectral density function

-PFD strategy. The resulting numerical efficiency of HEOM/DEOM is optimized to a great extent, especially in low temperature regimes that are usually inaccessible for other methods.

The
t
-PFD scheme is intrinsically based on least-squares fitting algorithm, in which the real and imaginary parts of TCF are fitted separately. It is easy to extract from the bosonic FDT (c.f. Eq.(
12
)) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}[C(t)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\ J_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega;\beta)\coth\left(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\right)\cos(\omega t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\ J(\omega)\cos(\omega t),

(23a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}[C(t)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\ J_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega;\beta)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\ J(\omega)\tanh(\beta\omega/2)\sin(\omega t),

(23b)

where the second line in Eq.(
23a
) and (
23b
) are the explicit results for
S=1/2
case. Intriguingly, temperature dependence is only carried by the imaginary part of TCF, in line with fermion bath but the reverse of boson bath.

Next, we target at optimizing
K=K_{r}+K_{i}
in

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}[C(t)]=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{r}}\zeta_{k}e^{-\lambda_{k}t},\ \ \ \mathrm{Im}[C(t)]=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\zeta^{\prime}_{k}e^{-\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t},

(24)

where
K_{r}
and
K_{i}
are the number of terms in real and imaginary part fitting, respectively. We accordingly denote the
t
-PFD strategy as
K_{r}+K_{i}
. See Ref.
62
and reference therein for detailed procedures of
t
-PFD, as well as the numerical benchmarks for several commonly used spectral density functions. We also provide more illustrations and examples on the performances of TCF fitting using
t
-PFD in Appendix
B
, with respect to the most challenging SSB models we tested.

IV
Concluding Remarks

We present the numerical benchmark results of the spin relaxation dynamics of various SSB models with continuous spectral density function by using DEOM with
t
-PFD. The idea of mapping boson bath with temperature-dependent effective spectral density is validated from a microscopic perspective, and generalized to arbitrary bath spin quantum number
S
. The highlight of this strategy is that one can approximate a class of non-Gaussian bath to the Gaussian one under the linear response limit. By applying the
t
-PFD to the bath TCF, one obtains the exponential decay basis to construct DEOM and propagate. The accuracy and numerical efficiency of this strategy is illustrated by various of examples. DEOM with
t
-PFD provides an excellent agreement with the ML-MCTDH results reported in literature, despite the rather challenging model parameters. Moreover, comparing to previous research work on the SSB model based on ML-MCTDH, modular path integral,
etc.
, DEOM equipped with
t
-PFD has the advantage that the bath spectral density is rigorously continuous, no B-O type of approximation is needed; and the finite-temperautre strategy is based on FDT rather than stochastic sampling, thus allowing long time propagation. In summary, it serves as a novel and more efficient benchmark scheme for quantum dynamics with a spin bath.

The presented theoretical work provide new insights to extend HEOM/DEOM to certain types of non-Gaussian environments with arbitrary bath TCFs or spectral density functions. Future research work on methods development shall be carried out on more complicated types of interacting system-bath models that listed in Eq.(
3
), with incorporation of quadratic or higher order system-bath couplings, as well as the nonlinear response effects; we would also like to seek for applications in the simulation of radical pair spin relaxation dynamics that exposed to an enviroment of nuclear spins.

Appendix A
FDT for the spin bath, and the effective bosonic environment

A.1
S=1/2
case

For spin-1/2 particles, we adopt the well-known Jordan-Wigner correspondence
Jordan and Wigner 1929
for the spin operators:

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{s}^{j}_{x}+i\hat{s}^{j}_{y},

(28a)

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{s}^{j}_{x}-i\hat{s}^{j}_{y},

(28b)

\displaystyle\hat{s}^{j}_{z}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{d}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}.

(28c)

where
\hat{d}_{j}
,
\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}
are the mapping
fermionic
annihilation/creation operators with anti-commutation relation on the same site. However, on different sites we have bosonic commutation relations, which means spins on different sites commute, unlike fermions which anti-commute. For this reason, spins are also referred to as
hard-core bosons
or
spinless fermions
,
Girardeau 1960
whose commutation relations can be summarized as

\displaystyle\{\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{j}\}=\{\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger},\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}\}=0,\ \ \ \{\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}\}=1;

(29a)

\displaystyle\{\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{k}\}=2\hat{d}_{j}\hat{d}_{k},\ \ \ \{\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j},\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=2\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k},

\displaystyle\{\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}\}=2\hat{d}_{j}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k},\ \ \ k\neq j;

(29b)

\displaystyle[\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{j}]=[\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger},\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}]=0,\ \ \ [\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}]=1-2\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{d}_{j};

(29c)

\displaystyle[\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{k}]=[\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger},\hat{d}_{k}^{\dagger}]=[\hat{d}_{j},\hat{d}_{k}^{\dagger}]=0,\ \ \ k\neq j.

(29d)

As is shown that the hard-core bosons possess characters of both bosons and fermions, one can either define the linear response functions using commutator or the single particle Green’s function using anti-commutator. Here we will show both possibilities. For convenience, let’s first write down the time evolution of single bath operators,

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{i\hat{h}_{\textsc{B}}t}\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}(0)e^{-i\hat{h}_{\textsc{B}}t}=\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}(0)e^{i\omega_{j}t},

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{i\hat{h}_{\textsc{B}}t}\hat{d}_{j}(0)e^{-i\hat{h}_{\textsc{B}}t}=\hat{d}_{j}(0)e^{-i\omega_{j}t},

(30)

where we have used the well-known Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff identity.
Hall 2015

We will start with the anti-commutator version. For each pair of mapping spinless fermionic creation/annihilation operators
\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{d}_{j}
that satisfy the commutation relations defined in Eq.(
29
), the single particle Green’s functions can be defined and evaluated as

\displaystyle\langle\{\hat{d}_{j}(t),\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}e^{-i\omega_{j}t},

\displaystyle\langle\{\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}(t),\hat{d}_{k}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}e^{i\omega_{j}t},

\displaystyle\langle\{\hat{d}_{j}(t),\hat{d}_{k}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}

\displaystyle=\langle\{\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}(t),\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{k}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}=0.

(31)

So we have

\displaystyle\langle\{\hat{F}(t),\hat{F}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}=\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\cos(\omega_{j}t).

(32)

As a result, the spectral density function is evaluated as (c.f. Eq.(
8b
))

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\ e^{i\omega t}\langle\{\hat{F}(t),\hat{F}(0)\}\rangle_{\textsc{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}[\delta(\omega-\omega_{j})+\delta(\omega+\omega_{j})],

(33)

which is an extension of the result given by Caldeira and Leggett
Caldeira and Leggett 1983
(see also Eq.(
4
)) to negative frequencies
\omega<0
, while ensuring it to be an even function.

The derivation via the commutator version is very similar to the anti-commutator one. Following the same procedure, one can easily obtain the bare-bath linear response function as

\displaystyle i\langle[\hat{F}(t),\hat{F}(0)]\rangle_{\textsc{B}}=\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\langle(1-2\hat{d}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{d}_{j})\rangle_{\textsc{B}}\sin(\omega_{j}t).

(34)

For independent spin
S=1/2
particles, we have

\displaystyle Z_{\textsc{B}}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{\textsc{B}}[e^{-\beta\hat{h}_{\textsc{B}}}]=\prod_{j}2\cosh\left(\frac{\beta\omega_{j}}{2}\right),

(35)

one immediately obtains

\displaystyle i\langle[\hat{F}(t),\hat{F}(0)]\rangle_{\textsc{B}}=\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\omega_{j}}{2}\right)\sin(\omega_{j}t).

(36)

And the corresponding spectral density function reads as (c.f. Eq.(
8a
))

\displaystyle J(\omega)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt\ e^{i\omega t}\langle[\hat{F}(t),\hat{F}(0)]\rangle_{\textsc{B}}

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\omega_{j}}{2}\right)[\delta(\omega-\omega_{j})-\delta(\omega+\omega_{j})],

which is an odd function. On the other hand, we can rewrite Eq.(
37
) as

\displaystyle J(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\right)[\delta(\omega-\omega_{j})+\delta(\omega+\omega_{j})]

\displaystyle=J^{\prime}(\omega)\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\right),

(38)

where
J^{\prime}(\omega)
is defined in Eq.(
A.1
), giving rise to the temperature-dependent effective spectral density.

Combining Eq.(

\displaystyle C(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\hat{F}(t)\hat{F}(0)\rangle_{\textsc{B}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}\left[\frac{e^{-i\omega_{j}t}}{1+e^{-\beta\omega_{j}}}+\frac{e^{i\omega_{j}t}}{1+e^{\beta\omega_{j}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}c^{2}_{j}[\delta(\omega-\omega_{j})+\delta(\omega+\omega_{j})]\frac{e^{-i\omega t}}{1+e^{-\beta\omega}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\frac{e^{-i\omega t}J^{\prime}(\omega)}{1+e^{-\beta\omega}},

(39)

which is just Eq.(

) in the main text, the FDT for spin bath. It has an equivalent bosonic FDT formalism if one takes

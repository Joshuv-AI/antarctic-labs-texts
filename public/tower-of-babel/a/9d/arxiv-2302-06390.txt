Neutrino mixing angle and neutrino oscillation in ALPs matter
Alexey Lichkunov, Alexander Studenikin

Abstract
Axions and axion-like particles (ALPs) are among of the most popular candidates for dark matter. Axions are also considered as new physics contributions to the muon g – 2 . Following the existed interest to ALPs we consider interaction between neutrinos and hypothetical axion-like particles and derive for the first time the probability of neutrino oscillations accounting for their interactions mediated by ALPs. The corresponding effective mixing angle is derived.



I
Neutrino evolution in ALPs background

Consider neutrino flavor mixing and oscillations in a background formed by axion-like particles. For simplicity we deal two-flavour neutrino case (
\nu_{e}
and
\nu_{\mu}
). A similar approach in the case of neutrino propagation in an external electromagnetic field and moving matter can be found in
Giunti:2014ixa
;
Pustoshny:2018jxb
.

The Lagrangian of interaction between flavour neutrinos and ALPs can be chosen in the following form (see also
Ma:2022
for the charged lepton interaction with ALPs):

L_{int}=-i\dfrac{\partial_{\mu}a}{F}\bar{\nu}_{i}^{(f)}\gamma^{\mu}\left({\bar{g}}_{V}^{ij}+{\bar{g}}_{A}^{ij}\gamma_{5}\right)\nu_{j}^{(f)},

(1)

where
\nu_{i}^{(f)}
is a neutrino flavor state,
a
is the axion-like pseudoscalar field,
{\bar{g}}_{V}^{ij}
and
{\bar{g}}_{A}^{ij}
are the vector and axial-vector interaction constants.

The neutrino flavour states by the mixing matrix
U
are connected to the neutrino mass states
\nu_{i}^{(p)}
,

\nu^{(f)}=U\nu^{(p)}.

(2)

The Lagrangian (
1
) can be converted (see, for instance,
Ma:2022
) to:

L_{int}=-i\dfrac{a}{F}\bar{\nu}_{i}^{(p)}\left(g_{V}^{ij}(m_{i}-m_{j})+g_{A}^{ij}(m_{i}+m_{j})\gamma_{5}\right)\nu_{j}^{(p)},

(3)

where
g_{V}^{ij}
and
g_{A}^{ij}
are the corresponding constants in the basis neutrino mass states.
Using this interaction Lagrangian the following Dirac equation for the neutrino field
\nu_{i}
can be derived,

E_{i}\nu_{i}=\left(\gamma_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma p_{i}$}+m_{i}\gamma_{0}\right)\nu_{i}+\dfrac{a}{F}\sum_{j}\left(g_{V}^{ij}(m_{i}-m_{j})-g_{A}^{ij}(m_{i}+m_{j})\gamma_{5}\right)\gamma_{0}\nu_{j},

(4)

where
E_{i}
is the energy of the neutrino mass state.

The shift of the neutrino evolution Hamiltonian in the ALPs background can be calculated by averaging the interaction term in (
4
) (similar to calculations described in detail in
Pustoshny:2018jxb
):

\Delta_{ij}=\left\langle\nu_{i}|H_{int}|\nu_{j}\right\rangle.

(5)

Finally, for the neutrino mass states evolution Hamiltonian we get

\displaystyle H=\begin{pmatrix}E_{1}&H_{12}\\
H_{21}&E_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where
H_{ij}=H_{ij}^{V}+H_{ij}^{A}
,

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{V}=\dfrac{a}{F}\left[g_{V}^{ij}(m_{i}-m_{j})\left(1+\dfrac{p^{2}}{(E_{i}+m_{i})(E_{j}+m_{j})}\right)\right]\sqrt{\dfrac{(E_{i}+m_{i})(E_{j}+m_{j})}{4E_{i}E_{j}}}

(7)

and

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{A}=-\dfrac{a}{F}\left[g_{A}^{ij}(m_{i}+m_{j})p\left(\dfrac{1}{E_{i}+m_{i}}-\dfrac{1}{E_{j}+m_{j}}\right)\right]\sqrt{\dfrac{(E_{i}+m_{i})(E_{j}+m_{j})}{4E_{i}E_{j}}}.

(8)

II
Probability of neutrino flavor oscillations

From (
6
), (
7
) and (
8
) we get for the effective evolution Hamiltonian for the flavour neutrino states

H^{\prime}=\dfrac{\Delta}{4p}\left(\sigma_{1}\sin 2\theta-\sigma_{3}\cos 2\theta\right)+H_{12}^{V}\left(\sigma_{1}\cos 2\theta+\sigma_{3}\sin 2\theta\right)-iH_{12}^{A}\sigma_{2},

(9)

where
\theta
is the neutrino mixing angle in vacuum,
\Delta=m^{2}_{2}-m^{2}_{1}
is the neutrino mass square difference,
and
\sigma_{i}
are the Pauli matrices.
For the ultra-relativistic neutrinos (
E\gg m_{i}
the Hamiltonian can be approximated by

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{V}=\dfrac{a}{F}g^{ij}_{V}(m_{i}-m_{j}),

(10)

\displaystyle H_{ij}^{A}=0.

(11)

The probability of neutrino flavour oscillations in the ALPs background is just straigthforward:

\displaystyle P_{\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}}(x)=\sin^{2}2\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}\sin^{2}\dfrac{\pi x}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(12)

where the oscillation length is given by

L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\Delta}{4p}\right)^{2}+\left(H_{12}^{V}\right)^{2}}}.

(13)

For the effective mixing angle we get

\sin^{2}2\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{\Delta}{4p}\sin 2\theta+H_{12}^{V}\cos 2\theta\right)^{2}}{\left(\dfrac{\Delta}{4p}\right)^{2}+\left(H_{12}^{V}\right)^{2}}.

(14)

This equation can be rearranged to the form:

\sin^{2}2\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sin^{2}2(\theta+\theta_{\mathrm{add}}),

(15)

where

\theta_{\mathrm{add}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\arctan\dfrac{4pH_{12}^{V}}{\Delta}=\dfrac{1}{2}\arctan\dfrac{4pag^{12}_{V}}{F(m_{1}+m_{2})}.

(16)

It is interesting to note that in the case of the considered neutrino propagation in an ALPs background the neutrino mixing and oscillations exhibit the linear dependence on the neutrino masses.

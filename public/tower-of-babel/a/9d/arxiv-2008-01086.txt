Implications for new physics from a novel puzzle in 𝑩 ¯ → ( 𝒔 ) 𝟎 𝑫 ( 𝒔 ) ( ∗ ) + { 𝝅 − , 𝑲 − } \kern 2.0699pt\overline{\kern-2.0699ptB}{}{}^{0}_{(s)}\to D^{(\ast)+}_{(s)}\{\pi^{-},K^{-}\} decays
Syuhei Iguro, Teppei Kitahara

Abstract
Recently, the standard model predictions for the B B -meson hadronic decays, B ¯ 0 → D ( ∗ ) + K − \kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}\to D^{(\ast)+}K^{-} and B ¯ 0 s → D ( ∗ ) + s π − \kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(\ast)+}_{s}\pi^{-} , have been updated based on the QCD factorization approach. This improvement sheds light on a novel puzzle in the B B -meson hadronic decays: there are mild but universal tensions between data and the predicted branching ratios. Assuming the higher-order QCD corrections are not huge enough to solve the tension, we examine several new physics interpretations of this puzzle. We find that the tension can be partially explained by a left-handed W ′ W^{\prime} model, which can be compatible with other flavor observables and collider bounds.



I
Introduction

To test the standard model (SM) and search for physics beyond the SM,
precision measurements of meson decays, especially
B
-meson decays,
have been considerably investigated over the past 30 years.
In the meantime,
the experimental uncertainty has been surprisingly reduced by experimentalists.
On the other hand, theorists have
played an equally important role:
several approaches that can evaluate the QCD corrections have been invented, and
the SM predictions have been sharpened.

Very recently, SM predictions for several
B
-meson hadronic decays are improved by Ref.

:

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(1.86\pm 0.20\right)\times 10^{-4}\,,\\

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(2.12\pm 0.15\right)\times 10^{-4}\,,\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}_{s}^{0}\to D^{+}_{s}\pi^{-})^{\rm exp}_{\rm SM}

Within the SM,
there are two possibilities that these tensions are alleviated.
The first possibility is an input value of
|V_{cb}|

For
|V_{cb}|
, the authors of Ref.
Bordone:2020gao
use an average of the inclusive and exclusive determinations in the

;
Bordone:2019guc

Another possibility is higher-order QCD corrections.

The above puzzled situation could be resolved by introducing new physics contributions to

Therefore, the following questions are interesting:
whether such a new physics is still allowed by the other flavor constraints and by the hadron collider constraints, and
how much the tensions can be alleviated by a valid new physics model.
Below we will refer to this puzzle as
b\to c\bar{u}q

In this Letter,
we examine several new physics scenarios to explain the
b\to c\bar{u}q

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{q}V_{cb}V_{uq}^{\ast}\sum_{i=1,2}C_{i}^{q}(\mu)\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{q}(\mu)\,,

(9)

\displaystyle=(\bar{c}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}T^{a}b_{L})(\bar{q}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}T^{a}u_{L})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{q}

\displaystyle=(\bar{c}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L})(\bar{q}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}u_{L})\,,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}^{\rm NP}(m_{b})\\

-0.19&1.07\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}^{\rm NP}(1\,{\rm TeV})\\

III
Minimal flavor violation

First, we study the most simple possibility for new physics scenario:
minimal flavor violation (MFV) hypothesis

DAmbrosio:2002vsn
;
Isidori:2012ts
.
The detailed calculations for this section can be found in Appendix
B
.

We examine a dimension-six operator,

\mathcal{L}=1/(2\Lambda^{2})(\bar{Q}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{L})^{2}
, whose flavor off-diagonal components are controlled by the quark Yukawa.
In the quark mass-diagonal basis,
this operator produces

C_{2}^{q,{\rm MFV}}(\Lambda)\sim-1/(2\sqrt{2}G_{F}\Lambda^{2})
.
Then, the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly in Eq. (
13
) suggests
\Lambda\lesssim 0.49\,{\rm TeV}
.

Among the various flavor and collider constraints, a nonresonant dijet angular distribution search in the LHC gives the most stringent constraint on this scenario.
The result is reported by the ATLAS collaboration
at
\sqrt{s}=13\,{\rm TeV}
with
\int dt\mathcal{L}=37
fb
-1

Aaboud:2017yvp
.
We interpret the result and obtain a
95\%
C.L. exclusion limit as

\Lambda<3.7\,{\rm TeV}
, which excludes the suggested
\Lambda\sim 0.49\,{\rm TeV}
.
From this collider constraint, we obtain a bound

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}^{\rm MFV}(m_{b})}{C_{2}^{\rm SM}(m_{b})}\gtrsim-0.002\,.

(18)

Hence, this scenario never explains the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly.

Note that both our study and above experimental analyses have considered only the
s
-channel productions of
W^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime}
, although there are several
t
-channel contributions.
Since the
t
-channel process does not show a resonant nature, and there is a huge QCD
t
-channel background in the dijet production,
we suppose that inclusion of the
t
-channel processes in the signal could not amplify the signal-to-noise ratio in the resonance searches.
Such
t
-channel contributions, which are insensitive to the width, would be potentially accessible in the angular distribution search.

As long as we allow the broad width scenario,
we find that the bound from
\Delta M_{s}
in Eq. (
25

C_{2}^{W^{\prime}}/C_{2}^{\rm SM}
, which is
independent of the ratio of
g_{11}
and
g_{33}
.
For these reasons, we conclude
C_{2}^{W^{\prime}}/C_{2}^{\rm SM}\gtrsim-0.05
when

g_{23}=0
.

g_{11}
and

For the second scenario,

g_{33}=0
and consider
g_{11}
and
g_{23}
in Eq. (

Such a flavor structure can be obtained when
n_{\rm VF}=2
.
In this scenario, the
b\to c\bar{u}q

\displaystyle 0.54\,({\rm TeV})^{-1}\lesssim\sqrt{|g_{11}g_{23}|}/M_{V}\lesssim 0.78\,({\rm TeV})^{-1}\,.

(27)

Although the size of the required coupling product is much smaller than the previous scenario,
a severe bound on
g_{23}
comes from
\Delta M_{s}
,
where there is a tree-level
Z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta M_{{s}}^{Z^{\prime}}}{\Delta M_{{s}}^{\rm SM}}\simeq\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}\frac{16\pi^{2}g_{23}^{2}}{g_{W}^{4}(V_{ts}V_{tb}^{\ast})^{2}x_{V}x_{t}f(x_{t})}\,,

and find that

\Delta M_{{s}}

;
DiLuzio:2019jyq

\displaystyle|g_{23}|/M_{V}\lesssim 0.01\,({\rm TeV})^{-1}\,.

g_{11}\gtrsim 30\,(M_{V}/{\rm TeV})\gg 4\pi
is required by Eqs. (
27
) and (
29
), which implies that the
b\to c\bar{u}q

In this scenario,
|g_{23}|\ll|g_{11}|
should be satisfied.
This simplifies the collider constraints
because the production cross section
is controlled only by
|g_{11}|
,
and the heavy gauge bosons decay into jets with
\mathcal{B}\simeq 1

The constraints are shown in Fig.

C_{2}^{W^{\prime}}/C_{2}^{\rm SM}\gtrsim-0.01

g_{23}=-0.01(M_{V}/{\rm TeV})

V
Discussion

Motivated by a recent improvement of the SM predictions on

\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}\to D^{(\ast)+}K^{-}
and

\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(\ast)+}_{s}\pi^{-}
,
we investigated the size of possible several new physics contributions to these processes.
In spite of severe bounds from the other flavor observables and the LHC searches,
we conclude that a
-10\%
shift in the
b\to c\bar{u}q
amplitude is possible by the left-handed
W^{\prime}
model.
Such a new physics contribution can reduce the tension
in the
b\to c\bar{u}q
processes.

Since
g_{22}=g_{11}
is a necessary condition,
this model also produces new physics contributions to

b\to c\bar{c}s
processes with the same size
Jager:2017gal
;
Jager:2019bgk
.
Although they,
e.g.
,
B^{+}\to J/\psi K^{+}
, have been measured precisely,
the SM predictions suffer from large nonfactorizable corrections

Li:2006vq
;
Liu:2010zh
;
Li:2020app
.
We, therefore, expect that the
b\to c\bar{c}s
processes are less sensitive than
b\to c\bar{u}q
.

It is unclear whether the new physics scalar operator can explain the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly,
but
it is an interesting direction to consider it.
For instance, within a general two Higgs doublet model,
a charged Higgs interaction is
Iguro:2017ysu

\displaystyle{\cal L}=-H^{+}\bar{u}^{i}(V\rho_{d}P_{R}-\rho_{u}^{\dagger}VP_{L})_{ij}d^{j}+{\rm H.c.}\,,

(33)

where
(V\rho_{d})_{23}
is stringently constrained by
\Delta M_{s}
via a heavy neutral Higgs exchange,
while
(\rho_{u}^{\dagger}V)_{23}
is less constrained
by the flavor and collider observables
Iguro:2018qzf
;
Hou:2018zmg
.
Therefore, a potentially large contribution to the
b\to c\bar{u}q
processes would be expected.

Appendix A
Renormalization-group evolution

The LO RG evolution in the effective Lagrangian in Eq. (
5
) is
given as
Chetyrkin:1996vx

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{C}(\mu)}{d\ln\mu}=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\begin{pmatrix}-4&12\\
\frac{8}{3}&0\end{pmatrix}\vec{C}(\mu)\,.

(34)

According to Ref.
Buras:1991jm
,
we obtain an analytic solution of the LO RG evolution as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}^{\rm NP}(m_{W})\\
C_{2}^{\rm NP}(m_{W})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{3}\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}+\frac{2}{3}\eta^{-\frac{4}{7}}&\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}-\eta^{-\frac{4}{7}}\\
\frac{2}{9}\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}-\frac{2}{9}\eta^{-\frac{4}{7}}&\frac{2}{3}\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}+\frac{1}{3}\eta^{-\frac{4}{7}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}^{\rm NP}(\Lambda)\\
C_{2}^{\rm NP}(\Lambda)\end{pmatrix}\,,

(35)

with
\eta=\alpha_{s}(\Lambda)/\alpha_{s}(m_{W})
.
At the weak scale,
the SM contributions enter as
Gorbahn:2004my

\displaystyle C_{1}^{q}(m_{W})=15\frac{\alpha_{s}(m_{W})}{4\pi}+C_{1}^{q,{\rm NP}}(m_{W})\,,\qquad C_{2}^{q}(m_{W})=1+C_{2}^{q,{\rm NP}}(m_{W})\,,

(36)

and their RG evaluation from the weak scale to the hadronic scale is

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}(m_{b})\\
C_{2}(m_{b})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{3}\bar{\eta}^{\frac{6}{23}}+\frac{2}{3}\bar{\eta}^{-\frac{12}{23}}&\bar{\eta}^{\frac{6}{23}}-\bar{\eta}^{-\frac{12}{23}}\\
\frac{2}{9}\bar{\eta}^{\frac{6}{23}}-\frac{2}{9}\bar{\eta}^{-\frac{12}{23}}&\frac{2}{3}\bar{\eta}^{\frac{6}{23}}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{\eta}^{-\frac{12}{23}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}(m_{W})\\
C_{2}(m_{W})\end{pmatrix}\,,

(37)

with
\bar{\eta}=\alpha_{s}(m_{W})/\alpha_{s}(m_{b})
.

Appendix B
Minimal flavor violation

In this section, we give the detailed calculations
for the MFV scenario.
In the MFV hypothesis,
the SU(3
)_{Q_{L}}\times
SU(3
)_{U_{R}}\times
SU(3
)_{D_{R}}
flavor symmetry is introduced and it is broken
only by the Yukawa interactions
DAmbrosio:2002vsn
;
Isidori:2012ts
.
Under this hypothesis, the flavor structure is the same as the SM one:
the flavor-changing neutral currents are automatically suppressed.
For the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly,
we consider the following dimension-six operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left\{\bar{Q}_{L}^{i}\left[\delta_{ij}+a(Y^{u}Y^{u{\dagger}})_{i\neq j}\right]\gamma^{\mu}Q_{L}^{j}\right\}^{2}\,,

(38)

with
Y^{u}=V^{{\dagger}}\textrm{diag}(y_{u},y_{c},y_{t})
, and
a
is a dimensionless coupling.
In the quark mass-diagonal basis (
u_{L}^{\rm diag}=Vu_{L},~d_{L}^{\rm diag}=d_{L}
),
this operator produces

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\simeq\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}(V_{cb}+ay_{t}^{2}V_{ts}^{\ast})V_{uq}^{\ast}\left(\bar{c}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}\right)\left(\bar{q}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}u_{L}\right)\,.

(39)

So, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{2}^{q,{\rm MFV}}(\Lambda)=-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4G_{F}}\left(1+ay_{t}^{2}\frac{V_{ts}^{\ast}}{V_{cb}}\right)\,.

(40)

From the operator in Eq. (
38
),
we also obtain a constraint from
the
B_{s}
-meson mass difference (
\Delta M_{{s}}
)
as (cf., Ref.
Silvestrini:2018dos
),

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda/a\right|\gtrsim 7.9\,{\rm TeV}\,,

(41)

where the LO RG effect is taken into account
Bagger:1997gg
,

\displaystyle C_{LL}(m_{W})=\eta^{\frac{2}{7}}C_{LL}(\Lambda)\,,

(42)

with
\eta=\alpha_{s}(\Lambda)/\alpha_{s}(m_{W})
,
and the latest SM estimation of
\Delta M_{s}

is adopted
Blanke:2018cya
;
DiLuzio:2019jyq
.
We required the new physics contribution to
\Delta M_{s}
does not change the SM prediction at
2\sigma
level.

On the other hand,
from the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly in Eq. (
9
),
we find

\displaystyle\Lambda\sim\sqrt{1-a}\left(0.43{}^{+5}_{-3}\right){\rm TeV}\,.

(43)

Therefore,
we reach a requirement for the anomaly:

\displaystyle\Lambda\lesssim 0.49\,{\rm TeV}\,\textrm{~and~}\,|a|\lesssim 0.06\,.

(44)

However, such a contact interaction can be probed by
a non-resonant dijet angular distribution search in the LHC.
The result is reported by the ATLAS collaboration
at
\sqrt{s}=13\,{\rm TeV}
with
\int dt\mathcal{L}=37
fb
-1

Aaboud:2017yvp
.
We interpret the result in terms of the operator in Eq. (
38
), and obtain a
95\%
CL exclusion limit,

\displaystyle\Lambda<3.7\,{\rm TeV}\,\textrm{~and~}\,4.9\,{\rm TeV}<\Lambda<8.3\,{\rm TeV}\,.

(45)

This bound is clearly incompatible with Eq. (
44
).
From this constraint, we obtain a bound

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}^{\rm MFV}(m_{b})}{C_{2}^{\rm SM}(m_{b})}\gtrsim-0.002\,.

(46)

Therefore, this new physics scenario never explains the
b\to c\bar{u}q
anomaly.

Appendix D
W^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime}
contributions to

The effective Lagrangian for the
b\to s\gamma
process is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{ts}^{\ast}V_{tb}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{6}C_{i}(\mu)\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\mu)+C_{7\gamma}(\mu)Q_{7\gamma}(\mu)+C_{8g}(\mu)Q_{8g}(\mu)\right]\,,

(51)

with

\displaystyle Q_{7\gamma}=\frac{e}{8\pi^{2}}m_{b}\bar{s}\sigma^{\mu\nu}(1+\gamma_{5})bF_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad Q_{8g}=\frac{g_{s}}{8\pi^{2}}m_{b}\bar{s}\sigma^{\mu\nu}T^{a}(1+\gamma_{5})bG^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(52)

and the operators
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
–
\mathcal{Q}_{6}
are defined in Ref.
Buras:1998raa
.

By integrating out the heavy gauge bosons, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{2}(M_{V})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{g_{11}g_{33}}{g_{W}^{2}}\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle C_{3}(M_{V})

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{g_{11}g_{23}}{2g_{W}^{2}V_{ts}^{\ast}}\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\,,

(53)

\displaystyle C_{7}(M_{V})

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{11}g_{33}}{g_{W}^{2}}\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}f_{\gamma}\left(\frac{m_{t}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle C_{8}(M_{V})

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{11}g_{33}}{g_{W}^{2}}\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}f_{g}\left(\frac{m_{t}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)\,,

(54)

and remaining coefficients are set to zero at
\mu=M_{V}
.
To obtain new physics contributions at the hadronic scale, we solved the corresponding RG evolution down to
\mu=m_{b}
numerically:

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{C}(\mu)}{d\ln\mu}=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\left(\hat{\gamma}^{(0){\rm eff}}\right)^{T}\vec{C}(\mu)\,,\qquad\vec{C}=\left(C_{1},\,C_{2},\,\cdots,\,C_{6},\,C_{7}^{\rm eff},\,C_{8}^{\rm eff}\right)\,,

(55)

where the anomalous dimension matrix
\hat{\gamma}^{(0){\rm eff}}
is given in Refs.
Ciuchini:1993fk
;
Buras:1998raa
.
The
C_{7}^{\rm eff},\,C_{8}^{\rm eff}
are the effective Wilson coefficients which are required to cancel a regularization scheme dependence
Buras:1993xp
.
In this model,
C_{7}^{\rm eff}(M_{V})=C_{7}(M_{V})
and
C_{8}^{\rm eff}(M_{V})=C_{8}(M_{V})
.

Using
C_{7}^{\rm eff}(m_{b})
, we obtain a constraint from
b\to s\gamma
data, where we required the new physics contributions are within a
2\sigma
uncertainty range

Misiak:2015xwa
;
Endo:2017ums
.
The bound is sensitive to
g_{23}
which comes from the
Z^{\prime}
contribution to
C_{3}(M_{V})
.
For
g_{23}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{|g_{11}g_{33}|}/M_{V}\lesssim 2.5\,\left({\rm TeV}\right)^{-1}\,.

(56)

This bound is significantly alleviated for
g_{23}/g_{33}>0
region, while it becomes stronger for
g_{23}/g_{33}<0
region.

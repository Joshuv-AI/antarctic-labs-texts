Quantum and Semiquantum Pseudometrics and applications
François Golse, Thierry Paul

Abstract
. We establish a Kantorovich duality for he pseudometric ℰ ℏ \mathcal{E}_{\hbar} introduced in [F. Golse, T. Paul, Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. 223 (2017), 57–94], obtained from the usual Monge-Kantorovich distance dist MK , 2 \operatorname{dist_{MK,2}} between classical densities by quantization of one side of the two densities involved. We show several type of inequalities comparing dist MK , 2 \operatorname{dist_{MK,2}} , ℰ ℏ \mathcal{E}_{\hbar} and M ​ K ℏ MK_{\hbar} , a full quantum analogue of dist MK , 2 \operatorname{dist_{MK,2}} introduced in [F. Golse, C. Mouhot, T. Paul, Commun. Math. Phys. 343 (2016), 165–205], including an up to ℏ \hbar triangle inequality for M ​ K ℏ MK_{\hbar} . Finally, we show that, when nice optimal Kantorovich potentials exist for ℰ ℏ \mathcal{E}_{\hbar} , optimal couplings induce classical/quantum optimal transports and the potentials are linked by a semiquantum Legendre type transform.



1.
Introduction and statement of some main results

The Monge-Kantorovich distance, also called
Wasserstein distance, of exponent two on the phase-space
T^{*}\mathbf{R}^{d}\sim\mathbf{R}^{2d}
is defined, for two probability measures
by

(1)

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\inf_{\pi\in\pi[\mu.\nu]}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}\times\mathbf{R}^{2d}}((q-q^{\prime})^{2}+(q-p^{\prime})^{2})\pi(dqdp,dq^{\prime}dp^{\prime})

where
\pi[\mu,\nu]
is the set of couplings
\pi
of
\mu,\nu
, i.e. the set of probability measures
\pi
on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}\times\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that for all test functions
a,b\in C_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
we have that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}\times\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(a(q,p)\!+\!b(q^{\prime},p^{\prime}))\pi(dqdp,dq^{\prime}dp^{\prime})\!=\!\!\!\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(a(q,p)\mu(dqdp)\!+\!b(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\nu(dq^{\prime}dp^{\prime})).

Among the many properties of
\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}
, let us mention the Kantorovich duality wich stipulates that

(2)

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\\
a(q,p)+b(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})\leq(q-q^{\prime})^{2}+(p-p^{\prime})^{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(a(q,p)\mu(dqdp)+b(q,p)\nu(dqdp),

and the Knott-Smith-Brenier Theorem which says that, under certain conditions on
\mu,\nu
, any coupling
\pi_{op}
satisfying

(3)

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}\times\mathbf{R}^{2d}}((q-q^{\prime})^{2}+(q-p^{\prime})^{2})\pi_{op}(dqdp,dq^{\prime}dp^{\prime})

is supported in the graph of the convex function
\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{2}+p^{2})-a_{op}(q,p)
where
a_{op}
is an optimal function such that
a_{op},b_{op}
provide the max in (
2
) for some
b_{op}
.

Finally,
\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{2}+p^{2})-a_{op}(q,p)
and
\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{2}+p^{2})-b_{op}(q,p)
are proven to be the Legendre transform of each other.

A quantum version of
\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}
was proposed in
[
6
]
following the general rules of quantization consisting in replacing

•

probability measures
\mu.\nu
on phase-space
T^{*}\mathbf{R}^{d}
by quantum states
R,S
, i.e. density operators, i.e. positive trace one operators on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

•

\int_{T^{*}\mathbf{R}^{d}}
by
\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}

•

couplings of
\mu,\nu

by density operators
\Pi
on

L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that, for any bounded operators
A,B
,
\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{(A\otimes I)\Pi)}=\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{AS}
and
\ \operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{(I\otimes B)\Pi)}=\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{BR}
.

•

the cost function
(q-q^{\prime})^{2}+(q-p^{\prime})^{2}
by its Weyl pseudodifferential quantization

C=(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(-i\hbar\nabla_{x}+i\hbar\nabla_{x^{\prime}})^{2}
on

L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

These considerations lead to the definition, for two density operators
R,S
on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2})
,

(4)

MK_{\hbar}(R,S)^{2}=\inf_{\Pi\mbox{ coupling }R\mbox{ and }S}\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{C\Pi}.

The pseudometric
MK_{\hbar}
has been extensively studied in
[
6
]
, with applications to the study of the quantum mean-field limit uniformly in
\hbar

[

(5)

A\otimes I+I\otimes B\leq C\end{subarray}}\operatorname{trace}{(AR+BS)}

and the supremum was proven to be attended for two oparors
\bar{A},\bar{B}
defined respectively on two Gelfand triplest surrounding
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(see
[
2
]
)

Though

is symmetric in its argument, it is not a distance as one can easily show (

) that

. Nevertheless, one of the main result of this article will be to prove the following (approximate) triangle inequality, valid for density operators

(iii)

Actually, (

and\ \int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\Pi(q,p)=R\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},dx)}{((q-x)^{2}+(p+i\hbar\nabla_{x})^{2})\Pi(q,p)}dqdp.

The pseudometric

(8)

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R)^{2}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\ B\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))\\
a(q,p)+B\leq(q-x)^{2}+(p+i\hbar\nabla_{x})^{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(q,p)f(q,p)dqdp+\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}BR,

and then apply this duality to derive inequalities, such as (

.

In the last section of the paper, Section

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R)^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a_{op}(q,p)f(q,p)dqdp+\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}B_{op}R,

then

2.
Preliminaries

We have gathered together in this section some functional analytic remarks used repeatedly in the sequel.

2.1.
Monotone Convergence

We recall the analogue of the Beppo Levi monotone convergence theorem for operators in the form convenient for our purpose.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and
0\leq T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
. For each complete orthonormal system
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{H}
, set

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)=\|T\|_{1}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle e_{j}|T|e_{j}\rangle\in[0,+\infty]\,.

See Theorem 2.14 in
[
13
]
; in particular the expression on the last right hand side of these equalities is independent of the complete orthonormal system
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
. Then

T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})\iff\|T\|_{1}<\infty\,.

Lemma 2.1

(Monotone convergence)
.

Consider a sequence
T_{n}=T_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
such that

0\leq T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq\ldots\leq T_{n}\leq\ldots\,,\qquad\text{ and }\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle<\infty\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}\,.

Then

(a) there exists
T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
T_{n}\to T
weakly as
n\to\infty
, and

(b)
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\in[0,+\infty)
is nondecreasing for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\to\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle=:q(x)\in[0,+\infty)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}

as
n\to\infty
. Hence

\langle x|T_{n}|y\rangle=\langle y|T_{n}|x\rangle\to\tfrac{1}{4}(q(x+y)-q(x-y)+iq(x-iy)-iq(x+iy))=:b(x,y)\in\mathbf{C}

as
n\to+\infty
. By construction,
b
is a nonnegative sesquilinear form on
\mathcal{H}
.

Consider, for each
k\geq 0
,

F_{k}:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\text{ s.t. }\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\leq k\text{ for each }n\geq 1\}\,.

The set
F_{k}
is closed for each
k\geq 0
, being the intersection of the closed sets defined by the inequality
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\leq k
as
n\geq 1
. Since the sequence
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle
is bounded for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\bigcup_{k\geq 0}F_{k}=\mathcal{H}\,.

Applying Baire’s theorem shows that there exists
N\geq 0
such that
\mathring{F}_{N}\not=\varnothing
. In other words, there exists
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

|x-x_{0}|\leq r\implies|\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle|\leq N\text{ for all }n\geq 1\,.

By linearity and positivity of
T_{n}
, this implies

|\langle z|T_{n}|z\rangle|\leq\tfrac{2}{r}(M+N)\|z\|^{2}\text{ for all }n\geq 1\,,\quad\text{ with }M:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x_{0}|T_{n}|x_{0}\rangle\,.

In particular

\sup_{|z|\leq 1}q(z)\leq\tfrac{2}{r}(M+N)\,,\quad\text{ so that }|b(x,y)|\leq\frac{2}{r}(M+N)|\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}

for each
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that

T=T^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad b(x,y)=\langle x|T|y\rangle\,.

This proves (a). Observe that
T\geq T_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
, so that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)\,.

In particular

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})=+\infty\implies\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)=+\infty\,.

Since the sequence
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})
is nondecreasing,

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\to\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

By the noncommutative variant of Fatou’s lemma (Theorem 2.7 (d) in
[
13
]
),

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})<\infty\implies T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})\text{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\,.

Since the opposite inequality is already known to hold, this proves (b).
∎

Here is a convenient variant of this lemma.

Corollary 2.2
.

Consider a sequence
T_{n}=T_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
such that

0\leq T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq\ldots\leq T_{n}\leq\ldots\,,\qquad\text{ and }\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})<\infty\,.

Then there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
such that
T_{n}\to T
weakly as
n\to\infty
, and

T=T^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})\,.

Proof.

Since any
x\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}
can be normalized and completed into a complete orthonormal system of
\mathcal{H}
, one has

\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T|x\rangle\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})<\infty\,.

One concludes by applying Lemma
2.1
(a) and (b).
∎

2.2.
Finite Energy Condition

In the sequel, we shall repeatedly encounter the following typical situation. Let
A=A^{*}\geq 0
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
with domain
\operatorname{Dom}(A)
, and let
E
be its spectral decomposition.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfy
T=T^{*}\geq 0
, and let
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
T
with
Te_{j}=\tau_{j}e_{j}
and
\tau_{j}\in[0,+\infty)
for each
j\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that

(9)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle<\infty\,.

Then

T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}:=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|

satisfies

0\leq T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})

and

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\,.

Proof.

For each Borel
{\omega}\subset\mathbf{R}
and each
x,y\in\mathscr{H}
, one has

|\langle x|E({\omega})|y\rangle|=|\langle E({\omega})x|E({\omega})y\rangle|\leq\|E({\omega})x\|\|E({\omega})y\|=\langle x|E({\omega})|x\rangle^{1/2}\langle y|E({\omega})|y\rangle^{1/2}

since
E({\omega})
is a self-adjoint projection. In particular, for each
{\alpha}>0
, one has

2|\langle x|E({\omega})|y\rangle|\leq{\alpha}\langle x|E({\omega})|x\rangle+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}}\langle y|E({\omega})|y\rangle\,.

Hence

a_{jk}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\in\mathbf{C}

and satisfies

2|a_{jk}|^{2}\leq{\alpha}a_{jj}+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}}a_{kk}

for all
{\alpha}>0
, so that

|a_{jk}|^{2}\leq a_{jj}a_{kk}

for all
j,k\geq 1
.

Since
(\tau_{j}a_{jj})_{j\geq 1}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbf{N}^{*})
by (
9
) and since

\langle e_{j}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{k}\rangle=\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}a_{jk}=\overline{\langle e_{k}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{j}\rangle}\,,

one concludes that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathscr{H})
. Moreover, for each
x\in\mathscr{H}

\displaystyle\langle x|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|x\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\overline{\langle e_{j}|x\rangle}\langle e_{k}|x\rangle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\bigg\langle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle T^{1/2}x|E(d{\lambda})|T^{1/2}x\rangle\geq 0\,,

so that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\geq 0
.

Finally

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 1}\langle e_{l}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{l}\rangle=\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)\rangle e_{l}|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right){\delta}_{lj}{\delta}_{lk}=\sum_{l\geq 1}\tau_{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{l}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle<\infty

so that

\|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\|_{1}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\sum_{l\geq 1}\tau_{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{l}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{l}\rangle<\infty

and in particular
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfy
T=T^{*}\geq 0
and (
9
). Let
\Phi_{n}:\,\mathbf{R}_{+}\to\mathbf{R}_{+}
be a sequence of continuous, bounded and nondecreasing functions such that

0\leq\Phi_{1}(r)\leq\Phi_{2}(r)\leq\ldots\leq\Phi_{n}(r)\to r\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

Set

\Phi_{n}(A):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}({\lambda})E(d{\lambda})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\,.

Then
\Phi_{n}(A)=\Phi_{n}(A)^{*}\geq 0
for each
n\geq 1
and, for each
T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
such that
T=T^{*}\geq 0
, the sequence
T^{1/2}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{1/2}
converges weakly to
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T\Phi_{n}(A))\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})\qquad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

Proof.

Since
E
is a resolution of the identity on
[0,+\infty)
, and since
\Phi_{n}
is continuous, bounded and with values in
[0,+\infty)
, the operators
\Phi_{n}(A)
satisfy

0\leq\Phi_{n}(A)=\Phi_{n}(A)^{*}\leq\left(\sup_{z\geq 0}\Phi_{n}(z)\right)I_{\mathcal{H}}

and

0\leq\Phi_{1}(A)\leq\Phi_{2}(A)\leq\ldots\leq\Phi_{n}(A)\leq\ldots

Set
R_{n}:=T^{1/2}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{1/2}
; by definition
0\leq R_{n}=R_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
and one has

0\leq R_{1}\leq R_{2}\leq\ldots\leq R_{n}\leq\ldots

together with

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(R_{n})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}({\lambda})\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle<\infty

by (
9
). Applying Corollary
2.2
shows that
R_{n}
converges weakly to some
R\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
such that
R=R^{*}\geq 0
. Finally

T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|

so that

\displaystyle\langle x|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}|x\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\bigg\langle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|\sum_{k\geq 1}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\langle e_{k}|x\rangle e_{k}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle T^{1/2}x|E(d{\lambda})|T^{1/2}x\rangle\geq 0\,.

Hence

0\leq T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})

so that

\displaystyle\|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}\|_{1}=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\to 0

as
n\to\infty
by monotone convergence. Hence
R_{n}\to T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
and one has in particular

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T\Phi_{n}(A))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{1/2})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})\,.

∎

2.3.
Energy and Partial Trace

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be two separable Hilbert spaces. Let
A=A^{*}\geq 0
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}_{1}
with domain
\operatorname{Dom}(A)
, and let
E
be its spectral decomposition. Let
S\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
satisfy

S=S^{*}\geq 0
, and let
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a complete orthonormal system of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
of eigenvectors of
S
, with eigenvalues
({\sigma}_{j})_{j\geq 1}
such that
Se_{j}={\sigma}_{j}e_{j}
for each
j\geq 1
. Assume that

\sum_{j\geq 1}{\sigma}_{j}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle<\infty\,.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
satisfy the partial trace condition

\operatorname{trace}(T|\mathcal{H}_{2})=S\,.

Then
T^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
and

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T^{1/2}(A\otimes I)T^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(S^{1/2}AS^{1/2})\,.

Proof.

For all
n\geq 1
, set
A_{n}=\Phi_{n}(A)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
, with

\Phi_{n}(r):=\frac{r}{1+\frac{1}{n}r}\,,\qquad\text{ for all }r\geq 0\,.

By construction, one has

A_{n}=A_{n}^{*}\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{1}\leq A_{2}\leq\ldots\leq A_{n}\leq\ldots

Hence
T^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2})^{*}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
, and

T^{1/2}(A_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}\leq T^{1/2}(A_{2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}\leq\ldots\leq T^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}\leq\ldots

and since

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T(A_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(SA_{n})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(S^{1/2}AS^{1/2})

as
n\to\infty
by the partial trace condition and Corollary
2.4
, we conclude from Corollary
2.2
that

T^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}})T^{1/2})^{*}\geq 0

and that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T^{1/2}(A\otimes I)T^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(S^{1/2}AS^{1/2})\,.

∎

3.
Couplings

Let
\mathfrak{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. An operator
R\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
is a density operator if

R=R^{*}\geq 0\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\operatorname{trace}(R)=1\,.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
the set of density operators on
\mathfrak{H}
, and define

\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H}):=\{R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})\hbox{ s.t. }\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}(|y|^{2}-{\Delta}_{y})R^{1/2})<\infty\}\,.

The set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
the set of Borel probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}(|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})\mu(dxd\xi)<\infty\,.

The set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
is denoted
\mathcal{P}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
We set
\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathcal{P}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and we identify elements of
\mathcal{P}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
with their densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
; a coupling of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
is an element
\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
such that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((A\otimes I+I\otimes B)\mathcal{R})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R_{1}A)+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R_{2}B)\,.

The set of couplings of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}(R_{1},R_{2})
. Obviously the tensor product
R_{1}\otimes R_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(R_{1},R_{2})
, so that
\mathcal{C}(R_{1},R_{2})\not=\varnothing
.

Let
f
be a probability density on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, and let
R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
. A coupling of
f
and
R
is an ultraweakly measurable operator-valued function
(x,\xi)\mapsto Q(x,\xi)
defined a.e. on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
with
values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
such that

\displaystyle Q(x,\xi)=Q(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}Q(x,\xi)dxd\xi=R

\displaystyle\hbox{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi))=f(x,\xi)\hbox{Ê for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}

\displaystyle.

The set of couplings of
f
and
R
will also be denoted by
\mathcal{C}(f,R)
. Since the map
(x,\xi)\mapsto f(x,\xi)R
(henceforth denoted
f\otimes R
) obviously belongs to
\mathcal{C}(f,R)
, one has
\mathcal{C}(f,R)\not=\varnothing
.

In general, one does not know much about the general structure of couplings between two density operators. However, the case where one of the density operators is a rank
1
projection is particularly simple.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
P=P^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
be a rank
1
projection. Then

(i) for each probability density
f
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, one has
\mathcal{C}(f,P)=\{f\otimes P\}
;

(ii) for each
R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has
\mathcal{C}(P,R)=\{P\otimes R\}
and
\mathcal{C}(R,P)=\{R\otimes P\}
.

This is in complete analogy with the following elementary observation: if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
y_{0}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
, the only coupling of
\mu
and
{\delta}_{y_{0}}
is
\mu\otimes{\delta}_{y_{0}}
. In other words, self-adjoint rank-
1
projections
are the quantum analogue of points in this picture.

Proof.

Let
Q\in\mathcal{C}(f,P)
; one has

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((I-P)Q(x,\xi)(I-P))dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}\left((I-P)\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}Q(x,\xi)dxd\xi(I-P\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((I-P)P(I-P))=0

\displaystyle.

Since
(I-P)Q(x,\xi)(I-P)\geq 0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, this implies that

(I-P)Q(x,\xi)(I-P)=0\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

Since
Q(x,\xi)=Q(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we deduce from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that, for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{H}

\displaystyle|\langle P\phi|Q(x,\xi)|(I-P)\psi\rangle|^{2}=|\langle(I-P)\psi|Q(x,\xi)|P\phi\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle P\phi|Q(x,\xi)|P\phi\rangle^{1/2}\langle(I-P)\psi|Q(x,\xi)|(I-P)\psi\rangle^{1/2}=0

\displaystyle.

Hence
(I-P)Q(x,\xi)P=PQ(x,\xi)(I-P)=0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, so that

Q(x,\xi)=PQ(x,\xi)P\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

Writing
P
as
P=|u\rangle\langle u|
where
u\in\mathfrak{H}
is a unit vector, we conclude that

Q(x,\xi)=\langle u|Q(x,\xi)|u\rangle P\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

Finally

\operatorname{trace}(Q(x,\xi))=f(x,\xi)=\langle u|Q(x,\xi)|u\rangle\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,.

This concludes the proof of (i).

As for (ii), let
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{C}(R,P)
. Then

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((I\otimes(I-P))\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes(I-P)))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(I-P)P(I-P))=0\,.

Hence

(I\otimes(I-P))\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes(I-P)=0\,.

Since
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{*}\geq 0
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that, for all
\phi,\phi^{\prime},\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}

\displaystyle|\langle\phi\otimes\psi|(I\otimes P)\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes(I-P))|\phi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}\rangle|=|\langle\phi^{\prime}\otimes\psi|^{\prime}(I\otimes(I-P))\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes P)|\phi\otimes\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\langle\phi\otimes\psi|(I\otimes P)\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes P)|\phi\otimes\psi\rangle^{1/2}\langle\phi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}|(I\otimes(I-P))\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes(I-P))|\phi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}\rangle^{1/2}

so that

(I\otimes P)\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes(I-P))=(I\otimes(I-P))\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes P)=0\,.

Hence

\mathcal{Q}=(I\otimes P)\mathcal{Q}(I\otimes P)\,.

Writing
P=|u\rangle\langle u|
with
u\in\mathfrak{H}
and
|u|=1
as above, we conclude that

\langle\phi\otimes\psi|\mathcal{Q}|\phi^{\prime}\otimes\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\phi\otimes u|\mathcal{Q}|\phi^{\prime}\otimes u\rangle\langle u|\psi\rangle\langle u|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\,.

This shows that
\mathcal{Q}=L\otimes|u\rangle\langle u|=L\otimes P
, where
L=L^{*}
is the element of
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
such that

\langle\phi|L|\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\phi\otimes u|\mathcal{Q}|\phi^{\prime}\otimes u\rangle

for each
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}
. (Observe indeed that
(\phi,\phi^{\prime})\mapsto\langle\phi\otimes u|\mathcal{Q}|\phi^{\prime}\otimes u\rangle
is a continuous, symmetric bilinear functional on
\mathfrak{H}
, and is therefore represented by a unique self-adjoint element of
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
.)
We conclude by observing that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((A\otimes I)\mathcal{Q})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(AR)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(LR)

for each finite rank operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
, and this implies that
\mathcal{Q}=R\otimes P
.

The case of
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(P,R)
is handled similarly.
∎

Next we explain how to “disintegrate” a coupling with respect to one of its marginals when this marginal is a probability density.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, let
R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
and let
Q\in\mathcal{C}(f,R)
. There exists a
{\sigma}(\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}))
weakly measurable function
(x,\xi)\mapsto Q_{f}(x,\xi)
defined a.e. on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
with values in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})

such that

Q_{f}(x,\xi)=Q^{*}_{f}(x,\xi)\geq 0\,,\quad\operatorname{trace}(Q_{f}(x,\xi))=1\,,\quad\hbox{ and }Q(x,\xi)=f(x,\xi)Q_{f}(x,\xi)

for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1}
be a Borel measurable function defined on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
and such that
f(x,\xi)=f_{1}(x,\xi)
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be the Borel measurable set defined as follows:

\mathcal{N}:=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\hbox{ s.t. }f(x,\xi)=0\}
, and let
u\in\mathfrak{H}
satisfy
|u|=1
.
Consider the function

(x,\xi)\mapsto Q_{f}(x,\xi):=\frac{Q(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}(x,\xi)|u\rangle\langle u|}{f_{1}(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}(x,\xi)}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})

defined a.e. on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The function
f_{1}+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}>0
is Borel measurable on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
while
(x,\xi)\mapsto\langle\phi|Q(x,\xi)|\psi\rangle
is measurable and defined a.e. on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
for each
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{H}
.
Set
\mathcal{A}:\,\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\times(0,+\infty)\ni(T,{\lambda})\mapsto{\lambda}^{-1}T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
; since
\mathcal{A}
is continuous, the function
Q_{f}:=\mathcal{A}(Q+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes|u\rangle\langle u|,f_{1}+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}})
is weakly measurable on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Since
f_{1}+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}>0
,
and since
Q(x,\xi)=Q^{*}(x,\xi)\geq 0
, one has
(Q(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes|u\rangle\langle u|)^{*}=Q(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes|u\rangle\langle u|\geq 0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
. On the other hand, for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, one has

\operatorname{trace}(Q(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes|u\rangle\langle u|)=f(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}(x,\xi)
, so that
\operatorname{trace}(Q_{f}(x,\xi))=1
. Finally

f(x,\xi)Q_{f}(x,\xi)=\frac{f(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)}{f_{1}(x,\xi)+{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}(x,\xi)}=Q(x,\xi)\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\,,

since
f=f_{1}
a.e. on
\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
and
{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{N}}(x,\xi)=0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that
f(x,\xi)>0
. Since
Q_{f}
satisfies
\operatorname{trace}(Q_{f}(x,\xi))=1
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
and is weakly measurable on

\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, it is
{\sigma}(\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}),\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}))
weakly measurable.
∎

4.
Triangle Inequalities

The following “pseudo metrics” have been defined in
[
6
]
and in
[
7
]
respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

For all
R,S\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and all
f\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, we set

MK_{\hbar}(R,S):=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(A^{1/2}CA^{1/2})^{1/2}

where

C:=C(x,y,{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{y})=|x-y|^{2}+|{\hbar}D_{x}-{\hbar}D_{y}|^{2}\,.

Similarly, we set

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R):=\inf_{a\in\mathcal{C}(f,R)}\left(\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(a(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)a(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi\right)^{1/2}

where

c(x,\xi):=c(x,\xi,y,{\hbar}D_{y})=|x-y|^{2}+|\xi-{\hbar}D_{y}|^{2}\,.

The above “pseudometrics” satisfy the following inequalities.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and let
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
. The following inequalities hold true:

(a)
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{1})\leq\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,R_{1})
;

(b)
MK_{\hbar}(R_{1},R_{3})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{1})+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{3})
;

(c) if
\text{rank}(R_{2})=1
, then

MK_{\hbar}(R_{1},R_{3})\leq MK_{\hbar}(R_{1},R_{2})+MK_{\hbar}(R_{2},R_{3})\,,

(d) if
\text{rank}(R_{2})=1
, then

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{2})+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,R_{2})\,,

(e) if
\text{rank}(R_{2})=1
, then

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{3})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{2})+MK_{\hbar}(R_{2},R_{3})\,.

The proofs of all these triangle inequalities make use of some inequalities between the (classical and/or quantum) transportation cost operators. We begin with an elementary, but useful lemma, which can be viewed as the Peter-Paul
inequality for operators.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T,S
be unbounded self-adjoint operators on
\mathfrak{H}=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
, with domains
\operatorname{Dom}(T)
and
\operatorname{Dom}(S)
respectively such that
\operatorname{Dom}(T)\cap\operatorname{Dom}(S)
is dense in
\mathfrak{H}
. Then, for all
{\alpha}>0
, one has

\langle v|TS+ST|v\rangle\leq{\alpha}\langle v|T^{2}|v\rangle+\frac{1}{{\alpha}}\langle v|S^{2}|v\rangle\,,\qquad\text{ for all }v\in\operatorname{Dom}(T)\cap\operatorname{Dom}(S)\,.

Proof.

Indeed, for each
{\alpha}>0
and each
v\in\operatorname{Dom}(T)\cap\operatorname{Dom}(S)
, one has

\displaystyle{\alpha}\langle v|T^{2}|v\rangle+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}}\langle v|S^{2}|v\rangle-\langle v|TS+ST|v\rangle

\displaystyle=|\sqrt{{\alpha}}Tv|^{2}+|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\alpha}}}Sv|^{2}-\langle\sqrt{{\alpha}}Tv|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\alpha}}}Sv\rangle-\langle\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\alpha}}}Sv|\sqrt{{\alpha}}Tv\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\sqrt{{\alpha}}Tv-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\alpha}}}Sv\right|^{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For each
x,\xi,y,\eta,z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
and each
{\alpha}>0
, one has

\displaystyle c(x,\xi;z,{\hbar}D_{z})\leq(1+{\alpha})(|x-y|^{2}+|\xi-\eta|^{2})+(1+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}})c(y,\eta;z,{\hbar}D_{z})\,,

\displaystyle C(x,z,{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{z})\leq(1+{\alpha})c(y,\eta;x,{\hbar}D_{x})+(1+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}})c(y,\eta;z,{\hbar}D_{z})\,,

\displaystyle C(x,z,{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{z})\leq(1+{\alpha})C(x,y;{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{y})+(1+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}})C(y,z;{\hbar}D_{y},{\hbar}D_{z})\,,

\displaystyle|x-z|^{2}+|\xi-\zeta|^{2}\leq(1+{\alpha})c(x,\xi;y,{\hbar}D_{y})+(1+{\alpha})c(z,\zeta;y,{\hbar}D_{y})\,,

\displaystyle c(x,\xi;z,{\hbar}D_{z})\leq(1+{\alpha})c(x,\xi;y,{\hbar}D_{y})+(1+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}})C(y,z;{\hbar}D_{y},{\hbar}D_{z})\,.

All these inequalities are of the form
A\leq B
where
A
and
B
are unbounded self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
for some
n\geq 1
, with

\mathcal{W}:=\{\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\text{ s.t. }|x|\psi\in\mathcal{H}\}\subset\operatorname{Dom}_{f}(A)\cap\operatorname{Dom}_{f}(B)\,,

denoting by
\operatorname{Dom}_{f}(A)
(resp.
\operatorname{Dom}_{f}(B)
) the form-domain of
A
(resp. of
B
) — see §VIII.6 in
[
9
]
on pp. 276–277. The inequality
A\leq B
means that the bilinear form associated to
B-A
is nonnegative, i.e. that

\langle w|A|w\rangle\leq\langle w|B|w\rangle\,,\qquad\text{ for all }w\in\mathcal{W}\,.

Proof.

All these inequalities are proved in the same way. Let us prove for instance the third inequality:

\displaystyle C(x,z,{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{z})=

\displaystyle|x-y+y-z|^{2}+|{\hbar}D_{x}-{\hbar}D_{y}+{\hbar}D_{y}-{\hbar}D_{z}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(x,y;{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{y})+C(y,z;{\hbar}D_{y},{\hbar}D_{z})

\displaystyle+2(x-y)\cdot(y-z)+2({\hbar}D_{x}-{\hbar}D_{y})\cdot({\hbar}D_{y}-{\hbar}D_{z})\,.

Observe indeed that the multiplication operators by
(x-y)
and by
(y-z)
commute; likewise
({\hbar}D_{x}-{\hbar}D_{y})
and
({\hbar}D_{y}-{\hbar}D_{z})
commute. By Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle 2(x-y)\cdot(y-z)+2({\hbar}D_{x}-{\hbar}D_{y})\cdot({\hbar}D_{y}-{\hbar}D_{z})

\displaystyle\leq{\alpha}C(x,y;{\hbar}D_{x},{\hbar}D_{y})+\frac{1}{{\alpha}}C(y,z;{\hbar}D_{y},{\hbar}D_{z})

\displaystyle,

which concludes the proof of the third inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
(a).

By Theorem 2.12 in chapter 2 of
[
14
]
, there exists an optimal coupling for
W_{2}(f,g)
, of the form
f(x,\xi){\delta}_{{\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi)}(dyd\eta)

\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}

In particular

(10)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{1})^{2}\leq\int\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q_{g}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi))^{1/2}c_{\hbar}(x,\xi)Q_{g}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi))^{1/2})f(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,.

By the first inequality in Lemma
4.4
, one has

c_{\hbar}(x,\xi;z,{\hbar}D_{z})\leq(1+{\alpha})|(x,\xi)-{\nabla}\phi(x,\xi)|^{2}+(1+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}})c_{\hbar}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi);z,{\hbar}D_{z})

for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
and all
{\alpha}>0
. Since
g\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{ac}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
R_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and
Q\in\mathcal{C}(g,R_{1})
, then

\displaystyle\int\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(y,\eta)^{1/2}c_{\hbar}(y,\eta)Q(y,\eta)^{1/2})dyd\eta

\displaystyle=\int\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q_{g}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi))^{1/2}c_{\hbar}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi))Q_{g}({\nabla}\Phi(x,\xi))^{1/2})f(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle<\infty\,.

For each

can indeed be thought of as a quantum deformation of the quadratic Monge-Kantorovich-Wasserstein

Theorem 5.1

f,g\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Assume that

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{\hbar})\to 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,S_{\hbar})\to 0

as
{\hbar}\to 0
. Then

\lim_{{\hbar}\to 0}MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})=\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)\,.

This statement is to be compared with the lower bound

MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})^{2}\geq\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar}),\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S_{\hbar}))^{2}-2d{\hbar}\,,

which is Theorem 2.3 (2) in
[
6
]
, and with the upper bound obtained in the special case of Töplitz operators

MK_{\hbar}(\operatorname{OP}^{T}_{\hbar}((2\pi{\hbar})^{d}\mu),\operatorname{OP}^{T}_{\hbar}((2\pi{\hbar})^{d}\nu))^{2}\leq\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}+2d{\hbar}\,,

stated as Theorem 2.3 (1) in
[
6
]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
(a)-(b),

\displaystyle MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{\hbar})+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S_{\hbar})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{\hbar})+\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,S_{\hbar})\,.

Hence

\varlimsup_{{\hbar}\to 0^{+}}MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})\leq\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)\,.

By Theorem 2.4 (2) in
[
7
]

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,\widetilde{W}(R_{\hbar}))^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{\hbar})^{2}+d{\hbar}

(notice the slight change of normalization in the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}
between
[
7
]
and the present paper), so that our assumption implies that

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,\widetilde{W}(R_{\hbar}))\to 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(g,\widetilde{W}(S_{\hbar}))\to 0

as
{\hbar}\to 0
. From the inequality

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar}),\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S_{\hbar}))^{2}\leq MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})^{2}+2d{\hbar}\,,

(Theorem 2.3 (2) in
[
6
]
), we deduce that

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)\leq\varliminf_{{\hbar}\to 0}MK_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar},S_{\hbar})\,.

Notice that this last lower bound is a variant of the last inequality in Theorem 2.3 of
[
6
]
, except that in the present case the assumption on
R_{\hbar}
and
S_{\hbar}
is slightly different (in other words, we have assumed that

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,R_{\hbar})\to 0
instead of assuming that
\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(R_{\hbar})\to f
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d})
.)
∎

6.
Kantorovich duality for
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and let
p\equiv p(x,\xi)
be a probability density on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})p(x,\xi)dxd\xi<+\infty\,.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(p,S)^{2}=

\displaystyle\min_{Q\in\mathcal{C}(p,S)}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\,B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\atop a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\leq c(x,\xi)}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\right)\,.

Notice that the duality theorem implies in particular the existence of at least one optimal coupling
Q\in\mathcal{C}(p,S)
.

Proof.

The proof is split in several steps.

Step 1: the functions
f
and
g
.
Consider the Banach space
E:=C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}))
, with

\|T\|_{E}:=\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|T(x,\xi)\|\,,

and set

f(T):=\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&0\quad&&\text{ if }T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\geq-c(x,\xi)\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\,,\\
&+\infty&&\text{ otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

while

g(T):=\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}ap(x,\xi)dxd\xi\!+\!\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\quad&&\text{ if }T(x,\xi)\!=\!T(x,\xi)^{*}\!=\!a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}\!+\!B\\
&&&\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\,,\\
&+\infty&&\text{ otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

The constraint
T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\geq-c(x,\xi)
means that, for each
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, one has

\langle\phi(x,\xi)|T(x,\xi)+c(x,\xi)|\phi(x,\xi)\rangle\geq 0

for all
\phi\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(c(x,\xi))
. On the other hand, the nullspace of the linear map

C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\ni(a,B)\mapsto{\Gamma}(a,B)\equiv a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\in E

is

\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{L})=\{(t,-tI_{\mathfrak{H}})\,,\quad t\in\mathbf{R}\}\,.

Since

g((a\!+\!t)I_{\mathfrak{H}}\!+\!(B\!-\!tI_{\mathfrak{H}}))\!=\!g(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}\!+\!B)\!+\!t\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\!-\!t\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S)\!=\!g(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}\!+\!B)\,,

the prescription above defines
g
on
\operatorname{Ran}({\Gamma})\simeq(C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}))/\operatorname{Ker}({\Gamma})
. Observe that

\displaystyle g((aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B)^{*})=g(\bar{a}I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B^{*})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\overline{a(x,\xi)}p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B^{*}S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi}+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((SB)^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi}+\overline{\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(SB)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{g(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B)}\,,

so that
(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B)^{*}=aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\implies g(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B)\in\mathbf{R}
. Thus the definition above implies that
g
takes its values in
(-\infty,+\infty]
.

The functions
f
and
g
are convex. Indeed,
g
is the extension by
+\infty
of a
\mathbf{R}
-linear functional defined on the set of self-adjoint elements of
\operatorname{Ran}({\Gamma})
, which is a linear subspace of
E
. As for
f
, it is the indicator function
(in the sense of the definition in §4 of
[
11
]
on p. 28) of the convex set

\{T\in E\text{ s.t. }T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\geq-c(x,\xi)\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\}

and is therefore convex. Besides
f(0)=g(0)=0
, and
f
is continuous at
0
. Indeed, by the Heisenberg inequality

c(x,\xi)\geq d{\hbar}I_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\,,

so that, for each
T\in E

\displaystyle T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\text{ and }\|T(x,\xi)\|<\tfrac{1}{2}d{\hbar}\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}

\displaystyle\implies T(x,\xi)\geq-c(x,\xi)\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\implies f(T)=0

\displaystyle.

In particular
f
is continuous at
0
.

Step 2: applying convex duality.
By the Fenchel-Rockafellar convex duality theorem (Theorem 1.12 in
[
1
]
)

\inf_{T\in E}(f(T)+g(T))=\max_{{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}}(-f^{*}(-{\Lambda})-g^{*}({\Lambda}))\,.

Let us compute the Legendre duals
f^{*}
and
g^{*}
.

First

f^{*}(-{\Lambda})=\sup_{T\in E}(\langle-{\Lambda},T\rangle-f(T))=\sup_{T\in E\atop T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\geq-c(x,\xi)}\langle-{\Lambda},T\rangle\,.

If
{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}
is not a nonnegative linear functional, there exists
T_{0}\in E
such that
T_{0}(x,\xi)=T_{0}(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0
such that
\langle{\Lambda},T_{0}\rangle=-{\alpha}<0
. Since

nT_{0}(x,\xi)=nT_{0}(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0\geq-d{\hbar}I_{\mathfrak{H}}\geq-c(x,\xi)\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}

one has

f^{*}(-{\Lambda})\geq\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle-{\Lambda},nT_{0}\rangle=\sup_{n\geq 1}(n{\alpha})=+\infty\,.

For
{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}
such that
{\Lambda}\geq 0
, we define

\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle:=\sup_{T\in E\atop T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\leq c(x,\xi)}\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle\in[0,+\infty]\,.

(Observe indeed that
T=0
satisfies the constraints since
c(x,\xi)=c(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0
for each
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
.) With this definition, one has clearly

f^{*}(-{\Lambda}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle\quad&&\text{ if }{\Lambda}\geq 0\,,\\
&0\quad&&\text{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Next

\displaystyle g^{*}({\Lambda})=

\displaystyle\sup_{T\in E}(\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-g(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{T\in E\atop T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}=a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B}\left(\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\right)\,.

If there exists
a\equiv a(x,\xi)\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},\mathbf{R})
and
B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
such that either

\langle{\Lambda},aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\rangle>\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)

or

\langle{\Lambda},aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\rangle<\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\,,

one has either

g({\Lambda})\geq\sup_{n\geq 1}\left(\langle{\Lambda},n(aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B)\rangle-n\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi-n\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\right)=+\infty\,,

or

g({\Lambda})\geq\sup_{n\geq 1}\left(\langle{\Lambda},n(-aI_{\mathfrak{H}}-B)\rangle+n\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+n\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\right)=+\infty\,.

Hence

g^{*}({\Lambda}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&0\quad&&\text{ if }\langle{\Lambda},aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)\\
&&&\text{ for each }a\equiv a(x,\xi)\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},\mathbf{R})\text{ and }B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\,,\\
&+\infty\quad&&\text{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Notice that the prescription

\langle{\Lambda},aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BS)

defines a unique linear functional on the set of
T\in\operatorname{Ran}{\Gamma}
such that
T(x,\xi)^{*}=T(x,\xi)
for each
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
by the same argument as in Step 1.

Therefore, the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality theorem in this case results in the equality

\displaystyle\inf_{T\in E}(f(T)+g(T))=\inf_{a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},\mathbf{R}),\,B=B^{*}\atop a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\geq-c(x,\xi)}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(SB)\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}}(f^{*}(-{\Lambda})+g^{*}({\Lambda}))=\max_{0\leq{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime},\,\,\,\langle{\Lambda},aI\mathfrak{H}+B\rangle\atop=\int a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}(SB)}-\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle

or, equivalently

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},\mathbf{R}),\,B=B^{*}\atop a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\leq c(x,\xi)}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(SB)\right)

\displaystyle=\min_{0\leq{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime},\,\,\,\langle{\Lambda},aI_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\rangle\atop=\int a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}(SB)}\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle

\displaystyle.

Step 3: representing the optimal
{\Lambda}
.
Define a linear map
F_{\Lambda}:\,C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\to\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
by the formula

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(KF_{\Lambda}(a))={\Lambda}(aK)\,,\qquad\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})\,.

Indeed, since
K\mapsto{\Lambda}(aK)
is a linear functional on
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})
which is continuous for the norm topology, and since
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
, this linear functional is represented by a trace-class operator
F_{\Lambda}(a)
. Since
{\Lambda}

is linear, the map
F_{\Lambda}
is linear.

Since
{\Lambda}\geq 0
, one has
F_{\Lambda}(a)=F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*}\geq 0
for each
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that
a(x,\xi)\geq 0
for each
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
. Indeed, for
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{R})
, set

T_{1}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(F_{\Lambda}(a)+F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*})\,,\qquad T_{2}:=-\tfrac{1}{2}i(F_{\Lambda}(a)-F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*})\,.

Then, for each
K=K^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has

{\Lambda}(aK)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(T_{1}K)+i\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(T_{2}K)

with

\overline{Tr_{\mathfrak{H}}(T_{j}K)}=Tr_{\mathfrak{H}}((T_{j}K)^{*})=Tr_{\mathfrak{H}}(K^{*}T_{j}^{*})=Tr_{\mathfrak{H}}(KT_{j})=Tr_{\mathfrak{H}}(T_{j}K)

for
j=1,2
. Since
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{R})
and
K=K^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has

-\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|I_{\mathfrak{H}}\leq aK\leq\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|I_{\mathfrak{H}}

so that

-\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|\leq{\Lambda}(aK)\leq\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|\quad\text{ since }{\Lambda}(I_{\mathfrak{H}})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}p(x,\xi)dxd\xi=1\,.

In particular,
{\Lambda}(aK)\in\mathbf{R}
, so that
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(T_{2}K)=0
for each
K=K^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})
. Since
T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
, specializing this identity to the case where
K
is the orthogonal projection on any eigenvector of
T_{2}

shows that
T_{2}=0
. Thus

a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{R})\implies F_{\Lambda}(a)=F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*}\,.

Moreover

a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{R})\text{ and }a\geq 0\implies\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)K)\geq 0\text{ for each }K=K^{*}\geq 0\text{ in }\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})

and specializing this last inequality to the case where
K
is the orthogonal projection on any eigenvector of
F_{\Lambda}(a)=F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
shows that all the eigenvalues of
F_{\Lambda}(a)
are nonnegative, so that
F_{\Lambda}(a)\geq 0
.

Next we deduce from the defining identity for
F_{\Lambda}
, i.e.

{\Lambda}(aK)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)K)\text{ for each }a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{C})\text{ and }K\in\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})

that

\|F_{\Lambda}(a)\|_{1}\leq\|{\Lambda}\|\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}\,.

Next we specialize this defining identity to the case where
a\geq 0
on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
while
K=\Pi_{n}
is the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, with
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
a complete orthonormal system in
\mathfrak{H}
.
One has

{\Lambda}(a\Pi_{n})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)\Pi_{n})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a))=\|F_{\Lambda}(a)\|_{1}\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty

while

a(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})\geq 0\text{ so that }{\Lambda}(a\Pi_{n})\leq{\Lambda}(aI_{\mathfrak{H}})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi

so that

a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\text{ and }a\geq 0\implies\|F_{\Lambda}(a)\|_{1}\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,.

More generally, for each
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathbf{R})
, one has
-|a|\leq a\leq|a|
so that

|\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)|=|{\Lambda}(a|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)|\leq{\Lambda}(|a||e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)

for each
j\geq 1
, where
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,)
is a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
F_{\Lambda}(a)=F_{\Lambda}(a)^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
. Hence

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)|\leq{\Lambda}\left(|a|\sum_{j=1}^{n}|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|\right)\leq{\Lambda}(|a|I_{\mathfrak{H}})\,,

and since

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)|\to\|F_{\Lambda}(a)\|_{1}\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty

we conclude that

\|F_{\Lambda}(a)\|_{1}\leq{\Lambda}(|a|I_{\mathfrak{H}})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}|a(x,\xi)|p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,.

Since
C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
is dense in
L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},pdxd\xi)
, this inequality, applied to the real and the imaginary part of
a
, shows that
F_{\Lambda}
is a continuous linear operator from
L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}
to
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})

is separable and is the dual of the Banach space
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})
(the norm closure in
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
of the set of finite rank operators), we conclude from the Dunford-Pettis theorem (Theorem 1 in §3 of chapter III in
[
5
]
) that

\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
has the Radon-Nikodym property. By Theorem 5 in §1 of chapter III in
[
5
]
, the operator
F_{\Lambda}
is Riesz-representable: in other words, there exists
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},pdxd\xi;\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}))
such that

F_{\Lambda}(a)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,,\quad\text{ for all }a\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},pdxd\xi)\,.

Step 4: defining the optimal coupling.
We have seen that

a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\text{ and }a\geq 0\implies F_{\Lambda}(a)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\geq 0\,.

This implies that
q(x,\xi)=q(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
.

Next, one has

{\Lambda}(K)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(1)K)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(KS)\,,\qquad K\in\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})\,,

so that

F_{\Lambda}(1)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi=S\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})=\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})^{\prime}\,.

On the other hand, for each
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
such that
a\geq 0
, one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}\left(P_{n}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(F_{\Lambda}(a)P_{n})={\Lambda}(aP_{n})

\displaystyle\leq{\Lambda}(aI_{\mathfrak{H}})=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)d(x,\xi)dxd\xi

where
P_{n}
is the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, with
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
being a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})\,.

Letting
n\to\infty
, one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}\left(P_{n}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right)

\displaystyle,

so that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(q(x,\xi))p(x,\xi)dxd\xi\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)d(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,.

Since this holds for each
a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that
a\geq 0
, we conclude that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(q(x,\xi))\leq 1\quad\text{ for $p(x,\xi)dxd\xi$--a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\,.

Moreover

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(1-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(q(x,\xi)))p(x,\xi)dxd\xi=1-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S)=0

so that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(q(x,\xi))=1\quad\text{ for $p(x,\xi)dxd\xi$--a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\,.

In other words, we have proved that
(x,\xi)\mapsto Q(x,\xi)=p(x,\xi)q(x,\xi)
defines an element of
\mathcal{C}(p,S)
.

Step 5: extending the representation formula for
{\Lambda}
.
For each
B\in E
, we define

\langle L,B\rangle:=\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi))dxd\xi\,.

Let us prove that

B\in E\text{ and }B(x,\xi)=B(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\implies\langle L,B\rangle\geq 0\,.

Pick
{\epsilon}>0
, and let
Q_{\epsilon}
be a simple
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
-valued function on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|Q(x,\xi)-Q_{\epsilon}(x,\xi)\|_{1}dxd\xi<{\epsilon}\,.

Write

Q_{\epsilon}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bf 1}_{{\Omega}_{j}}(x,\xi)Q_{j}\,,\quad 0\leq Q_{j}=Q_{j}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})\text{ for each }j=0,\ldots,N\,,

where
{\Omega}_{j}
are bounded, pairwise disjoint measurable sets in
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
. For each
j=1,\ldots,N
, let
(e_{j,1},e_{j,2},\ldots)
designate a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
Q_{j}
, and let
P_{j,n}

be the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{span}\{e_{j,1},\ldots,e_{j,n}\}
. Define

\Pi_{n}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bf 1}_{{\Omega}_{j}}(x,\xi)P_{j,n}\,.

One easily checks that
\Pi_{n}(x,\xi)=\Pi_{n}(x,\xi)^{*}=\Pi_{n}(x,\xi)^{2}
for each
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
. Then, for each
B\in E
such that
B(x,\xi)=B(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0
for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, one has

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\langle{\Lambda},(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})B(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})\rangle=\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\langle{\Lambda},\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})Q)(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})Q_{\epsilon})(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})(Q_{\epsilon}-Q))(x,\xi)dxd\xi\,.

By construction, keeping
{\epsilon}>0
fixed, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})Q_{\epsilon})(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B\Pi_{n}Q_{\epsilon}\Pi_{n})(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle\to\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BQ_{\epsilon})(x,\xi)dxd\xi

as
n\to\infty
, so that

\displaystyle 0\leq\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\langle{\Lambda},(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})B(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})\rangle=\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BQ)(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle+\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})(Q_{\epsilon}-Q))(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B(Q-Q_{\epsilon}))(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})(Q_{\epsilon}-Q))(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}|\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})(Q_{\epsilon}-Q))(x,\xi)|dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|(\Pi_{n}B+B\Pi_{n}-\Pi_{n}B\Pi_{n})(x,\xi)\|\|(Q_{\epsilon}-Q)(x,\xi)\|_{1}dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq 3\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|B(x,\xi)\|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|(Q_{\epsilon}-Q)(x,\xi)\|_{1}dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq 3{\epsilon}\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|B(x,\xi)\|

while, by the same token,

\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B(Q-Q_{\epsilon}))(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right|\leq{\epsilon}\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|B(x,\xi)\|\,.

Finally

\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(BQ)(x,\xi)dxd\xi\geq-4{\epsilon}\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\|B(x,\xi)\|

and since this holds for each
{\epsilon}>0
, we conclude that

B\in E\text{ and }B(x,\xi)=B(x,\xi)^{*}\geq 0\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}\implies\langle L,B\rangle\geq 0\,.

By a classical argument, this implies that
\|L\|=\langle L,I_{\mathfrak{H}}\rangle
.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\langle L,I_{\mathfrak{H}}\rangle=\langle L,I_{\mathfrak{H}}\rangle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(q(x,\xi))p(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S)-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}p(x,\xi)dxd\xi=0

so that
L=0
. In other words, the representation formula

\langle{\Lambda},B\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(B(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi))dxd\xi

holds for each
B\in E
, and not only for
B\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H}))
.

Step 6: computing
\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle
.
As explained in Step 2

\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle=\sup_{T\in E\atop T(x,\xi)=T(x,\xi)^{*}\leq c(x,\xi)}\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle\,.

For each
n\geq 1
, set

c_{n}(x,\xi):=(I_{\mathfrak{H}}+\tfrac{1}{n}c(x,\xi))^{-1}c(x,\xi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\,,

so that

0\leq c_{1}(x,\xi)=c_{1}(x,\xi)^{*}\leq\ldots\leq c_{n}(x,\xi)=c_{n}(x,\xi)^{*}\leq\ldots\leq c(x,\xi)=c(x,\xi)^{*}\,.

Thus, by definition

\langle{\Lambda},c_{n}\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)c_{n}(x,\xi))dxd\xi\leq\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle

for each
n\geq 1
, so that, by Corollary
2.4

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)c_{n}(x,\xi))dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle

\displaystyle.

On the other hand, let
(e_{1}(x,\xi),e_{2}(x,\xi),\ldots,)
designate a complete orthonormal system in
\mathfrak{H}
of eigenfunctions of
c(x,\xi)
, with
c(x,\xi)e_{j}(x,\xi)={\lambda}_{j}e_{j}(x,\xi)
for
j\geq 1
. Since
c(x,\xi)
is a phase space translate of
the harmonic oscillator
H:=\tfrac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}-{\hbar}^{2}{\Delta}_{x})
, the eigenvalues
{\lambda}_{j}
are independent of
(x,\xi)
. Set

t_{kl}(x,\xi):=\langle e_{k}(x,\xi)|Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}|e_{l}(x,\xi)\rangle\,,\quad k,l\geq 1\,.

Since
(x,\xi)\mapsto Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d};\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}))
, one has

v_{k}(x,\xi):=\sum_{l\geq 1}t_{kl}(x,\xi)e_{l}(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(c(x,\xi))\quad\text{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l\geq 1}{\lambda}_{l}|t_{kl}(x,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|c(x,\xi)|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle<\infty

for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, since

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi<\infty\,.

Taking this last inequality for granted, we conclude as follows. Let
a\equiv a(x,\xi)\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and
B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
satisfy the constraint

a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\leq c(x,\xi)\,,\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}

in the sense that

a(x,\xi)\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\langle\phi|B|\phi\rangle\leq\langle\phi|c(x,\xi)|\phi\rangle\text{ for each }\phi\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(c(x,\xi))\,.

Since
v_{k}(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(c(x,\xi))
for a.e.
(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
and each
k\geq 1

\displaystyle a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)B)

\displaystyle=a(x,\xi)\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi))+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}BQ(x,\xi)^{1/2})

\displaystyle=a(x,\xi)\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle+\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|B|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|c(x,\xi)|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})

\displaystyle.

Integrating in
(x,\xi)
shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(SB)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi

since, by construction,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}Q(x,\xi)dxd\xi=S\,.

Thus

\displaystyle\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle=\sup_{a\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\,B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\atop a(x,\xi)I_{\mathfrak{H}}+B\leq c(x,\xi)}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(SB)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi\leq\langle{\Lambda},c\rangle

\displaystyle,

where the first equality follows from convex duality as explained in Step 2, while the last inequality has been obtained above at the beginning of Step 6. This completes the proof.

It remains to prove that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi<\infty\,.

Since

c(x,\xi)\leq(|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})I_{\mathfrak{H}}+H

one has

v_{k}(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(H)\implies v_{k}(x,\xi)\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(c(x,\xi))

and

\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|c(x,\xi)|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle\leq(|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})\|v_{k}(x,\xi)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|H|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle\,.

Let
(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots)
be a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
H
in
\mathfrak{H}
(the Hermite functions), with eigenvalues
\mu_{j}
. Since

\sum_{k\geq 1}\overline{t_{km}(x,\xi)}t_{kn}(x,\xi)=\langle e_{m}(x,\xi)|Q(x,\xi)|e_{n}(x,\xi)\rangle

by definition of
t_{kl}(x,\xi)
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}|\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|h_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\overline{t_{km}(x,\xi)}t_{kn}(x,\xi)\langle e_{m}(x,\xi)|h_{j}\rangle\langle h_{j}|e_{n}(x,\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\langle e_{m}(x,\xi)|Q(x,\xi)|e_{n}(x,\xi)\rangle\langle e_{m}(x,\xi)|h_{j}\rangle\langle h_{j}|e_{n}(x,\xi)\rangle=\langle h_{j}|Q(x,\xi)|h_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle v_{k}(x,\xi)|H|v_{k}(x,\xi)\rangle dxd\xi=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\langle h_{j}|Q(x,\xi)|h_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}\langle h_{j}|S|h_{j}\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S^{1/2}|H|S^{1/2})<\infty

\displaystyle,

and since

\sum_{k\geq 1}\|v_{k}(x,\xi)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi))=p(x,\xi)\,,

one concludes that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q(x,\xi)^{1/2}c(x,\xi)Q(x,\xi)^{1/2})dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})p(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S^{1/2}HS^{1/2})<\infty

\displaystyle.

∎

Theorem 8.1
.

Let

and let

. Then

(i) one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,g)\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)^{2}+d{\hbar}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,S)^{2}+d{\hbar}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,S)+d{\hbar}\,;

(ii) one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,T)\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S))+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S),T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)^{2}+d{\hbar}}+\sqrt{MK_{\hbar}(S,T)^{2}+d{\hbar}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)+MK_{\hbar}(S,T)+d{\hbar}\,;

(iii) one has

\displaystyle MK_{\hbar}(R,T)\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S),R)+\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S),T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{MK_{\hbar}(R,S)^{2}+d{\hbar}}+\sqrt{MK_{\hbar}(S,T)^{2}+d{\hbar}}

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}
implies that

[
7

implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(f,\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S))\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)+d{\hbar}}\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\widetilde{W}_{\hbar}(S),g)\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(g,S)+d{\hbar}}\,.

, one has the obvious elementary inequality

\sqrt{X+Y}<X+\tfrac{1}{2}Y\,.

This inequality obviously applies to the present case since
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(f,S)\geq d{\hbar}
and

by Theorem 2.4 (2) of

with

Remark.
It is interesting to compare the inequality (iii) above with the “genereliazed triangle inequality” in
[
4
]
. Let us recall that DePalma and Trevisan have constructed a pseudo-distance on density operators on
\mathfrak{H}

which is similar to ours to some extent. The DePalma-Trevisan distance
D
is defined through a different notion of coupling than in
[
6
]
; specifically, their notion of couplings is based on “quantum channels” (completely positive
linear maps on the set of density operators): see Definition 1 in
[
4
]
. While the transport cost in formula (19) of
[
4
]
is in some sense reminiscent of the transport cost used in
[
6
]
, these two costs are in fact
significantly different. For instance, the transport cost used in the definition of
MK_{\hbar}
in
[
6
]
, and in the present paper, has compact resolvent, and therefore its spectrum consists of eigenvalues only. On the contrary, the cost
operator in
[
4
]
in the case of Gaussian quantum systems has continuous spectrum on
[0,+\infty)
.

In Theorem 2 of
[
4
]
, DePalma and Trevisan prove what they call a “triangle inequality” for their distance
D
, of the form

D(R,T)\leq D(R,S)+D(S,S)+D(S,T)

(inequality (35) in
[
4
]
). Of course, if
D
was a real distance,
D(S,S)=0
, and the inequality above coincides with the usual triangle inequality. In
[
4
]
, there is an explicit formula for
D(S,S)
in terms of the canonical
purification of
S
(Corollary 1, formula (34) in
[
4
]
).

With the distance
MK_{\hbar}
defined in
[
6
]
, one has

MK_{\hbar}(R,S)\geq 2d{\hbar}\,,\qquad\text{ for all }R,S\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})\,,

so that Theorem
8.1
(iii) implies that

MK_{\hbar}(R,T)<MK_{\hbar}(R,S)+MK_{\hbar}(S,S)+MK_{\hbar}(S,T)\,.

In other words,
MK_{\hbar}
satisfies the same “generalized triangle inequality” as the DePalma-Trevisan distance
D
, with a strict inequality.

9.1.
A classical/quantum optimal transport

Let
r
be a probability density on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
and
S
a density operator on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

We suppose that an optimal operator
\widetilde{B}
and an optimal function
\widetilde{a}
exists for the Kantorovich duality formulation of
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(r,S)
, as in Theorem
6.1
, and that
\widetilde{a}\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and
\widetilde{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})

\widetilde{a}(q,p)+\widetilde{B}\leq(Z-z)^{2}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(r,S)^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\widetilde{a}(z)r(z)dz+\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}{(\widetilde{B}S)}.

Here we have used the notation
z=(q,p)
,
dz=dqdp
, and
Z=(Q,P)
.

Let us denote by
\Pi(z)
an optimal coupling of
r,S
and let us define

\displaystyle a(z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(|z|^{2}-\widetilde{a}(z))

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(|Z|^{2}-\widetilde{B}).

One has

(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\geq 0\mbox{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}dz=0,

Therefore, since
\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0

\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0\mbox{ a.e},.

In other words,

\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{*}=0

which implies that

(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Pi(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0\mbox{ a.e.}

and (forgetting the “a.e.” in the sequel)

(13)

(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\Pi(z)=0.

Hence, the range of
\Pi
consists in functions
\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ni z\mapsto\psi_{z}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(14)

(a(z)+B-z\cdot Z)\psi_{z}=0\Longleftrightarrow(B-z\cdot Z)\psi_{z}=-a(z)\psi_{z}:

the vectors
\psi_{z}
are the eigenvectors of
B-z\cdot Z
with eigenvalue
-a(z)
.

But

B+a(z)-z\cdot Z\geq 0
. Therefore

-a(z)
is the lowest eigenvalue of
B-2z\cdot Z
.

From now on, we will suppose that the fundamental of
B-z\cdot Z
is non degenerate. This means that
\Pi(z)
is proportional to

|\psi_{z}\rangle\langle\psi_{z}|
and therefore, since
\Pi(z)
is a coupling between
r
and

9.2.
A semiquantum Legendre
transform

As we have seen,
-a(z)
is the fundamental of the operator
B-z\cdot Z
. Therefore, by the variational characterization of the lowest eigenvalue,

-a(z)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\operatorname{Dom}(B)\\
\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1\end{subarray}}(\langle\phi|B|\phi\rangle-z\cdot\langle\phi|Z|\phi\rangle),

to be faced to the classical definition of the Legendre transform

a(z)=\sup_{z^{\prime}}(z\cdot z^{\prime}-b(z^{\prime})).

Let us define the semiquantum Legendre transform by

B^{sq*}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\operatorname{Dom}(B)\\
\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1\end{subarray}}(z\cdot\langle\phi|Z|\phi\rangle-\langle\phi|B|\phi\rangle).

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
a(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}(|z|^{2}-\widetilde{a}(z)),B=\tfrac{1}{2}(|Z|^{2}-\widetilde{B})
where
\widetilde{a}(z)
and
\widetilde{B}
are bounded optimal Kantorovich potentials for
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}(r,S)
. Then

a=B^{sq*}.

Proof.

We just recall the variational argument.

Let
A\geq 0
and
A|\phi_{0}\rangle=0
. Then,

\langle\phi_{0}|A|\phi_{0}\rangle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\operatorname{Dom}(B)\\
\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1\end{subarray}}\langle\phi|A|\phi\rangle

and

\langle\phi_{0}+\delta\phi_{0}|A|\phi_{0}+\delta\phi_{0}\rangle=\langle\delta\phi|A|\delta\phi).

∎

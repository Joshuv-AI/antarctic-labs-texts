Hankel matrices acting on the Dirichlet space
Guanlong Bao, Kunyu Guo, Fangmei Sun and Zipeng Wang

Abstract
. The characterization of the boundedness of operators induced by Hankel matrices on analytic function spaces can be traced back to the work of Z. Nehari and H. Widom on the Hardy space, and has been extensively studied on many other analytic function spaces recently. However, this question remains open in the context of the Dirichlet space [ 20 ] . By Carleson measures, the Widom type condition and the reproducing kernel thesis, this paper provides a comprehensive solution to this question. As a beneficial product, characterizations of the boundedness and compactness of operators induced by Cesàro type matrices on the Dirichlet space are given. In addition, we also show that a random Dirichlet function almost surely induces a compact Hankel type operator on the Dirichlet space.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of nonnegative integers. Suppose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers. An infinite Hankel matrix
\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
induced by the sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is given by

\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=(\lambda_{j+k})_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}}.

The Hankel matrix
\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is initially defined for all finitely supported sequences in
\ell^{2}
. The celebrated Nehari theorem
[
35
]
illustrates that
\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
represents a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}
if and only if there exists a function
\psi
in
L^{\infty}
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
such that
\lambda_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
\psi
.

For
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
in
H(\mathbb{D})
, the class of functions analytic in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Hankel matrix
\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
acts on the function
f
via

\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{n+k}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\ \ z\in\mathbb{D},

whenever the right-hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. For a space
X\subseteq H(\mathbb{D})
equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, the Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

is bounded on
X
if
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)
is well defined in
H(\mathbb{D})
for any
f\in X
and there exits a positive constant
C
independent of
g
such that
\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(g)\|_{X}\leq C\|g\|_{X}
for all
g
in
X
.

By the Fourier transform, the Hankel matrix
\boldsymbol{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
represents a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{2}
. See
[
36
]
for details.

When
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the moment sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
, where
\mu_{n}=\int_{[0,1)}t^{n}d\mu(t)
, the related Hankel matrix is denoted by
\boldsymbol{H}_{\mu}
in the literature. Write
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
for the corresponding Hankel type operator.
H. Widom
[
42
]
proved that
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is bounded on
H^{2}
if and only if
\mu_{j}=O(\frac{1}{j+1})
.
It is worth noting that
(\frac{1}{j+1})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is the moment sequence of the Lebesgure measure on
[0,1)
, which corresponds to the classical Hilbert matrix
\boldsymbol{H}=((j+k+1)^{-1})_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}}
. See
[
11
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
28
,
29
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
45
]
for developments of the Hilbert matrix acting on analytic function spaces.

Since then, operator questions regarding the Hankel matrix have been investigated in various other function spaces of analytic functions on the unit disk, including Hardy spaces, weighted Bergman spaces, Dirichlet type spaces, and Möbius invariant function spaces. In
[
15
]
, E. Diamantopoulos’ work illustrates that H. Widom’s condition remains true for the Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
with
0<\alpha<2
. He also noted that the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on the classical Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
and the Bergman space
A^{2}
coincides. Under a milder condition, using Carleson measures for
\mathcal{D}
, P. Galanopoulos and J. Peláez
[
23
]
completely characterized the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on the Dirichlet space. C. Chatzifountas, D. Girela and J. Peláez studied the boundedness and compactness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
between distinct Hardy spaces in
[
13
]
. D. Girela and N. Merchán
[
24
]
developed a method to give complete descriptions of the boundedness and compactness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on some Hardy spaces and Möbius invariant function spaces. G. Bao, F. Ye and K. Zhu
[
8
]
considered bounded
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on analytic functions spaces in terms of so-called Hankel measures.

In their recent work
[
20
]
, P. Galanopoulos, D. Girela, A. Mas, and N. Merchán demonstrated that a 1-logarithmic 1-Carleson measure ensures the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on the Dirichlet space. However, they also pointed out that the characterization of the finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
for which the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is bounded on the Dirichlet space remains unresolved.
Via Carleson measures, Widom type conditions and the reproducing kernel thesis, the current work answers this question.

Recall that the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
is a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
equipped with the Dirichlet inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}=f(0)\overline{g(0)}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}dA(z).

(1.1)

Here
dA(z)=\pi^{-1}dxdy
for
z=x+iy
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}
. A finite positive Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{D}
is said to be a Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space if there is a positive constant
C
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}d\nu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}

(1.2)

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
. Denote by
CM(\mathcal{D})
the set of Carleson measures for
\mathcal{D}
. The smallest such
C
in (
1.2
) is denoted by
\|\nu\|_{CM(\mathcal{D})}
, the Carleson measure norm of
\nu
. If the identity map
I_{d}:\mathcal{D}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{D},d\nu)
is compact, then we say that
\nu
is a vanishing Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. See
[
2
,
5
,
19
,
23
,
41
,
43
]
for this definition of Carleson measures for
\mathcal{D}
and related investigations.

In this paper, we first obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers. Then the Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and the measure
|h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, where
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{n}}z^{n}
.

In the case of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
by Theorem
1.1
, the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
|h^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
, where

h_{\mu}(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-tz}d\mu(t).

Note that in
[
41
]
D. Stegenga’s beautiful characterization of
CM(\mathcal{D})
is related to the logarithmic capacity
of a finite union of intervals of the unit circle. Based on certain integrals involve the Carleson box and the
heightened box, N. Arcozzi, R. Rochberg and E. Sawyer
[
2
, Theorem 1]
gave a complete description of
CM(\mathcal{D})
. There still lacks a direct relation between moment sequences and the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on the Dirichlet space.
We will establish these relations through the use of Widom type conditions and the reproducing kernel thesis.

Corresponding to the Dirichlet inner product (
1.1
), the reproducing kernel of the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
at a point
w\in\mathbb{D}
is

K_{w}(z)=1+\log\frac{1}{1-z\overline{w}}

and its normalized reproducing kernel

k_{w}(w)=\frac{K_{w}(z)}{\sqrt{K_{w}(w)}}.

Now, we state our characterization of the bounded Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on the Dirichlet spaces as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mu
is a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

The Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

The reproducing kernel thesis holds; that is,

\sup_{t\in[0,1)}\|\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(k_{t})\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\infty,

where
k_{t}
is the normalized reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}
at
t
in
[0,1)
.

(iii)

The Widom type condition is true; that is,

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n\mu_{n}^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need Cesàro type matrices. As benefit products, the necessary and sufficient condition for the boundedness of Cesàro type matrix acting on the Dirichlet space is obtained.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complex numbers. Recall that the Cesàro type matrix
\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is the following infinite lower triangular matrix:

\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{0}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
\eta_{1}&\eta_{1}&0&0&\cdots\\
\eta_{2}&\eta_{2}&\eta_{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

In the same way as operators induced by Hankel matrices, the matrix

\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
induces a Cesàro type operator
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\eta_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\Big)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(1.3)

for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
H(\mathbb{D})
, whenever the right-hand side of (
1.3
) makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.

If
\eta_{n}=1/(n+1)
for every
n
, then the Cesàro type matrix
\boldsymbol{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is the classical Cesàro matrix
\boldsymbol{C}
. In
[
14
]
, N. Danikas and A. Siskakis showed that the operator induced by
\boldsymbol{C}
is bounded from
H^{\infty}
to
BMOA
. A. Siskakis
[
38
,
39
]
studied the action of
\boldsymbol{C}
on Hardy spaces.

Given a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
, if

\eta_{n}=\mu_{n}=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{1}{1-tz}d\mu(t)

for each
n
, then we write
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
as
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
. The operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is said to be the Cesàro-like operator which was introduced in

[
22
,
26
]
recently. We refer to
[
7
,
9
,
20
,
21
]
for more results about
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
on some spaces of analytic functions. From Theorem 5 in
[
20
]
, if
\mu
is a 1-logarithmic 1-Carleson measure, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is a bounded on
\mathcal{D}
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
, then
\mu
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-logarithmic 1-Carleson measure.
We obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of complex numbers. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

The Cesàro type operator
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

The reproducing kernel thesis holds; that is,

\sup_{t\in[0,1)}\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\infty,

where
k_{t}
is the normalized reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}
at
t
in
[0,1)
.

(iii)

The Widom type condition is true; that is,

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n|\eta_{n}|^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

Following our previous work on the boundedness of Hankel type operators and Cesàro type operators on the Dirichlet space, we also establish their compactness counterparts. These results are included in Section 4.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) real random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
. For a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of complex numbers, one formally defines a random power series

\varphi_{\omega}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}(\omega)\alpha_{n}z^{n}.

By Z. Nehari’s theorem, almost surely the random analytic function
\varphi_{\omega}
induces a bounded Hankel type operator on the Hardy space
H^{2}
if and only if the symbol function
\varphi_{\omega}
is a BMOA function almost surely. The famous unsolved J. Anderson’s question
[
1
]
is to seek the necessary and sufficient condition to characterize random BMOA functions. One can consult
[
27
]
for recent progress and more details in this subject.

According to Theorem
1.1
, the symbol function
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
of a bounded Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\mathcal{D}
must be in the Dirichlet space. However, our next corollary illustrates that, under milder conditions, a random Dirichlet function almost surely induced a compact Hankel type operator, and hence almost surely induced a bounded Hankel type operator.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers.
Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables satisfying that
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]
is finite.
If
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{D}
, then almost surely the Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
,
where
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\left\{X_{n}\overline{\lambda_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be a set. As defined by W. Rudin in
[
37
]
, the integrable function
f
on the unite circle
\mathbb{T}
is called an
E
-function if
\hat{f}(n)
, the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
, is equal to zero for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus E
. Suppose
p>2
, the set
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is called a Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set if there is a constant
C
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

for all trigonometric polynomials whose coefficients are equal to
0
on
\mathbb{Z}\setminus E
. The Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set is a natural generalization of the classical lacunary sequences (the Hadamard set) which plays important roles in many aspects of analysis (see
[
37
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
10
]
for more details). Recall that an increasing sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers is said to be lacunary if there exists
q
in
(1,\infty)
such that
n_{k+1}/n_{k}>q
for all
k
. It is also known that there exist Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
sets rather than Hadamard sets.

Our next result presents a characterization of bounded and compact Hankel type operators on
\mathcal{D}
when the non zero Fourier coefficients of the symbol function are located in a Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
p>2
, the set
E
is a Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set and

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}:n\in E\}
is a sequence of complex numbers. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

The Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

The Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
.

(iii)

The symbol function
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}(z)=\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}z^{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
; that is,

\sum_{n\in E}(n+1)|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

Throughout this paper, the symbol
A\thickapprox B
means that
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
. We say that
A\lesssim B
if there exists a positive
constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
.

2.
Bounded Hankel type operators
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\mathcal{D}

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.1
. We also consider the Möbius invariance of the norm of bounded
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on the Dirichlet space. For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, we say that
f\in\mathcal{X}
if

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}=|f(0)|^{2}+\||f^{\prime}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\mathcal{D})}<\infty.

Denote by
\mathcal{X}_{0}
the norm closure in
\mathcal{X}
of the space of polynomials. Equivalently,
f\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
if and only if
|f^{\prime}|^{2}dA
is a vanishing Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. We refer to
[
3
,
44
]
for the theory of

\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Given a function
b
in
H(\mathbb{D})
, define a Hankel type bilinear form on the Dirichlet space, initially for
f
,
g
in
\mathcal{P}
, as

T_{b}(f,g):=\langle fg,b\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}.

Here
\mathcal{P}
is the space of polynomials.
The norm of the bilinear form
T_{b}
is

\|T_{b}\|_{\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}}=\sup\left\{|T_{b}(f,g)|:\ \ \|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}=\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}=1,\ f,g\in\mathcal{P}\right\}.

For a bounded bilinear form
T_{b}
on
\mathcal{D}
,
T_{b}
is said to be compact on
\mathcal{D}
if
T_{b}(B_{\mathcal{D}}\times B_{\mathcal{D}})
is precompact in
\mathbb{C}
, where
B_{\mathcal{D}}=\{f\in\mathcal{D}:\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\leq 1\}
. In other words,
T_{b}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if for all bounded sequences
\{(f_{n},g_{n})\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
, the sequence

\{T_{b}(f_{n},g_{n})\}
has a convergent subsequence. See
[
6
]
for more descriptions on compact bilinear operators.

With respect to the normalized basis
\{(n+1)^{-1/2}z^{n}\}
of the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, the matrix representations of
T_{b}
is

\left(\frac{j+k}{\sqrt{j+1}\sqrt{k+1}}\overline{b_{j+k}}\right),\ \ \ \ j,k=0,1,\cdots,

where
b_{j+k}
is the
(j+k)
-th Taylor coefficient of
b
.
N. Arcozzi, R. Rochberg, E. Sawyer and B. Wick characterized the boundedness and compactness of the bilinear form on the Dirichlet space as follows.

Theorem A
.

[
4
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
b\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

T_{b}
extends to a bounded bilinear form on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
b\in\mathcal{X}
.

(ii)

T_{b}
extends to a compact bilinear form on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
b\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given a sequence of complex number
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\{\lambda_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
, suppose
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and the measure
|h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. Then

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\in\mathcal{X}
. It follows that
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{D}
, and

\|h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}=|\lambda_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)|\lambda_{n+1}|^{2}<\infty.

Let
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}c_{k}z^{k}
belong to
\mathcal{D}
.
Then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j+k}c_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda_{j+k}|^{2}}{k+1}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(j+k+1)|\lambda_{j+k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\|h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Write
a_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j+k}c_{k}
.
Then
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence of complex numbers and hence
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}z^{j}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
.
Note that

\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)|a_{j+1}|^{2}.

Let
m
be a positive integer. For any polynomial
g(z)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}b_{j}z^{j}
with
\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|a_{0}\overline{b_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(j+1)a_{j+1}\overline{b_{j+1}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}||c_{0}||b_{0}|+|b_{0}|\Big|\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\lambda_{k}c_{k}\Big|+\Big|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}\overline{b_{j+1}}(j+1)\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\lambda_{j+1+k}c_{k}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}||c_{0}||b_{0}|+|b_{0}|\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}\overline{h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)}dA(z)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)g^{\prime}_{1}(z)\ \overline{h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)}dA(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}|\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}\Big|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|g_{1}^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}dA(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
g_{1}(z)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}\overline{b_{j}}z^{j}
.
Since
|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\bigg|a_{0}\overline{b_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(j+1)a_{j+1}\overline{b_{j+1}}\bigg|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Therefore

\bigg(|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(j+1)|a_{j+1}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}

and
\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

On the other hand, suppose the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
. Then for any
f
and
g
in
\mathcal{D}
,

|\langle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}|\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

This implies that
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)<\infty.

Moreover, for any polynomial
f
and
g
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)g^{\prime}(z)\ \overline{h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)}dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f)^{\prime}(z)g^{\prime}(\overline{z})dA(z),

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)g(z)\overline{h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)}dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g)^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(\overline{z})dA(z).

(2.2)

Consequently,

\left|f(0)g(0)\overline{h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(0)}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}(f(z)g(z))^{\prime}\overline{h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)}dA(z)\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Therefore,
T_{h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
is a bounded bilinear form on
\mathcal{D}
.
It follows from Theorem
A
that
h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
|h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. This completes the whole proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Denote by
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
the Möbius group which consists of all one-to-one analytic functions that map
\mathbb{D}
onto itself. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
be the space of functions
f
in
\mathcal{D}
with
f(0)=0
.
For the bounded
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
, the norm of
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is Möbius invariant in the following sense.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complex numbers. Suppose the Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
. Then there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

depending only on
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that

C_{1}\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|\leq\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda_{\phi}}}\|\leq C_{2}\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|

for all
\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\boldsymbol{\lambda_{\phi}}=\left\{\overline{\lambda_{\phi,n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\circ\phi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\phi,n}z^{n}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
, the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
yields

\||h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})}<\infty.

From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depending only on
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that

C_{1}\||h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})}\leq\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|\leq C_{2}\||h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})}.

(2.3)

Let
\phi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. For
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
, by the change of variables, we see

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|^{2}|(h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\circ\phi)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(\phi^{-1}(w))|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(w)|^{2}dA(w).

Also,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(g\circ\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(w)|^{2}dA(w).

Thus there is
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|^{2}|(h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\circ\phi)^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

for all
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k(w)|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(w)|^{2}dA(w)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

for all
k\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
. This means

\||h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})}=\||(h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\circ\phi)^{\prime}|^{2}dA\|_{CM(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})}.

Combining this with (
2.3
), we get the desired result.
∎

3.
Bounded Hankel type operators
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and Cesàro type operators on the Dirichlet space

This section is devoted to an intrinsic description of the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on
\mathcal{D}
. Indeed, we can characterize bounded Hankel type operators
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
on
\mathcal{D}
induced by a decreasing sequence of positive numbers. The proof is based on our characterization of bounded Cesàro type operators on
\mathcal{D}
.

The following Hilbert’s double theorem for the Dirichlet space plays a crucial role in our proofs. By using Schur’s test, one can show that inequality (see the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
40
]
for more details).

Theorem B
.

[
40
, p. 814]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
in
\mathcal{D}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
f
such that

\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}||a_{m}|}{\log(n+m+1)}\leq C\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}n|a_{n}|^{2}.

We first prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
\|k_{w}\|_{\mathcal{D}}=1
for any
w\in\mathbb{D}
, we obtain the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
.

Next, for each
t\in[0,1)
, recall that the normalized reproducing kernel
k_{t}
for the Dirichlet space

k_{t}(z)=\left(1+\log\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}t^{n}z^{n}\right).

By direct computations,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\log\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(|\eta_{0}|^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\Big(1+\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}\frac{t^{k}}{k}\Big)^{2}\right).

Thus for any positive integer
m
and
t\in[0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\gtrsim

\displaystyle\left(\log\frac{e}{1-t}\right)^{-1}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\Big(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{t^{k}}{k}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\left(\log\frac{e}{1-t}\right)^{-1}t^{2m}(\log(m+1))^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}.

In particular, let
t=\frac{m}{m+1}\in(0,1)
,

\frac{1}{\log(m+1)}\gtrsim\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}.

By (ii), we have
\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\lesssim 1
and

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n|\eta_{n}|^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right),

which gives
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. The Widom type condition gives

|\eta_{n}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(n+1)\log(n+2)},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}
in
\mathcal{D}
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{n}\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}\right|\leq

\displaystyle|\eta_{n}|\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}\frac{1}{k+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}(k+1)|a_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\eta_{n}|\left(\log(n+2)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}

for all nonnegative integers
n
.
Thus
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(f)
is well defined in
H(\mathbb{D})
.

Because of
(iii)
,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}<\infty.

(3.1)

Let
g(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}
be in the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\eta_{0}a_{0}|^{2}+2\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}|a_{0}|^{2}+2\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}.

Using (
3.1
), (iii), and Theorem
B
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}+\small{\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|a_{k}|\chi_{\{k\leq n+1\}}(k)\bigg)\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{j}|\chi_{\{j\leq n+1\}}(j)\bigg)}

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{k}||a_{j}|\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{n=\max\{k-1,j-1\}}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{|a_{k}||a_{j}|}{\log(k+j+1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}},

where
\chi_{\{k\leq n+1\}}(k)
is the characteristic function on
{\{k\leq n+1\}}\cap\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Here
\mathbb{N}^{+}
is the set of positive integers. Hence
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
follows from the next more general result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\{\lambda_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
is a decreasing sequence of positive real numbers. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

The Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

The reproducing kernel thesis holds; that is,

\sup_{t\in[0,1)}\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}k_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\infty,

where
k_{t}
is the normalized reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}
at
t
in
[0,1)
.

(iii)

The Widom type condition is true; that is,

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n\lambda_{n}^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

Proof.

If
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
, it is clear that (ii) holds.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. Since
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a decreasing sequence of positive numbers,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}k_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(1+\log\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-1}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\lambda_{n+k+1}\frac{t^{k}}{k}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1+\log\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-1}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}\lambda_{n+k+1}\frac{t^{k}}{k}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1+\log\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-1}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)\lambda_{2n+1}^{2}\Big(\sum^{m}_{k=1}\frac{t^{k}}{k}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\left(\log\frac{e}{1-t}\right)^{-1}t^{2m}(\log(m+1))^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)\lambda_{2n+1}^{2},

for all
t\in[0,1)
and all positive integers
m
.

For any fixed integer
m
, taking
t=\frac{m}{m+1}
in the above, we get

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(2n+1)\lambda_{2n+1}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log(m+1)}

and

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(2n+2)\lambda_{2n+2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log(m+1)}.

Then the desired result holds.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. It follows from (iii) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{k\log(k+1)}

(3.2)

for all positive integers
k
.
Let
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}
belong to
\mathcal{D}
. By (
3.2
) and the monotonicity of
\{\lambda_{k}\}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\lambda_{n+k}a_{k}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}|a_{0}|+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\lambda_{n+k}|a_{k}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{n+k}}{k+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}\log(k+1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}

for all nonnegative integers
n
.
Then
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)
is analytic on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for any
f
in
\mathcal{D}
.

Observe that by condition (iii), we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\lambda_{n+1}^{2}<\infty.

(3.3)

Then the Hölder inequality gives

\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\lambda_{k}|a_{k}|\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\lambda_{k}|a_{k}|\right)^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\lambda_{n+k+1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=0}\lambda_{n+k+1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}\lambda_{n+k+1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}.

(3.4)

By Theorem
1.3
and the monotonicity of the sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, it is true that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=0}\lambda_{n+k+1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\lambda_{n+1}^{2}\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=0}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f_{2}\|^{2}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}},

(3.5)

where
f_{2}(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}|a_{k}|z^{k}
with the same Dirichlet norm of
f
.

Note that the monotonicity of the sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
again, the Hölder inequality and (
3.3
). Then

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}\lambda_{n+k+1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}\lambda_{k}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}(n+1)\bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{k}}{k}\bigg)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{k}}{k}\sum^{k-1}_{n=0}(n+1)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}k\lambda^{2}_{k}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}.

(3.6)

Joining (
3
), (
3
), and (
3
), we get the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
\mathcal{D}
. The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Compact Hankel and Cesàro type operators on
\mathcal{D}

In this section, we give corresponding results about the compactness of Hankel and Cesàro type operators on
\mathcal{D}
.

The result below is the compact version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers. Then the Hankel type operator
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is compact on the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and the measure
|h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a vanishing Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Suppose
h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Then the identity map

I_{d}:\mathcal{D}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{D},|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}|^{2}dA)

is compact. Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}
such that
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
tends
to 0 uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
k\to\infty
.
From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f_{k},g\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}||f_{k}(0)||g(0)|+\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\frac{f_{k}(z)-f_{k}(0)}{z}\Big|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{k}(z)|^{2}|h^{\prime}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
g\in\mathcal{D}
. Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is an integer
k_{0}
such that

|\langle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f_{k},g\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{D}}\ \ \varepsilon

for all
k>k_{0}
and all
g\in\mathcal{D}
. Then
\|\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Thus
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
.

Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
. For a bounded sequence
\{(f_{n},g_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
, both
\{f_{n}\}
and
\{g_{n}\}
are bounded sequences in
\mathcal{D}
.
Then both
\{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f_{n})\}
and
\{\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(g_{n})\}
have convergent subsequences.
Because of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
T_{h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}
extends to a compact bilinear form on
\mathcal{D}
.
Then Theorem
A
yields
h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. The proof is complete.
∎

For the compactness of
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
on
\mathcal{D}
, we also have the following conclusion.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

(i)

The Cesàro type operator
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is compact on

(ii)

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}}=0,

where
k_{t}
is the normalized reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}
at
t
in
[0,1)

(iii)

The Widom type condition is true; that is,

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n|\eta_{n}|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Note that
\|k_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}}=1
for each
t\in[0,1)
and
k_{t}
tends to zero uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)

1.3

\displaystyle\log(m+1)\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\lesssim\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}k_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}

(4.1)

for all positive integers
m
and all
t
in
[0,1)
. Taking
t\to 1^{-}
in (
4.1
), we obtain (iii).

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let
m
be a positive integer. For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in

\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(m)}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\Big(\eta_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\Big)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(m)}
is a finite rank operator. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(m)}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
. Because of (iii), for every
\epsilon>0

N
such that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}<\frac{\epsilon}{\log(m+2)}

1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{k}||a_{j}|\sum^{\infty}_{n=\max\{k-1,j-1,m\}}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{|a_{k}||a_{j}|}{\log(k+j+m)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}

for all

. Thus, for

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(m)})(f)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}(n+1)|\eta_{n+1}|^{2}\Big(\sum^{n+1}_{k=1}|a_{k}|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}.

In other words,
\|\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(m)}\|\to 0
as

. Hence
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is compact on
\mathcal{D}
. The proof is finished.
∎

5.
Random Hankel type operators on
\mathcal{D}
and a result related to Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
. Many known real random variables sequences
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
1.4
. First of all, all bounded mean zero random variables are included in Thereom
1.4
. The typical example is the
Bernoulli random variables sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. In other words,
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is independent such that
\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=1)=\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=-1)=1/2
. In addition, for the classical independent normal distribution sequence
X_{n}=N(0,1)
, we have
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]=3
(cf.
[
30
, p. 2]
). Therefore, Theorem
1.4
can be applied to the corresponding random Gaussian analytic Dirichlet functions.

For the
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
complex number sequence and a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables

(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]<\infty
.
If
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{D}
, then
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty
. Moreover, by i.i.d. and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{2}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n}X_{n}|^{2}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\mathbb{E}[|X_{n}|^{2}]<\infty

and almost surely

h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\omega}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}(\omega)\lambda_{n}z^{n}

is analytic on the unit disk.

For our proof, we need the following elementary inequalities which may be well known. However, we cannot locate literature.
For the sake of completeness, we have included a brief proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables with
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]=1
. Then for all finite complex numbers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\bigg|^{4}\bigg]\leq 3\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}\bigg]^{2}.

Proof.

For any fixed natural number
n\geq 1
,
let

A_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}.

It is easy to see

|A_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}X_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i<j}Re(a_{i}\bar{a}_{j})X_{i}X_{j}.

Note that
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables with
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
for each
n
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|A_{n}|^{4}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}X_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{2}\right]+4\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg(\sum_{i<j}Re(a_{i}\bar{a}_{j})X_{i}X_{j}\bigg)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}|a_{i}|^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]+4\sum_{i<j}(Re(a_{i}\bar{a}_{j}))^{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j}|a_{i}|^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]+4\sum_{i<j}|a_{i}\bar{a}_{j}|^{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}].

Observe that
\big(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]\big)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{4}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j}^{4}]=1
, hence

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\bigg|^{4}\bigg]\leq 3\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}\bigg]^{2},

and the proof is completed.
∎

Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. For
0<p<\infty
and
0\leq r<1
, let

M_{p}(f,r)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

M_{\infty}(f,r)=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}|f(re^{i\theta})|.

Denote by
H^{p}
the Hardy space consisting of those functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{H^{p}}=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(f,r)<\infty.

It is well known (cf.
[
19
, p. 3]
) that
\mathcal{D}
is a subset of
H^{p}
for any
p>0
.

From a result of L. Brown and A. Shields
[
12
, p. 300]
, if an analytic function
f
satisfies
M_{p}(f^{\prime},r)\in L^{2}([0,1],dr)
with
2<p\leq\infty
, then

|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. We strengthen this conclusion as follows.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
2<p\leq\infty
. Suppose
\phi
is an analytic function in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
M_{p}(\phi^{\prime},r)\in L^{2}([0,1],dr)
. Then

|\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a vanishing Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space.

Proof.

Let
2<p<\infty
. Suppose
\{f_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{D}
such that
\sup_{m}\|f_{m}\|_{\mathcal{D}}<\infty
and functions
f_{m}
tend to zero uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
m\to\infty
.
Due to
M_{p}(\phi^{\prime},r)\in L^{2}([0,1],dr)
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta
in
(0,1)
such that

\int_{\delta}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\phi^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}dr<\epsilon.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|>\delta\}}|f_{m}(z)|^{2}|\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{m}(re^{i\theta})|^{2}|\phi^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\delta}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\phi^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}dr\right)\left(\int_{\delta}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{m}(re^{i\theta})|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\ \|f_{m}\|_{H^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon\ \|f_{m}\|_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon.

Since functions
f_{m}
tend to zero uniformly in
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|\leq\delta\}
, there is a positive integer
N
such that

\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|\leq\delta\}}|f_{m}(z)\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\leq\epsilon

for all
m>N
. Consequently,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{m}(z)\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)=0.

Thus
|\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a vanishing Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
. The proof of the case of
p=\infty
is similar. We omit it.

∎

Note that if
p=2
and
\phi\in H(\mathbb{D})
, then
M_{p}(\phi^{\prime},r)\in L^{2}([0,1],dr)
if and only if
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
. In general, the measure

|\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
with
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
is not a vanishing Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space.

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]=0
, then
X_{n}=0
almost surely. Hence
without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]=1
.
Recall that

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}(\omega)\overline{\lambda_{n}}z^{n}.

By Theorem
1.1
, we shall show that almost surely

|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)

is vanishing Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
.
By Lemma
5.2
, it is sufficient to show that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}dr\bigg]<\infty.

It follows from Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}dr\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}d\mathbb{P}(w)dr.

Applying the Hölder inequality with respect to the probability measure
d\mathbb{P}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}d\mathbb{P}(w)dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}d\mathbb{P}(w)\bigg)^{1/2}dr.

Consequently,

\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}d\mathbb{P}(w)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\Omega}|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}d\mathbb{P}(w)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

Recall that

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{i(n-1)\theta}X_{n}(\omega)

and Lemma
5.1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{i(n-1)\theta}X_{n}\bigg|^{4}d\mathbb{P}(w)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}|\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}|^{2}dr.

Therefore, there is a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\omega}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{4}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}dr\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

This completes the whole proof.
∎

To prove Corollary
1.5
, we start with a simple observation.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
p>2
,
E
is a Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
set and

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}:n\in E\}
is a sequence of complex numbers. If
\sum_{n\in E}n|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty
, then
|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a vanishing Carleson measure for the Dirichlet space, where

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(z)=\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}z^{n}.

Proof.

Let
p>2
. For any
0<r<1
,
observe that

h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})=e^{-i\theta}\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{in\theta},

we have

\|h_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})\|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi],d\theta)}=\bigg\|\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{in\theta}\bigg\|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi],d\theta)}.

Since
E
is a Rudin’s
\Lambda(p)
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{in\theta}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,2\pi],d\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg\|\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{in\theta}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\pi],d\theta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{n\in E}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}n^{2}r^{2(n-1)}.

Recall that
\sum_{n\in E}n|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty
, therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\|\sum_{n\in E}\overline{\lambda_{n}}nr^{n-1}e^{in\theta}\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{p}([0,2\pi],d\theta)}dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n\in E}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}n^{2}r^{2(n-1)}dr

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle+\infty.

The lemma then follows from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded on
\mathcal{D}
. By Theorem
1.1
,
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}\in\mathbb{D}
. By the definition of Dirichlet norm, the condition (iii) holds.
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is clear. The implication
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
follows from Lemma
5.3
and Theorem
4.1
. The proof is complete.
∎

6.
Final remarks

In this section, we give some remarks about some functions in
\mathcal{X}
and the action of Hankel matrices on the Bergman space
A^{2}
.

As mentioned in
[
3
, p. 16]
,
very little is known about functions in
\mathcal{X}
. Our results in this paper give a satisfactory description of functions in
\mathcal{X}
with decreasing Taylor’s sequences of positive numbers. More precisely,
suppose
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\{\lambda_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
is a decreasing sequence of positive numbers. From Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
3.1
, the measure
|h^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
(i.e.
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{X}
) if and only if

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n\lambda_{n}^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

By Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
, the measure
|h^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(z)|^{2}dA(z)
is a vanishing Carleson measure for
\mathcal{D}
(i.e.
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
) if and only if

\sum^{\infty}_{n=m}n\lambda_{n}^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{\log(m+2)}\right).

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of complex numbers.
Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables with
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}^{4}]<\infty
.
If
h_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{D}
, Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
4.1
yield that

\mathbb{P}(h_{{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}},\omega}}\in\mathcal{X})=\mathbb{P}(h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}},\omega}\in\mathcal{X}_{0})=1,

where
h_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}},\omega}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}\overline{\lambda_{n}}z^{n}
.

Next we consider Hankel type operators
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on the Bergman space
A^{2}
which is also a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
and it is equipped with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{A^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dA(z).

From
[
15
, p. 349]
,
(A^{2})^{*}\cong\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}=A^{2}
under the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}b_{k},

(6.1)

where
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}
and
g(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}b_{k}z^{k}
. For a sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of complex numbers, it is easy to see

\langle\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\rangle.

Consequently,
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded (resp. compact) on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if it is bounded (resp. compact) on
A^{2}
.
If we replace the pairing (
6.1
) by the Cauchy pairing

(f,g)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}\overline{b_{k}}.

Then

(\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f,g)=(f,\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}g).

Hence we also get that
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is bounded (resp. compact) on
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
is bounded (resp. compact) on
A^{2}
.

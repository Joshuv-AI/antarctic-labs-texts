Approximation Algorithms for Packing Cycles and Paths in Complete Graphs
Jingyang Zhao, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
Given an edge-weighted (metric/general) complete graph with n n vertices, the maximum weight (metric/general) k k -cycle/path packing problem is to find a set of n k \frac{n}{k} vertex-disjoint k k -cycles/paths such that the total weight is maximized. In this paper, we consider approximation algorithms. For metric k k -cycle packing, we improve the previous approximation ratio from 3 / 5 3/5 to 7 / 10 7/10 for k = 5 k=5 , and from 7 / 8 ⋅ ( 1 − 1 / k ) 2 7/8\cdot(1-1/k)^{2} for k > 5 k>5 to ( 7 / 8 − 0.125 / k ) ​ ( 1 − 1 / k ) (7/8-0.125/k)(1-1/k) for constant odd k > 5 k>5 and to 7 / 8 ⋅ ( 1 − 1 / k + 1 k ⁡ ( k − 1 ) ) 7/8\cdot(1-1/k+\frac{1}{k(k-1)}) for even k > 5 k>5 . For metric k k -path packing, we improve the approximation ratio from 7 / 8 ⋅ ( 1 − 1 / k ) 7/8\cdot(1-1/k) to 27 ​ k 2 − 48 ​ k + 16 32 ​ k 2 − 36 ​ k − 24 \frac{27k^{2}-48k+16}{32k^{2}-36k-24} for even 10 ≥ k ≥ 6 10\geq k\geq 6 . For the case of k = 4 k=4 , we improve the approximation ratio from 3 / 4 3/4 to 5 / 6 5/6 for metric 4-cycle packing, from 2 / 3 2/3 to 3 / 4 3/4 for general 4-cycle packing, and from 3 / 4 3/4 to 14 / 17 14/17 for metric 4-path packing. Keywords: Approximation Algorithms, Cycle Packing, Path Packing

Preliminaries

We use
G=(V,E)
to denote an undirected complete graph with
n
vertices such that
n\bmod k=0
. There is a non-negative weight function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on the edges in
E
. For an edge
uv\in E
, we use
w(u,v)
to denote its weight. A graph is called a
metric
graph if the weight function satisfies the triangle inequality; Otherwise, it is called a
general
graph. For any weight function
w:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define
w(Y)=\sum_{x\in Y}w(x)
for any
Y\subseteq X
.

Two subgraphs or subsets of edges of a graph are
vertex-disjoint
if they do not appear a common vertex. We only consider simple paths and simple cycles with more than two vertices. The
length
of a path/cycle is the number of vertices it contains. A
cycle packing
is a set of vertex-disjoint cycles such that the length of each cycle is at least three and all vertices in the graph are covered. Given a cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
, we use
l(\mathcal{C})
to denote the minimum length of cycles in
\mathcal{C}
. We also use
\mathcal{C}^{*}
to denote the maximum weight cycle packing.
A path (resp., cycle) on
k
different vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}\}
is called a
k
-path
(resp.,
k
-cycle
), denoted by
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{k}
(resp.,
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{k}v_{1}
). A
k
-path packing
(resp.,
k
-cycle packing
) in graph
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint
n/k

k
-paths (resp.,
k
-cycles) such that all vertices in the graph are covered.
Note that we can obtain a
k
-cycle packing by completing every
k
-path of a
k
-path packing. Let
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}
) denote the maximum weight
k
-path packing (resp.,
k
-cycle packing). We can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{*})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})
for
k\geq 3
.

A
2
-path packing is usually called a
matching
of size
n/2
. The maximum weight matching of size
n/2
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{*}
.
An
n
-cycle is also called a
Hamiltonian
cycle. MAX TSP is to find a maximum weight Hamiltonian cycle. Since we consider maximization problems, we simply use general/metric TSP to denote MAX TSP in general/metric graphs. We use
H^{*}
to denote the maximum weight Hamiltonian cycle. For a
k
-path
P=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{k}
where
k
is even, we define

\widetilde{w}(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{k/2}w(v_{2j-1},v_{2j}).

3.1
A Better Black-Box

Given an

Step 1.
Obtain a Hamiltonian cycle

Step 2.

Lemma 4
.

It holds that
w(\mathcal{C}_{k})\geq\frac{k-2}{k-1}w(\mathcal{P}_{k})+\frac{2}{k-1}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P}_{k})
.

Proof.

Since
C_{ij_{i}}
is the maximum weight cycle from the
k-1
cycles, we have

\displaystyle w(C_{ij_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}w(C_{ij})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(w(P_{i})+w(v_{i1},v_{i(j+1)})+w(v_{ij},v_{ik})-w(v_{ij},v_{i(j+1)}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left((k-1)w(P_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(w(v_{i1},v_{i(j+1)})+w(v_{ij},v_{ik}))-w(P_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k-1}\left((k-2)w(P_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(w(v_{i1},v_{i(j+1)})+w(v_{ij},v_{ik}))\right).

By the triangle inequality, we can get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}w(v_{i1},v_{i(j+1)})=

\displaystyle\ w(v_{i1},v_{i2})+\sum_{j=2}^{k/2}(w(v_{i1},v_{i(2j-1)})+w(v_{i1},v_{i(2j)}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ w(v_{i1},v_{i2})+\sum_{j=2}^{k/2}w(v_{i(2j-1)},v_{i(2j)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{j=1}^{k/2}w(v_{i(2j-1)},v_{i(2j)})=\widetilde{w}(P_{i}).

Similarly, we can get
\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}w(v_{ij},v_{ik})\geq\widetilde{w}(P_{i})
. Hence, we can get

\displaystyle w(C_{ij_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\left((k-2)w(P_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(w(v_{i1},v_{i(j+1)})+w(v_{ij},v_{ik}))\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(k-2)w(P_{i})+2\widetilde{w}(P_{i})}{k-1}.

By doing this for all
k
-paths in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, we can get a
k
-cycle packing

such that
w(\mathcal{C}_{k})\geq\frac{(k-2)w(\mathcal{P}_{k})+2\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P}_{k})}{k-1}
.
∎

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{C}_{k})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(k-2)w(\mathcal{P}_{k})+2\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P}_{k})}{k-1}

, and the approximation ratio is
\alpha\cdot(1-0.5/k)(1-1/k+\frac{1}{k(k-1)})
.
∎

3.3
An Improved Algorithm Based on Matching

Consider metric
k
CP with odd

. By deleting the least weighted edge from every
k
-cycle in
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}
, we can get a
k
-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{k}

w(\mathcal{P}_{k})\geq(1-1/k)w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})

\mathcal{P}_{k}
can be decomposed into two edge-disjoint matchings of size

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{p}
be the maximum weight matching of size

, which can be computed in polynomial time
[
10
,
20
]

2w(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{p})\geq w(\mathcal{P}_{k})\geq(1-1/k)w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}).

Note that there are also
n/k

isolated vertices
not covered by
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{p}
. Next, we construct a
k
-cycle packing using

with the isolated vertices. The algorithm, denoted by Alg.3, is shown as follows.

Step 1.
Arbitrarily partition the
p

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{p}
into
n/k
sets with the same size, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{n/k}
. Note that each edge set contains
m\coloneqq(k-1)/2
edges. For each of the
n/k

Step 2.
Consider an arbitrary edge set
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}\}
with the isolated vertex
v
. Assume w.o.l.g. that
w(e_{1})\geq w(e_{m})\geq w(e_{i})
for
2\leq i<m
, i.e.,
w(e_{1})+w(e_{m})\geq(2/m)w(\mathcal{S}_{i})
. Orient each edge
e_{i}
uniformly at random from the two choices. Let
t_{i}
(resp.,
h_{i}
) denote the tail (resp., head) vertex of

. Construct a
k

such that
C_{i}=vt_{1}h_{1}t_{2}h_{2}\cdots t_{m}h_{m}v

Step 3.
Get a
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}
by packing the
k
-cycles from the edge sets and the isolated vertices.

Alg.3 can be derandomized efficiently by the method of conditional expectations
[
30
]
.

Next, we analyze the expected weight of
C_{i}=vt_{1}h_{1}t_{2}h_{2}\cdots t_{m}h_{m}v
, obtained from the edge set
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and the isolated vertex
v
.

Lemma 9
.

It holds that
\mathbb{E}[w(v,t_{1})]\geq\frac{1}{2}w(e_{1})
,
\mathbb{E}[w(v,h_{m})]\geq\frac{1}{2}w(e_{m})
, and
\mathbb{E}[w(h_{i},t_{i+1})]\geq\frac{1}{4}(w(e_{i})+w(e_{i+1}))
for
1\leq i<m
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathbb{E}[w(v,t_{1})]
. Since we orient the edge
e_{1}
uniformly at random, each vertex of
e_{1}
has a probability of
1/2
being
t_{1}
. Hence, we can get
\mathbb{E}[w(v,t_{1})]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in e_{1}}w(v,u)\geq\frac{1}{2}w(e_{1})
by the triangle inequality. Similarly, we can get
\mathbb{E}[w(v,h_{m})]\geq\frac{1}{2}w(e_{m})
.

Consider
\mathbb{E}[w(h_{i},t_{i+1})]
. We can get
\mathbb{E}[w(h_{i},t_{i+1})]=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{u\in e_{i}}\sum_{w\in e_{i+1}}w(u,w)
. Let
e_{i}=u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}
and
e_{i+1}=o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}
. By the triangle inequality, we can get that

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in e_{i}}\sum_{w\in e_{i+1}}w(u,w)=

\displaystyle\ w(u^{\prime},o^{\prime})+w(u^{\prime},o^{\prime\prime})+w(u^{\prime\prime},o^{\prime})+w(u^{\prime\prime},o^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ w(o^{\prime},o^{\prime\prime})+w(u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime})=\ w(e_{i})+w(e_{i+1}).

Therefore,
\mathbb{E}[w(h_{i},t_{i+1})]\geq\frac{1}{4}(w(e_{i})+w(e_{i+1}))
for
1\leq i<m
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

It holds that
\mathbb{E}[w(C_{i})]\geq\frac{3m+1}{2m}w(\mathcal{S}_{i})
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle w(C_{i})=

\displaystyle\ w(v,t_{1})+w(v,h_{m})+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(w(t_{i},h_{i})+w(h_{i},t_{i+1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ w(\mathcal{S}_{i})+w(v,t_{1})+w(v,h_{m})+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}w(h_{i},t_{i+1}).

We can get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[w(C_{i})]\geq

\displaystyle\ w(\mathcal{S}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}(w(e_{1})+w(e_{m}))+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(w(e_{i})+w(e_{i+1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ w(\mathcal{S}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}(w(e_{1})+w(e_{m}))+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{S}_{i})-\frac{1}{4}(w(e_{1})+w(e_{m}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{S}_{i})+\frac{1}{4}(w(e_{1})+w(e_{m}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2m}\right)w(\mathcal{S}_{i})=\frac{3m+1}{2m}w(\mathcal{S}_{i}),

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
9
, and the second from
w(e_{1})+w(e_{m})\geq(2/m)w(\mathcal{S}_{i})
by the algorithm.
∎

, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}+\frac{2k+3}{4k}\right)\cdot\max\{w(\mathcal{P}_{k}),w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k})\}

\displaystyle\ \geq\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}w(\mathcal{P}_{k})+\frac{2k+3}{4k}w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle\ \geq\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{5k-10}{8k}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})+\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{2k+3}{4k}(w(\mathcal{M}_{n/2})+w(\mathcal{M}_{p}))

\displaystyle\ =\left(\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{5k-10}{8k}+\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{2k+3}{4k}\right)w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{9k-4}{8k}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k}).

Hence,
\max\{w(\mathcal{P}_{k}),w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{k})\}\geq\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}\cdot\frac{9k-4}{8k}/(\frac{3k-4}{2k-4}+\frac{2k+3}{4k})w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})=\frac{27k^{2}-48k+16}{32k^{2}-36k-24}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{k})
. ∎

The approximation ratio in Theorem
18
is better than
7/8\cdot(1-1/k)
for even
10\geq k\geq 6
(see Table

). For metric
4
PP, the ratio is even worse than the ratio
3/4
in
[
11
]
. But, in the next section, we will show an improved
14/17\approx 0.823
-approximation algorithm for this case.

5.1
General 4CP

Some structural properties between the minimum weight
4
-cycle packing and the minimum weight matching of size
n/2
were proved in
[
23
,

. The properties can be directly extended to the maximum weight
4
-cycle packing and the maximum weight matching of size
n/2
.

Lemma 19

(
[
23
,
31
]
)
.

Given the maximum weight 4-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}
and the maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
of size
n/2
, there is a way to color edges in
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}
with red and blue such that

(1)

the blue (resp., red) edges form a matching of size
n/2

\mathcal{M}_{b}
(resp.,
\mathcal{M}_{r}
);

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}=\mathcal{M}_{b}\cup\mathcal{M}_{r}
;

(3)

\mathcal{M}_{b}\cup\mathcal{M}^{*}
is a cycle packing such that the length of every cycle is divisible by 4.

Next, we describe the approximation algorithm for general 4CP, denoted by Alg.6.

Step 1.
Find a maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
of size
n/2
.

Step 2.
Construct a multi-graph
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
such that there are
n/2
super-vertices one-to-one corresponding to the
n/2
edges in
\mathcal{M}^{*}
, i.e., there is a function
f
, and for two super-vertices
f(e_{i}),f(e_{j})
such that
e_{i},e_{j}\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
, there are four super-edges
f(e_{i})f(e_{j})
between them, corresponding to the four edges
uv
with a weight of
w(u,v)
(
u\in e_{i},v\in e_{j}
).

Step 3.
Find a maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
of size
n/4
in graph
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}^{*}\cup\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
corresponds to a
4
-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
in graph
G
.

Step 4.
Obtain a
4
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{4}
by completing the
4
-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
.

We can get that
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{P}_{4})=w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})
.

Lemma 20
.

w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{b})
.

Proof.

For every cycle of
\mathcal{M}_{b}\cup\mathcal{M}^{*}
, since the length is divisible by 4, the number of blue edges is even, and then it corresponds to an even cycle in
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
. Hence, we can obtain two matchings of size
n/4
by decomposing the blue edges in
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
is the maximum weight matching, we can get
w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{b})
.
∎

Lemma 21
.

w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
.

Proof.

Recall that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})=w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})
. By Lemma
20
, we have

w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{b}).

Note that
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq\max\{w(\mathcal{M}_{b}),w(\mathcal{M}_{r})\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}=\mathcal{M}_{b}\cup\mathcal{M}_{r}
. Hence, we can get

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{P}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{b})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{r})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}).

∎

Theorem 22
.

For general 4CP, Alg.6 is a polynomial-time
3/4
-approximation algorithm.

Proof.

Recall that
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{P}_{4})
.
By Lemma
21
, we have
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
. Moreover, by Lemma
7
, we have
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
. So,
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq\frac{3}{4}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
.
∎

5.2
Metric 4CP

Li and Yu
[
21
]
proved an almost trivial approximation ratio of
3/4
. We show that their algorithm, denoted by Alg.7, actually achieves an approximation ratio of
5/6
. Moreover, we will give a tight example for this algorithm.

Step 1.
Find a maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
of size
n/2
.

Step 2.
Construct a multi-graph
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
such that there are
n/2
super-vertices one-to-one corresponding to the
n/2
edges in
\mathcal{M}^{*}
, i.e., there is a function
f
, and for two super-vertices
f(e_{i}),f(e_{j})
such that
e_{i},e_{j}\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
, there are two super-edges
f(e_{i})f(e_{j})
between them, corresponding to the edge sets
\{uz,xy\}
and
\{uy,xz\}
with a weight of
w(u,z)+w(x,y)
and
w(u,y)+w(x,z)
(
ux\in e_{i},yz\in e_{j}

Find a maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
of size
n/4
in graph
G/\mathcal{M}^{*}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}^{*}\cup\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
corresponds to a
4
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{4}
in graph
G
if we decompose each super-edge of
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
into two normal edges.

Step 4.
Return
\mathcal{C}_{4}
.

Note that
\mathcal{C}_{4}
is the maximum weight 4-cycle packing containing the edges of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
by the optimality of
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
. Recall that we can get a 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
such that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})

\mathcal{P}_{4}=\{u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}

\mathcal{M}^{*}
represents the edge set
\{u_{i}x_{i},y_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}}
denote the edge set
\{u_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
. We have the following bounds.

Lemma 23
.

w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq\frac{3}{4}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})+w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}})
.

Proof.

Obtain a
4
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
such that
C^{\prime}_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}u_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
for every 4-path
u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{4}
. Then, we can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})=w(\mathcal{P}_{4})+w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}})
. Since
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
contains the edges of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}_{4}
is the maximum weight 4-cycle packing containing the edges of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
, we have
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})
.
Recall that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
. Moreover, by the proof of Theorem

, we have
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{3}{4}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
. So, we have

w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})\geq\frac{3}{4}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})+w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}}).

∎

Lemma 24
.

w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})-2w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}})
.

Proof.

Obtain a
4
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4}
such that
C^{\prime\prime}_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}z_{i}y_{i}u_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4}
for every 4-path
u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{4}
. Then, we can get

w(C^{\prime\prime}_{i})=w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(x_{i},z_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})\geq 2(w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i}))-2w(u_{i},z_{i})

since
w(x_{i},z_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})+2w(u_{i},z_{i})\geq w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i})
by the triangle inequality. Recall that
\mathcal{M}^{*}
represents the edge set
\{u_{i}x_{i},y_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
and
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}}
is the edge set
\{u_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
. Then, we can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{*})-2w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}})
. Since
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4}
contains the edges of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}_{4}
is the maximum weight 4-cycle packing containing the edges of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
, we have
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4})
. Recall that
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
by Lemma
9
. So, we have

w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})-2w(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}}).

∎

Theorem 25
.

For metric 4CP, Alg.7 is a polynomial-time

and

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})+w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})=\frac{5}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4}).

Therefore, we have
w(\mathcal{C}_{4})\geq\frac{5}{6}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
, and the approximation ratio is
5/6
.

5.3
Metric 4PP

At last, we will consider metric 4PP. Recall that we can get a 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
such that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
by Lemma
21
. For metric 4PP, we will construct another 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4}
. The algorithm, denoted by Alg.8, is shown as follows.

Step 1.
Obtain a 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}_{4}
such that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
using Alg.6.

Step 2.
Obtain a maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
of size
n/4
in graph
G
. Note that there are also
n/2
isolated vertices not covered by
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
.

Step 3.
Arbitrarily assign two isolated vertices
u_{i},z_{i}
for each edge
x_{i}y_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
. Assume w.l.o.g. that
w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},z_{i})\geq w(z_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})
.

Step 4.
Obtain another 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4}
by taking a
4
-path
u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}
for every edge
x_{i}y_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
with the two isolated vertices
u_{i},z_{i}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{4}
be the 4-cycle packing obtained by completing the maximum weight 4-path packing
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}
, i.e., for every 4-path
P_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{4}
, obtain a 4-cycle
C_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}u_{i}
. Then, let
\mathcal{C}_{4}=\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}\cup\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{C}_{4}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are two matchings of size
n/2
, and
\mathcal{M}_{1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}=\emptyset
.
Obtain another 4-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
such that for every 4-path
P_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{4}
there is a 4-cycle
C^{\prime}_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}z_{i}y_{i}u_{i}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
.

We have the following bounds.

Lemma 27
.

w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\max\{\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}),\frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})\}
.

Proof.

Firstly, we show that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})
.
Recall that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
,
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}_{4})=w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})
, and
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq w(\mathcal{M}_{1})
. Then, we have

w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}_{4})=\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}).

Then, we show that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})
. Note that we can get a 4-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4}
such that
C^{\prime}_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}z_{i}y_{i}u_{i}
for every 4-path
P_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}
. We have

w(C^{\prime}_{i})=w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(x_{i},z_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})\geq 2(w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i}))-2w(u_{i},z_{i})

since
w(x_{i},z_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})+2w(u_{i},z_{i})\geq w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(z_{i},y_{i})
by the triangle inequality. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{1}
represents the edge set
\{u_{i}x_{i},y_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
and
\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}
represents the edge set
\{x_{i}y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
. Hence, we can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-2w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})
. Recall that
w(\mathcal{P}_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})
,
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{4})\geq w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})
, and
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq w(\mathcal{M}_{1})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{P}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})=\frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}).

∎

Lemma 28
.

w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})-2w(\mathcal{M}_{1})
.

Proof.

For every 4-path
P^{\prime}_{i}=u_{i}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4}
, we have

w(P^{\prime}_{i})=w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(x_{i},y_{i})+w(y_{i},z_{i})\geq 2w(x_{i},y_{i})

since
w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},z_{i})\geq w(z_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i})
by Step 3 of the algorithm, and then
w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},z_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}(w(u_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},z_{i})+w(z_{i},x_{i})+w(y_{i},u_{i}))\geq w(x_{i},y_{i})
by the triangle inequality. Note that
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
represents the edge set
\{x_{i}y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n/4}
. Then, we have
w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{4}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}}=\emptyset
, we know that
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{1}
is a matching of size
n/4
. Recall that
\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4}
is the maximum weight matching of size
n/4
. We can get
w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})\geq w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})-w(\mathcal{M}_{1})
. Then, we have

w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{**}_{n/4})\geq 2w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})-2w(\mathcal{M}_{1}).

∎

Theorem 29
.

For metric 4PP, there is a polynomial-time
14/17
-approximation algorithm.

Proof.

The algorithm returns a better 4-path packing from
\mathcal{P}_{4}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4}
. By Lemmas
27
and
28
, we have

\displaystyle 17\cdot\max\{w(\mathcal{P}_{4}),w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})\}

\displaystyle\geq 12w(\mathcal{P}_{4})+5w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})

\displaystyle\geq 8\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+\frac{1}{2}w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})\right)+4\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+5\cdot\left(2w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})-2w(\mathcal{M}_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=4w(\mathcal{M}_{1})+4w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})+4w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})+6w(\mathcal{M}_{1})-4w(\overline{\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}})+10w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})-10w(\mathcal{M}_{1})

\displaystyle=14w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4}).

Therefore, we have
\max\{w(\mathcal{P}_{4}),w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{4})\}\geq\frac{14}{17}w(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{4})
, and the approximation ratio is
14/17
.
∎

Note that the approximation ratio is achieved by doing a trade-off between two 4-path packings. A tight example may not be easy to construct. So, we will not consider the tightness of its analysis. We also remark that on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs the current-best approximation ratio is
9/10

[
23
]
.

6
Approximation algorithms on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs

In this section, we study approximation algorithms on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs.
Our motivation is that there are many studies on
\{0,1\}
-weighted graphs
[
2
,
16
,
4
]
but a few on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs
[
23
]
. We show that we can reduce
k
CP/PP on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graph to
k
CP/PP on
\{0,1\}
-weighted graphs with some properties related to the approximation ratios.

Theorem 30
.

For
k
CP/
k
PP, given a
\rho
-approximation algorithm on
\{0,1\}
-weighted graphs, we can obtain an
(1+\rho)/2
-approximation algorithm on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs.

Proof.

We only consider
k
CP, and the analysis can be extended to
k
PP.

The reduction is shown as follows. Given an
\{1,2\}
-weighted graph
G
, we first obtain a
\{0,1\}
-weighted graph
G^{\prime}
by regarding each 1-weighted edge in
G
as a 0-weighted edge and each 2-weighted edge in
G
as an 1-weighted edge. Then, we call a
\rho
-approximation algorithm for
k
CP on
G^{\prime}
to obtain a
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k}
, which corresponds to a
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}
in
G
.
Note that

w(\mathcal{C}_{k})=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k})+n.

It is easy to see that the reduction takes polynomial time.
Next, we analyze the quality of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k}
) denote the optimal solution of
k
CP on
G
(resp.,
G^{\prime}
). Similarly, we have
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n
.
Then, the number of 1-weighted edges in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k}
is
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})
, and the number of 0-weighted edges in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k}
is
n-w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})
. Hence, we have

n-w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})\geq 0.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k}
is a
\rho
-approximate solution on
G^{\prime}
, we have
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k})\geq\rho\cdot w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})
. We can get

\displaystyle\frac{w(\mathcal{C}_{k})}{w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{k})+n}{w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho\cdot w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n}{w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n}=\frac{(1+\rho)\cdot w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n-w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})}{2w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})+n-w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})}\geq\frac{1+\rho}{2},

where the last inequality follows from
n-w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{k})\geq 0
and
1\geq\rho\geq 0
.

Therefore, we can get an
(1+\rho)/2
-approximate
k
-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{k}
on
G
in polynomial time.
∎

For 3CP on
\{0,1\}
-weighted graphs, there is a
3/5
-approximation algorithm
[
2
]
. By Theorem
30
, we can directly get a
4/5
-approximation algorithm for 3CP on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs. We show that based on the property of their algorithm, we can get an approximation ratio of
9/11
.

Theorem 31
.

For 3CP on
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs, there is a
9/11
-approximation algorithm.

Proof.

We use
G
to denote an
\{1,2\}
-weighted graph, and
G^{\prime}
to denote the
\{0,1\}
-weighted graph obtained by the reduction in the proof of Theorem
30
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{3}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3}
) denote the optimal solution of 3CP on
G
(resp.,
G^{\prime}
), and
n_{i}
denote the number of
i
-weighted 3-cycles in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3}
, where
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. Note that
n/3=n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+n_{0}
and
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3})=3n_{3}+2n_{2}+n_{1}
.

We use the
3/5
-approximation algorithm
[
2
]
to get a 3-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{3}
on
G^{\prime}
. Bar-Noy et al.
[
2
]
proved that

w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{3})\geq\max\{2n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1},\ \delta\cdot(2n_{3}+2n_{2}+n_{3})\},

where
\delta=3/4
is the approximation ratio of 3PP on
\{0,1\}
-weighted graphs
[
2
]
.

By the proof of Theorem
30
, the obtained 3-cycle packing
\mathcal{C}_{3}
(by the reduction) with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{3}
has a weight of
w(\mathcal{C}_{3})=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{3})+n
, and it holds that
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{3})=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3})+n
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{C}_{3})

\displaystyle=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{3})+n

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{2n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1},\ \frac{3}{4}\cdot(2n_{3}+2n_{2}+n_{3})\right\}+n

\displaystyle\geq\frac{9}{11}(2n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1})+\frac{2}{11}\left(\frac{3}{2}n_{3}+\frac{3}{2}n_{2}+\frac{3}{4}n_{3}\right)+n

\displaystyle=\frac{21}{11}n_{3}+\frac{12}{11}n_{2}+\frac{21}{22}n_{1}+n

\displaystyle=\frac{21}{11}n_{3}+\frac{12}{11}n_{2}+\frac{21}{22}n_{1}+(3n_{3}+3n_{2}+3n_{1})+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{54}{11}n_{3}+\frac{45}{11}n_{2}+\frac{87}{22}n_{1}+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{54}{11}n_{3}+\frac{45}{11}n_{2}+\frac{36}{11}n_{1}+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1}).

Recall that
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{3})=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3})+n
and
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime*}_{3})=3n_{3}+2n_{2}+n_{1}
. We can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{3})=3n_{3}+2n_{2}+n_{1}+n=6n_{3}+5n_{2}+4n_{1}+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1})
. Then, we have

\frac{w(\mathcal{C}_{3})}{w(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{3})}\geq\frac{\frac{54}{11}n_{3}+\frac{45}{11}n_{2}+\frac{36}{11}n_{1}+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1})}{6n_{3}+5n_{2}+4n_{1}+(n-3n_{3}-3n_{2}-3n_{1})}\geq\frac{9}{11},

where the last inequality follows from
n=3n_{3}+3n_{2}+3n_{1}+3n_{0}\geq 3n_{3}+3n_{2}+3n_{1}
.
∎

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider approximation algorithms for metric/general
k
CP and
k
PP. Most of our results are based on simple algorithms but with deep analysis.
In the future, it would be interesting to improve these approximation ratios, even on
\{0,1\}
-weighted or
\{1,2\}
-weighted graphs. One challenging direction is to design better algorithms for metric/general 3CP and 3PP.
